## The three statements

1. **Describe** translocation as the movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources to sinks.
2. **Describe** sources as the parts of plants that **release** sucrose or amino acids, and sinks as the parts that **use or store** them.
3. **Explain** why some parts of a plant may act as a source and a sink at different times.

## The definition, built from four parts

> **Sucrose and amino acids** + move in **phloem** + **from sources** + **to sinks**.

Leave out any one of the four and the answer is incomplete.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Translocation | movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources to sinks |
| Phloem | tissue that transports sucrose and amino acids |
| Source | part of a plant that releases sucrose or amino acids |
| Sink | part of a plant that uses or stores sucrose or amino acids |
| Sucrose | soluble sugar made from glucose; the sugar carried in phloem |
| Amino acids | made using sugars and nitrate ions; used to build proteins |
| Storage organ | part that holds a food store, such as a potato tuber or carrot root |

## Common sources and sinks

| Usually a source | Usually a sink |
|---|---|
| mature, photosynthesising leaves | growing shoot tips and root tips |
| storage organs being emptied (for example a sprouting tuber) | roots (use sugar in respiration) |
| food store of a germinating seed | developing fruits and seeds |
| | storage organs being filled |
| | flowers (growth and nectar) |

## What sinks do with the sugar

- **Respiration** → energy (growing tips, roots).
- **Cellulose** → new cell walls (growing tips).
- **Starch** → storage (tubers, seeds, roots of storage plants).
- **Nectar** → attracts insects for pollination (flowers).
- Amino acids → **proteins** in new cells.

## Direction of flow

- Phloem carries substances **up or down**, from wherever the source is to wherever the sink is.
- One leaf can supply a shoot tip above it and roots below it at the same time.
- Xylem, by contrast, carries water upwards only.

## Role switching: the rule

- **Source** when the part releases more sucrose or amino acids than it uses.
- **Sink** when the part is growing or storing, so it uses or stores more than it releases.

| Part | Sink when | Source when |
|---|---|---|
| Leaf | young and growing | mature and photosynthesising |
| Potato tuber | being filled with starch in summer | sprouting new shoots in spring |
| Seed | developing on the parent plant | germinating, before its leaves photosynthesise |
| Carrot root | storing starch in its first year | supplying the flowering shoot in its second year |

## Method in steps: explaining a role switch

1. Name the stage when the part is a **sink** and say what it does (uses for growth, or stores as starch).
2. Name the stage when the part is a **source** and say what it releases (sucrose from photosynthesis, or from breaking down its store).
3. Link the change to growth, maturity or season.

## Links to other sub-topics

- **Photosynthesis (6.1):** glucose made in leaves is converted to sucrose for transport in the phloem.
- **Mineral ions (6.1):** nitrate ions absorbed by root hairs are used to make amino acids.
- **Xylem and phloem (8.1):** phloem lies on the outer side of stem bundles and the lower side of leaf veins.

## Answer patterns

- **"Describe translocation"**: the four-part definition in one sentence.
- **"Identify the source"**: look for photosynthesising leaves or a store being used.
- **"Describe the movement in this plant"**: source first, then every sink, with direction (up or down).
- **"Explain why X is a source and a sink"**: two stages, each with its reason.

## Exam traps

- Sucrose, not glucose.
- Phloem, not xylem.
- A sink **uses or stores**; "store" alone is not enough.
- Phloem flow is not only downwards.
- Young leaves are sinks, not sources.
- Roots are usually sinks for sugar, even though they take up water and mineral ions.

## Self-test

1. Define translocation.
2. Name the tissue in which translocation happens.
3. What is a source?
4. What is a sink?
5. Give **two** examples of sinks.
6. Why are roots sinks for sucrose?
7. Explain why a mature leaf is a source.
8. Explain why a young leaf is a sink.
9. A potato tuber is a sink in summer. Explain why.
10. Explain why the same tuber is a source in spring.
11. In which directions can substances move in phloem?
12. State what a growing shoot tip does with the sucrose it receives.

### Answers

1. The movement of sucrose and amino acids in phloem from sources to sinks.
2. Phloem.
3. A part of a plant that releases sucrose or amino acids.
4. A part of a plant that uses or stores sucrose or amino acids.
5. Any two: growing shoot tips; root tips; roots; developing fruits or seeds; storage organs being filled; flowers.
6. They cannot photosynthesise, so they use sucrose from the leaves for respiration and growth.
7. It photosynthesises and makes more sugar than it uses, so it releases sucrose into the phloem.
8. It is still growing and cannot make enough sugar for its own growth, so it receives and uses sucrose.
9. It receives sucrose from the leaves and stores it as starch.
10. Its starch is broken down to sugar, which is released to the new shoots growing from it.
11. Up and down.
12. Uses it in respiration and to make cellulose for new cell walls.
