## Questions

**1.** Hydrogen peroxide oxidises iron(II) ions in acid: H₂O₂ + 2Fe²⁺ + 2H⁺ → 2Fe³⁺ + 2H₂O. Identify the oxidising agent and the reducing agent, using oxidation numbers to explain. **[2]**

**2.** Acidified dichromate(VI) ions oxidise tin(II) ions to tin(IV) ions. The reduction half-equation is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O. Write the oxidation half-equation and construct the overall ionic equation. **[3]**

**3.** In one reaction, hydrazine, N₂H₄, is oxidised to nitrogen gas by iodate(V) ions, IO₃⁻, which are reduced to iodide ions. Water is the only other product. Use oxidation numbers to construct the balanced equation. **[3]**

**4.** Acidified iron(II) solution is titrated with potassium manganate(VII). Explain why no indicator is added, state the end-point colour, and explain why dilute sulfuric acid, not hydrochloric acid, is used. **[4]**

**5.** 5.880 g of impure ammonium iron(II) sulfate, (NH₄)₂Fe(SO₄)₂·6H₂O (Mr = 392.0), is used to prepare 250.0 cm³ of solution in dilute sulfuric acid. Samples of 25.0 cm³ react with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. The titres are: rough 15.35 cm³; then 14.70, 14.95 and 14.80 cm³. Only the iron(II) reacts.

**(a)** Select the titres you would use and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage purity of the sample. **[5]**

**6.** 10.0 cm³ of a bleach is diluted to 250.0 cm³. A 25.0 cm³ portion is added to excess acidified potassium iodide: ClO⁻ + 2I⁻ + 2H⁺ → Cl⁻ + I₂ + H₂O. The iodine reacts with 14.20 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium thiosulfate.

**(a)** State when starch is added and the colour change at the end point. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration of ClO⁻ in the undiluted bleach. **[4]**

**7.** A 1.012 g sample of hydrated vanadyl sulfate, VOSO₄·xH₂O, is used to prepare 100.0 cm³ of solution in dilute sulfuric acid. 20.0 cm³ portions need a mean titre of 16.00 cm³ of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. Use these half-equations and Ar values (V 50.9, S 32.1, O 16.0, H 1.0) to find x.

VO²⁺ + H₂O → VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻
MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O **[5]**

**8.** A student plans to measure the standard electrode potential of the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ half-cell.

**(a)** Describe the half-cell containing the iron ions. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the reference half-cell it is connected to. **[3]**
**(c)** Name two other items needed and explain why one must have a high resistance. **[2]**

**9.** Use these values: Fe²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Fe, E⦵ = −0.44 V; Ag⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Ag, E⦵ = +0.80 V. A standard cell is built from the two half-cells.

**(a)** Calculate the standard cell potential. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the positive electrode and the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the equation for the reaction in the cell. **[1]**
**(d)** The silver nitrate solution is replaced by 0.10 mol dm⁻³ silver nitrate. Explain the effect on the cell potential. **[2]**

**10.** Use these values:

| Half-equation | E⦵ / V |
|---|---|
| Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn | −0.76 |
| Sn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Sn | −0.14 |
| 2H⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ H₂ | 0.00 |
| Fe³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺ | +0.77 |
| Br₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Br⁻ | +1.09 |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ ⇌ 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O | +1.33 |

**(a)** Identify the strongest oxidising agent and the strongest reducing agent in the table. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that bromine can oxidise Fe²⁺ ions and write the equation. **[2]**
**(c)** Predict whether Fe³⁺ ions oxidise Br⁻ ions. Explain. **[1]**
**(d)** Tin is predicted to react with 1 mol dm⁻³ H⁺(aq), yet little reaction may be seen. Give the cell potential and suggest a reason. **[2]**
**(e)** Dichromate(VI) is predicted to oxidise bromide ions. Explain why this may not happen if the solution is only weakly acidic. **[2]**

**11.** A silver oxide button cell is not rechargeable. Use these values: Ag₂O + H₂O + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Ag + 2OH⁻, E⦵ = +0.34 V; Zn(OH)₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn + 2OH⁻, E⦵ = −1.25 V.

**(a)** Calculate the cell potential. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the negative-electrode half-equation for the cell in use. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the overall cell reaction. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one benefit and one risk of lithium-based cells. **[2]**

**12.** A direct methanol fuel cell has an acidic electrolyte. Use these values: CO₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻ ⇌ CH₃OH + H₂O, E⦵ = +0.02 V; O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ ⇌ 2H₂O, E⦵ = +1.23 V.

**(a)** Identify the negative electrode and write the half-equation for the reaction there. **[2]**
**(b)** Construct the overall equation for the cell. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the cell potential. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how a fuel cell differs from a storage cell in the way it supplies energy. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** H₂O₂ is the oxidising agent: O goes from −1 to −2 [1]. Fe²⁺ is the reducing agent: Fe goes from +2 to +3 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the species with its oxidation number change; "oxygen" or "iron" alone is imprecise.

**2.** Sn²⁺ → Sn⁴⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]. Multiply by 3 to match six electrons [1]. **Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 3Sn²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 3Sn⁴⁺ + 7H₂O** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check charges match (+18 each side); no electrons may remain in the final equation.

**3.** Each N rises from −2 to 0, so each N₂H₄ loses 4 electrons [1]. I falls from +5 to −1, gaining 6 [1]. Lowest common multiple 12: **3N₂H₄ + 2IO₃⁻ → 3N₂ + 2I⁻ + 6H₂O** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* N₂H₄ has two N atoms, so it loses 4 electrons, not 2.

**4.** Manganate(VII) is purple and is reduced to almost colourless Mn²⁺, so it acts as its own indicator [1]. End point: first permanent pale pink [1]. H⁺ ions are needed for the reduction of MnO₄⁻ [1]. HCl is not used because MnO₄⁻ would oxidise Cl⁻ to chlorine, giving a titre that is too large [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Purple to colourless" is wrong; the end point is the first permanent pink.

**5. (a)** Concordant titres: 14.70 and 14.80 cm³ (within 0.10 cm³) [1]; mean = **14.75 cm³** [1]
**(b)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 14.75 ÷ 1000 = 2.95 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]. n(Fe²⁺) in 25.0 cm³ = 5 × 2.95 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.475 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. In 250.0 cm³: 1.475 × 10⁻² mol [1]. Mass of pure salt = 1.475 × 10⁻² × 392.0 = 5.782 g [1]. Purity = 5.782 ÷ 5.880 × 100 = **98.3%** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State which titres you averaged; including 14.95 cm³ or the rough titre gives the wrong mean.

**6. (a)** Starch is added when the iodine colour has faded to pale yellow [1]. End point: blue-black to colourless [1]
**(b)** n(S₂O₃²⁻) = 0.100 × 14.20 ÷ 1000 = 1.42 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. n(I₂) = n(ClO⁻) = 7.10 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 25.0 cm³ [1]. In 250.0 cm³, from 10.0 cm³ of bleach: 7.10 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Concentration = 7.10 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0100 = **0.710 mol dm⁻³** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* 0.0284 mol dm⁻³ is the diluted value; the question asks for the undiluted bleach.

**7.** Ratio MnO₄⁻ : VO²⁺ = 1 : 5 [1]. n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0100 × 16.00 ÷ 1000 = 1.60 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]. n(VO²⁺) in 20.0 cm³ = 8.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol; in 100.0 cm³ = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]. Mr = 1.012 ÷ 4.00 × 10⁻³ = 253.0; Mr(VOSO₄) = 163.0 [1]. 18.0x = 90.0, so **x = 5** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each line (moles of what, in what volume); a bare x = 5 shows no method.

**8. (a)** A platinum electrode [1] dipping into a solution containing Fe³⁺ and Fe²⁺ ions at 1 mol dm⁻³ each (or equal concentrations) [1]
**(b)** Hydrogen gas at 100 kPa [1] bubbled over a platinum electrode [1] in 1 mol dm⁻³ H⁺(aq), at 298 K [1]
**(c)** A salt bridge and a voltmeter [1]. The voltmeter has a high resistance so that almost no current flows, giving the maximum potential difference [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), list each condition; "standard conditions" alone is too vague.

**9. (a)** 0.80 − (−0.44) = **1.24 V** [1]
**(b)** The silver electrode is positive [1]. Electrons flow from the iron electrode to the silver electrode through the wire [1]
**(c)** **Fe + 2Ag⁺ → Fe²⁺ + 2Ag** [1]
**(d)** Lower [Ag⁺] shifts Ag⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Ag to the left, so the silver electrode potential becomes less positive [1]. The cell potential **decreases** below 1.24 V [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d), link the equilibrium shift to the electrode potential, then to the cell.

**10. (a)** Strongest oxidising agent: **Cr₂O₇²⁻** [1]. Strongest reducing agent: **Zn** [1]
**(b)** E⦵cell = 1.09 − 0.77 = +0.32 V, positive so feasible [1]. **Br₂ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2Br⁻ + 2Fe³⁺** [1]
**(c)** No: Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (+0.77 V) is less positive than Br₂/Br⁻ (+1.09 V), so E⦵cell would be −0.32 V [1]
**(d)** E⦵cell = 0.00 − (−0.14) = +0.14 V [1]. The reaction may have a high activation energy, so it is very slow [1]
**(e)** H⁺ is on the left of the dichromate half-equation, so lower [H⁺] shifts it left and its electrode potential becomes less positive [1]. It may fall below +1.09 V, so the reaction is no longer feasible [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Oxidising agents are on the left, reducing agents on the right; naming Cr³⁺ or Zn²⁺ is a common slip.

**11. (a)** 0.34 − (−1.25) = **1.59 V** [1]
**(b)** **Zn + 2OH⁻ → Zn(OH)₂ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(c)** **Zn + Ag₂O + H₂O → Zn(OH)₂ + 2Ag** [1]
**(d)** Benefit: e.g. high energy for their mass, or rechargeable [1]. Risk: e.g. toxic materials, or fire if damaged [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), cancel the 2OH⁻ that appears on both sides.

**12. (a)** The methanol electrode is negative (less positive E⦵) [1]. **CH₃OH + H₂O → CO₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻** [1]
**(b)** Methanol half-equation × 2, oxygen half-equation × 3 (12 electrons), then add [1]. Cancel H⁺ and H₂O: **2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O** [1]
**(c)** 1.23 − 0.02 = **1.21 V** [1]
**(d)** A fuel cell uses energy from fuel and oxygen supplied continuously [1]; a storage cell has a fixed store of chemicals, so it runs down and must be recharged or replaced [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Fuel cell equations are given, not recalled; reverse the half-equation with the less positive E⦵.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Averaging a non-concordant titre or including the rough titre.
- Forgetting the dilution factor, or the volume of the original sample, in multi-step titrations.
- Writing "colourless to blue-black" for the thiosulfate end point.
- Using an iron electrode for an Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ half-cell.
- Multiplying E⦵ when a half-equation is doubled.
- Treating a positive E⦵cell as proof that a reaction will be fast.
