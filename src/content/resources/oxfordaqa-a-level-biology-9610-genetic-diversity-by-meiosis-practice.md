## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which event occurs in anaphase I of meiosis?

A: Sister chromatids separate.

B: Homologous chromosomes separate.

C: DNA replicates.

D: Crossing over takes place. **[1]**

**2.** A diploid cell has 12 chromosomes. State the number of chromosomes in each daughter cell after meiosis, and the number of daughter cells formed. **[2]**

**3.** Define the term *homologous chromosomes*. **[2]**

**4.** After meiosis I, a human cell contains 23 chromosomes. State the number of chromatids in this cell, and the number of chromatids in each cell after meiosis II. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** A species of mammal has a diploid chromosome number of 10.

**(a)** Calculate the number of different combinations of chromosomes that can be produced in the gametes by independent segregation. Ignore crossing over. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of different zygotes that could be produced by two unrelated parents of this species, from independent segregation and random fertilisation. **[2]**

**6.** A domestic cat has a diploid number of 38. Calculate the number of genetically different gametes one cat could make by independent segregation alone. Give your answer in full. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how crossing over leads to genetic variation. **[3]**

**8.** A parent has the genotype AB on one chromosome and ab on the homologous chromosome. A large number of gametes was analysed.

| Gamete | Number |
|---|---|
| AB | 410 |
| ab | 400 |
| Ab | 95 |
| aB | 95 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of gametes that are recombinant. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the recombinant gametes were formed. **[2]**

**9.** A student viewed a photograph of dividing cells.

Cell P has four chromosomes lined up in two pairs along the equator. Each chromosome appears as two chromatids joined at a centromere.

Cell Q has two chromosomes lined up singly along the equator in each of two cells. Each chromosome appears as two chromatids.

**(a)** Identify the stage of division shown by cell P and give the evidence. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the stage shown by cell Q and give the evidence. **[2]**

**10.** Compare mitosis and meiosis. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how meiosis and fertilisation together lead to genetic diversity within a species. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Sister chromatids separate in anaphase II, not anaphase I.

**2.** Six chromosomes in each daughter cell [1]; four daughter cells [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A pair of chromosomes, one maternal and one paternal [1], that have the same genes at the same loci, although they may carry different alleles [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 46 chromatids, because each of the 23 chromosomes has two chromatids [1]. After meiosis II each cell has 23 chromatids (single chromatids) [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Haploid number = 10 / 2 = 5 [1]. 2^5 = 32 combinations [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 32 x 32 [1] = 1,024 [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Haploid number = 19 [1]. 2^19 = 524,288 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Give in full" means writing 524,288 and not 2^19.

**7.** In prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair up and non-sister chromatids break and rejoin to each other [1], so sections of chromatids are exchanged between maternal and paternal chromosomes [1]. This produces new combinations of alleles on the same chromosome, so the daughter cells differ genetically from each other and from the parent [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Recombinant gametes = 95 + 95 = 190 [1]. Total = 410 + 400 + 95 + 95 = 1,000, so 190 / 1,000 x 100 = 19 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Crossing over occurred between the two genes in prophase I [1], exchanging sections between non-sister chromatids, so that A became linked with b and a with B [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Metaphase I [1]. Homologous chromosomes are paired at the equator [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Metaphase II [1]. There are single chromosomes (each with two chromatids) at the equator in each of two cells, because the cell has already divided once and the homologous pairs are no longer together [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Any four from: mitosis has one nuclear division but meiosis has two [1]; mitosis forms two cells but meiosis forms four [1]; mitosis produces diploid cells but meiosis produces haploid cells [1]; mitosis produces genetically identical cells but meiosis produces genetically different cells [1]; homologous chromosomes pair up and cross over in meiosis but not in mitosis [1]; in mitosis chromatids separate, but in meiosis homologous chromosomes separate in the first division [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Meiosis halves the chromosome number, producing haploid gametes, so the diploid number is restored at fertilisation [1]. In prophase I crossing over exchanges sections between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes, giving new combinations of alleles [1]. In metaphase I homologous pairs line up independently of each other [1], so the maternal and paternal chromosomes are distributed at random to the daughter cells [1]. This gives 2^n different chromosome combinations in the gametes [1]. At fertilisation, any male gamete can fuse with any female gamete at random, which further increases the variety of genotypes in the offspring [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that sister chromatids separate in anaphase I.
- Not giving the numbers of cells and chromosomes in meiosis.
- Describing crossing over without saying between non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes.
- Writing "random assortment" without saying that it applies to whole chromosomes.
- Forgetting that fertilisation is a separate source of variation.

Not necessarily. The specification asks you to describe and explain chromosome behaviour in drawings or photographs of meiosis. Practise with a few labelled sketches.
