## The seven characteristics — MRS GREN

| Letter | Process | Definition |
|---|---|---|
| **M** | Movement | An action by an organism or part of an organism causing a change of position or place |
| **R** | Respiration | The chemical reactions in cells that **break down nutrient molecules and release energy for metabolism** |
| **S** | Sensitivity | The ability to detect and respond to changes in the internal or external environment |
| **G** | Growth | A **permanent** increase in size and dry mass |
| **R** | Reproduction | The processes that make more of the same kind of organism |
| **E** | Excretion | The removal of **the waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements** |
| **N** | Nutrition | The taking in of materials for energy, growth and development |

Two definitions carry the marks and are usually the ones dropped:

- **Growth** must say *permanent* and *dry mass* — a plant that absorbs water gains mass without growing.
- **Excretion** is waste made **by the body's own reactions**. Egestion is undigested food passing out — it was never absorbed, so it is not excretion. Confusing the two is the most common error in the topic.

## Classification

Species: a group of organisms that can reproduce to produce **fertile** offspring.

**Binomial system** — *Genus species*. Genus capitalised, species lower case, both italicised or underlined. It is universal, avoiding the confusion caused by common names varying between languages and regions.

**(Supplement)** Modern classification uses **DNA base sequences**: the more similar the sequences of two species, the more closely related they are and the more recently they shared a common ancestor. This is more reliable than appearance, and it usually confirms groupings originally based on morphology.

## The five kingdoms (Supplement)

| Kingdom | Cells | Wall | Nutrition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Animal** | Multicellular, no wall | — | Ingest food |
| **Plant** | Multicellular, chloroplasts | Cellulose | **Photosynthesis** |
| **Fungus** | Multicellular (mostly), hyphae | **Chitin** | Saprotrophic — secrete enzymes, absorb |
| **Prokaryote** | Single, **no nucleus** | Peptidoglycan | Various |
| **Protoctist** | Mostly single, some multicellular; **has a nucleus** | Sometimes | Various |

The distinction between prokaryote and protoctist is the presence of a **nucleus**, not size.

## Vertebrate groups

| Group | Skin | Eggs | Temperature |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fish** | Wet scales | In water, no shell | Variable |
| **Amphibian** | Moist, smooth | In water, jelly-coated | Variable |
| **Reptile** | Dry scales | On land, **soft leathery shell** | Variable |
| **Bird** | **Feathers** | On land, **hard shell** | Constant |
| **Mammal** | **Hair or fur**, mammary glands | Live young (mostly) | Constant |

Feathers, fur and the shell type are the single features that identify each group without ambiguity.

## Arthropods

All have a **hard exoskeleton, jointed legs and a segmented body**. They are separated by legs and body parts:

| Group | Legs | Body | Antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Insect** | **6** | 3 parts (head, thorax, abdomen) | 1 pair |
| **Arachnid** | **8** | 2 parts | None |
| **Crustacean** | Many, varied | Body parts vary; chalky exoskeleton | **2 pairs** |
| **Myriapod** | Very many, one or two pairs per segment | Many segments | 1 pair |

**Counting legs answers most classification questions.** Six means insect; eight means arachnid.

## Plant groups (Supplement)

**Ferns** — have leaves called fronds, reproduce by **spores**, no flowers or seeds.
**Flowering plants** — reproduce by **seeds** produced in flowers.

- **Monocotyledons** — one cotyledon, **parallel** leaf veins, flower parts in threes.
- **Dicotyledons** — two cotyledons, **branching (net-like)** leaf veins, flower parts in fours or fives.

## Dichotomous keys

A series of **paired** statements. At each step choose the one that applies and follow it to the next pair or to a name.

When constructing one: use **observable, contrasting** features only, phrase each pair so exactly one must apply, and avoid anything relative such as "large" without a comparison. Features like colour or behaviour that vary with age or season make unreliable keys.

## Exam traps

- Defining growth without "permanent" or without "dry mass".
- Calling egestion excretion.
- Saying respiration is breathing — respiration is a chemical reaction inside cells.
- Getting the fungal cell wall wrong: chitin, not cellulose.
- Forgetting that reptile eggs have soft shells and bird eggs hard ones.
- Building a key on features that are not clearly contrasting.

## Self-test

1. Give MRS GREN in full, with the definitions of growth and excretion.
2. What is the difference between excretion and egestion?
3. **(Supplement)** Name the five kingdoms and the key feature of each.
4. How do you tell an insect from an arachnid?
5. **(Supplement)** Give two differences between monocotyledons and dicotyledons.

**Answers:** 1. Movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition; growth is a permanent increase in size and dry mass; excretion is the removal of the waste products of metabolism and substances in excess of requirements. 2. Excretion removes waste produced by the body's own chemical reactions; egestion removes undigested food that was never absorbed. 3. Animal (multicellular, ingests food), Plant (chloroplasts, cellulose wall, photosynthesis), Fungus (chitin wall, saprotrophic), Prokaryote (no nucleus), Protoctist (has a nucleus, mostly single-celled). 4. Insects have six legs and three body parts; arachnids have eight legs and two body parts and no antennae. 5. One cotyledon versus two; parallel versus branching leaf veins (also flower parts in threes versus fours or fives).

## Frequently asked questions

**What does MRS GREN stand for in IGCSE Biology?**
MRS GREN stands for movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion and nutrition, the seven characteristics of living organisms in Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610.

**What is the difference between a prokaryote and a protoctist?**
Prokaryotes are single-celled organisms with no nucleus, while protoctists have a nucleus. The presence of a nucleus, not size, is the distinguishing feature.

**How do you tell a monocotyledon from a dicotyledon?**
Monocotyledons have one cotyledon, parallel leaf veins and flower parts in threes. Dicotyledons have two cotyledons, branching (net-like) veins and flower parts in fours or fives.

**What makes a good dichotomous key?**
A good key uses paired statements based on observable, contrasting features. Avoid features that vary with age, season or individual, such as size or colour that changes.
