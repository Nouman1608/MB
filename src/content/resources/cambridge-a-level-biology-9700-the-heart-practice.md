The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

## Section A

**1.** Which structure prevents the atrioventricular valves from turning inside out?

A: coronary artery

B: septum

C: semilunar valve

D: tendinous cords **[1]**

**2.** During one stage of the cardiac cycle, left ventricular pressure is 12 kPa, left atrial pressure is 0.5 kPa and aortic pressure is 10 kPa. Which row is correct?

A: bicuspid valve open; semilunar valve open

B: bicuspid valve closed; semilunar valve open

C: bicuspid valve open; semilunar valve closed

D: bicuspid valve closed; semilunar valve closed **[1]**

**3.** State where the sinoatrial node is found and its role. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the walls of the atria are thinner than the walls of the ventricles. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why cardiac muscle is described as myogenic. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the internal structure of the mammalian heart. **[5]**

**7.** The left ventricle wall is much thicker than the right ventricle wall, yet both ventricles pump the same volume of blood per beat. Explain this difference. **[4]**

**8.** The table shows pressures on the left side of a heart at five times during one cycle.

| Time / s | Left atrium / kPa | Left ventricle / kPa | Aorta / kPa |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.04 | 1.4 | 1.0 | 10.4 |
| 0.12 | 0.9 | 8.5 | 10.1 |
| 0.22 | 0.7 | 16.0 | 15.2 |
| 0.38 | 0.8 | 9.0 | 12.6 |
| 0.52 | 1.1 | 0.6 | 10.9 |

**(a)** For each time, state whether the bicuspid valve and the semilunar valve are open or closed.

**(b)** Explain why the volume of blood in the left ventricle does not change at 0.12 s.

**(c)** Explain why aortic pressure at 0.52 s is still well above zero. **[8]**

**9.** In a different heart, the semilunar valve opens at 0.14 s and closes at 0.38 s. The next cycle starts at 0.70 s.

**(a)** Calculate the heart rate.

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the cycle during which blood is ejected into the aorta. **[3]**

**10.** Explain the roles of the sinoatrial node, the atrioventricular node and the Purkyne tissue in coordinating a heartbeat. **[6]**

**11.** In some people, the tissue between the atria and the ventricles does not insulate properly, so waves of excitation can pass directly from the atria into the ventricle walls. Suggest and explain the effect on the filling of the ventricles. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why the heart has its own blood supply through the coronary arteries, and suggest the effect of a blocked branch of a coronary artery on the heart muscle it supplies. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** In the wall of the right atrium [1]; acts as the pacemaker, sending out regular waves of excitation [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Atria only pump blood into the ventricles, a short distance, at low pressure [1]; ventricles pump blood out of the heart through a whole circulation, needing a higher pressure, so more muscle [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** It contracts without needing stimulation by nerves (the beat starts within the muscle) [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**6.** Any five from: four chambers, two atria above two ventricles; septum separates left and right sides; tricuspid valve between right atrium and right ventricle; bicuspid (mitral) valve between left atrium and left ventricle; tendinous cords attach AV valves to papillary muscles; semilunar valves at the base of the aorta and pulmonary artery; left ventricle wall thicker than right; atria walls thinner than ventricles [5]. Total: 5 marks.

**7.** The left ventricle pumps blood through the systemic circulation [1]; which is long and has high resistance, so a high pressure is needed [1]; more cardiac muscle produces a stronger contraction and higher pressure [1]; the right ventricle pumps only to the nearby lungs, where a lower pressure avoids damaging the capillaries [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**8. (a)** 0.04 s: bicuspid open, semilunar closed [1]; 0.12 s: both closed [1]; 0.22 s: bicuspid closed, semilunar open [1]; 0.38 s: both closed [1]; 0.52 s: bicuspid open, semilunar closed [1].

**(b)** Ventricular pressure is higher than atrial, so the bicuspid valve is closed [1]; but lower than aortic, so the semilunar valve is closed; no blood can enter or leave [1].

**(c)** The elastic wall of the aorta recoils after being stretched during systole, maintaining pressure [1]. Total: 8 marks.

*Examiner insight:* work every row with the two pressure rules; guessing from the time of the cycle fails on rows such as 0.38 s.

**9. (a)** 60 ÷ 0.70 = **86 beats per minute** [1].

**(b)** 0.38 − 0.14 = 0.24 s [1]; 0.24 ÷ 0.70 × 100 = **34 %** [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**10.** SAN is the pacemaker, sending out waves of excitation [1]; which spread across both atria, so they contract together [1]; non-conducting tissue prevents the wave passing directly to the ventricles [1]; AVN passes the wave on after a short delay [1]; so atria empty fully before ventricles contract [1]; Purkyne tissue carries the wave to the apex so ventricles contract from the apex upwards, pushing blood into the arteries [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**11.** The ventricles would start to contract at about the same time as the atria, without the delay normally given by the AVN [1]; so the atria would not have emptied fully into the ventricles [1]; so less blood would fill the ventricles and less would be pumped out per beat [1]. Total: 3 marks.

**12.** Heart muscle contracts continuously and needs a constant supply of oxygen (and glucose) for aerobic respiration [1]; the thick wall cannot be supplied by diffusion from the blood in the chambers [1]; a blockage means that muscle receives too little oxygen, so it cannot respire aerobically and may be damaged or die [1]. Total: 3 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating valve positions without comparing the two relevant pressures.
- Explaining wall thickness with "more blood" rather than "higher pressure".
- Calling the SAN signal a nerve impulse.
- Forgetting why the AVN delay matters.
- Heart rate calculated with multiplication instead of division.
