**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. The [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-americas-cold-war/) gives the explanation, and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-americas-cold-war-practice/) let you rehearse essay plans. The study has parts on the USA, Canada and one Latin American country, so keep an evidence set for each.

## Key terms

- **Containment**: policy of stopping the spread of communism.
- **Rio Pact (1947)**: inter-American mutual defence treaty.
- **OAS (1948)**: Organization of American States.
- **McCarthyism**: anti-communist accusations without proof, in the early 1950s.
- **New Look**: Eisenhower's reliance on nuclear weapons and covert action.
- **Alliance for Progress**: Kennedy's aid and reform programme (1961).
- **Operation Condor**: cooperation of South American military regimes against opponents (from 1975).
- **Contras**: US-backed rebels fighting the Sandinistas.
- **NORAD**: joint US-Canadian air defence command.
- **Iran-Contra**: scandal over secret arms sales and funds for the Contras.

## Truman and Eisenhower

- Containment and the Rio Pact; Latin America expected aid but received little.
- McCarthyism: HUAC, Hiss, Rosenbergs, McCarthy's accusations, censure (1954); effects on careers and dissent.
- New Look: nuclear deterrence, alliances and CIA covert action.
- Guatemala (1954): Árbenz's land reform hit United Fruit; CIA backed coup; Castillo Armas; later repression.
- Anti-US feeling shown by Nixon's 1958 reception in Caracas.

## The Cuban Revolution's impact

| Event | Key points |
|---|---|
| Regional reactions | Inspired the left; alarmed conservatives and the US |
| Bay of Pigs (1961) | About 1,400 exiles; no air cover; failed in days; strengthened Castro |
| Missile Crisis (1962) | Soviet missiles; US quarantine; deal: Soviet withdrawal, no US invasion, secret removal of missiles from Turkey |
| Diplomatic impact | Cuba excluded from OAS (1962); some states abstained; Mexico kept relations; militaries strengthened |

## Kennedy and Johnson

- Alliance for Progress: about $20 billion; uneven growth; elites resisted land reform; security took priority.
- Dominican Republic (1965): about 22,000 troops; fear of "another Cuba"; OAS force later.
- Vietnam at home: credibility gap, protests, draft, limits on the Great Society, Tet (1968), Johnson not standing again.
- Regional views: little Latin American support; Pearson's Temple speech (1965); Canada took war resisters.

## US policy, 1968 to 1988

| Leader / theme | Key points |
|---|---|
| School of the Americas / Condor | Counterinsurgency training; cross-border repression by South American regimes |
| Nixon and Chile | Allende elected 1970; CIA attempts to block; coup 11 September 1973; Pinochet |
| Carter | Human rights stress; Panama Canal Treaties (1977), transfer by 1999; narrow Senate approval |
| Reagan | Contras against Sandinistas; Boland Amendments; Iran-Contra; ICJ ruling (1986); Grenada (1983); El Salvador |

## Canada

- Anti-communism: Gouzenko affair (1945); security checks; milder than in the US.
- NATO (1949) and NORAD (1957 to 1958).
- UN peacekeeping: Pearson and the Suez force (1956); Nobel Prize 1957.
- Tension: Bomarc missile dispute under Diefenbaker; trade with Cuba continued; Vietnam disagreement.

## One Latin American country: Cuba

- Rio Pact and OAS: signatory, then excluded (1962).
- Domestic: one-party state, repression, large military; 1976 Constitution.
- US: embargo, Bay of Pigs, lasting hostility, limited cooperation on migration.
- USSR: oil, aid and sugar purchases; Comecon (1972); troops in Angola (1975); Special Period after 1991.

## Debates

- Anti-communism or economic interest as the driver of US intervention?
- Did US policy cause the radicalism it feared?
- Missile Crisis: Kennedy's victory or compromise?
- Carter: real change or rhetoric?

## Essay skeleton

1. Define the argument and your line.
2. Use examples from at least two presidencies or countries.
3. Rank motives or factors.
4. Use a perspective (for example orthodox and revisionist views of US motives).
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was fear of communism the main reason for US intervention in Latin America?"

1. Line: anti-communism was the stated and often sincere reason, closely linked with economic and strategic interests.
2. Evidence for: Guatemala 1954, the Dominican Republic 1965, Chile 1970 to 1973, Nicaragua in the 1980s.
3. Other motives: United Fruit and Guatemala's land reform; ITT and copper in Chile; security of the Panama Canal.
4. Perspective: orthodox accounts (defence against communism), revisionist accounts (economic and imperial interests), post-revisionist accounts (mixed motives).
5. Counter-example: Carter's human rights policy and the Panama Canal Treaties.
6. Judgement: anti-communism was the language, and economic and strategic interests decided where it was applied.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up Bay of Pigs and Missile Crisis.
- Ignoring Canada or the Latin American country in questions on them.
- Narrative without evaluation.

## Self-test

1. What was the Rio Pact?
2. Why did the US overthrow Árbenz?
3. Name one result of the Bay of Pigs.
4. What was the Alliance for Progress?
5. Why did the US intervene in the Dominican Republic in 1965?
6. What did the Panama Canal Treaties provide?

**Answers:** 1. A 1947 mutual defence treaty among American states. 2. His land reform hurt United Fruit and the US suspected communist influence. 3. Castro's position was strengthened and he sought Soviet protection. 4. A 1961 programme of aid and reform for Latin America. 5. To prevent what Johnson called "another Cuba". 6. Transfer of the canal to Panama by the end of 1999.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much on Vietnam?**
Only its domestic impact on the USA and the regional reactions in the Americas.

**Is the Missile Crisis in this study or only in the Cuban topic?**
It is named under the Cuban Revolution's impact.
