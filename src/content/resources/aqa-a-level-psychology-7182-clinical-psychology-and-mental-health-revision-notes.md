## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Statistical infrequency | Characteristic that is rare in the population |
| Deviation from social/cultural norms | Behaviour that breaks a society's unwritten rules |
| Failure to function adequately | Inability to cope with the ordinary demands of daily life |
| Deviation from ideal mental health | Falling short of criteria for good mental health, such as Jahoda's |
| Phobia | Irrational, persistent fear of an object or situation |
| Depression | Mood disorder marked by persistent low mood and loss of interest |
| OCD | Disorder of obsessions (intrusive thoughts) and compulsions (repetitive behaviours) |
| Two-process model | Phobias acquired by classical conditioning and maintained by operant conditioning |
| Negative reinforcement | Behaviour strengthened because it removes something unpleasant |
| Systematic desensitisation | Gradual exposure, with relaxation, up an anxiety hierarchy |
| Reciprocal inhibition | Two opposite emotions, such as fear and relaxation, cannot exist together |
| Flooding | Immediate, prolonged exposure to the most feared stimulus until fear subsides |
| Extinction | Loss of a conditioned response when the stimulus no longer leads to harm |
| Negative triad | Beck's negative views of the self, the world and the future |
| Faulty information processing | Attending to the negative and ignoring the positive |
| ABC model | Ellis: Activating event, Belief, Consequence |
| Musturbation | Ellis's term for irrational "must" beliefs |
| CBT | Therapy that identifies and challenges irrational thoughts and changes behaviour |
| Concordance | Rate at which both members of a pair share a disorder |
| Polygenic | Caused by many genes acting together |
| SSRI | Drug that blocks serotonin reuptake so more remains in the synapse |

## Definitions at a glance

| Definition | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Statistical infrequency | Objective, useful for score-based diagnoses | Some rare traits are desirable; common disorders missed |
| Deviation from social norms | Considers social effect of behaviour | Norms vary by culture and era; risks labelling non-conformity |
| Failure to function adequately | Includes the person's own distress | "Adequate" is subjective; some people with disorders function well |
| Deviation from ideal mental health | Positive focus on what good mental health needs | Too demanding; reflects individualist values |

Jahoda's criteria: positive self-attitude; self-actualisation; autonomy; resistance to stress; accurate perception of reality; environmental mastery.

## Characteristics

| | Behavioural | Emotional | Cognitive |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phobias | Avoidance, panic, freezing | Excessive anxiety and fear | Selective attention, irrational beliefs |
| Depression | Low activity, sleep and appetite changes | Low mood, anger, low self-esteem | Negative focus, absolutist thinking |
| OCD | Compulsions, avoidance | Anxiety, guilt, disgust | Obsessive thoughts, insight, hypervigilance |

## Explanations and treatments

| Disorder | Explanation | Key evidence | Treatment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Phobias | Two-process model (Mowrer) | Watson and Rayner (1920): Little Albert's conditioned fear of a white rat | Systematic desensitisation (Wolpe); flooding |
| Depression | Beck's negative triad; Ellis's ABC model | Negative thinking linked to depression | CBT: challenge irrational thoughts; Ellis's D (dispute) and E (effective belief); homework |
| OCD | Genetic (family and twin studies, polygenic) and neural (low serotonin, orbitofrontal cortex) | Lewis (1936): 37% of patients had a parent with OCD; Nestadt et al. (2010): MZ 68%, DZ 31% | SSRIs such as fluoxetine; tricyclics such as clomipramine if SSRIs fail |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Acquisition versus maintenance**: classical conditioning explains how a phobia starts; operant conditioning explains why it persists.
- **SD versus flooding**: gradual with relaxation versus immediate and prolonged.
- **Beck versus Ellis**: lasting schemas and a triad versus beliefs about specific events.
- **Genetic versus neural**: what is inherited versus how the brain works.
- **Treating symptoms versus causes**: drugs reduce symptoms; CBT targets thinking.

## Exam traps

- Calling negative reinforcement punishment.
- Swapping SD and flooding procedures.
- Listing Beck's triad wrongly (it is self, world and future).
- Saying SSRIs increase serotonin production.
- Using one evaluation point for all four definitions.
- Giving characteristics without the behavioural, emotional and cognitive labels.
- Concluding from twin studies that OCD is entirely genetic, when MZ concordance is below 100%.

## Self-test

1. Which definition of mental health would class a very high IQ as unusual?
2. State two of Jahoda's criteria for ideal mental health.
3. Give one cognitive characteristic of OCD.
4. Explain how a phobia is maintained according to the two-process model.
5. What is reciprocal inhibition?
6. Give one ethical issue with flooding.
7. What do the letters A, B and C stand for in Ellis's model?
8. Give an example of "musturbation".
9. What does the D stand for in Ellis's ABCDE extension?
10. What did Nestadt et al. (2010) report about twin concordance for OCD?
11. Explain how SSRIs work.
12. Give one limitation of drug therapy for OCD.

### Answers

1. Statistical infrequency.
2. Any two: positive attitude towards oneself; self-actualisation; autonomy; resistance to stress; accurate perception of reality; environmental mastery.
3. Any one: obsessive, intrusive thoughts; insight that the thoughts are excessive; hypervigilance.
4. Avoiding the feared stimulus reduces anxiety, which is negative reinforcement, so avoidance is repeated and the fear is never unlearned.
5. Two opposite emotional states, such as fear and relaxation, cannot be experienced at the same time.
6. It is highly distressing, so patients need fully informed consent, and some leave early, which can worsen the phobia.
7. Activating event, Belief, Consequence.
8. "I must always do well", or "everyone must like me".
9. Dispute: challenging the irrational belief.
10. 68% concordance for MZ twins and 31% for DZ twins.
11. They block the reuptake of serotonin at the synapse, so more serotonin remains to stimulate the postsynaptic neuron.
12. Any one: side effects; symptoms return when medication stops; not all patients respond; it treats symptoms rather than causes.
