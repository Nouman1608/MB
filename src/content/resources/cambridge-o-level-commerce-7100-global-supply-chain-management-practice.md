## Section A

**1.** Identify the links in a simple supply chain. **[3]**

**2.** State what is meant by **upstream** in a supply chain. **[1]**

**3.** State **two** benefits of global supply chain management. **[2]**

**4.** Name **two** types of risk to a global supply chain. **[2]**

**5.** State what is meant by **risk assessment**. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A company needs 25,000 units of a part each year. Home production costs $4.80 per unit. An overseas supplier charges $3.10 per unit. Shipping and customs add $0.65 per unit.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per unit when the part is bought overseas. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total saving per year by buying overseas. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **one** risk that the company should consider before changing supplier. **[2]**

**7.** Describe the difference between a simple and a complex supply chain. **[3]**

**8.** A toy company designs toys, buys plastic from a supplier and sells the toys to shops through a distributor. Identify one upstream, one enterprise and one downstream activity. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why suppliers are important to a business. **[3]**

**10.** A clothing company buys cotton from a country where a river flood destroys the harvest. Name the type of risk and explain **two** effects on the business. **[4]**

**11.** A supplier is found to be using child labour. Name the type of risk and explain **one** effect on the business that buys from it. **[3]**

**12.** Explain **two** actions a business can take after a risk assessment to reduce the chance of supply problems. **[4]**

**13.** Discuss whether a small furniture business should buy its wood from an overseas supplier rather than a local one. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Supplier [1], manufacturer [1], consumer [1].

**2.** The suppliers and activities that come before the business, such as sourcing raw materials and components [1].

**3.** Any two from: lower cost of production; lower labour costs; lower cost of purchasing; advancement in logistics; advancement in communication [1 each].

**4.** Any two from: environmental; natural disasters; financial; ethical; technological; political [1 each].

**5.** Identifying the risks, judging how likely and how serious they are, and deciding what to do about them [1].

**6.** **(a)** 3.10 + 0.65 = $3.75 [1].

**(b)** Saving per unit = 4.80 − 3.75 = $1.05 [1]. Total saving = 1.05 × 25,000 [1] = $26,250 [1].

**(c)** Any one developed: delivery could be delayed by distance or bad weather [1], so production could stop [1]; the supplier could fail or the exchange rate could change [1], so the cost could rise [1]; a political change such as a new tariff [1] could remove the saving [1].

**7.** A simple supply chain has few links, supplier, manufacturer and consumer [1]. A complex supply chain has more links, supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer and consumer [1]. Each extra link adds cost, but also does a job such as finding customers or breaking bulk [1].

**8.** Upstream: buying plastic from the supplier [1]. Enterprise: designing or making the toys [1]. Downstream: distribution to shops or selling in shops [1].

**9.** They provide the materials, parts or services the business needs [1]. Reliable suppliers deliver on time and in good quality, so production can continue and goods are not faulty [1]. If a supplier fails, production may stop and customers are let down [1]. Accept: fair prices and credit terms help the business's costs.

**10.** Natural disaster [1]. The business may not be able to buy cotton, so production stops or slows [1]. It may have to buy from another supplier at a higher price, so costs rise [1]. Maximum 4 (accept late deliveries and lost sales).

**11.** Ethical risk [1]. When customers find out, they may stop buying from the business [1], so it loses sales and its reputation is damaged [1].

**12.** Any two developed: use more than one supplier [1] so that if one fails the other can supply [1]; hold extra stock [1] so production can continue during a delay [1]; buy insurance [1] to cover losses from disasters [1]; choose suppliers in stable countries [1] to reduce the chance of political problems [1]. Maximum 4.

**13.** Any six from: overseas may be cheaper because wood or labour costs are lower [1]; may offer a wider choice of wood types [1]; the business may get bulk discounts [1]; but shipping and customs add to the cost [1]; delivery takes longer, so stock may run out [1]; risks include floods, exchange rate changes and political problems [1]; a local supplier is more reliable and easier to visit [1]; a justified conclusion, for example using a local supplier for urgent orders and an overseas supplier for bulk wood [1]. Maximum 6.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: adding a wholesaler or retailer to the simple chain.
- Question 6(b): forgetting to include the shipping and customs cost.
- Question 8: placing the distributor upstream.
- Question 10: naming the risk as "environmental" because a river is involved. A flood is a natural disaster.
- Question 11: not naming the type or giving the effect only.
- Question 13: only one side of the argument and no conclusion.

## Quick reference for checking your answers

| Item | Key point |
|---|---|
| Simple chain | Supplier, manufacturer, consumer |
| Complex chain | Supplier, agent, wholesaler, retailer, consumer |
