This is A Level content, examined on Paper 4. All yields, masses and spray counts are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

## Section A

**1.** (19.3.1) State what is meant by a genetically modified organism (GMO). **[1]**

**2.** (19.3.1) GM salmon carry a promoter from the ocean pout next to the growth hormone gene. Explain the purpose of this promoter. **[2]**

**3.** (19.3.2) Explain why GM salmon are produced as sterile females and farmed in land-based tanks. **[2]**

**4.** (19.3.1) Explain why herbicide-resistant soybean plants survive when the field is sprayed with glyphosate. **[2]**

**5.** (19.3.1) Explain how Bt toxin reduces damage to cotton crops. **[2]**

**6.** (19.3.1) Give two reasons why genetic engineering can improve a crop more quickly than selective breeding. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (19.3.1, 19.3.2) A farm compared two soybean fields of equal area. Field A grew conventional soybean, with weeds controlled only before sowing. Field B grew herbicide-resistant soybean, sprayed with glyphosate during growth.

| Field | Yield / tonnes per hectare |
|---|---|
| A (conventional) | 2.6 |
| B (herbicide-resistant) | 3.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield in field B compared with field A. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the higher yield in field B. **[3]**

**(c)** After several years of spraying only glyphosate, weeds in field B were no longer killed. Explain how this happened. **[3]**

**8.** (19.3.1, 19.3.2) In one region, farmers growing non-Bt cotton sprayed insecticide 12 times per season; farmers growing Bt cotton sprayed 3 times.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in the number of sprays. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain two benefits of this reduction. **[2]**

**(c)** Farmers growing Bt cotton are advised to plant some non-Bt cotton nearby as a refuge. Explain how this slows the evolution of resistance in pests. **[3]**

**9.** (19.3.1) At 12 months, the mean mass of GM salmon on a farm was 2.4 kg and that of unmodified salmon of the same age was 0.9 kg.

**(a)** Calculate how many times heavier the GM salmon were. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the GM salmon grew faster. **[3]**

**10.** (19.3.2) Discuss the ethical and social implications of using genetically modified organisms in food production. **[6]**

**11.** (19.3.1, 19.3.2) A student says: "GM crops always reduce the use of chemicals on farms." Evaluate this statement using herbicide-resistant soybean and insect-resistant cotton. **[4]**

**12.** (19.3.2) Pollen from a herbicide-resistant crop can fertilise a closely related wild plant growing beside the field. Explain why this is a concern. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** An organism whose genetic material has been altered by genetic engineering, e.g. by inserting a gene from another organism [1].

**2.** It keeps the growth hormone gene switched on / transcribed all year round [1], so growth hormone is made continuously, not only in warmer months, and the fish grow faster [1].

**3.** If they escape, sterile fish cannot interbreed with wild salmon and pass on the transgene [1]; land-based tanks make escape into the wild very unlikely, reducing competition with wild fish [1].

**4.** They carry a bacterial gene coding for a version of the enzyme that glyphosate does not inhibit [1], so they still make the amino acids they need while weeds, with the normal enzyme, die [1].

**5.** Plants make the toxin in their cells; larvae that eat the plant ingest it and it damages their gut, killing them [1]. Fewer larvae survive to feed on leaves and bolls, so yield is higher [1].

**6.** Any two [2]: a specific gene is added in one generation rather than over many; genes can come from unrelated species; no unwanted alleles are carried along with the wanted one.

**7. (a)** (3.1 − 2.6) ÷ 2.6 × 100 [1] = **19.2%** [1].

**(b)** Glyphosate kills weeds during the growing season but not the GM soybean [1]. Less competition from weeds [1] for light, water and mineral ions, so more photosynthesis and growth of the crop [1].

**(c)** A random mutation gave some weeds resistance to glyphosate [1]. Repeated spraying killed susceptible weeds, but resistant ones survived and reproduced [1], passing on the resistance allele, so its frequency increased over generations [1].

**8. (a)** (12 − 3) ÷ 12 × 100 = **75%** [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: lower costs of insecticide and labour; less harm to non-target insects such as pollinators and predators of pests; less exposure of farm workers to insecticide; less pollution of water.

**(c)** Susceptible pests survive in the refuge because it has no Bt toxin [1]. They mate with any resistant pests from the Bt field [1], so most offspring do not inherit two resistance alleles / the resistance allele stays at low frequency [1].

**9. (a)** 2.4 ÷ 0.9 = **2.7 times** [1].

**(b)** They carry a growth hormone gene from Chinook salmon [1] with a promoter that keeps it switched on all year [1], so growth hormone is produced continuously and growth does not stop in colder months [1].

**10.** Indicative content, one mark each, to a maximum of 6. Credit at least one benefit and one concern for full marks.
- Higher yields help meet global demand for food.
- Bt crops reduce insecticide use; herbicide-resistant crops reduce ploughing.
- Faster-growing salmon need less feed and time per fish.
- Patented seed is expensive and must be bought each season; hard for poorer farmers.
- A few companies control the seed supply.
- Resistant weeds and pests may evolve.
- Transgenes may spread to wild relatives.
- Possible unknown health effects or allergens; consumers want labelling and choice.
- Objections to moving genes between species, or to altering animals.

**11.** For: Bt cotton needs far fewer insecticide sprays [1]. Against: herbicide-resistant soybean is designed to be sprayed with herbicide, so herbicide is still used, possibly more [1]. Resistance in weeds or pests can lead to more spraying, or extra chemicals, later [1]. Conclusion: the statement is not always true; it depends on the modification and how it is managed [1].

**12.** The resistance gene could be transferred to the wild plant, producing herbicide-resistant hybrids [1]. These would not be killed by the herbicide [1], so they could spread as weeds that are difficult to control / compete with other wild plants [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not linking the inserted gene to the protein and then to yield.
- Saying the herbicide or toxin causes resistance mutations.
- Confusing the source of the growth hormone gene and the promoter in GM salmon.
- Evaluations that do not reach a conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**How are these questions organised?**
Section A has short questions; Section B uses data and extended writing. The learning outcome is in brackets at the start of each question.

**Do I need examples other than salmon, soybean and cotton?**
No. These three are the examples named in the 9700 syllabus for section 19.3. Use them in every answer unless a question gives you a different organism.
