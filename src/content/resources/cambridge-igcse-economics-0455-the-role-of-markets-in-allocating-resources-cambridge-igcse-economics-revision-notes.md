## 2.2.1 The market system

- **Market:** any arrangement that brings buyers and sellers together to trade.
- **Market system:** an economy in which most resource decisions are made through markets and prices.
- **Buyers** reveal what they want through spending; **sellers** offer what earns them revenue and profit.
- **Product markets:** households buy goods and services from firms.
- **Factor markets:** firms buy or hire labour, land and capital from households, paying wages, rent and interest.
- **Equilibrium:** quantity demanded equals quantity supplied; no pressure on price.
- **Disequilibrium:** shortage (price too low) pushes price up; surplus (price too high) pushes price down.

## 2.2.2 The three key questions

| Question | Meaning | Market answer in one line |
|---|---|---|
| What to produce? | Which goods and how many | What earns profit because buyers pay for it |
| How to produce? | Which factors and methods | The method with the lowest cost per unit at current factor prices |
| For whom? | Who receives the output | Those willing and able to pay the price |

The questions exist because of the economic problem: scarce resources, unlimited wants.

## 2.2.3 The price mechanism

**Definition:** the system in which demand and supply set prices, and prices guide the decisions of buyers and sellers.

| Function | What happens |
|---|---|
| Signalling | Price changes tell producers and consumers that conditions have changed |
| Incentive | Higher profit attracts firms and factors; lower profit pushes them out |
| Rationing | A rising price limits a scarce product to those willing and able to pay |

### The reallocation chain

```
Demand rises -> shortage -> price rises -> profit rises
-> firms expand and new firms enter -> they hire more factors
-> wages and rents rise in that industry -> factors move in
```

The chain runs in reverse for a product whose demand falls.

## Calculation reminders

**Choosing a method.** A firm makes 4,000 units a month.

```
Method A: 12 workers x 250 = 3,000; no machine
Method B: 4 workers x 250 = 1,000; machine hire 1,600; total 2,600
Cost per unit: A = 3,000 / 4,000 = 0.75; B = 2,600 / 4,000 = 0.65
Choose B. If the wage fell to 150: A = 1,800; B = 600 + 1,600 = 2,200; choose A
```

**Rationing by price.** Six buyers would pay at most 80, 70, 65, 50, 45 and 30 for one of 3 tickets.

```
At 45: five buyers want a ticket -> shortage of 2
At 65: three buyers (80, 70, 65) -> quantity wanted = 3 tickets
```

**Profit signal.** Price rises from 25 to 30; cost per unit is 20.

```
Profit per unit: 25 - 20 = 5, then 30 - 20 = 10 (doubles)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market | Buyers and sellers brought together to trade |
| Product market | Where goods and services are bought and sold |
| Factor market | Where labour, land and capital are hired |
| Price mechanism | Prices set by demand and supply guide resource decisions |
| Shortage | Quantity demanded is greater than quantity supplied at the current price |
| Surplus | Quantity supplied is greater than quantity demanded at the current price |
| Labour-intensive | Using a high proportion of labour relative to capital |
| Capital-intensive | Using a high proportion of capital relative to labour |

## Strengths and limits (for "Discuss")

| Strengths | Limits |
|---|---|
| Responds quickly to changes in what people want | Output follows ability to pay, not need |
| No central planner needs all the information | Products people cannot be charged for may not be made |
| Profit motive encourages low costs and new products | Effects on third parties are ignored |
| Wide choice for consumers | Shortages and surpluses take time to clear |

## Exam traps

- Answering "for whom" with "people who need it". The market answer is people who can pay.
- Leaving out factor markets when explaining how resources move.
- Explaining "how" without comparing costs of factors.
- Describing a price rise without the profit link that moves resources.
- Confusing shortage (price below equilibrium) with surplus (price above equilibrium).

## Quick self-test

1. Define the price mechanism.
2. State the three key allocation questions.
3. Who are the buyers in a factor market?
4. Demand for a product rises. Give the next two steps in the reallocation chain.
5. A firm's price rises from 12 to 15 and its cost per unit is 9. By how much does profit per unit rise?
6. Method X costs 1,800 for 600 units; Method Y costs 1,500 for 600 units. Which is chosen, and what is its cost per unit?
7. Why might a firm switch to a labour-intensive method?
8. What does the rationing function do?
9. Name one weakness of allocating "for whom" by price.
10. At the current price, quantity supplied is 900 and quantity demanded is 650. Name the situation and the likely price change.
11. Why do resources leave an industry whose product falls in popularity?
12. Why does the economic problem create the three questions?

### Answers

1. The system in which demand and supply set prices, and prices guide the decisions of buyers and sellers.
2. What to produce, how to produce, for whom to produce.
3. Firms.
4. A shortage appears; the price rises (then profit rises and resources move in).
5. From 3 to 6, a rise of 3.
6. Y; 1,500 / 600 = 2.5 per unit.
7. Wages have fallen relative to the cost of capital, so labour gives a lower cost per unit.
8. It limits a scarce product to those willing and able to pay the market price.
9. People with low incomes may go without even when their need is great.
10. Surplus of 250; the price is likely to fall.
11. Its price and profit fall, so firms cut output and pay less for factors, which move to better-paid uses.
12. Resources are scarce and wants are unlimited, so choices must be made about their use.
