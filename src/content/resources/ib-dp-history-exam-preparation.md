To prepare for IB DP History exams in the course examined until 2027,
practise each paper's own skill: Paper 1 rewards precise source work
in four fixed question types, Paper 2 rewards two comparative essays
from different world history topics, and HL Paper 3 rewards three
sustained regional essays. The
[assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-assessment-revision-notes/)
give the weightings; this page turns them into an exam-day plan, with a
worked example and a checklist.

## How do you tackle Paper 1? (1 hour, 24 marks; 30% SL, 20% HL)

Paper 1 gives four sources (A–D) on your prescribed subject and four
questions. Use the five minutes' reading time to read the questions
first, then the sources with those questions in mind.

- **Question 1(a), 3 marks:** "What, according to Source A, …?" Give
  three distinct points from the source, in your own words. No own
  knowledge.
- **Question 1(b), 2 marks:** the message of a source, frequently a
  cartoon, poster or photograph. Give two points about what it is
  trying to say, not just what it shows.
- **Question 2, 4 marks:** "With reference to its origin, purpose and
  content, analyse the value and limitations of Source X for an
  historian studying …". Make every point about value or limitation
  *for that historian's topic*.
- **Question 3, 6 marks:** compare and contrast what two sources
  reveal. Work point by point, with several similarities and
  differences, and refer to both sources in every point.
- **Question 4, 9 marks:** "Using the sources and your own
  knowledge, …". Build an argument that uses the sources as evidence
  and adds accurate own knowledge; finish with a judgement.

**Timing:** at 2.5 minutes per mark, about 12 minutes for Question 1,
10 for Question 2, 15 for Question 3 and 22–23 for Question 4.

## How do you tackle Paper 2? (1 hour 30 minutes, 30 marks; 45% SL, 25% HL)

Paper 2 sets two questions on each of the twelve world history topics.
You write **two essays, each from a different topic**, each marked out
of 15. Spend about 45 minutes on each: 5 to plan, 35 to write, 5 to
check. Read the wording closely. Many questions ask you to "compare
and contrast" or "discuss" two examples "each chosen from a different
region", and an answer using two states from the same region cannot
reach the top markbands. Build your bank of evidence by theme, not by
country, so you can compare quickly: for Authoritarian states, for
instance, a grid of methods of consolidation, treatment of opposition
and economic policies for each state you studied.

## How do you tackle HL Paper 3? (2 hours 30 minutes, 45 marks; 35% HL)

Paper 3 sets 36 questions on your region (two on each of 18 sections)
and you answer three. Because you study three sections, check your
question choice carefully: you may answer two questions from the same
section, which gives flexibility if one section's questions suit you
better. Spend about 50 minutes per essay. Paper 3 rewards depth: precise
dates, named individuals, statistics where you are sure of them, and
awareness of historians' debates where relevant. Never borrow time from
the third essay; an unfinished third answer costs more marks than a
slightly shorter first answer gains.

## What do the essay markbands reward?

Paper 2 and Paper 3 share the same five markbands (0–15 per essay). The
top band, 13–15, describes a response that is clearly focused on the
demands of the question, well structured, supported by accurate and
relevant knowledge, evaluates different perspectives, and argues
consistently to a reasoned conclusion. Answers that narrate events in
order, even accurately, tend to sit in the 7–9 band because they do not
analyse.

## How should you use the six key concepts?

Change, continuity, causation, consequence, significance and
perspectives tell you what the question is really asking. "Why" and
"to what extent was X the main reason" questions are about causation;
"examine the impact" questions are about consequence; "how far did …
change" questions need both change and continuity. Identify the
concept in the first minute of planning and organise your paragraphs
around it.

## Worked example: Paper 1 Question 2

*Source (invented for practice): a 1960 speech by a South African
government minister defending the pass laws, published in a
pro-government newspaper.* Question: "With reference to its origin,
purpose and content, analyse the value and limitations of this source
for an historian studying the government's justification of apartheid
policies."

```
Origin:   a government minister, 1960, speech reported in a
          pro-government newspaper.
Purpose:  to defend the pass laws and persuade the white electorate.
Value:    direct evidence of the official justification at the time,
          from a person responsible for the policy; the content shows
          which arguments the government chose to use publicly.
Limits:   a public speech for supporters may not reveal private
          motives; the newspaper may have selected or edited it; it
          gives no view of how the policy was received.
```

Each point links back to "the government's justification", which is
what Question 2 rewards. A 4-mark answer needs explicit value *and*
limitations, each linked to origin, purpose or content.

## How should you prepare for the historical investigation?

The IA is not an exam, but its deadlines fall in the same year. Keep
Section 1 focused on **two** sources analysed in depth, write Section 2
as an argument rather than a report, and use Section 3 to reflect
specifically on historians' methods (selection of evidence, bias,
interpretation) as they arose in your investigation. Stay within 2,200
words. See the
[historical investigation study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-investigation-ia/).

## Before and during the exam: checklist

- I know my prescribed subject's two case studies in detail.
- I have practised every Paper 1 question type under time.
- I have a thematic evidence grid for both of my world history topics.
- HL: I can write three essays from my three sections in 150 minutes.
- In every essay I identify the key concept and the command term first.
- I finish every Paper 1 answer by linking back to the question asked.
- I plan every essay for about five minutes before writing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use my own knowledge in Paper 1?**
Only in Question 4, which asks you to use the sources *and* your own
knowledge. Questions 1 to 3 are answered from the sources.

**Can my two Paper 2 essays come from the same topic?**
No. They must come from two different world history topics.

**Can I answer two Paper 3 questions from the same section?**
Yes. You answer any three of the 36 questions for your region, so two
can come from one section if that suits your preparation.

**How long should each Paper 3 essay take?**
About 50 minutes, including roughly five minutes of planning.
