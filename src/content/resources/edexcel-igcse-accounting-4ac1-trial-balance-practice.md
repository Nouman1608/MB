## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State two purposes of a trial balance. **[2]**

**2.** State whether each balance appears in the debit or credit column of a trial balance.

**(a)** Provision for irrecoverable debts. **[1]**
**(b)** Drawings. **[1]**
**(c)** Returns outwards. **[1]**
**(d)** Carriage outwards. **[1]**
**(e)** Bank overdraft. **[1]**
**(f)** Discount allowed. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why a trial balance normally shows opening inventory and not closing inventory. **[2]**

**4.** Explain two errors that would not be revealed by a trial balance. **[4]**

**5.** The debit balances of a business total 96,450. The credit balances, excluding capital, total 38,275. Calculate the capital. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: to check the arithmetical accuracy of the double entry [1]; to provide the figures for the financial statements [1]; to help locate errors. **[2]**

**2.** (a) Credit [1]. (b) Debit [1]. (c) Credit [1]. (d) Debit [1]. (e) Credit [1]. (f) Debit [1]. **[6]**

**3.** Closing inventory is counted at the year end and recorded only when the financial statements are prepared [1], so the inventory account in the ledger still shows the balance from the start of the year [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two, each with explanation. Omission [1]: a transaction is left out completely, so no debit and no credit are made and the totals are unaffected [1]. Principle [1]: an amount is posted to the wrong class of account, such as equipment charged to expenses, but equal debits and credits are still made [1]. Also acceptable: commission, original entry, complete reversal, compensating errors. **[4]**

**5.** 96,450 − 38,275 [1] = **58,175** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Debits 640 + 1,280 + 795 = 2,715; credits 1,200 + 85 + 24 = 1,309 [1]. Balance **1,406 debit** [1].
**(b)** 900 × 3 = 2,700 [1], **credit** column [1]. **[4]**

Heading with "at" and the date [1]. Non-current assets debit and provision credit [1]. Inventory, purchases and returns inwards debit [1]. Sales and returns outwards credit [1]. Carriage inwards and expenses debit [1]. Discount received and commission received credit [1]. Trade receivables and bank debit, trade payables credit [1]. Drawings debit, loan credit [1]. Capital 133,475 − 115,000 = 18,475 [1]. Totals on the same line [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the capital last: total the debits, total the credits without capital, and take the difference.

**8.** Debit: 118,260 + 9,000 [1] − 340 − 420 = **126,500** [1]. Credit: 134,740 − 9,000 [1] + 340 + 420 = **126,500** [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** The difference is 630 [1], which is divisible by 9 (9 × 70), so two digits may have been transposed [1].
**(b)** The difference equals the insurance balance [1], so insurance has probably been left out of the debit column [1].
**(c)** Half the difference is 1,240 [1], so rent received has probably been entered in the debit column instead of the credit column [1]. **[6]**

**10.** (a) Agrees; omission [1]. (b) Agrees; original entry [1]. (c) Does not agree; single-sided entry [1]. (d) Agrees; principle [1]. (e) Does not agree; addition (casting) error [1]. (f) Agrees; commission [1]. **[6]**

**11.** It shows balances at one date only and not the movements during the period [1]. It does not show whether amounts are reasonable, such as whether a provision is adequate [1]. Year-end adjustments, such as other payables or depreciation for the year, may not yet be included [1]. **[3]**

**12.**
- An agreed trial balance shows that total debits equal total credits, so some errors (one-sided entries, casting errors) are unlikely [1].
- But errors of omission, principle, commission, original entry, reversal and compensating errors leave the totals equal [1], and several of these change profit, for example a repair charged to equipment overstates profit [1].
- Conclusion: the statement is wrong; agreement is a useful check but does not prove profit is correct, and year-end adjustments must still be made [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting provisions or drawings in the wrong column.
- Including closing inventory.
- Treating carriage inwards as income.
- Forgetting to use "at" in the heading.
- Correcting a wrongly placed balance by moving it once instead of adjusting both columns.
- Saying an agreed trial balance proves the books are correct.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find a missing capital figure?**
Total the debit column, total the credit column without capital, and subtract. The difference is capital, which goes in the credit column.

**What does a difference divisible by 9 suggest?**
Two digits may have been swapped, for example 1,290 written as 1,920, giving a difference of 630.
