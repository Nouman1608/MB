## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State the rule for valuing inventory and name the accounting concept it applies. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "net realisable value". **[2]**

**3.** A shop holds 60 shirts that cost $12 each and normally sell for $20. Fifteen of them are stained and can be sold for $9 each after cleaning costing $1 each. Calculate the value of the shirts. **[3]**

**4.** Explain why carriage inwards is included in the cost of inventory but carriage outwards is not. **[2]**

**5.** Closing inventory was overvalued by $2,000. State the effect on each of the following for the year:

**(a)** gross profit **[1]**

**(b)** profit for the year **[1]**

**(c)** current assets **[1]**

**(d)** equity. **[1]**

**6.** Goods were sold for $4,800. Calculate their cost if:

**(a)** the mark-up is 20% **[1]**

**(b)** the margin is 20%. **[1]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Bina owns a bookshop. Her inventory at 31 December 2027 was:

| Line | Units | Cost per unit | Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Textbooks | 200 | $18.00 | Sell for $25.00 |
| 2027 diaries | 150 | $6.00 | Out of date; price cut to $2.50 |
| Atlases | 40 | $30.00 | Sell for $34.00; courier cost of $6.00 each to deliver to customers |
| Damaged novels | 25 | $9.00 | Can be sold for $10.00 after rebinding costing $3.00 each |
| Pens | 500 | $0.80 | Sell for $1.50 |

**(a)** Calculate the value of Bina's inventory at 31 December 2027. **[6]**

**(b)** Calculate the value that would be obtained by comparing total cost with total net realisable value, and explain why this method is wrong. **[3]**

**(c)** State the effect on Bina's gross profit of valuing inventory correctly rather than at cost. **[1]**

**8.** Ilyas's financial year ended on 31 March 2028. His inventory was counted on 7 April 2028 and valued at cost, $24,350. Between 1 and 7 April 2028:

1. Goods were sold for $3,750. Ilyas adds a mark-up of 25% on cost.

2. Goods costing $2,100 were received from suppliers.

3. A customer returned goods that had been sold for $500.

4. Goods costing $260 were returned to a supplier.

5. Ten units included in the count, each costing $35, had been damaged before 31 March and can be sold for only $20 each.

**(a)** Calculate the value of Ilyas's inventory at 31 March 2028. **[6]**

**(b)** Explain why the goods sold between 1 and 7 April are added back at cost and not at selling price. **[2]**

**9.** Yasmin's closing inventory at 31 December 2027 was overvalued by $1,300. Her draft profits were $22,500 for 2027 and $25,100 for 2028. Her closing inventory at 31 December 2028 was valued correctly.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for each year. **[2]**

**(b)** State the effect of the error on Yasmin's capital at 31 December 2027 and at 31 December 2028. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why an owner might be tempted to overvalue inventory, and why this should not be done. **[2]**

**10.** For the year ended 31 December 2027 a business had revenue of $96,000, opening inventory $8,200, purchases $61,500 and carriage inwards $700. Closing inventory was recorded at cost, $9,400. This includes goods costing $1,100 that have a net realisable value of $650.

**(a)** Prepare the cost of sales and gross profit section of the statement of profit or loss, using the correct inventory value. **[4]**

**(b)** State the inventory figure in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2027. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Lower of cost and net realisable value, item by item [1]. Prudence [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The amount the goods are expected to be sold for [1], less any further costs of completing, repairing or selling them [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Undamaged: 45 × $12 = $540 [1]. Stained: NRV $9 − $1 = $8, lower than cost: 15 × $8 = $120 [1]. Total **$660** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Carriage inwards is a cost of bringing goods to their present location, so it is part of what the goods cost [1]. Carriage outwards is a cost of delivering goods to customers, a selling expense, so it is not part of the cost of inventory [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** Overstated by $2,000 [1]

**(b)** Overstated by $2,000 [1]

**(c)** Overstated by $2,000 [1]

**(d)** Overstated by $2,000 [1] **[4]**

**6.** **(a)** $4,800 × 100/120 = **$4,000** [1]

**(b)** $4,800 × 80/100 = **$3,840** [1] **[2]**

**7.** **(a)**

```
Line             Cost     NRV        Lower   Units    Value $
Textbooks       18.00    25.00       18.00     200     3,600
2027 diaries     6.00     2.50        2.50     150       375
Atlases         30.00    28.00       28.00      40     1,120
Damaged novels   9.00     7.00        7.00      25       175
Pens             0.80     1.50        0.80     500       400
                                                     -------
Total                                                  5,670
                                                     =======
```

Textbooks at cost $3,600 [1]; diaries at NRV $375 [1]; atlases NRV $34 − $6 = $28, value $1,120 [1]; novels NRV $10 − $3 = $7, value $175 [1]; pens at cost $400 [1]; total **$5,670** [1].

**(b)** Total cost $6,325; total NRV $5,000 + $375 + $1,120 + $175 + $750 = $7,420, so this method gives $6,325 [1]. It is wrong because the expected profits on textbooks and pens hide the losses on diaries, atlases and novels [1], so inventory and profit are overstated by $655, which breaks prudence [1].

**(c)** Gross profit is $655 lower ($6,325 − $5,670) [1]. **[10]**

**8.** **(a)**

```
                                                           $
Inventory at cost on 7 April 2028                     24,350
Add  Goods sold, at cost ($3,750 × 100/125)            3,000
Less Goods received from suppliers                    (2,100)
Less Sales returns, at cost ($500 × 100/125)            (400)
Add  Goods returned to supplier                          260
Less Write-down of damaged units (10 × ($35 − $20))     (150)
                                                      ------
Inventory at 31 March 2028                            24,960
                                                      ======
```

+$3,000 [1]; −$2,100 [1]; −$400 [1]; +$260 [1]; −$150 [1]; **$24,960** [1].

**(b)** Inventory is valued at cost (or lower), never at selling price [1]. The selling price includes a profit that is earned only when the goods are sold, so adding back at selling price would overstate inventory by the mark-up [1]. **[8]**

**9.** **(a)** 2027: $22,500 − $1,300 = **$21,200** [1]. 2028: $25,100 + $1,300 = **$26,400** [1].

**(b)** At 31 December 2027 capital is overstated by $1,300 [1]. At 31 December 2028 capital is correct, because the extra 2027 profit is cancelled by the lower 2028 profit [1].

**(c)** A higher closing inventory raises profit and assets, so the business looks more successful to a lender or buyer [1]. It breaks prudence, misleads users, and reduces next year's profit by the same amount, so it only moves profit between years [1]. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)**

```
                                                $          $
Revenue                                                96,000
Opening inventory                           8,200
Purchases                                  61,500
Carriage inwards                              700
                                           ------
                                           70,400
Less Closing inventory                      8,950
                                           ------
Cost of sales                                          61,450
                                                       ------
Gross profit                                           34,550
                                                       ======
```

Closing inventory $9,400 − ($1,100 − $650) = $8,950 [1]; carriage inwards included in cost of sales [1]; cost of sales $61,450 [1]; gross profit $34,550 [1].

**(b)** $8,950 [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing total cost with total net realisable value.
- Deducting selling or repair costs from cost instead of from selling price.
- Adding back post-year-end sales at selling price.
- Using a margin when the question gives a mark-up, or the reverse.
- Stating only one effect of an inventory error when the question asks for several.
