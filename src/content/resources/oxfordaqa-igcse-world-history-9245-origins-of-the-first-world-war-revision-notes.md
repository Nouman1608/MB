**Condensed notes on the Section B option "The origins of the First World War, c1890-1915".** The specification names two parts: "Two armed camps and increasing tension between the powers, up to 1913" and "The outbreak of the First World War". The full explanation is in the [origins of the First World War study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-origins-of-the-first-world-war/); the question types are in the [Paper 1 Section B revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-b-revision-notes/).

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1882 | Triple Alliance: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy |
| 1890 | Wilhelm II dismisses Bismarck; the treaty with Russia lapses |
| 1894 | Franco-Russian Alliance |
| 1898 | First German Navy Law (Tirpitz) |
| 1904 | Entente Cordiale (Britain and France) |
| 1905 | First Moroccan Crisis (Tangier) |
| 1906 | HMS Dreadnought launched; Algeciras Conference |
| 1907 | Anglo-Russian Agreement |
| 1908-09 | Bosnian Crisis |
| 1911 | Second Moroccan Crisis (Agadir) |
| 1912-13 | Balkan Wars |
| 28 June 1914 | Assassination at Sarajevo |
| 23 July 1914 | Austrian ultimatum to Serbia |
| 28 July 1914 | Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia |
| 1-4 August 1914 | Germany declares war on Russia and France; invades Belgium; Britain declares war |
| 1914-15 | Japan, the Ottoman Empire and Italy enter the war |

## The alliance system

| Group | Members | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Triple Alliance | Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy | Italy stayed out in 1914 and joined the Allies in 1915 |
| Franco-Russian Alliance | France, Russia | Germany feared a war on two fronts |
| Entente Cordiale and Anglo-Russian Agreement | Britain with France, and with Russia | Not a binding military alliance |

- Britain ended "Splendid Isolation".
- Germany feared **encirclement**.

## Wilhelm II and the arms race

- **Weltpolitik:** a world role, colonies, "a place in the sun".
- **Navy Laws** (Tirpitz) against British naval supremacy; **HMS Dreadnought** (1906) started a new race.
- **Militarism:** armies and navies expanded; Russia's army was the largest.
- Effect: Britain moved closer to France and Russia.

## North Africa

| Crisis | What happened | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| First Moroccan (1905) | Kaiser at Tangier supports Moroccan independence | Algeciras backs France; Germany isolated; Entente strengthened |
| Second Moroccan (1911) | German gunboat *Panther* at Agadir | Britain backs France; Germany gets French Congo land; Anglo-French military talks |

## The Balkans

- **Slav nationalism** worried Austria-Hungary; Serbia wanted to unite south Slavs; Russia presented itself as the Slavs' protector.
- **Bosnian Crisis, 1908-09:** Austria-Hungary annexes Bosnia; Serbia angry; Russia forced to back down.
- **Balkan Wars, 1912-13:** Ottoman Empire defeated; Serbia stronger; Albania created; Austria-Hungary alarmed.

## The outbreak

| Stage | Detail |
|---|---|
| Trigger | Franz Ferdinand killed at Sarajevo by **Gavrilo Princip**; **Black Hand** link |
| German support | The **blank cheque** (early July) |
| Ultimatum | 23 July: ten demands; Serbia accepts most but not all |
| Declaration | 28 July: Austria-Hungary declares war on Serbia |
| Mobilisation | Russia mobilises to defend Serbia |
| Germany | Declares war on Russia (1 August) and France (3 August) |
| Schlieffen Plan | Defeat France quickly via Belgium, then turn on Russia |
| Britain | Invasion of Belgium; Treaty of London (1839); war declared 4 August |

## Responsibility

- **Germany:** blank cheque, Schlieffen Plan, naval race.
- **Austria-Hungary:** an ultimatum designed to be hard to meet.
- **Serbia:** nationalism; link to the Black Hand.
- **Russia:** early mobilisation.
- **Britain and France:** alliances; no clear warning; French wish for revenge.
- **Diplomacy failed:** commitment to alliances and military timetables.

## Key people

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Kaiser Wilhelm II | German emperor; Weltpolitik and the naval race |
| Admiral Tirpitz | Built up the German navy |
| Franz Ferdinand | Heir to the Austro-Hungarian throne, killed at Sarajevo |
| Gavrilo Princip | The Bosnian Serb who shot him |
| Emperor Franz Joseph | Austro-Hungarian emperor who received Germany's blank cheque |
| Tsar Nicholas II | Russian ruler who mobilised in support of Serbia |
| Schlieffen | Author of the German plan to defeat France quickly |

## Writing good answers

- **3 marks:** three separate, precise points.
- **6 marks:** one way, with knowledge, an explanation and a consequence.
- **9 marks:** two separate ways, each explained.
- **12 marks:** weigh the given factor against others, compare and judge.
- Separate long-term causes (alliances, arms race, rivalries) from the trigger (Sarajevo) and the July crisis.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alliance | An agreement between states to support each other |
| Entente | An understanding, less binding than an alliance |
| Splendid Isolation | Britain's policy of avoiding alliances |
| Weltpolitik | The Kaiser's "world policy" |
| Dreadnought | A new type of battleship, launched in 1906 |
| Militarism | The belief that military power should be central to the state |
| Slav nationalism | The wish of Slav peoples for unity or independence |
| Blank cheque | Germany's unconditional promise of support to Austria-Hungary |
| Schlieffen Plan | Germany's plan for a quick defeat of France |
| Mobilisation | Preparing armed forces for war |

## Exam traps

- The assassination was the trigger, not the only cause.
- The Entente was not the same as the Triple Alliance.
- The Moroccan Crises strengthened the Entente.
- Britain entered the war because of Belgium as well as France.
- The Schlieffen Plan was made before 1914.
- For 12 marks, compare factors and give a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Which countries formed the Triple Alliance?
2. What was the Franco-Russian Alliance?
3. What was Weltpolitik?
4. Why was HMS Dreadnought important?
5. What happened at Algeciras?
6. What was the Bosnian Crisis?
7. Who was Gavrilo Princip?
8. What was the blank cheque?
9. What was the Schlieffen Plan?
10. Why did Britain declare war on 4 August 1914?
11. Which countries entered the war in 1914-15 beyond the original five powers?
12. Why did diplomacy fail in July 1914?

## Answers

1. Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy.
2. A 1894 alliance in which France and Russia promised support, threatening Germany with war on two fronts.
3. The Kaiser's policy of becoming a world power through colonies and a navy.
4. It made existing battleships obsolete and started a new naval race between Britain and Germany.
5. The 1906 conference supported France over Morocco and left Germany isolated.
6. Austria-Hungary's annexation of Bosnia and Herzegovina in 1908, which angered Serbia and humiliated Russia.
7. The Bosnian Serb who shot Franz Ferdinand on 28 June 1914.
8. Germany's unconditional promise of support to Austria-Hungary in July 1914.
9. Germany's plan to defeat France quickly by invading through Belgium before turning on Russia.
10. Germany invaded neutral Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed in 1839; the Entente also mattered.
11. Japan, the Ottoman Empire and Italy.
12. The powers were committed to their alliances and military timetables, and proposals for a conference were not accepted.
