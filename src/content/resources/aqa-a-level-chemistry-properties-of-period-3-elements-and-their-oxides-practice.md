These questions cover section **3.2.4 Properties of Period 3 elements and their oxides** of the AQA AS and
A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), version 1.2, July 2026, for AS and A-level exams June
2016 onwards. The whole section is **A-level only**. Inorganic chemistry is on A-level Paper 1, and A-level
Paper 3 can test any content.

## Questions

**1.** Which oxide reacts with water to form a solution with a pH of about 9?

- A Na₂O
- B MgO
- C SiO₂
- D SO₂

**[1]**

**2.** A small piece of sodium is added to a trough of cold water.

**(a)** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Give two observations you would make. **[2]**

**3.** This question is about magnesium and water.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of magnesium with cold water. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of magnesium with steam and give one observation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why sodium reacts more vigorously with cold water than magnesium does. **[2]**

**4.** This question is about the reactions of Period 3 elements with oxygen.

**(a)** Write equations for the burning of aluminium and of phosphorus in excess oxygen. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the oxidation state of sulfur in SO₂ and in SO₃, and state what is needed to convert SO₂ into
SO₃. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why a sheet of aluminium foil does not appear to react with oxygen at room temperature. **[1]**

**5.** This question is about the melting points of the highest oxides of Period 3.

**(a)** Explain why the melting point of SiO₂ is much higher than that of SO₃. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the melting point of MgO is higher than that of Na₂O. **[3]**
**(c)** Al³⁺ has a higher charge than Mg²⁺, but Al₂O₃ has a lower melting point than MgO. Suggest why. **[2]**

**6.** For each oxide, write an equation for its reaction with water and give the approximate pH of the
solution formed.

**(a)** P₄O₁₀ **[2]**
**(b)** SO₂ **[2]**
**(c)** Na₂O **[2]**

**7.** Explain, in terms of bonding, why Na₂O forms an alkaline solution in water, SO₃ forms an acidic
solution, and SiO₂ has no effect on the pH of water. **[5]**

**8.** This question is about the products of P₄O₁₀ and SO₂ with water.

**(a)** Draw the structure of phosphoric(V) acid, H₃PO₄. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw the structure of the sulfate(IV) ion, SO₃²⁻, showing any lone pairs on sulfur. **[2]**
**(c)** State the shape of the sulfate(VI) ion, SO₄²⁻. **[1]**

**9.** Aluminium oxide is amphoteric.

**(a)** State the meaning of the term *amphoteric*. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of Al₂O₃ with sulfuric acid, and an ionic equation for its
reaction with hydroxide ions. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution that reacts exactly with
1.53 g of Al₂O₃ to form NaAl(OH)₄. (Ar: Al = 27.0, O = 16.0) **[3]**

**10.** Phosphorus(V) oxide reacts with sodium hydroxide solution to form sodium phosphate, Na₃PO₄, and
water.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of NaOH needed to react exactly with 1.42 g of P₄O₁₀.
(Ar: H = 1.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, P = 31.0) **[3]**

**11.** Three white solid oxides of Period 3 elements, X, Y and Z, are tested.

- X: very high melting point, insoluble in water, reacts with both hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide.
- Y: high melting point, insoluble in water, no reaction with hydrochloric acid, reacts with hot,
  concentrated sodium hydroxide.
- Z: low melting point, formed directly when its element burns in excess oxygen, reacts with water to give
  a solution of pH about 0.

**(a)** Identify X, Y and Z. **[3]**
**(b)** State the type of structure in Y and in Z. **[2]**
**(c)** Write an equation for the reaction of Y with sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(d)** Write an equation for the reaction of Z with magnesium oxide, with no water present. **[1]**
**(e)** X is classed as amphoteric but Y as acidic. Use the test results to explain why. **[1]**

**12.** A 0.240 g sample of an oxide of sulfur, either SO₂ or SO₃, is dissolved in water. The solution needs
60.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide to react completely. Each mole of the acid formed reacts with
two moles of NaOH. (Ar: O = 16.0, S = 32.1)

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of NaOH used. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of the oxide in the sample. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the Mr of the oxide and identify it. **[2]**
**(d)** Write an equation for the reaction of the acid formed with sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(e)** Solutions of the two possible acids are made at the same concentration. State which has the higher
pH and explain why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** (MgO) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give one letter only; two letters score nothing.

**2. (a)** `2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂` balanced [1]; state symbols (s), (l), (aq), (g) correct [1]
**(b)** Any two from: floats; melts into a ball; fizzes; moves around; disappears [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hydrogen is produced" is a conclusion, not an observation; give what you would see, such as fizzing.

**3. (a)** `Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂` [1]
**(b)** `Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂` [1]; burns with a bright white flame, or white solid forms [1]
**(c)** Na loses one electron per atom, but Mg must lose two [1]; the total energy needed to form Mg²⁺ is
much greater, so Mg reacts more slowly [1]
*Examiner insight:* Swapping the products (MgO for cold water, Mg(OH)₂ for steam) loses both equation marks, even when each equation is balanced.

**4. (a)** `4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃` [1]; `P₄ + 5O₂ → P₄O₁₀` [1]
**(b)** +4 in SO₂ and +6 in SO₃ [1]; a catalyst (vanadium(V) oxide) and heat, with more oxygen [1]
**(c)** The surface is covered by a thin protective layer of Al₂O₃ that stops further reaction [1]
*Examiner insight:* Oxidation states need the sign; "4" and "6" without "+" may not be accepted.

**5. (a)** SiO₂ is macromolecular, with strong covalent bonds throughout [1]; SO₃ is simple molecular, held
by weak van der Waals forces between molecules [1]; much more energy is needed to break the many covalent
bonds in SiO₂ than to overcome the intermolecular forces in SO₃ [1]
**(b)** Both are giant ionic lattices [1]; Mg²⁺ has a higher charge and a smaller radius than Na⁺ [1]; so
the attraction between Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ is stronger and needs more energy to overcome [1]
**(c)** Al³⁺ is small and highly charged, so it polarises the oxide ions [1]; the bonding has some covalent
character, so it is not fully ionic [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), saying covalent bonds break in SO₃ contradicts your answer and costs the SO₃ mark.

**6. (a)** `P₄O₁₀ + 6H₂O → 4H₃PO₄` [1]; pH 0 to 1 [1]
**(b)** `SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃` [1]; pH 2 to 3 [1]
**(c)** `Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH` [1]; pH 13 to 14 [1]
*Examiner insight:* A pH outside a reasonable range (for example pH 5 for SO₂) loses the mark even if the equation is right.

**7.** Na₂O is ionic and contains O²⁻ ions [1]; oxide ions react with water to form OH⁻ ions,
`O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻` [1]; SO₃ is simple molecular covalent [1]; it reacts with water to form H₂SO₄, which
releases H⁺ ions [1]; SiO₂ is macromolecular with strong covalent bonds, so it is insoluble and the pH stays
7 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the bonding type for each oxide; describing reactions alone misses the "in terms of bonding" marks.

**8. (a)** Correct structure: P with one P=O and three P–O–H bonds [1]

```
         O
         ‖
  H–O – P – O–H
         |
         O–H
```

**(b)** S with one S=O and two S–O⁻ bonds [1]; one lone pair shown on S and overall charge 2− [1]

```
          O
          ‖
   ⁻O – S – O⁻     (one lone pair on S)
```

**(c)** Tetrahedral [1]
*Examiner insight:* SO₃²⁻ drawn with three double bonds and no charges, or without the lone pair, loses the second mark in (b).

**9. (a)** Reacts with (or behaves as) both an acid and a base [1]
**(b)** `Al₂O₃ + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂O` [1]; `Al₂O₃ + 2OH⁻ + 3H₂O → 2Al(OH)₄⁻` [1]
**(c)** Mr Al₂O₃ = 102.0, so moles = 1.53 / 102.0 = 0.0150 mol [1]; moles NaOH = 2 × 0.0150 = 0.0300 mol [1];
volume = 0.0300 / 2.00 × 1000 = **15.0 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* With a wrong mole ratio in (c), the volume step can still score as error carried forward if it follows from your ratio.

**10. (a)** `P₄O₁₀ + 12NaOH → 4Na₃PO₄ + 6H₂O` [1]
**(b)** Mr P₄O₁₀ = 284.0, so moles = 1.42 / 284.0 = 0.00500 mol [1]; moles NaOH = 12 × 0.00500 = 0.0600 mol [1];
mass = 0.0600 × 40.0 = **2.40 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take the 1 : 12 ratio from the P₄O₁₀ equation, not 1 : 3 from H₃PO₄ + 3NaOH.

**11. (a)** X is Al₂O₃ [1]; Y is SiO₂ [1]; Z is P₄O₁₀ [1]
**(b)** Y: macromolecular (giant covalent) [1]; Z: simple molecular [1]
**(c)** `SiO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SiO₃ + H₂O` [1]
**(d)** `P₄O₁₀ + 6MgO → 2Mg₃(PO₄)₂` [1]
**(e)** X reacts with both an acid and a base, but Y reacts only with a base [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), give formulas or full names; "aluminium" or "phosphorus" without "oxide" names the element, not the oxide.

**12. (a)** 60.0 / 1000 × 0.100 = 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
**(b)** 6.00 × 10⁻³ / 2 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]
**(c)** Mr = 0.240 / 3.00 × 10⁻³ = 80.0 [1]; this matches SO₃ (Mr 80.1), not SO₂ (Mr 64.1), so the oxide is
**SO₃** [1]
**(d)** `H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O` [1]
**(e)** H₂SO₃ (from SO₂) has the higher pH [1]; it is a weak acid that only partly dissociates, so it releases
fewer H⁺ ions [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the identification must be justified by comparing your Mr with both possible values; a bare "SO₃" scores only the calculation mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving MgO as the product of magnesium with cold water.
- Writing H₂SO₄ instead of H₂SO₃ for the product of SO₂ and water.
- Saying Al₂O₃ and SiO₂ "dissolve to give a neutral solution" rather than "are insoluble".
- Describing melting of P₄O₁₀ or SO₃ as breaking covalent bonds.
- Missing the three water molecules when Al₂O₃ reacts with hydroxide ions.
- Drawing sulfate(IV) without the lone pair on sulfur, or with the wrong charge.
