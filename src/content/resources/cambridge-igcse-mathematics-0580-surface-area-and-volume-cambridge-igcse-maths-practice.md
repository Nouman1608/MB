These questions cover subtopic 5.4, Surface area and volume, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are identical, so every question suits both tiers. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Use the List of formulas for the cylinder, cone, sphere, prism and pyramid; the cuboid formulas are not given.

For explanations, read the [surface area and volume study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-surface-area-and-volume/); for a summary, use the [surface area and volume revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-surface-area-and-volume-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A cuboid measures 7 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its surface area. **[2]**

**2.** (non-calculator) A closed cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 9 cm. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[1]**

**(b)** Find its curved surface area. **[1]**

**(c)** Find its total surface area. **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) A sphere has diameter 18 cm. Give each answer in terms of π.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[2]**

**(b)** Find its surface area. **[1]**

**4.** (non-calculator) A prism is 20 cm long. Its cross-section is a trapezium with parallel sides 4 cm and 10 cm, which are 5 cm apart. Find the volume of the prism. **[3]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A pyramid has a rectangular base measuring 9 cm by 8 cm and a perpendicular height of 12 cm. Find its volume. **[2]**

**6.** (calculator) A solid cone has base radius 7 cm and perpendicular height 24 cm.

**(a)** Find its slant height. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its volume. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate its total surface area. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) A cylindrical can holds exactly 1 litre. Its internal diameter is 10 cm. Calculate its internal height. **[3]**

**8.** (calculator) A sphere has volume 2000 cm³. Calculate its surface area. **[4]**

**9.** (calculator) 9 litres of water are poured into an empty cylindrical tank of radius 10 cm. Calculate the depth of the water. **[3]**

**10.** (non-calculator) A pyramid has a square base of side 10 cm. Its apex is 12 cm vertically above the centre of the base.

**(a)** Find its volume. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the height of each triangular face (the slant height). **[2]**

**(c)** Find the total surface area of the pyramid. **[2]**

**11.** (non-calculator) A solid metal sphere of radius 6 cm is melted down and made into solid cones, each with radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. No metal is wasted. How many cones are made? **[3]**

**12.** (non-calculator) A cylinder of radius 5 cm and height 8 cm has the same volume as a cone of radius 10 cm. Find the height of the cone. **[3]**

**13.** (calculator) A wedge of cheese is a prism. Its cross-section is a sector of a circle with radius 15 cm and angle 72°, and it is 4 cm thick.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the wedge. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its total surface area. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 7 × 5 × 3 = **105 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 2(35 + 21 + 15) [1] = **142 cm²** [1]

**2. (a)** π × 16 × 9 = **144π cm³** [1]

**(b)** 2π × 4 × 9 = **72π cm²** [1]

**(c)** 72π + 2 × 16π = **104π cm²** [1]

**3. (a)** r = 9 cm [1]; V = (4/3)π × 729 = **972π cm³** [1]

**(b)** 4π × 81 = **324π cm²** [1]

**4.** Cross-section area = ½ × (4 + 10) × 5 [1] = 35 cm² [1]; volume = 35 × 20 = **700 cm³** [1]

**5.** (1/3) × 9 × 8 × 12 [1] = **288 cm³** [1]

**6. (a)** l = √(7² + 24²) = **25 cm** [1]

**(b)** (1/3)π × 49 × 24 = 392π [1] = **1230 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(c)** π × 7 × 25 + π × 49 = 224π [1] = **704 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* the curved area uses the slant height, 25 cm; the volume uses the perpendicular height, 24 cm. Swapping them is a common error.

**7.** 1 litre = 1000 cm³ and r = 5 cm [1]; π × 25 × h = 1000 [1]; h = **12.7 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**8.** (4/3)πr³ = 2000 [1]; r³ = 477.46…, r = 7.815… cm [1]; SA = 4π × 7.815…² [1] = **768 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* rounding the radius to 7.8 cm first gives 765 cm², which loses the accuracy mark; keep the unrounded radius.

**9.** 9 litres = 9000 cm³ [1]; π × 100 × d = 9000 [1]; d = **28.6 cm** (3 s.f.) [1]

**10. (a)** (1/3) × 100 × 12 = **400 cm³** [1]

**(b)** The slant height runs from the apex to the midpoint of a base edge: √(12² + 5²) [1] = **13 cm** [1]

**(c)** Base 100 cm² + 4 × ½ × 10 × 13 = 100 + 260 [1] = **360 cm²** [1]

**11.** Sphere: (4/3)π × 216 = 288π cm³ [1]; cone: (1/3)π × 9 × 4 = 12π cm³ [1]; 288π ÷ 12π = **24 cones** [1]

**12.** Cylinder: π × 25 × 8 = 200π [1]; (1/3)π × 100 × h = 200π [1]; **h = 6 cm** [1]

**13. (a)** Sector area = 72/360 × π × 225 = 45π cm² [1]; volume = 45π × 4 = 180π = **565 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** Two sector faces: 2 × 45π = 90π [1]; arc = 72/360 × 30π = 6π, so curved face = 6π × 4 = 24π [1]; two rectangles: 2 × 15 × 4 = 120 [1]; total = 114π + 120 = **478 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1]

*Examiner insight:* list the faces before adding; the two flat rectangular faces where the wedge was cut are the ones most often missed.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the slant height and the perpendicular height of a cone or pyramid.
- Forgetting the (1/3) for pyramids and cones.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Missing faces in a total surface area, or including a lid that is not there.
- Converting litres to cm³ with 100 instead of 1000.
- Rounding a radius found from a volume before using it again.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the surface area of a cuboid given in the 0580 exam?**
No. The List of formulas covers the cylinder, cone, sphere, prism and pyramid, but not the cuboid. Find the area of each pair of faces and add.

**How do I find the slant height of a square-based pyramid?**
Use Pythagoras in the triangle formed by the perpendicular height, half the base side and the slant height of a face.
