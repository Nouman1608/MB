Condensed notes on the External influences on marketing content (3.13 on this site) in Marketing within the external environment, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and the Cavell Home Goods and Teague Snacks worked examples are in the [external influences on marketing study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-external-influences-on-marketing/); test yourself with the [external influences on marketing practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-external-influences-on-marketing-practice/). The overview of the whole topic is in the [marketing and marketing strategies revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-marketing-and-marketing-strategies-revision-notes/).

## The specification point

Evaluate the following influences on marketing: social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological, international.

## Core recall

### The eight influences

| Influence | Covers | Effects on marketing |
|---|---|---|
| Social | Population, age, lifestyle, tastes, fashion | New segments; changes in products and promotion |
| Legal | Consumer protection, advertising rules, data protection, safety, labelling | Limits claims and data use; compliance cost; fines |
| Ethical | Honesty, fair treatment, marketing to children | Brand reputation; ethical sourcing as a selling point; higher costs |
| Environmental | Climate, waste, packaging, resources | Demand for green products; packaging costs; greenwashing risk |
| Economic | Growth, income, unemployment, inflation, interest rates, exchange rates | Demand for luxuries and essentials; costs; export prices |
| Political | Policy, taxes, regulation, trade policy, stability | Taxes change prices; trade barriers affect exports |
| Technological | E-commerce, social media, data, mobile payments | New channels and promotion; faster rivals |
| International | Overseas markets, competition, trade agreements, culture | Export opportunities; imported competition |

### Calculations to know

| Calculation | Method |
|---|---|
| Income change | % change in demand = YED × % change in income |
| Exchange rate | New price = sterling price × new rate; % change in price; × PED for change in quantity |
| Cost of a new rule | Unit cost increase × units; compare absorbing with passing on, using PED |
| Ranking influences | Score likelihood (1 to 5) × impact (1 to 5) |

### Evaluating the effects

- Size: how many customers or sales are affected?
- Time: immediate or gradual?
- Control: can the business influence it or only respond?
- Opportunity or threat: often both.
- Response: cost and speed of changing the mix.
- Stakeholders: customers, employees, suppliers, shareholders, communities.

### Responses

| Change | Possible response |
|---|---|
| Recession | Value range, instalment payments, focus on essentials |
| Stronger pound | Cut sterling price, hedge, produce overseas |
| New packaging law | Absorb or pass on; promote the benefit |
| New technology | Online sales, data-led promotion |
| Ethical pressure | Fair sourcing policy, transparent labelling |

## Quick worked reminder

Cavell Home Goods (invented): incomes fall 3%. Premium YED +1.8 gives −5.4%, so £2.0m becomes £1.892m. Budget YED −0.4 gives +1.2%, so £1.5m becomes £1.518m. Total £3.410m, a fall of £90,000.

Exchange rate: a £50 pan is $62.50 at $1.25; at $1.40 it is $70.00, a rise of 12%. PED −1.5 gives −18% in quantity.

Teague Snacks: 2,000,000 bags; price £4.00; variable cost £2.60; new packaging £0.06. Absorb: £1.34 × 2,000,000 = £2,680,000. Pass on at £4.06 with PED −1.2: 1,964,000 bags × £1.40 = £2,749,600.

## Effects by element of the mix

| Element | Typical external pressures |
|---|---|
| Product | Social tastes, environmental rules, safety law, technology |
| Price | Inflation, exchange rates, taxes, competition from imports |
| Place | E-commerce, transport costs, trade barriers, political stability |
| Promotion | Advertising law, data protection, social media, ethical expectations |

## Link to other topics

- Income and cross elasticities (3.9) show how sales respond to economic change and to rivals' prices.
- The product life cycle (3.6) shows when social change or technology may end a product's growth.
- SWOT (3.2) sorts these influences into opportunities and threats.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| PESTLE | A checklist of political, economic, social, technological, legal and environmental influences |
| Greenwashing | Making misleading claims about environmental benefits |
| Exchange rate | The price of one currency in terms of another |
| Absorbing a cost | Accepting lower profit instead of raising the price |
| Hedging | Reducing the risk of exchange rate changes |
| Consumer protection | Laws that safeguard buyers from unfair practices |

## Evaluation prompts

- Which influence matters most for this business and why?
- Is the effect an opportunity, a threat or both?
- Can the business influence the change, or only react?
- What would the response cost, and how quickly could it be done?
- Do two influences interact, such as exchange rates and exports?
- Finish with a judgement about the marketing mix in the case.

## Exam traps

- Select the influences that fit the case, not all eight.
- Link each effect to a specific element of the mix.
- An influence can be an opportunity as well as a threat.
- Use figures to size the effect.
- Do not assume the business cannot respond.

## Command words

- **Evaluate:** choose the influences that matter most for the business, show the effect on the mix, and judge.
- **Calculate:** use YED, PED and exchange rates step by step, and interpret the result.
- **Analyse:** link the influence to a specific element of the mix and to a stakeholder.
- **Justify:** give the evidence from the case that shows the influence matters.

## Self-test

1. List the eight influences named in the specification.
2. Give one effect of an ageing population on marketing.
3. Give one legal control on advertising.
4. What is greenwashing?
5. Income falls 5%; YED is +2. Find the change in demand.
6. A pan costs £40; the exchange rate rises from $1.20 to $1.30. Find the new dollar price.
7. A rule adds £0.10 to a unit cost for 500,000 units. Find the extra cost.
8. Give one response to a stronger pound for an exporter.
9. Give one way technology helps marketing.
10. Distinguish a legal from an ethical influence.
11. Why might an influence be both an opportunity and a threat?
12. Likelihood 4 and impact 3. Find the score.

### Answers

1. Social, legal, ethical, environmental, economic, political, technological, international.
2. Any one: demand for mobility or healthcare products; a new target segment.
3. Any one: rules against misleading claims; data protection consent; age limits.
4. Making misleading claims that a product is environmentally friendly.
5. 2 × (−5%) = **−10%**.
6. 40 × 1.30 = **$52** (it was $48).
7. 0.10 × 500,000 = **£50,000**.
8. Any one: cut the sterling price; hedge; produce overseas.
9. Any one: online sales; social media promotion; data analytics.
10. Legal influences are required by law; ethical influences are about what is considered right, which a business may follow even when not required.
11. For example online shopping opens new markets but threatens shops.
12. 4 × 3 = **12**.
