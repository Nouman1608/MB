**Key question 1.5 (Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O Level 2147) covers why
nations built overseas empires and what that did to the colonised.**
Learn the five motive types, three African case studies, India in
1857–58 and the USA's war with Spain in 1898, and be ready to compare
them. Full explanations are in the
[overseas empires study guide](/resources/cambridge-world-history-overseas-empires-nineteenth-century/).

## Five motives (the syllabus's own categories)

| Motive | Key example |
|---|---|
| **Economic** | Raw materials (rubber, palm oil, cotton), markets, investment |
| **Military** | Naval bases and coaling stations; the Suez Canal route (opened 1869) |
| **Geopolitical** | Rivalry and prestige; Scramble for Africa; Berlin Conference 1884–85 |
| **Religious** | Christian missions, e.g. David Livingstone |
| **Cultural** | "Civilising mission"; Kipling's "The White Man's Burden" (1899) |

## Three ways of ruling Africa

| | France — Senegal | Britain — Nigeria | Belgium — Congo |
|---|---|---|---|
| Key figure | Louis Faidherbe (governor 1854–61, 1863–65) | Frederick Lugard (High Commissioner, Northern Nigeria, from 1900) | King Leopold II |
| System | **Assimilation**, **direct rule** | **Indirect rule** through emirs | **Private** colony, run for profit |
| Key facts | Expanded inland; *tirailleurs sénégalais* (1857); Four Communes elected a deputy to Paris | Conquered Sokoto Caliphate by 1903; amalgamation 1914; *The Dual Mandate* (1922) | Congo Free State recognised 1885; rubber quotas; Force Publique brutality |
| Impact on Africans | Citizenship for very few; French culture imposed | Traditional rulers kept but strengthened; failed where no chiefs existed | Forced labour, mutilation, millions of deaths; Casement report 1904; taken over by Belgium 1908 |

## India, 1857–58

- **Began:** Meerut, 10 May 1857; rebels took Delhi and named Bahadur
  Shah II their leader. Other centres: Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi.
- **Causes:** greased cartridges (religious offence); annexations, the
  Doctrine of Lapse, Awadh (1856); fear of Christian conversion; army
  grievances.
- **Results:** Government of India Act 1858 — Company abolished, Crown
  rule, Secretary of State and Viceroy; Queen's Proclamation promised
  religious non-interference and no more annexations; more British
  troops relative to Indian; last Mughal emperor exiled to Rangoon.

## The USA and Spain's colonies

- Cuban war of independence from **1895**; US press sensationalism.
- **USS *Maine*** explodes in Havana, **15 February 1898**; war from
  April; Dewey wins at **Manila Bay**, 1 May.
- **Treaty of Paris, December 1898:** Cuba freed from Spain; Puerto
  Rico, Guam and the Philippines to the USA ($20 million).
- **Debate:** imperialists (markets, bases, "duty") against the
  **Anti-Imperialist League** (Mark Twain, Andrew Carnegie).
- **Philippines:** Aguinaldo's independence declaration (June 1898)
  ignored; **Philippine–American War 1899–1902**; then US-run schools,
  English, assembly from 1907.
- **Cuba:** Teller Amendment (no annexation) but **Platt Amendment
  1901** (right to intervene, Guantánamo); occupation to 1902;
  intervention again in **1906**.

## Worked example: a "how far" answer on motives

*'Economic motives were the most important reason for European
imperialism in Africa.' How far do you agree?*

1. **For:** the Congo was run to extract rubber and ivory; West
   African trade in palm oil drew in British and French merchants;
   industrial economies wanted raw materials and markets.
2. **Against:** strategic motives — Britain's concern for the Suez and
   Cape routes to India; geopolitical rivalry — the Scramble and the
   Berlin Conference show powers grabbing land to deny it to rivals,
   even where it brought little profit; religious and cultural
   motives — missionaries and the "civilising mission" won public
   support at home.
3. **Judgement:** for example, economic motives explain why
   businesses and some governments wanted colonies, but rivalry and
   prestige explain the speed of the Scramble after 1884. State which
   mattered more and why.

## Exam traps

- Listing motives without saying which mattered most, or for whom.
- Confusing direct and indirect rule.
- Calling the Congo Free State a Belgian colony before 1908 — it was
  Leopold's personal possession.
- Treating 1857 only as a mutiny of soldiers: civilians and rulers
  such as the Rani of Jhansi joined it.
- Forgetting the "with what effects" half of the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which case study shows the harshest impact on Africans?** Leopold
II's Congo, where forced rubber collection was enforced by violence
and caused millions of deaths.

**Why did Britain use indirect rule in Nigeria?** It was cheap, needed
few British officials and worked through the emirs' existing
government and taxes in the north.

**Did Cuba become fully independent in 1898?** Not fully. The Platt
Amendment let the USA intervene, which it did again in 1906.

## Self-test

1. Name the five types of motive in the syllabus.
2. What did assimilation mean in French Senegal?
3. Give two causes of the 1857 rising.
4. What did the Government of India Act 1858 do?
5. What did Spain give up in the Treaty of Paris (1898)?

**Answers:** 1. Economic, military, geopolitical, religious, cultural.
2. Africans who adopted French language and culture could in theory
become French citizens; rule was direct. 3. Any two: greased
cartridges; annexations and the Doctrine of Lapse; fear of conversion;
army grievances. 4. Ended East India Company rule and put India under
the Crown, with a Secretary of State and a Viceroy. 5. Cuba
(independence) and Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines (to the USA).
