These questions cover sections **1.2 Fractions** and **1.3 Decimals** of Topic 1, Numbers and the number system, in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on that specification. Section 1.2 and the Foundation content of 1.3 are for both tiers. Questions marked **(Higher)** test converting recurring decimals into fractions, which the specification lists as **Higher tier only**. A calculator is allowed on every 4MA1 paper, but where a question says "show your working" or "show that", the written method earns the marks.

Before you start, read the [fractions and decimals study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-fractions-and-decimals/) or the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-fractions-and-decimals-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** Write 54/90 in its simplest form. **[2]**

**2.**

**(a)** Write 29/6 as a mixed number. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 3 4/9 as an improper fraction. **[1]**

**3.** Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first. **[3]**

0.62, 5/8, 0.6̇, 11/18

**4.** Show your working clearly. Give each answer as a mixed number in its simplest form.

**(a)** Work out 2 3/4 + 1 5/6. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out 4 1/3 − 2 4/5. **[3]**

**5.**

**(a)** Show that 1 7/8 × 2 2/5 = 4 1/2. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out 2 2/3 ÷ 1 1/9. Show your working and give your answer as a mixed number in its simplest form. **[3]**

**6.** A school has 840 students. 3/7 of the students are in the lower school. 5/12 of the lower-school students walk to school.

**(a)** Calculate how many lower-school students walk to school. **[3]**
**(b)** 230 upper-school students also walk to school. Write the total number of students who walk to school as a fraction of all 840 students. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of all the students who are lower-school students who walk. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**7.**

**(a)** Write 7/20 as a decimal and as a percentage. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 7/12 as a recurring decimal, using dot notation. **[1]**
**(c)** Write 1.45 as a fraction in its simplest form and as a percentage. **[2]**

**8.**

**(a)** Write down the value of the digit 7 in the number 3.0472. **[1]**
**(b)** Write these decimals in order of size, smallest first: 0.307, 0.37, 0.3, 0.073, 0.317 **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Complete the statement: 9 ÷ 4 = 9 × ___ **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why dividing a number by 0.2 gives the same result as multiplying it by 5. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out 3/4 ÷ 6 by multiplying by a unit fraction. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**10.** **(Higher)** Prove algebraically that the recurring decimal 0.15̇ can be written as 7/45. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher)** x = 0.4̇5̇ and y = 0.36̇.

**(a)** Write x as a fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**
**(b)** Hence work out x ÷ 2 3/11, giving your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that y = 11/30. **[3]**

**12.** A water tank holds 480 litres when full. It starts the week full. On Monday, 2/5 of the full tank is used. On Tuesday, 3/4 of the water left in the tank is used.

**(a)** Calculate the fraction of a full tank used on Tuesday. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction of a full tank left at the end of Tuesday. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many litres are left at the end of Tuesday. **[1]**
**(d)** Write the amount left at the end of Tuesday as a percentage of a full tank. **[1]**
**(e)** On Wednesday, 1/6 of a full tank of water is added. Calculate the fraction of the tank that is now full, and the number of litres in the tank. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Divide numerator and denominator by a common factor, for example 54/90 = 27/45 = 9/15, or by the HCF 18 [1]; **3/5** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A partly cancelled fraction such as 9/15 earns the method mark only; the accuracy mark needs the fraction in its lowest terms.

**2. (a)** 29 ÷ 6 = 4 remainder 5, so **4 5/6** [1]
**(b)** 3 × 9 + 4 = 31, so **31/9** [1]
*Examiner insight:* One-mark parts have no method mark, so check by converting back (4 × 6 + 5 = 29) before moving on.

**3.** 5/8 = 0.625 [1]; 11/18 = 0.6111... [1]; **11/18, 0.62, 5/8, 0.6̇** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs the numbers written in their original form; a list of decimals (0.611, 0.62, 0.625, 0.666) does not answer the question as set.

**4. (a)** 2 3/4 = 11/4 and 1 5/6 = 11/6 [1]; 33/12 + 22/12 [1]; = 55/12 = **4 7/12** [1]
**(b)** 4 1/3 = 13/3 and 2 4/5 = 14/5 [1]; 65/15 − 42/15 [1]; = 23/15 = **1 8/15** [1]
*Examiner insight:* When the question says "show your working", a correct answer copied from a calculator's fraction key with no common-denominator line can score nothing, so write the converted fractions.

**5. (a)** 15/8 × 12/5 [1]; cancel 15 and 5 by 5: 3/8 × 12/1 = 36/8 [1]; = 9/2 = **4 1/2** [1]
**(b)** 8/3 ÷ 10/9 [1]; = 8/3 × 9/10 [1]; = 72/30 = 12/5 = **2 2/5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" the given answer 4 1/2 earns nothing on its own; the marks are for the improper fractions and an unsimplified product such as 36/8 or 180/40 before the final line.

**6. (a)** Lower school = 840 ÷ 7 × 3 = 360 [1]; walkers = 360 ÷ 12 × 5 [1]; = **150** [1]
**(b)** 150 + 230 = 380, giving 380/840 [1]; = **19/42** [1]
**(c)** 150/840 × 100 [1]; = 17.857...% = **17.9%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Parts (b) and (c) can usually earn follow-through marks from a wrong answer to (a), but only when the method using that answer is written down.

**7. (a)** **0.35** [1]; **35%** [1]
**(b)** 7 ÷ 12 = 0.58333..., so **0.583̇** [1]
**(c)** 145/100 = **29/20** (or 1 9/20) [1]; **145%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the dot goes over the 3 only; 0.5̇83̇ would mean 0.583583..., a different number, and loses the mark.

**8. (a)** **7 thousandths** (7/1000 or 0.007) [1]
**(b)** Decimals padded to 0.307, 0.370, 0.300, 0.073, 0.317, or four of the five in the correct order [1]; **0.073, 0.3, 0.307, 0.317, 0.37** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "7" alone does not answer (a); the mark needs the place value, as a word, fraction or decimal.

**9. (a)** **1/4** [1]
**(b)** 0.2 = 1/5 [1]; dividing by 1/5 is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal, 5, because 1/5 × 5 = 1 [1]
**(c)** 3/4 × 1/6 [1]; = 3/24 = **1/8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An "explain" mark needs the reason, not just an example; showing 10 ÷ 0.2 = 50 and 10 × 5 = 50 illustrates the fact but does not explain it.

**10.** Let x = 0.1555...; 10x = 1.555... and 100x = 15.555... [1]; 100x − 10x: 90x = 14 [1]; x = 14/90 = **7/45** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Prove algebraically" needs the two multiples and the subtraction written out; checking that 7 ÷ 45 = 0.1555... works backwards and earns no marks.

**11. (a)** 100x = 45.4545... [1]; 100x − x: 99x = 45 [1]; x = 45/99 = **5/11** [1]
**(b)** 5/11 ÷ 25/11 = 5/11 × 11/25 [1]; = 5/25 = **1/5** [1]
**(c)** 10y = 3.666... and 100y = 36.666... [1]; 90y = 33 [1]; y = 33/90 = **11/30** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Hence" in (b) means use your answer from (a); a decimal calculation with 0.4545... does not use the fraction and may not earn the method mark.

**12. (a)** Left after Monday = 1 − 2/5 = 3/5 [1]; Tuesday = 3/4 × 3/5 = **9/20** [1]
**(b)** 3/5 − 9/20 = 12/20 − 9/20 [1]; = **3/20** [1]
**(c)** 3/20 × 480 = **72 litres** [1]
**(d)** 3/20 = **15%** [1]
**(e)** 3/20 + 1/6 = 9/60 + 10/60 [1]; = **19/60** [1]; 19/60 × 480 = **152 litres** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), 3/4 is a fraction of what was left, not of the full tank; using 3/4 of 480 = 360 litres loses both marks in (a) and carries the error through.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding denominators (2 3/4 + 1 5/6 written as 3 8/10).
- Stopping at a partly simplified fraction such as 9/15 or 72/30.
- Multiplying or dividing mixed numbers without converting to improper fractions.
- Flipping the first fraction instead of the second when dividing.
- Leaving an ordering answer as decimals instead of the numbers given.
- Writing only a digit (such as 7) when the question asks for its value.
- Taking a fraction of the whole amount when the question says "of what was left".
- Placing recurring dots over the wrong digits (Higher).
- Working backwards from the given fraction on a "prove" question (Higher).
