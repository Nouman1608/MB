These questions cover section **3.3.1 Introduction to organic chemistry** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry
specification (7404/7405), version 1.2, July 2026, for AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards:
3.3.1.1 Nomenclature, 3.3.1.2 Reaction mechanisms and 3.3.1.3 Isomerism. It is all AS content, so nothing
here is A-level only. The section is examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level, and A-level Paper 3 can test any
content.

## Questions

**1.** Define each term.

**(a)** Structural isomers **[1]**
**(b)** General formula **[1]**

**2.** State three characteristics of a homologous series. **[3]**

**3.** Compound Z contains 62.1% carbon, 10.3% hydrogen and 27.6% oxygen by mass. Its Mr is 116.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of Z. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of Z. **[2]**
**(c)** Z is an unbranched carboxylic acid. Name Z and give its structural formula. **[2]**

**4.** Give the IUPAC name of each compound.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CHBrCH₃ **[1]**
**(b)** (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CHO **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃CH=CHCH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(d)** CH₃C(CH₃)(OH)CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**5.** Give the structural formula of each compound.

**(a)** 1,2-dichloropropane **[1]**
**(b)** pentan-3-one **[1]**
**(c)** 2,2-dimethylbutanoic acid **[1]**

**6.** Each name below is wrong. Give the correct IUPAC name and explain the error.

**(a)** 3-methylbutane **[2]**
**(b)** 2-ethylpropan-1-ol **[2]**

**7.** Cyclohexane reacts with bromine in ultraviolet light to form bromocyclohexane, C₆H₁₁Br, by a
free-radical mechanism.

**(a)** Write an equation for the initiation step. **[1]**
**(b)** Write equations for the two propagation steps. **[2]**
**(c)** Write an equation for a termination step that forms a hydrocarbon with twelve carbon atoms. **[1]**
**(d)** State what the dot in a radical such as Br• represents. **[1]**

**8.** This question is about curly arrows.

**(a)** A hydroxide ion reacts with 2-bromopropane to form propan-2-ol and a bromide ion in a single step
that needs two curly arrows. For each arrow, state where it starts and where it ends. **[4]**
**(b)** State what a curly arrow represents. **[1]**
**(c)** In the first step of the reaction between propene and hydrogen bromide, a new bond forms between a
carbon atom and a hydrogen atom. State where the curly arrow that forms this bond starts, and explain why it
does not start from a lone pair. **[2]**

**9.** There are four structural isomers of C₄H₉Cl.

**(a)** Name the four isomers. **[4]**
**(b)** From your answer, identify one pair of chain isomers and one pair of position isomers. **[2]**

**10.** This question is about E–Z isomerism.

**(a)** Explain why but-2-ene has E–Z isomers but but-1-ene does not. **[2]**
**(b)** An isomer of 1-chloro-2-methylbut-1-ene has this structure:

```
    Cl       CH₂CH₃
      \     /
       C = C
      /     \
     H       CH₃
```

**(i)** Use the CIP priority rules to identify the higher-priority group on each carbon of the C=C. Explain
your choices. **[3]**
**(ii)** State whether this is the E or the Z isomer. **[1]**

**11.** A 0.350 g sample of hydrocarbon W contains 0.300 g of carbon. The Mr of W is 70.0.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula and the molecular formula of W. **[3]**
**(b)** W is an alkene with an unbranched chain. Name the two possible structures of W. **[2]**
**(c)** One of these structures has E–Z isomers. Name it and draw both stereoisomers, labelling each E or Z.
**[3]**
**(d)** Name a cyclic compound that is a functional group isomer of W. **[1]**

**12.** This question is about skeletal formulas.

**(a)** The skeletal formula of compound P is a hexagon with an OH group on one corner. Give the molecular
formula and the name of P. **[2]**
**(b)** The skeletal formula of compound Q is a zigzag of five lines, with =O on the second carbon from one
end. Give the name and the molecular formula of Q. **[2]**
**(c)** State the type of isomerism shown by P and Q. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Compounds with the same molecular formula but a different structural formula [1].
**(b)** An algebraic formula that applies to every member of a homologous series, e.g. CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Same formula, different structure" is too vague for (a); say which formula is the same
and which differs.

**2.** Same general formula [1]; successive members differ by CH₂ [1]; same functional group so similar
chemical properties, or a gradual trend in physical properties [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Similar properties" with no word "chemical" is usually too vague — physical properties
change gradually along the series.

**3. (a)** Moles: C = 62.1/12.0 = 5.175; H = 10.3/1.0 = 10.3; O = 27.6/16.0 = 1.725 [1]. Divide by
1.725: C = 3.00, H = 5.97, O = 1.00 [1]. Empirical formula **C₃H₆O** [1].
**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 58.0, and 116/58.0 = 2 [1]; molecular formula **C₆H₁₂O₂** [1].
**(c)** **Hexanoic acid** [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂COOH [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division by Ar; an error in (a) can usually be carried forward into (b), but
only if the working is visible.

**4. (a)** **2-bromobutane** [1]
**(b)** **3-methylbutanal** [1]
**(c)** **hex-2-ene** [1]
**(d)** **2-methylbutan-2-ol** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b), numbering starts at the CHO carbon, so "2-methylbutanal" counts from the wrong
end and earns nothing.

**5. (a)** CH₂ClCHClCH₃ [1]
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ [1]
**(c)** CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂COOH [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A structural formula must be unambiguous; writing C₆H₁₂O₂ for (c) is a molecular formula
and gains no credit.

**6. (a)** **2-methylbutane** [1]. The chain must be numbered from the end nearer the branch, giving the lower
locant [1].
**(b)** **2-methylbutan-1-ol** [1]. The longest chain containing the C–OH has four carbons, not three; the
"ethyl" group is part of the main chain [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Drawing the structure from the wrong name first, then renaming it, is the safest route;
the explanation mark needs the rule, not just the new name.

**7. (a)** Br₂ → 2Br• [1]
**(b)** Br• + C₆H₁₂ → C₆H₁₁• + HBr [1]; C₆H₁₁• + Br₂ → C₆H₁₁Br + Br• [1]
**(c)** 2C₆H₁₁• → C₁₂H₂₂ [1]
**(d)** An unpaired electron [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A radical written without its dot is usually not credited, and curly arrows are not
needed in these steps.

**8. (a)** Arrow 1 starts from a lone pair on the oxygen of OH⁻ [1] and ends at the carbon bonded to bromine
(the δ+ carbon) [1]. Arrow 2 starts from the C–Br bond [1] and ends at the bromine atom, which leaves as Br⁻
[1].
**(b)** The movement of a pair of electrons [1].
**(c)** It starts from the C=C double bond [1]. The electron pair for the new bond comes from a covalent bond
(the double bond), not from a lone pair, because propene has no lone pairs [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An arrow drawn from the minus sign of OH⁻ instead of from a lone pair is a common way to
lose the first mark in (a).

**9. (a)** 1-chlorobutane [1]; 2-chlorobutane [1]; 1-chloro-2-methylpropane [1]; 2-chloro-2-methylpropane
[1].
**(b)** Chain isomers: 1-chlorobutane and 1-chloro-2-methylpropane (or 2-chlorobutane and
2-chloro-2-methylpropane) [1]. Position isomers: 1-chlorobutane and 2-chlorobutane (or
1-chloro-2-methylpropane and 2-chloro-2-methylpropane) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming one compound twice under different names (for example 3-chlorobutane for
2-chlorobutane) does not count as a fourth isomer.

**10. (a)** In but-2-ene each carbon of the C=C has two different groups (H and CH₃), and there is restricted
rotation about the C=C [1]. In but-1-ene, carbon 1 has two identical groups (H and H), so no E–Z isomers
[1].
**(b)(i)** Left carbon: Cl has a higher atomic number (17) than H (1), so Cl is higher priority [1]. Right
carbon: CH₃ and CH₂CH₃ are both attached through carbon, a tie [1]; at the next atoms CH₂CH₃ has (C, H, H)
and CH₃ has (H, H, H), so CH₂CH₃ is higher priority [1].
**(ii)** Cl and CH₂CH₃ are on the same side: **Z** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b)(i) the tie at the first atom must be stated before comparing further out; "ethyl
is bigger" does not earn the mark.

**11. (a)** Mass of H = 0.350 − 0.300 = 0.050 g; moles C = 0.300/12.0 = 0.0250, H = 0.050/1.0 = 0.050 [1].
Ratio 1 : 2, empirical formula **CH₂** [1]. 70.0/14.0 = 5, molecular formula **C₅H₁₀** [1].
**(b)** **pent-1-ene** [1]; **pent-2-ene** [1].
**(c)** Pent-2-ene [1].

```
   H₃C       CH₂CH₃            H₃C       H
      \     /                     \     /
       C = C                       C = C
      /     \                     /     \
     H       H                   H       CH₂CH₃
       (Z)                          (E)
```

Z isomer with CH₃ and CH₂CH₃ on the same side [1]; E isomer with them on opposite sides [1].
**(d)** Cyclopentane (or methylcyclobutane) [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) each drawing needs the correct label; two correct drawings with the labels swapped
lose both drawing marks.

**12. (a)** C₆H₁₂O [1]; cyclohexanol [1].
**(b)** Hexan-2-one [1]; C₆H₁₂O [1].
**(c)** Functional group isomerism (same molecular formula, different functional groups) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A five-line zigzag has six carbons, because both line ends count; giving pentan-2-one
loses the name mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not showing the mole ratio step in empirical formula work, so no method credit can be given.
- Losing count of carbons in a skeletal formula by missing the line ends.
- Numbering from the wrong end, or adding a locant to -al or -oic acid names.
- Choosing a "longest chain" that leaves out the functional group carbon.
- Writing radicals without dots, or adding curly arrows to free-radical steps.
- Starting curly arrows from atoms or charges rather than from lone pairs or bonds.
- Counting the same isomer twice under two names.
- Ranking CIP groups by size or mass rather than by atomic number.
- Explaining E–Z isomerism without mentioning restricted rotation about the C=C.
