## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by nuclear fusion. **[2]**

**2.** Complete the nuclear equation by giving the mass number and atomic number of nucleus X.

¹₁H + ²₁H → ᴬ_Z X **[2]**

**3.** Two hydrogen-2 nuclei fuse: ²₁H + ²₁H → ³₂He + Y.

**(a)** Find the mass number and atomic number of Y. **[2]**

**(b)** Name particle Y. **[1]**

**4.** Which of these is a fusion reaction? Give a reason for your choice.

A: ²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴¹₅₆Ba + ⁹²₃₆Kr + 3 ¹₀n

B: ²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n

C: ²²⁶₈₈Ra → ²²²₈₆Rn + ⁴₂He **[2]**

**5.** State the process by which energy is released in stars, and name the part of a star where most of it happens. **[2]**

**6.** Two helium-3 nuclei fuse: ³₂He + ³₂He → ⁴₂He + 2X. Identify particle X, showing how you used the mass numbers and the atomic numbers. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Explain why two hydrogen nuclei will only fuse at a very high temperature and a very high pressure. **[4]**

**8.** A student writes: "In fusion the mass numbers on each side are equal, so no mass can be lost. The energy must come from the electrons."

**(a)** Explain what is wrong with the student's statement. **[3]**

**(b)** State where the energy released by fusion comes from. **[1]**

**9.** These mass values are supplied for the reaction ²₁H + ³₁H → ⁴₂He + ¹₀n.

| Particle | Mass (atomic mass units, u) |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen-2 nucleus | 2.01355 |
| Hydrogen-3 nucleus | 3.01550 |
| Helium-4 nucleus | 4.00151 |
| Neutron | 1.00866 |

**(a)** Calculate the total mass before and after the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the decrease in mass as a percentage of the mass before. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what has happened to the mass that is "missing". **[1]**

**10.** Fusing two helium-4 nuclei requires a much higher temperature than fusing two hydrogen nuclei. Explain why. **[3]**

**11.** Compare nuclear fission with nuclear fusion. Give three differences. **[3]**

**12.** The core of a main sequence star is far hotter and denser than its outer layers.

**(a)** Explain why most fusion takes place in the core rather than the outer layers. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the nuclei that fuse during the main sequence and the nuclei they form. **[2]**

**(c)** A much more massive star has a hotter core. Suggest how this affects the nuclei it can make by fusion. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Two light nuclei [1] join to form a heavier nucleus [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Atoms join" does not score. The word nuclei is essential, and so is the idea of a heavier product.

**2.** A = 1 + 2 = **3** [1]; Z = 1 + 1 = **2** [1] (helium-3). **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* With nothing else on the right, the new nucleus takes all the mass number and all the atomic number.

**3. (a)** Mass number = 4 − 3 = **1** [1]; atomic number = 2 − 2 = **0** [1].

**(b)** A **neutron** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A particle with mass number 1 and atomic number 0 has no charge, so it cannot be a proton.

**4.** **B** [1], because two light nuclei join to make a heavier nucleus [1]. (A is fission; C is alpha decay.) **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The reason must describe fusion. "Because it has hydrogen in it" is not a reason.

**5.** Nuclear **fusion** [1], mainly in the **core** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Burning" is a chemical process and does not score.

**6.** Mass numbers: 3 + 3 = 6, and 6 − 4 = 2 left for two particles, so each has mass number 1 [1]. Atomic numbers: 2 + 2 = 4, and 4 − 2 = 2 left, so each has atomic number 1 [1]. X is a **proton**, ¹₁H [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The "2" in front of X means two particles. Giving X the numbers (2, 2) is the most common error.

**7.** Nuclei are positively charged, so they repel each other [1]. The repulsion must be overcome for them to get close enough to fuse [1]. At a very high temperature the nuclei move very fast, with enough kinetic energy to get close despite the repulsion [1]. At a very high pressure the nuclei are packed closely, so these close collisions are frequent [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what each condition does. "It needs to be hot and squashed" names the conditions without explaining them.

**8. (a)** Mass number counts protons and neutrons, and that count is conserved [1]. The actual mass of the products is slightly less than the mass of the nuclei that fused [1]. Fusion involves nuclei only, so the electrons are not the source of the energy [1].

**(b)** Some of the mass of the smaller nuclei is converted into energy [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate "mass number" from "mass" explicitly. That distinction is the point of the question.

**9. (a)** Before: 2.01355 + 3.01550 = **5.02905 u** [1]. After: 4.00151 + 1.00866 = **5.01017 u** [1].

**(b)** Decrease = 5.02905 − 5.01017 = 0.01888 u [1]; 0.01888 ÷ 5.02905 × 100 = **0.38%** [1].

**(c)** It has been converted into energy, carried away mainly as kinetic energy of the products [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep all five decimal places until the subtraction. Rounding the masses first makes the difference vanish.

**10.** A helium-4 nucleus has 2 protons, so its charge is +2, twice that of a hydrogen nucleus [1]. Two helium nuclei therefore repel more strongly [1]. The nuclei need more kinetic energy to get close enough to fuse, so a higher temperature is needed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Link charge, then repulsion, then kinetic energy, then temperature. Leaving out the charge loses the first mark.

**11.** Any three, each comparing both processes:
- Fission splits one large nucleus, whereas fusion joins two light nuclei [1].
- Fission produces two smaller nuclei, whereas fusion produces one heavier nucleus [1].
- Fission starts when a nucleus absorbs a neutron, whereas fusion needs very high temperature and pressure [1].
- Fission is used in nuclear reactors, whereas fusion releases energy in stars. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A one-sided statement such as "fusion happens in stars" is not a comparison. Use "whereas".

**12. (a)** The core has the highest temperature, so nuclei have enough kinetic energy to overcome repulsion [1], and the highest density (pressure), so collisions between nuclei are frequent [1].

**(b)** Hydrogen nuclei [1] fuse to form helium nuclei [1].

**(c)** A hotter core gives nuclei more kinetic energy, so nuclei with larger charges can be fused [1], so the star can make heavier nuclei, such as carbon from helium [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), connect temperature to the size of charge that can be overcome; "it is bigger so it makes more" does not answer the question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing atoms instead of nuclei.
- Saying mass number decreases in fusion.
- Naming high temperature and pressure without explaining the repulsion they overcome.
- Missing the factor of two in "2X".
- Rounding mass data too early.
- Describing stars as burning fuel.
- Writing comparison answers that describe only one process.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions written for revision of section 3.7.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203). Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Do I need an equation for the energy released in fusion?**
No. The specification asks you to know that some mass is converted into energy. Mass data, when given, are used for subtraction and percentages only.
