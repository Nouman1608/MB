These notes summarise section 5.2, Area and perimeter, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [area and perimeter study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-area-and-perimeter/), and exam-style questions are in the [area and perimeter practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-area-and-perimeter-practice/).

## What is examined

Carry out calculations involving the perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium. Except for the area of a triangle, formulas are not given in the examination, so learn all of them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Perimeter | the distance around the outside of a shape |
| Area | the surface inside a shape, in square units |
| Base | the side chosen to measure from |
| Perpendicular height | the height at 90° to the base |
| Parallel sides | the two sides of a trapezium that never meet |
| Slant height | a sloping side, not used for area |
| Composite shape | a shape made from simpler shapes |

## Formulas to learn

| Shape | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle | l × w | 2(l + w) |
| Square, side s | s² | 4s |
| Triangle | 1/2 × b × h (given in the exam) | sum of the sides |
| Parallelogram | b × h | 2(a + b) |
| Rhombus (a parallelogram) | b × h | 4 × side |
| Trapezium | 1/2 × (a + b) × h | sum of the sides |

## Method box: area and perimeter

1. Sketch the shape and label the known lengths.
2. Decide what is needed: area (square units) or perimeter (units).
3. Make sure the height is perpendicular to the base.
4. If a length is missing, look for a right-angled triangle and use Pythagoras.
5. Substitute, calculate, and write the unit.

## Method box: working backwards

1. Write the formula with the known values.
2. Solve for the unknown.

```
triangle: area 54, base 9      →  1/2 × 9 × h = 54  →  h = 12
trapezium: area 91, sides 9, 17 →  1/2 × 26 × h = 91 →  h = 7
parallelogram: area 105, base 15 → h = 7
```

## Using Pythagoras for the height

```
isosceles triangle 10, 10, 12:  h² = 10² − 6² = 64, h = 8, area = 48
isosceles trapezium 6 and 14, legs 5:  overhang 4, h² = 25 − 16 = 9, h = 3, area = 30
```

## Borders and paths

```
path area = outer area − inner area
outer = (length + 2 × width) × (breadth + 2 × width)
```

## Worked patterns

| Question type | Key step |
|---|---|
| Rectangle, given perimeter and one side | l + w = half the perimeter |
| Rectangle, given perimeter and area | l + w = p/2, then solve lw = area |
| Triangle on a grid | find a base and perpendicular height from coordinates |
| Triangle with three sides | find a height using Pythagoras |
| Cost of covering | area × price per unit area |
| Fencing | perimeter ÷ post spacing |

## Why the formulas work

- A **triangle** is half of a rectangle or parallelogram with the same base and height, so its area is 1/2 bh.
- A **parallelogram** can be cut and rearranged into a rectangle with the same base and perpendicular height, so its area is bh.
- A **trapezium** is half of a parallelogram made from two copies, with base a + b, so its area is 1/2 (a + b) h.

Knowing these links makes the formulas easy to rebuild if you forget one.

## Quick checks

1. A triangle's area is less than the area of the rectangle on the same base and height.
2. A trapezium's area lies between the areas of rectangles on its two parallel sides with the same height.
3. For a fixed perimeter, a square has the greatest area among rectangles.
4. Perimeters of composite shapes include only the outside edges, not the inner joins.

### Example of check 3

Rectangles with perimeter 20 cm: 9 × 1 has area 9 cm², 8 × 2 has 16 cm², 7 × 3 has 21 cm², 6 × 4 has 24 cm² and 5 × 5 has 25 cm². The square gives the largest area.

## Must-know distinctions

| Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|
| inside the shape | around the edge |
| square units: cm², m² | units: cm, m |
| uses the perpendicular height | uses the slanting side |

## Exam traps

- Using the slanting side in an area calculation.
- Omitting the factor 1/2.
- Adding only the base and height for a parallelogram's perimeter.
- Mixing units, such as m and cm, in one product.
- Forgetting that a path adds its width twice to each dimension.
- Writing a perimeter in square units.

## Self-test

1. Find the area of a trapezium with parallel sides 7 cm and 11 cm and height 6 cm.
2. Find the area of a triangle with base 14 cm and height 9 cm.
3. A parallelogram has sides 9 cm and 4 cm and a perpendicular height of 3.5 cm to the 9 cm side. Find its area and perimeter.
4. A rectangle has width 6 cm and area 51 cm². Find its perimeter.
5. A triangle has area 60 cm² and base 10 cm. Find its height.
6. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 cm and one shorter side 5 cm. Find its area.
7. A rectangle has a length twice its width, and perimeter 36 cm. Find its area.
8. A trapezium has parallel sides 5 cm and 9 cm and area 28 cm². Find the distance between them.
9. A square lawn of side 10 m has a path 1 m wide round the outside. Find the area of the path.
10. A rhombus has side 10 cm and perpendicular height 8 cm. Find its area.

### Answers

1. 1/2 × (7 + 11) × 6 = **54 cm²**.
2. 1/2 × 14 × 9 = **63 cm²**.
3. Area = 9 × 3.5 = **31.5 cm²**. Perimeter = 2(9 + 4) = **26 cm**.
4. Length = 51 ÷ 6 = 8.5 cm. Perimeter = 2(6 + 8.5) = **29 cm**.
5. 1/2 × 10 × h = 60, so **h = 12 cm**.
6. The other side = √(169 − 25) = 12 cm, so area = 1/2 × 5 × 12 = **30 cm²**.
7. w + 2w = 18, so w = 6 and l = 12. Area = **72 cm²**.
8. 1/2 × 14 × h = 28, so **h = 4 cm**.
9. 12 × 12 − 10 × 10 = 144 − 100 = **44 m²**.
10. 10 × 8 = **80 cm²**.
