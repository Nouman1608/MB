Condensed, exam-focused notes for Component 2 of Cambridge AS & A Level Global Perspectives & Research
(9239), 2026-2028 series.

## Format

- Continuous prose, **1750-2000 words** on a self-chosen global issue.
- Accurate word count (excluding title, references, citations) must be stated. Anything beyond 2000
  words is **not marked**.
- Worth 40 marks, externally marked.

## Choosing the question

- Must be phrased as a **single question**, not a statement or generic topic area.
- Must create genuine opportunities for **globally contrasting perspectives**.
- A question focused on an exclusively local or national issue is unlikely to be appropriate.

## Research and sourcing

- Identify and explore context/basis of arguments from different global perspectives.
- Select a range of sources; **assess and justify why those sources were chosen** — not just cite them.

## Evaluation of perspectives

- Compare and evaluate contrasting perspectives.
- Reflect on how alternative perspectives affect your own viewpoint.
- Reach a **personal, supported conclusion** on the question.

## Referencing

- Quotations must be fully referenced; a list of sources is required.
- Referencing accuracy is assessed as part of AO3.

## Assessment objectives

- **AO1**: analysing the question, synthesising arguments into contrasting perspectives, selecting/
  justifying sources, comparing/evaluating perspectives.
- **AO2**: reflecting on how alternative perspectives affect your own view, suggesting further relevant
  research.
- **AO3**: writing a clear, structured essay with effective referencing.

## The role of the teacher

Teachers may lead whole-class background research into a general issue, but each candidate must devise
their own question individually. Teachers support planning, source citation and word-count discipline
but must **not** give detailed advice on improving a specific draft to meet the mark scheme, and must
not correct or edit draft coursework directly.

## Exam technique for this component

Spend real time on the question before researching — a weak question (one that only supports a single
perspective, or a local issue dressed up as global) makes every later stage harder however good the
research is. A strong question is phrased as a genuine question, allows at least two globally
contrasting perspectives developed in real depth, and sustains 1750-2000 words of engaged argument
rather than padded restatement. Keep a brief running note while researching on *why* each source was
chosen — its authority, its standpoint, what perspective it represents — rather than reconstructing that
justification from memory once drafting starts. Draft with the word limit in mind from the start: at
1750-2000 words there is only room for two or three perspectives developed with real evidence, not four
or five sketched briefly. Reserve the closing paragraphs for the personal, supported view AO2 requires —
an essay that ends by simply summarising the perspectives already given has not completed the task.

## Worked example: developing genuinely contrasting perspectives

Take the essay question "Should social media companies be legally responsible for misinformation shared
on their platforms?" A weak response develops one perspective in depth (for example, "yes, because
misinformation causes real-world harm") and then adds a second "perspective" that is really just a
softer restatement of the same underlying position. A stronger response develops perspectives from
genuinely different standpoints — for instance, a perspective centred on user protection and public
harm, drawing on evidence from public health or misinformation researchers, set directly against a
perspective centred on free expression and the practical difficulty of a private company policing
global speech, drawing on evidence from digital rights or legal scholars. The two perspectives disagree
not just on the conclusion but on which underlying values and evidence should be weighed most heavily —
this is what "globally contrasting" actually requires, rather than two versions of the same argument
phrased with different intensity.

## Worked example: justifying a source selection

For AO1's source-justification requirement, a strong essay doesn't simply cite a source in a footnote —
it briefly explains, in the essay's own body or its supporting notes, why that specific source was
chosen: is it an authoritative primary source close to the issue (a national statistics agency, an
international organisation's report), a scholarly analysis offering interpretation and context, or a
source explicitly representing one of the perspectives being compared (a named advocacy organisation, a
government position paper)? Being explicit about a source's standpoint — rather than treating all
sources as equally neutral evidence — strengthens both the source-justification marks under AO1 and the
overall credibility of the essay's argument, since a reader can then judge how much weight a given piece
of evidence should carry given who produced it and why.

## Self-test

1. What is the word range for the essay, and what happens beyond the upper limit?
2. What must the essay title be phrased as?
3. What does AO1 specifically require regarding source selection?
4. What must the closing section of the essay include, per AO2?
5. Name one thing a teacher must NOT do for this component.

**Answers:** 1. 1750-2000 words; anything beyond 2000 words is not marked. 2. A single question. 3.
Selecting sources AND justifying why they were chosen, not just citing them. 4. A personal, supported
conclusion on the question. 5. Give detailed advice on how a specific draft could be improved to meet the
mark scheme, or correct/edit draft coursework directly.

## A final note on independence

Because the Essay is the one component candidates complete almost entirely independently, time
management and self-discipline matter as much as research skill — set interim deadlines for choosing a
question, completing research, and finishing a first draft well ahead of the final submission date,
since there is no team or teacher-set structure to create those checkpoints automatically.
