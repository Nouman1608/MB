Paper 2 to what you need to recall under time pressure.** Learn the definitions word-perfect, link

## Definitions to learn exactly

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Raw material | A natural substance used to make a product (limestone, seed cotton, cane) |
| Processed | Changed in form or condition (cotton ginned, yarn spun) |
| Refined | Purified by removing unwanted material (crude oil to petrol; cane juice to sugar) |
| Manufactured | Made into a finished product, usually by machine (cloth into shirts) |
| Value-added | Increase in value created at each stage of production |
| Infrastructure | Basic structures a country needs: roads, rail, ports, power, water, telecoms |
| Services | Activities that help people and businesses rather than make goods |
| Primary industry | Extracts natural resources: farming, fishing, mining, forestry |
| Secondary industry | Processes and manufactures goods |
| Tertiary industry | Provides services: transport, trade, banking, health, tourism |
| Formal sector | Registered, taxed businesses with regular paid jobs |
| Informal sector | Unregistered, untaxed work, often self-employed or casual |
| EPZ | Export processing zone: duty-free fenced estate for export production |

## One-line location reason per industry

- **Cement:** limestone is heavy → quarry-side plants (D.G. Khan, Daudkhel, Pezu, Thatta).
- **Ginning:** seed cotton is bulky → in the cotton belt (Rahim Yar Khan, Sanghar).
- **Textiles and garments:** labour, power and port → Faisalabad, Lahore, Karachi.
- **Sugar:** cane spoils fast → beside cane fields (Jhang, Mardan, Badin).
- **Fertiliser:** gas is feedstock → on gas fields and pipelines (Daharki, Sadiqabad, Multan).
- **Iron and steel:** imported ore and coal → coastal Pipri at Port Qasim; scrap from Gadani.
- **Brick:** clay plus local market → kilns ringing every city.
- **Oil refining:** imported crude at the coast (Karachi, Hub); local crude in the Potwar (Morgah);
  inland market (Mahmood Kot).
- **Sports goods and surgical instruments:** inherited skills and export links → Sialkot.
- **Crafts:** tradition and local materials → Multan pottery, Hala lacquer work, Chiniot furniture.

## Domestic or export?

| Mainly export | Both | Mainly domestic |
|---|---|---|
| Sports goods, surgical instruments | Cotton yarn, cloth and garments; cement; crafts | Fertiliser, steel, bricks, refined fuel, sugar |

## Informal sector: balanced view

**For:** absorbs migrants without qualifications; little capital needed; cheap goods and repairs;
recycling of scrap; flexible subcontracting for exporters.
**Against:** low and irregular pay; no safety rules; child and bonded labour (notably in some brick
kilns); no taxes for government; no access to bank credit, so firms stay small.
**Judgement line:** the informal sector keeps millions employed and supplies the formal sector,
but low productivity holds back GDP growth, so the aim is to help it formalise, not to remove it.

## Government promotion: name and explain

| Measure | Example | Why |
|---|---|---|
| EPZ | Karachi, Sialkot, Risalpur, Gujranwala; Saindak, Duddar | Foreign investment, exports, jobs, technology |
| Industrial estate | SITE Karachi and Hyderabad, Sundar (Lahore), Hattar, Gadoon Amazai, Hub | Serviced plots; spread industry |
| Special economic zone | Rashakai, Allama Iqbal Industrial City (CPEC) | Chinese and local investment along CPEC routes |
| Credit and training | Small-business loans, technical institutes | Capital and skilled labour |

**Characteristics of an EPZ:** fenced and secure; ready-built sheds; reliable power and water;
duty-free imports of inputs and machinery; quick customs clearance; most output exported.

| | Helps | Hinders |
|---|---|---|
| Call centres and IT | Young, English-speaking graduates; low wages; IT exports near US$3 billion (mid-2020s) | Power cuts; uneven broadband; skills gap; competition |
| Tourism | Mountains (Fairy Meadows, Deosai); heritage (Makli, Rohtas, Takht-i-Bahi); religious tourism (Kartarpur) | Access, landslides, seasonality, security image, environmental damage |

## Exam traps

- **Confusing "processed" with "manufactured".** Ginning is processing; stitching a shirt is
  manufacturing.
- **Giving a location without a factor.** "Sialkot" alone scores less than "Sialkot, because of a
  skilled workforce built up over generations".
- **Saying the informal sector is "illegal".** Most informal work is legal but unregistered.
- **Listing tourist sites without judging feasibility.** The syllabus asks you to assess tourism's
  contribution to jobs, GNP and GDP.
- **Calling Pakistan Steel the main current supplier.** It has been largely idle since about 2015.

## Self-test

1. Define value-added.
2. Why is urea made close to gas fields?
3. Name two EPZs in Pakistan.
4. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of the informal sector.
5. Name a cultural attraction near Thatta.
6. Which city leads sports goods and surgical instruments?

**Answers:** 1. The increase in value created at each stage of processing or manufacturing.
2. Natural gas is both raw material and fuel. 3. Any two of Karachi, Sialkot, Risalpur,
Gujranwala, Saindak, Duddar, Gwadar. 4. For example jobs for unqualified people; low pay and no
protection. 5. The Makli necropolis. 6. Sialkot.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is tourism a secondary or tertiary industry?**
Tertiary, because it provides a service (travel, accommodation, guiding) rather than making goods.

**Which industries in the list are cottage industries?**
Crafts are the clearest example, along with home-based stitching for sports goods and garments.

**What is the difference between an EPZ and a special economic zone?**
An EPZ is aimed at export production with duty-free inputs; a special economic zone offers
incentives to firms that may sell at home as well as abroad.
