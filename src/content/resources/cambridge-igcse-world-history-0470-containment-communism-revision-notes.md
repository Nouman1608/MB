These notes condense **key question 5 of Core Content Option B** in Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "How effectively did
the United States contain the spread of communism?" The syllabus sets three case studies: Korea (1950-53), Cuba
(1959-62) and Vietnam (1955-75). For explanation and worked answers, read the
[containment study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-containment-communism/); to apply the facts,
try the [containment practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-containment-communism-practice/).

## Korea, 1950-53: decision timeline

| Date | Event | Containment link |
|---|---|---|
| April 1950 | NSC-68 calls for a massive US military build-up | Containment becomes global and military |
| 25 June 1950 | North Korea invades the South | Seen in Washington as a Soviet-backed test |
| June-July 1950 | Security Council resolutions; USSR absent; UN Command under MacArthur | Action given UN legitimacy |
| August-September 1950 | UN forces hold the Pusan perimeter | South Korea survives |
| 15 September 1950 | Inchon landing behind North Korean lines | North Koreans driven back |
| October 1950 | UN forces cross the 38th parallel towards the Yalu | Shift from containment to rollback |
| October-November 1950 | Chinese forces enter the war | UN driven back south; Seoul lost again in January 1951 |
| April 1951 | Truman dismisses MacArthur; Ridgway takes over | Limited war chosen over a war with China |
| July 1951 | Armistice talks begin; front settles near the 38th parallel | Two years of stalemate follow |
| 27 July 1953 | Armistice at Panmunjom | South Korea stays non-communist |

**Verdict line:** the original aim succeeded; rollback failed; China became a lasting enemy.

## Cuba, 1959-62: decision timeline

| Date | Event | Containment link |
|---|---|---|
| January 1959 | Castro takes power | Revolution not at first openly communist |
| 1960 | Soviet trade deal; US cuts sugar imports; embargo on most US exports | Pressure pushes Cuba towards Moscow |
| January 1961 | USA breaks off diplomatic relations | Isolation policy |
| April 1961 | Bay of Pigs: CIA-trained exiles defeated | Humiliation; Castro turns to the USSR for protection |
| January 1962 | Cuba expelled from the Organisation of American States | Regional isolation |
| 14-16 October 1962 | U-2 photographs of missile sites shown to Kennedy | Threat on America's doorstep |
| 22 October 1962 | Kennedy announces a naval quarantine | Firmness without an attack |
| 26-27 October 1962 | Khrushchev's two letters; U-2 shot down over Cuba | Most dangerous moment |
| 28 October 1962 | Khrushchev agrees to remove the missiles | Public no-invasion pledge; secret Turkey deal |
| 1963 | Hotline; Partial Test Ban Treaty; Jupiter missiles leave Turkey | Start of more cautious relations |

**Verdict line:** failed to stop or remove communism in Cuba; succeeded in removing the missiles without war.

## Vietnam, 1955-75: decision timeline

| Date | Event | Containment link |
|---|---|---|
| 1954 | Geneva Accords divide Vietnam; SEATO formed | USA takes over from France as protector of the South |
| 1955-56 | Diem's republic; reunification elections not held | USA backs an unpopular anti-communist |
| 1961-63 | Kennedy expands advisers; strategic hamlets | Deeper commitment |
| November 1963 | Diem overthrown and killed in a coup | Instability in the South |
| August 1964 | Gulf of Tonkin incident and Resolution | Johnson gains power to use force |
| March 1965 | Rolling Thunder bombing; Marines land at Da Nang | Combat war begins |
| January 1968 | Tet Offensive | Public confidence collapses |
| 1969 onwards | Nixon's Vietnamisation and troop withdrawals | Exit strategy |
| 1970 | Attacks on bases in Cambodia; Kent State shootings | Protest intensifies |
| January 1973 | Paris Peace Accords | US combat role ends |
| April 1975 | Fall of Saigon; Laos and Cambodia also communist | Containment failed in Indochina |

**Verdict line:** clear failure by its own aim; huge cost in lives, money and reputation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Containment | Stopping communism spreading to new countries |
| Rollback | Pushing communism back from a country where it already ruled |
| NSC-68 | 1950 policy paper calling for a large US military build-up |
| Limited war | A war fought for restricted aims without using full force, such as nuclear weapons |
| Domino theory | Belief that if one state fell to communism its neighbours would follow |
| ExComm | Kennedy's group of advisers during the Missile Crisis |
| Quarantine | Kennedy's term for the 1962 naval blockade of Cuba |
| Brinkmanship | Pushing a crisis to the edge of war to force the other side to back down |
| SEATO | South-East Asia Treaty Organisation (1954), a defensive alliance |
| Attrition | Wearing down the enemy by inflicting losses faster than they can be replaced |
| Search and destroy | Missions to find and kill Viet Cong fighters, then withdraw |
| Vietnamisation | Nixon's policy of handing the fighting to South Vietnamese forces |
| Ho Chi Minh Trail | Supply route from North Vietnam through Laos and Cambodia |

## Comparing the three cases

| | Korea | Cuba | Vietnam |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who attacked or changed first | North Korea invaded | Castro's revolution | Communist insurgency in the South |
| Main US method | UN war | Pressure, invasion attempt, blockade | Advisers, then full-scale war |
| Allies | UN members | OAS support in 1962 | Few allies; little UN role |
| Outcome | South saved | Castro stayed; missiles removed | Whole of Vietnam communist |

## Exam traps

- **Korea and the UN.** It was a UN action; the Soviet absence from the Security Council explains why there was no
  veto.
- **Containment versus rollback.** Crossing the 38th parallel was rollback; credit only the defence of the South as
  containment.
- **Cuba's two crises.** The Bay of Pigs (1961) and the Missile Crisis (1962) are separate events.
- **Tet.** A military failure for the communists but a political turning point in the USA.
- **Vietnam's start date.** The syllabus starts in 1955, not with the combat troops of 1965.
- **Judging without criteria.** "Effective" needs a measure: aim, cost or wider consequences.

## Self-test

1. What did NSC-68 recommend? *A massive increase in American military strength to resist communism everywhere.*
2. Why could the USSR not veto UN action in Korea in June 1950? *It was boycotting the Security Council over China's seat.*
3. Where did the UN force land in September 1950? *Inchon.*
4. Why was MacArthur dismissed? *He publicly demanded a wider war against China, against Truman's policy of limited war.*
5. Where was the Korean armistice signed? *Panmunjom, in July 1953.*
6. Give one American economic measure against Cuba in 1960. *Cutting imports of Cuban sugar, or an embargo on most exports.*
7. Why did Kennedy call his blockade a "quarantine"? *A blockade was an act of war under international law.*
8. What did Khrushchev's second letter demand? *Removal of American missiles from Turkey.*
9. Name two agreements of 1963 that followed the crisis. *The hotline and the Partial Test Ban Treaty.*
10. What did the Gulf of Tonkin Resolution allow? *It let President Johnson use armed force in Vietnam without a declaration of war.*
11. What was Vietnamisation? *Nixon's policy of withdrawing US troops while South Vietnam's army took over the fighting.*
12. Which three countries became communist in 1975? *Vietnam, Laos and Cambodia.*
