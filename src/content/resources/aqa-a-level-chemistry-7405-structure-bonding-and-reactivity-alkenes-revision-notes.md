These notes condense section **3.3.4.1 Structure, bonding and reactivity** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 at AS and A-level. ## What you must be able to do

- State that alkenes are **unsaturated hydrocarbons**.
- Describe the **double covalent bond** as a centre of **high electron density**.
- Link the double bond to the **reactivity** of alkenes with electrophiles.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hydrocarbon | Compound containing carbon and hydrogen only |
| Saturated | Only single carbon–carbon bonds |
| Unsaturated | Contains at least one C=C (or C≡C) bond |
| Sigma (σ) bond | Head-on overlap of orbitals; electron density between the nuclei |
| Pi (π) bond | Sideways overlap of p orbitals; electron density above and below the plane |
| Electrophile | Electron-pair acceptor |
| Restricted rotation | No twisting about C=C without breaking the pi bond |
| Stereoisomers | Same structural formula, different arrangement of atoms in space |

## Families and formulae

| Family | General formula | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Alkane | CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ | C₃H₈ |
| Alkene | CₙH₂ₙ | C₃H₆ (propene) |
| Cycloalkane | CₙH₂ₙ | C₃H₆ (cyclopropane) |

An alkene and a cycloalkane can share a formula. Use **bromine water** to tell them apart: the alkene decolourises it (orange to colourless).

## The double bond

- C=C = **one sigma bond + one pi bond**.
- Each C of the double bond has **three sigma bonds** and **no lone pairs**.
- Shape around each of these carbons: **trigonal planar**, **about 120°**.
- Pi electron density lies **above and below** the plane of the molecule.
- Ethene has 5 sigma bonds and 1 pi bond, all six atoms in one plane.

## Restricted rotation and E/Z isomerism

- Rotation about C=C would break the pi bond, so groups are fixed in position.
- E/Z isomers need **two different groups on each carbon** of the double bond.
- **Z**: higher-priority groups on the same side. **E**: on opposite sides.
- Priority is decided by atomic number of the atoms attached to each carbon.

| Alkene | E/Z isomers? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| But-1-ene | No | Two H on one carbon |
| But-2-ene | Yes | H and CH₃ on each carbon |
| 2-methylpropene | No | Two CH₃ on one carbon |
| Pent-2-ene | Yes | H and an alkyl group on each carbon |

## Why alkenes are reactive

1. The pi bond is a region of **high electron density**.
2. Pi electrons are above and below the plane, **exposed and less tightly held**.
3. They attract **electrophiles**.
4. The pi bond breaks and two new sigma bonds form, so the reaction is an **addition**.

Alkanes have only sigma bonds, which are non-polar and have no exposed electrons, so they react only with radicals (under ultraviolet light).

## Bond enthalpies (kJ mol⁻¹)

| Bond | Mean bond enthalpy |
|---|---|
| C–C | 347 |
| C=C | 614 |
| C–H | 413 |
| H–H | 436 |

- C=C is **less than twice** C–C, so the pi bond is **weaker** than the sigma bond.
- Pi bond enthalpy ≈ 614 − 347 = **267 kJ mol⁻¹**.
- Hydrogenation of ethene: broken 614 + 436 = 1050; formed 347 + 2(413) = 1173; ΔH = **−123 kJ mol⁻¹**.

## Counting bonds

| Molecule | σ bonds | π bonds |
|---|---|---|
| Ethene, C₂H₄ | 5 | 1 |
| Propene, C₃H₆ | 8 | 1 |
| But-2-ene, C₄H₈ | 11 | 1 |
| Buta-1,3-diene, C₄H₆ | 9 | 2 |

Method: count each C–H and each C–C as one sigma bond, and add one more sigma bond for each double bond (the other bond of the double bond is pi).

## Formula checks

- Alkenes have empirical formula CH₂ (Mᵣ 14.0) and **85.7 % carbon** by mass.
- n = Mᵣ ÷ 14.0 gives the number of carbons for an alkene with one C=C.
- Example: Mᵣ 56.0 gives n = 4, so C₄H₈.

## Command words

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Explain | Pi bond, high electron density, electrophile attack |
| Calculate | Bonds broken minus bonds formed, with signs |
| State | The fact, with a number if one is asked for |
| Deduce | A formula or structure from the data given |

## Exam traps

- "A double bond is two sigma bonds."
- Saying the double bond is reactive because it is strong.
- Bond angle of 109.5° at the double bond.
- Pi electrons placed "between" the nuclei.
- Saying alkenes attract nucleophiles.
- Accepting CₙH₂ₙ as proof of an alkene.
- Writing "no rotation" without explaining the sideways overlap of p orbitals.
- Orange to "clear" for the bromine water test; use colourless.

## Self-test

1. What is a hydrocarbon?
2. What does unsaturated mean?
3. Name the two types of bond in a C=C double bond.
4. Where is the electron density of a pi bond found?
5. State the shape and bond angle around a carbon atom in ethene.
6. Why can there be no free rotation about a C=C bond?
7. What is an electrophile?
8. Why are alkenes more reactive than alkanes?
9. How many sigma and pi bonds are in propene?
10. Use bond enthalpies to estimate the pi bond enthalpy.
11. What is the empirical formula of every alkene with one C=C bond?
12. A hydrocarbon has Mᵣ 84.0 and decolourises bromine water. Suggest its molecular formula.

### Answers

1. A compound containing only carbon and hydrogen.
2. It contains at least one carbon–carbon multiple bond, so it holds fewer hydrogens than the saturated equivalent.
3. One sigma bond and one pi bond.
4. Above and below the plane of the molecule, in the region of the sideways overlap of p orbitals.
5. Trigonal planar, about 120°.
6. Rotation would break the sideways overlap of the p orbitals, which breaks the pi bond.
7. A species that accepts a pair of electrons to form a covalent bond.
8. They have a pi bond: a region of high electron density that attracts electrophiles.
9. Eight sigma bonds and one pi bond.
10. About 614 − 347 = 267 kJ mol⁻¹.
11. CH₂.
12. 84.0 ÷ 14.0 = 6, so C₆H₁₂ (a hexene).
