OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) introduces students to the way organisations record
financial transactions, check those records, prepare financial statements and use the results to
judge performance. It suits students who want a practical, numerate subject with clear links to
business, economics and later study of accounting. The qualification is linear and has no
coursework: it is assessed by two written papers, each lasting 105 minutes, worth 75 marks and
carrying 50% of the grade. First teaching was in September 2024 and the first exams were sat in
May/June 2026.

## How the course is organised

The specification has five content areas, which follow the natural sequence of accounting work.

**1. Sources and recording of data.** Where financial information comes from and how it is entered
into the books in the first place.

**2. Verification of accounting records.** Checking the double entry records, preparing and using a
trial balance, trade payables and trade receivables ledger control accounts, bank reconciliation
statements, correcting errors in double entry records and the effect of errors on profit.

**3. Development of the accounting model.** General accounting concepts and their use; capital and
revenue expenditure and income; depreciation and disposal of non-current assets; adjustments for
payables, receivables and income; and irrecoverable debts, provisions for doubtful debts and
inventory.

**4. Preparation of financial statements.** A comparison of sole traders, partnerships and limited
companies, followed by the financial statements of sole traders, partnership statements with
capital and current accounts, the internal financial statements of limited companies, manufacturing
accounts, the statements of clubs and non-profit making organisations, and incomplete records and
single entry.

**5. Interpretation, analysis and communication of financial information.** Stakeholders in a
business, accounting ratios and financial measures, appraising performance, cash and profit, the
limitations of financial statements and ratio analysis, and computers in accounting.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Introduction to Book-keeping and Financial Accounting | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Financial Statements | 105 minutes | 75 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed written exams taken at the end of the course. There is no
tiering and no coursework, so the whole grade comes from these two equally weighted papers.

## How the parts fit together

The five content areas form a chain. Data recorded in area 1 is checked in area 2, refined by the
concepts and adjustments in area 3, turned into financial statements in area 4 and interpreted in
area 5. A mistake at any point carries forward: an unadjusted accrual or a misposted entry changes
the profit figure, which in turn changes every ratio built on it.

The paper titles reflect this flow. Paper 1, Introduction to Book-keeping and Financial Accounting,
points towards the recording, checking and adjusting side of the course, while Paper 2, Financial
Statements, points towards preparing statements for different types of organisation. Even so, it is
unwise to treat them as separate halves. Preparing a set of statements depends on knowing how to
handle depreciation, irrecoverable debts and other adjustments, and interpreting statements depends
on understanding how they were put together.

Following a single transaction makes the chain concrete. A credit sale is first captured from a
source document and recorded in the books. It then appears in the trade receivables ledger control
account and the trial balance, where errors can be detected and corrected. At the year end, the
same debt may need an adjustment if it proves irrecoverable or a provision for doubtful debts is
made. The result flows into the statement of profit and the closing position of the business, and
finally into ratios that stakeholders use to judge liquidity and performance. Each content area adds
one step, and examiners can enter the chain at any point.

The comparison of sole traders, partnerships and limited companies in area 4 is another linking
idea. The same recording and adjusting techniques apply to all three, but the way capital,
ownership and profit sharing are presented differs, so it is worth learning the similarities
first and the differences second.

Two skills run through everything: accurate, well-laid-out working, and the ability to explain what
the numbers mean. Many questions reward a short written judgement as well as a correct figure, so
practise both from the start.

## Planning your study

A sensible first half of the course covers areas 1 to 3 thoroughly. Practise double entry
until it is second nature, then build the verification tools (trial balance, control accounts, bank
reconciliations and error correction) and the adjustments in area 3. These are the techniques every
later statement relies on.

The second half can then focus on area 4, starting with sole traders and moving through
partnerships, limited companies, manufacturing accounts, clubs and incomplete records, before
finishing with area 5 on ratios, performance appraisal and the limitations of financial statements.
Interpretation can also be introduced earlier, whenever a set of statements is prepared, so that
analysis becomes a habit rather than a final topic.

Keep a running list of statement layouts and ratio formulas, and revisit area 2 and 3 techniques
regularly as the statements topics progress. Begin timed papers once the course has been covered, and use the
exam-preparation page for advice on working through the two papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting assessed?**
By two written papers, Paper 1 Introduction to Book-keeping and Financial Accounting and Paper 2 Financial Statements. Each is 105 minutes, 75 marks and 50% of the grade.

**Is there coursework in 9215?**
No. The qualification is linear and assessed entirely by the two written papers taken at the end of the course.

**Is IGCSE Accounting tiered?**
No. All candidates sit the same two papers.

**When was 9215 first examined?**
First teaching began in September 2024, and the first exams were in May/June 2026.

**What are the five content areas?**
Sources and recording of data; verification of accounting records; development of the accounting model; preparation of financial statements; and interpretation, analysis and communication of financial information.
