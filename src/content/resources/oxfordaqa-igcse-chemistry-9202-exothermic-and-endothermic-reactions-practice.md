This set practises section 3.9.1 Exothermic and endothermic reactions (statements a to d) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A is definitions and classification; Section B uses data, everyday products and reversible reactions. The course is untiered.

## Section A

**1.** Define an endothermic reaction. **[1]**

**2.** Which reaction is endothermic?

A: burning natural gas

B: neutralising hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide

C: heating zinc carbonate to form zinc oxide and carbon dioxide

D: rusting of iron

**[1]**

**3.** State the sign of ΔH for a combustion reaction and explain what the sign tells you. **[2]**

**4.** Give one example of an oxidation reaction, other than combustion, that is exothermic. **[1]**

**5.** State the colour change seen when blue hydrated copper sulfate is heated. **[1]**

**6.** A reaction has ΔH = +56 kJ/mol. State whether the temperature of the surroundings rises or falls during the reaction. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student mixes pairs of substances in a polystyrene cup and records the temperatures. The results are **invented** for practice.

| Mixture | Start / °C | End / °C |
|---|---|---|
| P | 19.5 | 31.0 |
| Q | 19.5 | 12.5 |
| R | 19.5 | 19.5 |
| S | 19.5 | 23.0 |

**(a)** Identify the endothermic reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the sign of ΔH for mixture P and explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what the result for R shows. **[1]**

**(d)** The student says S released less energy than P. Explain why this conclusion may not be valid. **[2]**

**8.** Methane burns in a Bunsen burner: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

**(a)** State the type of energy change. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why methane does not burn until a lighted splint is held near it, even though the reaction is of this type. **[2]**

**9.** Copper carbonate decomposes when heated: CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂

**(a)** Name this type of reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the student can tell from observations that the reaction is endothermic. **[2]**

**10.** Self-heating cans and sports injury packs both contain substances that are kept apart until the product is used.

**(a)** State whether the reaction in each product is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the reactants are kept apart until the product is needed. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one property a reaction should have to be suitable for a hand warmer. **[1]**

**11.** Hydrated copper sulfate and anhydrous copper sulfate are linked by a reversible reaction.

hydrated copper sulfate ⇌ anhydrous copper sulfate + water

**(a)** State whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[1]**

**(b)** A few drops of water are added to anhydrous copper sulfate. Describe two observations. **[2]**

**(c)** The energy taken in when 1 mol of hydrated copper sulfate is fully dehydrated is *x* kJ. State the energy transferred when the anhydrous salt is fully rehydrated, and its direction. **[2]**

**12.** Calcium carbonate decomposes on heating: CaCO₃(s) → CaO(s) + CO₂(g), ΔH = +178 kJ/mol.

**(a)** Is the decomposition exothermic or endothermic? **[1]**

**(b)** State ΔH for the reverse reaction, CaO(s) + CO₂(g) → CaCO₃(s). **[1]**

**(c)** Calcium oxide reacts with water, and the mixture becomes very hot. State the sign of ΔH for this reaction, and suggest a use for it from the specification. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A reaction that takes in energy from the surroundings [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** Negative [1]; the reacting chemicals lose energy, transferring it to the surroundings (the reaction is exothermic) [1].

**4.** Rusting of iron (or iron powder oxidising in a hand warmer, or a metal reacting slowly with oxygen) [1].

**5.** Blue to white [1].

**6.** Falls [1].

**7. (a)** Q [1].

**(b)** Negative [1]; the temperature rose, so energy was transferred from the reacting chemicals to the surroundings [1].

**(c)** There is no measurable energy change (or no reaction took place) [1].

**(d)** The amounts of reactants or volumes of solution may have been different [1]; a fair comparison needs the same quantities so the temperature change per mole can be compared [1].

**8. (a)** Exothermic [1].

**(b)** The particles need a minimum amount of energy, the activation energy, to react [1]; the splint supplies this, then the energy released keeps the reaction going [1].

**9. (a)** Thermal decomposition [1].

**(b)** The reaction only happens while the solid is heated [1]; it stops when heating stops, showing it needs a continuous energy supply [1].

**10. (a)** Self-heating can: exothermic [1]; sports injury pack: endothermic [1].

**(b)** So the reaction (and the energy change) happens only when it is wanted [1].

**(c)** It should release energy slowly over a long time (or reach a safe temperature, not too hot) [1].

**11. (a)** Endothermic [1].

**(b)** The white powder turns blue [1]; the mixture gets warm [1].

**(c)** *x* kJ [1], transferred to the surroundings (released; exothermic) [1].

**12. (a)** Endothermic [1].

**(b)** −178 kJ/mol [1].

**(c)** Negative [1]; self-heating cans [1].

*Examiner insight:* In 12(b) the size of the energy change stays the same and only the sign changes; a different number, such as −89, shows a misunderstanding of reversible reactions.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Linking a rise in temperature to a positive ΔH.
- Calling combustion endothermic because a flame is needed.
- Giving "combustion" as an answer when the question asks for an example other than combustion.
- Copper sulfate colours given the wrong way round.
- Comparing temperature changes from experiments that used different amounts.
