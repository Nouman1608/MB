These questions cover sub-topic 4(d) Alkenes of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.23 to 4.28, none of which has a C reference. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, Br = 80, and take the molar volume of a gas as 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure. Draw structures on paper, showing every bond in displayed formulae. Revise with the [alkenes study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkenes/) and the [alkenes revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the general formula of the alkenes. **[1]**

**2.** Name the functional group in an alkene. **[1]**

**3.** Name the alkene with the formula C₃H₆. **[1]**

**4.** State the colour change when ethene is shaken with bromine water. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by an unsaturated hydrocarbon. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Butene, C₄H₈, has two unbranched isomers.

**(a)** Draw the structural formula of each and name them. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why these two are isomers. **[1]**

**7.** Draw the displayed formula of propene. **[2]**

**8.** Ethene reacts with bromine.

**(a)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the product. **[1]**
**(c)** State the type of reaction and explain how you know. **[2]**
**(d)** State two differences between this reaction and the reaction of methane with chlorine. **[2]**

**9.** A student has two colourless liquids, hexane (an alkane) and hex-1-ene (an alkene).

**(a)** Describe a test to tell them apart, with the result for each. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the result for hex-1-ene. **[2]**

**10.** 0.84 g of ethene (Mr = 28) reacts completely with bromine (Mr = 160) to form 1,2-dibromoethane (Mr = 188).

**(a)** Calculate the moles of ethene. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of bromine that reacts. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of 1,2-dibromoethane formed. **[1]**
**(d)** Show that your answers agree with the conservation of mass. **[1]**

**11.** An alkene has a relative molecular mass of 70.

**(a)** Use the general formula to find the number of carbon atoms. **[2]**
**(b)** Give its molecular formula and its empirical formula. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why ethene burns with a more smoky flame than ethane. **[2]**

**13.** 7.0 g of propene (Mr = 42) is shaken with excess bromine water. Calculate the mass of bromine (Mr = 160) that reacts, to one decimal place. **[3]**

**14.** Four hydrocarbons are C₃H₈, C₃H₆, C₅H₁₀ and C₆H₁₄. State which are alkenes and give the rule you used. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** CₙH₂ₙ [1].

**2.** A carbon-carbon double bond (C=C) [1].

**3.** Propene [1].

**4.** Orange (brown) to colourless [1].

**5.** A hydrocarbon that contains a carbon-carbon double bond [1] so it has fewer hydrogen atoms than the alkane with the same number of carbons (it can bond to more atoms) [1].

**6. (a)** But-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ [1 for the formula, 1 for the name]; but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃ [1 for the formula, 1 for the name].
**(b)** They have the same molecular formula (C₄H₈) but different structural formulae [1].

**7.** Three carbon atoms in a chain with one C=C double bond between the first two [1]; each carbon has four bonds in total, with six hydrogen atoms (two on the first carbon, one on the second and three on the third) [1].

**8. (a)** C₂H₄ + Br₂ → C₂H₄Br₂ (or CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br) [1].
**(b)** 1,2-dibromoethane [1].
**(c)** Addition [1], because two molecules join to form a single product [1].
**(d)** Any two from: this reaction is addition, whereas the methane reaction is substitution; this gives one product, but the methane reaction gives two; this does not need UV radiation, whereas the methane reaction does [2].

**9. (a)** Add bromine water to each liquid and shake [1]. Hex-1-ene turns the bromine water from orange (brown) to colourless [1]. Hexane leaves the bromine water orange (no change) [1].
**(b)** Hex-1-ene has a C=C double bond [1] so bromine adds across it and the coloured bromine is used up [1].

**10. (a)** 0.84 / 28 = 0.030 mol [1].
**(b)** 0.030 x 160 = 4.8 g [1].
**(c)** 0.030 x 188 = 5.64 g [1].
**(d)** 0.84 + 4.8 = 5.64 g, the same as the mass of product [1].

**11. (a)** 14n = 70 [1], so n = 5 [1].
**(b)** C₅H₁₀ [1]; CH₂ [1].

**12.** Ethene has a higher percentage of carbon than ethane (85.7% compared with 80%) [1], so combustion is more likely to be incomplete and produce soot (carbon) [1].

**13.** Moles of propene = 7.0 / 42 = 0.167 mol [1]. The ratio is 1 : 1, so moles of Br₂ = 0.167 mol [1]. Mass = 0.167 x 160 = 26.7 g [1].

**14.** C₃H₆ and C₅H₁₀ are alkenes [1]. They fit the general formula CₙH₂ₙ (twice as many hydrogens as carbons); C₃H₈ and C₆H₁₄ fit CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, so they are alkanes [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that bromine water turns "clear" rather than colourless.
- Giving the general formula as CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
- Forgetting the position number in but-1-ene or but-2-ene.
- Drawing the double bond with only one line.
- Writing that the bromine addition needs UV radiation.
- Describing the test without saying what happens with the alkane.
