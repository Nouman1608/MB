The syllabus is not tiered, so every question applies to every candidate.

## Questions

**1.** A baker leaves bread dough in a warm place for an hour. Explain why the volume of the dough increases. **[3]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by genetic modification. **[2]**

**3.** In ideal conditions, a species of bacterium divides into two every 20 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate how many cells are produced from one cell after 3 hours. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this feature makes bacteria useful in biotechnology. **[1]**
**(c)** Discuss two other reasons why bacteria are useful in genetic modification. **[4]**

**4.** A company grows a fungus in a fermenter to make an antibiotic.

**(a)** Explain why the fermenter has a jacket through which cold water flows. **[2]**
**(b)** During a run, the pH probe shows that the pH is falling. Suggest a cause and state how the problem is corrected. **[2]**
**(c)** Sterile air is bubbled into the fermenter and paddles stir the contents. Explain why. **[2]**
**(d)** State two other conditions, apart from temperature, pH and oxygen, that are controlled in a fermenter. **[2]**

**5.** A student made three identical balls of dough, put each in a measuring cylinder, and kept them at different temperatures. The height of each dough was 4.0 cm at the start. The table shows the heights after 40 minutes.

| Temperature / °C | Height after 40 minutes / cm |
|---|---|
| 20 | 5.2 |
| 35 | 8.8 |
| 60 | 4.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of rise at 35 °C, in cm per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the dough rose more at 35 °C than at 20 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result at 60 °C. **[2]**
**(d)** State two variables the student kept constant. **[2]**

**6.** A cloth stained with egg was washed with biological and non-biological washing powder at different temperatures. The table shows the percentage of the stain removed.

| Temperature / °C | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Biological / % | 40 | 65 | 85 | 55 | 30 |
| Non-biological / % | 20 | 25 | 35 | 40 | 45 |

**(a)** State the difference between the two powders at 40 °C. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the results for the biological powder between 20 °C and 60 °C. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the type of enzyme that removes the egg stain. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one environmental advantage of using biological powder. **[1]**

**7.** 100 g of crushed plums gave 12 cm³ of juice in 15 minutes. The same mass of crushed plums treated with pectinase gave 21 cm³ in 15 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in juice volume. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how pectinase increases the volume of juice. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe a suitable control for this experiment. **[1]**
**(d)** State one other benefit of using pectinase in juice production. **[1]**

**8.** Some milk is sold as lactose-free.

**(a)** Name the enzyme used to make lactose-free milk. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the two products formed. **[2]**
**(c)** State who benefits from drinking lactose-free milk. **[1]**

**9.** Human insulin is now made by bacteria.

**(a)** Outline how bacteria have been changed so that they make human insulin, and how large amounts are produced. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest why insulin made this way can supply large numbers of people with diabetes. **[1]**

**10.** A farmer grew a herbicide-resistant variety of maize in one field and a non-modified variety in a similar field. Both fields were sprayed in the same way to control weeds. The yields were 3.2 tonnes per hectare for the non-modified maize and 4.0 tonnes per hectare for the herbicide-resistant maize.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how herbicide resistance leads to a higher yield. **[3]**
**(c)** Outline how genetic modification can make a crop more nutritious. **[2]**

**11.** Cotton plants have been genetically modified to resist insect pests. Discuss the potential advantages and risks of growing this cotton. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Yeast respires anaerobically, using sugar in the dough [1]. Carbon dioxide is released [1]. The gas is trapped as bubbles in the dough, so the volume increases [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The gas must be named as carbon dioxide; "air", "oxygen" or "alcohol" in its place gives an incorrect mechanism.

**2.** Changing the genetic material of an organism [1] by removing, changing or inserting individual genes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the syllabus wording; "changing DNA" alone does not say how, so it describes only half of the definition.

**3. (a)** 3 hours = 180 minutes; 180 ÷ 20 = 9 divisions [1]; 2⁹ = **512 cells** [1]
**(b)** A large population, and so a large amount of product, is made in a short time [1].
**(c)** There are no ethical concerns over their manipulation and growth [1], so they can be changed and grown in large numbers without the objections raised by using animals [1]. They contain plasmids [1], so a gene can be inserted into a plasmid and taken up by the bacterium [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "discuss" means each reason needs its consequence; a bare list of "ethics, plasmids" states features but does not explain them.

**4. (a)** Respiration of the fungus releases heat [1]; cooling keeps the temperature at the optimum so enzymes are not denatured [1].
**(b)** Acidic waste products are building up [1]; alkali is added to raise the pH [1].
**(c)** Oxygen is needed for aerobic respiration, so the fungus grows and makes product [1]; stirring spreads oxygen and nutrients evenly through the mixture [1].
**(d)** Nutrient supply [1]; removal of waste products [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The syllabus limits fermenter conditions to five; answers such as "light" or "pressure" fall outside that list and are not the conditions asked about.

**5. (a)** Rise = 8.8 − 4.0 = 4.8 cm [1]; 4.8 ÷ 40 = **0.12 cm per minute** [1]
**(b)** At 35 °C the yeast enzymes are closer to their optimum, so they work faster [1]; anaerobic respiration is faster, so more carbon dioxide is released and trapped in the dough [1].
**(c)** The dough did not rise at all [1]; at 60 °C the yeast enzymes are denatured, so no respiration takes place and no carbon dioxide is made [1].
**(d)** Any two: mass of dough; mass of yeast; mass of sugar; time [1] [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), subtract the starting height first; dividing 8.8 by 40 gives a rate that includes the dough's original size.

**6. (a)** Biological removes 50 percentage points more stain (85% compared with 35%) [1].
**(b)** From 20 °C to 40 °C stain removal increases because the enzymes have more kinetic energy and work faster [1]; the optimum is about 40 °C, where 85% is removed [1]; above 40 °C removal falls, to 30% at 60 °C, because the enzymes are denatured [1].
**(c)** Protease [1].
**(d)** Clothes can be washed at a lower temperature, so less energy is used to heat the water [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), quote values from the table with units, and use "denatured" rather than "killed" for the fall.

**7. (a)** Increase = 21 − 12 = 9 cm³ [1]; (9 ÷ 12) × 100 = **75%** [1]
**(b)** Pectinase breaks down pectin in the plant cell walls [1], so more juice is released from the cells [1].
**(c)** The same mass of crushed plums with boiled (denatured) pectinase added, kept for the same time at the same temperature [1].
**(d)** The juice is clearer [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A control needs the enzyme to be inactive while everything else stays the same; "no plums" or "a different fruit" changes more than one variable.

**8. (a)** Lactase [1]
**(b)** Glucose [1] and galactose [1]
**(c)** People who are lactose intolerant (cannot digest lactose) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the spelling: lactose is the sugar and lactase is the enzyme; swapping them changes the meaning of the answer.

**9. (a)** The gene that controls the production of human insulin is cut from human DNA [1]. It is inserted into bacterial DNA, using a plasmid [1]. The bacteria are grown in a fermenter, where they reproduce rapidly [1], and the insulin they make is extracted and purified [1].
**(b)** Bacteria reproduce rapidly, so a very large amount of insulin can be made in a short time [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the gene precisely as the human insulin gene; "insulin is put into bacteria" confuses the protein with the gene that codes for it.

**10. (a)** Increase = 4.0 − 3.2 = 0.8 tonnes per hectare [1]; (0.8 ÷ 3.2) × 100 = **25%** [1]
**(b)** The herbicide kills the weeds but not the resistant maize [1]; there is less competition for light, water and mineral ions [1]; so the maize grows better and the yield is higher [1].
**(c)** A gene is inserted into the crop [1] so that it makes an additional vitamin, or a substance the body converts into one, for example Golden Rice making beta-carotene for vitamin A [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), name what the weeds compete for; "the weeds are gone so the crop grows better" lacks the link to resources.

**11.** Advantages: less of the crop is damaged by insect pests, so yields are higher [1]; less insecticide needs to be sprayed, saving money [1]; fewer insects that are not pests are killed by sprayed insecticide [1]. Risks: the pests may evolve resistance to the toxin, so the benefit is lost [1]; insects that are not pests may be harmed if they feed on the cotton [1]; the inserted gene may spread to wild relatives by pollination [1]. Other valid points, such as the cost of buying seed each year, are also creditworthy. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer needs points on both sides; six advantages with no risks does not answer the question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the gas that makes bread rise as oxygen or air instead of carbon dioxide.
- Giving the features of bacteria without the reason each one helps.
- Writing that a fermenter needs heating, when respiration means it usually needs cooling.
- Listing conditions outside the five in the syllabus.
- Calculating rates from the final height rather than from the change.
- Using "killed" for enzymes instead of "denatured".
- Writing a control that changes more than one variable.
- Confusing lactose and lactase, or giving glucose as the only product.
- Saying insulin, rather than the insulin gene, is put into bacteria.
- Treating a "discuss" question as a list of advantages only.
