These notes condense **3.2.1.1 Classification** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: "An element is classified as s, p, d or f block according to its position in the Periodic Table, which is determined by its proton number." It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1, and it relies on electron configurations up to Z = 36 from 3.1.1.3.

The [classification study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-1-1/) explains the reasoning with worked examples. Test yourself with the [classification practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-1-1-practice/). For the whole of section 3.2.1, see the [periodicity revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-periodicity-revision-notes/).

## The chain to remember

Proton number → number of electrons → electron configuration → position (period and group) → block.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton number, Z | Number of protons in the nucleus; sets the element's position in the table |
| Period | A horizontal row; its number is the outermost occupied shell |
| Group | A vertical column; for s and p blocks the number of outer-shell electrons (Group 0 has a full outer shell) |
| Block | The region of the table in which a particular type of sub-shell (s, p, d or f) is being filled |
| Sub-shell | s, p, d or f, holding 2, 6, 10 or 14 electrons |

## The four blocks

| Block | Where | Width | Examples up to Z = 36 |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | Groups 1 and 2, and helium | 2 columns | H, He, Li, Be, Na, Mg, K, Ca |
| p | Groups 3 to 0 (except helium) | 6 columns | B to Ne, Al to Ar, Ga to Kr |
| d | Between Groups 2 and 3 | 10 columns | Sc to Zn |
| f | Lanthanide and actinide rows | 14 columns | none (all have Z above 36) |

## Filling order and configurations

```
1s  2s  2p  3s  3p  4s  3d  4p
```

- 4s fills before 3d, but in d-block ions the 4s electrons are lost first.
- Shorthand: [Ar] = 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.
- Exceptions to learn: **Cr = [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹** and **Cu = [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹**. Both remain d block.

## Reading position from configuration

- Period = number of the outermost shell (for example 4s² 4p⁵ means Period 4).
- s and p blocks: group = number of outer-shell electrons (4s² 4p⁵ means Group 7, bromine).
- d block: the 3d sub-shell is the one being filled across that part of Period 4 (Sc to Zn).

## Special cases

- **Hydrogen**: 1s¹, s block.
- **Helium**: 1s², s block, though placed in Group 0 because its outer shell is full.
- **Zinc**: d block, but not a transition metal in this specification (transition metal properties are described for Ti to Cu).
- **Ions**: classify the element, not the ion. Cu²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁹ and copper is d block; Cl⁻ has the argon configuration and chlorine is p block.
- **Aluminium**: a metal, but p block (3s² 3p¹).
- **Period 3** has no d-block elements: 3d fills only after 4s.

## Classification drill

Work across each row: configuration first, then period, group and block.

| Z | Element | Configuration | Period | Group | Block |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Li | 1s² 2s¹ | 2 | 1 | s |
| 9 | F | 1s² 2s² 2p⁵ | 2 | 7 | p |
| 12 | Mg | [Ne] 3s² | 3 | 2 | s |
| 16 | S | [Ne] 3s² 3p⁴ | 3 | 6 | p |
| 19 | K | [Ar] 4s¹ | 4 | 1 | s |
| 23 | V | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² | 4 | (d block) | d |
| 30 | Zn | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² | 4 | (d block) | d |
| 35 | Br | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁵ | 4 | 7 | p |

[Ne] is shorthand for 1s² 2s² 2p⁶. Notice that bromine's ten 3d electrons do not make it d block: the sub-shell being filled at bromine's position is 4p.

## Exam traps

- Putting helium in the p block.
- Writing the configuration of Fe²⁺ as [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s².
- Assuming "metal" means s or d block.
- Classifying an ion instead of the element.
- Writing chromium as [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² or copper as [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s².
- Treating "d block" and "transition metal" as identical.
- Assuming every element printed in the detached rows must be f block when the table supplied shows otherwise.

## Quick self-test

1. What determines an element's position in the Periodic Table?
2. State the block of the element with Z = 15.
3. State the block of the element with Z = 22.
4. State the block of helium and explain why.
5. Write the electron configuration of a chromium atom.
6. Write the electron configuration of Cu²⁺.
7. Explain why Period 3 has no d-block elements.
8. Why does the d block contain ten columns?
9. An element is in Period 4, Group 6. Write its configuration and state its block.
10. Is zinc a d-block element? Is it a transition metal in this specification?
11. An element is in one of the two detached rows of the table. State its block.
12. State the block of sodium, and say whether Na⁺ changes the answer.

### Answers

1. Its **proton number**.
2. Z = 15 is phosphorus, 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p³: **p block**.
3. Z = 22 is titanium, [Ar] 3d² 4s²: **d block**.
4. **s block**: its only electrons (1s²) are in an s sub-shell.
5. **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁵ 4s¹** ([Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹).
6. **[Ar] 3d⁹** (the 4s electron and one 3d electron are lost).
7. After 3p, the next sub-shell to fill is 4s, which starts Period 4; 3d fills after 4s.
8. A d sub-shell has five orbitals, each holding two electrons: ten electrons.
9. **[Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁴** (selenium): **p block**.
10. **Yes**, it is d block. **No**, the specification describes transition metal properties for Ti to Cu only.
11. **f block**.
12. Sodium is **s block**. The ion does not change the classification: blocks belong to elements.
