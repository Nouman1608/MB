## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In
the 4-mark "explain" questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it, up to
two marks for each of the two; the 4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant,
accurate detail. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a
general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question.
The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Hazrat Salman al-Farsi (RA) [1].

**2.** Abdullah ibn Ubayy [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: they were treated kindly and fed; some were released on payment of a ransom; literate prisoners could earn freedom by teaching Muslim children to read; some were released without ransom.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: they could not cross the trench; the siege dragged on and supplies ran short; distrust grew between the allied tribes; a fierce cold wind destroyed their camp (33:9).

**5.** Any four, [1] each: about fifty archers were placed on a hill; under the command of Abdullah ibn Jubayr (RA); they were ordered not to leave their position whatever happened; when the Muslims seemed to be winning, most of them left to collect spoils; Abdullah ibn Jubayr and a few stayed and were killed; Khalid ibn al-Walid's cavalry came round the hill and attacked the Muslims from behind.

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Security** [1]: it removed a hostile base north of Madinah that had supported the confederates [1].
- **Mercy in victory** [1]: the people were allowed to stay, keep their religion and farm the land [1].
- **Economic strength** [1]: half of the oasis's produce supported the community in Madinah [1].
- **Courage of Ali (RA)** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) gave him the flag and he took the fort, showing the reward for love of Allah and His Messenger [1].

**7.** For each of two events: event identified [1] and explained [1].

- **The ambush** [1]: the Hawazin attacked at dawn in a narrow valley, and many Muslims fled [1].
- **The Prophet (pbuh) stood firm** [1]: he called out that he was the Prophet (pbuh) and did not retreat, giving the army a point to rally to [1].
- **Abbas (RA) called the companions back** [1]: the Ansar and others regrouped and won the battle [1].
- **Distribution of spoils** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) gave generously to new Muslims and reassured the Ansar [1].

**8.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Peaceful spread of Islam** [1]: tribes came to Madinah of their own accord to accept Islam [1].
- **Unity of Arabia** [1]: rival tribes came under one faith and one leadership [1].
- **The Prophet (pbuh) as statesman** [1]: he received delegations, made agreements, and sent teachers and governors such as Mu'adh ibn Jabal (RA) [1].
- **Fulfilment of revelation** [1]: Surah an-Nasr describes people "entering into the religion of Allah in multitudes" [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Survival** [1]: defeat could have ended the community; victory secured it [1].
- **Proof of divine help** [1]: about 313 Muslims beat a force about three times larger, which strengthened faith [1].
- **Standing in Arabia** [1]: other tribes now took the Muslims seriously as a power [1].
- **Moral example** [1]: the humane treatment of prisoners and their use as teachers set standards of conduct [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Allah had given you victory at Badr while you were weak" (Qur'an 3:123) [2].
- The Qur'an calls Badr "the day of criterion" (8:41), the day that separated truth from falsehood [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Value of consultation** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) adopted Salman's idea, so good advice is welcomed whatever its source [1].
- **Leadership by example** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) dug with the companions and shared their hunger [1].
- **Unity under pressure** [1]: the believers held together under siege while betrayal came from outside the community of believers [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: relief came through the wind and the breakdown of the alliance, not by fighting [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "O you who have believed, remember the favour of Allah upon you when armies came to [attack] you and We sent upon them a wind and armies [of angels] you did not see" (Qur'an 33:9) [2].
- "Consult them in the matter" (Qur'an 3:159) supports the lesson of shura [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **Badr:** a small force defeated a larger one.
- **Hunayn:** the largest Muslim army nearly lost because people relied on its size (9:25).
- **Trench:** the confederates withdrew without a full battle.
- **Lesson for today:** Muslims rely on Allah in difficulties rather than on wealth, status or numbers alone.
- **Against the statement:** the Prophet (pbuh) still planned, posted troops, dug the trench and consulted; the Qur'an expects effort as well as trust; Uhud was lost through human error, not lack of divine help.
- **Balance:** victory comes from Allah, but Allah's help comes to those who obey, plan and are patient.

*Use of the passage*

- "Allah had given you victory at Badr while you were weak" supports the statement directly.
- "Upon Allah the believers should rely" gives the principle.
- "Two parties among you were about to lose courage, but Allah was their ally" shows Allah strengthening the weak.
- "To post the believers at their stations" shows that planning was still part of the Prophet's (pbuh) leadership.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **Uhud teaches obedience:** disobeying the command to stay on the hill turned success into loss.
- **Uhud teaches about greed:** the archers left for spoils.
- **Uhud teaches patience** in grief, after the martyrdom of Hamza (RA) and many others.
- **Badr teaches faith and gratitude:** victory against the odds shows Allah's help and the need to thank Him.
- **Against the statement:** both teach complementary lessons; Badr shows what Allah gives to the faithful, Uhud what is lost by disobedience; many would say neither teaches "more".

*Use of the passage*

- "If you remain patient and conscious of Allah ... your Lord will reinforce you" shows that help depends on patience and taqwa, the qualities lost at Uhud.
- "To reassure your hearts thereby" shows that the angels at Badr were reassurance, not the real cause of victory.
- "Victory is not except from Allah" applies to both battles: at Badr it was given, at Uhud it was withheld.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Telling the story of a battle when the question asks for its importance.
- Getting the order of events wrong, especially placing Khaibar or Hunayn too early.
- Saying Uhud was lost because the Muslims were outnumbered.
- Treating Aam ul Wufood as a battle.
- In the 12-mark questions, giving only one side or not quoting the passage.
