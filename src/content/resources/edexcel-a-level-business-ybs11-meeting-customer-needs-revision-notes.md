## The market in five lines

- **Mass market:** many customers, standardised product, low unit cost, low margin, intense price competition.
- **Niche market:** small specialised group, premium price, higher margin, fewer rivals, but exposed if the niche shrinks or a large firm moves in.
- **Market size** = total sales of all firms (value or volume). **Market share** = firm's sales ÷ market size × 100. **Market growth** = change in market size ÷ original size × 100.
- **Dynamic markets** change through online retailing, shifting tastes, innovation and new entrants; firms must adapt or lose customers.
- **Competition** caps price, forces better quality and service, and squeezes margins.

## Risk versus uncertainty

| | Risk | Uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Can it be estimated? | Yes, from data or past experience | No reliable basis for an estimate |
| Example | Sales dip each February, recorded for six years | A new law bans a key ingredient overnight |
| Can research help? | Yes, it narrows the estimate | Only partly: research can turn some uncertainty into risk, never all of it |

## Market research at a glance

**Three purposes (specification wording):** identify and anticipate customer needs and wants; quantify likely demand; gain insight into consumer behaviour.

**Primary methods:** surveys/questionnaires; focus groups/consumer panels; face-to-face/telephone interviews; product trials/test marketing.

**Secondary methods:** websites/social media; newspapers/magazines/TV/radio; reports; databases.

| | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Specific to the question, up to date, not available to rivals | Slow and expensive; quality depends on the sample |
| Secondary | Quick and cheap; may cover the whole market | May be out of date or collected for a different purpose |
| Quantitative | Measures how many; easy to compare | Does not explain why |
| Qualitative | Explains reasons and feelings | Small groups; hard to generalise |

## Sampling in one table

| Method | How it works | Best when | Drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random | Every member has an equal chance | A full list of the population exists | Needs that list; may miss small groups by chance |
| Quota | Researcher fills set numbers per group | Time and money are short | Researcher chooses who to ask, so bias can creep in |
| Stratified | Random selection within each group, in proportion to group size | Groups behave differently | Needs accurate population proportions |

**Stratified arithmetic:** group's share of population × sample size. A café with 3,000 members, 1,200 of them students, choosing a sample of 150, picks 1,200 ÷ 3,000 × 150 = 60 students.

## Market positioning

- **Product orientation:** make first, then find buyers. **Market orientation:** research first, then design.
- **Market map:** two customer-relevant variables; shows clusters and gaps; a gap needs demand behind it.
- **Segmentation bases:** demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural.
- **Competitive advantage:** a reason customers choose you over rivals, through lower cost or through being different.
- **Differentiation:** the purpose is to make the product distinct so customers are less likely to switch on price.
- **Adding value:** selling price minus the cost of bought-in inputs; raised through brand, design, quality, convenience or service.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Niche market | A small, specialised part of a larger market |
| Brand | A name, symbol or design that identifies a product and sets it apart |
| Online retailing | Selling to customers through websites and apps rather than shops |
| Innovation | Bringing a new product or process successfully to market |
| Consumer panel | A group of customers consulted repeatedly over time |
| Test marketing | Launching in a small area first to measure real sales |
| Quota sample | A sample filled to set numbers per group, chosen by the researcher |
| Market mapping | Plotting brands against two variables to show positioning |
| Market orientation | Basing decisions on research into what customers want |
| Value added | The difference between selling price and the cost of bought-in inputs |

## Exam traps

- **Percentage points:** share rising from 7.5% to 8% is 0.5 percentage points, not 0.5%.
- A firm's sales can rise while its share falls, if the market grows faster.
- Adding value is not the same as profit: value added ignores the firm's own wages, rent and other overheads.
- A quota sample is not random: the researcher picks who fills each quota.
- An "Analyse" answer needs no judgement; an "Assess" answer without one cannot reach the top level.
- Do not say research "removes risk"; it reduces risk and turns some uncertainty into risk.

## Self-test

1. Give two characteristics of a niche market.
2. A firm sells $2.4m in a $30m market. Calculate its market share.
3. The market grows from $30m to $33m. Calculate market growth.
4. Distinguish risk from uncertainty.
5. Which research purpose does test marketing serve most directly?
6. Name two methods of secondary research from the specification.
7. Why might a quota sample be biased?
8. A gym has 2,000 members: 500 aged over 50. In a stratified sample of 80, how many should be over 50?
9. Distinguish product orientation from market orientation.
10. What is the purpose of product differentiation?
11. A bakery buys ingredients for $0.90 a cake and sells it for $3.20. Calculate value added per cake.
12. Give one reason a gap on a market map may not be an opportunity.

### Answers

1. Small number of customers with specialised needs; premium prices and fewer rivals (also: higher margins, lower volumes).
2. 2.4 ÷ 30 × 100 = **8%**.
3. (33 − 30) ÷ 30 × 100 = **10%**.
4. A risk can be estimated from data; uncertainty has no reliable basis for an estimate.
5. Quantifying likely demand, because it measures actual sales in a trial area.
6. Any two: websites/social media; newspapers/magazines/TV/radio; reports; databases.
7. The researcher chooses who fills each quota, so may approach people who are easy to reach or seem friendly.
8. 500 ÷ 2,000 × 80 = **20**.
9. Product orientation starts with what the firm can make; market orientation starts with research into what customers want.
10. To make the product distinct from rivals so customers are less willing to switch on price, allowing a higher price or greater loyalty.
11. 3.20 − 0.90 = **$2.30** per cake.
12. There may be no demand at that position, or serving it may not be profitable.
