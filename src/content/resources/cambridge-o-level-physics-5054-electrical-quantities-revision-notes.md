## Equations and units

```
current          I = Q / t          A  (= C/s)
e.m.f.           E = W / Q          V  (= J/C)    W = work done BY the source
p.d.             V = W / Q          V  (= J/C)    W = work done ON a component
resistance       R = V / I          Ω
wire             R ∝ length,  R ∝ 1 / cross-sectional area
```

## Charge (4.2.1)

- Positive and negative charge; unit coulomb (C).
- Unlike charges attract; like charges repel.
- Friction charging moves **electrons only**.
  - Polythene rubbed with a cloth: gains electrons → negative.
  - Acetate rubbed with a cloth: loses electrons → positive.
  - The cloth gets an equal and opposite charge.
- Test: hang one charged rod in a stirrup; bring another near; watch for
  repulsion or attraction.

## Electric fields

- **Definition:** a region in which an electric charge experiences a force.
- **Direction:** the force on a **positive** charge.

| Arrangement | Field lines |
|---|---|
| Point charge | Radial: outward from +, inward to − |
| Charged conducting sphere | Radial from the surface, at 90° to it |
| Parallel plates | Parallel, evenly spaced, + → −; uniform between the plates |

## Conductors and insulators

| Conductors | Insulators |
|---|---|
| Copper, aluminium, other metals, graphite | Plastic, rubber, glass, dry wood |
| Free electrons that can move through the material | Electrons bound to atoms |

Test: material between crocodile clips in a series circuit with a cell and
a lamp or ammeter.

## Current (4.2.2)

- Current = charge passing a point per unit time.
- In metals: flow of free electrons.
- Conventional current: + → −. Electron flow: − → +.
- d.c. one direction; a.c. repeatedly reverses.
- Ammeter: **series**; choose a range just above the expected value.

## E.m.f. and p.d. (4.2.3)

- Both in volts; 1 V = 1 J/C.
- Voltmeter: **parallel**.
- Cells in series, same way round: add. Reversed cell: subtract.
- Identical cells in parallel: e.m.f. of one cell.

## Resistance (4.2.4)

- R = V / I.
- **Ohm's law:** current in a metallic conductor is directly proportional
  to the p.d. across it, provided the temperature is constant.
- Double length → double R. Double area → half R. Double diameter →
  quarter R.
- Higher temperature → higher resistance in a metal filament.

## I–V graphs

| Component | Graph | Resistance |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed resistor | Straight line through origin | Constant |
| Filament lamp | S-shaped curve through origin, flattening at higher V either way | Increases as it heats |
| Diode | Near-zero current in reverse; steep rise above a small forward p.d. | Very high in reverse, low forward |

Find R at a point by R = V ÷ I using that point's readings.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Electric current | Charge passing a point per unit time |
| E.m.f. | Electrical work done by a source in moving unit charge round a complete circuit |
| P.d. | Work done by unit charge passing through a component |
| Resistance | p.d. across a component ÷ current through it |
| Electric field | Region in which an electric charge experiences a force |
| Conventional current | Direction from positive to negative terminal outside the source |
| Direct current | Current in one direction only |
| Alternating current | Current that repeatedly reverses direction |

## Exam traps

- Minutes → seconds before I = Q / t.
- "Positive charge moves in friction charging" is wrong; electrons move.
- E.m.f. is not a force, despite its name; it is energy per unit charge.
- Identical cells in parallel do **not** add.
- Ohm's law needs "constant temperature".
- A filament lamp does **not** obey Ohm's law over its range.
- Diameter questions: area depends on diameter squared.
- Field direction is set by a **positive** charge.
- Ammeter in series, voltmeter in parallel — swapping them is a common
  circuit-diagram error.

## Self-test

1. State the unit of charge.
2. A polythene rod becomes negative when rubbed. Explain why.
3. Describe the electric field between two oppositely charged parallel
   plates.
4. 45 C flows in 30 s. Find the current.
5. A current of 2.0 A flows for 5.0 minutes. Find the charge.
6. In which direction do electrons flow in a circuit, compared with
   conventional current?
7. A 12 V battery moves 5.0 C round a circuit. Find the work done.
8. Find the total e.m.f. of two 1.5 V cells and one 2.0 V cell in series,
   all the same way round.
9. A resistor has 6.0 V across it and 0.40 A through it. Find R.
10. A wire of resistance 12 Ω is replaced by a wire of the same material
    and length with double the diameter. Find the new resistance.
11. State Ohm's law.
12. Explain why the resistance of a filament lamp increases as the current
    increases.

**Answers**

1. The coulomb (C).
2. Electrons are transferred from the cloth to the rod.
3. Evenly spaced parallel lines from the positive plate to the negative
   plate (a uniform field).
4. I = 45 ÷ 30 = **1.5 A**.
5. Q = 2.0 × 300 = **600 C**.
6. Opposite: electrons flow from negative to positive.
7. W = E Q = 12 × 5.0 = **60 J**.
8. 1.5 + 1.5 + 2.0 = **5.0 V**.
9. R = 6.0 ÷ 0.40 = **15 Ω**.
10. Area × 4, so R = 12 ÷ 4 = **3.0 Ω**.
11. The current in a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the
    p.d. across it, provided the temperature remains constant.
12. The current heats the filament; at higher temperature the metal ions
    vibrate more, so electrons collide with them more often and the
    resistance rises.
