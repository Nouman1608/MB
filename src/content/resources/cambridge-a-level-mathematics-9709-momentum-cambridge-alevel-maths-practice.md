These questions cover subtopic 4.3 Momentum of Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 (Paper 4). Take g = 10 m s⁻² and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. All motion is in a straight line, and all surfaces are smooth unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** A ball of mass 0.15 kg moving at 12 m s⁻¹ hits a vertical wall at right angles and rebounds at 8 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of the change in the momentum of the ball. **[2]**

**2.** Particle P, of mass 3 kg, moving at 4 m s⁻¹, collides with particle Q, of mass 1 kg, which is at rest. The particles coalesce. Find their common speed after the collision. **[2]**

**3.** Particle A, of mass 0.2 kg, moving at 6 m s⁻¹, collides directly with particle B, of mass 0.4 kg, moving at 3 m s⁻¹ towards A. After the collision A moves at 2 m s⁻¹ in the opposite direction to its original motion. Find the speed and direction of B after the collision. **[3]**

**4.** Particle A, of mass m kg, moving at 5 m s⁻¹, collides with particle B, of mass 2 kg, moving at 1 m s⁻¹ in the same direction. After the collision A moves at 2 m s⁻¹ and B moves at 4 m s⁻¹, both in the original direction. Find m. **[3]**

**5.** Two particles, of masses 0.5 kg and 0.7 kg, move towards each other, each with speed 3 m s⁻¹. They collide and coalesce. Find the speed of the combined particle and state the direction in which it moves. **[3]**

**6.** A truck of mass 2000 kg moving at 3 m s⁻¹ on a straight horizontal track runs into a stationary wagon of mass 1000 kg, and the two couple together. Find the common speed after coupling and the kinetic energy lost. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Particles A, B and C, of masses 0.4 kg, 0.6 kg and 0.3 kg, lie in that order on a straight line on a smooth horizontal surface. A is projected towards B at 6 m s⁻¹, and B is at rest. After A hits B, B moves at 3 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity of A immediately after this collision.

**(b)** B then collides with C, which is moving towards B at 2 m s⁻¹. B and C coalesce. Find the velocity of the combined particle.

**(c)** Determine whether A collides with the combined particle. **[7]**

**8.** Particle P, of mass 0.5 kg, is projected at 8 m s⁻¹ along a rough horizontal surface. The coefficient of friction between each particle and the surface is 0.4. After travelling 3 m, P collides with particle Q, of mass 1.5 kg, which is at rest. The particles coalesce.

**(a)** Find the speed of P immediately before the collision.

**(b)** Find the speed of the combined particle immediately after the collision.

**(c)** Find the distance travelled by the combined particle before it comes to rest. **[8]**

**9.** Particle A, of mass 4 kg, moving at u m s⁻¹, collides with particle B, of mass 1 kg, which is at rest. The particles coalesce. The kinetic energy lost in the collision is 40 J. Find u. **[5]**

**10.** Particle A, of mass 2 kg, moving at 5 m s⁻¹, collides directly with particle B, of mass 3 kg, moving at 2 m s⁻¹ towards A. After the collision the speed of A is 1 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Assuming that A continues in its original direction, find the velocity of B, and explain why this case is impossible.

**(b)** Hence find the velocity of B after the collision. **[6]**

**11.** A bullet of mass 0.02 kg, moving horizontally at 400 m s⁻¹, embeds itself in a block of mass 1.98 kg at rest on a smooth horizontal surface.

**(a)** Find the speed of the block immediately after the impact.

**(b)** Find the kinetic energy lost in the impact.

**(c)** The block then moves onto a rough horizontal surface for which the coefficient of friction is 0.5. Find the distance the block travels on the rough surface before it stops. **[7]**

**12.** Particle A, of mass 0.3 kg, moving at 4 m s⁻¹, collides with particle B, of mass 0.2 kg, which is at rest. After the collision both particles move in A's original direction, and the speed of B is three times the speed of A.

**(a)** Find the speeds of A and B after the collision.

**(b)** Find the kinetic energy lost in the collision, giving your answer as an exact fraction. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Taking the original direction as positive, change = 0.15(−8) − 0.15(12) [1] = −3, so magnitude **3 kg m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* using 12 − 8 = 4 gives 0.6, which scores nothing. The velocity changes sign at the wall.

**2.** 3 × 4 + 0 = 4V [1], so **V = 3 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**

**3.** Taking A's original direction as positive: 0.2(6) + 0.4(−3) = 0.2(−2) + 0.4v [1]; 0 = −0.4 + 0.4v [1]; v = 1, so B moves at **1 m s⁻¹ in A's original direction** [1] **[3]**

**4.** 5m + 2(1) = 2m + 2(4) [1]; 3m = 6 [1]; **m = 2** [1] **[3]**

**5.** Taking the 0.5 kg particle's direction as positive: 0.5(3) + 0.7(−3) = 1.2V [1]; −0.6 = 1.2V, V = −0.5 [1]; speed **0.5 m s⁻¹, in the direction in which the 0.7 kg particle was moving** [1] **[3]**

**6.** 2000 × 3 = 3000V, so **V = 2 m s⁻¹** [1]. KE before = ½(2000)(9) = 9000 J; KE after = ½(3000)(4) = 6000 J [1]; loss **3000 J** [1] **[3]**

**7. (a)** 0.4(6) = 0.4v + 0.6(3) [1]; v = 1.5, so A moves at **1.5 m s⁻¹ in its original direction** [1]

**(b)** 0.6(3) + 0.3(−2) = 0.9V [1]; 1.2 = 0.9V [1]; **V = 4/3 ≈ 1.33 m s⁻¹ in the original direction of A** [1]

**(c)** A moves at 1.5 m s⁻¹ and the combined particle at 1.33 m s⁻¹, both in the same direction, with A behind [1]. A is faster, so **A does collide with the combined particle** [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* the conclusion needs the comparison of the two velocities; "yes" alone earns no credit.

**8. (a)** Friction = 0.4 × 0.5 × 10 = 2 N, deceleration = 4 m s⁻² [1]; v² = 8² − 2(4)(3) = 40 [1]; v = √40 = **6.32 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** 0.5√40 = 2V [1]; **V = 1.58 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** Deceleration of the 2 kg particle = 0.4 × 2 × 10 ÷ 2 = 4 m s⁻² [1]; 0 = V² − 2(4)s with V² = 40/16 = 2.5 [1]; **s = 0.3125 ≈ 0.313 m** [1] **[8]**

**9.** 4u = 5V, so V = 0.8u [1]. KE before = ½(4)u² = 2u² [1]. KE after = ½(5)(0.8u)² = 1.6u² [1]. 2u² − 1.6u² = 40, so 0.4u² = 40 [1]; **u = 10** [1] **[5]**

**10. (a)** Taking A's original direction as positive: 2(5) + 3(−2) = 2(1) + 3v [1]; 4 = 2 + 3v, v = 2/3 [1]. B would move at 0.667 m s⁻¹ in the positive direction while A, which is behind B, moves at 1 m s⁻¹ in the same direction [1]; A would have to pass through B, so this is **impossible** [1]

**(b)** A rebounds: 4 = 2(−1) + 3v [1]; **v = 2 m s⁻¹ in A's original direction** [1] **[6]**

**11. (a)** 0.02 × 400 = 2V [1]; **V = 4 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** KE before = ½(0.02)(400²) = 1600 J [1]; KE after = ½(2)(4²) = 16 J [1]; loss **1584 J** [1]

**(c)** Deceleration = 0.5 × 10 = 5 m s⁻² [1]; 0 = 16 − 2(5)s, so **s = 1.6 m** [1] **[7]**

**12. (a)** 0.3(4) = 0.3v + 0.2(3v) [1]; 1.2 = 0.9v [1]; A: **4/3 ≈ 1.33 m s⁻¹**, B: **4 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(b)** KE before = ½(0.3)(16) = 2.4 J; KE after = ½(0.3)(16/9) + ½(0.2)(16) = 4/15 + 1.6 = 28/15 J [1]; loss = 36/15 − 28/15 = **8/15 J** [1] **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- A velocity entered without its sign when two bodies move towards each other.
- A direction asked for but not stated in words.
- Kinetic energy assumed to be conserved, or a negative velocity squared to a negative energy.
- Rounded speeds carried between stages of a multi-stage problem, giving a final answer outside the accepted range.
- A further-collision question answered without comparing the two velocities.
