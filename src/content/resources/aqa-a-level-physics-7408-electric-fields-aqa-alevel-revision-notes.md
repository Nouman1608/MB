Condensed notes for sub-section 3.7.3 Electric fields of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2. Worked examples are in the [electric fields study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-electric-fields-aqa-alevel/); test yourself with the [electric fields practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-electric-fields-aqa-alevel-practice/). ## Equations

| Equation | Meaning | Units |
|---|---|---|
| F = (1/4πε₀)Q₁Q₂/r² | Coulomb's law, point charges in vacuum | N |
| E = F/Q | field strength definition | N C⁻¹ |
| E = V/d | uniform field between plates | V m⁻¹ |
| E = (1/4πε₀)Q/r² | radial field | N C⁻¹ |
| V = (1/4πε₀)Q/r | radial potential | V |
| ΔW = QΔV | work moving charge through ΔV | J |
| E = ΔV/Δr | field from potential gradient (magnitude) | V m⁻¹ |
| eV = ½mv² | particle accelerated from rest through V | J |

1/4πε₀ = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹ (permittivity of free space).

## 3.7.3.1 Coulomb's law: recall

- Force ∝ Q₁Q₂ and ∝ 1/r²; along the line joining the charges.
- Like charges repel, unlike attract; equal and opposite forces on each.
- **Air** can be treated as a vacuum.
- A charged **sphere** acts as a point charge at its centre (outside it).
- Between subatomic particles the electrostatic force is vastly larger than gravity (about 10³⁹ for proton and electron), so gravity is ignored.

## 3.7.3.2 Field strength: recall

- E = force per unit **positive** charge; a vector.
- Field lines: from + to −; never cross; density shows strength.
- **Uniform field**: E = V/d. Derivation: work Fd = QV, so F/Q = V/d.
- **Radial field**: E ∝ 1/r²; outward from +, inward to −.
- Mapping: conducting paper (2D), electrolytic tank (3D).

**Particle entering a uniform field at right angles**

| Direction | Force | Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Along plates | none | constant velocity |
| Across plates | QE, constant | uniform acceleration a = QE/m |
| Path between plates | — | parabola |
| After the plates | none | straight line |

- Positive particles deflect towards the negative plate; electrons towards the positive plate.
- Time between plates t = L/v; deflection y = ½at²; exit angle tan θ = at/v.
- After acceleration through V_a, deflection y = V_d L²/(4dV_a), independent of charge and mass.

## 3.7.3.3 Potential: recall

- **Absolute potential**: work done per unit positive charge bringing it from infinity; V = 0 at infinity.
- Positive near a positive charge, negative near a negative charge.
- **Potential difference**: work done per unit charge between two points; ΔW = QΔV.
- **Equipotentials**: perpendicular to field lines; no work along them; equally spaced planes in uniform fields; spheres spreading outwards in radial fields.
- Potential is a **scalar**: sum with signs.

**Graphs outside a point charge**

| Graph | Shape | Gives |
|---|---|---|
| E against r | ∝ 1/r² | area = ΔV |
| V against r | ∝ 1/r | gradient = E (magnitude) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Permittivity of free space, ε₀ | Constant in Coulomb's law for a vacuum |
| Electric field strength | Force per unit positive charge |
| Uniform field | Field of equal strength and direction everywhere |
| Absolute electric potential | Work per unit positive charge from infinity to the point |
| Electric potential difference | Work per unit charge moving between two points |
| Equipotential | Surface of constant potential |
| Electronvolt | Energy gained by an electron accelerated through 1 V, 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J |

## Exam traps

- r is centre to centre, in metres; nC is 10⁻⁹ C, μC is 10⁻⁶ C.
- E ∝ 1/r² but V ∝ 1/r.
- E = V/d uses the **separation**, not the plate length.
- Zero potential does not mean zero field (midpoint of +Q and −Q).
- MeV to J: × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³.
- The horizontal speed of a deflected particle does not change.

## Self-test

**1.** State Coulomb's law.
**2.** Define electric field strength.
**3.** Derive E = V/d (outline).
**4.** Plates 5.0 mm apart have 250 V across them. Find E.
**5.** Describe the path of an electron entering a uniform field at right angles.
**6.** Define absolute electric potential.
**7.** Find V at 0.30 m from a +6.0 nC point charge.
**8.** What does the gradient of a V–r graph give?
**9.** Work done moving 2.0 μC through 150 V?
**10.** What is the potential midway between +Q and −Q?
**11.** Why is gravity ignored when calculating forces between protons?
**12.** An electron is accelerated from rest through 500 V. Find its kinetic energy in joules.

## Self-test answers

**1.** The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
**2.** Force per unit positive charge, E = F/Q.
**3.** Work moving Q across the gap = Fd = QV, so E = F/Q = V/d.
**4.** E = 250/5.0 × 10⁻³ = 5.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹.
**5.** A parabola curving towards the positive plate; constant velocity along the plates, constant acceleration across them.
**6.** Work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small positive charge from infinity to the point.
**7.** V = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁹/0.30 = 180 V.
**8.** The magnitude of the electric field strength.
**9.** W = QΔV = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 150 = 3.0 × 10⁻⁴ J.
**10.** Zero.
**11.** The electrostatic force is about 10³⁶ times larger, so gravity is negligible.
**12.** E_k = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 500 = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁷ J.
