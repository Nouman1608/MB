This guide covers **Exercise 1** of Cambridge O Level Second Language Urdu 3248 Paper 1 Reading and Writing. The syllabus describes it as **Short-answer questions**: "candidates read a short text printed in the question paper and answer a series of questions that test their skimming and gist-reading skills and require short answers of a single word or phrase". It is worth **8 marks** and tests reading objectives **R1, R2, R3 and R4**. For the whole paper and its five exercises, see the [O Level Urdu Paper 1 study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-and-second-language/).

## Where Exercise 1 sits

Exercise 1 is the first of five compulsory exercises in a paper of 1 hour 45 minutes and 50 marks. It uses the **shortest text** on the paper. All questions and answers are in Urdu, and dictionaries may not be used. The syllabus says the text will be one of these types: **advertisement, brochure, leaflet, guide, report, manual, instructions, newspaper or magazine article**.

These are texts that give information, so the exercise rewards candidates who can find facts quickly. The subject content describes the same skills: "understand public notices and signs (including timetables and advertisements)" and "identify and select correct details from simple texts".

| Objective | In Exercise 1 |
|---|---|
| R1 identify and select relevant information | find the exact detail that answers the question |
| R2 understand ideas, opinions and attitudes | recognise what an organiser or writer recommends or feels |
| R3 show understanding of the connections between ideas | link a reason in one sentence to a result in another |
| R4 understand what is implied but not directly stated | work out gist or purpose when the text does not say it outright |

## Skimming and gist

**Skimming** means reading quickly to learn what the text is about and how it is organised. **Gist** is the main idea of the whole text, or of a paragraph. The exercise tests both, so use this order.

1. **Look at the title and any headings.** They name the topic (*سائنس میلہ*، *داخلہ*، *پارکنگ*).
2. **Read the first sentence of each paragraph.** This shows the plan of the text.
3. **Read the questions.** Underline the question word and the key noun.
4. **Return to the text** and read closely only the part that matches each question.
5. **Write the shortest answer that is complete.**

Do not translate the text in your head word for word. Look for numbers, names, times and key nouns.

## Reading the question word

The question word tells you what kind of answer is needed.

| Question word | Meaning | Answer is usually |
|---|---|---|
| کیا | what | a thing, action or idea |
| کب | when | a time, date or day |
| کہاں | where | a place |
| کون / کس | who / whom or which | a person, group or thing |
| کتنا / کتنے | how much / how many | a number or amount |
| کیوں | why | a reason |
| کیسے | how | a method |

A question beginning *کیوں* needs a **reason**, so a bare noun will not do. A question with *کتنے* needs a **number**, perhaps with its unit (دن، روپے، منٹ).

## Worked example

The leaflet below is original, written for this guide.

> **شہری سائنس میلہ**
>
> شہر کی بلدیہ کی طرف سے سالانہ سائنس میلہ اس سال بھی منعقد ہو رہا ہے۔ میلہ 12 اکتوبر سے 14 اکتوبر تک ٹاؤن ہال کے بڑے ہال میں لگے گا اور روزانہ صبح نو بجے سے شام پانچ بجے تک کھلا رہے گا۔
>
> میلے میں اسکولوں کے طلبہ اپنے بنائے ہوئے ماڈل پیش کریں گے۔ ان میں شمسی توانائی سے چلنے والی گاڑی، بارش کا پانی جمع کرنے کا نظام اور پلاسٹک کے کچرے سے بنے کھلونے خاص طور پر قابلِ ذکر ہیں۔ بچوں کے لیے ایک خصوصی گوشہ بھی ہے جہاں وہ آسان تجربات خود کر سکیں گے۔
>
> بالغوں کے لیے داخلہ پچاس روپے ہے، جبکہ اسکول کی وردی میں آنے والے طلبہ کے لیے داخلہ مفت ہے۔ ٹکٹ صرف مرکزی دروازے سے ملیں گے۔ گاڑیوں کے لیے پارکنگ ٹاؤن ہال کے پیچھے میدان میں ہے، لیکن وہاں جگہ کم ہے، اس لیے منتظمین مشورہ دیتے ہیں کہ لوگ بس یا رکشہ استعمال کریں۔
>
> آخری دن شام تین بجے بہترین ماڈل کو انعام دیا جائے گا۔

Questions, with model answers and the reasoning for each.

| Question | Model answer | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| میلہ کتنے دن جاری رہے گا؟ | تین دن | 12, 13 and 14 October: count both ends |
| میلہ کہاں لگے گا؟ | ٹاؤن ہال (کے بڑے ہال) میں | a place, taken from the first paragraph |
| میلہ روزانہ کب بند ہوتا ہے؟ | شام پانچ بجے | the closing time, not the opening time |
| شمسی توانائی سے کیا چلتا ہے؟ | گاڑی | one noun from the list in paragraph 2 |
| بچے خصوصی گوشے میں کیا کر سکتے ہیں؟ | آسان تجربات | what the corner offers |
| کن لوگوں کے لیے داخلہ مفت ہے؟ | اسکول کی وردی میں آنے والے طلبہ | the group, with its condition |
| منتظمین لوگوں کو کیا مشورہ دیتے ہیں؟ | بس یا رکشہ استعمال کرنا | advice, linked to the reason about parking |
| انعام کب دیا جائے گا؟ | آخری دن شام تین بجے | day and time both given in the text |

Two points deserve comment. First, the answer to the question about days needs **counting**: from 12 to 14 is three days, not two. Second, the answer about free entry needs the **whole group**: *طلبہ* alone is incomplete, because only students in school uniform are admitted free.

## How long should an answer be?

The syllabus asks for "short answers of a single word or phrase". That means:

- Do **not** write a full sentence. It wastes time and risks adding a wrong detail.
- Do **not** write one word if the idea needs a short phrase: *وردی میں طلبہ* is better than *طلبہ*.
- Take the words from the text where you can, but **only the part that answers**.
- If a question asks for **two** things, give two clear items.

| Question | Too long | Too short | Right |
|---|---|---|---|
| انعام کب دیا جائے گا؟ | انعام آخری دن شام تین بجے بہترین ماڈل کو دیا جائے گا | شام | آخری دن شام تین بجے |
| بچے کیا کر سکتے ہیں؟ | وہاں بچے آسان تجربات خود کر سکیں گے | کر سکیں گے | آسان تجربات خود کرنا |

## Gist and implied questions

One or two questions may ask about the **purpose** or overall message, not a single fact. Examples:

- *اس اشتہار کا مقصد کیا ہے؟* The answer is to invite people to the fair, which no single sentence states directly (R4).
- *منتظمین بس یا رکشہ کا مشورہ کیوں دیتے ہیں؟* The answer uses a reason (parking space is small), which requires linking two ideas (R3).

For these, read the heading and the first and last sentences again, and ask "why was this text written?"

## Common mistakes

- **Giving the opening time** when the question asks for closing, or the reverse.
- **Counting days wrongly** by forgetting that both the first and last day count.
- **Writing the whole sentence** from the text instead of the part that answers.
- **Adding extra information** in one answer that contradicts the text, so the mark is lost.
- **Leaving out the condition** in the answer, such as *وردی میں*.
- **Answering from memory** instead of returning to the text for the exact detail.
- **Misreading the question word**: answering *کہاں* with a time, or *کیوں* with a noun.

## Exam technique

- Spend only a few minutes on this exercise. It is worth 8 of the 50 marks, and the writing exercises carry more.
- Skim first, then read the questions, then locate the answers.
- Underline the key word in each question before you search.
- Check numbers and times twice.
- Write each answer in the space given, in neat Urdu script, without crossing out more than necessary.
