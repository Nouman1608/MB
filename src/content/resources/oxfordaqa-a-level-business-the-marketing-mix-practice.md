The current 9625 text (Version 3.1) uses seven Ps, adds the Boston Matrix, product life cycle, price discrimination and exhibitions, and does not print cross-price elasticity or the NPD stages in this section, so questions 3 and 5 follow 9725 only; see the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-the-marketing-mix/) for 9625. ## Questions

**1.** Redmayne Bakery makes several changes. State which element of the marketing mix each change affects.

**(a)** It signs a contract for a supermarket chain to stock its bread. **[1]**
**(b)** It runs a "buy one, get one free" week. **[1]**
**(c)** It launches a gluten-free loaf. **[1]**

**2.** Define the term "own label brand". **[2]**

**3.** Stainforth Audio gives a working prototype headset to 150 testers and records faults.

**(a)** Identify the stage of new product development shown. **[1]**
**(b)** State the stage that follows. **[1]**

**4.** Ullesthorpe Garden Tools raised the price of a strimmer from £50 to £56. Monthly sales fell from 6,000 to 5,100.

**(a)** Calculate the price elasticity of demand. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in monthly revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend whether Ullesthorpe should keep the price at £56, using your answers. **[3]**

**5.** Welbourne Digital sells streaming sticks. A rival raised its price by 10% and Welbourne's sales rose from 8,000 to 8,600 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the cross-price elasticity of demand. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the sign of your answer shows. **[1]**
**(c)** Welbourne cut the price of its games console by 8% and sales of its console accessories rose by 12%. Calculate the cross-price elasticity and state the relationship. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **two** influences on the price a business sets for a product. **[4]**

**7.** Cawthorne Software has developed a payroll package for small firms. It has few rivals, the package is distinctive and development was costly. Analyse why price skimming might suit the launch. **[6]**

**8.** Elmstone Joinery makes a chair at a unit cost of £84 and uses cost-plus pricing with a 45% mark-up.

**(a)** Calculate the selling price. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** disadvantage of cost based pricing. **[2]**

**9.** Linthwaite Bikes is a small firm with a limited budget that wants to reach 16 to 25 year olds. Analyse the advantages and disadvantages of using social media to promote its bikes. **[6]**

**10.** Kelderwick Dairy sells its cheese only to a wholesaler, who supplies shops. It is considering also selling online direct to customers. Analyse the advantages and disadvantages of adding this second channel. **[6]**

**11.** Redmayne Bakery plans a national promotion. Explain **one** way the plan affects its operations function and **one** way it affects its finance function. **[4]**

**12.** Stainforth Audio's Model R headset is in maturity. Sales were 40,000, 36,000 and 31,000 over the last three years. The price is £60 and unit cost is £38. Management considers two extension strategies. Option A is to cut the price by 10%, where research estimates PED at −1.4. Option B is a £200,000 advertising campaign at the current price. Evaluate which option Stainforth should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Place (distribution) [1]. (b) Promotion (a sales promotion) [1]. (c) Product [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the element of the mix, not just the action; a method such as sales promotion is more precise when the stem points to it.

**2.** A product sold under a retailer's own name [1], rather than the manufacturer's brand, often made for it by another producer [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns credit for the key idea and for a distinguishing detail; repeating the words "own label" without explaining them does not.

**3.** (a) Product testing [1]. (b) Launch [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Match the stage to the evidence in the stem; a working prototype signals product testing, not concept testing.

**4.** (a) %ΔP = 6 ÷ 50 = +12% [1]; %ΔQ = −900 ÷ 6,000 = −15% [1]; PED = −15 ÷ 12 = **−1.25** [1].
(b) Before 50 × 6,000 = £300,000; after 56 × 5,100 = £285,600 [1]; revenue falls by **£14,400** [1].
(c) Demand is elastic (size above 1), so the rise cut revenue [1]. Returning to £50 should lift revenue if elasticity holds [1]. However, 900 fewer units also cut costs and PED is only an estimate, so Ullesthorpe should compare profit before deciding; on revenue grounds, reverse the rise [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the percentage changes and the formula. A bare number gives less to reward, and a recommendation needs the revenue evidence to be used, not just the elasticity label.

**5.** (a) %ΔQ = 600 ÷ 8,000 = +7.5% [1]; XED = 7.5 ÷ 10 = **+0.75** [1].
(b) Positive: substitutes [1].
(c) XED = +12 ÷ −8 = **−1.5** [1]; complements [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign carries the meaning. Answers that drop the minus sign cannot show which relationship applies.

**6.** Any two, each with explanation. Level of demand: strong demand allows a higher price [1] because customers will still buy, whereas weak demand may force a lower one [1]. Competitors' prices: rivals' prices set a reference point [1], so a higher price risks losing sales unless the product is clearly different [1]. (Also acceptable: costs, marketing objectives, target market, PED, other mix elements.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming an influence earns little on its own. The second mark comes from saying what it does to the price.

**7.** Few rivals and a distinctive package mean buyers have little choice, so a high price is less likely to push them away [1]; this allows early adopters among small firms to pay a premium [1]. High development costs can be recovered quickly from early sales [1], which reduces financial risk [1]. The price can later be lowered as rivals enter or to reach firms that were not willing to pay the high price [1], widening the market [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis means developing a chain of reasoning for each point, using the facts given (few rivals, costly development) rather than describing skimming in general.

**8.** (a) 84 × 1.45 [1] = **£121.80** [1].
(b) It ignores demand and rivals' prices [1], so the price could deter buyers, or be lower than customers would have paid [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For a calculation show the mark-up step. For the disadvantage, add the consequence for the business.

**9.** Advantages (up to 4): social media can target age groups precisely [1], so spend reaches 16 to 25 year olds rather than a wide audience [1]; it is cheaper than much traditional advertising, which suits a small budget [1], and posts can be measured and adjusted [1]. Disadvantages (up to 4): negative comments spread quickly and the firm has little control [1]; posts need staff time and constant updates [1]. Reward a clear balance between sides. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each point to the case (small budget, young target market). Generic advantages that would fit any firm limit the marks available.

**10.** Advantages: selling direct keeps the margin otherwise taken by the wholesaler [1] and reaches customers beyond the wholesaler's shops [1]. Disadvantages: the dairy must handle delivery, cold storage and customer service, raising costs [1] and requiring new skills [1]. The wholesaler may feel threatened and cut orders [1], though spreading sales across channels reduces reliance on one customer [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both sides of multi-channel distribution; a one-sided list of benefits does not answer an "advantages and disadvantages" question.

**11.** Operations: a national promotion could increase orders beyond current capacity [1], so the bakery needs more output or stock to avoid late deliveries [1]. Finance: the promotion needs a budget and may reduce margins on discounted sales [1], so cash must be available to pay for ingredients and advertising before extra revenue arrives [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the marketing decision to the other function and give the consequence. Naming "operations" alone does not show an interrelationship.

**12.** Current revenue is 60 × 31,000 = £1,860,000 and contribution is (60 − 38) × 31,000 = £682,000 [1]. Option A: price £54, quantity up 14% to 35,340 [1]; revenue £1,908,360, a rise of £48,360 [1]. Unit contribution falls to £16, so total contribution is £565,440, down £116,560 [1]. Revenue rises, but profit falls, which weakens Option A [1]. Option B: a £200,000 cost needs about 9,091 extra units at £22 contribution (29% more than 31,000) to pay back [1], and advertising effects fade [1]. Sales have fallen about 14% in the last year, so a 29% rise looks hard [1]. Judgement: neither is clearly good. Option A is rejected because profit falls, while Option B is risky; the choice depends on whether advertising can lift sales by more than 29%, and Stainforth should test it on a small scale first or consider adapting the product [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers in the stem. Evaluation needs a judgement that says what it depends on, not only a list of pros and cons.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating PED but not saying what it means for revenue.
- Using the new figure instead of the original as the base for a percentage change.
- Losing the sign on XED.
- Confusing concept testing with product testing.
- Treating revenue rises as profit rises.
- Writing about pricing methods without using the case facts.
- Ignoring B2B or B2C context when the stem gives it.
- Giving only advantages when the command word asks for both sides.
