These notes condense section 2.6 (Communication) of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024, for SL and HL. Methods for a given situation are AO3; barriers are AO2. For the method-scoring example and an annotated model answer, read the [2.6 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-business-dp-business-management-ib-dp-business-2-6/). ## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Communication | Transfer of information from a sender to a receiver so that it is understood |
| Formal communication | Uses official channels, often the chain of command; usually recorded |
| Informal communication | Happens outside official channels, such as conversations and chat |
| Grapevine | The unofficial network through which news and rumour spread |
| Vertical communication | Moves up or down the hierarchy |
| Horizontal communication | Moves between people at the same level |
| One-way communication | No response expected, such as a notice |
| Two-way communication | Receiver can reply, so understanding can be checked |
| Feedback | The receiver's response showing whether the message was understood |
| Barrier to communication | Anything that stops a message being sent, received or understood as intended |

## Methods by medium

| Medium | Examples | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Written | Letters, reports, memos, procedures | Permanent record; detail | Slow; no instant feedback |
| Oral | Meetings, briefings, phone calls | Questions answered; tone shows feeling | Easily forgotten; no record unless minuted |
| Visual | Diagrams, charts, posters, demonstrations | Crosses language barriers | Limited detail |
| Digital | Email, intranet, messaging apps, video calls | Fast; reaches many places | Overload; access problems; misread tone |

## Choosing a method (AO3)

1. Take criteria from the case: urgency, sensitivity, complexity, audience size and location, need for a record, need for feedback, language and culture.
2. Judge at least two methods against those criteria.
3. Give a limitation of the preferred method and how to reduce it.
4. Conclude with a condition: what would change your choice.

**Rules of thumb (not rules):**

* Sensitive news such as redundancies: face-to-face first, written confirmation after.
* Legal or safety information: formal, written record plus a two-way check of understanding.
* Urgent, simple messages: digital alerts or text messages.
* Complex changes: meetings with questions, supported by written detail.
* Remote or international teams: video plus shared written summaries.

## Informal communication: balance

* Uses: early warning of problems, checking understanding, building cooperation between departments.
* Risks: rumour, distortion, no record, uneven reach.
* Best use: support formal messages when accuracy matters.

## Barriers and cures

| Barrier | Cure |
|---|---|
| Jargon | Plain language; check understanding |
| Language differences | Translation; visuals; demonstrations |
| Cultural differences | Awareness training; agreed norms for meetings |
| Long chain of command | Fewer levels; direct briefings |
| Information overload | Prioritise and label urgent messages |
| Distance and remote work | Video calls; regular check-ins |
| Time zones | Overlapping hours; shared written updates |
| Status or fear of speaking up | Anonymous feedback; open-door policy |
| Low trust | Consistent and honest messages over time |
| Wrong medium | Match medium to message sensitivity and complexity |
| Technology failure or no access | Back-up channels; make sure all staff have access |

## Worked mini-example

Garroway Clinics (invented) sends all policy updates by email. Cleaning and catering staff, 35 of its 140 employees, have no work email. That is 25% of staff missing every update. Barrier: no access to the channel. Effect: these staff break rules they never received, and morale falls. Cure: printed updates in staff rooms and short briefings by supervisors, with a sign-off sheet. Cost: supervisor time each month.

## Exam traps

* Listing methods without criteria from the case.
* Writing that informal communication is always bad.
* Naming a barrier without its effect in context.
* Using "communication" as a cure for a communication barrier with no detail.
* Ignoring the need for a record in safety or legal situations.
* Forgetting to state a condition in an AO3 judgement.

## Quick self-test

1. State the two content points of section 2.6 and their depth.
2. Distinguish between formal and informal communication.
3. What is the grapevine?
4. Give one benefit of two-way communication.
5. Which medium is most useful for crossing a language barrier, and why?
6. Give one reason a long chain of command is a barrier.
7. Why should redundancy news usually be given face-to-face first?
8. Explain information overload.
9. A firm has 250 staff and 60 have no access to the intranet. What percentage of staff miss intranet messages?
10. Suggest one cure for a time zone barrier.
11. Give one use of informal communication for managers.
12. What must an AO3 answer on methods include?

### Answers

1. Formal and informal methods of communication for an organization in a given situation (AO3); barriers to communication (AO2).
2. Formal uses official channels and is usually recorded; informal happens outside official channels, such as conversations, and has no record.
3. The unofficial network through which news and rumour spread between employees.
4. The sender can check understanding and answer questions.
5. Visual, such as diagrams or demonstrations, because it does not depend on reading or speaking the same language.
6. Messages pass through many people, so they are delayed or altered.
7. It is sensitive, so staff need the chance to ask questions and see respect shown; written confirmation can follow.
8. Receiving so many messages that important ones are missed or ignored.
9. 60 ÷ 250 × 100 = 24%.
10. Overlapping working hours for live discussion, or shared written updates with confirmation.
11. Early warning of problems or rumours; checking whether a formal message was understood.
12. Criteria from the case, at least two methods weighed, a limitation, and a justified judgement with a condition.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is section 2.6 Communication SL or HL?**
Section 2.6 applies to both SL and HL. Section 2.7, Industrial/employee relations, is the HL only section that follows it.

**Is informal communication bad for a business?**
No. It can give managers early warning of problems and reinforce formal messages, but it risks rumour and has no record, so it should support formal channels when accuracy matters.
