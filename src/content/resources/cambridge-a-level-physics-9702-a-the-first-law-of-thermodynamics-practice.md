Use ΔU = q + W, where q is the energy transferred to the system by heating and W is the work done on the system.

## Section A

**1.** State the first law of thermodynamics, explaining the meaning of each term. **[3]**

**2.** A gas is contained in a cylinder by a piston of area A. Show that the work done by the gas when it expands at constant pressure p is pΔV, where ΔV is the increase in volume. **[3]**

**3.** A gas is compressed at a constant pressure of 2.5 × 10⁵ Pa from a volume of 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.8 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate the work done on the gas. **[2]**

**4.** A gas in a sealed rigid container is heated, receiving 420 J of energy. State the work done on the gas and calculate its change in internal energy. **[2]**

**5.** A gas expands so rapidly that no energy is transferred to or from it by heating. It does 85 J of work on its surroundings.

**(a)** Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas.

**(b)** State and explain what happens to the temperature of the gas. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why the change in internal energy of a gas taken once round a complete cycle is zero. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A gas is held at a constant pressure of 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa. It is heated, receiving 800 J, and its volume increases from 1.5 × 10⁻³ m³ to 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the change in internal energy of the gas. **[2]**

**(c)** The gas is ideal. State what happens to its temperature. **[1]**

**8.** A fixed mass of ideal gas is taken round the cycle A → B → C → D → A:

- A (1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³) to B (1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, 5.0 × 10⁻³ m³)
- B to C (0.50 × 10⁵ Pa, 5.0 × 10⁻³ m³)
- C to D (0.50 × 10⁵ Pa, 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³)
- D back to A

The energy transferred to the gas by heating in each stage is: A → B, +1125 J; B → C, −750 J; C → D, −375 J; D → A, +300 J.

**(a)** Calculate the work done on the gas in each of the four stages. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the change in internal energy in each stage. **[2]**

**(c)** Show that your values in (b) are consistent with the first law applied to the whole cycle, and explain why they must be. **[2]**

**(d)** State the net work done by the gas in one cycle. **[1]**

**9.** 0.050 kg of water at 100 °C is boiled at a constant atmospheric pressure of 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa to form steam at 100 °C.
(specific latent heat of vaporisation = 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹; density of water = 1000 kg m⁻³; density of steam at 100 °C = 0.60 kg m⁻³)

**(a)** Calculate the energy supplied by heating. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the work done by the steam in pushing back the atmosphere. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the increase in internal energy. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain, in terms of molecules, what the increase in internal energy represents. **[2]**

**10.** The outlet of a bicycle pump is sealed. The handle is pushed in quickly, doing 24 J of work on the air inside.

**(a)** Assuming no energy is transferred by heating during the stroke, state the change in internal energy of the air. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the temperature of the air rises. **[2]**

**(c)** The handle is then held still and the air cools back to its original temperature. State the values of W, ΔU and q for this cooling stage. **[2]**

**11.** A student writes: "The gas absorbs 600 J of energy by heating and does 250 J of work, so ΔU = 600 + 250 = 850 J." Identify the error and calculate the correct value of ΔU. **[3]**

**12.** A gas is cooled while it is compressed at a constant pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Its volume decreases by 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ and its internal energy decreases by 300 J. Calculate the energy transferred by heating, and state its direction. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** ΔU = q + W [1]; ΔU is the **increase in internal energy** of the system [1]; q is the energy transferred **to** the system by heating and W is the work done **on** the system [1].

**2.** Force on piston F = pA [1]. Work done = FΔx = pAΔx, where Δx is the distance the piston moves [1]. AΔx is the increase in volume ΔV, so W = pΔV [1].

**3.** W = pΔV = 2.5 × 10⁵ × (3.0 − 1.8) × 10⁻³ [1] = **300 J** (done on the gas, so W = +300 J) [1].

**4.** No volume change, so **W = 0** [1]; ΔU = q = **+420 J** [1].

**5. (a)** q = 0 and W = −85 J, so ΔU = **−85 J** [1].

**(b)** The internal energy falls, so the mean kinetic energy of the molecules falls [1]; the temperature **falls** [1].

**6.** Internal energy is a **function of state** [1]; after a complete cycle the gas is back in its original state, so it has its original internal energy [1].

**7. (a)** pΔV = 1.2 × 10⁵ × 2.5 × 10⁻³ [1] = **300 J** [1].

**(b)** W = −300 J, so ΔU = 800 + (−300) [1] = **+500 J** [1].

**(c)** It **rises** (ΔU is positive) [1].

**8. (a)** A → B: expansion at 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa by 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³, W = **−450 J** [1]. B → C and D → A: no volume change, W = **0** [1]. C → D: compression at 0.50 × 10⁵ Pa by 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³, W = **+150 J** [1].

**(b)** ΔU = q + W: A → B, 1125 − 450 = **+675 J**; B → C, **−750 J** [1]; C → D, −375 + 150 = **−225 J**; D → A, **+300 J** [1].

**(c)** Sum of ΔU = 675 − 750 − 225 + 300 = **0** [1]. The gas returns to state A, and internal energy is a function of state [1].

**(d)** Net W on gas = −450 + 150 = −300 J, so net work done **by** the gas = **300 J** [1].

**9. (a)** q = mL = 0.050 × 2.26 × 10⁶ = **1.13 × 10⁵ J** [1].

**(b)** V_steam = 0.050 ÷ 0.60 = 0.0833 m³; V_water = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³; ΔV = 0.0833 m³ [1]. pΔV = 1.01 × 10⁵ × 0.0833 = **8.4 × 10³ J** [1].

**(c)** W = −8.4 × 10³ J, so ΔU = 1.13 × 10⁵ − 8.4 × 10³ [1] = **1.05 × 10⁵ J** [1].

**(d)** The temperature is constant, so the mean kinetic energy of the molecules is unchanged [1]; the increase is in the **potential energy** of the molecules, which are separated against their attractive forces [1].

**10. (a)** ΔU = q + W = 0 + 24 = **+24 J** [1].

**(b)** Internal energy increases [1]; for the air this means the mean (random) kinetic energy of the molecules increases, so the temperature rises [1].

**(c)** **W = 0** (volume constant) [1]; **ΔU = −24 J** (back to the original temperature, so the original internal energy) and therefore **q = −24 J**, energy lost by heating [1].

**11.** The gas does work, so the work done **on** the gas is **negative**: W = −250 J [1]. ΔU = 600 + (−250) [1] = **+350 J** [1].

**12.** W = +pΔV = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = **+200 J** (work done on the gas) [1]. q = ΔU − W = −300 − 200 [1] = **−500 J**: 500 J is transferred **from the gas** to the surroundings by heating [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the work done by the gas instead of subtracting it.
- Giving W a non-zero value for a stage with no volume change.
- Forgetting to convert cm³ or dm³ to m³.
- Leaving out the direction of energy transfer when asked for q.
- Assuming q = 0 means no temperature change, or that constant temperature means no energy transfer.
- Not checking that ΔU sums to zero round a cycle.
