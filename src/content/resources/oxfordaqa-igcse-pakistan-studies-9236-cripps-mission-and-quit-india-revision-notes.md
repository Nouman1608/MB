These notes condense the opening content points of key development 3.1.1.4 of the 9236
specification: "the Second World War and its effects on the Indian subcontinent, including the 'Day
of Deliverance'" and "the Quit India movement – aims and impact; the 1942 Cripps mission". The full
explanation is in the
[Cripps Mission and Quit India study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-cripps-mission-and-quit-india/);
the wider Pakistan movement is in the

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 3 Sep 1939 | Linlithgow declares India at war without consulting Indian leaders |
| Oct–Nov 1939 | Congress provincial ministries resign |
| 22 Dec 1939 | Day of Deliverance from Congress rule |
| Mar 1940 | Lahore Resolution |
| Aug 1940 | August Offer |
| Feb 1942 | Fall of Singapore to Japan |
| Mar 1942 | Cripps Mission |
| 8 Aug 1942 | Quit India resolution in Bombay |
| 9 Aug 1942 | Gandhi and Congress leaders arrested |

## The August Offer (1940): three points

- More Indians on the Viceroy's Executive Council.
- A constitution-making body after the war.
- No constitution without the agreement of large minorities: in effect a League veto.

## The Cripps proposals at a glance

| Proposal | Congress view | League view |
|---|---|---|
| Dominion status after the war | Too slow; wanted power now | Acceptable in principle |
| Post-war constitution-making body | Accepted the idea | Feared Hindu majority |
| Provinces may stay out of the Union | Opposed: could break up India | Welcome step, but Pakistan not clearly conceded |
| Britain keeps defence during the war | Rejected | Not the main issue |

**Result:** both rejected it. **Importance:** first British plan to let provinces opt out, which
Muslims could later cite as accepting the principle of Pakistan.

## Quit India in brief

- **Aims:** immediate British withdrawal; Indian government to decide India's role in the war;
  mass struggle if refused; "do or die".
- **Events:** leaders arrested within hours; attacks on railways, telegraph lines and police
  stations; crushed by troops and mass arrests.
- **League:** refused to join; answered with "Divide and Quit".

## Impact for the Pakistan movement

| Congress | Muslim League |
|---|---|
| Leaders imprisoned for most of the war | Free to organise and recruit |
| Unable to negotiate | Formed ministries in several Muslim-majority provinces |
| Seen by Britain as a wartime threat | Seen by Britain as more co-operative |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Day of Deliverance | 22 December 1939, marking the end of Congress provincial rule |
| August Offer | Linlithgow's 1940 offer, including the minority-consent pledge |
| Dominion status | Self-government within the Commonwealth, with the right to leave |
| Opt-out (non-accession) | A province's right to stay out of a new Indian Union |
| Quit India | Congress demand of August 1942 for immediate British withdrawal |
| "Do or die" | Gandhi's call to struggle in the Quit India campaign |
| "Divide and Quit" | League reply to Quit India: partition before British departure |
| Executive Council | The Viceroy's council of ministers |

## Key people

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Lord Linlithgow | Viceroy 1936-43; declared war for India; August Offer |
| Muhammad Ali Jinnah | Called the Day of Deliverance; led League rejection of Cripps |
| Sir Stafford Cripps | Labour minister who brought the 1942 proposals |
| Gandhi | Rejected Cripps; launched Quit India with "do or die" |
| A. K. Fazlul Haq | Moved the Lahore Resolution in 1940 |
| Lord Wavell | Viceroy from 1943; called the Simla Conference in 1945 |

## Linking forward

- **1944:** Gandhi released; Gandhi-Jinnah talks fail.
- **1945:** Simla Conference breaks down over who nominates Muslim members.
- **1945-46:** elections test the League's wartime growth.

## Exam traps

- Mixing up Cripps (1942) with the Cabinet Mission (1946).
- Saying the League took part in Quit India.
- Putting the Day of Deliverance in 1940.
- Calling Quit India wholly peaceful.
- Explaining Cripps's failure from only one party's view.

## Self-test

1. Who declared India at war in September 1939?
2. Why did the Congress ministries resign in 1939?
3. On what date was the Day of Deliverance observed?
4. Which point of the August Offer most helped the League?
5. Why did Britain send Cripps in March 1942?
6. State two Cripps proposals.
7. Why did Congress reject the Cripps proposals?
8. Why did the League reject them?
9. When and where was the Quit India resolution passed?
10. What happened to Congress leaders after the resolution?
11. What slogan did the League use in reply to Quit India?
12. Give two ways Quit India helped the League.

## Answers

1. The Viceroy, Lord Linlithgow.
2. India was declared at war without consultation, and Britain refused Congress's demand for
   independence and immediate power.
3. 22 December 1939.
4. The pledge that no constitution would be adopted without the agreement of large minorities.
5. Japan had taken Singapore and was advancing through Burma; Britain needed Indian support, and the
   United States pressed for a settlement.
6. Any two: dominion status after the war; a post-war constitution-making body; provinces could stay
   out of the Union; Britain to keep defence during the war.
7. It wanted real power immediately, including defence, and opposed the provincial opt-out.
8. Pakistan was not clearly conceded, and the opt-out method could go against Muslims where their
   majority was narrow.
9. 8 August 1942, in Bombay.
10. They were arrested and imprisoned, most for the rest of the war.
11. "Divide and Quit".
12. Any two: Congress leaders were absent; the League recruited freely; it formed provincial
    ministries; Britain saw it as more co-operative.
