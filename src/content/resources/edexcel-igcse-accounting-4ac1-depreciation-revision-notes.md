## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain the causes of depreciation.
- (b) Distinguish between straight line and reducing balance.
- (c) Calculate and record depreciation in the books of account.
- (d) Calculate and record profit or loss on disposal of non-current assets.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | The part of a non-current asset's cost used up in a period, charged as an expense |
| Cost | Price plus all costs of making the asset ready for use |
| Residual value | Amount expected from the asset at the end of its useful life |
| Useful life | Number of periods the business expects to use the asset |
| Accumulated depreciation | Total depreciation charged on an asset to date |
| Carrying value | Cost less accumulated depreciation (also called net book value) |
| Provision for depreciation | Ledger account holding accumulated depreciation; credit balance |
| Disposal account | Account used to calculate profit or loss when an asset is sold |

## Causes: four to learn

- Wear and tear (use): machinery, vehicles.
- Obsolescence (outdated): computers, technology.
- Passage of time (fixed life): leases.
- Inadequacy (no longer big enough): equipment in a growing business.

## The two methods

| | Straight line | Reducing balance |
|---|---|---|
| Formula | (Cost − residual value) ÷ useful life | Rate × opening carrying value |
| Pattern | Equal charge each year | Falling charge each year |
| Residual value | Deducted first | Not deducted |
| Best for | Even use over life | Assets losing value fast early; rising repairs |

Quick figures: cost 12,000, residual 2,000, life 5 years: straight line 2,000 a year. Reducing balance 25% on 12,000: Year 1 3,000, Year 2 2,250, Year 3 1,687.50.

## Part-year policies

- Full year in year of purchase, none in year of disposal.
- Or month by month: cost × rate × months ÷ 12.

Example: 7,200 at 15% on cost, bought 1 August, year end 31 December: 7,200 × 15% × 5/12 = 450.

## Recording each year

| Entry | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Year's charge | Depreciation expense | Provision for depreciation |
| Year-end transfer | Income statement | Depreciation expense |

The asset account stays at cost. Statement of financial position: cost, less accumulated depreciation, equals carrying value.

## Recording a disposal

| Step | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Transfer cost | Disposal | Asset at cost |
| 2 Transfer that asset's accumulated depreciation | Provision for depreciation | Disposal |
| 3 Proceeds | Bank | Disposal |
| 4a Profit (debit side of disposal is short) | Disposal | Income statement |
| 4b Loss (credit side of disposal is short) | Income statement | Disposal |

Check: proceeds − carrying value = profit (positive) or loss (negative).

Mini-example: cost 6,000, accumulated depreciation 3,840, sold for 2,500. Carrying value 2,160; profit 340.

## Exam traps

- Reducing balance on cost every year: it is on the opening carrying value.
- Deducting residual value under reducing balance.
- Crediting the asset account with depreciation.
- Transferring the whole provision to disposal, not just the sold asset's share.
- Charging depreciation in the year of sale when the policy forbids it.
- Treating depreciation as a cash fund for replacement.
- Mixing up which side the profit or loss on disposal goes on: a profit is a debit in the disposal account and a credit (income) in the income statement.

## Quick self-test

1. Name four causes of depreciation.
2. A lease of 30,000 runs for 10 years. Which cause applies, and what is the annual straight line charge?
3. Machinery cost 20,000 with residual value 2,000 and a 6-year life. Calculate straight line depreciation.
4. The same machinery at 30% reducing balance: Year 1 and Year 2 charges?
5. Why does a reducing balance carrying value never reach nil?
6. Give the double entry for the year's depreciation charge.
7. Equipment cost 4,800, bought 1 October, year end 31 December, 20% on cost, month by month. Calculate the first year's charge.
8. An asset costing 9,000 with accumulated depreciation of 5,400 is sold for 3,100. Profit or loss?
9. Which account is credited with the sale proceeds of an asset?
10. Which method gives higher profit in Year 1, all else equal, if the reducing balance rate produces a larger first charge?
11. What three columns are shown for each class of non-current asset in the statement of financial position?
12. Does charging depreciation reduce the cash balance?

### Answers

1. Wear and tear, obsolescence, passage of time, inadequacy.
2. Passage of time; 30,000 ÷ 10 = 3,000 a year.
3. (20,000 − 2,000) ÷ 6 = 3,000.
4. Year 1: 20,000 × 30% = 6,000. Year 2: 14,000 × 30% = 4,200.
5. The percentage is always taken from a remaining balance, so some value always remains.
6. Debit depreciation (expense), credit provision for depreciation.
7. 4,800 × 20% × 3/12 = 240.
8. Carrying value 3,600; proceeds 3,100; loss of 500.
9. The disposal account (debit bank).
10. Straight line, because its Year 1 charge is smaller.
11. Cost, accumulated depreciation, carrying value.
12. No. It is a non-cash expense; cash left when the asset was bought.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between depreciation and accumulated depreciation?**
Depreciation is the charge for one period; accumulated depreciation is the total charged since the asset was bought, held in the provision account.

**Where does a profit on disposal go?**
It is credited to the income statement as income, after being calculated as the balancing figure in the disposal account.
