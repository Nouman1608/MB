**Condensed notes on the Section B option "Hitler's foreign policy and the origins of the Second World War, 1933-41".** The specification names two parts: "Increasing international tensions, 1933-38" and "Appeasement, the outbreak of war and the global conflict 1938-41". The full explanation is in the [Hitler's foreign policy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-hitlers-foreign-policy-second-world-war/); the question types are in the [Paper 1 Section B revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-b-revision-notes/).

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| October 1933 | Germany leaves the Disarmament Conference and League of Nations |
| January 1934 | German-Polish Non-Aggression Pact |
| January 1935 | Saar returns to Germany after a plebiscite |
| March 1935 | Conscription announced; Luftwaffe revealed |
| April 1935 | Stresa Front (Britain, France, Italy) |
| June 1935 | Anglo-German Naval Agreement |
| October 1935 | Italy invades Abyssinia |
| March 1936 | Rhineland remilitarised |
| 1936 | Rome-Berlin Axis; Anti-Comintern Pact; Spanish Civil War begins |
| March 1938 | Anschluss with Austria |
| September 1938 | Munich Agreement; Sudetenland to Germany |
| March 1939 | Germany occupies the rest of Czechoslovakia |
| 31 March 1939 | British guarantee to Poland |
| May 1939 | Pact of Steel |
| 23 August 1939 | Nazi-Soviet Pact |
| 1 September 1939 | Germany invades Poland |
| 3 September 1939 | Britain and France declare war |
| 22 June 1941 | Germany invades the USSR (Barbarossa) |
| 7 December 1941 | Japan attacks Pearl Harbor |

## Hitler's aims

- Overturn the Treaty of Versailles.
- Rearm Germany.
- Unite German-speaking peoples (Austria, Sudetenland).
- **Lebensraum** in the east.
- Destroy communism.

## 1933-35: tensions develop

| Event | What happened | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving the League and Disarmament Conference | Hitler claimed unequal treatment | Free to rearm; League shown to be weak |
| Polish Pact | Ten-year pact | Eased fears; weakened French links with Poland |
| Saar | About 90 per cent vote to return | Legal success; propaganda |
| Conscription | Breaks Versailles | Stresa Front |
| Naval Agreement | Germany allowed 35 per cent of Britain's navy | Britain broke the Stresa Front; accepted treaty breaking |

## 1936-38: escalation

| Event | Detail |
|---|---|
| Rhineland (March 1936) | German troops enter; France will not act alone; Britain does not oppose |
| Rome-Berlin Axis | Friendship between Italy and Germany after Abyssinia |
| Anti-Comintern Pact | Germany and Japan against communism; Italy joins in 1937 |
| Spanish Civil War | Condor Legion helps Franco; Guernica; non-intervention by Britain and France |
| Anschluss | Troops enter Austria, 12 March 1938; Britain and France only protest |

## Appeasement

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Memory of the First World War | Encouraged Hitler to demand more |
| Fear of bombing | Abandoned small states |
| Rearmament not complete | Wasted the chance to ally with the USSR |
| Versailles seen as harsh | Churchill's criticism |
| Fear of communism | Hitler could not be trusted |
| Buys time | Weakened Czechoslovakia's defences |

## Munich, 1938

- Sudetenland: three million Germans; Henlein.
- Chamberlain met Hitler at Berchtesgaden and Godesberg.
- Munich (29-30 September): Britain, France, Germany and Italy; Czechoslovakia and the USSR not invited.
- Hitler promised no more territorial demands.

## The ending of appeasement and the outbreak

- **March 1939:** the rest of Czechoslovakia taken; Munich broken.
- **Guarantee to Poland,** 31 March 1939; conscription in Britain.
- **Pact of Steel,** May 1939.
- **Nazi-Soviet Pact,** 23 August 1939; secret protocol to divide Poland.
- **Invasion of Poland,** 1 September; war declared 3 September.

## The global conflict

- 1939-40: Poland, Denmark, Norway, the Low Countries and France fall.
- Battle of Britain.
- North Africa; Japan at war with China since 1937.
- **Barbarossa,** June 1941.
- **Pearl Harbor,** December 1941: the USA enters the war.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lebensraum | "Living space" in the east for Germans |
| Anschluss | The union of Germany and Austria |
| Appeasement | Giving way to demands to avoid war |
| Plebiscite | A vote by the people on a single question |
| Stresa Front | 1935 agreement between Britain, France and Italy to oppose treaty breaking |
| Axis | The partnership between Germany and Italy, later with Japan |
| Sudetenland | The German-speaking border region of Czechoslovakia |
| Nazi-Soviet Pact | The 1939 non-aggression pact between Germany and the USSR |
| Guarantee | A promise to defend a state if it is attacked |

## Exam traps

- Rhineland is 1936; Anschluss is 1938.
- Czechoslovakia was not invited to Munich.
- The Stresa Front (1935) is not the Axis (1936).
- Appeasement had reasons; do not describe it as simple weakness.
- The Nazi-Soviet Pact had secret terms.
- The war began over Poland, not Czechoslovakia.

## Self-test

1. Why did Hitler leave the Disarmament Conference?
2. What was the result of the Saar plebiscite?
3. What was the Stresa Front?
4. Why did the Anglo-German Naval Agreement damage the Stresa Front?
5. Why was the Rhineland remilitarisation a risk for Hitler?
6. Which two countries signed the Anti-Comintern Pact in 1936?
7. What happened at Guernica?
8. Give two reasons for appeasement.
9. Which states met at Munich?
10. What happened in March 1939?
11. Why did the Nazi-Soviet Pact matter?
12. Which events in 1941 made the war global?

## Answers

1. He claimed that other nations were not disarming and that Germany was treated unequally; leaving let him rearm.
2. About 90 per cent voted to return to Germany.
3. A 1935 agreement between Britain, France and Italy to oppose further breaking of treaties.
4. Britain made the deal without consulting its partners, and implied that it accepted German rearmament.
5. Germany's army was weak and could have been defeated if France had resisted.
6. Germany and Japan.
7. German aircraft bombed the Basque town, with great loss of civilian life, during the Spanish Civil War.
8. Any two: fear of war and bombing; weak rearmament; belief that Versailles was harsh; fear of communism; the Dominions' attitude.
9. Britain, France, Germany and Italy.
10. Germany occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia, breaking the Munich Agreement.
11. It removed the threat of a war on two fronts, and it secretly divided Poland.
12. Germany's invasion of the USSR (June) and Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor (December).
