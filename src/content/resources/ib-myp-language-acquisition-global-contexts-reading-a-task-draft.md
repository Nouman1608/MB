# Global contexts: reading a task

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use.

Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/). The reviewed [global contexts study guide](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-global-contexts-and-topics/) shows how to build word banks. This page shows how to use a context to *read* a task.

## What the brief says

You explore key and related concepts through six global contexts: identities and relationships; orientation in space and time; personal and cultural expression; scientific and technical innovation; globalization and sustainability; fairness and development. Criterion C asks you to interact on topics of personal, local and global interest and significance.

## A context is a lens

A topic is what you talk about. A context is the angle. The example topics below are Marlbridge suggestions, not an IB list.

| Context | Angle it gives | Example topic angle |
|---|---|---|
| Identities and relationships | Who people are and how they relate | Friendship in a new school |
| Orientation in space and time | Places, journeys, change over time | My street fifty years ago |
| Personal and cultural expression | How people express themselves | A local festival |
| Scientific and technical innovation | How inventions change life | Phones in the classroom |
| Globalization and sustainability | Links between places and the environment | Where our clothes come from |
| Fairness and development | Rights, access, community action | Access to libraries |

## Worked example: one topic, two contexts

Topic: **a school library**.
- Through *fairness and development*: who can use it, and who cannot? Question: should opening hours be longer for students who live far away?
- Through *scientific and technical innovation*: how do e-books change it? Question: will printed books disappear?
The words, examples and opinions you need change with the lens.

## Worked example: reading a speaking prompt

Prompt (original): "Talk about how your family spends holidays." Steps: (1) topic: holidays; (2) most natural context: orientation in space and time (journeys) or personal and cultural expression (traditions); (3) pick personal, local and global levels: your family, your region, tourism in general; (4) prepare one opinion and one reason.

## Common errors

- Naming a context but not changing the content.
- Staying only personal; criterion C also expects local and global interest.
- Treating the suggested topics as an official list.

## Check your understanding

1. Name the six contexts. 2. Which context fits a task on recycling? *Globalization and sustainability.* 3. What is the difference between a topic and a context? *A topic is the subject; a context is the lens.*
