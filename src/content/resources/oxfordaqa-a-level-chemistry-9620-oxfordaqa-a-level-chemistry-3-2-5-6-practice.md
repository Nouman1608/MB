## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which is an example of homogeneous catalysis?

A: Fe in the Haber process

B: V₂O₅ in the Contact process

C: Fe²⁺(aq) in the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻

D: Platinum in a catalytic converter **[1]**

**2.** The catalyst in a catalytic converter is a thin coating of metal on a ceramic honeycomb. Explain why the metal is used in this form. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why using leaded fuel in a car with a catalytic converter makes the converter less effective. **[2]**

**4.** Give the oxidation state of vanadium in V₂O₅ and in V₂O₄. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why Mn²⁺ is described as an autocatalyst in the reaction between MnO₄⁻ and C₂O₄²⁻. **[1]**

**6.** Identify the intermediate species when Fe²⁺ ions catalyse the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Vanadium(V) oxide catalyses a key step in the Contact process.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction it catalyses. **[1]**

**(b)** Write two equations to show how V₂O₅ acts as a catalyst. **[2]**

**(c)** V₂O₅ reacts with sulfur dioxide. Explain why it is still described as a catalyst. **[1]**

**(d)** State, with a reason, whether V₂O₅ acts as a heterogeneous or a homogeneous catalyst. **[1]**

**8.** Two hypothetical ion pairs have these electrode potentials (values invented for this question):

| Half-equation | E⦵ / V |
|---|---|
| S₂O₈²⁻ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2SO₄²⁻ | +2.01 |
| X³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ X²⁺ (hypothetical) | +1.20 |
| I₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2I⁻ | +0.54 |
| Y³⁺ + e⁻ ⇌ Y²⁺ (hypothetical) | +0.20 |

**(a)** Use E⦵cell values to show that X²⁺ ions could catalyse the reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why Y²⁺ ions could not catalyse this reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a catalysed route of this kind is faster than the direct reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻. **[1]**

**9.** In the Haber process, the iron catalyst is gradually poisoned by sulfur compounds in the hydrogen feed.

**(a)** Explain how the sulfur compounds make the catalyst less efficient. **[2]**

**(b)** Give two ways in which poisoning increases the cost of producing ammonia. **[2]**

**10.** A student mixes acidified KMnO₄ with excess sodium ethanedioate at constant temperature and records [MnO₄⁻] with a colorimeter.

| Time / s | 0 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 | 250 | 300 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [MnO₄⁻] / 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ | 4.00 | 3.90 | 3.60 | 2.60 | 1.20 | 0.40 | 0.10 |

**(a)** Describe and explain how the rate of reaction changes during the experiment, using the data. **[3]**

**(b)** The experiment is repeated with a little MnSO₄ added at the start. Describe how the results would differ. **[1]**

**(c)** Write two equations to show how Mn²⁺ catalyses the reaction. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about enthalpy profiles.

**(a)** Describe how the enthalpy profile for the Fe²⁺-catalysed reaction between S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ differs from that of the uncatalysed reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the catalyst does not change the overall enthalpy change of the reaction. **[1]**

**12.** Compare heterogeneous and homogeneous catalysis by transition metals and their compounds. Refer to how each type works, to the importance of variable oxidation states, and to practical issues for industry. Use named examples. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** C [1]

**2.** A thin layer on a support gives a very large surface area, so there are many active sites [1]; only a small mass of the expensive metal is needed, which minimises the cost [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.** Lead compounds are adsorbed onto the active sites and block them (poison the catalyst) [1]; fewer active sites remain for the exhaust gases, so less CO, NOₓ and hydrocarbon is converted [1]

**Total: 2**

**4.** V₂O₅: +5 [1]; V₂O₄: +4 [1]

**Total: 2**

**5.** Mn²⁺ is a product of the reaction, and it catalyses the reaction [1]

**6.** Fe³⁺ [1]

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ (or SO₂ + ½O₂ → SO₃) [1]

**(b)** V₂O₅ + SO₂ → V₂O₄ + SO₃ [1]; V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅ [1]

**(c)** It is regenerated in the second step, so it is not used up overall [1]

**(d)** Heterogeneous, because it is a solid and the reactants are gases (different phases) [1]

**Total: 5**

**8.**

**(a)** S₂O₈²⁻ oxidises X²⁺: E⦵cell = 2.01 − 1.20 = +0.81 V, feasible [1]; X³⁺ oxidises I⁻: E⦵cell = 1.20 − 0.54 = +0.66 V, feasible [1]

**(b)** S₂O₈²⁻ would oxidise Y²⁺ to Y³⁺ (E⦵cell = +1.81 V) [1]; but Y³⁺ could not oxidise I⁻, because E⦵cell = 0.20 − 0.54 = −0.34 V, so Y²⁺ is not regenerated [1]

**(c)** Each catalysed step is between ions of opposite charge, which attract, so the activation energy is lower than for two negative ions that repel [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* The catalyst's E⦵ must lie between the two reactant couples. Answers that check only the first step miss the second mark in (b).

**9.**

**(a)** Sulfur compounds are adsorbed strongly onto the active sites on the iron surface and are not desorbed [1]; this blocks the sites, so fewer are available for nitrogen and hydrogen and the rate falls [1]

**(b)** Any two: lower output of ammonia per hour; cost of replacing or regenerating the catalyst more often; cost of removing sulfur compounds from the feed gas first [2]

**Total: 4**

**10.**

**(a)** Slow at first: only 0.40 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ used in the first 100 s, because little Mn²⁺ is present and the reaction is between two negative ions [1]; fastest between 150 s and 200 s (1.40 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ in 50 s) because Mn²⁺ has built up and catalyses the reaction [1]; slows down after 200 s because MnO₄⁻ is being used up [1]

**(b)** There is no slow start: [MnO₄⁻] falls rapidly from the beginning [1]

**(c)** MnO₄⁻ + 4Mn²⁺ + 8H⁺ → 5Mn³⁺ + 4H₂O [1]; 2Mn³⁺ + C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 2CO₂ [1]

**Total: 6**

*Examiner insight:* "Describe" marks need numbers from the table. "It speeds up then slows down" without data or reasons scores at most one mark.

**11.**

**(a)** The catalysed profile has two humps (one for each step), with an intermediate between them [1]; each hump is lower than the single hump of the uncatalysed reaction (lower activation energy) [1]

**(b)** The reactants and products are the same, so the enthalpy difference between them is unchanged; the catalyst only changes the route [1]

**Total: 3**

**12.** Indicative content; award one mark per valid point, up to 6 [6]

- Heterogeneous catalysts are in a different phase from the reactants, such as Fe (solid) with gaseous N₂ and H₂ in the Haber process, or V₂O₅ in the Contact process.
- Reaction happens at active sites on the surface, by adsorption, reaction and desorption.
- A support medium maximises surface area and minimises the cost of expensive catalysts.
- Impurities can poison active sites, which lowers efficiency and adds cost.
- Homogeneous catalysts are in the same phase as the reactants, such as Fe²⁺(aq) with S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻, and the reaction proceeds through an intermediate species.
- In both types, variable oxidation states allow the catalyst to be oxidised and reduced and regenerated: V +5/+4 in V₂O₅, Fe +2/+3, Mn +2/+3 in autocatalysis.
- In the Fe²⁺ example, each step is between oppositely charged ions, so the activation energy is lower.

**Total: 6**

*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs points about both types. An answer that describes only heterogeneous catalysis, however detailed, cannot reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining heterogeneous as "solid" rather than "different phase".
- Leaving cost out of answers about supports and poisons.
- Writing the V₂O₅ steps without showing regeneration.
- Using E⦵ arguments alone to explain why the Fe²⁺ route is faster.
- Describing autocatalysis graphs without data.
