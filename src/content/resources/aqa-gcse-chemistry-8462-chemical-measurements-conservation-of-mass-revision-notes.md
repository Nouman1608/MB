These notes condense **4.3.1 Chemical measurements, conservation of mass and the quantitative interpretation of
chemical equations** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016, version
1.2). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only statements. For full explanations, see the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-measurements-conservation-of-mass/); then practise with
the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemical-measurements-conservation-of-mass-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Law of conservation of mass:** no atoms are lost or made in a chemical reaction, so the **mass of the
  products equals the mass of the reactants**.
- Reactions are shown by **balanced symbol equations**: the same number of atoms of each element on both sides.
- The **relative formula mass (Mr)** of a compound is the **sum of the relative atomic masses** of the atoms in
  the formula.
- In a balanced equation, the **sum of the Mr of the reactants equals the sum of the Mr of the products**.
- **Percentage by mass** of an element in a compound uses Mr and Ar.
- **Mass changes** in non-enclosed systems are explained by a gas being added or escaping.
- Every measurement has **uncertainty**. The **range about the mean** is a measure of it.

## Formulae and multipliers

| Notation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 3H₂O | Large number multiplies the whole formula: 6 H, 3 O |
| H₂O | Subscript: 2 H, 1 O |
| Ca(OH)₂ | Brackets: 1 Ca, 2 O, 2 H |
| 2Al₂O₃ | 4 Al, 6 O |

## Balancing method

1. Write the formulae. Do not change them.
2. Count the atoms of each element on each side.
3. Change only the large numbers in front of formulae.
4. Balance one element at a time, leaving the elements that appear in the most formulae until last.
5. Recount.

Examples: 4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃; 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃; Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂.

## Calculations

| Calculation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mr | Sum of Ar values in the formula | (NH₄)₂SO₄: 28 + 8 + 32 + 64 = 132 |
| Percentage by mass | (Ar × atoms ÷ Mr) × 100 | Fe in Fe₂O₃: 112 ÷ 160 × 100 = 70% |
| Mass change | Compare start and end masses | 10.0 g CaCO₃ gives 5.6 g solid, so 4.4 g CO₂ lost |
| Mean | Sum ÷ number of results | 24.0, 24.6, 24.3 gives 24.3 |
| Uncertainty | Range ÷ 2 | Range 0.6, so ± 0.3 |

Ar values used: H 1, C 12, N 14, O 16, Na 23, Mg 24, Al 27, S 32, Cl 35.5, Ca 40, Fe 56, Cu 63.5.

## Mass changes in open containers

| Observation | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Metal burns in air: mass goes up | Oxygen from the air joins the metal to make the oxide |
| Metal carbonate heated: mass goes down | Carbon dioxide gas escapes into the air |
| Closed flask: mass stays the same | No gas can escape |

Particle model: gas particles spread out and leave the container, so their mass is not on the balance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conservation of mass | Total mass of reactants equals total mass of products |
| Balanced equation | Same number of atoms of each element on both sides |
| Ar | Relative atomic mass of an element |
| Mr | Relative formula mass of a compound |
| Uncertainty | The range within which the true value probably lies |
| Enclosed system | A closed container from which no matter can escape |
| Thermal decomposition | Breaking down a compound by heating |

## Worked examples in brief

**Mr with brackets.** Mg(OH)₂: 24 + 2 × (16 + 1) = 58.

**Percentage by mass.** Nitrogen in NH₄NO₃: Mr = 80; N = 28; 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = 35%.

**Mass change.** 20.0 g of a carbonate leaves 11.2 g of solid after heating in an open tube. Mass of gas lost =
20.0 - 11.2 = 8.8 g.

**Check by Mr.** CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂: 100 = 56 + 44.

**Uncertainty.** Results 9.8, 10.2 and 10.0 have a mean of 10.0, a range of 0.4 and an uncertainty of ± 0.2.

## Command words

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| State | The law or fact, in one line |
| Balance | The corrected equation with big numbers only |
| Calculate | Working, answer and unit |
| Show that | Every step, ending with the value you are asked to show |
| Explain | The cause, naming the gas and using the particle model |

## Exam traps

- **Changing subscripts** to balance. Only the big numbers change.
- **Forgetting brackets.** In Ca(OH)₂ there are two O and two H.
- **Mixing Ar and Mr.**
- **Percentage by mass with the wrong total.**
- **"Mass is destroyed."** Name the gas that escaped.
- **No unit with the mean.** Give ± and the unit.

## Quick self-test

1. State the law of conservation of mass.
2. How many atoms of each element are in 2Ca(OH)₂?
3. Balance: Mg + O₂ → MgO.
4. Calculate the Mr of CaCO₃.
5. Calculate the percentage of magnesium in MgO.
6. A student burns magnesium in an open crucible and finds the mass increases. Explain.
7. A student heats copper carbonate in an open tube and the mass decreases. Explain.
8. Mass of reactants in a closed flask is 52.3 g. What is the mass of products?
9. Find the mean of 12.1, 12.3 and 12.2.
10. Find the uncertainty for results with a range of 0.8.

### Answers

1. No atoms are lost or made, so the mass of the products equals the mass of the reactants.
2. 2 Ca, 4 O, 4 H.
3. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.
4. 40 + 12 + 48 = 100.
5. 24 ÷ 40 × 100 = 60%.
6. Oxygen gas from the air combines with the magnesium, so the oxide is heavier.
7. Carbon dioxide gas is produced and escapes, so less solid remains.
8. 52.3 g.
9. (12.1 + 12.3 + 12.2) ÷ 3 = 12.2.
10. 0.8 ÷ 2 = 0.4.
