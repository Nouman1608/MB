**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.10 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The legal impact of sources of authority on the character of a Muslim: "The importance of rulings and testimony. The prohibition of interest and the importance of Islamic wills. The key characteristics of an Islamic state."** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 2:275.

The first two parts of this section of the specification looked at how the Qur'an and Sunnah shape a Muslim's personal and social character. This part looks at the legal side: how the sources of authority guide the way a Muslim gives and receives justice, handles money and plans for death, and how they describe the community's government.

## Rulings and justice

**Rulings.** A ruling (*hukm*) is a decision about what Shariah requires in a matter. A judge (*qadi*) gives a binding judgment in a case. A *mufti* gives an opinion (*fatwa*) that is not binding but guides the person who asked.

**The Qur'an on judging.** The Qur'an commands: "when you judge between people, judge with justice" (4:58). It also tells believers to be "persistently standing firm in justice, as witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives" (4:135), and not to let hatred of a people lead them to injustice (5:8).

**The Sunnah.**

- The Prophet (pbuh) applied the same rule to the powerful and the weak. When people asked that a noble woman be excused for theft, he refused and said he would apply it even to his own daughter.
- He warned that he was only a man, and that a clever speaker might persuade him to award something that belonged to another. If so, he said, he would be giving that person a piece of the Fire (Sahih al-Bukhari). A judgment cannot make a wrong right.
- A judge who strives and is correct receives two rewards, and one who strives and errs receives one (Sahih al-Bukhari).

**Why rulings matter.**

- They protect life, faith, mind, family and property, the purposes that scholars say Shariah exists to protect.
- They make sure disputes end in a decision and not in revenge.
- They apply the same standard to everyone.
- They give Muslims confidence that the law rests on divine guidance.

## Testimony (*shahadah*)

**Meaning.** Testimony is evidence given by a witness about what he saw or heard. Because rulings depend on evidence, the truthfulness of witnesses is central to justice.

**Evidence.**

- **Give testimony truthfully, even against yourself** (4:135).
- **Do not hide it.** "Do not conceal testimony, for whoever conceals it - his heart is indeed sinful" (2:283).
- **Written records and witnesses.** The Qur'an advises writing down debts and calling witnesses (2:282).
- **Honest witnesses.** "And bring to witness two just men from among you" (65:2).
- **False testimony.** The servants of the Merciful are those who "do not testify to falsehood" (25:72). The Prophet (pbuh) listed false testimony among the gravest sins (Sahih al-Bukhari).

**Strict standards.** For the most serious accusations, such as adultery, the Qur'an demands four witnesses, and anyone who accuses without them is punished (24:4). This protects innocent people from slander.

**Impact on character.** A Muslim is taught that his word is a trust before Allah. Honest evidence, even when unpleasant, is an act of worship; false evidence harms others and is a major sin.

## The prohibition of interest (*riba*)

**Meaning.** Riba is an increase charged on a loan in return for time, fixed in advance whatever the outcome. If someone borrows £5,000 and agrees to repay £6,000 only because a year has passed, the extra £1,000 is riba.

**The Qur'an.** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 2:275, which says that those who consume interest are like a person struck by Satan, because they say that trade is like interest, when Allah has permitted trade and forbidden interest. It warns that those who return to it will be companions of the Fire. Other verses say that Allah destroys interest and gives increase to charity (2:276), and forbid consuming interest "doubled and multiplied" (3:130).

**The Sunnah.** The Prophet (pbuh) cursed the one who takes interest, the one who pays it, the one who records it and the two who witness it, saying that they are all the same (Sahih Muslim). In the Final Sermon he cancelled the riba of the pre-Islamic period, beginning with that owed to his own uncle, Abbas.

**Why it is forbidden.** Muslims give several reasons.

- **Exploitation.** The lender gains a fixed profit while the borrower, often poor, carries all the risk.
- **Debt.** Interest can multiply a debt until it cannot be repaid.
- **Fairness.** In trade, profit is earned by effort and shared risk. Interest makes money from money alone.
- **Concentration of wealth.** It moves wealth from the poor to the rich.
- **Obedience.** It is a command of Allah, whatever the reasons.

**Trade is permitted.** The verse separates trade, in which profit is tied to goods, work and risk, from interest. A Muslim may earn money by honest trade and investment.

**Alternatives.** Islamic finance offers ways to do business without interest.

| Method | How it works |
|---|---|
| *Mudarabah* | One partner provides money, the other provides work; profit is shared in agreed proportions and losses fall on the investor |
| *Musharakah* | Partners invest together and share profit and loss |
| *Murabaha* | The bank buys an item and sells it to the customer at an agreed profit, paid in instalments |
| *Ijarah* | Leasing: the bank owns an item and rents it to the customer |
| *Qard hasan* | A benevolent loan repaid without extra |

**Impact on character.** The ban asks a Muslim to be fair in money matters, to help the borrower, and to see wealth as a trust.

## Islamic wills (*wasiyyah*)

**Why they matter.** Death is certain, and Islam asks the believer to prepare for it. A will makes sure that debts are paid, that the family is treated fairly and that some of the wealth can be used for good.

**The Qur'an and Sunnah.**

- The Qur'an commands a bequest for parents and relatives (2:180) and sets out fixed shares of inheritance (4:11-12).
- The Prophet (pbuh) said that a Muslim who has something to leave should not let two nights pass without his will written (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- When Sa'd ibn Abi Waqqas (RA), who was ill, asked to leave two-thirds, and then half, the Prophet (pbuh) said one-third, "and one-third is much" (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- A will cannot favour an heir who already has a fixed share.

**The order of payment.** First funeral costs and debts; then the will, up to one-third of the estate, for people or causes not already entitled to a share; then the fixed shares (*faraid*) among the heirs.

**Worked example.** Mr Hassan leaves an estate of £144,000 after funeral costs and debts. His heirs are his wife, one son and one daughter. He has left one-third to charity.

1. One-third for the will: £144,000 ÷ 3 = **£48,000** to charity.
2. Remainder: £144,000 − £48,000 = **£96,000**.
3. His wife's share is one-eighth because he leaves children (4:12): £96,000 ÷ 8 = **£12,000**.
4. Left for the children: £96,000 − £12,000 = **£84,000**.
5. A son takes twice a daughter's share (4:11), so the estate divides into three parts: £84,000 ÷ 3 = £28,000. The son receives 2 × £28,000 = **£56,000**; the daughter receives **£28,000**.
6. Check: £48,000 + £12,000 + £56,000 + £28,000 = £144,000.

**Impact on character.** A will reminds a Muslim of accountability, protects the family from dispute, and gives a way to leave charity.

## The key characteristics of an Islamic state

The specification asks for the key characteristics, not for a political theory. Muslims hold different views about modern forms of government, so describe the principles and give the early examples.

1. **Sovereignty of Allah.** The law is based on the Qur'an and Sunnah. Rulers carry it out; they do not make it up.
2. **Justice.** Courts treat all people equally. Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) said in his first speech as caliph that people should obey him as long as he obeyed Allah and the Messenger.
3. **Consultation (*shura*).** The Qur'an praises those "whose affair is [determined by] consultation among themselves" (42:38) and tells the Prophet (pbuh) to consult (3:159).
4. **Trustworthy leadership.** Positions are a trust (*amanah*) and are given to people who can bear them (4:58).
5. **Protection of life, property and faith.** This includes minorities. The Charter of Madinah guaranteed freedom of religion to the Jewish tribes, and the Qur'an says "there shall be no compulsion in religion" (2:256).
6. **Social welfare.** Zakah is collected and spent on the poor, and the state looks after the needy, the orphan and the traveller.
7. **Defence and security.** The state protects its people and keeps the peace.
8. **Accountability.** Rulers answer to the law and to Allah.

The model usually pointed to is the Prophet's (pbuh) government in Madinah and the rule of the first four caliphs.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 2:275

| Phrase | Use |
|---|---|
| "Those who consume interest cannot stand ... except as one stands who is being beaten by Satan into insanity" | A picture of the confusion and harm brought by interest |
| "Trade is [just] like interest" | The argument of those who defend interest, which the Qur'an rejects |
| "Allah has permitted trade and has forbidden interest" | The clear ruling: trade is lawful, interest is forbidden |
| "Whoever has received an admonition from his Lord and desists may have what is past" | A way back: those who stop are not punished for what has gone |
| "Whoever returns ... are the companions of the Fire" | A strong warning |

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Islam forbids interest. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> Interest is unfair to the borrower [1], because the lender receives a fixed profit while the borrower, who is often poor, takes the whole risk and can be pushed deeper into debt [1]. It is also forbidden because it is a command of Allah [1], and a Muslim obeys even when he does not see the full reason [1]. Qur'an 2:275 says that Allah has permitted trade and forbidden interest, which shows that honest profit is allowed but profit from lending money alone is not [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Saying that all profit is forbidden.** Trade and investment are permitted.
- **Writing that a will can divide everything as the person likes.** A will is limited to one-third for non-heirs.
- **Forgetting the order of payment.** Debts and funeral costs come first.
- **Presenting one model of state as the only view.** Describe principles and early examples.
- **Treating testimony as a minor matter.** False testimony is among the gravest sins.
