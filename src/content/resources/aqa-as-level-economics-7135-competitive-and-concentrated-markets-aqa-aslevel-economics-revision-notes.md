These notes condense **3.1.4 Competitive and concentrated markets** of AQA AS Economics (7135), specification version 1.3, parts 3.1.4.1 to 3.1.4.5. The [3.1.4 study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-competitive-and-concentrated-markets-aqa-aslevel-economics/) explains how to analyse monopoly at AS without theory-of-firm diagrams; the [3.1.4 practice questions](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-competitive-and-concentrated-markets-aqa-aslevel-economics-practice/) test it. All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market structure | The competitive environment of a market, set by number of firms, product differentiation and ease of entry |
| Perfect competition | Many small firms, identical products, perfect information, free entry and exit |
| Price taker | A firm that must accept the market price because it is too small to influence it |
| Pure monopoly | A single seller supplying the whole market |
| Monopoly power | Ability to raise price above the competitive level without losing all sales |
| Barrier to entry | Anything that makes it hard for new firms to join a market |
| Product differentiation | Making a product appear different from rivals' products |
| Concentration ratio | Combined market share of the largest n firms |
| Misallocation of resources | Output differs from the level consumers would choose at a price equal to cost |
| Non-price competition | Competing through quality, features, service or brand rather than price |

## 3.1.4.1 Structures

- The range runs from perfect competition, through markets with many firms selling differentiated products and markets dominated by a few large firms, to pure monopoly.
- Classify any market using the three factors: **number of firms, product differentiation, ease of entry**.
- No theory-of-firm diagrams at AS.

## 3.1.4.2 Objectives

- Profit is important for most firms. Others: **survival, growth, market share**.
- Detailed knowledge of objectives is not needed, but you must link objective to behaviour: a firm chasing market share may cut prices and accept lower profit; a firm fighting to survive may sell below its usual margin to keep cash flowing.

## 3.1.4.3 Competitive markets

- Characteristics: many buyers and sellers, homogeneous product, perfect information, no barriers.
- Price is set by market demand and supply; each firm is a price taker.
- Profits are likely to be lower than in markets dominated by a few large firms because high profits attract entry, supply rises and the price falls; buyers can switch to identical products.
- The formal model and its diagrams are not required.

## 3.1.4.4 Monopoly and monopoly power

**Influences on monopoly power:** barriers to entry, the number of competitors, advertising, product differentiation.

**Concentration ratio:** add the market shares of the largest n firms.

```
Shares: 26%, 21%, 18%, 11%, 9%, rest 15%
CR3 = 26 + 21 + 18 = 65%
CR4 = 65 + 11 = 76%
From sales: top firm sales 480 of a market of 1,600 -> 30% share
```

**Costs of monopoly (basic model):** higher prices and profits, lower output, possible inefficiency, so a misallocation of resources compared with a competitive market. Show with a demand curve: a price rise above the competitive level moves up the curve, so quantity falls.

**Potential benefits:** economies of scale (show with an LRAC curve: a large firm sits lower on LRAC than small rivals); profits that fund invention and innovation.

## 3.1.4.5 The competitive market process

- Firms compete by improving products, reducing costs and improving service quality, not only by cutting prices.
- Many large firms compete vigorously, but monopoly power may lead to consumers being exploited.

## Exam traps

- Drawing MC/MR monopoly diagrams in an AS answer.
- Calling any large firm a "monopoly" when it has monopoly power in a market with rivals.
- Using a concentration ratio as proof of high prices; it shows structure, not behaviour.
- Saying perfect competition earns "no profit"; at AS say profits are likely to be lower because entry competes them away.
- Missing the benefits of monopoly when asked to evaluate.
- Ignoring the objective stated in the data.

## Self-test

1. Name the three factors used to distinguish market structures.
2. State four characteristics of a perfectly competitive market.
3. Why is a firm in perfect competition a price taker?
4. Explain why profits are likely to be lower in a competitive market than in one dominated by a few firms.
5. Distinguish between pure monopoly and monopoly power.
6. State two barriers to entry.
7. Shares are 34%, 22%, 14%, 10%, 8% and 12% for all others. Calculate CR2 and CR4.
8. The top three firms have sales of 210, 150 and 90 in a market worth 900. Calculate CR3.
9. Why might a firm aiming for market share set a low price?
10. Explain how a demand curve shows that monopoly can cut output.
11. State two potential benefits of monopoly.
12. Give two forms of non-price competition.

### Answers

1. Number of firms, degree of product differentiation, ease of entry.
2. Many buyers and sellers; identical products; perfect information; no barriers to entry or exit.
3. It is too small relative to the market to affect the price, and buyers can buy an identical product elsewhere.
4. High profits attract new entrants, supply rises and the price falls until the extra profit is competed away.
5. Pure monopoly is a single seller with the whole market; monopoly power is the ability of any firm to price above the competitive level.
6. Any two: economies of scale; patents; control of raw materials; high set-up costs; strong brand loyalty.
7. CR2 = 56%; CR4 = 80%.
8. (210 + 150 + 90) / 900 = 450 / 900 = 50%.
9. Lower prices attract customers from rivals, so its share of sales rises even if profit per unit falls.
10. Raising price above the competitive level moves up the demand curve, so the quantity bought falls.
11. Economies of scale that lower average cost; profits that fund innovation.
12. Any two: product improvement; better service; advertising and branding; loyalty schemes.
