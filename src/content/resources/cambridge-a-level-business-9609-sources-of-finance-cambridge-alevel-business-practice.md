## Section A

**1.** Define the term *venture capital*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** external sources of finance available to a sole trader. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** difference between a bank overdraft and a bank loan. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why retained earnings are not a free source of finance. **[2]**

**5.** Ruston Logistics sells invoices worth $50 000 to a factor, which pays 80% immediately and the rest, less a fee of $1 500, when customers pay.

**(a)** Calculate the amount Ruston receives immediately.

**(b)** Calculate the total Ruston receives from the factor. **[4]**

**6.** Explain why a private limited company may find it easier to raise finance than a sole trader. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Hartfield Dental needs a $45 000 dental chair. It can lease the chair for 48 payments of $1 050, or buy it on hire purchase with a $9 000 deposit and 48 payments of $900.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of each option.

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why Hartfield might choose the more expensive option. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **two** advantages to a hotel group of using sale and leaseback of its buildings. **[4]**

**9.** Analyse how the level of existing debt might affect a company's choice of source of finance. **[4]**

**10.** Ebele runs a small food stall in a developing economy and has no bank account history. Explain why micro-finance may be suitable for her. **[4]**

**11.** Ostrava Games, a new private limited company, is developing a video game and plans to raise $80 000 through crowd funding. Analyse **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of this choice. **[4]**

**12.** Corbel Furniture plc wants $15 million to build a new factory. It already has large bank loans. The directors are choosing between a share issue and debentures. Evaluate which source Corbel should use. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Finance provided by specialist investors to new or growing businesses considered risky [1], in return for a share of ownership and often some influence over decisions [1].

**2.** Any two: bank loan [1]; bank overdraft [1]; leasing; hire purchase; trade credit; micro-finance; crowd funding; government grant.

**3.** An overdraft is flexible, with interest charged only on the amount overdrawn, and can be withdrawn at short notice [1], whereas a loan is a fixed sum repaid in set instalments over an agreed period, usually at a lower interest rate [1].

**4.** Profit kept in the business could have been paid to owners as dividends or income [1], and it could have earned a return if used elsewhere, so it has an opportunity cost [1].

**5.** **(a)** 0.80 × 50 000 [1] = $40 000 [1].

**(b)** Remaining 20% = $10 000, less fee $1 500 = $8 500 [1]. Total = 40 000 + 8 500 = $48 500 [1].

**6.** A private limited company has limited liability, so investors risk only what they put in [1], making them more willing to buy shares [1]. It can also sell shares to family, friends or employees, which a sole trader cannot do [1].

**7.** **(a)** Lease: 48 × 1 050 = $50 400 [1] [1]. Hire purchase: 9 000 + 48 × 900 = $52 200 [1] [1].

**(b)** Hire purchase costs more, but Hartfield owns the chair at the end [1]; if the chair will last many years after the payments end, owning it may be better value than leasing again [1]. Allow choosing the lease for reasons other than cost, such as included maintenance, if explained.

**8.** Any two, explained. It raises a large sum of cash quickly [1], which can fund expansion such as refurbishing other hotels [1]. The group keeps using the buildings [1], so customers notice no change and trading continues [1]. Allow: no new shareholders, so control is kept.

**9.** A company with high existing debt already pays large interest costs [1], so further borrowing would raise its fixed costs and the risk of being unable to pay if profit falls [1]. Lenders may refuse or charge higher interest [1], so the company is more likely to choose share capital or internal sources such as retained earnings [1].

**10.** Ebele has no credit record or security, so a bank is unlikely to lend to her [1]. Micro-finance provides very small loans designed for such people [1], so she can buy stock or equipment for her stall [1], with repayments sized to a small business's income [1].

**11.** Advantage: backers may pre-order the game, which raises finance and tests demand [1], showing whether the game is likely to sell before full development [1]. Disadvantage: the campaign may not reach its target [1], and publishing the idea online may let rivals copy it [1].

**12.** For a share issue: no interest is payable, which matters because Corbel already has large loans [1]; the finance never has to be repaid, reducing risk if profits fall [1]; lenders may see a large extra debt as too risky and charge high interest [1].
For debentures: existing shareholders keep control and their share of future profits [1]; interest on debentures is fixed, so if the factory is very profitable the extra profit goes to shareholders [1]; a share issue may be costly to arrange and may lower the share price if investors think it is a sign of weakness [1].
Judgement: Corbel should probably use a share issue [1], because adding $15 million of debt to large existing loans increases the risk of being unable to meet interest payments if demand falls [1]. The decision depends on the interest rate on debentures, the expected profit from the factory and how much existing shareholders value control [1]. A combination, such as part shares and part debentures, could balance the two [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Recommending shares to a business that cannot issue them.
- Forgetting the deposit in a hire-purchase calculation.
- Saying debt factoring gives the full invoice value.
- Ignoring existing debt when recommending a loan.
- Giving a recommendation with no condition or drawback.
