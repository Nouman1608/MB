## Condensed recall

**Transformations**
- Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂; light energy → chemical energy; inorganic → organic
  matter.
- Respiration: C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O; chemical energy released, much as heat; happens in all
  organisms, all the time.

**Thermodynamics**
- First law: energy is conserved, so budgets must balance.
- Second law: each transformation degrades some energy to heat (entropy rises), so energy flows one
  way and must be replaced by sunlight.

**Feeding relationships**
- Food chain = one pathway; food web = interlinked chains.
- Omnivores sit at more than one trophic level.
- Decomposers and detritivores receive energy from every level.

**Consumer energy budget**
- Ingested = egested + assimilated.
- Assimilated (GSP) = respiration + new biomass (NSP) + excretion.
- Herbivores egest a lot (cellulose). Endotherms respire a lot (body heat).

**Productivity and efficiency**
- NPP = GPP − R. NSP = GSP − R.
- Efficiency = energy in new biomass at a level ÷ energy at the level below × 100.
- Overall efficiency across several levels = top-level value ÷ producer value × 100.
- 10% is a rough guide; many real transfers are lower.

**Units**
- Productivity: kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ or g m⁻² yr⁻¹.
- 1 ha = 10,000 m²; 1 t = 1,000 kg = 1,000,000 g.

**Estimating NPP by harvest**
- Increase in dry mass per area ÷ time. Underestimates: misses grazing, litter fall and roots.

**Trophic position and people**
- Each step loses most of the energy, so eating lower in the chain feeds more people per hectare.
- Limits of the argument: grazing land unsuitable for crops; nutritional value of animal foods.

**Persistent pollutants**
- Bioaccumulation: build-up in one organism over time.
- Biomagnification: concentration increases up trophic levels.
- Biomagnifying substances: persistent, fat-soluble, poorly excreted (DDT, PCBs, methyl mercury).

## Method in steps: efficiency questions

```
1. Identify the two levels being compared.
2. Make sure both values use the same units.
3. If needed, find NSP: ingested − egested − respired.
4. Efficiency = upper value ÷ lower value × 100.
5. Round sensibly (3 s.f.) and add "%".
6. Explain the loss: not eaten, egested, respired as heat.
```

Mini example (invented): primary consumers store 640 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ from producer NPP of 8,000 kJ m⁻²
yr⁻¹. Efficiency = 640 ÷ 8000 × 100 = **8.0%**. Secondary consumers store 48 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹, so the next
transfer is 48 ÷ 640 × 100 = **7.5%**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trophic level | Position of an organism in a food chain |
| Producer | Organism that makes its own food, usually by photosynthesis |
| Detritivore | Animal feeding on dead organic matter |
| Ingestion | Taking food into the body |
| Egestion | Removing undigested food as faeces |
| Assimilation | Absorbing digested food into the body's tissues |
| Gross primary productivity | Rate at which producers fix energy |
| Net primary productivity | GPP minus producers' respiration |
| Net secondary productivity | Assimilated energy minus consumers' respiration |
| Ecological efficiency | Percentage of energy passed into new biomass at the next level |
| Entropy | Measure of disorder; increases with each transformation |
| Bioaccumulation | Build-up of a substance in an organism over time |
| Biomagnification | Increasing concentration of a substance up a food chain |

## Must-know distinctions

- **GPP vs NPP:** all energy fixed by producers vs what remains after their own respiration.
- **GSP vs NSP:** energy assimilated by consumers vs what remains as new consumer biomass.
- **Egestion vs excretion:** undigested food leaving as faeces vs metabolic waste such as urea.
- **Pyramid of biomass vs productivity:** standing stock at one moment, which can be inverted, vs
  energy flow over time, which never is.
- **Bioaccumulation vs biomagnification:** build-up within one organism vs increase up a food chain.

## Exam traps

- **Energy is not used up.** Write "transformed to heat" or "lost as heat".
- **Divide by the lower level.** Efficiency of 120 % means the division is upside down.
- **Units must match.** Do not compare g m⁻² yr⁻¹ with kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹.
- **Plants respire.** A producer's budget includes respiration.
- **Biomagnification needs the food chain.** Bioaccumulation is about one organism.
- **Show the three loss routes.** Not eaten, not digested, respired.

## Self-test

1. Write the word equation for photosynthesis.
2. State the first law of thermodynamics and its use in an energy budget.
3. Why must ecosystems receive a continuous input of sunlight?
4. A herbivore ingests 900 kJ and egests 500 kJ. Calculate assimilation.
5. It respires 320 kJ. Calculate its NSP.
6. Why do endotherms have lower efficiencies than ectotherms?
7. Convert 600 g m⁻² yr⁻¹ to t ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹.
8. Why does the harvest method underestimate NPP?
9. Producers 20,000 and tertiary consumers 30 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹. Calculate overall efficiency.
10. Distinguish bioaccumulation from biomagnification.
11. Give two properties of a substance that biomagnifies.
12. Give one limitation of the argument that people should eat lower in food chains.

### Answers

1. Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (using light energy).
2. Energy is neither created nor destroyed, so inputs must equal storage plus outputs.
3. Energy flows one way and is lost as heat at each transformation, so it must be replaced.
4. 900 − 500 = **400 kJ**.
5. 400 − 320 = **80 kJ**.
6. They respire much of their assimilated energy to keep a constant body temperature.
7. 600 × 10,000 = 6,000,000 g ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹ = **6 t ha⁻¹ yr⁻¹**.
8. Biomass eaten, shed as litter or grown below ground during the period is not measured.
9. 30 ÷ 20,000 × 100 = **0.15%**.
10. Bioaccumulation is build-up within one organism over time; biomagnification is increasing
    concentration at each higher trophic level.
11. Any two: persistent; fat-soluble; not easily excreted or broken down.
12. Some grazing land cannot grow crops, and animal foods provide important nutrients.
