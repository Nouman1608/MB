## Section A — Reading (practise on any short unseen extract of your choice)

**1.** Choose a short (200-400 word) extract of prose fiction. List **four** pieces of information the extract states explicitly about the setting or a character. **[4]**

**2.** From the same extract, select **one** phrase you find effective and explain the writer's use of language, including the effect created. **[8]**

**3.** Explain how the writer has structured the extract to interest the reader, referring to the opening, the middle and the ending. **[8]**

**4.** "The writer makes this a genuinely engaging opening." To what extent do you agree? Evaluate the extract's opening two paragraphs, using the text to support your response. **[20]**

## Section B — Writing

**5.** Write a description suggested by the following image or scenario: *An abandoned house at the edge of a town, seen at dusk.* **[40]**

---

## Answers (worked approach, since Section A depends on your chosen extract)

**1.** Look strictly for information the text **states**, not information you infer — e.g. a name, a place, a time of day, a physical description given directly. Four separate, clearly stated details score full marks.

**2.** A strong answer: (a) quotes a short, precise phrase; (b) names the technique (e.g. "the verb choice…", "this metaphor…"); (c) explains the specific effect on the reader/meaning, not a generic "it makes it interesting." Two well-developed points at this depth typically score in the top band.

**3.** Track the extract's shape across all three stages: what the writer focuses on **first** and why, how the focus **shifts** in the middle (zooming in on detail, changing pace, introducing a twist), and how the **ending** resolves, withholds or redirects the reader's attention. Naming specific structural features (a short sentence for pace, a paragraph break for a shift in focus) with a clear "why" is what separates this from a plot summary.

**4.** State a clear judgement (fully agree / partly agree / disagree) and sustain it. Support with **specific textual evidence** from the opening two paragraphs, evaluating rather than just describing — e.g. explaining *why* a particular technique succeeds or falls short in creating engagement, not simply identifying that it's present.

**5.** Mark against: content and organisation (structure, ambitious vocabulary, a clear shape with an opening, development and ending) and technical accuracy (a range of correctly punctuated sentence structures, accurate spelling). A strong response plans a clear structure before writing — for example, moving from an establishing wide shot of the house, to closer sensory detail, to a final image or reflection — rather than a list of description with no shape.

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Retelling what happens in the extract instead of analysing the writer's choices.
- Identifying a technique without explaining its specific effect.
- Writing a continuation of the extract for the creative writing task rather than an original piece.
- Running out of time on Section B because too long was spent perfecting Section A answers.
- Uneven sentence variety in the creative writing response — several sentences in a row with the same structure and length.
