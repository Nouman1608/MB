## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which item appears in a club's receipts and payments account but not in its
income and expenditure account?

A: purchase of new equipment

B: depreciation of equipment

C: subscriptions in arrears at the year end

D: surplus for the year

**[1]**

**2.** A club received subscriptions of 9,400 during the year. At the start of the
year subscriptions of 200 were in arrears; at the end, 350 had been received in
advance. There were no other balances. What is the subscriptions income for the year?

A: 8,850

B: 9,950

C: 9,550

D: 9,250

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term "accumulated fund". **[2]**

**4.** State two differences between a receipts and payments account and an income
and expenditure account. **[2]**

**5.** At 1 January 2028 a chess club had: equipment 15,000; café inventory 600;
bank 2,100; subscriptions in arrears 150; subscriptions in advance 300; rent
accrued 250. Calculate the accumulated fund at 1 January 2028. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a club shows the profit on its refreshments in the income and
expenditure account rather than the refreshment takings. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Hillcrest Tennis Club has a 31 December year end. At 1 January 2028
subscriptions in arrears were 480 and subscriptions in advance were 360. During 2028
the club received subscriptions of 21,450, which included 400 for 2027 and 600 for
2029. The remaining 2027 arrears are to be written off. At 31 December 2028,
subscriptions of 520 for 2028 were in arrears.

**(a)** Prepare the subscriptions account for 2028. **[6]**

**(b)** State how the closing balances are shown in the statement of financial
position. **[2]**

**8.** The club's bar had opening inventory of 1,250 and closing inventory of 1,080.
Payments to bar suppliers were 7,640. Bar suppliers were owed 520 at the start of
the year and 690 at the end. Bar takings were 12,300 and the bar steward's wages
were 2,900.

**(a)** Calculate bar purchases for the year. **[2]**

**(b)** Prepare the bar trading account to show the profit on the bar. **[4]**

**9.** Brookside Drama Society provides the following for the year ended
31 December 2028: subscriptions for the year 6,400; profit on refreshments 820;
ticket sales for shows 3,950; costs of putting on shows 2,780; hall hire paid
2,400, with a further 200 owing; insurance paid 900, of which 150 is for 2029;
postage 260. Costumes and stage equipment were valued at 4,200 on 1 January, new
items costing 800 were bought, and everything was valued at 4,300 on 31 December.

Prepare the income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 December 2028. **[7]**

**10.** A club's accumulated fund was 23,600 at the start of the year. At the end
of the year its assets totalled 27,900 and its liabilities 1,750. No capital was
added by members. Calculate the surplus or deficit for the year. **[2]**

**11.** A club's treasurer reports a deficit of 1,800 for the year, but the bank
balance has risen by 3,000. Explain three possible reasons. **[3]**

**12.** Oakfield Social Club's income and expenditure account shows subscriptions
of 12,000, a loss on the café of 600 and an overall deficit of 900. The committee
proposes raising subscriptions by 10%. Advise the committee. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. Equipment is capital expenditure, so it is not revenue expenditure in
the income and expenditure account. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. 9,400 − 200 (last year's arrears) − 350 (next year's) = 8,850. **[1]**

**3.** The club's total assets less its total liabilities [1]; it is the members'
interest in the club, equivalent to the capital of a business [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two from: receipts and payments records cash and bank only, whereas
income and expenditure records income earned and expenditure incurred; receipts and
payments includes capital items, income and expenditure only revenue items;
receipts and payments ends with a cash or bank balance, income and expenditure
with a surplus or deficit; income and expenditure includes non-cash items such as
depreciation [1] [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Assets 15,000 + 600 + 2,100 + 150 = 17,850; liabilities 300 + 250 = 550 [1].
Accumulated fund = **17,300** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The takings are not all gain: refreshments have to be bought and served,
so their cost must be set against the takings [1]. Showing only the profit tells
members whether the activity adds to or drains the club's funds [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

```
Subscriptions account ($)
Balance b/d (arrears)        480    Balance b/d (in advance)        360
Income and expenditure    21,330    Bank                         21,450
Balance c/d (in advance)     600    Subscriptions written off        80
                                    Balance c/d (arrears)           520
                          ------                                 ------
                          22,410                                 22,410
                          ======                                 ======
Balance b/d (arrears)        520    Balance b/d (in advance)        600
```

Opening balances on the correct sides [1]; bank 21,450 [1]; 80 written off [1];
closing arrears 520 as balance c/d on the credit side [1]; closing advance 600 as
balance c/d on the debit side [1]; income 21,330 [1].

**(b)** Subscriptions in arrears 520: current asset [1]. Subscriptions in advance
600: current liability [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** 7,640 − 520 + 690 [1] = **7,810** [1].

**(b)**

```
Bar trading account
Bar takings                                    12,300
Opening inventory                    1,250
Purchases                            7,810
                                     9,060
Closing inventory                   (1,080)     7,980
Gross profit                                    4,320
Bar steward's wages                             2,900
Profit on bar                                   1,420
```

Cost of sales 7,980 [1]; gross profit 4,320 [1]; wages deducted [1]; profit 1,420 [1]. **[6]**

**9.**

```
Brookside Drama Society: Income and expenditure account for the year ended 31 December 2028
Income
   Subscriptions                                          6,400
   Profit on refreshments                                   820
   Shows: ticket sales 3,950 less costs 2,780             1,170
                                                          8,390
Expenditure
   Hall hire (2,400 + 200)                     2,600
   Insurance (900 − 150)                         750
   Depreciation of costumes and equipment        700
   Postage                                       260      4,310
Surplus for the year                                      4,080
```

Subscriptions and refreshments [1]; shows surplus 1,170 [1]; hall hire 2,600 [1];
insurance 750 [1]; depreciation 4,200 + 800 − 4,300 = 700 [1]; total expenditure
4,310 [1]; surplus 4,080, correctly labelled [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Closing accumulated fund = 27,900 − 1,750 = 26,150 [1]. Surplus = 26,150
− 23,600 = **2,550** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Any three from: depreciation is expenditure but no cash is paid; subscriptions
received in advance for next year increase bank but are not this year's income;
arrears from last year were collected this year; suppliers or other expenses are
still owed at the year end; a capital receipt such as the sale of equipment or a
donation for a building increased bank but is not income [1] [1] [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Without the café loss of 600 the deficit would be only 300, so the café
causes two-thirds of the deficit [1]. A 10% rise gives 1,200
more income, turning the deficit into a surplus of 300 [1]. However, higher fees may
cause members to leave, reducing income [1]. The committee should first review the
café's prices and costs, and raise subscriptions only by the amount needed after
that [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using subscriptions received instead of subscriptions for the year.
- Putting capital items in the income and expenditure account.
- Using payments to suppliers instead of purchases.
- Calling a surplus "profit" or the accumulated fund "capital".
- Leaving out the liabilities when calculating the accumulated fund.
