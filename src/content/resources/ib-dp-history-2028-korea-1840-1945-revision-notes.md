These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. Korea (1840 to 1945) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-korea-1840-1945/) for the full account and try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-korea-1840-1945-practice/) afterwards.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Joseon | The Korean dynasty that ruled from 1392 to 1897 and then became the Korean Empire |
| Sedo politics | Rule by powerful in-law families in the name of child kings, from 1800 |
| Donghak | "Eastern Learning"; a native religious and social movement founded by Choe Je-u in 1860 |
| Daewongun | Title of the regent father of a king; Gojong's father held it 1864 to 1873 |
| Eulsa Treaty | The 1905 treaty making Korea a Japanese protectorate |
| Gabo reforms | Reforms of 1894 to 1896 under Japanese influence |
| Gwangmu reforms | Reforms of 1897 to 1904 under the Korean Empire |
| Independence Club | Reform society, 1896 to 1898 |
| Righteous armies | Armed resisters to Japanese control, 1905 to 1910 and after |
| March First Movement | Mass non-violent protest for independence in 1919 |
| Comfort women | Women forced into the Japanese military's sexual slavery system |

## Why was Korea opened and why did it lose independence?

1. **Internal weakness:** Sedo politics, corruption, peasant unrest, a weak treasury.
2. **Regional rivalry:** China, Japan and Russia competed in the 1880s and 1890s.
3. **Unequal treaties:** Ganghwa (1876) set the pattern.
4. **Reform too slow:** Gwangmu reforms were real but short of money and time.
5. **Japanese victories:** over China in 1895 and over Russia in 1905, then the Eulsa Treaty and annexation.

## Reform movements compared

| Reform | Dates | Main features | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Daewongun's reforms | 1864 to 1873 | Cut Sedo power; tax reform; isolation | Isolation made Korea weak against pressure |
| Gapsin Coup | 1884 | Radical reform plan; Japanese help | Defeated in three days by Chinese troops |
| Gabo reforms | 1894 to 1896 | End of class system, slavery, old exams | Japanese-imposed; unpopular |
| Gwangmu reforms | 1897 to 1904 | Land survey, railways, schools, new currency | Lack of funds; Japan's growing power |
| Independence Club | 1896 to 1898 | Newspaper, public debate, assembly demand | Closed by the government |

## Japanese rule in brief

- **1910 to 1919:** military-police rule; land survey; bans on political groups.
- **1919:** March First Movement; Provisional Government in Shanghai.
- **1920s "cultural rule":** Korean newspapers; more police; rice shipped to Japan.
- **1930s:** industrial growth in the north; assimilation policies; shrines, language and names.
- **1937 to 1945:** wartime mobilisation; forced labour; conscription from 1944; comfort women.

## Economic and social balance sheet

| Gains | Costs |
|---|---|
| Railways, ports, banks, factories | Profits mostly to Japanese owners |
| Higher rice output | Rice shipped away; peasants became tenants |
| Schools and a Korean press (1920s) | Language and culture suppressed (1930s) |
| Growth of a modern working class | Forced labour and wartime exploitation |

## Perspectives

- Nationalist: colonial exploitation and heroic resistance.
- "Colonial modernisation": infrastructure and growth under Japan.
- "Colonial modernity" (Shin and Robinson): change was real but experienced under foreign control.
- Questions of collaboration: who worked with Japan and why.

## Worked example: a plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the impact of Japanese rule on Korean society between 1910 and 1945."

1. Thesis: the impact was transformative but mainly harmful, and it changed in phases.
2. Political: control and repression, and the March First Movement.
3. Social and cultural: schools, language and name policies.
4. Economic: infrastructure and growth set against exploitation.
5. Wartime: forced labour, conscription and comfort women.
6. Perspectives and a judgement.

## Common mistakes

- Treating 1910 to 1945 as one uniform period.
- Calling the Gabo reforms purely Korean or purely Japanese.
- Leaving out the Chinese decade.
- Describing Kim Il Sung's wartime role without noting how far it was later exaggerated.

## Self-test

1. What was Sedo politics?
2. Who founded Donghak?
3. What did the Treaty of Ganghwa do?
4. Why was the Gapsin Coup defeated?
5. What was the Triple Intervention?
6. In what year was the Korean Empire proclaimed?
7. What did the Eulsa Treaty do?
8. What happened on 1 March 1919?

**Answers:** 1. Rule by in-law families through child kings. 2. Choe Je-u (1860). 3. Opened Korean ports to Japan and treated Korea as independent of China. 4. Chinese troops under Yuan Shikai crushed it. 5. Russia, France and Germany forced Japan to return the Liaodong peninsula in 1895. 6. 1897. 7. Made Korea a Japanese protectorate. 8. The declaration of independence and the start of nationwide non-violent protests.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know about Kim Il Sung and Syngman Rhee?**
Yes, in outline: the guide ends with liberation and the establishment of their governments.

**How should I write about the comfort women?**
State the facts carefully, note that the issue is still disputed in politics, and avoid unsupported numbers.
