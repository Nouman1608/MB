OxfordAQA International A-Level Accounting (9615) is a modular qualification of four papers, sat over
the AS and A-level teaching period. AS Paper 1 (Introduction to Financial Accounting) and AS Paper 2
(Financial & Management Accounting) are each 2 hours, 80 marks and 20% of the full A-level. The A-level papers,
Paper 3 (Financial Accounting) and Paper 4 (Accounting for Analysis and Decision Making), step up
to 2 hours 15 minutes, 90 marks, and 30% of the full A-level each. This is a recently launched
specification -- first teaching September 2024, first AS exams May/June 2025, first A-level exams
May/June 2026 -- already examined at both levels as of this record's verification. These notes
complement the site's guides to
[the Role of the Accountant](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-accounting-role-of-the-accountant/) and
[Types of Business Organisation](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-accounting-types-of-business-organisation/).

## AS marks are worth less, individually, than A-level marks

Since AS papers carry 80 marks for 20% and A-level papers carry 90 marks for 30%, each A-level mark is
worth proportionally more of the final grade than each AS mark. **Exam-preparation priority**: once AS
technique is secure, shift revision time towards the two A-level papers, which together carry 60% of
the qualification against the AS papers' 40%.

## Financial Accounting threads through all four papers

AS Paper 1 introduces double-entry, ledger accounts and basic financial statements; Paper 3
builds directly on that foundation with more complex adjustments and statement preparation.
**Exam-preparation priority**: treat AS Paper 1 technique as the base layer for Paper 3 revision
rather than revising the two in isolation -- errors carried up from AS-level bookkeeping habits cost
marks at A-level too.

## Paper 4 is genuinely new analytical territory

Accounting for Analysis and Decision Making moves beyond statement preparation into ratio analysis,
budgeting and investment appraisal -- skills that AS Paper 2's financial management content only
partially anticipates. **Exam-preparation priority**: give this paper dedicated revision time rather
than assuming AS-level financial management coverage carries across automatically.

## Presentation format is examined, not just the numbers

Financial statements must follow standard professional layout -- correct headings, subtotals in the
right place, workings shown -- and a clear layout makes your figures easy to follow and check. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise reproducing statement formats from memory until the
layout itself needs no thought, freeing attention for the figures.

## AS content is assessed on the AS papers only

AS Papers 1 and 2 assess the AS content (sections 3.1.1 to 3.1.10; sections 3.1.1 to 3.1.3, including
the double entry model, appear on both). A-level Papers 3 and 4 assess sections 3.2.1 to 3.2.10 only,
although the specification notes that they build upon the knowledge and skills from the AS course.
**Exam-preparation priority**:
do not defer AS-specific content to "revise later at A-level" -- once the AS papers are sat, that
specific content is not directly re-examined, even though the underlying skills feed forward.

## Worked routine: adjusting a trial balance before preparing statements

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - list every adjustment given in the question:
Accruals, prepayments, depreciation, bad debts -- one line each.

Step 2 - work each adjustment through both affected accounts:
Every adjustment touches at least two ledger balances; find both
before moving on.

Step 3 - update the trial balance figures:
Cross out and replace, don't just annotate in the margin --
markers need to see the final figure used.

Step 4 - build the statements from the adjusted figures only:
Never carry forward an unadjusted trial balance number by habit.

Step 5 - check the balance sheet balances:
If it doesn't, an adjustment in Step 2 was applied to only one
side -- go back before submitting, not after.
```

Step 5 is the safeguard: a balance sheet that fails to balance is the clearest possible signal that an
earlier adjustment was applied incompletely, and it is far cheaper to catch in the exam room than to
lose the marks silently.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise AS Paper 1 bookkeeping technique as the foundation for Paper 3,
  not in isolation; give Paper 4's analysis and decision-making content its own dedicated
  revision time; drill statement formats from memory.
- **During any paper**: work through every stated adjustment methodically before starting the final
  statements, and show workings for partial credit.
- **Before submitting**: check that prepared balance sheets actually balance as a built-in error check.

## Self-test

1. How do the AS and A-level papers differ in marks and weighting?
2. Why should AS Paper 1 and Paper 3 be revised together rather than separately?
3. What kind of content does Paper 4 introduce that AS Paper 2 does not fully cover?
4. What should you do if a prepared balance sheet does not balance?

**Answers:** 1. AS papers are 80 marks each for 20% of the A-level; A-level papers are 90 marks each for
30% -- so each A-level mark carries more weight. 2. Because Paper 3 builds directly on AS Paper
1's bookkeeping and statement-preparation foundations, and errors carried up from AS technique cost
marks at A-level too. 3. Ratio analysis, budgeting and investment appraisal -- analytical skills only
partially anticipated by AS Paper 2's financial management content. 4. Go back through the adjustments
before submitting -- an unbalanced balance sheet almost always means an adjustment was applied to only
one side of the accounts.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International A-level Accounting 9615?**
Four. AS Paper 1 (Introduction to Financial Accounting) and AS Paper 2 (Financial and Management Accounting) are each 2 hours, 80 marks and 20% of the A-level; Paper 3 (Financial Accounting) and Paper 4 (Accounting for Analysis and Decision Making) are each 2 hours 15 minutes, 90 marks and 30%.

**Are AS marks worth the same as A-level marks in 9615?**
No. AS papers carry 80 marks for 20% and A-level papers carry 90 marks for 30%, so each A-level mark is worth proportionally more of the final grade. Together the two A-level papers carry 60% of the qualification.

**When were the first 9615 exams?**
The specification was first taught from September 2024, with first International AS exams in May/June 2025 and first International A-level exams in May/June 2026.

**Does presentation matter in OxfordAQA 9615 accounting exams?**
Yes. Financial statements should follow a standard professional layout, with correct headings, subtotals in the right place and workings shown, so the figures are easy to follow and check.
