## 1.3 (a) Benefits of technology

| Tool | Benefit | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Accounting software | Speed | One entry is posted to every linked account |
| Accounting software | Accuracy | The program does the arithmetic and posts both sides |
| Accounting software | Reports on demand | Trial balance or statement of profit or loss without re-adding |
| Accounting software | Easy searching and storage | Records are electronic, found by name or number |
| Spreadsheet | Formulas | Totals and ratios recalculate when inputs change |
| Spreadsheet | What-if testing | Change one assumption and every result updates |
| Spreadsheet | Templates | Monthly layouts reused |
| Spreadsheet | Charts | Trends in sales or costs shown visually |

Limit to remember: technology processes what it is given. A wrong figure typed in stays wrong.

### Spreadsheet reminder

Straight-line depreciation in a cell: =(cost-residual)/life.

For cost 7,200, residual value 600 and life 6 years: (7,200 − 600) ÷ 6 = 1,100 a year. Change the life to 5 years and the charge becomes (7,200 − 600) ÷ 5 = 1,320, with no re-typing.

### Method in steps: "Explain a benefit" answers

1. Name the tool (software or spreadsheet).
2. State the benefit (for example, speed).
3. Give the reason (one entry posts to all accounts).
4. Give the effect on the business (less time, fewer errors, sooner information).

## 1.3 (b) Security issues

| Issue | Meaning | Typical causes | Possible effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data loss | Records destroyed or unreadable | Hardware failure, virus, power cut, fire, theft, accidental deletion | Cannot show who owes or is owed; time lost re-keying |
| Access | Who can open or change records | No passwords, shared login, room left open | Fraud, altered figures, deleted records |
| Confidentiality | Information kept private | Hacking, printouts left out, staff talking, visible screens | Lost trade, harm to customers, legal trouble |

Must-know distinction: access is about who gets in. Confidentiality is about what happens to the information. A person with proper access can still breach confidentiality by passing information on.

## 1.3 (c) Protection methods

| Hardware | Software |
|---|---|
| Backups on external drives, kept off-site | Passwords and user access levels |
| Fireproof safe for backup media | Encryption of files and backups |
| Uninterruptible power supply | Antivirus and firewall, kept updated |
| Locked server room or cabinets | Scheduled automatic backups, including online copies |
| Keycards or fingerprint scanners | Audit trail showing who changed what |

Match issue to method: backups and UPS for data loss; passwords, locks and access levels for access; encryption and restricted access for confidentiality.

### Backup arithmetic reminder

A business keys 6 transactions an hour for 8 hours a day. If a disk fails after 3 hours of a day and the last backup was at the end of the previous day, work lost is 3 × 6 = 18 transactions. If 2 full days of work were done since the last backup, work lost is (2 × 8 + 3) × 6 = 114 transactions.

## 1.4 (a) Principles of professional ethics

The specification asks you to describe the principles without listing them. A widely used set is the five fundamental principles in the international code for professional accountants.

| Principle | One-line meaning | Quick test |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | Honest and straightforward | Would I be linked with a false statement? |
| Objectivity | Judgement free from bias and pressure | Is someone's wish deciding the figure? |
| Professional competence and due care | Up-to-date knowledge, careful work | Am I applying the rules properly? |
| Confidentiality | Do not disclose or misuse work information | Am I sharing something I learned through my job? |
| Professional behaviour | Obey law, protect profession's reputation | Would this harm trust in accountants? |

## 1.4 (b) Applying principles to roles and functions

| Role or function | Typical situation | Principle tested |
|---|---|---|
| Bookkeeper recording | Asked to leave a purchase unrecorded to reduce costs shown | Integrity |
| Accounts clerk paying suppliers | Supplier offers a gift to be paid first | Objectivity |
| Accountant preparing statements | Not sure how to treat a new item, but guesses | Professional competence and due care |
| Payroll clerk | Friend asks about a colleague's pay | Confidentiality |
| Auditor | Client is also a close relative | Objectivity |
| Any accountant | Takes part in a scheme to avoid tax unlawfully | Professional behaviour |

### Method in steps: "Apply" answers

1. Pick out the facts that matter in the situation.
2. Name the principle the facts test.
3. Say why the action would breach it.
4. State what the accountant should do (refuse, correct, seek advice, keep quiet).

### Worked reminder

Sunita Pell, bookkeeper at Anvil Road Cycles, is told to leave out a supplier invoice of 9,000 so that profit of 36,400 looks higher. Profit would show 36,400 + 9,000 = 45,400, which is 24.7% higher (9,000 ÷ 36,400 × 100). Principle: integrity, because the figures would mislead; competence and due care also applies, since the expense belongs to the period. Action: record the invoice and explain why.

## 1.4 (c) Public interest

Definition: the wellbeing of the wider community, including people who rely on accounts but are not the client or employer, such as lenders, investors, suppliers, employees and tax authorities.

| Role | How it is applied |
|---|---|
| Bookkeeper or accountant | Prepares truthful records that others will use |
| Accountant advising | Declines to help conceal income or inflate results |
| Auditor | Reports honestly, even when the client objects |

Must-know distinction: public interest is not the same as the employer's interest. When they conflict, the accountant must not mislead others for the employer's gain.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Issue and method | Data loss is an issue; a backup is a method of protecting against it |
| Software (benefit) and software (protection) | Accounting software helps record and report; security software such as antivirus protects the data |
| Hardware and software protection | Hardware is physical equipment (drive, UPS, lock); software is a program or setting (password, encryption) |
| Integrity and objectivity | Integrity is honesty in what you say and record; objectivity is keeping judgement free from bias and pressure |
| Confidentiality and public interest | Confidentiality limits disclosure; public interest can mean an accountant must not help hide wrongdoing from those who rely on the accounts |
| Spreadsheet and accounting software | A spreadsheet is flexible and built by the user; accounting software posts to linked accounts automatically |

### Worked reminder: choosing protections

Scenario (invented): Coastline Pet Supplies keeps its records on one office computer. The risks are a power cut, a theft and a former employee who still knows the shared password. Suitable protections: a UPS (hardware, data loss); a backup drive kept off-site (hardware, data loss); individual passwords that are removed when staff leave (software, access); encryption of the backup (software, confidentiality).

## Quick self-test

1. State two benefits of accounting software.
2. Give two benefits of using a spreadsheet for a budget.
3. A spreadsheet cell holds =(B1-B2)/B3, with B1 = 9,600, B2 = 1,200 and B3 = 7. Calculate the result.
4. Identify the data security issue when a flood destroys the only copy of the sales records.
5. Explain how a user password helps with the access issue.
6. Name one hardware and one software method of protecting data against data loss.
7. A clerk emails a customer list to a rival in return for payment. Name the principle breached.
8. Describe the principle of objectivity.
9. Explain the public interest in the work of an auditor.
10. A business backs up at 17:00 each day and enters 5 transactions an hour. The system fails at 12:00 and work began at 09:00. How many transactions must be re-entered?

### Answers

1. Any two with reasons: speed (automatic posting), accuracy (calculations done by the program), reports on demand, easy searching.
2. Any two: formulas recalculate, what-if testing, templates, charts.
3. (9,600 − 1,200) ÷ 7 = **1,200**.
4. **Data loss**.
5. It restricts entry to people who know the password, and it identifies who made changes.
6. Hardware: external drive backup kept off-site (or a UPS). Software: scheduled automatic backup (or antivirus, or encryption).
7. **Confidentiality** (and integrity or professional behaviour would also apply).
8. Not letting bias, conflict of interest or pressure from others override professional judgement.
9. An auditor's report is relied on by people outside the business, such as lenders and investors. An honest report protects them even if the client dislikes the result.
10. 3 hours × 5 = **15 transactions**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "it is faster" or "it is more secure" with no reason.
- Describing a problem when the question asks for a method of protection, or the reverse.
- Naming a method without saying which issue it addresses.
- Answering an "apply" question in general terms without using the facts given.
- Saying a situation is "unethical" without naming the principle.
- Mixing up access (who gets in) and confidentiality (what is done with the information).
- Treating public interest as the employer's interest, or as only the interest of the client.
- Claiming that software removes all errors.
