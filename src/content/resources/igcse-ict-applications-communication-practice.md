## Section A

**1.** State one advantage of a poster over a website as a communication medium. **[1]**

**2.** State the key difference between media streaming and an ePublication, in terms of what happens to the data. **[2]**

**3.** State what VoIP stands for. **[1]**

**4.** Give one disadvantage of a website compared with a printed newsletter as a way of reaching an audience with no internet access. **[1]**

---

## Section B

**5.** A charity wants to publish its annual report so that supporters can read it in full even in areas with unreliable internet access.

**(a)** Recommend a suitable communication medium. **[1]**
**(b)** Justify your recommendation, referring to what happens to the data. **[3]**

**6.** A business currently makes all its international calls over the standard mobile network and wants to reduce costs by switching to VoIP.

**(a)** Explain why VoIP calls can be cheaper than standard mobile calls, especially internationally. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one risk the business accepts by making this switch. **[2]**

**7.** A local council is deciding between a printed newsletter and a website to inform residents about roadworks that change week to week.

**(a)** Recommend the more suitable medium. **[1]**
**(b)** Justify your recommendation. **[3]**

---

## Section C

**8.** A university wants to deliver an engaging welcome talk to new students at an in-person open day, and separately wants those same students to be able to review detailed course information independently at any time afterward.

Recommend a suitable communication medium for each of the two needs, and explain why a single medium would not serve both needs equally well. **[6]**

**9.** Explain why a video call can be considered an extension of VoIP technology, rather than a completely separate form of communication. **[3]**

---

## Worked answers

**1.** A poster can be viewed without needing a device or an internet connection. **[1]**

**2.** Media streaming plays the content as data arrives, without downloading the whole file first; an ePublication is downloaded as a complete file before (or as) it is used, after which no ongoing connection is needed. **[2]**

**3.** Voice over Internet Protocol. **[1]**

**4.** A website cannot be reached at all by someone without a suitable internet-connected device, whereas a printed newsletter can be read by anyone who receives a physical copy, regardless of internet access. **[1]**

**5. (a)** An ePublication (e.g. a downloadable PDF report). **[1]**
**(b)** An ePublication is downloaded as a complete file, so once it has been downloaded it can be read entirely offline; this suits supporters in areas with unreliable internet access, unlike a webpage or streamed content, which would need an ongoing connection to view. **[3]**

**6. (a)** VoIP carries voice as data over the business's existing internet connection rather than over a dedicated voice channel charged per minute by a mobile network operator, so international calls avoid the higher per-minute charges that standard mobile networks apply for international routing. **[2]**
**(b)** Call quality now depends on the reliability and available bandwidth of the internet connection at the time of the call; if the connection is poor or congested, the business risks delayed, dropped or lower-quality audio in a way a standard mobile voice call, which does not share bandwidth with other internet traffic, would not experience. **[2]**

**7. (a)** A website. **[1]**
**(b)** Roadworks information that changes week to week needs to be corrected and updated regularly, which a website supports (it can be updated at any time) but a printed newsletter cannot (it is fixed once distributed and would need a completely new print run for every change), making a website far more practical and cost-effective for frequently changing information. **[3]**

**8.** For the in-person welcome talk, a multimedia presentation is the more suitable choice, since it can combine text, images, audio and video to deliver an engaging, richer experience at a live event. For independent review of detailed course information afterward, a website is more suitable, since it can be updated at any time, holds far more content than a single presentation, and remains available for students to access whenever they choose rather than only at the original event. A single medium would not serve both needs equally well because a multimedia presentation is not practical for students to revisit independently afterward in the same way a website is, while a website alone would be a weaker choice for delivering an engaging, in-person experience at a welcome event, since it relies on the audience actively navigating it rather than experiencing a delivered presentation. **[6]** (Marks for correctly matching each need to a distinct, justified medium, and for explaining why one medium could not equally serve both purposes.)

**9.** A video call, like a standard VoIP voice call, carries its communication as data over the internet rather than over a dedicated telecommunications voice channel; it simply adds a live video feed alongside the audio data, using the same fundamental internet-based, data-carried approach rather than a fundamentally different underlying technology. This is why a video call shares VoIP's core characteristics, such as needing a sufficiently fast internet connection and depending on connection quality, rather than being a wholly separate category of communication. **[3]**
