These notes condense Criterion C (communicating) from the IB *Mathematics guide for the MYP* (first assessment 2022). Worked examples and an annotated rewrite are in the [Criterion C study guide](/resources/ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-c-communicating/); practise with the [Criterion C practice questions](/resources/ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-c-communicating-practice/). ## The criterion in one table

| Strand | Year 5 focus | In one phrase |
|---|---|---|
| i | Mathematical language in oral and written explanations | Right symbols, right words |
| ii | Appropriate forms of representation | Right picture for the information |
| iii | Moving between forms (not in Year 1) | Same facts, linked forms |
| iv | Complete, coherent and concise lines of reasoning | Every step, in order, nothing extra |
| v | Logical structure | A reader can follow and find things |

Maximum 8. Usually assessed alongside another criterion in the same task.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Notation | The symbols used to write mathematics (², √, ≤, ∠, ≈) |
| Terminology | The correct mathematical words (gradient, median, hypotenuse) |
| Form of representation | Words, table, graph, diagram, equation, chart |
| Complete | No needed step is missing |
| Coherent | Each step follows logically from the last |
| Concise | No unnecessary steps or repetition |
| Logical structure | Aim, information, method, results, conclusion in order |
| Defined variable | A letter whose meaning and units are stated before use |

## Notation rules to keep

- One equals sign per statement of equality; never chain unequal quantities.
- Powers as superscripts (x², 10⁻³), not ^ or calculator E notation.
- Use × or a dot for multiplication in numbers, not the letter x.
- Brackets to make fractions clear: 1/(2x) is not (1/2)x.
- ≈ or a stated degree of accuracy when a value is rounded.
- Units on every final answer and on axis labels.
- ∠ABC and ° for angles; AB for a length; AB with an arrow or bold for a vector where used.
- ≡ for an identity; = for an equation.

## Choosing a representation

| If you need to show | Use |
|---|---|
| Exact values for a few cases | Table with headed columns and units |
| A general relationship | Equation or formula with defined variables |
| A trend, maximum or rate | Graph with title, labels, units and even scale |
| Geometry | Labelled diagram, not to scale unless stated |
| Categorical data | Bar chart or pie chart |
| Distribution of numerical data | Histogram or box-and-whisker diagram |
| The answer to the question | A sentence in words |

## Moving between forms: what links them

| From | To | The link you should state |
|---|---|---|
| Words | Equation | What each variable stands for |
| Equation | Table | Values substituted for chosen inputs |
| Table | Graph | Each row is a plotted point |
| Graph | Equation | Gradient and intercept, or roots and symmetry |
| Any form | Words | What the result means in the situation |

Worked reminder: "A gym charges 30 to join and 12 per month." Equation C = 30 + 12m. Table m = 0, 1, 2, 3 gives C = 30, 42, 54, 66. Graph: a straight line, intercept 30 (joining fee), gradient 12 (monthly fee). Words: after 6 months the total is 102.

## Writing reasoning

- Start by naming the fact or method used ("Angles on a straight line add to 180°").
- One step per line, each following from the last.
- Use connectives: so, because, therefore, which means, hence.
- End with a sentence that answers the question.
- Remove rough work that does not lead anywhere.

## Exam traps

- Chained equals signs, such as 3 × 4 = 12 + 5 = 17.
- A graph without axis labels or units.
- A table with no headings.
- Changing a variable's letter, or never defining it.
- A correct final value with no concluding sentence.
- Using "it" or "the number" where a precise term is needed.
- Presenting two forms without explaining how they connect.
- Leaving out units after a conversion, so the reader cannot tell which unit the answer is in.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Expression vs equation vs identity**: no equals sign; true for some values; true for all values.
- **Representation vs moving between forms**: strand ii is choosing a form; strand iii is translating between forms with understanding.
- **Complete vs concise**: complete adds needed steps; concise removes unneeded ones. Year 5 needs both.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the five strands of Criterion C.
2. In which year is strand iii not assessed?
3. Rewrite 4.2E−3 in correct notation.
4. What is wrong with 7 + 5 = 12 × 2 = 24?
5. Which representation best shows the maximum of a quadratic model?
6. Give three things every graph needs.
7. Is 3(x + 2) ≡ 3x + 6 an equation or an identity?
8. A taxi charges 4 plus 1.5 per km. Write an equation for the cost C for d km, defining the variables.
9. What does the gradient of the taxi graph represent?
10. What does "concise" add to "complete and coherent" in Year 5?
11. State the five parts of a logically structured write-up.
12. Rewrite "x = 3.4641016" for a length given to 3 significant figures.

### Answers

1. Mathematical language; forms of representation; moving between forms; lines of reasoning; logical structure.
2. **Year 1**.
3. **4.2 × 10⁻³**.
4. 12 is not equal to 24; write 7 + 5 = 12, then 12 × 2 = 24.
5. A **graph** (or a table that shows the symmetry), followed by a sentence.
6. Any three of: a title, labelled axes, units, an even scale, a key for more than one line.
7. An **identity**: it is true for every value of x.
8. C = 4 + 1.5d, where C is the cost and d is the distance in km.
9. The **cost per km**, 1.5.
10. Nothing unnecessary is included.
11. Aim; information and assumptions; method; results; conclusion.
12. **x = 3.46** (3 s.f.), with the unit of length.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does handwriting or neatness count in Criterion C?**
What counts is whether a reader can follow your mathematics. Neat, ordered work helps, but the strands are about notation, representation, reasoning and structure, not style.

**Should I include rough work?**
Only if it is part of the reasoning. Rough calculations that lead nowhere make the line of reasoning harder to follow and work against conciseness.
