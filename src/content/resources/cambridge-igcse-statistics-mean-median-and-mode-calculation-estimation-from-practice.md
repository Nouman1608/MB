## Questions

**1.** The number of cars sold by a garage on nine days was 18, 23, 19, 23, 27, 21, 23, 30 and 16. Calculate the mean, and find the median and the mode. **[3]**

**2.** The mean of five numbers is 14. Four of the numbers are 11, 16, 9 and 18. Find the fifth number. **[3]**

**3.** A class has 15 girls with a mean test mark of 62 and 25 boys with a mean test mark of 54. Calculate the mean mark for all 40 students. **[3]**

**4.** The number of people in each of 25 cars passing a checkpoint is shown.

| People in car (x) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Number of cars (f) | 2 | k | 9 | 7 | 3 |

The mean number of people per car is 3.2.

**(a)** Find the value of k. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the median number of people per car. **[2]**

**5.** The shoe sizes of 30 members of a club are recorded.

| Shoe size | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | 11 | 8 | 5 | 2 |

Find the mean, the median and the mode. **[5]**

**6.** The number of words in each of 60 sentences from a magazine is summarised below.

| Words | 1–5 | 6–10 | 11–15 | 16–20 | 21–25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 15 | 22 | 11 | 6 |

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate an estimate of the mean number of words per sentence. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why your answer to part (b) is only an estimate. **[1]**

**7.** The masses of 80 parcels are recorded. The table shows the number of parcels with mass below each value.

| Mass m (kg) below | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 4 | 18 | 47 | 71 | 80 |

**(a)** How many parcels have a mass of 30 kg or more but less than 40 kg? **[1]**
**(b)** Use linear interpolation to estimate the median mass. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe how you would find the median from a cumulative frequency graph of this data. **[2]**

**8.** The times spent by 116 visitors on a website are grouped as shown.

| Time t (minutes) | 0 ≤ t < 1 | 1 ≤ t < 3 | 3 ≤ t < 6 | 6 ≤ t < 10 | 10 ≤ t < 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 18 | 30 | 36 | 20 | 12 |

**(a)** Which class has the highest frequency? **[1]**
**(b)** Find the modal class, showing your working. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate an estimate of the mean time. **[3]**

**9.** Eight houses on one road were sold for the following prices, in thousands of dollars: 118, 121, 124, 124, 127, 130, 133, 790.

**(a)** Calculate the mean price and find the median price. **[2]**
**(b)** An estate agent wants to describe the typical sale price on the road. Which average should be used? Give a reason using your answers to part (a). **[2]**
**(c)** A café owner records the flavour chosen by each customer and wants to decide which flavour to stock most of. Which average should be used, and why? **[2]**

**10.** The distances travelled to work by 50 employees are shown.

| Distance d (km) | 0 ≤ d < 5 | 5 ≤ d < 10 | 10 ≤ d < 20 | 20 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 14 | 19 | 12 | 5 |

**(a)** Find the modal class. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the median distance. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the mean cannot be estimated reliably from this table. **[1]**

**11.** A helpline records the length of 40 calls, t minutes.

| Time t (minutes) | 0 ≤ t < 10 | 10 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 30 | 30 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | p | 14 | 8 | 2 |

The estimated mean call length is 22.5 minutes.

**(a)** Find the value of p. **[4]**
**(b)** Estimate the median call length. **[3]**
**(c)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(d)** One more call is recorded, lasting 95 minutes. Find the new estimated mean and the new estimated median, and state which measure is affected more. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Sum = 18+23+19+23+27+21+23+30+16 = 200, so mean = 200 ÷ 9 = **22.2** [1]. Ordered: 16, 18, 19, 21, 23, 23, 23, 27, 30, so the 5th value gives median = **23** [1]. 23 appears three times, so mode = **23** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum before dividing, so a slip in the final digits can still be seen.

**2.** Total of five = 14 × 5 = 70 [1]. Total of four = 11+16+9+18 = 54 [1]. Fifth number = 70 − 54 = **16** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Mean given" questions are about totals. Writing mean × number of values first is the safest way to show the method.

**3.** Girls' total = 15 × 62 = 930 [1]. Boys' total = 25 × 54 = 1350 [1]. Combined mean = (930 + 1350) ÷ 40 = 2280 ÷ 40 = **57** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Averaging 62 and 54 to get 58 is a common wrong answer, because it ignores the different group sizes.

**4. (a)** Σf = 21 + k and Σfx = 2 + 2k + 27 + 28 + 15 = 72 + 2k [1]. (72 + 2k) ÷ (21 + k) = 3.2, so 72 + 2k = 67.2 + 3.2k [1]. 4.8 = 1.2k, so k = **4** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** n = 25, so the median is the 13th value [1]. Cumulative frequencies are 2, 6, 15, 22, 25, so the 13th value is in the x = 3 column. Median = **3** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write Σf and Σfx as expressions in k before forming the equation. Showing each one makes your method easy to follow.

**5.** Σfx = 20+66+56+40+18 = 200 [1], so mean = 200 ÷ 30 = **6.67** [1]. n = 30, so the median is halfway between the 15th and 16th values [1]. Cumulative frequencies are 4, 15, 23, 28, 30. The 15th value is 6 and the 16th is 7, so median = **6.5** [1]. Highest frequency is 11, so mode = **6** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The median of a table can fall between two columns. State which two positions you use.

**6. (a)** The highest frequency is 22, so the modal class is **11–15** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Midpoints are 3, 8, 13, 18 and 23 [1]. Σfx = 18+120+286+198+138 = 760 [1]. Mean ≈ 760 ÷ 60 = **12.7** words [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The actual number of words in each sentence is not known, so each sentence is assumed to be at the midpoint of its class [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation of "estimate" needs to mention that values inside a class are unknown or assumed. "It is not accurate" alone is too vague.

**7. (a)** 71 − 47 = **24** parcels [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** n/2 = 40, which lies in the class 20 ≤ m < 30, since 18 are below 20 and 47 are below 30 [1]. Median ≈ 20 + (40 − 18) ÷ 29 × 10 [1] = **27.6 kg** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Plot cumulative frequency against the upper class boundary and join the points [1]. Read across from 40 (half of 80) on the vertical axis to the curve, then down to read the mass on the horizontal axis[1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the upper boundary and half of the total. Reading at the wrong cumulative frequency is a common loss.

**8. (a)** The class **3 ≤ t < 6** has frequency 36 [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The class widths are unequal, so compare frequency density = frequency ÷ width [1]. The densities are 18, 15, 12, 5 and 1.2 [1]. The highest is 18, so the modal class is **0 ≤ t < 1** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Midpoints are 0.5, 2, 4.5, 8 and 15 [1]. Σfx = 9+60+162+160+180 = 571 [1]. Mean ≈ 571 ÷ 116 = **4.92 minutes** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A row of frequency densities is a clear way to justify the modal class. The class with the highest frequency answers a different question.

**9. (a)** Sum = 1667, so mean = 1667 ÷ 8 = **208.4** thousand dollars (to 1 d.p.) [1]. Ordered, the 4th and 5th values are 124 and 127, so median = **125.5** thousand dollars [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the **median** [1]. One sale of 790 pulls the mean to 208.4, which is higher than seven of the eight prices, whereas the median of 125.5 reflects a typical sale [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the **mode** [1], because the data is categorical, so the mean and median cannot be calculated, and the mode shows the most popular flavour [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason such as "not affected by extreme values" earns more when tied to the figures given, here 790 against the rest.

**10. (a)** The classes have different widths, so use frequency density. 0–5: 14 ÷ 5 = 2.8; 5–10: 19 ÷ 5 = 3.8; 10–20: 12 ÷ 10 = 1.2 [1]. The modal class is **5 ≤ d < 10** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** n/2 = 25; cumulative frequencies are 14, 33, 45, 50 so the median is in 5 ≤ d < 10 [1]. Median ≈ 5 + (25 − 14) ÷ 19 × 5 [1] = **7.89 km** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The last class has no upper limit, so it has no midpoint and the total distance cannot be estimated [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For an open-ended class, say the midpoint is unknown, not just that the mean is "not possible".

**11. (a)** Σf = 30 + p [1]. Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35, 45 give Σfx = 30 + 15p + 350 + 280 + 90 = 750 + 15p [1]. (750 + 15p) ÷ (30 + p) = 22.5, so 750 + 15p = 675 + 22.5p [1]. 75 = 7.5p, so p = **10** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** n = 40, so n/2 = 20; cumulative frequencies are 6, 16, 30, 38, 40, so the median is in 20 ≤ t < 30 [1]. Median ≈ 20 + (20 − 16) ÷ 14 × 10 [1] = **22.9 minutes** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The highest frequency is 14 and all widths are equal, so the modal class is **20 ≤ t < 30** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** New mean = (900 + 95) ÷ 41 = **24.3 minutes** (up 1.8) [1]. With n = 41, n/2 = 20.5, so new median ≈ 20 + (20.5 − 16) ÷ 14 × 10 = **23.2 minutes** (up 0.4) [1]. The mean is affected more, because it uses the size of every value and the median only depends on position [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In a comparison, give both new values, the size of each change, then a reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting the frequency (such as 22) as the mode instead of the value or class.
- Averaging two group means directly instead of using the totals.
- Using class limits (such as 6 and 10) instead of the midpoint 8, or dividing by the number of classes.
- Taking the median of a grouped table as the middle class instead of interpolating within it.
- Using n + 1 over 2 as the grouped position, or the wrong cumulative frequency before the class.
- Choosing the modal class by frequency when widths are unequal.
- Giving a grouped answer with no word "estimate".
- Justifying an average with a general statement that does not use the data in the question.
