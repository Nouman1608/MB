**These notes condense Specialist Option 1, Clinical Psychology, of Cambridge International A Level Psychology 9990 (syllabus for 2024 to 2026).** Each of the five topics has the same three-part shape: diagnose and measure, explain (biological and psychological), then treat and manage. Learn the grid below, the five key studies and the evaluation hooks, and you can answer any Paper 3 or Paper 4 question on this option.

For full explanations read the [Clinical Psychology study guide](/resources/a-level-psychology-clinical-psychology/) first. Test yourself afterwards with the [Clinical Psychology practice questions](/resources/a-level-psychology-clinical-psychology-practice/).

## What does the 9990 syllabus list for Clinical Psychology?

| Topic | Diagnosis and measure | Explanations | Treatments |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1.1 Schizophrenia | ICD-11 positive and negative symptoms; case study; delusions via VR (Freeman et al., 2003) | Genetic; dopamine hypothesis; cognitive | Typical and atypical antipsychotics; ECT; CBT (e.g. Sensky et al., 2000) |
| 1.2 Mood disorders | ICD-11 depressive disorder and bipolar disorder; Beck Depression Inventory | Biochemical; genetic (Oruč et al., 1997); Beck's cognitive theory; learned helplessness/attributional style (e.g. Seligman et al., 1988) | Tricyclics, MAOIs, SSRIs; Beck's cognitive restructuring; Ellis's REBT |
| 1.3 Impulse control | Kleptomania, pyromania, gambling disorder; K-SAS | Dopamine; positive reinforcement; Miller's feeling-state theory | Drugs vs placebo (Grant et al., 2008); covert sensitisation (e.g. Glover, 1985); imaginal desensitisation (e.g. Blaszczynski and Nower, 2003) |
| 1.4 Anxiety and fear | GAD, agoraphobia, blood-injection-injury phobia; GAD-7; BIPI (e.g. Mas et al., 2010) | Genetic (e.g. Öst, 1992); classical conditioning (Little Albert); psychodynamic (Little Hans) | Systematic desensitisation; CBT with applied tension (Chapman and DeLapp, 2013) |
| 1.5 OCD | Obsessions and compulsions (e.g. Rapoport, 1989); MOCI; Y-BOCS | Biochemical; genetic; cognitive; operant; psychodynamic | SSRIs; ERP (e.g. Lehmkuhl et al., 2008); telephone CBT (Lovell et al., 2006) |

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Positive symptom | An addition to normal experience, e.g. hallucinations, delusions |
| Negative symptom | A loss of normal function, e.g. avolition, alogia, flat affect |
| Delusion of persecution | A false, fixed belief that others intend harm |
| Dopamine hypothesis | Excess dopamine activity or D2 sensitivity causes positive symptoms of schizophrenia |
| Negative triad | Beck: negative views of self, world and future |
| Attributional style | Habitual way of explaining events; depressive style is internal, stable, global |
| Manic episode | At least one week of euphoria or irritability with raised activity or energy |
| Kleptomania | Repeated failure to resist urges to steal items not needed for use or money |
| Pyromania | Repeated fire-setting without an obvious motive, with tension before and fascination with fire |
| Feeling-state | Miller: an intense positive feeling fixed to a specific behaviour |
| Covert sensitisation | Pairing an imagined act with an imagined aversive outcome |
| Applied tension | Tensing large muscles to raise blood pressure and prevent fainting |
| Reciprocal inhibition | Two incompatible states (relaxation and fear) cannot occur together |
| Obsession / compulsion | Intrusive unwanted thought / repetitive act performed to reduce anxiety |
| ERP | Exposure to a trigger while the compulsion is prevented |

## Which measures do you need, and how do they work?

| Measure | Disorder | Format | Scoring |
|---|---|---|---|
| Beck Depression Inventory | Depression | 21-item self-report | 0–3 per item; higher = more severe |
| K-SAS | Kleptomania | Self-report, past week | Higher = more severe urges and stealing |
| GAD-7 | Generalised anxiety | 7-item self-report, last two weeks | 0–3 per item; total 0–21 |
| BIPI | Blood-injection phobia | Self-report inventory | Cognitive, physiological, behavioural responses |
| MOCI | OCD | 30 true/false items | Checking, cleaning, slowness, doubting |
| Y-BOCS | OCD | 10-item clinician-rated scale | 0–4 per item; total 0–40 |

All of these produce **quantitative** data that can be compared before and after treatment. Self-report scales risk **social desirability** bias; clinician-rated scales such as the Y-BOCS reduce this but depend on inter-rater reliability.

## Key studies at a glance

| Study | Method and sample | Main finding | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freeman et al. (2003) | Pilot study; 24 non-clinical participants in a neutral VR library | Some had persecutory thoughts about avatars; these people had higher interpersonal sensitivity and anxiety | Standardised setting, but small non-clinical sample |
| Oruč et al. (1997) | Genetic association; 42 bipolar I patients, 40 controls | No significant association overall; trends for 5-HT2C and 5-HTT genes in women | Only a minor susceptibility effect, so reductionist to call it the cause |
| Grant et al. (2008) | Data from two double-blind placebo trials; 284 gamblers | Family history of alcoholism best predicted response to opiate antagonists | Placebo control and double-blind design support validity |
| Chapman and DeLapp (2013) | Case study; one adult; nine CBT sessions with applied tension | Fear and avoidance reduced over treatment | Rich detail but cannot generalise from one person |
| Lovell et al. (2006) | Randomised non-inferiority trial; 72 OCD patients; ten sessions | Telephone ERP as effective as face to face at six months | Real patients and random allocation; therapists not blind |

## Must-know comparisons

- **Typical vs atypical antipsychotics**: both reduce positive symptoms; typicals block D2 and carry more movement side effects; atypicals also act on serotonin and may help negative symptoms.
- **Tricyclics vs MAOIs vs SSRIs**: tricyclics block reuptake of serotonin and noradrenaline; MAOIs stop the enzyme breaking down monoamines (diet restrictions); SSRIs block serotonin reuptake only and are usually better tolerated.
- **Beck vs Ellis**: Beck targets automatic negative thoughts by testing evidence; Ellis disputes irrational beliefs, especially "musts", using the ABC model.
- **Covert sensitisation vs imaginal desensitisation**: the first adds an aversive image; the second adds relaxation while imagining resisting the urge.
- **Little Albert vs Little Hans**: both case studies of children; one behavioural (conditioning in a lab), one psychodynamic (interpretation via the father's reports).
- **SSRIs vs ERP**: drugs act fast and need little effort but symptoms may return after stopping; ERP is demanding and initially distressing but teaches a lasting skill.

## Mini essay plan: "Evaluate explanations of schizophrenia" [10]

1. **Genetic**: twin and family evidence (Gottesman, 1991); but concordance is below 100%, so nature and nurture interact.
2. **Dopamine hypothesis**: drug evidence supports it, but it is reductionist and cannot easily explain negative symptoms.
3. **Cognitive**: explains hallucinations as misattributed inner speech; but does not say what causes the fault (could be biological).
4. **Determinism**: biological explanations reduce blame but imply limited control.
5. **Usefulness**: each explanation leads to a treatment (antipsychotics, CBT), so a combined approach is most useful.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Writing DSM criteria from memory when the question says ICD-11; use ICD-11 wording, though Cambridge accepts other terms used appropriately.
- Confusing **negative symptoms** with "bad" symptoms; negative means a loss.
- Saying Oruč et al. "found the gene for depression"; they found only trends in women, in a bipolar I sample.
- Describing Grant et al. as a single trial; it pooled two trials of two different drugs.
- Describing ERP as "facing your fears" without the **response prevention** part.
- In a 10-mark evaluation, listing strengths and weaknesses without a conclusion or comparison.

## Quick self-test

1. State the minimum duration for a depressive episode in ICD-11.
2. Name the three types of antidepressant on the syllabus.
3. What three things does Beck's negative triad refer to?
4. What is the difference between kleptomania and ordinary theft?
5. Which variable best predicted response to opiate antagonists in Grant et al. (2008)?
6. Why does blood-injection-injury phobia need applied tension rather than relaxation?
7. What maximum score can the Y-BOCS give?
8. How many patients took part in Lovell et al. (2006)?

**Answers:** (1) two weeks; (2) tricyclics, MAOIs, SSRIs; (3) self, world, future; (4) items are not needed for personal use or money, and there is tension before and relief after; (5) a family history of alcoholism; (6) because people often faint from a drop in blood pressure, and tension raises it; (7) 40; (8) 72.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much detail do I need on the "e.g." studies?**
Enough to describe the aim, method and main result in a few sentences. The five key studies need full detail because they can be asked about directly.

**Can I use the same evaluation points for every disorder?**
The issues are similar (nature–nurture, reductionism, determinism), but examiners reward points applied to the specific disorder and study. Name the evidence each time.
