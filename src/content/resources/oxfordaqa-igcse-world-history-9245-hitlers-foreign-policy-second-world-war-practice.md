Content tested: "Increasing international tensions, 1933-38" and "Appeasement, the outbreak of war and the global conflict 1938-41".

## Section A: knowledge (3-mark questions)

**1.** Give three ways in which Hitler broke the Treaty of Versailles between 1935 and 1936. **[3]**

**2.** Give three features of the Munich Agreement of 1938. **[3]**

**3.** Give three features of the Nazi-Soviet Pact of August 1939. **[3]**

**4.** Give three features of German and Italian involvement in the Spanish Civil War. **[3]**

## Section B: explanation and judgement

**5.** Explain one way in which the Anglo-German Naval Agreement of 1935 affected relations between the western powers. **[6]**

**6.** Explain one way in which the remilitarisation of the Rhineland affected Hitler's foreign policy. **[6]**

**7.** Explain one way in which the Munich Agreement affected the attitude of the USSR towards Britain and France. **[6]**

**8.** Explain two reasons why Britain followed a policy of appeasement in the 1930s. **[9]**

**9.** Explain two reasons why Britain and France guaranteed the independence of Poland in 1939. **[9]**

**10.** "The Nazi-Soviet Pact was the main reason war broke out in September 1939." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**11.** "The failure of the League of Nations was the main reason why Hitler was able to carry out his foreign policy in the years 1933-38." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**12.** "The Anschluss of 1938 was the most important step towards the outbreak of war in 1939." How far do you agree? **[12]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** 3-mark questions: [1] per relevant point. 6-mark: way identified [1], precise knowledge [2], explanation of how it affected the development [2], further developed detail or consequence [1]. 9-mark: each of two ways or reasons [4] (identified [1], precise knowledge [1], explained link [2]) plus [1] for accurate, well-sequenced detail. 12-mark: the given factor [3], at least one other factor [4], comparison in a line of reasoning [3], supported judgement [2].

**1.** Any three, [1] each: he announced conscription in March 1935; he revealed the existence of the Luftwaffe; he expanded the army far beyond 100,000 men; he built a navy that Versailles had forbidden; he remilitarised the Rhineland in March 1936; he began open rearmament.

**2.** Any three, [1] each: it was agreed on 29-30 September 1938 by Britain, France, Germany and Italy; the Sudetenland was given to Germany; Czechoslovakia was not invited; the USSR was not invited; Hitler promised that he had no more territorial demands in Europe; Chamberlain and Hitler signed a declaration of friendship.

**3.** Any three, [1] each: it was signed on 23 August 1939; Germany and the USSR promised not to attack each other; it contained secret terms to divide Poland between them; the Baltic states were to be divided into spheres of influence; it ended the fear of a war on two fronts for Hitler; it was a surprise to Britain and France.

**4.** Any three, [1] each: Germany sent aircraft and the Condor Legion to help Franco; Italy sent troops and aircraft; the bombing of Guernica in 1937; the powers tested new weapons and tactics; it brought Hitler and Mussolini closer together; Britain and France followed non-intervention.

**5.** Way identified [1]: it divided Britain from France and Italy. Precise knowledge [2]: in June 1935 Britain agreed that Germany could build a navy up to 35 per cent of the size of the Royal Navy, without consulting France or Italy, a few weeks after the Stresa Front. Explanation [2]: France and Italy felt that Britain was putting its own interests first and accepting a breach of Versailles, so the Stresa Front lost its meaning and Hitler saw that the western powers could be divided. Further development [1]: Mussolini moved closer to Germany later in 1935-36.

**6.** Way identified [1]: it encouraged Hitler to take bigger risks. Precise knowledge [2]: in March 1936 German troops entered the Rhineland, in breach of Versailles and the Locarno Treaties; France did not act without British support, and Britain did not oppose it. Explanation [2]: the lack of resistance showed that Britain and France would not use force, so Hitler felt more able to demand the Anschluss and the Sudetenland. Further development [1]: Germany began to fortify the Rhineland, making a French attack on Germany less likely and weakening France's allies in eastern Europe.

**7.** Way identified [1]: it increased Soviet mistrust of Britain and France. Precise knowledge [2]: the USSR was not invited to the Munich Conference, though it had a treaty with Czechoslovakia; Britain and France agreed to the Sudetenland going to Germany. Explanation [2]: Stalin concluded that the West was prepared to give way to Hitler and might even direct him eastwards, so he could not rely on an alliance with them. Further development [1]: this made the Nazi-Soviet Pact in August 1939 more attractive to Stalin.

**8.** Reason one [4]: fear of another war [1]; many people remembered the casualties of 1914-18 and feared the bombing of cities [1]; so there was strong public support for avoiding war, and politicians such as Chamberlain believed that reasonable demands should be met [2]. Reason two [4]: Britain's weak rearmament [1]; its armed forces were not ready in 1938, and its empire needed protection in several places [1]; so appeasement bought time to rearm, for example for building radar and fighters [2]. Accurate, well-sequenced detail [1], for example the Munich Conference in 1938 or the view that Versailles was too harsh.

**9.** Reason one [4]: Hitler had broken the Munich Agreement [1]; in March 1939 he occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia, which was not a case of uniting Germans [1]; so the guarantee aimed to show that Britain would resist further aggression, since appeasement had failed [2]. Reason two [4]: public opinion and Poland's position [1]; the British public no longer trusted Hitler, and Poland was the next target, with demands for Danzig and the Polish Corridor [1]; so Chamberlain's government felt it had to draw a line, and the guarantee, on 31 March 1939, was meant to deter Hitler [2]. Accurate, well-sequenced detail [1], for example France's support and the guarantees to Romania and Greece.

**10.** Given factor [3]: the Pact of 23 August 1939 removed the fear of a war on two fronts; it divided Poland secretly; Hitler invaded on 1 September, a week later. Other factors [4]: Hitler's aims for Lebensraum; appeasement; the failure of the League; the Polish guarantee and Poland's refusal to give up Danzig; the ending of the Munich agreement. Comparison [3]: the Pact was the immediate cause of the timing, because it made invasion safe, but Hitler's aims were the underlying cause and he had planned for Poland before the Pact; the pact itself was a result of failed talks with the West. Judgement [2]: for example, the Pact allowed war in 1939, but it was not the reason for the war, because the underlying causes were Hitler's aims and the weaknesses of the western powers.

**11.** Given factor [3]: the League could not stop Japan in Manchuria and Italy in Abyssinia; Germany left it in 1933; the League had no army and relied on Britain and France. Other factors [4]: Hitler's skill and the weakness of the western powers; appeasement; the legacy of Versailles; the Depression; Britain's and France's rearmament; the support of Mussolini. Comparison [3]: the League was a weak institution, but it was the great powers that decided what to do, so the policies of Britain and France explain more than the League itself. Judgement [2]: for example, the League's failure removed a barrier, but appeasement and the unwillingness of Britain and France to act were more important.

**12.** Given factor [3]: the Anschluss in March 1938 broke Versailles; Austria's resources and strategic position helped Germany; it surrounded Czechoslovakia; the western powers only protested. Other factors [4]: the Rhineland in 1936; Munich and the Sudetenland; the occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939; the Nazi-Soviet Pact; the Polish crisis. Comparison [3]: the Anschluss showed that Hitler would take what he wanted and led to the Sudetenland crisis, but Munich and the taking of Prague had more effect because they ended appeasement and led to the Polish guarantee. Judgement [2]: for example, the Anschluss was a major step because it encouraged Hitler and made Czechoslovakia vulnerable, but the events of March 1939 were the turning point that made war likely.
