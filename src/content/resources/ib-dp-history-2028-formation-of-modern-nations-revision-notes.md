**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. Every question on this study asks about **one country**. The notes use the USA, with Argentina and Canada as comparisons. For fuller explanations see the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-formation-of-modern-nations/), and test yourself with the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-formation-of-modern-nations-practice/).

## Key terms

- **Neocolonialism / dependency**: economic reliance on foreign capital and markets that limits independent development.
- **Progressivism**: reform movement to regulate business and improve government and social conditions.
- **Social Darwinism**: applying "survival of the fittest" to society.
- **Nativism**: favouring native-born people over immigrants.
- **Indigenismo**: movement valuing Indigenous culture and seeking reform, mostly led by non-Indigenous thinkers.
- **Manifest destiny**: belief in the US right to expand across the continent.
- **Jim Crow**: system of racial segregation in the US South.
- **Great Migration**: movement of Black Americans from the South to northern cities.

## Economic transformation

- Railways: about 30,000 miles (1860) to about 190,000 (1900) in the US. They linked markets and required steel, coal and capital.
- Industry and cities: Carnegie, Rockefeller, Ford. More than half of Americans were urban by 1920. Slums, disease and pollution as costs.
- Trade: Argentine beef and wheat to Britain; Brazilian coffee; US search for markets after 1890.
- Dependency: reliance on a few exports and foreign capital. Two views: a constraint, or a successful growth model.

## Ideas

| Idea | Effect |
|---|---|
| Liberalism | Free trade and property rights |
| Progressivism | Regulation and reform (TR, La Follette, Wilson) |
| Social Darwinism | Defended inequality, empire and restriction |
| Expansionism | Frontier settlement; overseas ambitions in the 1890s |
| Indigenismo | Valued Indigenous culture, often led from outside |
| Nativism | Quotas (1924); Klan revival after 1915 |

## Politics and society

- Reforms: 16th to 19th Amendments; Pendleton Act; Sáenz Peña law.
- Parties: US two-party system, Populists (1892); Argentine Radicals and Yrigoyen (1916).
- Suffrage: US women 1920; Canadian women 1917 to 1918; Argentine women 1947. Black Americans in the South were barred by tests and violence.
- Arts: realism, Chicago architecture, jazz, Harlem Renaissance.

## Leaders

- Theodore Roosevelt: trust regulation, conservation.
- Wilson: tariff and banking reform, Federal Reserve.
- Du Bois and Booker T. Washington: competing strategies for Black progress.
- Yrigoyen: mobilised middle-class and immigrant voters.
- Macdonald: National Policy of railways, tariffs and settlement.

## Challenges

| Challenge | Examples |
|---|---|
| Inequality | Concentrated wealth; low wages; farmers' debt |
| Marginalised groups | Segregation, lynching, exclusion, land loss |
| Labour conflict | Haymarket, Homestead, Pullman, 1919 steel strike; Tragic Week; Winnipeg |
| Internal conflict | Red Scare, race violence (Tulsa, 1921) |

## Debates

- Industrial growth versus political reform as the driver of change.
- Progressivism: democratising or limited?
- Dependency: constraint or excuse?
- Turner's frontier thesis and its critics.

## Essay skeleton

1. Name your country and answer the question in one line.
2. Use two or three factors, ranked.
3. Show who gained and who lost.
4. Bring in a perspective.
5. Conclude with a judgement.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent did economic transformation shape the formation of one modern nation between 1860 and 1929?"

1. Choose the country (for example the USA) and state a line: economic change was the base, but politics and ideas decided who benefited.
2. Economic evidence: railways (about 30,000 miles in 1860 to about 190,000 in 1900), steel and oil, urbanisation, immigrant labour.
3. Political and social evidence: Progressive reforms, the 19th Amendment, Jim Crow, labour conflict.
4. Ideas: Social Darwinism and nativism versus Progressivism.
5. Perspective: Turner's frontier thesis and its critics; dependency arguments if you use Argentina.
6. Judgement: economic change set the pace, but the type of nation depended on political choices and on who was excluded.

## Common mistakes

- Several countries, thin evidence.
- Ignoring marginalised groups.
- Equating change with progress for everyone.

## Self-test

1. What did the Dawes Act do?
2. Give two features of Social Darwinism.
3. What was the Sáenz Peña law?
4. Name two labour conflicts in the US.
5. What does "dependency" mean?
6. Give one reason why women's suffrage came later in Argentina than in the USA.

**Answers:** 1. Broke up communal Native land into individual allotments. 2. Applied "survival of the fittest" to society; used to defend inequality, empire and restriction. 3. A 1912 law with secret, compulsory male suffrage. 4. Any two: Haymarket (1886), Homestead (1892), Pullman (1894), steel strike (1919). 5. Reliance on foreign capital and a few exports that limits independent development. 6. For example, the conservative elite and the Church resisted, and the women's movement had less access to the political system; Argentina's reforms focused first on male voters.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I mix countries?**
Choose one for depth. You can bring in others for comparison if the question allows.

**Which concept should I use?**
Continuity and change is most useful, because it forces you to say who changed.
