These questions practise Criterion A (knowing and understanding) from the IB *Mathematics guide for the MYP* (first assessment 2022). Section A has familiar, single-method problems. Section B has problems that hide their method or combine topics, which is what the top band asks for. The marks are Marlbridge practice tariffs that show how much working each question needs. Read the [Criterion A study guide](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-a-knowing-and-understanding/) and [revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-myp-mathematics-myp-mathematics-ib-myp-mathematics-assessment-criteria-a-knowing-and-understanding-revision-notes/) first. Use a calculator where needed and give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Questions

### Section A: familiar problems

**1.** **(a)** Write 0.000 405 in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate (3.2 × 10⁵) × (4 × 10⁻²), giving your answer in standard form. **[2]**

**2.** Solve 5(2x − 3) = 3x + 20. **[3]**

**3.** Flour and sugar are mixed in the ratio 5 : 2. The mixture has a mass of 840 g. Find the mass of flour. **[2]**

**4.** Find the size of one interior angle of a regular 12-sided polygon. **[2]**

**5.** The mean of the five numbers 6, 9, x, 14 and 11 is 10. Find x. **[2]**

**6.** After a 15% increase, the price of a ticket is 69. Find the price before the increase. **[2]**

### Section B: unfamiliar and combined problems

**7.** A ladder 6.5 m long leans against a vertical wall. Its top is 6.0 m above the ground. How far is the foot of the ladder from the wall? **[2]**

**8.** A closed cylindrical can has radius 4.0 cm and volume 704 cm³.
**(a)** Find the height of the can. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total surface area of the can. **[3]**

**9.** A theatre sells adult and child tickets. Three adult and four child tickets cost 86. Two adult and five child tickets cost 76. Find the cost of each type of ticket. **[4]**

**10.** A ramp rises 0.45 m over a horizontal distance of 5.4 m.
**(a)** Find the angle the ramp makes with the horizontal. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the ramp's sloping surface. **[2]**

**11.** A bag holds 4 red counters and 6 blue counters. Two counters are taken out at random, one after the other, without replacement. Find the probability that both counters are the same colour. **[4]**

**12.** A square photograph of side x cm is mounted on a square card so that there is a border 3 cm wide all the way round. The card has area 400 cm².
**(a)** Find x. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the area of the border. **[1]**

**13.** A car is bought for 18 000. Its value falls by 12% each year.
**(a)** Find its value after 4 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of complete years after which its value first falls below half the price paid. **[3]**

**14.** Pipe P alone fills a tank in 6 hours. Pipe R alone fills the same tank in 9 hours. Both pipes are opened together. How long does the tank take to fill? Give your answer in hours and minutes. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** **4.05 × 10⁻⁴** [1]
**(b)** 3.2 × 4 = 12.8 and 10⁵ × 10⁻² = 10³, giving 12.8 × 10³ [1] = **1.28 × 10⁴** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* 12.8 × 10³ is not in standard form, because the first number must be at least 1 and less than 10.

**2.** 10x − 15 = 3x + 20 [1]. 7x = 35 [1]. **x = 5** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Check: 5(10 − 3) = 35 and 15 + 20 = 35. Expanding the bracket on only the first term (10x − 3) is the usual slip.

**3.** One part is 840 ÷ 7 = 120 g [1]. Flour = 5 × 120 = **600 g** [1] **[2]**

**4.** Exterior angle = 360° ÷ 12 = 30° [1]. Interior angle = 180° − 30° = **150°** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The route through the exterior angle is shorter than (12 − 2) × 180° ÷ 12, though both give 150°.

**5.** Total = 5 × 10 = 50 [1]. x = 50 − (6 + 9 + 14 + 11) = 50 − 40 = **10** [1] **[2]**

**6.** 69 is 115% of the original, so the original is 69 ÷ 1.15 [1] = **60** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Subtracting 15% of 69 gives 58.65, which is wrong because 15% was taken of the original, not of 69. Check: 60 × 1.15 = 69.

**7.** Distance² = 6.5² − 6.0² = 42.25 − 36 = 6.25 [1]. Distance = **2.5 m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The ladder is the hypotenuse, so subtract. Adding the squares gives a distance longer than the ladder, which a context check rules out.

**8.** **(a)** 704 = π × 4.0² × h [1], so h = 704 ÷ (16π) = **14.0 cm** [1]
**(b)** Two circles: 2π × 4.0² = 100.5 cm² [1]. Curved surface: 2π × 4.0 × 14.006 = 352.0 cm² [1]. Total = **453 cm²** (452.5) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Closed" means both ends count. Use the unrounded height; with h = 14 exactly the total is 452.4, which still rounds to 452 rather than 453, so carrying full accuracy matters here.

**9.** Let a and c be the adult and child prices: 3a + 4c = 86 and 2a + 5c = 76 [1]. Multiply by 2 and 3: 6a + 8c = 172 and 6a + 15c = 228 [1]. Subtract: 7c = 56, so **c = 8** [1]. Then 3a + 32 = 86, so **a = 18** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Defining the letters is part of selecting the method. Check in the second equation: 36 + 40 = 76.

**10.** **(a)** tan θ = 0.45 ÷ 5.4 [1], so **θ = 4.76°** [1]
**(b)** Length = √(5.4² + 0.45²) [1] = √29.3625 = **5.42 m** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The rise and horizontal distance are opposite and adjacent to the angle, so tangent is the ratio to select. In (b), a length only slightly more than 5.4 m is what a shallow ramp should give.

**11.** P(both red) = (4/10) × (3/9) = 12/90 [1]. P(both blue) = (6/10) × (5/9) = 30/90 [1]. These are mutually exclusive, so add [1]: 42/90 = **7/15** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Without replacement, the second denominator is 9. Using 10 again treats the counter as returned.

**12.** **(a)** The card's side is x + 6 [1]. (x + 6)² = 400, so x + 6 = 20 (the side is positive) [1]. **x = 14** [1]
**(b)** 400 − 14² = 400 − 196 = **204 cm²** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The border adds 3 cm on each side, so 6 cm in total. Using x + 3 is the most frequent error.

**13.** **(a)** 18 000 × 0.88⁴ [1] = **10 800** (10 794.52) [1]
**(b)** Half the price is 9000; need 0.88ⁿ < 0.5 [1]. 0.88⁵ = 0.528, giving 9499, which is above 9000 [1]. 0.88⁶ = 0.464, giving 8359, which is below, so **6 years** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A loss of 12% each year is not 48% after 4 years; the multiplier 0.88 is applied each year. Showing the values either side of the target justifies the whole number.

**14.** In one hour the pipes fill 1/6 + 1/9 of the tank [1] = 5/18 [1]. Time = 18/5 = 3.6 hours = **3 hours 36 minutes** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Averaging 6 and 9 hours gives 7.5 hours, which is longer than pipe P alone and cannot be right. Working with the fraction filled per hour is the method to select.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Undoing a percentage change by adding or subtracting the percentage of the new value.
- Adding instead of subtracting squares when the hypotenuse is known.
- Forgetting one of the two circular ends of a closed cylinder.
- Rounding a height or radius before using it again.
- Replacing counters by mistake in a "without replacement" problem.
- Using x + 3 instead of x + 6 for a border on both sides.
- Treating repeated percentage change as simple multiplication of the rate by the number of years.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are some of these questions marked as unfamiliar?**
They do not tell you which mathematics to use. You have to recognise, for example, that a mounted photograph leads to a quadratic or that two filling pipes call for rates, which is the selecting skill that strand i of Criterion A describes.

**How should I check an answer to a problem like these?**
Substitute back, estimate with rounded figures or apply the reverse operation. For the ticket problem, putting a = 18 and c = 8 into the equation you did not use confirms both values.
