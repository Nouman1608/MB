These questions cover sub-topic 2.2, Size of specimens, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2 (the formula and calculations in millimetres) are Core. Questions on outcome 3, converting between millimetres and micrometres, are labelled **(Extended)**, and a Core candidate can skip them. All measurements are invented, and images are described in words.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which expression gives the actual size of a specimen? **[1]**

A: image size × magnification

B: image size ÷ magnification

C: magnification ÷ image size

D: magnification − image size

**2.** State the formula that links magnification, image size and actual size. **[1]**

**3.** A drawing of a beetle is 84 mm long. The beetle is 12 mm long. Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**

**4.** A photograph taken at ×250 shows a cell 35 mm long. Calculate the actual length of the cell in mm. **[2]**

**5.** A cell is 0.06 mm wide. Calculate its width in a photograph taken at ×400. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** A micrograph has a scale bar labelled 0.2 mm. On the page the scale bar measures 30 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the micrograph. **[2]**

**(b)** An organism in the same micrograph measures 54 mm long. Calculate its actual length. **[2]**

**7.** A student draws a flower. The drawing is 96 mm wide and the flower is 24 mm wide.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** The student writes the answer as "×4 mm". Explain the error. **[1]**

**8.** Six cells lie end to end across 42 mm of a photograph taken at ×350. Calculate the mean actual length of one cell, in mm. **[3]**

**9.** An image of a cell is 50 mm long. The actual cell is 0.25 mm long. A student writes: "magnification = 0.25 ÷ 50 = 0.005".

**(a)** Identify the student's error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct magnification. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Convert:

**(a)** 0.35 mm to μm **[1]**

**(b)** 7500 μm to mm. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* An electron micrograph shows a bacterium 18 mm long. The actual bacterium is 2 μm long. Calculate the magnification. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A drawing of a cell is 45 mm long at ×1500. Calculate the actual length of the cell in μm. **[3]**

**13.** *(Extended)* A cheek cell is 60 μm across. Calculate how wide it would appear, in mm, in an image at ×500. **[3]**

**14.** In question 8 the student measured six cells together instead of one cell. Explain why this gives a more reliable value for the length of one cell. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Actual size = image size ÷ magnification. **[1]**

**2.** Magnification = image size ÷ actual size [1]. **[1]**

**3.** 84 ÷ 12 [1] = **×7** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Actual size = 35 ÷ 250 [1] = **0.14 mm** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Image size = 400 × 0.06 [1] = **24 mm** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 30 ÷ 0.2 [1] = **×150** [1].

**(b)** 54 ÷ 150 [1] = **0.36 mm** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b), use the magnification you found in (a). If (a) is wrong, a correct method in (b) can still earn the marks (error carried forward).

**7. (a)** 96 ÷ 24 [1] = **×4** [1].

**(b)** Magnification is a ratio of two lengths, so it has no units; it should be written as ×4 [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Actual length of six cells = 42 ÷ 350 [1] = 0.12 mm [1]. One cell = 0.12 ÷ 6 = **0.02 mm** [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** The student divided actual size by image size, the wrong way round [1].

**(b)** 50 ÷ 0.25 [1] = **×200** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A magnification below 1 for a magnified image is a signal to check the order of division.

**10. (a)** 0.35 × 1000 = **350 μm** [1].

**(b)** 7500 ÷ 1000 = **7.5 mm** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Convert the image to μm: 18 mm × 1000 = 18 000 μm [1]. Magnification = 18 000 ÷ 2 [1] = **×9000** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 18 ÷ 2 = ×9 is a common wrong answer; the units were not made the same.

**12.** Actual length = 45 ÷ 1500 [1] = 0.03 mm [1]. 0.03 × 1000 = **30 μm** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Image size = 500 × 60 [1] = 30 000 μm [1]. 30 000 ÷ 1000 = **30 mm** [1]. **[3]**

**14.** A single small cell is hard to measure precisely with a ruler, so the measuring error is large compared with its length [1]. Measuring across six cells gives a longer length, so the same ruler error is a smaller proportion, and dividing by six gives a mean that allows for differences between cells [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing in the wrong order, giving a magnification below 1.
- Adding units to a magnification, or leaving out the × sign.
- Mixing mm and μm in one division.
- Skipping the working: each step can earn a method mark.
- Forgetting to divide by the number of cells when several are measured together.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I lose marks if my ruler measurement is slightly different?**
Examiners usually allow a small range for measurements, so write your measured value down and use it consistently.

**Can a Core question give a size in micrometres?**
Core calculations use millimetres; converting between millimetres and micrometres is Extended content.
