These questions cover section **3.3.8 Aldehydes and ketones** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry
specification (7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards. The whole
section is **A-level only**. It is examined on A-level Paper 2, and Paper 3 can test any content. Questions 7,
10 and 11 also draw on alcohols, optical isomerism and organic analysis.

Learn the ideas first in the [aldehydes and ketones study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-aldehydes-and-ketones/)
and the [aldehydes and ketones revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-aldehydes-and-ketones-revision-notes/).

H = 1.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Cu = 63.5, Ag = 107.9.

## Questions

**1.** Explain why the carbon atom of a carbonyl group is attacked by nucleophiles. **[2]**

**2.** 2-methylpropanal is warmed with Tollens' reagent.

**(a)** State what is observed. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the organic product, and state the role of 2-methylpropanal in this reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why butanone gives no reaction with Tollens' reagent. **[1]**

**3.** Write an overall equation, using [H], for each reduction by NaBH₄ in aqueous solution. Name each
organic product.

**(a)** pentan-3-one **[2]**
**(b)** 3-methylbutanal **[2]**

**4.** Outline the mechanism for the reduction of propanal by NaBH₄ in aqueous solution. Show the nucleophile
as H⁻. **[4]**

**5.** Write an overall equation, using HCN, for the formation of a hydroxynitrile from each compound. Name each
product.

**(a)** propanal **[2]**
**(b)** pentan-3-one **[2]**

**6.** Potassium cyanide is used, followed by dilute acid, to make hydroxynitriles.

**(a)** State two hazards of using KCN in this reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one reason why KCN is used rather than hydrogen cyanide. **[1]**

**7.** Butanone reacts with KCN followed by dilute acid.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism for this reaction. **[4]**
**(b)** The product is a mixture that has no effect on plane-polarised light. Explain why. **[3]**

**8.** A student warms 2.16 g of butanal (Mr 72.0) with an excess of a mild oxidising agent.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of silver formed if the reagent is Tollens'. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of copper(I) oxide formed if the reagent is Fehling's solution. Assume one
mole of Cu₂O forms per mole of butanal. **[2]**

**9.** 4.40 g of ethanal is converted into 2-hydroxypropanenitrile, CH₃CH(OH)CN, using KCN followed by dilute
acid. The student obtains 5.33 g of product. Calculate the percentage yield. **[4]**

**10.** X, Y and Z are isomers with the formula C₄H₈O. Each contains a C=O group and no C=C bond.

- X and Y each give a red precipitate when warmed with Fehling's solution. Z does not.
- Reduction of X with NaBH₄ gives an alcohol with an unbranched chain.

**(a)** Identify X, Y and Z by name. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the name of the product when Z is reduced by NaBH₄, and state whether this product is formed as a
racemic mixture. Explain your answer. **[3]**
**(c)** The IR spectrum of the reduction product of Y shows a broad absorption between 3230 and 3550 cm⁻¹ and
no absorption between 1680 and 1750 cm⁻¹. Using these ranges, state what each feature shows. **[2]**

**11.** Describe a two-step synthesis of 2-hydroxybutanenitrile from propan-1-ol. For each step give the
reagent(s) and conditions, the organic product, and an equation (use [O] for the oxidising agent and HCN for
the overall second step). **[5]**

**12.** Methanal, propanone and pentan-2-one each react with KCN followed by dilute acid. State which of these
gives a mixture of enantiomers, and explain why the other two do not. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Oxygen is more electronegative than carbon, so the C=O bond is polar [1]; the carbon is δ+ (electron
deficient), so an electron-pair donor is attracted to it [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "The carbon is positive" without linking it to the electronegativity of oxygen usually earns only one of the two marks.

**2. (a)** A **silver mirror** forms [1]
**(b)** **2-methylpropanoic acid** (as its carboxylate in the alkaline reagent) [1]; it is a **reducing agent**
(it is oxidised) [1]
**(c)** Ketones are not easily oxidised: the C=O carbon has no hydrogen atom to lose [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Silver precipitate" or "grey solid" is usually accepted in place of a mirror, but "red" or "white" is not.

**3. (a)** CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ [1]; **pentan-3-ol** [1]
**(b)** (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CHO + 2[H] → (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH₂OH [1]; **3-methylbutan-1-ol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An equation with a single [H] is unbalanced and loses the equation mark even when the product formula is right.

**4.** Cδ+=Oδ− shown on CH₃CH₂CHO and an arrow from the lone pair on H⁻ to the carbonyl carbon [1]; arrow
from the C=O bond to the oxygen [1]; intermediate CH₃CH₂CH₂–O⁻ drawn with the negative charge on O [1];
arrow from a lone pair on O⁻ to H of H₂O (or to H⁺), giving **propan-1-ol** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The first arrow must end on the carbonyl carbon; an arrow from H⁻ to the oxygen shows the wrong atom being attacked and the later arrows then make no sense.

**5. (a)** CH₃CH₂CHO + HCN → CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN [1]; **2-hydroxybutanenitrile** [1]
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ + HCN → CH₃CH₂C(OH)(CN)CH₂CH₃ [1]; **2-ethyl-2-hydroxybutanenitrile** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The nitrile carbon counts in the chain, so (a) is a butanenitrile, not a propanenitrile; a wrong chain length loses the name mark.

**6. (a)** KCN is highly toxic [1]; with acid it releases HCN, which is highly toxic and volatile [1]
**(b)** HCN is a weak acid and supplies few CN⁻ ions (or HCN is volatile and hard to handle safely) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Toxic" twice is one point; the second mark needs the separate idea of HCN being released when acid is added.

**7. (a)** Cδ+=Oδ− on butanone and an arrow from the lone pair on the carbon of :CN⁻ to the carbonyl carbon [1];
arrow from the C=O bond to O [1]; intermediate CH₃C(CN)(O⁻)CH₂CH₃ [1]; arrow from a lone pair on O⁻ to H⁺,
giving 2-hydroxy-2-methylbutanenitrile [1]
**(b)** The C=O group is planar (trigonal planar carbon) [1]; CN⁻ attacks from either side with equal
probability [1]; the two enantiomers form in equal amounts, a racemic mixture, whose opposite rotations
cancel [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) "the product is chiral" is not an explanation; the marks come from the planar group, equal attack from both sides and equal amounts of each enantiomer.

**8. (a)** n(butanal) = 2.16 ÷ 72.0 = 0.0300 mol, so n(Ag) = 2 × 0.0300 = 0.0600 mol [1];
mass = 0.0600 × 107.9 = **6.47 g** [1]
**(b)** Mr(Cu₂O) = 2(63.5) + 16.0 = 143.0, n(Cu₂O) = 0.0300 mol [1]; mass = 0.0300 × 143.0 = **4.29 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using a 1 : 1 ratio for silver gives 3.24 g; the method mark depends on the 1 : 2 ratio, so show it.

**9.** n(ethanal) = 4.40 ÷ 44.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; Mr(C₃H₅NO) = 71.0 [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 71.0
= 7.10 g [1]; yield = 5.33 ÷ 7.10 × 100 = **75.1 %** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An error in Mr is normally carried forward if the rest of the method is right, so write the Mr down rather than going straight to the answer.

**10. (a)** X is **butanal** [1]; Y is **2-methylpropanal** [1]; Z is **butanone** [1]
**(b)** **Butan-2-ol** [1]; yes, it is racemic [1]: C2 of butan-2-ol carries four different groups (H, OH, CH₃,
C₂H₅) and H⁻ attacks the planar C=O from either side equally [1]
**(c)** Broad 3230–3550 cm⁻¹: O–H of an alcohol is present [1]; nothing at 1680–1750 cm⁻¹: no C=O, so the
reduction is complete [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), names must match the evidence: the Fehling's result separates the aldehydes from the ketone, and the unbranched product separates X from Y.

**11.** Step 1: acidified potassium dichromate(VI) [1]; heat gently with the alcohol in excess and distil the
aldehyde off as it forms [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O, giving propanal [1]. Step 2: KCN followed by
dilute acid [1]; CH₃CH₂CHO + HCN → CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN, giving 2-hydroxybutanenitrile [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Reflux" in step 1 gives propanoic acid, not propanal, so the condition mark is lost and the second step no longer works.

**12.** Pentan-2-one gives a mixture of enantiomers [1]; methanal has two H atoms on the C=O carbon [1] and
propanone has two CH₃ groups, so in each case the product carbon does not have four different groups and is not
chiral [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the identical groups in each non-chiral case (two H atoms; two CH₃ groups); "it is symmetrical" alone does not show why no chiral centre forms.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing the cyanide arrow from nitrogen instead of the lone pair on carbon.
- Omitting the arrow from the C=O bond to oxygen.
- Leaving the alkoxide O⁻ unprotonated at the end of a mechanism.
- Writing one [H] in reduction equations.
- Using a 1 : 1 aldehyde-to-silver ratio.
- Miscounting carbons when naming hydroxynitriles.
- Explaining optical inactivity without "planar" and "equal attack from both sides".
- Saying symmetrical ketones give enantiomers.
- Refluxing a primary alcohol when the aldehyde is the target.
