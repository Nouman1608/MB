These questions cover section 2.1, *Microeconomics and macroeconomics*, of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: the difference between the two and the decision makers involved in each. All countries, firms and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide on microeconomics and macroeconomics](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-microeconomics-and-macroeconomics/) and the [revision notes on microeconomics and macroeconomics](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-microeconomics-and-macroeconomics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a macroeconomic issue? **[1]**

A: The price of coffee in a city market

B: The wage paid to electricians

C: The rate of inflation in the economy

D: The number of workers employed by a supermarket

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a microeconomic decision maker? **[1]**

A: A trade union representing teachers

B: A government setting national income tax rates

C: A central bank setting the interest rate for the economy

D: A government aiming for economic growth

**3.** A government places a tax on plastic bags. Why is this a microeconomic decision? **[1]**

A: It is made by the government

B: It affects one market

C: It raises tax revenue

D: It changes the general price level

**4.** Define *macroeconomics*. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** decision makers that are studied in microeconomics. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a rise in the price of one good is not the same as inflation. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** For each statement, identify whether it is a microeconomic or macroeconomic issue and give a reason.

**(a)** A shoe factory decides to employ 20 more workers. **[2]**

**(b)** The country's total output grows by 3% in a year. **[2]**

**(c)** A bank raises the interest rate it charges one business customer. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** differences between microeconomics and macroeconomics. **[4]**

**9.** In Country M, 2,500 firms each reduce their workforce by 6 workers during a recession. The labour force is 3 million.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of jobs lost. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the rise in the unemployment rate, in percentage points, assuming all these workers become unemployed. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how this example shows a link between microeconomics and macroeconomics. **[2]**

**10.** The central bank raises interest rates for the whole economy. Explain how this macroeconomic decision could affect **one** household and **one** firm. **[4]**

**11.** A student writes: "Microeconomics is about small firms and macroeconomics is about large firms." Explain why this is incorrect. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss whether a government decision to set a minimum price for rice should be classified as microeconomic or macroeconomic. **[4]**

**13.** Total spending in an economy is made up of the spending decisions of millions of individual households. Explain why the study of total spending is part of macroeconomics. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* the decision maker being the government does not make it macro. The test is whether it concerns one market or the whole economy.

**4.** The study of the economy as a whole [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two, one mark each: households (consumers) [1]; firms [1]; workers; trade unions; banks. **[2]**

**6.** A rise in the price of one good concerns one market and may be offset by falls in other prices [1]; inflation is a sustained rise in the general price level across the whole economy [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Micro [1]: it is one firm's decision about its own workforce [1].

**(b)** Macro [1]: total output is a measure for the whole economy [1].

**(c)** Micro [1]: it concerns one bank and one customer [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Two from, two marks each: micro studies individual decision makers and markets [1] while macro studies the economy as a whole [1]; micro looks at the price of one good, while macro looks at the general price level and inflation; micro looks at employment in one firm or occupation [1], while macro looks at total employment and unemployment [1]; micro decision makers are households, workers, firms, trade unions and banks, while the main macro decision maker is the government. **[4]**

**9. (a)** 2,500 × 6 = **15,000** jobs [1].

**(b)** 15,000 ÷ 3,000,000 × 100 [1] = **0.5 percentage points** [1].

**(c)** Each firm's decision to cut jobs is a micro decision [1]; added together, the decisions raise the national unemployment rate, which is a macro outcome [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Household: higher interest rates raise the cost of borrowing [1], so the household may postpone buying a car or house on credit, or save more because saving earns more [1]. Firm: borrowing to buy new machines becomes more expensive [1], so the firm may delay investment or expansion [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The difference is not about the size of firms [1]. Microeconomics studies individual decision makers and markets, which includes very large firms [1]; macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole, such as total output, inflation and unemployment [1]. **[3]**

**12.** It is microeconomic because it intervenes in one market, the market for rice [1], and changes the price and quantity traded in that market only [1]. However, if rice is a staple food bought by most households, the decision could affect living standards and the general price level noticeably, giving it macro effects [1]. Conclusion: the decision is microeconomic, though its wider effects may need macro analysis [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "discuss" rewards seeing both sides. Classify it, give the reason, show a wider effect, and conclude.

**13.** Macroeconomics studies the economy as a whole, and total spending is an aggregate measure for the whole economy [1]; although each household's choice is a micro decision, the question being asked is about the combined effect on the economy, such as on total output or employment [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* the same activity can be studied at both levels. What decides the branch is whether the question is about one decision maker or the total.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by who acts (government means macro) rather than by scale.
- Defining micro and macro by firm size.
- Treating one price rise as inflation.
- Giving a classification with no reason.
- Forgetting to show both the micro and macro sides when a question asks about the link.
