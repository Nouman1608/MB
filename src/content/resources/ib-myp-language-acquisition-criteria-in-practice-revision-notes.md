The [assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-assessment-revision-notes/) and
[eAssessment exam preparation](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-eassessment-exam-preparation/)
cover the four criteria and how the two eAssessment components are structured. These notes go further,
working through what genuinely top-band evidence looks like for each criterion within your own
proficiency level, since a top-band response at the emergent level looks very different from a
top-band response at the proficient level for the same criterion.

## Criterion A: Listening

Top-band evidence interprets and constructs meaning from spoken multimodal text, and understands how
images or other spatial elements presented alongside the oral text contribute to meaning -- not just
transcribing individual words heard.

**Worked example:** A capable-level listening task might play a short spoken announcement accompanied
by a relevant image. Top-band evidence does not just identify factual details stated aloud (time,
place) -- it also explains how the accompanying image reinforces or adds to the spoken message (e.g.
an image of a closed sign supporting an announcement about a temporary closure), demonstrating genuine
multimodal interpretation rather than audio comprehension alone.

## Criterion B: Reading

Top-band evidence constructs meaning from written text while also interpreting its spatial and visual
aspects -- layout, images, formatting -- as sources of meaning in their own right, not just decoration
around the words.

**Worked example:** Given a public information poster combining short text with icons and colour
coding, top-band evidence explains what the icons and colour choices themselves communicate (e.g. a
red icon signalling urgency or warning) in addition to correctly comprehending the written text,
rather than treating the visual elements as irrelevant to the reading task.

## Criterion C: Speaking

Top-band evidence interacts spontaneously on topics of personal, local and global interest, applying
linguistic and literary concepts with increasing skill, range and accuracy for the student's
proficiency level.

**Worked example:** At the proficient level, a top-band spoken response to an unexpected follow-up
question does not fall back to a memorised, unrelated prepared answer -- it responds directly and
coherently to what was actually asked, using a genuine range of vocabulary and structures appropriate
to that level, demonstrating real interactive competence rather than rehearsed recitation.

## Criterion D: Writing

Top-band evidence recognises and uses language suitable to audience and purpose across different
registers (home, classroom, formal/informal, social and academic), applying understanding of language,
form, mode and medium to express ideas creatively and meaningfully.

**Worked example:** Asked to write both a text message to a friend and a formal email to a school
administrator on a related topic, top-band evidence produces genuinely different register, vocabulary
and structure for each -- informal, abbreviated language for the text message; formal greetings,
complete sentences and polite requests for the email -- rather than writing the same content with only
minor wording changes between the two tasks.

## How the four criteria map onto the two eAssessment components

As the [eAssessment exam preparation](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-eassessment-exam-preparation/)
notes explain, Criteria A, B and D are assessed together in the on-screen paper (listening, reading,
writing), while Criterion C (speaking) is assessed separately as an internally assessed, interactive
task. This split matters for revision planning: strengthening Criterion A or B does nothing to prepare
Criterion C's genuinely spontaneous, unscripted speaking demand, and vice versa -- each criterion's
top-band evidence, as worked through above, has to be practised in its own right rather than assuming
strength in one skill transfers automatically to another.

## Why proficiency level changes what "top-band" looks like

Because these four criteria are assessed against a student's own proficiency level (emergent, capable
or proficient) rather than a single fixed standard for all students, top-band evidence at the emergent
level might be a short, simple but accurate response using basic vocabulary and structures, while
top-band evidence at the proficient level requires nuanced, extended, spontaneous language use. A
common revision mistake is comparing your own work against an unrealistic standard drawn from a
different, more advanced proficiency level, rather than against your own level's actual descriptors.

## Self-test

1. What must top-band Criterion A evidence demonstrate beyond identifying factual details heard aloud?
2. What must top-band Criterion B evidence interpret beyond the literal written words?
3. Why does falling back on a memorised, unrelated answer fail to demonstrate top-band Criterion C
   evidence?
4. What must top-band Criterion D evidence show across different writing tasks?
5. Why can two students at different proficiency levels both receive a top achievement level for the
   same criterion?

**Answers:** 1. How the accompanying image or spatial element contributes to or reinforces the spoken
message's meaning, demonstrating genuine multimodal interpretation. 2. The spatial and visual aspects
of the text (layout, images, colour, formatting) as sources of meaning in their own right. 3. Because
top-band speaking requires genuine spontaneous interaction responding directly to what was actually
asked, not rehearsed recitation unrelated to the specific question posed. 4. Genuinely different
register, vocabulary and structure appropriate to each task's specific audience and purpose, not the
same content with only minor wording changes -- for instance, an informal text message and a formal
email on the same topic should read as two distinctly different pieces of writing, not one piece of
writing lightly edited to look more polite. 5. Because the criteria are assessed against each
student's own proficiency level descriptors, so a top achievement level reflects the best possible
evidence for that student's level, not an identical fixed standard applied to every student regardless
of level.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four assessment criteria in IB MYP Language Acquisition?**
The four criteria are A listening, B reading, C speaking and D writing. Each is assessed against the student's own proficiency level: emergent, capable or proficient.

**What does register mean in MYP Language Acquisition writing?**
Register is the choice of vocabulary, structure and tone suited to an audience and purpose, such as informal language for a text message to a friend and formal sentences for an email to a school administrator. Top-band Criterion D evidence shows genuinely different register across tasks, not just a changed greeting.

**Does practising reading help with the MYP Language Acquisition speaking assessment?**
Not directly. Strengthening Criterion A or B does nothing to prepare Criterion C's spontaneous, unscripted speaking demand, so each criterion's top-band evidence has to be practised in its own right.

**What is a common mistake when judging my own MYP Language Acquisition work?**
Comparing your work against the descriptors for the wrong proficiency level. Identify your own level first, because the same response can be top-band at one level and only mid-band at a more advanced one.
