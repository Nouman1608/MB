These questions practise **4.2.3.1 Plant tissues** and **4.2.3.2 Plant organ system** from the AQA GCSE Biology
(8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018. Both are assessed on Paper 1 at
Foundation and Higher Tier, and none of the content is (HT only). Questions 7 to 10 use the practical activities the
specification suggests (measuring transpiration by water uptake, and stomatal distribution) and the maths skills
MS 1a, 1c, 2a, 2d and 5c. The answers say what earns each mark.

## How should you use these questions?

Answer in full before checking. In calculations, show the working and give the unit. In "explain" questions, give
the effect and then the reason, linked with "because" or "so".

## Questions

**1.** Name the plant tissue found at the growing tips of shoots and roots, and give its function. **[2]**

**2.** Explain how the structure of the palisade mesophyll is related to its function. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how the spongy mesophyll is adapted for gas exchange. **[2]**

**4.** Root hair cells absorb water and mineral ions from the soil.

**(a)** Name the process by which each is absorbed. **[2]**
**(b)** Root hair cells contain many mitochondria. Explain why. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** other adaptation of a root hair cell and explain how it helps. **[2]**

**5.** Compare the structure and function of xylem and phloem. **[4]**

**6.** Describe the role of stomata and guard cells. **[2]**

**7.** A student measured water uptake by a leafy shoot with a potometer. The air bubble moved 54 mm in 9 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of water uptake in mm per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** The capillary tube has a radius of 0.4 mm. Calculate the volume of water taken up in 9 minutes, in mm cubed.
Use pi = 3.14. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **two** precautions when setting up the potometer, and explain why each matters. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the rate of water uptake is only an estimate of the rate of transpiration. **[1]**

**8.** The student then placed a fan at different distances from the shoot. Results:

| Distance of fan (cm) | Bubble movement in 5 minutes (mm) |
|---|---|
| 20 | 40 |
| 40 | 31 |
| 60 | 24 |
| 80 | 20 |
| No fan | 18 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of water uptake with the fan at 20 cm. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe and explain the pattern in the results. **[3]**
**(c)** Name **two** variables the student should keep the same. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how an increase in humidity and an increase in light intensity would each affect the rate of
transpiration. **[4]**

**10.** A student counted stomata in six fields of view on the lower surface of a leaf: 22, 19, 25, 21, 18, 27.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of stomata per field of view. **[1]**
**(b)** The area of the field of view is 0.15 mm squared. Calculate the number of stomata per mm squared. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the student counted six fields of view rather than one. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why there are far fewer stomata on the upper surface. **[2]**

**11.** Describe the process of translocation. **[2]**

**12.** Describe how water moves from the soil to the air around a leaf. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Meristem [1]. Its cells divide to make new cells that can differentiate into different types of plant cell
(for growth) [1].

**2.** Cells contain many chloroplasts [1] and are near the top of the leaf / tightly packed where they absorb the
most light, for photosynthesis [1].

**3.** Loosely packed cells with large air spaces [1], so carbon dioxide and oxygen can diffuse easily between the
cells and the stomata [1].

**4. (a)** Water: osmosis [1]. Mineral ions: active transport [1].
**(b)** Mineral ions are absorbed by active transport, against the concentration gradient [1], which needs energy
from respiration, which happens in mitochondria [1].
**(c)** Long, thin extension (root hair) [1] gives a large surface area for absorption [1].

**5.** Xylem carries water and mineral ions [1] up from the roots to the stems and leaves [1]; it is made of hollow
tubes strengthened by lignin [1]. Phloem carries dissolved sugars from the leaves to the rest of the plant, made of
tubes of elongated cells with pores in the end walls [1]. (Accept any four correct, comparative points.)

**6.** They control gas exchange (carbon dioxide in, oxygen out) [1] and water loss; guard cells open and close the
stomata [1].

**7. (a)** 54 / 9 = **6 mm per minute** [1].
**(b)** Volume = 3.14 x 0.4 x 0.4 x 54 [1] = 3.14 x 0.16 x 54 = **27.1 mm cubed** [1]. (27.13 to 4 s.f.)
**(c)** Any two [1 each]: cut and fit the shoot under water, so no air enters the xylem and blocks water flow; make
the joints airtight (for example with petroleum jelly), so the only water lost is through the shoot; dry the leaves,
so water on the surface does not affect evaporation.
**(d)** Some of the water taken up is used by the plant (for example in photosynthesis) rather than lost [1].

**8. (a)** 40 / 5 = **8 mm per minute** [1].
**(b)** The closer the fan, the faster the water uptake (from 20 mm to 40 mm in 5 minutes) [1]. Faster air movement
removes water vapour from around the leaf [1], keeping a steep concentration gradient, so water vapour diffuses out
of the stomata faster [1].
**(c)** Any two [1 each]: temperature; light intensity; humidity; the same shoot (or leaf area); time of each
reading.

**9.** Higher humidity decreases the rate [1], because the air already contains more water vapour so the
concentration gradient from leaf to air is smaller [1]. Higher light intensity increases the rate [1], because
stomata open wider in light, so more water vapour diffuses out [1].

**10. (a)** Total = 22 + 19 + 25 + 21 + 18 + 27 = 132; mean = 132 / 6 = **22 stomata per field of view** [1].
**(b)** 22 / 0.15 = **147 stomata per mm squared** (146.7) [1].
**(c)** Stomata are not evenly spread, so a larger sample gives a more representative mean [1].
**(d)** The upper surface is in direct sunlight and is warmer [1], so fewer stomata there reduce water loss by
transpiration [1].

**11.** Movement of dissolved sugars (food molecules) through the phloem [1] from the leaves to the rest of the
plant for immediate use or storage [1].

**12.** Level-of-response answer. A full answer includes:
- Water enters root hair cells by osmosis, because the soil water is more dilute than the cell sap.
- Root hair cells have a large surface area, increasing uptake.
- Water moves into the xylem and up the stem in hollow, lignin-strengthened tubes (the transpiration stream).
- In the leaf, water moves into mesophyll cells and evaporates into the air spaces.
- Water vapour diffuses out through the stomata, controlled by guard cells.
- Loss of water from the leaf draws more water up the xylem.

5-6 marks: a clear sequence from soil to air with correct process names. 3-4 marks: most steps with some processes
named. 1-2 marks: a few correct statements.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying minerals enter roots by osmosis or diffusion.
- Saying phloem carries water, or that translocation is the loss of water.
- Leaving the unit off a rate.
- Using the radius as the diameter (or the reverse) in the volume formula.
- Explaining the light effect without mentioning stomata.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the structure of phloem in detail?**
No. AQA says the detailed structure of phloem and the mechanism of transport are not required. Know that phloem is
tubes of elongated cells with pores in the end walls.

**What is the formula for the volume of water in a capillary tube?**
Volume = pi x r squared x distance moved by the bubble, where r is the radius of the tube.

**Which way does humidity change the rate?**
Higher humidity slows transpiration because the concentration gradient of water vapour is smaller.
