The AQA GCSE English Language (8700) spoken language endorsement is a compulsory presentation of, as a
guide, up to ten minutes, followed by questions and feedback, graded Pass, Merit or Distinction against
AO7 (presenting), AO8 (listening and responding) and AO9 (spoken Standard English). These practice tasks
cover all three content strands of 3.1.3. The bracketed marks are for self-checking: the real
endorsement has no marks.

## Section A: Presenting information and ideas (3.1.3, first strand)

**1.** Your agreed topic is "Should our town ban cars from the centre?" Write a one-sentence purpose
statement, identify the audience, and list four sections for your talk. **[4]**

**2.** Rewrite this opening so that it engages the audience: "Hello. My talk is about recycling. I will
talk about recycling." **[3]**

**3.** Name three strategies, other than slides, that could help a presentation reach the Distinction
criterion "an effective range of strategies to engage the audience". **[3]**

## Section B: Spoken Standard English (3.1.3, third strand)

**4.** Rewrite each sentence in spoken Standard English suitable for a formal presentation. **[4]**
(a) "Me and my mate done a survey."
(b) "They was well surprised by the results."
(c) "It's kind of like, really bad for you and that."
(d) "You lot probably ain't thought about this."

**5.** Explain why using spoken Standard English does not mean speaking without any personal voice or
humour. **[2]**

## Section C: Responding to spoken language (3.1.3, second strand)

**6.** After a talk arguing for a four-day school week, an audience member asks: "Wouldn't parents who
work five days struggle with childcare?" Write three answers, one at each grade (Pass, Merit, Distinction).
**[6]**

**7.** Explain what a student should do if a question is unclear. **[2]**

## Section D: Applying the criteria

**8.** Decide the grade each student would receive and justify it using the criteria. **[3 each]**
(a) Priya gives an engaging talk on climate migration with a sophisticated vocabulary and a clear
structure, but answers every question with a single sentence.
(b) Tom's talk is clear, organised and achieves its purpose of explaining how vaccines work; his answers
are formal and reasonably detailed.
(c) Lucy prepares nothing and speaks for two minutes, reading facts about her favourite singer from her
phone, mostly audibly and in Standard English.

**9.** A student is ill on the day of the presentations and never attempts it. State what will appear for
the endorsement and why. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Award 1 mark each for: a clear purpose (for example, "to persuade the audience that a car-free
centre would make the town healthier and safer"); an identified audience (classmates and teacher, as local
residents); and four logical sections, such as the problem (traffic, pollution), evidence from other
towns, answering objections (shops, disabled access), and a call to action [2 for the sections].

**2.** Any opening that hooks and states a purpose: for example, "Every one of us threw away about a
kilogram of rubbish this week. Today I want to convince you that our school could cut that in half." [1
for a hook, 1 for a clear purpose, 1 for addressing the audience].

**3.** Any three [1 each]: a short anecdote; a rhetorical question; a striking statistic; a demonstration
or prop; inviting a show of hands; varied pace and pauses; direct address; a callback to the opening at the
end.

**4.** [1 each] (a) "My friend and I carried out a survey." (b) "They were very surprised by the
results." (c) "It is genuinely harmful to your health." (d) "Many of you may not have considered this."

**5.** Spoken Standard English concerns grammatical forms and a register appropriate to a formal setting
[1]; a speaker can still use a personal anecdote, humour or a distinctive voice while keeping standard
grammar [1].

**6.** [2 each] Model answers:
- **Pass (straightforward):** "Yes, that might be a problem, but I still think it's a good idea."
- **Merit (formal, some detail):** "That is a fair concern. Schools could run supervised clubs on the
  fifth day, which would help working parents."
- **Distinction (perceptive, elaborates):** "That is the main objection, and the answer depends on what
  the fifth day is for. If it is simply a day off, you are right that it shifts a burden onto families.
  But in the schools I described, the fifth day offered optional sport and study clubs, so childcare was
  covered and students gained activities. That suggests the idea only works if it is funded properly,
  which strengthens my point about investment."

**7.** Ask politely for clarification [1], for example "Could you say a little more about what you mean
by…?", which is itself an appropriate response to spoken language (AO8) [1].

**8.**
(a) **Merit** [1]. Content and structure meet Distinction [1], but responding perceptively and
elaborating is required for Distinction; one-sentence answers fit Merit's "some detail" at best, and under
the competency rule every criterion must be met [1].
(b) **Merit** [1]. Clear organisation for the audience and an achieved purpose [1]; formal responses in
some detail [1].
(c) **Pass or Not Classified** [1]. If she is audible and uses Standard English, and her talk shows an
attempt at structure and at meeting audience needs, she meets Pass [1]; AQA notes that lack of preparation
is likely to prevent access to higher grades [1].

**9.** Not Classified [1]: AQA records Not Classified for students who do not attempt the endorsement [1].
(Schools must give every student the opportunity to present.)

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there a list of set topics?**
No. The topic is agreed between you and your teacher in advance, so choose one that lets you develop
challenging ideas.

**Will my presentation be filmed?**
Possibly. Schools record a sample of presentations for moderation; in a centre with 30 or fewer students,
all are recorded.

**Can I present with a partner?**
The specification allows debates and dialogues as forms of presentation, and the criteria describe each
learner's own performance; ask your teacher how your school organises a shared format.
