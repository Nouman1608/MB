These notes condense section 3.18, The impact of ethical considerations, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127) specification (version 1.0, 22 December 2016, for A-level exams from June 2019 onwards). The qualification is not tiered, and the specification lists section 18 for both Paper 1 and Paper 2. For full explanations and a worked scenario, read the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-the-impact-of-ethical-considerations/), then test yourself with the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-the-impact-of-ethical-considerations-practice/). See also the [AQA A-level Accounting hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/accounting/), the [7127 checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/accounting/) and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## 3.18 at a glance

1. The five fundamental principles.
2. How they affect accountants in business and in public practice, boards, auditors, the remuneration committee, corporate governance and CSR.
3. Legal and regulatory frameworks: FRC, government, EU.
4. Professional bodies: CCAB and CIMA.
5. Acting ethically with clients, suppliers, colleagues and stakeholders.
6. What to do when you suspect an unethical or illegal act.

## The five principles

| Principle | One-line definition | Key word to use |
|---|---|---|
| Integrity | Straightforward and honest | Honesty |
| Objectivity | No bias, conflict of interest or undue influence | Independence of judgement |
| Professional competence and due care | Up-to-date knowledge applied carefully | Skill and diligence |
| Confidentiality | No disclosure without authority; no personal use | Trust |
| Professional behaviour | Obey laws and regulations; do not discredit the profession | Reputation |

## Threats and safeguards

CIMA's Code (aligned with the IESBA international code) uses three steps: **identify, evaluate, address** threats.

| Threat | Trigger | Typical safeguard |
|---|---|---|
| Self-interest | Bonus, shares, fee dependence, gifts | Remove the person from the decision; independent review |
| Self-review | Checking your own work | Different person performs the review |
| Advocacy | Promoting a client's or employer's case | Separate the advocacy and reporting roles |
| Familiarity | Long or close relationship | Rotate staff |
| Intimidation | Threat of dismissal or loss of the client | Escalate to the audit committee or board |

A safeguard reduces a threat to an acceptable level. It may not remove it. If nothing reduces it enough, decline or withdraw.

## Who is affected and how

- **Accountant in business:** one employer; pressure from managers to flatter results.
- **Accountant in public practice:** serves clients (audit, tax, advice); independence from clients is central.
- **Board of directors:** executives plus independent NEDs who challenge them; chair and chief executive roles divided.
- **Auditors:** independent opinion on whether the statements give a **true and fair view**; shareholders of a public company normally appoint them by ordinary resolution.
- **Remuneration committee** (printed in the specification as "remittance committee"): independent NEDs set directors' pay; no director decides their own.
- **Corporate governance:** how companies are directed and controlled; UK Corporate Governance Code, **comply or explain**.
- **CSR:** responsibility to employees, customers, suppliers, community and environment, not only shareholders. Companies Act 2006 s172 lists these factors for directors.

## Audit opinions

| Opinion | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unmodified | True and fair |
| Qualified | True and fair **except for** a specific material matter |
| Adverse | Not true and fair: material and pervasive misstatement |
| Disclaimer | No opinion: not enough evidence |

## Regulators and frameworks

| Body | Remember |
|---|---|
| FRC (printed as "Finance Reporting Council") | UK accounting standards; UK Corporate Governance and Stewardship Codes; regulates auditors, accountants and actuaries; investigates misconduct |
| Government | Company law (Companies Act 2006); Companies House; Insolvency Service. BIS (named in the specification) became BEIS in July 2016; business functions moved to the Department for Business and Trade in February 2023 |
| EU | IAS Regulation (EC 1606/2002): publicly traded companies use international standards for consolidated accounts. UK left on 31 January 2020; EU-adopted standards were frozen into UK law at the end of 2020, then UK-adopted standards apply |
| CCAB | Forum and common voice to government for ICAEW, ACCA, CIPFA, ICAS and Chartered Accountants Ireland |
| CIMA | Management accountants' body; not a CCAB member (announced its exit in 2011); own Code of Ethics built on the five principles |

**Consequences of breaking the rules:** sanctions from the FRC or a professional body (up to exclusion), legal action under company law, a modified audit opinion, loss of investor and lender confidence, and personal loss of job or reputation.

## Acting ethically with each group

| Group | Do | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Clients | Keep their affairs confidential; take on only work you are competent to do | Using inside knowledge for personal gain |
| Suppliers | Pay on agreed terms; choose on price, quality and reliability | Accepting gifts or hospitality that could sway a contract |
| Colleagues | Support and supervise; raise concerns through proper channels | Pressuring a junior to change figures |
| Stakeholders | Publish complete, truthful information on time | Window dressing or hiding bad news |

In every case you apply three layers together: the **principles**, the **codes of practice** of the CCAB bodies and CIMA, and the **regulatory framework** (Companies Act, FRC standards and codes).

## Method in steps: answering a scenario

Each point in a scenario answer works best with four parts:

```
Principle   name it (e.g. objectivity)
Evidence    quote the fact from the scenario
Effect      who is misled and how (profit, assets, ratios)
Action      what the accountant should do next
```

For an "evaluate" question, weigh the options, such as raising it internally versus reporting externally, and finish with a judgement that depends on the facts given (the size of the amount, who is involved, whether the act is illegal).

## Method in steps: suspected wrongdoing

```
1  Establish the facts and keep records
2  Name the principle(s) threatened and the threat type
3  Evaluate significance: amount, who is harmed, legal or not
4  Apply safeguards
5  Resolve conflicts of interest (disclose, step back)
6  Escalate internally: manager -> audit committee / board
7  Seek external or professional help (professional body, lawyer;
   PIDA 1998 protects qualifying disclosures)
8  Refuse to take part; last resort: resign or withdraw
```

## Small worked reminder

A sales director wants £30,000 of goods sent to a customer on 2 January included in December's revenue. Gross margin is 40%.

```
Overstated revenue      = 30,000
Overstated gross profit = 30,000 x 40% = 12,000
```

Breaches: integrity (false revenue) and professional competence and due care (the realisation concept). The accountant records the sale in January and explains why, escalating if pressed.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Integrity vs objectivity:** honesty vs freedom from bias. A biased estimate can be honestly believed.
- **Qualified vs adverse:** one material issue vs material and pervasive.
- **Adverse vs disclaimer:** the auditor disagrees vs the auditor cannot tell.
- **Accountant in business vs public practice:** employer vs clients.
- **Comply or explain vs law:** the Code allows explained departures; the Companies Act does not.
- **CCAB vs CIMA:** an umbrella forum vs a single professional body outside it.
- **Safeguard vs elimination:** safeguards reduce a threat to an acceptable level.
- **Executive vs non-executive director:** runs the business day to day vs gives independent challenge and oversight.
- **Internal vs external escalation:** go through managers, the audit committee and the board first; go outside when internal routes fail or are compromised.

## Quick self-test

1. Which principle is breached by an accountant who shares a client's profit figures with a journalist?
2. An audit manager has worked on the same client for twelve years. Name the threat.
3. What does "except for" signal in an audit report?
4. State who sets directors' pay under good governance.
5. What does "comply or explain" mean?
6. Name the UK body that sets accounting standards and the UK Corporate Governance Code.
7. Is CIMA a member of CCAB?
8. A finance director's bonus rises if inventory is valued higher. Name the threat.
9. Revenue of £50,000 is recorded a year early; gross margin is 25%. By how much is gross profit overstated?
10. Give the first step to take on suspecting a colleague of fraud.
11. Which opinion is given when the auditor cannot obtain enough evidence?
12. Name two factors s172 of the Companies Act 2006 requires directors to have regard to.

### Answers

1. Confidentiality.
2. Familiarity.
3. A qualified opinion: true and fair apart from a specific material matter.
4. A remuneration committee of independent non-executive directors.
5. A listed company follows each provision of the Code or explains why it has not.
6. The Financial Reporting Council (FRC).
7. No. It announced its withdrawal in 2011.
8. Self-interest.
9. 50,000 × 25% = £12,500.
10. Establish the facts and keep a record before raising it.
11. A disclaimer of opinion.
12. Any two of: long-term consequences, employees, suppliers and customers, the community and the environment, high standards of business conduct, fairness between members.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing principles with no link to a named person or fact in the scenario.
- Calling every breach "integrity" when objectivity or competence fits better.
- Missing the threat type when the question says "evaluate the threat".
- Jumping to external reporting without internal escalation or advice.
- Mixing up qualified, adverse and disclaimer opinions.
- Putting executives on the remuneration committee.
- Writing that the government "sets accounting standards"; that is the FRC.
- Ignoring the effect on profit or ratios when figures are given.
- Ending an evaluation with no judgement.
