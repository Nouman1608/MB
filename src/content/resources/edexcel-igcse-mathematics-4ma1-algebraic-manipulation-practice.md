## Section A

**1.** Simplify 9pq − 5p + 3pq + 2p. **[2]**

**2.** Expand and simplify 5(2y − 3) − 2(4y − 7). **[2]**

**3.** Factorise fully 20a³b − 15a²b². **[2]**

**4.** Expand and simplify (2p − 3)(p + 6). **[2]**

**5.** Factorise

**(a)** x² + 4x − 21 **[2]**

**(b)** x² − 121 **[1]**

**6.** a = −2 and b = 3/4. Work out the value of a³ − 8b. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Expand and simplify (x − 5)². **[2]**

**(b)** Show that (x − 5)² − (x + 5)² = −20x. **[2]**

**8.** **(Higher tier only)** Expand and simplify (x − 4)(2x + 1)(x + 3). **[3]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** Factorise fully

**(a)** 6x² − 11x − 10 **[2]**

**(b)** 4x² − 36 **[2]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Simplify fully (x² + 2x − 15)/(x² − 9). **[3]**

**(b)** Write 3/(x + 2) + x/(x − 1) as a single fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)**

**(a)** Write x² + 8x + 3 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence write down the minimum value of x² + 8x + 3 and the value of x at which it occurs. **[2]**

**(c)** Write 5 − 12x − 3x² in the form a + b(x + c)², where a, b and c are integers. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** Prove that (3n + 1)² − (3n − 1)² is a multiple of 12 for every positive integer n. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 9pq + 3pq = 12pq [1]; −5p + 2p = −3p, so **12pq − 3p** [1].

**2.** 10y − 15 − 8y + 14 [1] = **2y − 1** [1].

**3.** HCF is 5a²b [1]; **5a²b(4a − 3b)** [1].

**4.** 2p² + 12p − 3p − 18 [1] = **2p² + 9p − 18** [1].

**5. (a)** Product −21, sum 4: +7 and −3 [1]; **(x + 7)(x − 3)** [1].

**(b)** **(x − 11)(x + 11)** [1].

**6.** a³ = (−2)³ = −8 and 8b = 6 [1]; −8 − 6 = **−14** [1].

**7. (a)** (x − 5)(x − 5) = x² − 5x − 5x + 25 [1] = **x² − 10x + 25** [1].

**(b)** (x + 5)² = x² + 10x + 25 [1]; (x² − 10x + 25) − (x² + 10x + 25) = −20x, as required [1].

**8.** (x − 4)(2x + 1) = 2x² − 7x − 4 [1]; (2x² − 7x − 4)(x + 3) = 2x³ + 6x² − 7x² − 21x − 4x − 12 [1] = **2x³ − x² − 25x − 12** [1].

**9. (a)** a × c = −60; −15 and 4; 6x² − 15x + 4x − 10 = 3x(2x − 5) + 2(2x − 5) [1]; **(3x + 2)(2x − 5)** [1].

**(b)** 4(x² − 9) [1] = **4(x − 3)(x + 3)** [1]. ((2x − 6)(2x + 6) is correct but not fully factorised: it scores 1 mark.)

**10. (a)** Numerator (x + 5)(x − 3) [1]; denominator (x − 3)(x + 3) [1]; **(x + 5)/(x + 3)** [1].

**(b)** Common denominator (x + 2)(x − 1) [1]; numerator 3(x − 1) + x(x + 2) = 3x − 3 + x² + 2x [1]; **(x² + 5x − 3)/[(x + 2)(x − 1)]** [1].

**11. (a)** (x + 4)² − 16 + 3 [1] = **(x + 4)² − 13** [1].

**(b)** Minimum value **−13** [1], when **x = −4** [1].

**(c)** −3x² − 12x + 5 = −3(x² + 4x) + 5 [1]; = −3[(x + 2)² − 4] + 5 = −3(x + 2)² + 12 + 5 [1]; **17 − 3(x + 2)²**, so a = 17, b = −3, c = 2 [1].

**12.** (3n + 1)² = 9n² + 6n + 1 and (3n − 1)² = 9n² − 6n + 1 [1]; difference = 12n [1]; 12n has a factor of 12, so the expression is a multiple of 12 for every positive integer n [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: writing −2(4y − 7) as −8y − 14. The minus multiplies the −7 too, giving +14.
- Question 3: taking out only 5a or 5ab, which leaves a common factor inside the bracket.
- Question 6: working out a³ as −6 (multiplying by 3 instead of cubing) or as +8.
- Question 7(b): subtracting only the x² term of the second bracket.
- Question 9(b): stopping at (2x − 6)(2x + 6) or at 4(x² − 9). Both still have more factorising to do.
- Question 10(a): cancelling x² or the 9 and 15 directly. Only whole factors such as (x − 3) cancel.
- Question 11(c): forgetting to multiply −4 by −3, giving 5 − 3(x + 2)² − 4 or similar.
- Question 12: checking n = 1, 2 and 3 only, or reaching 12n without the concluding sentence.

## Checking your answers

Every answer on this page can be checked with one substitution. For question 8, put x = 1 into the original: (−3)(3)(4) = −36; the answer gives 2 − 1 − 25 − 12 = −36. For question 10(b), put x = 2: 3/4 + 2/1 = 2.75, and (4 + 10 − 3)/(4 × 1) = 11/4 = 2.75. For question 11(c), put x = 0: 5 on the left, and 17 − 3 × 4 = 5 on the right. Avoid a value that makes a denominator zero, such as x = 1 or x = −2 in question 10(b), and avoid x = 0 when checking a question about factors of x.
