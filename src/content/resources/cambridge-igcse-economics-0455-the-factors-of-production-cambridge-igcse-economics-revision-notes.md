## 1.2.1 The four factors and their rewards

| Factor | Definition | Reward | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Land | All natural resources | Rent | Gift of nature; renewable (fish, forests, wind) or non-renewable (oil, ores); area largely fixed |
| Labour | Human physical and mental effort in production | Wages | Skilled or unskilled; cannot be separated from the worker; productivity depends on education, health, experience |
| Capital | Man-made goods used to produce other goods and services | Interest | Uses resources to make; wears out; specific or general; money is **not** capital |
| Enterprise | Organising the other factors and bearing the risk | Profit | Decides, organises, takes risks, innovates; profit is uncertain and may be negative |

**Memory aid:** Land–Rent, Labour–Wages, Capital–Interest, Enterprise–Profit.

## Classification depends on use

- Family car → consumer good; same car used as a taxi → capital.
- River in its natural state → land; canal dug by people → capital.
- Small shop owner: enterprise (organises, takes risk) and labour (serves customers).
- Money → not a factor; it buys factors.
- Roads, ports and schools used in production → capital (infrastructure).

## 1.2.2 Mobility

- **Geographical mobility**: moving between places.
- **Occupational mobility**: moving between jobs or uses.

| Factor | Geographical | Occupational |
|---|---|---|
| Land | Immobile | Depends on its nature and planning rules |
| Labour | Limited by moving and housing costs, family ties, information, language, immigration rules | Limited by skills, qualifications, cost and time of retraining, age, licensing |
| Capital | General capital (vans, computers) mobile; specific or fixed capital (furnace, oil rig, buildings) not | General capital easily reused; specific capital hard to convert |
| Enterprise | Fairly mobile | Fairly mobile; limited by market knowledge, finance and rules |

**Why it matters:** when demand shifts, resources must move from declining to growing industries. Immobile factors stay idle, so unemployment and vacancies can exist at the same time.

## 1.2.3 Quantity and quality

**Quantity** = how much. **Quality** = how productive each unit is.

| Factor | Quantity up | Quantity down | Quality up | Quality down |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Land | New mineral finds; reclamation; irrigation of desert | Overfishing; wells running dry; flooding | Irrigation; fertiliser | Erosion; pollution |
| Labour | Larger working-age population; immigration; more people choosing to work; later retirement | Emigration; ageing population; longer education | Education; training; healthcare; experience | Poor health; out-of-date skills |
| Capital | Investment greater than wear and tear | Wear and tear not replaced; war or disaster | New technology | Poor maintenance; ageing equipment |
| Enterprise | Low profit taxes; easy loans; simple start-up rules | High profit taxes; heavy regulation; hard to borrow | Business education; mentoring | Lack of experience or information |

**Chain for explanations:** cause → change in quantity or quality of a factor → change in the economy's ability to produce → effect on output.

**Quick example:** a farm with 100 hectares produces 4 tonnes per hectare: 400 tonnes. Irrigation raises yield to 5 tonnes, but 10 hectares are lost to erosion: 90 × 5 = 450 tonnes. Quality gain outweighs quantity loss.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Factors of production | Resources used to produce goods and services |
| Rent | Reward to land |
| Wages | Reward to labour |
| Interest | Reward to capital |
| Profit | Reward to enterprise |
| Entrepreneur | Person who organises production and bears the risk |
| Geographical mobility | Ease of moving a factor between places |
| Occupational mobility | Ease of moving a factor between uses |
| Renewable resource | Natural resource that can regenerate |
| Non-renewable resource | Natural resource that runs out once used |

## Exam traps

- Money is not capital.
- Mobility answers must say which kind.
- Quantity and quality are different: more workers versus better workers.
- Each factor has its own reward.
- An item's factor depends on how it is used.
- In multiple choice, read whether the option describes the factor or its reward.

## Self-test

1. State the reward to each factor of production.
2. Define capital.
3. Give one renewable and one non-renewable example of land.
4. Why is money not a factor of production?
5. Is a sewing machine at home a factor of production? When would it be?
6. Distinguish geographical from occupational mobility.
7. Give two influences on the occupational mobility of labour.
8. Which factor is geographically immobile?
9. Give two causes of a rise in the quantity of labour.
10. Give two causes of a rise in the quality of labour.
11. A factory has 50 machines; 5 wear out and 8 new ones are bought. What is the new number of machines?
12. Give one reason why the quantity of enterprise might fall.

### Answers

1. Land – rent; labour – wages; capital – interest; enterprise – profit.
2. Man-made goods used to produce other goods and services.
3. Renewable: fish, forests or wind. Non-renewable: oil, coal or metal ores.
4. It does not produce anything itself; it is used to buy factors.
5. Not when used for personal sewing at home; it is capital when used to make clothes for sale.
6. Geographical: moving between places. Occupational: moving between jobs or uses.
7. Any two: skills and qualifications, cost and time of retraining, age, licensing rules.
8. Land.
9. Any two: larger working-age population; immigration; more people choosing to work; later retirement.
10. Any two: education, training, healthcare, experience.
11. 50 − 5 + 8 = **53**.
12. Any one: higher taxes on profit; heavy regulation; difficulty borrowing.
