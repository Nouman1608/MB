Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) is a single-science
course covering the variety of living organisms, how they work, how
they reproduce and inherit characteristics, how they interact with
their environment and how humans use biological resources. It is taken
by students who want a full biology qualification rather than a
combined science award, and it is a useful foundation for further study
in the sciences. Assessment is linear and untiered: Paper 1 lasts
2 hours for 110 marks (61.1%), and Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 15 minutes for
70 marks (38.9%). The specification was first taught in September 2017
and first assessed in June 2019, and the current version is Issue 3.

## How the course is organised

The specification is set out in five content areas.

1. **The nature and variety of living organisms** introduces the main
   groups of living things and the features that distinguish them.
2. **Structures and functions in living organisms** is by far the
   largest area. It works through levels of organisation, cell
   structure, biological molecules, the movement of substances into and
   out of cells, nutrition, respiration, gas exchange, transport,
   excretion, and co-ordination and response.
3. **Reproduction and inheritance** covers reproduction in plants and
   animals and the principles of inheritance.
4. **Ecology and the environment** examines how organisms interact with
   each other and with their surroundings.
5. **Use of biological resources** looks at how biological knowledge is
   applied in food production and other human uses.

Within the specification, some content is marked in bold. That
distinction matters for assessment, as the next section explains.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content assessed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4BI1/1B | Biology Paper 1 | 2 hours | 110 | 61.1% | Core (non-bold) content only |
| 4BI1/2B | Biology Paper 2 | 1 hour 15 minutes | 70 | 38.9% | All content, including bold |

Both papers are externally assessed written exams taken in the same
series. There is no tier choice: every candidate sits the same two
papers.

The key point is the role of the bold-referenced content. Paper 1 tests
only the core statements. Paper 2 can test anything in the
specification, including the bold material, which is examined only on
that paper. So although Paper 2 is the shorter paper, it is the only
place where the full range of the specification can appear. This
pattern is shared with Pearson's International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1)
and Physics (4PH1).

## How the parts fit together

The second content area, structures and functions, is the backbone of
the course, and most other areas lean on it. Respiration and gas
exchange explain how energy is released and how oxygen reaches cells;
transport explains how materials move around the body; nutrition and
the movement of substances into and out of cells explain how organisms
obtain and use what they need. Once those are understood, ecology makes
more sense, because energy flow and nutrient cycling are the same
processes viewed at the scale of whole communities.

Inheritance links back to cell structure and biological molecules, as
genes and chromosomes are part of the cell's machinery. The use of
biological resources draws on almost everything: an understanding of
microorganisms, plant growth, reproduction and genetics all feed into
how humans exploit living things.

Because Paper 2 can draw on any part of the specification, it rewards
students who connect topics rather than treating them as separate
chapters. Practical understanding also runs throughout, since methods, data
handling and evaluation belong to every area of biology, so practical
work is best treated as part of each topic rather than a separate one.

## Planning your study

A sensible order is to start with the foundations and build outwards:

- **Start with the variety of living organisms and the opening sections
  of structures and functions,** particularly levels of organisation,
  cell structure, biological molecules and movement across membranes.
- **Continue through the rest of structures and functions,** taking
  nutrition, respiration, gas exchange, transport, excretion and
  co-ordination in turn.
- **Move to reproduction and inheritance,** then **ecology** and the
  **use of biological resources**, which draw together earlier
  learning.

Across a two-year course, a reasonable target is to complete most of
structures and functions in the first year and the remaining areas in
the second, leaving time for review. Keep a clear record of which
statements are bold, so that you revise them deliberately for Paper 2
rather than by accident. Begin full past papers once all five areas
have been covered, and use the exam-preparation page for paper-by-paper
advice on timing.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Edexcel IGCSE Biology 4BI1 tiered?**
No. The qualification is untiered, so every candidate sits the same two papers.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2 in 4BI1?**
Paper 1 lasts 2 hours, is worth 110 marks (61.1%) and assesses only the core, non-bold content. Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 15 minutes, is worth 70 marks (38.9%) and can assess all content, including bold-referenced material.

**What does bold content mean in IGCSE Biology?**
Some specification statements are marked in bold. These are assessed only on Paper 2, while Paper 1 covers the remaining core content.

**Are both 4BI1 papers taken in the same series?**
Yes. The qualification is linear, so both papers are taken in the same series.

**When was the current 4BI1 specification first assessed?**
It was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in June 2019. The current version is Issue 3.
