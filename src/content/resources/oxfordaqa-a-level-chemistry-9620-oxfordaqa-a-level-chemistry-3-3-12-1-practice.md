This set practises **3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [condensation polymers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-12-1/) or the [condensation polymers revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-12-1-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which polymer is made from a single type of monomer by condensation polymerisation?

A: poly(propene)

B: nylon 6, from 6-aminohexanoic acid

C: Terylene

D: nylon 6,6

**[1]**

**2.** The repeating unit of a polymer is –[O–CH₂CH₂CH₂–O–CO–C₆H₄–CO]–, where C₆H₄ is a benzene ring bonded at positions 1 and 4.

**(a)** Name the type of linkage and the type of polymer. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the two monomers. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each repeating unit belongs to an addition polymer or a condensation polymer.

**(a)** –[CH₂–CH(CN)]– **[1]**

**(b)** –[NH–CH(CH₃)–CO]– **[1]**

**4.** A nylon is made from butane-1,4-diamine and hexanedioic acid. Give the name of the nylon. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**5.** Propane-1,3-diol, HO–(CH₂)₃–OH, reacts with hexanedioic acid, HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit of the polymer. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the polymerisation of n molecules of each monomer. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the Mr of the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(d)** A chain has Mr = 3.72 × 10⁴. Calculate the number of repeating units in it. **[1]**

**6.** A section of a polymer chain is:

`–O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–(CH₂)₃–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–(CH₂)₃–CO–`

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the structures and names of the two monomers. **[2]**

**7.** A polyamide made from two amino acids contains this section:

`–NH–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–CO–NH–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–CO–`

**(a)** Give the structures of the two amino acids. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**8.** Nylon 6,10 and a polyester of similar chain length are compared. Nylon 6,10 has the higher melting point. Explain this difference in terms of the forces between the chains. **[4]**

**9.** This question is about Kevlar.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit of Kevlar. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why Kevlar fibres are very strong. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in Kevlar. The repeating unit is C₁₄H₁₀N₂O₂. **[1]**

**10.** The repeating unit of a polyamide has the formula C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂. One of its monomers is hexane-1,6-diamine, C₆H₁₆N₂.

**(a)** Deduce the molecular formula of the other monomer and name it. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the polymer. **[1]**

**11.** Nylon 6 is made from 6-aminohexanoic acid, H₂N–(CH₂)₅–COOH (Mr = 131.0).

**(a)** Write an equation for the polymerisation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of monomer needed to make 1.00 kg of nylon 6, assuming complete conversion. **[2]**

**12.** State one typical use of nylon 6,6 and one typical use of Terylene, and link each use to a property of the polymer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Poly(propene) is an addition polymer; Terylene and nylon 6,6 each need two monomers.

**2. (a)** Ester link; polyester [1]

**(b)** Propane-1,3-diol, HO–(CH₂)₃–OH [1]; benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid, HOOC–C₆H₄–COOH [1]

**3. (a)** Addition polymer (from CH₂=CHCN) [1]

**(b)** Condensation polymer (a polyamide from an amino acid) [1]

**4.** Nylon 4,6 [1]

**5. (a)** –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–O–(CH₂)₃–O]–. Correct ester links and residues [1]; brackets with bonds through them [1]

**(b)** n HOOC–(CH₂)₄–COOH + n HO–(CH₂)₃–OH → –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–O–(CH₂)₃–O]ₙ– + 2n H₂O [1]

**(c)** C₉H₁₄O₄: 146.0 + 76.0 − 36.0 = **186.0** [1]

**(d)** 3.72 × 10⁴ ÷ 186.0 = **200** [1]

**6. (a)** –[O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–(CH₂)₃–CO]– (or starting at the CO) [1]

**(b)** HO–CH₂CH₂–OH, ethane-1,2-diol [1]; HOOC–(CH₂)₃–COOH, pentanedioic acid [1]

*Examiner insight:* count the carbons between and including the two C=O groups: three CH₂ plus two C=O makes five, so the acid is pentanedioic acid, not propanedioic acid.

**7. (a)** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COOH (2-aminopropanoic acid) [1]; H₂N–CH₂–COOH (aminoethanoic acid) [1]

**(b)** –[NH–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–CO]– [1]

**8.** Nylon 6,10 chains are held together by hydrogen bonds [1]; between the δ+ H of N–H groups and lone pairs on the δ− O of C=O groups on neighbouring chains [1]; polyester chains have no H bonded to O or N, so only permanent dipole–dipole forces act between C=O groups [1]; hydrogen bonds are stronger, so more energy is needed to separate nylon chains [1]

**9. (a)** –[CO–C₆H₄–CO–NH–C₆H₄–NH]–, with 1,4-substituted rings [1]

**(b)** The benzene rings make the chains flat and rigid [1]; the chains line up parallel to each other [1]; so many N–H and C=O groups on neighbouring chains form hydrogen bonds [1]

**(c)** 28.0 ÷ 238.0 × 100 = **11.8%** [1]

**10. (a)** C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂ + 2H₂O − C₆H₁₆N₂ = C₆H₁₀O₄ [1]; hexanedioic acid [1]

**(b)** Nylon 6,6 [1]

**11. (a)** n H₂N–(CH₂)₅–COOH → –[NH–(CH₂)₅–CO]ₙ– + n H₂O [1]

**(b)** Repeating unit Mr = 131.0 − 18.0 = 113.0; n(units) = 1000 ÷ 113.0 = 8.85 mol [1]; mass of monomer = 8.85 × 131.0 = 1.16 × 10³ g = **1.16 kg** [1]

**12.** Nylon 6,6: ropes (or clothing, carpets), because its fibres are strong due to hydrogen bonding between chains [1]; Terylene: drinks bottles (or clothing fibres), because it is strong and can be moulded or spun into fibres [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Bonds not drawn through the brackets of a repeating unit.
- Wrong number of water molecules in the equation.
- Miscounting carbons when naming diacids and nylons.
- Hydrogen bonding claimed between polyester chains.
- Forces explained without naming the groups on neighbouring chains.
