Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580) is a broad secondary mathematics course covering number, algebra, geometry, trigonometry, vectors, probability and statistics. It is tiered: Core candidates sit Paper 1, Non-calculator (Core), and Paper 3, Calculator (Core), each 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, while Extended candidates sit Paper 2, Non-calculator (Extended), and Paper 4, Calculator (Extended), each 2 hours and 100 marks. On either tier, the two papers are worth 50% each. Core candidates are eligible for grades C to G and Extended candidates for A* to E. This page sets out the content, explains the two tiers and offers a general plan for working through the course.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus content is arranged in nine areas.

- **Number** is the largest area, running from types of number, sets, powers and roots, and fractions, decimals and percentages, through standard form, estimation, limits of accuracy, ratio and proportion, rates and percentages, to using a calculator, time, money, exponential growth and decay, and surds.
- **Algebra and graphs** covers algebraic manipulation and fractions, indices, equations, inequalities, sequences, proportion, graphs in practical situations, graphs of functions, sketching curves, differentiation and functions.
- **Coordinate geometry** deals with coordinates, drawing linear graphs, gradient, length and midpoint, equations of linear graphs, and parallel and perpendicular lines.
- **Geometry** includes geometrical terms and constructions, scale drawings, similarity, symmetry, angles and circle theorems.
- **Mensuration** covers units of measure, area and perimeter, circles, arcs and sectors, surface area and volume, and compound shapes.
- **Trigonometry** runs from Pythagoras' theorem and right-angled triangles through exact values and trigonometric functions to non-right-angled triangles and problems in 3D.
- **Transformations and vectors** covers transformations, vectors in two dimensions, magnitude of a vector and vector geometry.
- **Probability** includes relative and expected frequencies, combined events and conditional probability.
- **Statistics** covers classifying and interpreting data, averages and range, charts and diagrams, scatter diagrams, cumulative frequency diagrams and histograms.

Not every subtopic is examined on both tiers. Check with your teacher, or the syllabus itself, which content is for Extended candidates only.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is linear: both papers for your tier are taken in the same series at the end of the course.

| Tier | Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Core | Paper 1 | Non-calculator (Core) | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Core | Paper 3 | Calculator (Core) | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Extended | Paper 2 | Non-calculator (Extended) | 2 hours | 100 | 50% |
| Extended | Paper 4 | Calculator (Extended) | 2 hours | 100 | 50% |

You are entered for one tier only, and the choice affects which grades are available: C to G on Core, A* to E on Extended. The decision is usually made by your school, based on your progress, so it is worth discussing early. On both tiers, one paper is taken without a calculator and one with, so mental and written methods matter as much as calculator skills. The syllabus covers the 2025-2027 examination window and was first examined in 2025.

## How the parts fit together

The nine content areas are listed separately, but in practice mathematics questions rarely stay inside one of them.

Number underpins everything: fractions, percentages, ratio, standard form and estimation appear in geometry, mensuration and statistics questions as often as in number questions. Algebra is the language that connects the rest. You will use it to form and solve equations from a geometry diagram, to find the equation of a line in coordinate geometry, and to work with expressions in trigonometry and vectors.

Some links are worth noticing early. Graphs of functions and sketching curves connect algebra with coordinate geometry. Similarity in geometry supports trigonometry and scale drawings. Vectors draw on coordinate ideas, and probability and statistics both depend on secure work with fractions and decimals.

The split between non-calculator and calculator papers also runs across all nine areas. Any topic can appear on either paper, so practise each one both ways: exact methods and sensible estimation without a calculator, and efficient, accurate calculator use where it is allowed.

## Planning your study

A sensible order starts with Number and the early parts of Algebra and graphs, because almost every later topic depends on them. From there, Coordinate geometry, Geometry and Mensuration can follow, with Trigonometry after similarity and Pythagoras' theorem are secure. Transformations and vectors, Probability and Statistics can be fitted in alongside or after.

In general terms:

- **Early in the course:** number skills, algebraic manipulation, equations and coordinates.
- **Middle of the course:** geometry, mensuration, sequences, graphs and the start of trigonometry.
- **Later in the course:** the remaining algebra and trigonometry, transformations and vectors, probability and statistics, followed by mixed revision across the whole syllabus.

Revisit earlier topics regularly with short mixed exercises so that skills do not fade. Start exam-style questions as each topic is completed, and work through full past papers for your tier in the final months, both with and without a calculator. ## Frequently asked questions

**Which papers do Core and Extended candidates sit in Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580?**
Core candidates sit Paper 1 Non-calculator (Core) and Paper 3 Calculator (Core). Extended candidates sit Paper 2 Non-calculator (Extended) and Paper 4 Calculator (Extended).

**What grades are available on each tier?**
Core candidates are eligible for grades C to G. Extended candidates are eligible for grades A* to E.

**How long are the 0580 papers?**
The Core papers are 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks each. The Extended papers are 2 hours and 100 marks each.

**Is there a non-calculator paper?**
Yes. On each tier, one paper is non-calculator (Paper 1 or Paper 2) and one allows a calculator (Paper 3 or Paper 4). Each is worth 50%.

**What are the main content areas of 0580?**
Number, Algebra and graphs, Coordinate geometry, Geometry, Mensuration, Trigonometry, Transformations and vectors, Probability, and Statistics.
