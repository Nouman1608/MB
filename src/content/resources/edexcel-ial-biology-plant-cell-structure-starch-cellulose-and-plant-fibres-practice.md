## Questions

**1.** State the function of each of the following in a plant.

**(a)** The middle lamella. **[1]**
**(b)** The tonoplast. **[1]**
**(c)** Plasmodesmata. **[1]**

**2.** Compare the ultrastructure of a palisade mesophyll cell with that of a human liver cell. Give two differences and two similarities. **[4]**

**3.** An electron micrograph shows part of a plant cell wall. A scale bar on the image is 20 mm long and represents 0.5 µm. The wall measures 46 mm across on the image.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the image. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the actual thickness of the wall, in µm. **[2]**

**4.** Starch is stored in potato tubers.

**(a)** Describe the structural differences between amylose and amylopectin. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two ways in which the properties of starch suit it as an energy store. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how the structure of cellulose, including the role of hydrogen bonds, makes cell walls strong. **[4]**

**6.** Explain how secondary thickening and the arrangement of cellulose microfibrils adapt xylem vessels for their functions. **[5]**

**7.** A transverse section of a herbaceous stem shows vascular bundles in a ring.

**(a)** State where sclerenchyma fibres, xylem vessels and phloem are found in the stem. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two structural differences between a xylem vessel and a phloem sieve tube, and link each to function. **[3]**

**8.** In Core Practical 7 a student prepares a plan diagram from a stem cross-section. The stem section has an actual diameter of 4.0 mm and the drawing is 120 mm across.

**(a)** State two rules for drawing a plan diagram. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the magnification of the drawing. **[1]**
**(c)** The xylem in one vascular bundle measures 15 mm radially on the drawing. Calculate its actual width, in µm. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why individual cells are not drawn on a plan diagram. **[1]**

**9.** Structure P is surrounded by two membranes and contains stacks of flattened discs in a granular background, with a few small pale oval grains. Structure R is also surrounded by two membranes, but contains several large pale grains and no stacked discs. Structure T is bounded by a single membrane and has a clear, featureless interior that occupies most of the cell.

**(a)** Identify P, R and T. **[3]**
**(b)** State one function of R. **[1]**

**10.** A company compares the carbon dioxide released in making 1 kg of three packaging materials. The data are supplied: oil-based plastic 3.1 kg, starch-based plastic 1.9 kg, hemp-fibre composite 2.4 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in carbon dioxide released for each plant-based material compared with the oil-based plastic. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the use of plant-based materials to replace oil-based plastics. **[4]**

**11.** The table gives data supplied for three fibre samples.

| Sample | Breaking force (N) | Cross-sectional area (mm²) | Microfibril angle (degrees) |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 540 | 0.9 | 10 |
| Y | 810 | 1.5 | 25 |
| Z | 380 | 0.4 | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate the breaking stress (force ÷ area) for each sample, in N per mm². **[3]**
**(b)** Describe and explain the relationship between microfibril angle and breaking stress. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why sclerenchyma fibres are suited to being made into rope. **[2]**

**12.** Describe and explain how water and dissolved substances could move from one parenchyma cell to the next through the wall, and how the structure of the wall allows this. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** It cements adjacent cells together (pectin-rich layer). [1]
**(b)** It is the membrane surrounding the vacuole and controls movement of substances into and out of it. [1]
**(c)** They are channels through cell walls carrying cytoplasm, allowing transport and communication between cells. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each part needs a function, not just a description of where the structure is; "found in the wall" alone is a description.

**2.** Differences, any two: plant cell has a cellulose cell wall, liver cell does not; plant cell has chloroplasts, liver cell does not; plant cell has a large permanent vacuole bounded by a tonoplast, liver cell has none or small vesicles (any two for [1][1]). Similarities, any two: both have a nucleus, mitochondria, rough ER, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes or a plasma membrane [1][1]. Credit any two differences and any two similarities; maximum 4.
*Examiner insight:* A comparison answer should name the feature in both cells; saying only "plants have a wall" may not show the comparison clearly.

**3. (a)** 20 mm = 20 000 µm, so magnification = 20 000 ÷ 0.5 [1] = **×40 000** [1]
**(b)** Actual = 46 000 ÷ 40 000 [1] = **1.15 µm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion; an answer in mm that is not converted is the usual way to lose the final mark.

**4. (a)** Amylose is unbranched with α-1,4 bonds and coiled into a helix [1]; amylopectin has α-1,4 chains with α-1,6 branches [1].
**(b)** It is insoluble, so does not affect water potential or leave the cell [1]; it is compact (coiled, or in grains) so it stores a lot of glucose in a small space, or it is easily hydrolysed to glucose for respiration [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" requires the property and the reason it matters for storage; naming the property alone gains little.

**5.** Cellulose is made of β-glucose in straight unbranched chains [1] because alternate residues are inverted in the β-1,4 links [1]. Hydrogen bonds form between –OH groups of parallel chains [1]. Each bond is weak but there are very many, forming microfibrils with high tensile strength that resist the cell bursting [1].
*Examiner insight:* State that the hydrogen bonds are between chains and that there are many; confusing them with glycosidic bonds is a common slip.

**6.** Secondary thickening adds extra wall inside the primary wall [1] containing lignin, which is rigid and waterproof [1]. This stops the vessel collapsing inwards under the tension of transpiration [1]. Microfibrils in layers at different angles give strength in different directions [1]. Lignin laid in rings, spirals or networks leaves unthickened gaps, and pits, which allow lateral water movement, and the dead empty lumen with no end walls forms a continuous tube [1].
*Examiner insight:* The points are credited when linked to function; a list of features without "so that" reasoning may not reach the top of the range.

**7. (a)** Sclerenchyma: outer part of vascular bundles or in the cortex [1]; xylem: inner side of each vascular bundle [1]; phloem: outer side of each vascular bundle, outside the xylem [1].
**(b)** Xylem vessel has no end walls and an empty lumen, giving continuous flow of water [1]; sieve tube has sieve plates and living cytoplasm so solutes can be loaded and moved [1]; xylem walls are lignified for strength and support under tension, sieve tube walls are thin and unlignified [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give a difference and the link to function for each point; a bare difference does not usually earn the explanation mark.

**8. (a)** Any two: draw outlines of tissues only, no shading, sharp single lines, correct proportions, title and magnification, ruled label lines **[1][1]**
**(b)** 120 ÷ 4.0 = **×30** [1]
**(c)** 15 ÷ 30 [1] = 0.5 mm = **500 µm** [1]
**(d)** A plan diagram shows only the arrangement and proportions of the tissues, and cell detail would obscure this. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the final answer in the requested unit; 0.5 mm alone may be treated as incomplete because the question asks for µm.

**9. (a)** P is a chloroplast [1]; R is an amyloplast [1]; T is a vacuole [1].
**(b)** It stores starch. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Support each identification with a visible feature if asked to "justify"; here the stacked discs distinguish P from R.

**10. (a)** Starch-based: (3.1 − 1.9) ÷ 3.1 × 100 [1] = 38.7% [1]. Hemp composite: (3.1 − 2.4) ÷ 3.1 × 100 = **22.6%** [1]
**(b)** Advantages: renewable crops, carbon dioxide taken up by the plants as they grow, and less carbon dioxide released in manufacture on these figures [1]; many products biodegrade, reducing persistent plastic waste [1]. Limitations: land, water and fertiliser needed, with possible competition with food crops [1]; processing uses energy and some bioplastics need industrial composting; the data cover only carbon dioxide from manufacture, not the whole life [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs both sides and a judgement based on the data; quoting a percentage is a start, but a conclusion is needed.

**11. (a)** X: 540 ÷ 0.9 = 600 [1]; Y: 810 ÷ 1.5 = 540 [1]; Z: 380 ÷ 0.4 = **950** N per mm² [1]
**(b)** The smaller the microfibril angle, the greater the breaking stress [1] (5° gives 950, 10° gives 600, 25° gives 540). [1] Microfibrils nearly parallel to the fibre axis share the pulling force along their length, with hydrogen-bonded chains resisting being pulled apart. [1]
**(c)** They have thick lignified walls and high tensile strength [1], and they are long and flexible and can be twisted together. [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Describe and explain" needs data quoted from the table and a reason; a trend without figures is only part of an answer.

**12.** Water and solutes can move through the cell wall itself, because the cellulose and matrix are fully permeable [1]. At a pit, the wall is thinner because no secondary wall has been laid down [1], so less material is crossed [1]. Plasmodesmata are channels through the wall, lined with the plasma membrane and containing cytoplasm [1], so substances can pass directly from cytoplasm to cytoplasm without crossing a membrane [1].
*Examiner insight:* Distinguish a pit (thin wall, no channel) from a plasmodesma (channel with cytoplasm); using the terms interchangeably loses marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing where a structure is when asked for its function.
- Saying the cell wall "controls" what enters, when it is fully permeable and the plasma membrane controls entry.
- Not converting units before working out magnification.
- Writing that hydrogen bonds join glucose units in a chain.
- Calling cellulose branched or helical.
- Stating that xylem is living, or omitting lignin when explaining strength.
- Drawing cells or shading in a plan diagram.
- Quoting percentages in an evaluation without a balanced judgement.
- Describing a trend without figures from the table.
