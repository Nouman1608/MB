These notes condense **3.2.2.4 Motivation** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [motivation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-motivation-9725/) applies each theory and costs a piece rate scheme; the [motivation practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-motivation-9725-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **Benefits** of motivated employees: productivity, quality, lower turnover and absence, better service.
- **Theories**: Taylor's scientific management; Maslow's hierarchy (self-actualisation, esteem, social, safety, basic); Herzberg's two-factor (hygiene and motivators).
- **Financial methods**: wages, piece rate pay, commission, salaries, performance-related pay, fringe benefits.
- **Non-financial methods**: job enlargement, job enrichment, job rotation, empowerment, teamwork.
- **Influences** on choosing a method and judging its effectiveness.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The will to work and achieve goals |
| Scientific management | Taylor's approach: find the best method for each task, train, supervise and pay by output |
| Hierarchy of needs | Maslow's five levels of need, lower levels to be met before higher ones motivate |
| Hygiene factors | Herzberg: features that cause dissatisfaction if poor, such as pay and conditions |
| Motivators | Herzberg: features that create lasting satisfaction, such as achievement and responsibility |
| Wages | Pay per hour worked, usually paid weekly |
| Piece rate pay | Pay per unit produced |
| Commission | Pay as a percentage of the value of sales made |
| Salary | A fixed annual amount, paid monthly |
| Performance-related pay | Extra pay for meeting agreed targets |
| Fringe benefits | Non-wage rewards such as a company car, health insurance or discounts |
| Job enlargement | More tasks at the same level |
| Job enrichment | More responsibility and more demanding tasks |
| Job rotation | Moving employees between different tasks |
| Empowerment | Giving employees authority to make decisions about their own work |

## Theories in three lines each

**Taylor**: money motivates; break jobs into simple tasks; pay by output. Suits routine, measurable work. Ignores social needs; quality can suffer.

**Maslow**: basic → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation. Meet the lowest unmet need. Needs differ between people and change over time.

**Herzberg**: fix hygiene factors to remove dissatisfaction; use motivators (enrichment, responsibility, recognition) to motivate. Pay is a hygiene factor.

## Matching methods to theories

| Method | Theory link |
|---|---|
| Piece rate, commission | Taylor |
| Secure contracts, safe conditions | Maslow: safety; Herzberg: hygiene |
| Teamwork | Maslow: social |
| Recognition, promotion | Maslow: esteem; Herzberg: motivators |
| Job enrichment, empowerment | Maslow: self-actualisation; Herzberg: motivators |
| Job enlargement, rotation | Variety only; little extra responsibility |

## Pay calculations

```
Piece rate pay        = rate per unit × units produced
Commission            = commission rate × value of sales
Total pay (basic + commission) = basic pay + commission
Performance bonus     = bonus % × salary
Labour cost per unit  = total pay ÷ units produced (or ÷ good units if faults are given)
```

## Influences on choice

Nature of work · cost and cash · employees' needs and expectations · management style and culture · staff skills · competition for labour.

## Judging effectiveness

- Compare **before and after**: productivity, turnover, absence, quality, customer satisfaction.
- Allow time; look for other causes.
- Weigh the **cost** of the scheme against the **gain**: check labour cost per unit.

## Applying a theory in four steps

1. **Identify** the problem in the case (high turnover, boredom, low output, poor quality).
2. **Diagnose** it with a theory: which Maslow level is unmet, or which Herzberg hygiene factor is causing dissatisfaction?
3. **Prescribe** a method that fits the diagnosis and the nature of the work.
4. **Judge** it: cost, time to work, and how success will be measured.

**Example.** Call-centre staff are paid above the local average but leave after a few months, saying the work is dull. Pay (a hygiene factor) is already sound, so more pay is unlikely to help. Job enrichment, such as letting experienced staff handle complex cases and train new starters, targets motivators and esteem needs. Measure labour turnover and the one-year retention rate after six months.

## Exam traps

- Pay is a **hygiene factor** for Herzberg, not a motivator.
- Enlargement (more tasks) ≠ enrichment (more responsibility).
- Taylor is about efficiency and pay by output, not about teamwork.
- Apply a theory to the workers in the case; do not just describe it.
- A method only "works" if it improves a measurable outcome by more than it costs.

## Self-test

1. Name the five levels of Maslow's hierarchy as listed in the specification.
2. Give two of Herzberg's hygiene factors.
3. Give two of Herzberg's motivators.
4. What did Taylor believe was the main motivator for workers?
5. A worker is paid $0.40 per unit and makes 900 units. Calculate their pay.
6. A salesperson earns a basic $900 a month plus 5% commission on sales of $18,000. Calculate total pay.
7. A manager on a $38,000 salary earns a 6% performance bonus. Calculate the bonus.
8. Distinguish job enlargement from job enrichment.
9. Give one disadvantage of piece rate pay.
10. Which non-financial method best meets social needs?
11. Give one influence on the choice of motivation method.
12. How could a business assess whether a new bonus scheme has worked?

### Answers

1. Basic, safety, social, esteem, self-actualisation.
2. Any two: pay, working conditions, supervision, company policy, relationships at work.
3. Any two: achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself, advancement.
4. **Money**.
5. 0.40 × 900 = **$360**.
6. 900 + (0.05 × 18,000) = 900 + 900 = **$1,800**.
7. 0.06 × 38,000 = **$2,280**.
8. Enlargement adds more tasks at the same level; enrichment adds responsibility and more demanding work.
9. Workers may rush, so quality falls (or income becomes unpredictable).
10. **Teamwork**.
11. Nature of the work, cost, staff needs, management style, staff skills, competition for labour (any one).
12. Compare productivity, labour turnover or absence before and after, against the cost of the scheme.
