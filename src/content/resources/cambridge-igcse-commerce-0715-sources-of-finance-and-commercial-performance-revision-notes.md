Condensed notes for section 5.4 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, two worked examples and an annotated answer, use the [sources of finance study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-sources-of-finance-and-commercial-performance/).

## The syllabus in three parts

1. **5.4.1 Raising finance:** entrepreneurship, short-term and long-term sources, advantages and disadvantages, choosing a source, financial terms.
2. **5.4.2 Commercial calculations:** revenue, gross profit, gross profit margin, net profit, working capital, mark-up.
3. **5.4.3 Improving commercial performance:** raising turnover, profit, mark-up and working capital.

## Short-term sources (under about a year)

| Source | Key point | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Trade credit | Buy now, pay later | Lose cash discount; supplier may stop credit |
| Debt factoring | Sell invoices for cash now | Factor takes a fee |
| Hire purchase | Deposit plus instalments | Costs more than cash price |
| Leasing | Rent equipment | Never own it |
| Sale and leaseback | Sell asset, rent it back | Lose ownership; pay rent |
| Overdraft | Spend above balance to a limit | High interest; can be withdrawn |
| Selling assets | Cash from unused items | One-off |
| Owner's savings | Own money in the business | Limited; owner's risk |
| Retained profit | Profit kept back | Needs profit first |

## Long-term sources (over about a year)

| Source | Key point | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Ordinary shares | Owners' money; variable dividends | Shared control |
| Preference shares | Fixed dividend paid first | Dividend must be paid first |
| Loans | Fixed sum, repaid with interest | Interest must be paid; may need security |
| Debentures | Long-term loan with fixed interest and repayment date | Interest must be paid |
| Venture capital | Investment in risky or new firms for a share | Investors take part of ownership |
| Crowdfunding | Many small sums from the public | May not reach the target |

## Choosing a source: six tests

Mnemonic **"Large Hats Cost Cash, Raising Risk"**:

| Letter | Test | Ask |
|---|---|---|
| L | Length of time | Short need or long? |
| H | How much | Small or large sum? |
| C | Cost | Interest, fees, profit shared? |
| C | Control | Will owners lose control? |
| R | Repayment ability | Can the firm afford it? |
| R | Risk and security | Is there security? Is the business new? |

Matching examples: **stock before payday** is overdraft or trade credit. **A new van** is hire purchase, leasing or a loan. **A risky start-up** is crowdfunding or venture capital. **A new factory** is debentures, shares or a long-term loan. **Unpaid invoices** are debt factoring.

## Financial terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneurship | Setting up and running a business, taking risk |
| Sales | Goods or services sold |
| Revenue | Money from sales |
| Profit | Revenue minus costs |
| Working capital | Current assets minus current liabilities |
| Assets | Things the business owns |
| Liabilities | Money the business owes |
| Interest | Cost of borrowing or reward for saving |
| Capital | Money put into the business |
| Cash discount | Price reduction for quick or cash payment |
| Trade discount | Price reduction between businesses, often for large orders |

## The six formulae

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Price per unit x units sold |
| Gross profit | Revenue - cost of sales |
| Gross profit margin (%) | Gross profit / revenue x 100 |
| Net profit | Gross profit - expenses |
| Working capital | Current assets - current liabilities |
| Mark-up (%) | (Selling price - unit cost) / unit cost x 100 |

Worked pattern: sell 500 units at $12; cost of sales $3,900; expenses $1,100.

- Revenue = 500 x 12 = $6,000.
- Gross profit = 6,000 - 3,900 = $2,100.
- Gross profit margin = 2,100 / 6,000 x 100 = 35%.
- Net profit = 2,100 - 1,100 = $1,000.
- Unit cost = 3,900 / 500 = $7.80. Mark-up = (12 - 7.80) / 7.80 x 100 = 53.8%.

Margin is based on the **selling price**; mark-up is based on the **cost**.

## Ways to improve performance

| Aim | Methods |
|---|---|
| Raise turnover | Improve advertising; new products; change prices; wider range |
| Raise profit | Reduce expenses; raise mark-up; cut cost of sales through better discounts |
| Raise mark-up | Raise the selling price or buy cheaper stock |
| Improve working capital | Collect debts faster; hold less stock; use trade credit; delay big payments |

Each method has a risk: cutting expenses may harm service; raising prices may lose customers; advertising costs money; a new product may fail.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Short-term finance | Money needed for less than about a year |
| Long-term finance | Money needed for more than a year |
| Debt factoring | Selling invoices to a firm for immediate cash |
| Leasing | Renting equipment |
| Sale and leaseback | Selling an asset and renting it back |
| Debenture | Long-term loan to a company with fixed interest |
| Venture capital | Investment in risky or new firms in return for a share |
| Crowdfunding | Raising small sums from many people, usually online |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Cost of sales | Cost of buying the stock that was sold |

## Exam traps

- **Gross profit** is before expenses; **net profit** is after expenses.
- Margin uses **revenue**; mark-up uses **cost**.
- Multiply by 100 for percentages.
- Match the source to the **time**, **amount** and **risk** in the case.
- A working capital figure is **not** cash in hand.
- Retained profit and owner's savings are **internal** sources; most others come from outside the business.

## Self-test

1. Define entrepreneurship.
2. Name **three** short-term sources of finance.
3. Name **three** long-term sources of finance.
4. How does debt factoring help a business?
5. State **one** disadvantage of leasing.
6. What is the difference between ordinary and preference shares?
7. What is crowdfunding?
8. Which source suits a firm with unpaid customer invoices?
9. Define working capital.
10. Give the formula for gross profit margin.
11. A firm has revenue $80,000, cost of sales $48,000 and expenses $20,000. Calculate gross profit, gross profit margin and net profit.
12. An item costs $40 and sells for $55. Calculate the mark-up percentage.
13. State **two** ways of improving net profit.

**Answers**

1. Setting up a business, taking the risks and organising the resources.
2. Any three: trade credit, debt factoring, hire purchase, leasing, sale and leaseback, overdraft, selling assets, owner's savings, retained profit.
3. Any three: ordinary shares, preference shares, loans, debentures, venture capital, crowdfunding.
4. The business receives most of the value of its invoices at once, instead of waiting for customers to pay.
5. Any one: the item is never owned; payments continue; cost over time may be higher.
6. Ordinary shares give variable dividends and usually voting rights; preference shares have a fixed dividend paid first and usually no vote.
7. Raising small sums from many people, usually through a website.
8. Debt factoring.
9. Current assets minus current liabilities.
10. Gross profit / revenue x 100.
11. Gross profit = 80,000 - 48,000 = $32,000. Margin = 32,000 / 80,000 x 100 = 40%. Net profit = 32,000 - 20,000 = $12,000.
12. (55 - 40) / 40 x 100 = 37.5%.
13. Any two: reduce expenses; increase mark-up; change prices; improve advertising; introduce new products.
