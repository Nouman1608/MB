## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule |
| Oxidation number (Roman numeral) | For a simple metal ion, equal to its charge: iron(III) = Fe³⁺ |
| Ionic equation | Equation showing only the species that change; no spectator ions |
| Spectator ion | Ion present but unchanged on both sides of an equation |
| Hydrated | Crystalline compound containing water of crystallisation |
| Anhydrous | Without water of crystallisation |
| Water of crystallisation | Water molecules held in a crystal, written after a dot |

## Writing ionic formulas

**Method**
1. Write both ions with charges.
2. Find the lowest common multiple of the charge sizes.
3. Use enough of each ion to reach it.
4. Bracket a polyatomic ion if more than one is needed.

| Compound | Formula |
|---|---|
| Calcium phosphate | Ca₃(PO₄)₂ |
| Ammonium sulfate | (NH₄)₂SO₄ |
| Zinc nitrate | Zn(NO₃)₂ |
| Iron(II) hydroxide | Fe(OH)₂ |
| Copper(I) oxide | Cu₂O |
| Lead(II) nitrate | Pb(NO₃)₂ |
| Sodium hydrogencarbonate | NaHCO₃ |
| Silver phosphate | Ag₃PO₄ |

## Equations

- Balance with coefficients only; never change subscripts.
- State symbols: (s) solid or precipitate, (l) liquid, (g) gas, (aq) dissolved in water.

**Method: ionic equation**
1. Full balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Split only aqueous ionic compounds (and strong acids in solution) into ions.
3. Cancel spectator ions.
4. Check atoms and total charge.

| Reaction type | Ionic equation |
|---|---|
| Acid + alkali | H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) |
| Silver halide precipitate | Ag⁺(aq) + Br⁻(aq) → AgBr(s) |
| Solid carbonate + acid | CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) |
| Metal displacement | Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) → Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s) |
| Carbonate ions + acid | CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) |

## Empirical and molecular formulas

**Method: empirical formula**
1. Mass (or %) of each element.
2. Divide by Ar to get moles.
3. Divide by the smallest.
4. If a ratio ends in .5, .33, .67, .25 or .75, multiply all by 2, 3 or 4.

**Molecular formula** = empirical formula × (Mr ÷ empirical formula mass).

**From combustion data**
- Mass of C = mass of CO₂ × 12.0 ÷ 44.0.
- Mass of H = mass of H₂O × 2.0 ÷ 18.0.
- Mass of O = sample mass − (C + H), if positive.

| Data | Empirical | Molecular |
|---|---|---|
| 40.0 % C, 6.7 % H, 53.3 % O; Mr 60.0 | CH₂O | C₂H₄O₂ |
| 72.4 % Fe, 27.6 % O | Fe₃O₄ | (ionic: none) |
| 0.600 g → 1.76 g CO₂ + 1.08 g H₂O; Mr 30.0 | CH₃ | C₂H₆ |

## Hydrates

**Method: find x in MX·xH₂O**
1. Mass of water = hydrated mass − anhydrous mass.
2. Moles of water = mass ÷ 18.0.
3. Moles of anhydrous salt = mass ÷ its formula mass.
4. x = moles of water ÷ moles of salt.

Example: 2.50 g CuSO₄·xH₂O → 1.60 g CuSO₄: x = 0.0500 ÷ 0.0100 = 5.

Heat to constant mass, or water remains and x is too low.

## Exam traps

- Brackets missing: Ca₃(PO₄)₂, not Ca₃PO₄₂.
- Roman numeral used as a subscript.
- Solids, water and gases split into ions.
- Charges unbalanced in an ionic equation.
- Ratios like 1 : 1.5 rounded instead of doubled.
- Dividing % by the wrong Ar, or by Mr of the compound.
- Ignoring oxygen in combustion analysis.

## Self-test

**1.** Give the formulas of aluminium sulfate and ammonium phosphate.

**2.** Give the formula of iron(III) nitrate.

**3.** Name the compound Cu(OH)₂.

**4.** Write the ionic equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of barium chloride solution with sodium sulfate solution to form a precipitate of barium sulfate.

**5.** Identify the spectator ions in the reaction of hydrochloric acid with sodium hydroxide.

**6.** Define empirical formula.

**7.** A hydrocarbon is 85.7 % carbon by mass and has Mr 56.0. Find its empirical and molecular formulas.

**8.** A phosphorus oxide contains 0.31 g P combined with 0.40 g O. Find its empirical formula.

**9.** What does "anhydrous" mean?

**10.** 2.86 g of Na₂CO₃·xH₂O gives 1.06 g of anhydrous Na₂CO₃ (formula mass 106.0) on heating. Find x.

**11.** Why must a hydrate be heated to constant mass?

**12.** Balance: Al(s) + O₂(g) → Al₂O₃(s).

## Self-test answers

**1.** Al₂(SO₄)₃; (NH₄)₃PO₄.

**2.** Fe(NO₃)₃.

**3.** Copper(II) hydroxide.

**4.** Ba²⁺(aq) + SO₄²⁻(aq) → BaSO₄(s).

**5.** Na⁺ and Cl⁻.

**6.** The simplest whole-number ratio of the atoms of each element in a compound.

**7.** C 7.14 mol, H 14.3 mol; ratio 1 : 2; CH₂ (mass 14.0); 56.0 ÷ 14.0 = 4; C₄H₈.

**8.** P 0.0100 mol, O 0.0250 mol; ratio 1 : 2.5 = 2 : 5; P₂O₅.

**9.** Containing no water of crystallisation.

**10.** Water 1.80 g = 0.100 mol; Na₂CO₃ 0.0100 mol; x = 10.

**11.** So that all the water of crystallisation is removed; otherwise the value of x is too low.

**12.** 4Al(s) + 3O₂(g) → 2Al₂O₃(s).
