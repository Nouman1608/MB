## Section A

**1.** Name the piece of apparatus used in a titration to:

**(a)** measure exactly 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a flask **[1]**

**(b)** add acid to the alkali and measure the volume added **[1]**

**2.** Which indicator is **not** suitable for an acid–base titration?

A: methyl orange

B: thymolphthalein

C: universal indicator

D: both A and B are unsuitable

**[1]**

**3.** Methyl orange is added to aqueous sodium hydroxide in a conical flask. Dilute hydrochloric acid is added from a burette.

**(a)** State the colour of the solution before any acid is added. **[1]**

**(b)** State the colour at the end-point. **[1]**

**4.** Define the term **end-point** of a titration. **[1]**

**5.** A burette reads 0.50 cm³ at the start of a titration and 21.35 cm³ at the end. Calculate the titre. **[1]**

**6.** State **two** reasons for using a conical flask rather than a beaker in a titration. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student carries out a titration but makes three errors. For each error, state the effect on the measured titre (too high, too low or no effect) and explain your answer.

**(a)** The burette was rinsed with distilled water but not with the acid before filling. **[2]**

**(b)** The conical flask still contained a few drops of distilled water. **[2]**

**(c)** An air bubble in the burette tip escaped during the titration. **[2]**

**8.** The results of a titration are shown.

| | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final reading / cm³ | 19.20 | 37.85 | 18.75 | 37.40 |
| Initial reading / cm³ | 0.00 | 19.20 | 0.00 | 18.75 |
| Titre / cm³ | 19.20 | | | |

**(a)** Complete the titre row. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the concordant results. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean titre to use. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how to carry out a titration to find the volume of dilute nitric acid needed to neutralise 25.0 cm³ of aqueous potassium hydroxide, using thymolphthalein. Your answer should name the apparatus and state the observation at the end-point. **[5]**

**10.** Explain why a student adds acid drop by drop as the end-point approaches, and why the solution is swirled after each addition. **[2]**

**11.** 25.0 cm³ of aqueous sodium hydroxide is neutralised by a mean titre of 18.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ nitric acid.

HNO₃ + NaOH → NaNO₃ + H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of nitric acid used. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide, in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**12.** A student wants to make pure crystals of sodium sulfate from aqueous sodium hydroxide and dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Explain why the salt is made by titration rather than by adding an excess of a solid to the acid. **[1]**

**(b)** After finding the volume of acid needed using methyl orange, the student repeats the titration. Describe how the repeat differs and why. **[2]**

**13.** A student reads the burette with their eye above the level of the liquid surface. Suggest what error this causes and how to avoid it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Volumetric pipette [1]

**(b)** Burette [1]

**[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* universal indicator changes colour gradually across a range of pH, so the end-point cannot be seen as a single sharp change.

**3. (a)** Yellow [1]

**(b)** Orange [1]

**[2]**

**4.** The point at which the indicator changes colour (permanently), showing that the acid and alkali have exactly reacted / neutralised [1]. **[1]**

**5.** 21.35 − 0.50 = 20.85 cm³ [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two [2]: it can be swirled without the contents spilling; its narrow neck reduces loss of liquid by splashing; the narrow neck sits under the burette tip so every drop lands inside.

**[2]**

**7. (a)** Too high [1]. Water left in the burette dilutes the acid, so more acid solution is needed to neutralise the alkali [1].

**(b)** No effect [1]. The water does not change the amount (moles) of alkali in the flask [1].

**(c)** Too high [1]. The volume of the bubble is recorded as acid added, although no acid was delivered for that volume [1].

**[6]**

**8. (a)** Titration 1: 18.65 cm³; titration 2: 18.75 cm³; titration 3: 18.65 cm³. Two correct [1]; all three correct [1].

**(b)** All three accurate titres are within 0.10 cm³ of each other: 18.65, 18.75 and 18.65 cm³ [1].

**(c)** (18.65 + 18.75 + 18.65) ÷ 3 = 18.68 cm³ [1] (accept 18.683 or 18.7 cm³ with working).

**[4]**

**9.** Five marks from:

- use a volumetric pipette (and filler) to put 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide into a conical flask [1]
- add a few drops of thymolphthalein; the solution turns blue [1]
- fill a burette with nitric acid and record the initial reading [1]
- add acid while swirling, dropwise near the end-point [1]
- stop when the solution turns from blue to colourless and record the final reading [1]
- repeat to obtain concordant titres (max 5)

**[5]**

**10.** Dropwise: so that the end-point is not overshot / a single drop can be seen to cause the colour change [1]. Swirling: so the acid mixes with all the alkali, and a colour change is not caused by acid that has not yet mixed [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** moles = 0.100 × (18.40 ÷ 1000) [1] = 0.00184 mol [1]

**(b)** 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.00184 mol NaOH; concentration = 0.00184 ÷ (25.0 ÷ 1000) [1] = 0.0736 mol/dm³ [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* the most common slip is forgetting to divide the volumes by 1000 to convert cm³ into dm³.

**12. (a)** Both reactants are soluble / sodium hydroxide is an alkali in solution, so there is no excess solid that could be filtered off [1].

**(b)** The same volumes of acid and alkali are mixed but **no indicator** is added [1], so the salt is not contaminated by indicator [1].

**[3]**

**13.** Parallax error: the scale reading does not match the true level of the meniscus [1]. Read with the eye level with the bottom of the meniscus [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "until neutral" or "until it changes colour" instead of naming the colours.
- Stating that methyl orange is orange in alkali.
- Averaging the rough titre with the accurate ones.
- Forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³ in concentration calculations.
- Saying that water in the flask makes the titre too low.
- Giving burette readings to one decimal place.
