## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Specialised cell | A cell with a structure suited to one particular function |
| Stem cell | An unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to produce cells that can become specialised |
| Tissue | Group of similar cells working together for a shared function |
| Organ | Several tissues working together for a specific function |
| Organ system | Several organs working together for a body function |
| Organism | A complete living thing |
| Magnification | Image size ÷ actual size; no unit |
| Scale bar | A line on an image labelled with the actual length it represents |

## Specialised cells: feature → function

| Cell | Feature(s) | Function it serves |
|---|---|---|
| Root hair cell | Long, thin extension; mitochondria | Large surface area for water and ion uptake; energy for active transport of ions |
| Xylem vessel | Lignified thick walls; no contents; no cross walls, cells end to end | Support; continuous empty tube for water and ions |
| Palisade mesophyll cell | Many chloroplasts, near the upper leaf surface | Maximum light absorption for photosynthesis |
| Guard cell | Pair around a stoma; changes shape | Opens and closes the stoma |
| Red blood cell | Haemoglobin | Oxygen transport |
| Phagocyte | Changes shape | Engulfs pathogens |
| Lymphocyte | Features not required | Produces antibodies |
| Ciliated cell | Cilia that beat | Moves mucus and trapped particles away from the lungs |
| Goblet cell | Secretes mucus | Traps particles and pathogens |
| Sperm cell | Flagellum; mitochondria; acrosome enzymes | Swims; releases energy; digests a way into the egg |
| Egg cell | Energy stores; jelly coat that changes at fertilisation | Energy for the early embryo; stops more sperm entering |

**Answer pattern:** feature, then "which...", then "so...". Example: "Palisade cells contain many chloroplasts, which absorb light, so the rate of photosynthesis is high."

## Levels of organisation

```
cell -> tissue -> organ -> organ system -> organism
```

| Item | Level |
|---|---|
| Guard cell, sperm cell, root hair cell | Cell |
| Xylem, phloem, blood, muscle tissue | Tissue |
| Leaf, root, stem, heart, lung, stomach | Organ |
| Digestive system, circulatory system | Organ system |

A leaf is an organ because it contains several tissues: epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem.

## Magnification toolkit

```
magnification = image size / actual size
actual size   = image size / magnification
image size    = actual size x magnification
1 mm = 1000 µm
```

- **From a scale bar:** magnification = measured length of bar ÷ length written on the bar (same units).
- **Worked reminder:** bar 20 mm labelled 50 µm gives ×400; a cell 48 mm long on the image is 48 ÷ 400 = 0.12 mm = 120 µm.
- **Image size:** a 40 µm guard cell at ×500 is 0.04 × 500 = 20 mm long in the drawing.
- **Sense check:** typical cells are tens of micrometres across.

## Exam traps

- Feature with no function, or function with no feature: half marks.
- Xylem vessels have no cell contents; they are not living.
- Sperm features are limited to flagellum, mitochondria and acrosome enzymes; egg features to energy stores and the changing jelly coat.
- Blood is a tissue; a leaf is an organ; the heart is an organ, not a system.
- Convert units before dividing; µm and mm must not be mixed.
- Magnification has no unit.
- Keep full figures until the final step.

## Self-test

1. Define tissue and give one plant example.
2. Why is a leaf classed as an organ?
3. State two features of a xylem vessel and how each helps.
4. Explain why root hair cells need mitochondria.
5. Give the function of goblet cells and of ciliated cells.
6. Give the three features of a sperm cell named in the syllabus.
7. What happens to the jelly coat of an egg cell at fertilisation, and why does it matter?
8. What is a stem cell?
9. A drawing of a cell is 36 mm long and the cell is actually 12 µm long. Calculate the magnification.
10. A scale bar is 10 mm long and labelled 25 µm. What is the magnification?
11. At ×300, an image of a cell is 24 mm long. Find the actual length in µm.
12. Put in order, smallest first: organ system, cell, organ, organism, tissue.

### Answers

1. A group of similar cells working together for a shared function; for example xylem or palisade mesophyll.
2. It is made of several different tissues working together for a function.
3. Lignified thick walls give support and strength; no cell contents and no cross walls give an empty, continuous tube so water flows freely.
4. To release energy by aerobic respiration for the active transport of mineral ions into the cell.
5. Goblet cells secrete mucus that traps particles and pathogens; ciliated cells beat to move the mucus away from the lungs.
6. Flagellum, mitochondria, enzymes in the acrosome.
7. It changes so that no more sperm can enter, ensuring only one sperm fertilises the egg.
8. An unspecialised cell that divides by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised.
9. 12 µm = 0.012 mm; 36 ÷ 0.012 = ×3000.
10. 25 µm = 0.025 mm; 10 ÷ 0.025 = ×400.
11. 24 ÷ 300 = 0.08 mm = 80 µm.
12. Cell, tissue, organ, organ system, organism.
