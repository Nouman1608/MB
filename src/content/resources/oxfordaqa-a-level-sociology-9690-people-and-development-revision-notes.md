## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Great Divergence | The widening gap in wealth and industry between western Europe (and later North America) and most of the world from the nineteenth century |
| Imperialism | Extension of a state's power over other territories through conquest, trade control or rule |
| Colonialism | Direct political control of a territory and extraction of its resources and labour |
| Neocolonialism | Continued control of formally independent states through debt, trade terms and investment |
| Enslavement | Forced ownership and transport of people as property, central to the Atlantic plantation economy |
| Development | Improvement in life expectancy, living standards, education, health, rights and democracy, not income alone |
| Globalisation | Growing interconnection of societies through flows of goods, money, people, ideas and culture |
| Cultural imperialism | Domination of local cultures by Western, especially American, media and brands |
| Global village | McLuhan's image of electronic media shrinking the world into one community |
| Glocalisation | Adapting global products or ideas to local cultures |
| Transnational corporation (TNC) | A company that produces or operates in several countries |
| NGO | Non-governmental organisation: a charity or campaign group independent of states |
| Grassroots movement | Collective action organised from below by local people |
| Structural adjustment | Loan conditions from the IMF and World Bank requiring cuts in state spending and market reforms |
| Push and pull factors | Conditions driving people from origin and attracting them to destination |
| Refugee | Under the 1951 Convention, a person outside their country with a well-founded fear of persecution on set grounds |
| Undocumented worker | Someone living or working without legal permission |
| Digital nomad | A remote worker who moves between countries while working online |
| Diaspora | A dispersed population keeping ties with a homeland |
| Remittances | Money migrants send to family in their country of origin |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without compromising future generations' ability to meet theirs (Brundtland, 1987) |

## Theories and studies at a glance

| Who | What | Key claim or finding | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rostow (1960) | Modernisation: five stages of growth | Societies develop by adopting Western values and investment until "take-off" | Ethnocentric; assumes one route for all |
| Weber (1905) | Protestant ethic | Calvinist values encouraged saving, work and reinvestment | Capitalism also grew in non-Protestant settings |
| Frank | Dependency | The metropolis grows by draining satellites; satellites grew most when ties were weakest | Cannot explain the rise of the East Asian "tigers" |
| Rodney (1972) | *How Europe Underdeveloped Africa* | Enslavement and colonial rule moved Africa's wealth and labour to Europe | Critics say it underplays African agency and internal factors |
| Williams (1944) | *Capitalism and Slavery* | Slave-trade profits helped finance British industry | Historians debate how large that share was |
| Pomeranz (2000) | *The Great Divergence* | Coal and colonial land, not unique values, explain Britain's lead | Challenges both Weber and pure exploitation accounts |
| Braudel | *Longue durée* | Slow structures of trade and geography shape history | Long time scale can hide human agency |
| Wallerstein (1974) | World-systems | Core, semi-periphery, periphery | Too economic; underplays culture |
| Polanyi (1944) | *The Great Transformation* | Markets "disembed" from society; society pushes back (double movement) | Written before modern globalisation; must be applied with care |
| Ohmae (1990) | Positive globalist | Borderless world; markets spread wealth | States still control borders and trade |
| Bello | Negative globalist | "Deglobalisation": produce for local and national markets | Could reduce access to export earnings |
| Held et al. (1999) | Transformationalist | Globalisation reshapes states and cultures; mixed outcomes | Can seem to avoid a clear judgement |
| Robertson | Glocalisation | Global and local combine | Shows local adaptation, but global brands still profit |
| Sachs (2005) | Aid as a "big push" | Targeted aid breaks the poverty trap | Moyo and Easterly argue aid breeds dependency |
| Lee (1966) | Push-pull | Migration follows push, pull and intervening obstacles | Treats migrants as isolated rational individuals |
| Cohen (1997) | *Global Diasporas* | Types of diaspora, including victim, labour, trade and imperial | Categories overlap in real communities |
| Mies (1986) | Feminist: housewifisation | Women's work is treated as cheap or unpaid | Factory jobs may also give women income |
| Collier (2007) | Conflict trap | War destroys investment and makes more war likely | Economic focus; less on identity and history |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Colonialism vs neocolonialism.** Direct rule versus indirect control after independence.
- **Growth vs development.** Rising GDP versus better lives across health, education, rights.
- **Modernisation vs dependency.** Internal obstacles (values) versus external obstacles (exploitation).
- **Cultural imperialism vs glocalisation.** One-way domination versus two-way blending.
- **Positive vs negative globalist vs transformationalist.** Good for all; harmful for most; real but mixed.
- **Refugee vs economic migrant.** Fleeing persecution versus moving for work. Forced migration is wider than the legal refugee category.
- **Internal vs international migration.** Within one state versus across borders under visa control.
- **TNC vs NGO.** Profit-making firm versus non-profit organisation.
- **Bilateral vs multilateral aid.** State to state versus through agencies such as the World Bank.

## Mini essay plan

**Question type:** "Evaluate the view that dependency on rich countries is the main obstacle to progress for less developed countries."

1. **Introduction.** Define development and dependency. State the debate in one sentence.
2. **For (Marxist).** Frank's metropolis and satellite. Rodney on Africa. Wallerstein's core and periphery. Bello on trade rules. Apply to a country you know: export of raw materials, imported manufactured goods.
3. **Analysis.** Link to neocolonialism: debt and structural adjustment cut health and education spending.
4. **Against (modernisation).** Traditional values and practices slow change. Rostow's stages.
5. **Against (feminist).** Patriarchy as an obstacle that both capitalist and traditional structures reproduce (Mies).
6. **Other obstacles.** Wars and the conflict trap (Collier). Climate crisis hitting poorer countries.
7. **Evaluation.** East Asian growth challenges dependency; inequality within countries challenges modernisation.
8. **Judgement.** Weigh, then give a reasoned conclusion based on your own society example.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two indicators of development listed in the specification, other than income.
2. Who wrote *How Europe Underdeveloped Africa*?
3. What is the "double movement"?
4. Give one example of political globalisation.
5. What does a transformationalist believe about globalisation?
6. Define glocalisation.
7. State one argument for and one against development aid.
8. What three parts make up Lee's model of migration?
9. Are people displaced only by climate change refugees under the 1951 Convention?
10. Name two of Cohen's types of diaspora.
11. What is a "conflict trap"?
12. How did Weber explain the rise of capitalism in northern Europe?

### Answers

1. Any two of: life expectancy, education, health, rights of women and other groups, human rights, democracy, reduced poverty.
2. Walter Rodney (1972).
3. Polanyi's idea that when markets disembed from society, society reacts to protect itself, for example through labour laws.
4. The spread of democracy, human rights or gender equality as global norms, for example through UN bodies.
5. It is real and changes states and cultures, but outcomes are mixed and states still matter.
6. Global products or ideas adapted to fit local cultures.
7. For: Sachs's "big push" breaks poverty traps. Against: Moyo argues aid creates dependency and corruption.
8. Push factors, pull factors and intervening obstacles.
9. No. The Convention covers persecution on set grounds, not environmental displacement.
10. Any two of: victim, labour, trade, imperial.
11. Collier's idea that war destroys investment and institutions, making more war likely.
12. Calvinist belief in predestination led to disciplined work and reinvestment, the "spirit of capitalism".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining development as income only, and missing the specification's list of social indicators.
- Naming a theory without a named sociologist or study to support it.
- Mixing up modernisation (internal values) and dependency (external exploitation) explanations.
- Writing "globalisation" in general when the question asks about one type.
- Leaving out own-society examples in the extended answers that require them.
- Using "refugee" for all migrants.
- Describing push and pull factors without evaluating the model.
- Presenting one side only on aid or migration, which leaves little to evaluate (AO3).
- Ending an essay without a judgement that follows from the evidence.
