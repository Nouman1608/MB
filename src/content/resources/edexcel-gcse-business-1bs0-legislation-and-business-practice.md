These questions cover content item 1.5.3, Legislation and business, in Topic 1.5 of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. All businesses, figures and wage rates are invented, and the mark splits are the writer's own. Calculators may be used; show your workings. Laws are described in general terms, as the specification does not name particular Acts.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a principle of consumer law named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Recruitment

B: Quality

C: Pay

D: Discrimination

**2.** Which **two** of the following are principles of employment law named in the specification? **[2]**

A: Health and safety

B: Consumer rights

C: Pay

D: Exchange rates

E: Market share

**3.** Define the term discrimination in the context of employment. **[2]**

**4.** State **one** consequence for a business of failing to meet its consumer law obligations. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a government makes laws about health and safety at work. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of meeting its employment law obligations. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Bramble Day Nursery employs 12 staff who each work 35 hours a week for 48 weeks a year. All are paid the legal minimum rate, which rises from £11.20 to £11.80 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in the nursery's annual wage bill. **[3]**

**(b)** The nursery's annual profit before the rise was £60,000. Calculate the new profit as a percentage of the old profit, assuming nothing else changes. **[2]**

**8.** Outline **one** way Bramble Day Nursery could respond to the increase in its wage bill. **[2]**

**9.** Quickfit Tyres advertises that it fits tyres "in 30 minutes, guaranteed", but often takes over an hour. Outline **one** way consumer law could affect Quickfit Tyres. **[2]**

**10.** Pine & Plank makes wooden furniture in a small workshop with power saws. Analyse the impact on Pine & Plank of meeting its health and safety obligations. **[6]**

**11.** Ravi owns a shop that sells refurbished laptops for £280 each. He sells 900 a year and 6% are returned faulty for a full refund. He could pay a technician £5,000 a year to test every laptop, which he expects to cut returns to 2%.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of refunds now and with the technician. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate whether employing the technician would save Ravi money. **[1]**

**12.** Hollybank Hotel has 30 staff. A manager has been recruiting only people under 30 for reception jobs "to give the hotel a young image". The owner wants to keep costs low and is reluctant to spend £3,500 on training managers in fair recruitment. Evaluate whether the owner should pay for the training. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Treating an employee or job applicant less favourably than others [1] because of a characteristic such as their sex, race, age, disability or religion [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: paying refunds or compensation; fines; bad publicity and lost sales [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Employees could be injured or made ill by unsafe working conditions [1]. Employers may be tempted to cut safety spending to reduce costs [1], so the law sets a minimum standard that every business must meet, protecting workers whichever business they work for [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A business that treats staff fairly and pays them correctly gains a good reputation as an employer [1]. This makes it easier to attract good applicants [1] and means staff are more likely to stay, reducing the cost of recruiting and training replacements [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Hours = 12 × 35 × 48 = 20,160 [1]. Rise per hour = £11.80 − £11.20 = £0.60 [1]. Increase = 20,160 × £0.60 = **£12,096** [1]

**(b)** New profit = £60,000 − £12,096 = £47,904 [1]. £47,904 ÷ £60,000 × 100 = **79.8%** of the old profit (to one decimal place) [1] **[5]**

**8.** The nursery could raise its fees to parents to cover the extra £12,096 [1]; this protects profit, but some parents may move their children to a cheaper nursery, so the rise must be small enough to keep places filled [1]. **[2]**

**9.** The advert promises something that is not true, so customers may be entitled to a remedy, such as a refund of part of the price, when fitting takes over an hour [1]; to avoid complaints and refunds, Quickfit may have to change the advert or add staff so it can meet the promise [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Pine & Plank must assess the risks of using power saws and provide guards, protective equipment and training [1]. This costs money, which reduces profit in the short run, especially for a small workshop [1]. However, safer working means fewer injuries, so fewer working days are lost and orders are completed on time [1]. It also reduces the chance of fines or compensation if a worker is injured [1], which could be far more expensive than the safety equipment [1]. A safe workshop also helps Pine & Plank recruit skilled carpenters, who may avoid an employer with a poor safety record [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Now: 6% × 900 = 54 returns × £280 = **£15,120** [1]. With technician: 2% × 900 = 18 returns [1] × £280 = **£5,040** [1]

**(b)** Saving on refunds = £15,120 − £5,040 = £10,080, minus £5,000 wage = **£5,080 saved**, so yes [1] **[4]**

**12.** The training costs £3,500, which reduces profit [1], and the owner may feel the reception staff the manager has hired are doing a good job [1].

However, refusing to consider applicants over 30 is likely to be age discrimination in recruitment [1], so the hotel is not meeting its employment law obligations [1]. A rejected applicant could take legal action, and the hotel might have to pay compensation that is far more than £3,500 [1]. If the story became public, the hotel's reputation could suffer, reducing bookings [1]. Recruiting from all age groups also widens the pool of applicants, so the hotel may find more experienced receptionists [1].

Judgement: the owner should pay for the training, because the hotel is currently failing a legal obligation and the possible consequences, such as compensation and lost bookings, are likely to cost much more than £3,500 [1]. The training is especially important because the manager believes the policy is acceptable, so without it the problem is likely to continue in other jobs [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The source shows a clear breach of the recruitment and discrimination principles. Strong answers name the principle, weigh the £3,500 against the consequences, and give a reasoned judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing consumer law and employment law principles in Questions 1 and 2.
- Forgetting to multiply by weeks as well as hours in Question 7.
- Writing only about fines in Question 10 and missing the benefits of compliance.
- Recommending against training in Question 12 without recognising the breach of the law.
