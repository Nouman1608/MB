These notes condense **3.2.2.3 Organisational design** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [organisational design study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-organisational-design-9725/) works through a full structure and two delayering options; the [organisational design practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-organisational-design-9725-practice/) test them. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

Influences on and impact of decisions about: **span of control**, **levels of hierarchy**, **tall versus flat structures**, **delegation**, **centralisation and decentralisation**, **employee representation rates**. Advantages and disadvantages of **restructuring, including delayering**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational structure | The way roles, authority and communication are arranged in a business |
| Span of control | Number of people reporting directly to one manager |
| Levels of hierarchy | Number of layers of authority from top to bottom |
| Chain of command | The line through which instructions pass down and information passes up |
| Tall structure | Many levels, narrow spans |
| Flat structure | Few levels, wide spans |
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to someone lower in the hierarchy |
| Accountability | Being answerable for the outcome; stays with the manager who delegates |
| Centralisation | Key decisions kept at the top or at head office |
| Decentralisation | Decision-making authority passed to branches, regions or lower levels |
| Employee representation rate | Proportion of employees represented by a trade union or other employee body |
| Restructuring | Changing the design of the organisation |
| Delayering | Removing one or more levels of hierarchy |

## Calculations you may meet

```
Total staff             = sum of people at each level
Span at a level         = people at the level below ÷ managers at this level (if spans are equal)
Annual saving from delayering = number of posts removed × salary per post
Representation rate (%) = employees represented ÷ total employees × 100
```

**Quick example.** 1 owner, 4 managers, 48 staff. Levels = 3; owner's span = 4; each manager's span = 48 ÷ 4 = 12. Remove the managers and the owner's span becomes 48.

## Tall vs flat: the trade-off

- **Tall**: close supervision and many promotion steps, but slow communication and high management costs.
- **Flat**: fast communication, lower costs, more responsibility, but overloaded managers and fewer promotion steps.

## Influences on structure

Size · nature of the work (routine or complex) · skills of staff · skills of managers · management style and culture · geography (number of sites) · cost of managers · technology.

## Impacts to analyse

| Decision | Main impacts |
|---|---|
| Wider spans | Lower cost, more autonomy; risk of weak supervision |
| More levels | Closer control; slower decisions; higher cost |
| More delegation | Motivation and faster decisions; risk of errors without training |
| Centralisation | Consistency, cost control, bulk buying; slow local response |
| Decentralisation | Local responsiveness, motivated managers; inconsistency |
| High representation rate | Strong collective voice, one body to consult; change may need longer negotiation |

## Delayering: for and against

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Lower salary costs | Redundancy costs |
| Faster communication | Loss of experience |
| Empowered staff | Overloaded managers |
| Closer to customers | Fewer promotions; anxiety among survivors |

## Centralise or decentralise? Quick test

| Factor | Points to centralisation | Points to decentralisation |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Similar everywhere | Differ by region or country |
| Speed needed | Decisions can wait | Local response must be fast |
| Branch managers | Inexperienced | Skilled and trusted |
| Brand | Must be identical everywhere | Local variation acceptable |
| Costs | Bulk buying matters | Local suppliers are cheaper |

## Delayering in one example

A firm removes 6 middle managers on $50,000 each: saving 6 × 50,000 = $300,000 a year. If each receives a one-off redundancy payment of $20,000, the first-year net saving is 300,000 − 120,000 = $180,000. Then weigh the wider spans and lost experience against the saving.

## Exam traps

- Delegation ≠ decentralisation: one passes tasks; the other moves decision-making power across the organisation.
- Accountability is not delegated.
- A flat structure is not automatically better: it depends on staff skills and the work.
- Include redundancy costs and workload when judging delayering savings.
- Read whether the question asks for **influences on** or **impact of**.

## Self-test

1. Define span of control.
2. A firm has 1 director, 5 managers and 60 staff, with equal spans. How many levels of hierarchy are there, and what is each manager's span?
3. If the managers in Question 2 are removed, what is the director's span?
4. The 5 managers in Question 2 each earn $42,000. Calculate the annual salary saving if they are removed.
5. Give one advantage of a tall structure.
6. Give one disadvantage of a flat structure.
7. Distinguish delegation from decentralisation.
8. Give one influence that allows a wider span of control.
9. Give one advantage of centralised purchasing for a restaurant chain.
10. 84 of 120 employees are union members. Calculate the representation rate.
11. Give one advantage to managers of a high representation rate.
12. State two disadvantages of delayering.

### Answers

1. The number of people who report directly to one manager.
2. **3 levels**; span = 60 ÷ 5 = **12**.
3. **60**.
4. 5 × 42,000 = **$210,000**.
5. Close supervision, or more promotion opportunities (any one).
6. Managers may be overloaded with too many direct reports (any valid point).
7. Delegation passes authority for particular tasks to a subordinate; decentralisation moves decision-making power from the centre to branches or lower levels across the organisation.
8. Skilled, experienced staff; routine work; capable managers; good technology (any one).
9. Bulk buying lowers costs, or consistent quality across restaurants (any one).
10. 84 ÷ 120 × 100 = **70%**.
11. One body to consult and negotiate with, which can make communication and agreement easier.
12. Any two: redundancy costs, lost experience, overloaded managers, fewer promotion opportunities, lower morale.
