Relative atomic masses: Na = 23, C = 12, O = 16, Al = 27, Cl = 35.5, Fe = 56, Zn = 65.

## Questions

**1.** Metals are obtained from the Earth's crust.

**(a)** State what is meant by an ore. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why gold is found as the uncombined element. **[1]**

**2.** Mild steel is an alloy.

**(a)** State what is meant by an alloy. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the non-metal element that is mixed with iron in mild steel. **[1]**

**3.** Carbon lies between aluminium and zinc in the reactivity series. Lead lies between iron and copper.

For each metal, state how it is extracted from its oxide and give a reason.

**(a)** magnesium **[2]**
**(b)** lead **[2]**

**4.** Copper is used for electrical wiring and for water pipes in houses. Explain why copper is suitable for:

**(a)** electrical wiring **[2]**
**(b)** water pipes **[2]**

**5.** Zinc is extracted by heating zinc oxide with carbon.

`ZnO + C → Zn + CO`

**(a)** Identify the substance that is reduced. Explain your answer in terms of oxygen. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the reducing agent. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the maximum mass of zinc that can be obtained from 8.10 tonnes of zinc oxide. **[3]**
**(d)** A works obtains 5.85 tonnes of zinc from 8.10 tonnes of zinc oxide. Calculate the percentage yield. **[2]**

**6.** Choose low-carbon (mild) steel, high-carbon steel or stainless steel for each object. Give a property that explains each choice.

**(a)** a car door panel, which is pressed into a curved shape **[2]**
**(b)** the cutting blade of a wood chisel **[2]**
**(c)** a set of surgical instruments that are washed many times **[2]**

**7.** Aircraft bodies are made from an alloy of aluminium containing small amounts of copper and magnesium.

**(a)** Explain why the alloy is harder than pure aluminium. **[3]**
**(b)** Give **two** properties of aluminium, other than hardness, that make its alloys suitable for aircraft. **[2]**

**8.** Aluminium is extracted by electrolysis of aluminium oxide. The electrodes are made of graphite.

**(a)** Explain why aluminium cannot be extracted by heating aluminium oxide with carbon. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the positive electrodes must be replaced regularly. **[2]**
**(d)** An ore contains 51.0% aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, by mass. Calculate the maximum mass of aluminium that can be extracted from 200 tonnes of this ore. **[3]**
**(e)** Making aluminium from its oxide uses about 15 kWh of electrical energy per kilogram. Recycling aluminium uses about 5% of this energy. Calculate the energy saved if your answer to (d) were made by recycling instead. **[2]**

**9.** Read this information about the extraction of sodium.

> Sodium is extracted by electrolysis of molten sodium chloride. Calcium chloride is added, so the mixture is molten at about 600 °C instead of 801 °C. The anode is carbon and the cathode is iron. The sodium and chlorine formed are directed into separate chambers.
> `2NaCl → 2Na + Cl₂`

**(a)** Use the reactivity series to explain why sodium is extracted by electrolysis. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why calcium chloride is added. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the sodium and chlorine must be kept apart. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass of chlorine formed when 230 kg of sodium is produced. **[3]**

**10.** An engineer is choosing a metal for overhead power cables. The table gives data for four metals, A to D.

| Metal | Density in g/cm³ | Electrical conductivity (copper = 100) | Corrosion in air | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8.9 | 100 | very slow | high |
| B | 2.7 | 61 | none (protective oxide layer) | medium |
| C | 7.9 | 17 | rusts | low |
| D | 10.5 | 106 | none | very high |

Use the data to choose the most suitable metal. Justify your choice by comparing it with the other metals. **[4]**

**11.** In the blast furnace, coke burns to form carbon dioxide. The carbon dioxide reacts with more coke to form carbon monoxide, which reduces iron(III) oxide to iron.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of carbon dioxide with carbon. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why iron can be extracted using carbon but magnesium cannot. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of iron in iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃. **[2]**
**(d)** Iron from the blast furnace contains about 4% carbon. Explain why most of it is made into steel rather than used directly. **[1]**
**(e)** Suggest one environmental problem caused by extracting iron in this way. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A rock containing enough of a metal compound to make extracting the metal worthwhile [1].
**(b)** Gold is very unreactive, so it does not react with substances in the Earth's crust [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "a rock containing metal" without the idea of extraction being worthwhile does not describe an ore and would not score.

**2. (a)** A mixture of a metal with one or more other elements (usually other metals or carbon) [1].
**(b)** **Carbon** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "A compound of two metals" contradicts the definition: an alloy is a mixture, so this answer scores zero.

**3. (a)** Electrolysis of a molten compound [1], because magnesium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove the oxygen [1].
**(b)** Heating the oxide with carbon [1], because lead is less reactive than carbon, so carbon can remove the oxygen [1].
*Examiner insight:* The reason mark needs a comparison with carbon; "magnesium is very reactive" on its own does not score the second mark.

**4. (a)** Copper is a very good conductor of electricity [1]. It is ductile, so it can be drawn into wires [1].
**(b)** Copper does not react with water, so the pipes do not corrode [1]. It can be bent into shape to fit around a building [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a property; in (b), "it is unreactive" without linking it to water or corrosion is usually too vague for credit.

**5. (a)** **Zinc oxide** [1], because it loses oxygen [1].
**(b)** **Carbon** [1].
**(c)** Mr(ZnO) = 65 + 16 = 81 [1]. Mass of Zn = 8.10 × 65/81 [1] = **6.50 tonnes** [1].
**(d)** Percentage yield = 5.85/6.50 × 100 [1] = **90.0%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Zinc is reduced" scores zero in (a): zinc is the product, and it is the zinc **oxide** that loses oxygen. Error carried forward from (c) is allowed in (d).

**6. (a)** Low-carbon (mild) steel [1]: it is malleable, so it can be pressed into shape [1].
**(b)** High-carbon steel [1]: it is very hard, so it keeps a sharp edge [1].
**(c)** Stainless steel [1]: it resists corrosion, so it does not rust when washed [1].
*Examiner insight:* A correct property attached to the wrong steel is not credited.

**7. (a)** Copper and magnesium atoms are a different size from aluminium atoms [1]. They distort the regular layers of aluminium atoms [1], so the layers cannot slide over each other as easily [1].
**(b)** Low density [1]. Resists corrosion, because of a protective oxide layer [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Light" is not accepted in place of "low density", and in (a) "atoms get in the way" without mentioning the layers misses the last two marks.

**8. (a)** Aluminium is more reactive than carbon [1], so carbon cannot remove oxygen from aluminium oxide [1].
**(b)** Aluminium oxide has a very high melting point [1]. Dissolving it in cryolite lets electrolysis take place at a lower temperature, which saves energy [1].
**(c)** Oxygen forms at the positive electrodes [1]. At the high temperature it reacts with the carbon, forming carbon dioxide, so the electrodes burn away [1].
**(d)** Mass of Al₂O₃ = 0.510 × 200 = 102 tonnes [1]. Mr(Al₂O₃) = 2(27) + 3(16) = 102, containing 54 of aluminium [1]. Mass of Al = 102 × 54/102 = **54.0 tonnes** [1].
**(e)** Energy from oxide = 54 000 kg × 15 = 810 000 kWh [1]. Energy saved = 0.95 × 810 000 = **769 500 kWh** (accept 770 000 kWh) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), "the electrodes wear out" scores nothing; you need oxygen reacting with carbon. In (e), forgetting to convert tonnes to kilograms loses the first mark, with the second available by error carried forward.

**9. (a)** Sodium is more reactive than carbon [1], so carbon cannot reduce sodium compounds and electrolysis is needed [1].
**(b)** It lowers the melting point of the electrolyte [1], so less energy is needed to keep it molten, which reduces cost [1].
**(c)** They would react together, forming sodium chloride again [1].
**(d)** Moles of Na = 230 000/23 = 10 000 mol [1]. Moles of Cl₂ = 10 000/2 = 5000 mol [1]. Mass of Cl₂ = 5000 × 71 = 355 000 g = **355 kg** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "to lower the melting point" earns one mark; the second needs the consequence (energy or cost). Using 35.5 instead of 71 for Cl₂ in (d) gives 177.5 kg and loses the last mark.

**10.** **Metal B** [1]. It has the lowest density, so cables are lighter and need fewer or lighter supports than A, C or D [1]. Its conductivity is good, much higher than C [1]. It does not corrode, unlike C, and costs less than A and D [1].
*Examiner insight:* Justification marks need comparisons; quoting "density 2.7" without saying it is the lowest may not score.

**11. (a)** `C + CO₂ → 2CO` [1].
**(b)** Iron is less reactive than carbon, so carbon can remove oxygen from iron oxide [1]. Magnesium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove oxygen from magnesium oxide [1].
**(c)** Mr(Fe₂O₃) = 2(56) + 3(16) = 160 [1]. % Fe = 112/160 × 100 = **70.0%** [1].
**(d)** The carbon makes it brittle, so steel (with less carbon, or other added metals) is more useful [1].
**(e)** Carbon dioxide is released, a greenhouse gas that may contribute to climate change [1]. (Allow: mining damages land.)
*Examiner insight:* In (b), each half needs both metals compared with carbon; "magnesium is too reactive" with no reference to carbon gains only one of the two marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the metal as reduced instead of the metal oxide.
- Giving the method of extraction but no comparison with carbon.
- Saying anodes "wear out" without explaining that oxygen reacts with carbon to form carbon dioxide.
- Missing the consequence (lower temperature, less energy, lower cost) when explaining why something is added.
- Giving a use, or a property, without linking the two.
- Writing "light" instead of "low density", or "strong" when the question asks for another property.
- Leaving out "different-sized atoms" in the alloy explanation.
- Using Ar of chlorine (35.5) instead of Mr of Cl₂ (71).
