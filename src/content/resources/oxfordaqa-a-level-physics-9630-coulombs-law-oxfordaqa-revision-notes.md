These notes condense section 3.8.1, Coulomb's law, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), part of Section 3.8 Electric fields and capacitance (International A-level only). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [Coulomb's law study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-coulombs-law-oxfordaqa/); test yourself with the [Coulomb's law practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-coulombs-law-oxfordaqa-practice/). Field lines and field strength are summarised in the [Coulomb's law and electric field strength revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-coulombs-law-and-electric-field-strength-revision-notes/).

Constants: 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².

## The equation

F = Q₁Q₂ / (4πε₀r²)

| Symbol | Meaning | Unit | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | force on each charge | N | equal and opposite on the two charges |
| Q₁, Q₂ | the two charges | C | nC = 10⁻⁹ C, μC = 10⁻⁶ C, pC = 10⁻¹² C |
| r | separation of point charges, or of sphere centres | m | cm to m before squaring |
| ε₀ | permittivity of free space | F m⁻¹ (= C² N⁻¹ m⁻²) | given on the data sheet |

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Coulomb's law | The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation |
| Point charge | A charge whose size is negligible compared with the distances involved |
| Permittivity of free space, ε₀ | The constant in Coulomb's law for charges in a vacuum |
| Charged sphere | Treated as a point charge at its centre, for points outside it |
| Inverse-square law | Doubling the distance reduces the quantity to a quarter |
| Third-law pair | The forces on the two charges: equal size, opposite direction, acting on different charges |

## Condensed recall

- Like charges repel; unlike charges attract. The force acts along the line joining the charges.
- The equation gives the magnitude. Decide direction from a sketch.
- Air can be treated as a vacuum: its relative permittivity is so close to 1 that the force is changed negligibly.
- With several charges, find each force separately, then add as vectors (same line: add or subtract; right angles: Pythagoras and tan⁻¹).
- Between two like charges there is a point where the forces on a third charge cancel. It is nearer the smaller charge and does not depend on the third charge.
- Between subatomic particles, the electrostatic force is vastly larger than the gravitational force. For two protons the ratio is about 10³⁶, independent of separation.

## Scaling rules

| Change | Force becomes |
|---|---|
| Q₁ × 3 | 3F |
| Q₁ × 2 and Q₂ × 2 | 4F |
| r × 2 | F/4 |
| r × 1/2 | 4F |
| r × 3 and one charge × 3 | F/3 |
| One charge reversed in sign | same size, opposite direction |

To change the force by a factor n at fixed charges, multiply r by 1/√n.

## Method boxes

**Force from several charges**

1. Sketch the charges and mark each distance.
2. Calculate each force's magnitude with the equation.
3. Add an arrow for each force: away from a like charge, towards an unlike charge.
4. Combine as vectors and state the direction of the resultant.

**Balance point between two like charges, Q_A and Q_B, distance d apart**

1. Let x be the distance from Q_A.
2. Write Q_A/x² = Q_B/(d − x)².
3. Square-root: √Q_A/x = √Q_B/(d − x). Solve the linear equation.
4. Check the point is nearer the smaller charge.

**Hanging charged spheres**

1. Forces on one sphere: weight mg, tension T, electric force F (horizontal).
2. F = mg tan θ, where θ is the thread's angle to the vertical.
3. Separation = 2L sin θ for two threads of length L from one point.
4. Use Coulomb's law with that separation to find the charge.

## Worked reminders

- +12 nC and −4.0 nC, 0.060 m apart: F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻⁹ × 4.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.060² = **1.2 × 10⁻⁴ N**, attractive.
- Sphere of radius 2.0 cm with a charge 3.0 cm from its surface: use **r = 5.0 cm**.
- +9.0 nC and +4.0 nC, 0.50 m apart: 3/x = 2/(0.50 − x) gives **x = 0.30 m** from the 9.0 nC charge.

## Exam traps

- Squaring the charges as well as r, or forgetting to square r.
- Using the distance to a sphere's surface.
- Adding magnitudes of forces that act in different directions.
- Converting 5.0 cm to 0.5 m: it is 0.050 m.
- Looking for a balance point between unlike charges. There is none between them; it lies outside, beyond the smaller charge.
- Writing "gravity is weaker" with no calculation when a comparison is asked for.
- Saying the larger charge feels the larger force. Both charges feel the same size of force.

## Self-test

1. State Coulomb's law.
2. Give the unit of ε₀ in terms of C, N and m.
3. The force between two charges is 36 μN. The separation is tripled. Find the new force.
4. Both charges are halved and the separation is halved. What happens to the force?
5. Why may ε₀ be used for charges in air?
6. Calculate the force between +5.0 nC and +5.0 nC at 0.10 m.
7. A sphere of radius 1.5 cm carries +20 nC. A +2.0 nC charge is 4.5 cm from its surface. Find the force.
8. +16 nC and +1.0 nC are 0.25 m apart. Where between them does a third charge feel no force?
9. Two forces of 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ N and 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ N act on a charge at right angles. Find the resultant.
10. Why is the ratio of electrostatic to gravitational force between two protons the same at every separation?
11. Two hanging spheres sit with their threads at 10° to the vertical. Each has weight 4.0 × 10⁻³ N. Find the electric force on each.
12. The force between two charges must be reduced from 8.0 mN to 2.0 mN by moving them apart. They start 0.15 m apart. Find the new separation.

### Answers

1. The force between two point charges is proportional to the product of the charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation.
2. **C² N⁻¹ m⁻²** (equivalent to F m⁻¹).
3. 36/9 = **4.0 μN**.
4. Charges give × 1/4; separation gives × 4; force **unchanged**.
5. Air's relative permittivity is very close to 1, so the force differs from the vacuum value by a negligible amount.
6. 8.99 × 10⁹ × (5.0 × 10⁻⁹)² / 0.10² = **2.2 × 10⁻⁵ N**, repulsive.
7. r = 0.060 m; F = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 20 × 10⁻⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.060² = **1.0 × 10⁻⁴ N**, repulsive.
8. 4/x = 1/(0.25 − x), so **x = 0.20 m** from the 16 nC charge.
9. √(3.0² + 4.0²) × 10⁻⁵ = **5.0 × 10⁻⁵ N**, at 53° to the 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ N force.
10. Both forces are proportional to 1/r², so r cancels in the ratio.
11. F = mg tan θ = 4.0 × 10⁻³ × tan 10° = **7.1 × 10⁻⁴ N**.
12. r = 0.15 × √(8.0/2.0) = **0.30 m**.
