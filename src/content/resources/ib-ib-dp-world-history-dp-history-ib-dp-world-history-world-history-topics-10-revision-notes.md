These notes condense world history topic 10 for Paper 2: how authoritarian states emerged, how leaders consolidated and kept power, and what their policies achieved. The comparative arguments are set out in the [authoritarian states study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-10/); test yourself afterwards with the [authoritarian states practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-10-practice/).

## The three parts of the topic

| Part | Content headings in the guide |
|---|---|
| Emergence | Conditions: economic factors, social division, impact of war, weakness of the political system. Methods: persuasion and coercion, role of leaders, ideology, use of force, propaganda |
| Consolidation and maintenance | Legal methods; use of force; charismatic leadership; propaganda; nature, extent and treatment of opposition; impact of foreign policy success or failure |
| Aims and results of policies | Economic, political, cultural and social policies; impact on women and minorities; extent to which control was achieved |

## Case timelines

### Stalin's USSR (Europe)

- 1922: Stalin becomes General Secretary.
- January 1924: Lenin dies.
- 1924-29: Stalin defeats Trotsky (exiled from the USSR 1929), then Zinoviev and Kamenev, then Bukharin and the "Right".
- 1928: first Five-Year Plan begins; collectivization from 1929.
- 1932-33: famine, especially in Ukraine and Kazakhstan.
- December 1934: Kirov murdered; used to justify purges.
- 1936-38: show trials and the Great Terror.
- 1936: new constitution; abortion banned.

### Hitler's Germany (Europe)

- 30 January 1933: appointed chancellor.
- 28 February 1933: Reichstag fire decree suspends civil liberties.
- 23 March 1933: Enabling Act.
- May 1933: trade unions abolished; German Labour Front created.
- July 1933: Nazis the only legal party; Concordat with the Vatican.
- 30 June 1934: Night of the Long Knives.
- August 1934: Hindenburg dies; Hitler becomes Führer; army oath.
- 1935: Nuremberg Laws.
- 1936: Four-Year Plan under Göring.
- November 1938: *Kristallnacht*.

### Mao's China (Asia and Oceania)

- 1 October 1949: People's Republic of China proclaimed.
- 1950: Agrarian Reform Law; Marriage Law.
- 1951-52: Three-Anti and Five-Anti campaigns.
- 1953-57: first Five-Year Plan.
- 1956-57: Hundred Flowers campaign; then the Anti-Rightist campaign (1957).
- 1958-62: Great Leap Forward; famine.
- 1959: Lushan Conference; Peng Dehuai dismissed.
- 1966: Cultural Revolution begins; Red Guards.
- 1976: Mao dies.

### Castro's Cuba (the Americas)

- 26 July 1953: attack on the Moncada barracks.
- December 1956: *Granma* landing; guerrilla war in the Sierra Maestra.
- 1 January 1959: Batista flees.
- May 1959: Agrarian Reform Law.
- 1960: Committees for the Defence of the Revolution; Federation of Cuban Women; nationalization of US-owned businesses.
- April 1961: Bay of Pigs invasion defeated; revolution declared socialist.
- 1961: literacy campaign.
- October 1962: missile crisis.
- 1965: Communist Party of Cuba formed.

## Comparison grid

| Theme | Stalin | Hitler | Mao | Castro |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ideology | Marxism-Leninism; "socialism in one country" | Racial nationalism; *Volksgemeinschaft* | Marxism-Leninism adapted to a peasant society | Nationalism, anti-imperialism, then Marxism-Leninism |
| Main coercive tools | NKVD, Gulag | SS, Gestapo, concentration camps | Mass campaigns, *laogai* labour camps | Trials, imprisonment, CDR surveillance |
| Propaganda | Personality cult, socialist realism | Goebbels' ministry, rallies, radio | Mass campaigns, Little Red Book | Speeches, mass rallies, revolutionary heroes |
| Economic policy | Five-Year Plans, collectivization | Public works, rearmament, autarky | Five-Year Plan, Great Leap Forward | Land reform, nationalization, sugar and Soviet aid |
| Women | Equal rights in law; "double burden" of work and home | Motherhood ideal; employment rose with rearmament | Marriage Law 1950; labour mobilization | Education and health gains; Family Code (1975) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Authoritarian state | A state where power is concentrated in a leader or party not accountable to free elections |
| Single-party state | A state in which only one party is legally allowed |
| Totalitarianism | A contested concept describing regimes that seek total control of public and private life |
| Cult of personality | Propaganda that glorifies a leader as infallible |
| *Gleichschaltung* | "Co-ordination": bringing all German institutions under Nazi control |
| Collectivization | Merging private farms into state or collective farms |
| Purge | Removal of opponents or suspected opponents from party or state |
| Autarky | Economic self-sufficiency |
| Socialist realism | Soviet art and literature required to glorify socialism and its heroes |
| Mass mobilization | Drawing the population into campaigns organized by the regime |

## Exam traps

- **Conditions versus methods.** "Why did X emerge?" and "How did X gain power?" need different answers.
- **"Control" as a given.** The guide asks how far control was achieved; include resistance, non-conformity and inefficiency.
- **Hitler and Stalin as a "different region" pair.** Both are in Europe.
- **Policy without results.** Every policy paragraph needs an effect and a judgement.
- **Outside the date limits.** Topic 10 is twentieth century; check that a regime falls within it.

## Self-test

1. Name the four conditions for emergence in the guide. *Economic factors, social division, impact of war, weakness of the political system.*
2. What post gave Stalin control of party appointments? *General Secretary (from 1922).*
3. What did the Enabling Act allow? *The government to make laws without the Reichstag for four years.*
4. Why was the Night of the Long Knives important? *It removed the SA leadership and won the army's support for Hitler.*
5. What was the Hundred Flowers campaign, and what followed? *An invitation to criticize the party (1956-57), followed by the Anti-Rightist campaign against critics.*
6. Give two results of the Great Leap Forward. *Any two: famine with tens of millions of deaths, falsified statistics, Mao's temporary retreat from day-to-day policy, economic disruption.*
7. When did Batista flee Cuba? *1 January 1959.*
8. What were the CDRs? *Committees for the Defence of the Revolution: neighbourhood organizations for surveillance and mobilization in Cuba.*
9. Give one way foreign policy strengthened an authoritarian leader. *Hitler's remilitarization of the Rhineland (1936) or Castro's defeat of the Bay of Pigs invasion (1961).*
10. What were the Nuremberg Laws? *1935 laws that stripped Jews of German citizenship and banned marriage between Jews and non-Jewish Germans.*
11. What did Mao's Marriage Law of 1950 do? *Banned arranged marriages and concubinage and allowed divorce.*
12. Why is "totalitarianism" a debated term? *Historians disagree on how far regimes achieved total control, given the role of ordinary people's co-operation and the limits of state power.*
