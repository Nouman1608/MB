## Questions

**1.** Name the organic product when each compound reacts with NaBH₄.

**(a)** hexanal **[1]**
**(b)** cyclopentanone **[1]**
**(c)** State the type of reaction in which NaBH₄ adds to the C=O group. **[1]**

**2.** 2-Phenylethanal, C₆H₅CH₂CHO, is converted into a carboxylic acid.

**(a)** Give the reagents. **[1]**
**(b)** State the conditions. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe the colour change. **[1]**
**(d)** Write an equation for the reaction, using [O]. **[1]**

**3.** Cyclopentanone reacts with NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq).

**(a)** Show the structure of the hydroxynitrile formed. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest why the reagent is NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) rather than hydrogen cyanide itself. **[1]**
**(c)** State why this reaction is useful in organic synthesis. **[1]**
**(d)** Name the type of reaction. **[1]**

**4.** Describe the mechanism for the reaction of propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO, with NaBH₄ in water. Show the dipole, all curly arrows, the intermediate and the products. **[4]**

**5.** 3-Methylbutanal, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CHO, reacts with HCN.

**(a)** Describe the mechanism, using H⁺ for the protonation step. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**6.** Three unlabelled bottles contain heptanal, heptan-2-one and heptan-2-ol. Describe two chemical tests that identify all three. Give the observations and explain the result for the aldehyde. **[6]**

**7.** Tollens' reagent is used to tell aldehydes from ketones.

**(a)** State what Tollens' reagent is. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the test is carried out safely and the positive result. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the different results for an aldehyde and a ketone in terms of oxidation and reduction. Include a half-equation for silver. **[3]**

**8.** 0.648 g of an aldehyde, CₙH₂ₙO, reacts with excess Tollens' reagent. 1.94 g of silver is deposited.

**(a)** Calculate the Mᵣ of the aldehyde. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the structures of the two aldehydes that fit. **[2]**

**9.** An unknown ketone (molecular formula C₆H₁₂O) is mixed with 2,4-DNP solution, and an orange solid separates out.

**(a)** Describe how the precipitate is turned into a sample whose melting point can be used. **[3]**
**(b)** The prepared sample melts sharply at 129–130 °C. Use these values for the derivatives to identify the ketone: hexan-2-one 107 °C; hexan-3-one 130 °C; 4-methylpentan-2-one 95 °C; 3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one 125 °C. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why measuring the melting point of the unpurified precipitate could have led to the wrong answer. **[1]**

**10.** 3.42 g of heptan-2-one, CH₃CO(CH₂)₄CH₃, is reduced with NaBH₄. 2.61 g of heptan-2-ol is collected. Write an equation using [H] and calculate the percentage yield. **[4]**

**11.** Compound R contains 72.0% carbon, 12.0% hydrogen and 16.0% oxygen by mass. Its Mᵣ is 100.

**(a)** Show that the molecular formula of R is C₆H₁₂O. **[3]**
**(b)** R reacts with NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) to give 2-hydroxy-3-methylhexanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH(OH)CN. Give the structure and name of R. **[2]**
**(c)** State what is seen when R is warmed with Tollens' reagent, and write an equation for the organic change using [O]. **[2]**
**(d)** Name the product of reducing R with NaBH₄ and write the equation using [H]. **[2]**
**(e)** Predict the observation when R is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Hexan-1-ol** [1]
**(b)** **Cyclopentanol** [1]
**(c)** **Nucleophilic addition** (also a reduction) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (c), "reduction" describes the overall change but "nucleophilic addition" names the reaction type the question asks for; give the mechanism type.

**2. (a)** K₂Cr₂O₇ (potassium dichromate) acidified with dilute H₂SO₄ [1]
**(b)** Heat under reflux (with excess oxidising agent) [1]
**(c)** Orange to green [1]
**(d)** **C₆H₅CH₂CHO + [O] → C₆H₅CH₂COOH** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give "heat under reflux" as the condition; "distil" is how a primary alcohol is stopped at the aldehyde, which is the opposite of what is wanted here.

**3. (a)** The ring carbon that was C=O now carries OH and CN: **C₅H₈(OH)CN**, a cyclopentane ring with –OH and –C≡N on the same carbon (1-hydroxycyclopentane-1-carbonitrile) [1]
**(b)** HCN is a very toxic, volatile compound; NaCN(aq) with acid supplies CN⁻ and H⁺ in solution instead [1]
**(c)** It forms a new C–C bond, increasing the carbon chain length by one [1]
**(d)** **Nucleophilic addition** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), put both groups on the former carbonyl carbon; placing OH and CN on different carbons shows a different compound.

**4.** Dipole Cδ+=Oδ− shown; H⁻ lone pair attacks the carbonyl carbon (curly arrow) [1]. π-bond pair of C=O moves onto O (arrow starts on the double bond) [1]. Alkoxide intermediate **CH₃CH₂CH₂O⁻**, charge on O [1]. O⁻ lone pair takes H from H₂O, the O–H pair returning to the water's O, giving **propan-1-ol and OH⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Curly arrows begin on electrons, so draw the lone pair on H⁻ and start there, and make the arrow end clearly on the carbonyl carbon, not on the oxygen.

**5. (a)** CN⁻ attacks the δ+ carbonyl carbon, the arrow beginning at the lone pair on its **carbon** atom [1]. The C=O π pair moves onto O [1]. Intermediate (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH(CN)O⁻ [1]. O⁻ lone pair bonds to H⁺, giving (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH(OH)CN [1]
**(b)** **2-Hydroxy-4-methylpentanenitrile** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An arrow from the nitrogen of CN⁻ implies a C–N–C link; check that the arrow starts where the new bond forms.

**6.** Add 2,4-DNP to each [1]. Orange precipitate with heptanal and heptan-2-one; no precipitate with **heptan-2-ol**, so that bottle is identified [1]. Warm the other two with Tollens' reagent in a water bath [1]. Silver mirror with **heptanal** [1]; no change with **heptan-2-one** [1]. Heptanal is oxidised to heptanoic acid, and Ag⁺ is reduced to Ag [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the observation for every compound in each test, including "no change"; a test described for only one bottle does not show that the other two are distinguished.

**7. (a)** Ammoniacal silver nitrate (silver nitrate solution with aqueous ammonia) [1]
**(b)** Heat gently using a water bath; a naked flame could ignite the organic compound [1]; a silver mirror (or grey-black precipitate) forms with an aldehyde [1]
**(c)** The aldehyde is oxidised to a carboxylic acid, RCHO + [O] → RCOOH [1]. The silver ions are reduced: **Ag⁺ + e⁻ → Ag** [1]. A ketone is not oxidised by Tollens' reagent, so Ag⁺ is not reduced and there is no change [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c), say which species is oxidised and which is reduced; "the aldehyde reacts with silver" gives neither process.

**8. (a)** n(Ag) = 1.94 ÷ 107.9 = 0.0180 mol [1]; one aldehyde reduces two Ag⁺, so n(aldehyde) = 0.00899 mol [1]; Mᵣ = 0.648 ÷ 0.00899 = **72** [1]
**(b)** 14n + 16 = 72 gives n = 4, so C₄H₈O: **CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO** (butanal) [1] and **(CH₃)₂CHCHO** (2-methylpropanal) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using a 1 : 1 ratio of aldehyde to silver halves the Mᵣ to 36; write the half-equation first so the ratio is clear.

**9. (a)** Filter off the precipitate [1]; recrystallise it from the minimum volume of hot solvent, cool, filter and wash with cold solvent [1]; dry it before measuring the melting point [1]
**(b)** **Hexan-3-one** [1]
**(c)** A crude solid melts below its true value and over a range, so it could seem to match a lower entry such as 125 °C (3,3-dimethylbutan-2-one) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "purify it" alone is too vague; name recrystallisation and the key steps.

**10.** CH₃CO(CH₂)₄CH₃ + 2[H] → CH₃CH(OH)(CH₂)₄CH₃ [1]; n(heptan-2-one) = 3.42 ÷ 114.0 = 0.0300 mol [1]; theoretical mass of heptan-2-ol = 0.0300 × 116.0 = 3.48 g [1]; yield = 2.61 ÷ 3.48 × 100 = **75.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The product Mᵣ (116.0) differs from the reactant's (114.0); dividing 2.61 by 3.42 gives 76.3% and compares two different substances.

**11. (a)** C 72.0 ÷ 12.0 = 6.00; H 12.0 ÷ 1.0 = 12.0; O 16.0 ÷ 16.0 = 1.00 [1]; ratio 6 : 12 : 1, empirical formula C₆H₁₂O [1]; empirical mass 6(12.0) + 12(1.0) + 16.0 = 100, equal to Mᵣ, so R is **C₆H₁₂O** [1]
**(b)** Remove the CN carbon and turn CH(OH) back into C=O: **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO** [1], **2-methylpentanal** [1]
**(c)** A silver mirror [1]; **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)COOH** [1]
**(d)** **2-Methylpentan-1-ol** [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CHO + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂OH [1]
**(e)** Orange to green [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a) a "show that" needs the comparison of empirical mass with Mᵣ written out; quoting C₆H₁₂O without it leaves the last step unsupported.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the cyanide arrow at nitrogen instead of at the carbon lone pair.
- Leaving out the arrow from the C=O bond to oxygen, or the protonation step.
- Writing H₂ instead of 2[H], or O₂ instead of [O], in organic redox equations.
- Using a 1 : 1 aldehyde : silver ratio in silver-mirror calculations.
- Giving a 2,4-DNP result as a colour change rather than a precipitate.
- Describing a test without the "no change" result for the compounds that do not react.
- Heating Tollens' reagent with a flame.
- Naming hydroxynitriles with the OH carbon as C1.
- Dividing product mass by reactant mass in yield questions.
