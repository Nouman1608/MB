## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Species (biological) | Organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring |
| Species (morphological) | Organisms that share the same structural (observable) features |
| Species (ecological) | Organisms that share the same ecological niche |
| Domain | Highest rank: Archaea, Bacteria or Eukarya |
| Prokaryote | Cell with no nucleus or membrane-bound organelles (Archaea, Bacteria) |
| Taxon | Any named group at any rank, e.g. Felidae |
| Binomial name | *Genus species*, italicised, genus capitalised |
| Peptidoglycan | Polymer in bacterial cell walls (also called murein) |
| Chitin | Polysaccharide in fungal cell walls |
| Hyphae / mycelium | Fungal threads / the network they form |

## 18.1.1 Species concepts: test and weakness

| Concept | Test | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Biological | Fertile offspring | Useless for asexual organisms, fossils, populations that never meet |
| Morphological | Same structure | Sexual dimorphism, larvae, look-alike species |
| Ecological | Same niche | Niches hard to define; can change with place or life stage |

*Reminder:* horse × donkey → mule, which is **infertile**, so horses and donkeys are separate species.

## 18.1.2–18.1.3 Domains

- Three domains: **Archaea, Bacteria, Eukarya**.
- Based on **rRNA base sequences** (present in all cells, slow to change).
- Archaea and Bacteria are both **prokaryotes**.

| Difference | Archaea | Bacteria |
|---|---|---|
| Membrane lipids | Branched chains, **ether** bonds | Unbranched fatty acids, **ester** bonds |
| rRNA | Different base sequences | Different base sequences |
| Cell wall | No peptidoglycan | **Peptidoglycan** |

**Similarities (not differences):** no nucleus, 70S ribosomes, circular DNA.

## 18.1.4 Taxonomic hierarchy

**Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species**

- Lower rank = fewer, more closely related organisms.
- Same genus → also same family, order, class, phylum, kingdom.
- Binomial: *Panthera leo* (lion); *Panthera tigris* (tiger) = same genus.

## 18.1.5 The four eukaryotic kingdoms

| Feature | Protoctista | Fungi | Plantae | Animalia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cells | Mostly unicellular | Hyphae (yeasts unicellular) | Multicellular | Multicellular |
| Cell wall | Some do, some don't | **Chitin** | **Cellulose** | **None** |
| Nutrition | Photosynthetic or heterotrophic | Heterotrophic, extracellular digestion | Photosynthetic | Heterotrophic, ingestion |
| Carbohydrate store | Varies | **Glycogen** | **Starch** | **Glycogen** |
| Other | "Leftover" eukaryotes, e.g. algae, *Amoeba*, *Plasmodium* | Spores | Large vacuole, chloroplasts | Nervous coordination |

**Method in steps: placing an unknown eukaryote**

1. Cell wall? None → Animalia (or a protoctist).
2. Wall of chitin → Fungi; wall of cellulose plus chloroplasts in a multicellular organism → Plantae.
3. Mostly single cells, or simple with no tissues → Protoctista.
4. Back up with the carbohydrate store (glycogen or starch).

## 18.1.6 Viruses

- Not cells → **no domain**.
- Classified by **DNA or RNA** and **single- or double-stranded**.

| Group | Examples |
|---|---|
| dsDNA | Herpes viruses, smallpox virus |
| ssDNA | Parvoviruses |
| dsRNA | Rotaviruses |
| ssRNA | HIV, influenza, measles, tobacco mosaic virus |

**Base data rule:** double-stranded → A = T (or U) and C = G. Unequal pairs → single-stranded. Uracil → RNA; thymine → DNA.

## Exam traps

- Leaving out "fertile" from the biological definition.
- "Archaea have no nucleus but bacteria do." Both lack a nucleus.
- Swapping ether (archaea) and ester (bacteria).
- Writing the species name with a capital letter.
- Fungal walls "cellulose"; fungi "photosynthesise".
- Calling viruses prokaryotes.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the test used by the biological species concept.
2. Give one organism group for which the biological species concept cannot be used, and explain why.
3. Why can the morphological species concept wrongly split one species into two?
4. Name the molecule whose base sequences were used to propose the three domains.
5. State the difference in membrane lipids between Archaea and Bacteria.
6. Which domain has cell walls containing peptidoglycan?
7. Put in order, highest first: genus, class, family, phylum, order.
8. Two organisms are in the same family. Must they be in the same genus?
9. Give three features of fungi.
10. A nucleic acid has A 22%, T 22%, G 28%, C 28%. Classify it.
11. A nucleic acid contains uracil, with A 31%, U 18%, G 27%, C 24%. Classify it.
12. Why are viruses not placed in any domain?

### Answers

1. They can interbreed to produce **fertile** offspring.
2. Bacteria (or other asexual organisms) do not interbreed; or fossils, because breeding cannot be tested.
3. Males and females, or larvae and adults, can look very different.
4. **Ribosomal RNA** (rRNA).
5. Archaea: branched chains joined by **ether** bonds; Bacteria: fatty acids joined by **ester** bonds.
6. **Bacteria**.
7. **Phylum, class, order, family, genus.**
8. **No**: one family can contain several genera.
9. Any three: chitin cell walls; hyphae and mycelium; heterotrophic by extracellular digestion; store glycogen; reproduce by spores; no chlorophyll.
10. **Double-stranded DNA** (thymine present; A = T, G = C).
11. **Single-stranded RNA** (uracil present; A ≠ U, G ≠ C).
12. They are not cells: no cytoplasm, ribosomes or metabolism of their own.

## Where marks are usually lost

- One-sided "discuss" answers that define the concepts but give no limitations.
- Vague kingdom features such as "fungi are like plants".
- Naming only ranks, not their order, when asked to describe the hierarchy.
- Classifying a virus by shape or host rather than by nucleic acid.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which three species concepts are on the 9700 syllabus?**
Only the biological, morphological and ecological species concepts. Other concepts are not required.

**Do I need to know any other differences between Archaea and Bacteria?**
No. The syllabus limits the differences to membrane lipids, ribosomal RNA and cell wall composition.
