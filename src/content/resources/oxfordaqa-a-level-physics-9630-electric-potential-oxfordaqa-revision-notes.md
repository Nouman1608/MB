These notes condense section 3.8.3, Electric potential, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), in Section 3.8 Electric fields and capacitance (International A-level only). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [electric potential study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-potential-oxfordaqa/); test yourself with the [electric potential practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-potential-oxfordaqa-practice/). Capacitors, the next subtopic, are in the [capacitors revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-capacitors-revision-notes/).

Constants: 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

## Equations

| Equation | Meaning | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ΔW = QΔV | Work done moving charge Q through p.d. ΔV | Route does not matter |
| V = Q/(4πε₀r) | Potential at distance r from a point charge | Keep the sign of Q |
| E = ΔV/Δr | Field strength from potential gradient | E points towards falling V |
| ΔV = area under E–r graph | P.d. between two radii | Units of one square: V m⁻¹ × m |
| Ek = QV | Energy gained from rest through p.d. V | Gives v = √(2QV/m) |

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Absolute electric potential | Work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to the point |
| Zero of potential | At infinity |
| Potential difference | Work done per unit charge moving a positive charge between two points |
| Equipotential surface | Surface joining points of equal potential; no work is done moving a charge along it |
| Potential gradient | Change of potential per unit distance, ΔV/Δr |
| Electric potential energy | Energy of a charge Q at a point of potential V, equal to QV |

## Condensed recall

- V is a scalar: add potentials from several charges as signed numbers.
- Near a positive charge V > 0 and falls with r; near a negative charge V < 0 and rises towards zero.
- Positive charges move towards lower potential; negative charges towards higher potential.
- Field lines cross equipotentials at right angles.
- Equipotentials for equal steps of V: equally spaced planes in a uniform field; spreading circles (spheres) round a point charge, since r ∝ 1/V.
- V ∝ 1/r; E ∝ 1/r². The E–r curve falls more steeply.
- E at a point = magnitude of the tangent gradient on the V–r graph.
- Closest approach: initial Ek = charge of particle × potential due to the target nucleus.

## Graph shapes for a positive point charge

| Graph | Shape | What it tells you |
|---|---|---|
| V against r | 1/r curve, positive, falling to zero | Tangent gradient gives E |
| E against r | 1/r² curve, falling faster | Area between radii gives ΔV |
| V × r against r | Horizontal line | Value equals Q/(4πε₀), a test of V ∝ 1/r |

For a negative charge, V is negative and rises towards zero.

## Worked reminders

- −30 nC at 0.25 m: V = −8.99 × 10⁹ × 30 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.25 = **−1.1 × 10³ V**.
- Electron through 450 V from rest: Ek = 7.2 × 10⁻¹⁷ J, v = **1.3 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹**.
- +12 nC: Q/(4πε₀) = 107.9 V m, so the 270 V equipotential is at r = 107.9/270 = **0.40 m**.

## Exam traps

- Using r² in the potential equation.
- Treating potential as a vector and resolving it.
- Losing the sign of a negative charge or a negative potential.
- Confusing potential (V, J C⁻¹) with potential energy (J).
- Assuming E = 0 where V = 0. Between unlike charges V can be zero while E is large.
- Using a chord instead of a tangent for E at a point.
- Drawing equipotentials round a point charge equally spaced.

## Self-test

1. Define absolute electric potential.
2. Why does no work get done moving a charge along an equipotential?
3. Find V at 0.20 m from a +8.0 nC charge.
4. Find the work done moving +3.0 μC through a p.d. of 1.5 kV.
5. Find the speed of an electron accelerated from rest through 100 V.
6. A point is 0.10 m from +5.0 nC and 0.10 m from −5.0 nC. Find V there.
7. Is E zero at the point in question 6? Explain.
8. The 900 V equipotential round a point charge is at 0.10 m. Where is the 300 V equipotential?
9. What does the gradient of a V–r graph give?
10. What does the area under an E–r graph give?
11. An alpha particle has 4.0 MeV of kinetic energy. Through what p.d. must it climb to stop?
12. A table gives V × r as the same value at each distance. What does that show?

### Answers

1. The work done per unit positive charge in bringing a small test charge from infinity to the point.
2. ΔV = 0 along the surface, so ΔW = QΔV = 0.
3. 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.20 = **360 V**.
4. 3.0 × 10⁻⁶ × 1500 = **4.5 × 10⁻³ J**.
5. v = √(2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 100 / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **5.9 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹**.
6. The two potentials are equal and opposite: **V = 0**.
7. No. Both fields point towards the negative charge there and add, so E is not zero.
8. V ∝ 1/r, so one third of the potential is at three times the distance: **0.30 m**.
9. The magnitude of the field strength at that distance.
10. The potential difference between the two radii.
11. Charge 2e, so V = Ek/Q = 4.0 × 10⁶ eV / 2e = **2.0 × 10⁶ V**.
12. V ∝ 1/r, as for a point charge; the constant equals Q/(4πε₀).
