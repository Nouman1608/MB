These questions cover section 2.5, Simple molecules and covalent bonds, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Where a question asks for a dot-and-cross diagram, the answer describes what a correct diagram shows. Revise first with the [simple molecules and covalent bonds study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-simple-molecules-and-covalent-bonds/) and the [simple molecules and covalent bonds revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-simple-molecules-and-covalent-bonds-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a *covalent bond*. **[2]**

**2.** State the number of shared pairs of electrons between the two atoms in each molecule.

**(a)** Cl₂ **[1]**

**(b)** O₂ **[1]**

**(c)** N₂ **[1]**

**3.** State the number of non-bonding pairs of electrons on the central atom in:

**(a)** a water molecule **[1]**

**(b)** an ammonia molecule **[1]**

**4.** Hydrogen chloride is a gas at room temperature. Give **one** other physical property you would expect it to have as a pure substance, based on its structure. **[1]**

**5.** A student writes: "Methane has a low boiling point because the bonds between carbon and hydrogen are weak." Explain the error. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of ammonia, NH₃. Show outer-shell electrons only. **[3]**

**7.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of nitrogen, N₂. **[2]**

**8.** Draw a dot-and-cross diagram for a molecule of methanol, CH₃OH. Use a key to show the electrons from each kind of atom. **[3]**

**9.** Ethene, C₂H₄, contains a carbon–carbon double bond.

**(a)** State what is meant by a double bond. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that each carbon atom in ethene has a noble gas electronic configuration. **[2]**

**10.** Hydrogen sulfide, H₂S, is a simple molecular compound. Sulfur is in Group VI.

**(a)** Predict the number of covalent bonds and non-bonding pairs on the sulfur atom. **[2]**

**(b)** Hydrogen sulfide boils at about −60 °C. Explain why its boiling point is low. **[2]**

**11.** Tetrachloromethane, CCl₄, is a colourless liquid.

**(a)** Describe the dot-and-cross diagram of a CCl₄ molecule. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why liquid tetrachloromethane does not conduct electricity. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the structure and bonding of carbon dioxide with that of oxygen, O₂. Refer to the number and type of bonds and the non-bonding pairs in each molecule. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why water molecules remain as H₂O molecules when water boils, but hydrogen and oxygen are produced when water is electrolysed. **[2]**

**14.** The table gives information about three substances.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C | Electrical conductivity of the liquid |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | −98 | 65 | does not conduct |
| Q | 801 | 1413 | conducts |
| R | −169 | −104 | does not conduct |

**(a)** Identify the **two** substances that are simple molecular. Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table. **[3]**

**(b)** State the physical state of R at room temperature. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A **pair of electrons shared** between two atoms [1], leading to **noble gas electronic configurations** for both [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** 1 [1]

**(b)** 2 [1]

**(c)** 3 [1]

**[3]**

**3. (a)** 2 [1]

**(b)** 1 [1]

**[2]**

**4.** Does not conduct electricity / low melting point (any one) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The C–H **covalent bonds are strong** and are **not broken** when methane boils [1]; the boiling point is low because the **intermolecular forces between methane molecules are weak** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** **Three** shared pairs between N and each H, each pair one dot and one cross [1]; **one non-bonding pair** on nitrogen [1]; nitrogen has **8** outer electrons and each hydrogen **2** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** **Three shared pairs** (a triple bond) in the overlap, with each pair one dot and one cross [1]; **one non-bonding pair** on each nitrogen atom, giving each 8 outer electrons [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Carbon forms **four single bonds**: three to H and one to O [1]; oxygen forms **two single bonds** (C–O and O–H) and has **two non-bonding pairs** [1]; each shared pair shown as one electron from each atom, with a key, and C and O have 8 outer electrons, each H has 2 [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** **Two shared pairs** of electrons between the same two atoms [1]

**(b)** Each carbon shares **2 pairs** with the other carbon (4 electrons) [1] and **1 pair** with each of two hydrogens (4 electrons), so it has **8** outer electrons [1]

**[3]**

**10. (a)** **2** covalent bonds [1]; **2** non-bonding pairs [1]

**(b)** Hydrogen sulfide is made of small, separate molecules with **weak intermolecular forces** between them [1]; **little energy** is needed to overcome these forces [1]

**[4]**

**11. (a)** Carbon forms **four single bonds**, one to each chlorine, each a shared pair with one dot and one cross [1]; each chlorine has **three non-bonding pairs**, giving it 8 outer electrons [1]

**(b)** The molecules are **neutral** [1]; there are **no ions or delocalised electrons** free to move and carry charge [1]

**[4]**

**12.** CO₂ has **two double bonds** (C=O), four shared pairs in total [1]; O₂ has **one double bond** (O=O) [1]; in both molecules each oxygen atom has **two non-bonding pairs** [1]; carbon in CO₂ has **no non-bonding pairs** [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Boiling overcomes only the **weak intermolecular forces** between water molecules; the O–H covalent bonds are not broken [1]. Electrolysis is a **chemical change** that **breaks the covalent bonds** within the molecules, forming new substances [1]. **[2]**

**14. (a)** **P and R** [1]; they have **low** melting and boiling points [1]; their liquids **do not conduct** electricity [1]

**(b)** Gas (room temperature is above its boiling point of −104 °C) [1]

**[4]**

*Examiner insight:* P is methanol and R is ethene, two of the named molecules; Q is sodium chloride, an ionic compound.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "weak covalent bonds" to explain low boiling points.
- Missing the non-bonding pair on nitrogen in ammonia.
- Drawing a single bond in N₂ or O₂.
- Electrons drawn outside the overlap for a shared pair.
- Saying the substance has "no electrons" instead of "no delocalised electrons or ions".
