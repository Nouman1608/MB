These notes condense section 1.2 (statements 1.2.1 and 1.2.2) of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017, plus the Practical Endorsement rules in section 5g. H432 is the full A level, so nothing here is labelled AS or A2. The [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement/) explains each technique in full, and the [practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-practical-skills-practical-endorsement-practice/) let you check your recall.

Course pages are on the [OCR A Level Chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/a-level/chemistry/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/a-level/chemistry/) lists every statement, and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/) show where to focus.

## The endorsement in one box

```
Component 04, teacher assessed, reported separately: Pass or Not Classified
Minimum 12 assessed practical activities, covering PAG1 to PAG12
Every 1.2.1 skill and every 1.2.2 technique shown before the course ends
Judged against CPAC 1 to 5, "consistently and routinely"
Group work allowed; evidence of competence must be your own
Keep a record of all practical work (log book or portfolio)
A previous result can be carried forward if you retake
```

Practical techniques can also be tested in the written papers through 1.1.2(a).

| 11 | pH measurement | Measure pH |
| 12 | Research skills | Investigative approaches; research; citing sources |

Safe handling of hazardous substances, 1.2.2(k), is covered across your selection of activities.

## CPAC in five lines

1. **Follows written procedures** correctly.
2. **Applies investigative approaches**: uses apparatus and ICT with minimal help, works methodically, controls variables, picks equipment for accurate results.
3. **Works safely**: identifies hazards, assesses risks, uses safety equipment with minimal prompting.
4. **Makes and records observations**: accurate, precise, sufficient data with units.
5. **Researches, references and reports**: uses software; cites sources.

## 1.2.1 skills: what each one means

| Statement | In practice |
|---|---|
| (a) investigative approaches | Plan your own method to answer a question |
| (b) safe use | Hazard = could cause harm; risk = chance it does; control matches the hazard |
| (c) written instructions | Follow steps in order |
| (d)–(e) observations, records | Dated lab book; raw data in ink; units in headers |
| (f) present data | Tables and graphs with labelled axes |
| (g) software | Spreadsheets, graph plotting, data loggers |
| (h) research | Books, journals and reputable websites |
| (i) citation | Author, title, date, URL and access date |
| (j) instruments | Wide range of apparatus across the course |

## Measurement, heating and pH, 1.2.2(a)–(c)

| Quantity | Apparatus | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | Balance | Weigh by difference |
| Time | Stopwatch or data logger | Start timing as reactants mix |
| Volume of liquid | Burette, pipette, volumetric flask, measuring cylinder | Eye level with the meniscus bottom |
| Volume of gas | Gas syringe, or inverted burette or measuring cylinder over water | Pick a capacity above the predicted volume |
| Temperature | Thermometer or temperature probe | Stir before reading |
| pH | pH chart, pH meter, or probe on a data logger | Calibrate meters with buffers |

Heating: water bath (gentle, to about 100 °C), electric heater such as a heating mantle (no flame), sand bath (even heat above 100 °C). Choose a flameless method for flammable organic liquids.

## Useful relationships

```
mass for a standard solution = c x V(dm3) x M
mean titre = average of concordant titres only
gas volume at RTP (cm3) = n x 24 000
Rf = (baseline to spot centre) / (baseline to solvent front)
E(cell) = E(positive electrode) - E(negative electrode)
initial rate is proportional to 1/t (clock reactions)
% uncertainty = uncertainty / measured value x 100
```

## 1.2.2 techniques: method in steps

### Standard solution, 1.2.2(e)

1. Weigh solid by difference.
2. Dissolve in distilled water in a beaker.
3. Transfer to a volumetric flask; rinse beaker, rod and funnel into it.
4. Make up so the meniscus bottom touches the line at eye level.
5. Stopper and invert to mix.

### Titration, 1.2.2(d)(i)

1. Rinse the pipette and burette with their own solutions.
2. Pipette with a filler; let it drain.
3. Rough titre, then accurate titres added dropwise near the end point.
4. Mean of concordant titres only (usually within 0.10 cm³).

### Recrystallisation, 1.2.2(g)(i)

Minimum hot solvent → hot filtration (fluted paper) → slow cooling, then ice → filtration under reduced pressure → wash with ice-cold solvent → dry.

### Liquid purification, 1.2.2(g)(ii)

Separating funnel (vent often) → keep the organic layer → dry with an anhydrous salt → distil.

### Rates, 1.2.2(l)

| Initial rate (PAG10) | Continuous monitoring (PAG9) |
|---|---|
| Clock reaction: time t for fixed small extent | Measure gas volume, mass or colour against time |
| Rate ∝ 1/t | Gradient of curve gives rate |
| Repeat at several concentrations | Constant half-life means first order |

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Reflux vs distillation | Reflux: vertical condenser, vapour returns. Distillation: condenser leads to collector, thermometer at side arm |
| Fluted paper vs reduced pressure | Fluted: fast gravity, hot, removes insoluble impurities. Reduced pressure: collects and dries crystals |
| Hazard vs risk | The property vs the chance of harm in your method |
| Water bath vs sand bath | Water bath to about 100 °C; sand bath for higher temperatures; both avoid naked flames |
| pH chart vs pH meter | Chart: rough. Meter or probe: precise after calibration with buffers |
| Pure vs impure solid | Pure: sharp melting point. Impure: lower and over a range |

## Indicator choice, 1.2.2(f)

| Acid | Base | Indicator |
|---|---|---|
| Strong | Strong | Methyl orange or phenolphthalein |
| Weak | Strong | Phenolphthalein |
| Strong | Weak | Methyl orange |
| Weak | Weak | No sharp end point; use a pH meter |

## Tests to recall, 1.2.2(d)(iii)

| Test for | Reagent | Positive result |
|---|---|---|
| Carbonate | Dilute acid | Effervescence |
| Sulfate | Barium ions | White precipitate |
| Halide | Silver nitrate, then aqueous ammonia | White, cream or yellow precipitate |
| Ammonium | NaOH, warm | Gas turns damp red litmus blue |
| Alkene | Bromine water | Orange to colourless |
| Aldehyde | Tollens' reagent | Silver mirror |
| Carbonyl | 2,4-DNP | Orange or yellow precipitate |
| Primary/secondary alcohol or aldehyde | Acidified dichromate, warm | Orange to green |
| Carboxylic acid | Carbonate | Effervescence |

Ion test order: carbonate, then sulfate, then halide.

## Cells, 1.2.2(j)

Two half-cells, salt bridge, high-resistance voltmeter. E(cell) = E(positive) − E(negative). The salt-bridge solution must not react with either half-cell; avoid chloride with silver ions.

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish a hazard from a risk.
2. Calculate the mass of sodium carbonate (M = 106.0 g mol⁻¹) for 100.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ solution.
3. Titres: rough 19.20; then 18.65, 18.90, 18.60, 18.70 cm³. Find the mean titre.
4. Choose an indicator for ethanoic acid against sodium hydroxide.
5. On a paper chromatogram the solvent front is 5.5 cm from the baseline and a spot is 3.3 cm from it. Find Rf.
6. Use Fe²⁺/Fe −0.44 V and Ag⁺/Ag +0.80 V to find the standard cell potential.
7. In a clock reaction, doubling one concentration cuts the time from 50 s to 25 s. State the order.
8. A pH meter reads 2.00 for 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Is this the expected value?
9. Why is fluted filter paper used for hot filtration?
10. Which PAG covers research skills, and what must you do with sources?
11. Predict the hydrogen volume at RTP from 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol of zinc reacting with excess acid (1 : 1).
12. State the heating method for refluxing a mixture containing ethanol, and why.

### Answers

1. Hazard: the property that could cause harm. Risk: the chance of harm from the way you use it.
2. 0.150 × 0.1000 × 106.0 = **1.59 g**.
3. Concordant: 18.65, 18.60, 18.70 → **18.65 cm³** (18.90 is not concordant).
4. **Phenolphthalein** (weak acid, strong base).
5. 3.3 / 5.5 = **0.60**.
6. 0.80 − (−0.44) = **+1.24 V**.
7. 1/t doubles from 0.020 to 0.040 s⁻¹: **first order**.
8. **Yes**: pH = −log(0.0100) = 2.00 for a strong acid.
9. It filters fast, so the product does not crystallise in the funnel.
10. **PAG12**; cite them correctly.
11. 1.50 × 10⁻³ × 24 000 = **36.0 cm³**.
12. A **water bath or electric heater**: ethanol is flammable, so no naked flame.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a generic control measure ("take care") that is not linked to the named hazard.
- Including the rough titre, or a non-concordant titre, in the mean.
- Leaving the rough titre unrecorded; your record should show every reading.
- Putting the thermometer bulb in the liquid during distillation instead of level with the side arm.
- Suggesting an indicator for a weak acid against a weak base.
- Using excess hot solvent when recrystallising, then wondering why the yield is low.
- Saying an impure solid melts at a higher temperature; it melts lower and over a range.
- Measuring the Rf distance to the top of the plate, not to the marked solvent front.
- Forgetting to calibrate a pH meter with buffers before use.
- Calling a clock reaction a continuous monitoring method.
