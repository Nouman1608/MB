This practice set covers outcomes 17.18 to 17.33 of Topic 17: Transition Metals and their Chemistry, including Core Practical 14, from the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry (XCH11/YCH11) specification, Issue 1, September 2017. These outcomes are examined in the IA2 paper Unit 5: Transition Metals and Organic Nitrogen Chemistry. Attempt them only after the unit's [study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-transition-metals-redox-and-catalysis/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-transition-metals-redox-and-catalysis-revision-notes/); the [checklist](/checklists/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) and [Edexcel chemistry hub](/boards/edexcel/a-level/chemistry/) help you see what is left.

Use these values throughout. E⦵ / V: VO₂⁺/VO²⁺ +1.00; VO²⁺/V³⁺ +0.34; V³⁺/V²⁺ −0.26; I₂/I⁻ +0.54; Zn²⁺/Zn −0.76; Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ +1.33; Cr³⁺/Cr²⁺ −0.41; O₂/H₂O +1.23; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77; S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ +2.01. Ar: O 16.0, Cu 63.5, H 1.0, S 32.1, N 14.0. R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Zinc granules are added to acidified ammonium vanadate(V). The colours seen in order are yellow, green, blue, green, violet.

**(a)** Give the formula of the vanadium species responsible for the blue colour and for the violet colour. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why green is seen twice. **[2]**

**2.** This question uses the E⦵ values above.

**(a)** Show that iodide ions can reduce VO₂⁺ to VO²⁺ but cannot reduce VO²⁺ to V³⁺. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the overall ionic equation for the reaction of I⁻ with VO₂⁺. **[2]**
**(c)** A solution of V²⁺ is mixed with an equal amount of VO²⁺ in acid. Predict the vanadium product, justify it with E⦵cell, and write the equation. **[3]**

**3.** Orange potassium dichromate(VI) solution is acidified and left with excess zinc in a flask that lets hydrogen escape but keeps air out.

**(a)** Describe the colour changes and give the chromium species at the end. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate E⦵cell for the final reduction step and write its equation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, using E⦵ values, why air must be kept out. **[1]**

**4.** A student converts Cr³⁺(aq) into dichromate(VI).

**(a)** State what is seen when excess NaOH(aq) is added to Cr³⁺(aq), and write the equation for the dissolving step. **[2]**
**(b)** Hydrogen peroxide is added and the mixture warmed. State the colour change and write the equation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the excess H₂O₂ is removed before the solution is acidified, and state the final colour. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is seen when dilute acid is added to yellow potassium chromate(VI) solution, and why the change is not a redox reaction. **[3]**

**6.** Solutions P, Q and R each hold a single cation from 17.22.

| | P | Q | R |
|---|---|---|---|
| Solution colour | pink | colourless | green |
| Few drops NaOH(aq) | blue ppt | white ppt | green ppt |
| Excess NaOH(aq) | no change | ppt dissolves | no change |
| Excess NH₃(aq) | straw-coloured solution, darkens in air | ppt dissolves | blue solution |

**(a)** Identify the cation in each of P, Q and R. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the equation for Q's precipitate dissolving in excess NaOH(aq), and name this behaviour. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the equation for R's precipitate dissolving in excess NH₃(aq). **[1]**

**7.** Hexaaquairon(III) ions react with aqueous ammonia.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction and name the type of reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the iron(III) precipitate does not dissolve in excess ammonia, although the copper(II) precipitate does. **[1]**

**8.** A pink solution of cobalt(II) chloride is treated with concentrated hydrochloric acid, then diluted with water.

**(a)** Write the equation for the change with concentrated acid and state the colour change. **[2]**
**(b)** State the shape and coordination number of each complex. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the effect of then adding water. **[2]**

**9.** Ethane-1,2-diamine (en) replaces ammonia in a cobalt(II) complex:

[Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3en → [Co(en)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃

Use these values at 298 K: ΔH = −7.0 kJ mol⁻¹, ΔSsystem = +88 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Explain why ΔSsystem is positive. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate ΔStotal. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate K and explain what it shows about the stability of [Co(en)₃]²⁺. **[3]**

**10.** This question is about heterogeneous catalysis.

**(a)** Explain, with equations and oxidation numbers, how V₂O₅ catalyses the oxidation of sulfur dioxide. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how a catalytic converter removes CO and NO from exhaust gas. Include an equation. **[3]**

**11.** This question is about homogeneous catalysis.

**(a)** Explain why S₂O₈²⁻ reacts slowly with I⁻ without a catalyst, even though E⦵cell is large. **[1]**
**(b)** Use E⦵ values to show that Fe²⁺ can catalyse this reaction. Write both steps. **[4]**
**(c)** Acidified KMnO₄ is added to warm ethanedioate solution. Describe how [MnO₄⁻] changes with time, and explain the shape. **[3]**

**12.** Core Practical 14. A student prepares tetraamminecopper(II) sulfate, [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄·H₂O, from 3.00 g of CuSO₄·5H₂O dissolved in a little water. Concentrated ammonia is added until the precipitate redissolves, then ethanol is added and the crystals are filtered off.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of CuSO₄·5H₂O and of the product. **[2]**
**(b)** The dry product weighs 2.10 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why ethanol is added. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest two causes of the yield falling short of 100%. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Blue: **VO²⁺** [1]; violet: **V²⁺** [1]
**(b)** First green: a mixture of yellow VO₂⁺ and blue VO²⁺ [1]; second green: V³⁺ [1]
*Examiner insight:* The right colour with the wrong formula, such as VO₂⁺ for blue, does not gain credit; work out V's oxidation number from the formula.

**2. (a)** VO₂⁺ → VO²⁺: E⦵cell = 1.00 − 0.54 = +0.46 V, positive so feasible [1]; VO²⁺ → V³⁺: 0.34 − 0.54 = −0.20 V, negative so not feasible [1]
**(b)** Electrons balanced, two VO₂⁺ per I₂ [1]: **2VO₂⁺ + 4H⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2VO²⁺ + I₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** E⦵cell = 0.34 − (−0.26) = +0.60 V, so VO²⁺ is reduced and V²⁺ oxidised [1]; both give **V³⁺** [1]; **VO²⁺ + V²⁺ + 2H⁺ → 2V³⁺ + H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs both E⦵cell values written out with signs and a conclusion for each; a bare "yes/no" is not enough.

**3. (a)** Orange to green (Cr³⁺) then blue [1]; final species **Cr²⁺** [1]
**(b)** E⦵cell = −0.41 − (−0.76) = **+0.35 V** [1]; **2Cr³⁺ + Zn → 2Cr²⁺ + Zn²⁺** [1]
**(c)** Oxygen would oxidise Cr²⁺ back to Cr³⁺: E⦵cell = 1.23 − (−0.41) = +1.64 V, which is positive [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the colour sequence in order with the species; "turns blue" alone misses the green Cr³⁺ stage.

**4. (a)** Grey-green precipitate that dissolves to a dark green solution [1]; **[Cr(OH)₃(H₂O)₃] + 3OH⁻ → [Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ + 3H₂O** [1]
**(b)** Dark green to yellow [1]; equation: **2[Cr(OH)₆]³⁻ + 3H₂O₂ → 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2OH⁻ + 8H₂O**, balanced [1]
**(c)** In acid, leftover H₂O₂ would reduce the dichromate(VI) back to Cr³⁺ [1]; final colour **orange** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Balance the H₂O₂ equation by electrons first (each Cr loses 3, each H₂O₂ gains 2), then check charge.

**5.** Yellow turns orange [1]; H⁺ shifts 2CrO₄²⁻ + 2H⁺ ⇌ Cr₂O₇²⁻ + H₂O to the right [1]; chromium is +6 in both ions, so no oxidation number changes [1]
*Examiner insight:* Quote the oxidation number of Cr in both species; "it is an equilibrium" does not explain why it is not redox.

**6. (a)** P **Co²⁺** [1]; Q **Zn²⁺** [1]; R **Ni²⁺** [1]
**(b)** **[Zn(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 2OH⁻ → [Zn(OH)₄]²⁻ + 4H₂O** [1]; amphoteric behaviour [1]
**(c)** **[Ni(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 6NH₃ → [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ + 4H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* R's green solution alone could suggest Fe²⁺ or Cr³⁺; use the excess-reagent results to justify the identity.

**7. (a)** **[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3NH₃ → [Fe(OH)₃(H₂O)₃] + 3NH₄⁺** [1]; deprotonation (acid-base) [1]
**(b)** NH₃ does not replace the ligands on iron(III) to form a soluble ammine complex, whereas with copper(II) ligand exchange gives [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ [1]
*Examiner insight:* With ammonia as the base, the products include NH₄⁺, not H₂O; check that charges balance.

**8. (a)** **[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]; pink to blue [1]
**(b)** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺: octahedral, 6 [1]; [CoCl₄]²⁻: tetrahedral, 4 [1]
**(c)** Water increases the H₂O concentration and lowers [Cl⁻], so the equilibrium shifts left [1]; the solution turns pink again [1]
*Examiner insight:* Explain the shape change by the larger size of Cl⁻; "the charge changes" does not account for four ligands.

**9. (a)** Four particles become seven, so disorder increases [1]
**(b)** ΔSsurroundings = −ΔH/T = +7000/298 = +23.5 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]; ΔStotal = 88 + 23.5 = **+111.5 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** ln K = ΔStotal/R = 111.5/8.31 = 13.4 [1]; K = **7 × 10⁵** (about) [1]; K is very large, so the equilibrium lies far to the right and the chelate complex is much more stable [1]
*Examiner insight:* Change ΔH from kJ to J (×1000) before you divide by T; skipping this makes ΔSsurroundings 1000 times too small.

**10. (a)** **SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄**, V reduced from +5 to +4 [1]; **V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅**, V oxidised from +4 to +5 [1]; V₂O₅ is regenerated, so it is a catalyst [1]
**(b)** CO and NO adsorb onto the metal surface, weakening their bonds [1]; they react: **2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂** [1]; CO₂ and N₂ desorb, freeing the surface [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name both adsorption and desorption; the specification describes both steps.

**11. (a)** S₂O₈²⁻ and I⁻ are both anions; their mutual repulsion gives a high activation energy [1]
**(b)** Step 1: **S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺** [1]; E⦵cell = 2.01 − 0.77 = +1.24 V [1]; step 2: **2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂** [1]; for step 2, 0.77 − 0.54 gives +0.23 V, so both steps are feasible and Fe²⁺ is regenerated [1]
**(c)** Slow fall at first [1]; then a steep fall as Mn²⁺ (a product) catalyses the reaction [1]; then levels off once the reactants run out [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), each step needs its own E⦵cell; one overall value does not show that iron can both gain and lose electrons.

**12. (a)** CuSO₄·5H₂O = **249.6** [1]; [Cu(NH₃)₄]SO₄·H₂O = **245.6** [1]
**(b)** n(CuSO₄·5H₂O) = 3.00/249.6 = 0.01202 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.01202 × 245.6 = 2.952 g [1]; yield = 2.10/2.952 × 100 = **71.1%** [1]
**(c)** The complex is less soluble in ethanol, so more crystallises out [1]
**(d)** Any two: product left in the filtrate [1]; losses on transfer or filtering, or impurity removed on washing [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mole ratio is 1 : 1 Cu to complex; round only at the end to avoid drift in the yield.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping VO₂⁺ and VO²⁺.
- Subtracting E⦵ values the wrong way, or writing E⦵cell without a sign.
- Stopping a reduction at the wrong oxidation state because the next step was not checked.
- Giving one observation when two (precipitate and excess) are needed.
- Writing precipitates as charged complexes.
- Missing NH₄⁺ in deprotonation by ammonia.
- Stating the chelate effect without counting particles.
- Leaving out desorption in heterogeneous catalysis.
- Describing autocatalysis as a steady decrease.
- Forgetting water of crystallisation in Mr values.
