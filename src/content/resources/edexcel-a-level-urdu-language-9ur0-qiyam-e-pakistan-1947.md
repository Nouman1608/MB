This guide covers **sub-theme 4.1, the creation of Pakistan in 1947 (قیامِ پاکستان)**, of Theme 4 in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). The specification frames the sub-theme through its consequences, in three aspects:

- **پاکستان پر سیاسی اثرات**: the political effects on Pakistan
- **پاکستان کے عوام پر تقسیمِ پاک و ہند کے سماجی اثرات**: the social effects of partition on the people of Pakistan
- **پاکستان میں جمہوریت کا ارتقا**: the evolution of democracy in Pakistan

The [creation of Pakistan revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-creation-of-pakistan-1947-revision-notes/) cover the road to 1947: the Muslim League, the two-nation theory, the Lahore Resolution and the events of August 1947. Notice that the specification's aspects look **forward from 1947**, so this guide concentrates on what followed. Theme 4 is set in Pakistan. The sub-theme is not a research subject, so it appears in translation, reading and Paper 3 tasks. Practise with the [1947 practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-qiyam-e-pakistan-1947-practice/).

Dates on this page are well established; even so, check any detail against a source before you rely on it in writing.

## Aspect 1: political effects on Pakistan

The new state had to build a government almost from nothing. Organise the political effects into five areas.

1. **Building the state.** Karachi became the first capital; Muhammad Ali Jinnah was Governor-General and Liaquat Ali Khan Prime Minister. Offices, records, staff and funds had to be created or divided, and the division of assets with India caused disputes.
2. **Loss of founding leaders.** Jinnah died in September 1948 and Liaquat Ali Khan was assassinated in Rawalpindi in October 1951. Many historians link early instability to this loss of leadership.
3. **Defining the state.** The Constituent Assembly passed the **قراردادِ مقاصد** (Objectives Resolution) in March 1949, setting out principles for a future constitution. The question of the place of religion in the state has been debated ever since.
4. **Two wings, one state.** Pakistan had a western and an eastern wing separated by India. Disputes over language (the demand that Bengali be recognised alongside Urdu, which led to the language movement of 1952), representation and economic fairness grew, and in 1971 East Pakistan became Bangladesh.
5. **Relations with India.** The Kashmir dispute began in 1947–48 and has shaped Pakistan's politics, defence spending and foreign policy ever since.

## Aspect 2: social effects of partition on the people of Pakistan

**سماجی اثرات** means social effects: on families, communities and identity. Develop these strands:

- **Migration and resettlement.** Millions of Muslims arrived from India, many with nothing. Refugee camps, the allocation of property left by Hindus and Sikhs who had moved to India (**متروکہ املاک**, evacuee property), and the struggle for work shaped the early years.
- **Violence and loss.** Communal violence killed very large numbers and left deep trauma; women suffered particular violence and abduction. Many survivors spoke little about it for decades.
- **Divided families.** Relatives found themselves on opposite sides of a new border, and visits became difficult. This separation is a recurring subject in Urdu literature and drama.
- **New identities.** In Karachi and other cities of Sindh, Urdu-speaking migrants came to be known as **مہاجر**, and that identity later became politically significant. Punjab absorbed the largest numbers in its western half.
- **Culture and memory.** Partition shaped Urdu literature: Saadat Hasan Manto's short stories, such as *Toba Tek Singh*, and Faiz Ahmad Faiz's poem *Subh-e-Azadi* (August 1947) express the cost behind the celebration. Oral history projects now record survivors' memories.

When you write about social effects, keep the human scale: a sentence about one family's experience often says more than a sentence of general statistics, and you should avoid quoting precise death or migration totals because estimates vary.

## Aspect 3: the evolution of democracy in Pakistan

**ارتقا** means evolution, gradual development. The aspect asks how democracy has developed, which means tracing a path with interruptions. Learn this outline:

| Year | Development |
|---|---|
| 1956 | First constitution; Pakistan becomes an Islamic Republic |
| 1958 | Martial law; Ayub Khan comes to power |
| 1962 | Second constitution, with a presidential system |
| 1969 | Ayub Khan hands over to General Yahya Khan |
| 1970 | First general election held on the basis of adult franchise |
| 1971 | Separation of East Pakistan |
| 1973 | Third constitution, still in force, with a parliamentary system |
| 1977 | Martial law under General Zia-ul-Haq |
| 1988 | Return to elected civilian government after Zia's death |
| 1999 | General Pervez Musharraf takes power |
| 2008 | Elections and return to civilian rule |
| 2010 | The Eighteenth Amendment restores parliamentary powers and expands provincial autonomy |
| 2013 | First transfer of power from one elected government that completed its term to another |

**Analytical questions:** Why has democracy been interrupted? (Weak early institutions, the loss of founding leaders, the power of the military and bureaucracy, regional tensions.) What has strengthened it? (The 1973 constitution's endurance, an active judiciary and media at times, the Eighteenth Amendment, consecutive elected governments.) A balanced answer recognises both continuity and fragility.

Useful terms: **آئین** (constitution), **مارشل لا** (martial law), **پارلیمانی نظام** (parliamentary system), **صدارتی نظام** (presidential system), **بالغ رائے دہی** (adult franchise), **انتقالِ اقتدار** (transfer of power), **صوبائی خود مختاری** (provincial autonomy), **آئینی ترمیم** (constitutional amendment).

## Worked example: translating into English

Original passage, written for this guide:

> میرے دادا اکثر بتاتے تھے کہ جب وہ امرتسر سے لاہور پہنچے تو ان کے پاس ایک صندوق کے سوا کچھ نہ تھا۔ کئی ہفتے مہاجر کیمپ میں گزارنے کے بعد انھیں ایک چھوٹی سی دکان ملی، اور وہیں سے انھوں نے زندگی دوبارہ شروع کی۔

**Model translation:** My grandfather often used to tell us that when he reached Lahore from Amritsar he had nothing but a trunk. After spending several weeks in a refugee camp he was given a small shop, and it was from there that he started life again.

**Points to notice:**

- **بتاتے تھے** with **اکثر** is habitual: "often used to tell".
- **کے سوا کچھ نہ تھا** = "had nothing but"; **نہ تھا** is a literary alternative to نہیں تھا.
- **مہاجر کیمپ** is best translated "refugee camp" in this context, though مہاجر literally means migrant.
- **انھیں ... ملی**: the dative construction "to him a shop came" becomes "he was given" or "he got".
- **وہیں سے**: the emphatic ہی gives "it was from there that".

## Worked example: a sentence into Urdu

"The constitution of 1973 is still in force, although it has been amended many times."

**Model:** 1973 کا آئین آج بھی نافذ ہے، اگرچہ اس میں کئی بار ترامیم کی جا چکی ہیں۔

**ترامیم کی جا چکی ہیں** is a passive in the perfect, agreeing with **ترامیم** (feminine plural); **اگرچہ** introduces the concession.

## Common mistakes

- Writing only about the causes of 1947 when the aspects ask about effects and the evolution of democracy.
- Mixing up the constitutions (1956, 1962, 1973) or the martial-law years.
- Presenting democracy's history as simple success or simple failure.
- Writing about India in hostile terms; analyse events, do not insult.
- Using **جمہوریت** with masculine agreement; it is feminine (**جمہوریت مضبوط ہوئی**).

## Exam technique

- In reading questions, historical texts often use the pluperfect (**ہو چکا تھا**, **پہنچ چکے تھے**) and literary negatives (**نہ تھا**). Read tense carefully before answering.
- When asked to evaluate a claim such as "democracy has failed in Pakistan", weigh interruptions against signs of consolidation and reach a reasoned judgement.
- In Paper 3 summaries, distinguish a historian's analysis from a survivor's personal memory; each has a different kind of authority.
