These notes condense the written route of Unit 4 (section 3.4.1) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Language (9670) specification, Version 5.2. For the full explanation and a worked example, use the [written unit study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-written-unit/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-written-unit-practice/). The other route is covered in the [non-exam assessment revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-language-9670-non-exam-assessment-nea-revision-notes/).

## The task in brief

- 2 hours 30 minutes. 50 marks. 30% of the International A-level.
- No sections. **One question from a choice of two.**
- Each question gives **different data**: spoken, written and multimodal. It may include lists of items (book titles, shop names, terms of address) and longer texts (advertising, news articles, speech extracts).
- The data are linked by a **common topic or theme**. No more than **three** texts or data sets.
- You **devise a research question** to suit the data, using some or all of it.
- You **plan and carry out an analysis** using **at least two** texts or data sets.
- You **write a report**.

## The four-part report

| Part | What it must do | Typical length in a plan |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Aim(s) | State what you are investigating | 2 to 4 sentences |
| 2. Method | Say which data were chosen and why; say how they will be analysed | A short paragraph |
| 3. Analysis | Present findings in categories, explored in detail | The longest part |
| 4. Conclusion | Draw conclusions and link them to the aim(s) | A short paragraph |

The lengths in the right-hand column are Marlbridge suggestions. The specification gives the four parts but not a word count for the exam.

## Skills tested (the unit is synoptic)

Critical thinking; planning and organisation; analysis; knowledge of linguistic methods, concepts and issues; data handling; academic argument and report writing.

## Assessment objectives

| AO | What it asks | Weighting in Unit 4 | What it looks like in the report |
|---|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Understanding of methods, terminology, concepts and issues | About 12% of the qualification | Precise aim, suitable frameworks, accurate terms |
| AO2 | Analysing how meanings are shaped in texts and data | About 12% of the qualification | Close analysis with examples and links to context |
| AO3 | Using English in different ways | About 6% of the qualification | Organisation, register, accuracy |

The unit is 30% of the qualification, so these are about 40%, 40% and 20% of the unit. The non-exam criteria in the specification are marked out of 20, 20 and 10, which matches. Check the published mark scheme for the exam route.

## The language levels (your toolkit)

- Phonetics and phonology: speech sounds and effects.
- Lexis and semantics: meaning at word and phrase level.
- Grammar: sentence, clause, phrase and word structure.
- Pragmatics: inference, assumed meaning, context.
- Discourse: extended stretches of language, genre and mode.
- Graphology: visual design and appearance.

## Turning data into a question

| Prompt | Question it generates |
|---|---|
| Genre | What are the distinctive features of this kind of language? |
| Function | What is the language used to do? |
| Attitudes | How do people feel about this language? |
| Users | Who uses this kind of language? |

A good question is **specific**, **answerable from the data** and uses **at least two data sets**.

## An analysis paragraph in five moves

1. **Claim** (point about a pattern).
2. **Example** (quoted from the data, with its data set).
3. **Term** (a language level or concept).
4. **Effect** (what it does to meaning or relationship).
5. **Link** (to the aim, and a comparison if possible).

## Exam traps

- A question that needs a data set you do not have.
- Only one data set used in the analysis.
- Describing the topic instead of the language.
- A list of features with no effects.
- A method that says what you did but not why you chose the data.
- A conclusion that makes claims about everyone from a small sample.
- No time left for the conclusion.

## Time plan (suggestion)

| Stage | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Read all data and note patterns | 10 |
| Choose a question, frameworks, categories and examples | 20 |
| Write aim and method | 20 |
| Write analysis | 80 |
| Write conclusion | 15 |
| Check | 5 |

These timings are a guide to adjust to your own writing speed. They add up to 150 minutes, the length of the paper.

## Quick self-test

1. How long is the written unit, and how many marks is it worth?
2. How many questions do you answer?
3. What is the maximum number of texts or data sets in a task?
4. What is the minimum number that you must use in your analysis?
5. Name the four suggested parts of the report.
6. What must the Method section explain?
7. Name three kinds of source the data may be drawn from.
8. Give one example of a "list of language items" given in the specification.
9. Which assessment objective covers organisation and register?
10. Why is the analysis section organised by findings rather than by text?
11. Write one research question suited to a list of shop names and a short advertisement.
12. Give one way of limiting a claim in a conclusion.

### Answers

1. 2 hours 30 minutes; 50 marks.
2. One, from a choice of two.
3. Three.
4. Two.
5. Aim(s), Method, Analysis, Conclusion.
6. Which data have been selected and why, and how they will be analysed.
7. Any three from spoken, written, multimodal.
8. Any one from book titles, shop names, terms of address.
9. AO3.
10. So that patterns can be compared across the data and linked to the aim.
11. For example: How do shop names and an advertisement use language to attract local customers?
12. For example, acknowledging that the sample is small and that the findings cannot be applied to all texts of the type.
