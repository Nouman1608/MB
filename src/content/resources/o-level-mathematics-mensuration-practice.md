These questions cover mensuration for Cambridge O Level Mathematics 4024: area and volume units, circles, arcs and sectors, and the cylinder, cone and sphere. Work through them in order. Questions marked *(no calculator)* are in the style of the non-calculator paper, and the rest allow a calculator. Write every step in your working, because most marks are for method. Where a question asks for an answer in terms of π, do not use a decimal. Otherwise give three significant figures unless the question says something else.

---

## Questions

**1.** Convert 3.2 m² to cm². **[1]**

**2.** *(no calculator)* A sector of a circle has radius 9 cm and arc length 5π cm. Find the angle of the sector and its area, giving the area in terms of π. **[3]**

**3.** *(no calculator)* A circle has area 36π cm². Find its circumference in terms of π. **[2]**

**4.** *(no calculator)* A cylinder has radius 5 cm and height 8 cm. Find its volume in terms of π. **[2]**

**5.** A solid is made by joining a cone to a hemisphere. Both have diameter 12 cm. The total height of the solid is 20 cm. Find the volume of the solid. **[4]**

**6.** A trapezium has parallel sides of 7 cm and 11 cm, and a perpendicular height of 5 cm. Find its area. **[2]**

**7.** A cone has base radius 7 cm and slant height 25 cm. Calculate its curved surface area. **[3]**

**8.** A sphere has volume 288π cm³. Find its radius, and then calculate its surface area. **[4]**

**9.** A sector of a circle has radius 12 cm and angle 75°. Calculate (a) the length of the arc, (b) the perimeter of the sector, (c) the area of the sector. **[5]**

**10.** A cylindrical jug has radius 10 cm and height 24 cm. It is full of water. All the water is poured into an empty rectangular tank with a base measuring 30 cm by 20 cm. Calculate the depth of water in the tank. **[5]**

**11.** A fictional storage tank is made of a cylinder of radius 3 m and height 4 m, with a hemisphere of radius 3 m fixed on top, forming one sealed container. The base of the cylinder is flat. (11 marks in total)

(a) Show that the volume of the tank is 54π m³. **[3]**

(b) Calculate the total outside surface area, including the flat base. **[4]**

(c) One litre of paint covers 8 m². Paint is sold only in whole litres. How many litres are needed to paint the whole outside surface once? **[2]**

(d) The tank is completely full of water. Calculate the volume of water in litres, to 3 significant figures. (1 m³ = 1000 litres.) **[2]**

**12.** O is the centre of a circle of radius 8 cm. A and B are points on the circle and angle AOB = 90°. The shaded region is bounded by the chord AB and the minor arc AB. (7 marks in total)

(a) Calculate the area of sector OAB. **[2]**

(b) Calculate the area of triangle OAB. **[1]**

(c) Find the area of the shaded region. **[1]**

(d) Calculate the perimeter of the shaded region. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 1 m² = 100 × 100 = 10 000 cm², so 3.2 m² = **32 000 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the scale factor for area is the square of the length factor, and candidates who multiply by 100 lose the mark at once.

**2.** 5π = (θ/360) × 2π × 9, so θ = **100°** [1]. Area = (100/360) × π × 9² [1] = **22.5π cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* find the angle from the arc length first, then reuse it for the area. Leaving the answer as 22.5π is correct when the question says "in terms of π".

**3.** πr² = 36π, so r = 6 cm [1]. Circumference = 2π × 6 = **12π cm** [1].

*Examiner insight:* work back to the radius before using any other formula. Using 36 as the radius is the usual slip.

**4.** V = π × 5² × 8 [1] = **200π cm³** [1].

*Examiner insight:* square the radius, not the diameter, and keep π in the answer when asked.

**5.** Radius = 6 cm, so the height of the cone = 20 − 6 = 14 cm [1]. Hemisphere = ⅔π × 6³ = 144π [1]. Cone = ⅓π × 6² × 14 = 168π [1]. Total = 312π = **980 cm³** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* the hemisphere's height is its radius, so subtract it from the total height to get the cone's height. Using 20 cm for the cone loses the first three marks.

**6.** ½ × (7 + 11) × 5 [1] = **45 cm²** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the height must be perpendicular to the parallel sides. A slanted side gives the wrong area.

**7.** Curved surface area = πrl [1] = π × 7 × 25 = 175π [1] = **550 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* the formula uses the slant height, not the vertical height. If the vertical height is given instead, you must find the slant height with Pythagoras first.

**8.** (4/3)πr³ = 288π, so r³ = 288 × 3 ÷ 4 = 216 [1]. r = **6 cm** [1]. Surface area = 4πr² [1] = 4π × 36 = 144π = **452 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* take the cube root only at the end of the rearrangement. Candidates often square-root instead of cube-root, or forget to divide by 4/3.

**9.** (a) Arc = (75/360) × 2π × 12 [1] = 5π = **15.7 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].
(b) Perimeter = 12 + 12 + 15.708 [1] = **39.7 cm** (3 s.f.).
(c) Area = (75/360) × π × 12² [1] = 30π = **94.2 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* the perimeter of a sector includes both straight radii. Candidates who give only the arc length lose the method mark.

**10.** Volume of jug = π × 10² × 24 = 2400π [1] = 7540 cm³ (3 s.f.) [1]. Base area of tank = 30 × 20 = 600 cm² [1]. Depth = volume ÷ base area [1] = 7539.8 ÷ 600 = **12.6 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* keep the unrounded volume in your calculator for the division. Using the rounded 7540 still gives 12.6 here, but rounding earlier can change the third significant figure.

**11.** (a) Cylinder = π × 3² × 4 = 36π [1]. Hemisphere = ⅔ × π × 3³ = 18π [1]. Total = 36π + 18π = **54π m³** [1].
(b) Curved surface of cylinder = 2π × 3 × 4 = 24π [1]. Curved surface of hemisphere = 2π × 3² = 18π [1]. Flat base = π × 3² = 9π [1]. Total = 51π = **160 m²** (3 s.f.) [1].
(c) 160.22 ÷ 8 = 20.03 [1], so **21 litres** are needed [1].
(d) 54π = 169.6 m³ [1], so volume = **170 000 litres** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* in "show that" questions you must show each volume before adding. In part (b) the flat base is included but there is no circle where the hemisphere meets the cylinder, so do not add it. In part (c), rounding 20.03 to 20 is wrong because 20 litres would not cover the surface.

**12.** (a) Area of sector = (90/360) × π × 8² [1] = 16π = **50.3 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].
(b) Area of triangle = ½ × 8 × 8 = **32 cm²** [1].
(c) Shaded area = 50.27 − 32 = **18.3 cm²** (3 s.f.) [1].
(d) Arc AB = (90/360) × 2π × 8 = 4π = 12.57 cm [1]. Chord AB = √(8² + 8²) = 8√2 = 11.31 cm [1]. Perimeter = 12.57 + 11.31 = **23.9 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* the perimeter of the shaded region uses the chord, not the two radii. Mark the chord on a sketch, and use Pythagoras for its length because the triangle is right-angled and isosceles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Converting m² to cm² by multiplying by 100 instead of 10 000, or m³ to cm³ by 1000 instead of 1 000 000.
- Using the diameter as the radius, especially when a solid is described by its diameter.
- Using the total height of a composite solid as the cone's height instead of subtracting the hemisphere's radius.
- Using the vertical height in the curved surface area of a cone, where the formula needs the slant height.
- Forgetting the straight edges when asked for a perimeter of a sector or segment.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-step problem, which changes the final third significant figure.
- Giving a decimal when the question says "in terms of π", or leaving π in an answer that asks for 3 significant figures.
- Giving 20 when 20.03 litres of paint are needed, instead of rounding up to the next whole number.
- Mixing units, such as a depth in cm with a volume in m³, without converting first.
