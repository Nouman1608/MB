## Section A

**1.** Identify three ways in which the Great Depression affected Germany, 1929-32. **[3]**

**2.** Explain two reasons why the SA helped the growth of Nazi support before 1933. **[4]**

**3.** Identify three actions by Papen or Hindenburg in January 1933 that led to Hitler's appointment. **[3]**

**4.** Explain how the Enabling Act was passed. **[3]**

**5.** Identify three organisations of the Nazi terror state. **[3]**

**6.** Explain two reasons why Schacht's policies were replaced by the Four Year Plan. **[4]**

**7.** Give three measures used to raise the birth rate or keep women in the home. **[3]**

**8.** Identify three steps in the persecution of Jews in Germany, 1933-38. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "The Nazis came to power in 1933 because of the Great Depression." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the success of Nazi policies towards the Christian churches. **[30]**

**11.** Assess the extent to which Nazi foreign policy, 1933-41, followed a consistent plan. **[30]**

**12.** "Nazi policies towards young people were successful." Evaluate this view. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: American loans were recalled after the Wall Street Crash (1); industrial production and exports fell sharply (1); registered unemployment reached about six million by early 1932 (1); banks failed in 1931 (1); farmers suffered falling prices and debt (1); parliamentary government broke down as cabinets relied on Article 48 (1). **[3]**

**2.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: the SA's marches and uniforms projected discipline and strength at a time of chaos, attracting voters who wanted order (2); street battles with communists presented the Nazis as the main defence against the KPD, appealing to the middle classes and elites (2); the SA gave unemployed young men food, shelter and a sense of purpose, growing the movement's active base (2). **[4]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: Papen met Hitler at Schröder's house in Cologne on 4 January 1933 (1); Papen agreed to serve as Vice-Chancellor in a Hitler cabinet with only three Nazis (1); Hindenburg refused Schleicher's request for emergency powers and a dissolution (1); Hindenburg accepted Papen's argument that Hitler could be controlled by conservatives (1); Hindenburg appointed Hitler on 30 January 1933 (1). **[3]**

**4.** It needed a two-thirds majority to change the constitution (1); KPD deputies were arrested or in hiding, and SA and SS men surrounded the Kroll Opera House to intimidate deputies (1); the Centre Party voted in favour after promises about the Church, so the Act passed by 444 to 94, with only the SPD voting against (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: the SS (1); the Gestapo (1); the SD (1); block wardens (1); the People's Court and Nazified judiciary (1); concentration camps such as Dachau (1). **[3]**

**6.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: rearmament and recovery caused a foreign-currency and raw-materials crisis in 1935-36, as imports of food and raw materials competed (2); Hitler wanted the economy and army ready for war within four years, whereas Schacht urged slower rearmament and more exports (2); Göring's political power and Hitler's preference for autarky over trade sidelined Schacht, who resigned from the economics ministry in 1937 (2). **[4]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: marriage loans, with a quarter cancelled for each child (1); the Mother's Cross for large families (1); pressure on women to leave professions, such as the ban on women serving as judges (1); propaganda for *Kinder, Küche, Kirche* (1); restrictions on contraception and harsher abortion penalties (1); the Lebensborn programme (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: the boycott of Jewish shops and businesses (April 1933) (1); exclusion of Jews from the civil service (April 1933) (1); the Nuremberg Laws removing citizenship and banning marriage with non-Jews (September 1935) (1); the seizure of Jewish businesses ("Aryanisation") (1); Kristallnacht (November 1938) (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** the Depression destroyed confidence in Weimar, brought six million unemployed and turned the Nazis from 12 seats (1928) into the largest party (1932); it broke Müller's coalition and led to rule by decree; it boosted the KPD, frightening elites into seeking a mass anti-communist ally. **Against:** Nazi organisation, propaganda and Hitler's appeal turned discontent into votes, whereas other parties failed to; long-term weaknesses -- Article 48, hostility to the Republic among the army, judiciary and elites; above all, the intrigue of Papen, Hindenburg and the conservative elites, who appointed Hitler when the Nazi vote was already falling. **Criterion:** distinguish necessary conditions from the decisive cause. **Judgement:** the Depression was necessary but not sufficient; the decision of a narrow elite in January 1933 explains why it was Hitler and why then. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Catholic Church:** the Concordat (July 1933) secured early neutrality and the dissolution of the Centre Party, a short-term success; but the regime broke its promises, closed Catholic youth groups and schools, and faced the papal encyclical *Mit brennender Sorge* (1937) and Galen's 1941 sermon, which forced the official euthanasia programme to be halted. **Protestant churches:** the German Christians and Reich Bishop Müller failed to unify Protestantism under Nazi control; the Pastors' Emergency League and Confessing Church resisted, though Niemöller's arrest (1937) showed the price. **German Faith Movement:** made little headway. **Criterion:** success measured as control and conformity rather than replacement of belief. **Judgement:** the regime neutralised the churches as political forces and most clergy avoided open opposition, but it failed to Nazify Christianity or replace it, so success was partial. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Consistency:** aims in *Mein Kampf* (Versailles, Lebensraum, anti-communism) were pursued; the sequence of rearmament, conscription (1935), the Rhineland, Anschluss, Czechoslovakia and Poland fits a step-by-step plan; the Hossbach Memorandum (1937) shows Hitler discussing war; Barbarossa (1941) fulfilled the drive east. **Against:** the Anschluss was improvised in days after Schuschnigg called a plebiscite; the Nazi-Soviet Pact contradicted anti-communism; Britain was not meant to be an enemy, yet war came in 1939; Hitler exploited the opportunities that appeasement and the Spanish Civil War created. **Criterion:** distinguish long-term aims from methods and timing. **Judgement:** consistent aims, opportunist methods -- a common and defensible conclusion, which strong answers test against particular episodes. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Success:** most young people joined the Hitler Youth or BDM even before compulsion in 1939; camps, sport and military training were popular; syllabuses taught race science and nationalist history, and teachers joined the NSLB; elite Napolas and Adolf Hitler Schools trained future leaders; many young soldiers in 1939-41 showed strong commitment. **Limits:** attendance and enthusiasm fell among older teenagers as activities became military drill; the Swing movement and Edelweiss Pirates rejected the regime; academic standards and university enrolment fell; family and church loyalties persisted. **Criterion:** compare membership with genuine belief. **Judgement:** policies were broadly successful in organising and influencing youth, but the growth of non-conformity by the late 1930s shows indoctrination was incomplete. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
