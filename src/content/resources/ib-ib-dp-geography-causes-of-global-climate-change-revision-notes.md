Condensed recall for **9.1 Causes of global climate change** (Unit 2: Global climate --
vulnerability and resilience). For the full explanation, read the
[Global Climate study guide](/resources/ib-dp-geography-global-climate/).

## Energy balance

- The Earth receives **incoming shortwave radiation** from the Sun and emits **outgoing longwave
  radiation**.
- When the two are in balance, global temperature is stable.
- Changes in the balance come from factors including **solar radiation variations** (including global
  dimming due to volcanic eruptions) and **terrestrial albedo changes**.

## Greenhouse effect

Greenhouse gases absorb some of the longwave radiation the surface emits and re-emit it, warming
the lower atmosphere. This **natural greenhouse effect** keeps the planet warmer than it would
otherwise be. Human activity that raises greenhouse gas concentrations **enhances** the effect.

## Natural and human factors

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Volcanic aerosols | Reflect incoming sunlight (global dimming), causing temporary cooling |
| Variations in solar radiation | Change the energy received |
| Albedo change | Lighter surfaces reflect more; darker surfaces absorb more |
| Higher greenhouse gas concentrations | More outgoing longwave radiation absorbed and re-emitted |

## Feedback loops

- **Positive feedback** amplifies change. Example: warming melts ice, which lowers albedo, so the
  surface absorbs more energy, so warming increases further.
- A **negative feedback** works against change and moves a system back toward balance.

## Exam traps

- Confusing shortwave (incoming) with longwave (outgoing) radiation.
- Describing the greenhouse effect as harmful in itself; it is the **enhanced** effect that is the
  concern.
- Treating "positive" feedback as meaning beneficial.
- Mixing up causes (this subtopic) with responses (adaptation and mitigation).

## Self-test

1. What happens to incoming shortwave radiation at equilibrium?
2. How do greenhouse gases warm the lower atmosphere?
3. Why do volcanic eruptions cause temporary cooling?
4. Explain the ice-albedo feedback loop.
5. Why is it called positive feedback?

**Answers:** 1. It is balanced by outgoing longwave radiation. 2. They absorb and re-emit some
outgoing longwave radiation. 3. Aerosols reflect incoming sunlight. 4. Melting ice lowers albedo, more
energy is absorbed, warming increases and melts more ice. 5. Because it amplifies the original change.
