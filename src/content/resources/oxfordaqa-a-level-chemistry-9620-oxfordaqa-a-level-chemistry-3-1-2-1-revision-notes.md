These notes condense **3.1.2.1 Relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1, drawn on by Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. The [relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-1/) has the full explanations and worked examples; the [relative atomic mass and relative molecular mass practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-1-practice/) test them.

## The subtopic in five lines

1. Masses of atoms are compared with one-twelfth of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
2. Ar is the average mass of an atom of an element on this scale; Mr is the average mass of a molecule.
3. Ionic compounds have a relative formula mass, because they have no molecules.
4. Relative masses have no units.
5. Mr = sum of Ar values of all atoms in the formula.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | Average mass of an atom of an element relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative isotopic mass | Mass of an atom of a particular isotope relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative molecular mass, Mr | Average mass of a molecule relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12 |
| Relative formula mass | Sum of the Ar values in the formula of an ionic compound (or other giant structure) |
| Water of crystallisation | Water molecules held within a crystal, shown after a dot in the formula |
| Molar mass | Mass of one mole, in g mol⁻¹; numerically equal to Mr |

## Why Ar is rarely a whole number

Ar is a weighted mean of the isotopic masses of the element's naturally occurring isotopes. Chlorine: (35 × 75 + 37 × 25) ÷ 100 = 35.5. Carbon-12 has a relative isotopic mass of exactly 12 by definition.

## Method for Mr

1. Count each type of atom, expanding brackets and including water of crystallisation.
2. Multiply each count by Ar.
3. Add, keeping the precision of the Ar data.

Practice values (Ar: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Al 27.0, P 31.0, S 32.1, K 39.1, Cr 52.0, Fe 55.8, Cu 63.5):

| Formula | Working | Mr |
|---|---|---|
| Al₂(SO₄)₃ | 2(27.0) + 3(32.1) + 12(16.0) | 342.3 |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 63.5 + 32.1 + 9(16.0) + 10(1.0) | 249.6 |
| (NH₄)₃PO₄ | 3(14.0) + 12(1.0) + 31.0 + 4(16.0) | 149.0 |
| K₂Cr₂O₇ | 2(39.1) + 2(52.0) + 7(16.0) | 294.2 |
| Na₂CO₃·10H₂O | 2(23.0) + 12.0 + 13(16.0) + 20(1.0) | 286.0 |
| Fe₂(SO₄)₃ | 2(55.8) + 3(32.1) + 12(16.0) | 399.9 |

## Reverse problems

- **Unknown element in a formula:** subtract the known atoms from Mr, divide by the number of unknown atoms. M₂O₃ with Mr 159.6: (159.6 − 48.0) ÷ 2 = 55.8, so M is iron.
- **Water of crystallisation:** x = (Mr of hydrate − Mr of anhydrous salt) ÷ 18.0.
- **Percentage by mass:** (atoms × Ar ÷ Mr) × 100. Water in CuSO₄·5H₂O: 90.0 ÷ 249.6 × 100 = 36.1%.

## Precision and checking

- Ar values on the exam Periodic Table are given to one decimal place; keep that precision in every Mr.
- Quote Mr to one decimal place, for example 58.0 for butane, not 58.
- Sense check: the Mr of a hydrate must be larger than the Mr of its anhydrous salt by a multiple of 18.0.
- Sense check: in a reverse problem the Ar you find should match an element in the Periodic Table to within about 0.1; if it does not, recheck the atom count.
- Ar can be found from a mass spectrum (subtopic 3.1.1.2) and then used straight away to find an Mr, so carry the calculated value rather than a rounded one.

## Exam traps

- No units for Ar or Mr; g mol⁻¹ belongs to molar mass.
- Definitions need "average", "one-twelfth" and "carbon-12" (not just "carbon").
- Ar is for atoms of an element; Mr is for molecules; ionic compounds take relative formula mass.
- Brackets multiply everything inside; the dot in a hydrate means add.
- Oxygen atoms in water of crystallisation must be counted with the other oxygen atoms.
- Use the Ar values given, to one decimal place; do not round chlorine to 35 or 36.

## Self-test

1. Define relative atomic mass.
2. Define relative molecular mass.
3. Why is the term relative formula mass used for magnesium oxide?
4. Why do Ar and Mr have no units?
5. Calculate Mr of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆.
6. Calculate the relative formula mass of Mg₃(PO₄)₂ (Ar: Mg 24.3, P 31.0, O 16.0).
7. Calculate the relative formula mass of BaCl₂·2H₂O (Ar: Ba 137.3, Cl 35.5).
8. A chloride XCl₃ has relative formula mass 133.5. Find Ar of X and identify it.
9. MgSO₄·xH₂O has Mr 246.4. Find x (Mr of MgSO₄ = 120.4).
10. Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃.
11. What is the relative isotopic mass of carbon-12?
12. What is the molar mass of ethanoic acid, CH₃COOH?

### Answers

1. The average mass of an atom of an element relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
2. The average mass of a molecule relative to one-twelfth of the mass of an atom of carbon-12.
3. It is ionic: a lattice of Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ ions with no molecules.
4. They are ratios of two masses, so the units cancel.
5. 6(12.0) + 12(1.0) + 6(16.0) = 180.0
6. 3(24.3) + 2(31.0) + 8(16.0) = 262.9
7. 137.3 + 2(35.5) + 4(1.0) + 2(16.0) = 244.3
8. 133.5 − 106.5 = 27.0; aluminium.
9. (246.4 − 120.4) ÷ 18.0 = 7
10. 28.0 ÷ 80.0 × 100 = 35.0%
11. Exactly 12.
12. 60.0 g mol⁻¹
