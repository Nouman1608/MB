Recall notes for **4.4.1 Atoms and isotopes** (4.4.1.1 to 4.4.1.3) in AQA GCSE Physics (8463). Paper 1, both
tiers.

Worked examples and the reasoning behind the atomic model are in the [atoms and isotopes study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-atoms-and-isotopes-aqa/).
Practise with the [atoms and isotopes practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-atoms-and-isotopes-aqa-practice/).

## Numbers to know

| Fact | Value |
|---|---|
| Radius of an atom | About 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m |
| Radius of a nucleus | Less than 1/10 000 of the atom's radius |
| Where the mass is | Mostly in the nucleus |
| Neutron discovery | About 20 years after the nucleus was accepted |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Positively charged centre made of protons and neutrons |
| Energy level | A specific distance from the nucleus at which electrons are found |
| Atomic number | Number of protons |
| Mass number | Number of protons + neutrons |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons |
| Positive ion | Atom that has lost one or more outer electrons |
| Plum pudding model | Ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it |
| Nuclear model | Tiny, dense, positive nucleus with electrons around it; mostly empty space |

## Structure facts

- Positive nucleus (protons and neutrons) surrounded by negative electrons.
- Neutral atom: number of electrons = number of protons, so no overall charge.
- Every atom of an element has the same number of protons.
- Absorb electromagnetic radiation → electron moves **further out** (higher level).
- Emit electromagnetic radiation → electron moves **closer in** (lower level).

## Nuclear notation

- Mass number top left, atomic number bottom left of the symbol.
- Neutrons = mass number − atomic number.
- Isotopes: **same bottom number, different top number**.

| Atom | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ²³₁₁Na | 11 | 12 | 11 |
| ³⁵₁₇Cl | 17 | 18 | 17 |
| ³⁷₁₇Cl | 17 | 20 | 17 |
| ²³⁸₉₂U | 92 | 146 | 92 |

The two chlorine atoms are isotopes.

## Ions

- Lose electrons → positive ion. Protons unchanged, so still the same element.
- Example: sodium atom (11 protons) loses one electron → 10 electrons, charge +1.

## Scattering observations decoded

| Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Most alpha particles went straight through | Atom mostly empty space |
| Some deflected | Charged centre repels them |
| A very few bounced back | Tiny, dense, concentrated mass and charge |

## Plum pudding versus nuclear model

| | Plum pudding | Nuclear |
|---|---|---|
| Positive charge | Spread through the atom | Concentrated in the nucleus |
| Mass | Spread evenly | Concentrated in the nucleus |
| Empty space | None | Mostly empty space |

## Standard form toolkit

The specification expects you to recognise sizes given in standard form.

- 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m = 0.000 000 000 1 m (the atom).
- Dividing: (a × 10ᵐ) ÷ (b × 10ⁿ) = (a ÷ b) × 10ᵐ⁻ⁿ.
- Example: atom radius 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m ÷ nucleus radius 2 × 10⁻¹⁵ m = 0.5 × 10⁵ = **5 × 10⁴**, so the nucleus radius
  is 1/50 000 of the atom's.
- "Less than 1/10 000" means the ratio of atom radius to nucleus radius is **more than** 10 000.

## Why the history matters

The development of the atomic model is the specification's example of how scientific methods and theories change
over time. Each step followed new evidence:

| New evidence | Change to the model |
|---|---|
| Before any of this | Atoms thought to be tiny spheres that could not be divided |
| Discovery of the electron | Indivisible sphere → plum pudding |
| Alpha particle scattering | Plum pudding → nuclear model |
| Bohr's theoretical calculations agreeing with experimental observations | Niels Bohr: electrons orbit at specific distances (energy levels) |
| Later experiments on nuclear charge | Positive charge is a whole number of identical particles: protons |
| James Chadwick's experimental work | Neutrons in the nucleus, about 20 years after the nucleus was accepted |

## Exam traps

- **Size trap:** the nucleus is **less than** 1/10 000 of the atom's radius.
- **Notation trap:** reading the top number as protons.
- **Isotope trap:** isotopes differ in neutrons, never protons.
- **Ion trap:** positive ions **lose electrons**; they do not gain protons.
- **Energy-level trap:** absorbing radiation moves electrons **away from** the nucleus.
- **History trap:** the plum pudding model has no nucleus; Chadwick discovered the neutron, not the proton.

## Self-test

1. State the approximate radius of an atom.
2. Where is most of the mass of an atom?
3. What happens to an electron when it emits electromagnetic radiation?
4. Define atomic number.
5. Define mass number.
6. How many neutrons are in ²⁷₁₃Al?
7. Define isotopes.
8. ⁶₃Li and ⁷₃Li: what is the same and what is different?
9. A calcium atom (20 protons) loses two electrons. State the number of electrons and the charge.
10. Describe the plum pudding model.
11. Which observation in the scattering experiment showed the atom is mostly empty space?
12. Whose work provided evidence for the neutron?

### Answers

1. About 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m.
2. In the nucleus.
3. It moves closer to the nucleus, to a lower energy level.
4. The number of protons in an atom.
5. The total number of protons and neutrons in an atom.
6. 27 − 13 = **14**.
7. Atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons.
8. Same number of protons (3) and electrons; different numbers of neutrons (3 and 4) and mass numbers.
9. **18** electrons; charge **+2**.
10. A ball of positive charge with negative electrons embedded in it.
11. Most alpha particles passed straight through the foil.
12. James Chadwick.
