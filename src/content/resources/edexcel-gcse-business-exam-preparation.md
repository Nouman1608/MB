Pearson Edexcel GCSE Business (1BS0) is not tiered and is assessed in a single May/June series through two externally assessed written papers: Theme 1, Investigating small business (1BS0/01), and Theme 2, Building a business (1BS0/02). Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 90 marks and carries 50% of the qualification. Each paper has three sections (A 35 marks, B 30 marks, C 25 marks) containing calculations, multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions, and calculators may be used. Two good places to practise are the notes on [business calculations](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-business-calculations-revision-notes/) and on [cash flow and sources of finance for a small business](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-cash-flow-and-sources-of-finance-for-a-small-business-revision-notes/).

## Theme 1 and Theme 2: two equal papers

The two papers are identical in length, marks and layout, so your approach to the paper itself can be the same for both. What differs is the content: Theme 1 is about investigating small business, and Theme 2 is about building a business. The specification lists ten topics, from enterprise and entrepreneurship through to making human resource decisions, and each belongs to one of the two themes. Before you start timed practice, write a one-page list showing which theme each topic sits in, using the specification, so that you revise evenly and do not assume one paper will repeat the other.

Because both papers are worth exactly 90 marks, neither can be treated as the lesser one. A weak performance on one theme cannot easily be rescued by the other, since each contributes half the qualification.

## Working through Sections A, B and C

The three sections add up to 35 + 30 + 25 = 90 marks. The record describes the section content as calculations, multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing questions, though it does not break down which type appears in which section, so read the front of each paper to see how it is laid out.

- **Section A (35 marks)**: the largest section, so a brisk start matters. Use a steady pace on short questions and do not sink time into one calculation.
- **Section B (30 marks)**: take the time to read any case material fully before answering and refer to it in your answers.
- **Section C (25 marks)**: the smallest section, but extended-writing questions appear on the paper, so keep enough minutes in hand to plan and finish a proper conclusion rather than rushing.

Whichever order you answer in, keep one eye on the clock. Marks in Section A are as valuable as marks in Section C, and a missed short question costs the same as a missed line of an essay.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Each paper: 105 minutes for 90 marks is 105 / 90 = 1.17 minutes per mark (about 1 minute 10 seconds).
- Section A: 35 x 1.1667 = 40.8 minutes.
- Section B: 30 x 1.1667 = 35.0 minutes.
- Section C: 25 x 1.1667 = 29.2 minutes.
- Check: 40.8 + 35.0 + 29.2 = 105 minutes.

In practice, aim to finish each section a few minutes early so you have a reserve for checking. A 6-mark question should therefore take about 6 x 1.17 = 7 minutes, and a 2-mark question a little over 2 minutes.

## Question styles and command words

General guidance for Business papers:

- **Calculate**: write the formula, substitute, and give the answer with the correct unit (for example, a percentage or a currency amount). Where the question asks for rounding, follow it.
- **State** or **identify**: a brief answer, no explanation.
- **Explain**: a point plus a reason or effect.
- **Analyse**: a chain of reasoning, such as higher costs leading to lower profit margin leading to a need for a response.
- **Evaluate** or **justify**: weigh the options, use the context and end with a judgement.
- **Multiple-choice**: read all options before choosing, and eliminate those that are clearly wrong.

## Worked practice scenario: a two-month cash flow

Question: "A small online gift shop starts May with 2,000 in the bank. In May it expects cash inflows of 9,500 and cash outflows of 11,200. In June it expects inflows of 10,400 and outflows of 9,800. Calculate the net cash flow and closing balance for each month, and state one way the owner could respond to the May result."

```
May:   Net cash flow = 9,500 - 11,200 = -1,700
       Closing balance = 2,000 + (-1,700) = 300
June:  Opening balance = 300
       Net cash flow = 10,400 - 9,800 = +600
       Closing balance = 300 + 600 = 900
```

Response: the closing balance in May is still positive (300), but it is very low, so a small delay in customer payments could push the business into overdraft. The owner could arrange an overdraft facility in advance, ask suppliers for longer payment terms, or delay a non-essential purchase. Note that a positive balance does not mean the business is profitable, because cash flow measures timing of money in and out, not profit.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Know which theme you are sitting and the matching topics.
- Take a calculator, pens and a watch, and check the permitted equipment.
- Mark the time you should finish Sections A, B and C on the front of the paper.
- Read each calculation question twice before using the calculator.
- Write formulae and units, even for short calculations.
- Quote the business context in written answers.
- Leave time to write a conclusion where the command word is evaluate or justify.
- Do not leave multiple-choice questions blank.

## Self-test

1. How many marks does each section carry, and what do they add up to?
2. What does the record say about the series in which GCSE Business is assessed?
3. Work out the closing balance if opening cash is 1,500, inflows are 6,200 and outflows are 5,900.
4. Roughly how many minutes should 25 marks take?
5. Why is a positive cash balance not the same as profit?

**Answers:** 1. Section A 35, Section B 30, Section C 25, giving 90 marks. 2. All assessment is in a single May/June series. 3. Net cash flow is 6,200 - 5,900 = +300, so the closing balance is 1,500 + 300 = 1,800. 4. About 25 x 1.1667 = 29.2 minutes. 5. Cash flow records when money moves in and out, whereas profit compares revenue with costs over a period.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel GCSE Business 1BS0?**
There are two externally assessed written papers, Theme 1 (1BS0/01) and Theme 2 (1BS0/02), and the qualification is not tiered.

**How long is each paper and how many marks is it worth?**
Each paper lasts 1 hour 45 minutes and is worth 90 marks, which is 50% of the qualification.

**How is each paper laid out?**
Each has three sections: Section A is 35 marks, Section B is 30 marks and Section C is 25 marks. Questions include calculations, multiple-choice, short-answer and extended-writing.

**When can I sit the exams?**
All assessment is in a single May/June series, and the qualification is linear. First assessment was in 2019.

**Can I use a calculator?**
Yes, calculators may be used on both papers, so practise numerical work with the calculator you will use in the exam.
