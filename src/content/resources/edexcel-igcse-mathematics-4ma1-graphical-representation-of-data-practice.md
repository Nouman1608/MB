## Section A

**1.** In a pictogram, one symbol represents 12 emails.

**(a)** Monday is shown by 2½ symbols. How many emails is this? **[1]**

**(b)** On Tuesday there were 39 emails. How many symbols should be drawn? **[1]**

**2.** 45 students are surveyed and the results are shown in a pie chart. 13 students chose drama. Work out the angle of the drama sector. **[2]**

**3.** In a pie chart, the sector for "walk" has an angle of 72° and represents 18 students. Work out the total number of students in the survey. **[2]**

**4.** In a pie chart showing the choices of 540 students, the French sector is 110° and the German sector is 80°. How many more students chose French than German? **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the classes "0–10, 10–20, 20–30" are not suitable for grouping times measured in minutes, and write the first two classes in a better way. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** School A has 200 students and school B has 600 students. In a pie chart for each school, the sector for students who cycle to school is 90° for A and 45° for B.

Jamal says, "More students cycle to school A than to school B." Is Jamal correct? Show working to support your answer. **[3]**

**7.** 60 students travel to school by bus, car or on foot. 28 of them are girls. 15 students walk, and 9 of these are girls. 12 boys come by car. 20 students come by bus.

**(a)** Draw and complete a two-way table to show this information. **[3]**

**(b)** How many girls come by car? **[1]**

**8.** The times, in minutes, that 20 people waited at a clinic were:

12, 7, 18, 25, 31, 9, 14, 22, 27, 16, 3, 19, 28, 11, 35, 20, 24, 8, 15, 30

**(a)** Complete a grouped frequency table using the classes 0 < t ≤ 10, 10 < t ≤ 20, 20 < t ≤ 30 and 30 < t ≤ 40. **[2]**

**(b)** State which class the value 20 belongs to, and explain how the inequalities show this. **[1]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** The table shows the masses, m kg, of 68 parcels.

| Mass m (kg) | 0 < m ≤ 5 | 5 < m ≤ 10 | 10 < m ≤ 20 | 20 < m ≤ 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 10 | 18 | 24 | 16 |

Work out the frequency density for each class, ready to draw a histogram. **[3]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** A histogram for a continuous variable x has these bars:

| x | 0 < x ≤ 10 | 10 < x ≤ 15 | 15 < x ≤ 20 | 20 < x ≤ 30 | 30 < x ≤ 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency density | 1.2 | 4 | 5.6 | 2.1 | 0.45 |

**(a)** Work out the total frequency. **[3]**

**(b)** Estimate the number of values between 25 and 40. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** The table shows the distances, d km, travelled by 100 people.

| Distance d (km) | 0 < d ≤ 10 | 10 < d ≤ 20 | 20 < d ≤ 30 | 30 < d ≤ 40 | 40 < d ≤ 50 | 50 < d ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 14 | 22 | 30 | 16 | 10 |

**(a)** Work out the cumulative frequencies. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the points to plot for the cumulative frequency diagram. **[1]**

**(c)** Joining the points with straight lines, estimate the number of people who travelled more than 35 km. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 2½ × 12 = **30** [1].

**(b)** 39 ÷ 12 = **3¼ symbols** [1].

**2.** 360 ÷ 45 = 8° per student [1]; 13 × 8 = **104°** [1].

**3.** 72 ÷ 18 = 4° per student [1]; 360 ÷ 4 = **90 students** [1].

**4.** 360° represents 540, so 1° represents 1.5 students [1]. Difference = 30° × 1.5 = **45 students** [1].

**5.** A value on a boundary, such as 10, would fit in two classes [1]. Better: 0 < t ≤ 10, 10 < t ≤ 20 [1].

**6.** School A: 90/360 × 200 = 50 [1]. School B: 45/360 × 600 = 75 [1]. Jamal is **not correct**: more students cycle to school B; A has a larger proportion, not a larger number [1].

**7. (a)** All six unknown cells correct [3] (2 marks for four or five correct; 1 mark for two or three correct).

| | Bus | Car | Walk | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boys | 14 | 12 | 6 | 32 |
| Girls | 6 | 13 | 9 | 28 |
| Total | 20 | 25 | 15 | 60 |

**(b)** **13** [1].

**8. (a)** Frequencies 4, 8, 6, 2 [2] (1 mark for three correct). Check: 4 + 8 + 6 + 2 = 20.

**(b)** **10 < t ≤ 20** [1]: the ≤ sign means 20 is included in this class, and the < sign in 20 < t ≤ 30 means 20 is not in the next one.

**9.** Class widths 5, 5, 10, 20 [1]. Frequency densities **2, 3.6, 2.4, 0.8** [2] (1 mark for two or three correct).

**10. (a)** Frequencies = density × width: 1.2 × 10 = 12, 4 × 5 = 20, 5.6 × 5 = 28, 2.1 × 10 = 21, 0.45 × 20 = 9 [2] (1 mark for three correct). Total = **90** [1].

**(b)** 25 to 30: 5 × 2.1 = 10.5; 30 to 40: 10 × 0.45 = 4.5 [1]. Estimate = **15** [1].

**11. (a)** **8, 22, 44, 74, 90, 100** [1].

**(b)** **(0, 0), (10, 8), (20, 22), (30, 44), (40, 74), (50, 90), (60, 100)** [1].

**(c)** At 35 km the cumulative frequency is about 44 + ½ × 30 = 59 [1]; more than 35 km: 100 − 59 = **41** (accept 39 to 43 from a curve) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 4: forgetting that 1° is 1.5 students, not 1 student, when the total is 540.
- Question 6: comparing the angles directly. A pie chart shows proportions; numbers need the totals.
- Question 8: putting 20 or 30 in the higher class.
- Question 10(a): adding the frequency densities instead of the frequencies.
- Question 11(b): plotting at 5, 15, 25, ... (the midpoints) instead of the upper boundaries.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do histograms with unequal class widths use frequency density?**
Because the area of each bar must represent the frequency. Dividing by the class width stops wide classes from looking bigger than they are.

**Can you compare two pie charts directly?**
Only as proportions. To compare numbers, convert each sector to a frequency using that chart's own total.
