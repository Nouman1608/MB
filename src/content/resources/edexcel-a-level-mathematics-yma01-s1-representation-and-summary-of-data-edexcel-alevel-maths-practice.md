## Section A

**1.** Eleven ordered values are 14, 17, 19, 22, 23, 23, 26, 29, 31, 35, 40. Find the median and the lower and upper quartiles. **[3]**

**2.** On a histogram, the class 12–20 has frequency 36. Find its frequency density. The bar for the class 20–24 has frequency density 6.5. Find the frequency of that class. **[2]**

**3.** For 12 values, Σx = 132 and Σx² = 1610. Find the mean and the standard deviation. **[3]**

**4.** Data are coded using y = (x − 60)/3. The coded data have mean −1.5 and standard deviation 2.2. Find the mean and standard deviation of x. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** The heights, h cm, of 80 students are summarised below.

| h | 140–150 | 150–155 | 155–160 | 160–170 | 170–190 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 8 | 14 | 22 | 30 | 6 |

On a histogram, the bar for 150–155 is 2 cm wide and 7 cm tall.

**(a)** Find the width and height of the bar for 160–170. **[4]**

**(b)** Use interpolation to estimate the median and the interquartile range. **[5]**

**(c)** Estimate the number of students taller than 165 cm. **[2]**

**6.** Use the data in Question 5, with Σfx² = 2 050 675 where x is the class midpoint.

**(a)** Estimate the mean and standard deviation of the heights. **[4]**

**(b)** One measure of skewness is 3(mean − median)/standard deviation. Evaluate it and describe the skewness. **[2]**

**7.** The weights, x grams, of 16 loaves are coded by y = (x − 3000)/50, giving Σy = −12 and Σy² = 160. Find the mean and standard deviation of x. **[5]**

**8.** The times, in seconds, taken by two machines to complete a cycle are shown.

```
            Machine P | stem | Machine Q
                      |  3   | 0 6 9
            8 7 5 4 1 |  4   | 2 6 9
          7 5 4 2 1 0 |  5   | 3 6
                      |  6   | 0 3 6
                    4 |  7   | 0
Key: 1 | 4 | 2 means 41 s for P and 42 s for Q
```

**(a)** Find the median and quartiles for each machine. **[4]**

**(b)** An outlier is a value more than 1.5 × IQR above Q3 or below Q1. Show that Machine P has exactly one outlier and that Machine Q has none. **[3]**

**(c)** Compare the two machines. **[2]**

**9.** Group A has 8 values with mean 20 and standard deviation 4. Group B has 12 values with mean 25 and standard deviation 5. Find the mean and standard deviation of all 20 values combined. **[5]**

**10.** The mean of six numbers is 9. Five of them are 4, 7, 9, 11 and 13.

**(a)** Find the sixth number. **[2]**

**(b)** A seventh number equal to 9 is added. State, with a reason, the effect on the standard deviation. **[2]**

**11.** Using the data in Question 5, estimate the 10% to 90% interpercentile range of the heights. **[3]**

**12.** A box plot shows minimum 2, lower quartile 8, median 11, upper quartile 20 and maximum 31.

**(a)** Describe the skewness of the distribution, giving a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** State which measures of location and spread would be most suitable, with a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** n/4 = 2.75, so Q1 is the 3rd value: **Q1 = 19** [1]. Median is the 6th value: **23** [1]. 3n/4 = 8.25, so Q3 is the 9th value: **Q3 = 31** [1].

**2.** 36 ÷ 8 = **4.5** [1]. Frequency = 6.5 × 4 = **26** [1].

**3.** Mean = 132/12 = **11** [1]. Variance = 1610/12 − 11² = 13.17 [1]. Standard deviation = **3.63** (3 s.f.) [1].

**4.** x̄ = 3(−1.5) + 60 = **55.5** [1]. sd(x) = 3 × 2.2 = **6.6** [1].

**5. (a)** Width: 5 cm of height data → 2 cm, so 0.4 cm per cm; the class 160–170 is **4 cm** wide [1] [1]. Height: fd(150–155) = 14/5 = 2.8 → 7 cm, so 2.5 cm per unit [1]; fd(160–170) = 3, so the bar is **7.5 cm** tall [1].

**(b)** Cumulative frequencies: 8, 22, 44, 74, 80 [1]. Median (40th): 155 + (40 − 22)/22 × 5 = **159.1 cm** [1]. Q1 (20th): 150 + (20 − 8)/14 × 5 = 154.3 [1]. Q3 (60th): 160 + (60 − 44)/30 × 10 = 165.3 [1]. **IQR = 11.0 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(c)** Half of the 160–170 class lies above 165: (5/10) × 30 = 15 [1]; plus 6 above 170 gives **21 students** [1].

**6. (a)** Midpoints 145, 152.5, 157.5, 165, 180; Σfx = 12 790 [1], so mean = **159.9 cm** (159.875) [1]. Variance = 2 050 675/80 − 159.875² = 73.42 [1], so standard deviation = **8.57 cm** [1].

**(b)** 3(159.875 − 159.09)/8.569 = **0.275** [1]. It is positive, so there is slight **positive skew** [1].

**7.** ȳ = −12/16 = −0.75 [1]. var(y) = 160/16 − 0.75² = 9.4375 [1], so sd(y) = 3.072 [1]. x̄ = 50(−0.75) + 3000 = **2962.5 g** [1]. sd(x) = 50 × 3.072 = **154 g** (3 s.f.) [1].

**8. (a)** n = 12 for each, so Q1 is the mean of the 3rd and 4th values, the median the mean of the 6th and 7th, and Q3 the mean of the 9th and 10th [1]. P: values 41, 44, 45, 47, 48, 50, 51, 52, 54, 55, 57, 74, so **Q1 = 46, median = 50.5, Q3 = 54.5** [1]. Q: values 30, 36, 39, 42, 46, 49, 53, 56, 60, 63, 66, 70 [1], so **Q1 = 40.5, median = 51, Q3 = 61.5** [1].

**(b)** P: IQR = 8.5, limits 46 − 12.75 = 33.25 and 54.5 + 12.75 = 67.25 [1]; only 74 lies outside, so P has exactly one outlier [1]. Q: IQR = 21, limits 40.5 − 31.5 = 9 and 61.5 + 31.5 = 93; all values lie within them, so Q has none [1].

**(c)** The medians are similar (50.5 s and 51 s), so typical cycle times are about the same [1]. Q's IQR (21 s) is much larger than P's (8.5 s), so P is more consistent, apart from its one outlier [1].

**9.** Group A: Σx = 160, Σx² = 8(4² + 20²) = 3328 [1]. Group B: Σx = 300, Σx² = 12(5² + 25²) = 7800 [1]. Combined mean = 460/20 = **23** [1]. Variance = 11 128/20 − 23² = 27.4 [1]. Standard deviation = **5.23** (3 s.f.) [1].

**10. (a)** Total = 6 × 9 = 54 [1]; sixth number = 54 − 44 = **10** [1].

**(b)** The standard deviation **decreases** [1], because the new value equals the mean, so the sum of squared deviations is unchanged while it is divided by a larger n [1].

**11.** P10 is the 8th value: 150 + (8 − 8)/14 × 5 = 150 [1]. P90 is the 72nd value: 160 + (72 − 44)/30 × 10 = 169.3 [1]. Interpercentile range = **19.3 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

**12. (a)** Q3 − Q2 = 9 and Q2 − Q1 = 3 [1]; since Q3 − Q2 > Q2 − Q1, the distribution has **positive skew** [1].

**(b)** Median and interquartile range [1], because they are not distorted by the skew or by extreme values [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one scale factor for both width and height of a histogram bar.
- Using the ungrouped quartile rule on grouped data, or the reverse.
- Rounding the mean before finding the variance in Questions 3, 6 and 9.
- Averaging two standard deviations in Question 9 instead of rebuilding Σx and Σx².
- Giving a comparison in Question 8 without context or without a comment on spread.
