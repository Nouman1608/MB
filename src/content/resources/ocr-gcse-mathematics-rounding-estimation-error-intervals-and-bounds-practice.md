These questions follow section 4.01 of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): rounding (4.01a), estimation (4.01b) and error intervals and bounds (4.01c). Questions 1 to 6 and 12 are Foundation and Higher content. Questions 7 to 11 are **(Higher tier only)**. ## Questions

**1.** Round each number as stated.

**(a)** 5482 to the nearest 100. **[1]**
**(b)** 0.04068 to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** 12.996 to 2 decimal places. **[1]**

**2.** A ribbon 9.4 m long is cut into 7 equal pieces. Work out the length of one piece. Give your answer to a suitable degree of accuracy. **[2]**

**3.** Do not use a calculator.

**(a)** Estimate the cost of 4.1 kg of grapes at £5.95 per kg. **[2]**
**(b)** Estimate the value of (5.82 × 39.6) ÷ √80.9. Show the rounded values you use. **[3]**

**4.** Write down an error interval for each value.

**(a)** a = 8.4, rounded to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(b)** b = 7600, rounded to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** c = 0.9, truncated to 1 decimal place. **[1]**
**(d)** d = 15 000, rounded to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**5.** A speaker is 60 cm wide, to the nearest centimetre.

**(a)** A shelf gap is 61 cm wide, to the nearest centimetre. Can you be sure the speaker fits in the gap? Show your reasoning. **[3]**
**(b)** A second gap is 60 cm wide, to the nearest centimetre. Can you be sure the speaker fits? Explain. **[2]**

**6.** A timer shows an athlete's time as 4.7 seconds, truncated to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Write an error interval for her time t. **[1]**
**(b)** A second athlete's time is shown as 4.8 seconds, truncated to 1 decimal place. Can you be sure the second athlete was slower? Explain. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) A floor is 8.4 m by 5.7 m, with each length given to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Calculate the upper bound of the area. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the lower bound of the area. **[2]**
**(c)** Write the area to a suitable degree of accuracy, justifying your answer. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) A = 15.8 and B = 6.4, each to 1 decimal place.

**(a)** Find the upper bound of A − B. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the lower bound of A ÷ B, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** A fair has 1200 visitors to the nearest hundred. Write the possible range for the number of visitors n. **[2]**
**(b)** A parcel has mass 1200 g to the nearest 100 g. Write the possible range for the mass m. **[2]**
**(c)** Each visitor paid exactly £8. Find the greatest and least possible total takings. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A cyclist rides 840 m, to the nearest 10 m, in 105 seconds, to the nearest second. Find the upper and lower bounds of her average speed in m/s, then give the speed to a suitable degree of accuracy. **[6]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A metal block measures 4.2 cm by 3.5 cm by 2.0 cm, each to 1 decimal place. Its mass is 118 g to the nearest gram.

**(a)** Find the upper and lower bounds of the volume. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the upper and lower bounds of the density (mass ÷ volume). **[3]**
**(c)** Give the density to a suitable degree of accuracy, with a reason. **[2]**

**12.** Mia buys 19 tiles at £3.85 each and 2.4 kg of adhesive at £6.15 per kg.

**(a)** Estimate her total bill by rounding each number to 1 significant figure. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out the exact bill. Was your estimate an overestimate or an underestimate? **[2]**
**(c)** Her bank shows the bill as £88 to the nearest pound. Write an error interval for the bill B, and say whether £87.91 is consistent with it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) 5500 [1]. (b) The first significant figure is 4. Two significant figures gives 0.040, then the next digit 6 rounds up: **0.041** [1]. (c) 12.99|6 rounds up through the 9s: **13.00** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the final zeros in 13.00 show the stated accuracy, so keep them.

**2.** 9.4 ÷ 7 = 1.342857... [1]. **1.34 m** (3 significant figures, matching the data) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* quoting every digit of the display claims more accuracy than the measurements support.

**3.** (a) 4 × 6 [1] = **£24** [1]. **[2]**
(b) 5.82 → 6, 39.6 → 40, √80.9 → √81 = 9 [1]. 6 × 40 = 240 [1]. 240 ÷ 9 ≈ **27** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* write the rounded values down. A bare number gives the reader nothing to follow, so show each rounding.

**4.** (a) **8.35 ≤ a < 8.45** [1]. (b) The unit is 100, half is 50: **7550 ≤ b < 7650** [1]. (c) Truncated, so the unit is a whole 0.1: **0.9 ≤ c < 1.0** [1]. (d) The unit is 1000, half is 500: **14 500 ≤ d < 15 500** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the lower end uses ≤ and the upper end uses <. Writing ≤ at both ends is a common slip.

**5.** (a) Speaker: 59.5 ≤ w < 60.5 [1]. Gap: 60.5 ≤ g < 61.5 [1]. The speaker is always narrower than 60.5 and the gap is at least 60.5, so **yes, it fits** [1]. **[3]**
(b) The speaker could be up to just under 60.5 while the gap could be as small as 59.5 [1]. **No, you cannot be sure** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* compare the largest possible object with the smallest possible space, not the stated sizes.

**6.** (a) **4.7 ≤ t < 4.8** [1]. (b) The second time is at least 4.8 and the first is below 4.8 [1], so **yes, the second was slower** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name both intervals before you decide. A truncated interval starts at the stated value.

**7.** (a) Upper limits 8.45 and 5.75 [1]. 8.45 × 5.75 = **48.5875 m²** [1]. **[2]**
(b) Lower limits 8.35 and 5.65 [1]. 8.35 × 5.65 = **47.1775 m²** [1]. **[2]**
(c) To 2 significant figures the bounds are 49 and 47, which differ, but to 1 significant figure both are 50 [1]. Area = **50 m² (1 significant figure)** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* justify the accuracy by comparing the rounded bounds. Quoting 48 m² with no reason leaves the choice unsupported.

**8.** (a) Upper limit of A is 15.85 and lower limit of B is 6.35 [1]. 15.85 − 6.35 = **9.5** [1]. **[2]**
(b) Lower limit of A is 15.75 [1]. Upper limit of B is 6.45 [1]. 15.75 ÷ 6.45 = 2.4418... = **2.44** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* for a difference or a quotient, one value takes its upper limit and the other its lower. State which you use.

**9.** (a) Counted, so whole numbers only: **1150 ≤ n ≤ 1249** [2]. **[2]**
(b) Measured, so any value is possible: **1150 ≤ m < 1250** [2]. **[2]**
(c) Least: 1150 × 8 = **£9200** [1]. Greatest: 1249 × 8 = **£9992** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* for a count, the greatest possible value is 1249. Using 1250 or 1249.9 does not describe a number of people.

**10.** Distance: 835 ≤ d < 845. Time: 104.5 ≤ t < 105.5 [1]. Upper bound of speed = 845 ÷ 104.5 [1] = 8.086... m/s. Lower bound of speed = 835 ÷ 105.5 [1] = 7.914... m/s [1]. Both round to 8 to 1 significant figure [1], so speed = **8 m/s (1 significant figure)** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the largest speed combines the largest distance with the smallest time. Pairing the wrong limits gives the wrong bound.

**11.** (a) Limits: 4.15 to 4.25, 3.45 to 3.55, 1.95 to 2.05 [1]. Upper bound = 4.25 × 3.55 × 2.05 = **30.93 cm³** [1]. Lower bound = 4.15 × 3.45 × 1.95 = **27.92 cm³** [1]. **[3]**
(b) Mass limits are 117.5 and 118.5 [1]. Upper bound = 118.5 ÷ 27.919 = **4.24 g/cm³** [1]. Lower bound = 117.5 ÷ 30.929 = **3.80 g/cm³** [1]. **[3]**
(c) Both bounds round to 4 to 1 significant figure, but not to 2 [1]. Density = **4 g/cm³** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* keep the unrounded bounds for the next step, and round only when you decide the final accuracy.

**12.** (a) 19 → 20, 3.85 → 4, 2.4 → 2, 6.15 → 6 [1]. 20 × 4 = 80 and 2 × 6 = 12 [1]. **≈ £92** [1]. **[3]**
(b) 19 × 3.85 = 73.15, 2.4 × 6.15 = 14.76, so the bill is **£87.91** [1]. The estimate is bigger, so it is an **overestimate** [1]. **[2]**
(c) **87.5 ≤ B < 88.5** [1]. £87.91 lies inside this interval, so it is consistent [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* give the direction of the error with a reason. Saying "close" is not enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping a final zero when rounding to a stated number of decimal places or significant figures.
- Writing ≤ at both ends of a rounded interval for a measured quantity.
- Adding half a unit to a truncated value instead of a whole unit.
- Comparing the stated sizes in a fit question instead of the worst cases.
- Using the lower bound of the denominator when you need the upper bound of a quotient.
- Rounding bounds before using them in the next stage of a calculation.
- Quoting an answer to more figures than both bounds share, with no justification.
- Giving 250 or 249.9 as the greatest value of a counted quantity.
- Showing an estimate with no rounded values written down.
