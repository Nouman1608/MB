## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a *heterogeneous catalyst* and give one example from the 9701
syllabus. **[2]**

**2.** Which is an example of homogeneous catalysis?

A: iron in the Haber process

B: platinum in a catalytic converter

C: nitrogen dioxide in the oxidation of atmospheric sulfur dioxide

D: nickel in the hydrogenation of an alkene gas

**[1]**

**3.** Which quantity is changed when a catalyst is added to a reaction?

A: the enthalpy change

B: the equilibrium constant

C: the activation energy

D: the products formed

**[1]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by saying that a homogeneous catalyst is "used in one step and
reformed in a later step". **[2]**

**5.** Explain why heterogeneous catalysts are used in a form with a large surface area. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Describe how iron catalyses the reaction between nitrogen and hydrogen in the Haber
process. **[4]**

**7.** A catalytic converter removes oxides of nitrogen from car exhaust gases.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of NO₂ with CO in the converter. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the mode of action of the catalyst in removing NO. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the catalyst metals are spread as a thin layer over a ceramic honeycomb.
**[1]**

**8.** Nitrogen dioxide in the atmosphere catalyses the oxidation of sulfur dioxide.

**(a)** Write equations for the two steps. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the catalyst and the intermediate, and explain your choices. **[2]**

**(c)** Write an equation showing how the product of this reaction contributes to acid rain.
**[1]**

**9.** Peroxodisulfate ions oxidise iodide ions slowly: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂.

**(a)** Explain why the uncatalysed reaction is slow. **[2]**

**(b)** Write equations for the two steps when Fe²⁺ ions catalyse the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why adding Fe³⁺ ions instead of Fe²⁺ ions also catalyses the reaction. **[1]**

**10.** Two hypothetical ion pairs have these standard electrode potentials: X³⁺/X²⁺ = +1.10 V
and Y³⁺/Y²⁺ = +0.40 V. E⦵(I₂/I⁻) = +0.54 V and E⦵(S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻) = +2.01 V. Deduce which pair
could catalyse the reaction in Question 9, showing the E⦵<sub>cell</sub> values for each step.
**[3]**

**11.** Describe how the energy profile for the Fe²⁺-catalysed reaction in Question 9 differs
from the profile for the uncatalysed reaction. **[3]**

**12.** Classify each catalyst as homogeneous or heterogeneous.

**(a)** H⁺(aq) in the reaction of propanone with iodine in aqueous solution **[1]**

**(b)** Mn²⁺(aq) formed during the reaction of acidified MnO₄⁻(aq) with C₂O₄²⁻(aq) **[1]**

**(c)** solid rhodium in the reduction of NO(g) **[1]**

**(d)** NO₂(g) in the oxidation of SO₂(g) **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A catalyst in a different phase from the reactants [1]. Example: iron (solid) in the Haber
process, or Pt/Pd/Rh in a catalytic converter [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. NO₂ is a gas, like SO₂ and O₂.

**3.** **C** [1]. A catalyst provides an alternative route with lower activation energy.

**4.** The catalyst reacts with a reactant in the first step, forming an intermediate [1]. The
intermediate reacts in a later step that regenerates the catalyst, so it is not used up overall
and does not appear in the overall equation [1].

**5.** Reactions occur only at active sites on the surface, so a larger surface area gives more
sites and a faster rate [1].

**6.** N₂ and H₂ molecules are adsorbed onto active sites on the iron surface [1]. Bonds within
the adsorbed molecules, especially the strong N≡N bond, are weakened [1], so the adsorbed atoms
react together to form NH₃ with a lower activation energy [1]. Ammonia molecules desorb, freeing
the active sites [1].

**7. (a)** 4CO + 2NO₂ → 4CO₂ + N₂ [1].

**(b)** NO and CO are adsorbed onto the metal surface [1]. The N–O bond is weakened, allowing
nitrogen atoms to form N₂ and oxygen to be transferred to CO, forming CO₂ [1]. N₂ and CO₂ desorb
from the surface [1].

**(c)** It gives a very large surface area of catalyst (more active sites) using only a small
mass of expensive metal [1].

**8. (a)** SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO [1]; NO + ½O₂ → NO₂ [1].

**(b)** Catalyst: NO₂, because it is used in step 1 and reformed in step 2 [1]. Intermediate:
NO, because it is formed in step 1 and used in step 2 [1].

**(c)** SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ [1].

**9. (a)** Both reactants are negative ions [1], so they repel each other and the activation
energy is high [1].

**(b)** S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ [1]; 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ [1].

**(c)** Fe³⁺ oxidises I⁻ first, forming Fe²⁺, which then reduces S₂O₈²⁻ and regenerates Fe³⁺;
the same two steps occur in the opposite order [1].

**10.** X³⁺/X²⁺: S₂O₈²⁻ oxidises X²⁺ (2.01 − 1.10 = +0.91 V) and X³⁺ oxidises I⁻ (1.10 − 0.54 =
+0.56 V); both feasible [1]. Y³⁺/Y²⁺: Y³⁺ cannot oxidise I⁻ (0.40 − 0.54 = −0.14 V) [1]. So only
**X³⁺/X²⁺** could catalyse the reaction [1].

**11.** The catalysed profile has two peaks (two steps) instead of one [1]; each peak is lower
than the single uncatalysed peak, so the activation energy is lower [1]; the reactant and
product energy levels (and so ΔH) are the same, with an intermediate in the dip between the
peaks [1].

**12. (a)** homogeneous [1].

**(b)** homogeneous [1].

**(c)** heterogeneous [1].

**(d)** homogeneous [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "absorb" instead of "adsorb".
- Describing heterogeneous catalysis without all three stages, or without naming bonds weakened.
- Defining homogeneous by comparison with the products rather than the reactants.
- Writing unbalanced Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ steps.
- Naming NO as the catalyst in SO₂ oxidation.
- Checking only one step when deciding whether an ion pair can catalyse a reaction.
