These notes condense section 11.4, The management of change in manufacturing industry, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for 2025 and 2026 (Paper 4, Topic 11 option). For the full case study and model paragraph, see the [11.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-change-in-manufacturing-industry/); then try the [11.4 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-change-in-manufacturing-industry-practice/).

## What the syllabus demands

One country's **industrial policy** for manufacturing, and the **consequent changes** in the **character**, **location** and **organisation** of manufacturing. Show **issues faced** and **evaluate the attempted solutions**.

## The three key words

| Word | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Character | What is made? Labour- or capital-intensive? Low or high value? |
| Location | Where is it made? Which regions gained or lost? |
| Organisation | Who owns and controls it: state, conglomerates, small firms, foreign TNCs? |

## Policy types

- **Import substitution:** protect infant industries with tariffs and quotas to supply the home market.
- **Export orientation:** export zones, tax relief and cheap credit for exporters.
- **Sector targeting:** the state picks strategic industries and backs them.
- **Regional policy:** grants and new complexes to spread industry.
- **Restructuring:** manage decline, retraining, support for new sectors.

## South Korea in three phases

| Phase | Policy | Character | Location | Organisation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1960s | Five-year plans from 1962; export promotion; Masan Free Export Zone (1970) | Textiles, clothing, footwear, wigs, plywood | Seoul and Busan | Private firms; state-directed credit |
| 1970s | Heavy and Chemical Industry drive (1973) | Steel, ships, machinery, chemicals, electronics | South-east coast complexes: Pohang (POSCO steel, 1973), Ulsan (ships, cars, petrochemicals), Changwon (machinery); Gumi (electronics) | Chaebol favoured with loans and licences |
| 1980s on | Research, education, high technology; gradual liberalisation | Semiconductors, displays, cars, ships | Seoul metropolitan region, especially Gyeonggi | Chaebol become global TNCs |

## Issues and responses

- **Debt:** over-borrowed chaebol hit by the 1997 Asian financial crisis; IMF rescue; Daewoo collapsed in 1999. Response: restructuring and tighter bank rules.
- **Wages and labour:** strong unions after democratisation in 1987. Response: automation; labour-intensive work moved abroad (such as phone assembly in Vietnam).
- **Regional imbalance:** concentration on the Seoul–Busan axis. Response: regional complexes, relocation of public bodies, Sejong administrative city.
- **Decline:** shipbuilding downturns in the 2010s; General Motors closed its Gunsan plant in 2018. Response: crisis-area support.
- **Pollution:** heavy industry on the coast. Response: regulation and cleaner technology.

## Evaluation criteria

| Criterion | One-line test | South Korea |
|---|---|---|
| Growth | Did output and exports rise? | Strongly |
| Upgrading | Higher-value products? | Strongly |
| Distribution | Did lagging regions and workers gain? | Partly |
| Resilience | Did firms survive crises? | Survived 1997 after painful restructuring |
| Ownership | Domestic capability? | Strong domestic firms |
| Environment | Pollution controlled? | Improved over time |

**Comparison point:** Malaysia relied more on foreign TNC branch plants; South Korea built domestic conglomerates. Use this to judge "organisation".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Industrial policy | Government action to shape what, where and how industry produces |
| Infant industry | A new domestic industry protected until it can compete |
| Import substitution | Producing at home goods that were previously imported |
| Export orientation | Industrial strategy aimed at selling to world markets |
| Chaebol | Large, family-controlled South Korean business groups |
| Industrial complex | A planned area for linked industries, often state-built |
| Value added | Increase in value at each stage of production |
| Upgrading | Moving into higher-value products and processes |
| Deindustrialisation | Long-term decline in manufacturing's share of jobs or output |
| Restructuring | Reorganising firms or industries after crisis or decline |
| Regional imbalance | Uneven distribution of industry and income between regions |
| Branch plant | A factory controlled by a firm headquartered elsewhere |

## Exam traps

- **Narrative without the three words.** Organise by character, location and organisation.
- **General TNC answers.** The focus is a country's industrial policy.
- **Issues without solutions.** Each issue needs a response and a judgement.
- **Growth only.** Add distribution, resilience and environment.
- **Uncertain detail.** Leave out a figure you cannot recall accurately.
- **Location explained by policy alone.** Use 11.3 factors (ports, labour, agglomeration) as well.

## Linking 11.4 to 11.3

- **Transport:** the 1970s complexes sit on the coast because steel and shipbuilding import bulky materials and export heavy products (break-of-bulk advantage).
- **Agglomeration:** once a cluster forms, as in Gyeonggi for semiconductors, localisation economies hold it in place against regional policy.
- **Labour:** rising wages after 1987 reduced the pull of cheap labour, so labour-intensive work moved abroad.
- **Inertia:** older industrial cities such as Ulsan keep industry despite downturns, because of sunk investment and skills.
- **Government:** policy is decisive where it creates a new site with real location advantages, weaker where it fights them.

## Self-test

1. What three aspects of manufacturing must the 11.4 case study cover?
2. Distinguish import substitution from export orientation.
3. When did South Korea's five-year development plans begin?
4. Name two products of Phase 1.
5. What did the 1973 policy target?
6. Name the steel company and its location.
7. What are chaebol?
8. Where is high-technology production concentrated?
9. What crisis exposed chaebol debt, and which group collapsed?
10. Give two responses to rising wages after 1987.
11. Name one attempt to reduce regional imbalance.
12. Give one criterion on which South Korea's policy scores only partly.

### Answers

1. Character, location, organisation.
2. Import substitution makes goods for the home market behind protection; export orientation makes goods for world markets with export incentives.
3. 1962.
4. Any two: textiles, clothing, footwear, wigs, plywood.
5. Steel, shipbuilding, machinery, electronics, chemicals, non-ferrous metals (heavy and chemical industry).
6. POSCO, at Pohang.
7. Large, family-controlled business groups such as Hyundai, Samsung, LG.
8. The Seoul metropolitan region, especially Gyeonggi province.
9. The 1997 Asian financial crisis; Daewoo (1999).
10. Automation; moving labour-intensive work abroad.
11. Regional industrial complexes, relocation of public bodies or Sejong administrative city.
12. Distribution (regional balance or job security).
