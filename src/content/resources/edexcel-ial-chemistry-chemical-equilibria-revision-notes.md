## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homogeneous equilibrium | All species in the same phase |
| Heterogeneous equilibrium | Species in two or more phases |
| Kc | Equilibrium constant in terms of equilibrium concentrations (mol dm⁻³) |
| Kp | Equilibrium constant in terms of equilibrium partial pressures (atm) |
| Mole fraction, x(A) | Moles of gas A ÷ total moles of gas |
| Partial pressure, p(A) | x(A) × total pressure; the pressure A would exert alone |
| Position of equilibrium | The relative amounts of reactants and products in the mixture |

## Formulae at a glance

| Quantity | Expression |
|---|---|
| Kc for aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD | [C]^c [D]^d / ([A]^a [B]^b) |
| Kp for the same reaction (all gases) | p(C)^c p(D)^d / (p(A)^a p(B)^b) |
| Partial pressure | p(A) = x(A) × P(total) |
| Sum of partial pressures | p(A) + p(B) + … = P(total) |
| Entropy of surroundings | ΔSsurroundings = −ΔH / T |
| Total entropy | ΔStotal = ΔSsystem + ΔSsurroundings |
| Link to K | ΔStotal = R lnK, with R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |

## 13.1 and 13.2: building expressions

**Method in steps**
1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Cross out every solid. For Kp, keep only gases.
3. Products on top, reactants underneath, coefficients as powers.
4. Units: replace each term with mol dm⁻³ (Kc) or atm (Kp) and cancel.

**Quick reminders**
- 2NO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ 2NOCl(g) type: three gas moles go to two, so Kc has units dm³ mol⁻¹ and Kp has units atm⁻¹.
- Equal gas moles on each side: no units.
- Zn(s) + Cu²⁺(aq) ⇌ Zn²⁺(aq) + Cu(s): Kc = [Zn²⁺] / [Cu²⁺], no units.
- MgCO₃(s) ⇌ MgO(s) + CO₂(g): Kp = p(CO₂), units atm.

## 13.3: calculating K from data

**Method in steps**
1. Set up an initial / change / equilibrium table in moles.
2. Use the reaction ratio for the change row.
3. For Kc, divide by volume (unless it cancels). For Kp, find total moles, then mole fractions, then partial pressures.
4. Substitute, calculate, give units.

**Small worked reminder (Kp).** CO(g) + Cl₂(g) ⇌ COCl₂(g). At equilibrium p(CO) = 0.40 atm, p(Cl₂) = 0.25 atm and p(COCl₂) = 1.10 atm.

Kp = 1.10 / (0.40 × 0.25) = **11.0 atm⁻¹**

**Small worked reminder (partial pressure).** A gas with mole fraction 0.35 in a mixture at 4.0 atm has p = 0.35 × 4.0 = **1.4 atm**.

**Small worked reminder (Kc with a volume).** For 2X(g) ⇌ Y(g), a 2.0 dm³ flask holds 0.40 mol X and 0.15 mol Y at equilibrium. [X] = 0.40 / 2.0 = 0.20 mol dm⁻³ and [Y] = 0.15 / 2.0 = 0.075 mol dm⁻³. Kc = 0.075 / 0.20² = **1.9 dm³ mol⁻¹**. Using moles directly would give 0.94, which is wrong.

**When the volume cancels in Kc.** If the total powers on top and bottom are equal, you can use moles directly. The ester equilibrium (two on each side) is the classic case. Otherwise, convert every amount to mol dm⁻³ first.

## 13.4 and 13.5: changing conditions at constant temperature

| Change | Composition | Value of K |
|---|---|---|
| Add a reactant | Shifts towards products | Unchanged |
| Remove a product | Shifts towards products | Unchanged |
| Increase total pressure | Shifts to the side with fewer gas moles | Unchanged |
| Increase pressure, equal gas moles each side | No change | Unchanged |
| Add a catalyst | No change; equilibrium reached sooner | Unchanged |
| Add more of a solid | No change | Unchanged |

**How to explain it with K.** After the change, the fraction in the K expression no longer equals K. The mixture reacts until it does. For example, doubling the pressure on 2A(g) ⇌ B(g) halves the fraction p(B) / p(A)², so more A turns into B.

## 13.6 and 13.7: temperature changes K

| Forward reaction | Raise T | Lower T |
|---|---|---|
| Exothermic | K smaller; equilibrium moves left | K larger; equilibrium moves right |
| Endothermic | K larger; equilibrium moves right | K smaller; equilibrium moves left |

The order of the explanation matters: **temperature changes K first**, and the composition then changes so that the fraction matches the new K.

## 13.8: entropy, temperature and K

- ΔSsurroundings = −ΔH / T, so its size falls as T rises.
- Exothermic: ΔSsurroundings positive, gets smaller with rising T → ΔStotal falls → K falls.
- Endothermic: ΔSsurroundings negative, gets less negative → ΔStotal rises → K rises.
- ΔStotal = R lnK, so K = e^(ΔStotal / R).

**Small worked reminder (temperature).** For a reaction with ΔH = −40 kJ mol⁻¹, ΔSsurroundings = 40 000 / 400 = +100 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ at 400 K but only 40 000 / 500 = +80 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ at 500 K. ΔStotal is 20 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ lower at the higher temperature, so K is smaller.

**Small worked reminder (finding K).** ΔStotal = +45 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ gives lnK = 45 / 8.31 = 5.4, so K ≈ **2.2 × 10²**.

## 13.9: extent of reaction

| K | ΔStotal / J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (approx.) | Extent |
|---|---|---|
| > 10¹⁰ | > +200 | Goes essentially to completion |
| 10⁻¹⁰ to 10¹⁰ | −200 to +200 | Equilibrium mixture |
| < 10⁻¹⁰ | < −200 | Barely reacts |

These are rough guides. A large K does not mean a fast reaction: kinetic stability (outcome 12.11) can stop a thermodynamically favoured change.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Position vs constant.** Concentration and pressure move the position; only temperature changes K.
- **Homogeneous vs heterogeneous.** One phase or more than one. Solids never appear in K.
- **Kc vs Kp.** Concentrations in mol dm⁻³ vs partial pressures in atm. Kp uses gases only.
- **Moles vs concentrations.** Kc needs concentrations unless the volume cancels.
- **Extent vs rate.** K tells you how far, not how fast.
- **ΔSsystem vs ΔStotal.** Only ΔStotal is linked to K by R lnK.

## Further suggested practicals: one-line summaries

- Ethanol + ethanoic acid: titrate the equilibrium mixture with NaOH; subtract the catalyst blank.
- Fe²⁺ + Ag⁺: titrate the silver ions left with thiocyanate.
- Distribution between immiscible solvents: titrate both layers; the ratio of concentrations is constant.
- Ammonium chloride: solid in equilibrium with two gases.
- 2NO₂ ⇌ N₂O₄: colour in a syringe shows temperature and pressure effects.

## Quick self-test

1. Write Kc for 2NO(g) + Br₂(g) ⇌ 2NOBr(g) and give its units.
2. Write Kp for MgCO₃(s) ⇌ MgO(s) + CO₂(g).
3. A gas has mole fraction 0.20 in a mixture at 2.5 atm. What is its partial pressure?
4. For A(g) ⇌ 2B(g), [A] = 0.50 mol dm⁻³ and [B] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³ at equilibrium. Calculate Kc with units.
5. A catalyst is added to an equilibrium mixture. State the effect on Kc.
6. The forward reaction is exothermic. State the effect of raising the temperature on Kp.
7. ΔStotal = −12 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ at a certain temperature. Calculate K.
8. K = 1 × 10⁻¹⁵ for a reaction. Calculate ΔStotal and state the extent of reaction.
9. For an endothermic reaction, state how raising the temperature affects ΔSsurroundings and ΔStotal.
10. Why does adding more Fe(s) not affect the equilibrium 3Fe(s) + 4H₂O(g) ⇌ Fe₃O₄(s) + 4H₂(g)?

### Answers

1. Kc = [NOBr]² / ([NO]² [Br₂]); units **dm³ mol⁻¹**.
2. Kp = **p(CO₂)**; units atm.
3. 0.20 × 2.5 = **0.50 atm**.
4. Kc = 0.30² / 0.50 = **0.18 mol dm⁻³**.
5. **No effect**; equilibrium is reached sooner.
6. Kp **decreases**.
7. K = e^(−12 / 8.31) = **0.24**.
8. ΔStotal = 8.31 × ln(1 × 10⁻¹⁵) = **−287 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹**; the reaction **barely happens**.
9. ΔSsurroundings becomes **less negative**, so ΔStotal **increases** (and K increases).
10. Fe is a solid, so it does **not appear in Kp**; adding more does not change the fraction.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including solids, or including aqueous ions in a Kp expression.
- Using equilibrium moles in Kc when the volume does not cancel.
- Using initial amounts instead of equilibrium amounts.
- Forgetting the 2 : 1 ratio when working out moles of N₂O₄-type products.
- Finding mole fractions but never multiplying by total pressure.
- Giving units when there are none, or leaving off units when there are some.
- Writing "Kp increases with pressure".
- Explaining a temperature effect without saying that K changes.
- Mixing kJ and J in ΔSsurroundings.
- Treating a large K as proof that a reaction is fast.
