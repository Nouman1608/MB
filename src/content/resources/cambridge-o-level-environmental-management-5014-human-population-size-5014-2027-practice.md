These questions cover sub-topic 7.2, Human population size, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge O Level
Environmental Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are a provincial town, a country's changing
fertility and a fast-growing coastal city.

## Section A

**1.** Define *life expectancy*. **[1]**

**2.** State the difference between *emigration* and *immigration*. **[1]**

**3.** Define *urbanisation*. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by an *informal urban settlement*. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A provincial town had 180 000 people at the start of a year. During the year there were 4 200 births,
1 600 deaths, 2 300 immigrants and 3 900 emigrants.
(a) Calculate the natural change. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the net migration. **[1]**
(c) Calculate the population at the end of the year. **[1]**
(d) Suggest **two** reasons why more people left the town than arrived. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows the average number of children per woman in a country. *The data are invented for
practice.*

| Year | Children per woman |
|---|---|
| 1980 | 6.1 |
| 2000 | 4.2 |
| 2020 | 2.6 |

(a) Describe the trend shown. **[2]**
(b) Explain **three** possible reasons for this trend. **[3]**

**7.** Life expectancy in the same country rose from 52 years in 1980 to 71 years in 2020. Explain how
changes in life expectancy and children per woman together affected the rate of population growth. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why young adults make up a large share of rural-urban migrants. **[2]**

**9.** A country's population rose from 30 million to 38 million while its urban population rose from
12 million to 19 million.
(a) Calculate the urban share at the start and the end. **[2]**
(b) Explain whether the country experienced urbanisation. **[1]**

**10.** Describe how rapid population growth can affect the availability of land around a city. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how population growth in a city can increase **two** types of pollution. **[4]**

**12.** Describe **two** ways that population growth can change employment opportunities. **[2]**

## Section C

**13.** A coastal city has grown to more than 10 million people. Discuss the view that the main problems of
its growth are caused by the speed of growth rather than the number of people. **[6]**

**14.** Discuss whether falling birth rates or migration has the greater effect on the population size of
a country. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** The average number of years a person can expect to live. **[1]**

**2.** Emigration is leaving a country to live elsewhere; immigration is arriving to live in a country. **[1]**

**3.** An increase in the proportion of a country's population living in urban areas. **[1]**

**4.** Housing built without legal permission, often lacking services such as clean water, sanitation and
electricity. **[1]**

**5.** (a) 4 200 - 1 600 = **+2 600**. **[1]**
(b) 2 300 - 3 900 = **-1 600**. **[1]**
(c) 180 000 + 2 600 - 1 600 = **181 000**. **[1]**
(d) Any two push factors: few jobs or low wages **[1]**; lack of services such as hospitals or universities
**[1]**; pull of a larger city with more jobs **[1]**; decline of a local industry **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**6.** (a) Children per woman fell steadily **[1]**, from 6.1 in 1980 to 2.6 in 2020, a fall of 3.5 **[1]**.
(b) Any three: better child survival, so parents need fewer births **[1]**; more girls educated and women in
paid work, delaying marriage **[1]**; wider access to contraception **[1]**; urbanisation makes children more
costly and less needed for farm work **[1]**; government policy **[1]**. **[max 3]**

**7.** Rising life expectancy means death rates fell **[1]**; at first children per woman stayed high, so births
greatly exceeded deaths and growth was rapid **[1]**; as children per woman fell towards replacement level,
the gap narrowed and growth slowed **[1]**.

**8.** Young adults are most likely to be looking for work or education **[1]** and have fewer ties such as
land, property or dependent children, so they can move more easily **[1]**.

**9.** (a) Start: 12 / 30 x 100 = **40%** **[1]**; end: 19 / 38 x 100 = **50%** **[1]**.
(b) Yes, because the proportion living in urban areas increased from 40% to 50%. **[1]**

**10.** More land is needed for housing, roads and industry **[1]**, so farmland and natural habitats on the
city edge are built over **[1]**; land prices rise, so poorer migrants build informal settlements on unsafe or
unused land such as slopes or floodplains **[1]**.

**11.** Two explained types, two marks each. Air pollution: more vehicles and industry burn fuel **[1]**,
releasing exhaust gases and particulates **[1]**. Water pollution: sewage and industrial waste increase
faster than treatment systems **[1]** and enter rivers and the sea **[1]**. (Accept noise, light, land or
visual pollution with a suitable explanation.)

**12.** Any two: more workers attract new factories and services, creating jobs **[1]**; demand for
construction, transport and retail rises **[1]**; if jobs do not keep pace, unemployment and informal work
increase **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**13.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Speed: housing, sanitation and water supply cannot be built as
fast as people arrive **[1]**, so informal settlements grow **[1]**; infrastructure falls behind, causing traffic
congestion **[1]**. Number: a megacity needs huge amounts of water, energy and food whatever its growth
rate **[1]**; waste and pollution rise with the number of people **[1]**. Other factors: wealth and planning
decide whether the city can cope **[1]**. Conclusion: speed causes the most visible problems, but size
creates lasting resource demands **[1]**. **[max 6]**

**14.** **[6]** One mark per point, up to six. Falling birth rates: affect every year's natural change **[1]**;
below about 2.1 children per woman a population eventually declines without migration **[1]**; changes are
slow but long-lasting **[1]**. Migration: can change a population quickly **[1]**; migrants are mostly young
adults, so they add workers and later births **[1]**; emigration can cause decline in sending countries even
with high birth rates **[1]**. Conclusion: for most countries natural change matters more over the long term,
but migration can dominate in some countries **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the sign of net migration (a negative value reduces the population).
- Describing a trend without quoting the start and end values.
- Saying a rising urban population proves urbanisation without checking the share.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which measure of birth rate does the syllabus use?**
The 7.2.1 wording links birth rates to children per woman, the average number of children a woman has in
her lifetime. It shows family size directly, which makes it easy to compare countries.

**What counts as a megacity?**
A city with more than 10 million people.
