These questions cover section **3.3.4 Humans and their effects on the environment** of the OxfordAQA International
GCSE Biology (9201) specification, Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered.
The specification marks the whole section as Biology-only content. All data are invented for practice. A
calculator is useful for questions 5 to 8.

## Section A

**1.** Explain why a rapid increase in the human population can lead to more pollution. **[2]**

**2.** Name a pollutant for each of these. **[3]**

**(a)** Water.
**(b)** Air.
**(c)** Land.

**3.** Name the gas, released into the air, that contributes to acid rain. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** gases whose increasing levels in the atmosphere contribute to global warming. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Fertiliser washed into a pond from nearby fields. The table shows measurements over six weeks.

| Week | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Algae (cells per cm³) | 1 500 | 12 000 | 60 000 | 20 000 |
| Dissolved oxygen (mg per dm³) | 8.8 | 9.1 | 6.0 | 3.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in dissolved oxygen from week 0 to week 6. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the algae increased between weeks 0 and 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the oxygen concentration fell between weeks 4 and 6. **[4]**
**(d)** State what would happen to fish in the pond, and why. **[2]**

**6.** A student counted species of trees and shrubs in four plots of tropical forest and four plots of nearby land that had been cleared.

| Plot | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Forest (species) | 30 | 34 | 28 | 32 |
| Cleared (species) | 5 | 7 | 4 | 6 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of species in each type of plot. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction in the mean number of species after clearing. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe **two** other effects of large-scale deforestation. **[2]**

**7.** The carbon dioxide concentration at a monitoring station was 355 ppm in 1992 and 405 ppm in 2022.

**(a)** Calculate the rise in ppm. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the student can and cannot conclude from these two values about the cause of any change in temperature. **[2]**

**8.** Nitrate in a river was measured four times upstream and four times downstream of a farm.

| Reading | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Upstream (mg per dm³) | 2.0 | 2.2 | 1.8 | 2.0 |
| Downstream (mg per dm³) | 6.4 | 6.0 | 6.6 | 6.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean nitrate concentration at each site. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the downstream mean is than the upstream mean. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Evaluate the method used to collect these data. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **three** possible effects of a rise in the Earth's temperature of a few degrees Celsius. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why pesticides sprayed on farmland may harm organisms in a river. **[2]**

**11.** A farmer wants to prevent eutrophication of a stream beside the fields. Suggest **two** actions the farmer could take and give a reason for each. **[4]**

**12.** Describe the sequence of events by which sewage in a river can lead to the death of fish. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** More people produce more waste (and a rising standard of living adds to this) [1]; unless waste and chemicals are handled properly, more pollution results [1]

**2. (a)** Any one of: sewage; fertiliser; toxic chemicals [1]
**(b)** Any one of: smoke; sulfur dioxide [1]
**(c)** Any one of: pesticides; herbicides; other toxic chemicals [1]

**3.** Sulfur dioxide [1]

**4.** Carbon dioxide [1]; methane [1]

**5. (a)** (8.8 − 3.1) ÷ 8.8 × 100 [1] = 64.8, so **65%** [1]
**(b)** The fertiliser increased the concentration of mineral ions in the water [1]; which stimulated the growth of the algae [1]
**(c)** The large number of algae stopped light reaching some of them, so they could not photosynthesise and died [1]; the population of microorganisms feeding on the dead algae increased [1]; the microorganisms respire, using oxygen [1]; this depleted the oxygen concentration in the water [1]
**(d)** The fish would die [1]; because they are aerobic organisms and there would be too little oxygen [1]

**6. (a)** Forest: (30 + 34 + 28 + 32) ÷ 4 = 124 ÷ 4 = **31.0** [1]; cleared: (5 + 7 + 4 + 6) ÷ 4 = 22 ÷ 4 = **5.5** [1]
**(b)** (31.0 − 5.5) ÷ 31.0 × 100 [1] = 82.3, so **82%** [1]
**(c)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: less photosynthesis, so less carbon dioxide removed from the air; burning or decay of the trees releases carbon dioxide; habitats are destroyed so animals lose food and shelter; soil may be washed away

**7. (a)** 405 − 355 = **50 ppm** [1]
**(b)** 50 ÷ 355 × 100 [1] = **14.1%** [1]
**(c)** Carbon dioxide has increased and may contribute to global warming [1]; two values alone show a trend over time, not that the carbon dioxide caused the temperature change (a correlation is not proof of cause, and temperature data are needed) [1]

**8. (a)** Upstream: (2.0 + 2.2 + 1.8 + 2.0) ÷ 4 = 8.0 ÷ 4 = **2.0** [1]; downstream: (6.4 + 6.0 + 6.6 + 6.2) ÷ 4 = 25.2 ÷ 4 = **6.3** [1]
**(b)** 6.3 ÷ 2.0 = **3.2 times** [1]
**(c)** Any three of the following, 1 mark each [3]: four readings at each site that agree closely suggest the data are reliable; the readings were taken on one occasion only, so they may not represent other times or seasons; the farm may not be the only source of nitrate (for example sewage), so the cause is not certain; samples should be taken at the same depth, time and distance; more sites or repeat sampling would improve validity

**9.** Any three of the following, 1 mark each [3]: big changes in the Earth's climate; a rise in sea level; changes in migration patterns, for example of birds; changes in the distribution of species

**10.** Pesticides on land may be washed into water [1]; they are toxic chemicals, so they may poison organisms in the river [1]

**11.** Any two actions with reasons, 2 marks each [4]: use less fertiliser or apply it when rain is unlikely, so less is washed into the stream and there are fewer mineral ions to stimulate algal growth; leave a strip of uncultivated land beside the stream, so fertiliser is taken up by plants before it reaches the water; keep animal waste and sewage away from the stream, which stops mineral ions entering

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- sewage increases the concentration of mineral ions in the water
- this stimulates the growth of algae (and/or plants)
- the algae/plants grow so much that some cannot receive enough light for photosynthesis
- those that cannot photosynthesise die
- the population of microorganisms that feed on the dead organisms increases
- respiration of the microorganisms depletes the oxygen, leading to the death of aerobic organisms such as fish

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that the algae use up all the oxygen. The microorganisms' respiration is the key step.
- Leaving out the death of the algae due to lack of light.
- Mixing up global warming gases and sulfur dioxide.
- Writing percentage calculations without the starting value in the denominator.
- Concluding that correlation proves cause.
- Evaluating a method without mentioning repeats, validity or reliability.
