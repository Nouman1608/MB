## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Distinguish between delegation and decentralisation. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by span of control. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** advantage of a matrix structure. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by organisational culture. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following describes a flat organisational structure?

A: Many layers of management with narrow spans of control.

B: Few layers of management with wide spans of control.

C: A structure with no managers.

D: A structure in which all decisions are made at the top.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Pemberton Retail has 180 front-line staff. At present every manager supervises 5 people. The directors plan to widen the span of control to 9 at every level.

**(a)** Calculate the number of managers needed at each level and in total under the current structure. Round up at each level. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the total number of managers needed with a span of 9, and the number of management posts that would be removed. **[3]**

**(c)** Each manager costs £40,000 a year and redundancy costs £20,000 per post. Calculate the annual saving and the number of years needed to recover the redundancy cost. **[3]**

**(d)** Evaluate whether Pemberton Retail should delayer. **[8]**

**7.** Calderwood Engineering is organised by function (production, sales, finance, design). Projects often run late because the departments blame each other. A consultant suggests a matrix structure with a project manager for each major order.

Evaluate the usefulness of a matrix structure to Calderwood Engineering and its stakeholders. **[9]**

**8.** Analyse **two** factors that may have shaped the culture of a long-established family firm that employs 40 people. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Delegation is passing a task, along with the authority to carry it out, to a subordinate [1], whereas decentralisation is a policy of taking decisions at lower levels of the organisation [1]. With delegation the manager stays accountable for the result, and it is usually for a specific task, whereas decentralisation is a standing arrangement [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Span of control is the number of people a manager directly supervises [1]. A wide span means a manager has many direct reports, and a narrow span means few [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Specialists can be shared between projects, so skills are used fully [1], and staff from different departments work together, improving communication and speed on a project [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Organisational culture is the shared values, beliefs and habits of the people in a business [1], which together make up "how things are done here" [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** 180 ÷ 5 = 36 managers at the first level [1]. 36 ÷ 5 = 7.2, so 8 [1]. 8 ÷ 5 = 1.6, so 2 [1]. 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4, so 1. Total = 36 + 8 + 2 + 1 = **47 managers** [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** 180 ÷ 9 = 20 [1]. 20 ÷ 9 = 2.2, so 3; then 3 ÷ 9 = 0.33, so 1. Total = 20 + 3 + 1 = **24 managers** [1]. Posts removed = 47 − 24 = **23** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Annual saving = 23 × £40,000 = **£920,000** [1]. Redundancy cost = 23 × £20,000 = £460,000 [1]. Recovered in £460,000 ÷ £920,000 = **0.5 years** [1]. **[3]**

**(d)** The saving of £920,000 a year is large and the redundancy cost is recovered within six months [1]. Communication should be faster with one layer fewer [1], and staff may have more responsibility, which can raise motivation [1].
But a span of 9 may be too wide for staff who need close help, such as new shop assistants, and standards may fall [1]. The 23 redundancies could damage morale among those left and lose experienced people [1]. Decisions once made by middle managers must be taken lower down or higher up, which may need training [1].
The figures assume the business still works as well with 24 managers as with 47, which is uncertain [1].
Judgement: Pemberton should delayer, but gradually and with training, perhaps first in stores with experienced staff, since the cost saving is large while the risks to service can be reduced by testing the wider span [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Benefits to Calderwood: each project has one manager responsible for delivery, so delays are less likely to be blamed on other departments [1]. Specialists from design, production and finance work in the project team, which improves communication and problem solving [1]. Staff may find the work more varied and feel more involved [1]. Customers should receive orders more reliably [1].
Drawbacks: staff report to two managers, the functional head and the project manager, and may receive conflicting instructions [1]. Functional heads and project managers may compete over staff time [1]. It may need more managers and meetings, which raises cost [1].
Evaluation: the structure suits a business with distinct projects, as here, but needs clear rules on who decides what [1]. Judgement: a matrix is worth trying for the larger orders, because the present delays suggest a coordination problem, but it should be introduced with training on dealing with two managers, and its effect on lateness should be measured [1]. **[9]**

**8.** Any two factors, 3 marks each. Leadership: the founder's values, such as treating staff like family, set the tone and are copied by managers [1]; this may produce loyalty and low turnover [1], but also resistance to change if new ideas are seen as a challenge to the founder's way [1]. History: the firm has traditions built over decades, so long-serving staff expect things to be done as they always were [1]; this gives stability and shared pride [1], but makes it hard to adopt new technology or working practices [1]. Accept size (a small firm tends to be informal), staff, or sector, with explanation. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing delegation with decentralisation.
- Not rounding up when working out the number of managers at each level.
- Saying flat structures are always better.
- Describing a structure without linking it to the business in the case.
- Writing about culture in general terms without a cause or an effect.
