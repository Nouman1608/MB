## Section A

**1.** Identify three ways in which the Great Depression helped the Nazis to power in Germany. **[3]**

**2.** State two reasons why the USA remained isolationist in the 1930s. **[2]**

**3.** Give three reasons why the League did not impose sanctions on Japan over Manchuria. **[3]**

**4.** Identify three ways in which the Soviet Union changed its foreign policy in response to Nazism. **[3]**

**5.** State two reasons why France adopted a defensive military strategy in the 1930s. **[2]**

**6.** Identify three foreign interventions in the Spanish Civil War. **[3]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which British rearmament prepared the country for war by 1939. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain why the World Disarmament Conference failed. **[10]**

**9.** "The Abyssinian crisis did more damage to the League of Nations than the Manchurian crisis." How far do you agree? **[20]**

**10.** Read the two invented sources below. Compare Sources A and B as evidence about the Munich Agreement.

*Source A (invented)* -- From a letter by a British civil servant to his sister, October 1938. "The crowds outside Downing Street cheered until they were hoarse. We have given away a country that was not ours to give, but we have also bought a year in which every aircraft factory in England will work day and night. I would not have wished for war this autumn with the fighters we have."

*Source B (invented)* -- From a Czech newspaper, October 1938. "The great powers met in Munich and decided our fate without us. Our frontier fortresses, built with the savings of the people, have been handed over without a shot. France has broken her treaty with us. Let no small nation trust the word of the democracies again." **[15]**

**11.** Read the two further invented sources below, together with Sources A and B in Question 10. How far do Sources A to D support the view that appeasement was a sensible policy for Britain in the 1930s?

*Source C (invented)* -- From a speech by an opposition MP in the House of Commons, October 1938. "We are told peace has been saved. It has been bought, and the price has been paid by others. Each surrender makes the dictator stronger and the next surrender more certain. The time we have gained, Germany has gained also."

*Source D (invented)* -- From a memorandum by a British defence planner, 1937. "The fleet, the army and the air force together cannot hold the home islands, the Mediterranean route and the Far East if all three dictatorships move at once. The services cannot shorten the list of our enemies. Only diplomacy can do that." **[25]**

**12.** Explain why Britain and France did not oppose the remilitarisation of the Rhineland in March 1936. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: the recall of American loans after the Crash caused a collapse in German industry and mass unemployment (1); the coalition government broke up in 1930 over unemployment benefits, leading to rule by presidential decree (1); Brüning's deflationary policies deepened suffering and discredited democratic parties (1); the middle classes and farmers, fearing ruin and communism, turned to the Nazis (1); Nazi propaganda promised work and national revival (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Depression focused attention on domestic recovery (1); the Nye Committee hearings (1934-36) suggested bankers and arms makers had drawn the USA into the First World War (1); Congress passed Neutrality Acts from 1935 banning arms sales to warring nations (1); disillusion with the peace settlement and with European war debts (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: Britain and France had few forces in the Far East and their own economic difficulties (1); the USA, Japan's main trading partner, was not a member (1); some in Britain saw Japan as a force for order in a disordered China and valued trade with it (1); the League took a year to report through the Lytton Commission, by which time Manchukuo existed (1); members feared that sanctions would hurt their own economies in the Depression (1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: it joined the League of Nations in 1934 (1); it signed mutual assistance pacts with France and Czechoslovakia in 1935 (1); the Comintern adopted the Popular Front policy (1935), calling for co-operation with socialists and liberals against fascism (1); it supplied the Spanish Republic with arms and advisers from 1936 (1); Litvinov argued for collective security at Geneva (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: France's heavy losses in the First World War made leaders determined to avoid another offensive war (1); its smaller and older population compared with Germany's limited the size of its army (1); the Maginot Line was built to protect the frontier and save manpower (1); political divisions and economic problems made a costly offensive strategy hard to fund or agree (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Germany sent the Condor Legion's aircraft and men to support Franco (1); Italy sent tens of thousands of troops and aircraft to the Nationalists (1); the USSR sent tanks, aircraft and advisers to the Republic (1); the Comintern organised the International Brigades of foreign volunteers (1); Portugal supported the Nationalists (1). **[3]**

**7.** Two ways, 2 marks each: the RAF was expanded with an emphasis on modern fighters such as the Hurricane and Spitfire, to defend against bombing (2); a chain of radar stations gave early warning of air attack (2); air-raid precautions -- wardens, gas masks and shelters -- prepared civilians for bombing (2); conscription was introduced in 1939 to enlarge the army (2). **[4]**

**8.** Indicative content. **France and Germany:** France would not disarm without guarantees of security, while Germany demanded equality of treatment; the concession of equality in principle (December 1932) satisfied neither. **International events:** Japanese aggression in China showed that disarmament offered no protection; Hitler's appointment in January 1933 made France still more cautious. **Hitler's choice:** in October 1933 Germany left the conference and the League, removing the main reason for it. **Technical disputes:** powers could not agree which weapons were "offensive"; each defined the weapons it relied on as defensive. **Economic and political climate:** the Depression intensified nationalism and made governments unwilling to accept limits. **Linking:** the Franco-German deadlock was the root cause; Hitler's withdrawal turned deadlock into failure. **[10]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For Abyssinia:** the League did act -- naming the aggressor and imposing sanctions -- and its failure was therefore more public; sanctions excluded oil, and the Suez Canal stayed open; the Hoare-Laval Plan showed Britain and France negotiating away a member state; Abyssinia was a European power's aggression against a League member in Africa, close to British and French interests, so inaction could not be excused by distance; the crisis destroyed the Stresa Front and pushed Mussolini towards Hitler; Hitler remilitarised the Rhineland while attention was on Africa. **For Manchuria:** it was the first test and set the precedent that a great power could defy the League; the League's slow procedure gave Japan time; Japan's withdrawal began the exodus of great powers; the USA's absence was exposed. **Judgement:** strong answers argue, for example, that Manchuria showed the League's weakness in distant regions, but Abyssinia showed that even where it could act it would not, which ended collective security as a policy. **[20]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Similarities:** both agree that Czechoslovakia lost territory it did not agree to give up ("not ours to give" / "without us"). **Differences:** A sees gains -- a year for aircraft production, and avoiding a war Britain was not ready for; B sees only betrayal, including the loss of its frontier fortresses and France's broken treaty. **Explaining:** A is a private letter by a British official, perhaps aware of the state of air defences, writing amid public relief; B is a Czech newspaper expressing national humiliation and anger at France, which had a treaty with Czechoslovakia. Knowledge: Czechoslovakia was not represented at Munich; the Sudetenland included its main fortifications; Britain's fighter strength and radar were incomplete in 1938. **[15]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Support:** D shows a defence planner warning that Britain could not fight Germany, Italy and Japan together and needed diplomacy to reduce its enemies -- a strategic case for appeasement; A argues that Munich bought time for rearmament. Knowledge: the Ten-Year Rule's legacy, economic constraints, imperial commitments, public opinion (the Peace Ballot), and fears of air attack all limited choices; by 1939 Britain entered war with public and Dominion support. **Challenge:** C argues each concession strengthened Hitler and that Germany gained time too; B shows the moral and diplomatic cost -- small states lost faith in Britain and France. Knowledge: Germany gained Czech fortifications and the Skoda works; Hitler's confidence grew; the USSR was excluded from Munich and later turned to Germany. **Provenance:** D is a confidential planning document, not written to defend a political choice, which gives it weight; its view matches the real warnings of the Chiefs of Staff in the late 1930s; C is a partisan speech. **Judgement:** the sources suggest appeasement was a reasonable response to Britain's strategic position, but less sensible once Hitler's aims became clear -- after Prague, even its supporters abandoned it. **[25]**

**12.** Indicative content. **Public opinion:** many in Britain felt Germany was only "walking into its own back garden"; few would support war over German soldiers entering German territory. **Military weakness:** French commanders overestimated German strength and would not act without mobilisation; Britain had no forces ready. **French politics:** France had a caretaker government weeks before elections, and its strategy was defensive, based on the Maginot Line. **Abyssinia:** Britain and France were still absorbed in the Abyssinian crisis and divided over Italy. **Treaty doubts:** the Franco-Soviet Pact gave Hitler a pretext; British opinion saw the Versailles restriction as unfair. **Linking:** without British support France would not move, and British opinion made support impossible. **[10]**
