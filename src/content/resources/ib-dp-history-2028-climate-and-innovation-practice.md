## Part 1: a paper 1 style set (24 marks)

**Inquiry question: How did climate conditions shape innovation?**

**Source A** (constructed for practice). From a historian's survey of the Valley of Mexico, published in the late twentieth century.

> "Land was scarce on the lake shore and the water was often too salty to farm. The chinampa builders answered this by raising garden plots from the lake bed. Because the plots were surrounded by water, they could be watered all year, which allowed more than one harvest. When the rains failed in the 1450s, these gardens were less exposed than fields on the dry shore."

**Source B** (constructed for practice). From a saga-style account of Greenland, composed in Iceland in the thirteenth century.

> "That summer the sea was calm and the fjords free of ice. Erik's men found grass in the valleys, enough for their cattle, and they built halls of turf. Many people came from Iceland that year, for there was little room left at home."

**Source C** (constructed for practice). An entry in a church annal written in Iceland in about 1350, quoted in a modern textbook.

> "The ice came early in the north this year, and ships from the west did not arrive. The bishop's tithe was not paid."

*Context for source C:* The annal was kept by a clergyman who never visited Greenland. Annals recorded events in the Church's year and the arrival of goods and revenue.

**1.** Explain how the **content** of both source A and source B can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**2.** Analyse how the **context** of source C influences how it can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[6]**

**3.** Examine how the **perspectives** in all the sources can be used to answer the inquiry question. **[12]**

## Part 2: short knowledge questions

**4.** Explain one reason why the Triple Alliance mattered to the growth of the Aztec Empire. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Marking points (6). Credit specific content from both sources linked to the inquiry question:
- Source A: scarce land and salty water led to an innovation, the chinampas **[1]**; surrounding water allowed year-round watering and several harvests **[1]**; in the dry 1450s the gardens were less exposed than shore fields, so the innovation helped the Mexica cope with drought **[1]**.
- Source B: calm sea and ice-free fjords made the voyage and settlement possible **[1]**; grass in the valleys fed cattle, which supported farming in Greenland **[1]**; the mention of little room in Iceland suggests that land pressure, as well as climate, prompted the move **[1]**.
Markbands: 1 to 2 for description with little explanation; 3 to 4 for partial explanation (maximum 3 if only one source is used); 5 to 6 for a range of specific content from both sources with developed links to the question.

**2.** Marking points (6). Credit analysis of how context influences use:
- Author: a clergyman in Iceland who never visited Greenland, so the report is second-hand and cannot show conditions there directly **[1 to 2]**.
- Date: about 1350, a later period than the earliest voyages, so it may reflect colder conditions and cannot show the situation in 1000 **[1 to 2]**.
- Purpose and type: an annal recording Church revenue, so the focus is the unpaid tithe rather than the environment; useful to show that ice could disrupt voyages and trade **[1 to 2]**.
- Link to the question: the source can be used to show climate limiting as well as enabling activity, but cannot show how climate prompted early innovation **[1]**.
Reward references to context that are linked to use. A list of context with no link to use stays in the lower bands.

**3.** Marking points (12). Perspectives to identify:
- Source A: a modern historian's view, which stresses human adaptation to a difficult environment (innovation as a response to constraint) **[2]**.
- Source B: a Norse-Icelandic view, composed centuries later and celebrating settlement; it emphasises favourable conditions and pressure on land at home **[2]**.
- Source C: a Church view from Iceland, interested in revenue; it shows climate as an obstacle to connection **[2]**.
Compare:
- Similarity: A and B both link environment to opportunity (more harvests; grass for cattle); all three show that the environment mattered **[2]**.
- Difference: A and B see climate as shaping innovation positively or by challenge; C shows climate limiting activity. A is analytical and retrospective; B and C are contemporary or near-contemporary but selective **[2]**.
- Combining: together the sources suggest that climate is both a condition that enabled innovation (B) and a pressure to which people responded (A), and that it could also set limits (C) **[2]**.
Markbands: 1 to 3 minimal understanding; 4 to 6 partial understanding with some similarities and differences; 7 to 9 understanding of at least two sources (maximum 9 if only two sources); 10 to 12 insightful understanding of all three sources, with similarities and differences examined and supported by references.

**4.** For example: it united three cities (Tenochtitlan, Texcoco and Tlacopan) after defeating Azcapotzalco in 1428 **[1]**; this gave a larger pool of warriors and a shared share of tribute **[1]**; so Tenochtitlan could expand its conquests under rulers such as Moctezuma I **[1]**.

**5.** For example: the period was one of generally warmer conditions in the North Atlantic **[1]**; this meant less sea ice, so sea routes between Iceland and Greenland, and onward, were more passable **[1]**; this encouraged regular voyages and the founding of settlements **[1]**.
