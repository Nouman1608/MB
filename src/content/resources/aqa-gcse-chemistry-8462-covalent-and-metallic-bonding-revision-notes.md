## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Covalent bond**: a shared pair of electrons between two atoms. Covalent bonds are **strong**.
- **Single, double, triple bond**: one, two or three shared pairs between the same two atoms.
- **Small molecule**: a fixed, small number of atoms held together by covalent bonds (H₂O, NH₃, CO₂).
- **Polymer**: a very large molecule made of many repeating units joined by covalent bonds.
- **Giant covalent structure**: a huge number of atoms joined by covalent bonds in a continuous network (diamond, silicon dioxide).
- **Molecular formula**: the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule.
- **Delocalised electrons**: outer-shell electrons from metal atoms that are free to move through the whole structure.
- **Metallic bond**: the strong bond that comes from sharing delocalised electrons among the metal atoms in a giant regular structure.

## Bond counts (4.2.1.4)

| Atom | Group | Outer electrons | Bonds it forms |
|---|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| C | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| N | 5 | 5 | 3 |
| O | 6 | 6 | 2 |
| Cl | 7 | 7 | 1 |

For groups 4 to 7: **bonds = 8 − group number**. Hydrogen needs only 2 electrons, so it forms 1 bond.

## The eight dot and cross molecules

| Molecule | Line form | Shared pairs | Non-shared pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen, H₂ | H–H | 1 | 0 |
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | Cl–Cl | 1 | 3 per Cl |
| Oxygen, O₂ | O=O | 2 | 2 per O |
| Nitrogen, N₂ | N≡N | 3 | 1 per N |
| Hydrogen chloride, HCl | H–Cl | 1 | 3 on Cl |
| Water, H₂O | H–O–H | 2 | 2 on O |
| Ammonia, NH₃ | N with three N–H | 3 | 1 on N |
| Methane, CH₄ | C with four C–H | 4 | 0 |

### Method: drawing any dot and cross diagram

```
1. Write the outer electrons for each atom.
2. Bonds per atom = electrons needed to fill the shell.
3. Overlap the shells; put ONE dot + ONE cross
   in the overlap for each bond.
4. Put the leftover outer electrons in pairs on
   their own atom.
5. Count: H = 2, every other atom = 8.
```

### Worked reminder: ammonia

```
N: 5 outer, needs 3  ->  3 N–H bonds
Each overlap: 1 dot (N) + 1 cross (H)
Left on N: 5 - 3 = 2  ->  1 non-shared pair
Check N: 3 x 2 shared + 2 = 8   Each H: 2
```

## Three kinds of covalent substance

| Kind | How to spot it in a diagram | Examples you must know |
|---|---|---|
| Small molecules | Separate groups of a few atoms, space between them | H₂, Cl₂, O₂, N₂, HCl, H₂O, NH₃, CH₄ |
| Polymers | Repeating unit in brackets, bonds through the brackets, *n* after | poly(ethene) |
| Giant covalent | Network with bonds running off every edge | diamond, silicon dioxide |

**Formula trap.** CO₂ is a small molecule. SiO₂ is giant covalent. For a giant structure the formula gives the ratio of atoms only. In silicon dioxide each Si bonds to 4 O and each O bonds to 2 Si.

## Representing bonds with lines

- One line = one shared pair (a single bond). Two lines = double, three = triple.
- **Polymer**: draw one repeating unit in brackets, with a line through each bracket and *n* outside. *n* is a large number.

```
   [  H   H  ]
 --[--C---C--]--     poly(ethene)
   [  H   H  ] n     (H atoms bond to each C, above and below)
```

- **Giant structure**: draw part of the network, with lines leaving the edge to show it carries on. Diamond: every C bonded to 4 other C.

### Method: molecular formula from a model

```
1. Use the key: which colour/letter is which element.
2. Count each element. Tick atoms as you count them.
3. Write C first, H second, then the rest
   alphabetically (e.g. C2H6O).
4. Check bond totals: C 4, N 3, O 2, H 1, Cl 1.
5. Do NOT cancel to a simpler ratio.
```

## Limitations of each representation

| Representation | Main limitation |
|---|---|
| Dot and cross | Does not show 3D shape; atom sizes not shown; electrons look different when they are identical |
| 2D displayed formula | No 3D shape; no electrons shown, so non-shared pairs are missing |
| Ball and stick | Bonds are not sticks and atoms are not spaced apart; no electrons; not to scale |
| 3D space-filling | Bonds are hard to see or count |
| Diagram of a giant structure | Shows only a tiny part of a structure that continues in all directions |

Give a **specific** limitation for the named model. "It is not accurate" scores nothing.

## Metallic bonding (4.2.1.5)

- Metals are **giant structures of atoms in a regular pattern**.
- **Outer-shell** electrons are **delocalised**: free to move through the **whole structure**.
- Sharing the delocalised electrons gives **strong metallic bonds**: electrostatic attraction between positive metal ions and the delocalised electrons.
- Diagram: regular rows of + ions with e⁻ between them.
- Found in metallic elements and alloys.

Number of delocalised electrons per atom = number of outer electrons:

| Metal | Group | Delocalised e⁻ per atom | Ion left |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium | 1 | 1 | Na⁺ |
| Calcium | 2 | 2 | Ca²⁺ |
| Aluminium | 3 | 3 | Al³⁺ |

## Must-know distinctions

| | Covalent | Metallic |
|---|---|---|
| Elements | Non-metals only | Metals and alloys |
| Electrons | Shared in pairs between two particular atoms | Delocalised across the whole structure |
| Structure | Small molecule, polymer or giant covalent | Always giant, regular |
| Bond strength | Strong | Strong |

- **Covalent bond** (strong, inside a molecule) vs **forces between molecules** (weak, section 4.2.2.4). Melting a small molecular substance does not break covalent bonds.
- **Molecular formula** (actual numbers, small molecules) vs **formula of a giant structure** (ratio only).
- **Shared pair** (in the overlap, one dot + one cross) vs **non-shared pair** (on one atom, both the same symbol).

## Quick self-test

1. How many pairs of electrons are shared in a nitrogen molecule?
2. How many non-shared pairs of electrons are on the oxygen atom in water?
3. What is the total number of electrons shared in a methane molecule?
4. A displayed formula shows two N atoms joined by a single line, with two H atoms on each N. Give its molecular formula.
5. In the molecule in question 4, how many non-shared pairs are on each N atom?
6. Classify each as small molecules or giant covalent: CO₂, SiO₂, diamond, HCl.
7. In a polymer diagram, what does *n* represent?
8. How many bonds does each carbon atom form in diamond?
9. State what is meant by delocalised electrons in a metal.
10. How many delocalised electrons does each aluminium atom provide?
11. Give one limitation of a ball and stick model.
12. Why is the formula of diamond written simply as C?

### Answers

1. **3** (a triple bond, N≡N).
2. **2**. Oxygen has 6 outer electrons; 2 are shared, leaving 4, which is 2 pairs.
3. **8** electrons (4 shared pairs).
4. **N₂H₄** (do not cancel to NH₂).
5. **1**. Each N has 5 outer electrons and uses 3 in bonds (1 N–N and 2 N–H), leaving 2: one pair.
6. CO₂ small molecules; SiO₂ giant covalent; diamond giant covalent; HCl small molecules.
7. A **large number**: the number of repeating units in the chain.
8. **4**, each to another carbon atom.
9. Outer-shell electrons that are not held by one atom and are free to move through the whole structure.
10. **3** (aluminium is in Group 3).
11. Any one: bonds are not really sticks; atoms are not really spaced apart; atoms not drawn to scale; electrons are not shown.
12. Diamond is a giant structure of only carbon atoms, with no fixed number of atoms, so the formula shows just the element.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Shared pair drawn as two dots or two crosses, or placed outside the overlap of the two shells.
- Missing non-shared pairs on O, N or Cl, so the atom does not have 8 outer electrons.
- N₂ drawn with fewer than three shared pairs, or O₂ with a single bond.
- Calling covalent bonds weak when the question is about the bond itself.
- Classifying SiO₂ as a small molecule because its formula looks like CO₂.
- A molecular formula cancelled to a ratio, or an atom missed in the count from a model.
- A vague limitation ("it's only a model") instead of a specific one for the named diagram.
- Metallic bonding described as "free electrons" without saying they are **delocalised outer-shell** electrons moving through the **whole structure**.
- Forgetting the word "regular" (or "pattern") when describing how metal atoms are arranged.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a covalent bond in AQA GCSE Chemistry 8462?**
A covalent bond is a shared pair of electrons between two atoms, and covalent bonds are strong. A single, double or triple bond has one, two or three shared pairs between the same two atoms.

**Is silicon dioxide a small molecule like carbon dioxide?**
No. Silicon dioxide has a giant covalent structure, a continuous network of atoms joined by covalent bonds, even though its formula looks like CO₂. Classifying it as a small molecule is a common error.

**How many shared pairs are there in nitrogen and oxygen molecules?**
A nitrogen molecule, N₂, has a triple bond (three shared pairs) and an oxygen molecule, O₂, has a double bond (two shared pairs). Drawing either with too few shared pairs loses marks.

**What are delocalised electrons in a metal?**
They are the outer-shell electrons from the metal atoms, which are free to move through the whole structure. Sharing these delocalised electrons gives strong metallic bonding in a giant regular structure.
