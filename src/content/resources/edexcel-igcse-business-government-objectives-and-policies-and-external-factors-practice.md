## Questions

**1.** State two public services that a government may provide using money raised from taxation. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term infrastructure and explain how one example of it could help a business that delivers goods. **[3]**

**3.** Explain one constraint on how much a government can spend. **[3]**

**4.** A government places a 10% tariff on imported padlocks. A retailer buys padlocks from an overseas supplier at 30 each. A home producer sells similar padlocks at 35.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of one imported padlock after the tariff. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the imported padlock is now cheaper or dearer than the home-produced one. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one effect of the tariff on the retailer. **[2]**

**5.** Verlen Print has a loan of 90,000 at an interest rate of 4% a year. The rate rises to 7%.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly interest at each rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the extra yearly cost and the percentage increase in the interest bill. **[3]**
**(c)** Outline one other way the rate rise may affect Verlen Print. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a rise in interest rates may reduce the sales of a furniture store. **[3]**

**7.** A government adds a sales tax of 8% to the price of treadmills. Sporlen Fitness sells them for 250 before tax and sells 90 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the tax and the price the customer pays. **[2]**
**(b)** After the tax, monthly sales fall to 80. Calculate the percentage change in the number sold. **[2]**
**(c)** Sporlen receives the pre-tax price. Calculate its monthly sales revenue before and after the tax. **[2]**

**8.** Marlow Textiles exports 24,000 scarves a year at 18 each to a country that is outside the trading bloc Marlow belongs to. That country introduces a 10% tariff on imported scarves.

**(a)** Calculate the price a customer in that country pays for one scarf after the tariff, assuming Marlow still receives 18. **[2]**
**(b)** Marlow expects sales volume to fall by 15%. Calculate the new annual revenue and the fall in revenue. **[3]**
**(c)** Outline one action Marlow could take in response. **[2]**

**9.** Identify the external factor (social, technological, environmental or political) shown in each statement.

**(i)** Newly elected leaders announce new rules on imports. **[1]**
**(ii)** More shoppers choose to order groceries through a phone application. **[1]**
**(iii)** The share of the population aged over 65 rises. **[1]**
**(iv)** Residents complain after waste from a factory pollutes a nearby river. **[1]**

**10.** Lorrenby Foods is a small business with annual sales of 540,000. Online orders are 12% of sales. The owner expects online orders to grow by 50% over the next year if customers keep switching to ordering through its website. Other sales stay the same.

**(a)** Calculate the value of online orders now, and after a 50% increase, and the rise. **[3]**
**(b)** Analyse how this technological and social change could affect Lorrenby Foods. **[5]**

**11.** Tarwick Plastics is a large business. It has a loan of 400,000 at 4%, and the rate is expected to rise to 7%. Its profit before interest is 95,000. A new environmental law will also require equipment costing 60,000 as a one-off payment. The directors are considering delaying a planned expansion.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly interest at 4% and 7%, and the extra cost. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Tarwick Plastics should delay its expansion. **[5]**

**12.** Kestrel Bikes needs 5,000 frames. An overseas supplier charges 52 each, but a 25% tariff applies to imports. A home supplier charges 61 each. The imported frames are lighter and sell at a higher price, but take six weeks to arrive. The home supplier delivers within two days.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of 5,000 frames from each supplier and the difference. **[3]**
**(b)** Justify which supplier Kestrel Bikes should choose. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: health care, education, policing, defence, public transport [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name two distinct services; two names for the same service, or a private business, gain no credit for the second.

**2.** Infrastructure is the basic physical networks, such as roads, rail, ports, power and communications, that businesses rely on [1]. A good road network cuts delivery times [1], so the firm can serve more customers or use less fuel and driver time [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The example must be tied to a delivery business; a general statement that infrastructure "is useful" does not answer the question set.

**3.** Tax revenue is limited [1], because raising taxes too far upsets voters and businesses [1], so the government cannot raise unlimited money to spend [1]. (Accept borrowing must be repaid with interest, or opportunity cost, with two developed points.) **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An "explain" answer gives one fact then expands it twice. Listing three constraints with no development spreads the points too thin.

**4. (a)** Tariff = 30 × 10% = 3 [1]. Cost = **33** [1].
**(b)** The import at 33 is still **cheaper** than 35 [1].
**(c)** The retailer's cost per padlock rises by 3 [1], so it must raise its price or accept a lower profit on each one [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs a comparison of the two numbers you have, so write both figures. A tariff does not always make imports dearer than home goods.

**5. (a)** 90,000 × 4% = **3,600** [1]; 90,000 × 7% = **6,300** [1].
**(b)** Extra = 6,300 − 3,600 = **2,700** [1]. Percentage increase = 2,700 ÷ 3,600 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1].
**(c)** Higher borrowing costs reduce profit [1], so it may delay buying new machines [1]. (Accept demand falling as customers borrow less.)
*Examiner insight:* The base for a percentage increase is the original bill, 3,600, not the new one. Dividing by 6,300 gives a different result.

**6.** Furniture is often bought on credit [1]. A rise in rates makes borrowing dearer, so monthly payments are higher [1], and fewer customers will buy, so sales fall [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the link to credit is the step that earns the explanation; "people have less money" with no mention of borrowing is a weaker chain.

**7. (a)** Tax = 250 × 8% = **20** [1]. Customer pays 250 + 20 = **270** [1].
**(b)** (80 − 90) ÷ 90 × 100 [1] = **−11.1%** (a fall of 11.1%) [1].
**(c)** Before: 90 × 250 = **22,500** [1]. After: 80 × 250 = **20,000** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the pre-tax price in (c), because the sales tax is passed to the government. Using 270 would overstate Sporlen's revenue.

**8. (a)** Tariff = 18 × 10% = 1.80 [1]. Customer pays **19.80** [1].
**(b)** New volume = 24,000 × 85% = 20,400 [1]. New revenue = 20,400 × 18 = **367,200** [1]. Old revenue = 432,000, so fall = **64,800** [1].
**(c)** Cut its price to offset the tariff [1], or find a market inside the bloc where no tariff applies [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), show the old revenue as well as the new, as the fall needs both figures. Identify the action in (c) and add why it helps.

**9.** (i) Political [1]. (ii) Technological [1]. (iii) Social [1]. (iv) Environmental [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* One word per statement is enough. Statement (iv) could be argued as social (resident complaints), but pollution is the stronger link, so name the factor the statement leads with.

**10. (a)** Now: 540,000 × 12% = **64,800** [1]. After: 64,800 × 1.5 = **97,200** [1]. Rise = **32,400** [1].
**(b)** More online orders widen the customer base beyond the local area [1], which could raise sales above the 540,000 level [1]. It may need to spend on a website, an application and delivery [1], which raises costs and could cut the profit made on each order [1]. A small business may have fewer staff to handle the extra work, so it must decide whether the extra sales outweigh the cost [1]. **[8 in total]**
*Examiner insight:* In "analyse", each point should be developed into a consequence for this business. Use the figures from (a) in the answer rather than leaving them behind.

**11. (a)** Interest at 4% = 400,000 × 4% = **16,000** [1]. At 7% = **28,000** [1]. Extra = **12,000** [1].
**(b)** Profit after interest falls from 79,000 at 4% to 67,000 at 7%, because 95,000 − 16,000 = 79,000 and 95,000 − 28,000 = 67,000 [1]. The one-off 60,000 for equipment comes on top, so the first-year extra burden is 72,000 [1]. An expansion would probably need more borrowing at the higher rate, which supports delay [1]. Against this, the equipment must be bought whatever is decided, as the law applies either way, and a delay could let rivals take market share [1]. Overall, Tarwick should delay the expansion while rates are expected to rise, because 72,000 is a large share of its 95,000 profit before interest, and it can restart once costs are clearer [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer needs a stated judgement supported by the figures. Presenting both sides without choosing one leaves the question unanswered.

**12. (a)** Imports: 52 × 1.25 = 65 each; 65 × 5,000 = **325,000** [1]. Home: 61 × 5,000 = **305,000** [1]. Difference = **20,000** [1].
**(b)** The home supplier is 20,000 cheaper, because the tariff erases the import's price advantage [1]. The imports are lighter, which could justify a higher selling price, but six weeks' delay ties up cash and risks missed sales [1]. Kestrel should choose the home supplier, because lower cost and quick delivery outweigh the benefit of the lighter frame unless the extra selling price exceeds 4 per frame [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "justify" answer must pick one option and give a reason drawn from the information provided; recommending without referring to the 20,000 or the delivery times does not use the data.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a general point about government policy without saying how it affects the business in the stem.
- Treating a tariff as a tax on exports instead of imports.
- Calculating the percentage increase from the new figure instead of the original.
- Confusing percentage points with percentage change when rates rise.
- Using the customer price including tax as the business's revenue.
- Listing external factors without linking one to a decision.
- Giving one side only in evaluate and justify questions, or stopping without a conclusion.
- Ignoring figures supplied in the case study.
