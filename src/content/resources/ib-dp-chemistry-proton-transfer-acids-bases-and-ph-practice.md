Data for every question: Kw = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ at 298 K. Ar values: C 12.01, Ca 40.08, O 16.00. Assume 298 K unless a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** Hydrogensulfate ions react with methanoate ions:

HSO₄⁻(aq) + HCOO⁻(aq) ⇌ SO₄²⁻(aq) + HCOOH(aq)

For the forward reaction, identify which species acts as the Brønsted–Lowry acid, which acts as the base, and the two conjugate acid–base pairs. **[3]**

**2.** Deduce the formula of:

**(a)** the conjugate base of each of NH₄⁺, HCOOH and H₂PO₄⁻ **[2]**
**(b)** the conjugate acid of each of CH₃NH₂, SO₄²⁻ and H₂O **[2]**

**3.** The hydrogenphosphate ion, HPO₄²⁻, is amphiprotic.

**(a)** State what amphiprotic means and which feature of HPO₄²⁻ lets it accept a proton. **[1]**
**(b)** Write equations for HPO₄²⁻ reacting with H₃O⁺(aq) and with OH⁻(aq). **[2]**
**(c)** Classify Na₂O, Al₂O₃ and P₄O₁₀ as acidic, basic or amphoteric, and write an equation for P₄O₁₀ reacting with water. **[3]**

**4.**

**(a)** A solution contains 4.6 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ of H⁺ ions. Calculate its pH. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate [H⁺] in a solution of pH 9.45. **[1]**
**(c)** A solution of nitric acid, concentration 0.0060 mol dm⁻³, is diluted by a factor of 100. Calculate the pH after dilution. **[2]**

**5.**

**(a)** Calculate the pH of 0.0350 mol dm⁻³ lithium hydroxide. **[2]**
**(b)** Three solutions at 298 K have: A, [H⁺] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³; B, [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³; C, [OH⁻] = 6.3 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³. Classify each as acidic, neutral or basic, using the relative values of [H⁺] and [OH⁻]. **[3]**

**6.** At 313 K, Kw = 2.9 × 10⁻¹⁴ (use this value).

**(a)** Calculate [H⁺] and the pH of pure water at 313 K. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this water is neutral even though its pH is not 7. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why Kw is larger at 313 K than at 298 K. **[2]**

**7.** A 0.050 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak monoprotic acid, HA, has pH 3.15.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of HA molecules that are ionized. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the pH of 0.050 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times greater [H⁺] is in the hydrochloric acid than in the HA solution. **[1]**
**(d)** A student describes HA as "a dilute acid" to explain its higher pH. Comment on this. **[2]**

**8.**

**(a)** Nitrous acid, HNO₂, is a stronger acid than ethanoic acid. Predict, with a reason, whether this equilibrium lies to the left or the right:
CH₃COOH(aq) + NO₂⁻(aq) ⇌ CH₃COO⁻(aq) + HNO₂(aq) **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why HBr is a stronger acid than HF. **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Write balanced equations for: (i) nitric acid with calcium carbonate; (ii) butanoic acid with potassium hydroxide; (iii) ammonia with hydrobromic acid; (iv) methylamine with nitric acid. **[5]**
**(b)** Identify the parent acid and base of lithium methanoate and of magnesium sulfate. **[2]**

**10.** 20.00 cm³ of 0.0600 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide is placed in a flask and titrated with 0.150 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid, while pH is recorded with a probe.

**(a)** Calculate the pH at the start. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of acid needed to reach the equivalence point. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the pH after 20.00 cm³ of acid has been added. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe the shape of the pH curve, giving the pH-axis intercept, the equivalence point and the pH the curve approaches. **[2]**

**11.** A lake affected by acid rain has pH 4.30. A sample of unpolluted rain from the same area has pH 5.60.

**(a)** Sulfur trioxide in the air dissolves in rainwater. Write the equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater [H⁺] is in the lake than in the unpolluted rain. **[2]**
**(c)** Powdered limestone, CaCO₃, is added to the lake. Write an ionic equation for its reaction with H⁺(aq) and identify the Brønsted–Lowry base. **[2]**
**(d)** The lake holds 2.0 × 10⁶ dm³ of water. Assuming only the free H⁺ must be neutralized, calculate the mass of CaCO₃ needed. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** HSO₄⁻ donates a proton, so it is the acid [1]. HCOO⁻ accepts it, so it is the base [1]. Pairs: **HSO₄⁻ / SO₄²⁻ and HCOOH / HCOO⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write each pair acid first and include charges; HSO₄⁻ / HCOOH differs by more than one proton, so it is not a conjugate pair at all.

**2. (a)** NH₃ and HCOO⁻ [1]; **HPO₄²⁻** [1]
**(b)** CH₃NH₃⁺ and HSO₄⁻ [1]; **H₃O⁺** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check every charge: adding H⁺ raises it by one, removing H⁺ lowers it by one. A correct formula with the wrong charge is a wrong species.

**3. (a)** It can act as a Brønsted–Lowry acid or base; it has lone pairs on its oxygen atoms that can accept a proton [1]
**(b)** HPO₄²⁻(aq) + H₃O⁺(aq) → H₂PO₄⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]; HPO₄²⁻(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → PO₄³⁻(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]
**(c)** Na₂O basic, Al₂O₃ amphoteric, P₄O₁₀ acidic [1] [1]; **P₄O₁₀(s) + 6H₂O(l) → 4H₃PO₄(aq)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (a), name both roles, donor and acceptor; mentioning only one leaves the definition incomplete.

**4. (a)** pH = −log₁₀(4.6 × 10⁻³) = **2.34** [1]
**(b)** [H⁺] = 10⁻⁹·⁴⁵ = **3.5 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(c)** After dilution [H⁺] = 0.0060 / 100 = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **4.22** (up 2 units from 2.22) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the log expression you used; a bare pH that is slightly off leaves no method for an examiner to reward.

**5. (a)** [OH⁻] = 0.0350 mol dm⁻³, so [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 0.0350 = 2.9 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.54** [1]
**(b)** A: [OH⁻] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁹, so [H⁺] > [OH⁻], **acidic** [1]. B: [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ = [OH⁻], **neutral** [1]. C: [H⁺] = 1.6 × 10⁻¹¹ < [OH⁻], **basic** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Compare [H⁺] with [OH⁻] as asked: work out the missing concentration each time rather than quoting pH alone.

**6. (a)** [H⁺] = [OH⁻], so [H⁺] = √(2.9 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 1.7 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **6.77** [1]
**(b)** [H⁺] still equals [OH⁻], which is what neutral means [1]
**(c)** The ionization of water is endothermic [1]; raising the temperature shifts the equilibrium to the right, increasing [H⁺] and [OH⁻], so Kw rises [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Neutral" is defined by equal concentrations; an answer that just says "pH 7 is neutral" contradicts the calculation and loses the explanation.

**7. (a)** [H⁺] = 10⁻³·¹⁵ = 7.1 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; percentage ionized = (7.1 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.050) × 100 = **1.4%** [1]
**(b)** HCl is fully ionized, [H⁺] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³, pH = **1.30** [1]
**(c)** 0.050 / 7.08 × 10⁻⁴ = **71 times** [1]
**(d)** The two acids have the same concentration, so "dilute" does not explain the difference [1]; HA is weak, only partly ionized, so it releases fewer H⁺ ions [1]
*Examiner insight:* Strength and concentration are separate ideas; an answer that swaps "weak" and "dilute" misses the exact point the question is testing.

**8. (a)** HNO₂ is the stronger acid, so CH₃COOH is the weaker acid [1]; CH₃COO⁻ is then the stronger base and NO₂⁻ the weaker base [1]; the equilibrium lies towards the weaker acid and base, so **to the left** [1]
**(b)** The H–Br bond is longer and weaker than the H–F bond [1], so HBr loses its proton (ionizes) more readily in water [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), link bond strength to ease of losing a proton; quoting "Br is less electronegative" alone does not explain the trend.

**9. (a)** (i) 2HNO₃(aq) + CaCO₃(s) → Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) [1] [1]
(ii) CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH(aq) + KOH(aq) → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOK(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]
(iii) NH₃(aq) + HBr(aq) → NH₄Br(aq) [1]
(iv) CH₃NH₂(aq) + HNO₃(aq) → CH₃NH₃NO₃(aq) [1]
**(b)** Lithium methanoate: **methanoic acid and lithium hydroxide** [1]. Magnesium sulfate: **sulfuric acid and magnesium oxide or hydroxide** (or carbonate) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (i) check all three products, including CO₂, and that the nitrate ions balance; in (iii) and (iv) a water product makes the equation wrong.

**10. (a)** [OH⁻] = 0.0600 mol dm⁻³; [H⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 0.0600 = 1.7 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.78** [1]
**(b)** n(NaOH) = 0.0600 × 0.02000 = 1.20 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; V(HCl) = 1.20 × 10⁻³ / 0.150 = 8.00 × 10⁻³ dm³ = **8.00 cm³** [1]
**(c)** n(HCl) = 0.150 × 0.02000 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol; excess H⁺ = 1.80 × 10⁻³ mol in 40.00 cm³, [H⁺] = 0.045 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.35** [1]
**(d)** Starts at pH 12.78 and falls slowly, then drops almost vertically around 8.00 cm³, passing pH 7 at the equivalence point [1]; then levels off, approaching but staying above pH 0.82 (the pH of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ HCl) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the total volume is 40.00 cm³, not 20.00 cm³; leaving out the added acid volume overstates [H⁺] and gives the wrong pH.

**11. (a)** **SO₃(g) + H₂O(l) → H₂SO₄(aq)**, giving sulfuric acid [1]
**(b)** Ratio = 10⁻⁴·³⁰ / 10⁻⁵·⁶⁰ = 10¹·³⁰ [1] = **20 times** [1]
**(c)** CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g) [1]; the base is the carbonate ion, **CO₃²⁻** (in CaCO₃) [1]
**(d)** [H⁺] = 10⁻⁴·³⁰ = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³; n(H⁺) = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁶ = 1.0 × 10² mol [1]; n(CaCO₃) = 50 mol [1]; mass = 50 × 100.09 = **5.0 × 10³ g (5.0 kg)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A pH difference of 1.30 is not "1.3 times"; convert both pH values to [H⁺] or use 10 to the power of the difference.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Pairing a reactant with a product that differs by more than one proton, or listing the base first in a pair.
- Writing a conjugate with the wrong charge.
- Using "amphoteric" and "amphiprotic" as if they mean the same.
- For alkalis, taking −log₁₀[OH⁻] and calling it pH.
- Treating a pH difference as a simple ratio rather than a power of 10.
- Forgetting CO₂ with carbonates, or adding water with ammonia and amines.
- Ignoring the extra volume of titrant when finding a concentration after mixing.
- Sketching a pH curve whose steep section is not vertical, or whose end plateau reaches the pH of the titrant.
- Saying neutral water must have pH 7 at all temperatures.
