Here you practise section 4.1.1, Basic concepts of organic chemistry, of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The questions test every learning outcome, 4.1.1(a) to (i), from Module 4, Core organic chemistry. H432 is the full A level. Questions 8, 10 and 11 are longer and draw on several outcomes at once.

## Questions

**1.** Give the systematic name of each compound.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(b)** CH₃CHClCH(CH₃)CH₂OH **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH=C(CH₃)CH₃ **[1]**

**2.** Write the structural formula of each compound.

**(a)** 4-ethyl-2-methylheptane **[1]**
**(b)** but-3-en-2-ol **[1]**
**(c)** 1,1,2-trichloroethane **[1]**

**3.** For each compound, state whether it is aliphatic (but not alicyclic), alicyclic or aromatic, and whether it is saturated or unsaturated.

**(a)** methylcyclohexane **[1]**
**(b)** ethylbenzene **[1]**
**(c)** CH₃CH₂C≡CH **[1]**
**(d)** cyclopentene **[1]**

**4.**

**(a)** Define the term homologous series. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is meant by a functional group. **[1]**
**(c)** Give the general formula of an alkyl group and the name of the alkyl group with four carbon atoms. **[1]**

**5.**

**(a)** A chloroalkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₁Cl has Mᵣ = 120.5. Find its molecular formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Predict the molecular formula of the alcohol in the series CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH that has twelve carbon atoms. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that every alkene with one C=C bond has the same percentage by mass of carbon, and give that value to three significant figures. **[2]**

**6.** Ethyl propanoate is an ester. Its molecule has a CH₃CH₂ group bonded to a C=O carbon, which is bonded through an O atom to a CH₂CH₃ group.

**(a)** State the molecular formula of ethyl propanoate. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the empirical formula. **[1]**
**(c)** Write its structural formula, showing the ester group the way the specification expects. **[1]**
**(d)** State which atoms a skeletal formula of ethyl propanoate leaves out. **[1]**

**7.** Heptane, C₇H₁₆, has several structural isomers.

**(a)** Explain the term structural isomers. **[1]**
**(b)** Five isomers of C₇H₁₆ have a longest chain of exactly five carbon atoms. Name all five. **[5]**

**8.** Alcohol P belongs to the series CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH and has Mᵣ = 102.0.

**(a)** Find the molecular formula of P. **[2]**
**(b)** Three isomers of P have an unbranched carbon chain. Give the structural formula and name of each. **[3]**
**(c)** A student names one of these "hexan-4-ol". Explain the error and state the correct name. **[1]**
**(d)** Give the structural formula of 2-methylpentan-2-ol and state, with a reason, whether it is a structural isomer of P. **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Describe homolytic fission of a covalent bond. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the homolytic fission of the C–C bond in ethane. **[1]**
**(c)** The C–I bond in 2-iodo-2-methylbutane, CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂I, breaks heterolytically. Write an equation for this fission, show the dipole on the bond, and explain which atom receives the bonding pair. **[3]**
**(d)** From this list, identify every radical: Br•, H⁺, (CH₃)₃C•, CN⁻, Cl⁻. **[1]**

**10.** Ammonia reacts with bromomethane in one step to form CH₃NH₃⁺ and Br⁻.

**(a)** State what a curly arrow represents. **[1]**
**(b)** Show the dipole on the C–Br bond and explain its origin. **[2]**
**(c)** A student's mechanism has two arrows. Arrow 1 starts on an H atom of NH₃ and ends on the carbon atom. Arrow 2 starts on the Br atom and ends on the carbon atom. Identify the error in each arrow and describe the correct arrow. **[4]**

**11.** Compound W contains 24.4% carbon, 3.4% hydrogen and 72.2% chlorine by mass. Its Mᵣ is 147.5.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of W. **[3]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of W. **[1]**
**(c)** W is one of five structural isomers with this molecular formula, all on an unbranched three-carbon chain. Name all five. **[5]**
**(d)** State whether W is saturated or unsaturated, and give a reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Six-carbon chain, two methyls on C3: **3,3-dimethylhexane** [1]
**(b)** Four-carbon chain containing C–OH, numbered from the OH end: **3-chloro-2-methylbutan-1-ol** [1]
**(c)** Number from the end nearest the C=C: **2-methylpent-2-ene** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), numbering from the CH₃ end gives the substituents the same locants, {2, 3}, but puts the OH on C4; the suffix group takes priority, so "2-chloro-3-methylbutan-4-ol" is unlikely to gain credit.

**2. (a)** **CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH(C₂H₅)CH₂CH₂CH₃** [1]
**(b)** **CH₂=CHCH(OH)CH₃** [1]
**(c)** **CHCl₂CH₂Cl** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A structural formula must be unambiguous; C₄H₈O for (b) is a molecular formula and describes several compounds, so it does not answer the question.

**3. (a)** Alicyclic, saturated [1]
**(b)** Aromatic, unsaturated [1]
**(c)** Aliphatic, unsaturated (C≡C) [1]
**(d)** Alicyclic, unsaturated (C=C in the ring) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Here each mark needs both parts right; calling cyclopentene aromatic because it is a ring is a common way to lose (d).

**4. (a)** A series of organic compounds with the same functional group [1], each successive member differing by CH₂ [1]
**(b)** The group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound [1]
**(c)** CₙH₂ₙ₊₁; **butyl** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Similar chemical properties" is not a substitute for "the same functional group"; the specification's definition names the functional group and the CH₂ difference.

**5. (a)** Mᵣ = 14n + 1 + 35.5, so 14n + 36.5 = 120.5 [1]; n = 6, **C₆H₁₃Cl** [1]
**(b)** 2(12) + 1 = 25 hydrogens on the chain: **C₁₂H₂₅OH** [1]
**(c)** % C = (12n ÷ 14n) × 100, and n cancels [1]; **85.7%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" part, quoting 85.7% from one example such as ethene does not show it holds for every member; the algebra with n is the point.

**6. (a)** **C₅H₁₀O₂** [1]
**(b)** The ratio 5 : 10 : 2 has no common factor, so **C₅H₁₀O₂** [1]
**(c)** **CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃** [1]
**(d)** The hydrogen atoms bonded to carbon in the alkyl chains [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), dividing by 2 gives non-whole numbers, which is not a formula; when no common factor exists, the empirical formula equals the molecular formula.

**7. (a)** Compounds with an identical molecular formula whose structural formulae differ [1]
**(b)** **2,2-dimethylpentane** [1]; **2,3-dimethylpentane** [1]; **2,4-dimethylpentane** [1]; **3,3-dimethylpentane** [1]; **3-ethylpentane** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Names such as "2-ethylpentane" or "4,4-dimethylpentane" describe chains numbered or chosen wrongly; each is really another listed isomer or a hexane, so it adds nothing to the list.

**8. (a)** Mᵣ = 14n + 18 = 102.0 [1]; n = 6, **C₆H₁₃OH** [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂OH, hexan-1-ol** [1]; **CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃, hexan-2-ol** [1]; **CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, hexan-3-ol** [1]
**(c)** The chain is numbered from the wrong end; the OH must get the lowest number, so it is **hexan-3-ol** [1]
**(d)** **(CH₃)₂C(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃** and it is a structural isomer: same molecular formula C₆H₁₄O, different structure [1] [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), drawing hexan-1-ol a second time with the OH on the far end looks like a new isomer but is the same compound; naming each structure exposes the repeat.

**9. (a)** Each bonding atom receives one electron from the shared pair, forming two radicals [1]
**(b)** **CH₃–CH₃ → CH₃• + CH₃•** [1]
**(c)** CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂–I with Cδ+ and Iδ− [1]; **CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)₂I → CH₃CH₂C⁺(CH₃)₂ + I⁻** [1]; iodine takes both electrons because it is more electronegative than carbon [1]
**(d)** **Br• and (CH₃)₃C•** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), writing CH₃⁺ and CH₃⁻ describes heterolytic fission; the question asks for homolytic, and the dots on both products are what show it.

**10. (a)** The movement of a pair of electrons [1]
**(b)** Cδ+–Brδ− [1]; bromine is more electronegative than carbon, so it attracts the bonding pair more strongly [1]
**(c)** Arrow 1: an H atom has no lone pair to donate [1]; the arrow should start from the lone pair on N and end on the δ+ carbon [1]. Arrow 2: it points the wrong way and starts on an atom [1]; it should start from the C–Br bond and end on the Br atom [1]
*Examiner insight:* Examiners look at exactly where each arrow tail sits; a tail that starts near the N but not on the lone pair or a charge can read as starting from the atom.

**11. (a)** C: 24.4 ÷ 12.0 = 2.03; H: 3.4 ÷ 1.0 = 3.4; Cl: 72.2 ÷ 35.5 = 2.03 [1]; ratio 1 : 1.67 : 1 [1]; multiply by 3: **C₃H₅Cl₃** [1]
**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 36.0 + 5.0 + 106.5 = 147.5, equal to Mᵣ, so **C₃H₅Cl₃** [1]
**(c)** **1,1,1-trichloropropane** [1]; **1,1,2-trichloropropane** [1]; **1,1,3-trichloropropane** [1]; **1,2,2-trichloropropane** [1]; **1,2,3-trichloropropane** [1]
**(d)** Saturated: only single C–C bonds; the formula fits CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ with three H replaced by Cl [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 1.67 to 2 in (a) gives CH₂Cl, which does not fit the Mᵣ of 147.5; a ratio ending near .33 or .67 is a signal to multiply by 3.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Picking a chain that is not the longest, as in questions 1 and 7.
- Numbering from the end that suits the substituents instead of the OH or C=C, as in question 8.
- Writing prefixes in number order rather than alphabetical order.
- Giving a molecular formula when a structural formula is asked for.
- Calling an alicyclic alkene aromatic.
- Leaving "same functional group" or "differ by CH₂" out of the homologous series definition.
- Listing the same isomer twice under two names.
- Dropping the dots on radicals or writing them as ions.
- Starting a curly arrow on an atom, or pointing it away from where the pair goes.
- Rounding a mole ratio such as 1.67 instead of scaling it.
