Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.3.2 Gas exchange**. Full explanations, including the counter-current principle and a spirometer calculation, are in the [gas exchange study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-gas-exchange-aqa-alevel-biology/). Test yourself with the [gas exchange practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-gas-exchange-aqa-alevel-biology-practice/).

## The three-feature checklist

For any gas exchange surface, say which of these it provides:

1. **Large surface area**
2. **Short diffusion distance**
3. **Maintained concentration gradient** (ventilation, blood flow or use of the gas)

## Four systems at a glance

| Organism | Surface | Large area | Short distance | Gradient kept by |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Single-celled | Cell-surface membrane | High SA:V | Small cell | Respiration using oxygen |
| Insect | Tracheoles | Many, branched | Tracheoles reach cells | Respiration; abdominal pumping |
| Fish | Gill lamellae on filaments | Many lamellae on many filaments | Thin epithelium, capillaries near surface | Counter-current flow; constant water and blood flow |
| Dicot leaf | Mesophyll cell surfaces | Many cells exposed to air spaces | Thin leaf; air spaces | Photosynthesis or respiration; diffusion through stomata |
| Human | Alveolar epithelium | Very many alveoli | Epithelium and capillary wall each one cell thick | Ventilation and blood flow |

## Insect tracheal system

- Spiracles (open/close) → tracheae (chitin rings keep them open) → tracheoles (thin, reach cells).
- Gas diffuses down gradients; ventilation by abdominal movements in some insects.
- Active muscle: lactate lowers water potential → water leaves tracheole ends by osmosis → more air-filled surface near muscle.
- Oxygen delivered directly to tissues, not carried by blood.

## Fish gills

- Gill filaments carry many gill lamellae.
- Blood flows through lamellae opposite to water flow: **counter-current principle**.
- Blood always meets water with a higher oxygen concentration → gradient along the whole lamella.
- Parallel flow would reach equilibrium partway along, after which no more oxygen diffuses in.

## Leaves

- Stomata (mostly lower epidermis) → air spaces in spongy mesophyll → mesophyll cell surfaces.
- No ventilation or gas transport needed: every cell is close to air.
- Guard cells close stomata to cut water loss.

## Gas exchange against water loss

| Insects | Xerophytes |
|---|---|
| Waxy cuticle | Thick waxy cuticle |
| Spiracles close | Sunken stomata in pits |
| Hairs around spiracles | Hairs and rolled leaves |
| Small SA:V | Fewer stomata; needle-like leaves |

Key wording: "traps moist air, which reduces the water potential gradient, so less water diffuses out."

## Human system

- Trachea → two bronchi → bronchioles → alveoli, in two lungs.
- Alveolar epithelium: one layer of flattened cells; capillary wall one cell thick.
- Very many alveoli; dense capillary network.
- Ventilation keeps alveolar oxygen high and carbon dioxide low; blood flow removes oxygen and brings carbon dioxide.

## Breathing

**Inspiration:** external intercostals contract, internal relax → ribs up and out; diaphragm contracts and flattens → thoracic volume up → pressure falls below atmospheric → air in.

**Expiration at rest:** external intercostals and diaphragm relax; lungs recoil → volume down → pressure above atmospheric → air out.

**Forced expiration:** internal intercostals contract, pulling ribs down and in.

External and internal intercostals are **antagonistic**.

## Data skills

- Tidal volume: volume per normal breath.
- Breathing rate: breaths per minute = 60 ÷ time for one breath in seconds.
- Pulmonary ventilation = tidal volume × breathing rate.
- Disease reasoning: thicker walls → longer distance; fewer or damaged alveoli → less surface area; narrow airways → less airflow; less elasticity → harder to breathe out.
- Correlation ≠ causation: name a confounding factor; ask for a mechanism or controlled evidence.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Spiracle | Pore in an insect's body surface leading to the tracheae |
| Tracheole | Narrow, thin-walled branch of a trachea that reaches the tissues |
| Gill lamella | Thin plate on a gill filament where gas exchange occurs |
| Counter-current principle | Blood and water flow in opposite directions, maintaining a gradient along the whole exchange surface |
| Xerophyte | Plant adapted to limit water loss in dry conditions |
| Alveolar epithelium | Single layer of flattened cells lining an alveolus |
| Antagonistic muscles | Pair of muscles that produce opposite movements |
| Tidal volume | Volume of air in or out in one normal breath |

## Exam traps

- Internal intercostals do not contract during normal inspiration.
- "Lungs suck air in" earns nothing; describe volume and pressure.
- Insects do not use blood to carry oxygen to tissues.
- "Thin walls" needs "short diffusion distance" to score.
- Counter-current answers must explain the gradient along the whole lamella.
- Correlation in smoking data does not prove causation.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three parts of the insect tracheal system in the order air passes through them.
2. Explain how lactate production increases gas exchange in insect flight muscle.
3. What is the counter-current principle?
4. Why does parallel flow give a lower oxygen uptake than counter-current flow?
5. Give two ways a dicot leaf is adapted for gas exchange.
6. Explain how sunken stomata reduce water loss.
7. List the airways from trachea to alveoli.
8. Give two features of the alveolar epithelium that speed up diffusion.
9. Describe what the diaphragm does during inspiration.
10. Which intercostal muscles contract in forced expiration?
11. A breath takes 4 s and tidal volume is 0.5 dm³. Calculate pulmonary ventilation.
12. Why does breakdown of alveolar walls reduce gas exchange?

### Answers

1. Spiracles, tracheae, tracheoles.
2. Lactate lowers the water potential of muscle cells, so water moves out of the tracheoles by osmosis; more of each tracheole fills with air, giving faster diffusion of oxygen to the muscle.
3. Blood and water flow across the gill lamellae in opposite directions.
4. In parallel flow, oxygen concentrations in blood and water become equal partway along, so diffusion stops; in counter-current flow a gradient is kept along the whole lamella.
5. Any two: many stomata; air spaces in spongy mesophyll; large surface area of mesophyll cells; thin leaf so short distance.
6. Moist air is trapped in the pit, reducing the water potential gradient, so less water vapour diffuses out.
7. Trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli.
8. Any two: one cell thick (flattened cells); next to capillaries with walls one cell thick; very large total surface area.
9. It contracts and flattens, increasing the volume of the thoracic cavity.
10. Internal intercostal muscles.
11. Rate = 60 ÷ 4 = 15 breaths per minute; 0.5 × 15 = **7.5 dm³ per minute**.
12. It reduces the surface area of the alveoli, so less oxygen diffuses into the blood per unit time.
