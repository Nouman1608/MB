## Section A

**1.** Write down whether each angle is acute, obtuse, reflex or a right angle.

**(a)** 172° **[1]**

**(b)** 89° **[1]**

**(c)** 205° **[1]**

**2.** Three angles on a straight line are 36°, 3x° and x°. Work out the value of x. **[2]**

**3.** Four angles at a point are x°, 2x°, 3x° and 150°. Work out the value of x. **[2]**

**4.** Each base angle of an isosceles triangle is 34°. Work out the third angle. **[2]**

**5.** A right-angled triangle has one angle of 41°. Work out the size of the third angle. **[1]**

**6.** Two interior angles of a triangle are 47° and 85°. Work out the exterior angle at the third vertex. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** AB and CD are parallel lines, with AB above CD. A and C are at the left-hand ends. A transversal crosses AB at P and CD at Q. Angle APQ = (3x + 10)° and angle PQD = (5x − 30)°.

**(a)** Give the name of the pair of angles APQ and PQD. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the value of x and the size of angle APQ. **[2]**

**8.** Using the same parallel lines and transversal as question 7, a different diagram has angle BPQ = (2y + 16)° and angle PQD = (3y − 1)°.

**(a)** Explain why 5y + 15 = 180. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the size of angle PQD. **[1]**

**9.** In triangle PQR, PQ = PR. Angle PQR = (2x + 10)° and angle QPR = (x + 20)°. Work out the size of each angle of the triangle. **[3]**

**10.** In triangle ABC, angle BAC = (x + 30)° and angle ABC = (2x − 10)°. Side BC is extended to D. The exterior angle ACD = (4x − 20)°.

**(a)** Work out the value of x. **[3]**

**(b)** Show that triangle ABC is isosceles. **[1]**

**11.** Prove that the angles of a triangle add up to 180°. You may use facts about parallel lines and straight lines. **[3]**

**12.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC and angle BAC = 40°. D is a point on AC such that BD = BC. Work out the size of angle ABD. Give reasons for each stage of your working. **[4]**

**13.** Two straight lines cross. One of the angles formed is (4x + 12)° and the angle vertically opposite it is (6x − 20)°.

**(a)** Work out the value of x. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the size of each of the other two angles formed. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Obtuse** [1].

**(b)** **Acute** [1].

**(c)** **Reflex** [1].

**2.** 36 + 3x + x = 180 (angles on a straight line add up to 180°) [1]; 4x = 144, **x = 36** [1].

**3.** x + 2x + 3x + 150 = 360 (angles at a point add up to 360°) [1]; 6x = 210, **x = 35** [1].

**4.** 180 − 2 × 34 [1] = **112°** [1].

**5.** 90 − 41 = **49°** [1].

**6.** 47 + 85 [1] = **132°** (the exterior angle equals the sum of the interior opposite angles) [1]. (Or 180 − 132 = 48° for the interior angle, then 180 − 48.)

**7. (a)** **Alternate angles** [1].

**(b)** Alternate angles are equal, so 3x + 10 = 5x − 30 [1]; 2x = 40, x = 20, and angle APQ = **70°** [1].

**8. (a)** Angles BPQ and PQD are allied angles (on the same side of the transversal, between the parallel lines) [1], so they add up to 180°: 2y + 16 + 3y − 1 = 180, which is 5y + 15 = 180 [1].

**(b)** 5y = 165, y = 33, so angle PQD = 99 − 1 = **98°** [1].

**9.** PQ = PR, so angle PRQ = angle PQR = (2x + 10)° (base angles of an isosceles triangle) [1]; (x + 20) + 2(2x + 10) = 180, so 5x + 40 = 180 and x = 28 [1]; **angle QPR = 48°, angle PQR = angle PRQ = 66°** [1].

**10. (a)** Exterior angle = sum of the interior opposite angles [1]: (x + 30) + (2x − 10) = 4x − 20 [1]; 3x + 20 = 4x − 20, **x = 40** [1].

**(b)** Angle BAC = 70° and angle ABC = 70°; two angles are equal, so the triangle is isosceles (angle ACB = 180 − 140 = 40°) [1].

**11.** Draw a line through one vertex, A, parallel to the opposite side BC [1]. The angles between this line and AB, and between this line and AC, are equal to angles B and C, because alternate angles are equal [1]. These two angles and angle A lie on a straight line, so angles A + B + C = 180° [1].

**12.** Angle ABC = angle ACB = (180 − 40) ÷ 2 = 70° (base angles of isosceles triangle ABC; angles in a triangle add up to 180°) [1]. In triangle BDC, BD = BC, so angle BDC = angle BCD = 70° (base angles of an isosceles triangle) [1]. Angle DBC = 180 − 70 − 70 = 40° (angles in a triangle add up to 180°) [1]. Angle ABD = 70 − 40 = **30°** [1].

**13. (a)** Vertically opposite angles are equal, so 4x + 12 = 6x − 20 [1]; 2x = 32, **x = 16** [1].

**(b)** The angle is 4(16) + 12 = 76°, so the other two are each 180 − 76 = **104°** (angles on a straight line) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1(a): calling 172° reflex because it is "nearly a straight line".
- Question 4: halving 180 − 34 instead of subtracting both base angles.
- Question 7: writing "Z angles" as the name. The name is alternate angles.
- Question 8: setting the allied angles equal instead of adding them to 180°.
- Question 9: making angle QPR equal to angle PQR. The equal angles are opposite the equal sides PQ and PR, so they are at Q and R.
- Question 10: adding the exterior angle to the two interior opposite angles and setting the total to 180°.
- Question 11: checking one triangle with a protractor, which does not prove the result for every triangle.
- Question 12: giving the answer 30° with no reasons, or with one reason at the end only.
- Question 13(b): giving only one of the two remaining angles.

## Setting out a reasons answer

In question 12 each line has a value and a reason. Write the reason after the value, in words, using the standard phrases: "base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal", "angles in a triangle add up to 180°". Name the triangle you are working in. Short, precise reasons are better than long explanations: the examiner is checking that each fact you use is the right one.
