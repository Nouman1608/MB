## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the main process of thermal energy transfer in each case.

**(a)** along a metal rod with one end in a flame **[1]**
**(b)** from the Sun to the Earth through space **[1]**
**(c)** through the water in a kettle heated from below **[1]**

**2.** Choose from: dull black, shiny silver. State which surface is:

**(a)** the best absorber of infrared radiation **[1]**
**(b)** the best reflector of infrared radiation. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why a wooden spoon, rather than a metal one, is used to stir hot soup. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the heating element of an electric kettle is at the bottom. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why energy from the Sun cannot reach the Earth by conduction or convection. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why metals are much better thermal conductors than non-metal solids such as glass. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A piece of ice is weighted so that it sinks to the bottom of a test tube of cold water. The tube is heated near the top.

**(a)** Describe what is observed. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the tube is heated at the top rather than the bottom. **[1]**
**(c)** State what the experiment shows about water. **[1]**

**8.** This question is about convection in air.

**(a)** Describe an experiment to show a convection current in air. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the observations in terms of density. **[3]**

**9.** A family wants to heat a room efficiently.

**(a)** Explain why the heater should be placed near the floor. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how thick curtains drawn over a window at night reduce the energy lost through the window. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A student compares two identical metal cans, one painted dull black and one polished shiny. Each is filled with hot water at 80 °C. After 10 minutes the black can's water is at 62 °C and the shiny can's water is at 71 °C.

**(a)** State **three** things the student must keep the same. **[3]**
**(b)** State which surface is the better emitter, and use the data to justify your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** State how the rate of emission from each can changes as the water cools. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A dark-coloured car is parked in bright sunshine.

**(a)** Explain why its temperature rises at first. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why its temperature eventually stops rising. **[2]**
**(c)** The Earth's average temperature depends on a similar balance. Explain how an increase in greenhouse gases in the atmosphere affects the Earth's temperature. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* This question is about applications in which more than one type of transfer is important.

**(a)** Describe how thermal energy is transferred from a coal fire to a person sitting in front of it, and to the air outside the house. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how a car radiator transfers energy from the hot coolant to the air. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** conduction [1] **(b)** radiation [1] **(c)** convection [1]

**2. (a)** dull black [1] **(b)** shiny silver [1]

**3.** Wood is a poor thermal conductor (an insulator) [1]; so little energy is conducted to the hand and the handle stays cool [1].

**4.** Water near the element is heated, expands and becomes less dense [1]; it rises and cooler, denser water sinks to the element [1]; a convection current forms, heating all the water [1].

**5.** Space is a vacuum: there is no medium [1]; conduction and convection both need particles, but radiation does not [1].

**6.** Metals contain free (delocalised) electrons [1]; these move quickly through the metal and carry energy to colder parts, in addition to the slower lattice vibrations that all solids have [1].

**7. (a)** The water at the top boils [1]; the ice at the bottom stays unmelted (for some time) [1].
**(b)** Hot water stays at the top, so convection cannot carry energy down to the ice [1].
**(c)** Water is a poor thermal conductor [1].

**8. (a)** A box with a glass front has two chimneys [1]; a lit candle stands under one chimney [1]; smoke from smouldering paper is held over the other chimney and its path is watched [1].
**(b)** The air above the candle is heated, expands and becomes less dense, so it rises up the chimney [1]; cooler, denser air is drawn in to replace it [1]; so smoke is drawn down the other chimney, across the box and up above the candle [1].

**9. (a)** Air next to the heater is heated, expands and becomes less dense [1]; it rises and cooler, denser air sinks to replace it [1]; a convection current carries warm air round the whole room [1].
**(b)** A layer of air is trapped between the curtain and the window [1]; trapped air is a poor conductor and cannot circulate, so convection currents near the cold glass are reduced [1].

**10. (a)** Any three: same volume of water; same starting temperature; same size and type of can; same surroundings (room temperature, no draughts); same time; lids on both or neither [1 each]
**(b)** Dull black [1]; its water fell by 18 °C, twice as much as the shiny can's 9 °C in the same time [1].
**(c)** It decreases (a cooler surface emits more slowly) [1].

**11. (a)** The dark surface is a good absorber of the Sun's radiation [1]; it receives energy faster than it transfers energy away, so its temperature rises [1].
**(b)** As it gets hotter it emits radiation (and loses energy to the air) faster [1]; when the rate of losing energy equals the rate of receiving energy, the temperature stays constant [1].
**(c)** Greenhouse gases absorb some of the infrared emitted by the Earth's surface and send some back [1]; less energy escapes to space, so the Earth warms until the rates balance again at a higher temperature [1].

**12. (a)** The glowing coal emits infrared radiation, which travels across the room and is absorbed by the person [1]; hot gases from the fire expand, become less dense and rise up the chimney by convection [1], carrying energy to the outside air [1]; some energy is conducted through the metal grate and the hearth [1].
**(b)** Energy is conducted from the coolant through the thin metal walls of the tubes and fins [1]; air flowing over the large surface area of the fins carries the energy away by convection [1].
*Examiner insight:* Despite its name, the radiator's main processes are conduction and convection; an answer based only on radiation scores little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "heat rises" instead of "warm, less dense fluid rises".
- Saying convection can happen in a solid.
- Calling a shiny surface a good absorber or emitter.
- *(Extended)* Explaining conduction in metals without free electrons.
- *(Extended)* Saying an object at constant temperature receives no energy.
