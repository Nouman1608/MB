> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **20.1 Food supply** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Everything in this sub-topic is Core, so all candidates should attempt every question. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [food supply study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-supply-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [food supply revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-supply-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which method of increasing food production reduces competition between crop plants and weeds?

A: herbicides

B: insecticides

C: agricultural machinery

D: selective breeding

**[1]**

**2.** Which mineral ion do plants need to make chlorophyll?

A: calcium

B: magnesium

C: nitrate

D: sodium

**[1]**

**3.** State how agricultural machinery has helped humans to increase food production. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how insecticides improve the quality of a crop of apples. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** resources for which weeds compete with crop plants. **[2]**

**6.** Give **one** example of how selective breeding has improved production by crop plants. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A farmer added different amounts of nitrogen fertiliser to five plots of wheat. The table shows the yields.

| Fertiliser added / kg per hectare | 0 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Yield / tonnes per hectare | 3.1 | 5.0 | 6.4 | 7.0 | 7.1 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of the fertiliser on yield. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield when 100 kg per hectare was added, compared with no fertiliser. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why the farmer might choose to add 150 kg per hectare rather than 200 kg per hectare. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why adding nitrate increases the yield of wheat. **[2]**

**8.** A field of carrots was divided into two halves. One half was sprayed with herbicide; the other half was not. The yield from the sprayed half was 5.6 tonnes per hectare and from the unsprayed half 4.2 tonnes per hectare.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in yield in the sprayed half. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why spraying with herbicide increased the yield. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** property the herbicide must have for use on this crop. **[1]**

**9.** A country grows a single variety of banana over many thousands of hectares.

**(a)** Describe **two** advantages of growing bananas as a large-scale monoculture. **[2]**

**(b)** A fungal disease of bananas arrives in the country. Explain why it could spread rapidly and destroy much of the crop. **[3]**

**10.** The table compares pigs reared by two methods.

| | Free-range | Intensive |
|---|---|---|
| Feed needed for 1 kg of growth / kg | 4.0 | 2.8 |
| Space per pig / m² | 40 | 1.0 |

**(a)** Calculate how much less feed an intensive pig needs than a free-range pig to gain 90 kg. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why intensive pigs need less feed for each kilogram of growth. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe **two** disadvantages of intensive livestock production. **[2]**

**11.** A farmer's maize yields are low. The plants are small with pale leaves, many leaves have been eaten by caterpillars, weeds grow between the rows, the farm is worked by hand, and the variety grown was chosen many years ago.

Describe how the farmer could use **each** of the five methods in the syllabus to increase production. **[5]**

**12.** A farmer removes the hedgerows between several small fields to create one large field of oilseed rape.

**(a)** Describe the effect on biodiversity. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the farmer may then need to use more insecticide. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1].

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** Machinery allows larger areas of land to be farmed [1] and improves efficiency / makes sowing and harvesting faster with fewer workers [1].

**4.** Insecticides kill insect pests that damage the apples [1], so fewer apples are eaten, marked or rotting, and more are good enough to sell [1].

**5.** Any two from: light; water; mineral ions; space [2].

**6.** Any one: higher-yielding varieties / larger grains or fruit; disease resistance; drought tolerance; short, strong stems [1].

**7. (a)** Yield increases as more fertiliser is added [1]; the increase gets smaller above 100 kg per hectare and yield levels off at about 7.0–7.1 tonnes per hectare [1].

**(b)** (6.4 − 3.1) ÷ 3.1 × 100 [1] = 106% [1].

**(c)** Adding 200 kg rather than 150 kg increases yield by only 0.1 tonnes per hectare [1]; the extra fertiliser costs money / more could be washed into rivers and cause pollution [1].

**(d)** Nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids / proteins [1], so the plants grow more and produce more grain [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), the second mark is for the pattern at high doses. Quote figures: "only 7.0 to 7.1 tonnes above 150 kg".

**8. (a)** (5.6 − 4.2) ÷ 4.2 × 100 [1] = 33.3% [1].

**(b)** Herbicide killed the weeds [1], so the carrots had less competition for light, water and mineral ions and grew larger [1].

**(c)** It must kill the weeds but not the carrots / be selective [1].

**9. (a)** Any two from: machinery can be used efficiently for one crop; conditions (fertiliser, pesticide) can be tailored to bananas; large yield at low cost; uniform crop that ripens together [2].

**(b)** All the plants are the same variety / genetically similar [1], so if one is susceptible, all are susceptible [1]; the plants are close together over a large area, so the fungus can spread easily from plant to plant [1].

**10. (a)** Free-range: 4.0 × 90 = 360 kg; intensive: 2.8 × 90 = 252 kg [1]; difference = 108 kg [1].

**(b)** Intensive pigs have little space, so they move less [1] and they are kept warm indoors [1]; less energy from food is released in respiration for movement and keeping warm, so more is used for growth [1].

**(c)** Any two from: disease spreads quickly between crowded animals; antibiotics overused; waste can pollute water; poor animal welfare; large amounts of feed must be grown; energy needed for heating and lighting [2].

**11.** One mark for each method applied to the problem:

- use chemical fertiliser containing nitrate (and magnesium) to improve yield / correct the pale leaves [1]
- use insecticide to kill the caterpillars, improving quality and yield [1]
- use herbicide to kill the weeds and reduce competition [1]
- use agricultural machinery to farm a larger area more efficiently [1]
- grow a variety produced by selective breeding with higher yield / disease resistance [1]

*Examiner insight:* Each method must be linked to the farm. "Use herbicide" alone shows the method; "to kill the weeds between the rows, reducing competition" shows understanding.

**12. (a)** Biodiversity decreases [1]; hedgerow habitats for many species are removed and only one crop species is grown [1].

**(b)** Predators of insect pests (such as birds and ladybirds) lived in the hedgerows / pests spread easily through one large area of the same crop [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping herbicides and insecticides.
- Describing the fertiliser trend without the levelling off.
- Dividing by the new value instead of the original in a percentage increase.
- Explaining intensive growth as "the pigs eat more".
- Naming methods without linking them to the problem described.
