## Making alkenes

| Route | Reagents and conditions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Elimination of HX | NaOH in **ethanol**, heat | CH₃CHBrCH₃ → CH₂=CHCH₃ + HBr (as NaBr + H₂O) |
| Dehydration of an alcohol | heated Al₂O₃, or concentrated H₂SO₄ | CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O |
| Cracking | heat with Al₂O₃ | C₈H₁₈ → C₆H₁₄ + C₂H₄ |

An unsymmetrical secondary halogenoalkane or alcohol (2-bromobutane, butan-2-ol) gives a mixture:
but-1-ene, cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene.

## Reactions of alkenes

| Reaction | Reagents and conditions | Ethene gives |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogenation | H₂(g), Pt/Ni catalyst, heat | ethane |
| Hydration | H₂O(g), H₃PO₄ catalyst | ethanol |
| Hydrogen halide | HX(g), room temperature | halogenoethane, e.g. CH₃CH₂Br |
| Halogen | X₂, room temperature | 1,2-dihaloethane, e.g. CH₂BrCH₂Br |
| Mild oxidation | cold, dilute, acidified KMnO₄ | ethane-1,2-diol (purple → colourless) |
| Strong oxidation | hot, concentrated, acidified KMnO₄ | C=C broken; =CH₂ ends → CO₂ + H₂O |
| Polymerisation | (many monomers) | poly(ethene), –[CH₂–CH₂]– |
| Test | aqueous bromine, room temperature | orange → colourless |

## Hot concentrated KMnO₄: the product rules

| End of the C=C | Product |
|---|---|
| =CH₂ | CO₂ + H₂O |
| =CHR | RCOOH |
| =CRR′ | RCOR′ (ketone) |

Examples: but-1-ene → propanoic acid + CO₂; but-2-ene → 2 ethanoic acid;
2-methylbut-2-ene → propanone + ethanoic acid.

## Electrophilic addition: the steps

**Br₂ + ethene**

1. π electrons induce a dipole in Br₂ (Brᵟ⁺–Brᵟ⁻).
2. Curly arrow: π bond → Brᵟ⁺; curly arrow: Br–Br bond → Brᵟ⁻ (heterolytic fission).
3. Carbocation CH₂BrCH₂⁺ + Br⁻.
4. Curly arrow: lone pair on Br⁻ → C⁺. Product CH₂BrCH₂Br.

**HBr + propene**

1. HBr is permanently polar, Hᵟ⁺–Brᵟ⁻.
2. Curly arrow: π bond → Hᵟ⁺; curly arrow: H–Br bond → Br.
3. Secondary carbocation CH₃CH⁺CH₃ (favoured) or primary CH₃CH₂CH₂⁺.
4. Br⁻ attacks C⁺. Major product 2-bromopropane; minor 1-bromopropane.

## Carbocation stability

- Alkyl groups are **electron-donating** (positive inductive effect).
- They reduce the charge density on C⁺, stabilising it.
- Stability: **tertiary (3 alkyl groups) > secondary (2) > primary (1)**.
- The more stable cation has the lower-energy route, so it gives the **major product**
  (Markovnikov addition: H goes to the carbon with more H atoms already).

## Major and minor products at a glance

| Alkene + reagent | Cation route to major product | Major product | Minor product |
|---|---|---|---|
| propene + HBr | secondary | 2-bromopropane | 1-bromopropane |
| but-1-ene + HCl | secondary | 2-chlorobutane | 1-chlorobutane |
| 2-methylpropene + HBr | tertiary | 2-bromo-2-methylpropane | 1-bromo-2-methylpropane |
| propene + steam | secondary | propan-2-ol | propan-1-ol |
| but-2-ene + HBr | secondary (both routes) | 2-bromobutane only | none: the alkene is symmetrical |

## Polymers

| Monomer | Polymer | Repeat unit |
|---|---|---|
| ethene, CH₂=CH₂ | poly(ethene) | –CH₂–CH₂– |
| propene, CH₂=CHCH₃ | poly(propene) | –CH₂–CH(CH₃)– |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrophile | electron-pair acceptor, attracted to electron-rich sites |
| Electrophilic addition | addition across C=C started by an electrophile attacking the π bond |
| Carbocation | ion with a positively charged carbon atom |
| Inductive effect | electron donation or withdrawal along σ bonds |
| Markovnikov addition | addition of HX or H₂O giving the product via the more stable carbocation |
| Diol | compound with two OH groups |
| Addition polymerisation | joining of many alkene monomers through their C=C bonds, with no other product |

## Exam traps

- Elimination needs **ethanolic** NaOH. Aqueous NaOH gives an alcohol.
- Steam hydration needs **H₃PO₄**, not H₂SO₄.
- Cold dilute KMnO₄ keeps the carbon chain (diol); hot concentrated KMnO₄ **splits** it.
- The first curly arrow starts at the **π bond**, not at an atom.
- Show the lone pair on Br⁻ in the final step.
- In poly(propene), CH₃ is a **side group**.
- 2-methylpropene + HBr gives **2-bromo-2-methylpropane** (tertiary cation), not 1-bromo-2-methylpropane.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions to make propene from 2-bromopropane.
2. Name all the alkenes formed when butan-2-ol is dehydrated.
3. State the conditions for adding steam to ethene.
4. Name the product of propene with cold dilute acidified KMnO₄.
5. Name the products of pent-2-ene with hot concentrated acidified KMnO₄.
6. An alkene gives only propanone with hot concentrated KMnO₄. Name the alkene.
7. Why does Br₂, a non-polar molecule, act as an electrophile towards ethene?
8. Explain why 2-bromopropane is the major product from propene and HBr.
9. Put the cations (CH₃)₃C⁺, CH₃CH₂⁺ and (CH₃)₂CH⁺ in order of decreasing stability.
10. Draw the repeat unit of poly(propene).
11. Describe the test for a C=C bond, with results.
12. What is the major product of but-1-ene with HBr?

**Answers:**

1. NaOH dissolved in ethanol, heat.
2. But-1-ene, cis-but-2-ene and trans-but-2-ene.
3. H₂O(g) with an H₃PO₄ catalyst.
4. Propane-1,2-diol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH.
5. Ethanoic acid and propanoic acid.
6. 2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=C(CH₃)₂.
7. The π electrons repel the electrons in Br–Br, inducing a dipole; the δ+ bromine is attacked by the
   π bond.
8. Addition goes via the secondary cation CH₃CH⁺CH₃, which is stabilised by two electron-donating methyl
   groups, rather than the primary cation with one alkyl group.
9. (CH₃)₃C⁺ > (CH₃)₂CH⁺ > CH₃CH₂⁺.
10. –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–, with the CH₃ as a branch.
11. Shake with aqueous bromine at room temperature: an alkene turns it from orange to colourless; with no
    C=C it stays orange.
12. 2-bromobutane, CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃ (via the secondary cation).

## Frequently asked questions

**Which bromine reaction is the mechanism question about?**
The syllabus names bromine with ethene and hydrogen bromide with propene as the examples for the
mechanism of electrophilic addition, so learn both in full.

**Do I need conditions for addition of halogens?**
The syllabus lists X₂ without extra conditions; room temperature is the usual answer, and no catalyst or
UV light is needed.
