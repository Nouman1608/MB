These notes condense section 9.4, Statistical charts and diagrams, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Worked examples are in the [statistical charts and diagrams study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-statistical-charts-and-diagrams/), and exam-style questions are in the [statistical charts and diagrams practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-statistical-charts-and-diagrams-practice/).

## What you must draw and interpret

- Bar charts, including **composite (stacked)** and **dual (side-by-side)** bar charts.
- Pie charts.
- Pictograms.
- Simple frequency distributions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bar chart | equal-width bars with gaps, heights showing frequency |
| Dual bar chart | bars for two or more groups side by side in each category, with a key |
| Composite bar chart | the parts of each total stacked in one bar, with a key |
| Pie chart | a circle split into sectors with angles proportional to frequency |
| Sector | a "slice" of a pie chart |
| Pictogram | repeated symbols, each worth the amount in the key |
| Key | states what a colour, shading or symbol represents |
| Frequency distribution | a list of values with their frequencies, drawn as bars or vertical lines |

## Bar charts checklist

- Equal bar widths and equal gaps.
- Frequency axis starts at **zero**, with an even scale.
- Both axes labelled; categories labelled under the bars.
- A key for dual and composite charts.
- Composite: section size = reading at its top − reading at its bottom.

Example: a stacked bar has boundaries at 18, 30 and 42. The sections are 18, 30 − 18 = 12 and 42 − 30 = 12, and the total is 42.

## Pie chart formulas

```
degrees per item  = 360 ÷ total
sector angle      = frequency × degrees per item
frequency         = sector angle ÷ degrees per item
percentage        = sector angle ÷ 360 × 100
```

- 60 people: each person is 6°. A frequency of 13 gives 78°.
- A sector of 80° represents 20 people: each degree is 0.25 people, so the total is 360 × 0.25 = 90.
- A sector of 72° is 72 ÷ 360 = 20% of the total.

Check: angles add to 360°; frequencies add to the total.

## Pictograms

- Value = number of symbols × key.
- Half symbol = ½ × key; quarter symbol = ¼ × key; three-quarter symbol = ¾ × key.
- Key 8: 3¾ symbols = 30. A value of 20 is 2½ symbols.
- Choose a key that divides the data into simple fractions.

## Frequency distributions

- Values on the horizontal axis, frequency on the vertical axis.
- Discrete values: bars with gaps, or vertical lines.
- The tallest bar gives the mode.
- Proportions such as "how many scored at least 3" come from adding frequencies.

## Choosing a chart

| Want to show | Use |
|---|---|
| Frequencies of categories | bar chart |
| Two groups compared category by category | dual bar chart |
| Totals and their parts | composite bar chart |
| Shares of a single whole | pie chart |
| A simple visual count | pictogram |
| A discrete frequency table | bar chart or vertical line graph |

## Drawing a pie chart: steps

1. Find the total frequency.
2. Work out 360 ÷ total, the angle for one item.
3. Multiply each frequency by it; check the angles add to 360°.
4. Draw a circle and one radius; measure each angle from the last line drawn.
5. Label each sector or add a key.

## Reading a dual bar chart

- Read each bar from the scale; every bar starts at zero.
- Compare the two bars within a category to compare the groups.
- Add the bars for one group across all categories to get that group's total.
- The largest difference is where the two bars in a category are furthest apart in height.

## Exam traps

- Upper composite sections read directly from the scale.
- Comparing two pie charts' sectors when their totals differ.
- Pie angles rounded so they add to 359° or 361°.
- A truncated or uneven frequency axis.
- Partial pictogram symbols miscounted.
- Missing key, title or labels.

## Self-test

1. 60 people are shown in a pie chart. What angle represents 13 people?
2. A sector of 80° represents 20 people. How many people does the whole chart represent?
3. What percentage of the total is a 72° sector?
4. A stacked bar has section boundaries at 18, 30 and 42. Find the size of each section.
5. In a pictogram, one symbol represents 8 cars. How many cars do 3¾ symbols show?
6. With the key in question 5, how would you show 20 cars?
7. When is a dual bar chart more useful than a composite bar chart?
8. Pie charts of the same size show 200 people and 800 people. A 90° sector appears in each. How many people does each sector represent?
9. In a frequency distribution of goals, the bars for 0, 1, 2, 3 have heights 5, 9, 4, 2. What is the mode, and how many matches were there?
10. Using question 9, what percentage of matches had at least 2 goals?
11. A student's pie chart angles are 100°, 95°, 90° and 70°. What is wrong?
12. Why must a bar chart's frequency axis start at zero?

### Answers

1. 13 × 6 = **78°**.
2. 1° = 0.25 people, so 360 × 0.25 = **90**.
3. 72 ÷ 360 × 100 = **20%**.
4. **18, 12 and 12**.
5. 3.75 × 8 = **30**.
6. **Two and a half symbols**.
7. When the **parts** must be compared directly across categories.
8. **50** and **200** people: each is a quarter of its own total.
9. Mode **1 goal**; **20** matches.
10. (4 + 2) ÷ 20 = **30%**.
11. They add to **355°**, not 360°.
12. Otherwise bar heights are **not in proportion** to frequencies, which makes differences look larger.
