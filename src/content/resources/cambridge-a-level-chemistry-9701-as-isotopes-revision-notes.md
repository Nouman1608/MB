## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons |
| Mass (nucleon) number | Protons + neutrons; the superscript in isotope notation |
| Proton (atomic) number | Number of protons; the subscript in isotope notation |
| Electronic configuration | Arrangement of electrons in shells, sub-shells and orbitals |
| Chemical properties | How a substance reacts; decided by its electrons |
| Physical properties (for 1.2) | Limited to mass and density |
| Deuterium (D) | Hydrogen-2: one proton, one neutron |
| Heavy water | D₂O, water made with deuterium |

## Definition and notation

- **Isotopes:** same number of protons, different numbers of neutrons.
- Notation: mass number top left, proton number bottom left, e.g. ³⁷₁₇Cl; often written ³⁷Cl or chlorine-37.
- Neutrons = mass number − proton number.
- Ions of isotopes: add the charge top right, e.g. ³⁷₁₇Cl⁻ (17 p, 20 n, 18 e).

| Check | Isotopes? |
|---|---|
| Same proton number, different mass number | Yes |
| Same mass number, different proton number (e.g. ⁴⁰Ar, ⁴⁰Ca) | No: different elements |
| Same isotope, atom and ion (e.g. ³⁵Cl, ³⁵Cl⁻) | No: differ in electrons, not neutrons |

## Isotopes to know by example

| Isotope | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons (atom) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ¹H | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| ²H (D) | 1 | 1 | 1 |
| ³H (T) | 1 | 2 | 1 |
| ¹²C | 6 | 6 | 6 |
| ¹³C | 6 | 7 | 6 |
| ¹⁴C | 6 | 8 | 6 |
| ³⁵Cl | 17 | 18 | 17 |
| ³⁷Cl | 17 | 20 | 17 |

The electron column never changes within an element: that is the whole reason for identical chemistry.

## Answer frame: compare two isotopic species

1. **Similarity:** same chemical properties.
2. **Reason:** same number of protons, so same number of electrons and same configuration; reactions involve electrons.
3. **Difference:** different mass (and so different density).
4. **Reason:** different numbers of neutrons; extra neutrons add mass but not size.
5. **Quantify if asked:** ratio of relative masses, then density from the ratio or from molar mass ÷ 24.0 for a gas.

## Same chemical properties

1. Same number of protons, so same number of electrons in the neutral atom.
2. Same electronic configuration, same outer electrons.
3. Reactions involve electrons; neutrons take no part in bonding.

Examples: ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl both form Cl⁻, NaCl and HCl; D₂O reacts with sodium like H₂O, forming D₂.

## Different physical properties: mass and density

**Mass**
- Each extra neutron adds about 1 unit of mass.
- Atom size is fixed by electrons, so it hardly changes.

| Species | Approximate relative mass |
|---|---|
| H₂O | 18 |
| D₂O | 20 |
| ³⁵Cl₂ / ³⁵Cl³⁷Cl / ³⁷Cl₂ | 70 / 72 / 74 |

**Density of gases**
- Equal volumes at the same temperature and pressure contain equal numbers of molecules.
- Density (g dm⁻³) = molar mass ÷ 24.0 at room conditions.
- ³⁵Cl₂ 2.92 g dm⁻³; ³⁷Cl₂ 3.08 g dm⁻³.

**Density of liquids and solids**
- Molecules of isotopic forms are almost the same size, so density is roughly proportional to molecular mass.
- H₂O about 1.00 g cm⁻³; D₂O about 1.10 g cm⁻³ (predicted 1.00 × 20/18 = 1.11).
- D₂O ice sinks in liquid H₂O.

**Method: predicted density from mass ratio**
1. Find the two relative masses.
2. Divide heavier by lighter.
3. Multiply the known density by this ratio.

## Exam traps

- "Different mass number" alone is not a definition of isotopes.
- Same mass number does not mean isotopes.
- Isotopes do not have different numbers of electrons.
- "Same chemistry because same element" is circular; explain through electrons and configuration.
- Heavier isotopes are not larger atoms.
- Physical properties for this subtopic: mass and density only.

## Self-test

**1.** Define isotopes.

**2.** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in ¹⁸₈O²⁻.

**3.** Are ⁶⁴₂₉Cu and ⁶⁴₃₀Zn isotopes? Explain.

**4.** Give the full notation for an atom with 12 protons and 14 neutrons.

**5.** Explain why ¹²C and ¹⁴C both form CO₂ when burned.

**6.** State two physical properties in which isotopes differ, as required by the syllabus.

**7.** Why are the atoms of two isotopes of an element almost the same size?

**8.** Calculate the relative molecular mass of D₂¹⁶O.

**9.** Calculate the density of ³⁷Cl₂ gas at room conditions (molar volume 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹).

**10.** Predict the density of a liquid made of T₂O molecules (tritium oxide, ³H₂¹⁶O) if H₂O has density 1.00 g cm⁻³ and the molecules are the same size.

**11.** Why does a D₂O ice cube sink in ordinary water?

**12.** Which has more neutrons: ³⁵Cl⁻ or ³⁷Cl?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

**2.** 8 protons, 10 neutrons, 10 electrons.

**3.** No: they have different proton numbers (29 and 30), so they are different elements, even though the mass numbers are equal.

**4.** ²⁶₁₂Mg.

**5.** Both have 6 protons and 6 electrons with the same configuration, and reactions involve electrons, so they react in the same way.

**6.** Mass and density.

**7.** Atomic size is set by the electrons, and isotopes have the same number and arrangement of electrons; neutrons add mass to the tiny nucleus only.

**8.** 2 × 2 + 16 = 20.

**9.** 74 ÷ 24.0 = 3.08 g dm⁻³.

**10.** T₂O has Mr 2 × 3 + 16 = 22, so 1.00 × 22 ÷ 18 = 1.22 g cm⁻³.

**11.** D₂O molecules are heavier but about the same size as H₂O molecules, so D₂O ice is denser than liquid H₂O.

**12.** ³⁷Cl (20 neutrons, compared with 18 in ³⁵Cl⁻).
