## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by a tissue. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the organ system that contains the stomach and the pancreas. **[1]**

**2.** Put these in order of size, smallest first: organ system, cell, organ, tissue. **[2]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by differentiation. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the stomach is an organ and not a tissue. **[2]**

**5.** Give the level of organisation (cell, tissue, organ or organ system) for each of these. **[4]**

**(a)** A palisade cell.
**(b)** Xylem.
**(c)** A leaf.
**(d)** The digestive system.

## Section B

**6.** Two cube-shaped organisms have sides of 5 mm and 10 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of each cube. **[3]**
**(b)** Use your answers to explain why a large organism needs a system for exchanging materials. **[2]**

**7.** A human cell is 40 μm across. Calculate how many of these cells could be lined up across 2 mm. **[2]**

**8.** A micrograph of a tissue is 56 mm wide. The magnification is ×700. Three cells of equal width span the micrograph.

**(a)** Calculate the real width of the micrograph in micrometres (μm). **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the width of one cell, to the nearest micrometre. **[1]**

**9.** A student wants to estimate the number of cells in a 1 cm cube of tissue. Each cell is approximately a cube with sides of 25 μm. 1 cm = 10 000 μm.

**(a)** Calculate the number of cells along one edge of the cube. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the number of cells in the cube. **[2]**
**(c)** Give **one** reason why this is only an estimate. **[1]**

**10.** A single-celled organism does not have specialised organs, but a human does. Explain why. **[3]**

**11.** The leaf of a plant is an organ. Explain this statement, naming **two** tissues that are found in a leaf. **[3]**

**12.** Describe how the human digestive system shows the principles of organisation, from cells to organ system. Use the stomach as your example organ. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A group of cells with similar structure and function [1]
**(b)** Digestive system [1]

**2.** Cell, tissue, organ, organ system [2] (1 mark if three of the four are in the correct order)

**3.** The process in a developing multicellular organism in which cells become specialised [1]; so that they can perform different functions [1]

**4.** An organ is made of tissues (more than one) [1]; the stomach contains muscular, glandular and epithelial tissue, not a single tissue [1]

**5. (a)** Cell [1]
**(b)** Tissue [1]
**(c)** Organ [1]
**(d)** Organ system [1]

**6. (a)** 5 mm cube: surface area = 6 × 5² = 150 mm²; volume = 5³ = 125 mm³; ratio = 150 ÷ 125 = **1.2** [1]; 10 mm cube: surface area = 6 × 10² = 600 mm²; volume = 10³ = 1000 mm³; ratio = 600 ÷ 1000 = **0.6** [1]; correct working shown for both (surface area and volume) [1]
**(b)** The larger cube has a smaller surface area to volume ratio, so less surface for each unit of volume [1]; so diffusion across the outside surface cannot supply the inside, and exchange organs and transport systems are needed [1]

**7.** 2 mm = 2000 μm [1]; 2000 ÷ 40 = **50 cells** [1]

**8. (a)** 56 ÷ 700 = 0.08 mm [1] = **80 μm** [1]
**(b)** 80 ÷ 3 = 26.7, so **27 μm** [1]

**9. (a)** 10 000 ÷ 25 = **400** [1]
**(b)** 400 × 400 × 400 [1] = 64 000 000 (6.4 × 10⁷) [1]
**(c)** Any one of the following [1]: real cells are not perfect cubes; cells vary in size; there are gaps between cells

**10.** A single-celled organism has a large surface area to volume ratio, so all the exchanges can occur across its surface membrane [1]; a human is large and has many cells, most far from the outside, so diffusion across the body surface alone is too slow [1]; so organs and organ systems are specialised for exchange and transport of materials [1]

**11.** A leaf is made of several tissues working together [1]; any two of the following named tissues, 1 mark each [2]: palisade mesophyll (photosynthesis); spongy mesophyll (air spaces for diffusion of gases); epidermal tissue (covers the leaf); xylem; phloem

**12.** Marking points, 1 mark each [6]:
- cells differentiate so that they can do different jobs
- a tissue is a group of cells with similar structure and function
- examples of stomach tissues: muscular, glandular and epithelial
- muscular tissue contracts to move the contents; glandular tissue produces digestive juices; epithelial tissue covers the inside and outside
- the stomach is an organ because it is made of several tissues
- the stomach, small intestine, liver, pancreas and other organs work together as the digestive system, an organ system that performs a particular function

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "similar structure and function" in the definition of a tissue.
- Calling the stomach or a leaf a tissue.
- Reversing the order of organ and organ system.
- Dividing real size by image size, or forgetting to convert mm to μm.
- Saying large organisms need exchange systems "because they are heavy" instead of using surface area to volume ratio.
