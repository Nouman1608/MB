**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. The first two topics are about Cuba. The next four need **one Latin American country** each. The notes use Argentina (populism, dictatorship, democratisation) and Peru (guerrilla movement), with Chile for comparison. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-latin-american-politics/) for explanations and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-latin-american-politics-practice/) to test yourself.

## Key terms

- **Populism**: leadership that appeals directly to "the people", often with welfare and nationalism.
- **Descamisados**: "shirtless ones", Perón's working-class supporters.
- **Guerrilla**: irregular fighter in a small-scale war.
- **Disappeared**: people seized by the state and never seen again.
- **Dirty War**: Argentine state campaign against opponents (1976 to 1983).
- **Transitional justice**: truth commissions, trials and reparations after dictatorship.
- **Nunca Más**: report of Argentina's CONADEP (1984).
- **Special Period**: Cuba's crisis after the Soviet collapse.
- **Rondas**: Peruvian peasant self-defence patrols.

## The Cuban Revolution: causes

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | Batista's 1952 coup; corruption; no peaceful change |
| Economic | Sugar dependence; US ownership; rural unemployment; city-countryside gap |
| Social | Frustrated middle class and students; inequality; race divisions |
| Leaders | Castro (Moncada, "History Will Absolve Me", Sierra Maestra); Guevara; urban underground |

## Castro's rule

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | One-party state; revolutionary courts; CDRs; Communist Party (1965); 1976 Constitution |
| Social | Education and health; Literacy Campaign (1961); Martí and nationalism; gains for women but male leadership |
| Economic | Land reform; nationalisation; Soviet subsidies; failed 1970 sugar target; Special Period after 1991 |
| Opposition | Imprisonment; UMAP camps; Mariel boatlift (1980); 2003 arrests |

## Argentina: challenges to democracy

- Populism: Perón (from 1946), unions, welfare, women's vote (1947), Evita.
- Crisis: coup in 1955, Peronism banned, coups 1962 and 1966.
- Failure of leaders: Perón's return 1973, death 1974, Isabel Perón, Triple A.
- Guerrillas: Montoneros and ERP.
- Coup: 24 March 1976.

## Peru: Shining Path

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Origin | Maoist; Guzmán; Ayacucho, 1980 |
| Social | About 69,000 dead (2003 Commission); mostly rural Quechua speakers; rondas |
| Political | Army stronger; Fujimori's self-coup (1992); Guzmán captured 1992 |
| Economic | Rural disruption; hyperinflation in the 1980s |
| Women | Fighters and victims of sexual violence; survival networks |

## Dictatorship in Argentina

- Politics: junta under Videla; Congress closed; unions and parties banned.
- Repression: secret detention centres; about 30,000 disappeared according to human rights groups (CONADEP documented almost 9,000).
- Economy: Martínez de Hoz; debt up; real wages down.
- Society: censorship, fear, exile.
- Comparison: Pinochet (1973 to 1990), DINA, Chicago Boys, 1980 Constitution.

## Democratisation in Argentina

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | Falklands defeat; divided military; protest |
| Economic | Inflation, debt, recession |
| Social | Mothers of the Plaza de Mayo; churches; human rights groups |
| Transitional justice | CONADEP and *Nunca Más* (1984); Trial of the Juntas (1985); Full Stop (1986), Due Obedience (1987); pardons (1989 to 1990); laws annulled 2003 to 2005 |

## Debates

- Cuba: popular revolution or collapse of a weak regime?
- Castro: social gains against lost freedom.
- Why democracies failed: weak institutions, crisis, polarisation, Cold War.
- Transitional justice: truth, trials or amnesty?

## Essay skeleton

1. Choose your country and state your line.
2. Rank two or three factors.
3. Use a perspective.
4. Compare with a second country for contrast.
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent did repression, rather than popular support, explain how a military dictatorship stayed in power?"

1. Line: repression was the main tool, but some groups gave support, and economic and ideological arguments helped.
2. Repression: secret detention, disappearances, banned parties and unions, censorship (Argentina 1976 to 1983).
3. Support: parts of the middle class and business who feared guerrillas and inflation; the language of national security.
4. Economic factors: free-market policies that weakened unions; the debt that later weakened the regime.
5. Perspective: those who stress fear and coercion versus those who stress consent among elites. Compare Chile.
6. Judgement: repression sustained the regime, while limited support and Cold War context gave it room, until economic failure and the Falklands defeat removed both.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up Montoneros, ERP and Shining Path.
- Using different countries for each topic without depth in any.
- Description of repression without explaining how power was kept.

## Self-test

1. What did Castro say at his 1953 trial?
2. Why was Peronism banned after 1955?
3. What was the Dirty War?
4. Who led Shining Path?
5. Which event discredited the Argentine junta in 1982?
6. What was the Trial of the Juntas?

**Answers:** 1. "History Will Absolve Me". 2. The army and opponents saw Perón as authoritarian and wanted to end his movement's influence. 3. The military's campaign of kidnapping, torture and killing opponents, 1976 to 1983. 4. Abimael Guzmán. 5. Defeat in the Falklands/Malvinas War. 6. The 1985 civilian trial that convicted Videla and Massera.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need Peru if I study Argentina?**
Not necessarily. Any country with a guerrilla movement, such as Colombia, can fill that topic.

**How do I show evaluation?**
Weigh causes, and use named views about why the regimes rose and fell.
