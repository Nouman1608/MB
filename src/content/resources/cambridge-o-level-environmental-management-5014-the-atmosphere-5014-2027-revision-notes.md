Condensed notes for sub-topic 4.1 The atmosphere in the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus for 2027 to 2029. For explanations and worked examples, read [the atmosphere study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-the-atmosphere-5014-2027/); then try [the atmosphere practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-the-atmosphere-5014-2027-practice/).

## Three statements, three skills

| Statement | Skill | Must-know content |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1.1 | Describe structure and composition | Four layers in order; ozone layer in the lower stratosphere; 78% nitrogen, 21% oxygen, plus carbon dioxide, argon and water vapour |
| 4.1.2 | Define | Climate = weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years |
| 4.1.3 | Describe | Five steps of the natural greenhouse effect |

## Structure (4.1.1a and b)

From the ground up:

1. Troposphere (lowest; weather and nearly all water vapour)
2. Stratosphere (ozone layer in its **lower** part)
3. Mesosphere
4. Thermosphere (outermost of the four)

Memory aid: **T**all **S**tudents **M**ake **T**ables.

Why the ozone layer matters: it absorbs much of the Sun's ultraviolet radiation, which links directly to sub-topic 4.4.

## Composition of clean air (4.1.1c)

| Gas | Share by volume |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | 78% |
| Oxygen | 21% |
| Argon | just under 1% |
| Carbon dioxide | about 0.04% |
| Water vapour | variable |

- Only nitrogen and oxygen have exact syllabus figures.
- Carbon dioxide and water vapour are tiny shares but are greenhouse gases.
- "Clean" air has no pollutants such as sulfur dioxide or oxides of nitrogen.

## Weather and climate (4.1.2)

| Weather | Climate |
|---|---|
| Conditions at one time | Conditions based on the weather over many years |
| "Today is 34 °C and humid" | "Summers here are usually hot and humid" |
| Changes hour to hour | Described with long-term averages |

Climate change is a shift in these long-term averages, not one unusual season.

## Natural greenhouse effect (4.1.3)

1. Solar radiation passes through the atmosphere.
2. Some is absorbed by land and oceans, heating the Earth.
3. Some is reflected back into space (clouds, ice, snow).
4. Some is absorbed and re-emitted back to the surface by greenhouse gases and clouds.
5. The Earth is kept warm enough to support life.

Key verbs: passes through, absorbed, reflected, re-emitted.

**Reading a greenhouse diagram**
- Arrow from the Sun through the atmosphere = step 1.
- Arrow ending at the ground or sea = absorption, step 2.
- Arrow bouncing off a cloud or ice back to space = reflection, step 3.
- Arrow from the gas layer pointing down to the surface = re-emission, step 4.
- If arrows are numbered, quote the numbers in your answer.

Natural effect = essential for life. Enhanced effect (sub-topic 4.2) = extra warming from human greenhouse gas emissions.

## Quick calculations

- Gas volume = total volume × percentage ÷ 100. In 500 m³ of clean air: nitrogen 390 m³, oxygen 105 m³, others about 5 m³.
- Mean of a climate record = total ÷ number of years. Range = highest − lowest.
- Percentages of the main gases: 78 + 21 = 99%, leaving about 1% for all others.

## How 4.1 links forward

- **4.2 Climate change**: greenhouse gases from 4.1.3 increase → enhanced greenhouse effect.
- **4.3 Acid rain**: pollutant gases react with oxygen and water vapour, both components of air from 4.1.1.
- **4.4 Ozone depletion**: the ozone layer in the lower stratosphere from 4.1.1(b) is damaged by CFCs.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atmosphere | The layer of gases surrounding the Earth |
| Troposphere | The lowest layer of the atmosphere |
| Stratosphere | The layer above the troposphere, containing the ozone layer in its lower part |
| Mesosphere | The layer above the stratosphere |
| Thermosphere | The layer above the mesosphere |
| Ozone layer | A region of the lower stratosphere rich in ozone that absorbs ultraviolet radiation |
| Clean air | Air without pollutants |
| Weather | The state of the atmosphere at a particular time |
| Climate | The weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years |
| Solar radiation | Energy from the Sun |
| Greenhouse gas | A gas that absorbs radiation and emits the energy as thermal energy |

## Exam traps

- Order matters: troposphere is **below** stratosphere.
- Ozone layer: **lower stratosphere**, not troposphere and not "the top of the atmosphere".
- 78% is nitrogen, 21% is oxygen.
- Climate definitions must include "over many years".
- Greenhouse gases absorb and **re-emit**; they do not reflect.
- Do not mix up the greenhouse effect and the ozone layer: they are separate processes.
- Reflection by clouds is step 3; absorption by clouds is also part of step 4. Both are correct.

## Self-test

1. Name the four layers of the atmosphere in order, starting at the surface.
2. In which layer is the ozone layer found?
3. What percentage of clean air is oxygen?
4. Name the three gases that make up most of the remaining 1% of clean air.
5. Define climate.
6. Is "the town had a storm last night" a statement about weather or climate?
7. What happens to some solar radiation when it reaches the land and oceans?
8. Name two surfaces that reflect solar radiation back into space.
9. What do greenhouse gases do to radiation?
10. Why is the natural greenhouse effect important?
11. Calculate the volume of nitrogen in 200 m³ of clean air.
12. Which two minor components of clean air are greenhouse gases?

## Answers

1. Troposphere, stratosphere, mesosphere, thermosphere.
2. The lower stratosphere.
3. 21%.
4. Argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour.
5. The weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years.
6. Weather.
7. It is absorbed, heating the Earth.
8. Clouds, ice or snow (any two).
9. Absorb it and re-emit energy back towards the Earth's surface.
10. It keeps the Earth warm enough to support life.
11. 200 × 0.78 = 156 m³.
12. Carbon dioxide and water vapour.
