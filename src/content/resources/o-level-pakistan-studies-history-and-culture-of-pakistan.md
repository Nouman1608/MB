This guide covers **Paper 1 The History and Culture of Pakistan**, for
Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059, 2026 series (Version 1,
published September 2023). A genuine restructuring of this syllabus
takes effect from June 2028, moving Paper 1 to a 4-topic structure with
choice between topics — that later series is a distinct qualification
version and is not covered by this guide, which describes the syllabus
as it stands for 2026 exams.

## Where this fits in 2059

Paper 1 and Paper 2 are both compulsory, each worth 50% of the
qualification. Paper 1's content is divided into three sections, and
material within each section is explicitly inter-related — exam
questions may draw on more than one item within a section, testing
whether candidates can connect causes and consequences across the
material rather than recall isolated facts.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL PAKISTAN STUDIES 2059 — PAPER 1 THE HISTORY AND
CULTURE OF PAKISTAN**

- 1.1 Cultural and Historical Background to the Pakistan Movement —
built around Key Questions such as the success of religious reformers
like Shah Waliullah and Syed Ahmad Barelvi in spreading and reviving
Islam in the subcontinent, and the causes and consequences of the
decline of the Mughal Empire, including both internal factors
(Aurangzeb's policies, weak successors) and external factors (foreign
invasions, the East India Company's expansion)
- 1.2 The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47 — the political events,
organisations and figures leading from the formation of the All-India
Muslim League to independence in 1947
- 1.3 Nationhood, 1947–99 — the political, social and economic
development of Pakistan in the decades after independence

## How to approach it

Because each section is assessed through Key Questions that link
several focus points together, revision built around isolated facts
underperforms revision built around explaining relationships — for
1.1, practise explaining how internal weakness and external pressure
combined to bring down the Mughal Empire, rather than listing the two
sets of causes separately. Dates and named individuals (Shah
Waliullah, Syed Ahmad Barelvi, Haji Shariatullah) need to be tied to
their specific historical role and significance, since Key Questions
consistently ask "how important" or "how successful" someone or
something was — a judgement question, not just a recall one. As with
the equivalent IGCSE syllabus (0448), building a working timeline
across all three sections helps candidates place events from 1.1
through 1.3 in relation to one another, which supports the
cross-section connections examiners are specifically testing for.
