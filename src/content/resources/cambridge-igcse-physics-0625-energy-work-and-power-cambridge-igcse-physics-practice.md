These questions cover section 1.7, Energy, work and power (1.7.1 to 1.7.4), of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take g = 9.8 N/kg. All figures in the questions are invented for practice.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 5 and 7 to 9 use Core content only, and every candidate should do them. Questions 6 and 10 to 13, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (1.7.1 Supplement 4–6; 1.7.3 Supplement 4–7), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the main energy store in each of these:

**(a)** a moving car **[1]**
**(b)** a sandwich **[1]**
**(c)** a cup of hot water **[1]**

**2.** A worker pushes a crate 6.0 m across a floor with a steady force of 150 N.

**(a)** Calculate the work done. **[2]**
**(b)** State the energy transferred. **[1]**

**3.** A lift motor transfers 54 kJ of energy in 1.5 minutes. Calculate its power. **[3]**

**4.** A wind-up toy car is wound up, released and runs across the floor until it stops. Describe the energy transfers, naming the stores. **[3]**

**5.** This question is about energy resources.

**(a)** Name **two** renewable resources that generate electricity without using a boiler. **[2]**
**(b)** State the difference between a solar cell and a solar panel. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Calculate the kinetic energy of a 70 kg runner moving at 8.0 m/s. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Electricity is generated in a hydroelectric power station.

**(a)** Describe how the station generates electricity. **[3]**
**(b)** Give **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of hydroelectric power. **[4]**

**8.** A small island community gets its electricity from diesel generators. It is considering building wind turbines instead. Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of the change, using the ideas of renewability, reliability, scale and environmental impact. **[5]**

**9.** A student of mass 55 kg runs up a flight of stairs with a vertical height of 3.6 m in 4.5 s.

**(a)** Calculate the student's weight. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done against gravity. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the student's useful power. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A roller-coaster car of mass 500 kg starts from rest at the top of a track 30 m above the lowest point.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost when the car reaches the lowest point. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed at the lowest point if no energy is wasted. **[2]**
**(c)** The actual speed at the lowest point is 20 m/s. Calculate the energy wasted. **[2]**
**(d)** State what happens to the wasted energy. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* An electric motor lifts a 15 kg load through 2.0 m in 3.0 s. The input power to the motor is 160 W.

**(a)** Calculate the useful energy output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total energy input. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the efficiency of the motor. **[2]**
**(d)** State where most of the wasted energy goes. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* This question is about the origin of our energy resources.

**(a)** State the process by which the Sun releases energy. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the energy stored in coal came from the Sun. **[2]**
**(c)** Name **one** resource whose energy does not come from the Sun, and state its source. **[2]**
**(d)** State why research into nuclear fusion on the Earth is being carried out. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* In a car, every 100 kJ of chemical energy from the fuel gives 25 kJ of useful kinetic energy. 60 kJ is wasted by heating in the engine and the rest is wasted overcoming friction in the moving parts.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how a Sankey diagram would show these figures. **[2]**
**(c)** The 100 kJ is supplied in 5.0 s. Calculate the useful power output. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** kinetic [1] **(b)** chemical [1] **(c)** internal (thermal) [1]

**2. (a)** W = Fd = 150 × 6.0 [1] = **900 J** [1]
**(b)** **900 J** [1]

**3.** 54 kJ = 54 000 J and 1.5 min = 90 s [1]; P = ΔE/t = 54 000 ÷ 90 [1] = **600 W** [1]

**4.** Winding up stores energy in the elastic store of the spring [1]; when released, the spring does work on the wheels, so energy goes to the kinetic store of the car [1]; friction and air resistance transfer it by heating to the internal store of the surroundings (and some by sound) until the car stops [1].

**5. (a)** Any two: wind; hydroelectric; tides; waves; solar cells [1 each]
**(b)** A solar cell transfers light to electrical energy; a solar panel uses the Sun's radiation to heat water [1].

**6.** Ek = ½mv² = ½ × 70 × 8.0² [1] = **2240 J** (2200 J) [1]

**7. (a)** Water is stored behind a dam (in its gravitational potential store) [1]; it flows down through pipes and turns turbines [1]; the turbines drive generators that produce electricity [1].
**(b)** Advantages, any two: renewable; no carbon dioxide while operating; reliable; output can be started quickly; large scale [1 each, max 2]. Disadvantages, any two: floods valleys and destroys habitats; only possible where the geography suits; expensive to build; communities may need to move [1 each, max 2].

**8.** Any five, at least one from each side: wind is renewable but diesel will run out [1]; wind turbines release no carbon dioxide or other pollutants while operating, while burning diesel does [1]; no fuel has to be shipped to the island [1]; wind is unreliable, so electricity is not available when the wind does not blow [1]; diesel generators can supply power on demand [1]; several turbines are needed for the same output, using land and having a visual and noise impact [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A one-sided answer is capped; a discussion needs both advantages and disadvantages.

**9. (a)** 55 × 9.8 = **539 N** [1]
**(b)** W = Fd = 539 × 3.6 [1] = **1940 J** (1940.4 J) [1]
**(c)** P = W/t = 1940 ÷ 4.5 [1] = **431 W** (430 W) [1]

**10. (a)** ΔEp = mgΔh = 500 × 9.8 × 30 [1] = **147 000 J** [1]
**(b)** ½ × 500 × v² = 147 000, so v² = 588 [1]; v = **24 m/s** [1]
**(c)** Ek = ½ × 500 × 20² = 100 000 J [1]; wasted = 147 000 − 100 000 = **47 000 J** [1]
**(d)** Transferred by friction and air resistance to the internal store of the track, car and air (and by sound) [1].

**11. (a)** ΔEp = 15 × 9.8 × 2.0 [1] = **294 J** [1]
**(b)** 160 × 3.0 = **480 J** [1]
**(c)** efficiency = 294 ÷ 480 × 100% [1] = **61%** [1]
**(d)** The internal store of the motor and surroundings, by heating (and some by sound) [1].

**12. (a)** Nuclear fusion [1]
**(b)** Plants used light from the Sun to grow, storing chemical energy [1]; the remains of the plants were buried and over millions of years formed coal [1].
**(c)** One of: geothermal, from hot rocks inside the Earth; tidal, from the gravitational pull of the Moon on the oceans; nuclear, from uranium (nuclear fuel) in the ground [1 for resource, 1 for source]
**(d)** To find out whether energy released by fusion can be used to generate electrical energy on a large scale [1].

**13. (a)** 25 ÷ 100 = **25%** [1]
**(b)** One input arrow 100 units wide splits into a 25-unit useful arrow continuing in the same direction [1] and wasted arrows branching off: 60 units (heating in the engine) and 15 units (friction) [1].
**(c)** P = 25 000 ÷ 5.0 [1] = **5000 W** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying energy is "used up" without naming where it goes.
- Leaving kJ or minutes unconverted.
- Writing ½mv instead of ½mv².
- Vague comparisons of energy resources with no reason.
- *(Extended)* Dividing input by output and getting an efficiency over 100%.
