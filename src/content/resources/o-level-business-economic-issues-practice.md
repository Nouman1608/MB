## Questions

**1.** Define "Gross Domestic Product (GDP)". **[2]**

**2.** In one country GDP has fallen for two quarters in a row, unemployment is rising, but most firms are still trading.
**(a)** Identify the stage of the business cycle. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain one effect of this stage on a shoe retailer. **[2]**

**3.** GDP was 3,150 billion last year and is 3,276 billion this year. Calculate the GDP growth rate. **[2]**

**4.** A price index rises from 125.0 to 131.25.
**(a)** Calculate the inflation rate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way this inflation could reduce the profit of a bakery. **[2]**

**5.** A country has a labour force of 25 million. 1.5 million are unemployed.
**(a)** Calculate the unemployment rate. **[1]**
**(b)** Unemployment then falls steadily for two years. Explain how this could affect a mobile phone shop. **[3]**

**6.** Imbrey Foods has profit before tax of 340,000. The government raises the tax on company profit from 20% to 24%.
**(a)** Calculate the firm's profit after tax before and after the change. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one way the change could affect its decisions. **[2]**

**7.** Pinnacre Gifts sells a clock for 250 before sales tax. The sales tax rate rises from 8% to 12%.
**(a)** Calculate the price paid by a customer before and after the rise. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline two ways Pinnacre Gifts could respond. **[2]**

**8.** A government cuts its spending on new roads. Explain how this could affect:
**(a)** a construction firm that builds roads **[2]**
**(b)** a café next to a large road-building site. **[2]**

**9.** Cardrew Joinery has a loan of 150,000 at a variable rate. The rate rises from 6% to 9%. Its profit before interest is 36,000.
**(a)** Calculate the annual interest before and after the rise. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its profit after interest before and after the rise. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one way the owner might respond. **[2]**

**10.** Cardrew Textiles sells cloth to clothing makers. In a recession its sales revenue fell from 4,800 thousand in year 1 to 4,320 thousand in year 2 and to 3,888 thousand in year 3. Unemployment rose from 5% to 9%. The owner is choosing between:
Option A: cut prices by 8% to try to hold sales.
Option B: close its oldest workshop to reduce fixed costs, accepting lower output.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in sales revenue in each of years 2 and 3. **[2]**
**(b)** Recommend which option the owner should choose. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Pinnacre Cycles has a variable-rate loan of 240,000. Profit before interest and tax is 120,000. The interest rate rises from 4% to 7%. In the same year the government cuts the tax on company profit from 25% to 20%. The owner is considering a second shop, funded by a new loan of 180,000 at 7%.
**(a)** Calculate the annual interest on the existing loan before and after the rise. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate profit after tax before and after both changes. **[4]**
**(c)** Justify whether the owner should open the second shop now. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The **combined value** [1] of all goods and services **made within a country over a period** (usually a year) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say "the money a country has" or "the country's income from tax" describe something different. Name both goods and services, and say "produced".

**2. (a)** **Recession** [1].
**(b)** Households have less to spend, so the retailer's sales fall [1], and with lower revenue its profit may fall or it may cut stock and staff [1]. Shoes can be delayed, which makes the effect likely.
*Examiner insight:* The second mark usually comes from carrying the effect through to profit, jobs or stock. "Sales fall" alone is only a first step.

**3.** Change = 3,276 - 3,150 = 126 [1]. Growth = 126 / 3,150 × 100 = **4.0%** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula and the working. The usual slip is to divide by the new figure when the old one is needed.

**4. (a)** Change = 131.25 - 125.0 = 6.25 [1]. Inflation = 6.25 / 125.0 × 100 = **5.0%** [1].
**(b)** The prices of flour, energy and rent rise [1], so unless the bakery raises its prices, each loaf earns less profit [1]. If it raises prices, customers may buy less.
*Examiner insight:* Name a cost relevant to a bakery. Writing only "costs go up" gives the answer less application to the stem.

**5. (a)** 1.5 / 25 × 100 = **6%** [1].
**(b)** More households have wages [1], so more can afford new phones and demand rises [1]. The shop may also need to hire staff in a tighter job market, and pay may rise [1].
*Examiner insight:* A three-mark "explain" usually needs a chain: more jobs, more income, more sales. Add context from the shop.

**6. (a)** Before: 340,000 × 0.80 = 272,000 [1]. After: 340,000 × 0.76 = 258,400 [1]. Fall = **13,600** [1].
**(b)** Less profit is kept [1], so the firm may delay new equipment or expansion funded from profit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Be clear which tax is changing. A profit tax does not change the price on the label, so a link to cheaper goods and higher demand does not fit.

**7. (a)** Before: 250 × 1.08 = **270** [1]. After: 250 × 1.12 = **280** [1].
**(b)** For example: absorb part of the tax to keep the price down, accepting a lower margin [1]; promote cheaper lines or find cheaper suppliers [1].
*Examiner insight:* Responses should be stated, not just described as "do something about the price". One clear action for each mark is enough for an outline.

**8. (a)** The government is a main customer for road builders [1], so contracts fall, and revenue and jobs may fall [1].
**(b)** The site workers are the café's customers [1], so demand falls when work stops or slows [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) rewards reading the stem. The link goes from the workers' spending to the café, not from roads to the café in general.

**9. (a)** Before: 150,000 × 0.06 = **9,000**; after: 150,000 × 0.09 = **13,500** [2].
**(b)** Before: 36,000 - 9,000 = **27,000**; after: 36,000 - 13,500 = **22,500** [2].
**(c)** Repay part of the loan from cash [1] to cut future interest, or move to a fixed-rate loan [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show interest first, then subtract it. Joining the steps without working makes errors hard to credit.

**10. (a)** Year 2: (4,320 - 4,800) / 4,800 × 100 = **-10%** [1]. Year 3: (3,888 - 4,320) / 4,320 × 100 = **-10%** [1].
**(b)** Option A. A cut of 8% on every sale means revenue only holds if volume rises by about 8.7%, because 1 / 0.92 = 1.087 [1]. In a recession, with unemployment at 9% and clothing makers also cutting back, that rise is unlikely [1], and the lower price is paid on every sale [1]. Option B. Closing the oldest workshop cuts fixed costs, which helps profit when revenue has fallen by 10% for two years [1], and it protects cash [1]. The risk is lost capacity if the economy recovers, so the owner would need to be ready to hire or rent space when it does [1]. **Judgement: Option B is the better choice**, because the 8.7% rise in volume is unlikely in this recession while the fixed-cost saving is certain.
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer uses the numbers (10% falls, 9% unemployment) and weighs both options before choosing. An unsupported choice gains little.

**11. (a)** Before: 240,000 × 0.04 = **9,600**; after: 240,000 × 0.07 = **16,800** [2].
**(b)** Before: (120,000 - 9,600) × 0.75 = **82,800** [2]. After: (120,000 - 16,800) × 0.80 = **82,560** [2]. The two changes almost cancel.
**(c)** The new loan adds 180,000 × 0.07 = 12,600 interest a year [1], so the shop must earn more than 12,600 profit before tax just to cover it [1]. For: the tax cut and any growth stage help [1]. Against: the rate is variable and could rise again; the profit after tax is only 82,560, so there is little margin for error. A reasoned answer: wait until the shop's forecast profit clearly exceeds the interest, or fix the rate first [1].
*Examiner insight:* Judgement answers do better when they use the calculated figures from earlier parts and state what the decision depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining GDP as money held by the government rather than the value of output.
- Giving an effect with no link to the type of business in the stem.
- Dividing by the new figure when working out a percentage change.
- Treating a rise in profit tax as if it raised the shelf price.
- Writing "interest rises" without saying the loan costs more or what that does to profit.
- Choosing a response with no reason it suits the firm.
- Recommending without using the data or weighing the other option.
- Forgetting to show the working that leads to a figure.
