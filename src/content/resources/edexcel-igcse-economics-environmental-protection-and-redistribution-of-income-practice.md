## Questions

**1.** State two ways a building firm can cause noise pollution. **[2]**

**2.** What is meant by relative poverty? **[2]**

**3.** In Country D (illustrative), annual income is taxed at 0% on the first 7,000, 12% on income from 7,000 to 28,000, and 32% on income above 28,000.

**(a)** Calculate the net pay of a worker with gross pay of 15,000. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average tax rate for a worker with gross pay of 66,000. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why this tax system is progressive. **[2]**

**4.** A town council puts a tax of 0.60 dollars on each disposable cup. The table shows weekly demand and supply before the tax. The external cost of each cup is 0.50 dollars.

| Price (dollars) | Quantity demanded | Quantity supplied before tax |
|---|---|---|
| 1.00 | 700 | 400 |
| 1.30 | 640 | 520 |
| 1.50 | 600 | 600 |
| 1.90 | 520 | 760 |
| 2.10 | 480 | 840 |

**(a)** Find the market price and quantity once the tax applies. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the weekly tax revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the fall in weekly external cost. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain one reason the tax may reduce pollution by less than the council hopes. **[2]**

**5.** Country E pays a subsidy of 40 dollars on every solar panel sold. It sells 2,500 panels. The price paid by buyers falls from 300 dollars to 270 dollars.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of the subsidy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in the price paid by buyers. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the amount a seller receives per panel after the subsidy. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one disadvantage of using a subsidy to protect the environment. **[3]**

**6.** A factory saves 18,000 dollars a year by dumping waste in a river. Each year there is a 25% chance it will be caught. The fine is 40,000 dollars.

**(a)** Calculate the expected fine per year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the smallest fine that would remove the factory's gain. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a fine may fail to stop water pollution. **[3]**

**7.** Firms P and R must each cut emissions by 80 tonnes. Cutting costs P 25 dollars a tonne and R 70 dollars a tonne. The government instead lets them trade permits at 40 dollars each.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost if neither firm trades. **[2]**
**(b)** P cuts 160 tonnes and sells 80 permits to R. Calculate each firm's net cost. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain one drawback of pollution permits. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how a government-provided park may help protect the environment. **[3]**

**9.** Country A has eight households with monthly income per person (dollars): 60, 90, 120, 150, 190, 240, 330 and 625. Relative poverty is defined here as income below 50% of the median. The absolute poverty line is 100 dollars.

**(a)** Calculate the relative poverty line. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of households in absolute poverty. **[2]**
**(c)** Every income rises by 15%. Calculate the number of households below each poverty line. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain what your answers show about economic growth and poverty. **[2]**

**10.** Explain two reasons why a government may want to reduce income inequality. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse how government investment in education and healthcare may reduce poverty. **[6]**

**12.** Factories in a city produce air pollution. Evaluate whether taxation is the best way for the government to reduce it. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, for example: operating machinery or drills [1]; working at night or early morning, or running delivery vehicles on residential roads [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "State" answer needs only a short phrase each. Writing a paragraph does not earn more than two points here.

**2.** Income far lower than what is normal (the average or median) in that country [1]. It is judged by comparing with others, not against a fixed standard [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two separate parts to the definition help. "Being poor" alone does not mention comparison.

**3. (a)** Taxable income in the 12% band = 15,000 − 7,000 = 8,000 [1]. Tax = 0.12 × 8,000 = 960 [1]. Net pay = 15,000 − 960 = **14,040 dollars** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Tax in the 12% band = 0.12 × 21,000 = 2,520 [1]. Tax above 28,000 = 0.32 × 38,000 = 12,160, so total tax is 14,680 [1]. Average rate = 14,680 ÷ 66,000 × 100 = **22.2%** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The average rate climbs from 6.4% for 15,000 to 22.2% for 66,000 [1] because higher incomes are taxed at higher rates on the extra income [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each band separately. A single multiplication of the whole income by one rate makes the working hard to credit.

**4. (a)** The tax shifts supply up by 0.60, so sellers supply at price p what they supplied at p − 0.60 before [1]. At 1.90, supply equals the old supply at 1.30, which is 520 [1]. This equals demand at 1.90, so the price is **1.90 dollars and the quantity is 520** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Revenue = 0.60 × 520 [1] = **312 dollars** per week [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** External cost before = 0.50 × 600 = 300 [1]. After = 0.50 × 520 = 260 [1]. Fall = **40 dollars** per week [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Demand for cups may be inelastic because few cheap substitutes exist [1], so quantity falls by only 13.3% and most of the pollution continues [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), say how the supply curve moves. A bare "1.90" is a weaker answer than one that links it to the tax.

**5. (a)** 40 × 2,500 [1] = **100,000 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (300 − 270) ÷ 300 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 270 + 40 [1] = **310 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The subsidy costs the government 100,000 dollars [1], which may have to come from taxes or from cutting other spending, such as schools [1], so there is an opportunity cost [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Be careful to use the buyer's price in (b), not the seller's. A good explanation develops "it costs money" into what is given up.

**6. (a)** 0.25 × 40,000 [1] = **10,000 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Fine × 0.25 must reach 18,000 [1], so the fine is 18,000 ÷ 0.25 = **72,000 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** If inspections are rare, the chance of being caught is low [1], so the firm may treat the fine as an occasional business cost [1]. A fine also punishes after the damage and does not clean the river [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Context matters. Tie the answer to the factory's gain and the chance of being caught, not to a general statement about crime.

**7. (a)** Cost = 80 × 25 + 80 × 70 [1] = 2,000 + 5,600 = **7,600 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** P: cost of cutting 160 tonnes = 4,000, receives 80 × 40 = 3,200, so net cost **800 dollars** [1]. R: pays 3,200 for permits and cuts nothing, so net cost **3,200 dollars** [1]. Both are lower than 2,000 and 5,600 respectively [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** If the government sets the limit on emissions too generously [1], total pollution stays high [1] and firms that can afford permits keep polluting [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the firm's cost of cutting (4,000) separate from the money received for permits (3,200). Mixing them makes the working hard to follow.

**8.** Trees and plants take in some air pollution [1]. Grass and soil absorb rainwater, reducing runoff and flooding [1]. A park gives people green space away from traffic, which reduces the effects of noise and stress [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each statement needs the environmental effect, not just "people enjoy parks".

**9. (a)** Median = (150 + 190) ÷ 2 = 170 [1]. Line = 0.5 × 170 = **85 dollars** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Incomes below 100 are 60 and 90 [1]: **2 households** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** New incomes: 69, 103.5, 138, 172.5, 218.5, 276, 379.5, 718.75. Absolute poverty: only 69 is below 100, so **1 household** [1]. New median = (172.5 + 218.5) ÷ 2 = 195.5, so the line is 97.75 [1]. Only 69 is below it, so relative poverty is **1 household**, unchanged [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Growth reduced absolute poverty from 2 households to 1 [1] but did not change relative poverty, because every income rose by the same percentage [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Recalculate the median after the change. Keeping the old line leads to a wrong conclusion.

**10.** Reason 1: to meet basic needs [1], since people on very low incomes may lack food, shelter or medicine [1]. Reason 2: ethical, because large gaps in income seem unfair [1], and many people believe everyone should have a decent minimum standard of life [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Two developed points are stronger than four listed words.

**11.** Education raises skills [1], so workers can get better-paid jobs [1], which lifts household income [1]. Healthcare reduces illness [1], so people can work more days and for longer [1]. The effects take years, so poverty may not fall at once, and the spending has an opportunity cost [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An "Analyse" answer builds a chain. Show each link, from spending to skills to income to poverty.

**12.** Tax raises the cost of producing, which shifts supply left and raises price [1]. Output falls and so does pollution [1]. It makes polluters pay and raises revenue [1]. If demand is inelastic, output falls little [1]. Regulation (limits on emissions) gives a firmer result [1] but needs inspection and may be costly to enforce [1]. Taxes may raise prices for poorer households, and firms may move away [1]. Permits could also meet a cap at lower cost [1]. Judgement: tax is a good tool if demand is elastic and revenue is used well, but combined with regulation it is likely to work better because the limit on pollution is clear [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* An "Evaluate" answer should reach a conclusion supported by the points made. A list of methods without a decision stays descriptive.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Working out revenue or external cost from the pre-tax quantity.
- Using the wrong price in subsidy calculations.
- Giving a flat percentage of the whole income when asked about banded tax.
- Defining relative poverty without comparing with others.
- Forgetting to recalculate the median line after incomes change.
- Naming a policy without showing how it alters what firms or people do.
- Writing a list for "Evaluate" without a final judgement.
- Omitting units or the word "dollars" in money answers.
