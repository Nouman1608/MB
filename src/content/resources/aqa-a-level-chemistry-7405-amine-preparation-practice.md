These questions cover section **3.3.11.1 Preparation** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Preparation of amines study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amine-preparation/) and the [Amines revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-amines-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** Classify each amine as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂NH₂ **[1]**

**(b)** (CH₃)₂NH **[1]**

**(c)** (CH₃CH₂)₃N **[1]**

**2.** Bromoethane is converted into ethylamine using ammonia.

**(a)** State the reagent and conditions. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the reaction mixture is likely to contain other organic products. **[2]**

**3.** Describe how the use of excess ammonia improves the yield of the primary amine. **[1]**

**4.** Propylamine can be made from bromoethane in two steps.

**(a)** Write an equation for step 1, and state the reagent and conditions. **[3]**

**(b)** Write an equation for step 2, using [H] for the reducing agent, and give one suitable reagent. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this route gives a purer primary amine than the reaction of 1-bromopropane with ammonia. **[1]**

**5.** Phenylamine is made from nitrobenzene.

**(a)** State the reagents and conditions. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reduction, using [H]. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why sodium hydroxide solution is added at the end. **[2]**

**6.** Outline a method to prepare phenylamine starting from benzene, giving reagents and conditions for each step. **[4]**

**7.** State a use of phenylamine in industry. **[1]**

**8.** 12.3 g of nitrobenzene (Mᵣ = 123.0) is reduced and 6.51 g of phenylamine (Mᵣ = 93.0) is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**9.** Show that 4 moles of [H] are required to reduce one mole of propanenitrile to propylamine by balancing the equation. **[2]**

**10.** A student wishes to make butylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂, from 1-bromopropane.

**(a)** Explain why the student should not simply react 1-bromopropane with ammonia. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe a better two-step route and name the intermediate. **[3]**

**11.** Compare the preparation of a primary aliphatic amine from a halogenoalkane using ammonia with its preparation by reduction of a nitrile. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Primary [1]

**(b)** Secondary [1]

**(c)** Tertiary [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** A large excess of ammonia dissolved in ethanol [1]; heated in a sealed tube (under pressure) [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br [1]

**(c)** The primary amine is also a nucleophile [1]; it can react with more bromoethane to give secondary and tertiary amines (and a quaternary ammonium salt) [1]. **[5]**

**3.** The ammonia molecules greatly outnumber the amine molecules, so the halogenoalkane is more likely to react with ammonia than with the amine [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** CH₃CH₂Br + CN⁻ → CH₃CH₂CN + Br⁻ [1]; potassium cyanide in ethanol and water [1]; heat under reflux [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ [1]; LiAlH₄ in dry ether (or hydrogen with a nickel catalyst) [1]

**(c)** The nitrile reduction gives only the primary amine, because there is no halogenoalkane present for the amine to attack further [1]. **[6]**

**5. (a)** Tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid [1]; heat under reflux [1]

**(b)** C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O [1]

**(c)** In acid the amine is present as the salt, C₆H₅NH₃⁺ [1]; sodium hydroxide removes the proton to release the free phenylamine [1]. **[5]**

**6.** Nitration: concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid [1]; at about 50 °C, giving nitrobenzene [1]; reduction with tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid, heat under reflux [1]; then add excess sodium hydroxide solution to give phenylamine [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Making dyes [1]. **[1]**

**8.** n(nitrobenzene) = 12.3 ÷ 123.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 93.0 = 9.30 g [1]; percentage yield = 6.51 ÷ 9.30 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** CH₃CH₂CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ [1]; carbon 3 = 3, nitrogen 1 = 1, hydrogen 5 + 4 = 9 on the left and 7 + 2 = 9 on the right, so 4[H] balances [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Ammonia would replace Br by NH₂, giving propylamine with only three carbons [1]; and a mixture of primary, secondary and tertiary amines would be likely [1]

**(b)** Step 1: heat under reflux with potassium cyanide in aqueous ethanol to give butanenitrile [1]; step 2: reduce the butanenitrile with LiAlH₄ in dry ether (or H₂ and Ni) [1]; the product is butylamine, which has four carbons [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Ammonia route: one step [1]; but gives a mixture of amines so a poor yield of the primary amine [1]. Nitrile route: two steps [1]; gives a pure primary amine with one extra carbon, although it uses toxic cyanide [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A "compare" answer needs a point for each route, and the best answers state the trade-off: fewer steps against purity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating the solvent, excess or sealed tube for the ammonia route.
- Claiming the ammonia route gives a pure primary amine.
- Forgetting the sodium hydroxide step in the nitrobenzene reduction.
- Miscounting carbons in the nitrile route.
- Writing the wrong number of [H] in the reduction.
- Using water in place of dry ether with LiAlH₄.
