## Fossil fuel formation chains

- **Coal**: swamp plants die → waterlogged, low-oxygen ground stops full decay → peat builds up → buried
  under sediment → heat and pressure over millions of years → coal.
- **Oil and gas**: plankton and other marine organisms die → sink into mud on the sea bed → buried without
  oxygen → heat and pressure over millions of years → oil and gas → rise through permeable rock → trapped
  under impermeable rock.

Five key words: **remains, no oxygen, burial, heat and pressure, millions of years**.

## Classification

| Renewable (non-finite) | Non-renewable (finite) |
|---|---|
| Biofuels: bioethanol, biomass, biogas, wood | Coal |
| Geothermal | Petroleum (oil) |
| Hydro-electric | Natural gas |
| Tidal | Nuclear (uranium) |
| Wave | |
| Solar | |
| Wind | |

Nuclear is finite because uranium is mined. Wood is renewable only if replanted.

## Generation in one line each

| Resource | How electricity is generated |
|---|---|
| Coal, oil, gas | Burned to boil water; steam turns turbine; generator (gas can also drive a gas turbine directly) |
| Nuclear | Fission of uranium releases heat; boils water; steam turns turbine |
| Biomass, wood | Burned to boil water; steam turns turbine |
| Biogas | Methane from bacteria breaking down waste without oxygen is burned to drive a generator |
| Bioethanol | Fermented from sugar crops; burned in an engine or power station |
| Geothermal | Water heated by hot rocks becomes steam; turns turbine |
| Hydro-electric | Water stored behind a dam flows down through turbines |
| Tidal | Barrage traps water at high tide; released through turbines |
| Wave | Rising and falling water pushes air or water through a turbine |
| Solar | Photovoltaic cells convert light directly to electricity |
| Wind | Wind turns blades connected to a generator |

## Benefits and limitations checklist

For any resource ask: **reliable? emissions? location needs? costs and side-effects?**

- **Reliable output**: fossil fuels, nuclear, geothermal, hydro-electric; tidal is predictable.
- **Variable output**: solar, wind, wave.
- **Carbon dioxide while generating**: fossil fuels (high); biofuels (released, but taken in again as
  plants regrow); others low.
- **Location limits**: geothermal (hot rocks), tidal (large tidal range), hydro-electric (valleys, rain),
  solar (sunshine), wind (exposed sites).
- **Side-effects**: dams flood land; nuclear waste and accident risk; fuel crops replace food crops;
  turbines are noisy and visible.

## Demand factors

| Factor | Direction | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Transport | Up | More vehicles and journeys |
| Personal and national wealth | Up | More appliances, cars, industry |
| Climate | Up in hot or cold places | Cooling or heating |
| Human population size | Up | More people |
| Industry | Up | Factories use large amounts |
| Disruption to supply | Down or shifts | Supply cut by conflict or hazards |
| Unreliable supply | Shifts | Generators, batteries, other fuels |
| Scarcity of resources | Down or shifts | High prices |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fossil fuel | Fuel formed over millions of years from the remains of living things |
| Renewable (non-finite) | Replaced naturally as fast as it is used |
| Non-renewable (finite) | Used up faster than it is replaced |
| Turbine | Machine turned by steam, water or air, which drives a generator |
| Generator | Converts the turning motion into electricity |
| Fission | Splitting of uranium nuclei, releasing heat |
| Barrage | Dam across an estuary used for tidal power |
| Photovoltaic cell | Converts light directly into electricity |
| Biogas | Gas, mainly methane, made by bacteria breaking down waste without oxygen |

## Exam traps

- Coal is from **land plants**; oil and gas are from **marine organisms**.
- Nuclear is **non-renewable**.
- Solar photovoltaic cells have **no turbine**.
- Biofuels release carbon dioxide when burned: say they are renewable, not "carbon-free".
- In percentage questions, put nuclear in the non-renewable total.
- Demand answers need the reason, not just the factor.

## Self-test

1. Why did the plants that formed coal not decay completely?
2. What organisms formed oil and natural gas?
3. Why do oil and gas collect beneath an impermeable layer?
4. Classify wood, uranium, geothermal and natural gas.
5. What process in a nuclear power station releases heat?
6. Name the resource that uses a barrage across an estuary.
7. Which resource generates electricity without a turbine?
8. How is biogas produced?
9. Give **one** benefit and **one** limitation of hydro-electric power.
10. Why is wind power less reliable than coal?
11. Explain how a hot climate can increase energy demand.
12. A country generates 50 units: 20 coal, 10 nuclear, 15 hydro-electric, 5 wind. Calculate the
    percentage from renewable resources.

**Answers:** 1. The ground was waterlogged with little oxygen. 2. Tiny marine organisms such as plankton.
3. They rise through permeable rock until the impermeable layer stops them. 4. Wood and geothermal
renewable; uranium and natural gas non-renewable. 5. Fission of uranium. 6. Tidal power. 7. Solar
(photovoltaic). 8. Bacteria break down manure, sewage or food waste without oxygen, producing mainly
methane. 9. Benefit: reliable, adjustable output, or no fuel cost. Limitation: flooding land displaces
people and destroys habitats. 10. Output depends on wind speed, so it stops when the wind drops; coal can
be burned whenever needed. 11. Air conditioning and fans are used for cooling, which uses electricity.
12. (15 + 5) / 50 x 100 = 40%.
