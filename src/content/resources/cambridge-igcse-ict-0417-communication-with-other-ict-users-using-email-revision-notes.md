These notes condense section 10.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417: the "characteristics, uses and constraints of email communication" and the "characteristics and effects of spam email including the methods which can be used to help prevent spam". The [email study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-communication-with-other-ict-users-using-email/) explains each point with worked examples, and the [email practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-communication-with-other-ict-users-using-email-practice/) let you test yourself.

## Characteristics in six words

**Asynchronous, fast, recorded, multiple, attachable, cheap.** Turn each into a sentence in an answer: "Email is asynchronous, so the recipient can read the message when convenient."

## The four constraints

| Constraint | Key points |
|---|---|
| Acceptable language | No offensive, abusive or threatening content; laws in many countries; emails are a permanent record that can be forwarded |
| Employer guidelines | Acceptable use policy: limits on personal use, no confidential data sent out, approved tone and signature, monitoring, disciplinary action |
| Security | Strong password, two-factor authentication, log out on shared machines, check sender address, beware links and attachments, encrypt sensitive content |
| Netiquette | No capitals, clear subject, polite and brief, check spelling, one topic per email, quote only what is relevant, do not forward private messages without permission |

**Why employers set rules:** staff emails represent the organisation, so misuse can damage its reputation or lead to legal action.

## Features: purpose and pitfall

| Feature | Purpose | Pitfall |
|---|---|---|
| Email group | One name sends to many addresses; saves time and avoids missing anyone | Must be kept up to date; wrong group sends to wrong people |
| To | Main recipients expected to act | Everyone can see these addresses |
| cc | Others kept informed; visible to all | Overuse fills inboxes |
| bcc | Recipients hidden from each other | Forgetting it reveals a whole mailing list |
| Reply | Answers the sender only; attachments not included | Missing people who need the answer |
| Reply all | Answers sender and all To and cc recipients | Sends unnecessary copies |
| Forward | Sends message and attachments to a new person | May share content the sender wanted private |
| Attachment | Sends a file with the message | Size limits, malware, recipient may lack software |

**Attachment fixes:** compress to .zip, save as PDF, or share a cloud link.

## Spam

- **Characteristics:** unsolicited, sent in bulk, usually automated, often from false addresses, advertising or scams.
- **Effects:** inbox clutter, missed genuine emails, wasted time, storage and bandwidth used, phishing and malware risk.
- **Prevention:** spam filter, block senders, mark as spam, never reply or click links, do not publish your address, untick sharing boxes, separate sign-up address.
- **Filter drawback:** genuine emails can be wrongly sent to junk.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Asynchronous | Sender and recipient do not need to be online at the same time |
| Acceptable use policy | An employer's rules for using its ICT systems, including email |
| Netiquette | Accepted rules of polite behaviour online |
| Email group | A single address-book entry representing many addresses |
| cc | Carbon copy: a copy whose recipient is visible to all |
| bcc | Blind carbon copy: a copy whose recipient is hidden from others |
| Forward | Send a received email and its attachments to someone new |
| Attachment | A file sent along with an email |
| Spam | Unsolicited bulk email |
| Spam filter | Software that detects suspected spam and moves it to a junk folder |

## Exam traps

- bcc hides the **address**, not the message.
- Reply does not send attachments; forward does.
- Spam is not malware, though it may contain it.
- "Email is secure" is wrong unless the message is encrypted.
- Clicking "unsubscribe" in a suspicious email can **increase** spam.
- An employer may **legally monitor** work email if the policy says so; do not write that staff have a right to privacy on work accounts.
- Two netiquette rules must be different rules, not one rule said twice.

## Self-test

1. Give two characteristics of email that make it useful to a business.
2. State one reason why an employer sets guidelines for staff email.
3. Give two items likely to appear in an employer's email policy.
4. Describe two ways of keeping an email account secure.
5. Name the feature that lets one name stand for 40 addresses.
6. A teacher emails 30 parents and wants the head of year to see the message. Which fields should she use for each?
7. State one difference between reply and forward.
8. Give two problems with sending a very large attachment.
9. Define *spam*.
10. Give two effects of spam on an individual.
11. Give three methods of reducing spam.
12. Give one disadvantage of a spam filter.

## Self-test answers

1. Any two: fast, asynchronous, written record, many recipients at once, attachments, cheap.
2. Staff emails represent the organisation, so misuse could damage its reputation or lead to legal action.
3. Any two: limits on personal use; no confidential information sent outside; monitoring; approved signature or tone; disciplinary action for misuse.
4. Any two: strong password; two-factor authentication; log out on shared computers; do not open unknown attachments or links; encrypt sensitive messages.
5. An email group.
6. Parents in bcc; head of year in cc (her own or the school address in To).
7. Reply goes only to the sender without attachments; forward sends the message and its attachments to a new recipient.
8. Any two: may exceed the size limit; slow to send and download; fills the recipient's storage; may be blocked.
9. Unsolicited email sent in bulk to many addresses.
10. Any two: fills the inbox; wastes time; important emails missed; phishing or malware risk.
11. Any three: spam filter; block senders; do not reply or click links; do not publish address; untick sharing boxes; separate sign-up address.
12. Genuine emails may be wrongly placed in the junk folder and missed.
