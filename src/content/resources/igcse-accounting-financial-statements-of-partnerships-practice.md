## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State two items that a partnership agreement may contain. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a partnership agreement is important. **[2]**

**2.** Ulla runs a bicycle repair shop on her own and is short of cash. Kasimir, a skilled mechanic, offers to join her as a partner. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage to Ulla of forming a partnership. **[4]**

**3.** **(a)** State the purpose of an appropriation account. **[2]**
**(b)** For each item, state whether it is a business expense charged before profit for the year, added in the appropriation account, deducted in the appropriation account, or in neither (a debit in the current account only): interest on a partner's loan; interest on drawings; a partner's salary; interest on capital; drawings. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Any two, for example profit-sharing ratio [1] and salaries [1].
**(b)** It sets out in advance how profit and losses are shared and what each partner is entitled to [1], so the accounts can be prepared correctly and disputes are less likely [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Why important" needs a reason with a consequence for the partners, not just a description of what the agreement is.

**2.** Advantage: Kasimir would bring mechanical skill, which Ulla lacks or has little time for [1]; she can also share the workload and run the shop while one is away [1]. Disadvantage: Ulla would have to share the profit with Kasimir [1], so she may earn less than as a sole trader, or she may be liable for debts caused by his decisions [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the facts given (short of cash, a skilled mechanic). A point tied to Ulla is stronger than one that fits any business.

**3. (a)** It shows how the profit for the year is divided [1] between the partners, as set out in the agreement [1].
**(b)** Loan interest: a business expense before profit [1]; interest on drawings: added [1]; salary: deducted [1]; interest on capital: deducted [1]; drawings: neither, a debit in the current account only [1].
*Examiner insight:* Loan interest comes off before the profit for the year figure is reached. Putting it in the appropriation account is a common slip.

**6.** Capital holds the long-term investment [1]; the current account holds yearly items such as drawings, interest and profit share [1]. Capital normally stays fixed, while the current account moves every year [1], so it can end as a credit or a debit balance [1]. (Any two differences, each developed.)
*Examiner insight:* A difference needs both accounts. Describing only the capital account does not show a contrast.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting loan interest into the appropriation account rather than with the expenses.
- Deducting interest on drawings, which is added.
- Starting the appropriation account from profit before adjustments.
- Posting drawings or profit shares to a fixed capital account.
- Sharing the balance equally by habit.
- Charging the full allowance for irrecoverable debts rather than only its increase.
- Carrying a debit balance down on the wrong side of a ledger account.
- Giving advantages and disadvantages that ignore the business described.
