Section 2.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 lists the input devices you must know: **keyboard, numeric keypad, pointing devices, remote control, joystick/driving wheel, touch screen (as an input device), scanners, camera, microphone, sensors** and **light pen**. For each one the syllabus asks for four things: **characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages**. This guide takes the devices one at a time in that order, so you can revise each as a complete set of four. Direct data entry devices, such as bar code readers and RFID readers, are a separate subtopic (2.2).

Condensed versions are in the [input devices revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-input-devices-and-their-uses-revision-notes/); test yourself with the [input devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-input-devices-and-their-uses-practice/).

## Keyboard

- **Characteristics**: keys for letters, numbers, punctuation and functions; most use the QWERTY layout; each key press sends a code to the computer. Ergonomic keyboards are shaped to keep the wrists in a natural position.
- **Uses**: entering text and data into word processors, spreadsheets and databases; typing commands; keyboard shortcuts.
- **Advantages**: fast entry of text for a trained user; familiar to most people; reliable; on-screen display lets the user check what is typed.
- **Disadvantages**: slow for users who cannot touch-type; prolonged use can cause repetitive strain injury (RSI); difficult for people with limited hand movement; takes up desk space.

## Numeric keypad

- **Characteristics**: a small set of keys for the digits 0 to 9, often with Enter, decimal point and arithmetic keys.
- **Uses**: entering PINs at cash machines and payment terminals; quantities at shop tills; mobile phones; door entry systems and alarms.
- **Advantages**: faster than a full keyboard for entering numbers; small and simple, so it fits on devices with little space.
- **Disadvantages**: keys can be small and close together, causing errors; limited to numbers; PINs can be seen by onlookers ("shoulder surfing").

## Pointing devices

The syllabus groups these together. The three you should know are the **mouse**, **touchpad** and **trackerball**.

| Device | Characteristics | Uses | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mouse | Moved across a surface; buttons and scroll wheel; optical sensor detects movement | Selecting, dragging, opening files in a GUI | Fast, accurate selection; easy to use; no large desk area needed | Needs a flat surface; can cause RSI; can be hard for people with limited hand movement |
| Touchpad | Flat, touch-sensitive pad built into a laptop; finger movement moves the pointer | Pointing on laptops | Built in, so nothing to carry; no surface needed | Harder to control precisely than a mouse; uncomfortable for long use |
| Trackerball | A ball rotated by the fingers or thumb in a fixed holder | Users with limited movement; control rooms; some industrial and medical equipment | Device stays still, so it needs little space and less arm movement; can be easier for some disabilities | Not usually supplied with computers, so extra cost; takes practice to use accurately |

## Remote control

- **Characteristics**: hand-held device with buttons that sends signals, usually infrared or radio, to another device.
- **Uses**: televisions, media players, multimedia projectors, air conditioning units, toys and some industrial machines.
- **Advantages**: device can be operated from a distance; users do not need to move, which helps people with limited mobility.
- **Disadvantages**: infrared signals need a clear line of sight and can be blocked; small buttons can be hard to use; easily lost; batteries must be replaced.

## Joystick and driving wheel

- **Characteristics**: a joystick is a lever that moves in different directions, with buttons; a driving wheel is turned like a steering wheel, often with pedals.
- **Uses**: computer games; flight and driving simulators for training; controlling robotic arms and some machinery.
- **Advantages**: more realistic, natural control than a keyboard for games and simulators; quick, continuous control of movement.
- **Disadvantages**: harder to make precise selections such as choosing menu items; not useful for entering text; extra cost.

## Touch screen (as an input device)

- **Characteristics**: the user touches the screen with a finger or stylus to select options; the screen detects the position of the touch.
- **Uses**: smartphones and tablets; information kiosks; self-service tills; ticket machines; restaurant ordering.
- **Advantages**: intuitive, little training needed; no separate keyboard or mouse to damage or steal; selection is fast because the user touches what they see.
- **Disadvantages**: limited options can be shown at once; fingerprints and dirt on the screen; on-screen keyboards are slow for long text; heavy public use can spread germs; screens can be damaged.

Remember that a touch screen is both an input and an output device. In this section, write about its role in taking input.

## Scanners

- **Characteristics**: a light source and sensor move across a document or image and convert it into a digital image. A flatbed scanner holds the page on a glass plate. A **3D scanner** captures the shape of a solid object.
- **Uses**: digitising photographs and paper documents; scanning documents for OCR (see 2.2); 3D scanners for producing models of objects, for example to make replacement parts with a 3D printer.
- **Advantages**: paper images and documents can be stored, edited and sent electronically; faster than retyping or redrawing; old photographs can be restored.
- **Disadvantages**: image quality depends on the scanner's resolution and the quality of the original; scanned files can be large; the original must be flat (for a flatbed scanner).

## Camera

- **Characteristics**: captures still images or video and stores them as digital data. Types include digital cameras, video cameras and **webcams** built into or attached to computers.
- **Uses**: photography; recording video; video-conferencing using a webcam; security monitoring.
- **Advantages**: images can be checked immediately and unwanted ones deleted; no film to develop; images transferred straight to a computer for editing or sharing; webcams allow face-to-face communication at a distance.
- **Disadvantages**: high-resolution images and video take up a lot of storage; built-in cameras may give poor quality in low light; webcams raise privacy concerns if accessed without permission.

## Microphone

- **Characteristics**: converts sound into an electrical signal, which is analogue; a sound card converts it into digital data.
- **Uses**: voice calls and video-conferencing; recording speech, music and podcasts; voice commands for dialogue-based interfaces; speech-recognition software that turns speech into text.
- **Advantages**: faster than typing for many users; hands-free; useful for people who cannot use a keyboard.
- **Disadvantages**: background noise reduces quality and recognition accuracy; speech recognition can make errors with accents or unclear speech; sound files can be large.

## Sensors

- **Characteristics**: measure a physical quantity in the environment and send data to a computer automatically. Most produce an analogue signal that needs converting to digital. Named examples you can use include temperature, light, pressure, moisture, humidity, pH, gas, sound, infrared and motion sensors.
- **Uses**: monitoring systems, such as weather stations and hospital patient monitoring; control systems, such as central heating, greenhouses, burglar alarms and automatic doors.
- **Advantages**: readings are taken continuously, day and night, without a person present; more accurate than manual readings; readings can be taken in dangerous places; no human error when recording.
- **Disadvantages**: sensors can fail or give faulty readings if damaged or dirty; they need a computer or microprocessor to process the data; the analogue signal must be converted.

## Light pen

- **Characteristics**: a pen-shaped device with a light-sensitive tip, used directly on a suitable screen to select items or draw.
- **Uses**: selecting items or drawing on screen in specialist applications, such as some design and medical systems.
- **Advantages**: more precise than a finger on a screen; natural for drawing; no desk space needed.
- **Disadvantages**: works only with suitable screens; tiring to hold up against the screen for long periods; the hand and pen can block the user's view of the screen.

## Worked example: annotated model answer

*A hospital monitors patients' heart rate and temperature through the night. Explain why sensors are used rather than nurses taking readings by hand. [4]*

> Sensors take readings continuously (1), so a sudden change in a patient's condition is detected immediately rather than at the next hourly check (1). The readings are sent automatically to a computer, so there are no recording errors (1), and nurses are freed to care for patients instead of taking readings (1).

Each mark is a feature of sensors linked to a consequence for the hospital. An answer that only said "sensors are more accurate and faster" would score less, because neither point is developed.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a touch screen only as output.
- Saying a keyboard "is slow". It is slow for untrained users or for selecting items; it is fast for text entry by a trained typist.
- Writing that a sensor "decides" or "controls". Sensors only measure; the computer or microprocessor processes and decides.
- Confusing a scanner with OCR. The scanner produces an image; OCR software turns the image of text into editable text.
- Giving the same advantage for every device, such as "easy to use", without saying why.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** or **name** a device: one word, but choose the most specific one ("trackerball", not "pointing device", if the question describes a ball).
- **Describe** a use: say what the device does in that situation.
- **Give advantages** and **disadvantages**: make them specific to the device or the scenario.
- **Explain** or **justify** a choice: link a characteristic to a benefit with "so" or "because".
