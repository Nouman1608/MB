Condensed recall for **1.4 Water management futures** (IB DP Geography, Option A: Freshwater). The [Freshwater study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-freshwater-study-guide/) has the longer version.

## Two families of strategy

| Supply-side (add water) | Demand-side (reduce use) |
|---|---|
| Dams and reservoirs | Metering |
| Inter-basin transfers | Pricing |
| Desalination | Efficient irrigation |
| Rainwater harvesting | Leak repair |
| Groundwater abstraction | Changing diets and crops |

**Integrated water resources management (IWRM)** joins both sides and involves stakeholders: governments, farmers, industries, communities, non-governmental organisations and international bodies.

## Sustainability tests

Ask of any strategy:

- Does it meet today's needs without compromising future supply?
- Does it share costs and benefits fairly?
- Does it damage ecosystems or deplete a store faster than it recharges?
- Can it cope with an uncertain climate?

## Climate uncertainty

Climate change adds uncertainty through shifts in rainfall, loss of glaciers and snowpack, and more intense extremes. This weakens plans that assume past river flows will continue.

## Trade-offs to quote

- A reservoir can secure supply but displace communities and change downstream ecology.
- Groundwater abstraction is quick but can lower water tables if it exceeds recharge.
- Desalination gives a reliable source but is typically energy-intensive.
- Demand measures such as pricing can save water but raise fairness questions for poorer households.

## Building an evaluative answer

1. Define the strategy and say which side it acts on.
2. Give a located example you know well.
3. Weigh benefits and costs for different stakeholders.
4. Judge sustainability and fairness.
5. Conclude with a supported view and note the limits of your evidence.

## Exam traps

- Listing strategies with no evaluation of who gains and who loses.
- Treating one technology as a complete answer: IWRM combines approaches.
- Confusing supply-side and demand-side.
- Quoting invented cost or volume figures for a named scheme.

## Self-test

1. Give two supply-side and two demand-side approaches.
2. What does IWRM bring together?
3. State two questions that test whether a strategy is sustainable.
4. How does climate change complicate water planning?
5. Give one trade-off of building a reservoir.

**Answers:** 1. Supply: dams, desalination; demand: metering, leak repair. 2. Supply and demand measures, with all stakeholders. 3. Does it protect future supply? Does it share costs and benefits fairly? 4. It changes rainfall, snowpack and extremes, so past flows are a poor guide. 5. Secures supply but may displace communities and alter downstream ecology.
