## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which type of gene mutation always changes the reading frame of the triplets that follow it?

A: A substitution of the third base of a triplet

B: A substitution that produces a stop codon

C: A deletion of one base

D: A substitution that produces a codon for the same amino acid **[1]**

**2.** Give one physical mutagen and one chemical mutagen. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a benign tumour can still be dangerous. **[2]**

**4.** State the normal role of a proto-oncogene and of a tumour suppressor gene. **[2]**

**5.** A single cell in a tissue acquires a mutation. Explain why every cell in the tumour that later develops from it carries the same mutation. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** Part of the coding strand of a gene reads, from the start:

ATG CCA GAT TGG TCA TAA

Supplied DNA triplets: ATG Met; CCA and CCG Pro; GAT Asp; GAA Glu; TGG Trp; TCA Ser; CAG Gln; ATT Ile; GGT Gly; CAT His; TAA and TGA stop.

**(a)** Give the amino acid sequence coded for. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the effect of a substitution that changes CCA to CCG. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict the effect of a substitution that changes GAT to GAA. **[1]**

**(d)** Predict the effect of a substitution that changes TGG to TGA. **[1]**

**(e)** Predict the effect of deleting the first base of the second triplet (the C of CCA). Show the new triplets. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how a single base substitution in the gene for an enzyme could produce an enzyme that does not work. **[4]**

**8.** A study recorded the incidence of a cancer in workers exposed to different levels of a chemical at work.

| Exposure level | Workers studied | New cases over 10 years |
|---|---|---|
| None | 8 000 | 24 |
| Low | 5 000 | 25 |
| High | 2 000 | 18 |

**(a)** Calculate the incidence per 1000 workers over 10 years for each group. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate the conclusion that the chemical increases the risk of this cancer. **[3]**

**9.** In one cell, a proto-oncogene has mutated into an oncogene and a tumour suppressor gene has been inactivated. Explain why this cell is likely to divide much faster than a cell with only one of these mutations. **[3]**

**10.** A drug that inhibits DNA replication was added to a culture of cancer cells. The mitotic index fell from 9% to 1% over 24 hours. Explain this result. **[3]**

**11.** Anticancer drugs are often given as several doses separated by intervals of a few weeks. Suggest why. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why malignant tumours are usually more dangerous than benign tumours. **[4]**

**13.** Suggest why a substitution that changes one amino acid in an enzyme may have no effect on the enzyme's activity. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitutions keep the reading frame. Only the deletion shifts every following triplet.

**2.** Physical: ultraviolet light, or ionising radiation such as X-rays [1]. Chemical: a named or described chemical mutagen, such as certain chemicals in tobacco smoke [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Radiation" alone is vague; specify ultraviolet or ionising radiation.

**3.** It can grow large and press on nearby organs or tissues [1], damaging them or blocking vessels or ducts, for example in the brain [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Benign means it does not invade or spread, not that it is harmless.

**4.** Proto-oncogene: stimulates cell division [1]. Tumour suppressor gene: slows cell division [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Learn the pair as opposites: accelerator and brake.

**5.** The mutation is in the cell's DNA, which is replicated before each division [1]. Mitosis produces daughter cells with identical DNA, so every cell descended from the first carries the mutation [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link to the identical DNA produced by mitosis (section 3.2.10).

**6. (a)** Met-Pro-Asp-Trp-Ser [1].

**(b)** No change; CCG also codes for Pro (the code is degenerate) [1].

**(c)** Asp is replaced by Glu; the rest of the sequence is unchanged [1].

**(d)** TGA is a stop codon, so the chain is shortened to Met-Pro-Asp [1].

**(e)** New triplets: ATG CAG ATT GGT CAT AA [1]; sequence becomes Met-Gln-Ile-Gly-His, so every amino acid after Met changes and the original stop triplet is no longer read [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (e), re-divide from the start. Writing the triplets out earns the first mark even if a translation slips.

**7.** The substitution changes one triplet, so a different amino acid may be coded for [1]. The primary structure of the enzyme changes [1]. This can change the way the polypeptide folds, so its tertiary structure and the shape of the active site change [1]. The substrate no longer fits (is no longer complementary to) the active site, so no enzyme-substrate complexes form [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Build the chain from base to function. Skipping tertiary structure loses a mark.

**8. (a)** None: 24 ÷ 8000 × 1000 = **3.0**; low: 25 ÷ 5000 × 1000 = **5.0** [1]; high: 18 ÷ 2000 × 1000 = **9.0** per 1000 [1].

**(b)** Incidence rises with exposure: high exposure has three times the incidence of no exposure, which supports the conclusion [1]. However, this is a correlation; other variables such as age, smoking or other workplace exposures may differ between groups [1]. The numbers of cases are small, especially in the high group (18), so the difference could partly be due to chance; a mechanism (the chemical acting as a mutagen) would strengthen the conclusion [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The low group has the most cases in total but not the highest rate. Use rates throughout.

**9.** The oncogene stimulates the cell to divide too quickly [1]. The inactivated tumour suppressor gene no longer slows division [1]. With the accelerator stuck on and the brake lost at the same time, nothing restrains division, so the rate is higher than with either mutation alone [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat the two mutations as separate effects that add together.

**10.** Without DNA replication, cells cannot complete S phase [1]. They cannot proceed through G2 into mitosis, so fewer cells enter mitosis [1]. Cells already in mitosis finish dividing, so after some hours very few cells are in mitosis and the mitotic index falls [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Relate the drug to a specific phase of the cycle, as the specification asks.

**11.** The drugs also damage normal cells that divide often, such as those in the gut lining and hair follicles [1]. The intervals allow normal tissues to recover between doses, while repeated doses affect further cancer cells as they enter the stages the drug acts on [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Either point about recovery or about reaching more dividing cells earns credit; both are needed for two marks.

**12.** Malignant tumours usually grow faster [1]. They are not enclosed in a capsule, so they invade and destroy surrounding tissue [1]. Their cells can break away and travel in the blood or lymph [1] to form secondary tumours elsewhere in the body, so they are harder to remove completely [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Spread to form secondary tumours is the key difference; make sure it is in your answer.

**13.** The changed amino acid may not be in the active site, or may have a similar R group [1], so the tertiary structure and the shape of the active site are not significantly changed [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* This shows why a change in amino acid sequence does not always change the phenotype.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Re-dividing triplets from the mutation instead of from the start.
- Forgetting the degenerate code.
- Explaining enzyme failure without the tertiary structure step.
- Calling benign tumours harmless.
- Mixing up the roles of oncogenes and tumour suppressor genes.
- Comparing case numbers rather than incidence rates.
- Not linking anticancer drugs to a stage of the cell cycle.
