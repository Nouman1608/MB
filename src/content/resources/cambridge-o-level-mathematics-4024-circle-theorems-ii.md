This guide covers section 4.8, Circle theorems II, in the Geometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to use three symmetry properties of circles: equal chords are equidistant from the centre, the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre, and tangents from an external point are equal in length. You are expected to use the properties in your reasons. After this guide, use the [circle theorems II revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii-revision-notes/) and the [circle theorems II practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii-practice/).

## What section 4.8 asks for

These three properties are all about symmetry. They let you find lengths with Pythagoras' theorem and angles in kites and isosceles triangles. Typical questions ask for the length of a chord, the distance of a chord from the centre, the length of a tangent, or an angle in a diagram with tangents and radii.

## Property 1: the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre

Take a chord AB and its midpoint M. The line from the centre O to M is at right angles to AB. Equivalently, the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord, and the perpendicular bisector of any chord is a line through the centre.

Reason to write: **the perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord**, or **the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre**.

Why? OA and OB are radii, so triangle OAB is isosceles. The line of symmetry of an isosceles triangle runs from the apex O to the midpoint of the base AB, and meets it at 90°.

This produces a right-angled triangle OMA with:

- hypotenuse OA = the radius r,
- one side OM = the distance d from the centre to the chord,
- the other side AM = half of the chord.

```
r² = d² + (half the chord)²
```

### Worked example 1

A circle has radius 13 cm. A chord AB is 24 cm long. Find the distance from the centre to the chord.

```
AM = 24 ÷ 2 = 12      (the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord)
OM² = 13² − 12² = 169 − 144 = 25
OM = 5 cm
```

### Worked example 2

A circle has radius 10 cm. A chord is 6 cm from the centre. Find the length of the chord.

```
AM² = 10² − 6² = 100 − 36 = 64
AM = 8
chord = 2 × 8 = 16 cm
```

A common slip is to stop at 8, which is only half the chord.

### Worked example 3

O is the centre of a circle. Chord AB subtends an angle of 100° at O, and M is the midpoint of AB. Find angle OAM.

```
angle AOM = 100° ÷ 2 = 50°        (OM bisects the angle at the apex of the isosceles triangle OAB)
angle AMO = 90°                   (the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord)
angle OAM = 180° − 90° − 50° = 40°   (angle sum of a triangle)
```

## Property 2: equal chords are equidistant from the centre

Chords of the same length are the same distance from the centre. The converse is also true: chords that are the same distance from the centre have equal length.

Reason to write: **equal chords are equidistant from the centre**.

### Worked example 4

In a circle of radius 25 cm, chord AB is 7 cm from the centre and has length 48 cm. Chord CD is also 7 cm from the centre. Find the length of CD.

```
check AB: half = √(25² − 7²) = √(625 − 49) = √576 = 24, so AB = 48 cm
CD is the same distance from the centre as AB
so CD = 48 cm      (chords equidistant from the centre are equal)
```

### Worked example 5

Two equal chords PQ and RS in a circle of radius 10 cm each have length 16 cm. Find their distance from the centre.

```
half chord = 8
d² = 10² − 8² = 36
d = 6 cm  for both chords      (equal chords are equidistant from the centre)
```

As a chord moves closer to the centre, it gets longer. The longest chord is a diameter, at distance 0.

### Worked example 6

A circle has radius 13 cm. Two parallel chords have lengths 10 cm and 24 cm. Find the possible distances between the chords.

```
10 cm chord: half = 5, distance from centre = √(169 − 25) = √144 = 12 cm
24 cm chord: half = 12, distance from centre = √(169 − 144) = √25 = 5 cm
chords on opposite sides of the centre: 12 + 5 = 17 cm apart
chords on the same side of the centre:  12 − 5 = 7 cm apart
```

Parallel chords can lie on either side of the centre, so there are two answers unless the diagram shows which.

### Worked example 7

A chord AB of length 8 cm is drawn in a circle of radius 5 cm. Find the greatest height of the minor segment above the chord.

```
half chord = 4, distance from centre = √(25 − 16) = 3 cm
the segment height = radius − distance = 5 − 3 = 2 cm
```

## Property 3: tangents from an external point are equal

If two tangents are drawn to a circle from a point P outside it, touching the circle at A and B, then PA = PB.

Reason to write: **tangents from an external point are equal in length**.

Four useful facts about the diagram:

- OA and OB are radii, so OA = OB.
- Angle OAP = angle OBP = 90° (tangent and radius).
- OP is a line of symmetry. It bisects angle APB and angle AOB.
- OAPB is a kite, and triangle PAB is isosceles.

### Worked example 8

P is 17 cm from the centre O of a circle of radius 8 cm. A tangent from P touches the circle at T. Find PT.

```
angle OTP = 90°                    (tangent and radius)
PT² = OP² − OT² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225
PT = 15 cm
```

### Worked example 9

Tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B. Angle APB = 50°. Find angles PAB, OAB and AOB.

```
PA = PB                                  (tangents from an external point are equal)
angle PAB = (180° − 50°) ÷ 2 = 65°       (isosceles triangle, angle sum of a triangle)
angle OAP = 90°                          (tangent and radius)
angle OAB = 90° − 65° = 25°
angle AOB = 360° − 90° − 90° − 50° = 130°   (angle sum of the quadrilateral OAPB)
```

Check: triangle OAB has angles 25°, 25° and 130°, which add up to 180°.

### Worked example 10

The tangents from P have length 12 cm and the radius is 5 cm. Find OP and the area of the kite OAPB.

```
OP² = 12² + 5² = 169, so OP = 13 cm
area of triangle OAP = 1/2 × 5 × 12 = 30 cm²
area of the kite = 2 × 30 = 60 cm²
```

### Tangents from the three vertices of a triangle

A circle that touches all three sides of a triangle gives pairs of equal tangents at each vertex. For triangle ABC with AB = 13, BC = 14 and CA = 15, let the circle touch the sides at D, E and F.

```
AD = AF = x, BD = BE = y, CE = CF = z
x + y = 13, y + z = 14, x + z = 15
adding: 2(x + y + z) = 42, so x + y + z = 21
x = 21 − 14 = 7, y = 21 − 15 = 6, z = 21 − 13 = 8
```

So AD = AF = 7 cm, BD = BE = 6 cm and CE = CF = 8 cm.

## Common mistakes

- Using the whole chord rather than half in Pythagoras' theorem.
- Stopping after finding half of the chord.
- Using the radius as the distance from the centre to the chord.
- Forgetting that the distance from a point to a chord is the perpendicular distance.
- Writing "tangents are equal" without saying "from an external point".
- Missing the right angles between tangents and radii.

## Exam technique

- Draw the perpendicular from the centre to the chord, label the midpoint and mark the right angle.
- Write the reason next to each length or angle.
- **Calculate** the length: show Pythagoras with the numbers.
- Keep exact values or give three significant figures.
