These notes condense section **5.5 Clubs and societies** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. The section covers "receipts and payments accounts and income and expenditure accounts", accounts for "revenue-generating activities, e.g. refreshments, subscriptions", statements of financial position, the 5.1 adjustments and how to "define and calculate the accumulated fund". The full worked year for one club is in the [clubs and societies study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-clubs-and-societies/); test yourself with the [clubs and societies practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-clubs-and-societies-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Receipts and payments account | A summary of the club's cash or bank transactions for the year, ending with the closing balance |
| Income and expenditure account | The club's statement of income earned and expenses incurred for the year, on the accruals basis |
| Surplus | Income greater than expenditure |
| Deficit | Expenditure greater than income |
| Accumulated fund | Total assets less total liabilities of the club; the members' stake, in place of capital |
| Subscriptions in arrears | Subscriptions owed by members at a date; an other receivable |
| Subscriptions in advance | Subscriptions received for a later period; an other payable |
| Revenue-generating activity | An activity such as refreshments or an event that earns income, accounted for separately so its profit or loss is shown |

## Club words replace business words

| Business | Club |
|---|---|
| Capital | Accumulated fund |
| Profit for the year | Surplus of income over expenditure |
| Loss for the year | Deficit of expenditure over income |
| Statement of profit or loss | Income and expenditure account |
| Cash book summary | Receipts and payments account |

## The workings to know

**Opening accumulated fund** = all assets at the start (including cash, inventory and subscriptions in arrears) - all liabilities at the start (including subscriptions in advance and amounts owing).

**Subscriptions income** = received - arrears at start + advance at start + arrears at end - advance at end.

**Subscriptions account** (ledger form): debit side holds arrears b/d, the transfer to income and expenditure and advance c/d; credit side holds advance b/d, bank and arrears c/d.

**Refreshment purchases** = paid to suppliers - owing at start + owing at end.

**Cost of refreshments sold** = opening inventory + purchases - closing inventory. **Profit on refreshments** = sales - cost of refreshments sold (less any refreshment wages if the question gives them).

**Closing accumulated fund** = opening accumulated fund + surplus (or - deficit).

## What goes where

| Item | Receipts and payments | Income and expenditure | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash received for subscriptions | Yes | Adjusted figure | Arrears or advance at the end |
| Refreshment sales and purchases | Yes, in full | Profit or loss only | Refreshment inventory and payables |
| Purchase of equipment | Yes | No (depreciation only) | Non-current asset |
| Depreciation | No | Yes | Lower book value |
| Expenses owing at the end | No | Added to the expense | Current liability |
| Opening and closing bank | Yes | No | Current asset (or overdraft) |

## Exam traps

- Purchases of equipment never go in the income and expenditure account.
- Arrears at the start belong to last year; subtract them from cash received.
- Advances at the start are this year's income; add them.
- Show only the profit or loss on refreshments in the income and expenditure account.
- A subscription that will not be collected is written off, not kept as an asset.
- Use "surplus", "deficit" and "accumulated fund", not "profit" and "capital".
- The accumulated fund at the start must include every opening asset and liability, including cash.
- A receipts and payments account can show a high bank balance even when the club made a deficit.

## Self-test

1. Define the accumulated fund.
2. State two differences between a receipts and payments account and an income and expenditure account.
3. At the start of the year a club had equipment $5,000, bank $1,200, subscriptions in arrears $150 and subscriptions in advance $90. Calculate the accumulated fund.
4. Subscriptions received were $4,800. At the start, arrears were $200 and advances $120. At the end, arrears were $160 and advances $300. Calculate subscriptions income.
5. Refreshment sales were $3,600. Opening refreshment inventory was $300, purchases were $2,100 and closing inventory was $450. Calculate the profit on refreshments.
6. A club paid refreshment suppliers $2,000. It owed them $250 at the start and $180 at the end. Calculate refreshment purchases.
7. Where do subscriptions in advance at the end of the year appear?
8. Why is the cost of new equipment not shown in the income and expenditure account?
9. The accumulated fund at the start was $9,400 and the club made a deficit of $1,150. Calculate the closing accumulated fund.
10. What name is given to an excess of expenditure over income?
11. A club decides that $60 of subscriptions in arrears will never be paid. State the effect on subscriptions income and on the statement of financial position.
12. Explain why a receipts and payments account does not show whether a club covered its running costs.

### Answers

1. The total assets less the total liabilities of a club: the members' interest, which takes the place of capital.
2. Any two: receipts and payments records cash, income and expenditure records amounts earned and incurred; receipts and payments includes capital items, income and expenditure includes revenue items only; receipts and payments ends with a bank balance, income and expenditure ends with a surplus or deficit; only income and expenditure includes depreciation, accruals and prepayments.
3. Assets 5,000 + 1,200 + 150 = 6,350; less 90 = **$6,260**.
4. 4,800 - 200 + 120 + 160 - 300 = **$4,580**.
5. Cost of refreshments sold = 300 + 2,100 - 450 = $1,950. Profit = 3,600 - 1,950 = **$1,650**.
6. 2,000 - 250 + 180 = **$1,930**.
7. Under current liabilities, as an other payable.
8. It is capital expenditure on an asset that lasts several years; only the depreciation for the year is expenditure.
9. 9,400 - 1,150 = **$8,250**.
10. A deficit.
11. Subscriptions income (or the transfer to income and expenditure) falls by **$60**, and subscriptions in arrears in the statement of financial position fall by $60.
12. It records only cash, so it includes capital items such as equipment bought, and leaves out amounts owing, amounts prepaid and depreciation. Its closing figure is the bank balance, not a surplus or deficit.
