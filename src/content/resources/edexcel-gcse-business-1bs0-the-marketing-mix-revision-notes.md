These notes condense content item 1.4.3, The marketing mix, in Topic 1.4 (Making the business effective) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples are in the [marketing mix study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-marketing-mix/); test yourself with the [marketing mix practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-marketing-mix-practice/). The wider [marketing mix and business plan revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-the-marketing-mix-and-the-business-plan-revision-notes/) link 1.4.3 to 1.4.4.

## What the specification lists

- What the marketing mix is and the importance of each element: price, product, promotion, place.
- How the elements work together: balancing the mix based on the competitive environment; the impact of changing consumer needs; the impact of technology (e-commerce, digital communication).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | The combination of price, product, promotion and place a business uses to sell to its target market |
| Product | The good or service offered, including its features, quality and design |
| Price | The amount the customer pays |
| Promotion | Communication that informs customers and persuades them to buy |
| Place | How and where customers obtain the product |
| Balancing the mix | Making the four elements consistent with each other and with customer expectations |
| Competitive environment | The number and strength of rivals and what they offer |
| Consumer needs | What customers want or require from a product |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling online |
| Digital communication | Electronic communication with customers, such as email, social media, websites and messaging |

## Importance of each element at start-up

- **Product:** gives an unknown business a reason to be chosen; must meet a real need.
- **Price:** must cover costs and still attract first customers; also signals quality.
- **Promotion:** creates awareness; without it a new business is invisible.
- **Place:** makes buying easy; a poor place loses sales to more convenient rivals.

Which matters most depends on the business: convenience businesses lean on place; crowded markets lean on product and promotion.

## How the elements work together

A change in one element needs matching changes in others.

- Higher price → better product and promotion that justifies the price.
- New place (online) → price must survive easy comparison; promotion must drive traffic to the website.
- New product (premium) → place and promotion that suit premium buyers.

## Balancing against competitors

| If rivals... | A start-up might... |
|---|---|
| Are many and similar | Differentiate the product; promote the difference |
| Compete hard on price | Avoid a price war; compete on quality, service or convenience |
| Are few | Use freedom on price; spend on awareness |
| Sell only from shops | Use delivery or online ordering as the point of difference |

## Changing consumer needs

Triggers: tastes, lifestyles, incomes, concerns such as health or the environment.

- Always name which element changes and how.
- Responding keeps customers; ignoring change risks losing them to rivals.
- Responding to a passing trend can waste money.

## Technology and the mix

| Technology | Main elements affected | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| E-commerce | Place, price, product | Sell beyond the local area; easy price comparison; wider range shown online |
| Digital communication | Promotion, product | Cheap targeted promotion; quick feedback and reviews; rivals use the same channels |

## Calculations that appear with the mix

- Revenue = price × quantity.
- Percentage change in sales = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- Revenue from a promotion compared with its cost.

Example: a price cut from £8 to £7 raises weekly sales from 300 to 360. Revenue before = £2,400; after = £2,520; sales rise by 60 ÷ 300 × 100 = 20%.

## Exam traps

- Listing the four Ps separately when the question asks how they work together.
- Assuming lower price is always the answer to competition.
- Confusing e-commerce (selling online: place) with digital communication (promotion).
- Saying "change the mix" without naming the element.
- Forgetting that a promotion's revenue is not its profit.

## Self-test

1. Name the four elements of the marketing mix.
2. What does "balancing the mix" mean?
3. Why might a very low price harm a new premium product?
4. Give one reason promotion is especially important for a start-up.
5. A rival cuts its prices. Give one response other than cutting price.
6. Customers increasingly want to avoid plastic packaging. Which element changes first, and how?
7. Which element of the mix is most affected by e-commerce?
8. Give one benefit and one drawback of digital communication for promotion.
9. A café raises its price from £3.00 to £3.30. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. A shop sells 400 units at £5. After a promotion it sells 460 units at £5. Calculate the increase in revenue.
11. Explain why a higher price may need a change to the product.
12. Why is it a risk to respond to every change in consumer needs?

### Answers

1. Price, product, promotion, place.
2. Making the four elements consistent with each other and with what customers expect.
3. A low price can signal low quality, which contradicts the premium product and promotion.
4. A new business is unknown, so customers need to be made aware it exists.
5. Any one: improve the product; stress quality or service in promotion; offer greater convenience (place).
6. Product: switch to recyclable or refillable packaging (promotion may then highlight the change).
7. Place.
8. Benefit: low-cost, targeted promotion with quick feedback. Drawback: rivals use the same channels, or negative reviews spread quickly.
9. £0.30 ÷ £3.00 × 100 = 10%.
10. Before: £2,000. After: £2,300. Increase = £300.
11. Customers expect more for a higher price, so without a better product they may feel it is poor value and buy elsewhere.
12. Some trends are short-lived, so the business may spend money changing the mix and then lose the benefit.
