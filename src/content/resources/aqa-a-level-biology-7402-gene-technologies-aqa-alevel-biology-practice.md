These questions cover section 3.8.4 of the AQA AS and A-level Biology specification (7401/7402), for first teaching 2015: recombinant DNA technology (3.8.4.1), probes and screening (3.8.4.2) and genetic fingerprinting (3.8.4.3), all A-level only. ## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following enzymes joins a DNA fragment into a plasmid by forming phosphodiester bonds? **[1]**

A: Restriction endonuclease

B: Reverse transcriptase

C: DNA ligase

D: RNA polymerase

**2.** In gel electrophoresis, which DNA fragments travel the furthest from the wells in a given time? **[1]**

A: The longest fragments, towards the negative electrode

B: The shortest fragments, towards the positive electrode

C: The longest fragments, towards the positive electrode

D: The shortest fragments, towards the negative electrode

**3.** Explain why DNA made from mRNA using reverse transcriptase is suitable for producing a human protein in bacteria. **[2]**

**4.** A sample contains 12 molecules of a DNA fragment. Calculate the number of molecules after 15 cycles of PCR, assuming every molecule is copied in each cycle. **[2]**

**5.** A restriction endonuclease has four recognition sites in a linear piece of DNA and two recognition sites in a circular plasmid.

**(a)** How many fragments are produced from the linear DNA? **[1]**

**(b)** How many fragments are produced from the plasmid? **[1]**

**6.** State what is meant by a variable number tandem repeat (VNTR) and explain why VNTRs are useful for identifying individuals. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** A plasmid carries two marker genes: one gives resistance to antibiotic P and the other gives resistance to antibiotic Q. A gene was inserted into the middle of the gene for resistance to Q. Bacteria were mixed with the plasmids and then spread on agar containing antibiotic P, where 160 colonies grew. Samples of every colony were then transferred to agar containing antibiotic Q, where 124 colonies grew.

**(a)** How many colonies contain the recombinant plasmid? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of colonies on antibiotic P that contain the recombinant plasmid. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the gene was inserted into a marker gene rather than elsewhere in the plasmid. **[2]**

**8.** A company wants bacteria to produce a human hormone. Explain how it should obtain the gene and prepare it so that the bacteria will produce the hormone. **[4]**

**9.** DNA from a blood stain and from three suspects was amplified and separated by gel electrophoresis. Band sizes in kilobases are shown.

| Sample | Bands (kilobases) |
|---|---|
| Blood stain | 8.2, 5.6, 3.1, 1.9 |
| Suspect 1 | 8.2, 5.6, 2.4, 1.9 |
| Suspect 2 | 8.2, 5.6, 3.1, 1.9 |
| Suspect 3 | 7.0, 5.6, 3.1, 1.2 |

**(a)** Explain why the DNA from the blood stain was amplified by PCR before electrophoresis. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify which suspect's DNA matches the blood stain, and explain why this is strong but not absolute evidence. **[3]**

**10.** Conservationists fingerprinted 30 animals from each of two captive populations of an endangered species at the same VNTR regions. Population A showed 22 different band patterns and population B showed 7.

**(a)** Which population has the greater genetic variability? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest how the conservationists could use these results in a breeding programme. **[2]**

**11.** Patients can be screened with a DNA probe for an allele that slows the breakdown of a drug. In a trial, 40 patients with the allele and 160 without it were given the standard dose. Twelve patients with the allele and six without it had serious side effects.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group with serious side effects. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate the use of this screening before the drug is prescribed. **[4]**

**12.** A condition is caused by a recessive allele that affects cells lining the airways. Explain how recombinant DNA technology could be used in gene therapy for this condition, and suggest why the treatment might need repeating. **[4]**

**13.** A crop plant has been genetically modified to produce a protein that kills insect pests. Evaluate the use of this crop, considering humanitarian, environmental and anti-globalisation viewpoints. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The mRNA has already had its introns removed by splicing, so the cDNA has no introns [1]; bacteria cannot remove introns, so they can translate the cDNA into the correct polypeptide [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 12 × 2¹⁵ [1] = 12 × 32,768 = **393,216** molecules [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 5 [1].

**(b)** 2 [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* cutting a line n times gives n + 1 pieces; cutting a circle n times gives n pieces.

**6.** A short base sequence repeated one after another, where the number of repeats varies between individuals [1]; the probability of two individuals having the same VNTRs is very low, so the pattern of fragment lengths is almost unique to each person [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 160 − 124 = **36** colonies [1]. They grow on P, so they took up a plasmid, but not on Q, so the Q resistance gene has been disrupted by the inserted gene [1].

**(b)** 36 ÷ 160 × 100 = **22.5%** [1].

**(c)** The inserted gene disrupts the marker gene, so its product is no longer made [1]; this distinguishes bacteria with a recombinant plasmid from bacteria with a plasmid that has no inserted gene [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Isolate mRNA for the hormone from cells that make large amounts of it and use reverse transcriptase to make cDNA (or make the gene in a gene machine) [1], because the gene must have no introns, which bacteria cannot remove [1]. Add a promoter so that RNA polymerase binds and transcription starts, and a terminator so it stops [1]. Insert the gene into a plasmid using the same restriction endonuclease and DNA ligase, then transform the bacteria with it [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Only a tiny amount of DNA is present, so PCR makes enough copies to produce visible bands [1].

**(b)** Suspect 2: all four bands match the blood stain [1]. The probability of two people having the same VNTR pattern is very low, so this is strong evidence that the blood came from suspect 2 [1]. It is not absolute: only four bands are compared, close relatives such as identical twins share patterns, and contamination or errors are possible; other evidence is needed [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Population A [1]: more different band patterns means more different VNTR alleles, so more genetic variability [1].

**(b)** Choose breeding pairs with the most different fingerprints, to avoid inbreeding [1]; move animals from A into B (or between populations) to increase B's genetic variability [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** With the allele: 12 ÷ 40 × 100 = **30%** [1]. Without: 6 ÷ 160 × 100 = **3.75%** [1].

**(b)** Patients with the allele are 8 times more likely to have serious side effects, so screening identifies people who need a lower dose or a different drug: an example of personalised medicine [1]. It may also avoid wasted treatment and costs from side effects [1]. However, 70% of patients with the allele had no serious side effects, and some patients without it did, so the test only indicates a risk; screening has a cost and raises privacy concerns [1]. The trial is fairly small; overall screening seems worthwhile if it is cheap and the data are confirmed in a larger trial [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "evaluate" needs a judgement. Use the percentages, then say what the data cannot show.

**12.** Make the functional (dominant) allele, for example as cDNA, and add a promoter [1]. Insert it into a vector such as a modified harmless virus or a liposome [1]. The vector delivers the allele into the airway cells, which can then make the functional protein [1]. This is somatic therapy: the treated cells die and are replaced by cells without the allele, so the treatment needs repeating [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Humanitarian: higher yields from less crop loss can improve food supply and farmers' incomes [1]; less insecticide spraying may reduce cost and harm to farm workers [1]. Environmental: the protein may harm non-target insects, and the gene could spread to wild relatives [1]; pests may evolve resistance to the protein over time [1]. Anti-globalisation: seed is patented by large companies, so farmers may have to buy seed each season and poorer farmers may be disadvantaged [1]. Judgement: the crop is likely to be beneficial where pest losses are severe, provided its effects on other species are monitored and seed is affordable [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* one point from each viewpoint plus a reasoned conclusion is the minimum for a strong answer; a list of benefits alone cannot reach the top marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying cDNA is made from DNA, or that ligase cuts DNA.
- Forgetting promoter and terminator regions when explaining how a gene is expressed.
- In marker gene questions, not saying which antibiotic each colony grows on and why.
- Treating a fingerprint match as proof rather than strong evidence.
- Evaluating screening or GM crops with no use of data and no conclusion.
