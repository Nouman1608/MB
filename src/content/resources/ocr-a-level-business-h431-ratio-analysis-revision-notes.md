Condensed notes on the **Ratio analysis** content (6.12 on this site) in the Area of Study **Financial accounting**, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and worked ratios for two years are in the [ratio analysis study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-ratio-analysis/); test yourself with the [ratio analysis practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-ratio-analysis-practice/). The whole topic is in the [accounting and financial considerations revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-accounting-and-financial-considerations-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

- Liquidity: current ratio, acid test.
- Solvency: gearing, interest cover.
- Efficiency: creditor turnover, debtor turnover, non-current assets turnover, stock turnover.
- Profitability: gross profit margin, net profit margin, return on capital employed (ROCE), return on equity.
- Shareholder: dividend per share, dividend yield, earnings per share, price/earnings ratio.
- Evaluate the financial position of a business; evaluate the usefulness of ratio analysis.

## Core recall

### Formulae (standard form: use the form given in the question)

| Group | Ratio | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Liquidity | Current ratio | Current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Liquidity | Acid test | (Current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities |
| Solvency | Gearing | Non-current liabilities ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Solvency | Interest cover | Operating profit ÷ interest |
| Efficiency | Debtor turnover (days) | Receivables ÷ revenue × 365 |
| Efficiency | Creditor turnover (days) | Payables ÷ cost of sales × 365 |
| Efficiency | Non-current assets turnover | Revenue ÷ non-current assets |
| Efficiency | Stock turnover (times) | Cost of sales ÷ inventory |
| Profitability | Gross profit margin | Gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Profitability | Net profit margin | Profit for the year ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Profitability | ROCE | Operating profit ÷ capital employed × 100 |
| Profitability | Return on equity | Profit for the year ÷ shareholders' equity × 100 |
| Shareholder | Earnings per share | Profit for the year ÷ number of shares |
| Shareholder | Dividend per share | Dividends ÷ number of shares |
| Shareholder | Dividend yield | Dividend per share ÷ share price × 100 |
| Shareholder | Price/earnings | Share price ÷ earnings per share |

Capital employed = equity + non-current liabilities.

### What results mean

| Ratio | Higher means | Watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Current ratio | Better short-term cover | Too high: idle assets |
| Acid test | Cover without selling inventory | Below 1: may struggle |
| Gearing | More borrowing, more risk | High with falling profit |
| Interest cover | Easier to pay interest | Low: vulnerable |
| Debtor days | Customers pay slower | Cash tied up |
| Creditor days | Business pays slower | Supplier relationships |
| Stock turnover | Stock sold faster | Too high: stock-outs |
| Gross margin | Better price or cost control | Fall suggests cost pressure |
| Net margin | Better overall cost control | One-off items |
| ROCE, return on equity | Better return on funds | Compare with the cost of finance |
| EPS | More profit per share | Depends on shares in issue |
| Dividend yield | More cash return | Low yield may be growth stock |
| P/E | Investors expect growth | Compare with similar companies |

### Evaluating financial position: structure

1. Liquidity: can it pay short-term debts?
2. Solvency: is borrowing sustainable?
3. Efficiency: is cash tied up?
4. Profitability: is the return good and improving?
5. Shareholders: are they rewarded?
6. Judgement, and what could change it.

### Usefulness

- Strengths: comparable percentages; trends; benchmarking.
- Limits: historic; policies differ; snapshot; need benchmarks; ignore non-financial factors; show what, not why.

## Quick worked reminder

Marsden Textiles plc (invented), Year 2: current ratio 2.00 (down from 2.45); acid test 1.18; gearing 28.7%; interest cover 7.5; debtor days 54.8 (up from 49.2); stock turnover 5.78 (down from 6.40); gross margin 35.0% (down from 36.0%); net margin 8.7%; ROCE 19.1%; EPS 41.6p; yield 3.1%; P/E 12.5. Profitable and not over-borrowed, but cash is tied up.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liquidity | Ability to pay short-term debts |
| Solvency | Ability to meet long-term debts |
| Gearing | Share of capital employed that is borrowed long term |
| Capital employed | Equity plus non-current liabilities |
| ROCE | Operating profit as a percentage of capital employed |
| Return on equity | Profit for the year as a percentage of owners' equity |
| Earnings per share | Profit for the year per share |
| Dividend yield | Dividend as a percentage of the share price |
| Price/earnings ratio | Share price divided by earnings per share |

## Exam traps

- Debtor days use revenue; creditor days use cost of sales.
- Gearing uses non-current liabilities only (in the standard form).
- Interest cover uses operating profit, not net profit.
- Give units: %, times, days, pence.
- A ratio alone says little; compare with last year, rivals or targets.

## Self-test

1. Current assets £330,000, current liabilities £150,000. Calculate the current ratio.
2. Inventory is £180,000. Calculate the acid test.
3. Non-current liabilities £300,000; capital employed £1,080,000. Calculate gearing.
4. Operating profit £180,000; interest £30,000. Calculate interest cover.
5. Receivables £90,000; revenue £1,800,000. Calculate debtor days.
6. Cost of sales £1,170,000; inventory £180,000. Calculate stock turnover.
7. Operating profit £180,000; capital employed £1,080,000. Calculate ROCE.
8. Profit £120,000; 400,000 shares. Calculate EPS.
9. Dividends £60,000; 400,000 shares; price £4.50. Calculate dividend per share and yield.
10. Calculate the P/E ratio.
11. Why can a high current ratio be a problem?
12. Why might debtor days rising cause concern?

### Answers

1. £330,000 ÷ £150,000 = **2.2**.
2. (£330,000 − £180,000) ÷ £150,000 = **1.0**.
3. 300,000 ÷ 1,080,000 × 100 = **27.8%**.
4. 180,000 ÷ 30,000 = **6 times**.
5. 90,000 ÷ 1,800,000 × 365 = **18.25 days** (about 18).
6. 1,170,000 ÷ 180,000 = **6.5 times**.
7. 180,000 ÷ 1,080,000 × 100 = **16.7%**.
8. 120,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **30.0p**.
9. DPS = 60,000 ÷ 400,000 = 15.0p; yield = 15 ÷ 450 × 100 = **3.3%**.
10. 450 ÷ 30.0 = **15.0**.
11. It may mean too much money is tied up in stock or idle cash, earning little.
12. Customers are taking longer to pay, which ties up cash and raises the risk of bad debts.
