These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 6.02, Algebraic formulae (6.02a to 6.02e). Questions 1 to 8 and 11 are for both tiers. Questions 9 and 10 use content that is **Higher tier only** and are labelled. The specification says no formula sheet is provided, so recall the standard formulae before you start. Show your working on every question.

## Questions

**1.** A hall costs £90 to hire plus £6.50 for each guest.

**(a)** Write a formula for the total cost £C for n guests. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the cost for 48 guests. **[1]**
**(c)** A club has a budget of £500. Find the greatest number of guests it can afford. **[2]**

**2.** Work out the value of each expression.

**(a)** P = 3a − 2b when a = 4 and b = −3. **[2]**
**(b)** y = x³ − 3x² + 2 when x = −2. **[2]**
**(c)** T = (p − q²)/(2q) when p = 7 and q = −3. **[2]**

**3.** Rearrange each formula.

**(a)** Make x the subject of y = 4x − 9. **[2]**
**(b)** Make w the subject of V = lwh. **[1]**
**(c)** Make f the subject of c = 5(f − 32)/9. **[3]**

**4.** Rearrange each formula.

**(a)** Make r the subject of A = πr². **[2]**
**(b)** Make a the subject of v² = u² + 2as. **[2]**
**(c)** Make x the subject of y = 5√(x − 1). **[3]**

**5.** Rearrange each formula.

**(a)** Make x the subject of y = (x + 4)/(x − 2). **[4]**
**(b)** Make n the subject of 2/k = 1/m + 3/n. **[3]**

**6.** A circular lawn has diameter 9.6 m.

**(a)** Find its circumference to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its area to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** A different circle has area 64π cm². Find its circumference in terms of π. **[3]**

**7.** A ramp rises 0.45 m over a horizontal distance of 5.4 m.

**(a)** Find the length of the ramp to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the angle the ramp makes with the horizontal to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**8.** Use the constant-acceleration formulae.

**(a)** A car travelling at 8 m/s accelerates at 1.2 m/s² for 10 s. Find its final velocity and the distance it travels in this time. **[4]**
**(b)** A cyclist brakes from 20 m/s and stops after 80 m. Find the acceleration. **[3]**

**9.** **(Higher tier only)** Use the quadratic formula.

**(a)** Solve 3x² − 8x − 2 = 0. Give both answers to 2 decimal places. **[4]**
**(b)** An object has initial velocity 4 m/s and constant acceleration 2 m/s². Find the time it takes to travel 30 m, to 2 decimal places. **[4]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** In triangle PQR, PQ = 8.4 cm, PR = 6.8 cm and angle QPR = 63°.

**(a)** Find QR to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Find angle PQR to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the area of the triangle to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** A stone is dropped from rest from a bridge. Take downwards as positive and a = 9.8 m/s².

**(a)** Show that the distance fallen is s = 4.9t² after t seconds. **[1]**
**(b)** Make t the subject of s = 4.9t². **[2]**
**(c)** Find the time taken to fall 44.1 m. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the speed of the stone at that moment. **[2]**
**(e)** Use v² = u² + 2as to check your answer to part (d). **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Fixed charge 90 [1]; 6.5n for the guests, so **C = 90 + 6.5n** [1]
**(b)** 90 + 6.5 × 48 = 90 + 312 = **£402** [1]
**(c)** 90 + 6.5n ≤ 500 gives n ≤ 410/6.5 = 63.08 [1]; so **63 guests** (the next guest would take the cost over £500) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), a decimal such as 63.08 needs interpreting. Write the whole-number conclusion in words so the context is answered.

**2. (a)** 3(4) − 2(−3) = 12 + 6 [1]; **18** [1]
**(b)** (−2)³ − 3(−2)² + 2 = −8 − 12 + 2 [1]; **−18** [1]
**(c)** Top 7 − 9 = −2, bottom 2(−3) = −6 [1]; T = **1/3** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the substituted line with brackets around each negative value. If a sign slips later, a clear substitution line still shows the method.

**3. (a)** y + 9 = 4x [1]; **x = (y + 9)/4** [1]
**(b)** **w = V/(lh)** [1]
**(c)** 9c = 5(f − 32) [1]; 9c/5 = f − 32 [1]; **f = 9c/5 + 32** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Make one change per line and keep the equals signs aligned, so each step can be followed. Two operations at once is where errors creep in.

**4. (a)** A/π = r² [1]; **r = √(A/π)** [1]
**(b)** v² − u² = 2as [1]; **a = (v² − u²)/(2s)** [1]
**(c)** y/5 = √(x − 1) [1]; y²/25 = x − 1 [1]; **x = y²/25 + 1** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) isolate the root before squaring. Squaring 5√(x − 1) as it stands would also need the 5 squared, which is easy to forget.

**5. (a)** y(x − 2) = x + 4 [1]; xy − 2y = x + 4 [1]; xy − x = 4 + 2y [1]; **x = (2y + 4)/(y − 1)** [1]
**(b)** 3/n = 2/k − 1/m = (2m − k)/(km) [1]; n/3 = km/(2m − k) [1]; **n = 3km/(2m − k)** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* When the subject appears twice, the factorising line (x(y − 1) = 2y + 4) is the key step. Writing it out shows you have collected the subject terms correctly.

**6. (a)** C = π × 9.6 [1]; **30.2 m** [1]
**(b)** r = 4.8, so A = π × 4.8² [1]; **72.4 m²** [1]
**(c)** πr² = 64π, so r = 8 [1]; C = 2π × 8 [1]; **16π cm** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) gives the diameter, so use C = πd. The radius is needed for the area. Units differ too: m for length, m² for area.

**7. (a)** l² = 0.45² + 5.4² [1]; l² = 29.3625 [1]; **l = 5.42 m** [1]
**(b)** tan θ = 0.45/5.4 [1]; θ = tan⁻¹(1/12) [1]; **θ = 4.76°** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A sketch with the right angle and sides labelled hypotenuse, opposite and adjacent helps you choose between Pythagoras and a trigonometric ratio. Use the original values in part (b) and not the rounded length from part (a).

**8. (a)** v = 8 + 1.2 × 10 [1]; **v = 20 m/s** [1]; s = 8 × 10 + ½ × 1.2 × 10² [1]; **s = 140 m** [1]
**(b)** 0 = 20² + 2a(80) [1]; 160a = −400 [1]; **a = −2.5 m/s²** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Stops" tells you v = 0. List u, v, a, s and t before choosing a formula, and note the negative sign means the cyclist is slowing down.

**9. (a)** a = 3, b = −8, c = −2 [1]; b² − 4ac = 64 + 24 = 88 [1]; x = (8 ± √88)/6 [1]; **x = 2.90 or x = −0.23** [1]
**(b)** 30 = 4t + ½ × 2 × t², so t² + 4t − 30 = 0 [1]; t = (−4 ± √(16 + 120))/2 [1]; t = 3.83 or −7.83 [1]; time cannot be negative, so **t = 3.83 s** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Both ± values are worth stating in part (a). In part (b), explain why you reject the negative root, since time must be positive.

**10. (a)** QR² = 8.4² + 6.8² − 2(8.4)(6.8)cos 63° [1]; QR² = 64.94 [1]; **QR = 8.06 cm** [1]
**(b)** sin Q/6.8 = sin 63°/QR [1]; sin Q = 0.7519 [1]; **Q = 48.8°** [1]
**(c)** ½ × 8.4 × 6.8 × sin 63° [1]; **25.4 cm²** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded value of QR in part (b). Using the rounded 8.06 gives 48.7°, which would change the last digit. Round only the final answer.

**11. (a)** s = ut + ½at² with u = 0 and a = 9.8 gives **s = 4.9t²** [1]
**(b)** t² = s/4.9 [1]; **t = √(s/4.9)** [1]
**(c)** t = √(44.1/4.9) = √9 [1]; **t = 3 s** [1]
**(d)** v = u + at = 0 + 9.8 × 3 [1]; **v = 29.4 m/s** [1]
**(e)** v² = 29.4² = 864.36 [1]; 0 + 2 × 9.8 × 44.1 = 864.36, which agrees [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, write the formula, state u = 0, and substitute, so the link between the two is complete. A bare final line does not demonstrate the result.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping brackets around a negative number, so (−3)² becomes −9.
- Leaving the new subject on both sides of the formula.
- Squaring only part of an expression when removing a square root.
- Dividing only one term of a numerator by a number.
- Using the diameter as the radius in the area formula, or the other way round.
- Rounding intermediate values, which can shift the final digit of an answer.
- Missing hidden information such as "dropped" or "from rest" meaning u = 0, or "stops" meaning v = 0.
- Giving a decimal as the answer to a context question when a whole number is asked for.
- Reporting both roots of a quadratic when one is impossible in context.
- Leaving out units, or giving cm where cm² is needed.
