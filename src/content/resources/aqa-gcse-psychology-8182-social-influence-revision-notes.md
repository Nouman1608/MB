Condensed recall for **3.2.1 Social influence** (AQA GCSE Psychology 8182, Paper 2). For full
explanations and evaluation, read the
[Social Influence study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-psychology-8182-social-influence/) first.

## The one question to ask

Is the factor about the **situation** (social) or the **person** (dispositional)? Every behaviour
in this topic has both.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conformity | Changing behaviour or belief to fit in with a majority |
| Obedience | Following a direct order from an authority figure |
| Confederate | An actor who follows the researcher's script |
| Autonomous state | Acting on your own conscience; feeling responsible |
| Agentic state | Acting as an agent of authority; responsibility passed upward |
| Authoritarian Personality | Rigid, status-conscious personality from harsh upbringing (Adorno); measured by the F-scale |
| Diffusion of responsibility | Each bystander feels less responsible when others are present |
| Cost-reward | Bystanders weigh the cost of helping against the cost of not helping |
| Social loafing | Putting in less individual effort when working in a group |
| Deindividuation | Loss of personal identity and self-restraint in an anonymous crowd |

## Studies at a glance

**Asch (1951) -- conformity.** Male US students judged line lengths after confederates gave the
same wrong answer on critical trials. About a third of critical-trial answers conformed; about
three-quarters conformed at least once; about a quarter never did. Variations: conformity rose with
group size up to about three confederates, fell when answers were private, rose when the lines were
more similar.
*Evaluation:* controlled and standardised; but artificial task, narrow 1950s sample, deception.

**Milgram (1963) -- obedience.** 40 men, "teacher" role, fake shocks in 15 V steps to 450 V, verbal
prods from an experimenter in a lab coat. All went to 300 V; 65% went to 450 V. Obedience fell in a
run-down office (authority), with teacher and learner in the same room or touching (proximity),
and with orders given by telephone (proximity of authority).
*Evaluation:* controlled variations show which factors matter; but deception and distress raise
ethical issues, and the lab setting may lack ecological validity.

**Adorno (1950) -- dispositional obedience.** Harsh, strict parenting leads to an authoritarian
personality: black-and-white thinking, deference to higher status, hostility to lower status.
F-scale questionnaire.
*Evaluation:* obedient Milgram-type participants tended to score higher on the F-scale; but it
cannot explain obedience across a whole society.

**Piliavin (1969) -- bystanders.** Field experiment on New York subway trains: a confederate
collapsed, appearing ill (cane) or drunk. Cane victim helped on the great majority of trials, drunk
victim on about half; most first helpers were men; no diffusion of responsibility found.
*Evaluation:* high ecological validity; but no consent or debrief, and little control of
passengers.

## Factor table

| Behaviour | Social | Dispositional |
|---|---|---|
| Conformity | Group size, anonymity, task difficulty | Personality, expertise |
| Obedience | Agency, authority, culture, proximity | Authoritarian Personality |
| Bystander | Presence of others, cost of helping | Similarity to victim, expertise |
| Crowds | Social loafing, deindividuation, culture | Personality, morality |

## Crowd behaviour in one line each

- **Social loafing:** less effort per person in groups (Latané, Williams and Harkins: clapping and
  shouting).
- **Deindividuation:** anonymity reduces restraint (Diener's Halloween study: anonymous children in
  groups took more sweets).
- **Culture:** loafing appears less common in collectivist cultures.
- **Personality and morality:** strong personal standards help people resist the crowd or act
  prosocially.

## Exam traps

- Asch is conformity; Milgram is obedience. Never swap them.
- Adorno is dispositional, not social.
- Piliavin did **not** find diffusion of responsibility.
- In application questions, link every factor to a detail in the scenario.

## Self-test

1. Name the three social factors affecting conformity.
2. What is the agentic state?
3. Give one way Milgram showed proximity affects obedience.
4. Which questionnaire did Adorno use?
5. Why did the drunk victim in Piliavin's study receive less help?
6. Define deindividuation.
7. Give one ethical criticism of Piliavin's study.

**Answers:** 1. Group size, anonymity, task difficulty. 2. A state in which a person sees
themselves as acting for an authority figure and no longer feels responsible for their actions.
3. Any one: obedience fell when the teacher was in the same room as the learner, when the teacher
had to touch the learner, or when orders came by telephone. 4. The F-scale. 5. Helping seemed more
costly (risk, disgust, embarrassment), so by the cost-reward idea people helped less. 6. The loss
of personal identity and self-restraint that can happen in an anonymous crowd. 7. Passengers did
not give consent and were not debriefed.
