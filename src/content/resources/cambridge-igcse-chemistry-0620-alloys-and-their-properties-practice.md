## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement about brass is correct?

A: It is a compound of copper and zinc.

B: It is a mixture of copper and zinc.

C: It is a mixture of iron and zinc.

D: It is a pure metal. **[1]**

**2.** A student says: "An alloy is a mixture of two or more metals." Explain why this definition is not complete, using stainless steel as an example. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** properties that make stainless steel suitable for cutlery. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why an alloy has no fixed chemical formula. **[2]**

**5.** In the grids below, **O** and **o** represent atoms of different sizes, and **X** and **Y** represent particles of two different elements.

```
Grid 1          Grid 2          Grid 3
O O O O O       X Y X Y X       O O O O O
O O O O O       Y X Y X Y       O o O O O
O O O O O       X Y X Y X       O O O o O
```

Identify which grid represents an alloy. Give **one** reason for your choice. **[2]**

**6.** State why alloys are often more useful than the pure metals they are made from. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Pure iron is relatively soft and rusts easily.

**(a)** Name the alloy of iron that is used to make cutlery. **[1]**

**(b)** Name **three** other elements present in this alloy. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe **two** advantages of this alloy over pure iron for making a kitchen sink. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A metallurgist tests a sample of pure copper and a sample of brass. The brass is harder to bend.

**(a)** Describe the arrangement of atoms in pure copper. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the brass is harder to bend. **[3]**

**9.** A brass door handle has a mass of 320 g and contains 62.5 % copper by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of copper in the handle. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of zinc in the handle. **[1]**

**(c)** Another brass handle contains 70 % copper. Explain how this shows that brass is a mixture and not a compound. **[2]**

**10.** The table shows data for a pure metal, M, and two alloys of M, labelled F and G.

| Material | Relative hardness | Effect of damp air after six months |
|---|---|---|
| pure M | 30 | heavily corroded |
| alloy F | 75 | heavily corroded |
| alloy G | 70 | unchanged |

**(a)** State how the data show that alloys can be harder than the pure metal. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest which material should be used for surgical instruments that are cleaned in hot water many times. Give **two** reasons. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A student writes this explanation: "Alloys are harder because the atoms in an alloy are joined by stronger bonds, so they cannot move at all."

Identify **two** weaknesses in this explanation and write an improved version. **[4]**

**12.** Describe how you could tell, from diagrams of particles, the difference between a pure metal, an alloy and a mixture of two separate elements. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Stainless steel contains carbon, which is a non-metal [1], so an alloy is a mixture of a metal with **other elements**, not only other metals [1].

**3.** It is hard [1] and resists rusting [1].

**4.** The elements are mixed, not chemically combined [1], so their proportions can vary from one sample to another / there is no fixed ratio [1].

**5.** Grid 3 [1]: it has atoms of different sizes mixed within the rows of the main metal [1]. (Grid 1 is a pure metal; Grid 2 shows a strict alternating pattern, which represents a compound.)

**6.** They are harder and stronger (or resist corrosion) [1].

**7. (a)** Stainless steel [1].

**(b)** Chromium [1], nickel [1], carbon [1].

**(c)** It resists rusting, so the sink does not corrode in contact with water [1]; it is harder / stronger, so it does not dent or scratch easily [1].

**8. (a)** The atoms are all the same size [1] and are arranged in regular layers [1].

**(b)** Brass contains zinc atoms, which are a different size from copper atoms [1]. These disrupt the regular layers [1], so the layers can no longer slide over each other easily [1].

**9. (a)** 0.625 × 320 = **200 g** [1].

**(b)** 320 − 200 = **120 g** [1].

**(c)** Both are brass but the proportion of copper is different [1]; a compound always has a fixed ratio of elements, so brass must be a mixture [1].

**10. (a)** Both alloys have a higher relative hardness (75 and 70) than pure M (30) [1].

**(b)** Alloy G [1]: it is much harder than pure M [1] and it does not corrode in damp air / resists corrosion, which matters when it is washed in hot water many times [1].

**11.** Weaknesses, any two for [2]:

- the explanation is not about stronger bonds [1];
- it is the **layers** that cannot slide, not individual atoms that "cannot move" [1];
- it does not mention that the atoms are of **different sizes** [1].

Improved version [2]: "An alloy contains atoms of different sizes [1], which disrupt the regular layers of the metal, so the layers can no longer slide over each other [1]."

**12.** Pure metal: all particles the same size in regular rows [1]. Alloy: particles of a different size scattered within the main metal's structure, with no fixed pattern [1]. Mixture of separate elements: the two kinds of particle are in separate groups or regions, not mixed within one structure [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling brass or stainless steel a compound.
- Defining an alloy as a mixture of metals only.
- Giving one property for stainless steel cutlery instead of two.
- Choosing the alternating-pattern diagram as the alloy.
- In the Extended explanation, writing "atoms cannot move" or "stronger bonds" instead of "layers can no longer slide".
- Leaving out "different sized" in the Extended explanation.
- In data questions, choosing the hardest material without checking whether it corrodes.
