All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Kesia's Kitchen.

> **Case study: Kesia's Kitchen**
>
> Kesia worked as a cook in a hotel for six years. She was unhappy with
> the long shifts and the low pay, and she noticed that no business in
> her town sold ready-made meals for people who work late. She left her
> job and started Kesia's Kitchen, which cooks meals and sells them from
> a small shop. She spent her savings on a second-hand oven and a
> refrigerated display counter. Her ingredients come from local farms.
> Customers can also order meals through a website, which Kesia built
> herself. This year she must decide how to use her limited cooking time:
> in one hour she can cook either 12 family pies or 30 single-portion
> pasta meals. She plans to spend 6 hours a week cooking pies.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a service? **[1]**
- A. A loaf of bread
- B. A hair cut
- C. A tin of paint
- D. A pair of shoes

**2.** Which **one** of the following is part of the primary sector? **[1]**
- A. A farm growing wheat
- B. A bank
- C. A bakery factory
- D. A bus company

**3.** Define the term "opportunity cost". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** possible reasons why someone might start a business. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which technology can change a business. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Identify **one** example of capital and **one** example of land in the case study. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the number of pasta meals Kesia gives up each week by cooking pies for 6 hours. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** characteristics Kesia has shown as an entrepreneur. **[4]**

**9.** Kesia's ingredients come from local farms. Explain how this shows the primary and secondary sectors depend on each other. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** reasons why Kesia chose to start her own business. **[6]**

**11.** Explain what is meant by "the opportunity cost of the oven" for Kesia. **[3]**

**12.** Kesia is considering stopping her pie production to cook pasta meals only. Evaluate whether this is a good decision, using your answer to Question 7. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The value of the next best alternative [1] that is given up when a choice is made [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: to be their own boss [1]; to earn more money [1]; to fill a gap in the market [1]; flexible working hours [1]; to pursue an interest [1]; dissatisfaction with their current job [1]. **[2]**

**5.** For example, online selling lets a business reach customers outside its local area [1], which can raise sales without opening new shops [1]. Accept any valid effect, such as machines making production faster, with a reason. **[2]**

**6.** Capital: the oven or the refrigerated display counter [1]. Land: the shop site, or the farmland that supplies ingredients, accepted if it is a natural resource or site [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Meals given up per hour = 30; in 6 hours Kesia gives up 6 x 30 = **180** pasta meals [2]. Award 1 mark for 6 x 30 shown with a wrong final answer. **[2]**

**8.** **Innovative:** Kesia spotted that nobody sold ready-made meals for people who work late [1], which gave her a product that was not already supplied in the town [1].
**Willing to take a risk:** she left her job and spent her savings on equipment [1], so she could lose her money if the business fails [1].
Accept organised or hard-working with a developed reason, for example building the website herself. **[4]**

**9.** The farms in the primary sector grow the ingredients [1], and Kesia's Kitchen turns them into meals, which is secondary-sector production [1]. If the farms had a poor harvest or raised prices, Kesia would have fewer or costlier ingredients [1], so her production and sales would fall; equally, the farms depend on businesses like hers to buy their produce [1]. **[4]**

**10.** **Dissatisfaction with her job:** Kesia disliked the long shifts and low pay [1], so being her own boss gave her a chance to set her own hours and keep more of the income [1].
**Gap in the market:** nobody in town sold ready-made meals for late workers [1], so Kesia could expect customers with little direct competition [1].
Award up to 2 further marks for development of either reason linked to her decision, for example the chance to earn more than she did as a cook [1] or to avoid hotel shifts [1]. Maximum **[6]**.

**11.** The oven's opportunity cost is the next best use of the money [1] that Kesia spent on it, such as a delivery bike or advertising [1], which she gave up when she chose to buy the oven [1]. **[3]**

**12.** **For pasta only:** pasta meals are quicker, 30 an hour compared with 12 pies [1], so Kesia could make more items to sell in the same time [1], and single portions may suit the late workers she serves [1].
**Against:** by cooking pasta only, Kesia would give up the pie customers and their sales [1], and her business would depend on one product, which is riskier if the demand for pasta falls [1]. Making pies for 6 hours already costs 180 pasta meals, so she should compare the revenue from each, not just the number of items [1]. A family pie probably sells for more than a single meal [1].
**Judgement:** the decision depends on the selling prices and the profit on each item [1]. Kesia should compare the revenue from 72 pies (6 x 12) with the revenue from 180 pasta meals before deciding, and she could keep both products to give customers a choice [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the cost of an item (money spent) rather than the alternative given up as the opportunity cost.
- Listing a characteristic of an entrepreneur without saying how it appears in the case study.
- Placing a bakery or a restaurant in the tertiary sector. Cooking meals is secondary; selling them in a shop is tertiary.
- Answering evaluation questions from only one side without a judgement.
- Not using figures from earlier answers when the question tells you to.
