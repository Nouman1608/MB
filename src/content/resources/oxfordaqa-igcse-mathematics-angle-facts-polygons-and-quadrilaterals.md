## G1: Terms and notation

A **point** has position only. A **line** is straight and has no thickness; a line segment is the part between two points. An **edge** is a side of a polygon, or where two faces of a solid meet. **Parallel lines** never meet and are marked with matching arrowheads. **Perpendicular lines** meet at a **right angle**, marked with a small square. A **plane** is a flat surface. A **polygon** is a closed shape with straight edges. Its corners are **vertices**. A **regular polygon** has all sides equal and all angles equal.

Notation to use:

| Written | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AB | the line from A to B, or its length |
| AB ∥ CD | AB is parallel to CD |
| AB ⊥ CD | AB is perpendicular to CD |
| ∠ABC or angle ABC | the angle at B, between BA and BC |
| triangle ABC | the triangle with vertices A, B and C |

The middle letter of an angle name is the vertex. In triangle ABC, the side opposite A is labelled a, the side opposite B is b and the side opposite C is c. Equal sides carry matching tick marks.

## G2: Angles at a point, on a line and vertically opposite

- Angles at a point add up to 360°.
- Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
- Where two straight lines cross, the angles opposite each other are equal. They are **vertically opposite**.
- Perpendicular lines make four right angles, each 90°.

**Example 1.** Four angles meet at a point: 3a, (a + 35), (2a − 15) and 100°. Find a and the largest angle.

```
3a + (a + 35) + (2a − 15) + 100 = 360    (angles at a point)
6a + 120 = 360
6a = 240, so a = 40
Angles: 120°, 75°, 65°, 100°
```

The largest angle is 120°. Check: 120 + 75 + 65 + 100 = 360.

**Example 2.** Two straight lines cross. One angle is (5y − 20)° and the angle vertically opposite is (2y + 55)°. Find the other two angles.

```
5y − 20 = 2y + 55    (vertically opposite)
3y = 75, so y = 25
5y − 20 = 105°
Other angles: 180 − 105 = 75°    (angles on a straight line)
```

The four angles are 105°, 75°, 105° and 75°.

## G3: Parallel lines, triangles and quadrilaterals

### Parallel lines

A line that crosses a pair of parallel lines is called a transversal. It creates three pairs of related angles. The specification expects you to use the names alternate, corresponding and interior, and not colloquial names such as "Z angles".

| Type | Relationship | Look for |
|---|---|---|
| Corresponding | equal | the same position at each crossing, like an F shape |
| Alternate | equal | opposite sides of the transversal, between the parallel lines |
| Interior | add up to 180° | the same side of the transversal, between the parallel lines |

Interior angles are also called co-interior angles. Always name the property you use.

**Example 3.** Two corresponding angles on parallel lines are (7a − 12)° and (4a + 30)°. Find a and the angle.

```
7a − 12 = 4a + 30    (corresponding angles are equal)
3a = 42, so a = 14
Angle = 7 × 14 − 12 = 86°
```

**Example 4.** Two interior angles on parallel lines are (3b + 20)° and (5b − 40)°. Find both.

```
(3b + 20) + (5b − 40) = 180    (interior angles)
8b − 20 = 180
8b = 200, so b = 25
Angles: 3 × 25 + 20 = 95° and 5 × 25 − 40 = 85°
```

For forming and solving equations like these, see the [algebra practice](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-mathematics-algebra-practice/).

### Triangles

- Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
- An exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles. This follows because the exterior angle and the interior angle beside it lie on a straight line.

| Triangle | Sides | Angles |
|---|---|---|
| Equilateral | all 3 equal | all 60° |
| Isosceles | 2 equal | the 2 angles opposite the equal sides are equal |
| Scalene | none equal | none equal |
| Right-angled | | one angle is 90° |
| Acute-angled | | all angles below 90° |
| Obtuse-angled | | one angle above 90° |

A triangle can be two types at once, such as isosceles and right-angled.

**Example 5.** Triangle ABC has AB = AC and angle BAC = 48°. Side BC is extended to D. Find angle ACD.

```
Angle ABC = angle ACB = (180 − 48) ÷ 2 = 66°    (isosceles triangle)
Angle ACD = 180 − 66 = 114°                      (angles on a straight line)
```

Check with the exterior angle fact: 48° + 66° = 114°.

### Quadrilaterals

Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°. Drawing one diagonal makes two triangles, each with an angle sum of 180°, which gives 2 × 180° = 360° in total.

**Example 6.** Angles in a quadrilateral are 2x°, (x + 40)°, (3x − 25)° and 105°. Find x and the smallest angle.

```
2x + (x + 40) + (3x − 25) + 105 = 360
6x + 120 = 360
6x = 240, so x = 40
Angles: 80°, 80°, 95°, 105°
```

The smallest angle is 80°. Check: 80 + 80 + 95 + 105 = 360.

## G4: Interior and exterior angles of polygons

A polygon with n sides can be split into (n − 2) triangles from one vertex.

| Result | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sum of interior angles | (n − 2) × 180° |
| Sum of exterior angles | 360° |
| Exterior angle of a regular polygon | 360° ÷ n |
| Interior angle of a regular polygon | 180° − exterior angle |
| Angle at the centre of a regular polygon | 360° ÷ n |

An interior angle and the exterior angle at the same vertex lie on a straight line, so they add up to 180°. The angle at the centre is the angle between two lines from the centre to neighbouring vertices.

**Example 7.** Find the sum of the interior angles of an 11-sided polygon.

```
(11 − 2) × 180 = 9 × 180 = 1620°
```

**Example 8.** Six angles of a heptagon are 118°, 134°, 126°, 141°, 152° and 129°. Find the seventh.

```
Sum for 7 sides = 5 × 180 = 900°
Six angles total 800°
Seventh angle = 900 − 800 = 100°
```

**Example 9.** A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 18°. Find the number of sides and the interior angle.

```
n = 360 ÷ 18 = 20 sides
Interior angle = 180 − 18 = 162°
```

**Example 10.** The interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°. How many sides has it?

```
Exterior angle = 180 − 150 = 30°
n = 360 ÷ 30 = 12 sides
```

Working through the exterior angle is much quicker than using (n − 2) × 180 ÷ n = 150 as an equation.

**Example 11.** Find the angle at the centre of a regular 9-sided polygon, and each interior angle.

```
Angle at centre = 360 ÷ 9 = 40°
Exterior angle = 40°
Interior angle = 180 − 40 = 140°
```

## G5: Special quadrilaterals

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals |
|---|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | all 90° | equal; bisect each other at 90°; bisect the angles |
| Rectangle | opposite sides equal and parallel | all 90° | equal; bisect each other |
| Parallelogram | opposite sides equal and parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other |
| Rhombus | 4 equal; opposite sides parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other at 90°; bisect the angles |
| Kite | 2 pairs of adjacent equal sides | one pair of opposite angles equal | cross at 90°; one is bisected |
| Trapezium | one pair of parallel sides | the two angles at the ends of each non-parallel side add up to 180° | no special property |

An isosceles trapezium is a trapezium with its non-parallel sides equal. Its base angles are equal in pairs.

**Example 12.** ABCD is a rhombus. Angle DAB = 68°. The diagonals meet at M. Find angles ABC and ABD.

```
Angle ABC = 180 − 68 = 112°     (interior angles, AD ∥ BC)
Diagonal BD bisects angle ABC
Angle ABD = 112 ÷ 2 = 56°
```

Check using triangle ABD: AB = AD, so angle ABD = (180 − 68) ÷ 2 = 56°.

**Example 13.** Parallelogram PQRS has angle P = (3z + 20)° and angle Q = (2z + 10)°. Find z and angle R.

```
Angle P + angle Q = 180    (adjacent angles between parallel sides)
5z + 30 = 180, so z = 30
Angle P = 110°, angle Q = 70°
Angle R = angle P = 110°    (opposite angles equal)
```

## Common errors

- Giving a general reason such as "angles in a triangle" when the question needs the exact fact, such as "base angles of an isosceles triangle".
- Treating alternate angles as adding up to 180°. They are equal. Interior angles add up to 180°.
- Using (n − 2) × 180 for an exterior angle. Exterior angles of any polygon total 360°.
- Dividing 360 by the interior angle to find n. Divide by the exterior angle.
- Calling a shape a rhombus because the sides are equal when a diagram only shows two equal sides.
- Assuming angles are as drawn. Diagrams are not always to scale, so use only the given facts.
