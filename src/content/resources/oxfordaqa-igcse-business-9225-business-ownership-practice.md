All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Harbour Print Studio.

> **Case study: Harbour Print Studio**
>
> Hana and Leo run Harbour Print Studio as a partnership. Hana invested
> $36,000 and Leo invested $24,000. They share profits in the same ratio
> as their investment. Last year the profit was $50,000. They want to buy
> a large digital printing machine and expand, but the bank will lend them
> only part of the money needed. They are considering turning the business
> into a private limited company and selling 40,000 shares at $2.50 each
> to friends and relatives. Hana is worried because the partnership has
> debts of $70,000 while its assets are worth only $45,000, and she has
> heard that partners can be personally responsible for debts.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of a sole trader? **[1]**
- A. Owned by many shareholders
- B. Owned and controlled by one person
- C. Owned by the government
- D. Shares are sold on a stock exchange

**2.** Which **one** of the following is part of the public sector? **[1]**
- A. A family-owned clothing shop
- B. A private limited company
- C. A state-owned hospital
- D. A partnership of lawyers

**3.** Define the term "limited liability". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** features of a not-for-profit organisation. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** drawback of being a sole trader. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate how much of last year's profit Hana received. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate how much money the company would raise by selling 40,000 shares at $2.50 each. **[1]**

**8.** Calculate the amount by which the partnership's debts exceed its assets. **[1]**

**9.** Explain **two** advantages to Harbour Print Studio of changing from a partnership to a private limited company. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** disadvantage to Hana and Leo of changing to a private limited company. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse how limited liability would affect Hana if the business could not pay its debts. Use your answer to Question 8. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Harbour Print Studio should become a private limited company or remain a partnership. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The owners (shareholders) can lose no more than the amount they invested in the business [1]; their personal belongings cannot be used to pay the business's debts [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: aims to serve a social or community purpose rather than make profit for owners [1]; any surplus is reinvested in its work [1]; often relies on donations, grants or volunteers [1]; examples are charities and community clubs [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A sole trader has unlimited liability [1], so if the business cannot pay its debts the owner's personal property could be used to pay them [1]. Accept finance limited to the owner's savings and loans [1], so growth is harder [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Hana's share = 36,000 / 60,000 = 60% [1]; 60% x $50,000 = **$30,000** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** 40,000 x $2.50 = **$100,000** [1]. **[1]**

**8.** $70,000 - $45,000 = **$25,000** [1]. **[1]**

**9.** **Finance:** the company can sell shares and raise $100,000 [1], which may pay for the new machine without relying on a bank loan [1].
**Limited liability:** the partners would only lose what they invested in shares [1], so their homes and savings would be protected if the business failed [1]. Accept a continuing business if a shareholder leaves [1] and a more serious image with lenders [1]. **[4]**

**10.** One of: more legal rules and costs, such as filing accounts [1], which take time and money [1]. Or: profit has to be shared with the new shareholders [1], so Hana and Leo would receive less of each year's profit [1]. Or: control could be reduced by new shareholders' votes [1], so they would not decide everything alone [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **Limited liability:** as shareholders, Hana and Leo would lose only the money they put into the company [1], and the $25,000 shortfall would fall on the creditors, not on their personal property [1].
**Without it:** as partners with unlimited liability, they could be asked to pay the $25,000 from their own assets [1], and one partner may have to pay more if the other cannot [1].
**Effect on Hana:** she would risk her home and savings [1] in the partnership, but only her investment in the company, so she may feel safer taking on the expansion and its borrowing [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For the company:** limited liability protects Hana, who is already worried about debts of $25,000 beyond the assets [1]; selling shares raises $100,000 [1], which helps pay for the machine and reduces reliance on the bank [1]; investors may be more willing to buy shares when their risk is limited [1].
**For staying a partnership:** the partnership is simpler and cheaper, with fewer rules about accounts [1]; Hana and Leo keep all the profit ($50,000 last year) and keep full control [1]; the machine could still be funded by a bank loan [1].
**Judgement:** a limited company is probably better, because the business is expanding, needs finance and has debts above its assets [1]. However, the partners should check that giving up some profit and control is acceptable, and they could sell shares only to people they trust so that they keep a majority [1]. **[9]** Award full marks for a well-supported opposite judgement, such as staying a partnership because the bank loan may be enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that limited liability means a shareholder pays nothing. They can lose the money they invested.
- Calculating Hana's share as half the profit. The question says profits are shared in the ratio of investment, 36 to 24.
- Writing about "the law" or "rules" without saying what the business must do and what it costs.
- Giving one-sided answers to evaluation questions and not reaching a judgement.
- Confusing public sector with public limited company.
