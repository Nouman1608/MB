## Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the most suitable apparatus for each measurement.

**(a)** the time taken for a solution to change colour **[1]**

**(b)** the mass of a sample of copper(II) carbonate **[1]**

**(c)** exactly 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution to put in a conical flask **[1]**

**2.** Which apparatus is most suitable for measuring the volume of hydrogen given off when magnesium reacts with acid?

A: burette

B: gas syringe

C: volumetric pipette

D: balance

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term *solvent*. **[1]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by a *saturated solution*. **[2]**

**5.** A mixture of chalk powder and water is poured through a filter paper. Name the residue and the filtrate. **[2]**

**6.** A thermometer has graduations every 1 °C. The liquid level is exactly halfway between 24 °C and 25 °C. Write down the reading the student should record. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student adds 30 cm³ of dilute acid to a beaker using a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder.

**(a)** Suggest a more accurate piece of apparatus for measuring this volume. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why using a 50 cm³ measuring cylinder instead of a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder would also improve accuracy. **[1]**

**(c)** State **one** advantage of using a measuring cylinder rather than a burette. **[1]**

**8.** A student investigates how the concentration of hydrochloric acid affects the volume of carbon dioxide produced in 60 s when it reacts with marble chips.

**(a)** Identify the independent variable and the dependent variable. **[2]**

**(b)** State **two** variables that must be controlled. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest how many different concentrations should be used, and why. **[1]**

**9.** A student collects carbon dioxide by bubbling it into an upturned measuring cylinder full of water.

**(a)** Suggest **one** disadvantage of this method compared with using a gas syringe. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** advantage of this method compared with using a gas syringe. **[1]**

**10.** A student records these results in a table.

| Time | Temperature |
|---|---|
| 0 | 21 °C |
| 30 s | 25.5 |
| 60 | 28 °C |

**(a)** Describe **two** ways in which the table should be improved. **[2]**

**(b)** The thermometer has 1 °C divisions. Explain why "21 °C" should be recorded as "21.0 °C". **[1]**

**11.** A student dissolves potassium nitrate in 50 cm³ of water at 20 °C until no more dissolves and some solid remains at the bottom.

**(a)** Name the solute and the solvent. **[2]**

**(b)** State the term for the solution formed. **[1]**

**(c)** The mixture is heated to 50 °C and the remaining solid dissolves. Explain why the definition of a saturated solution must refer to a temperature. **[1]**

**12.** Salt solution is heated in an evaporating basin until all the water has gone. A white solid remains.

**(a)** State the term for the white solid that remains. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the apparatus used to measure its mass. **[1]**

**13.** A student measures the temperature change when a powdered metal is added to copper(II) sulfate solution in a glass beaker.

**(a)** Suggest **one** improvement to the apparatus and explain how it improves the results. **[2]**

**(b)** State **one** safety precaution. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** Stop-watch [1].

**(b)** Balance [1].

**(c)** Volumetric pipette [1]. *A measuring cylinder is not accurate enough for an exact volume.*

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** A substance that dissolves a solute [1].

**4.** A solution containing the maximum concentration of a solute dissolved in the solvent [1] at a specified temperature [1].

**5.** Residue: chalk [1]. Filtrate: water [1].

**6.** 24.5 °C [1].

**7.** **(a)** Burette [1]. *A burette measures any volume up to 50 cm³ accurately; a standard volumetric pipette delivers only one fixed volume, such as 25.0 cm³.*

**(b)** A smaller cylinder has finer graduations / smaller scale divisions, so the volume can be read more precisely [1].

**(c)** It is quicker / easier to use / can measure a large volume in one step [1].

**8.** **(a)** Independent: concentration of the hydrochloric acid [1]. Dependent: volume of carbon dioxide (produced in 60 s) [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: volume of acid; mass of marble chips; size of marble chips (surface area); temperature.

**(c)** At least five, spread over the range, so that a pattern / trend can be seen reliably (and a graph plotted) [1].

**9.** **(a)** Carbon dioxide is slightly soluble in water, so some dissolves and the measured volume is too low [1]. *Accept: the reading is harder to take because the cylinder must be held upside-down.*

**(b)** Any one [1]: the apparatus is cheaper / easily available; there is no risk of the syringe plunger sticking; a larger volume can be collected by choosing a larger cylinder.

**10.** **(a)** Any two [2]:

- put the units in the column headings, e.g. "Time / s" and "Temperature / °C", not in the cells;
- record all times with units shown consistently (the 0 and 60 have no unit);
- record all temperatures to the same precision, e.g. 21.0, 25.5, 28.0.

**(b)** Readings should all be recorded to the same precision; the thermometer can be read to 0.5 °C, so one decimal place shows the reading was made to that precision [1].

**11.** **(a)** Solute: potassium nitrate [1]. Solvent: water [1].

**(b)** Saturated solution [1].

**(c)** The maximum mass of solute that dissolves changes with temperature, so a solution saturated at one temperature is not saturated at another [1].

**12.** **(a)** Residue [1].

**(b)** Balance [1].

**13.** **(a)** Use a polystyrene cup (an insulated container) instead of a glass beaker [1]; this reduces heat loss to the surroundings, so the measured temperature change is closer to the true value [1]. *Accept: add a lid to reduce heat loss; stir to ensure an even temperature (with the matching explanation).*

**(b)** Wear eye protection (goggles) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "pipette" when "volumetric pipette" is needed, or choosing a measuring cylinder for an exact volume.
- Leaving "at a specified temperature" out of the definition of a saturated solution.
- Mixing up residue and filtrate.
- Recording thermometer and burette readings with inconsistent decimal places.
- Writing improvements without saying how they improve the results.
- Giving "human error" as a source of error without saying what the error is.
