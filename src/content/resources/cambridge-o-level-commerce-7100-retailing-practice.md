## Section A

**1.** Identify the type of retailer described in each case: (a) a seller who cycles between villages selling cooking pots; (b) a store with separate sections for clothes, cosmetics, furniture and toys under one roof; (c) a shop selling only fishing equipment; (d) a very large out-of-town store with food, electrical goods and a car park for 2,000 cars. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** advantages of a street market to customers. **[2]**

**3.** Define *own brand*. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** benefits of barcoding to a retailer. **[2]**

**5.** What is meant by *subscription commerce*? **[2]**

**6.** Define *dropshipping*. **[2]**

**7.** State **two** negative impacts of ecommerce on traditional commerce named in the syllabus. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain **three** reasons why a small independent grocery shop survives near a large supermarket. **[6]**

**9.** Compare a multiple chain store with an independent retailer. **[4]**

**10.** Explain the difference between multi-channel retailing and omnichannel retailing, using an example. **[4]**

**11.** Maya runs a shop selling washing machines and fridges. Explain **two** retail selling techniques that would increase her sales. **[4]**

**12.** Kofi owns a bookshop in a town centre. He is considering selling online as well. Discuss whether ecommerce will benefit his business. **[8]**

**13.** A supermarket plans to introduce **two** digital retail trends: self-service checkouts and click and collect. Assess whether these will benefit the supermarket. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Itinerant retailer [1]; (b) department store [1]; (c) speciality shop [1]; (d) hypermarket [1].

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: lower prices because stallholders pay low rents; customers can bargain over prices; a wide variety of stalls in one place; fresh local produce; a lively atmosphere [1] [1].

**3.** A product sold under the retailer's own name, made for it by a manufacturer [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: faster checkout; fewer pricing errors; stock records updated automatically; sales data showing which products sell best [1] [1].

**5.** Customers pay a regular fee [1] to receive repeat deliveries of a product, such as monthly pet food [1].

**6.** A retailer sells goods that it does not keep in stock [1]; the supplier sends the goods directly to the customer [1].

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: increased delivery costs; staff training costs; larger warehousing space needed; website maintenance costs [1] [1].

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each (reason, then development applied to the grocery shop): **personal service**, because the owner knows regular customers and their preferences, which builds loyalty; **flexible opening hours**, so it can open early or late when the supermarket is closed; **additional services**, such as delivering to elderly customers or selling phone credit; **convenient location** near homes, so customers can walk there for a few items; **informal credit**, letting trusted customers pay at the end of the week [1] [1] [1] [1] [1] [1].

**9.** **Similarity:** both sell to the final consumer in small quantities [1]. **Differences:** a multiple chain store has many branches under one name, while an independent retailer usually has one shop [1]; the chain buys in bulk centrally so it can charge lower prices, while the independent pays more per item [1]; the independent can offer more personal service and adapt to local tastes, while the chain follows central decisions [1].

**10.** **Multi-channel** retailing means selling through several channels, such as a shop, a website and an app, which may operate separately [1]. **Omnichannel** retailing joins the channels into one experience, with shared accounts, stock information and rewards [1]. Example: in an omnichannel shoe retailer, a customer can check stock in the app, reserve a size and collect it in the shop, earning the same loyalty points [1], whereas in a multi-channel retailer the online and shop prices, stock and points might be separate [1].

**11.** Any two, 2 marks each: **after-sales service**, such as free installation and a repair warranty, gives customers confidence to buy expensive appliances; **informal credit**, letting trusted customers pay in parts, helps customers who cannot pay the full price at once; **direct mail**, sending offers to customers whose old machines are due for replacement, targets likely buyers; **loyalty programmes** encourage customers to return for other appliances [1] [1] [1] [1].

**12.** **Benefits:** online selling allows **24/7 sales**, even when the shop is closed [1]; Kofi can reach **more potential customers** beyond the town, increasing revenue [1]; **targeted marketing**, such as emailing readers about new books by authors they have bought before, can raise repeat sales [1]. **Drawbacks:** **delivery costs** for heavy books reduce profit on each order [1]; **website maintenance** and security add ongoing costs [1]; he may need **staff training** to process online orders [1]; large online retailers may undercut his prices [1]. **Judgement:** ecommerce is likely to benefit him if he combines it with the shop's strengths, such as signed copies or local-interest books that large online sellers do not offer [1].

**13.** **Self-service checkouts:** fewer cashiers are needed, cutting wage costs [1]; queues move faster at busy times [1]; however, they cost money to install and maintain, and theft or scanning errors may increase [1]. **Click and collect:** customers can order online and collect at a convenient time, attracting busy shoppers [1]; collecting customers may buy extra items in store [1]; however, staff time is needed to pick orders and storage space for orders awaiting collection [1]. **Judgement:** both trends are likely to benefit the supermarket [1], especially if it has many busy shoppers, though it should keep some staffed tills for customers who prefer them [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: calling the very large out-of-town store a supermarket.
- Question 8: listing "personal service" three times in different words.
- Question 9: describing each type separately without comparing them.
- Question 10: giving two definitions with no example.
- Question 12: giving only advantages, or a judgement that ignores the bookshop.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does compare mean in Question 9?** The syllabus defines compare as identifying or commenting on similarities and/or differences, so each point should show both retailers side by side.

**Can I use a technique not in the syllabus list in Question 11?** Use the named techniques: the list in 2.3.2 is what the examiners expect.
