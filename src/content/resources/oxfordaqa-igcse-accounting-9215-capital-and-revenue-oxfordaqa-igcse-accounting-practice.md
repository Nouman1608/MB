## Section A

**1.** Define capital expenditure. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each of the following as capital expenditure or revenue expenditure: **(a)** legal fees paid on buying a warehouse, **(b)** fuel for a delivery van, **(c)** fitting a security system to a shop for the first time, **(d)** servicing a photocopier. **[4]**

**3.** Classify each of the following receipts as capital income or revenue income: **(a)** commission received, **(b)** sale of an old delivery van, **(c)** the owner paying her savings into the business bank account. **[3]**

**4.** A business receives a bank loan of 20,000. Explain why this is not revenue income. **[2]**

**5.** Which of the following is revenue expenditure?

A: Installation of a new air-conditioning unit

B: Wages of the firm's own builders constructing a new storeroom

C: Replacing a cracked window pane with an identical pane

D: Architect's fees for designing an extension

**[1]**

**6.** State the accounting concept that explains why delivery charges on a new machine are added to the cost of the machine. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Tamsin Okafor runs a bakery. During the year she paid:

- 9,500 for a new mixer
- 300 for delivery of the mixer
- 650 for an annual maintenance contract on the mixer
- 1,400 to replace the bakery's worn floor tiles with identical tiles.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of capital expenditure. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount of revenue expenditure. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the maintenance contract is revenue expenditure even though it relates to a new asset. **[2]**

**8.** Prepare journal entries, without narratives, to correct each error.

**(a)** Installation costs of 720 for new equipment were debited to general expenses. **[2]**
**(b)** A repair to a van of 460 was debited to the van account. **[2]**
**(c)** Capital introduced of 3,000 was credited to sales. **[2]**

**9.** Halden Garage's draft profit for the year was 27,800. The following errors were then found:

1. A hydraulic lift costing 6,000 had been debited to repairs.
2. Routine servicing of the garage's own breakdown truck, 540, had been debited to the truck account.

Equipment and vehicles are depreciated at 15% of cost a year, with a full year charged in the year of purchase. The truck was bought several years ago, and the draft depreciation on it was based on the incorrect account balance.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. Show your workings. **[5]**
**(b)** State the overall effect of the corrections on the net book value of non-current assets. **[2]**

**10.** Explain the effect on the financial statements if the proceeds of 2,500 from selling an old machine are credited to the sales account. **[4]**

**11.** Lindiwe Dube's draft statements show opening capital of 42,000, profit for the year of 33,000 and drawings of 15,000. It is later found that 6,000 she paid into the business from her savings was credited to sales.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate closing capital before and after the correction. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the error matters even though one of your answers in **(b)** does not change. **[2]**

**12.** A shop owner says: "Anything we spend money on that lasts more than a year should be capitalised." Assess this view. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Spending on buying, creating or improving a non-current asset [1], whose benefit lasts for more than one accounting period, so it is recorded in the statement of financial position [1].

**2. (a)** Capital [1]. **(b)** Revenue [1]. **(c)** Capital, as it adds a new asset to the premises [1]. **(d)** Revenue [1].

**3. (a)** Revenue income [1]. **(b)** Capital income [1]. **(c)** Capital income [1].

**4.** A loan must be repaid, so it is a liability [1]. It is credited to a loan account and shown in the statement of financial position, not earned through trading [1].

**5.** C [1]. The new pane restores the window to its previous condition.

**6.** Historic cost [1]. (Accept accruals or matching if linked to spreading the full cost over the machine's life.)

**7. (a)** 9,500 + 300 [1] = **9,800** [1]

**(b)** 650 + 1,400 [1] = **2,050** [1]

**(c)** The contract keeps the mixer in working order rather than improving it [1], and its benefit is used up within the year, so it is a running cost charged in the income statement [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Debit equipment 720 [1]; credit general expenses 720 [1].

**(b)** Debit van repairs (or motor expenses) 460 [1]; credit van 460 [1].

**(c)** Debit sales 3,000 [1]; credit capital 3,000 [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)**

| Step | Effect | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| Draft profit | | 27,800 |
| Lift removed from repairs | +6,000 [1] | 33,800 |
| Depreciation on lift, 15% of 6,000 | −900 [1] | 32,900 |
| Servicing charged as an expense | −540 [1] | 32,360 |
| Depreciation over-charged on 540, 15% of 540 | +81 [1] | 32,441 |

Corrected profit = **32,441** [1]

**(b)** Net book value rises by 6,000 − 900 − 540 + 81 [1] = **4,641**, the same as the increase in profit [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Revenue is overstated by 2,500, so gross profit and profit for the year are overstated [1]. The proceeds should be credited to the disposal account [1], where they are compared with the machine's net book value to find a profit or loss on disposal, which is missing from the income statement [1]. The machine's cost and its provision for depreciation are still shown in the statement of financial position, so non-current assets are overstated [1].

**11. (a)** 33,000 − 6,000 = **27,000** [1]

**(b)** Before: 42,000 + 33,000 − 15,000 = **60,000** [1]. After: 42,000 + 6,000 + 27,000 − 15,000 [1] = **60,000** [1].

**(c)** Profit is overstated by 6,000, so the owner or a lender would think the business performs better than it does [1]; ratios such as profit in relation to revenue and gross profit margin are distorted, as revenue is also overstated [1]. **[6]**

**12.** The view is broadly right, because spending that gives benefit over more than one period should be spread through depreciation under the accruals concept [1]. However, materiality means low-cost items such as staplers or waste bins are usually expensed, as capitalising them costs effort and does not change decisions [1]. Also, spending on an existing asset that lasts more than a year can still be revenue if it only restores the asset, such as replacing worn parts [1]. So the test is whether the spending creates or improves a non-current asset and is material, not only how long it lasts [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Classifying by the account the item was posted to rather than by what the spending achieved.
- Leaving out the depreciation adjustment when correcting profit.
- Giving only the profit effect when the question asks about the financial statements.
- Treating owner's capital or a loan as income.
- Writing journal entries with the debit and credit reversed.
