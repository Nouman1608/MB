OxfordAQA International A-Level Biology (9610) is modular, assessed through five papers, each worth
exactly 20% of the full A-level. The two AS papers -- AS Paper 1 (The Diversity of Living Organisms)
and AS Paper 2 (Biological Systems and Disease) -- are each 1 hour 30 minutes and 75 marks. The three
A-level papers -- A-level Paper 1 (Populations and Genes), A-level Paper 2 (Control) and A-level Paper
3 (Synoptic Paper) -- are the same length and mark total. Students complete **ten required practical
activities across the course, assessed through the written papers rather than a separate practical
exam**. AS results can be cashed in as a standalone AS-level award or carried forward towards the full
A-level, and papers can be resat an unlimited number of times with the best result counting. These
notes complement the site's guides to
[The Diversity of Living Organisms](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-biology-diversity-of-living-organisms/)
and [The Causes of Disease](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-biology-causes-of-disease/).

## Unlimited resits change the strategic calculation, but not the value of first-attempt preparation

Because any paper can be resat and the best result counts, a weak paper is recoverable in a way it is
not on a linear qualification. **Exam-preparation priority**: this should not be read as licence to
under-prepare a first sitting -- a strong result now avoids the time cost of retaking a paper later,
alongside ongoing A2 content. Treat the resit policy as insurance, not as a plan.

## Paper 3 is genuinely synoptic and needs cross-unit revision

A-level Paper 3 draws on the whole specification rather than a single unit's content. **Exam-
preparation priority**: reserve dedicated cross-topic revision time before Paper 3 specifically,
connecting ideas across the diversity, disease, populations, genetics and control content rather than
revising unit by unit right up to the exam.

## The ten required practicals are examined as written content, not performed under exam conditions

Because there is no separate practical exam, the practical skills -- apparatus, technique, sources of
error, evaluation -- are tested through questions in the written papers. **Exam-preparation priority**:
for each of the ten required practicals, be able to write from memory what was measured, what was
controlled, the main source of uncertainty, and one specific improvement. This is written revision, not
laboratory revision, even though the underlying skill is practical.

## Ninety minutes for 75 marks is 1.2 minutes per mark

**Exam-preparation priority**: practise complete papers to that budget, since extended-response and
data-analysis questions typically carry the largest mark allocations and sit at points throughout the
paper rather than only at the end.

## Unfamiliar contexts test transfer of principle, not recall of a specific example

Questions routinely present an organism, disease or study not directly covered in class and ask for
principles to be applied to it. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise on unfamiliar material
specifically, and build the habit of naming the underlying principle -- diffusion and surface area,
natural selection, feedback control, gene expression -- before engaging with the unfamiliar detail.

## Worked routine: describing a required practical from memory

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - state the independent and dependent variables:
What was deliberately changed, and what was measured. Correctly
naming which is which is the foundation of everything after it.

Step 2 - name two control variables and explain why each mattered:
The explanation, not just the name, is what earns the mark.

Step 3 - identify one specific source of uncertainty:
Not "human error" -- the specific measurement that was hard to
take precisely, and why.

Step 4 - give an improvement that targets that specific source:
A data logger for a judgement made by eye; a water bath for
temperature drift -- specific to the named uncertainty.

Step 5 - state the conclusion the results support, with its limit:
"These results support X within the range tested" rather than an
unqualified generalisation.
```

Step 2 is where readily available marks sit: naming a control variable alone is worth little; naming
it and explaining why it had to be controlled is a complete answer.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: treat the resit policy as insurance rather than a reason to under-prepare a
  first sitting; reserve cross-unit revision time specifically for Paper 3; write four facts from
  memory (variables, control, uncertainty, improvement) for each of the ten required practicals.
- **During any paper**: budget roughly 1.2 minutes per mark and expect extended-response questions
  throughout the paper, not only at the end.
- **On unfamiliar-context questions**: name the underlying principle before engaging with the specific
  unfamiliar organism or study.
- **On practical questions**: answer in terms of variables, controlled factors, uncertainty and
  targeted improvement rather than the result alone.

## Self-test

1. How many papers make up the qualification, and how are they weighted?
2. How is practical work assessed on this specification?
3. Why does the resit policy not reduce the value of first-attempt preparation?
4. What is the first move when facing an unfamiliar organism or study in a question?

**Answers:** 1. Five papers, each worth 20% of the full A-level -- two AS papers and three A-level
papers. 2. Through the written papers: ten required practical activities are examined via questions
about method, variables, uncertainty and evaluation, rather than through a separate practical exam.
3. Because a strong first-attempt result avoids the time cost of retaking a paper alongside ongoing A2
content -- the resit policy is insurance, not a substitute for preparation. 4. Naming the underlying
biological principle before engaging with the specific unfamiliar detail, since the principle is
almost always one that has been studied even when the example has not.
