Every person, hospital and data set below is fictional unless a named published study is referred to.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Outline the function of the somatic nervous system. **[2]**

**2.** Identify the type of neuron described in each statement.

> A: It has a long axon and carries impulses from the spinal cord to a muscle.
> B: It lies entirely within the central nervous system and connects other neurons.
> C: It carries impulses from a heat receptor in the skin towards the spinal cord.

**[3]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by summation at a synapse. **[2]**

**4.** After a stroke, Mr Okafor understands what people say to him, but his speech is slow and effortful and he struggles to find words. Identify the brain area most likely to be damaged and explain your answer. **[3]**

**5.** A split-brain patient fixes her eyes on a central point. The word "KEY" is flashed briefly to her left visual field. Explain what she would say she saw, and what she could do with her left hand. **[4]**

**6.** Name the gland that releases each hormone: (a) melatonin; (b) cortisol. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** A fictional hospital recorded how many stroke patients regained useful speech within a year.

| Age group | Patients | Regained useful speech (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Under 40 | 25 | 72 |
| 40-59 | 40 | 55 |
| 60 and over | 35 | 38 |

**(a)** Calculate the number of patients aged 40-59 who regained useful speech. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship between age and recovery shown in the table. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your knowledge of plasticity to explain this relationship. **[3]**

**8.** Before an exam, Ellie's heart pounds, her mouth goes dry and her hands shake. After the exam she feels calm again. Explain these changes with reference to the autonomic nervous system and adrenaline. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **one** limitation of the fight or flight response as an explanation of how people respond to threats. **[3]**

**10.** Outline the structure of a motor neuron and explain the role of the myelin sheath. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate research into localisation of function in the brain. **[9]**

**12.** Discuss plasticity and functional recovery of the brain after trauma. **[12]**

## Answers

Questions 11 and 12 are extended responses. The real exam marks these with levels of response, not point by point; the [1] points show the kind of content that earns credit.

**1.** The somatic nervous system controls voluntary movement by carrying signals from the CNS to skeletal muscles [1], and carries sensory information from the skin, muscles and joints to the CNS [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A: motor neuron [1]. B: relay neuron [1]. C: sensory neuron [1]. **[3]**

**3.** A postsynaptic neuron receives many excitatory and inhibitory inputs at once [1]. These are added together, and the neuron fires only if the net effect reaches the threshold [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Broca's area [1], usually in the left frontal lobe [1], which is responsible for speech production; his comprehension is intact, so Wernicke's area appears undamaged [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The left visual field is processed by the right hemisphere [1]. Language is usually in the left hemisphere, and with the corpus callosum cut the information cannot cross, so she would say she saw nothing [1]. The right hemisphere controls the left hand [1], so she could pick out a key by touch from objects hidden behind a screen [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** Pineal gland [1]. **(b)** Adrenal cortex [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 40 x 0.55 = 22 patients [1].
**(b)** As age increases, the percentage regaining useful speech falls [1], from 72% for under-40s to 38% for those aged 60 and over [1].
**(c)** Plasticity, the brain's ability to reorganise, declines with age [1]. Younger brains form new connections, for example through axonal sprouting, more readily [1], and are more able to recruit homologous areas in the other hemisphere to take over language [1]. **[6]**

**8.** The exam is perceived as a threat, so the hypothalamus activates the sympathetic branch of the autonomic nervous system [1]. This stimulates the adrenal medulla to release adrenaline [1], which raises heart rate and inhibits salivation, explaining the pounding heart and dry mouth [1]. After the exam, the parasympathetic branch restores the resting state, so she feels calm [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The response suits physical threats, but most modern stressors, such as exams, do not need fighting or fleeing [1], so the response may be unhelpful and, if repeated, harmful to health [1]. Other responses occur too, such as freezing (Gray, 1988) or tend and befriend in females (Taylor et al., 2000) [1]. **[3]**

**10.** A motor neuron has short dendrites, a cell body in the CNS and a long axon ending in terminals at a muscle or gland [1]. The axon is covered by a myelin sheath with gaps called nodes of Ranvier [1]. Myelin insulates the axon [1], so the impulse jumps between nodes and travels much faster [1]. **[4]**

**11.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Localisation means specific areas have specific functions [1]. Broca (1861) linked a speech production deficit to left frontal damage [1]. Wernicke (1874) linked comprehension problems to left temporal damage [1]. Strength: brain scans show language tasks activating these areas, supporting localisation [1]. Strength: clinical evidence has helped predict the effects of strokes [1]. Limitation: Lashley (1950) found maze learning in rats was not tied to any single area [1]. Limitation: modern scans suggest language depends on networks, and Broca's original patients had damage beyond Broca's area [1]. Plasticity shows functions can move after damage, so localisation is not fixed [1]. Judgement: basic sensory and motor functions are well localised, but complex functions rely on networks [1]. **[9]**

**12.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Plasticity: the brain changes structure and function with experience [1]. Maguire et al. (2000) found a larger posterior hippocampus in taxi drivers [1], with volume linked to years of driving [1]. Functional recovery: after trauma the brain reorganises [1]. Axonal sprouting forms new connections [1]. Homologous areas in the opposite hemisphere can take over functions [1]. Denervation supersensitivity makes remaining neurons more responsive [1]. Strength: understanding recovery supports rehabilitation such as speech therapy [1]. Limitation: Maguire's evidence is correlational [1]. Limitation: much research uses animals, so generalising to humans is uncertain [1]. Recovery varies with age and the extent of damage [1]. Judgement: strong evidence the brain can reorganise, though recovery is often incomplete [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the visual field and hemisphere the wrong way round.
- Naming a brain area without its lobe or function.
- Describing fight or flight without the role of adrenaline.
- Percentages applied to the wrong group size.
- Extended answers that describe studies without evaluating them.
