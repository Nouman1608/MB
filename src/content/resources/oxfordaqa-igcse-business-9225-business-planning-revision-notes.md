Condensed recall for section 3.1.6 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-business-planning/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Why businesses make plans: **setting up a new business, raising finance, setting objectives, organising the functions**.
- The **main sections** of a business plan.
- **Benefits and drawbacks** of planning.
- **Variable costs, fixed costs, total costs**, and **revenue, profit and loss**.
- You do **not** have to write a business plan.

## Reasons for a business plan

1. **Set up a new business** by thinking through the idea, costs and risks.
2. **Raise finance**: lenders want evidence the business can repay.
3. **Set objectives** and targets.
4. **Organise functions**: who does what in production, marketing, staffing and finance.

## Sections of a plan

| Section | What it does |
|---|---|
| Executive summary | Short overview of the whole plan |
| Business description | The idea, structure, owner and location |
| Aims and objectives | Targets to aim for |
| Market research | Customers, competitors and demand |
| Marketing plan | Product, price, promotion, place |
| Operations plan | Premises, equipment, suppliers, production |
| Human resources plan | Staff numbers, skills, recruitment and pay |
| Financial plan | Start-up costs, sales and cash flow forecasts, profit |

## Benefits and drawbacks

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Clear thinking about risks | Takes time and effort |
| Easier to raise finance | Forecasts can be wrong |
| Gives targets to measure progress | Can become out of date |
| Helps avoid costly mistakes | Too rigid a plan may miss opportunities |
| Helps staff and departments coordinate | Does not guarantee success |

Remember: a plan **improves the chance** of success; it does not **guarantee** it.

## Financial terms and formulae

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Money received from sales = price x quantity |
| Fixed costs | Do not change with output in the short term (rent, insurance, salaries) |
| Variable costs | Change with output (materials, packaging, hourly labour) |
| Total costs | Fixed costs + variable costs |
| Profit | Revenue - total costs |
| Loss | Total costs - revenue (when costs are higher) |

Variable costs = variable cost per unit x quantity.

## Worked example

Fixed costs $2,400 a month; variable cost $1.20 a loaf; price $3.

| Loaves sold | Revenue | Variable costs | Total costs | Profit or loss |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1,000 | $3,000 | $1,200 | $3,600 | Loss $600 |
| 2,200 | $6,600 | $2,640 | $5,040 | Profit $1,560 |
| 3,000 | $9,000 | $3,600 | $6,000 | Profit $3,000 |

Fixed costs stay at $2,400 whatever the sales, so low sales can cause a loss.

## Why forecasts matter

- Plans use **forecasts** of sales and costs, which are estimates.
- A cautious plan includes a lower sales scenario.
- Compare actual results with the plan, find the reason for differences and change the plan.
- Lenders trust plans that use evidence such as market research.

## Exam traps

- Revenue is not profit.
- Rent is a **fixed** cost; materials are **variable**.
- Show each step in calculations: revenue, variable costs, total costs, then profit.
- Do not say that a plan "guarantees" success.
- Connect each plan section to its purpose.
- Explain why a lender wants the financial plan: to see whether the loan can be repaid.

## Self-test

1. State two reasons why a new business writes a business plan.
2. Name three sections of a business plan.
3. What does the financial plan usually include?
4. Give one benefit and one drawback of business planning.
5. Distinguish between fixed and variable costs.
6. Classify these costs as fixed or variable: insurance, packaging, flour, rent.
7. Calculate revenue from selling 450 items at $12.
8. A firm has fixed costs of $5,000 and variable costs of $4 a unit. Calculate total costs for 1,500 units.
9. If the firm in Question 8 sells 1,500 units at $9, calculate the profit or loss.
10. Explain why a bank might ask to see a business plan.
11. Why might a forecast turn out to be wrong?
12. Explain one way a business can use its plan after it has started.

### Answers

1. Any two: set up the business; raise finance; set objectives; organise functions.
2. Any three: executive summary; business description; aims and objectives; market research; marketing plan; operations plan; human resources plan; financial plan.
3. Start-up costs, sales forecasts, cash flow forecasts, expected costs and profit.
4. Benefit: for example, makes it easier to raise finance. Drawback: for example, takes time or forecasts may be wrong.
5. Fixed costs do not change with output in the short term; variable costs change with output.
6. Insurance: fixed. Packaging: variable. Flour: variable. Rent: fixed.
7. 450 x $12 = **$5,400**.
8. Variable costs = 1,500 x $4 = $6,000; total costs = $5,000 + $6,000 = **$11,000**.
9. Revenue = 1,500 x $9 = $13,500; profit = $13,500 - $11,000 = **$2,500 profit**.
10. The plan shows what the money is for and whether the business can repay, so the bank can judge the risk.
11. For example, the owner may overestimate sales for a new business with no sales record, or costs may rise.
12. For example, compare actual sales with the forecast and decide whether to cut costs or increase marketing.
