## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a feature of bipolar depression but **not** of unipolar
depression? Shade one box. **[1]**

- A Low mood
- B Periods of mania
- C Changes in sleep
- D Reduced energy

**2.** Distinguish between **substance misuse** and **dependence**. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **two** effects of significant mental health problems on society. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why the recorded incidence of mental health problems may have increased
over time. **[2]**

**5.** Outline what is meant by a **negative schema**. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Read the scenario.

*For the past two months, Ali has felt low nearly every day. He has stopped going to football, which
he used to love, and says he is "always tired". He wakes at 4am and cannot get back to sleep, and he
tells his doctor he is "useless at everything".*

**(a)** Identify **three** symptoms of depression shown by Ali, referring to the scenario. **[3]**
**(b)** Ali's doctor suggests CBT. Explain how CBT might help Ali. **[3]**

**7.** Describe Wiles' study of the effectiveness of CBT. **[4]**

**8.** Read the scenario.

*Jess started smoking at 14 when her friends did. She now smokes more each day than she used to,
feels irritable and shaky if she goes without, and has carried on smoking even after her doctor said
it was damaging her lungs.*

**(a)** Identify **three** features of dependence shown by Jess. **[3]**
**(b)** Use the psychological explanation of addiction to explain why Jess started smoking. **[2]**

**9.** Evaluate Kaij's twin study as evidence for a biological explanation of addiction. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate aversion therapy and self-management programmes as interventions for addiction. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss biological and psychological explanations of depression. Refer to interventions in
your answer. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** Periods of mania [1].

**2.** Substance misuse is harmful or unintended use of a substance, such as on a single occasion,
without necessarily being dependent [1]. Dependence is a compulsion to use despite harm, with
features such as tolerance and withdrawal [1].

**3.** Any two: greater need for health and care services [1]; cost to the economy through lost
working days or benefits [1]; possible links with crime [1].

**4.** Any one, identified [1] and explained [1]: increased **recognition** -- better understanding
means more problems are diagnosed; **reduced stigma** -- people are more willing to seek help, so more
cases are recorded; **challenges of modern living** -- isolation or pressure may lead more people to
develop problems.

**5.** A mental framework, often formed through early experience [1], that leads a person to
interpret themselves, the world and the future in a negative way [1].

**6. (a)** Any three, each linked to the scenario [1] each: **low mood** -- "felt low nearly every
day"; **loss of interest and enjoyment** -- stopped going to football; **reduced energy** -- "always
tired"; **changes in sleep** -- wakes at 4am; **decreased self-confidence** -- "useless at
everything".
**(b)** CBT would help Ali identify negative, irrational thoughts such as "I'm useless at
everything" [1]; the therapist would challenge these by asking for evidence, helping Ali replace them
with more balanced thoughts [1]; behavioural tasks, such as gradually returning to football, would
increase positive experiences and improve his mood [1].

**7.** Up to 4 marks. A randomised controlled trial with patients in UK GP practices whose depression
had not responded to antidepressants [1]. Patients were randomly allocated to usual care or usual care
plus CBT [1]. Symptoms were measured with the Beck Depression Inventory [1]. The CBT group showed a
greater reduction in symptoms at six months, and the benefit was still present at twelve months [1].

**8. (a)** Any three [1] each: **tolerance** -- smokes more each day than she used to; **withdrawal**
-- irritable and shaky without cigarettes; **persistent use despite harm** -- continued after being
told it was damaging her lungs.
**(b)** Peer influence: Jess started when her friends did, so she may have imitated them and wanted to
fit in with the group [1]; smoking may have seemed normal in her group, or brought approval from
friends, which reinforced the behaviour [1].

**9.** Up to 4 marks, two points explained.
- *Strength:* identical twins share all their genes and non-identical twins about half, so higher
  concordance in identical twins suggests a genetic contribution to alcohol abuse [1] [1].
- *Weakness:* concordance was not 100%, so environment must also play a part [1]; identical twins may
  be treated more alike and share a more similar environment, so the difference may not be due to
  genes alone [1].
- *Also creditable:* sample of Swedish men only, so may not generalise to women or other cultures;
  older methods of deciding twin type were less reliable.

**10.** Level-based answer, up to 6 marks.
- *Aversion therapy:* uses classical conditioning to pair the substance with an unpleasant response,
  such as nausea; can reduce desire quickly; but it is unpleasant, raising ethical concerns, patients
  may drop out, and the learned association may fade outside the clinic.
- *Self-management programmes:* self-help groups and twelve-step programmes give support from people
  with similar experiences and a new peer group; free and widely available; but drop-out rates are
  high, success depends on motivation, and they are hard to evaluate because members are anonymous
  and self-selected.
- *Judgement:* neither works for everyone; self-management may address the social causes suggested by
  peer influence, while aversion therapy targets the behaviour directly.
**5--6 marks:** both interventions evaluated with clear strengths and weaknesses and a judgement.
**3--4:** both covered, some explanation. **1--2:** limited or one intervention only.

**11.** Level-based answer, up to 9 marks.
- **Biological:** imbalance of neurotransmitters, especially low serotonin activity. *Intervention:*
  antidepressants such as SSRIs increase serotonin availability. *Evaluation:* antidepressants help
  many patients, supporting the explanation; but they take weeks to work, do not help everyone, and
  it is unclear whether low serotonin is a cause or an effect; side effects and relapse after
  stopping.
- **Psychological:** negative schemas and an internal, stable, global attributional style.
  *Intervention:* CBT challenges negative thoughts and changes behaviour. *Evaluation:* Wiles (2013)
  found adding CBT to usual care improved symptoms in patients not helped by antidepressants alone;
  but negative thinking may result from depression; CBT requires motivation and effort.
- **Discussion:** nature versus nurture; the two can be combined, as Wiles' study shows; neither
  alone explains why some people exposed to the same risk factors do not become depressed.
**7--9 marks:** accurate, detailed knowledge of both, effective discussion with evidence and a
reasoned conclusion. **4--6:** both described with some discussion. **1--3:** limited or one-sided.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing symptoms without linking each one to the scenario (Questions 6 and 8).
- Defining depression as "extreme sadness" rather than using clinical features.
- Treating Kaij's findings as proof that addiction is entirely genetic.
- Describing two explanations side by side without comparing or evaluating them.
