**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.5 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Opposition to Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh): "The reasons for the opposition he faced from the leaders of Makkah, including the boycott of Banu Hashim, the events at Ta'if and the plots to kill Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh)."** It explains why the leaders of Makkah turned against the new message, how they tried to stop it, and how the Prophet (pbuh) and his followers responded. It ends with the prescribed passage, Surah al-Masad (Qur'an 111:1-5).

## Why did the leaders of Makkah oppose him?

Opposition grew as the message became public. The Quraysh had four main reasons.

| Reason | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Religious | Tawhid condemned the idols and the religion of their ancestors; people said they would follow "what we found our fathers doing" (2:170) |
| Economic | Tribes made pilgrimage to the Ka'aba and its idols each year, bringing trade and income to Makkah |
| Political and tribal | Leadership rested on tribal honour. Many leaders did not want to follow a man from Banu Hashim. Leaders such as Abu Jahl and Abu Lahab feared losing power |
| Social | Islam taught that all are equal before Allah. This threatened the privilege of the wealthy over slaves and the poor, and the new religion attracted the weak, which angered the strong |

Another objection was the teaching of the Day of Judgement and life after death, which they found hard to believe, as the Qur'an says (for example, 17:49). It also meant that rich leaders would be held accountable.

## Methods of opposition

The Quraysh tried several methods, in increasing order of severity.

1. **Mockery and insults.** They called the Prophet (pbuh) a poet, a madman or a sorcerer, and mocked his followers.
2. **Bargaining.** Leaders offered him wealth, leadership or marriage if he would stop preaching. He refused, saying that he would not give up his mission even if they set the sun in his right hand and the moon in his left.
3. **Pressure on his protector.** They went to Abu Talib and asked him to stop his nephew. Abu Talib told the Prophet (pbuh) of their demand, but the Prophet (pbuh) kept to his task. Abu Talib continued to protect him.
4. **Persecution of weaker Muslims.** Those with no tribal protection suffered most. **Bilal (RA)** was dragged over hot sand with a stone on his chest, and kept firm in his faith until Abu Bakr (RA) bought and freed him. **Sumayyah** and her husband **Yasir** were tortured, and Sumayyah is remembered as the first martyr of Islam. Their son **Ammar** survived.
5. **Migration to Abyssinia.** Around 615 CE the Prophet (pbuh) advised a group of Muslims to move to Abyssinia, where the Christian king (the Negus) was just. The Quraysh sent envoys to bring them back, but the Negus protected them after hearing the Qur'an's words about Maryam and Isa.

## The boycott of Banu Hashim

When Hazrat Hamza (RA) and Hazrat Umar (RA) accepted Islam, the Quraysh feared that the movement could not be stopped by force. Their leaders agreed to a **boycott** of Banu Hashim and Banu Muttalib, who were protecting the Prophet (pbuh). The agreement was written on a document and hung in the Ka'aba. It said that no one would:

- trade with them,
- marry from them or give them in marriage,
- speak to them or sell them food.

The clans were forced to live in a narrow valley (the **Shi'b of Abu Talib**) for about three years (around 616 to 619 CE). They suffered severe hunger. Their children cried from lack of food, and they had to eat leaves and hides. Some relatives secretly sent food. Eventually, several Quraysh members felt shame and moved to cancel the boycott. According to tradition, the Prophet (pbuh) said that termites had eaten the document except for the name of Allah. The boycott ended.

**Importance of the boycott:** it tested the loyalty of the clan, showed the patience of the Muslims, and left them weak and ill.

## The Year of Sorrow and Ta'if

Soon after the boycott, in about 619 CE, both Abu Talib and Hazrat Khadija (RA) died within a short time. This is called the **Year of Sorrow**. The Prophet (pbuh) lost his protector and his greatest comfort. After Abu Talib's death, the Quraysh attacked him more freely.

### The journey to Ta'if

Seeking help and a new home for Islam, the Prophet (pbuh) travelled to the town of **Ta'if**, about sixty miles away, with Zayd ibn Harithah (RA). He invited the leaders of the tribe of Thaqif to Islam and asked them to protect him.

- **They refused.** The leaders rejected him rudely.
- **They set the people on him.** Children and slaves threw stones until his feet bled. Zayd (RA) tried to shield him and was wounded.
- **He took refuge.** The Prophet (pbuh) found shelter in a vineyard belonging to two Makkan men, who sent him grapes by a Christian servant, Addas, who was moved by the Prophet's (pbuh) words.
- **His prayer.** He raised his hands and prayed, "O Allah, to You I complain of my weakness, my lack of resources and my low standing before people... If You are not angry with me, I do not mind."
- **The angel's offer.** According to the hadith, the angel in charge of the mountains offered to crush the two mountains over the people of Makkah. The Prophet (pbuh) refused and said he hoped that Allah would bring from them people who worshipped Him alone.

**Importance of Ta'if:** it shows his patience, his forgiveness, his trust in Allah and his hope for people's guidance. Shortly after this, the journey of Isra and Mi'raj took place (the next topic).

## The plots to kill the Prophet (pbuh)

After the Year of Sorrow and as more people in Yathrib accepted Islam, the Quraysh feared that the Prophet (pbuh) would be safe in a new base. At a meeting at the Dar al-Nadwa, they decided to kill him. Abu Jahl proposed that one strong young man from each clan should strike him together, so that the guilt would be shared among all the clans and Banu Hashim could not take revenge on everyone. The Qur'an refers to this plot: "when those who disbelieved plotted against you to restrain you or kill you or evict you. But they plan, and Allah plans, and Allah is the best of planners" (8:30).

Allah informed the Prophet (pbuh) through Jibril (AS). That night he left with Abu Bakr (RA), and Ali (RA) slept in his bed. The plot failed. This leads to the Hijrah.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 111:1-5

"May the hands of Abu Lahab be ruined, and ruined is he. His wealth will not avail him or that which he gained. He will [enter to] burn in a Fire of [blazing] flame. And his wife [as well] - the carrier of firewood. Around her neck is a rope of [twisted] fibre."

- **Abu Lahab** was the Prophet's (pbuh) uncle, one of the loudest opponents. After the call from Mount Safa he insulted him. The surah is a warning that even close family receive no protection if they oppose the truth.
- **"His wealth will not avail him"** shows that wealth and status cannot save a person from Allah's judgement.
- **"the carrier of firewood"** refers to his wife, Umm Jamil, who is said to have spread thorns in the Prophet's (pbuh) path and spread slander.
- The surah shows that Allah defends His Messenger.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the leaders of Makkah opposed the Prophet (pbuh). Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

> First, they were worried about their income [1]. Pilgrims visited the Ka'aba and its idols each year, and the leaders feared that worship of one God would end the trade that made Makkah rich [1]. Second, they feared losing their status [1], since the message that all are equal before Allah threatened the power of rich leaders over the slaves and the poor [1]. The Qur'an says of Abu Lahab, "His wealth will not avail him" (111:2), which shows that wealth and rank cannot protect a person from Allah's judgement [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Giving one reason only, such as "they did not believe him".** Use religious, economic, political and social reasons.
- **Mixing up the boycott and Ta'if.** The boycott was in Makkah, in the valley; Ta'if was a journey to another town.
- **Saying that Abu Talib became Muslim.** He protected the Prophet (pbuh) but did not accept Islam.
- **Forgetting the Year of Sorrow.** It links the boycott to Ta'if.
- **Not applying the passage.** Quote and explain it.

## Exam technique

**Give** questions need a name (Abu Lahab, Zayd, Thaqif). **Describe** questions need the events in order. **Explain** questions need a reason and its effect. In the 12-mark question on Surah al-Masad, use "may the hands of Abu Lahab be ruined" for Allah's judgement on an opponent, "his wealth will not avail him" for the limits of wealth, and consider whether the surah is a lesson about one man or a warning to all who oppose truth.
