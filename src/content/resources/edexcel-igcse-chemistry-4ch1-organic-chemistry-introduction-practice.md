These questions cover sub-topic 4(a) Introduction of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.1 to 4.6, none of which has a C reference. Where an example uses an alcohol or carboxylic acid, the question is marked **(Paper 2 only)**. Revise with the [organic chemistry introduction study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-introduction/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-introduction-revision-notes/). Draw structures on paper, showing all the bonds in displayed formulae.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a hydrocarbon. **[1]**

**2.** Four compounds are C₂H₆, C₂H₅OH, C₄H₈ and CH₃Cl. State which are hydrocarbons. **[2]**

**3.** Give the empirical formulae of C₄H₈ and C₆H₁₄. **[2]**

**4.** State the general formula of the alkenes. **[1]**

**5.** Name the compound CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₃. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A hydrocarbon contains 82.8% carbon and 17.2% hydrogen by mass. Its relative molecular mass is 58.

**(a)** Calculate its empirical formula. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its molecular formula. **[1]**
**(c)** Draw a displayed formula of one isomer with this molecular formula. **[1]**

**7.** Pentane, C₅H₁₂, has isomers.

**(a)** State what is meant by isomers. **[1]**
**(b)** Draw the structural formulae and give the names of the three isomers of C₅H₁₂. **[3]**

**8.** Butene has the molecular formula C₄H₈.

**(a)** Draw the structural formulae and name the two unbranched alkene isomers. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how these two isomers differ. **[1]**

**9.** Ethane, C₂H₆, and ethene, C₂H₄, are both hydrocarbons.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of ethene. **[2]**
**(b)** State the functional group in ethene. **[1]**
**(c)** State two features that all members of a homologous series have in common. **[2]**

**10.** Classify each reaction as substitution, addition or combustion, giving a reason.

**(a)** C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl (in ultraviolet light) **[2]**
**(b)** C₃H₆ + Br₂ → C₃H₆Br₂ **[2]**
**(c)** C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O **[2]**

**11.** A student names the compound CH₃CH(CH₂CH₃)CH₂CH₃ as 2-ethylbutane.

**(a)** Explain why this name is wrong. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the correct name. **[1]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) Name the compounds with these structural formulae.

**(a)** CH₃CH₂CH₂OH **[1]**
**(b)** CH₃CH₂COOH **[1]**
**(c)** State the functional group in each compound. **[2]**

**13.** A compound has the formula C₂H₅Br.

**(a)** Name the compound. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether it is a hydrocarbon and give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the type of reaction when ethane reacts with bromine in ultraviolet radiation to form it. **[1]**

**14.** Calculate the percentage by mass of carbon in propane, C₃H₈, and in propene, C₃H₆, and state which would burn with the smokier flame. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A compound containing hydrogen and carbon only [1].

**2.** C₂H₆ [1] and C₄H₈ [1]. (C₂H₅OH contains oxygen and CH₃Cl contains chlorine.)

**3.** C₄H₈ is CH₂ [1]; C₆H₁₄ is C₃H₇ [1].

**4.** CₙH₂ₙ [1].

**5.** Butane [1].

**6. (a)** Moles: C = 82.8 / 12 = 6.9; H = 17.2 / 1 = 17.2 [1]. Ratio 6.9 : 17.2 = 1 : 2.5 = 2 : 5, so the empirical formula is C₂H₅ [1].
**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 29; 58 / 29 = 2, so the molecular formula is C₄H₁₀ [1].
**(c)** Displayed formula of butane (four carbons in a chain with ten hydrogens, each carbon with four bonds) or of 2-methylpropane (a carbon bonded to three CH₃ groups and one hydrogen) [1].

**7. (a)** Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae [1].
**(b)** Pentane, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]; 2-methylbutane, CH₃CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ [1]; 2,2-dimethylpropane, CH₃C(CH₃)₂CH₃ [1].

**8. (a)** But-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ [1]; but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃ [1].
**(b)** The C=C double bond is in a different position in the chain [1].

**9. (a)** Two carbon atoms joined by a double bond (two lines) [1], each carbon with two single bonds to hydrogen atoms (four C-H bonds in total) [1].
**(b)** C=C (a carbon-carbon double bond) [1].
**(c)** Any two from: the same functional group; the same general formula; similar chemical properties; a gradual trend in physical properties; each member differs from the next by CH₂ [2].

**10. (a)** Substitution [1], because a hydrogen atom is replaced by a chlorine atom and two products form [1].
**(b)** Addition [1], because bromine adds across the double bond to give one product [1].
**(c)** Combustion [1], because the hydrocarbon burns in oxygen to give carbon dioxide and water [1].

**11. (a)** The longest chain contains five carbon atoms, not four [1], so the compound should be named as a pentane with a methyl branch, not as a butane with an ethyl branch [1].
**(b)** 3-methylpentane [1].

**12. (a)** Propan-1-ol (or propanol) [1].
**(b)** Propanoic acid [1].
**(c)** -OH (alcohol) in (a) [1]; -COOH (carboxyl) in (b) [1].

**13. (a)** Bromoethane [1].
**(b)** It is not a hydrocarbon, because it contains bromine as well as carbon and hydrogen [1].
**(c)** Substitution [1].

**14.** Propane: 36 / 44 x 100 = 81.8% [1]. Propene: 36 / 42 x 100 = 85.7% [1]. Propene has the higher percentage of carbon, so it is more likely to burn incompletely and give soot, and has the smokier flame [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an alcohol or a chloroalkane as a hydrocarbon.
- Forgetting that the empirical formula is the simplest ratio, not the real number of atoms.
- Drawing the same isomer twice by bending the chain.
- Using the wrong chain length when naming.
- Not giving the reason in a classification of reaction.
- Writing "same formula" without "different structural formula" for isomers.
