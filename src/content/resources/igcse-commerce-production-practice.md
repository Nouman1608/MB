## Section A

**1.** Define production and distinguish between direct and indirect production. **[3]**

**2.** Name the four factors of production and their rewards. **[4]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain **three** advantages and **two** disadvantages of the division of labour for a manufacturer. **[10]**

**4.** Explain the functions of a retailer, giving **four** functions. **[8]**

**5.** Compare a small independent retailer with a supermarket chain on: buying power, range of goods, and personal service. **[6]**

**6.** Explain **three** benefits and **two** drawbacks of international trade for a developing country. **[10]**

---

## Answers

**1. Production** is the **creation of goods and services to satisfy human wants** [1]. **Direct production** is producing for **one's own use, with no exchange** — subsistence [1]. **Indirect production** is producing for **exchange with others**, which requires specialisation, trade and money [1].

**2.** **Land** — rent [1]. **Labour** — wages [1]. **Capital** — interest [1]. **Enterprise** — profit [1].

**3. Advantages**, 2 marks each: **workers become more skilled at a single task through repetition**, so output per worker and quality both rise [1] [1]. **Less time is lost moving between tasks and changing tools**, so production is faster [1] [1]. It allows the use of **specialised machinery and the assembly line**, which cuts unit costs and makes mass production possible [1] [1]. **Training is quicker and cheaper**, as each worker learns only one operation [1] [1].
**Disadvantages**, 2 marks each: **monotony reduces motivation**, raising absenteeism, turnover and the risk of careless errors [1] [1]. **Interdependence** — if one stage of the line stops through a breakdown or a strike, **the entire process halts**, so production is highly vulnerable [1] [1]. (Also accept: workers become deskilled and are vulnerable to structural unemployment.)

**4.** Any four, 2 marks each: **breaking bulk** — buying in quantity and selling in the small amounts consumers want [1] [1]. **Holding stock** — so consumers can buy at any time without waiting for the producer [1] [1]. **Providing choice** — stocking competing brands so consumers can compare [1] [1]. **Providing information and after-sales service** — advice, demonstrations, guarantees and returns [1] [1]. **Offering credit and convenience** — payment options, delivery, and a location close to the consumer [1] [1]. **Feeding information back to producers** about what is selling [1] [1].

**5. Buying power** — the chain buys in **enormous quantities and negotiates large discounts**, so its costs and prices are lower; the independent buys small quantities, often through a wholesaler, at higher unit cost [1] [1].
**Range of goods** — the chain stocks **tens of thousands of lines under one roof**, including its own brands; the independent stocks a **narrow range**, though it may specialise in goods the chain does not carry [1] [1].
**Personal service** — the independent offers **knowledge of individual customers, advice and flexibility**, such as informal credit or special orders; the chain offers **standardised, impersonal service** delivered by staff with less product knowledge [1] [1].

**6. Benefits**, 2 marks each: **access to a much larger market**, so producers can specialise and exploit economies of scale beyond the size of the home market [1] [1]. **Import of capital goods and technology** that the country cannot produce itself, raising productivity [1] [1]. **Foreign exchange earnings** to pay for essential imports and service debt [1] [1]. **Greater consumer choice and lower prices**, and competition that forces domestic firms to become efficient [1] [1].
**Drawbacks**, 2 marks each: **over-dependence on one or two primary commodity exports**, whose world prices are volatile, so export earnings fluctuate unpredictably [1] [1]. **Infant industries may be destroyed by established foreign competition** before they can reach an efficient scale, leaving the country locked into low-value production [1] [1]. (Also accept: a persistent trade deficit; the loss of local culture; environmental damage from extraction for export.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing direct production with primary production.
- Giving advantages of division of labour to the worker when the question asks about the manufacturer.
- Listing retail functions without explaining the benefit.
- Discussing trade benefits without any drawback when both are asked for.
