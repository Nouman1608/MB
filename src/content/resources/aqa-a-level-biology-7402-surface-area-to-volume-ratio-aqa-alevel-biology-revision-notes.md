Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.3.1 Surface area to volume ratio**. Worked examples with cylinders and same-volume shapes are in the [surface area to volume ratio study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-surface-area-to-volume-ratio-aqa-alevel-biology/). Exam-style questions are in the [surface area to volume ratio practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-surface-area-to-volume-ratio-aqa-alevel-biology-practice/), and the whole topic is summarised in the [exchange with the environment guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-exchange-with-environment/).

## The three statements to know

1. As the size of an organism or structure increases, its surface area to volume ratio decreases.
2. Larger organisms have changes in body shape and develop systems (exchange surfaces and mass transport) that make exchange possible as the ratio falls.
3. Smaller animals have a higher surface area to volume ratio and a higher metabolic rate per unit mass.

## Formulae for regular shapes

| Shape | Surface area | Volume | SA:V simplifies to |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube, side *a* | 6*a*² | *a*³ | 6 ÷ *a* |
| Cuboid *l* × *w* × *h* | 2(*lw* + *lh* + *wh*) | *lwh* | — |
| Sphere, radius *r* | 4π*r*² | (4/3)π*r*³ | 3 ÷ *r* |
| Cylinder, radius *r*, length *h* | 2π*r*² + 2π*rh* | π*r*²*h* | — |

Circumference of a circle = 2π*r*. A cylinder's curved surface is the circumference × length.

### Calculation routine

1. Put every dimension in the same unit.
2. Work out surface area, then volume, with units.
3. Divide surface area by volume.
4. Write as *n* : 1 (or as the number the question asks for), to sensible significant figures.
5. Comment: higher ratio means more surface per unit volume.

**Quick check:** spheres of radius 2 µm and 20 µm have SA:V of 1.5 : 1 and 0.15 : 1. Ten times the radius gives one-tenth of the ratio.

## Why falling SA:V is a problem

- Need for exchange depends on volume (amount of respiring tissue).
- Rate of exchange depends on surface area and on diffusion distance.
- Large organism: too little surface per unit volume, and most cells far from the surface.
- Single-celled organism: diffusion across the body surface is enough.

## How larger organisms solve it

| Solution | How it helps | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Flattened body | Large SA:V, short distance to every cell | Flatworm |
| Long, thin body | Raises SA:V for the same volume | Worm-like shapes |
| Specialised exchange surface | Adds surface without much volume; thin barrier | Alveoli, gill lamellae, tracheoles, villi, leaf mesophyll |
| Mass transport system | Moves substances between surface and deep cells; keeps gradients | Blood circulation; xylem and phloem |

## Metabolic rate link

- Heat made in the body volume by respiration; lost through the surface.
- Small mammal: high SA:V → loses heat faster per unit mass.
- So: higher rate of respiration per unit mass to keep body temperature.
- So: needs more food and oxygen per gram; often faster breathing and heart rate.
- Large mammal: higher *total* metabolic rate, lower rate *per unit mass*.
- Cold climates favour compact bodies with small extremities; hot climates favour features that raise surface area, such as large ears.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Surface area to volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume, usually written *n* : 1 |
| Exchange surface | Specialised surface across which substances pass between an organism and its environment |
| Mass transport | Bulk movement of fluid carrying substances around an organism |
| Diffusion distance | Distance a substance must diffuse, from source to where it is used |
| Metabolic rate | Rate of energy use by an organism, often measured as rate of oxygen uptake |
| Per unit mass | Divided by body mass, so animals of different sizes can be compared |

## Exam traps

- Writing the ratio upside down.
- Using different units for area and volume.
- "Large animals have a small surface area": the area is larger; the *ratio* is smaller.
- Leaving out "per unit mass" in metabolic rate answers.
- Explaining a flattened shape only as "bigger surface"; add "shorter diffusion distance".
- Forgetting that a mass transport system is needed as well as an exchange surface.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate the SA:V of a cube of side 5 cm.
2. What happens to SA:V when the length of a cube is doubled?
3. Give the SA:V of a sphere of radius 4 µm.
4. Explain why a single-celled organism does not need a specialised gas exchange surface.
5. Give two features of body shape that increase SA:V.
6. A block is 2 cm × 2 cm × 8 cm. Calculate its SA:V.
7. Explain why a mouse has a higher metabolic rate per gram than a horse.
8. Why do large organisms need a mass transport system as well as an exchange surface?
9. Name two specialised exchange surfaces in animals and one in plants.
10. Suggest why mammals living in very cold places tend to have small ears.
11. A student says "the elephant has a lower metabolic rate than the shrew". Why is this statement incomplete?
12. Give the formula for the surface area of a cylinder of radius *r* and length *h*.

### Answers

1. SA = 150 cm², V = 125 cm³; SA:V = **1.2 : 1**.
2. It halves.
3. 3 ÷ 4 = **0.75 : 1** (µm⁻¹).
4. Its SA:V is high and diffusion distances are short, so diffusion across the body surface is fast enough to meet its needs.
5. Flattened; long and thin (elongated).
6. SA = 2(4 + 16 + 16) = 72 cm²; V = 32 cm³; SA:V = **2.25 : 1**.
7. A mouse has a larger SA:V, so it loses heat faster per unit mass and must respire faster per unit mass to keep its body temperature.
8. Most cells are too far from the exchange surface for diffusion alone; mass transport carries substances to and from them and maintains gradients.
9. Animals: any two of alveoli, gill lamellae, tracheoles, villi. Plants: leaf mesophyll (with stomata).
10. Smaller ears reduce surface area, so less heat is lost.
11. The elephant's total metabolic rate is higher; only its rate per unit mass is lower.
12. 2π*r*² + 2π*rh*.
