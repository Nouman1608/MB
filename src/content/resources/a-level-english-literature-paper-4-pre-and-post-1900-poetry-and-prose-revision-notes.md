Paper 4 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Literature in English (9695) is a two-hour, closed-book
A Level paper in which you write **two essays, one on a pre-1900 set text (Section A) and one on a
post-1900 set text (Section B), and one of the two must be on poetry and the other on prose**. Each
essay is worth 25 marks, and every question assesses all five assessment objectives, including AO5,
the evaluation of other opinions and interpretations. For the full method, use the
[Paper 4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-paper-4-pre-and-post-1900-poetry-and-prose-study-guide/).

## Format at a glance

| Detail | Value (syllabus for 2024, 2025 and 2026) |
|---|---|
| Duration | 2 hours |
| Marks | 50 (25 per section) |
| Weighting | 25% of the A Level; not part of the AS Level |
| Sections | A: Pre-1900 Poetry and Prose; B: Post-1900 Poetry and Prose |
| Questions answered | Two: one from each section |
| Form rule | One essay on a poetry set text, one on a prose set text |
| Choice per text | (a) essay question or (b) question on a printed passage or poem |
| Books | Set texts **not** allowed; no dictionaries |
| AOs | AO1–AO5, all in every question |

**Timing:** the sections carry equal marks, so give each essay about one hour, including a short plan.

## Which routes include Paper 4?

Paper 4 belongs only to the A Level routes. On the staged route you take Papers 1 and 2 for AS Level
in Year 1 and complete the A Level with Papers 3 and 4 in Year 2. On the single-series route you take
all four papers together. AS-only candidates never sit Paper 4.

## The form rule: the most expensive mistake

You study **two** set texts for Paper 4, one from each section, and the pair must be **one poetry text
and one prose text**. Poetry texts carry an asterisk in the set-text list.

| If your Section A text is… | …your Section B text must be |
|---|---|
| Poetry (e.g. Shakespeare's *Selected Sonnets*) | Prose (e.g. Mansfield's *Selected Stories*) |
| Prose (e.g. *Middlemarch*) | Poetry (e.g. Glück's *The Wild Iris*) |

Two prose essays, or two poetry essays, break the rubric.

## The five assessment objectives

At A Level each AO is worth 20%, and on Paper 4 each is 20% of the paper.

| AO | Name | What it means in a Paper 4 essay |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Knowledge and understanding | Secure knowledge of the whole text, with relevant contexts that illuminate readings |
| AO2 | Analysis | How choices of language, form and structure shape meaning and effect |
| AO3 | Personal response | Your informed, independent opinion and interpretation |
| AO4 | Communication | A relevant, structured, supported argument in an appropriate register |
| AO5 | Evaluation of opinion | Discussing and evaluating varying opinions and interpretations of the text |

AO5 is the A Level addition. It does not require you to memorise critics' names; it requires you to
**weigh** alternative readings, which can come from critics, from different critical approaches, or
from different readers and audiences, and to say which you find more convincing and why.

## (a) or (b)? Choosing your question

| | (a) Essay question | (b) Passage or poem question |
|---|---|---|
| What is printed | Question only | A passage from the novel or a poem from the selection |
| Demands | Range across the whole text from memory | Close analysis of the printed text, linked to the wider text |
| Suits you if | You know the text's structure and can quote from memory | You analyse closely and can connect a passage to the whole |
| Common risk | Generalising without detail | Line-by-line paraphrase with no wider reference |

Read both before deciding; the syllabus lets you choose freely.

## Pre-1900 versus post-1900: what to expect

- **Pre-1900 texts** often reward attention to period conventions: Chaucer's frame narrative and estates
  satire, the sonnet form and its volta, the omniscient narrator of Eliot and Hardy, Austen's free
  indirect style and irony.
- **Post-1900 texts** often reward attention to experiment: fragmented chronology in *Beloved*,
  allegorical setting in *Waiting for the Barbarians*, Mansfield's interior focus and open endings,
  the sequence structure of *The Wild Iris* and *Native Guard*.
- In both, context is useful only where it helps explain a writer's choice.

## How to build an AO5 sentence

1. State a reading: "One reading sees Dorothea's marriage to Casaubon as the novel's central tragedy."
2. Test it against the text: "Yet the Finale claims that 'the growing good of the world is partly dependent on unhistoric acts'."
3. Judge: "This makes the stronger case that Eliot values unhistoric influence over visible success."

## Worked example: a (b) opening on a sonnet

Question: *Discuss Shakespeare's presentation of time in Sonnet 73, considering it in relation to your
wider reading of the selection.*

Strong opening: "Sonnet 73 divides its first twelve lines into three quatrains, each a smaller unit of
time (a season, a day, a fire), so that the speaker's ageing seems to speed up as the images contract.
The final couplet then turns from the speaker to the listener, and from decay to love; readers who find
the turn consoling and those who find it a demand for pity are both responding to that structural
shift." This addresses AO2 (structure), AO3 (argument), AO1 (wider text implied) and AO5 (two
readings) in three sentences.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Breaking the form rule.** One poetry essay and one prose essay, always.
- **Treating AO5 as name-dropping.** A critic quoted without evaluation earns little; an evaluated
  alternative reading earns AO5.
- **(b) answers that stay inside the passage.** Link the passage or poem to the wider text.
- **(a) answers with no detail.** Closed book means you must learn short quotations and precise
  references.
- **Biography instead of analysis.** Context must illuminate a choice in the text.
- **Uneven timing.** Each section is 25 marks; a short second essay costs as much as a weak first one.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I take my set texts into the Paper 4 exam?**
No. The 9695 syllabus states that set texts are not allowed in the examination room, so you need to know
key passages and quotations from memory.

**Do I have to write about one poem or several?**
For a poetry (a) question you should range across your studied selection. A (b) question prints one poem
for close analysis, but strong answers relate it to the wider selection.

**Is AO5 assessed in every Paper 4 question?**
Yes. The syllabus says all questions on Paper 4 assess AO1, AO2, AO3, AO4 and AO5.

**Why does Paper 4 not count for AS Level?**
Paper 4 and Paper 3 are A Level components. AS Level consists of Papers 1 and 2, and AO5 carries 0% at
AS Level.
