## Definitions to know cold

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolute poverty | Income or consumption below a fixed level needed for basic needs; the line is fixed in real terms |
| Relative poverty | Income far below the typical income in the same society; the line moves with the median |
| Headcount ratio | People below the line as a percentage of the population |
| Poverty gap | Average shortfall of the poor below the line |
| Income | A flow of earnings over a period |
| Wealth | A stock of assets less debts |
| Lorenz curve | Cumulative share of income against cumulative share of households, poorest first |
| Gini coefficient | A / (A + B); 0 is perfect equality, 1 is total inequality |
| Poverty trap | Weak incentive to earn more because benefits are withdrawn and tax is paid |

## 1 Poverty: measures and method (outcomes 1a, 1b)

**Absolute measures:** headcount ratio, poverty gap, an international dollars-a-day line, multidimensional indices (health, education, living standards).
**Relative measures:** share below a set fraction of median income (60% is a common choice), and ratios such as the top 10% to the bottom 10%.

**Method: headcount ratio and poverty gap**
1. Count households below the line.
2. Headcount = poor / total x 100.
3. Gap for each poor household = line - income.
4. Average gap among the poor = total gap / number of poor.

*Reminder:* Country Delta has 40 million people and 6 million are poor. Headcount = 6 / 40 = 15%.

**Method: relative line**
1. Find the median income.
2. Multiply by the chosen fraction.
3. Compare each household with the new line each year.

*Reminder:* Median 1,800, 60% line = 1,080. A household on 700 is 380 below and a household on 900 is 180 below. Total gap 560, average 280.

**Must-know distinction:** absolute poverty can fall while relative poverty rises, because the relative line moves up with the median. Real values matter for absolute lines: nominal income of 2,090 after a 10% rise in prices is worth 2,090 / 1.10 = 1,900 at old prices, a fall of 100 on a base of 2,000.

## 2 Causes of changes in poverty (outcome 1c)

| Cause | Chain of reasoning | Evaluation point |
|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | Higher output, more jobs, more income and tax | Depends on who gains; check the distribution |
| Education and training | Skills, productivity, wages | Long lag; the poorest may drop out |
| Welfare benefits | Direct income, smaller gap | Poverty trap; fiscal cost |
| Tax structure | Progressive taxes help; regressive indirect taxes hurt | Depends on the mix of taxes |
| Structural change | Jobs shift from low to high productivity | Structural unemployment; regional decline |
| Aid | Funds schools, health, infrastructure | Tied aid, corruption, dependency |
| Conflict | Destroys capital, displaces people, deters investment | Poverty can persist after peace |

**Worked reminder: poverty trap.** A worker earns 50 more, loses 30 in benefits and pays 10 in tax. Keeps 10. The effective marginal tax rate is (30 + 10) / 50 = 80%.

**Worked reminder: growth and poverty.** Mean income grows 5% and the headcount falls from 30% to 27%. That is a change of -10%, so the response is -10 / 5 = -2.0.

**Worked reminder: regressive indirect tax.** A 20% sales tax on spending. A household on 8,000 spending all of it pays 1,600, or 20% of income. A household on 80,000 spending 40% pays 6,400, or 8% of income. The poorer household bears the heavier burden.

## 3 Inequality: wealth, income, measurement (outcomes 2a, 2b)

- Wealth is a stock; income is a flow.
- Wealth is usually more unequal than income because it builds up and is inherited.
- Wealth earns income, so wealth gaps feed income gaps.

**Method: Gini from grouped data**
1. Write the cumulative shares of income, starting at 0.
2. Find each trapezium: width of group x average of its two cumulative shares.
3. Add them to get B, the area under the Lorenz curve.
4. Gini = 1 - 2B.

*Reminder:* Country Z has income shares by fifth of 4, 10, 16, 24, 46 per cent.
- Cumulative: 0, 4, 14, 30, 54, 100.
- B = 0.2 x (0.02 + 0.09 + 0.22 + 0.42 + 0.77) = 0.2 x 1.52 = 0.304.
- Gini = 1 - 0.608 = 0.392.

Country W has shares 7, 11, 16, 22, 44, cumulative 7, 18, 34, 56, 100, B = 0.330 and a Gini of 0.340. W is more equal than Z.

**Reading the diagram.** The curve starts at the origin and ends at 100% on both axes. A curve closer to the 45-degree line means less inequality. A curve that moves towards the line after taxes and benefits shows redistribution.

**Limits:** grouped data hide the top; two curves that cross can share a Gini; no information about absolute living standards; it depends on whether income is measured before or after tax.

## 4 Causes of inequality (outcome 2c)

**Within countries:** skills and education gaps, discrimination, ownership of assets and inheritance, market power, unemployment and low-wage work, tax and benefit design, regional differences, technology favouring skills.

**Between countries:** capital, human capital, technology, institutions, reliance on a few primary exports, conflict and weak governance, market access, debt, health and population growth.

**Worked reminder: gaps.** Country R has income per head of 12,000 and Country S has 600, a ratio of 20. R grows 1% and S grows 5%.
- R: 12,120. S: 630.
- Ratio: 12,120 / 630 = 19.2, so the ratio narrows.
- Absolute gap: 11,400 rises to 11,490, so it widens.

## 5 Impact of inequality (outcome 2d)

| Area | Effect that may be positive | Effect that may be negative |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise | Rewards risk-taking | Poor face credit barriers; weak demand |
| Incentives | Pay rewards effort and skills | Extreme gaps demotivate; benefit withdrawal blunts effort |
| Savings | Rich save more, funding investment | Weak consumer demand can slow growth |
| Education | Returns to schooling motivate study | Poor families cannot afford it; waste of talent |
| Migration | Remittances; skilled workers fill gaps | Brain drain from poor areas |
| Life expectancy | None | Poorer nutrition, housing and care shorten lives |

## 6 Economic change, development and capitalism (outcomes 2e, 2f)

- Kuznets hypothesis: inequality may rise in early development, then fall. It is a pattern, not a law.
- Globalisation and technology can widen skill premiums, yet trade can also create jobs.
- Free market capitalism rewards productivity and risk. It can drive growth but does not redistribute.
- Intervention (taxes, transfers, public services) trades equity against efficiency. Judge by size, time frame and design.

**Evaluation checklist:** short run or long run, size of the effect, who gains, quality of data, other policies in place.

## Quick self-test

1. State one difference between absolute and relative poverty.
2. A country has 25 poor people in a sample of 250. Calculate the headcount ratio.
3. Median income is 1,800. Find the 60% line.
4. Why can relative poverty rise when all incomes rise?
5. Shares by fifth are 6, 10, 16, 24, 44. State the cumulative share held by the poorest 60%.
6. A worker earns 50 more, loses 25 in benefits and pays 15 in tax. Calculate the effective marginal tax rate.
7. State whether wealth is a stock or a flow.
8. Mean income rises 4% and the poverty rate falls from 25% to 23%. Calculate the percentage change in the poverty rate.
9. Name one cause of inequality between countries.
10. Give one reason why inequality may raise national saving.

### Answers

1. The absolute line is fixed in real terms; the relative line moves with median income.
2. 25 / 250 = 10%.
3. 0.6 x 1,800 = 1,080.
4. The median rises, so the line rises; households whose income grows more slowly fall below it.
5. 6 + 10 + 16 = 32%.
6. (25 + 15) / 50 = 80%.
7. A stock.
8. (23 - 25) / 25 = -8%.
9. For example: differences in capital and technology, or reliance on a few primary exports.
10. The rich have a higher marginal propensity to save, so more income in their hands raises saving.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using only the headcount ratio and ignoring the depth of poverty.
- Treating a fall in absolute poverty as a fall in relative poverty.
- Starting a Lorenz curve away from the origin or labelling axes wrongly.
- Misreading a high Gini as high equality.
- Not stating which gap, ratio or absolute, you are describing.
- Listing causes without a chain of reasoning.
- Giving effects of inequality in one direction only.
- Concluding with a bare assertion rather than a judgement based on size, time and context.
