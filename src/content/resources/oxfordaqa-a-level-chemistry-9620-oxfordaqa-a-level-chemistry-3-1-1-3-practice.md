These questions cover **3.1.1.3 Electron configuration** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, and available to Unit 5). Ionisation energies are approximate values in kJ mol⁻¹. If you get stuck, go back to the [electron configuration study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-3/) or the [electron configuration revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-1-3-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Write the full electron configuration, using s, p and d notation, of:

**(a)** a copper atom (Z = 29) **[1]**

**(b)** an Fe³⁺ ion (Z = 26 for Fe) **[1]**

**(c)** a Br⁻ ion (Z = 35 for Br) **[1]**

**2.** State the number of orbitals in a 3d sub-shell and the maximum number of electrons it can hold. **[1]**

**3.** An atom in its ground state has the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s². Identify the element and state the block of the Periodic Table it is in. **[2]**

**4.** Which ion has the configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰?

A: Cu²⁺

B: Zn²⁺

C: Ni²⁺

D: Ga⁺

**[1]**

**5.** Write an equation, with state symbols, to represent the second ionisation energy of strontium. **[1]**

**6.** Give the formulas of two ions, one positive and one negative, that have the same electron configuration as a neon atom. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why a titanium atom (Z = 22) forms a Ti²⁺ ion with the configuration [Ar] 3d² rather than [Ar] 4s². **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** The first ionisation energies of the Group 2 elements are: Be 900, Mg 738, Ca 590, Sr 550, Ba 503.

**(a)** Define the term first ionisation energy. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the trend in these values. **[3]**

**(c)** State what this trend shows about the arrangement of electrons in atoms. **[1]**

**9.** The first ionisation energies of the Period 3 elements from silicon to argon are: Si 787, P 1012, S 1000, Cl 1251, Ar 1521.

**(a)** Explain the general increase from silicon to argon. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why sulfur's value is lower than phosphorus's. **[2]**

**(c)** Draw the arrangement of electrons in the 3p orbitals of a phosphorus atom and of a sulfur atom, using arrows for electrons. **[2]**

**10.** The second ionisation energies of four Period 3 elements are: Na 4562, Mg 1451, Al 1817, Si 1577.

**(a)** Explain why the second ionisation energy of sodium is much higher than that of magnesium. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the second ionisation energy of silicon is lower than that of aluminium. **[2]**

**11.** The first eight successive ionisation energies of an element X in Period 3 are: 1000, 2252, 3357, 4556, 7004, 8496, 27107, 31719.

**(a)** Deduce the group of X and identify the element. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the electron configuration of the X⁶⁺ ion. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the very large increase between the sixth and seventh values. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why there is also a noticeable increase between the fourth and fifth values, although both electrons come from the same shell. **[2]**

**12.** Explain how the first ionisation energies across Period 3 and down Group 2 together provide evidence that electrons are arranged in both shells and sub-shells. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.**

**(a)** 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ [1]

**(b)** 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁵ [1]

**(c)** 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p⁶ [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For Fe³⁺, both 4s electrons go before any 3d electron.

**2.** Five orbitals, holding a maximum of ten electrons [1] **[1]**

**3.** 26 electrons, so the element is **iron** [1]; it is in the **d block** [1] **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Cu²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁹ and Ni²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁸. Ga⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s², because gallium loses its 4p electron first.

**5.** Sr⁺(g) → Sr²⁺(g) + e⁻ [1] **[1]**

**6.** Any one positive ion, such as Na⁺, Mg²⁺ or Al³⁺ [1]; any one negative ion, such as F⁻, O²⁻ or N³⁻ [1] **[2]**

**7.** Once the 3d sub-shell is occupied, the 4s electrons are lost first when the ion forms, so both electrons come from 4s [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** The energy needed to remove one mole of electrons [1] from one mole of gaseous atoms to form one mole of gaseous 1+ ions [1]

**(b)** Down the group each atom has an extra shell, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus [1]; there is more shielding by inner electrons [1]; these effects outweigh the increase in nuclear charge, so the attraction for the outer electron decreases [1]

**(c)** Electrons are arranged in shells at increasing distances from the nucleus [1] **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** Nuclear charge increases across the period [1]; the electrons are removed from the same shell with similar shielding, so the attraction for the outer electron increases [1]

**(b)** In sulfur, two electrons occupy the same 3p orbital [1]; repulsion between the paired electrons makes one of them easier to remove [1]

**(c)** Phosphorus: three 3p orbitals each with one electron, spins parallel [1]; sulfur: one orbital with two electrons of opposite spin and two orbitals with one electron each [1] **[6]**

**10.**

**(a)** Na⁺ is 1s² 2s² 2p⁶, so its second electron is removed from the 2p sub-shell [1]; Mg⁺ loses a 3s electron [1]; the 2p electron is in a shell closer to the nucleus with less shielding, so it is held much more strongly [1]

**(b)** Si⁺ is [Ne] 3s² 3p¹, so it loses a 3p electron, whereas Al⁺ is [Ne] 3s² and loses a 3s electron [1]; the 3p electron is higher in energy and partly shielded by the 3s electrons, so it is easier to remove [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** The large jump comes after six electrons, so X is in **Group 16** [1]; in Period 3, X is **sulfur** [1]

**(b)** 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ [1]

**(c)** The seventh electron is removed from the second shell (2p) [1], which is closer to the nucleus and less shielded than the third shell [1]

**(d)** The first four electrons are 3p electrons and the fifth is a 3s electron [1]; 3s is lower in energy than 3p, so more energy is needed (it is also being removed from a more positive ion) [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A modest jump inside a shell marks a change of sub-shell; a huge jump marks a change of shell.

**12.** Indicative content; one mark per valid point, up to 6 [6] **[6]**

- First ionisation energy decreases down Group 2 because each element has one more shell, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus.
- Shielding increases down the group and outweighs the increase in nuclear charge, which supports electrons being in successive shells.
- Across Period 3 the general increase comes from increasing nuclear charge with electrons added to the same shell.
- The drop from Mg to Al shows a new, higher-energy sub-shell (3p) starts at aluminium.
- The 3p electron is partly shielded by the 3s electrons, so it is easier to remove.
- The drop from P to S shows that electrons pair in orbitals within the 3p sub-shell, and paired electrons repel.

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the specific sub-shells and the specific elements, and say what each trend is evidence for.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Removing 3d electrons before 4s in d-block ions.
- Writing second ionisation energy equations that remove two electrons.
- Explaining dips with "stability" instead of energy level, shielding or repulsion.
- Using the configuration of the atom rather than the ion when explaining successive values.
