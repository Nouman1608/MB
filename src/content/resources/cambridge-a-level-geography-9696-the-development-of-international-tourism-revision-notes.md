These notes condense section 13.3, The development of international tourism, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for 2025 and 2026 (Paper 4, Topic 13 option). For full explanations and a worked multiplier, use the [13.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-development-of-international-tourism/); then test yourself with the [13.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-development-of-international-tourism-practice/).

## The syllabus statements

1. Reasons for, and trends in, the growth of international tourism.
2. Impacts on the environments, societies and economies (local and national) of destinations.
3. Carrying capacity and the tourism multiplier effect.
4. Recent developments in types of tourism (including ecotourism).
5. Critically evaluate the life cycle model of tourism.

## Reasons for growth

| Demand (wanting and affording travel) | Supply (making travel easier) |
|---|---|
| Rising real incomes; growing middle class in MICs | Jet and wide-bodied aircraft |
| Paid holidays, more leisure time | Low-cost airlines; more routes |
| Long, healthy retirements | Package holidays; online booking |
| Media, social media, education | Easier visas; countries opening up |
| Family links from migration | Government marketing and infrastructure |

## Trends

- Long-term growth, broken by shocks (terrorism, financial crises, disease); sharpest fall in 2020 with COVID-19.
- Share of arrivals in Asia and the Pacific rising.
- Fast growth of outbound travel from MICs; China the largest source of tourism spending in the 2010s.
- More short breaks, long-haul trips, cruises, city breaks and independent travel.

## Impacts checklist

- **Economic:** foreign exchange, tax, jobs, investment, local business; but leakage, seasonality, low pay, dependence, rising land prices.
- **Social:** heritage funding, pride, services; but displacement, crime, crowding, loss of authenticity.
- **Environmental:** conservation funding, protected areas; but erosion, water shortage, waste, habitat loss, aviation emissions.
- **Scale:** national gains can hide local losses.

## Carrying capacity

- **Physical:** space and infrastructure.
- **Ecological:** what ecosystems can absorb.
- **Perceptual or social:** what residents and visitors will accept.
- The lowest limit sets the practical capacity.
- **Limits of acceptable change:** manage indicators against thresholds instead of a single visitor number.
- **Doxey's irritation index (1975):** euphoria → apathy → annoyance → antagonism.

## Multiplier

- Direct income → indirect (suppliers) → induced (wages spent).
- **Leakage:** imports, foreign profits, savings, taxes.
- Simple estimate: total local income ≈ initial spending ÷ leakage share.
- 10 million spent, 40% leakage → 25 million; multiplier 2.5.
- 80% leakage → 12.5 million; multiplier 1.25.
- **Enclave tourism** (all-inclusive resorts) keeps spending inside the resort, shrinking the multiplier.

## Recent types of tourism

| Type | Key feature | Main criticism |
|---|---|---|
| Ecotourism | Conserves nature, supports locals, educates | Greenwashing; long-haul emissions |
| Community-based | Locally owned and managed | Small scale; needs skills and access |
| Cruise | Many visitors, short stays | Crowding, low spending ashore |
| Medical | Treatment abroad | Draws doctors away from local patients |
| Dark | Sites of death or disaster | Ethical concerns |
| Volunteer | Travel plus volunteering | May serve visitors more than hosts |
| Adventure, wellness | Remote and specialist | Pressure on fragile areas |

**Overtourism:** visitor numbers above social or ecological capacity.

## Butler's model (1980)

**Stages:** exploration → involvement → development → consolidation → stagnation → rejuvenation, stabilisation or decline.

**Plog (1974):** allocentric travellers arrive early; psychocentric travellers arrive at the mass stage.

**Strengths:** simple; links growth to carrying capacity; warns planners; several possible outcomes.

**Weaknesses:** stages visible only in hindsight; stage lengths vary; several markets at different stages; external shocks; intervention changes the path; hard to define the area.

**Verdict:** good description and warning; poor predictor.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| International tourism | Travel to another country for leisure, business or other purposes, staying at least one night |
| Tourism receipts | Money spent by international visitors in a country |
| Leakage | Tourist spending that leaves the local or national economy |
| Enclave tourism | Tourism concentrated in self-contained resorts |
| Seasonality | Variation in visitor numbers through the year |
| Carrying capacity | Maximum use without unacceptable damage |
| Multiplier effect | Total income generated by an initial injection of spending |
| Ecotourism | Responsible nature-based tourism that conserves and benefits locals |
| Overtourism | Tourism exceeding social or ecological capacity |
| Allocentric | Adventurous travellers seeking new places |
| Psychocentric | Travellers who prefer familiar, safe places |
| Rejuvenation | Renewed growth after stagnation |

## Exam traps

- Separate demand and supply reasons.
- State local or national for each impact.
- Carrying capacity is not one fixed number.
- Multipliers must include leakage.
- "Critically evaluate" needs strengths, weaknesses and a verdict.
- Ecotourism is not automatically sustainable.

## Self-test

1. Give two demand and two supply reasons for growth.
2. Name two trends in international tourism.
3. Give one local economic and one national economic impact.
4. Name the three types of carrying capacity.
5. State the four stages of Doxey's index.
6. Tourists spend 4 million; leakage is 50%. Estimate total local income.
7. Why do all-inclusive resorts reduce the multiplier?
8. Define ecotourism.
9. Give one criticism of cruise tourism.
10. List Butler's stages up to stagnation.
11. Give two weaknesses of Butler's model.
12. What is an allocentric traveller?

### Answers

1. Demand: incomes, paid holidays, retirement, media. Supply: cheaper flights, low-cost airlines, online booking, easier visas.
2. Any two: long-term growth; shocks; shift towards Asia-Pacific; growth of MIC source markets; more short breaks or cruises.
3. Local: jobs or rising house prices. National: foreign exchange, tax or leakage.
4. Physical, ecological, perceptual (social).
5. Euphoria, apathy, annoyance, antagonism.
6. 4 ÷ 0.5 = 8 million.
7. Spending stays inside the resort, often foreign-owned with imported supplies.
8. Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, supports local people and involves education.
9. Large crowds for short stays with little spending ashore.
10. Exploration, involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation.
11. Any two: hindsight only; varied stage lengths; multiple markets; shocks; intervention; area definition.
12. An adventurous traveller who seeks new, less-visited places.
