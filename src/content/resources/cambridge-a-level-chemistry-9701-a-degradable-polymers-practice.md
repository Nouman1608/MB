## Section B

**5.** PET has the repeat unit –OCH₂CH₂OOCC₆H₄CO–.

**(a)** Write an equation for the hydrolysis of one repeat unit by NaOH(aq). **[2]**

**(b)** The alkaline mixture is then acidified. Name the organic compound that is formed from the
sodium salt. **[1]**

**6.** A 1.44 kg sample of poly(lactic acid), –[OCH(CH₃)CO]ₙ–, is hydrolysed completely by NaOH(aq).
Calculate the mass of NaOH used. **[3]**

**7.** Nylon-6,6 has the repeat unit –NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO–.

**(a)** Name the two organic products of its complete hydrolysis by hot HCl(aq). **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the acid hydrolysis of one repeat unit. **[2]**

**8.** A polyamide section is –NH(CH₂)₅CO–NH(CH₂)₅CO–.

**(a)** Give the structure of the organic product of hydrolysis by HCl(aq). **[1]**

**(b)** Give the structure of the organic product of hydrolysis by NaOH(aq). **[1]**

**9.** Alkaline hydrolysis of polymer **T** gives only propane-1,3-diol and sodium hexanedioate.

**(a)** State the type of polymer **T** is. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the repeat unit of **T**. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why poly(lactic acid) can be broken down by hydrolysis but poly(ethene) cannot. **[3]**

**11.** A shopping bag is advertised as "photodegradable, so it disappears completely". Give two
reasons why this claim is misleading. **[2]**

**12.** A section of a polypeptide is –NHCH₂CO–NHCH(CH₃)CO–NHCH(CH₂OH)CO–. It is heated under reflux
with HCl(aq) until hydrolysis is complete. Give the structures of the three organic products as they
exist in the acidic solution. **[3]**

**13.** PET waste can be hydrolysed back to the compounds it was made from, but poly(ethene) waste
cannot be turned back into ethene by any similar reaction with water.

**(a)** Explain why hydrolysis returns PET to its monomers. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why no similar reaction with water returns poly(ethene) to ethene. **[1]**

**14.** 113 g of nylon-6,6 (repeat unit C₁₂H₂₂N₂O₂, Mᵣ = 226.0) is hydrolysed completely by hot
HCl(aq).

**(a)** State the number of moles of HCl that react with the amine groups released. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of hexanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH, that could be obtained.
**[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any one: it has only strong C–C and C–H bonds; the bonds are non-polar so there is no site for
attack; there is no functional group in the chain [1].

**2.** Exposure to light (especially ultraviolet light) [1].

**3.** Hydrolysis [1].

**4.** Hydrogen chloride, HCl (accept carbon monoxide) [1].

**5. (a)** –OCH₂CH₂OOCC₆H₄CO– + 2NaOH → HOCH₂CH₂OH + NaOOCC₆H₄COONa: correct products [1]; 2NaOH
[1].

**(b)** Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid, HOOCC₆H₄COOH [1].

**6.** Mᵣ(repeat unit C₃H₄O₂) = 72.0, so n = 1440 / 72.0 = 20.0 mol [1]; one ester link per repeat
unit, so 20.0 mol NaOH [1]; mass = 20.0 × 40.0 = 800 g [1].

**7. (a)** Hexanedioic acid [1]; hexane-1,6-diammonium chloride (accept the hexane-1,6-diammonium
ion, ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺) [1].

**(b)** –NH(CH₂)₆NHCO(CH₂)₄CO– + 2H₂O + 2HCl → HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH + ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₆NH₃⁺ 2Cl⁻: correct products
[1]; balanced with 2H₂O and 2HCl [1].

**8. (a)** ⁺H₃N(CH₂)₅COOH [1].

**(b)** H₂N(CH₂)₅COO⁻ (accept the sodium salt) [1].

**9. (a)** A polyester [1].

**(b)** –O(CH₂)₃OOC(CH₂)₄CO–: one diol residue and one diacid residue joined by an ester link [1];
open bonds at both ends with no end groups [1].

**10.** Poly(lactic acid) contains ester links, each with a δ+ carbonyl carbon [1], which water can
attack in acid or alkali, breaking the C–O bond and splitting the chain into 2-hydroxypropanoic acid
(or its salt) [1]; poly(ethene) has only strong, non-polar C–C and C–H bonds and no functional group,
so it is inert to hydrolysis [1].

**11.** Any two [2]: it only degrades when exposed to light, so buried or covered waste barely breaks
down; it breaks into smaller fragments, which may still persist, rather than disappearing; the
fragments are not necessarily broken down further into small molecules.

**12.** ⁺H₃NCH₂COOH [1]; ⁺H₃NCH(CH₃)COOH [1]; ⁺H₃NCH(CH₂OH)COOH [1] (accept as chloride salts).

**13. (a)** Hydrolysis breaks each ester link, which is the reverse of the condensation that formed
it, so the diol and the dicarboxylic acid (or its salt) are regenerated [1].

**(b)** Poly(ethene) has no functional group in its chain: forming it opened C=C bonds into strong,
non-polar C–C bonds, and water cannot attack these to re-form the C=C [1].

**14. (a)** n(repeat units) = 113 / 226.0 = 0.500 mol, each giving two –NH₂ groups, so 1.00 mol HCl
[1].

**(b)** One hexanedioic acid per repeat unit, so 0.500 mol [1]; Mᵣ(C₆H₁₀O₄) = 146.0, so mass = 0.500 ×
146.0 = 73.0 g [1].

## Choosing the right form for each product

Most lost marks in this subtopic come from writing a correct monomer in the wrong form. Use the
conditions to decide:

| Group in the monomer | In acid hydrolysis | In alkaline hydrolysis |
|---|---|---|
| –COOH | –COOH | –COO⁻ (sodium salt) |
| –NH₂ | –NH₃⁺ (chloride salt) | –NH₂ |
| –OH (alcohol) | –OH | –OH |

Apply the table to every end of every product, including amino acids, which carry both groups.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one NaOH per repeat unit for PET or nylon-6,6, which each have two links per repeat unit.
- Giving the free acid in alkaline conditions or the free amine in acidic conditions.
- Explaining poly(alkene) inertness only as "they are saturated".
- Claiming photodegradable polymers break down in landfill or disappear completely.
- Forgetting the diol, which is released unchanged whatever the conditions.
- Drawing a repeat unit from hydrolysis products with the salt groups (–COO⁻Na⁺) still attached.
