## 1.2.1 Organelles by number of membranes

Sorting organelles by their membranes answers a surprising number of Paper 1 questions.

| Membranes | Structures |
|---|---|
| **Two** (an envelope) | Nucleus (nuclear envelope with pores), mitochondria, chloroplasts |
| **One** | Cell surface membrane, RER, SER, Golgi body, lysosomes, vesicles, tonoplast |
| **None** | Ribosomes, nucleolus, centrioles, microtubules, cell wall |

Cilia and microvilli are extensions of the cell, so they are **covered by** the cell surface membrane rather than having their own.

## Structure and function in one line each

| Structure | Key structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cell surface membrane | Phospholipid bilayer with proteins | Partially permeable; controls entry and exit |
| Nuclear envelope | Two membranes, nuclear pores; outer one joins RER | Separates DNA from cytoplasm; pores let substances through |
| Nucleolus | Dense region, no membrane | Makes rRNA; assembles ribosome subunits |
| RER | Flattened sacs with ribosomes | Transports proteins made on its ribosomes |
| SER | Tubules, no ribosomes | Makes lipids, including steroids |
| Golgi body | Curved stack of sacs plus vesicles | Modifies and packages proteins; makes lysosomes |
| Mitochondria | Double membrane, cristae, matrix, **circular DNA**, 70S ribosomes | Aerobic respiration; makes ATP |
| Ribosomes | Two subunits, RNA + protein | Protein synthesis |
| Lysosomes | Single membrane, hydrolytic enzymes | Digest worn organelles and engulfed material |
| Microtubules | Hollow tubes of tubulin | Cytoskeleton; move organelles; spindle |
| Centrioles | Pair of cylinders, 9 triplets of microtubules | Organise the spindle (animal cells) |
| Cilia | 9 + 2 microtubules, basal body | Beat to move fluid or mucus |
| Microvilli | Folds of cell surface membrane | Increase surface area for absorption |
| Chloroplasts | Double membrane, grana, stroma, **circular DNA**, 70S ribosomes | Photosynthesis |
| Cell wall | Cellulose; freely permeable | Shape; resists bursting |
| Plasmodesmata | Channels through walls, lined by membrane | Link cytoplasm of neighbouring cells |
| Vacuole + tonoplast | Cell sap inside a partially permeable membrane | Turgor; storage |

## Ribosome sizes

```
80S  -> cytoplasm (free or on RER) of eukaryotic cells
70S  -> mitochondria and chloroplasts; all of a bacterium's ribosomes
S    -> measures sedimentation rate: larger S = larger particle
```

## 1.2.2 Micrograph method in steps

1. **Identify the image type.** Colour or a stain and low detail means a light micrograph. Black and white with membranes visible means TEM. A 3-D surface means SEM.
2. **Look for a nucleus.** No nucleus and no double-membrane organelles means a prokaryote.
3. **Look for a wall, chloroplasts and a large vacuole.** Any of these means a plant cell.
4. **Count the membranes** around each body to name it (see the table above).
5. **Use the scale bar** if you must give a size. Measure the bar and the object in mm, then actual size = (object ÷ bar) × bar value.

Light microscope resolution is about 200 nm, so ribosomes, ER and Golgi detail need an electron micrograph.

## Small worked reminders

**Scale bar.** A bar labelled 5 µm measures 20 mm on a micrograph. A chloroplast in the same image measures 28 mm.

```
actual length = (28 / 20) x 5 um = 7 um
magnification = 20 mm / 5 um = 20 000 um / 5 um = x 4000
```

Magnification has no units. Give the size in µm, the unit that suits a cell or organelle.

**"Explain why this cell is a prokaryote." [2]** Write: there is no nucleus (no nuclear envelope), so the DNA lies free in the cytoplasm [1]; there are no organelles surrounded by a double membrane, such as mitochondria [1]. Do not write "it has no organelles": the ribosomes are visible.

**"Suggest why this cell has many mitochondria." [2]** Write: mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration, which makes ATP [1]; the cell carries out an energy-requiring process, such as protein synthesis or active transport, which uses that ATP [1]. Name a process that fits the cell in the question.

**Reading a secretory cell.** Lots of RER, a large Golgi body, many vesicles near the cell surface and many mitochondria together point to a cell that makes and releases protein. The whole route is set out in the [Cell structure study guide](/resources/a-level-biology-cell-structure/).

## 1.2.3 Plant versus animal: the short list

Only in plant cells: **cellulose cell wall, plasmodesmata, chloroplasts, large permanent vacuole with tonoplast**.
Only in animal cells: **centrioles**, and in some cells **cilia** and **microvilli**.
In both: nucleus, ER, Golgi body, **mitochondria**, ribosomes, lysosomes, microtubules, cell surface membrane.

## 1.2.4 ATP

State it plainly: cells use **ATP from respiration** for **energy-requiring processes**, such as protein synthesis, active transport, cell division and muscle contraction. More mitochondria means a higher energy demand.

## 1.2.5 and 1.2.6 Typical bacterium

| Feature | Bacterium | Eukaryotic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Cells per organism | **Unicellular** | Often many |
| Diameter | **Generally 1–5 µm** | Usually larger |
| Wall | **Peptidoglycan** | Cellulose (plant) or none (animal) |
| DNA | **Circular**, free in cytoplasm | Linear, inside a nucleus |
| Ribosomes | **70S** only | 80S in cytoplasm, 70S in mitochondria and chloroplasts |
| Double-membrane organelles | **Absent** | Nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts (plants) |

## 1.2.7 Viruses

**Non-cellular.** Nucleic acid core (either **DNA or RNA**) + protein **capsid**. Some have an outer **envelope** of **phospholipids**.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Cilia vs microvilli.** Cilia: long, 9 + 2 microtubules, beat. Microvilli: short folds, no microtubule core, absorb.
- **Centriole vs basal body.** Same structure; a centriole organises the spindle, a basal body anchors a cilium.
- **Nucleus vs nucleolus.** The nucleus has an envelope; the nucleolus is a region inside it with no membrane.
- **Tonoplast vs cell surface membrane.** Both partially permeable; the tonoplast surrounds the vacuole.
- **Capsid vs envelope.** Every virus has a protein capsid; only some have a phospholipid envelope.
- **70S in bacteria vs 70S in eukaryotes.** Same size; in eukaryotes they are only inside mitochondria and chloroplasts.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the two organelles in a plant cell that contain small circular DNA.
2. Which structure makes ribosomal RNA, and is it membrane-bound?
3. State two differences between cilia and microvilli.
4. Name the membrane around the large permanent vacuole.
5. A cell has a peptidoglycan wall and 70S ribosomes only. What kind of cell is it?
6. Name three structures surrounded by a double membrane in a plant cell.
7. State what cells use ATP from respiration for.
8. Give the three possible components of a virus and say which one only some viruses have.
9. A virus 100 nm across has an image 25 mm across. Calculate the magnification.
10. A mitochondrion 1.2 µm long is photographed at × 25 000. How long is its image in mm?
11. Why can't ribosomes be seen with a light microscope?
12. Name two structures that let you tell a TEM of a liver cell from one of a bacterium.

### Answers

1. Mitochondria and chloroplasts.
2. The nucleolus; no, it has no membrane.
3. Any two: cilia contain a 9 + 2 arrangement of microtubules, microvilli do not; cilia beat to move fluid, microvilli increase surface area for absorption; cilia are much longer.
4. The tonoplast.
5. A prokaryotic cell (a bacterium).
6. Nucleus (nuclear envelope), mitochondria, chloroplasts.
7. As the energy source for energy-requiring processes, for example protein synthesis or active transport.
8. Nucleic acid core (DNA or RNA), protein capsid, phospholipid envelope; only some have the envelope.
9. 25 mm = 25 000 000 nm; 25 000 000 ÷ 100 = **× 250 000**.
10. 1.2 µm × 25 000 = 30 000 µm = **30 mm**.
11. They are smaller than the resolution of a light microscope (about 200 nm), so they cannot be distinguished.
12. Any two: nucleus with nuclear envelope; mitochondria; RER or Golgi body (any double-membrane organelle or membrane system).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "mitochondria contain DNA" without "circular" when the question asks how it differs from nuclear DNA.
- Saying 70S ribosomes are found only in prokaryotes.
- Describing the nuclear envelope as a single membrane, or forgetting the pores.
- Calling the nucleolus an organelle with a membrane.
- Giving "cell membrane" when the syllabus term is cell surface membrane, or "vacuole membrane" when tonoplast is asked for.
- Listing plant features in a "compare" answer without saying what the animal cell has instead.
- Stating that a bacterium has "no organelles". It has no double-membrane organelles, but it has ribosomes.
- Saying viruses are made of cells, or have a cell wall.
- Confusing the basal body of a cilium with a microvillus.
- Forgetting unit conversion (mm to µm or nm) before using a scale bar or magnification.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where are 70S and 80S ribosomes found?**
80S ribosomes are in the cytoplasm of eukaryotic cells, free or on the rough ER. 70S ribosomes are in mitochondria and chloroplasts, and they are the only type in bacteria.

**What is the difference between cilia and microvilli?**
Cilia are long, have a 9 + 2 arrangement of microtubules and beat. Microvilli are short folds of the cell surface membrane with no microtubule core.

**Why can't ribosomes be seen with a light microscope?**
The resolution of a light microscope is about 200 nm, which is too coarse to show ribosomes, ER or Golgi detail. An electron micrograph is needed.

**Does a bacterium have organelles?**
It has no double-membrane organelles, such as a nucleus, mitochondria or chloroplasts, but it does have ribosomes. Writing that a bacterium has "no organelles" loses marks.

**What is the tonoplast?**
The tonoplast is the partially permeable membrane around the large permanent vacuole of a plant cell. Use this term rather than "vacuole membrane" when the name is asked for.
