These questions cover **E.5, Fusion and stars**, in the IB *Physics guide*, first assessment 2025, for Standard Level and Higher Level. E.5 has no additional higher level content, so every question is suitable for both. Data: σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W m⁻² K⁻⁴; L☉ = 3.83 × 10²⁶ W; R☉ = 6.96 × 10⁸ m; T☉ = 5800 K; c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; 1 u = 1.661 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, equivalent to 931.5 MeV c⁻²; 1 MeV = 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ J; 1 pc = 3.09 × 10¹⁶ m = 3.26 ly; k = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; kB = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹; Wien constant 2.9 × 10⁻³ m K.

## Questions

**1.** A star has a parallax angle of 0.0400 arc-second. Calculate its distance in parsecs, in light years and in metres. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why a main sequence star is stable, and why a small change in the core fusion rate is corrected. **[4]**

**3.** Two protons approach to a separation of 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ m.
**(a)** Calculate their electric potential energy in MeV. **[2]**
**(b)** The core of a star is at 2.0 × 10⁷ K. Calculate the mean thermal energy 3/2 kB T of a nucleus in keV. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answers to explain why fusion still occurs, and why high density is also needed. **[2]**

**4.** The table gives data for four stars.

| Star | Luminosity / L☉ | Surface temperature / K |
|---|---|---|
| A | 3.0 × 10⁻³ | 14 000 |
| B | 1.0 × 10⁵ | 3400 |
| C | 1.0 | 5800 |
| D | 1.0 × 10⁴ | 25 000 |

**(a)** State the region of the Hertzsprung-Russell (HR) diagram where each star lies. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the radius of star B in terms of R☉. **[3]**

**5.** Carbon-12 and helium-4 can fuse to form oxygen-16. The masses are carbon-12 12.000000 u, helium-4 4.002603 u and oxygen-16 15.994915 u.
**(a)** Calculate the energy released by one reaction, in MeV and in joules. **[3]**
**(b)** A star releases energy at 5.0 × 10²⁹ W by this reaction alone. Calculate the number of reactions per second. **[2]**

**6.** Compare the evolution of a star of one solar mass with that of a star of fifteen solar masses. **[6]**

**7.** The spectrum of star X has its peak at 1.45 × 10⁻⁷ m. Star Y peaks at 8.0 × 10⁻⁷ m.
**(a)** Calculate the surface temperature of X. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how dark lines in a stellar spectrum show the composition of the star. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the surface temperature of Y. **[1]**

**8.** A star has a parallax angle of 0.0310 arc-second and an apparent brightness of 2.6 × 10⁻⁹ W m⁻². Its surface temperature is 6200 K.
**(a)** Calculate its distance in parsecs and in metres. **[2]**
**(b)** Use b = L / 4πd² to calculate its luminosity. **[2]**
**(c)** Express the luminosity in terms of L☉. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the radius of the star in R☉. **[3]**
**(e)** Suggest why the star is not on the main sequence. **[1]**

**9.** A star keeps constant luminosity while its surface temperature falls from 6000 K to 3000 K.
**(a)** Determine the factor by which its radius changes. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the star moves on an HR diagram. **[2]**

**10.** A star has a luminosity of 1.0 × 10²⁹ W. Take 26.7 MeV as the energy released when four hydrogen atoms fuse into one helium atom. The mass of a hydrogen atom is 1.007825 u.
**(a)** Calculate the number of helium nuclei formed per second. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of hydrogen fused per second. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass converted into energy per second. **[1]**
**(d)** The star has 3.0 × 10²⁹ kg of hydrogen available in its core. Estimate how long it lasts, in years (1 year = 3.16 × 10⁷ s). **[2]**
**(e)** The Sun fuses hydrogen at about 6 × 10¹¹ kg per second. Suggest what this comparison says about massive stars. **[1]**

**11.** Describe the sketch of an HR diagram that you would draw, naming the features you would include. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** d = 1/p = 1/0.0400 = **25.0 pc** [1]. In light years: 25.0 × 3.26 = **81.5 ly** [1]. In metres: 25.0 × 3.09 × 10¹⁶ = **7.7 × 10¹⁷ m** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the one-line rearrangement and then each conversion separately.

**2.** Gravity acts inward on every layer [1]. Outward pressure from hot gas and radiation acts against it [1]. The star is stable when these are in balance at each depth [1]. If the fusion rate rose, the core would expand and cool, reducing the rate again; if it fell, the core would contract and heat, raising it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both pressure sources and say "balance". "Fusion pushes against gravity" is too loose.

**3. (a)** U = k e² / r = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.602 × 10⁻¹⁹)² / 2.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 1.15 × 10⁻¹³ J [1] = **0.72 MeV** [1].
**(b)** 3/2 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 2.0 × 10⁷ = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁶ J [1] = **2.6 keV** [1].
**(c)** The barrier is roughly 280 times the mean thermal energy, yet fusion occurs because a small fraction of nuclei move much faster than average, and quantum effects allow fusion without the full barrier energy [1]. High density makes collisions frequent, so the fusion rate is large enough to power the star [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert to the same unit before comparing, and state the ratio.

**4. (a)** A is a white dwarf (faint but hot); B is a red super giant (very luminous but cool); one mark for these two [1]. C and D are main sequence stars, D near the hot, luminous top left [1].
**(b)** R/R☉ = √(L/L☉) × (T☉/T)² [1] = √(1.0 × 10⁵) × (5800/3400)² = 316 × 2.91 [1] = **920 R☉** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Support a region with a reason from the data, such as "low L, high T so small".

**5. (a)** Δm = 12.000000 + 4.002603 − 15.994915 = 0.007688 u [1]. E = 0.007688 × 931.5 = **7.16 MeV** [1]. In joules: 7.16 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ = **1.15 × 10⁻¹² J** [1].
**(b)** Rate = P / E = 5.0 × 10²⁹ / 1.147 × 10⁻¹² (unrounded energy) [1] = **4.4 × 10⁴¹ s⁻¹** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded masses until after the subtraction.

**6.** One solar mass: lower core temperature and a slow fusion rate, so a lifetime of billions of years [1]. It leaves the main sequence and swells to a red giant [1]. It sheds its outer layers and the core remains as a white dwarf [1]. Fifteen solar masses: hotter core and far higher luminosity, so the fuel is used much faster and the lifetime is far shorter [1]. It becomes a super giant and fuses heavier elements up to iron [1]. The core collapses in a supernova, leaving a neutron star or a black hole [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both stars addressed on the same feature, such as lifetime or end state. 

**7. (a)** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / λmax [1] = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 1.45 × 10⁻⁷ = **2.0 × 10⁴ K** [1].
**(b)** Atoms in the cooler outer layers absorb photons whose energies match transitions between their energy levels [1]. The missing wavelengths appear as dark lines, and each element has its own pattern, so matching the pattern identifies the element [1].
**(c)** T = 2.9 × 10⁻³ / 8.0 × 10⁻⁷ = **3.6 × 10³ K** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the transition between energy levels in part (b). "Different elements give different lines" alone does not explain why.

**8. (a)** d = 1/0.0310 = **32.3 pc** [1] = 32.3 × 3.09 × 10¹⁶ = **9.97 × 10¹⁷ m** [1].
**(b)** L = 4πd²b = 4π × (9.97 × 10¹⁷)² × 2.6 × 10⁻⁹ [1] = **3.2 × 10²⁸ W** [1].
**(c)** 3.25 × 10²⁸ / 3.83 × 10²⁶ = **85 L☉** [1].
**(d)** R = √(L / 4πσT⁴) [1]; 4π × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × 6200⁴ = 1.053 × 10⁹ so R² = 3.09 × 10¹⁹ [1]; R = 5.6 × 10⁹ m = **8.0 R☉** [1].
**(e)** It is only slightly hotter than the Sun yet about 85 times as luminous, so it is much larger than a main sequence star of that temperature, and lies above the main sequence [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values between parts. Part (e) needs a comparison with a reference star, not just "it is big".

**9. (a)** At constant L, R² ∝ 1/T⁴ so R ∝ 1/T² [1]. Halving T makes R larger by a factor of **4** [1].
**(b)** The star moves horizontally to the right (to lower temperature at the same luminosity) [1], into the region of red giants and super giants [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the proportionality before substituting. Stating "R is 4 times larger" with no link to L and T is a bare answer.

**10. (a)** E = 26.7 × 1.602 × 10⁻¹³ = 4.28 × 10⁻¹² J [1]. Rate = 1.0 × 10²⁹ / 4.28 × 10⁻¹² = **2.3 × 10⁴⁰ s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** Each fusion uses 4 × 1.007825 × 1.661 × 10⁻²⁷ = 6.70 × 10⁻²⁷ kg [1]; mass = 2.34 × 10⁴⁰ × 6.70 × 10⁻²⁷ = **1.6 × 10¹⁴ kg s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** m = L / c² = 1.0 × 10²⁹ / (3.00 × 10⁸)² = **1.1 × 10¹² kg s⁻¹** [1].
**(d)** t = 3.0 × 10²⁹ / 1.56 × 10¹⁴ = 1.9 × 10¹⁵ s [1] = **6.1 × 10⁷ years** [1].
**(e)** Massive stars fuse hydrogen at a rate hundreds of times higher than the Sun, so they exhaust their fuel far sooner [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is smaller than part (b) because only a small fraction of the mass becomes energy; saying so shows the link.

**11.** Axes: luminosity on the vertical axis and surface temperature on the horizontal axis, with temperature increasing towards the left [1]. A diagonal main sequence band from top left to bottom right [1]. Red giants and super giants in the upper right, white dwarfs in the lower left [1]. The near-vertical instability strip through the giant region, plus straight lines of constant radius, with larger radii towards the upper right [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Label axes and regions on the sketch itself, so the temperature direction can be credited.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting hot stars on the right of the HR diagram.
- Using the parallax angle in degrees, or leaving it as p when d is asked.
- Using L in solar units alongside σ in SI units.
- Writing fusion "balances" gravity rather than outward pressure balancing gravity.
