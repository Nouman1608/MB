This guide covers sub-topic **8.1 Xylem and phloem** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 and 2 are Core, so every candidate needs them. Statement 3, relating the structure of xylem vessels to their function, is Supplement content, so it is **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4).

The [transport in plants study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-transport-in-plants/) gives the functions, a position table and one stem example. This page teaches you to read unfamiliar sections confidently: how to tell a root from a stem, how to locate the two tissues in each organ, how to compare them, and how to write full-mark structure answers. Condense it with the [xylem and phloem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-xylem-and-phloem-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [xylem and phloem practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-xylem-and-phloem-practice/).

## What 8.1 asks you to do

| Statement | Command word | Content | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8.1.1 | State | Functions: xylem transports water and mineral ions, and gives support; phloem transports sucrose and amino acids | Core |
| 8.1.2 | Identify | The position of xylem and phloem in sections of roots, stems and leaves of non-woody dicotyledonous plants | Core |
| 8.1.3 | Relate | Xylem vessel structure to function, limited to: thick walls with lignin; no cell contents; cells joined end to end with no cross walls to form a long continuous tube | Extended only |

"Non-woody dicotyledonous" means a plant such as a sunflower, bean or buttercup, with soft green stems rather than bark and wood. All the sections you will be shown come from plants like these.

## Functions (8.1.1)

### Xylem

- **Transports water and mineral ions** from the roots, up the stem, to the leaves and other parts above ground.
- **Gives support.** Xylem walls are thickened and strengthened, so the tissue helps to hold stems upright and keeps leaves spread out to the light.

Water and mineral ions travel together in xylem: mineral ions are dissolved in the water. Movement in xylem is one way, upwards from the roots.

### Phloem

- **Transports sucrose and amino acids** from the parts of the plant that make or release them to the parts that use or store them.
- Phloem can carry these substances **up or down**, depending on where they are needed. (Translocation, the Extended detail of this movement, is in sub-topic 8.4.)

### Side-by-side comparison

| | Xylem | Phloem |
|---|---|---|
| Substances carried | water and mineral ions | sucrose and amino acids |
| Direction | upwards only | up or down |
| Support | yes | not listed as a function |
| Position in a stem bundle | inner side | outer side |
| Position in a leaf vein | upper side | lower side |

A good "compare" answer makes each point about both tissues in the same sentence: "xylem carries water and mineral ions, whereas phloem carries sucrose and amino acids".

## Finding xylem and phloem (8.1.2)

### Step 1: decide which organ you are looking at

| Clue | Root | Stem | Leaf |
|---|---|---|---|
| Overall shape of section | round | round (sometimes ridged) | thin and flat, often with a thicker midrib |
| Arrangement of transport tissue | one central core | separate bundles in a ring near the edge | bundles in the midrib and veins |
| Other clue | root hairs may show on the outside | a large central region of packing cells | palisade and spongy mesophyll present |

### Step 2: locate the tissues

- **Root:** xylem forms a **central star or cross shape**; phloem is in small patches **between the arms** of the xylem.
- **Stem:** each vascular bundle has **phloem towards the outside** of the stem and **xylem towards the centre**.
- **Leaf:** in each vein or the midrib, **xylem is on the upper side** of the bundle and **phloem on the lower side**.

### Step 3: check with cell appearance

Xylem vessels are usually the **widest cells** in a bundle, with **thick walls** and **empty-looking centres**. Phloem cells are smaller with thinner walls. If position and appearance agree, your answer is safe.

### Worked example 1: three sections

**Sections A, B and C are described below. Identify each organ and the tissue labelled X.**

- **A:** round; one central mass of tissue with four arms; X is a small group of cells between two arms. → **Root**; X is **phloem**.
- **B:** round; nine separate bundles in a ring; X is the group of wide, thick-walled cells on the side of a bundle nearer the centre. → **Stem**; X is **xylem**.
- **C:** flat and thin, with a thickened middle region; X is the group of small cells on the lower side of the central bundle. → **Leaf** (midrib); X is **phloem**.

### Worked example 2: using a stain

A young bean plant is left for several hours with its roots in water containing a dye that only moves through xylem. Sections are then cut. **Predict where the dye appears in the root, stem and leaf.**

- Root: in the **centre**, in the star-shaped xylem.
- Stem: in the **inner side of each bundle**, as a ring of coloured spots.
- Leaf: in the **upper side of each vein**, so the veins look coloured.

This is the same idea as the staining investigation in [water uptake](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-water-uptake/): the dye marks where water travels, which shows where the xylem is.

## Structure of xylem vessels (Extended only)

The syllabus limits you to three features. Each one is worth a mark only when you link it to how it helps the function.

| Feature | How it helps |
|---|---|
| **Thick walls with lignin** | lignin strengthens the walls, so vessels do not collapse as water is pulled up, and they help support the plant |
| **No cell contents** | there is no cytoplasm or nucleus to obstruct the flow, so water and mineral ions move through freely |
| **Cells joined end to end with no cross walls** | they form a **long continuous tube** from root to leaf, so water can move up as an unbroken column |

Details of how lignin is laid down are not required.

### Annotated model answer

**Explain how the structure of xylem vessels is related to their function.** **[3]**

> Xylem vessels have thick walls containing lignin, which make them strong so they do not collapse and so they support the plant [1]. They have no cell contents, so nothing blocks the movement of water and mineral ions [1]. The cells are joined end to end with no cross walls, forming a long continuous tube from the roots to the leaves [1].

Why it scores: three features, each followed by "so" or "which" and its effect on transport or support. Writing "they are hollow" without saying why it matters would earn nothing.

The continuous column links forward to the transpiration pull in [transpiration](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-transpiration/): a tube with no cross walls lets the column of water be drawn up without breaks.

## Common mistakes

- Writing that phloem carries "glucose" or "food". It carries **sucrose** and **amino acids**.
- Giving xylem only one function. Support is the second function.
- Placing xylem on the outside of a stem bundle, or on the lower side of a leaf vein.
- Identifying a root section as a stem because it is round. Look for one central core versus a ring of bundles.
- Writing "xylem transports water" for a two-mark answer; mineral ions earn the second mark.
- (Extended) Saying lignin "makes the vessel waterproof" as the only point. The syllabus link is strength and support.
- (Extended) Describing "dead cells" without saying what follows: no cell contents, so no obstruction.

## Exam technique for 8.1

- **State** functions: two for xylem (transport of water and mineral ions; support) and one for phloem (sucrose and amino acids).
- **Identify** in a diagram: decide the organ first, then the position, then check appearance.
- **Relate** structure to function (Extended): feature + "so" + effect, three times.
- **Compare**: one sentence per point covering both tissues.
