These notes condense subtopic **19.1 Energy flow** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the Sun and photosynthesis, food chains and webs, trophic levels, the inefficiency of energy transfer, the efficiency of eating plants and the three kinds of pyramid. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [19.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-energy-flow/). Then try the [19.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-energy-flow-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Energy source | The Sun is the principal source of energy for most biological systems |
| Dependence | Most forms of life depend on photosynthesis |
| Energy in a food chain | Light, then chemical energy in organisms, then heat to the environment |
| Typical transfer | About 10 per cent of energy passes to the next trophic level |

## Terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Producer | Makes organic nutrients by photosynthesis |
| Consumer | Feeds on other organisms |
| Herbivore | Eats plants |
| Carnivore | Eats animals |
| Decomposer | Feeds on dead and waste matter (bacteria and fungi) |
| Food chain | A line showing the transfer of energy; arrows point to the eater |
| Food web | A network of interconnected food chains |
| Trophic level | A feeding level: producer, primary consumer, secondary consumer and so on |

## Why energy transfer is inefficient

1. Energy is lost as heat in respiration.
2. Some parts are not eaten (bones, roots, shells).
3. Some food is not digested and is lost in faeces.
4. Energy is lost in excretion.
5. Some organisms die and pass to decomposers.

**Result:** food chains usually have fewer than five trophic levels, because there is too little energy left to support another level.

## Eating plants or livestock

| Route | Trophic levels | Energy available to humans |
|---|---|---|
| Humans eat crop plants | Producer, human | Much higher |
| Humans eat livestock fed on crops | Producer, livestock, human | About 10 per cent of the energy in the crop |

Eating crops directly is more energy efficient, since one stage with large losses is removed. The same land can feed more people.

## Pyramids

| Pyramid | Shows | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | Number of organisms at each level | Usually narrows upwards but can be inverted (one tree, many insects) |
| Biomass | Dry mass at each level | Usually narrows upwards |
| Energy | Energy per area per time at each level | Always narrows upwards |

Construct them with the producers at the bottom and bars to scale.

## Worked calculations

**Energy efficiency.** A herbivore takes in 5 000 kJ and stores 450 kJ. Percentage stored = 450 divided by 5 000 x 100 = 9 per cent. The energy lost is 5 000 - 450 = 4 550 kJ.

**Chain of transfers.** If 20 000 kJ is stored by plants, and 10 per cent passes at each stage: herbivores 2 000 kJ; carnivores 200 kJ; top carnivores 20 kJ.

**Land use.** A field gives 30 000 kJ of crop. If fed to cattle at 10 per cent efficiency, 3 000 kJ is available from the beef. Ratio of crop to beef energy = 10 : 1.

## Reading food webs

- Follow the arrows to see who eats whom.
- Producers have arrows leaving but none arriving from other organisms.
- If one population falls, predators of it may fall and its food may rise.
- Remove one species and describe the likely knock-on effects.

## Short model answers

**Explain why most life depends on photosynthesis.** Photosynthesis transfers light energy into chemical energy in carbohydrates, which are the source of food for all other organisms. It also releases oxygen.

**Explain why energy transfer is inefficient.** Much energy is lost as heat in respiration, as uneaten parts, in faeces and in excretion, so only about 10 per cent passes on.

**Explain why food chains are short.** Energy is lost at each stage, so there is too little energy to support more than four or five trophic levels.

**Explain why it is more efficient for humans to eat crops.** There are fewer stages, so less energy is lost as heat and waste before it reaches humans.

**State what the arrows in a food chain represent.** The transfer of energy from one organism to the next.

## Exam traps

- Arrows point **from food to feeder**.
- Energy is lost as **heat**; it is not "used up".
- Energy **flows**; nutrients are **recycled**.
- Short chains are due to **energy loss**, not hunting or space.
- Pyramids of numbers can be **inverted**; pyramids of energy cannot.
- Decomposers are not shown as a trophic level in a simple chain but are important.
- Percentage efficiency uses the energy **available** as the divisor.
- Humans eating plants are **primary** consumers.

## Self-test

1. What is the principal source of energy for most ecosystems?
2. Why do most forms of life depend on photosynthesis?
3. Define a producer.
4. What is a decomposer?
5. What do the arrows in a food chain show?
6. What is a food web?
7. Roughly what percentage of energy passes to the next level?
8. Give two reasons why energy is lost between trophic levels.
9. Why do food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels?
10. Why is it more efficient for humans to eat crops?
11. Which type of pyramid is always pyramid-shaped?
12. Calculate the efficiency if a herbivore takes in 800 kJ and stores 80 kJ.

### Answers

1. The Sun.
2. Photosynthesis makes the carbohydrates that are food for all other organisms.
3. An organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually by photosynthesis.
4. An organism that feeds on dead or waste organic matter (bacteria and fungi).
5. The transfer of energy from one organism to the next.
6. A network of interconnected food chains.
7. About 10 per cent.
8. Any two of: heat from respiration; uneaten parts; faeces; excretion.
9. Energy is lost at each stage, so too little remains to support more levels.
10. There are fewer stages, so less energy is lost.
11. The pyramid of energy.
12. 80 divided by 800 x 100 = 10 per cent.
