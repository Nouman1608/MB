Condensed recall for **13.2 Environmental risks** (Unit 6: Global risks and resilience). For the
full explanation and the scoring heuristic, read the
[Global risks and resilience study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-global-risks-resilience-study-guide/).

## Environmental risks

Risks arising from natural processes, human impact on the environment, or the interaction of the two.

| Risk | Key points |
|---|---|
| Climate change | Rising temperatures, changing rainfall, sea-level rise, more intense extremes; consequences for water, food, health and settlement |
| Biodiversity loss and ecosystem decline | Habitat loss, overexploitation and pollution weaken ecosystem services |
| Pollution and resource pressure | Air, water and plastic pollution, soil degradation, water scarcity |
| Disease and pandemics | Spread along travel and trade networks; impact depends on health systems and living conditions |
| Natural hazards | Earthquakes, storms, floods and droughts become disasters when they meet vulnerable populations |

## Models of risk

- **Disaster risk** depends on hazard, exposure and vulnerability, **reduced by capacity to cope**.
- **Pressure and release model** (Blaikie and colleagues): vulnerability results from **root causes**,
  such as poverty and political structures, that become **dynamic pressures** and **unsafe
  conditions**.

## Scoring heuristic (teaching tool, not an official formula)

Risk score = (hazard x vulnerability) / capacity to cope, each scored 1 to 10, with invented regions:

- Region X: (6 x 8) / 4 = 12. Region Y: (8 x 4) / 8 = 4.
- Raising X's capacity from 4 to 8 gives 48 / 8 = 6, halving its risk with no change in hazard.

Evaluate the method: scores are subjective, factors interact and one number hides variation within
a region.

## Transboundary nature

Environmental risks often cross borders, so one country's actions can create risks for another. This
links the subtopic to global governance.

## Exam traps

- Calling any natural hazard a disaster; a disaster needs vulnerable people.
- Quoting the scoring heuristic as an official formula.
- Naming a risk without explaining who is most exposed and why.

## Self-test

1. Name three environmental risks.
2. State the factors in disaster risk.
3. What are the stages of the pressure and release model?
4. Why is the scoring heuristic only a teaching tool?
5. Why are environmental risks often transboundary?

**Answers:** 1. For example climate change, biodiversity loss and pandemics. 2. Hazard, exposure and
vulnerability, reduced by capacity to cope. 3. Root causes, dynamic pressures and unsafe conditions.
4. Scores are subjective and a single number hides variation. 5. Their effects cross borders, so
one country's actions affect others.
