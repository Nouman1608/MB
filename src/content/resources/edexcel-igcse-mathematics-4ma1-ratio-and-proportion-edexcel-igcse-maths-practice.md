This set covers **section 1.7, Ratio and proportion**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. It is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes, so every question is for both tiers. Show your working.

Related: [section 1.7 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-ratio-and-proportion-edexcel-igcse-maths/) and [section 1.7 revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-ratio-and-proportion-edexcel-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Write the ratio 3 kg : 600 g in its simplest form. **[2]**

**2.** Write the ratio 4 : 26 in the form 1 : n. **[1]**

**3.** Write the ratio 0.45 : 1.2 in its simplest form. **[2]**

**4.** Share 132 in the ratio 5 : 6. **[2]**

**5.** At a show, the ratio of adults to children is 3 : 8. There are 154 people at the show. How many are children? **[2]**

**6.** 12 eggs cost £3.18. Work out the cost of 30 of the same eggs. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Three friends share £540 in the ratio 4 : 5 : 9.

**(a)** Work out how much each friend receives. **[2]**

**(b)** What fraction of the money does the second friend receive? **[1]**

**8.** In a school orchestra, a : b = 2 : 5 and b : c = 3 : 4, where a, b and c are the numbers of brass, string and woodwind players.

**(a)** Find a : b : c in its simplest form. **[2]**

**(b)** There are 205 players in total. How many woodwind players are there? **[2]**

**9.** A bag contains red and blue beads in the ratio 2 : 3. Eight red beads are added, and the ratio of red to blue becomes 6 : 5. How many beads of each colour were in the bag at the start? **[3]**

**10.** The distance d km travelled by a train is directly proportional to the time t hours. When t = 1.5, d = 24.

**(a)** Find d when t = 4. **[2]**

**(b)** Find t when d = 56. **[1]**

**11.** A map has a scale of 1 : 125 000.

**(a)** Two lakes are 7.2 cm apart on the map. Work out the real distance in kilometres. **[2]**

**(b)** A road is 15 km long. Work out its length on the map in centimetres. **[2]**

**12.** A recipe for 8 biscuits uses 120 g of flour, 75 g of butter and 45 g of sugar. Leon has 500 g of flour, 300 g of butter and 200 g of sugar. Work out the greatest number of biscuits he can make. Show your working. **[4]**

**13.** The width and length of a rectangle are in the ratio 2 : 5. Its perimeter is 63 cm. Work out the area of the rectangle. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** 3000 : 600 [1] = **5 : 1** [1]

**2.** **1 : 6.5** [1]

**3.** 45 : 120 [1] = **3 : 8** [1]

**4.** 11 parts, 1 part = 12 [1]; **60 and 72** [1]

**5.** 11 parts, 1 part = 14 [1]; 8 × 14 = **112 children** [1]

**6.** 1 egg = 3.18 ÷ 12 = £0.265 [1]; 30 × 0.265 = **£7.95** [1]

**7. (a)** 18 parts, 1 part = £30 [1]; **£120, £150 and £270** [1]

**(b)** **5/18** [1]

**8. (a)** a : b = 6 : 15 and b : c = 15 : 20 [1]; **6 : 15 : 20** [1]

**(b)** 41 parts, so 1 part = 205 ÷ 41 = 5 [1]; woodwind = 20 × 5 = **100** [1]

**9.** Red = 2k, blue = 3k; (2k + 8) : 3k = 6 : 5, so 5(2k + 8) = 6 × 3k [1]; 10k + 40 = 18k, so k = 5 [1]; **10 red and 15 blue** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Check the answer in the new ratio: 18 : 15 simplifies to 6 : 5. A trial-and-improvement route that reaches the same answer with a check can also earn full marks.

**10. (a)** d ÷ t = 24 ÷ 1.5 = 16 [1]; d = 16 × 4 = **64** [1]

**(b)** t = 56 ÷ 16 = **3.5** [1]

**11. (a)** 7.2 × 125 000 = 900 000 cm [1] = **9 km** [1]

**(b)** 15 km = 1 500 000 cm [1]; 1 500 000 ÷ 125 000 = **12 cm** [1]

**12.** From flour: 500 ÷ 120 = 4.17 recipes, so 33.3 biscuits [1]; from butter: 300 ÷ 75 = 4 recipes, so 32 biscuits [1]; from sugar: 200 ÷ 45 = 4.44 recipes, so 35.6 biscuits [1]; butter runs out first, so the greatest number is **32 biscuits** [1]

**13.** Width + length = 63 ÷ 2 = 31.5 cm [1]; 7 parts = 31.5, so 1 part = 4.5 cm [1]; width = 9 cm and length = 22.5 cm [1]; area = 9 × 22.5 = **202.5 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The ratio applies to one width and one length, not to the whole perimeter; halving the perimeter first avoids sharing 63 in the ratio 2 : 5 by mistake.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 1 : 200 in question 1 by not converting kg to g.
- Dividing 154 by 8 in question 5 instead of by 11 parts.
- Scaling only one ratio in question 8(a), giving 2 : 5 : 4.
- Adding 8 to both colours in question 9.
- Converting cm to km by dividing by 1000 in question 11.
- Choosing the flour as the limit in question 12 without checking every ingredient.
