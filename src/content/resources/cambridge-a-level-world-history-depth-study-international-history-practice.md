The International option of Cambridge 9489 Paper 4 requires two 30-mark essays in 1 hour 45 minutes, using the command words **assess** and **evaluate**. The twelve questions below follow the option's twelve key questions, one each, with indicative content showing the knowledge and arguments examiners credit.

## How Paper 4 essays are marked

Published 9489 Paper 4 mark schemes award up to 15 marks for AO1 and 15 for AO2.

| Level | AO2: analysis and judgement (15) | AO1: knowledge (15) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (13-15) | Valid, wide-ranging criteria; consistently analytical; balanced; clear, sustained judgement | Carefully selected, wide-ranging, precise |
| 4 (10-12) | Valid criteria; analytical but uneven; supported judgement | Appropriate; range uneven in depth |
| 3 (7-9) | Attempted criteria; some description; lacks balance | Mostly relevant; narrow; descriptive in places |
| 2 (4-6) | Implicit criteria; descriptive; assertion | Narrative, not linked to the argument |
| 1 (1-3) | Addresses topic, not question | Limited or inaccurate |

## Topic 7: The Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe, 1953-91

**1.** "Military force was the main reason Soviet control over the satellite states survived until 1968." Assess this view. **[30]** *(7.1)*

*Indicative content.* Force: suppression of East Germany (1953) and Hungary (1956); the Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia (1968) and the Brezhnev Doctrine. Other factors: communist parties' political monopoly; censorship, secret police such as the Stasi, controls on the Church and on emigration, culminating in the Berlin Wall (1961); limited concessions, such as Gomułka's settlement in Poland (1956); rising living standards in some states. Judgement: force was the guarantee of last resort; everyday control rested on party and police structures.

**2.** Evaluate the reasons for political and economic stagnation in the Soviet Union, 1964-85. **[30]** *(7.2)*

*Indicative content.* Brezhnev's preference for stability; the nomenklatura and corruption; gerontocracy -- Andropov and Chernenko died within three years of each other; failure of reforms and the 10th Five Year Plan; defence spending and the arms race; Afghanistan (from 1979) and the Second Cold War. Judgement: link the political system (no incentive to reform) to the economic symptoms.

**3.** Assess the impact of Gorbachev's "new thinking" on the Soviet Union, 1985-91. **[30]** *(7.3)*

*Indicative content.* Glasnost exposed failures, including Chernobyl (1986), and released nationalist demands; perestroika and the 12th Five Year Plan disrupted planning without creating a market, producing shortages and fragmentation; demokratizatsiya weakened the party's monopoly; foreign policy transformed relations with the USA through the Geneva, Reykjavik and Washington summits. Judgement: successful abroad, destabilising at home.

**4.** "The collapse of Soviet control in Eastern Europe was caused more by opposition within the satellite states than by events in the Soviet Union." Assess this view. **[30]** *(7.4)*

*Indicative content.* Opposition: Solidarity from August 1980 -- the 21 Demands, survival under martial law (1981) and re-legalisation (1989); nationalist movements in Hungary, Czechoslovakia and Romania; the fall of the Berlin Wall (November 1989). Soviet events: the Sinatra Doctrine; economic crisis; dissent in Kazakhstan and the Baltic states; the August coup (1991) and Gorbachev's resignation. Judgement: the satellites' opposition was long-standing, but it succeeded when Moscow stopped intervening.

## Topic 8: End of minority rule in South Africa and Zimbabwe, 1948-94

**5.** "Afrikaner nationalism was the main reason for the National Party's victory in 1948." Assess this view. **[30]** *(8.1)*

*Indicative content.* Afrikaner nationalism and fear of urbanisation of Black workers; the impact of the Second World War (wartime divisions, Smuts's focus on international affairs); the United Party's weak campaign; the electoral system's rural weighting, which gave the National Party and its ally more seats with fewer votes. Judgement: nationalism supplied the motive; the electoral system turned a minority of votes into a majority of seats.

**6.** Evaluate the effectiveness of resistance to apartheid inside South Africa, 1960-77. **[30]** *(8.2)*

*Indicative content.* PAC anti-pass campaign; Sharpeville and Langa (1960); Umkhonto we Sizwe's sabotage; the Rivonia Trial (1963-64) removing leaders; Biko, SASO and the Black Consciousness Movement; the Soweto uprising (1976). Government response: state of emergency, banning of ANC and PAC, police and defence force powers under Vorster, Biko's death (1977). Judgement: resistance kept apartheid under pressure and gained international attention but could not defeat the state before 1977.

**7.** "International pressure was the most important reason for the end of apartheid." Assess this view. **[30]** *(8.3)*

*Indicative content.* International: sanctions and disinvestment; the Anti-Apartheid Movement; the end of the Cold War reducing the "communist threat" argument; newly independent neighbouring states. Internal: township unrest, school boycotts, the UDF; economic difficulties; Botha's failure to make reform and repression work; de Klerk's reforms from 1990; the leadership of Mandela; negotiations despite the Third Force, white extremists and Inkatha. Judgement: international pressure raised the costs; internal resistance and leadership determined timing and outcome.

**8.** Assess the reasons why white minority rule ended in Zimbabwe in 1980. **[30]** *(8.4)*

*Indicative content.* The guerrilla war of ZANLA and ZIPRA, increasingly costly for Rhodesia by the late 1970s; loss of South African and Portuguese support after Mozambique's independence (1975); sanctions; the failure of the Internal Settlement to win recognition; British diplomacy at Lancaster House (1979); the 1980 elections. Judgement: military pressure forced Smith to negotiate; diplomacy shaped the settlement.

## Topic 9: The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909-67

**9.** "Nationalism in Malaya before 1945 was weak and divided." Assess this view. **[30]** *(9.1)*

*Indicative content.* Weak: divide and rule and immigration separated Malays, Chinese and Indians; nationalism was mostly cultural and religious (Kaum Muda) or elite (English-educated Malays, associations and clubs); Clementi's policies failed to build a Malayan identity. Growing: KMM's radical aims; the impact of Japanese occupation in encouraging Malay nationalism (KMM, PETA, KRIS); the communist MPAJA's ambitions. Judgement: weak and divided before 1941; the occupation sharpened it.

**10.** Evaluate the reasons why the Federation of Malaysia was formed in 1963. **[30]** *(9.2)*

*Indicative content.* The failure of the Malayan Union and the role of UMNO; British victory in the Emergency through the Briggs Plan and Templer; the Alliance's electoral success (1955) and Merdeka (1957); Tunku Abdul Rahman's support; political, economic and security reasons for merging Singapore, and the differences that led to separation in 1965. Judgement: British interest in a stable, anti-communist federation coincided with Malay elite leadership.

**11.** "The Japanese occupation was the most important factor in the growth of Indonesian nationalism." Assess this view. **[30]** *(9.3)*

*Indicative content.* Pre-war foundations: the Ethical Policy and Western education created a nationalist elite; the Volksraad; PKI, PNI and Sukarno; Partindo, Gerindo and Hatta's group; the Soetardjo Petition and GAPI. The occupation: anti-Western propaganda, the Triple A movement, Putera and PETA gave nationalists organisation and arms; promises of self-government; underground resistance. Judgement: the Dutch had suppressed a well-developed movement; Japan gave it the means to act in 1945.

**12.** Assess the main challenges facing the Republic of Indonesia from independence to the mid-1960s. **[30]** *(9.4)*

*Indicative content.* Securing independence against the Dutch -- re-occupation, the failure of the Linggadjati Agreement, UN and US involvement; constitutional debates and unstable governments in the 1950s; Sukarno's leadership and Guided Democracy; economic problems and social tensions; Konfrontasi with Malaysia (1963); the 30 September Movement, the purge of communists, KAMI and Supersemar leading to the New Order. Judgement: political instability underlay the economic and military challenges.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I answer two questions from different topics?**
Yes, as long as both are from the International option section of the paper.

**How detailed should dates be?**
Precise dates strengthen AO1, but accuracy of argument matters more than a list of dates.

**What is the best way to practise?**
Plan each of the twelve questions in five minutes, then write two in full under timed conditions.
