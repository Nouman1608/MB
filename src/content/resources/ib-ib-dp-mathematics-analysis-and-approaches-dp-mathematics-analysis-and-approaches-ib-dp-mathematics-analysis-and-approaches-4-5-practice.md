## Section A

**1.** A fair spinner numbered 1 to 8 is spun once. Using this experiment, give an example of

**(a)** a trial; **[1]**
**(b)** an outcome; **[1]**
**(c)** an event, written as a set. **[1]**

**2.** A letter is chosen at random from the word STATISTICS. Find the probability that it is

**(a)** a T; **[1]**
**(b)** a vowel; **[1]**
**(c)** not an S. **[1]**

**3.** A card is chosen at random from cards numbered 1 to 20. Find the probability that the number is

**(a)** a multiple of 3; **[1]**
**(b)** prime. **[1]**

**4.** The probability that it rains on a given day in April is 0.35.

**(a)** Find the probability that it does not rain on a given day. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the expected number of rainy days in the 30 days of April. **[1]**

**5.** A die is thrown 250 times and shows a six 47 times.

**(a)** Estimate the probability of a six with this die. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the expected number of sixes in 600 further throws. **[1]**

**6.** U = {1, 2, 3, ..., 12} and A is the set of multiples of 4 in U. Find n(A′) and P(A′). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled.

**(a)** Find the probability that the total is 7. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that the total is at least 10. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the probability that the two scores differ by exactly 2. **[2]**
**(d)** The two dice are rolled 90 times. Find the expected number of times that both show the same score.
**[2]**

**8.** A family has three children. Assume that each child is equally likely to be a boy or a girl,
independently.

**(a)** List the sample space. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly two are girls. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that at least one is a boy. **[2]**
**(d)** A student says "the number of girls is 0, 1, 2 or 3, so P(exactly two girls) = 1/4". Explain the
error. **[1]**

**9.** A four-sided spinner numbered 1 to 4 is spun 80 times with these results.

| Score | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 14 | 26 | 19 | 21 |

**(a)** Write down the relative frequency of a score of 2. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the expected frequency of each score if the spinner were fair. **[1]**
**(c)** Comment on whether these results show that the spinner is biased. **[2]**
**(d)** Using the relative frequency, estimate the number of 2s in 500 spins. **[1]**

**10.** A fair coin is tossed and a fair six-sided die is rolled.

**(a)** Write down the number of outcomes in the sample space. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability of a head and an even number. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability of a tail or a six (or both). **[2]**

**11.** A bag contains red, blue and green counters only. A counter is chosen at random. P(red) = 0.4 and
P(blue) = 0.25.

**(a)** Find P(green). **[1]**
**(b)** The bag contains 14 green counters. Find the total number of counters. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Spinning the spinner once [1].
**(b)** For example, landing on 5 [1].
**(c)** For example, "an odd number" = {1, 3, 5, 7} [1]. **[3]**

**2.** STATISTICS has 10 letters: S three times, T three times, I twice, A and C once.
**(a)** 3/10 [1].
**(b)** Vowels A, I, I: 3/10 [1].
**(c)** 1 − 3/10 = 7/10 [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** {3, 6, 9, 12, 15, 18}: 6/20 = 3/10 [1].
**(b)** {2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, 17, 19}: 8/20 = 2/5 [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 1 − 0.35 = 0.65 [1].
**(b)** 30 × 0.35 = 10.5 days [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 47/250 = 0.188 [1].
**(b)** 600 × 0.188 = 112.8 [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A = {4, 8, 12}, so n(A′) = 12 − 3 = 9 [1] and P(A′) = 9/12 = 3/4 [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** n(U) = 36 [1]; (1, 6), (2, 5), (3, 4), (4, 3), (5, 2), (6, 1) give 6/36 = 1/6 [1].
**(b)** Totals 10, 11, 12: 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 outcomes [1], so 6/36 = 1/6 [1].
**(c)** (1, 3), (3, 1), (2, 4), (4, 2), (3, 5), (5, 3), (4, 6), (6, 4): 8 outcomes [1], so 8/36 = 2/9 [1].
**(d)** P(double) = 6/36 = 1/6 [1]; 90 × 1/6 = 15 [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** GGG, GGB, GBG, BGG, GBB, BGB, BBG, BBB [1].
**(b)** 3/8 [1].
**(c)** 1 − P(GGG) [1] = 1 − 1/8 = 7/8 [1].
**(d)** The four values are not equally likely: two girls can occur in three ways, three girls in only one
[1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** 26/80 = 0.325 [1].
**(b)** 80 × 1/4 = 20 each [1].
**(c)** The frequencies differ from 20, with 2 appearing most often [1], but 80 spins is a small number of
trials and some variation is expected, so the results do not show clear bias; more spins are needed [1].
**(d)** 500 × 0.325 = 162.5, about 163 [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** 2 × 6 = 12 [1].
**(b)** H2, H4, H6: 3/12 = 1/4 [1].
**(c)** Tails with any score: 6 outcomes; plus H6: 1 outcome [1]; 7/12 [1]. **[4]**
*Marking note:* adding 6/12 and 2/12 counts T6 twice and gives the wrong answer 8/12.

**11. (a)** 1 − 0.4 − 0.25 = 0.35 [1].
**(b)** 0.35 × total = 14 [1], so total = 14/0.35 = 40 counters [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting totals or numbers of girls as if they were equally likely (questions 7 and 8).
- Counting an outcome twice in "or" questions (question 10(c)).
- Treating relative frequency from a short experiment as proof of bias (question 9).
- Rounding expected numbers that should be left exact (questions 4 and 5).
