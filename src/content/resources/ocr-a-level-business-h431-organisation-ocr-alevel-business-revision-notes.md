Condensed notes on the Organisation content (5.2 on this site) in the area of study Human resource management within a business environment, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and a worked delayering example are in the [organisation study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-organisation-ocr-alevel-business/). Test yourself with the [organisation practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-organisation-ocr-alevel-business-practice/). All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- Organisation structure: line and staff relationships, chain of command, span of control, authority, responsibility, centralisation, decentralisation, delegation, delayering.
- Models: hierarchy; by function, by product; system, matrix, division.
- Tall against flat; usefulness of structures; significance to a business and its stakeholders.
- Organisational culture: meaning, factors, impact and importance.

## Core recall

### Structure terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line relationship | Direct reporting link from manager to staff |
| Staff relationship | Advice or support from a specialist with no direct authority |
| Chain of command | Route of authority from top to bottom |
| Span of control | Number of people a manager directly supervises |
| Authority | The right to make decisions and give instructions |
| Responsibility | Duty to do a task and answer for the result |
| Centralisation | Decisions taken at the top |
| Decentralisation | Decisions passed down to lower levels |
| Delegation | Passing a task with the authority to do it; the manager stays accountable |
| Delayering | Removing levels of management |

### Models of structure

| Model | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Hierarchy | Clear authority | Slow and inflexible |
| By function | Expertise; no duplication | Departments work in silos |
| By product | Focus on each market | Duplicated resources |
| Division (region or unit) | Local response | Duplicated cost; weaker central control |
| Matrix | Specialists shared; teamwork | Two bosses; conflict |
| System | Work flows across functions to the customer | Needs clear process ownership (use the definition given in class) |

### Tall against flat

| | Tall | Flat |
|---|---|---|
| Span | Narrow | Wide |
| Layers | Many | Few |
| Communication | Slow | Faster |
| Supervision | Close | Loose |
| Promotion | Many steps | Few steps |
| Cost | More managers | Fewer managers |

- With fixed staff numbers, a wider span means fewer layers.

### Delayering: benefits and risks

- Benefits: lower wage cost, faster communication, more staff responsibility.
- Risks: loss of experience, morale of survivors, overload, weaker supervision, fewer promotion steps, redundancy cost.

### Organisational culture

- Shared values, beliefs and habits: "how things are done here".
- Factors: leadership; history; size and structure; sector; staff; reward systems; technology; external environment; mergers.
- Impact: motivation, loyalty, productivity, turnover, service, resistance to change.
- Hard to change because it is held by people and built over years.

## Worked reminder

Hartwell Insurance (invented), 240 front-line staff.

- Span 6: 40 + 7 + 2 + 1 = **50 managers**, 4 layers.
- Span 10: 24 + 3 + 1 = **28 managers**, 3 layers.
- Posts removed: 22; at £45,000 each saves **£990,000**. Redundancy at £25,000 each is £550,000: recovered in 550,000 ÷ 990,000 = **0.56 years**.
- Round up at each level; every level needs a whole number of managers.

## Evaluating a structure: checklist

1. Fit with size and activity.
2. Speed of communication and decisions.
3. Cost of management.
4. Control against flexibility.
5. Staff experience and motivation.
6. Closeness to the customer.
7. Cost and disruption of change.

## Quick case practice

A firm (invented) has 90 front-line staff and a span of control of 5 at every level.

- Managers: 90 ÷ 5 = 18; 18 ÷ 5 = 3.6, so 4; 4 ÷ 5 = 0.8, so 1. Total **23**.
- With a span of 9: 90 ÷ 9 = 10; 10 ÷ 9 = 1.1, so 2; then 1. Total **13**.
- Posts removed = 23 − 13 = **10**; at £42,000 each the saving is **£420,000** a year.

Model response outline: delayering saves money and speeds communication, but a span of 9 needs experienced staff and good systems. Consider redundancy cost, morale and loss of promotion steps, then give a judgement about whether the work is routine enough to be supervised at a wider span.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hierarchy | Layers of authority arranged like a pyramid |
| Accountability | Being answerable for results |
| Silo | A department that works in isolation |
| Matrix structure | Staff report to a functional manager and a project manager |
| Culture | Shared values and habits of an organisation |
| Redundancy | Loss of a job because the post is no longer needed |
| Empowerment | Giving staff the authority to decide |

## Exam traps

- Delegation is a task passed down; decentralisation is a standing policy of decisions at lower levels.
- Authority can be delegated; accountability stays with the manager.
- Flat is not always better; it depends on staff and the task.
- Round up when counting managers.
- Link culture to a factor and a performance effect.
- Quote the case when judging a structure.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between a line and a staff relationship.
2. What does span of control mean?
3. Distinguish between delegation and decentralisation.
4. A firm has 120 staff and a span of 6 at every level. How many managers are needed at each level and in total?
5. Give two advantages of a flat structure.
6. Give two disadvantages of a matrix structure.
7. Give two factors that affect organisational culture.
8. Give one risk of delayering.
9. Why is culture hard to change?
10. Give one effect on stakeholders of a strong positive culture.

### Answers

1. A line relationship is a direct reporting link between manager and staff; a staff relationship is advice or support from a specialist with no direct authority over the line staff.
2. The number of people a manager directly supervises.
3. Delegation is passing a task with the authority to do it; decentralisation is a policy of taking decisions at lower levels generally.
4. 120 ÷ 6 = 20; 20 ÷ 6 = 3.33, so 4; 4 ÷ 6 = 0.67, so 1. Total = 20 + 4 + 1 = **25 managers**.
5. Any two: faster communication; lower management cost; more staff responsibility; decisions closer to customers.
6. Any two: two bosses; conflict over priorities; unclear responsibility; slow decisions.
7. Any two: leadership; history; size; sector; staff; reward systems.
8. Any one: loss of experience; morale of survivors; overload; weaker supervision.
9. It consists of shared habits and beliefs held by people and built up over years.
10. Any one: staff more motivated and loyal; lower turnover; better service for customers.
