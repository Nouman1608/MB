These questions cover section 3.1.5 Business location of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Business location study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-business-location/) and the [Business location revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-business-location-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by *proximity to the market* as a location factor. **[2]**

**2.** Give **two** reasons why a business that uses heavy raw materials might locate close to its supplier. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** questions a business should ask about the labour available in an area before choosing a site. **[2]**

**4.** Pentreath Bakery delivers fresh bread to cafés before 7am each morning. Explain **one** reason why it might locate close to its customers. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage for a restaurant of locating on a street with many other restaurants. **[4]**

**6.** A printing business is comparing two sites. Site P has rent of £2,600 a month and transport costs of £900 a month. Site Q has rent of £1,750 a month and transport costs of £1,450 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the total monthly cost of rent and transport for each site. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much the cheaper site would save over a year. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Inkwell Direct sells stationery only through its website and posts orders to customers across the UK. It is moving to a larger warehouse. Analyse why costs and labour may matter more to Inkwell Direct than proximity to the market. **[6]**

**8.** Tidewell Seafoods buys fish landed at a harbour, fillets and freezes it, and sells packs to supermarkets across the country. The fish must be processed within hours of landing.

| Monthly figure | Harbour site | Motorway site near a city |
|---|---|---|
| Rent | £4,000 | £5,500 |
| Chilled transport of fish to the site | £500 | £3,800 |
| Delivery of frozen packs to supermarkets | £5,200 | £2,600 |
| Experienced fish processors living within 15 miles | 40 | 5 |
| Warehouse workers looking for jobs within 15 miles | 30 | 300 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference in total monthly costs between the two sites. **[2]**
**(b)** Evaluate which site Tidewell Seafoods should choose. **[9]**

**9.** Ironbridge Fitness is choosing between two gym sites. At Site 1 it expects 600 members paying £30 a month and would pay rent of £7,500 a month. At Site 2 it expects 420 members paying £28 a month and would pay rent of £4,000 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly revenue at each site. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate revenue minus rent at each site and state which site leaves more. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain **one** reason why these forecasts might not be reliable. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how a business's location decision can affect its human resources function. **[4]**

**11.** Orchard Press makes apple juice from fruit grown on farms in one county. Fresh apples bruise easily and begin to spoil within days of picking. Analyse why availability of raw materials could be the most important location factor for Orchard Press. **[6]**

**12.** Moorgate Motors sells used cars from a site on the edge of a small town, where it has no nearby rivals. It is considering moving to a road in a city where four other car dealers are based. Rent would rise by £3,000 a month. Justify whether Moorgate Motors should move. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Proximity to the market means how close the site is to the business's customers [1]. Being close matters when customers visit in person or need goods quickly, because it brings more sales or cheaper, faster delivery [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Heavy materials are expensive to transport, so a short journey cuts transport costs [1]. Lower transport costs reduce the cost of each unit produced, so profit per unit rises (or deliveries are quicker and more reliable) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two of: are there enough workers available [1]; do the workers have the skills the business needs [1]; what wage rates are normal in the area. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" needs only the questions themselves. Save explanation for later questions.

**4.** The bread must reach cafés before they open, and it is best eaten fresh [1]. A site close to the cafés shortens delivery journeys, so the bakery can bake later and still deliver on time [1], which keeps quality high and helps it keep its café customers (or lowers delivery costs) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Advantage: diners who want a choice of places to eat are drawn to the street, so more potential customers pass the restaurant [1], raising the chance of extra sales without extra advertising [1]. Disadvantage: those customers can easily choose a rival instead [1], so the restaurant may have to lower prices or spend more on promotion, reducing profit [1]. **[4]**

**6. (a)** Site P: £2,600 + £900 = £3,500 [1]. Site Q: £1,750 + £1,450 = £3,200 [1].
**(b)** Site Q saves £300 a month, so £300 × 12 = £3,600 a year [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Inkwell Direct's customers never visit the warehouse because they order online [1], so being close to them brings no extra sales [1]. Its profit depends instead on keeping costs low: a cheaper site with lower rent and rates leaves more profit on each order [1]. A warehouse needs many staff to pick and pack orders [1], so it must locate where enough workers can reach the site at an affordable wage [1]. If it cannot recruit, orders will be delayed, which damages its reputation with customers who expect quick delivery, so labour and cost matter more than closeness to customers, provided the site has good courier links [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Harbour: £4,000 + £500 + £5,200 = £9,700. Motorway: £5,500 + £3,800 + £2,600 = £11,900 [1]. Difference = £11,900 − £9,700 = £2,200 a month [1].
**(b)** **Costs:** the harbour site is £2,200 a month cheaper, which is £26,400 a year, a significant saving [1]. **Raw materials:** fish must be processed within hours of landing, so the harbour keeps the fish fresh [1], whereas the motorway site adds journey time and the risk of quality falling [1]. **Market:** the motorway site halves delivery costs to supermarkets, from £5,200 to £2,600 [1], because frozen packs travel long distances to customers across the country [1]. **Labour:** the harbour has 40 experienced processors, while the motorway site has only 5, so Tidewell would need to train many of the 300 warehouse workers [1], adding cost and the risk of mistakes while they learn [1]. **Judgement:** the harbour site is the better choice because it is cheaper overall, protects the freshness of the raw material and has the skilled labour already [1]. This would change if Tidewell won much larger supermarket contracts, because delivery costs would then rise sharply from the harbour and the motorway site's larger workforce would be needed [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a decision that follows from the evidence. Use the table's figures rather than repeating them.

**9. (a)** Site 1: 600 × £30 = £18,000 [1]. Site 2: 420 × £28 = £11,760 [1].
**(b)** Site 1: £18,000 − £7,500 = £10,500 [1]. Site 2: £11,760 − £4,000 = £7,760 [1]. Site 1 leaves £2,740 a month more [1].
**(c)** The membership numbers are estimates made before the gym opens [1]; if a rival gym opens nearby, or fewer people join than expected, revenue at Site 1 could fall below the forecast and its higher rent would then reduce profit [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Location decides which workers can reach the business [1]. A site in an area with a large supply of skilled workers makes recruitment quicker and cheaper [1]. A site where workers are scarce may force the business to raise wages or pay for training [1]. If an existing business relocates, some staff may not be able to travel, so it loses experienced workers and must recruit replacements [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Orchard Press's raw material, fresh apples, bruises and spoils within days [1], so a long journey from the farms would lower the quality of the juice [1]. Locating near the orchards means apples can be pressed soon after picking [1], so less fruit is wasted and fewer apples need to be bought for each litre [1]. Apples are also bulky compared with the juice made from them, so transporting them is more expensive than transporting juice [1]. This lowers the cost per bottle, allowing a competitive price or a higher profit margin, which is why raw materials may outweigh being close to customers [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Moving to the city road would bring Moorgate Motors closer to many more potential buyers [1], and car buyers like to compare several dealers, so the cluster could attract people who would not travel to a small town [1]. However, the rent rises by £3,000 a month, or £36,000 a year [1], and customers at the cluster can easily buy from one of the four rivals instead [1]. Moorgate should move only if it expects to sell enough extra cars to cover £36,000 a year, for example if each car earns about £1,000 profit it would need roughly 36 extra sales [1]. Because it currently has no rivals at all, it should stay unless its own market research shows its town customers are already travelling to the city to buy [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Justify" needs a clear choice. Either decision can score full marks if it is supported by the case.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a factor without explaining its effect on sales, costs or quality.
- Choosing the cheaper site without considering what is lost, such as skilled labour or customers.
- Forgetting to multiply monthly figures by 12 when a question asks about a year.
- Treating competitors only as a threat and missing the pull of a cluster.
- Writing an evaluation that weighs only one factor.
- Ending with "it depends" and no clear decision.
