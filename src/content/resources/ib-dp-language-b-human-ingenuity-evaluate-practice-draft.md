# Human ingenuity: evaluate practice

The model texts are in English and every example is only an illustration of a skill; do the language-producing tasks in the target language of your school course and ask your school teacher to check them. The brief's recommended topics are optional and its possible questions are only possible, so your teacher chooses what to teach. ## Questions

**1.** Name three optional recommended topics for Human ingenuity. **[3]**

### Passage A (original text written for Marlbridge)

> **A phone for the whole class?** At Harbourside School each pupil now borrows a tablet. Teachers say homework is handed in more often. Some parents worry that children spend less time talking. "I read more," says Dina, 14, "but I miss the library."

**2.** (a) What do teachers say has improved? **[1]** (b) Give one worry. **[1]** (c) What does Dina miss? **[1]**

**3.** Identify two different viewpoints in the passage and quote support. **[4]**

**4.** Explain how the passage relates to the possible question about how the media change the way we relate to each other. **[3]**

**5.** Plan a Paper 1 task: *Write an opinion piece for a school magazine on whether schools should lend tablets.* Give context, audience, purpose, and a five-part frame. **[5]**

**6.** Write one sentence of concession ("although...") about technology in your target language and give an English gloss. **[2]**

## Model answers

**1.** Any three: entertainment; artistic expressions; communication and media; technology; scientific innovation [1 each].

**2.** (a) Homework is handed in more often [1]. (b) Less time talking [1]. (c) The library [1].

**3.** Positive: teachers, with quotation [2]; concerned: parents or Dina, with quotation [2].

**4.** Technology changes how pupils interact [1]; less talking is a relationship effect [1]; a balanced reading with Dina's quote [1].

**5.** Context: magazine [1]; audience: students, teachers, parents [1]; purpose: to give an opinion [1]; frame: claim, evidence, effect, counterpoint, judgement [2].

**6.** Accept a correct concession in the target language with an English gloss [2: 1 structure, 1 accuracy].
