These questions cover section 3.2.5 Legislation of AQA GCSE Business (8132). Revise first with the [Legislation study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-legislation-aqa-gcse-business/) and the [Legislation revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-8132-legislation-aqa-gcse-business-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and wage figures are not real legal rates.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Identify the **two** employment laws named in the AQA GCSE Business specification. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** responsibilities of an employer under the Health and Safety at Work Act (1974). **[2]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by consumer law on trade descriptions. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** way the Equality Act (2010) affects how a business recruits staff. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of providing a safe working environment. **[3]**

**6.** Rowan Cleaning employs 14 cleaners on the legal minimum wage. Each works 1,300 hours a year. The legal minimum rises by 45p an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the increase in Rowan Cleaning's yearly wage bill. **[2]**
**(b)** State **one** way Rowan Cleaning could respond to the higher cost. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and case-study questions

**7.** Analyse the impact of an increase in the national minimum wage on a small, family-run restaurant. **[6]**

**8.** Hartcliffe Joinery employs 25 workers using saws and sanding machines. A worker was recently injured using a machine without a guard.

**(a)** Identify **two** actions Hartcliffe Joinery should take to comply with health and safety law. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse the possible consequences for Hartcliffe Joinery of failing to follow health and safety law. **[6]**

**9.** Explain how the Equality Act (2010) could create training needs for a business. **[3]**

**10.** Glenmore Outdoor sells jackets online described as "fully waterproof". Customers report that the jackets leak in heavy rain. Explain **two** possible consequences for Glenmore Outdoor. **[4]**

**11.** Ferncliffe Hotels employs 120 staff, many on the legal minimum wage. Its owner wants to pay all staff the voluntary real Living Wage, which is higher than the legal minimum. Analyse **one** benefit and **one** drawback of this decision. **[6]**

**12.** Kestrel Couriers employs 60 delivery drivers and warehouse staff. It is growing fast and recruiting often. Assess the impact of employment law and health and safety law on Kestrel Couriers. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** National minimum wage/living wage [1]; the Equality Act (2010) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: assess risks in the workplace [1]; provide safe equipment and working conditions [1]; give staff health and safety information and training; supervise work so it is done safely. **[2]**

**3.** A business must describe its products accurately [1], and must not make false or misleading claims about them on packaging, in adverts or online [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The Act makes it unlawful to discriminate because of protected characteristics such as age, sex or race [1]. So the business must word job adverts so they do not exclude groups unfairly and choose candidates using criteria linked to the job [1], which may mean writing clearer person specifications and interview questions, adding time to the recruitment process [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A safe workplace means fewer accidents [1], so fewer staff are absent through injury [1], which reduces disruption to production and the cost of covering for absent workers (or improves motivation and reduces staff turnover) [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** 14 × 1,300 × £0.45 = £8,190 [1] [1].
**(b)** For example: raise its prices; reduce staff hours; find savings elsewhere; accept lower profit [1]. **[3]**

**7.** A restaurant employs many staff, such as waiters and kitchen assistants, who are often on the minimum wage [1], so wages are a large share of its costs and the wage bill rises significantly [1]. A small family business may have low profit margins, so the rise cuts its profit noticeably [1]. It may raise menu prices, but customers could eat elsewhere, reducing sales [1]. Alternatively it may cut staff hours, which could lead to slower service and fewer returning customers [1]. On the other hand, higher pay may make staff more motivated and less likely to leave, reducing the cost of recruiting and training replacements [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Any two of: fit guards to all machines [1]; carry out a risk assessment of machine use [1]; train workers to use machines safely; provide protective equipment.
**(b)** Inspectors could order Hartcliffe to stop using unsafe machines until they are made safe [1], so production halts and orders are delayed [1]. Serious breaches could lead to prosecution and a fine [1], reducing profit [1]. The injured worker may claim compensation, and other workers may lose confidence and leave [1]. News of the accident could also damage Hartcliffe's reputation, making it harder to win contracts and recruit skilled joiners [1]. **[8]**

**9.** The business must avoid discrimination in recruitment, pay and promotion [1]. Managers who interview, appraise or promote staff may not know what the law requires [1], so they need training in fair practice, which costs money and takes time away from their normal work [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Customers can ask for refunds or replacements because the jackets are not as described [1], which costs Glenmore money and reduces its profit [1]. Glenmore could also face action from Trading Standards for a misleading description [1], and poor online reviews may reduce future sales as customers lose trust in its claims [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Benefit:** higher pay can motivate staff and make them less likely to leave [1]. With 120 staff, lower staff turnover reduces the cost and disruption of recruiting and training replacements [1], and motivated staff may give better service, improving guests' experience and repeat bookings [1]. **Drawback:** paying above the legal minimum to many staff raises the wage bill significantly [1]. Unless Ferncliffe raises room prices, its profit falls [1]; if it does raise prices, it may lose guests to cheaper hotels [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Employment law:** Kestrel recruits often, so it must make sure adverts and selection follow the Equality Act, using job-related criteria [1]. Managers may need training in fair recruitment, adding cost [1], but fair recruitment gives access to a wider pool of drivers and warehouse staff, which helps a fast-growing business fill vacancies [1]. Many warehouse staff may be on the legal minimum wage, so any rise increases costs across a large workforce [1]. **Health and safety law:** driving and warehouse work involve risks such as road accidents and heavy lifting [1], so Kestrel must pay for training, safe vehicles and equipment [1]. Failing to comply could lead to accidents, compensation claims and orders to stop work, which would delay deliveries and damage its reputation with customers [1]. **Judgement:** the impact of health and safety law is likely to be greater [1], because the risks in its work are high and an accident could disrupt the whole business; however, since it is recruiting often, the cost of compliance with both laws is a necessary part of growing, and the benefits of a safe, fairly treated workforce help it keep staff [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing long descriptions of the laws instead of their effects.
- Forgetting that businesses differ in how much a law affects them.
- Giving only costs and missing benefits such as motivation and lower staff turnover.
- Leaving out the consequences of failing to comply.
- Treating the real Living Wage as a legal requirement.
- Giving no judgement in an "assess" question.
