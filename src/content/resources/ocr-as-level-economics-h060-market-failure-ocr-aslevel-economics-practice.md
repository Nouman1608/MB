## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define market failure. **[2]**

**2.** Which of the following is equal to marginal social cost?

A: MPC − MEC

B: MPB + MEB

C: MPC + MEC

D: MSB − MPB **[1]**

**3.** The marginal private cost of a unit is £22 and its marginal social cost is £30. Calculate the marginal external cost. **[1]**

**4.** Distinguish between partial and complete market failure, using an example of each. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by a third party, using an example. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a free market equilibrium can be allocatively efficient when there are no external costs or benefits. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** In an invented market for plastic packaging, MPB = 90 − 3Q and MPC = 10 + Q, where Q is thousand tonnes and prices are in pounds. Each tonne creates an external cost of £12.

**(a)** Calculate the market output and price. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the MSC equation and calculate the socially optimal output. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the welfare loss at the market output. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows an invented market for apprenticeship training places (Q in thousands, values in £ hundreds).

| Q | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| MPB | 50 | 44 | 38 | 32 | 26 | 20 |
| MSB | 62 | 56 | 50 | 44 | 38 | 32 |
| MPC = MSC | 14 | 20 | 26 | 32 | 38 | 44 |

**(a)** State the market output. **[1]**

**(b)** State the socially optimal output. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the marginal external benefit. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why the free market provides too few training places. **[2]**

**9.** Explain, with the aid of a diagram, why there is a welfare loss when marginal social cost is greater than marginal private cost. **[5]**

**10.** Explain two causes of market failure other than externalities, and state one government intervention that could address each. **[4]**

**11.** Evaluate the view that market failure always justifies government intervention. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Market failure occurs when the free market allocates resources inefficiently [1], so output differs from the socially optimal level and society's welfare is not maximised [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** MEC = MSC − MPC = 30 − 22 = **£8** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Partial market failure: a market exists but produces the wrong quantity, for example too much electricity from coal because pollution is ignored [1]. Complete market failure: no market exists, for example there is no market in which residents can buy quiet from a nearby airport [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A third party is someone affected by a transaction who is neither the buyer nor the seller [1], for example people living near a busy road who suffer from the noise of drivers' journeys [1]. **[2]**

**6.** With no externalities, MPB = MSB and MPC = MSC [1]. The market output where MPB = MPC is then also where MSB = MSC, which is the allocatively efficient output [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 90 − 3Q = 10 + Q, so Q = **20** thousand tonnes [1] and P = 10 + 20 = **£30** [1].

**(b)** MSC = 22 + Q [1]. 90 − 3Q = 22 + Q gives Q = **17** thousand tonnes [1].

**(c)** At Q = 20, MSC = 42 and MSB = 30, a gap of £12 [1]. Welfare loss = ½ × (20 − 17) × 12 = **£18 thousand** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The welfare-loss height is the gap between MSC and MSB at the market output, here equal to the MEC because MEC is constant.

**8. (a)** **4 thousand** places, where MPB = MPC = 32 [1].

**(b)** **5 thousand** places, where MSB = MSC = 38 [1].

**(c)** MEB = MSB − MPB = **12** (£1,200 per place) [1].

**(d)** Employers and trainees base decisions on private benefit and ignore the benefit to others, such as future employers and customers who gain from a more skilled workforce [1]. MSB is therefore above MPB, so the market stops at 4 thousand places when society would gain from 5 thousand [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Diagram with MPB = MSB downward sloping, MPC upward sloping and MSC above MPC [1], with market output (MPB = MPC) beyond the optimum (MSB = MSC) [1] and the welfare-loss triangle between MSC and MSB from the optimum to the market output shaded [1]. Producers ignore the external cost, so the market produces where MPB = MPC [1]. For each unit beyond the optimum, the cost to society (MSC) exceeds the benefit (MSB), and the sum of these excess costs is the welfare loss [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Poor information: consumers may overestimate the benefit of a product, so demand and output are too high [1]; information provision, such as health warnings, could correct this [1]. Market power: a dominant firm restricts output to raise price, so output is below the efficient level [1]; competition policy could limit this [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Price instability or missing markets are also acceptable causes, with buffer stocks or legislation as matching interventions.

**11.** Award up to 10 marks across knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation.
- Market failure means the free market output differs from the social optimum, causing a welfare loss [1].
- Intervention such as a tax equal to the MEC could move output to the optimum and remove the loss [1].
- Where the welfare loss is large, as with serious pollution, the case for intervention is strong [1].
- But the MEC is hard to value in money, so the government may set the wrong tax or limit [1].
- Intervention has administrative and enforcement costs, which may exceed a small welfare loss [1].
- Intervention may cause government failure, such as unintended consequences or black markets [1].
- Private solutions may work without government, for example negotiation between a firm and its neighbours, or firms protecting their reputation [1].
- Some external effects cross borders or appear only in the future, so one government cannot correct them alone [1].
- Judgement: market failure is a necessary condition for intervention but not a sufficient one [1]; intervention is justified when the loss is large, measurable and a low-cost tool exists [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing MSC = MPC − MEC.
- Shading the welfare loss on the wrong side of the optimum.
- Explaining market failure without the words private, external and social.
- Concluding that every market failure needs government action.
- Leaving units off calculated answers.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between private and social cost?**
Private cost is the cost to the producer or consumer making the decision. Social cost adds the external cost to third parties, so MSC = MPC + MEC.

**Where is the welfare loss on a market failure diagram?**
It is the triangle between MSC and MSB, between the socially optimal output and the market output.
