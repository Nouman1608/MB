Condensed notes for sub-topic 6.2, Ethics: the meaning of ethics, fair trade and two ethical issues.

## Four facts to start with

1. **Ethics** is about what is right and wrong, beyond what the law says.
2. **Fair trade** gives producers a fair price and decent conditions.
3. **Misleading advertising** gives a false impression to sell a product.
4. **Harmful disposal** damages people or the environment.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Ethics | Moral principles about right and wrong |
| Stakeholder | Anyone affected by a business: customers, workers, suppliers, owners, community |
| Fair trade | System giving producers a fair price and good conditions |
| Fair trade premium | Extra payment for community projects |
| Misleading advertising | Advertising that gives a false or unclear impression |
| Greenwashing | Falsely claiming a product is eco-friendly |
| Harmful disposal | Getting rid of waste in a way that causes damage |
| E-waste | Discarded electronic goods |

## Fair trade

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Guaranteed minimum price | Protects farmers when market prices fall |
| Fair trade premium | Pays for community projects |
| Safe conditions | No child or forced labour |
| Long-term contracts | Producers can plan |

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Better income for producers | Higher prices |
| Protects workers | Not all producers can join |
| Attracts ethical customers | Checking costs |

## Misleading advertising: forms

False claims; exaggeration; hidden charges; fake reviews; misleading images; greenwashing.

Effects: customers lose money and trust; competitors lose sales; the business faces fines, refunds and damaged reputation.

## Harmful disposal: examples and alternatives

| Harmful | Alternative |
|---|---|
| Dumping chemicals in rivers | Treat hazardous waste safely |
| Throwing away electronics | Recycling and take-back schemes |
| Burning plastic | Design recyclable packaging |
| Landfilling unsold goods | Donate or resell |

## Worked examples

**Fair trade price.** 500 kg at market price $0.80 = $400. At fair trade $1.20 = $600. Extra $200, which is a 50% increase. Premium at $0.15 per kg = $75.

**Misleading advertising cost.** 800 refunds × $25 = $20,000 plus a $15,000 fine = $35,000.

## Ethical or only legal?

| Action | Legal? | Ethical? |
|---|---|---|
| Paying the legal minimum wage when workers cannot live on it | Yes | Questionable |
| Hiding charges in small print | Sometimes | No |
| Safe recycling of old batteries | Yes | Yes |
| Dumping chemicals secretly | No | No |

## Model answers to learn

**What is meant by ethics?** Ethics is the set of moral principles about what is right and wrong. In business it means being honest and fair to customers, workers, suppliers and the environment.

**Why might a business choose fair trade products?** They attract customers who care about producers' welfare and improve the business's reputation, although they cost more.

## Stakeholders and ethical issues

| Stakeholder | Harmed by | Helped by |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Misleading advertising | Honest information |
| Producers and workers | Very low prices and pay | Fair trade |
| Community and environment | Harmful disposal | Safe recycling |
| Competitors | Unfair claims | Honest competition |
| The business | Fines, lost trust | Good reputation |

## Developing an ethics point

| Weak | Developed |
|---|---|
| "It is unfair" | "Customers pay for a cream that does not work, so they lose money and trust the business less" |
| "Fair trade is good" | "Fair trade gives farmers a guaranteed minimum price, so their income is secure when market prices fall" |
| "It pollutes" | "Dumping batteries releases harmful metals into the soil, which can enter the water supply" |

## Exam traps

- **Ethics as law.**
- **Fair trade as free goods.**
- **Cost of ethical actions ignored.**
- **Only business effects given.**
- **Sustainability and ethics mixed up.**
- **Example missing.**

## Self-test

1. Define ethics.
2. What is fair trade?
3. Give two features of fair trade.
4. Give one advantage of fair trade to farmers.
5. Give one disadvantage of fair trade to a retailer.
6. What is misleading advertising?
7. Give two forms of misleading advertising.
8. Give two examples of harmful disposal.
9. Give one effect of harmful disposal.
10. Why might a business act ethically even when it costs more?

**Answers**

1. Moral principles about what is right and wrong.
2. A system that gives producers a fair price and good working conditions.
3. Any two: guaranteed minimum price, fair trade premium, safe conditions, long-term contracts, environmental standards.
4. Any one: better, more secure income; protection from price falls.
5. Any one: higher purchase prices; higher prices may put customers off.
6. Advertising that gives a false or unclear impression of a product.
7. Any two: false claims, exaggeration, hidden charges, fake reviews, misleading images, greenwashing.
8. Any two: dumping chemicals, discarding electronics, burning plastic, landfilling unsold goods.
9. Any one: pollution, harm to health or wildlife, clean-up cost, fines, damaged reputation.
10. It builds trust and reputation, attracts loyal customers and employees, and avoids penalties.

## Command words to expect

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Identify, State, Give | A short answer with no explanation |
| Define | A precise meaning |
| Describe | The main points or features |
| Explain | A reason, linked to the situation, with a development |
| Analyse | Break the issue into parts and show how they connect |
| Assess, Consider | A reasoned judgement about importance or effect |
| Discuss | Both sides of an issue, in a structured way |
| Justify | A case backed with evidence |
| Evaluate | A judgement of value, ending with a conclusion |
