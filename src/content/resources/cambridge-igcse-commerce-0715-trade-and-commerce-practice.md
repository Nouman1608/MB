## Section A - short questions

**1.** Which of the following is an example of trade rather than an aid to trade? **[1]**
A insuring a cargo   B storing goods in a warehouse   C a shop selling a radio to a customer   D a bank lending money to a farmer

**2.** A wholesaler in Malaysia buys 2,000 tins of paint from a Malaysian factory. This is an example of: **[1]**
A retail trade   B home trade   C an export   D an import

**3.** Which aid to trade removes the barrier of risk? **[1]**
A advertising   B banking   C insurance   D transport

**4.** A customer sees a video on a social media platform and buys the jacket shown through the platform's own shop. This is: **[1]**
A traditional commerce   B scommerce   C wholesale trade   D home trade only

**5.** Define commerce. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** differences between exports and imports. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Rania runs a bakery in a busy town. She sells bread over the counter, takes orders through her own website, and has started selling cakes through a social media page.*
(a) Identify **three** types of commerce Rania uses, linking each to her business. (3 marks)
(b) Explain **two** aids to trade, other than advertising, that Rania's bakery relies on. (4 marks) **[7]**

**8.** Explain the difference between retail trade and wholesale trade, giving an example of each. **[4]**

**9.** *SunHarvest exports dried mangoes to supermarkets in Europe. It uses a freight forwarder and rents space in a warehouse near the port.*
(a) Define logistics. (2 marks)
(b) Explain how each of the three elements of logistics helps SunHarvest. (6 marks) **[8]**

**10.** Explain how **four** aids to trade remove barriers between a producer of cotton shirts and its consumers in another country. **[8]**

**11.** Using the example of a car manufacturer, explain how production, trade and commerce are interdependent. **[4]**

**12.** *A family-owned electronics shop sells only from its high-street store. The owner's son wants to start selling through mcommerce.*
Discuss whether the shop should begin selling through mcommerce. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C. A shop selling a radio is buying and selling, so it is trade; the others are aids to trade. **[1]**

**2.** B. Both buyer and seller are in Malaysia, and this is wholesale, not retail, so it is home trade. **[1]**

**3.** C. Insurance transfers the financial effect of loss or damage to the insurer. **[1]**

**4.** B. The sale takes place through a social media channel. **[1]**

**5.** Trade (the buying and selling of goods and services) [1] together with the aids to trade that help it take place, such as transport, banking and insurance [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: exports are goods or services sold to other countries, imports are bought from other countries [1]; exports bring money into the country, imports send money out [1]; one country's export is the other country's import [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Traditional commerce: selling bread over the counter in the bakery [1]. Ecommerce: taking orders through her website [1]. Scommerce: selling cakes through a social media page [1]. (3 marks)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each (1 for the aid, 1 for the applied explanation): transport, to deliver website orders or collect flour and other supplies, overcoming distance [2]; banking, so customers can pay by card or transfer and Rania can pay suppliers safely [2]; insurance, against fire in the ovens or theft from the shop, so a loss does not close the business [2]; communications, so customers can place orders and Rania can contact suppliers [2]; warehousing or storage, so ingredients are held until needed [2]. (max 4 marks) **[7]**

**8.** Retail trade is selling goods in small quantities to final consumers [1], e.g. a pharmacy selling a packet of plasters to a shopper [1]. Wholesale trade is buying large quantities from producers and selling smaller quantities to retailers [1], e.g. a food wholesaler selling cases of rice to corner shops [1]. **[4]**

**9.** (a) The planning and management of the movement and storage of goods through a supply chain [1] so that the right goods arrive at the right place and time at the lowest reasonable cost [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Transport: moves the mangoes from the factory to the port and by sea or air to Europe [1], so the product reaches buyers far away [1]. Freight forwarder: books space with a shipping line or airline and handles export paperwork [1], so SunHarvest does not need its own expertise in international shipping [1]. Warehousing: stores packed mangoes near the port until the ship sails [1], so goods are ready to load and supply can be matched to orders [1]. (6 marks) **[8]**

**10.** Any four, 2 marks each (1 for the barrier, 1 for how the aid removes it in context):
Transport removes **distance**: ships or aircraft carry the shirts from the factory to the overseas market [2].
Warehousing removes **time**: shirts made throughout the year are stored until the shops need them for the season [2].
Insurance removes **risk**: if a container of shirts is lost at sea, the insurer compensates the producer [2].
Banking removes **payment** barriers: a bank arranges payment in a foreign currency or a letter of credit [2].
Advertising removes **lack of knowledge**: consumers abroad learn the brand exists and why to buy it [2].
Communications removes **information** barriers: buyer and seller exchange prices, orders and delivery details by email or video call [2]. **[max 8]**

**11.** One mark per link, applied to the car manufacturer:
The manufacturer produces far more cars than it could use, so it depends on trade, selling to car dealers and buyers, to earn revenue [1].
Dealers depend on the manufacturer's production; without it they have nothing to sell [1].
Trade in cars depends on commerce: cars must be transported to dealers, buyers often need bank loans, and cars must be insured [1].
Banks, insurers and transport firms earn income only because cars are produced and traded, so each depends on the others [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Level-style answer, up to 6 marks: up to 2 for arguments for, up to 2 for arguments against, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
For: customers can buy at any time on their smartphones, so sales are not limited to opening hours [1]; the shop can reach customers outside its town, raising revenue [1]; electronics are often researched on phones, so the shop may lose sales to rivals if it is not there [1].
Against: an app or mobile-friendly site costs money to build and maintain [1]; delivery and returns add costs, and electronics are valuable and fragile in transit [1]; the shop's strength may be face-to-face advice, which is harder on a phone [1].
Conclusion: for example, the shop should start with a simple mobile-friendly site offering click-and-collect, keeping its advice-based store, because this tests demand at low cost [2]. Any reasoned conclusion that weighs both sides and uses the scenario is accepted. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "buying and selling" as the definition of commerce.
- Naming an aid to trade without saying which barrier it removes or how it helps the named business.
- Mixing up the direction of exports and imports.
- Giving only two of the three logistics elements, or describing the freight forwarder as a transport company that owns its own fleet.
- In "discuss" questions, arguing one side only and giving no conclusion.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many aids to trade does the 0715 syllabus name?** Six: communications, transport, warehousing, advertising, banking and insurance.

**Is mcommerce a type of ecommerce?** Yes. Mcommerce and scommerce both use the internet, so they are kinds of ecommerce, defined by the channel used: a mobile device or social media.

**What are the elements of logistics in the syllabus?** Transport, freight forwarders and warehousing.
