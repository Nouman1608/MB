## Section A

**1.** Explain what is meant by an *effective collision*. **[2]**

**2.** Which change increases the frequency of collisions without changing the fraction of collisions that are effective?

A: raising the temperature

B: adding a catalyst

C: increasing the concentration of a reactant

D: lowering the temperature **[1]**

**3.** State the units of rate when the concentration of a reactant is measured in mol dm⁻³ and time in seconds. **[1]**

**4.** In a reaction, 0.0360 mol dm⁻³ of product forms in the first 90 s. Calculate the mean rate of formation of the product. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the rate of most reactions decreases as the reaction proceeds. **[2]**

**6.** In a clock reaction, a colour appears after 25 s. Calculate the relative rate. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Hydrogen and iodine react in a sealed container: H₂(g) + I₂(g) → 2HI(g). The volume of the container is halved at constant temperature.

**(a)** State the effect on the concentration of each gas. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the effect on the rate of reaction. **[3]**

**(c)** A student writes that "the molecules move faster at higher pressure". Comment on this. **[1]**

**8.** Magnesium ribbon reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. The volume of hydrogen is recorded.

| Time / s | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume / cm³ | 0 | 22 | 38 | 50 | 57 | 60 | 60 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate over the first 20 s. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean rate between 20 s and 40 s. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the rate in (b) is lower than in (a). **[2]**

**(d)** State the approximate time at which the reaction stopped. **[1]**

**(e)** Calculate the mass of magnesium used, assuming room conditions. **[2]**

**9.** A concentration–time graph is plotted for the loss of reactant R.

**(a)** A tangent drawn at t = 100 s passes through (0 s, 0.450 mol dm⁻³) and (250 s, 0.050 mol dm⁻³). Calculate the rate at 100 s. **[2]**

**(b)** The tangent at t = 0 passes through (0 s, 0.600 mol dm⁻³) and (120 s, 0 mol dm⁻³). Calculate the initial rate. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the initial rate is greater than the rate at 100 s. **[1]**

**10.** Hydrogen peroxide decomposes: 2H₂O₂(aq) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g). Oxygen is formed at 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol s⁻¹.

**(a)** State the rate at which H₂O₂ is used up, in mol s⁻¹. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of oxygen produced per second at room conditions, in cm³. **[2]**

**11.** Calcium carbonate reacts with excess hydrochloric acid in a flask on a balance. A loose cotton wool plug is placed in the neck. In 240 s the mass falls by 1.32 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of mass loss in g s⁻¹. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of formation of CO₂ in mol s⁻¹. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the mass of the flask decreases. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest the purpose of the cotton wool plug. **[1]**

**12.** Two experiments use 0.100 g of magnesium ribbon with 50.0 cm³ of hydrochloric acid at the same temperature. Experiment A uses 1.0 mol dm⁻³ acid; experiment B uses 2.0 mol dm⁻³ acid. The acid is in excess in both.

**(a)** Compare the initial rates of the two experiments, explaining your answer in terms of collisions. **[3]**

**(b)** Compare the final volumes of hydrogen, with a reason. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A collision between reacting particles that results in a reaction [1]; the particles collide with energy at least equal to the activation energy (and in a suitable orientation) [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].

**4.** 0.0360 / 90 [1] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].

**5.** Reactants are used up, so their concentration falls [1]; collisions, and therefore effective collisions, become less frequent [1].

**6.** 1/25 = 0.040 s⁻¹ [1].

**7. (a)** Both concentrations double [1].

**(b)** There are twice as many molecules per unit volume [1], so collisions between H₂ and I₂ molecules are more frequent [1]. The frequency of effective collisions increases, so the rate increases [1].

**(c)** Incorrect: at constant temperature the molecules have the same average speed and energy; only the frequency of collisions changes [1].

**8. (a)** 38 / 20 = 1.9 cm³ s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** (57 − 38) / 20 = 0.95 cm³ s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** Magnesium and acid have been used up, so there are fewer reactant particles available [1]; collisions, and effective collisions, are less frequent [1].

**(d)** About 50 s (between 40 s and 50 s) [1].

**(e)** n(H₂) = 60 / 24 000 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol, and n(Mg) = n(H₂) [1]; mass = 2.50 × 10⁻³ × 24.3 = 0.0608 g [1].

**9. (a)** Gradient = (0.450 − 0.050) / (250 − 0) [1] = 1.60 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 0.600 / 120 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** At t = 0 the concentration of R is highest, so effective collisions are most frequent [1].

**10. (a)** 2 × 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 24 000 [1] = 0.72 cm³ s⁻¹ [1].

**11. (a)** 1.32 / 240 = 5.5 × 10⁻³ g s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** n(CO₂) = 1.32 / 44.0 = 0.0300 mol [1]; rate = 0.0300 / 240 = 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ mol s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** Carbon dioxide gas escapes from the flask [1].

**(d)** To stop acid spray escaping (so that only CO₂ leaves), while allowing the gas out [1].

**12. (a)** B has the faster initial rate [1]. The acid in B has twice as many H⁺ ions per unit volume [1], so collisions with the magnesium surface are more frequent and effective collisions are more frequent [1].

**(b)** The final volumes are the same [1], because magnesium is the limiting reactant and the same mass is used in both [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "more collisions" without "per unit time" or "more frequent".
- Saying higher concentration or pressure gives particles more energy.
- Using a chord between two data points when a tangent is asked for.
- Giving rates without units.
- Confusing rate with the amount of product: the final volume depends on the limiting reactant.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need orders of reaction for subtopic 8.1?**
No. Rate equations, orders and rate constants are A Level content in Topic 26. At AS you calculate rates from data and explain them using collisions.
