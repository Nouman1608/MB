This guide covers section 4.7, Circle theorems I, in the Geometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to calculate unknown angles and give explanations using six properties of circles: the angle in a semicircle is 90°; the angle between a tangent and a radius is 90°; the angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference; angles in the same segment are equal; opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°; and the alternate segment theorem. You must give reasons using these properties. After this guide, use the [circle theorems I revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i-revision-notes/) and the [circle theorems I practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i-practice/).

## What section 4.7 asks for

Each question gives a diagram with some angles and asks you to find others. Every step needs a correct reason, written in words. The skill is to spot which theorem applies, and to add the isosceles-triangle and angle-sum facts that connect them.

Useful vocabulary: the **circumference** is the curve, a **chord** joins two points on it, an **arc** is part of the curve, a **tangent** touches it at one point and a **segment** is the region between a chord and the arc.

## Theorem 1: the angle in a semicircle

If AB is a diameter and C is any other point on the circle, then angle ACB = 90°.

Reason to write: **angle in a semicircle is 90°**.

### Worked example 1

AB is a diameter of a circle. C is on the circumference and angle CAB = 36°. Find angle ABC.

```
angle ACB = 90°                        (angle in a semicircle)
angle ABC = 180° − 90° − 36° = 54°     (angle sum of a triangle)
```

## Theorem 2: tangent and radius

A tangent to a circle is perpendicular to the radius drawn to the point of contact.

Reason to write: **angle between a tangent and a radius is 90°**.

### Worked example 2

The tangent from P touches the circle, centre O, at T. Angle TPO = 28°. Find angle TOP.

```
angle OTP = 90°                        (tangent is perpendicular to the radius)
angle TOP = 180° − 90° − 28° = 62°     (angle sum of a triangle)
```

## Theorem 3: angle at the centre

The angle at the centre of a circle is twice the angle at the circumference when both are standing on the same arc.

Reason to write: **angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference**.

### Worked example 3

A, B and C are on a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = 124°, where the angle is subtended by the minor arc AB, and C is on the major arc. Find angle ACB.

```
angle ACB = 124° ÷ 2 = 62°      (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
```

### Worked example 4

A, B and C are on a circle with centre O. C is on the minor arc AB and angle ACB = 130°. Find the (obtuse) angle AOB.

```
reflex angle AOB = 2 × 130° = 260°     (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
angle AOB = 360° − 260° = 100°         (angles at a point sum to 360°)
```

Take care here. The angle at the circumference is obtuse, so it faces the major arc, and the angle at the centre it stands on is the reflex one.

### Worked example 5

A, B and C are on a circle with centre O. Angle ACB = 34°. Find the base angles of triangle OAB.

```
angle AOB = 2 × 34° = 68°               (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
OA = OB                                  (radii)
angle OAB = angle OBA = (180° − 68°) ÷ 2 = 56°   (isosceles triangle, angle sum of a triangle)
```

Radii are equal, so a triangle formed by two radii and a chord is isosceles. Drawing in the radii is often the key step.

## Theorem 4: angles in the same segment

Angles at the circumference standing on the same arc, and on the same side of the chord, are equal.

Reason to write: **angles in the same segment are equal**.

### Worked example 6

A, B, C and D are on a circle. Angle ACB = 41°, and C and D are on the same side of AB. Find angle ADB.

```
angle ADB = 41°        (angles in the same segment are equal)
```

## Theorem 5: cyclic quadrilaterals

A **cyclic quadrilateral** has all four vertices on a circle. Its opposite angles sum to 180° (they are supplementary).

Reason to write: **opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°**.

### Worked example 7

ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Angle DAB = 95° and angle ABC = 78°. Find angles BCD and CDA.

```
angle BCD = 180° − 95° = 85°     (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°)
angle CDA = 180° − 78° = 102°    (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°)
check: 95 + 78 + 85 + 102 = 360
```

### Worked example 8

The opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are (2x + 20)° and (3x − 10)°. Find x and the angles.

```
2x + 20 + 3x − 10 = 180
5x + 10 = 180
x = 34
angles: 88° and 92°
```

An exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle. If you extend one side, the exterior angle and the adjoining interior angle lie on a straight line, so the exterior angle matches the opposite interior angle.

## Theorem 6: the alternate segment theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord, drawn from the point of contact, is equal to the angle in the alternate segment. The alternate segment is the one on the opposite side of the chord from that angle.

Reason to write: **angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment**.

### Worked example 9

The tangent PTQ touches a circle at T. TA is a chord, and B is a point on the circle on the opposite side of the chord TA from P. Angle PTA = 64°. Find angle TBA.

```
angle TBA = 64°     (angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment)
```

In the diagram, angle PTA is the angle between the tangent and the chord TA. The alternate segment is the segment on the other side of TA from that angle, so B must lie in it.

## Why some theorems follow from others

The angle in a semicircle is a special case of the angle at the centre. A diameter makes an angle of 180° at the centre, a straight line, so the angle at the circumference on the same arc is half of that, 90°. Seeing the links helps you remember the facts and check that your answer is sensible.

### Worked example 10

ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. The side BC is extended to E, and angle DCE = 74°. Find angle DAB.

```
angle BCD = 180° − 74° = 106°     (angles on a straight line)
angle DAB = 180° − 106° = 74°     (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°)
```

So the exterior angle DCE equals the interior opposite angle DAB. In an exam you may quote this shortcut as "exterior angle of a cyclic quadrilateral equals the interior opposite angle", but the two-step method above shows every step.

## Combining theorems

Most questions use two or three facts together. Work through the diagram step by step.

### Worked example 11

AB is a diameter of a circle. C is on the circle and angle ABC = 25°. The tangent at C meets BA extended at D. Find angle ACD.

```
angle ACB = 90°                          (angle in a semicircle)
angle CAB = 180° − 90° − 25° = 65°       (angle sum of a triangle)
angle ACD = angle CBA = 25°              (angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment)
```

## Common mistakes

- Writing "angle at the centre is double" without saying "at the circumference".
- Applying the angle-at-centre theorem when the angle is not standing on the same arc.
- Forgetting that opposite angles in a cyclic quadrilateral are paired across the shape, not next to each other.
- Choosing the wrong segment for the alternate segment theorem.
- Not drawing in radii to make an isosceles triangle.
- Giving only the answer without the reason.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** and **give a reason** both need a fact written in words.
- **Show that** needs every step to be justified.
- Mark every right angle and equal radius on the diagram.
- Look for diameters, tangents and cyclic quadrilaterals before trying anything else.
