## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why Lyndon B. Johnson was able to pass the Great Society legislation in 1964 and 1965. **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract (constructed for practice).

> "The country was tired of lies. When the tapes were made public, the argument that the President was above the law could no longer be sustained, and even his own party told him to go." (Constructed for practice: a paraphrase of an editorial of August 1974.)

**(a)** Identify two reasons for Nixon's resignation suggested in the extract. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the extract helps to assess the effects of Watergate, and one limitation of using it alone. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish between the causes of the Quiet Revolution and its effects. **[3]**

**4.** Analyse the impact of Ronald Reagan's economic policies on the USA. **[6]**

**5.** Explain why the October Crisis of 1970 was significant in Canada. **[6]**

**6.** Examine the reasons for the growth of political division in the USA between 2001 and 2020. **[8]**

**7.** Discuss the achievements and failures of Brian Mulroney's governments (1984 to 1993). **[8]**

**8.** To what extent was the Truth and Reconciliation Commission (from 2008) a turning point in the relationship between the Canadian state and Indigenous peoples? **[8]**

**9.** Paper 3 style essay question: "Watergate was the most important political development in the USA between 1961 and 1977." To what extent do you agree? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Two developed points, 2 marks each. Political skill and context: Johnson's experience as Senate leader enabled him to pass bills through Congress [1], and the memory of Kennedy's assassination created public support for his programme [1]. Majorities: the 1964 landslide gave the Democrats large majorities in both houses [1], which allowed reforms to pass despite southern opposition [1]. Public mood and activism: the civil rights movement, including events at Birmingham and Selma, created pressure for the Civil Rights Act and the Voting Rights Act [1], and broad prosperity made expensive social programmes seem affordable [1]. **[4]**

**2. (a)** The release of the tapes, showing the President's involvement [1]; loss of support from his own party, and the principle that the President was not above the law [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It shows that Watergate was seen as a test of the rule of law and that it damaged trust in the presidency, supporting the view that it strengthened accountability [1], and that even party loyalty could not protect Nixon [1]. Limitation: an editorial states an opinion and may overstate the consensus [1]; it does not show longer effects such as the pardon controversy, cynicism, or reforms in campaign finance [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Causes: a church-dominated, rural society with English-speaking control of the economy; the rise of a new, educated French-speaking middle class; and Lesage's promise to become "masters in our own house" [1]. Effects: the state took over education and health, Hydro-Québec was nationalised, and society became more secular [1]. Longer effects included stronger Quebec nationalism, the Parti Québécois and separatism [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Tax cuts (1981 and 1986) reduced top rates sharply [1]. Deregulation and higher defence spending [1]. After the recession of 1981 to 1982, the economy grew and inflation fell, helped by high interest rates set by the Federal Reserve [2]. Deficits and debt rose [1]. Inequality widened as benefits went mostly to the wealthy [1]. **[6]**

**5.** The FLQ kidnapped James Cross and Pierre Laporte, and Laporte was killed [1]. Trudeau invoked the War Measures Act, sending troops to Quebec and arresting about 450 people [2]. It tested civil liberties: supporters said it was necessary, critics said it was excessive [1]. The FLQ lost support, which moved separatism towards elections and the Parti Québécois [1]. The Crisis shaped views of Trudeau and national unity [1]. **[6]**

**6.** Media: partisan news channels and social media create separate information environments [2]. Political structure: gerrymandering and primaries reward more extreme candidates [1]. Money: *Citizens United* (2010) and large donations [1]. Issues: the Iraq War, the 2008 crisis, health care, immigration and race fuelled anger [2]. Leaders: Tea Party and Trump's populist style [1]. Judgement on the main cause [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Achievements: Free Trade Agreement (1988), privatisations, the Goods and Services Tax as a reform of taxation, an unprecedented majority in 1984 [3]. Failures: the Meech Lake and Charlottetown Accords failed, deepening divisions [2]; the GST was unpopular; recession and deficits; the Progressive Conservative collapse in 1993 to two seats, with Reform and the Bloc taking its support [2]. Judgement: significant policy change at a high political cost [1]. **[8]**

**8.** Turning point: the 2008 apology and the TRC documented abuse in residential schools and set out 94 Calls to Action [2]; it gave a public record and a language for reconciliation [1]. Limits: implementation of the Calls to Action has been slow [2]; earlier developments, such as the 1990s Royal Commission on Aboriginal Peoples and the 2007 Settlement Agreement, had already begun the shift [2]. Judgement: significant in changing public understanding, but less so in material change [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Use the Paper 3 markbands. A top answer (13 to 15 marks) shows in-depth understanding of the argument, evaluates a range of perspectives and reaches a substantiated judgement. For Watergate (about 4 marks): the only presidential resignation, loss of trust in government, reforms to oversight and campaign finance, the effect on the 1974 and 1976 elections. Other developments (about 4 marks): the Great Society and civil rights legislation, the effects of Vietnam, the realignment of parties through the Southern strategy, détente. Perspectives (about 3 marks): views that Watergate showed institutions working (courts, press, Congress) and views that it deepened cynicism; those who see the social legislation of 1964 to 1965 as having longer effects. Judgement (about 4 marks): for example, that Watergate was significant for trust and institutions but less so than the Great Society and party realignment for the long-term shape of American politics. **[15]**
