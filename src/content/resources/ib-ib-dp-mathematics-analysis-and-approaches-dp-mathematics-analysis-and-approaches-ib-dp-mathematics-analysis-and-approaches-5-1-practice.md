rate of change** (SL content, examined at SL and HL). Section A is calculator-free apart from simple

## Section A

**1.** The table shows values of f(x) = (x³ − 1)/(x − 1).

| x | 0.9 | 0.99 | 1.01 | 1.1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| f(x) | 2.71 | 2.9701 | p | 3.31 |

**(a)** Find the value of p. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down an estimate of the limit of f(x) as x → 1. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why f(1) cannot be found by substitution. **[1]**

**2.** A function is defined by f(x) = x + 1 for x < 2, f(x) = 5 − x for x > 2, and f(2) = 1.

**(a)** Write down the limit of f(x) as x → 2. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether this limit equals f(2). **[1]**

**3.** f(x) = x² + 3x. Find the average rate of change of f between x = 1 and x = 3. **[2]**

**4.** The chord joining (2, 0.5) to a nearby point (2 + h, 1/(2 + h)) on y = 1/x has gradient g(h).
Some values are shown.

| h | 0.1 | 0.01 | 0.001 | −0.01 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| g(h) | −0.238095 | −0.248756 | −0.249875 | −0.251256 |

**(a)** Estimate the gradient of y = 1/x at x = 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the values with negative h are included. **[1]**

**5.** A is the area of a circular patch of oil, in m², and t is the time in hours. At t = 2,
dA/dt = 15.

**(a)** Write down the units of dA/dt. **[1]**

**(b)** Interpret dA/dt = 15 at t = 2. **[2]**

**6.** For a function f, f(4) = 10 and f′(4) = −3. State, with a reason, which of the two numbers
gives

**(a)** the y-coordinate of the point on the curve where x = 4; **[1]**

**(b)** information about whether the curve is rising or falling at x = 4, and say which. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Using a table of values on your GDC, with x in radians, estimate the limit of (sin x)/x as
x → 0. Show at least four values in your table. **[3]**

**8.** The temperature of a cup of coffee t minutes after it is poured is T = 20 + 70(0.9)ᵗ °C.

**(a)** Find the average rate of change of T over the first 10 minutes. **[2]**

**(b)** By calculating the gradient of a chord with h = 0.01, estimate the instantaneous rate of
change of T at t = 5. **[2]**

**(c)** Interpret your answer to (b) in context. **[1]**

**9.** A particle's displacement is s = 4t² − t³ metres after t seconds.

**(a)** Find the average velocity between t = 2 and t = 2 + h for h = 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence estimate the velocity at t = 2. **[1]**

**10.** The volume of a sphere of radius r cm is V = (4/3)πr³ cm³.

**(a)** Estimate dV/dr when r = 3, using chords with h = 0.1, 0.01 and 0.001. **[3]**

**(b)** State the units of dV/dr and interpret your answer. **[2]**

**11.** For f(x) = x³, the gradient of the chord from x to x + 0.001 was found at several points.

| x | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| chord gradient | 2.997 | 0.000 | 3.003 | 12.006 |

**(a)** Write down estimates of f′(−1), f′(0), f′(1) and f′(2). **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest an expression for the gradient function f′(x). **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the curve is never decreasing, using your answer to (b). **[1]**

**12.** The graph of y = g(x) passes through (1, 4), where its tangent has equation y = 2x + 2.

**(a)** Write down g(1) and g′(1). **[2]**

**(b)** A chord joins (1, 4) to the point on the curve where x = 1.1. Explain why its gradient is
likely to be close to 2. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** p = (1.01³ − 1)/0.01 = 0.030301/0.01 = 3.0301 [1]

**(b)** The values approach 3 from both sides, so the limit is 3 [1].

**(c)** Substituting x = 1 gives 0/0, which is undefined [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** From the left, f(x) → 2 + 1 = 3 [1]; from the right, f(x) → 5 − 2 = 3; so the limit is 3
[1].

**(b)** No: f(2) = 1, which is different from the limit 3 [1]. **[3]**

**3.** f(3) = 18 and f(1) = 4 [1]; average rate = (18 − 4)/(3 − 1) = 7 [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** The values approach −0.25 [1].

**(b)** To check that the chord gradients approach the same value from the left as from the right
[1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** m² per hour [1]

**(b)** At the instant t = 2 hours [1], the area of the patch is increasing at a rate of 15 m² per
hour [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** f(4) = 10, because f(4) is the output (height) of the function at x = 4 [1].

**(b)** f′(4) = −3, because the derivative is the gradient [1]; it is negative, so the curve is
falling at x = 4 [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Table values, for example x = 0.1: 0.998334; x = 0.01: 0.999983; x = −0.01: 0.999983;
x = −0.1: 0.998334 [2: values on both sides]. The limit is 1 [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** T(0) = 90 and T(10) = 44.41 [1]; average rate = (44.41 − 90)/10 = −4.56 °C per minute
[1].

**(b)** (T(5.01) − T(5))/0.01 [1] ≈ −4.35 °C per minute [1].

**(c)** Five minutes after pouring, the coffee is cooling at about 4.35 °C per minute [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** h = 0.1: 3.79 m s⁻¹ [1]; h = 0.01: 3.9799 m s⁻¹ [1]; h = 0.001: 3.997999 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** The averages approach 4, so the velocity at t = 2 is 4 m s⁻¹ [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** h = 0.1: 116.9; h = 0.01: 113.5; h = 0.001: 113.1 [2]; so dV/dr ≈ 113 at r = 3 [1].

**(b)** cm³ per cm [1]. When the radius is 3 cm, the volume is increasing at about 113 cm³ for each
extra centimetre of radius [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** f′(−1) ≈ 3, f′(0) ≈ 0, f′(1) ≈ 3, f′(2) ≈ 12 [2: all four correct; 1 for three]

**(b)** f′(x) = 3x² [1]

**(c)** 3x² ≥ 0 for every x, so the gradient is never negative [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** g(1) = 4 [1]; g′(1) = 2, the gradient of the tangent [1].

**(b)** As the second point moves towards (1, 4), chord gradients approach the tangent gradient 2,
and x = 1.1 is close to 1 [1]. **[3]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1, 2, 4 and 7 practise estimating limits from tables and from a piecewise definition.
Questions 3, 8 and 9 contrast average and instantaneous rates. Questions 5, 6, 10 and 12 test the
interpretation and notation of a derivative, and question 11 builds a gradient function from
individual gradients, which is the central idea of section 5.1.
