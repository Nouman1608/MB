These questions cover section **4.5.2 Work done and energy transfer** of the AQA GCSE Physics (8463)
specification, examined on Paper 2 at both tiers. Nothing here is Higher tier only. Some questions also use
the Topic 1 equations for kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy and power. A calculator is allowed
throughout; take g = 9.8 N/kg.

Learn the content first in the
[work done study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-work-done-and-energy-transfer-aqa/) and the
[work done revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-work-done-and-energy-transfer-aqa-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by work done on an object. **[1]**

**2.** Write down the equation that links work done, force and distance. **[1]**

**3.** Convert 3.5 kN m into joules. **[1]**

**4.** A child pulls a sledge 25 m along the line of an 18 N force. Calculate the work done. **[2]**

**5.** A person holds a parcel still at chest height. Explain why no work is done on the parcel. **[1]**

**6.** A block is dragged across a rough bench. Name the energy store that work done against friction
transfers energy to, and state its effect on the block. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A worker lifts a 25 kg box at a steady speed from the ground onto the floor of a lorry, 1.8 m higher.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the box. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done lifting the box. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the energy transfer that takes place as the box is lifted. **[2]**
**(d)** The worker then carries the box 6.0 m across the level floor of the lorry. Explain why the upward
force of his arms does no work on the box during this stage. **[1]**

**8.** A car of mass 1200 kg is travelling at 15 m/s. The driver brakes and the car stops in a distance of
45 m.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the car before braking. **[2]**
**(b)** Assume all of this energy is transferred by the work done by the brakes. Calculate the average
braking force. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the temperature of the brakes rises. **[2]**

**9.** A tractor pulls a plough 350 m across a field with a constant force of 12 kN.

**(a)** Calculate the work done. Give your answer in MJ. **[3]**
**(b)** The tractor takes 7.0 minutes. Calculate the power of the tractor in pulling the plough. **[2]**

**10.** A student pushes a trolley of weight 400 N up a ramp. The ramp is 4.0 m long and raises the trolley
through a height of 1.0 m. The push is 120 N, along the ramp.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the student's push. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gain in the trolley's gravitational potential energy. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between your answers to (a) and (b). **[2]**

**11.** A lift motor raises a lift of weight 7200 N through a height of 18 m at a steady speed.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the motor. **[2]**
**(b)** The lift takes 24 s. Calculate the power output of the motor. **[2]**

**12.** A space capsule returning to Earth enters the atmosphere at very high speed. Explain, in terms of
work done and energy stores, why the capsule needs a heat shield. **[3]**

**13.** A student writes: "work done = 50 N × 300 cm = 15 000 J". Identify the error and give the correct
answer. **[2]**

**14.** A weightlifter raises a bar of weight 1200 N through 2.0 m, then holds it still above her head for
3.0 s. Compare the work done on the bar in the two stages. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Work is done when a force causes an object to move through a distance (a displacement) [1]

**2.** Work done = force × distance (moved along the line of action of the force), W = F s [1]

**3.** **3500 J** [1]

**4.** W = 18 × 25 [1]; = **450 J** [1]

**5.** The parcel does not move, so there is no displacement [1]

**6.** The thermal energy store (of the block and bench) [1]; the block's temperature rises [1]

**7. (a)** W = 25 × 9.8 = **245 N** [1]
**(b)** W = 245 × 1.8 [1]; = 441 J, about **440 J** [1]
**(c)** Energy is transferred from the chemical energy store of the worker [1]; to the gravitational
potential energy store of the box [1]
**(d)** The box moves horizontally, at right angles to the upward force, so it does not move along that
force's line of action [1]

**8. (a)** Eₖ = ½ × 1200 × 15² [1]; = **135 000 J** [1]
**(b)** F = W / s = 135 000 / 45 [1]; = **3000 N** [1]
**(c)** Friction between the brakes and the wheel does work as the car slows [1]; energy is transferred
from the kinetic store of the car to the thermal energy store of the brakes, so their temperature rises [1]

**9. (a)** F = 12 000 N [1]; W = 12 000 × 350 = 4 200 000 J [1]; = **4.2 MJ** [1]
**(b)** t = 7.0 × 60 = 420 s [1]; P = 4 200 000 / 420 = **10 000 W** (10 kW) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Leaving the time in minutes gives 600 000 W, a common error in power questions.

**10. (a)** W = 120 × 4.0 [1]; = **480 J** [1]
**(b)** Eₚ = weight × height = 400 × 1.0 [1]; = **400 J** [1]
**(c)** 480 − 400 = 80 J of the work done is not stored as gravitational potential energy [1]; it is work
done against friction, transferred to the thermal stores of the trolley, ramp and surroundings [1]
*Examiner insight:* The push acts along the ramp, so the distance in (a) is the ramp length, not the height.

**11. (a)** W = 7200 × 18 [1]; = 129 600 J, about **1.3 × 10⁵ J** [1]
**(b)** P = 129 600 / 24 [1]; = **5400 W** [1]

**12.** Air resistance acts on the capsule, opposing its motion [1]; work is done against air resistance,
transferring energy from the capsule's kinetic energy store to the thermal energy store of the capsule and
the air [1]; the capsule's surface temperature rises greatly, so a heat shield is needed to protect the
inside [1]

**13.** The distance has not been converted to metres: 300 cm = 3.0 m [1]; W = 50 × 3.0 = **150 J** [1]

**14.** Raising: W = 1200 × 2.0 = **2400 J** of work is done on the bar [1]; holding: **0 J**, because the bar
does not move, so there is no displacement [1]; she tires while holding because her muscles transfer
energy internally, but none of it is work done on the bar [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the vertical height with a force that acts along a ramp, or the ramp length with a weight.
- Not converting kN, cm or minutes.
- "Energy is lost to friction" with no store named.
- Rounding too early in multi-step calculations.
- Saying work is done whenever effort is felt.
