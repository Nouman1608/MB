These notes condense section 5.2, Depreciation, from Topic 5 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. Topic 5 is examined in Paper 2, where depreciation arrives as a note under a trial balance. The worked examples are in the [depreciation study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-depreciation-4ac1-eop/), and exam-style questions are in the [depreciation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-4ac1-depreciation-4ac1-eop-practice/). Amounts are in dollars without a sign; all figures are invented.

## The four outcomes

- 5.2 a: explain the causes of depreciation.
- 5.2 b: distinguish between straight line and reducing balance.
- 5.2 c: calculate and record depreciation in the books of account.
- 5.2 d: calculate and record profit or loss on disposal of non-current assets.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Depreciation | The part of a non-current asset's cost charged as an expense for a period of use |
| Cost | Price plus everything needed to bring the asset into use |
| Residual value | The amount expected when the business finishes with the asset |
| Provision for depreciation | Credit balance holding depreciation to date; the asset account stays at cost |
| Carrying value | Cost less depreciation to date |
| Disposal account | Account that collects cost, depreciation to date and proceeds of an asset sold |
| Profit or loss on disposal | Proceeds less carrying value at the date of sale |

## Trial balance rules

1. Asset at cost (debit) and provision for depreciation (credit) are both opening positions for depreciation. This year's charge is still to be made.
2. Straight line base: cost, or cost less residual value, as the note says.
3. Reducing balance base: cost less the provision in the trial balance.
4. Charge goes to the income statement as an expense.
5. New provision = trial balance provision + charge.
6. Statement of financial position: cost, depreciation to date, carrying value, for each class and in total.

Reminder: machinery cost 50,000, provision 18,000, 25% reducing balance. Charge = 25% × 32,000 = 8,000. New provision 26,000; carrying value 24,000.

## Causes, matched to assets

| Cause | Fits | Say why |
|---|---|---|
| Wear and tear | Vans, machines, furniture | Use damages the asset |
| Obsolescence | Computers, specialist equipment | Better models make it outdated |
| Passage of time | A lease for a fixed number of years | Value runs out as the years pass |

A 5.2 a answer names the cause, links it to the asset in the case, and says it is why part of the cost is charged each year.

## Choosing a method

- Straight line: equal charge; suits assets giving equal benefit each year.
- Reducing balance: high early charges; suits assets that lose usefulness quickly or need more repairs later.
- Over the whole life, both charge cost less residual value if the rate is set to reach it. The method changes the timing of profit, not the total.
- Once chosen, keep it (consistency).

## Part-year policies

Follow the question's note:

- Full year in year of purchase, none in year of sale.
- Monthly: charge × months owned ÷ 12.
- Only on assets held at the year end.

Reminder (monthly, 10% on cost): a machine costing 9,600 bought on 1 May with a 31 December year end owned 8 months, so the charge is 9,600 × 10% × 8/12 = 640.

## Disposal routine

1. Charge any depreciation for the year of sale first (if the policy requires it).
2. Debit disposal, credit asset at cost (cost).
3. Debit provision for depreciation, credit disposal (all depreciation to date on that asset).
4. Debit bank, credit disposal (proceeds).
5. Balance the account: a balancing figure needed on the debit side is a profit; one needed on the credit side is a loss. Transfer it to the income statement.
6. Take the sold asset's cost out before calculating a full-year charge on the rest.

Reminder: cost 15,000, depreciation to date 9,600, sold for 6,100. Carrying value 5,400, so a profit of 700.

## Effects to state

| Item | Profit | Assets | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|
| Depreciation charge | Down | Carrying value down | No change |
| Profit on disposal | Up | Asset removed | Up by proceeds |
| Loss on disposal | Down | Asset removed | Up by proceeds |
| Charge omitted (error) | Overstated | Overstated | No change |

## Exam traps

- Taking a reducing balance percentage of cost in a later year because the trial balance lists cost.
- Leaving the opening provision as the closing one.
- Giving a full year's charge to an asset bought mid-year when the note says monthly.
- Transferring only last year's depreciation on a sold asset, missing the year-of-sale charge.
- Writing the profit on disposal on the credit side of the disposal account.
- Saying depreciation is cash set aside to replace an asset.

## Self-test

1. Which two trial balance figures give the reducing balance base?
2. Vehicles cost 40,000; provision 22,400; 20% reducing balance. Calculate the charge.
3. Fixtures cost 18,000, residual value 2,000, life 8 years. Calculate the straight line charge.
4. Name the cause of depreciation for a five-year lease.
5. Equipment costing 7,200 is bought on 1 October; 10% on cost, monthly; year end 31 December. Calculate the charge.
6. An asset cost 12,000; depreciation to date is 7,500; it is sold for 3,900. State the profit or loss.
7. In the disposal account, which side carries a loss as the balancing figure?
8. A depreciation charge of 3,600 is omitted. State the effect on profit and on carrying value.
9. Why does depreciation not change the bank balance?
10. A business charges a full year in the year of purchase, none in the year of sale. A machine bought on 1 November costs 6,000 at 15% on cost. What is the charge for that year?
11. Which concept is broken by switching method to raise one year's profit?

### Answers

1. Asset at cost and the provision for depreciation (cost less provision).
2. 20% × (40,000 − 22,400) = 20% × 17,600 = 3,520.
3. (18,000 − 2,000) ÷ 8 = 2,000.
4. Passage of time.
5. 7,200 × 10% × 3/12 = 180.
6. Carrying value 12,000 − 7,500 = 4,500; proceeds 3,900; loss of 600.
7. The credit side.
8. Profit overstated by 3,600; carrying value overstated by 3,600.
9. It is an allocation of cost already paid; no money leaves the business when it is charged.
10. A full year: 6,000 × 15% = 900.
11. Consistency.
