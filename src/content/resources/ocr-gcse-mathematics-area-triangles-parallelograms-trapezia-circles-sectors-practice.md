These questions cover section 10.03 (Area calculations) of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): 10.03a to 10.03e. Questions 11 and 12 are Higher tier only; the rest are Foundation and Higher content. Diagrams are described in words, so sketch each one before you start. Round answers to 3 significant figures unless a question gives other instructions.

## Questions

**1.** (a) A triangle has base 15 cm and perpendicular height 7.6 cm. Calculate its area. **[2]**
(b) Another triangle has area 54 cm² and base 12 cm. Calculate its perpendicular height. **[2]**

**2.** Parallelogram ABCD has AB = 14 cm and AD = 8.5 cm. The perpendicular distance between AB and DC is 7.2 cm.
(a) Calculate the area of the parallelogram. **[2]**
(b) A rectangle has the same area and a width of 9 cm. Calculate its length. **[2]**

**3.** (a) A trapezium has parallel sides of 8.4 cm and 13.6 cm, which are 5 cm apart. Calculate its area. **[2]**
(b) A different trapezium has parallel sides of 12 cm and 18 cm and an area of 105 cm². Calculate the distance between its parallel sides. **[3]**

**4.** (a) A circle has diameter 15.2 cm. Calculate its area. **[3]**
(b) A second circle has area 50 cm². Calculate its radius. **[3]**

**5.** A circular pond has radius 4 m. A path of width 1.5 m goes all the way round it. Find the area of the path, first in terms of π and then to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**6.** (a) A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 80°. Find its area in terms of π. **[2]**
(b) Another sector has angle 210° and area 21π cm². Find its radius. **[3]**

**7.** A window is a rectangle 1.8 m wide and 1.2 m high, with a semicircle on top. The diameter of the semicircle is the 1.8 m top edge of the rectangle. Glass costs £48 per square metre. Find the cost of the glass for the whole window, to the nearest penny. **[5]**

**8.** ABCD is a square of side 14 cm. A quarter circle, centre A and radius 14 cm, passes through B and D. Find the area of the part of the square that is outside the quarter circle. **[4]**

**9.** A plot of land is a trapezium. Its parallel sides are 32 m and 20 m, and they are 14 m apart. A triangular corner of the plot, with base 8 m and perpendicular height 5 m, is paved. The rest is lawn. One box of lawn seed covers 40 m². How many boxes are needed to cover the lawn? **[5]**

**10.** O is the centre of a circle of radius 14 cm. Points P and Q are on the circle with angle POQ = 90°. Find the area of the region between the chord PQ and the arc PQ. **[4]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) In triangle XYZ, XY = 12.4 cm, XZ = 9.8 cm and angle YXZ = 38°.
(a) Calculate the area of the triangle. **[3]**
(b) Another triangle has sides of 11 cm and 8.5 cm, and an area of 41 cm². The angle between those two sides is not stated to be acute or obtuse. Find both possible sizes of that angle, to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A circle has centre C and radius 11 cm. Points M and N are on the circle with angle MCN = 80°. Calculate the area of the region between the chord MN and the arc MN. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Area = ½ × 15 × 7.6 [1] = **57 cm²** [1]
(b) 54 = ½ × 12 × h = 6h [1], so h = **9 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the formula with numbers substituted before the answer. Dividing 54 by 12 and stopping at 4.5 forgets that the 54 is already half of base × height.

**2.** (a) Area = base × perpendicular height = 14 × 7.2 [1] = **100.8 cm²** [1]
(b) Length = 100.8 ÷ 9 [1] = **11.2 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The 8.5 cm side is slanted and is not needed. Using it in place of the perpendicular distance is a very common slip.

**3.** (a) Area = ½ × (8.4 + 13.6) × 5 [1] = **55 cm²** [1]
(b) 105 = ½ × (12 + 18) × h [1], so 105 = 15h [1], h = **7 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Add the two parallel sides before multiplying by the height. Showing the sum 12 + 18 makes your method easy to follow.

**4.** (a) Radius = 15.2 ÷ 2 = 7.6 [1]. Area = π × 7.6² [1] = 181.45... = **181 cm²** [1]
(b) πr² = 50 [1], r² = 50 ÷ π = 15.91... [1], r = **3.99 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Halving the diameter is the step most often missed in part (a). In part (b), undo the square with a square root only after dividing by π.

**5.** Outer radius = 4 + 1.5 = 5.5 m [1]
Path = π × 5.5² − π × 4² [1] = 30.25π − 16π = **14.25π m²** [1]
= 44.76... = **44.8 m²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the two circle areas. Squaring the width of the path (1.5) does not give its area.

**6.** (a) Area = 80/360 × π × 9² [1] = 2/9 × 81π = **18π cm²** [1]
(b) 210/360 × πr² = 21π [1], so 7/12 × r² = 21 [1], r² = 36, r = **6 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The factor π appears on both sides in part (b), so it cancels. Keeping the fraction 210/360 as 7/12 avoids decimal rounding.

**7.** Rectangle = 1.8 × 1.2 = 2.16 m² [1]
Semicircle radius = 0.9 m, so area = ½ × π × 0.9² [1] = 1.272... m² [1]
Total area = 3.432... m² [1]
Cost = 3.432... × 48 = **£164.75** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded area in your calculator for the cost. Using 3.43 first gives £164.64, which is a penny-level slip when the question asks for the nearest penny.

**8.** Square = 14² = 196 cm² [1]
Quarter circle = ¼ × π × 14² = 49π = 153.93... [1]
Required area = 196 − 153.93... [1] = **42.1 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say clearly which area is being subtracted from which. Getting 153.9 − 196 gives a negative area, which is a signal to reverse the subtraction.

**9.** Trapezium = ½ × (32 + 20) × 14 [1] = 364 m² [1]
Triangle = ½ × 8 × 5 = 20 m² [1]
Lawn = 364 − 20 = 344 m² [1]
344 ÷ 40 = 8.6, so **9 boxes** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Round up for a quantity that must cover the whole area. Writing 8.6 as the final answer or rounding to 9 without the division shown leaves the reasoning unclear.

**10.** Sector = ¼ × π × 14² = 49π = 153.93... cm² [1]
Triangle OPQ is right-angled at O, so area = ½ × 14 × 14 = 98 cm² [1]
Region = 49π − 98 [1] = **55.9 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two radii are the base and height of the triangle because they meet at 90°. No extra length is needed.

**11.** (a) Area = ½ × 12.4 × 9.8 × sin 38° [1] = 60.76 × 0.6156... [1] = **37.4 cm²** [1]
(b) 41 = ½ × 11 × 8.5 × sin C [1], so sin C = 82 ÷ 93.5 = 0.877... [1]
C = **61.3°** [1] or 180° − 61.3° = **118.7°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the angle that sits between the two sides you multiply. When no diagram fixes the angle, give the obtuse value as well as the acute one from your calculator.

**12.** Sector = 80/360 × π × 11² [1] = 84.47... cm² [1]
Triangle MCN = ½ × 11 × 11 × sin 80° [1] = 59.58... cm² [1]
Region = 84.47... − 59.58... [1] = **24.9 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* This question joins three ideas: a sector, ½ab sin C and subtraction. Writing each part with its own heading keeps the working easy to follow, and keeping unrounded values avoids a slip in the last digit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a slanting side in place of the perpendicular height.
- Forgetting the ½ in a triangle or trapezium.
- Using the diameter as the radius in πr².
- Rounding π or a sub-total early, so the last digit of the answer changes.
- Not reading whether the answer should be in terms of π or rounded.
- Subtracting in the wrong order when finding a path, ring or cut-out area.
- Leaving off the unit, or writing cm instead of cm².
- Stopping at 8.6 boxes, tins or bags when a whole number is needed.
- Higher tier only: giving only the acute angle when a sine value has two possible angles.
