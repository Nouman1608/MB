## Key definitions

- **Arene / aromatic compound:** contains a benzene ring.
- **Delocalised electrons:** shared by more than two atoms; in benzene, six π-electrons spread round the ring.
- **π-system:** the region formed by sideways overlap of p-orbitals, above and below the ring plane.
- **Electrophile:** an electron pair acceptor (NO₂⁺, Br⁺, R⁺, RCO⁺).
- **Electrophilic substitution:** an electrophile replaces a hydrogen on the ring; the ring's delocalisation is kept.
- **Halogen carrier:** Fe, FeBr₃, FeCl₃, AlCl₃ or AlBr₃; it generates the stronger electrophile.
- **Directing group:** a substituent that decides which ring position the next group takes.

## 6.1.1(a): Kekulé versus delocalised

| Feature | Kekulé model | Delocalised model |
|---|---|---|
| Bonds in ring | 3 C=C and 3 C–C, alternating | 6 identical bonds |
| π-electrons | Localised in three π-bonds | Delocalised over six carbons |
| Shape | Flat ring | Flat ring, 120° angles |
| Predicted reactions | Electrophilic addition, like alkenes | Substitution; resists addition |

How the π-system forms: each carbon uses three electrons in σ-bonds and has one electron in a p-orbital. Adjacent p-orbitals overlap sideways all round the ring.

## 6.1.1(b): Three pieces of evidence

1. **Bond lengths:** all six equal, between C–C and C=C.
2. **Hydrogenation:** less exothermic than three times the value for cyclohexene, so benzene is more stable than Kekulé predicts.
3. **Resistance to reaction:** no electrophilic addition with bromine at room temperature.

Method for the hydrogenation comparison: multiply the cyclohexene value by three, then subtract it from the measured benzene value. The positive difference is the extra stability.

## 6.1.1(c): Naming in four rules

1. Prefix + benzene: bromobenzene, 1,4-dichlorobenzene.
2. Parent names: methylbenzene, phenol, phenylamine, benzoic acid. The parent group is on carbon 1.
3. Lowest locants; prefixes in alphabetical order.
4. Ring as a substituent: phenyl (e.g. phenylethanone).

Check: 2,4-dinitromethylbenzene has CH₃ on carbon 1 and NO₂ on carbons 2 and 4.

## 6.1.1(d): Reactions to learn

| Reaction | Reagents | Conditions | Electrophile | Product with benzene |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nitration | Conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ | About 50 °C | NO₂⁺ | Nitrobenzene + H₂O |
| Bromination | Br₂ | Halogen carrier, room temp | Br⁺ | Bromobenzene + HBr |
| Chlorination | Cl₂ | Halogen carrier, room temp | Cl⁺ | Chlorobenzene + HCl |
| Alkylation | Haloalkane, e.g. CH₃Cl | AlCl₃ | R⁺ | Alkylbenzene + HCl |
| Acylation | Acyl chloride, e.g. C₂H₅COCl | AlCl₃ | RCO⁺ | Phenyl ketone + HCl |

Friedel–Crafts reactions form a new C–C bond to the ring, so they are used to build carbon skeletons in synthesis (see 6.2.4).

## 6.1.1(e): Mechanism in steps

**Form the electrophile.**

- HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O
- Br₂ + FeBr₃ → Br⁺ + FeBr₄⁻ (or Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻)

**Three arrows on the ring.**

1. Arrow from the π-system (circle) to the electrophile.
2. Intermediate: broken horseshoe over five carbons, + inside, H and E on the sixth carbon.
3. C–H electron pair returns to the ring (arrow); H⁺ is lost.

**Regenerate the catalyst.**

- H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → H₂SO₄
- H⁺ + FeBr₄⁻ → FeBr₃ + HBr

## 6.1.1(f): Benzene versus alkene with bromine

| | Alkene (e.g. cyclohexene) | Benzene |
|---|---|---|
| π-electrons | Localised in one C=C | Delocalised over the ring |
| Electron density | High between two carbons | Lower at any one bond |
| Effect on Br₂ | Induces a dipole; Br₂ reacts | Cannot polarise Br₂ enough |
| Result | Bromine water decolourised | No reaction without a halogen carrier |

## 6.1.1(g): Unfamiliar reactions

Spot the δ+ or positive species in the reagents. That is the electrophile. Use the same three arrows. The ring loses one H⁺. Read any extra information the question gives you, such as which atom carries the δ+ charge.

## 6.1.1(h): Phenol is a weak acid (PAG7)

- C₆H₅OH ⇌ C₆H₅O⁻ + H⁺ (partial dissociation)
- With NaOH(aq): C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O
- With Na₂CO₃(aq): no reaction, no effervescence

| Test | Ethanol | Phenol | Carboxylic acid |
|---|---|---|---|
| NaOH(aq) | No reaction | Reacts (dissolves) | Reacts |
| Na₂CO₃(aq) | No reaction | No reaction | Effervescence (CO₂) |

## 6.1.1(i): Reactions of phenol

| Reagent | Conditions | Observation | Product(s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bromine water | Room temperature, no carrier | Decolourised; white precipitate | 2,4,6-tribromophenol + 3HBr |
| Dilute HNO₃ | Room temperature, no H₂SO₄ | Mixture of two isomers forms | 2-nitrophenol and 4-nitrophenol + H₂O |

Equations: C₆H₅OH + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃OH + 3HBr; C₆H₅OH + HNO₃ → C₆H₄(OH)NO₂ + H₂O.

## 6.1.1(j): Why phenol reacts faster

Oxygen lone pair (in a p-orbital) → partly delocalised into the ring → ring electron density increases → ring more susceptible to electrophilic attack → Br₂ polarised without a carrier.

Do not explain it through the stability of the intermediate.

## 6.1.1(k) and (l): Directing effects

| Existing group | Effect on ring | New group goes to |
|---|---|---|
| –OH | Electron-donating | 2 and 4 (and 6) |
| –NH₂ | Electron-donating | 2 and 4 (and 6) |
| –NO₂ | Electron-withdrawing | 3 (and 5) |

Positions 2 and 6 are equivalent, as are 3 and 5. Any other group's effect will be supplied in the question.

Synthesis rule: put the directing group on first if you want its pattern. Nitrate before you halogenate to get a 3-substituted nitro compound.

## Small worked reminders

- Nitrating nitrobenzene gives 1,3-dinitrobenzene.
- Bromine water with phenylamine gives 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine and 3HBr.
- 3-nitrophenol cannot be made by nitrating phenol, because OH sends NO₂ to 2 and 4.
- Mole ratio phenol : Br₂ is 1 : 3; phenol : NaOH is 1 : 1.
- Benzene with chloromethane and AlCl₃ gives methylbenzene, C₆H₅CH₃, and HCl: a new C–C bond to the ring.
- Iodination with ICl: chlorine is more electronegative, so iodine is δ+ and becomes the electrophile; the product is iodobenzene.
- Phenol and dilute HNO₃ give two isomers, so the products must be separated before either is used in a later step.

For the contrast with electrophilic addition to C=C, revisit [alkenes for OCR](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-alkenes-revision-notes/).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Addition vs substitution:** alkenes add Br₂; benzene substitutes Br.
- **Concentrated vs dilute HNO₃:** benzene needs concentrated HNO₃ and H₂SO₄; phenol needs dilute HNO₃ only.
- **Phenol vs carboxylic acid:** both react with NaOH; only the acid reacts with carbonate.
- **Alkylation vs acylation:** haloalkane gives an alkylbenzene; acyl chloride gives a phenyl ketone.
- **2,4- vs 3-directing:** OH and NH₂ donate electron density; NO₂ withdraws it.

## Quick self-test

1. State what overlaps to form the π-system in benzene.
2. Give the bond angle in benzene.
3. Name the compound with CH₃ on carbon 1 and Cl on carbon 3 of the ring.
4. Write the equation for forming NO₂⁺.
5. Name a halogen carrier for chlorination.
6. Give the organic product of benzene with ethanoyl chloride and AlCl₃.
7. Why does cyclohexene decolourise bromine water without a catalyst?
8. What do you see when phenol is added to sodium carbonate solution?
9. Calculate the volume of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ NaOH that reacts with 2.35 g of phenol (Mr 94.0).
10. Predict the product when 4-methylphenol reacts with excess bromine water. How many moles of Br₂ react per mole?
11. Which position does bromine take when nitrobenzene is brominated with FeBr₃?
12. Why does phenol react with bromine without a halogen carrier?

### Answers

1. p-orbitals on adjacent carbons overlap sideways (one electron from each carbon).
2. 120°.
3. 3-chloromethylbenzene.
4. HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O.
5. AlCl₃ (or FeCl₃, or iron).
6. Phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃ (plus HCl).
7. Its localised π-bond has high electron density, which polarises Br₂.
8. No effervescence; no visible reaction.
9. n = 2.35 ÷ 94.0 = 0.0250 mol; volume = 0.0250 dm³ = **25.0 cm³**.
10. 2,6-dibromo-4-methylphenol; position 4 is already taken, so **2 mol** Br₂ react.
11. Position 3: 1-bromo-3-nitrobenzene.
12. An oxygen p-orbital lone pair is partly donated into the ring, raising its electron density so it polarises Br₂.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing p-orbitals overlapping "end-on" or forming only three π-bonds.
- Quoting hydrogenation evidence without saying benzene is **more stable** than predicted.
- Naming with locants that are not the lowest set, or ignoring alphabetical order.
- Missing "concentrated" for nitration of benzene, or using it for phenol.
- Starting the first curly arrow on a single carbon instead of the π-system.
- Drawing the intermediate's horseshoe over all six carbons or omitting its + charge.
- Forgetting to regenerate H₂SO₄ or FeBr₃.
- Writing "benzene has no double bonds so it can't react" instead of comparing electron density.
- Predicting 2,4,6-substitution for phenol when position 4 is already occupied.
- Treating Na₂CO₃ as a test that phenol passes.
