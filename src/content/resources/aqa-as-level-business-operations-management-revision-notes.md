## Always-on prompts

For any operations case study, ask five questions the specification tells you to consider: how does technology affect this decision, what are the ethical and environmental influences, what do market conditions and competition require, how does the decision make the business more competitive, and how does it affect marketing, finance or people?

## 3.4.1 Operational objectives

| Objective | Meaning | Typical measure |
|---|---|---|
| Costs | Keep unit costs low | Unit cost |
| Quality | Meet customer expectations | Returns, complaints, defect rate |
| Speed of response and flexibility | Fast orders; cope with change | Lead time, order turnaround |
| Dependability | Deliver as promised | Percentage of deliveries on time |
| Environmental | Reduce waste, energy and emissions | Waste per unit, energy use |
| Added value | Selling price less the cost of bought-in inputs | Pounds or percentage of price |

- Value of setting objectives: direction, a yardstick for performance, a basis for decisions, support for business objectives.
- Internal influences: finance, staff skills, equipment, other objectives.
- External influences: competitors, customers, technology, suppliers, legislation, environmental concern.
- Objectives can clash (low cost against high quality), so a judgement is needed.

## 3.4.2 Operations data

| Measure | Standard form |
|---|---|
| Labour productivity | Output ÷ number of employees |
| Unit cost (average cost) | Total costs ÷ output |
| Capacity | Maximum output in a period |
| Capacity utilisation | Actual output ÷ capacity × 100 |

**Method in steps.**

1. Write the formula.
2. Check the time period matches (a week for both output and capacity).
3. Substitute and calculate.
4. Add units: items per employee, pounds per item, per cent.
5. Say what the figure means or what has changed.

**Small worked reminder.** A workshop makes 7,200 units with 18 staff at a total cost of £28,800. Capacity is 9,000 units. Productivity = 7,200 ÷ 18 = 400 per employee. Unit cost = £28,800 ÷ 7,200 = £4.00. Utilisation = 7,200 ÷ 9,000 = 80%.

Use of data: supports decisions on hiring, equipment, suppliers and orders. Limits: out of date, hides causes, needs a benchmark.

## 3.4.3 Efficiency and productivity

- **Capacity** limits how much can be sold. **Efficiency** is output compared with resources used. **Labour productivity** affects unit cost and competitiveness.
- **Increase productivity:** training, motivation, better equipment, workflow changes, less waste, technology.
- **Difficulties:** costs before gains, staff resistance, little left to gain, pressure on quality, extra output cannot be sold.
- **Use capacity efficiently:** win orders, share or rent spare capacity, subcontract, cut surplus capacity. Full capacity leaves no slack.

### Lean production and Just in Time (JIT)

| | Points |
|---|---|
| Lean production | Cut waste in time, materials, stock and effort |
| JIT | Materials arrive as needed; goods made to meet orders; little inventory |
| Benefits | Lower holding costs; less storage; less cash tied up; less obsolete stock |
| Difficulties | Needs reliable suppliers; delays stop output; no cover for demand spikes; lost bulk discounts; needs trained staff |

### Resource mix

| | Labour intensive | Capital intensive |
|---|---|---|
| Suits | Low volume, bespoke work | High volume, repetitive work |
| Cost pattern | Lower fixed, higher variable | Higher fixed, lower variable |
| Flexibility | Easier to change product | Costly to change |
| Risk | Staff costs rise | High cost if demand falls |

Technology: automation, computer-aided design and manufacture, robots and online links with customers and suppliers. Gains: speed and fewer errors. Costs: investment, training, system failure, job losses.

## 3.4.4 Quality

- Importance: repeat sales, reputation, price premium, fewer returns.
- **Quality assurance** builds quality into every stage to prevent faults. This is different from checking finished goods to catch them.
- Other methods: training, better materials and equipment, customer feedback.
- Benefits: fewer complaints and less waste. Difficulties: cost, time, staff resistance, customers may not pay more.
- Consequences of poor quality: returns, wasted materials, lost sales, damaged reputation, compensation, low morale.

## 3.4.5 Inventory and supply chains

### Flexibility, speed, dependability

- **Mass customisation:** many items made at scale, each tailored to the customer. Needs flexible equipment and information systems.
- Speed wins impatient customers. Dependability builds trust.

### Matching supply to demand

| Method | Gain | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Outsourcing | Extra capacity at peaks | Less control |
| Temporary and part-time staff | Staffing follows demand | Training, lower commitment |
| Producing to order | Little unsold stock | Customers wait |

### Inventory control formulas and terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lead time | Time from placing an order to receiving it |
| Buffer level | Minimum stock held for delays or demand rises |
| Re-order level | Stock level that triggers an order (standard form: daily usage × lead time + buffer) |
| Re-order quantity | Amount ordered each time |

Chart reading: stock falls at the rate of use; an order is placed at the re-order level; stock jumps when the delivery arrives; maximum stock is buffer plus re-order quantity (when the delivery arrives just as stock reaches the buffer). Steeper line means faster use.

**Small worked reminder.** Usage 30 a day, lead time 5 days, buffer 90, order 300. Re-order level = 30 × 5 + 90 = 240. Maximum = 90 + 300 = 390. Cycle = 300 ÷ 30 = 10 days.

### Suppliers, supply chain, outsourcing

- Choice of supplier: price, quality, reliability, speed, flexibility, payment terms, location, ethics and environment.
- Supply chain management: share information, build relationships, monitor performance, use technology. Value: lower cost, fewer delays, faster response.
- Outsourcing: expertise, lower cost, focus on core work. Risks: less control, dependence, reputation.

## Case-study technique

Use one chain on each point: state the idea, apply it to the named business with a figure from the case, explain the effect on cost, quality, speed or reputation, then weigh it against a limit or alternative. For a calculation question, show the formula and working. For "evaluate" or "justify", end with a decision and the condition it depends on, such as the level of demand at which two methods cost the same, or whether the supplier can be trusted to deliver. Link to other functions: a faster delivery promise needs staff from people management and cash from finance.

## Must-know distinctions

- Productivity (output per worker) versus production (total output).
- Capacity (maximum possible) versus output (actual).
- Efficiency (use of resources) versus effectiveness (meeting the goal).
- Quality assurance (prevent) versus checking at the end (detect).
- Labour intensive versus capital intensive.
- Buffer level versus re-order level.
- JIT (little stock held) versus holding a safety stock.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the six operational objectives in the specification.
2. A bakery makes 5,400 pies with a capacity of 6,000. Calculate capacity utilisation.
3. A team of 15 makes 6,900 items. Calculate labour productivity.
4. Output per worker rises from 400 to 460. Calculate the percentage change.
5. A firm sells an item for £85 using £52 of bought-in materials. Calculate the added value.
6. Total costs are £31,500 for 4,500 units. Calculate unit cost.
7. Usage is 30 a day, lead time 5 days, buffer 90. Calculate the re-order level.
8. Output of 7,650 is 85% of capacity. Calculate capacity.
9. State one benefit and one difficulty of Just in Time.
10. Give two ways a business can match supply to demand at a seasonal peak.
11. What is the difference between quality assurance and checking finished goods?

### Answers

1. Costs, quality, speed of response and flexibility, dependability, environmental objectives, added value.
2. 5,400 ÷ 6,000 × 100 = **90%**.
3. 6,900 ÷ 15 = **460 items per employee**.
4. (460 − 400) ÷ 400 × 100 = **15% increase**.
5. £85 − £52 = **£33**, which is 38.8% of the price.
6. £31,500 ÷ 4,500 = **£7.00**.
7. 30 × 5 + 90 = **240**.
8. 7,650 ÷ 0.85 = **9,000**.
9. Benefit: lower holding costs. Difficulty: any supplier delay stops production.
10. Any two of outsourcing, temporary or part-time staff, producing to order.
11. Quality assurance prevents faults throughout the process; checking finished goods only finds faults after they have been made.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a formula and no workings, or leaving out the units.
- Mixing time periods, such as monthly output with weekly capacity.
- Calculating unit cost from variable costs only.
- Forgetting the buffer in the re-order level, or reading maximum stock off the chart wrongly.
- Describing JIT or lean production without applying it to the business in the case.
- Listing benefits of improving quality with no reference to the cost of doing so.
- Calling a rise in productivity good without asking whether the extra output can be sold.
- Evaluation that ends with "it depends" and no reasoned decision.
