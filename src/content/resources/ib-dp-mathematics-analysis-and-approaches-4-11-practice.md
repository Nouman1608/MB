## Section A

**1.** P(A ∩ B) = 0.18 and P(B) = 0.45. Find P(A | B). **[2]**

**2.** P(A) = 0.35, P(B) = 0.6 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.21. Determine whether A and B are independent. **[2]**

**3.** P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.4 and P(A | B) = 0.25.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[1]**

**(b)** Find P(B | A). **[1]**

**(c)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[1]**

**(d)** Find P(A | B′). **[2]**

**4.** P(A) = 0.3 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.65. Find P(B) if

**(a)** A and B are mutually exclusive; **[1]**

**(b)** A and B are independent. **[3]**

**5.** A fair six-sided die is rolled once. A is the event "the score is even" and B is the event "the
score is at most 4". Show that A and B are independent. **[3]**

**6.** Prove that if A and B are independent events, then A′ and B′ are also independent. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** In a trial, 200 patients received either a new treatment or a placebo.

| | Improved | Not improved | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Treatment | 78 | 42 | 120 |
| Placebo | 36 | 44 | 80 |
| Total | 114 | 86 | 200 |

A patient is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that the patient improved. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that the patient improved, given that they received the treatment. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the probability that the patient received the treatment, given that they improved. **[2]**

**(d)** Determine whether "improved" and "received the treatment" are independent events. **[2]**

**8.** A bag holds 4 red and 6 green counters. Two counters are taken at random without replacement.
R₁ is the event "the first counter is red" and R₂ is "the second counter is red".

**(a)** Write down P(R₂ | R₁). **[1]**

**(b)** Find P(R₂). **[2]**

**(c)** Find P(R₁ | R₂). **[2]**

**(d)** Determine whether R₁ and R₂ are independent. **[2]**

**9.** On a Venn diagram, P(A ∩ B′) = 0.2, P(A ∩ B) = x and P(A′ ∩ B) = 0.3. A and B are
independent.

**(a)** Show that x² − 0.5x + 0.06 = 0. **[3]**

**(b)** Solve the equation to find the two possible values of x. **[2]**

**(c)** Given also that P(A′ ∩ B′) = 0.3, find x and hence find P(B | A). **[2]**

**10.** Two fair six-sided dice are rolled.

**(a)** Find the probability that the total is 7, given that at least one die shows a 2. **[3]**

**(b)** Determine whether the events "the total is 7" and "at least one die shows a 2" are
independent. **[2]**

**11.** On any day, the probability that a machine in a factory develops a fault is 0.05 and the
probability of a power cut is 0.02. These events are independent.

**(a)** Find the probability that on a given day there is both a fault and a power cut. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the probability that on a given day at least one of these happens. **[2]**

**(c)** Given that at least one of them happens on a particular day, find the probability that there
was a power cut. Give your answer to three significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** P(B) = 0.5, P(A | B) = 0.6 and P(A | B′) = 0.2.

**(a)** Find P(A). **[2]**

**(b)** Find P(B | A). **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the given information shows immediately that A and B are not independent. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** P(A | B) = P(A ∩ B)/P(B) = 0.18/0.45 [1] = 0.4 [1]. **[2]**

**2.** P(A)P(B) = 0.35 × 0.6 = 0.21 [1]. This equals P(A ∩ B), so A and B are independent [1].
**[2]**

**3. (a)** P(A ∩ B) = P(B)P(A | B) = 0.4 × 0.25 = 0.1 [1]

**(b)** P(B | A) = 0.1/0.5 = 0.2 [1]

**(c)** P(A ∪ B) = 0.5 + 0.4 − 0.1 = 0.8 [1]

**(d)** P(A ∩ B′) = 0.5 − 0.1 = 0.4 [1]; P(A | B′) = 0.4/0.6 = 2/3 [1]. **[5]**

**4. (a)** 0.65 = 0.3 + P(B), so P(B) = 0.35 [1].

**(b)** Let P(B) = x; P(A ∩ B) = 0.3x [1]. 0.65 = 0.3 + x − 0.3x [1], so 0.35 = 0.7x and x = 0.5 [1].
**[4]**

**5.** A = {2, 4, 6}, B = {1, 2, 3, 4}, A ∩ B = {2, 4} [1]. P(A) = 1/2, P(B) = 2/3 and
P(A ∩ B) = 1/3 [1]. P(A)P(B) = (1/2)(2/3) = 1/3 = P(A ∩ B), so A and B are independent [1]. **[3]**

**6.** P(A′ ∩ B′) = 1 − P(A ∪ B) [1] = 1 − P(A) − P(B) + P(A ∩ B) [1]
= 1 − P(A) − P(B) + P(A)P(B), using independence [1]
= (1 − P(A))(1 − P(B)) = P(A′)P(B′), so A′ and B′ are independent [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** 114/200 = 0.57 [1]

**(b)** Restrict to the treatment row [1]: 78/120 = 0.65 [1]

**(c)** Restrict to the improved column [1]: 78/114 = 13/19 (0.684) [1]

**(d)** P(improved | treatment) = 0.65 but P(improved) = 0.57 [1]. These are not equal, so the events
are not independent [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** 3/9 = 1/3 [1]

**(b)** P(R₂) = (4/10)(3/9) + (6/10)(4/9) [1] = 12/90 + 24/90 = 36/90 = 2/5 [1]

**(c)** P(R₁ | R₂) = P(R₁ ∩ R₂)/P(R₂) = (12/90)/(36/90) [1] = 1/3 [1]

**(d)** P(R₂ | R₁) = 1/3 but P(R₂) = 2/5 [1]; these differ, so R₁ and R₂ are not independent [1].
**[7]**

**9. (a)** P(A) = 0.2 + x and P(B) = 0.3 + x [1]. Independence: x = (0.2 + x)(0.3 + x) [1].
Expanding, x = 0.06 + 0.5x + x², so x² − 0.5x + 0.06 = 0 [1].

**(b)** (x − 0.2)(x − 0.3) = 0 [1], so x = 0.2 or x = 0.3 [1].

**(c)** 0.2 + x + 0.3 + 0.3 = 1, so x = 0.2 [1]. P(B | A) = 0.2/0.4 = 0.5 [1] (which equals P(B),
as independence requires). **[7]**

**10. (a)** Outcomes with at least one 2: 36 − 25 = 11 [1]. Of these, the total is 7 for (2, 5) and
(5, 2) [1]. P = 2/11 [1].

**(b)** P(total is 7) = 6/36 = 1/6 [1]. Since 2/11 ≠ 1/6, the events are not independent [1].
**[5]**

**11. (a)** 0.05 × 0.02 = 0.001 [1]

**(b)** 0.05 + 0.02 − 0.001 [1] = 0.069 [1]

**(c)** The event "power cut" lies inside "at least one", so P = 0.02/0.069 [1] = 0.290 (3 s.f.) [1].
**[5]**

**12. (a)** P(A) = P(B)P(A | B) + P(B′)P(A | B′) = 0.5 × 0.6 + 0.5 × 0.2 [1] = 0.4 [1]

**(b)** P(B | A) = 0.3/0.4 [1] = 0.75 [1]

**(c)** P(A | B) = 0.6 ≠ 0.2 = P(A | B′), so knowing whether B occurred changes the probability of A
[1]. **[5]**

## How this set builds

Questions 1 to 4 drill the definition and the addition rule. Questions 5 and 6 are the "show that"
and proof style the formal definitions make possible. Questions 7 to 12 put the formulae into
tables, trees, Venn diagrams and sample spaces, and each asks you to justify a decision about
independence with an explicit comparison.
