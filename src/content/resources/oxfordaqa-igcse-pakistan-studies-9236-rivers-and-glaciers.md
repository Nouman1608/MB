Key development 3.2.1.1 of the 9236 specification, Physical landscape, includes "formation,
processes and landforms of rivers and glaciers in Pakistan". This guide covers that content point:
how the Indus river system is organised, the processes that shape river valleys and glaciated
mountains, and the landforms they create, with Pakistani examples. It is part of the compulsory topic
examined in Paper 2 Section A. For the landscape, climate and resources topic as a whole, see the
[Landscape and Natural Resources study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-landscape-and-natural-resources/).

## Why rivers and glaciers matter in Pakistan

Almost all of Pakistan's rivers belong to one system, the **Indus**. Its water comes from monsoon
rain and, above all in summer, from the melting of snow and glaciers in the **Karakoram, Himalaya and
Hindu Kush**. The same rivers that carved deep mountain gorges built the fertile plains of Punjab and
Sindh, where most Pakistanis live and farm. Understanding the processes explains both the landscape
and why water supply depends on ice far upstream.

## The Indus river system

- **Source.** The Indus rises in Tibet and flows north-west through Ladakh into **Gilgit-Baltistan**,
  passing Skardu.
- **Mountain course.** It is joined by the Gilgit River, then turns south around the **Nanga Parbat**
  massif through deep gorges. It is dammed at **Tarbela**.
- **Kabul River.** The Kabul River, from Afghanistan, joins the Indus at **Attock**.
- **Leaving the mountains.** At **Kalabagh** the Indus emerges from the hills onto the plains.
- **Eastern rivers.** The **Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi** and **Sutlej** (which has already received the Beas
  in India) flow south-west across Punjab. They combine into the Chenab and then the **Panjnad**,
  meaning "five rivers", which joins the Indus near **Mithankot**.
- **Lower course.** The Indus crosses Sindh past Sukkur and Kotri and divides into the channels of its
  **delta** south of Thatta before reaching the **Arabian Sea**.

The land between two rivers is a **doab**. Punjab's doabs, such as the **Sindh Sagar** (Indus and
Jhelum), **Chaj** (Chenab and Jhelum), **Rechna** (Ravi and Chenab) and **Bari** (Beas or Sutlej and
Ravi), are among the most productive farmland in Pakistan.

## River processes

**Erosion** wears away the bed and banks:

- **hydraulic action**: the force of water breaks rock apart;
- **abrasion**: stones carried by the river scrape the bed and banks;
- **attrition**: stones knock against each other and become smaller and rounder;
- **solution**: some rocks dissolve in the water.

**Transportation** moves the load: large boulders roll along the bed (**traction**), pebbles bounce
(**saltation**), fine silt and clay are carried in the water (**suspension**), and dissolved minerals
travel in **solution**. The Indus carries a very heavy load of silt, much of it from the eroding
mountains.

**Deposition** happens when the river slows and loses energy: on the inside of bends, on flood plains
during floods, where it enters a lake or reservoir, and at the coast.

## River landforms

**Upper course (mountains).** Vertical erosion is dominant.

- **V-shaped valleys and gorges**: the Indus gorge around Nanga Parbat is among the deepest river
  gorges in the world.
- **Interlocking spurs**: the river winds around ridges that project from each side.
- **Rapids and waterfalls**: where bands of hard rock resist erosion.

**Middle and lower course (plains).** Lateral erosion and deposition are dominant.

- **Meanders**: faster water on the outer bend erodes a steep **river cliff**; slower water on the
  inner bend deposits a gentle **slip-off slope**.
- **Oxbow lakes**: erosion narrows the neck of a meander until, in a flood, the river cuts through;
  deposition seals off the old loop, leaving a curved lake.
- **Flood plains**: wide, flat areas built from silt left by repeated floods; the fertile soils of
  Punjab and Sindh were formed this way.
- **Levees**: raised banks formed when coarser sediment is dropped first as floodwater spills over;
  they are strengthened by artificial embankments called bunds.
- **Braided channels**: where the Indus carries a heavy load and flow varies by season, it splits
  into many channels around sandbars.
- **Alluvial fans**: fan-shaped deposits where mountain streams reach flatter land, as along the
  edges of the western ranges.
- **Delta**: at the mouth, sediment builds a broad, low area cut by creeks, with mangrove forests.
  The Indus delta is shrinking because dams and barrages hold back water and silt, so less sediment
  reaches the coast and seawater moves inland.

## Glacier formation and processes

A glacier forms where more snow falls in winter than melts in summer. Layers of snow are compressed
into granular **firn** and then into dense **glacier ice**, which moves downhill under its own weight.
In the upper **accumulation zone** snowfall adds ice; in the lower **ablation zone** melting and
evaporation remove it. Whether a glacier advances or retreats depends on this balance.

The Karakoram holds some of the longest glaciers outside the polar regions, including the **Siachen,
Baltoro, Biafo, Hispar** and **Batura** glaciers.

**Glacial erosion:**

- **plucking**: ice freezes onto rock and pulls pieces away as it moves;
- **abrasion**: rock fragments in the ice scrape the valley floor and sides;
- **freeze-thaw weathering**: water in cracks freezes, expands and splits rock, supplying debris.

## Glacial landforms

- **Cirques (corries)**: armchair-shaped hollows where glaciers begin, sometimes holding small lakes.
- **Arêtes**: knife-edge ridges between two cirques.
- **Pyramidal peaks**: sharp peaks formed where several cirques erode back into one mountain; the
  steep pyramid of **K2** is often given as an example.
- **U-shaped valleys**: glaciers widen and deepen river valleys into steep-sided troughs.
- **Hanging valleys**: smaller side valleys left high above the main trough, often with waterfalls.
- **Moraines**: ridges of debris carried and dropped by ice. **Lateral** moraines form along the
  sides, **medial** moraines where two glaciers join (the dark stripes on the Baltoro Glacier at
  Concordia), and **terminal** moraines at the snout.

## Linking processes to people

- **Meltwater** feeds the Indus in summer, when farmers need it most, and fills reservoirs such as
  Tarbela and Mangla.
- **Glacial lake outburst floods (GLOFs)** occur when a lake held back by ice or moraine bursts. In
  May 2022 a flood linked to the Shisper Glacier in Hunza destroyed the Hassanabad bridge on the
  Karakoram Highway.
- **Blocked rivers.** In January 2010 a landslide blocked the Hunza River and formed Attabad Lake,
  flooding villages and part of the highway.
- **Karakoram anomaly.** Some Karakoram glaciers have been stable or advancing, unlike most mountain
  glaciers worldwide, but many glaciers in the Himalaya and Hindu Kush are retreating.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain how an oxbow lake forms. **[4]**

```
1  erosion on the outer bends of a meander and deposition on the
   inner bends make the meander loop larger
2  the neck of land between two outer bends becomes narrower
3  during a flood the river cuts through the neck, taking the
   shorter, steeper route
4  deposition seals the ends of the old loop, leaving a curved
   oxbow lake
```

## Common mistakes

- **Confusing V-shaped (river) and U-shaped (glacial) valleys.**
- **Describing landforms without processes.** "Explain the formation" needs erosion, transport and
  deposition.
- **Giving no Pakistani examples.** Name the Indus, Nanga Parbat gorge, Baltoro or Hunza.
- **Mixing attrition and abrasion.** Attrition is stones hitting each other; abrasion is stones
  wearing the bed.
