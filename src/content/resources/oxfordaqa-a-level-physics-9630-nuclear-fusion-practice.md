These questions cover section 3.12.5, Nuclear fusion, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level only content. Learn the content first with the [nuclear fusion study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-nuclear-fusion/) and the [nuclear fusion revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-nuclear-fusion-revision-notes/). Use e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻², k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, R₀ = 1.2 fm, 1 u = 931.5 MeV = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain why light nuclei with one proton are the most suitable for fusion. **[2]**

**2.** State what a plasma is and why the fuel in a fusion reactor is a plasma. **[2]**

**3.** Complete the fusion reaction ²₁H + ²₁H → ³₁H + X by identifying X, and show that nucleon number and charge are conserved. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why fusion requires a very high temperature. **[2]**

**5.** State two problems that must be overcome to build a practical fusion reactor. **[2]**

**6.** Write the overall equation for the solar hydrogen cycle and state two quantities conserved in it. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A proton and a deuterium nucleus approach each other head-on with equal kinetic energies.

**(a)** Calculate their centre-to-centre separation when their surfaces just touch. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the electric potential energy at this separation, in J and in MeV. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the temperature at which the mean kinetic energy of each particle equals the energy it needs. **[2]**

**(d)** The Sun's core is at about 1.5 × 10⁷ K. Give one reason why fusion still happens there. **[1]**

**8.** In a fusion reactor ²H + ³H → ⁴He + n. Atomic masses: ²H 2.014102 u, ³H 3.016049 u, ⁴He 4.002603 u; neutron mass 1.008665 u.

**(a)** Calculate the energy released in one reaction, in MeV. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy released per kilogram of D–T fuel. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of D–T fuel used per day by a reactor producing 1.0 GW of fusion power. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Write equations for the three steps of the solar hydrogen cycle. **[3]**

**(b)** Atomic masses: ¹H 1.007825 u, ⁴He 4.002603 u. Calculate the total energy released when four protons become one helium-4 nucleus. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the final step of the cycle requires nuclei of higher energy than the first two. **[1]**

**10.** **(a)** Calculate the Coulomb barrier, in MeV, for two helium-3 nuclei just touching. **[2]**

**(b)** The barrier for two protons just touching is 0.60 MeV. Calculate the ratio of the helium-3 barrier to the proton barrier. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why fusion of carbon-12 nuclei needs a far higher temperature than fusion of hydrogen. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain why a plasma at 10⁸ K cannot be held in a solid container. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why magnetic fields can confine the nuclei and electrons of the plasma but not the neutrons produced. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how tritium for the fuel can be produced inside the reactor. **[2]**

**12.** In a test, a plasma is heated with 50 MW of power for 5.0 s. During this time D–T fusion occurs at 1.0 × 10¹⁹ reactions per second, each releasing 17.6 MeV.

**(a)** Calculate the fusion power. **[2]**

**(b)** State whether the device produced more energy than it used for heating, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest two changes to the plasma that would increase the fusion power. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The Coulomb barrier is proportional to the product of the charges, Z₁Z₂ [1]; nuclei with one proton have the smallest possible barrier, so less kinetic energy (a lower temperature) is needed [1] **[2]**

**2.** A plasma is an ionised gas of bare nuclei and free electrons [1]; at the temperatures needed for fusion, atoms lose all their electrons [1] **[2]**

**3.** X is a proton, ¹₁H [1]; nucleons 2 + 2 = 3 + 1 and charge 1 + 1 = 1 + 1 [1] **[2]**

**4.** The nuclei are positively charged and repel [1]; they need very large kinetic energies to get close enough for the strong interaction to act, and high temperature means high mean kinetic energy [1] **[2]**

**5.** Any two: reaching and keeping about 10⁸ K; confining the plasma away from the walls; holding it dense enough for long enough to gain energy; neutron damage to the structure; supply of tritium [1] [1] **[2]**

**6.** 4¹H → ⁴He + 2e⁺ + 2ν [1]; any two of charge, nucleon number, lepton number, energy [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** r = 1.2 × (1 + 2^(1/3)) = 2.71 fm [1]

**(b)** E_p = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/2.71 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 8.5 × 10⁻¹⁴ J [1]; = 0.53 MeV [1]

**(c)** Each needs 4.24 × 10⁻¹⁴ J = ³⁄₂kT [1]; T = 2 × 4.24 × 10⁻¹⁴/(3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³) = 2.0 × 10⁹ K [1]

**(d)** Either: some nuclei have energies far above the mean; or quantum tunnelling allows fusion below the barrier [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Each particle needs only half the barrier when both approach with equal energy.

**8. (a)** Δm = (2.014102 + 3.016049) − (4.002603 + 1.008665) = 0.018883 u [1]; energy = 17.6 MeV [1]

**(b)** Fuel mass = 5.030151 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ = 8.35 × 10⁻²⁷ kg [1]; energy per kg = 17.6 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³/8.35 × 10⁻²⁷ = 3.4 × 10¹⁴ J kg⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** 1.0 × 10⁹ × 86 400/3.37 × 10¹⁴ = 0.26 kg [1] **[5]**

**9. (a)** ¹H + ¹H → ²H + e⁺ + ν [1]; ²H + ¹H → ³He + γ [1]; ³He + ³He → ⁴He + ¹H + ¹H [1]

**(b)** Δm = 4 × 1.007825 − 4.002603 = 0.028697 u [1]; energy = 26.7 MeV [1]

**(c)** It fuses two helium-3 nuclei, each with charge +2e, so the repulsion and barrier are larger than for steps involving hydrogen nuclei [1] **[6]**

**10. (a)** r = 2 × 1.2 × 3^(1/3) = 3.46 fm [1]; E_p = 8.99 × 10⁹ × (2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹)²/3.46 × 10⁻¹⁵ = 2.66 × 10⁻¹³ J = 1.7 MeV [1]

**(b)** 1.66/0.60 = 2.8 [1]

**(c)** Carbon-12 has six protons, so Z₁Z₂ = 36, giving a barrier many times larger than for hydrogen [1]; a much higher mean kinetic energy, and so temperature, is needed for nuclei to overcome it [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* The radius also increases with A, but only as A^(1/3); the charge product dominates.

**11. (a)** Any wall in contact would cool the plasma below the temperature needed for fusion [1], and the wall would be melted or damaged [1]

**(b)** Nuclei and electrons are charged, so a magnetic field exerts a force on them and they spiral around the field lines [1]; neutrons are uncharged, so they feel no magnetic force and travel straight out to the walls [1]

**(c)** Neutrons from fusion are absorbed by lithium-6 in a blanket around the plasma [1]; ⁶Li + n → ⁴He + ³H produces tritium, which is extracted and used as fuel [1] **[6]**

**12. (a)** Power = 1.0 × 10¹⁹ × 17.6 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ [1] = 2.8 × 10⁷ W (28 MW) [1]

**(b)** No: the fusion power (28 MW) is less than the heating power (50 MW), so less energy was produced than supplied [1]

**(c)** Any two: increase the plasma temperature; increase the plasma density; use a D–T mixture closer to equal numbers of each nucleus [1] [1] **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the full barrier to one nucleus.
- Using R₀A for a radius or forgetting to add both radii.
- Using charge e for helium-3.
- Writing the solar cycle in one step.
- Saying magnetic fields confine neutrons.
