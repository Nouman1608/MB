These questions cover section 5 of Unit M1: Mechanics 1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): forces as vectors, resolution of forces, equilibrium of a particle under coplanar forces, and the coefficient of friction with F ≤ μR in equilibrium. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers that use g to 3 significant figures.

## Section A

**1.** A particle of mass 2.5 kg rests on a rough horizontal plane, with coefficient of friction 0.3. A horizontal force acting on it is slowly increased from zero. Find the size of the force at the moment the particle is about to move. **[2]**

**2.** Forces (pi + 2j) N, (3i + qj) N and (−5i − 7j) N act on a particle, which is in equilibrium. Find p and q. **[2]**

**3.** A particle is placed at rest on a rough plane inclined at 35° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.6. Determine whether the particle stays at rest. **[2]**

**4.** A particle of mass 1.5 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string is horizontal. The other makes an angle of 40° with the horizontal. Find the tension in each string. **[4]**

**5.** A particle of weight 20 N rests on a smooth plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. It is held in equilibrium by a force P N acting up the plane at 20° above the line of greatest slope. Find P and the normal reaction. **[4]**

**6.** A particle of mass 3 kg rests on a smooth plane inclined at 40° to the horizontal. It is held in equilibrium by a horizontal force P N, in the vertical plane containing the line of greatest slope. Find P and the normal reaction. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** A particle of mass 3 kg rests on a rough plane inclined at 25° to the horizontal. The coefficient of friction is 0.2. A force of magnitude P N acts on the particle up the line of greatest slope.

**(a)** Find the normal reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the least value of P for which the particle stays at rest. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the greatest value of P for which the particle stays at rest. **[2]**

**(d)** Given that P = 10, find the size and direction of the friction force. **[2]**

**8.** A particle of mass 6 kg hangs in equilibrium from two light strings. One string makes 30° with the horizontal and the other makes 60° with the horizontal, on the other side of the particle, so the strings are at right angles to each other. Find the tension in each string. **[5]**

**9.** A crate of mass 10 kg rests on rough horizontal ground, with coefficient of friction 0.3. It is pulled by a rope inclined at 20° above the horizontal.

**(a)** Find the tension in the rope when the crate is on the point of moving. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain why pulling at 20° above the horizontal needs a smaller force than pulling horizontally. **[1]**

**10.** A particle is in equilibrium under three forces: (6i − 2j) N, (−2i + 7j) N and F N.

**(a)** Find F. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of F and the angle F makes with the negative i direction. **[3]**

**(c)** The force F is removed. State the magnitude of the resultant of the other two forces. **[1]**

**11.** A particle of mass 2 kg rests on a rough plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. A force of 15 N acts on the particle at an angle of 20° above the line of greatest slope, up the plane. The particle is on the point of moving up the plane.

**(a)** Find the normal reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the coefficient of friction. **[4]**

**12.** A light rod AB is horizontal, with A against a vertical wall. A particle of mass 2 kg hangs from B. A light string joins B to a point C on the wall above A, and makes 30° with the horizontal. The system is in equilibrium.

**(a)** Find the tension in the string. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the force in the rod, and state whether it is a tension or a thrust. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** R = 2.5 × 9.8 = 24.5 N. Limiting: F = μR = 0.3 × 24.5 [1] = **7.35 N** [1].

**2.** i: p + 3 − 5 = 0, so **p = 2** [1]. j: 2 + q − 7 = 0, so **q = 5** [1].

**3.** The particle stays at rest only if μ ≥ tan 35° = 0.700 [1]. Since 0.6 < 0.700, the friction available is not enough, so **the particle slides down** [1].

**4.** Vertical: T₂ sin 40° = 1.5 × 9.8 = 14.7 [1], so **T₂ = 22.9 N** [1].

Horizontal: T₁ = T₂ cos 40° [1] = **17.5 N** [1].

**5.** Along the plane: P cos 20° = 20 sin 30° = 10 [1], so **P = 10.6 N** [1].

Perpendicular: R + P sin 20° = 20 cos 30° [1], so R = 17.32 − 3.64 = **13.7 N** [1].

**6.** Along the plane: P cos 40° = 3g sin 40° [1], so P = 3g tan 40° = **24.7 N** [1].

Perpendicular: R = 3g cos 40° + P sin 40° [1] = **38.4 N** (equal to 3g/cos 40°) [1].

**7. (a)** R = 3g cos 25° = **26.6 N** [1].

**(b)** Least P: about to slide down, so friction acts up the plane [1].

P + 0.2(26.65) = 3g sin 25° [1], so P = 12.42 − 5.33 = **7.10 N** [1].

**(c)** Greatest P: about to slide up, friction down the plane: P = 12.42 + 5.33 [1] = **17.8 N** [1].

**(d)** P + F = 12.42, with F up the plane, so F = **2.42 N** [1], acting **up the plane** [1].

**8.** Horizontal: T₁ cos 30° = T₂ cos 60° [1]. Vertical: T₁ sin 30° + T₂ sin 60° = 6 × 9.8 = 58.8 [1].

From the first, T₂ = √3 T₁. Then T₁(0.5 + 1.5) = 58.8 [1], so **T₁ = 29.4 N** [1] and **T₂ = 50.9 N** [1].

(Alternative: the strings are perpendicular, so resolving along each string gives T₁ = 58.8 cos 60° and T₂ = 58.8 cos 30°.)

**9. (a)** Vertical: R + T sin 20° = 98 [1], so R = 98 − T sin 20° [1].

Horizontal, limiting: T cos 20° = 0.3R [1].

T cos 20° = 0.3(98 − T sin 20°), so T(cos 20° + 0.3 sin 20°) = 29.4 [1].

**T = 28.2 N** [1].

**(b)** The upward component of the pull reduces the normal reaction, so the greatest friction, μR, is smaller [1].

**10. (a)** F = −[(6 − 2)i + (−2 + 7)j] [1] = **(−4i − 5j) N** [1].

**(b)** |F| = √(16 + 25) = √41 = **6.40 N** [1].

F points below the negative i direction: tan⁻¹(5/4) [1] = **51.3°** [1].

**(c)** The other two forces have resultant −F, so its magnitude is **6.40 N** [1].

**11. (a)** Perpendicular: R + 15 sin 20° = 2g cos 30° [1], so R = 16.97 − 5.13 = **11.8 N** [1].

**(b)** About to move up, so friction acts down the plane [1].

Along: 15 cos 20° = 2g sin 30° + F [1], so F = 14.10 − 9.8 = 4.30 N [1].

μ = F/R = 4.30/11.84 = **0.363** [1].

**12. (a)** Vertical at B: T sin 30° = 2 × 9.8 [1], so **T = 39.2 N** [1].

**(b)** Horizontal at B: the string pulls B towards the wall with force T cos 30° [1].

The rod must push B away from the wall with a force of 39.2 cos 30° = **33.9 N** [1], so the rod is in **thrust** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing R = mg cos α when an applied force has a component perpendicular to the plane.
- Using F = μR for a particle that is only "at rest".
- Taking friction in the same direction for both limits of a range.
- Mixing up sin and cos when resolving the weight on a slope.
- Answering "will it move?" with a number but no comparison.
- Calling a pushing force in a rod a tension.
