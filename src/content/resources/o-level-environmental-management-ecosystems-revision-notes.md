Topic 5 of Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) joins ecology to practical skills. You learn
how energy and carbon move through ecosystems, why forests are cleared and why they should be managed
sustainably, how biodiversity is conserved, and how to plan a fieldwork investigation that collects
reliable data. Paper 2 often sets a fieldwork method or a data table from an investigation, so the
practical part deserves as much revision as the theory.

## 5.1 Ecosystems: build it from the bottom up

**Level of organisation.** Organism → **population** (one species in an area) → **community** (all species in
an area) → ecosystem (community plus abiotic environment). Each species has a **habitat** (where it lives)
and a **niche** (its role). A **species** is a group able to breed and produce fertile offspring.

**Feeding roles.** Producers make food by photosynthesis → primary consumers (herbivores) → secondary
consumers → tertiary consumers; an **apex predator** has no natural predators. **Decomposers** break down
dead matter. Predators hunt prey.

**Abiotic factors**: temperature, water, oxygen, carbon dioxide, salinity, light, pH.

**Interactions**
- Native species belong in an area; invasive species are introduced and spread, often out-competing natives.
- Competition for limited resources; predation; pollination.

**Pollination**: pollen moves from an anther to a stigma by insect pollinators or by wind → fertilisation →
seeds and fruit.

**Two equations to learn as a pair**
- Photosynthesis: carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (sunlight energy; chlorophyll captures light).
- Aerobic respiration: glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water (energy released).

**Energy flow**
- About 10% of energy passes to the next trophic level.
- Losses: movement, respiration, digestion and excretion.
- Pyramids of energy narrow upwards every time; pyramids of numbers can be inverted (many insects on one
  tree).

**Worked example.** Phytoplankton in a lake fix 80 000 kJ. Zooplankton receive 8 000 kJ, small fish 800 kJ,
pike 80 kJ. If a fourth consumer were added it would receive only 8 kJ - too little to support a viable
population, which limits food chain length.

**Carbon cycle - six processes**: photosynthesis (air to plants); feeding (plants to animals); respiration
(organisms to air); decomposition (dead matter to air); fossil fuel formation (buried remains to coal, oil,
gas); combustion (fuels to air).

## 5.2 Forest ecosystems: causes → impacts → management

| Cause | Typical example |
|---|---|
| Logging and timber extraction | Hardwood for furniture |
| Subsistence and commercial farming | Shifting cultivation; cattle ranching; plantations |
| Roads and settlements | Access roads open forest to settlers |
| Rock, ore and mineral extraction | Open-pit mines |
| Hydro-electric power stations | Reservoirs flood valleys |
| Climate change | Drought and fire stress forests |

**Impacts**: habitat loss; biodiversity loss; soil erosion and desertification; silting and flooding; global
warming; changed rainfall; genetic depletion.

**Sustainable management of forests** balances the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans while
conserving forests for future generations. Needed because forests regulate climate (carbon sinks and
stores), drive the water cycle (interception, uptake, transpiration, less run-off), control floods, prevent
soil erosion, are a genetic resource, supply food, medicine and raw materials, and support recreation,
ecotourism and education.

## 5.3 Managing biodiversity: protect, store or regulate

- **Protect in the wild**: national parks and reserves; wildlife corridors; sustainable harvesting;
  sustainable forestry; ecotourism.
- **Store or breed outside the wild**: seed banks; zoos and captive breeding.
- **Regulate people**: international cooperation against animal trade; regulation of trade in vulnerable
  and endangered species; classifying species as threatened, endangered or extinct.

Discussion pattern: in-the-wild methods protect whole ecosystems but need enforcement and funding; storage
and breeding protect genes but not habitats; regulation works only with monitoring and cooperation between
countries.

## 5.4 Fieldwork: the investigation cycle

1. **Aim and hypothesis** - a testable prediction.
2. **Plan** - sampling strategy, technique, questionnaires or surveys; a pilot study to test the method.
3. **Variables** - independent (changed), dependent (measured), controlled (kept the same).
4. **Repeats and replicates** - improve reliability and allow a mean.
5. **Record, analyse, identify anomalies, conclude.**

| Sampling | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Random | Unbiased | May miss rare species or parts of the site |
| Systematic | Shows change along a gradient | May miss what falls between sample points |

| Technique | Best for | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Pitfall trap | Ground invertebrates | Predators may eat catch; check often |
| Pooter | Small insects | Only very small animals |
| Sweep net | Insects in grass | Misses animals near the ground |
| Quadrat | Plants, slow animals | Percentage cover is an estimate |
| Transect | Change across an area | Time-consuming |
| Aerial photos and drones | Large or inaccessible areas | Cost; cannot see under canopy |
| Automated sampling | Continuous data, camera traps | Equipment cost and data handling |

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Writing that producers "make energy" - they convert light energy into chemical energy in glucose.
- Calling decomposers consumers.
- Confusing a control variable with a control experiment.
- Saying a pilot study collects the final results - it tests the method first.
- Giving zoos as protecting habitats.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do food chains rarely have more than five levels?** Only about 10% of energy passes up each level,
so too little energy remains to support animals at higher levels.

**What is a pilot study?** A small trial run of the method, used to find and fix problems before the main
data collection.

**How does deforestation change rainfall?** Fewer trees means less transpiration, so less water vapour enters
the atmosphere and local rainfall can decrease.
