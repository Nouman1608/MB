These questions cover section **3.3.2.3 Combustion of alkanes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Combustion of alkanes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-combustion-of-alkanes/) and the [Combustion of alkanes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-combustion-of-alkanes-revision-notes/). Use Mᵣ values propane = 44.0, octane = 114.0, CO₂ = 44.0, SO₂ = 64.1, CaCO₃ = 100.1, and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of each alkane.

**(a)** ethane, C₂H₆ **[1]**

**(b)** pentane, C₅H₁₂ **[1]**

**2.** Write a balanced equation for the incomplete combustion of butane, C₄H₁₀, to form carbon monoxide and water. **[1]**

**3.** State two reasons why incomplete combustion of a hydrocarbon fuel is a problem. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how nitrogen oxides are formed in a car engine, and state one environmental problem they cause. **[3]**

**5.** State what is meant by an unburned hydrocarbon in the exhaust of a petrol engine and name one other pollutant formed by incomplete combustion. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how a catalytic converter reduces pollution from a car. In your answer, name the type of material used and write an equation for a reaction that takes place. **[4]**

**7.** Explain why a catalytic converter is made as a honeycomb coated with a thin layer of metal. **[2]**

**8.** Some sulfur compounds are present in coal.

**(a)** Write an equation to show how sulfur dioxide forms when sulfur in coal is burned. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how sulfur dioxide causes environmental problems. **[2]**

**9.** Sulfur dioxide can be removed from the flue gases of a power station using calcium carbonate.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why this reaction removes SO₂. **[2]**

**(c)** State one commercial use of the product after oxidation. **[1]**

**10.** 20.0 g of propane burns completely in excess oxygen. Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at room temperature and pressure. **[3]**

**11.** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed when 1.00 kg of octane burns completely. **[3]**

**12.** A power station produces 3.20 kg of sulfur dioxide. Calculate the mass of calcium carbonate needed to remove it, assuming the equation CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂. **[3]**

**13.** A student says that burning hydrocarbons in plenty of air produces no pollution. Evaluate this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O (or C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O) [1]

**(b)** C₅H₁₂ + 8O₂ → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O [1]. **[2]**

**2.** 2C₄H₁₀ + 9O₂ → 8CO + 10H₂O (or C₄H₁₀ + 4½O₂ → 4CO + 5H₂O) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Carbon monoxide is toxic [1]; less energy is released than in complete combustion (or soot is produced, which blackens surfaces and causes breathing problems) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Nitrogen and oxygen from the air react at the high temperature and spark in the engine (N₂ + O₂ → 2NO) [1]; NO can react further with oxygen to give NO₂ [1]; NOₓ cause acid rain (or photochemical smog or respiratory problems) [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Fuel that leaves the engine without burning [1]; carbon monoxide or carbon (soot) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The converter contains a honeycomb ceramic support coated with platinum, palladium or rhodium [1]; hot exhaust gases pass over the surface where CO, NOₓ and unburned hydrocarbons react [1]; for example 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ [1]; this converts harmful gases into carbon dioxide, nitrogen and water [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The honeycomb gives a large surface area for the gases to react on [1]; the thin layer of metal minimises the amount of expensive precious metal needed [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** S + O₂ → SO₂ [1]

**(b)** SO₂ dissolves in rain water to form acidic solutions (sulfurous acid, then sulfuric acid), causing acid rain [1]; acid rain damages buildings, trees and aquatic life [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** CaCO₃ + SO₂ → CaSO₃ + CO₂ [1]

**(b)** Sulfur dioxide is an acidic oxide [1]; calcium carbonate is a base that reacts with the acid, neutralising it and trapping the sulfur as a solid [1]

**(c)** Calcium sulfate (gypsum) is used in plasterboard [1]. **[4]**

**10.** n(C₃H₈) = 20.0 ÷ 44.0 = 0.4545 mol [1]; n(CO₂) = 3 × 0.4545 = 1.364 mol [1]; volume = 1.364 × 24.0 = **32.7 dm³** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** n(C₈H₁₈) = 1000 ÷ 114.0 = 8.77 mol [1]; n(CO₂) = 8 × 8.77 = 70.2 mol [1]; mass = 70.2 × 44.0 = 3090 g = **3.09 kg** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** n(SO₂) = 3200 ÷ 64.1 = 49.9 mol [1]; n(CaCO₃) = 49.9 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 49.9 × 100.1 = **4997 g (5.00 kg)** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** The statement is wrong [1]; complete combustion produces carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas, and at high temperatures in an engine nitrogen oxides also form from the air; fuels containing sulfur produce sulfur dioxide [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The pollutants NOₓ come from the air, not from the fuel. That point is often tested.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Missing water from combustion equations.
- Saying NOₓ comes from the fuel.
- Forgetting that the converter produces CO₂.
- Calling calcium carbonate a catalyst.
- Using the mass instead of moles in volume calculations.
