## Structure of commerce — draw this from memory

```
COMMERCE
├── TRADE
│   ├── Home trade ...... wholesale, retail
│   └── Foreign trade ... import, export, ENTREPOT
└── AIDS TO TRADE
    ├── Banking ......... finance and payment
    ├── Transport ....... creates PLACE utility
    ├── Warehousing ..... creates TIME utility
    ├── Insurance ....... absorbs risk
    ├── Communication ... transmits information
    └── Advertising ..... informs and persuades
```

Commerce covers **distribution only** — it is not production.

## The chain of production

| Stage | Activity | Problem it leaves |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | Extraction, farming, mining, fishing | Wrong **form** |
| Secondary | Manufacturing, processing, construction | Wrong **place** and **time** |
| Tertiary | Commercial and personal services | — resolves the above |

## Utility — three types

- **Form utility** — changing a product so it becomes useful (timber → furniture).
- **Place utility** — moving it to where the consumer is (transport).
- **Time utility** — having it available when wanted (warehousing).

## Specialisation

**Advantages:** higher output per worker, mechanisation possible, economies of scale, better quality, faster production.

**Disadvantages:** repetitive and demotivating, loss of craftsmanship, machines displace labour, standardised output reduces choice, and — crucially — **interdependence**.

Interdependence requires exchange, which requires commerce. That is the link examiners want.

Levels: country · region · town · firm/worker.

## Home vs foreign trade

| | Home | Foreign |
|---|---|---|
| Boundaries | Within one country | Across countries |
| Currency | One | Different, exchange-rate risk |
| Documentation | Simple | Extensive; customs |
| Barriers | None | Tariffs, quotas |

**Entrepot** = goods imported then re-exported without domestic consumption.

## Exam traps

- Commerce ≠ trade. Trade is one branch of commerce.
- Banking is an **aid to trade**, not part of trade.
- Warehousing gives **time** utility; transport gives **place** utility — don't swap them.
- Don't forget entrepot when classifying foreign trade.
- State the aid to trade **and** the obstacle it removes.

## Self-test

1. Name the two branches of commerce and their subdivisions.
2. Which utility does warehousing create, and why?
3. Define entrepot trade.
4. Why does specialisation make commerce necessary?
5. Give three disadvantages of specialisation.

**Answers:** 1. Trade (home: wholesale/retail; foreign: import/export/entrepot) and aids to trade (banking, transport, warehousing, insurance, communication, advertising). 2. Time utility — it stores goods so they are available when consumers want them, bridging the gap between production and demand. 3. Goods imported into a country and then re-exported, often after storage or minor processing, without being consumed domestically. 4. Specialisation means no one produces everything they need, creating interdependence; exchange becomes essential, and commerce is what makes exchange possible. 5. Any three: boring repetitive work, loss of craftsmanship, unemployment from mechanisation, reduced consumer choice, high interdependence and vulnerability to disruption.
