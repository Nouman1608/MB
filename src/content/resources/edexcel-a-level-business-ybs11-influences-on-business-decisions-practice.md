Each question uses one of the specification's command words at its published International A Level tariff: **define** (2), **calculate** (4), **explain** (4), **analyse** (6), **discuss** (8) and **assess** (12). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: Thornbury Dairy**
>
> Thornbury is a family-owned dairy. The founder's daughter, now managing
> director, makes nearly every decision herself. Staff say they follow
> instructions and rarely suggest ideas. This year's profit for the year is
> $1.8m. The board is choosing between two plans.
>
> | | Plan A | Plan B |
> |---|---|---|
> | Dividends | 75% of profit | 45% of profit |
> | Pay rise for staff | none | $270,000 |
> | Reducing plastic packaging | none | $270,000 |
> | Retained in the business | the rest | the rest |
>
> **Source 2: Kalmar Fashions**
>
> Kalmar has revenue of $20m and profit of $3m. It could save $400,000 a
> year by switching to a supplier whose factory has poor working conditions.
> A news report could expose this and the company estimates that 4% of
> revenue could be lost, at a gross margin of 35%. The chief executive earns
> $1.2m a year and the average employee earns $40,000. Kalmar advertises
> itself as an ethical brand.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "stakeholder". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the dividends under Plan A and Plan B and the amount retained under each plan. **[4]**

**3.** Calculate the percentage fall in dividends if Plan B is chosen. **[4]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why Thornbury's culture can be described as a power culture. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** problem that Thornbury might face if it tried to change its culture to a task culture. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse how Plan B could benefit Thornbury in the long term. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "corporate social responsibility". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the increase in Kalmar's profit if it switches supplier and no sales are lost, and the percentage increase. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the lost gross profit if news of the supplier is made public and 4% of revenue is lost, and the net gain from switching. **[4]**

**10.** Calculate the pay ratio between Kalmar's chief executive and its average employee, and the difference in their annual pay. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss whether Kalmar should switch to the cheaper supplier. **[8]**

**12.** Assess whether a business should put the interests of shareholders before those of other stakeholders. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** An individual or group with an interest in a business [1] and which is affected by, or can affect, its activities [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Plan A: 75% x 1.8m = **$1,350,000** dividends; retained = 1.8m - 1.35m = **$450,000** [2]. Plan B: 45% x 1.8m = **$810,000** dividends; retained = 1.8m - 0.81m - 0.27m - 0.27m = **$450,000** [2]. **[4]**

**3.** Fall = 1,350,000 - 810,000 = $540,000 [1]. Percentage = 540,000 / 1,350,000 [1] x 100 [1] = **40%** [1]. **[4]**

**4.** In a power culture, control is held by one person or a small group at the centre [1]. At Thornbury the managing director makes nearly every decision herself [1], and staff follow instructions and rarely suggest ideas, so there is little shared decision-making [1]. This allows quick decisions but makes the business depend on one person [1]. **[4]**

**5.** A task culture needs staff to use their own initiative and work in teams [1], but Thornbury's employees are used to following instructions [1]. They may resist the change, and trust may be lacking [1]. Culture is deeply embedded and the managing director may also find it hard to give up control, so the change could take years [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Plan B pays staff $270,000 more, which could raise motivation and reduce staff turnover [1], saving recruitment and training costs [1]. A more motivated workforce may be more productive and give better customer service [1]. Spending $270,000 on reducing plastic packaging may attract environmentally concerned customers [1], and improve the brand's reputation [1], so that sales and profit could rise in future years, which may make up for the 40% cut in dividends [1]. **[6]**

**7.** A business acting responsibly towards society and the environment [1] beyond what the law requires [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Extra profit = **$400,000** [1]. New profit = 3,000,000 + 400,000 = $3.4m [1]. Percentage increase = 400,000 / 3,000,000 x 100 [1] = **13.3%** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Lost revenue = 4% x 20m = $800,000 [1]. Lost gross profit = 35% x 800,000 = **$280,000** [1]. Net gain = 400,000 - 280,000 [1] = **$120,000** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** 1,200,000 / 40,000 [1] = **30:1** [1]. Difference = 1,200,000 - 40,000 [1] = **$1,160,000** [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **For switching:** it saves $400,000 a year, a 13.3% rise in profit [1]; if the practice stays unknown, shareholders gain [1].
**Against:** it conflicts with Kalmar's advertised ethical brand [1]; if exposed, it loses about $280,000 of gross profit, cutting the net gain to $120,000 [1]; the net gain does not include damage to staff morale, legal costs or loss of trust, which may be more than $120,000 [1]; workers in the factory suffer poor conditions [1].
**Judgement:** Kalmar should not switch, because the small net gain of $120,000 (4% of profit) is outweighed by the risk to a brand built on ethics [1]. It would be better to negotiate lower prices with an ethical supplier or to improve its own efficiency [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **For putting shareholders first:** shareholders own the business and take the risk, so they have a right to a return [1]; dividends and share price attract investment, which allows growth and jobs [1]; managers have a clear target, which makes decisions simpler and easier to judge [1]; a business that does not make a profit cannot help other stakeholders at all [1].
**Against:** focusing on short-term returns can lead to cutting staff pay, using cheap suppliers or damaging the environment [1]; unhappy staff, customers and communities can damage long-term profit, as the Kalmar supplier example shows [1]; stakeholders such as employees and suppliers also take risks and invest their time or resources [1]; customers increasingly choose businesses with ethical practices [1].
**Judgement:** a business should not put shareholders first in every case, because in the long term the interests are linked: profit depends on motivated staff, loyal customers and a good reputation [1]. The balance depends on the business: a firm that markets itself on ethics, like Kalmar, must protect other stakeholders to keep its shareholders' returns [1]. In a very competitive market with thin margins, a business may have less freedom [1], but should still avoid harming stakeholders where the cost of doing so is small [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating the retained amount for Plan B without deducting the staff and plastic spending.
- Working out the dividend fall as a percentage of Plan B instead of Plan A.
- Forgetting that lost gross profit uses the gross margin, not the whole of lost revenue.
- Writing "power culture" without evidence from the source.
- Concluding without a judgement tied to Kalmar's ethical brand.
