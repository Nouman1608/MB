These questions cover section **4.5.5 Pressure and pressure differences in fluids** of the AQA GCSE Physics
(8463) specification, a physics only section examined on Paper 2 at both tiers. Questions 7, 8, 9, 10, 12
and 13 test content marked (HT only) and are labelled **Higher tier only**. Take g = 9.8 N/kg and the
density of water as 1000 kg/m³. A calculator is allowed throughout.

Learn the content first in the
[pressure in fluids study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-pressure-and-pressure-differences-in-fluids/)
and the
[pressure in fluids revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-8463-pressure-and-pressure-differences-in-fluids-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the direction of the force that the pressure of a fluid exerts on a surface. **[1]**

**2.** An elephant of weight 48 000 N stands on four feet. Each foot has an area of 0.12 m² in contact with
the ground. Calculate the pressure on the ground. **[3]**

**3.** Convert an area of 25 cm² into m². **[1]**

**4.** Explain why tractors used on soft, muddy fields have very wide tyres. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why atmospheric pressure decreases as height above the ground increases. **[2]**

**6.** A gas at a pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa pushes on a piston of area 2.0 × 10⁻³ m². Calculate the force on
the piston. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (Higher tier only) A water tank is filled to a depth of 0.60 m.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure due to the water at the bottom of the tank. **[2]**
**(b)** A drain plug at the bottom of the tank has an area of 2.0 cm². Calculate the force of the water on
the plug. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) A reservoir is held back by a concrete dam.

**(a)** Calculate the difference in pressure between the water surface and a point 40 m below the surface.
**[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the dam is built much thicker at its base than at its top. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A cube with sides of 5.0 cm is held completely under water. Its top face is 30 cm
below the surface and its bottom face is 35 cm below the surface.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure due to the water on the top face and on the bottom face. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force of the water on the top face and on the bottom face. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the upthrust on the cube. **[1]**
**(d)** The cube weighs 3.0 N. State whether it floats or sinks when released, and explain why. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A cargo ship sails from the sea into a freshwater river. Fresh water is less dense
than seawater. Explain why the ship floats lower in the water in the river. **[3]**

**11.** This question is about the atmosphere.

**(a)** Describe a simple model of the Earth's atmosphere and of atmospheric pressure. **[3]**
**(b)** The air pressure is 101 000 Pa at an airport and 89 000 Pa at the height where a small aircraft
flies (invented values). The cabin is kept at the airport pressure. Calculate the resultant force on a
cabin window of area 0.080 m², and state its direction. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A tall plastic bottle filled with water has three small holes in its side, one
above the other. Water spurts out of each hole at right angles to the bottle's surface, and the jet from the
lowest hole travels furthest. Explain both observations. **[4]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) Describe the factors that decide whether an object floats or sinks in a liquid.
**[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Normal (at right angles) to the surface [1]

**2.** Total area = 4 × 0.12 = 0.48 m² [1]; p = F / A = 48 000 / 0.48 [1]; = **100 000 Pa** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the area of one foot gives a pressure four times too large.

**3.** 25 × 10⁻⁴ = **0.0025 m²** [1]

**4.** The wide tyres have a larger area in contact with the ground [1]; so the same weight produces a smaller
pressure and the tractor sinks less into the mud [1]

**5.** The higher the surface, the fewer air molecules there are above it [1]; so the weight of air above it
is smaller, and the pressure is lower [1]

**6.** F = p A = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ [1]; = **300 N** [1]

**7. (a)** p = h ρ g = 0.60 × 1000 × 9.8 [1]; = **5880 Pa** [1]
**(b)** A = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² [1]; F = 5880 × 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ = **1.2 N** [1]

**8. (a)** Δp = 40 × 1000 × 9.8 [1]; = **392 000 Pa** (3.9 × 10⁵ Pa) [1]
**(b)** Pressure in the water increases with depth, because there is a taller column of water above [1];
so the force on each square metre of the dam is greatest at the base, which must be thicker to withstand
it [1]

**9. (a)** Top: 0.30 × 1000 × 9.8 = **2940 Pa** [1]; bottom: 0.35 × 1000 × 9.8 = **3430 Pa** [1]
**(b)** Face area = 0.050 × 0.050 = 0.0025 m²; top force = 2940 × 0.0025 = **7.35 N** [1]; bottom force =
3430 × 0.0025 = **8.58 N** [1]
**(c)** 8.58 − 7.35 = **1.2 N** upwards [1]
**(d)** It sinks [1]; its weight (3.0 N) is greater than the upthrust (1.2 N) even when it is fully
submerged, so the resultant force is downwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* Forgetting to convert 5.0 cm to 0.050 m before squaring gives an area of 25 m².

**10.** The ship floats where the upthrust equals its weight, and its weight does not change [1]; in less
dense fresh water, pressure increases more slowly with depth [1]; so the ship must sink deeper before the
pressure on its hull gives an upthrust equal to its weight [1]

**11. (a)** The atmosphere is a thin layer of air around the Earth, thin compared with the Earth's size [1];
it becomes less dense with increasing altitude [1]; atmospheric pressure is caused by air molecules
colliding with surfaces [1]
**(b)** Δp = 101 000 − 89 000 = 12 000 Pa [1]; F = 12 000 × 0.080 = **960 N** [1]; outwards, from the
cabin towards the outside air [1]

**12.** Fluid pressure acts normal to any surface [1]; so the water is pushed out at right angles to the
bottle's side [1]; the lowest hole is deepest, with the tallest column of water above it, so the pressure
there is greatest [1]; the water is pushed out with the greatest force per unit area, so it leaves fastest
and travels furthest [1]

**13.** The object's weight compared with the upthrust: it floats if the upthrust can equal its weight
before it is completely submerged [1]; it sinks if its weight is greater than the upthrust even when fully
submerged [1]; the density of the object compared with the liquid: less dense floats, more dense sinks, and
a hollow shape such as a ship's hull lowers the overall density [1]; the density of the liquid: a denser
liquid gives a greater upthrust at the same depth, so an object floats higher in it [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one foot's or one tyre's area when the weight is spread over several.
- Converting cm² to m² by dividing by 100.
- Using the total depth in a pressure difference question.
- Explaining upthrust without comparing the top and bottom surfaces.
- "Less gravity higher up" as the reason for lower atmospheric pressure.
- Saying the ship floats lower because it is "heavier" in fresh water.
