These questions cover section 3.1.2, **Fractions, decimals and percentages** (N10, N11 and N12), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification, version 1.0, for teaching from September 2015 with exams from May/June 2017 onwards. Questions on converting between recurring decimals and fractions are labelled **(Higher)**, because that skill is Higher tier only; everything else suits both tiers. Each question says whether it is non-calculator (Paper 1 style) or calculator (Papers 2 and 3 style).

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-fractions-decimals-and-percentages/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-fractions-decimals-and-percentages-revision-notes/). These questions are different from the topic-level [Number practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-mathematics-number-practice/), so use both. The course hub is [AQA GCSE Mathematics](/boards/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/gcse/mathematics/) lists every statement.

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 0.35 as a fraction in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 9/40 as a decimal. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Write these numbers in order of size. Start with the smallest.

17/25 &nbsp;&nbsp; 0.7 &nbsp;&nbsp; 69% &nbsp;&nbsp; 0.695 &nbsp;&nbsp; 11/16 **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Work out

**(a)** 3/8 of 72 **[1]**
**(b)** 45% of 160 **[2]**
**(c)** 135% of 60 **[1]**

**4.** (calculator)

**(a)** Write down the multiplier for an increase of 8%. **[1]**
**(b)** A gym membership costs £42.50 per month. The cost increases by 8%. Work out the new monthly cost. **[2]**
**(c)** A car is worth £14 600. Its value decreases by 22%. Use a multiplier to work out its new value. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A paint colour is made by mixing red, blue and yellow paint in the ratio 2 : 3 : 5.

**(a)** What fraction of the mixture is blue paint? **[1]**
**(b)** The amount of red paint is what fraction of the amount of yellow paint? Give your answer in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(c)** Lena makes 6 litres of the mixture. How much yellow paint does she use? **[2]**

**6.** (non-calculator) Amir and Beth share a bag of sweets. Amir gets 5/9 of the sweets. Amir gets 12 more sweets than Beth. How many sweets were in the bag? **[3]**

**7.** (non-calculator) A garden centre has 240 plants. 3/8 of the plants are shrubs and the rest are flowers. 2/5 of the shrubs and 1/3 of the flowers are in pots.

**(a)** What fraction of all the plants are in pots? Give your answer in its simplest form. **[4]**
**(b)** How many plants are in pots? **[1]**

**8.** (Higher, non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 7/12 as a recurring decimal. Use dot notation. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that 0.2̇7̇ = 3/11 **[3]**

**9.** (Higher, non-calculator) Write 0.40̇9̇ as a fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher, non-calculator) x = 0.6̇ and y = 0.1̇5̇

**(a)** Write x and y as fractions in their simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out x + y. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(c)** Write your answer to (b) as a recurring decimal. **[1]**

**11.** (calculator) A shop buys 400 phone cases at £3.20 each. It sells 3/5 of the cases at £7.50 each. It sells the rest in a sale at 40% off the £7.50 price.

The manager says, "Our total income from the cases is more than double what we paid for them."

Is the manager correct? Show your working. **[6]**

**12.** (calculator) A colony of 2400 birds increases by 1/6 in the first year. In the second year the number decreases by 15%. In the third year it increases by 5%.

**(a)** Work out the single multiplier that gives the overall change over the three years. Give it as an exact decimal. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the number of birds after three years. **[1]**
**(c)** Work out the overall percentage change over the three years. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 35/100 = **7/20** [1]
**(b)** 9/40 = 225/1000 = **0.225** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Simplest form" means fully cancelled; 35/100 on its own would not earn the mark in (a).

**2.** Converts to decimals with the same places: 0.6800, 0.7000, 0.6900, 0.6950, 0.6875 [1]. Order: **17/25, 11/16, 69%, 0.695, 0.7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark is usually given for correct conversions even if the order slips, but the final mark needs the original values in the right order, not your decimals.

**3. (a)** 72 ÷ 8 × 3 = **27** [1]
**(b)** 10% = 16, so 40% = 64 and 5% = 8 [1]; 64 + 8 = **72** [1]
**(c)** 100% = 60 and 35% = 21, so 135% = **81** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a non-calculator paper, the method mark in (b) comes from showing the building blocks (10%, 5%), so write them down.

**4. (a)** **1.08** [1]
**(b)** 42.50 × 1.08 [1] = **£45.90** [1]
**(c)** Multiplier 1 − 0.22 = 0.78 [1]; 14 600 × 0.78 = **£11 388** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write money to two decimal places when pence are involved; £45.9 is usually penalised on the accuracy mark.

**5. (a)** 2 + 3 + 5 = 10 parts, so blue = **3/10** [1]
**(b)** red/yellow = 2/5, so **2/5** [1]
**(c)** Yellow = 5/10 of the mixture [1]; 1/2 × 6 = **3 litres** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the denominator is the yellow share (5), not the total (10); 2/10 scores nothing.

**6.** Amir gets 5/9, so Beth gets 4/9, and Amir : Beth = 5 : 4 [1]. The difference is 1 part = 12 sweets [1]. Total = 9 parts = 9 × 12 = **108 sweets** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A check (Amir 60, Beth 48, difference 12) is quick and catches the 5 : 9 error before you lose the method marks.

**7. (a)** Flowers = 1 − 3/8 = 5/8 [1]. Shrubs in pots = 2/5 × 3/8 = 3/20; flowers in pots = 1/3 × 5/8 = 5/24 [1]. 3/20 + 5/24 = 18/120 + 25/120 [1] = **43/120** [1]
**(b)** 43/120 × 240 = **86** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) follow-through is normally allowed from a wrong fraction in (a), so finish the calculation even if you doubt (a).

**8. (a)** 7 ÷ 12 = 0.58333... [1] = **0.583̇** [1]
**(b)** Let x = 0.272727...; then 100x = 27.272727... [1]. Subtract: 99x = 27 [1]. x = 27/99 = **3/11** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that", every line must be written, including the subtraction; writing only 27/99 = 3/11 gets at most one mark.

**9.** Let x = 0.4090909...; 10x = 4.090909... and 1000x = 409.090909... [1]. Subtract: 990x = 405 [1]. x = 405/990 = **9/22** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two equations you subtract must have identical recurring tails; 100x and x do not line up here, and that method earns no marks.

**10. (a)** x = 6/9 = **2/3** [1]; y = 15/99 = **5/33** [1]
**(b)** 22/33 + 5/33 = 27/33 = **9/11** [1]
**(c)** **0.8̇1̇** (0.818181...) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs dot notation on both repeating digits, or the decimal written with enough repeats to show the pattern.

**11.** Cost = 400 × 3.20 = £1280 [1]. Full-price cases = 3/5 × 400 = 240 [1]; income = 240 × 7.50 = £1800 [1]. Sale price = 7.50 × 0.6 = £4.50 [1]; income = 160 × 4.50 = £720 [1]. Total = £2520, but double the cost is £2560, so **the manager is not correct** (£40 short) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs a decision backed by the two figures being compared; "No" on its own, or a correct total with no comparison, does not earn it.

**12. (a)** 7/6 × 0.85 × 1.05 [1] = **1.04125** [1]
**(b)** 2400 × 1.04125 = **2499 birds** [1]
**(c)** 1.04125 − 1 = 0.04125 [1], so a **4.125% increase** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rounding 7/6 to 1.17 early gives a different number of birds; keep the exact fraction in the calculator until the end.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not cancelling a fraction fully when the question asks for its simplest form.
- Writing an ordered list as converted decimals instead of the original numbers.
- Reading 0.7 as smaller than 0.695 because 695 looks bigger.
- Using the total, not the other part, as the denominator in "what fraction of" ratio questions.
- Turning "Amir gets 5/9" into the ratio 5 : 9.
- Adding fractions of different groups without first multiplying by the group sizes.
- (Higher) Subtracting equations whose recurring tails do not match.
- (Higher) Leaving out steps on a "show that".
- Rounding an intermediate multiplier and getting a final answer that is not exact.
- Giving a yes/no judgement without the two figures it depends on.
