These notes condense subtopic **3.1 Coordinates** from Topic 3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core and Extended statements are the same: "Use and interpret Cartesian coordinates in two dimensions." Every line below is for both tiers. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [coordinates study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-coordinates/); to test yourself further, use the [coordinates practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-coordinates-practice/).

## The essentials in six lines

1. A point is an ordered pair (x, y): across first, then up or down.
2. Same y-coordinate: the points are on a **horizontal** line, y = that value.
3. Same x-coordinate: the points are on a **vertical** line, x = that value.
4. Distance along a grid line = larger coordinate – smaller coordinate.
5. A **step** between two points = (change in x, change in y).
6. Parallel equal sides of a shape repeat the same step.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Origin | Where the axes cross | (0, 0) |
| x-coordinate | Horizontal position, written first | 4 in (4, –2) |
| y-coordinate | Vertical position, written second | –2 in (4, –2) |
| Ordered pair | Two coordinates in a fixed order | (4, –2) is not (–2, 4) |
| Quadrant | One of the four regions made by the axes | (–3, 5) is top left |
| Vertex (plural vertices) | Corner of a shape | P in parallelogram PQRS |
| Step | Change in x and change in y from one point to another | P(1, 2) to Q(6, 3) is across 5, up 1 |

## Distances along grid lines

```
Horizontal: same y.  Length = difference of the x-coordinates.
Vertical:   same x.  Length = difference of the y-coordinates.

(-7, 2) to (3, 2):   3 - (-7) = 10 units
(4, -5) to (4, 6):   6 - (-5) = 11 units
(-8, 1) to (-2, 1): -2 - (-8) = 6 units
```

A length is never negative. If you get –6, you subtracted the wrong way round; the length is 6.

A point on the **x-axis** has y = 0. A point on the **y-axis** has x = 0. The distance of (5, –3) from the x-axis is 3; its distance from the y-axis is 5.

## Steps and missing vertices

```
Step from A to B = (xB - xA, yB - yA)

Parallelogram PQRS (letters in order):
  step P -> Q  =  step S -> R
  step Q -> R  =  step P -> S

Square: turn a step 90 degrees.
  across a, up b  ->  anticlockwise turn: across -b, up a
                  ->  clockwise turn:     across b,  up -a
```

**Reminder example.** Rectangle KLMN has K(–1, –2), L(5, –2) and M(5, 3). KL is horizontal and LM is vertical, so N is level with M and directly above K: **N = (–1, 3)**.

If the order of the letters is not fixed, a parallelogram with three given vertices has **three** possible fourth vertices. Read the question to see which one is meant.

## Areas from coordinates

- Use a horizontal or vertical side as the base.
- Height = horizontal or vertical distance from the opposite vertex to the line containing the base.
- No horizontal or vertical side? Enclose the shape in a grid-aligned rectangle and subtract the corner triangles.

```
Triangle J(0, 0), K(6, 0), L(2, 5):
  base JK = 6, height = 5 (L is 5 above the x-axis)
  area = 1/2 x 6 x 5 = 15 square units
```

## Interpreting descriptions

| Description | Translate to |
|---|---|
| "lies on the x-axis" | y = 0 |
| "lies on the y-axis" | x = 0 |
| "x-coordinate equals y-coordinate" | x = y, e.g. (3, 3) or (–2, –2) |
| "y-coordinate is 4 less than x-coordinate" | y = x – 4 |
| "directly above P" | same x as P, larger y |
| "level with P" | same y as P |

## Exam traps

- Writing (y, x) instead of (x, y), especially when reading a point in the second or fourth quadrant.
- 2 – (–5) worked out as –3 instead of 7.
- Counting grid lines (one too many) instead of the gaps between them.
- Using the step Q → R from the wrong starting point, which puts the missing vertex on a diagonal.
- Assuming one small square is one unit; check each axis scale.
- Giving "3, 6" without brackets, or using a semicolon or a decimal point in place of the comma.

## Self-test

1. Write down the coordinates of the point 4 units left of the y-axis and 2 units below the x-axis.
2. Find the distance from (–6, 4) to (9, 4).
3. Find the distance from (2, –7) to (2, –1).
4. Is the line joining (3, 8) and (3, –1) horizontal or vertical?
5. Write down the step from A(–2, 5) to B(4, 1).
6. Parallelogram ABCD has A(0, 0), B(5, 1) and C(7, 4). Find D.
7. Square PQRS has P(1, 1) and Q(4, 1), and the square lies above PQ. Find R and S.
8. Find the area of the triangle with vertices (–3, 0), (5, 0) and (1, 6).
9. A point lies on the line y = 2 and its x-coordinate is three times its y-coordinate. Write down its coordinates.
10. Which point is further from the x-axis: (7, –2) or (–1, 5)?
11. A rectangle has vertices (–4, –1), (2, –1), (2, 3) and (–4, 3). Find its perimeter.
12. Point M is on the y-axis and level with (6, –5). Write down the coordinates of M.

### Answers

1. **(–4, –2)**
2. 9 – (–6) = **15 units**
3. –1 – (–7) = **6 units**
4. Same x-coordinate, so **vertical**
5. Across 4 – (–2) = 6, up 1 – 5 = –4: **(6, –4)**, i.e. 6 across and 4 down
6. Step B → C is across 2, up 3; D = A + that step = **(2, 3)**
7. PQ is across 3 along y = 1, so the side is 3 units: **R(4, 4), S(1, 4)**
8. Base 5 – (–3) = 8, height 6; area = ½ × 8 × 6 = **24 square units**
9. y = 2, x = 3 × 2 = 6: **(6, 2)**
10. Distance from the x-axis is the size of the y-coordinate: 2 and 5, so **(–1, 5)**
11. Length 2 – (–4) = 6, width 3 – (–1) = 4; perimeter = 2 × (6 + 4) = **20 units**
12. On the y-axis, x = 0; level with (6, –5), y = –5: **(0, –5)**
