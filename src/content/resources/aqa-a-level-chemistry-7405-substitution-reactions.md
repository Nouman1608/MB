## What a substitution reaction is

In a ligand substitution (ligand exchange) reaction, one or more ligands in a complex are replaced by different ligands. The central metal ion stays the same, the bonds to the old ligands break, and new co-ordinate bonds form. The oxidation state of the metal does not change.

## Monodentate ligands: water, ammonia and chloride

A monodentate ligand forms **one** co-ordinate bond to the metal ion, using one lone pair on one donor atom.

| Ligand | Formula | Donor atom | Charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Water | H₂O | O | 0 |
| Ammonia | NH₃ | N | 0 |
| Chloride | Cl⁻ | Cl | 1– |

### Exchange of water and ammonia: no change in co-ordination number

Water and ammonia are similar in size and both are uncharged. An ammonia molecule can therefore take the place of a water molecule without changing the arrangement of the ligands around the metal ion. The co-ordination number stays at 6, and the shape stays octahedral.

```
[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ ⇌ [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O       (pink to straw-coloured)
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ ⇌ [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O  (blue to deep blue)
```

For copper(II), **substitution is incomplete**: four water molecules are replaced but two remain, giving [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺. Even with excess ammonia, copper does not form [Cu(NH₃)₆]²⁺ in water. This is a standard example to quote.

Because the ligands are uncharged and similar in size, the overall charge of the complex does not change (2+ before and after).

### Exchange of water and chloride: change in co-ordination number

A chloride ion is much larger than a water molecule or an ammonia molecule. Only four chloride ions can fit round the central metal ion, while six water molecules can. The co-ordination number falls from 6 to 4, and the shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral.

```
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O        (blue to yellow-green)
[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O        (pink to blue)
[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [FeCl₄]⁻ + 6H₂O         (yellow-brown to yellow)
```

These reactions need a high concentration of chloride ions, so concentrated hydrochloric acid is used. They are reversible: adding water makes the equilibrium shift back towards the aqua complex. Notice that the charge on the complex changes, as the chloride ligand is charged. Also notice that the number of particles rises from 5 on the left (1 complex and 4 chloride ions) to 7 on the right (1 complex and 6 water molecules), which is a gain in entropy.

### Worked example 1: write the equation

Write an equation for the reaction when concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to a solution containing [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺, and state two observations.

- Cl⁻ replaces H₂O, and the co-ordination number changes from 6 to 4.
- Equation: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O.
- Check atoms (Co 1, Cl 4, H 12, O 6 on both sides) and charge (2+ and 4– gives 2–; 2– on the right).
- The solution changes from pink to blue.

## Bidentate and multidentate ligands

A **bidentate** ligand has two donor atoms, each with a lone pair, and forms two co-ordinate bonds to the same metal ion. A **multidentate** ligand has more than two donor atoms.

| Ligand | Formula | Donor atoms | Type |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1,2-diaminoethane (en) | H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ | 2 N | Bidentate |
| Ethanedioate | C₂O₄²⁻ | 2 O | Bidentate |
| EDTA⁴⁻ | (⁻OOCCH₂)₂NCH₂CH₂N(CH₂COO⁻)₂ | 2 N and 4 O | Hexadentate (multidentate) |

Each bidentate ligand occupies two positions around the metal ion, so three of them fill an octahedral complex.

```
[Ni(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 3H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ → [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ + 6H₂O
[Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ + 3C₂O₄²⁻ → [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ + 6H₂O
[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O
```

EDTA wraps around the metal ion using all six donor atoms, so one EDTA ion replaces six water molecules. The co-ordination number remains 6.

## Biological examples: haem, oxygen and carbon monoxide

**Haem** is an iron(II) complex containing a multidentate ligand (a ring with four nitrogen donor atoms). It is the part of haemoglobin that carries oxygen. The iron(II) is also bonded to a nitrogen of the protein, and the sixth position is used by an oxygen molecule.

**Oxygen transport:** oxygen forms a reversible co-ordinate bond to Fe(II) in haemoglobin. In the lungs, where there is a lot of oxygen, it binds. In the tissues it is released.

**Carbon monoxide poisoning:** carbon monoxide forms a co-ordinate bond to the Fe(II) in haemoglobin that is much stronger than the bond to oxygen, and it replaces the oxygen. The haemoglobin can no longer carry oxygen, which is why CO is toxic.

## The chelate effect

When a bidentate or multidentate ligand replaces monodentate ligands, the new complex is more stable than expected. This is the **chelate effect**. The explanation is in terms of entropy and enthalpy.

Consider the reaction:

```
[Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 3H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂ → [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ + 6NH₃
```

- **Enthalpy:** six Ni–N bonds are broken and six Ni–N bonds are formed, of very similar strength, so the enthalpy change is close to zero.
- **Entropy:** there are 4 particles on the left (1 complex + 3 ligand molecules) and 7 on the right (1 complex + 6 ammonia molecules). More particles means more ways of arranging energy, so there is an increase in disorder: ΔS is positive.
- **Free energy:** ΔG = ΔH – TΔS. With ΔH close to zero and ΔS positive, ΔG is negative. The reaction is feasible and the equilibrium lies strongly on the product side.

### Worked example 2: a chelate calculation

For a substitution of this type, suppose ΔH = –2.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +120 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ at 298 K. Calculate ΔG. (These are illustrative values.)

```
TΔS = 298 × 0.120 = 35.8 kJ mol⁻¹
ΔG = ΔH – TΔS = –2.0 – 35.8 = –37.8 kJ mol⁻¹
```

ΔG is negative, so the reaction is feasible, and it is driven by the entropy increase.

### Worked example 3: EDTA

Explain why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O goes to completion.

There are 2 particles on the left and 7 on the right, so entropy increases greatly (ΔS is positive). Six Cu–O bonds are broken but six Cu–O or Cu–N bonds form, so ΔH is small. ΔG = ΔH – TΔS is therefore large and negative, and the product is much more stable.

## Common mistakes

- Saying water and ammonia exchange changes the co-ordination number. It does not.
- Forgetting that the substitution of copper by ammonia is incomplete in water.
- Explaining the change to four-co-ordinate with chloride as "chloride is bigger and has a charge" without saying that fewer can fit.
- Writing the equation for chloride without balancing the charge.
- Explaining the chelate effect with enthalpy alone. The main factor is the increase in entropy.
- Saying CO is toxic because it "reacts with" haemoglobin. It replaces oxygen, binding more strongly.
- Counting a bidentate ligand as one bond when working out the co-ordination number.
- Thinking the oxidation state of the metal changes in substitution.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Write an equation**: balance atoms and charge; use ⇌ for chloride and ammonia exchange.
- **Explain the chelate effect**: count particles on each side, state the sign of ΔS, say ΔH is about zero because the same number and type of bonds form, and conclude ΔG is negative.
- **Explain why CO is toxic**: it forms a stronger bond to Fe(II) than oxygen and replaces it.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does a four-co-ordinate complex form with chloride but not with ammonia?**
Chloride ions are larger than ammonia molecules and repel each other, so fewer fit round the metal ion. Ammonia is similar in size to water, so the same number fits.

**Does the metal ion change colour in every substitution?**
Usually, because the ligand changes the energy gap between d orbitals. This is explained in the study guide for coloured ions.

**Is the chelate effect only about entropy?**
Entropy is the main reason when the bond enthalpy change is close to zero. If the new bonds are stronger, enthalpy contributes too.
