## Equations (all recall and use)

| Equation | Tier | Rearranged |
|---|---|---|
| R = V / I | Core | V = IR; I = V / R |
| P = IV | Core | I = P / V |
| E = IVt | Core | t = E / (IV) |
| energy (kW h) = power (kW) × time (h) | Core | cost = kW h × price |
| I = Q / t | Extended only | Q = It |
| E = W / Q (e.m.f.) | Extended only | W = QE |
| V = W / Q (p.d.) | Extended only | W = QV |

- 1 kW h = 1000 W × 3600 s = 3.6 × 10⁶ J.
- Convert: kΩ × 1000 → Ω; mA ÷ 1000 → A; minutes × 60 → s.

## 4.2.1 Charge

- Two kinds: positive and negative. Like repel, unlike attract.
- Charging by friction: **only electrons (negative charge) are transferred**.
- Gains electrons → negative; loses electrons → positive.
- Detecting charge: a charged rod attracts small pieces of paper; a suspended charged rod turns towards or away from another charged rod.
- Conductor test: put the sample in a series circuit with a cell and lamp (or ammeter); lamp lights → conductor.
- Metals conduct: free electrons. Insulators (plastic, rubber, glass, dry wood): electrons held in atoms.

**Extended only**

- Charge is measured in **coulombs (C)**.
- **Electric field:** region in which an electric charge experiences a force; direction = force on a **positive** charge.
- Point charge or charged sphere: radial lines (outwards from +, inwards to −).
- Parallel plates: parallel, evenly spaced lines from + plate to − plate.

## 4.2.2 Current

- Current is related to the flow of charge; in metals, a flow of **free electrons**.
- Ammeter in **series**; choose the range just above the expected value.
- d.c. flows one way; a.c. keeps reversing.
- **Extended only:** current = charge per unit time; conventional current + → −; electrons − → +.

## 4.2.3 e.m.f. and p.d.

- **e.m.f.:** electrical work done by a source in moving unit charge around a complete circuit.
- **p.d.:** work done by unit charge passing through a component.
- Both in **volts**; voltmeter in **parallel**.

## 4.2.4 Resistance

- R = V/I in ohms (Ω).
- Experiment: component in series with supply, ammeter and variable resistor; voltmeter across the component; several readings; R = V/I each time; average.
- Longer wire → more R. Thicker wire → less R.
- **Extended only:** R ∝ length; R ∝ 1 / cross-sectional area.

**I–V graphs (Extended only)**

| Component | Shape | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Resistor (constant temperature) | Straight line through origin | R constant |
| Filament lamp | Curve, less steep at high p.d. | Filament heats up, R increases |
| Diode | Zero in reverse; rises steeply forward after a small p.d. | Very high R reverse, low R forward |

## 4.2.5 Energy and power

- Energy goes source → components → surroundings (heating, light).
- P = IV; E = IVt.
- **Kilowatt-hour:** energy transferred by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conductor | Material with free electrons that allows charge to flow |
| Insulator | Material whose electrons cannot move freely |
| Direct current | Current in one direction only |
| Alternating current | Current that repeatedly reverses direction |
| e.m.f. | Work done by a source per unit charge round a complete circuit |
| p.d. | Work done per unit charge passing through a component |
| Resistance | p.d. across a component ÷ current through it |
| Kilowatt-hour | Energy used by a 1 kW appliance in 1 hour |
| Electric field (Extended only) | Region where a charge experiences a force |

## Exam traps

- Positive charges do **not** move during charging by friction.
- Time must be in **seconds** for E = IVt in joules, but in **hours** for kW h.
- A thicker wire has **less** resistance.
- The lamp curve needs the reason: temperature rises, so resistance rises.
- Electric field direction is the force on a **positive** charge.
- A voltmeter in series or an ammeter in parallel loses the mark at once.

## Self-test

1. A polythene rod becomes negative when rubbed with a cloth. Explain why.
2. Explain, using electrons, why copper conducts but plastic does not.
3. What is the difference between a.c. and d.c.?
4. Find the resistance of a component with 9.0 V across it and 0.30 A through it.
5. A 3.0 V torch bulb takes 0.30 A for 20 minutes. Find the power and the energy transferred.
6. A 3.0 kW heater is used for 2.5 hours a day for 30 days. Electricity costs 18 cents per kW h. Find the cost.
7. How does the resistance of a wire change if it is made longer?
8. *(Extended)* 90 C passes a point in 1.5 minutes. Find the current.
9. *(Extended)* A battery transfers 36 J to 4.0 C of charge. Find its e.m.f.
10. *(Extended)* A 1.2 m wire has resistance 4.0 Ω. Find the resistance of 3.6 m of the same wire with three times the cross-sectional area.
11. *(Extended)* Describe the electric field between two oppositely charged parallel plates.
12. *(Extended)* Why does the I–V graph for a filament lamp curve?

### Answers

1. **Electrons** move from the cloth to the rod, so the rod has extra electrons.
2. Copper has **free electrons** that move through it; in plastic the electrons are held in their atoms.
3. d.c. flows in **one direction** only; a.c. **repeatedly reverses** direction.
4. R = 9.0 ÷ 0.30 = **30 Ω**.
5. P = 0.30 × 3.0 = **0.90 W**; E = 0.90 × (20 × 60) = **1080 J**.
6. Energy = 3.0 × 2.5 × 30 = 225 kW h; cost = 225 × 18 = **4050 cents**.
7. It **increases**.
8. I = 90 ÷ 90 = **1.0 A**.
9. E = 36 ÷ 4.0 = **9.0 V**.
10. × 3 for length = 12 Ω; ÷ 3 for area = **4.0 Ω**.
11. **Parallel, evenly spaced** lines from the positive plate to the negative plate.
12. As the current increases the filament **gets hotter**, so its **resistance increases**.
