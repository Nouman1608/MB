## Key definitions

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Microeconomics | The study of the decisions of individual households, workers and firms, and of individual markets |
| Macroeconomics | The study of the economy as a whole |
| Decision maker | A person or organisation that chooses how to use resources |
| General price level | The average level of prices across the economy |
| Inflation | A sustained rise in the general price level |
| Total output | The value of all goods and services produced in the economy |

## Side by side

| Feature | Micro | Macro |
|---|---|---|
| Focus | One household, worker, firm or market | The whole economy |
| Prices | Price of one good | General price level, inflation |
| Jobs | Wages and jobs in one occupation or firm | Total employment and unemployment |
| Output | One firm or industry | Total output and economic growth |
| Trade | One firm's exports | The country's balance of payments |
| Decision makers | Households, workers, firms, trade unions, banks | Government, with the central bank |
| Syllabus home | Topics 2 and 3 | Topic 4 |

Topics 5 and 6 use both.

## Decision makers and examples

**Micro**

- Households: buy a new fridge or repair the old one.
- Workers: accept a job offer in another city.
- Firms: raise the price of a product; hire two more staff.
- Trade unions: negotiate wages for nurses.
- Banks: approve or refuse a loan to a business.
- Government in one market: tax on tobacco; minimum price for milk.

**Macro**

- Government: change income tax to influence total spending (fiscal policy).
- Central bank: change interest rates or the money supply (monetary policy).
- Government: policies to raise productive capacity, such as training schemes (supply-side policy).

## The links

- **Micro → macro:** the totals are built from individual decisions. Many households spending less → total spending falls.
- **Macro → micro:** economy-wide conditions change individual choices. Higher interest rates → a household delays borrowing for a car; a firm delays investment.

**Worked reminder (invented).** 800 shops in a city each cut 5 staff in a downturn: 800 × 5 = 4,000 jobs. If the labour force is 500,000, unemployment rises by 4,000 ÷ 500,000 × 100 = **0.8 percentage points**. Each shop's choice is micro; the change in the unemployment rate is macro.

## Classification test

Ask: **is the question about one decision maker or market, or about the economy as a whole?**

| Statement | Answer |
|---|---|
| The price of cooking oil doubles after a drought | Micro (one market) |
| Prices across the economy rise by 4% a year | Macro (inflation) |
| A government sets a minimum wage for all workers in the country | Macro in scope, but it works through labour markets; say both |
| A car maker moves production abroad | Micro |
| National output falls for two quarters | Macro |
| A trade union calls a strike at one port | Micro |

Mixed cases are common: name the micro decision and the macro cause or effect.

## Signal words

- **Macro:** total, general, national, the economy, aggregate, the country's.
- **Micro:** a firm, a household, a market, an industry, one occupation, a product.

## Must-know distinctions

- Size of firm is not the test: a giant firm is still micro.
- Government is not automatically macro: intervening in one market is micro.
- A rise in one price is not inflation.
- Unemployment in one firm is micro; the national unemployment rate is macro.

## Exam traps

- Defining micro as "small" and macro as "big" with no reference to individual units versus the whole economy.
- Leaving out decision makers when the question asks "who".
- Treating a rise in the price of one good as inflation.
- Classifying every government action as macro.
- Giving a classification with no reason.

## Quick self-test

1. Define microeconomics.
2. Define macroeconomics.
3. Name **three** microeconomic decision makers listed in the syllabus.
4. Name the main macroeconomic decision maker.
5. Is a rise in the price of petrol a micro or macro issue?
6. Is a rise in the general price level a micro or macro issue?
7. A government puts a tax on sugary drinks. Micro or macro? Why?
8. A government cuts income tax for everyone to raise total spending. Micro or macro? Why?
9. Give **one** way a macro event can affect a micro decision.
10. Give **one** way micro decisions add up to a macro outcome.
11. Why is a very large firm's pricing decision still micro?
12. 2,000 firms each cut 3 jobs and the labour force is 1.5 million. By how many percentage points does the unemployment rate rise?

### Answers

1. The study of the decisions of individual households, workers and firms, and of individual markets.
2. The study of the economy as a whole.
3. Any three: households, workers, firms, trade unions, banks.
4. The government (with the central bank for monetary policy).
5. Micro: one market.
6. Macro: it is economy-wide (inflation).
7. Micro: it targets one market.
8. Macro: it aims to change total spending in the whole economy.
9. Any one: higher interest rates make a household delay a loan; a recession makes a firm cut jobs.
10. Any one: many households saving more reduces total spending; many firms cutting jobs raises unemployment.
11. It concerns one decision maker and one market, not the economy as a whole.
12. 6,000 ÷ 1,500,000 × 100 = 0.4 percentage points.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can one event be both micro and macro?**
Yes. A firm closing a factory is a micro decision, but if it happens because of a recession, its cause is macro, and many closures together raise national unemployment.

**Which parts of the 2281 syllabus are microeconomics?**
Topics 2 and 3 are mainly microeconomics and Topic 4 is macroeconomics. Topics 5 and 6 draw on both.
