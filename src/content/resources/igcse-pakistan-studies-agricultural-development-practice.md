**What do these questions practise?** Every part of Section 4 Agricultural development in
Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448 Paper 2: farming systems, crop and livestock locations,
natural and human factors, irrigation, waterlogging and salinity, and government action. Work
through Section A for recall, then attempt the longer explain and evaluate items under timed
conditions.

## Section A: recall

**1.** Name **one** kharif crop and **one** rabi crop. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** uses of cotton other than making cloth. **[2]**

**3.** Name **one** area of Pakistan well known for growing rice. **[1]**

**4.** What is meant by *barani* farming? **[1]**

**5.** State **two** products obtained from buffalo. **[2]**

**6.** Name **one** fruit grown in Pakistan, state where it is grown and give **one** use. **[3]**

## Section B: explanation

**7.** Using the idea of a system, describe the inputs, processes and outputs of a small-scale
subsistence farm growing rice. **[5]**

**8.** Explain the climatic requirements of cotton. **[4]**

**9.** Describe how a karez works and give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of it. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how perennial canal irrigation can lead to waterlogging and salinity. **[4]**

**11.** Describe how land damaged by salinity can be restored. **[3]**

**12.** Explain what is meant by transhumance and why it is practised in the mountains of
Pakistan. **[3]**

## Section C: evaluation

**13.** Compare the tubewell and the inundation canal as methods of irrigation for a farmer growing
wheat. Which is more suitable? **[6]**

**14.** "Government action has been successful in increasing agricultural production in Pakistan."
How far do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Kharif: cotton, rice, sugar cane, maize or millet [1]. Rabi: wheat, gram or another pulse, or
an oilseed such as mustard [1].

**2.** Any two: cottonseed oil for cooking [1]; seed cake for animal feed [1]; stalks as fuel [1].

**3.** Gujranwala, Sialkot or Sheikhupura (the rice belt of northern Punjab), or Larkana in northern
Sindh [1].

**4.** Farming that depends on rainfall, with no irrigation [1].

**5.** Any two: milk, meat, hides (leather), dung (fuel or manure), draught power [2].

**6.** Example: mangoes [1], grown around Multan in southern Punjab (or in Sindh) [1], eaten fresh,
exported, or made into juice and pickles [1]. Accept other correct fruit–location–use sets, such as
dates from Khairpur, or apples from the Balochistan uplands.

**7.** Inputs (up to 2): small plot of land, family labour, seed, oxen, canal water, small amounts of
manure [1–2]. Processes (up to 2): ploughing, raising seedlings in a nursery, transplanting by
hand, flooding fields, weeding, harvesting and threshing [1–2]. Outputs (up to 2): rice mainly for
family food, with a small surplus for sale; straw for fodder [1–2]. Maximum 5.

**8.** A long frost-free growing season of around six months [1]; high summer temperatures [1];
plenty of sunshine [1]; a reliable water supply, usually from irrigation [1]; dry, sunny weather at
picking time so the bolls are not damaged [1]. Maximum 4.

**9.** A karez is a tunnel dug from the water table at the foot of a hill, sloping gently down to
the fields, with vertical shafts for access and cleaning [1–2]. Advantage: water flows by gravity
and is protected from evaporation [1]. Disadvantage: tunnels are costly in labour to dig and keep
clear, and may collapse [1].

**10.** Water seeps from unlined canals and from over-irrigated fields into the ground [1], so the
water table rises [1]. When it reaches near the surface the soil is waterlogged and roots are
starved of air [1]. High temperatures then evaporate water at the surface, leaving behind salts,
causing salinity [1].

**11.** Any three: tubewells pump water out to lower the water table [1]; drainage channels remove
salty water [1]; fields are flushed with fresh water to wash salts down [1]; salt-tolerant crops
are planted while the soil recovers [1].

**12.** Transhumance is the seasonal movement of herders and animals between pastures [1]. In summer,
flocks go up to high meadows where snow has melted and grass grows [1]; in winter, cold and snow
force them down to valleys or plains where fodder is available [1].

**13.** Tubewell, for: water available whenever the crop needs it, including in winter for rabi
wheat [1]; also lowers a high water table [1]. Against: diesel or electricity costs; power cuts
interrupt pumping; over-pumping lowers groundwater [1]. Inundation canal, for: cheap, no pumping
cost [1]. Against: water only in the summer flood season, which is the wrong season for wheat
sown in winter [1]. Judgement: the tubewell is more suitable for wheat because wheat is a rabi crop
grown when inundation canals are dry [1]. Maximum 6.

**14.** For: land reforms set ceilings on holding size [1]; high-yielding seed, fertilisers and
pesticides raised yields of wheat and rice [1]; loans for tractors and tubewells [1]; training
through extension services [1]. Against: larger farmers benefited most because they had capital
and credit [1]; land reform had limited effect and holdings remain fragmented [1]; chemicals
raised costs and polluted water [1]; waterlogging and salinity still reduce output [1]. A
supported judgement is needed for full marks. Maximum 6.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I score full marks on a compare question?**
Discuss both methods with advantages and
disadvantages, apply them to the crop named, and finish with a clear choice.

**Do I need production statistics?**
Not usually for this section, but you must link each crop to
named areas and give its natural requirements.
