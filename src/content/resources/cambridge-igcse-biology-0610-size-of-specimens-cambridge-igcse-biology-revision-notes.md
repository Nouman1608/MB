## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnification | How many times larger an image is than the real object; no units, written as ×n |
| Image size | The size of the specimen as it appears in a drawing, photograph or micrograph |
| Actual size | The real size of the specimen |
| Scale bar | A line on an image labelled with the actual length it represents |
| Millimetre (mm) | One thousandth of a metre |
| Micrometre (μm) | One thousandth of a millimetre (Extended) |

## The formula

**magnification = image size ÷ actual size**

| Find | Formula |
|---|---|
| Magnification | image ÷ actual |
| Actual size | image ÷ magnification |
| Image size | magnification × actual |

**Triangle:** I on top; A and M below. Cover what you want.

## Five-step method

1. Write the formula.
2. Measure the image in mm if needed, and write the value down.
3. Make both sizes the **same unit**.
4. Substitute and calculate.
5. Give the answer with the unit (mm or μm) or with × for magnification.

## Sense checks

- For a magnified image, magnification is **greater than 1**.
- Actual size must be **smaller** than image size.
- If magnification comes out below 1, you divided the wrong way round.
- (Extended) A length in μm is a **bigger number** than the same length in mm.

## Scale bars in two steps

1. Magnification = measured length of scale bar ÷ the length written on it.
2. Actual size of the specimen = its measured image size ÷ that magnification.

**Example:** bar measures 25 mm and is labelled 0.1 mm → ×250. Cell measures 15 mm → 15 ÷ 250 = **0.06 mm**.

## Several cells in a row

Measure across *n* cells, find the actual length, then divide by *n*. **Example:** 4 cells span 48 mm at ×400 → 48 ÷ 400 = 0.12 mm → 0.12 ÷ 4 = **0.03 mm** per cell.

## Extended only: mm and μm

**1 mm = 1000 μm**

- mm → μm: **× 1000**. 0.4 mm = 400 μm.
- μm → mm: **÷ 1000**. 15 μm = 0.015 mm.

**Mixed-unit example:** image 30 mm; actual 6 μm. 30 mm = 30 000 μm; 30 000 ÷ 6 = **×5000**.

## Quick conversions to practise

| mm | μm |
|---|---|
| 1 | 1000 |
| 0.5 | 500 |
| 0.02 | 20 |
| 0.003 | 3 |
| 2.5 | 2500 |

## Your own drawing on a practical paper

1. Measure a stated line on your drawing, in mm.
2. Use the actual size you are given, or that you measured on the specimen with a ruler.
3. Magnification of the drawing = your drawing's length ÷ the specimen's actual length.
4. **Example:** your drawing of a seed is 64 mm long; the real seed is 16 mm long → 64 ÷ 16 = **×4**.

A drawing is usually larger than the specimen, so expect a magnification above 1.

## Rounding

- Give magnification as a whole number unless the question asks otherwise, for example ×583 rather than ×583.33.
- Give sizes to a sensible number of decimal places, matching the data given.
- Keep the unrounded value in your calculator for the next step.

## Exam traps

- No units on magnification; include the × sign.
- Never divide mm by μm; convert first.
- Measure from 0 on the ruler, in mm.
- Write down your measurement before using it.
- Round only at the end.
- "Calculate" questions give method marks: show the formula and substitution.

## Quick self-test

1. State the formula for magnification.
2. Does magnification have units?
3. A drawing of a leaf is 90 mm long; the leaf is 30 mm long. Calculate the magnification.
4. A drawing at ×5 is 45 mm long. Calculate the actual length.
5. A cell 0.05 mm wide is shown at ×800. Calculate the image width.
6. A scale bar labelled 1 mm measures 40 mm. What is the magnification?
7. In the same image as question 6, a cell is 12 mm across. Calculate its actual size.
8. A student gets a magnification of ×0.25 for a microscope image. What has gone wrong?
9. (Extended) How many μm are in 1 mm?
10. (Extended) Convert 0.008 mm to μm.
11. (Extended) Convert 450 μm to mm.
12. (Extended) An image is 24 mm wide; the actual cell is 12 μm wide. Calculate the magnification.

### Answers

1. Magnification = image size ÷ actual size.
2. No. It is written as ×n.
3. 90 ÷ 30 = **×3**.
4. 45 ÷ 5 = **9 mm**.
5. 800 × 0.05 = **40 mm**.
6. 40 ÷ 1 = **×40**.
7. 12 ÷ 40 = **0.3 mm**.
8. They divided actual size by image size (the wrong way round).
9. 1000.
10. 0.008 × 1000 = **8 μm**.
11. 450 ÷ 1000 = **0.45 mm**.
12. 24 mm = 24 000 μm; 24 000 ÷ 12 = **×2000**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the wrong way round.
- Leaving out the × sign, or adding units to magnification.
- Using a measurement in mm with an actual size in μm without converting.
- Not writing down the measured length, so no method mark can be given.

## Frequently asked questions

**What unit should I measure images in?**
Millimetres, with a ruler; Core calculations use millimetres throughout.

**What do I do if the actual size is given in micrometres?**
Extended candidates convert one measurement so both are in the same unit, using 1 mm = 1000 μm, before dividing.
