> **Case study: Nyota Leather Goods**
>
> Nyota Leather Goods is based in a developing country, where wages are
> lower than in its main markets. It makes handbags by hand and exports
> 12,000 a year to retailers in developed countries at a price of $45
> each. Each bag costs $30 to make, including $6 of imported zips and
> buckles. Two of its most skilled leatherworkers recently left to work
> abroad. The home currency has just strengthened sharply. The managing
> director is choosing between two options:
>
> - **Option A:** keep the price at $45. Retailers will pay more in their
>   own currencies, and sales are expected to fall to 10,500 bags.
> - **Option B:** cut the price to $42, so that retailers pay about the
>   same as before. Sales are expected to stay at 12,000 bags.
>
> Because of the stronger currency, the imported zips and buckles will
> now cost $5 a bag.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a form of globalisation named in the specification? **[1]**
- A. Inflation
- B. Movement of labour
- C. Interest rates
- D. National minimum wage

**2.** A country's currency weakens. Which **one** of these businesses in that country is most likely to benefit directly? **[1]**
- A. A factory that imports its machinery
- B. A farm that exports fruit
- C. A haulage firm that buys imported fuel
- D. A shop that sells imported clothes

**3.** Define the term "globalisation". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** drawbacks of globalisation for the economy of a developing country. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which specialisation by country can benefit a business. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate Nyota's current yearly profit from its exports. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate Nyota's yearly profit under Option A and under Option B, using the new cost of zips and buckles. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which globalisation has benefited Nyota Leather Goods. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how the movement of labour has affected Nyota. **[3]**

**10.** Analyse the effects on Nyota of severe fluctuations in the exchange rate. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate whether Nyota should choose Option A or Option B. Use your answers to Questions 6 and 7. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The process by which countries become more closely linked [1] through trade, investment and the movement of people and ideas across borders [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: local businesses may close because they cannot compete with large foreign firms [1]; skilled workers may emigrate [1]; profits of foreign-owned businesses may leave the country; the economy may depend on a few foreign buyers. **[2]**

**5.** A business in a country that specialises, for example in growing coffee, can produce in very large volumes [1], which lowers its cost per unit and lets it sell competitively to buyers in many countries [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Profit per bag = $45 − $30 = $15 [1]. Yearly profit = 12,000 × $15 = **$180,000** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** New cost per bag = $30 − $6 + $5 = $29 [1]. Option A: profit per bag = $45 − $29 = $16; profit = 10,500 × $16 = **$168,000** [1]. Option B: profit per bag = $42 − $29 = $13 [1]; profit = 12,000 × $13 = **$156,000** [1]. **[4]**

**8.** **Larger markets:** Nyota sells to retailers in developed countries [1], so it reaches far more customers than in its home market alone, supporting sales of 12,000 bags a year [1].
**Relative cost advantage:** wages are lower in Nyota's country [1], so it can make hand-made bags more cheaply than businesses in its developed-country markets, helping it win orders [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Two of Nyota's most skilled leatherworkers have left to work abroad [1], so Nyota has lost skills that are hard to replace in hand-made production [1]. It may have to spend time and money training new workers, and quality or output could fall in the meantime [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Nyota's costs and prices are in home currency, but its customers pay in their own currencies [1]. A sharp swing means the price retailers pay can change suddenly [1], so Nyota cannot be sure how many bags will be ordered, which makes it hard to plan production and staffing [1]. Its imported zips and buckles can also change in cost between ordering and paying [1], so its profit per bag becomes uncertain [1]. This uncertainty may make it harder to borrow or invest in new equipment, because Nyota cannot forecast profit with confidence [1]. **[6]**

**11.** **For Option A:** it gives the higher profit, $168,000 against $156,000 [1]; Nyota keeps a higher price per bag, which supports its image as a hand-made product [1]; it makes 1,500 fewer bags, which helps while it is short of skilled workers [1].
**For Option B:** it keeps all 12,000 sales, so retailers are not tempted to try rival suppliers [1]; profit is still $156,000, only $24,000 below the original $180,000 [1]; if the currency weakens again, Nyota can return to its old price without having lost customers [1].
**Judgement:** Option A is better on the figures, by $12,000, and suits Nyota's shortage of skilled staff [1]. However, it depends on whether the fall to 10,500 is an accurate forecast; if retailers are more sensitive to price and buy fewer bags, Option A could earn less than Option B [1]. If Nyota believes the strong currency is temporary, Option B protects relationships with retailers for the future [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting that the stronger currency also lowers the cost of imported zips and buckles.
- Getting the direction wrong: a stronger home currency makes exports dearer, not cheaper, for foreign buyers.
- Writing about globalisation in general instead of using Nyota's lower wages and export markets.
- Treating movement of labour as only a benefit; here it has cost Nyota skilled staff.
- Choosing an option on profit alone without considering how reliable the sales forecast is.
