## Questions

Where a question needs chemical shift ranges, use these approximate values, written for this practice
set. For your own revision, work from the AQA Chemistry Data Booklet itself.

| ¹H environment | δ / ppm | ¹³C environment | δ / ppm |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkyl CH₃, CH₂, CH | 0.7–1.7 | Alkyl C–C | 5–50 |
| H–C next to C=O | 2.0–2.7 | C–O | 50–90 |
| H–C next to O | 3.3–4.3 | C=O in acids and esters | 160–185 |
| Aldehyde –CHO | 9.0–10.0 | C=O in aldehydes and ketones | 190–220 |
| Acid –COOH | 10.0–12.0 | | |

For infrared, take O–H (alcohols) as a broad absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹, O–H (acids) as a very broad
absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹, and C=O at 1680–1750 cm⁻¹.

**1.** Tetramethylsilane (TMS) is added to samples before NMR spectra are recorded. Give **three**
reasons why TMS is a suitable standard. **[3]**

**2.** A student plans to dissolve a sample in trichloromethane, CHCl₃, to record its ¹H NMR spectrum.
Explain why CDCl₃ or CCl₄ should be used instead. **[2]**

**3.** State the number of peaks in the ¹³C NMR spectrum of each compound.

**(a)** propan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂OH **[1]**
**(b)** propan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ **[1]**
**(c)** 2,2-dimethylbutane, (CH₃)₃CCH₂CH₃ **[1]**
**(d)** cyclohexane, C₆H₁₂ **[1]**

**4.** Compound P has the molecular formula C₇H₁₄O₂. Its ¹H NMR spectrum has three peaks:

| δ / ppm | Splitting | Integration trace height / mm |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | quartet | 6 |
| 1.3 | triplet | 9 |
| 1.2 | singlet | 27 |

**(a)** Use the integration data to find the number of H atoms in each environment. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the splitting of each peak. **[3]**
**(c)** Use the chemical shift of the quartet to suggest a structure for P. **[1]**

**5.** 1,1-dichloroethane and 1,2-dichloroethane are isomers. Predict the ¹H NMR spectrum of each,
giving the number of peaks, their relative integration and their splitting, and explain how the spectra
distinguish the two compounds. **[4]**

**6.** Three ketones have the formula C₅H₁₀O: pentan-2-one, pentan-3-one and 3-methylbutan-2-one. State
the number of peaks in the ¹³C NMR spectrum of each, and so explain whether ¹³C NMR can distinguish all
three. **[3]**

**7.** Predict the ¹H NMR spectrum of ethanal, CH₃CHO. For each peak, give its approximate chemical shift
range, its splitting and its relative integration. **[3]**

**8.** Compound Q has the formula C₄H₁₀O.

- Infrared: broad absorption at 3360 cm⁻¹.
- ¹³C NMR: two peaks, at δ 69 and δ 31.
- ¹H NMR: two singlets, with integration ratio 9 : 1.

**(a)** Identify the functional group shown by the infrared spectrum. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the structure of Q, explaining how the NMR data support it. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain why the ¹³C spectrum rules out butan-1-ol. **[1]**

**9.** Compound R gives a molecular ion at m/z = 102. Its molecular formula is C₅H₁₀O₂. Its infrared
spectrum has a strong absorption at 1740 cm⁻¹ and no absorption from O–H. Its ¹H NMR spectrum is:

| δ / ppm | Splitting | Relative integration |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1 | quartet | 2 |
| 2.3 | quartet | 2 |
| 1.3 | triplet | 3 |
| 1.1 | triplet | 3 |

**(a)** Deduce the structure of R. Explain how each piece of data supports your answer. **[6]**
**(b)** State the number of peaks you would expect in the ¹³C NMR spectrum of R. **[1]**

**10.** Compound S is an isomer of R in question 9. It also gives a molecular ion at m/z = 102.

- Infrared: a very broad absorption across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and a strong absorption at 1700 cm⁻¹.
- ¹³C NMR: three peaks, at δ 185, 39 and 27.
- ¹H NMR: a singlet at δ 11.9 (1H) and a singlet at δ 1.2 (9H).

**(a)** Use all the data to deduce the structure of S. Explain your reasoning. **[6]**
**(b)** Pentanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOH, is another isomer. Give **two** differences between its NMR
spectra and those of S. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why ¹³C NMR spectra are simpler than ¹H NMR spectra. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** All 12 H atoms are equivalent, so it gives a single strong peak [1]. Its peak is at lower δ than
almost all organic compounds, so it does not overlap the sample's peaks [1]. It is inert, so it does not
react with the sample [1]. (Being volatile, so easily removed, can replace any one of these.) **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "It gives a peak at zero" is a definition, not a reason; give a property and why it helps.

**2.** CHCl₃ contains ¹H, which would give a large peak that swamps or overlaps the sample's peaks [1].
CDCl₃ contains deuterium instead, which gives no signal in a ¹H spectrum, and CCl₄ contains no H at
all [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the solvent gives **no signal**; calling it "inert" or "a good solvent"
does not answer the question.

**3. (a)** **3** [1]
**(b)** **2** (the two CH₃ carbons are equivalent) [1]
**(c)** **4** (three equivalent CH₃, the quaternary C, the CH₂ and the end CH₃) [1]
**(d)** **1** (all six carbons are equivalent) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mark the symmetry on a displayed formula before counting environments.

**4. (a)** Divide by the smallest height: 6 : 9 : 27 = 2 : 3 : 9 [1]. 2 + 3 + 9 = 14, which equals the
14 H in C₇H₁₄O₂, so the peaks are **2H (δ 4.1), 3H (δ 1.3) and 9H (δ 1.2)** [1].
**(b)** The 2H quartet is next to a carbon with 3 H, a CH₃ [1]. The 3H triplet is next to a carbon with
2 H, a CH₂, so these two form an ethyl group [1]. The 9H singlet is three equivalent CH₃ groups on a
carbon with no H, a (CH₃)₃C– group [1].
**(c)** δ 4.1 means the CH₂ is bonded to O, so P is the ester **(CH₃)₃CCOOCH₂CH₃** (ethyl
2,2-dimethylpropanoate) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the ratio and the check against the formula; a bare list of H numbers gives no
evidence that the integration was used.

**5.** 1,1-dichloroethane, CH₃CHCl₂: **two peaks**, ratio 3 : 1 [1]; the CH₃ is a **doublet** (next to
1 H) and the CH is a **quartet** (next to 3 H) [1]. 1,2-dichloroethane, ClCH₂CH₂Cl: **one peak** [1], a
**singlet**, because all four H are equivalent and equivalent protons do not split each other [1]. So the
number of peaks (and the splitting) distinguishes them. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Applying n+1 blindly to ClCH₂CH₂Cl gives a triplet, which is wrong; state the
equivalence explicitly.

**6.** Pentan-2-one: **5** peaks [1]. Pentan-3-one: **3** peaks [1]. 3-methylbutan-2-one: **4** peaks,
so all three give different numbers of peaks and ¹³C NMR **can** distinguish them [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The conclusion rests on three different counts, so one wrong count breaks it.

**7.** Two peaks: the –CHO H at δ 9.0–10.0, a **quartet** (adjacent carbon
has 3 H), integration 1 [1]; the CH₃ at δ 2.0–2.7 (next to C=O), a **doublet** (adjacent carbon has 1 H),
integration 3 [1]. Ratio **1 : 3** with both peaks identified [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give shift, splitting and integration for each peak; leaving one out loses credit.

**8. (a)** **Alcohol O–H** (broad, 3230–3550 cm⁻¹) [1].
**(b)** Two ¹³C peaks for four carbons means three carbons are equivalent [1]. δ 69 is a C–O carbon and
δ 31 is the three equivalent CH₃ carbons [1]. The 9H singlet is three equivalent CH₃ groups on a carbon
with no H, and the 1H singlet is the O–H [1]. So Q is **2-methylpropan-2-ol, (CH₃)₃COH** [1].
**(c)** Butan-1-ol has four different carbon environments, so it would give **4** peaks, not 2 [1].
**[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each conclusion to a named piece of data; "the NMR fits" without saying which
peak shows what does not show the reasoning asked for.

**9. (a)** The IR absorption at 1740 cm⁻¹ is C=O, and with no O–H the compound is an ester, not an
acid [1]. There are two quartet (2H) + triplet (3H) pairs, so R contains **two ethyl groups** [1]. The
CH₂ at δ 4.1 is bonded to O, so one ethyl group is the O–CH₂CH₃ of an ester [1]. The CH₂ at δ 2.3 is
next to C=O, so the other ethyl group is attached to the C=O [1]. Structure: **CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₃, ethyl
propanoate** [1]. Check: C₅H₁₀O₂, Mr = 5(12.0) + 10(1.0) + 2(16.0) = 102.0, matching m/z = 102 [1].
**(b)** **5** peaks (every carbon is in a different environment) [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Both ethyl groups give the same splitting, so only the chemical shifts place them;
reasoning that never uses δ 4.1 and δ 2.3 cannot fix which side of the ester each one is on.

**10. (a)** The very broad IR absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ with C=O at 1700 cm⁻¹ shows a carboxylic
acid [1]. The ¹H singlet at δ 11.9 is the –COOH proton [1]. The 9H singlet is three equivalent CH₃
groups on a carbon with no H: a (CH₃)₃C– group [1]. ¹³C: δ 185 is the acid C=O, δ 27 the three
equivalent CH₃ carbons and δ 39 the central carbon, giving three environments [1]. C₅H₁₀O₂ has
Mr = 102.0, matching the molecular ion [1]. S is **(CH₃)₃CCOOH, 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid** [1].
**(b)** Pentanoic acid gives **5** ¹³C peaks, not 3 [1]. Its ¹H spectrum has **5** peaks, not 2 (also
accept: no 9H singlet) [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In a combined-data question, use every data set; an answer built on the NMR alone
leaves the IR and mass spectrum evidence unused.

**11.** In ¹³C spectra each carbon environment gives a single line, with no splitting to interpret [1].
The δ range for ¹³C is much wider than for ¹H, so peaks rarely overlap [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two separate ideas are needed; rewording one idea does not add a second.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting atoms instead of **environments**, especially in (CH₃)₃C– groups and symmetrical molecules.
- Using n+1 on the protons in the group itself instead of those on **adjacent** carbons.
- Splitting a CH₃ next to C=O or O, which has no H neighbours and is a **singlet**.
- Forgetting that equivalent protons do not split each other.
- Giving an integration ratio without scaling it to the H count of the molecular formula.
- Explaining TMS only as "the zero point" and missing the single-peak, inert and volatile points.
- Ignoring the IR or mass spectrum in a combined question.
