OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu (9264) is a language qualification that
develops reading and writing in Urdu through a set of everyday,
social and future-focused themes. OxfordAQA's own title for the
qualification is simply "Urdu"; Marlbridge lists it under Urdu Language.
It is linear and assessed by two written papers, Reading and Writing,
each lasting 105 minutes, each marked out of 70 and each worth 50%, with
no coursework. The specification was first taught in September 2024 and
first examined in June 2026.

## How the course is organised

The content is organised in three themes, each broken into topics. The
themes supply the subject matter for texts you read and for the writing
you produce.

**Theme 1, Identity and Culture**

- Me, my family, friends and people I know
- Technology in everyday life
- Free-time activities

**Theme 2, Local, National, International and Global Areas of Interest**

- Home, town, neighbourhood and region
- Social issues
- Global issues
- Travel and tourism

**Theme 3, Current and Future Study and Employment**

- My studies
- Life at school or college
- Education post-16
- Jobs, career choices and ambitions

The themes move outward: from yourself and the people close to you, to
your community and the wider world, and then forward to your education
and working life. That progression also matches how your Urdu develops:
early topics invite simple description in the present, while later ones
call for opinions, comparisons and plans for the future.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Skill | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Reading | 105 minutes | 70 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Writing | 105 minutes | 70 | 50% |

The two papers are written exams, marked externally, and there is a
single tier for everyone. The Reading paper includes translation from English into Urdu,
so it tests your ability to produce accurate Urdu as well as to
understand it. The Writing paper assesses your own written Urdu.

The qualification is linear, so both papers are sat in the same series.
Note that OxfordAQA does not offer Urdu at AS or A-level, so this
International GCSE is the board's only Urdu qualification. This page
follows Version 1.1 of the specification.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers test different skills, but they draw on the same themes
and the same language:

- **Vocabulary is shared across both papers.** The words and phrases you
  learn for a topic such as travel and tourism will help you understand a
  reading text on it and write about it.
- **Translation sits between reading and writing.** Although it is part
  of Paper 1, translating into Urdu uses the same grammar, spelling and
  sentence-building skills as Paper 2.
- **Grammar underpins everything.** Verb forms, agreement and word order
  affect comprehension, translation and writing alike, so grammatical
  accuracy is a course-wide skill rather than a single topic.
- **The themes overlap.** Technology in everyday life (Theme 1) connects
  to global issues (Theme 2); my studies (Theme 3) connects to life at
  school (Theme 3) and to free-time activities (Theme 1). Expressing
  opinions and giving reasons works across all of them.

## Planning your study

A language course rewards regular, cumulative practice more than
intensive bursts. A workable outline:

- **First year:** Theme 1, then the first topics of Theme 2. Build a
  personal vocabulary list for each topic, and practise short pieces of
  writing about yourself, your family and your interests. Start
  translating short English sentences into Urdu early.
- **Second year, first part:** the rest of Theme 2, then Theme 3. Move
  from describing to explaining and giving opinions with reasons, so
  that your writing goes beyond simple statements.
- **Second year, final months:** revise vocabulary from all three themes,
  read a wide range of short Urdu texts, and practise both papers under
  timed conditions.

Read some Urdu every week, whether news, stories or school texts, and
write regularly, checking spelling and grammar carefully.

Translation deserves its own routine. Because the Reading paper asks you
to translate from English into Urdu, it helps to keep a notebook of
English phrases that do not translate word for word, together with the
natural Urdu way of saying them. Translate a few sentences on each new
topic as soon as you have learned its vocabulary, then compare your
version with your teacher's. Over two years, this builds the accuracy
that both papers reward, and it shows you quickly which grammar points
still need work. For guidance
on how to approach each paper, use the course's exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA IGCSE Urdu 9264 assessed?**
By two written papers, Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing. Each lasts 105 minutes, is marked out of 70 and is worth 50%.

**Is there translation in the exam?**
Yes. The Reading paper includes translation from English into Urdu.

**Is there a speaking or listening exam?**
The assessment is made up of the two papers, Reading and Writing; no other component is listed, and there is no coursework.

**What are the three themes?**
Identity and Culture; Local, National, International and Global Areas of Interest; and Current and Future Study and Employment.

**Why is it called Urdu Language here?**
OxfordAQA's official title is simply "Urdu"; Marlbridge lists the course under Urdu Language. The board does not offer Urdu at AS or A-level.
