## Questions

**1.** Define monetary policy. **[2]**

**2.** A household has a variable-rate loan of 90,000. The interest rate rises from 4% to 6%. Calculate the extra interest paid in a year and the extra paid each month. **[3]**

**3.** Explain two ways in which a rise in interest rates could affect consumers. **[4]**

**4.** A firm borrows 60,000 for a project that is expected to add 4,200 a year to profit before interest.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly interest cost at 5% and at 9%. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the profit after interest at each rate. **[2]**

**(c)** State what the firm is likely to do at 9%. **[1]**

**5.** The consumer price index (CPI) in Country A rises from 108.0 to 112.32 in a year. The central bank's inflation target is 2%.

**(a)** Calculate the inflation rate. **[2]**

**(b)** State how the central bank might change interest rates. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Explain what is meant by asset purchasing by a central bank. **[2]**

**(b)** A bond pays 6 a year. Heavy buying lifts its price from 100 to 120. Calculate the yield at each price. **[2]**

**7.** The central bank of Country A cuts interest rates and its currency falls from 4.00 to 3.20 units of Country B's currency per A-unit. An A-unit export is priced at 25 A-units and an import at 96 B-units.

**(a)** Calculate the export's price in B-units before and after the fall. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the import's price in A-units before and after the fall. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the likely effect on Country A's current account. **[2]**

**8.** A government limits a river's pollution with tradable pollution permits.

**(a)** Explain how pollution permits work. **[2]**

**(b)** State two disadvantages of pollution permits. **[2]**

**9.** A firm saves 90,000 a year by breaking a pollution law. The chance of being caught is 30% and the fine is 120,000.

**(a)** Calculate the expected fine. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the fine may not stop the firm. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the smallest fine that would make the expected fine exceed the saving. **[1]**

**10.** A new safety regulation costs each firm 9,000 a year. A small firm has yearly sales of 180,000 and a large firm has yearly sales of 4,500,000.

**(a)** Calculate the cost as a percentage of sales for each firm. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the regulation may harm the small firm more. **[1]**

**(c)** State one advantage and one disadvantage of legislation as a way to control firms. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how a cut in interest rates could reduce unemployment but raise inflation. **[6]**

**12.** In Country A inflation is 6.5%. Part of it comes from dearer imported fuel and part from strong consumer spending. The central bank raises interest rates from 3% to 5%. Evaluate whether this is the best response. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The central bank's use of changes in interest rates [1] to influence the economy, such as spending, prices and jobs [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns credit for naming who acts and what the tool is; "the government controlling money" is too vague to show either.

**2.** Change in rate = 6% − 4% = 2 percentage points [1]. Extra yearly interest = 90,000 x 0.02 = **1,800** [1]. Extra a month = 1,800 / 12 = **150** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication, and give the time period with each answer; a bare number with no "a year" or "a month" is easy to misread.

**3.** Two developed points, for example:
Mortgage and loan payments rise [1], leaving less disposable income, so spending on goods and services falls [1].
Saving pays more [1], so some people save instead of spend, which cuts consumption [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each way needs a cause and an effect on spending. Listing "higher payments" and "more saving" without the link to consumption tends to gain only part credit.

**4.** **(a)** At 5%: 60,000 x 0.05 = **3,000** [1]. At 9%: 60,000 x 0.09 = **5,400** [1].
**(b)** At 5%: 4,200 − 3,000 = **+1,200** [1]. At 9%: 4,200 − 5,400 = **−1,200** [1].
**(c)** It will probably not go ahead, since it makes a loss [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the same method at both rates and show each subtraction, so every step of your working is visible.

**5.** **(a)** (112.32 − 108.0) / 108.0 = 4.32 / 108.0 [1] = **4.0%** [1].
**(b)** Inflation is above the 2% target, so it is likely to raise rates [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula as a fraction. For part (b), give a direction and link it to the target by naming the target in your answer.

**6.** **(a)** The central bank creates money electronically [1] and uses it to buy assets, mainly government bonds [1].
**(b)** At 100: 6 / 100 = **6%** [1]. At 120: 6 / 120 = **5%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Asset purchasing is a separate tool from changing the policy rate. Answers that describe it as "lowering rates" without the buying of bonds miss the idea.

**7.** **(a)** Before: 25 x 4.00 = **100** B-units [1]. After: 25 x 3.20 = **80** B-units [1].
**(b)** Before: 96 / 4.00 = **24** A-units [1]. After: 96 / 3.20 = **30** A-units [1].
**(c)** Exports are cheaper abroad, so more are sold [1]. Imports are dearer at home, so fewer are bought, which tends to improve the current account [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply to convert A-units to B-units and divide the other way. Marks are lost when the direction is reversed, so check that the export price falls and the import price rises.

**8.** **(a)** The government sets a limit on total pollution and issues permits that match it [1]. Firms may release only as much as their permits allow, and can usually buy or sell them [1].
**(b)** Any two from: the cap may be set too high; monitoring is needed; large firms may buy many permits; pollution may stay high in one area [2]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A disadvantage should be about permits specifically. "It costs money" is general; "the limit may be set too high" is specific to the control.

**9.** **(a)** 0.30 x 120,000 [1] = **36,000** [1].
**(b)** The expected fine of 36,000 is less than the saving of 90,000, so breaking the law still pays [1].
**(c)** 90,000 / 0.30 = **300,000**, so any fine above this [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the expected fine with the saving, not the headline fine with the saving. The headline fine looks large but ignores the chance of being caught.

**10.** **(a)** Small: 9,000 / 180,000 x 100 = **5.0%** [1]. Large: 9,000 / 4,500,000 x 100 = **0.2%** [1].
**(b)** The same fixed cost takes a much bigger share of a small firm's sales [1].
**(c)** Advantage: it applies to everyone and has legal force [1]. Disadvantage: it is slow to pass or costly to enforce [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is stronger when it uses your own percentages from part (a). Quote both figures rather than saying "small firms are poorer".

**11.** Rate falls, so borrowing is cheaper [1]. Households have lower payments and firms find investment more worthwhile [1]. Consumption and investment rise [1]. Aggregate demand rises [1]. Firms produce more and take on workers, so unemployment falls [1]. If the economy is near full capacity, firms cannot raise output and put up prices, so demand-pull inflation rises [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain scores when each step follows from the one before it. Writing "unemployment falls and inflation rises" without the steps tends to earn little.

**12.** Points that can earn credit, up to ten:
Higher rates raise the cost of borrowing [1]. Consumption and investment fall [1]. AD falls, so the demand-pull part of inflation eases [1]. The currency may rise, making imported fuel cheaper in home currency [1], which helps with the cost-push part [1]. Inflation should therefore fall [1]. However, growth slows and unemployment may rise [1]. There are time lags before the full effect [1]. A rate rise does not lower the world price of fuel, so the cost-push part may remain [1]. Judgement: it is a useful tool for the spending part, but it works best alongside other policies, and the best response depends on which cause is larger [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* To evaluate, weigh the policy against the specific causes in the question rather than writing about rate rises in general. Finish with a clear verdict that says what it depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying what a rate rise "does" without stating whether spending, AD or inflation goes up or down.
- Giving a calculation answer with no working or no time period.
- Reversing the exchange-rate conversion (multiplying where you should divide).
- Treating asset purchasing as a change in the interest rate.
- Using the headline fine instead of the expected fine.
- Listing advantages of a control with no disadvantage in an evaluation.
- Writing a long chain of reasoning with missing links.
- Finishing an evaluation with no verdict, or a verdict that ignores the data given.
