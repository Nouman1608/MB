Quick-recall notes for section 4.1, Angles, lines and triangles, in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1). The section is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also covers, so it applies to both tiers. For explanations, proofs and worked examples, read the [angles, lines and triangles study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-angles-lines-and-triangles/); for exam-style questions, use the [angles, lines and triangles practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-angles-lines-and-triangles-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acute angle | Less than 90° |
| Right angle | Exactly 90° |
| Obtuse angle | Between 90° and 180° |
| Reflex angle | Between 180° and 360° |
| Vertically opposite angles | The angles opposite each other where two straight lines cross |
| Transversal | A line crossing two or more other lines |
| Corresponding angles | Angles in matching positions on two parallel lines |
| Alternate angles | Angles on opposite sides of a transversal, between two parallel lines |
| Allied (co-interior) angles | Angles on the same side of a transversal, between two parallel lines |
| Interior angle | An angle inside a triangle at a vertex |
| Exterior angle | The angle between one side and the extension of the next side |
| Isosceles triangle | Two equal sides and two equal base angles |
| Equilateral triangle | Three equal sides; each angle 60° |

## The facts and their exam wording

```
on a straight line           add to 180°   "Angles on a straight line add up to 180°"
at a point                   add to 360°   "Angles at a point add up to 360°"
vertically opposite          equal         "Vertically opposite angles are equal"
corresponding (parallel)     equal         "Corresponding angles are equal"
alternate (parallel)         equal         "Alternate angles are equal"
allied (parallel)            add to 180°   "Allied angles add up to 180°"
in a triangle                add to 180°   "Angles in a triangle add up to 180°"
exterior angle of triangle   = sum of the two interior opposite angles
isosceles                    base angles equal
equilateral                  60°, 60°, 60°
right-angled                 other two angles add to 90°
```

## Quick results

| Given | Result |
|---|---|
| Isosceles, apex angle 46° | base angles (180 − 46)/2 = 67° each |
| Isosceles, base angle 52° | apex angle 180 − 104 = 76° |
| Right-angled, one other angle 27° | third angle 63° |
| Two interior angles 52° and 71° | exterior angle at the third vertex 123° |
| Angle 130° where two lines meet | the other side of the point is the reflex angle 230° |

## Why the triangle facts hold

- **Angle sum:** draw a line through one vertex parallel to the opposite side; the two alternate-angle pairs and the third angle lie on a straight line.
- **Exterior angle:** the exterior angle and its neighbouring interior angle make 180°, and so do all three interior angles, so the exterior angle equals the other two interior angles.

You may be asked to "show" or "prove" one of these in a question; the outline above is the argument.

## Problem-solving moves

1. Mark every given angle and every equal side on a sketch.
2. Look for straight lines, points, parallel arrows and equal-side dashes.
3. Find whatever angle you can, mark it, and repeat.
4. For a "bent" angle between parallel lines, draw an extra parallel line through the bend.
5. For algebra, form one equation from one fact (a sum of 180° or 360°, or two equal angles), solve, then substitute back.

## Exam traps

- **Looks parallel is not parallel.** Use parallel-line facts only when the lines are marked or stated parallel.
- **Allied versus alternate.** Allied angles add up to 180°; alternate angles are equal.
- **Which angles are equal?** In an isosceles triangle, the equal angles are opposite the equal sides.
- **Reasons.** "Z angles" or "because it is parallel" will not earn reason marks.
- **Finish.** If asked for the largest angle, substitute x back in.
- **Not to scale.** Do not measure "Diagram NOT accurately drawn" figures.
- **Three-letter names.** Angle PQR is at Q.

## Self-test

1. Classify 95°, 270°, 34° and 90°.
2. Angles on a straight line are 48°, x° and 2x°. Find x.
3. Four angles at a point are 2x°, 3x°, 4x° and 90°. Find x.
4. An isosceles triangle has an apex angle of 38°. Find each base angle.
5. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. One allied angle is 64°. Find the other.
6. Two parallel lines are crossed by a transversal. An alternate angle to 72° is how big?
7. A triangle has angles (x + 15)°, 2x° and (3x − 15)°. Find x.
8. The interior angles of a triangle at A and B are 54° and 68°. Find the exterior angle at C.
9. An exterior angle of a triangle is 140°, and one interior opposite angle is 70°. Find the other interior opposite angle, and say what kind of triangle it is.
10. A reflex angle is four times the non-reflex angle at the same point. Find the reflex angle.
11. Explain in one sentence why every angle of an equilateral triangle is 60°.
12. Point E lies between two parallel lines; the angles between the lines and the segments to E, on the side facing E, are 29° and 54°. Find the angle at E.

### Answers

1. **Obtuse, reflex, acute, right**.
2. 3x = 132, so **x = 44**.
3. 9x = 270, so **x = 30**.
4. (180 − 38)/2 = **71°**.
5. 180 − 64 = **116°**.
6. **72°**: alternate angles are equal.
7. 6x = 180, so **x = 30**.
8. 54 + 68 = **122°**.
9. 140 − 70 = **70°**; two equal angles, so the triangle is **isosceles**.
10. 5x = 360, so x = 72 and the reflex angle is **288°**.
11. The three angles are equal and add up to 180°, so each is 180° ÷ 3 = 60°.
12. 29 + 54 = **83°**.
