## Section B

**6.** Yeast is used in the production of bread and of ethanol.

**(a)** Write the word equation for fermentation by yeast. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the dough rises when yeast is added. **[3]**

**(c)** State why oxygen is excluded when ethanol is produced. **[1]**

**7.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on the rate of fermentation by yeast. She counted the bubbles of gas produced in 3 minutes.

| Temperature (degrees Celsius) | Bubbles in 3 minutes |
|---|---|
| 15 | 21 |
| 30 | 63 |
| 45 | 96 |
| 60 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the number of bubbles per minute at 45 degrees Celsius. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the number of bubbles from 15 to 30 degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the very low number of bubbles at 60 degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**(d)** State **two** variables that should be kept constant. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **three** reasons why bacteria are useful in biotechnology. **[3]**

**9.** The diagram shows a fermenter.

**(a)** Explain why the temperature in the fermenter is controlled. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why air may be bubbled into a fermenter. **[2]**

**(c)** State **two** other conditions that are monitored. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why a stirrer is used. **[1]**

**10.** A fruit juice company adds pectinase to crushed apples. 2.0 kg of apples gave 900 cm3 of juice without pectinase and 1 170 cm3 with pectinase.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in the volume of juice. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how pectinase increases the volume of juice. **[2]**

**(c)** State **one** other way in which pectinase improves the juice. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why biological washing powders can clean clothes at 30 degrees Celsius but are less effective at 80 degrees Celsius. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why people who are lactose intolerant can drink milk treated with lactase. **[2]**

**13.** Suggest why the concentration of ethanol in a fermenter cannot rise above a certain level when yeast is used to make it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A small ring of DNA in a bacterium, separate from the main chromosome [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Sugar (glucose) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any one of: breaks down protein stains such as blood or egg [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** Glucose [1] gives ethanol + carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** The yeast ferments sugar in the dough [1] and releases carbon dioxide [1]. The gas forms bubbles that are trapped in the dough and make it rise [1].

**(c)** Fermentation takes place without oxygen (anaerobic conditions) [1]. **[6]**

**7.**

**(a)** 96 divided by 3 = 32 bubbles per minute [1].

**(b)** (63 - 21) divided by 21 x 100 [1] = 200 per cent [1].

**(c)** The enzymes in the yeast have been denatured [1], so fermentation stops [1].

**(d)** Any two of: mass of yeast; volume and concentration of sugar solution; time [1 each]. **[7]**

**8.** Any three of: they reproduce rapidly, so a lot of product is made in a short time [1]; they can make complex molecules such as proteins [1]; there are few ethical concerns about growing them [1]; they contain plasmids into which genes can be inserted [1]. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** Enzymes work best at an optimum temperature [1]; if it is too high they are denatured, if too low the reactions are slow [1].

**(b)** Many organisms need oxygen for aerobic respiration [1], which releases the energy for growth and for making the product [1].

**(c)** Any two of: pH; nutrient supply; waste products [1 each].

**(d)** To mix the contents so that nutrients, oxygen and temperature are even [1]. **[7]**

**10.**

**(a)** (1 170 - 900) divided by 900 x 100 [1] = 30 per cent [1].

**(b)** Pectinase breaks down pectin in the cell walls [1], so the cells release more juice [1].

**(c)** It makes the juice clearer [1]. **[5]**

**11.** The enzymes in the powder work well at moderate temperatures [1]. At 80 degrees Celsius they would be denatured, as their shape changes [1], so they could not break down the stains [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Lactase breaks down lactose [1] into glucose and galactose, which the person can digest [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Ethanol is toxic to yeast at high concentrations [1], so the yeast cells die and fermentation stops [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that ethanol makes bread rise.
- Writing "bacteria are small" as a reason for use in biotechnology.
- Leaving out the reason (enzymes) when explaining why temperature is controlled.
- Using the final volume instead of the starting volume as the divisor.
- Saying that enzymes are "killed" rather than denatured.
