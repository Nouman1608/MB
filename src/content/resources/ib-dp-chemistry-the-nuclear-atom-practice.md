## Questions

**1.** Copy and complete the table for these three species.

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⁴⁵₂₁Sc³⁺ | | | |
| ¹²⁷₅₃I⁻ | | | |
| ²⁰⁷₈₂Pb²⁺ | | | |

**[3]**

**2.** A species contains 34 protons, 46 neutrons and 36 electrons. Write its nuclear symbol, including its charge. **[2]**

**3.** This question is about the particles in an atom.

**(a)** Give the relative charge and the relative mass of a proton and of an electron. **[2]**
**(b)** A hydrogen-1 atom has one proton and one electron. The mass of a proton is 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and the mass of an electron is 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg. Calculate the percentage of the atom's mass that is due to its electron. **[2]**

**4.** ⁵⁸Ni and ⁶⁰Ni are isotopes of nickel.

**(a)** What is meant by the term isotopes? **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why ⁵⁸Ni and ⁶⁰Ni have identical chemical properties. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one physical property that differs between pure samples of the two isotopes. **[1]**

**5.** Thallium contains 29.5% ²⁰³Tl (relative isotopic mass 202.97) and 70.5% ²⁰⁵Tl (relative isotopic mass 204.97). Calculate the relative atomic mass of thallium, to two decimal places. **[3]**

**6.** Indium has two naturally occurring isotopes, ¹¹³In (relative isotopic mass 112.90) and ¹¹⁵In (relative isotopic mass 114.90). Its relative atomic mass is 114.82. Calculate the percentage abundance of each isotope. **[4]**

**7.** Ordinary water is ¹H₂¹⁶O. Heavy water is ²H₂¹⁶O, in which both hydrogen atoms are hydrogen-2.

**(a)** Using mass numbers, calculate the relative molecular mass of each molecule. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the electron count of each molecule, and use it to explain why the two waters take part in the same chemical reactions. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest, with a reason, which liquid has the higher density. **[1]**

**8.** *(HL only)* The mass spectrum of a metal shows two peaks, both from 1+ ions. The peak at m/z 151 has a relative height of 91.6 and the peak at m/z 153 has a relative height of 100.0.

**(a)** State what each peak represents. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage abundance of the isotope with m/z 151. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of the metal, to two decimal places. **[2]**
**(d)** Identify the metal, using these relative atomic masses: Sm 150.36, Eu 151.96, Gd 157.25. **[1]**

**9.** *(HL only)* The mass spectrum of a sample of nickel gives these results for 1+ ions.

| m/z | 58 | 60 | 61 | 62 | 64 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative abundance / % | 68.1 | 26.2 | 1.2 | 3.6 | 0.9 |

**(a)** State the number of isotopes in the sample. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of nickel from these data, to two decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why your answer to (b) lies much nearer 58 than 64. **[1]**

**10.** Rubidium has two isotopes: ⁸⁵Rb (relative isotopic mass 84.91) and ⁸⁷Rb (relative isotopic mass 86.91). The atomic number of rubidium is 37.

**(a)** Deduce the proton, neutron and electron counts for a ⁸⁷Rb⁺ ion. **[1]**
**(b)** A natural sample of rubidium has a relative atomic mass of 85.47. Calculate the percentage abundance of ⁸⁵Rb in this sample. **[3]**
**(c)** A laboratory prepares a sample that is 50.0% of each isotope. Calculate its relative atomic mass, to two decimal places. **[2]**
**(d)** Rubidium chloride made from the prepared sample and from the natural sample react in the same way with silver nitrate solution. Explain why. **[2]**

**11.** *(HL only)* The mass spectrum of a transition metal shows peaks for 1+ ions at the m/z values below. The heights are given relative to the tallest peak.

| m/z | 90 | 91 | 92 | 94 | 96 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Relative height | 100.0 | 21.8 | 33.3 | 33.8 | 5.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage abundance of the isotope with m/z 90, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of the metal, to two decimal places. **[3]**
**(c)** Identify the metal, using these relative atomic masses: Y 88.91, Zr 91.22, Nb 92.91. Explain why your Ar is not exactly equal to the value you matched. **[2]**
**(d)** The isotope with m/z 94 forms an ion with a 4+ charge. Deduce how many protons, neutrons and electrons this ion has. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** ⁴⁵₂₁Sc³⁺: **21, 24, 18** [1]
¹²⁷₅₃I⁻: **53, 74, 54** [1]
²⁰⁷₈₂Pb²⁺: **82, 125, 80** [1]
*Examiner insight:* One slip in a row, such as 52 electrons for the iodide ion, loses that row's mark here; write neutrons = A − Z and electrons = Z − charge beside the table so you can check each entry.

**2.** A = 34 + 46 = 80 and Z = 34, so the element is selenium [1]; charge = 34 − 36 = −2, giving **⁸⁰₃₄Se²⁻** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A symbol without the charge, or with 2+ instead of 2−, describes a different species; check that more electrons than protons means a negative ion.

**3. (a)** Proton: relative mass **1**, relative charge **+1** [1]; electron: relative mass **negligible (about 1/1836)**, relative charge **−1** [1]
**(b)** Percentage = 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ / (1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ + 9.109 × 10⁻³¹) × 100 [1] = **0.0544%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the denominator is the whole atom (proton plus electron); dividing by the proton's mass alone gives a slightly different value, so write out the full set-up before you calculate.

**4. (a)** Atoms with the same proton number (same element) but different neutron numbers [1]
**(b)** Both have 28 protons, so the neutral atoms have the same number of electrons [1]; chemical reactions depend on electrons, so the two isotopes react in the same way [1]
**(c)** Any one of: **density**, melting point, boiling point (the ⁶⁰Ni sample is denser because its atoms are heavier) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Different number of particles" is too vague for (a); name the neutron, and say "same element" or "same number of protons".

**5.** Ar = (202.97 × 29.5 + 204.97 × 70.5) / 100 [1]
= (5987.615 + 14450.385) / 100 = 20438.000 / 100 [1]
= **204.38** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep every digit until the last line; rounding the products early can move the second decimal place, and the question asks for two decimal places.

**6.** Let the fraction of ¹¹³In be x: 112.90x + 114.90(1 − x) = 114.82 [1]
114.90 − 2.00x = 114.82, so 2.00x = 0.08 [1]
x = 0.040, so ¹¹³In = **4.0%** [1]
¹¹⁵In = 100 − 4.0 = **96.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Set up the equation in terms of one unknown; two answers that do not add to 100% show the setting-up step went wrong.

**7. (a)** ¹H₂¹⁶O: **18**; ²H₂¹⁶O: **20** [1]
**(b)** Each molecule has **10 electrons** [1]; the isotopes differ only in neutrons, and the electrons, which take part in reactions, are the same, so the chemical reactions are the same [1]
**(c)** **Heavy water**: its molecules are heavier but about the same size, so a given volume has more mass [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b) the link to electrons carries the explanation mark; "they are both water" restates the question rather than explaining it.

**8. (a)** Each peak is one isotope of the metal (its m/z equals the isotope's mass for a 1+ ion) [1]
**(b)** 91.6 / (91.6 + 100.0) × 100 = **47.8%** [1]
**(c)** Ar = (151 × 91.6 + 153 × 100.0) / 191.6 [1] = **152.04** [1]
**(d)** 152.04 is closest to 151.96, so the metal is **europium** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 100 in (c) instead of the total height 191.6 gives an Ar above 290, which is impossible for these peaks; check that your Ar lies between the two m/z values.

**9. (a)** **5** [1]
**(b)** Ar = (58 × 68.1 + 60 × 26.2 + 61 × 1.2 + 62 × 3.6 + 64 × 0.9) / 100 [1] = **58.76** [1]
**(c)** ⁵⁸Ni makes up most of the sample (68.1%), so it dominates the weighted mean [1]
*Examiner insight:* A plain average of the five m/z values (61.0) ignores abundance; a weighted mean needs each mass multiplied by its own abundance before dividing.

**10. (a)** 37 protons, 87 − 37 = 50 neutrons, 37 − 1 = 36 electrons: **37, 50, 36** [1]
**(b)** 84.91x + 86.91(1 − x) = 85.47 [1]
86.91 − 2.00x = 85.47, so x = 1.44 / 2.00 = 0.720 [1]
⁸⁵Rb = **72.0%** [1]
**(c)** Ar = (84.91 × 50.0 + 86.91 × 50.0) / 100 [1] = **85.91** [1]
**(d)** Both samples contain rubidium atoms with 37 protons, so the Rb⁺ ions have the same electron arrangement [1]; the reaction with silver nitrate depends on the ions present, not on the neutron number, so it is the same [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d) a mark depends on naming protons or electrons as the reason; saying "isotopes have the same chemical properties" only repeats the stem.

**11. (a)** Total height = 100.0 + 21.8 + 33.3 + 33.8 + 5.4 = 194.3 [1]
100.0 / 194.3 × 100 = **51.5%** [1]
**(b)** Ar = (90 × 100.0 + 91 × 21.8 + 92 × 33.3 + 94 × 33.8 + 96 × 5.4) / 194.3 [1]
= 17743.0 / 194.3 [1]
= **91.32** [1]
**(c)** 91.32 is closest to 91.22, so the metal is **zirconium** [1]; the calculation uses whole-number m/z values rather than exact isotopic masses, so it differs slightly [1]
**(d)** Zirconium has Z = 40: **40 protons** and 94 − 40 = **54 neutrons** [1]; 40 − 4 = **36 electrons** [1]
*Examiner insight:* When heights are measured against the tallest peak, the total is not 100; showing the total of 194.3 makes the rest of your working easy to follow.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing electrons = Z + charge for positive ions, which adds electrons instead of removing them.
- Changing A or Z when an ion forms; only the electron count changes.
- Using the mass of the most abundant isotope, or a plain average of m/z values, as Ar.
- *(HL only)* Dividing by 100 when the abundances are relative heights that do not total 100.
- Rounding intermediate products, so a two-decimal-place answer comes out wrong.
- Reverse calculations where the two percentages do not add to 100%.
- Linking isotopes' identical chemistry to "the same mass" instead of to the same electrons.
- Giving a physical difference between isotopes without saying it comes from the different mass.
- *(HL only)* Describing how a mass spectrometer works when asked to interpret a spectrum.
- *(HL only)* Identifying the element from the tallest peak alone instead of from the calculated Ar.
