These questions cover subtopic **2.11 Sketching curves** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use Core content (C2.11): linear and quadratic sketches with roots and symmetry. Questions marked **(Extended)** use E2.11 content: turning points by completing the square, cubic, reciprocal and exponential sketches, and asymptotes. None of them needs a calculator. Answers describe the sketch in words; draw your own and compare the labelled features.

For worked examples, read the [sketching curves study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-sketching-curves/); for a summary, use the [sketching curves revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-sketching-curves-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Find where the line 4x − 3y = 12 crosses each axis. **[2]**

**2.** Write down the roots and the y-intercept of y = x² − 9. **[2]**

**3.** Match each equation to one description.

Equations: A y = (x − 2)², B y = x² − 4, C y = 4 − x, D y = 4x − x².

Descriptions: P a U-shaped curve touching the x-axis at one point; Q an n-shaped curve through the origin; R a straight line with negative gradient; S a U-shaped curve symmetrical about the y-axis. **[2]**

**4.** For y = (x + 1)(x − 5), write down the roots, the y-intercept and the equation of the line of symmetry. **[3]**

**5.** (Extended) By completing the square, find the coordinates of the turning point of y = x² − 10x + 21. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) Write down the equations of the asymptotes of y = 3/x + 2. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Sketch y = 3 + 2x − x². Show the roots, the y-intercept and the line of symmetry. **[4]**

**8.** (Extended)

**(a)** Write 2x² − 8x + 11 in the form a(x + p)² + q. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the turning point of y = 2x² − 8x + 11 and say whether it is a maximum or a minimum. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the graph does not meet the x-axis. **[1]**

**(d)** Write down the y-intercept you would label on a sketch. **[1]**

**9.** (Extended)

**(a)** Factorise x³ − 2x² − 8x completely. **[2]**

**(b)** Sketch y = x³ − 2x² − 8x, labelling the points where it meets the axes. **[2]**

**10.** (Extended) Sketch y = 27 − x³, labelling the points where it meets the axes. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) Sketch y = −4/x + 1. Show the asymptotes and the coordinates of any point where the curve meets an axis. **[4]**

**12.** (Extended) Sketch y = 2 × 3ˣ − 6. Show the asymptote and the coordinates of the points where the curve meets the axes. **[4]**

**13.** (Extended)

**(a)** Sketch y = x(x − 3)², showing how the curve meets the x-axis. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your sketch to state how many solutions x(x − 3)² = −1 has. Give a reason. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** y = 0: 4x = 12, **(3, 0)** [1]. x = 0: −3y = 12, **(0, −4)** [1].

**2.** Roots **x = 3 and x = −3** [1]; y-intercept **(0, −9)** [1].

**3.** **A → P, B → S, C → R, D → Q**. [1] for two correct, [1] for all four.
*Examiner insight:* decide from one feature at a time: (x − 2)² is a square, so it touches the axis; 4x − x² has no constant and a negative x², so it is an n-shape through the origin.

**4.** Roots **x = −1 and x = 5** [1]. y-intercept 1 × (−5): **(0, −5)** [1]. Symmetry **x = 2** [1].

**5.** (x − 5)² − 25 + 21 = (x − 5)² − 4 [1]. Turning point **(5, −4)** [1].

**6.** **x = 0** [1] and **y = 2** [1].

**7.** y = −(x² − 2x − 3) = −(x − 3)(x + 1) [1]. Roots **(−1, 0) and (3, 0)** [1]. **n-shape** through **(0, 3)** [1]. Line of symmetry **x = 1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* drawing a U-shape for a negative x² loses the shape mark, even with every intercept correct.

**8. (a)** 2(x² − 4x) + 11 [1]. 2[(x − 2)² − 4] + 11 [1] = **2(x − 2)² + 3** [1].

**(b)** **(2, 3), a minimum** [1].

**(c)** The least value of y is 3, which is above 0, so the curve never reaches the x-axis [1].

**(d)** **(0, 11)** [1].

**9. (a)** x(x² − 2x − 8) [1] = **x(x − 4)(x + 2)** [1].

**(b)** Roots labelled **(−2, 0), (0, 0) and (4, 0)** [1]. Positive cubic shape: rising from the bottom left through −2, turning, falling through the origin, turning, and rising through 4 [1].

**10.** y-intercept **(0, 27)** [1]. Root: x³ = 27, **(3, 0)** [1]. Negative cubic shape, falling from top left to bottom right and flattening as it passes (0, 27) [1].

**11.** Asymptote **x = 0** [1]. Asymptote **y = 1** [1]. Root: 4/x = 1, **(4, 0)** [1]. a = −4 < 0, so the branches are top left and bottom right of (0, 1), with the bottom-right branch crossing the x-axis at (4, 0) [1].

**12.** y-intercept 2 − 6: **(0, −4)** [1]. Asymptote **y = −6** [1]. Root: 3ˣ = 3, **(1, 0)** [1]. Increasing curve, approaching y = −6 on the left without touching it [1].
*Examiner insight:* the asymptote is the constant term, −6, not the y-intercept. Drawing the curve below y = −6 shows this confusion.

**13. (a)** Crosses the x-axis at **(0, 0)** [1]. **Touches** the x-axis at **(3, 0)**, where it turns [1]. Positive cubic: from the bottom left up through the origin, turning above the axis, down to touch at x = 3 and rising again [1].

**(b)** **One solution** [1]: the line y = −1 meets the curve only once, to the left of the origin, because the curve is never negative for x > 0.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the sign of the turning point wrong after completing the square.
- U-shapes for negative x² coefficients, and cubics drawn the wrong way round.
- A curve crossing the x-axis at a repeated root.
- Asymptotes left unlabelled, or curves drawn through them.
- Writing x-values only where the question asks for coordinates.
