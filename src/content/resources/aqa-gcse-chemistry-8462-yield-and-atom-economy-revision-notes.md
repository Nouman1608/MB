These notes condense **4.3.3 Yield and atom economy of chemical reactions (chemistry only)** of the AQA GCSE
Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content. Two
skills are **Higher Tier only** (HT only): calculating the theoretical mass of a product from a given mass of
reactant, and explaining why a reaction pathway is chosen. Everything else is for both tiers. For full
explanations, see the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-yield-and-atom-economy/); then practise
with the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-yield-and-atom-economy-practice/).

## Core facts

- Yield can be less than the calculated amount because the reaction may be **reversible**, product may be
  **lost on separation**, or reactants may undergo **side reactions**.
- The **percentage yield** compares the product made with the **maximum theoretical mass**.
- **Atom economy** measures how much of the starting materials end up as useful products. High atom economy
  matters for **sustainable development** and for **economic reasons**.

## Formulae

```
percentage yield = (actual mass of product / maximum theoretical mass) x 100
atom economy (%) = (Mr of desired product / sum of Mr of all reactants) x 100
```

Use the balanced equation and include the balancing numbers when finding Mr totals.

## Worked calculations

| Question | Working | Answer | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Theoretical 28.0 g, actual 22.4 g | 22.4 ÷ 28.0 × 100 | 80% | Both |
| Atom economy, CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂, desired CaO | 56 ÷ 100 × 100 | 56% | Both |
| Atom economy, Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂, desired Fe | 112 ÷ 244 × 100 | 45.9% | Both |
| Atom economy, C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆ | 30 ÷ 30 × 100 | 100% | Both |
| Theoretical mass of CaO from 50.0 g CaCO₃ | 0.500 mol × 56 | 28.0 g | HT only |

A reaction that makes only one product has an atom economy of 100%.

## Theoretical mass method (HT only)

1. Balanced equation.
2. Moles of the reactant = mass ÷ Mr.
3. Moles of product from the equation ratio.
4. Theoretical mass = moles × Mr.
5. Then percentage yield = actual ÷ theoretical × 100.

## Choosing a pathway (HT only)

| Data | What a higher value means |
|---|---|
| Atom economy | Less waste |
| Yield | More product from the same raw materials |
| Rate | More product per hour |
| Equilibrium position | More product in a reversible reaction |
| Useful by-products | Low atom economy matters less |

Compare both routes on every item given, then state a decision with a reason.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Yield | The amount of product obtained |
| Theoretical yield | The maximum mass calculated from the equation |
| Percentage yield | Actual yield as a percentage of the theoretical yield |
| Atom economy | Percentage of reactant mass that ends up in the desired product |
| By-product | A product other than the one wanted |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without damaging future resources |
| Reversible reaction | One that can go both ways and may not go to completion |

## Yield compared with atom economy

| | Percentage yield | Atom economy |
|---|---|---|
| Based on | Experiment | Balanced equation |
| Reasons it is low | Reversible, losses on separation, side reactions | Large amounts of unwanted by-product |
| Can be 100% | Only if nothing is lost | If there is a single product |

## Worked examples in brief

**Salt preparation.** Theoretical 12.5 g, actual 9.0 g: 9.0 ÷ 12.5 × 100 = 72%.

**Choosing the desired product.** Mg + CuO → MgO + Cu: for copper, 63.5 ÷ 103.5 × 100 = 61.4%; for magnesium
oxide, 40 ÷ 103.5 × 100 = 38.6%.

**Sustainability link.** A low atom economy means a lot of waste. Waste may need disposal, which costs money and can
harm the environment, so chemists look for high atom economy routes.

## Exam traps

- **Wrong product on top** in an atom economy calculation.
- **Leaving out balancing numbers** in the Mr total.
- **Confusing yield with atom economy.** Yield comes from an experiment; atom economy from the equation.
- **Percentage above 100%.** Check which value is on top.
- **Stating only one reason for a low yield** when two are asked.
- **Choosing a pathway without referring to the data.**

## Quick self-test

1. Give one reason why a yield may be below 100%.
2. Write the formula for percentage yield.
3. Calculate the percentage yield when 15.0 g is made and the theoretical mass is 20.0 g.
4. Write the formula for atom economy.
5. What is the atom economy of a reaction with only one product?
6. Calculate the atom economy for making CaO from CaCO₃.
7. Why is a high atom economy important for sustainable development?
8. (HT only) 10.0 g of CaCO₃ is heated. Calculate the theoretical mass of CaO.
9. (HT only) Name two pieces of data that help to choose a reaction pathway.
10. How does the usefulness of a by-product affect the choice?

### Answers

1. The reaction is reversible; product lost on separation; side reactions.
2. (Actual mass ÷ maximum theoretical mass) × 100.
3. 15.0 ÷ 20.0 × 100 = 75%.
4. (Mr of desired product ÷ sum of Mr of all reactants) × 100.
5. 100%.
6. 56 ÷ 100 × 100 = 56%.
7. Less waste is made and raw materials are used more efficiently.
8. 0.100 mol × 56 = 5.6 g.
9. Any two of atom economy, yield, rate, equilibrium position, usefulness of by-products.
10. If the by-product can be sold, a low atom economy matters less.
