## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Usual effect |
|---|---|---|
| Delegation | Passing authority for a task to a subordinate; the manager stays accountable | Develops staff; frees managers' time |
| Span of control | Number of subordinates reporting directly to one manager | Wide span means less close supervision |
| Levels of hierarchy | Layers of authority from top to bottom | More levels means a taller structure |
| Chain of command | The line of authority from the top to each employee | Longer chain means slower messages |
| Bureaucracy | Formal rules, procedures and roles | Consistency, but slower and less initiative |
| Centralization | Decisions kept at the top or head office | Control and uniformity |
| Decentralization | Decisions passed to lower levels or branches | Local speed and motivation |
| Delayering | Removing one or more levels of hierarchy | Wider spans, shorter chain, cost saving |
| Matrix structure | Staff report to a functional manager and a project manager | Mixed-skill teams; risk of conflicting orders |

## Links between the terms

* Same workforce, wider spans: fewer levels, shorter chain of command.
* Delayering: fewer levels, so spans widen and the chain shortens.
* Decentralization depends on delegation.
* Bureaucracy and centralization often go together.
* A matrix structure adds a second reporting line across the hierarchy.

## Chart calculations (AO4)

* **Span of control**: count only direct reports.
* **Levels**: count layers in the longest branch, from top to front line.
* **Staff below a manager**: add every person in that branch.
* **Chain of command length**: count links from an employee up to the top.
* **Minimum levels for a given span**: multiply level by level. With a span of 5 throughout: 1, 5, 25, 125 per layer, so 156 people fit in four levels.

**Worked mini-example.** Wrenfield Dairy (invented) has a managing director with 4 department heads. Production has 3 supervisors, each with 12 operatives. Sales, Finance and HR heads each have 4 staff.

* Total: 1 + 4 + 3 + 36 + 12 = 56 employees.
* Levels: 4 in Production; 3 elsewhere.
* Widest span: supervisors, 12.
* Staff below the Production head: 3 + 36 = 39.

## Chart types

| Type | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Tall/vertical | Close supervision; clear promotion steps | Slow messages; high management cost |
| Flat/horizontal | Fast communication; more responsibility | Managers overloaded; few promotion steps |
| By function | Specialist expertise | Silo thinking |
| By product | Focus on each product's customers | Duplicated roles |
| By region | Local knowledge; fits local laws | Inconsistency between regions |

## Appropriateness given external change (AO3)

Method: change, then demand, then compare two structures, then judge with a condition.

| External change | What it demands | Structure that often fits |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid technological change | Speed and flexibility | Flatter, decentralized, matrix or project-based |
| Entry to new countries | Local knowledge | By region, more decentralization |
| Stricter regulation | Consistency and control | Centralized, bureaucratic for compliance |
| Recession | Lower costs | Delayering, but watch manager overload |
| Seasonal or uncertain demand | Flexible capacity | Shamrock-style flexible labour (HL) |

## HL only: changes in structure

**Project-based organization.** Temporary cross-functional teams form for a project and disband after it. Fast and focused, but staff may feel insecure between projects.

**Handy's Shamrock Organization.** Three leaves:

* Core: essential permanent staff.
* Contractual fringe: outside firms or specialists doing non-core work for a fee.
* Flexible labour force: part-time, temporary or seasonal staff.

Benefits: lower fixed labour cost, quick changes in capacity. Limits: weaker loyalty outside the core, contract management, job-security concerns.

## Exam traps

* Counting everyone in a branch as a manager's span.
* Saying delayering removes workers; it removes levels.
* Assuming every branch of a chart has the same number of levels.
* Saying flat is always better; fit with the external change decides.
* Confusing a tall structure (many levels) with centralization (where decisions are made).
* Describing the Shamrock without applying each leaf to the case.

## Quick self-test

1. Define span of control.
2. What happens to the chain of command when a business delayers?
3. Distinguish between centralization and decentralization.
4. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a matrix structure.
5. A manager has 5 supervisors, each with 8 staff. How many people are below the manager?
6. With a span of 4 at every level, how many people fit in three levels?
7. Which basis of grouping suits a firm selling in several countries with different laws?
8. State one disadvantage of a bureaucratic structure.
9. Name the three leaves of Handy's Shamrock Organization.
10. Why might delayering harm motivation?
11. Which structure might suit a firm facing rapid technological change, and why?
12. Explain why delegation is needed for decentralization.

### Answers

1. The number of subordinates who report directly to one manager.
2. It becomes shorter, because there are fewer levels between the top and front-line staff.
3. Centralization keeps decision-making at the top or head office; decentralization passes it to lower levels, branches or teams.
4. Advantage: brings mixed skills together on a project. Disadvantage: two managers may give conflicting instructions.
5. 5 + (5 × 8) = 45 people.
6. 1 + 4 + 16 = 21 people.
7. By region.
8. Any one: slow decisions; less initiative; rigid rules that do not fit unusual cases.
9. Core; contractual fringe; flexible labour force.
10. Remaining managers carry wider spans and heavier workloads, promotion steps disappear, and staff may fear further cuts.
11. A flatter, decentralized or matrix structure, because decisions are made closer to the work and can respond quickly.
12. Decentralization moves decision-making authority down the organisation, which is only possible if managers pass authority to subordinates.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Handy's Shamrock Organization SL or HL?**
Changes in organizational structure, including project-based organization and Handy's Shamrock Organization, are HL only in the IB DP Business Management guide, first assessment 2024.

**How do you count the levels of hierarchy on a chart?**
Count the layers in the longest branch, from the top position down to front-line staff. Other branches may have fewer levels.
