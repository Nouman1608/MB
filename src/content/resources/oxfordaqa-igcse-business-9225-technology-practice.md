All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Mosswood Toys.

> **Case study: Mosswood Toys**
>
> Mosswood Toys is a family business with three shops in small towns. It
> sells 900 toys a month at an average price of $20 and orders stock from
> suppliers by telephone. The owners plan a website that delivers anywhere
> in the country. They forecast 400 online sales a month at $20, but
> expect 150 of these to come from customers who would otherwise have
> bought in a shop. Delivery costs $3.50 per online sale and the website
> costs $500 a month. The three shop managers meet at head office every
> month; each journey costs $60 and takes 3 hours, and managers are paid
> $18 an hour. Video conferencing software would cost $35 a month.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of e-commerce? **[1]**
- A. A customer telephones a shop to ask about opening hours
- B. A customer buys a book through a business's website
- C. A manager posts a printed letter to a supplier
- D. A shop places a poster in its window

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a drawback of video conferencing for a business? **[1]**
- A. It removes the cost of travel to meetings
- B. It allows people in different places to see each other
- C. A poor internet connection can interrupt the meeting
- D. It lets documents be shared during the meeting

**3.** Define the term "digital communication". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which the changing use of ICT can affect the human resources activity of a business. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of using email to communicate with its suppliers. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** **(a)** Calculate the extra monthly sales revenue Mosswood would gain from the website. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the extra monthly revenue left after paying the delivery and website costs. **[2]**

**7.** Explain **two** ways the website could help Mosswood access wider markets. **[4]**

**8.** Calculate how much Mosswood would save in a year by holding the managers' monthly meeting by video conference instead of in person. **[3]**

**9.** Analyse **one** drawback for Mosswood of holding the managers' meetings by video conference. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **two** ways Mosswood could use digital communication with its customers. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse the risks to Mosswood of starting to sell online. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether Mosswood Toys should launch its website. Use the case study and your answers to Questions 6 and 8 to support your judgement. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Sharing information with stakeholders using electronic methods [1], such as email, video conferencing or social media [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: jobs can be advertised online, reaching more applicants [1]; training can be delivered by video or online courses [1]; staff can work from home; staff may need training to use new systems. **[2]**

**5.** Email gives both the business and the supplier a written record of what was ordered [1], so if the wrong goods or quantity arrive, the business can show what it asked for and resolve the problem quickly [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Extra online sales = 400 − 150 = 250 [1]. Extra revenue = 250 × $20 = **$5,000** a month [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Delivery = 400 × $3.50 = $1,400 (paid on all online sales); costs = $1,400 + $500 = $1,900 [1]. Extra revenue after these costs = $5,000 − $1,900 = **$3,100** a month [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **Place:** Mosswood can only sell to people who visit its three small-town shops [1], but the website delivers anywhere in the country, so it can reach families in cities and other regions [1].
**Time:** the shops only take sales during opening hours [1], but the website takes orders day and night, so parents can buy in the evening after work [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Travel saved = 3 × $60 = $180 a month [1]. Time saved = 3 managers × 3 hours × $18 = $162 a month, so total = $342 [1]. Net saving = ($342 − $35) × 12 = $307 × 12 = **$3,684** a year [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Managers would no longer meet in person [1], so they may find it harder to discuss problems such as displays or difficult customers, and could not see toy samples properly on screen [1]. This could lead to poorer decisions or weaker team spirit between the three shops, which may cost more than the $3,684 saved [1]. **[3]**

**10.** **Email:** Mosswood could email customers who sign up about new toys and seasonal offers [1], which costs little and could bring them back into the shops or onto the website [1].
**Social media:** it could post videos of new toys and answer questions online [1], building interest among local parents and helping the website reach customers in other towns [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Cannibalisation:** 150 of the 400 online sales would come from existing shop customers [1], so sales revenue rises by only $5,000, not $8,000, while delivery is paid on all 400 orders [1].
**Competition:** online, customers can compare Mosswood's prices with large online toy sellers in seconds [1], so Mosswood may have to cut prices, reducing the $3,100 it keeps each month [1].
**Systems and returns:** the website could fail or be attacked, losing sales and customer trust [1], and returned toys bring extra handling and delivery costs that the forecast does not include [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For the website:** it is forecast to add $5,000 a month in revenue and $3,100 after delivery and website costs [1], or $37,200 a year [1]; it reaches customers far beyond three small towns [1].
**Against:** the $3,100 is before the cost of the toys themselves, so the extra profit will be much smaller [1]; the forecast of 400 sales is only an estimate, and large online rivals may take many of them [1]; the family has no experience of running a website or handling returns [1].
**Judgement:** Mosswood should launch the website, because the extra reach is large and the monthly cost is small compared with the forecast revenue [1]. The decision depends on whether its prices can compete online and whether the profit on each toy is large enough to cover delivery [1]; it could reduce risk by starting with its best-selling or more unusual toys that large rivals do not stock [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting all 400 online sales as extra revenue instead of subtracting the 150 moved from the shops.
- Paying delivery only on the 250 extra sales, when every online order needs delivering.
- Forgetting the cost of managers' time when calculating the saving from video conferencing.
- Giving a drawback of video conferencing without saying how it affects this business.
- Reaching a judgement in Question 12 without saying what it depends on.
