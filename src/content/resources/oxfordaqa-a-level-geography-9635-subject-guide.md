OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Geography (9635) combines physical
and human geography with a strand of geographical and fieldwork skills.
It suits students who are curious about how natural systems work, how
people and places are connected, and how evidence is gathered in the
field. The specification was first taught in September 2018, with first
AS exams in May/June 2019 and first A-level exams in May/June 2020. The
full A-level is assessed by five written papers, each 90 minutes long and
each worth 20% of the A-level, with one choice of option in the first
physical geography paper.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged in five units.

**Unit 1: Physical geography 1 (AS).** Section A, Living with hazards, is
studied by everyone. Students then take one of Section B, Hot desert
systems and landscapes, or Section C, Coastal systems and landscapes.

**Unit 2: Human geography 1 (AS).** Section A, Global systems and
governance, and Section B, Resource security, look at how the world is
connected and how societies secure what they need.

**Unit 3: Physical geography 2 (A-level).** Section A, Water, carbon and
life on earth, and Section B, Ecosystems under stress, cover the large
cycles that sustain life and the pressures on natural systems.

**Unit 4: Human geography 2 (A-level).** Section A, Changing places, and
Section B, People and contemporary urban environments, focus on how
places are experienced, represented and transformed.

**Unit 5: Geographical and Fieldwork skills (A-level).** A geographical
skills checklist and fieldwork skills, assessed in their own paper.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Content | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1a | Physical Geography 1: Living with Hazards; Hot Desert Systems and Landscapes | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS Paper 1b | Physical Geography 1: Living with Hazards; Coastal Systems and Landscapes | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Human Geography 1: Global Systems and Governance; Resource Security | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Physical Geography 2: Water, Carbon and Life on Earth; Ecosystems Under Stress | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Human Geography 2: Changing Places; People and Contemporary Urban Environments | 90 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 3 | Fieldwork and Geographical Skills | 90 minutes | 60 | 20% |

AS Paper 1a and AS Paper 1b are alternatives: you sit one, depending on
whether your school teaches hot deserts or coasts. That means five
papers in all for the full A-level, each equally weighted. All are
externally assessed written exams with no tiers. The qualification is
modular, so the two AS papers can be sat at the end of the first year and
count as an International AS on their own.

## How the parts fit together

The course alternates between physical and human geography at each
stage, and the two stages are closely linked.

On the physical side, Living with hazards and the landscape option in
Unit 1 introduce systems thinking: inputs, processes, outputs and
feedback. Unit 3 applies that thinking to global cycles of water and
carbon and to ecosystems, which is a step up in scale. Hot desert or
coastal landscapes also give you a detailed example of a physical system
to compare with ecosystems under stress.

On the human side, Global systems and governance and Resource security in
Unit 2 look at connections between places and the management of scarce
resources. Unit 4 moves closer in, to how particular places and cities
change and how people experience those changes.

Unit 5 ties both sides together. Fieldwork and geographical skills,
including data handling, graphs and maps, are used throughout the
course, and A-level Paper 3 tests them directly. Because Paper 3 carries
the same weight as each content paper, skills deserve the same attention
as any topic.

## Planning your study

In the first year, many schools teach Living with hazards and the
landscape option alongside Global systems and governance, then Resource
security. Use real case studies from different parts of the world
throughout, and record them in a consistent format so they are easy to
revisit.

In the second year, Water, carbon and life on earth and Changing places
are good starting points, followed by Ecosystems under stress and People
and contemporary urban environments. Work on fieldwork and the skills
checklist throughout rather than leaving them until the end: any
fieldwork you take part in is best written up while it is fresh, and
skills practice sticks best when it is regular.

Start exam-style questions on each section as soon as it is taught, and
move to full timed papers before each exam series. The course's
exam-preparation page explains how to handle the option choice and the
fieldwork paper, so use it for paper strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in OxfordAQA A-level Geography 9635?**
Students sit five papers: AS Paper 1a or 1b, AS Paper 2, A-level Paper 1, A-level Paper 2 and A-level Paper 3. Each is 90 minutes long and worth 20% of the A-level.

**What is the difference between AS Paper 1a and AS Paper 1b?**
Both cover Living with Hazards. Paper 1a adds Hot Desert Systems and Landscapes, and Paper 1b adds Coastal Systems and Landscapes. You sit one, depending on the option your school teaches.

**Is fieldwork assessed in 9635?**
Yes. A-level Paper 3, Fieldwork and Geographical Skills, is a 90-minute written paper worth 60 marks and 20% of the A-level.

**Is 9635 modular?**
Yes. The AS papers can be taken at the end of the first year and the A-level papers in the second.

**When was 9635 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2018, with first AS exams in May/June 2019 and first A-level exams in May/June 2020.
