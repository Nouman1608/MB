Quick-recall notes for section 3.3, Graphs, in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1). Foundation statements A to I are for both tiers; Higher statements A to G (curve types, transformations, tangents, intersections, parallel and perpendicular lines) are Higher tier only. For explanations and worked examples, read the [graphs study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-graphs-edexcel-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [graphs practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-graphs-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Steepness: increase in y ÷ increase in x |
| y-intercept | Where a graph crosses the y-axis, (0, c) |
| Midpoint | The point halfway along a line segment |
| Linear graph | A straight line, equation y = mx + c or ax + by = c |
| Quadratic graph | A parabola, from y = ax² + bx + c |
| Line of symmetry | The vertical line through a parabola's turning point |
| Tangent | A straight line touching a curve at one point, with the same gradient there |
| Translation | A slide of a graph with no change of shape |
| Stretch | A scaling of a graph parallel to one axis |
| Perpendicular | At right angles; gradients multiply to −1 |

## Real-life graphs (both tiers)

| Graph | Gradient means | Horizontal section means | Area under graph means |
|---|---|---|---|
| distance–time | speed | stationary | (not used) |
| speed–time | acceleration | constant speed | distance travelled |
| conversion | exchange rate | (not possible) | (not used) |

Speed–time area: split into triangles, rectangles and trapezia. Check the axis units first.

## Coordinates and lines (both tiers)

```
midpoint     ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
gradient     (y₂ − y₁) ÷ (x₂ − x₁)
y = mx + c   gradient m, intercept (0, c)
```

| Equation | Gradient | y-intercept |
|---|---|---|
| y = 3x − 2 | 3 | (0, −2) |
| y = 5 − 2x | −2 | (0, 5) |
| 2y − 3x = 8 → y = 1.5x + 4 | 1.5 | (0, 4) |
| y = 7 | 0 (horizontal) | (0, 7) |
| x = −3 | vertical; no gradient | none |

To draw ax + by = c, find the two intercepts (x = 0, then y = 0) and join.

## Quadratic graphs (both tiers)

- Fill in the table carefully: bracket negative inputs, (−2)² = 4.
- The table is symmetrical about the line of symmetry; use this to check values.
- Join with a smooth curve; the bottom (or top) is rounded, not pointed.
- The x-intercepts are the solutions of y = 0.

## Higher: curve shapes

```
y = x³, y = x³ − 3x    S-shape, bottom left to top right
y = −x³                top left to bottom right
y = k/x (k > 0)        two branches in quadrants 1 and 3; asymptotes the axes
y = k/x²               two branches, both above the x-axis
y = sin x              wave, max 1 at 90°, min −1 at 270°, period 360°
y = cos x              wave, max 1 at 0° and 360°, min −1 at 180°
y = tan x              repeats every 180°, undefined at 90°, 270°
```

## Higher: transformations

| Graph | Transformation | (p, q) goes to |
|---|---|---|
| f(x) + a | translation (0, a): up a | (p, q + a) |
| f(x + a) | translation (−a, 0): left a | (p − a, q) |
| af(x) | stretch parallel to y-axis, factor a | (p, aq) |
| f(ax) | stretch parallel to x-axis, factor 1/a | (p/a, q) |

Inside the bracket: the opposite of what you might expect.

## Higher: lines and intersections

```
line through two points:  find m, then substitute a point to find c
parallel:                 same m
perpendicular:            m₁ × m₂ = −1, so m₂ = −1/m₁
intersection:             set curve = line; solve curve − line = 0
line to draw:             rearrange target equation to "curve = line"
```

Gradient of a curve by a tangent: draw it touching the curve, extend it, and use two points far apart on it.

## Exam traps

- **Rearrange first.** The gradient of 2y = 6x + 1 is 3, not 6.
- **Sign of the gradient.** Downhill left to right means negative.
- **Speed–time.** Distance is the area, not the gradient.
- **Smooth curves.** Straight segments between quadratic points lose accuracy marks.
- **f(x + a).** Moves left, not right.
- **f(2x).** Halves the x-coordinates.
- **Perpendicular.** Negative reciprocal: for m = 2/3, the perpendicular gradient is −3/2.
- **Vertical lines.** x = 4 is vertical; y = 4 is horizontal.

## Self-test

1. Find the midpoint of (−3, 5) and (7, −1).
2. Find the gradient of the line through (−3, 5) and (7, −1).
3. Write down the gradient and y-intercept of 2y − 3x = 8.
4. Where does 3x + 4y = 12 cross the axes?
5. On a speed–time graph, a car goes from rest to 20 m/s in 8 s. What is the acceleration, and how far does it travel in that time?
6. Complete y = x² − 2x − 3 for x = −2 and x = 4.
7. What is the line of symmetry of y = x² − 2x − 3?
8. In parallelogram ABCD, A(1, 2), B(5, 3), C(7, 7). Find D.
9. (Higher) f(x) = x² − 4. State the minimum point of y = f(x − 3).
10. (Higher) Describe the transformation from y = f(x) to y = f(2x).
11. (Higher) Find the line through (2, −1) and (6, 7).
12. (Higher) The graph of y = x² − 5x + 2 is drawn. Which line solves x² − 7x + 6 = 0?

### Answers

1. **(2, 2)**.
2. −6 ÷ 10 = **−0.6**.
3. y = 1.5x + 4: gradient **1.5**, y-intercept **(0, 4)**.
4. **(4, 0)** and **(0, 3)**.
5. **2.5 m/s²**; ½ × 8 × 20 = **80 m**.
6. Both give **5**.
7. **x = 1**.
8. **(3, 6)**.
9. **(3, −4)**.
10. A **stretch parallel to the x-axis, scale factor ½**.
11. **y = 2x − 5**.
12. **y = 2x − 4**.
