## Section A

**1.** Explain the difference between *degenerate* and *non-degenerate* d orbitals. **[2]**

**2.** Name the d orbitals that make up the lower-energy set in an octahedral complex. **[1]**

**3.** Give the electronic configuration of the V³⁺ ion and state whether its complexes can be coloured by a d–d transition. **[2]**

**4.** A solution of a nickel(II) complex absorbs mainly red light. State the colour of the solution. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why ΔE for a tetrahedral complex is smaller than for an octahedral complex of the same metal ion with comparable ligands. **[2]**

**6.** Copper(I) sulfate, Cu₂SO₄, is a white solid. Use the electronic configuration of Cu⁺ to explain why. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Aqueous cobalt(II) chloride is pink. When concentrated hydrochloric acid is added, the solution turns blue.

**(a)** Give the formulae of the cobalt species responsible for the pink colour and for the blue colour. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the change. **[1]**

**(c)** State the coordination number and shape of each species. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the colour changes. **[3]**

**(e)** Water is then added to the blue solution. Predict and explain what you see. **[2]**

**8.** A complex ion X absorbs light most strongly at 620 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of this light. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔE for one ion of X, in J. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔE in kJ mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**(d)** The ligands in X are replaced by a ligand that produces a larger ΔE. State, with a reason, whether the wavelength absorbed becomes longer or shorter. **[2]**

**9.** Draw energy-level diagrams to show how the five 3d orbitals of a Cu²⁺ ion are arranged:

**(a)** in the isolated gaseous ion; **[1]**

**(b)** in an octahedral complex, showing the electrons of Cu²⁺ and labelling ΔE. **[3]**

**10.** Explain fully why [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is coloured. **[4]**

**11.** A student adds aqueous ammonia drop by drop to aqueous copper(II) sulfate until it is in excess.

**(a)** Describe what the student sees, from the first drops to excess. **[2]**

**(b)** Write equations for the two changes, giving the formula of each copper species. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why ammonia causes two different changes. **[2]**

**12.** Two complexes of the same metal ion in the same oxidation state are compared. Complex P is blue; complex Q is yellow.

**(a)** Using the colour wheel, suggest the main colour of light absorbed by each. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce which complex has the larger ΔE, explaining your reasoning. **[2]**

**13.** Scandium is in the d block, but Sc³⁺ compounds are white. Titanium(III) compounds are coloured. Explain this difference in terms of electron configurations and d–d transitions. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Degenerate d orbitals have the same energy [1]; non-degenerate d orbitals are of the same sub-shell but have different energies, because ligands repel them unequally [1].

**2.** 3dxy, 3dxz and 3dyz [1].

**3.** V³⁺ is [Ar] 3d² (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d²) [1]. Yes: it has d electrons to promote and vacant orbitals in the upper set [1].

**4.** Green-blue (cyan) [1].

**5.** Only four ligands surround the ion, rather than six [1], and in a tetrahedral complex no ligand points directly along the lobes of any d orbital, so the repulsion difference between the sets is smaller [1].

**6.** Cu⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰: the 3d sub-shell is full [1], so there is no vacancy for an electron to be promoted into; no visible light is absorbed by a d–d transition, so the solid is white [1].

**7. (a)** Pink: [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ [1]; blue: [CoCl₄]²⁻ [1].

**(b)** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ ⇌ [CoCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O [1].

**(c)** [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺: coordination number 6, octahedral [1]; [CoCl₄]²⁻: coordination number 4, tetrahedral [1].

**(d)** The ligand changes from H₂O to Cl⁻ and the shape changes from octahedral to tetrahedral, so the d orbitals are split differently and ΔE changes (it becomes smaller) [1]. A different frequency of visible light is absorbed when a d electron is promoted, ΔE = hf [1]. A different complementary colour is therefore seen [1].

**(e)** The solution turns pink again [1]; adding water shifts the position of equilibrium to the left, re-forming [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ [1].

**8. (a)** f = c/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ / 620 × 10⁻⁹ = 4.84 × 10¹⁴ Hz [1].

**(b)** ΔE = hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 4.84 × 10¹⁴ = 3.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1].

**(c)** 3.21 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.93 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ = 193 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(d)** Shorter [1]: a larger ΔE means a higher frequency of light is absorbed, and wavelength is inversely proportional to frequency (λ = c/f) [1].

**9. (a)** Five orbitals drawn on one level, all at the same energy [1].

**(b)** Two levels: three orbitals on the lower level and two on the higher level [1]; ΔE labelled as the gap between them [1]; nine electrons shown: six paired in the lower three orbitals and three in the upper two (one pair and one single) [1].

**10.** The six water ligands split the 3d orbitals of Cu²⁺ into two non-degenerate sets, two higher and three lower [1]. Cu²⁺ is 3d⁹, so there is an electron in the lower set and a vacancy in the upper set [1]. An electron absorbs a photon of visible light of frequency given by ΔE = hf and is promoted to the upper set [1]. The remaining light is transmitted, and the complementary colour (pale blue) is seen [1].

**11. (a)** A pale blue precipitate forms at first [1]; in excess ammonia the precipitate dissolves to give a deep blue solution [1].

**(b)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ → [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 2H₂O [1]; [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O, or from the precipitate: [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 2OH⁻ + 2H₂O [1].

**(c)** At first ammonia acts as a base, reacting with water to give OH⁻ ions, which precipitate the hydroxide [1]. In excess, ammonia acts as a ligand, replacing water (and hydroxide) ligands to form a soluble ammine complex [1].

**12. (a)** P (blue) absorbs mainly orange light [1]; Q (yellow) absorbs mainly violet light [1].

**(b)** Q [1]: violet light has a higher frequency than orange light, and ΔE = hf, so the complex absorbing violet light has the larger gap [1].

**13.** Sc³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁰: it has no d electrons [1], so no electron can be promoted between split d orbitals and no visible light is absorbed [1]. Ti³⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹: its single d electron can absorb visible light and be promoted to the higher set, so the complementary colour is seen [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining colour with electrons falling back and emitting light.
- Giving the absorbed colour as the observed colour, or forgetting to say "complementary".
- Writing the splitting pattern the wrong way round for tetrahedral complexes.
- Removing 3d electrons before 4s when working out the configuration of an ion.
- Leaving nm unconverted in f = c/λ, or giving ΔE per ion when kJ mol⁻¹ is asked for.
- Describing the ammonia change in one step and missing the precipitate stage.
