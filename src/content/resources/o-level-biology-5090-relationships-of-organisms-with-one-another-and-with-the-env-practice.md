## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by each term.

**(a)** population **[1]**
**(b)** community **[1]**
**(c)** ecosystem **[2]**

**2.** In a pond, water fleas eat algae. Snails eat algae and pondweed. Dragonfly larvae eat water fleas. Small fish eat water fleas, snails and dragonfly larvae. Herons eat small fish.

**(a)** Name the producers in this food web. **[1]**
**(b)** Write out a food chain from this web that contains five organisms. **[1]**
**(c)** Name one herbivore in this web. **[1]**
**(d)** Most dragonfly larvae die of a disease. Suggest and explain the effect on the water fleas and the small fish. **[3]**
**(e)** Explain why herons depend on photosynthesis. **[2]**

**3.** The energy in each trophic level of a food chain was measured in kJ per m² per year: producers 30,000; primary consumers 3,600; secondary consumers 270; tertiary consumers 27.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer from producers to primary consumers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the producers' energy that reaches the tertiary consumers. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the transfer of energy from one trophic level to the next is inefficient. **[3]**
**(d)** Use the data to explain why a fifth trophic level is unlikely. **[2]**

**4.** One apple tree (dry mass 800 kg) supports 40,000 aphids, each of dry mass 0.5 g. The aphids are eaten by 600 ladybirds, each of dry mass 0.04 g.

**(a)** Calculate the total dry biomass, in kg, of the aphids and of the ladybirds. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers for this food chain and explain why the pyramid of biomass has a different shape. **[3]**

**5.** A field of maize contains 6,000,000 kJ of energy per year. Assume that 10% of the energy is passed on at each transfer.

**(a)** Calculate the energy available to humans who eat the maize. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy available to humans who eat chickens that were fed on the maize. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why eating crop plants is more energy efficient than eating livestock fed on them. **[2]**

**6.** This question is about the carbon cycle.

**(a)** Describe how the carbon in a fallen tree is returned to the atmosphere. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how carbon in organisms that lived millions of years ago is returned to the atmosphere today. **[3]**

**7.** This question is about the nitrogen cycle.

**(a)** Name the process in which ammonium ions are converted to nitrate ions. **[1]**
**(b)** State two ways in which nitrogen gas is fixed. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how nitrogen in the protein of a dead plant becomes available to a living plant. **[3]**
**(d)** Name the process that returns nitrogen gas to the air. **[1]**

**8.** Dissolved oxygen was measured along a river near a pipe releasing untreated sewage.

| Distance from pipe / m (− = upstream) | −50 | 100 | 200 | 400 | 800 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dissolved oxygen / mg per dm³ | 9.0 | 6.3 | 2.7 | 4.5 | 8.1 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in dissolved oxygen from −50 m to 200 m. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the dissolved oxygen is low at 200 m. **[5]**
**(c)** Suggest the effect on fish at 200 m. **[1]**

**9.** Large areas of a tropical forest are being cleared.

**(a)** Describe the consequences of this deforestation. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain how quotas and replanting can help to conserve forests. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two reasons for conserving species that live only in this forest. **[2]**

**10.** This question is about pollution.

**(a)** Name two greenhouse gases. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two likely effects of global warming. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a non-biodegradable plastic bag in the sea can harm a turtle. **[2]**

**11.** Goats were introduced to an island where they had no predators.

**(a)** Suggest why the number of native plant species on the island fell. **[3]**
**(b)** A farmer on the island sprays an insecticide on a crop. Explain how this could reduce the yield of a nearby fruit orchard. **[2]**

**12.** A fish population has a mass of 50,000 tonnes. Reproduction and growth add 8,000 tonnes each year.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the population after one year if 11,000 tonnes are caught. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain whether a catch of 11,000 tonnes a year is sustainable. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how three methods could help to conserve this fish stock. **[3]**
**(d)** Other than fishing, state two factors that affect the rate of growth of the fish population. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest why the growth of the human population makes over-fishing more likely. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time [1]
**(b)** All of the populations of different species in an ecosystem [1]
**(c)** A unit containing the community of organisms and their environment [1], interacting together [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A population needs one species, same area and same time; "animals living together" is too vague.

**2. (a)** Algae and pondweed [1]
**(b)** algae → water fleas → dragonfly larvae → small fish → herons [1]
**(c)** Water fleas or snails [1]
**(d)** Water fleas increase [1] because fewer are eaten [1]. Small fish may decrease (less food), or stay the same by eating more water fleas and snails [1].
**(e)** The fish they eat fed on consumers of algae or pondweed [1]; that energy entered the chain by photosynthesis in the producers [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (d) each predicted change needs its own reason.

**3. (a)** (3,600 ÷ 30,000) × 100 [1] = **12%** [1]
**(b)** (27 ÷ 30,000) × 100 [1] = **0.09%** [1]
**(c)** Any three [3]: not all of the organism is eaten; some is not digested (faeces); energy released in respiration is lost as heat; energy is used for movement; energy is lost in excretory products.
**(d)** Only 27 kJ per m² per year reaches the tertiary consumers [1]; after another transfer too little would remain to support a population [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for a percentage, so give 12%, not the decimal 0.12.

**4. (a)** Aphids: 40,000 × 0.5 g = 20,000 g = **20 kg** [1]; ladybirds: 600 × 0.04 g = 24 g = **0.024 kg** [1]
**(b)** The pyramid of numbers has a narrow base (one tree), a very wide middle (aphids) and a narrower top (ladybirds) [1]. The pyramid of biomass is a true pyramid: 800 kg, 20 kg, 0.024 kg [1], because biomass allows for size: one tree is heavier than all the aphids [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for kg; an answer in grams loses the mark.

**5. (a)** 6,000,000 × 0.1 = **600,000 kJ** [1]
**(b)** 6,000,000 × 0.1 × 0.1 = **60,000 kJ** [1]
**(c)** Eating livestock adds an extra trophic level [1], and energy is lost at every transfer, so less energy reaches humans [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation must be about energy lost between trophic levels; points about cost or health gain nothing.

**6. (a)** Decomposers (fungi and bacteria) feed on the dead tree [1] and respire, releasing carbon dioxide [1].
**(b)** The dead organisms did not decay and were compressed over millions of years into fossil fuels [1]. These are burned (combustion) [1], releasing carbon dioxide [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) name respiration by decomposers; "the tree rots" does not explain how carbon dioxide is released.

**7. (a)** Nitrification [1]
**(b)** Lightning [1]; nitrogen-fixing bacteria [1]
**(c)** Decomposers break down the protein to ammonium ions [1]; nitrifying bacteria convert ammonium ions to nitrate ions [1]; the living plant absorbs the nitrate ions through its roots [1].
**(d)** Denitrification [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Spell process names exactly; "nitrogenation" or a mix of "nitrification" and "fixation" is not credited.

**8. (a)** (9.0 − 2.7) ÷ 9.0 × 100 [1] = **70%** [1]
**(b)** The sewage increases the availability of nitrate and other ions [1]. Producers such as algae grow more [1]. When they die, there is more decomposition [1] and more aerobic respiration by decomposers (bacteria) [1]. This reduces the dissolved oxygen [1].
**(c)** Fish die (or move away) because they need dissolved oxygen [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Each step in the sequence is a separate point; leaving out the decomposers' aerobic respiration loses that mark.

**9. (a)** Any five [5]: habitats lost, so biodiversity falls; species found only there become extinct; no roots to hold soil, so soil is lost; less water taken up, so flooding; less photosynthesis; burning trees releases carbon dioxide, raising its atmospheric concentration.
**(b)** Quotas limit how many trees are felled [1]; replanting replaces trees that are felled [1].
**(c)** Any two [2]: to maintain biodiversity; to reduce extinction; to protect a vulnerable environment. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "causes global warming" is too vague; link it to carbon dioxide concentration.

**10. (a)** Carbon dioxide [1]; methane [1]
**(b)** Any two [2]: melting ice and rising sea levels; more extreme weather; changes to where species can live; extinction of species unable to adapt.
**(c)** The bag is not broken down by decomposers, so it persists [1]; the turtle swallows it and starves, or becomes entangled and drowns [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) give carbon dioxide and methane, the two gases the syllabus names.

**11. (a)** With no predators the goat population grew quickly [1]. Goats ate native plants faster than they could regrow [1]. Some plant species died out, so biodiversity fell [1].
**(b)** Insecticide kills non-target insects such as pollinators [1], so fewer fruit flowers are pollinated and fewer fruits form [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the answer to the orchard; "harms the environment" does not explain the lower yield.

**12. (a)** 50,000 + 8,000 − 11,000 = **47,000 tonnes** [1]
**(b)** Not sustainable: fish are removed faster than they are produced (11,000 > 8,000 tonnes) [1], so the stock falls and will run out [1].
**(c)** Any three, each with how it works [3]: closed seasons let fish breed; larger mesh lets young fish escape; quotas limit the catch; protected areas ban fishing; monitoring shows stock sizes so quotas can be set; education about sustainable fishing.
**(d)** Any two [2]: food supply; competition; predation; disease.
**(e)** More people means a greater demand for food, so more fish are caught [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) compare the two figures using the definition of a sustainable resource.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Food chain arrows pointing from predator to prey.
- Efficiency calculated the wrong way up (30,000 ÷ 3,600), or left as a decimal.
- Energy described as "used up" or "recycled" rather than lost as heat.
- Confusing nitrification with nitrogen fixation.
- Leaving decomposers out of eutrophication and the carbon cycle.
- Biodiversity defined as the number of organisms, not species.
