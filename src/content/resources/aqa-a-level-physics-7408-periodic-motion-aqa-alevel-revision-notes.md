Condensed notes for sub-section 3.6.1 Periodic motion of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 1. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [periodic motion study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-periodic-motion-aqa-alevel/); test yourself afterwards with the [periodic motion practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-periodic-motion-aqa-alevel-practice/). ## Equations

| Equation | Use | Units |
|---|---|---|
| θ = s/r | angle in radians | rad |
| ω = v/r = 2πf = 2π/T | angular speed | rad s⁻¹ |
| a = v²/r = ω²r | centripetal acceleration | m s⁻² |
| F = mv²/r = mω²r | resultant force towards centre | N |
| a = −ω²x | defining equation of SHM | m s⁻² |
| x = A cos ωt | displacement, timed from x = +A | m |
| v = ±ω√(A² − x²) | speed at displacement x | m s⁻¹ |
| v_max = ωA; a_max = ω²A | at x = 0; at x = ±A | m s⁻¹; m s⁻² |
| T = 2π√(m/k) | mass-spring | s |
| T = 2π√(l/g) | simple pendulum, small angles | s |
| E_total = ½mω²A² = ½kA² | energy of undamped oscillator | J |

## 3.6.1.1 Circular motion: recall

- Constant speed in a circle still means acceleration: the **velocity direction** changes.
- The resultant force and acceleration point to the **centre**; the force does no work because it is perpendicular to the velocity.
- Method: list real forces → resultant towards centre → set equal to mv²/r.
- **Top of a hump**: mg − N = mv²/r; contact is lost when v = √(gr).
- **Bottom of a dip**: N − mg = mv²/r; the apparent weight is larger.
- rpm → Hz: divide by 60.

## 3.6.1.2 SHM: recall

- **Condition**: a ∝ −x (proportional to displacement, directed to equilibrium).
- **Phase**: v is a quarter cycle ahead of x; a is in antiphase with x.
- v–t is the gradient of x–t; a–t is the gradient of v–t.
- **a–x graph**: straight line through origin, gradient −ω².
- **v–x graph**: an ellipse (a circle if axes are scaled by ω).
- From x = +A, the time to reach x = A/2 is T/6; to reach x = 0 is T/4.
- Period is independent of amplitude (isochronous).

## 3.6.1.3 Simple harmonic systems: recall

- Spring: F = −kx gives ω² = k/m. Same for vertical or horizontal springs.
- Pendulum: restoring force mg sin θ ≈ mgθ for small θ in radians, so ω² = g/l. Mass of bob does not affect T.
- Other oscillators (such as liquid in a U-tube) appear with full information given.
- **Energy against x**: E_p is a parabola, E_k an inverted parabola, total a horizontal line.
- **Energy against t**: E_k and E_p each oscillate at **2f**, out of phase with each other.

**Damping types**

| Type | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| Light | Oscillates; amplitude decays gradually; period almost unchanged |
| Critical | Returns to equilibrium in shortest time, no oscillation |
| Heavy | Returns slowly, no oscillation |

**Required practical 7**: time 10 or more oscillations, repeat, use a fiducial marker at equilibrium. Plot T² against m (gradient 4π²/k) or T² against l (gradient 4π²/g).

## 3.6.1.4 Forced vibrations and resonance: recall

- **Free vibration**: oscillates at its natural frequency after an initial displacement.
- **Forced vibration**: oscillates at the driving frequency.
- **Resonance**: driving frequency = natural frequency → maximum energy transfer → maximum amplitude.
- More damping → lower, broader peak, shifted slightly to lower frequency.
- Mechanical examples: swings, car suspension, structures driven by wind. Stationary-wave examples: driven strings, air columns.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Radian | Angle where arc length equals radius |
| Angular speed | Angle swept per unit time |
| Centripetal force | Resultant force towards the centre of the circle |
| Simple harmonic motion | Acceleration proportional to displacement and directed towards equilibrium |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement from equilibrium |
| Natural frequency | Frequency of free oscillation |
| Damping | Dissipation of energy from an oscillating system, reducing amplitude |
| Critical damping | Damping that returns the system to equilibrium fastest without oscillation |
| Resonance | Maximum amplitude when driving frequency equals natural frequency |

## Exam traps

- Calculators in degrees when evaluating cos ωt.
- Using total extension instead of displacement from equilibrium for a vertical spring.
- Claiming the pendulum period depends on mass or amplitude.
- Forgetting ±: the velocity at a given x can be in either direction.
- Writing that energy oscillates at frequency f, not 2f.
- Saying a damped system resonates at a higher frequency.

## Self-test

**1.** Convert 1500 rpm to angular speed.
**2.** State the condition for SHM.
**3.** Where in the motion is the acceleration of an oscillator greatest?
**4.** What is the gradient of an a–x graph for SHM?
**5.** An oscillator has ω = 4.0 rad s⁻¹ and A = 0.10 m. Find v_max.
**6.** Why is a pendulum only approximately simple harmonic?
**7.** A spring has k = 80 N m⁻¹ and carries 0.20 kg. Find T.
**8.** At what frequency does kinetic energy vary for an oscillator of frequency f?
**9.** Define critical damping.
**10.** State two effects of increased damping on a resonance curve.
**11.** What force provides the centripetal force for a car on a flat bend?
**12.** What is the gradient of a T²–l graph for a pendulum?

## Self-test answers

**1.** f = 25 Hz; ω = 2π × 25 = 157 rad s⁻¹.
**2.** Acceleration proportional to displacement and directed towards the equilibrium position.
**3.** At maximum displacement, x = ±A.
**4.** −ω².
**5.** v_max = ωA = 0.40 m s⁻¹.
**6.** The restoring force is proportional to sin θ, which is approximately θ only for small angles.
**7.** T = 2π√(0.20/80) = 0.31 s.
**8.** 2f.
**9.** Damping that returns the system to equilibrium in the shortest possible time without oscillating.
**10.** Lower peak amplitude; broader peak (also a slightly lower resonant frequency).
**11.** Friction between the tyres and the road.
**12.** 4π²/g.
