Condensed recall for section 3.6.2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-cash-flow/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- The **importance of cash** to businesses.
- **Consequences** of cash flow problems; effect of **positive** cash flow.
- **How and why** forecasts are constructed.
- **Complete and interpret sections** of a forecast: **inflows, outflows, net cash flow, opening and closing balance** (no need to build a whole forecast).
- **Cash against profit.**
- **Evaluate solutions**: rescheduling payments, overdrafts, reducing outflow, increasing inflow, new sources of finance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cash flow | Movement of money into and out of a business |
| Cash inflow (receipt) | Money coming in |
| Cash outflow (payment) | Money going out |
| Net cash flow | Inflows minus outflows for a period |
| Opening balance | Cash at the start of a period |
| Closing balance | Cash at the end of a period |
| Cash flow forecast | Prediction of cash in and out over future months |
| Insolvency | Unable to pay debts when they fall due |
| Credit sale | A sale where the customer pays later |
| Working capital | Money for day-to-day costs |

## Formulae

- **Net cash flow = inflows - outflows**
- **Closing balance = opening balance + net cash flow**
- **Opening balance (next month) = closing balance (this month)**

Example: opening $2,500; inflows $16,000; outflows $17,000. Net = -$1,000. Closing = $2,500 - $1,000 = $1,500.

## Cash against profit

| | Cash | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Money available now | Revenue - costs for a period |
| Timing | When money moves | When sales and costs happen |
| Credit sales | Cash arrives later | Profit recorded at the sale |

Example: sell goods for $10,000 on 60 days' credit; costs paid $6,000. Profit $4,000, cash -$6,000 until the customer pays.

A profitable business can run out of cash; a business can have cash but make a loss (for example after taking a loan).

## Why cash matters

- Pay wages, suppliers, rent and tax on time.
- Buy stock and materials.
- Take opportunities, such as bulk discounts.
- Avoid expensive borrowing.

**Consequences of shortages:** suppliers refuse credit; staff leave; missed chances; insolvency.
**Positive cash flow:** pay bills early, invest, build a reserve.

## Why produce forecasts?

- Spot shortages in advance.
- Plan big payments.
- Support loan applications.
- Compare actual with plan.

**Limits:** estimates only; sales may be lower; customers pay late; costs rise; new businesses have no past data.

## Interpreting a forecast

1. Find months with **negative net cash flow**.
2. Find months with **negative closing balance**: borrowing or action needed.
3. Look for **patterns**: seasonal peaks, regular shortages.
4. Check the cause: low sales, high costs, or timing of payments.
5. Suggest an action that suits the **size**, **length** and **cause** of the shortage.

## Solutions

| Solution | Helps by | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Reschedule payments | Keeps cash longer | Loss of goodwill; loss of discounts |
| Overdraft | Quick short-term cover | High interest; can be withdrawn |
| Cut outflows | Reduces payments | May harm quality or growth |
| Increase inflows | Faster customer payments; discounts; sell assets | Discounts cut profit |
| New finance | Adds cash | Interest or loss of control |

- **Temporary** shortage: short-term finance.
- **Permanent** problem: change costs, prices or sales.

## Exam traps

- Cash is **not** profit.
- A negative closing balance is a **cash shortage**, not a loss.
- Closing balance of one month = opening balance of the next.
- A forecast is an **estimate**.
- Do not give inflows that are not cash.
- Choose a solution that fits the **cause** and give a drawback.

## Self-test

1. Define cash flow.
2. Give two examples of cash inflows.
3. Give two examples of cash outflows.
4. Opening balance $3,000; inflows $9,000; outflows $11,500. Calculate net cash flow and closing balance.
5. What is the opening balance of the next month in Question 4?
6. Explain the difference between cash and profit.
7. State two reasons why a business produces a cash flow forecast.
8. Give two consequences of cash flow problems.
9. Give one way of increasing cash inflow.
10. Give one drawback of using an overdraft.
11. A business sells goods worth $8,000 on credit that cost $5,000. State the profit and the immediate effect on cash.
12. Why is a forecast for a new business less reliable?

### Answers

1. The movement of money into and out of a business.
2. Any two: cash sales; payments from customers; loans received; owner's investment; sale of assets.
3. Any two: wages; rent; materials; loan repayments; equipment purchases; tax.
4. Net = 9,000 - 11,500 = **-$2,500**; closing = 3,000 - 2,500 = **$500**.
5. **$500**.
6. Cash is money available now, whereas profit is revenue minus costs, recorded when sales and costs happen, even if money has not changed hands.
7. Any two: spot shortages; plan payments; support a loan application; compare with actual results.
8. Any two: cannot pay suppliers or staff; suppliers refuse credit; missed opportunities; high-interest borrowing; insolvency.
9. For example, ask customers to pay sooner, offer discounts for prompt payment, or sell unwanted assets.
10. High interest, or the bank can withdraw it at short notice.
11. Profit = $8,000 - $5,000 = **$3,000**; cash falls by $5,000 until the customer pays.
12. It has no past sales or costs to base estimates on.
