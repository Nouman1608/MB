## Section A

**1.** Which process is a chemical change?

A: distilling sea water

B: fermenting glucose

C: dissolving copper(II) sulfate in water

D: freezing water

**[1]**

**2.** Which process is a physical change?

A: cracking an alkane

B: electrolysing molten zinc chloride

C: separating dyes by chromatography

D: neutralising an acid

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a physical change. **[1]**

**4.** State whether each of the following is a physical or a chemical change: (a) ethanol evaporating; (b) magnesium reacting with hydrochloric acid; (c) wax solidifying. **[3]**

**5.** Use the equation H₂O(s) → H₂O(l) to explain why melting ice is a physical change. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Iron filings and powdered sulfur are mixed in a dish. A magnet is held over the mixture.

**(a)** Describe what would be seen, and explain why this shows that mixing is a physical change. **[2]**

**(b)** The mixture is heated strongly. It glows, and the black solid left is not attracted to a magnet. State **two** pieces of evidence that a chemical change has occurred. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the new substance formed. **[1]**

**7.** Crude oil is first separated by fractional distillation. Some fractions are then cracked.

**(a)** Identify which process is a physical change and which is a chemical change. **[1]**

**(b)** Give a reason for each answer. **[2]**

**8.** Describe what happens to the particles during (a) boiling, a physical change, and (b) the burning of hydrogen, a chemical change. **[3]**

**9.** Blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate crystals are heated. They turn into a white powder and steam is given off. When water is added to the cooled powder, it turns blue and becomes warm.

**(a)** State whether heating the crystals is a physical or a chemical change. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** A student says: "Because the colour change can be reversed, the change must be physical." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why a burning candle involves both a physical change and a chemical change. **[2]**

**11.** Copper(II) carbonate is heated in an open test tube. The green powder turns black, and the mass of the solid decreases.

**(a)** Explain why this is a chemical change. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the mass of the solid decreases, even though mass is conserved in chemical reactions. **[2]**

**12.** Describe **two** differences between a physical change and a chemical change. **[2]**

**13.** Salt is obtained from rock salt by dissolving, filtering and evaporating. Explain why the salt obtained is chemically the same as the salt in the rock. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Fermentation forms new substances: ethanol and carbon dioxide.

**2.** **C** [1]. The dyes are separated but not changed.

**3.** A change in which **no new substance is formed** (e.g. a change of state) [1].

**4.** (a) **Physical** [1]; (b) **chemical** [1]; (c) **physical** [1].

**5.** The **same substance, H₂O, is on both sides**; only the state changes, so no new substance forms [1].

**6.**

**(a)** The **iron filings are attracted to the magnet** and are removed, leaving the sulfur [1]; the iron is unchanged and can be separated by a physical method, so **no new substance** has formed [1].

**(b)** Any two: it **glows** (energy given out); a **black solid** forms (colour change); the product is **not magnetic** (different properties) [1] [1].

**(c)** **Iron(II) sulfide** [1].

**7.**

**(a)** Fractional distillation: **physical**; cracking: **chemical** [1].

**(b)** Fractional distillation **separates hydrocarbons that are already present** by boiling point [1]; cracking **breaks large molecules into new, smaller molecules** (alkanes and alkenes) [1].

**8.**

**(a)** Boiling: the particles **gain energy and move far apart**, but each particle is **unchanged** [1].

**(b)** Burning hydrogen: **bonds in H₂ and O₂ break** [1] and **new bonds form**, rearranging the atoms into **water molecules** [1].

**9.**

**(a)** **Chemical** [1]; a **new substance** (anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, a white powder) forms as the water of crystallisation is removed [1].

**(b)** Some chemical changes **can be reversed** [1]; the test is whether a **new substance forms**, and here different substances (hydrated and anhydrous copper(II) sulfate) form in each direction [1].

**10.** The wax **melts** (and vaporises) — a physical change, as it is still wax [1]. The wax vapour **burns**, forming **new substances** (carbon dioxide and water) — a chemical change [1].

**11.**

**(a)** A **new substance** forms — the black solid (copper(II) oxide) — and a gas is given off [1].

**(b)** **Carbon dioxide gas** is produced [1] and **escapes** from the open tube, so it is not weighed with the solid; the total mass of products equals the mass of reactant [1].

**12.** A chemical change **forms a new substance**; a physical change does **not** [1]. Physical changes are usually **easily reversed by physical methods**; chemical changes usually are **not** (or: bonds are broken and made in a chemical change, but not within particles in a physical change) [1].

**13.** Dissolving, filtering and evaporating are **physical changes** [1]; **no new substance is formed** at any step, so the salt is not chemically changed [1].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the type of change without giving a reason when the question says "identify" and "explain".
- Using reversibility as the deciding test, so hydrated/anhydrous copper(II) sulfate is wrongly called physical.
- Describing only one type of change in a "differences" answer.
- Missing the physical stage in a two-stage process such as a burning candle.
- Stating that mass is lost or destroyed when a gas simply escapes.
- Treating any bubbles or colour change as proof of a reaction without checking for a new substance.
