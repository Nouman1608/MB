These questions cover section 3.11.2, Energy transfer by conduction, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level only content. Learn the content first with the [conduction study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-energy-transfer-by-conduction/) and the [conduction revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-energy-transfer-by-conduction-revision-notes/). Assume steady-state conditions unless a question says otherwise. Take the specific latent heat of fusion of ice as 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the equation for the rate of energy transfer by conduction through a slab, and state the unit of thermal conductivity. **[2]**

**2.** Use the relationship U = k/L to show that the unit of U is W m⁻² K⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A glass pane measures 0.80 m by 1.2 m and is 6.0 mm thick. The thermal conductivity of the glass is 0.96 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ and its two faces differ in temperature by 0.80 K. Calculate the rate of energy transfer through the pane. **[2]**

**4.** Two lagged rods are made of the same metal and have the same temperature difference between their ends. Rod Y is twice as long as rod X and has twice its diameter. Calculate the ratio rate through Y : rate through X. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why metals are much better thermal conductors than non-metals. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why a jacket filled with loose fibres keeps a person warm, even though the fibres themselves are not especially poor conductors. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** An aluminium bar of length 0.30 m and cross-sectional area 4.0 cm² is well lagged. One end is held at 95 °C and the other at 15 °C. The thermal conductivity of aluminium is 210 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of energy transfer along the bar. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the temperature at a point 0.10 m from the hot end. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the answer to (b) would be wrong if the bar were not lagged. **[2]**

**8.** A room is kept at 18 K above the outside temperature. Its outer surfaces are a wall of area 30 m² (U = 0.35 W m⁻² K⁻¹), a window of area 6.0 m² (U = 2.0 W m⁻² K⁻¹) and a ceiling of area 20 m² (U = 0.25 W m⁻² K⁻¹).

**(a)** Calculate the total rate of energy loss through these surfaces. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy lost in 8.0 hours, in joules and in kWh. **[2]**

**(c)** The owner can either halve the U-value of the window or halve the U-value of the wall. Determine which gives the larger reduction in the rate of loss. **[2]**

**9.** A builder fills a wall cavity with foam of thermal conductivity 0.030 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum thickness of foam needed for a U-value no greater than 0.25 W m⁻² K⁻¹. **[2]**

**(b)** The builder uses 80 mm of foam instead. Calculate the rate of energy loss through 40 m² of this layer when the temperature difference is 20 K. **[2]**

**10.** A student measures the thermal conductivity of a sheet of plastic. The sheet, of area 0.040 m² and thickness 5.0 ± 0.1 mm, is clamped between a heater plate and a cold plate. In the steady state the heater supplies 12.0 W and thermometers in the two plates read 34.5 °C and 22.5 °C, each with an uncertainty of ±0.2 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the thermal conductivity of the plastic. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the thickness and in the temperature difference, and hence the absolute uncertainty in k. Ignore uncertainties in the area and power. **[3]**

**(c)** Some energy escapes from the edges of the heater plate. State and explain the effect on the calculated value of k. **[1]**

**11.** A polystyrene cool box has a total surface area of 0.60 m² and walls 2.5 cm thick. The thermal conductivity of polystyrene is 0.033 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹. The box contains melting ice at 0 °C and the air outside is at 30 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of energy transfer into the box. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ice that melts in 6.0 hours. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two changes to the box that would reduce this mass, and explain each using the conduction equation. **[2]**

**12.** A hot-water tank has a surface area of 2.4 m² and is kept 60 K above its surroundings. Two lagging jackets are available: jacket A is 50 mm thick with k = 0.040 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹; jacket B is 80 mm thick with k = 0.050 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the U-value of each jacket and state which is the better insulator. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy lost in one day through the better jacket, in kWh. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Rate of energy transfer = kAΔθ/L [1]; unit of k is W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Define the symbols if the question does not; A is the area at right angles to the flow and L the thickness.

**2.** k has unit W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ and L has unit m [1]; W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ ÷ m = W m⁻² K⁻¹ [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Show the division explicitly; quoting the answer alone does not "show" anything.

**3.** A = 0.80 × 1.2 = 0.96 m² and L = 6.0 × 10⁻³ m [1]; rate = 0.96 × 0.96 × 0.80 / 6.0 × 10⁻³ = 1.2 × 10² W [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A small temperature difference across glass still gives a large rate because the pane is so thin.

**4.** Area of Y is 4 times that of X; length is 2 times [1]; rate ∝ A/L, so ratio = 4/2 = 2 : 1 [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Doubling the diameter multiplies the area by four, not two.

**5.** Metals contain free (delocalised) electrons that move through the lattice [1] and carry energy quickly from hot to cold regions, in addition to energy passed on by lattice vibrations [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Lattice vibrations alone occur in all solids, so they cannot explain the difference.

**6.** The fibres trap a large volume of still air, which has a very low thermal conductivity [1]; trapped air cannot circulate, so energy is not carried away by convection [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Credit needs both ideas: air conducts poorly and the fibres stop it moving.

**7. (a)** A = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² and Δθ = 80 K [1]; rate = 210 × 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 80 / 0.30 = 22 W (22.4 W) [1]

**(b)** The temperature falls by 80 K over 0.30 m, so by 26.7 K over 0.10 m: 95 − 26.7 = 68 °C [1]

**(c)** Energy would escape through the sides of the bar [1], so the rate would fall along the bar and the temperature gradient would not be uniform; the temperature profile would not be linear [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* In (c) state the consequence for the gradient; "energy is lost" alone earns only the first mark.

**8. (a)** Wall 0.35 × 30 × 18 = 189 W; window 2.0 × 6.0 × 18 = 216 W [1]; ceiling 0.25 × 20 × 18 = 90 W [1]; total = 495 W, about 5.0 × 10² W [1]

**(b)** Energy = 495 × 8.0 × 3600 = 1.4 × 10⁷ J [1]; 0.495 kW × 8.0 h = 4.0 kWh [1]

**(c)** Halving the window U saves 216/2 = 108 W; halving the wall U saves 189/2 = 94.5 W [1]; the window change gives the larger reduction [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* Compare the UA (or rate) of each surface; the one with the larger product gives the bigger saving for the same fractional change.

**9. (a)** L = k/U [1] = 0.030/0.25 = 0.12 m (120 mm) [1]

**(b)** U = 0.030/0.080 = 0.375 W m⁻² K⁻¹ [1]; rate = 0.375 × 40 × 20 = 300 W [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A thinner layer than required gives a larger U-value; checking that 0.375 exceeds 0.25 confirms the answer makes sense.

**10. (a)** Δθ = 12.0 K and L = 5.0 × 10⁻³ m [1]; k = 12.0 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ / (0.040 × 12.0) = 0.125 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ [1]

**(b)** Thickness: 0.1/5.0 = 2.0% [1]; temperature difference uncertainty = 0.2 + 0.2 = 0.4 K, so 0.4/12.0 = 3.3% [1]; total 5.3%, so k = 0.125 ± 0.007 W m⁻¹ K⁻¹ [1]

**(c)** Less than 12.0 W passes through the sheet, so the true rate is smaller than the value used and the calculated k is too large [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* A difference of two readings takes the sum of their absolute uncertainties.

**11. (a)** L = 0.025 m [1]; rate = 0.033 × 0.60 × 30 / 0.025 = 24 W (23.8 W) [1]

**(b)** Energy = 23.8 × 6.0 × 3600 = 5.1 × 10⁵ J [1]; mass = 5.13 × 10⁵ / 3.34 × 10⁵ = 1.5 kg [1]

**(c)** Any two, each with its reason: thicker walls, because the rate is inversely proportional to L [1]; a material of lower k, because the rate is proportional to k; or a smaller surface area for the same contents, because the rate is proportional to A [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Each suggestion must name the factor in the equation that it changes.

**12. (a)** U(A) = 0.040/0.050 = 0.80 W m⁻² K⁻¹; U(B) = 0.050/0.080 = 0.625 W m⁻² K⁻¹ [1]; B is the better insulator because it has the lower U-value [1]

**(b)** Rate = 0.625 × 2.4 × 60 = 90 W [1]; energy = 0.090 kW × 24 h = 2.2 kWh [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* B is made of a better conductor than A but is thick enough to insulate better, which is why U, not k, decides.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving thicknesses in mm or areas in cm².
- Doubling the area when the diameter doubles.
- Adding U-values instead of the products UA.
- Giving a rate when the question asks for energy.
- Stating that a material with a high U-value is a good insulator.
- Suggesting improvements without linking them to k, A, Δθ or L.
