## Questions

**1.** A circle has centre (4, −3) and radius 5.

**(a)** Write down its equation. **[1]**
**(b)** Expand the equation and hence show that the circle passes through the origin. **[2]**

**2.** Find the centre and radius of the circle with equation x² + y² + 12x − 6y + 20 = 0. **[3]**

**3.** Find the centre and the exact radius of the circle 3x² + 3y² − 6x + 18y − 6 = 0. **[3]**

**4.** The circle C has equation x² + y² − 4x + 10y + 4 = 0.

**(a)** Determine the centre and the radius of C. **[2]**
**(b)** C is translated by the vector (3, −1). Find the equation of the image. **[1]**
**(c)** A different translation maps C onto a circle with centre (−1, 6). Find the vector of this translation. **[1]**

**5.** The segment from A(−2, 1) to B(6, 7) is a diameter of circle C.

**(a)** Find the equation of the circle. **[3]**
**(b)** The point P has coordinates (5, 8). Use gradients to show that angle APB is a right angle, and state what this tells you about P. **[2]**

**6.** A circle has its centre on the line x = 2. It passes through P(−3, 4) and Q(9, 0). Find the equation of the circle. **[6]**

**7.** A circle has equation x² + y² + 4x − 2y − 125 = 0.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the tangent at A(7, 8), with a, b and c whole numbers, written as ax + by = c. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the normal at A. **[2]**

**8.** The circle C has equation (x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 25. The line l has equation 3x + 4y = 27.

**(a)** Show that l is the tangent to C at T(5, 3). **[3]**
**(b)** The line l meets the x-axis at A. Find the area of triangle ACT, where C is the centre of the circle. **[2]**

**9.** A circle has equation x² + y² − 2x − 6y − 24 = 0. The line y = x − 2 meets the circle at two points, P and Q.

**(a)** Show that the x-coordinates of P and Q satisfy x² − 6x − 4 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the midpoint M of PQ. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact length of PQ. **[2]**

**10.** The circle C has equation x² + y² − 6x − 4y − 12 = 0. The points P(−1, 5) and Q(6, 6) lie on C.

**(a)** Determine the centre and the radius of C. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the tangent at P, with a, b and c whole numbers, written as ax + by + c = 0. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the tangent at Q, in the form ax + by = c. **[2]**
**(d)** The two tangents meet at R. Find the coordinates of R. **[2]**

**11.** The circle C has equation x² + y² − 8x + 4y + 11 = 0.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius of C. **[3]**
**(b)** C is translated by the vector (−3, k), where k is a constant. The image touches the x-axis and lies above it. Find k. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the coordinates of the points where the image circle meets the y-axis, giving exact values. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (x − 4)² + (y + 3)² = 25 [1]
**(b)** Expanding: x² − 8x + 16 + y² + 6y + 9 = 25, so x² + y² − 8x + 6y = 0 [1]. There is no constant term, so (0, 0) satisfies the equation and the circle passes through the origin [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs the expanded equation written out and a clear statement about the origin. Writing only "it works" gives nothing to credit.

**2.** Complete the square on both variables: (x + 6)² − 36 + (y − 3)² − 9 + 20 = 0 [1]. So (x + 6)² + (y − 3)² = 25 [1]. Centre **(−6, 3)**, radius **5** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The sign flip is the usual slip. Writing the completed-square form first lets a reader see your centre came from correct working.

**3.** Divide by 3: x² + y² − 2x + 6y − 2 = 0 [1]. Then (x − 1)² − 1 + (y + 3)² − 9 − 2 = 0, so (x − 1)² + (y + 3)² = 12 [1]. Centre **(1, −3)**, radius **2√3** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by the common coefficient is part of the method. If you complete the square without it, the right-hand side is wrong and so is the radius.

**4. (a)** (x − 2)² − 4 + (y + 5)² − 25 + 4 = 0 [1]. So (x − 2)² + (y + 5)² = 25: centre **(2, −5)**, radius **5** [1].
**(b)** New centre (2 + 3, −5 − 1) = (5, −6). Image: **(x − 5)² + (y + 6)² = 25** [1].
**(c)** Vector = (−1 − 2, 6 − (−5)) = **(−3, 11)** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A translation leaves the radius unchanged. State the new centre explicitly so that a sign slip in the equation is easy to spot.

**5. (a)** Centre is the midpoint of AB: ((−2 + 6)/2, (1 + 7)/2) = (2, 4) [1]. r² = (6 − 2)² + (7 − 4)² = 16 + 9 = 25 [1]. Equation: **(x − 2)² + (y − 4)² = 25** [1].
**(b)** Gradient AP = (8 − 1)/(5 + 2) = 1 and gradient BP = (8 − 7)/(5 − 6) = −1 [1]. The product is −1, so AP is perpendicular to BP and angle APB = 90°. Since the angle in a semicircle is a right angle, P lies on the circle with diameter AB [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) asks you to say what the right angle tells you. Stating the conclusion in words is part of the answer, not an extra.

**6.** Midpoint of PQ = (3, 2) [1]. Gradient PQ = (0 − 4)/(9 + 3) = −1/3 [1]. Perpendicular gradient = 3 [1]. The centre lies on the perpendicular bisector of the chord PQ: y − 2 = 3(x − 3) [1]. Putting x = 2 gives y = −1, so the centre is (2, −1) [1]. r² = (−3 − 2)² + (4 + 1)² = 50, so the equation is **(x − 2)² + (y + 1)² = 50** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the property you use (the centre sits on the perpendicular bisector of any chord). Check your centre by confirming that Q is the same distance away: 49 + 1 = 50.

**7. (a)** (x + 2)² − 4 + (y − 1)² − 1 − 125 = 0 [1], so (x + 2)² + (y − 1)² = 130. Centre **(−2, 1)**, radius **√130** [1].
**(b)** Gradient of radius from (−2, 1) to (7, 8) = 7/9 [1]. Tangent gradient = −9/7 [1]. y − 8 = −(9/7)(x − 7), so 7y − 56 = −9x + 63, giving **9x + 7y = 119** [1].
**(c)** The normal has gradient 7/9: y − 8 = (7/9)(x − 7) [1], which gives **7x − 9y + 23 = 0**. It passes through the centre: −14 − 9 + 23 = 0 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) has two independent routes to a check: the normal must pass through the centre, and the tangent and normal gradients must multiply to −1. Use one.

**8. (a)** T is on the circle: (5 − 2)² + (3 + 1)² = 9 + 16 = 25, and on l: 15 + 12 = 27 [1]. Gradient of l = −3/4. Gradient of radius CT = (3 + 1)/(5 − 2) = 4/3 [1]. The product is −1, so l is perpendicular to the radius at T, and therefore l is the tangent at T [1].
**(b)** When y = 0, x = 9, so A is (9, 0) [1]. The angle at T is a right angle, so area = ½ × CT × AT. CT = 5 and AT = √(16 + 9) = 5, so area = **12.5** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* To show a line is a tangent you must show both that the point is on the line and circle and that the gradients are perpendicular. Checking only one of these is incomplete.

**9. (a)** Replacing y by x − 2 in the circle equation: x² + (x − 2)² − 2x − 6(x − 2) − 24 = 0 [1]. Expanding: x² + x² − 4x + 4 − 2x − 6x + 12 − 24 = 0 [1], so 2x² − 12x − 8 = 0, and dividing by 2 gives **x² − 6x − 4 = 0** [1].
**(b)** The sum of the roots is 6, so the midpoint has x = 3 [1]. Then y = 3 − 2 = 1, so **M = (3, 1)** [1].
**(c)** x = 3 ± √13, so the x-coordinates differ by 2√13 [1]. The line has gradient 1, so the y-coordinates differ by the same amount and PQ = √2 × 2√13 = **2√26** [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Check with the chord property: the centre is (1, 3), CM² = 4 + 4 = 8, and 8 + 26 = 34 equals r². Spotting that kind of agreement catches slips before you hand in.

**10. (a)** (x − 3)² − 9 + (y − 2)² − 4 − 12 = 0 [1], so (x − 3)² + (y − 2)² = 25. Centre **(3, 2)**, radius **5** [1].
**(b)** Gradient of radius to P = (5 − 2)/(−1 − 3) = −3/4 [1]. Tangent gradient = 4/3 [1]. y − 5 = (4/3)(x + 1), so 3y − 15 = 4x + 4, giving **4x − 3y + 19 = 0** [1].
**(c)** Gradient of radius to Q = (6 − 2)/(6 − 3) = 4/3, so tangent gradient = −3/4 [1]. y − 6 = −(3/4)(x − 6), so 4y − 24 = −3x + 18, giving **3x + 4y = 42** [1].
**(d)** Solve 4x − 3y = −19 and 3x + 4y = 42. Multiplying by 4 and 3 and adding: 25x = 50, so x = 2 [1]. Then y = 9, so **R = (2, 9)** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute R into both tangent equations as a final check: 8 − 27 + 19 = 0 and 6 + 36 = 42. A long multi-part question rewards a quick check at the end of each part.

**11. (a)** (x − 4)² − 16 + (y + 2)² − 4 + 11 = 0 [1], so (x − 4)² + (y + 2)² = 9 [1]. Centre **(4, −2)**, radius **3** [1].
**(b)** The image has centre (4 − 3, −2 + k) = (1, k − 2) and radius 3 [1]. It touches the x-axis from above, so the centre is 3 above the axis: k − 2 = 3, so **k = 5** [1].
**(c)** The image is (x − 1)² + (y − 3)² = 9 [1]. At x = 0: 1 + (y − 3)² = 9, so (y − 3)² = 8 [1]. Points **(0, 3 + 2√2)** and **(0, 3 − 2√2)** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The phrase "touches the x-axis and lies above it" fixes the sign. Without "above", the centre could also be at height −3 and you would need to deal with both cases.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the centre with the wrong sign from a completed-square form.
- Giving r² as the radius, or leaving it unsquare-rooted.
- Forgetting to divide by a common coefficient before completing the square.
- Mixing up the radius gradient and the tangent gradient.
- Not checking that a given point is on the circle before building a tangent from it.
- Moving the centre the wrong way under a translation, or changing the radius.
- Not stating the geometric property used (semicircle, chord bisector, tangent perpendicular to radius) when asked to show or justify.
- Rounding surd answers to decimals when the question asks for exact values.
- Leaving a tangent equation in a form that does not match the requested format.
