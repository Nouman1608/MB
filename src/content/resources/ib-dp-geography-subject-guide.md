IB Diploma Programme Geography is a dynamic subject firmly grounded in
the real world, focusing on the interactions between individuals,
societies and physical processes in both time and space. The course
seeks to identify trends and patterns in these interactions, and
investigates how people adapt and respond to change, evaluating actual
and possible management strategies associated with it. Geography
describes and helps explain the similarities and differences between
different places, at a variety of scales and from different
perspectives.

## How it's assessed

DP Geography is assessed differently at Standard Level and Higher
Level, both in the number of papers sat and in how much weight each
carries. SL students sit two written papers: Paper 1 examines two
elective geography themes chosen by the school and is worth 35% of the
final grade, while Paper 2, covering the core geography topics common
to all students, is worth 40%. HL students sit a third paper in
addition: their Paper 1 (covering three, rather than two, elective
themes) is also worth 35%, Paper 2 is worth 25%, and Paper 3 -- an
HL-only paper on global interactions, requiring more extended,
synoptic answers -- adds a further 20%.

Exam length also differs by level. SL students are given one hour
thirty minutes for Paper 1 and one hour fifteen minutes for Paper 2;
HL students have two hours ten minutes for the longer Paper 1 and the
same one hour fifteen minutes for Paper 2. Paper 3, sat only by HL
students, runs for one hour plus five minutes of reading time and
carries a maximum of 28 marks.

Alongside the written papers, every DP Geography student completes an
internally assessed fieldwork investigation, worth 25% at SL and 20%
at HL. Students formulate their own geographic research question,
collect primary data during fieldwork, apply appropriate geographic
techniques to analyse that data, and write up their findings as a
report that draws reasoned, evidence-based conclusions. Because the
investigation is built around data the student has collected
themselves, no two students' fieldwork reports address quite the same
question, even where several students investigate a similar theme or
location.

The elective themes examined in Paper 1 are chosen by each school from
options set out in the syllabus, so the specific topics a Marlbridge
student sits in their Paper 1 exam depend on which options their
school has taught -- something worth clarifying with a school's
geography department well before study begins in earnest.
