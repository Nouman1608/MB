## Questions

**1.** Simplify.
**(a)** 9m − 4n + 2m + 7n **[2]**
**(b)** 5x² + 3x − 2x² − 8x **[2]**

**2.** Here are four items.
(i) 3x + 7  (ii) 4t > 20  (iii) P = 2l + 2w  (iv) 5y − 1 = 9
**(a)** For each item, write expression, equation, formula or inequality. Each word is used once. **[4]**
**(b)** Write down a factor of 12ab that is a letter. **[1]**

**3.**
**(a)** Say whether each of these is an equation or an identity. **[2]**
(i) 3(x + 2) = 3x + 6  (ii) 5x − 1 = 14
**(b)** Show that (x + 4)(x − 4) ≡ x² − 16. **[2]**
**(c)** Ben says that (x + 3)² = x² + 9. Use x = 1 to show that Ben is wrong. **[1]**

**4.** A triangle has sides of length (4x − 3) cm, (2x + 5) cm and (3x + 1) cm.
**(a)** Show that the perimeter of the triangle is (9x + 3) cm. **[2]**
**(b)** Jo says: "If x is a whole number, the perimeter is always odd." Show that Jo is wrong. **[2]**

**5.** Expand.
**(a)** 4(3a − 2b) **[2]**
**(b)** Expand and simplify 5(2x + 3) − 2(3x − 4) **[3]**

**6.** Simplify.
**(a)** 3a²b × 5ab³ **[2]**
**(b)** 20x⁵y² ÷ 8x²y **[2]**
**(c)** 15m⁴n / 10mn³ **[2]**

**7.**
**(a)** Expand and simplify (x + 6)(x − 2). **[2]**
**(b)** Expand and simplify (3x − 4)(2x + 5). **[3]**
**(c)** Expand (2a − 5)². **[2]**
**(d)** Show that (x + 3)² − (x − 3)² ≡ 12x. **[3]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Simplify.
**(a)** 2a⁶ × 4a⁻² **[2]**
**(b)** 12y³ ÷ 3y⁵, giving your answer as a fraction **[2]**
**(c)** (27p⁶)^(2/3) **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only)
**(a)** Expand and simplify (x + 4)(x − 1)(x + 2). **[4]**
**(b)** Expand and simplify (x + 2)³. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Prove that the sum of four consecutive integers is never a multiple of 4. **[4]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) Prove that (2n + 3)² − (2n − 1)² is a multiple of 8 for every integer n. **[5]**

**12.** A cuboid has dimensions x cm, (x + 2) cm and (x + 5) cm.
**(a)** Show that its volume is (x³ + 7x² + 10x) cm³. **[2]**
**(b)** Find an expression for the total surface area of the cuboid. Give your answer as simplified as possible. **[4]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) A second cuboid has dimensions (x − 2) cm, (x + 1) cm and (x + 4) cm. Show that its volume is (x³ + 3x² − 6x − 8) cm³. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** m terms: 9m + 2m = 11m [1]; n terms: −4n + 7n = 3n. Answer: **11m + 3n [1]**
**(b)** x² terms: 5x² − 2x² = 3x² [1]; x terms: 3x − 8x = −5x. Answer: **3x² − 5x [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat x² and x as different kinds of term; adding them into one term is a common slip that makes the final answer wrong.

**2. (a)** (i) expression [1]; (ii) inequality [1]; (iii) formula [1]; (iv) equation [1]
**(b)** Any of a, b: for example **a [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Look for the symbol. An equals sign with several letters linking real quantities is a formula, while an equals sign you could solve for one letter is an equation.

**3. (a)** (i) identity, because it is true for every x [1]; (ii) equation, because it is true only when x = 3 [1]
**(b)** (x + 4)(x − 4) = x² − 4x + 4x − 16 [1] = x² − 16, which matches the right-hand side [1]
**(c)** At x = 1 the left side is 4² = 16 and the right side is 1 + 9 = 10, so they differ and Ben is wrong [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that", write the expanded line before the simplified line, and end by saying the result matches the target. A bare final answer gives the reader nothing to follow.

**4. (a)** Perimeter = (4x − 3) + (2x + 5) + (3x + 1) [1] = 9x + 3 [1]
**(b)** Choose x = 3, which gives sides 9, 11 and 10 [1]. The perimeter is 9 × 3 + 3 = 30, which is even, so Jo is wrong [1]
*Examiner insight:* A disproof needs one specific value that is allowed in the question and a clear statement of what it shows. Check that your chosen value gives valid side lengths.

**5. (a)** 4 × 3a = 12a [1] and 4 × (−2b) = −8b. Answer: **12a − 8b [1]**
**(b)** 5(2x + 3) = 10x + 15 [1]; −2(3x − 4) = −6x + 8 [1]; combined: 10x + 15 − 6x + 8 = **4x + 23 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Write each bracket as its own line first. The second bracket has a negative multiplier, so check that both signs inside have changed.

**6. (a)** Numbers: 3 × 5 = 15 [1]; a: a² × a = a³, b: b × b³ = b⁴. Answer: **15a³b⁴ [1]**
**(b)** Numbers: 20 ÷ 8 = 5/2 [1]; x⁵ ÷ x² = x³ and y² ÷ y = y. Answer: **5x³y / 2 [1]**
**(c)** Numbers: 15/10 = 3/2 [1]; m⁴ ÷ m = m³, and n ÷ n³ leaves n² on the bottom. Answer: **3m³ / 2n² [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Deal with the number, then each letter in turn. Keep a letter that ends up on the bottom of a fraction there; do not turn it into a negative power unless the question asks.

**7. (a)** x² − 2x + 6x − 12 [1] = **x² + 4x − 12 [1]**
**(b)** 6x² + 15x − 8x − 20 [2] = **6x² + 7x − 20 [1]**
**(c)** (2a − 5)(2a − 5) = 4a² − 10a − 10a + 25 [1] = **4a² − 20a + 25 [1]**
**(d)** (x + 3)² = x² + 6x + 9 [1]; (x − 3)² = x² − 6x + 9 [1]; subtracting: 12x [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the squared bracket out twice. The error (a − b)² = a² − b² removes the middle term and leaves the answer wrong.

**8. (a)** Numbers: 2 × 4 = 8 [1]; a^(6 + (−2)). Answer: **8a⁴ [1]**
**(b)** 12 ÷ 3 = 4 [1]; y^(3 − 5) = y⁻² = 1/y². Answer: **4 / y² [1]**
**(c)** 27^(2/3) = 3² = 9 [1]; p^(6 × 2/3) = p⁴. Answer: **9p⁴ [1]**
*Examiner insight:* For a fractional power, take the root first, then the power. Treating every part of the bracket separately, number and letter, shows the method clearly.

**9. (a)** (x + 4)(x − 1) = x² − x + 4x − 4 [1] = x² + 3x − 4 [1]. Multiply by (x + 2): x³ + 2x² + 3x² + 6x − 4x − 8 [1]. Answer: **x³ + 5x² + 2x − 8 [1]**
**(b)** (x + 2)² = x² + 4x + 4 [1]; (x² + 4x + 4)(x + 2) = x³ + 2x² + 4x² + 8x + 4x + 8 [1]. Answer: **x³ + 6x² + 12x + 8 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Tidy the first product before bringing in the third bracket. Substituting x = 1 in the original and your answer is a quick check: here (a) gives 5 × 0 × 3 = 0 and 1 + 5 + 2 − 8 = 0.

**10.** Let the integers be n, n + 1, n + 2 and n + 3 [1]. Sum = 4n + 6 [1] = 4(n + 1) + 2 [1]. This is a multiple of 4 plus 2, so it leaves remainder 2 and is never a multiple of 4 [1]
*Examiner insight:* A proof needs general letters, a simplified form and a closing sentence. Four worked numerical examples would not prove a statement about every integer.

**11.** (2n + 3)² = 4n² + 12n + 9 [1]; (2n − 1)² = 4n² − 4n + 1 [1]. Difference = 16n + 8 [1] = 8(2n + 1) [1]. As 2n + 1 is an integer, the expression is 8 times an integer, so it is a multiple of 8 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Put brackets around the second square before subtracting, so the sign of every term is changed. The factorised form and the final sentence are what complete the proof.

**12. (a)** (x + 2)(x + 5) = x² + 7x + 10 [1]; multiply by x: **x³ + 7x² + 10x [1]**
**(b)** Face areas: x(x + 2) = x² + 2x, x(x + 5) = x² + 5x and (x + 2)(x + 5) = x² + 7x + 10 [2]. Sum = 3x² + 14x + 10 [1]. Each face appears twice, so the total is **6x² + 28x + 20 [1]**
**(c)** (x − 2)(x + 1) = x² − x − 2 [1]; (x² − x − 2)(x + 4) = x³ + 4x² − x² − 4x − 2x − 8 [1] = x³ + 3x² − 6x − 8, which matches the target [1]
*Examiner insight:* In extended questions, define each face before adding. Remember the factor of 2 for opposite faces. In part (c), expand two brackets and tidy before using the third.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Expanding (x + a)² as x² + a², with the middle term missing.
* Not changing every sign inside a bracket that follows a minus sign or a negative multiplier.
* Collecting terms that are not like terms, such as x² and x.
* Adding indices when raising a power to a power, or multiplying when adding is needed.
* Reading a negative power as a negative number.
* Using one letter for two different numbers in a proof.
* Giving numerical examples as a proof instead of a general argument.
* Leaving a proof without a final sentence connecting the form to the claim.
* Working on both sides at once in a "show that" question.
