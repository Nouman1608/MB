Condensed recall for section 3.3.6 of Unit 3. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Managing change study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-managing-change/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-managing-change-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Cost of a rollout           = lost sales per day x days of disruption (or monthly cost x months)
Expected loss from a risk   = chance per year x cost if it happens
Cost of an unplanned exit   = recruitment cost + cost of lost output during the vacancy
Net benefit of mitigation   = reduction in expected loss - cost of mitigation
```

The expected-loss calculation is an illustration. The specification asks you to identify and rank risks and to plan mitigation, not to use one formula.

## Key factors in change

| Factor | Effect on change |
|---|---|
| Culture | Flexible, team-based cultures adapt more easily; role cultures and long-established strong cultures resist |
| Size | Small: fast, simple, owner decides. Large: slower, costlier, more communication; but more resources and room for pilots |
| Speed | Rapid: early benefits but high risk and shock. Gradual: lower risk, time to adjust, but slower and can drag on |
| Resistance | Fear of job loss, loss of status, lack of skills, mistrust, poor communication |
| Leadership | Transformative leaders inspire commitment to a vision |

- **Big bang:** suits crises and hard deadlines. **Phased:** suits large, complex change.
- Compare costs and benefits: a cheaper phased route may delay benefits by many months.

## Managing resistance

| Method | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Communicate early and honestly | Reduces fear and rumour | Takes time; must be consistent |
| Involve staff in planning | Ownership and better ideas | Slow; may raise expectations |
| Train staff | Builds skills and confidence | Cost and time |
| Incentives and job security | Reduces fear of loss | Cost; may seem like a bribe |
| Pilot schemes | Quick visible success; lessons learned | Delay |
| Use authority | Fast | Damages trust and morale |

## Transformative leadership

- Sets a clear vision and inspires staff to commit to it.
- Leads by example, encourages ideas and develops people.
- Builds **commitment**, not just compliance.
- Suits major, long-term change.
- **Drawbacks:** relies on one person; the vision may be wrong; slow to build; can exhaust the leader; poor in an emergency requiring instant decisions.

## Contingency planning

| Stage | What it involves |
|---|---|
| Identify risks | Natural disasters, IT systems failure, loss of key staff (and others relevant to the business) |
| Assess risks | Judge likelihood and impact; rank them |
| Mitigate | Business continuity planning, succession planning, insurance |
| Test and update | Run drills; revise the plan as the business changes |

- **Business continuity:** backup data and systems off-site, alternative premises or suppliers, insurance, emergency communication, named responsibilities, regular testing.
- **Succession planning:** identify and train future leaders, train deputies, document knowledge, offer career paths.
- **Limitations:** costs time and money; untested plans may fail; trained deputies may leave; chosen successors may cause resentment.
- Justify spending by comparing the cost of the plan with the cost of downtime avoided.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resistance to change | Opposition by staff to a planned change |
| Transformative leadership | Leadership that inspires commitment to a vision |
| Contingency plan | Plan for responding to a disruptive event |
| Risk assessment | Identifying and ranking risks by likelihood and impact |
| Business continuity | Keeping the business running, or restarting quickly, after disruption |
| Succession planning | Preparing people to replace key staff |

## Exam traps

- Resistance is not always irrational: staff may spot real problems.
- Transformative is not the same as autocratic: it seeks commitment, not obedience.
- Do not simply list the three risks. Rank them and relate them to the business in the source.
- A business continuity plan that has not been tested is a weak defence.
- Rapid change is not always better. Refer to the cost of failure.
- For **assess**, tie the judgement to size, culture, urgency and the strength of staff relationships.

## Answer frames

- **Factor in change:** name the factor, link it to evidence in the source and show how it speeds or slows the change.
- **Resistance:** identify why staff would resist, choose a method that deals with that cause, then give its cost.
- **Contingency planning:** pick the likeliest and costliest risk in the source, give a mitigation, a cost and a limit.
- **Judgement:** state what your view depends on, for example the urgency of the change or the trust between managers and staff.

## Self-test

1. A rollout causes 2 days of disruption at $25,000 lost sales a day. Calculate the cost.
2. A phased rollout costs $4,000 a month for 10 months. Calculate the cost and the difference from your answer to question 1.
3. A risk has a 5% yearly chance and costs $400,000 if it happens. Calculate the expected yearly loss.
4. A second risk has a 15% chance and costs $80,000. Calculate the expected loss and the total for both.
5. A key employee earning $60,000 leaves. Recruitment costs 25% of salary and two months of vacancy lose $30,000 of output. Calculate the total cost.
6. State one reason why staff resist change.
7. State one way to reduce resistance.
8. Name the three key risks the specification lists for risk assessment.
9. What is succession planning?
10. Give one drawback of rapid change.

### Answers

1. 2 x 25,000 = **$50,000**.
2. 10 x 4,000 = **$40,000**; the phased rollout is **$10,000** cheaper.
3. 0.05 x 400,000 = **$20,000**.
4. 0.15 x 80,000 = **$12,000**; total = 20,000 + 12,000 = **$32,000**.
5. Recruitment = 25% x 60,000 = $15,000; plus lost output $30,000 = **$45,000**.
6. Any one: fear of job loss; loss of status; lack of skills; mistrust; poor communication.
7. Any one: communicate early; involve staff; train; incentives; pilot schemes.
8. Natural disasters, IT systems failure, loss of key staff.
9. Identifying and developing people to take over key roles when the current post-holders leave.
10. Any one: shock to staff; high risk of errors affecting everyone; little time to adjust.

## Linking the ideas

- Culture, size and speed interact: a large business with a role culture changing rapidly faces the highest chance of resistance and error.
- Resistance falls when communication, involvement and training come before the change, not after it.
- Transformative leadership supports these methods but cannot replace them: a vision does not remove fear of job loss.
- Contingency planning is a form of managing change in advance. A business that has tested its continuity and succession plans copes with sudden change far better.
- In extended answers, connect the two parts of the section: a business that manages planned change well also builds the flexibility to cope with unplanned disruption.
