## Section A

**1.** Define *standard enthalpy change of formation*. **[3]**

**2.** State the standard conditions of temperature and pressure assumed in this syllabus. **[1]**

**3.** Which equation represents the standard enthalpy change of formation of water?

A: 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(l)

B: H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(g)

C: H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l)

D: H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) **[1]**

**4.** A reaction has an activation energy of +75 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔH = −110 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the activation energy of the reverse reaction. **[1]**

**5.** The data booklet lists the Cl–Cl bond energy as an exact value but the C–Cl bond energy as an average. Explain why. **[2]**

**6.** Explain, in terms of bonds, why a reaction can be exothermic. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The decomposition of calcium carbonate is endothermic.

Describe the reaction pathway diagram for this reaction, stating what is on each axis, the relative positions of reactants and products, and how E_A and ΔH are shown. **[4]**

**8.** Excess zinc powder is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ copper(II) sulfate solution in a polystyrene cup. The temperature rises by 10.4 K.

Zn(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + Cu(s)

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy released, q. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount of copper(II) sulfate used. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔH for the reaction in kJ mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the amount of copper(II) sulfate, not zinc, is used for n. **[1]**

**(e)** Suggest one improvement that would make the measured ΔT more accurate. **[1]**

**9.** 2.00 g of ammonium nitrate (Mr 80.0) is dissolved in 50.0 g of water. The temperature falls by 3.1 K.

**(a)** Calculate q. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the enthalpy change of solution, with its sign, in kJ mol⁻¹. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the sign is positive although the temperature fell. **[1]**

**10.** Use bond energies (N≡N 944, H–H 436, N–H 390 kJ mol⁻¹) for the reaction N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g).

**(a)** Calculate ΔH for the equation as written. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence give the enthalpy change per mole of ammonia formed. **[1]**

**(c)** The measured standard enthalpy change of formation of ammonia is −46 kJ mol⁻¹. Suggest why your answer differs. **[1]**

**11.** For 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g), ΔH = −484 kJ mol⁻¹. The H–H bond energy is 436 kJ mol⁻¹ and the O=O bond energy is 496 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the O–H bond energy. **[3]**

**12.** 0.320 g of methanol (Mr 32.0) is burned under a copper can containing 100 g of water. The water temperature rises from 20.0 °C to 36.5 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change of combustion of methanol. **[3]**

**(b)** The data-book value is about −726 kJ mol⁻¹. Give two reasons why the experimental value is less exothermic. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The enthalpy change when one mole of a compound is formed [1] from its elements in their standard states [1] under standard conditions (298 K, 101 kPa) [1].

**2.** 298 K and 101 kPa [1].

**3.** C [1].

**4.** 75 − (−110) = +185 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**5.** Cl–Cl occurs only in Cl₂, so its bond energy can be measured exactly [1]; C–Cl occurs in many different compounds, and its strength varies slightly with the rest of the molecule, so a mean value is quoted [1].

**6.** Bond breaking absorbs energy and bond making releases energy [1]; the reaction is exothermic if more energy is released forming the new bonds than is absorbed breaking the old ones [1].

**7.** Enthalpy on the vertical axis and progress of reaction on the horizontal axis [1]. The products (CaO + CO₂) are drawn higher than the reactant (CaCO₃) [1]. E_A is shown by an arrow from the reactant level up to the peak of the curve [1]. ΔH is shown by an upward arrow from the reactant level to the product level, and is smaller than E_A [1].

**8. (a)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 10.4 = 2174 J (2.17 kJ) [1].

**(b)** n = 0.0500 × 0.200 = 0.0100 mol [1].

**(c)** ΔH = −2174 / 0.0100 = −217 400 J mol⁻¹ [1] = −217 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(d)** Copper(II) sulfate is the limiting reactant; all of it reacts, while some zinc is left over [1].

**(e)** Any one: take temperature readings at regular intervals and extrapolate back to the time of mixing to allow for cooling; add a lid; insulate the cup further [1].

**9. (a)** q = 50.0 × 4.18 × 3.1 = 648 J [1].

**(b)** n = 2.00 / 80.0 = 0.0250 mol [1]; ΔH = +648 / 0.0250 = +25.9 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** The system took in energy from the water (the surroundings), so the surroundings cooled while the system gained enthalpy [1].

**10. (a)** Broken: 944 + 3(436) = 2252 kJ [1]. Formed: 6(390) = 2340 kJ [1]. ΔH = 2252 − 2340 = −88 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** −88 / 2 = −44 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** The N–H bond energy is an average value, not exact for ammonia [1].

**11.** Broken: 2(436) + 496 = 1368 kJ [1]. Formed: 4 × E(O–H). 1368 − 4E(O–H) = −484, so 4E(O–H) = 1852 [1]. E(O–H) = 463 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**12. (a)** q = 100 × 4.18 × 16.5 = 6897 J [1]; n = 0.320 / 32.0 = 0.0100 mol [1]; ΔH_c = −6897 / 0.0100 = −689 700 J mol⁻¹ = −690 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** Any two: heat lost to the surroundings (air) rather than to the water; heat absorbed by the copper can; incomplete combustion of methanol; evaporation of methanol before weighing [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the volume of only one solution for m in a mixing experiment.
- Using the mass of fuel instead of the mass of water in q = mcΔT.
- Dropping the sign of ΔH, or giving J mol⁻¹ when kJ mol⁻¹ is expected.
- Subtracting bonds broken from bonds formed instead of the other way round.
- Missing "one mole" or "standard states" in definitions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does excess zinc not change the calculation?**
Only the copper(II) sulfate is used up completely, so it limits the amount of reaction and the energy released. The extra zinc does not react.
