## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by customer service in the context of a service business. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** way in which the production process for a service differs from the production process for a manufactured good. **[3]**

**3.** Distinguish between the front office and the back office of a service business. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**4.** Ridgeway Insurance runs a telephone helpline that receives 450 calls a day. Each call takes 5 minutes. An agent is productive for 375 minutes a day and costs £110 a day.

**(a)** Calculate the number of calls one agent can handle in a day. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of agents needed to answer all calls. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the daily staff cost and the cost per call. **[2]**

**(d)** The manager cuts the team to 5 agents. Calculate how many calls are not answered promptly each day. **[2]**

**(e)** Evaluate whether Ridgeway Insurance should cut the team to 5 agents. **[6]**

**5.** Ridgeway has 6,000 customers. Each contributes £27 a year. Currently 65% of customers renew each year. A programme of staff training and a new complaints process would cost £10,000 a year and is expected to raise renewal to 74%.

**(a)** Calculate the number of extra customers retained each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the net gain in the first year. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the average customer life before and after, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(d)** Evaluate the importance of high-quality customer service to Ridgeway Insurance and its stakeholders. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The support a business gives its customers before, during and after a purchase [1]; in a service business it is part of the product itself, as the way the service is delivered is the service [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A service is produced and consumed at the same time, often with the customer present [1], so there is no stock and quality cannot be checked before delivery [1], whereas a manufactured good can be inspected and stored before sale [1]. Accept other valid differences, such as perishability or variability. **[3]**

**3.** The front office is where customers meet staff or use the service, such as a reception desk or website [1]. The back office is the unseen work of processing, scheduling and support [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 375 ÷ 5 = **75 calls** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 450 ÷ 75 = **6 agents** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 6 × 110 = **£660** a day [1]; 660 ÷ 450 = **£1.47** a call [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** Capacity = 5 × 75 = 375 calls [1]. Unanswered = 450 − 375 = **75 calls** [1]. **[2]**

**(e)** For: cutting an agent saves £110 a day, about £40,000 a year if the helpline works every day [1]; some calls may be handled by self-service on the website [1].
Against: 75 calls a day (one in six) would wait or be abandoned [1]; insurance customers with claims or problems may leave if they cannot get through [1]; if 2,000 customers a year (one in three) left as a result, the lost contribution would be 2,000 × £27 = £54,000, more than the £40,000 saved [1].
Judgement: Ridgeway should not cut the team without first providing a good alternative such as online claims, since the saving is small compared with the potential loss of customers and reputation [1]. **[6]**

**5. (a)** Retained now = 6,000 × 0.65 = 3,900. After = 6,000 × 0.74 = 4,440 [1]. Extra = **540 customers** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Extra contribution = 540 × 27 = £14,580 [1]. Net gain = 14,580 − 10,000 = **£4,580** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Before: 1 ÷ 0.35 = **2.9 years** [1]. After: 1 ÷ 0.26 = **3.8 years** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** Importance: a retained customer is worth £27 a year and costs less to keep than a new customer costs to win [1]; the programme gives a first-year gain of £4,580 and lengthens the average customer life by about one year [1]; insurance is a service whose quality customers judge mainly when they claim, so service at that moment shapes reputation and recommendations, and good complaint handling reduces the cost of repeat contacts [1].
Stakeholders: customers receive faster answers and fairer treatment [1]; employees may gain skills and confidence, but may face pressure from targets [1]; shareholders gain from the higher retained income [1].
Limits: the gain depends on the rise in renewal being 9 points, and if renewal reached only 70%, the extra customers would be 300 (6,000 × 0.05) and the contribution £8,100, which is a loss of £1,900 [1]; some customers leave because of price or events outside service quality [1].
Judgement: service quality is very important in insurance, since price differences are small and customers rely on trust, but the programme should be tracked against renewal rates [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Ignoring that capacity in a service cannot be stored.
- Dividing minutes per call by productive minutes instead of the other way round.
- Describing customer service as only complaints handling.
- Claiming the benefit of better service without comparing it with the cost.
