## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term *entropy*. **[2]**

**2.** Which process has a negative entropy change?

A: I₂(s) → I₂(g)

B: CO₂(g) → CO₂(aq)

C: NaCl(s) → Na⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq)

D: water heated from 280 K to 320 K

**[1]**

**3.** Which reaction would be expected to have the most positive entropy change?

A: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g)

B: H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g)

C: NH₄Cl(s) → NH₃(g) + HCl(g)

D: 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g)

**[1]**

**4.** State the units of standard entropy and explain why S⦵ values for elements must be
included when calculating ΔS⦵ for a reaction. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the entropy of a sample of liquid water increases as its temperature rises
from 290 K to 330 K. **[2]**

**6.** Predict the sign of the entropy change for each process and explain your answer.

**(a)** Potassium nitrate crystallises from a saturated solution as it cools. **[2]**

**(b)** Ice melts at 273 K. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Nitrogen monoxide reacts with oxygen: 2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g).
Standard entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): NO(g) 210.8; O₂(g) 205.0; NO₂(g) 240.1.

**(a)** Predict, with a reason, the sign of ΔS⦵ for this reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔS⦵. **[2]**

**8.** Iron(III) oxide is reduced in a blast furnace: Fe₂O₃(s) + 3CO(g) → 2Fe(s) + 3CO₂(g).
Standard entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): Fe₂O₃(s) 87.4; CO(g) 197.6; Fe(s) 27.3; CO₂(g) 213.6.

**(a)** Explain why the sign of ΔS⦵ cannot be predicted confidently from the equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔS⦵. **[2]**

**9.** Ammonium chloride decomposes on heating: NH₄Cl(s) → NH₃(g) + HCl(g).
Standard entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): NH₄Cl(s) 94.6; NH₃(g) 192.3; HCl(g) 186.9.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the value is large and positive. **[1]**

**10.** Calcium oxide reacts with water: CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(s).
Standard entropies (J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹): CaO(s) 39.7; H₂O(l) 69.9; Ca(OH)₂(s) 83.4.

**(a)** Calculate ΔS⦵. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the value is much smaller in magnitude than for the reaction in
Question 9. **[1]**

**11.** For N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g), ΔS⦵ = +25.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. S⦵(N₂) = 191.6 and
S⦵(O₂) = 205.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Calculate S⦵(NO). **[2]**

**12.** Bromine boils at about 332 K. At 298 K, S⦵(Br₂, l) = 152.2 and S⦵(Br₂, g) =
245.5 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the entropy change for Br₂(l) → Br₂(g) using these values. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the entropy change for boiling bromine is much larger than the entropy
change for melting it. **[2]**

**13.** A student calculates ΔS⦵ for 2H₂O₂(l) → 2H₂O(l) + O₂(g) using S⦵(H₂O₂, l) = 109.6
and S⦵(H₂O, l) = 69.9 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. The student writes:
ΔS⦵ = 2(69.9) − 2(109.6) = −79.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, saying "oxygen is an element so its entropy is
zero".

**(a)** Explain the error, and why the student's sign should have looked wrong. **[2]**

**(b)** Given S⦵(O₂) = 205.0 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, calculate the correct value. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of possible arrangements of the particles [1] and their energy in a given
system [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. Gas molecules become confined in the liquid, so there are fewer possible
arrangements. A, C and D all increase entropy.

**3.** **C** [1]. No gas becomes two moles of gas. A and D reduce the number of gas moles; B
leaves it unchanged.

**4.** J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ [1]. Elements have non-zero (positive) standard entropies, so leaving them
out gives the wrong total [1].

**5.** The molecules gain energy, so more energy levels become accessible [1] and there are
more ways of distributing the energy among the molecules, so there are more possible
arrangements [1].

**6. (a)** Negative [1]. Ions that were spread through the solution become fixed in a
crystal lattice, so there are fewer possible arrangements [1].

**(b)** Positive [1]. Molecules leave fixed positions in the solid and can move past each
other in the liquid, giving more possible arrangements [1].

**7. (a)** Negative [1]: three moles of gas form two moles of gas [1].

**(b)** ΔS⦵ = 2(240.1) − [2(210.8) + 205.0] = 480.2 − 626.6 [1] =
**−146.4 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**8. (a)** There are three moles of gas on each side, so the number of gas moles does not
change [1].

**(b)** ΔS⦵ = [2(27.3) + 3(213.6)] − [87.4 + 3(197.6)] = 695.4 − 680.2 [1] =
**+15.2 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**9. (a)** ΔS⦵ = (192.3 + 186.9) − 94.6 [1] = **+284.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** A solid with no gas forms two moles of gas, which have far more possible arrangements
[1].

**10. (a)** ΔS⦵ = 83.4 − (39.7 + 69.9) [1] = **−26.2 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** No gas is involved on either side; a solid and a liquid form a solid, so the change in
possible arrangements is small [1].

**11.** 25.0 = 2S⦵(NO) − (191.6 + 205.0) [1], so 2S⦵(NO) = 421.6 and S⦵(NO) =
giving **210.8 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**12. (a)** 245.5 − 152.2 = **+93.3 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** On boiling the molecules separate completely and move freely through a much larger
volume [1], so the number of possible arrangements increases far more than on melting, where
the molecules stay close together [1].

**13. (a)** Elements do not have zero standard entropy, so S⦵(O₂) must be included [1]. The
reaction forms a mole of gas from liquids only, so ΔS⦵ should be positive, not negative [1].

**(b)** ΔS⦵ = [2(69.9) + 205.0] − 2(109.6) = 344.8 − 219.2 = **+125.6 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining entropy as "disorder" without mentioning arrangements of particles and their energy.
- Treating elements as having zero entropy, as they would for enthalpy change of formation.
- Forgetting stoichiometric coefficients when summing S⦵ values.
- Counting liquids or solids when comparing moles of gas.
- Assuming every dissolving process increases entropy, including gases dissolving.
- Giving units as kJ K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ when the data are in joules.
