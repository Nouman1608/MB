## The four specification points

1. Explain what is meant by productivity.
2. Calculate measures of productivity.
3. Evaluate the impact and importance of productivity to a business and its stakeholders.
4. Recommend and justify methods to improve productivity in a business.

## Core recall

### Definition and measures

Productivity measures how efficiently inputs are turned into outputs. It is not the same as production (total output).

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Labour productivity | Output ÷ number of employees |
| Output per hour | Output ÷ total hours worked |
| Capital productivity | Output ÷ capital employed |
| Labour cost per unit | Total labour cost ÷ output |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |

Productivity rises if output grows faster than inputs. Labour cost per unit falls when productivity rises faster than pay.

### Importance

| Stakeholder | Impact |
|---|---|
| Owners | Lower unit costs; higher profit |
| Employees | Scope for pay and security; but pressure or redundancy if gains come from cutting staff |
| Customers | Lower prices or better quality |
| Suppliers | Higher volumes if the business grows |
| Economy | Source of rising living standards; competitiveness |

Limits of the measure: ignores quality; hard to compare different products; may rise for reasons other than efficiency (a boom); a rise in labour productivity can hide a fall in capital productivity.

### Methods to improve productivity

| Method | Helps by | Costs and risks |
|---|---|---|
| Training | Faster work, fewer errors | Cost, time off work; staff may leave |
| Technology and machinery | More work per worker | High cost; job losses; maintenance |
| Motivation and incentives | Raises effort | Recurring cost; speed over quality |
| Job redesign, teamwork | More variety and flexibility | Needs time and support |
| Layout and lean methods | Cuts waste and waiting | Disruption during change |
| Management and communication | Clear targets; fewer delays | Hard to measure |

### Appraising a method

- Extra output = current output × percentage rise.
- Extra contribution = extra output × contribution per unit.
- Payback = one-off cost ÷ extra contribution per year.
- A recurring cost (a bonus) must be compared with the benefit each year.
- Check the effect on capital productivity and on staff.
- The extra output must be sold.

### Recommending

1. Identify the cause of low productivity.
2. Choose one or two methods with figures.
3. Compare cost, benefit and payback.
4. Consider staff and quality.
5. State a risk and how success will be measured.

## Quick worked reminder

Orchard Joinery (invented): Year 1 48,000 units with 40 employees gives 1,200 each. Year 2 52,500 units with 41 employees gives 1,280.5 (+6.7%). Labour cost per unit £23.33 falls to £21.87 with wages of £28,000 each.

Methods (base output 52,000; contribution £8; capital £400,000): training £30,000 gives +6% = £24,960 a year, payback 1.20 years. Machinery £120,000 gives +15% = £62,400 a year, payback 1.92 years, but capital productivity falls from 0.13 to 0.115. Bonus £18,000 a year gives +4% = £16,640, a net loss of £1,360 a year.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output compared with the inputs used |
| Production | The total amount made |
| Labour productivity | Output per employee or per hour |
| Capital productivity | Output per pound of capital employed |
| Unit labour cost | Labour cost per unit of output |
| Payback | Time taken for extra contribution to repay the cost |
| Lean methods | Methods that cut waste and waiting |

## Applying the formulas

- Per employee: output ÷ employees. Per hour: output ÷ (employees × hours each).
- Output up 10%, employment up 4%: productivity up by 1.10 ÷ 1.04 = 1.058, about 5.8%.
- If pay per employee rises faster than productivity, labour cost per unit rises.
- Compare like with like: same period, same product, same definition of inputs.
- Productivity, capacity utilisation and unit cost each show a different side of efficiency; look at them together.

## Evaluation prompts

- Is higher productivity coming from efficiency or just from higher output?
- Does the cost of the method pay back, and over what period?
- What is the effect on quality, morale and jobs?
- Can the extra output be sold?
- Has capital productivity fallen?
- Finish with a judgement and a risk.

## Exam traps

- Divide by employees or hours; total output alone is not productivity.
- Compare recurring costs with yearly benefits.
- Use percentage changes.
- Mention employees as stakeholders.
- Extra output is only valuable if there is demand.

## Self-test

1. Define productivity.
2. Output is 9,000 units with 30 employees. Find labour productivity.
3. Output rises to 9,900 with 30 employees. Find the percentage change in productivity.
4. Total labour cost is £450,000 for 9,000 units. Find labour cost per unit.
5. Output 9,000 with capital employed of £300,000. Find capital productivity.
6. Give two methods of improving productivity.
7. Give one cost of using new technology.
8. A one-off training cost of £12,000 gives £8,000 extra contribution a year. Find payback.
9. A bonus costs £10,000 a year and brings £7,000 extra contribution a year. Is it worthwhile?
10. Give one limit of productivity as a measure.
11. Why might employees worry about a productivity drive?
12. Why can labour cost per unit fall while wages stay the same?

### Answers

1. How efficiently inputs are turned into outputs; output compared with the inputs used.
2. 9,000 ÷ 30 = **300 units per employee**.
3. 9,900 ÷ 30 = 330; (330 ÷ 300 − 1) × 100 = **10%**.
4. 450,000 ÷ 9,000 = **£50**.
5. 9,000 ÷ 300,000 = **0.03** units per £.
6. Any two: training; technology; incentives; job redesign; lean methods; better management.
7. Any one: high cost; job losses; maintenance; retraining.
8. 12,000 ÷ 8,000 = **1.5 years**.
9. **No**: the yearly cost of £10,000 is more than the yearly benefit of £7,000 (net −£3,000).
10. Any one: ignores quality; hard to compare products; may rise for other reasons.
11. It may lead to pressure on staff or job cuts.
12. Because output per employee rises, so the same pay is spread over more units.
