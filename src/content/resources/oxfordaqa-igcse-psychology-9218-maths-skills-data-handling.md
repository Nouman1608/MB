**This guide covers one statement of 3.1.4.1 Data handling in OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology
(9218), Version 1.1: "Recognise and use expressions in decimal form: use ratios, fractions and
percentages, estimate results, find arithmetic means and use an appropriate number of significant
figures."** The specification says that Research methods, including data handling, is the same
material in Paper 1 (topic 4) and Paper 2 (topic 8), so these skills can be tested on either paper. The
[Research Methods (Paper 2) study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-research-methods-paper2/)
covers mean, median, mode, range, graphs and the normal distribution; this page concentrates on the
number skills that sit underneath every calculation. All data on this page is invented for practice.

## Decimals, fractions and percentages: one value, three forms

A result can be written in three equivalent ways. Questions often give one form and ask for another.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 1/2 | 0.5 | 50% |
| 1/4 | 0.25 | 25% |
| 3/4 | 0.75 | 75% |
| 1/5 | 0.2 | 20% |
| 1/8 | 0.125 | 12.5% |
| 1/3 | 0.333... | 33.3% (3 s.f.) |

**Conversions**
- Fraction → decimal: divide the top by the bottom. 3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
- Decimal → percentage: multiply by 100. 0.375 × 100 = 37.5%.
- Percentage → decimal: divide by 100. 62% = 0.62.
- Simplifying a fraction: divide top and bottom by the same number. 18/24 = 3/4 (both divided by 6).

**Worked example.** In a memory experiment, 18 of 24 participants recalled more words in the quiet
condition than in the noisy one.

```
Fraction: 18/24 = 3/4
Decimal:  3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
Percentage: 0.75 × 100 = 75%
```

## Percentages of a total, and percentage change

**Percentage of a total** = (part ÷ total) × 100.

In an observation of a staged fall, 12 passers-by helped and 30 did not. The total is 42, so the
percentage who helped = 12 ÷ 42 × 100 = 28.571..., which is **28.6%** to three significant figures.
A common error is dividing by 30 (the "did not help" group) instead of the total.

**Percentage change** = (new − original) ÷ original × 100.

If a group's mean recall rose from 40% to 52% after training, the change in percentage points is 12,
but the percentage increase is (52 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **30%**. Make sure you know which a question
asks for.

## Ratios

A **ratio** compares the sizes of two or more parts, written with a colon.

- **Part : part.** Helped : did not help = 12 : 30. Divide both by 6 to simplify: **2 : 5**.
- **Part : whole.** To turn a ratio into a fraction of the total, add the parts: 2 + 5 = 7, so 2/7
  helped and 5/7 did not.
- **Check:** 2/7 = 0.2857..., which matches the 28.6% found above.

Ratios are useful for comparing groups of different sizes. If 9 of 36 participants in one condition and
6 of 24 in another reported the smoke, the ratios reported : did not report are 9 : 27 = 1 : 3 and
6 : 18 = 1 : 3. The proportions are the same, even though the raw numbers are not.

## Arithmetic mean

The **mean** is the total of all values divided by the number of values.

**Worked example.** Reaction times (seconds) on a perception task: 0.42, 0.38, 0.51, 0.47, 0.40.

```
Total = 0.42 + 0.38 + 0.51 + 0.47 + 0.40 = 2.18
Mean  = 2.18 ÷ 5 = 0.436 seconds
```

To two significant figures, matching the precision of the data, the mean is **0.44 seconds**.

Keep the unit throughout and in the final answer. A mean without its unit can lose a mark.

## Estimating results

**Estimation** means working out a rough answer quickly, usually by rounding each number to one
significant figure. It is used to check that a calculated answer is sensible and to make predictions.

- 312 participants, 48% of whom conformed at least once: estimate 300 × 0.5 = **150**. The exact value
  is 312 × 0.48 = 149.76, so roughly 150 people. If your calculator gives 1497.6, the estimate shows
  you have made a slip.
- Mean of 19, 21, 18, 22 and 20 words: the values cluster around 20, so the mean should be close to
  **20** (it is exactly 20).
- A researcher predicts how many of 200 shoppers will help if about a quarter helped in a pilot study:
  200 × 1/4 = **50**.

Estimation questions often say "estimate" or "approximately". Show the rounded numbers you used.

## Significant figures

**Significant figures** (s.f.) are the digits that carry meaning about a number's precision, counted
from the first non-zero digit.

| Number | 1 s.f. | 2 s.f. | 3 s.f. |
|---|---|---|---|
| 28.5714 | 30 | 29 | 28.6 |
| 0.004615 | 0.005 | 0.0046 | 0.00462 |
| 12,846 | 10,000 | 13,000 | 12,800 |
| 0.436 | 0.4 | 0.44 | 0.436 |

**Rules**
1. Start counting at the first non-zero digit. Leading zeros (0.00...) are not significant.
2. Look at the next digit: 5 or more, round up; 4 or less, leave it.
3. Keep the size of the number: 12,846 to 2 s.f. is 13,000, not 13.

**What is "appropriate"?** Give an answer to the same number of significant figures as the **least
precise** data used, or as the question asks. Writing a mean of 0.436 seconds as 0.43600000 implies a
precision the stopwatch did not have. Writing it as 0.4 throws away useful information.

**Significant figures versus decimal places.** Decimal places count digits after the decimal point; 
significant figures count from the first non-zero digit. 0.0046 has four decimal places but two
significant figures.

## Worked example: a full data-handling question

**Question.** In an invented study of serial position, eight participants recalled the following
numbers of words from the middle of a list: 12, 15, 9, 14, 10, 13, 11, 16.
(a) Calculate the mean. (b) Three of the eight participants recalled more than 13 words. Express
this as a fraction and a percentage. (c) Give the mean to two significant figures. [5]

> **(a)** Total = 12 + 15 + 9 + 14 + 10 + 13 + 11 + 16 = 100 **[1]**; mean = 100 ÷ 8 = **12.5 words**
> **[1]**.
>
> **(b)** Fraction = **3/8** **[1]**; percentage = 3 ÷ 8 × 100 = **37.5%** **[1]**.
>
> **(c)** 12.5 to 2 s.f. is **13 words** (the next digit is 5, so round up) **[1]**.

## Common mistakes

- **Dividing by the wrong total** when finding a percentage.
- **Not simplifying ratios** or fractions when asked to give them "in their simplest form".
- **Rounding too early.** Keep full values during the calculation and round only the final answer.
- **Counting leading zeros** as significant figures.
- **Dropping units** from means and estimates.
- **Confusing a change in percentage points with a percentage change.**

## Exam technique

- **Calculate:** write the calculation, then the answer with units. Working can earn marks even if the
  final answer is wrong.
- **Give your answer to … significant figures:** do exactly that; extra figures can lose the mark.
- **Estimate:** show the rounded values you used.
- **Express as a fraction / ratio:** simplify unless told otherwise.
