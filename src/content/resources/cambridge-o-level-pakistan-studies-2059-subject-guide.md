Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies (2059) is a two-part course that asks
students to think both as historians and as geographers. One half
examines how Pakistan emerged as an independent state and how it
developed afterwards; the other examines the country's land, resources,
economy and people. Two written papers make up the assessment, each
90 minutes long, marked out of 75 and worth 50% of the overall result.
The description below follows the 2026 edition of the syllabus, which
Cambridge publishes as a single-year edition.

## How the course is organised

Each half of the syllabus belongs to one paper, so the organisation of
the course and the organisation of the exams are the same thing.

### Paper 1 – The History and Culture of Pakistan

Three sections take the story from its roots to the end of the
twentieth century:

- **Cultural and Historical Background to the Pakistan Movement**, which
  explains the context in which Muslim political identity developed.
- **The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47**, the period of organised
  political struggle that ended with independence.
- **Nationhood, 1947–99**, which follows the state through its early
  challenges and later political, economic and international
  developments.

### Paper 2 – The Environment of Pakistan

Eight sections cover the country's geography:

- The land of Pakistan
- Natural resources – an issue of sustainability
- Power
- Agricultural development
- Industrial development
- Trade
- Transport and telecommunications
- Population and employment

The first two sections are about what the country has to work with;
the middle three are about how it produces energy, food and goods; and
the final three are about movement, exchange and people.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Share of grade |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | The history and culture of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 75 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | The environment of Pakistan | 90 minutes | 75 | 50% |

Both papers are written exams marked externally by Cambridge, and all
answers are written in English. There are no tiers or optional routes:
every candidate takes both papers at the end of the course, as the
qualification is linear.

The papers are built differently. In Paper 1, Section A is a compulsory
source-based question worth 25 marks, and Section B, worth 50 marks,
asks you to answer two of four essay-style questions. In Paper 2 you
choose three of five questions, each worth 25 marks. The 2026 syllabus
is examined in both the June and November series.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers sit side by side rather than in sequence, but a student
who keeps both in view will write better answers on each. The
*Nationhood, 1947–99* section of Paper 1 is, in effect, the political
background to the economy described in Paper 2: questions about why
industry developed where it did, or why power generation became a
national priority, are easier to answer if you know the history.
Likewise, an understanding of the physical land, its rivers and its
resources helps make sense of disputes and decisions studied in
Paper 1.

The skills, however, are distinct and need separate practice:

- **Source handling** for Paper 1 Section A, where you interpret and
  use material you have not seen before.
- **Extended historical writing** for Section B, where explanation and
  judgement are required across a sustained answer.
- **Geographical interpretation** for Paper 2, using maps, graphs and
  data, and discussing issues such as sustainability with balance.

Because you have a choice of questions in Section B of Paper 1 and in
Paper 2, some students are tempted to drop sections. That is risky: a
narrow revision base leaves little room if your chosen questions are
harder than expected.

## Planning your study

A workable sequence for the course is to run the two papers in
parallel, matching foundations with foundations:

1. Pair the cultural and historical background with *The land of
   Pakistan* and *Natural resources*.
2. Pair *The Emergence of Pakistan, 1906–47* with power, agriculture and
   industry.
3. Pair *Nationhood, 1947–99* with trade, transport and
   telecommunications, and population and employment.

Leave a clear block before the exam series you are entered for (June or
November) to consolidate both papers. Start answering source questions
and short essay plans as soon as the relevant history is taught, and
begin Paper 2 data questions once the first geography sections are
complete. Full timed papers and paper-specific technique come at the
end, and are covered in the exam preparation guide rather than here.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the two papers in O Level Pakistan Studies 2059?**
Paper 1 is The history and culture of Pakistan and Paper 2 is The environment of Pakistan. Each is a 90-minute written exam worth 75 marks and 50% of the qualification.

**How is Paper 1 structured?**
Section A is a compulsory source-based question worth 25 marks. Section B is worth 50 marks and you answer two of four essay-style questions.

**How many questions do I answer in Paper 2?**
Three of the five questions set, each worth 25 marks.

**When can I sit O Level Pakistan Studies?**
The 2026 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.

**Is O Level 2059 the same as IGCSE 0448?**
The paper titles, timings and mark split are the same as Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448, but they are separate syllabuses, so check which one you are entered for.
