**Here are two Paper 2 style questions on Section 4 Agricultural development, each worth 25 marks
and set out in parts (a) to (d) as in the 2059 exam.** Question 1 concentrates on crops, seasons and
farm systems; Question 2 on irrigation, waterlogging and government action. The figures in the
resources are illustrative only.

## Question 1: Crops and farming systems

**Fig. 1.1** (illustrative): a farming calendar for a farm in southern Punjab.

| Month | Activity |
|---|---|
| April–May | Cotton sown |
| June–September | Cotton irrigated and sprayed |
| October–December | Cotton picked |
| November–December | Wheat sown after cotton |
| April | Wheat harvested |

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 1.1, name the kharif crop grown on this farm. **[1]**

**(ii)** Name **one** district in Punjab where this farming pattern is common. **[1]**

**(iii)** Using Fig. 1.1, explain why the farmer may be short of labour in November. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw and label a systems diagram for a cash crop farm like the one in Fig. 1.1, showing
**two** inputs, **two** processes and **two** outputs. **[4]**

**(c) (i)** For **each** of the following, name an area where it is grown and give **one** use:
apricots; tobacco; millet. **[3]**

**(ii)** Explain how natural and human factors limit the yield of rice grown by traditional
methods on small, fragmented holdings. **[5]**

**(d)** "Small subsistence farmers in Pakistan should switch to growing cash crops." Do you agree?
Give reasons for your answer, referring to examples in Pakistan. **[9]**

## Mark scheme: Question 1

**(a) (i)** Cotton [1].

**(ii)** Any cotton-belt district, for example Vehari, Bahawalnagar, Khanewal, Rahim Yar Khan,
Multan [1].

**(iii)** Cotton picking is still going on [1] at the same time as wheat must be sown, so two
labour-intensive jobs overlap [1].

**(b)** Diagram as a flow from inputs to processes to outputs, 1 mark for correct structure with
arrows, up to 3 for content. Inputs: certified seed, fertiliser, tubewell or canal water, tractor,
hired labour, capital. Processes: ploughing, sowing, spraying, irrigating, picking. Outputs: seed
cotton and wheat for sale, cotton sticks for fuel, profit. Credit a feedback arrow from profit to
next year's inputs.

**(c) (i)** One mark per crop for area and use together. Apricots: Hunza, Skardu or another
Gilgit-Baltistan valley; dried fruit or fresh food. Tobacco: Mardan or Swabi; cigarettes. Millet:
Thal, Thar margins or other sandy rain-fed areas; grain for food or fodder.

**(ii)** Natural (up to 3): unreliable monsoon or canal supply [1]; pests and diseases such as
stem borers [1]; floods damaging nursery beds [1]; soils exhausted without fertiliser [1].
Human (up to 3): plots too small and scattered for machinery [1]; lack of capital for fertiliser,
pesticide or good seed [1]; slow hand transplanting and threshing [1]; little advice from extension
workers [1]. Maximum 5.

**(d)** Levels marking: Level 3 (7–9) a balanced, developed answer with Pakistani examples and a
judgement; Level 2 (4–6) developed on one side or simple on both; Level 1 (1–3) basic points.

**For switching:** cash crops such as cotton or sugar cane can earn more per acre; income buys
food, schooling and better inputs; supplies mills and exports. **Against:** cash crops need costly
inputs and loans, so a failed season (pest outbreak, flood such as 2022) leaves debt; prices
fluctuate; less food grown for the family; tiny fragmented plots may be too small to profit.
**Judgement:** a strong answer may conclude "partly", for example growing a mix such as wheat for
food plus a high-value vegetable or fruit for sale.

## Mark scheme: Question 2

**(a) (i)** Salt / salinity [1].

**(ii)** Water seeps from the unlined canal [1], raising the water table close to the surface [1];
in hot weather water rises by capillary action and evaporates, leaving salt behind [1].

**(iii)** Any two: tubewells pump the water table down [1]; surface or tile drains carry saline
water away [1]; flushing with fresh water [1]; adding gypsum [1]; planting salt-tolerant crops [1].

**(b)** Comparison needed, not two separate descriptions. Up to 4 from: karez uses gravity so has
no fuel cost, while a tubewell needs diesel, electricity or solar panels [1]; karez loses little
water to evaporation because it is underground [1]; a tubewell gives water on demand and in larger
amounts [1]; karez needs constant community labour to clean, a tubewell needs capital to drill and
run [1]; tubewells lower the water table, which can dry up nearby karez [1].

**(c) (i)** A barrage raises the level of the river so water can be diverted into canals [1]; a link
canal carries water from one river to another, for example from the western rivers to areas once
fed by eastern rivers [1].

**(ii)** In summer herders take flocks up to high mountain pastures where grass grows after
snowmelt [1]; in autumn they bring them down to valleys or plains before snow covers the pastures
[1]; this gives year-round grazing without buying fodder and lets lower pastures recover [1].

**(d)** Levels marking: Level 3 (8–10); Level 2 (4–7); Level 1 (1–3).

**Successes:** Green Revolution high-yielding wheat raised output from the 1960s; land reforms in
1959, 1972 and 1977 set ceilings and gave some tenants land; credit through the Zarai Taraqiati
Bank and subsidised fertiliser increased input use; research at institutes such as Faisalabad's
Ayub Agricultural Research Institute produced new varieties; SCARP tubewells and drains reclaimed
land.

**Limitations:** reforms were evaded and large estates remained; benefits went mainly to larger
farmers with capital; small tenants cannot meet loan collateral; overuse of fertiliser and
pesticide pollutes water; yields per acre still lag behind many countries; groundwater is
falling. A Level 3 answer reaches a judgement, for example that action has raised total output but
has been less successful at helping small farmers or making farming sustainable.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does "compare" require in Paper 2?**
The syllabus glossary says you must explain what is similar and different about two things; two
separate descriptions do not make a comparison. Use linking words such as "whereas".

**How do I pick up marks on a photograph question?**
Name the crop or animal, then point to the evidence in the photograph, such as cotton bolls or
flooded fields in rows.

**How much should I write for a 9 or 10 mark part (d)?**
Enough to develop two or three points on each side with Pakistani examples, then a clear
judgement. Quality of reasoning matters more than length.
