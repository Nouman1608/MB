## Questions

**1.** For Criterion A, state what changes between the 3-4 band and the 5-6 band, and between the 5-6 band and the 7-8 band. **[3]**

**2.** (Year 1 level) A box of 15 notebooks costs 48.75. At the same rate, find the cost of 22 notebooks. **[3]**

**3.** (Year 5 level) A loading ramp rises 0.36 m over a horizontal distance of 4.32 m.

**(a)** Find the length of the sloping surface, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the slope as a ratio 1 : k. **[1]**

**4.** (Year 5 level) A bamboo shoot is 30 cm tall and grows 8% taller each week. After how many whole weeks is it first taller than 100 cm? **[5]**

**5.** (Year 1 level) Hexagonal tiles with side 1 are joined edge to edge in a straight row. One tile has perimeter 6, two tiles have perimeter 10, and three tiles have perimeter 14.

**(a)** Describe the pattern as a rule for n tiles. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the perimeter of 25 tiles, and verify the rule with 4 tiles. **[2]**

**6.** (Year 3 level) The first five terms of a pattern are 3, 8, 15, 24, 35.

**(a)** Find a rule for the nth term. **[3]**
**(b)** Verify your rule for the 6th term, which is 48, and justify it by rewriting the rule. **[2]**

**7.** (Year 3 level) A task asks you to investigate the number of dots in stage n of a growing pattern, where stages 1 to 4 have 4, 7, 10 and 13 dots. Three students write:

- Student A: "It goes up by 3 each time, so stage 5 has 16."
- Student B: "Dots = 3n + 1, because the table suggests it."
- Student C: "Dots = 3n + 1. Stage 8 has 25 dots, which I checked by drawing it. The pattern starts with one dot and adds 3 new dots at each stage."

For each student, say which Criterion B strands are evidenced and what is missing. **[4]**

**8.** (Year 5 level) Work out (2k − 1)(2k + 1) + 1 for k = 1, 2, 3 and describe what you notice. Then prove your conjecture for every positive integer k. **[6]**

**9.** (Year 5 level) A student's table contains an arithmetic slip in the fourth row. The quadratic rule she gives fits her table exactly and is as complex as the correct rule. State the highest Criterion B level she can reach, and explain why. **[3]**

**10.** (Year 5 level) A tree diagram starts with 3 branches at stage 1. At each later stage every branch from the previous stage splits into 2. Let u(n) be the number of branches at stage n and T(n) be the total number of branches over stages 1 to n.

**(a)** Complete a table of u(n) and T(n) for n = 1 to 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest general rules for u(n) and T(n). **[2]**
**(c)** Test your rule for T(n) with n = 6, which has 189 branches in total. **[1]**
**(d)** Prove the rule for T(n). **[3]**

**11.** (Year 3 or 5 level) A school club hires a hall for 180 and spends 6 on food per ticket sold. Tickets cost 15 each.

**(a)** Write an expression for the profit P when t tickets are sold. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of tickets needed to break even. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the smallest number of tickets that gives a profit of at least 400. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** From 3-4 to 5-6, the problems change from more complex to challenging, while staying in familiar situations [1]. From 5-6 to 7-8, the problems stay challenging but now include unfamiliar situations as well as familiar ones [1]. The strands (select, apply, solve) are worded the same way in every band [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer names both what changes (problem difficulty, then situation) and what stays the same; listing only the band labels shows less understanding.

**2.** Unit rate: 48.75 ÷ 15 = 3.25 per notebook [1]. 3.25 × 22 [1] = **71.50 [1]**. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit rate as a separate step. A bare final figure leaves no evidence for the selection and application strands.

**3. (a)** Select Pythagoras for the right-angled triangle [1]. Length² = 0.36² + 4.32² = 0.1296 + 18.6624 = 18.792 [1]. Length = **4.33 m** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** 4.32 ÷ 0.36 = 12, so the slope is **1 : 12 [1]**. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why Pythagoras applies, since the problem never names it. Giving the length to more or fewer figures than asked is an avoidable loss.

**4.** Select exponential growth: height = 30 × 1.08^t [1]. Set up 30 × 1.08^t > 100, so 1.08^t > 3.33… [1]. Take logarithms (extended) to get t > ln(10/3) ÷ ln 1.08 = 15.64, or narrow down by testing whole values [1]. Check: t = 15 gives 95.2 cm (below 100) and t = 16 gives 102.8 cm (above) [1]. First whole number of weeks: **16 weeks [1]**. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The answer must be rounded up to 16, not down to 15, because 15 weeks has not yet reached 100 cm. The check line shows you understand why.

**5. (a)** Each extra tile adds 4 to the perimeter (6, 10, 14) [1]. Rule: **perimeter = 4n + 2 [1]**.
**(b)** For n = 25: 4 × 25 + 2 = **102 [1]**. Verify with 4 tiles: the rule gives 18, and 14 + 4 = 18 from the pattern [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Strand iii credit comes from testing a case that was not used to build the rule. Reusing n = 1 to 3 does not show this.

**6. (a)** First differences: 5, 7, 9, 11 [1]. Second differences are constant at 2, so the rule has n² as its leading term [1]. Subtracting n² leaves 2, 4, 6, 8, 10, which is 2n, so **u(n) = n² + 2n [1]**.
**(b)** n = 6: 36 + 12 = 48 [1]. Justify: n² + 2n = (n + 1)² − 1, so each term is one less than a square, and 4, 9, 16, 25, 36 each less 1 gives the given terms [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A rewrite such as (n + 1)² − 1 explains why the pattern behaves as it does, which goes beyond checking one more value.

**7.** Student A states a prediction consistent with the pattern (strand ii, lower band) but gives no rule in n and no verification [1]. Student B suggests a general rule (strand ii at a higher band) but shows no test or justification, so strand iii is missing [1]. Student C gives the rule, tests it on a new case (stage 8: 3 × 8 + 1 = 25) and justifies it from the structure of the pattern, so all of strands ii and iii are evidenced [1]. None of the three shows how a technique was chosen (strand i), such as a table or differences, which is also needed; and if the task told them which method to use, the level would be capped at 4 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Match each statement to a strand and say what extra step would raise it. Avoid assigning a level without naming the evidence.

**8.** k = 1: 1 × 3 + 1 = 4; k = 2: 3 × 5 + 1 = 16; k = 3: 5 × 7 + 1 = 36 [1]. The results are 4, 16, 36, which are 4k², so the conjecture is that the result is always (2k)², a perfect square and a multiple of 4 [1]. Let the two odd numbers be 2k − 1 and 2k + 1 [1]. Their product is (2k − 1)(2k + 1) = 4k² − 1 [1]. Adding 1 gives 4k² [1], which equals (2k)² for every positive integer k, so it is a perfect square and a multiple of 4 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Three values suggest the conjecture; the algebra with a general k is what proves it. State the conclusion for every k in words.

**9.** The highest level is **6 [1]**. The rule is consistent with her findings, but the findings are incorrect [1]. Provided the rule is as complex as the correct one, the cap is 6 and no higher [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the condition that the rule is of equivalent complexity; without it, the cap statement is incomplete.

**10. (a)**
| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| u(n) | 3 | 6 | 12 | 24 |
| T(n) | 3 | 9 | 21 | 45 |

Two correct rows [2].
**(b)** u(n) doubles each time from 3, so **u(n) = 3 × 2^(n−1) [1]**. T(n) = 3, 9, 21, 45 fits **T(n) = 3(2^n − 1) [1]**.
**(c)** n = 6: 3 × (64 − 1) = 189, matching the given value [1].
**(d)** T(n) = 3(1 + 2 + 4 + … + 2^(n−1)). Let S = 1 + 2 + 4 + … + 2^(n−1) [1]. Then 2S = 2 + 4 + … + 2^n, so 2S − S = 2^n − 1 [1]. Hence S = 2^n − 1 and **T(n) = 3(2^n − 1)** for every n [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A proof must cover all n. The doubling trick above does, while testing n = 6 only supports the rule.

**11. (a)** Income 15t and cost 180 + 6t [1]. P = 15t − (180 + 6t) = **9t − 180 [1]**.
**(b)** 9t − 180 = 0, so t = 180 ÷ 9 = **20 tickets [1]**.
**(c)** 9t − 180 ≥ 400, so 9t ≥ 580 [1]. t ≥ 64.4… [1]. Tickets are whole, so **65 tickets [1]**. Check: 9 × 65 − 180 = 405 (at least 400) and 9 × 64 − 180 = 396 (less) **(no extra mark)**. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An inequality with whole items must be rounded up. Rounding 64.4 to 64 gives profit 396, below the target.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not saying why a method was chosen, so strand i has no evidence.
- Rounding the wrong way when a question asks for the first whole value that passes a limit.
- Stating "it goes up by 3" without a rule in n.
- Testing a rule only on the cases used to find it.
- Treating several correct checks as a proof when the question asks you to prove.
- Writing a rule from two data points and not checking it against a third.
- Ignoring units or the number of significant figures asked for.
- Describing a band by its label alone and not by what changes in the descriptor.
