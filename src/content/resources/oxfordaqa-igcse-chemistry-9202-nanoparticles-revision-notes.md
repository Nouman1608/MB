These notes condense section 3.2.4, Nanoparticles, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2. This section is chemistry-only content. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [nanoparticles study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-nanoparticles/); then try the [nanoparticles practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-nanoparticles-practice/).

## What the specification says

- Nanoscience: structures **1–100 nm** in size, **a few hundred atoms**.
- Nanoparticles have properties **different from the bulk** material.
- They have a **high surface area to volume ratio**.
- Possible applications: new computers, new catalysts, new coatings, highly selective sensors, stronger and lighter construction materials, new cosmetics such as suntan creams and deodorants.
- No specific examples or properties to learn; questions give information to apply.

## Units

| Unit | In metres | In nanometres |
|---|---|---|
| 1 nm | 1 × 10⁻⁹ m | 1 |
| 1 µm | 1 × 10⁻⁶ m | 1000 |
| 1 mm | 1 × 10⁻³ m | 1 000 000 |

```
metres → nm:  divide by 1 × 10⁻⁹  (multiply by 10⁹)
µm → nm:      multiply by 1000
```

Quick checks:

- 6 × 10⁻⁸ m = 60 nm → nanoparticle.
- 0.2 µm = 200 nm → not a nanoparticle.
- 3 × 10⁻⁹ m = 3 nm → nanoparticle.

## Surface area to volume ratio

For a cube of side L: **SA = 6L²**, **V = L³**, **SA : V = 6 ÷ L**.

| Side / nm | SA / nm² | V / nm³ | SA : V per nm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 | 1 | 6 |
| 10 | 600 | 1000 | 0.6 |
| 100 | 60 000 | 1 000 000 | 0.06 |

Rule: **side ÷ 10 → SA : V × 10.**

Consequence: for the same mass, nanoparticles expose far more surface, so a much larger fraction of their atoms can contact other substances.

## Applications and the reason behind each

| Application | Why the nanoscale helps |
|---|---|
| Catalysts | more surface per gram for reactions |
| Sensors | large surface to detect small amounts; can be made to respond to one substance |
| Coatings | very thin, even layers |
| Construction materials | stronger and lighter |
| Computers | components built on a tiny scale |
| Suntan creams, deodorants | new cosmetic properties from tiny particles |

## How to answer an information question

1. Pick out the **size** and check it is 1–100 nm.
2. Pick out the **property** described.
3. Link the property to **SA : V** or to **particle size**.
4. Link that to the **use**.
5. If the information mentions a concern, weigh it against the benefit.

## Worked recall: comparing two powders

Powder A has particles 50 µm across; powder B has particles 50 nm across.

```
50 µm = 50 000 nm
size ratio  = 50 000 ÷ 50 = 1000
SA : V of B = 1000 × SA : V of A
```

Same mass of each: B exposes about 1000 times more surface, so it is far more effective wherever surface contact matters (catalysts, sensors).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nanoscience | the study of structures 1–100 nm in size |
| Nanoparticle | particle 1–100 nm across, a few hundred atoms |
| Nanometre | 1 × 10⁻⁹ m |
| Bulk material | the same substance in ordinary-sized pieces |
| Surface area to volume ratio | surface area divided by volume; increases as particles get smaller |
| Selective sensor | a sensor that responds to one particular substance |

## Exam traps

- The range is **nano**metres, 1–100 nm, not micrometres.
- "Large surface area" alone is not enough: say **surface area to volume ratio**.
- A nanoparticle is the **same substance** as the bulk material, with different properties.
- Convert all sizes to the same unit before comparing.
- Do not recall your own examples when the question provides information: use what is given.
- A 10-times smaller side means a 10-times larger ratio, not 100 times.

## Self-test

1. What size range does nanoscience cover?
2. Roughly how many atoms does a nanoparticle contain?
3. Convert 4.5 × 10⁻⁸ m into nanometres. Is it a nanoparticle?
4. Convert 0.15 µm into nanometres. Is it a nanoparticle?
5. A cube has side 5 nm. Calculate its surface area to volume ratio.
6. A particle's side is reduced from 50 nm to 5 nm. By what factor does the surface area to volume ratio change?
7. Why can a small mass of nanoparticle catalyst be as effective as a larger mass of the same catalyst as a powder?
8. Name three possible applications of nanoparticles listed in the specification.
9. Why can nanoparticles have different properties from the same material in bulk?
10. Explain what "highly selective sensor" means.
11. Give the formula for the surface area to volume ratio of a cube of side L.
12. A powder's particles are 40 µm across and a nanopowder's are 40 nm across. How many times larger is the surface area to volume ratio of the nanopowder?

## Answers

1. 1–100 nm.
2. Of the order of a few hundred.
3. 45 nm; yes.
4. 150 nm; no.
5. SA = 6 × 25 = 150 nm²; V = 125 nm³; ratio = 150 ÷ 125 = 1.2 per nm.
6. It increases 10 times.
7. Nanoparticles have a much higher surface area to volume ratio, so more of the catalyst is at the surface where the reaction happens.
8. Any three: new computers, catalysts, coatings, highly selective sensors, stronger and lighter construction materials, cosmetics such as suntan creams and deodorants.
9. A much larger fraction of their atoms are at the surface (high surface area to volume ratio).
10. A sensor that detects one particular substance and does not respond to others.
11. 6 ÷ L.
12. 40 µm = 40 000 nm, so the side is 1000 times smaller and the ratio is 1000 times larger.
