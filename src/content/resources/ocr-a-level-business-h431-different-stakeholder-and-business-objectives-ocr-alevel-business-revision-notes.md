These notes condense the OCR A Level Business (H431) content "Different stakeholder and business objectives" (specification Version 2.1, January 2026; final assessment summer 2027). The specification asks you to distinguish aims and the levels of objective, explain the importance of SMART objectives and the hierarchy of objectives, evaluate setting aims and objectives, explain the effect of sector, explain and evaluate communication, explain why objectives change, and recommend and justify aims and objectives and how changes are implemented. Businesses named are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organisational aim | Broad, long-term purpose; usually not a number |
| Corporate (business) objective | Measurable target for the whole business |
| Strategic objective | Medium to long-term target showing how the corporate objective will be met |
| Tactical objective | Shorter-term target for a department or function |
| Operational objective | Day-to-day target for a team, person or process |
| Hierarchy of objectives | The chain linking each level to the one above |
| SMART | Specific, measurable, achievable, realistic, time-bound |
| Line of sight | Staff can see how their target supports the level above |
| Sector | Private, public or third (voluntary/social) sector |

## Four tests to classify any objective

1. **Who sets it?** Board → senior managers → middle managers → supervisors.
2. **Scope?** Whole business → major part → department → team or task.
3. **Time span?** Years → months → days.
4. **Resources at stake?** Large and hard to reverse → small.

The same target can sit at different levels in a small and a large business. Say which test you used.

## SMART: test it, do not just name it

- **Measurable**: can the target be stated as a figure? Target revenue = current revenue × (1 + growth rate).
- **Achievable**: check capacity, finance and staff in the case. If output must rise by the same percentage as revenue, required capacity use = current use × (1 + growth rate).
- **Time-bound**: is there a deadline?

**Quick check**: a business at 80% capacity aiming for 30% more output would need 80% × 1.30 = 104% of capacity: not achievable without investment.

| Stakeholder | Why SMART objectives matter to them |
|---|---|
| Managers | Monitor progress, act early |
| Employees | Clear expectations, fair appraisal |
| Shareholders | Judge directors' performance |
| Lenders | Assess likelihood of repayment |
| Customers, community | Hold the business to published promises |

**Limitation**: what is measured gets attention; harder-to-measure goals such as quality may suffer.

## Hierarchy: how it breaks

- Departments given conflicting targets.
- Targets passed down without the reason.
- Top-level objective changed but lower levels not updated.

## Evaluating the importance of objectives: it depends on...

| Factor | Objectives matter more when... |
|---|---|
| Size | The business is large and spread across sites |
| Market change | The market is stable enough for long-term targets to stay relevant |
| Ownership | Outside shareholders need reassurance |
| Quality | The objectives are well chosen, few and linked |

## Sector

| Sector | Typical objectives | Measured by |
|---|---|---|
| Private | Profit, growth, market share, survival | Financial results |
| Public | Service quality, access, value for money, budget | Service targets, cost per user |
| Third | Social or environmental purpose | Impact: people helped, outcomes |

Third-sector and public objectives can still be SMART, for example "cut average waiting time from 35 to 21 days within a year".

## Communication

- **Match method to audience**: briefings for one site; intranet or video for many sites; annual report for shareholders; website and press for the public.
- **Mis-communication chain**: target misread → effort misdirected → objective missed → blame → low morale and trust → later messages doubted; externally, investor confidence falls.
- **Better communication**: explain the reason, make it two-way, check understanding, repeat at review points.
- **Evaluate on**: cost and time against risk of error, number and spread of staff, complexity of the change.

## Why objectives change

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| New owners or managers | Recession or recovery |
| Growth to a new size | New competitor |
| Objective met or clearly missed | New technology |
| Change in finance available | New law or regulation |
| Stage of the business life cycle | Changing social attitudes |

## Recommend and justify: five moves

1. One clear objective, written SMART.
2. Evidence from the case that supports it.
3. A calculation if data is given.
4. How the change will be implemented: explain the reason, communicate, set lower-level targets, review.
5. One risk or alternative, weighed, then a final judgement.

## Exam traps

- Classifying by wording alone.
- Calling a target SMART without checking achievability against the case.
- Saying public or third-sector bodies cannot have measurable objectives.
- Communication methods listed with no audience.
- A recommendation with no implementation.
- A judgement with no "it depends on" condition.

## Self-test

1. Give the four tests for classifying an objective.
2. Why might "open a second outlet" be strategic for one business and tactical for another?
3. A firm has revenue of £2.5m and targets 20% growth. What is the target revenue?
4. A firm at 90% capacity targets a 15% rise in output. What capacity use is needed, and is it achievable?
5. Give two reasons why SMART objectives matter to employees or lenders.
6. Give one limitation of SMART objectives.
7. State two ways the hierarchy of objectives can break down.
8. Give two factors that affect how important objectives are to a business.
9. Write a SMART objective for a public library service.
10. Give two consequences of mis-communicating an objective.
11. Give one internal and one external reason why objectives may change.
12. What must a "recommend and justify" answer on objectives include besides the objective itself?

### Answers

1. Who sets it; its scope; its time span; the resources at stake.
2. It commits a large share of a small firm's resources and changes its direction, but is one manager's project in a large chain.
3. £2.5m × 1.20 = £3.0m.
4. 90% × 1.15 = 103.5%; not achievable without extra capacity.
5. Employees know what is expected and can be appraised fairly; lenders can judge whether repayment is likely.
6. Attention goes to what is measured, so harder-to-measure goals such as quality may suffer.
7. Any two: conflicting departmental targets; targets passed down without reasons; lower levels not updated after a change at the top.
8. Any two: size; pace of market change; ownership; quality of the objectives.
9. For example: increase the number of active library members from 8,000 to 9,000 within 12 months.
10. Any two: effort misdirected; objective missed; low morale; reduced trust in later messages; falling investor confidence.
11. Internal: new managers, growth, target met or missed, change in finance. External: recession, new competitor, new technology, new law, social change.
12. Evidence from the case, a calculation where data is given, how the change will be implemented, and a weighed risk leading to a judgement.
