The prescribed subjects are the content for IB DP History Paper 1, a one-hour source paper worth 24 marks (30% at SL, 20% at HL in the course first assessed in 2017). You study one of five prescribed subjects, each with two case studies, and answer four questions on a set of sources: comprehension, origin-purpose-value-limitation, comparison, and a synthesis that combines the sources with your own knowledge.

Related: [Prescribed subjects study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-study-guide/) and
[practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-prescribed-subjects-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage (DP History, first assessment 2017)

| No. | Prescribed subject | Case study 1 | Case study 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Military leaders | Genghis Khan (c.1200-1227) | Richard I of England (1173-1199) |
| 2 | Conquest and its impact | The final stages of Muslim rule in Spain (1482-1492) | The conquest of Mexico and Peru (1519-1551) |
| 3 | The move to global war | Japanese expansion in East Asia (1931-1941) | German and Italian expansion (1933-1940) |
| 4 | Rights and protest | Civil rights movement in the United States (1954-1965) | Apartheid South Africa (1948-1964) |
| 5 | Conflict and intervention | Rwanda (1990-1998) | Kosovo (1989-2002) |

Sources in the exam are set on one case study per prescribed subject, so revise both case studies of your subject.

## The four Paper 1 questions

| Question | Marks | Skill | Key rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1(a) | 3 | Comprehension of a written source | Three distinct points from the source |
| 1(b) | 2 | Message of a visual or second source | Two points; describe what the image conveys |
| 2 | 4 | Origin, purpose and content: value and limitations | Link each point to the named topic |
| 3 | 6 | Compare and contrast two sources | Running comparison: similarities *and* differences |
| 4 | 9 | Synthesis of sources and own knowledge | Use the sources *and* own knowledge to answer the question |

Timing at 2.5 minutes per mark: Q1 about 12 minutes, Q2 10, Q3 15, Q4 about 23.

## PS1 Military leaders

| Genghis Khan (c.1200-1227) | Richard I (1173-1199) |
|---|---|
| Unified Mongol tribes; proclaimed Genghis Khan at the *kurultai* of 1206 | Joined the revolt against Henry II (1173-74); Duke of Aquitaine |
| Campaigns against Western Xia, the Jin (Zhongdu taken 1215) and the Khwarazmian Empire (from 1219) | Crowned 1189; took the cross and financed the Third Crusade |
| Methods: decimal army organisation, mobility, horse archers, captured siege engineers, intelligence, deliberate terror | Siege of Acre (1191), execution of Muslim prisoners; victory at Arsuf (1191); Jaffa (1192) |
| Rule: the *Yassa* law code, merit-based appointment, religious tolerance, a courier system | Treaty of Jaffa with Saladin (1192); Jerusalem not retaken |
| Debate: brilliant organiser or destroyer? Sources are often written by enemies or much later | Captured on his return, ransomed (1194); wars with Philip II; died at Châlus (1199) |

## PS2 Conquest and its impact

| Muslim Spain (1482-1492) | Mexico and Peru (1519-1551) |
|---|---|
| Nasrid kingdom of Granada divided by civil war (Boabdil against his father and uncle) | Cortés lands in 1519; alliance with Tlaxcala; Moctezuma; the *Noche Triste* (1520); fall of Tenochtitlan (1521) |
| Ferdinand and Isabella's war from 1482: Alhama (1482), Málaga (1487) | Role of Malinche, disease (smallpox), indigenous allies and steel weapons |
| Treaty of Granada (1491) promises religious tolerance; surrender January 1492 | Pizarro captures Atahualpa at Cajamarca (1532), executes him (1533); Inca civil war aided the Spanish |
| Impact: expulsion of the Jews (1492); forced conversions of Muslims from 1499-1502; the Inquisition | Impact: *encomienda*, the New Laws (1542), conquistador civil wars, the Valladolid debate (1550-51) between Las Casas and Sepúlveda |

## PS3 The move to global war

| Japanese expansion (1931-1941) | German and Italian expansion (1933-1940) |
|---|---|
| Causes: nationalism and militarism; Depression; army influence over government | Causes: fascist and Nazi ideology; economic aims; changing alignments; end of collective security |
| Mukden Incident and invasion of Manchuria (1931); Manchukuo (1932) | German challenges to the settlement 1933-38: leaving the League, rearmament, Rhineland, Anschluss |
| Lytton Report; Japan leaves the League (1933) | Italy: Abyssinia (1935-36), Albania (1939) |
| Sino-Japanese War from 1937 (Marco Polo Bridge); Nanjing; the Second United Front in China | Sudetenland and Munich (1938); Czechoslovakia (March 1939); Pact of Steel; Nazi-Soviet Pact; Poland |
| Tripartite Pact (1940); US embargoes; Pearl Harbor (December 1941) | Responses: Stresa, Hoare-Laval, appeasement, the guarantee to Poland; Italy enters the war (June 1940) |

## PS4 Rights and protest

| US civil rights (1954-1965) | Apartheid South Africa (1948-1964) |
|---|---|
| Discrimination: segregation, voting barriers, racial violence, the Ku Klux Klan | Legislation: Population Registration, Group Areas, Bantu Authorities, Bantu Education; "petty" and "grand" apartheid |
| *Brown v. Board of Education* (1954); Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955-56); Little Rock (1957) | Defiance Campaign (1952); bus boycotts; Freedom Charter (1955) |
| Sit-ins (1960), Freedom Rides (1961), Birmingham and the March on Washington (1963), Freedom Summer (1964), Selma (1965) | Sharpeville (1960); bans on the ANC and PAC; turn to armed struggle |
| Actors: King, Malcolm X, Johnson; NAACP, SCLC, SNCC | Actors: Mandela, Luthuli; ANC, SACP, Umkhonto we Sizwe |
| Civil Rights Act (1964); Voting Rights Act (1965) | Rivonia Trial (1963-64): leaders imprisoned |

## PS5 Conflict and intervention

| Rwanda (1990-1998) | Kosovo (1989-2002) |
|---|---|
| Hutu-Tutsi tensions rooted in colonial classification; RPF invasion (1990) | Milošević removes Kosovo's autonomy (1989) |
| Arusha Accords (1993); UNAMIR under Dallaire | Rugova's non-violent resistance; rise of the KLA in the later 1990s |
| President Habyarimana's plane shot down (6 April 1994); genocide of about 800,000 people in some 100 days | Račak (January 1999); failed Rambouillet talks |
| Limited UN response; French Operation Turquoise; RPF victory (July 1994) | NATO bombing (March-June 1999) without UN Security Council authorisation; mass flight of Kosovo Albanians |
| Impact: refugee crisis, wars in Zaire/DRC from 1996; International Criminal Tribunal for Rwanda; *gacaca* courts | Resolution 1244; KFOR and UNMIK; Milošević handed to The Hague (2001), trial opens 2002 |

## Worked example: a Question 2 paragraph

*Source: a speech by a civil rights leader to a church congregation, 1963, on the value of non-violence.*

"The origin -- a leading campaigner speaking in 1963 at the height of the Birmingham protests -- gives the source value for a historian studying the methods of the movement, as it shows how leaders justified non-violence to supporters. Its purpose, to persuade and inspire a sympathetic audience, limits it: it presents an idealised picture and does not show how far ordinary participants shared the commitment or how opponents responded."

The paragraph links origin, purpose and content to the named topic -- the requirement of the top markband for Question 2.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Q1 with outside knowledge.** Comprehension marks come from the source only.
- **Generic OPVL.** "It is biased" earns little; say *how* the purpose affects value for the stated topic.
- **End-on comparison in Q3.** Describing Source A then Source B is juxtaposition; examiners reward explicit linkage.
- **Q4 without own knowledge.** The top band requires both sources and own knowledge woven into an argument.
- **Running out of time.** Q4 carries more than a third of the marks.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many sources are there on Paper 1?**
Four sources per prescribed subject, usually including at least one visual source, set on one of its two case studies.

**Do I need historians for Paper 1?**
Sources may include extracts from historians, which you must evaluate like any other source. You are not required to quote historians from memory.

**Is Paper 1 the same at SL and HL?**
Yes: the same paper and the same 24 marks, but weighted 30% at SL and 20% at HL.

## Self-test

1. Which case studies make up PS3? *Japanese expansion 1931-41; German and Italian expansion 1933-40.*
2. What happened at Cajamarca in 1532? *Pizarro captured Atahualpa.*
3. What was the Freedom Charter? *The 1955 Congress of the People's statement of a non-racial South Africa.*
4. When did NATO bomb Yugoslavia over Kosovo? *March-June 1999.*
5. How many marks is Question 3? *6.*
