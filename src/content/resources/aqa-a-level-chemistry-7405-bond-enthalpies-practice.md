These questions cover section **3.1.4.4 Bond enthalpies** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Bond enthalpies study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bond-enthalpies/) and the [Bond enthalpies revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-bond-enthalpies-revision-notes/). Mean bond enthalpies (kJ mol⁻¹): C–H 413, C–C 347, C=C 612, C–O 358, C=O 805, O–H 464, O=O 498, H–H 436, N–H 391, N≡N 945, Cl–Cl 243, H–Cl 432, C–Cl 346.

## Questions

**1.** Define mean bond enthalpy. **[3]**

**2.** State whether each process is exothermic or endothermic.

**(a)** Breaking a bond **[1]**

**(b)** Making a bond **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction 2H₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2H₂O(g). **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction C₂H₄(g) + H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g). **[3]**

**5.** Calculate the enthalpy change for the addition of hydrogen chloride to ethene, C₂H₄(g) + HCl(g) → C₂H₅Cl(g). **[3]**

**6.** Propane burns in oxygen to give carbon dioxide and steam: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(g). Propane contains 8 C–H bonds and 2 C–C bonds.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy needed to break the bonds in the reactants. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total energy released when the bonds in the products form. **[2]**

**(c)** Hence calculate the enthalpy change for the reaction. **[1]**

**7.** The enthalpy change for N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g) is −92 kJ mol⁻¹. Use the data to calculate the mean bond enthalpy of an N–H bond. **[3]**

**8.** For CH₄(g) + Cl₂(g) → CH₃Cl(g) + HCl(g), ΔH = −122 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the mean bond enthalpy of the C–Cl bond. **[3]**

**9.** The standard enthalpy of combustion of methane, found using enthalpies of formation, is −890 kJ mol⁻¹ and produces liquid water. A bond enthalpy calculation for the same reaction, producing gaseous water, gives −818 kJ mol⁻¹. Give two reasons for the difference. **[2]**

**10.** A student calculates the enthalpy change for H₂(g) + Cl₂(g) → 2HCl(g) as +185 kJ mol⁻¹. Explain the error and give the correct value. **[3]**

**11.** The mean bond enthalpy of the C–H bond is 413 kJ mol⁻¹ but the energy needed to break the first C–H bond in methane is 435 kJ mol⁻¹. Explain the difference. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why bond enthalpy calculations are limited to reactions in the gaseous phase. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The enthalpy change when one mole of a given type of bond is broken [1]; in the gaseous state [1]; averaged over a range of different compounds containing that bond [1]. **[3]**

**2. (a)** Endothermic [1]

**(b)** Exothermic [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Bonds broken: 2 × 436 + 498 = 1370 [1]; bonds formed: 4 × 464 = 1856 [1]; ΔH = 1370 − 1856 = **−486 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Bonds broken: C=C 612 + H–H 436 = 1048 [1]; bonds formed: C–C 347 + 2 × C–H (826) = 1173 [1]; ΔH = 1048 − 1173 = **−125 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Bonds broken: C=C 612 + H–Cl 432 = 1044 [1]; bonds formed: C–C 347 + C–H 413 + C–Cl 346 = 1106 [1]; ΔH = 1044 − 1106 = **−62 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** 8 × 413 + 2 × 347 + 5 × 498 [1] = 3304 + 694 + 2490 = **6488 kJ** [1]

**(b)** 6 × 805 + 8 × 464 [1] = 4830 + 3712 = **8542 kJ** [1]

**(c)** ΔH = 6488 − 8542 = **−2054 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[5]**

**7.** Bonds broken: 945 + 3 × 436 = 2253 [1]; bonds formed: 6 × (N–H) = 6x, so 2253 − 6x = −92 [1]; x = 2345 ÷ 6 = **391 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Bonds broken: C–H 413 + Cl–Cl 243 = 656 [1]; bonds formed: C–Cl (x) + H–Cl 432; 656 − (x + 432) = −122 [1]; x = **346 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Bond enthalpies are mean values, which are not exact for the bonds in these molecules [1]; the bond enthalpy calculation gives water as a gas, whereas the other value is for liquid water, so the energy released on condensing the water is not included [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The student has subtracted in the wrong order (bonds formed minus bonds broken) [1]. Bonds broken = 436 + 243 = 679; bonds formed = 2 × 432 = 864 [1]; correct ΔH = 679 − 864 = **−185 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The mean value is an average over different compounds and over every stage of breaking the bonds [1]; the first C–H bond in methane is a specific bond in a specific molecule, and the energies of the successive C–H bonds in methane are different from each other [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Bond enthalpies are defined for bonds broken in the gas phase, with no intermolecular forces [1]; in liquids or solids, extra energy is needed to separate the particles, and this is not included in the bond enthalpy values [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting in the wrong order.
- Not multiplying by the number of bonds, particularly for two moles of product.
- Missing bonds in a displayed formula.
- Leaving out "gaseous" or "mean" from the definition.
- Forgetting that the state of water affects the comparison with Hess's law values.
