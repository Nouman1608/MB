Cambridge A Level World History (9489, 2027-2029 syllabus, first assessed in 2027) is assessed through four externally assessed written papers: Paper 1 Historical Sources (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20% of the A Level), Paper 2 Outline Study (1 hour 45 minutes, 60 marks, 30%), Paper 3 Historical Interpretations (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) and Paper 4 Depth Study (1 hour 45 minutes, 60 marks, 30%). This page turns that structure into a preparation plan, and works best alongside the topic pages on [Modern Europe, 1774-1924](/resources/a-level-world-history-modern-europe-1750-1921/), [the history of the USA, 1820-1941](/resources/a-level-world-history-usa-1820-1941/) and [International history, 1870-1939](/resources/a-level-world-history-international-1870-1945/).

## How the four papers fit together

The papers form two halves. Papers 1 and 2 make up the AS Level: Paper 1 is 40% of the AS and Paper 2 is 60% of the AS. Papers 3 and 4 complete the full A Level, and they count only towards the A Level. Because the full A Level weightings are 20 + 30 + 20 + 30 = 100%, the two shorter papers are each worth two-thirds of a longer paper. The route matters for planning: a candidate may select any of the three Paper 3 and 4 options independently of the Paper 1 and 2 choice, so do not assume your AS option dictates your A2 reading.

## Paper 1: Historical Sources (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20%)

Paper 1 is the source-based paper. The title in the syllabus is Historical Sources; it was called Document question under the 2026 syllabus (697368), which remains current for the November 2026 series only, so be careful with older past-paper labels. At 75 minutes for 40 marks you have 1.875 minutes per mark, which leaves real thinking time for reading the material. Practise reading each source for provenance, purpose and what it can and cannot show, then comparing sources rather than describing them one after another.

## Paper 2: Outline Study (1 hour 45 minutes, 60 marks, 30%)

Paper 2 is the larger AS paper and tests the breadth of the outline study you have taught. The syllabus rotates topics for Papers 1 and 2, and that rotation table is headed "Topics assessed on Paper 1 and Paper 2", so check which topics are in play for your examination series before narrowing your revision. At 105 minutes for 60 marks you have 1.75 minutes per mark. Build timelines and thematic summaries for the whole period: for example, a pass through [France, 1774-1814](/resources/a-level-world-history-france-1774-1814-study-guide/) and [Germany, 1815-71](/resources/a-level-world-history-germany-1815-71-study-guide/) is more useful when you can link them to Europe-wide change.

## Paper 3: Historical Interpretations (1 hour 15 minutes, 40 marks, 20%)

Paper 3 is A Level only and was renamed from Interpretations question under the 2026 syllabus. It asks you to engage with how historians have interpreted a past event or development. The timing is the same as Paper 1: 75 minutes for 40 marks, or 1.875 minutes per mark. Revision here is about argument, not extra facts: practise stating what each interpretation claims, what evidence it leans on, and how you would weigh it against another.

## Paper 4: Depth Study (1 hour 45 minutes, 60 marks, 30%)

Paper 4 is A Level only. Questions are set on the whole of the chosen option, with no topic rotation prescribed, so revise the whole option and not a predicted selection. Under this syllabus the A Level topics for Paper 4 are reduced from four to three. At 105 minutes for 60 marks you again have 1.75 minutes per mark, which suits extended, sustained writing. Where a depth study overlaps with a topic page such as [Russia from autocracy to revolution, 1881-1924](/resources/a-level-world-history-russia-1881-1924-study-guide/), use the study guide to build a detailed knowledge base, then practise turning it into a line of argument.

## Timing and marks per minute

Work the arithmetic before exam day and write your own timing plan on a revision card:

- Paper 1: 75 minutes / 40 marks = 1.875 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 105 minutes / 60 marks = 1.75 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 75 minutes / 40 marks = 1.875 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 105 minutes / 60 marks = 1.75 minutes per mark.
- All four papers together: 75 + 105 + 75 + 105 = 360 minutes for 40 + 60 + 40 + 60 = 200 marks.

A 20-mark answer on a 1.75 minutes-per-mark paper therefore deserves roughly 35 minutes in total, including planning. Keep five minutes at the end of each paper for checking.

## Question styles and command words

In history, the command word tells you what shape of answer to build. "Compare" asks for similarities and differences, supported from the material. "Assess", "evaluate" and "how far do you agree" ask for a judgement that you argue and sustain, usually by weighing factors against each other. "Explain" asks for reasons with developed links, not a list. Whatever the word, a clear line of argument in the opening sentences, then evidence that serves the argument, scores better than narrative. Check the current syllabus for the exact command-word definitions Cambridge uses.

## Worked practice scenario

Original question: "'Industrial growth was the main cause of social conflict in the USA between 1870 and 1920.' How far do you agree?"

Outline plan:

1. Introduction: state the judgement. Industrial growth was a major cause of conflict, but not the sole one, because political and cultural tensions also mattered.
2. Paragraph 1, for the view: factory work, long hours and wage disputes produced strikes such as Homestead (1892) and Pullman (1894).
3. Paragraph 2, complicating factor: immigration and urban crowding created social friction that was related to industry but not reducible to it.
4. Paragraph 3, other causes: political corruption and reform movements show that conflict also arose from disagreement about how government should respond.
5. Conclusion: weigh the factors and commit to a final judgement, explaining why industry was the underlying condition rather than the only cause.

Model paragraph: "Industrial growth created the conditions in which conflict became likely, because it concentrated large numbers of workers under a few powerful employers. The Pullman strike of 1894 shows this clearly: a dispute over wages and rents in a company town escalated into a national confrontation. Yet to call industry the main cause overlooks that the same period produced reform movements responding to political corruption and urban poverty. Industry therefore explains why many grievances existed, but not why they took the forms that they did." The paragraph states a claim, supports it with a specific example, then qualifies it. Pair this approach with the [Gilded Age and Progressive Era study guide](/resources/a-level-world-history-gilded-age-progressive-era-study-guide/).

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are sitting the AS Level (Papers 1 and 2) or the full A Level (all four papers).
- Check which topics are assessed on Papers 1 and 2 in your series using the syllabus rotation table.
- Revise the whole of your chosen option for Paper 4, not a predicted selection.
- Practise timed source analysis on 75-minute conditions.
- Practise at least one timed essay at 1.75 minutes per mark.
- On the day, underline the command word and the date range in every question.
- Plan every essay in two or three minutes before writing.
- Leave five minutes at the end to check names, dates and that every part is answered.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level World History 9489?**
There are four externally assessed written papers: Paper 1 Historical Sources, Paper 2 Outline Study, Paper 3 Historical Interpretations and Paper 4 Depth Study.

**How long is each paper and how many marks does it carry?**
Papers 1 and 3 are 1 hour 15 minutes and 40 marks each, and Papers 2 and 4 are 1 hour 45 minutes and 60 marks each.

**What are the A Level weightings?**
Paper 1 is 20%, Paper 2 is 30%, Paper 3 is 20% and Paper 4 is 30% of the A Level. On the AS-only route, Paper 1 is 40% and Paper 2 is 60% of the AS Level.

**Can I take the AS Level on its own?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 can be a standalone AS Level or the first half of the full A Level, completed by adding Papers 3 and 4. Candidates may select any of the three Paper 3 and 4 options independent of their Paper 1 and 2 choice.

**Do the topics rotate on every paper?**
No. The rotation table is headed "Topics assessed on Paper 1 and Paper 2" and covers those two papers only. Paper 4 questions are set on the whole of the chosen option with no rotation prescribed.
