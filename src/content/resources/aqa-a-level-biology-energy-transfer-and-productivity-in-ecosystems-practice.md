Learn the ideas first from the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-energy-transfer-and-productivity-in-ecosystems/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-energy-transfer-and-productivity-in-ecosystems-revision-notes/). Use 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ for the specific heat capacity of water, and 1 ha = 10 000 m². Other values are given in the question. To check your starting level, try the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

## Questions

**1.** Define gross primary production (GPP) and net primary production (NPP), and state the equation that links them. **[3]**

**2.** A student collects 340 g of fresh grass from a 2.0 m² area. After drying, the grass has a mass of 91.8 g.

**(a)** Describe how the student should dry the grass so that all the water is removed without burning the tissue. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the dry mass per m². **[1]**
**(c)** Give this value in kg ha⁻¹. **[1]**

**3.** A sample of dried plant tissue of mass 0.75 g is burned under 40.0 g of water. The water temperature rises from 19.5 °C to 47.5 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred to the water, in J, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the energy per gram of tissue, in kJ g⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** The true energy content of the tissue is higher than your answer. Suggest one reason. **[1]**

**4.** A lake has gross primary production of 14 600 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹. Respiratory loss by the producers is 5 110 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the net primary production. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the respiratory loss as a percentage of GPP. **[1]**
**(c)** Express the net primary production in kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹. **[1]**
**(d)** State what happens to the net primary production in the lake. **[1]**

**5.** A herd of sheep ingests 6.2 × 10⁶ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ of food energy. 2.1 × 10⁶ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ is lost in faeces and urine. Their net production is 8.8 × 10⁵ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the energy lost through respiration. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net production as a percentage of the energy ingested. **[1]**
**(c)** State what is meant by secondary productivity. **[1]**

**6.** In a meadow, net primary production is 8.40 × 10⁶ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹. Primary consumers have net production of 6.72 × 10⁵ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ and secondary consumers have 5.04 × 10⁴ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹.

**(a)** Work out what percentage of the energy in the producers' net production is stored in the primary consumers, and what percentage of that is then stored in the secondary consumers. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the overall efficiency from producers to secondary consumers. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the overall efficiency is lower than either single transfer. **[1]**
**(d)** State two ways in which energy leaves a consumer's food intake without becoming new consumer biomass. **[1]**

**7.** Calves are reared for meat in two barns. In barn A they are kept in small pens. In barn B they are kept in large pens with room to run. Data are energy per calf over the rearing period, in MJ.

| Barn | Ingested (I) | Lost in faeces and urine (F) | Lost in respiration (R) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 118 | 31.0 | 61.5 |
| B | 131 | 33.0 | 83.7 |

**(a)** Calculate the net production of a calf in each barn. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net production as a percentage of ingested energy in each barn. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between the barns. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how farmers can increase the efficiency of energy transfer to humans by simplifying food webs and by reducing respiratory losses in livestock. Give one disadvantage of either approach. **[6]**

**9.** A wheat field receives 3.2 × 10¹⁰ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ of light energy. Its GPP is 4.1 × 10⁸ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ and its NPP is 2.9 × 10⁸ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹. The energy in the harvested grain is 1.16 × 10⁸ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate GPP as a percentage of the light energy received. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the respiratory loss. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the grain as a percentage of NPP. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest what happens to the rest of the NPP. **[2]**

**10.** The table shows productivity in a mature forest and in a managed crop. Both are in kJ m⁻² year⁻¹.

| Ecosystem | GPP | Respiratory loss |
|---|---|---|
| Forest | 38 500 | 21 600 |
| Crop | 29 400 | 9 800 |

**(a)** Calculate the net primary production of each ecosystem. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the respiratory loss as a percentage of GPP for each. **[2]**
**(c)** The forest has the higher GPP but the lower NPP. Explain this, using the data. **[3]**

**11.** The dry biomass of a woodland is 12 400 kg ha⁻¹. Carbon makes up 46% of dry mass in this tissue.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of carbon in kg ha⁻¹. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon, in tonnes, in a 3.0 ha wood (1 tonne = 1 000 kg). **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why biomass is measured as dry mass, or as mass of carbon, and not as fresh mass. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** GPP is the chemical energy store in plant biomass, in a given area or volume [1]. NPP is the chemical energy store in plant biomass after respiratory losses to the environment have been taken into account [1]. NPP = GPP - R, where R is respiratory loss [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State "after respiratory losses"; "what is left" alone is vague.

**2. (a)** Heat in an oven at a temperature low enough not to burn the tissue [1]. Re-weigh at intervals until the mass stops changing (constant mass) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 91.8 ÷ 2.0 = **45.9 g m⁻²** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 45.9 × 10 000 = 459 000 g ha⁻¹ = **459 kg ha⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Dry it" alone says little; the repeat weighing to constant mass is the point that shows how you know it is dry.

**3. (a)** Rise = 47.5 - 19.5 = 28.0 °C [1]. Energy = 40.0 × 4.18 × 28.0 = 4 681.6 J, so **4 680 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 4 681.6 ÷ 0.75 = 6 242 J g⁻¹ = **6.24 kJ g⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Heat was lost to the surroundings or apparatus, or combustion was incomplete [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the J to kJ conversion; writing the unit at each step makes a slip of 1 000 easy to spot.

**4. (a)** NPP = 14 600 - 5 110 = **9 490 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 5 110 ÷ 14 600 × 100 = **35.0%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 9 490 × 10 000 = **9.49 × 10⁷ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** It is available for plant growth and reproduction, and for herbivores and decomposers [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and the unit so that your working can be followed.

**5. (a)** R = I - F - N = 6.2 × 10⁶ - 2.1 × 10⁶ [1] - 8.8 × 10⁵ = **3.22 × 10⁶ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 8.8 × 10⁵ ÷ 6.2 × 10⁶ × 100 = **14.2%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The rate of secondary production, that is biomass of consumers in a given area in a given time, for example kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹ [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* When R is unknown, rearrange first, then substitute.

**6. (a)** 6.72 × 10⁵ ÷ 8.40 × 10⁶ × 100 = **8.0%** [1]; 5.04 × 10⁴ ÷ 6.72 × 10⁵ × 100 = **7.5%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 5.04 × 10⁴ ÷ 8.40 × 10⁶ × 100 = **0.60%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Energy is lost at each transfer, so the losses compound [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Any two of: lost in faeces and urine; lost as heat in respiration [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the level below, not the level above. A result above 100% is a signal that the fraction is upside down.

**7. (a)** A: 118 - (31.0 + 61.5) = **25.5 MJ** [1]. B: 131 - (33.0 + 83.7) = **14.3 MJ** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A: 25.5 ÷ 118 × 100 = **21.6%** [1]. B: 14.3 ÷ 131 × 100 = **10.9%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Calves in barn A move less, so respiratory loss is a smaller share of intake (52.1% against 63.9%) [1]. More of the food energy is stored in biomass, so net production is a higher percentage of intake [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Back the comparison with numbers from the data; "A is better" on its own gains little.

**8.** Simplifying food webs: herbicides kill weeds that compete with the crop [1]. Pesticides kill pests that eat the crop [1]. Less energy is lost to non-human food chains, so more NPP is stored in crops or livestock feed [1]. Reducing respiratory losses: restricting movement or keeping animals warm reduces respiration [1]. R is smaller, so N is larger and more energy goes into growth [1]. Disadvantages, for example: pesticides may harm non-target species or reduce biodiversity; confinement reduces animal welfare [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the energy loss that each practice reduces. "More efficient" with no loss identified is a common weakness.

**9. (a)** 4.1 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.2 × 10¹⁰ × 100 = **1.28%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** R = GPP - NPP = 4.1 × 10⁸ - 2.9 × 10⁸ = **1.2 × 10⁸ kJ ha⁻¹ year⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 1.16 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.9 × 10⁸ × 100 = **40.0%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** It remains in parts that are not harvested, such as stems, leaves and roots [1]. Some passes to decomposers or to pests [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check what the denominator is: light, GPP and NPP give three different percentages.

**10. (a)** Forest: 38 500 - 21 600 = **16 900 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹** [1]. Crop: 29 400 - 9 800 = **19 600 kJ m⁻² year⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Forest: 21 600 ÷ 38 500 × 100 = **56.1%** [1]. Crop: 9 800 ÷ 29 400 × 100 = **33.3%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** NPP = GPP - R [1]. The forest loses 56.1% of its GPP in respiration against 33.3% in the crop [1]. The forest's R is more than twice the crop's (21 600 against 9 800), which outweighs its GPP advantage of 9 100 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures and a comparison, not just "one is bigger".

**11. (a)** 12 400 × 0.46 = **5 704 kg ha⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 5 704 × 3.0 = 17 112 kg = **17.1 tonnes** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Fresh mass includes water, whose amount varies with conditions [1]. Dry mass or carbon measures the biological molecules that make up the biomass [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say why water is a problem: its amount varies. "Inaccurate" is too vague.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting R from N = I - (F + R), or treating F as the only loss.
- Mixing up GPP and NPP, or adding R when finding NPP.
- Giving a rate with no time unit.
- Mixing m² and ha in one calculation.
- Dividing by the wrong trophic level in an efficiency.
- Writing "energy is lost" without saying how.
- Describing farming practices with no link to the energy loss that they reduce.
- Not stating constant mass when describing how to dry a sample.
