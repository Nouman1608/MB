These questions cover sections 3.2.3 (Equilibrium) and 3.2.4 (Density and pressure) of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Use g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ and 1000 kg m⁻³ for the density of fresh water. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-moments-equilibrium-density-and-pressure/) or [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-moments-equilibrium-density-and-pressure-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** (a) State what is meant by the moment of a force. **[1]**
**(b)** A force of 36 N pushes at right angles to a gate, 0.45 m from its hinge. Calculate the moment about the hinge. **[2]**

**2.** A tap is turned by a finger and thumb exerting forces of 7.5 N in opposite directions on opposite sides of the head. The lines of action are 0.12 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the torque of this couple. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a couple produces rotation but does not accelerate the centre of mass. **[2]**

**3.** A uniform beam is 2.4 m long and weighs 90 N. It is pivoted 0.20 m to the right of its centre. A crate of weight 300 N rests on the beam 0.90 m to the right of the pivot. A counterweight W is placed 1.0 m to the left of the pivot and the beam balances. Calculate W. **[4]**

**4.** Outline an experiment to locate the centre of gravity of an irregular piece of thin plywood, and explain why it works. **[4]**

**5.** A uniform metre rule of mass 0.150 kg is pivoted at the 25.0 cm mark. A mass of 0.400 kg hangs from the 5.0 cm mark. A mass m hangs from the 80.0 cm mark and the rule balances horizontally. Calculate m. **[4]**

**6.** A uniform ladder of length 5.0 m and weight 240 N leans against a smooth vertical wall, at 70° to the horizontal ground. A person of weight 700 N stands 3.0 m along the ladder from its foot.

**(a)** Explain why the friction force at the ground equals the force from the wall. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force from the wall. **[4]**
**(c)** Calculate the size of the resultant force that the ground exerts on the ladder. **[2]**

**7.** A sphere of weight 36 N hangs from a string fixed to a smooth vertical wall. The string makes 20° with the wall and the sphere touches the wall.

**(a)** State the direction of the force from the wall and explain why it takes this direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Use a triangle of forces to calculate the tension in the string and the force from the wall. **[3]**

**8.** A rectangular tank measures 2.5 m × 1.2 m × 0.80 m (length, width, depth) and is full of oil of density 870 kg m⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of oil. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure of the oil on the base of the tank in two ways. **[2]**

**9.** A tracked vehicle of mass 4200 kg has two tracks, each in contact with the ground over 2.8 m × 0.45 m.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure it exerts on level ground. **[3]**
**(b)** A person of mass 55 kg stands on both feet, with a total contact area of 0.036 m². Compare the person's pressure with the vehicle's. **[2]**

**10.** A submersible has a circular window of diameter 0.30 m. It is 120 m below the surface of sea water of density 1030 kg m⁻³. The air pressure inside is atmospheric.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure due to the water at this depth. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resultant force on the window due to the pressure difference across it. **[3]**

**11.** A uniform block has a square base of side 0.15 m and a height of 0.20 m. It floats upright in water with 0.12 m of its height below the surface.

**(a)** Calculate the difference in pressure between the bottom and top faces. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the upthrust and explain, using Archimedes' principle, how it relates to the weight of the block. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the density of the block. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass that must be placed on the block to make it just fully submerged. **[2]**

**12.** An object of volume 7.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³ weighs 4.60 N in air. When fully immersed in a liquid, a force meter reads 3.85 N. Calculate the density of the liquid. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The force times the shortest distance between the pivot and the force's line [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Moment = Fx = 36 × 0.45 [1] = **16.2 N m** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distance from the pivot" alone is too loose; say the distance is perpendicular to the force's line.

**2. (a)** Torque = Fd = 7.5 × 0.12 [1] = **0.90 N m** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The forces are equal and opposite, so the resultant force is zero [1]. So the centre of mass does not accelerate, but the separated lines of action give a torque [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both facts: zero resultant force, and a torque from separated lines of action.

**3.** Take moments about the pivot. The beam's weight acts at its centre, 0.20 m to the left of the pivot, giving 90 × 0.20 = 18 N m anticlockwise [1]. Crate: 300 × 0.90 = 270 N m clockwise [1]. Clockwise = anticlockwise: 270 = 18 + W × 1.0 [1]. W = **252 N** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each moment clockwise or anticlockwise first; the beam's weight is easy to put on the wrong side.

**4.** Make small holes near the edge and hang the plywood freely from a pin through one hole [1]. Hang a weighted string from the same pin and mark where it lies on the plywood [1]. Repeat with at least two other holes [1]. The lines cross at the centre of gravity, because at rest the weight has no moment about the pin, so the centre of gravity must be vertically below it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Hang it up and draw a line" is thin; say it hangs freely, the string is on the same pin, and you repeat.

**5.** Anticlockwise about the pivot: 0.400 g × 0.200 m = 0.0800 g [1]. The rule's weight acts at 50.0 cm, 0.250 m to the right: 0.150 g × 0.250 = 0.0375 g [1]. The mass m is 0.550 m to the right: 0.0800 g = 0.0375 g + 0.550 m g [1]. m = 0.0425 / 0.550 = **0.0773 kg** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* g cancels, so the equation can be written in masses. Do not forget the rule's own weight.

**6. (a)** The wall is smooth, so the only horizontal forces are the wall force and ground friction [1]. The ladder is in equilibrium, so the horizontal forces are equal and opposite [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Moments about the foot of the ladder. Ladder weight: 240 × 2.5 cos 70° = 205.2 N m [1]. Person: 700 × 3.0 cos 70° = 718.2 N m [1]. Wall force N has moment N × 5.0 sin 70° [1]. N = 923.4 / 4.698 = **197 N** [1] **[4]**
**(c)** The vertical force from the ground equals 240 + 700 = 940 N [1]. Resultant = √(940² + 197²) = **960 N** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking moments about the foot removes two unknown forces and keeps the working short.

**7. (a)** Horizontal, away from the wall [1]. The wall is smooth, so there is no friction and the force acts at right angles to the wall [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the closed triangle: weight 36 N vertical, wall force horizontal, tension along the string [1]. The angle between the tension and the vertical is 20°, so T = 36 / cos 20° = **38.3 N** [1]. R = 36 tan 20° = **13.1 N** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw the three forces head to tail so the arrows follow each other round a closed triangle.

**8. (a)** V = 2.5 × 1.2 × 0.80 = 2.4 m³ [1]. m = ρV = 870 × 2.4 = **2088 kg**, about 2.1 × 10³ kg [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Using p = F/A: weight = 2088 × 9.81 = 20 480 N, area = 2.5 × 1.2 = 3.0 m², so p = **6830 Pa** [1]. Using p = hρg: 0.80 × 870 × 9.81 = **6830 Pa**, the same [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both substitutions; two routes agreeing is a strong check.

**9. (a)** Weight = 4200 × 9.81 = 41 200 N [1]. Area = 2 × 2.8 × 0.45 = 2.52 m² [1]. p = 41 200 / 2.52 = **1.63 × 10⁴ Pa** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Person: p = 55 × 9.81 / 0.036 = **1.50 × 10⁴ Pa** [1]. The pressures are similar (the vehicle's is about 9% larger) because its much greater weight acts over a much larger area [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both numbers and a statement linking them to force and area.

**10. (a)** p = hρg = 120 × 1030 × 9.81 [1] = **1.21 × 10⁶ Pa** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Area = π × 0.15² = 0.0707 m² [1]. The atmospheric pressure acts on both sides, so the pressure difference is the pressure due to the water [1]. F = 1.2125 × 10⁶ × 0.0707 = **8.57 × 10⁴ N** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the radius, not the diameter. Atmospheric pressure cancels in a pressure difference.

**11. (a)** Δp = hρg = 0.12 × 1000 × 9.81 [1] = **1180 Pa** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Upthrust = ΔpA = 1177 × 0.15² [1] = **26.5 N**. This equals the weight of the water displaced, and the block floats at rest, so it also equals the block's weight [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Mass = 26.5 / 9.81 = 2.70 kg [1]. Volume = 0.15² × 0.20 = 4.5 × 10⁻³ m³, so ρ = 2.70 / 4.5 × 10⁻³ = **600 kg m⁻³** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Fully submerged upthrust = 1000 × 9.81 × 4.5 × 10⁻³ = 44.1 N, so the extra weight is 44.1 − 26.5 = 17.7 N [1]. Mass = 17.7 / 9.81 = **1.80 kg** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link part (b) to Archimedes in words: floating means the displaced water weighs the same as the block.

**12.** Upthrust = 4.60 − 3.85 = 0.75 N [1]. U = ρ g V, so ρ = 0.75 / (9.81 × 7.0 × 10⁻⁵) [1] = **1.1 × 10³ kg m⁻³** (1092 kg m⁻³) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The upthrust is the loss in apparent weight, not the weight in air.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating that the distance in a moment is perpendicular to the line of action.
- Missing the weight of a beam, plank or ladder.
- Taking moments about different points in one equation.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius for a circular area.
- Leaving atmospheric pressure in when asked for a pressure difference, or out when asked for total pressure.
- Describing the centre of gravity experiment without saying the card hangs freely.
- Quoting N for a torque or moment, or leaving the unit out.
