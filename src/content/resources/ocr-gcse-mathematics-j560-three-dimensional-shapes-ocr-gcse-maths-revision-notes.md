## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Face | A flat surface of a solid |
| Curved surface | A surface that is not flat, as on a cylinder, cone or sphere |
| Edge | A line where two surfaces meet |
| Vertex | A point where edges meet; the apex of a pyramid or cone is a vertex |
| Cross-section | The shape you get by slicing through a solid; constant all the way through a prism |
| Plan | The view from directly above |
| Front elevation | The view from the front |
| Side elevation | The view from a side |
| Isometric paper | Dot or line paper for drawing solids with vertical lines and two sets of lines at 30° to the horizontal |

## The solids

| Solid | Faces or surfaces | Edges | Vertices | Key property |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cube | 6 squares | 12 | 8 | All edges equal |
| Cuboid | 6 rectangles | 12 | 8 | Opposite faces congruent and parallel |
| Triangular prism | 2 triangles, 3 rectangles | 9 | 6 | Constant triangular cross-section |
| Pentagonal prism | 2 pentagons, 5 rectangles | 15 | 10 | Constant pentagonal cross-section |
| Square-based pyramid | 1 square, 4 triangles | 8 | 5 | Triangles meet at the apex |
| Tetrahedron (triangular-based pyramid) | 4 triangles | 6 | 4 | Any face can be the base |
| Cylinder | 2 circles, 1 curved | 2 | 0 | Constant circular cross-section |
| Cone | 1 circle, 1 curved | 1 | 1 | Curved surface meets at the apex |
| Sphere | 1 curved | 0 | 0 | All surface points the same distance from the centre |

## Counting formulae

```
prism, n-sided cross-section:    n + 2 faces    3n edges    2n vertices
pyramid, n-sided base:           n + 1 faces    2n edges    n + 1 vertices
check (flat faces only):         faces + vertices − edges = 2
```

A face count alone does not identify a solid: 8 faces could be a hexagonal prism (18 edges) or a heptagonal pyramid (14 edges).

## Views of common solids

| Solid | Plan | Front | Side |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cylinder (upright) | Circle | Rectangle | Rectangle |
| Cone (upright) | Circle with centre dot | Isosceles triangle | Isosceles triangle |
| Square-based pyramid | Square with diagonals | Triangle | Triangle |
| Triangular prism (lying, triangle facing you) | Rectangle | Triangle | Rectangle |
| Sphere | Circle | Circle | Circle |
| Hemisphere (flat face down) | Circle | Semicircle | Semicircle |

## Drawing views

```
1. Decide which direction each view is from.
2. Use the two dimensions you can see from that direction.
3. Draw every visible edge, including lines where height or depth changes.
4. Draw to the scale given; use a ruler.
5. Check: shared dimensions match between views.
```

Cuboid 5 cm long, 2 cm deep, 3 cm high: plan 5 × 2, front 5 × 3, side 2 × 3.

**Cube stacks from a plan with heights:** front elevation shows the tallest stack in each column; side elevation shows the tallest stack in each row; viewed from the right, the front of the solid is on your left.

## Isometric drawing

- Vertical edges stay vertical.
- The other edges follow the two 30° directions.
- No horizontal lines.
- Lengths to scale along each direction.
- Draw the nearest corner first, then build back.
- Only visible edges.

## Exam traps

- Pyramid and prism confused, or a solid named only as "prism" without its cross-section.
- Views drawn in perspective instead of flat.
- Missing internal lines where a solid steps up or down.
- Left and right mixed up on a side elevation.
- Isometric paper used sideways, so lines come out horizontal.
- Plan and elevation dimensions that do not agree.

## Quick self-test

1. How many faces, edges and vertices has an octagonal prism?
2. How many faces, edges and vertices has a hexagonal pyramid?
3. A solid has 2 flat circular faces and 1 curved surface. Name it.
4. Which solid has exactly one vertex and one curved surface?
5. A prism has 24 edges. What shape is its cross-section?
6. Describe the plan of an upright cone.
7. A solid has plan a square with both diagonals drawn and front elevation a triangle. Name it.
8. A cuboid is 6 cm long, 4 cm deep and 2 cm high. Give the dimensions of its plan and side elevation.
9. Stack heights, viewed from above with the front at the bottom: back row 1, 3; front row 2, 2. Give the column heights in the front elevation.
10. For the solid in question 9, how many cubes are there?
11. Which lines on isometric paper are drawn vertically?
12. Give two different solids that both have 8 faces.

### Answers

1. 10 faces, 24 edges, 16 vertices
2. 7 faces, 12 edges, 7 vertices
3. Cylinder
4. Cone
5. 3n = 24, so n = 8: an octagon
6. A circle with a dot at the centre (the apex)
7. Square-based pyramid
8. Plan 6 cm by 4 cm; side elevation 4 cm by 2 cm
9. Left column max(1, 2) = 2; right column max(3, 2) = 3
10. 1 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 8
11. The vertical edges of the solid
12. For example a hexagonal prism and a heptagonal pyramid
