## Questions

**1.** Define a trade union. **[2]**

**2.** Identify three matters that a trade union may negotiate through collective bargaining. **[3]**

**3.** A bakery in Country A employs 30 workers at 360 a week each and bakes 9,000 loaves a week. The union wins a 5% pay rise.

**(a)** Calculate the new weekly wage bill and the increase in the bill. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the labour cost per loaf before and after the rise. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a trade union may be weaker when unemployment is high. **[4]**

**5.** A tile maker employs 20 workers and produces 2,000 tiles a day. Demand for tiles rises and output must grow by 25%.

**(a)** Explain why the firm's demand for workers is a derived demand. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many workers are needed if productivity stays the same. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate how many workers are needed if training raises output per worker to 125 tiles. **[2]**

**6.** A workshop can make 400 units a week in two ways. Method X uses 10 workers and equipment costing 600 a week. Method Y uses 2 workers and equipment costing 2,300 a week. Each worker is paid 250 a week.

**(a)** State which method is capital-intensive. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost per unit of each method. **[2]**
**(c)** Wages fall to 200 a week. Calculate the new costs per unit and state which method is now cheaper. **[2]**
**(d)** Give one disadvantage to the firm of the capital-intensive method. **[1]**

**7.** A fishing fleet in Country B lands 500 tonnes with 25 crew in year 1 and 540 tonnes with 30 crew in year 2.

**(a)** Calculate output per crew member in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in production and in productivity. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why productivity might have fallen. **[2]**

**8.** Many bakeries sell similar bread in a town. Explain how the large number of firms is likely to affect price and quality. **[4]**

**9.** One firm sells 63,000 of the 70,000 units of electricity bought on a small island each month.

**(a)** State two characteristics of a monopoly. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the firm's market share. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one disadvantage of this market structure for consumers. **[2]**

**10.** A firm in Country C has 40 workers paid 300 a week each and produces 1,600 units a week. The union claims a 10% pay rise.

**(a)** Calculate the new wage bill and the new labour cost per unit. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the weekly output needed to keep labour cost per unit at its original level. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the firm should reject the claim. **[4]**

**11.** The government of a small country is considering allowing one firm to be the only supplier of piped water. Evaluate whether this would benefit the country. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** An organisation of workers [1] that exists to protect or improve members' pay, conditions or job security [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns more by naming both the group (workers) and the purpose; "a group that goes on strike" describes only one tactic.

**2.** Any three of: wages [1]; working hours [1]; working conditions such as safety or holidays [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Three different matters are needed, so "pay" and "wages" count as one idea only.

**3. (a)** New wage: 360 × 1.05 = 378 [1]. New bill: 30 × 378 = 11,340 [1]. Old bill 30 × 360 = 10,800, so the increase is **540** a week [1].
**(b)** Before: 10,800 ÷ 9,000 = **1.20** [1]. After: 11,340 ÷ 9,000 = **1.26** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show each stage of the working and include the units; a bare final figure gives the examiner nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**4.** High unemployment means many people are looking for work [1]. The employer can replace workers who strike or leave [1], so the threat of industrial action is less credible [1]. Members may also accept lower pay to protect their jobs, so the union wins smaller increases [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit by linking each step in a chain, from unemployment to replacement to bargaining power, instead of repeating "the union is weaker".

**5. (a)** Firms hire workers only to produce goods [1], so demand for workers comes from demand for tiles [1].
**(b)** Output 2,000 × 1.25 = 2,500 [1]. At 100 per worker, 2,500 ÷ 100 = **25 workers** [1].
**(c)** 2,500 ÷ 125 = **20 workers** [1], so no extra hiring is needed [1].
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) say what the figure means for the firm; a number with no conclusion is incomplete.

**6. (a)** **Method Y** [1].
**(b)** X: 10 × 250 + 600 = 3,100, so 3,100 ÷ 400 = **7.75** [1]. Y: 2 × 250 + 2,300 = 2,800, so 2,800 ÷ 400 = **7.00** [1].
**(c)** X: 10 × 200 + 600 = 2,600, so **6.50**. Y: 2 × 200 + 2,300 = 2,700, so **6.75** [1]. **X is now cheaper** [1].
**(d)** For example: high set-up cost or borrowing; breakdowns stop output; risky if demand falls [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) a comparison needs both costs per unit and a stated conclusion; give the reason, which is cheaper labour.

**7. (a)** Year 1: 500 ÷ 25 = **20** tonnes [1]. Year 2: 540 ÷ 30 = **18** tonnes [1].
**(b)** Production: 40 ÷ 500 = **+8%** [1]. Productivity: −2 ÷ 20 = **−10%** [1].
**(c)** For example: the five extra crew were less experienced or less trained [1], so each caught less on average [1]. Equally valid: poorer weather or more crew sharing the same boats. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that production and productivity moved in different directions, and name the cause as a link to output per worker.

**8.** Firms compete for customers [1], so none can charge much above its costs without losing sales [1], which holds prices down. To stand out, firms improve quality or service [1], and poor producers lose business [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the number of firms to rivalry before stating the effect on price and quality, since the economic reason is where the marks sit.

**9. (a)** Any two of: one dominant seller [1]; high barriers to entry [1]; few or no close substitutes; able to influence price. **[2]**
**(b)** 63,000 ÷ 70,000 = **90%** [1].
**(c)** With little competition the firm can charge higher prices [1] and consumers have no alternative supplier to choose [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the disadvantage and then explain why it follows from lack of competition; a list of costs without a reason is not enough.

**10. (a)** New wage: 300 × 1.10 = 330 [1]. New bill: 40 × 330 = **13,200** [1]. Per unit: 13,200 ÷ 1,600 = **8.25** [1].
**(b)** Original cost per unit: 12,000 ÷ 1,600 = 7.50 [1]. Output needed: 13,200 ÷ 7.50 = **1,760 units**, a 10% rise [1].
**(c)** Rejecting avoids a 1,200 rise in weekly costs and protects profit [1], but may cause industrial action and lower morale [1]. Accepting is more sensible if productivity can rise 10% [1], so the firm should link the rise to productivity and judge by demand for its product [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh both sides, then end with a conclusion that rests on named conditions, here productivity and demand for the product.

**11.** For: one supplier avoids duplicated pipes [1]; economies of scale may lower costs [1]; profits can fund investment [1]. Against: consumers have no alternative supplier [1]; price may be higher and quality lower with no competition [1]; the firm may be inefficient [1]. Judgement: the benefit depends on whether savings are passed to consumers, which regulation by the government can influence [1], so a regulated monopoly is more likely to benefit the country than an unregulated one [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to be stronger when they weigh both sides and finish with a judgement that names the deciding condition.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a trade union as a group that strikes rather than as an organisation of workers.
- Giving workers' viewpoint only when firms and the government are also asked for.
- Quoting a final figure without the working, or without units.
- Mixing production and productivity.
- Choosing between production methods without calculating cost per unit.
- Describing "monopoly" as simply "big" without barriers to entry or lack of substitutes.
- Stating "competition lowers prices" without explaining why.
- Writing a one-sided evaluation with no judgement.
