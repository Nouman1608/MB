These questions cover subtopic 4.4 Newton's laws of motion of Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 (Paper 4). Take g = 10 m s⁻² and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Strings are light and inextensible and pulleys are smooth.

Related: [Newton's laws study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-newtons-laws-of-motion-cambridge-alevel-maths/) | [Newton's laws revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-newtons-laws-of-motion-cambridge-alevel-maths-revision-notes/)

## Section A

**1.** A particle of mass 4 kg is pulled along a smooth horizontal surface by a horizontal force of 18 N. A resistance of 6 N opposes the motion. Find the acceleration of the particle. **[2]**

**2.** A lift of mass 600 kg is raised and lowered by a vertical cable.

**(a)** Find the tension in the cable when the lift accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s⁻².

**(b)** State the tension when the lift moves downwards at constant speed. **[3]**

**3.** A particle of mass 2 kg is released from rest on a smooth plane inclined at 30° to the horizontal. Find its acceleration and its speed after it has travelled 3 m down the plane. **[3]**

**4.** A box of mass 8 kg is pushed across a rough horizontal floor by a horizontal force of 35 N. The coefficient of friction is 0.25. Find the acceleration of the box. **[2]**

**5.** A car of mass 1000 kg moves along a straight horizontal road. The driving force is 2500 N and the resistance to motion is 700 N. Find the time taken for the car's speed to increase from 5 m s⁻¹ to 14 m s⁻¹. **[3]**

**6.** A particle of mass 0.5 kg is projected vertically upwards at 12 m s⁻¹. A constant air resistance of 1 N acts on it while it rises. Find the deceleration of the particle and the greatest height it reaches above the point of projection. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A crate of mass 6 kg rests on rough horizontal ground. The coefficient of friction is 0.35. A force of 40 N acts on the crate at 25° above the horizontal.

**(a)** Find the normal reaction on the crate.

**(b)** Find the acceleration of the crate.

**(c)** The force is now applied at 25° below the horizontal, pushing down on the crate. Find the new acceleration. **[7]**

**8.** Particles A and B, of masses 0.6 kg and 0.4 kg, are attached to the ends of a string passing over a fixed pulley. The system is released from rest with both parts of the string vertical and taut, and with A 2 m above the floor. B does not reach the pulley.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string.

**(b)** Find the speed of A when it reaches the floor.

**(c)** Find the greatest height of B above its starting position. **[8]**

**9.** Particle A, of mass 4 kg, lies on a rough horizontal table for which the coefficient of friction is 0.2. It is attached by a string passing over a pulley at the edge of the table to particle B, of mass 1.5 kg, which hangs freely 0.8 m above the floor. The system is released from rest. A does not reach the pulley.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

**(b)** Find the total distance travelled by A before it comes to rest. **[8]**

**10.** Particle A, of mass 3 kg, lies on a rough plane inclined at angle α to the horizontal, where sin α = 0.6 and cos α = 0.8. The coefficient of friction between A and the plane is 0.25. A string attached to A passes over a pulley at the top of the plane and carries particle B, of mass 4 kg, hanging freely. The string between A and the pulley is parallel to a line of greatest slope. The system is released from rest and B moves downwards. Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. **[6]**

**11.** A car of mass 1500 kg tows a trailer of mass 500 kg along a straight horizontal road by means of a light rope. The resistances to motion are 400 N on the car and 200 N on the trailer. The tension in the rope is 900 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system and the driving force of the car.

**(b)** The car then brakes sharply and the rope goes slack. Explain why the rope cannot slow the trailer, and find the deceleration of the trailer while the rope is slack. **[6]**

**12.** A particle is projected at 6 m s⁻¹ up a line of greatest slope of a rough plane inclined at 20° to the horizontal. It comes to rest after travelling 2.5 m.

**(a)** Find the deceleration of the particle.

**(b)** Find the coefficient of friction between the particle and the plane.

**(c)** Determine whether the particle remains at rest. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** 18 − 6 = 4a [1]; **a = 3 m s⁻²** [1] **[2]**

**2. (a)** T − 6000 = 600 × 0.5 [1]; **T = 6300 N** [1]

**(b)** Constant speed means zero resultant, so **T = 6000 N** [1] **[3]**

**3.** a = 10 sin 30° = **5 m s⁻²** [1]; v² = 0 + 2 × 5 × 3 = 30 [1]; **v = 5.48 m s⁻¹** [1] **[3]**

**4.** F = 0.25 × 80 = 20 N [1]; 35 − 20 = 8a, **a = 1.875 ≈ 1.88 m s⁻²** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 2500 − 700 = 1000a, a = 1.8 m s⁻² [1]; 14 = 5 + 1.8t [1]; **t = 5 s** [1] **[3]**

**6.** Weight and resistance both act downwards: 5 + 1 = 0.5a [1], deceleration **12 m s⁻²** [1]; 0 = 144 − 24s, **s = 6 m** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the resistance opposes motion, so while the particle rises it acts downwards, adding to the weight.

**7. (a)** R + 40 sin 25° = 60 [1]; **R = 43.1 N** [1]

**(b)** F = 0.35 × 43.10 = 15.08 N [1]; 40 cos 25° − 15.08 = 6a, so 36.25 − 15.08 = 6a [1]; **a = 3.53 m s⁻²** [1]

**(c)** R = 60 + 40 sin 25° = 76.91 N, F = 26.92 N [1]; 36.25 − 26.92 = 6a, **a = 1.56 m s⁻²** [1] **[7]**

**8. (a)** A: 6 − T = 0.6a [1]; B: T − 4 = 0.4a [1]; adding, 2 = a, **a = 2 m s⁻²** [1]; **T = 4.8 N** [1]

**(b)** v² = 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 [1]; **v = 2.83 m s⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** After A lands, B rises freely: extra height = 8 ÷ 20 = 0.4 m [1]; greatest height above start = 2 + 0.4 = **2.4 m** [1] **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* B has already risen 2 m when A lands. Answers of 0.4 m miss the first phase.

**9. (a)** Friction on A = 0.2 × 40 = 8 N [1]; A: T − 8 = 4a; B: 15 − T = 1.5a [1]; 7 = 5.5a, **a = 1.27 m s⁻²** (14/11) [1]; T = 15 − 1.5 × 14/11 = **13.1 N** [1]

**(b)** When B lands, v² = 2 × (14/11) × 0.8 = 2.036 [1]. A then decelerates at μg = 2 m s⁻² [1]; further distance = 2.036 ÷ 4 = 0.509 m [1]; total = 0.8 + 0.509 = **1.31 m** [1] **[8]**

**10.** R = 3 × 10 × 0.8 = 24 N, F = 0.25 × 24 = 6 N [1]; weight component down the plane = 3 × 10 × 0.6 = 18 N [1]; A: T − 18 − 6 = 3a; B: 40 − T = 4a [1]; adding, 16 = 7a [1]; **a = 2.29 m s⁻²** [1]; **T = 30.9 N** [1] **[6]**

**11. (a)** Trailer: 900 − 200 = 500a [1], **a = 1.4 m s⁻²** [1]; whole system: D − 600 = 2000 × 1.4 [1], **D = 3400 N** [1]

**(b)** A rope can only pull, not push, so it cannot exert a backward force on the trailer [1]; deceleration = 200 ÷ 500 = **0.4 m s⁻²** [1] **[6]**

**12. (a)** 0 = 36 − 2a(2.5) [1]; deceleration **7.2 m s⁻²** [1]

**(b)** 10 sin 20° + μ × 10 cos 20° = 7.2 [1]; 3.420 + 9.397μ = 7.2 [1]; **μ = 0.402** [1]

**(c)** Weight component down the plane per kg = 10 sin 20° = 3.42; maximum friction per kg = 0.402 × 10 cos 20° = 3.78 [1]. 3.42 < 3.78, so **the particle remains at rest** [1] **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* equivalently tan 20° = 0.364 < 0.402. A conclusion with no comparison scores nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Normal reaction taken as mg with an angled force or on a slope.
- A single system equation used to find a tension, which cancels out of it.
- The first phase forgotten when finding the greatest height after a string goes slack.
- Friction left pointing the same way after a particle reverses direction.
- g = 9.8 used instead of the expected 10 m s⁻².
