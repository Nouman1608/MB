## Questions

**1.** Sana Holroyd is a sole trader. Her business owes 34,000 to suppliers and the bank, and its assets would sell for 21,500. Explain what unlimited liability means for her. **[3]**

**2.** Classify each as a trading, service, manufacturing or combination business: (a) Lindenhurst Bakes makes loaves and sells them in its own shop; (b) Kamara Tutoring charges fees for lessons; (c) a bookshop that sells second-hand books and charges for rebinding. State which of the three has no cost of sales. **[4]**

**3.** Explain how (a) the statement of profit or loss and (b) the statement of financial position would help Hugo Tavares decide whether to take out a loan. **[4]**

**4.** Calculate: (a) the annual charge and the carrying value after three years for equipment costing 18,000 depreciated at 15% straight line; **[2]** (b) the charge for 2028 on a van bought on 1 January 2026 for 27,000 and depreciated at 30% reducing balance; **[2]** (c) the charge for tools valued at 3,400 at the start, 900 bought, valued at 3,150 at the end. **[1]**

**5.** State the expense or income for the year and the statement of financial position item for: (a) rent paid 7,200, which includes 1,200 for next year; (b) electricity paid 2,640 with 380 owing; (c) commission received 1,900, which includes 450 for next year; (d) bank interest received 340, with 120 earned but not received. **[4]**

**6.** Trade receivables are 14,600 before writing off a debt of 600. The allowance is 560 and is to be 5% of the remaining trade receivables. (a) Calculate the total charged to the statement of profit or loss for these items. **[3]** (b) Present trade receivables as they appear in the statement of financial position. **[2]**

**7.** Hugo Tavares has recorded drawings of 9,600. He also took goods that cost 340 (selling price 520), a computer with carrying value 450, and the business paid his private insurance of 275, charged to insurance. (a) Calculate corrected drawings. **[2]** (b) State the effect on profit. **[2]**

**8.** A draft profit is 12,400. Closing inventory was overstated by 600 and electricity of 350 owing was left out. (a) Calculate the corrected profit. **[2]** (b) State the effect on total assets, liabilities and closing capital. **[2]**

**9.** Dalia Fairchild is a mobile photographer. Year ended 30 June 2028: fees 41,300; travel 4,160; insurance 2,100; studio rent 6,000; telephone 960; assistant's wages 9,600; trade receivables 6,380; allowance for irrecoverable debts 250; camera equipment cost 12,000 with accumulated depreciation 4,800; props valued 700 on 1 July 2027 and 150 bought in the year. Adjustments: fees of 900 earned are not billed; insurance includes 500 for next year; rent of 500 is owing; telephone includes 210 of private calls; a debt of 380 is irrecoverable and the allowance is to be 3% of remaining trade receivables; cameras are depreciated at 25% reducing balance; props are valued at 600. Prepare her statement of profit or loss. **[10]**

**10.** Jonas Stanhope makes garden furniture. Year ended 31 December 2027: raw materials 3,100 opening and 3,600 closing; purchases 24,800; carriage inwards 450; direct wages 18,200; indirect factory wages 4,100; factory power 2,900 with 250 owing; machinery cost 25,000 depreciated at 12% straight line; work in progress 1,400 opening and 1,100 closing; finished goods 5,200 opening and 4,600 closing; revenue 82,500; sales returns 1,500. (a) Prepare a manufacturing account that ends at factory cost of production. **[7]** (b) Calculate cost of sales and gross profit. **[3]**

**11.** Tomas Kamara's balances at 31 December 2028, after all adjustments: land and buildings 70,000; vehicles cost 24,000 with accumulated depreciation 9,600; inventory 6,300; trade receivables 8,200; allowance for irrecoverable debts 410; other receivables 350; bank overdraft 1,900; trade payables 5,100; other payables 480; bank loan repayable 2034 15,000; capital at 1 January 2028 69,460; profit for the year 18,300; drawings 11,400. Prepare his statement of financial position. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Her business and she are the same legal person, so she is personally responsible for its debts [1]. The shortfall is 34,000 - 21,500 = 12,500 [1], which suppliers could recover from her private assets such as her home or car [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the figures from the question turns a definition into an explanation, which is what a "explain" command asks for.

**2.** (a) Manufacturing [1]. (b) Service [1]. (c) Combination [1]. Kamara Tutoring has no cost of sales [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the type, then use the last part to show what it means for the statement of profit or loss.

**3.** (a) It shows the profit made over the year [1], so he can judge whether profit is enough to cover loan interest and repayments [1]. (b) It shows what the business owns and owes on the year-end date [1], including existing borrowing and whether assets exceed liabilities, so he can judge the risk of adding a loan [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each statement needs its own named use. Saying both "show how the business is doing" gains little.

**4.** (a) 18,000 x 15% = 2,700 a year [1]; 18,000 - 3 x 2,700 = 9,900 [1]. (b) 2026: 27,000 x 30% = 8,100; 2027: 18,900 x 30% = 5,670 [1]; 2028: 13,230 x 30% = **3,969** [1]. (c) 3,400 + 900 - 3,150 = **1,150** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Reducing balance is a common place to slip by using cost each year. Writing each year's carrying value lets an examiner follow your method.

**5.** (a) Rent expense 6,000; other receivables 1,200 [1]. (b) Electricity expense 3,020; other payables 380 [1]. (c) Commission income 1,450; other payables 450 [1]. (d) Interest income 460; other receivables 120 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the statement of financial position heading, not just the amount, is part of what the question asks.

**6.** (a) Remaining 14,600 - 600 = 14,000 [1]; allowance 5% x 14,000 = 700, an increase of 140 [1]; total 600 + 140 = **740** [1]. (b) Trade receivables 14,000 [1] less allowance 700 = **13,300** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The statement of profit or loss takes only the change in the allowance, while the statement of financial position deducts all of it.

**7.** (a) Additional drawings 340 + 450 + 275 = 1,065 [1]; corrected drawings 9,600 + 1,065 = **10,665** [1]. (b) Purchases fall by 340 and insurance by 275 [1], so profit rises by **615**; the computer has no effect on profit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Goods taken for own use are valued at cost, so the selling price of 520 is a distractor.

**8.** (a) 12,400 - 600 = 11,800 [1]; 11,800 - 350 = **11,450** [1]. (b) Total assets fall by 600 and liabilities rise by 350 [1]; closing capital falls by 950, the fall in profit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say whether each balance rises or falls. A bare number with no direction is unclear.

**9.**
```
Dalia Fairchild: statement of profit or loss, year ended 30 June 2028
Fees (41,300 + 900)                      42,200  [1]
Add Decrease in allowance (250 - 180)        70  [1]
                                         42,270
Less Expenses
 Travel 4,160; wages 9,600                         [1]
 Insurance (2,100 - 500)                 1,600  [1]
 Studio rent (6,000 + 500)               6,500  [1]
 Telephone (960 - 210)                     750  [1]

Profit for the year                      17,230  [1]
```
Allowance: remaining receivables 6,000; 3% = 180. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the adjustment inside each expense line, as here, so a reader can see which adjustments you made even if the total is wrong.

**10.** (a) Raw materials used 3,100 + 24,800 + 450 - 3,600 = 24,750 [1]; prime cost 24,750 + 18,200 = 42,950 [1]; power 2,900 + 250 = 3,150 [1]; depreciation 12% x 25,000 = 3,000 [1]; overheads 3,150 + 3,000 + 4,100 = 10,250 [1]; add opening and deduct closing work in progress: 42,950 + 10,250 + 1,400 - 1,100 [1] = **53,500** [1]. (b) Net revenue 82,500 - 1,500 = 81,000 [1]; cost of sales 5,200 + 53,500 - 4,600 = **54,100** [1]; gross profit **26,900** [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Working in the order raw materials, prime cost, overheads and work in progress keeps each subtotal visible.

**11.** Non-current assets: vehicles 24,000 - 9,600 = 14,400 [1]; total 70,000 + 14,400 = 84,400 [1]. Current assets: trade receivables 8,200 - 410 = 7,790 [1]; total 6,300 + 7,790 + 350 = 14,440 [1]; total assets **98,840** [1]. Capital: 69,460 + 18,300 - 11,400 [1] = **76,360** [1]. Non-current liability: loan 15,000 [1]. Current liabilities: 5,100 + 480 + 1,900 = 7,480 [1]. Total 76,360 + 15,000 + 7,480 = 98,840. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat a bank overdraft as a current liability. A matching total is a useful check on your layout.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "unlimited liability" without saying what it means for the owner.
- Using cost, not carrying value, for reducing balance.
- Treating a prepayment as an addition to the expense.
- Charging the whole allowance, not the change, to the statement of profit or loss.
- Valuing goods taken at selling price.
- Showing no workings for adjusted lines.
- Placing a bank overdraft among current assets.
- Leaving out opening or closing work in progress.
