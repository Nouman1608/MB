Cambridge International AS and A Level History (9489) is a two-stage
course in which students choose between European, American and
International pathways and then extend their study with historical
interpretations and a depth study. It is taken by students who want to
work with sources, construct sustained arguments and weigh up how
historians disagree. The full A Level is made up of four externally
assessed papers: Paper 1 Historical Sources and Paper 3 Historical
Interpretations are each worth 20%, and Paper 2 Outline Study and
Paper 4 Depth Study are each worth 30%. This overview describes the
2027-2029 syllabus, first assessed in 2027.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged as three parallel options at each stage, plus
a separate interpretations topic at A Level.

**AS Level options (Papers 1 and 2)**

- **European option: Modern Europe, 1774–1924** covers France,
  1774–1814; liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815–71; and Russia
  from autocracy to revolution, 1881–1924.
- **American option: The history of the USA, 1820–1941** covers the
  Civil War and Reconstruction, 1820–77; the Gilded Age and the
  Progressive Era, 1870–1920; and the Great Crash, the Great Depression
  and the New Deal, 1920–41.
- **International option: International history, 1870–1939** covers
  imperialism and the emergence of world powers, c.1870–1918;
  international relations 1919–29; and international history 1929–39,
  the rise of extremism and the road to war.

**A Level content (Papers 3 and 4)**

- **Historical interpretations** is built around the origins of the
  First World War, the Holocaust, and the origins and development of
  the Cold War.
- **European option: European history, 1919–41** studies Mussolini's
  Italy, Stalin's Russia and Hitler's Germany.
- **American option: The USA, 1945–93** runs from Truman and
  Eisenhower through the challenges of 1961–74 to the USA, 1974–93.
- **International option: International history, 1909–94** covers the
  Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe, the end of minority rule in South
  Africa and Zimbabwe, and the route to independence in Malaysia and
  Indonesia.

Your school chooses which options it teaches, so check which pathway
you are following before you begin.

## How it's assessed

All four papers are written examinations, externally assessed.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (A Level) | Weighting (AS) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Historical Sources | 1 hour 15 minutes | 40 | 20% | 40% |
| Paper 2 | Outline Study | 1 hour 45 minutes | 60 | 30% | 60% |
| Paper 3 | Historical Interpretations | 1 hour 15 minutes | 40 | 20% | – |
| Paper 4 | Depth Study | 1 hour 45 minutes | 60 | 30% | – |

The course is staged. Papers 1 and 2 make up the AS Level, which can
be taken as a qualification in its own right or as the first half of
the full A Level. Adding Papers 3 and 4 completes the A Level, and the
syllabus allows candidates to choose their Paper 3 and 4 option
independently of the option they took for Papers 1 and 2.

Three changes in this syllabus are worth knowing:

- Paper 1 is now called Historical Sources (previously Document
  question) and Paper 3 is now Historical Interpretations (previously
  Interpretations question). The 2026 syllabus remains in use for the
  November 2026 series only.
- The number of topics in each AS option, and in each A Level depth
  study option, has been reduced from four to three.
- A rotation table in the syllabus sets which topics are assessed on
  Papers 1 and 2 in each series. Paper 4 questions can be set on the
  whole of the chosen option, with no rotation.

## How the parts fit together

The four papers test different historical skills, and the course is
designed so those skills build on one another.

Paper 1 is about working with evidence: reading sources critically,
comparing them and using them to reach a judgement. Paper 2 asks for
broader explanation and argument across a period. Together they give
the AS Level its balance of source analysis and essay writing.

At A Level, Paper 3 turns the focus from the past itself to how
historians have explained it. The habits learned in Paper 1, such as
asking who produced a piece of evidence and why, transfer directly to
evaluating historians' arguments. Paper 4 then asks for the most
detailed knowledge and the most sustained argument of the course,
building on the essay skills first practised for Paper 2.

There are also clear content links. Students who take the International
option at AS, which opens with imperialism and the emergence of world
powers up to 1918, meet some of the same background when studying the
origins of the First World War for Paper 3. European interwar history at A Level follows on chronologically
from the AS European option. These links are helpful, though the
syllabus does not require you to keep the same option at both stages.

## Planning your study

Because the rotation table decides which topics appear on Papers 1 and
2 in a given series, check with your teacher which topics are assessed
in your exam year before planning the AS stage in detail.

A typical route through the course looks like this:

1. **First year:** study the three topics in your AS option, building
   chronological knowledge alongside regular source work. Practise short
   source comparisons early, then longer outline essays once a topic is
   complete.
2. **Second year:** begin the historical interpretations topic, reading
   how historians have argued about it, and work through your depth
   study option in detail. Since Paper 4 can draw on the whole option,
   aim for even coverage rather than favouring one section.
3. **Final months:** bring all papers together with timed writing and
   review.

Start full past papers once each stage's content is complete, and use
the exam-preparation page for the paper-by-paper approach to timing and
question styles.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Cambridge A Level History 9489?**
There are four written papers. Papers 1 and 2 make up the AS Level, and Papers 3 and 4 complete the full A Level. All four are externally assessed.

**Can I take Cambridge AS Level History on its own?**
Yes. Papers 1 and 2 can be taken as a standalone AS Level, where Paper 1 is worth 40% and Paper 2 is worth 60%, or as the first half of the full A Level.

**Do I have to study the same option at AS and A Level?**
No. The syllabus allows candidates to choose their Paper 3 and 4 option independently of their Paper 1 and 2 choice.

**What has changed in the 2027-2029 syllabus?**
Paper 1 is renamed Historical Sources and Paper 3 is renamed Historical Interpretations, and the number of topics in each AS option and A Level depth study option falls from four to three. The 2026 syllabus remains for the November 2026 series only.

**Are all topics examined every year on Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
No. The syllabus includes a rotation table that sets which topics are assessed on Papers 1 and 2. Paper 4 can be set on the whole of the chosen option.
