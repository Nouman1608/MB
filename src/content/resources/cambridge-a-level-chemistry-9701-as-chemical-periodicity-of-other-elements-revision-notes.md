These notes condense subtopic **9.3, Chemical periodicity of other elements** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Worked examples are in the [9.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-chemical-periodicity-of-other-elements/). The [Periodicity Across Period 3 revision notes](/resources/as-chem-periodicity-revision-notes/) summarise the patterns you apply here.

## The two question types

- **Predict**: you are given the group (and often the period) and must predict properties.
- **Deduce**: you are given properties and must find the nature, position and identity of the element.

## Evidence-to-conclusion table

| Evidence | Conclusion |
|---|---|
| Chloride MCl, oxide M₂O | Group 1 (oxidation number +1) |
| Chloride MCl₂, oxide MO | Group 2 (+2) |
| Chloride MCl₃, oxide M₂O₃ | Group 13 (+3) |
| Chloride XCl₄, oxide XO₂ | Group 14 (+4) |
| Large jump after the nth ionisation energy | n outer electrons → group with n outer electrons (Groups 1, 2; 13 to 17 for n = 3 to 7) |
| Oxide gives alkaline solution; reacts with acid only | Metal, left of period; basic oxide |
| Oxide insoluble; reacts with acid **and** NaOH | Boundary; amphoteric (like Al₂O₃) |
| Oxide gives acidic solution; reacts with NaOH | Non-metal, right of period; acidic oxide |
| Chloride high melting point, neutral solution | Ionic chloride; metal |
| Chloride liquid, fumes in moist air, acidic solution | Covalent chloride; non-metal |
| Chloride solution about pH 3 | Small, highly charged cation (like Al³⁺) |
| Very high melting point, insoluble, non-conducting | Giant covalent (like Si or SiO₂) |
| Reacts with water more vigorously than a known member of its group | Lower in the group |

## Predicting: five steps

1. Outer electrons and highest oxidation number.
2. Formulas of oxide and chloride.
3. Metal or non-metal (position across the period).
4. Adjust for position down the group: lower = more metallic, more reactive metal, less acidic oxide.
5. Predict reactions with water, acid and NaOH, plus structure and physical properties.

Always quote the analogy: "like sodium", "like silicon", "like sulfur".

## Quick predictions (by analogy)

| Element | Group | Predictions |
|---|---|---|
| Potassium | 1 | K₂O basic → KOH (strongly alkaline); KCl ionic, neutral; reacts with water more vigorously than Na |
| Calcium | 2 | CaO basic; Ca(OH)₂ more soluble than Mg(OH)₂; reacts steadily with cold water: Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ |
| Germanium | 14 | GeCl₄ covalent liquid, hydrolysed: GeCl₄ + 2H₂O → GeO₂ + 4HCl; GeO₂ high-melting solid, less acidic than SiO₂ |
| Selenium | 16 | SeO₂ and SeO₃ acidic; SeO₂ + H₂O → H₂SeO₃ |

## Deducing: order of work

Formula → ionisation energies → oxide → chloride → physical data → reactivity. Write the inference from each line, then give the group, period and identity. If two elements fit, name the group and give an example.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chemical periodicity | The repeating pattern of chemical properties across each period |
| Highest oxidation number | Equal to the number of outer-shell electrons for Groups 1, 2 and 13 to 16 |
| Metallic character | Tendency to lose electrons and form basic oxides; increases down a group and decreases across a period |
| Successive ionisation energies | Energies to remove electrons one at a time; a large jump marks a change of shell |
| Analogy | A prediction based on a known element in the same group |
| Amphoteric | Reacts with both acids and bases |

## Exam traps

- Predicting with no reason given.
- Taking outer electrons from the period number.
- Treating an element below silicon or sulfur as identical to it, with no "down the group" adjustment.
- Misreading the ionisation energy jump by one.
- Choosing one identity when the data fit two elements equally.
- Ignoring one statement in a data question; the answer must fit **all** of them.
- Predicting an ionic chloride for an element on the right of a period.

## Self-test

1. An element's oxide has the formula XO₂ and its chloride XCl₄. Which group is it in?
2. Successive ionisation energies show a large jump between the second and third values. How many outer electrons?
3. Predict the formula of the chloride of rubidium (Group 1) and the pH of its solution.
4. Predict, with a reason, how barium reacts with water compared with calcium.
5. Predict the products of hydrolysis of germanium(IV) chloride.
6. Is GeO₂ expected to be more or less acidic than SiO₂? Why?
7. An oxide dissolves in water to give pH 1. Is the element a metal or a non-metal?
8. An oxide is insoluble in water but dissolves in both HCl and NaOH. What term describes it?
9. A chloride is a white solid of high melting point that gives a neutral solution. What bonding does it have?
10. An element forms MCl₂, a basic oxide, and reacts with cold water faster than magnesium. Suggest its identity.
11. Write an equation for SeO₂ with aqueous sodium hydroxide.
12. Why is it good practice to say "Group 2, below magnesium, for example calcium" rather than just "calcium"?

**Answers:**

1. Group 14.
2. Two (Group 2).
3. RbCl; neutral, pH 7.
4. More vigorously: barium is lower in Group 2, so it has a larger atom and lower ionisation energies and loses its outer electrons more easily.
5. GeO₂ and HCl (GeCl₄ + 2H₂O → GeO₂ + 4HCl).
6. Less acidic: germanium is lower in Group 14, so it has more metallic character.
7. A non-metal.
8. Amphoteric.
9. Ionic.
10. Calcium (or another Group 2 metal below magnesium).
11. SeO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SeO₃ + H₂O.
12. The data may not separate two Group 2 metals, so the group and position are the secure deductions and the name is an example consistent with them.
