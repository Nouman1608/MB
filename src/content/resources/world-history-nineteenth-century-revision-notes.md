## The alliance system

| Alliance | Members | Formed |
|---|---|---|
| Triple Alliance | Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy | 1882 |
| Triple Entente | Britain, France, Russia | 1907 |

Built from the Dual Alliance (1879), Franco-Russian Alliance (1894) and Entente Cordiale (1904). The effect was that a local dispute could drag in every major power.

## MAIN causes of the First World War

- **M**ilitarism — arms race, especially the Anglo-German naval race over dreadnoughts.
- **A**lliances — turned a Balkan quarrel into a continental war.
- **I**mperialism — competition for colonies, resources and prestige.
- **N**ationalism — Slav nationalism in the Balkans; French desire to regain Alsace-Lorraine.

## Flashpoints before 1914

```
1905  First Moroccan Crisis   -- Germany challenges French influence
1908  Bosnian Crisis          -- Austria annexes Bosnia; Serbia angered
1911  Agadir Crisis           -- second Moroccan clash; Britain backs France
1912-13 Balkan Wars           -- Ottoman decline; Serbia doubles in size
```

Each crisis hardened the alliances and deepened mistrust rather than resolving anything.

## The July Crisis 1914 — sequence

```
28 June   Franz Ferdinand assassinated in Sarajevo (Princip, Black Hand)
23 July   Austrian ultimatum to Serbia
28 July   Austria declares war on Serbia
30 July   Russia mobilises to defend Serbia
1 Aug     Germany declares war on Russia
3 Aug     Germany declares war on France; Schlieffen Plan begins
4 Aug     Germany invades Belgium; Britain declares war
```

Britain entered over the **Treaty of London (1839)** guaranteeing Belgian neutrality — and to prevent German domination of the Channel ports.

## The Schlieffen Plan

Designed to avoid a two-front war: knock out France in six weeks through Belgium, then turn on Russia. It failed because Belgium resisted, Britain intervened, and Russia mobilised faster than expected — producing the stalemate of trench warfare.

## Exam traps

- The assassination was the **trigger**, not the cause — long-term causes had been building for decades.
- Italy was in the Triple Alliance but joined the **Allies** in 1915.
- An **Entente** is an understanding, not a binding military alliance like the Triple Alliance.
- Always support a claim with a specific date, name or treaty.
- "How far" questions need a **judgement** weighing factors, not a list.

## Self-test

1. Name the members of each of the two alliance blocs.
2. What does MAIN stand for?
3. Why did Britain declare war on 4 August 1914?
4. State two reasons the Schlieffen Plan failed.
5. Why is the assassination described as a trigger rather than a cause?

**Answers:** 1. Triple Alliance: Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy. Triple Entente: Britain, France, Russia. 2. Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism. 3. Germany invaded Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed by the Treaty of London 1839; Britain also feared German control of the Channel ports. 4. Belgian resistance delayed the advance; Britain intervened; Russia mobilised faster than anticipated. 5. The underlying tensions — alliances, militarism, imperial rivalry and nationalism — had existed for decades; the assassination merely set them in motion.
