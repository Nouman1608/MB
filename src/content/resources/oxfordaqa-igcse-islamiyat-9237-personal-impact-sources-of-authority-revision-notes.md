**These notes condense content area 3.2.2.8 of 9237: "The importance of generosity and sacrifice, including sincerity (Ikhlas), piety, honesty, hiding the faults of others and the importance of physical and mental health."** The full explanation is in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-personal-impact-sources-of-authority/).

## Qualities at a glance

| Quality | Meaning | Source | Effect on character |
|---|---|---|---|
| Generosity | Giving freely: Zakah and sadaqah | Qur'an 3:92; 2:261. Hadith: the upper hand is better | Removes greed; builds gratitude |
| Sacrifice | Giving up what is valued for Allah or others | Ibraheem (AS); Ansar (59:9); Eid al-Adha | Self-control, courage, trust in Allah |
| Ikhlas | Doing deeds for Allah alone | Qur'an 98:5; "actions are by intentions" | Humility; no need for praise |
| Taqwa (piety) | Awareness of Allah | Qur'an 49:13; "Fear Allah wherever you are" | Good behaviour even when unseen |
| Honesty | Truth, promises, fairness, trusts | Hadith: truthfulness leads to righteousness; "whoever cheats is not of us" | Trust and a clear conscience |
| Hiding faults | Protecting others' privacy and dignity | Hadith: whoever hides a Muslim's faults, Allah hides his | Mercy and humility |
| Health | Care of body and mind | Hadith of Salman; Qur'an 7:31; 13:28 | Able to worship, work and serve |

## Generosity: key evidence

- Qur'an 3:92: spend from what you love.
- Qur'an 2:261: the grain that grows seven ears.
- Qur'an 76:8-9: feeding the poor, orphan and captive for Allah alone.
- Hadith: the upper hand is better than the lower; charity does not reduce wealth.
- Example: Abu Bakr (RA) gave all he had, saying "Allah and His Messenger" (Tirmidhi 3675).

## Sacrifice: key examples

- **Ibraheem (AS):** ready to sacrifice his son at Allah's command; remembered at Eid al-Adha.
- **Muhajirun:** left home and property for faith.
- **Ansar:** shared homes and wealth and preferred others over themselves (59:9).
- **Daily life:** Fajr, fasting, Zakah, saving for Hajj.

## Sincerity, piety, honesty

- **Ikhlas:** the opposite is *riya'* (showing off). A deed done for show loses its reward.
- **Taqwa:** an inner guard; the most honoured is the most righteous (49:13).
- **Honesty:** the Prophet (pbuh) was al-Amin; the Hadith links truth to Paradise.

## Hiding the faults of others

- Hadith (Muslim): whoever hides a Muslim's faults, Allah hides his faults in this world and the next.
- Qur'an 24:19 warns against loving the spread of indecency.
- **Limit:** do not hide injustice; report crimes to those who can stop them, and advise people privately.

## Physical health

| Principle | Evidence |
|---|---|
| The body has rights | Salman to Abu Darda: your Lord, your soul and your family have rights over you (Bukhari) |
| Strength is good | The strong believer is better and more beloved (Muslim) |
| Cleanliness | Cleanliness is half of faith (Muslim) |
| Moderation | "Eat and drink, but be not excessive" (7:31) |
| Avoid harm | Alcohol and drugs are forbidden; seek treatment because Allah sent a cure for every disease (Bukhari) |
| Ease in worship | The sick may break the fast (2:185) |

## Mental health

- Remembrance of Allah brings peace (13:28).
- "With hardship [will be] ease" (94:5-6).
- Allah does not burden a soul beyond its capacity (2:286).
- The Prophet (pbuh) taught a prayer for protection from worry and grief.
- Seeking help is not weakness of faith.

## The prescribed passage: Sahih Muslim 2564c

"Verily Allah does not look to your faces and your wealth but He looks to your heart and to your deeds."

| Phrase | Link |
|---|---|
| "does not look to your faces and your wealth" | Looks and money do not decide worth; guards against pride |
| "He looks to your heart" | Ikhlas and taqwa |
| "and to your deeds" | Generosity, sacrifice and honesty must be shown in action |

## Model answer: a 4-mark question

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why sincerity (Ikhlas) is important for a Muslim. **[4]**

> Sincerity makes a deed count in the sight of Allah [1], because the Hadith teaches that actions are judged by intentions, so charity given to show off loses its reward [1]. It also protects the believer from pride and the need for praise [1], which brings humility and inner peace [1].

## How the qualities connect

- **Ikhlas** gives generosity and sacrifice their value.
- **Taqwa** keeps a person honest when no one sees.
- **Honesty** and **hiding faults** protect the dignity of others.
- **Health** gives a person the strength to practise all of these.
- **The Hadith in Muslim 2564c** ties them together: Allah looks at the heart and the deeds.

**Exam use:** in a long answer, choose three qualities, give a source for each, and show how they work together to form one character.

## Exam traps

- Define each quality and give its effect on character.
- Ikhlas is sincerity; taqwa is God-consciousness.
- Include physical and mental health.
- Hiding faults does not mean ignoring injustice.
- Link the prescribed Hadith to inner qualities and outward deeds.

## Self-test

1. What is the difference between Zakah and sadaqah?
2. Which verse says that people will not attain righteousness until they spend from what they love?
3. What is Ikhlas?
4. What is riya'?
5. Give a Qur'anic verse about taqwa.
6. What does the Hadith say about hiding the faults of a Muslim?
7. Give two ways Islam teaches care of the body.
8. Give one teaching that helps with mental health.
9. What does Sahih Muslim 2564c say Allah looks at?
10. Why are generosity and sacrifice connected?

**Answers:** 1. Zakah is obligatory; sadaqah is voluntary. 2. Qur'an 3:92. 3. Sincerity: doing deeds purely for Allah. 4. Showing off in good deeds. 5. Qur'an 49:13: the most noble is the most righteous. 6. Allah will hide his faults in this world and the next. 7. Any two: cleanliness; moderation in food; avoiding harmful substances; seeking treatment; exercising strength. 8. Any one: remembrance of Allah brings peace; hardship is followed by ease; Allah does not burden beyond capacity. 9. Hearts and deeds, not faces and wealth. 10. Both involve giving up something valued, for Allah and for others.
