This set practises **3.3.4.4 Epoxyethane** from section 3.3.4 Alkenes of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [epoxyethane study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-4-4/) or the [epoxyethane revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-4-4-revision-notes/). Take Ar values as H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and O = 16.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which best explains the high reactivity of epoxyethane?

A: It contains a C=C double bond.

B: Its three-membered ring is strained, with bond angles of about 60°.

C: It has hydrogen bonds between its molecules.

D: Its C–H bonds are very polar. **[1]**

**2.** Write an equation for the production of epoxyethane from ethene. **[1]**

**3.** State why the production of epoxyethane is described as *partial* oxidation. **[1]**

**4.** Write an equation for the reaction of epoxyethane with methanol and name the product. **[2]**

**5.** State the role of water in its reaction with epoxyethane. **[1]**

**6.** Give one use of ethane-1,2-diol and the property that makes it suitable for that use. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Epoxyethane is made industrially from ethene and oxygen.

**(a)** Write an equation for the competing reaction that wastes ethene. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the temperature of the reactor must be carefully controlled. **[2]**

**(c)** Give **two** other hazards of the process and explain each one. **[2]**

**8.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why epoxyethane reacts readily with water but ethoxyethane, CH₃CH₂OCH₂CH₃, does not. **[4]**

**9.** Epoxyethane reacts with ethanol.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism for this reaction. **[4]**

**(b)** Name the product. **[1]**

**10.** When ethane-1,2-diol is made from epoxyethane, a by-product with formula C₄H₁₀O₃ also forms.

**(a)** Give the structure of the by-product and write an equation for its formation. **[2]**

**(b)** State how the conditions are chosen to reduce the amount of this by-product, and explain why this works. **[2]**

**11.** A plant converts 2.80 tonnes of ethene into epoxyethane with an 85.0 % yield.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of epoxyethane produced. **[2]**

**(b)** All of this epoxyethane reacts with excess water. Calculate the maximum mass of ethane-1,2-diol formed. **[2]**

**12.** A detergent manufacturer makes a non-ionic surfactant from hexadecan-1-ol, C₁₆H₃₃OH, and epoxyethane.

**(a)** Write a general equation for the reaction of hexadecan-1-ol with n molecules of epoxyethane. **[1]**

**(b)** The surfactant has Mr 682.0. Calculate n. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the structure of the surfactant allows it to act as a detergent. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why it is important that this surfactant breaks down readily in the environment. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** **2CH₂=CH₂ + O₂ → 2C₂H₄O** [1]

**3.** Only one oxygen atom is added per ethene molecule; ethene is not fully oxidised to carbon dioxide and water [1]

**4.** **C₂H₄O + CH₃OH → CH₃OCH₂CH₂OH** [1]; **2-methoxyethanol** [1]

**5.** Nucleophile (lone pair donor) [1]

**6.** Antifreeze in vehicle cooling systems [1]; it mixes with water and lowers its freezing point [1] (or: monomer for polyesters; it has two –OH groups that can each react)

**7. (a)** **CH₂=CH₂ + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 2H₂O** [1]

**(b)** Both reactions are exothermic, and combustion releases much more energy [1]; if the temperature rises, more combustion occurs, releasing more heat and risking a runaway reaction or explosion [1]

**(c)** Two from: ethene/epoxyethane are flammable and mixtures with oxygen can explode, so the gas ratio must be controlled [1]; epoxyethane is toxic and carcinogenic, so leaks must be prevented [1]; epoxyethane is very reactive, so contact with water or other contaminants must be avoided

**8.** Epoxyethane has a three-membered ring with bond angles of about 60° [1]; this is far from the normal angle of about 109.5°, so the ring is strained and its C–O bonds are weak [1]; the polar C–O bonds make the ring carbons δ+, so a lone pair on water attacks a ring carbon and breaking a C–O bond relieves the strain [1]; ethoxyethane has no ring and no strain, so its C–O bonds are not easily broken [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** Arrow from a lone pair on the O of ethanol to a ring carbon [1]; arrow from the ring C–O bond to the ring O [1]; intermediate C₂H₅O⁺(H)CH₂CH₂O⁻ with the correct charges [1]; proton transfer: arrow from a lone pair on O⁻ to the H on O⁺, and from the O⁺–H bond to O⁺, giving C₂H₅OCH₂CH₂OH [1]

**(b)** **2-Ethoxyethanol** [1]

**10. (a)** **HOCH₂CH₂OCH₂CH₂OH** [1]; **HOCH₂CH₂OH + C₂H₄O → HOCH₂CH₂OCH₂CH₂OH** [1]

**(b)** Use a large excess of water [1]; epoxyethane molecules are then much more likely to collide with water than with ethane-1,2-diol [1]

**11. (a)** n(ethene) = 2.80 × 10⁶ ÷ 28.0 = 1.00 × 10⁵ mol, so theoretical mass of epoxyethane = 1.00 × 10⁵ × 44.0 g = 4.40 tonnes [1]; actual mass = 4.40 × 0.850 = **3.74 tonnes** [1]

**(b)** n(epoxyethane) = 3.74 × 10⁶ ÷ 44.0 = 8.50 × 10⁴ mol [1]; mass of diol = 8.50 × 10⁴ × 62.0 g = **5.27 tonnes** [1]

**12. (a)** **C₁₆H₃₃OH + nC₂H₄O → C₁₆H₃₃(OCH₂CH₂)ₙOH** [1]

**(b)** Mr(C₁₆H₃₃OH) = 242.0 [1]; n = (682.0 − 242.0) ÷ 44.0 = **10** [1]

**(c)** The long hydrocarbon chain is non-polar and dissolves in grease or oil [1]; the polyether chain ending in –OH is polar and is attracted to water, so grease is lifted into the water [1]

**(d)** Surfactants washed into rivers can harm aquatic life, so they should not persist [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling water or alcohols electrophiles.
- Explaining reactivity without ring strain or without the 60° angle.
- Charges missing from the ring-opened intermediate.
- Hazards stated without a reason.
- Forgetting that ring-opened products still have –OH and can react again.
