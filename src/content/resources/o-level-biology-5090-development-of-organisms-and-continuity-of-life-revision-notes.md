## Key definitions

- **Chromosome**: structure in the nucleus containing DNA, which carries genetic information as genes.
- **Haploid nucleus**: a single set of chromosomes.
- **Diploid nucleus**: two sets of chromosomes; a pair of each type. Human diploid cells: **23 pairs**.
- **Mitosis**: nuclear division giving genetically identical cells; chromosome number maintained.
- **Meiosis**: reduction division; chromosome number halved, diploid to haploid; genetically different cells; makes gametes.
- **Stem cells**: unspecialised cells that divide by mitosis to give daughter cells that can become specialised.
- **Asexual reproduction**: genetically identical offspring from one parent.
- **Sexual reproduction**: fusion of haploid nuclei (fertilisation) to form a diploid zygote; genetically different offspring.
- **Pollination**: transfer of pollen from an anther to a stigma.
- **Fertilisation (humans)**: fusion of the nuclei of a sperm and an egg cell.

## 16.1 Nuclear division at a glance

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Chromosome number | Kept the same | Halved (diploid → haploid) |
| Daughter cells | Genetically identical | Genetically different |
| Used for | Growth, repair, replacing dying cells, asexual reproduction | Making gametes |

**Cancer** = uncontrolled cell division.

**Worked reminder.** Diploid number 20 → each gamete has 20 ÷ 2 = **10**; the zygote has 10 + 10 = **20**.

## 16.2 Asexual v sexual reproduction

Asexual examples: binary fission in bacteria, budding in yeast, potato tubers, strawberry runners, onion bulbs.

| | Asexual | Sexual |
|---|---|---|
| For | No mate or pollinator; fast; many offspring; good features kept | Variation; population can respond to environmental change; seeds spread widely |
| Against | No variation; one disease or change can affect all; crowding and competition | Two parents or pollen transfer needed; slower; more energy used |

## 16.3 Plants

### Flower parts

- **Sepals**: protect the bud.
- **Petals**: attract insects (insect-pollinated flowers).
- **Stamen** = **anther** (makes pollen) + **filament** (supports anther).
- **Carpel** = **stigma** (receives pollen) + **style** (holds stigma; pollen tube grows through) + **ovary** (holds ovules) + **ovules** (contain female gametes).

### Insect- v wind-pollinated

| | Insect | Wind |
|---|---|---|
| Petals | Large, coloured, scent, nectar | Small/absent, dull, no scent or nectar |
| Anthers | Inside flower | Hanging outside, long filaments |
| Stigmas | Inside, sticky | Outside, feathery |
| Pollen | Larger, sticky/spiky, less | Small, light, smooth, huge amounts |

### Self- v cross-pollination

| | Self | Cross |
|---|---|---|
| Pollen from | Same flower or same plant | Different plant, same species |
| Variation | Less | More |
| Response to environmental change | Less able | More able |
| Reliance on pollinators | Low | High |

### From pollen to seed: method in steps

1. Pollen lands on the stigma.
2. A pollen tube grows down the style to the ovary.
3. The tube enters an ovule (through the micropyle).
4. The male nucleus fuses with the female nucleus: fertilisation.
5. Ovule → **seed**; ovary → **fruit**.

### Seeds and dispersal

- **Testa**: protective coat. **Radicle**: first root. **Plumule**: first shoot. **Cotyledons**: food store.
- Dispersal lets plants **colonise new areas** and **reduces competition** for light, water and mineral ions.
- **Wind**: wings or parachutes, light, large surface area → stays airborne longer.
- **Animal**: hooks catch on fur, or fleshy coloured fruit is eaten and seeds are egested elsewhere.

### Germination

Conditions: **water**, **oxygen**, **suitable temperature**. Light is not one of them.

Process in steps:
1. Seed absorbs water; testa splits.
2. Water activates enzymes.
3. Amylase: starch → maltose. Proteases: protein → amino acids.
4. Soluble products move to the embryo for respiration and growth.
5. Radicle emerges, then plumule.

**Worked reminder.** 18 of 24 seeds germinate: 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = **75%**.

**Practical tips.** Use a control with all three conditions. Remove oxygen with boiled, cooled water under an oil layer. Use a refrigerator for low temperature. Keep seed type, number and time the same.

## 16.4 Humans

### Organs and functions

| Male | Function | Female | Function |
|---|---|---|---|
| Testes | Sperm, testosterone | Ovaries | Eggs, oestrogen, progesterone |
| Scrotum | Holds testes outside body | Oviducts | Carry egg; site of fertilisation |
| Sperm ducts | Carry sperm to urethra | Uterus | Implantation; fetus develops |
| Prostate gland | Fluid → semen | Cervix | Muscle ring at uterus base |
| Urethra | Carries semen or urine | Vagina | Receives semen; birth canal |
| Penis | Places semen in vagina | | |

### Gametes

- **Sperm**: flagellum (swims); mitochondria (energy for swimming); acrosome enzymes (digest jelly coat).
- **Egg**: energy stores (for the early embryo); jelly coat changes at fertilisation (blocks other sperm).

| | Sperm | Egg |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Smaller | Larger |
| Numbers | Millions | Usually one per cycle |
| Motility | Swims | Not motile |

**Worked reminder.** A sperm cell 0.05 mm long, drawn at ×400, gives an image 0.05 × 400 = **20 mm** long (image size = actual size × magnification).

### Puberty

- **Testosterone**: facial and body hair, deeper voice, muscle growth, sperm production starts.
- **Oestrogen**: breasts develop, hips widen, menstrual cycle starts.

### Menstrual cycle hormones

| Hormone | Made by | Role |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Egg develops in ovary; ovary makes oestrogen |
| Oestrogen | Ovary | Rebuilds uterus lining; inhibits FSH; triggers LH |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Ovulation |
| Progesterone | Ovary (empty follicle) | Keeps lining thick; inhibits FSH and LH |

Progesterone falls → lining breaks down (menstruation) → FSH rises again.

### Pregnancy

- Zygote → ball of cells (embryo) by mitosis → **implants** in uterus lining.
- **Amniotic sac** encloses the fetus and fluid; **amniotic fluid** protects against knocks.
- **Placenta**: O₂ and dissolved nutrients to fetus; CO₂ and urea to mother. Bloods do not mix.
- **Umbilical cord**: carries fetal blood to and from the placenta.
- Some **viruses** cross the placenta, e.g. rubella, HIV.

## Answer frames to learn

**"Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of asexual reproduction for a crop."** Give both sides and link each to the outcome:
- For: all plants are identical, so they keep the high yield of the parent; one parent is enough; plants are produced quickly.
- Against: no variation, so a new disease or a change in climate could affect the whole crop at once.

**"Discuss the effect of self-pollination on a population."**
- Less variation, so the population is less able to respond to a change in the environment.
- Does not rely on pollinators, so seeds still form when insects are scarce.

**"Explain how the placenta keeps the fetus supplied."**
- Oxygen and dissolved nutrients pass from the mother's blood to the fetus's blood across the placenta.
- Carbon dioxide and urea pass the other way, into the mother's blood.
- The umbilical cord carries the fetus's blood between the fetus and the placenta.

**"Explain why the uterus lining breaks down at the end of a cycle."**
- No pregnancy, so progesterone levels fall.
- Progesterone no longer keeps the lining thick, so it breaks down and is lost in menstruation.

**"Describe how to show that oxygen is needed for germination."**
- Set up a tube with seeds under boiled, cooled water covered with oil, and a control with seeds on damp cotton wool in air.
- Keep both at the same suitable temperature and use the same type and number of seeds.
- Count the seeds that germinate after the same time in each.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Mitosis v meiosis**: same number and identical v halved and different.
- **Pollination v fertilisation**: moving pollen v fusing nuclei.
- **Ovule v ovary**: seed v fruit.
- **Self- v cross-pollination**: same plant v different plant of the same species.
- **Haploid v diploid**: one set v two sets.
- **Amniotic sac v placenta**: protection v exchange.

## Quick self-test

1. How many chromosomes are in a human sperm cell?
2. State two roles of mitosis.
3. What type of cell divides by mitosis to give cells that can specialise?
4. Give one disadvantage of asexual reproduction.
5. State the function of the filament.
6. Give two features of pollen from a wind-pollinated flower.
7. What does the ovary develop into after fertilisation?
8. Which enzyme breaks down starch in a germinating seed, and what is the product?
9. Which hormone causes ovulation?
10. State one function of the amniotic fluid.
11. A plant species has a diploid number of 16. How many chromosomes are in the male gamete nucleus in one of its pollen grains?
12. Name the part of a seed that becomes the first shoot.

### Answers

1. **23** (haploid).
2. Any two: growth; repair of damaged tissues; replacement of dying cells; asexual reproduction.
3. **Stem cells**.
4. No variation, so a change in the environment or a disease can affect every offspring.
5. Holds the anther in position.
6. Any two: small; light; smooth; produced in very large amounts.
7. A **fruit**.
8. **Amylase**; **maltose**.
9. **LH**.
10. Protects the fetus from knocks (and supports it).
11. **8** (haploid: 16 ÷ 2).
12. The **plumule**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying meiosis gives "identical" cells or that mitosis "halves" the number.
- Writing "46 pairs" for humans. It is 23 pairs, 46 chromosomes.
- Defining pollination as pollen reaching "the flower" rather than the **stigma**.
- Calling self-pollination "pollen from the same species". Both kinds use the same species; self means the same plant.
- Giving "light" as a germination condition.
- Saying water "provides energy" in germination. Water activates enzymes; respiration releases energy.
- Saying the acrosome enzymes digest "the egg". They digest the jelly coat.
- Mixing up FSH and LH, or saying oestrogen causes ovulation.
- Writing that the fetus's and mother's blood mix in the placenta.
