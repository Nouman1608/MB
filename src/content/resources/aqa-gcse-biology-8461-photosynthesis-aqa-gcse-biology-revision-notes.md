## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Making glucose and oxygen from carbon dioxide and water, using light energy |
| Endothermic | Takes in energy from the surroundings; in photosynthesis, light energy is transferred to the chloroplasts |
| Chlorophyll | The green pigment in chloroplasts that absorbs light |
| Rate of photosynthesis | Amount of product made, or reactant used, per unit time |
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate increasing |
| Optimum temperature | The temperature at which the rate is fastest |
| Denatured | Enzyme shape changed by high temperature so it no longer works |
| Inverse square law | Light intensity ∝ 1/distance² (Higher tier only) |

## The equation

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (light energy needed; written above the arrow)

CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ (you must recognise these formulae; balancing is not required)

## The four factors in one line each

- **Light intensity:** provides the energy.
- **CO₂ concentration:** a reactant.
- **Temperature:** enzyme-controlled; rises to an optimum, then falls sharply as enzymes denature.
- **Amount of chlorophyll:** absorbs light; reduced by disease or magnesium deficiency.

## Graph rules

| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Line rising as x-axis factor increases | The x-axis factor is limiting |
| Line flat | The x-axis factor is no longer limiting; another factor is |
| Two lines identical at low x | The factor that differs between them is not limiting there |
| Two lines separate | The factor that differs between them is limiting where they separate (Higher tier only) |

## Required practical 6 in a table

| Variable type | In this practical |
|---|---|
| Independent | Light intensity (distance from lamp) |
| Dependent | Volume of oxygen or number of bubbles per unit time |
| Control | Temperature, CO₂ concentration, same pondweed, same time for each reading |

Key details: heat shield (beaker of water) and thermometer; allow the weed to adjust at each distance; repeat
and calculate a mean; collecting gas is more accurate than counting bubbles because bubbles vary in size.

## Calculations

**Rate:** amount ÷ time. 9.6 cm³ in 4 minutes → 9.6 ÷ 4 = **2.4 cm³ per minute**.

**Mean:** add the repeats and divide by the number of repeats, leaving out anomalies.

**Inverse square (Higher tier only):**

| Distance change | Intensity change |
|---|---|
| × 2 | × 1/4 |
| × 4 | × 1/16 |
| ÷ 3 | × 9 |

Example: 1/d² at 50 cm = 1/2500 = 0.0004.

**Greenhouse (Higher tier only):** gain = (extra yield × price) − cost. Positive gain → worth adding.

## Uses of glucose

1. Respiration.
2. Insoluble starch for storage.
3. Fat or oil for storage.
4. Cellulose to strengthen cell walls.
5. Amino acids for proteins, with **nitrate ions** absorbed from the soil.

## Explanation chains to learn

- **Light:** more light → more energy transferred to chloroplasts → faster rate, until another factor limits.
- **Carbon dioxide:** more CO₂ → more reactant available → faster rate, until another factor limits.
- **High temperature:** above the optimum → enzymes denatured → active sites change shape → rate falls sharply.
- **Magnesium deficiency or leaf disease:** less chlorophyll → less light absorbed → slower rate.
- **Lamp too close without a heat shield:** water warms → temperature changes as well as light → unfair test.

## Describing and explaining a graph

- **Describe:** "As light intensity increases from 0 to 4 units, the rate increases from 0 to 20 units; above 4
  units the rate stays at 20."
- **Explain:** "Light is the limiting factor up to 4 units; above that another factor, such as CO₂ concentration,
  is limiting."

## Exam traps

- Light written as a reactant.
- Calling photosynthesis exothermic.
- Naming the x-axis factor as limiting on the flat part of a graph.
- Saying enzymes are "killed" at high temperature.
- Using distance where a question asks for light intensity (Higher tier only).
- No unit on a rate, or dividing time by amount.
- Forgetting the heat shield's purpose: it keeps **temperature** constant, not light.
- Saying a greenhouse grower should add every factor; only the limiting factor raises yield.

## Self-test

1. Where in a cell does photosynthesis take place?
2. Why is photosynthesis described as endothermic?
3. Give the formula of glucose.
4. Why does the rate fall sharply above the optimum temperature?
5. Why might a plant with diseased leaves photosynthesise more slowly in bright light?
6. On a graph of rate against light intensity, the line is flat. Is light limiting?
7. In required practical 6, why is a beaker of water put between the lamp and the pondweed?
8. Why is collecting the gas better than counting bubbles?
9. Pondweed releases 7.2 cm³ of oxygen in 6 minutes. Calculate the rate.
10. (Higher tier only) A lamp is moved from 20 cm to 10 cm. How does the light intensity change?
11. (Higher tier only) Extra lighting costs £45 a week and adds crop worth £30 a week. Is it worth it?
12. Why do plants need nitrate ions as well as glucose?

### Answers

1. In the chloroplasts.
2. Energy is transferred from the environment to the chloroplasts by light.
3. C₆H₁₂O₆.
4. The enzymes are denatured.
5. Less chlorophyll is available to absorb the light.
6. No; another factor is limiting.
7. To keep the temperature constant (acts as a heat shield).
8. Bubbles vary in size, so gas volume is a more accurate measure.
9. 7.2 ÷ 6 = **1.2 cm³ per minute**.
10. It increases four times.
11. No: gain = 30 − 45 = −£15 a week.
12. To make amino acids, and so proteins.
