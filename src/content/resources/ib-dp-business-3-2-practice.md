These questions cover section 3.2 (Sources of finance) of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024, for SL and HL. Learn the method from the [3.2 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-3-2/) and the [3.2 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-3-2-revision-notes/). All businesses and figures are invented, and money is in dollars. Interest is simple interest unless stated.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *retained profit*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between share capital and loan capital. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each source is usually short term or long term: (i) overdraft, (ii) share capital, (iii) trade credit, (iv) loan capital. **[4]**

**4.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of crowdfunding for a new board-game publisher. **[4]**

**5.** Wickmere Stationers receives a 12,500 invoice. The supplier allows 45 days' credit, or a 2.5% discount for immediate payment.

**(a)** Calculate the discount lost by taking the full credit period. **[1]**

**(b)** Wickmere would pay early using its overdraft at 10% a year for the 45 days. Calculate the overdraft interest on the discounted amount, to the nearest dollar. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

Questions 6 to 9 use the case below.

Hollowell Fitness Ltd is a private limited company owned by three founders. It plans to open a second gym. The fit-out of the building will cost 120,000 and will last about ten years. Last year profit after tax was 70,000 and dividends paid were 40,000. A bank offers a five-year loan at 9% a year. A business angel offers 80,000 in return for 30% of the company's shares and a seat on the board. The founders value their independence, and gym membership fell during a recent economic downturn.

**6.** Calculate:

**(a)** Hollowell's retained profit for last year. **[1]**

**(b)** the amount still needed if all of last year's retained profit is used for the fit-out. **[1]**

**(c)** the total interest on a bank loan for that remaining amount. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why an overdraft would not be an appropriate source for the fit-out. **[2]**

**8.** Analyse the business angel's offer for Hollowell's founders. **[6]**

**9.** Recommend how Hollowell should finance the 120,000 fit-out. **[10]**

**10.** Hollowell also needs gym equipment. It could buy the equipment for 40,000 or lease it for 1,100 a month for four years, with servicing included.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of leasing over four years. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain one reason why Hollowell might still choose to lease. **[2]**

**11.** Amara runs a tailoring business as a sole trader in a rural area. She has no assets to pledge and no credit history. Explain why a microfinance provider may be more appropriate for her than a bank loan. **[4]**

**12.** Explain how sale of assets could provide finance for a manufacturer, and one risk of using it. **[3]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Profit after tax that is kept in the business rather than paid to owners as dividends [1], which can be used to fund future spending without borrowing [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Share capital is raised by selling shares, so investors become part-owners and the money does not have to be repaid [1]; loan capital is borrowed and must be repaid with interest, but the lender gains no ownership [1]. **[2]**

**3.** (i) Short term [1]. (ii) Long term [1]. (iii) Short term [1]. (iv) Long term [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Advantage: many backers each pay a small amount before launch [1], which tests demand for the game and builds a group of early customers [1]. Disadvantage: the campaign must be convincing and well promoted [1], and if the funding target is not reached the publisher may get no money [1]. **[4]**

**5. (a)** 2.5% × 12,500 = **312.50** [1]

**(b)** Discounted amount = 12,500 − 312.50 = 12,187.50 [1]; interest = 12,187.50 × 0.10 × 45 ÷ 365 = **150** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Paying early saves 312.50 but costs about 150 in interest, so it is the cheaper option. A comment like this turns a calculation into analysis.

### Section B

**6. (a)** 70,000 − 40,000 = **30,000** [1]

**(b)** 120,000 − 30,000 = **90,000** [1]

**(c)** 90,000 × 0.09 × 5 [1] = **40,500** [1] **[4]**

**7.** An overdraft is short-term finance that the bank can withdraw at short notice [1], whereas the fit-out will be used for about ten years, so the business could be forced to repay long before the asset has earned back its cost [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Benefit: 80,000 covers most of the 90,000 gap [1], and as share capital it has no interest or repayments, which matters after membership fell in the downturn [1]. Benefit: the angel may bring expertise and contacts from a board seat [1]. Drawback: the founders give up 30% of ownership, and therefore 30% of future dividends [1]. Drawback: a board seat means sharing decisions, which conflicts with the founders' wish for independence [1]. Overall, the offer reduces financial risk but at a cost in control that the founders clearly value [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Purpose: the fit-out lasts about ten years, so long-term finance is appropriate [1]. Retained profit of 30,000 can be used first, with no interest and no loss of control, although not all of it may be available as cash [1]. Option A, bank loan for 90,000: keeps full ownership [1], but adds 40,500 of interest and fixed repayments [1], which is risky because membership fell during the last downturn [1]. Option B, business angel for 80,000: no repayments and possible expertise [1], but 30% ownership and a board seat conflict with the founders' independence [1]. A combination, such as retained profit plus a smaller loan and leasing the equipment, may reduce the borrowing needed [1]. Recommendation: retained profit plus a bank loan, because the founders value independence and the gym will earn revenue for ten years to cover repayments [1]; but if forecasts show membership could fall again, the angel's equity would be safer [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* The highest marks go to answers that use the case's own priorities (independence, downturn risk) to decide between options.

**10. (a)** 1,100 × 48 = **52,800** [1]

**(b)** Leasing avoids a 40,000 payment now, which preserves cash while the second gym opens [1]; servicing is included, so Hollowell avoids unexpected repair costs, and it can update equipment when the lease ends [1] **[3]**

**11.** Banks usually require collateral and a credit history before lending [1], which Amara does not have, so a bank loan may be refused [1]. Microfinance providers specialise in small loans to entrepreneurs who lack collateral or credit history [1], and the amount a tailoring business needs is likely to be small; however, interest rates may be relatively high [1]. **[4]**

**12.** The manufacturer sells non-current assets it no longer needs, such as an unused warehouse or old machinery, turning them into cash [1] without taking on debt or giving up ownership [1]. Risk: if it sells an asset it later needs, it reduces productive capacity and may have to rent or rebuy it [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

* Recommending share capital for a sole trader.
* Ignoring the life of the asset when choosing between short- and long-term sources.
* Calculating interest but not commenting on what it means for the business.
* Weighing sources without using the owners' stated priorities.
* Ending a "recommend" answer without a clear choice and a condition.
