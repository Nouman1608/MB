For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Human ingenuity study guide](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-human-ingenuity/) first. These notes are for the final weeks.

They cover **Human ingenuity**, the third of the five prescribed themes in IB Diploma Programme Language B, aligned to the IB *Diploma Programme Subject Brief: Language B*, first assessment 2020. SL and HL students study the same theme; HL students handle more complex language and also study two literary works originally written in the target language. It follows the IB Language B subject brief for first assessment 2020, which remains the examined course until the new syllabus is first assessed in May 2029, so it applies to the 2026, 2027 and 2028 sessions.

Related pages: [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-human-ingenuity-practice/), [Language B course hub](/boards/ib/ib-dp/language-b/), [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-dp/language-b/), [assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-assessment-revision-notes/), and the parallel notes for [Identities](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-identities-revision-notes/) and [Sharing the planet](/resources/ib-dp-language-b-sharing-the-planet-revision-notes/).

## The theme on one screen

| Item | What the brief says |
|---|---|
| Guiding principle | Explore the ways in which human creativity and innovation affect our world |
| Optional recommended topics | Entertainment; artistic expressions; communication and media; technology; scientific innovation |
| Possible question 1 | What can we learn about a culture through its artistic expression? |
| Possible question 2 | How do the media change the way we relate to each other? |
| Conceptual understandings (whole course) | Context, audience, purpose, meaning, variation |
| Skills (whole course) | Receptive, productive, interactive |
| Place in the list | Third of five: identities, experiences, human ingenuity, social organization, sharing the planet |

## Where the theme can appear

| Component | Weighting | Fact from the brief | Human ingenuity angle |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (productive) | 25% | One writing task from a choice of three; 30 marks | A text about an invention, an art project or a media habit |
| Paper 2 listening | 25% | 25 marks | An interview with an inventor, a podcast on streaming |
| Paper 2 reading | 25% | 40 marks | An article on a new museum, a blog on screen time |
| Individual oral (internal) | 25% | 30 marks; SL visual image, HL literary excerpt | An image of a mural or robot (SL); a passage about creation or discovery (HL) |

HL Paper 1 tasks need more complex language, higher-order thinking and have a higher word range. Your teacher will share the criteria and formats.

## Vocabulary fields to build

Write each headword in your target language with gender or plural (if your language marks them) and one sentence of your own.

| Topic | Headwords |
|---|---|
| Entertainment | audience, performance, genre, subscription, review, live |
| Artistic expressions | heritage, exhibition, craftsperson, symbol, restore, preserve |
| Communication and media | source, headline, bias, privacy, follower, reliable |
| Technology | device, access, user, automate, replace, affordable |
| Scientific innovation | breakthrough, trial, laboratory, side effect, risk, ethical |

## Structures the theme demands

| Function | English frame | Check in your target language |
|---|---|---|
| Process | "It was designed, then tested." | Passive or impersonal form |
| Cause and effect | "As a result, ..." / "This has led to ..." | Connector plus verb form or word order |
| Then and now | "People used to ...; now they ..." | Habitual past |
| Speculation | "If ..., we would ..." | Conditional; hypothetical mood if your language has one |
| Evaluation | "The benefits outweigh the costs because ..." | Hedging words, precise adjectives |
| Interpretation | "This suggests that ..." | A set of reporting verbs |

## Method in steps

**Reading an artwork (possible question 1)**

1. Describe exactly what is there.
2. Infer what it shows about the makers: values, history, beliefs.
3. Mark guesses as guesses ("perhaps", "it may be that").
4. Answer the question in one sentence with one short quotation or detail.

**Arguing about media (possible question 2)**

1. Point: one change in how people relate.
2. Example: specific and concrete, never an invented statistic presented as fact.
3. Effect: closer, more divided, faster, shallower?
4. Counterpoint: who loses out, or what the opposite view would say.

**Paper 1 plan (five lines)**

1. Text type, audience, purpose, context.
2. Opening that fits the text type.
3. Two or three body points, each with an example.
4. Counterpoint or complication.
5. Close that fits the purpose: recommendation, call to action or reflection.

**SL oral from an image**

Describe, interpret, connect (theme plus a target-language culture), judge.

## Worked reminders

- *Weak:* "The picture shows a robot in a hospital." *Stronger:* "The robot carries medicine, which suggests that hospitals are using machines to free nurses for patient care, although some patients may find it cold."
- *Weak:* "Social media is bad for friendship." *Stronger:* "Social media keeps friends in touch after they move away; however, a stream of likes can replace the longer conversations that friendship needs."

- *Weak:* "The mural is colourful and big." *Stronger:* "The mural shows fishermen and factory workers side by side, which suggests the town is proud of both its old and its new ways of earning a living."

|---|---|
| A new treatment (Identities: health and well-being) | The research, the breakthrough and who gains access |
| A festival's new light show (Experiences: customs and traditions) | The creative idea and how it changes the tradition |
| Online learning (Social organization: education) | The technology and what it changes in how people learn |
| Solar panels (Sharing the planet: the environment) | The innovation itself and who can afford it |

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Invention vs innovation | An invention is a new thing; an innovation is a new idea or method put into use, often an improvement to something that exists |
| Technology vs scientific innovation | Technology is tools and devices in use; scientific innovation is new knowledge from research, such as a new treatment |
| Entertainment vs artistic expressions | Entertainment is made mainly to amuse an audience; artistic expression is made mainly to express or represent. Many works are both |
| Media vs medium | "Medium" is one channel (radio); "the media" are the channels together, or the organisations behind them |
| Guiding principle vs possible questions | The principle frames the whole theme; the questions are examples of inquiry, not a fixed list |
| Recommended vs required | The five topics are optional recommended topics; unseen texts may take any angle on the theme |
| SL vs HL oral stimulus | SL: a visual image; HL: an excerpt from one of two literary works |

## Quick self-test

1. State the guiding principle of Human ingenuity.
2. Name the five optional recommended topics.
3. Give both possible questions listed for the theme.
4. What is the stimulus for the individual oral at SL, and at HL?
5. What weighting does Paper 1 carry, and how many tasks do you answer?
6. How many marks are the Paper 2 reading and listening sections worth?
7. Name the five conceptual understandings.
8. What is the difference between an invention and an innovation?
9. Rewrite as a speculation: "Robots do the cleaning. People have more free time."
10. Which step of the artwork method is most often missing in weak answers?
11. Which extra demands does HL Paper 1 make?
12. Give a counterpoint to: "Video calls have made distance irrelevant."

### Answers

1. Explore the ways in which human creativity and innovation affect our world.
2. Entertainment, artistic expressions, communication and media, technology, scientific innovation.
3. What can we learn about a culture through its artistic expression? How do the media change the way we relate to each other?
4. SL: a visual image clearly relevant to one or more themes. HL: an excerpt from one of the two literary works studied.
5. 25%; one writing task from a choice of three.
6. Reading 40 marks; listening 25 marks (each 25% of the course).
7. Context, audience, purpose, meaning, variation.
8. An invention is a new thing; an innovation is a new idea or method put into use, often improving something that exists.
9. "If robots did the cleaning, people would have more free time." Then write it in your target language with its conditional form.
10. Step 2, inferring what the art shows about its makers.
11. More complex language and structures, higher-order thinking, and a higher word range.
12. Any sound point, for example: a call cannot replace physical presence, and people without devices or connections are excluded.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an invention, image or artwork in detail and never saying what it changes or reveals.
- Answering a reading question with the right idea but the wrong detail, because a negative ("not yet", "never") was missed.
- Treating the five topics as the only possible content, then freezing on an unseen text about architecture, robotics or dance.
- One-sided arguments that only praise or only condemn technology.
- Translating English structures word for word instead of using the target language's own process or conditional forms.
- Writing the right content in the wrong text type in Paper 1, for example an essay when a review was asked for.
- Vague vocabulary ("thing", "good") where a specific headword from the fields above was available.
- In the SL oral, spending all the time describing the image and never reaching a connection to a target-language culture.
- At HL, having no theme vocabulary ready for the literary works you studied.
