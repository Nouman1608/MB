Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Accounting (4AC1) is not tiered and is assessed by two externally assessed written papers: Paper 1, Introduction to Bookkeeping and Accounting (4AC1/01, 2 hours, 100 marks, 66.6%), and Paper 2, Financial Statements (4AC1/02, 1 hour 15 minutes, 50 marks, 33.3%). Calculators are allowed on both. This page turns that structure into a plan, and pairs well with the topic notes on [business documentation and books of original entry](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-business-documentation-and-books-of-original-entry-revision-notes/) and [capital and revenue expenditure and depreciation](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-capital-and-revenue-expenditure-and-depreciation-revision-notes/).

## Paper 1: Introduction to Bookkeeping and Accounting

Paper 1 is the larger paper: 2 hours for 100 marks and two thirds of the qualification. Its title tells you its emphasis: the bookkeeping system and the accounting that grows out of it. Because it is long, the main risk is not running out of knowledge but running out of stamina and time on a single multi-step record-keeping question.

Practical advice for Paper 1:

- Revise the whole bookkeeping chain as one connected process, from source documents and books of original entry, through ledger accounts, to the trial balance and the corrections that follow it. A mistake early in the chain often explains an error later on.
- Practise ledger and journal work by hand, with proper headings, dates, narrations and balancing figures. Presentation is part of what an accounting answer is.
- Keep a short list of recurring conventions, such as how a ledger account is balanced off and carried down, so that you do not spend thinking time on routine layout.

## Paper 2: Financial Statements

Paper 2 is shorter: 1 hour 15 minutes for 50 marks, one third of the qualification. The paper title points to the preparation of financial statements, and the specification topics on the preparation of financial statements and accounting for end of period adjustments will matter most here, supported by the notes on [applying accounting concepts](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-application-of-accounting-concepts-revision-notes/). Because the paper is compact, each mark has to be earned quickly, and a layout you have rehearsed saves valuable minutes.

Practical advice for Paper 2:

- Rehearse the full layout of each statement until you can lay out the headings from memory, then fill in the figures.
- Work in a fixed order: set out the statement skeleton, place the figures you are certain of first, then deal with adjustments one at a time, noting each on the question paper as you apply it.
- Leave a few minutes to check that totals agree and that every adjustment has been applied once only.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

The record gives enough information to work out a pace for each paper.

- Paper 1: 120 minutes for 100 marks is 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 75 minutes for 50 marks is 75 / 50 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Overall: 195 minutes for 150 marks, or 1.3 minutes per mark.

So Paper 2 gives you more time per mark than Paper 1, which suits the careful layout of financial statements. A 10-mark ledger question on Paper 1 should take about 10 x 1.2 = 12 minutes, while a 10-mark section on Paper 2 can take about 10 x 1.5 = 15 minutes. These are averages, so build in a few minutes at the end of each paper for checking, and move on if one question is eating more than its share.

The weightings also tell you where practice time pays off: Paper 1 is worth twice as much as Paper 2 (66.6% against 33.3%), so give bookkeeping and the early part of the course a proportionate share of your revision, while still practising Paper 2 layouts regularly.

## Question styles and command words

Without relying on any particular past paper, accounting questions tend to fall into a few families, and it helps to recognise them:

- **Calculate** and **prepare**: produce a figure or a full statement or account, with working shown. Marks are normally available for correct method as well as correct totals, so write your workings next to the answer.
- **State** and **identify**: a short recall response, with no explanation needed.
- **Explain**: give a reason, normally in a full sentence that links a cause to an effect.
- **Comment on** and **advise**: use the figures in front of you to reach a short, justified view, and refer to the numbers rather than making generic remarks. Practise this with the ideas in [accounting ratios](/resources/edexcel-igcse-accounting-accounting-ratios-revision-notes/).

## Worked practice scenario: reducing-balance depreciation

Question: "A business buys a machine on the first day of its financial year for 24,000. It depreciates machinery at 20% per year on the reducing balance method. Calculate the depreciation charge and the net book value at the end of Year 1 and Year 2. Explain one reason a business might choose this method."

```
Year 1
Depreciation = 20% x 24,000                = 4,800
Net book value = 24,000 - 4,800            = 19,200

Year 2
Depreciation = 20% x 19,200                = 3,840
Net book value = 19,200 - 3,840            = 15,360
```

Explanation: reducing balance charges a larger amount in the early years (4,800 then 3,840), which suits an asset that loses more of its value, or is more productive, when it is new. In the second year the charge is applied to the net book value of 19,200, not the original cost of 24,000, and that is the usual slip to avoid. A neat journal or ledger entry for each year would debit the depreciation expense and credit the provision for depreciation, with a narration.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check the date, paper code (4AC1/01 or 4AC1/02) and time allowed, and pack a permitted calculator.
- Write a rough time plan on the question paper using 1.2 minutes per mark for Paper 1 and 1.5 for Paper 2.
- Read the whole question before writing; underline the instruction and any dates or rates.
- Show every working next to the final figure and use clear headings on ledger accounts and statements.
- Apply each adjustment once, and tick it off as you go.
- Check that totals agree and that numbers carried between statements match.
- Do not leave a calculation blank: a method with a wrong figure can still earn marks.
- Use the last minutes to check units, signs and labels.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Edexcel IGCSE Accounting 4AC1?**
There are two externally assessed written papers and no tiers: Paper 1 (4AC1/01) and Paper 2 (4AC1/02).

**How long is each paper and how many marks is it worth?**
Paper 1 lasts 2 hours and is worth 100 marks (66.6%). Paper 2 lasts 1 hour 15 minutes and is worth 50 marks (33.3%).

**Can I use a calculator?**
Yes, the record shows calculators may be used on both papers, so practise with the calculator you will bring.

**When is the qualification assessed?**
It is a linear qualification, with the January and June series, and first assessment was in 2019. The modular route 4XAC1 is not covered on this page.

**What is the best way to spread revision between the two papers?**
Give Paper 1 the larger share because it carries twice the weighting, but practise preparing full financial statements against the clock throughout, since Paper 2 gives only 1 hour 15 minutes.
