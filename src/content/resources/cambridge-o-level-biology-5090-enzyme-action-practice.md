## Section A

**1.** Which statement describes a catalyst?

A: It is used up as it speeds up a reaction.

B: It increases the rate of a reaction and is not changed by it.

C: It is always a protein.

D: It increases the amount of product that can be made from a fixed amount of substrate.

**[1]**

**2.** What is the product when maltase acts on its substrate?

A: amino acids

B: fatty acids and glycerol

C: glucose

D: maltose

**[1]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by an enzyme. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by the term "active site". **[2]**

**5.** Explain why enzymes are needed for metabolic reactions in living organisms. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** The diagram in a textbook shows four stages of an enzyme-controlled reaction: W, enzyme and substrate separate; X, substrate in the active site; Y, products in the active site; Z, products leaving.

**(a)** Name the structure formed at stage X. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the enzyme at stage Z can take part in the reaction again. **[2]**

**(c)** Is this a breakdown or a synthesis reaction? Give a reason. **[1]**

**7.** Explain, using the lock-and-key hypothesis, why amylase catalyses the breakdown of starch but not of protein. **[4]**

**8.** Copy and complete the table.

| Enzyme | Substrate | Product(s) |
|---|---|---|
| Amylase | Starch | ? |
| ? | Protein | Amino acids |
| Lipase | ? | Fatty acids and glycerol |
| Maltase | Maltose | ? |

**[4]**

**9.** An enzyme called urease breaks down a substance called urea. A student mixes urease separately with urea and with glucose.

**(a)** Suggest which mixture shows a reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer. **[3]**

**10.** A student writes: "The substrate has the same shape as the active site, so the enzyme reacts with it and is used up." Identify and correct **two** errors in this statement. **[4]**

**11.** Explain why the digestive system produces several different enzymes rather than one. **[3]**

**12.** Explain how an enzyme catalyses a synthesis reaction. **[3]**

**13.** Explain why a change in the shape of an enzyme molecule can stop it working. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* D is a common distractor: a catalyst changes how fast product forms, not how much can form.

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A protein [1] that functions as a biological catalyst, increasing the rate of a metabolic reaction without being changed [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The region of an enzyme where the substrate binds [1]; it has a shape complementary to the substrate [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Metabolic reactions would be too slow at body temperature to keep the organism alive [1]. Raising the temperature enough to speed them up would damage cells [1]. Enzymes increase the rate of reaction at the organism's normal temperature [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Enzymes speed up reactions" repeats the definition; the marks are for explaining why living things cannot manage without them.

**6. (a)** Enzyme-substrate complex [1].

**(b)** The enzyme is not changed by the reaction [1], so once the products leave, the active site is free to bind another substrate molecule [1].

**(c)** Breakdown: one substrate is split into two or more products [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The active site of amylase has a specific shape [1] that is complementary to starch [1], so starch fits and forms an enzyme-substrate complex [1]. Protein has a different shape that does not fit the active site, so no complex forms and no reaction occurs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both halves are needed: why starch does react and why protein does not. Answers that explain only one side are capped at two marks.

**8.** Maltose [1]; protease [1]; lipids [1]; glucose [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Urease with urea [1].

**(b)** Urea has a shape complementary to the active site of urease [1], so it binds and an enzyme-substrate complex forms [1]. Glucose has a different shape, so it cannot fit the active site and is not broken down [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* You do not need to know urease; the name tells you the substrate, and the explanation uses shape and fit.

**10.** Error 1: "same shape" [1]; correction: the substrate's shape is complementary to the active site [1]. Error 2: "used up" [1]; correction: the enzyme is not changed by the reaction and can be reused [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Enzymes are specific [1]: each active site fits only one substrate shape [1], so different enzymes are needed for different food molecules, such as amylase for starch, protease for protein and lipase for lipids [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the specific enzymes with their substrates turns a general statement into a full answer.

**12.** Two (or more) substrate molecules bind in the active site [1], forming an enzyme-substrate complex that holds them close together [1]. They are joined into one product, which is released, leaving the enzyme unchanged [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Synthesis answers use the same terms as breakdown answers; only the direction changes, with substrates joined rather than split.

**13.** The substrate fits the active site because of its complementary shape [1]; if the enzyme's shape changes, the active site changes, so the substrate no longer fits and no enzyme-substrate complex forms [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* This idea links 5.1 to 5.2: heat and extreme pH stop enzymes working by changing this shape.

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "same shape" instead of "complementary".
- Saying enzymes are used up or are living.
- Missing the enzyme-substrate complex from an explanation.
- Giving only half of the catalyst definition.
- Not using the name of an unfamiliar enzyme to identify its substrate.
