**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Belief in Angels: "the qualities, importance and role of angels, including Jibril (AS), Mika'il (AS), Isra'fil (AS), Malak-al-Mowth (angel of death) (AS) and Kiraman Katibeen (AS)" and "the Hadith of Jibril, including its key teachings".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 35:1. The [Islam: Beliefs and Teachings study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-islamiyat-beliefs-and-teachings/) mentions angels briefly. This page goes through qualities, named angels, importance and the Hadith of Jibril in the order examiners tend to ask about them.

## What angels are

Angels (*malaikah*, singular *malak*) are beings created by Allah from light. A Hadith in Sahih Muslim says that angels were created from light, jinn from smokeless fire, and Adam from what has been described in the Qur'an, which is clay. They belong to the unseen (*ghayb*), so Muslims believe in them because the Qur'an and the Prophet (pbuh) say so, not because they can be seen. Belief in angels is one of the six key beliefs (Qur'an 2:285).

Muslims respect angels as honoured servants of Allah. They are not worshipped, not divine, and not "daughters of Allah", an idea the Qur'an rejects (43:19). They have no share in Allah's power.

## Qualities of angels

Learn these as a list, with a proof or example for each.

| Quality | Meaning | Evidence or example |
|---|---|---|
| **Created from light** | A different nature from humans and jinn | Hadith, Sahih Muslim |
| **Perfect obedience** | They never disobey Allah | "They do not disobey Allah in what He commands them but do what they are commanded" (66:6) |
| **Constant worship** | They glorify Allah day and night without tiring | "They exalt [Him] night and day [and] do not slacken" (21:20) |
| **Wings** | Some have two, three, four or more | "Who made the angels messengers having wings, two or three or four" (35:1) |
| **Do not eat or marry** | They do not need food or family life | The angels who visited Ibraheem (AS) did not reach for food (11:69-70) |
| **Not male or female in human terms** | They are not described as having a family | Qur'an rejects calling them females (43:19) |
| **Can appear in human form** | Allah allows this for His purposes | Jibril (AS) appeared to Maryam as a man (19:17) |
| **Very many** | Their number is known only to Allah | The Prophet (pbuh) said that seventy thousand angels visit the Bayt al-Ma'mur in heaven each day (Bukhari and Muslim) |

## Named angels and their roles

### Jibril (AS)

Jibril (Gabriel) is the angel of revelation and is described as the most honoured of angels. He brought Allah's messages to the prophets, and above all the Qur'an to Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh): "it is he who has brought it down upon your heart by permission of Allah" (2:97). He met the Prophet (pbuh) in the Cave of Hira, accompanied him on the night journey, and, according to Hadith, reviewed the Qur'an with him each Ramadhan. He also taught the Prophet (pbuh) the times of prayer.

### Mika'il (AS)

Mika'il (Michael) is named in the Qur'an beside Jibril (2:98). Islamic tradition says that he is in charge of rain and the growth of plants, so he is linked to provision. His role reminds Muslims that sustenance comes by Allah's command.

### Isra'fil (AS)

Isra'fil (Israfil) is the angel who will blow the trumpet (*as-Sur*) twice: first so that all creation dies, and then so that all are raised for judgement (39:68). His name comes from Hadith, while the Qur'an describes the trumpet. His role links angels to belief in the Last Day.

### Malak-al-Mowth (AS)

Malak-al-Mowth, the angel of death, takes the soul at the time Allah has fixed: "Say, 'The angel of death will take you, who has been entrusted with you'" (32:11). He works with helper angels. Popular tradition sometimes uses the name Azrail, but that name is not found in the Qur'an. This role teaches that death comes only by Allah's order and at the appointed time.

### Kiraman Katibeen (AS)

The *Kiraman Katibeen* are the "noble recorders". "And indeed, [appointed] over you are keepers, noble and recording; they know whatever you do" (82:10-12). Two angels accompany each person, one on the right and one on the left, recording deeds and words (50:17-18). Their records will be shown on the Day of Judgement.

| Angel | Main role | Link to another belief |
|---|---|---|
| Jibril | Revelation | Kutub, Risalah |
| Mika'il | Rain and provision | Allah as Provider |
| Isra'fil | Trumpet | Yaum e Akhirah |
| Malak-al-Mowth | Taking souls | Death and Barzakh |
| Kiraman Katibeen | Recording deeds | Accountability |

## Other tasks of angels

- **Praising Allah** and asking forgiveness for believers (40:7).
- **Helping believers**, as at Badr, when Allah says He will reinforce the Muslims with a thousand angels (8:9).
- **Guarding** people by Allah's command (13:11).
- **Greeting** the righteous in Paradise, and assigning the punishments of Hell.

## Why belief in angels is important

1. **It is part of faith.** Rejecting angels would be rejecting something Allah has stated.
2. **It protects trust in revelation.** The Qur'an reached the Prophet (pbuh) through Jibril (AS), so belief in angels supports belief in the Qur'an.
3. **It builds accountability.** Muslims believe their words and actions are recorded, which discourages wrongdoing in private.
4. **It brings comfort.** Angels support believers, and the Prophet (pbuh) taught that angels attend gatherings of remembrance.
5. **It shows Allah's greatness.** Allah rules unseen creatures and has no need of help.
6. **It teaches obedience.** Angels do exactly what they are told; humans are free and can earn great reward through choosing obedience.

A student should link **role to importance**: for example, "the Kiraman Katibeen record deeds, so a Muslim tries to behave well even when alone".

## The Hadith of Jibril

The Hadith of Jibril is among the most important Hadith because it summarises the religion in a single event. One day, as the companions sat with the Prophet (pbuh), a man appeared in clean white clothes, with no sign of travel and not known to anyone present. He sat close to the Prophet (pbuh), asked questions and, after he left, the Prophet (pbuh) said that this was Jibril (AS), who "came to teach the people their religion". The version printed in the specification is from Sahih al-Bukhari. Other reports (Sahih Muslim) give more detail and add belief in the decree.

**Key teachings.** Jibril asked four questions:

| Question | Answer in the Hadith | Teaching |
|---|---|---|
| What is faith (*iman*)? | To believe in Allah, His angels, meeting Him, His messengers and the Resurrection (Muslim's report adds the books and the decree) | The beliefs of the heart |
| What is Islam? | To worship Allah alone, pray, give Zakah and fast Ramadhan (Muslim's report lists the Shahadah and Hajj as well) | The actions of the body |
| What is *ihsan* (excellence)? | To worship Allah as if you see Him, and if you do not see Him, He sees you | Sincerity and awareness of Allah |
| When is the Hour? | The one asked knows no more than the one asking; only Allah knows | Humility about the unseen |

**What it shows:**

- Religion has three levels: belief, practice and excellence.
- An angel can come in human form to teach.
- The date of the Hour is hidden, and only signs of it are given.
- Faith and action belong together.

This connects directly to angels: the event itself proves that Jibril (AS) can appear as a man, and its teachings repeat belief in angels as part of faith.

## The passage: Qur'an 35:1

> "[All] praise is [due] to Allah, Creator of the heavens and the earth, [who] made the angels messengers having wings, two or three or four. He increases in creation what He wills. Indeed, Allah is over all things competent."

Use it for four points: praise belongs to Allah (angels glorify Him); Allah is Creator (angels are created, not divine); angels are "messengers" (they carry out His commands, including revelation); and "He increases in creation what He wills" and "over all things competent" (the angels do not limit Allah's power, which links to Qudrah).

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** roles of angels in Islam. **[4]**

> Jibril (AS) brought the revelation of the Qur'an to the Prophet (pbuh) [1], so Muslims believe the message comes from Allah and was delivered without change [1]. The Kiraman Katibeen record every person's words and deeds [1], which reminds Muslims that they will be held to account on the Day of Judgement [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Saying angels have free will like humans.** Muslims believe angels obey perfectly; humans and jinn have choice.
- **Saying Iblis was an angel.** The Qur'an says he was one of the jinn (18:50).
- **Naming roles without meaning.** Add why each role matters.
- **Giving the Hadith of Jibril as a story only.** Give the four questions and answers.
- **Forgetting the passage.** In a 12-mark answer, use "messengers having wings" and "Creator of the heavens and the earth".
