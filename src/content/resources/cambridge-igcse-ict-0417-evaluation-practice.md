## Section A: short answers

**1.** State **three** qualities of a solution that are judged during evaluation. **[3]**

**2.** Name the document produced during analysis that the new system is compared with during evaluation. **[1]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a *limitation* and an *improvement*. **[2]**

**4.** Give **two** methods of collecting users' responses to a new system. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why evaluation should not take place on the first day the system is used. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Greenway Taxis introduced a new online booking system six months ago. Its original requirements included:

- customers can book a taxi in under two minutes;
- drivers receive bookings on a tablet in their car;
- customers receive a text message when the taxi is on its way.

**(a)** Describe how the evaluator would check whether these requirements have been met. **[3]**

**(b)** Customers say the text message often arrives after the taxi. State what this shows about the requirements and suggest an improvement. **[2]**

**7.** Describe how the efficiency of Greenway's new system could be evaluated. Give **two** examples of evidence. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why a system could be efficient but still not easy to use. Give an example. **[3]**

**9.** Ashby Community Centre, run by volunteers, has bought a complex booking system designed for large conference venues.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by the *appropriateness* of a solution. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the new system may not be appropriate for the community centre. **[2]**

**10.** After the evaluation of a school's new reporting system, three limitations are found. Suggest a necessary improvement for each.

**(a)** Teachers cannot see reports from previous years. **[1]**

**(b)** The system crashes when more than 30 teachers log in at once. **[1]**

**(c)** Parents with visual impairments find the online reports hard to read. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why the users' responses to the results of testing should be included in an evaluation. **[3]**

**12.** A hotel has used a new reservations system for four months. Describe how the system should be evaluated. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Efficiency [1]; ease of use [1]; appropriateness [1]. **[3]**

**2.** The requirements specification / the original task requirements [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A limitation is something the system cannot do or does poorly [1]; an improvement is a specific change that would remove a limitation [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two of: questionnaires [1]; interviews [1]; feedback forms [1]; observing users [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**5.** Users are not yet familiar with the system, so their views would reflect unfamiliarity rather than the system's quality [1]; there would not yet be enough real use to measure efficiency or find limitations [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Go through each requirement in turn [1]; for example, time a sample of customers making bookings and check the average is under two minutes [1]; check with drivers that bookings appear on their tablets, and check records of text messages against taxi arrival times [1].

**(b)** The third requirement has only been partly met / not met [1]. Improvement: send the text as soon as the driver accepts the booking, using the car's GPS position to estimate arrival / send it earlier [1]. **[5]**

**7.** Compare how the new system uses time and resources with the old system [1]. Any two examples of evidence: average time to take a booking before and after [1]; number of call-handling staff needed [1]; number of booking errors or missed bookings [1]; time taxis spend waiting between jobs [1]. Maximum 3. **[3]**

**8.** Efficiency is about how quickly and with what resources the system does its work, while ease of use is about how easily the users can operate it [1]. A system can process data very quickly but have confusing screens or need users to remember codes [1]. Example: a stock system that updates instantly but needs staff to type long product codes from memory instead of selecting from a list [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Whether the solution suits the organisation, its users and the task it is meant for [1].

**(b)** The volunteers may not have the skills or time to learn a complex system [1]; it has many features for large conferences that a small centre does not need, and may cost more than the centre can afford [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Store earlier years' reports in the database and add an option to search or select by year [1].

**(b)** Upgrade the server or network so that it can handle more users at the same time [1].

**(c)** Allow text size and contrast to be changed, or make reports compatible with screen readers [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Users are the people who will rely on the system, so they can judge whether the results meet their needs in practice [1]; they may point out problems that the measured evidence missed, such as confusing outputs [1]; their responses show whether faults found in testing have been fixed to their satisfaction, which helps decide what improvements are needed [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Any six of:
- Compare the system with the original task requirements, checking whether each has been met [1].
- Evaluate efficiency, for example by timing how long a reservation takes compared with the old system [1].
- Evaluate ease of use, for example by asking reception staff in a questionnaire or interview [1].
- Evaluate appropriateness: whether the system suits the hotel's size, staff skills and needs [1].
- Collect and evaluate users' responses to the results of testing [1].
- Identify limitations, for example the system cannot handle group bookings [1].
- Suggest necessary improvements to match each limitation, for example adding a group booking option [1].
Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Is evaluation the same as testing?**
No. Testing checks that each part works correctly before implementation. Evaluation judges the whole system against its requirements once it is in use.

**How do I make a "describe how to evaluate" answer specific?**
Use the syllabus phrases and attach an example from the scenario to each one.
