## Section A

**1.** State what the symbol ⇌ shows in a chemical equation. **[1]**

**2.** Blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated. State the colour of the solid formed, and state how the original colour can be restored. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** features of a reversible reaction at equilibrium in a closed system. **[2]**

**4.** Write the symbol equation, with state symbols, for the production of ammonia in the Haber process. **[2]**

**5.** State the temperature, pressure and catalyst used in the Haber process. **[3]**

**6.** State **two** sources of the sulfur dioxide used in the Contact process. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Methanol can be made by this reversible reaction. The forward reaction is exothermic.

CO(g) + 2H₂(g) ⇌ CH₃OH(g)

Predict and explain the effect on the position of equilibrium of:

**(a)** increasing the temperature

**(b)** increasing the pressure

**(c)** adding a catalyst **[6]**

**8.** Iron(III) ions react reversibly with thiocyanate ions.

Fe³⁺(aq) + SCN⁻(aq) ⇌ FeSCN²⁺(aq)
pale yellow   colourless   deep red

**(a)** Predict what is seen when more thiocyanate ions are added to the equilibrium mixture, and explain your answer.

**(b)** Predict what is seen when a substance that removes Fe³⁺ ions is added. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why changing the pressure has no effect on the position of equilibrium for H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g). **[2]**

**10.** In the Haber process the forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** Explain why a temperature of 450 °C is used, rather than a lower or higher temperature.

**(b)** Explain why a pressure of 200 atm is used. **[6]**

**11.** In the Contact process, sulfur dioxide is converted to sulfur trioxide at 2 atm using a vanadium(V) oxide catalyst.

**(a)** Explain why a high pressure is not used, even though there are fewer gas molecules on the right-hand side.

**(b)** State the effect of the catalyst on the yield of sulfur trioxide. **[3]**

**12.** Describe how anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride is used to test for water, and explain the test in terms of a reversible reaction. **[3]**

**13.** A student writes: "At equilibrium, the reaction has stopped and there are equal amounts of reactants and products." Correct **both** errors in the statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The reaction is reversible / can go in both directions [1].

**2.** White [1]. Add water to the white solid [1].

**3.** The rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction [1]. The concentrations of reactants and products are no longer changing [1].

**4.** N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ correctly balanced [1]; ⇌ sign with (g) state symbols throughout [1].

**5.** 450 °C [1]; 20 000 kPa / 200 atm [1]; iron catalyst [1].

**6.** Burning sulfur [1]; roasting sulfide ores [1].

**7. (a)** The equilibrium shifts to the left [1], because increasing the temperature favours the endothermic direction, which is the reverse reaction [1].

**(b)** The equilibrium shifts to the right [1], because there are 3 gas molecules on the left and only 1 on the right, and higher pressure favours the side with fewer gas molecules [1].

**(c)** No change in position [1]; the catalyst speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is only reached faster [1].

**8. (a)** The mixture turns a deeper red [1], because adding a reactant shifts the equilibrium to the right, forming more FeSCN²⁺ [1].

**(b)** The red colour fades / becomes paler, as equilibrium shifts to the left [1].

**9.** There are 2 gas molecules on each side of the equation [1], so neither side is favoured by a change in pressure [1].

**10. (a)** A lower temperature would give a higher yield, because the forward reaction is exothermic [1], but the rate would be too slow [1]. A higher temperature would be faster but would give a lower yield, so 450 °C is a compromise between rate and yield [1].

**(b)** High pressure shifts the equilibrium to the right, because there are fewer gas molecules on the right (2 compared with 4), increasing the yield [1]; it also increases the rate [1]. Higher pressures would be more expensive and more dangerous, so 200 atm balances yield against cost and safety [1].

**11. (a)** The position of equilibrium already lies far to the right at low pressure, so a high yield is obtained [1]; high-pressure equipment is expensive and hazardous, and would give little extra yield [1].

**(b)** No effect on the yield [1].

**12.** Add the liquid to blue anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride (or cobalt chloride paper) [1]; it turns pink if water is present [1]. Water reacts with the anhydrous salt to re-form the hydrated salt, CoCl₂·6H₂O, which is the reverse of the dehydration reaction [1].

**13.** Both the forward and reverse reactions are still happening, at equal rates [1]. The concentrations of reactants and products are constant, but not necessarily equal [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 4: using a single arrow instead of ⇌, or writing NH₄ for ammonia.
- Question 5: giving 200 kPa (the Contact pressure) for the Haber process.
- Question 7(b): counting 2 molecules on the left by ignoring the coefficient of H₂.
- Question 7(c): stating that the catalyst shifts equilibrium to the right.
- Question 8: giving the correct colour without the explanation in terms of shifting equilibrium.
- Question 10(a): explaining only the yield. Rate is the reason a lower temperature is not chosen.
- Question 10(b): ignoring safety and cost, which the syllabus asks you to include.
- Question 12: describing the colour change the wrong way round. Anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride is blue and turns pink.
- Question 13: correcting only one error. "Stopped" and "equal amounts" are both wrong, and each correction earns a separate mark.
- Question 11(a): saying high pressure "would not increase the yield". It would, slightly; the point is that the gain is too small to justify the cost.
