These notes condense section 2.3 (demand) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. All figures are invented. Full explanations are in the [demand study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-demand-cambridge-igcse-economics/); then test yourself with the [demand practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-demand-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/).

## 2.3.1 Definition and diagram

- **Demand:** the willingness and ability to buy a product at a given price over a given period of time.
- **Demand schedule:** a table of quantities demanded at different prices.
- **Demand curve:** the schedule drawn as a graph; price on the vertical axis, quantity on the horizontal axis; slopes down from left to right.
- **Why it slopes down:** a lower price makes the product cheaper than alternatives, lets buyers afford more and brings in new buyers.

## 2.3.2 Movements along the curve

| Price change | Effect on quantity demanded | Term | Direction on the curve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price falls | Quantity demanded rises | Extension in demand | Down the curve |
| Price rises | Quantity demanded falls | Contraction in demand | Up the curve |

Only a change in the product's **own price** causes a movement.

Example: price of a sandwich rises from 3.00 to 3.60; quantity demanded falls from 500 to 410 a day.

```
Contraction = 500 - 410 = 90 sandwiches a day
Percentage fall = 90 / 500 x 100 = 18%
Spending before = 3.00 x 500 = 1,500; after = 3.60 x 410 = 1,476
```

## 2.3.3 Individual and market demand

- **Individual demand:** one buyer's demand.
- **Market demand:** the sum of all individual demands at each price (aggregation).
- Add quantities across at each price; never add prices.

| Price | Buyer X | Buyer Y | Buyer Z | Market |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 3 |
| 4 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 6 |
| 3 | 5 | 3 | 2 | 10 |

More buyers in the market shift market demand to the right.

## 2.3.4 Conditions of demand (shifts)

| Increase in demand (shift right) | Decrease in demand (shift left) |
|---|---|
| Higher disposable income (most products) | Lower disposable income |
| Substitute becomes dearer | Substitute becomes cheaper |
| Complement becomes cheaper | Complement becomes dearer |
| Favourable change in tastes or fashion | Product goes out of fashion or is linked with harm |
| Successful advertising | A rival's successful advertising |
| Larger population or more buyers in the relevant age group | Smaller population or fewer buyers |
| Lower income tax | Higher income tax |
| Lower interest rates, easier credit | Higher interest rates, harder credit |
| Favourable weather or season | Unfavourable weather or season |

- **Substitutes:** used instead of each other (rice and pasta).
- **Complements:** used together (cars and petrol).

Example: demand for umbrellas increases by 40% at every price during the rainy season.

```
At a price of 12: old quantity 300 -> new quantity 300 x 1.4 = 420
At a price of 15: old quantity 220 -> new quantity 220 x 1.4 = 308
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demand | Willingness and ability to buy at a given price over a given period |
| Quantity demanded | The amount buyers want at one particular price |
| Extension in demand | Rise in quantity demanded caused by a fall in the product's price |
| Contraction in demand | Fall in quantity demanded caused by a rise in the product's price |
| Increase in demand | More demanded at every price; curve shifts right |
| Decrease in demand | Less demanded at every price; curve shifts left |
| Market demand | Total of individual demands at each price |
| Substitute | A product that can replace another |
| Complement | A product used together with another |

## The movement or shift test

1. Did the product's own price change? Movement (extension or contraction).
2. Did anything else change? Shift (increase or decrease).
3. At the same price, is more now bought? Increase. Less? Decrease.

## Exam traps

- "Demand rises" after a price fall: wrong term. Write "quantity demanded extends".
- Shifting the curve for an own-price change.
- Price and quantity axes swapped.
- No time period in the definition.
- Complements and substitutes reversed.
- Adding the prices in an aggregation table.
- Drawing a shift without labelling D1, D2 and the new quantity.

## Quick self-test

1. Define demand.
2. Which axis shows price on a demand diagram?
3. The price of a product rises. Name the change in demand and the direction of movement.
4. Price falls from 8 to 6 and quantity demanded rises from 250 to 330. Calculate the percentage rise in quantity demanded.
5. Give the term for more being demanded at every price.
6. Using the buyer table above, what is market demand at a price of 4?
7. The price of coffee rises. What happens to demand for tea, and why?
8. The price of petrol rises. What happens to demand for large cars?
9. Name two conditions of demand other than income.
10. Demand rises by 20% at every price. At a price of 10, the old quantity was 450. Find the new quantity.
11. Why does a cut in income tax shift demand for many products?
12. An extra buyer joins a market. What happens to the market demand curve?

### Answers

1. The willingness and ability to buy a product at a given price over a given period of time.
2. The vertical axis.
3. Contraction in demand; movement up the curve.
4. 80 / 250 x 100 = 32%.
5. An increase in demand.
6. 3 + 2 + 1 = 6.
7. It increases, because tea is a substitute and is now relatively cheaper.
8. It decreases, because petrol is a complement and using large cars costs more.
9. Any two: prices of substitutes or complements, tastes, advertising, population, income tax, interest rates, weather.
10. 450 x 1.2 = 540.
11. Disposable income rises, so households can afford more at every price.
12. It shifts to the right.
