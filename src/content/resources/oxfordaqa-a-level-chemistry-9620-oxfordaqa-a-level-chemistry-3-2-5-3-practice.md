These questions cover **3.2.5.3 Shapes of complex ions** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 3 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [shapes of complex ions study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-3/) or the [shapes of complex ions revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-5-3-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which complex is linear?

A: [CuCl₄]²⁻

B: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺

C: [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂]

D: [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ **[1]**

**2.** State the shape of [Co(NH₃)₆]³⁺ and the angle between two adjacent N–Co bonds. **[2]**

**3.** Give the co-ordination number of the metal in each complex.

**(a)** [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ **[1]**

**(b)** [Cu(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ **[1]**

**4.** Excess concentrated hydrochloric acid is added to a solution of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Give the formula of the cobalt complex formed and its shape. **[2]**

**5.** State why [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ cannot show cis–trans isomerism, although [Ni(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ can. **[1]**

**6.** Cisplatin is [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂].

**(a)** State the shape of cisplatin. **[1]**

**(b)** State the Cl–Pt–Cl bond angle in cisplatin. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** Chromium(III) forms the complex ion [Cr(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺.

**(a)** Show that chromium has an oxidation state of +3 in this complex. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the shape of the complex and state its co-ordination number. **[2]**

**(c)** Draw the two stereoisomers of the complex and label each one cis or trans. **[2]**

**(d)** State the type of stereoisomerism shown and explain why it is described as a special case of E–Z isomerism. **[2]**

**8.** Iron(III) forms the complex ion [Fe(C₂O₄)₃]³⁻ with ethanedioate ions.

**(a)** Explain why ethanedioate is described as a bidentate ligand. **[1]**

**(b)** The complex exists as two stereoisomers. Name the type of stereoisomerism and explain why it occurs. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why one ethanedioate ion cannot bond to two positions that are opposite each other (180° apart) in the octahedron. **[1]**

**9.** Cobalt(III) forms the complex [Co(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₂Cl₂]⁺.

**(a)** Deduce the total number of stereoisomers of this complex. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your answer to (a), referring to symmetry. **[3]**

**10.** A student writes: "Tetrahedral and square planar complexes both have four ligands, so both can show cis–trans isomerism."

**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong about tetrahedral complexes. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why neither shape of four-co-ordinate complex shows optical isomerism when the ligands are all monodentate and of only two kinds, A and B, in the ratio 2 : 2. **[2]**

**11.** A solution of copper(II) sulfate contains [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺. Separate portions are treated as described.

**(a)** Excess concentrated hydrochloric acid is added. Write an equation for the reaction and give the shape of the copper complex formed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the co-ordination number changes in (a). **[1]**

**(c)** A solution of EDTA⁴⁻ is added to a second portion. Write an equation for the reaction and state the shape of the copper complex formed. **[2]**

**12.** Tollens' reagent is made by adding aqueous ammonia to silver nitrate solution until the precipitate first formed just dissolves.

**(a)** Give the formula of the silver complex in Tollens' reagent. **[1]**

**(b)** State its shape and bond angle, and explain the shape in terms of electron pairs. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why this complex cannot exist as stereoisomers. **[1]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Octahedral [1]; 90° [1]

**Total: 2**

**3.**

**(a)** 6 (three bidentate ligands, 3 × 2) [1]

**(b)** 6 (2 × 2 + 2 × 1) [1]

**Total: 2**

**4.** [CoCl₄]²⁻ [1]; tetrahedral [1]

**Total: 2**

*Examiner insight:* The charge must be 2−: Co(+2) plus four Cl⁻ (−4). A formula with the wrong charge does not score.

**5.** All six ligands in [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺ are identical, so there is only one arrangement; cis–trans isomerism needs two ligands of a different kind that can be adjacent or opposite [1]

**6.**

**(a)** Square planar [1]

**(b)** 90° [1]

**Total: 2**

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Cr + 4(0) + 2(−1) = +1, so Cr = +3 [1]

**(b)** Octahedral [1]; co-ordination number 6 [1]

**(c)** Octahedral drawing with the two Cl ligands at 90°, labelled cis [1]; octahedral drawing with the two Cl ligands at 180°, labelled trans [1]

**(d)** Cis–trans isomerism [1]; like E–Z isomers, the isomers have the same structural formula but a different arrangement in space, with the identical groups on the same side or opposite sides [1]

**Total: 7**

*Examiner insight:* Drawings must look three-dimensional. A flat cross with six bonds is accepted only if the cis and trans positions are clearly 90° and 180° apart.

**8.**

**(a)** It forms two co-ordinate bonds to the metal ion, each from a lone pair on a different oxygen atom [1]

**(b)** Optical isomerism [1]; the two forms are mirror images of each other [1]; they are non-superimposable because the complex has no plane of symmetry [1]

**(c)** The two donor oxygen atoms are close together in a short ligand, so they can only reach adjacent positions, 90° apart [1]

**Total: 5**

**9.**

**(a)** 3 [1]

**(b)** The two Cl⁻ ligands can be trans (180°) or cis (90°) [1]; the trans isomer has a plane of symmetry, so it is not chiral [1]; the cis isomer has no plane of symmetry, so it exists as two non-superimposable mirror images [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* "Two isomers, cis and trans" is the most common wrong answer. The cis form of this type of complex is always chiral.

**10.**

**(a)** In a tetrahedron every position is adjacent to every other position (all angles 109.5°) [1]; so there is no "opposite" arrangement, and only one form of [MA₂B₂] exists [1]

**(b)** The square planar complex has a plane of symmetry, the plane of the molecule [1]; the tetrahedral [MA₂B₂] complex also has a plane of symmetry (through M, both A and the midpoint of B–M–B), so its mirror image is superimposable [1]

**Total: 4**

**11.**

**(a)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O [1]; tetrahedral [1]

**(b)** Cl⁻ is larger than H₂O, so only four ligands fit around the copper ion [1]

**(c)** [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O [1]; octahedral [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* Check the charges: EDTA⁴⁻ with Cu²⁺ gives an overall charge of 2−.

**12.**

**(a)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ [1]

**(b)** Linear [1]; 180° [1]; the two bonding pairs (co-ordinate bonds from the nitrogen lone pairs) repel each other and get as far apart as possible [1]

**(c)** With only two ligands, both identical and opposite each other, there is only one possible arrangement in space [1]

**Total: 5**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the number of ligands instead of the number of co-ordinate bonds.
- Giving tetrahedral complexes cis–trans isomers.
- Counting only two stereoisomers for complexes of the type [M(bidentate)₂X₂].
- Writing the wrong overall charge on a complex after ligand substitution.
- Flat drawings of octahedral complexes in which cis and trans cannot be told apart.
