This guide covers subtopic **4.1 Geometrical terms** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. It is in both tiers and has three parts: use and interpret a list of geometrical terms; use and interpret the vocabulary of triangles, special quadrilaterals, polygons, nets and solids; and use and interpret the vocabulary of a circle. The syllabus notes add that candidates are **not expected to show that two shapes are congruent**.

The Core and Extended lists differ in a few words:

| Area | Both tiers | Extended only |
|---|---|---|
| General terms | point, vertex, line, parallel, perpendicular, bearing, right angle, acute, obtuse and reflex angles, interior and exterior angles, similar, congruent, scale factor | plane, perpendicular bisector |
| Solids | cube, cuboid, prism, cylinder, pyramid, cone, sphere, face, surface, edge | hemisphere, frustum (the Core notes say "hemisphere" is not required) |
| Circle | centre, radius (plural radii), diameter, circumference, semicircle, chord, tangent, arc, sector, segment | major and minor arc |

A glossary of every term, with tables, is in the [geometrical terms, constructions and scale drawings study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-terms-constructions-and-scale-drawings/). This page is about **using** the words accurately: classifying, choosing the most precise name, reading descriptions and avoiding the near-misses that cost marks. Afterwards, use the [geometrical terms revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-terms-revision-notes/) and the [geometrical terms practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-terms-practice/).

## Angles: naming and classifying

An angle can be named by its vertex letter alone when there is no doubt, or by three letters with the vertex in the **middle**: angle PQR is at Q, between QP and QR.

| Type | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | between 0° and 90° |
| Right angle | exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | between 90° and 180° |
| Reflex | between 180° and 360° |

At any point, an angle and its reflex angle make 360°.

### Worked example 1: reflex angles

Angle PQR = 128°. State the type of angle, and find the reflex angle PQR.

```
128 degrees is between 90 and 180: obtuse.
Reflex angle PQR = 360 - 128 = 232 degrees.
```

An **interior angle** of a polygon is inside the shape at a vertex. An **exterior angle** is between one side and the extension of the next side. They lie on a straight line, so interior + exterior = 180°. If an exterior angle is 40°, the interior angle at that vertex is 140°. Calculating angle sums is subtopic 4.6.

## Quadrilaterals: families and the most precise name

The special quadrilaterals form a family. A square has every property of a rectangle and of a rhombus, so it **is** both. A rectangle and a rhombus are both parallelograms.

| If a shape is a… | it is also a… |
|---|---|
| square | rectangle, rhombus, parallelogram |
| rectangle | parallelogram |
| rhombus | parallelogram |

When a question asks for "the mathematical name" of a shape, give the **most precise** name. A square described as "a rectangle" is not wrong, but it does not answer the question.

### Worked example 2: always, sometimes or never

Decide whether each statement is always, sometimes or never true.

```
(a) A square is a rectangle.                      always
(b) A rectangle is a square.                      sometimes (only if all sides are equal)
(c) A parallelogram has a right angle.            sometimes (when it is a rectangle)
(d) An equilateral triangle has an obtuse angle.  never (every angle is 60 degrees)
(e) A right-angled triangle is isosceles.         sometimes (angles 90, 45, 45)
(f) A scalene triangle has two equal angles.      never (equal angles need equal sides)
```

For "sometimes", a one-line example of when it is true and when it is not makes the answer convincing.

## Polygons: regular means sides AND angles

A polygon is **regular** when **all sides are equal and all angles are equal**. Both conditions are needed.

- A rhombus that is not a square has four equal sides but unequal angles: an **irregular** quadrilateral.
- A rectangle that is not a square has four equal angles but unequal sides: also **irregular**.
- The regular quadrilateral is the square; the regular triangle is the equilateral triangle.

Names to know: pentagon (5 sides), hexagon (6), octagon (8), decagon (10).

## Solids: faces, edges, vertices and surfaces

A **face** is a flat surface, an **edge** is where two faces meet, and a **vertex** is a corner. Curved solids use the word **surface**: a cylinder has two flat circular faces and one curved surface.

### Worked example 3: counting

Find the number of faces, edges and vertices of a hexagonal prism and of a pentagonal pyramid.

```
Hexagonal prism:   2 hexagons + 6 rectangles      = 8 faces
                   6 + 6 + 6 joining edges        = 18 edges
                   6 on each end                  = 12 vertices

Pentagonal pyramid: 1 pentagon + 5 triangles      = 6 faces
                    5 base + 5 sloping            = 10 edges
                    5 base + 1 apex               = 6 vertices
```

A **prism** has the same cross-section all the way through; a **pyramid** has a base and sloping triangular faces meeting at one vertex. A **frustum** (Extended) is what remains of a cone or pyramid when the top is cut off parallel to the base; a **hemisphere** (Extended) is half a sphere.

## Similar, congruent and scale factor

- **Congruent**: same shape and same size (one could be placed exactly on the other, perhaps after turning it over).
- **Similar**: same shape; angles equal and all lengths multiplied by the same **scale factor**.
- A scale factor greater than 1 enlarges; between 0 and 1 it reduces.

### Worked example 4

Triangle A has sides 4 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. Compare it with B (6 cm, 9 cm, 10.5 cm), C (7 cm, 4 cm, 6 cm) and D (2 cm, 3 cm, 3.5 cm).

```
B: 6/4 = 9/6 = 10.5/7 = 1.5     similar to A, scale factor 1.5
C: same three lengths            congruent to A (the order does not matter)
D: 2/4 = 3/6 = 3.5/7 = 0.5       similar to A, scale factor 0.5
```

Congruent shapes are also similar, with scale factor 1. You are not expected to prove congruence in 0580.

## Circle vocabulary in descriptions

Questions often describe a region rather than name it.

| Description | Term |
|---|---|
| Region between two radii and an arc | sector |
| Region between a chord and an arc | segment |
| Straight line touching the circle at exactly one point | tangent |
| Chord passing through the centre | diameter (the longest chord) |
| Half of a circle cut off by a diameter | semicircle |
| (Extended) The shorter arc between two points | minor arc |
| (Extended) The longer arc between two points | major arc |

A diameter is a special chord; a radius is not a chord, because a chord has both ends on the circle.

## Common mistakes

- **Calling a 200° angle obtuse.** Over 180° is reflex.
- **Naming an angle with the vertex letter first or last**, so angle PQR is measured at the wrong point.
- **Calling a rhombus "regular"** because its sides are equal.
- **Mixing sector and segment**, or chord and tangent.
- **Calling a cylinder a prism or a cone a pyramid** in an answer that needs the exact name.
- **Writing "radiuses"**: the plural is radii. "Vertices" is the plural of vertex.
- **Calling shapes with equal angles but different sizes congruent.** They are similar.

## Exam technique

- **Write down the mathematical name**: one precise word or phrase; no description needed.
- **State** the type of angle or triangle: use the syllabus term exactly (reflex, scalene, isosceles…).
- **Explain** why shapes are similar: compare every pair of corresponding lengths and state the common scale factor.
- Learn the spellings of terms such as "isosceles", "parallelogram" and "trapezium", so your answer cannot be read as a different word.
