**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 5 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): European imperialism and the partition of Africa, circa 1840 to 1920. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-partition-of-africa/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Growth of European activity | Traders, explorers, missionaries, technology, Ottoman weakness |
| New Imperialism | Economic, strategic, political and social motives |
| Vulnerability of African societies | Weapons gap, weak or divided states, treaties |
| Partition | Berlin 1884 to 1885, the scramble, European disputes |
| Resistance and collaboration | Why leaders chose to fight or cooperate |
| African responses | What worked and what did not |

## Causes: four groups

| Group | Points | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Raw materials, markets, investment | Rubber, palm oil, minerals; Hobson and Lenin |
| Strategic | Routes and naval bases | Suez 1869; Egypt 1882; the Cape |
| Political | National rivalry and prestige | France after 1871; Germany from 1884; Italy |
| Social | Mission and "civilising" ideas | Livingstone; Social Darwinism |

**Historians.** Hobson and Lenin: economic. Robinson and Gallagher (1961): strategic, driven by crises on the edge of empire. Later historians: a mixture, with African conditions also mattering.

## Technology and vulnerability

- Quinine reduced malaria deaths.
- Steamships and river steamers.
- Breech-loading rifles; Maxim gun (1884).
- Omdurman, 1898: gunboats and machine guns defeat the Mahdist army.
- Treaties signed with chiefs, such as Rudd's concession from Lobengula (1888), and Wuchale (1889), where versions differed.
- Disunity used by Europeans: rivals sought allies.

## Berlin Conference

- Dates: November 1884 to February 1885. Host: Bismarck. Fourteen states; no Africans present.
- General Act: free trade in the Congo and Niger basins; "effective occupation" for coastal claims; recognition of Leopold II's Congo.
- It did not draw the map. Borders came from later treaties.

## The scramble by power

| Power | Main areas |
|---|---|
| Belgium | Congo Free State, from 1885 |
| France | West and Equatorial Africa, Tunisia (1881), Madagascar (1896), Morocco (1912) |
| Germany | Togoland, Cameroon, South West Africa, East Africa |
| United Kingdom | Egypt (1882), Sudan, Nigeria, Gold Coast, East Africa, southern Africa |
| Italy | Eritrea, Somaliland, Libya (1911 to 1912) |
| Portugal | Angola, Mozambique |
| Spain | Small coastal territories, Rio Muni |

**Disputes:** Fashoda (1898), British ultimatum to Portugal (1890), Moroccan crises (1905, 1911).

## Resistance and collaboration

| Leader or event | Approach | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Samori Toure | Fought France for years | Captured 1898 |
| Menelik II | Weapons, diplomacy, army | Won at Adwa, 1896 |
| Khama, Lewanika | Negotiated protection | Retained some autonomy, under British oversight |
| Buganda chiefs | Collaboration | Gained influence |
| Maji Maji (1905 to 1907) | Rising against German rule | Crushed, with heavy loss of life |
| Herero and Nama (1904 to 1908) | Armed revolt | Crushed; widely regarded as genocide |

**Why choose resistance?** Strong leaders, firearms, a defendable homeland and resentment of taxes or forced labour. **Why collaborate?** Hope of protection, gains for elites, an assessment that resistance was hopeless.

## Impact of colonial rule

- Hut and poll taxes; forced labour; land loss.
- Cash crops over food crops.
- Borders joined or divided peoples.
- Missionary schools created new educated elites.
- In the Congo, brutality under Leopold's system led to an international campaign and Belgian takeover in 1908.

## Arguments to practise

1. "Technology explains European victory." For: Maxim gun, quinine. Against: Adwa; collaboration; European disunity delayed some conquests.
2. "Resistance was futile." For: most failed. Against: it delayed conquest, shaped later nationalism, and won concessions.
3. "The partition was mainly the result of rivalry between European powers." For: Fashoda, Berlin. Against: economic and missionary interests.

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent were economic factors the main reason for European imperialism in Africa after 1870?"

- Introduction: define "main" by weighing four groups of factors.
- Economic: raw materials, markets, Leopold's profits; but returns were slow and often disappointing.
- Strategic: Egypt 1882; the Cape.
- Political: national rivalry; the fear of being left out.
- Social: missions and the civilising idea.
- Conclusion: rivalry and strategy explain the timing; economic hopes gave a widely shared motive. Judgement depends on how "main" is defined.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the Berlin Conference started the scramble.
- Treating "Africans" as one group.
- Dating the partition after 1914; most borders were drawn by 1905.
- Not naming specific people and events.

## Self-test

1. Who hosted the Berlin Conference?
2. What did "effective occupation" mean?
3. Which battle in 1898 showed the effect of the Maxim gun?
4. Name one African leader who negotiated rather than fought.
5. Which two states stayed independent in 1914?
6. Which crisis of 1898 almost led France and Britain to war?

**Answers:** 1. Bismarck. 2. A coastal claim needed real administration or force to be recognised. 3. Omdurman. 4. Khama or Lewanika (or Buganda chiefs). 5. Ethiopia and Liberia. 6. Fashoda.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I discuss South Africa here?** Only as part of the strategic argument; the South African War and apartheid belong to Regional study 6.

**How should I treat Leopold II?** As a ruler of the Congo Free State from 1885 and as an example of how the scramble could produce extreme abuse.

## Key terms

- **New Imperialism:** the burst of European colonial expansion from about 1870 to 1914.
- **Scramble:** the rapid claiming of African territory after about 1880.
- **Effective occupation:** the Berlin rule that a claim needed real control.
- **Protectorate:** a territory run under the protection of a colonial power, often with local rulers kept in place.
- **Concession:** a grant of rights, such as mining, usually signed by an African ruler.
- **Civilising mission:** the idea that Europeans had a duty to bring Christianity and "progress" to Africa.
- **Collaboration:** cooperation by African rulers or groups with European powers.
