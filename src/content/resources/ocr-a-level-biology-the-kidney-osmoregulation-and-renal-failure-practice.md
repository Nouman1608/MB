## Questions

**1.** Describe how the structure of the renal corpuscle allows ultrafiltration of blood plasma. **[4]**

**2.** The question supplies these pressures for a glomerulus. Hydrostatic pressure in the capillary is 6.8 kPa, hydrostatic pressure in the capsule is 2.1 kPa, and plasma proteins pull water in with an effect equal to 3.2 kPa.
**(a)** Work out the net pressure driving fluid out of the capillary. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what would happen to filtration if the afferent arteriole constricted, and why. **[3]**

**3.** Explain how glucose in the filtrate is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule. **[5]**

**4.** Filtrate forms at 0.090 dm³ min⁻¹. The glucose carriers in the proximal tubules can reabsorb at most 270 mg of glucose per minute. A patient has a plasma glucose concentration of 4.0 g dm⁻³.
**(a)** Find how much glucose is filtered each minute, in mg. **[2]**
**(b)** Find how much glucose is left in the filtrate. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why this patient also produces an unusually large volume of urine. **[2]**

**5.** The table shows supplied data for three mammals. The data are not measurements of real species.

| Mammal | Length of loop of Henle (mm) | Maximum urine concentration (mOsm kg⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 6 | 1 100 |
| Q | 12 | 2 900 |
| R | 22 | 5 400 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times more concentrated the urine of R is than that of P. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the relationship shown. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how a longer loop of Henle allows more concentrated urine, and suggest which mammal lives in the driest habitat. **[3]**

**6.** A person eats a very salty meal and drinks no water for several hours. Explain how the body responds to restore the water potential of the blood. **[6]**

**7.** A patient with kidney disease has a plasma creatinine concentration of 0.060 mg cm⁻³. Urine creatinine is 0.90 mg cm⁻³ and urine flow is 1.0 cm³ min⁻¹. Creatinine is filtered and not reabsorbed.
**(a)** Calculate the patient's GFR. **[2]**
**(b)** The healthy value for this person was 120 cm³ min⁻¹. Calculate the present GFR as a percentage of this. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the blood potassium concentration of this patient may rise, and why this is dangerous. **[3]**

**8.** During haemodialysis, blood enters a dialyser at 250 cm³ min⁻¹. Its potassium concentration is 7.2 mmol dm⁻³ on entering and 4.4 mmol dm⁻³ on leaving.
**(a)** Calculate the mass of potassium removed during a 4 hour session, in mmol. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why dialysis fluid and blood flow in opposite directions. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the dialysis fluid contains glucose at the normal plasma concentration. **[2]**

**9.** A 34-year-old with kidney failure is offered haemodialysis now or a place on a transplant waiting list. Evaluate the two treatments. **[8]**

**10.** A home pregnancy test strip uses monoclonal antibodies.
**(a)** Explain how the strip shows a positive result. **[4]**
**(b)** Suggest why monoclonal antibodies are used rather than a mixture of antibodies. **[1]**
**(c)** State how the same technology can be used to screen an athlete for a banned drug. **[1]**

**11.** A student dissected a pig kidney and examined stained sections.
**(a)** The cortex is 9 mm thick in the kidney and 27 mm thick on the student's drawing. Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
**(b)** State two safety precautions for the dissection. **[2]**
**(c)** A glomerulus measures 56 mm on a micrograph with magnification ×400. Calculate its actual diameter in µm. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe how the student could tell a proximal tubule from a distal tubule in a stained section. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The efferent arteriole is narrower than the afferent one, so pressure builds up inside the glomerulus [1]. The capillary endothelium has gaps (fenestrations) so plasma can leave but blood cells cannot [1]. The basement membrane acts as a filter that holds back large proteins [1]. Podocytes of Bowman's capsule have slits between their pedicels so filtrate passes into the capsule [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that name the three layers and give the function of each are more convincing than "it is leaky".

**2. (a)** Net outward pressure = 6.8 − 2.1 − 3.2 [1] = **1.5 kPa** [1].
**(b)** Less blood enters the glomerulus, so capillary pressure falls and filtration decreases [1]. Net pressure would fall towards zero [1] because the capsule pressure and plasma protein effect stay the same [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and include the unit; "filtration falls" needs the cause (lower hydrostatic pressure) to be convincing.

**3.** Sodium ions are actively transported out of the cell into the blood by the sodium-potassium pump [1], which lowers the sodium ion concentration inside the cell [1]. Sodium ions diffuse in from the filtrate through co-transporter proteins [1], carrying glucose in against its concentration gradient [1]. Glucose then leaves the cell by facilitated diffusion into the blood [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which step is active and which is passive; "glucose is actively transported" with no mention of sodium is a common half-answer.

**4. (a)** Amount filtered = 4.0 × 0.090 [1] = 0.36 g min⁻¹ = **360 mg min⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 360 − 270 = **90 mg min⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** The remaining glucose lowers the water potential of the filtrate in the tubule [1], so less water is reabsorbed by osmosis and more is excreted [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert g to mg before comparing with the carrier limit; many slips come from mixing units.

**5. (a)** 5 400 ÷ 1 100 = **4.9 times** [1].
**(b)** The longer the loop, the more concentrated the urine (positive correlation) [1].
**(c)** The ascending limb pumps ions into the medulla but water cannot follow it out; a longer loop does this over a greater length, so the medulla has a lower water potential [1]. The collecting duct passes through it, so more water leaves by osmosis down a steeper gradient [1]. Mammal **R** lives in the driest habitat because it conserves most water [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking length to gradient and gradient to water leaving the collecting duct gains more than "longer loops reabsorb more".

**6.** The salt lowers the water potential of the blood, which osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect [1]. Nerve impulses cause the posterior pituitary gland to release more ADH [1]. The blood carries ADH to the collecting duct, where it attaches to receptors in the wall cells [1]. More aquaporins are placed in the cell-surface membrane, so the walls become more permeable to water [1]. More water is reabsorbed by osmosis, producing a small volume of concentrated urine [1]. The blood water potential rises towards normal, so less ADH is released (negative feedback) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that follow the sequence stimulus, detector, hormone, target, effect are easiest to credit; put the steps in order and name the organs.

**7. (a)** GFR = (0.90 × 1.0) ÷ 0.060 [1] = **15 cm³ min⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 15 ÷ 120 × 100 = **12.5%** [1].
**(c)** Fewer nephrons filter the blood, so less potassium is excreted and it accumulates [1]. A high potassium concentration disrupts the electrical activity of muscle and nerve cells [1], so the heartbeat can become irregular [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a GFR with its units; name the organ affected (the heart) rather than saying "it is harmful".

**8. (a)** Potassium removed per minute = 0.250 × (7.2 − 4.4) [1] = 0.70 mmol; for 240 min, 0.70 × 240 [1] = **168 mmol** [1].
**(b)** Counter-current flow keeps blood always next to dialysis fluid that has a lower concentration of waste [1], so a diffusion gradient is maintained along the whole membrane [1].
**(c)** Glucose in the blood is at the same concentration as in the fluid, so there is no net diffusion [1], so the patient does not lose glucose [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert cm³ to dm³ before using mmol dm⁻³; state the time conversion in the working.

**9.** Haemodialysis: available immediately without a donor [1]; removes urea and excess ions but only during sessions, so levels fluctuate [1]; needs several hours several times a week, limiting work and travel, and the blood access site can become infected [1]. Transplant: the new kidney filters continuously, allowing a more normal diet and fluid intake [1]; needs tissue-type matching and a donor, so there is a wait [1]; risk of rejection, so immunosuppressant drugs are needed [1], which raise the risk of infection [1]. A balanced conclusion is that dialysis is a bridge and a transplant is preferred long-term for a young patient [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs points for both treatments and a stated judgement; a list of facts for one treatment only does not answer the question.

**10. (a)** Urine moves along the strip and meets mobile monoclonal antibodies to hCG on coloured beads [1]. If hCG is present, it binds to these antibodies [1]. The complex is captured by immobilised antibodies at the test line, which turns coloured [1]. Unbound beads are captured at the control line, so two lines mean positive [1].
**(b)** A monoclonal antibody is specific to one antigen, so it binds only hCG [1].
**(c)** Use monoclonal antibodies specific to the drug in an immunoassay on a urine sample [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain what each line means; a control line shows the test has worked, not that the person is pregnant.

**11. (a)** Magnification = image ÷ actual [1] = 27 ÷ 9 = **×3** [1].
**(b)** Any two: wear gloves and eye protection; cut away from the body with a sharp scalpel on a board; disinfect the bench and wash hands afterwards [1] [1].
**(c)** Actual = 56 ÷ 400 [1] = 0.14 mm = **140 µm** [1].
**(d)** The proximal tubule has a brush border of microvilli on its cells [1] and the cells appear denser because they contain many mitochondria; the distal tubule has fewer microvilli and a wider, clearer lumen [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert mm to µm explicitly in a magnification answer, and give a feature that can be seen under the microscope rather than a function.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the kidney or "the nephron" without the glomerulus, Bowman's capsule or the collecting duct.
- Describing ultrafiltration as selective by carriers.
- Missing the sodium co-transport step in glucose reabsorption.
- Muddling the hypothalamus (detection) and the posterior pituitary (release).
- Leaving out aquaporins in an ADH answer.
- Missing unit conversions between g and mg, cm³ and dm³, or mm and µm.
- Evaluating only one treatment, or not reaching a judgement.
- Describing the control line of a pregnancy test as the result.
