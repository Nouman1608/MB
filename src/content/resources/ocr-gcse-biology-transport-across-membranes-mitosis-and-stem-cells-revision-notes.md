## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | Net movement of particles from more to less concentrated, down a concentration gradient. Passive. |
| Osmosis | Net movement of water across a partially permeable membrane from higher water potential to lower water potential. Passive. |
| Active transport | Movement of particles from lower to higher concentration, against the gradient, using energy from respiration. |
| Water potential | A measure of how freely water can move. Pure water is highest; more solute lowers it. |
| Mitosis | Nuclear division giving two genetically identical nuclei. |
| Cell cycle | Growth, DNA replication, more growth, mitosis, then division of the cytoplasm. |
| Differentiation | A cell becoming specialised for a job. |
| Stem cell | Undifferentiated cell that can divide and differentiate. |
| Meristem | Region in a plant, at root and shoot tips, where stem cells divide. |

## B2.1a Transport across membranes

### Comparison

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moves | Small particles | Water | Ions, molecules |
| Down or against | Down concentration gradient | Down water potential gradient | Against concentration gradient |
| Energy | None | None | From respiration |
| Helped by | Steep gradient, warmth, large surface | Same | Carrier proteins, mitochondria, oxygen |

### Examples to learn

- Diffusion: oxygen into blood at the alveoli, carbon dioxide out; dissolved food into the blood from the gut; urea out of cells.
- Osmosis: water into root hair cells; water into or out of animal and plant cells placed in solutions.
- Active transport: mineral ions into root hair cells; glucose from the gut into the blood when the gut concentration is lower.

### Cells in solutions

| Solution compared with cell | Animal cell | Plant cell |
|---|---|---|
| More dilute (higher water potential) | Gains water, swells, may burst | Gains water, becomes turgid |
| Same | No net change | No net change |
| More concentrated (lower water potential) | Loses water, shrinks | Loses water, becomes flaccid, membrane pulls away from the wall |

### Method in steps: osmosis investigation

1. Cut identical cylinders of plant tissue (same diameter and length).
2. Blot, then measure each initial mass.
3. Place one in each solution of a different sucrose concentration. Include pure water.
4. Keep every chip in its solution for an equal time, at one temperature.
5. Blot and measure final mass.
6. Calculate percentage change for each.
7. Draw a graph of percentage change against concentration. The point where the line meets zero shows no net water movement.

### Worked reminder: percentage change

Percentage change = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.
Chip: 2.50 g to 2.75 g gives +10.0%. Chip: 2.50 g to 2.20 g gives −12.0%.
A negative answer is a loss. Always divide by the initial value.

## B2.1b Mitosis and the cell cycle

### The cycle in order

1. Cell growth. More ribosomes, mitochondria and other subcellular structures.
2. DNA replication. Each chromosome becomes two identical chromatids.
3. More cell growth.
4. Movement of chromosomes (mitosis).
5. Cytoplasm and membrane divide: two daughter cells.

### Mitosis in short

- Chromosomes condense and line up across the middle.
- Spindle fibres pull chromatids to opposite poles.
- Two nuclei form; the cell divides.

### Numbers to remember

| Stage | Chromosomes (parent has 12) | DNA (parent 8 units) |
|---|---|---|
| Before replication | 12 | 8 |
| After replication | 12 (24 chromatids) | 16 |
| Each daughter cell | 12 | 8 |

- Daughter cells are genetically identical to the parent and to each other.
- Number after n divisions from one cell = 2ⁿ. Five divisions give 32 cells.
- Mitosis happens in growth, and also in repair and replacement of cells.

### Worked reminder: percentage in mitosis

200 cells counted, 18 in mitosis: 18 ÷ 200 × 100 = 9.0%. A high percentage means rapid growth in that tissue.

## B2.1c Differentiation

- Differentiation produces specialised cells.
- Specialised cells let an organism be more efficient, because different cells do different jobs.

| Cell | Key adaptations | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Sperm | Tail, mitochondria, enzymes in head | Reach and enter egg |
| Red blood cell | Biconcave, no nucleus, haemoglobin | Carry oxygen |
| Root hair cell | Hair-like extension | Take in water and ions |
| Palisade cell | Many chloroplasts | Photosynthesis |
| Muscle cell | Mitochondria, contractile fibres | Contract |
| Nerve cell | Long, branched | Carry impulses |

Answer pattern: name the feature, then say how it helps.

## B2.1d to B2.1f Stem cells

### Where

- Embryonic stem cells: animal embryos.
- Adult stem cells: tissues such as bone marrow.
- Meristems: tips of roots and shoots in plants.

### Functions

- Stem cells divide and differentiate to give cell types for development, growth and repair.
- Bone marrow stem cells keep making blood cells.
- Meristem cells divide throughout the life of a plant and differentiate to form tissues such as xylem and phloem.
- A cutting or a cauliflower fragment can grow into a whole plant, because meristem cells can become every plant cell type.

### Embryonic compared with adult

| | Embryonic | Adult |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Early embryo | Adult tissues such as bone marrow |
| Becomes | Any cell type | Limited range |
| Main role | Forming the embryo | Repair and replacement |

## Must-know distinctions

- Diffusion moves any small particle; osmosis moves only water.
- Diffusion and osmosis are passive; active transport uses energy.
- Mitosis is the division of the nucleus; the cell cycle includes the growth stages as well.
- A chromosome is copied into two chromatids; the chromatids then separate and become chromosomes in the daughter cells.
- Differentiation is the change into a specialised cell; a stem cell is one that has not yet differentiated.
- Embryonic stem cells can become any cell type; adult stem cells have a more limited range.

## Model explanations

Use these as patterns for longer answers.

**Why can a root hair cell take up nitrate when the soil has less nitrate than the cell?** Diffusion would move nitrate out of the cell, down the gradient. The cell uses active transport instead, with carrier proteins, against the gradient. The energy comes from respiration, which is why the root hair cell contains many mitochondria and needs oxygen.

**Why does a chip lose mass in a strong salt solution?** The solution has a lower water potential than the cytoplasm of the cells. Water leaves the cells by osmosis, crossing their partially permeable membranes, and the chip becomes lighter.

**Why does a growing root tip contain many cells in mitosis?** Cells in the meristem are dividing to make new cells. Each division follows growth and DNA replication, so the tip has a high percentage of cells with visible chromosomes.

**Why are stem cells useful for repair?** They can divide to make more cells and then differentiate into the types needed to replace damaged ones.

## Maths skills

- Percentage gain or loss in mass: (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100.
- Percentile: proportion of a group below a value. If 15 of 20 are lower, the value is at the 75th percentile.
- Use your own working. State units. Give the sign.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the process that moves water across a partially permeable membrane.
2. Why does active transport need oxygen?
3. A cell is in a solution with a lower water potential than its cytoplasm. Which way does water move?
4. Put in order: DNA replication, cell growth, movement of chromosomes.
5. A cell has 8 chromosomes. How many does each daughter cell have after mitosis?
6. One cell divides 5 times. How many cells are there?
7. A potato chip goes from 4.00 g to 3.60 g. Calculate the percentage change.
8. Name the plant tissue where stem cells divide.
9. State one way an adult stem cell differs from an embryonic stem cell.
10. Give one reason differentiation helps a large organism.

### Answers

1. Osmosis.
2. Oxygen is needed for aerobic respiration, which releases the energy.
3. Out of the cell.
4. Cell growth, DNA replication, (more cell growth), movement of chromosomes.
5. 8.
6. 2⁵ = 32 cells.
7. (3.60 − 4.00) ÷ 4.00 × 100 = −10.0%, a loss of 10.0%.
8. Meristem (at the tips of roots and shoots).
9. Adult stem cells form only a limited range of cell types, whereas embryonic stem cells can form any type.
10. Specialised cells do one job efficiently, so the organism as a whole works better.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "from high to low concentration" for osmosis without referring to water or water potential.
- Calling osmosis active, or saying water is "pumped".
- Not naming respiration as the energy source for active transport.
- Using the final mass as the divisor.
- Leaving out the minus sign for a loss.
- Missing the second growth stage in the cell cycle, or placing replication after mitosis.
- Saying daughter cells are "the same" without genetically identical.
- Describing stem cells only as embryonic, and forgetting adult stem cells and meristems.
- Listing a cell's features without linking them to its function.
