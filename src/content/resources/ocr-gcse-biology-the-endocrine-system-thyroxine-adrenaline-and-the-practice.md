Questions labelled Higher Tier only draw on B3.2b (thyroxine and adrenaline) or B3.2d (interactions of FSH, LH, oestrogen and progesterone), which are in bold in the specification. ## Questions

**1.** State two differences between control by hormones and control by the nervous system. **[2]**

**2.** Name the gland that produces each hormone.
**(a)** Oestrogen **[1]**
**(b)** Testosterone **[1]**
**(c)** FSH **[1]**

**3.** Hormones are released into the blood and travel all around the body, but each hormone affects only certain cells. Explain why. **[3]**

**4.** (Higher Tier only) A student's resting heart rate is 64 beats per minute. When a door slams behind her it rises to 104 beats per minute.
**(a)** Work out by what percentage her heart rate has risen. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the release of adrenaline helps her to run away. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher Tier only) A volunteer takes a tablet that raises blood thyroxine. Thyroxine was measured in arbitrary units.

| Hour after tablet | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Thyroxine (units) | 50 | 74 | 66 | 58 | 52 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in thyroxine between hour 0 and hour 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate at which thyroxine fell between hour 2 and hour 10. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the data show negative feedback. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) Describe how negative feedback brings blood thyroxine back to normal when the level rises above normal. **[3]**

**7.** Describe the roles of FSH, oestrogen and progesterone in the female menstrual cycle. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) The table shows hormone levels (arbitrary units) for one cycle.

| Day | 3 | 6 | 9 | 12 | 14 | 15 | 18 | 21 | 24 | 27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FSH | 9 | 10 | 8 | 5 | 6 | 4 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 6 |
| LH | 4 | 4 | 5 | 12 | 35 | 8 | 4 | 4 | 4 | 4 |
| Oestrogen | 25 | 40 | 90 | 200 | 170 | 110 | 90 | 130 | 150 | 50 |
| Progesterone | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 | 3 | 8 | 20 | 26 | 24 | 6 |

**(a)** State the day on which LH was highest. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in oestrogen from day 3 to day 12. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest on which day ovulation most likely happened, and give a reason. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the rise in progesterone between day 15 and day 21. **[1]**

**9.** State where testosterone is made, one function of testosterone, and one function of oestrogen at puberty. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) A woman's egg is not fertilised. Explain how changes in hormone levels cause menstruation and then start the next cycle. **[5]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) Hormone levels were measured in a woman who had no bleeding by day 32.

| Day | 20 | 24 | 28 | 32 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Progesterone (units) | 26 | 28 | 29 | 30 |
| FSH (units) | 2 | 2 | 2 | 2 |

**(a)** Describe how the progesterone pattern differs from the pattern in a cycle in which the egg is not fertilised. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in progesterone from day 20 to day 32. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest an explanation for the absence of bleeding. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why FSH stays low. **[2]**

**12.** A student says: "A fertilised egg can only embed when the lining of the uterus is breaking down."
**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong. **[3]**
**(b)** The lengths of five of one woman's cycles were 27, 29, 28, 30 and 26 days. Calculate the mean cycle length. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Hormones travel in the blood, nerves use electrical impulses [1]. Hormonal responses are slower and longer lasting (or hormones can affect a wider area) than nervous responses [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides, so "hormones are slow" alone is weaker than "hormones are slower than nerve impulses".

**2. (a)** Ovaries (ovary) [1]
**(b)** Testes [1]
**(c)** Pituitary gland [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the gland, not the whole system; "reproductive organs" is too vague to credit.

**3.** The blood carries each hormone to all tissues [1]. Only target cells have receptors that fit that hormone [1]. Cells without the receptor do not respond [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say only "it goes to the target organ" miss the explanation; you need the receptor point.

**4. (a)** 104 - 64 = 40 [1]; 40 / 64 x 100 = **62.5%** [1]
**(b)** Adrenaline increases heart rate so more blood carrying oxygen and glucose reaches the muscles [1]; the muscles can respire faster to release energy for running [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a percentage calculation, show the change divided by the starting value; a correct figure with working is easier to credit than a bare answer.

**5. (a)** 74 - 50 = 24 [1]; 24 / 50 x 100 = **48%** [1]
**(b)** Fall from hour 2 to hour 10 = 74 - 50 = 24 units [1]; 24 / 8 = **3 units per hour** [1]
**(c)** The level rose above normal (50) and then fell back to 50 [1]; a rise caused a response that reverses it, so less thyroxine was released and the level returned to normal [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a mean rate, divide by the time interval and include the unit; for the explanation, quote values from the table.

**6.** The raised thyroxine level is detected [1]; less thyroxine is released from the thyroid [1]; the level falls back to the normal range [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the response reverses the change; "the body balances it" is too vague to show negative feedback.

**7.** FSH makes an egg mature in the ovary [1] and stimulates the ovary to make oestrogen [1]; oestrogen thickens (repairs) the lining of the uterus [1]; progesterone maintains the lining [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Four marks means four separate points; give each hormone a different job rather than repeating "it controls the cycle".

**8. (a)** Day 14 [1]
**(b)** (200 - 25) = 175 [1]; 175 / 25 x 100 = **700%** [1]
**(c)** Ovulation is most likely on or just after day 14 [1] because the LH surge triggers ovulation [1]
**(d)** After ovulation the empty follicle makes progesterone, which rises to keep the lining thick [1]
*Examiner insight:* A reason that quotes the LH value (35 units on day 14) shows you have used the data, not just remembered the fact.

**9.** Made in the testes [1]. Any one: stimulates sperm production; brings about male puberty changes (such as voice deepening or facial hair) [1]. Oestrogen at puberty: brings about female changes such as breast development (or helps start the menstrual cycle) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Sex hormone" on its own describes neither function; name a specific change.

**10.** The egg is not fertilised, so the empty follicle breaks down and progesterone falls [1]; oestrogen also falls [1]; the lining can no longer be maintained and breaks down (menstruation) [1]; FSH is no longer inhibited so its level rises [1]; a new egg starts to mature in a follicle and the cycle restarts [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong answers run in order of cause and effect: what falls, what that allows, then what rises.

**11. (a)** Progesterone stays high (rises from 26 to 30) up to day 32 [1] instead of falling after about day 23 [1]
**(b)** (30 - 26) = 4 [1]; 4 / 26 x 100 = 15.38...%, so **15%** (2 significant figures) [1]
**(c)** Progesterone is maintaining the lining so it does not break down [1]; this suggests a fertilised egg has embedded in the lining [1]
**(d)** High progesterone inhibits FSH [1]; so no new egg matures [1]
*Examiner insight:* When asked for 2 significant figures, show the unrounded value first and then round.

**12. (a)** Ovulation happens at about day 14 when the lining is already thick [1]; a fertilised egg embeds in this thick lining [1]; the lining breaks down at menstruation if no egg embeds, so the student has the timing the wrong way round [1]
**(b)** 27 + 29 + 28 + 30 + 26 = 140 [1]; 140 / 5 = **28 days** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the timing of ovulation to the state of the lining; naming the hormones alone does not answer a "why" question.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying hormones travel along nerves or in tubes, rather than in the blood.
- Giving "only the target organ gets it" and leaving out receptors.
- Describing negative feedback without saying the response reverses the change.
- Working out a percentage change against the final figure rather than the starting figure.
- Leaving out units, such as units per hour for a rate.
- Mixing up FSH and LH, or saying both "make the egg".
- Writing that progesterone causes bleeding, when its fall is what allows the lining to break down.
- Describing a graph or table without quoting any values and days.
- Rounding to the wrong number of significant figures, or rounding before the final step.
