**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 22.1:
analyse an infrared spectrum of a simple molecule to identify functional groups, using the Data section.

## Core ideas

- Bonds vibrate and absorb IR radiation at characteristic wavenumbers (cm⁻¹).
- Spectrum: **transmittance (%)** on the vertical axis, **wavenumber** on the horizontal axis (high on the
  left); absorptions point **downwards**.
- The wavenumber of an absorption depends on the bond and the atoms in it, so it identifies a functional
  group.
- O–H absorptions are **broad** because of hydrogen bonding.

## Absorptions you will use most

| Bond | Range / cm⁻¹ | Indicates | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| O–H | 3200–3600 | alcohol (hydroxy) | broad |
| N–H | 3300–3500 | amine or amide | medium |
| C–H | 2850–2950 | alkane chain (nearly all organics) | sharp, several |
| O–H | 2500–3000 | carboxylic acid | very broad |
| C≡N | 2200–2250 | nitrile | sharp |
| C=O | 1710–1750 | ester | strong |
| C=O | 1670–1740 | aldehyde, ketone, carboxylic acid | strong |
| C=O | 1640–1690 | amide | strong |
| C=C | 1500–1680 | alkene, arene | medium to weak |
| C–O | 1040–1300 | alcohol, ester (also acids) | strong |

These values are given in the exam Data section, so focus on using them quickly.

## Seven-step routine

1. 3200–3600 broad? → alcohol O–H.
2. 3300–3500, narrower? → N–H.
3. 2500–3000 very broad? → carboxylic acid O–H.
4. 2200–2250 sharp? → C≡N.
5. 1640–1750 strong? → C=O (which kind depends on steps 1–3 and 6).
6. 1040–1300 strong? → C–O.
7. Combine with the molecular formula; name the compound or class.

## Fingerprint for each class (absorptions present)

| Class | Key absorptions |
|---|---|
| alkane | C–H only |
| alkene | C–H + C=C |
| alcohol | broad O–H (3200–3600) + C–O |
| aldehyde / ketone | C=O (1670–1740), no O–H |
| carboxylic acid | C=O + very broad O–H (2500–3000) |
| ester | C=O (1710–1750) + C–O, no O–H |
| nitrile | C≡N (2200–2250) |
| primary amine | N–H (3300–3500) |

## Following reactions by IR

| Change | Lost | Gained |
|---|---|---|
| primary alcohol → aldehyde (distil) | O–H 3200–3600 | C=O |
| primary alcohol → acid (reflux) | O–H 3200–3600 | C=O + O–H 2500–3000 |
| secondary alcohol → ketone | O–H 3200–3600 | C=O |
| alkene → alcohol (steam) | C=C | O–H 3200–3600 |
| halogenoalkane → nitrile | — | C≡N |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wavenumber | number of waves per centimetre, unit cm⁻¹; proportional to frequency |
| Transmittance | percentage of IR radiation that passes through the sample |
| Absorption | a dip in transmittance at a wavenumber absorbed by a bond |
| Functional group | the group of atoms that gives a compound its characteristic reactions |
| Hydrogen bonding | attraction between H on O or N and a lone pair on another O or N, broadening O–H peaks |

## Exam traps

- Two O–H ranges: **3200–3600** (alcohol) vs **2500–3000** very broad (acid). Do not mix them up.
- A peak near 2900 cm⁻¹ is usually **C–H**, not O–H.
- Ester needs C=O **and** C–O **and no O–H**.
- Aldehydes and ketones look alike in IR; use Tollens' or Fehling's reagent to separate them.
- Always link: **wavenumber → bond → functional group**.
- IR rarely distinguishes **positional** isomers such as propan-1-ol and propan-2-ol.
- Overlapping ranges (1645 cm⁻¹ could be C=C or amide C=O): use the **molecular formula**; no nitrogen
  means no amide.
- An absorption that **disappears** is as useful as one that appears when following a reaction.

## Self-test

1. What is plotted on each axis of an IR spectrum?
2. Why are O–H absorptions broad?
3. Give the range for the C≡N absorption.
4. Which absorption distinguishes propanoic acid from propan-1-ol?
5. Which absorption distinguishes propanal from propan-1-ol?
6. A compound has a strong absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ and a very broad one from 2500 to 3000 cm⁻¹. Name the
   class.
7. A compound C₄H₈O₂ shows 1740 cm⁻¹ and 1180 cm⁻¹ but no O–H. Name the class.
8. What would show that ethanol had been completely dehydrated to ethene?
9. Can IR tell butanal from butanone? Explain.
10. Which absorption confirms a halogenoalkane has been converted into a nitrile?
11. Which bond absorbs in the range 1500–1680 cm⁻¹?
12. What would you expect in the spectrum of propylamine that is absent from propane?

**Answers:**

1. Transmittance (%) against wavenumber (cm⁻¹).
2. Hydrogen bonding between O–H groups means a range of slightly different O–H frequencies is absorbed.
3. 2200–2250 cm⁻¹.
4. The very broad O–H at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and the C=O at 1670–1740 cm⁻¹, both present only in the acid.
5. C=O at 1670–1740 cm⁻¹ in propanal; broad O–H at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ only in propan-1-ol.
6. Carboxylic acid.
7. Ester.
8. The broad O–H absorption at 3200–3600 cm⁻¹ disappears and a C=C absorption at 1500–1680 cm⁻¹ appears.
9. Not reliably: both contain C=O with no O–H, so their spectra are very similar.
10. A sharp absorption at 2200–2250 cm⁻¹ (C≡N).
11. C=C (alkene or aromatic).
12. An N–H absorption at 3300–3500 cm⁻¹.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to memorise the IR wavenumbers for Cambridge 9701?**
No. The table of characteristic absorptions is in the Data section of the exam. You need to recognise which
bond each region represents and use the ranges confidently.

**Do I need to know how an infrared spectrometer works?**
The 22.1 outcome is about analysing spectra to identify functional groups, so practise reading spectra
rather than learning instrument details.
