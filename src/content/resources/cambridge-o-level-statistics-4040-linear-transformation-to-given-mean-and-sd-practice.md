These questions cover section 6.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: linear transformation of data to a given mean and standard deviation. Standard deviations divide by n. Recall the method from the [linear transformation study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-linear-transformation-to-given-mean-and-sd/) or the [linear transformation revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-linear-transformation-to-given-mean-and-sd-revision-notes/). Show your working and give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** A set of data has mean 24 and standard deviation 3. Find the values of a and b so that y = a + bx has mean 50 and standard deviation 12. **[3]**

**2.** Marks x have mean 64 and standard deviation 10. Show that the rule y = 0.8x + 18.8 gives marks with mean 70 and standard deviation 8. **[2]**

**3.** For 25 values of x, Σx = 1000 and Σx² = 40 900. The values are to be transformed to have mean 60 and standard deviation 15. Find the transformation in the form y = a + bx. **[4]**

**4.** A transformation y = a + bx changes a mark of 20 into 44 and a mark of 50 into 92. Find a and b. **[3]**

**5.** Data have mean 30 and standard deviation 5. They are to be transformed to have mean 50 and variance 100. Find the transformation. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** Sana scores 68 in Mathematics, where the class mean is 55 and the standard deviation is 10. She scores 61 in English, where the class mean is 52 and the standard deviation is 6. The marks in each subject are to be scaled to mean 60 and standard deviation 12.

**(a)** Find the scaling rule for each subject. **[4]**

**(b)** Find Sana's scaled mark in each subject. **[2]**

**(c)** State in which subject Sana performed better relative to her class, giving a reason. **[1]**

**7.** Five values are 4, 8, 10, 12 and 16.

**(a)** Calculate the mean and standard deviation. **[3]**

**(b)** Find a and b so that y = a + bx has mean 50 and standard deviation 10. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the five transformed values. **[2]**

**(d)** Use the transformed values to verify that their standard deviation is 10. **[2]**

**8.** Raw marks have mean 40 and standard deviation 6. They are scaled by y = a + bx so that the scaled marks have mean 65 and standard deviation 9.

**(a)** Find a and b. **[3]**

**(b)** A candidate's scaled mark is 80. Find the raw mark. **[2]**

**(c)** Show that this candidate is the same number of standard deviations above the mean on both scales. **[2]**

**9.** A test is marked out of 40. The marks have mean 22 and standard deviation 5. The lowest mark is 4 and the highest is 38. The marks are to be scaled to mean 50 and standard deviation 20, and reported out of 100.

**(a)** Find the scaling rule. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the scaled marks for the lowest and highest raw marks. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on your answers to (b). **[2]**

**(d)** The mean is to stay at 50. Find the largest standard deviation that can be used so that no scaled mark lies outside 0 to 100. **[3]**

**10.** The points scored by 16 players in a quiz are shown.

| Points (x) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 1 | 4 | 6 | 4 | 1 |

**(a)** Show that the mean is 30 and the standard deviation is 10. **[3]**

**(b)** The points are to be transformed to have mean 100 and standard deviation 15. Find the transformation. **[2]**

**(c)** Write out the frequency table for the transformed points. **[2]**

**(d)** State the median of the transformed points. **[1]**

**11.** Data x have mean x̄ and standard deviation s. A new variable is defined by y = M + (S ÷ s)(x − x̄), where M and S are constants and S > 0.

**(a)** Show that y has mean M and standard deviation S. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why each value has the same standardised score before and after the transformation. **[2]**

**12.** Two examiners mark the same paper for different groups. Examiner P's marks have mean 58 and standard deviation 9. Examiner Q's marks have mean 50 and standard deviation 12. Q's marks are to be scaled to have the same mean and standard deviation as P's.

**(a)** Find the scaling rule for Q's marks. **[3]**

**(b)** Q gave a candidate 74. Find the scaled mark. **[1]**

**(c)** Q gave candidate X 44 and candidate Y 38. Find the difference between their scaled marks. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** b = 12 ÷ 3 = **4** [1]. a = 50 − 4 × 24 [1] = **−46** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Substituting back, −46 + 4 × 24 = 50, confirms a in one line.

**2.** New mean = 0.8 × 64 + 18.8 = 51.2 + 18.8 = 70 [1]. New standard deviation = 0.8 × 10 = 8, as the constant 18.8 has no effect on spread [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs both calculations written out, ending with the target values.

**3.** x̄ = 1000 ÷ 25 = 40 [1]. s² = 40 900 ÷ 25 − 40² = 1636 − 1600 = 36, so s = 6 [1]. b = 15 ÷ 6 = 2.5 [1]. a = 60 − 2.5 × 40 = −40, so **y = 2.5x − 40** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the square root of the variance before dividing; 15 ÷ 36 is a frequent slip.

**4.** a + 20b = 44 and a + 50b = 92 [1]. Subtracting, 30b = 48, so **b = 1.6** [1]. a = 44 − 32 = **12** [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Target standard deviation = √100 = 10 [1]. b = 10 ÷ 5 = 2 [1]. a = 50 − 2 × 30 = −10, so **y = 2x − 10** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The word "variance" is the trap; b = 100 ÷ 5 = 20 earns nothing.

**6. (a)** Mathematics: b = 12 ÷ 10 = 1.2 [1], a = 60 − 1.2 × 55 = −6, so y = 1.2x − 6 [1]. English: b = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 [1], a = 60 − 2 × 52 = −44, so y = 2x − 44 [1].

**(b)** Mathematics: 1.2 × 68 − 6 = **75.6** [1]. English: 2 × 61 − 44 = **78** [1].

**(c)** **English**, because her scaled mark (78) is higher than in Mathematics (75.6), although her raw mark was lower [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The standardised scores, 1.5 in English and 1.3 in Mathematics, give the same conclusion and are an acceptable alternative route.

**7. (a)** Mean = 50 ÷ 5 = **10** [1]. Squared deviations 36, 4, 0, 4, 36, total 80 [1]. Variance 80 ÷ 5 = 16, standard deviation **4** [1].

**(b)** b = 10 ÷ 4 = **2.5** [1], a = 50 − 2.5 × 10 = **25** [1].

**(c)** y = 25 + 2.5x gives **35, 45, 50, 55, 65** [2] (1 mark for at least three correct).

**(d)** Mean of new values = 250 ÷ 5 = 50 [1]. Squared deviations 225, 25, 0, 25, 225, total 500; 500 ÷ 5 = 100, so the standard deviation is √100 = 10 [1]. **[9]**

**8. (a)** b = 9 ÷ 6 = **1.5** [1]. a = 65 − 1.5 × 40 [1] = **5** [1].

**(b)** 80 = 5 + 1.5x [1], so x = 75 ÷ 1.5 = **50** [1].

**(c)** Raw: (50 − 40) ÷ 6 = 1.67 [1]. Scaled: (80 − 65) ÷ 9 = 1.67, the same [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Working backwards means undoing a first, then b; dividing 80 by 1.5 and then subtracting 5 gives the wrong raw mark.

**9. (a)** b = 20 ÷ 5 = 4, a = 50 − 4 × 22 = −38 [1], so **y = 4x − 38** [1].

**(b)** Highest: 4 × 38 − 38 = **114** [1]. Lowest: 4 × 4 − 38 = **−22** [1].

**(c)** A mark out of 100 cannot exceed 100 or be negative, so both values are impossible [1]. The target standard deviation of 20 stretches the extreme marks too far from the mean of 50 [1].

**(d)** The highest mark is (38 − 22) ÷ 5 = 3.2 standard deviations above the mean, and the lowest is (22 − 4) ÷ 5 = 3.6 below [1]. Need 50 − 3.6S ≥ 0 and 50 + 3.2S ≤ 100, giving S ≤ 13.9 and S ≤ 15.6 [1]. Largest standard deviation **13.9** (3 s.f.) [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The lower end gives the tighter limit here because the lowest mark is further from the mean.

**10. (a)** Σfx = 10 + 80 + 180 + 160 + 50 = 480, mean = 480 ÷ 16 = 30 [1]. Σf(x − 30)² = 400 + 400 + 0 + 400 + 400 = 1600 [1]. Variance = 1600 ÷ 16 = 100, standard deviation 10 [1].

**(b)** b = 15 ÷ 10 = 1.5, a = 100 − 1.5 × 30 = 55 [1], so **y = 1.5x + 55** [1].

**(c)** Transformed points 70, 85, 100, 115, 130 [1] with frequencies 1, 4, 6, 4, 1 unchanged [1].

**(d)** The original median is 30, so the transformed median is **100** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Frequencies count players; transforming the points does not change how many players scored each value.

**11. (a)** Mean of y = M + (S ÷ s)(x̄ − x̄) [1] = M [1]. Standard deviation of y = (S ÷ s) × s = S, since M and the subtraction of x̄ do not affect spread [1].

**(b)** Rearranging gives (y − M) ÷ S = (x − x̄) ÷ s [1]. The left side is the standardised score on the new scale and the right side is the standardised score on the original scale, so they are equal for every value [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** b = 9 ÷ 12 = 0.75 [1]. a = 58 − 0.75 × 50 = 20.5 [1], so **y = 0.75x + 20.5** [1].

**(b)** 0.75 × 74 + 20.5 = **76** [1].

**(c)** The raw difference is 44 − 38 = 6 [1]. Differences are multiplied by b only, so the scaled difference is 0.75 × 6 = **4.5** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The shift a cancels when two scaled marks are subtracted, which saves transforming both marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Finding a before b, or dividing the standard deviations the wrong way round.
- Using a variance where a standard deviation is needed.
- Subtracting a when converting a standard deviation back to the original scale.
- Changing frequencies when transforming a table.
- Comparing raw marks across tests instead of scaled marks or standardised scores.
- Not commenting on impossible scaled values when a question asks about them.
