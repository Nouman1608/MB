All data and organisations below are invented. No technology is needed.

## Section A

**1.** State whether each variable is discrete or continuous.

**(a)** The number of emails a person receives in a day. **[1]**
**(b)** The time taken to run 100 metres. **[1]**
**(c)** Shoe size. **[1]**

**2.** A researcher wants to know the average nightly sleep of DP students at Hillcrest School. She asks 50
DP students from the school. Identify the population and the sample. **[2]**

**3.** A gym has a numbered list of 1200 members. A systematic sample of 60 members is needed.

**(a)** Find the sampling interval. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the first three members of the sample are chosen. **[1]**

**4.** A school has 210 students in Grade 9, 180 in Grade 10, 150 in Grade 11 and 120 in Grade 12. A
stratified sample of 44 students is taken. Find the number of students from each grade. **[3]**

**5.** For a data set, Q₁ = 32 and Q₃ = 47. Determine whether the values 8 and 70 are outliers. **[3]**

**6.** An interviewer stands outside a supermarket and asks the first 20 men and the first 20 women who
agree to answer.

**(a)** Name this sampling technique. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one reason why the results may be biased. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A school canteen manager wants students' views on a new menu. She hands questionnaires to students
eating in the canteen one Monday lunchtime.

**(a)** Name the sampling technique used. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the results may not represent all students in the school. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest a better sampling technique and explain how it would be carried out. **[2]**

**8.** The heights, in cm, of 12 seedlings two weeks after planting are:

2.1, 5.6, 6.0, 6.3, 6.8, 7.1, 7.4, 7.7, 8.0, 8.2, 8.9, 15.4

**(a)** Find the median, the lower quartile and the upper quartile. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the data set contains exactly two outliers. **[3]**
**(c)** Comment on whether each outlier should be kept in the data set. **[2]**

**9.** A phone manufacturer publishes a poll on its own website asking visitors to rate its newest phone.
86% of the 3000 respondents rate it "excellent".

**(a)** Give two reasons why this data source may be unreliable as a measure of how all owners rate the
phone. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether increasing the number of respondents to 30 000 would remove the problem, giving a
reason. **[1]**

**10.** A company employs the following staff.

| | Men | Women |
|---|---|---|
| Full-time | 132 | 108 |
| Part-time | 24 | 36 |

A sample of 25 staff is chosen, stratified by both contract type and gender.

**(a)** Find the number of staff in the sample from each of the four groups. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why stratified sampling is more suitable than simple random sampling here. **[1]**

**11.** Describe how to use a random number generator to choose a simple random sample of 30 people from a
list of 500. **[2]**

**12.** Fifteen values have a mean of 24. On checking, the largest value, 60, is found to be a recording
error and is removed.

**(a)** Find the mean of the remaining 14 values. **[2]**
**(b)** State, with a reason, whether the median is likely to change by more or less than the mean.
**[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Discrete [1].
**(b)** Continuous [1].
**(c)** Discrete: sizes come in fixed steps [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Population: all DP students at Hillcrest School [1]. Sample: the 50 DP students who were asked [1].
**[2]**

**3. (a)** 1200 ÷ 60 = 20 [1].
**(b)** Choose a random number from 1 to 20, for example 7; the sample begins with members 7, 27 and 47
[1]. **[2]**

**4.** Total 660 [1]. Grade 9: (210/660) × 44 = 14; Grade 10: 12 [1]; Grade 11: 10; Grade 12: 8 [1].
Check: 14 + 12 + 10 + 8 = 44. **[3]**

**5.** IQR = 15 and 1.5 × IQR = 22.5 [1]. Boundaries: 32 − 22.5 = 9.5 and 47 + 22.5 = 69.5 [1]. 8 < 9.5 and
70 > 69.5, so both are outliers [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Quota sampling [1].
**(b)** For example: only supermarket shoppers at that time are included; people who agree to answer may
differ from those who refuse; the interviewer chooses whom to approach [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Convenience sampling [1].
**(b)** Only students who eat in the canteen are asked [1], and they are likely to view the menu more
favourably than students who bring their own lunch or avoid the canteen; one day may also be untypical
[1].
**(c)** Stratified sampling by year group (or simple random sampling) [1]: obtain a full list of students,
work out each year group's share of the sample in proportion to its size, then choose that many students at
random from each year group's list [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Median = (7.1 + 7.4)/2 = 7.25 cm [1]. Q₁ = (6.0 + 6.3)/2 = 6.15 cm [1]. Q₃ = (8.0 + 8.2)/2
= 8.1 cm [1].
**(b)** IQR = 1.95 and 1.5 × IQR = 2.925 [1]. Boundaries: 6.15 − 2.925 = 3.225 and 8.1 + 2.925 = 11.025
[1]. Only 2.1 < 3.225 and only 15.4 > 11.025, so there are exactly two outliers [1].
**(c)** 2.1 cm is plausible for a slow-growing seedling, so it is probably genuine and should be kept [1].
15.4 cm is almost twice the next largest value, so it may be a recording error (for example a misread
ruler); it should be checked before deciding whether to keep it [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** Any two: the poll is run by the manufacturer, who benefits from a positive result [1];
respondents choose themselves and are likely to be enthusiasts [1]; there is no check that respondents
own the phone [1]; people may respond more than once [1].
**(b)** No: a larger sample collected by the same biased method is still biased [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Total 300 [1]. Full-time men: (132/300) × 25 = 11; full-time women: 9 [1]; part-time men: 2;
part-time women: 3 [1].
**(b)** The groups differ in size, and a simple random sample of 25 could by chance include few or no
part-time staff; stratifying guarantees each group is represented in proportion [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Number the people from 001 to 500 [1]. Generate random three-digit numbers, selecting the person
with each number, ignoring numbers above 500, 000 and repeats, until 30 people are chosen [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** Sum = 15 × 24 = 360; after removal, 360 − 60 = 300 [1]. Mean = 300/14 = 21.4 (3 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** Less: the median depends on the middle values, which barely move when the largest value is
removed [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "students" rather than "all DP students at Hillcrest School" in question 2.
- Explaining bias in question 7(b) without saying how canteen users differ from other students.
- Measuring outlier boundaries from the median in questions 5 and 8.
- In question 9(b), assuming a larger sample fixes a biased method.
- In question 3(b), forgetting the random start.
