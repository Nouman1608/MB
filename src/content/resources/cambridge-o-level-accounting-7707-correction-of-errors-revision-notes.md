## What the syllabus asks

1. Correct errors using journal entries.
2. Use a suspense account as a temporary measure to balance the trial
balance.
3. Correct errors using a suspense account.
4. Adjust a profit or loss after the correction of errors.
5. State the effect of corrections on a statement of financial position.

## The routine

**Done → Should have been → Correction.** Write the entries actually made,
then the correct entries; the correcting journal entry is the difference.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| General journal | Book of prime entry used for corrections and other non-routine entries |
| Narrative | Short explanation under a journal entry |
| Suspense account | Temporary account holding the trial balance difference until errors are found |
| Undercast | Total added up too low |
| Overcast | Total added up too high |
| Statement of corrected profit | Calculation that starts from draft profit and adds or deducts each correction |
| Draft profit | Profit calculated before the errors were found |

## Six non-suspense corrections

| Error | Correction pattern |
|---|---|
| Omission | Make the full entry now |
| Commission | Debit or credit the wrong account to cancel it; post to the right one |
| Principle | Move the amount from the wrong class to the right class |
| Original entry | Correct the **difference** only, in both accounts |
| Complete reversal | Correct **twice** the amount |
| Compensating | Reverse each error; the two halves are usually different accounts |

## Suspense account rules

- Debit total of the trial balance larger → difference on the **credit**
side of suspense. Credit total larger → difference on the **debit** side.
- Only errors that caused the difference involve suspense: one-sided
entries, unequal amounts, both entries on one side, casting errors, and
balances omitted or misplaced in the trial balance.
- When all are corrected, suspense has no balance.
- A balance left over means at least one error is still undiscovered.

**Suspense entries by error type**

| Error found | Journal entry |
|---|---|
| Revenue account (or sales journal) undercast | Dr Suspense, Cr Revenue |
| Expense account overcast | Dr Suspense, Cr Expense |
| Expense posted to the credit of the expense account instead of the debit | Dr Expense (twice the amount), Cr Suspense (twice the amount) |
| Receipt in cash book not posted to the customer | Dr Suspense, Cr Customer |
| Expense in cash book not posted to the expense account | Dr Expense, Cr Suspense |
| A ledger balance left out of the trial balance | No correcting entry in the ledger: include the balance in the redrafted trial balance |

The last row is a trap: an error made **only in the trial balance**, not in
the ledger, needs no correction to any ledger account. The trial balance is
redrafted and the suspense difference disappears.

## Effect on profit

- Correction **credits** an income or expense account → profit **up**.
- Correction **debits** an income or expense account → profit **down**.
- Correction between two statement of financial position accounts, or
involving drawings → profit **unchanged**.
- Corrections to purchases, purchases returns, sales returns, revenue or
carriage inwards also change **gross profit**.

## Effect on the statement of financial position

- Non-current assets change when a principle error involves them.
- Trade receivables and trade payables change when a personal account is
corrected.
- Capital changes by the change in profit, less any change in drawings.
- The suspense balance disappears.
- Final check: the change in assets equals the change in capital plus
liabilities (including removing suspense).

## Exam traps

- Opening the suspense account on the wrong side.
- Writing the narrative as "error corrected": name the error.
- Single amount for a complete reversal.
- Full amount for an original entry error.
- Putting drawings into the statement of corrected profit.
- Ignoring depreciation when the question gives a policy and a principle
error moves an amount into non-current assets.

## Quick self-test

1. What is a suspense account used for?
2. A trial balance has debits $52,300 and credits $51,950. On which side of
the suspense account is the difference entered, and how much is it?
3. Give the correction for a credit sale of $450 to P Rana that was not
recorded at all.
4. A purchase invoice for $1,380 was entered in the purchases journal as
$1,830. Give the correcting entry.
5. Wages of $500 were debited to bank and credited to wages. Give the
correcting entry.
6. The revenue account was undercast by $200. Give the correcting entry.
7. Electricity of $160 was entered in the cash book but not posted to the
electricity account. Give the correcting entry.
8. What is the effect on profit of the correction in question 7?
9. A $3,000 vehicle repair was debited to the motor vehicles account. State
the effect of the correction on profit and on non-current assets (ignore
depreciation).
10. Why does a correction to drawings not appear in a statement of
corrected profit?
11. After all corrections, the suspense account still has a balance. What
does this show?
12. A correction debits purchases $300 and credits suspense $300. What is
the effect on gross profit?

### Answers

1. Holding the trial balance difference temporarily so the trial balance
balances until the errors are found and corrected.
2. Credit side, $350.
3. Dr P Rana $450, Cr Revenue $450.
4. Dr the supplier's account $450, Cr Purchases $450 (the difference
only, because purchases were overstated).
5. Dr Wages $1,000, Cr Bank $1,000.
6. Dr Suspense $200, Cr Revenue $200.
7. Dr Electricity $160, Cr Suspense $160.
8. Profit falls by $160.
9. Profit falls by $3,000; non-current assets fall by $3,000.
10. Drawings are not an expense; they reduce capital directly.
11. At least one error that caused the difference has not yet been found.
12. Gross profit falls by $300.
