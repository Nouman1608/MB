Countries and figures are invented, and the [1] points in the answers are a revision aid, not IB marks. Revise first with the [3.1 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-1/) or the [3.1 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-1-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define *gross national income (GNI)*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between nominal GDP and real GDP. **[2]**

**3.** A cotton farm sells cotton to a spinner for 30 vars; the spinner sells yarn to a weaver for 55; the weaver sells cloth to a tailor for 95; the tailor sells shirts to consumers for 180. Calculate the total value added and explain why it is not 360. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the output, income and expenditure approaches should give the same value of GDP. **[2]**

**5.** GDP per capita in the fictional country of Varel is 2,400 US dollars at market exchange rates. A basket of goods costing 100 dollars in the United States costs the equivalent of 30 dollars in Varel. Calculate Varel's GDP per capita at PPP. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** phases of the business cycle and what happens to real GDP in each. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Varel's national accounts, in billions of vars:

| Item | Year 1 |
|---|---|
| Consumption | 410 |
| Investment | 95 |
| Government spending | 140 |
| Exports | 180 |
| Imports | 205 |
| Income earned by Varel residents abroad | 22 |
| Income paid to foreign owners of assets in Varel | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate nominal GDP in Year 1. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate GNI in Year 1. **[2]**

**(c)** In Year 2 nominal GDP is 682 billion vars and the GDP deflator is 105 (Year 1 = 100). Calculate real GDP in Year 2 and the real growth rate. **[2]**

**(d)** The population rose from 31 million to 32 million. Calculate the percentage change in real GDP per capita. **[2]**

**8.** Varel's quarterly real GDP, in billions of vars, was: 150, 153, 155, 154, 152, 151, 154.

**(a)** Identify the quarter at which the peak occurred and the quarter at which the trough occurred. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain whether Varel experienced a recession. **[2]**

**9.** Explain, using a business cycle diagram, the difference between the business cycle and the long-term growth trend. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why real GDP per capita may rise while the well-being of most people in a country does not. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how the Happy Planet Index differs from GDP as a measure of a country's success. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate the use of GDP as a measure of well-being. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The total income earned by a country's residents in a period [1]: GDP plus net income from abroad (income earned abroad minus income paid to non-residents) [1].

**2.** Nominal GDP is valued at current prices, so it rises with inflation [1]. Real GDP is adjusted for inflation, valued at base-year prices, so it shows changes in output [1].

**3.** 30 + 25 + 40 + 85 = **180 vars** of value added, equal to the final sale [1]. Adding all sales (360) would count intermediate goods several times (double counting) [1].

**4.** Every unit of spending on final output is received as income by those who produced it [1], and the value of output equals the spending on it, so all three measure the same circular flow [1].

**5.** Each dollar buys 100 ÷ 30 ≈ 3.33 times as much in Varel [1]. GDP per capita at PPP = 2,400 × 100 ÷ 30 = **8,000 dollars** [1].

**6.** Any two: expansion, real GDP rises [1]; peak, real GDP reaches its highest point [1]; contraction, real GDP falls; trough, real GDP reaches its lowest point.

**7. (a)** 410 + 95 + 140 + (180 - 205) = **620 billion vars** [1].

**(b)** Net income from abroad = 22 - 40 = -18 [1]. GNI = 620 - 18 = **602 billion vars** [1].

**(c)** Real GDP = 682 ÷ 105 × 100 = **649.5 billion vars** [1]. Real growth = (649.5 - 620) ÷ 620 × 100 ≈ **4.8%** [1].

**(d)** Year 1: 620 bn ÷ 31 m = 20,000 vars; Year 2: 649.5 bn ÷ 32 m ≈ 20,298 vars [1]. Change ≈ (20,298 - 20,000) ÷ 20,000 × 100 ≈ **1.5%** [1].

**8. (a)** Peak: quarter 3 (155) [1]. Trough: quarter 6 (151) [1].

**(b)** Real GDP fell in quarters 4, 5 and 6 [1], more than two consecutive quarters of decline, so by the common definition Varel was in recession [1].

**9.** Diagram: real GDP on the vertical axis and time on the horizontal axis [1], with an upward-sloping trend line and a wave fluctuating around it, phases labelled [1]. The trend line shows growth in potential output over the long term, driven by more and better resources and technology [1]. The business cycle shows short-term fluctuations of actual output above and below the trend, caused by changes in aggregate demand or shocks [1].

**10.** Real GDP per capita is an average, so a rise can be concentrated among high-income households while most incomes stagnate [1]. Growth may come with longer working hours or more stress, reducing leisure and quality of life [1]. Growth may cause pollution or resource depletion that harms health and future well-being [1]. Some of the extra output may be defensive spending, such as repairing damage, which does not make people better off [1].

**11.** GDP measures the value of market output only [1]. The Happy Planet Index combines measures of well-being and life expectancy with the ecological footprint [1], so it rewards countries that achieve good lives with low resource use, while GDP rewards higher output regardless of environmental cost [1].

**12.** Strengths: GDP is collected regularly using established methods, so it allows comparison over time [1]; real GDP per capita at PPP allows reasonable comparison between countries [1]; higher output usually funds better health, education and infrastructure [1]. Limitations: informal work and home production are omitted [1]; leisure, health and quality of life are ignored [1]; environmental damage is not subtracted [1]; the average hides how income is distributed [1]. Judgement: GDP is a useful measure of economic activity but a partial measure of well-being, so it is best used alongside alternatives such as the OECD Better Life Index, especially where informal work or inequality is large [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using gross income from abroad instead of **net** income when calculating GNI.
- Forgetting to multiply by 100 when using the deflator.
- Calling a slowdown in growth a recession.
- Listing weaknesses of GDP without explaining their effect on well-being.
