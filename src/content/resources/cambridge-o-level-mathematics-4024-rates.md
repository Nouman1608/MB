This guide covers subtopic **1.12 Rates** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. A rate compares two quantities with different units, such as money per hour or distance per hour. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page covers the common rates, the compound measures that the syllabus names, and the one formula that you must remember: speed, distance and time.

Companion pages: the [Rates revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-rates-revision-notes/) and the [Rates practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-rates-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

The syllabus lists three outcomes:

1. Use common measures of rate, such as hourly rates of pay, exchange rates between currencies, flow rates and fuel consumption.
2. Apply other measures of rate, such as pressure, density and population density. The notes say that **the required formulas will be given in the question**.
3. Solve problems involving average speed. Here the notes say that **knowledge of the speed, distance and time formula is required**.

The notation for rates is of the form m/s (metres per second) and g/cm³ (grams per cubic centimetre). The word "per" means "for each", and the slash means "divided by".

## What a rate is

A rate tells you how much of one quantity there is for each one unit of another. The method for almost every rate problem is the same:

1. Find the rate for **one** unit.
2. Multiply or divide to get what you need.

If you write the units in your working, they tell you whether to multiply or divide. For example, dollars per hour × hours gives dollars.

## Pay rates

**Worked example.** Maya earns $8.40 per hour. She works 37.5 hours in a week. Then she works 6 extra hours at "time and a half" (1.5 times her normal rate). Find her total pay.

```
normal pay:    8.40 x 37.5 = $315.00
overtime rate: 8.40 x 1.5  = $12.60 per hour
overtime pay:  12.60 x 6   = $75.60
total:         315.00 + 75.60 = $390.60
```

## Exchange rates

An exchange rate says how much of one currency equals one unit of another. To convert, multiply by the rate when going to the currency with the larger numbers, or divide when going back.

**Worked example.** The exchange rate is $1 = 4.5 francs. (a) Change $80 into francs. (b) Change 1 125 francs into dollars.

```
(a) 80 x 4.5 = 360 francs
(b) 1125 ÷ 4.5 = $250
```

A check: francs are the smaller unit, so there should be more francs than dollars. The answer in (a) is bigger than 80, and in (b) the dollar answer is smaller than 1125.

## Flow rates

A flow rate measures volume per unit of time, such as litres per minute.

**Worked example.** A tap delivers 12 litres per minute. How long does it take to fill a 540 litre tank?

```
time = volume ÷ rate = 540 ÷ 12 = 45 minutes
```

**Worked example.** A pool has an inlet pipe delivering 15 litres per minute and a leak that loses 6 litres per minute. How long will it take to put 450 litres into the pool?

```
net rate = 15 - 6 = 9 litres per minute
time = 450 ÷ 9 = 50 minutes
```

## Fuel consumption

Fuel consumption can be given as litres per 100 km or as kilometres per litre. Read the units carefully.

**Worked example.** A car uses 6.4 litres of fuel for every 100 km. (a) How much fuel is needed for 350 km? (b) How far can it go on 56 litres?

```
(a) 350 km is 3.5 lots of 100 km:   6.4 x 3.5 = 22.4 litres
(b) 56 ÷ 6.4 = 8.75 lots of 100 km: 8.75 x 100 = 875 km
```

## Density

Density measures how much mass there is in a given volume.

```
density = mass ÷ volume
```

The units come from the data: if mass is in grams and volume in cm³, the density is in g/cm³.

**Worked example.** A metal block has mass 540 g and volume 200 cm³. Find its density.

```
density = 540 ÷ 200 = 2.7 g/cm³
```

**Worked example.** Find the mass of 125 cm³ of a material with density 8.0 g/cm³.

```
mass = density x volume = 8.0 x 125 = 1000 g
```

Rearrange the formula as needed: mass = density × volume, and volume = mass ÷ density. A triangle with mass on top and density and volume at the bottom is a way to remember it. The syllabus says that such formulas will be supplied, but you still need to rearrange them.

## Pressure

Pressure measures force per unit area.

```
pressure = force ÷ area
```

**Worked example.** A force of 600 newtons acts on an area of 0.25 m². Find the pressure.

```
pressure = 600 ÷ 0.25 = 2400 N/m²
```

A smaller area gives a bigger pressure for the same force. That is why a sharp knife cuts more easily than a blunt one.

## Population density

Population density is the number of people per unit area.

```
population density = number of people ÷ area
```

**Worked example.** A region has 1 200 000 people in an area of 480 km². Find its population density.

```
1 200 000 ÷ 480 = 2500 people per km²
```

## Speed, distance and time

You must remember this formula:

```
speed = distance ÷ time
distance = speed x time
time = distance ÷ speed
```

Use the units consistently. If speed is in km/h, distance must be in km and time in hours. Convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60.

**Worked example.** A car travels 78 km in 1 hour 45 minutes. Find its average speed.

```
1 h 45 min = 1.75 h
speed = 78 ÷ 1.75 = 44.571... km/h
average speed = 44.6 km/h to 3 s.f.
```

**Worked example.** How long does a journey of 150 km take at 80 km/h?

```
time = 150 ÷ 80 = 1.875 hours
0.875 h = 0.875 x 60 = 52.5 minutes
time = 1 hour 52.5 minutes, which is 1 hour 52 minutes 30 seconds
```

### Average speed over a whole journey

The average speed for a journey with several stages is the **total distance divided by the total time**. It is not the mean of the speeds unless the times are equal.

**Worked example.** A train travels 120 km at 60 km/h and then 90 km at 45 km/h. Find its average speed for the whole journey.

```
time for the first part:  120 ÷ 60 = 2 hours
time for the second part:  90 ÷ 45 = 2 hours
total distance: 210 km,   total time: 4 hours
average speed = 210 ÷ 4 = 52.5 km/h
```

### Changing between km/h and m/s

To change km/h to m/s, multiply by 1000 and divide by 3600, which is the same as dividing by 3.6. To go from m/s to km/h, multiply by 3.6.

```
72 km/h = 72 ÷ 3.6 = 20 m/s
15 m/s  = 15 x 3.6 = 54 km/h
```

**Worked example.** A runner covers 400 m in 50 seconds. Find her speed in km/h.

```
speed = 400 ÷ 50 = 8 m/s
8 x 3.6 = 28.8 km/h
```

## Exam technique

- Write the formula that you are using, then substitute the numbers, then calculate.
- Check that the units match before you substitute.
- Convert minutes to hours before using km/h. 1 h 45 min is 1.75 hours, not 1.45.
- Give a unit with each answer (km/h, g/cm³, N/m²).
- For average speed, use total distance over total time.
- For hours, a decimal like 0.875 h must be converted to minutes by multiplying by 60.

## Common mistakes

- Writing 1 hour 45 minutes as 1.45 hours.
- Averaging speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
- Multiplying instead of dividing when a rate is given as "per" something.
- Using the wrong direction in an exchange rate problem.
- Forgetting to subtract the leak from the inflow in a net rate problem.
- Mixing units, such as metres with km/h.
- Mixing up mass and volume in density.
