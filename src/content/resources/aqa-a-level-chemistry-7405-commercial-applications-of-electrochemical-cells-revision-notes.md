## What you must be able to do

- Recall the **electrode reactions in a lithium cell**.
- Classify cells as **non-rechargeable, rechargeable or fuel cells**.
- **Use electrode data** to deduce the reactions in non-rechargeable and rechargeable cells.
- **Deduce the EMF** of a cell.
- Explain how electrode reactions **generate an electric current**.
- Write the **electrode reactions of an alkaline hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell**.
- Discuss **benefits and risks** to society.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electrochemical cell | Two electrodes in an electrolyte; chemical energy becomes electrical energy |
| Negative electrode | The more negative E value; oxidation; electrons leave here |
| Positive electrode | The more positive E value; reduction; electrons arrive here |
| EMF | E(positive) – E(negative), standard conditions, no current |
| Non-rechargeable cell | Irreversible; used until a reactant runs out |
| Rechargeable cell | Reversible reactions; an external current runs them backwards |
| Fuel cell | Fuel and oxidant supplied continuously; no electrical recharging |
| Electrolyte | Ionic conductor between the electrodes; ions carry the current inside the cell |

## The lithium cell

```
Positive electrode:  Li⁺ + CoO₂ + e⁻ → Li⁺[CoO₂]⁻
Negative electrode:  Li → Li⁺ + e⁻
Overall:             Li + CoO₂ → Li⁺[CoO₂]⁻
```

- Lithium is oxidised (0 to +1); cobalt is reduced (+4 to +3).
- Li⁺/Li has E = –3.04 V, so a very high EMF is possible.
- Lithium is light, so a lot of energy is stored for the mass.
- Lithium reacts with water, so the electrolyte is non-aqueous.
- Lithium cells can be non-rechargeable or rechargeable.

## Three types of cell

| Type | Key idea | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Non-rechargeable | Reaction goes one way; reactants used up | Zinc–carbon, alkaline, button cells |
| Rechargeable | Reaction reversed by an external supply | Lithium-ion, nickel–cadmium |
| Fuel cell | Reactants fed in from outside | Hydrogen–oxygen |

On **recharging**, the positive electrode is where **oxidation** happens and the negative electrode is where **reduction** happens: everything is the reverse of discharge.

## Method for electrode data

1. Write both half-equations as reductions with E values.
2. **More negative E = negative electrode.** Reverse that half-equation (oxidation).
3. **More positive E = positive electrode.** Keep it as reduction.
4. Balance electrons, add, cancel species that appear on both sides.
5. EMF = E(positive) – E(negative).

The E value is **not** multiplied when you multiply a half-equation.

## Worked examples

**Silver oxide cell.** Ag₂O + H₂O + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Ag + 2OH⁻, E = +0.34 V; Zn(OH)₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn + 2OH⁻, E = –1.25 V.

```
Negative: Zn + 2OH⁻ → Zn(OH)₂ + 2e⁻
Positive: Ag₂O + H₂O + 2e⁻ → 2Ag + 2OH⁻
Overall:  Zn + Ag₂O + H₂O → Zn(OH)₂ + 2Ag
EMF = +0.34 – (–1.25) = +1.59 V
```

**Nickel–cadmium cell.** NiO(OH) + H₂O + e⁻ ⇌ Ni(OH)₂ + OH⁻, E = +0.49 V; Cd(OH)₂ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Cd + 2OH⁻, E = –0.81 V.

```
Negative: Cd + 2OH⁻ → Cd(OH)₂ + 2e⁻
Positive: 2NiO(OH) + 2H₂O + 2e⁻ → 2Ni(OH)₂ + 2OH⁻
Overall:  Cd + 2NiO(OH) + 2H₂O → Cd(OH)₂ + 2Ni(OH)₂
EMF = +0.49 – (–0.81) = +1.30 V
```

## Alkaline hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell

```
Negative electrode:  H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻
Positive electrode:  ½O₂ + H₂O + 2e⁻ → 2OH⁻
Overall:             H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O
```

Data: O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ ⇌ 4OH⁻, +0.40 V; 2H₂O + 2e⁻ ⇌ H₂ + 2OH⁻, –0.83 V. EMF = 0.40 – (–0.83) = **+1.23 V**.

- Electrolyte: aqueous potassium hydroxide.
- Electrodes: porous, with a catalyst such as platinum, for a large surface area.
- Hydrogen releases electrons at the negative electrode; they flow through the external circuit to the positive electrode, where they reduce oxygen.
- Hydroxide ions move through the electrolyte to complete the circuit.
- Only water is made. The cell runs while fuel is supplied.

## Explaining how a current is generated

Use this model answer: (1) oxidation at the negative electrode releases electrons; (2) electrons flow through the external circuit to the positive electrode; (3) reduction at the positive electrode uses them; (4) ions move through the electrolyte to complete the circuit.

## Benefits and risks

| Cell | Benefit | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Non-rechargeable | Cheap, portable, long shelf life | Waste; toxic metals if not recycled |
| Rechargeable | Reused, less waste | Toxic cadmium; lithium fire risk; metal mining; capacity loss |
| Hydrogen fuel cell | Only water at point of use; efficient | Hydrogen flammable and hard to store; hydrogen often made from fossil fuels; costly catalyst |

## Exam traps

- Negative electrode given as the one with the **more positive** E.
- Forgetting to reverse the half-equation at the negative electrode.
- Adding E values instead of subtracting.
- Multiplying E by the number of electrons.
- Electrons left in the overall equation.
- Saying a fuel cell stores fuel inside it.
- Calling a hydrogen vehicle "zero emission" without mentioning how the hydrogen was made.
- Missing out the movement of ions in the electrolyte when asked how the circuit is completed.

## Self-test

1. Write the half-equation at the negative electrode of a lithium cell.
2. State the oxidation state of cobalt in CoO₂ and in Li⁺[CoO₂]⁻.
3. Why is a lithium cell given a non-aqueous electrolyte?
4. Cell X has E(positive) = +0.80 V and E(negative) = –0.13 V. Calculate the EMF.
5. In a rechargeable cell, what happens at the positive electrode during recharging?
6. Why does a non-rechargeable cell eventually stop working?
7. Write the half-equation for the hydrogen electrode in an alkaline fuel cell.
8. What is the EMF of the alkaline hydrogen–oxygen fuel cell, using +0.40 V and –0.83 V?
9. Why are fuel cell electrodes porous?
10. Give one risk of using cadmium-based rechargeable cells.
11. Does the E value change if a half-equation is doubled?
12. State one reason why a hydrogen fuel cell vehicle may not be carbon neutral.

### Answers

1. Li → Li⁺ + e⁻.
2. +4 in CoO₂; +3 in Li⁺[CoO₂]⁻.
3. Lithium reacts with water.
4. 0.80 – (–0.13) = +0.93 V.
5. Oxidation (the reverse of the discharge reduction).
6. A reactant is used up and the reaction cannot be reversed in practice.
7. H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻.
8. +1.23 V.
9. They give a large surface area where gas, electrolyte and catalyst meet.
10. Cadmium is toxic and can pollute if the cell is not recycled.
11. No.
12. The hydrogen may be made from fossil fuels, using energy and releasing carbon dioxide.
