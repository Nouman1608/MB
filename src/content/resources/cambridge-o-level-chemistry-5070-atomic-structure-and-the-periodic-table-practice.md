These questions cover section 2.2, Atomic structure and the Periodic Table, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). Use a copy of the Periodic Table for proton numbers. Revise first with the [atomic structure and the Periodic Table study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-atomic-structure-and-the-periodic-table/) and the [atomic structure and the Periodic Table revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-atomic-structure-and-the-periodic-table-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the relative charge of a proton and of an electron. **[2]**

**2.** Define *proton number*. **[1]**

**3.** An atom of manganese has proton number 25 and mass number 55. State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the atom. **[3]**

**4.** Write the electronic configuration of each of the following.

**(a)** A beryllium atom (Z = 4) **[1]**

**(b)** A sulfur atom (Z = 16) **[1]**

**(c)** A potassium ion, K⁺ (Z = 19) **[1]**

**5.** An element is in Period 3 and Group II. Write its electronic configuration. **[1]**

**6.** State why argon is unreactive, in terms of its electronic configuration. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table describes four particles.

| Particle | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8 | 8 | 10 |
| B | 12 | 12 | 10 |
| C | 10 | 10 | 10 |
| D | 8 | 10 | 8 |

**(a)** Identify the particle that is a positive ion and give its charge. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the particle that is a neutral atom of a noble gas. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the mass number of particle D. **[1]**

**(d)** Write the electronic configuration of particle A. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why the relative mass of an atom is approximately equal to its mass number. **[2]**

**9.** Element Q has the electronic configuration 2,8,7.

**(a)** State the group and period of Q. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify Q. **[1]**

**(c)** Q forms an ion by gaining one electron. Write the symbol and electronic configuration of the ion. **[2]**

**10.** A student writes: "A magnesium ion, Mg²⁺, has 2,8 electrons, so magnesium is in Period 2." Explain the error. **[2]**

**11.** Describe the structure of an atom of nitrogen-14 (proton number 7). Include the numbers and positions of all its subatomic particles. **[4]**

**12.** An atom of element X has three more protons than an atom of neon (Z = 10). An ion of X has 10 electrons.

**(a)** Identify X and write its electronic configuration as an atom. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the charge on the ion of X. Show your reasoning. **[2]**

**(c)** State the group and period of X. **[2]**

**13.** The table shows information about three elements.

| Element | Electronic configuration of atom |
|---|---|
| J | 2,8,1 |
| L | 2,8,8 |
| M | 2,6 |

**(a)** Identify the element that is a noble gas. Explain your choice. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the two elements that are in the same period, and state the period. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the electronic configuration of the ion formed when M gains two electrons, and name another particle with the same configuration. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Proton: **+1** [1]. Electron: **−1** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Protons **25** [1]; neutrons 55 − 25 = **30** [1]; electrons **25** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** 2,2 [1]

**(b)** 2,8,6 [1]

**(c)** 19 − 1 = 18 electrons: 2,8,8 [1]

**[3]**

**5.** 2,8,2 [1]. **[1]**

**6.** It has a **full outer shell** (2,8,8), so it does not need to lose, gain or share electrons [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** **B** [1]; charge **2+** (12 protons, 10 electrons) [1]

**(b)** **C** (neon: 10 protons and 10 electrons, full outer shell) [1]

**(c)** 8 + 10 = **18** [1]

**(d)** 10 electrons: **2,8** [1]

**[5]**

*Examiner insight:* A and D have the same number of protons, so they are both oxygen. A is the oxide ion O²⁻; D is a neutral oxygen atom with mass number 18.

**8.** Protons and neutrons each have a relative mass of **1** [1], while electrons have **negligible** mass (1/1840), so the mass is almost all due to the nucleons counted by the mass number [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Group **VII** [1]; Period **3** [1]

**(b)** Chlorine (17 electrons, so proton number 17) [1]

**(c)** **Cl⁻** [1]; **2,8,8** [1]

**[5]**

**10.** The period is given by the number of occupied shells in the **atom**, not the ion [1]. A magnesium atom is **2,8,2**, with three occupied shells, so magnesium is in **Period 3** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** A central **nucleus** [1] containing **7 protons** and **7 neutrons** (14 − 7) [1]; **7 electrons** in shells around the nucleus [1], arranged **2,5** (2 in the first shell, 5 in the second) [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** 10 + 3 = 13 protons, so X is **aluminium** [1]; atom configuration **2,8,3** [1]

**(b)** 13 protons (+13) and 10 electrons (−10) [1], so the charge is **3+** (Al³⁺): the atom has lost its three outer electrons [1]

**(c)** Group **III** (three outer electrons) [1]; Period **3** (three occupied shells in the atom) [1]

**[6]**

**13. (a)** **L** [1]: it has a **full outer shell** (2,8,8), which is the arrangement of the Group VIII noble gases (argon) [1]

**(b)** **J and L** [1]: both have three occupied shells, so both are in **Period 3** [1]

**(c)** M (oxygen) gains two electrons: O²⁻ with configuration **2,8** [1]; any one of Ne, F⁻, N³⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ [1]

**[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 0 for the relative mass of an electron.
- Adding the charge instead of subtracting it for positive ions.
- Overfilling shells: nine electrons in the second shell, or three in the first.
- Using an ion's configuration to find the period of the element.
- Describing the atom without giving positions of the particles.
