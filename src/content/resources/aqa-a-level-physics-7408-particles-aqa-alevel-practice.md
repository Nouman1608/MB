These questions cover section 3.2.1 Particles (3.2.1.1 to 3.2.1.7) of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408). Section A has short recall and calculation questions; Section B has structured questions. Learn the content first with the [Particles study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-particles-aqa-alevel/) and the [Particles revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-particles-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/).

Use e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, nucleon mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, 1 MeV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ J, and muon rest energy 105.7 MeV.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** An ion of chlorine is ³⁷₁₇Cl⁻.

**(a)** State the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in the ion. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the specific charge of the ion. **[2]**

**2.** State two properties of the strong nuclear force and explain why each is needed for a stable nucleus. **[3]**

**3.** Cobalt-60 (⁶⁰₂₇Co) decays by β⁻ emission to an isotope of nickel. Write the full decay equation. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the existence of the neutrino was hypothesised to account for β⁻ decay. **[2]**

**5.** Classify each of these as a baryon, antibaryon, meson or lepton: π⁺, μ⁻, n̄, K⁰. **[2]**

**6.** State the quark content of an antineutron and use it to show that its charge is zero and its baryon number is −1. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A high-energy photon produces a muon and an antimuon.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum energy of the photon, in J. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of a photon with this energy. **[1]**
**(c)** A photon of energy 250 MeV produces the pair. Calculate the total kinetic energy of the muon and antimuon, in MeV. **[1]**
**(d)** State why this process normally takes place near a nucleus. **[1]**

**8.** In electron capture, a proton in a nucleus interacts with an inner electron.

**(a)** Write the equation for this interaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the exchange particle and describe the diagram for the interaction, showing how charge is conserved at the proton vertex. **[3]**
**(c)** State how you can tell this is a weak interaction. **[1]**

**9.** For each interaction, state whether it is possible and name the quantity that is not conserved if it is not.

**(a)** p + p → p + n + π⁺ **[2]**
**(b)** e⁻ + p → n + ν̄ₑ **[2]**
**(c)** K⁰ → π⁺ + π⁻ **[2]**
**(d)** μ⁺ → e⁺ + νₑ + ν̄_μ **[2]**

**10.** A meson has quark content sd̄.

**(a)** Find its charge, baryon number and strangeness. **[3]**
**(b)** Identify the meson. **[1]**
**(c)** It decays into two pions. State which interaction is responsible and give a reason. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Calculate the ratio of the specific charge of a ¹²C nucleus to that of a ¹⁴C nucleus. **[2]**
**(b)** The specific charge of an electron is about 1800 times that of a proton. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** Carbon-11 decays by β⁺ emission: ¹¹₆C → ¹¹₅B + e⁺ + νₑ.

**(a)** State the quark change that takes place. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that charge, baryon number and lepton number are conserved in the decay. **[3]**

**13.** Kaons can be produced in the reaction p + p → p + p + K⁺ + K⁻.

**(a)** Show that strangeness is conserved in this reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why strange particles are always produced in pairs, and state which interaction they decay by. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 17 protons, 37 − 17 = 20 neutrons [1]; 17 + 1 = 18 electrons [1].
**(b)** Charge magnitude = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; mass = 37 × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ = 6.18 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; specific charge = **2.59 × 10⁶ C kg⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The ion's charge is the net charge, one electron's worth, not 17 or 18 times e.

**2.** It is attractive between about 0.5 fm and 3 fm, which overcomes the electrostatic repulsion between protons [1]. It is repulsive below about 0.5 fm [1], which stops the nucleons being pulled into each other and the nucleus collapsing [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Short range" alone needs its consequence: it acts only between neighbouring nucleons.

**3.** ⁶⁰₂₇Co → ⁶⁰₂₈Ni + ⁰₋₁β [1] + ν̄ₑ [1].

**4.** β⁻ particles are emitted with a continuous range of kinetic energies up to a maximum [1]; without another particle carrying the rest of the energy, energy would not be conserved, so the (anti)neutrino was proposed [1].

**5.** π⁺ meson and K⁰ meson [1]; μ⁻ lepton and n̄ antibaryon [1].

**6.** ūd̄d̄ [1]. Charge = −2/3 + 1/3 + 1/3 = 0, and B = −1/3 × 3 = −1 [1].

**7. (a)** E = 2 × 105.7 = 211.4 MeV [1]; 211.4 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹³ = **3.38 × 10⁻¹¹ J** [1].
**(b)** λ = hc/E = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) / 3.38 × 10⁻¹¹ = **5.88 × 10⁻¹⁵ m** [1].
**(c)** 250 − 211.4 = **38.6 MeV** [1].
**(d)** The nucleus takes up some momentum, so momentum and energy can both be conserved [1].
*Examiner insight:* The minimum is the rest energy of *both* particles. Using one muon's rest energy halves the answer.

**8. (a)** p + e⁻ → n + νₑ [1].
**(b)** W⁺ [1]. The proton line enters at the bottom and leaves as a neutron; a W⁺ travels from this vertex to the electron line, where the electron becomes an electron neutrino [1]. At the proton vertex: +1 → 0 + (+1), so charge is conserved [1].
**(c)** It involves a neutrino (or a quark changes flavour, u → d) [1].

**9. (a)** Q: 2 → 2; B: 2 → 2 [1]. **Possible** [1].
**(b)** Lₑ: +1 → −1 [1]. **Not possible**; lepton number is not conserved [1].
**(c)** Q: 0 → 0; B: 0 → 0; S: +1 → 0 [1]. **Possible** by the weak interaction, which allows strangeness to change by 1 [1].
**(d)** Lₑ: 0 → −1 + 1 = 0; L_μ: −1 → −1 [1]. **Possible** [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (b), the reaction e⁻ + p → n + νₑ is the possible one. Lepton numbers must balance for each family separately.

**10. (a)** Q = −1/3 + 1/3 = **0** [1]; B = 1/3 − 1/3 = **0** [1]; S = −1 + 0 = **−1** [1].
**(b)** The **anti-K⁰ (K̄⁰)** [1].
**(c)** The weak interaction [1], because strangeness changes from −1 to 0 [1].

**11. (a)** Same charge, so the ratio is the inverse of the mass ratio [1]: 14/12 = **1.17** [1].
**(b)** The electron and proton have charges of the same magnitude [1], but the proton's mass is about 1800 times the electron's, so its charge-to-mass ratio is about 1800 times smaller [1].

**12. (a)** An up quark changes to a down quark (u → d) [1].
**(b)** Charge: +6 → +5 + 1 + 0 [1]. Baryon number: 11 → 11 + 0 + 0 [1]. Lepton number: 0 → −1 (positron) + 1 (neutrino) = 0 [1].

**13. (a)** S: 0 + 0 → 0 + 0 + 1 − 1 = 0 [1].
**(b)** They are produced by the strong interaction, which conserves strangeness, so a particle with S = +1 must be made with one of S = −1 [1]; they decay by the weak interaction [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole nuclear charge for a singly charged ion.
- Forgetting the antineutrino, or writing a neutrino, in β⁻ decay.
- Using one particle's rest energy for pair production.
- Checking total lepton number rather than Lₑ and L_μ separately.
- Naming the wrong W boson in electron capture or β⁺ decay.
- Stating "strangeness is not conserved, so impossible" for a weak decay.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I decide whether an interaction is possible?**
Check charge, baryon number, electron lepton number and muon lepton number in turn; all must be conserved. Then check strangeness: it must be conserved in a strong interaction but can change by one in a weak interaction.

**Why does pair production need a minimum photon energy?**
The photon's energy must at least equal the total rest energy of the particle and antiparticle it creates. Any extra energy becomes their kinetic energy.
