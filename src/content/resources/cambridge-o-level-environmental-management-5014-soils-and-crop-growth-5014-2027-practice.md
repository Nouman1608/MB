## Section B

**6.** Describe how pore spaces in soil are important for crop growth. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why a crop grown in a clay soil may grow poorly after a long period of heavy rain. **[3]**

**8.** Explain **four** reasons why loam is a good medium for crop growth. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the role of decomposers in providing nutrients for crops. **[3]**

**10.** *Invented data.* The table shows climate data for a farming area.

| Month | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun | Jul | Aug | Sep | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean temperature (°C) | 2 | 4 | 8 | 12 | 16 | 19 | 21 | 20 | 17 | 12 | 7 | 3 |
| Daylight hours | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 15 | 16 | 16 | 14 | 12 | 10 | 9 | 8 |

A crop grows only when the mean temperature is at least 10 °C.

(a) Identify the length of the growing season in months.

(b) Describe how daylight hours change through the growing season.

(c) Explain why the crop grows fastest in June and July. **[6]**

**11.** In a region with a wet season and a dry season, the wet season starts three weeks later than usual.
Suggest how this could affect farmers' crops. **[3]**

**12.** A farmer has a sandy soil. Explain the problems this soil causes for crop growth and suggest how
adding organic matter could help. **[5]**

**13.** A farm has two fields. Field A has a loam soil and Field B has a clay soil. Compare the two soils
for drainage, air content and ease of cultivation. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Sand, silt, clay: all three named [1], in the correct order [1].

**2.** The day-to-day conditions of the atmosphere in a location [1].

**3.** (a) Nitrogen [1]. (b) Potassium [1].

**4.** Any two: living organisms such as plants, animals, fungi or bacteria [1]; organic matter from
decomposition [1]; dead leaves or roots [1]; animal waste [1].

**5.** Growth is reduced, because some nutrients are less available to the roots [1].

**6.** Any three:

- Pore spaces hold air [1], providing oxygen for root respiration [1].
- Pore spaces hold water [1], which roots take up for photosynthesis [1].
- Large pores allow excess water to drain, preventing waterlogging [1].
- Pores allow roots to grow through the soil [1].

**7.** Any three:

- Clay particles are tiny, so pores are very small [1].
- Water drains slowly, so the soil becomes waterlogged [1].
- The pore spaces fill with water, leaving little air [1].
- Roots cannot get enough oxygen for respiration, so growth slows or roots die [1].

**8.** Any four:

- It has an ideal combination of mineral particles and pore spaces, giving both air and water [1].
- It retains moisture, so roots do not dry out between rains [1].
- It contains organic content and inorganic ions, supplying nutrients [1].
- It allows excess water to drain, so it does not waterlog [1].
- It is easy to cultivate, so ploughing and planting are easier [1].

**9.** Any three:

- Decomposers, such as bacteria and fungi, break down dead plants and animal waste [1].
- This releases inorganic ions, such as nitrate, into the soil [1].
- The ions dissolve in soil water [1].
- Crop roots absorb the ions [1].

**10.** (a) April to October, **7 months** [1 for identifying the months, 1 for the length].
(b) Daylight hours rise from 14 in April to a peak of 16 in June and July [1], then fall to 10 in
October [1].
(c) These months have the highest temperatures, 19 °C and 21 °C [1], and the longest daylight, 16 hours,
so there is most time and warmth for photosynthesis [1].

**11.** Any three:

- Crops cannot be planted on time, shortening the growing season [1].
- Seeds already planted may fail through lack of water [1].
- Yields may fall, because the crop has less time to grow before the dry season [1].
- Farmers may need to irrigate, which costs money [1].
- Food shortages or lower income for farming families [1].

**12.** Problems, any three:

- Large pores mean water drains quickly, so the soil dries out [1].
- Nutrients are washed out of the soil (leached) [1].
- The crop is short of water and nutrients, so growth is poor [1].
- Little organic content, so few nutrients are released [1].

Organic matter, any two:

- Holds water, so the soil retains moisture [1].
- Releases nutrients as it decomposes [1].
- Binds sand grains together, improving soil structure [1].

**13.** Any four comparative points:

- Loam drains excess water well, whereas clay drains slowly [1].
- Clay can become waterlogged after rain, but loam rarely does [1].
- Loam has good air content in its larger pores, whereas clay has little air when wet [1].
- Loam is easy to plough and plant, whereas clay is heavy and sticky when wet [1].
- Clay sets hard and cracks when dry, but loam stays workable [1].

*Tip:* "compare" needs both soils in each point; describing each field separately scores less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing sand and clay when describing drainage and water holding.
- Forgetting that roots need oxygen, so waterlogging harms them.
- Listing loam's features without saying how each helps the crop.
- In 10(a), leaving out April or October (both 12 °C), or giving the months without the number.
- Describing data without quoting figures.
