These notes condense world history topic 9 for Paper 2: how democratic states emerged between 1848 and 2000, what threatened them and how their governments responded. The arguments are developed in the [democratic states study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-9/); practise using them with the [democratic states practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-9-practice/).

## The topic in three questions

| Strand | Ask of every case |
|---|---|
| Emergence | What conditions produced demands for democracy? What institutions were created, and how democratic were they? |
| Challenges and responses | What political, economic and social threats arose? What did governments do, and did it work? |
| Policies | What were the aims and results of economic, social and cultural policies, including for women and minorities? |

## Case 1: Weimar Germany, 1918-1933 (Europe)

- 9 November 1918: Kaiser abdicates; republic proclaimed. 11 November: armistice.
- January 1919: Spartacist rising crushed with help from the *Freikorps*.
- 1919: constitution adopted at Weimar; proportional representation, women vote, Article 48.
- June 1919: Treaty of Versailles signed; the republic blamed by nationalists ("stab in the back" myth).
- March 1920: Kapp Putsch defeated by a general strike.
- 1923: French and Belgian occupation of the Ruhr; hyperinflation; Munich Putsch (November).
- 1923-29: Stresemann era: Rentenmark (1923), Dawes Plan (1924), Locarno (1925), League of Nations (1926), Young Plan (1929).
- 1929 onwards: Depression; mass unemployment.
- March 1930: grand coalition collapses; Brüning governs by presidential decree.
- July 1932: Nazis become the largest party in the Reichstag.
- 30 January 1933: Hitler appointed chancellor.

**Verdict to argue:** survived the early crises but collapsed when economic disaster met structural weakness and elite hostility.

## Case 2: India, 1947-1977 (Asia and Oceania)

- 1947: independence and partition.
- 26 January 1950: constitution in force (drafting committee chaired by B. R. Ambedkar); universal adult suffrage; untouchability abolished; reservations for Scheduled Castes and Tribes.
- 1951-52: first general election; Congress wins under Nehru.
- Mid-1950s: Hindu Code Bills reform family law.
- 1956: states reorganized on linguistic lines.
- June 1975-March 1977: the Emergency under Indira Gandhi; civil liberties suspended, opponents jailed, press censored.
- 1977: Congress loses the election to the Janata Party.

**Verdict to argue:** a durable democracy in a poor, diverse society; the 1977 election showed that voters could remove a government that had suspended liberties.

## Case 3: South Africa, 1990-2000s (Africa and the Middle East)

- February 1990: Mandela released; ANC unbanned.
- 1993: interim constitution agreed in negotiations.
- 27 April 1994: first universal elections; Mandela president; Government of National Unity.
- 1994: Reconstruction and Development Programme (RDP).
- 1995: Truth and Reconciliation Commission established (chair: Desmond Tutu).
- 1996: final constitution with a Bill of Rights; GEAR economic strategy.
- 1999: Mbeki succeeds Mandela after elections.

**Verdict to argue:** a peaceful political transition and strong constitution, but deep inequality and unemployment limited social and economic change.

## Challenge types and examples

| Challenge | Example | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Armed attacks on the state | Kapp Putsch (1920) | Workers' general strike; the army refused to fire on the putschists |
| Economic collapse | Hyperinflation (1923) | Rentenmark, Dawes Plan |
| Economic depression | 1929 onwards in Germany | Austerity and rule by decree under Brüning |
| Religious or communal division | Partition and its aftermath in India | Secular constitution; protection of minority rights |
| Legacy of racial discrimination | Apartheid in South Africa | TRC; Bill of Rights; affirmative action and Black Economic Empowerment |
| Leaders undermining democracy | The Emergency (1975-77) in India | Elections of 1977 removed the government |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Franchise | The right to vote |
| Proportional representation | Seats allocated in proportion to votes; can fragment parliament |
| Coalition | A government formed by more than one party |
| Constitution | The fundamental rules that set out how a state is governed and what rights citizens have |
| Separation of powers | Division of authority between legislature, executive and judiciary |
| Civil society | Organizations independent of the state, such as unions, churches and the press |
| Emergency powers | Constitutional powers to suspend normal procedures in a crisis (Weimar's Article 48) |
| Reservation | Seats or posts set aside for disadvantaged groups (India) |
| Transitional justice | Ways of dealing with past abuses during a move to democracy, such as truth commissions |

## Exam traps

- **No definition of democracy.** Set criteria in the introduction and use them.
- **Telling the story of the Nazi rise.** Keep the focus on the democracy and why it failed to defend itself.
- **Equating "survived" with "successful".** India survived the Emergency only after civil liberties were suspended; South Africa's democracy survived but inequality persisted.
- **One-sided policy answers.** Separate aims from results, and legal change from social change.
- **Ignoring the date limits.** The topic begins in 1848; cases before that date are outside it.

## Self-test

1. What was Article 48? *The emergency decree power of the Weimar president.*
2. Name two features of the Weimar constitution. *Any two: proportional representation, votes for women, an elected president, Article 48, a bill of rights.*
3. How was the Kapp Putsch defeated? *By a general strike, after the army refused to act against it.*
4. List three achievements of the Stresemann era. *Any three: Rentenmark, Dawes Plan, Locarno, League membership, Young Plan.*
5. When did Brüning begin governing by presidential decree? *From 1930, after the grand coalition collapsed in March.*
6. Who chaired India's constitution drafting committee? *B. R. Ambedkar.*
7. What was the Emergency? *A period from 1975 to 1977 when Indira Gandhi suspended civil liberties and ruled by decree.*
8. What did the 1977 Indian election show? *That voters could remove a government peacefully; Congress lost to the Janata Party.*
9. When were South Africa's first universal elections? *27 April 1994.*
10. What was the difference between the RDP and GEAR? *The RDP (1994) stressed redistribution and basic services; GEAR (1996) stressed growth, fiscal discipline and market reform.*
11. Who chaired the Truth and Reconciliation Commission? *Desmond Tutu.*
12. Why is proportional representation a debated factor in Weimar's collapse? *It allowed many small parties and unstable coalitions, but other democracies used it without collapsing, so its importance must be weighed against the Depression and elite choices.*
