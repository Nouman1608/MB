All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Crestmoor Cakes.

> **Case study: Crestmoor Cakes**
>
> Crestmoor Cakes makes celebration cakes in one bakery and sells them
> in two shops. Its costs at three levels of monthly output are shown
> below.
>
> | Cakes made a month | Total cost |
> |---|---|
> | 2,000 | $14,000 |
> | 5,000 | $30,000 |
> | 9,000 | $58,500 |
>
> The owner, Femi, wants to grow. She is considering three options:
> (1) opening more shops, (2) selling cakes through a website with
> delivery, or (3) taking over a smaller local bakery, Pearl's Pastries.
> Femi says she wants to keep control of quality. At 9,000 cakes a month
> the bakery is crowded, managers struggle to organise the staff, and
> some bakers say they feel like "just a number".

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of external growth? **[1]**
- A. Opening a new shop
- B. Selling products through a website
- C. A takeover of another business
- D. Increasing advertising

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a cause of diseconomies of scale? **[1]**
- A. Buying materials in bulk
- B. Poor communication
- C. Using larger machines
- D. A fall in rent

**3.** Define the term "economies of scale". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** types of organic growth. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** advantage of franchising for the franchisor. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the average unit cost of a cake at 2,000, 5,000 and 9,000 cakes a month. **[3]**

**7.** State the output at which average unit costs are lowest, and describe what happens between 5,000 and 9,000 cakes. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **one** purchasing economy of scale that Crestmoor Cakes might gain as it grows. **[2]**

**9.** Using the case study, explain **two** causes of diseconomies of scale at Crestmoor Cakes. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to Femi of taking over Pearl's Pastries. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse why selling cakes through a website might help Crestmoor Cakes to grow. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate which of the three options Femi should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The advantages of producing on a larger scale [1] that lower the average cost of each unit [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: opening new shops [1]; selling online (e-commerce) [1]; franchising [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The franchisee provides the money to open the outlet [1], so the franchisor can grow quickly without risking its own capital [1]. Accept: the franchisor receives fees and a share of sales [1] without paying for the shop [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 2,000 cakes: $14,000 / 2,000 = $7.00 [1]; 5,000 cakes: $30,000 / 5,000 = $6.00 [1]; 9,000 cakes: $58,500 / 9,000 = $6.50 [1]. **[3]**

**7.** The lowest average unit cost is **$6.00** at 5,000 cakes [1]. Between 5,000 and 9,000, average cost rises from $6.00 to $6.50, a diseconomy of scale [1]. **[2]**

**8.** As Crestmoor makes more cakes it buys more flour, sugar and eggs [1], so suppliers may give a discount for large orders, which lowers the cost of ingredients per cake [1]. **[2]**

**9.** **Poor coordination:** managers struggle to organise the staff in a crowded bakery [1], so work is delayed and costs per cake rise [1].
**Reduced motivation:** bakers say they feel like "just a number" [1], so they may work less well, take time off or leave, which increases the costs of waste and recruitment [1]. **[4]**

**10.** **Advantage:** Crestmoor would grow quickly and remove a local competitor [1], gaining Pearl's customers and its skills [1]. **Disadvantage:** the takeover costs money, and Femi would have to pay for it [1]; the two sets of staff may clash, and Pearl's bakers may fear losing their jobs [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Larger market:** a website can reach customers in other towns or countries, not just those who pass the two shops [1], which could raise sales a great deal [1].
**Lower costs:** Crestmoor does not need to rent more premises [1], so fixed costs rise less than sales [1].
**Spreading costs:** more cakes made means more output, so the average unit cost may fall up to 5,000 cakes (from $7.00 to $6.00) [1]. But delivery and website costs must be paid, so the growth only helps if sales are high enough [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **Opening more shops:** Femi keeps control of quality [1], and the growth is steady [1]; but each shop needs money for rent and equipment, and the bakery is already crowded [1].
**Selling online:** it reaches many more customers without new shops [1]; delivery may harm quality if cakes arrive damaged, and the bakery could not cope with a lot more orders at 9,000 cakes [1].
**Taking over Pearl's:** it brings a second bakery, which could ease the crowding by sharing production between two sites [1]; but it costs money, risks staff conflict and gives Femi less control over quality [1].
**Judgement:** the best option is probably to take over Pearl's or open a new site, to avoid diseconomies at 9,000 cakes where the cost per cake rises to $6.50 [1]. Selling online should be added only when the bakery has room to produce more [1]. Accept a well-supported alternative, such as opening new shops while keeping production at about 5,000 cakes per bakery. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading the total cost as the cost of one cake. Divide total cost by output.
- Saying that average costs always fall as a business grows.
- Describing diseconomies in general terms without using the details of the bakery.
- Writing only advantages of a growth method without any drawbacks.
- Failing to make a recommendation or to say which option is least suitable.
