This study guide teaches subtopic **5.3 Circles, arcs and sectors** from Topic 5 Mensuration of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Both tiers study it, with one difference: in Core (C5.3) the sector angle is always **a factor of 360°**, while in Extended (E5.3) it can be any angle and **major and minor sectors** are both included.

Consolidate with the [circles, arcs and sectors revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [circles, arcs and sectors practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circles-arcs-and-sectors-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## What this subtopic covers

| Statement | What you must do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 5.3.1 | Carry out calculations involving the circumference and area of a circle | Core and Extended |
| 5.3.2 | Carry out calculations involving arc length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area of a circle, where the sector angle is a factor of 360° | Core |
| 5.3.2 | The same for any sector angle, including minor and major sectors | Extended |

The notes say answers **may be asked for in terms of π**, and that the formulas for the circumference and area of a circle are given in the List of formulas: C = 2πr and A = πr². The arc and sector formulas are **not** listed, but the syllabus wording tells you how to build them: as fractions of the circumference and the area.

## The circle

```
circumference C = 2πr = πd
area          A = πr²
```

The most common error is using the diameter in A = πr². Always write down r before you substitute.

### Worked example 1 (non-calculator, answers in terms of π)

A circle has diameter 14 cm. Find (a) its circumference, (b) its area, (c) the perimeter of a semicircle cut from it.

```
r = 7 cm
(a) C = 2 × π × 7 = 14π cm
(b) A = π × 7² = 49π cm²
(c) curved edge = half of 14π = 7π cm
    straight edge = the diameter = 14 cm
    perimeter = (7π + 14) cm
```

"In terms of π" means leave π as a symbol and simplify the number in front of it. Writing 43.98 instead of 14π does not answer the question asked.

### Worked example 2 (calculator): working backwards

A circle has circumference 50 cm. Find its area.

```
2πr = 50
r = 50 ÷ (2π) = 7.957 7...   (keep this in the calculator)
A = π × 7.957 7...² = 198.94... = 199 cm²  (3 s.f.)
```

Rounding r to 7.96 first gives 199.05…, which still rounds to 199 here, but early rounding can change the third significant figure in other questions. Use the π key or 3.142, as the syllabus requires.

## Arcs and sectors

A **sector** is the region between two radii and an arc, like a slice of pizza. Its angle at the centre, θ, tells you what fraction of the circle it is: θ/360.

```
arc length   = (θ/360) × 2πr
sector area  = (θ/360) × πr²
perimeter of a sector = arc length + 2r
```

The perimeter needs **both radii**. This is the mark most often lost in sector questions.

### Worked example 3 (Core, non-calculator)

A sector has radius 9 cm and angle 40°. Find its arc length, area and perimeter in terms of π.

```
fraction of the circle = 40/360 = 1/9
arc length  = 1/9 × 2π × 9 = 2π cm
area        = 1/9 × π × 81 = 9π cm²
perimeter   = (2π + 18) cm
```

When θ is a factor of 360°, the fraction simplifies to 1/n, which keeps non-calculator work short.

### Major and minor sectors (Extended)

Two radii split a circle into a **minor** sector (angle less than 180°) and a **major** sector (angle more than 180°). If you are given the minor angle and asked about the major sector, use 360° − θ.

### Worked example 4 (Extended, calculator)

A circle has radius 12 cm. A minor sector has angle 75°. Find the arc length and the area of the **major** sector.

```
major angle = 360° − 75° = 285°
arc length  = 285/360 × 2π × 12 = 19π = 59.7 cm  (3 s.f.)
area        = 285/360 × π × 144 = 114π = 358 cm²  (3 s.f.)
```

Check: the whole area is 144π and the minor sector is 75/360 × 144π = 30π; 144π − 30π = 114π.

### Worked example 5 (Extended, calculator): finding the angle

A sector of a circle of radius 8 cm has an arc length of 15 cm. Find the sector angle and the sector area.

```
θ/360 × 2π × 8 = 15
θ = 15 × 360 ÷ (16π) = 107.429...° = 107.4°  (1 d.p.)
area = 107.429.../360 × π × 64 = 60.0 cm²
```

Use the unrounded angle in the area. The exact area is 60 cm², which you can confirm because the sector is the same fraction of the area as the arc is of the circumference: (15 ÷ 16π) × 64π = 60.

### Worked example 6 (Extended, non-calculator): finding the radius

A sector has angle 135° and area 54π cm². Find its radius.

```
135/360 × πr² = 54π
3/8 × r² = 54
r² = 144
r = 12 cm
```

## Circles in context

### Worked example 7 (calculator): a turning wheel

A bicycle wheel has diameter 65 cm. How many complete turns does it make when the bicycle travels 1 km?

```
one turn moves the bicycle one circumference: 65π = 204.20... cm
1 km = 100 000 cm
100 000 ÷ 204.20... = 489.7...
489 complete turns
```

The units must match before dividing, and the question asks for **complete** turns, so round down.

## Exam technique

- **"Give your answer in terms of π"**: do not use a calculator value of π; simplify the coefficient.
- **"Show that"** with a decimal given: write at least one more significant figure than the value shown, then round to it.
- **"Calculate"**: π key or 3.142; final answers to 3 significant figures, angles to 1 decimal place.
- Write the fraction θ/360 explicitly. It earns method credit even if the arithmetic slips.

## Common mistakes

- Using the diameter as the radius in πr², or the radius in πd.
- Forgetting the two radii in the perimeter of a sector, or the diameter in the perimeter of a semicircle.
- Using the minor angle when the question asks about the major sector.
- Rounding the radius or angle before the final step.
- Giving a decimal when the question asks for an answer in terms of π.
- Mixing units, such as a diameter in cm and a distance in km.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the arc length and sector area formulas given in the 0580 exam?**
No. The List of formulas gives C = 2πr and A = πr². Arc length and sector area are found as fractions, θ/360, of those.

**Do Core candidates get major sectors?**
The Core statement limits sector angles to factors of 360°, such as 30°, 45°, 60°, 90° or 120°. Major and minor sectors, and any angle, are named in the Extended notes.

**What is the perimeter of a sector?**
The arc length plus two radii.
