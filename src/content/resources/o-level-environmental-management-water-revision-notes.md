> **Syllabus edition note.** These notes follow the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, in which Topic 3 Water has six sub-topics, including oil
> pollution and plastic pollution.

Topic 3 Water of Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) covers where fresh water comes from,
how it is made safe, what pollutes it, the diseases linked to it, how the sea is fished and farmed, and
the two marine pollution problems of oil and plastic. It is the largest topic in the syllabus, so learn
it as six short checklists and practise linking causes to impacts to management.

Full explanations: [Water study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-water-study-guide/).
Test yourself: [Water practice questions](/resources/o-level-environmental-management-water-practice/).

## 3.1 Checklist: water sources and supply

- **Stores**: oceans (salt water); ice sheets and glaciers, ground water, atmosphere, lakes and rivers
  (fresh water).
- **Transfers**: precipitation, interception, surface run-off, infiltration, through-flow, ground water
  flow, transpiration, evaporation, condensation.
- **Fresh water sources**: rain and snow; rivers, lakes and reservoirs; aquifers and wells; desalinated sea
  water.
- **Five oceans**: Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic.
- **Potable water** = water that is safe to drink. Treatment: **screening → sedimentation → filtration →
  chlorination**.
- **Availability** differs between water-rich and water-poor regions and between urban and rural areas;
  there are global inequalities in both water and sewage treatment.

**Desalination**
- Distillation: heat water until it boils; salt stays in the liquid; steam is cooled and condensed.
- Reverse osmosis: high pressure pushes water through a membrane that stops most ions.
- Benefit: a reliable supply where rivers and aquifers are scarce. Limitation: high energy use and cost,
  and salty waste (brine).

**Multipurpose dam uses**: flood control, hydro-electric power, irrigation, water storage, transport,
recreation, tourism, fish farming. Limitations include flooding of land and homes, loss of habitats,
sediment trapped behind the dam and high construction cost.

## 3.2 Checklist: water pollution

| Source | Impact |
|---|---|
| Sewage, domestic waste | Cholera bacteria; nutrient enrichment |
| Plastic waste | Entanglement; ingestion |
| Industrial processes | Toxic substances accumulate; bioaccumulation and biomagnification |
| Agricultural practices | Leaching of fertiliser → nutrient enrichment → eutrophication |
| Acid rain | Acidifies rivers and lakes, harming organisms |

- **Bioaccumulation** = build-up of a toxin in one organism over time.
- **Biomagnification** = increase in concentration of a toxin up a food chain.
- **Eutrophication chain**: nutrients → algal bloom → light blocked, plants die → bacteria decompose them,
  using oxygen → fish die.
- Improving quality: improved sanitation; sewage treatment; pollution control and legislation.

## 3.3 Checklist: malaria and cholera

**Malaria**: vector = **female Anopheles mosquito**; parasite = **Plasmodium**, passed to humans in a bite and
back to uninfected mosquitoes when they feed on infected blood.

| Malaria control | Cholera control |
|---|---|
| Personal: nets, repellent, vaccination, antimalarial drugs | Handwashing |
| Vector: cover or drain breeding sites, spray insecticide, sterilise male mosquitoes, biological control | Sanitation and sewage treatment; potable water by boiling and chlorination; vaccination |

Malaria is spread by a vector; cholera is spread by contaminated water - so their controls differ.

## 3.4 Checklist: exploitation and marine aquaculture

- **Exploitation impacts**: overfishing and overharvesting; effects on target and **bycatch** species;
  disrupted food chains.
- **Marine aquaculture** = farming of marine species in captivity: fish, crustaceans, seaweeds.
- **Impacts**: reduces pressure on wild fisheries; more food for people; but risk of escape, disease, effects
  on local food webs, nutrient enrichment from waste, energy use, and the need to catch wild fish as feed.
- **Managing harvesting**: limits on boat and net size; larger mesh so young fish escape; pole and line;
  quotas; closed seasons; limited fishing days; protected areas; conservation laws; international
  agreements with implementation and monitoring.

## 3.5 Checklist: oil pollution

- **Causes**: offshore and onshore extraction; pipelines; shipping; cleaning tanks at sea; refineries.
- **Impacts on**: birds (oiled feathers lose insulation and buoyancy); marine mammals; fish; crustaceans;
  seaweeds; coral reefs; beaches.
- **Prevention**: MARPOL (International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships);
  double-hulled tankers; risk assessments; regular maintenance.
- **Minimising impacts**: improved navigation systems; booms and sorbents; detergent sprays; skimmers;
  controlled burning.

## 3.6 Checklist: plastic pollution

| Type | Key point |
|---|---|
| Conventional plastics | Made from fossil fuels; generally non-biodegradable |
| Bioplastics | Fully or partly made from biological raw materials; can be biodegradable or not |
| Biodegradable plastics | Designed to decompose in water or soil by bacteria and fungi into water, biomass and gases (CO2, methane); rate depends on biotic and abiotic factors |
| Non-biodegradable plastics | Break down over a long period |
| Microplastics | Less than 5 mm; from larger plastics breaking down or used in products |

**Impacts on marine ecosystems**: visual pollution; entanglement; mistaken for food; bioaccumulation and
biomagnification. **Strategies**: alternative packaging; avoid single-use plastics; safe disposal;
recycling; legislation and enforcement.

## Worked example: biomagnification

A toxin in sea water is at 0.001 units. Concentration multiplies by 10 at each feeding level:
plankton 0.01 → small fish 0.1 → large fish 1 → seabird **10 units**. The seabird has 10 000 times the
concentration in the water, which is why top predators are worst affected.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Swapping bioaccumulation (one organism) and biomagnification (along a food chain).
- Saying malaria is spread by dirty water - it is spread by mosquito bites.
- Calling all bioplastics biodegradable.
- Forgetting that aquaculture needs feed, often from wild-caught fish.
- Listing oil-spill clean-up methods when the question asks about prevention.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four stages of water treatment in 5014?** Screening, sedimentation, filtration and
chlorination, in that order.

**What size is a microplastic?** Less than 5 mm in length. Microplastics form when larger plastics break
down, or are added to commercial products.

**What is MARPOL?** The International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships. The 5014
syllabus names it as a strategy for preventing oil spills.
