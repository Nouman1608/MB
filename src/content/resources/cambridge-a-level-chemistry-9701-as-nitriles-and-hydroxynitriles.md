This guide covers subtopic **19.2, Nitriles and hydroxynitriles**, from Topic 19, Nitrogen compounds, of
Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content. The
[Nitrogen Compounds: Amines and Nitriles study guide](/resources/as-nitrogen-compounds-amines-and-nitriles/)
contrasts the substitution and addition mechanisms by which cyanide reacts; this page concentrates on
naming, writing equations for unfamiliar molecules, the role of KCN as a catalyst, hydrolysis of
hydroxynitriles, and multi-step routes that lengthen a carbon chain.

## What the syllabus asks for

1. recall how **nitriles** are produced: (a) reaction of a halogenoalkane with **KCN in ethanol and heat**
2. recall how **hydroxynitriles** are produced: (a) reaction of aldehydes and ketones with **HCN, KCN as
   catalyst, and heat**
3. describe the **hydrolysis of nitriles** with dilute acid or dilute alkali followed by acidification to
   produce a carboxylic acid

The nucleophilic addition mechanism of HCN to carbonyl compounds is a learning outcome of 17.1, with ethanal
and propanone as the named examples, so it is regularly tested with this subtopic.

## Naming nitriles and hydroxynitriles

The carbon of the –C≡N group is counted in the stem and is C1. Nitrile names in 9701 are for **straight
chains only**.

| Formula | Name |
|---|---|
| CH₃CN | ethanenitrile |
| CH₃CH₂CN | propanenitrile |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CN | butanenitrile |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN | pentanenitrile |

A hydroxynitrile from a carbonyl compound has the OH on C2 (the carbon next to C≡N):

| Carbonyl compound | Hydroxynitrile | Name |
|---|---|---|
| ethanal, CH₃CHO | CH₃CH(OH)CN | 2-hydroxypropanenitrile |
| propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO | CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN | 2-hydroxybutanenitrile |
| propanone, CH₃COCH₃ | (CH₃)₂C(OH)CN | 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile |
| butanone, CH₃COCH₂CH₃ | CH₃CH₂C(CH₃)(OH)CN | 2-hydroxy-2-methylbutanenitrile |

In every case the product has **one more carbon** than the carbonyl compound.

## Making nitriles from halogenoalkanes

Heat the halogenoalkane with potassium cyanide dissolved in ethanol (under reflux):

    CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + KCN → CH₃CH₂CH₂CN + KBr      (1-bromopropane to butanenitrile)

The cyanide ion is the nucleophile: its carbon carries a lone pair and a negative charge. It attacks the δ+
carbon bonded to the halogen, and the halide ion leaves. Ethanol is used as the solvent so that water is
absent; with water present, hydroxide substitution would give an alcohol as a by-product.

**Why chemists use this reaction:** it is the only AS reaction that makes a new C–C bond and lengthens the
carbon chain by one carbon. A question that asks you to go from a compound with *n* carbons to one with
*n* + 1 carbons almost always needs a cyanide step.

## Making hydroxynitriles from aldehydes and ketones

Heat the aldehyde or ketone with HCN, with KCN as catalyst:

    CH₃CH₂CHO + HCN → CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CN      (propanal to 2-hydroxybutanenitrile)

**The mechanism (nucleophilic addition):**

1. The C=O bond is polar: Cᵟ⁺=Oᵟ⁻. A curly arrow goes from the **lone pair on the carbon of CN⁻** to the
   **δ+ carbonyl carbon**. A second curly arrow goes from the **C=O π bond to the oxygen**.
2. This gives an intermediate **alkoxide ion** with a negative charge on oxygen, CH₃CH₂CH(O⁻)CN.
3. The O⁻ removes H⁺ from a molecule of HCN, giving the hydroxynitrile and **regenerating CN⁻**.

**Why KCN is a catalyst:** HCN is a weak acid and provides very few CN⁻ ions on its own. KCN supplies CN⁻
to start the first step, and step 3 makes a new CN⁻ for each one used, so the KCN is not used up.

HCN is a highly toxic gas, which is why the reaction is carried out with great care.

**Chirality.** The hydroxynitrile from ethanal, CH₃CH(OH)CN, has a carbon bonded to H, OH, CH₃ and CN: a
chiral centre. The one from propanone, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CN, has two CH₃ groups on that carbon and is not chiral.
See the [Isomerism study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-isomerism/) for how to
check.

## Hydrolysis of nitriles

Heat under reflux with dilute acid, or with dilute alkali followed by acidification. The –C≡N becomes
–COOH, and the nitrogen ends up as NH₄⁺ (acid) or NH₃ (alkali):

    CH₃CH₂CH₂CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NH₄Cl

Hydroxynitriles hydrolyse in the same way, keeping their OH group:

    CH₃CH(OH)CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH(OH)COOH + NH₄Cl     (2-hydroxypropanoic acid)

More on the acid products is in the [Carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-carboxylic-acids/).

## Predicting products for unfamiliar molecules

Examiners often use a starting material you have not met. Apply the same rule each time: CN⁻ replaces a
halogen, or HCN adds across a C=O, and the C≡N carbon is new.

| Starting material | Reagent | Product | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1-chloropentane | KCN in ethanol, heat | CH₃(CH₂)₄CN | hexanenitrile |
| butanal, CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO | HCN, KCN catalyst, heat | CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(OH)CN | 2-hydroxypentanenitrile |
| pentan-3-one, CH₃CH₂COCH₂CH₃ | HCN, KCN catalyst, heat | (CH₃CH₂)₂C(OH)CN | 2-ethyl-2-hydroxybutanenitrile |
| hexanenitrile | dilute acid, heat | CH₃(CH₂)₄COOH | hexanoic acid |

The rule works for methanal too: HCHO + HCN → HOCH₂CN, 2-hydroxyethanenitrile. Its C2 carbon carries two
hydrogen atoms, so unlike the products from other aldehydes it has no chiral centre. Always check the
product's molecular formula: it should be the carbonyl compound's formula plus HCN, or the halogenoalkane's
formula with the halogen replaced by CN.

## Worked example 1: lengthening a chain

**Question.** Suggest a two-step synthesis of pentanoic acid from 1-bromobutane.

**Answer.** 1-bromobutane has four carbons; pentanoic acid has five, so a cyanide step is needed.

- **Step 1:** heat 1-bromobutane with **KCN in ethanol** to give **pentanenitrile**, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN.
- **Step 2:** heat pentanenitrile under reflux with **dilute HCl** to give **pentanoic acid**,
  CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂COOH.

## Worked example 2: a three-step route through a hydroxynitrile

**Question.** Suggest how 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanoic acid, (CH₃)₂C(OH)COOH, could be made from
propan-2-ol.

**Answer.** The target has four carbons and a C(OH) next to the COOH: that pattern comes from a
hydroxynitrile made from a ketone.

- **Step 1:** oxidise propan-2-ol with **acidified K₂Cr₂O₇** and distil, giving **propanone**.
- **Step 2:** heat propanone with **HCN and a KCN catalyst**, giving **2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile**.
- **Step 3:** heat with **dilute acid** to hydrolyse the nitrile group, giving the target acid.

## Common mistakes

- **Using aqueous KCN** for the halogenoalkane reaction. It must be **in ethanol**.
- **Calling KCN a reactant** in hydroxynitrile formation; it is a **catalyst**, and HCN is the reagent.
- **Forgetting to count the CN carbon** when naming: CH₃CH₂CN is propanenitrile, not ethanenitrile.
- **Describing the halogenoalkane reaction as addition** or the carbonyl reaction as substitution.
- **Starting the curly arrow from the N of CN⁻.** The lone pair used is on the carbon.
- **Leaving out acidification** after alkaline hydrolysis.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State** reagents and conditions precisely: "KCN in ethanol, heat"; "HCN with KCN catalyst, heat";
  "dilute acid, heat" or "dilute alkali, heat, then acidify".
- **Describe** the mechanism of nucleophilic addition: dipole on C=O, two curly arrows, alkoxide
  intermediate, protonation by HCN.
- **Suggest** a synthesis: compare carbon counts first, then decide whether a nitrile or hydroxynitrile
  step is needed.
- **Identify** a chiral centre in the product: list the four groups.
