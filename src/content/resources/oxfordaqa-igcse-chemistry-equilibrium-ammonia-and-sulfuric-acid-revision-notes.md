## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reversible reaction | Goes both ways: the products can turn back into the reactants (symbol ⇌) |
| Closed system | Nothing can enter or leave, so no substance escapes |
| Equilibrium (3.8.2 a) | In a closed system, the forward and backward reactions occur at exactly the same rate |
| Yield | How much of a product is present in the equilibrium mixture |
| Exothermic | Transfers energy to the surroundings |
| Endothermic | Takes in energy from the surroundings |
| Catalyst | Speeds up a reaction without being used up; at equilibrium it speeds up both directions |

**At equilibrium:** both reactions continue; amounts stay **constant**; amounts are usually **not equal**.

**3.8.2 b:** the relative amounts of all the reacting substances at equilibrium **depend on the conditions**.

## 3.8.2 c: temperature rules

| You change | Endothermic direction | Exothermic direction |
|---|---|---|
| Raise temperature | yield **increases** | yield **decreases** |
| Lower temperature | yield **decreases** | yield **increases** |

**Method in steps**

1. Read which direction is exothermic. The other direction is endothermic.
2. Raising temperature → the endothermic direction gains.
3. Lowering temperature → the exothermic direction gains.
4. Name the substance whose amount goes up or down.

**Worked reminder.** 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), forward exothermic. Cooling increases the exothermic (forward) yield, so **more NO₂**. Heating gives **less NO₂**.

## 3.8.2 d: pressure rules

Gases only. Count **molecules**, not atoms, from the balanced equation.

| You change | Favoured reaction |
|---|---|
| Increase pressure | The one producing the **fewest** molecules |
| Decrease pressure | The one producing the **most** molecules |
| Same number of molecules on both sides | No change in yield |

**Worked reminder.** PCl₅(g) ⇌ PCl₃(g) + Cl₂(g): 1 molecule left, 2 right. Increasing pressure favours the backward reaction, so **more PCl₅** remains.

**Worked reminder.** N₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO(g): 2 molecules each side, so pressure does **not** change the yield.

## 3.8.2 e: optimum industrial conditions

Industry balances four things:

- **Yield:** what the equilibrium rules favour.
- **Rate:** higher temperature, higher gas pressure and a catalyst all make the reaction faster.
- **Cost:** high pressure needs strong equipment and energy for compressing gases.
- **Safety:** very high pressures are more dangerous.

When the forward reaction is exothermic, a low temperature gives the best yield but is too slow. A **compromise temperature** gives a reasonable yield quickly. A **catalyst** lets equilibrium be reached faster; it never changes the yield.

## 3.8.3 a to c: the Haber process

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Product | Ammonia |
| Word equation | nitrogen + hydrogen ⇌ ammonia |
| Symbol equation | N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g) |
| Nitrogen from | The air |
| Hydrogen from | Natural gas or other sources |
| Pressure | About 200 atmospheres |
| Catalyst | Iron |
| Temperature | About 450 °C |
| Forward reaction | Exothermic; 4 molecules → 2 |
| Separation | Cooling: ammonia liquefies and is removed |
| Unreacted gases | Nitrogen and hydrogen recycled |

**Method in steps: explaining the Haber conditions**

1. Temperature: forward exothermic → low temperature, higher yield → but too slow → 450 °C is a compromise.
2. Pressure: 4 → 2 molecules → high pressure, higher yield and faster rate → limited by cost and safety → about 200 atmospheres.
3. Catalyst: iron speeds up reaching equilibrium; yield unchanged.
4. Recycling: unreacted gases return to the reactor, so raw materials are not wasted.

**Why cooling separates ammonia:** ammonia condenses at a much higher temperature than nitrogen or hydrogen, so it becomes liquid while they stay as gases. It can then be run off and stored, while the gases go back to the reactor.

## 3.8.3 b: fertilizers and food

- Ammonia is a **raw material for fertilizers**; it is used to make ammonium salts.
- Crops need **nitrogen** to make proteins and grow; most cannot take nitrogen from the air.
- Harvesting removes nutrients, so soil loses fertility.
- The **global population is rising** and farmland is limited.
- So fertilizers are needed to **maximise food yields** from each area of land.

## 3.8.3 d: the contact process

| Stage | What happens | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sulfur burned in air | S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g) (the specification prints this with ⇌) |
| 2 | Sulfur dioxide + more oxygen; exothermic, reversible | 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g) |
| 3 | Sulfur trioxide + water | H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq) |

**Stage 2 conditions:** vanadium(V) oxide (V₂O₅) catalyst, around 450 °C, atmospheric pressure.

**Method in steps: explaining Stage 2**

1. Catalyst: faster rate in both directions; no change to yield.
2. Temperature: forward exothermic, so lower temperature means higher yield but too slow; 450 °C is a compromise.
3. Pressure: 3 → 2 molecules, so high pressure would raise yield slightly. The yield is already high at atmospheric pressure, so the extra cost of high pressure is not justified.

## Haber and contact at a glance

| | Haber process | Contact process (Stage 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Reversible step | N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ |
| Molecules of gas | 4 → 2 | 3 → 2 |
| Forward reaction | Exothermic | Exothermic |
| Catalyst | Iron | Vanadium(V) oxide |
| Temperature | About 450 °C | Around 450 °C |
| Pressure | About 200 atmospheres | Atmospheric |
| Why that pressure | High pressure needed for a worthwhile yield | Yield already high, so high pressure not worth the cost |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Rate vs yield:** speed of reaction versus the share of product once equilibrium is reached.
- **Constant vs equal:** at equilibrium amounts are constant, not necessarily equal.
- **Atoms vs molecules:** pressure rules count molecules of gas.
- **Haber vs contact:** iron vs V₂O₅; 200 atmospheres vs atmospheric pressure; both around 450 °C.
- **Catalyst vs condition change:** temperature and pressure can change the yield; a catalyst cannot.

## Quick self-test

1. What two things must be true for a reversible reaction to be at equilibrium?
2. A forward reaction is endothermic. State the effect on its yield of lowering the temperature.
3. 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). How many gas molecules are on each side?
4. CH₃OH(g) ⇌ CO(g) + 2H₂(g). Which direction is favoured by a decrease in pressure?
5. Name the catalyst in the Haber process and the catalyst in Stage 2 of the contact process.
6. Give the source of the nitrogen and of the hydrogen for the Haber process.
7. What happens to the unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen?
8. Explain why a catalyst does not change the yield of ammonia.
9. Give two reasons why the Haber process does not use a pressure far higher than 200 atmospheres.
10. Write the equation for Stage 3 of the contact process.
11. Calculate the masses of nitrogen and hydrogen needed to make 17 kg of ammonia. (Aᵣ: N = 14, H = 1)
12. Calculate the maximum mass of sulfuric acid made from 16 tonnes of sulfur. (Aᵣ: S = 32, O = 16, H = 1)

### Answers

1. A closed system; forward and backward reactions at exactly the same rate.
2. The yield of the endothermic forward reaction **decreases**.
3. 2 + 1 = **3** on the left; **2** on the right.
4. The forward direction (1 molecule → 3 molecules), so **more CO and H₂**.
5. Haber: **iron**. Contact Stage 2: **vanadium(V) oxide, V₂O₅**.
6. Nitrogen from **the air**; hydrogen from **natural gas** (or other sources).
7. They are **recycled** back into the reactor.
8. It speeds up the forward and backward reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached sooner but the amounts at equilibrium are the same.
9. Higher pressure needs stronger, more expensive equipment and more energy to compress gases; it is also more dangerous.
10. H₂O(l) + SO₃(g) → H₂SO₄(aq).
11. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ gives a mass ratio 28 : 6 : 34. Halve it: **14 kg nitrogen** and **3 kg hydrogen** make 17 kg ammonia.
12. 1 S gives 1 H₂SO₄, so 32 t gives 98 t; 16 t gives **49 tonnes**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that equilibrium means the reaction has stopped, or that the amounts are equal.
- Leaving out "closed system" when defining equilibrium.
- Stating "the equilibrium shifts" without naming what happens to the yield of the product asked about.
- Counting atoms, or ignoring the numbers in front of formulae, in pressure questions.
- Saying a catalyst increases yield, or that it is used up.
- Explaining 450 °C as "to increase the yield"; for an exothermic forward reaction, a higher temperature lowers the yield, so the reason is rate.
- Giving only one side of a compromise (for example "high pressure is expensive") without the yield or rate benefit.
- Mixing up the two processes' catalysts and pressures.
- Answering the fertilizer question with "plants grow better" without nitrogen, food yields or population.
