**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 2 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): pre-colonial sub-Saharan African states, circa 800 to 1945. Every question on this study will require reference to **one sub-Saharan state**, so choose your state or states early and learn them against all six inquiry topics. The full account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-precolonial-african-states/).

## The six inquiry topics

| Inquiry topic | Ask yourself |
|---|---|
| Emergence and consolidation of the state | What combination of politics, war, society and economy created the state? |
| Growth of production and trade | Which goods, routes, lands and labour made it rich? |
| Maintenance of authority | How did leaders, institutions, succession and diplomacy keep control? |
| Impact of the state | What did it change in religion, culture, society and the lives of women? |
| Reasons for decline and collapse | Which internal and external pressures broke it? |
| Impact of decline and collapse | What replaced it, and who gained or lost? |

## Three state profiles

### Mali Empire (1235 to 1400)

- **Origin:** Sundiata Keita defeats Sumanguru of Sosso at Kirina, about 1235.
- **Economy:** gold (Bambuk, Bure), salt, copper, kola; trans-Saharan caravans; Niger farming; towns of Timbuktu, Gao, Djenné.
- **Authority:** mansa, provincial governors, tribute; Mansa Musa (c.1312 to 1337), pilgrimage 1324 to 1325.
- **Impact:** Islamic learning and building in Timbuktu; wide fame in North Africa and Europe.
- **Decline:** succession disputes, revolts, Songhai under Sunni Ali takes Timbuktu in 1468.

### Ashanti (1701 to 1902)

- **Origin:** Osei Tutu and Okomfo Anokye unite Akan clans around Kumasi, about 1701; Golden Stool; defeat of Denkyira.
- **Economy:** gold, kola, enslaved people; imports of cloth, guns and metal; control of roads to the coast.
- **Authority:** Asantehene, council of chiefs, queen mothers, matrilineal clans; Osei Bonsu (1804 to 1824).
- **Impact:** goldwork, kente, court ceremony; war and slavery shaped society.
- **Decline:** wars with Britain (1824, 1874, 1896, 1900); annexation in 1902.

### Zulu Kingdom (1816 to 1887)

- **Origin:** Shaka builds on Mthethwa power; age regiments, short stabbing spear, "buffalo horns" formation.
- **Economy:** cattle, land and royal control of labour.
- **Authority:** personal rule of the king; Shaka assassinated 1828; Dingane, Mpande, Cetshwayo.
- **Impact:** reshaped southern Africa, sending Ndebele and other groups north; debate about the *mfecane*.
- **Decline:** war with Britain in 1879 (Isandlwana, Ulundi); division, then annexation in 1887.

### Quick notes on other suggested states

- **Ghana Empire (830 to 1240):** Soninke state; gold-salt trade; capital Koumbi Saleh; fell to Sosso and Mali.
- **Swahili city-states (1000 to 1500):** Indian Ocean trade; Kilwa, Mombasa; Portuguese attacks from 1505.
- **Kongo (1400 to 1914):** Afonso I and Christianity; slave trade pressure; defeat at Mbwila, 1665.
- **Ethiopian Empire (1850 to 1945):** Menelik II beats Italy at Adwa, 1896; Haile Selassie; Italian occupation 1936 to 1941.

## Arguments to practise

| Argument | Support | Challenge |
|---|---|---|
| Trade made the African state | Mali's gold and salt; Ashanti's gold and guns | Zulu rose through cattle and warfare |
| Leaders were decisive | Sundiata, Osei Tutu, Shaka | Institutions and resources lasted beyond them |
| Islam transformed West African states | Mansa Musa, Timbuktu | Mostly an elite and urban change |
| Europeans caused the decline | Britain against Ashanti and Zulu; Italy against Ethiopia | Succession crises, rebellions, economic change |

## Perspectives to know

- **Written sources:** Arab travellers and scholars (for example Ibn Battuta, who visited Mali in 1352 to 1353) give valuable detail but wrote as outsiders.
- **Oral tradition:** Mande griots preserve the Sundiata story; useful on how the state saw itself, but shaped by later needs.
- **Colonial records:** valuable on wars and taxation but often hostile to the state they describe.
- **Modern African historians:** emphasise African agency and internal development, challenging older views that states were created or ended only by outsiders.

## Worked example: how to use "one state"

*Question:* "Evaluate the view that trade was the main reason for the decline of one pre-colonial state."

- Choose Ashanti. State the claim: changes in trade weakened the state.
- Evidence for: the end of the slave trade reduced revenue; British control of the coast cut access to firearms and markets.
- Evidence against: military defeat in 1874 and 1896, quarrels over succession, rebellion by tributary peoples, and British political aims.
- Perspective: one historian view stresses economic change; another stresses colonial aggression.
- Judgement: trade change was a background cause that made the state vulnerable, but conquest finished it.

## Command-term drill

- **Evaluate:** weigh strengths and limits, then judge.
- **To what extent:** place the claim on a scale and justify the position.
- **Discuss:** review several factors in a balanced way.
- **Examine:** look closely at assumptions and links between factors.

## Common mistakes

- Treating "Africa" as one place.
- Using colonial writers as if they were neutral.
- Writing a list of states instead of depth on one.
- Forgetting women's experiences, which are a specified content point in the fourth inquiry topic.

## Self-test

1. Which battle established Sundiata's power, and about when?
2. Name two commodities of the trans-Saharan trade.
3. What was the Golden Stool?
4. Who led the Zulu Kingdom at the time of the 1879 war with Britain?
5. Which battle in 1896 preserved Ethiopian independence?
6. Why is oral tradition valuable but difficult for historians?

**Answers:** 1. Kirina, about 1235. 2. Gold and salt (also copper, kola, slaves). 3. The symbol of Ashanti unity, linked to Osei Tutu and Okomfo Anokye. 4. Cetshwayo. 5. Adwa. 6. It preserves the society's own account, but it was repeated and shaped over generations, so details and purposes may change.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I use a state from the suggested list?** No, but your chosen state must be sub-Saharan and give enough material for all six topics.

**How many states should I know?** Two in depth is safer than four in outline.
