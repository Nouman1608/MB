## Questions

**1.** Methionine is an α-amino acid in which the side chain R is –CH₂CH₂SCH₃.

**(a)** State the general formula of an α-amino acid. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the structural formula and the molecular formula of methionine. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each compound as a primary amide, a secondary amide or neither.

**A** CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CONH₂ **B** C₆H₅CONHCH₃ **C** CH₃CH₂CH₂CONHCH(CH₃)₂ **D** CH₃COCH₂NH₂ **[4]**

**3.** 2-Amino-4-phenylbutanoic acid has the structure C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH.

**(a)** Write an equation for its reaction with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for its reaction with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the organic product of (b). **[1]**

**4.** Methionine, CH₃SCH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH, is converted into its propyl ester.

**(a)** State the other reagent, the catalyst and the condition needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the equation for this esterification. **[1]**
**(c)** 4.473 g of methionine gives 3.44 g of the propyl ester, C₈H₁₇NO₂S. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**5.** 1-Phenylethanamine has the structure C₆H₅CH(NH₂)CH₃.

**(a)** Explain why 1-phenylethanamine shows optical isomerism. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw 3-D diagrams of the two optical isomers. **[2]**

**6.** State the number of chiral centres in each molecule.

**(a)** 2,3-dimethylpentane, CH₃CH(CH₃)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃
**(b)** 2-hydroxy-3-methylbutanoic acid, (CH₃)₂CHCH(OH)COOH
**(c)** pentane-2,4-diol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH(OH)CH₃
**(d)** 3-bromopentane, CH₃CH₂CHBrCH₂CH₃
**(e)** 2-methylcyclohexan-1-one **[5]**

**7.** 2-Methylpentanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)COCl, is used to make two amides.

**(a)** Write an equation for its reaction with excess ammonia, and state the type of amide formed. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for its reaction with propylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂, using two moles of the amine, and state the type of amide formed. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify, by its position in the chain, the chiral centre in the amide from (b). **[1]**

**8.** Three 3-D drawings of heptan-2-amine are described. Each has the chiral carbon central, two neighbouring in-plane bonds, and one wedge and one hashed bond on the opposite side.

- Drawing 1: H up and CH₃ left (in plane), NH₂ on a wedge to the right, C₅H₁₁ on a hashed bond pointing down.
- Drawing 2: H up and CH₃ left (in plane), C₅H₁₁ on a wedge to the right, NH₂ on a hashed bond pointing down.
- Drawing 3: H down and CH₃ right (in plane), NH₂ on a wedge to the left, C₅H₁₁ on a hashed bond pointing up.

**(a)** State the relationship between drawings 1 and 2, and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** State the relationship between drawings 1 and 3. **[1]**

**9.** 2.90 g of an α-amino acid, **X**, in which R is a saturated alkyl group, reacts completely with aqueous sodium hydroxide. The water is evaporated, leaving 3.34 g of the dry sodium salt.

**(a)** Write a general equation for the reaction, using RCH(NH₂)COOH. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the mass increase to calculate the amount of **X** and its molar mass. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce the formula of R. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest a structure for R that gives **X** two chiral centres. **[1]**

**10.** Compound **V** is H₂NCH(CH₂C₆H₅)CONHCH(CH₃)COOCH₃.

**(a)** State whether the amide group in **V** is primary or secondary, and give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many chiral centres **V** has, and identify each one. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the organic product of **V** with dilute hydrochloric acid at room temperature. **[1]**
**(d)** **V** is made from **U**, H₂NCH(CH₂C₆H₅)CONHCH(CH₃)COOH. State the reagent and conditions, and write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**11.** An α-amino acid, **Z**, contains 10.7% nitrogen by mass. Its side chain R is a saturated, non-cyclic alkyl group.

**(a)** Calculate the molar mass of **Z**. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of R. **[1]**
**(c)** Draw the structures of the four possible amino acids that fit this data. **[2]**
**(d)** Identify the isomer with two chiral centres and mark both with asterisks. **[2]**
**(e)** Write an equation for this isomer with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(f)** Explain why all four isomers show optical isomerism. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **RCH(NH₂)COOH** [1]
**(b)** **CH₃SCH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH** [1]; **C₅H₁₁NO₂S** [1]
*Examiner insight:* NH₂ and COOH must sit on the same carbon; otherwise it is not an α-amino acid.

**2.** **A** primary amide [1]; **B** secondary amide [1]; **C** secondary amide [1]; **D** neither: a ketone and an amine, with no C=O on N [1]
*Examiner insight:* For **D**, C=O and NH₂ in one molecule are not an amide unless bonded to each other.

**3. (a)** **C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH + HCl → C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH(NH₃⁺)COOH Cl⁻** [1]
**(b)** **C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH + NaOH → C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **Sodium 2-amino-4-phenylbutanoate** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each reagent changes one group only; changing both NH₂ and COOH with one mole of reagent loses the mark.

**4. (a)** Propan-1-ol with concentrated sulfuric acid as catalyst [1]; heat [1]
**(b)** **CH₃SCH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH ⇌ CH₃SCH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O** [1]
**(c)** n(methionine) = 4.473 ÷ 149.1 = 0.0300 mol [1]; M(ester) = 191.1 g mol⁻¹, so n(ester) = 3.44 ÷ 191.1 = 0.0180 mol [1]; yield = 0.0180 ÷ 0.0300 × 100 = **60.0%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show both mole values; a ratio of masses ignores the change in molar mass.

**5. (a)** The CH carbon is a chiral centre: it carries four different groups, H, CH₃, NH₂ and C₆H₅ [1]; so the molecule exists as two mirror images that are non-superimposable [1]
**(b)** A 3-D tetrahedral drawing using in-plane, wedged and hashed bonds [1]; its reflection beside a mirror line, every group labelled [1]. For example: H up and CH₃ left in the plane, NH₂ wedge right, C₆H₅ hashed down; then H up, CH₃ right, NH₂ wedge left, C₆H₅ hashed down.
*Examiner insight:* Four plain bonds cannot show the mirror relationship; use wedges and label every group.

**6. (a)** **1** (C3) [1]
**(b)** **1** (C2) [1]
**(c)** **2** (C2 and C4) [1]
**(d)** **0**: C3 carries two ethyl groups [1]
**(e)** **1** (C2: H, CH₃, the C=O side of the ring and the CH₂ side of the ring) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), C2 holds two CH₃ groups, so it is not chiral; test every carbon for four different groups.

**7. (a)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CONH₂ + NH₄Cl** [1]; **primary** amide [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)COCl + 2CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CONHCH₂CH₂CH₃ + CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻** [1]; **secondary** amide [1]
**(c)** **C2**: H, CH₃, C₃H₇ and CONHC₃H₇ [1]
*Examiner insight:* With two moles of amine, the second must appear as the alkylammonium chloride, not as unused amine beside HCl.

**8. (a)** They are **optical isomers**, non-superimposable mirror images [1]; drawing 2 is drawing 1 with two groups (NH₂ and C₅H₁₁) exchanged, and one swap converts a chiral centre into its mirror-image form [1]
**(b)** They are the **same molecule**: drawing 3 is drawing 1 turned through 180° in the plane of the page [1]
*Examiner insight:* Try a rotation before deciding; say how one drawing turns into the other.

**9. (a)** **RCH(NH₂)COOH + NaOH → RCH(NH₂)COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O** [1]
**(b)** Na replaces H, so each mole gains 22.0 g; gain = 3.34 − 2.90 = 0.44 g [1]; n(X) = 0.44 ÷ 22.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; M = 2.90 ÷ 0.0200 = **145 g mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** CH(NH₂)COOH accounts for 74.0, so R = 145 − 74 = 71 = **C₅H₁₁** [1]
**(d)** For example **–CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₂CH₃**; also acceptable: –CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃ or –CH(CH₃)CH(CH₃)₂ [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), a straight pentyl chain gives only one chiral centre; R must contain a carbon with four different groups.

**10. (a)** **Secondary** [1]: the amide N is bonded to two carbons [1]
**(b)** **2** [1]: the CH carrying NH₂ and CH₂C₆H₅, and the CH carrying CH₃ and COOCH₃ [1]
**(c)** **Cl⁻ ⁺H₃NCH(CH₂C₆H₅)CONHCH(CH₃)COOCH₃** [1]
**(d)** Methanol with concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst, heated [1]; **U + CH₃OH ⇌ V + H₂O**, with U and V written out in full [1]
*Examiner insight:* The benzyl CH₂ and the ring carbons are not chiral; identify each chiral carbon by the groups on it.

**11. (a)** M = 14.0 ÷ 0.107 = 130.8, so **131 g mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** R = 131 − 74 = 57, so **C₄H₉** [1]
**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH(NH₂)COOH and CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH(NH₂)COOH [1]; (CH₃)₂CHCH₂CH(NH₂)COOH and (CH₃)₃CCH(NH₂)COOH [1]
**(d)** **CH₃CH₂C\*H(CH₃)C\*H(NH₂)COOH** [1]; asterisks on the α-carbon and on the side-chain CH carrying CH₃ and C₂H₅ [1]
**(e)** **CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH(NH₂)COOH + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH(CH₃)CH(NH₂)COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O** [1]
**(f)** Each α-carbon carries four different groups: H, NH₂, COOH and C₄H₉ [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction of 74 for CH(NH₂)COOH; a bare C₄H₉ with no working gives nothing to credit if it is wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing RCH(NH₃)COOH as the general formula (question 1).
- Calling compound **D** an amide (question 2).
- Showing HCl at the COOH group or NaOH at the NH₂ group (questions 3 and 9).
- Omitting "concentrated" for the H₂SO₄ catalyst (question 4).
- Finding percentage yield from masses, not moles (question 4).
- Omitting "non-superimposable" from the definition (question 5).
- Marking a carbon with two identical groups as chiral (questions 6 and 9).
- Missing a side-chain chiral centre (questions 9 to 11).
