## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Conformity | Change in behaviour or belief because of real or imagined group pressure |
| Compliance | Public agreement with the group without private acceptance; temporary |
| Internalisation | Public and private acceptance of the group's view; lasting |
| Normative social influence (NSI) | Conforming to be liked and accepted |
| Informational social influence (ISI) | Conforming because others are thought to be right |
| Obedience | Following a direct order from an authority figure |
| Agentic state | Seeing oneself as an agent of authority, not responsible for one's actions |
| Autonomous state | Acting on one's own judgement and taking responsibility |
| Agentic shift | Move from the autonomous to the agentic state |
| Moral strain | Anxiety felt when obeying orders believed to be wrong |
| Binding factors | Aspects of a situation that keep a person in the agentic state |
| Legitimacy of authority | Accepting that people higher in a social hierarchy have the right to give orders |
| Authoritarian Personality | Dispositional type marked by deference to authority and contempt for lower-status groups |
| F-scale | Adorno's questionnaire measuring authoritarian traits |
| Social support | Others who resist, showing that resistance is possible |
| Locus of control | Belief about whether outcomes depend on oneself (internal) or on external forces (external) |
| Minority influence | A small group changing the views of a majority, usually by internalisation |
| Augmentation principle | Commitment involving personal risk increases the minority's influence |

## Studies at a glance

| Study | Key finding | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Asch, group size | 1 confederate: very low conformity; 3: about 31.8%; more added little | Group size |
| Asch, unanimity | One dissenter cut conformity to about 5.5% | Unanimity; social support |
| Asch, task difficulty | Harder line task raised conformity | Task difficulty; ISI |
| Perrin and Spencer (1980) | Far less conformity among UK engineering students | Asch as "a child of its time" |
| Milgram (1963) | 65% to 450 V; 100% to 300 V | Baseline obedience |
| Milgram variations | Same room 40%; touch 30%; telephone 20.5%; run-down office 47.5%; ordinary member of the public 20% | Proximity, location, uniform |
| Bickman (1974) | Security-guard uniform obeyed more than milkman or ordinary clothes | Uniform |
| Hofling et al. (1966) | 21 of 22 nurses ready to give a dangerous dose | Real-world obedience |
| Rank and Jacobson (1977) | Most nurses refused when they knew the drug and could consult | Challenge to Hofling |
| Sheridan and King (1972) | High obedience with real shocks to a puppy | Defends Milgram's validity |
| Adorno et al. (1950) | Strict upbringing linked to authoritarian traits | Dispositional explanation |
| Elms and Milgram (1966) | Fully obedient participants scored higher on the F-scale | Supports the Authoritarian Personality |
| Milgram, disobedient peers | Obedience fell to 10% | Social support |
| Holland (1967) | 37% of internals resisted to the end, 23% of externals | Locus of control |
| Moscovici et al. (1969) | Consistent minority 8.42% "green" answers; inconsistent 1.25% | Consistency |

## Must-know distinctions

- **NSI versus ISI**: to be liked versus to be right. NSI leads to compliance; ISI to internalisation.
- **Situational versus dispositional**: agentic state, legitimacy, proximity, location and uniform are situational; the Authoritarian Personality is dispositional.
- **Conformity versus obedience**: conformity follows peers with no direct order; obedience follows an order from authority.
- **Majority versus minority influence**: majorities often produce compliance; minorities, through consistency, commitment and flexibility, produce internalisation.

## Mini essay plan: discuss the Authoritarian Personality as an explanation of obedience [16]

- AO1: Adorno et al. (1950); strict upbringing with conditional love; hostility displaced onto weaker groups; deference to authority; F-scale.
- AO3: Elms and Milgram (1966) support.
- AO3: correlation, not cause; education could explain both.
- AO3: F-scale measures right-wing authoritarianism only.
- AO3: situational variation in Milgram's studies (proximity, location) shows behaviour changing without personality changing.
- Judgement: personality may explain individual differences within a situation, while situational factors explain most of the overall level of obedience.

## Exam traps

- Writing about identification: Version 1.4 names compliance and internalisation only.
- Swapping NSI and ISI.
- Quoting Milgram percentages for the wrong variation.
- Calling the Authoritarian Personality situational.
- Saying people with an external locus resist more.
- Naming consistency, commitment and flexibility without explaining their effect.
- Evaluating with "it lacks ecological validity" without saying why for that study.

## Self-test

1. Define compliance.
2. Which explanation of conformity is linked to ambiguous situations?
3. What happened to conformity in Asch's study when one confederate gave the correct answer?
4. State the percentage of Milgram's baseline participants who reached 450 V.
5. What happened to obedience when the study moved to a run-down office?
6. Define the agentic state.
7. What did Bickman (1974) investigate?
8. Name the scale used to measure the Authoritarian Personality.
9. Give one limitation of the Authoritarian Personality explanation.
10. Why does an internal locus of control help people resist social influence?
11. What did Moscovici et al. (1969) find about consistency?
12. Explain the augmentation principle.

### Answers

1. Agreeing with the group in public while privately disagreeing; it stops when the group is absent.
2. Informational social influence.
3. It fell to about 5.5%.
4. 65%.
5. It fell to 47.5%.
6. A state in which a person sees themselves as carrying out an authority figure's wishes, so does not feel responsible for their actions.
7. The effect of uniform on obedience: a security-guard uniform produced more obedience than a milkman's uniform or ordinary clothes.
8. The F-scale.
9. Any one: the evidence is correlational; it measures right-wing attitudes only; it cannot explain why obedience changes with the situation.
10. Internals take personal responsibility for their actions and rely less on others' approval, so they are more likely to act on their own judgement.
11. A consistent minority produced more "green" answers (8.42%) than an inconsistent one (1.25%).
12. When a minority accepts personal risk for its view, others take the view more seriously, so its influence increases.
