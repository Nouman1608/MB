These notes cover section 3.3.1.4, Statistical skills, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Geographical skills can be tested in all three written exams. For full explanations and worked data sets, read the [statistical skills study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-statistical-skills/). For the wider skills picture, see the [geographical and fieldwork skills revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-geographical-and-fieldwork-skills-revision-notes/).

## Specification checklist

- **Central tendency:** mean, median, mode, modal class.
- **Spread:** range, quartiles, inter-quartile range (IQR), cumulative frequency.
- **Percentage increase or decrease**; **percentiles**.
- **Bivariate data:** trend lines, lines of best fit, predictions, interpolation and extrapolation.
- **Weaknesses** in selective presentation of data.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mean | Total divided by the number of values |
| Median | Middle value of ordered data |
| Mode | Most common value |
| Modal class | Class with the highest frequency |
| Range | Largest minus smallest |
| Quartile | Value that splits ordered data into quarters |
| IQR | Q3 minus Q1 |
| Cumulative frequency | Running total of frequencies |
| Percentile | Value below which a given percentage of data fall |
| Interpolation | Estimating inside the range of data |
| Extrapolation | Estimating beyond the range of data |
| Anomaly | Value far from the rest |

## Quick method card

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Mean | Add all values; divide by how many |
| Median (odd n) | Middle value after ordering |
| Median (even n) | Mean of the two middle values |
| Mode | Most frequent value |
| Range | Highest minus lowest |
| Q1 and Q3 (even n) | Median of lower half and of upper half |
| IQR | Q3 minus Q1 |
| Grouped mean | Sum of (midpoint × frequency) divided by total frequency |
| Percentage change | (new minus old) ÷ old × 100 |

## Worked example: ungrouped data

Pebble lengths (mm), 12 values: 12, 15, 15, 18, 20, 22, 25, 25, 25, 31, 34, 45.

- Total 287; mean = 287 ÷ 12 = **23.9 mm**.
- Median = (22 + 25) ÷ 2 = **23.5 mm**.
- Mode = **25 mm**.
- Range = 45 - 12 = **33 mm**.
- Q1 = (15 + 18) ÷ 2 = **16.5**; Q3 = (25 + 31) ÷ 2 = **28**; IQR = **11.5 mm**.

## Which average?

- **Mean:** uses every value, but is pulled by outliers.
- **Median:** resists outliers; best for skewed data.
- **Mode:** most common; good for categories.
- If the mean is above the median, a few large values are pulling the mean up.

## Grouped data and cumulative frequency

| Size (mm) | Frequency | Cumulative frequency |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to under 10 | 4 | 4 |
| 10 to under 20 | 10 | 14 |
| 20 to under 30 | 18 | 32 |
| 30 to under 40 | 12 | 44 |
| 40 to under 50 | 6 | 50 |

- Modal class: 20 to under 30 mm.
- Estimated mean: 1 310 ÷ 50 = 26.2 mm (sum of midpoint × frequency = 1 310).
- Plot cumulative frequency against the **upper class limit**; join with a smooth curve.
- Median at n ÷ 2 = 25: about 26.1 mm. Q1 at n ÷ 4 = 12.5: about 18.5 mm. Q3 at 3n ÷ 4 = 37.5: about 34.6 mm. IQR about 16.1 mm.
- 90th percentile at 45: about 41.7 mm.

## Percentage change

- Always divide by the **old** value.
- 48 000 to 66 000 visitors: +37.5%.
- 1 600 mm to 1 250 mm rainfall: about -21.9%.
- Do not confuse percentage change with a difference in percentage points.

## Scatter graphs and trend lines

- Describe: direction (positive or negative), strength (strong, weak or none), and any anomalies.
- Line of best fit: straight, by eye, roughly equal numbers of points either side. Do not join the dots. It does not have to go through the origin.
- **Interpolate:** read a value inside the data range; reliable.
- **Extrapolate:** extend beyond the data; less reliable because the pattern may change.
- Correlation is not proof of cause.

## Selective presentation: how data can mislead

| Weakness | Example |
|---|---|
| Truncated axis | Bars starting at 90, not 0 |
| Cherry-picked period | A start year chosen to show a rapid rise |
| Only the mean | Hides spread and outliers |
| Small or biased sample | 10 people from one street |
| Percentages of small numbers | "Up 100%" from 2 to 4 |
| Removing anomalies | Omitting inconvenient results |
| Distorted symbols | Doubling height and width so area quadruples |
| Unequal class intervals | Not adjusted on the chart |

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** A student draws a bar chart of rainfall for two towns using a vertical axis that starts at 800 mm. Town A has 820 mm and Town B has 840 mm. Explain why the chart is misleading. [3 marks]

**Model answer:** "The axis does not start at zero, so the bar for Town B looks twice as tall as the bar for Town A, but its rainfall is only 20 mm greater. This exaggerates a difference of about 2.4% (20 ÷ 820). A reader who looks only at the bar heights would wrongly think that Town B is much wetter."

How it scores: one mark for the truncated axis, one for the effect on bar heights, and one for the real size of the difference.

## Exam traps

- Put data in **order** before finding median and quartiles.
- Use class **upper limits** on cumulative frequency graphs.
- Divide by the **old** value for percentage change.
- Show **working** and give units.
- A trend line is **not** a join-the-dots line.
- Extrapolation needs a caution.
- When asked about bias or weakness, **say how** it misleads.

## Self-test

1. Find the median of: 4, 9, 2, 7, 5, 8.
2. Find the mean of: 6, 8, 10, 12, 14.
3. State the range of: 3, 11, 7, 20, 5.
4. What is the modal class of a grouped data set?
5. Q1 is 12 and Q3 is 30. Find the IQR.
6. A town grows from 40 000 to 46 000. Calculate the percentage increase.
7. What does the 25th percentile mean?
8. What is the difference between interpolation and extrapolation?
9. Why is the median often better than the mean for river pebble sizes?
10. Give **two** ways a graph can mislead.

**Answers**

1. Ordered: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9; median = (5 + 7) ÷ 2 = 6.
2. 50 ÷ 5 = 10.
3. 20 - 3 = 17.
4. The class interval with the highest frequency.
5. 30 - 12 = 18.
6. (46 000 - 40 000) ÷ 40 000 × 100 = 15%.
7. The value below which 25% of the data lie (the lower quartile).
8. Interpolation estimates inside the range of the data; extrapolation estimates beyond it.
9. A few very large pebbles can pull the mean up, but the median is not affected by them.
10. Any two: truncated axis; cherry-picked time period; distorted symbols; unequal intervals; leaving out data.
