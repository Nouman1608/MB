## Questions

**1.** Explain why a software engineer should join a professional ethical body such as the BCS or the IEEE. **[3]**

**2.** Saira works for a company that writes software for hospital drug-dosage pumps. Testing shows that the software occasionally rounds a dose incorrectly. Her manager tells her to release the software on time and fix the fault in a later update.

**(a)** Explain why releasing the software would be unethical. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe what Saira should do, acting ethically. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe **two** possible impacts on the company if it acts unethically in this situation. **[2]**

**3.** A developer at a web design company is asked to collect users' browsing data from a client's website and sell it to an advertising firm without telling the users. Identify **two** principles of a professional code of conduct that this would break, and explain how. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why copyright legislation is needed for software. **[3]**

**5.** A student copies 200 lines of code from a commercial game into his own game, which he sells online. Explain why this is a breach of copyright. **[2]**

**6.** For **each** situation, identify the most suitable type of software licence and justify your choice.

**(a)** A small company has written a photo editor. It wants people to try it for 30 days, then pay to keep using it. **[2]**
**(b)** A university research group wants other researchers to be able to use, study and change its data-analysis software, and wants every changed version to remain freely changeable. **[2]**
**(c)** A bank needs accounting software with guaranteed support and regular security updates from the supplier. **[2]**

**7.** Give **two** differences between software released under a Free Software Foundation licence and commercial software. **[2]**

**8.** Describe **two** applications of artificial intelligence. **[2]**

**9.** A city plans to use AI to control its traffic lights and to run driverless buses. Discuss the social, economic and environmental impact of this plan. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three: the engineer agrees to follow a published code of conduct, which shows employers and clients that they can be trusted [1]; the body sets standards for professional behaviour and gives guidance on ethical dilemmas [1]; it supports members in refusing an unethical instruction [1]; it offers training / continuing professional development so the engineer keeps up to date [1]; membership / recognised qualifications improve career prospects [1].

**2. (a)** Patients could be given the wrong dose and be harmed or killed, so public health and safety are not being put first [1]. Releasing software that is known to be faulty is dishonest towards the hospitals that buy it [1]. Saira would be failing in her professional responsibility / competence by approving work she knows is not fit for purpose [1].
**(b)** She should refuse to release the software / report the fault formally (in writing) to her manager and keep a record [1]; if the manager still insists, she should raise it with someone more senior or the relevant authority (and can seek guidance from her professional body) [1].
**(c)** Any two: legal action and compensation claims if patients are harmed [1]; loss of reputation and future contracts with hospitals [1]; the cost of recalling and fixing the pumps later is higher [1]; regulators may stop the company selling the product [1].

**3.** Any two principles, each named [1] and applied [1]:
- Public interest / privacy: users' personal data is used without their knowledge or consent, which harms their privacy [1+1].
- Honesty and integrity: the users are being deceived, as they are not told what is collected [1+1].
- Respect for the law: selling personal data without consent is likely to break data protection law [1+1].

**4.** Copyright gives the creator legal ownership of their work, so others cannot copy, distribute or adapt it without permission [1]. This lets developers earn income from their software, so they are willing to invest time and money creating it [1]. It gives the owner a legal remedy against piracy, such as suing people who sell illegal copies [1].

**5.** The code is the original work of the game's creators and is protected by copyright automatically [1]. Copying it and selling it without the owner's permission (and outside the licence terms) is unauthorised copying and distribution [1].

**6. (a)** Shareware [1]: users can try the full or limited program free for the trial period, and must pay to continue using it, which is what the company wants [1].
**(b)** A Free Software Foundation licence (e.g. GPL) [1]: the source code is available so researchers can study and change it, and the licence requires any modified version to be released with the same freedoms [1].
**(c)** Commercial software [1]: the bank pays for a licence that includes support and regular updates from the supplier, and the supplier is responsible for the software [1].

**7.** Any two: free software's source code is available, commercial software's is not [1]; users of free software can modify and redistribute it, commercial software cannot be changed or copied [1]; free software may be free of charge, commercial software must be paid for [1].

**8.** Any two, each with a short description [1 each]: medical diagnosis, where AI analyses scans to detect disease; autonomous vehicles that sense their surroundings and decide how to drive; voice assistants that recognise speech and answer questions; fraud detection that spots unusual card transactions; recommendation systems that suggest products or videos based on past behaviour.

**9.** Level-style marking: up to 2 marks for each of social, economic and environmental impact, with a point **for** and a point **against** in each area. Indicative content:
- Social: fewer accidents if driverless buses react faster than people, and better transport for people who cannot drive [1]; but passengers may not trust driverless buses, and it is unclear who is responsible if an AI system causes an accident [1].
- Economic: lower running costs and less time lost in traffic jams [1]; but bus drivers may lose their jobs, and the system is expensive to develop, install and maintain [1].
- Environmental: smoother traffic flow means less stopping and starting, so lower fuel use and pollution [1]; but the AI systems use electricity in data centres, and replacing vehicles and hardware creates electronic waste [1].

*Tip:* "discuss" needs both sides. Six social points earn at most 2 marks here.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying something is "illegal" when the question asks why it is unethical.
- Choosing open source or free software when the scenario needs guaranteed supplier support, or choosing commercial when it asks for others to change the code.
- Treating "free" as meaning "no cost".
- Giving general points about AI that are not about the city, hospital or company in the question.
- Repeating the scenario as if it were a point.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a "discuss" answer on AI be?** About one or two sentences per mark, covering each area named and both sides of each.

**Can a Free Software Foundation licence program be sold?** Yes. "Free" refers to the user's freedom to run, study, change and share it, not to price.

**Which ethical bodies does the 9618 syllabus name?** The BCS (British Computer Society) and the IEEE (Institute of Electrical and Electronic Engineers).
