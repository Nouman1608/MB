These questions cover key question 2.4 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, "Who was to blame for the Cold War?", which
was the Paper 2 prescribed topic for November 2024.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the decisions made at the Yalta Conference in February 1945. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** leaders who attended the Potsdam Conference. **[2]**

**3.** Describe how the communists took control of Poland between 1944 and 1947. **[4]**

**4.** Describe the Truman Doctrine. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** Soviet organisations set up to control Eastern Europe in 1947–49. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the Berlin Airlift. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why had relations between the USA and the USSR worsened by the end of 1946? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Stalin refuse Marshall Aid? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Soviet expansion into Eastern Europe was the main cause of the Cold War." How far do you agree? Explain your
answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The Berlin Blockade was a victory for the West." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined article in a Soviet newspaper, July 1947. "The Americans call it help. But help
does not come with conditions written in Washington, or with inspectors counting every loaf. This is a plan to buy
Europe with dollars and to build a wall of American banks around the Soviet Union."

Why do you think this article was published in July 1947? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer.
**[7]**

**12.** Study Source B, which is invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary American cartoon from 1948, written for this page. A large bear
labelled "USSR" sits on a map of Eastern Europe, its paws covering Poland, Hungary and Czechoslovakia. It is reaching
towards a small island labelled "Berlin", while planes fly overhead dropping parcels.

How far does Source B show who was to blame for the Cold War? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your
answer. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: Germany would be divided into four zones (1); Berlin would also be divided (1); Eastern
European countries would hold free elections (1); the USSR would join the war against Japan after Germany's defeat
(1); a United Nations organisation would be set up (1); Poland's border would move westwards (1); war criminals would
be tried (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: Stalin (1); Truman (1); Churchill (1); Attlee (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: the Red Army occupied Poland in 1944–45 (1); Stalin backed the communist-led Lublin
Committee as the government (1); the Red Army did not help the Warsaw Rising of 1944, which weakened the
non-communist Home Army (1); a coalition government included some non-communists such as Mikołajczyk (1);
communists controlled the police and security forces (1); opponents were arrested or intimidated (1); elections in
January 1947 were rigged (1); Mikołajczyk fled abroad (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: announced by Truman in March 1947 (1); Britain could no longer afford to support Greece
and Turkey (1); Truman asked Congress for aid to them (1); the USA would support free peoples resisting subjugation by
armed minorities or outside pressures (1); it described the world as a choice between two ways of life (1); it began
the policy of containment (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Cominform (1947) (1); Comecon (1949) (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: Stalin cut road, rail and canal routes to West Berlin in June 1948 (1); the USA and
Britain flew in supplies (1); food, coal and fuel were carried by aircraft (1); planes landed every few minutes at the
height of the airlift (1); it lasted almost eleven months (1); the Soviets did not shoot down the planes (1); Stalin
lifted the blockade in May 1949 (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **the common enemy
had gone**, so ideological differences returned; **Poland**, where Stalin installed a friendly government contrary to
the West's hopes for free elections; **change of leaders**: Truman was more hostile than Roosevelt; **the atomic bomb**
increased Soviet suspicion and US confidence; **1946 statements**: the Long Telegram and the Iron Curtain speech
encouraged a hard line, and the Novikov telegram did the same in Moscow. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **fear of US influence**: aid would bring American economic and
political influence into Eastern Europe; **conditions**: receiving countries had to open their economies and accounts
to US oversight; **control of satellites**: accepting aid might pull states such as Czechoslovakia towards the West, so
he forced them to refuse; **propaganda**: he presented it as "dollar imperialism" and set up the Cominform and later
Comecon in response. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:** Stalin
broke the Yalta promise of free elections; takeovers in Poland, Hungary and Czechoslovakia convinced the West he meant
to spread communism; the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Plan were responses to this. **Disagree:** ideological
hostility pre-dated 1945; the atomic bomb and Truman's attitude increased Soviet fear; Marshall Aid and the currency
reform in Germany provoked the Berlin Blockade; disagreements over Germany's future were at least as important; both
sides misread defensive moves as aggressive. **Judgement:** for example, Soviet expansion was the most visible cause,
but Germany was where the Cold War became a confrontation. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** West Berlin was not abandoned; Stalin backed down in May 1949; the airlift
was a propaganda success showing Western resolve without fighting; it led to NATO and a strong US commitment to Europe;
the Federal Republic was founded. **Disagree:** Germany was permanently divided; the USSR kept control of East Germany
and East Berlin; the crisis made the Cold War more dangerous and began a long contest over Berlin; the USSR tested its
atomic bomb in August 1949, ending the US monopoly. **Judgement:** a clear short-term Western victory, but at the cost
of a divided Germany and a more militarised Cold War. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 4 (6–7): explains a purpose using the source's content and the context of July 1947. Level 3
(4–5): explains the context or the message. Level 2 (2–3): describes the source's view. Level 1 (1): undeveloped
comment. Indicative content: **context**: the Marshall Plan had been announced in June 1947, and European states were
discussing it in Paris; Stalin had withdrawn the USSR and was pressing Eastern European states to refuse; **purpose**:
to justify Soviet refusal to the Soviet public; to warn other countries, especially in Eastern Europe, that accepting
aid meant American control; to present the USA as the aggressor ("a wall of American banks"). **[7]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (6–7): explains how far the cartoon shows blame, using details, provenance and knowledge.
Level 3 (4–5): explains what the cartoon suggests about blame. Level 2 (2–3): describes details. Level 1 (1): surface
reading. Indicative content: **it suggests the USSR was to blame**: the bear has already taken Eastern Europe and is
grasping for Berlin, while the West only drops supplies, showing Soviet aggression and Western defence; this matches
the takeovers of 1945–48 and the Blockade of 1948. **Limits:** it is American, produced to support the Western view;
it ignores the Western actions that the USSR saw as provocative, such as the new currency and plans for a West German
state; it shows one moment, not the origins in 1945–46. **Judgement:** it shows who an American cartoonist blamed, not
who was to blame. **[7]**
