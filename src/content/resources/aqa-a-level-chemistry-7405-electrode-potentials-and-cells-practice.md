These questions cover section **3.1.11.1 Electrode potentials and cells** of the AQA A-level Chemistry specification (7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 (and Paper 3). Learn the methods in the [Electrode potentials and cells study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-electrode-potentials-and-cells/). Use these standard electrode potentials (E⦵ / V): Mg²⁺/Mg −2.37; Zn²⁺/Zn −0.76; Fe²⁺/Fe −0.44; 2H⁺/H₂ 0.00; Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ +0.15; Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34; I₂/2I⁻ +0.54; Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77; Ag⁺/Ag +0.80; Br₂/2Br⁻ +1.07; Cl₂/2Cl⁻ +1.36; MnO₄⁻, H⁺/Mn²⁺ +1.51.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term standard electrode potential. **[3]**

**2.** Describe the standard hydrogen electrode, stating the conditions used. **[3]**

**3.** Write half-equations, in the IUPAC convention, for the electrode reactions of:

**(a)** zinc in zinc sulfate solution **[1]**

**(b)** a platinum electrode in a solution containing Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ ions **[1]**

**4.** A cell is made from a magnesium half-cell and a silver half-cell.

**(a)** Write the conventional cell diagram. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the EMF of the cell. **[2]**

**(c)** State the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. **[1]**

**5.** In an experiment to measure the EMF of a cell:

**(a)** state the purpose of the salt bridge **[1]**

**(b)** explain why the voltmeter has a high resistance **[1]**

**(c)** explain why a platinum electrode is used in the Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ half-cell **[1]**

**6.** Calculate the EMF of the cell Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Fe³⁺(aq), Fe²⁺(aq) | Pt(s). Write the overall equation for the cell reaction. **[3]**

**7.** Use the data to predict whether each reaction is feasible. Show the calculation.

**(a)** Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ **[2]**

**(b)** Cu + 2H⁺ → Cu²⁺ + H₂ **[2]**

**(c)** 2Fe³⁺ + Sn²⁺ → 2Fe²⁺ + Sn⁴⁺ **[2]**

**8.** A half-cell consists of a copper electrode in a 0.10 mol dm⁻³ solution of copper(II) sulfate, at 298 K. State and explain whether its electrode potential is more or less positive than the standard value of +0.34 V. **[2]**

**9.** Acidified potassium manganate(VII) is used to oxidise iodide ions to iodine.

**(a)** Calculate the EMF for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the overall equation. **[2]**

**10.** A student calculates the EMF of the zinc–copper cell as 0.34 + (−0.76) = −0.42 V. Identify the error and give the correct value. **[2]**

**11.** The data predict that a reaction between two reagents is feasible, but no reaction is seen when they are mixed. Suggest two reasons. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why a standard electrode potential does not change when the half-equation is multiplied by two. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The EMF of a cell [1] in which the half-cell being measured, under standard conditions, is connected to a standard hydrogen electrode [1]; the standard conditions are 298 K, 100 kPa and ion concentrations of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ [1]. **[3]**

**2.** A platinum electrode (coated with platinum black) [1]; in contact with hydrogen gas at 100 kPa [1]; and a solution containing H⁺ at 1.00 mol dm⁻³, at 298 K [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn(s) [1]

**(b)** Fe³⁺(aq) + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺(aq) [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Mg(s) | Mg²⁺(aq) || Ag⁺(aq) | Ag(s) [1 for species and order; 1 for correct use of the phase boundaries and salt bridge]

**(b)** EMF = E⦵(Ag⁺/Ag) − E⦵(Mg²⁺/Mg) = 0.80 − (−2.37) [1] = **+3.17 V** [1]

**(c)** From the magnesium (negative electrode) to the silver (positive electrode) [1]. **[5]**

**5. (a)** It completes the circuit by allowing ions to flow between the half-cells without the solutions mixing [1]

**(b)** So that almost no current flows, and the reading is the EMF [1]

**(c)** Both species are in solution and there is no metal, so an inert conductor is needed to carry electrons [1]. **[3]**

**6.** EMF = E⦵(right) − E⦵(left) = (+0.77) − (−0.76) [1] = **+1.53 V** [1]; overall: 2Fe³⁺ + Zn → 2Fe²⁺ + Zn²⁺ [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** E⦵ = 1.36 − 1.07 = +0.29 V [1]; positive, so feasible [1]

**(b)** E⦵ = 0.00 − 0.34 = −0.34 V [1]; negative, so not feasible [1]

**(c)** E⦵ = 0.77 − 0.15 = +0.62 V [1]; positive, so feasible [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Less positive [1]; the equilibrium Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Cu shifts to the left when the concentration of Cu²⁺ is lower, so the tendency to be reduced falls [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** EMF = 1.51 − 0.54 = **+0.97 V** [1]

**(b)** 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 10I⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 5I₂ [1 for species; 1 for balancing]. **[3]**

**10.** The EMF is the right-hand potential minus the left-hand potential (positive minus negative), not the sum [1]; correct value: 0.34 − (−0.76) = **+1.10 V** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The reaction may have a high activation energy and so be very slow [1]; the conditions may be non-standard (concentration, temperature) so the actual EMF differs from the standard value [1]. **[2]**

**12.** E⦵ is the potential per unit charge and does not depend on the amount of substance in the equation [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing half-equations the wrong way round.
- Subtracting in the wrong order, or adding potentials.
- Multiplying E⦵ by the number of electrons.
- Leaving out the platinum electrode for ion/ion half-cells.
- Missing out the standard conditions in a definition.
- Treating a positive EMF as proof of a fast reaction.
