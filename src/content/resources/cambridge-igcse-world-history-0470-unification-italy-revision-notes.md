**Key question 1.2, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "How was Italy unified?" Learn Austrian influence, Mazzini, the
events of 1848-49, Cavour and Plombières, the war of 1859, Garibaldi's invasion of Sicily and Naples, and the
completion of unity by 1870. This is the **Paper 2 prescribed topic for November 2026**. Explanations are in the
[Italy study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-unification-italy/); practise with the
[Italy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-unification-italy-practice/).

## The map in 1815-48

| State | Ruler |
|---|---|
| Lombardy and Venetia | Austria, directly |
| Tuscany, Modena, Parma | Habsburg relatives or Austrian allies |
| Papal States | The Pope |
| Kingdom of the Two Sicilies | Bourbon king in Naples |
| Piedmont-Sardinia | House of Savoy: independent, the only native dynasty able to stand up to Austria |

**Obstacles to unity:** Austrian armies; the Pope's lands across the centre; dialects and regional loyalty; poor
communications; separate currencies and tariffs; peasant indifference.

## Timeline to learn

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1831 | Mazzini founds Young Italy |
| 1848 | Five Days of Milan; Venice republic; Piedmont declares war; defeated at Custoza; Pope refuses to fight Austria |
| 1849 | Piedmont defeated at Novara; Charles Albert abdicates for Victor Emmanuel II; Roman Republic falls to France |
| 1852 | Cavour becomes prime minister of Piedmont |
| 1855-56 | Piedmont fights in the Crimean War; Congress of Paris |
| July 1858 | Plombières agreement between Cavour and Napoleon III |
| 1859 | War with Austria: Magenta and Solferino; armistice of Villafranca; Piedmont gains Lombardy |
| March 1860 | Plebiscites: central duchies and Romagna join Piedmont; Nice and Savoy go to France |
| May-September 1860 | Expedition of the Thousand: Marsala, Calatafimi, Palermo, Naples |
| October 1860 | Teano: Garibaldi hands his conquests to Victor Emmanuel |
| March 1861 | Kingdom of Italy proclaimed; Cavour dies in June |
| 1862, 1867 | Garibaldi fails to take Rome at Aspromonte and Mentana |
| 1866 | Italy allies with Prussia; gains Venetia after Austria's defeat |
| September 1870 | French garrison withdrawn; Italian troops enter Rome |

## Key people

| Person | Remember |
|---|---|
| Mazzini | Republican idealist; Young Italy; Roman Republic 1849; inspired but did not achieve unity |
| Charles Albert | King of Piedmont; granted the Statuto; lost to Austria in 1848-49 |
| Victor Emmanuel II | King of Piedmont from 1849; first King of Italy, 1861 |
| Cavour | Piedmont's prime minister; modernised; won French alliance at Plombières |
| Napoleon III | Fought Austria in 1859, then made peace early; gained Nice and Savoy; protected Rome until 1870 |
| Garibaldi | Soldier; led the Thousand; conquered the south; handed it over at Teano |
| Pius IX | Pope; refused war on Austria in 1848; lost Rome in 1870 |
| Radetzky | Austrian commander who defeated Piedmont in 1848-49 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Risorgimento | "Resurgence": the movement for Italian unity |
| Young Italy | Mazzini's society for a united, democratic republic |
| Statuto | Piedmont's 1848 constitution, kept after 1849 |
| Plombières | Secret 1858 deal: French help against Austria in return for Nice and Savoy |
| Villafranca | 1859 armistice ending the war before Venetia was won |
| Plebiscite | A vote of the people on a single question, such as joining Piedmont |
| Redshirts | Garibaldi's volunteers |
| Roman Question | The dispute over the Pope's lost territory after 1870 |

## Cavour versus Garibaldi: the argument in brief

| | Cavour | Garibaldi |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Strong northern kingdom under Piedmont, widened as events allowed | United Italy, with Rome as capital |
| Method | Diplomacy, alliances, regular armies | Volunteer military campaigns |
| Key achievement | Defeat of Austria in 1859; annexations of 1860 | Conquest of Sicily and Naples, 1860 |
| Weakness | Gave up Nice and Savoy; distrusted the south | Could not take Rome; risked war with France |

**A balanced judgement:** Garibaldi won the south, but Cavour's alliance and state made the gains last.

## Why other countries mattered

- **France:** essential ally in 1859; garrison in Rome blocked unity until 1870.
- **Austria:** the enemy; its defeats in 1859 and 1866 freed Lombardy and Venetia.
- **Prussia:** victory over Austria (1866) and France (1870) gave Italy Venetia and Rome.
- **Britain:** sympathetic, opposed intervention against Italy.

## Exam traps

- **1861 is not the end.** Venetia came in 1866 and Rome in 1870.
- **Plombières was not the treaty that ended the war.** It came before the war; Villafranca ended it.
- **Garibaldi was not a king's man by origin.** He had been a Mazzinian republican, but accepted Victor Emmanuel.
- **Italy did not win Venetia by its own victories.** It lost at Custoza and Lissa in 1866.
- **"Help or hinder" needs both columns.** Cavour's limited aims are evidence of hindrance.

## Self-test

1. Which parts of Italy did Austria rule directly before 1859? *Lombardy and Venetia.*
2. What kind of Italy did Mazzini want? *A united, democratic republic created by the people.*
3. Why was Pius IX's statement of April 1848 important? *He refused to fight Catholic Austria, ending hopes of a
   papal lead.*
4. Why did Piedmont join the Crimean War? *To win the goodwill of Britain and France and raise the Italian question
   at the peace congress.*
5. What did France gain at Plombières? *The promise of Nice and Savoy.*
6. Name the two victories over Austria in 1859. *Magenta and Solferino.*
7. Give two reasons Napoleon III made peace at Villafranca. *Heavy casualties; Prussian mobilisation; Catholic
   opinion in France; fear of a strong Piedmont (any two).*
8. How did central Italy join Piedmont in 1860? *By plebiscites after its rulers fled.*
9. Where did the Thousand land in Sicily? *Marsala.*
10. Why did Cavour send troops through the Papal States in 1860? *To reach Naples before Garibaldi could attack Rome
    and provoke France.*
11. What happened at Teano? *Garibaldi handed over southern Italy to Victor Emmanuel II.*
12. Why could Italy take Rome in 1870? *France withdrew its garrison to fight Prussia.*
