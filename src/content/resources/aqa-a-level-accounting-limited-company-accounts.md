This guide covers section 3.7, Limited company accounts, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127) specification (version 1.0, 22 December 2016, for A-level exams from June 2019 onwards). The qualification is not tiered. The specification places section 3.7 in the content assessed by Paper 1, a 3-hour written exam, and you must use a calculator in the exam.

Use it with the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-limited-company-accounts-revision-notes/) and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-limited-company-accounts-practice/). It builds directly on [financial statements of sole traders](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-preparation-of-financial-statements-of-sole-traders/), which teaches every year-end adjustment used below, and on [types of business organisation](/resources/a-level-aqa-accounting-types-of-business-organisation/), which explains limited liability, ordinary shares and debentures. ## What section 3.7 covers

| Specification content (3.7) | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Internal financial statements of limited liability companies | Prepare an income statement, a statement of changes in equity and a statement of financial position, for internal use, not for publication |
| Income statement | Show profit from operations, profit for the year before tax and profit for the year after tax |
| Statement of changes in equity | Show opening balances, share issues (including at a premium), dividends paid, profit or loss for the year and closing balances |
| Statement of financial position | Use the sub-headings non-current assets, current assets, current liabilities, non-current liabilities and equity |

The specification notes that **preference shares and general reserves will not be examined**. Statements of cash flow, revaluation, rights and bonus issues and published accounts belong to section 3.16, Accounting for limited companies, not here.

## What changes when the business is a company

The adjustments are the same as for a sole trader: accruals, prepayments, depreciation, irrecoverable debts, the provision for doubtful debts and closing inventory. What changes is who owns the business and how the owners are rewarded.

| Item | Sole trader | Limited company |
|---|---|---|
| Owners' stake | Capital account | Equity: share capital and reserves |
| Money taken by owners | Drawings, deducted from capital | Dividends paid, deducted from retained earnings |
| Pay for the people who run it | Not an expense (the owner's reward is profit) | Directors' remuneration is an expense |
| Tax on profit | Not in the business accounts | Tax is charged in the income statement |
| Long-term borrowing | Bank loan, mortgage | Also debentures, with debenture interest |

**Equity** for a 3.7 question has three parts:

- **Ordinary share capital** -- the nominal (face) value of the shares issued.
- **Share premium** -- the amount received above nominal value when shares are issued.
- **Retained earnings** -- profits after tax kept in the company, less dividends paid, built up over the years.

## Share issues at a premium

When a company issues shares for more than their nominal value, only the nominal value goes to share capital. The excess goes to the share premium account.

```
Dr Bank             (number of shares x issue price)
Cr Share capital    (number of shares x nominal value)
Cr Share premium    (number of shares x premium per share)
```

**Worked example.** Halden Tech Ltd issues 200,000 ordinary shares of £0.50 each at £1.20 per share. All money is received.

- Bank: 200,000 x £1.20 = **£240,000**
- Share capital: 200,000 x £0.50 = **£100,000**
- Share premium: 200,000 x (£1.20 - £0.50) = **£140,000**

Check: 100,000 + 140,000 = 240,000. Share premium is part of equity. It is not income, so it never appears in the income statement.

## The income statement: three levels of profit

The trading section is the same as a sole trader's. Below gross profit, the specification requires three profit lines:

```
Revenue
Less cost of sales
Gross profit
Less operating expenses (wages, directors' remuneration,
  rent, depreciation, irrecoverable debts ...)
Profit from operations
Less finance costs (debenture interest, loan interest)
Profit for the year before tax
Less tax
Profit for the year after tax
```

- **Profit from operations** shows how well the trading activity performed before any financing cost.
- **Finance costs** are the interest on borrowing. Charge the full year's debenture interest, whether or not it has all been paid (accruals). Any unpaid interest is a current liability.
- **Tax** is the company's tax on its profit for the year. Any unpaid amount is a current liability.
- **Dividends are not an expense.** They are a distribution of profit to shareholders, so they go in the statement of changes in equity.

## The statement of changes in equity

This statement explains the movement in each part of equity between the start and the end of the year. Use one column per component and a total column.

```
                          Share      Share   Retained
                        capital    premium   earnings    Total
                              £          £          £        £
Balance at start              x          x          x        x
Share issue                   x          x                   x
Profit for the year                                 x        x
Dividends paid                                     (x)      (x)
Balance at end                x          x          x        x
```

The profit line uses **profit for the year after tax**. If the company makes a loss, it is shown in brackets and reduces retained earnings. Each closing balance becomes a line in the equity section of the statement of financial position.

## The statement of financial position

A common layout lists assets first, then equity and liabilities, with these sub-headings:

- **Non-current assets** at carrying amount (cost less accumulated depreciation).
- **Current assets**: inventory, trade receivables (less any provision), other receivables (prepayments, income due), cash and cash equivalents.
- **Equity**: share capital, share premium, retained earnings.
- **Non-current liabilities**: debentures and loans not due for repayment within 12 months.
- **Current liabilities**: trade payables, other payables (accruals, accrued debenture interest), tax payable, bank overdraft.

Total assets must equal total equity plus total liabilities.

Brackwood Joinery Ltd. Trial balance at 31 December 2025:

| Rent and rates | 24,600 | |
| General expenses | 13,900 | |
| Debenture interest | 1,800 | |
| Premises at cost / provision for depreciation | 240,000 | 28,800 |
| Machinery at cost / provision for depreciation | 96,000 | 38,400 |
| Trade receivables / trade payables | 51,300 | 36,800 |
| Bank | 18,700 | |
| Dividends paid | 15,000 | |
| Ordinary shares of £1 each | | 150,000 |
| Share premium | | 40,000 |
| Retained earnings 1 January 2025 | | 33,200 |
| 6% debentures (repayable 2032) | | 60,000 |
| **Totals** | **999,200** | **999,200** |

Notes at 31 December 2025:

1. Closing inventory is £46,200.
2. Rent of £2,100 is prepaid; wages of £1,600 are accrued.
3. Only half a year's debenture interest has been paid.
4. Depreciate premises at 2% on cost and machinery at 25% reducing balance.
5. Tax on the profit for the year is estimated at £21,000.
6. During the year the company issued 50,000 ordinary shares at £1.60 each. This is already recorded in the trial balance.

**Working**

```

Wages                  98,400 + 1,600 = 100,000
Rent and rates         24,600 - 2,100 = 22,500
Depreciation premises  2% x 240,000 = 4,800

Debenture interest     6% x 60,000 = 3,600; accrued 3,600 - 1,800 = 1,800
Share issue            capital 50,000 x £1 = 50,000;
                       premium 50,000 x £0.60 = 30,000
```

## Common errors

- Showing dividends paid as an expense in the income statement.
- Charging only the debenture interest paid, instead of the full year's interest.
- Putting the whole share issue proceeds in share capital, with nothing in share premium.
- Using profit before tax, not after tax, in the statement of changes in equity.
- Treating directors' remuneration like drawings, so it never reaches the income statement.
- Forgetting tax payable as a current liability.
- Listing debentures repayable in several years' time as a current liability.
- Copying the trial balance share capital into the opening balance when shares were issued during the year.
