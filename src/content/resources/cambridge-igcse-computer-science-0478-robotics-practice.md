## Section A

**1.** Define the term **robotics**. **[2]**

**2.** State **three** characteristics of a robot. **[3]**

**3.** Give **one** example of a robot used in industry. **[1]**

**4.** Give **one** example of a robot used in a home. **[1]**

**5.** State **one** advantage of using a robot in a factory instead of a person. **[1]**

**6.** State **one** disadvantage of using a robot in a factory. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A car company introduces robots to weld car bodies. Explain **two** advantages of using robots. **[4]**

**8.** A robot vacuum cleaner has bump sensors, a microprocessor and motors. Describe how these work together when the cleaner meets a wall. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why a robot needs to be programmable. **[2]**

**10.** A farm uses a drone to monitor its crops. Describe **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using the drone. **[2]**

**11.** A hospital uses a robot to deliver medicines along the corridors. Explain **two** advantages of this to the hospital. **[4]**

**12.** Explain the difference between a robot and an automated system such as a thermostat. **[3]**

**13.** A student says: "A robot is a machine that looks like a person and can think for itself." Explain why this is not a correct description of robots. **[3]**

**14.** Discuss whether a company that makes toys should use robots in its factory. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** A branch of computer science [1] that incorporates the design, construction and operation of robots [1].

**2.** A mechanical structure or framework [1]; electrical components such as sensors, microprocessors and actuators [1]; programmable [1].

**3.** Any one from: a welding robot, a painting robot, an assembly robot, a robot that moves heavy loads [1].

**4.** Any one from: a robot vacuum cleaner, a lawn-mowing robot, a security robot [1].

**5.** Any one from: it can work without breaks; it is accurate and consistent; it can work in dangerous places; it is strong [1].

**6.** Any one from: high cost; job losses; breakdowns; cannot deal with unexpected situations [1].

**7.** A robot repeats the same weld in exactly the same way [1], so the quality is consistent and there are fewer faults [1]. A robot can work all day and night without breaks [1], so more cars can be made [1]. A robot can work in the heat and fumes of welding without risk [1], so workers are safer [1]. Maximum 4 (two developed advantages).

**8.** The bump sensor detects that the cleaner has hit the wall and sends data to the microprocessor [1]. The microprocessor decides that the cleaner must turn and sends a signal [1] to the wheel motors (actuators), which turn the cleaner away from the wall [1].

**9.** A robot only carries out the tasks it is programmed to do [1], so being programmable means the instructions can be changed to make it do a new task or to improve how it works [1].

**10.** Advantage, for example: the drone can cover a large field quickly without a person walking round it [1]. Disadvantage, for example: it is expensive to buy, or it cannot fly in strong wind or heavy rain [1].

**11.** Any two developed: the robot can deliver at any time of the day or night [1], so medicines reach wards quickly [1]; it frees nurses from delivery duties [1], so they have more time for patients [1]; it avoids human error in the route [1], so the right medicine goes to the right ward [1]. Maximum 4.

**12.** A robot has a mechanical structure that carries out physical tasks [1], whereas a thermostat is an automated system that has no robotic body [1]. Both use sensors, a microprocessor and an actuator [1]. Accept: a robot is programmable and can be given new tasks.

**13.** A robot does not have to look like a person; many, such as factory arms and drones, look nothing like people [1]. A robot follows a program written by people [1], so it does not think for itself unless artificial intelligence has been added [1].

**14.** Any five from: advantages such as the robots repeating the same task accurately, reducing waste [1]; working all day and night, increasing output [1]; lower labour costs over time [1]; disadvantages such as high cost of buying and installing the robots [1]; job losses for workers [1]; the cost of repairs and the need for skilled technicians [1]; a conclusion that is supported, for example that it depends on the scale of production [1]. Maximum 5.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: defining robotics as "robots" with no reference to design, construction and operation.
- Question 2: describing what robots do instead of what they have.
- Question 7: giving general advantages with no link to welding or cars.
- Question 8: leaving out the microprocessor's decision.
- Question 12: saying that a thermostat is not controlled by a program.
- Question 13: agreeing that robots think for themselves.
- Question 14: listing points with no conclusion.
