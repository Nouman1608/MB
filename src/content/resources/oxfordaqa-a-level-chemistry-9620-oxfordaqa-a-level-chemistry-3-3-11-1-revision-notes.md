These notes condense **3.3.11.1 Preparation** of amines from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 4 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For explanations and worked examples read the [amine preparation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-1/); then try the [amine preparation practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-1-practice/).

## The subtopic in four lines

1. Primary aliphatic amines: halogenoalkane + excess ammonia (same chain length, mixture of amines).
2. Primary aliphatic amines: halogenoalkane → nitrile → reduce (chain one carbon longer, primary amine only).
3. Aromatic amines: nitrate an arene, then reduce the nitro group with Sn and conc. HCl, then add NaOH.
4. Aromatic amines made this way are used to manufacture dyes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Primary amine | N bonded to one carbon: R–NH₂ |
| Aliphatic amine | N bonded to a chain or non-aromatic ring carbon |
| Aromatic amine | N bonded directly to a benzene ring carbon |
| Nitrile | R–C≡N; the name counts the C≡N carbon |
| Nitro compound | Contains –NO₂ bonded to carbon |
| Reduction (organic) | Gain of hydrogen or loss of oxygen; written with [H] |
| Sealed tube | Stops volatile ammonia escaping while heating |
| Ethanolic ammonia | Ammonia dissolved in ethanol, which also dissolves the halogenoalkane |

## Route summaries

**Route 1: ammonia**

- Reagents and conditions: excess NH₃ in ethanol, heat in a sealed tube.
- Equation pattern: RX + 2NH₃ → RNH₂ + NH₄X.
- Mechanism: nucleophilic substitution; second NH₃ removes H⁺.
- Drawback: RNH₂ is also a nucleophile, so R₂NH, R₃N and R₄N⁺X⁻ form too.

**Route 2: nitrile**

- Step 1: KCN in aqueous ethanol, reflux: RX + CN⁻ → RCN + X⁻.
- Step 2: H₂ with Ni catalyst, or LiAlH₄ in dry ether: RCN + 4[H] → RCH₂NH₂ (or RCN + 2H₂).
- Advantages: only the primary amine; adds one carbon.
- Drawback: KCN is highly toxic.

**Route 3: nitro**

- Step 1: conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄ (nitration).
- Step 2: Sn + conc. HCl, reflux, then NaOH(aq): ArNO₂ + 6[H] → ArNH₂ + 2H₂O.
- In acid the amine is present as ArNH₃⁺; NaOH frees it.
- Use: dyes.

## Counting hydrogen

| Group reduced | [H] needed | H₂ needed | Other product |
|---|---|---|---|
| –C≡N → –CH₂NH₂ | 4 | 2 | none |
| –NO₂ → –NH₂ | 6 | 3 | 2H₂O |

Mr check: each –NO₂ → –NH₂ lowers Mr by 30.0; each –CN → –CH₂NH₂ raises Mr by 4.0.

## Which route?

| Clue in the question | Route |
|---|---|
| Target has the same carbon count as the halogenoalkane | Ammonia (accept the mixture) |
| Target has one more carbon than the halogenoalkane | Nitrile |
| "Pure primary amine" wanted from a halogenoalkane | Nitrile |
| N bonded to a benzene ring | Nitration, then Sn/HCl, then NaOH |
| –CH₂NH₂ on a ring (benzylamine type) | Not an aromatic amine; use an aliphatic route |

## Worked reminders

- **Hydrogen volume.** 0.0400 mol of propanenitrile needs 0.0800 mol of H₂. At 298 K and 100 kPa, V = 0.0800 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 100 000 = 1.98 × 10⁻³ m³ = 1.98 dm³.
- **Two-step yield.** Halogenoalkane → nitrile at 80% and nitrile → amine at 75%: overall 0.80 × 0.75 = 0.60, so 60%.
- **Nitro count from Mr.** A dinitromethylbenzene, C₇H₆N₂O₄ (Mr 182.0), gives a diamine, C₇H₁₀N₂ (Mr 122.0): the drop of 60.0 confirms two –NO₂ groups were reduced.
- **Salt before NaOH.** Reducing nitrobenzene with Sn and HCl gives C₆H₅NH₃⁺; one OH⁻ per –NH₃⁺ group releases the amine.

## Names to practise

| Starting material | Route | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 1-Bromopropane | Excess NH₃ | propylamine (propan-1-amine) |
| 2-Bromopropane | Excess NH₃ | propan-2-amine |
| 1-Bromobutane | KCN, then reduce | pentan-1-amine via pentanenitrile |
| Butanenitrile | Reduce | butan-1-amine |
| Nitrobenzene | Sn/HCl, NaOH | phenylamine |
| 1,3-Dinitrobenzene | Sn/HCl, NaOH | benzene-1,3-diamine (12[H]) |

## Exam traps

- One NH₃ in the equation: use two and give the ammonium salt.
- "Excess ammonia stops further substitution": it only reduces it.
- Butanenitrile has four carbons, not five.
- 2[H] for a nitrile or 4[H] for a nitro group: wrong counts.
- No NaOH step after Sn/HCl.
- LiAlH₄ in water: it must be used in dry ether.
- Calling C₆H₅CH₂NH₂ an aromatic amine.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Give the reagent and conditions for making ethylamine from bromoethane in one step.
2. Write the equation for question 1.
3. Why is ammonia used in excess?
4. Why is the reaction done in a sealed tube?
5. Name the nitrile formed from 1-bromobutane and KCN.
6. Give one reducing agent for a nitrile.
7. Write an equation, using [H], for reducing propanenitrile.
8. State two advantages of the nitrile route.
9. Name the reagents used to reduce nitrobenzene in the laboratory.
10. Why is sodium hydroxide added after the reduction?
11. How many [H] are needed to reduce one nitro group?
12. Give one use of aromatic amines named in the specification.

### Answers

1. Excess ammonia in ethanol, heated in a sealed tube.
2. CH₃CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br.
3. To make it more likely that the halogenoalkane reacts with ammonia rather than with the amine formed, so the primary amine is the main product.
4. Ammonia is volatile and would escape on heating.
5. Pentanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN.
6. Hydrogen with a nickel catalyst (or LiAlH₄ in dry ether).
7. CH₃CH₂CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂.
8. Only the primary amine forms; the chain is lengthened by one carbon.
9. Tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid, heated under reflux.
10. The amine is protonated in the acid; OH⁻ removes H⁺ to release the free amine.
11. Six.
12. Manufacture of dyes.
