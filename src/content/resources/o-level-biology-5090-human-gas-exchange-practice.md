## Questions

**1.** State the approximate percentages of nitrogen and of carbon dioxide in atmospheric air. **[2]**

**2.** Identify each structure of the gas exchange system from its description.

**(a)** A wider region at the top of the windpipe, containing the vocal cords. **[1]**
**(b)** A tube in the neck, held open by rings of cartilage. **[1]**
**(c)** Narrow, branching tubes inside each lung. **[1]**
**(d)** Tiny air sacs, each covered by a network of blood vessels. **[1]**

**3.** Describe two features of human gas exchange surfaces, and explain how each one increases the rate of gas exchange. **[4]**

**4.** A person takes in 500 cm³ of air in each breath. Inspired air contains 21% oxygen and expired air contains 16% oxygen.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen in one inspired breath. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of oxygen absorbed from one breath, assuming 500 cm³ is also breathed out. **[2]**
**(c)** The person breathes 14 times per minute. Calculate the volume of oxygen absorbed per minute. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why expired air contains less oxygen than inspired air. **[2]**

**5.** A student compares inspired and expired air. Two test-tubes each contain 10 cm³ of hydrogencarbonate indicator. Inspired air is drawn through tube A and expired air is breathed out through tube B.

**(a)** State the colour change in the indicator when the carbon dioxide concentration increases. **[1]**
**(b)** Tube B changed colour after 25 s and tube A after 350 s. Calculate how many times faster tube B changed than tube A. **[2]**
**(c)** State two variables, other than the volume of indicator, that should be kept the same. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe a simple test to show that expired air contains more water vapour than inspired air. **[1]**
**(e)** State one safety precaution for this investigation. **[1]**

**6.** Explain how the ribs, intercostal muscles and diaphragm cause air to enter the lungs. **[5]**

**7.** During a hard cough, air is forced out of the lungs.

**(a)** Name the muscles that contract to pull the ribs down and in. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the external and internal intercostal muscles are described as antagonistic. **[2]**

**8.** A student's resting breathing rate was 14 breaths per minute. She ran for four minutes, then her breathing rate was measured every minute.

| Time after running / min | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Breathing rate / breaths per minute | 38 | 31 | 24 | 18 | 15 | 14 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in breathing rate from rest to time 0. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the change in breathing rate after running. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why breathing rate increased during running. **[4]**
**(d)** Suggest how the depth of breathing could be measured. **[1]**

**9.** At rest a man breathes 12 times per minute with 0.5 dm³ per breath. During cycling he breathes 30 times per minute with 2.4 dm³ per breath. Calculate the volume of air breathed per minute in each case, and how many times larger it is during cycling. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how goblet cells, mucus and ciliated cells protect the gas exchange system. **[4]**

**11.** This question is about the alveoli.

**(a)** The barrier between alveolar air and the blood in a capillary is 1.5 µm thick. Calculate its length, in mm, on a photomicrograph at ×2000. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how oxygen moves from the air in an alveolus into a red blood cell. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why carbon dioxide diffuses in the opposite direction. **[2]**
**(d)** A student wants to show the composition of atmospheric air as a pie chart. Calculate the angle for carbon dioxide and suggest why a pie chart shows it poorly. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Nitrogen about **78%** [1]; carbon dioxide about **0.04%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The two gases are separate points; writing 4% for carbon dioxide confuses atmospheric air with expired air and loses that mark.

**2. (a)** **Larynx** [1]
**(b)** **Trachea** [1]
**(c)** **Bronchioles** [1]
**(d)** **Alveoli** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Bronchi" and "bronchioles" are different structures, so a name with the wrong ending is a wrong answer, not a spelling slip; learn the endings exactly.

**3.** Any two pairs. Large surface area [1], so more gas can diffuse at the same time [1]. Thin surface [1], so the diffusion distance is short [1]. Also accept: good blood supply, which keeps a steep concentration gradient by carrying oxygen away; good air supply, which keeps oxygen concentration high in the alveoli. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A feature listed with no explanation usually gains only part of the credit for that pair; "thin, so short diffusion distance" is safer than "thin, so diffusion is quicker".

**4. (a)** 0.21 × 500 = **105 cm³** [1]
**(b)** Oxygen breathed out = 0.16 × 500 = 80 cm³ [1]; absorbed = 105 − 80 = **25 cm³** [1]
**(c)** 25 × 14 = **350 cm³ per minute** [1]
**(d)** Oxygen diffuses from the alveoli into the blood [1] and is used by cells in aerobic respiration [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Error carried forward is often allowed in linked calculation parts, so show the method in (c) even if you doubt (b); give a unit with every volume.

**5. (a)** From **red/orange to yellow** [1]
**(b)** 350 ÷ 25 [1] = **14 times** [1]
**(c)** Any two [2]: same concentration of indicator; same starting temperature; same person; same breathing pattern or rate; same size of tube.
**(d)** Breathe onto a cold, dry mirror and look for condensation [1].
**(e)** Any one [1]: wear eye protection; never suck liquid into the mouthpiece; use a clean mouthpiece for each person.
**[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Vague variables such as "same conditions" or "same amount" earn nothing; name the quantity (volume, concentration, temperature).

**6.** External intercostal muscles contract [1]; ribs move up and out [1]. Diaphragm contracts and flattens [1]. Volume of the thorax increases [1], so pressure inside falls below atmospheric pressure and air moves into the lungs [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Volume and pressure are separate points here, and an answer that says the lungs "suck in" air without a pressure change cannot earn the last mark.

**7. (a)** **Internal intercostal muscles** [1]
**(b)** They pull the ribs in opposite directions [1]; when one set contracts the other relaxes [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying the muscles "work against each other" is not enough for both marks; describe the opposite movements and the contract-relax pairing.

**8. (a)** (38 − 14) ÷ 14 × 100 [1] = **171.4%** [1]
**(b)** The rate falls after running [1], reaching the resting rate of 14 by 5 minutes [1].
**(c)** Muscles contract more, so respiration increases [1]. More carbon dioxide is produced, so its concentration in the blood rises [1]. The brain detects this rise [1] and increases the rate of breathing, supplying more oxygen and removing carbon dioxide faster [1].
**(d)** Use a spirometer (or measure chest expansion with a tape measure) [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) quoting data with units (for example "returns to 14 breaths per minute after 5 minutes") secures the second mark; describing only "it decreases" earns one.

**9.** Rest: 12 × 0.5 = **6.0 dm³ per minute** [1]. Cycling: 30 × 2.4 = **72 dm³ per minute** [1]. 72 ÷ 6.0 = **12 times** larger [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The two volumes are credited separately from the ratio, so show both even though the question ends with the comparison.

**10.** Goblet cells secrete mucus [1]. Mucus traps pathogens and particles [1]. Cilia on ciliated cells beat [1] and move the mucus up to the throat, away from the alveoli, where it is swallowed [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving the trapping role to the cilia, or saying the cilia kill bacteria, contradicts the correct points and loses those marks.

**11. (a)** Image size = actual size × magnification = 1.5 × 2000 = 3000 µm [1] = **3.0 mm** [1]
**(b)** Oxygen dissolves in the moist lining of the alveolus [1]. It diffuses down a concentration gradient [1] across the thin alveolar wall and capillary wall into the blood and into the red blood cell [1].
**(c)** The concentration of carbon dioxide in the blood is higher than in the alveolar air [1], because cells produce it in respiration; it diffuses from higher to lower concentration [1].
**(d)** 0.04 ÷ 100 × 360 = **0.144°** [1]; the sector is far too small to see or measure [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the unit conversion is its own step; an answer of "3000 mm" shows the right method but the wrong unit, so it cannot gain full credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 4% instead of 0.04% for carbon dioxide in atmospheric air.
- Naming a feature of the alveoli without linking it to diffusion distance, surface area or concentration gradient.
- Reversing the volume-pressure link when explaining breathing in.
- Saying the internal intercostals contract during quiet breathing in.
- Leaving out units on volumes or rates.
- Forgetting to convert µm to mm in magnification answers.
- Explaining faster breathing without carbon dioxide and the brain.
- Saying the cilia trap particles or move mucus down into the lungs.
- Naming vague controlled variables in the inspired-air investigation.
