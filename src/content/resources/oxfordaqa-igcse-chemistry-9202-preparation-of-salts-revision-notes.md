## The decision in three questions

1. **Is the salt soluble?** No → precipitation (mix two solutions). Yes → go on.
2. **Can the metal go straight into the acid?** Moderately reactive (Mg, Zn, Fe) → yes, metal in excess. Too reactive (Na, K, Li) or too unreactive (Cu, Ag) → no.
3. **Is the metal's oxide or hydroxide insoluble?** Yes → add it in excess and filter. No (soluble hydroxide, or ammonia for ammonium salts) → indicator method.

## Solubility facts to recall

| Always soluble | Mostly soluble, except | Mostly insoluble, except |
|---|---|---|
| Sodium, potassium, ammonium salts | Chlorides: not silver, lead(II) | Carbonates: sodium, potassium, ammonium are soluble |
| Nitrates | Sulfates: not barium, lead(II); calcium only slightly | Hydroxides: sodium, potassium, ammonium soluble; calcium slightly |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Soluble salt | A salt that dissolves in water; made in solution then crystallised |
| Insoluble salt | A salt that does not dissolve; made as a precipitate |
| Excess | More than enough of a reactant, so the other is used up completely |
| Filtrate | The liquid that passes through filter paper |
| Residue | The solid left on the filter paper |
| Saturated solution | A solution holding as much dissolved solute as it can at that temperature |
| Crystallisation | Forming crystals as a saturated solution cools |
| Precipitate | Insoluble solid formed when two solutions react |
| Effluent | Waste water released from industry or homes |

## Three soluble-salt routes compared

| Route | Example | Equation | Extra step |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acid + metal | Iron(II) chloride from iron | Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂ | Hydrogen given off; work away from flames |
| Acid + insoluble base | Copper(II) sulfate from copper(II) oxide | CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O | Warm acid speeds up reaction |
| Acid + alkali | Sodium sulfate from sodium hydroxide | 2NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O | Repeat without indicator |
| Ammonia + acid | Ammonium nitrate | NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ | Indicator method; no water formed |

An insoluble carbonate behaves like an insoluble base, but also gives carbon dioxide: ZnCO₃ + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂.

## Each step and its purpose

- **Excess solid:** all the acid reacts, so the salt is not contaminated with acid.
- **Filter (soluble salt):** remove unreacted solid; keep the **filtrate**.
- **Evaporate some water:** make the solution saturated.
- **Cool:** salt is less soluble when cold, so crystals form.
- **Dry between filter papers or in a warm place:** remove surface water without decomposing the salt.
- **Filter (insoluble salt):** keep the **residue**.
- **Wash precipitate with distilled water:** remove the soluble salt formed alongside it.

## Insoluble salt reagent pairs

| Salt | Solution supplying cation | Solution supplying anion |
|---|---|---|
| Barium sulfate | barium nitrate or chloride | sodium sulfate |
| Silver bromide | silver nitrate | potassium bromide |
| Copper(II) carbonate | copper(II) sulfate | sodium carbonate |
| Lead(II) iodide | lead(II) nitrate | potassium iodide |
| Calcium carbonate | calcium nitrate | sodium carbonate |

## Precipitation and water

- Drinking water: calcium ions (hardness) removed as calcium carbonate.
- Effluent: dissolved metal ions removed as insoluble hydroxides; phosphate removed as an insoluble phosphate.
- The solid is then settled out or filtered off.

## Exam traps

- Proposing copper + acid or sodium + acid.
- Forgetting the word **excess**, or giving no reason for it.
- Heating the solution to dryness.
- Keeping the wrong part after filtering.
- Writing "mix barium and sulfate" instead of naming two soluble compounds.
- Washing a precipitate with tap water.
- Leaving indicator in the final salt solution.

## Quick self-test

1. Is lead(II) nitrate soluble or insoluble?
2. Suggest a starting material and acid to make magnesium chloride.
3. Why can copper(II) nitrate not be made from copper and dilute nitric acid by the metal route?
4. Why is the insoluble base added in excess?
5. After filtering in the insoluble-base method, which part contains the salt?
6. How do you know a solution is saturated during evaporation?
7. Why is the solution left to cool rather than evaporated to dryness?
8. Name two solutions that react to form barium sulfate.
9. Why is a precipitate washed with distilled water?
10. Which method makes ammonium sulfate, and what two reagents are used?
11. Give one use of precipitation in treating effluent.
12. Write a balanced equation for making zinc chloride from zinc carbonate.

### Answers

1. Soluble (all nitrates are soluble).
2. Magnesium ribbon (or magnesium oxide) in excess with hydrochloric acid.
3. Copper is too unreactive to give hydrogen with dilute acids; use copper(II) oxide instead.
4. To make sure all the acid reacts.
5. The filtrate.
6. Crystals form on a glass rod dipped into the solution and removed.
7. Many salts decompose or lose water of crystallisation when heated to dryness; cooling gives crystals.
8. Barium nitrate (or barium chloride) solution and sodium sulfate solution.
9. To remove the soluble salt formed with it, without adding other dissolved ions.
10. The indicator (titration) method, using ammonia solution and sulfuric acid.
11. Removing dissolved metal ions as insoluble hydroxides (or phosphate as an insoluble phosphate).
12. ZnCO₃ + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂
