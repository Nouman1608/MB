## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a short-term source of finance named in the specification? **[1]**

A: Share capital

B: Retained profit

C: Trade credit

D: Venture capital

**2.** Which **one** of the following sources is **not** available to a business that has just started trading? **[1]**

A: Personal savings

B: Retained profit

C: Crowd funding

D: A bank loan

**3.** Define the term retained profit. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** long-term sources of finance named in the specification. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** advantage to a small business of using an overdraft. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** disadvantage of a bank loan for a new business. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why a business owner might choose crowd funding. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**8.** *Saltings Bakery* has traded for five years. It borrows £24,000 to buy a new oven and will repay £28,800 in total over 48 months.

**(a)** Calculate the interest on the loan as a percentage. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly repayment. **[1]**

**(c)** Outline **one** reason why a loan is more suitable than an overdraft for buying the oven. **[2]**

**9.** *Tinker's Yard* is a start-up making wooden toys. Its owner launched a crowd funding campaign with a target of £15,000 and has received pledges of £11,400.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the target that has been pledged. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how much more must be pledged to reach the target. **[1]**

**10.** *Ravensworth Escape Rooms* is a new business that needs £40,000 to fit out its premises. The owner, Leila, has no savings to spare. Her forecast profit in the first year is £20,000. She is considering two sources of finance.

Option 1: a bank loan of £40,000, repaying £50,000 in total over five years in equal monthly payments.

Option 2: venture capital of £40,000 from an investor, in return for 40% of the business.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly loan repayment in Option 1, to the nearest penny. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the investor's share of the first year's forecast profit in Option 2. **[1]**

**(c)** Justify which option Leila should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** C: Trade credit [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B: Retained profit [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Retained profit needs past profits, which a new business has not yet made.

**3.** Profit that is kept in the business to be used, rather than paid out to the owners [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: personal savings [1]; venture capital [1]; share capital; loans; retained profit; crowd funding. **[2]**

**5.** An overdraft is flexible, because the business only borrows when its bank balance falls below zero [1]. Interest is charged only on the amount overdrawn and only while it is used [1], so it is a low-cost way to cover a short cash-flow gap, such as waiting for customers to pay [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A loan must be repaid in fixed instalments with interest [1]. A new business's sales are uncertain, so in a poor month it may not have enough cash to make the repayment [1], which could lead to cash-flow problems or, at worst, insolvency [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Crowd funding raises money from many people who like the idea [1], so a business can obtain finance without a bank loan or giving a large share to one investor [1]. A successful campaign also shows there is demand for the product, which reduces the risk of launching it [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** (£28,800 − £24,000) ÷ £24,000 × 100 [1] = **20%** [1].

**(b)** £28,800 ÷ 48 = **£600** a month [1].

**(c)** The oven will be used for many years, so it should be paid for with long-term finance [1]. An overdraft is meant for short gaps, can be withdrawn by the bank and often charges a higher rate, whereas a loan's fixed repayments over 48 months spread the cost over the oven's working life [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** £11,400 ÷ £15,000 × 100 = **76%** [1].

**(b)** £15,000 − £11,400 = **£3,600** [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** £50,000 ÷ 60 = **£833.33** a month [1].

**(b)** 40% × £20,000 = **£8,000** [1].

**(c)** Option 1: the loan costs £10,000 in interest, which is 25% of the amount borrowed [1]. Leila keeps full ownership and control, and all profit after the repayments is hers [1]. However, she must pay £833.33 every month, about £10,000 a year, which is half of her forecast first-year profit [1], and as a start-up her forecast is uncertain, so a slow first year could leave her unable to repay [1].

Option 2: there are no repayments, which protects cash flow while the business builds up customers [1]. The investor may also bring experience and contacts [1]. However, Leila gives up 40% of the business, so £8,000 of first-year profit goes to the investor [1], and this continues every year, possibly for much larger sums if the business grows, and she loses some control over decisions [1].

Judgement: Leila should choose Option 2, because as a new business with no savings to fall back on, avoiding £833.33 of monthly repayments reduces the risk of failure in the first year; if she were confident that the forecast profit was reliable, the loan would be cheaper in the long run [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Either option can be recommended. The judgement is supported when it uses the start-up's uncertain forecast and the monthly repayment figure, and states what would change the decision.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling trade credit or an overdraft long-term.
- Saying a start-up can use retained profit.
- Dividing interest by the total repayment instead of the amount borrowed.
- Forgetting that venture capital costs a share of every future year's profit.
- Recommending a source without linking it to the business's situation.
