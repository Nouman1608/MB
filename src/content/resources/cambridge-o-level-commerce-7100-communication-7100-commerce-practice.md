## Section A

**1.** Identify the **four** types of communication named in the syllabus. **[4]**

**2.** State the type or types of communication shown by: (a) a poster; (b) a videoconference. **[2]**

**3.** Define *chatbot*. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** advantages of email for a business. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** disadvantages of using a letter to contact a supplier. **[2]**

**6.** Identify the factor affecting the choice of communication that is most important in each case: (a) a customer's bank details must be sent; (b) a fire alarm system has failed and all staff must be told now; (c) the business is in an area with frequent internet failures. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of a retailer using web-based chat, including chatbots, on its website. **[6]**

**8.** A company's head office is in one country and its factory is in another. Explain why videoconferencing may be more appropriate than face-to-face meetings for monthly management meetings. **[4]**

**9.** Compare telephone and email as methods for a business to place an order with a supplier. **[4]**

**10.** A furniture retailer receives a serious complaint from a customer whose sofa was delivered damaged. Recommend the most appropriate method for the manager to respond, justifying your choice using **two** factors. **[5]**

**11.** Green Valley Farms is a cooperative in a remote area with weak mobile signal. It needs to tell its 150 members about the date of a general meeting next month. Explain which method of communication would be most appropriate. **[4]**

**12.** A clothing shop wants to tell customers about a three-day sale starting next week. It is considering social media and posters. Discuss which method it should use. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Written [1]; oral [1]; electronic [1]; visual [1].

**2.** (a) Visual (usually also written) [1]; (b) oral, visual and electronic [1].

**3.** A computer program on a website or app that answers typed questions automatically [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: fast delivery; low cost per message; documents can be attached; provides a written record; can be sent to many people at once; can be read and answered at a convenient time [1] [1].

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: slow to arrive; cost of printing and postage; no instant reply; may be lost in the post [1] [1].

**6.** (a) Security [1]; (b) urgency [1]; (c) availability of networks [1].

**7.** **Advantages:** customers get instant answers while they browse [1], so they are more likely to complete a purchase instead of leaving the site [1]; chatbots answer routine questions, such as delivery times, at any hour without staff [1], cutting customer-service wage costs [1]. **Disadvantage:** chatbots cannot deal well with complex or unusual problems [1], so frustrated customers may leave or complain if they cannot reach a person [1].

**8.** **Distance**: the managers are in different countries, so face-to-face meetings need flights and hotels [1]; videoconferencing removes this travel time and cost every month [1]. Participants can still see each other and share documents, such as production reports, on screen [1], so most of the benefits of meeting in person are kept for a routine meeting [1].

**9.** Telephone gives an immediate reply, so the buyer can check availability at once, while email may take longer to be answered [1]. Email provides a written record of quantities and prices, while a telephone order has no record unless confirmed in writing [1]. Both can link buyer and supplier across distance at low cost [1]. Overall, email suits routine orders where accuracy matters; telephone suits urgent orders, ideally confirmed by email [1].

**10.** Recommendation: a telephone call or face-to-face visit, followed by a letter or email confirming what will be done [1]. **Importance of the message**: a serious complaint risks losing the customer and harming the shop's reputation, so personal contact shows the business takes it seriously [1], and the written confirmation gives a record of the agreed replacement or refund [1]. **Urgency**: the customer wants a quick answer [1], and a call gives an immediate response, whereas a letter alone would take days [1].

**11.** A letter (or printed notice) sent to each member [1]. **Availability of networks**: weak mobile signal means emails, messages or social media posts may not reach members [1]. **Urgency** is low because the meeting is next month, so the slower speed of post is acceptable [1], and the letter gives members a permanent record of the date and agenda [1].

**12.** **Social media for:** reaches many customers quickly at low cost [1]; posts can include photos of sale items and be shared by followers [1]; customers can ask questions and the shop can reply [1]. **Social media against:** only reaches people who follow the shop or see shared posts [1]; posts are quickly lost among many others [1]. **Posters for:** seen by everyone passing the shop, including people who do not use social media [1]. **Posters against:** one-way and limited detail, reaching only people near the shop [1]. **Judgement:** the shop should use both, or social media mainly, because it reaches more customers before the sale starts, with posters in the window to catch passing trade [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: giving a method ("email") instead of a type.
- Question 7: stating that chatbots can solve any problem.
- Question 9: writing about each method separately without comparing them.
- Question 10: naming a method but not linking it to two factors in the case.
- Question 11: recommending email or social media despite the weak signal.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is there one right answer to Question 10?** No. A justified choice using two factors in the case scores full marks; a confirming written record strengthens most answers.

**Can posters be electronic?** Digital signs exist, but the syllabus lists posters as a separate method; treat them as visual communication at a fixed place.
