These questions cover section **3.3.5 Alcohols** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification
(7404/7405), version 1.2, July 2026, for AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards: 3.3.5.1 to 3.3.5.3 and
Required practical 5. All of it is AS content, so nothing here is A-level only. The topic is examined on
Paper 2 at AS and A-level, and A-level Paper 3 can test any content.

Learn the ideas first in the [alcohols study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alcohols/) and the
[alcohols revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-alcohols-revision-notes/). The course hub is
[AQA A-Level Chemistry](/boards/aqa/a-level/chemistry/), and the
[printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/chemistry/) lists every specification point. Use
Ar values C = 12.0, H = 1.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** Classify each alcohol as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** pentan-2-ol **[1]**
**(b)** 3-methylpentan-3-ol **[1]**
**(c)** 2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol **[1]**

**2.** Explain the meaning of the term *biofuel*, and state why ethanol made by hydrating ethene from crude oil is not a biofuel. **[2]**

**3.** Ethanol is produced industrially by fermentation.

**(a)** Write an equation for the fermentation of glucose. **[1]**
**(b)** Justify the temperature used. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why air is kept out of the fermentation vessel. **[1]**
**(d)** State how the ethanol is separated from the mixture. **[1]**

**4.** A brewer wants 115 g of ethanol by fermentation.

**(a)** Calculate the minimum mass of glucose needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide released. **[1]**

**5.** Ethanol from sugar cane is sometimes described as carbon neutral.

**(a)** Write equations for photosynthesis, fermentation and the complete combustion of ethanol. **[3]**
**(b)** Use your equations to show why the fuel can be described as carbon neutral. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why the statement is not valid. **[2]**

**6.** 2-methylpropene reacts with steam in the presence of an acid catalyst.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism for the formation of the major product. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the minor product and explain why less of it forms. **[2]**

**7.** Propan-1-ol is oxidised by acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

**(a)** Write an equation, using [O], for the formation of propanal. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation, using [O], for the formation of propanoic acid from propan-1-ol. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the method is chosen to obtain propanal rather than propanoic acid. **[3]**
**(d)** State the colour change in the reaction mixture. **[1]**

**8.** Describe a test to distinguish butanal from butanone. Give the reagent, the condition and what is seen with each compound, and write an equation, using [O], for the reaction of butanal. **[4]**

**9.** Pentan-3-ol is heated with an acid catalyst.

**(a)** Name a suitable acid catalyst. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline the mechanism for the elimination of water from pentan-3-ol. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the products and explain why there is more than one. **[2]**

**10.** In Required practical 5, a student makes cyclohexene from cyclohexanol and purifies it.

**(a)** The distillate is shaken with sodium carbonate solution in a separating funnel. Explain why, and why the tap must be opened often. **[2]**
**(b)** Name a drying agent and state how you can tell the cyclohexene is dry. **[2]**
**(c)** 12.5 g of cyclohexanol (Mr 100.0) gives 7.38 g of cyclohexene (Mr 82.0). Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**11.** P, Q and R are isomers with formula C₄H₁₀O. Each is warmed with acidified potassium dichromate(VI).

- P turns the mixture green. When the product is distilled off, it gives a silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Elimination of P gives but-1-ene only.
- Q turns the mixture green. The product does not react with Tollens' reagent. Elimination of Q gives three alkenes.
- R leaves the mixture orange. Elimination of R gives 2-methylpropene only.

**(a)** Identify P, Q and R, giving one reason for each. **[6]**
**(b)** The equation for the oxidation of Q is Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 8H⁺ + 3C₄H₁₀O → 2Cr³⁺ + 3C₄H₈O + 7H₂O. Calculate the volume of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ potassium dichromate(VI) that reacts with 0.370 g of Q. **[3]**

**12.** A company plans to make poly(ethene) from sugar cane instead of crude oil.

**(a)** Outline the three stages from glucose to poly(ethene), giving the reagent or condition for each stage. **[3]**
**(b)** Discuss the environmental and ethical issues the company should consider. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Secondary** [1]
**(b)** **Tertiary** [1]
**(c)** **Primary**: the C–OH carbon is joined to one other carbon [1]
*Examiner insight:* Classification depends only on the carbon bonded to –OH; counting all branches in the molecule gives the wrong class.

**2.** A biofuel is made from biological material (biomass) that has been grown recently and can be regrown [1]. Ethene from crude oil comes from a finite fossil source, not recent biomass [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Renewable" alone may not score; tie the fuel to recently grown biological material.

**3. (a)** **C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂** [1]
**(b)** About 35 °C: a lower temperature gives a slow rate [1]; a higher temperature denatures the enzymes in yeast [1]
**(c)** Oxygen would oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid (or yeast would respire aerobically) [1]
**(d)** **Fractional distillation** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b) both sides of the compromise are needed; "optimum temperature for yeast" alone earns at most one mark.

**4. (a)** n(ethanol) = 115 ÷ 46.0 = 2.50 mol, so n(glucose) = 1.25 mol [1]; mass = 1.25 × 180.0 = **225 g** [1]
**(b)** n(CO₂) = 2.50 mol; mass = 2.50 × 44.0 = **110 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Set out each mole step, including the 1 : 2 ratio, so the method in (b) is clear even if (a) contains a slip.

**5. (a)** 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ [1]; C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ [1]; C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O [1]
**(b)** 6 mol CO₂ absorbed; 2 mol from fermentation and 4 mol from burning 2 mol ethanol are released, so no net CO₂ [1]
**(c)** Any two: fossil fuels used for farm machinery [1]; making fertiliser [1]; powering distillation; transport. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the combustion equation must be doubled (or the counting must allow for 2 mol ethanol); comparing 6 with 4 loses the mark.

**6. (a)** Arrow from the C=C bond to H⁺ (or to H of H₃O⁺) [1]; tertiary carbocation (CH₃)₃C⁺ drawn [1]; arrow from a lone pair on the O of H₂O to C⁺ [1]; loss of H⁺ from (CH₃)₃C–OH₂⁺ to give **2-methylpropan-2-ol** [1]
**(b)** **2-methylpropan-1-ol** [1]; it forms through a primary carbocation, which is less stable than the tertiary one [1]
*Examiner insight:* Arrows must start at a bond or a lone pair; an arrow starting from the H⁺ ion or from a charge is not credited.

**7. (a)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O** [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃CH₂COOH + H₂O** [1]
**(c)** Use excess propan-1-ol (limited oxidising agent) with gentle heat [1]; distil the propanal off as it forms [1]; propanal has a lower boiling point (no hydrogen bonding between its molecules), so it leaves before it is oxidised to the acid [1]
**(d)** **Orange to green** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Distil" without the reason it prevents further oxidation usually scores only one of the three marks in (c).

**8.** Tollens' reagent, warmed in a water bath [1]; butanal gives a silver mirror [1]; butanone shows no change [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂CHO + [O] → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH [1]. (Fehling's: butanal gives a red precipitate, butanone stays blue.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An observation must be given for both compounds; "no reaction" for butanone needs to be stated, not implied.

**9. (a)** Concentrated sulfuric acid or concentrated phosphoric acid [1]
**(b)** Lone pair on O attacks H⁺ to give the protonated alcohol [1]; C–O bond breaks and water leaves, giving the carbocation on C3 [1]; C–H bond electrons on C2 form C=C and H⁺ is lost [1]
**(c)** **(E)-pent-2-ene and (Z)-pent-2-ene** [1]; C2 and C4 are equivalent so only pent-2-ene forms, but rotation about C=C is restricted and each carbon of the double bond has two different groups [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Pentene" without the position and E/Z labels is not a name and does not score.

**10. (a)** To neutralise (remove) acid carried over in the distillate [1]; carbon dioxide forms and pressure builds up [1]
**(b)** Anhydrous calcium chloride or magnesium sulfate [1]; the liquid turns from cloudy to clear [1]
**(c)** n(cyclohexanol) = 12.5 ÷ 100.0 = 0.125 mol [1]; n(cyclohexene) = 7.38 ÷ 82.0 = 0.0900 mol [1]; yield = 0.0900 ÷ 0.125 × 100 = **72.0 %** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Comparing masses (7.38 ÷ 12.5) instead of moles gives 59.0 % and loses the method marks; always convert both to moles.

**11. (a)** P is **butan-1-ol** [1]: oxidised to an aldehyde, so primary, and an unbranched chain gives but-1-ene [1]. Q is **butan-2-ol** [1]: oxidised to a ketone, so secondary; three alkenes are but-1-ene and E/Z but-2-ene [1]. R is **2-methylpropan-2-ol** [1]: not oxidised, so tertiary, and it gives 2-methylpropene [1]
**(b)** n(Q) = 0.370 ÷ 74.0 = 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; n(Cr₂O₇²⁻) = 5.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 3 = 1.667 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; volume = 1.667 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0500 × 1000 = **33.3 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each identification needs evidence from the data; a correct name with no reason gets only the name mark.

**12. (a)** Fermentation of glucose with yeast, about 35 °C, no air [1]; ethanol (after fractional distillation) heated with concentrated phosphoric or sulfuric acid to give ethene [1]; addition polymerisation of ethene to poly(ethene) [1]
**(b)** Any four: sugar cane is renewable [1]; less use of finite crude oil [1]; land used for fuel crops is not used for food and may raise food prices [1]; clearing forest destroys habitats and releases CO₂ [1]; fertiliser run-off; fossil-fuel energy is still used in farming and distillation. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" expects points on both sides; four advantages with no drawbacks will not reach full marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving yeast or "no air" out of the fermentation conditions.
- Counting 4 mol CO₂ from combustion against 6 absorbed without doubling for 2 mol ethanol.
- Curly arrows starting from H⁺ or from a positive charge.
- Forgetting that H⁺ is regenerated in hydration and elimination.
- Writing "reflux" for making an aldehyde.
- Missing the "no change" observation for the ketone.
- Missing E/Z isomers in elimination products.
- Using masses instead of moles in percentage yield.
- Using a 1 : 1 ratio for dichromate and alcohol instead of the 1 : 3 ratio in the equation.
