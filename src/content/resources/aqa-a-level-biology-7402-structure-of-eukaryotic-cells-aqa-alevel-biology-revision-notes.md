## Where each structure occurs

| Structure | Animal | Plant | Alga | Fungus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cell-surface membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, Golgi apparatus, ribosomes, RER, SER | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Chloroplasts | No | Yes (photosynthesising cells) | Yes | No |
| Cell wall | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| Cell vacuole (as named in the specification) | No large vacuole | Yes | Not named | Not named |

The specification places chloroplasts "in plants and algae", the cell wall "in plants, algae and fungi" and the cell vacuole "in plants". It gives no such restriction for the other structures; lysosomes are usually studied in animal cells such as phagocytes.

## Structure and function

| Structure | Key structure | Function |
|---|---|---|
| Cell-surface membrane | Phospholipid bilayer with proteins | Controls entry and exit of substances |
| Nucleus | Nuclear envelope (double membrane) with pores; chromosomes of protein-bound linear DNA; one or more nucleoli | Holds genetic information; nucleolus makes ribosomes |
| Mitochondrion | Double membrane; cristae; matrix | Aerobic respiration; ATP resynthesis |
| Chloroplast | Double membrane; thylakoids in grana; stroma | Photosynthesis |
| Golgi apparatus | Stack of flattened membrane sacs | Modifies and packages proteins and lipids; forms lysosomes |
| Golgi vesicle | Small membrane sac | Transports products, e.g. to the membrane for secretion |
| Lysosome | Membrane-bound; contains hydrolytic enzymes | Hydrolyses pathogens and worn-out organelles |
| Ribosome | RNA and protein; no membrane | Protein synthesis |
| Rough ER | Membrane sacs with ribosomes | Synthesises and transports proteins |
| Smooth ER | Membrane sacs without ribosomes | Synthesises and processes lipids |
| Cell wall | Cellulose in plants and algae | Strength; prevents bursting |
| Cell vacuole | Membrane (tonoplast) around cell sap | Stores sap; keeps cell turgid |

## Membrane count

- **Two:** nucleus, mitochondrion, chloroplast.
- **One:** Golgi apparatus, Golgi vesicles, lysosomes, RER, SER, vacuole.
- **None:** ribosomes; the cell wall is outside the membrane.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Eukaryotic cell | A cell with a nucleus and membrane-bound organelles |
| Organelle | A specialised structure within a cell |
| Nucleolus | Dense region in the nucleus that makes ribosomes |
| Cristae | Folds of the inner mitochondrial membrane |
| Thylakoid | Flattened membrane sac in a chloroplast; stacks form grana |
| Hydrolytic enzyme | Enzyme that breaks bonds by hydrolysis |
| Tonoplast | Membrane around the plant cell vacuole |
| Tissue | Group of similar cells working together |
| Organ | Group of tissues working together |
| Organ system | Group of organs working together |

## Organelle chains

- **Secretion:** nucleus → ribosomes on RER → ER → vesicle → Golgi apparatus → Golgi vesicle → cell-surface membrane.
- **Destroying a pathogen:** engulfed in a vesicle → lysosomes fuse → hydrolytic enzymes digest it.
- **Energy:** mitochondria → ATP → protein synthesis, active transport, movement.

## Adaptations: feature, function, need

| Cell | Feature | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Phagocyte | Many lysosomes | Digest engulfed pathogens |
| Ileum epithelial cell | Many mitochondria | ATP for active transport |
| Palisade mesophyll cell | Many chloroplasts | Maximise photosynthesis |
| Plasma cell | Extensive RER and Golgi | Make and secrete antibody protein |
| Root cell | No chloroplasts | No light reaches it |

Mini reminder with new numbers: if one cell type has 6 mitochondria per section and another 27, the second has 27 ÷ 6 = 4.5 times as many, suggesting a higher demand for ATP. Counts from single sections need repeats to be reliable.

## Exam traps

- "Mitochondria make energy." Say they release energy in respiration and resynthesise ATP.
- Ribosomes given a membrane.
- "All plant cells have chloroplasts."
- Cell wall or large vacuole drawn in animal cells.
- "The Golgi makes proteins." Ribosomes make them; the Golgi modifies and packages them.
- Confusing nucleus and nucleolus.
- Naming a feature as an adaptation without linking it to the cell's function.
- Getting the levels of organisation out of order.

## Self-test

1. What does the nucleolus do?
2. Name the three structures surrounded by two membranes.
3. Describe the structure of a mitochondrion.
4. State the function of the rough endoplasmic reticulum.
5. How does smooth ER differ from rough ER in structure and function?
6. What do lysosomes contain, and what do they do?
7. Which eukaryotic organisms have cell walls, according to the specification?
8. Explain why a phagocyte has many lysosomes.
9. Explain why root cells lack chloroplasts.
10. Give the route taken by a protein that is secreted from a cell.
11. Define a tissue and give one example.
12. Which organelle forms lysosomes?

### Answers

1. Makes ribosomes.
2. Nucleus, mitochondrion and chloroplast.
3. A double membrane, the inner one folded into cristae, around a fluid matrix.
4. Synthesises and transports proteins.
5. Smooth ER has no ribosomes; it synthesises and processes lipids rather than proteins.
6. Hydrolytic enzymes; they hydrolyse pathogens, worn-out organelles and other material.
7. Plants, algae and fungi.
8. Its lysosomes release hydrolytic enzymes that digest the pathogens it engulfs.
9. No light reaches them, so they cannot photosynthesise.
10. Ribosomes on rough ER → through the ER → vesicle → Golgi apparatus → Golgi vesicle → cell-surface membrane.
11. A group of similar cells working together for a function, for example epithelium or xylem.
12. The Golgi apparatus.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which structures must I know for AQA 3.2.1.1?**
The cell-surface membrane, nucleus, mitochondria, chloroplasts, Golgi apparatus and vesicles, lysosomes, ribosomes, rough and smooth ER, cell wall and cell vacuole.

**Are ribosomes organelles with membranes?**
No. Ribosomes are made of RNA and protein and have no membrane.

**How are specialised cells organised?**
Into tissues, tissues into organs, and organs into systems.
