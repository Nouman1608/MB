## Questions

**1.** Weights are recorded to the nearest kilogram and grouped in a class written 60–64. State the class boundaries and the class midpoint. **[2]**

**2.** The masses of 40 parcels are summarised below.

| Mass m (kg) | 2 ≤ m < 4 | 4 ≤ m < 6 | 6 ≤ m < 8 | 8 ≤ m < 10 | 10 ≤ m < 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | 9 | 15 | 8 | 5 |

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of all the points you would plot to draw a frequency polygon, including the end points. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a polygon is extended to the horizontal axis at both ends. **[1]**

**3.** The marks (whole numbers) of 50 pupils in a test are grouped as follows.

| Mark | 21–30 | 31–40 | 41–50 | 51–60 | 61–70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 5 | 12 | 19 | 10 | 4 |

**(a)** For a histogram, state the boundaries of each bar and the height of each bar. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the bars of the histogram touch. **[1]**

**4.** Waiting times at a service desk were recorded for 110 customers.

| Time t (min) | 0 ≤ t < 5 | 5 ≤ t < 10 | 10 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 15 | 30 | 35 | 20 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the frequency density for each class. **[3]**
**(b)** State the modal class and explain your choice. **[1]**

**5.** In a histogram, the bar for the class 12 ≤ x < 20 has frequency density 2.5.
**(a)** Find the frequency of this class. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate how many items lie between 14 and 20. **[1]**
**(c)** State the assumption you made in part (b). **[1]**

**6.** A histogram of 100 items has four bars.

| Class | 0 ≤ x < 10 | 10 ≤ x < 25 | 25 ≤ x < 30 | 30 ≤ x < 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency density | 1.2 | 2.0 | not shown | 0.9 |

**(a)** Find the frequency of each class whose density is shown. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the frequency and the frequency density of the class 25 ≤ x < 30. **[2]**

**7.** On a histogram, the bar for the class 0 ≤ x < 10 is 3 cm tall and represents 24 students. The bar for the class 10 ≤ x < 25 represents 54 students. Find the height of this second bar. **[3]**

**8.** Two clubs recorded the ages of their members. Club A has 40 members and club B has 120.

| Age a (years) | 10 ≤ a < 20 | 20 ≤ a < 30 | 30 ≤ a < 40 | 40 ≤ a < 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Club A frequency | 4 | 16 | 14 | 6 |
| Club B frequency | 12 | 30 | 54 | 24 |

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the points for club A's frequency polygon, including end points. **[3]**
**(b)** A student draws both polygons on one graph and says club B has the older members because its line is higher. Comment, using proportions of each club to support your answer. **[2]**

**9.** The delivery times of 150 parcels are shown below.

| Time t (min) | 10 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 30 | 30 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 60 | 60 ≤ t < 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 18 | 42 | 36 | 30 | 24 |

**(a)** Calculate the frequency density of each class. **[3]**
**(b)** A histogram is drawn with 2 cm on the vertical axis representing 1 unit of frequency density. Find the height of each bar. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the number of parcels with delivery times between 35 and 50 minutes. **[3]**
**(d)** State the modal class. **[1]**

**10.** A histogram shows the ages of members of a society. The bars are given below.

| Age (years) | 10 ≤ a < 14 | 14 ≤ a < 18 | 18 ≤ a < 26 | 26 ≤ a < 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency density | 2.5 | 6 | 3 | 0.5 |

**(a)** Find the frequency of each class. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total number of members. **[1]**
**(c)** Estimate how many members are younger than 16. **[2]**
**(d)** Later, 18 new members join, all aged 50 ≤ a < 80. Find the frequency density of this class. **[1]**
**(e)** After they join, find the percentage of all members who are aged 18 or over, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** A student draws a histogram of the data below, using the frequency as the height of each bar.

| Time t (min) | 0 ≤ t < 10 | 10 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 30 | 36 | 48 | 40 |

**(a)** Explain what is wrong with the student's method. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the correct heights of the four bars. **[2]**
**(c)** Which class looks like the modal class on the student's diagram, and which is the modal class on a correct histogram? **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The boundaries are 59.5 and 64.5 [1]. The midpoint is (59.5 + 64.5) ÷ 2 = **62** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving 60 and 64 as the boundaries is a common slip; for whole-number classes, extend each limit by half a unit.

**2. (a)** The midpoints are 3, 5, 7, 9, 11 [1]. The points are (3, 3), (5, 9), (7, 15), (9, 8), (11, 5) [1]. The end points use the empty classes either side: (1, 0) and (13, 0) [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** It makes the polygon start and finish on the axis, so the diagram is closed [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Plot polygon points at midpoints and add the closing points; marking the midpoints on your axis makes your intention clear.

**3. (a)** The boundaries are 20.5–30.5, 30.5–40.5, 40.5–50.5, 50.5–60.5, 60.5–70.5 [1]. Every class width is 10, so the heights are the frequencies [1]: 5, 12, 19, 10, 4 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The data are continuous on the scale, so each class ends where the next begins and there are no gaps [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Labelling the axis with printed limits (21 and 30) leaves gaps; use boundaries and give a reason for "no gaps".

**4. (a)** Widths are 5, 5, 10, 20, 20 [1]. Densities are 15 ÷ 5 = 3, 30 ÷ 5 = 6, 35 ÷ 10 = 3.5, 20 ÷ 20 = 1, 10 ÷ 20 = 0.5 [2]. **[3]**
**(b)** The modal class is 5 ≤ t < 10 because it has the greatest frequency density (6) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The class 10 ≤ t < 20 has the largest frequency (35) but not the largest density; naming it without referring to density is a common way to lose the mark.

**5. (a)** Width = 8, so frequency = 2.5 × 8 = **20** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The width from 14 to 20 is 6, so 6 × 2.5 = **15** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The items are assumed to be evenly spread across the class [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A part-class answer is an estimate; writing "estimate" and naming the even-spread assumption shows you know it is not exact.

**6. (a)** 0 ≤ x < 10: 1.2 × 10 = 12 [1]. 10 ≤ x < 25: 2.0 × 15 = 30 [1]. 30 ≤ x < 50: 0.9 × 20 = 18 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Frequency = 100 − (12 + 30 + 18) = **40** [1]. Density = 40 ÷ 5 = **8.0** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving 40 as the bar's height is a common slip; the bar height is frequency density, so divide by the width of 5.

**7.** Density of the first bar = 24 ÷ 10 = 2.4, which is 3 cm, so 1 cm = 0.8 of density [1]. Density of the second bar = 54 ÷ 15 = 3.6 [1]. Height = 3.6 ÷ 0.8 = **4.5 cm** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing the two frequencies directly (54 against 24) ignores the different widths and leads to the wrong height; work through density.

**8. (a)** Midpoints are 15, 25, 35, 45 and the end classes have midpoints 5 and 55 [1]. Points: (5, 0), (15, 4), (25, 16), (35, 14), (45, 6), (55, 0) [2]. **[3]**
**(b)** Club B has three times as many members, so every class is higher, which does not show age [1]. As proportions, club A has 14 ÷ 40 = 35% and 6 ÷ 40 = 15% in the top two classes (50% in total), whereas club B has 54 ÷ 120 = 45% and 24 ÷ 120 = 20% (65% in total), so club B does have the older members, but the height of its line is not the evidence [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A convincing comparison uses proportions from each club and states a conclusion.

**9. (a)** Widths are 10, 10, 10, 20, 40 [1]. Densities: 18 ÷ 10 = 1.8, 42 ÷ 10 = 4.2, 36 ÷ 10 = 3.6 [1]; 30 ÷ 20 = 1.5, 24 ÷ 40 = 0.6 [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Height = 2 × density [1], giving 3.6 cm, 8.4 cm, 7.2 cm, 3.0 cm, 1.2 cm [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 35 to 40 minutes: 5 × 3.6 = 18 [1]. 40 to 50 minutes: 10 × 1.5 = 15 [1]. Total estimate = 18 + 15 = **33 parcels** [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** The modal class is **20 ≤ t < 30**, density 4.2 [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each part-class as width × density before adding, so your method is visible even if the total slips.

**10. (a)** Widths are 4, 4, 8, 24 [1]. Frequencies: 2.5 × 4 = 10, 6 × 4 = 24, 3 × 8 = 24, 0.5 × 24 = 12 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 10 + 24 + 24 + 12 = **70** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Under 16 is the whole of the first bar plus 2 years of the second: 10 + 2 × 6 [1] = **22** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Width = 30, so density = 18 ÷ 30 = **0.6** [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** New total = 70 + 18 = 88 [1]. Aged 18 or over = 24 + 12 + 18 = 54, so 54 ÷ 88 × 100 = **61.4%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Remember the total changes when new members join, and keep working unrounded until the final 3 significant figure answer.

**11. (a)** The class widths are unequal, so the height must be frequency density, not frequency [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Widths are 10, 10, 20, 40 [1]. Heights: 30 ÷ 10 = 3, 36 ÷ 10 = 3.6, 48 ÷ 20 = 2.4, 40 ÷ 40 = 1 [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The student's diagram suggests 20 ≤ t < 40, the tallest bar at 48 [1]. The correct modal class is 10 ≤ t < 20, with the greatest density of 3.6 [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For an explanation mark, name the specific error (unequal widths need frequency density) rather than saying only that the heights are wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using printed class limits rather than boundaries for whole-number data, so widths and midpoints are off.
- Leaving out the closing points on a frequency polygon.
- Plotting polygon points at the upper boundary or limit instead of the midpoint.
- Drawing gaps between histogram bars of grouped continuous data.
- Using frequency as bar height when widths are unequal.
- Giving a bar's height as the frequency, or its frequency as the height.
- Choosing the modal class from frequency instead of frequency density.
- Presenting an estimate for part of a class as exact, or not stating the even-spread assumption.
- Comparing two groups of different sizes using raw counts only.
