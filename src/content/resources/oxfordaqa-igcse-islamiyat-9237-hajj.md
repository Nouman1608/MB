**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.6 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Hajj: "The role and importance of Hajj, including its origins and the promoting of the spirit of global brotherhood and unity (Ummah)", "The importance of Hajj rituals and how Hajj is performed, including Ihram, Meeqat and the actions pilgrims perform at sites including the Ka'aba, Mina, Arafat and Muzdalifah" and "The role and importance of Umrah, including how Umrah is performed."** The course guides list Hajj among the Five Pillars. This page follows a pilgrim through the rites in order, explains what each one means and shows how to use the prescribed passage, Qur'an 2:197-199.

## What Hajj is and who must perform it

Hajj is the pilgrimage to Makkah. It takes place on set days in the month of **Dhul Hijjah**, the twelfth month of the Islamic calendar. It is the fifth Pillar of Islam. Allah makes it a duty on "those who are able to find a way" (3:97), so a Muslim must perform it once in a lifetime if they meet these conditions: they are an adult Muslim of sound mind, they are healthy enough to travel and perform the rites, they have the money for the journey and for the family's needs while they are away, and the journey is reasonably safe. Someone who cannot meet the conditions is not blamed.

## The origins of Hajj

Muslims trace Hajj to the Prophet Ibrahim (AS).

- **Building the Ka'aba.** Ibrahim (AS) and his son Ismail (AS) raised the foundations of the Ka'aba as a house for the worship of one God alone (2:127). Allah told Ibrahim to "proclaim to the people the Hajj" (22:27).
- **Hajar and Zamzam.** Ibrahim (AS) left his wife Hajar and the baby Ismail in the valley of Makkah on Allah's command. Hajar ran seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwa looking for water, and the spring of Zamzam appeared. The rite of *sa'i* remembers her trust in Allah.
- **The sacrifice.** Ibrahim (AS) was ready to sacrifice his son at Allah's command, and Allah replaced the sacrifice with a ram. The sacrifice during Hajj remembers this test of obedience.
- **Restoration by the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh).** In time the Ka'aba was filled with idols. After the conquest of Makkah, the Prophet (pbuh) cleansed it and restored worship of one God. He led his only Hajj in the tenth year after the Hijrah, known as the farewell pilgrimage, and showed Muslims how to perform it. His example is the model that pilgrims follow today.

## Spirit of unity: the Ummah

Hajj is the largest gathering of Muslims each year. Pilgrims come from every continent, in every language and from every background. Men wear the same two white cloths. A wealthy man and a poor man stand side by side in the same place and say the same words. This promotes the spirit of **global brotherhood and unity** (the Ummah) because it removes the marks of race, class and nationality and shows that all are equal before Allah. Muslims say that Hajj is also a preview of the Day of Judgement, when all will stand before Allah.

## Before Hajj: Ihram and Meeqat

**Meeqat.** These are the fixed boundary points around Makkah. A pilgrim travelling to perform Hajj or Umrah must not pass them without being in Ihram. Different places have different Meeqat according to the direction of travel. Those who fly usually enter Ihram before the plane crosses the boundary, or at the airport.

**Ihram.** This is the state of purity and focus that a pilgrim enters. It has three parts: washing, putting on the Ihram clothing, and stating the intention (*niyyah*) for Hajj. Men wear two plain white unsewn sheets. Women wear modest everyday clothing and cover their hair but not their faces. Pilgrims then say the *Talbiyah*, "Here I am, O Allah, here I am", which they repeat many times.

While in Ihram, pilgrims must avoid cutting hair or nails, using perfume, hunting, sexual relations, arguing and wrongdoing. The prescribed passage says it clearly: "no sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing during Hajj" (2:197). The point is to place all attention on Allah. Ihram also teaches equality and humility, because everyone looks the same.

## The rites of Hajj in order

| Day (Dhul Hijjah) | Place | Main actions |
|---|---|---|
| Before the 8th, or on arrival | Makkah | Enter Ihram; *Tawaf* around the Ka'aba; *sa'i* between Safa and Marwa |
| 8th | Mina | Pilgrims travel to Mina, pray and spend the day and night in worship |
| 9th | Arafat | Stand (*wuquf*) from noon until sunset, praying, asking forgiveness and listening to the sermon |
| 9th night | Muzdalifah | Pray and rest in the open; gather pebbles |
| 10th | Mina and Makkah | Stone the large pillar; sacrifice an animal; shave or trim hair; Tawaf al-Ifadah |
| 11th to 13th | Mina | Stone the three pillars each day |
| Last | Makkah | Farewell Tawaf before leaving |

### The Ka'aba and Tawaf

The Ka'aba is the cube-shaped building at the centre of the Grand Mosque. It is the direction in which Muslims face for prayer (the qiblah). In **Tawaf** pilgrims walk around it seven times, anticlockwise, with the Ka'aba on their left. They try to touch or point to the Black Stone at the start of each circuit. They then pray two rak'ahs near the Station of Ibrahim and drink Zamzam water. Tawaf shows that Allah is at the centre of a Muslim's life.

### Sa'i

Pilgrims walk or move briskly between Safa and Marwa seven times, beginning at Safa and ending at Marwa. This follows the steps of Hajar and shows trust in Allah's help.

### Mina

Mina is a valley a few miles from Makkah. Pilgrims stay in tents on the 8th, spending the day in prayer. They return on the 10th to the **Jamarat**, pillars that stand for temptation. They throw seven small stones at the large pillar to show that they reject Satan, as Ibrahim (AS) did when he was tempted to disobey Allah. Over the following days they stone all three pillars. The 10th day is also when the sacrifice is offered, and the meat is shared with the poor.

### Arafat

The Day of Arafat, the 9th, is the heart of Hajj. The Prophet (pbuh) said, "Hajj is Arafat." A pilgrim who misses the standing at Arafat has not completed Hajj. From noon to sunset pilgrims stand or sit on the plain, praying and asking for forgiveness. The farewell sermon was given there. It is often compared to the Day of Judgement, with everyone standing in the same simple clothes, hoping for mercy.

### Muzdalifah

After sunset pilgrims travel to Muzdalifah, where they pray Maghrib and Isha together and spend the night under the open sky. The passage mentions it: "when you depart from Arafat, remember Allah at al-Mash'ar al-Haram" (2:198), a name for the sacred area at Muzdalifah. Many gather pebbles there for the stoning at Mina.

### Ending Hajj

After the stoning and the sacrifice, men shave their heads or cut their hair short (women trim a small amount). This marks leaving Ihram. Pilgrims then return to Makkah for the second Tawaf, called *Tawaf al-Ifadah*, which is a required part of Hajj. Before leaving Makkah they make a farewell Tawaf.

## Umrah

Umrah is the "lesser pilgrimage". It can be made at any time of the year. Its rites are shorter: Ihram at the Meeqat, Tawaf around the Ka'aba, Sa'i between Safa and Marwa, then shaving or trimming the hair. It does not include Arafat, Muzdalifah or Mina. Scholars differ on whether Umrah is obligatory; most agree it is highly recommended and carries great reward. The Prophet (pbuh) said that one Umrah to the next wipes out the sins between them.

| | Hajj | Umrah |
|---|---|---|
| Timing | Set days in Dhul Hijjah | Any time |
| Status | Pillar of Islam (once if able) | Highly recommended; debated whether obligatory |
| Arafat, Muzdalifah, Mina | Yes | No |
| Tawaf and Sa'i | Yes | Yes |

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways that Hajj promotes unity among Muslims. **[4]**

> Pilgrims all wear simple white Ihram clothing [1], so differences of wealth and race are removed and everyone is equal before Allah [1]. They also perform the same rites together, such as standing at Arafat [1], which shows Muslims from every country as one Ummah worshipping the same God [1].

Each point is a way and is developed into unity. Writing only "lots of people go" would not earn the second mark.

## Common mistakes

- **Saying Hajj is obligatory for every Muslim.** The condition is that they are able.
- **Mixing up Hajj and Umrah.** Umrah has no Arafat and can be done at any time.
- **Placing Arafat on the wrong day.** It is the 9th, and the stand lasts from noon to sunset.
- **Describing the rites with no meaning.** Always add the reason, such as Hajar for Sa'i or the rejection of temptation for the stoning.
- **Forgetting the origins.** The specification asks for them, so mention Ibrahim, Ismail and Hajar.

## Exam technique

Use **name** and **give** for facts such as Ihram, Meeqat or the number of circuits. For **explain** questions pair each rite with its meaning. In a 12-mark question on 2:197-199, use "no sexual relations and no disobedience and no disputing" for the discipline of Ihram, "the best provision is fear of Allah" for taqwa, and "remember Allah at al-Mash'ar al-Haram" for Muzdalifah. Then judge: Hajj is a powerful experience of unity and humility, but its value depends on sincerity.
