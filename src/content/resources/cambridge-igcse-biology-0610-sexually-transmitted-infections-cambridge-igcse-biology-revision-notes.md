These notes condense sub-topic **16.6 Sexually transmitted infections** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. The whole sub-topic is **Core**.

For explanations and worked answers, read the [STI study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexually-transmitted-infections-cambridge-igcse-biology/). Then try the [STI practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-sexually-transmitted-infections-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## The five facts to learn

1. An **STI** is an infection that is **transmitted through sexual contact**.
2. **HIV** (human immunodeficiency virus) is a **pathogen** that causes an STI.
3. HIV infection **may lead to AIDS**.
4. HIV is transmitted in **blood, semen, vaginal fluids and breast milk**.
5. The spread of STIs is controlled by methods that **stop these fluids passing** between people, and by **finding and treating** infected people.

## HIV and AIDS

| | HIV | AIDS |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | a virus, a pathogen | a condition |
| Relationship | infection with HIV... | ...may lead to AIDS |
| Effect | damages part of the immune system | the body cannot defend itself well against other pathogens |

- HIV is a **virus**, so **antibiotics do not affect it** (topic 15).
- "May" matters: not everyone with HIV develops AIDS, especially with treatment.

## Methods of transmission

| Route | Fluid |
|---|---|
| Unprotected sexual intercourse | semen, vaginal fluids |
| Sharing needles | blood |
| Transfusion of infected blood or blood products | blood |
| Mother to child: across the placenta, at birth, in breast milk | blood, breast milk |

**Not transmitted by:** shaking hands, hugging, sharing cups or cutlery, toilet seats, coughing, swimming pools, insect bites.

**Test for any situation:** does blood, semen, vaginal fluid or breast milk from an infected person enter another person's body? If not, there is no risk of HIV transmission.

## Control methods: method + how

| Method | How it reduces spread |
|---|---|
| Condoms | barrier: body fluids not exchanged during sex |
| Fewer sexual partners, or none | fewer chances of contact with an infected person |
| Testing | infected people without symptoms find out and can be treated and take precautions |
| Contact tracing | partners of infected people are tested and treated |
| Treatment | antibiotics cure bacterial STIs; HIV drugs reduce virus in body fluids |
| Screening donated blood | infected blood is not used in transfusions |
| Clean needles / not sharing | infected blood not passed between users |
| Care for mothers with HIV | treatment in pregnancy; avoiding breastfeeding where safe alternatives exist |
| Education | people know how STIs spread and change their behaviour |

## Answer frames

- **"Explain how [method] reduces the spread of HIV"**: state the route the method blocks (which fluid, how it enters the body), then how the method blocks it. Two linked sentences usually earn two marks.
- **"Could HIV be transmitted in this situation?"**: answer yes or no, then name the fluid and the route, or say that no blood, semen, vaginal fluid or breast milk enters the other person.
- **Data on new cases after a programme**: quote the figures before and after; link the fall to the named measure; note that other factors may also have changed.

**Percentage practice:** 45 positive results from 1500 tests = 45 ÷ 1500 × 100 = **3%**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| STI | an infection transmitted through sexual contact |
| Pathogen | a disease-causing organism (topic 10) |
| HIV | human immunodeficiency virus, a pathogen that causes an STI |
| AIDS | a condition that HIV infection may lead to |
| Transmission | the passing of a pathogen from one person to another |
| Barrier method | a method, such as a condom, that stops body fluids being exchanged |
| Screening | testing donated blood, or people, for a pathogen |
| Contact tracing | finding and testing the sexual partners of an infected person |

## Exam traps

- HIV is **not** AIDS; you are infected with HIV.
- **Antibiotics** do not treat HIV.
- Everyday contact does **not** transmit HIV.
- Every control method needs a "how" in an "explain" question.
- Condoms are a **barrier**; they do not kill the virus.
- Mother-to-child transmission has **three** routes: placenta, birth, breast milk.
- With data, link a fall in cases to a specific measure, and do not claim it is the only possible cause.

## Self-test

**1.** Describe what is meant by a sexually transmitted infection.

**2.** What do the letters HIV stand for?

**3.** What type of pathogen is HIV?

**4.** State the condition that HIV infection may lead to.

**5.** Why do antibiotics not cure HIV infection?

**6.** Name three body fluids that can carry HIV.

**7.** Give two ways a mother with HIV can pass the virus to her child.

**8.** Explain why sharing needles can spread HIV.

**9.** Explain how using condoms reduces the spread of HIV.

**10.** Explain why testing people who have no symptoms helps control STIs.

**11.** Why is donated blood screened?

**12.** Can HIV be passed on by sharing a towel? Explain.

### Answers

**1.** An infection that is transmitted through sexual contact.

**2.** Human immunodeficiency virus.

**3.** A virus.

**4.** AIDS.

**5.** Antibiotics kill bacteria but do not affect viruses.

**6.** Any three of: blood, semen, vaginal fluids, breast milk.

**7.** Any two of: across the placenta in pregnancy; during birth; in breast milk.

**8.** Traces of infected blood left in a used needle can enter the next user's blood.

**9.** A condom is a barrier, so semen and vaginal fluids are not exchanged.

**10.** People can be infected without knowing; testing lets them be treated and avoid passing it on, and their partners can be traced.

**11.** So that infected blood is not given to patients in transfusions.

**12.** No: sharing a towel does not pass blood, semen, vaginal fluid or breast milk into another person's body.
