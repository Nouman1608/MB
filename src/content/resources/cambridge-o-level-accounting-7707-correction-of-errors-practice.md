## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Explain the purpose of a suspense account. **[2]**

**2.** Prepare the journal entries to correct the following errors.

**(a)** A credit purchase of $860 from Torr Ltd was not recorded. **[2]**

**(b)** Commission received of $240 by bank transfer was debited to commission received and credited to bank. **[2]**

**(c)** A sales invoice of $2,170 to M Haq was entered in the sales journal as $2,710. **[2]**

**3.** A trial balance shows debit totals of $64,920 and credit totals of $65,300. State the amount of the difference and the side of the suspense account on which it is entered. **[2]**

**4.** State the effect, if any, of each correcting entry on the profit for the year.

**(a)** Dr Machinery $1,400, Cr Repairs $1,400 **[1]**

**(b)** Dr Drawings $300, Cr Wages $300 **[1]**

**(c)** Dr T Lowe $90, Cr T Low $90 **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the correction of an error of complete reversal uses twice the amount of the original transaction. **[2]**

**6.** State **two** types of error that are corrected using a suspense account. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Zara's trial balance at 31 October 2027 did not agree. The difference was entered in a suspense account. Her draft profit for the year was $31,600. These errors were found:

1. The purchases returns journal total of $560 was posted to the debit of the purchases returns account.

2. Insurance of $840 paid by bank transfer was entered in the cash book only.

3. The total of the discount received column in the cash book, $215, was not posted to the discount received account.

4. A payment of $1,000 received from a customer, L Moyo, was entered correctly in the cash book but credited to L Moyo's account as $100.

5. Carriage outwards of $130 was debited to the carriage inwards account.

**(a)** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, to correct errors 1 to 5. **[10]**

**(b)** Prepare the suspense account, showing the original difference. **[5]**

**(c)** Prepare a statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 October 2027. **[4]**

**(d)** State the effect of correcting error 5 on Zara's gross profit. **[1]**

**8.** Bongani's trial balance at 31 December 2027 agreed and his draft profit for the year was $18,750. Machinery is depreciated at 10% per year on the cost held at the year end. These errors were then found:

1. Repairs to machinery of $1,600 had been debited to the machinery account, and depreciation had been charged on this amount.

2. Goods costing $380 taken by Bongani for his own use had not been recorded.

3. A credit sale of $920 to F Dube had been omitted from the books.

4. A payment of $510 to a supplier, H Shaw, had been debited to the account of another supplier, H Shah.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2027. **[5]**

**(b)** State the effect of the corrections on each of the following: machinery at net book value; trade receivables; total trade payables; Bongani's capital at 31 December 2027. **[5]**

**(c)** Explain why error 4 has no effect on the profit for the year. **[2]**

**9.** For each error, state whether a suspense account entry is needed and give the correcting journal entry.

**(a)** Sales returns of $250 were posted correctly to the customer's account but not to the sales returns account. **[2]**

**(b)** The purchases account was overcast by $1,000 and the wages account was undercast by $1,000. **[2]**

**(c)** A payment for advertising of $340 was credited in the cash book and credited to the advertising account. **[2]**

**(d)** Loan interest of $600 was debited to the loan account. **[2]**

**10.** Ravi's trial balance did not agree, and a suspense account was opened with a debit balance of $260. Two errors were then found:

1. The revenue account had been overcast by $100.

2. Discount allowed of $160 had not been posted to the discount allowed account.

**(a)** Prepare the suspense account after the corrections. **[3]**

**(b)** State the effect of each correction on Ravi's profit for the year. **[2]**

**11.** Copy and complete the table to show the effect of **correcting** each error. Write "increase", "decrease" or "no effect", with the amount.

| Error | Gross profit | Profit for the year | Current assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Carriage inwards of $210 debited to carriage outwards | | | |
| (b) A credit sale of $500 to N Ito entered in the sales journal as $50 | | | |
| (c) Cash of $120 taken by the owner for private use debited to general expenses | | | |

**[9]**

## Answers

**1.** It holds the difference in the trial balance temporarily, so that the trial balance totals agree [1], until the errors are found and corrected, when the suspense account is closed [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Dr Purchases $860 [1], Cr Torr Ltd $860 [1]

**(b)** Dr Bank $480 [1], Cr Commission received $480 [1]

**(c)** Dr Revenue $540 [1], Cr M Haq $540 [1] **[6]**

**3.** Difference $65,300 − $64,920 = $380 [1]. The credit total is larger, so $380 is entered on the debit side of the suspense account [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** Profit increases by $1,400 (repairs, an expense, are reduced) [1]

**(b)** Profit increases by $300 (wages are reduced) [1]

**(c)** No effect: both are personal accounts [1] **[3]**

**5.** The original entries were on the wrong sides, so the first amount only cancels the wrong entries and brings the accounts back to zero [1]; a second, equal amount is needed to record the transaction on the correct sides [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A one-sided entry, where one of the two entries is omitted [1]; entries of different amounts on the two sides [1]. Also accept: both entries on the same side; an account added up wrongly; a balance omitted from or misplaced in the trial balance. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)**

```
                                                    Debit $   Credit $
1  Suspense                                          1,120
     Purchases returns                                          1,120
   (Purchases returns of $560 posted to the debit side)
2  Insurance                                           840
     Suspense                                                     840
   (Insurance paid not posted to the insurance account)
3  Suspense                                            215
     Discount received                                            215
   (Discount received total not posted)

   (Receipt of $1,000 credited to L Moyo as $100)
5  Carriage outwards                                   130
     Carriage inwards                                             130
   (Carriage outwards posted to carriage inwards)
```

Entry 1 [2]; entry 2 [2]; entry 3 [2]; entry 4 [2]; entry 5 [2]. In each, one mark is for the correct accounts and sides and one for the correct amount with a suitable narrative. Entry 1 must be $1,120.

**(b)**

```
Suspense account
Date     Details                      $   Date     Details                      $
2027                                      2027
31 Oct   Purchases returns        1,120   31 Oct   Difference in trial      1,395
31 Oct   Discount received          215              balance

                                  -----                                     -----
                                  2,235                                     2,235
                                  =====                                     =====
```

Purchases returns $1,120 [1]; discount received $215 [1]; L Moyo $900 [1]; insurance $840 [1]; difference $1,395 on the credit side as the balancing figure [1].

**(c)**

```
Statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 October 2027
                                                $
Draft profit for the year                  31,600
Add  Purchases returns understated          1,120
Less Insurance omitted                       (840)
Add  Discount received omitted                215
                                           ------
Corrected profit for the year              32,095
                                           ======
```

+$1,120 [1]; −$840 [1]; +$215 [1]; $32,095 [1]. Errors 4 and 5 have no effect on profit.

**(d)** Gross profit increases by $130, because carriage inwards (part of cost of sales) falls [1]. **[20]**

**8.** **(a)**

```
Statement of corrected profit for the year ended 31 December 2027
                                                     $
Draft profit for the year                       18,750
Less Repairs wrongly capitalised                (1,600)
Add  Depreciation charged on repairs (10%)         160
Add  Goods taken for own use                       380
Add  Credit sale omitted                           920
                                                ------
Corrected profit for the year                   18,610
                                                ======
```

−$1,600 [1]; +$160 [1]; +$380 [1]; +$920 [1]; $18,610 [1].

**(b)** Machinery at net book value decreases by $1,600 − $160 = $1,440 [1]. Trade receivables increase by $920 [1]. Total trade payables are unchanged: H Shah's balance rises by $510 and H Shaw's falls by $510 [1]. Capital: profit falls by $140 and drawings rise by $380 [1], so capital at 31 December 2027 decreases by $520 [1].

**(c)** Both accounts are trade payables in the purchases ledger, so the correction is between two liability accounts [1]; no income or expense account is involved, so profit is unchanged [1]. **[12]**

**9.** **(a)** Suspense needed [1]: Dr Sales returns $250, Cr Suspense $250 [1]

**(b)** No suspense entry (compensating errors) [1]: Dr Wages $1,000, Cr Purchases $1,000 [1]

**(c)** Suspense needed [1]: Dr Advertising $680, Cr Suspense $680 [1]

**(d)** No suspense entry (error of principle) [1]: Dr Loan interest $600, Cr Loan $600 [1] **[8]**

**(b)** Error 1: profit decreases by $100 [1]. Error 2: profit decreases by $160 [1]. **[5]**

**11.**

| Error | Gross profit | Profit for the year | Current assets |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Decrease $210 [1] | No effect [1] | No effect [1] |
| (b) | Increase $450 [1] | Increase $450 [1] | Increase $450 [1] |
| (c) | No effect [1] | Increase $120 [1] | No effect [1] |

In (a) the cost moves from expenses into cost of sales. In (b) trade receivables rise by $450. In (c) the cash has already left the business, so only the classification changes: drawings rise and expenses fall. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Correcting a complete reversal or a misposted credit with the single amount.
- Opening the suspense account on the side with the larger total.
- Using suspense for errors of commission, principle or omission.
- Including drawings or personal-account corrections in a statement of corrected profit.
- Forgetting the depreciation charged on an amount wrongly capitalised.
