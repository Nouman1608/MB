Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-ideal-gases-and-kinetic-theory/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-physics-ideal-gases-and-kinetic-theory-revision-notes/). The course hub is [OCR A Level Physics](/boards/ocr/a-level/physics/) and the printable [checklist](/checklists/ocr/a-level/physics/) lists each statement. Use NA = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹, R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** A sealed flask contains 6.4 g of oxygen gas, O₂, of molar mass 32 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of oxygen in moles. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of molecules. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of atoms. **[1]**

**2.** State three assumptions of the kinetic theory model of an ideal gas. **[3]**

**3.** Explain how the molecules of a gas produce a steady pressure on the walls of a container. **[4]**

**4.** A cylinder of volume 0.030 m³ holds an ideal gas at 1.8 × 10⁶ Pa and 285 K.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of gas in moles. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of molecules. **[1]**
**(c)** Some gas leaks out until the pressure is 1.2 × 10⁶ Pa at the same temperature. Calculate the amount of gas lost. **[2]**

**5.** Describe an experiment to test Boyle's law for air, stating how you keep the conditions constant and how you analyse the results. **[4]**

**6.** A sealed flask of air at constant volume was heated in a water bath. The pressure p was measured at several temperatures θ.

| θ / °C | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p / kPa | 97.0 | 104.0 | 111.0 | 118.0 | 125.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the gradient of a graph of p against θ. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the graph to estimate absolute zero in °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the estimate differs from the accepted value of absolute zero and how the experiment could be improved. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Hydrogen gas at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa has density 0.090 kg m⁻³. Calculate the mean square speed and the r.m.s. speed of its molecules. **[2]**
**(b)** Five molecules have speeds of 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, 7.0 and 8.0 m s⁻¹. Calculate their mean speed, mean square speed and r.m.s. speed. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** Use pV = ⅓Nm<c²> and pV = NkT to show that the mean kinetic energy of a molecule is 3/2 kT. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that k = R/NA has the value 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹. **[1]**

**9.** Two Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution curves are drawn for the same sample of gas, one at 300 K and one at 600 K.

**(a)** Describe the general shape of the curve at 300 K. **[2]**
**(b)** State and explain how the 600 K curve differs in the position of its peak, its height and the area under it. **[3]**

**10.** Neon atoms have mass 3.35 × 10⁻²⁶ kg and helium atoms 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg.

**(a)** Calculate the r.m.s. speed of neon atoms at 450 K. **[3]**
**(b)** Compare the mean kinetic energy of a neon atom with that of a helium atom at the same temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the ratio of the r.m.s. speed of helium to that of neon at the same temperature. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain why the internal energy of an ideal gas is equal to the total kinetic energy of its molecules. **[1]**
**(b)** 3.0 mol of ideal gas is heated from 250 K to 400 K. Calculate the internal energy at each temperature and the increase. **[3]**
**(c)** State what happens to the internal energy if the pressure is doubled at constant temperature, and explain. **[1]**

**12.** A container of volume 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³ holds 0.15 mol of argon (molar mass 0.040 kg mol⁻¹) at 330 K. Treat argon as an ideal gas.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of atoms. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of one atom. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the internal energy of the gas. **[2]**
**(e)** Calculate the r.m.s. speed of the atoms. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** n = 6.4 / 32 = **0.20 mol** [1]
**(b)** N = 0.20 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.2 × 10²³ molecules** [1]
**(c)** Two atoms per molecule, so **2.4 × 10²³ atoms** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is where answers lose credit, because the question names the particle, so counting molecules again does not answer it.

**2.** Any three from the following, one mark each [3]: a large number of molecules in random, rapid motion; molecules occupy negligible volume compared with the gas; collisions are perfectly elastic; each collision lasts a negligible time compared with the gaps between collisions; negligible forces between molecules except during collisions.
*Examiner insight:* Write "negligible volume compared with the volume of the gas", since "no volume" overstates the assumption.

**3.** A molecule hits the wall and rebounds, so its momentum changes. [1] The rate of change of momentum is the force on the molecule, so by Newton's third law there is an equal force on the wall. [1] Very many molecules collide with the wall at random, so the average force is steady. [1] Pressure is this force divided by the area of the wall. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Four marks need four linked steps, so name momentum, force, the large number of collisions and force per unit area.

**4.** **(a)** n = pV / RT = (1.8 × 10⁶ × 0.030) / (8.31 × 285) [1] = **23 mol** [1]
**(b)** N = 22.8 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.4 × 10²⁵ molecules** [1]
**(c)** At the same V and T, n is proportional to p, so n = 22.8 × (1.2 / 1.8) = 15.2 mol [1]; lost = 22.8 − 15.2 = **7.6 mol** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded value of n for later parts, because early rounding to 23 mol would give a different final digit.

**5.** Trap a fixed mass of air in a sealed tube connected to a pressure gauge. [1] Change the volume in steps, waiting after each so the air returns to room temperature (change it slowly, so constant temperature is maintained). [1] Record p and V for each step in a table. [1] Plot p against 1/V: a straight line through the origin shows pV is constant. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say how temperature is kept constant, because a list of apparatus without that condition earns little.

**6.** **(a)** gradient = (125.0 − 97.0) / (90 − 10) [1] = **0.35 kPa °C⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** p = 0 when θ = 10 − 97.0 / 0.35 [1] = **−267 °C** [1]
**(c)** The line is extrapolated a long way beyond the data, so a small error in gradient makes a large error in the intercept. [1] Use a wider temperature range, keep the flask fully immersed and use a very short connecting tube. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Extrapolation answers should give a number with its unit, and a comparison with the accepted value of about −273 °C.

**7.** **(a)** <c²> = 3p / ρ = 3 × 1.0 × 10⁵ / 0.090 = **3.3 × 10⁶ m² s⁻²** [1]; c_rms = √(3.33 × 10⁶) = **1.8 × 10³ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** mean speed = 25.0 / 5 = **5.0 m s⁻¹** [1]; <c²> = (4 + 9 + 25 + 49 + 64) / 5 = **30.2 m² s⁻²** [1]; c_rms = √30.2 = **5.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Square first, then average, then take the root; taking the root before averaging is the usual error.

**8.** **(a)** ⅓Nm<c²> = NkT [1], so m<c²> = 3kT [1], so **½m<c²> = 3/2 kT**, the mean kinetic energy of a molecule [1]
**(b)** k = 8.31 / 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, every line of the algebra is needed, including the cancelling of N.

**9.** **(a)** The curve starts at the origin, [1] rises to a peak and falls with a long tail at high speed, so it is not symmetrical. [1]
**(b)** The peak moves to a higher speed [1] and is lower, with the curve broader, [1] because the molecules are spread over a wider range of speeds. The area is the same, because it represents the same number of molecules. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mention that the area is unchanged, and describe the shape change in words rather than leaving the sketch to speak for itself.

**10.** **(a)** c_rms = √(3kT / m) = √(3 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 450 / 3.35 × 10⁻²⁶) [1] = √(5.56 × 10⁵) [1] = **7.46 × 10² m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** They are **equal**, because mean kinetic energy is 3/2 kT. [1]
**(c)** ratio = √(3.35 × 10⁻²⁶ / 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷) = **2.25** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is a reasoning mark, so state that mean kinetic energy depends only on temperature.

**11.** **(a)** There are negligible forces between the molecules except in collisions, so there is no potential energy. [1]
**(b)** U = 3/2 nRT: at 250 K, 3/2 × 3.0 × 8.31 × 250 = **9.3 × 10³ J** [1]; at 400 K, **1.5 × 10⁴ J** [1]; increase = **5.6 × 10³ J** [1]
**(c)** It is **unchanged**, because U depends only on temperature for a fixed amount of ideal gas. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Calculate the increase from the unrounded values, which gives 5.6 × 10³ J and not 5.7 × 10³ J.

**12.** **(a)** p = nRT / V = (0.15 × 8.31 × 330) / (4.0 × 10⁻³) [1] = **1.0 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]
**(b)** N = 0.15 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **9.0 × 10²² atoms** [1]
**(c)** 3/2 kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 330 [1] = **6.83 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1]
**(d)** U = 3/2 nRT = 1.5 × 0.15 × 8.31 × 330 [1] = **6.2 × 10² J** [1]
**(e)** m = 0.040 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.64 × 10⁻²⁶ kg; c_rms = √(3kT / m) [1] = **4.5 × 10² m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A good check is that N times the mean kinetic energy per atom gives the same internal energy as 3/2 nRT.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using °C where kelvin is needed.
- Not converting cm³ or kPa to SI units.
- Rounding intermediate values and drifting from the final answer.
- Giving "no volume" instead of negligible volume as an assumption.
- Leaving out the momentum, force and area chain when explaining pressure.
- Averaging the speeds before squaring when finding r.m.s. speed.
- Drawing Maxwell-Boltzmann curves that are symmetrical or reach the axis at high speed.
- Treating 3/2 kT as the energy of the whole sample.
- Omitting how temperature is kept constant in a Boyle's law experiment.
