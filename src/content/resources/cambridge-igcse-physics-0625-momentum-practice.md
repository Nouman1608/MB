## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Define momentum. **[1]**
**(b)** State the unit of momentum. **[1]**

**2.** *(Extended)* Calculate the momentum of a 0.16 kg cricket ball moving at 25 m/s. **[2]**

**3.** *(Extended)* **(a)** Define impulse. **[1]**
**(b)** A boot exerts an average force of 2500 N on a football for 0.0040 s. Calculate the impulse on the ball. **[2]**

**4.** *(Extended)* A runner of mass 65 kg has a momentum of 455 kg m/s. Calculate the runner's speed. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the principle of the conservation of momentum. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates from rest to 25 m/s in 10 s. Use the change in momentum to calculate the average resultant force on the car. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** *(Extended)* A trolley of mass 1.5 kg moving at 4.0 m/s collides with a stationary trolley of mass 0.50 kg. The trolleys stick together.

**(a)** Calculate their speed just after the collision. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy lost in the collision. **[3]**
**(c)** State what happens to the kinetic energy that is lost. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* An astronaut of mass 50 kg floats at rest outside a space station. She throws a 2.0 kg tool away from her at 5.0 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate her velocity after the throw. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, using momentum, why she moves. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A tennis ball of mass 0.058 kg reaches a racket at 20 m/s and leaves it in the opposite direction at 30 m/s. The ball is in contact with the racket for 0.0050 s.

**(a)** Calculate the change in momentum of the ball. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the average force exerted on the ball by the racket. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Car A, mass 1000 kg, travels east at 15 m/s. Car B, mass 1500 kg, travels west at 8.0 m/s. They collide head-on and move off together.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of the cars just after the collision. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in momentum of car A. **[2]**
**(c)** The collision lasts 0.15 s. Calculate the average force on car A. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* This question is about car safety.

**(a)** Explain how crumple zones and seat belts reduce the risk of injury in a collision. **[4]**
**(b)** A 70 kg passenger moving at 12 m/s is brought to rest. Calculate the average force on the passenger if the stopping time is (i) 0.30 s and (ii) 0.050 s. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Trolley X, mass 0.80 kg, moves at 2.5 m/s towards a stationary trolley Y of mass 1.2 kg. After the collision, Y moves forwards at 1.6 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the velocity of X after the collision. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the impulse on Y. **[1]**
**(c)** State the impulse on X. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Mass × velocity [1]
**(b)** kg m/s (or N s) [1]

**2.** p = mv = 0.16 × 25 [1] = **4.0 kg m/s** [1]

**3. (a)** Force × time for which the force acts [1]
**(b)** 2500 × 0.0040 [1] = **10 N s** [1]

**4.** v = p/m = 455 ÷ 65 [1] = **7.0 m/s** [1]

**5.** The total momentum before a collision (or explosion) equals the total momentum after it [1]; provided no external resultant force acts on the objects [1].

**6.** Δp = 1200 × 25 − 0 = 30 000 kg m/s [1]; F = Δp/Δt = 30 000 ÷ 10 [1] = **3000 N** [1]

**7. (a)** momentum before = 1.5 × 4.0 = 6.0 kg m/s [1]; 6.0 = (1.5 + 0.50)v [1]; v = **3.0 m/s** [1]
**(b)** Ek before = ½ × 1.5 × 4.0² = 12 J [1]; Ek after = ½ × 2.0 × 3.0² = 9.0 J [1]; loss = **3.0 J** [1]
**(c)** It is transferred to internal (thermal) energy of the trolleys and surroundings, and by sound [1].
*Examiner insight:* Momentum is conserved; kinetic energy is not. Do not use conservation of kinetic energy to find v.

**8. (a)** total momentum before = 0 [1]; 0 = (2.0 × 5.0) + 50v [1]; v = −0.20 m/s, so **0.20 m/s in the opposite direction to the tool** [1]
**(b)** Total momentum must stay zero [1]; the tool gains momentum in one direction, so she gains an equal momentum in the opposite direction [1].

**9. (a)** Taking the final direction as positive: initial p = 0.058 × (−20) = −1.16 kg m/s; final p = 0.058 × 30 = 1.74 kg m/s [1]; Δp = 1.74 − (−1.16) [1] = **2.9 kg m/s** [1]
**(b)** F = Δp/Δt = 2.9 ÷ 0.0050 [1] = **580 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 0.058 × (30 − 20) = 0.58 kg m/s ignores the reversal and loses the method marks.

**10. (a)** East positive: momentum before = 1000 × 15 + 1500 × (−8.0) [1] = 15 000 − 12 000 = 3000 kg m/s [1]; 3000 = 2500v [1]; v = **1.2 m/s east** [1]
**(b)** Δp = 1000 × (1.2 − 15) [1] = **−13 800 kg m/s** (13 800 kg m/s westwards) [1]
**(c)** F = 13 800 ÷ 0.15 = **92 000 N** (westwards) [1]

**11. (a)** In a collision the passenger's change in momentum is fixed (from their speed to zero) [1]; crumple zones crush so the car, and its occupants, take longer to stop [1]; seat belts stretch, increasing the time for the passenger to stop (and keep them from hitting the windscreen) [1]; a longer time means a smaller rate of change of momentum, so a smaller force (F = Δp/Δt) [1].
**(b)** Δp = 70 × 12 = 840 kg m/s; (i) 840 ÷ 0.30 = **2800 N** [1]; (ii) 840 ÷ 0.050 = **16 800 N** [1]

**12. (a)** before: 0.80 × 2.5 = 2.0 kg m/s [1]; after: 0.80v + 1.2 × 1.6 = 0.80v + 1.92 [1]; v = 0.08 ÷ 0.80 = **0.10 m/s forwards** [1]
**(b)** 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.92 N s** forwards [1]
**(c)** **1.92 N s backwards** (equal and opposite) [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out minus signs for opposite directions.
- Ignoring the reversal of velocity in a rebound.
- Using combined mass for objects that bounce apart.
- Saying a safety feature "absorbs momentum" instead of increasing the stopping time.
- Giving a final velocity without its direction.
