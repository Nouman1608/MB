## Equations to know

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)² | Kinetic theory prediction for N molecules of mass m |
| p = ⅓ρ(c_rms)² | The same equation divided by V; ρ is the gas density |
| ½m(c_rms)² = 3kT/2 = 3RT/2N_A | Average kinetic energy of one molecule |
| U = N × 3kT/2 = 3nRT/2 = 3pV/2 | Internal energy of an ideal gas |
| c_rms = √(3kT/m) | Follows from the kinetic energy equation |
| m = M/N_A | Mass of one molecule from molar mass |

## Condensed recall

- **Brownian motion:** visible particles (smoke in air, pollen fragments in water) move randomly and jerkily. They are hit unevenly by invisible molecules in fast random motion. It is evidence that atoms and molecules exist and move.
- Smaller particles and higher temperatures give more vigorous Brownian motion.
- **Pressure** comes from momentum changes of molecules rebounding from the walls.
- Boyle: same speeds, but more frequent collisions in a smaller volume.
- Pressure law: faster molecules hit harder and more often.
- Charles: faster molecules, so the gas expands to keep the collision rate per unit area constant.
- The gas laws are **empirical** (from experiment); kinetic theory is a **theoretical model**. Their agreement supports the model.
- Mean kinetic energy depends only on the kelvin temperature, not on the type of gas.
- At one temperature, c_rms ∝ 1/√m: lighter molecules move faster.
- (c_rms)² ∝ T: four times the temperature doubles c_rms.
- Ideal gas: no intermolecular forces except in collisions, so no potential energy; internal energy is all kinetic.

## The assumptions

1. A very large number of identical molecules in random motion.
2. Collisions between molecules and with the walls are perfectly elastic.
3. The volume of the molecules is negligible compared with the volume of the container.
4. There are no forces between molecules except during collisions.
5. The duration of a collision is negligible compared with the time between collisions.
6. Newton's laws of motion apply to the molecules.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Brownian motion | Random motion of small visible particles suspended in a fluid, caused by molecular impacts |
| Root mean square speed, c_rms | Square root of the mean of the squares of the molecular speeds |
| Empirical law | A relationship found from experimental observation |
| Theoretical model | An explanation built from assumptions and known laws, which makes predictions |
| Elastic collision | A collision in which total kinetic energy is conserved |
| Internal energy (ideal gas) | Total kinetic energy of the random motion of the molecules |

## Exam traps

- Saying molecules speed up when a gas is compressed at constant temperature.
- Giving heavier molecules more kinetic energy at the same temperature.
- Using M (kg mol⁻¹) where m (kg) is needed.
- Using the mean speed instead of c_rms.
- Writing "collisions are perfect" or "molecules have no volume" instead of the precise assumptions.
- Using °C in 3kT/2.
- Forgetting that the total internal energy depends on the amount of gas as well as the temperature.

## Quick self-test

1. What does Brownian motion provide evidence for?
2. Why do smaller suspended particles show more vigorous Brownian motion?
3. Give the assumption that justifies taking the momentum change at a wall as 2mc_x.
4. Four molecules have speeds 300, 400, 500 and 600 m s⁻¹. Find c_rms.
5. Find the mean kinetic energy of a gas molecule at 27 °C.
6. Gas of density 1.6 kg m⁻³ is at 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa. Find c_rms.
7. Hydrogen (M = 0.0020 kg mol⁻¹) and oxygen (M = 0.032 kg mol⁻¹) are at the same temperature. Find the ratio of their r.m.s. speeds.
8. The kelvin temperature of a gas is doubled. By what factor does c_rms change?
9. Find the internal energy of 2.0 mol of an ideal gas at 400 K.
10. Explain, using the molecular model, why the pressure of a gas rises when it is heated at constant volume.
11. Explain the difference between an empirical law and a theoretical model.
12. A container holds 0.040 m³ of ideal gas at 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa. Find the total kinetic energy of the molecules.

### Answers

1. That matter consists of atoms or molecules in continuous random motion.
2. They receive fewer impacts at a time, so the imbalance between sides is relatively larger, and their smaller mass gives a larger acceleration.
3. Collisions are perfectly elastic, so the molecule rebounds with the same speed.
4. Mean of squares = (9 + 16 + 25 + 36) × 10⁴ / 4 = 2.15 × 10⁵ m² s⁻²; c_rms = 464 m s⁻¹.
5. 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 300 = 6.2 × 10⁻²¹ J.
6. c_rms = √(3p/ρ) = √(3 × 1.2 × 10⁵ / 1.6) = 474 m s⁻¹.
7. c_rms ∝ 1/√M, so hydrogen : oxygen = √(0.032/0.0020) = √16 = 4.
8. c_rms ∝ √T, so it rises by √2 = 1.41.
9. U = 3nRT/2 = 1.5 × 2.0 × 8.31 × 400 = 1.0 × 10⁴ J.
10. Higher temperature means a higher mean kinetic energy, so molecules move faster; each collision with a wall gives a larger momentum change and collisions are more frequent, so the force per unit area rises.
11. An empirical law summarises experimental results without explaining them; a theoretical model starts from assumptions and known laws and predicts results that experiments can test.
12. U = 3pV/2 = 1.5 × 2.0 × 10⁵ × 0.040 = 1.2 × 10⁴ J.
