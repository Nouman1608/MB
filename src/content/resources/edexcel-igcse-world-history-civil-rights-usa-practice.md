## Question 1 — the Red Scare and the 1950s

**Extract A** (invented for this exercise).

> "McCarthy never found a single spy. What he found was something more useful to him: a country
> frightened enough to believe that he might."

**1(a)** What impression does the author give about McCarthy's influence? You must use Extract A to
explain your answer. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** effects of the Montgomery Bus Boycott (1955–56). **[8]**

**1(c)** "The Supreme Court was the main reason for progress in civil rights in the 1950s." How far
do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- Brown v Board of Education (1954)
- Little Rock (1957)

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 2 — protest, 1960–68

**Extract B** (invented for this exercise).

> "King's genius was to make the violence of his opponents do his work for him. In Birmingham and
> Selma, the cameras carried his argument into every living room in America."

**2(a)** What impression does the author give about King's methods? You must use Extract B to explain
your answer. **[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** effects of the growth of Black Power in the later 1960s. **[8]**

**2(c)** "The work of Martin Luther King was the main reason for the civil rights laws of 1964 and
1965." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the March on Washington (1963)
- Mississippi Freedom Summer (1964)

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 3 — other protest and Watergate

**3(b)** Explain **two** effects of the Watergate scandal on US politics. **[8]**

**3(c)** "The women's movement achieved little in the years 1961–74." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the work of Betty Friedan and NOW
- the opposition of Phyllis Schlafly

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

The author suggests McCarthy's power came from **public fear, not evidence**. "Never found a single
spy" stresses the emptiness of his claims; "something more useful" and "frightened enough to believe"
imply he exploited Cold War anxiety for his own ends.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Bus segregation ended:** the Supreme Court's 1956 ruling (Browder v Gayle) declared Montgomery's
  bus segregation unconstitutional.
- **New leadership and methods:** King became a national figure, and the success of a mass,
  non-violent boycott led to the founding of the SCLC (1957) and inspired later campaigns.

### 1(c) [16]

**Agree:** Brown (1954) overturned the legal basis of school segregation; the Court's 1956 ruling
ended Montgomery's bus segregation; court rulings gave protesters legal backing.

**Other factors:** the NAACP brought the cases; grassroots action (Parks, the Montgomery
Improvement Association, the Little Rock Nine); Eisenhower's use of troops at Little Rock; media
coverage of the Till case; the Civil Rights Act 1957. Limits: Brown set no deadline; Southern
resistance and the KKK revival.

**Judgement:** the Court provided the legal framework, but change required protest and, at times,
federal force.

### 2(a) [6]

The author presents King as a **shrewd strategist who used publicity**. "Genius" is admiring; "make
the violence of his opponents do his work" suggests deliberate provocation of a violent response;
"carried his argument into every living room" stresses the role of television.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Divisions in the movement:** SNCC under Carmichael moved away from non-violence and integration,
  splitting from King and the SCLC and losing some white support.
- **Black pride and community action:** the Black Panthers ran breakfast and health programmes;
  symbolic protests such as the 1968 Olympics raised international awareness.

### 2(c) [16]

**Agree:** Birmingham's images and the March on Washington helped push Kennedy and then Johnson to
pass the 1964 Act; Selma led directly to the 1965 Voting Rights Act.

**Other factors:** SNCC, CORE and local activists (sit-ins, Freedom Rides, Freedom Summer); Johnson's
political skill in Congress after Kennedy's assassination; the media; the violence of opponents;
the fear of more radical alternatives such as Malcolm X.

**Judgement:** King's leadership was central to national campaigns, but he relied on local
activists and on presidents willing to act.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Restraints on the presidency:** the War Powers Act (1973) limited the president's power to send
  troops abroad, and the Congressional Budget Control Act (1974) strengthened Congress over spending.
- **Loss of trust in government:** Nixon's resignation and Ford's pardon left many Americans
  cynical about politicians; new election finance and privacy laws (1974) tried to restore
  confidence.

### 3(c) [16]

**Agree (little achieved):** the Equal Rights Amendment was not ratified; opposition from Schlafly and
conservatives; continuing pay gaps and few women in senior jobs.

**Disagree:** Eleanor Roosevelt's Commission (1961); *The Feminine Mystique* (1963); NOW (1966) fought
discrimination; Title VII of the Civil Rights Act 1964 banned sex discrimination in employment; Roe
v Wade (1973); women's liberation changed attitudes.

**Judgement:** significant legal and cultural gains, but resistance limited further change.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Over-narrating events without linking them to the question.
- Confusing the 1957, 1964 and 1965 Acts.
- Using only King as evidence in essays about the movement as a whole.
