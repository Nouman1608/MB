## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Name the ion that is a component of haemoglobin, and the part of the molecule it is found in. **[2]**

**2.** Name two biological molecules in which phosphate ions are components. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the pH of a solution with a hydrogen ion concentration of 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³. Give your answer to 2 decimal places. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate the hydrogen ion concentration of a solution at pH 8.1. Give your answer in standard form to 2 significant figures. **[1]**

**5.** Blood plasma has a pH of 7.4. During intense exercise, the pH in a muscle falls to 6.8. Calculate how many times greater the hydrogen ion concentration has become. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**6.** A student says: "The hydrogen ion concentration in cytoplasm is so low that it cannot matter." Use the specification's description of inorganic ions to explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why a shortage of iron in the diet can lead to tiredness during exercise. **[3]**

**8.** State the role of sodium ions in the absorption of amino acids by cells lining the ileum. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** An enzyme from the stomach has an optimum pH of 2.0, and an enzyme from the small intestine has an optimum of 7.5.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the hydrogen ion concentration is at pH 2.0 than at pH 7.5. Give your answer in standard form to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the stomach enzyme has very little activity in the small intestine. **[3]**

**10.** A drug stops the active transport of sodium ions out of ileum epithelial cells into the blood.

**(a)** Explain why this would reduce the absorption of glucose from the ileum. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest why the drug would not stop glucose leaving the epithelial cells into the blood, at least at first. **[1]**

**11.** Phosphate is often a limiting nutrient for plant growth.

**(a)** Using your knowledge of 3.1.8, suggest two reasons why a plant short of phosphate ions would grow more slowly. **[4]**

**(b)** DNA moves towards the positive electrode when an electric current is applied to it in a gel. Use your knowledge of phosphate ions to explain why. **[2]**

**12.** For each process, name the inorganic ion involved and describe its role.

**(a)** Haemoglobin unloads oxygen in a respiring muscle. **[2]**

**(b)** Glucose is absorbed from the ileum against its concentration gradient. **[2]**

**(c)** An enzyme loses activity when acid is added. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Iron ion (Fe²⁺) [1]; in the haem group [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two: DNA, RNA, ATP, phospholipids [1][1]. **[2]**

**3.** pH = −log₁₀(4.0 × 10⁻⁶) = **5.40** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** [H⁺] = 10⁻⁸·¹ = **7.9 × 10⁻⁹ mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Difference = 7.4 − 6.8 = 0.6 [1]; 10⁰·⁶ = **4.0 times** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Do not subtract the concentrations or divide the pH values. The logarithmic scale means the ratio is 10 raised to the difference.

**6.** The specification says some ions occur in very low concentrations yet each has a specific role [1]; small changes in hydrogen ion concentration change pH, which alters the tertiary structure and activity of enzymes [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Iron is part of the haem groups that bind oxygen in haemoglobin [1], so less iron means less haemoglobin able to carry oxygen to respiring muscles [1]; less aerobic respiration means less ATP is resynthesised, so the muscles tire sooner [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Sodium ions are kept at low concentration inside the cell by active transport, creating a gradient from the lumen into the cell [1]; sodium ions diffuse into the cell through a co-transporter protein, carrying amino acids with them [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Difference = 5.5 pH units [1]; 10⁵·⁵ = **3.2 × 10⁵ times** [1].

**(b)** At pH 7.5 the hydrogen ion concentration is far lower than the stomach enzyme's optimum, so the charges on its R groups change [1]; ionic and hydrogen bonds in its tertiary structure are disrupted [1], so the active site changes shape and is no longer complementary to the substrate, and few enzyme-substrate complexes form [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** Without active transport, sodium ions are no longer pumped out of the cell, so the sodium ion concentration inside rises [1]; the concentration gradient from the lumen into the cell is lost [1], so sodium ions no longer diffuse in through the co-transporter and glucose is not carried in with them [1].

**(b)** Glucose leaves the cell by facilitated diffusion, which does not depend on the sodium gradient, so glucose already in the cell can still move into the blood down its concentration gradient [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Phosphate is needed to make nucleotides for DNA (and RNA) [1], so DNA replication before cell division is limited and fewer new cells are produced [1]. Phosphate is needed for ATP [1], so less energy is available for energy-requiring processes such as active transport and building polymers [1]. Also accept phospholipids for new membranes.

**(b)** Each nucleotide contains a negatively charged phosphate group [1], so the DNA backbone carries an overall negative charge and is attracted to the positive electrode [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) applies 3.1.8 to an unfamiliar context. Naming the phosphate in the backbone and its charge is enough.

**12. (a)** Hydrogen ions [1]; carbon dioxide from respiration raises the hydrogen ion concentration, lowering haemoglobin's affinity for oxygen so more is unloaded [1].

**(b)** Sodium ions [1]; their concentration gradient, maintained by active transport, drives co-transport of glucose into the epithelial cell [1].

**(c)** Hydrogen ions [1]; a rise in their concentration disrupts ionic and hydrogen bonds in the enzyme's tertiary structure, changing the active site [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating pH as a linear scale.
- Saying higher pH means more hydrogen ions.
- Saying ATP carries glucose directly in co-transport.
- Writing "iron carries oxygen" with no mention of haemoglobin or haem.
- Giving only DNA when asked for molecules containing phosphate.
- Naming the ion without stating its role in the context.

**How do you compare hydrogen ion concentrations at two pH values?**
Subtract the pH values and raise 10 to that power. For example, pH 5 and pH 7 differ by 10² = 100 times.
