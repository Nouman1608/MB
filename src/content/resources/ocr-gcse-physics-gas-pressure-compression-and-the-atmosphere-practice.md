## Questions

**1.** A sealed, rigid metal bottle of air is left in direct sunshine and its temperature rises. Explain why the pressure of the air inside rises. **[3]**

**2.** A student warms a sealed flask of air in a water bath and records the gauge pressure at four temperatures. The volume does not change.

| Temperature (°C) | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure (kPa) | 98 | 105 | 112 | 118 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern in the results. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the pattern using ideas about particles. **[3]**
**(c)** State one quantity that must stay constant during the experiment, other than the volume. **[1]**

**3.** A student says: "When a balloon of gas is heated, the gas particles expand. They take up more room, so they hit each other more and the pressure goes up." Identify the error and write a correct version of the explanation. **[3]**

**4.** **(Higher Tier only)** A syringe containing air has its nozzle sealed.
**(a)** Explain why the air can be compressed a long way but a syringe full of sand cannot. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the gas pressure on the inside wall of the syringe produces a force at right angles to the wall. **[2]**

**5.** **(Higher Tier only)** A sealed container has a flat lid of area 0.12 m². The pressure inside is 60 kPa and the pressure outside is 100 kPa. Calculate the net force on the lid and state its direction. **[4]**

**6.** **(Higher Tier only)** Assume the temperature is constant in each part.
**(a)** A gas of volume 150 cm³ is at a pressure of 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. It is compressed to 40 cm³. Calculate the new pressure. **[3]**
**(b)** A tank of volume 0.30 m³ holds gas at 4.0 × 10⁵ Pa. The gas then spreads into a volume of 1.5 m³. Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**

**7.** **(Higher Tier only)** A marshmallow contains many tiny gas bubbles. It is placed in a bell jar and air is pumped out. The marshmallow swells. Explain why. **[4]**

**8.** **(Higher Tier only)** A hand pump is used to inflate a football quickly, and the metal barrel of the pump becomes warm.
**(a)** Explain why the air in the pump becomes warmer. **[3]**
**(b)** In one stroke the average force on the piston is 60 N and it moves through 0.25 m. Calculate the work done on the air. **[2]**

**9.** **(Higher Tier only)**
**(a)** Describe a simple model of the Earth's atmosphere. **[2]**
**(b)** At ground level the atmospheric pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. A flat roof has area 2.5 m². Calculate the force of the air on the roof. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to find the mass of air above the roof. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher Tier only)** The table gives atmospheric pressure at four heights.

| Height (km) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pressure (kPa) | 101 | 79 | 62 | 47 |

**(a)** Describe how the pressure changes with height. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why atmospheric pressure falls with height. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher Tier only)** A weather balloon has volume 5.0 m³ at the ground, where the pressure is 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. It rises to a height where the pressure is 20 kPa.
**(a)** Assume the temperature stays constant. Calculate the volume of the balloon at that height. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of particles and forces, why the balloon expands as it rises. **[3]**
**(c)** The air at that height is colder than at the ground. Suggest the effect of this on the volume you calculated. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The air particles gain energy when heated and their average speed rises [1]. Wall collisions then happen more frequently [1], and every collision pushes harder, which raises the force on each unit of wall area [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both the frequency and the strength of the collisions. An answer that says only "faster particles" leaves the link to pressure unfinished.

**2. (a)** Pressure increases as temperature increases [1]. The increase is about the same for each 20 °C step, roughly 6 to 7 kPa [1].
**(b)** Heating makes the particles move faster [1]. They collide with the walls more often and with more force [1]. The volume is fixed, so the force per unit area, the pressure, rises [1].
**(c)** The amount (mass) of gas, so no leaks [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Support a description with a figure from the table. A particle explanation scores better when it says why the volume being fixed matters.

**3.** The particles do not expand when heated; they move faster [1]. Pressure is caused by collisions with the walls, not by collisions between particles [1]. Faster particles hit the walls more often and harder, so the force on each unit area increases [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A "what is wrong" question rewards naming the error and then replacing it. Repeating the student's wording does not correct anything.

**4. (a)** A gas is mostly empty space, so the particles can be pushed closer together [1]. Sand grains are already touching, so they cannot be squeezed together [1].
**(b)** Particles hit the wall from many directions [1]. Components of their bounces along the wall cancel, leaving a net force at right angles to it [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention "net" and "right angles" together in part (b). Saying "pressure acts in all directions" does not answer the question about the wall.

**5.** Pressure difference = 100 − 60 = 40 kPa = 4.0 × 10⁴ Pa [1]. Force = pressure × area = 4.0 × 10⁴ × 0.12 [1] = **4800 N** (4.8 × 10³ N) [1]. The force acts inwards, because the outside pressure is greater [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kPa to Pa before multiplying, and write the unit. Give the direction of a net force as well as its size.

**6. (a)** p₁V₁ = p₂V₂, so 1.0 × 10⁵ × 150 = p₂ × 40 [1]. p₂ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 150 ÷ 40 [1] = **3.75 × 10⁵ Pa** [1].
**(b)** p₂ = 4.0 × 10⁵ × 0.30 ÷ 1.5 [1] = **8.0 × 10⁴ Pa** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep volume units the same on both sides; cm³ can stay as cm³. A final pressure larger than the starting pressure after an expansion is a clue that the rearrangement is upside down.

**7.** Pumping out air lowers the pressure outside the marshmallow [1]. The gas inside the bubbles is at a higher pressure than the surroundings, so there is a net outward force on the bubble walls [1]. The bubbles expand [1]. As the volume increases, the particles hit the walls less often, so the pressure inside falls until it matches the outside pressure [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer compares the inside and outside pressures. Avoid saying the vacuum "pulls" the gas out.

**8. (a)** The piston does work on the air [1]. Particles rebound from the moving piston with greater speed [1], so their average kinetic energy increases and the temperature rises [1].
**(b)** Work = force × distance = 60 × 0.25 [1] = **15 J** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the energy transfer, "work done", rather than "heat from friction". Friction may be mentioned as a smaller extra effect.

**9. (a)** A layer of gas surrounds the Earth, held by gravity [1]. The density is assumed to be the same throughout the layer [1].
**(b)** Force = pressure × area = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.5 [1] = **2.5 × 10⁵ N** [1].
**(c)** Mass = 2.5 × 10⁵ ÷ 10 = **2.5 × 10⁴ kg** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), use the idea of a uniform-density model, because the specification says layers are not expected. In (c), say that the force is the weight of the air.

**10. (a)** The pressure decreases as height increases [1]. The drop per 2 km gets smaller: 22 kPa, then 17 kPa, then 15 kPa [1].
**(b)** There is less air above at greater height, so the weight of air pressing is smaller [1]. The air is also less dense higher up, because lower air is compressed by the air above it [1]. Fewer particles hit each unit of area, so fewer collisions and less force [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from the table when you describe. The explanation needs both the smaller weight and the thinner air.

**11. (a)** 20 kPa = 2.0 × 10⁴ Pa [1]. 1.0 × 10⁵ × 5.0 = 2.0 × 10⁴ × V₂ [1]. V₂ = **25 m³** [1].
**(b)** Higher up there is less air above, so the outside pressure is lower [1]. The gas inside hits the skin harder than the air outside, giving a net outward force [1]. The balloon expands until the inside pressure falls to match, because the particles hit the walls less often in a bigger volume [1].
**(c)** Colder gas has slower particles, so for a given volume its pressure is lower [1]. The skin is pushed out less, so the real volume is smaller than 25 m³ [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion line in (a). In (c), argue from particle speed and collisions, and state the direction of change.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying particles expand, get bigger or get hotter instead of moving faster.
- Describing only faster particles, with no link to collisions per second and force per collision.
- Forgetting to convert kPa to Pa before using force = pressure × area.
- Rearranging p₁V₁ = p₂V₂ upside down.
- Using pV = constant without saying the temperature is constant.
- Leaving out the direction of a net force.
- Explaining the warm pump as only friction.
- Saying there is no air at height, instead of less air above and thinner air.
