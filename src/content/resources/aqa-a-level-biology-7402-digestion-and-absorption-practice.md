These questions cover AQA A-level Biology (7402) section 3.3.3, *Digestion and absorption*. All data are invented. Teaching is in the [digestion and absorption study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-digestion-and-absorption/) and the [digestion and absorption revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-digestion-and-absorption-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** **(a)** Name the type of bond hydrolysed by lipase. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the type of bond hydrolysed by amylase. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the product of starch digestion by amylase. **[1]**

**2.** Give the products of the hydrolysis of sucrose. **[1]**

**3.** Explain how bile salts increase the rate of digestion of lipids. **[2]**

**4.** Disaccharidases are bound to the cell-surface membranes of epithelial cells in the ileum. Suggest one advantage of this. **[1]**

**5.** Some adults produce very little lactase. Suggest why drinking milk would not raise the concentration of glucose in their blood as much as it would in other adults. **[2]**

**6.** In an experiment, 36 µmol of glucose was absorbed by 2.0 g of ileum tissue in 15 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the rate of glucose absorption in µmol per minute per gram. **[2]**

**(b)** When a substance that inhibits the sodium–potassium pump was added, the rate fell to 0.36 µmol per minute per gram. Calculate the percentage decrease. **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** Explain why digestion of proteins by endopeptidases and exopeptidases together is faster than digestion by exopeptidases alone. **[3]**

**8.** Describe how glucose is absorbed from the lumen of the ileum into the blood. **[5]**

**9.** Use your answer to question 8 to explain why the substance in question 6(b) reduced glucose absorption. **[3]**

**10.** The rate of glucose absorption by ileum tissue was measured at different glucose concentrations in the lumen. The rate rose as concentration increased, then levelled off at high concentrations. Suggest why the rate levelled off. **[2]**

**11.** Explain the role of micelles in the absorption of lipids. **[3]**

**12.** Epithelial cells lining the ileum have microvilli and contain many mitochondria. Explain how each of these features is an adaptation for absorption. **[4]**

**13.** Describe how a triglyceride in food is digested and the products absorbed into an epithelial cell of the ileum. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Ester bond [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Glycosidic bond [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Maltose [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Glucose and fructose [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Bile salts emulsify lipids, breaking large droplets into many smaller droplets [1]. This increases the surface area for lipase to act on, so hydrolysis is faster [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "Bile breaks down fat" scores nothing; the key word is emulsify.

**4.** The monosaccharides are produced right next to the transport proteins that absorb them; or the enzymes are not lost in the gut contents [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Lactose is not hydrolysed into glucose and galactose [1], and lactose (a disaccharide) cannot be absorbed by the epithelial cells, so little glucose enters the blood [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 36 ÷ 15 = 2.4 µmol per minute [1]; 2.4 ÷ 2.0 = **1.2 µmol per minute per gram** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** (1.2 − 0.36) ÷ 1.2 × 100 = **70%** [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Endopeptidases hydrolyse peptide bonds within the polypeptide, producing many shorter chains [1]. This produces more ends [1], so there are more sites for exopeptidases to hydrolyse terminal peptide bonds and release amino acids at the same time [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Sodium ions are actively transported out of the epithelial cell into the blood by the sodium–potassium pump, using ATP [1]. This keeps the concentration of sodium ions in the cell lower than in the lumen [1]. Sodium ions diffuse into the cell down their concentration gradient through a co-transporter protein [1], carrying glucose with them against its concentration gradient [1]. Glucose then moves out of the cell into the blood by facilitated diffusion [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Name the membrane each step happens at and the direction of the sodium ion gradient.

**9.** With the pump inhibited, sodium ions are not removed from the epithelial cells, so the sodium ion concentration inside rises [1]. The sodium ion concentration gradient from lumen to cell is lost [1], so sodium ions no longer diffuse in through the co-transporter and glucose is not co-transported into the cell [1]. **[3]**

**10.** All the co-transporter (carrier) proteins are in use / working at their maximum rate [1], so the number of co-transporter proteins becomes the limiting factor, not glucose concentration [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Micelles are formed from bile salts, monoglycerides and fatty acids [1]. They carry monoglycerides and fatty acids through the watery contents of the gut to the epithelial cell surface [1]. They release these molecules next to the membrane, maintaining a concentration gradient so they diffuse into the cell [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Microvilli give a larger surface area [1], providing more room for carrier proteins and membrane-bound enzymes, so more absorption per unit time [1]. Many mitochondria release ATP by aerobic respiration [1], which is used for active transport by the sodium–potassium pump, maintaining the sodium ion gradient for co-transport [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Bile salts emulsify the lipid into small droplets, increasing surface area [1]. Lipase hydrolyses the ester bonds in the triglyceride [1], producing monoglycerides and fatty acids [1]. These combine with bile salts to form micelles, which carry them to the epithelial cell surface [1]. The monoglycerides and fatty acids are released and, being non-polar, diffuse across the phospholipid bilayer into the cell [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep emulsification (physical) and hydrolysis (chemical) as separate marking points.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying amylase produces glucose.
- Saying bile salts break down or digest lipids.
- Describing glucose itself as actively transported into the cell.
- Getting the side of the cell where the sodium–potassium pump works wrong.
- Leaving out the bond name or the word hydrolysis.
