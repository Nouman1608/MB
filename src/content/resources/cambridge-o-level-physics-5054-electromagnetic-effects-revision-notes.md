## Induction (4.5.1)

- E.m.f. induced when the field through a coil **changes**, or a wire
  **cuts** field lines.
- No relative movement (or no change) → no e.m.f.
- Bigger e.m.f.: **faster** change (faster movement, stronger magnet);
  **more turns**.
- Reverse the motion or the pole → induced current reverses.

**Lenz's law:** the induced current opposes the change producing it.

| Action | Pole induced at the near end of the coil |
|---|---|
| N pole pushed in | N (repels) |
| N pole pulled out | S (attracts) |
| S pole pushed in | S (repels) |
| S pole pulled out | N (attracts) |

## a.c. generator (4.5.2)

- Coil rotates in a magnetic field; **slip rings and brushes** keep
  contact without twisting wires.
- Rotating-magnet type: fixed coil, no slip rings.

| Coil plane relative to field | E.m.f. |
|---|---|
| Parallel (sides cutting lines fastest) | Maximum |
| Perpendicular (sides moving along lines) | Zero |

One turn: 0 → max → 0 → min → 0. Faster rotation → taller peaks **and**
higher frequency.

## Magnetic effect of a current (4.5.3)

| Conductor | Field pattern | Direction rule |
|---|---|---|
| Straight wire | Concentric circles, weaker further out | Right-hand grip: thumb = current, fingers = field |
| Solenoid | Bar-magnet pattern outside; strong, uniform inside | Fingers = current; thumb = N pole |

More current → stronger field. Reverse current → field reverses.

**Relay:** coil current → soft-iron core magnetised → armature attracted →
contacts close (or open) in a second circuit. Small current controls a
large or mains current (car starter motor, mains switching).

**Loudspeaker:** a.c. in a coil in a permanent magnet's field → force
reverses with the current → coil and cone vibrate → sound at the signal
frequency.

## Force on a conductor (4.5.4)

- Current at right angles to a field → force.
- Reverse current **or** field → force reverses; reverse both → unchanged.
- **Fleming's left-hand rule:** First finger = Field, seCond = Current,
  thuMb = Motion.
- Parallel wires: **same** direction currents **attract**; **opposite**
  directions **repel**.

## d.c. motor (4.5.5)

Turning effect increased by:

1. more turns
2. more current
3. stronger field

**Split-ring commutator + brushes:** reverses the current in the coil every
half turn, so the coil keeps turning the same way.

## Transformer (4.5.6)

```
Vp / Vs = Np / Ns
```

- Primary and secondary coils on a soft-iron core.
- a.c. in primary → changing field in core → alternating e.m.f. induced in
  secondary.
- Step-up: Ns > Np. Step-down: Ns < Np.
- Does not work on steady d.c.

**High-voltage transmission:** same power, higher V → lower I → much
smaller heating losses in cables (loss = I × p.d. across cable, and that
p.d. = I × R).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic induction | Producing an e.m.f. by changing the magnetic field through a conductor |
| Lenz's law | The induced current opposes the change producing it |
| Slip rings | Continuous rings connecting a generator coil to the circuit |
| Split-ring commutator | Divided ring that reverses the coil current every half turn in a d.c. motor |
| Solenoid | A long coil of wire |
| Armature (relay) | Iron piece attracted by the relay's electromagnet, moving the contacts |
| Step-up transformer | Increases the alternating p.d.; more secondary turns |
| Step-down transformer | Decreases the alternating p.d.; fewer secondary turns |

## Exam traps

- Motor force: **left** hand. Grip rule for fields: **right** hand.
- Slip rings → generator (a.c.); split ring → motor (d.c.).
- Generator e.m.f. is **zero** when the coil is perpendicular to the field.
- Faster rotation changes **both** amplitude and frequency.
- Transformers need **a.c.**
- High-voltage transmission reduces the **current**, and so the
  **losses**, not the power delivered.
- Lenz's law answers must name the pole and say "opposes".

## Self-test

1. A magnet rests inside a coil. Is an e.m.f. induced?
2. Give two ways of increasing the e.m.f. induced by moving a magnet into a
   coil.
3. State Lenz's law.
4. An S pole is pushed into a coil. Which pole forms at the near end?
5. What is the purpose of slip rings in an a.c. generator?
6. At what coil position is the generator e.m.f. zero?
7. Describe the magnetic field around a straight current-carrying wire.
8. Two parallel wires carry currents in the same direction. Do they
   attract or repel?
9. Name the three factors that increase the turning effect on a motor
   coil.
10. What does the split-ring commutator do?
11. A transformer has 600 primary turns on 240 V; the output is 12 V. Find
    the secondary turns.
12. Why are power cables operated at high voltage?

**Answers**

1. No — the field is not changing.
2. Move the magnet faster; use a stronger magnet; use more turns (any two).
3. The induced current opposes the change producing it.
4. An S pole (repelling the approaching S pole).
5. They connect the rotating coil to the external circuit without twisting
   the wires.
6. When the plane of the coil is perpendicular to the field.
7. Concentric circles centred on the wire, further apart with distance;
   direction from the right-hand grip rule.
8. Attract.
9. More turns, larger current, stronger magnetic field.
10. Reverses the current in the coil every half turn so it keeps rotating
    in the same direction.
11. Ns = 600 × 12 ÷ 240 = **30 turns**.
12. For the same power, the current is smaller, so less energy is lost
    heating the cables.
