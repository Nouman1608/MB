These questions cover section 3.8, Analysis and evaluation of financial information, of the AQA A-level Accounting (7127) specification (version 1.0, 22 December 2016, A-level exams June 2019 onwards). The qualification is not tiered, and the specification lists section 8 in the content assessed by both Paper 1 and Paper 2. Use a calculator throughout. Give percentages and ratios to two decimal places and days to one decimal place. All businesses and figures are invented.

## Questions

**1.** State the formula for the liquid capital ratio and explain why it leaves out inventory. **[2]**

**2.** Felling Hardware has revenue of £250,000 and cost of sales of £175,000. Calculate the gross profit margin and the markup. **[3]**

**3.** Orla Gifts prices every product at cost plus 25%. Its revenue for the year was £180,000. Calculate the gross profit margin and the cost of sales. **[3]**

**4.** Weir Stationers had revenue of £210,000, gross profit of £79,800 and expenses of £54,600. Calculate expenses in relation to revenue and profit in relation to revenue, and state how they link to the gross profit margin of 38.00%. **[3]**

**5.** Pike Pet Supplies had cost of sales of £252,000, opening inventory of £18,000 and closing inventory of £24,000. Calculate the rate of inventory turnover in times and in days. **[3]**

**6.** Ravel Electrical had trade receivables of £41,000 and credit sales of £410,000. Trade payables were £22,500 and credit purchases £219,000.

**(a)** Calculate trade receivable days. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate trade payable days. **[2]**
**(c)** Comment on the relationship between the two results. **[1]**

**7.** Tamsin Print Ltd's statement of financial position shows share capital £250,000, share premium £50,000, retained earnings £100,000 and a bank loan repayable in 2031 of £200,000. Profit from operations was £72,000 and loan interest £16,000.

**(a)** Calculate return on capital employed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate capital gearing. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the gearing figure shows. **[1]**

**8.** A business has current assets of £60,000, including inventory of £24,000 and bank of £8,000. Current liabilities are £30,000. Treat each transaction separately. For each, calculate the new current ratio and liquid capital ratio, and state the effect on profit. **[8]**

- (i) Paid a trade payable £6,000 from the bank.
- (ii) Bought inventory of £5,000 on credit.
- (iii) Sold goods that cost £4,000 for £6,000 on credit.
- (iv) Charged depreciation of £3,000.

**9.** Brennan Plumbing made a profit for the year of £35,000. During the year: depreciation was £6,000; inventory rose by £4,000; trade receivables rose by £7,000; trade payables fell by £2,000; a van was bought for £18,000 cash; drawings were £22,000; and a loan of £5,000 was received.

**(a)** Calculate the change in the bank balance for the year. **[4]**
**(b)** The owner says, "We made £35,000 profit, so the bank must be up." Explain why he is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** Explain two financial limitations and one non-financial limitation of using ratio analysis to assess a business's performance. **[6]**

**11.** Linton Cycles is a sole trader. Extracts for the year ended 31 March 2026:

| Item | £ |
|---|---:|
| Revenue (credit sales £240,000) | 300,000 |
| Cost of sales | 195,000 |
| Expenses (including loan interest £3,000) | 75,000 |
| Capital at 31 March 2026 | 160,000 |
| Bank loan (non-current) | 40,000 |
| Inventory | 26,000 |
| Trade receivables | 30,000 |
| Bank | 2,000 |
| Trade payables | 29,000 |

Last year's ratios: gross profit margin 38.00%; profit in relation to revenue 12.50%; ROCE 19.00%; current ratio 1.80:1; liquid capital ratio 1.20:1; trade receivable days 32.0.

**(a)** Calculate the same six ratios for this year, using closing capital. **[6]**
**(b)** Evaluate the performance of Linton Cycles over the two years. **[6]**

**12.** A bank is deciding whether to lend £100,000 to one of two companies in the same industry.

| | Halden Ltd £ | Marsh Ltd £ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Share capital | 200,000 | 150,000 |
| Retained earnings | 80,000 | 50,000 |
| Non-current liabilities | 120,000 | 200,000 |
| Profit from operations | 60,000 | 48,000 |
| Current ratio | 1.40:1 | 0.90:1 |

**(a)** Calculate capital gearing and ROCE for each company. **[4]**
**(b)** Evaluate which company the bank should lend to. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** (Current assets - inventory) / current liabilities, expressed as x:1 [1]. Inventory must be sold before it becomes cash, so it is the least liquid current asset [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A formula written in words is fine; give the result in the x:1 form the specification uses for this ratio.

**2.** Gross profit = 250,000 - 175,000 = £75,000 [1]. Margin = 75,000 / 250,000 × 100 = **30.00%** [1]. Markup = 75,000 / 175,000 × 100 = **42.86%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Swapping the denominators is the classic slip; write the formula before substituting so the base is visible.

**3.** Margin = 25 / 125 × 100 = **20.00%** [1]. Gross profit = 20% × 180,000 = £36,000 [1]. Cost of sales = 180,000 - 36,000 = **£144,000** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Taking 25% of revenue as gross profit gives £45,000 and a wrong cost of sales; the 25% applies to cost, not to revenue.

**4.** Expenses to revenue = 54,600 / 210,000 × 100 = **26.00%** [1]. Profit = 79,800 - 54,600 = £25,200, so profit to revenue = **12.00%** [1]. Gross margin minus expenses to revenue equals profit to revenue: 38.00 - 26.00 = 12.00 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The link is a quick arithmetic check on your own answers; use it whenever both ratios are asked for.

**5.** Average inventory = (18,000 + 24,000) / 2 = £21,000 [1]. Turnover = 252,000 / 21,000 = **12 times** [1]. Days = 21,000 / 252,000 × 365 = **30.4 days** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Using closing inventory alone gives 10.5 times; the specification's formula uses average inventory.

**6. (a)** 41,000 / 410,000 × 365 [1] = **36.5 days** [1].
**(b)** 22,500 / 219,000 × 365 [1] = **37.5 days** [1].
**(c)** Ravel collects about a day before it pays suppliers, so supplier credit finances its receivables, with little margin if collection slows [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment needs a consequence for cash, not just "payable days are higher".

**7. (a)** Capital employed = 250,000 + 50,000 + 100,000 + 200,000 = £600,000 [1]. ROCE = 72,000 / 600,000 × 100 = **12.00%** [1].
**(b)** 200,000 / 600,000 × 100 = **33.33%** [1] [1].
**(c)** A third of long-term finance is borrowed, so the company has fixed interest to pay whatever its profit; this is moderate gearing [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For a limited company use profit from operations, not profit after interest; £56,000 here would understate ROCE.

**8.** Start: current ratio 2.00:1, liquid 1.20:1.

| | Current ratio | Liquid ratio | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|
| (i) | 54,000 / 24,000 = **2.25:1** [1] | 30,000 / 24,000 = **1.25:1** | No effect [1] |
| (ii) | 65,000 / 35,000 = **1.86:1** [1] | 36,000 / 35,000 = **1.03:1** | No effect [1] |
| (iii) | 62,000 / 30,000 = **2.07:1** [1] | 42,000 / 30,000 = **1.40:1** | Up £2,000 [1] |
| (iv) | **No change** [1] | **No change** | Down £3,000 [1] |

**[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In this scheme each ratio mark needs both ratios right; show the new totals so a slip in one figure can be followed through.

**9. (a)** Non-cash item: 35,000 + 6,000 = 41,000 [1]. Working capital: 41,000 - 4,000 - 7,000 - 2,000 = 28,000 [1]. Other cash flows: 28,000 - 18,000 - 22,000 + 5,000 [1] = **bank fell by £7,000** [1].
**(b)** Profit counts sales and expenses when earned or incurred, not when cash moves, and ignores cash spent on the van and drawings [1]. Here cash went into inventory, receivables, the van and drawings, so the bank fell despite the profit [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Watch the signs: a fall in trade payables is a cash outflow, since suppliers were paid more than was bought.

**10.** Financial limitation 1: data is historic [1], so it may not predict next year if, for example, a key customer has just been lost [1]. Financial limitation 2: businesses use different policies, such as depreciation methods [1], so ratios comparing two firms may reflect policy, not performance [1]. Non-financial limitation: staff morale and skill are not shown [1], yet they affect future productivity and service [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs a consequence after each limitation; a list of six limitations without development is likely to earn less than three developed ones.

**11. (a)** Gross profit = 105,000, so GP margin = **35.00%** [1]. Profit = 105,000 - 75,000 = 30,000, so profit to revenue = **10.00%** [1]. ROCE = (30,000 + 3,000) / 200,000 × 100 = **16.50%** [1]. Current assets = 58,000; current ratio = 58,000 / 29,000 = **2.00:1** [1]. Liquid ratio = 32,000 / 29,000 = **1.10:1** [1]. Receivable days = 30,000 / 240,000 × 365 = **45.6 days** [1].
**(b)** Profitability has fallen: gross margin is down 3 points and profit to revenue 2.5 points [1], possibly from discounting or higher supplier prices [1]. ROCE has fallen from 19.00% to 16.50%, a weaker return on the owner's and lender's funds [1]. The current ratio has risen but the liquid ratio has fallen, so the improvement rests on inventory, and bank is only £2,000 [1]. Receivable days rose from 32.0 to 45.6, suggesting weaker credit control and explaining much of the cash shortage [1]. Judgement: performance has worsened; Linton should chase receivables and review pricing, though one year-end may be untypical [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation credit usually goes to linked points (receivable days explaining low cash) and a clear judgement, not to restating each ratio.

**12. (a)** Halden: capital employed 400,000; gearing = **30.00%** [1]; ROCE = **15.00%** [1]. Marsh: capital employed 400,000; gearing = **50.00%** [1]; ROCE = **12.00%** [1].
**(b)** Halden is less geared, so a new loan adds less risk and its interest burden is lower [1]. Halden earns a higher ROCE, so it generates more profit to cover extra interest [1]. Marsh's current ratio below 1:1 suggests it may struggle to pay short-term debts, a warning for any lender [1]. A further £100,000 would push Marsh's gearing to 60.00% [1]. The bank should still check security and management quality, which ratios do not show [1]. Recommendation: lend to Halden [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation should follow from the figures; sitting on the fence without a decision limits the credit for "evaluate".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using total revenue instead of credit sales for receivable days.
- Using closing inventory instead of average inventory.
- Confusing margin (on revenue) with markup (on cost of sales).
- Forgetting to add back loan interest in sole-trader ROCE.
- Writing liquidity ratios as percentages instead of x:1.
- Treating depreciation as a cash outflow in a cash versus profit question.
- Describing changes without causes, consequences or a judgement.
