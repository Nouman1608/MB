These questions cover **3.3.2.3 Combustion of alkanes** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [combustion of alkanes study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-3/) or the [combustion of alkanes revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-2-3-revision-notes/) first.

Take Ar values as H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0, S = 32.1 and Ca = 40.1, and R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Where does the nitrogen in the NOₓ emitted by a petrol engine come from?

A: the fuel

B: the air

C: the catalytic converter

D: the engine oil **[1]**

**2.** Write an equation for the complete combustion of decane, C₁₀H₂₂. **[1]**

**3.** Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of propane to carbon monoxide and water. **[1]**

**4.** Write an equation for the complete combustion of butanal, C₄H₈O. **[1]**

**5.** Give two reasons why incomplete combustion of a fuel is undesirable. **[2]**

**6.** State two features of the design of a catalytic converter and explain how each one helps. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** 10 cm³ of butane is mixed with 100 cm³ of oxygen and burned completely. All volumes are measured at the same room temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Write the equation for the complete combustion of butane. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total volume of gas left after the mixture has cooled to room temperature. **[2]**

**(c)** The gas is then passed through aqueous sodium hydroxide. State the volume and identity of the gas that remains. **[1]**

**8.** 2.30 g of ethanol, C₂H₅OH, burns completely.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of carbon dioxide formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of this carbon dioxide, in dm³, at 100 kPa and 298 K. **[2]**

**9.** This question is about catalytic converters.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction that removes carbon monoxide and nitrogen monoxide together. **[1]**

**(b)** Unburned butane reacts with nitrogen monoxide in the converter to form carbon dioxide, water and nitrogen only. Write the equation. **[1]**

**(c)** In the reaction in (b), identify the species that is oxidised and the species that is reduced. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why fitting a catalytic converter does not reduce the amount of carbon dioxide a car emits. **[1]**

**10.** Nitrogen oxides form in petrol engines.

**(a)** Explain how nitrogen monoxide forms in the engine, and write an equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the conversion of nitrogen monoxide into nitrogen dioxide in air. **[1]**

**11.** A power station burns 500 tonnes of coal containing 1.5 % sulfur by mass.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of sulfur dioxide from sulfur. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide formed, in tonnes. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why calcium oxide can be used to remove sulfur dioxide from the flue gases. Include an equation. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the mass of calcium oxide needed to remove all of this sulfur dioxide. **[1]**

**12.** 0.580 g of an alkane burns completely to give 1.76 g of carbon dioxide and 0.900 g of water.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of carbon atoms in the sample. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of hydrogen atoms in the sample. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce the empirical formula and the molecular formula of the alkane. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** B [1]

**2.** C₁₀H₂₂ + 15½O₂ → 10CO₂ + 11H₂O (or doubled) [1]

**3.** C₃H₈ + 3½O₂ → 3CO + 4H₂O [1]

**4.** C₄H₈O + 5½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 4H₂O [1]

*Examiner insight:* The products contain 12 O atoms, and butanal supplies one, so only 11 O atoms (5½ O₂) come from oxygen gas.

**5.** Carbon monoxide is formed, which is toxic [1]; less energy is released per mole of fuel (or soot is produced) [1]

**Total: 2**

**6.** Any two, each with its explanation [2]:

- A ceramic honeycomb support gives a very large surface area for the reactions.
- Only a thin coating of the expensive catalyst metals (such as Pt and Rh) is needed, which keeps the cost down.
- It is placed in the exhaust where the gases are hot, so the reactions on the surface are fast.

**Total: 2**

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** C₄H₁₀ + 6½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O [1]

**(b)** O₂ used = 6.5 × 10 = 65 cm³, so 35 cm³ of O₂ is left; CO₂ formed = 40 cm³ [1]; total gas = 35 + 40 = 75 cm³ (water is liquid) [1]

**(c)** 35 cm³ of oxygen [1]

**Total: 4**

**8.**

**(a)** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O [1]

**(b)** n(C₂H₅OH) = 2.30 ÷ 46.0 = 0.0500 mol; n(CO₂) = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 44.0 = 4.40 g [1]

**(c)** V = nRT ÷ p = 0.100 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 100 000 = 2.48 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]; = 2.48 dm³ [1]

**Total: 5**

*Examiner insight:* Pressure must be in Pa (100 kPa = 100 000 Pa); the volume then comes out in m³ and is multiplied by 1000 for dm³.

**9.**

**(a)** 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂ [1]

**(b)** C₄H₁₀ + 13NO → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O + 6½N₂ [1]

**(c)** Butane is oxidised (carbon to CO₂) [1]; nitrogen monoxide is reduced (to N₂) [1]

**(d)** The converter turns CO and hydrocarbons into CO₂, so carbon dioxide is a product, not a pollutant it removes [1]

**Total: 5**

**10.**

**(a)** At the very high temperature in the cylinder, nitrogen and oxygen from the air react [1]; N₂ + O₂ → 2NO [1]

**(b)** 2NO + O₂ → 2NO₂ [1]

**Total: 3**

**11.**

**(a)** S + O₂ → SO₂ [1]

**(b)** mass of S = 1.5 % of 500 t = 7.5 t [1]; mass of SO₂ = 7.5 × 64.1 ÷ 32.1 = 15.0 t [1]

**(c)** SO₂ is an acidic oxide and calcium oxide is a basic oxide, so they react in a neutralisation, forming a solid [1]; CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃ [1]

**(d)** 14.98 × 56.1 ÷ 64.1 = 13.1 t [1]

**Total: 6**

**12.**

**(a)** n(CO₂) = 1.76 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0400 mol, so 0.0400 mol of C atoms [1]

**(b)** n(H₂O) = 0.900 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0500 mol, so 0.100 mol of H atoms [1]

**(c)** C : H = 0.0400 : 0.100 = 2 : 5, so the empirical formula is C₂H₅ [1]; an alkane must fit CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, so the molecular formula is C₄H₁₀ [1]

**Total: 4**

*Examiner insight:* Each H₂O contains two hydrogen atoms. Forgetting to double gives C : H = 4 : 5, which is not a possible hydrocarbon ratio.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not subtracting the oxygen in an oxygen-containing fuel.
- Counting liquid water in a gas volume.
- Pressure in kPa in pV = nRT.
- Tracing NOₓ to the fuel rather than the air.
- SO₂ removal answers without "acidic" and "basic".
