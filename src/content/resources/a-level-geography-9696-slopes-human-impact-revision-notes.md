Condensed recall for **3.4 The human impact** on slope stability, and the strategies used to reduce mass movement. For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Slope stability | How resistant a slope is to failure |
| Grading | Reshaping a slope to a gentler angle |
| Pinning | Fixing rock in place with bolts or anchors |
| Netting | Mesh over a rock face to hold loose material |
| Afforestation | Planting trees, whose roots bind soil |
| Gabion | Wire basket of stones used as a retaining wall |

## How human activity reduces stability

- **Deforestation:** removes root strength and interception.
- **Building and loading:** extra weight at the top of a slope.
- **Cutting into slopes** for roads, quarries and building plots: steepens the slope and removes support.
- **Altering drainage:** leaking pipes, irrigation and blocked drains raise pore water pressure.
- **Mining, blasting and vibration.**
- **Overgrazing and ploughing downslope:** expose soil.

## How people can increase stability

- Vegetation: planting trees and grass.
- Drainage: surface and sub-surface drains, to lower pore water pressure.
- Slope regrading and terracing: lower stress.
- Retaining walls and gabions at the base: add support.
- Pinning and netting rock faces.
- Controlling construction and avoiding unstable sites through planning.

## Evaluating strategies

| Consideration | Point |
|---|---|
| Cost | Engineering works are expensive; vegetation is cheaper but slow |
| Effectiveness | Drainage addresses the cause; walls and nets address symptoms |
| Environment | Works change appearance and habitats |
| Scale | Small slopes can be treated; large landslide-prone areas often cannot |

## Required case study

Know one place where human activity affected slope stability: the activities, the effect on stability, and an evaluation of attempts to reduce mass movement.

## Exam traps

- Link each activity to stress or strength.
- Planting trees is not instant; mention time.
- Avoid unsupported statistics from memory.

## Self-test

1. Give two human causes of slope instability.
2. What is grading?
3. Why does drainage help?
4. Name a way to stabilise a rock face.
5. Why might afforestation be a limited solution alone?
6. What must the 3.4 case study evaluate?

**Answers:** 1. Deforestation, cutting into slopes, adding weight, altering drainage. 2. Reshaping a slope to a gentler angle. 3. It lowers pore water pressure and weight. 4. Pinning or netting. 5. Slow to establish and ineffective on deep or large failures. 6. Attempts to reduce mass movement.
