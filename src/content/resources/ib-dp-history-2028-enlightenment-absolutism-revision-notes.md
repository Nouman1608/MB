**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 3, Enlightenment and Absolutism (1600 to 1800), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. Questions about rulers refer to **one enlightened despot**; the model case is Catherine II of Russia. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-enlightenment-absolutism/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for a thorough evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement, not a list of reforms.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Enlightenment | Movement valuing reason, evidence and criticism over tradition |
| Absolutism | Rule in which the monarch holds supreme authority |
| Enlightened despot | A monarch who used absolute power for reforms inspired by Enlightenment ideas |
| Physiocrats | French thinkers who saw land and agriculture as the source of wealth |
| Separation of powers | Montesquieu's idea of dividing government functions |
| Social contract | Idea that government rests on an agreement with the governed |
| Toleration | Acceptance of different religious beliefs |

## Origins of Enlightenment ideas

- **Intellectual:** ancient writers; Renaissance humanism; Scientific Revolution (Copernicus, Galileo, Newton); Bacon and Descartes; Locke.
- **Political:** religious wars and civil conflict; Glorious Revolution; growing states needing new ideas of rule.
- **Religious:** Reformation encouraged individual judgement; toleration after wars of religion.
- **Individuals:** Montesquieu, Voltaire, Rousseau, Diderot, Kant.

## Developments in the era

| Area | Key points |
|---|---|
| Economy | Overseas trade; early industry in Britain; Physiocrats and Smith |
| Society | Growth of cities; salons, coffee houses; reading public |
| Science | Academies and societies; electricity, chemistry, inoculation |
| Agriculture | New crops, rotation, breeding, enclosure; serfdom remained in the east |

## Impact of the ideas

- **Political:** criticism of divine right and absolutism; rule of law; idea of the enlightened despot.
- **Social:** equality before the law, toleration, attacks on torture (Beccaria).
- **Economic:** challenge to monopolies and controls.
- **Women:** salon hosts and a few reformers (Wollstonecraft, de Gouges); most thinkers kept women in the home.

## Catherine II: the case

| Theme | Points |
|---|---|
| Legitimacy | Foreign-born usurper after 1762 coup; correspondence with Voltaire and Diderot; *Nakaz* |
| Centralization | Provincial Reform (1775); secularization of church land (1764) |
| Force | Pugachev crushed; Radishchev punished |
| Image | "Mother of the Fatherland"; patron of culture; statue of Peter the Great |
| Domestic | Commission without a code; Charter to the Nobility; Smolny; Hermitage; serfdom harsher |
| Foreign | Ottoman wars; Crimea; Polish partitions; Armed Neutrality; Alaska traders |

## Other despots at a glance

- **Frederick II:** first servant of the state; toleration; legal reform; kept army and nobility.
- **Joseph II:** toleration, ended personal serfdom; reforms too fast; revolts in Hungary and the Netherlands.
- **Charles III:** administrative and economic reform; expelled Jesuits (1767); Esquilache riots (1766).

## Historians' views

- Is "enlightened despotism" a real category or a label put on ordinary state-building?
- Did ideas drive reforms, or were rulers using them to strengthen states and fight wars?
- How much did peasants benefit? Often very little.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Enlightenment as one anti-religious doctrine.
- Mixing two despots in one essay when the question names "one".
- Describing reforms as intended when they were not carried out.
- Ignoring the gap between image and action.
- Forgetting women and the limits of equality.

## Self-test

1. Which book by Montesquieu set out the separation of powers?
2. Why was legitimacy a problem for Catherine II?
3. Give one effect of the Pugachev revolt.
4. Name one reform by Joseph II.
5. Give one reason the label "enlightened despot" is debated.

**Answers:** 1. *The Spirit of the Laws* (1748). 2. She was a foreign-born princess who took the throne by a coup against her husband. 3. She relied more on the nobility and strengthened local government. 4. The Edict of Toleration (1781) or ending personal serfdom. 5. Reforms may have served state power more than principle, and ideas may have been decoration.

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent did one enlightened despot you have studied put Enlightenment ideas into practice?"

1. **Thesis:** Catherine II adopted Enlightenment language and some reforms, but she acted mainly to strengthen her throne and the nobility, so ideas were applied only in part.
2. **Paragraph 1, ideas used:** Nakaz drawing on Montesquieu and Beccaria; letters to Voltaire and Diderot; schooling at Smolny; limited religious toleration.
3. **Paragraph 2, limits:** the Legislative Commission produced no code; serfdom grew harsher; Pugachev was crushed; Radishchev was punished.
4. **Paragraph 3, power and war:** Charter to the Nobility (1785), Provincial Reform (1775), and expansion in Crimea and Poland served the state.
5. **Perspective:** one view says ideas were decoration; another says they shaped real changes in law and administration.
6. **Judgement:** ideas shaped style and some policy, but power and the nobility set the limits.

## Quick comparison table

| Ruler | Strongest reform | Main limit |
|---|---|---|

## Frequently asked questions

**How many Enlightenment thinkers should I name?**
Three or four that you can link to arguments: Locke, Montesquieu, Voltaire and Rousseau are a safe core.

**Do I need to compare despots?**
Not unless asked; brief comparison can add depth if kept clear.
