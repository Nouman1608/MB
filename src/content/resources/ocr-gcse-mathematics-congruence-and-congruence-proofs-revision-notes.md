## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Congruent | exactly the same shape and size; can be moved onto each other by translation, rotation or reflection |
| Corresponding | matching sides or angles in congruent shapes; they are equal |
| Included angle | the angle between two named sides |
| Common side | a side shared by both triangles in a diagram |
| Hypotenuse | the longest side of a right-angled triangle, opposite the right angle |
| Isosceles triangle | two equal sides; the two angles opposite them are equal |

Congruent is not the same as similar. Similar shapes have the same shape but may differ in size. Congruent shapes are identical in both.

## The four conditions (9.02a)

| Name | Facts needed | Picture in words |
|---|---|---|
| SSS | three pairs of equal sides | all three lengths match |
| SAS | two equal side pairs, with the equal angle lying between those sides | angle sits inside the two sides |
| ASA | two pairs of equal angles and one pair of equal sides | side matches in the same position |
| RHS | right angle, equal hypotenuses, one more equal side | both triangles right-angled |

Not accepted as proof: AAA (same shape, size may differ) and SSA, meaning two sides and a non-included angle (can give two different triangles).

## Method in steps

1. Name the triangles with matching letters, for example ABC ≅ ZXY.
2. Write three facts about equal pairs, each with a reason.
3. Say which condition applies and that the triangles are congruent.
4. State what follows from corresponding sides or angles.

Reasons to quote:

| Situation | Reason |
|---|---|
| Crossing lines | vertically opposite angles are equal |
| Shared side | common side |
| Parallel lines | alternate angles are equal |
| Corner of a square or rectangle | angle of 90° |
| Midpoint | divides the line into two equal lengths |
| Bisector of an angle | splits the angle into two equal angles |
| Equal sides in an isosceles triangle | base angles are equal |

## Worked reminders

### Identify congruent triangles

Triangle 1 has sides 6 cm, 7 cm, 9 cm. Triangle 2 has sides 9 cm, 6 cm, 7 cm. All three sides match, so they are congruent (SSS). Triangle 3 has angles 50°, 60°, 70° and a 4 cm side, while triangle 4 has the same angles and a 9 cm side. They are not congruent, because AAA does not prove congruence.

### Isosceles triangles (9.02b)

If AB = AC, the angles at B and C are equal. To prove it, draw the bisector of angle A to meet BC at D. Triangles ABD and ACD have AB = AC, equal angles at A and the common side AD, so they are congruent (SAS). Then angle B = angle C.

Two uses:

```
Apex angle 56°  →  each base angle = (180° − 56°) ÷ 2 = 62°
Base angle 35°  →  apex angle = 180° − 2 × 35° = 110°
```

In an equilateral triangle all three sides are equal, so each pair of base angles is equal and every angle is 60°.

### Perpendicular from the apex (RHS)

In an isosceles triangle ABC with AB = AC, let M be the foot of the perpendicular from A to BC. The triangles AMB and AMC share AM, have equal hypotenuses AB and AC, and each contains a right angle. They are congruent by RHS, so BM = MC. This result is often used with Pythagoras.

```
Right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 cm and one side 6 cm
Other side² = 10² − 6² = 64, so the other side = 8 cm
Any other right-angled triangle with hypotenuse 10 cm and a side of 6 cm
is congruent to it (RHS).
```

### Algebra with congruence

If corresponding sides are 5x − 4 and 3x + 10, set them equal.

```
5x − 4 = 3x + 10
2x = 14
x = 7, so each side = 5(7) − 4 = 31
```

Always substitute back into both expressions to check they agree.

### Proof of equal diagonal segments

Lines PQ and RS cross at O. O is the midpoint of both lines. Triangles POR and QOS have PO = QO, equal vertically opposite angles at O, and RO = SO, so they are congruent (SAS). Hence PR = QS.

## Proof templates

Most proofs follow one of these patterns. Learn the pattern, then fit the facts to it.

| Pattern | Typical facts |
|---|---|
| SAS from crossing lines | two pairs of equal lengths from midpoints, plus vertically opposite angles |
| ASA in a parallelogram | two pairs of alternate angles from the parallel sides, plus a common diagonal |
| SSS in a kite or rhombus | pairs of equal sides, plus the diagonal as a common side |
| SAS in an isosceles triangle | equal sides, base angles equal, common base |
| RHS from perpendiculars | two right angles, equal hypotenuses such as radii, one equal side |

### A proof using a shared angle

Triangle ABC has AB = AC. D lies on AB and E lies on AC with AD = AE. Prove BE = CD.

```
Triangles ABE and ACD
AB = AC (given)
Angle A is common
AE = AD (given)
So ABE ≅ ACD (SAS), and BE = CD
```

A shared angle at the apex counts as a common angle, so write "angle A is common" as the reason.

## Must-know distinctions

- SAS needs the angle between the two sides. Two sides and a different angle is SSA, which fails.
- ASA needs matching positions for the equal side. Two angles fix the third, so a side in the same position on both triangles works.
- RHS needs a right angle in both triangles. It is not a general SSA rule.
- Congruent has equal sizes. Similar does not.
- Corresponding means in the same position, not in the same place on the page.
- To prove congruence you must give three facts. State a condition without the three facts and the proof is incomplete.

## Quick self-test

1. What does it mean to say two triangles are congruent?
2. Triangle 1 has sides 5 cm, 6 cm, 7 cm. Triangle 2 has sides 6 cm, 7 cm, 5 cm. Which condition shows they are congruent?
3. Why do three equal angles not prove two triangles are congruent?
4. In triangle ABC, AB = AC and angle A = 56°. Find angle B.
5. An isosceles triangle has a base angle of 35°. Find the angle at the apex.
6. Two right-angled triangles each have a hypotenuse of 10 cm and one other side of 6 cm. State the condition that shows they are congruent and find the third side.
7. Corresponding sides of two congruent triangles are 5x − 4 and 3x + 10. Find x and the side length.
8. In the statement ABC ≅ ZXY, which side of triangle ZXY is equal to BC?
9. An equilateral triangle has side 8 cm. Give the size of each angle and explain why.
10. Two triangles have sides 8 cm and 6 cm and a 40° angle, but in one triangle the 40° angle is between the sides and in the other it is not. Can you say they are congruent?

### Answers

1. They are exactly the same shape and size, so all corresponding sides and angles are equal.
2. SSS, because all three sides match.
3. They have the same shape but can be different sizes. A small and a large triangle can have the same angles.
4. Base angles are equal, so angle B = (180° − 56°) ÷ 2 = **62°**.
5. Angle at apex = 180° − 2 × 35° = **110°**.
6. **RHS** (right angle, hypotenuse, one side). The third side is √(10² − 6²) = **8 cm**.
7. 5x − 4 = 3x + 10 gives 2x = 14, so **x = 7** and each side = **31**.
8. B matches X and C matches Y, so BC = **XY**.
9. The three sides are equal, so by the base angles result all angles are equal. Each is 180° ÷ 3 = **60°**.
10. **No.** The angle must be between the sides for SAS. Two sides and a non-included angle does not prove congruence.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the congruence statement with letters in the wrong order.
- Quoting a condition with no supporting facts, or giving a fact with no reason.
- Using SSA or AAA as if they were accepted conditions.
- Calling a side "common" when it is not shared by both triangles.
- Assuming a right angle or a pair of parallel lines from the picture alone.
- Forgetting the final deduction, such as "so AC = BD" or "so angle B = angle C".
- Applying the isosceles angle result to the wrong pair of angles (the equal angles are opposite the equal sides).
- Setting corresponding sides equal in the wrong pairs when forming an equation in x.
- Giving a fully worked proof of a result the question only asked you to use.
