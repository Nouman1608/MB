These notes condense section 3.02, Standard form, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026): 3.02a interpreting, ordering and converting numbers in standard form, and 3.02b calculating with them, by calculator and without one. All of it is content for **both tiers**.

The [3.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-standard-form-ocr-gcse-maths/) explains each method with worked examples; the [3.02 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-standard-form-ocr-gcse-maths-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes. For standard form with indices and roots, see the [powers, roots and standard form revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-powers-roots-laws-of-indices-and-standard-form-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Standard form | A × 10ⁿ with 1 ≤ A < 10 and n an integer | 7.2 × 10⁷ |
| A (the coefficient) | The number between 1 and 10 | 7.2 |
| n (the index) | The power of 10 | 7 |
| Ordinary number | The number written out in full | 72 000 000 |
| Tidying | Adjusting A and n so that A is between 1 and 10 | 13.8 × 10⁷ → 1.38 × 10⁸ |

## Converting

```
ordinary → standard form:
  place the point after the first non-zero digit;
  count the places it moved: left → positive n, right → negative n
  72 000 000 = 7.2 × 10⁷        0.000508 = 5.08 × 10⁻⁴

standard form → ordinary:
  move the point n places: right for positive n, left for negative n
  6.3 × 10⁻⁵ = 0.000063

tidy a "nearly" standard form number:
  260 × 10⁻⁷ = 2.6 × 10² × 10⁻⁷ = 2.6 × 10⁻⁵
```

## Sizes at a glance

| Index | Size of the number |
|---|---|
| n ≥ 1 | 10 or more |
| n = 0 | Between 1 and 10 |
| n ≤ −1 | Between 0 and 1 |
| A larger power of 10 | A larger number (for positive numbers) |

## Ordering

```
1. Put every number into standard form.
2. Compare the powers of 10 first.
3. If the powers match, compare A.
4. Answer in the original forms.
```

Reminder: 3 × 10⁴ < 30 500 < 0.31 × 10⁵ < 2.9 × 10⁵, because 30 500 = 3.05 × 10⁴ and 0.31 × 10⁵ = 3.1 × 10⁴.

## The four operations without a calculator

| Operation | Method | Example |
|---|---|---|
| × | Multiply A values; add indices; tidy | (2.3 × 10⁴)(6 × 10³) = 13.8 × 10⁷ = 1.38 × 10⁸ |
| ÷ | Divide A values; subtract indices; tidy | (8.1 × 10⁷) ÷ (9 × 10⁻³) = 0.9 × 10¹⁰ = 9 × 10⁹ |
| Power | Raise A and 10ⁿ to the power; tidy | (4 × 10⁻²)² = 16 × 10⁻⁴ = 1.6 × 10⁻³ |
| + or − | Same power of 10 first; combine A; tidy | 3.08 × 10⁵ − 0.9 × 10⁵ = 2.18 × 10⁵ |

**Tidying rule:** when A is divided by 10, add 1 to the index; when A is multiplied by 10, subtract 1.

## With a calculator

- Enter each number with the ×10ˣ key, not by typing "× 10 ^".
- Bracket the whole numerator and the whole denominator.
- Write down the full display, then round.
- Rewrite the display as A × 10ⁿ: 8.14E5 means 8.14 × 10⁵.

## Context reminders

- "How many times larger" means divide.
- Convert units before calculating: 1 cm = 1 × 10⁻² m, 1 mm = 1 × 10⁻³ m, 1 km = 1 × 10³ m.
- When a question says "give your answer in standard form", an ordinary number does not answer it.

## Worked reminder

(4 × 10⁻³) ÷ (8 × 10⁵): divide the A values, 4 ÷ 8 = 0.5; subtract the indices, −3 − 5 = −8; tidy 0.5 × 10⁻⁸ to **5 × 10⁻⁹**. A check by size: a small number divided by a large one must give a very small answer, so a large negative index is expected.

## Exam traps

- **A out of range:** 13.8 × 10⁷ and 0.9 × 10¹⁰ are not in standard form.
- **Index moved the wrong way** when tidying.
- **Indices added in an addition sum:** 4 × 10³ + 2 × 10³ = 6 × 10³, not 6 × 10⁶.
- **Subtracting a negative index:** 10⁷ ÷ 10⁻³ = 10¹⁰.
- **Half a bracket squared:** (4 × 10⁻²)² needs 4² and (10⁻²)².
- **Calculator display copied** instead of written in standard form.
- **Comparing A first** when the indices differ.
- **Negative index read as a negative number:** 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ is positive.

## Quick self-test

1. Write 72 000 000 in standard form.
2. Write 0.000508 in standard form.
3. Write 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ as an ordinary number.
4. Write 260 × 10⁻⁷ in standard form.
5. Write 3 × 10⁴, 2.9 × 10⁵, 0.31 × 10⁵ and 30 500 in order, smallest first.
6. Work out (2.3 × 10⁴) × (6 × 10³) in standard form.
7. Work out (8.1 × 10⁷) ÷ (9 × 10⁻³) in standard form.
8. Work out (4 × 10⁻²)² in standard form.
9. Work out 3.08 × 10⁵ − 9 × 10⁴ in standard form.
10. Work out 5 × 10⁻³ + 7.8 × 10⁻² in standard form.
11. Work out (1.25 × 10⁹) ÷ (5 × 10⁵) in standard form.
12. Use a calculator to work out (3.7 × 10⁻⁴) × (2.2 × 10⁹).

3. **0.000063**.
4. **2.6 × 10⁻⁵**.
