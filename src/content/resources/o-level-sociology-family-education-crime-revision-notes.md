Condensed recall notes for **Paper 2 Family, Education and Crime**,
Cambridge O Level Sociology (2251), 2025–2027 series — identical
subject-content structure to sibling Cambridge IGCSE Sociology (0495).
Use these alongside the full study guide on Paper 2 for explanation and
evidence; these notes are for fast recall of the paper's exam format
and content in the final stretch of revision.

## Paper format at a glance

- Written paper, **1 hour 45 minutes**, **80 marks**, weighted **50%**
  of the qualification.
- **Three questions** set, one per topic (Family, Education, and Crime,
  deviance and social control) — candidates answer **two of the
  three**.
- Every question has the **same six-part structure (a–f)**, each worth
  **40 marks** total.
- Parts (a) and (b): short answers testing knowledge and understanding
  of terms and ideas.
- Parts further into the question build up to part (e), which requires
  discussion of a sociological issue or view in a structured, evidenced
  way (assessing AO1, AO2, AO3), and part (f), a short essay requiring
  reasoned arguments on both sides of a debate (also AO1, AO2, AO3).

## Topic 4: Family — key recall points

- **Family structures**: nuclear, extended (modified, horizontal,
  vertical/beanpole), reconstituted, lone parent, empty nest, childless
  — plus variation by culture, social class and ethnicity.
- **Sociological views on diversity**: New Right (diversity =
  dysfunction, favours the nuclear family) versus Postmodernist
  (diversity = positive, evidence of choice).
- **Alternatives to the family**: single-person households, shared
  households, friends as family.
- **Perspectives on the family's role** — one mechanism per view:
  functionalism's "warm bath" theory and loss-of-functions debate;
  Marxism's family-as-unit-of-consumption and ideological control;
  feminism's joint versus segregated conjugal roles and domestic
  division of labour.
- **Changing family life**: demographic drivers (birth rates, life
  expectancy, ageing population), industrialisation/urbanisation,
  changing patterns in marriage/divorce/cohabitation, and changing
  conjugal, child and grandparent roles (e.g. sandwich generation,
  boomerang children).

## Topic 5: Education — key recall points

- **Formal vs informal education**, and the **hidden curriculum**
  (norms, values, punctuality, conformity, gender roles taught without
  being explicitly named as lessons).
- **Types of schools** (state, private, selective, single-sex,
  co-educational, faith, international) and **alternative approaches**
  (online learning, homeschooling, unschooling, vocational, progressive).
- **Perspectives on education's functions** — functionalism's
  meritocracy and role allocation; Marxism's reproduction of class
  inequality via cultural capital and the "myth of meritocracy";
  feminism's patriarchal culture and gendered subject choice.
- **Explaining achievement differences** by social class (material,
  cultural/cultural capital, in-school factors like labelling and
  pro-/anti-school subcultures), ethnicity, gender, and global
  differences.

## Topic 6: Crime, deviance and social control — key recall points

- **Crime vs deviance**: crime breaks the law, deviance breaks social
  norms; both are **relative** across societies, cultures and time.
- **Formal social control**: police, courts, armed forces, government,
  penal system.
- **Informal social control**: family, education, workplace, peer
  group, media, religion.
- **Types of crime**: violent, property, white-collar/corporate,
  expressive, instrumental, gang, green.
- Effectiveness of control agencies is assessed comparatively — be
  ready to weigh a formal method against an informal one for a given
  scenario.

## Command word reminders

Parts (a) and (b) reward precise, concise recall — a one- or
two-sentence factual answer is enough, and padding with unrequested
extra detail wastes time without earning extra marks. From part (c)
onward, examiners expect **named sociological concepts** used
precisely (e.g. "ascribed status" rather than "the status you're born
with"), since vague paraphrasing of a concept is marked down compared
with using its correct term. Part (e)'s discussion answers should be
structured around at least two perspectives or pieces of evidence
before reaching a conclusion, and part (f)'s essay must show *both*
sides of the named debate — a one-sided essay, however well argued,
cannot reach the top mark band because AO2 explicitly requires
weighing alternative views.

## Quick recall checklist

- Can I state the New Right and Postmodernist positions on family
  diversity without swapping them?
- Do I have one concrete mechanism (not just a label) for each
  perspective — functionalist, Marxist, feminist — on both family and
  education?
- Can I explain the hidden curriculum with an example beyond the
  syllabus's own?
- Can I correctly sort an achievement-difference explanation as
  material, cultural or in-school?
- Do I know the difference between crime and deviance, and can I give
  an example of something deviant but not criminal?
- Can I name all seven crime types and place an example crime into the
  correct category?

## Related-topic connections worth remembering

Paper 2's three topics are not isolated from Paper 1's toolkit: a
question on Family or Education can still ask candidates to suggest or
evaluate a research method (from Paper 1's Research Methods topic) for
studying a specific claim, and questions on Crime routinely draw on the
Identity and Inequality content from Paper 1 — for example, explaining
crime patterns using social class or ethnicity inequality. Keeping this
cross-paper link in mind while revising Paper 2 avoids being caught out
by a question that blends the two papers' content.

## How to approach it

Since you only answer two of the three questions, decide during
revision which two topics you will prepare in full depth, while keeping
enough working knowledge of the third topic to adapt if the paper's
specific parts (a)–(f) favour it in a given series. Within your chosen
topics, practise part (e) and part (f) answers specifically — these
carry the heaviest marks and require a structured, evidenced discussion
or a genuinely two-sided essay, not a one-sided description, so timed
practice on these two part-types pays off more than further factual
recall once the basic content above is secure.
