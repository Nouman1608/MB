## Section A

**1.** State the equation used to calculate an Rf value. **[1]**

**2.** In a chromatogram, the solvent front is 10.0 cm from the baseline. Which distance moved by a spot gives an Rf value of 0.35?

A: 0.35 cm

B: 2.9 cm

C: 3.5 cm

D: 6.5 cm

**[1]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why the baseline of a chromatogram must be drawn in pencil and placed above the level of the solvent. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by a **locating agent**. **[1]**

**5.** A pure substance and an impure substance are run on the same chromatogram. State how the results for the two substances differ. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why an Rf value has no units. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student uses paper chromatography to investigate a black ink, K. Four known dyes, P, Q, R and S, are run on the same paper. The solvent front is 6.4 cm above the baseline.

| Sample | Distance of spot(s) from baseline / cm |
|---|---|
| K | 1.6, 3.2 and 4.8 |
| P | 1.6 |
| Q | 2.4 |
| R | 3.2 |
| S | 5.6 |

**(a)** State how many substances K contains. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the known dyes present in K. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the Rf value of the substance in K that does not match any of the known dyes. **[2]**

**8.** Describe how to carry out paper chromatography to separate the dyes in a sample of food colouring. **[5]**

**9.** A mixture of two amino acids is colourless.

**(a)** Explain why a locating agent is needed when this mixture is separated by chromatography. **[1]**

**(b)** State when, during the method, the locating agent is applied. **[1]**

**10.** A student repeats a chromatography experiment with the same dye but uses ethanol instead of water. The dye's spot moves 2.1 cm in water when the solvent front moves 7.0 cm. In ethanol, the solvent front moves 6.0 cm and the dye moves 4.5 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of the dye in each solvent. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why Rf values found in different solvents cannot be compared to identify a substance. **[1]**

**11.** A green plant extract is spotted on chromatography paper and run in water. After the run, a single green spot remains on the baseline.

**(a)** Suggest why the spot did not move. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest what the student should do to separate the substances in the extract. **[1]**

**(c)** The student concludes that the extract is pure because there is only one spot. Explain why this conclusion is not valid. **[2]**

**12.** Dye T has an Rf value of 0.80 in a solvent. The solvent front travels 7.5 cm. Calculate the distance travelled by dye T. **[2]**

**13.** A student's chromatogram gives a spot that is a smear stretching from 1.0 cm to 4.0 cm above the baseline. Suggest one cause of this result and how to prevent it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Rf = distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* 0.35 × 10.0 = 3.5 cm. A is the result of forgetting to multiply by the solvent distance.

**3.** Pencil (graphite) does not dissolve in the solvent / ink would separate and give extra spots [1]. Above the solvent so that the samples do not dissolve into the solvent in the beaker [1]. **[2]**

**4.** A substance applied (sprayed) onto a chromatogram to make colourless spots visible [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The pure substance gives one spot [1]; the impure substance gives two or more spots [1]. **[2]**

**6.** It is a ratio of two distances measured in the same unit, so the units cancel [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Three [1]

**(b)** P [1] and R [1]

**(c)** 4.8 ÷ 6.4 [1] = 0.75 [1]

**[5]**

**8.** Five marks from:

- draw a baseline in pencil near the bottom of the chromatography paper [1]
- place a small spot of the food colouring on the baseline (dry and re-spot) [1]
- stand the paper in a beaker of water / suitable solvent with the solvent below the baseline [1]
- cover with a lid and leave the solvent to rise up the paper [1]
- remove before the solvent reaches the top and mark the solvent front [1]
- dry the paper; the dyes appear as separate spots at different heights (max 5)

**[5]**

**9. (a)** The amino acids are colourless, so their spots cannot be seen without it [1].

**(b)** After the run, when the chromatogram has been dried [1].

**[2]**

**10. (a)** Water: 2.1 ÷ 7.0 = 0.30 [1]. Ethanol: 4.5 ÷ 6.0 = 0.75 [1].

**(b)** The Rf value of a substance depends on the solvent, so the same substance has different Rf values in different solvents [1].

**[3]**

**11. (a)** The substances are insoluble in water [1].

**(b)** Use a different solvent in which the substances dissolve (such as ethanol) [1].

**(c)** Nothing has moved, so no separation has taken place [1]; the spot could contain several substances that would separate in a suitable solvent [1].

**[4]**

**12.** distance = Rf × solvent distance = 0.80 × 7.5 [1] = 6.0 cm [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Too much sample / the spot was too large or too concentrated [1]; use a small spot, letting it dry between applications [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the Rf equation upside down.
- Measuring distances from the bottom of the paper.
- Identifying dyes by colour instead of by height or Rf.
- Forgetting to mark the solvent front.
- Describing the use of a locating agent without saying it makes spots visible.
- Treating a spot on the baseline as evidence of purity.
