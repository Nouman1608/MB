| Equation | Units | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| W = F d = ΔE | J = N × m | Core |
| P = W / t | W = J/s | Core |
| P = ΔE / t | W = J/s | Core |
| Ek = ½ m v² | J (kg, m/s) | Extended only |
| ΔEp = m g Δh | J (kg, N/kg, m) | Extended only |
| efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy input (× 100%) | no unit, or % | Extended only |
| efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input (× 100%) | no unit, or % | Extended only |

Useful conversions: 1 kJ = 1000 J; 1 MJ = 10⁶ J; 1 kW = 1000 W; minutes × 60 = s.

## Energy (1.7.1)

**Seven stores:** kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic (strain), nuclear, electrostatic, internal (thermal).

**Four ways to transfer energy between stores:**

1. forces (mechanical work done);
2. electrical currents (electrical work done);
3. heating;
4. waves: electromagnetic, sound and others.

**Conservation of energy:** energy is not created or destroyed; total energy out = total energy in. Wasted energy usually ends up in the internal store of the surroundings.

**Flow diagrams (Core):** boxes for stores, arrows for transfers.

*(Extended only)* **Sankey diagrams:** arrow widths in proportion to energy; useful output continues straight on, wasted energy branches off; widths out add up to width in. Multi-stage chains: apply conservation at each stage.

**Quick energy facts (Extended only)**

- Doubling speed → Ek × 4.
- Doubling mass → Ek × 2.
- Falling with no air resistance: mgΔh = ½mv², so v = √(2gΔh), whatever the mass.

## Work (1.7.2)

- Mechanical or electrical work done = energy transferred.
- W = Fd, with d the distance moved **in the direction of the force**.
- Lifting at steady speed: F = weight = mg, so W = mgΔh.
- A force at right angles to the motion does no work.

## Energy resources (1.7.3)

**Turbine–generator chain**

- Boiler → steam → turbine → generator: fossil fuels, biofuels, nuclear, geothermal.
- Water or air turns the turbine directly: hydroelectric, tides, waves, wind.
- No turbine: solar cells (light → electricity).
- Solar panels heat water; they generate no electricity.

**Five comparison criteria:** renewability, availability, reliability, scale, environmental impact.

| Resource | Renewable? | Reliable? | One big disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fossil fuels | no | yes | carbon dioxide; will run out |
| Biofuels | yes | yes | large areas of land |
| Nuclear | no | yes | long-lived radioactive waste |
| Hydroelectric | yes | yes | floods valleys and habitats |
| Tidal | yes | predictable | few suitable sites |
| Wave | yes | no | small scale |
| Geothermal | yes | yes | few suitable places |
| Wind | yes | no | output depends on the weather |
| Solar cells | yes | no (none at night) | large area for large output |

**Efficiency (Core):** the fraction of input energy transferred usefully; always less than 100%.

**Extended only**

- The Sun is the main source of energy for all resources **except geothermal, nuclear and tidal**.
- The Sun releases energy by **nuclear fusion**.
- Research is being carried out into large-scale electricity generation from **fusion**.

## Power (1.7.4)

- Power: **work done per unit time** or **energy transferred per unit time**.
- 1 W = 1 J/s.
- Same job done faster → same work, more power.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Energy store | Way energy is held: kinetic, gravitational potential, chemical, elastic, nuclear, electrostatic, internal |
| Work done | Energy transferred by a force or an electrical current |
| Power | Work done (or energy transferred) per unit time |
| Renewable | Will not run out; replaced naturally |
| Turbine | Machine turned by steam, water or air that drives a generator |
| Efficiency | Useful output ÷ total input |
| Sankey diagram (Extended) | Energy diagram with arrow widths in proportion to energy |

## Exam traps

- "Energy is lost": say **where** it went (internal store of surroundings, by heating or sound).
- Using mass (kg) for force in W = Fd.
- Writing kinetic energy as ½mv (no square).
- Power from energy and minutes without converting to seconds.
- Mixing solar cells and solar panels.
- Advantages that are vague ("cheap", "clean") with no reason.
- *(Extended)* Efficiency over 100%: you divided the wrong way.
- *(Extended)* Listing tidal energy as coming from the Sun.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the store of energy in a stretched spring.
2. State the principle of conservation of energy.
3. A force of 80 N pushes a trolley 2.5 m. Find the work done.
4. A 1500 W hairdryer runs for 4.0 minutes. Find the energy transferred.
5. A motor does 6000 J of work in 30 s. Find its power.
6. Name the three parts of a coal-fired power station that the energy passes through after the coal is burned.
7. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of tidal energy.
8. *(Extended)* Find the kinetic energy of a 0.20 kg ball moving at 15 m/s.
9. *(Extended)* Find the gain in gravitational potential energy of a 50 kg climber who climbs 120 m.
10. *(Extended)* A lamp takes 60 J of electrical energy and gives out 12 J as light. Find its efficiency.
11. *(Extended)* Name the three energy resources that do not get their energy from the Sun.
12. *(Extended)* A 2.0 kg stone is dropped from 20 m. Ignoring air resistance, find its speed just before it lands.

### Answers

1. **Elastic (strain)** store.
2. Energy cannot be **created or destroyed**; it is transferred between stores, and the total stays the same.
3. 80 × 2.5 = **200 J**.
4. 1500 × 240 = **360 000 J** (360 kJ).
5. 6000 ÷ 30 = **200 W**.
6. **Boiler, turbine, generator.**
7. Advantage: renewable / predictable / no carbon dioxide in operation. Disadvantage: few suitable estuaries / affects habitats / output varies with the tides.
8. ½ × 0.20 × 15² = **22.5 J**.
9. 50 × 9.8 × 120 = **58 800 J**.
10. 12 ÷ 60 × 100% = **20%**.
11. **Geothermal, nuclear, tidal.**
12. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 20) = √392 = **19.8 m/s** (20 m/s).
