These notes condense content item 2.3.4 The sales process from Topic 2.3 Making operational decisions in Theme 2 of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples are in the [sales process study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-sales-process/) and exam-style questions in the [sales process practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-sales-process-practice/). Businesses and figures are invented.

## The specification in one view

- **The sales process:** product knowledge; speed and efficiency of service; customer engagement; responses to customer feedback; post-sales service.
- **The importance to businesses of providing good customer service.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sales process | The stages a business goes through to make a sale and keep the customer |
| Product knowledge | Staff understanding of what they sell, so they can advise customers |
| Speed of service | How quickly customers are served or receive their order |
| Efficiency of service | Serving customers accurately, without errors or wasted effort |
| Customer engagement | Building a relationship with customers through how the business interacts with them |
| Customer feedback | Customers' views, from surveys, reviews, complaints and comments |
| Post-sales service | Support after purchase: delivery, installation, guarantees, repairs, returns, help lines |
| Customer service | How well a business looks after its customers before, during and after a sale |
| Conversion rate | Sales ÷ enquiries or visitors × 100 (standard form) |

## The five parts at a glance

| Part | Example | Benefit to the business |
|---|---|---|
| Product knowledge | Bike shop staff advising on frame size | More sales, fewer returns, trust |
| Speed and efficiency | Self-service tills; accurate orders | Shorter queues, fewer mistakes, more customers served |
| Customer engagement | Greeting by name; personal emails; loyalty schemes | Customers feel valued and return |
| Responses to feedback | Replying to reviews; changing menus after comments | Problems fixed; unhappy customers won back |
| Post-sales service | Warranties, repairs, easy returns | Confidence to buy; loyalty; extra income from service plans |

Remember the specification says **responses** to feedback: acting on it, not just collecting it.

## Why good customer service matters

1. **Repeat custom:** loyal customers buy regularly and cost less to keep than new ones cost to win.
2. **Word of mouth and reviews:** recommendations bring new customers cheaply.
3. **Competitive advantage:** service can set a business apart when products and prices are similar.
4. **Premium prices:** customers will pay more for excellent service.
5. **Lower costs:** fewer complaints, returns and mistakes.

Poor service reverses all five, and bad reviews spread quickly online.

**Chain to learn.** Staff know the product → customers get the right item first time → fewer returns and complaints → positive reviews → more new customers at low promotion cost.

## When each part matters most

- **Product knowledge:** expensive, technical or unfamiliar products.
- **Speed:** convenience purchases, such as lunch, fuel or takeaways.
- **Engagement:** businesses relying on regular customers, such as hairdressers or gyms.
- **Feedback:** any business, but especially those reviewed online.
- **Post-sales service:** durable, complex products, such as cars, boilers or laptops.

## Useful calculations

- Conversion rate = sales ÷ enquiries × 100.
- Percentage satisfied = customers rating highly ÷ total responses × 100.
- Mean waiting time = total of times ÷ number of customers.
- Extra contribution from better service = extra sales × contribution per sale.

**Quick calculation.** Kingsway Opticians had 800 eye tests and 360 spectacle sales: conversion = 360 ÷ 800 × 100 = **45%**. After staff training, 416 sales from 800 tests = **52%**. With £60 contribution per sale, the extra 56 sales add £3,360.

## Exam traps

- Listing the five parts without linking them to sales or loyalty.
- Saying "collect feedback" rather than respond to it.
- Treating good service as important for its own sake, without a business benefit.
- Ignoring the cost of better service, such as training or extra staff.
- Forgetting post-sales service for expensive products.

## Self-test

1. Name the five parts of the sales process in the specification.
2. Define post-sales service.
3. Why is product knowledge especially important when selling laptops?
4. Give one way technology can improve speed of service.
5. What is the difference between speed and efficiency of service?
6. Give one example of customer engagement.
7. Why is it important to respond to negative online reviews?
8. A shop has 1,200 enquiries and 300 sales. Calculate the conversion rate.
9. Waiting times for five customers are 2, 5, 3, 7 and 3 minutes. Calculate the mean.
10. Give two reasons why good customer service is important to a business.
11. How can good customer service help a small business compete with a large one?
12. A warranty plan costs £30 to provide and sells for £50. 250 customers buy it. Calculate the contribution.

### Answers

1. Product knowledge; speed and efficiency of service; customer engagement; responses to customer feedback; post-sales service.
2. Support given to customers after they have bought, such as delivery, installation, guarantees, repairs or help lines.
3. Laptops are technical and expensive, so customers need advice to choose the right one and staff who can answer questions build trust.
4. Self-service tills, online ordering, booking apps or delivery tracking.
5. Speed is how quickly customers are served; efficiency is serving them accurately without mistakes or wasted effort.
6. Greeting regular customers by name, personalised emails, replying to social media comments or a loyalty scheme.
7. Other customers read the reply; a polite response that fixes the problem shows the business cares and can win the customer back.
8. 300 ÷ 1,200 × 100 = **25%**.
9. (2 + 5 + 3 + 7 + 3) ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5 = **4 minutes**.
10. Any two: repeat custom; positive reviews and recommendations; competitive advantage; premium prices; fewer complaints and returns.
11. It can offer personal, expert service that a large business may not, giving customers a reason to pay more or stay loyal.
12. 250 × (£50 − £30) = **£5,000**.
