subtopic 29.3 Shapes of aromatic organic molecules; σ and π bonds.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **sp² hybridisation** | one s and two p orbitals mix to give three equivalent hybrid orbitals, 120° apart in a plane |
| **σ bond** | formed by head-on (end-to-end) overlap of orbitals, along the line between the nuclei |
| **π bond** | formed by sideways overlap of p orbitals, above and below the line between the nuclei |
| **Delocalised π system** | π electrons spread over more than two atoms, here round the whole ring |
| **Aromatic** | containing a benzene ring (or similar planar ring) with a delocalised π system |

## Benzene in five lines

1. Each C is **sp²**: three hybrid orbitals in a plane at **120°**, plus one **unhybridised p orbital**
   perpendicular to the plane.
2. Each C forms **3 σ bonds**: 2 to C, 1 to H. Total: **12 σ bonds** (6 C–C, 6 C–H).
3. All 12 atoms are in **one plane**: a **regular hexagon**.
4. Each p orbital **overlaps sideways with both neighbours**.
5. Six p electrons form **one delocalised π system**, as rings of electron density **above and below** the
   plane.

## Evidence summary

| Observation | Explained by delocalisation |
|---|---|
| all C–C bonds 0.139 nm (C–C 0.154, C=C 0.134) | no separate single and double bonds |
| hydrogenation releases about 208 kJ mol⁻¹, not the 360 expected (3 × 120) | about 152 kJ mol⁻¹ extra stability |
| substitution, not addition, with electrophiles | addition would destroy the delocalised system |

## Atom-by-atom shapes

| Atom in an aromatic molecule | σ bonds | Hybridisation | Angles | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ring carbon | 3 | sp² | 120° | every benzene carbon |
| carbon of C=O | 3 (+1 π) | sp² | 120° | –COOH in benzoic acid, C=O in phenylethanone |
| side-chain C with 4 single bonds | 4 | sp³ | 109.5° | –CH₃ in methylbenzene |
| C≡N carbon | 2 (+2 π) | sp | 180° | –CN in a nitrile |

**Lone pairs next to the ring:** O in phenol, N in phenylamine and Cl in chlorobenzene each have a lone pair in a
p orbital that overlaps with the ring's π system. Effects: activated ring (phenol, phenylamine), stronger C–Cl
bond (chlorobenzene), stabilised phenoxide ion.

**Naphthalene, C₁₀H₈:** two fused rings, all ten carbons sp², planar, ten delocalised p electrons.

## Counting bonds

- Every bond between two atoms has **one σ bond**.
- Double bond = 1 σ + 1 π; triple bond = 1 σ + 2 π.
- Quick check, one ring: **σ bonds = number of atoms**. No rings: σ bonds = atoms − 1. Each extra ring adds 1.
- Benzene ring π electrons: **one delocalised system of 6 electrons**, described rather than counted as three
  π bonds.

| Molecule | Atoms | σ bonds | Localised π | Delocalised ring |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| benzene, C₆H₆ | 12 | 12 | 0 | 6 electrons |
| methylbenzene, C₇H₈ | 15 | 15 | 0 | 6 electrons |
| benzoic acid, C₇H₆O₂ | 15 | 15 | 1 (C=O) | 6 electrons |
| phenylethanone, C₈H₈O | 17 | 17 | 1 (C=O) | 6 electrons |

## Exam traps

- Overlap is with **both** neighbouring p orbitals, not one.
- π density is **above and below** the plane, not in it.
- The ring is **planar** because p orbitals must be **parallel** to overlap.
- **Side-chain sp³ carbons** are tetrahedral (109.5°) even though the ring is flat.
- **Cyclohexane** (from hydrogenating benzene) is **sp³**, not planar.
- Do **not** describe benzene with alternating single and double bonds in a shape answer.
- Give the **angle** (120°) and the word **planar**; both are usually credited.

## Self-test

1. What orbitals mix to form sp² hybrids, and what is left over?
2. State the H–C–C bond angle in benzene.
3. How many σ bonds are there in benzene?
4. Explain why the C–C bonds in benzene are all the same length.
5. Where is the π electron density in benzene located?
6. Why must benzene be planar for delocalisation to occur?
7. State the hybridisation and bond angle of the CH₃ carbon in methylbenzene.
8. How many σ bonds are there in phenol, C₆H₅OH?
9. Explain why the C–Cl bond in chlorobenzene is shorter than in chloroethane.
10. Give the hybridisation of the carbonyl carbon in benzoic acid.
11. What is the molecular formula of naphthalene, and how many p electrons are delocalised?
12. Explain why benzene's enthalpy change of hydrogenation is less exothermic than three times that of
    cyclohexene.

**Answers:**

1. One 2s and two 2p orbitals; one 2p orbital remains unhybridised.
2. 120°.
3. 12 (6 C–C and 6 C–H).
4. The six p electrons are delocalised evenly over all six carbons, so every C–C bond has the same bond order,
   between a single and a double bond.
5. In two ring-shaped regions, above and below the plane of the ring.
6. Sideways overlap needs the p orbitals to be parallel, which only happens if all ring carbons lie in one plane.
7. sp³; 109.5°.
8. 13 atoms, one ring: 13 σ bonds (6 C–C, 5 C–H, 1 C–O, 1 O–H).
9. A lone pair on Cl overlaps with the ring's π system, giving the C–Cl bond partial double-bond character.
10. sp² (three σ bonds and one π bond; 120°).
11. C₁₀H₈; ten.
12. Delocalisation makes benzene more stable (lower in energy) than a structure with three localised C=C
    bonds, so less energy is released when it is hydrogenated to cyclohexane.
