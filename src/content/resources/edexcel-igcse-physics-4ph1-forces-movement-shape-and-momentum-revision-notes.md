Condensed notes on sub-topic **1(c) Forces, movement, shape and momentum**
(statements 1.11 to 1.33) of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics
(4PH1), Issue 4. Statements 1.25P to 1.33P are Physics-only. For full
explanations and worked examples, use the
[Forces, movement, shape and momentum study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-forces-movement-shape-and-momentum/).

## Equations

```
F = m × a                       (know and use; F is the unbalanced force)
W = m × g                       (know and use; g = 10 N/kg)
stopping distance = thinking distance + braking distance
p = m × v                       (P, know and use)
F = (mv − mu) / t               (P, use)
moment = F × perpendicular distance from pivot     (P, know and use)
sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments   (P, equilibrium)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| scalar | magnitude only (mass, speed, distance, energy, time) |
| vector | magnitude and direction (force, velocity, acceleration, momentum, displacement) |
| resultant force | single force with the same effect as all the forces acting |
| friction | force opposing motion between surfaces; drag in air or water |
| thinking distance | distance travelled during the driver's reaction time |
| braking distance | distance travelled while the brakes are applied |
| terminal velocity | constant velocity reached when drag equals weight |
| Hooke's law | extension proportional to force in the initial linear region |
| elastic behaviour | recovering original shape when deforming forces are removed |
| momentum (P) | mass × velocity, kg m/s, a vector |
| centre of gravity (P) | point through which the weight acts |
| principle of moments (P) | in equilibrium, clockwise moments = anticlockwise moments |

## Forces in brief

- Forces can change speed, shape or direction. Types include
  gravitational, electrostatic, magnetic, friction, drag, tension and
  normal contact force.
- Along a line: take one direction as positive, add with signs.
- Balanced forces: zero resultant, so at rest or constant velocity.

## Stopping distance factors

| Thinking distance increases with | Braking distance increases with |
|---|---|
| greater speed | greater speed |
| longer reaction time: tiredness, alcohol, drugs, distraction | greater mass |
| | wet or icy road, worn tyres, worn brakes |

Thinking distance = speed × reaction time.

## Terminal velocity sequence

Weight > drag, so accelerates → speed rises, drag rises → resultant
force falls, acceleration falls → drag = weight, resultant zero → constant
terminal velocity. Parachute opens: drag > weight, decelerates to a new,
lower terminal velocity.

## Springs, wires and rubber bands (practical 1.22)

- Measure original length; add equal loads; extension = new length −
  original length.
- Pointer and eye level to avoid parallax; eye protection.
- Spring and wire: straight line through the origin, then curves beyond
  the limit of proportionality.
- Rubber band: curved for most of the range; elastic but not Hooke's law.
- Metal wire: long wire needed because the extension is tiny.

## Momentum and safety (P)

- Total momentum before = total momentum after, if no external forces.
- Things at rest that push apart: momenta after are equal and opposite.
- Crumple zones, airbags, seat belts, helmets and crash mats increase the
  stopping time, so for the same change in momentum the force is smaller.

## Newton's third law (P)

Equal, opposite, same type, acting on **different** bodies. Example: a
swimmer pushes water backwards; the water pushes the swimmer forwards.

## Moments and beams (P)

- Use perpendicular distance in metres.
- Beam on two end supports: take moments about one support to find the
  other support's force; then upward forces = downward forces.
- The support nearer the load carries more of it.

## Exam traps

- Using the driving force instead of the resultant force in F = m × a.
- Saying mass affects thinking distance (it does not).
- "The parachute makes the skydiver go up": no, it reduces the downward
  velocity.
- Losing the minus sign of a velocity in the opposite direction.
- Weight and normal contact force on a book are not a third-law pair.
- Measuring length instead of extension.
- Forgetting to convert grams to kg before W = m × g (100 g = 0.10 kg,
  weight 1.0 N).

## Self-test

1. Name two vector quantities and two scalar quantities.
2. A 1200 kg car has a driving force of 3000 N and drag of 1200 N.
   Calculate its acceleration.
3. State two factors that affect braking distance but not thinking
   distance.
4. A driver's reaction time is 0.60 s. Calculate the thinking distance at
   25 m/s.
5. Why does a falling object reach terminal velocity?
6. What is meant by elastic behaviour?
7. Why must a metal wire be long in the extension practical?
8. Calculate the momentum of a 0.15 kg ball moving at 30 m/s.
9. A 1.0 kg trolley at 3.0 m/s hits and sticks to a stationary 2.0 kg
   trolley. Calculate their velocity afterwards.
10. A 60 kg gymnast lands at 6.0 m/s and stops in 0.40 s. Calculate the
    average force.
11. A 4.0 m light plank is supported at both ends. A 500 N child stands
    1.0 m from end X. Calculate the upward force at the other end, Y.
12. State Newton's third law.

## Answers

1. Vectors: any two of force, velocity, acceleration, momentum,
   displacement. Scalars: any two of mass, speed, distance, time, energy.
2. Resultant = 3000 − 1200 = 1800 N; a = 1800 ÷ 1200 = 1.5 m/s².
3. Any two: mass of the vehicle, road condition (wet or icy), condition of
   tyres, condition of brakes.
4. 25 × 0.60 = 15 m.
5. Drag increases with speed until it equals the weight; the resultant
   force is then zero, so there is no acceleration and the velocity stays
   constant.
6. The ability of a material to recover its original shape after the
   forces causing deformation are removed.
7. The extension of a metal wire is very small, so a long wire gives an
   extension large enough to measure accurately.
8. p = 0.15 × 30 = 4.5 kg m/s.
9. 1.0 × 3.0 = 3.0 kg m/s before; 3.0 = 3.0 × v, so v = 1.0 m/s.
10. F = (0 − 60 × 6.0) ÷ 0.40 = −900 N, so 900 N.
11. Moments about X: R_Y × 4.0 = 500 × 1.0, so R_Y = 125 N (and the force
    at X is 375 N).
12. If body A exerts a force on body B, then B exerts a force on A that is
    equal in size, opposite in direction and of the same type.
