## Section A

**1.** Which of the following is an injection into the circular flow of income?

A: A household's purchase of imported shoes

B: A firm's purchase of new machinery from a domestic producer

C: An increase in income tax paid by workers

D: A rise in households' bank deposits

**[1]**

**2.** In an open economy, national income is in equilibrium when

A: saving equals investment

B: government spending equals taxation

C: exports equal imports

D: saving plus taxation plus imports equals investment plus government spending plus exports

**[1]**

**3.** Distinguish between a closed economy and an open economy. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why saving is a leakage from the circular flow. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why an unemployment benefit payment is not counted as an injection. **[2]**

**6.** Identify the real flow and the money flow between households and firms in the factor market. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Country P has the following flows.

| Saving | Taxation | Imports | Investment | Government spending | Exports |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 85 | 150 | 120 | 95 | 170 | 100 |

**(a)** Calculate total injections and total leakages. **[2]**

**(b)** State and explain what will happen to national income. **[3]**

**(c)** Investment falls by 10. State the new position of the economy. **[1]**

**8.** Classify each transaction as an injection, a leakage or neither, giving a reason.

**(a)** An overseas airline buys aircraft parts from a factory in the country. **[2]**

**(b)** A household pays a sales tax when buying a fridge. **[2]**

**(c)** A firm pays wages to its workers. **[2]**

**9.** Country Q is in equilibrium. Households become worried about the future and save a larger share of their income.

**(a)** Explain the effect on national income, describing how firms respond. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why national income eventually stops falling. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how a rise in government spending on new hospitals affects the circular flow of income in an economy with spare capacity. **[4]**

**11.** In Country R, saving exceeds investment by 20 and exports exceed imports by 15, while the government's spending is 5 more than its tax revenue. Is Country R in equilibrium? Explain your answer. **[3]**

**12.** "An economy in equilibrium in the circular flow has no economic problems." Comment on this statement. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Investment is spending on capital goods that does not come from household consumption.

**2.** **D** [1]. Only total leakages and total injections need to be equal.

**3.** A closed economy has **no international trade** [1]; an open economy **trades with the rest of the world**, so exports and imports enter the flow [1].

**4.** Saving is income that is **not spent** on goods and services [1], so it does **not return to firms** as revenue and is withdrawn from the flow of spending on domestic output [1].

**5.** It is a **transfer payment**: no good or service is produced or bought in return [1]. It only affects spending if the recipient spends it, and then it appears as **consumer spending**, not as an injection [1].

**6.** Real flow: households supply **factor services** (labour, land, capital, enterprise) to firms [1]. Money flow: firms pay **factor incomes** (wages, rent, interest, profit) to households [1].

**7. (a)** Injections = 95 + 170 + 100 = **365** [1]. Leakages = 85 + 150 + 120 = **355** [1].

**(b)** National income will **rise** [1]. Injections exceed leakages by 10, so spending on domestic output is greater than the income firms paid out [1]. Firms raise output and employment, incomes rise and leakages rise until they equal injections [1].

**(c)** Injections = 355 = leakages, so the economy is **in equilibrium** [1].

**8. (a)** **Injection** [1]: it is spending by a non-resident on domestic output, an export [1].

**(b)** **Leakage** [1]: the tax is paid to the government and is not received by firms as revenue [1].

**(c)** **Neither** [1]: wages are a factor income flowing from firms to households within the circle [1].

**9. (a)** Higher saving **raises leakages** above injections [1]. Spending on domestic output falls below the income paid out, so **unsold stocks build up** [1]. Firms **cut output and employment** [1], so household incomes and **national income fall** [1].

**(b)** As income falls, households **save less, pay less tax and buy fewer imports** [1], so leakages fall until they again **equal injections** at a lower level of national income [1].

**10.** Government spending is an **injection**, so injections rise above leakages [1]. Construction firms and suppliers receive more revenue and **increase output and employment** [1]. Households earn more income and spend more, raising firms' revenue further [1]. As income rises, leakages rise until they equal the higher injections at a **higher equilibrium national income** [1].

**11.** Leakages minus injections = (S - I) + (T - G) + (M - X) [1] = 20 + (-5) + (-15) = **0** [1]. Total leakages equal total injections, so **Country R is in equilibrium**, even though no pair balances on its own [1].

**12.** Equilibrium only means national income has **no tendency to change** [1]. It may occur at a level with **high unemployment** or with output above what the economy can sustain, causing rising prices [1]. So the statement is incorrect: whether equilibrium is desirable must be judged against objectives such as low unemployment and price stability [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating the pension or benefit in Question 5 as an injection.
- Checking pairs rather than totals in Question 11.
- Giving the size of the change in income instead of its direction.
- Missing the firms' response (stocks and output) in Question 9(a).
