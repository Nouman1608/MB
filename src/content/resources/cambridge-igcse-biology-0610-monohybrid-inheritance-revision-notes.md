These notes condense sub-topic **17.4 Monohybrid inheritance** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 12 are Core; outcomes 13 to 18 are **Extended only**.

For worked examples, read the [monohybrid inheritance study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-monohybrid-inheritance/). Then try the [monohybrid inheritance practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-monohybrid-inheritance-practice/).

## Key terms -- Core

| Term | Syllabus meaning |
|---|---|
| Inheritance | the transmission of genetic information from generation to generation |
| Genotype | the genetic make-up of an organism, in terms of the alleles present |
| Phenotype | the observable features of an organism |
| Homozygous | having two identical alleles of a particular gene |
| Heterozygous | having two different alleles of a particular gene |
| Pure-breeding | two identical homozygous individuals that breed together; a heterozygous individual is not pure-breeding |
| Dominant allele | an allele that is expressed if it is present in the genotype |
| Recessive allele | an allele that is only expressed when there is no dominant allele of the gene present |
| Codominance | both alleles in a heterozygous organism contribute to the phenotype (Extended) |
| Sex-linked characteristic | a feature whose gene is on a sex chromosome, making it more common in one sex (Extended) |

## Genetic diagram checklist -- Core

1. Parents' phenotypes
2. Parents' genotypes
3. Gametes: **one allele each**
4. Punnett square
5. Offspring genotypes
6. Offspring phenotypes and ratio

## Ratio rules -- Core

| Cross | Genotype ratio | Phenotype ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Rr × Rr | 1 RR : 2 Rr : 1 rr | 3 : 1 |
| Rr × rr | 1 Rr : 1 rr | 1 : 1 |
| RR × any | all have R | all dominant |
| rr × rr | all rr | all recessive |

**Working backwards:** about 3 : 1 → both heterozygous. About 1 : 1 → heterozygous × homozygous recessive. Any recessive offspring → both parents carry the recessive allele.

**From counts:** divide both counts by the smaller one. 150 : 52 → 2.9 : 1 ≈ 3 : 1. Real counts vary from expected because fertilisation is random.

## Pedigrees -- Core

- Read the key: squares, circles, shading.
- Two unaffected parents with an affected child → the allele is **recessive**, and both parents are **heterozygous**.
- An affected person (recessive condition) is **homozygous recessive**.
- An unaffected person with an affected child or an affected parent is **heterozygous**.
- Check children as well as parents before deciding a genotype.

## Test cross -- Extended only

Cross the unknown dominant-phenotype individual with a **homozygous recessive** one.

| Offspring | Unknown parent |
|---|---|
| all dominant phenotype | homozygous dominant (more offspring = more confidence) |
| about 1 dominant : 1 recessive | heterozygous |

## ABO blood groups -- Extended only

Alleles: **Iᴬ**, **Iᴮ** (codominant), **Iᵒ** (recessive to both).

| Group | Genotypes |
|---|---|
| A | IᴬIᴬ, IᴬIᵒ |
| B | IᴮIᴮ, IᴮIᵒ |
| AB | IᴬIᴮ |
| O | IᵒIᵒ |

Useful crosses:

| Parents | Children |
|---|---|
| IᴬIᴮ × IᵒIᵒ | 1 A : 1 B |
| IᴬIᵒ × IᴮIᵒ | 1 A : 1 B : 1 AB : 1 O |
| IᴬIᴮ × IᴬIᴮ | 1 A : 2 AB : 1 B |

## Sex linkage -- Extended only

- Red-green colour blindness: gene on the **X chromosome**; no allele on Y.
- Xᴮ = normal vision; Xᵇ = colour-blind.
- Males: XᴮY normal, XᵇY colour-blind (one allele is enough).
- Females: XᴮXᴮ normal, XᴮXᵇ normal **carrier**, XᵇXᵇ colour-blind.

| Parents | Sons | Daughters |
|---|---|---|
| XᵇXᵇ × XᴮY | all colour-blind | all carriers |
| XᴮXᵇ × XᴮY | 1/2 colour-blind | 1/2 carriers, none colour-blind |
| XᴮXᵇ × XᵇY | 1/2 colour-blind | 1/2 colour-blind, 1/2 carriers |

A son's X always comes from his **mother**.

## Exam traps

- Gametes carry **one** allele: never write "Rr" as a gamete.
- Answer the ratio asked for: **genotype** or **phenotype**.
- Expected ratios are **probabilities**, not guarantees.
- (Extended) Test cross partner must be **homozygous recessive**.
- (Extended) Use Iᴬ, Iᴮ and Iᵒ, not A, B and O.
- (Extended) Always show X and Y in sex-linked genotypes.

## Self-test

**1.** Define phenotype.

**2.** Define heterozygous.

**3.** Why is a heterozygous individual not pure-breeding?

**4.** Give the phenotype ratio from Tt × Tt, where T (tall) is dominant.

**5.** Give the genotype ratio from Tt × Tt.

**6.** Offspring are 88 tall and 90 short. What are the likely parental genotypes?

**7.** Two unaffected parents have an affected child. What does this show?

**8.** *(Extended)* What must a dominant-phenotype animal be crossed with in a test cross?

**9.** *(Extended)* Define codominance.

**10.** *(Extended)* Give the possible genotypes of a person with blood group B.

**11.** *(Extended)* Why is red-green colour blindness more common in males?

**12.** *(Extended)* Can a colour-blind man pass the allele to his son? Explain.

### Answers

**1.** The observable features of an organism.

**2.** Having two different alleles of a particular gene.

**3.** It can pass on either allele, so its offspring are not all the same as it.

**4.** 3 tall : 1 short.

**5.** 1 TT : 2 Tt : 1 tt.

**6.** Tt × tt (ratio about 1 : 1).

**7.** The allele is recessive and both parents are heterozygous.

**8.** A homozygous recessive individual.

**9.** Both alleles in a heterozygous organism contribute to the phenotype.

**10.** IᴮIᴮ or IᴮIᵒ.

**11.** The gene is on the X chromosome; males have one X, so one recessive allele causes the condition.

**12.** No. A son receives the Y chromosome from his father, and the allele is on the X.
