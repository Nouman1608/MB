Quick-recall notes for **3.1.3 Biopsychology**, Section C of Paper 1 in OxfordAQA International GCSE
Psychology (9218). For full explanations and a worked example, use the
[Biopsychology study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-biopsychology/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Central nervous system** | Brain and spinal cord |
| **Peripheral nervous system** | Nerves outside the brain and spinal cord |
| **Somatic nervous system** | Voluntary movement and sensation (skeletal muscles, skin) |
| **Autonomic nervous system** | Involuntary control of organs and glands |
| **Hormone** | Chemical messenger released by a gland into the blood |
| **Neurotransmitter** | Chemical messenger released into the synapse |
| **Synapse** | The junction, including a small gap, between two neurons |
| **Reuptake** | The sending neuron reabsorbing leftover neurotransmitter |
| **Excitation** | Makes the next neuron more likely to fire |
| **Inhibition** | Makes the next neuron less likely to fire |
| **Localisation of function** | Specific brain areas perform specific functions |
| **Cognitive neuroscience** | Studying how brain structure and activity relate to thought and behaviour |

## Nervous and endocrine systems

- **Nervous system:** electrical and chemical signals; very fast; short-lived effects.
- **Endocrine system:** hormones in the blood; slower; longer-lasting effects.
- **Pituitary:** controls other glands. **Adrenal glands:** release adrenaline.

**Fight or flight sequence:** threat perceived, autonomic arousal, adrenaline released, heart and
breathing rate up, pupils dilate, muscles tense, digestion slows. Afterwards the body returns to rest.

**James-Lange:** event, then arousal, then interpretation, then emotion. Criticism (Cannon): arousal is
too slow to explain how fast emotions appear, and similar arousal occurs in different emotions.

## Neurons

| Sensory | Relay | Motor |
|---|---|---|
| Receptors to central nervous system | Inside central nervous system | Central nervous system to muscles and glands |
| Long dendrites, short axon | Short dendrites, short axon | Short dendrites, long axon |

## Synaptic transmission in five steps

1. Impulse reaches the axon terminal.
2. Vesicles release neurotransmitter into the gap.
3. Neurotransmitter binds to receptors on the next neuron.
4. Effect is excitatory or inhibitory.
5. Reuptake (or breakdown) ends the signal.

**Interaction:** a neuron fires only if the combined excitatory input outweighs the inhibitory input.

**Hebb:** neurons that repeatedly fire together form stronger connections and **cell assemblies**;
learning physically changes the brain.

|---|---|---|
| Frontal lobe | Planning, voluntary movement, speech production | Motor area, Broca's area |

| Occipital lobe | Vision | Visual area |

## Named studies

| Study | What happened | Shows | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Penfield** | Awake epilepsy patients had areas of cortex electrically stimulated; temporal lobe stimulation sometimes triggered vivid memories and sensations | Localisation; the temporal lobe's role in memory | Unusual sample; only a minority reported experiences |
| **Tulving ('gold')** | Participants injected with radioactive gold thought about episodic or semantic memories while activity was measured | Episodic linked to the front of the cortex, semantic to the back | Very small sample; pattern not clear for everyone |

## Brain damage effects

| Damage to | Likely effect |
|---|---|
| Motor area (one side) | Weakness or paralysis on the opposite side of the body |
| Broca's area | Slow, effortful speech; understanding mostly intact |
| Wernicke's area | Fluent speech that lacks meaning; poor understanding |
| Cerebellum | Clumsy movement, poor balance |
| Frontal lobe | Changes in personality, planning and self-control |

## Evaluating the scanning techniques

| Question | CT | PET | fMRI |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shows activity during a task? | No | Yes | Yes |
| Involves radiation? | Yes (X-rays) | Yes (radioactive tracer) | No |
| Best use | Checking for damage such as a stroke or tumour | Comparing activity in different mental tasks | Linking brain activity to thinking without radiation |

## Linking biopsychology to other Paper 1 topics

- **Memory:** Hebb's cell assemblies explain how rehearsal strengthens memories; Tulving links
  episodic and semantic memory to different areas.
- **Perception:** the visual area in the occipital lobe processes sensation before interpretation.
- **Research methods:** Penfield and Tulving both used very small, unusual samples, so generalisation
  is a standard evaluation point.

## Exam traps

- **Broca's = frontal = producing speech.** **Wernicke's = temporal = understanding.**
- Hormones travel in the **blood**; neurotransmitters cross the **synapse**.
- **Reuptake** is into the **sending** neuron.
- Somatic is **voluntary**; autonomic is **involuntary**. Both are peripheral.
- CT shows **structure**; PET and fMRI show **activity**.
- James-Lange puts **arousal first**.

## Self-test

1. Name the two parts of the central nervous system.
2. Which division of the nervous system controls heart rate?
3. Give two physical changes in the fight or flight response.
4. What does the James-Lange theory say comes first, emotion or arousal?
5. Which neuron connects sensory and motor neurons?
6. What is reuptake?
7. What decides whether a neuron fires when it receives many signals?
8. What did Hebb mean by a cell assembly?
9. Which lobe contains the visual area?
10. What is the function of Wernicke's area?
11. Which scan uses a radioactive tracer?
12. What did Tulving's study suggest about episodic and semantic memory?

**Answers:**
1. Brain and spinal cord.
2. The autonomic nervous system.
3. Any two: increased heart rate, faster breathing, dilated pupils, tense muscles, more sweating,
   slower digestion.
4. Arousal.
5. Relay neuron.
6. The sending neuron taking back leftover neurotransmitter from the synapse.
7. The balance of excitatory and inhibitory inputs; it fires if excitation outweighs inhibition.
8. A group of neurons that have repeatedly fired together and become strongly connected, representing
   something learned.
9. Occipital lobe.
10. Understanding language.
11. PET.
12. They involve different areas: episodic towards the front of the cortex, semantic towards the back.
