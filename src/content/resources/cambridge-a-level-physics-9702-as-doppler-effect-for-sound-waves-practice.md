Take the speed of sound in air as 340 m s⁻¹. In every question the observer is stationary.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the Doppler effect for sound waves. **[2]**

**2.** A student writes: "The observed frequency is higher when a source approaches because the sound travels faster towards the observer." Explain what is wrong with this statement, and give the correct reason. **[2]**

**3.** A source emitting sound of frequency 1000 Hz moves directly towards an observer at 17 m s⁻¹. Calculate the observed frequency. **[2]**

**4.** A car sounding its horn passes a stationary observer at constant speed along a straight road. Describe how the frequency heard by the observer changes from long before the car arrives until long after it has passed. **[2]**

**5.** A loudspeaker is fixed at the side of a track, and a cyclist rides towards it. State why the expression f₀ = fₛv/(v ± vₛ) does not apply to the frequency the cyclist hears. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A train approaches a level crossing at 35 m s⁻¹, sounding a horn of frequency 400 Hz. An observer stands at the crossing.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency heard by the observer as the train approaches. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound reaching the observer as the train approaches. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the frequency heard after the train has passed the crossing and is moving away at the same speed. **[2]**

**7.** A drone carrying a buzzer of frequency 2.0 kHz flies directly away from an observer at constant speed. The observer hears a frequency of 1.90 kHz. Calculate the speed of the drone. **[3]**

**8.** A motorcycle travels at constant speed along a straight road past a stationary observer. The observer hears a frequency of 720 Hz as it approaches and 640 Hz as it moves away.

**(a)** Show that the speed of the motorcycle is 20 m s⁻¹. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency of the sound emitted by the motorcycle. **[2]**

**9.** A small buzzer emitting 3.0 kHz is fixed to the edge of a turntable of radius 0.50 m, which rotates once every 0.40 s. An observer stands some distance away, level with the turntable.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the buzzer. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum and minimum frequencies heard by the observer. **[3]**

**(c)** State how many times per second the observer hears the maximum frequency. **[1]**

**10.** Explain, with reference to the spacing of wavefronts, why an observer positioned behind a moving sound source hears a frequency lower than the source frequency. **[3]**

**11.** A source emits sound of frequency 500 Hz.

**(a)** Calculate the speed at which it must move directly towards an observer for the observed frequency to be 600 Hz. **[2]**

**(b)** The source's speed is doubled. Calculate the new observed frequency, and state whether the change in observed frequency has also doubled. **[2]**

**12.** An observer hears a frequency 1.05 times the source frequency from a source moving directly towards her.

**(a)** Determine the speed of the source as a fraction of the speed of sound. **[2]**

**(b)** The source continues past her at the same speed. Determine the observed frequency as a multiple of the source frequency. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** When a source of sound moves relative to a stationary observer [1], the **observed frequency differs from the source frequency**: higher when the source approaches, lower when it moves away [1].

**2.** The speed of sound **depends only on the medium** and is not changed by the source's motion [1]. The frequency rises because the wavefronts are emitted **closer together**, so the observed wavelength is shorter, and f = v/λ [1].

**3.** f₀ = 1000 × 340 ÷ (340 − 17) [1] = **1050 Hz** (1053 Hz) [1].

**4.** While approaching, a **constant frequency higher** than the source frequency is heard [1]. As the car passes, the frequency **falls**, then stays **constant and lower** than the source frequency as it recedes [1].

**5.** The expression applies to a **moving source and a stationary observer**; here the observer moves and the source is stationary [1].

**6. (a)** f₀ = 400 × 340 ÷ (340 − 35) [1] = **446 Hz** [1].

**(b)** λ' = (v − vₛ)/fₛ = 305 ÷ 400 [1] = **0.76 m** [1] (or v/f₀ = 340 ÷ 446).

**(c)** f₀ = 400 × 340 ÷ (340 + 35) [1] = **363 Hz** [1].

**7.** Source moving away, so f₀ = fₛv/(v + vₛ) [1]. 340 + vₛ = 2000 × 340 ÷ 1900 = 357.9 [1]. vₛ = **18 m s⁻¹** [1].

**8. (a)** 720 = fₛv/(v − vₛ) and 640 = fₛv/(v + vₛ) [1]. Dividing: 720/640 = (v + vₛ)/(v − vₛ), so 720(v − vₛ) = 640(v + vₛ) [1]. 80v = 1360vₛ, vₛ = 340 × 80 ÷ 1360 = **20 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** fₛ = 720 × (340 − 20) ÷ 340 [1] = **678 Hz** [1].

**9. (a)** vₛ = 2πr ÷ T = 2π × 0.50 ÷ 0.40 [1] = **7.9 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Maximum when moving directly towards the observer: 3000 × 340 ÷ (340 − 7.85) = **3070 Hz** [1]. Minimum when moving directly away: 3000 × 340 ÷ (340 + 7.85) = **2930 Hz** [1]. Correct sign choice for each [1].

**(c)** Once per revolution: 1 ÷ 0.40 = **2.5 times per second** [1].

**10.** The source emits wavefronts at regular intervals [1], but moves away from the observer between emissions, so wavefronts behind it are **further apart**: the observed wavelength is longer [1]. The **speed of sound is unchanged**, so f = v/λ is **lower** [1].

**11. (a)** 340 − vₛ = 500 × 340 ÷ 600 = 283.3 [1]; vₛ = **57 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** f₀ = 500 × 340 ÷ (340 − 113.3) = **750 Hz** [1]. The change rises from 100 Hz to 250 Hz, so it has **more than doubled**: the relationship is not linear [1].

**12. (a)** 1.05 = v/(v − vₛ), so v − vₛ = v/1.05 [1]; vₛ = (1 − 1/1.05)v = **0.048v** (1/21 of v) [1].

**(b)** f₀ = fₛv/(v + 0.0476v) = **0.95fₛ** (0.955fₛ) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the wrong sign: approaching sources need v − vₛ (Questions 6, 9 and 11).
- Saying the sound "travels faster" from an approaching source (Question 2).
- Algebra slips when dividing two Doppler equations (Question 8).
- Using the rotation rate instead of the linear speed 2πr/T (Question 9).
- Assuming the observed frequency shift is proportional to the source speed (Question 11).
- Describing a change in loudness when the question asks about frequency (Question 4).
