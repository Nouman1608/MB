These notes condense section 2.6 (price changes) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: causes of price changes (2.6.1) and their consequences for equilibrium price and sales (2.6.2). All figures are invented. Worked explanations are in the [price changes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-price-changes-cambridge-igcse-economics/); test yourself with the [price changes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-price-changes-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/).

## 2.6.1 Causes of price changes

- Equilibrium price changes only when a condition of demand or supply changes.
- A change in the product's own price is a result, shown by movements along the curves.

### Four questions for any event

1. Which market?
2. Demand side or supply side?
3. Shift right or left?
4. Shortage or surplus at the old price, so which way does the price move?

| Demand shifts right | Demand shifts left | Supply shifts right | Supply shifts left |
|---|---|---|---|
| Higher incomes (most goods) | Lower incomes | Lower costs | Higher costs |
| Substitute dearer | Substitute cheaper | Better technology | New indirect tax |
| Complement cheaper | Complement dearer | Subsidy | Subsidy removed |
| Favourable tastes, advertising | Unfavourable publicity | Good weather | Bad weather, disease |
| Larger population | Smaller population | More firms | Firms leave |

## 2.6.2 Consequences: the four single shifts

| Shift | Equilibrium price | Equilibrium sales |
|---|---|---|
| Demand right | Rises | Rise |
| Demand left | Falls | Fall |
| Supply right | Falls | Rise |
| Supply left | Rises | Fall |

### Worked reminder from a schedule

Market for rice sacks (invented): demand 900 at 10, 800 at 12, 700 at 14; supply 500 at 10, 700 at 12, 900 at 14.

```
Original: gap +100 at 12 and -200 at 14; equilibrium where gap = 0
Gap closes by 300 per 2 rise, so 150 per 1: price = 12 + 100/150 = 12.67
```

Simpler case: supply rises by 200 at every price.

```
New supply: 700 at 10, 900 at 12
At 10: demand 900, supply 700 -> shortage 200
At 12: demand 800, supply 900 -> surplus 100
Gap closes by 300 per 2 rise; 200 / 150 = 1.33 -> price about 11.33
Sales = 900 - (1.33 x 50) = about 833
```

Price falls and sales rise, as the table predicts.

### Double shifts

| Both shift | Price | Sales |
|---|---|---|
| Demand right, supply right | Depends on sizes | Rise |
| Demand left, supply left | Depends on sizes | Fall |
| Demand right, supply left | Rises | Depends on sizes |
| Demand left, supply right | Falls | Depends on sizes |

Rule: when the two shifts push a variable the same way, that change is certain; when they push it opposite ways, the result depends on which shift is larger.

### Size of the change

- A steep supply curve: a demand shift changes price a lot and sales a little.
- A flat demand curve: a supply shift changes sales a lot and price a little.
- This links to price elasticity (sections 2.7 and 2.8).

## Related markets

| Change in market X | Effect in market Y | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Price of X rises; Y is a substitute | Demand for Y increases | Buyers switch to Y |
| Price of X rises; Y is a complement | Demand for Y decreases | X and Y are used together |
| Price of X rises; X is an input to Y | Supply of Y decreases | Y's costs rise |
| Output of X falls | Demand for workers in X falls | Fewer workers needed |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Change in market conditions | A change in a condition of demand or supply |
| Shift | Movement of the whole curve to a new position |
| New equilibrium | Where the new curves cross after a shift |
| Shortage at the old price | Created by demand right or supply left |
| Surplus at the old price | Created by demand left or supply right |
| Total spending | Equilibrium price x equilibrium sales |

## Exam traps

- Circular reasoning: "price rises so demand falls so price falls". After a supply shift, buyers move along the unchanged demand curve.
- Shifting demand for a cost change, or supply for a change in tastes.
- Giving price but not sales (or the reverse).
- Claiming a definite result for the uncertain variable in a double shift.
- Not labelling P1, P2, Q1, Q2 on the diagram.

## Quick self-test

1. A new tax is placed on fizzy drinks. Which curve shifts, and which way?
2. What happens to equilibrium price and sales when demand shifts left?
3. A frost damages the orange crop. What happens to the price and sales of oranges?
4. Following question 3, what happens to demand for apple juice, a substitute?
5. Demand rises and supply rises at the same time. Which result is certain?
6. Why is "price rises, so demand falls, so price falls" wrong?
7. At the old price of 8, after a shift, quantity demanded is 450 and quantity supplied is 300. Which way will the price move?
8. Equilibrium moves from price 6 and sales 1,000 to price 7 and sales 900. Calculate the change in total spending.
9. Which shift could cause the change in question 8?
10. Name one event that would lower the price of smartphones.
11. Coffee prices rise. What happens to demand for coffee cups, a complement?
12. Give one condition for a demand shift to change price a lot but sales only a little.

### Answers

1. Supply; it shifts left.
2. Both fall.
3. Supply shifts left, so price rises and sales fall.
4. It increases (shifts right), raising its price and sales.
5. Sales rise.
6. The price rise causes a contraction along the unchanged demand curve, not a shift of demand.
7. Upward: there is a shortage of 150.
8. 6 x 1,000 = 6,000; 7 x 900 = 6,300; a rise of 300.
9. A decrease in supply (shift left).
10. Any one: lower component costs, better technology, more firms, a subsidy; or a fall in demand.
11. It decreases, because fewer cups of coffee are bought.
12. Supply is steep (unresponsive), for example when output cannot be raised quickly.
