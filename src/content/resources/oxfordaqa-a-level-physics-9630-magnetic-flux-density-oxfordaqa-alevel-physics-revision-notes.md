## The equation

F = BIL (field perpendicular to current)

| Symbol | Quantity | Unit | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| F | force on the wire | N | perpendicular to both I and B |
| B | magnetic flux density | T | mT = 10⁻³ T |
| I | current | A | conventional current |
| L | length of wire in the field | m | not the whole wire |

For a coil of N turns, the force on one side is NBIL.

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Magnetic flux density B | Force per unit current per unit length on a conductor at right angles to the field |
| Tesla | Flux density that gives a force of 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field |
| Fleming's left-hand rule | First finger field, second finger current, thumb force (motion) |
| Conventional current | Flow from positive to negative; opposite to electron flow |

Tesla in other units: 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = 1 kg s⁻² A⁻¹ = 1 Wb m⁻².

## Condensed recall

- Force is largest with the wire perpendicular to the field and zero with the wire parallel to it.
- Reverse current or field: force reverses. Reverse both: force unchanged.
- Field lines run from north pole to south pole.
- Opposite sides of a coil carry current in opposite directions: equal and opposite forces form a couple that turns the coil.
- B is a vector, along the field lines.
- Graph methods: F against I has gradient BL; F against L has gradient BI.
- Levitation: BIL = mg.

## Method boxes

**Direction of the force**

1. Mark the field direction (N to S, or into/out of the page).
2. Mark the conventional current direction.
3. Left hand: first finger along the field, second finger along the current.
4. The thumb gives the force.

**B from a balance experiment**

1. Change in balance reading × g = force on the wire (Newton's third law: the magnet is pushed the other way).
2. Plot F against I.
3. Gradient = BL, so B = gradient / L.

**Rod held up by a magnetic force**

1. Weight down, magnetic force up.
2. BIL = mg, so I = mg/(BL).
3. Any extra current gives a resultant force BIL − mg and an acceleration (BIL − mg)/m.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Compare |
|---|---|
| B and E | B: force per unit current per unit length. E: force per unit charge |
| Wire across the field and along it | Across: F = BIL. Along: F = 0 |
| Left-hand rule and right-hand rule | Left: motor effect, force on a current. The right hand is not used for this force |
| Couple and resultant force | Forces on opposite coil sides sum to zero but still turn the coil |

## Worked reminders

- 0.25 m of wire, 3.2 A, 0.15 T: F = **0.12 N**.
- 0.050 m between poles, 0.40 T, force 0.030 N: I = 0.030/(0.40 × 0.050) = **1.5 A**.
- 40-turn coil, side 0.050 m, 0.15 T, 0.80 A: F on one side = 40 × 0.15 × 0.80 × 0.050 = **0.24 N**.

## Exam traps

- Using the full length of a wire that only partly lies in the field.
- Leaving out N for a coil.
- Following electron flow with Fleming's rule.
- Defining the tesla without "at right angles" or without the 1 N, 1 A, 1 m values.
- Claiming a wire parallel to the field feels a smaller but non-zero force.
- Forgetting to multiply a change in balance reading (in kg) by g.

## Self-test

1. Define the tesla.
2. Express the tesla in SI base units.
3. A wire lies parallel to a magnetic field. What force acts on it?
4. Find the force on 0.20 m of wire carrying 5.0 A at right angles to a 0.30 T field.
5. A vertical wire carries current upwards in a field pointing east. Which way is the force?
6. Both the current and the field in question 5 are reversed. What happens to the force?
7. A balance reading rises by 2.4 g when current flows. Find the force on the wire.
8. A graph of F against I for 0.050 m of wire has gradient 4.0 × 10⁻³ N A⁻¹. Find B.
9. A 10 g rod of length 0.20 m lies across a 0.050 T field. What current supports it?
10. Why does a coil carrying a current in a field turn but not move off sideways?
11. A coil of 60 turns has a side of length 0.030 m across a 0.10 T field and carries 0.50 A. Find the force on that side.
12. How would you show from data that F ∝ I?

### Answers

1. The flux density that gives a force of 1 N on a 1 m wire carrying 1 A at right angles to the field.
2. **kg s⁻² A⁻¹**.
3. **Zero**.
4. 0.30 × 5.0 × 0.20 = **0.30 N**.
5. **North** (horizontal).
6. It is **unchanged**.
7. 2.4 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 = **0.024 N**.
8. B = 4.0 × 10⁻³ / 0.050 = **0.080 T**.
9. I = 0.010 × 9.81 / (0.050 × 0.20) = **9.8 A**.
10. The forces on opposite sides are equal and opposite, so the resultant force is zero, but they act along different lines and form a couple.
11. 60 × 0.10 × 0.50 × 0.030 = **0.090 N**.
12. Plot F against I: a straight line through the origin shows proportionality.
