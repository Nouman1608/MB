## Section A

**1.** State the SI base unit of amount of substance, and state what is meant by one mole of a substance. **[2]**

**2.** State the unit of the Avogadro constant, and explain why amount of substance is not the same quantity as mass. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the amount of substance and the number of molecules in 2.0 kg of water (molar mass 18 g mol⁻¹). **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the mass, in kg, of one argon atom. The molar mass of argon is 40 g mol⁻¹. **[1]**

**5.** A sample of nitrogen gas contains 1.5 × 10²⁴ molecules. The molar mass of nitrogen is 28 g mol⁻¹. Calculate the mass of the sample. **[2]**

**6.** State the number of atoms in 1.0 mol of carbon dioxide, CO₂. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A gold ring has mass 5.0 g. The molar mass of gold is 197 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of gold in the ring. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of gold atoms. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of one gold atom from the molar mass. **[1]**

**(d)** Show that your answer to (c) is consistent with a gold atom having a mass of 197 u. **[1]**

**8.** Aluminium has density 2700 kg m⁻³ and molar mass 27 g mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the volume occupied by one mole of aluminium. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume per atom. **[1]**

**(c)** Hence estimate the spacing between neighbouring aluminium atoms. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the number of atoms per cubic metre. **[1]**

**9.** A cylinder of volume 0.050 m³ contains 2.0 mol of gas.

**(a)** Calculate the number of molecules per unit volume. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate the average spacing between the gas molecules. **[1]**

**(c)** The spacing of molecules in a liquid is about 3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. Comment on what your answer to (b) suggests about the volume of the molecules in the gas compared with the volume of the gas. **[1]**

**10.** A container holds 4.0 g of hydrogen gas (H₂, molar mass 2.0 g mol⁻¹) and 32 g of oxygen gas (O₂, molar mass 32 g mol⁻¹).

**(a)** Calculate the amount of each gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total number of molecules in the container. **[1]**

**(c)** State the fraction of the molecules that are oxygen molecules. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes: "One mole of oxygen and one mole of helium have the same mass, because they contain the same number of particles." Correct the student's statement. **[2]**

**12.** The molar mass of a substance is the mass of one mole.

**(a)** State the SI unit of molar mass. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that the product of the unified atomic mass unit and the Avogadro constant is 1.00 g mol⁻¹. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The mole (mol) [1]. One mole is the amount of substance containing a number of particles equal to the Avogadro constant, 6.02 × 10²³ [1].

**2.** mol⁻¹ [1]. Amount of substance counts the number of particles; equal amounts of different substances have different masses because their particles have different masses [1].

**3.** n = m/M = 2.0 ÷ 0.018 = 111 mol [1]; N = 111 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.7 × 10²⁵ molecules [1].

**4.** m₀ = M/N_A = 0.040 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.6 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1].

**5.** n = N/N_A = 1.5 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 2.49 mol [1]; m = nM = 2.49 × 0.028 = 0.070 kg [1].

**6.** Each molecule has 3 atoms, so 3 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.8 × 10²⁴ atoms [1].

**7. (a)** n = 5.0 ÷ 197 = 0.025 mol (2.54 × 10⁻² mol) [1].

**(b)** N = 0.0254 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.5 × 10²² atoms [1].

**(c)** m₀ = 0.197 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.27 × 10⁻²⁵ kg [1].

**(d)** 197 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ = 3.27 × 10⁻²⁵ kg, the same value [1].

**8. (a)** Volume = mass ÷ density = 0.027 ÷ 2700 [1] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³ [1].

**(b)** 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.7 × 10⁻²⁹ m³ [1].

**(c)** Spacing ≈ ∛(1.66 × 10⁻²⁹) = 2.6 × 10⁻¹⁰ m [1].

**(d)** 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 1.0 × 10⁻⁵ = 6.0 × 10²⁸ m⁻³ [1].

**9. (a)** N = 2.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.20 × 10²⁴ [1]; N/V = 1.20 × 10²⁴ ÷ 0.050 = 2.4 × 10²⁵ m⁻³ [1].

**(b)** Spacing ≈ ∛(1 ÷ 2.4 × 10²⁵) = 3.5 × 10⁻⁹ m [1].

**(c)** The gas molecules are about ten times further apart than their own size, so the volume of the molecules is a very small fraction (about one thousandth) of the volume of the gas [1].

**10. (a)** Hydrogen: 4.0 ÷ 2.0 = 2.0 mol [1]; oxygen: 32 ÷ 32 = 1.0 mol [1].

**(b)** 3.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.8 × 10²⁴ molecules [1].

**(c)** One third [1].

**11.** They contain the same number of particles (N_A) [1], but an oxygen molecule has a much greater mass than a helium atom (32 g mol⁻¹ compared with 4.0 g mol⁻¹), so the mole of oxygen has eight times the mass [1].

**12. (a)** kg mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg × 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ = 9.99 × 10⁻⁴ kg mol⁻¹ ≈ 1.00 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹ = 1.00 g mol⁻¹ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving N_A without its unit, mol⁻¹.
- Dividing a mass in kilograms by a molar mass in grams per mole.
- Counting molecules when atoms are asked for, or the reverse.
- Forgetting the cube root when estimating spacing from volume per particle.
- Describing the mole as a mass rather than an amount.
- Giving huge particle numbers without standard form, or with the wrong power of ten.
