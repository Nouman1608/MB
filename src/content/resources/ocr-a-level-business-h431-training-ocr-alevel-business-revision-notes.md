Condensed notes on the Training content (5.7 on this site) in the area of study The workforce, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and two costed examples are in the [training study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-training-ocr-alevel-business/). Test yourself with the [training practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-training-ocr-alevel-business-practice/). All figures are invented.

## What the specification lists

- What is meant by training.
- Methods: on-the-job, off-the-job, induction, academic, vocational, apprenticeship.
- Training needs analysis (TNA): meaning and importance.
- Usefulness of methods; impact and importance of training.
- Recommend and justify training methods.

## Core recall

### Definition

- **Training:** teaching employees the skills, knowledge and attitudes they need to do their jobs, now or in future.
- Reasons: new staff, new technology, legal requirements, quality, promotion, motivation.

### Methods

| Method | Meaning | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | Introduction to the business, rules, safety, people | Settles new staff; legal briefings | Information overload; short-lived |
| On-the-job | Learning while doing, with a supervisor | Cheap; relevant | Trainer quality; bad habits; slows trainer |
| Off-the-job | Courses and workshops away from the job | Specialists; new ideas | Costly; absence; transfer to the job |
| Academic | Education for formal qualifications | Recognised; broad | Long; costly; less practical |
| Vocational | Training for a specific job or trade | Relevant; practical | Narrow |
| Apprenticeship | Job plus structured training and qualification | Earns while learning; shaped to employer | Long; wage and supervision cost; may leave |

### Training needs analysis

1. Identify the skills the business needs.
2. Assess the skills staff have (appraisals, audits, performance data).
3. Find the gaps.
4. Rank by importance and cost.
5. Choose and plan training.
6. Evaluate the results.

- Importance: targets spending on real gaps, links training to aims, allows payback to be calculated.
- Limits: takes time; needs honest information; benefits are estimates.

### Costs and benefits of training

| Costs | Benefits |
|---|---|
| Fees, trainers, materials | Productivity and quality |
| Lost output while training | Fewer accidents and less waste |
| Supervisor time | Flexible, multi-skilled staff |
| Risk that trained staff leave | Motivation, loyalty, lower turnover |
| Benefit hard to measure | Promotion pool; legal compliance; reputation |

### Matching the method to the need

| Need | Likely method |
|---|---|
| New starters | Induction, then on-the-job |
| Specific machine skill | On-the-job with a coach |
| Specialist or certified skill | Off-the-job course |
| Long-term supply of trade skills | Apprenticeship |
| Management development | Academic study or vocational courses |

## Worked reminder

Haddow Print (invented), 30 staff.

- TNA: 18 need digital training at £420 = **£7,560**; 9 need health and safety at £150 = **£1,350**; total **£8,910**.
- Benefits: errors £14,000 × 40% = **£5,600**; accidents £4,200 × 50% = **£2,100**; total **£7,700**. Payback = 8,910 ÷ 7,700 = **1.2 years**.
- Training everyone in both areas would cost 30 × £420 + 30 × £150 = **£17,100**; the TNA saves **£8,190**.
- On-the-job per trainee: £352 supervisor + £540 lost output = **£892**. Off-the-job: £650 + £270 + £60 = **£980**. Difference £88.

## Recommending: six steps

1. Start from the gap in the TNA.
2. Match the method to the skill.
3. Consider the number of trainees.
4. Count cost and lost output.
5. State how success will be measured.
6. State the main risk (staff leaving) and how to reduce it.

## Quick case practice

A firm (invented) trains 12 staff at £300 each. Waste costs £10,000 a year and training is expected to cut it by 30%.

- Cost = 12 × £300 = **£3,600**.
- Saving = 30% × £10,000 = **£3,000** a year.
- Payback = £3,600 ÷ £3,000 = **1.2 years**.

Model response outline: payback is acceptable, but the saving is an estimate; other benefits include safety and morale. A training needs analysis would show whether all 12 need the course. The risk is that trained staff leave, so link training to career steps.

A second check: if the course is taken off-the-job, each trainee is also away for two days and a day of lost output is worth £80, an extra 12 × 2 × £80 = £1,920. The true cost is then £3,600 + £1,920 = £5,520, and payback becomes £5,520 ÷ £3,000 = 1.8 years. Always ask whether lost output has been counted before accepting a payback figure.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Training | Teaching staff the skills to do their jobs |
| Development | Growing abilities for future roles |
| TNA | Analysis of the gap between skills needed and skills held |
| Induction | Introduction of a new employee to the business |
| Apprenticeship | Paid job with structured training and a qualification |
| Poaching | A rival hiring staff you have trained |
| Skills audit | A review of the skills held by staff |

## Exam traps

- Induction is not on-the-job training.
- Off-the-job is not always better; it costs more and the skills may not transfer.
- Include indirect costs, such as lost output.
- A TNA comes first.
- Trained staff may leave; say how to reduce the risk.
- Quote the case when recommending.

## Self-test

1. Define training.
2. Distinguish between induction and on-the-job training.
3. Give one advantage of an apprenticeship to an employer.
4. Give one disadvantage of off-the-job training.
5. What is a TNA?
6. Training 20 staff costs £250 each. Calculate the total cost.
7. The training cuts errors costing £12,000 a year by 25%. Calculate the annual saving.
8. Calculate the payback period for the training in question 6.
9. Give two benefits of training to employees.
10. Give one way to reduce the risk that trained staff leave.

### Answers

1. Teaching employees the skills, knowledge and attitudes they need to do their jobs.
2. Induction introduces a new employee to the business, its rules and people; on-the-job training teaches the tasks of the job while doing it.
3. Any one: shapes skills to the business; trainee earns and works while learning; builds a supply of skilled staff.
4. Any one: costly; trainee is absent from work; skills may not transfer.
5. An analysis of the gap between the skills the business needs and those its employees have.
6. 20 × £250 = **£5,000**.
7. 25% × £12,000 = **£3,000**.
8. £5,000 ÷ £3,000 = **1.7 years**.
9. Any two: new skills; confidence; career prospects; higher pay; job security.
10. Any one: offer career progression; agree that the employee will repay fees if they leave early; improve pay and conditions.
