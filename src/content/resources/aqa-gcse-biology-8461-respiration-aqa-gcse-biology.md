## What respiration is, and is not

Respiration is an **exothermic** reaction that is **continuously occurring in living cells**. It transfers the
energy that cells need. It is a chemical reaction, which is why it happens in every living cell, in plants and
animals, by day and by night.

Two confusions cost marks every year:

- **Respiration is not breathing.** Breathing is the movement of air into and out of the lungs. It supplies
  oxygen for respiration and removes carbon dioxide, but it is not the reaction itself.
- **Respiration does not "make" energy.** Energy cannot be created. Respiration **transfers** energy stored in
  glucose so that cells can use it.

Organisms need the energy for three purposes named in the specification:

1. **chemical reactions to build larger molecules**, such as proteins from amino acids;
2. **movement**, such as muscle contraction;
3. **keeping warm**, in mammals and birds.

## Comparing aerobic and anaerobic respiration

The specification asks you to compare the processes in terms of **the need for oxygen, the products and the
relative amounts of energy transferred**. Learn the comparison as three paired sentences:

- Aerobic respiration **needs oxygen**, whereas anaerobic respiration **does not**.
- Aerobic respiration produces **carbon dioxide and water**, whereas anaerobic respiration produces **lactic acid**
  in muscles, or **ethanol and carbon dioxide** in plant and yeast cells.
- Aerobic respiration transfers **much more energy** per glucose than anaerobic respiration, because in anaerobic
  respiration the **oxidation of glucose is incomplete**.

```
aerobic:              glucose + oxygen  ->  carbon dioxide + water
anaerobic (muscle):   glucose           ->  lactic acid
anaerobic (plants,    glucose           ->  ethanol + carbon dioxide
 yeast):
```

Formulae to recognise: C₆H₁₂O₆, O₂, CO₂, H₂O.

Anaerobic respiration in yeast cells is called **fermentation**. It has economic importance in the manufacture of
**bread** and **alcoholic drinks**. In bread, the carbon dioxide makes the dough rise and the ethanol evaporates
during baking. In alcoholic drinks, the ethanol is the product that is wanted.

**Why "incomplete oxidation" matters.** In aerobic respiration glucose is broken down completely to carbon
dioxide and water. In anaerobic respiration the products, lactic acid or ethanol, still contain energy that has
not been transferred. That is the reason the energy transferred is so much smaller.

## 4.4.2.2 The response to exercise as a chain

During exercise the body reacts to the **increased demand for energy**:

1. Muscles contract more, so they need more energy from respiration.
2. **Heart rate**, **breathing rate** and **breath volume** increase.
3. These supply the muscles with more **oxygenated blood** and remove more carbon dioxide.
4. If **insufficient oxygen** is supplied, **anaerobic respiration** takes place in the muscles.
5. The incomplete oxidation of glucose causes a **build-up of lactic acid** and creates an **oxygen debt**.
6. During long periods of vigorous activity, muscles become **fatigued** and stop contracting efficiently.

**Worked example 1: interpreting recovery data** (invented figures)

| Time after a 400 m run (minutes) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Athlete A heart rate (beats per minute) | 170 | 130 | 100 | 78 | 70 | 70 |
| Athlete B heart rate (beats per minute) | 175 | 155 | 135 | 118 | 100 | 88 |

Resting rate for both: 70 beats per minute.

- **Describe:** athlete A's heart rate falls from 170 to 70 and returns to resting by 8 minutes; athlete B's falls
  from 175 to 88 and is still above resting at 10 minutes.
- **Explain:** heart rate stays high after exercise to carry blood rich in lactic acid to the liver and to supply
  the extra oxygen needed. Athlete A recovers faster, which suggests a smaller oxygen debt or a fitter heart and
  muscles.

## Recovery and oxygen debt (Higher tier only)

- **Blood flowing through the muscles transports the lactic acid to the liver**, where it is **converted back
  into glucose**.
- **Oxygen debt** is the amount of extra oxygen the body needs after exercise to react with the accumulated
  lactic acid and remove it from the cells.

This is the reason breathing rate stays high after exercise has stopped: the extra oxygen is still being taken in.

**Worked example 2: calculating an oxygen debt** (invented figures)

A cyclist's resting oxygen uptake is 0.25 dm³ per minute. In the 8 minutes after a sprint, her mean uptake is
0.95 dm³ per minute.

```
extra oxygen per minute = 0.95 − 0.25 = 0.70 dm³
oxygen debt             = 0.70 × 8   = 5.6 dm³
```

The debt is the **extra** oxygen above resting, not the total oxygen used.

## 4.4.2.3 Metabolism

**Metabolism** is the sum of all the reactions in a cell or the body. The energy transferred by respiration is used
by the organism for the continual **enzyme-controlled** processes of metabolism that synthesise new molecules.

The specification wants you to explain the importance of **sugars, amino acids, fatty acids and glycerol** in
building up and breaking down large molecules:

| Small units | Built into | Where it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose (a sugar) | Starch, glycogen, cellulose | Starch stores energy in plants; glycogen in animals; cellulose strengthens plant cell walls |
| One glycerol + three fatty acids | Lipid | Energy store |
| Glucose + nitrate ions | Amino acids | Plants make their own amino acids |
| Amino acids | Proteins | Enzymes, structural proteins and more |

Breakdown reactions are also metabolism: **respiration** breaks down glucose, and **excess proteins are broken
down to form urea** for excretion.

**Annotated model answer**

*Question: Explain why metabolism depends on respiration. [2]*

> The reactions that build large molecules, such as joining amino acids into proteins, need energy [1]. This
> energy is transferred by respiration [1].

## Links to other parts of the course

- **Cell structure (4.1.1):** aerobic respiration takes place in the **mitochondria**, which is why muscle cells
  and sperm cells, which need a lot of energy, contain many of them.
- **Photosynthesis (4.4.1):** plants respire the glucose they make, so a plant's net oxygen output in light is
  photosynthesis minus respiration.
- **Hormonal coordination (4.5.3):** excess glucose is stored as glycogen in liver and muscle cells, the same
  glycogen that muscles can draw on during exercise.

## Common mistakes

- "Respiration produces energy." Write "transfers" or "releases".
- Adding carbon dioxide to the muscle anaerobic equation.
- Saying anaerobic respiration happens instead of aerobic for the whole race; muscles respire aerobically too,
  with anaerobic respiration added when oxygen supply is insufficient.
- Calculating oxygen debt from total oxygen used, without subtracting the resting value (Higher tier only).
- Writing that lactic acid is "breathed out" or "excreted in urine".
- Confusing glycogen (a store in animals) with glucagon (a hormone in Topic 5).

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Compare**: three paired points using "whereas" (oxygen, products, energy).
- **Explain** the response to exercise: link each change to supplying oxygen or removing lactic acid.
- **Calculate**: subtract resting values before multiplying by time; show units.
- **Describe** data: give the trend, the values and when the data return to resting.
