# Allah's relationship with the created world: passages 6 to 10 of Appendix 1

This is the second of the three groups in Appendix 1. Related pages: [Major Themes of the Qur'an study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran/) and [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran-revision-notes/).

## How Question 1 is set (applies to every passage below)

The syllabus (Paper 1, Question 1) says three passages are set from Appendix 1 and candidates may choose any two, for 8 marks. Section 5.1 says candidates explore (1) the major theme or themes in each passage and (2) the importance of those themes in the lives of Muslims today, and must show knowledge of the original Arabic as well as the translation. The site's own record of the 2058/11 June 2024 paper (decision log E697) gives the wording "(a) themes [4], (b) importance in a Muslim's life today [4]". The 4 and 4 split is **not re-verified against the paper in this draft**.

**Answer pattern for each passage you choose:** (1) name the theme in the passage's own terms; (2) point to the verse number or Arabic word that shows it; (3) for part (b), give one concrete way a Muslim acts or thinks differently today because of it. Do not narrate the passage line by line.

## 6. Sura 1 (al-Fatiha, verses 1-7)

**Syllabus group:** Allah's relationship with the created world (Appendix 1, passage 6)

**Themes it addresses:** God as merciful Lord of the worlds; His mastery of the Day of Judgement; worship of Him alone and seeking help only from Him; the request for guidance on the straight way.

**Link to the group theme:** The first part praises God; the second part is the human response (worship, help, guidance). That two-way relationship is the group theme.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** Rabb al-'alamin, al-Rahman al-Rahim, yawm al-din, al-sirat al-mustaqim.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Separate the praise (verses 1-4) from the human request (verses 5-7). In (b) link to the sura's place in Muslim prayer.

## 7. Sura 2.21-22

**Syllabus group:** Allah's relationship with the created world (Appendix 1, passage 7)

**Themes it addresses:** Humankind called to worship its Lord who created them and earlier generations; the earth, sky and rain as provision; do not set up rivals to God.

**Link to the group theme:** God as Creator and Provider calls people to worship and to avoid shirk; creation is the evidence for the relationship.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** 'ibada (worship), rabb, shirk.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Make the link: He created and provides, therefore worship Him alone. Cite verse 21 for creation and worship and verse 22 for provision and no rivals. In (b), link gratitude and responsible use of what God provides.

## 8. Sura 96.1-5

**Syllabus group:** Allah's relationship with the created world (Appendix 1, passage 8)

**Themes it addresses:** The command to read in the name of the Lord who created; creation of man; God as most bountiful; He taught by the pen and taught man what he did not know.

**Link to the group theme:** God creates human beings and teaches them; knowledge is a gift from Him. The relationship is creation plus teaching.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** iqra', 'alaq, qalam.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today. These verses also appear in the revelation story (subtopics 1.2 and 1.3); be ready to connect them, using what the syllabus and your class texts say.

**How to answer:** Name two themes (creation; knowledge and teaching) and cite the verse. In (b), link to the value of learning for Muslims. Do not add detail of the revelation story unless the question asks.

## 9. Sura 99

**Syllabus group:** Allah's relationship with the created world (Appendix 1, passage 9)

**Themes it addresses:** The earth's shaking on the Last Day; people sorted into groups and shown their deeds; every deed, even an atom's weight of good or evil, will be seen.

**Link to the group theme:** Accountability: God's relationship with humans includes judging their deeds. This is the akhirah theme.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** zilzal (shaking), mithqal dharra (an atom's weight), akhirah.

**How the exam asks about it:** The decision log (E697) records this passage as set in the 2058/11 June 2024 paper; that is a site record, not checked against the paper for this draft. As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Name accountability and the Last Day, cite verses 6-8, and link the idea of deeds being shown to everyday conduct. In (b), give an example such as taking small acts seriously.

## 10. Sura 114

**Syllabus group:** Allah's relationship with the created world (Appendix 1, passage 10)

**Themes it addresses:** Seeking refuge with God as Lord, King and God of mankind from the whisperer who withdraws and whispers into hearts, among jinn and people.

**Link to the group theme:** The believer turns to God for protection; the relationship is dependence and trust.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** a'udhu, rabb, malik, ilah, al-waswas al-khannas.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Name protection and seeking refuge; cite the three titles in verses 1-3 and the whisperer in verses 4-6. In (b), say how this shapes a Muslim's response to temptation.
