## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** A cell in G1 contains 20 chromosomes. How many chromatids does it contain at metaphase of mitosis? **[1]**

A: 10

B: 20

C: 40

D: 80

**2.** Which feature of a chromosome is made of repeated, non-coding base sequences? **[1]**

A: Centromere

B: Histone

C: Sister chromatid

D: Telomere

**3.** State what happens during the S phase of the cell cycle. **[1]**

**4.** Describe the role of the centromere. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why chromosomes condense before mitosis. **[2]**

**6.** Define the term stem cell. **[2]**

| Stage | Chromosomes in the cell | DNA molecules in the cell |

**[4]**

**10.** Outline how telomeres prevent the loss of genes from the ends of chromosomes during DNA replication. **[3]**

**11.** Outline the role of stem cells in the bone marrow in replacing blood cells. **[3]**

**12.** Explain how a mutation in a single cell can result in the formation of a tumour. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. After S phase each of the 20 chromosomes has two chromatids. **[1]**

**2.** **D** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** DNA replicates, so each chromosome becomes two sister chromatids [1]. **[1]**

**4.** It holds the two sister chromatids together [1] and is where spindle microtubules attach during mitosis [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Tight coiling of DNA around histones makes chromosomes short and thick [1], so they can be separated and moved by the spindle without tangling or breaking [1]. **[2]**

**6.** An unspecialised cell that can divide repeatedly by mitosis [1] and whose daughter cells can differentiate into specialised cells [1]. **[2]**

**7.** The new cells carry exactly the same genes as the cells they replace [1], so they can carry out the same functions and the repaired tissue works like the original [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 1 unit: G1; between: S; 2 units: G2 and mitosis (2 marks for all three; 1 mark for two) [2].

**(b)** 320 ÷ 1 600 = 0.2 [1]; 0.2 × 18 = **3.6 hours** [1].

**(c)** Cells are dividing at random times, so the proportion of cells in a phase equals the proportion of time spent in it [1].

**(d)** Cells in G2 and in mitosis both have doubled DNA content, so DNA content alone cannot tell them apart [1]. **[6]**

**9.** (i) 14 [1]; (ii) 28 [1]; (iii) 28 [1]; (iv) 28 [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* At anaphase the centromeres have divided, so each chromatid now counts as a chromosome.

**10.** DNA polymerase cannot fully copy the very end of each DNA strand, so a short length is lost at each replication [1]. Telomeres are long repeated sequences that do not code for polypeptides [1]. The lost DNA comes from the telomere, so genes near the ends are not lost [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Stem cells divide by mitosis repeatedly [1]. Some daughter cells differentiate into red or white blood cells [1], while others remain stem cells so that the supply continues, replacing blood cells that have short lifespans [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The mutation is in a gene that controls the cell cycle [1]. The cell divides repeatedly by mitosis without control [1]. All daughter cells inherit the mutation because mitosis produces genetically identical cells [1], so they also divide without control, forming a mass of cells: a tumour [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Growth: cells dividing at a root or shoot tip, or in a growing embryo [1]. Replacement: cells lining the small intestine or red blood cells [1]. Asexual reproduction: budding in *Hydra*, or new plants from runners or tubers [1]. **[3]**

**14.** In G1 the cell grows and synthesises proteins, including enzymes needed for DNA replication, and makes organelles [1]. In S phase all the DNA is replicated [1]. In G2 the cell grows further and makes proteins needed for division, such as spindle proteins [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a named phase and a named activity. "The cell is busy preparing" is too vague to score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Doubling the chromosome number after S phase instead of the chromatid number.
- Forgetting that chromosome number doubles briefly at anaphase.
- Saying telomeres prevent loss of DNA rather than loss of genes.
- Defining stem cells without the ability to differentiate.
- Leaving out the inheritance of the mutation in tumour formation.
