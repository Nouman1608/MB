## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *commission*. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** non-financial methods of motivating employees. **[2]**

**3.** Halsey Bakery pays its bakers £12.60 an hour. A baker works 38 hours a week. Calculate the baker's weekly wage. **[2]**

**4.** A manager's annual salary is £34,800. Calculate the monthly salary. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which a motivated workforce can reduce a business's costs. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why a fringe benefit might fail to motivate an employee. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Ottery Cars pays each salesperson £1,600 a month plus 2% commission on the value of cars sold. In March, Jess sold £64,000 of cars and Sam sold £28,000.

**(a)** Calculate Jess's total pay for March. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much more Jess earned than Sam in March. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse **one** risk to Ottery Cars of relying on commission to motivate sales staff. **[4]**

**8.** Brennan Builders made a profit of £140,000 last year. It shared 6% of the profit equally between its 28 employees.

**(a)** Calculate the amount each employee received. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **one** benefit to Brennan Builders of profit sharing. **[3]**

**9.** Penlow Call Centre has 40 advisers who handle 9,600 calls a week between them. After a change in management style, the same 40 advisers handle 10,560 calls a week.

**(a)** Calculate the number of calls per adviser each week before and after the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in calls per adviser. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how a change in management style could have caused this increase. **[3]**

**10.** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using training to motivate employees. **[4]**

**11.** Fenner Hotels has high staff turnover among its receptionists. Exit interviews show that receptionists feel their pay is fair but say they have no say in how the front desk is run and no chance of promotion. Evaluate whether Fenner Hotels should give receptionists a pay rise or greater responsibility to improve retention. **[6]**

**12.** Ravel Interiors is a small, family-owned furniture shop with six staff. Cash is tight after a slow year. The owner wants to improve motivation and is considering two options: introducing commission on sales, or offering training courses with the chance of promotion to assistant manager. Justify which option the owner should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A payment to an employee calculated as a percentage [1] of the value of the sales they make [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: styles of management (such as consulting staff), training, greater responsibility, fringe benefits [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** £12.60 × 38 [1] = **£478.80** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** £34,800 ÷ 12 = **£2,900** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Motivated staff are more likely to stay [1], so fewer replacements need to be recruited [1], which saves the cost of advertising, interviewing and training new staff [1]. Also accept: higher productivity leading to lower cost per unit, or fewer errors leading to less waste, with development. **[3]**

**6.** The employee may not value the benefit, for example a gym membership for someone who does not exercise [1], so it does not change how they feel about their work or their effort [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The answer works because it says why the benefit fails for this person, not just that benefits can fail.

**7. (a)** Commission: 0.02 × £64,000 = £1,280 [1]; total pay £1,600 + £1,280 = **£2,880** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Sam's commission: 0.02 × £28,000 = £560, so total pay £2,160 [1]; difference £2,880 − £2,160 = **£720** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Salespeople may push customers into cars they cannot afford or do not need [1] to raise their own pay [1]. Customers who feel pressured may complain or leave poor reviews [1], damaging Ottery Cars' reputation and future sales [1]. Also accept: staff competing rather than cooperating, or neglecting after-sales service, with a developed chain. **[4]**

**8. (a)** 0.06 × £140,000 = £8,400 [1]; £8,400 ÷ 28 = **£300** each [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** All employees benefit when the business does well [1], so builders have a reason to reduce waste and finish jobs efficiently [1], which can raise profit and cut costs for Brennan Builders [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Before: 9,600 ÷ 40 = **240** calls [1]. After: 10,560 ÷ 40 = **264** calls [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (264 − 240) ÷ 240 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Managers may have started consulting advisers and listening to their ideas [1], so advisers feel valued and trusted [1], which makes them more willing to work hard, so they handle more calls in the same time [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Advantage: training builds skills and shows the employee that the business is investing in them [1], so they feel valued and are more likely to stay, saving recruitment costs [1]. Disadvantage: training costs money and time away from work [1], and newly skilled staff may leave for a better-paid job elsewhere, so the business loses its investment [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Receptionists say pay is fair, so a pay rise does not address the cause of turnover [1] and adds to costs every year [1]. Greater responsibility, such as letting senior receptionists plan rotas, responds to the complaint about having no say [1], building trust and achievement so they are more likely to stay [1]. Responsibility alone does not solve the lack of promotion, so it may need training to prepare staff for supervisor roles [1]. Judgement: greater responsibility, because the exit interviews point to lack of involvement, not pay, as the reason for leaving; it is also cheaper [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Knowledge: commission is a percentage of the value of sales; training develops skills and can prepare staff for promotion [1].
Application: Ravel is small, with six staff, and cash is tight [1]; furniture sales can be measured per person, but customers make large, infrequent purchases [1].
Analysis: commission costs nothing unless sales rise, which protects cash in a slow year [1], and gives staff a direct reason to sell more [1]; training and a promotion route give staff a future in the business, so they are more likely to stay [1].
Evaluation: commission may cause pressure selling or rivalry in a team of only six, harming the friendly service of a family shop [1]; training needs money now, which is scarce, and there is only one assistant manager post, so most staff cannot be promoted [1]. Judgement: commission, because it is affordable in a cash shortage and links reward to higher sales, provided the owner sets clear rules on service; training could follow when cash improves [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Either option can earn full marks if the decision follows from the cash position, the size of the business and the nature of its sales.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating commission on the wrong figure, or forgetting to add the basic pay.
- Dividing a yearly salary by 52 when the question asks for a monthly figure.
- Recommending a pay rise when the case says pay is not the problem.
- Explaining motivation methods in general with no link to the business named.
- Leaving out the cost of training or fringe benefits in an evaluation.
- Writing about motivation theories, which the specification says will not be examined.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I calculate total pay with commission?**
Multiply the commission rate by the value of sales, then add the basic salary or wage for the same period.

**How do I pick between motivation methods in an evaluate question?**
Find the cause of the problem in the case, check what the business can afford and what the staff value, then choose the method that fixes that cause.
