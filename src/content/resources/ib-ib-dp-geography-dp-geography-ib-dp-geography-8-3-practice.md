## Section A

**1.** Define the old-age support ratio. **[1]**

**2.** State the approximate natural sex ratio at birth. **[1]**

**3.** Distinguish between people trafficking and people smuggling. **[2]**

**4.** Outline why the demographic dividend window eventually closes. **[2]**

**5.** Outline what is meant by a pay-as-you-go pension system. **[2]**

**6.** Suggest two reasons why son preference may develop in a society. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows an invented country at two dates.

| | Earlier | Later |
|---|---|---|
| Aged 0-14 (%) | 40 | 25 |
| Aged 15-64 (%) | 55 | 65 |
| Aged 65 and over (%) | 5 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the dependency ratio at each date. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the later date offers an economic opportunity. **[2]**

**(c)** State two conditions needed for the opportunity to be realised. **[2]**

**8.** An invented country has 45 million people aged 15-64 and 15 million aged 65 and over. By a future date these are projected to be 40 million and 25 million. Each older person receives a pension of 15,000 a year funded from current taxes.

**(a)** Calculate the old-age support ratio now and in the future. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the pension cost per working-age person now and in the future. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest two policies that could reduce the future cost per working-age person. **[2]**

**9.** An invented province records 112,000 male and 96,000 female births in one year.

**(a)** Calculate the sex ratio at birth. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the approximate shortfall of female births compared with the natural ratio. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain one long-term consequence of this imbalance. **[2]**

**10.** Outline the three main commitments of the international protocol on trafficking in persons adopted in 2000, and give one limitation of this approach. **[4]**

## Section C

**11.** "A youthful population is more of an opportunity than a challenge." To what extent do you agree? **[10]**

**12.** Evaluate the effectiveness of policies designed to change fertility rates. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of people aged 15-64 for each person aged 65 and over [1]. **[1]**

**2.** About 105 male births per 100 female births [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Trafficking moves people by force, fraud or coercion for exploitation, such as forced labour [1]; smuggling is a paid service to cross a border illegally, and the relationship usually ends on arrival [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The large cohorts that created the high working-age share grow old [1], so the share of older dependants rises and the dependency ratio increases again [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Pensions for today's older people are paid from the taxes or contributions of today's workers [1], rather than from savings built up by the pensioners themselves [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two, for example: inheritance or land passing through sons [1]; sons expected to support parents in old age [1]; marriage costs, such as dowry, associated with daughters [1]; cultural or religious roles for sons [1]. Maximum [2]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Earlier: (40 + 5) / 55 x 100 = **81.8** [1]. Later: (25 + 10) / 65 x 100 = **53.8** [1].

**(b)** There are fewer dependants for each person of working age [1], so households and governments can save and invest more, including in education and infrastructure, which can raise growth [1].

**(c)** Any two, for example: enough jobs for the larger workforce [1]; good education and health [1]; effective governance and investment [1]; women's participation in paid work [1]. Maximum [2]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Now: 45 / 15 = **3.0**; future: 40 / 25 = **1.6** [2].

**(b)** Now: 15 million x 15,000 / 45 million = **5,000**; future: 25 million x 15,000 / 40 million = **9,375** [2].

**(c)** Any two, for example: raise the pension age [1]; increase employment among women and older people [1]; admit working-age immigrants [1]; invest in automation to raise output per worker [1]; reduce pension levels [1]. Maximum [2]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** 112,000 / 96,000 x 100 = **116.7** [1].

**(b)** Expected girls = 112,000 / 1.05 = about 106,667 [1]. Shortfall = 106,667 - 96,000 = **about 10,700** [1].

**(c)** For example, in two decades there will be a surplus of young men [1], many unable to find partners, which may increase social tension or the trafficking of women for marriage [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Prevention of trafficking, for example through awareness and reducing vulnerability [1]; protection of victims, treating them as victims rather than as illegal migrants [1]; prosecution of traffickers [1]. Limitation: for example, trafficking is hidden so its scale is hard to measure, or enforcement varies widely between countries, or strict border controls can push migrants towards traffickers [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Indicative content:

- **Opportunity:** a large future workforce and the possibility of a demographic dividend as fertility falls [1]; East Asian economies such as South Korea are often linked to a dividend combined with education investment [1].
- **Market and innovation:** young consumers and entrepreneurs [1].
- **Challenges:** demand for schools, maternal and child health care [1]; need for large numbers of new jobs [1]; youth unemployment and emigration pressure if jobs are not created [1].
- **Momentum:** continued growth adds pressure on land, housing and resources [1].
- **Conditions:** education, health, governance and investment decide the outcome [1].
- **Named example:** accurate use of a country with very high fertility, such as Niger [1].
- **Judgement:** for example, agree only where fertility is falling and policy creates jobs; otherwise it is mainly a challenge [1]. **[10]**

**12.** Indicative content:

- **Criteria:** effectiveness, attribution, unintended consequences, rights, cost [1].
- **Anti-natalist, coercive:** China's one-child policy slowed growth [1]; but contributed to a distorted sex ratio and rapid ageing, and restricted individual choice [1] [1].
- **Attribution:** fertility also fell in countries without coercion, because of female education and urbanisation [1].
- **Pro-natalist:** France's family support is associated with relatively high fertility for Europe [1]; Singapore's incentives have not lifted fertility far [1].
- **Gender and education:** Kerala reached low fertility through female literacy rather than coercion [1].
- **Timescale:** effects take decades, and policies are often reversed as structure changes, as in China and Singapore [1].
- **Judgement:** for example, policies that expand women's choices tend to be more effective and lasting than coercive or purely financial measures [1]. **[10]**

*Examiner insight:* Paper 2 essays on policy reward evaluation against criteria, named examples with accurate dates and a clear final judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the total dependency ratio when the question asks about ageing.
- Calling a sex ratio of 103 abnormal.
- Confusing trafficking with smuggling.
- Judging a policy only by whether fertility changed.
