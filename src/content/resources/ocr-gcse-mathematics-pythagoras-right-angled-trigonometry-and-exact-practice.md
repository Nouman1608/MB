These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, specification Version 2.1 (August 2026), statements 10.05a, 10.05b and 10.05c. Questions 1 to 8 are in the Foundation tier and Higher tier content. Questions 9 to 11 are **Higher tier only**. Read the [study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-pythagoras-right-angled-trigonometry-and-exact/) first and keep the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-pythagoras-right-angled-trigonometry-and-exact-revision-notes/) to hand. See also the [OCR GCSE Mathematics hub](/boards/ocr/gcse/mathematics/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/gcse/mathematics/) and the [free 10-minute diagnostics](/diagnostics/). Unless told otherwise, round lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place. Diagrams are not drawn: sketch each one yourself.

## Questions

**1.** A rectangular field measures 45 m by 28 m.

**(a)** Find the length of the diagonal path across the field. **[2]**
**(b)** How much shorter is the diagonal path than walking along two sides? **[1]**

**2.** A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 14.2 cm and one short side 8.7 cm. Find the length of the other short side. **[3]**

**3.** A triangle has sides 9.6 cm, 7.2 cm and 12 cm.

**(a)** Show that the triangle is right-angled. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its area, and the perpendicular distance from the right angle to the longest side. **[2]**

**4.** In triangle PQR, angle Q = 90°, angle P = 41° and PR = 15.6 cm.

**(a)** Find QR. **[2]**
**(b)** Find PQ. **[2]**

**5.** A ramp rises 0.9 m over a horizontal distance of 4.8 m.

**(a)** Find the angle the ramp makes with the horizontal. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the ramp. **[2]**

**6.** A kite string is pulled straight at 36° to the horizontal. The kite is 28 m above the hand holding the string. Find the length of the string. **[3]**

**7.**

**(a)** Write down the exact values of sin 30°, cos 45° and tan 60°. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that (tan 60°)² + (cos 30°)² = 15/4. **[3]**

**8.** Triangle ABC has a right angle at B, angle A = 60° and AB = 2√3 cm. Give answers in exact form.

**(a)** Find BC. **[2]**
**(b)** Find AC. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the perimeter in the form a + b√3. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) In triangle ABC, D is a point on AC and BD is perpendicular to AC. AB = 9.4 cm, angle BAD = 62° and DC = 6.5 cm.

**(a)** Find BD. **[2]**
**(b)** Find angle BCD. **[2]**
**(c)** Find BC. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A cuboid ABCDEFGH has a rectangular base ABCD with AB = 9 cm and BC = 4 cm. The height AE is 6 cm, and G is the vertex opposite A.

**(a)** Find the length of AC. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of AG. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the angle between AG and the base ABCD. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A pyramid has a square base of side 8 cm. Its apex is 10 cm vertically above the centre of the base.

**(a)** Find the length of a sloping edge. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the angle between a sloping edge and the base. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the angle between a sloping face and the base. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** d² = 45² + 28² = 2025 + 784 = 2809 [1], so d = **53 m [1]**
**(b)** 45 + 28 = 73, and 73 − 53 = **20 m [1]**
*Examiner insight:* A perfect square under the root is a sign you have added correctly. Write the units with the final answer.

**2.** a² = 14.2² − 8.7² [1] = 201.64 − 75.69 = 125.95 [1], so a = **11.2 cm [1]**
*Examiner insight:* The hypotenuse is the largest square. Subtracting in the other order gives a negative number, a clear signal to swap.

**3. (a)** 9.6² + 7.2² = 92.16 + 51.84 = 144 [1]; 12² = 144, so the squares match and the triangle is right-angled [1]
**(b)** Area = ½ × 9.6 × 7.2 = **34.56 cm² [1]**. Using 12 as the base: ½ × 12 × h = 34.56, so h = **5.76 cm [1]**
*Examiner insight:* A show-that answer reads best when both sides are evaluated and a conclusion is written in words.

**4. (a)** QR is opposite P, and PR is the hypotenuse, so sin 41° = QR/15.6 [1]; QR = 15.6 × sin 41° = **10.2 cm [1]**
**(b)** PQ is adjacent to P, so cos 41° = PQ/15.6 [1]; PQ = 15.6 × cos 41° = **11.8 cm [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the sides first (opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse) is the quickest way to avoid picking the wrong ratio.

**5. (a)** tan θ = 0.9/4.8 [1]; θ = tan⁻¹(0.1875) = **10.6° [1]**
**(b)** Length² = 0.9² + 4.8² = 0.81 + 23.04 = 23.85 [1]; length = **4.88 m [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) can also be done with sine, but using the given lengths avoids relying on a rounded angle.

**6.** The height is opposite the 36° angle and the string is the hypotenuse: sin 36° = 28/L [1]; L = 28 / sin 36° [1] = **47.6 m [1]**
*Examiner insight:* When the unknown is the denominator, dividing is needed. Check that the answer is longer than 28 m, because a hypotenuse must exceed a short side.

**7. (a)** sin 30° = **1/2 [1]**, cos 45° = **√2/2 [1]** (also written 1/√2), tan 60° = **√3 [1]**
**(b)** tan² 60° = (√3)² = 3 [1]; cos² 30° = (√3/2)² = 3/4 [1]; 3 + 3/4 = **15/4 [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Exact-value questions ask for surds or fractions, so a rounded decimal answers a different question.

**8. (a)** tan 60° = BC/AB, so BC = 2√3 × √3 [1] = **6 cm [1]**
**(b)** cos 60° = AB/AC, so AC = 2√3 ÷ 1/2 = **4√3 cm [1]**
**(c)** Perimeter = 2√3 + 6 + 4√3 = **6 + 6√3 cm [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Combine surds only when they are like terms. Here 2√3 + 4√3 = 6√3, but the 6 stays separate.

**9. (a)** In triangle ABD, sin 62° = BD/9.4 [1]; BD = 9.4 × sin 62° = **8.30 cm [1]**
**(b)** In triangle BDC, tan C = BD/DC = 8.2997/6.5 [1]; C = **51.9° [1]**
**(c)** BC² = 8.2997² + 6.5² [1]; BC = **10.5 cm [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is the check on part (b): sin 51.9° × 10.5 should return about 8.3. Keep the unrounded BD when you use it again.

**10. (a)** AC² = 9² + 4² = 97 [1]; AC = √97 = **9.85 cm [1]**
**(b)** AG² = AC² + CG² = 97 + 36 = 133 [1]; AG = √133 = **11.5 cm [1]**
**(c)** The shadow of AG on the base is AC, so tan θ = 6/√97 [1]; θ = **31.4° [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the exact √97 rather than 9.85 gives a more reliable angle. Mark the right angle at C in triangle ACG before you start.

**11. (a)** Half the base diagonal = ½ × √(8² + 8²) = 4√2 [1]; edge² = (4√2)² + 10² = 32 + 100 = 132 [1]; edge = √132 = **11.5 cm [1]**
**(b)** tan θ = 10/(4√2) [1]; θ = **60.5° [1]**
**(c)** The base of the right-angled triangle runs from the centre to the middle of a base edge, which is 4 cm: tan φ = 10/4 [1]; φ = **68.2° [1]**
*Examiner insight:* Parts (b) and (c) use different horizontal distances. Say which point on the base you are measuring to, since a clear diagram makes that visible.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the squares of the sides when a short side is wanted.
- Leaving the answer as a square without taking the square root.
- Labelling opposite and adjacent from the right angle instead of from the angle in the question.
- Multiplying when the unknown is the denominator of the ratio.
- Writing a decimal where an exact answer was requested.
- Rounding part-way through, then using the rounded value in the next step.
- Missing units on lengths and areas.
- In 3D, picking the half-diagonal of the base when the distance to the middle of an edge is needed, or the other way round.
