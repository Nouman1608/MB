These notes condense section 1.1, Solids, liquids and gases, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All five outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [solids, liquids and gases study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-solids-liquids-and-gases/); then try the [solids, liquids and gases practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-solids-liquids-and-gases-practice/).

## The five outcomes in one line each

1. **Properties**: solids have fixed shape and volume; liquids have fixed volume but flow; gases fill their container and compress easily.
2. **Structure**: describe separation, arrangement and motion for each state.
3. **Changes of state**: melting, boiling, evaporating, freezing, condensing, explained with particle energy and forces.
4. **Curves**: read heating and cooling curves; flat sections are changes of state.
5. **Gas volume**: higher temperature (constant pressure) gives larger volume; higher pressure (constant temperature) gives smaller volume.

## Particle summary

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Separation | very close, touching | close, touching | far apart |
| Arrangement | regular lattice | irregular | random |
| Motion | vibrate on the spot | slide past each other | fast, random, all directions |
| Shape | fixed | takes container's shape | fills container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | not fixed |
| Compressible? | no | hardly | yes |

Memory hook: a liquid is a "jumbled solid", not a "squashed gas". Its particles are as close as in a solid; only the order is lost.

## Changes of state

| Process | Direction | Energy | Particle explanation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melting | s → l | in | vibrations overcome forces holding fixed positions |
| Boiling | l → g | in | particles throughout the liquid escape; occurs at the boiling point only |
| Evaporating | l → g | in | fastest surface particles escape; any temperature below boiling point |
| Freezing | l → s | out | particles settle into fixed positions as forces act |
| Condensing | g → l | out | slower particles are pulled together by forces |

Melting point = freezing point for a pure substance. Boiling point = condensing point.

## Using melting and boiling point data

- Temperature < melting point: **solid**.
- Melting point < temperature < boiling point: **liquid**.
- Temperature > boiling point: **gas**.
- Liquid range = boiling point − melting point (watch negative signs: 50 − (−20) = 70 °C).

## Heating curve, section by section

```
temperature
   |                         ______/  gas
   |                        / boiling (l + g)
   |            ___________/
   |           / liquid
   |   _______/
   |  / melting (s + l)
   | / solid
   |/________________________________ time
```

- Sloping parts: one state; energy raises particle kinetic energy, so temperature rises.
- Flat parts: two states present; energy overcomes forces between particles, so temperature is constant.
- The boiling plateau is longer than the melting plateau: separating particles fully needs more energy.
- Cooling curve: the reverse. On the freezing plateau, energy released as particles are attracted into fixed positions balances heat lost to the surroundings.

## Gas volume explanations

| Change | Particle reason | Effect on volume |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature up, pressure constant | particles move faster, hit walls harder and more often, gas expands until pressures balance | increases |
| Temperature down, pressure constant | particles slow, fewer and weaker collisions | decreases |
| Pressure up, temperature constant | particles forced closer together into the gaps | decreases |
| Pressure down, temperature constant | particles spread further apart | increases |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kinetic particle theory | the model that all matter is made of tiny particles in constant motion, with energy that rises with temperature |
| Melting point | the temperature at which a solid turns to a liquid |
| Boiling point | the temperature at which a liquid boils throughout, forming bubbles of gas |
| Evaporation | escape of particles from the surface of a liquid below its boiling point |
| Condensation | change of a gas into a liquid as it cools |
| Plateau | flat section of a heating or cooling curve, showing a change of state |
| Heating curve | graph of temperature against time as a substance is heated steadily |
| Cooling curve | graph of temperature against time as a substance loses heat |

## Exam traps

- "Particles expand when heated": wrong. They move faster and further apart; the particles themselves do not change size.
- Liquid particles drawn far apart lose the separation mark.
- Temperature does not change during melting or boiling, however strongly you heat.
- Boiling happens at a fixed temperature throughout the liquid; evaporation happens at the surface at any temperature.
- On a cooling curve the freezing plateau is **at** the freezing point, not below it.
- "Explain" needs particles, energy and forces. "Describe" needs only what is observed or what the particles are like.
- In a gas-volume question, say which variable is kept constant before giving the effect.

## Self-test

1. State **two** properties that distinguish a gas from a liquid.
2. Describe the arrangement and motion of particles in a liquid.
3. A substance melts at −39 °C and boils at 357 °C. State its physical state at 25 °C and at 400 °C.
4. Name the change of state that occurs at the surface of a liquid below its boiling point.
5. Why does a liquid cool down as it evaporates?
6. What is happening to a substance during the first flat section of a heating curve?
7. Why is the temperature constant during boiling?
8. Why is the boiling plateau usually longer than the melting plateau?
9. On a cooling curve, the temperature stays at 63 °C for four minutes. State the freezing point and the melting point of the substance.
10. Explain why a gas can be compressed but a solid cannot.
11. Explain why the volume of a balloon increases when it is warmed.
12. A gas is squeezed into a smaller volume at constant temperature. Describe what happens to the spacing of its particles.

## Answers

1. Any two: a gas has no fixed volume; it fills its container; it is easily compressed; it has a much lower density.
2. Close together but irregularly arranged; the particles move around, sliding past one another.
3. 25 °C: liquid (between −39 °C and 357 °C). 400 °C: gas (above 357 °C).
4. Evaporation.
5. The most energetic particles escape, so the average kinetic energy of the particles left behind falls.
6. It is melting: solid and liquid are both present, and energy is being used to overcome forces between particles.
7. The energy supplied overcomes the forces between particles instead of increasing their kinetic energy.
8. More energy is needed to separate the particles completely (boiling) than to free them from fixed positions (melting).
9. Both are 63 °C.
10. Gas particles have large spaces between them that they can be pushed into; solid particles are already touching.
11. The gas particles gain kinetic energy and move faster, hitting the balloon wall harder and more often, so the balloon expands until the pressures inside and outside balance.
12. The particles are pushed closer together; they still move randomly.
