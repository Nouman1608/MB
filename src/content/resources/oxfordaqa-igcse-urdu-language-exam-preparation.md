OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu (9264) is not tiered and is a linear qualification with no coursework. It is examined through two equally weighted written papers: Paper 1, Reading (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks, 50%), which includes English-to-Urdu translation, and Paper 2, Writing (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks, 50%). First examined in June 2026, it is organised around three themes. Revise these alongside the topic pages on [identity and culture](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-identity-and-culture/), [local, national, international and global areas of interest](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international/) and [current and future study and employment](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-study-employment/). (OxfordAQA's own title for the qualification is simply "Urdu"; this site lists it under Urdu Language.)

## Paper 1: Reading

Paper 1 lasts 105 minutes and is worth 70 marks, half of the qualification. It tests reading comprehension and includes translation from English into Urdu. Because the exam is wholly externally assessed, all your marks depend on what you can do in that sitting.

How to prepare:

- Read a wide range of Urdu texts on the three themes, such as articles, letters, notices and short narratives, and practise saying in one sentence what each paragraph is about.
- Build theme vocabulary lists in Urdu with the English meaning beside each word, and test yourself both ways, since the translation task moves from English to Urdu.
- For translation, practise translating the sense of complete sentences accurately rather than word by word, and check verb endings, gender agreement and word order in your Urdu.
- Read the questions before the text, so that you know what to look for while reading.

## Paper 2: Writing

Paper 2 also lasts 105 minutes and is worth 70 marks, the other half of the qualification. It assesses your written Urdu. The record does not give the number of tasks or word limits, so consult the specification and sample assessments for those, then rehearse writing under timed conditions to whatever lengths they set.

How to prepare:

- Write regularly in Urdu on topics from each theme: yourself and your culture, your area and the wider world, and study and work.
- Plan before you write: choose three or four points, an opening and a closing line, and think of the vocabulary you will need.
- Vary your sentence structures, with connectives, opinions and a mix of tenses, since a range of structures reads better than a string of short simple sentences.
- Leave time to proofread for spelling, diacritics where used, punctuation and agreement.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Paper 1: 105 minutes for 70 marks is 105 / 70 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 105 minutes for 70 marks is also 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Together: 210 minutes for 140 marks is again 1.5 minutes per mark.

On a 10-mark task, then, you have about 10 x 1.5 = 15 minutes, including planning and checking. Writing Urdu script by hand is slower than many students expect, so practise timed writing in script, not just speaking or typing, and add a margin of five to ten minutes for proofreading in Paper 2.

## Question styles and skills

Since the record does not list individual question types, the following skills are the safe general ones for a reading and a writing paper in a language:

- **Locating information**: find the detail in the text that answers the question, and copy only what is asked for.
- **Inference and opinion**: work out what a writer means or feels when it is not stated directly.
- **Translation**: convey the full meaning of each English sentence in accurate Urdu.
- **Extended writing**: respond to the task, organise ideas into paragraphs, give reasons and opinions, and use accurate language.

Read the instruction on every task, as it tells you the audience, the purpose and the theme.

## Worked practice scenario: planning a writing response

Task (original): "Write an article for your school magazine about a family tradition that is important to you, and say whether you think traditions matter for young people."

Outline plan (about five minutes):

1. Opening: name the tradition (for example, a family gathering at Eid) and say why you chose it.
2. What happens: who attends, the food, the prayers and the visits.
3. Why it matters: it keeps families together and connects the young to their roots.
4. Another view: some young people see it as just a holiday.
5. Conclusion: your own opinion, supported by one reason.

Model paragraph (Urdu, with gloss):

عید ہمارے خاندان کے لیے صرف ایک تہوار نہیں ہے بلکہ ایک ایسا موقع ہے جب سب لوگ ایک جگہ جمع ہوتے ہیں۔ میرے خیال میں یہ روایت ہمیں اپنی جڑوں سے جوڑے رکھتی ہے، اگرچہ بعض نوجوان اب اسے صرف چھٹی سمجھتے ہیں۔

Gloss: "For our family Eid is not only a festival but an occasion when everyone gathers in one place. In my opinion this tradition keeps us connected to our roots, although some young people now see it only as a holiday." Notice the contrast connective (بلکہ, "but rather"), the opinion phrase (میرے خیال میں, "in my opinion") and the concession (اگرچہ, "although"), which together show range.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check whether you are sitting Paper 1 (Reading) or Paper 2 (Writing) and the time allowed.
- Revise vocabulary for all three themes in both directions.
- Practise handwriting Urdu at speed so that the script stays legible.
- Read the questions first on the Reading paper.
- Spend a few minutes planning each writing task.
- Answer every translation sentence, even if you are unsure of a word, by using a nearby alternative.
- Keep time for proofreading.
- Do not leave any task unattempted.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu 9264?**
There are two equally weighted written papers, Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing, each worth 50%.

**How long is each paper?**
Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes (105 minutes) and carries 70 marks.

**Is there coursework?**
No. The qualification is linear with no coursework, so assessment is by the two exam papers.

**Does the Reading paper include translation?**
Yes, Paper 1 includes English-to-Urdu translation.

**When was it first examined?**
The first examination was in June 2026, with first teaching in September 2024. OxfordAQA does not offer Urdu at AS or A-level.
