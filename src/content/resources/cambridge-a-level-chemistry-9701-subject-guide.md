Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry (9701) is a two-stage
course that takes physical, inorganic and organic chemistry, together
with practical and analytical skills, well beyond IGCSE or O Level
depth. The syllabus
edition described here covers the 2025-2027 examination series. The full
A Level is assessed through five papers: three AS papers worth 15.5%, 23%
and 11.5% of the A Level, and two A Level papers worth 38.5% and 11.5%.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus contains 37 numbered topics: Topics 1 to 22 are AS content
and Topics 23 to 37 are A Level content. Within each stage the topics
group into the three traditional strands of chemistry.

**AS Level (Topics 1 to 22)**
- *Physical chemistry (Topics 1 to 8):* atomic structure; atoms,
  molecules and stoichiometry; chemical bonding; states of matter;
  chemical energetics; electrochemistry; equilibria; and reaction
  kinetics.
- *Inorganic chemistry (Topics 9 to 12):* the Periodic Table and chemical
  periodicity, Group 2, Group 17, and nitrogen and sulfur.
- *Organic chemistry and analysis (Topics 13 to 22):* an introduction to
  AS Level organic chemistry, then hydrocarbons, halogen compounds,
  hydroxy compounds, carbonyl compounds, carboxylic acids and
  derivatives, nitrogen compounds, polymerisation, organic synthesis, and
  analytical techniques (infrared spectroscopy and mass spectrometry).

**A Level (Topics 23 to 37)**
- *Physical chemistry (Topics 23 to 26):* chemical energetics (lattice
  energy, Born-Haber cycles, entropy and Gibbs free energy),
  electrochemistry (electrolysis, electrode potentials and the Nernst
  equation), equilibria (acids and bases, partition coefficients) and
  reaction kinetics (rate equations and orders of reaction).
- *Inorganic chemistry (Topics 27 and 28):* Group 2 and the chemistry of
  transition elements.
- *Organic chemistry and analysis (Topics 29 to 37):* an introduction to
  A Level organic chemistry, then arenes, halogen compounds, alcohols and
  phenol, carboxylic acids and derivatives including acyl chlorides,
  nitrogen compounds including amides and amino acids, condensation
  polymerisation, organic synthesis, and analytical techniques
  (chromatography and NMR spectroscopy).

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (full A Level) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 75 minutes | 40 | 15.5% |
| Paper 2 | AS Level Structured Questions | 75 minutes | 60 | 23% |
| Paper 3 | Advanced Practical Skills | 120 minutes | 40 | 11.5% |
| Paper 4 | A Level Structured Questions | 120 minutes | 100 | 38.5% |
| Paper 5 | Planning, Analysis and Evaluation | 75 minutes | 30 | 11.5% |

Paper 3 is a practical paper and the others are written examinations.
The weightings above are percentages of the full A Level and add up to
100%.

9701 uses a staged structure. Papers 1 to 3 on their own can be
certificated as a standalone Cambridge International AS Level, where they
are weighted 31%, 46% and 23% of the AS Level. A candidate can instead
continue to Papers 4 and 5 to complete the full A Level. Cambridge has
also published a newer 2028-2030 edition of the syllabus for later
cohorts; if your exams fall in 2028 or after, check that edition rather
than relying on the details here.

## How the parts fit together

The most useful thing to notice about 9701 is that most areas appear
twice. Chemical energetics, electrochemistry, equilibria, reaction
kinetics, Group 2, halogen compounds, hydroxy compounds, carboxylic acids,
nitrogen compounds, polymerisation, organic synthesis and analytical
techniques all have an AS topic and an A Level topic. The A Level version
extends the AS one: Hess's law leads to Born-Haber cycles and Gibbs free
energy; redox and oxidation numbers lead to electrode potentials;
Brønsted-Lowry acids lead to buffers and partition coefficients; addition
polymerisation leads to condensation polymers. A Level work goes far more
smoothly when the matching AS foundations are secure.

Bonding and structure run through everything. Shapes of molecules,
electronegativity and intermolecular forces from Topic 3 explain trends
in the Periodic Table topics and reactivity in organic chemistry, while
transition element complexes at A Level depend on ideas about electrons
and orbitals from Topic 1. Practical skills form a separate thread: Paper
3 tests practical work directly, and Paper 5 assesses planning, analysis
and evaluation in writing, so titrations, rate experiments and
energetics measurements done during the course are valuable preparation.

## Planning your study

A workable sequence alternates strands rather than finishing all physical
chemistry before starting organic chemistry:

- **AS, first half:** Topics 1 to 4 (atoms, moles, bonding and states of
  matter), then begin organic chemistry with Topic 13.
- **AS, second half:** Topics 5 to 12 alongside Topics 14 to 22, so that
  inorganic and organic work reinforce each other.
- **A Level, first half:** Topics 23 to 26, revisiting each matching AS
  topic first, together with Topics 29 to 32.
- **A Level, second half:** Topics 27 and 28, then Topics 33 to 37,
  finishing with organic synthesis and analysis, which draw the whole
  organic strand together.

Your school's teaching order may differ, and that is fine; what matters is
revisiting each AS topic before its A Level partner. Begin past-paper
questions topic by topic as soon as each block is complete, and keep
timed full papers and paper-specific technique for the revision period,
using the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many topics are in Cambridge A Level Chemistry 9701?**
There are 37 topics. Topics 1 to 22 are AS content and Topics 23 to 37 are A Level content.

**Can I stop at AS Level Chemistry?**
Yes. Papers 1 to 3 can be certificated as a standalone Cambridge International AS Level, weighted 31%, 46% and 23% of the AS Level. Alternatively you continue to Papers 4 and 5 for the full A Level.

**Which 9701 paper is worth the most?**
Paper 4, A Level Structured Questions, is worth 38.5% of the full A Level. It lasts 120 minutes and carries 100 marks.

**What is Paper 5 in A Level Chemistry?**
Paper 5 is Planning, Analysis and Evaluation, a 75-minute written paper worth 30 marks and 11.5% of the full A Level.

**Is there a newer 9701 syllabus?**
Cambridge has published a 2028-2030 edition for future cohorts. This page describes the 2025-2027 edition, so check the newer one if you are sitting your exams from 2028.
