Take 0 °C as 273 K, k as 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, R as 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹ and N_A as 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.**
**(a)** Convert 25 °C and −73 °C to kelvin. **[2]**
**(b)** State what is meant by absolute zero. **[1]**

**2.** A syringe traps 80 cm³ of air at 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa. The plunger is pushed in until the volume is 32 cm³. The temperature is constant.
**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the plunger should be pushed in slowly. **[1]**
**(c)** State which graph of pressure gives a straight line through the origin. **[1]**

**3.** A sealed rigid flask contains air at 1.02 × 10⁵ Pa and 293 K. It is heated to 373 K.
**(a)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure rises. **[2]**

**4.** A flask of volume 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³ contains 4.0 g of helium at 300 K. The molar mass of helium is 4.0 g mol⁻¹. Treat helium as an ideal gas.
**(a)** Calculate the number of moles. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of one helium atom. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the number of atoms in the flask. **[1]**

**5.** A bubble of volume 0.80 cm³ forms at the bottom of a lake, at 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 6 °C. It rises to the surface, at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 15 °C. Assume the gas is ideal and none dissolves. Calculate the volume at the surface. **[4]**

**6.** A gas in a cylinder expands against a piston at a constant pressure of 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa. Its volume increases from 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 4.5 × 10⁻³ m³.
**(a)** Calculate the work done. **[2]**
**(b)** In a second expansion the pressure falls uniformly from 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa to 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa while the volume rises from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³. Calculate the work done. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether work is done on the gas or by the gas in part (a). **[1]**

**7.** Smoke in a lit glass cell is viewed through a microscope. Describe what is seen and explain what it shows about air. **[4]**

**8.**
**(a)** State four assumptions made in the kinetic theory of an ideal gas. **[4]**
**(b)** Give the reason an ideal gas has no potential energy, so that its internal energy is purely kinetic. **[1]**

**9.** Show that, for N molecules of mass m in a cube of side l, the pressure p and volume V satisfy pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)². Start from one molecule moving with velocity component c_x towards a wall. **[6]**

**10.** A container of volume 0.020 m³ holds 4.0 × 10²³ molecules, each of mass 5.3 × 10⁻²⁶ kg. The mean square speed is 2.5 × 10⁵ m² s⁻².
**(a)** State the root mean square speed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the temperature. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the average kinetic energy of a molecule. **[1]**

**11.** A vessel holds 0.40 mol of neon at 350 K. The molar mass of neon is 0.0200 kg mol⁻¹.
**(a)** Calculate the average kinetic energy of a neon atom. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the internal energy of the gas. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate c_rms for neon at this temperature. **[3]**
**(d)** Helium has a molar mass of 0.0040 kg mol⁻¹. Calculate the temperature at which neon atoms have the same c_rms as helium atoms at 350 K. **[2]**

**12.** In a Charles's law experiment, dry air is trapped by a liquid index in a uniform capillary tube sealed at one end. The tube is placed in a stirred water bath. The length l of the air column is measured at different temperatures θ.

| θ / °C | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| l / mm | 84.5 | 91.0 | 97.5 | 102.5 | 109.0 |

**(a)** Explain why l can be used in place of volume, and why the pressure of the air is constant. **[2]**
**(b)** A line of best fit has gradient 0.3025 mm °C⁻¹ and cuts the l axis at 81.8 mm. Use these values to estimate absolute zero on the Celsius scale. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage difference between your estimate and −273 °C. **[1]**
**(d)** Give two precautions that improve the reliability of the readings. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** 25 + 273 = **298 K** [1]. −73 + 273 = **200 K** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The lowest possible temperature, 0 K (about −273 °C), where an ideal gas would have zero volume (or pressure) when extrapolated [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Define absolute zero as 0 K, the lowest temperature. "Very cold" is not a definition.

**2.** **(a)** Boyle's law: p₂ = p₁V₁ / V₂ [1] = 1.00 × 10⁵ × 80 / 32 = **2.5 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Fast compression warms the gas, so T would change [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** p against 1/V [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Units of volume cancel in a ratio, so cm³ is safe here. Show the ratio 80 / 32 so the method is visible.

**3.** **(a)** Volume is constant, so p₂ = p₁T₂ / T₁ [1] = 1.02 × 10⁵ × 373 / 293 = **1.30 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Higher temperature means a higher average speed (kinetic energy) [1], so molecules hit the walls harder and more often, giving a larger force per unit area [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Harder" and "more often" are separate ideas. Give both.

**4.** **(a)** n = 4.0 / 4.0 = **1.0 mol** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** p = nRT / V [1] = (1.0 × 8.31 × 300) / 2.5 × 10⁻³ = **1.0 × 10⁶ Pa** (9.97 × 10⁵ Pa) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** m = M / N_A = 0.0040 / 6.02 × 10²³ = **6.6 × 10⁻²⁷ kg** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** N = nN_A = **6.0 × 10²³** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Pair grams with g mol⁻¹, or convert both to SI.

**5.** The mass is fixed, so p₁V₁ / T₁ = p₂V₂ / T₂ [1]. T₁ = 279 K and T₂ = 288 K [1]. V₂ = (3.0 × 10⁵ × 0.80 × 288) / (279 × 1.0 × 10⁵) [1] = **2.5 cm³** (2.48 cm³) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the kelvin values on the page.

**6.** **(a)** Work = pΔV [1] = 1.2 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **180 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Average pressure = ½(3.0 + 1.0) × 10⁵ = 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa [1], so work = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = **400 J** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The gas expands, so it does work **by the gas** on its surroundings [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For a straight-line graph use the trapezium area.

**7.** Bright specks of smoke move in small, jerky, random paths [1]. The particles are visible but air molecules are not [1]. The molecules, moving randomly, hit each particle with unequal numbers on opposite sides at any moment, so the net force changes size and direction [1]. This is evidence that air consists of molecules in continuous random motion [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The visible smoke particles move. The invisible molecules cause it.

**8.** **(a)** Four from: very many molecules in random motion [1]; negligible molecular volume compared with the container [1]; no intermolecular forces except in collisions [1]; elastic collisions [1]. Also acceptable: collision time negligible compared with the time between collisions; Newton's laws apply. **[4]**
**(b)** There are no forces between molecules except during collisions [1], so no potential energy and the internal energy is the sum of the molecular kinetic energies. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare "negligible volume" with the container.

**9.** The rebound is elastic, so the momentum change is 2mc_x [1]. The molecule travels 2l between hits on one wall, so the time between hits is 2l / c_x [1]. Force = rate of change of momentum = 2mc_x / (2l / c_x) = mc_x² / l [1]. For N molecules, the total force is F = (Nm / l)⟨c_x²⟩, and the pressure is p = F / l² = Nm⟨c_x²⟩ / V [1]. Motion is random, so ⟨c²⟩ = ⟨c_x²⟩ + ⟨c_y²⟩ + ⟨c_z²⟩ = 3⟨c_x²⟩ [1]. Hence **pV = ⅓Nm⟨c²⟩ = ⅓Nm(c_rms)²** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question write every step, including the reason for the factor of three, because the final line is given.

**10.** **(a)** c_rms = √(2.5 × 10⁵) = **5.0 × 10² m s⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** p = Nm(c_rms)² / 3V [1] = (4.0 × 10²³ × 5.3 × 10⁻²⁶ × 2.5 × 10⁵) / (3 × 0.020) = **8.8 × 10⁴ Pa** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** T = pV / Nk [1] = (8.83 × 10⁴ × 0.020) / (4.0 × 10²³ × 1.38 × 10⁻²³) = **3.2 × 10² K** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Kinetic energy = 3kT/2 = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 320 = **6.6 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry unrounded values from part (b) into part (c). Early rounding can shift the last figure.

**11.** **(a)** Kinetic energy = 3kT/2 [1] = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 350 = **7.2 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** U = 3nRT/2 [1] = 1.5 × 0.40 × 8.31 × 350 = **1.7 × 10³ J** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** m = 0.0200 / 6.02 × 10²³ = 3.32 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]. (c_rms)² = 2 × 7.245 × 10⁻²¹ / 3.32 × 10⁻²⁶ = 4.36 × 10⁵ [1], so c_rms = **6.6 × 10² m s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** c_rms is proportional to √(T/M), so T / 0.0200 = 350 / 0.0040 [1] and T = **1.8 × 10³ K** (1750 K) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (d) the ratio method is quicker.

**12.** **(a)** The tube has a uniform cross-section, so the volume is proportional to the length [1]. The index is free to move, so the pressure of the air equals atmospheric pressure [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** l = 0 where 81.8 + 0.3025θ = 0 [1], so θ = −81.8 / 0.3025 [1] = **−2.70 × 10² °C** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Difference = (273 − 270.4) / 273 × 100 = **about 1%** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Two from: allow time for the air to reach the bath temperature before reading [1]; keep the whole air column below the water surface [1]. Also acceptable: stir the water; place the thermometer next to the tube. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a precaution with its reason. "Be careful" is too vague to earn credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Celsius temperatures in any gas-law equation.
- Using cm³, dm³ or grams in pV = nRT without converting.
- Using the mass of a mole where the mass of one molecule is needed.
- Quoting (c_rms)² as a speed, or calling c_rms the mean speed.
- Saying "faster molecules" to explain a change at constant temperature.
- Saying that the molecules themselves can be seen in Brownian motion.
