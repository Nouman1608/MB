These questions cover 6.3.1 (Magnetic fields) and 6.3.2 (Motion of charged particles) of the H556 Physics A specification from OCR (Version 3.0, March 2026). Take g = 9.81 m s⁻², e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg, proton mass 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and 1 u = 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. ## Questions

**1.** (a) Describe the field inside and outside a long solenoid carrying a current, and how to find its north end. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the field lines around a long straight wire carrying a current. **[2]**

**2.** A horizontal wire carries a conventional current towards the east. A uniform magnetic field points vertically downwards.
**(a)** State the direction of the force on the wire. **[2]**
**(b)** An electron travels east through the same field. State the direction of the force on it. **[2]**

**3.** A straight wire has 0.25 m of its length in a uniform field of 0.18 T. The force on this length is 0.027 N when the wire is at right angles to the field.
**(a)** Calculate the current. **[2]**
**(b)** The wire is rotated so it makes 40° with the field lines and carries 0.60 A. Calculate the force. **[2]**

**4.** Define the tesla and show that its base units are kg s⁻² A⁻¹. **[3]**

**5.** Magnets on a yoke sit on a digital balance, and a horizontal wire passes through the gap at right angles to the field. The length of wire in the field is 0.045 m. With the balance zeroed, a student records the increase in reading for several currents.

| I / A | 0.50 | 1.00 | 1.50 | 2.00 | 2.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| increase in reading / g | 0.31 | 0.62 | 0.95 | 1.24 | 1.55 |

**(a)** Explain why the increase in reading is proportional to the current. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the gradient of a graph of mass (in kg) against current. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the flux density. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one source of uncertainty and one improvement. **[1]**

**6.** A horizontal rod of mass 12 g and length 0.15 m lies at right angles to a horizontal uniform field of 0.40 T.
**(a)** Calculate the current that makes the magnetic force equal to the weight. **[2]**
**(b)** The current is then doubled. Calculate the initial acceleration of the rod. **[3]**

**7.** An electron moves at 3.2 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ at right angles to a uniform field of 0.080 T.
**(a)** Calculate the force on the electron. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate its acceleration. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe its path and say what happens to its speed. **[2]**

**8.** An alpha particle (mass 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, charge +2e) and a proton each enter a uniform field of 0.40 T at right angles at 1.5 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹.
**(a)** Show that the radius of an orbit is r = mv / (BQ) and calculate it for the alpha particle. **[3]**
**(b)** Compare it with the radius for the proton. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the period of each orbit. **[2]**

**9.** (a) Show that the period of a charged particle in a uniform field at right angles to its velocity is independent of its speed. **[3]**
**(b)** Two protons enter a field of 0.30 T at right angles at 2.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹ and 8.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹. Calculate the radius of each orbit. **[2]**

**10.** A velocity selector has parallel plates 4.0 mm apart with a p.d. of 240 V, and a uniform magnetic field of 0.015 T at right angles to the electric field.
**(a)** Calculate the speed of particles that pass undeflected. **[3]**
**(b)** A proton enters at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. Explain which force is larger and how the proton is deflected. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain whether an alpha particle at 4.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ would also pass through. **[2]**

**11.** Singly charged ions of neon-20 (20 u) and neon-22 (22 u) pass through a velocity selector with E = 3.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ and B = 0.10 T. They then enter a region of uniform field 0.50 T only and move through semicircles.
**(a)** Calculate the speed of the ions that leave the selector. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why both isotopes pass. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the radius of each semicircle. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the distance between the two landing points. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why the heavier ion lands further out. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Inside, away from the ends, the lines are straight, parallel and equally spaced, so the field is uniform [1]. Outside, the pattern matches a bar magnet, with lines leaving one end and returning to the other [1]. Curl the fingers of the right hand along the current: the thumb points to the north end [1].
**(b)** The lines are rings centred on the wire, each in a plane perpendicular to it [1], getting further apart with distance, so the field weakens [1].
*Examiner insight:* State inside and outside separately, and mention line spacing, not just "circles".

**2.** (a) Thumb is force, first finger is field (downwards), second finger is conventional current (east) [1]. The force is towards the north [1].
**(b)** The conventional current for an electron moving east points west [1], so the force is reversed, towards the south [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say explicitly that you reversed the current direction for the electron.

**3.** (a) F = BIL, so I = F / (BL) [1] = 0.027 / (0.18 × 0.25) = **0.60 A** [1]
**(b)** F = BIL sinθ = 0.18 × 0.60 × 0.25 × sin 40° [1] = **0.017 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the equation before substituting, and show sin 40° so the angle used is visible.

**4.** Flux density is force per unit current per unit length, for a conductor at right angles to the field [1]. One tesla is the flux density for which 1 m of conductor carrying 1 A perpendicular to the field feels 1 N [1]. B = F/(IL), so 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ and with 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻², T = kg s⁻² A⁻¹ [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write each step of a units derivation, including the base units of the newton.

**5.** (a) The force on the wire is F = BIL, and L and B are constant [1]. The magnets feel an equal and opposite force, so Δm g = F and Δm is proportional to I [1].
**(b)** Using the end points, gradient = (1.55 − 0.31) × 10⁻³ / (2.50 − 0.50) [1] = **6.2 × 10⁻⁴ kg A⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** B = gradient × g / L [1] = 6.2 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.81 / 0.045 = **0.135 T** [1]
**(d)** Any one: the field is not uniform near the edges, so the effective length is uncertain; use larger pole faces. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert grams to kilograms first, and make the improvement fit the error you named.

**6.** (a) W = 0.012 × 9.81 = 0.118 N [1]. I = W / (BL) = 0.118 / (0.40 × 0.15) = **2.0 A** [1]
**(b)** The new current is 3.92 A, so the magnetic force is BIL = 0.40 × 3.92 × 0.15 = 0.235 N [1]. Net force = 0.235 − 0.118 = 0.118 N upwards [1]. a = 0.118 / 0.012 = **9.8 m s⁻²** upwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the weight before using F = ma, and state the direction.

**7.** (a) F = BQv = 0.080 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 3.2 × 10⁶ [1] = **4.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ N** [1]
**(b)** a = F/m = 4.1 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ [1] = **4.5 × 10¹⁶ m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** The path is a circle in the plane at right angles to the field [1]. The speed is constant, because the force is perpendicular to the velocity and does no work [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason the speed is constant, not just the statement.

**8.** (a) The magnetic force is the centripetal force: BQv = mv²/r [1], so r = mv / (BQ) [1]. For the alpha: r = 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ × 1.5 × 10⁶ / (2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.40) = **0.078 m** [1]
**(b)** For the proton, r = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ × 1.5 × 10⁶ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.40) = 0.039 m [1]. The alpha's radius is twice as large (four times the mass, twice the charge) [1]
**(c)** T = 2πm / (BQ): alpha **3.3 × 10⁻⁷ s** [1]; proton **1.6 × 10⁻⁷ s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use Q = 2e for the alpha; omitting the 2 changes every later answer.

**9.** (a) BQv = mv²/r gives r = mv / (BQ) [1]. T = 2πr / v [1], so T = 2πm / (BQ), in which v has cancelled [1]
**(b)** r = mv/(eB) = 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2.0 × 10⁵ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.30) = **7.0 × 10⁻³ m** [1], and for 8.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹, **2.8 × 10⁻² m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Start the derivation from the force equation and show the cancellation.

**10.** (a) E = V/d = 240 / 4.0 × 10⁻³ = 6.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]. For no deflection QE = BQv [1], so v = E/B = **4.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The magnetic force per unit charge is Bv = 0.015 × 2.0 × 10⁶ = 3.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹, which is less than E [1]. So the electric force is larger and the proton is deflected in the direction of the electric force, towards the negative plate [1]
**(c)** Yes. The condition v = E/B does not contain charge or mass [1], and 4.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ equals E/B, so the alpha is undeflected [1]
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two forces with numbers, then state the direction.

**11.** (a) v = E/B [1] = 3.0 × 10⁴ / 0.10 = **3.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Speed v = E/B does not depend on mass or charge [1]
**(c)** r = mv/(eB). Neon-20: 20 × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ × 3.0 × 10⁵ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 0.50) [1] = **0.1245 m** [1]. Neon-22: **0.1370 m** [1]
**(d)** A semicircle's landing point is 2r from the entry point [1], so the separation is 2 × (0.1370 − 0.1245) = **0.025 m** [1]
**(e)** For equal speed, charge and field, r is proportional to m [1], so the heavier ion has the larger radius and lands further from the entry point [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep four significant figures in the radii and round only the separation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using grams in the balance calculation, or forgetting g.
- Taking θ from the wrong line.
- Not reversing the current direction for an electron.
- Stating v = E/B without saying the two forces are equal and opposite.
