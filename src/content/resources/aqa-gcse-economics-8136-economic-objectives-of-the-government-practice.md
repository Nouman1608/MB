These questions cover **section 3.2.2.1 Economic objectives of the government** in the AQA GCSE Economics (8136) specification for first teaching from September 2017: the principal objectives, conflicts between them, other objectives such as reducing inequality and managing environmental change, and how pursuing an objective can harm groups of people. There are no tiers. All countries and figures are invented. For explanations, use the [government objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-economics-economic-objectives-of-the-government-conflicts-other/) and its [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-economics-economic-objectives-of-the-government-conflicts-other-revision-notes/); for more questions, the [government objectives practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-economics-economic-objectives-of-the-government-conflicts-other-practice/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a principal economic objective of the government? **[1]**

A: Raising more tax revenue

B: Achieving economic growth

C: Increasing the value of imports

D: Reducing the number of firms

**2.** Explain what is meant by full employment as a government objective. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** government objectives other than the four principal objectives. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why a government wants price stability. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how pursuing economic growth may conflict with managing environmental change. **[3]**

**6.** Explain how cutting the top rate of income tax to encourage growth could conflict with reducing inequality. **[3]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Country Q's economic data for two years is shown below.

| Indicator | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Real GDP growth | 2.8% | 3.6% |
| Inflation (CPI) | 1.9% | 4.4% |
| Unemployment rate | 4.1% | 3.6% |
| Current account balance (% of GDP) | −1.2% | −2.5% |

**(a)** Identify **two** objectives that Country Q moved closer to achieving and **two** that it moved further from, using the data. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain how faster economic growth could have caused the change in the current account. **[3]**

**8.** Country S's central bank raises interest rates to bring inflation down. Explain how this could affect **two** different groups of people. **[4]**

**9.** Country T's government plans to reduce unemployment by spending more on job-creation schemes, paid for by borrowing. Explain **one** way this could conflict with another government objective. **[3]**

**10.** Country V aims for inflation of 2%. Its consumer price index rose from 150.0 to 156.0 over a year. Calculate the rate of inflation and state by how many percentage points it missed the target. **[3]**

**11.** Country W introduces a tax on petrol and diesel to manage environmental change. Explain how this could harm low-income households in rural areas and conflict with **one** other objective. **[4]**

**12.** "Achieving economic growth should always be the government's most important objective." Evaluate this view. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A situation where almost everyone who wants a job and is able to work has one [1]; it does not mean zero unemployment, because some people are always between jobs [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Reducing inequality [1]; managing environmental change [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Stable prices let households and firms plan, because they know what money will buy in the future [1], so firms are more confident to invest and people on fixed incomes do not lose purchasing power [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Growth means producing more goods and services [1], which usually uses more energy and raw materials [1], increasing pollution and carbon emissions, so it works against protecting the environment [1]. **[3]**

**6.** A cut in the top rate gives the largest gain to people on the highest incomes [1], so their after-tax income rises more than that of low earners [1], which widens the gap between rich and poor [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Closer: economic growth, because real GDP grew faster (2.8% to 3.6%) [1]; full employment, because unemployment fell from 4.1% to 3.6% [1]. Further: price stability, because inflation rose from 1.9% to 4.4% [1]; balance of payments, because the current account deficit grew from 1.2% to 2.5% of GDP [1].

**(b)** Faster growth raises incomes [1], so consumers spend more, including on imported goods [1]; imports rise faster than exports, so the current account deficit widens [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Borrowers, such as households with variable-rate mortgages, face higher repayments [1], leaving less to spend on other things [1]. Savers receive more interest on their savings [1], so their incomes rise and they gain [1]. Also accept firms that borrow to invest, or workers whose jobs are put at risk as spending falls. **[4]**

**9.** Borrowing to spend more raises total demand [1]. If the economy is already close to full capacity, firms may raise prices rather than output [1], so inflation rises, conflicting with price stability [1]. Also accept: higher borrowing means more interest to pay in future, leaving less for other spending. **[3]**

**10.** (156.0 − 150.0) ÷ 150.0 × 100 [1] = **4%** [1]; 4 − 2 = **2 percentage points** above target [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Rural households often have no public transport and must drive [1], and fuel takes a bigger share of a low income, so they lose a larger proportion of their income [1]. This conflicts with reducing inequality [1], and higher transport costs may also push up prices, conflicting with price stability [1]. **[4]**

**12.** *For the view:*
- Growth raises incomes and living standards [1].
- More output means more jobs, helping full employment [1].
- Higher incomes raise tax revenue for public services without higher tax rates [1].

*Against the view:*
- Rapid growth can push up inflation, harming people on fixed incomes [1].
- Growth can increase pollution and use up resources, conflicting with managing environmental change [1].
- The gains may go mainly to the better-off, widening inequality [1].
- Growth that raises imports may worsen the current account [1].

*Judgement:* Growth is important, but not always the most important objective: when inflation is high or the environment is under pressure, those objectives may need priority [1], so the right priority depends on the state of the economy at the time [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying full employment means nobody is unemployed.
- Naming a conflict without explaining the chain of cause and effect.
- Reading a falling unemployment rate as a worsening of the objective.
- Forgetting "percentage points" when comparing two percentages.
- Answering the 9-mark question without a judgement.
