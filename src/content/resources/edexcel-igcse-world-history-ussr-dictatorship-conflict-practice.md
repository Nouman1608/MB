## Question 1 — the leadership struggle and the economy

**Extract A** (invented for this exercise).

> "Trotsky argued in public and lost; Stalin said little and counted votes. By the time his rivals
> understood what the General Secretary's office could do, it had already done it."

**1(a)** What impression does the author give about how Stalin defeated his rivals? You must use
Extract A to explain your answer. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** effects of collectivisation on the Soviet countryside. **[8]**

**1(c)** "The Five-year Plans of 1928–41 were a success." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the work of Gosplan and the Stakhanovites
- living and working conditions in the towns

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 2 — terror and control

**Extract B** (invented for this exercise).

> "The portrait on every wall and the knock on every door were two halves of the same system: the
> people were taught to love Stalin and reminded, every night, what happened to those who did not."

**2(a)** What impression does the author give about how Stalin controlled the Soviet people? You
must use Extract B to explain your answer. **[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** effects of the murder of Kirov (1934). **[8]**

**2(c)** "Terror was the main reason Stalin kept control of the USSR in the 1930s." How far do you
agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the NKVD and the gulags
- the cult of personality

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 3 — war and after, 1941–53

**3(b)** Explain **two** effects of the German invasion of 1941 on the Soviet Union. **[8]**

**3(c)** "War production was the main reason for the Soviet victory over Germany." How far do you
agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Battle of Stalingrad
- the relocation of industry

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

The author suggests Stalin won through **quiet control of the party machine rather than argument**.
The contrast between Trotsky who "argued in public and lost" and Stalin who "said little and counted
votes" presents Stalin as a patient tactician. The final sentence implies his rivals underestimated
him until too late.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Famine:** requisitioning continued while harvests fell, causing the famine of 1932–33, which
  killed millions, especially in Ukraine and Kazakhstan.
- **Destruction of the kulaks and loss of livestock:** better-off peasants were deported or killed,
  and peasants slaughtered animals rather than surrender them, damaging farming for years.

Credit also: state control of the grain supply, mechanisation via Motor Tractor Stations, and
migration of peasants to the new industrial towns.

### 1(c) [16]

**Agree:** Gosplan's targets drove huge increases in coal, steel and electricity; projects such as
Magnitogorsk and the Dnieper dam; Stakhanovite propaganda encouraged productivity; the industrial
base made wartime production possible.

**Disagree:** targets encouraged false figures and poor-quality goods; consumer goods and housing
were neglected; workers faced strict labour laws, low wages and overcrowding; forced labour in the
gulags; the purges removed managers and engineers.

**Judgement:** success depends on the measure — a success for heavy industry and military power, a
failure for living standards.

### 2(a) [6]

The author presents control as a combination of **propaganda and fear**. "The portrait on every wall"
represents the cult of personality; "the knock on every door" represents NKVD arrests. Describing
them as "two halves of the same system" suggests they worked together, while "every night"
stresses constant fear.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Mass arrests:** a decree after the murder sped up trials of "terrorists", and thousands were
  arrested in Leningrad and beyond.
- **The show trials:** Zinoviev and Kamenev were accused of involvement and later tried and executed
  (1936), beginning the Moscow Show Trials and the Great Terror.

### 2(c) [16]

**Agree:** the NKVD under Yezhov, the Great Terror, show trials and gulags removed opponents and
silenced the population; the army purge eliminated any military threat.

**Other factors:** the cult of personality and propaganda; censorship and socialist realism; control
of education and the rewriting of history; genuine support from those who gained through promotion,
education or Stakhanovite rewards; patriotism.

**Judgement:** terror made opposition impossible, but propaganda and opportunity explain why many
actively supported Stalin.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Huge early losses:** the Red Army lost millions of men captured or killed in 1941, and Germany
  occupied Ukraine and western Russia, besieging Leningrad.
- **Economic reorganisation:** factories were dismantled and moved east of the Urals, and the economy
  was turned over entirely to war production.

### 3(c) [16]

**Agree:** relocated factories and new plants produced vast numbers of tanks (such as the T-34),
aircraft and guns, outproducing Germany by 1943.

**Other factors:** Stalingrad (1942–43) as the turning point; the defence of Moscow; the endurance
of the population and patriotism; improved generalship (Zhukov); Allied Lend-Lease aid; German
mistakes and over-extension; the Russian winter.

**Judgement:** production made victory possible, but Stalingrad showed that leadership and sacrifice
decided where and when the war turned.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining effects of the wrong event (e.g. collectivisation instead of industrialisation).
- Making unsupported general claims about "terror" without named examples.
- Ending an essay without a clear judgement on the question's factor.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is each Paper 1 depth study question structured?**
Each 4HI1 Paper 1 question has three parts: (a) what impression an extract gives (6 marks), (b) explain two effects (8 marks) and (c) a 'how far do you agree' essay (16 marks), with bullet prompts and your own knowledge.

**What do the extracts suggest about Stalin's rise?**
That he won through quiet control of the party machine rather than public argument with Trotsky.

**What is a common error in part (b)?**
Explaining the effects of the wrong policy, for example collectivisation when the question asks about industrialisation.
