## Section A

**1.** Name the natural source of sulfur dioxide given in the syllabus. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** substances in the atmosphere that sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen react with to form acid rain. **[2]**

**3.** Name the device fitted to vehicles that removes oxides of nitrogen from exhaust gases. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why buildings made of limestone are damaged by acid rain. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by *defoliation*. **[1]**

**6.** Suggest why acid rain can fall on a country that has very few power stations or vehicles. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Rainfall pH was measured at four sites downwind of an industrial region. The figures are invented for practice.

| Distance downwind (km) | 10 | 50 | 200 | 600 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean rainfall pH | 4.4 | 4.6 | 4.9 | 5.4 |

(a) Describe how rainfall pH changes with distance from the industrial region. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
(b) Calculate how many times more acidic the rain is at 10 km than at 600 km. **[1]**
(c) Suggest why rain at 600 km is still affected. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how acid rain falling on farmland can lead to reduced crop yields. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how acid rain falling on a lake can affect its aquatic food web. **[3]**

**10.** A coal-fired power station releases 25,000 tonnes of sulfur dioxide a year. It installs flue-gas desulfurisation equipment that removes 92% of the sulfur dioxide.
(a) Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide removed each year. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the mass of sulfur dioxide still released each year. **[1]**
(c) State **one** limitation of using flue-gas desulfurisation at this power station. **[1]**

**11.** A large city has heavy traffic and frequent smog. Describe how catalytic converters and transport policies could each reduce the city's contribution to acid rain. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why international cooperation is important in managing acid rain. **[2]**

**13.** A country generates most of its electricity from coal and its cities have heavy traffic. Discuss the benefits and limitations of the three syllabus strategies for managing acid rain in this country. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Volcanic gases **[1]**.

**2.** Oxygen **[1]**; water vapour **[1]**.

**3.** Catalytic converter **[1]**.

**4.** Limestone reacts with the acid, so the stone is slowly dissolved and worn away **[1]**.

**5.** The loss of leaves from plants **[1]**.

**6.** Wind carries sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen from other countries before they form acid rain and fall **[1]**.

**7.** (a) pH increases with distance, so the rain becomes less acidic further away **[1]**: from pH 4.4 at 10 km to pH 5.4 at 600 km **[1]**.
(b) The difference is 1 pH unit, so the rain at 10 km is 10 times more acidic **[1]**.
(c) Winds carry the pollutant gases long distances before they react and fall as acid rain **[1]**.

**8.** Acid rain makes the soil more acidic and washes out nutrients such as calcium and magnesium **[1]**. It releases aluminium, which damages roots, so plants take up less water and nutrients **[1]**. Acid also damages leaves directly, causing defoliation, so less photosynthesis takes place and crop yields fall **[1]**.

**9.** Acid rain lowers the pH of the lake **[1]**. Acid-sensitive organisms such as some insects, snails and plankton die, and fish eggs and young fish fail to develop **[1]**. Animals that feed on them, such as fish and fish-eating birds, lose their food supply, so the food web is disrupted **[1]**.

**10.** (a) 25,000 × 0.92 = 23,000 tonnes **[1]**.
(b) 25,000 − 23,000 = 2,000 tonnes **[1]**.
(c) Expensive to install and run, or needs large amounts of quarried limestone, or releases carbon dioxide **[1]**.

**11.** Catalytic converters contain a catalyst in the exhaust **[1]** that converts oxides of nitrogen into nitrogen before the gases leave the vehicle **[1]**. Transport policies such as better public transport, cycle lanes or charges for driving in the city centre **[1]** reduce the number of vehicles running, so fewer oxides of nitrogen are produced **[1]**.

**12.** Pollutant gases are carried by wind across borders, so one country's emissions can cause acid rain in another **[1]**; only if all the countries in a region reduce emissions will acid rain fall significantly **[1]**.

**13.** One mark per point; at least one benefit and one limitation for at least two strategies and a judgement for full marks.
- Flue-gas desulfurisation benefit: coal power stations are large single sources, so fitting the equipment removes most of the country's sulfur dioxide **[1]**.
- Flue-gas desulfurisation limitation: high installation and running costs and the need for large amounts of quarried limestone, which also releases carbon dioxide **[1]**.
- Catalytic converters benefit: they reduce oxides of nitrogen from every vehicle fitted with one **[1]**.
- Catalytic converters limitation: older vehicles may not have them, and gains are offset if the number of vehicles keeps rising **[1]**.
- Transport policies: reduce traffic and so oxides of nitrogen, with extra benefits for congestion and carbon dioxide, but need investment in public transport and may be unpopular **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, desulfurisation should be the priority because coal is the main source, combined with converters and transport policies for the cities, and with neighbouring countries also cutting emissions **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: giving "water" only, or "carbon dioxide" instead of oxygen.
- **Question 7(a)**: saying the pH "decreases" with distance when it rises.
- **Question 7(b)**: answering 1 time or 100 times; one pH unit is a factor of ten.
- **Question 8**: describing damage to plants without linking it to soil changes.
- **Question 13**: giving all three strategies equal weight without considering that coal is the main source.
