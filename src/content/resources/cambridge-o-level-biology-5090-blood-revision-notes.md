These notes condense subtopic **11.4 Blood** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the components of blood, how to recognise red and white blood cells, what each component does, clotting, and the transfer of substances between capillaries, tissue fluid and body cells. The full explanations and worked calculations are in the [11.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood/). Then try the [11.4 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood-practice/).

## Components and functions

| Component | Function |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | Oxygen transport |
| White blood cells: lymphocytes | Antibody production |
| White blood cells: phagocytes | Engulfing pathogens |
| Platelets | Clotting: soluble fibrinogen converted to insoluble fibrin; prevents blood loss and entry of pathogens |
| Plasma | Transport |

## Recognising the cells

| Cell | Appearance on a slide |
|---|---|
| Red blood cell | Small, round, pink-red, pale centre, **no nucleus**, most numerous |
| Phagocyte | Large, **lobed or irregular nucleus**, granular cytoplasm |
| Lymphocyte | Round, **large round nucleus filling most of the cell**, little cytoplasm |
| Platelet | Tiny fragments, no nucleus |

## Red blood cell adaptations

- Haemoglobin carries oxygen.
- Biconcave disc: large surface area to volume ratio.
- No nucleus: more room for haemoglobin.
- Small and flexible: squeezes through capillaries.

## Clotting in order

1. Blood vessel is damaged.
2. Platelets help start the clotting process.
3. Soluble **fibrinogen** is converted to insoluble **fibrin**.
4. Fibrin forms a mesh that traps red blood cells and forms a clot.
5. The clot **prevents blood loss** and stops **pathogens entering**.

## What plasma carries

Blood cells, ions, glucose, amino acids, hormones, carbon dioxide, urea, vitamins and plasma proteins.

## Tissue fluid

- Formed from plasma that is forced out through the gaps in the capillary walls under pressure.
- Red blood cells and large plasma proteins stay in the blood.
- **Into cells** (by diffusion): oxygen, glucose, amino acids.
- **Out of cells** (by diffusion): carbon dioxide, urea.
- Most fluid returns to the capillaries; the rest drains into lymph vessels and returns to the blood.

## Calculations

- Magnification = image size divided by actual size. A red cell of 7 micrometres (0.007 mm) shown as 28 mm gives 28 divided by 0.007 = x4000.
- Ratio of red to white cells: 5 000 000 divided by 7500 = about 670 : 1.

## Drawing blood cells

- Sharp pencil, clear single lines, no shading.
- Draw the right proportions: white cells are larger than red cells.
- Show the nucleus in white cells; show a pale centre and **no nucleus** in red cells.
- Label with ruled lines that touch the structure, and add a title and the magnification.

## Using size and features

| Clue | Cell |
|---|---|
| About 7 micrometres, pale centre, no nucleus | Red blood cell |
| About twice the width of a red cell, lobed nucleus | Phagocyte |
| Slightly larger than a red cell, big round nucleus, little cytoplasm | Lymphocyte |
| Tiny, no nucleus, much smaller than a red cell | Platelet |

Always give a feature you can see as the reason for naming a cell.

## Quick comparison of the cell types

| | Red blood cell | Phagocyte | Lymphocyte | Platelet |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | No | Yes, lobed | Yes, large and round | No |
| Number | Most numerous | Fewer | Fewer | Many fragments |
| Function | Oxygen transport | Engulfs pathogens | Makes antibodies | Clotting |
| Colour on stained slide | Pink-red | Stained nucleus | Stained nucleus | Small purple fragments |

## Short model answers

**State the function of red blood cells.** Transport of oxygen.

**Describe how a lymphocyte differs from a phagocyte in appearance.** A lymphocyte has a large round nucleus that fills most of the cell. A phagocyte has a lobed nucleus and more cytoplasm.

**Explain how platelets prevent infection.** They help clotting, in which fibrinogen is converted into fibrin. The clot seals the wound, so bacteria cannot enter.

**Describe how oxygen reaches a body cell from the blood.** It diffuses from the red blood cells, through the capillary wall into the tissue fluid, and then into the cell.

## Exam traps

- Red blood cells have **no nucleus**; white blood cells **do**.
- Lymphocytes make **antibodies**; phagocytes **engulf**.
- **Fibrinogen** (soluble) becomes **fibrin** (insoluble).
- Platelets are **cell fragments**.
- Red blood cells do not leave the capillaries.
- Clotting prevents blood loss **and** entry of pathogens.
- Plasma transports; it does not make antibodies itself.

## Self-test

1. List the four components of blood.
2. State the function of red blood cells.
3. Why do red blood cells have no nucleus?
4. How do you recognise a lymphocyte on a slide?
5. What do phagocytes do?
6. What do lymphocytes produce?
7. What are platelets?
8. What is converted to fibrin?
9. State two functions of blood clotting.
10. Name three substances carried in plasma.
11. Name two substances that diffuse from tissue fluid into cells.
12. A red cell has a diameter of 6 micrometres. What is the image size at x5000, in mm?

### Answers

1. Red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets, plasma.
2. Oxygen transport.
3. To make more room for haemoglobin.
4. Large round nucleus filling most of the cell, with little cytoplasm.
5. Engulf and digest pathogens.
6. Antibodies.
7. Small fragments of cells.
8. Fibrinogen.
9. It prevents blood loss and prevents entry of pathogens.
10. Any three: glucose, amino acids, hormones, urea, carbon dioxide, ions, vitamins, plasma proteins.
11. Oxygen and glucose (or amino acids).
12. 6 x 5000 = 30 000 micrometres = 30 mm.
