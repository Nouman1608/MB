OxfordAQA International A-Level English Literature (9675) is structurally identical in shape to English
Language 9670 but built around literary texts. AS Paper 1 (Aspects of Dramatic Tragedy) is closed-book:
2 hours, 50 marks, 20%. AS Paper 2 (Place in Literary Texts) is open-book: 2 hours, 50 marks, 20%.
A-level Paper 1 (Elements of Crime and Mystery) is compulsory and closed-book: 2 hours, 50 marks, 30%.
The final 30% is either Route A -- A-level Paper 2, a closed exam on Literary Representations using
unseen prose and poetry (2 hours 30 minutes, 50 marks) -- or Route B -- a non-exam assessment of two
essays, one on prose and one on poetry, 1,250-1,500 words each. These notes complement the site's guides
to [Aspects of Dramatic Tragedy](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-english-literature-aspects-of-dramatic-tragedy/)
and [Place in Literary Texts](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-english-literature-place-in-literary-texts/).

## Closed-book and open-book papers need entirely different revision

Two of the qualification's four fixed-choice papers -- AS Paper 1 and A-level Paper 1 -- are
closed-book, meaning no text is permitted in the exam room; AS Paper 2 is open-book. Route A's Literary
Representations paper works on unseen prose and poetry printed directly into the exam paper, so it does
not turn on memorised quotations from a previously studied text the way the closed-book set-text papers
do. **Exam-preparation priority**: memorise specific, well-chosen quotations for the two closed-book
set texts specifically, since open-book navigation skill will not transfer to a paper where the text
itself is unavailable, and unseen-text skill is a third, separate skill again.

## Confirm your Route A/Route B entry early

As with English Language, Route A is an exam on unseen prose and poetry printed into the paper itself,
while Route B is two extended coursework essays on previously studied texts, with their own drafting and
moderation timeline. **Exam-preparation priority**: confirm your cohort's route with your teacher well
before the final unit begins -- Route B candidates need essay topics and drafts planned on a coursework
timeline, not an exam-revision one, while Route A candidates need unseen-text analysis practice instead
of quotation memorisation for that specific paper.

## Elements of Crime and Mystery is compulsory and closed-book at A-level

A-level Paper 1 is the one fixed A-level paper every candidate sits, closed-book, 30% of the
qualification. **Exam-preparation priority**: prioritise memorised quotation coverage for this text
specifically, since -- unlike the AS papers -- there is no open-book alternative to fall back on if
recall is incomplete.

## Route B's two essays need independent development, not exam-style planning

Each Route B essay is 1,250-1,500 words on a single text (one prose, one poetry), assessed as coursework
rather than under timed conditions. **Exam-preparation priority**: Route B candidates should focus
revision time on redrafting for precision and depth across multiple drafts, since the coursework format
rewards refinement in a way a single timed sitting cannot.

## Worked routine: building quotation recall for a closed-book text

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - select 8-10 quotations per text, not more:
Depth of understanding beats breadth of half-remembered lines.

Step 2 - tag each quotation with two or three themes it could
support:
A single strong quotation usually serves more than one essay
question.

Step 3 - practise writing the quotation from memory, not just
reading it:
Recognising a quotation on the page is not the same skill as
recalling it under pressure.

Step 4 - practise embedding each quotation in a full analytical
sentence:
Not the quotation alone -- the sentence that explains its effect.

Step 5 - test recall against a range of essay questions, not
the same one repeatedly:
Confirms the quotation transfers across contexts, not just the one
you rehearsed it in.
```

Step 2 is what makes a small, well-chosen quotation bank flexible enough to answer questions the exact
wording of which cannot be predicted in advance.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: memorise a focused quotation bank (8-10 per text) for every closed-book paper;
  confirm your Route A/Route B entry early; give A-level Paper 1's compulsory closed-book text priority
  quotation coverage.
- **During a closed-book paper**: rely on the memorised, theme-tagged quotation bank rather than
  attempting to reconstruct passages from memory in full.
- **During an open-book paper**: use annotation and navigation speed instead, since the text itself is
  available.

## Self-test

1. Which papers in this qualification are closed-book, and which is open-book?
2. What distinguishes Route A's Literary Representations paper from the closed-book set-text papers?
3. Why does A-level Paper 1 deserve particular priority for quotation memorisation?
4. Why should Route B essay preparation focus on redrafting rather than exam-style timed planning?

**Answers:** 1. AS Paper 1 and A-level Paper 1 are closed-book; AS Paper 2 is open-book. 2. It works on
unseen prose and poetry printed directly into the exam paper, so it rewards unseen-text analysis skill
rather than memorised quotations from a previously studied text. 3. Because it is the one compulsory
A-level paper and is closed-book, with no open-book alternative if quotation recall is incomplete. 4.
Because coursework is assessed on refined final drafts rather than a single timed sitting, so revision
time is better spent redrafting for precision than practising exam pacing.
