## Absorption costing

Under absorption costing a product's **total cost** is its **direct costs** (materials, labour, direct expenses) plus a share of the **production overheads** of each department it passes through.

### The vocabulary

- **Allocation** charges a cost in full to the one product or cost centre it belongs to: direct costs to products, or a department's own indirect wages to that department.
- **Apportionment** divides an indirect cost that several departments share, using a basis that reflects how much each one benefits.
- **Absorption** loads each production department's overhead total onto the units it works on, at an **overhead absorption rate (OAR)**.
- **Under-absorption**: overheads charged to output are less than overheads actually incurred.
- **Over-absorption**: overheads charged to output exceed overheads actually incurred.

### Choosing bases

| Shared overhead | Sensible basis |
|---|---|
| Factory rent, rates, lighting, heating | Floor area occupied |
| Machinery depreciation or insurance | Cost (or carrying amount) of machinery |
| Power used by machines | Machine hours |
| Staff welfare, supervision, personnel | Number of employees |

The OAR is fixed before the year starts, from budgets:

```
OAR = budgeted production overheads of the department
      ÷ budgeted activity of that department
```

Use **machine hours** for machine-led departments, **direct labour hours** for hand work, and **units** only if every unit is identical.

### Worked example 1: Tarnbrook Instruments

Tarnbrook makes violins in two production departments. Carving uses computer-controlled cutting machines; Varnishing is hand work. Budget for the year:

| Overhead | Total | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect wages: Carving 48,600, Varnishing 33,800 | 82,400 | Allocated |
| Factory rent | 84,000 | Floor area: Carving 1,400 m², Varnishing 700 m² |
| Machinery depreciation | 36,000 | Machinery cost: Carving 270,000, Varnishing 30,000 |
| Staff welfare | 21,000 | Employees: Carving 9, Varnishing 12 |
| Electricity for machines | 28,800 | Machine hours: Carving 20,000, Varnishing 4,000 |
| **Total** | **252,200** | |

**Step 1 – allocate and apportion.** For rent, Carving's share is 1,400 ÷ 2,100 × 84,000 = 56,000.

| | Carving | Varnishing |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect wages (allocated) | 48,600 | 33,800 |
| Factory rent | 56,000 | 28,000 |
| Machinery depreciation | 32,400 | 3,600 |
| Staff welfare | 9,000 | 12,000 |
| Electricity | 24,000 | 4,800 |
| **Department total** | **170,000** | **82,200** |

Check: 170,000 + 82,200 = 252,200, the budget total.

**Step 2 – OARs.** Varnishing budgets 13,700 direct labour hours.

```
Carving:    170,000 ÷ 20,000 machine hours      = 8.50 per machine hour
Varnishing:  82,200 ÷ 13,700 direct labour hours = 6.00 per direct labour hour
```

**Step 3 – total cost of one violin.** Materials cost 142.50. Labour is 9 hours at 18 an hour (3 hours in Carving, 6 in Varnishing). The violin needs 5 machine hours in Carving.

| Cost of one violin | |
|---|---|
| Direct materials | 142.50 |
| Direct labour (9 × 18) | 162.00 |
| Carving overhead (5 × 8.50) | 42.50 |
| Varnishing overhead (6 × 6.00) | 36.00 |
| **Total cost** | **383.00** |

### Under- and over-absorption at Tarnbrook

Overheads absorbed during the year = predetermined OAR × **actual** activity.

| | Carving | Varnishing |
|---|---|---|
| Actual activity | 21,200 machine hours | 13,100 labour hours |
| Overheads absorbed | 21,200 × 8.50 = 180,200 | 13,100 × 6.00 = 78,600 |
| Actual overheads | 176,300 | 81,050 |
| Outcome | **3,900 over-absorbed** | **2,450 under-absorbed** |

Net, Tarnbrook over-absorbed 3,900 − 2,450 = **1,450**. Product costs carried 1,450 too much overhead, so 1,450 is added back to profit in the statement of profit or loss.

Why? Carving ran 1,200 machine hours above budget, which absorbed 1,200 × 8.50 = 10,200 extra; it also spent 6,300 more than its 170,000 budget, so the net result is 10,200 − 6,300 = 3,900 over. Varnishing worked 600 hours below budget (3,600 less absorbed) and spent 1,150 below budget, leaving 3,600 − 1,150 = 2,450 under.

## Activity based costing (ABC)

A single labour-hour or machine-hour rate assumes overheads rise with volume. Many do not: cleaning a line, testing a batch or raising a purchase order costs the same whatever the batch size. ABC traces these overheads through the activities that cause them.

### The vocabulary

- A **cost pool** collects the overheads of one activity, for example all line changeover costs.
- A **cost driver** is the factor whose quantity makes the cost of that pool rise or fall, for example the number of changeovers.
- **Attribution** is charging each pool to particular products in proportion to the cost driver each product uses.

Direct costs are still **allocated** to products. For overheads: driver rate = pool cost ÷ total driver quantity; attribute rate × each product's driver use; divide each product's total by its output.

### Worked example 2: Hazelford Drinks

Hazelford bottles two cordials. Classic runs in long batches; Botanical is a small-batch range with many flavours. Production overheads total 288,000.

| Per case | Classic | Botanical |
|---|---|---|
| Cases produced | 30,000 | 6,000 |
| Direct materials | 8.60 | 14.40 |
| Direct labour at 16 an hour | 0.3 hours = 4.80 | 0.5 hours = 8.00 |

**Absorption costing (one factory rate per direct labour hour).** Labour hours = 30,000 × 0.3 + 6,000 × 0.5 = 12,000. OAR = 288,000 ÷ 12,000 = **24 per direct labour hour**. Classic absorbs 0.3 × 24 = 7.20 a case; Botanical absorbs 0.5 × 24 = 12.00.

**ABC.**

| Cost pool | Cost | Cost driver | Classic | Botanical | Driver rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Line changeovers | 96,000 | Production runs | 20 | 60 | 1,200 per run |
| Batch testing | 54,000 | Tests | 40 | 50 | 600 per test |
| Supplier ordering | 30,000 | Purchase orders | 30 | 70 | 300 per order |
| Bottling line running | 108,000 | Line hours | 3,000 | 600 | 30 per line hour |

Attribution:

| | Classic | Botanical |
|---|---|---|
| Changeovers | 20 × 1,200 = 24,000 | 60 × 1,200 = 72,000 |
| Testing | 40 × 600 = 24,000 | 50 × 600 = 30,000 |
| Ordering | 30 × 300 = 9,000 | 70 × 300 = 21,000 |
| Line running | 3,000 × 30 = 90,000 | 600 × 30 = 18,000 |
| **Total attributed** | **147,000** | **141,000** |
| Per case | 147,000 ÷ 30,000 = **4.90** | 141,000 ÷ 6,000 = **23.50** |

Check: 147,000 + 141,000 = 288,000.

| Total cost per case | Classic | Botanical |
|---|---|---|
| Direct costs | 13.40 | 22.40 |
| Absorption total (+7.20 / +12.00) | **20.60** | **34.40** |
| ABC total (+4.90 / +23.50) | **18.30** | **45.90** |

Botanical is a sixth of output but causes three-quarters of the changeovers; ABC charges those costs to it rather than spreading them over Classic's volume.

## Setting a selling price

Either total cost can become a price by adding profit:

```
Mark-up on cost:          price = total cost × (1 + mark-up %)
Margin on selling price:  price = total cost ÷ (1 − margin %)
```

**Tarnbrook's violin (total cost 383.00).** A 35% mark-up gives 383.00 × 1.35 = **517.05**. A 20% margin gives 383.00 ÷ 0.80 = **478.75**; check: (478.75 − 383.00) ÷ 478.75 = 20%.

**Hazelford at a 30% mark-up:**

| | Classic | Botanical |
|---|---|---|
| From absorption cost | 20.60 × 1.3 = **26.78** | 34.40 × 1.3 = **44.72** |
| From ABC cost | 18.30 × 1.3 = **23.79** | 45.90 × 1.3 = **59.67** |

Priced from absorption cost, each Botanical case sells for 44.72 against an ABC cost of 45.90: a loss of 1.18 a case that the absorption figures hide. Classic is priced 2.99 above its ABC-based price, which could cost Hazelford sales to rivals.

## Absorption vs ABC vs marginal costing

### Inventory and profit

- **Marginal costing** values closing inventory at variable production cost. Fixed production overheads are written off in full in the period.
- **Absorption costing** values inventory at total production cost, so some fixed overhead moves into the next period inside closing inventory.
- **ABC** also values inventory at total production cost, but the overhead in each unit comes from driver rates, so the figure usually differs from absorption.

### Worked example 3: Hazelford's year

There was no opening inventory. Hazelford sold 28,500 Classic at 27 and 5,200 Botanical at 52, leaving 1,500 Classic and 800 Botanical in closing inventory. Treat materials and labour as variable and the 288,000 of overheads as fixed; actual overheads and activity matched the budget, so nothing was under- or over-absorbed. Selling and administration expenses were 95,000.

| | Marginal | Absorption | ABC |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1,039,900 | 1,039,900 | 1,039,900 |
| Production cost | 536,400 | 824,400 | 824,400 |
| Less closing inventory | (38,020) | (58,420) | (64,170) |
| Cost of sales | 498,380 | 765,980 | 760,230 |
| Contribution / gross profit | 541,520 | 273,920 | 279,670 |
| Fixed production overheads | (288,000) | – | – |
| Selling and administration | (95,000) | (95,000) | (95,000) |
| **Profit for the year** | **158,520** | **178,920** | **184,670** |

**Reconciliation.** The profit gap equals the fixed overhead carried forward in closing inventory:

```
Absorption: 1,500 × 7.20 + 800 × 12.00 = 20,400   158,520 + 20,400 = 178,920
ABC:        1,500 × 4.90 + 800 × 23.50 = 26,150   158,520 + 26,150 = 184,670
```

ABC profit is highest because each unsold Botanical case holds 23.50 of overhead. Falling inventory would reverse the order.

### Benefits and limitations

| Method | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Absorption | Full-cost prices aim to recover all production costs; fixed overhead matched to the inventory it helped make; simple | Apportionment bases are judgement; volume-based rates ignore what causes overheads; under- or over-absorption; building inventory lifts profit |
| ABC | Costs reflect the activities each product uses, so prices and profitability are more reliable; drivers show which activities to reduce | Costly to set up and maintain; pools and drivers are still judgement; rent and similar costs lack a driver; little benefit if products are alike |
| Marginal | No apportionment; profit follows sales, not output; contribution suits short-term decisions | Inventory excludes fixed production overhead; marginal-cost prices may not cover fixed costs; semi-variable costs must be split |

## Common errors

- Calculating overheads absorbed with **budgeted** hours. The rate comes from the budget; the hours multiplied by it are **actual**.
- Calling an excess of absorbed over actual "under-absorbed". Absorbed > actual is **over**-absorbed and increases profit.
- Charging a machine-hour department's overhead on labour hours (Tarnbrook's violin uses 5 machine hours but only 3 labour hours in Carving).
- Dividing an ABC pool by total units rather than total driver quantity.
- Treating a margin as a mark-up: 20% margin on 383.00 is 478.75, not 459.60.
