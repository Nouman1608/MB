These questions cover sub-topic 4(h) Synthetic polymers of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 4.44 to 4.50. Points 4.48C to 4.50C (condensation polymers, polyesters and biopolyesters) are Paper 2 only, and questions on them are marked **(Paper 2 only)**. Use Ar values: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, F = 19, Cl = 35.5. Draw structures on paper. Revise with the [synthetic polymers study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-synthetic-polymers/) and the [organic chemistry revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-organic-chemistry-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by a monomer. **[1]**

**2.** Name the monomer used to make poly(propene). **[1]**

**3.** Give the structural formula of chloroethene. **[1]**

**4.** State why the repeat unit of an addition polymer does not contain a double bond. **[1]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) Name the small molecule lost when a polyester forms. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Ethene forms poly(ethene).

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of the repeat unit of poly(ethene), using the n notation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the polymerisation is called addition polymerisation. **[2]**

**7.** The repeat unit of a polymer is -[CH₂-CHCl]-.

**(a)** Name the polymer and its monomer. **[2]**
**(b)** The polymer has a relative molecular mass of 62,500. Calculate the number of repeat units (Mr of repeat unit = 62.5). **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of polymer made from 5.0 kg of the monomer, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**8.** Draw the repeat unit of poly(tetrafluoroethene) and give its relative formula mass. **[2]**

**9.** A section of an addition polymer has the structure -CH₂-CH(CH₃)-CH₂-CH(CH₃)-.

**(a)** Deduce the structural formula of the monomer. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the monomer. **[1]**

**10.** Poly(chloroethene) is disposed of by burning. It burns: C₂H₃Cl + 2.5O₂ → 2CO₂ + H₂O + HCl.

**(a)** Name the toxic gas formed that contains chlorine. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of this gas formed when 12.5 g of the polymer burns completely (Mr of repeat unit = 62.5; Mr of HCl = 36.5). **[3]**
**(c)** Give two other problems with disposing of addition polymers. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why addition polymers are not biodegradable, and state one problem this causes. **[3]**

**12.** (Paper 2 only) A polyester is made from butanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₂COOH (Mr = 118), and ethane-1,2-diol, HOCH₂CH₂OH (Mr = 62).

**(a)** Explain why this is called condensation polymerisation. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the repeat unit of the polyester. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of water lost when 10 mol of each monomer react completely, and the mass of polyester formed. **[3]**
**(d)** State why a biopolyester is better for the environment than poly(ethene). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A small molecule that can join with many others to make a polymer [1].

**2.** Propene [1].

**3.** CH₂=CHCl [1].

**4.** The double bond has opened (it has become a single bond) during polymerisation [1].

**5.** Water [1].

**6. (a)** Two carbon atoms joined by a single bond inside brackets [1], each carbon bonded to two hydrogens, with open bonds at both ends of the bracket and n outside it: -( CH₂-CH₂ )ₙ- drawn with every bond [1].
**(b)** Many ethene monomers join together by their C=C bonds opening [1], and only one product forms, with nothing else lost [1].

**7. (a)** Poly(chloroethene) (PVC) [1]; the monomer is chloroethene [1].
**(b)** 62,500 / 62.5 = 1,000 [1].
**(c)** 5.0 kg [1], because no other product forms in addition polymerisation, so mass is conserved [1].

**8.** -( CF₂-CF₂ )ₙ- with each carbon bonded to two fluorine atoms [1]; Mr = 24 + 4 x 19 = 100 [1].

**9. (a)** The repeat unit is -CH₂-CH(CH₃)-, so the monomer is CH₂=CHCH₃ [1].
**(b)** Propene [1].

**10. (a)** Hydrogen chloride [1].
**(b)** Moles of repeat unit = 12.5 / 62.5 = 0.20 mol [1]. Moles of HCl = 0.20 mol (1 : 1) [1]. Mass = 0.20 x 36.5 = 7.3 g [1].
**(c)** Any two from: they are not biodegradable, so they remain in landfill for a long time; they are unreactive (inert), so they pile up; burning produces carbon dioxide (and carbon monoxide if incomplete); they harm wildlife as litter [2].

**11.** The polymer chains have strong covalent bonds (C-C and C-H) and are unreactive (inert) [1]. Microorganisms cannot break them down [1]. A problem: they stay in the environment for hundreds of years in landfill or as litter, harming wildlife [1].

**12. (a)** Two different monomers join [1] and a small molecule (water) is lost each time a link forms [1].
**(b)** -OC-CH₂CH₂-CO-O-CH₂CH₂-O- (one repeat unit with two ester links), with open bonds at each end [2] (1 mark for the correct ester links, 1 mark for the correct number of carbon atoms in each part).
**(c)** Water lost = 2 x 10 mol = 20 mol, 20 x 18 = 360 g [1]. Total mass of monomers = 10 x 118 + 10 x 62 = 1,180 + 620 = 1,800 g [1]. Polyester = 1,800 - 360 = 1,440 g [1].
**(d)** It can be broken down by microorganisms (it is biodegradable), whereas poly(ethene) is not [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the double bond in the repeat unit.
- Forgetting the side groups (CH₃, Cl, F) on the repeat unit.
- Naming the monomer as an alkane.
- Giving "carbon dioxide" as the only problem with burning.
- Forgetting the water lost in condensation polymerisation.
- Missing the two ester links in the polyester repeat unit.
