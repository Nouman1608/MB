These notes condense sub-topic **3.3 Macroeconomic objectives** from the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022). Constructing a weighted price index is an **HL-only calculation**. For full explanations read the [3.3 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-3/); then test yourself with the [3.3 practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-3-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unemployment | People of working age, willing and able to work and actively seeking work, without a job |
| Labour force | Employed plus unemployed |
| Hidden unemployment | Jobless people not counted, such as discouraged workers |
| Underemployment | Working fewer hours, or at a lower skill level, than wanted |
| Natural rate of unemployment | Frictional + seasonal + structural unemployment; no cyclical unemployment |
| Inflation | Sustained rise in the general price level |
| Disinflation | A fall in the rate of inflation (prices still rising) |
| Deflation | Sustained fall in the general price level |
| Consumer price index (CPI) | Index of the price of a typical household basket |
| Demand-pull inflation | AD rising faster than productive capacity |
| Cost-push inflation | Rising costs shift SRAS left |
| Phillips curve | Relationship between unemployment and inflation |
| Government debt | Total owed by the government; built up from past budget deficits |
| Debt servicing | Interest (and repayments) paid on government debt |

## Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Unemployment rate | Unemployed ÷ labour force × 100 |
| Inflation rate | (CPI new - CPI old) ÷ CPI old × 100 |
| Weighted price index (HL only) | Σ(weight × item index) ÷ Σ weights |
| Growth rate | (real GDP new - real GDP old) ÷ real GDP old × 100 |
| Debt-to-GDP ratio | Government debt ÷ nominal GDP × 100 |

**Worked reminders.**

- Labour force 24 million, unemployed 1.8 million: 1.8 ÷ 24 × 100 = **7.5%**.
- CPI from 140.0 to 145.6: 5.6 ÷ 140 × 100 = **4.0%**.
- (HL only) Food weight 50, index 104; energy weight 20, index 115; clothing weight 30, index 98: (5,200 + 2,300 + 2,940) ÷ 100 = **104.4**, so prices rose 4.4%. Clothing prices fell, but energy's rise outweighs it.
- Debt 300 billion, GDP 400 billion: debt-to-GDP **75%**.

## Unemployment summary

| Type | Cause | Policy fit |
|---|---|---|
| Frictional | Moving between jobs | Better job information |
| Seasonal | Demand varies with seasons | Diversify local activity |
| Structural | Skills or location mismatch | Retraining, mobility support |
| Cyclical (demand-deficient) | Falling AD | Expansionary demand-side policy |

**Costs:** lost output, lower tax revenue, higher benefit spending, deskilling, inequality, health and social problems.

## Inflation summary

- **Costs of inflation:** lower real value of fixed incomes; savers lose when interest rates lag; uncertainty deters investment; exports less competitive.
- **Costs of deflation:** delayed spending; heavier real debt burden; falling profits, investment and jobs.
- **"Good" vs "bad" deflation:** rising AS and productivity versus collapsing AD.
- **CPI problems:** different spending patterns; quality changes; new products; substitution.

## Phillips curve

- **SRPC:** downward-sloping; lower unemployment with higher inflation.
- **LRPC:** vertical at the natural rate of unemployment.
- Pushing unemployment below the natural rate raises inflation expectations; SRPC shifts up; unemployment returns to the natural rate at higher inflation.
- **Stagflation** (higher inflation and unemployment together) follows negative supply shocks.

## Government debt

- Debt is a **stock**; a deficit is a **yearly flow** that adds to it.
- Costs of high debt: debt servicing costs with an opportunity cost; risk of credit rating downgrades and higher borrowing costs; less room for fiscal policy in a recession.
- The debt-to-GDP ratio can fall even with a deficit if nominal GDP grows fast enough.

## Conflicts

- Unemployment vs inflation (SRPC).
- Growth vs inflation (inflationary gap).
- Growth vs debt (borrowing to stimulate).
- Growth vs current account and environment.
- Supply-side growth can ease these conflicts.

## Ready-made consequence chains

- Unexpected inflation → interest rates lag behind → real interest rates fall → savers lose, borrowers gain.
- Long-term unemployment → skills fade → lower employability → structural unemployment persists.
- Higher debt → more interest paid → less spending on public services, or higher taxes later.

## Exam traps

- Dividing by population instead of the labour force.
- Calling disinflation deflation.
- Using an unweighted average in the HL index.
- Treating debt and deficit as the same.
- Drawing the LRPC as downward-sloping.
- Assuming every kind of unemployment responds to higher AD.

## Quick self-test

1. Define unemployment.
2. Labour force 12 million, unemployed 0.9 million. Calculate the unemployment rate.
3. Add 0.3 million discouraged workers to question 2. Recalculate.
4. Distinguish disinflation from deflation.
5. CPI rises from 110.0 to 112.2. Calculate inflation.
6. (HL only) Two items: weight 60, index 110; weight 40, index 95. Calculate the weighted index.
7. Why can the CPI overstate inflation when products improve?
8. Which type of unemployment is caused by falling AD?
9. What is the natural rate of unemployment?
10. Why is the LRPC vertical?
11. Debt is 250 billion and GDP 500 billion. The deficit is 20 billion and nominal GDP grows 3%. Calculate the new debt-to-GDP ratio.
12. Give two costs of high government debt.

### Answers

1. People of working age who are willing and able to work and actively seeking work but have no job.
2. 0.9 ÷ 12 × 100 = **7.5%**.
3. 1.2 ÷ 12.3 × 100 ≈ **9.8%**.
4. Disinflation: inflation falls but prices still rise. Deflation: the price level falls.
5. 2.2 ÷ 110 × 100 = **2.0%**.
6. (6,600 + 3,800) ÷ 100 = **104.0**.
7. Part of a price rise pays for better quality, not just higher prices.
8. Cyclical (demand-deficient).
9. Frictional + seasonal + structural unemployment, when the economy is at potential output.
10. Expectations adjust, so attempts to hold unemployment below the natural rate only raise inflation.
11. Debt 270; GDP 515; 270 ÷ 515 × 100 ≈ **52.4%** (up from 50%).
12. Any two: debt servicing costs; credit rating downgrades; less room for fiscal policy.
