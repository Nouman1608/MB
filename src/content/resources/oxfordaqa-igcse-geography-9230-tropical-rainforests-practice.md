## Section A

**1.** Name the layer of a rainforest that contains the tallest trees. **[1]**

**2.** State the typical temperature range of a tropical rainforest. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** adaptations of rainforest plants. **[2]**

**4.** What is meant by selective logging? **[1]**

**5.** Name **two** causes of deforestation. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain why rainforest soils contain few nutrients even though the forest is lush. **[3]**

**7.** The table shows the area of forest cleared each year in an invented rainforest region.

| Year | Area cleared (km²) |
|---|---|
| 2000 | 18 000 |
| 2005 | 25 000 |
| 2010 | 7 000 |
| 2015 | 6 000 |
| 2020 | 11 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the change in the area cleared between 2005 and 2010. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the trend shown by the data. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons why the rate of clearance might rise again after 2015. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how plants and animals in a rainforest depend on each other. **[4]**

**9.** For a named rainforest, explain the causes of deforestation. **[6]**

**10.** Explain how deforestation can lead to soil erosion. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a loss of biodiversity matters. **[3]**

**12.** For a named rainforest, assess the economic benefits and the environmental costs of deforestation. **[6]**

**13.** "Ecotourism is the best way of managing a tropical rainforest sustainably." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Emergent layer [1]. **[1]**

**2.** About 26 to 28°C (accept 25 to 30°C) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two from: drip tips; waxy leaves; buttress roots; shallow roots; lianas; epiphytes; tall emergent trees [1 each]. **[2]**

**4.** Cutting only some mature trees, leaving the rest standing [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two from: cattle ranching; subsistence farming; commercial farming (for example soya); logging; road building; mining; dams; settlement; population growth [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.** Heat and moisture cause dead material to decompose very quickly [1]. The nutrients are taken up at once by shallow roots and stored in the trees [1]. Heavy rain leaches any nutrients left in the soil [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 7 000 - 25 000 = decrease of 18 000 km² [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Clearance rose from 18 000 km² in 2000 to a peak of 25 000 km² in 2005 [1]. It then fell sharply to 7 000 km² in 2010 and 6 000 km² in 2015 [1]. It then rose again to 11 000 km² in 2020, so the trend is not constant [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Any two from: weaker enforcement of laws; higher prices for beef, soya or minerals; new roads or settlement; a change of government policy; illegal logging or mining [1 each]. **[2]**

**8.** Plants provide food and shelter for animals [1]. Animals pollinate flowers and spread seeds, helping plants to reproduce [1]. Animal waste and dead animals decompose and return nutrients to the soil for plants [1]. If one species declines, others that depend on it are affected, because food webs are linked [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Named rainforest [1]. Any causes with developed detail, up to 5 further marks. For example in the Amazon: cattle ranching clears large areas for pasture and beef for export [2]; road building such as the Trans-Amazonian highway opened up the forest to settlers and loggers [2]; mining and dams clear forest and flood land [2]; logging for hardwoods creates tracks that farmers then use [2]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**10.** Trees intercept rain and their roots bind soil [1]. When trees are removed, rain hits bare soil directly and washes the topsoil away [1]. The soil flows into rivers, which become silted, and the land becomes less fertile [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Rainforests hold a very large share of the world's species, so loss is large [1]. Many species provide medicines and food that may be of future value [1]. Biodiversity makes ecosystems more resilient, and once species are extinct they cannot be recovered [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Named rainforest [1]. Economic benefits, for example jobs and income from cattle, soya, timber and minerals; export earnings; development of roads [2]. Environmental costs, for example soil erosion, loss of habitats and species, carbon dioxide release adding to climate change [2]. Assessment: the benefits are often short-term because soils are exhausted, while the environmental costs are long-term and may be irreversible [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: ecotourism gives forest a value in its standing state, which gives local people income without clearing [1].
- Agree: it can pay for conservation and create jobs for guides and rangers [1].
- Agree: it raises awareness and low impact keeps habitats intact [1].
- Disagree: not all areas are accessible or attractive to tourists, so it cannot protect the whole forest [1].
- Disagree: too many visitors can cause damage and pollution, and money may not reach local people [1].
- Disagree: other strategies such as protected areas, certification and debt reduction work at a larger scale [1].
- Judgement: ecotourism is useful locally but limited, so it works best as part of a combination of strategies; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing about the rainforest in general in case-study questions, with no named place.
- Describing the soil as fertile.
- Quoting figures from a table without units or years in a "describe the trend" question.
- Giving a cause of deforestation without saying how it leads to clearance.
- Listing management strategies with no limitation.
- Judgements that have no reasoning.
