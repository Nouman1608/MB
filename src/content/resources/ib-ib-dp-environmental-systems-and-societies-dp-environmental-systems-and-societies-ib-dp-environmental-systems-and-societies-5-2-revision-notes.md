## Condensed recall

### Food system

- Stages: inputs → production → processing → packaging → transport → retail → consumption → waste.
- Impacts at every stage; reducing waste downstream reduces land, water and fertiliser needed upstream.
- Agriculture is the largest human use of land; it links to biodiversity loss, water use and emissions.

### Nitrogen

- **Haber-Bosch process** (early twentieth century): industrial ammonia from atmospheric nitrogen; energy-intensive.
- Surplus nitrogen is lost as nitrate (leaching, eutrophication), nitrous oxide (greenhouse gas) and ammonia (air pollution).
- **NUE** = N in harvest ÷ N applied × 100. **Surplus** = N applied − N in harvest.
- Short reminder: 160 kg applied, 104 kg harvested → NUE = **65%**, surplus = **56 kg ha⁻¹**.
- Raise NUE: soil testing, split applications, precision spreading, legumes, cover crops.

### Yield and land

- Land needed = production required ÷ yield.
- Higher yield spares land, but uses more inputs per hectare, and profits can drive more clearance.

### Green Revolution (1940s–1970s)

- High-yielding wheat and rice varieties plus irrigation, fertiliser and pesticides.
- Norman Borlaug: dwarf wheat; Nobel Peace Prize 1970.
- Benefits: much higher grain output, less land per tonne, cheaper food.
- Costs: pollution, groundwater depletion, fewer varieties grown, gains concentrated among wealthier farmers.

### GM crops

- **Bt crops**: bacterial gene → protein toxic to certain insect pests; less spraying.
- **Herbicide-tolerant crops**: survive a specific herbicide.
- For: yields, less insecticide, possible drought tolerance or added nutrients.
- Against: resistance in pests and weeds, gene flow, seed patents, possible rise in herbicide use.

### Pollinators

- Declines linked to habitat loss, disease and pesticides.
- **Neonicotinoids**: systemic, present in pollen and nectar; EU banned outdoor use of three in 2018.
- **Integrated pest management**: rotation, resistant varieties, natural predators, spraying only above a threshold.

### Security and sovereignty

- **Food security**: access to enough safe, nutritious food.
- **Food sovereignty** (promoted by La Via Campesina): peoples' right to define their own food and farming systems; local production, small farmers, control of seed and land.
- Cheap imports can raise security while weakening sovereignty.

### Sustainable models

| Model | Core idea | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainable intensification | More food per hectare, fewer impacts | May still rely on fossil-fuel inputs |
| Regenerative agriculture | Rebuild soil and biodiversity | Benefits take years; yields may dip at first |
| Agroforestry | Trees with crops or livestock | Trees compete for light and water |
| Organic | No synthetic fertiliser or pesticide | Lower yields, so more land |
| Polyculture | Several crops together | Harder to mechanise |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Food system | All stages from inputs to waste |
| Haber-Bosch process | Industrial manufacture of ammonia from nitrogen gas |
| Nitrogen use efficiency | Share of applied nitrogen that ends up in the harvest |
| Nitrogen surplus | Applied nitrogen not removed in the harvest |
| Green Revolution | Spread of high-yielding varieties and inputs, 1940s–1970s |
| GM crop | Crop whose DNA has been altered directly |
| Bt crop | GM crop producing an insecticidal protein from *Bacillus thuringiensis* |
| Integrated pest management | Combined methods that minimise pesticide use |
| Food sovereignty | Right of peoples to control their own food systems |
| Sustainable intensification | Raising yields while reducing environmental impact |
| Regenerative agriculture | Farming that aims to rebuild soil health |
| Agroforestry | Combining trees with crops or livestock |

## Exam traps

- NUE is harvested ÷ applied, not the other way round.
- Intensification spares land only if the spared land is protected.
- The Green Revolution needs both benefits and costs, social as well as environmental.
- "GM crops contain pesticide": explain the Bt protein and its target pests.
- Food security and food sovereignty are different ideas.
- No single sustainable model fits everywhere: name the context.

## Self-test

1. List four stages of a food system after production.
2. State three forms in which surplus nitrogen is lost from farmland.
3. 200 kg N ha⁻¹ is applied and 120 kg N ha⁻¹ is harvested. Calculate NUE and the surplus.
4. How much land is needed to grow 9 000 t of rice at 4.5 t ha⁻¹?
5. Give two environmental costs of the Green Revolution.
6. Who developed high-yielding dwarf wheat, and what award did he receive in 1970?
7. How does a Bt crop reduce insecticide use?
8. Give two arguments against GM crops.
9. Why are neonicotinoids a particular concern for bees?
10. Define integrated pest management.
11. Distinguish between food security and food sovereignty.
12. Give one trade-off of organic farming.

### Answers

1. Any four: processing, packaging, transport, retail, consumption, waste disposal.
2. Nitrate leached to water; nitrous oxide released to air; ammonia gas to air.
3. NUE = 120 ÷ 200 × 100 = **60%**; surplus = **80 kg ha⁻¹**.
4. 9 000 ÷ 4.5 = **2 000 ha**.
5. Any two: water pollution from fertilisers and pesticides; groundwater depletion; fewer crop varieties grown.
6. Norman Borlaug; the Nobel Peace Prize.
7. It makes its own protein toxic to target insect pests, so fewer sprays are needed.
8. Any two: pest or weed resistance; gene flow to wild relatives; reliance on patented seed; possible increase in herbicide use.
9. They are taken up throughout the plant, including pollen and nectar that bees collect.
10. Combining rotation, resistant varieties, natural predators and targeted spraying, used only when pests pass a threshold.
11. Food security is access to enough safe, nutritious food; food sovereignty is the right of peoples to control their own food and farming systems.
12. Lower yields per hectare, so more land is needed for the same food.
