These notes condense section **3.3.9.2 Acylation** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2, and Required practical 10 can be tested on Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Acylation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-acylation/), and exam-style questions are in the [Acylation practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-acylation-practice/). The previous sub-topic is covered in the [Carboxylic acids and derivatives revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-carboxylic-acids-and-derivatives-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Draw the structures of **acid anhydrides, acyl chlorides and amides**.
- Write the **nucleophilic addition–elimination** reactions of **water, alcohols, ammonia and primary amines** with acyl chlorides and acid anhydrides.
- Explain the **industrial advantages of ethanoic anhydride** over ethanoyl chloride for making aspirin.
- **Outline the mechanism** for acyl chlorides with water, alcohols, ammonia and primary amines.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acyl group | R–C=O |
| Acyl chloride | R–COCl |
| Acid anhydride | (RCO)₂O |
| Amide | R–CONH₂ (or with a group on nitrogen) |
| Acylation | Introducing an acyl group into a molecule |
| Nucleophilic addition–elimination | Nucleophile adds to C=O, then a group is eliminated |
| Leaving group | The atom or group that departs with the bonding pair |

## Structures

| Compound | Formula | Name |
|---|---|---|
| Ethanoyl chloride | CH₃COCl | Acyl chloride |
| Ethanoic anhydride | (CH₃CO)₂O | Acid anhydride |
| Ethanamide | CH₃CONH₂ | Amide |
| N-methylethanamide | CH₃CONHCH₃ | N-substituted amide |

## Reactivity

- The carbonyl carbon is **strongly δ+**, because it is bonded to two electronegative atoms (O and Cl).
- **Cl⁻** is a good leaving group.
- Acyl chlorides react with weak nucleophiles at room temperature.
- Acid anhydrides are **less reactive** than acyl chlorides, so they react more slowly and need warming.

## The mechanism in four arrows

1. Lone pair of the nucleophile → δ+ carbon of C=O.
2. C=O pi bond → onto oxygen (O⁻). **Intermediate** formed.
3. Lone pair on O⁻ → reforms C=O.
4. C–Cl bond → onto Cl, leaving as Cl⁻.

Then a proton is lost from the atom that attacked (taken by Cl⁻, or by a second NH₃ or amine).

## Products from acyl chlorides

| Nucleophile | Product | Other product |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | Carboxylic acid | HCl |
| R′OH | Ester | HCl |
| NH₃ (2 mol) | Amide | NH₄Cl |
| R′NH₂ (2 mol) | N-substituted amide | R′NH₃Cl |

Equations for ethanoyl chloride:

```
CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl
CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + HCl
CH₃COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + NH₄Cl
CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃Cl
```

## Products from acid anhydrides

| Nucleophile | Product | Other product |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | Carboxylic acid | Carboxylic acid |
| R′OH | Ester | Carboxylic acid |
| NH₃ | Amide | Carboxylic acid (or its ammonium salt) |
| R′NH₂ | N-substituted amide | Carboxylic acid (or its salt) |

```
(CH₃CO)₂O + H₂O → 2CH₃COOH
(CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + CH₃COOH
```

## Observations

- Acyl chloride with water: **steamy white fumes** of HCl, vigorous reaction.
- Acyl chloride with ammonia or an amine: **white solid** (ammonium or alkylammonium chloride) and fumes.
- Reactions go to completion; they are **not reversible**.

## Aspirin

```
C₆H₄(OH)COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₄(OCOCH₃)COOH + CH₃COOH
```

2-hydroxybenzoic acid reacts with ethanoic anhydride to give aspirin (2-ethanoyloxybenzoic acid) and ethanoic acid.

### Anhydride compared with acyl chloride

| Factor | Ethanoic anhydride | Ethanoyl chloride |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Cheaper | More expensive |
| Reaction with water | Slower, easier to handle | Violent with moisture |
| By-product | Ethanoic acid, less hazardous | Corrosive HCl |
| Corrosion | Less | More |

## Required practical 10: preparing aspirin

1. Warm 2-hydroxybenzoic acid with ethanoic anhydride and a catalyst in a water bath.
2. Add cold water; filter off the crude solid with suction.
3. **Recrystallise**: dissolve in the minimum volume of hot solvent, filter hot, cool slowly, filter again, wash with ice-cold solvent, dry.
4. **Melting point**: sharp and equal to the data value means pure; lower and wider range means impure.

## Calculations

- Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ theoretical mass × 100.
- Acid chloride to amide is 1 : 1 even though 2 mol NH₃ are used.
- Atom economy = Mᵣ of desired product ÷ sum of Mᵣ of reactants × 100.

Example: 0.0250 mol of ethanoyl chloride gives at most 0.0250 mol × 59.0 = 1.475 g of ethanamide.

## Exam traps

- First arrow not starting at the lone pair of the nucleophile.
- Missing the O⁻ and charges in the intermediate.
- Giving water as a product of acyl chloride and alcohol.
- One mole of NH₃ or amine per mole of acyl chloride.
- Writing "amide" with no N-substituent for the amine reaction.
- Calling the anhydride "more reactive" as an advantage. It is less reactive, which is safer.
- Using a reversible arrow for these reactions.
- Missing HCl, or giving only "fumes" without naming them.

## Self-test

1. Draw the structure of ethanoic anhydride.
2. Why is the carbonyl carbon in ethanoyl chloride strongly δ+?
3. What are the two stages in the mechanism?
4. Write an equation for ethanoyl chloride with water.
5. Name the ester formed from ethanoyl chloride and methanol.
6. Why are two moles of ammonia needed per mole of ethanoyl chloride?
7. Name the product of ethanoyl chloride with ethylamine.
8. What is formed when ethanoic anhydride reacts with ethanol?
9. Give two industrial advantages of ethanoic anhydride over ethanoyl chloride.
10. Name the product made from 2-hydroxybenzoic acid and ethanoic anhydride.
11. How is the purity of the solid checked?
12. Calculate the percentage yield if 2.70 g of aspirin (Mᵣ 180.0) is obtained from 0.0200 mol of 2-hydroxybenzoic acid.

### Answers

1. (CH₃CO)₂O: two CH₃–C(=O)– groups joined by one oxygen.
2. It is bonded to two electronegative atoms, oxygen and chlorine, which withdraw electron density.
3. Nucleophilic addition, then elimination of chloride.
4. CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl.
5. Methyl ethanoate.
6. One acts as the nucleophile and one removes H⁺ to form NH₄⁺.
7. N-ethylethanamide.
8. Ethyl ethanoate and ethanoic acid.
9. Cheaper; less corrosive; less violent reaction with water; the by-product is not corrosive HCl.
10. Aspirin, 2-ethanoyloxybenzoic acid.
11. Measure the melting point and compare with the data value.
12. Theoretical = 0.0200 × 180.0 = 3.60 g; yield = 2.70 ÷ 3.60 × 100 = 75.0 %.
