> **Syllabus edition note.** These notes follow the Cambridge O Level Environmental Management 5014
> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, sub-topic 5.3.

Sub-topic 5.3 is two outcomes: describe ten strategies for conserving the biodiversity and genetic
resources of natural ecosystems (5.3.1), and discuss their benefits and limitations (5.3.2). Learn the ten
in the syllabus order (a) to (j), then learn one benefit and one limitation for each.

Full explanations: [managing biodiversity study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-managing-biodiversity-5014-2027/).
Test yourself: [managing biodiversity practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-5014-managing-biodiversity-5014-2027-practice/).

## The ten strategies: one line each

| | Strategy | How it conserves | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (a) | Sustainable harvesting | Take no more than the population replaces | Food and income continue | Needs data and enforcement |
| (b) | Sustainable forestry | Fell selectively, replant, keep habitat | Timber and habitat both kept | Lower short-term profit |
| (c) | National parks and reserves | Legal protection of whole areas | Whole ecosystems conserved | Costly; conflict with local people |
| (d) | Wildlife corridors | Habitat strips link isolated areas | Movement, mating, gene flow | Land cost; roads cut them |
| (e) | Seed banks | Dried, cold-stored seeds | Many varieties stored cheaply | Plants only; no habitat protection |
| (f) | Zoos and captive breeding | Breed endangered animals, maybe release | Saves species near extinction | Few animals; low genetic variety |
| (g) | Ecotourism | Visitor income pays for conservation | Wildlife worth more alive | Disturbance; income can fall |
| (h) | International cooperation against animal trade | Countries act together on illegal trade | Tackles cross-border problem | Needs all countries to enforce |
| (i) | Regulation of trade in vulnerable and endangered species | Bans or permits for trade | Cuts demand-driven harvesting | Smuggling and forged permits |
| (j) | Classifying threat level | Threatened, endangered, extinct | Sets priorities for action | Protects nothing on its own |

## Sorting the ten

- **In the wild (in situ):** (a), (b), (c), (d), (g). Protect species in their habitats, so evolution and
  ecological relationships continue.
- **Away from the wild (ex situ):** (e), (f). A back-up for genes and species; does not protect habitats.
- **Rules and priorities:** (h), (i), (j). Reduce pressure from people and focus effort.

**Memory hook:** "Protect it, store it, police it."

## What each type protects

| Type | Species | Genetic resources | Habitats |
|---|---|---|---|
| In the wild | Yes | Yes, and genes keep adapting | Yes |
| Away from the wild | Selected species only | Yes, but frozen in time | No |
| Rules and priorities | Indirectly | Indirectly | Indirectly |

## Threat levels (5.3.1 j)

- **Threatened**: at risk of extinction if the causes of decline continue.
- **Endangered**: at high risk of extinction in the wild.
- **Extinct**: no living individuals remain.

These describe **risk**, not just rarity. Classification is the starting point for strategies such as trade
regulation, which applies to vulnerable and endangered species.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biodiversity | The variety of living things in an area |
| Genetic resources | Genes in wild species that may be useful in future for food, farming or medicine |
| Sustainable harvesting | Taking wild species at a rate the population can replace |
| Quota | A limit on the amount that may be harvested |
| Sustainable forestry | Managing forests so timber is produced while the forest and habitat remain |
| National park / reserve | An area protected by law from damaging use |
| Wildlife corridor | A strip of habitat linking two or more separated habitats |
| Seed bank | A store of dried seeds kept at low temperature |
| Captive breeding | Breeding animals in zoos or centres, often for release |
| Ecotourism | Tourism to natural areas that supports conservation and local people |
| Poaching | Illegal hunting or capture of wild animals |
| CITES | The international agreement regulating trade in endangered wild animals and plants |

## Building a "discuss" answer

1. Name the strategy and **how** it conserves (1 point).
2. Give a **benefit**, linked to biodiversity or genetic resources (1 point).
3. Give a **limitation**, ideally a cost, an enforcement problem or what it fails to protect (1 point).
4. Repeat for the second strategy if two are named.
5. **Conclude**: which works better here, and why, or how they work best together.

**Useful linking judgements**
- Habitat protection fails if poaching or trade continues, so pair (c) with (h) or (i).
- Captive breeding needs a habitat to release animals into, so pair (f) with (c) or (d).
- Rules need local support, so pair (i) with (a) or (g) to give people income.

## Exam traps

- "Zoos protect habitats." They do not.
- "Seed banks save animals." Seeds are plants only.
- Using "extinct" for a species that survives only in zoos. That species still has living individuals.
- Ignoring local people. Many limitations are about lost land, lost income or conflict.
- Writing a benefit and a limitation that are the same point reversed ("cheap" and "not expensive").
- Percentage change in data questions: divide the change by the starting value.

## Self-test

1. List the five strategies that protect species in their natural habitats.
2. Explain how a wildlife corridor reduces genetic depletion.
3. Give one reason why sustainable harvesting can fail.
4. Why might local people oppose a new national park?
5. State two limitations of seed banks.
6. Explain why released captive-bred animals may not survive.
7. Distinguish between strategies (h) and (i).
8. What is the difference between an endangered and an extinct species?
9. Explain how ecotourism can harm the wildlife it depends on.
10. Why is classification of threat level useful even though it protects nothing directly?
11. *Invented data:* a fish stock falls from 8 000 tonnes to 6 800 tonnes after a quota is removed.
    Calculate the percentage fall.
12. Suggest which **two** strategies would best protect an animal threatened mainly by poaching for export.

### Answers

1. Sustainable harvesting, sustainable forestry, national parks and reserves, wildlife corridors,
   ecotourism.
2. It lets animals move between separated populations and breed, so genes mix and variety is kept.
3. Population data may be unreliable, so the limit is set too high; or the limit is not enforced.
4. They may lose land, grazing, hunting or farming income, or be moved away.
5. Any two: plants only; some seeds cannot survive drying and cold; habitat not protected; stored seeds do
   not adapt to changing conditions.
6. The habitat may still be damaged or the original threat may remain, and captive animals may lack the
   skills to find food or avoid predators.
7. (h) is countries cooperating against the animal trade; (i) is regulating trade in vulnerable and
   endangered species with bans or permits.
8. Endangered: at high risk of extinction in the wild. Extinct: no living individuals remain.
9. Visitors and vehicles disturb animals, and lodges, roads and waste damage habitats.
10. It shows which species are most at risk, so effort and money go where they are needed most.
11. 1 200 / 8 000 x 100 = 15%.
12. Regulation of trade in vulnerable and endangered species and international cooperation against animal
    trade (a national park with patrols is also creditable).
