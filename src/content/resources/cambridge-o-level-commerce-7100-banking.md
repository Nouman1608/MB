Banks keep money safe, make payments easy and lend money to individuals and businesses. Sub-topic 5.2 of Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100) has two parts: **banking services** (5.2.1) and **digital technology in banking** (5.2.2). Exam questions often ask you to pick the most **appropriate** service for a person or business, so learn what each service is for as well as what it is.

## 5.2.1 Banking services

The syllabus groups services into four headings: accounts, customer services, payments and sources of finance.

### Accounts

| Account | Features | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **Current account** | Used for everyday money. Money can be paid in and taken out at any time. A debit card, cheques and regular payments are usually available. Little or no interest | Receiving wages and paying bills |
| **Deposit / savings account** | Money is kept for a longer time and earns **interest**. Withdrawals may be limited or need notice | Saving for the future |

**Interest** is the payment a bank makes to savers, or charges to borrowers, for the use of money.

*Worked example.* A saver puts $2,000 in a savings account paying 3% interest a year. Interest after one year = 3% × 2,000 = $60. The balance becomes $2,060.

### Customer services

| Service | What it does |
|---|---|
| **ATM (automated teller machine)** | Lets customers withdraw cash, check balances and deposit money at any time using a card and PIN |
| **Night safe** | Lets a business deposit its day's takings in a secure container at the bank when the bank is closed |
| **Foreign currency** | Changes money into another currency for travel or international trade |
| **Financial advice** | Staff advise customers on saving, investment, loans and insurance |
| **Telephone banking** | Lets customers check balances and make payments by phone |

### Payments

| Method | How it works | Points to note |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash** | Notes and coins handed over | Immediate and simple; risk of theft; not suitable for large or distant payments |
| **Cheque** | A written order telling the bank to pay a sum from the writer's account | Slower; can be refused ("bounce") if the account has too little money |
| **Credit card** | Lets the holder borrow from the card company to pay, then repay later | Interest charged if not repaid in full on time; convenient for large purchases |
| **Debit card** | Takes money directly from the holder's current account at the time of purchase | No borrowing; only works if money is available |
| **Store card** | A card issued by one shop or chain for use only in that shop, often with credit | Can offer discounts but interest rates are often high |
| **Credit transfer** | Money moved directly from one bank account to another | Useful for paying wages, suppliers; traceable |
| **Standing order** | A fixed amount paid at regular times to the same person, ordered by the payer | Rent or savings; the same amount each time |
| **Direct debit** | The receiver collects varying amounts from the payer's account when due, with the payer's permission | Utility bills; amounts may change |
| **Electronic transfer** | Funds moved immediately through electronic systems | Fast; used for business and international payments |
| **Documentary credit (letter of credit)** | A bank promises to pay an exporter once the exporter shows the right documents | Gives the exporter security of payment in international trade |

**Standing order against direct debit.** With a standing order the **payer** tells the bank to pay a fixed amount on set dates. With a direct debit the **receiver** collects an amount that can vary. Rent is a typical standing order. An electricity bill that changes each month is a typical direct debit.

### Sources of finance from banks

| Source | What it is | Used for |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan** | A fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed period | Buying equipment or starting a business |
| **Overdraft** | Permission to spend more than is in the current account, up to a limit; interest is charged only on the amount overdrawn | Short-term gaps in cash flow |
| **Mortgage** | A long-term loan for buying property, with the property as security | Buying a home or business premises |
| **Hire purchase** | Pay a deposit and then regular instalments; goods are owned only when the final payment is made | Buying expensive goods such as vehicles or machines |

*Worked example: loan.* A business borrows $6,000 for 2 years at 8% simple interest a year.

1. Interest for one year = 8% × 6,000 = $480.
2. Interest for two years = 2 × 480 = $960.
3. Total to repay = 6,000 + 960 = $6,960.
4. Monthly repayment = 6,960 ÷ 24 = **$290**.

*Worked example: hire purchase.* A machine has a cash price of $650. On hire purchase the buyer pays a $200 deposit and 12 monthly payments of $45.

1. Instalments = 12 × 45 = $540.
2. Total paid = 200 + 540 = $740.
3. Extra cost of HP = 740 − 650 = **$90**.

The extra $90 is the price of spreading payment over a year.

### Choosing appropriate services

| Situation | Suitable service | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Shopkeeper wants to bank takings after closing | Night safe | Safe deposit when the bank is shut |
| Business needs to cover a temporary shortage of cash | Overdraft | Flexible, interest only on the amount used |
| Family buying a house | Mortgage | Long-term borrowing for property |
| Exporter worried about non-payment | Documentary credit | Bank guarantees payment on shipment |
| Pay the same rent every month | Standing order | Fixed amount on a set date |
| Pay a gas bill that changes | Direct debit | Amount can vary |
| Person wants to avoid debt | Debit card | Money comes from the account, not borrowed |

## 5.2.2 Digital technology in banking

**Digital banking** is the use of the internet and mobile technology to carry out banking.

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Internet banking** | Customers use a website on a computer to check balances, pay bills and transfer money |
| **Mobile banking** | Customers use a smartphone app, which can also scan cheques and use fingerprints to log in |

### Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Available 24 hours a day, anywhere with a connection | Needs internet access and a device; not everyone has them |
| Quick: payments and transfers in seconds | Risk of fraud, phishing and hacking |
| Saves travel and queuing | Customers who cannot use technology may be excluded |
| Lower costs for banks, so fees may be lower | Technical failure can stop access |
| Easy to see transaction history and set up regular payments | Less face-to-face advice |

### Impact of digital banking

- **Closure of bank branches.** Fewer customers visit, so banks close branches to save rent and staff costs. Some people, especially older customers and people in rural areas, lose easy access.
- **More digital-only banks.** Some banks operate entirely online or by app, with no branches, so costs are low.
- **Less use of cash (a cashless society).** More people pay by card or phone, which can be faster and more hygienic, but people without bank accounts or in places with poor networks may struggle.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** A small shop owner takes cash from customers during the day and closes after the bank. Explain **one** banking service that would help and why. [3]

> The owner could use a night safe [1]. This allows the day's takings to be deposited at the bank after it has closed [1], so the owner does not need to keep cash in the shop overnight, which reduces the risk of theft [1].

**Why it scores 3:** the service is named, defined in context and linked to a benefit.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up standing orders and direct debits.
- Saying a credit card takes money straight from the account (that is a debit card).
- Describing a mortgage as a short-term loan.
- Ignoring the scenario and just defining the service.
- Saying digital banking has no disadvantages.
- Saying an overdraft is a gift of money. It is a loan with interest.

## Exam technique

- **Name** the service, then **justify** it using details from the scenario.
- **Calculate** interest from the amount borrowed and add it to find the total repaid.
- **Evaluate** digital banking by giving advantages, disadvantages and a judgement.
