The specification says the accounting ratios will not be supplied in the examination, so learn every formula below. All figures are in dollars and every business is invented.

## Formulas to know by heart

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Sales revenue | selling price × sales volume |
| Total costs | fixed costs + total variable costs |
| Average cost | total costs ÷ output |
| Contribution per unit | selling price − variable cost per unit |
| Break-even output | total fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety | actual output − break-even output |
| Net cash flow | inflows − outflows |
| Closing balance | opening balance + net cash flow |
| Variance | actual − budget |
| Gross profit | revenue − cost of sales |
| Operating profit | gross profit − other operating expenses |
| Profit for the year (net profit) | operating profit − interest |
| Gross profit margin | gross profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Operating profit margin | operating profit ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Profit for the year margin | profit for the year ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities |
| Acid test ratio | (current assets − inventory) ÷ current liabilities |
| Working capital | current assets − current liabilities (standard form) |

The margin of safety as a percentage of actual output, and the working capital formula, are standard forms rather than formulas printed in the specification.

## 2.3.2 Financial planning

### Sales, revenue and costs

- **Fixed costs** do not vary with output; **variable costs** do.
- Average cost falls as output rises while fixed costs are spread wider.
- **Improving sales volume or revenue**: promotion, product improvement, wider distribution, price change. A price change moves volume and revenue per unit in opposite directions.

### Sales forecasting

| Point | Notes |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Base for budgets and cash-flow forecast; plans production, inventory and staffing; supports finance requests |
| Factors | Consumer trends; economic variables; actions of competitors |
| Difficulties | Uncertainty; no history for new products; fast-changing trends; competitor reactions; longer forecasts are weaker |

### Break-even in steps

1. Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit.
2. Break-even output = fixed costs ÷ contribution.
3. Margin of safety = actual output − break-even output.
4. Read the chart: revenue line crosses total cost line at break-even; the gap above is profit, below is loss.

**Reminder, Alderney Frames**: price 24, variable cost 15, fixed costs 18,000. Contribution 9; break-even 18,000 ÷ 9 = 2,000 frames. At 2,600 frames the margin of safety is 600 frames.

**Limitations**: assumes all output is sold; constant price and variable cost per unit; fixed costs stay fixed; best for one product; relies on forecasts; ignores cash timing and competitor reactions.

### Cash-flow forecasts

- Lay out opening balance, inflows, outflows, net flow and closing balance each month.
- Next month's opening balance is this month's closing balance.
- **Uses**: spot shortages early, support borrowing requests, time purchases.
- **Limitations**: forecast sales may be wrong; late payers and unplanned costs; needs regular updates; says nothing about profit.

### Budgets

| Point | Notes |
|---|---|
| Purposes | Targets; control; allocate resources; coordinate; motivate; compare with actual |
| Based on historical figures | Last period adjusted; quick; repeats old inefficiency |
| Zero based | Start at zero, justify every item; challenges waste; slower |
| Variance analysis | Actual − budget. Favourable raises profit; adverse lowers it |
| Difficulties | Forecast errors; time; padded bids; rigidity; demotivation |

For a cost, an actual above budget is adverse. For revenue, an actual above budget is favourable. Always write the label.

## 2.3.3 Managing finance

### Profit and profitability

The statement of comprehensive income (profit and loss account) runs: revenue, minus cost of sales, gives gross profit; minus other operating expenses, gives operating profit; minus interest, gives profit for the year (net profit).

**Reminder, Tansley Trims**: revenue 210,000; cost of sales 126,000; other operating expenses 52,500; interest 4,200.

```
Gross profit     = 84,000   margin 40%
Operating profit = 31,500   margin 15%
Profit for the year = 27,300   margin 13%
```

**Ways to increase profit**: raise revenue or cut costs. **Ways to improve margins**: raise prices; cut cost of sales through cheaper materials; cut other operating expenses; cut interest by repaying debt. More volume alone raises profit but not always margin.

### Profit versus cash

| | Profit | Cash |
|---|---|---|
| What it is | Revenue minus costs for a period | Money actually received and paid |
| Timing | When earned or incurred | When money moves |
| Credit sales | Counted at once | Arrive later |

A profitable business can run out of cash, for example by paying suppliers quickly, offering long credit and building inventory.

### Liquidity

The statement of financial position (balance sheet) shows current assets (inventory, trade receivables, cash) and current liabilities (trade payables, overdraft).

**Reminder, Mossdale Foods**: current assets 50,000 including inventory 32,000; current liabilities 40,000.

```
Current ratio   = 50,000 / 40,000 = 1.25
Acid test ratio = 18,000 / 40,000 = 0.45
```

Mossdale looks acceptable on the current ratio but very weak on the acid test, because most current assets are inventory.

| Way to improve liquidity | How it helps | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Sell assets | Cash now | Loses use of the asset |
| Longer supplier credit terms | Cash leaves later | Suppliers may refuse or raise prices |
| Factoring | Cash for trade receivables now | Fee; customers may deal with the factor |
| Just in time inventory | Less cash tied up | Depends on reliable suppliers |

**Working capital** funds day-to-day running. **The importance of cash**: wages, suppliers and bills fall due on fixed dates, whatever the profit.

### Business failure

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Poor management of cash flow | Market conditions |
| Overestimation of sales | Competition |
| Overtrading (growth outpacing cash) | Economic conditions |
| Poor inventory control | Exchange rates |
| Poor marketing | Interest rates |
| Poor quality | Government regulations |
| | Supplier problems |
| | Natural phenomena |

Internal causes are within management control; external causes are not, though a business can prepare for them.

## Quick self-test

1. Alderney Frames has fixed costs of 18,000 and contribution of 9 a frame. Find break-even output.
2. Sales are 4,000 units and break-even is 3,000 units. State the margin of safety in units and as a percentage of actual output.
3. Fixed costs 12,000; variable cost 4 a unit; output 1,500 units. Find average cost.
4. Find sales revenue for 640 units sold at 27.50.
5. A cost was budgeted at 22,000 and was 24,200. State the variance.
6. Opening balance 1,200; inflows 9,000; outflows 10,100. Find the closing balance.
7. Revenue 210,000; cost of sales 126,000; other operating expenses 52,500; interest 4,200. Find the operating profit margin.
8. Current assets 50,000 (inventory 32,000); current liabilities 40,000. Find the acid test ratio.
9. Current assets 55,000; current liabilities 38,000. Find working capital.
10. Define overtrading.
11. Name two external causes of business failure.
12. State one difference between zero-based budgets and budgets based on historical figures.

### Answers

1. 18,000 ÷ 9 = **2,000 units**.
2. 4,000 − 3,000 = **1,000 units**; 1,000 ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **25%** (standard form).
3. (12,000 + 4 × 1,500) ÷ 1,500 = 18,000 ÷ 1,500 = **12.00**.
4. 640 × 27.50 = **17,600**.
5. 24,200 − 22,000 = **2,200 adverse** (10% over budget).
6. 1,200 + 9,000 − 10,100 = **100**.
7. Gross profit 84,000; operating profit 31,500; 31,500 ÷ 210,000 × 100 = **15%**.
8. (50,000 − 32,000) ÷ 40,000 = **0.45**.
9. 55,000 − 38,000 = **17,000**.
10. Expanding output or sales faster than the business can fund the cash needed, so it runs short of cash.
11. Any two of: market conditions, competition, economic conditions, exchange rates, interest rates, government regulations, supplier problems, natural phenomena.
12. Zero-based budgets start from nothing and justify every item; historical budgets start from last period's figures and adjust them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing fixed costs by price instead of contribution.
- Quoting break-even in the wrong unit, or leaving out the unit.
- Carrying the wrong closing balance forward in a cash-flow forecast.
- Writing a variance with no favourable or adverse label.
- Leaving inventory in the acid test.
- Calling every fall in profit a cash problem.
- Giving causes of failure as a list without linking them to the case.
- Claiming factoring or selling assets is free of cost.
- Showing no working on a calculate question, since the command word asks for workings.
- Judging a ratio with no benchmark such as a past year or a rival.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you calculate the margin of safety?**
Margin of safety is actual (or budgeted) output minus break-even output. It shows how far sales can fall before the business starts to make a loss.

**What is the difference between the current ratio and the acid test ratio?**
The current ratio is current assets divided by current liabilities. The acid test ratio removes inventory from current assets first, so it shows whether short-term debts can be covered without selling stock.

**What is the difference between historical and zero-based budgeting?**
A historical budget starts from last period's actual figures, adjusted for expected changes, which is quick but carries over past inefficiency. A zero-based budget starts at zero, so every item of spending must be justified.

**What is overtrading?**
Overtrading means expanding output faster than the cash available to fund it. It is one of the internal causes of business failure in the Edexcel IAL Business (YBS11) specification.
