## Questions

**1.** A DNA molecule has both strands labelled with heavy nitrogen. It is placed in a medium with only light nitrogen and replicates 5 times.

**(a)** State how many molecules contain a heavy strand. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction of all molecules that contain a heavy strand. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why no molecule contains two heavy strands after round 1. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Describe the role of helicase at a replication fork. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how complementary base pairing helps make the copy accurate. **[2]**

**3.** A PCR run uses template DNA, two primers, free nucleotides and Taq polymerase.

**(a)** Describe what happens at the annealing and extension steps. **[2]**
**(b)** State why Taq polymerase is used. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the ideal number of copies of one target molecule after 28 cycles. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why the real number is lower. **[1]**

**4.** DNA fragments are run on a gel. The ladder bands are 800 bp at 15 mm, 400 bp at 27 mm and 200 bp at 39 mm from the well. A sample band is at 33 mm.

**(a)** Explain why the fragments separate by size. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate the size of the sample band. **[3]**

**5.** Band sizes (bp) for three markers in a paternity test:

| Marker | Mother | Child | Alleged father |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 120, 150 | 120, 175 | 175, 200 |
| 2 | 80, 95 | 95, 110 | 110, 110 |
| 3 | 210, 230 | 230, 260 | 140, 260 |

**(a)** Use the table to evaluate whether the alleged father could be the father. **[3]**
**(b)** The chances that an unrelated man matches at the markers are 1 in 6, 1 in 14 and 1 in 22. Calculate the combined chance. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the effect of adding a fourth marker. **[1]**

**6.** (HL only) **(a)** State which end of a growing strand DNA polymerase adds nucleotides to. **[1]**
**(b)** Distinguish between leading and lagging strand synthesis. **[3]**

**7.** (HL only) A circular chromosome of 1.8 × 10⁶ base pairs is copied from a single origin by two forks moving apart, each at 750 nucleotides per second (data given). Lagging strand fragments are 1200 nucleotides long.

**(a)** Calculate the time needed to copy the chromosome, in minutes. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of Okazaki fragments made at one fork. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the total number of RNA primers used. **[1]**

**8.** (HL only) **(a)** State the functions of primase and DNA ligase. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe proofreading by DNA polymerase III. **[2]**
**(c)** A polymerase makes 1 error per 10⁵ bases without proofreading and 1 per 10⁷ with it (data given). Calculate the expected errors in copying a 2.4 × 10⁶ bp genome in each case. **[2]**

**9.** An mRNA reads 5' AUG AAA GAU CUC UGG UAA 3'.

**(a)** Give the amino acid sequence it codes for (Met, Lys, Asp, Leu, Trp). **[1]**
**(b)** Classify each substitution and state its effect: AAA to AAG; GAU to GAA; UGG to UGA. **[3]**
**(c)** The first C of CUC is deleted. Give the new sequence (Ser, Gly also occur) and name the mutation type. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** State two causes of gene mutation. **[2]**
**(b)** A new-mutation rate is 1.2 × 10⁻⁸ per base pair per generation. A 1500 bp coding region is present in two copies in each of 50 000 children. Calculate the expected number of new mutations in this region. **[2]**
**(c)** State what randomness means for mutation. **[1]**

**11.** **(a)** Contrast the consequences of a mutation in a germ cell and a somatic cell. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why mutation is essential for evolution even though most mutations are harmful or neutral. **[3]**
**(c)** A commercial genetic test reports a SNP linked to raised disease risk. Suggest two problems of interpreting the result. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) Mice with gene M knocked out take a mean of 15.1 s to cross a balance beam; normal mice take 8.4 s.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in time. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what this indicates about gene M and one limitation. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline how CRISPR-Cas9 could produce a knockout. **[2]**
**(d)** Gene M is highly conserved. Give two hypotheses. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) **2** molecules [1].
(b) 2/32 = **1/16** [1].
(c) Each heavy strand templates a new light strand, so every molecule is a hybrid [1].
*Examiner insight:* give the reason in terms of templates; a bare number answers only the first parts.

**2.** (a) Helicase unwinds the double helix [1] by breaking hydrogen bonds between the strands [1].
(b) Each base pairs only with its complement (A with T, G with C) [1], so the template sequence determines the new strand [1].
*Examiner insight:* name both the bond and the pairing rule; "it unzips DNA" alone is vague.

**3.** (a) Annealing (about 55 to 60 °C): primers bind to complementary ends of the target [1]. Extension (about 72 °C): Taq polymerase adds nucleotides to the primers [1].
(b) It is heat stable so it is not denatured at the strand-separating step [1].
(c) 2²⁸ = **268,435,456** [1].
(d) Not every strand is fully copied in each cycle, or reagents run out [1].
*Examiner insight:* temperatures are approximate values, so tie each to what happens; link "heat stable" to the denaturing step.

**4.** (a) DNA is negatively charged (phosphate groups) so moves towards the positive electrode [1]; the gel is a mesh [1]; small fragments pass more easily so travel further [1].
(b) Size halves every 12 mm [1]. The sample is 6 mm past 400 bp, so 400 × 2^(−6/12) [1] = **about 280 bp** [1].
*Examiner insight:* state the method and give the answer as an estimate with units.

**5.** (a) Marker 1: child's 175 is not maternal and is present in the father [1]. Marker 2: child's 110 is not maternal and is present in the father [1]. Marker 3: child's 260 is present in the father, so he could be the father [1].
(b) 6 × 14 × 22 = 1848, so **1 in 1848** [1].
(c) A fourth marker lowers the chance of a false match further, so the result is more reliable [1].
*Examiner insight:* name the paternal band at each marker; "bands match" is too general.

**6.** (a) The **3' end** [1].
(b) Leading strand: made continuously [1], one primer needed [1]. Lagging strand: made discontinuously as Okazaki fragments, each with its own primer [1].
*Examiner insight:* use the terms continuous, discontinuous and Okazaki fragments, and compare both strands.

**7.** (a) Each fork copies 9 × 10⁵ bp [1]; 900 000 ÷ 750 = 1200 s [1]; = **20 min** [1].
(b) 900 000 ÷ 1200 [1] = **750 fragments** [1].
(c) About 750 per lagging strand, 1500, plus 2 for the leading strands = **about 1502** [1].
*Examiner insight:* show the per-fork length; using the whole chromosome gives a time that is double.

**8.** (a) Primase makes RNA primers [1]; ligase joins fragments by sealing the backbone [1].
(b) Polymerase III detects a mismatched nucleotide at the 3' end [1], removes it and adds the correct one [1].
(c) Without: 2.4 × 10⁶ ÷ 10⁵ = **24** [1]. With: 2.4 × 10⁶ ÷ 10⁷ = **0.24** [1].
*Examiner insight:* name the enzyme and the end of the strand it acts on.

**9.** (a) **Met-Lys-Asp-Leu-Trp-stop** [1].
(b) AAA to AAG: Lys still, silent [1]. GAU to GAA: Asp becomes Glu, missense [1]. UGG to UGA: stop codon, nonsense, polypeptide shortened [1].
(c) AUG AAA GAU UCU GGU AA: **Met-Lys-Asp-Ser-Gly**, with the stop codon no longer read [1]; a **frameshift** deletion [1].
*Examiner insight:* compare amino acids before naming a mutation; regroup into triplets after an indel.

**10.** (a) Errors in replication or repair that are not corrected [1]; mutagens, either chemical or radiation such as ultraviolet [1].
(b) 50 000 × 2 × 1500 × 1.2 × 10⁻⁸ [1] = **1.8** [1].
(c) A mutation can happen at any base, with no natural mechanism aiming it to change a trait [1].
*Examiner insight:* give specific mutagens, not "pollution".

**11.** (a) Germ cell: can be inherited [1], present in all cells of offspring [1]. Somatic: not passed on but can cause cancer [1].
(b) Mutation creates new alleles [1]; selection acts on variation [1]; a rare beneficial allele can spread over time [1].
(c) Risk is probabilistic not certain [1]; results may cause worry or be misread without expert advice [1].
*Examiner insight:* link "source of variation" to selection acting on it.

**12.** (a) (15.1 − 8.4) ÷ 8.4 × 100 [1] = **80%** (79.8) [1].
(b) Gene M helps balance or coordination [1]; limitation: other effects or small sample, so cause is uncertain [1].
(c) A guide RNA directs Cas9 to cut the gene [1]; repair errors disable it [1].
(d) Its product has a function that tolerates little change, so selection removes variants [1]; the region mutates more slowly [1].
*Examiner insight:* give a limitation tied to this data, not a generic one.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving fractions without the 2 ÷ 2ⁿ reasoning.
- Not naming the bond helicase breaks.
- Stating "heat" without the temperatures' purpose in PCR.
- Saying large fragments travel furthest on a gel.
- Using the whole genome length for a single fork.
- Calling a mutation silent without comparing amino acids.
- Missing the triplet regrouping after a deletion.
- Writing that mutations happen because an organism needs them.
