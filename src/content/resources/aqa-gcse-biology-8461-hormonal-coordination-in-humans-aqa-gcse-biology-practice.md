## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Name the gland that:

**(a)** secretes insulin **[1]**
**(b)** secretes FSH and LH **[1]**
**(c)** secretes testosterone **[1]**

**2.** Give **two** differences between the effects of the endocrine system and the nervous system. **[2]**

**3.** Explain what happens in the body when blood glucose concentration rises after a meal. **[3]**

**4.** Describe how a progesterone implant prevents pregnancy. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher tier only) Name the hormone released by the adrenal glands in times of fear or stress, and give
**two** of its effects. **[3]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A study recorded body mass index (BMI) group and the percentage of adults in each group who had Type 2
diabetes.

| BMI group | Healthy weight | Overweight | Obese |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adults with Type 2 diabetes (%) | 2 | 5 | 14 |

**(a)** How many times more common is Type 2 diabetes in the obese group than in the healthy weight group? **[1]**
**(b)** A newspaper says: "Obesity causes Type 2 diabetes." Explain why the data do not prove this. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how Type 2 diabetes is usually treated, and explain why this treatment differs from that for Type 1. **[3]**

**7.** Biology only.

**(a)** Explain why body cells do not function efficiently if the blood becomes too concentrated. **[2]**
**(b)** (Higher tier only) Describe how excess amino acids are made safe for excretion. **[3]**

**8.** Biology only. A patient with kidney failure has dialysis.

| Substance | Concentration in blood entering machine (arbitrary units) | Concentration in dialysis fluid (arbitrary units) |
|---|---|---|
| Glucose | 90 | 90 |
| Urea | 40 | 0 |
| Sodium ions | 150 | 140 |

**(a)** Explain why the dialysis fluid contains glucose at the same concentration as normal blood. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what happens to urea during dialysis. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage by which the sodium ion concentration in the dialysis fluid is lower than in the blood entering the machine. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Blood levels of four hormones were measured over one menstrual cycle.

| Day | 2 | 8 | 13 | 14 | 21 | 27 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| FSH (arbitrary units) | 8 | 6 | 14 | 10 | 3 | 7 |
| LH (arbitrary units) | 6 | 7 | 60 | 30 | 5 | 5 |
| Oestrogen (arbitrary units) | 2 | 8 | 15 | 10 | 9 | 3 |
| Progesterone (arbitrary units) | 1 | 1 | 1 | 3 | 18 | 2 |

**(a)** Use the data to suggest the day on which ovulation happens. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the low levels of FSH and LH on day 21. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why FSH rises again by day 27. **[2]**

**10.** A couple are deciding on a method of contraception. Compare condoms with the oral contraceptive pill. **[4]**

**11.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Explain why a woman having IVF is given FSH and LH. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why only one or two embryos are inserted into the uterus. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Explain how insulin and glucagon interact to keep blood glucose concentration within
normal limits during a day when a person eats breakfast and then misses lunch. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Pancreas [1]
**(b)** Pituitary gland [1]
**(c)** Testes [1]

**2.** Endocrine effects are slower [1]; endocrine effects act for longer [1]
*Examiner insight:* Allow "hormones are carried in the blood, whereas nerve impulses travel along neurones" as one of the two differences.

**3.** The pancreas detects the rise and releases insulin [1]; insulin causes glucose to move from the blood into cells [1]; in liver and muscle cells excess glucose is converted to glycogen for storage [1]

**4.** It releases progesterone slowly [1]; which inhibits the maturation and release of eggs (for months or years) [1]

**5.** Adrenaline [1]; any two effects: increases heart rate; boosts delivery of oxygen to the brain and muscles; boosts delivery of glucose to the brain and muscles [2]

**6. (a)** 14 ÷ 2 = **7 times** [1]
**(b)** The data show a correlation between obesity and Type 2 diabetes [1]; but other factors may be involved, so the data alone do not show that one causes the other [1]
**(c)** A carbohydrate-controlled diet and an exercise regime [1]; in Type 2 the pancreas still produces insulin but the body cells no longer respond to it [1]; so injecting insulin is not the main treatment, whereas in Type 1 the pancreas does not produce enough insulin, so injections replace it [1]

**7. (a)** Water moves out of the cells by osmosis [1]; cells that lose too much water do not function efficiently [1]
**(b)** Amino acids are deaminated in the liver [1]; forming ammonia [1]; which is toxic, so it is immediately converted to urea [1]

**8. (a)** So there is no concentration gradient for glucose [1]; so glucose does not diffuse out of the blood and is not lost [1]
**(b)** There is no urea in the dialysis fluid, so there is a concentration gradient [1]; urea diffuses from the blood, across the partially permeable membrane, into the fluid [1]
**(c)** (150 − 140) ÷ 150 × 100 = 6.67 [1]; **6.7%** [1]

**9. (a)** Day 13 to 14 [1]; because LH peaks at day 13, and LH stimulates release of the egg [1]
**(b)** Progesterone is high on day 21 [1]; progesterone inhibits FSH and LH [1]
**(c)** Progesterone has fallen (no pregnancy) [1]; so FSH is no longer inhibited and rises to start maturing a new egg [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers must use the hormone interactions, not just describe the numbers.

**10.** One mark for each point, up to 4:
- condoms are a barrier method that stops sperm reaching an egg, whereas the pill contains hormones that inhibit FSH so no eggs mature [1]
- condoms protect against sexually transmitted infections, whereas the pill does not [1]
- condoms are used at the time of intercourse, whereas the pill must be taken regularly [1]
- the pill may have side effects from the hormones, whereas condoms have no hormonal side effects [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs paired points; two separate descriptions earn fewer marks.

**11. (a)** To stimulate the maturation of several eggs [1]; so that several eggs can be collected and fertilised in the laboratory [1]
**(b)** To reduce the chance of multiple births, which are a risk to the babies and the mother [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- after breakfast, blood glucose rises and the pancreas releases insulin [1]
- insulin causes glucose to move from the blood into cells [1]
- in liver and muscle cells, excess glucose is converted to glycogen and stored [1]
- when lunch is missed, blood glucose falls and the pancreas releases glucagon [1]
- glucagon causes glycogen to be converted into glucose, which is released into the blood [1]
- this is negative feedback: each hormone reverses the change that caused its release, so the concentration stays within normal limits [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mixing up glucagon and glycogen anywhere in the answer limits the mark, because it reverses the meaning.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Glucagon written for glycogen, or the reverse.
- Treating correlation as proof of cause.
- Forgetting that dialysis fluid keeps useful substances in the blood.
- Describing hormone data without the inhibiting and stimulating effects.
- Separate descriptions instead of a comparison.
