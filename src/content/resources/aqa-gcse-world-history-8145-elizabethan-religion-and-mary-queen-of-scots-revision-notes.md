## The religious spectrum

| Group | Beliefs | Threat to Elizabeth |
|---|---|---|
| Catholics | Loyal to the pope; Mass; many saw Elizabeth as illegitimate | Rebellion, plots, foreign invasion, especially after 1570 |
| Church of England | 1559 Settlement; Elizabeth Supreme Governor | None: the official church |
| Puritans | Remove "popish" practices; preaching; some wanted Presbyterianism | Challenged her authority over the church |
| Separatists | Leave the Church of England altogether | Seen as a threat to order; harshly punished |

## Why Mary was a threat, and why Elizabeth kept her alive

**Threat:** strong claim through Margaret Tudor; Catholic; focus for rebellion and plots; possible French or Spanish support.
**Dilemma:** freeing her risked rebellion; sending her abroad risked foreign support; executing a queen set a dangerous precedent. So she was kept in custody for nineteen years.

## The plots in order

1. **Ridolfi (1571):** Spanish invasion; Mary to marry Norfolk; Norfolk executed 1572.
2. **Throckmorton (1583):** French-led invasion; Spanish ambassador expelled.
3. **Babington (1586):** kill Elizabeth; Walsingham's intercepted beer-barrel letters; Mary's approval proved.

## Government responses

- **Laws:** Treason Act 1571; 1581 Act (£20 a month fine; conversion to Catholicism treason); 1585 Act (priests to leave or face treason); 1593 Act against separatists.
- **Spying:** Walsingham's network of agents and double agents.
- **Executions:** more than a hundred priests; Campion (1581); plotters.
- **Against Puritans:** Grindal suspended (1577); Whitgift enforces conformity; crackdown after Marprelate.
- **Parliament:** pressed for Mary's execution from 1572; Bond of Association (1584).

## Impact of Mary's execution

- Main Catholic claimant removed; heir now Protestant James VI.
- Philip II freed to invade without helping a French-linked queen; Armada 1588.
- Most English Catholics stayed loyal during the Armada crisis.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recusant | Someone who refused to attend Church of England services |
| Excommunication | Expulsion from the Catholic Church by the pope |
| Papal bull | A formal order from the pope |
| Seminary priest | English Catholic priest trained abroad, for example at Douai |
| Jesuit | Member of the Society of Jesus, a Catholic missionary order |
| Priest hole | A hiding place for priests in Catholic houses |
| Puritan | Protestant wanting further reform of the Church of England |
| Prophesying | Puritan meeting for preaching and Bible study |
| Separatist | Protestant who wanted to leave the Church of England |
| Bond of Association | 1584 oath to kill anyone in whose name a plot was made |

## Exam traps

- Seminary priests (from 1574) are not the same as Jesuits (from 1580).
- Put the plots in order: Ridolfi, Throckmorton, Babington.
- Puritans were Protestants, not Catholics.
- The 1559 Settlement is background; the study begins c1568.
- In a question 1 interpretation answer, test the interpretation against precise knowledge, not general impressions.

## Self-test

1. What did the Act of Uniformity require?
2. Why did many Catholics see Mary as the rightful queen?
3. Where was Mary defeated before fleeing to England?
4. What happened in Durham Cathedral in 1569?
5. What did *Regnans in Excelsis* do?
6. Who founded the college at Douai?
7. Which Jesuit was executed in 1581?
8. Name the three main plots in order.
9. How did Walsingham trap Mary in 1586?
10. When was Mary executed?
11. What did Puritans want?
12. Why was Grindal suspended?

## Answers

1. Attendance at parish church using the Book of Common Prayer, with fines for absence.
2. They saw Elizabeth as illegitimate, and Mary descended from Henry VIII's sister Margaret.
3. At Langside, in 1568.
4. The Northern rebels celebrated Mass there.
5. Excommunicated Elizabeth and released Catholics from obedience to her.
6. William Allen.
7. Edmund Campion.
8. Ridolfi (1571), Throckmorton (1583), Babington (1586).
9. He intercepted letters smuggled in beer barrels and obtained Mary's approval of the plot.
10. 8 February 1587.
11. Further reform: removing "popish" practices, more preaching, and for some no bishops.
12. He refused to suppress prophesyings.
