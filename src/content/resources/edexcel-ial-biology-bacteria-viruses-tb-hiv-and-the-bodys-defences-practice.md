## Questions

**1.** Compare the structure of a bacterium with that of a virus. **[4]**

**2.** For each part, name the virus or viruses from this list: Ebola virus, TMV, HIV, lambda phage.
**(a)** The virus that infects bacteria. **[1]**
**(b)** The two viruses that have an envelope. **[1]**
**(c)** The virus with double-stranded DNA. **[1]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a lytic infection and latency, using lambda phage as your example. **[3]**

**4.** Explain how Mycobacterium tuberculosis causes damage to the lungs after it is inhaled. **[5]**

**5.** A patient infected with HIV has a CD4 count of 640 cells per mm³ at diagnosis and 280 cells per mm³ four years later.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in the count. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of fall per year. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why this patient is more likely to develop tuberculosis. **[3]**

**6.** An electron micrograph shows a bacterium 52 mm long at a magnification of ×20 000. Another micrograph shows a TMV particle 90 mm long at ×300 000.
**(a)** Calculate the real length of the bacterium in µm. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the real length of the TMV particle in nm. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times longer the bacterium is than the TMV particle. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** Name two routes by which pathogens can enter the body, with an example of each. **[2]**
**(b)** A patient takes a long course of broad-spectrum antibiotics and then develops a gut infection. Explain why. **[2]**

**8.** A splinter breaks the skin and the area becomes red and swollen. Explain these changes. **[4]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain how lysozyme in tears helps to protect the eye. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why interferon protects neighbouring cells but not the cell that releases it. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how a neutrophil removes a bacterium from the tissues. **[4]**

**11.** A single B cell is selected and divides by mitosis every 5 hours for 60 hours.
**(a)** Calculate the number of cells produced. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two features that allow a plasma cell to secrete large quantities of antibody. **[2]**

**12.** Describe the structure of an antibody and explain how antibodies, plasma cells and antigen-presenting cells together lead to the removal of a pathogen. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Bacterium is a cell with cytoplasm and ribosomes whereas a virus has no cytoplasm or ribosomes [1]. Bacterium has a cell wall (peptidoglycan) whereas a virus has a protein capsid [1]. Bacterial genetic material is DNA only; a virus has either DNA or RNA [1]. Bacterium has its own metabolism and divides by binary fission whereas a virus can replicate only inside a host cell [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison answer gains credit when both organisms appear in the same statement; two separate lists of features often show no clear difference.

**2.** **(a)** **Lambda phage** [1]
**(b)** **Ebola virus and HIV** [1]
**(c)** **Lambda phage** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Both" or "two" in the question means you must give all required names; naming only one of the enveloped viruses cannot earn the mark.

**3.** In a lytic infection the phage DNA is copied at once and new phages are assembled [1], then the bacterium bursts, releasing the phages [1]. In latency the phage DNA is integrated into the bacterial chromosome and copied at each division without making new phages [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Define both terms and tie each to the phage; stating only "lytic means lysis" is too thin for three marks.

**4.** Bacteria in droplets reach the alveoli, where macrophages engulf them [1]. The bacteria resist digestion and multiply inside the macrophages [1]. White blood cells wall off the infected cells, forming tubercles [1]. If immunity weakens, bacteria multiply and tubercles break down, forming cavities and destroying lung tissue [1]. The result is a persistent cough, coughing blood, weight loss and eventually lung failure or spread to other organs [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the process in order and show survival inside macrophages; "it damages the lungs" repeats the question and gains nothing.

**5.** **(a)** Fall = 640 − 280 = 360 [1]. 360 ÷ 640 × 100 = **56.3%** [1]
**(b)** 360 ÷ 4 = **90 cells per mm³ per year** [1]
**(c)** HIV destroys helper T cells [1], so the immune response to other pathogens is weak [1]; the tubercles cannot be kept intact, so latent M. tuberculosis is reactivated or a new infection is not controlled [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and division. Calculate the change against the starting count, not the final count, since the final count gives a different and misleading percentage.

**6.** **(a)** 52 ÷ 20 000 = 0.0026 mm [1], which is **2.6 µm** [1]
**(b)** 90 ÷ 300 000 = 0.0003 mm = **300 nm** [1]
**(c)** 2.6 µm = 2 600 nm; 2 600 ÷ 300 = **8.7 times** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Unit conversions are where most marks are lost; write the unit on every line and convert both lengths to the same unit before dividing.

**7.** **(a)** Any two of: inhalation (TB droplets) [1]; ingestion (contaminated water); skin breaks (needles); mucous membranes (HIV); across the placenta [1]. 
**(b)** The antibiotics kill gut flora as well as pathogens [1], so there is less competition and a pathogen can colonise the gut [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An example is needed with each route; naming the route alone does not show understanding.

**8.** Damaged cells and mast cells release histamine [1]. Arterioles dilate, so more blood flows to the area and it becomes red and hot [1]. Capillary walls become more permeable [1], so fluid leaks into the tissue and causes swelling, and phagocytes enter [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each sign to a mechanism. Writing "inflammation causes swelling" restates the question.

**9.** **(a)** Lysozyme hydrolyses peptidoglycan in bacterial cell walls [1], so the wall weakens and the bacterium bursts by osmosis [1].
**(b)** The infected cell releases interferon, which binds to receptors on neighbouring cells [1]; they make proteins that block viral replication, but the infected cell is already making virus [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Kills bacteria" is not enough for lysozyme; name the substrate and the consequence for the cell.

**10.** The neutrophil recognises and binds the bacterium [1]. It engulfs it in a phagosome [1]. A lysosome fuses with the phagosome [1] and enzymes digest the bacterium [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A description is a sequence. Put the steps in order and use the terms phagosome and lysosome.

**11.** **(a)** 60 ÷ 5 = 12 divisions [1]; 2¹² = **4 096 cells** [1]
**(b)** Extensive rough endoplasmic reticulum for protein synthesis [1]; many mitochondria for ATP, or Golgi apparatus to modify and package antibody [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For "explain two features", each feature needs its function; a bare list of organelles gains only partial credit.

**12.** Antibody has four polypeptide chains, two heavy and two light [1], joined by disulfide bonds [1]. The variable regions form two binding sites complementary to one antigen [1]. A macrophage engulfs the pathogen and presents its antigen, becoming an antigen-presenting cell [1]. A helper T cell with a matching receptor binds and is activated [1]. A B cell with matching surface antibody binds the antigen and is stimulated to divide [1]. The clone forms plasma cells that secrete antibody [1]. Antibodies bind the pathogen causing agglutination or neutralisation, so phagocytes can engulf it [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For an extended answer, order the steps and name each cell; a vague "the immune system makes antibodies" cannot access the later marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a virus a cell, or giving it ribosomes or its own metabolism.
- Failing to compare in a single statement when the command word is compare.
- Describing latency as "dormant" without saying the genetic material is integrated.
- Forgetting that M. tuberculosis survives inside macrophages.
- Calculating percentage change from the wrong value or mixing mm, µm and nm.
- Writing "interferon kills viruses" instead of the effect on neighbouring cells.
- Saying antibodies "kill" pathogens rather than naming agglutination, neutralisation or opsonisation.
- Confusing a plasma cell with the macrophage that presents antigen.
