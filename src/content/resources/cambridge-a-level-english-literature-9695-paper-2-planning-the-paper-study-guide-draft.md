# Planning Paper 2 as a whole

**Paper 2 (Prose and Unseen) is a 2-hour written paper worth 50 marks, in two sections of 25 marks each.** Section A: Prose asks for one essay on a prose set text, with a choice between (a) an essay question and (b) a question based on a passage from the set text printed on the paper. Section B: Unseen asks you to answer one question from a choice of two, each on a previously unseen text printed on the paper. Paper 2 is 50 per cent of the AS Level and 25 per cent of the A Level. Set texts and dictionaries are not allowed.

Related pages: [Section A: Prose](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-9695-section-a-prose/) and [Section B: Unseen](/resources/cambridge-a-level-english-literature-9695-section-b-unseen/).

## What the unseen section says

The syllabus states that the unseen passages cover **two of the categories prose, poetry and drama**, and that all passages are from works **originally written in English**. The authors and dates are not given, and knowledge of the literary or historical background is not expected. The questions test your ability to read literature critically and to show, by informed discussion and opinion, how meaning is expressed through a writer's choices of language, form and structure. The candidate must also show knowledge and understanding of the **text type**, which helps to inform a response.

## Assessment objectives

All Paper 2 questions assess AO1 to AO4, each worth 25 per cent. In Section B, AO1 is shown through knowledge of the text type rather than of a set text and its context.

## A reading routine for the unseen text (Marlbridge guidance)

1. **Read once for sense:** who is speaking, to whom, where and when (as far as the passage says).
2. **Identify the text type:** prose, poem or drama, and what that implies (for example, line breaks, stage directions, narrative voice).
3. **Read again with a pencil:** mark three or four choices (a repeated word, a shift in tone, a sentence length change, a structural turn).
4. **State a one-sentence argument** about how the writer creates meaning.
5. **Write on the choices in order of the passage, or in order of importance**, with a short quotation for each.

## Choosing in Section A

Use the same test as in Paper 1: can you name three pieces of evidence without the book? If yes, (a) is workable. If you prefer close reading, (b) starts from a printed passage but you should still link it to the rest of the text.

## Time and balance

The sections are equal in marks, so give them similar time. The syllabus does not set a time for each; practise full essays and note your own timings.

## Common traps

- Writing a long prose essay and leaving too little time for the unseen.
- On the unseen, retelling or guessing at the author, date or context. The syllabus says no background is expected.
- Forgetting to name the text type.
- Listing techniques with no effect.

## What a teacher must confirm

- That the unseen routine suits the class.
- That nothing here is read as set-text content: no set text is named.
