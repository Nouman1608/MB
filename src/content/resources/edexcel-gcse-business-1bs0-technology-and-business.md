This guide covers content item 1.5.2, Technology and business, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. It belongs to Theme 1, examined in Paper 1. The broader [guide to stakeholders and technology](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-stakeholders-and-technology/) defines the four technologies and works through the costs of an online store. This page takes each technology in turn, separates short-run from long-run effects, and gives more practice at weighing the costs of technology against its benefits. All businesses and figures are invented.

## What 1.5.2 contains

| Heading in the specification | Points listed |
|---|---|
| Different types of technology used by business | E-commerce; social media; digital communication; payment systems |
| How technology influences business activity in terms of | Sales; costs; marketing mix |

Technology is listed in Topic 1.5 because it is an **external** influence: a small business does not invent these technologies, but it must respond as customers and rivals adopt them. Responses to technological change are covered in 1.5.5.

## The four technologies, one at a time

### E-commerce

E-commerce is buying and selling goods and services online, through the business's own website, an app or an online marketplace.

- **Sales:** customers anywhere can buy at any time, so the market grows beyond the local area and beyond opening hours.
- **Costs:** a website, hosting, packaging, postage and handling returns; possibly lower rent if a large shop is no longer needed. Selling through a marketplace usually means paying a fee on each sale.
- **Marketing mix:** mainly place (a new channel) and price (customers compare quickly).

### Social media

Social media are online platforms where users share content and businesses can post, reply to customers and pay for adverts.

- **Sales:** posts and adverts raise awareness; customers who share posts spread the word for free.
- **Costs:** an account is usually free, but someone's time is needed to create content and reply, and paid adverts cost money.
- **Marketing mix:** mainly promotion, but comments also feed back into product decisions.
- **Risk:** a complaint can be seen by thousands of people quickly, so poor service becomes public.

### Digital communication

Digital communication is the electronic exchange of information, such as email, messaging apps, video calls and shared online documents, both inside the business and with customers and suppliers.

- **Sales:** quick replies to enquiries and email newsletters can turn interest into orders.
- **Costs:** cuts printing, postage and travel; meetings by video call save time.
- **Marketing mix:** promotion (emails to customers) and place (customers order by message).
- Internally, staff in different places can work together, and orders reach suppliers faster.

### Payment systems

Payment systems are the methods used to pay and be paid electronically: card readers, contactless payments, online checkouts and mobile phone payments.

- **Sales:** customers who carry no cash can still buy; quick contactless payment shortens queues; online checkouts make e-commerce possible.
- **Costs:** card readers may be bought or rented, and a fee is usually charged on each transaction. Against this, less cash means less time counting it, fewer trips to the bank and lower risk of theft.
- **Marketing mix:** mainly place, because paying is part of how the customer obtains the product.

## Short run and long run

A common weakness is saying technology "saves money" without qualification. It usually costs money first and saves money later, if at all.

| | Short run | Longer run |
|---|---|---|
| Costs | Buying equipment, building a website, training staff | Lower running costs, for example less printing or rent |
| Sales | May dip while staff learn new systems | Wider market and more convenient buying can raise sales |
| Risk | New systems may fail or be hacked | Rivals adopt the same technology, so the advantage shrinks |

## Worked example: should Fern & Fig go cashless?

Fern & Fig is a café that currently accepts cash and card. The owner is considering card-only payment. Weekly sales revenue is £6,000. At present 70% is paid by card, at a fee of 1.5% of the card sales.

1. Card sales now = 70% × £6,000 = £4,200
2. Fee now = 1.5% × £4,200 = £63 a week

If the café goes card-only and all £6,000 is paid by card:

3. Fee = 1.5% × £6,000 = £90 a week, an extra £27

Savings: the owner spends 3 hours a week counting and banking cash, which she values at £12 an hour.

4. Time saved = 3 × £12 = £36 a week
5. Net saving = £36 − £27 = £9 a week

Card-only saves only £9 a week on these figures. But if some customers who prefer cash go elsewhere, the café could lose sales worth far more than £9. If just 1% of revenue was lost (£60 a week), going cashless would leave the café worse off. The decision depends on how many customers still want to pay in cash, and that is the kind of judgement an Evaluate answer needs.

## Technology and the marketing mix: linking the elements

Technology rarely changes one element alone.

- An online store (place) puts the business's prices next to rivals' (price), so it may need to promote what makes it different (promotion).
- Customer reviews on social media (promotion) reveal what customers dislike, leading to product changes (product).
- Fast online payment (place) supports impulse buying, which can make promotional offers more effective (promotion).

When you answer, name the element, then follow the chain to a second element.

## Annotated model answer

**Question (Analyse style):** Analyse the impact on Tidewater Surf School of using social media to promote its lessons. [6]

> Tidewater could post videos of lessons, which are free to share *(technology identified)*, so it reaches people planning trips to the coast without paying for printed adverts *(cost effect)*. If customers share the videos, more people hear about the school, which could increase bookings *(sales effect)*. However, someone must create posts and reply to messages, which takes time from teaching *(hidden cost)*. A bad review after a cancelled lesson would also be visible to many potential customers *(risk)*, so poor service could reduce bookings rather than raise them.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the four technologies as one thing called "the internet".
- Claiming technology always cuts costs.
- Forgetting the fees charged by payment systems and online marketplaces.
- Ignoring that rivals can adopt the same technology.
- Writing about the marketing mix without naming an element.

## Exam technique

- **Give / State:** name a technology or an effect.
- **Explain:** one effect on sales, costs or the mix, plus two expansions.
- **Analyse:** use the business in the source and follow effects through.
- **Justify / Evaluate:** compare the costs and benefits of a technology using the figures given, and state what the decision depends on.
