## Short-answer questions

Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** Which document is sent by a seller to a buyer to reduce the amount the buyer owes?

A: Debit note

B: Credit note

C: Invoice

D: Receipt

**[1]**

**2.** Which document is the source for entering bank charges in the cash book?

A: Bank statement

B: Cheque counterfoil

C: Paying-in slip

D: Statement of account

**[1]**

**3.** Name the document described in each case.

**(a)** The part of a cheque kept by the business that wrote it. **[1]**

**(b)** A form completed when cash and cheques are deposited at the bank. **[1]**

**(c)** A summary sent by a supplier listing invoices, credit notes and payments for the month. **[1]**

**(d)** Proof given to a customer that a payment has been received. **[1]**

**4.** State the book of prime entry in which each business records the document.

**(a)** The seller records an invoice for goods sold on credit. **[1]**

**(b)** The buyer records a credit note received. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **two** uses of a debit note. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why trade discount shown on an invoice is not recorded in the ledger accounts. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** On 4 May Ashby Furniture sold 30 chairs to Carlow Cafe on credit. The list price was $85 per chair and Ashby allows a trade discount of 15%.

**(a)** Calculate the total of the invoice. **[2]**

**(b)** On 8 May Carlow returned 4 damaged chairs. Calculate the value of the credit note Ashby will issue. **[2]**

**(c)** At 1 May Carlow already owed Ashby $1,240, which it paid in full on 20 May. Prepare the statement of account Ashby will send Carlow for May, showing the balance at 31 May. **[4]**

**8.** For each document, state who issues it and give **one** way it is used as a source of information.

**(a)** Cheque counterfoil. **[2]**

**(b)** Paying-in slip. **[2]**

**(c)** Bank statement. **[2]**

**9.** Ferris Hardware received a statement of account from its supplier showing a balance owing of $2,310. Ferris's own account for the supplier in its purchases ledger shows $2,050.

**(a)** Explain why Ferris compares the statement with its own ledger account. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** reasons for the difference of $260. **[2]**

**10.** A trader buys goods with a list price of $1,600. The supplier gives a trade discount of 25% and offers a cash discount of 2% if the invoice is paid within 10 days.

**(a)** Calculate the amount shown as due on the invoice. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount the trader would pay if the invoice is paid within 10 days. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the cash discount is not deducted on the invoice itself. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how a receipt and a cheque counterfoil each provide evidence for the business that pays a supplier by cheque. **[4]**

**12.** Mehwish runs a small catering business and writes all her invoices and receipts by hand. Discuss whether she should produce her business documents digitally. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. A credit note reduces the amount owed. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. Bank charges first appear on the bank statement. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Cheque counterfoil [1].

**(b)** Paying-in slip [1].

**(c)** Statement of account [1].

**(d)** Receipt [1]. **[4]**

**4. (a)** Sales journal [1].

**(b)** Purchases returns journal [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A buyer sends a debit note to the seller to list goods being returned and request a credit note [1]. A seller may send a debit note to a buyer to charge an extra amount, for example to correct an undercharge on an invoice [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Trade discount is a reduction in the list price given before the sale is agreed, so the amount actually charged is the net figure [1]. Only that net amount is owed and recorded; the list price was never part of the transaction [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 30 × 85 = 2,550; less trade discount 15% = 382.50 [1]; invoice total **$2,167.50** [1].

**(b)** 4 × 85 × 85% [1] = **$289.00** [1].

**(c)** **Statement of account: Ashby Furniture to Carlow Cafe, May**

| Date | Details | Debit $ | Credit $ | Balance $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| May 1 | Balance brought forward | | | 1,240.00 |
| May 4 | Invoice | 2,167.50 | | 3,407.50 |
| May 8 | Credit note | | 289.00 | 3,118.50 |
| May 20 | Payment received | | 1,240.00 | 1,878.50 |
| May 31 | Amount due | | | 1,878.50 |

Marks: opening balance [1]; invoice and credit note on the correct sides [1]; payment [1]; closing balance **$1,878.50** [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Issued by the payer from its own cheque book [1]; the source for the payments side of the cash book (bank column), showing date, payee and amount [1].

**(b)** Completed by the business paying money into the bank [1]; the stamped counterfoil is the source for the receipts side of the cash book (bank column) [1].

**(c)** Issued by the bank [1]; used to enter items not yet in the cash book, such as bank charges or direct debits, and to check the cash book [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** To check that both businesses agree on the amount owed [1], so that missing documents or errors can be found before payment is made [1].

**(b)** Any two: an invoice of $260 not yet received or recorded by Ferris [1]; a payment or credit note recorded by Ferris but not yet by the supplier [1]; an error in either set of records. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 1,600 × 75% = **$1,200** [1].

**(b)** Cash discount 1,200 × 2% = 24 [1]; amount paid **$1,176** [1].

**(c)** The discount is earned only if payment is made within 10 days, and the trader may not pay in time [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Receipt: issued by the supplier [1], it proves the supplier received the payment, giving amount, date and method, so it can settle any dispute [1]. Cheque counterfoil: completed by the business when writing the cheque [1], it records the date, payee and amount, so the payment can be entered in the cash book and traced later [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Benefits: invoices could be produced faster and totals calculated automatically, reducing arithmetic errors [1]; copies would be stored automatically and easy to search [1]; invoices and receipts could be emailed to customers, saving paper and postage [1].

Limitations: Mehwish would need equipment and a program, with a cost and time to learn [1]; digital records need protecting from loss and unauthorised access [1].

Conclusion: if she issues many invoices, digital documents would save time and reduce errors, so she should change, keeping back-up copies of her records [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the buyer issues the credit note.
- Valuing returned goods at list price.
- Recording trade discount, or deducting cash discount on the invoice.
- Treating the statement of account as a source for a new entry.
- Giving only who issues a document when asked how it is used.
