## Section A

**1.** Identify the document described in each case: (a) sent by a buyer to ask about prices and delivery times; (b) sent with the goods and signed by the buyer on arrival; (c) shows the balance owed at the end of a month; (d) proves that payment has been made. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** items of information found on a quotation. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a seller might issue a credit note. **[2]**

**4.** Distinguish between a trade discount and a cash discount. **[2]**

**5.** State the purpose of a certificate of origin. **[2]**

**6.** State **one** similarity and **one** difference between a bill of lading and an air waybill. **[2]**

**7.** Identify **three** examples of digital technology used in trading documents. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Classic Stationers orders 120 notebooks at $2.50 each. The supplier gives a 15% trade discount and a 2% cash discount for payment within 10 days.
(a) Calculate the amount shown as due on the invoice.
(b) Calculate how much Classic Stationers pays if it pays within 10 days. **[6]**

**9.** Classic Stationers returns 8 damaged pens that were invoiced at $6.00 each, less 25% trade discount. Calculate the value of the credit note. **[2]**

**10.** At 1 May Classic Stationers owed its supplier $350. During May it received an invoice for $255 and a credit note for $30, and paid $350. Calculate the balance shown on the statement of account at 31 May, showing your working. **[3]**

**11.** Describe the stages of the online ordering process, from the buyer browsing the website to receiving the order. **[6]**

**12.** An exporter of machine parts is selling to a new buyer overseas for the first time. Explain how a documentary credit (letter of credit) reduces the risk for both the exporter and the importer. **[4]**

**13.** A travel company is replacing paper tickets and receipts with etickets and electronic receipts stored in the cloud. Assess the benefits and drawbacks of this change for the company. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Enquiry [1]; (b) delivery note [1]; (c) statement of account [1]; (d) receipt [1].

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: price of the goods; trade discount; cash discount terms; delivery date or method; payment terms; how long the offer is valid [1] [1].

**3.** To reduce the amount the buyer owes [1], for example because goods were returned, arrived damaged, or the buyer was overcharged on the invoice [1].

**4.** A trade discount is a reduction given to traders buying goods for resale, deducted on the invoice [1]; a cash discount is a reduction for paying within a stated time, taken only if payment is prompt [1].

**5.** It states the country in which the goods were made [1], so that customs can charge the correct duties or apply trade agreement terms [1].

**6.** Similarity: both are receipts for goods and evidence of the contract of carriage [1]. Difference: a bill of lading is used for sea freight and is a document of title, while an air waybill is used for air freight and is not a document of title [1].

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: digital signatures; secure access (passwords, encryption); cloud-based storage; electronic receipts; etickets [1] [1] [1].

**8.** (a) 120 x $2.50 = $300 [1]. Trade discount = 15% x $300 = $45 [1]. Amount due = $300 − $45 = **$255** [1]. (b) Cash discount = 2% x $255 [1] = $5.10 [1]. Amount paid = $255 − $5.10 = **$249.90** [1].

**9.** 8 x $6.00 = $48, less 25% ($12) [1] = **$36** [1].

**10.** $350 + $255 [1] − $30 − $350 [1] = **$225** [1].

**11.** Any six stages in a logical order, 1 mark each: the buyer browses and compares products; selects items and adds them to a basket; goes to checkout and signs in or continues as a guest; enters delivery details and chooses a delivery option; pays securely online; receives an order confirmation or electronic receipt; is told the goods are dispatched and given tracking; receives the order (with returns available if needed) [1] [1] [1] [1] [1] [1].

**12.** **Exporter:** the importer's bank promises to pay [1], so the exporter does not have to rely on a new, unknown buyer's promise and is protected against non-payment [1]. **Importer:** the bank pays only when the exporter presents the agreed documents, such as the bill of lading [1], so the importer has evidence the goods have been shipped as agreed before money is released [1].

**13.** **Benefits:** tickets reach customers instantly by email or app [1]; printing and postage costs are removed [1]; cloud storage lets staff in any office find a booking or receipt quickly [1]; electronic tickets cannot be lost in the post, reducing replacement requests [1]. **Drawbacks:** risk of hacking and theft of customer data [1]; the company depends on internet access and its cloud provider, so an outage can stop check-in [1]; customers without smartphones may find etickets difficult [1]. **Judgement:** the change is likely to benefit the company overall, provided it invests in secure access and offers a printable option for customers who need it [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: confusing the statement of account with the invoice.
- Question 4: saying a cash discount is "for paying in cash"; it is for paying promptly.
- Question 8: working out the cash discount on $300 instead of $255.
- Question 10: adding the credit note instead of subtracting it.
- Question 11: listing stages out of order or stopping at payment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the cash discount depend on paying in cash?** No. It is earned by paying within the stated period by any method, such as a bank transfer.

**How many stages are needed in Question 11?** Six clear stages in a logical order earn full marks here; combining two stages in one sentence still counts if both are shown.
