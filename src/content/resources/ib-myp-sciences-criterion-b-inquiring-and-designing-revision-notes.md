## What the guide states

- Criterion B is about designing, analysing and performing scientific investigations. The MYP emphasises experimental work and scientific inquiry.
- When you design an investigation you develop a method that collects **sufficient data** to answer the question.
- Teachers must give an **open-ended problem**: one with several independent variables suitable for investigation and enough scope to identify both independent and controlled variables.
- For the top level of the method strand, a method is **logical, complete and safe**, includes only relevant information and is correctly sequenced.
- Maximum 8, in bands 1-2, 3-4, 5-6 and 7-8.

## The four Year 5 strands

| Strand | Top band (7-8) | Plain words |
|---|---|---|
| i | explain a problem or question to be tested | say what you will test and why it is testable |
| ii | formulate and explain a testable hypothesis using correct scientific reasoning | prediction plus a scientific reason |
| iii | explain how to manipulate the variables and how sufficient, relevant data will be collected | change one, measure one, keep the rest fixed, repeat |
| iv | design a logical, complete and safe method selecting appropriate materials and equipment | step-by-step plan anyone could follow |

## Key terms

- **Hypothesis**: a tentative explanation for an observation or phenomenon that requires experimental confirmation. It can be a question or a statement.
- **Prediction**: an expected result of an upcoming action or event.
- **Independent variable**: the variable that is selected and manipulated by the investigator.
- **Dependent variable**: the variable whose values are measured in the experiment.
- **Controlled variables**: factors kept constant so the test is fair.
- **Quantitative data**: numerical measurements. **Qualitative data**: non-numerical information.
- **Sufficient data**: enough values and repeats to answer the question.
- **Relevant data**: data that bears on the question.

## Hypothesis template

"If [independent variable changes in a stated way], then [dependent variable changes in a stated way], because [scientific reason]."

Check it three ways.

1. Does it name both variables?
2. Does it say the direction of change?
3. Does the "because" use a scientific idea, such as particles, energy, forces or cell processes?

Example: if the angle of a ramp is increased, then a trolley will reach a greater speed at the bottom, because more of its gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy.

## Variables in a table

| Type | Question to ask | Example (dissolving a tablet) |
|---|---|---|
| Independent | What will I change? | water temperature (10, 20, 30, 40, 50 °C) |
| Dependent | What will I measure? | time to dissolve (s) |
| Controlled | What must stay the same? | volume of water, tablet type, beaker, no stirring, same timer |

For every controlled variable, say **how** you keep it the same. "Keep the volume the same" is incomplete. "Measure 100 cm³ in a measuring cylinder each time" is complete.

## Method checklist

Tick each item.

- A researchable question that names both variables.
- A hypothesis with a reason.
- Equipment list with sizes, ranges and units (for example 100 cm³ measuring cylinder, thermometer 0-100 °C).
- Numbered steps in a logical order.
- At least five values of the independent variable, where possible, spread over a sensible range.
- At least three repeats at each value.
- A results table planned with headings and units.
- A safety step linked to this investigation (for example eye protection for fizzing).
- No irrelevant detail.

## Why repeats and range matter

- **Range**: five or more values show the pattern. Two values show only that something is different.
- **Repeats**: with three results you can calculate a mean and notice a result that does not fit.
- **Equal steps**: even spacing (10, 20, 30, 40, 50) makes the graph easier to read.

## Open-ended tasks

1. List all factors that could change the result.
2. Choose one factor to change.
3. State why the others must be controlled.
4. Check that you can measure the dependent variable with the equipment available.

## Command terms for Criterion B

| Term | What to do |
|---|---|
| State | give the question or hypothesis in a short sentence |
| Outline | give a brief account of the variables or data plan |
| Describe | give a detailed account of how you will change a variable and collect data |
| Explain | give reasons: why the method will give valid data |
| Formulate | express the hypothesis precisely and systematically |
| Design | produce a plan |
| Select | choose equipment from a list |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Prediction vs hypothesis**: a hypothesis adds a scientific reason.
- **Independent vs dependent**: you change the first and measure the second.
- **Controlled vs dependent**: controlled factors stay the same. The dependent variable is allowed to change.
- **Qualitative vs quantitative**: prefer numbers where possible, because they can be processed and graphed.
- **Relevant vs sufficient**: relevant is about type. Sufficient is about amount.

## Where marks are usually lost

- A question or hypothesis that does not name the variables.
- A hypothesis with "because" followed by something that is not science.
- Two independent variables changed together.
- No method for measuring or controlling a variable.
- Only one trial, or only two values.
- No units or equipment sizes.
- Missing or generic safety.
- Steps in the wrong order, or missing steps that a classmate would need.

## Quick self-test

1. State the independent and dependent variables in: "How does the length of a pendulum affect the time for ten swings?"
2. Write a hypothesis for question 1 with a reason.
3. Name two controlled variables for question 1 and say how to control each.
4. Why should you do three trials at each length?
5. A method says "measure the temperature of the water". Say what is missing.

### Answers

1. Independent: length of the pendulum. Dependent: time for ten swings.
2. If the length of the pendulum is increased, then the time for ten swings will increase, because a longer pendulum has a longer path to travel on each swing and takes longer to complete it.
3. The mass of the bob (use the same bob for every length) and the starting angle (release from the same angle, measured with a protractor, for example 20°). Also acceptable: the same stopwatch and person timing.
4. Repeats allow a mean to be calculated and make it possible to spot a result that does not fit the pattern, so the data is more reliable.
5. The equipment (for example a thermometer with a range and its precision), when to measure (for example just before adding the tablet) and the unit.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a hypothesis be?**
Usually one to three sentences: what you expect, in which direction, and the scientific reason.

**Do I need a risk table?**
The descriptors ask for a safe method. A short list of hazards, each with a precaution that fits your investigation, is enough unless your teacher asks for a table.

**Can I use a method from a textbook?**
At the top band the guide expects you to design the method yourself and to select the materials and equipment, so a copied method is unlikely to show this.
