These notes condense subtopic **4.5 Symmetry** from Topic 4 Geometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Statement 1 (line and rotational symmetry in two dimensions) is the same for Core and Extended. Statement 2 (symmetry of prisms, cylinders, pyramids and cones) is **Extended only**. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [symmetry study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-symmetry/); to test yourself, use the [symmetry practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-symmetry-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Line of symmetry | A line that folds the shape onto itself: each point has a mirror image the same distance away on the other side |
| Order of rotational symmetry | The number of positions in one full turn (360°) in which the shape looks exactly the same |
| Order 1 | The shape only fits onto itself after a full turn; it has **no** rotational symmetry |
| Angle of turn | The smallest turn that maps the shape onto itself; angle of turn = 360° ÷ order |
| Plane of symmetry | A flat plane that cuts a solid into two halves that are mirror images (Extended) |
| Axis of symmetry | A line through a solid about which it can be turned to fit onto itself; its order counts the fits in one full turn (Extended) |

## Triangles and quadrilaterals

| Shape | Lines of symmetry | Order of rotational symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| Equilateral triangle | 3 | 3 |
| Isosceles triangle | 1 | 1 |
| Scalene triangle | 0 | 1 |
| Square | 4 | 4 |
| Rectangle | 2 | 2 |
| Rhombus | 2 (the diagonals) | 2 |
| Parallelogram | 0 | 2 |
| Kite | 1 (one diagonal) | 1 |
| Isosceles trapezium | 1 | 1 |
| Trapezium (general) | 0 | 1 |

A rectangle's lines of symmetry join midpoints of opposite sides; a rhombus's are its diagonals. A parallelogram has order 2 but **no** lines of symmetry: folding along a diagonal does not split the end angles equally.

## Properties that come from symmetry

- **Isosceles triangle.** The line of symmetry through the apex bisects the apex angle, meets the base at 90° and cuts the base in half. So the two base angles are equal.
- **Kite.** The diagonal that is a line of symmetry cuts the other diagonal in half at 90°. The pair of angles not on that diagonal are equal.
- **Rhombus.** Each diagonal is a line of symmetry, so the diagonals cross at 90°, cut each other in half and bisect the corner angles.
- **Rectangle.** Rotational symmetry of order 2 about the centre makes the diagonals bisect each other; the lines of symmetry make the diagonals equal in length.
- **Parallelogram.** Order 2 about the point where the diagonals cross, so the diagonals bisect each other and opposite angles are equal.
- **Isosceles trapezium.** The line of symmetry joins the midpoints of the parallel sides, so the base angles at each parallel side are equal and the diagonals are equal.

## Regular polygons

A regular polygon with **n** sides has **n lines of symmetry** and rotational symmetry of **order n**.

```
angle of turn          = 360° ÷ n   (the same size as one exterior angle)
n odd   -> every line joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side
n even  -> n/2 lines join opposite vertices,
           n/2 lines join midpoints of opposite sides
```

A circle has infinitely many lines of symmetry and fits onto itself after any turn about its centre.

## Symmetry of solids (Extended only)

Count planes and axes for the **right** solids the syllabus names.

| Solid | Planes of symmetry | Axes of rotational symmetry |
|---|---|---|
| Cuboid, all edges different (a × b × c) | 3 | 3 axes, each of order 2 |
| Cuboid with a square end (a × a × b) | 5 | 1 axis of order 4 and 4 axes of order 2 |
| Cube | 9 | 13 axes: 3 of order 4, 4 of order 3, 6 of order 2 |
| Prism with a regular n-sided cross-section | n + 1 | 1 axis of order n along the prism, n axes of order 2 across it |
| Cylinder | infinitely many through the axis, plus 1 across the middle | the central axis (any angle of turn works) |
| Right pyramid, regular n-sided base | n (all through the apex) | 1 axis of order n through the apex |
| Right pyramid, rectangular base (not square) | 2 | 1 axis of order 2 |
| Right circular cone | infinitely many, all through the apex | the central axis only |

Quick method for a prism: take the lines of symmetry of the cross-section (each becomes a plane along the prism) and add **one** plane cutting the prism across its middle. For a pyramid or cone there is **no** plane across the middle, because the apex is only at one end.

## Exam traps

- Writing "order 0". A shape with no rotational symmetry has order **1**.
- Counting the diagonals of a rectangle as lines of symmetry. They are not, unless the rectangle is a square.
- Giving a parallelogram lines of symmetry because it "looks balanced". It has none.
- For a regular polygon, giving the interior angle as the angle of turn. The angle of turn is the exterior angle, 360° ÷ n.
- For solids, forgetting the plane across the middle of a prism or cylinder, or adding one to a pyramid or cone.

## Self-test

1. Write down the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry of a kite.
2. Which quadrilateral has rotational symmetry of order 2 and no lines of symmetry?
3. A shape fits onto itself after a turn of 30° and no smaller turn. Write down its order of rotational symmetry.
4. A regular polygon has an exterior angle of 24°. How many lines of symmetry does it have?
5. Name a triangle with exactly one line of symmetry and state what that line does to the base.
6. The diagonals of a quadrilateral are both lines of symmetry, but the quadrilateral is not a square. Name it.
7. In a regular nonagon, does each line of symmetry pass through two vertices? Explain.
8. (Extended) How many planes of symmetry does a cuboid measuring 3 cm by 3 cm by 8 cm have?
9. (Extended) How many planes of symmetry does a right pyramid with a regular hexagonal base have?
10. (Extended) A prism has a cross-section that is an isosceles triangle (not equilateral). How many planes of symmetry does it have?
11. (Extended) State the order of rotational symmetry of a cube about an axis through two opposite vertices.
12. (Extended) Why does a right circular cone have no plane of symmetry perpendicular to its axis?

### Answers

1. **1** line of symmetry; order **1**.
2. **Parallelogram**.
3. 360 ÷ 30 = **12**.
4. 360 ÷ 24 = 15 sides, so **15** lines of symmetry.
5. **Isosceles** triangle; the line meets the base at 90° and cuts it in half.
6. **Rhombus**.
7. **No.** Nine is odd, so each line joins a vertex to the midpoint of the opposite side.
8. The square ends give 4 planes along the length, plus 1 across the middle: **5**.
9. A regular hexagon has 6 lines of symmetry, so **6** planes, all through the apex.
10. The triangle has 1 line of symmetry, giving 1 plane along the prism, plus 1 across the middle: **2**.
11. Order **3**.
12. A plane across the middle would have to swap the apex with the circular base, and those are different shapes, so the two halves are not mirror images.
