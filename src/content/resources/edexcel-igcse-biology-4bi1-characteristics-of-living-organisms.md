This study guide teaches sub-topic 1(a), **Characteristics of living organisms** (specification point 1.1), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Point 1.1 is not bold-referenced, so it can be examined on **either paper**. It applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3.

Point 1.1 is short, but it is the base for the whole course. Every later topic, from respiration to inheritance, is an expansion of one of the eight characteristics. ## What point 1.1 asks you to do

The specification says you must **understand how living organisms share** eight characteristics. The key word is *understand*. A list from memory is the start, but exam questions ask you to apply each characteristic to a named organism or to decide whether something is alive.

| Characteristic | Short meaning |
|---|---|
| Nutrition | Taking in substances that supply energy and the materials for growth and repair |
| Respiration | Chemical reactions in cells that release energy from nutrients |
| Excretion | Removing the waste products of metabolism |
| Response | Detecting a change in surroundings and reacting to it |
| Movement | A change of position or of place, using energy |
| Control of internal conditions | Keeping the inside of the organism steady |
| Reproduction | Producing new individuals of the same kind |
| Growth and development | Getting bigger and becoming more complex |

A useful memory aid is the word **MRS GREN** (movement, respiration, sensitivity, growth, reproduction, excretion, nutrition). It misses "control of internal conditions", which the Edexcel list includes, so add it yourself.

## The eight characteristics in depth

### 1. Nutrition

**Nutrition** is taking in or making the substances an organism needs. Those substances are used for two jobs: as a source of **energy** and as **raw materials** to build new cells and repair old ones.

- **Animals** take in ready-made organic food (carbohydrates, proteins, lipids) by eating.
- **Green plants** make their own food from carbon dioxide and water by **photosynthesis**, using light energy absorbed by chlorophyll. They also absorb mineral ions from the soil.
- **Fungi** such as *Mucor* digest food outside their bodies and absorb the products.

Two different words are often confused. Plants do not "eat soil". Soil supplies water and ions, not the energy-rich organic food, which the plant makes itself.

### 2. Respiration

**Respiration** is the chemical process in living cells that releases energy from nutrient molecules such as glucose. The energy is used for movement, growth, active transport, keeping warm and many other jobs. Respiration is **not** the same as breathing. Breathing is the movement of air in and out of the lungs. Respiration happens in every living cell, including the cells of plants, all day and all night.

### 3. Excretion

**Excretion** is the removal from the body of the **waste products of metabolism**. Metabolism means all the chemical reactions in cells. Examples are carbon dioxide from respiration, urea from the breakdown of excess amino acids, and oxygen from photosynthesis in plants. Faeces are mostly undigested food that never entered the body's cells, so passing faeces is **egestion**, not excretion.

### 4. Response to surroundings

Organisms detect changes in their environment, called **stimuli**, and respond. A stimulus might be light, temperature, touch, gravity, chemicals or sound. Animals usually respond quickly using nerves and muscles: a hand pulls away from a hot surface. Plants respond more slowly, often by growing: a shoot grows towards light and a root grows towards water and downwards with gravity. Both count as response.

### 5. Movement

**Movement** is a change in position or place. Many animals move their whole bodies from place to place, using muscles. Plants do not move from place to place, but parts of them move. A sunflower head turns, leaves fold, and shoots bend. These movements are usually slow and are caused by growth or by changes in cells. The word "movement" in an exam answer is not the same as "locomotion", so a plant should still be credited with movement of parts.

### 6. Control of internal conditions

Organisms keep conditions inside the body within narrow limits, even when the outside world changes. Humans keep the temperature of the blood close to 37 °C and control the water content and glucose concentration of the blood. Plants control water loss by opening and closing stomata. Keeping conditions steady lets enzymes work at a good rate. This idea is called **homeostasis** in later topics.

### 7. Reproduction

**Reproduction** produces new individuals of the same kind. It may be sexual or asexual. An individual organism can live a full life without reproducing, but a **species** can only survive if its members reproduce. That is why reproduction is a characteristic of living things in general, not of every single individual.

### 8. Growth and development

**Growth** is a permanent increase in size or mass, normally by cell division and cell enlargement. **Development** is the change in form and complexity over time, for example a tadpole becoming a frog or a seed becoming a flowering plant. A balloon gets bigger when you blow it up, but this is not growth, because the increase is not permanent and does not come from the organism making new material.

## Worked examples

### Worked example 1: applying the list to a named organism

*Explain how a cat shows the eight characteristics of living organisms.*

1. **Nutrition:** it eats prey or cat food, which supplies energy and materials.
2. **Respiration:** its cells release energy from glucose using oxygen.
3. **Excretion:** it breathes out carbon dioxide and passes urea in urine.
4. **Response:** it turns its head towards a sudden noise.
5. **Movement:** it walks, runs and jumps using muscles.
6. **Control of internal conditions:** it keeps its body temperature steady, for example by panting when hot.
7. **Reproduction:** it produces kittens.
8. **Growth and development:** a kitten grows into an adult cat.

Notice the method. Each characteristic is named, then matched to one specific piece of evidence. A mark scheme will want the evidence, not just the label.

### Worked example 2: a plant

*A bean plant has no legs and does not eat. Is it alive? Support your answer.*

The bean plant is alive, and each characteristic can be shown for it.

1. It makes its own food by photosynthesis and absorbs ions. (nutrition)
2. Its root and shoot cells respire. (respiration)
3. Oxygen from photosynthesis and carbon dioxide from respiration leave through the stomata. (excretion)
4. Its shoot grows towards a window. (response)
5. The leaves open and close, and the shoot bends. (movement of parts)
6. Guard cells control water loss. (control of internal conditions)
7. It makes flowers, seeds and pods. (reproduction)
8. It grows from a seed into a mature plant. (growth and development)

The lack of legs and of "eating" does not matter. The characteristics are met in a different way.

### Worked example 3: is it alive?

*A car engine uses fuel, releases energy, produces waste gases and moves. A student says the car is alive. Evaluate the claim.*

The car appears to have three characteristics. It uses a fuel (like nutrition), burns it (a bit like respiration), and releases waste gases (like excretion). It also moves.

But the car does not:

- grow or develop by itself
- reproduce
- respond to its surroundings by itself, because it responds only when a driver controls it
- control its internal conditions using its own cells.

Also, burning fuel in an engine is combustion, a chemical reaction, and not respiration in living cells. So the claim fails. **To be alive, an object must show all eight characteristics, not just a few.**

### Worked example 4: calculating growth

*A seedling is 12 mm tall on day 1 and 18 mm tall on day 6. Calculate its percentage increase in height.*

```
increase = 18 − 12 = 6 mm
percentage increase = increase ÷ original × 100
                    = 6 ÷ 12 × 100
                    = 50%
```

The step to remember is that the **original** value is the denominator. The mean growth rate over the five days is 6 ÷ 5 = 1.2 mm per day.

## Borderline cases

Exam questions like to test the edges of the list.

| Case | What to say |
|---|---|
| A mule cannot reproduce | The individual is still alive. It shows the other seven characteristics, and the horse and donkey species reproduce |
| A dormant seed | It respires very slowly and shows little else until it germinates. It is alive because it can grow later |
| A virus | Not a living organism in this specification. It reproduces only inside a host cell, and has no cell structure |
| A crystal that grows | Grows by adding layers from outside, but does not respire, excrete or reproduce |
| A flame | Uses fuel and releases energy, but fails nearly every other test |

The last two rows show a common trick. A single characteristic is not enough evidence that something is alive.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "respiration" when you mean breathing, or the reverse.
- Saying plants do not move or do not respond. They do, more slowly.
- Calling faeces an excretory product. That is egestion.
- Saying that a living thing must reproduce as an individual. The requirement is about the species.
- Describing "nutrition" as simply "eating". Plants make their own food, so use "taking in or making".
- Forgetting the eighth item on the Edexcel list, control of internal conditions.
- Giving only the name of a characteristic without evidence in a "how does X show..." question.

## Self-check

1. Name the eight characteristics of living organisms in the Edexcel list.
2. Explain why respiration is not the same as breathing.
3. Give one example of a plant responding to its surroundings.
4. Explain why the growth of a crystal is not the same as the growth of an organism.
5. A student states "a plant does not excrete". Correct the statement.

**Answers:** (1) nutrition, respiration, excretion, response, movement, control of internal conditions, reproduction, growth and development. (2) Respiration is a chemical process in cells that releases energy. Breathing is moving air in and out of the lungs. (3) A shoot grows towards light, or a root grows towards water. (4) A crystal adds layers from outside; an organism makes new cells from the nutrients it takes in. (5) A plant excretes carbon dioxide from respiration and oxygen from photosynthesis through its stomata.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many characteristics of living organisms must I know?**
Eight: nutrition, respiration, excretion, response, movement, control of internal conditions, reproduction, and growth and development.

**Is a virus alive?**
In the Edexcel 4BI1 specification, viruses are not living organisms. They can reproduce only inside living cells and have no cellular structure.

**Do plants really move?**
Parts of plants move, mostly by growth, for example a shoot bending towards light. Plants do not usually move from place to place.
