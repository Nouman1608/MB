Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻², mass of the Earth = 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg and radius of the Earth = 6.37 × 10⁶ m where needed.

## Section A

**1.** A small sphere of mass 0.20 kg is placed with its centre 0.15 m from the centre of a uniform sphere of mass 5.0 kg. Calculate the gravitational force between them. **[2]**

**2.** Two identical uniform spheres, each of mass 8.0 kg and radius 0.10 m, are placed so that their surfaces touch. Calculate the gravitational force between them. **[2]**

**3.** The gravitational force on a satellite at the Earth's surface is 4800 N. Calculate the gravitational force on it in an orbit at a height of 2R above the surface, where R is the Earth's radius. **[2]**

**4.** The mass of the Sun is 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg and the mean distance from the Sun to the Earth is 1.50 × 10¹¹ m. Calculate the gravitational force between the Sun and the Earth. **[2]**

**5.** A student says that any satellite with an orbital period of 24 hours is geostationary. Explain why this is not necessarily true. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why every circular orbit around the Earth must be centred on the centre of the Earth. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A satellite moves in a circular orbit of radius 1.2 × 10⁷ m around the Earth.

**(a)** Show that the orbital speed v is given by v = √(GM/r), where M is the mass of the Earth. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the orbital speed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the orbital period in hours. **[2]**

**(d)** A second satellite of twice the mass is placed in the same orbit. State and explain its period. **[1]**

**8.** A geostationary satellite orbits the Earth.

**(a)** Calculate the radius of its orbit. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate its height above the Earth's surface. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why it must orbit from west to east. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why geostationary orbits are used for satellites that relay television signals. **[1]**

**9.** A moon orbits a distant planet in a circular orbit of radius 5.0 × 10⁸ m with a period of 12.0 days.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the planet. **[3]**

**(b)** A second moon orbits the same planet at a radius of 1.25 × 10⁸ m. Calculate its period in days without calculating the planet's mass again. **[2]**

**10.** Two satellites, A and B, orbit the same planet in circular orbits. The orbital radius of B is four times that of A. Determine the ratios:

**(a)** speed of A ÷ speed of B

**(b)** period of A ÷ period of B

**(c)** centripetal acceleration of A ÷ centripetal acceleration of B. **[3]**

**11.** An astronaut of mass 70 kg is in a space station orbiting 4.0 × 10⁵ m above the Earth's surface.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational force on the astronaut. **[2]**

**(b)** Express this as a percentage of the astronaut's weight at the Earth's surface, taking g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹ there. **[1]**

**12.** The law of gravitation is written for point masses, but it is routinely applied to planets.

**(a)** State the condition that allows a planet to be treated as a point mass. **[1]**

**(b)** State where the point mass is located, and hence how the separation r must be measured. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** F = Gm₁m₂/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.0 × 0.20 ÷ 0.15² [1] = 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ N [1].

**2.** Centre-to-centre separation r = 0.20 m [1]; F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 8.0 × 8.0 ÷ 0.20² = 1.1 × 10⁻⁷ N [1].

**3.** Orbital radius = R + 2R = 3R, so the force is divided by 3² = 9 [1]; F = 4800 ÷ 9 = 530 N [1].

**4.** F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.99 × 10³⁰ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ ÷ (1.50 × 10¹¹)² [1] = 3.5 × 10²² N [1].

**5.** A geostationary orbit must also be directly above the Equator and travel from west to east [1]; a 24-hour orbit that is tilted, or that moves east to west, does not stay above the same point on the surface [1].

**6.** The only force on the satellite is gravity, which always acts towards the centre of the Earth [1]; the resultant force in circular motion points to the centre of the circle, so the centre of the circle must be the centre of the Earth [1].

**7. (a)** The gravitational force provides the centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r [1]; cancelling m and one r gives v² = GM/r, so v = √(GM/r) [1].

**(b)** v = √(6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ ÷ 1.2 × 10⁷) [1] = 5.8 × 10³ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** T = 2πr/v = 2π × 1.2 × 10⁷ ÷ 5760 = 1.31 × 10⁴ s [1] = 3.6 hours [1].

**(d)** The same period, 3.6 hours, because the satellite's mass cancels from the equation [1].

**8. (a)** T = 24 × 3600 = 86 400 s [1]; r³ = GMT²/4π² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 86 400² ÷ 4π² = 7.53 × 10²² m³ [1]; r = 4.22 × 10⁷ m [1].

**(b)** h = 4.22 × 10⁷ − 6.37 × 10⁶ = 3.59 × 10⁷ m [1].

**(c)** The Earth rotates from west to east, so the satellite must move in the same direction to stay above the same point [1].

**(d)** The satellite stays above one point, so receiving dishes can be fixed in one direction without tracking [1].

**9. (a)** T = 12.0 × 86 400 = 1.04 × 10⁶ s [1]; M = 4π²r³/(GT²) = 4π² × (5.0 × 10⁸)³ ÷ (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (1.04 × 10⁶)²) [1] = 6.9 × 10²⁵ kg [1].

**(b)** T² ∝ r³, so T ∝ r^(3/2); the radius is a quarter, so the period is (1/4)^(3/2) = 1/8 of the first [1]: 12.0 ÷ 8 = 1.5 days [1].

**10. (a)** v ∝ 1/√r, so v_A/v_B = √4 = 2 [1].

**(b)** T ∝ r^(3/2), so T_A/T_B = 1/8 [1].

**(c)** a = GM/r², so a_A/a_B = 4² = 16 [1].

**11. (a)** r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 4.0 × 10⁵ = 6.77 × 10⁶ m [1]; F = GMm/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ × 70 ÷ (6.77 × 10⁶)² = 610 N [1].

**(b)** 608 ÷ (70 × 9.81) × 100 = 89% [1].

**12. (a)** The point considered must be outside the planet, and the planet must be a uniform sphere [1].

**(b)** At the centre of the planet [1]; r is measured from the centre of the planet (centre to centre for two spheres), not from its surface [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the height above the surface, or the surface-to-surface gap, as r.
- Writing "at height 2R" and then using r = 2R instead of 3R.
- Missing the equating step "gravitational force provides the centripetal force" in derivations.
- Leaving periods in days or hours inside T² = 4π²r³/(GM).
- Listing only the 24-hour condition for a geostationary orbit.
- Rounding the orbital radius too early, then subtracting the Earth's radius.
