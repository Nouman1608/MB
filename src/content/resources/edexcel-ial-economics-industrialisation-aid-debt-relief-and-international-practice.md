## Questions

**1.** In Country A, 90 farm workers produce 2,700 units a year, and 30 of them are surplus labour with zero marginal product. The modern sector pays a wage 20% above the farm subsistence wage.

**(a)** Calculate the subsistence wage and the modern wage. **[2]**
**(b)** Thirty workers move to modern firms, where each adds 60 units of output. Calculate modern-sector profit. **[2]**
**(c)** Firms reinvest 60% of profit and each new job needs capital of 108. Calculate the new jobs created. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate farm output and average product after the 30 workers leave. **[2]**

**2.** Explain two reasons why the Lewis model may overstate the speed of industrialisation. **[4]**

**3.** Tourists in Country G number 400,000 and each spends 500 in the year. Of this spending, 45% leaks out. Residents spend 50% of any extra income on local goods.

**(a)** Calculate total tourist spending. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the multiplier and the total local income created. **[3]**
**(c)** Arrivals fall by 25%. Calculate the fall in total local income created. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one reason why tourism may raise development less than these figures suggest. **[2]**

**4.** In Country H, a tonne of raw crop sells for 400 and uses 90 of inputs. The same crop, processed locally, sells for 1,100 using the 400 raw crop and 250 of other inputs.

**(a)** Calculate value added at each stage. **[2]**
**(b)** The crop is 70% of export earnings and its price falls 15%. Calculate the fall in total export earnings. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one barrier to moving into processing. **[2]**

**5.** Country J has debt service of 320 and export earnings of 1,600.

**(a)** Calculate the debt service ratio. Lenders then waive 25% of payments. Calculate the new ratio and the money freed. **[4]**
**(b)** A loan of 1,000 carries 6% interest on the outstanding balance and is repaid in equal yearly instalments of principal. Compare repaying over 10 years with 20 years, in first-year payment and total interest. **[3]**

**6.** Assess whether debt relief will raise development in a highly indebted country. **[6]**

**7.** Country K receives aid of 600m, has GNI of 12,000m and a population of 30 million. Of the aid, 60% is tied and tied goods cost 20% above world prices.

**(a)** Calculate aid as a percentage of GNI and aid per person. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the real value of the aid. **[2]**
**(c)** A donor with GNI of 400,000 gives 1,200. Calculate aid as a percentage of its GNI. **[1]**
**(d)** Distinguish between bilateral and multilateral aid. **[2]**

**8.** Explain two reasons why aid may fail to promote development and one reason why it may succeed. **[6]**

**9.**
**(a)** Distinguish between the roles of the World Bank and the IMF. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain two ways in which an NGO may achieve results that government aid does not. **[4]**

**10.** Country L has 55% of workers in low-productivity farming, a debt service ratio of 28%, aid worth 8% of GNI and one mineral that gives 70% of export earnings. It also has a long coastline. Evaluate which strategy the government should prioritise. **[10]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Subsistence wage = 2,700 / 90 = 30 [1]. Modern wage = 30 x 1.2 = **36** [1].
**(b)** Output = 30 x 60 = 1,800 and wage bill = 30 x 36 = 1,080 [1]. Profit = **720** [1].
**(c)** Reinvested = 720 x 0.6 = 432 [1]. New jobs = 432 / 108 = **4** [1].
**(d)** Output stays at **2,700**, since marginal product was zero [1]. Average product = 2,700 / 60 = **45** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the output and wage bill separately before subtracting, so a reader can see how you reached profit even if one input was misread.

**2.** Any two, each with a reason and a link to speed:
Profits may be spent on imports or sent abroad, not reinvested at home, so capital grows slowly [1] and few jobs appear [1].
Firms may choose capital-intensive methods, so each unit of investment creates few jobs [1], and surplus labour moves slowly [1].
Other acceptable reasons: marginal product is not truly zero, so farm output falls and the modern wage must rise sooner; weak infrastructure or skills limit modern output per worker.
*Examiner insight:* Naming a limitation earns little unless you say why it slows the transfer of labour.

**3. (a)** 400,000 x 500 = **200m** [1].
**(b)** Local share = 200 x 0.55 = 110m [1]. Multiplier = 1 / (1 - 0.5) = 2 [1]. Local income = 110 x 2 = **220m** [1].
**(c)** Arrivals 300,000 so spending is 150m [1]. Income falls by 55, from 220 to 165 [1].
**(d)** Leakage means much of the spending leaves through imports or foreign profit [1], so local income and tax revenue rise less than visitor spending [1]. Also valid: seasonal, insecure work; environmental damage.
*Examiner insight:* Label the first-round local amount before applying the multiplier. Applying the multiplier to total spending overstates income.

**4. (a)** Raw stage: 400 - 90 = **310** [1]. Processing stage: 1,100 - 400 - 250 = **450** [1].
**(b)** 0.70 x 15 = **10.5%** [2].
**(c)** For example, processing needs reliable power and finance [1], which are scarce, so firms cannot expand [1]. A different barrier, such as foreign tariffs on processed goods, also works if it ends in lower export demand or investment.
*Examiner insight:* A barrier needs a consequence. Naming a missing resource alone is a description.

**5. (a)** DSR = 320 / 1,600 x 100 = **20%** [1]. After waiver, payments = 320 x 0.75 = 240 [1]. New ratio = 240 / 1,600 = **15%** [1]. Freed = **80** [1].
**(b)** Principal 100 a year over 10 years: first payment = 100 + 60 = **160**. Principal 50 over 20 years: first payment = 50 + 60 = **110** [1]. Total interest over 10 years = 0.06 x 1,000 x 5.5 = **330**. Over 20 years = 0.06 x 1,000 x 10.5 = **630** [1]. A longer period lowers each year's burden but costs 300 more interest [1].
*Examiner insight:* Compare both measures, yearly burden and total cost. Giving only one makes the comparison one-sided.

**6.** Relief lowers debt service, so the government has more funds [1]. If spent on schools, clinics or roads, productivity and growth rise [1]. A drawback is moral hazard: expecting relief may encourage reckless borrowing [1], and lenders may cut future lending [1]. Judgement: the outcome depends on whether freed funds go into human capital or infrastructure and whether governance is sound [1], so relief helps most alongside reforms [1].
*Examiner insight:* Assessment is shown by saying when relief works or fails and why, not by listing pros and cons.

**7. (a)** 600 / 12,000 x 100 = **5%** [1]. 600m / 30m = **20** [1].
**(b)** Tied = 360, untied = 240. Real tied value = 360 / 1.2 = 300 [1]. Total real value = 240 + 300 = **540** [1].
**(c)** 1,200 / 400,000 x 100 = **0.3%** [1].
**(d)** Bilateral aid passes straight from a single donor government to the recipient [1]. Multilateral aid comes from a pool of donor funds distributed by an institution [1].
*Examiner insight:* Define both terms in contrast to each other, not by one example each.

**8.** Reason aid may fail: corruption diverts funds [1], so projects are not completed [1]. Another: tied aid raises costs [1], so its real value is lower [1]. Reason it may succeed: aid can fund roads, schools and health care that private markets underprovide [1], raising productivity [1].
*Examiner insight:* Link each reason to a result for development. Repeating the word "corruption" is not enough.

**9. (a)** The World Bank gives long-term loans and advice for development and poverty reduction [1], such as infrastructure and education [1]. The IMF monitors economies and gives temporary loans for balance of payments problems [1], usually with policy conditions [1].
**(b)** Local knowledge lets NGOs target communities government misses [1], so aid reaches the poorest [1]. NGOs can act quickly in emergencies with fewer procedures [1], saving lives sooner [1].
*Examiner insight:* Contrast roles directly. Writing separate paragraphs about each body without a contrast is weaker.

**10.** Industrialisation: 55% in farming suggests surplus labour [1], so moving workers into modern jobs can raise output without losing farm production [1]. Tourism: the coast could earn foreign exchange and jobs [1], but leakage and seasonality reduce gains [1]. Debt relief: a 28% ratio absorbs a large share of export earnings [1], so relief would free funds for human capital [1]. Primary dependence: one mineral gives 70% of exports, so diversification reduces price risk [1]. Aid at 8% of GNI offers funding but may bring dependence [1]. Judgement: prioritise industrialisation supported by infrastructure and skills, because it addresses low productivity [1]. Give conditions: if debt payments block investment, relief comes first [1].
*Examiner insight:* A recommendation is stronger when it names a condition under which another choice would be better.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Skipping the working in profit, multiplier and ratio questions.
- Dividing by the wrong base, such as GDP instead of exports for the debt service ratio.
- Applying a multiplier to total spending instead of the amount retained.
- Describing a strategy with no chain of reasoning to development.
- Listing pros and cons with no judgement or condition.
- Confusing the World Bank and the IMF.
- Forgetting that tied aid cuts real value.
- Quoting real statistics from memory in place of the data supplied.
