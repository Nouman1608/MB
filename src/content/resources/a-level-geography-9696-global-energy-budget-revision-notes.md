Condensed recall for **2.2 The global energy budget** (latitudinal imbalance and atmospheric and oceanic transfers). For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Greenhouse effect | Gases absorb outgoing longwave radiation and re-emit it, warming the lower atmosphere |
| Latitudinal imbalance | Surplus of energy at low latitudes, deficit at high latitudes |
| Hadley cell | Circulation between the equator and about 30 degrees |
| Ferrel cell | Circulation between about 30 and 60 degrees |
| Polar cell | Circulation between about 60 degrees and the pole |
| ITCZ | Low-pressure belt where trade winds converge |
| Coriolis effect | Deflection of moving air: right in the northern hemisphere, left in the southern |
| Pressure gradient | Difference in pressure over distance, which drives wind from high to low pressure |
| Subtropical high | Belt of high pressure near 30 degrees where air sinks |
| Jet stream | Fast, high-altitude wind band along the boundaries between cells |
| Thermohaline circulation | Deep ocean circulation driven by differences in temperature and salinity |

## Where the energy goes

Roughly **30 per cent** of incoming solar radiation is reflected back to space by cloud, the atmosphere and the surface (the planetary albedo). Of the rest, some is absorbed by the atmosphere, especially by water vapour, ozone and cloud, and roughly half is absorbed by the surface. The surface then passes energy to the atmosphere by longwave radiation, sensible heat and latent heat (evaporation, released when vapour condenses in cloud). Over a long period the Earth as a whole radiates as much energy to space as it absorbs, so the global average temperature stays stable unless something alters the balance.

## Why the imbalance exists

- Near the equator the sun is high, so radiation is concentrated and passes through less atmosphere.
- Near the poles radiation is spread over a larger area, passes through more atmosphere, and ice and snow have high albedo.
- Result: a surplus at low latitudes and a deficit at high latitudes. Without transfer the tropics would keep warming and the poles cooling; instead heat is moved poleward.

The boundary between surplus and deficit lies at roughly **35 to 40 degrees** of latitude in each hemisphere. Outgoing longwave radiation varies far less with latitude than incoming solar radiation does, which is why the imbalance appears.

## Transfers

- **Atmospheric circulation:** the three-cell model moves heat and moisture.
- **Ocean currents:** warm currents carry heat poleward; cold currents return towards the tropics.
- Seasonal change: the overhead sun, and so the pressure and wind belts and ITCZ, shift north and south through the year, with a lag.

**Transfer by latent heat:** water evaporated in the tropics carries energy with it; when that vapour condenses at higher latitudes, the latent heat is released. This is an important part of poleward transfer, alongside the movement of warm air.

**Ocean currents in detail:** surface currents are driven mainly by the winds and form large loops (gyres). The **Gulf Stream** and its extension, the North Atlantic Drift, carry warm water towards north-west Europe, which is milder in winter than places at the same latitude in eastern Canada. Cold currents, such as the **Humboldt (Peru) Current** off western South America, bring cold water towards the equator, cooling the coast and contributing to coastal desert conditions. The deep **thermohaline circulation** carries cold, salty, dense water from polar regions along the ocean floor, completing the global loop.

## Three-cell model

| Zone | Pressure | Air movement |
|---|---|---|
| Equator (ITCZ) | Low | Air rises |
| About 30 degrees | High | Air sinks |
| About 60 degrees | Low | Air rises |
| Poles | High | Air sinks |

Surface winds: trade winds blow towards the equator, westerlies at mid-latitudes, polar easterlies at high latitudes.

**How the Hadley cell works:** intense heating at the equator makes air rise, cool and condense, giving heavy convectional rain. At the tropopause the air spreads poleward, cools by radiation and converges, then sinks near 30 degrees. Sinking air warms by compression, so it is dry and cloud-free, producing the subtropical high-pressure belt and many of the world's hot deserts. Surface air then returns towards the equator as the trade winds, deflected by Coriolis into the north-east trades (northern hemisphere) and south-east trades (southern hemisphere).

## Seasonal variations

- The overhead sun moves between the Tropic of Cancer (June) and the Tropic of Capricorn (December).
- The ITCZ and the pressure and wind belts follow, but with a lag, and they shift further over continents, which heat and cool faster than oceans.
- Over South Asia, the northward summer shift of the ITCZ and strong heating of the land help draw in moist south-westerly winds, giving the **summer monsoon**.
- Areas between the equatorial and subtropical belts have a wet season when the ITCZ is overhead and a dry season when the subtropical high returns; this is the savanna rainfall pattern.
- Land-sea contrasts also shape temperature: continental interiors have hotter summers and colder winters than coasts at the same latitude.

## Annotated model paragraph

*"Explain how the latitudinal energy imbalance is corrected."*

> Low latitudes have an energy surplus and high latitudes a deficit (**cause**). The Hadley, Ferrel and Polar cells move warm air and latent heat poleward (**atmospheric transfer**). Warm currents such as the Gulf Stream carry heat poleward and cold currents return water towards the tropics (**oceanic transfer**). Together these keep latitudinal temperatures stable over time (**outcome**).

## Exam traps

- The Ferrel cell is driven by its neighbours; it is not heated directly.
- Say the imbalance is corrected by **transfer**, not that the equator "keeps getting hotter".
- Check hemisphere before stating Coriolis deflection.
- Sinking air means high pressure at the surface; rising air means low pressure.
- The ITCZ does not move exactly with the overhead sun; it lags and shifts more over land.
- Winds are named after the direction they blow **from**: a westerly blows from the west.
