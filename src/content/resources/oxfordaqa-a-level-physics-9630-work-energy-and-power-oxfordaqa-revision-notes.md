These notes condense **3.2.7 Work, energy and power** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [work, energy and power study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-work-energy-and-power-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions, use the [work, energy and power practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-work-energy-and-power-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Energy transferred W = Fs cos θ, θ the angle between F and s.
- Area under a force–displacement graph = work done.
- Power = rate of doing work = rate of energy transfer: P = ΔW/Δt = Fv.
- Quantitative questions on variable forces.
- Efficiency = useful output power ÷ input power; may be a percentage.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Work done | Force × distance moved in the direction of the force; equals energy transferred |
| Joule (J) | Work done when 1 N moves its point of application 1 m along its direction |
| Power | Rate of doing work or of transferring energy |
| Watt (W) | 1 J s⁻¹; in base units kg m² s⁻³ |
| Efficiency | Useful output ÷ total input (power or energy); no unit |
| Variable force | A force whose size changes as the object moves |

## Formulae

| Quantity | Equation | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Work | W = Fs cos θ | J |
| Work (variable force) | area under F–s graph | J |
| Power | P = ΔW/Δt | W |
| Power at constant velocity | P = Fv | W |
| Efficiency | useful output power ÷ input power | none (or %) |

## When is work done?

| Situation | Work done by the force? |
|---|---|
| Force along the motion | Yes, W = Fs |
| Force at angle θ to motion | Yes, W = Fs cos θ |
| Force perpendicular to motion (normal force on level floor) | No |
| Force with no movement (pushing a wall) | No |
| Force opposite to motion (friction) | Negative: energy is removed |
| Weight on an object moving down a slope | Yes, W = mgΔh (vertical drop only) |

## Method boxes

**Work at an angle**

1. Identify the displacement direction.
2. Find θ between F and s (not the angle to some other line).
3. W = F × s × cos θ.

**Work from a graph**

1. Split straight-line areas into rectangles and triangles; or
2. For a curve, find the value of one square (force per square × displacement per square) and count squares.

**Top speed problem**

1. At top speed: driving force = resistive force.
2. v = P ÷ F.
3. At a lower speed: driving force = P ÷ v, then resultant = driving − resistance, and a = resultant ÷ m.

**Efficiency**

1. Find useful output (energy or power).
2. Divide by total input of the same kind.
3. Multiply by 100 for a percentage; check it is below 100%.

## Worked reminders

- **Angle.** 50 N at 60° to the motion over 4.0 m: 50 × 4.0 × 0.5 = 100 J.
- **Graph.** Force rising from 0 to 30 N over 0.10 m: ½ × 0.10 × 30 = 1.5 J.
- **Power.** A crane lifts 400 kg through 12 m in 20 s: 400 × 9.81 × 12 ÷ 20 = 2.4 kW.
- **P = Fv.** A boat at a steady 5.0 m s⁻¹ against 1600 N of drag needs 8.0 kW of useful power.
- **Efficiency.** A motor 80% efficient delivering 2.0 kW needs 2.0 ÷ 0.80 = 2.5 kW input.

## Exam traps

- Distance along a path used where displacement in the force's direction is needed.
- sin θ in place of cos θ.
- Work claimed for a perpendicular force or a stationary object.
- Time left in minutes or hours in P = W/t.
- Resultant force used in P = Fv for engine power (use driving force).
- Efficiency over 100% or given a unit.

## Quick self-test

1. Define work done.
2. Define power.
3. A 50 N force pulls a box 4.0 m in the direction of the force. Find the work done.
4. The same force acts at 60° to the 4.0 m displacement. Find the work done.
5. How much work does the normal contact force do on a box sliding across a level floor?
6. A machine does 3.6 kJ of work in 1.0 minute. Find its power.
7. A truck moves at a steady 15 m s⁻¹ with a driving force of 800 N. Find the power.
8. A device gives 450 J of useful energy from 600 J input. Find its efficiency.
9. A force rises uniformly from 0 to 30 N over 0.10 m. Find the work done.
10. A motor is 80% efficient and delivers 2.0 kW of useful power. Find the input power.
11. Explain why a car with a fixed maximum power has a top speed.
12. Express the watt in SI base units.

### Answers

1. Force × distance moved in the direction of the force.
2. Rate of doing work (rate of energy transfer).
3. 50 × 4.0 = **200 J**.
4. 50 × 4.0 × cos 60° = **100 J**.
5. **Zero**: the force is perpendicular to the motion.
6. 3600 ÷ 60 = **60 W**.
7. 800 × 15 = **12 kW**.
8. 450 ÷ 600 = **0.75 (75%)**.
9. ½ × 0.10 × 30 = **1.5 J**.
10. 2.0 ÷ 0.80 = **2.5 kW**.
11. At fixed power the driving force P/v falls as speed rises, while resistance rises; when they are equal the resultant force is zero, so the car cannot accelerate further.
12. **kg m² s⁻³**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating which angle is θ in a diagram-based question.
- Counting squares without stating what one square is worth.
- Explanations of top speed that mention resistance but not the falling driving force.
