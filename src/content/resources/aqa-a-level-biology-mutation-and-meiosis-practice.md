## Questions

**1.** A base substitution occurs in a gene, but the polypeptide it codes for is unchanged. Explain how this is possible. **[3]**

**2.** A short piece of mRNA reads AUG CCU GUA AAA CAC UGA. Codon data: AUG Met, CCU Pro, GUA Val, GUG Val, AAA Lys, AAC Asn, CAC His, ACU Thr, UGA stop.
**(a)** Give the amino acid sequence it codes for. **[1]**
**(b)** The third codon changes from GUA to GUG. Describe and explain the effect on the polypeptide. **[2]**
**(c)** Instead, the first A of the fourth codon (AAA) is deleted. Give the new amino acid sequence for the codons that can be read and explain why the effect is greater than in part (b). **[3]**

**3.** Explain how non-disjunction during meiosis can lead to a child with 47 chromosomes in each body cell. **[4]**

**4.** A bacterial strain cannot grow without an added amino acid. Colonies that grow on plates lacking it have mutated. Plates without a test chemical gave 14, 16 and 12 colonies. Plates with the chemical gave 63, 70 and 77 colonies.
**(a)** Calculate the mean number of colonies for each condition. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase caused by the chemical. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what the results suggest about the chemical. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest one change that would make the conclusion more reliable. **[1]**

**5.** A diploid cell has four chromosomes: one long pair and one short pair. DNA has been replicated.
**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in each cell after meiosis I and how many chromatids each chromosome has. **[2]**
**(b)** State the number of cells after meiosis II, and the number of chromosomes and chromatids per chromosome in each. **[2]**

**6.** Give four differences between mitosis and meiosis. **[4]**

**7.** A species has 2n = 18. Ignore crossing over.
**(a)** Calculate the number of different gamete types one individual could make and the number of different zygotes from two such parents. **[3]**
**(b)** Another species can produce 4096 different types of gamete. Calculate its diploid number. **[2]**

**8.** In a seaweed, haploid filaments release gametes. Two gametes fuse to form a zygote, which grows into a large diploid plant. Cells in this plant divide to form spores. Each spore grows into a haploid filament.
**(a)** State where meiosis occurs. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how you know. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the gametes are made by mitosis and not meiosis. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how independent segregation, crossing over and random fertilisation each increase genetic variation. **[6]**

**10.** A gene has 900 bases. Data supplied give a spontaneous mutation rate of 4 × 10⁻⁹ per base per replication.
**(a)** Calculate the expected number of mutations in this gene over 3 × 10⁶ replications. **[2]**
**(b)** A mutagenic agent increases the rate eight-fold. Calculate the new expected number. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why deleting one base near the start of the gene is usually more harmful than substituting one base at the same place. **[3]**

**11.** A species has 2n = 10. In one meiotic cell, the chromatids of one chromosome fail to separate during meiosis II in one of the two cells.
**(a)** State the chromosome numbers of the four gametes. **[2]**
**(b)** State the chromosome number of a zygote formed from the largest gamete and a normal gamete. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of gamete types from independent segregation alone and the number of zygote types. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why one meiotic cell cannot make all of these gamete types. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The genetic code is degenerate, so most amino acids have more than one codon [1]. The substitution changed a codon into another codon for the same amino acid [1]. The amino acid sequence, and so the protein, is therefore unchanged [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* name degeneracy and say that the new triplet still codes for the same amino acid; "it doesn't matter" is too vague to explain anything.

**2. (a)** Met-Pro-Val-Lys-His, then stop [1]
**(b)** No effect [1]; GUA and GUG both code for Val, because the code is degenerate [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The mRNA becomes AUG CCU GUA AAC ACU GA, giving Met-Pro-Val-Asn-Thr [1]. A deletion shifts the reading frame [1], so every codon from the deletion onwards changes and the original stop codon is lost, whereas the substitution changed one codon only [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* regroup the bases into triplets in your working so the shifted frame is visible; comparing both mutations completes the explanation.

**3.** The pair of chromosomes 21 fails to separate in meiosis I, or chromatids fail to separate in meiosis II [1]. This gives a gamete with 24 chromosomes (an extra chromosome 21) [1]. It fuses with a normal gamete of 23 [1]. The zygote has 24 + 23 = 47 chromosomes, which are copied by mitosis into all body cells [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give the arithmetic and say which gamete has the extra chromosome; "chromosomes don't separate" alone is too vague.

**4. (a)** Without: (14 + 16 + 12) / 3 = **14**; with: (63 + 70 + 77) / 3 = **70** [2]
**(b)** (70 − 14) / 14 × 100 = **400%** [1]
**(c)** The chemical increases the rate of mutation, so it is a mutagenic agent [1]
**(d)** Use more plates or repeat the experiment [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* divide by the control value for percentage change; a conclusion should say the data suggest rather than prove.

**5. (a)** Two chromosomes (one long, one short) [1], each with two chromatids [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Four cells [1]; each has two chromosomes with one chromatid each [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* the chromosome number halves at meiosis I, and the chromatid number changes at meiosis II; keep the two counts separate.

**6.** Any four from: mitosis has one division, meiosis two [1]; mitosis gives two cells, meiosis four [1]; mitosis keeps the chromosome number, meiosis halves it [1]; mitosis gives identical cells, meiosis genetically different cells [1]; homologous chromosomes pair and cross over in meiosis only (a further valid point). **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* each difference needs both sides stated; "meiosis makes gametes" is a use, not a comparison.

**7. (a)** n = 9 [1]; 2⁹ = **512** gamete types [1]; 512 × 512 = **262 144** zygotes [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 2ⁿ = 4096, so n = 12 [1]; 2n = **24** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* write n = 9 before using the formula; using 18 in place of 9 is the usual error.

**8. (a)** In the diploid plant, where spores form [1]
**(b)** The chromosome number halves from diploid to haploid there [1]
**(c)** The filaments are already haploid [1], so mitosis keeps the number at n and fertilisation restores 2n [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* argue from chromosome number at each stage; a life-cycle answer without ploidy labels is hard to justify.

**9.** Independent segregation: homologous pairs line up at random in meiosis I [1], so each gamete receives a different mix of maternal and paternal chromosomes [1]. Crossing over: non-sister chromatids of homologous chromosomes swap sections [1], giving new combinations of alleles on the chromatids [1]. Random fertilisation: which male gamete reaches and fuses with which female gamete is a matter of chance [1], giving many possible genotypes in the zygotes [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* each process needs a place or timing and a result; "creates variation" repeats the question.

**10. (a)** 900 × 4 × 10⁻⁹ = 3.6 × 10⁻⁶ per replication [1]; × 3 × 10⁶ = **10.8** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 10.8 × 8 = **86.4** [1]
**(c)** A deletion moves every later base up one place [1], so all triplets after the deletion are changed [1]. A substitution alters at most one triplet, which may be the same amino acid because of degeneracy [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* show the rate multiplied by bases first, then replications; quote the data in each step.

**11. (a)** The affected cell gives gametes of 6 and 4 chromosomes [1]; the other cell gives two gametes of 5 [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 6 + 5 = **11** [1]
**(c)** n = 5, so 2⁵ = **32** gamete types [1]; 32 × 32 = **1024** zygote types [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** After meiosis I, each of the two cells has one fixed combination of chromosomes [1]. Meiosis II only separates chromatids, so the two cells yield at most two types in total [1]. Many meiotic cells with different random orientations are needed [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* in part (d) say that meiosis II does not reshuffle chromosomes; that reasoning is the core of the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying a silent mutation has "no mutation" rather than naming degeneracy.
- Forgetting that a deletion moves the reading frame, so every later codon changes.
- Writing triplets across the deletion without regrouping them.
- Giving the diploid number instead of the number of pairs when using 2ⁿ.
- Putting the halving of chromosome number at meiosis II.
- Writing "chromosomes" when chromatids are meant, and the reverse.
- Dividing by the new value when calculating a percentage increase.
- Treating all gametes as products of meiosis in plants and algae.
