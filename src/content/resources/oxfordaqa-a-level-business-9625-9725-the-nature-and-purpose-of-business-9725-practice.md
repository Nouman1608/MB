## Section A: Short-answer and calculation questions

**1.** State **two** non-financial motivations for setting up in business. **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "unique selling point". **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between an entrepreneur being creative and being innovative. **[3]**

**4.** Copperleaf Candles' business plan forecasts sales of 500 candles a month at £12 each. Variable cost is £5 per candle and fixed costs are £2,800 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the forecast monthly profit. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the monthly profit if sales are 20% below the forecast. **[2]**

**5.** Ridgeway Gym set the objective of increasing membership from 800 to 1,000 by the end of the year. By June it has 880 members.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in membership the objective requires. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the target increase achieved by June. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** reason why cash flow might be the main objective for a business that is growing rapidly. **[3]**

## Section B: Extended-response questions

**7.** Nadia has developed a way to turn plastic waste into building blocks. Two banks have doubted her idea and her first prototype cracked in testing. She has redesigned it and is now talking to local builders. Analyse how **two** characteristics of an entrepreneur would help Nadia start her business. **[6]**

**8.** Extract from Pembury Pedals' business plan, for an electric bike hire business in a tourist town:

| Element | Content |
|---|---|
| Ownership | Sole trader |
| Objectives | "To be successful" |
| Target market and market analysis | Tourists visiting the town |
| Competitor analysis | "No competitors known" |
| USP | Electric bikes |
| Financial analysis | Revenue forecast assumes every bike is hired every day for 12 months |

Analyse **two** weaknesses of this business plan. **[6]**

**9.** A bank receives a business plan from an entrepreneur asking for a loan to start a restaurant. Assess how useful the business plan is to the bank in deciding whether to lend. **[9]**

**10.** Kestrel Software was founded by two programmers who wrote all the code and dealt with every customer themselves. It now has 60 employees. Explain how the founders' roles are likely to have changed. **[4]**

**11.** Bluefin Laundry has lost customers since a new low-price launderette opened nearby. The owner does not want to cut prices. Analyse **two** ways Bluefin could improve its competitiveness without cutting prices. **[6]**

**12.** Tamarind Threads makes clothing and employs refugees who have struggled to find work, giving them training and a fair wage. Its founder's main objective is social. Profit has been low for two years, and an investor has offered funding if Tamarind changes its main objective to profit growth. Evaluate whether Tamarind Threads should make profit growth its main objective. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: independence, or being one's own boss [1]; pursuing an interest or passion (or flexible hours, or meeting a social need) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A feature of a product or business that makes it different from its rivals [1] and gives customers a reason to choose it over competitors [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Being creative means generating new ideas [1], whereas being innovative means turning those ideas into products or processes that work and sell in the market [1]. An entrepreneur can have many creative ideas but succeed only if at least one is developed into a viable product [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Revenue = 500 × 12 = £6,000; variable costs = 500 × 5 = £2,500 [1]; profit = 6,000 − 2,500 − 2,800 = **£700** [1].

**(b)** Sales = 400; revenue = £4,800; variable costs = £2,000 [1]; profit = 4,800 − 2,000 − 2,800 = **£0**, so the business only breaks even [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The calculation shows how sensitive a new business's plan is to its sales forecast.

**5. (a)** (1,000 − 800) ÷ 800 × 100 [1] = **25%** [1].

**(b)** Increase so far = 80; target increase = 200 [1]; 80 ÷ 200 × 100 = **40%** [1]. **[4]**

**6.** A rapidly growing business must pay for extra stock, staff and equipment before the extra sales bring in cash [1]. If cash runs out, it cannot pay suppliers or wages on time [1], so even a profitable business could fail; making cash flow the main objective helps it survive the growth period [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Resilience [1]: two banks have doubted her and her prototype failed, but Nadia redesigned it rather than giving up [1], which means she is likely to keep approaching other lenders and builders until she finds support [1]. Innovation [1]: she has turned an idea into a product that solves a real problem, using waste to make building blocks [1], which could give her a clear USP that builders and investors find attractive, provided the redesigned blocks pass testing (also credit: creative, adaptable, risk taker, applied) [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Weakness 1: the objective "to be successful" is not SMART [1]. It has no measure, owner or deadline, so the owner cannot judge progress [1], and a lender cannot see what the business is trying to achieve [1]. Weakness 2: the financial analysis is unrealistic [1]. Assuming every bike is hired every day for a year ignores bad weather and the quiet season in a tourist town [1], so revenue is overstated and the business could run short of cash if actual hire is much lower (also credit: "no competitors known" suggests little research; target market too vague) [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Useful: the plan shows whether the entrepreneur has researched the target market and competitors [1], helping the bank judge whether there is demand for another restaurant [1]. The financial analysis shows forecast sales, costs and cash flow, so the bank can see whether loan repayments can be afforded [1]. The ownership section shows who is responsible and whether there is personal liability [1]. Limitations: the forecasts are the entrepreneur's own estimates and may be optimistic [1]; restaurants face strong competition and changing tastes, so conditions may differ from the plan [1]. The plan cannot show the entrepreneur's character, such as resilience and hard work [1]. Judgement: the plan is useful as a starting point, but the bank should test its assumptions [1], for example by asking for security and comparing the forecasts with similar restaurants, before relying on it [1]. **[9]**

**10.** At the start the founders wrote the code and dealt with customers themselves [1]. With 60 employees they cannot do this, so they must delegate programming and customer service to staff and managers [1]. Their role shifts to leading the business: setting objectives, deciding which products to develop and finding finance [1]. They may also need to learn new skills, such as managing people, or appoint experienced managers to do so [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Way 1: improve service quality [1]. For example, Bluefin could offer collection and delivery or a same-day service [1], giving busy customers a reason to pay more than at the low-price rival, which protects revenue without a price war [1]. Way 2: build a distinctive brand or USP [1], such as eco-friendly detergents or a loyalty scheme [1]; this targets customers who value these features, although it adds costs, so Bluefin must check that enough customers will pay for them [1]. **[6]**

**12.** For profit growth: profit has been low for two years, so Tamarind may not survive without more finance [1]. The investor's funding could pay for equipment and marketing, increasing sales [1], and a profitable business can employ more refugees in the long run [1]. Against: the social objective is the reason the founder started the business [1], and changing it could mean cutting training or wages to raise profit [1], harming the people the business exists to help [1]. Customers who buy because of the social aim may stop buying if it is weakened [1], so profit might not even rise [1]. Alternatives: Tamarind could look for social investors or grants that accept a social main objective [1]. Judgement: Tamarind should not make profit growth its main objective [1], but it should set a profit objective alongside the social one, such as breaking even within a year [1], and accept the investor only if the social aim is protected in the agreement [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* An evaluation that recognises survival depends on profit, without abandoning the social purpose, shows balanced judgement.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing characteristics without linking them to the entrepreneur in the case.
- Treating creativity and innovation as the same.
- Describing plan elements without judging their quality or realism.
- Writing "Achievable" for the A in SMART.
- Calculating a percentage of the wrong base.
- Treating competitiveness only as low prices.
- Evaluations that end without a judgement or a condition.
