This guide covers **4.9.1 The composition and evolution of the Earth's atmosphere** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. It sits in topic 4.9, Chemistry of the atmosphere, and
is assessed on Paper 2 at both Foundation and Higher Tier. It is not chemistry only, and there is no Higher tier
only statement.

Use the [composition and evolution of the atmosphere practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-the-composition-and-evolution-of-the-earths-atmosphere-practice/)
to test yourself, and the [chemistry of the atmosphere revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-chemistry-of-the-atmosphere-revision-notes/)

## 4.9.1.1 The proportions of gases today

For about **200 million years**, the proportions of the gases in the atmosphere have been much the same as they
are today:

| Gas | Approximate proportion |
|---|---|
| Nitrogen | About **four-fifths (80%)** |
| Oxygen | About **one-fifth (20%)** |
| Others | **Small proportions**: carbon dioxide, water vapour and noble gases (such as argon) |

The specification uses approximate values, 80% and 20%. More precisely, nitrogen is about 78%, oxygen about 21%,
and the rest (argon, carbon dioxide at about 0.04%, and water vapour in varying amounts) makes up around 1%.

### Worked example 1: using the proportions

*A sealed room contains 600 dm³ of air. Estimate the volume of oxygen.*

```
oxygen is about one-fifth: 600 / 5 = 120 dm3
or 20% of 600 = 0.20 x 600 = 120 dm3
```

### Worked example 2: a practical to find the percentage of oxygen

*100 cm³ of air is passed back and forth over heated copper in a syringe system until the volume stops changing.
The final volume is 79 cm³. Calculate the percentage of oxygen.*

```
oxygen used = 100 - 79 = 21 cm3
percentage = 21 / 100 x 100 = 21 %
```

The copper reacts with the oxygen (2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO), so the volume falls by the volume of oxygen. The remaining
gas is mainly nitrogen.

## 4.9.1.2 The Earth's early atmosphere

The Earth is **4.6 billion years** old. **Evidence** for the early atmosphere is **limited** because of the
time scale, so scientists' ideas are **theories** that have **changed and developed over time**.

### The volcanic theory

One theory says that during the **first billion years** there was **intense volcanic activity**. The volcanoes
released the gases that formed the early atmosphere, together with **water vapour**.

1. **Volcanoes** released carbon dioxide (the main gas), water vapour, nitrogen and small amounts of methane and
   ammonia.
2. The early atmosphere may have been like those of **Mars and Venus today**: **mainly carbon dioxide** with
   **little or no oxygen**.
3. As the Earth **cooled**, the **water vapour condensed** and formed the **oceans**.
4. **Nitrogen** gradually built up in the atmosphere. It is unreactive, so it was not removed.
5. When the oceans formed, **carbon dioxide dissolved** in the water, and **carbonates were precipitated**,
   making sediments. This **reduced the amount of carbon dioxide** in the atmosphere.

No knowledge of other theories is required.

### Evaluating theories

You may be given evidence and asked to interpret it or compare theories. Good habits:

- Say what the evidence **supports** and what it **cannot show**.
- Note how old the evidence is, and whether it is indirect (for example, the atmospheres of Mars and Venus are
  used for comparison).
- Say the theory can change if new evidence appears.
- Remember that the **time scale** (billions of years) means direct evidence is rare.

## 4.9.1.3 How oxygen increased

**Algae** and **plants** produced the oxygen in the atmosphere by **photosynthesis**:

carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen (in light, using chlorophyll)

**6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂**

- **Algae first produced oxygen about 2.7 billion years ago**, and soon after this oxygen appeared in the
  atmosphere.
- Over the next **billion years**, **plants evolved** and the **percentage of oxygen gradually increased** to a
  level that allowed **animals** to evolve.

The oxygen first reacted with other substances (such as dissolved iron), so the build-up in the air took time.

Photosynthesis therefore changed the atmosphere twice over: it **added oxygen** and it **removed carbon dioxide**.

## 4.9.1.4 How carbon dioxide decreased

Carbon dioxide in the atmosphere decreased because of:

1. **Photosynthesis** by algae and plants, which takes in carbon dioxide.
2. **Dissolving in the oceans** and the formation of **carbonates** that built up as sediments.
3. The formation of **sedimentary rocks** and **fossil fuels** that **contain carbon**, which locked it away.

### Formation of deposits

You must be able to describe and explain the formation of **limestone, coal, crude oil and natural gas**.

| Deposit | How it formed |
|---|---|
| **Limestone** | Shells and skeletons of marine organisms, made of **calcium carbonate**, settled on the sea floor. Over millions of years they were compressed into sedimentary rock. |
| **Coal** | **Plant material** was buried, and heat and pressure over millions of years changed it into coal. |
| **Crude oil and natural gas** | Remains of **plankton** (tiny sea organisms) were buried in mud. Heat and pressure over millions of years, without oxygen, changed them into oil and gas. |

All of these hold **carbon** that was originally taken from the carbon dioxide in the air, so carbon dioxide was
removed from the atmosphere.

### Summary of the changes over time

| Time | Main gases | What caused the change |
|---|---|---|
| First billion years | Carbon dioxide, water vapour, nitrogen, little or no oxygen | Volcanic activity |
| Cooling | Less water vapour | Condensation to form oceans |
| After oceans | Less carbon dioxide | Carbon dioxide dissolved; carbonates precipitated |
| From about 2.7 billion years ago | Oxygen rising, carbon dioxide falling | Algae, then plants, photosynthesising |
| Last 200 million years | Nitrogen about 80%, oxygen about 20% | Steady balance |

## Exam technique

- Quote the **numbers**: about 80% nitrogen, about 20% oxygen, 4.6 billion years, 2.7 billion years ago.
- Use **cause and effect**: volcanoes released gases; cooling condensed water; plants made oxygen.
- When describing deposits, name the **organisms**, the **burial**, and the **heat and pressure over millions of
  years**.
- For evidence questions, comment on how reliable the evidence is and what it cannot show.
- For photosynthesis, give the equation or the word equation, and say it takes in carbon dioxide.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the early atmosphere had a lot of oxygen. It had little or none.
- Saying the early atmosphere was mainly nitrogen. It was mainly carbon dioxide at first.
- Saying that oxygen appeared as soon as the Earth formed. Algae started producing it about 2.7 billion years
  ago.
- Saying the oceans formed before the Earth cooled. The water vapour had to condense first.
- Saying photosynthesis uses oxygen and gives carbon dioxide (that is respiration).
- Saying fossil fuels formed from dinosaurs. Coal is from plants, oil and gas from plankton.

## Putting it together: a model extended answer

*Describe how the Earth's atmosphere has changed over 4.6 billion years and give reasons for the changes.*

> The early atmosphere was formed by gases from intense volcanic activity in the first billion years. It was
> mainly carbon dioxide with water vapour, nitrogen, and small amounts of methane and ammonia, with little or no
> oxygen, like Mars and Venus today. As the Earth cooled, the water vapour condensed to form the oceans. Carbon
> dioxide dissolved in the oceans and carbonates were precipitated as sediments, which reduced the carbon dioxide.
> About 2.7 billion years ago algae began to photosynthesise, taking in carbon dioxide and releasing oxygen,
> and later plants did the same, so the percentage of oxygen gradually rose to about 20%, allowing animals to
> evolve. Carbon was also locked into fossil fuels and limestone. Nitrogen, which is unreactive, built up to about
> 80%.
