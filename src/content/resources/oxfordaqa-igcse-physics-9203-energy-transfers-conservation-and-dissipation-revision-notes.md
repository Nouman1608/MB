Condensed recall for **3.2.2 Energy transfers, conservation and dissipation of energy** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Statement b (pendulum) is P content. Explanations and worked examples are in the [energy transfers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-conservation-and-dissipation/); exam-style questions are in the [energy transfers practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-conservation-and-dissipation-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| System | An object or group of objects being studied |
| Kinetic energy, Ek | Energy of a moving object; ½ m v² |
| Gravitational potential energy, Ep | Energy gained when an object is raised; m g h |
| Elastic potential energy, Ee | Energy stored in a stretched or compressed elastic object; ½ k e² |
| Conservation of energy | Energy can be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated, but cannot be created or destroyed |
| Dissipated energy | Energy spread into the surroundings and stored in less useful ways; often called "wasted" |
| Useful energy | The energy transferred in the way the device is designed for |
| Efficiency | Useful output ÷ total input (energy or power); no unit |
| Sankey diagram | A flow diagram whose arrow widths are proportional to amounts of energy |
| Oscillation | A repeated back-and-forth motion, such as a pendulum swing |

## Equations used in this section

```
Ek = 1/2 x m x v^2
Ep = m x g x h
Ee = 1/2 x k x e^2
efficiency = useful energy out / total energy in
efficiency = useful power out / total power in
```

## Condensed recall

**Systems (a).** When a system changes, energy is transferred between stores or out to the surroundings. Name the stores and say which increase and which decrease.

**Pendulum (b, P).**
- Highest points: Ek = 0, Ep maximum.
- Lowest point: Ek maximum, Ep minimum.
- Air resistance and friction at the pivot dissipate energy, so the amplitude decreases.
- With no dissipation: Ep lost = Ek gained, so v = √(2gh), independent of mass.

**Conservation (c).** Total energy before = total energy after. "Lost" energy has gone to the surroundings.

**Dissipation (d, e).** Friction and air resistance dissipate energy by heating the surroundings. Dissipated energy is spread out and hard to use.

**Reducing dissipation.** Lubrication, streamlining, thermal insulation.

**Efficiency (f).** Decimal or percentage. Never above 1 (100%). No unit.

**Sankey diagrams (g).** Input on the left; useful output straight on; wasted output bends away. Output widths add up to the input width.

## Sankey method

1. Choose a scale, for example 1 mm to 10 J.
2. Width of each arrow = energy ÷ scale value.
3. Check the outputs sum to the input.
4. Label each arrow with the energy type and amount.
5. Efficiency = useful width ÷ input width.

## Energy stores in common situations

| Situation | Store decreasing | Store increasing | Dissipation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ball falling | Ep | Ek | Air resistance heats the air |
| Car braking | Ek | Thermal energy of brakes | Brakes heat the surroundings |
| Catapult firing | Ee of band | Ek of stone | Band warms slightly |
| Cyclist riding uphill at steady speed | Chemical energy of the cyclist | Ep | Friction and air resistance |

## Worked check: conservation with dissipation

A 0.20 kg ball is dropped from 4.0 m (g = 9.8 N/kg) and is moving at 8.0 m/s just before it lands.

- Ep lost = 0.20 × 9.8 × 4.0 = 7.84 J.
- Ek gained = ½ × 0.20 × 8.0² = 6.4 J.
- Dissipated by air resistance = 7.84 − 6.4 = 1.44 J.
- Efficiency of the transfer to kinetic energy = 6.4 ÷ 7.84 = 0.82.

## Exam traps

- **Never "used up".** Say transferred, stored or dissipated.
- **Efficiency greater than 1** means a calculation error; check numerator and denominator.
- **Square the speed** in ½ m v², and only the speed.
- **Mass in kg, height in m, g as given.**
- **Sankey outputs must add up** to the input.
- **Pendulum mass cancels** in the speed calculation, but not in the energy.
- **"Wasted" energy still exists**; it is stored in less useful ways.

## Self-test

**1.** Define a system.

**2.** State the principle of conservation of energy.

**3.** Where is the kinetic energy of a pendulum bob greatest?

**4.** Why does a swinging pendulum gradually stop?

**5.** A 2.0 kg ball falls 5.0 m. Find the Ep lost (g = 9.8 N/kg).

**6.** If no energy is dissipated, find the ball's kinetic energy after the fall in question 5.

**7.** A motor receives 400 J and gives 260 J of useful energy. Find its efficiency as a percentage.

**8.** A lamp takes in 40 W and is 0.15 efficient. Find its useful power output.

**9.** A Sankey input arrow is 60 mm wide at 1 mm to 5 J. What energy does it represent?

**10.** Name two ways of reducing unwanted energy dissipation in a machine.

**11.** A student calculates an efficiency of 1.25. What has gone wrong?

**12.** Describe the energy changes when a catapult fires a stone straight upwards to its highest point.

## Self-test answers

**1.** An object or group of objects.

**2.** Energy can be transferred usefully, stored or dissipated, but cannot be created or destroyed.

**3.** At the lowest point of the swing.

**4.** Air resistance and friction at the pivot dissipate energy by heating the surroundings.

**5.** Ep = 2.0 × 9.8 × 5.0 = 98 J.

**6.** 98 J.

**7.** 260 ÷ 400 = 0.65 = 65%.

**8.** 0.15 × 40 = 6.0 W.

**9.** 60 × 5 = 300 J.

**10.** Any two: lubricate moving parts; streamline; insulate.

**11.** The values are the wrong way up (total ÷ useful), or a wrong value was used; efficiency cannot exceed 1.

**12.** Ee of the band decreases and the stone's Ek increases; as the stone rises, Ek decreases and Ep increases until Ek is zero at the top; some energy is dissipated by air resistance.
