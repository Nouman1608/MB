## Questions

**1.** Describe the relationship between health and disease. **[3]**

**2.** State whether each condition is communicable or non-communicable. **[5]**
Scurvy; athlete's foot; cystic fibrosis; malaria; a cancer of the lung.

**3.** **(a)** Explain why a person infected with HIV is more likely to develop tuberculosis. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how infection with HPV is linked to cervical cancer. **[3]**

**4.** Influenza spreads quickly through a school.
**(a)** Explain why the virus spreads so easily there. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two ways the school could reduce the spread. **[2]**

**5.** A culture begins with 25 bacteria. Each bacterium divides every 20 minutes.
**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria after 3 hours, assuming every cell keeps dividing. **[3]**
**(b)** Write your answer to part (a) in standard form. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why the real number would be lower. **[1]**

**6.** A town has 8500 residents. A random sample of 250 residents is tested for a pathogen and 38 test positive.
**(a)** Estimate the number of infected residents in the town. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the sample should be random. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how the estimate could be made more reliable. **[1]**

**7.** Town P has 21 000 people. It recorded 63 new cases of a disease in a year. After a hygiene campaign it recorded 45.
**(a)** Calculate the incidence before the campaign, in cases per 100 000 people. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in the number of cases. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why incidence is better than the number of cases for comparing two towns. **[1]**

**8.** A clinic counted mosquitoes caught in traps each month and the number of malaria cases that month.

| Mosquitoes caught | 12 | 25 | 31 | 44 | 52 | 60 | 75 | 88 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Malaria cases | 4 | 9 | 8 | 15 | 19 | 18 | 27 | 31 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation shown by the data, quoting figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the data alone do not prove that more mosquitoes cause more cases. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way to reduce mosquito numbers. **[1]**

**9.** A gardener sees mosaic-patterned leaves on tomato plants and suspects a viral disease.
**(a)** Describe two ways a fungal disease could spread between plants in a greenhouse. **[2]**
**(b)** Name two methods, other than looking at the leaves, that could detect the pathogen. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why visual identification alone may be unreliable. **[2]**

**10.** Three hospital wards recorded infections per 1000 patient-days for six months before and after changes.

| Ward | Change made | Before | After |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Alcohol gel at every bed and staff reminders | 8.4 | 5.1 |
| B | Staff given a leaflet only | 7.9 | 7.2 |
| C | No change | 8.1 | 7.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease for ward A. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the data to evaluate which change was most effective. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why the comparison between wards may not be fair. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how alcohol gel helps break the route of spread. **[2]**

**11.** A grower tested treatments against a fungal leaf disease. Each treatment used 100 plants. The table shows the number infected after six weeks.

| Treatment | Plants infected |
|---|---|
| None | 64 |
| Fungicide spray | 21 |
| Wider spacing | 38 |
| Spray and wider spacing | 9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in infection produced by the spray alone. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage reduction for the combined treatment. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how wider spacing reduces the spread of a disease carried by spores. **[2]**
**(d)** Evaluate whether the grower should use the spray on every crop. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Health is a state of physical, mental and social wellbeing, not just the absence of disease [1]. Disease can reduce health in one or more of these areas [1]. Poor health, for example from a poor diet, can make disease more likely [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers usually gain credit for naming all three parts of health and for showing the link runs both ways; "health means not ill" alone does not describe a relationship.

**2.** Scurvy: non-communicable [1]. Athlete's foot: communicable [1]. Cystic fibrosis: non-communicable [1]. Malaria: communicable [1]. Lung cancer: non-communicable [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each item is judged separately, so a pattern guess loses credit; cystic fibrosis is inherited but still not communicable, and cancer is not caught.

**3. (a)** HIV damages the immune system [1]. The body is less able to destroy TB bacteria [1]. So TB infection is more likely to take hold or become active [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** HPV is a virus that infects cells of the cervix [1]. Long-lasting infection with some types changes the cells [1]. They divide uncontrollably and form a tumour; the cancer is not itself communicable [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit by showing the direction of effect and the mechanism; "HIV causes TB" or "HPV is cancer" are the usual wrong turns.

**4. (a)** Pupils are close together so droplets from coughs and sneezes reach many people [1]. Shared surfaces and hands also pass the virus on [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two from: cover coughs and sneezes, wash hands, ventilate rooms, keep ill pupils at home [1] [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Crowded" alone is weak; answers earn the mark when the crowding is linked to the route of transmission.

**5. (a)** 3 hours = 180 minutes, so 9 divisions [1]. 25 × 2⁹ = 25 × 512 [1]. **12 800 bacteria [1]**. **[3]**
**(b)** **1.28 × 10⁴ [1]**.
**(c)** Nutrients or space run out, or the body's defences kill some cells [1].
*Examiner insight:* Showing the number of divisions first makes the working easy to follow; a standard-form answer needs a number between 1 and 10 multiplied by a power of ten.

**6. (a)** Proportion = 38 ÷ 250 = 0.152 [1]. 0.152 × 8500 [1]. **1292 infected [1]**. **[3]**
**(b)** A random sample is not biased towards people with symptoms, so it represents the whole town [1].
**(c)** Test a larger sample (or repeat with several random samples) [1].
*Examiner insight:* State that this is an estimate; "random" earns credit when tied to avoiding bias, not just repeated as a word.

**7. (a)** 63 ÷ 21 000 × 100 000 [1]. **300 per 100 000 [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** (63 − 45) ÷ 63 × 100 [1]. **28.6% decrease [1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** It allows for different population sizes [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give a unit with incidence and say "decrease"; a percentage change needs the original value as the divisor.

**8. (a)** Positive correlation [1], for example 12 mosquitoes with 4 cases and 88 mosquitoes with 31 cases (strong, points close to a line) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Correlation shows an association only [1]. Another factor, such as rainfall or season, could raise both mosquito numbers and cases [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Drain standing water, or use insecticide on breeding sites [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quoting two data points shows you read the table; avoid "proves" when only a correlation is given.

**9. (a)** Wind-blown spores [1]. Water splash, or contact on hands and tools [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Antigen test [1]. DNA testing [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Different problems can cause similar symptoms [1]. Infected plants may show no symptoms early on [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct points are needed where two are asked for; rewording the same idea gains nothing.

**10. (a)** 8.4 − 5.1 = 3.3 [1]. 3.3 ÷ 8.4 × 100 = **39.3% [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** Ward A fell by 39.3%, ward B by 8.9% and control ward C by 2.5% [1]. So alcohol gel with reminders was most effective [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Wards may differ in patient illness or size [1]. Other changes, such as staffing or season, may have affected infections [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Gel kills pathogens on hands [1]. This stops contact passing them to patients [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing percentages, not raw changes, and using the control ward as a baseline are what turn a statement into evaluation.

**11. (a)** (64 − 21) ÷ 64 × 100 [1]. **67.2% [1]**. **[2]**
**(b)** (64 − 9) ÷ 64 × 100 [1]. **86% [1]**. **[2]**
**(c)** Spores must travel further between plants [1]. Fewer reach a leaf, and leaves are less likely to touch [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Spray cut infection by about two thirds, and combining gave the lowest infection [1]. Limits: spray does not remove all infection, costs money, and may harm other organisms or lead to resistant fungi [1]. Judgement: use it where disease risk is high, together with spacing and removing infected plants, not blindly on every crop [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers gain credit when a benefit, a limit and a clear judgement all appear, with at least one figure quoted.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing health as only physical.
- Labelling cancer, scurvy or cystic fibrosis as communicable.
- Writing "HIV causes TB" instead of explaining the weakened immune system.
- Treating the vector as the pathogen.
- Giving a control method that does not match the route.
- Dropping units or "per 100 000" from incidence.
- Using the new value, not the original, as the divisor for percentage change.
- Calling an estimate from a sample an exact figure.
- Claiming that a correlation proves cause.
