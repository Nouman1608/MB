This set practises **3.3.11.3 Nucleophilic properties** of amines from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [amine nucleophilic properties study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-3/) or the [amine nucleophilic properties revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-3-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, Cl = 35.5 and Br = 79.9.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of these cannot act as a nucleophile?

A: (CH₃)₃N

B: (CH₃)₂NH

C: (CH₃)₄N⁺ I⁻

D: CH₃NH₂

**[1]**

**2.** Classify each compound as a primary, secondary or tertiary amine or a quaternary ammonium salt.

**(a)** (CH₃CH₂CH₂)₂NH **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH₂N⁺(CH₃)₃ Br⁻ **[1]**

**(c)** (CH₃)₂NCH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**3.** Name the type of mechanism for the reaction of methylamine with:

**(a)** 1-iodobutane **[1]**

**(b)** butanoyl chloride **[1]**

**4.** Write an equation for the reaction of diethylamine with iodomethane to form a tertiary amine. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a quaternary ammonium salt with one long hydrocarbon chain can act as a cationic surfactant. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Butylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂, reacts with bromomethane to form a secondary amine.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism. Describe where each curly arrow starts and ends, and give the structure of the intermediate. **[4]**

**(b)** Name the secondary amine formed. **[1]**

**7.** Ammonia is heated with an excess of chloromethane.

**(a)** Give the formulae of the four nitrogen-containing products formed in turn. **[2]**

**(b)** One product, a white solid, has Mr = 109.5. Identify it. **[1]**

**(c)** State how the conditions should be changed to make methylamine the main product. **[1]**

**8.** Trimethylamine reacts with 1-bromotetradecane, CH₃(CH₂)₁₃Br.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the product. **[1]**

**(c)** The product is used in a fabric softener. Explain how its structure allows it to interact with fabric fibres, which carry negative charges, and with oily substances. **[2]**

**9.** Propylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂, reacts with butanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl.

**(a)** Outline the mechanism, describing each curly arrow and the intermediate. **[4]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** Write an overall equation for the reaction, using two molecules of propylamine. **[1]**

**10.** Propanoic anhydride, (CH₃CH₂CO)₂O, reacts with excess ethylamine.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction and name the amide formed. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why no hydrogen chloride is formed. **[1]**

**11.** An amide, V, has the molecular formula C₄H₉NO.

**(a)** V is made from ethanoyl chloride and a primary amine. Identify the amine and give the name of V. **[2]**

**(b)** An isomer of V is made from an acyl chloride and ammonia. Give the name of one such isomer. **[1]**

**12.** N-Ethylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃, is to be made from bromoethane in two steps. Give the reagents and conditions for each step and name the intermediate. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. The nitrogen in a quaternary ammonium ion has no lone pair.

**2. (a)** Secondary amine [1]

**(b)** Quaternary ammonium salt [1]

**(c)** Tertiary amine [1]

**3. (a)** Nucleophilic substitution [1]

**(b)** Nucleophilic addition–elimination [1]

**4.** (CH₃CH₂)₂NH + CH₃I → (CH₃CH₂)₂NCH₃ + HI (accept 2(CH₃CH₂)₂NH + CH₃I → (CH₃CH₂)₂NCH₃ + (CH₃CH₂)₂NH₂⁺I⁻) [1]

**5.** The positively charged N⁺ head is ionic and interacts with water (and with negatively charged surfaces) [1]; the long non-polar hydrocarbon tail mixes with oil and grease [1]

**6. (a)** Arrow from the lone pair on N of butylamine to the C of CH₃Br [1]; arrow from the C–Br bond to Br, which leaves as Br⁻ [1]; intermediate CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂N⁺H₂CH₃ [1]; a second butylamine molecule removes H⁺ from N⁺, with an arrow from the N–H bond to N [1]

**(b)** N-Methylbutylamine (accept N-methylbutan-1-amine), CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NHCH₃ [1]

*Examiner insight:* the intermediate must carry the + on nitrogen; a + on carbon suggests a carbocation, which is not formed here.

**7. (a)** CH₃NH₂; (CH₃)₂NH; (CH₃)₃N; (CH₃)₄N⁺Cl⁻. All four [2]; two or three [1]

*Examiner insight:* the quaternary product is a salt, so its formula must show both ions; writing (CH₃)₄N without the charge and the chloride is not credited.

**(b)** Tetramethylammonium chloride, (CH₃)₄N⁺Cl⁻ (C₄H₁₂NCl = 109.5) [1]

**(c)** Use a large excess of ammonia [1]

**8. (a)** (CH₃)₃N + CH₃(CH₂)₁₃Br → CH₃(CH₂)₁₃N⁺(CH₃)₃ Br⁻ [1]

**(b)** C₁₇H₃₈NBr: 204.0 + 38.0 + 14.0 + 79.9 = **335.9** [1]

**(c)** The positive N⁺ head is attracted to the negative charges on the fibres [1]; the long non-polar hydrocarbon tail mixes with (dissolves in) oily substances [1]

**9. (a)** Arrow from the lone pair on N of propylamine to the δ+ carbonyl carbon, and arrow from the C=O π bond to O [1]; intermediate CH₃CH₂CH₂–C(O⁻)(Cl)–N⁺H₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ [1]; arrow from O⁻ lone pair re-forming C=O, and arrow from the C–Cl bond to Cl, which leaves as Cl⁻ [1]; a second propylamine molecule removes H⁺ from N⁺ [1]

**(b)** N-Propylbutanamide [1]

**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl + 2CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CONHCH₂CH₂CH₃ + CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]

**10. (a)** (CH₃CH₂CO)₂O + 2CH₃CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ + CH₃CH₂COO⁻ ⁺H₃NCH₂CH₃ [1]; N-ethylpropanamide [1]

**(b)** The group that leaves is a propanoate ion, not a chloride ion, so the by-product is propanoic acid (here as its ethylammonium salt) [1]

**11. (a)** Ethylamine [1]; V is N-ethylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ [1]

**(b)** Butanamide, CH₃CH₂CH₂CONH₂ (or 2-methylpropanamide) [1]

**12.** Step 1: excess ammonia in ethanol, heated in a sealed tube [1]; intermediate ethylamine [1]; step 2: ethanoyl chloride (or ethanoic anhydride), using two moles of ethylamine per mole of acyl chloride, at room temperature [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- A positive charge drawn on carbon instead of nitrogen in the intermediate.
- Forgetting the second amine molecule that removes H⁺.
- A quaternary salt drawn with an N–H bond.
- Calling the acyl chloride reaction substitution.
- HCl given as a product of an anhydride.
