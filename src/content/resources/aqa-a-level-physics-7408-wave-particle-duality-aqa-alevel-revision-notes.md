## Equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| c = 1/√(μ₀ε₀) | Maxwell's speed of EM waves in a vacuum |
| c = fλ | Hertz: speed of radio waves from frequency and wavelength |
| t = 1/(2Nf); c = 2d/t | Fizeau's toothed wheel (N teeth, f rev s⁻¹, mirror at d) |
| E = hf | Planck's quanta; Einstein's photons |
| hf = φ + E_k(max) | Photoelectric equation |
| λ = h/p | de Broglie |
| λ = h/√(2meV) | Electron accelerated through V |
| V = h²/(2meλ²) | Anode voltage for a chosen wavelength |

## Timeline of the argument

| Stage | Model of light (or matter) | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Newton vs Huygens | Particles preferred | Reflection, refraction, sharp shadows; Newton's authority |
| Young | Waves | Interference fringes from two slits |
| Maxwell, Hertz, Fizeau | Light is an EM wave | Predicted c matches measured speed; radio waves behave like light |
| Planck, Einstein | Light also has particle nature | Black-body spectrum; photoelectric effect |
| de Broglie | Matter has wave nature | Electron diffraction |
| Electron microscopes | Using electron waves | TEM imaging; STM tunnelling |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corpuscle | Newton's particle of light |
| Secondary wavelets | Huygens' construction for the next wavefront |
| Coherent sources | Constant phase difference, same frequency |
| Permittivity of free space ε₀ | Relates to electric field strength due to a charged object in free space |
| Permeability of free space μ₀ | Relates to magnetic flux density due to a current-carrying wire in free space |
| Ultraviolet catastrophe | Classical prediction of unlimited intensity at short wavelengths |
| Quantum | Discrete packet of energy hf |
| Threshold frequency | Lowest frequency that releases photoelectrons |
| Quantum tunnelling | Electrons crossing a gap they could not cross classically |

## Newton vs Huygens

- Refraction: Newton, light **faster** in water; Huygens, **slower** in water.
- Newton preferred: reputation; simple explanation of reflection, refraction and sharp shadows; speed in water could not yet be measured.
- Settled for waves when light was measured to be slower in water.

## Young

- Fringes = superposition of coherent waves: in phase → bright; antiphase → dark.
- Interference cannot be explained by corpuscles.
- Acceptance delayed by Newton's authority. No calculations needed in this section.

## Photoelectricity: wave theory vs observation

| Wave theory predicted | Observed |
|---|---|
| Any frequency works if intense enough | Threshold frequency |
| Delay with weak light | Instant emission |
| Brighter light → faster electrons | E_k(max) depends on frequency only; intensity sets number |

Einstein: one photon, one electron; light has a particle nature.

## Electron diffraction and microscopes

- Thin crystal or graphite → rings on screen: electrons behave as waves.
- Higher p.d. → higher speed and momentum → shorter λ → **smaller rings**.
- TEM: filament, anode, magnetic condenser lens, thin sample, magnetic objective and projector lenses, fluorescent screen. Limits: sample thickness (slows electrons, spreads λ), aberrations.
- STM: sharp tip about 1 nm above a conducting surface; tunnelling current very sensitive to gap; constant-height or constant-current mode.

## Exam traps

- Newton predicted light **faster** in water, not slower.
- Young's experiment supports **waves**; the photoelectric effect supports **particles**.
- The ultraviolet catastrophe is a failure of **classical** theory, not of Planck's.
- Intensity changes the **number** of photoelectrons, not their maximum energy.
- Faster electrons give **smaller** rings.
- The STM depends on **tunnelling**, not a transmitted beam.

## Self-test

1. What did Newton's theory predict about the speed of light in water?
2. Give two reasons Newton's theory was preferred.
3. Why does Young's experiment support the wave theory?
4. Hertz's waves have frequency 750 MHz. What is the spacing of nodes in the stationary wave?
5. A Fizeau wheel has 600 teeth and the mirror is 6.0 km away. At what rotation frequency is the returning light first blocked? (c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹)
6. What was the ultraviolet catastrophe?
7. What did Planck assume to solve it?
8. Give one photoelectric observation that wave theory cannot explain.
9. Calculate the de Broglie wavelength of an electron accelerated through 300 V.
10. What happens to the rings in electron diffraction when the accelerating p.d. increases?
11. Why must TEM samples be very thin?
12. What physical effect does the STM rely on?

### Answers

1. **Faster** than in air (corpuscles attracted into the denser medium).
2. Any two: Newton's **reputation**; it explained **reflection, refraction and sharp shadows** simply; the speed of light in water **could not be measured** yet.
3. **Interference** fringes come from superposition of waves; particles cannot produce them.
4. λ = 3.0 × 10⁸/750 × 10⁶ = 0.40 m; nodes are λ/2 apart: **0.20 m**.
5. t = 2d/c = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ s; f = 1/(2Nt) = **21 rev s⁻¹**.
6. Classical theory predicted black-body intensity rising **without limit** as wavelength decreases.
7. Energy is emitted in **quanta** of E = hf.
8. Any one: **threshold frequency**; **instant** emission; E_k(max) **independent of intensity**.
9. λ = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴/√(2 × 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 300) = **7.1 × 10⁻¹¹ m**.
10. They become **smaller** (shorter wavelength, less diffraction).
11. Thick samples **slow the electrons**, increasing and spreading their wavelengths, which blurs the image.
12. **Quantum tunnelling** of electrons across the gap between tip and surface.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did the measured speed of light matter so much?**
Fizeau's direct measurement of c agreed with the speed Maxwell predicted from μ₀ and ε₀ for electromagnetic waves. This agreement supported the conclusion that light is an electromagnetic wave.

**What does wave-particle duality mean?**
Light, long treated as a wave, shows particle behaviour in the photoelectric effect, while electrons, treated as particles, show wave behaviour in diffraction. Both light and matter need both models.
