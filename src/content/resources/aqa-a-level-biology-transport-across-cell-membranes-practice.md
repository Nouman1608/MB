specification (A-level 7402), for first teaching 2015, exams June 2016 onwards. The section is AS content.
Questions 8 to 11 are based on required practicals 3 and 4 and use MS 3.2 and MS 3.4 (plotting data and using an
intercept). Every answer shows the working and the marking points.

## How should you use these questions?

Answer each question fully before checking. In osmosis answers, always use the term water potential and say which
value is higher or lower. In practical questions, name the measuring instrument and the controlled variables.

## Questions

**1.** Describe the fluid-mosaic model of membrane structure. **[4]**

**2.** Explain the role of cholesterol in cell membranes. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why oxygen can cross a membrane by simple diffusion, but glucose cannot. **[2]**

**4.** Compare facilitated diffusion and active transport. **[3]**

**5.** Three plant cells are arranged in a row: cell X (-450 kPa), cell Y (-620 kPa) and cell Z (-380 kPa).

**(a)** Which cell has the highest water potential? **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the net movement of water between the cells. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how sodium ions and glucose are absorbed by epithelial cells in the mammalian ileum, and how glucose
reaches the blood. **[5]**

**7.** Epithelial cells of the ileum have microvilli and many mitochondria. Explain how each adaptation increases
the rate of absorption. **[3]**

**8.** A student needs 20 cm cubed of each of these sucrose solutions, made from a 1.0 mol dm-3 stock solution:
0.25, 0.50 and 0.75 mol dm-3. Give the volumes of stock and of distilled water for each. **[2]**

**9.** The student placed potato cylinders in sucrose solutions for 24 hours.

| Sucrose (mol dm-3) | Initial mass (g) | Final mass (g) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.60 | 1.84 |
| 0.2 | 1.55 | 1.67 |
| 0.4 | 1.62 | 1.58 |
| 0.6 | 1.58 | 1.45 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mass for the 0.0 and 0.4 mol dm-3 cylinders. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why percentage change in mass is calculated rather than change in mass. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the student would use a graph of these results, and a calibration curve, to find the water
potential of the potato tissue. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why the cylinders were blotted before weighing. **[1]**

**10.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on the permeability of beetroot cell membranes, measuring
the absorbance of the surrounding water with a colorimeter.

**(a)** Explain why the beetroot pieces were washed before the experiment. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** variables that should be kept the same. **[2]**
**(c)** Absorbance rose slowly from 20 to 50 degrees Celsius and sharply above 50 degrees Celsius. Explain these
results. **[3]**

**11.** Suggest why the rate of glucose uptake by facilitated diffusion stops rising at high glucose
concentrations, although the rate of oxygen uptake by simple diffusion keeps rising. **[2]**

**12.** Red blood cells were placed in a solution with a water potential lower than that of the cells. Describe and
explain what happens to them. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four [1 each]: phospholipid bilayer with hydrophilic heads facing outwards and hydrophobic tails inwards;
proteins scattered through the bilayer (mosaic), some spanning it (channel / carrier proteins); phospholipids and
proteins can move sideways (fluid); glycoproteins and glycolipids with carbohydrate chains on the outer surface;
cholesterol between phospholipids.

**2.** It restricts the movement of other molecules in the membrane (makes it less fluid / more stable) [1].

**3.** Oxygen is small and non-polar, so it passes through the hydrophobic core of the bilayer [1]. Glucose is polar
/ water-soluble (and larger), so it cannot pass through the hydrophobic tails and needs a carrier protein [1].

**4.** Both use carrier proteins [1]. Facilitated diffusion is down a concentration gradient and is passive (no
ATP), whereas active transport is against a concentration gradient [1] and uses energy from the hydrolysis of ATP
[1].

**5. (a)** Cell Z (-380 kPa) [1].
**(b)** Water moves from Z to X and from Z to Y [1], and from X to Y; Y has the lowest (most negative) water
potential, so it gains water from both [1].

**6.** Sodium ions are actively transported out of the epithelial cell into the blood [1], using ATP [1], keeping a
low concentration of sodium ions inside the cell [1]. Sodium ions diffuse into the cell from the lumen through a
co-transporter protein, carrying glucose with them against glucose's concentration gradient [1]. Glucose moves out
of the cell into the blood by facilitated diffusion [1].

**7.** Microvilli increase the surface area of the cell-surface membrane [1], providing more space for carrier and
co-transporter proteins, so more absorption at once [1]. Many mitochondria release ATP by respiration for active
transport of sodium ions [1].

**8.** [2 marks for all three correct; 1 mark for two]
- 0.25: 5 cm cubed stock + 15 cm cubed water.
- 0.50: 10 cm cubed stock + 10 cm cubed water.
- 0.75: 15 cm cubed stock + 5 cm cubed water.

**9. (a)** 0.0: (1.84 - 1.60) / 1.60 x 100 = **+15%** [1]. 0.4: (1.58 - 1.62) / 1.62 x 100 = **-2.5%** (-2.47) [1].
**(b)** The cylinders had different starting masses, so percentage change allows a fair comparison [1].
**(c)** Plot percentage change in mass against sucrose concentration and draw a line of best fit [1]; read the
concentration where the line crosses zero change (where there is no net osmosis) [1]; use the calibration curve to
convert this concentration to a water potential, which equals that of the tissue [1].
**(d)** To remove surface solution, which would add to the mass and is not water inside the tissue [1].

**10. (a)** To remove pigment released from cells damaged when cutting [1].
**(b)** Any two [1 each]: size / surface area of the beetroot pieces; volume of water; time in the water bath;
source of beetroot.
**(c)** Between 20 and 50 degrees Celsius, phospholipids gain kinetic energy and move more, so the membrane becomes
more permeable and some pigment leaks [1]. Above 50 degrees Celsius membrane proteins denature [1], leaving gaps
in the membrane, so much more pigment leaks out and absorbance rises sharply [1].

**11.** Facilitated diffusion depends on a limited number of carrier proteins, which become saturated [1]. Oxygen
diffuses through the phospholipid bilayer, so the rate depends only on the concentration gradient [1].

**12.** Water leaves the cells by osmosis, from higher to lower water potential [1], so the cells shrink / become
crenated [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "concentration of water" instead of water potential.
- Reading -620 kPa as higher than -450 kPa.
- Saying glucose is co-transported out of the cell into the blood (it leaves by facilitated diffusion).
- Leaving out ATP hydrolysis when explaining active transport.
- Missing the sign of a percentage change.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the names of the transport proteins in the ileum?**
No. The specification only requires the sequence: active transport of sodium out, co-transport of sodium and glucose
in, facilitated diffusion of glucose into the blood.

**What does the calibration curve add in practical 3?**
The graph gives the sucrose concentration at which there is no change in mass. The calibration curve converts that
concentration into a water potential in kPa.

**Can I use ethanol instead of temperature in practical 4?**
Yes. The specification says "a named variable". Ethanol concentration is a common choice; it dissolves membrane
lipids.
