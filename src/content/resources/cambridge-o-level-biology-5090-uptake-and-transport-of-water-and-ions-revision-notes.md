## Root hair cell: feature and function

| Feature | Function |
|---|---|
| Long, thin root hair | Large surface area for uptake of water and ions |
| Thin wall and membrane | Short distance for diffusion and osmosis |
| Sugars and ions in the cell sap | Lower water potential than the soil, so water enters by osmosis |
| Many mitochondria | Energy from aerobic respiration for active transport of ions |
| No chloroplasts | Underground, no light, no photosynthesis |

## How substances enter

| Substance | Process | Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Water | Osmosis | Soil water has a higher water potential than the cell sap |
| Mineral ions | Active transport | Soil concentration lower than inside the cell; uses energy from respiration |
| Mineral ions | Diffusion | If the soil concentration is higher than inside the cell |

## The pathway of water

1. **Root hair cell**: water enters by osmosis.
2. **Root cortex cells**: water passes from cell to cell down a water potential gradient.
3. **Xylem**: water enters the xylem in the root and travels up the stem to the leaf.
4. **Mesophyll cells**: water leaves the xylem and enters the mesophyll cells.
5. Some water evaporates from the mesophyll cell surfaces into the air spaces and leaves through the stomata (transpiration).

Memory line: **root hair, cortex, xylem, mesophyll**.

## Water potential gradient

- Water moves from a **higher** water potential to a **lower** water potential.
- A more concentrated cell sap means a lower water potential.
- Water therefore moves into the root hair, then into cells with progressively more concentrated sap.
- If the soil water had a lower water potential than the root hair cell (for example very salty soil), water would leave the plant.

## Stain practical in steps

1. Cut the base of a leafy stem **under water**.
2. Stand it in water containing stain (red ink or eosin).
3. Leave in a warm, bright place for a set time.
4. Cut a thin transverse section and a longitudinal section.
5. Examine with a hand lens or microscope.

**Result:** only the xylem is stained, so the xylem carries the water. Leaf veins are also stained.

**Calculating rate:** rate = distance divided by time. Example: 45 mm in 30 minutes = 1.5 mm per minute = 90 mm per hour.

**Control:** a shoot in unstained water; keep light, temperature and time the same.

## When uptake goes wrong

| Situation | Effect | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Waterlogged soil | Poor ion uptake | Little oxygen, so less aerobic respiration and less energy for active transport |
| Very salty soil | Plant wilts | Soil water has a lower water potential than the cell, so water leaves |
| Damaged roots | Less uptake | Fewer root hairs, so smaller surface area |
| Dry soil | Wilting | Less water available than the leaves lose |

## Short model answers

**Explain how a root hair cell is adapted for absorbing water.** It has a long, thin extension that gives a large surface area. It has a thin cell wall, so water has a short distance to travel. Its cell sap is concentrated, so its water potential is lower than the soil and water enters by osmosis.

**Explain why root hair cells have many mitochondria.** The mitochondria release energy by aerobic respiration, which is needed for the active transport of mineral ions into the cell.

**Outline the pathway taken by water from the soil to a leaf.** Water enters a root hair cell, passes through the root cortex cells, enters the xylem, travels up the stem to the leaf, and leaves the xylem to enter the mesophyll cells.

**Describe an experiment to show which tissue carries water in a stem.** Stand a cut shoot in coloured water, leave it, then cut sections. The xylem is stained, so it carries water.

## Exam traps

- Water is absorbed by **osmosis**; ions by **active transport** or diffusion.
- Give a reason with every feature ("large surface area, so more can be absorbed").
- The pathway order is root hair, cortex, xylem, mesophyll.
- Water is carried in the **xylem**, not the phloem.
- In the practical, cut the stem **under water** so air does not block the xylem.
- Root hair cells have **no chloroplasts**.
- Do not say that root hairs "suck up" water.

## Self-test

1. Name the cell that absorbs water from the soil.
2. Give two adaptations of this cell.
3. By what process does water enter the cell?
4. Why does the cell contain many mitochondria?
5. Put these in order: xylem, mesophyll cells, root cortex cells, root hair cell.
6. In which direction does water move, in terms of water potential?
7. State one thing the cell sap contains that lowers its water potential.
8. What would happen to water uptake in a very salty soil?
9. Why is the stem cut under water in the stain experiment?
10. Which tissue is stained in the stem?
11. A stain moves 36 mm in 24 minutes. What is the rate in mm per minute?
12. Give one control for the stain experiment.

### Answers

1. The root hair cell.
2. Any two: long thin extension (large surface area); thin cell wall; concentrated cell sap; many mitochondria.
3. Osmosis.
4. They release energy by aerobic respiration for active transport of ions.
5. Root hair cell, root cortex cells, xylem, mesophyll cells.
6. From a higher water potential to a lower water potential.
7. Sugars and ions (dissolved substances).
8. Uptake would fall, and water could even leave the root, because the soil water would have a lower water potential than the cell.
9. So that air does not enter and block the xylem vessels.
10. The xylem.
11. 36 divided by 24 = 1.5 mm per minute.
12. A shoot in unstained water, with the same light, temperature and time.
