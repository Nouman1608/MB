These questions are for **section 11.1 Video editing** (Topic 11, AS Level) of Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology (9626), 2025–2027. Calculators are not allowed in the theory paper, so the sums are written to be done by hand. All organisations are invented.

## Section A

**1.** Define the term aspect ratio. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** reasons for trimming a video clip. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the difference between subtitles and captions. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why credits at the end of a video should include source information. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** ways of making text on a video easier to read. **[2]**

**6.** A 90-second clip is played at 150% speed. State the new length of the clip and give **one** reason for editing speed in this way. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Harbour Lane Studio wants to use a photograph that is 3200 pixels wide and 2400 pixels high in a video whose frames are 1280 pixels wide and 720 pixels high.

**(a)** Calculate the height the photograph must be cropped to, if its full width is kept. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** State the scale factor used to resize the cropped photograph to the frame size. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the photograph should be cropped before it is resized. **[2]**

**8.** A video is encoded at 4 megabits per second and lasts 90 seconds.

**(a)** Calculate the size of the file in megabytes. Show your working. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the shortest time to download the file on a connection of 12 megabits per second. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why uncompressed video is rarely stored or streamed. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how lossy compression affects the quality of a video. **[3]**

**11.** Pinegate Learning will release the same lesson video on a DVD, on its website and for use on mobile phones. Explain how the resolution and compression should differ for **each** medium. **[6]**

**12.** A school sports club must choose between MP4 and WMV for a match highlights video that will be viewed on both school computers and pupils' phones. Evaluate the two formats and recommend one. **[6]**

**13.** Explain **three** reasons why a film maker would add fading effects and transitions when joining clips. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The ratio of the width of the frame to its height, such as 16:9 [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any two from: removes unwanted footage (such as the start before a speaker begins) [1]; keeps the video focused for the audience [1]; reduces the length and file size [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Subtitles show the words that are spoken, usually as a translation or a copy of the speech [1]. Captions label or describe what is on screen, such as a speaker's name or a sound effect [1]. **[2]**

**4.** To acknowledge the people who created the material used [1], and to show where music, images or clips came from so that copyright and licence terms are respected [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: large clear font [1]; strong contrast between the text and the background [1]; short lines of text [1]; leaving text on screen long enough to be read [1]; keeping text inside the safe area [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 90 ÷ 1.5 = 60 seconds [1]. Reason, for example: to shorten a slow process for the viewer, as in a time-lapse [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Cropped height = 3200 × 9 ÷ 16 [1] = 1800 pixels [1].

**(b)** 1280 ÷ 3200 = 0.4 [1].

**(c)** The photograph is 4:3 and the frame is 16:9 [1]. Resizing without cropping would stretch the picture, distorting people and objects [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Total bits = 4 × 90 = 360 megabits [1]. Divide by 8 to convert bits to bytes [1]: 360 ÷ 8 = 45 MB [1].

**(b)** 360 ÷ 12 [1] = 30 seconds [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Each frame holds a lot of data because every pixel stores its colour [1]. At 25 or 30 frames per second the data rate is very high, so files are huge [1]. They would fill storage quickly and cannot be streamed over ordinary internet connections without long delays [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Lossy compression permanently removes information that is least noticeable [1]. The more it is compressed, the more detail is lost, giving blocky or blurred areas and less accurate colour [1]. The file is much smaller, but the original cannot be restored [1]. **[3]**

**11.** DVD: standard definition resolution that the players expect [1], compressed so that the whole video fits on the disc [1]. Website: a higher resolution such as 1280 × 720 or 1920 × 1080 [1], with a moderate bit rate so that it loads without long waits [1]. Mobile: a lower resolution such as 640 × 360 [1], with a low bit rate because the screen is small and it saves data allowance and storage [1]. **[6]**

**12.** MP4: plays on almost all computers and phones [1]; gives good quality in a small file [1]. Disadvantage: lossy compression, so some detail is lost [1]. WMV: small files and plays on Windows computers [1]. Disadvantage: limited support on many phones [1]. Recommendation: MP4, because the video must play on both school computers and phones [1]. **[6]**

**13.** Any three from: make scene changes smooth rather than abrupt [1]; fading in or out marks the start and end of the video [1]; transitions show a change of time or place [1]; make the final video look more professional [1]; help the audience follow the story [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a dimension without showing the working, in a calculation question.
- Forgetting to divide by 8 when changing bits to bytes.
- Saying lossy compression "reduces the quality" without saying what is lost and why the file is smaller.
- Recommending a format with no link to the devices in the scenario.
- Describing compression in general terms for all three media instead of matching each to its screen and connection.
