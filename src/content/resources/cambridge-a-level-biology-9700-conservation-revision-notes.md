## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Extinct | No living individuals of the species remain |
| Endangered | At high risk of extinction in the wild |
| Conservation in place (in situ) | Protecting a species in its natural habitat, e.g. national or marine park |
| Conservation elsewhere (ex situ) | Keeping organisms or genetic material away from the natural habitat, e.g. zoo, seed bank |
| Captive breeding | Breeding endangered animals in zoos using records to avoid inbreeding |
| Frozen zoo | Store of sperm, eggs, embryos and tissue in liquid nitrogen |
| Seed bank | Store of dried seeds kept at low temperature |
| IVF | In vitro fertilisation: eggs fertilised by sperm outside the body |
| Embryo transfer | Placing an embryo into the uterus of a female |
| Surrogacy | A female carries an embryo that is not genetically her own |
| Alien species | Introduced outside its natural range |
| Invasive species | Alien species that spreads and causes harm |

## 18.3.1 Causes of extinction

| Cause | How it reduces numbers |
|---|---|
| Climate change | Habitat no longer suitable; species cannot adapt or move fast enough; food timing mismatched |
| Competition | Another species takes food, space or light |
| Hunting by humans | Adults removed faster than replaced, especially slow breeders |
| Habitat degradation and loss | Less food, shelter and breeding sites; fragmentation into small isolated populations |

**Small-population chain:** low genetic diversity → genetic drift loses alleles → inbreeding → harmful recessives expressed → less able to survive disease or change → extinction risk rises.

## 18.3.2 Reasons to maintain biodiversity

- **Ethical**: right to exist; human responsibility.
- **Ecosystem services**: pollination, nutrient cycling, water purification, soil, flood control.
- **Ecological**: stable food webs; loss of one species affects others.
- **Resources**: food, materials, medicines, crop-relative alleles (e.g. disease resistance).
- **Economic and social**: ecotourism, recreation, aesthetic and cultural value.

## 18.3.3 Conservation methods

| Method | Role | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| National / marine parks | Protect whole habitats; restrict hunting, fishing, logging, building | Costly to enforce; may be too small; conflict with local people |
| Zoos | Captive breeding with studbooks; reintroduction; research; education | Small founder group; loss of natural behaviour |
| Botanic gardens | Grow and propagate rare plants; reintroduction; research | Limited space; small genetic sample |
| Seed banks | Dried seeds at about −20 °C; viability tested; regrown when needed | Some seeds cannot survive drying or freezing |
| Frozen zoos | Gametes, embryos, tissue in liquid nitrogen (about −196 °C) | Needs assisted reproduction to use |

## 18.3.4 Assisted reproduction (mammals)

**Method in steps**

1. Hormones to stimulate many follicles in the female.
2. Collect eggs.
3. Collect sperm (or thaw frozen sperm).
4. **IVF**: fertilise in a dish.
5. Culture embryos for a few days.
6. **Embryo transfer** into a hormone-prepared uterus (or freeze embryos).
7. **Surrogacy**: carried by another female, same or closely related species.

**Why:** more offspring per valuable female; breed animals that will not or cannot mate; add genetic diversity using stored gametes.

## 18.3.5 Invasive alien species: reasons to control

- Compete with native species.
- Prey on native species.
- Carry disease to native species.
- Poison native predators.
- Change habitats.
- Have no natural predators or pathogens, so numbers rise.
- Cause economic damage.

| Example | Harm |
|---|---|
| Grey squirrel (Britain) | Out-competes red squirrels; carries squirrelpox virus |
| Cane toad (Australia) | Toxic to native predators that eat it |
| Nile perch (Lake Victoria) | Preyed on native cichlid fish, contributing to many losses |
| Water hyacinth | Mats block light and reduce oxygen in water |

## 18.3.6 IUCN and CITES

- **IUCN**: assesses conservation status → **Red List** (Least Concern, Near Threatened, Vulnerable, Endangered, Critically Endangered, Extinct in the Wild, Extinct). Informs governments and priorities.
- **CITES**: international agreement controlling **trade**. Appendix I: commercial trade banned. Appendix II: permits needed. Appendix III: listed by one country asking for help.

## Exam traps

- Zoos do not protect habitats; parks do.
- IVF is not cloning.
- A surrogate does not provide the egg.
- IUCN does not make laws; CITES controls trade between countries, not hunting within one.
- "Introduced" is not the same as "invasive".
- "Hunting" answers need a mechanism: adults removed faster than offspring replace them.

6. What is stored in a frozen zoo, and at what temperature?
7. List the steps of IVF and embryo transfer.
8. Why might a surrogate of a related species be used?

10. What does the IUCN publish?

### Answers

1. Climate change, competition, hunting by humans, degradation and loss of habitats.
2. Small isolated populations have low genetic diversity, more inbreeding and drift, and can be wiped out by one event.
3. Any three: ethical; ecosystem services; ecological stability; resources such as food and medicines; economic and aesthetic.
4. It protects the whole habitat and all interacting species in place, and animals keep natural behaviour.
5. To lower water content so seeds survive low temperature, metabolism slows and they stay viable for longer.
6. Sperm, eggs, embryos and tissue samples, in liquid nitrogen at about −196 °C.
7. Hormones → collect eggs → collect sperm → fertilise in vitro → culture embryos → place in uterus of a prepared female.
8. Few females of the endangered species are available, so a more common relative carries the pregnancy.
9. Any three: competition; predation; disease; habitat change; no natural predators; economic harm.
10. The **Red List** of Threatened Species.
11. Commercial international trade in the species is banned.
12. They record ancestry so that matings maximise genetic diversity and avoid inbreeding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming conservation methods without their role.
- Describing assisted reproduction steps out of order.
- Mixing up the purposes of IUCN and CITES.
- Generic "it is bad for the environment" points about invasive species.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which assisted reproduction methods are on the 9700 syllabus?**
Only IVF, embryo transfer and surrogacy, for endangered mammals.

**What is the difference between IUCN and CITES?**
The IUCN assesses how threatened species are and publishes the Red List. CITES is an agreement between governments that controls international trade in endangered species and their products.
