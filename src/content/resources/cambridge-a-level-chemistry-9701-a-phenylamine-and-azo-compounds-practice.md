## Section A

**1.** Name the electrophile in the nitration of benzene and state the temperature range used.
**[2]**

**2.** Give the formula of the nitrogen-containing organic species present at the end of the tin and
concentrated HCl reduction of nitrobenzene, before any alkali is added. **[1]**

**3.** State the two reagents used to make nitrous acid, HNO₂, in the reaction flask. **[1]**

**4.** Give the formula of the benzenediazonium ion and the formula of the azo group. **[2]**

**5.** State one use of azo compounds. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** 12.3 g of nitrobenzene is reduced, and after the addition of NaOH(aq) and purification, 7.44
g of phenylamine is obtained.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage yield of phenylamine. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of [H] needed to reduce 12.3 g of nitrobenzene completely.
**[1]**

**7.** 0.930 g of phenylamine is shaken with excess bromine water at room temperature.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of organic product. **[2]**

**(b)** State the number of moles of Br₂ that react. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why benzene does not react with bromine water under the same conditions. **[2]**

**8.** Outline a route to the azo dye 4-hydroxyazobenzene, C₆H₅N=NC₆H₄OH, in which benzene is the
only aromatic starting material and phenol is made during the route. Give reagents and conditions
for each step. **[5]**

**9.** A student writes: "Phenylamine is a weaker base than ammonia because the large benzene ring
gets in the way of the proton."

**(a)** Explain why the student's reason is not accepted. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the correct explanation. **[2]**

**10.** An azo dye has the structure HOC₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄Cl, with the chlorine atom at the 4-position of
its ring.

**(a)** Name the aromatic amine and the coupling partner used to make it. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the conditions for the diazotisation step and for the coupling step. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the coupling partner is dissolved in NaOH(aq). **[1]**

**11.** A student makes a solution of benzenediazonium chloride but leaves it on the bench, where it
warms to room temperature and above.

**(a)** State what the student would observe. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction that occurs. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the yield of azo dye from this solution would be lower. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why 4-hydroxyazobenzene is coloured, but phenylamine and phenol are colourless.
**[2]**

## Answers

**1.** NO₂⁺, the nitronium (nitryl) ion [1]; between 25 °C and 60 °C [1].

**2.** C₆H₅NH₃⁺ (phenylammonium ion, accept as its chloride salt) [1].

**3.** Sodium nitrite (NaNO₂) and dilute hydrochloric acid (both needed) [1].

**4.** C₆H₅N₂⁺ (C₆H₅–N≡N⁺) [1]; –N=N– [1].

**5.** Dyes (colouring agents) [1].

**6. (a)** n(C₆H₅NO₂) = 12.3 / 123.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass of C₆H₅NH₂ = 0.100 × 93.0
= 9.30 g [1]; yield = 7.44 / 9.30 × 100 = 80.0% [1].

**(b)** 6 × 0.100 = 0.600 mol [H] [1].

**7. (a)** n(C₆H₅NH₂) = 0.930 / 93.0 = 0.0100 mol; Mᵣ(C₆H₂Br₃NH₂) = 329.7 [1]; mass = 0.0100 ×
329.7 = 3.30 g [1].

**(b)** 0.0300 mol [1].

**(c)** Benzene's delocalised π electrons are not increased by any donating group, so its electron
density is too low to polarise Br₂ [1]; a halogen carrier such as AlBr₃ is needed to generate a
strong enough electrophile [1].

**8.** Any five of the following points [5]:

- Nitrate benzene with concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ at 25–60 °C to give nitrobenzene.
- Reduce with tin and concentrated HCl, heated, to give the phenylammonium ion.
- Add NaOH(aq) to release phenylamine.
- Treat phenylamine with NaNO₂ and dilute HCl below 10 °C to give benzenediazonium chloride.
- Warm part of this diazonium solution with water to make phenol (N₂ given off).
- Dissolve the phenol in NaOH(aq) and add the remaining cold diazonium salt solution to couple and
  form the dye.

**9. (a)** The proton bonds to the lone pair on nitrogen, which is outside the ring; basicity is
explained by electron availability, not by blocking [1].

**(b)** The nitrogen lone pair overlaps with the π system of the ring and is partly delocalised into
it [1], so it is less available to accept H⁺ than the lone pair in ammonia [1].

**10. (a)** 4-chlorophenylamine (diazotised) [1]; phenol [1].

**(b)** Diazotisation: NaNO₂ and dilute HCl, below 10 °C [1]; coupling: phenol in NaOH(aq), kept
cold [1].

**(c)** In alkali phenol forms the phenoxide ion, whose ring is more strongly activated, so the weak
diazonium electrophile can attack it [1].

**11. (a)** Bubbles of gas (nitrogen) [1].

**(b)** C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + HCl [1].

**(c)** The diazonium salt has decomposed, so less is available to couple with phenol [1].

**12.** In the azo compound the –N=N– group links the two rings into one extended delocalised system
[1]; this absorbs light in the visible region, while the smaller delocalised systems in phenylamine
and phenol absorb only outside the visible region (in the ultraviolet) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing dilute HCl, or omitting heat, for the tin reduction.
- Forgetting the NaOH(aq) step that releases the free amine.
- Using 1 mol of Br₂ per mole of phenylamine instead of 3.
- Warming the diazonium salt when the question asks for an azo dye, or keeping it cold when the
  question asks for phenol.
- Writing the azo group as –N≡N–.
- Explaining basicity by size or "steric hindrance" instead of delocalisation of the lone pair.
- Splitting an azo dye at the wrong bond when working backwards: always break the N=N link.
