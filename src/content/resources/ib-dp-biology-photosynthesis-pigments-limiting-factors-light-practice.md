## Questions

**1.** Write the word equation for photosynthesis and state the source of the oxygen released. **[2]**

**2.** A leaf extract was separated by paper chromatography. The solvent front moved 6.4 cm. Four spots were found: A (orange-yellow) at 6.1 cm, B (yellow) at 4.4 cm, C (blue-green) at 3.3 cm and D (yellow-green) at 2.2 cm. In the same system, reference Rf values are carotene 0.95, chlorophyll a 0.52 and chlorophyll b 0.34.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of B. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify pigment D, using both colour and Rf. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the start line was drawn in pencil. **[1]**

**3.** The absorbance of a pigment extract was measured at several wavelengths.

| Wavelength / nm | 400 | 430 | 470 | 520 | 560 | 620 | 660 | 700 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.55 | 0.90 | 0.35 | 0.05 | 0.04 | 0.25 | 0.70 | 0.10 |

**(a)** State the wavelength of greatest absorption. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why leaves containing this pigment look green. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline what happens inside a pigment molecule when it absorbs a photon. **[1]**

**4.** A leaf was sealed in a chamber and lit for 20 minutes with light of one wavelength at a time. The carbon dioxide concentration started at 400 ppm each time.

| Wavelength / nm | 440 | 520 | 600 | 660 | 720 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Final concentration / ppm | 310 | 372 | 350 | 318 | 396 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of carbon dioxide uptake at 520 nm, in ppm per minute. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate at 660 nm as a percentage of that at 440 nm (nearest whole number). **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the action spectrum these data would give. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why photosynthesis still occurs at 520 nm. **[1]**

**5.** Compare absorption spectra and action spectra. **[3]**

**6.** A student plans a bubble-counting experiment on an aquatic plant, to see how light intensity affects its photosynthesis rate.

**(a)** State a hypothesis. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the independent and the dependent variable. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify two variables to control and state how each is controlled. **[2]**
**(d)** State how light intensity can be varied. **[1]**

**7.** The table shows bubbles released per minute by an aquatic plant at constant light intensity and carbon dioxide concentration.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bubbles per minute | 4 | 9 | 17 | 21 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in rate as the temperature goes from 20 °C to 30 °C. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the change in rate from 40 °C to 50 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** At 10 °C, raising the light intensity gave almost no increase in rate. Explain this. **[2]**

**8.** A field crop was grown in open-air plots at ambient carbon dioxide and in plots enriched using FACE. Mean grain yield was 5.8 t ha⁻¹ in ambient plots and 6.5 t ha⁻¹ in enriched plots. The mean photosynthetic rate was 19.8 and 24.6 µmol m⁻² s⁻¹ respectively.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in grain yield. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in photosynthetic rate. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why the change in yield is smaller than the change in rate. **[1]**
**(d)** State one advantage of FACE over an enclosed greenhouse experiment. **[1]**
**(e)** State one limitation of using these results to predict future yields. **[1]**
**(f)** State one variable to control between plots. **[1]**

**9.** (HL only)

**(a)** Describe the structure of a photosystem. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the advantage of this structure compared with a single chlorophyll molecule. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only)

**(a)** State where photolysis occurs and name its three products. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how a proton gradient is formed and used to make ATP in a thylakoid. **[4]**
**(c)** Outline how NADP is reduced. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) In a chloroplast, 18 carbon dioxide molecules were fixed by the Calvin cycle. Assume each GP converted to TP uses 1 ATP and 1 reduced NADP, and each RuBP regenerated uses 1 ATP.

**(a)** State the number of GP molecules formed. **[1]**
**(b)** State how many TP molecules are used to regenerate RuBP. **[1]**
**(c)** State how many RuBP molecules are regenerated. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate how many glucose molecules could be made from the remaining TP. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the total ATP and reduced NADP used. **[2]**
**(f)** State the fraction of TP converted back to RuBP. **[1]**

**12.** (HL only) Algae photosynthesising steadily were moved to darkness. Relative amounts of RuBP and GP were measured.

| Time after darkness / s | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| RuBP | 100 | 62 | 35 | 20 |
| GP | 100 | 135 | 160 | 170 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in each compound by 20 seconds. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain these changes. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why removing carbon dioxide would stop photosystem II working. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen [1]; the oxygen comes from the splitting of water [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name reactants and products in full; saying the oxygen comes from carbon dioxide is a frequent error.

**2. (a)** 4.4 ÷ 6.4 = **0.69** [1]
**(b)** D has Rf 2.2 ÷ 6.4 = 0.34 [1], matching chlorophyll b, and it is yellow-green; so D is **chlorophyll b** [1].
**(c)** Pencil is insoluble in the solvent, so it stays put and gives no extra spots [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division and use both colour and Rf, as the question asks.

**3. (a)** **430 nm** [1]
**(b)** Absorbance is low at about 520 to 560 nm, so green light is not absorbed [1]; it is reflected or transmitted to the eye [1].
**(c)** An electron moves up to a higher energy level, becoming excited [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a wavelength with units, and link the green wavelengths to their low absorbance.

**4. (a)** (400 − 372) ÷ 20 = **1.4 ppm min⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Rate at 660 = (400 − 318) ÷ 20 = 4.1 [1]; rate at 440 = 90 ÷ 20 = 4.5; 4.1 ÷ 4.5 × 100 = **91%** [1]
**(c)** Two peaks, in blue-violet (440 nm, 4.5 ppm min⁻¹) and red (660 nm, 4.1) [1]; low rate in green (520 nm, 1.4) and almost none at 720 nm (0.2) [1].
**(d)** Accessory pigments absorb some green light and pass energy on [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from the table and name the regions of the spectrum.

**5.** Similarity: both show high values in blue-violet and red and low values in green [1]. Difference: an absorption spectrum records the light a pigment takes in [1], whereas an action spectrum shows the rate of photosynthesis against wavelength [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A difference needs both sides stated.

**6. (a)** If light intensity increases, the rate of photosynthesis (bubbles per minute) will increase, because more photons are absorbed [1].
**(b)** Independent: light intensity [1]. Dependent: bubbles per minute (rate of oxygen release) [1].
**(c)** Temperature, controlled by placing a water-filled tank between lamp and plant or using a water bath [1]; carbon dioxide concentration, controlled by using the same hydrogencarbonate solution [1].
**(d)** Change the distance between lamp and plant [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the method of control as well as the name of the variable.

**7. (a)** (17 − 9) ÷ 9 × 100 = **89%** (88.9) [1]
**(b)** Enzymes, including Rubisco, denature at high temperature [1]; the active site shape changes, so less carbon is fixed and the rate falls sharply [1].
**(c)** At 10 °C, enzyme activity is too low, so temperature is the limiting factor [1]; more light cannot raise the rate because the Calvin cycle cannot use the extra ATP and reduced NADP faster [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the limiting factor and say why more light has no effect.

**8. (a)** (6.5 − 5.8) ÷ 5.8 × 100 = **12.1%** [1]
**(b)** (24.6 − 19.8) ÷ 19.8 × 100 = **24.2%** [1]
**(c)** Other factors, such as mineral nutrients or water, limit growth, or carbon is used in respiration or in parts not harvested [1].
**(d)** Natural open-air conditions, so results apply better to real ecosystems [1].
**(e)** Other future changes, such as higher temperature, are not included in the experiment [1].
**(f)** Species or variety, soil type, watering or plot size [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change divides by the original value; name a specific controlled variable.

**9. (a)** A group of chlorophyll and accessory pigment molecules arranged in an array in a membrane [1]; a special chlorophyll at the centre is the reaction centre, where an excited electron is given off [1].
**(b)** Different pigments absorb different wavelengths, so more light is captured [1]; the energy is passed to the reaction centre, which a single molecule could not use to produce an emitted electron [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to both the range of wavelengths and the transfer to the reaction centre.

**10. (a)** In photosystem II, in the thylakoid space [1]; products are electrons, protons and oxygen [1].
**(b)** Excited electrons from photosystem II pass along carriers [1]; the energy released moves protons out of the stroma and into the space inside the thylakoid [1]; photolysis adds more protons, so the space has a high proton concentration [1]; protons then move down their gradient into the stroma through ATP synthase, joining ADP to phosphate to give ATP [1].
**(c)** Two electrons from photosystem I [1] and a hydrogen ion from the stroma join NADP, forming reduced NADP [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction of proton movement at each step.

**11. (a)** **36** GP [1]
**(b)** 36 TP are formed; 6 leave; so **30** TP regenerate RuBP [1]
**(c)** 30 TP give 18 RuBP (5 TP to 3 RuBP: 30 ÷ 5 × 3) [1]
**(d)** 6 TP ÷ 2 = **3 glucose** [1]
**(e)** ATP: 36 + 18 = **54** [1]; reduced NADP: **36** [1]
**(f)** **Five-sixths** (30 ÷ 36) [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show a carbon check: 18 × 5 + 18 = 108 carbons = 36 × 3.

**12. (a)** RuBP: (35 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = **−65%** [1]; GP: (160 − 100) ÷ 100 × 100 = **+60%** [1]
**(b)** In darkness neither ATP nor reduced NADP is produced [1]; GP cannot be converted to TP, so it accumulates [1]; RuBP is still used in fixation but not regenerated, so it falls [1].
**(c)** Without carbon dioxide, the Calvin cycle stops and reduced NADP is not used [1]; NADP is not regenerated, so electron carriers cannot pass on electrons [1]; photosystem II cannot emit electrons to the chain, so photolysis stops [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each effect step by step; "the cycle stops" alone does not explain photosystem II.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating that oxygen comes from carbon dioxide.
- Taking Rf distances from the paper edge, or giving Rf units.
- Describing a spectrum without values.
- Mixing up absorption and action spectra.
- Naming a controlled variable but not saying how it is controlled.
- Dividing by the final value in a percentage change.
- Placing photolysis or carbon fixation in the wrong part of the chloroplast.
