## 1.1 Integers: definitions

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Integer | Whole number: positive, negative or zero | −4, 0, 17 |
| Odd / even | Even divides exactly by 2; odd does not | 0 is even |
| Factor of n | Divides into n with no remainder | 6 is a factor of 54 |
| Multiple of n | In the n times table | 54 is a multiple of 6 |
| Prime | Exactly two factors: 1 and itself | 2, 3, 5, 7, 11, 13, … |
| Prime factor | A factor that is prime | prime factors of 54: 2, 3 |
| Common factor | Divides both numbers | 3 is a common factor of 12 and 21 |
| Common multiple | In both times tables | 42 is a common multiple of 6 and 7 |

**1 is not prime.** 2 is the only even prime.

## Directed numbers

```
add a negative       ->  subtract      6 + (-10) = -4
subtract a negative  ->  add          -2 - (-7)  = 5
same signs (x or /)      ->  positive
different signs (x or /) ->  negative
```

In context: a rise is +, a fall is −. The **difference** between two temperatures is higher minus lower. From −12 °C to 3 °C is a rise of 3 − (−12) = 15 degrees.

**Ordering:** on a number line, further left is smaller. −20 < −3 even though 20 > 3.

## Order of operations

```
1. Brackets
2. Powers (and roots)
3. Multiply and divide, LEFT to RIGHT
4. Add and subtract, LEFT to RIGHT
```

**Worked reminder.** 7 + 24 ÷ (5 − 9) = 7 + 24 ÷ (−4) = 7 + (−6) = **1**.

**Watch (−a)² against −a².** (−4)² = 16 but −4² = −(4²) = −16. On a calculator, type the bracket.

## Index laws (both tiers)

| Law | Rule | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Multiply, same base | aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ | 2⁶ × 2⁻⁴ = 2² |
| Divide, same base | aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ | 10² ÷ 10⁵ = 10⁻³ |
| Zero power | a⁰ = 1 | 13⁰ = 1 |
| Negative power | a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ | 6⁻² = 1/36 |

Only combine powers of the **same base**. With a negative index, watch the signs: 5³ ÷ 5⁻² = 5³⁻⁽⁻²⁾ = 5⁵.

## Prime factors, HCF and LCM

> **Method in steps: product of prime factors**
> 1. Divide by the smallest prime that goes in (2, then 3, then 5, …).
> 2. Repeat on the answer until you reach 1.
> 3. Write the primes in index form, smallest first.
> 4. Check by multiplying back.

```
600 / 2 = 300, / 2 = 150, / 2 = 75, / 3 = 25, / 5 = 5, / 5 = 1
600 = 2³ × 3 × 5²
```

> **Method in steps: HCF and LCM**
> 1. Write both numbers as products of prime powers.
> 2. HCF: primes common to both, **lower** power of each.
> 3. LCM: every prime in either, **higher** power of each.

**Worked reminder.** 90 = 2 × 3² × 5 and 126 = 2 × 3² × 7.

```
HCF = 2 × 3² = 18
LCM = 2 × 3² × 5 × 7 = 630
check: 18 × 630 = 11 340 = 90 × 126
```

A Venn diagram of the prime factors works too: the overlap multiplies to the HCF, and everything in the diagram multiplies to the LCM.

**Which one does the question want?**

| Wording in the question | Use |
|---|---|
| "greatest number of identical groups", "largest square tile", "cut into equal lengths, none wasted" | HCF |
| "next time together", "smallest number of packs so the totals match", "first number in both lists" | LCM |

After finding the HCF or LCM, reread the question. It often wants something you get from it: a clock time, a number of packs, or how many items go in each group.

**Roots from prime factors.** If every power in the prime factorisation is even, the number is a square. Halve each power to get the square root. If every power is a multiple of 3, the number is a cube. Divide each power by 3 to get the cube root.

```
3375 = 3³ × 5³   ->   ∛3375 = 3 × 5 = 15
```

## Surds (H)

A **surd** is an irrational root, such as √3 or √10. √36 is not a surd.

```
√a × √b = √(ab)        √a × √a = a        √a / √b = √(a/b)
√(m²k) = m√k           (b + √a)(b - √a) = b² - a
```

- **Simplify:** take out the largest square factor. √98 = √(49 × 2) = 7√2.
- **Add or subtract:** only like surds. 4√3 − √3 = 3√3.
- **Expand:** every term times every term; √a × √a = a.
- **Rationalise √a:** multiply top and bottom by √a. 10/√5 = 10√5/5 = 2√5.
- **Rationalise b ± √a:** multiply top and bottom by the conjugate, b ∓ √a.

## Fractional and negative powers (H)

```
a^(1/n)  = nth root of a
a^(m/n)  = (nth root of a)^m        root first
a^(-m/n) = 1 / a^(m/n)              flip
(a^m)^n  = a^(mn)                   both tiers (spec 2.1)
```

**Worked reminder.** 81^(3/4): fourth root of 81 is 3, and 3³ = **27**.

For a fraction raised to a negative power, flip the fraction first, then apply the positive power.

## Must-know distinctions

| Don't confuse | Difference |
|---|---|
| Factor and multiple | Factors of 12 are 12 or less (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 12); multiples are 12 or more (12, 24, 36, …) |
| HCF and LCM | HCF ≤ the smaller number; LCM ≥ the larger number |
| a⁻ⁿ and −aⁿ | 3⁻² = 1/9; −3² = −9 |
| a^(1/2) and a/2 | 16^(1/2) = 4; 16/2 = 8 |
| Prime factors and factors | Prime factors of 30 are 2, 3, 5; its factors are 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 10, 15, 30 |
| Surd and root | √5 is a surd; √25 = 5 is a root but not a surd |

## Quick self-test

1. Work out −14 − (−6) + 3.
2. Work out 40 − 3 × (2 − 6)².
3. Write down the two prime numbers between 50 and 60.
4. Write 1764 as a product of powers of its prime factors. Use your answer to find √1764.
5. Find the HCF and the LCM of 48 and 150.
6. Simplify 3⁴ × 3⁻⁶ ÷ 3⁻⁵ and evaluate it.
7. Work out ∛(−216).
8. (H) Simplify √12 + √75.
9. (H) Rationalise 3/(√7 − 2).
10. (H) Evaluate 64^(−2/3).
11. (H) Expand and simplify (3 − √2)².

### Answers

1. −14 + 6 + 3 = **−5**.
2. (−4)² = 16; 3 × 16 = 48; 40 − 48 = **−8**.
3. **53 and 59**.
4. 1764 = **2² × 3² × 7²**, so √1764 = 2 × 3 × 7 = **42** (halve each power).
5. 48 = 2⁴ × 3, 150 = 2 × 3 × 5². HCF = 2 × 3 = **6**. LCM = 2⁴ × 3 × 5² = **1200**.
6. Power: 4 + (−6) − (−5) = 3, so 3³ = **27**.
7. (−6)³ = −216, so **−6**.
8. 2√3 + 5√3 = **7√3**.
9. Multiply by (√7 + 2)/(√7 + 2): denominator 7 − 4 = 3, so 3(√7 + 2)/3 = **√7 + 2**.
10. 64^(1/3) = 4, 4² = 16, flip: **1/16**.
11. 9 − 6√2 + 2 = **11 − 6√2**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including 1 in a list of primes, or leaving out 2.
- Giving 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 × 5 × 5 when the question asks for a product of **powers** of prime factors.
- Using the higher powers for the HCF, or only the common primes for the LCM.
- Answering an LCM context question with the LCM itself when the question asks for a clock time or a number of packs.
- Working left to right across + and × together: 7 + 24 ÷ (−4) is 1, not −7.75.
- Writing 6⁻² = −36 or −12.
- Adding unlike surds: √12 + √75 is not √87.
- (H) Expanding (3 − √2)² as 9 + 2, missing the −6√2 term.
- (H) Rationalising with the same sign instead of the conjugate.
- (H) Applying the power before the root, producing huge numbers and arithmetic slips.
