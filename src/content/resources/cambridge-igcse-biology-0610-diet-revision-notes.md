Full teaching and worked examples are in the [diet study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet/); marked questions are in the [diet practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet-practice/). ## The three statements

1. Describe what is meant by a **balanced diet**.
2. State the principal dietary **sources** and describe the **importance** of carbohydrates, fats and oils, proteins, vitamins (**C and D only**), mineral ions (**calcium and iron only**), fibre (roughage) and water.
3. State the **causes** of scurvy and rickets.

## Balanced diet: the definition

> A diet containing **all** the nutrients **in the correct proportions** to maintain health.

Both halves are needed. "Eating a bit of everything" scores nothing. Needs vary: active people need more energy; growing children need relatively more protein.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Nutrient | A substance in food that the body uses for energy, growth, repair or keeping healthy |
| Balanced diet | All nutrients in the correct proportions |
| Fibre (roughage) | Plant material that is not digested; adds bulk to food in the gut |
| Haemoglobin | Red protein in red blood cells that carries oxygen; needs iron |
| Deficiency disease | A disease caused by a lack of a nutrient in the diet |
| Scurvy | Deficiency disease caused by lack of vitamin C |
| Rickets | Deficiency disease caused by lack of vitamin D (or calcium) |

## Sources and importance at a glance

| Nutrient | Two good sources | Importance in one line |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | bread, rice | main energy source (glucose for respiration) |
| Fats and oils | butter, cooking oil | energy source and store; insulation |
| Proteins | meat, beans | growth and repair; enzymes and antibodies |
| Vitamin C | oranges, fresh vegetables | healthy skin, gums and blood vessels; wound healing |
| Vitamin D | oily fish, egg yolk (and sunlight on skin) | absorbing calcium for bones and teeth |
| Calcium | milk, cheese | strong bones and teeth; blood clotting |
| Iron | red meat, spinach | making haemoglobin to carry oxygen |
| Fibre | wholemeal bread, vegetables | bulk for food to be pushed along the gut; prevents constipation |
| Water | drinks, fruit | solvent for reactions; transport in plasma; urine; cooling by sweat |

### Memory hooks

- "**C** for **C**uts and gums": vitamin C keeps gums and skin healthy; lack gives scurvy.
- "**D** for **D**airy partner": vitamin D helps the body absorb calcium from dairy foods; lack gives rickets.
- "Iron carries oxygen": iron → haemoglobin → oxygen.
- Fat has about twice the energy per gram of carbohydrate, which makes it a good store.

## Scurvy and rickets

| | Scurvy | Rickets |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | lack of vitamin C | lack of vitamin D (or calcium) |
| Signs | bleeding gums, slow-healing wounds | soft, weak bones; bowed legs in children |
| Prevent by eating | fresh fruit and vegetables | oily fish, eggs, dairy foods; time in sunlight |

Vitamin C is destroyed by heating, so a diet of only cooked or preserved food can lead to scurvy.

## Answer patterns

- **Explain a deficiency:** food missing → nutrient missing → disease or effect.
- **Suggest a change:** name a food rich in the missing nutrient.
- **Compare foods from data:** quote values with units, then give the biology.
- **Why does person X need more of nutrient Y?** Link Y's job to X's situation (growth, activity, blood loss).

## Exam traps

- Vitamins and mineral ions do not provide energy.
- Fibre is not digested or absorbed.
- Calcium is for bones; iron is for haemoglobin. Do not swap them.
- Rickets is vitamin D (or calcium), not vitamin C.
- Water counts as a nutrient group; do not leave it out of lists.
- Name foods precisely: "citrus fruit" is better than "fruit"; "meat" is better than "food".
- Protein's main role is growth and repair, not energy.

## Self-test

1. Describe what is meant by a balanced diet.
2. Name the nutrient group that is the body's main source of energy.
3. Give one importance of fats in the body other than energy.
4. State two uses of protein in the body.
5. Name the deficiency disease caused by a lack of vitamin C.
6. Explain why vitamin D is needed for strong bones.
7. Name one dietary source of iron and state why the body needs iron.
8. Fibre is not digested. Explain why it is still important in the diet.
9. State two reasons why the body needs water.
10. A child rarely goes outside and drinks no milk. Suggest which disease she is at risk of, and why.
11. A person eats only tinned and boiled food for many months. Suggest why he develops bleeding gums.
12. Why might an athlete in training need more carbohydrate than an office worker?

### Answers

1. A diet containing all the nutrients in the correct proportions to stay healthy.
2. Carbohydrates.
3. Insulation (under the skin), or a store of energy.
4. Growth and repair of cells; making enzymes or antibodies.
5. Scurvy.
6. Vitamin D is needed for the body to absorb calcium, and calcium strengthens bones.
7. Red meat, liver, beans or spinach; to make haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells.
8. It adds bulk, so the muscles of the gut wall can push food along, preventing constipation.
9. Any two: solvent for chemical reactions; transport in blood plasma; removing waste in urine; cooling by sweating.
10. Rickets: little sunlight means little vitamin D is made in her skin, and no milk means little calcium.
11. Heating destroys vitamin C, so his diet lacks vitamin C and he develops scurvy.
12. The athlete uses more energy, and carbohydrate supplies glucose for respiration to release that energy.
