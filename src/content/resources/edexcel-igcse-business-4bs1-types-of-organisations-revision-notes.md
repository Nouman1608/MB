Quick recall for section 1.2, Types of organisations, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [types of organisations study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-types-of-organisations/); exam-style questions are in the [types of organisations practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-types-of-organisations-practice/). The specification names each form without defining it, so meanings here are standard ones. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists

- 1.2.1 Sole trader, partnerships, limited companies (private and public), public corporations.
- 1.2.2 Risk, ownership and limited liability; reasons for and against public ownership; ownership, control, sources of finance, use of profits, stakeholders and shareholders; appropriateness of different forms.
- 1.2.3 Franchises, social enterprises, multinationals.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | Business owned by one person |
| Partnership | Business owned by two or more people who share profits |
| Private limited company | Company whose shares are not offered to the general public |
| Public limited company | Company whose shares can be bought by the general public |
| Public corporation | Business owned by the state |
| Unlimited liability | Owners can be made to pay business debts from personal assets |
| Limited liability | Shareholders can lose only the money they invested |
| Shareholder | Owner of shares in a company |
| Stakeholder | Any group with an interest in a business |
| Dividend | Share of profit paid to shareholders |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Franchisor / franchisee | Brand owner / operator who pays to use the brand |
| Social enterprise | Trades to earn income, with social or environmental objectives |
| Multinational | Operates in more than one country |

## Comparison grid

| | Sole trader | Partnership | Private limited | Public limited | Public corporation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Owners | One | Two or more | Chosen shareholders | Public shareholders | State |
| Liability | Unlimited | Unlimited | Limited | Limited | Not borne by individuals |
| Control | Owner | Partners | Directors | Directors | Government-appointed managers |
| Finance | Savings, loans | Partners' capital, loans | Private share sales, loans | Public share sales, loans | Government, revenue, borrowing |
| Profit | Owner | Partners | Dividends, retained | Dividends, retained | Kept or to government |

## Control and shares

- More than half the voting shares usually gives control.
- Issuing new shares raises finance but dilutes existing owners.
- Reminder: owners with 51,000 of 100,000 shares (51%) issue 20,000 more; they now hold 51,000 ÷ 120,000 = 42.5%.

## Public ownership

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Essential but unprofitable services provided | Less pressure to be efficient |
| Affordable prices | Taxpayers may cover losses |
| Public interest first | Political, not commercial, decisions |

## Franchise reminders

- Franchisee pays a start-up fee and continuing fees, often a percentage of revenue.
- Reminder: revenue 150,000, other costs 110,000, fee 6% = 9,000; profit 31,000.
- Franchisee gains: known brand, support, tested methods. Loses: fees, rules, less independence.
- Franchisor gains: fast growth funded by franchisees. Risks: reputation depends on others.

## Choosing a form: five questions

1. Finance needed?
2. Risk the owners will accept?
3. Control they want to keep?
4. Growth plans?
5. Purpose (profit or social)?

Then: decision, case fact linked to a feature, the weakness of the alternative.

## Paper 1 and Paper 2 angles

- Paper 1 (small business, up to 49 employees): sole trader or partnership against a private limited company; liability, control and finance for growth; becoming a franchisee.
- Paper 2 (large business, more than 250 employees): public limited companies, shareholders and directors, dividends against retained profit, franchising as a way to grow, operating as a multinational.

## Exam traps

- Public limited company and public corporation confused.
- "Limited liability means no risk."
- Partners given limited liability.
- Shareholders and stakeholders confused.
- A recommendation that ignores the owners' stated priority.
- Franchise answers written from the wrong side (franchisor instead of franchisee).
- Treating a social enterprise as a charity that does not trade.

## Self-test

1. Name the four main types of business ownership in 1.2.1.
2. Define unlimited liability.
3. Who controls a public limited company day to day?
4. Give one source of finance open to a public limited company but not a private one.
5. State one reason for public ownership.
6. State one reason against public ownership.
7. A family holds 45,000 of 80,000 shares. Calculate its percentage holding.
8. The company issues 20,000 new shares to outsiders. What is the family's new percentage?
9. A franchisee's revenue is 200,000; the fee is 4% of revenue. Calculate the fee.
10. Give one benefit of franchising to a franchisor.
11. Explain why every shareholder is a stakeholder.
12. Give two facts in a case that would point towards forming a private limited company.

### Answers

1. Sole trader, partnerships, limited companies (private and public), public corporations.
2. Owners can be made to pay the business's debts from their personal assets.
3. Directors, elected by the shareholders.
4. Selling shares to the general public.
5. Essential services can be provided even if unprofitable (or prices kept affordable).
6. Less pressure to be efficient (or taxpayers may cover losses).
7. 45,000 ÷ 80,000 × 100 = 56.25%.
8. 45,000 ÷ 100,000 × 100 = 45%, so it loses its majority.
9. 4% × 200,000 = 8,000.
10. Growth without paying for each outlet (or income from fees).
11. Shareholders own part of the company, so they have an interest in how it performs.
12. For example: owners want to protect personal assets; they need more finance than they have; they want to keep shares out of public hands.
