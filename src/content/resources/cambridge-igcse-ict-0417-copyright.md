Section 9.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 is short in the syllabus but often examined. It asks for two things: "the need for copyright legislation and the principles of copyright relating to computer software (e.g. software piracy)", and "the methods that software producers employ to prevent software copyright legislation being broken". This guide concentrates on software, because that is where the syllabus wording points. The broader [Audience study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-audience-study-guide/) covers copyright for text, images and music in documents you produce.

Use the [copyright revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-copyright-revision-notes/) for quick recall and the [copyright practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-copyright-practice/) to test yourself.

## Why software needs copyright legislation

Software is unusual among products because a copy costs almost nothing to make and is identical to the original. A pirated copy of a program works exactly like the one the company sold. A company may spend years paying programmers, testers and designers before it earns anything, and it recovers that cost only by selling licences. Without a law that makes unauthorised copying illegal, anyone could buy one copy and sell thousands.

Copyright legislation is therefore needed to:

- **protect the producer's income**, so that the people who did the work are paid for it;
- **encourage investment and new software**, because a company will not fund development if its product can be copied freely;
- **give the producer a legal remedy**: the right to take offenders to court and to claim compensation;
- **protect the producer's reputation**, because tampered copies may contain malware or bugs that users blame on the original company;
- **set clear rules** so that users and businesses know what they may and may not do with a program.

Each country has its own copyright law; for example, in the United Kingdom it is the Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988. You do not need the name of any law for 0417. You need to explain why such a law is needed and what it allows.

## The principles of copyright relating to software

### You buy a licence, not the program

When you "buy" software you are normally buying a **licence**: permission to use the program under stated conditions. The copyright stays with the producer. The **licence agreement** (often called an end-user licence agreement) sets the conditions, and you accept it during installation.

### What the law and a typical licence do not allow

Without the copyright owner's permission it is illegal to:

- make copies of the software to give away, lend or sell;
- install the software on more computers, or for more users, than the licence covers;
- rent or lend the software to others;
- change (modify) the program code, or use parts of it in your own product;
- remove copyright notices, or bypass the protection built into the software;
- run more simultaneous users on a network than the network licence permits.

Many licences allow the purchaser to keep one back-up copy for their own use, but that copy cannot be passed to anyone else.

### Types of licence

| Licence | What it permits |
|---|---|
| Single-user | One person, or one computer |
| Multi-user | A stated number of users or computers |
| Site licence | All computers at one site, such as a school |
| Network licence | A set number of people using the program at the same time over a network |
| Subscription | Use for as long as payments continue |

Even software that is free to download is still protected by copyright: its licence decides whether it may be copied, shared or changed.

### Software piracy

**Software piracy** is the illegal copying, distribution or use of software. Common forms are:

- copying a program and giving it to friends;
- a business installing one licensed copy on many computers;
- downloading illegal copies from file-sharing sites;
- selling counterfeit copies that look like the genuine product;
- using a "crack" or a key generator to get round activation.

Piracy harms producers (lost income, less money for development) and users (no updates or technical support, a higher risk of malware, and the risk of fines or prosecution).

## Methods producers use to prevent copyright being broken

| Method | How it works | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Licence agreement | User must accept the terms before installing | Relies on honesty; does not physically stop copying |
| Product key | A unique code must be typed in during installation | Keys can be shared or generated illegally |
| Online activation | The key is checked against the producer's server and linked to a device; only a set number of activations are allowed | Needs an internet connection |
| Registration | The user registers the product with personal details | Easy to give false details |
| Dongle | A small hardware device must be plugged in for the software to run | Can be lost; costs money to make |
| Original media required | The original CD or DVD must be in the drive | Many computers no longer have optical drives |
| Holograms and packaging | Help buyers tell genuine products from counterfeit ones | Does not stop digital copying |
| Encryption of the program | The program code is encrypted and only runs with the correct decryption key | Adds cost and complexity |
| Digital rights management (DRM) | Restricts how files can be copied, shared or played | Can annoy legitimate users |
| Subscription or cloud account | Software runs only while the user is logged in to a paid account | Needs regular internet access |

Most producers combine several methods, for example a product key, online activation and regular online checks that the licence is still valid.

## Worked example: a school computer room

*Scenario.* A school buys a single-user licence for a design program and a technician installs it on all 30 computers in a room.

- *Is copyright broken?* Yes. The licence covers one computer, so 29 installations are unlicensed copies. This is software piracy, even though the school paid for one copy.
- *What should the school have bought?* A multi-user licence for 30 computers, or a site licence covering the whole school.
- *How would online activation have stopped this?* The product key would be activated on the first computer. When the technician tried to activate it on a second computer, the producer's server would recognise that the key had already been used and refuse to activate the program.

The answer pattern is: identify the rule, apply it to the numbers in the scenario, then name the protection and explain how it works in this case.

## Annotated model answer

*Question: Explain why copyright legislation is needed for computer software.* **[4]**

> Software costs a great deal of time and money to develop (1), but it can be copied very easily and each copy is identical to the original (1). Copyright law makes copying without permission illegal, so the producer can earn income from each licence sold (1) and can take people who copy the software to court (1). This protects the money the company needs to fund new and updated software.

*Why it scores:* each mark is a separate point, and each point is explained, not just listed. The answer is about software, not about photographs or music.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that buying software means you own the program. You own a licence to use it.
- Writing that a product key stops copying. It stops a copy being *installed and used* without a valid key; the files can still be copied.
- Thinking piracy only means selling copies. Giving a copy to a friend free of charge is still piracy.
- Confusing a licence agreement with copyright. Copyright is the legal right; the licence is the set of terms the producer grants under it.
- Listing protection methods with no explanation when the question says **describe**.
- Describing antivirus software or a firewall as a copyright protection method. Those protect data, not the producer's rights.

## Exam technique

- **State** or **give**: a short, precise answer, such as "a dongle".
- **Describe**: say what the method is and what the user must do, for example "a unique product key must be entered during installation before the software will run".
- **Explain**: give a reason, linked by "because" or "so", as in the model answer above.
- **Discuss** or **evaluate**: give strengths and limitations of the methods, then a judgement. A strong judgement is that several methods together are more effective than any single one.

## Check your understanding

1. Why can software be pirated more easily than a physical product? *Copies cost almost nothing to make and are identical to the original.*
2. What does a software licence give the buyer? *Permission to use the software under stated conditions, not ownership of the copyright.*
3. A family installs one single-user copy on three laptops. Is this legal? *No, unless the licence covers three devices.*
4. Describe how a dongle protects software. *The software runs only when the dongle is plugged into the computer, so a copy without the dongle will not run.*
5. Give one disadvantage of online activation for a user. *It needs an internet connection, and activations may run out if the computer is replaced.*
6. Give two effects of piracy on users of pirated copies. *No updates or support, and a higher risk of malware.*
