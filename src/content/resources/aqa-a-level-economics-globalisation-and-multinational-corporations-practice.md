## Questions

**1.** Define globalisation. **[2]**

**2.** Explain how lower transport costs and developments in information technology have contributed to globalisation. **[4]**

**3.** Between year 0 and year 12 in Country A, an index of the volume of trade rises from 100 to 156 and an index of real output rises from 100 to 126.

**(a)** Calculate the average annual rate of growth of trade volume, to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate trade volume relative to output in year 12 (year 0 = 100). **[2]**

**4.** Country J imports parts worth 36 and exports the assembled product for 45.

**(a)** Calculate the value added in Country J as a share of the export value. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why gross export figures can overstate the benefit a country gets from taking part in a supply chain. **[3]**

**5.** The table shows data for Country K.

| | Year 1 | Year 10 |
|---|---|---|
| Exports plus imports as % of GDP | 38 | 47 |
| FDI inflow as % of GDP | 2.0 | 3.5 |
| Foreign-owned firms' share of manufacturing output (%) | 18 | 31 |
| Average tariff (%) | 14 | 6 |

Identify two characteristics of globalisation shown in the data, and explain one limitation of the data in judging who gains. **[4]**

**6.** In Country L, imported electronics make up 10% of household spending and their price falls by 30%. Other prices are unchanged. Calculate the new price level (old = 1.00) and the change in the real purchasing power of a fixed money income. **[3]**

**7.** Country M has GDP of 450. Foreign MNCs send profits of 36 abroad. Residents earn 9 from abroad.

**(a)** Calculate gross national income (GNI). **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why GNI may be a better guide than GDP to the living standards of residents in Country M. **[2]**

**8.** Explain two ways in which globalisation may benefit a less-developed country and one way in which it may harm it. **[6]**

**9.** An MNC has subsidiary P in a country taxing profit at 35% and subsidiary R in a country taxing profit at 15%. P earns 80 and R earns 20 before tax. Using transfer prices, the MNC shifts 30 of profit from P to R.

**(a)** Calculate the tax the MNC saves. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the government where P operates may be concerned. **[2]**

**10.** An MNC opens a plant in Country N with 1,500 direct jobs. Each direct job supports 0.8 further jobs locally. Direct workers earn 6,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of jobs supported. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the annual wage bill for the direct jobs. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the net gain in employment may be smaller than your answer to (a). **[1]**

**11.** Assess whether globalisation harms workers in more-developed countries. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate the extent to which multinational corporations promote economic development in less-developed countries. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The growing integration of national economies [1] as goods, services, capital, labour, technology or ideas cross borders [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both the closer linking and at least one type of flow; "trading more" alone covers only one part of the process.

**2.** Transport: lower costs cut the delivered price of goods [1], so trade rises and production can be split across countries [1]. Information technology: services can be traded and plants managed from a distance [1], so firms can build longer supply chains [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each factor needs its own chain from cause to effect; naming two factors with no link to globalisation leaves the explanation incomplete.

**3. (a)** Growth = (156/100)^(1/12) - 1 [1] = **3.8% a year** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 156/126 x 100 [1] = **123.8**, so trade has grown about 24% more than output [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the 12th root and show the formula line; dividing the total rise by 12 gives a different (simple) figure.

**4. (a)** Value added = 45 - 36 = 9 [1]. Share = 9/45 = **20%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Exports record 45 although 36 is imported content [1]. Inputs are counted again each time they cross a border [1]. The country's own income comes from value added of only 9, so its gain is smaller than the gross figure implies [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers from the question in part (b); a general statement about supply chains reads as less applied.

**5.** Trade rising relative to output (38% to 47% of GDP) [1]. More FDI and a larger MNC role (inflow 2.0% to 3.5% of GDP, foreign share of manufacturing 18% to 31%) [1]. Limitation: the data show integration but not distribution [1], such as whether gains reach poorer households or whether profits leave the country [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The tariff row is a cause of globalisation rather than a characteristic, so it does not answer the first part of the question.

**6.** New price level = 0.90 + 0.10 x 0.70 = **0.97** [1], a fall of 3.0% [1]. Purchasing power = 1/0.97 - 1 = **+3.1%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Weight the price fall by the budget share; applying the whole 30% to the price level overstates the gain.

**7. (a)** Net income from abroad = 9 - 36 = -27 [1]. GNI = 450 - 27 = **423** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** GNI measures income of residents [1], whereas GDP includes profit that is sent abroad and not available to residents [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Make clear that GDP measures output inside the borders and GNI measures income of residents; the two are often swapped.

**8.** Benefit one: FDI brings capital, jobs and technology [1] that raise output and incomes [1]. Benefit two: access to export markets allows specialisation and growth [1], with more foreign exchange and tax revenue [1]. Harm: reliance on a few exports leaves the country open to falling world prices [1], which can cut incomes and government revenue (or local firms may be undercut by imports) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Giving two benefits and one harm exactly as asked is safer than a long list; extra points do not replace a missing development of one chain.

**9. (a)** Tax before = 0.35 x 80 + 0.15 x 20 = 28 + 3 = 31 [1]. After: P = 50 and R = 50, so 17.5 + 7.5 = 25 [1]. Saving = **6** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The P government loses 10.5 of tax (28 - 17.5) [1], though the real activity occurs in its economy, so it has less to spend on public services [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the before and after tax lines separately; the saving can then be traced to the shifted profit.

**10. (a)** Indirect jobs = 1,500 x 0.8 = 1,200 [1]. Total = **2,700** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 1,500 x 6,000 = **9,000,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Some jobs may replace jobs lost at local firms driven out by the MNC (or workers may move from other jobs) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason for a smaller net gain must be a specific effect, such as crowding out, and not just "some people might not work".

**11.** Harm: offshoring and import competition remove jobs in some industries [1], leading to structural unemployment, especially where workers' skills do not match new jobs [1]. Lower-skilled wages may fall relative to skilled wages, widening inequality [1]. Benefits: cheaper imports raise real income [1] and exports and service sectors create new jobs [1]. Judgement: the outcome depends on the speed of adjustment and on retraining and mobility, so the harm is concentrated and may be temporary for some workers but lasting for others [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A supported judgement that says who is harmed and under what conditions is stronger than a verdict of "harms" or "benefits" alone.

**12.** Benefits: MNCs bring capital and technology [1], create jobs and wages above local alternatives [1], and raise tax revenue and export earnings [1]. Costs: profit sent home means GNI grows more slowly than GDP [1]. Local firms may lose sales and close [1], and transfer pricing can cut the tax the host receives [1]. Evaluation: the effect depends on the type of investment, as manufacturing with local suppliers links more with the economy than extraction does [1]. It depends on the host's skills, bargaining power, tax and regulation [1]. Development also needs health, education and institutions, so an MNC is one input only [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Development is wider than growth, so an answer that discusses only output and jobs leaves evaluation material unused.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a definition of globalisation that covers only trade.
- Writing "lower costs lead to globalisation" without the chain that links the cost to trade or investment.
- Using a simple average instead of a compound rate for growth over several years.
- Confusing GDP with GNI, or ignoring profit sent abroad.
- Treating all jobs created by an MNC as net new jobs.
- Applying a price fall to the whole price level instead of weighting it by the budget share.
- Describing benefits and costs without saying who gains, who loses and over what period.
- Ending an evaluation with a summary of points and no judgement.
