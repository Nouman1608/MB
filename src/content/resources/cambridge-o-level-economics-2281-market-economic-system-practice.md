This set tests section 2.9 **Market economic system** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026. Countries named V and W are invented. Teaching is in the [market economic system study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-market-economic-system/) and the [market economic system revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-market-economic-system-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define a market economic system. **[2]**

**2.** In a market economic system, what mainly decides what is produced?

A: Government output targets

B: Consumer spending and the profit it creates for firms

C: Tradition and custom

D: Trade union agreements

**[1]**

**3.** Which is a disadvantage of a market economic system?

A: Firms have an incentive to cut costs

B: Prices respond quickly to changes in demand

C: Public goods may not be provided

D: Consumers have a wide choice of products

**[1]**

**4.** Identify the function of prices shown in each case.

**(a)** A rising price for engineers tells young people that engineering skills are wanted. **[1]**

**(b)** Higher profit from tomatoes leads farmers to plant more tomatoes. **[1]**

**(c)** When a new games console is scarce, only buyers willing to pay the high price get one. **[1]**

**5.** State two features of a market economic system other than the use of prices. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by consumer sovereignty. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Read the passage (original, written for this practice set).

*Country V removed most price controls ten years ago. Shops now stock a wide range of goods, and many new private firms have opened. However, several large state factories closed, and unemployment in the old industrial towns is high. Fees are charged at most clinics, and some low-income families no longer visit a doctor.*

**(a)** Identify two pieces of evidence that Country V has moved towards a market economic system. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain one advantage of the change shown in the passage. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain two disadvantages of the change shown in the passage. **[4]**

**8.** Demand for electric cars rises sharply. Explain how the price mechanism reallocates resources towards electric cars. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why competition between firms in a market economy may benefit consumers. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why a market economic system may lead to the over-production of goods that cause pollution. **[3]**

**11.** Country W relies on markets for most goods but provides free schooling. Explain why its government chose to provide schooling rather than leave it to the market. **[3]**

**12.** Discuss whether a market economic system is the best way to answer the questions of what, how and for whom to produce. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A system in which resources are allocated mainly by the price mechanism, through demand and supply in markets [1], with resources privately owned and decisions made by households and firms rather than the government [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. Consumers' spending decides which products are profitable, so firms produce them. **[1]**

**3.** C [1]. Non-payers cannot be excluded from public goods, so firms cannot profit from them. A, B and D are advantages. **[1]**

**4. (a)** Signalling. [1]

**(b)** Incentive. [1]

**(c)** Rationing. [1]

**5.** Any two from: private ownership of resources; the profit motive; competition; consumer sovereignty; a limited role for government [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.** Consumers' spending decides what is produced [1], because firms only make products that consumers are willing and able to buy at a profitable price [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Any two from: price controls removed; many new private firms; state factories closed; fees charged at clinics [1 each]. **[2]**

**(b)** Shops stock a wide range of goods [1], because firms now earn profit by producing what consumers want, so choice has increased (credit also: new firms show incentives for enterprise) [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** First: unemployment in old industrial towns [1], because workers from closed state factories cannot easily move to new industries elsewhere or lack the right skills [1]. Second: unequal access to healthcare [1], because clinics charge fees and low-income families cannot pay, so a merit good is under-consumed [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Rising demand creates a shortage at the old price, so the price of electric cars rises [1]. The higher price raises profit for producers [1], so existing firms expand output and new firms enter [1]. They hire workers and buy materials and machines, drawing these resources from products whose demand and profit are falling, such as petrol cars [1]. **[4]**

**9.** With many rival firms, a firm that charges more than others loses customers [1], so firms must keep prices down and cut costs [1]. They also improve quality and offer new products to win customers, so consumers get better value and more choice [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Firms base output on their own costs and revenues [1]. The cost of pollution falls on third parties, such as people living near a factory, and firms do not pay it [1], so the price does not reflect the full cost and more is produced than is best for society [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Education is a merit good: people may undervalue its benefits [1], and some families could not afford fees [1], so a market would provide too little; free schooling raises take-up and benefits society through a more skilled workforce [1]. **[3]**

**12.** For: the price mechanism responds quickly to what consumers want, so the "what" question is answered by consumer choice [1]; competition and profit push firms to use the lowest-cost methods, answering "how" efficiently [1]. Against: "for whom" is decided by ability to pay, so people with low incomes may go without essentials [1]; public goods may not be produced at all and goods with external costs may be over-produced [1]; a firm with monopoly power can restrict output and raise prices [1]. Judgement: markets answer "what" and "how" well for most private goods where there is competition, but most countries add government action for public goods, merit goods and income support, which is why mixed systems are common [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Organise the answer around the three questions. It keeps the discussion focused and makes the final judgement easier to support.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining the system without mentioning prices or private ownership.
- Describing a market economy as having no government at all.
- Naming advantages and disadvantages without explaining how each comes about.
- Not using the passage when the question asks for evidence from it.
- Stopping the price mechanism chain at "the price rises" without explaining how resources move.
- Ending a discussion without a supported judgement.
