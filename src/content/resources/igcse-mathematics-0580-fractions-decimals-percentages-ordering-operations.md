This guide teaches three subtopics of Topic 1 Number in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027: **1.4 Fractions, decimals and percentages**, **1.5 Ordering** and **1.6 The four operations**. It covers both tiers. Core candidates (Papers 1 and 3) need everything except the parts marked **Extended only**; Extended candidates (Papers 2 and 4) need all of it. The only Extended-only content in these three subtopics is recurring decimal notation and converting between recurring decimals and fractions (E1.4).

Calculators are not allowed on Papers 1 and 2, so every method here is shown by hand.

## 1.4 Fractions, decimals and percentages

### The language

- A **proper fraction** has a numerator smaller than its denominator, such as 5/9.
- An **improper fraction** has a numerator larger than its denominator, such as 29/6.
- A **mixed number** is a whole number plus a proper fraction, such as 4⅚.
- A **decimal** uses place value after the decimal point: in 0.425 the 4 is tenths, the 2 hundredths and the 5 thousandths.
- A **percentage** is a number of hundredths: 42.5% means 42.5/100.

**Simplest form** means the numerator and denominator have no common factor other than 1. Divide both by their highest common factor. For 30/72, the HCF is 6, so 30/72 = **5/12**.

### Converting between the forms

| From | To | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Fraction | Decimal | Divide numerator by denominator |
| Decimal | Fraction | Write over 10, 100, 1000 … by place value, then simplify |
| Decimal | Percentage | Multiply by 100 |
| Percentage | Decimal | Divide by 100 |
| Percentage | Fraction | Write over 100, then simplify |
| Mixed number | Improper fraction | Whole number × denominator, add numerator, keep the denominator |
| Improper fraction | Mixed number | Divide; the remainder goes over the same denominator |

**Worked example 1 (non-calculator).** Write 7/16 as a decimal.

Short division of 7 by 16, adding zeros after the decimal point:

```
7.0000 ÷ 16
70 ÷ 16 = 4 remainder 6     ->  0.4
60 ÷ 16 = 3 remainder 12    ->  0.43
120 ÷ 16 = 7 remainder 8    ->  0.437
80 ÷ 16 = 5 remainder 0     ->  0.4375
```

So 7/16 = **0.4375**, which is 43.75%.

**Worked example 2.** Write 1.15 as (a) a fraction in its simplest form, (b) a mixed number, (c) a percentage.

```
(a) 1.15 = 115/100     HCF of 115 and 100 is 5    ->  23/20
(b) 23 ÷ 20 = 1 remainder 3                         ->  1 3/20
(c) 1.15 × 100                                      ->  115%
```

A percentage over 100% means more than one whole.

**Core: what to do with a fraction that never ends.** Some fractions give a decimal that goes on for ever, such as 2/3 = 0.666… At Core you do not need recurring notation. Write enough digits to show the pattern, or round to the accuracy the question asks for: 2/3 = 0.667 to 3 decimal places.

### Recurring decimals (Extended only)

A **recurring decimal** has a digit or block of digits that repeats for ever. The syllabus notation puts a dot over the first and last digit of the repeating block:

- 0.4̇ means 0.4444… (one digit repeats)
- 0.26̇ means 0.2666… (only the 6 repeats)
- 0.3̇6̇ means 0.363636… (the block 36 repeats)
- 0.2̇16̇ means 0.216216216… (dots on the 2 and the 6: the block 216 repeats)

When there are two dots, **every digit from the first dot to the second** is in the repeating block, including any digits between them.

**Fraction to recurring decimal.** Divide as usual and stop when a remainder repeats, because from then on the digits repeat too.

```
5/11:  50 ÷ 11 = 4 r 6,  60 ÷ 11 = 5 r 5,  50 ÷ 11 = 4 r 6 ...   ->  0.454545... = 0.4̇5̇
7/12:  70 ÷ 12 = 5 r 10, 100 ÷ 12 = 8 r 4, 40 ÷ 12 = 3 r 4 ...   ->  0.58333...  = 0.583̇
```

**Recurring decimal to fraction.** Let x be the decimal. Multiply by powers of 10 so that two versions of x have **identical** recurring tails, then subtract to remove the tail.

**Worked example 3 (Extended).** Write 0.3̇6̇ as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
x      = 0.363636...
100x   = 36.363636...       (block of 2 digits, so multiply by 100)
100x - x = 36
99x    = 36
x      = 36/99 = 4/11
```

**Worked example 4 (Extended).** Write 0.26̇ as a fraction in its simplest form.

Only the 6 repeats, so first move the non-repeating 2 past the decimal point.

```
x      = 0.2666...
10x    = 2.666...
100x   = 26.666...
100x - 10x = 26.666... - 2.666...
90x    = 24
x      = 24/90 = 4/15
```

Check: 4 ÷ 15 = 0.2666… The two lines you subtract must have the same digits after the point, so that the difference is a whole number.

**Worked example 5 (Extended).** Write 0.2̇16̇ as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
x       = 0.216216...
1000x   = 216.216216...      (block of 3 digits, so multiply by 1000)
999x    = 216
x       = 216/999 = 8/37     (divide top and bottom by 27)
```

## 1.5 Ordering

### The symbols

| Symbol | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| = | is equal to | 0.4 = 2/5 |
| ≠ | is not equal to | 0.4 ≠ 0.04 |
| > | is greater than | 0.71 > 0.705 |
| < | is less than | -2.4 < -2.04 |
| ⩾ | is greater than or equal to | "at least 18" is x ⩾ 18 |
| ⩽ | is less than or equal to | "no more than 30" is n ⩽ 30 |

"At least" includes the value (⩾); "more than" does not (>).

### Ordering numbers in different forms

Convert everything to decimals, write them to the same number of decimal places, then compare digit by digit from the left.

**Worked example 6.** Write these in order of size, smallest first: 0.71, 7/10, 72%, 5/7, 0.705

```
0.71    = 0.7100
7/10    = 0.7000
72%     = 0.7200
5/7     = 0.7142...
0.705   = 0.7050
```

Smallest first: **7/10, 0.705, 0.71, 5/7, 72%**. Answer in the original forms.

### Negative numbers

On a number line, numbers get smaller as you move left. A negative number further from zero is **smaller**.

**Worked example 7.** Order -2.4, -2¼, -2.04, -2⅓, smallest first.

```
-2.4 = -2.400     -2 1/4 = -2.250     -2.04 = -2.040     -2 1/3 = -2.333...
```

Smallest first: **-2.4, -2⅓, -2¼, -2.04**.

### Inequalities and lists of integers

**Worked example 8.** List the integers n such that -3 ⩽ n < 2.

-3 is included (⩽), 2 is not (<): **-3, -2, -1, 0, 1**.

### Ordering recurring decimals (Extended only)

Write out enough digits to see where the numbers differ.

**Worked example 9 (Extended).** Place these in order, smallest first, and state which are equal: 0.4̇5̇, 0.45̇, 0.454, 5/11

```
0.4̇5̇  = 0.454545...
0.45̇   = 0.455555...
0.454   = 0.454000
5/11    = 0.454545...
```

Order: **0.454 < 0.4̇5̇ = 5/11 < 0.45̇**. The = sign is correct here: 0.4̇5̇ and 5/11 are the same number.

## 1.6 The four operations

### Order of operations

Work in this order:

1. **Brackets**
2. **Indices** (powers and roots)
3. **Multiplication and division**, from left to right
4. **Addition and subtraction**, from left to right

× and ÷ have equal priority, as do + and -.

**Worked example 10.** Work out 18 - 12 ÷ 4 × 3.

```
12 ÷ 4 = 3       (÷ and × left to right)
3 × 3  = 9
18 - 9 = 9
```

**Worked example 11.** Work out (5 - 2³) × 4 + 6 ÷ (-2).

```
brackets:  2³ = 8, so 5 - 8 = -3
then:      -3 × 4 = -12   and   6 ÷ (-2) = -3
finally:   -12 + (-3) = -15
```

**Worked example 12.** Insert one pair of brackets to make 6 + 9 ÷ 3 - 1 = 4 true.

Without brackets the value is 6 + 3 - 1 = 8. Try grouping the addition: (6 + 9) ÷ 3 - 1 = 15 ÷ 3 - 1 = 5 - 1 = 4. So **(6 + 9) ÷ 3 - 1 = 4**.

### Negative numbers

- Subtracting a negative is the same as adding: -7 - (-12) = -7 + 12 = **5**.
- Multiplying or dividing: same signs give a positive, different signs give a negative. (-4) × (-6) ÷ 3 = 24 ÷ 3 = **8**.

**Worked example 13 (temperature).**
(a) At 06 00 the temperature is -8 °C. By noon it has risen by 13 degrees. Find the temperature at noon.
(b) A freezer is at -18 °C and the kitchen is at 21 °C. Find the difference.

```
(a) -8 + 13 = 5            ->  5 °C
(b) 21 - (-18) = 21 + 18   ->  39 degrees
```

### Fractions and mixed numbers

- **Add or subtract:** change mixed numbers to improper fractions (or deal with whole parts separately), then use a common denominator.
- **Multiply:** change mixed numbers to improper fractions, cancel common factors, then multiply tops and bottoms.
- **Divide:** multiply by the reciprocal of the second fraction.

**Worked example 14.** Work out 3⅝ - 1⅚.

```
3 5/8 = 29/8      1 5/6 = 11/6      common denominator 24
29/8 = 87/24      11/6 = 44/24
87/24 - 44/24 = 43/24 = 1 19/24
```

**Worked example 15.** Work out 2⅖ × 1⅞.

```
12/5 × 15/8
cancel 12 and 8 by 4:   3/5 × 15/2
cancel 15 and 5 by 5:   3/1 × 3/2
= 9/2 = 4 1/2
```

**Worked example 16.** Work out 4½ ÷ 1⅕.

```
9/2 ÷ 6/5 = 9/2 × 5/6 = 45/12 = 15/4 = 3 3/4
```

### Decimals

- **Add and subtract:** line up the decimal points. 12.5 - 3.87 = 12.50 - 3.87 = **8.63**.
- **Multiply:** multiply the digits as whole numbers, then count the decimal places. 36 × 7 = 252, and 3.6 × 0.07 has 1 + 2 = 3 decimal places, so the answer is **0.252**.
- **Divide:** multiply both numbers by the same power of 10 so that you divide by a whole number. 4.83 ÷ 0.3 = 48.3 ÷ 3 = **16.1**.

### Practical problems

**Worked example 17.** A ribbon 7½ m long is cut into pieces each ⅝ m long. How many pieces are there?

```
7 1/2 ÷ 5/8 = 15/2 × 8/5 = 120/10 = 12 pieces
```

Check: ⅝ m = 0.625 m and 12 × 0.625 = 7.5 m.

## Common errors

- Leaving a fraction answer unsimplified, such as 36/99 instead of 4/11.
- (Extended) Putting the dots on the wrong digits. 0.26̇ (0.2666…) is not the same as 0.2̇6̇ (0.2626…).
- (Extended) Multiplying by the wrong power of 10. For 0.3̇6̇, 10x - x = 3.6363… - 0.3636… does not cancel the tail; a 2-digit block needs 100x.
- Giving an ordering answer in decimals when the question listed fractions and percentages.
- Saying -2.04 < -2.4 because 2.04 < 2.4.
- Doing × before ÷ (or + before -) instead of working left to right.
- Multiplying mixed numbers part by part: 2⅖ × 1⅞ is not 2 × 1 plus ⅖ × ⅞.
- Dividing by a fraction without turning the **second** fraction upside down.
