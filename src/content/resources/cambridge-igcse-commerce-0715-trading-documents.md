Every sale between businesses leaves a trail of documents: they record what was asked for, what was promised, what was sent and what was paid. Section 2.5 of the Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715) syllabus covers three groups: **documents of home trade** (2.5.1), **documents of international trade** (2.5.2) and the **digital use of trading documents** (2.5.3). Examiners often give a scenario and ask which document is needed, who sends it, or what a figure on it means. This page goes further than the document sequence in the [Commercial Operations study guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-commerce-commercial-operations/) by following one transaction from start to finish and working through the figures.

## What are the eight documents of home trade? (2.5.1)

| Document | Sent by | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Enquiry | Buyer to possible sellers | Asks for prices, terms, delivery times and availability |
| Quotation | Seller to buyer | Replies with prices, discounts, delivery date and payment terms |
| Order | Buyer to chosen seller | States exactly what the buyer wants: items, quantities, prices, delivery address |
| Invoice | Seller to buyer | The bill: shows goods supplied, amount owed, discounts and payment terms |
| Delivery note | Seller, with the goods | Lists the goods delivered; the buyer checks and signs it as proof of delivery |
| Credit note | Seller to buyer | Reduces the amount owed, for example when goods are returned damaged or the buyer was overcharged |
| Statement of account | Seller to buyer, usually monthly | Summarises invoices, credit notes and payments, and shows the balance still owed |
| Receipt | Seller to buyer | Proof that payment has been made |

A useful memory line: **ask, offer, buy, bill, deliver, correct, summarise, pay**.

### Following one transaction

Dalia runs a hotel and needs new chairs.

1. She sends an **enquiry** to three furniture suppliers.
2. Each replies with a **quotation**. One offers chairs at $45 each, a 20% trade discount and a 5% cash discount for payment within 14 days.
3. Dalia chooses this supplier and sends an **order** for 20 chairs.
4. The chairs arrive with a **delivery note**. Dalia counts 20 chairs, finds two damaged, notes this and signs.
5. The supplier sends an **invoice** for the 20 chairs.
6. The two damaged chairs are returned, and the supplier issues a **credit note**.
7. At the end of the month, a **statement of account** shows the invoice, the credit note and the balance.
8. Dalia pays, and the supplier sends a **receipt**.

The delivery note and invoice can arrive in either order; some sellers send the invoice with the goods.

## Worked example: the figures on the documents (invented figures)

**Invoice**

| | $ |
|---|---|
| 20 chairs at $45 | 900.00 |
| Less trade discount 20% (0.20 x 900) | 180.00 |
| **Invoice total** | **720.00** |
| Terms: 5% cash discount if paid within 14 days | |

**Credit note** for 2 damaged chairs: 2 x 45 = $90, less 20% trade discount ($18) = **$72.00**.

**Statement of account**

| Date | Detail | Debit $ | Credit $ | Balance $ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3rd | Invoice | 720.00 | | 720.00 |
| 9th | Credit note | | 72.00 | 648.00 |

The balance owed is **$648.00**. If Dalia pays within 14 days and takes the 5% cash discount: 0.05 x 648 = $32.40, so she pays 648.00 - 32.40 = **$615.60**.

Points examiners test: the **trade discount** is deducted on the invoice because Dalia is buying as a business; the **cash discount** is only taken if she pays quickly; the credit note value must also have the trade discount removed, because she was never charged the full $45.

## What are the documents of international trade? (2.5.2)

Exporting adds distance, customs and the risk of not being paid, so four more documents are named.

| Document | What it is | Why it is needed |
|---|---|---|
| Certificate of origin | States the country where the goods were made | Customs use it to decide what duty applies and whether the goods may enter, for example whether lower duties apply within a trading bloc |
| Bill of lading | Issued by the shipping company for goods sent by sea: a receipt for the goods, evidence of the contract of carriage and a document of title | Whoever holds it can claim the goods at the destination port, so it can be passed to the buyer once payment is arranged |
| Air waybill | Issued by the airline for goods sent by air: a receipt and evidence of the contract of carriage | Air cargo arrives quickly, so the goods are released to the named consignee; it is not a document of title |
| Documentary credit (letter of credit) | A promise by the importer's bank to pay the exporter once the exporter presents the agreed documents, such as the bill of lading | Reduces the risk of non-payment: the exporter relies on a bank, not only on an unknown buyer abroad |

**Bill of lading versus air waybill**: both prove the carrier received the goods, but only the bill of lading is a document of title. That difference matters because sea journeys are long and goods may be sold while still at sea.

## How does the online ordering process work? (2.5.3)

The syllabus asks for the stages **from buyer browsing website to receiving order**:

1. **Browse** the website or app and search for products.
2. **Select** items and add them to the basket.
3. **Log in or create an account** (or check out as a guest).
4. **Enter delivery details** and choose a delivery method or click and collect.
5. **Pay securely** online by card, digital wallet or bank transfer.
6. **Receive an order confirmation**, usually by email, which acts as an electronic receipt.
7. **Receive dispatch notification** with tracking information.
8. **Receive the order**, sometimes with an electronic proof of delivery.

Each stage replaces a paper document. The basket and checkout replace the order; the confirmation email replaces the receipt; tracking and electronic proof of delivery replace the signed delivery note.

## Which digital technology is used in trading documents? (2.5.3)

| Technology | Use |
|---|---|
| Digital signatures | Electronic signatures that prove who approved a document and that it has not been changed, such as signing a contract or accepting a delivery |
| Secure access | Passwords, encryption and two-step verification so only authorised people see invoices and accounts |
| Cloud-based storage | Documents kept on internet servers, available from anywhere and backed up |
| Electronic receipts | Emailed or in-app receipts in place of paper |
| Etickets | Electronic tickets for travel or events, held on a phone and scanned at entry |

Benefits: speed, less paper and storage space, fewer lost documents, easy searching. Drawbacks: risk of hacking or data loss, cost of systems and training, and dependence on internet access.

## What are the common mistakes in 2.5?

- Saying the buyer sends the quotation; the seller does.
- Confusing an invoice (a request for payment) with a receipt (proof of payment).
- Mixing up a credit note and a statement of account.
- Taking the cash discount when the question does not say payment was made in time.
- Calling the air waybill a document of title.
- Describing documentary credit as a loan; it is a bank's promise to pay.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** the document: read who is sending it and at what stage.
- **Calculate**: deduct trade discount first, then cash discount only if the terms are met.
- **Explain** why a document is needed: link it to a problem it solves, such as non-payment or proof of delivery.
