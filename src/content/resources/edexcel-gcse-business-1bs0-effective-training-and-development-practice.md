## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is an example of informal training? Select one answer.

A: A one-day first-aid course run by an outside provider

B: A new employee watching an experienced colleague and copying the method

C: An employee studying for a college qualification on day release

D: An annual meeting to compare performance with targets

**[1]**

**2.** Define the term "self-learning". **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** ways a business can train and develop its employees, other than formal and informal training. **[2]**

**4.** A garage employs 45 staff. In one year 9 left. Calculate the percentage of staff who left. **[2]**

**5.** A training course costs £240 per person. A business sends 14 staff. Calculate the total cost of the course. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of holding performance reviews. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why a business might retrain its employees to use new technology. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Explain **one** risk to a business of spending money on training its employees. **[3]**

**9.** Hartwell Call Centre has 50 staff. Last year 14 left. After introducing ongoing training for all employees at a cost of £7,500, only 8 left this year. Replacing each leaver costs £2,000.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of staff who left in each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the net benefit of the training to Hartwell. **[3]**

**(c)** Outline **one** reason why the whole fall in leavers might not be caused by the training. **[2]**

**10.** Rookwood Pharmacy has installed a computer system that checks prescriptions and records stock. Some long-serving staff are worried they will not be able to use it.

Analyse the impact on Rookwood Pharmacy of retraining these staff to use the new system. **[6]**

**11.** Brook Street Motors wants its 6 sales staff to learn about the electric cars it now sells.

Option 1: send all 6 staff on a two-day formal course run by the manufacturer at £350 per person.

Option 2: buy online self-learning modules at £40 per person, which staff complete when the showroom is quiet.

Justify which **one** of these options Brook Street Motors should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Selby Fashion has 12 shops. Sales per employee vary widely between shops and many young staff leave within a year, saying they "don't know how they are doing". There are no targets or performance reviews at present.

Evaluate whether introducing target setting and performance reviews would solve Selby Fashion's problems. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* A and C are formal training. D is a performance review.

**2.** Learning that the employee directs and carries out themselves [1], for example by working through online modules or manuals at their own pace [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: self-learning [1]; ongoing training for all employees [1]; target setting; performance reviews. **[2]**

**4.** 9 ÷ 45 × 100 [1] = **20%** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 14 × £240 [1] = **£3,360** [1] **[2]**

**6.** At a performance review the manager compares the employee's work with agreed targets [1]. This identifies gaps in the employee's skills [1], so the business can arrange the right training and performance improves [1]. **[3]**

*Other creditable points:* praise for success raises motivation; new targets give direction for the coming months.

**7.** Existing skills may not suit the new equipment or software [1]. Retraining lets current staff use it effectively [1], so the business gains the efficiency it paid for without the cost of recruiting new staff [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Once trained, employees are more skilled and more attractive to other employers [1]. Competitors may offer them higher pay [1], so the business could lose both the employee and the money it spent training them [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Last year: 14 ÷ 50 × 100 = **28%**; this year: 8 ÷ 50 × 100 = **16%** [1] [1]

**(b)** Leavers avoided = 14 − 8 = 6 [1]. Saving = 6 × £2,000 = £12,000 [1]. Net benefit = £12,000 − £7,500 = **£4,500** [1]

**(c)** Other call centres nearby may have had fewer vacancies this year [1], so staff had fewer chances to leave whether or not they were trained [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Retraining would let the worried staff use the new system confidently [1], so prescriptions are checked faster and with fewer errors, improving efficiency [1]. Staff who feel supported are less anxious about their jobs [1], so they are more motivated and more likely to stay, keeping their knowledge of customers [1]. However, staff time spent training cannot be spent serving customers [1], so Rookwood may need extra cover during training, which adds to costs [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Option 1 costs 6 × £350 = £2,100 [1]; Option 2 costs 6 × £40 = £240 [1], so Option 2 is £1,860 cheaper [1]. Option 2 also avoids having all sales staff away for two days, so the showroom keeps selling [1]. However, self-learning is hard to check, and staff may rush the modules or not finish them [1]. Option 1 is run by the manufacturer, so staff get consistent, expert knowledge [1] and can ask questions, which matters for an expensive product where customers ask detailed questions [1]. Staff may also feel valued by being sent on a course, raising motivation [1]. Judgement: Option 1, because electric cars are a new and technical product and sales depend on confident advice; the extra £1,860 could be recovered from a few extra sales. Option 2 would suit better if staff were already well informed [1]. **[9]**

**12.** Targets would tell staff exactly what is expected, such as a sales figure per shift [1], which could raise sales per employee in the weaker shops [1]. Performance reviews would give young staff regular feedback [1], answering their complaint that they "don't know how they are doing", so they may feel valued and stay longer [1]. Reviews would also reveal skill gaps that training could fix [1]. However, targets that are too hard could cause stress and make more staff leave [1], and reviews take managers' time across 12 shops [1]. Sales differences may also be caused by shop location rather than staff effort [1]. Judgement: introducing targets and reviews would help, especially with keeping young staff, because it directly answers their complaint; but the targets must be realistic for each shop's location and linked to training for staff who miss them [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a performance review a form of formal training in Question 1.
- Forgetting to subtract the training cost when asked for a net benefit.
- Giving "training improves motivation" without the link to retention.
- In Justify, choosing the cheaper option without weighing what is being learned.
- In Evaluate, not using the case evidence, such as the staff complaint in Question 12.
