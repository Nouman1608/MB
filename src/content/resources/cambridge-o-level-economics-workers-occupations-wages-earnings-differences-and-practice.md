## Questions

**1.** Identify two non-wage factors that may affect a person's choice of occupation and explain how each might influence the choice. **[4]**

**2.** Job X pays 3,150 a month for 21 working days of 8 hours, with 2 hours of commuting each day. Job Y pays 2,940 a month for 21 working days of 8 hours, working from home.

**(a)** Calculate the pay per hour committed (working hours plus commuting hours) for each job. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate by what percentage the monthly pay of Job X exceeds that of Job Y. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a worker might still choose Job X. **[2]**

**3.** The table shows the market for security guards in Region R (illustrative). Wage is per hour.

| Wage | 7 | 8 | 9 | 10 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Demand (guards) | 650 | 600 | 550 | 500 | 450 |
| Supply (guards) | 350 | 450 | 550 | 650 | 750 |

**(a)** Use the table to find the equilibrium wage and employment. **[2]**
**(b)** A minimum wage of 10 is introduced. Calculate the number employed, the number of jobs lost and the surplus of labour. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the change in the total wage bill caused by the minimum wage. **[2]**
**(d)** Demand then rises by 150 guards at every wage. State the new equilibrium wage and number employed. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain what now happens to the effect of the minimum wage of 10. **[2]**
**(f)** Give one reason why demand for security guards might have risen. **[1]**

**4.** A worker in Country C earns 8,000 a year unskilled. Training takes 2 years with no earnings and costs 4,000 in fees. A trained worker then earns 13,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of training. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many years of the extra pay are needed to pay back that cost. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this helps to explain why skilled workers earn more than unskilled workers. **[2]**

**5.** In Country D, average annual earnings (thousands) are 5.0 in the primary sector, 8.0 in the secondary sector and 11.5 in the tertiary sector. Employment shares are 40%, 20% and 40% respectively.

**(a)** Calculate by what percentage tertiary earnings exceed primary earnings. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate by what percentage primary earnings are below tertiary earnings. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate average earnings across the whole economy. **[2]**

**6.** In Town Z, men earn on average 20.0 an hour and women 16.2.

**(a)** Calculate the gender pay gap as a percentage of male pay. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons for a gender pay gap. **[4]**
**(c)** State one government policy that could reduce the gap. **[1]**

**7.** A public sector job pays 2,400 a month plus a pension contribution of 15% of pay. A private sector job pays 2,600 plus 5%.

**(a)** Calculate the value of each package and say which is higher. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why public sector pay may be set differently from private sector pay. **[3]**

**8.** A furniture workshop has 5 workers paid 60 a day each. Working alone, each makes 4 chairs a day. With division of labour the workshop makes 25 chairs a day.

**(a)** Calculate the labour cost per chair when the workers work alone. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the labour cost per chair after division of labour and the percentage fall. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage rise in output per worker. **[2]**

**9.** Discuss whether division of labour benefits an economy. **[8]**

**10.** **(a)** Explain, using a labour market diagram described in words, how discrimination might lower the wage of one group of workers. **[4]**
**(b)** State one government policy to reduce discrimination and one limit on its effectiveness. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a surgeon usually has stronger bargaining power than a cleaner. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** First factor identified, such as job security [1]; explained, for example a secure job lets a worker plan, so a worker may accept lower pay [1]. Second factor, such as the journey to work [1]; explained, for example a long commute costs time and money, so a worker may refuse a distant job [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A factor named but not linked to a choice is a description, not an explanation; always say what the factor makes a worker more or less likely to do.

**2. (a)** Job X hours: (8 + 2) × 21 = 210 [1]; 3,150 ÷ 210 = **15.00** [1]. Job Y hours: 8 × 21 = 168 [1]; 2,940 ÷ 168 = **17.50** [1].
**(b)** (3,150 − 2,940) ÷ 2,940 [1] = 210 ÷ 2,940 = **7.1%** [1].
**(c)** Job X may have non-wage benefits such as promotion prospects or training [1], or the worker may value the higher monthly income or the office environment more than the time cost [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the hours committed beside each result lets a reader see your method, and the base of the percentage in (b) is the lower-paid job.

**3. (a)** Demand equals supply at 550 [1]: wage **9**, employment **550** [1].
**(b)** At 10, demand gives employment of **500** [1]; jobs lost 550 − 500 = **50** [1]; surplus 650 − 500 = **150** [1].
**(c)** Before: 9 × 550 = 4,950 [1]; after: 10 × 500 = 5,000, an increase of **50** [1].
**(d)** New demand at 10 is 650 and supply is 650 [1]: wage **10**, employment **650** [1].
**(e)** The market wage now equals the minimum [1], so the minimum no longer causes a surplus or lost jobs: it is no longer binding [1].
**(f)** For example, more shops and offices opened, raising the demand for security [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Show demand and supply separately at the new wage; surplus is supply minus demand, not the change in employment, and mixing them up is a common slip.

**4. (a)** Earnings lost: 2 × 8,000 = 16,000 [1]; plus fees: **20,000** [1].
**(b)** Extra earnings: 13,000 − 8,000 = 5,000 a year [1]; 20,000 ÷ 5,000 = **4 years** [1].
**(c)** Training is costly and delays earnings, so fewer people choose it and supply of skilled workers is smaller [1]; skilled workers must be paid more to make training worthwhile, so the wage is higher [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the calculation back to supply; a number alone does not explain an earnings difference.

**5. (a)** (11.5 − 5.0) ÷ 5.0 [1] = **130%** [1].
**(b)** (11.5 − 5.0) ÷ 11.5 [1] = **56.5%** [1].
**(c)** 0.4 × 5.0 + 0.2 × 8.0 + 0.4 × 11.5 = 2.0 + 1.6 + 4.6 [1] = **8.2** (thousand) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The question wording tells you the base: "exceed primary" means divide by the primary figure and "below tertiary" means divide by the tertiary figure.

**6. (a)** (20.0 − 16.2) ÷ 20.0 [1] = **19.0%** [1].
**(b)** Reason 1: discrimination, so employers pay women less or keep them out of better-paid jobs [1], lowering average female earnings [1]. Reason 2: part-time work and career breaks for caring [1], which reduce hours and promotion [1]. (Crowding into few occupations also gains credit.)
**(c)** Equal pay legislation [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Two different reasons each need a developed link to pay; a bare list of causes earns little.

**7. (a)** Public: 2,400 × 1.15 = **2,760** [1]; private: 2,600 × 1.05 = **2,730** [1]; the public package is higher by 30 [1].
**(b)** The government is the employer and sets pay scales [1], with a limited budget funded by taxation [1], so pay depends less on profit and local supply and demand [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing basic pay alone would reverse the ranking, so say that you are comparing the whole package.

**8. (a)** Alone: 5 × 4 = 20 chairs [1]; wage bill 5 × 60 = 300, so 300 ÷ 20 = **15.00** [1].
**(b)** 300 ÷ 25 = **12.00** [1]; fall (15 − 12) ÷ 15 = **20%** [1].
**(c)** Output per worker rises from 4 to 25 ÷ 5 = 5 [1]; (5 − 4) ÷ 4 = **25%** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Work out the wage bill once and reuse it; the unit cost falls because the same bill is spread over more chairs.

**9.** Advantage: output rises as workers become skilled at one task [1], so more goods are available and prices can fall [1]. Advantage: lower costs let firms compete, which can raise exports [1], increasing national income [1]. Disadvantage: if a specialist industry declines, workers with narrow skills may not move to new jobs, causing structural unemployment [1], which wastes resources and cuts output [1]. Judgement: benefits are greater in large markets with training and mobile workers, and smaller where skills are narrow [1]; a reasoned overall conclusion [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A discussion answer usually gains by weighing the two sides and saying what the outcome depends on, rather than listing advantages and then disadvantages.

**10. (a)** Draw wage against quantity of labour for the group [1]. Employers' discrimination reduces demand for that group, so the demand curve shifts left [1]. The new equilibrium shows a lower wage and lower employment [1], so the group is paid less than equally able workers elsewhere [1].
**(b)** Equal pay or anti-discrimination law [1]; limit: it is hard to prove or enforce, or discrimination may be hidden in hiring [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the axes and the shifting curve in words; an unlabelled description cannot show the wage effect.

**11.** Surgeons have scarce skills because training takes many years [1], so supply is small [1]. Demand for them is high because their work is highly valuable [1]. They are hard to replace, so an employer would lose more from a dispute [1]. Professional bodies can limit entry and act together [1]. Cleaners are easily replaced, which weakens their position. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Contrast the two groups through a shared cause, supply and replaceability, rather than describing each separately.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a factor, but not saying how it changes a choice.
- Dividing by the wrong figure in a percentage difference.
- Forgetting commuting hours when asked for time committed.
- Reading demand and supply at the wrong wage in a table.
- Saying a minimum wage always causes unemployment, without checking whether it is above equilibrium.
- Giving a cost saving from division of labour without showing the output change that causes it.
- Ranking pay without comparing the full package.
- Stating a conclusion in a "discuss" answer without any reason or condition.
