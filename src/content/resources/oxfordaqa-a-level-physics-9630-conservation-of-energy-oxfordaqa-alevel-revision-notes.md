These notes condense **3.2.8 Conservation of energy** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [conservation of energy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-conservation-of-energy-oxfordaqa-alevel/); for exam-style questions, use the [conservation of energy practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-conservation-of-energy-oxfordaqa-alevel-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Principle of conservation of energy.
- ΔEp = mgΔh and Ek = ½mv².
- Quantitative and qualitative application to examples with gravitational potential, kinetic and elastic potential energy, and work done against resistive forces.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conservation of energy | Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred; the total is constant |
| Kinetic energy | Energy of a moving object, ½mv² |
| Gravitational potential energy | Energy stored by position in a gravitational field; changes by mgΔh |
| Elastic potential energy | Energy stored in a stretched or compressed object, ½FΔL |
| Resistive force | Friction, drag or air resistance, opposing motion |
| Work against resistive forces | Energy transferred to internal energy of surroundings, F × s |
| Closed system | One in which no energy enters or leaves |

## The energy account

**Start energy = end energy + work against resistive forces**

| No resistance | With resistance |
|---|---|
| mgΔh = ½mv² | mgΔh = ½mv² + Fs |
| v = √(2gΔh), mass cancels | F = (mgΔh − ½mv²) ÷ s |
| v² = u² + 2gΔh for a throw | s is the path length, not the height |

## Method: any energy question

1. Choose start and end points.
2. List the energy in each store at each point (kinetic, gravitational, elastic).
3. Add work against resistive forces to the end side.
4. Equate and solve.
5. Check: is the speed or height plausible? Does it exceed the no-friction value? It should not.

## Common transfers to describe

| Situation | Transfers |
|---|---|
| Object falling | Ep → Ek (+ internal energy of air if drag acts) |
| Pendulum swing | Ep ↔ Ek, with a slow transfer to internal energy |
| Spring launcher | Elastic → Ek → Ep |
| Bungee jump | Ep → Ek → elastic; zero Ek at the lowest point |
| Bouncing ball | Ep → Ek → elastic during contact → Ek → Ep, with internal energy and sound at each bounce |
| Vehicle braking | Ek → internal energy of brakes |

## Worked reminders

- **Free fall.** From rest through 5.0 m: v = √(2 × 9.81 × 5.0) = 9.9 m s⁻¹.
- **Throw up.** At 8.0 m s⁻¹: h = 8.0² ÷ (2 × 9.81) = 3.3 m.
- **Braking.** A 1000 kg car stopping from 20 m s⁻¹ transfers ½ × 1000 × 20² = 2.0 × 10⁵ J to the brakes.
- **Rebound.** 2.0 m drop, 1.4 m rebound: 70% kept, 30% transferred.
- **Resistance.** An 80 kg cyclist freewheels down a vertical drop of 8.0 m, speeding up from 3.0 to 10 m s⁻¹. Ep lost = 6280 J; Ek gained = ½ × 80 × (10² − 3.0²) = 3640 J; work against resistance = 2.6 kJ.

## Exam traps

- Slope length used for Δh.
- ½mv² with v not squared, or ½mv² written as (½mv)².
- Initial kinetic energy left out for a thrown object.
- "Energy is lost" without naming internal energy (heating) of the surroundings.
- Work against friction divided by height instead of distance.
- Using energy to find a direction of motion.

## Quick self-test

1. State the principle of conservation of energy.
2. Find the kinetic energy of a 0.15 kg ball moving at 20 m s⁻¹.
3. Find the gain in gravitational potential energy when a 50 kg mass is raised 4.0 m.
4. An object falls from rest through 5.0 m. Find its speed, ignoring air resistance.
5. A ball is thrown up at 8.0 m s⁻¹. Find its maximum height.
6. How much energy is transferred to the brakes when a 1000 kg car stops from 20 m s⁻¹?
7. The kinetic energy of an object doubles. By what factor does its speed change?
8. A ball dropped from 2.0 m rebounds to 1.4 m. What percentage of its energy is transferred in the bounce?
9. Find the work done against resistance by the cyclist in the worked reminder above, to 2 s.f.
10. Find the energy stored in a spring of spring constant 250 N m⁻¹ compressed by 0.040 m.
11. Why must the first hill of a roller coaster be the highest?
12. Why does the mass cancel when finding the speed of a falling object without air resistance?

### Answers

1. Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one store to another; the total energy of a closed system is constant.
2. ½ × 0.15 × 20² = **30 J**.
3. 50 × 9.81 × 4.0 = **1960 J**.
4. **9.9 m s⁻¹**.
5. **3.3 m**.
6. **2.0 × 10⁵ J**.
7. **√2**, about 1.41.
8. **30%**.
9. 6280 − 3640 = **2.6 kJ**.
10. ½ × 250 × 0.040² = **0.20 J**.
11. Resistive forces transfer energy to internal energy throughout the ride, so the car never has enough energy to climb higher than its starting height.
12. Both Ep and Ek are proportional to mass, so m appears on both sides of mgΔh = ½mv².

## Where marks are usually lost

- Energy accounts written as numbers with no statement of which store each is.
- Qualitative answers that skip the elastic store during a bounce or a bungee jump.
- Final speeds larger than the no-friction value, which signals a sign error.
