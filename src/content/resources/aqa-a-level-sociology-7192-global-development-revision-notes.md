These notes cover Global Development, section 4.2.6 of the AQA AS and A-level Sociology (7191, 7192) specification, Version 1.2 (14 October 2021), listed as 3.2.6 in the specification at a glance. It is assessed in A-level Paper 2: Topics in Sociology (2 hours, 80 marks, 33.3% of the A-level). The specification states that Section A is "one from option 1: 4.2.1, 4.2.2, 4.2.3 or 4.2.4" and Section B is "one from option 2: 4.2.5, 4.2.6, 4.2.7 or 4.2.8"; Global Development is in option 2, so you answer it in Section B ("extended writing, 40 marks").

Test yourself afterwards with the [Global development practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-sociology-7192-global-development-practice/). The [AQA A-level Sociology hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/sociology/) and the [AQA A-level Sociology checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/sociology/) list the rest of the course.

## The five content points (as printed)

Students are expected to be familiar with sociological explanations of:
1. development, underdevelopment and global inequality
2. globalisation and its influence on the cultural, political and economic relationships between societies
3. the role of transnational corporations, non-governmental organisations and international agencies in local and global strategies for development
4. development in relation to aid and trade, industrialisation, urbanisation, the environment, and war and conflict
5. employment, education, health, demographic change and gender as aspects of development.

The specification also requires you to link every topic to the two core themes (socialisation, culture and identity; social differentiation, power and stratification) and to theories, methods and research design.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Development | Positive social and economic change; what counts as "positive" is contested |
| Underdevelopment | (Dependency theory) a society made poorer through unequal links with richer ones |
| Global South / North | Broad labels for poorer and richer regions; not strictly geographic |
| GNI per capita | Gross national income divided by population; an average that hides inequality |
| HDI | UNDP index (from 1990): life expectancy, schooling, GNI per capita |
| Neo-colonialism | Control of former colonies through economic means such as debt and trade terms |
| Structural adjustment | Loan conditions (often IMF and World Bank) requiring market reforms and spending cuts |
| TNC | Transnational corporation: a firm operating in several countries |
| NGO | Non-governmental organisation: non-profit, independent of the state |
| Tied aid | Aid that must be spent on goods or services from the donor country |
| Bilateral / multilateral aid | Aid from one government to another / through an international agency |
| Terms of trade | The price of a country's exports relative to its imports |
| Export processing zone | Area with tax breaks and relaxed rules to attract foreign factories |
| Over-urbanisation | Cities growing faster than formal jobs and housing |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without compromising future generations (Brundtland, 1987) |
| Demographic dividend | Growth boost when the working-age share of the population rises |
| Informal sector | Work outside contracts, tax and legal protection |
| Microcredit | Small loans to people without access to banks, often women |

## Theories and studies at a glance

| Who | What | Key idea or finding | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rostow (1960) | Modernisation | Five stages, from traditional society to high mass consumption | Ethnocentric; ignores colonial history |
| Frank (1966) | Dependency | Metropolis drains surplus from satellites | Cannot easily explain East Asian growth |
| Wallerstein (1974) | World systems | Core, semi-periphery, periphery; mobility possible | Gives little weight to local agency |
| Neoliberals | Free markets | State failure and protectionism block growth | Structural adjustment cuts hit health and education |
| Sen (1999) | Capability approach | Development as expanding real freedoms | Hard to measure freedom directly |
| Escobar (1995) | Post-development | "Development" is a Western discourse | Offers few large-scale alternatives |
| Ohmae (1990) | Hyperglobalist | Borderless world; nation state weakens | States still regulate trade and migration |
| Hirst and Thompson (1996) | Sceptics | Most firms remain national; regulation still possible | Underplays speed of digital and financial flows |
| Ritzer (1993) | McDonaldization | Efficiency and predictability spread worldwide | Ignores local adaptation (glocalisation) |
| Sklair | Transnational capitalist class | Global elites share interests across borders | Elites also compete with each other |
| Hayter (1971) | Aid as imperialism | Aid serves donor interests | Ignores life-saving humanitarian aid |
| Sachs (2005) | Pro-aid | Large targeted aid can break poverty traps | Critics doubt top-down planning |
| Moyo (2009) | Anti-aid | State-to-state aid breeds dependency and corruption | Underplays clear gains from health and emergency aid |
| Collier (2007) | Four traps | Conflict, natural resources, landlocked, bad governance | Focuses on internal causes over global ones |
| Davis (2006) | Planet of Slums | Urban growth without jobs creates slums | Some migrants still gain from city life |
| Kaldor | New wars | Intra-state conflicts, identity politics, civilian targets | Disputed how "new" these are |
| Elson and Pearson (1981) | Gender and factory work | "Nimble fingers" used to justify low pay for women | Waged work can also increase women's independence |
| Boserup (1970) | Gender | Development projects often bypassed women | Later critics: adding women is not enough |
| Malthus (1798) / Ehrlich (1968) | Population | Population growth outstrips resources | Food output has generally kept pace |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Growth versus development.** Rising GNI is growth; development also includes health, education, rights and equality.
- **Underdevelopment versus undevelopment.** Frank's underdevelopment is caused by exploitation; "undeveloped" just means not yet developed.
- **Dependency versus world systems.** Dependency has two tiers and little mobility; world systems has three tiers and movement between them.
- **Modernisation versus neoliberalism.** Both favour capitalism, but modernisation backs aid and state planning, while neoliberalism wants the state to step back.
- **NGOs versus international agencies.** NGOs are non-profit and independent; agencies such as the World Bank are funded and governed by states.
- **Humanitarian versus development aid.** Emergency relief is far less disputed than long-term state-to-state aid.
- **Women in Development versus Gender and Development.** WID adds women to existing projects; GAD challenges the gender power relations behind inequality.
- **Cultural imperialism versus glocalisation.** One sees Western culture replacing local culture; the other sees global products adapted locally.

## Mini essay plan

*Typical extended question:* "Evaluate the view that transnational corporations do more to help than to harm development."

1. **Introduction (short).** Define TNCs and development. State the debate: modernisation and neoliberal theorists see benefit; dependency and world systems theorists see exploitation.
2. **For the view (modernisation, neoliberalism).** Jobs, skills, technology, tax, links to world markets. Apply to an export processing zone. Analyse: factory wages may exceed farm incomes.
3. **Against (dependency, world systems).** Profit repatriation, low wages, weak safety standards; Rana Plaza (2013) as evidence about supply chains. Sklair: elites benefit across borders.
4. **Gender angle.** Elson and Pearson: women hired as cheap labour, yet earnings can shift household power. Links to the stratification core theme.
5. **Evaluation of evidence.** Average wage data can hide unsafe conditions; case studies may not generalise. Mention research design.
6. **Judgement.** Effects depend on regulation, sector and state strength. Reach your own reasoned conclusion and support it.

## Quick self-test

1. Name Rostow's third stage of growth.
2. What three dimensions make up the HDI?
3. What did Frank call the rich and poor nations in his model?
4. What does the semi-periphery add to dependency theory?
5. Give one feature of structural adjustment programmes.
6. Define tied aid.
7. Name two of Collier's four traps.
8. What is over-urbanisation?
9. Give the Brundtland definition of sustainable development in your own words.
10. What is the difference between WID and GAD?
11. What did Hirst and Thompson argue about globalisation?
12. Which option of Paper 2 contains Global Development?

### Answers

1. Take-off.
2. A long and healthy life (life expectancy), knowledge (years of schooling) and standard of living (GNI per capita).
3. The metropolis and satellites.
4. A middle tier, and the idea that countries can move between tiers.
5. Any one: privatisation, trade liberalisation, cuts to public spending, as conditions for loans.
6. Aid that must be spent on the donor's goods or services.
7. Any two: the conflict trap; the natural resource trap; landlocked with bad neighbours; bad governance in a small country.
8. Cities growing faster than formal jobs and housing, producing informal settlements.
9. Meeting today's needs without damaging the ability of future generations to meet theirs.
10. WID adds women to development projects; GAD challenges the power relations that produce gender inequality.
11. That it is exaggerated: most firms remain nationally based and regulation is still possible.
12. Option 2, answered in Section B.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a theory and never applying it to the question or the source material.
- Treating modernisation and neoliberal theory as the same thing.
- Quoting HDI or GNI as if they measured everything; say what each leaves out.
- Using "Africa" or "the developing world" as one case instead of naming specific contexts.
- Evaluating only with "some sociologists disagree" instead of naming the rival theory and its evidence.
- Ignoring gender, even when the question invites it; the specification lists gender as an aspect of development.
- No link to the core themes or to how evidence was gathered (research design).
- Writing a one-sided answer to an "evaluate" question with no judgement at the end.
