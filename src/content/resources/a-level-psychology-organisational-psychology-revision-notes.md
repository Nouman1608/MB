**These notes condense Specialist Option 4, Organisational Psychology, of Cambridge International A Level Psychology 9990 (syllabus for 2024 to 2026).** The option has five topics: motivation to work, leadership and management, group behaviour, work conditions, and satisfaction at work. Much of the marks come from naming the parts of a model accurately and applying them to a workplace in the question.

For full explanations read the [Organisational Psychology study guide](/resources/a-level-psychology-organisational-psychology/), then try the [Organisational Psychology practice questions](/resources/a-level-psychology-organisational-psychology-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Extrinsic motivator | A reward from outside the task, e.g. pay, bonus, profit-sharing |
| Intrinsic motivator | A reward from the work or social recognition, e.g. praise, empowerment |
| Transformational leader | Inspires followers to put group goals above self-interest |
| Adaptive challenge | A problem with no technical fix that requires people to change |
| Followership | Actively supporting a leader and the group's goals |
| Groupthink | Cohesive group puts agreement above critical evaluation |
| Social facilitation | Better performance on simple tasks when others are present |
| Social loafing | Less individual effort when working in a group |
| Evaluation apprehension | Arousal caused by fear of being judged |
| Hawthorne effect | Behaviour changes because people know they are studied |
| Rapid / slow rotation | Shifts change every few days / every few weeks |
| Flexitime | Choosing start and finish times around core hours |
| Token economy | Tokens for desired behaviour exchanged for rewards |
| Job enrichment | Adding responsibility and control (vertical) |
| Job enlargement | Adding more tasks at the same level (horizontal) |
| Sabotage | Deliberate damage to an organisation's property, output or reputation |

## Key studies at a glance

| Study | Focus | Core idea | Evaluate with |
|---|---|---|---|
| Landry et al. (2019) | Lab experiments on cash rewards (study 1) | Effect of money depends on whether it supports or frustrates competence and autonomy | Controlled; small lab rewards differ from salaries |
| Cuadrado et al. (2008) | Experiment varying leader gender and style | Evaluation of a style depends on leader's gender | Controlled; written descriptions are artificial |
| Claypoole and Szalma (2019) | Vigilance task under monitoring (experiment 1) | Electronic monitoring can produce social facilitation effects | Lab control; short task |
| Swat (1997) | Monitoring accidents and near misses in industrial plants | Recording risk events gives fuller picture of risk | Objective records over time; one context |
| Giacalone and Rosenfeld (1987) | Employees judge reasons for sabotage | Some reasons, such as responses to unfair treatment, seen as more justifiable | Self-report attitudes, not actual behaviour |

## Supporting studies to name

- Saeednia (2011) – a scale measuring Maslow's needs.
- Earley (1993) – collectivists loafed with out-groups, performed best with in-groups; individualists best alone.
- Einarsen (1999) – bullying: predatory vs dispute-related; phases and causes.
- Kompier (2006) – the Hawthorne story as a myth.
- Oldham and Brass (1979) – satisfaction and motivation fell after a move to open-plan.
- Gold et al. (1992) – rotating-shift nurses reported more sleep problems and sleepiness-related accidents.
- Fox et al. (1987) – token economy in open-pit mines cut lost days.

## Mini essay plan: "Evaluate theories of job satisfaction" [10]

1. **Herzberg**: useful split of hygiene vs motivators; but based on critical-incident interviews, where people may credit themselves for good events and blame the job for bad (self-serving bias).
2. **Hackman and Oldham**: specific, measurable characteristics that guide job design; reductionist to five features.
3. **Individual differences**: not everyone wants enrichment; some prefer simple, stable jobs.
4. **Cultural differences**: what counts as a motivator may differ across cultures.
5. **Application**: both lead to job design techniques (enrichment, rotation, enlargement).
6. **Conclusion**: complementary; Hackman and Oldham is more testable.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Swapping **instrumentality** and **expectancy** in Vroom: expectancy is effort to performance; instrumentality is performance to reward.
- Calling all extrinsic rewards bad; self-determination theory says it depends on whether they feel controlling.
- Mixing Muczyk and Reimann's two dimensions: decision participation (autocratic/democratic) vs execution (directive/permissive).
- Writing "Ohio University" theory content as Michigan, and vice versa.
- Confusing **enlargement** (more tasks) with **enrichment** (more responsibility).
- Describing Swat (1997) without the idea of near misses or risk events.

## Quick self-test

1. What three beliefs make up Vroom's VIE theory?
2. Name Scouller's three levels of leadership.
3. Which Kelley follower style is low on both dimensions?
4. What does social impact theory say affects the impact of others?
5. What are the four types of human error?
6. Which job characteristic produces knowledge of results?
7. Give the five facets of the JDI.
8. In Blau and Boal's model, what is a "lone wolf"?

**Answers:** (1) expectancy, instrumentality, valence; (2) public, private, personal; (3) passive; (4) strength, immediacy and number of sources; (5) omission, commission, sequencing, timing; (6) feedback; (7) work itself, pay, promotion, supervision, co-workers; (8) an employee high in job involvement but low in organisational commitment.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many leadership models does the 9990 syllabus name?**
Several: universalist theories (great person, charismatic, transformational), Ohio and Michigan behavioural explanations, Heifetz, Muczyk and Reimann, Scouller and Kouzes and Posner, plus Kelley's followership.

**Do I need dates for the theorists?**
Dates are useful but marks come from describing the model accurately and applying it. Learn the dates the syllabus prints for its named studies.
