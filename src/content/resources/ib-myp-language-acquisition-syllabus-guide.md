This guide covers the full framework of **IB Middle Years Programme
Language Acquisition**, from 2020. It complements the
[subject overview](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-subject-guide/)
and the
[assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-assessment-revision-notes/)
already on the site by setting out the four assessment criteria and the
proficiency-level structure that shape how the subject is actually
examined -- not just its aims.

The ability to communicate in more than one language sits close to the
centre of the IB's wider mission of promoting intercultural
understanding, and this subject is where that mission is most directly
taught: acquiring an additional language, and reflecting on the
cultural perspectives of one's own and other communities, is treated as
central to developing critical thinking and international-mindedness,
not as a separate add-on skill. MYP Language Acquisition is compulsory
in every year of the programme,
except for bilingual students pursuing courses of study in multiple
languages within the MYP Language and Literature subject group instead.
The MYP requires at least 50 hours of teaching time for the subject in
each year of the programme, rising to a recommended 70 hours of guided
learning per year in MYP years 4 and 5 for students pursuing MYP
eAssessment.

## Key and related concepts

The framework is organised around key concepts of **communication,
connections, creativity and culture**, explored through related
concepts such as word choice, conventions and idiom. As in every MYP
subject group, students explore these through the same six global
contexts -- identities and relationships, orientation in space and
time, personal and cultural expression, scientific and technical
innovation, globalization and sustainability, and fairness and
development.

## The four assessment criteria

- **Criterion A: Listening** -- interpreting and constructing meaning
  from spoken multimodal text, understanding how images and other
  spatial elements presented alongside oral text convey ideas, values
  and attitudes.
- **Criterion B: Reading** -- constructing meaning and interpreting
  written, spatial and visual aspects of texts.
- **Criterion C: Speaking** -- developing communication skills by
  interacting on topics of personal, local and global interest,
  applying linguistic and literary concepts with increasing skill and
  effectiveness.
- **Criterion D: Writing** -- recognising and using language suitable
  to audience and purpose (home, classroom, formal/informal, social and
  academic registers), applying understanding of language, form, mode
  and medium to express ideas creatively and meaningfully.

Each criterion carries equal weight and is scored on eight achievement
levels (1-8), grouped into four descriptor bands -- the same
level-and-band structure used across every MYP subject group. Below the
lowest band, a level of 0 is awarded for work that does not reach any
descriptor, so the attainable range for each criterion is 0-8.

## Six teaching phases, three reporting levels

Teaching and learning in MYP Language Acquisition is organised into **six phases** forming a
continuum. Phases do not correspond to age or MYP year group -- students can begin at any phase
depending on prior experience, and move between phases individually, at their own pace, as their
proficiency actually develops.

Separately, where a school offers MYP eAssessment, a student's course results are formally reported at
one of three proficiency **levels** -- **emergent, capable or proficient** -- reflecting how far a
student has actually progressed in the target language, not simply which year group they are in. This
is a coarser, summative categorisation used for reporting eAssessment results, distinct from the finer
six-phase continuum used for ongoing classroom teaching. This level-based structure matters for
revision: preparing for the correct proficiency level's expectations is more important than preparing
generically for "the exam", since the complexity examined differs meaningfully between levels, and a
student assessed as emergent is not expected to demonstrate the same range of vocabulary, structure or
spontaneous fluency as one assessed as proficient.

## Two mark scales -- do not mix them up

This subject uses **two different numeric scales**, and confusing them is the single most
common mistake in revision material about assessment structure:

- **Classroom assessment** uses the eight achievement levels (1-8) described above for each
  criterion (plus level 0 below the lowest band), so a student's classroom total across all four
  criteria is out of **32**, with an attainable range of 0-32.
- **The eAssessment blueprint** (below) allocates **32 marks** to each criterion: 96 marks
  for the on-screen examination (criteria A, B and D) and 32 for the speaking examination
  (criterion C). The distribution of marks may vary by no more than three marks from the
  blueprint. The figure 32 therefore means something different on each scale: a classroom total
  of the four 0-8 levels, or the marks for a single criterion in the eAssessment.

## MYP eAssessment structure

Students seeking MYP course results or the MYP certificate demonstrate
achievement through **two separate components**:

- An **end-of-course on-screen examination**, covering receptive and
  productive skills through three tasks of about 35 minutes each (1 hour
  45 minutes in all, 96 marks). Tasks 1 and 2 each pair a written-visual
  text with an audio-visual text and assess listening to spoken
  multimodal texts (criterion A, 16 marks per task) and reading written
  multimodal texts (criterion B, 16 marks per task) equally. Task 3 is
  writing in response to multimodal texts (criterion D, 32 marks). Criteria
  A, B and D are therefore worth 32 marks each.
- An **internally assessed, IB-moderated speaking examination**
  (criterion C, 32 marks, 15 to 17 minutes including preparation time), an interactive summative task marked by the
  student's own classroom teacher and externally moderated by the IB
  from a sample of each school's students.

Because speaking is assessed separately from the other three skills,
strong performance on the written on-screen paper does not substitute
for oral preparation -- all four criteria are independently marked and
independently weighted, each worth an equal 32 marks in the eAssessment
blueprint, so no single skill can be allowed to dominate revision time
at the expense of the others.

The marks and timings above come from the Middle Years Programme Language acquisition guide, for use
from September 2020/January 2021 ("MYP eAssessment" and "Language acquisition examination blueprint",
pages 51 and 52). Confirm the arrangements for your sitting with your school.

## How to approach it

Because listening, reading and writing are assessed together in a
single on-screen paper while speaking is assessed separately as its
own interactive task, build a genuinely separate revision routine for
oral fluency and interaction -- rehearsing spontaneous, interactive
speaking under time pressure, not just prepared monologues -- alongside
written and receptive-skills practice, rather than assuming
comprehensive written revision automatically prepares you for the
speaking component too. Because MYP language acquisition teaching is
organised into six phases rather than by year group, and eAssessment
results are reported at one of three proficiency levels (emergent,
capable or proficient), confirm with your teacher which phase you are
working in and which level your own assessment will actually target,
since practising with material pitched at the wrong level either
underprepares or wastes revision time.
MYP language acquisition examination tasks are explicitly aligned with
the skills needed for high levels of achievement in both DP Language
Acquisition and DP Studies in Language and Literature courses, so
strong criterion-by-criterion habits built here -- particularly writing
for a specified audience and purpose, and confident, unscripted
speaking -- transfer directly into whichever DP language pathway a
student later chooses.

The IB's grading and awards page says the IB MYP certificate and the
**IB MYP bilingual certificate** are available to students who complete
the IB's broad and balanced course of study, but it does not set out
separate qualifying conditions for the bilingual certificate. Students should confirm those
conditions with their school's MYP coordinator rather than assuming
whether this subject counts towards it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many teaching hours does IB MYP Language Acquisition need?**
The MYP requires at least 50 hours of teaching time for the subject in each year of the programme, rising to a recommended 70 hours of guided learning per year in MYP years 4 and 5 for students pursuing eAssessment.

**What is the difference between the classroom mark scale and the eAssessment marks?**
Classroom assessment uses achievement levels 0–8 for each criterion, giving a total out of 32. The eAssessment blueprint allocates 32 marks to each criterion (96 marks for the on-screen examination covering A, B and D, and 32 for speaking), and the distribution of marks can vary by no more than three marks from the blueprint.

**Is MYP Language Acquisition compulsory?**
Yes, in every year of the programme, except for bilingual students pursuing courses in multiple languages within the MYP Language and Literature subject group.

**What are the global contexts in MYP Language Acquisition?**
As in every MYP subject group, students explore concepts through six global contexts: identities and relationships, orientation in space and time, personal and cultural expression, scientific and technical innovation, globalization and sustainability, and fairness and development.
