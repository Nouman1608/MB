These questions cover subtopic 3.6 Parallel lines of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The statement is the same for both tiers. Questions marked **(Extended)** also need a line in the form ax + by = c or a gradient calculated from coordinates, which are Extended skills. ## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) Write down the gradient of a line parallel to y = –2x + 7. **[1]**

**2.** (non-calculator) Write down the equation of the line parallel to y = 4x + 1 that crosses the y-axis at (0, –6). **[1]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Find the equation of the line parallel to y = –3x + 5 that passes through (1, 4). **[2]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Write down the letters of the two lines that are parallel to y = ½x + 2.

A: y = 2x + ½

B: y = ½x – 9

C: y = –½x + 2

D: y = 0.5x **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) Write down the equation of the line through (–2, 9) that is

**(a)** parallel to the x-axis, **[1]**

**(b)** parallel to the y-axis. **[1]**

**6.** (non-calculator) The line y = (3k – 2)x + 1 is parallel to the line y = 10x – 4. Find the value of k. **[2]**

**7.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Find the gradient of a line parallel to 5x – 2y = 3. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** (non-calculator) Line L has equation y = 2x – 3.

**(a)** Find the equation of the line parallel to L that passes through (4, 1). **[2]**

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where your line crosses the x-axis. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that (5, 3) lies on your line. **[1]**

**9.** (non-calculator) A straight line drawn on a grid passes through the grid points (0, 1) and (2, 5). Find the equation of the line parallel to it that passes through (–1, –4). **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A line is parallel to 3x + 4y = 12 and passes through (–4, 5).

**(a)** Find its equation in the form ax + by = c. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the y-intercept of this line. **[1]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A quadrilateral has vertices A(0, 0), B(6, 3), C(4, 5) and D(0, 3). Use gradients to show that ABCD is a trapezium. **[4]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A line passes through (1, 2) and (3, 8). Find the equation of the line parallel to it that passes through (–2, 1). **[3]**

**13.** (non-calculator) The lines y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x – 4 cross the y-axis at P and Q.

**(a)** Find the distance PQ. **[1]**

**(b)** A third line, parallel to both, crosses the y-axis at the midpoint of PQ. Write down its equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the lines y = 2x + 1 and y = 2x – 4 never meet. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **–2** [1]

**2.** **y = 4x – 6** [1]

**3.** 4 = –3(1) + c, so c = 7 [1]; **y = –3x + 7** [1]

*Examiner insight:* y = –3x + 5 keeps the old intercept and does not pass through (1, 4); substitute the point to check.

**4.** **B** [1] and **D** [1]

*Examiner insight:* D, y = 0.5x, has gradient 0.5 = ½ even though it passes through the origin. C has the opposite gradient.

**5. (a)** **y = 9** [1]

**(b)** **x = –2** [1]

**6.** 3k – 2 = 10 [1]; **k = 4** [1]

**7.** –2y = 3 – 5x, so y = 2.5x – 1.5 [1]; gradient **5/2** (2.5) [1]

**8. (a)** 1 = 2(4) + c, so c = –7 [1]; **y = 2x – 7** [1]

**(b)** 2x – 7 = 0, so x = 3.5: **(3.5, 0)** [1]

**(c)** 2(5) – 7 = 10 – 7 = 3, so (5, 3) lies on the line [1]

**9.** Gradient = (5 – 1)/2 = 2 [1]; –4 = 2(–1) + c, so c = –2 [1]; **y = 2x – 2** [1]

**10. (a)** 3x + 4y = d with 3(–4) + 4(5) = d [1]; **3x + 4y = 8** [1]

**(b)** x = 0 gives 4y = 8: **2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* rearranging to y = –0.75x + 2 is an equally valid method and gives the same intercept.

**11.** Gradient AB = 3/6 = ½ [1]; gradient DC = (5 – 3)/(4 – 0) = ½ [1]; gradient BC = (5 – 3)/(4 – 6) = –1, and AD is vertical, so BC and AD are not parallel [1]; exactly one pair of parallel sides, so ABCD is a trapezium [1]

*Examiner insight:* showing one pair is parallel is not enough for a trapezium; you must also show the other pair is not parallel, or the shape could be a parallelogram.

**12.** Gradient = (8 – 2)/(3 – 1) = 3 [1]; 1 = 3(–2) + c, so c = 7 [1]; **y = 3x + 7** [1]

**13. (a)** P(0, 1) and Q(0, –4): PQ = **5 units** [1]

**(b)** Midpoint of PQ is (0, –1.5): **y = 2x – 1.5** [1]

**(c)** They have the **same gradient (2)** but **different y-intercepts**, so they are parallel and never meet [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Keeping the given line's intercept.
- Using the perpendicular gradient instead of the same gradient.
- Reading the gradient of ax + by = c without rearranging.
- Confusing lines parallel to the x-axis with lines parallel to the y-axis.
- Concluding "trapezium" after checking only one pair of sides.
