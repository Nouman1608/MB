These questions cover sub-topic 2(a) Group 1 (alkali metals) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024): points 2.1 to 2.4C. Questions marked **(Paper 2 only)** test point 2.4C or use the molar volume from point 1.35C. Revise with the [Group 1 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-group-1-alkali-metals/) and the [Group 1 revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-group-1-alkali-metals-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the products formed when sodium reacts with water. **[2]**

**2.** State why the Group 1 elements are called the alkali metals. **[1]**

**3.** A piece of lithium is cut with a knife. Describe how the cut surface changes over a few minutes and explain the change. **[2]**

**4.** Give the formula of potassium hydroxide and the charge on the potassium ion. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** safety precautions when a teacher demonstrates potassium reacting with water. **[2]**

**6.** Put lithium, potassium and sodium in order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A teacher adds a small piece of potassium to a trough of water containing a few drops of universal indicator.

**(a)** Describe **four** observations. **[4]**
**(b)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for the reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the potassium melts during the reaction. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how the reactions of lithium, sodium and potassium with water show that they belong to the same family of elements. **[3]**

**9.** The table gives information about some alkali metals.

| Metal | Melting point / °C | Reaction with water |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 | Fizzes steadily |
| Sodium | 98 | Melts and fizzes rapidly |
| Potassium | 63 | Lilac flame |
| Rubidium | 39 | ? |
| Caesium | ? | ? |

**(a)** Describe the trend in melting point. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the melting point of caesium. **[1]**
**(c)** Predict how rubidium reacts with water. **[1]**
**(d)** Write the formula of the compound formed when caesium reacts with chlorine, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** 1.17 g of potassium reacts completely with water. (Ar: K = 39, O = 16, H = 1)

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of potassium. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of potassium hydroxide formed. **[2]**
**(c)** **(Paper 2 only)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen produced at rtp, in cm³. (Molar volume = 24 000 cm³ at rtp.) **[2]**

**11.** **(Paper 2 only)** Lithium has the electronic configuration 2,1 and potassium 2,8,8,1. Explain why potassium is more reactive than lithium. **[4]**

**12.** A student is given three unlabelled alkali metals, X, Y and Z, which are lithium, sodium and potassium in some order. Describe how she could identify them using only their reactions with water and air. In your answer, state the results you would expect. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Sodium hydroxide [1] and hydrogen [1].

**2.** They react with water to form alkaline solutions (of their hydroxides) [1].

**3.** It starts shiny but becomes dull [1] because it reacts with oxygen in the air to form an oxide layer (it tarnishes) [1].

**4.** KOH [1]; 1+ [1].

**5.** Any two [1 each]: use a very small piece; use a safety screen; wear eye protection; handle the metal with tongs, not fingers; keep the audience at a distance.

**6.** Potassium, sodium, lithium [1].

**7. (a)** Any four [1 each]: floats; fizzes / bubbles; moves quickly over the surface; melts into a ball; lilac flame; gets smaller and disappears; indicator turns purple / blue.
**(b)** 2K(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g): correct formulae and balancing [1]; correct state symbols [1].
**(c)** The reaction gives out heat (is exothermic) and potassium has a low melting point [1].
*Marking note:* "It bursts into flames" alone does not score in (a); the lilac colour is the observation.

**8.** All three react with water in the same way [1]. All produce a metal hydroxide and hydrogen [1]. All form an alkaline solution / form 1+ ions, so the equations have the same form [1].

**9. (a)** Melting point decreases down the group [1].
**(b)** Any value from 20 °C to 35 °C [1].
**(c)** More violently than potassium / explosively [1].
**(d)** CsCl [1]. Caesium forms Cs⁺, like the other Group 1 ions, and chloride is Cl⁻ [1].

**10. (a)** 1.17 ÷ 39 = 0.030 mol [1].
**(b)** Mr KOH = 39 + 16 + 1 = 56 [1]. Mass = 0.030 × 56 = 1.68 g [1].
**(c)** Moles of H₂ = 0.030 ÷ 2 = 0.015 mol [1]. Volume = 0.015 × 24 000 = 360 cm³ [1].

**11.** Potassium has more occupied shells, so its outer electron is further from the nucleus [1]. There is more shielding by inner electrons [1]. So the attraction between the nucleus and the outer electron is weaker [1], and the outer electron is lost more easily [1].

**12.** One mark per point, up to 6:
- Cut a small piece of each and watch the fresh surfaces [1].
- The surface that dulls fastest is potassium; the one that stays shiny longest is lithium [1].
- Add a small piece of each to water (with a safety screen) [1].
- Lithium fizzes steadily and does not melt [1].
- Sodium melts into a ball and fizzes rapidly [1].
- Potassium reacts most violently and gives a lilac flame [1].
*Marking note:* Both lines of evidence should agree; an answer using only the water test can still reach full marks if each metal's result is given.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "the metal dissolves" instead of reacts.
- Missing state symbols when asked for.
- Forgetting to halve the moles of metal for hydrogen.
- Stopping the reactivity explanation at "more shells".
- Predicting a melting point that rises down the group.
