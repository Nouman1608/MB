## The two Core definitions

> **Sexual reproduction** is a process involving the **fusion of the nuclei of two gametes** to form a **zygote** and the production of offspring that are **genetically different from each other**.

> **Fertilisation** is the **fusion of the nuclei of gametes**.

Read each phrase carefully, because each is a separate idea.

- **Gametes** are sex cells. In humans they are sperm and egg cells. In flowering plants the male nucleus is carried in the pollen grain and the female nucleus is in the ovule.
- **Fusion of the nuclei**, not just of the cells, is the key event. When a sperm reaches an egg, the cells meet, but fertilisation is the moment the two nuclei join.
- The **zygote** is the single cell formed by fertilisation. It divides to form an embryo.
- **Genetically different from each other**: the offspring of one pair of parents are not identical to one another, and not identical to either parent.

### Fertilisation in animals and plants

| | Humans | Flowering plants |
|---|---|---|
| Male gamete | sperm | nucleus in the pollen grain |
| Female gamete | egg cell | nucleus in the ovule |
| Where nuclei fuse | in the oviduct | inside the ovule |
| What follows | zygote → embryo → fetus | zygote → embryo inside a seed |

Sub-topics 16.3 and 16.4 add the details for plants and for humans. In 16.2 you need the general idea that applies to both.

## Haploid and diploid (Extended only)

- A **haploid** nucleus contains a **single set** of chromosomes. Gamete nuclei are haploid.
- A **diploid** nucleus contains **two sets** of chromosomes. The zygote nucleus is diploid, and so are the body cells that grow from it.

Fertilisation joins one set from each parent:

```
haploid gamete + haploid gamete  →  diploid zygote
   1 set       +     1 set       →     2 sets
```

### Worked example 1: chromosome numbers in four species

| Species | Body cell (diploid) | Gamete (haploid) | Zygote (diploid) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Human | 46 | 46 ÷ 2 = **23** | 23 + 23 = **46** |
| Fruit fly | 8 | **4** | **8** |
| Garden pea | 14 | **7** | **14** |
| Dog | 78 | **39** | **78** |

Two checks catch most errors. A gamete number is always half the body-cell number, and a zygote number always equals the body-cell number.

### Why gametes must be haploid

If gametes contained the full diploid number, a human zygote would have 46 + 46 = 92 chromosomes, and the number would double in every generation. Because each gamete carries one set, the zygote has two sets, and the chromosome number of the species stays the same from one generation to the next. Gametes are made by meiosis, the reduction division in sub-topic 17.3.

## Why the offspring are genetically different

Two features of sexual reproduction produce differences between offspring:

1. Each parent makes many gametes, and these gametes **carry different combinations of alleles**.
2. **Which** male gamete fuses with **which** female gamete is a matter of chance.

So each zygote gets a new combination of alleles, half from each parent. This is why brothers and sisters, apart from identical twins, differ from each other. The variation topic (18.1) lists meiosis, random mating and random fertilisation as sources of genetic variation.

## Advantages and disadvantages (Extended only)

The key idea behind every advantage is **variation**. The key idea behind most disadvantages is that sexual reproduction is **slower and depends on gametes meeting**.

### (a) A population of a species in the wild

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Offspring vary, so some may survive if the environment changes, for example a new disease, predator or climate. | Usually needs two parents, so time and energy are spent finding a mate. |
| The population can adapt over generations by natural selection. | Plants may depend on pollinators or wind for pollination. |
| Seeds and many animal young can spread to new areas, reducing competition with the parents. | Slower than asexual reproduction, so populations grow more slowly. |
| | A parent well suited to its environment does not pass on its exact combination of features. |

### (b) Crop production

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Breeders can cross two varieties to combine useful features, such as high yield and disease resistance, in new varieties. | Offspring vary, so a crop grown from seed is less uniform in yield, size or ripening time. |
| Variation means some plants may resist a new disease, so the whole crop is less likely to be lost. | A good combination of features may not be passed on intact. |
| Seeds can be stored for long periods and transported easily. | Depends on pollination; poor weather or few pollinators can reduce seed set. |
| | Slower: plants must flower, be pollinated and set seed. |

### Annotated model answer

*A species of wild flower reproduces sexually. Its habitat is becoming warmer and drier. Discuss whether sexual reproduction is an advantage to this population.* **[4]**

> Sexual reproduction produces offspring that are genetically different from each other [1]. Some offspring may have features that help them survive in warmer, drier conditions, so they survive and reproduce and the population can adapt [1]. However, the plants depend on pollination, which may fail if pollinators are scarce [1], and reproduction is slower than asexual reproduction, so numbers may recover only slowly [1].

Notice how each point is tied to the situation in the question. "Sexual reproduction gives variation" on its own is a fact, not a discussion.

## Comparing the two methods

| Feature | Asexual (16.1) | Sexual (16.2) |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | one | usually two |
| Gametes and fertilisation | none | yes |
| Offspring | genetically identical | genetically different from each other |
| Speed | usually fast | usually slower |
| Ability of population to adapt | low | higher |

Both methods can occur in the same organism. A strawberry plant grows runners and also makes flowers and seeds.

## Common mistakes

- Writing "fertilisation is the fusion of gametes". The syllabus says fusion of the **nuclei** of gametes.
- Saying offspring are genetically different from their parents, but forgetting the syllabus wording "**from each other**". Include both if you can.
- Calling the zygote haploid. It is **diploid**.
- Halving the number twice: a gamete of a species with 14 chromosomes in its body cells has 7, not 3.5.
- Saying sexual reproduction always needs two separate individuals. Self-pollinating plants use gametes from one plant, and it is still sexual.
- Treating "variation" as an automatic advantage without explaining how it helps survival in the context given.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** sexual reproduction: fusion of nuclei, two gametes, zygote, offspring genetically different from each other.
- **State** (Extended): use the words haploid and diploid exactly; numbers help, for example "23 chromosomes in a human gamete".
- **Calculate**: halve for gametes, add two gamete numbers for the zygote, and show both lines.
- **Discuss** (Extended): at least one advantage and one disadvantage, each with a reason, in the context of the question: a wild population or a crop.
