## Definitions to learn exactly

| Term | Definition (specification wording) |
|---|---|
| Diffusion | The spreading out of the particles of any substance in solution, or particles of a gas, resulting in a net movement from an area of higher concentration to an area of lower concentration |
| Osmosis | The diffusion of water from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution through a partially permeable membrane |
| Active transport | Moves substances from a more dilute solution to a more concentrated solution (against a concentration gradient); requires energy from respiration |

Key words that carry marks: **net**, **concentration** (diffusion), **partially permeable membrane** (osmosis),
and **respiration** (active transport).

## 4.1.3.1 Diffusion

**Named examples in the specification:**

- oxygen and carbon dioxide in gas exchange
- urea from cells into the blood plasma, for excretion in the kidney.

**Rate of diffusion goes up when:**

| Factor | Increase rate by | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Concentration gradient | Making it steeper | Bigger net movement per second |
| Temperature | Raising it | Particles move faster |
| Surface area of membrane | Increasing it | More particles cross at once |

### Surface area to volume ratio (SA:V)

```
cube:   SA = 6 × L²        V = L³
cuboid: SA = 2 × (lw + lh + wh)    V = l × w × h
ratio = SA ÷ V, written as  x : 1
```

**Method in steps**

1. Work out total surface area (all six faces).
2. Work out volume.
3. Divide SA by V. Give it as x : 1.
4. Compare: the larger the ratio, the more surface there is for each unit of volume.

**Small reminder:** a cube of side 0.5 cm has SA = 6 × 0.25 = 1.5 cm² and V = 0.125 cm³, so SA:V = **12 : 1**.
Halving the side doubles the ratio.

**Why multicellular organisms need more than their outer surface:**

- Single-celled organism: relatively large SA:V, so diffusion through its surface meets its needs.
- Multicellular organism: small SA:V, so the outer surface is too small and the inner cells are too far away.
- So it needs **exchange surfaces** (large area) and a **transport system** (to carry substances to and from every
  cell).

### Exchange surfaces

**Four features that increase effectiveness** (from the specification):

1. large surface area
2. thin membrane, so a short diffusion path
3. efficient blood supply (animals)
4. ventilation (animals, gas exchange).

Features 3 and 4 work by **keeping the concentration gradient steep**.

| Surface | Area | Short path | Gradient kept by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small intestine | Villi | Wall one cell thick | Capillary network |
| Lungs | Alveoli | Thin alveolar and capillary walls | Blood flow and breathing |
| Gills | Filaments with thin plates | Thin layer between water and blood | Blood flow and water flowing over |
| Roots | Root hair cells | Thin cell wall | Water and ions moving on into the root |
| Leaves | Flat, thin; internal air spaces | Short distance for gases | Stomata open to the air |

## 4.1.3.2 Osmosis

**What happens to plant tissue:**

| Solution compared with cell contents | Net water movement | Mass |
|---|---|---|
| More dilute | Into the cells | Increases |
| Same concentration | None | No change |
| More concentrated | Out of the cells | Decreases |

**Calculations you must be able to do:**

```
percentage change in mass = (final − initial) ÷ initial × 100
rate of water uptake      = mass of water gained ÷ time taken   (e.g. g/min, g/h)
mean                      = sum of repeats ÷ number of repeats
```

- Gains are positive, losses negative. Keep the sign.
- Always divide by the **initial** mass.
- Give units for every rate.

**Small reminder:** 5.00 g → 4.60 g is (4.60 − 5.00) ÷ 5.00 × 100 = **−8.0 %**.

### Required practical activity 3

**Aim:** investigate the effect of a range of concentrations of salt or sugar solutions on the mass of plant
tissue.

**Method in steps**

1. Cut equal-sized cylinders of plant tissue (for example potato) with a cork borer.
2. Blot, then measure the initial mass of each.
3. Put each in a different concentration of solution, same volume, same time, same temperature.
4. Remove, blot the same way, measure the final mass.
5. Calculate percentage change; repeat; find the mean.
6. Plot concentration (x-axis) against percentage change in mass (y-axis); draw a line of best fit.

| Variable | In this practical |
|---|---|
| Independent | Concentration of salt or sugar solution |
| Dependent | Percentage change in mass |
| Control | Tissue type, cylinder size, volume of solution, time, temperature, blotting method |

**Reading the graph:** where the line crosses 0 %, the solution has the same concentration as the cell contents.
Points above zero mean water entered; points below zero mean water left.

**Main source of error:** uneven blotting. Extra surface water raises the final mass.

## 4.1.3.3 Active transport

- Moves substances **against** the concentration gradient (dilute → concentrated).
- Needs **energy from respiration**.

| Example | From | To | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mineral ions into root hair cells | Very dilute soil solution | Root hair cell (higher concentration) | Plants need ions for healthy growth |
| Sugar from the gut into the blood | Lower concentration in the gut | Blood (higher sugar concentration) | Sugar is used for cell respiration |

Less respiration (for example, little oxygen in waterlogged soil) means less active transport.

## Must-know distinctions

| | Diffusion | Osmosis | Active transport |
|---|---|---|---|
| Substance | Any, in solution or a gas | Water only | Solutes such as ions, sugar |
| Gradient | Down | Down (for water) | Against |
| Energy from respiration | No | No | Yes |
| Membrane | Any it can cross | Partially permeable | Cell membrane |

- **Osmosis vs diffusion:** osmosis is a special case of diffusion. Only water moves, and a partially permeable
  membrane is needed.
- **Osmosis vs active transport:** both can be described as "dilute to concentrated solution". In osmosis water
  moves down its own gradient with no energy. In active transport the solute moves up its gradient using energy.
- **Surface area vs SA:V:** a bigger organism has more surface area in total but a smaller ratio.

## Quick self-test

1. State the waste product that diffuses from cells into the blood plasma.
2. Give three factors that affect the rate of diffusion.
3. Calculate the SA:V of a cube of side 3 cm.
4. Which has the larger SA:V, a cube of side 3 cm or a cube of side 0.5 cm?
5. State two features of all effective exchange surfaces.
6. Define osmosis.
7. A piece of tissue goes from 1.60 g to 1.84 g. Calculate the percentage change in mass.
8. A cylinder gains 0.60 g of water in 40 minutes. Calculate the rate of water uptake in g/min.
9. In required practical 3, why is percentage change used rather than change in mass?
10. Why does active transport stop if respiration stops?
11. Give one example of active transport in plants and one in animals.
12. On a graph of percentage change in mass against concentration, what does the point where the line crosses
    zero tell you?

### Answers

1. Urea.
2. Concentration gradient, temperature, surface area of the membrane.
3. SA = 6 × 3² = 54 cm²; V = 3³ = 27 cm³; SA:V = 54 ÷ 27 = **2 : 1**.
4. The 0.5 cm cube (12 : 1 compared with 2 : 1).
5. Any two of: large surface area; thin membrane (short diffusion path); efficient blood supply (animals);
   ventilated (animals, gas exchange).
6. The diffusion of water from a dilute solution to a concentrated solution through a partially permeable
   membrane.
7. (1.84 − 1.60) ÷ 1.60 × 100 = 0.24 ÷ 1.60 × 100 = **+15 %**.
8. 0.60 ÷ 40 = **0.015 g/min**.
9. The pieces start at different masses, so percentage change allows a fair comparison.
10. Active transport needs energy, and that energy comes from respiration.
11. Plants: mineral ions absorbed into root hair cells from the soil. Animals: sugar absorbed from the gut into the
    blood.
12. The concentration of solution that is the same as the concentration of the cell contents (no net movement of
    water).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "concentration" in "from a higher to a lower concentration".
- Saying osmosis is the movement of "particles" or "solute" instead of water.
- Forgetting "partially permeable membrane" in the osmosis definition.
- Writing "active transport uses energy" without "from respiration".
- Using L² instead of 6 × L² for the surface area of a cube.
- Dividing by the final mass in a percentage change calculation, or losing the minus sign for a loss.
- Giving a rate of water uptake with no unit.
- Naming an exchange-surface feature without saying how it speeds up diffusion.
- Saying a larger organism has a "smaller surface area" when the point is a smaller surface area **to volume
  ratio**.
