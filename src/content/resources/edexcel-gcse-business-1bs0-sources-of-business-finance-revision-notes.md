## The specification in brief

Sources of finance for a start-up or established small business:
- short-term sources: overdraft and trade credit;
- long-term sources: personal savings, venture capital, share capital, loans, retained profit and crowd funding.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Short-term finance | Money needed for a short period, usually under a year, often to cover cash-flow gaps |
| Long-term finance | Money needed for years, for start-up costs, equipment, premises or growth |
| Overdraft | Bank permission to spend beyond the current account balance up to a limit; interest on the amount overdrawn |
| Trade credit | Buying from a supplier now and paying later |
| Personal savings | The owner's own money put into the business |
| Loan | A fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed time |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business rather than paid to the owners |
| Share capital | Money raised by selling shares in a company; shareholders become part-owners |
| Venture capital | Investment from a specialist investor in return for a share of the business |
| Crowd funding | Raising money from many people, usually online, in return for rewards, shares or interest depending on the terms |

## Condensed recall

### Choosing: six questions
1. How much is needed?
2. What is it for, and for how long?
3. What does it cost (interest, share of profit, lost use of savings)?
4. Who controls the business afterwards?
5. Is it available to a start-up, or only an established business?
6. What happens if trading goes badly (repayments still due)?

### Quick matches
| Need | Suitable source |
|---|---|
| Temporary cash gap | Overdraft |
| Buying stock | Trade credit |
| Small start-up costs | Personal savings |
| Equipment or a vehicle | Loan or retained profit |
| Large growth plan, owner willing to share | Venture capital or share capital |
| Testing an idea with an enthusiastic audience | Crowd funding |

### Ownership and repayment
- Keeps full ownership: personal savings, loans, retained profit, overdraft, trade credit.
- Gives up some ownership: share capital, venture capital (and some crowd funding).
- Must be repaid with interest: loans, overdraft.

### Calculations
- Interest (%) = (total repayment − borrowed amount) ÷ borrowed amount × 100 (Appendix 3).
- Total repayment from a rate = amount borrowed × (1 + rate ÷ 100).
- Monthly repayment = total repayment ÷ number of months.
- Profit given away = share sold (%) × expected profit.
- Crowd funding shortfall = target − pledges.

Mini example: a £10,000 loan with total repayment of £11,800 over 36 months. Interest = £1,800 ÷ £10,000 × 100 = 18%. Monthly payment = £11,800 ÷ 36 = £327.78.

## Answer frame for choosing a source

These are suggested sentence starters, not specification wording.

- "The business needs £... for ..., which will be used for ... years, so a long-term / short-term source fits."
- "Source X would cost ... (interest or a share of profit), but the owner would keep / lose control."
- "As a start-up / established business, it can / cannot use ... because ..."
- "Overall, source X is better because ..., unless ..."

## Exam traps

- Calling an overdraft long-term, or retained profit short-term.
- Saying a start-up can use retained profit.
- Treating share capital as a loan.
- Forgetting that loan repayments are cash outflows every month.
- Ignoring loss of control when recommending venture capital.
- Naming a source without matching it to the amount and purpose in the case.

## Self-test

1. Name the two short-term sources in the specification.
2. Name the six long-term sources in the specification.
3. Why is an overdraft suitable for a temporary cash-flow gap?
4. Give one disadvantage of trade credit for a new business.
5. Why can a start-up not use retained profit?
6. Give one advantage of a loan over venture capital.
7. Give one advantage of share capital over a loan.
8. A business borrows £15,000 and repays £17,700. Calculate the interest percentage.
9. A business borrows £8,000 at 15% total interest. Calculate the total repayment.
10. An investor takes 20% of a business expected to make £45,000 profit a year. How much profit goes to the investor each year?
11. A crowd funding target is £20,000 and pledges are £14,200. What percentage of the target has been reached?
12. Explain why a seasonal business might prefer venture capital to a loan.

### Answers

1. Overdraft and trade credit.
2. Personal savings, venture capital, share capital, loans, retained profit, crowd funding.
3. The business borrows only what it needs, when it needs it, and pays interest only on the amount overdrawn.
4. Suppliers may refuse credit until the business has a payment record (or late payment damages the relationship).
5. It has not yet made any profit to keep.
6. The owner keeps full ownership and control (and does not share future profit).
7. It does not have to be repaid and there is no interest.
8. (£17,700 − £15,000) ÷ £15,000 × 100 = 18%.
9. £8,000 × 1.15 = £9,200.
10. 20% × £45,000 = £9,000.
11. £14,200 ÷ £20,000 × 100 = 71%.
12. A loan needs fixed monthly repayments even in quiet months with little income, which could cause cash-flow problems; venture capital has no repayments.
