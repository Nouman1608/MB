Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives (0457) asks students to investigate issues that matter across the world, from climate change to the digital world, and to build skills of research, evaluation and reflection that can be used well beyond the course. All candidates complete three components: Component 1, the Written Exam (1 hour 25 minutes, 70 marks, 35%), Component 2, the Individual Report (60 marks, 30%) and Component 3, the Team Project (70 marks, 35%). It suits students who enjoy discussion and independent enquiry. This page sets out how the course is put together and how to plan a route through it.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus lists 22 global topics. They are not taught one after another as units of content to memorise; instead, they provide the contexts in which you practise the skills that the components assess. Grouped loosely by theme, they are:

- **Society and culture:** Arts in society; Change in culture and communities; Education for all; Social identity and inclusion; Sport and recreation; Values and beliefs; Health and wellbeing.
- **Environment and resources:** Climate change, energy and resources; Environment, pollution and conservation; Water, food and agriculture.
- **Economy and work:** Development, trade and aid; Employment; Globalisation; Poverty and inequality; Technology, industry and innovation; Transport, travel and tourism.
- **Power, rights and communication:** Conflict and peace; Law and criminality; Political power and action; Media and communication; Digital world; Migration and urbanisation.

This grouping is ours, offered only to make the list easier to scan; the syllabus itself presents the topics as a single list. Your school will decide which topics are explored in class and when, and you will usually choose a topic yourself for the Individual Report.

## How it's assessed

The course is component-based and not tiered, with grades from A* to G. All three components are compulsory.

| Component | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting | How it is marked |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Component 1 | Written Exam | 1 hour 25 minutes | 70 | 35% | Externally assessed |
| Component 2 | Individual Report | n/a | 60 | 30% | Internally set, externally marked |
| Component 3 | Team Project | n/a | 70 | 35% | Internally assessed, externally moderated |

**Component 1** contains four compulsory source-based questions. **Component 2** is a report of 1500-2000 words on a global issue you choose yourself; the task is set in school but marked by Cambridge examiners. **Component 3** combines a collaborative Team Element with an individually written Reflective Paper of 750-1000 words; it is marked by your teachers and moderated by Cambridge. The syllabus covers examinations from 2025 to 2027.

## How the parts fit together

Each component looks at global issues from a different angle, but the same set of skills runs through all three.

The Written Exam tests those skills under timed conditions using unfamiliar sources: you are asked to identify perspectives, assess the reliability of evidence and judge how well an argument is supported. Practising these questions early helps with every other part of the course, because the habit of questioning a source is just as important when you research your own report.

The Individual Report is where you apply the skills independently. You choose an issue, research it, compare perspectives on it and reach your own supported conclusion. A topic from the list above gives you the starting point, but the marks come from how well you research and evaluate rather than from how much factual content you include.

The Team Project adds collaboration and reflection. You work with others on a shared issue in the Team Element, and then write your own Reflective Paper about your contribution and what you learned. The Reflective Paper rewards honest self-evaluation, so keeping notes during the project is far more useful than trying to reconstruct the process at the end.

Put simply: Component 1 trains the analysis, Component 2 applies it alone, and Component 3 applies it with others while asking you to reflect on how you worked.

## Planning your study

Because two of the three components are produced over time, the course needs steady attention rather than a last-minute push.

A sensible order is to begin with source-analysis skills, using short articles on a range of the 22 topics, so that you become comfortable identifying claims, evidence and perspectives. Once those skills are in place, the Individual Report can be planned: choose an issue that genuinely interests you, check that there is enough varied evidence available, and allow time for drafting and improvement. The Team Project usually runs at a different point in your school's calendar; keep a log of meetings and decisions from the start.

In general terms, the early part of the course is for skill-building and exploring topics, the middle part for the two coursework components, and the final part for revising source analysis ahead of the Written Exam. Start practising Component 1-style questions early and move to full past papers in the final months. For detail on preparing each component, use the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the three components of Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives 0457?**
Component 1 Written Exam (70 marks, 35%), Component 2 Individual Report (60 marks, 30%) and Component 3 Team Project (70 marks, 35%). All candidates take all three.

**Who marks the Individual Report?**
The Individual Report is internally set but externally marked, so Cambridge examiners do the marking.

**How is the Team Project assessed?**
It combines a collaborative Team Element with an individually written Reflective Paper of 750-1000 words. It is internally assessed by your teachers and externally moderated by Cambridge.

**How many global topics are there in 0457?**
The syllabus lists 22 global topics, from Arts in society to Water, food and agriculture. They provide contexts for practising skills rather than content to be learned for its own sake.

**Is IGCSE Global Perspectives tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered and candidates are graded A* to G.
