Study first with the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-japan-politics-and-economy/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-japan-politics-and-economy-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why Japanese leaders resented the outcome of the Washington Naval Conference (1921 to 1922). **[4]**

**2.** Read the extract, which is *constructed for practice* and is not a real quotation.

> "The ministers talk of budgets while the village sells its daughters. Parties and merchants have grown fat. Only a bold act of loyal men can restore the Emperor's rule." (A pamphlet by a nationalist society, constructed for practice)

**(a)** Explain what the extract suggests about the appeal of ultra-nationalism in the early 1930s. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain one limitation of the pamphlet as evidence of public opinion. **[3]**

**3.** Analyse the impact of the Great Depression on Japanese politics. **[6]**

**4.** To what extent was the American embargo the main reason for Japan's decision to attack Pearl Harbor in 1941? **[15]**

**5.** Evaluate the reasons for Japan's "economic miracle" between the 1950s and the early 1970s. **[15]**

**6.** Explain the importance of the Reverse Course in the occupation of Japan. **[6]**

**7.** Explain two social challenges facing Japan between 1989 and 2020. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the reason, 1 for explanation). The 5:5:3 ratio in capital ships was seen as treating Japan as inferior to Britain and the USA, particularly after the rejection of the racial equality clause in 1919; the Four-Power Treaty ended the Anglo-Japanese Alliance, which had protected Japan's position; the return of Shandong to China limited Japan's gains from the First World War; naval officers believed that the limits would leave Japan unable to defend its interests in the western Pacific. **[4]**

**2.** **(a)** The extract suggests that rural distress and anger at corruption made radical solutions attractive (1), that parties and businessmen were blamed for the crisis (1), and that nationalists argued for direct action and imperial rule in place of party government (1). **[3]**
**(b)** Award up to 3. A pamphlet by a nationalist society is propaganda that aims to persuade, so it exaggerates (1); it shows the views of a minority group and cannot tell how many people supported violence (1); many Japanese still voted for party candidates in the early 1930s, and the army's power grew as much from fear and the Manchurian success as from popular belief (1). **[3]**

**3.** Marking points (up to 6): falling silk and rice prices caused rural poverty and anger (up to 2); the return to the gold standard in 1930 worsened the crisis, which discredited party governments (up to 1); the Depression boosted ultra-nationalist groups and the army's claim that expansion on the mainland was necessary (up to 2); recovery under Takahashi came quickly, but his budget cuts to the military led to his assassination in 1936 (up to 1). **[6]**

**4.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis and criteria for "main reason" (up to 2).
- The embargo: US restrictions from 1940, the freezing of assets in July 1941 and the oil halt in August 1941; Japan's dependence on imported oil (up to 4).
- Other factors: the war in China and the army's refusal to withdraw, the Tripartite Pact, the Co-Prosperity Sphere and the plan to seize resources in Southeast Asia, the belief that the US fleet could be crippled (up to 4).
- Political factors: army and navy rivalry, the fall of the moderate Konoe government, Tōjō's ascent (up to 2).
- Perspectives: historians who say Japan was forced and those who say its leaders chose war (up to 1).
- A reasoned judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**5.** Marking points (up to 15):
- A thesis that ranks reasons (up to 2).
- MITI and government policy: protection of industries, guidance of investment, technology imports (up to 3).
- Business and society: high savings, investment, education, company loyalty, the keiretsu and main banks (up to 3).
- External factors: US aid and the Korean War boom, access to the US market, low defence spending under Article 9, cheap oil (up to 3).
- Political stability under the LDP from 1955 and the Income Doubling Plan (up to 1).
- Perspectives: Johnson's developmental-state view and its critics (up to 1).
- A judgement (up to 2). **[15]**

**6.** Marking points (up to 6): the Cold War and the communist victory in China changed US priorities from reform to stability (up to 2); the purge of wartime leaders was eased, some returned to public life, and radical unions were curbed (up to 1); the Dodge Line and the Korean War boom helped recovery and the zaibatsu were not fully broken up (up to 2); it led to the Security Treaty and a strong conservative state, so critics say reforms were left incomplete (up to 1). **[6]**

**7.** Any two, each developed (1 mark for the challenge, 1 for explanation). An ageing society, with a large share of people over 65, put pressure on pensions and health care; a falling birth rate, about 1.4, reduced the workforce and the school-age population; a work culture of long hours led to karōshi and strained family life; fewer secure jobs for young people after the bubble, with more part-time and temporary work. **[4]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Is "economic miracle" the right term?**
It is used in the guide. Show that you understand it as a judgement and test it, for example by noting that growth also had costs such as pollution.

**How far should I include the 1930s in essays on the economy?**
Include them only if the question covers that period, such as the Takahashi recovery.
