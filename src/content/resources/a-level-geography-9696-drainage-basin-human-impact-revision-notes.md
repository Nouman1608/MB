Condensed recall for **1.4 The human impact** on drainage basins: flood risk and its management. For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flood risk | Likelihood of flooding combined with the exposure of people and property |
| Hard engineering | Built structures that control the river |
| Soft engineering | Working with natural processes and land-use planning |
| Floodplain zoning | Restricting what can be built on land likely to flood |
| Afforestation | Planting trees, to increase interception and slow runoff |
| Washland | Floodplain deliberately left to store flood water |

## How people increase flood risk

- **Urbanisation:** impermeable surfaces and drains cut infiltration and speed runoff, giving a shorter lag time and higher peak.
- **Deforestation:** less interception and transpiration, more overland flow, and soil erosion that silts channels.
- **Agriculture:** compaction, ploughing down slopes and removing hedges.
- **Channel changes and building on floodplains:** put more people and property in harm's way.
- **Draining wetlands:** removes natural storage.

## Management strategies

| Strategy | Type | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Dams and reservoirs | Hard | Store flood water, may generate power | Costly, trap sediment, affect habitats |
| Embankments / levées | Hard | Raise channel capacity | Can raise flood height downstream; failure is severe |
| Channel straightening or deepening | Hard | Moves water away faster | Passes the problem downstream |
| Flood relief channels | Hard | Divert excess water | Costly, land needed |
| Floodplain zoning | Soft | Cheap, reduces exposure | Needs enforcement; limits development |
| Afforestation | Soft | Reduces runoff | Slow to take effect |
| Washlands and wetland restoration | Soft | Natural storage, habitats | Land taken from other uses |
| Forecasting and warnings | Soft | Allow evacuation | Does not stop flooding |

## Required case study

The syllabus asks for a **recent river flood event**: its causes, its impacts on people and the environment, and an evaluation of attempts to reduce its impact. Prepare one named event with your own checked facts.

## Exam traps

- Floods have both human and physical causes; separate them.
- Evaluate: who benefits, who pays, and does the scheme just move the problem?
- Do not claim hard engineering always fails or soft always works.

3. Why may channel straightening be criticised?
4. How does afforestation reduce flood risk?
5. What is a washland?
