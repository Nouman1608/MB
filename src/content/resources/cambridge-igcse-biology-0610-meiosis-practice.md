## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** *(Extended)* Which process is meiosis involved in? **[1]**

A: growth of a seedling

B: production of gametes

C: repair of a broken bone

D: replacement of skin cells

**2.** *(Extended)* What happens to the chromosome number during meiosis? **[1]**

A: it doubles

B: it halves, from diploid to haploid

C: it halves, from haploid to diploid

D: it stays the same

**3.** *(Extended)* Name the organ in a human female in which meiosis takes place. **[1]**

**4.** *(Extended)* The body cells of a species contain 24 chromosomes. State the number of chromosomes in each of its gametes. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the term for a nucleus that contains a single set of chromosomes. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State **two** ways in which the cells produced by meiosis differ from the cells produced by mitosis. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** *(Extended)* A diploid cell contains four pairs of chromosomes, labelled pair A, pair B, pair C and pair D.

**(a)** State the number of chromosomes in a gamete produced from this cell by meiosis. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe which chromosomes the gamete contains. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why a gamete containing both chromosomes of pair A, but no chromosome from pair D, would be abnormal. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A species of flowering plant has 20 chromosomes in each leaf cell. Complete the table. **[4]**

| Cell or nucleus | Number of chromosomes |
|---|---|
| Leaf cell | 20 |
| Nucleus in a pollen grain | (i) |
| Female gamete nucleus in an ovule | (ii) |
| Zygote | (iii) |
| Root tip cell of the seedling | (iv) |

**9.** *(Extended)* Explain why meiosis is necessary in organisms that reproduce sexually. **[3]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A guinea pig is heterozygous for fur colour, Bb. The alleles B and b are on the two chromosomes of one pair.

**(a)** State the alleles found in its gametes and the proportion of gametes carrying each. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of chromosomes, why each gamete carries only one allele of this gene. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Explain how meiosis contributes to genetic variation among the offspring of two parents. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* The table shows the number of chromosomes in the body cells of three species.

| Species | Body cells | Gametes |
|---|---|---|
| Rye | 14 | (i) |
| Maize | 20 | (ii) |
| Chimpanzee | 48 | (iii) |

**(a)** Complete the table. **[3]**

**(b)** State the number of chromosomes in a chimpanzee zygote. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* A student counted the chromosomes in three cells taken from a mouse: cell X had 40, cell Y had 20 and cell Z had 40.

**(a)** Identify which cell could be a gamete. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest an organ from which cell Y could have been taken. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the type of nuclear division that produced cell Y. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** **B** [1]

**3.** **Ovary** [1]

**4.** **12** [1]

**5.** **Haploid** [1]

**6.** Meiosis cells have **half the chromosome number / are haploid**, while mitosis keeps the same number [1]. Meiosis cells are **genetically different**, while mitosis cells are **genetically identical** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each difference must say what both divisions do, or use a clear comparison word; "meiosis cells are different" alone is too vague.

**7. (a)** **4** [1]

**(b)** **One chromosome from each pair**: one from A, one from B, one from C and one from D [1].

**(c)** It would **lack the genes carried on pair D** [1] and have **an extra copy of chromosome A**; meiosis should give **one chromosome from each pair** [1].

**8.** (i) **10** [1]; (ii) **10** [1]; (iii) **20** [1]; (iv) **20** [1].

**9.** Meiosis **halves the chromosome number**, producing **haploid gametes** [1]. At fertilisation two gametes fuse, so the **zygote is diploid** [1]. This keeps the **chromosome number constant** from one generation to the next; without halving it would double every generation [1].

*Examiner insight:* The link to fertilisation is the mark most often missed; "meiosis makes gametes" on its own does not explain why meiosis is necessary.

**10. (a)** Gametes carry **B or b** [1], in **equal proportions: half B, half b** [1].

**(b)** Meiosis puts **one chromosome of each pair** into each gamete [1]. B and b are on **different chromosomes of the same pair**, so each gamete receives only one of them [1].

**11.** Meiosis produces **genetically different gametes** [1]. Each gamete receives one chromosome of each pair, and **which one varies** from gamete to gamete, so gametes carry **different combinations of alleles** [1]. Different gametes fuse at fertilisation, so each offspring has a **different combination** of alleles from its parents [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs one step of the chain: gametes differ, why they differ, and the random fusion of gametes at fertilisation.

**12. (a)** (i) **7** [1]; (ii) **10** [1]; (iii) **24** [1].

**(b)** **48** [1]

**13. (a)** **Cell Y** [1]

**(b)** **Testis or ovary** [1]

**(c)** **Meiosis** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Cells X and Z have the diploid number, so they are body cells made by mitosis; only a haploid cell, with half the number, can be a gamete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying meiosis takes place in body cells or in the gametes themselves.
- Confusing haploid and diploid.
- Describing halving as cutting chromosomes in half.
- Saying meiosis produces identical cells.
- Explaining variation without saying that gametes carry different combinations of alleles.
