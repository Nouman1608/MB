Each question uses one of the specification's command words at its published International A Level tariff: **define** (2), **calculate** (4), **explain** (4), **analyse** (6), **discuss** (8) and **assess** (12). All countries, businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: Corvane Mining in Delmar**
>
> Corvane is an MNC that has invested $90m in a copper mine in Delmar, a
> developing country. The mine employs 1,200 local people at $7,500 a year;
> the local average wage is $5,000. Corvane makes $30m profit a year and
> sends 80% of it to its home country. Delmar granted a three-year tax
> holiday on its 25% corporation tax. The mine imports 60% of its equipment
> and has been accused of letting waste flow into a river that local
> farmers use. Delmar has weak environmental laws and few inspectors.
>
> **Source 2: Lumina Apparel**
>
> Lumina is an MNC that sells clothing in 50 countries. A pressure group
> has reported that a supplier in Karval pays workers $1.80 an hour for
> 11 hours a day, six days a week, while an independent estimate of the
> living wage is $160 a week. Switching to an ethical supplier would add
> $0.60 to the cost of each of 5 million garments a year. Lumina's profit
> is $40m a year. The story is spreading on social media, and Lumina
> expects to lose 6% of its $400m revenue if customers boycott it, at a
> gross margin of 30%.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "multinational corporation". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the investment per job and the wage premium paid by Corvane. **[4]**

**3.** Calculate the tax Delmar's government gives up during the holiday. **[4]**

**4.** Calculate the annual amount of profit sent out of Delmar. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** way in which Corvane's mine could benefit Delmar's local economy. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse how the mine could harm the local community and environment. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "self-regulation". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the weekly pay of the Karval supplier's workers and the shortfall against the living wage, as a percentage. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the extra annual cost of switching to an ethical supplier and its percentage of Lumina's profit. **[4]**

**10.** Calculate the profit lost through a boycott, and the net gain or loss from switching supplier compared with being boycotted. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss whether Delmar's government should regard Corvane's investment as a success. **[8]**

**12.** Assess how far Lumina can be controlled by external pressure rather than its own self-regulation. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that owns or controls operations [1] in more than one country [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Investment per job = 90m / 1,200 [1] = **$75,000** [1]. Wage premium = 7,500 / 5,000 - 1 [1] = **50%** above the local average [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Annual tax = 25% x $30m = $7.5m [1]. Over three years = 3 x 7.5m [1] = **$22.5m** [2]. **[4]**

**4.** 80% x $30m [1] = **$24m** [1] a year. This leaves only $6m in the country as retained profit [1], and the money is a financial outflow that reduces the benefit to Delmar's balance of payments [1]. **[4]**

**5.** The mine employs 1,200 people at $7,500, a total wage bill of $9m [1], and the wages are 50% above the local average [1]. Workers spend this money in local shops and services, which raises local demand and jobs [1], so the benefit reaches beyond the mine's own employees [1]. *(Other accepted: training and skills; infrastructure; local suppliers.)* **[4]**

**6.** Waste flowing into the river could pollute the water that local farmers use [1], which could damage crops and animals and reduce farmers' incomes [1]. Delmar's weak laws and few inspectors mean the problem may go unpunished [1], so Corvane has little financial incentive to change [1]. Mining may also bring traffic, noise and dust, and housing pressure from incoming workers [1]. If the damage is lasting, the community may lose its main livelihood long after the mine closes, which makes the harm greater than the wage benefits suggest [1]. **[6]**

**7.** A business setting and policing its own standards of behaviour [1], rather than being controlled by law or an outside body [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Weekly pay = 1.80 x 11 x 6 [1] = **$118.80** [1]. Shortfall = 160 - 118.80 = $41.20 [1]. As a percentage = 41.20 / 160 x 100 = **25.75%** (about 25.8%) [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Extra cost = 5 million x $0.60 [1] = **$3.0m** [1]. As a percentage of profit = 3.0 / 40 x 100 [1] = **7.5%** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Lost revenue = 6% x 400m = $24m [1]. Lost gross profit = 30% x 24m = **$7.2m** [1]. Net result = 7.2 - 3.0 [1] = switching would be **$4.2m better** than a boycott [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Successes:** 1,200 jobs at wages 50% above the local average, a $9m wage bill [1]; FDI of $90m brings capital and mining technology [1]; once the holiday ends, Delmar will receive $7.5m a year in tax [1].
**Failures:** $22.5m of tax is given up in three years [1]; $24m of profit a year leaves the country and 60% of equipment is imported, so the benefit to the local economy is limited [1]; the river pollution harms farmers and weak laws allow it [1].
**Judgement:** the investment is a partial success. The jobs and higher wages are real gains, but the tax holiday and profit outflows reduce the national benefit, and pollution creates a serious cost [1]. It would be a clearer success if Delmar enforced environmental rules, required more local purchasing and ensured that the tax holiday was not renewed [1]. The judgement depends on what would have happened without the mine, which the source does not say [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **External pressure:** social media is spreading the story quickly and a boycott could cost 6% of revenue, which is $7.2m of lost gross profit [1]; the pressure group has shown the pay shortfall of $41.20 a week and given consumers the facts [1]; consumers can choose other brands, so the threat is credible [1]; legal control is possible in the 50 countries where Lumina sells, if a government passes supply chain laws [1].
**Limits of external pressure:** consumers may not know or care, and may not boycott [1]; Karval's laws may be weak and Lumina can change suppliers [1]; social media stories fade [1]; and a supplier's abuses are hard for outsiders to see [1].
**Self-regulation:** a code of conduct and audits of suppliers can prevent abuses before they become public [1]; Lumina can spend the extra $3.0m, which is less than the $7.2m a boycott might cost [1]; but self-regulation depends on honesty and can be used merely for public relations [1].
**Judgement:** external pressure is the main reason Lumina is likely to act, because a boycott would cost more than switching supplier [1]. Self-regulation would be more effective in preventing the problem in the first place, but only if it is checked independently [1]. The balance depends on how sensitive Lumina's customers are to ethical issues, and on how reliably its audits work [1]. The best protection combines pressure, law and self-regulation [1]. (Award a maximum of 12 marks.) **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating one year's tax instead of three.
- Treating all of the profit as staying in Delmar.
- Calculating the wage shortfall as a percentage of pay rather than of the living wage.
- Comparing the cost of switching with revenue, not with lost gross profit.
- Describing benefits of the mine without noting the costs, or the reverse.
- Listing methods of control without judging their strength.
