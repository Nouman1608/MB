These questions cover subtopic 9.2 Uses of metals of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028). Revise first with the [Uses of metals study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-uses-of-metals/) and the [Uses of metals revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-uses-of-metals-revision-notes/).

Densities for calculations: aluminium 2.70 g cm⁻³; iron 7.87 g cm⁻³; copper 8.96 g cm⁻³.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State the property of aluminium that makes it suitable for making aircraft. **[1]**

**2.** Which pair of properties explains the use of copper in electrical wiring?

A: low density and ductility

B: good electrical conductivity and ductility

C: resistance to corrosion and low density

D: good thermal conductivity and high melting point

**[1]**

**3.** State why aluminium is used to make food containers. **[1]**

**4.** Define the term ductile. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** properties of aluminium that make it suitable for overhead electrical cables. **[2]**

**6.** Calculate the mass of 250 cm³ of copper. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Aluminium is a reactive metal, yet aluminium food trays do not corrode.

**(a)** Explain why aluminium does not corrode. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why corrosion resistance is important for a food container. **[1]**

**8.** An engineer compares aluminium and copper for a section of overhead cable containing 20 000 cm³ of metal.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of metal in the section if it is made from aluminium. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass if it is made from copper. **[1]**

**(c)** Copper is the better electrical conductor. Use your answers to explain why aluminium is used. **[2]**

**9.** Copper is used for wiring inside houses.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of structure, why copper is ductile. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why copper's high density does not prevent its use for house wiring. **[1]**

**10.** The table shows some properties of three metals.

| Metal | Density / g cm⁻³ | Electrical conductivity | Resistance to corrosion in air |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminium | 2.70 | Good | Good (oxide layer) |
| Copper | 8.96 | Very good | Good |
| Iron | 7.87 | Fair | Poor (rusts) |

**(a)** Use the table to explain why iron is not used for aircraft bodies. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the table to explain why iron is not used for food containers unless it is coated. **[1]**

**11.** Complete each sentence, giving a property.

**(a)** Aluminium is used for aircraft because ... **[1]**

**(b)** Copper is used for electrical wiring because ... **[2]**

**(c)** Aluminium is used for food containers because ... **[1]**

**12.** A company makes ladders for electricians. Some ladders are made of aluminium. Suggest **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of aluminium for a ladder used near electrical cables. Use properties of aluminium in your answer. **[2]**

**13.** A designer must choose between aluminium and copper for two products. For each product, choose the more suitable metal and give a reason based on a physical property.

**(a)** The thin wires inside a mobile phone charger cable. **[2]**

**(b)** A lightweight drinks can. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Low density [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** It resists corrosion [1].

**4.** Can be drawn out into wires [1].

**5.** Low density [1]; good electrical conductivity [1].

**6.** 8.96 × 250 = 2240 g [1].

**7. (a)** A thin layer of aluminium oxide forms on the surface [1]; it stops air and water reaching the metal underneath [1].

**(b)** Corrosion products could contaminate the food / the container would weaken or leak [1].

**8. (a)** 2.70 × 20 000 = 54 000 g = 54 kg [1].

**(b)** 8.96 × 20 000 = 179 200 g = 179.2 kg [1].

**(c)** The aluminium section is about one third of the mass of the copper section [1]; aluminium still conducts electricity well, so lighter cables can hang between pylons with less support [1].

**9. (a)** The ions are in layers that can slide over each other [1]; the delocalised electrons keep holding the ions together, so the wire does not break as it is drawn out [1].

**(b)** House wiring is short and supported by the building, so its mass is not important (good conductivity matters more) [1].

**10. (a)** Iron has a much higher density (7.87 compared with 2.70 g cm⁻³) [1], so an iron aircraft would be much heavier and need more fuel [1].

**(b)** Iron has poor resistance to corrosion / rusts, which would damage the container and contaminate the food [1].

**11. (a)** it has a low density [1].

**(b)** it is a good electrical conductor [1] and it is ductile / can be drawn into wires [1].

**(c)** it resists corrosion [1].

**12.** Advantage: low density, so the ladder is light and easy to carry [1]. Disadvantage: aluminium is a good electrical conductor, so it could conduct electricity if it touched a live cable [1].

**13. (a)** Copper [1]; it is the better electrical conductor and is ductile, so it can be drawn into thin wires; the mass of a short cable is unimportant [1].

**(b)** Aluminium [1]; it has a low density, so the can is light, and it resists corrosion, so the drink is not contaminated [1].

*Marking note:* the reason mark needs a property, not just the name of the metal again. In (b), either low density or resistance to corrosion is enough for the mark; giving both is good practice.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "light" instead of "low density".
- Giving one property where the syllabus pairs two.
- Calling aluminium unreactive instead of describing its oxide layer.
- Confusing thermal and electrical conductivity for wiring.
- Dropping units or mixing grams and kilograms in density calculations.
