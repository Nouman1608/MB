These notes condense sub-topic **12.1 Energy** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content for Paper 4, with the respirometer work relevant to Paper 5. For explanations and worked examples, use the [energy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-energy/); for exam-style questions, use the [energy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-energy-practice/).

## Why energy is needed

| Use | Example |
|---|---|
| Active transport | Carrier proteins moving ions against a gradient (root hair cells; proton pumps) |
| Movement | Muscle contraction; cilia and flagella; chromosomes moved by spindle fibres |
| Anabolic reactions | Joining nucleotides in DNA replication; joining amino acids in protein synthesis |

## ATP

- Structure: **adenine + ribose + three phosphates**.
- ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi releases energy (about 30.5 kJ mol⁻¹).
- Regenerated from ADP + Pi using energy from respiration.

**Features that make it the universal currency**
- One-step hydrolysis → **immediate** release.
- **Small amounts** of energy → little waste.
- **Small, soluble** → moves easily within cells.
- **Quickly regenerated** → small pool, rapid turnover.
- Found in **all** organisms.

ATP is a short-term carrier, not a long-term store.

## Two ways to make ATP

| | Substrate-linked | Chemiosmosis |
|---|---|---|
| How | Phosphate moved from substrate to ADP | Proton gradient drives ATP synthase |
| Where | Glycolysis; Krebs cycle | Inner mitochondrial membrane; thylakoid membranes |
| Membrane? | No | Yes |
| Amount | Small | Most ATP |

## Energy values

| Substrate | Approx. energy / kJ g⁻¹ | Typical RQ |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate | 16 | 1.0 |
| Protein | 17 | 0.9 |
| Lipid | 39 | 0.7 |

**Why lipid is highest**: more hydrogen atoms per gram → more reduced NAD/FAD → more ATP by oxidative phosphorylation. Also needs more O₂, so lower RQ.

## RQ

**Definition**: ratio of the number of molecules of CO₂ produced to the number of molecules of O₂ taken in, as a result of respiration.

RQ = CO₂ ÷ O₂ (volumes are fine at the same temperature and pressure).

### Worked reminder: RQ from an equation

```
oleic acid: C₁₈H₃₄O₂ + 25.5O₂ → 18CO₂ + 17H₂O
RQ = 18 ÷ 25.5 = 0.71
```

### Reading RQ values

- ~0.7 lipid; ~0.9 protein; 1.0 carbohydrate.
- 0.7–1.0: mixture.
- >1.0: some anaerobic respiration, or carbohydrate → lipid conversion.

## Respirometer method in steps

1. Organisms + **KOH** (absorbs CO₂); control tube with **glass beads** of equal volume.
2. Equilibrate in a water bath with the system open.
3. Close; measure distance liquid moves towards organisms in a set time → **O₂ uptake**.
4. Replace KOH with **water**; repeat → **O₂ uptake − CO₂ output**.
5. Volume = πr² × distance.
6. CO₂ = O₂ − (water reading); RQ = CO₂ ÷ O₂.

### Worked reminder: RQ from distances

KOH run 30 mm; water run 3 mm towards organisms.

```
RQ = (30 − 3) ÷ 30 = 0.90
```

If the water-run liquid moves **away** from the organisms, CO₂ out > O₂ in → RQ > 1.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATP | Adenosine triphosphate: nucleotide that transfers energy within cells |
| Hydrolysis | Breaking a bond by adding water |
| Anabolic reaction | Building larger molecules from smaller ones |
| Substrate-linked phosphorylation | ATP made by transferring phosphate directly from a substrate |
| Chemiosmosis | ATP made using energy from a proton gradient across a membrane |
| Respiratory substrate | Molecule oxidised in respiration to release energy |
| Respiratory quotient | CO₂ produced ÷ O₂ taken in during respiration |
| Respirometer | Apparatus measuring gas exchange in respiration |

## Exam traps

- RQ upside down (O₂ ÷ CO₂).
- Taking the water-run reading as O₂ uptake.
- "Lipids are bigger molecules" as the reason for higher energy value.
- "ATP stores lots of energy".
- Missing control tube or equilibration in a respirometer plan.
- Forgetting to use the radius, not the diameter, in πr².

## Self-test

1. Give one example of each use of energy named in the syllabus.
2. Name the three components of ATP.
3. Give three features that make ATP suitable as an energy currency.
4. Where does chemiosmosis occur in a plant cell?
5. Name two stages of respiration that produce ATP by substrate-linked phosphorylation.
6. Explain why lipids release more energy per gram than carbohydrates.
7. Define RQ.
8. Calculate the RQ for glucose.
9. What does an RQ of 1.3 suggest?
10. What is the purpose of potassium hydroxide in a respirometer?
11. Why is a tube of glass beads set up?
12. KOH run 48 mm; water run 12 mm towards the seeds. Calculate RQ and suggest the substrate.

### Answers

1. Active transport: ion uptake by root hair cells; movement: muscle contraction; anabolic: joining nucleotides in DNA replication or amino acids in protein synthesis.
2. Adenine, ribose, three phosphate groups.
3. Any three: immediate release in one step; small manageable amounts; small and soluble; easily regenerated; universal.
4. Inner mitochondrial membrane and thylakoid membranes of chloroplasts.
5. Glycolysis and the Krebs cycle.
6. They contain more hydrogen per gram, so more reduced NAD/FAD form and more ATP is made by oxidative phosphorylation.
7. Number of molecules of CO₂ produced ÷ number of molecules of O₂ taken in, as a result of respiration.
8. 6 ÷ 6 = 1.0.
9. Some anaerobic respiration (or conversion of carbohydrate to lipid).
10. Absorbs CO₂ so that movement shows O₂ uptake only.
11. A control to detect changes in temperature and pressure not caused by respiration.
12. (48 − 12) ÷ 48 = **0.75**; mainly lipid, or a lipid and carbohydrate mixture.
