These notes condense sub-topic **19.4 Populations** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 6 are Core. Outcome 7 (explaining each phase of the sigmoid curve) is **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [populations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-populations-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [populations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-populations-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [organisms and their environment revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-organisms-and-their-environment-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 19.

## Core recall: three descriptions

| Term | Syllabus description | Must include |
|---|---|---|
| Population | a group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time | one species, same area, same time |
| Community | all of the populations of different species in an ecosystem | all populations, different species |
| Ecosystem | a unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together | community + environment + interacting |

**Trap:** "all the beetles in a wood" is not a population if there are several beetle species.

## Core recall: the four growth factors

| Factor | More of it → | Acts on |
|---|---|---|
| Food supply | faster growth | more births, fewer deaths from starvation |
| Competition | slower growth | fewer survive or breed |
| Predation | slower growth | more deaths |
| Disease | slower growth | more deaths, fewer able to breed |

Competition and disease get stronger as a population becomes crowded.

## Core recall: the sigmoid curve

For a population growing with **limited resources**:

| Phase | Graph shape |
|---|---|
| **Lag** | nearly flat at the start, slow rise |
| **Exponential (log)** | steepest rise |
| **Stationary** | levels off |
| **Death** | falls |

The S-shape = lag + exponential + stationary.

## Core recall: graph skills

- **Rate of growth = change in number ÷ time taken**, with units such as "cells per hour".
- Read values from the axes carefully; note the scale and any "millions" or "thousands" in the label.
- **Predator–prey graphs:** prey peak first, predator peak follows; predators rise when prey are plentiful, then prey fall, then predators fall.

## Extended recall: explaining the phases

| Phase | Birth and death rates | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Lag | births only slightly above deaths | few individuals reproducing; adjusting to conditions or maturing |
| Exponential | births far exceed deaths | plentiful food and space; little competition, predation or disease |
| Stationary | births = deaths | **limiting factors**: food, water, space or oxygen short; waste builds up; more competition |
| Death | deaths exceed births | food runs out; toxic waste accumulates |

**Limiting factor:** a factor in short supply that restricts the growth of the population.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Population | Organisms of one species in the same area at the same time |
| Community | All the populations of different species in an ecosystem |
| Ecosystem | A community and its environment, interacting together |
| Birth rate | Number of new individuals produced in a given time |
| Death rate | Number of individuals dying in a given time |
| Sigmoid curve | S-shaped curve of population growth with limited resources |
| Exponential (log) phase | Period of fastest growth, with numbers doubling regularly |
| Stationary phase | Period when numbers stay constant because births equal deaths |
| Limiting factor (Extended) | A factor in short supply that restricts population growth |

## Exam traps

- **Missing a condition** in the population description: one species, same area, same time.
- **Community = animals.** It includes all organisms: plants, animals, fungi, bacteria.
- **Ecosystem without "interacting".**
- **Extra factors.** When asked to state factors, give the four in the syllabus.
- **Stationary = no reproduction.** Births continue; they equal deaths.
- **Death phase in the S.** The S-shape stops at stationary; the death phase comes after.
- **No units** in a growth-rate answer.
- **(Extended) No limiting factor.** Name one, such as food or oxygen, when explaining stationary.

## Command-word reminders

- **Identify** a phase: use the syllabus name.
- **Calculate** a rate: show subtraction, division and the unit.
- **Explain** (Extended): limiting factor → births and deaths.

## Self-test

1. Describe a population.
2. All the plants, animals and microorganisms in a field form what?
3. Describe an ecosystem.
4. State the four factors in the syllabus that affect the rate of population growth.
5. How does an increase in predators affect a prey population's growth rate?
6. Why does disease often spread faster in a large population?
7. Name the phase in which a population grows fastest.
8. Name the phase in which numbers stay constant.
9. A population rises from 400 to 1 600 in 3 days. Calculate the mean rate of growth.
10. On a predator–prey graph, which peak comes first?
11. (Extended) Explain the lag phase.
12. (Extended) Explain the stationary phase, referring to limiting factors.

### Answers

1. A group of organisms of one species, living in the same area, at the same time.
2. A community.
3. A unit containing the community of organisms and their environment, interacting together.
4. Food supply, competition, predation and disease.
5. It slows the growth rate (or causes a decrease), because more individuals are killed.
6. Individuals are closer together, so the pathogen passes between them more easily.
7. Exponential (log) phase.
8. Stationary phase.
9. (1 600 − 400) ÷ 3 = 400 individuals per day.
10. The prey peak.
11. Few individuals are present and reproducing; they are adjusting to the new conditions or maturing, so numbers rise slowly.
12. Limiting factors such as food, space or oxygen run short and waste builds up, so competition increases; birth rate falls and death rate rises until they are equal.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "same time" in the population description.
- Confusing community and ecosystem.
- Misreading graph scales or forgetting units.
- Describing the stationary phase without birth and death rates.
- (Extended) Giving a phase explanation with no named limiting factor.
