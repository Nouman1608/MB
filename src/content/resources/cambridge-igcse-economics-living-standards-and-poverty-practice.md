## Questions

**1.** Define real GDP per head. **[2]**

**2.** In an illustrative year, Country K has nominal GDP of 1,260 billion, a price index of 105 (base year 100) and a population of 40 million.

**(a)** Calculate real GDP. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate real GDP per head. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why real GDP is better than nominal GDP for comparing living standards over time. **[2]**

**3.** Country L has consumption of 520, investment of 160, government spending of 150, exports of 140 and imports of 90 (billions).

**(a)** Calculate GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Imports rise to 120 with everything else unchanged. Calculate the new GDP. **[2]**

**4.** Country M has real GDP of 800 billion and a population of 25 million in year 1, and real GDP of 840 billion and a population of 25.5 million in year 2.

**(a)** Calculate real GDP per head in each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in real GDP per head, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why a rise in real GDP may not mean higher living standards. **[2]**

**5.** A simplified index gives equal weight to a health index, an education index and an income index. The health index is (life expectancy - 20) / 65.

**(a)** Identify the three components of the Human Development Index. **[3]**
**(b)** Country P has life expectancy 65 years, education index 0.70 and income index 0.45. Calculate its simplified index to 3 decimal places. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain one advantage of the HDI over real GDP per head. **[2]**

**6.** Analyse two reasons why living standards are lower in some countries than in others. **[6]**

**7.** Mean income per person in both Country R and Country S is 8,000. Income shares by quintile (fifths of households) are:

| | Poorest | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | Richest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Country R | 3% | 6% | 12% | 19% | 60% |
| Country S | 7% | 12% | 17% | 24% | 40% |

**(a)** Calculate income per person in the poorest and richest quintiles in Country R. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times larger the richest share is than the poorest share in each country, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain two reasons why income may be distributed unequally within a country. **[4]**

**8.** In Country T the cost of a basic basket of necessities is 4,500 a year. Median household income is 15,000. Relative poverty is defined as income below 60% of the median. Four households earn: A 4,000; B 8,500; C 9,500; D 16,000.

**(a)** Distinguish between absolute poverty and relative poverty. **[4]**
**(b)** Identify the households in absolute poverty and in relative poverty. **[3]**
**(c)** Later, median income rises to 18,000 and household B earns 9,200. Explain why B is relatively poor although its income has risen. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how each of the following can cause poverty.

**(a)** Age **[3]**
**(b)** Low wages **[3]**

**10.** Country U taxes income at 0% on the first 6,000, 12% on income from 6,000 to 30,000 and 32% on income above 30,000.

**(a)** Calculate the tax paid and the average tax rate on incomes of 10,000 and 40,000, to 2 decimal places where needed. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why this tax is progressive. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse how the government could use the revenue to reduce poverty. **[2]**

**11.** In a labour market, demand equals supply at a wage of 4 with 1,000 workers. A national minimum wage of 6 is introduced. At 6, demand is 850 workers and supply is 1,150.

**(a)** Calculate the number of unemployed workers at the minimum wage. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in the total pay bill. **[3]**
**(c)** Discuss whether a national minimum wage is an effective way to reduce poverty. **[6]**

**12.** Country Z's real GDP rises by 3% and its population by 3.5%.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in real GDP per head, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Discuss whether improved education is likely to reduce poverty more effectively than more generous state benefits in Country Z. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Real GDP (GDP adjusted for price changes) [1] divided by the population [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Real" and "divided by population" are the two ideas to state; "average income" on its own is too vague to show both.

**2. (a)** 1,260 / 105 x 100 [1] = **1,200 billion**. **[1]**
**(b)** 1,200 billion / 40 million = **30,000** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Nominal GDP can rise purely because prices rise [1]; real GDP removes inflation, so changes show changes in output and what people can buy [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division by the price index. The explanation needs the contrast between price change and output change.

**3. (a)** GDP = C + I + G + (X - M) = 520 + 160 + 150 + (140 - 90) [1] = **880** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 520 + 160 + 150 + (140 - 120) [1] = **850** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Imports are subtracted. Adding them is a common slip, so write out the formula before substituting.

**4. (a)** Year 1: 800 billion / 25 million = **32,000** [1]; year 2: 840 billion / 25.5 million = **32,941** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (32,941 - 32,000) / 32,000 x 100 [1] = **2.94%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Population may grow as fast as or faster than output, so output per person falls [1]; or the gains may go to a few people, so most households see no improvement [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Real GDP grew 5%, yet per head grew less than 3%. Quote both numbers if you can, to prove you used the data.

**5. (a)** Health (life expectancy) [1]; education (years of schooling) [1]; income per head [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Health index = (65 - 20) / 65 = 0.692 [1]; sum = 0.692 + 0.70 + 0.45 = 1.842 [1]; average = 1.842 / 3 = **0.614** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The HDI includes health and education, not only income [1], so it shows well-being better where income is high but people are unhealthy or poorly educated [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An advantage needs a "so" or "because" that links it to living standards. Saying "it is more accurate" is a bare claim.

**6.** Reason 1 identified, for example low capital or technology [1]; developed: less output per worker, so lower incomes, so less spending and saving [1] and lower living standards [1]. Reason 2, for example poor education and health [1]; developed: lower productivity, so lower wages [1] and less tax revenue for services [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Other reasons that work: unstable institutions, debt, conflict, lack of trade links. Each needs a chain; a list of four reasons earns less than two developed ones.

**7. (a)** Poorest: 0.03 x 5 x 8,000 = **1,200** [1]; richest: 0.60 x 5 x 8,000 = **24,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** R: 60 / 3 = **20.0** [1]; S: 40 / 7 = **5.7** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Reason 1, for example unequal ownership of land or wealth [1], so some earn rents and profits others cannot [1]. Reason 2, for example differences in education and skills [1], so pay differs widely between jobs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Both countries have the same mean income, so use that to say that the mean alone hides the difference.

**8. (a)** Absolute: income too low to buy basic necessities, a fixed standard [1][1]. Relative: income much lower than most others in the same society, a standard that moves with average incomes [1][1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Relative line = 0.6 x 15,000 = 9,000. Absolute: **A** only [1]. Relative: **A** [1] and **B** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** New line = 0.6 x 18,000 = 10,800 [1]. B's 9,200 is below this [1]. The line rose by 20% but B's income by only 8.2%, so B fell behind the typical household [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the line calculation. C is above the line (9,500), a common check you can make.

**9. (a)** Older people may have retired and rely on small pensions or savings [1]; young and elderly people cannot earn, so their households depend on others' wages [1]; spending needs, such as health care, may rise as incomes fall [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Low wages arise from low skills or low productivity [1]; wages may not cover basics, especially in households with many dependants [1]; weak bargaining power or part-time hours keep pay down [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain from cause to low income to unmet needs gains more credit than the phrase "they cannot afford things".

**10. (a)** 10,000: 0.12 x 4,000 = **480** [1]; average 480 / 10,000 = **4.8%** [1]. 40,000: 0.12 x 24,000 + 0.32 x 10,000 = 2,880 + 3,200 = **6,080** [1]; average 6,080 / 40,000 = **15.2%** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** The average rate rises as income rises (4.8% to 15.2%) [1], so higher earners pay a higher proportion of income [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Revenue can fund benefits or education [1], which raises incomes of poor households or their future earning power [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Progressive concerns the proportion, not the amount. Quote both average rates to prove it.

**11. (a)** Unemployed = supply - demand [1] = 1,150 - 850 = **300** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Before: 1,000 x 4 = 4,000 [1]. After: 850 x 6 = 5,100 [1]. Change = **+1,100** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** For: those who keep jobs earn more [1], lifting some households out of poverty [1]. Against: demand for labour falls, so some lose jobs [1] and may become poorer than before [1]. Evaluation: the effect depends on the level of the minimum wage relative to the market wage [1] and on how responsive demand for labour is; a judgement is reached [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data: 300 unemployed and a pay bill 1,100 higher. A judgement that names a condition is better than "it depends".

**12. (a)** 1.03 / 1.035 [1] - 1 = **-0.48%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Education: raises skills, productivity and wages [1], reduces unemployment and so reliance on benefits [1]; but it is costly [1] and takes years to work, with the poorest possibly not attending [1]. Benefits: raise income at once [1] and help those who cannot work, such as the old and ill [1]; but cost taxpayers and may reduce work incentives [1]. Judgement with reason: education is better for long-run reduction, benefits for immediate relief; with falling output per head, benefits may be needed first [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Weigh the two policies against each other on speed, cost and who gains. Two separate descriptions with no comparison leave the judgement unsupported.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "real" or "per head" in definitions.
- Using nominal GDP in a question about real values, or forgetting to divide by population.
- Subtracting the wrong way in unemployment: supply minus demand.
- Describing the HDI without naming its three parts.
- Treating relative poverty as the same as being at the bottom of the distribution in a poor country.
- Defining progressive tax by the amount paid rather than the proportion.
- Writing "it depends" instead of a reasoned judgement with a stated condition.
- Not showing the line or formula before substituting numbers.
