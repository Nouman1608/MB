These questions cover section M2.1 (Mathematical modelling) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2, in International A-level Unit M2. Use g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. Read the [mathematical modelling study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m2-mathematical-modelling/) first, or refresh the ideas with the [mathematical modelling revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m2-mathematical-modelling-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A rod in a mechanics problem is described as light. Explain what this means. **[1]**

**2.** Two particles are connected by an inextensible string. State what this assumption allows you to say about their motion. **[1]**

**3.** A parcel slides across a floor that is modelled as smooth. State what this assumption means for the forces acting on the parcel from the floor. **[2]**

**4.** A football is kicked and modelled as a particle moving under gravity. State two assumptions contained in this model. **[2]**

**5.** A train 200 m long travels at a constant 25 m s⁻¹ through a station whose platform is 100 m long.

**(a)** Find the time from the front of the train reaching the start of the platform until the back of the train leaves the end of the platform. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why modelling the train as a particle would give a poor answer to part (a). **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A stone is dropped from rest from the top of a cliff 45 m above the sea. Model the stone as a particle moving under gravity.

**(a)** Find the time taken to reach the sea and the speed of the stone on impact. **[3]**

**(b)** The stone is observed to take 3.4 s to reach the sea. Comment on the model and suggest one refinement. **[2]**

**7.** A crate of mass 40 kg rests on a horizontal floor. A horizontal force of 60 N is applied to it and it moves from rest.

**(a)** Model the floor as smooth. Find the distance the crate moves in 4 s. **[2]**

**(b)** In fact the crate moves 9.6 m in 4 s. Refine the model by including a constant frictional force, and find the coefficient of friction. **[4]**

**(c)** State one assumption that remains in your refined model. **[1]**

**8.** Particles A, of mass 5 kg, and B, of mass 3 kg, are attached to the ends of a light inextensible string that passes over a smooth fixed pulley. The particles hang vertically and are released from rest.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain where you used the facts that the string is light and inextensible. **[2]**

**(c)** In an experiment, the measured acceleration is 2.1 m s⁻². Suggest one reason for the difference. **[1]**

**9.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards with speed 19.6 m s⁻¹. Model the ball as a particle moving under gravity.

**(a)** Find the greatest height reached and the time taken to reach it. **[3]**

**(b)** State, with a reason, the effect of air resistance on the greatest height and on the time taken to reach it. **[2]**

**10.** A car brakes so that its velocity, t seconds after the brakes are applied, is v = 18 − 1.5t m s⁻¹ and its displacement is s = 18t − 0.75t² m.

**(a)** Find the time taken for the car to stop and the distance it travels while braking. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the formula for s should not be used to find the car's displacement when t = 15. **[2]**

**11.** A ball of mass 0.5 kg is rolled across a horizontal floor at 3 m s⁻¹. The floor is first modelled as smooth. The ball comes to rest after travelling 9 m.

**(a)** Explain why the smooth model is not valid here. **[1]**

**(b)** Assuming instead a constant resistance to motion, find its magnitude. **[3]**

**(c)** State one way in which the assumption in part (b) may be unrealistic. **[1]**

**12.** A plank resting on two supports is modelled as a uniform rod.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by "uniform" and by "rod" in this model. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the plank cannot be modelled as a particle when finding the reactions at the supports. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Its mass is negligible, so its weight is ignored [1]. Total: 1

**2.** While the string is taut, the particles have the same speed and the same magnitude of acceleration [1]. Total: 1

**3.** There is no friction [1]; the only force from the floor is the normal reaction, perpendicular to the floor [1]. Total: 2

**4.** Any two, each in context: the ball has no size and does not spin [1]; air resistance is ignored, so its weight is the only force; the acceleration due to gravity is constant at 9.8 m s⁻² [1]. Total: 2

**5. (a)** The front must travel 100 + 200 = 300 m [1], taking 300 ÷ 25 = **12 s** [1].

**(b)** A particle has no length, so it would give 100 ÷ 25 = 4 s; the train's length is large compared with the platform, so it cannot be ignored [1]. Total: 3

**6. (a)** 45 = ½(9.8)t² [1], so t = √(90/9.8) = **3.03 s** [1]. v = 9.8 × 3.03… = **29.7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** The real time is longer than predicted, which suggests air resistance slows the stone [1]. Refinement: include a resistive force opposing the motion (or repeat the timing to check it) [1]. Total: 5

**7. (a)** a = 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 m s⁻², so s = ½(1.5)(4²) = **12 m** [1] [1].

**(b)** 9.6 = ½a(16), so a = 1.2 m s⁻² [1]. 60 − F = 40 × 1.2, so F = 12 N [1]. R = 40 × 9.8 = 392 N [1]. µ = 12 ÷ 392 = **0.0306** [1].

**(c)** Any one: the friction is constant; the applied force is exactly horizontal; the crate is a particle; there is no air resistance [1]. Total: 7

**8. (a)** A: 5g − T = 5a; B: T − 3g = 3a [1]. Adding: 2g = 8a [1], so **a = 2.45 m s⁻²** [1]. T = 3(9.8 + 2.45) = **36.75 N, or 36.8 N** (3 s.f.) [1].

**(b)** Light: the tension is the same at both ends, so one T was used for both particles [1]. Inextensible: both particles have the same size of acceleration, so one a was used [1].

**(c)** Any one: the pulley is not smooth, so friction acts there; the pulley has mass; air resistance acts on the particles [1]. Total: 7

**9. (a)** 0 = 19.6² − 2(9.8)s [1], so **s = 19.6 m** [1]. 0 = 19.6 − 9.8t, so **t = 2 s** [1].

**(b)** On the way up, air resistance acts downwards with the weight, so the deceleration is greater than 9.8 m s⁻² [1]. The ball therefore stops sooner: the greatest height is less than 19.6 m and it is reached in less than 2 s [1]. Total: 5

**10. (a)** 18 − 1.5t = 0 gives **t = 12 s** [1]. s = 18(12) − 0.75(144) [1] = **108 m** [1].

**(b)** At t = 15 the formula gives s = 270 − 168.75 = 101.25 m, less than 108 m [1]. This would mean the car moved backwards after stopping, which it does not, so the model is valid only for 0 ≤ t ≤ 12 [1]. Total: 5

**11. (a)** On a smooth floor no horizontal force acts, so the ball would not slow down; it would keep moving at 3 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 0 = 3² − 2a(9) [1], so a = 0.5 m s⁻² [1]. Resistance = 0.5 × 0.5 = **0.25 N** [1].

**(c)** Any one: resistance may depend on speed; the floor may not be uniform; the ball rolls, so treating it as a sliding particle ignores rotation [1]. Total: 5

**12. (a)** Uniform: the mass is evenly distributed, so the centre of mass is at the midpoint [1]. Rod: the plank has length but negligible width and thickness, and does not bend [1].

**(b)** The reactions depend on where the weight acts relative to the supports, so moments must be taken; a particle has no length, so moments cannot be found [1]. Total: 3

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating "the stone is a particle" when the question already said so, instead of a new assumption.
- Explaining "light" as "small" or "thin".
- Giving the effect of air resistance without its direction: does the prediction go up or down?
- Using the braking formula after the car has stopped.
- Leaving µ as a fraction with an unsimplified decimal, or using R = 40 instead of 40g.
- Giving a reason for a difference from data that does not relate to any assumption in the model.
