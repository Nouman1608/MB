Condensed recall for **4.2 Geophysical hazard risks** (IB DP Geography, Option D: Geophysical hazards). The [Geophysical hazards study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-geophysical-hazards-study-guide/) has the longer account.

## Risk

**Risk** is the probability that a hazard will cause harm. It depends on the hazard's nature and on the **exposure** of people and assets.

A **hazard profile** records: magnitude, frequency, duration, speed of onset, spatial extent, predictability. Frequency and magnitude are typically inversely related: large events are rarer.

Conceptual formula: **risk = (hazard x vulnerability) / capacity to cope.** Worked check with illustrative scores: (6 x 8) / 4 = 12 versus (6 x 3) / 9 = 2. Risk is not normally calculated this way in practice.

## Monitoring and warning

| Hazard | What can be done |
|---|---|
| Volcanoes | Precursors (seismic swarms, ground deformation, gas changes) allow warnings, though timing and scale remain uncertain |
| Earthquakes | Precise prediction is not currently possible; focus on long-term probability forecasts, early warning giving seconds of notice, and building design |

Distinguish the terms:

- **Prediction:** when, where and how big.
- **Forecasting:** long-term probability.
- **Warning:** alert after an event has begun or signs appear.

## Hazard mapping

Seismic hazard maps and volcanic hazard zones show spatial patterns of risk and guide planning.

## Why people live near hazards

Fertile volcanic soils, trade routes, geothermal energy, or simply lack of choice.

## Exam traps

- Saying earthquakes can be predicted.
- Confusing hazard (the event) with risk (the probability of harm).
- Ignoring exposure and vulnerability when explaining risk.
- Treating the risk formula as a measured quantity.

## Self-test

1. Define risk.
2. Name four features of a hazard profile.
3. Why are large events usually less frequent?
4. Why is warning more successful for volcanoes than earthquakes?
5. Distinguish forecasting from prediction.
6. Give two reasons people live in hazardous areas.

**Answers:** 1. The probability that a hazard causes harm. 2. For example magnitude, frequency, speed of onset, predictability. 3. Frequency and magnitude are inversely related. 4. Volcanoes show measurable precursors; earthquakes give little or no advance sign. 5. Forecasting gives long-term probability; prediction would state when, where and how big. 6. For example fertile soils and geothermal energy.
