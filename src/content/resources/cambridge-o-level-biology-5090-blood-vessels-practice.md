## Section A

**1.** Which vessel brings blood from the small intestine to the liver?

A: hepatic artery

B: hepatic portal vein

C: hepatic vein

D: renal vein

**[1]**

**2.** Which feature is found in veins but not in arteries?

A: a thick wall

B: elastic tissue

C: muscle

D: valves

**[1]**

**3.** Name the vessel that carries blood from the heart to the lungs. **[1]**

**4.** State the function of capillaries. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** features of arteries that allow them to withstand high blood pressure. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table compares three blood vessels.

| Vessel | Wall thickness | Lumen diameter | Valves |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | thick | narrow | absent |
| Q | thin | wide | present |
| R | one cell thick | very narrow | absent |

**(a)** Identify the vessels P, Q and R. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why vessel Q has valves. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why vessel R has a wall one cell thick. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the wall of vessel P contains elastic tissue. **[2]**

**7.** A photomicrograph of a section through an artery and a vein has a magnification of x25. On the image the artery wall measures 10 mm and the vein wall measures 2.5 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the actual thickness of each wall. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times thicker the artery wall is than the vein wall. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the difference in thickness. **[2]**

**8.** Name the vessels that carry blood into and out of the liver and state the difference between the two vessels that carry blood into it. **[4]**

**9.** The pressure in the aorta is much higher than in the vena cava. Describe how the structure of the walls of these two vessels differs, and explain why. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why red blood cells pass through capillaries in single file, and why this helps exchange. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a person who stands still for a long time may have blood collecting in the veins of the legs, and how valves reduce this. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why a pulse can be felt in an artery at the wrist but not in a vein. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** D [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The pulmonary artery [1]. **[1]**

**4.** To allow the exchange of substances between the blood and the body cells [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two of: thick wall; muscle in the wall; elastic tissue in the wall; narrow lumen [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** P: artery [1]. Q: vein [1]. R: capillary [1].

**(b)** The pressure in veins is low, so blood could flow backwards [1]. The valves close to stop this, so blood flows only towards the heart [1].

**(c)** The aim of a capillary is exchange of substances, so a thin wall gives a short diffusion distance [1], which means oxygen and glucose can pass quickly to the cells and carbon dioxide can pass back [1].

**(d)** Blood arrives in surges at high pressure [1]. The elastic tissue stretches and recoils, which evens out the flow of blood [1]. **[9]**

**7.**

**(a)** Artery: 10 divided by 25 = 0.4 mm [1]. Vein: 2.5 divided by 25 = 0.1 mm [1].

**(b)** 0.4 divided by 0.1 = 4 times [1].

**(c)** The artery carries blood at high pressure, so it needs a thick wall to withstand it [1]. The vein carries blood at low pressure, so a thin wall is enough [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Blood flows into the liver in the hepatic artery and the hepatic portal vein [1], and out in the hepatic vein [1]. The hepatic artery brings oxygenated blood from the aorta [1]. The hepatic portal vein brings blood from the small intestine, rich in absorbed nutrients [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The aorta has a thick wall containing much muscle and elastic tissue, and a narrow lumen [1]. The vena cava has a thin wall with little muscle and elastic tissue, a wide lumen and valves [1]. The aorta must withstand and smooth out the high pressure of blood leaving the heart [1], but in the vena cava the pressure is low, so a thin wall is enough and valves prevent backflow [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The lumen of a capillary is about as wide as a red blood cell [1]. The cells are close to the wall and move slowly [1], giving time and a short distance for oxygen to diffuse out of the cells and through the wall to the tissues [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Pressure in the veins is low, so blood moves slowly back to the heart and can pool in the legs [1]. The valves close when blood begins to flow backwards [1], so the blood can only move towards the heart when leg muscles squeeze the veins [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The artery wall is elastic and is stretched by each surge of blood from the heart [1]. The pressure in a vein is low and the surges have been smoothed out, so no pulse can be felt [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that arteries carry oxygenated blood and veins deoxygenated blood in every case.
- Giving a structural feature without linking it to the pressure.
- Forgetting that the liver receives blood from two vessels.
- Dividing by the wrong number when using the magnification formula.
- Describing the wall of a capillary as "thin" instead of "one cell thick".
