These questions cover section 3.6, Parallel lines, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The method is explained in the [parallel lines study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-parallel-lines/), and the [parallel lines revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-parallel-lines-revision-notes/) summarise it. Show your working and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** Write down the gradient of a line parallel to y = 5x + 3. **[1]**

**2.** Find the gradient of a line parallel to 4x + 8y = 5. **[2]**

**3.** Find the equation of the line parallel to y = −2x + 9 that passes through (3, −5). **[2]**

**4.** Find the equation of the line parallel to x − 2y = 4 that passes through (−6, −1). Give your answer in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**

**5.** Show that the lines 3x + 4y = 10 and 6x + 8y = 3 are parallel. **[2]**

**6.** Find the equation of the line parallel to 3x + 4y = 10 that passes through (8, −2). Give your answer in the form 3x + 4y = c. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** P is the point (−1, 4) and Q is the point (5, −8).

**(a)** Find the gradient of PQ.

**(b)** Find the equation of the line through (2, 9) that is parallel to PQ.

**(c)** Does this line pass through (7, 3)? Show how you decide. **[5]**

**8.** The line kx + 3y = 12 is parallel to y = 4x − 7. Find the value of k. **[3]**

**9.** A is (−2, 1), B is (4, 3), C is (7, 9) and D is (1, 7).

**(a)** Show that ABCD is a parallelogram.

**(b)** Find the equation of the line through D that is parallel to AB. Give your answer in the form ax + by = c, where a, b and c are integers. **[6]**

**10.** The line l has equation y = 3x − 2. The line m is parallel to l and passes through (0, 5). Find the coordinates of the point where m crosses the x-axis. **[3]**

**11.** A line parallel to y = 2x − 3 passes through (1, 5). Find the coordinates of the point where it crosses the x-axis. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why the lines y = 4x + 1 and y = 4x + 1 are not described as two parallel lines, and state what must change so that they are. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The gradient is **5** [1].

**2.** 8y = −4x + 5 [1], so y = −1/2 x + 5/8 and the gradient is **−1/2** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The shortcut −a/b gives −4/8 = −1/2 as a check.

**3.** The gradient is −2 [1]. y = −2x + c, so −5 = −6 + c, c = 1, giving **y = −2x + 1** [1].

**4.** Rearranging gives y = 1/2 x − 2, so the gradient is 1/2 [1]. −1 = 1/2(−6) + c [1], so c = 2 and **y = 1/2 x + 2** [1].

**5.** 3x + 4y = 10 gives y = −3/4 x + 5/2, so the gradient is −3/4 [1]. 6x + 8y = 3 gives y = −3/4 x + 3/8, so the gradient is also −3/4 [1]. The gradients are equal, so the lines are parallel. The intercepts differ, so they are different lines.

**6.** Parallel lines have the same left-hand side, so substitute (8, −2) into 3x + 4y [1]: 24 − 8 = 16 [1]. The equation is **3x + 4y = 16** [1].

**7. (a)** Gradient = (−8 − 4)/(5 − (−1)) = −12/6 = **−2** [1].

**(b)** y − 9 = −2(x − 2) [1], so y = −2x + 4 + 9 and **y = −2x + 13** [1].

**(c)** When x = 7, y = −14 + 13 = −1 [1]. This is not 3, so the line **does not** pass through (7, 3) [1].

**8.** 3y = −kx + 12, so y = −k/3 x + 4 [1]. The gradient is −k/3 and it must equal 4 [1]. So **k = −12** [1].

**9. (a)** Gradient of AB = (3 − 1)/(4 + 2) = 1/3 and gradient of DC = (9 − 7)/(7 − 1) = 1/3 [1]. Gradient of AD = (7 − 1)/(1 + 2) = 2 and gradient of BC = (9 − 3)/(7 − 4) = 2 [1]. Both pairs of opposite sides are parallel, so ABCD is a parallelogram [1].

**(b)** The gradient is 1/3 [1]. y − 7 = 1/3(x − 1) [1]. Multiplying by 3 gives 3y − 21 = x − 1, so **x − 3y = −20** (or equivalent) [1].

**10.** The gradient of m is 3 and it passes through (0, 5), so y = 3x + 5 [1]. At the x-axis y = 0, so 3x = −5 [1]. The point is **(−5/3, 0)** [1].

**11.** The gradient is 2, so y − 5 = 2(x − 1), giving y = 2x + 3 [1]. Putting y = 0 gives 2x = −3 [1]. The point is **(−1.5, 0)** [1].

**12.** The two equations are identical, so they describe **the same line** and not two different lines [1]. To be parallel the gradients must stay equal but the y-intercepts must differ, for example y = 4x + 6 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing only part of the equation by the coefficient of y when rearranging.
- Taking the gradient of 4x + 8y = 5 as 4 or as 1/2 without the minus sign.
- Using the intercept of the given line as well as its gradient.
- Sign slips when the point has a negative coordinate.
- Forgetting that two lines with identical gradients and intercepts are one line.
