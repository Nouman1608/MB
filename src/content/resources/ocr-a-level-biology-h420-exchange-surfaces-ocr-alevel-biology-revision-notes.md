## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Surface area to volume ratio (SA:V) | Surface area ÷ volume; falls as an organism gets larger |
| Exchange surface | A surface specialised for the movement of substances between an organism and its environment |
| Tidal volume | Volume of air in or out in one normal breath |
| Vital capacity | Maximum volume breathed out after a maximum breath in |
| Ventilation rate | Tidal volume × breathing rate |
| Countercurrent flow | Blood and water flow in opposite directions across the gill lamellae |
| Spiracle | Pore in an insect's body wall that opens into a trachea |
| Tracheole | Narrow end branch of an insect trachea, in contact with cells |

## Why exchange surfaces are needed: 3.1.1(a)

- Small organisms: large SA:V, short diffusion distances; diffusion across the surface is enough.
- Large organisms: small SA:V, cells far from the surface.
- Active organisms: high metabolic rate, so high demand for oxygen.
- Calculation: SA of a cube = 6 × side²; volume = side³.

## Features of efficient surfaces: 3.1.1(b)

| Feature | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Large surface area | root hair cells; many alveoli; gill lamellae | more area for diffusion |
| Thin layer | alveolar and capillary walls, one squamous cell each | short diffusion distance |
| Good blood supply | capillaries around alveoli; lamellae | removes absorbed gas, keeps gradient steep |
| Ventilation | breathing; water flow over gills | replaces the external medium, keeps gradient steep |

## Airway tissues: 3.1.1(c)

| | Cartilage | Cilia | Goblet cells | Smooth muscle | Elastic fibres |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Trachea | C-shaped rings | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Bronchi | plates | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Bronchioles | ✗ | larger only | few or none | ✓ | ✓ |
| Alveoli | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | ✓ |

- **Cartilage**: keeps airways open as pressure falls.
- **Ciliated epithelium**: wafts mucus up and away from the lungs.
- **Goblet cells**: secrete mucus to trap particles and microorganisms.
- **Smooth muscle**: contracts to narrow airways.
- **Elastic fibres**: stretch on inspiration, recoil on expiration.

## Ventilation in mammals: 3.1.1(d)

**Inspiration (active):** external intercostals contract → ribs up and out; diaphragm contracts and flattens → thorax volume up → pressure down below atmospheric → air in.

**Expiration at rest (passive):** external intercostals relax; diaphragm relaxes and domes; elastic fibres recoil → volume down → pressure up → air out.

**Forced expiration:** internal intercostals contract, pulling ribs down and in; abdominal muscles push the diaphragm up.

## Spirometry: 3.1.1(e) and PAG10

- Subject breathes oxygen from the chamber; soda lime absorbs CO₂.
- Tidal volume = height of one normal breath on the trace.
- Breathing rate = number of peaks per minute.
- Oxygen uptake = fall in the trace ÷ time.
- Exercise: tidal volume, breathing rate, ventilation rate and oxygen uptake all rise.
- Safety: healthy subject, nose clip, disinfected mouthpiece, fresh soda lime, chamber filled with medical-grade oxygen.

## Fish and insects: 3.1.1(f)

**Bony fish**
- Mouth opens, buccal floor lowers, volume up, pressure down: water in.
- Mouth closes, buccal floor rises, pressure up: water over gills, operculum opens, water out.
- Gill filaments carry gill lamellae: large area, thin, many capillaries.
- Countercurrent flow keeps blood meeting water of higher oxygen concentration along the whole lamella.

**Insects**
- Spiracles → tracheae (chitin rings) → tracheoles → tracheal fluid → cells.
- Oxygen dissolves in tracheal fluid and diffuses into cells.
- In active tissue, fluid is withdrawn from tracheole ends, increasing the air-filled surface.
- Thoracic and abdominal movements pump air.
- Spiracles can close to limit water loss.

## Practical: 3.1.1(g)-(h)

- PAG2: dissect fish gills (filaments separate in water); view insect tracheae (spiral chitin).
- PAG1: slides of trachea (cartilage, cilia, goblet cells) and lung (alveoli, capillaries).
- Drawings: sharp pencil, continuous lines, no shading, labels with ruled lines, magnification or scale bar.

## Writing good answers

- Link each feature to the rate of diffusion.
- Use "pressure falls below atmospheric pressure", not "the lungs suck in air".
- For countercurrent flow, mention the gradient along the **entire** lamella.

## Exam traps

- Cartilage or cilia placed in alveoli.
- Diaphragm said to "move up" on inspiration.
- Tidal volume confused with vital capacity.
- Countercurrent flow described as "blood and water flowing together".
- Insect gases carried in blood; insects deliver air directly via tracheoles.
- Ventilation rate given without multiplying by breathing rate.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is expiration active or passive?**
At rest it is mainly passive: the muscles relax and the elastic fibres recoil. Forced expiration is active, using the internal intercostal muscles and abdominal muscles.

**Why can a flatworm manage without gas exchange organs?**
Its flat body gives a large SA:V and keeps every cell close to the surface, so diffusion across the body surface supplies enough oxygen.

**What does the downward slope of a spirometer trace show?**
The rate of oxygen uptake. Carbon dioxide breathed out is absorbed by soda lime, so the only net change in the chamber is the oxygen used.
