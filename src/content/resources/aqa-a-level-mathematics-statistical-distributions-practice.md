These questions cover **Section N: Statistical distributions** (N1 to N3) of the **AQA A-level
Mathematics (7357) specification**, version 1.3, for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. Section N
is Paper 3 content in the specification. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, so every
question here assumes you have one with binomial, Normal and inverse Normal functions. Questions 9
to 11 are extended problems that combine several outcomes. Give answers to 3 significant figures
unless a question says otherwise.

## Questions

**1.** A discrete random variable X has probability function P(X = x) = c/x for x = 1, 2, 3, 6.

**(a)** Show that c = 1/2. **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(X < 6). **[1]**

**2.** A quiz has 25 multiple-choice questions, each with four options. Jo guesses every answer at
random. X is the number Jo gets right.

**(a)** State the distribution of X, and give two conditions needed for it, in context. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that Jo gets at least 10 right. **[2]**

**3.** X ~ B(15, 0.35). Find:

**(a)** P(X = 5) **[2]**
**(b)** P(X < 4) **[2]**
**(c)** P(4 ≤ X ≤ 8) **[2]**

**4.** A seed company states that 85% of its seeds germinate. A gardener plants 40 seeds.

**(a)** Find the probability that at least 36 germinate. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that fewer than 32 germinate. **[2]**
**(c)** The seeds are all planted in one tray on a cold windowsill. Explain why a binomial model
may not be appropriate. **[1]**

**5.** The heights of adult men in a town are modelled by X ~ N(170, 8²), in cm.

**(a)** Find P(X > 180). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(160 < X < 175). **[2]**
**(c)** Find the height exceeded by 5% of the men. **[2]**

**6.** The curve of a Normal distribution has points of inflection at x = 38 and x = 52.

**(a)** Find the mean and standard deviation of the distribution. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that a value from this distribution is greater than 50. **[2]**

**7.** The time, T minutes, that customers spend in a shop is Normally distributed. 20% of
customers spend less than 40 minutes and 10% spend more than 70 minutes. Find the mean and
standard deviation of T. **[6]**

**8.** A random sample of 400 leaves from a tree has mean length 24.6 mm and standard deviation
1.8 mm. A histogram of the lengths is symmetrical and bell-shaped.

**(a)** Using a Normal model, estimate how many of the 400 leaves are longer than 27 mm. **[3]**
**(b)** In the sample, 38 leaves were longer than 27 mm. Comment on the model. **[1]**

**9.** For each situation, state whether a binomial model, a Normal model or neither is appropriate,
with a reason.

**(a)** The number of hearts when 5 cards are dealt from one standard pack of 52 cards. **[2]**
**(b)** The masses of eggs laid at a large farm, shown by a symmetrical histogram. **[2]**
**(c)** The number of times a dice is rolled until the first six appears. **[2]**
**(d)** Delivery delays have mean 5 minutes and standard deviation 4 minutes. Find P(X < 0) under
N(5, 4²), and use it to comment on a Normal model for the delays. **[3]**

**10.** The lengths of bolts made by a machine are modelled by X ~ N(50, 0.4²), in mm. A bolt is
rejected if its length is less than 49.3 mm or more than 50.6 mm.

**(a)** Show that the probability a bolt is rejected is 0.107, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Bolts are packed in boxes of 30. Find the probability that a box contains more than 3
rejected bolts. **[3]**
**(c)** State one assumption needed for your model in (b). **[1]**
**(d)** The mean stays at 50 mm. Find the standard deviation needed so that only 1% of bolts are
longer than 50.6 mm. **[3]**

**11.** In a town, 45% of adults hold a library card. A random sample of 150 adults is taken.
X is the number with a library card.

**(a)** Find P(X ≤ 60). **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why X can be approximated by a Normal distribution, and state its mean and
variance. **[3]**
**(c)** Use the Normal approximation, with a continuity correction, to estimate P(X ≤ 60). **[3]**
**(d)** Compare your answers to (a) and (c). **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** c(1 + 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6) = 1 [1], so 2c = 1 and **c = 1/2** [1].
**(b)** P(X < 6) = (1/2)(1 + 1/2 + 1/3) = **11/12** [1].
*Examiner insight:* (a) is a "show that", so the sum of the fractions equal to 1 must appear;
writing only "c = 1/2" earns nothing.

**2. (a)** **X ~ B(25, 0.25)** [1]. Each guess is independent of the others [1]. The probability
of a correct guess is 1/4 for every question, as each has four options [1].
**(b)** P(X ≥ 10) = 1 − P(X ≤ 9) [1] = **0.0713** [1].
*Examiner insight:* conditions copied from a textbook without the quiz context usually earn at most
one of the two marks.

**3. (a)** ¹⁵C₅ (0.35)⁵ (0.65)¹⁰ [1] = **0.212** [1].
**(b)** P(X < 4) = P(X ≤ 3) [1] = **0.173** [1].
**(c)** P(X ≤ 8) − P(X ≤ 3) [1] = **0.785** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the method mark is for the correct rewrite; P(X ≤ 4) for (b) loses both marks
even with a correct calculator entry.

**4. (a)** X ~ B(40, 0.85) [1]. P(X ≥ 36) = 1 − P(X ≤ 35) [1] = **0.263** [1].
**(b)** P(X < 32) = P(X ≤ 31) [1] = **0.135** [1].
**(c)** Seeds in the same tray share conditions, so if it is cold, germination of one seed is
linked to others and they are not independent (or p may be lower than 0.85) [1].
*Examiner insight:* stating the distribution earns the first mark in (a); a correct probability
with no model shown risks losing it.

**5. (a)** P(X > 180) with σ = 8 [1] = **0.106** [1].
**(b)** P(160 < X < 175) [1] = **0.628** [1].
**(c)** P(X < h) = 0.95, inverse Normal [1], h = 183.2 = **183 cm** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c), using 0.05 instead of 0.95 gives a value below the mean; a quick check
that the answer is above 170 catches this.

**6. (a)** Inflection points are at μ ± σ, so **μ = 45** [1] and **σ = 7** [1].
**(b)** X ~ N(45, 7²) [1], P(X > 50) = **0.238** [1].
*Examiner insight:* σ is half the distance between the points of inflection; quoting σ = 14 loses
the second mark in (a) and the follow-on accuracy in (b).

**7.** P(T < 40) = 0.2 gives z = −0.8416 [1], so (40 − μ)/σ = −0.8416 [1].
P(T > 70) = 0.1 gives z = 1.2816 [1], so (70 − μ)/σ = 1.2816 [1].
Subtracting: 30 = 2.1232σ, so **σ = 14.1** [1], and **μ = 51.9** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the z for 0.2 must be negative; using +0.8416 makes the equations inconsistent
and loses the accuracy marks that follow.

**8. (a)** X ~ N(24.6, 1.8²) [1]. P(X > 27) = 0.0912 [1]. 400 × 0.0912 = 36.5, so **about 36 or
37 leaves** [1].
**(b)** 38 is close to the expected 36.5, so the Normal model fits well in this tail [1].
*Examiner insight:* an expected frequency need not be a whole number, but you lose the final mark
if you leave the answer as a probability.

**9. (a)** **Neither binomial nor Normal** [1]: cards are dealt without replacement, so the
probability of a heart changes and the deals are not independent [1].
**(b)** **Normal** [1]: mass is continuous and the data are symmetrical and bell-shaped [1].
**(c)** **Neither** [1]: the number of trials is not fixed, so it is not binomial, and the variable
is discrete [1].
**(d)** P(X < 0) = **0.106** [1]. A delay cannot be negative [1], yet the model gives about 11% of
deliveries a negative delay, so a Normal model is not appropriate (the data are likely skewed) [1].
*Examiner insight:* the reason mark needs the context; "p is not constant" without saying why
(cards are not replaced) is unlikely to earn it.

**10. (a)** P(X < 49.3) = 0.0401 [1]. P(X > 50.6) = 0.0668 [1]. Sum = 0.10687 = **0.107** [1].
**(b)** R ~ B(30, 0.107) [1]. P(R > 3) = 1 − P(R ≤ 3) [1] = **0.401** [1].
**(c)** Bolts are rejected independently of each other [1].
**(d)** P(X > 50.6) = 0.01 gives z = 2.3263 [1]. 0.6/σ = 2.3263 [1], **σ = 0.258 mm** [1].
*Examiner insight:* (a) is a "show that", so give both tail probabilities and an answer to more
than 3 s.f. before rounding; quoting 0.107 alone earns nothing.

**11. (a)** **0.125** [1].
**(b)** n is large and p is close to 0.5 (np = 67.5 and n(1 − p) = 82.5 are both large) [1].
Mean = 150 × 0.45 = **67.5** [1]. Variance = 150 × 0.45 × 0.55 = **37.125** [1].
**(c)** P(X ≤ 60) ≈ P(Y < 60.5) [1]. z = (60.5 − 67.5)/√37.125 = −1.149 [1], so P = **0.125** [1].
**(d)** The approximation agrees with the exact value to 3 s.f. [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c), using 60 instead of 60.5 loses the continuity-correction mark and
gives 0.109, which then fails the accuracy mark too.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the variance into the Normal function: N(170, 8²) needs σ = 8, and N(40, 9) needs σ = 3.
- Rewriting "at least" or "more than" one step out for a binomial.
- Stating binomial conditions in general terms with no link to the context.
- Missing the sign of z when the given probability is below the mean.
- Rounding z to 2 decimal places before multiplying by σ in a "find σ" question.
- Using "show that" answers as the only working, so no method can be credited.
- Judging a Normal model without checking for impossible values such as negative times.
- Leaving an expected frequency as a probability.
- Forgetting the continuity correction when using the Normal link to the binomial.
