## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between a reductionist approach and a systems approach to an environmental
issue. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each of the following as an open, closed or isolated system, giving a reason for
each: (i) a river drainage basin, (ii) planet Earth. **[2]**

**3.** A solar panel receives 1,000 W of sunlight. It produces 180 W of electrical power, loses 650 W
as heat and reflects the rest.

**(a)** Calculate the power reflected. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of the panel. **[1]**
**(c)** State which output arrow would be widest on a Sankey diagram. **[1]**

**4.** A wetland stores 120 million m³ of water. Its yearly flows (million m³) are: rainfall 14, river
inflow 36, evaporation 18, river outflow 29, abstraction for farms 5.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual change in storage. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify one transfer and one transformation in this system. **[2]**
**(c)** Using total inputs, calculate the residence time of water in the wetland. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate how many years it would take the storage to fall by 10% if the flows stay the same.
**[1]**

**5.** Outline why the residence time of water in a deep aquifer matters when the aquifer is
polluted. **[2]**

**6.** Construct a feedback chain showing how thawing permafrost could affect global temperature,
and state the type of feedback. **[3]**

**7.** In a meadow, rabbits graze grass. Explain how negative feedback keeps the rabbit population
close to a steady state. **[3]**

**8.** Outline **three** characteristics of a tipping point. **[3]**

**9.** Two grasslands are hit by the same drought. Grassland X has 40 plant species and deep soil.
Grassland Y is a sown pasture with 2 species on thin soil. Explain which is likely to be more
resilient. **[3]**

**10.** Define *emergent property* and give an example from an ecosystem. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a lake that has shifted to a turbid, algae-dominated state may not return to a
clear state when nutrient inputs are reduced. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate the use of models to predict the future state of an environmental system. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** A reductionist approach studies individual parts separately, such as one species [1]; a
systems approach studies the interactions between parts and the system as a whole [1]. **[2]**

**2.** (i) Open: water, sediment, energy and organisms cross its boundary [1]. (ii) Closed
(approximately): energy enters and leaves but almost no matter crosses the boundary [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 1000 − 180 − 650 = **170 W** [1]
**(b)** 180 / 1000 × 100 = **18%** [1]
**(c)** Heat loss (650 W) [1] **[3]**

**4. (a)** Inputs = 14 + 36 = 50; outputs = 18 + 29 + 5 = 52 [1]; net change = 50 − 52 =
**−2 million m³ per year** [1]
**(b)** Transfer: river inflow, river outflow or abstraction [1]. Transformation: evaporation (liquid
to gas) [1].
**(c)** 120 / 50 = **2.4 years** [1]
**(d)** 10% of 120 = 12 million m³; 12 / 2 = **6 years** [1] **[6]**
*Marking note:* a negative sign or the word "loss" is needed in (a); "2" alone does not show the
direction.

**5.** Water stays in the aquifer for a long time, so pollutants are flushed out very slowly [1];
the aquifer may remain unusable for decades after the source of pollution stops [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Warming thaws permafrost [1]; decomposition of the thawed organic matter releases carbon
dioxide and methane, increasing the greenhouse effect [1]; this causes further warming and more
thaw, so it is positive feedback [1]. **[3]**

**7.** As rabbit numbers rise, grass per rabbit falls [1]; food shortage lowers birth rates and raises
death rates (and predators may increase) [1]; numbers fall, grass recovers and the population
returns towards its earlier level, so the change is counteracted [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Any three: a threshold beyond which the system shifts to a new state [1]; little visible change
before the threshold is crossed [1]; the shift is driven by positive feedback [1]; the new state is
self-reinforcing and hard to reverse [1]; the exact position of the threshold is hard to predict [1].
**[3]**

**9.** Grassland X is likely to be more resilient [1]. With 40 species, some are likely to be
drought-tolerant and can maintain cover and function while others decline [1]. Deep soil is a large
storage of water, nutrients and seeds, allowing faster recovery after the drought [1]. **[3]**

**10.** A property of a whole system that arises from the interactions between its parts and is not
shown by the parts alone [1]; for example, the stability of a food web or the regulation of local
climate by a forest [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The turbid state has its own reinforcing feedbacks [1]. Algae shade the lake bed, so rooted
plants cannot regrow [1], and nutrients stored in the sediments continue to be released into the
water, so nutrient levels stay high even after inputs fall [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Award up to 7: strengths, up to 3 [1] [1] [1]; limitations, up to 3 [1] [1] [1]; a judgement
that answers the question [1].
- *Strengths:* simplify complex systems so they can be understood [1]; allow predictions of what
  happens when inputs change, such as emission scenarios [1]; can be tested against observations and
  improved [1]; let managers compare options without real-world risk [1].
- *Limitations:* leave out storages and flows that may matter [1]; rely on assumptions that may be
  wrong [1]; input data may be limited or uncertain [1]; uncertainty grows with time and with
  distance beyond the data used [1]; tipping points are hard to model [1].
- *Judgement:* for example, models are essential for exploring possible futures but should be used
  as ranges of outcomes with stated uncertainty, not as exact forecasts [1]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving units off residence times and net changes.
- Writing a feedback chain without naming the type of feedback.
- Calling Earth an isolated system.
- Evaluating a model with no judgement at the end.
