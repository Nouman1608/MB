## What this sub-topic asks you to do

- Explain what global power is and the different forms it takes.
- Examine which states and non-state actors hold power, and how this has changed over time.
- Analyse how global interactions, through groupings and institutions, reflect and reinforce power.
- Evaluate ways of measuring power and the idea of superpower status.

## How power is built and kept

Power is not only something states have; it is something they maintain through the systems that connect places. Four mechanisms explain why power can persist even when a state's share of world output falls.

- **Rules.** States that write the rules of institutions gain lasting advantages. Voting shares in the IMF and World Bank still reflect contributions made decades ago.
- **Currency.** When trade, debt and reserves are held mainly in one currency, the issuing country can borrow cheaply and can cut others off from the financial system through sanctions.
- **Standards and platforms.** Whoever sets technical standards, operating systems or payment systems shapes how others connect, and gathers data about them.
- **Alliances and bases.** Military alliances and overseas bases allow a state to project force far from its own territory.

Seen this way, power is **relational**: it exists in the relationships between places, and it is reproduced every time other places use the systems a powerful state controls.

## Two less obvious forms of power

### Regulatory power

The legal scholar Anu Bradford named the **Brussels effect**: because the European Union is a very large, wealthy market, companies that want to sell there adopt its rules, and often apply them worldwide rather than run separate systems. The General Data Protection Regulation, applied from 2018, led many firms outside Europe to change how they handle personal data. The EU has relatively little military power, so the example shows that rule-setting can be a form of global power in its own right.

### Energy power

Control of energy exports can be used as leverage. Russia cut gas supplies through Ukraine during price disputes in January 2006 and January 2009, affecting countries further along the pipelines in Europe. European dependence on Russian gas then became a major vulnerability after the full-scale invasion of Ukraine in 2022. The lesson is that interdependence is not symmetrical: when one side can switch suppliers more easily than the other, interdependence becomes a source of power for the less dependent side.

## Judging superpower status

A **superpower** is usually described as a state able to project influence globally across several dimensions at once. Useful criteria are:

| Criterion | Question to ask |
|---|---|
| Economic | Does it have a very large economy and influence over global trade and finance? |
| Military | Can it project force worldwide, not only regionally? |
| Political | Does it shape decisions in global institutions, including through a veto? |
| Cultural | Do its ideas, media, universities and brands attract people worldwide? |
| Demographic and resource | Does it have the population and resources to sustain influence? |

The test is breadth. A state strong on one or two criteria, such as a large economy without global military reach, is better described as an emerging power or a regional power. Applying the criteria explicitly is better than asserting that a country "is" or "is not" a superpower.

## Worked example: indicators and weighting

Scores are invented, on a scale of 0 to 100, for three states.

```
               Economic   Military   Soft power
State X           90         95          80
State Y           85         60          50
State Z           40         30          95

Equal weights (one third each)
  X: (90 + 95 + 80) / 3 = 88.3
  Y: (85 + 60 + 50) / 3 = 65.0
  Z: (40 + 30 + 95) / 3 = 55.0
  Ranking: X, Y, Z

Soft power weighted at 50%, economic 25%, military 25%
  X: 0.25 x 90 + 0.25 x 95 + 0.5 x 80 = 22.5 + 23.75 + 40 = 86.25
  Y: 0.25 x 85 + 0.25 x 60 + 0.5 x 50 = 21.25 + 15 + 25 = 61.25
  Z: 0.25 x 40 + 0.25 x 30 + 0.5 x 95 = 10 + 7.5 + 47.5 = 65.0
  Ranking: X, Z, Y
```

The data are unchanged, but a different weighting makes State Z overtake State Y. The lesson to write: any ranking of power reflects choices about which kinds of power matter most, so an evaluation of a power index should name what it includes, how it weights components, and whose view of power that weighting represents.

## Changing power over time

Power shifts as economies grow, as technologies change and as alliances form or break. Three features of recent change are worth explaining:

- **Economic convergence.** Emerging economies, above all China and India, have taken a growing share of world output and trade since the 1990s.
- **New institutions.** Emerging powers have built alternatives alongside the post-war system, such as the New Development Bank set up by the BRICS countries.
- **Technology.** Power over data, semiconductors and digital platforms has become a new arena of competition, involving corporations as much as states.

## Annotated model answer

*Examine the view that global power is shifting from the USA to China.* **(sample paragraph)**

"In economic terms the shift is real: China has become the leading trading nation and, through the Belt and Road Initiative, a major source of infrastructure finance across Asia and Africa **[evidence for the view, specific]**. However, power is maintained through rules and currency as well as output, and the dollar remains the dominant currency for trade and reserves, giving the USA financial leverage China lacks **[counter-evidence using a mechanism of power]**. Military reach is also unequal, with the USA maintaining alliances and bases worldwide **[a second dimension]**. The shift is therefore partial and uneven between dimensions of power, rather than a simple transfer **[examined judgement]**."

## Common mistakes

- Equating power with the size of an economy.
- Treating interdependence as always equal between partners.
- Calling a state a superpower without applying criteria.
- Forgetting that institutions and rules can preserve power after economic weight declines.
- Using a power ranking without questioning its indicators and weights.

## Exam technique

- **Examine** questions on power reward separating its dimensions and judging each with evidence.
- **Discuss** needs more than one perspective, for example those of an established power, an emerging power and a smaller state.
- **To what extent** needs a clear position on a scale: a partial shift, a complete shift or no shift.
- **Evaluate** a measure of power by naming its indicators, weights and omissions.
- Use located, dated evidence you are confident of, and define power at the start.
