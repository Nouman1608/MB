Section 2.3 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 lists the output devices you must know: **monitors, touch screen (as an output device), multimedia projector, laser printer, inkjet printer, dot matrix printer, plotter, 3D printers, speaker** and **actuator**. As with input devices, the syllabus asks for the **characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages** of each. The printers are the most frequently compared group, so this guide gives them a comparison table of their own.

For condensed recall use the [output devices revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-devices-and-their-uses-revision-notes/), and test yourself with the [output devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-output-devices-and-their-uses-practice/).

## Soft copy and hard copy

Output is either **soft copy**, which is temporary and shown on a screen or played as sound, or **hard copy**, which is a permanent physical result such as a printout or a 3D object. Actuators are different again: their output is **movement or action** in a control system.

## Monitors

- **Characteristics**: display the computer's output as images on a screen made of pixels. Most modern monitors are flat **LCD** screens lit from behind by **LEDs**; **OLED** screens produce their own light from each pixel, so they can be very thin and even flexible.
- **Uses**: showing the output of almost every computer system; large monitors for design and video editing; information displays at stations and airports.
- **Advantages**: output is seen immediately; can be changed instantly without wasting paper; flat screens are light, thin and use relatively little power; OLED gives high contrast and can be curved.
- **Disadvantages**: output is temporary (soft copy); screens are hard to read in bright sunlight; prolonged use can cause eye strain and headaches; large high-resolution monitors are expensive.

## Touch screen (as an output device)

- **Characteristics**: the same screen displays output and accepts touch input, so the options the user can choose are shown exactly where they must be touched.
- **Uses**: smartphones and tablets; kiosks; self-service checkouts; car dashboards.
- **Advantages**: no separate display and input device needed; menus and buttons can change depending on the task.
- **Disadvantages**: fingerprints and smears reduce clarity; small screens limit how much output can be shown; screen can be scratched or cracked.

## Multimedia projector

- **Characteristics**: receives signals from a computer or other source and projects an enlarged image onto a screen or wall.
- **Uses**: presentations in classrooms and conference rooms; showing films in home cinemas; advertising displays.
- **Advantages**: a very large image that many people can see at once; can show video and animation; one computer can display to a whole room.
- **Disadvantages**: image is harder to see in a bright room; the lamp or light source can be expensive and eventually wears out; can be noisy because of cooling fans; people walking in front of it block the image; setting up and focusing takes time.

## Laser printer

- **Characteristics**: uses **toner** (a fine powder) and a drum. An electrostatic charge is used to attract toner to the parts of the page to be printed, and heat fuses it onto the paper. A whole page is processed at a time.
- **Uses**: high-volume printing in offices and schools; letters, reports and leaflets.
- **Advantages**: very fast for large numbers of pages; high quality, sharp text; low cost per page; toner cartridges print many pages before needing replacing; quiet.
- **Disadvantages**: more expensive to buy than an inkjet printer, especially for colour; toner cartridges are expensive to replace; can give off ozone and toner particles, so it should be in a well-ventilated area; larger and heavier.

## Inkjet printer

- **Characteristics**: sprays tiny droplets of liquid **ink** through nozzles in a print head onto the paper as it moves.
- **Uses**: home printing; small numbers of high-quality colour photographs; printing occasional documents.
- **Advantages**: cheap to buy; excellent colour and photo quality; small and light; quiet.
- **Disadvantages**: ink cartridges are expensive and run out quickly, so cost per page is high; slower than a laser printer for large volumes; ink can smudge before it dries; nozzles can block if not used for a while.

## Dot matrix printer

- **Characteristics**: an **impact printer**. A print head with a grid of pins strikes an inked ribbon against the paper, making characters out of small dots. It often uses continuous paper fed by sprockets.
- **Uses**: printing **multi-part** forms with carbon or carbonless copies, such as invoices and delivery notes; environments that are dirty or damp, such as some factories and warehouses.
- **Advantages**: can print several copies at once because the impact goes through the layers; continues to work in dusty or damp conditions where other printers may fail; very low running costs.
- **Disadvantages**: noisy; slow; poor print quality compared with laser and inkjet printers; limited or no colour.

## Plotter

- **Characteristics**: produces large, very accurate drawings. Traditional pen plotters move a pen across the paper to draw lines; many modern large-format plotters use inkjet technology. Paper can be very large, such as A0 size, or on rolls.
- **Uses**: architectural plans, engineering drawings, maps, large posters and banners, output from CAD software.
- **Advantages**: very high precision; very large printouts; can print on a range of materials.
- **Disadvantages**: expensive to buy; slow; takes up a lot of space; not suitable for everyday small documents.

## 3D printers

- **Characteristics**: build a solid object **layer by layer** from a digital design, usually produced with CAD software. Materials include plastics, resins and powdered metals. This is called **additive manufacturing**.
- **Uses**: prototypes of new products; replacement parts; customised medical items such as prosthetic limbs and dental parts; models for architecture and education.
- **Advantages**: one-off and customised items can be made without expensive moulds; prototypes are produced quickly and cheaply, so designs can be tested and changed; less waste than cutting objects from a solid block.
- **Disadvantages**: slow for large objects; printers and some materials are expensive; the surface finish may need further work; can be used to make illegal or dangerous items, or to copy designs without permission.

## Speaker

- **Characteristics**: converts electrical signals into sound. The computer's digital sound data is converted to an analogue signal (by a sound card's digital-to-analogue converter) and amplified to move the speaker's cone.
- **Uses**: playing music and video; voice output from assistants and screen readers; alarms and alerts; video-conferencing.
- **Advantages**: sound can be heard by many people at once; helps people with visual impairments through screen-reading software; gives alerts without the user looking at a screen.
- **Disadvantages**: can disturb others; output is not private; sound quality depends on the speaker; output is temporary.

## Actuator

- **Characteristics**: an output device that produces **movement or action** in response to a signal from a computer or microprocessor. Examples include **motors**, **pumps**, **valves**, **solenoids** and heating elements switched on by the system. Many need a digital-to-analogue conversion or a switching circuit.
- **Uses**: control systems: opening greenhouse windows, switching heaters on and off, turning on sprinklers, controlling robot arms, opening automatic doors, moving valves in industrial processes.
- **Advantages**: allows a computer to act on the physical world automatically; works continuously and responds quickly; can act in places that are dangerous for people.
- **Disadvantages**: can fail mechanically and need maintenance; a wrong signal or sensor fault can cause the wrong action; adds cost to a system.

## Printer comparison

| | Laser | Inkjet | Dot matrix | Plotter | 3D printer |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| How it prints | Toner fused by heat | Ink droplets sprayed | Pins strike ribbon | Pen or ink on large sheets | Layers of material |
| Speed | Fast | Slow to medium | Slow | Slow | Very slow |
| Quality | High | High, best for photos | Low | Very precise | Depends on printer |
| Cost to buy | Higher | Low | Low to medium | High | Medium to high |
| Cost per page or item | Low | High | Very low | High | Varies with material |
| Best for | High volume documents | Small runs of colour | Multi-part forms | Large technical drawings | Physical objects |

## Worked example: justifying a printer choice

*An architect's office prints about 2,000 pages of black-and-white letters each week, and occasional large building plans. Recommend printers for these tasks and justify your choices. [4]*

> A laser printer for the letters (1), because it is fast and has a low cost per page, which suits a high volume of printing each week (1). A plotter for the building plans (1), because it can print very large sheets with the precise lines needed for technical drawings (1).

## Common mistakes

- Saying a laser printer "uses ink". It uses toner powder.
- Describing a dot matrix printer as "out of date" without giving its real advantage: multi-part forms and harsh environments.
- Calling an actuator a sensor. Sensors measure (input); actuators act (output).
- Saying a plotter prints "faster". Plotters are chosen for size and accuracy.
- Forgetting the conversion step for speakers: digital data becomes an analogue signal.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** the device from a description: look for clues such as toner, impact, layers, large drawings or movement.
- **Describe** how a device works: give two or three steps in order.
- **Compare** printers: use speed, quality, cost to buy, cost per page and suitability for the volume of printing.
- **Justify** a choice: tie each feature to the scenario's needs.
