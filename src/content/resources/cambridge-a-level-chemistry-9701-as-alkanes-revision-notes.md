Alkanes.

## Making alkanes

| Route | Reagents and conditions | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogenation of an alkene | H₂(g), Pt or Ni catalyst, heat | CH₂=CHCH₃ + H₂ → CH₃CH₂CH₃ |
| Cracking a longer-chain alkane | heat with Al₂O₃ | C₁₂H₂₆ → C₆H₁₄ + C₆H₁₂ |

Cracking always gives a shorter alkane and at least one alkene in the equations you will write. Uses:
lower-Mr alkanes for fuels such as petrol; alkenes for polymers and chemical synthesis. It matches the
supply of crude-oil fractions to demand.

## Combustion

| Type | Oxygen supply | Carbon product | Example (pentane) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Complete | plentiful | CO₂ | C₅H₁₂ + 8O₂ → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O |
| Incomplete | limited | CO | C₅H₁₂ + 5½O₂ → 5CO + 6H₂O |
| Incomplete | very limited | C (soot) | C₅H₁₂ + 3O₂ → 5C + 6H₂O |

Balancing order: carbon, then hydrogen, then oxygen. For gases at the same temperature and pressure,
volume ratio = mole ratio.

## Why alkanes are unreactive

- C–H (410 kJ mol⁻¹) and C–C (350 kJ mol⁻¹) bonds are **strong**.
- C (2.5) and H (2.1) have similar electronegativity, so C–H bonds are **almost non-polar**.
- No δ+ carbon for nucleophiles, no π electrons or lone pairs for electrophiles.
- They react only in combustion and with halogen radicals in UV light.

## Free-radical substitution (ethane + Cl₂ or Br₂, UV light)

| Stage | What happens | Equations (chlorine) |
|---|---|---|
| Initiation | UV light causes homolytic fission of the halogen | Cl₂ → 2Cl• |
| Propagation | radical used, radical regenerated: a chain reaction | Cl• + C₂H₆ → C₂H₅• + HCl; C₂H₅• + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + Cl• |
| Termination | two radicals combine | Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂; C₂H₅• + Cl• → C₂H₅Cl; 2C₂H₅• → C₄H₁₀ |

- Product mixture: further substitution (C₂H₄Cl₂ and beyond) plus butane from termination.
- Excess alkane favours the monosubstituted product.
- Number of monosubstituted isomers = number of distinct H environments (propane: 2; butane: 2;
  2,2-dimethylpropane: 1).

## Engine pollutants and catalytic converters

| Pollutant | Origin | Effect | Removed by |
|---|---|---|---|
| CO | incomplete combustion | toxic: binds to haemoglobin | oxidation to CO₂ |
| NO, NO₂ | N₂ + O₂ at high engine temperature | acid rain, photochemical smog | reduction to N₂ |
| Unburnt hydrocarbons | fuel not fully burnt | photochemical smog | oxidation to CO₂ + H₂O |

Key converter equation: **2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂** (Pt/Rh catalyst).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Saturated | contains only single C–C bonds |
| Cracking | breaking long-chain alkanes into shorter alkanes and alkenes |
| Hydrogenation | addition of H₂ across a C=C |
| Free radical | species with an unpaired electron |
| Homolytic fission | bond breaking in which each atom keeps one electron |
| Chain reaction | propagation steps regenerate the radical, so one initiation leads to many reactions |

## Exam traps

- Free-radical substitution needs **UV light**, not heat or a catalyst.
- Propagation steps make HCl or HBr, never H•.
- Radicals need the dot; molecules do not.
- NOx comes from **atmospheric N₂**, not from the fuel.
- Hydrogenation needs a **catalyst** (Pt or Ni); cracking uses **Al₂O₃**. Do not swap them.
- "Alkanes are unreactive because they are stable" is not an explanation: give bond strength **and**
  polarity.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions to convert propene into propane.
2. Complete the cracking equation C₁₈H₃₈ → C₁₂H₂₆ + ___ when the only other product is propene.
3. Write the equation for the complete combustion of butane.
4. Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of methane to give carbon monoxide.
5. State two reasons why alkanes do not react with nucleophiles.
6. Write the initiation step for the reaction of ethane with bromine.
7. Write the two propagation steps for the chlorination of methane.
8. Explain why the chlorination of ethane is called a chain reaction.
9. How many monochloro isomers can be formed from butane? Name them.
10. Explain how nitrogen monoxide forms in a car engine.
11. Write an equation for the removal of CO and NO in a catalytic converter.
12. 10 cm³ of ethane is burned completely. What volume of oxygen is used, measured at the same
    temperature and pressure?

**Answers:**

1. H₂(g) with a Pt or Ni catalyst and heat.
2. The remaining fragment is C₆H₁₂, so the answer is 2C₃H₆: C₁₈H₃₈ → C₁₂H₂₆ + 2C₃H₆.
3. C₄H₁₀ + 6½O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O (or 2C₄H₁₀ + 13O₂ → 8CO₂ + 10H₂O).
4. CH₄ + 1½O₂ → CO + 2H₂O (or 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O).
5. C–H bonds are strong; C–H bonds are almost non-polar, so there is no δ+ carbon to attack.
6. Br₂ → 2Br• (UV light).
7. Cl• + CH₄ → CH₃• + HCl; CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•.
8. Each propagation step regenerates a chlorine radical, so one initiation can lead to many
   substitutions.
9. Two: 1-chlorobutane and 2-chlorobutane.
10. At the high temperature in the engine, nitrogen and oxygen from the air combine: N₂ + O₂ → 2NO.
11. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
12. C₂H₆ + 3½O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O, so 3.5 × 10 = 35 cm³.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need further substitution mechanisms for 9701?**
You need to know that further substitution happens and gives a mixture; the named example is ethane, and
the mechanism is asked for the monosubstitution steps.

**Which catalysts should I name for a catalytic converter?**
Platinum and rhodium are the usual answers; the syllabus asks you to recognise catalytic removal of the
pollutants rather than the converter's construction.
