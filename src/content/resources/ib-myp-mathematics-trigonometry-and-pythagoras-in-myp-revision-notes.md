## Methods in steps

**Choosing a tool (algorithmic thinking)**
1. Sketch and label. Mark right angles and north lines.
2. Right angle present? Two sides known and a side wanted: Pythagoras. An angle involved: sin, cos or tan.
3. No right angle? Side and its opposite angle known, plus one more value: sine rule. Two sides and the included angle, or all three sides: cosine rule.
4. Check the result against the sketch. Round at the end.

**Right-angled trigonometry**
1. Mark the angle you are using. Label opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse from it.
2. Pick the ratio that links the two sides involved.
3. Unknown on top of the fraction: multiply. Unknown on the bottom: divide.
4. For an angle, use the inverse function.

**Bearings**
1. Draw a north line at the starting point of each leg.
2. Measure clockwise to the direction of travel.
3. For the return direction, add or subtract 180°.
4. Join legs with triangles and use the rules above.

## Small worked reminders

- **Unknown on top.** Angle 52°, adjacent 15 m, find the opposite: opposite = 15 tan 52° = **19.2 m**.
- **Unknown on the bottom.** Opposite 6 cm, angle 25°, find hypotenuse: 6 ÷ sin 25° = **14.2 cm**.
- **Find an angle.** Opposite 4.5, hypotenuse 12: sin θ = 4.5/12, so θ = **22.0°**.
- **Back bearing.** Bearing 072° out means 252° back.
- **Cosine rule order.** Square and add the two sides, subtract 2bc cos A, then take the square root last.
- **Ambiguous case.** sin B = 0.857 gives B = 59.0° and B = 121.0°. Test each with the angle sum.
- **Converse.** 15, 20, 25: 225 + 400 = 625 = 25², so right-angled. 5, 6, 8: 25 + 36 = 61 but 8² = 64, so not.

## Two fuller reminders

**Isosceles triangle with the apex angle given.** Equal sides 7 cm, apex 40°. Split it down the middle into two right-angled triangles. Each has hypotenuse 7 and an angle of 20° at the apex. Half the base is 7 sin 20°, so the base is 2 × 7 × sin 20° = **4.79 cm**. The base angles are (180 − 40)/2 = 70° each.

**Bearings with a right angle.** A walker goes 5 km on bearing 090°, then 12 km on bearing 180°. The two legs meet at a right angle.
- Distance from the start: √(25 + 144) = **13 km**.
- The angle below the east line is tan⁻¹(12/5) = 67.4°, so the bearing from the start is 090° + 67.4° = **157.4°**.
- The bearing of the start from the end is 157.4° + 180° = **337.4°**.

## Enrichment reminders

- **Area rule.** Sides 6 and 7 enclosing 50°: ½ × 6 × 7 × sin 50° = **16.1**.
- **Unit circle.** At 210° the point is (cos 210°, sin 210°) = (−0.866, −0.5). Both are negative because the point is in the third quadrant.
- **Radians.** 3π/2 rad = 270°. And 0.8 rad = 0.8 × 180/π = 45.8°.
- **Circle at the origin.** A circle with centre (0, 0) through (−6, 8) has r² = 36 + 64 = 100, so the equation is **x² + y² = 100**.
- **Identity use.** If cos θ = −0.6 and θ is obtuse, then sin θ = √(1 − 0.36) = 0.8 and tan θ = 0.8 ÷ (−0.6) = **−4/3**.
- **Proving an identity.** Show that (1 − cos²θ) / sin θ = sin θ. Replace 1 − cos²θ with sin²θ, then divide: sin²θ / sin θ = sin θ.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Pythagoras vs trigonometry.** Pythagoras links sides only. Trigonometry links a side with an angle.
- **Sine rule vs cosine rule.** The sine rule needs a matching side and angle pair. The cosine rule needs two sides and the included angle, or three sides.
- **Exact vs rounded.** Keep full values in the calculator and round once, at the end.
- **Elevation vs depression.** Elevation is upward from the horizontal. Depression is downward from the horizontal.
- **Bearing vs angle in the diagram.** A bearing is clockwise from north. The triangle angle you need is often a different angle, found from parallel north lines.
- **Radians vs degrees.** Multiply degrees by π/180 to get radians. The two modes must match the angle you enter.

## Presenting solutions

- Write the formula first, then substitute, then give the answer. This shows your reasoning (criterion C) and lets a reader follow where a slip happened.
- Give a diagram for any bearings or elevation problem, with the given values marked on it.
- State the degree of accuracy you use, for example 3 significant figures, and keep to it.
- In a real-life task (criterion D), finish with a sentence that checks the answer against the situation: is a 240 m ladder reasonable? Does the distance fit the map?
- In an investigation (criterion B), look for a pattern first, for example in Pythagorean triples such as 3, 4, 5 and 5, 12, 13, then test it on new cases before claiming a general rule.

## Triangle property checks

- Interior angles: add them. If they do not reach 180°, something is missing.
- Exterior angle: use it as a shortcut instead of finding the third angle first.
- Sides: before drawing, test the triangle inequality for the three lengths.
- Isosceles: if two angles are equal, the sides opposite them are equal too.
- Right-angled: if the hypotenuse is given, no other side can be longer than it.

## Quick self-test

1. Two angles of a triangle are 52° and 67°. Find the third.
2. The bearing of B from A is 072°. Find the bearing of A from B.
3. A right-angled triangle has legs 7 and 24. Find the hypotenuse.
4. A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 13 and one leg 5. Find the other leg.
5. Find the side opposite 35° when the hypotenuse is 9.0 cm. Give 3 s.f.
6. Find θ if tan θ = 0.75, to 1 d.p.
7. (Extended) Is a triangle with sides 8, 15 and 17 right-angled? Show why.
8. (Extended) In a triangle, a = 10, A = 30° and B = 100°. Find b to 3 s.f.
9. (Extended) Two sides, 5 and 6, enclose an angle of 60°. Find the third side to 3 s.f.
10. (Enrichment) Find the distance from (0, 0, 0) to (2, 3, 6), and write 100° in radians as a multiple of π.
11. (Enrichment) If sin θ = 12/13 and θ is acute, find cos θ.

### Answers

1. 180 − 52 − 67 = **61°**.
2. 072° + 180° = **252°**.
3. √(49 + 576) = √625 = **25**.
4. √(169 − 25) = √144 = **12**.
5. 9.0 × sin 35° = **5.16 cm**.
6. θ = tan⁻¹(0.75) = **36.9°**.
7. 64 + 225 = 289 = 17², so **yes**, right-angled.
8. b = 10 × sin 100° ÷ sin 30° = **19.7**.
9. √(25 + 36 − 2 × 5 × 6 × cos 60°) = √(61 − 30) = √31 = **5.57**.
10. √(4 + 9 + 36) = **7**. 100° = 100π/180 = **5π/9**.
11. cos²θ = 1 − 144/169 = 25/169, so cos θ = **5/13**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not drawing a sketch, so the wrong sides or angles are used.
- Choosing sin when the sides given are opposite and adjacent.
- Adding the squares when the unknown is a leg, rather than subtracting.
- Measuring a bearing anticlockwise, or writing two figures instead of three.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-step problem.
- Giving only one angle from an ambiguous case without checking the other.
- Writing the answer with no units, or an angle with no degree sign.
- Not justifying a converse test: show both sides of the comparison.
- Writing a final answer that does not fit the context, such as a height longer than the ladder. Criterion D asks you to justify whether an answer makes sense.
- Stating a conclusion without a clear line of reasoning, which affects criterion C.
