This guide covers **3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation** in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1, drawn on by Unit 2 and available to the synoptic Unit 5. The subtopic is one equation, but nearly every error made with it is a unit error, so this guide concentrates on getting units right every time.

## What the specification asks for

- The ideal gas equation pV = nRT with the variables in SI units.
- Use the equation in calculations.
- You will not be expected to recall the value of the gas constant, R; it is given when needed. These pages use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## The equation and its units

| Symbol | Quantity | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| p | pressure | pascal, Pa |
| V | volume | cubic metre, m³ |
| n | amount of gas | mol |
| R | gas constant | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| T | temperature | kelvin, K |

The units are consistent because 1 Pa × 1 m³ = 1 J. If p is in Pa and V in m³, then pV is in joules, and dividing by R (in J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹) and T (in K) leaves mol. Any other combination, such as kPa with dm³ used without care, breaks this.

### The conversions you must know

| From | To SI | Operation |
|---|---|---|
| kPa | Pa | × 1000 (× 10³) |
| MPa | Pa | × 10⁶ |
| dm³ | m³ | × 10⁻³ (÷ 1000) |
| cm³ | m³ | × 10⁻⁶ (÷ 1 000 000) |
| °C | K | + 273 |

A useful memory aid for volumes: 1 m³ = 1000 dm³ = 1 000 000 cm³. Because a cubic metre is so large, gas volumes in m³ are usually small numbers in standard form.

### Rearranging

- n = pV ÷ RT
- V = nRT ÷ p
- p = nRT ÷ V
- T = pV ÷ nR

Rearrange before substituting. This keeps the working clear and makes errors easier to spot.

## Worked example 1: volume of a gas

**Question.** Calculate the volume, in cm³, occupied by 0.0500 mol of a gas at 25 °C and 101 kPa. (R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹)

**Method.**

1. Convert: T = 25 + 273 = 298 K; p = 101 × 10³ = 101 000 Pa.
2. V = nRT ÷ p = (0.0500 × 8.31 × 298) ÷ 101 000 = 1.23 × 10⁻³ m³.
3. Convert to cm³: 1.23 × 10⁻³ × 10⁶ = **1230 cm³** (1.23 × 10³ cm³).

**Check.** About 24 dm³ per mole is typical for a gas near room temperature and atmospheric pressure, so 0.05 mol should occupy about 1.2 dm³. The answer is sensible.

## Worked example 2: relative molecular mass of a volatile liquid

A common application is to vaporise a known mass of a volatile liquid, measure the gas volume, and use pV = nRT to find n and so Mr.

**Question.** 0.180 g of a volatile hydrocarbon is vaporised. The vapour occupies 75.0 cm³ at 373 K and 102 kPa. Calculate Mr and suggest a formula for the hydrocarbon.

**Method.**

1. Convert: V = 75.0 × 10⁻⁶ = 7.50 × 10⁻⁵ m³; p = 102 000 Pa.
2. n = pV ÷ RT = (102 000 × 7.50 × 10⁻⁵) ÷ (8.31 × 373) = 2.47 × 10⁻³ mol.
3. Mr = m ÷ n = 0.180 ÷ 2.47 × 10⁻³ = **72.9**.
4. An alkane CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ with Mr near 73 is pentane, C₅H₁₂ (Mr 72.0).

**Comment.** The experimental Mr is slightly high. Examiners may ask why; a sensible suggestion is that some of the vapour condensed, or the mass was measured inaccurately. Keep the explanation tied to how it changes n or m.

## Worked example 3: finding temperature

**Question.** 0.0400 mol of gas is held in a 2.00 dm³ flask at a pressure of 75.0 kPa. Calculate its temperature in °C.

**Method.**

1. Convert: p = 75 000 Pa; V = 2.00 × 10⁻³ m³.
2. T = pV ÷ nR = (75 000 × 2.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (0.0400 × 8.31) = 451 K.
3. In °C: 451 − 273 = **178 °C**.

## Linking with other relationships

The ideal gas equation often sits inside a longer calculation.

- **Gas density.** Combining n = m ÷ Mr with pV = nRT gives m ÷ V = pMr ÷ RT. Denser gases at the same p and T have larger Mr. Carbon dioxide (Mr 44.0) at 298 K and 101 kPa has a density of about 1.79 g dm⁻³.
- **Number of molecules.** Find n with pV = nRT, then multiply by the Avogadro constant (subtopic 3.1.2.2).
- **Reacting volumes.** Find moles of a reactant from its mass, use the equation's mole ratio to find moles of gas, then use pV = nRT to find the gas volume (subtopic 3.1.2.5).

Notice that pV = nRT does not depend on which gas is involved: at the same p and T, equal volumes of gases contain equal amounts in moles.

## Significant figures

Give answers to the same number of significant figures as the least precise data, usually three. Temperatures converted with + 273 keep the precision of the original value; 25 °C becomes 298 K.

## Common mistakes

- Leaving pressure in kPa, giving answers 1000 times too small (for n) or too large (for V).
- Converting cm³ to m³ by dividing by 1000 instead of 10⁶.
- Using temperature in °C, which can even give a negative or zero result.
- Forgetting to convert the final answer back to the unit asked for, such as cm³ or kPa.
- Rearranging incorrectly, for example writing n = pV × RT.
- Using the mass of gas in place of n.
- Rounding n before using it to find Mr.

## Exam technique for OxfordAQA command words

- **Calculate**: state the rearranged equation, convert every quantity to SI first, show the substitution, then give the answer with units.
- **Suggest**: for an unexpected result, name a specific practical cause and say whether it raises or lowers the calculated value.
- **Deduce**: from an Mr found by experiment, pick the formula from a list or homologous series whose Mr is closest, and state the comparison.
- **Show that**: give each conversion explicitly, because the conversion is usually where the marks are.
