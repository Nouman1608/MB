This guide covers section 1.7, External factors, in Topic 1 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Its one point, 1.7.1, names "the external factors affecting business decisions": **social**, **technological**, **environmental** and **political**. The key word is *decisions*. Examiners want to see a factor turned into a choice a business makes about its products, prices, methods, staff or location.

An external factor is one outside the business's control: it cannot stop it, only respond to it. Paper 1 businesses are small and often feel a change quickly but have little money to respond; Paper 2 businesses are large and global, so they face different social and political conditions in each country. The first overview, alongside section 1.6, is in the [government objectives and policies and external factors guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-government-objectives-and-policies-and-external-factors/). Companion pages: the [external factors revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors-revision-notes/) and the [external factors practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-external-factors-practice/). Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars. The specification names the four factors without describing them, so the examples are standard ones.

## 1. From factor to decision

| Factor | What changes | Decisions it can lead to |
|---|---|---|
| Social | Tastes, lifestyles, the age make-up of the population, attitudes to health and ethics | New or changed products, new target markets, opening hours, recruitment |
| Technological | New equipment, software and ways to sell and communicate | Automation, online selling, retraining staff, dropping out-of-date products |
| Environmental | Concern about pollution, waste, resources and climate | Greener materials and packaging, energy saving, changes to transport and suppliers |
| Political | Government decisions and stability: policies on tax, spending, laws and trade; elections | Location, pricing, investment timing, whether to enter a country |

A complete point has three links: the factor, its effect on the business (cost, demand, reputation, risk), and the decision.

## 2. Social factors

Social change works mainly through demand.

### Worked example 1: responding to healthier tastes

Pellow Snacks sells 400,000 packs of crisps a year at 1.20, with a cost of 0.70 a pack. Demand is falling as customers cut salt and fat. It can reformulate: the new recipe costs 0.78 a pack, and it expects sales to recover to 460,000.

- Profit per pack now: 1.20 − 0.70 = 0.50. Total: 400,000 × 0.50 = 200,000.
- After reformulating: 1.20 − 0.78 = 0.42. Total: 460,000 × 0.42 = 193,200.

On these figures profit falls by 6,800 in the first year, but if Pellow does nothing, demand may keep falling. The decision depends on how fast the social trend is moving. A good answer says so.

Other social changes and decisions: an ageing population leads a gym to add classes for older people; more people working from home leads a city-centre café to open near housing; customers who care about fair treatment of workers lead a clothing firm to check its suppliers.

## 3. Technological factors

Technology works mainly through costs and through new ways to reach customers.

### Worked example 2: automating a task

Corbie Packaging can buy a machine for 60,000 that replaces two packing jobs paid 18,000 a year each. The machine costs 6,000 a year to maintain.

- Yearly saving: (2 × 18,000) − 6,000 = 30,000.
- Time to recover the cost: 60,000 ÷ 30,000 = **2 years**.

The figures support buying it, but the business must also weigh the effect on remaining staff, the cost of training them to use the machine, and the risk that a better machine soon makes this one out of date.

Technology also changes how businesses sell (websites and apps), communicate (email and video calls) and design products. It can make an existing product or skill obsolete, which is a threat as well as an opportunity.

## 4. Environmental factors

Environmental concern reaches a business through customers, the government and the wider public. Responding usually costs money now and may save money or win customers later.

### Worked example 3: solar panels

Orchard Garden Centre is quoted 24,000 for solar panels. They should cut electricity bills by 4,800 a year.

- Time to recover the cost: 24,000 ÷ 4,800 = **5 years**.
- After that, the 4,800 a year is a saving for the rest of the panels' life.

The garden centre can also tell customers about the change, which may suit people who buy plants and care about nature. Against this, 24,000 is a large sum for a small business, and the money might earn more elsewhere.

## 5. Political factors

Political factors are the decisions and stability of governments. Policies on tax, spending, laws and trade (section 1.6) are political decisions. So are elections, changes of government and unrest. For a large business operating in many countries, political conditions differ from place to place.

- A stable government with clear policies lets businesses plan and invest with confidence.
- A coming election may lead a business to delay investment until it knows which policies will apply.
- Unrest or sudden policy changes raise the risk of operating in a country, so a multinational may choose another location or keep its investment small.

## 6. Factors that combine

The four factors often work together, and the strongest answers show the link.

*Rising public concern about plastic waste (environmental) leads the government to tax single-use plastic packaging (political). New plant-based materials (technological) let a drinks firm switch. Customers who prefer greener brands (social) reward the change with loyalty.*

## 7. Which factor matters most?

"Evaluate" questions often ask which factor is most significant for a business. Judge on three tests:

1. **Size of effect.** How much could it change costs or revenue?
2. **Speed.** Is it happening now or slowly over years?
3. **Control of the response.** Can the business adapt cheaply, or does it need large investment?

### Annotated "evaluate" answer

**Evaluate which external factor is most significant for a small taxi firm.**

- *Technological:* ride-hailing apps let rivals take bookings instantly, so the firm may lose customers fast unless it builds or joins an app.
- *Environmental:* pressure to use cleaner cars may mean expensive new vehicles.
- *Political:* the local council's licensing rules decide who can operate.
- *Judgement:* technology is most significant now, because it affects demand immediately and the response (joining an app) is affordable; environmental change will matter more when its vehicles are due for replacement.

## Common mistakes

- Listing the four factors with no business decision attached.
- Confusing internal problems (such as poor management) with external factors.
- Assuming every response raises costs; some, like energy saving, cut them over time.
- Treating political factors as only "new laws"; stability, elections and trade policy count too.
- Ignoring how the same factor affects small and large businesses differently.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four external factors in 4BS1?**
Social, technological, environmental and political, as listed in specification point 1.7.1.

**Why are external factors hard for businesses to manage?**
They are outside the business's control, so it can only respond, often with costs, and it may not know how fast the change will happen.
