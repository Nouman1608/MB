These questions cover statements 7.16 to 7.22 (feedback, homeostasis, the kidney, osmoregulation and gene switching) of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level Biology specification (YBI11), Issue 2. Topic 7 is International A2 (IA2) and there are no tiers. Learn the content in the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-biology-homeostasis-the-kidney-osmoregulation-and-gene/) and [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-biology-homeostasis-the-kidney-osmoregulation-and-gene-revision-notes/) first. ## Questions

**1.** (a) Explain what is meant by negative feedback. **[2]**
(b) Explain why positive feedback is not used to maintain a steady internal environment. **[1]**

**2.** (a) Define homeostasis. **[1]**
(b) Explain how exercise disturbs the internal environment and why this is described as a dynamic equilibrium. **[2]**
(c) State why a small rise in core temperature during exercise is acceptable but a large rise is not. **[1]**

**3.** A person walks from a warm room into a cold outdoor space. Describe how the hypothalamus and effectors bring about a response to keep core temperature near its set point. **[5]**

**4.** (a) State the region of the kidney that contains the Bowman's capsule and the region through which most loops of Henle and collecting ducts run. **[2]**
(b) Name the tube that carries urine from the kidney to the bladder. **[1]**
(c) Name the blood vessel that carries blood into the glomerulus. **[1]**

**5.** A micrograph shows a section through a kidney tubule. The image of one tubule is 52 mm wide and its real width is 130 µm. A second structure measures 18 mm on the image.
(a) Calculate the magnification. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the real width of the second structure in µm. **[1]**

**6.** (a) Describe how urea is produced in the liver from excess amino acids. **[3]**
(b) A person has plasma urea of 4.0 mmol dm⁻³ and urine urea of 220 mmol dm⁻³. Calculate the factor by which urine urea concentration exceeds plasma urea concentration. **[1]**

**7.** The values below are supplied as data. Hydrostatic pressure in a glomerular capillary is 9.0 kPa. Hydrostatic pressure in the Bowman's capsule is 2.5 kPa. The plasma proteins give an opposing pressure of 3.8 kPa.
(a) Calculate the net filtration pressure. **[2]**
(b) Explain how a high hydrostatic pressure is produced in the glomerulus. **[1]**
(c) Describe the three layers that separate blood from the space in the capsule. **[3]**

**8.** The glomerular filtration rate of a person is 110 cm³ min⁻¹ and plasma glucose is 6.0 mmol dm⁻³.
(a) Calculate the glucose filtered each minute. **[2]**
(b) The urine contains no glucose. Calculate the amount reabsorbed in one hour. **[1]**
(c) Explain how glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule. **[3]**

**9.** The table gives data supplied for three mammals.

| Mammal | Loop of Henle length (mm) | Maximum urine concentration (mOsm kg⁻¹) |
|---|---|---|
| P | 6 | 1200 |
| Q | 10 | 2100 |
| R | 16 | 3600 |

(a) Calculate the maximum urine concentration per millimetre of loop for mammal R. **[1]**
(b) Describe the relationship shown by the data. **[2]**
(c) Explain how the loop of Henle acts as a countercurrent multiplier. **[4]**

**10.** A volunteer drinks no water for 18 hours. Data for the volunteer are shown.

| Measurement | Normal hydration | After 18 hours |
|---|---|---|
| Urine flow (cm³ min⁻¹) | 1.5 | 0.5 |
| Urine concentration (mOsm kg⁻¹) | 400 | 1100 |
| Plasma ADH (pmol dm⁻³) | 2.0 | 6.5 |

(a) Calculate the percentage change in urine flow. **[2]**
(b) Explain how the change in ADH produces the change in urine flow. **[4]**
(c) Explain how this is an example of negative feedback. **[2]**

**11.** (a) Explain what is meant by a transcription factor. **[2]**
(b) Describe how a steroid hormone such as oestrogen switches on a gene. **[3]**
(c) Explain why a peptide hormone must act differently. **[2]**

**12.** A runner loses sweat at a mean rate of 0.8 dm³ per hour for 2.5 hours. The runner's total body water is 42 dm³.
(a) Calculate the total volume of sweat lost and express it as a percentage of body water, to one decimal place. **[3]**
(b) Explain how sweating lowers body temperature. **[2]**
(c) Explain how the runner's kidneys respond to this water loss. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) A change away from the set point is detected by receptors [1]. The response reverses the change and returns the variable to the set point [1]. **[2]**
(b) Positive feedback increases the change, so the variable would move further from the set point [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), say what the response does to the original change. Writing only "it keeps things the same" does not show that the change is reversed.

**2.** (a) Maintaining a stable internal environment within narrow limits [1]. **[1]**
(b) Respiring muscle cells release extra heat and carbon dioxide and use glucose and oxygen, which moves the values away from the set point [1]. Corrective responses are constantly switched on and off, so values fluctuate either side of the set point [1]. **[2]**
(c) A large rise would reduce enzyme activity as proteins denature, while a small rise is within the range that enzymes tolerate [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), link the temperature rise to enzymes instead of saying only that the body "overheats".

**3.** Central thermoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect lower blood temperature, and skin receptors send impulses to it [1]. The hypothalamus coordinates the response and sends impulses to effectors [1]. Skin arterioles vasoconstrict, so the skin receives less blood and loses less heat to the air [1]. Skeletal muscle shivers, releasing heat from respiration [1]. As temperature rises, the stimulus is reduced and the responses stop, which is negative feedback [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the hypothalamus as the coordinator and the muscles or arterioles as effectors. Answers that say the hypothalamus itself shivers or narrows vessels are describing the wrong part.

**4.** (a) Cortex [1]. Medulla [1]. **[2]**
(b) Ureter [1]. **[1]**
(c) Afferent arteriole [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the specific vessel name. "Renal artery" is the vessel into the kidney, whereas the afferent arteriole leads to a single glomerulus.

**5.** (a) Magnification = image size ÷ actual size = 52 mm ÷ 0.130 mm [1] = **×400** [1]. **[2]**
(b) 18 mm ÷ 400 = 0.045 mm = **45 µm** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversion, since dividing 52 by 130 directly mixes mm and µm.

**6.** (a) Excess amino acids are deaminated in the liver, removing the amino group [1]. The amino group becomes ammonia, which is combined with carbon dioxide to form urea [1]. The remaining carbon skeleton is respired or converted to carbohydrate or fat [1]. **[3]**
(b) 220 ÷ 4.0 = **55 times** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The ornithine cycle is not required. Cover deamination, ammonia to urea, and the fate of the carbon skeleton.

**7.** (a) 9.0 − 2.5 − 3.8 [1] = **2.7 kPa** [1]. **[2]**
(b) The afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent arteriole, so blood enters faster than it leaves [1]. **[1]**
(c) Capillary endothelium has gaps (fenestrations) [1]. A basement membrane of glycoproteins acts as a filter [1]. Podocytes with slits form the inner layer of the capsule [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b), compare the two arterioles. Saying only "the pressure is high" restates the question.

**8.** (a) Filtered = 6.0 × 0.110 [1] = **0.66 mmol min⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
(b) 0.66 × 60 = **39.6 mmol** [1]. **[1]**
(c) Sodium–potassium pumps in the basal membrane move sodium ions out of the cell, lowering sodium concentration inside it [1]. Sodium ions enter from the filtrate with glucose on a co-transporter, so glucose enters against its concentration gradient [1]. Glucose leaves to the blood by facilitated diffusion, and the cells have microvilli and many mitochondria [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), convert 110 cm³ to 0.110 dm³ before multiplying by a concentration in mmol per dm³.

**9.** (a) 3600 ÷ 16 = **225 mOsm kg⁻¹ per mm** [1]. **[1]**
(b) As the loop gets longer, maximum urine concentration increases [1]. R's loop is about 2.7 times that of P and its urine is 3.0 times as concentrated [1]. **[2]**
(c) Ions are transported out of the ascending limb, which is impermeable to water, so the medulla has a low water potential [1]. Water leaves the descending limb by osmosis, so the filtrate becomes more concentrated towards the bend [1]. The flow in the two limbs is in opposite directions, so a small difference at each level is multiplied into a steep gradient [1]. The collecting duct passes through this gradient, so water can leave by osmosis [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use figures from the table when describing data. A statement such as "R is longest" does not show a relationship, so quote a comparison.

**10.** (a) (0.5 − 1.5) ÷ 1.5 × 100 [1] = **−66.7%** [1]. **[2]**
(b) Osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect the low water potential of the plasma [1]. ADH made in hypothalamic cells passes to the posterior pituitary, which secretes it into the blood [1]. ADH binds to collecting duct cells, which insert aquaporins in the membrane [1]. More water leaves by osmosis into the concentrated medulla, so a small volume of concentrated urine forms [1]. **[4]**
(c) Reabsorbing more water makes plasma less concentrated and raises blood volume [1]. Osmoreceptors are less stimulated, ADH release falls, and the system returns towards its normal state [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the sign in (a). In (b), keep the sequence in order: detector, hormone release, target cells, effect.

**11.** (a) A protein that binds to a specific DNA sequence near a gene [1]. It affects whether RNA polymerase can begin transcription, switching the gene on or off [1]. **[2]**
(b) Oestrogen is lipid-soluble and diffuses through the cell surface membrane [1]. It binds a receptor inside the cell and the hormone–receptor complex enters the nucleus [1]. The complex binds DNA as a transcription factor, so transcription of the gene starts [1]. **[3]**
(c) A peptide hormone is water-soluble and cannot cross the phospholipid bilayer, so it binds a receptor on the cell surface [1]. This triggers a second messenger, such as cyclic AMP, that activates transcription factors inside the cell [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Contrast the two hormone types by where the receptor is. A peptide hormone does not enter the nucleus, so do not write that it does.

**12.** (a) 0.8 × 2.5 = **2.0 dm³** [1]. 2.0 ÷ 42 × 100 [1] = **4.8%** [1]. **[3]**
(b) Sweat evaporates from the skin [1] and takes latent heat from the body, lowering temperature [1]. **[2]**
(c) The plasma becomes more concentrated and osmoreceptors in the hypothalamus detect it [1]. More ADH is released from the posterior pituitary [1]. The collecting duct becomes more permeable because of aquaporins, so more water is reabsorbed [1]. A small volume of concentrated urine is produced, conserving water [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Make the link explicit: water loss raises plasma concentration, which is the stimulus.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not stating that negative feedback reverses the change.
- Naming the hypothalamus when the effector is asked for.
- Not converting units before calculating magnification or glucose.
- Writing that ultrafiltration is selective for useful substances.
- Mixing up which limb of the loop is permeable to water.
- Saying ADH is made in the pituitary gland.
- Omitting aquaporins when explaining the permeability change.
- Saying a peptide hormone enters the nucleus.
- Quoting no figures when describing a table.
