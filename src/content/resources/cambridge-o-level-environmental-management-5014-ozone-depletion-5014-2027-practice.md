## Section A

**1.** State the part of the atmosphere in which the ozone layer is found. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** appliances that used CFCs as refrigerants. **[2]**

**3.** Name the international agreement under which countries agreed to phase out CFCs. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why CFCs reach the stratosphere instead of breaking down near the ground. **[1]**

**5.** State what is meant by a *cataract*. **[1]**

**6.** Name the type of solar radiation that increases at the Earth's surface when the ozone layer thins. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the mean maximum spring ultraviolet (UV) index in a city in the far south of the Southern Hemisphere. A higher index means more ultraviolet radiation. The figures are invented for practice.

| Year | 1980 | 1990 | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean maximum UV index | 8 | 10 | 11 | 11 | 10 |

(a) Describe the trend in the UV index between 1980 and 2020. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the UV index rose between 1980 and 2000. **[2]**
(c) Suggest why the UV index had fallen only slightly by 2020. **[1]**

**8.** Explain how ozone depletion can lead to lower crop yields and a reduced food supply. **[3]**

**9.** Global production of CFCs was 1,100 thousand tonnes in 1986, 150 thousand tonnes in 1996 and 15 thousand tonnes in 2006. The figures are invented for practice.
(a) Calculate the percentage fall in production between 1986 and 2006, to one decimal place. **[2]**
(b) Explain how international agreement and policies led to this fall. **[2]**

**10.** In a city, old fridges are broken up in the open at an informal scrap yard.
(a) Explain why this practice contributes to ozone depletion. **[2]**
(b) Describe how safe disposal of CFCs would be carried out. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why the use of some alternatives to CFCs is described as a limitation of the CFC ban. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the causes and the main effects of ozone depletion and climate change. **[4]**

**13.** "Ozone depletion is an environmental problem that has been solved." Discuss the extent to which you agree with this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The lower stratosphere **[1]**.

**2.** Fridges or freezers **[1]**; air conditioners **[1]**.

**3.** The Montreal Protocol **[1]**.

**4.** CFCs are unreactive, so they are not broken down in the lower atmosphere and drift slowly upwards **[1]**.

**5.** Clouding of the lens of the eye, which reduces vision **[1]**.

**6.** Ultraviolet radiation **[1]**.

**7.** (a) The index rose from 8 in 1980 to a peak of 11 in 2000 and 2010 **[1]**, then fell slightly to 10 in 2020 **[1]**.
(b) CFCs from aerosols and refrigerants destroyed ozone in the lower stratosphere **[1]**, so the thinner ozone layer absorbed less ultraviolet radiation and more reached the surface **[1]**.
(c) CFCs released earlier remain in the atmosphere for decades, so the ozone layer is recovering only slowly even though production has stopped **[1]**.

**8.** A thinner ozone layer allows higher levels of ultraviolet radiation to reach the surface **[1]**. Ultraviolet radiation damages plant tissues, so crops grow less **[1]**. Harvests fall, reducing the food available to people and to livestock **[1]**.

**9.** (a) 1,100 − 15 = 1,085 thousand tonnes **[1]**; 1,085 ÷ 1,100 × 100 = 98.6% **[1]**.
(b) Under the Montreal Protocol, countries committed to phase out the production and use of CFCs on agreed timetables **[1]**; each country then passed laws banning CFCs and requiring alternatives, so manufacturers stopped making them **[1]**.

**10.** (a) Old fridges still contain CFCs in their cooling systems and insulation **[1]**; breaking them up releases the CFCs, which rise to the stratosphere and destroy ozone **[1]**.
(b) Fridges are collected and taken to specialist facilities **[1]**, where trained workers remove the CFCs from the cooling system and foam and store them so they cannot escape **[1]**.

**11.** Some alternatives still damaged ozone a little, or are powerful greenhouse gases **[1]**, so replacing CFCs with them has added to another problem, climate change **[1]**.

**12.** Ozone depletion is caused by CFCs, whereas climate change is caused by greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour **[1]**. CFCs come mainly from aerosols and refrigerants, whereas greenhouse gases come from many activities, including burning fossil fuels and agriculture **[1]**. The main effect of ozone depletion is more ultraviolet radiation reaching the surface **[1]**, whereas the main effect of climate change is global warming and long-term changes in weather patterns **[1]**.

**13.** One mark per point; points for and against and a judgement for full marks.
- Agree: the Montreal Protocol was joined by almost every country, so action was global **[1]**.
- Agree: CFC production has almost stopped, because bans were supported by alternatives for aerosols and refrigerants **[1]**.
- Disagree: CFCs already released last for decades, so the ozone layer is still thinner than before and will take many years to recover **[1]**.
- Disagree: old appliances still contain CFCs, and unsafe disposal or illegal production can still release them **[1]**.
- Disagree: some alternatives are greenhouse gases, so the solution has added to climate change **[1]**.
- Judgement: for example, the cause has largely been dealt with, but the problem is not yet solved because recovery is incomplete and depends on continued compliance **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4**: saying CFCs are "light" rather than explaining that they are unreactive.
- **Question 7(c)**: suggesting countries are still using CFCs, instead of their long lifetime.
- **Question 9(a)**: calculating the fall from 1996 instead of 1986.
- **Question 12**: describing each problem separately with no comparative words.
- **Question 13**: agreeing completely without considering slow recovery.
