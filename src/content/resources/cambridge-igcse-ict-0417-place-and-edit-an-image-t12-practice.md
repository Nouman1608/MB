## Section A: short answers

**1.** State what is meant by *contrast* in an image. **[1]**

**2.** Which **one** of these edits swaps the left and right sides of an image?

A: rotate 90° clockwise

B: flip vertically

C: flip horizontally

D: send to back **[1]**

**3.** Name the setting that must be switched on so that an image keeps its proportions when one dimension is changed. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** commands used to change the front-to-back order of overlapping objects. **[2]**

**5.** An image is 7 cm wide and 3 cm high. It is rotated through 90°. State its new width and height. **[1]**

**6.** State the difference between *grouping* and *ungrouping* objects. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A photograph is 16 cm wide and 12 cm high. It must be placed in a report.

**(a)** The photograph must be resized to 10 cm wide, maintaining its aspect ratio. Calculate the new height. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how to resize the photograph to exactly 10 cm wide while maintaining the aspect ratio. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why dragging a side handle would not be suitable. **[1]**

**8.** A picture is 14 cm wide and 9 cm high. The instructions are: "Crop 3 cm from the top and 1 cm from the bottom, then resize the picture to 7 cm wide, maintaining the aspect ratio."

**(a)** State the size of the picture after cropping. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the size after resizing. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the crop is done before the resize. **[1]**

**9.** A website needs a square profile image, 5 cm by 5 cm, made from a photograph that is 12 cm wide and 9 cm high.

**(a)** Explain what would happen to the photograph if both dimensions were simply set to 5 cm. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the photograph could be made into a 5 cm square without being distorted. **[2]**

**10.** A designer is making a poster. A photograph of a runner faces off the left edge of the page, away from the poster's text on the right.

**(a)** Name the edit that would make the runner face the text, and explain why it is better than rotating the photograph. **[2]**

**(b)** The photograph is too dark. Describe the adjustment that should be made. **[1]**

**(c)** After brightening, the photograph looks washed out and grey. Name the further adjustment that would make it look more vivid. **[1]**

**11.** A logo is made from a circle, a star image and a text box. When the designer moves the circle, the star and text stay behind. The text box is also hidden behind the circle.

**(a)** Describe how to make the text box visible. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how to make the three objects move together. **[2]**

**(c)** The colour of the text must later be changed. Explain what the designer may need to do first. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how to place an image precisely so that it is centred horizontally between the margins, directly below a heading, with text above and below but not beside it. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The difference between the light and dark areas of the image (1). **[1]**

**2.** C: flip horizontally. **[1]**

**3.** Lock aspect ratio (1). **[1]**

**4.** Any two: bring to front (1); send to back (1); bring forward (1); send backward (1). **[2]**

**5.** 3 cm wide and 7 cm high (1). **[1]**

**6.** Grouping joins several objects so they behave as one object (1); ungrouping separates them so each can be edited or moved on its own (1). **[2]**

**7. (a)** Scale factor = 10 / 16 = 0.625 (1); new height = 12 x 0.625 = 7.5 cm (1).

**(b)** Select the image and open its size settings (1); with lock aspect ratio switched on, enter a width of 10 cm so the height is set automatically (1).

**(c)** A side handle changes only the width, so the image would be squashed (distorted) (1). **[5]**

**8. (a)** 14 cm wide by 5 cm high (9 - 3 - 1 = 5) (1).

**(b)** Scale factor = 7 / 14 = 0.5 (1); new size 7 cm wide by 2.5 cm high (1).

**(c)** If the picture were resized first, the crop would then reduce it further, so the final size would not be as specified (1). **[4]**

**9. (a)** The width would be scaled by 5 / 12 (about 0.42) but the height by 5 / 9 (about 0.56) (1), so the image would be distorted, appearing stretched vertically or squashed horizontally (1).

**(b)** Crop the photograph to a square first, for example 9 cm by 9 cm by removing 1.5 cm from each side (1); then resize to 5 cm by 5 cm with the aspect ratio locked (1). **[4]**

**10. (a)** Flip (reflect) horizontally (1). Rotating would turn the runner upside down or on their side, whereas a flip mirrors them so they face the other way and stay upright (1).

**(b)** Increase the brightness (1).

**(c)** Increase the contrast (1). **[4]**

**11. (a)** Select the text box and bring it to the front (or send the circle to the back) (1).

**(b)** Select all three objects (1) and use the group command, so they move as one object (1).

**(c)** Ungroup the objects, or select the text box within the group, before changing the colour, then regroup (1). **[4]**

**12.** Any three: insert the image and set text wrapping to top and bottom (1); place it in the paragraph directly after the heading (or anchor it there) (1); use the alignment or position settings to centre it horizontally relative to the margins (1); check in print preview that it is within the margins and that no text appears beside it (1). Maximum 3. **[3]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Is it ever right to change the aspect ratio?**
Yes, when a task gives both a width and a height that do not match the original proportions. The syllabus says "resize an image as specified to maintain or adjust the aspect ratio".

**What is the quickest way to make an upside-down copy of an image?**
Rotate it by 180°. A vertical flip also turns it upside down, but as a mirror image.
