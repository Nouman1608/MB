## Questions

### Section A: knowledge of the route

**1.** State the number of essays in Unit 4b, the word limits for each and the total number of marks. **[3]**

**2.** Name the four areas from which each text must be linked. **[2]**

**3.** State two rules in the specification about the poetry text. **[2]**

### Section B: text choices

**4.** Four students propose these choices for their coursework. Say whether each choice meets the specification's rules as described, and give the reason.

- (i) A poetry text made up of selected poems by three different poets, linked to the area of war.
- (ii) A prose text that is a novel on the Unit 2 prose list, linked to childhood.
- (iii) A novel in translation, linked to race, class and culture, and a collection of poems by a single poet, linked to childhood.
- (iv) A collection of poems by a single poet and a novel, both linked to the area of women and men.

**[8]**

**5.** A student chose a prose text and a poetry text that meet all the rules. Give three questions the student should ask to test whether a text will support an argued essay. **[3]**

### Section C: task titles and planning

**6.** Which of these is the better coursework title? Explain your choice.

- (i) Childhood in the poetry of the collection.
- (ii) 'The collection presents childhood as a time of loss rather than growth.' Exploring the poet's representation of childhood, say how far you agree with this view.

**[3]**

**7.** Write a task title of your own, in the same form as the specification's example, for a prose text linked to the area of war. Use a placeholder for the writer. **[2]**

**8.** A student drafts two essays of 1,380 words and 1,520 words.

**(a)** Calculate the total number of words. **[1]**

**(b)** Say whether each essay is within the permitted range and state by how much any essay is outside it. **[2]**

**9.** Essay 1 was marked 19 out of 25 and essay 2 was marked 22 out of 25.

**(a)** Calculate the total mark and express it as a percentage of 50. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the band for each essay. **[2]**

### Section D: judging writing

**10.** Three students each wrote a paragraph about the poem "Night Shift" (an original poem in the [Unit 4a practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4a-examination-practice/)), as part of an essay on the representation of men and class.

> **Student X:** The poet shows the father is tired. He has a lunchbox and boots. This shows he works hard. The poem is about a working family. The poet uses a simile which makes it better.
>
> **Student Y:** The poem presents the father as a worker whose life is organised by his shift. The simile "his lunchbox rattling like a small, tame engine" compares him to a machine, which shows how his job controls his routine. The family is quiet to let him sleep, which shows they respect him.
>
> **Student Z:** The father is represented through the objects and routines he leaves behind: "the folded sleeves, the boots set heel to heel". The precise, tidy detail suggests both his discipline and the household's careful management of his absence, so masculinity is defined by labour. Yet the plural "we" means that the children's perception frames him, and the final line makes the silence a shared act of love: "so he can pretend he does not hear us pretend". A reader might see this as tender; another might see in it the cost of a class position that leaves emotional life unspoken.

**(a)** For each paragraph, suggest the band that best describes it and give two reasons based on the specification's descriptors. **[6]**

**(b)** Give two improvements that would move student Y's paragraph up one band. **[2]**

## Answers

Marking guidance is Marlbridge's own. Award marks for the points listed or for equally valid alternatives.

**1.** Two essays [1]; 1,250 to 1,500 words each [1]; 50 marks in total (25 for each essay) [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Childhood; war; women and men; race, class and culture [2 for all four; 1 for two or three]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two from: it must be by a single author [1]; the number of poems must be equivalent to those seen in Unit 2 and Unit 3 [1]; it must not be on any AS or A-level set text list [1]; it must allow access to a range of critical views and interpretations [1]. **[2]**

**4.** (i) Does not meet the rules, because the poetry text must be by a single author [2]. (ii) Does not meet the rules, because students cannot choose texts from any set text list, and Unit 2 has a prose list [2]. (iii) Meets the rules: translation is allowed, the poetry text is by a single author and the two texts are linked to different areas [2]. (iv) Does not meet the rules, because each text must be linked to a different area [2]. **[8]**

**5.** Any three from: Is it by a single author and of a comparable length to the set-text units? [1] Can I find more than one way of reading it? [1] Does it fit one of the four areas clearly, and not the area of my other text? [1] Is it absent from every set text list? [1] Does it have enough detail of voice, form, structure and imagery for close analysis? [1] **[3]**

**6.** Title (ii) [1]. It states a view that can be argued for or against [1], and it names the aspect and ends with an invitation to judge, so it leads to a sustained argument, whereas title (i) is only a topic [1]. **[3]**

**7.** A title of the form: "'[Writer] presents war as [a claim].' Exploring [Writer]'s representation of [aspect], say how far you agree with this view." [1] The claim should be arguable, for example "an experience that only the survivors can describe" [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 1,380 + 1,520 = 2,900 words [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Essay 1 is within the range [1]. Essay 2 is over the upper limit of 1,500 words by 20 words [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 19 + 22 = 41 [1]; 41 / 50 x 100 = 82 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Essay 1: Band 4, as 19 is in the range 16 to 20 [1]. Essay 2: Band 5, as 22 is in the range 21 to 25 [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Student X: Band 1 or low Band 2 [1], because the points are simple and generalised, with no quotation or particular detail, and the method is named without an effect [1]. Student Y: Band 3 [1], because the ideas are straightforward and relevant, with a quotation and a sensible explanation, but there is no alternative reading or context [1]. Student Z: Band 4 or 5 [1], because the argument is coherent and precise, with quotations woven in, and it offers two interpretations that connect methods and the representation [1]. **[6]**

**(b)** Any two from: offer an alternative reading of the machine simile [1]; explore why "small" and "tame" matter to the representation of masculinity [1]; link the family's quiet to class and the structure of work [1]; use more than one quotation per point [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Breaking a text rule, such as using a set text or a collection by several authors.
- Two texts linked to the same area.
- A title that does not state a view to argue.
- Essays outside the word limits.
- General comments with no quotation.
- Only one reading offered.
