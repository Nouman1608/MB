**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level** subtopic 2.4: reacting masses with percentage yield, gas volumes, solution volumes and concentrations, limiting and excess reagent, and deducing stoichiometric relationships.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mole ratio | Ratio of amounts of substances, from the coefficients in a balanced equation |
| Theoretical yield | Maximum mass of product calculated from the equation |
| Percentage yield | Actual yield ÷ theoretical yield × 100 |
| Molar gas volume | 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ for any gas at room conditions |
| Concentration | Amount of solute per dm³ of solution (mol dm⁻³) |
| Mass concentration | Mass of solute per dm³ of solution (g dm⁻³) |
| Titre | Volume of solution added from the burette to reach the end-point |
| Limiting reagent | Reactant completely used up; it fixes the amount of product |
| Excess reagent | Reactant left over when the reaction stops |

## Conversions

    n = m ÷ M                 (g, g mol⁻¹)
    n = V ÷ 24.0              (gas, dm³, room conditions)
    n = c × V                 (solution, mol dm⁻³ × dm³)
    cm³ ÷ 1000 = dm³
    g dm⁻³ = mol dm⁻³ × M

**Method: any reacting quantity**
1. Balanced equation.
2. Known quantity to moles.
3. Mole ratio.
4. Moles to the quantity asked.
5. Round at the end, with units.

## (a) Reacting masses and yield

- Example: 25.0 g CaCO₃ → 14.01 g CaO theoretical; 12.6 g obtained; yield 89.9 %.
- Yield below 100 %: incomplete reaction, side reactions, losses in separation or purification.

## (b) Gas volumes

- Same temperature and pressure: volume ratio = mole ratio.
- Water from combustion is liquid at room temperature: its volume is ignored.

| Hydrocarbon combustion | Volume ratio (gas : O₂ : CO₂) |
|---|---|
| CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O | 1 : 2 : 1 |
| C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O | 1 : 5 : 3 |
| C₄H₁₀ + 6.5O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O | 1 : 6.5 : 4 |

**Method: formula from combustion volumes**
1. CO₂ volume = contraction on adding NaOH(aq) (it absorbs CO₂).
2. x = CO₂ volume ÷ hydrocarbon volume.
3. O₂ used = O₂ added − O₂ left.
4. x + y/4 = O₂ used ÷ hydrocarbon volume; solve for y.

## (c) Solutions and titrations

- n = c × V(dm³).
- In a titration, use the known solution to find moles, apply the ratio, then divide by the other volume.
- Example: 21.40 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄ neutralises 25.0 cm³ NaOH; ratio 2 : 1; c(NaOH) = 0.171 mol dm⁻³ (6.85 g dm⁻³).

| Acid + base | Ratio acid : base |
|---|---|
| HCl + NaOH | 1 : 1 |
| H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH | 1 : 2 |
| 2HCl + Na₂CO₃ | 2 : 1 |

## (d) Limiting and excess reagent

**Method**
1. Moles of each reactant.
2. Divide each by its coefficient; the smaller result is limiting.
3. Product from the limiting reagent only.
4. Excess left = moles present − moles used.

Example: 3.00 g Al + 10.0 g Cl₂ (2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃): Cl₂ limiting; 12.5 g AlCl₃; 0.465 g Al left.

## (e) Deducing stoichiometry

1. Measured quantities to moles.
2. Divide by the smallest; find whole-number ratio.
3. Write the equation with that ratio and balance the rest.

Example: 0.0500 mol metal X → 0.0750 mol H₂: ratio 2 : 3, so X forms X³⁺; 2X + 6HCl → 2XCl₃ + 3H₂.

## Significant figures

- Same as the least precise data, or one more.
- Keep unrounded values between steps.
- Titre to 2 d.p. (e.g. 21.40 cm³) often allows four figures; data to three figures limits the answer to three or four.

## Exam traps

- cm³ used without dividing by 1000.
- Mole ratio forgotten (2 : 1 with H₂SO₄ or Na₂CO₃).
- Limiting reagent chosen by mass.
- Water counted as a gas at room temperature.
- Yield inverted.
- Too many or too few significant figures.

## Self-test

**1.** State the volume of 0.250 mol of any gas at room conditions.

**2.** How many moles are in 35.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ HCl?

**3.** Calculate the concentration of a solution of 2.00 g of NaOH (M 40.0) in 250 cm³.

**4.** What volume of oxygen is needed to burn 10.0 cm³ of ethane, C₂H₆, completely?

**5.** 80.0 g Fe₂O₃ (M 159.6) is reduced by CO: Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Calculate the theoretical mass of iron (Fe 55.8).

**6.** 50.2 g of iron was obtained in Question 5. Calculate the percentage yield.

**7.** 23.65 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ HCl reacts with 25.0 cm³ of Na₂CO₃ solution. Find c(Na₂CO₃).

**8.** 5.00 g of zinc (Ar 65.4) is added to 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Which is limiting?

**9.** What volume of hydrogen forms in Question 8?

**10.** 20 cm³ of a hydrocarbon burned in 150 cm³ O₂ leaves 110 cm³ of gas after cooling, and 50 cm³ after NaOH. Find the formula.

**11.** 0.540 g of a metal (Ar 27.0) gives 0.720 dm³ of H₂ with excess acid. Find the mole ratio of metal to hydrogen.

**12.** Why is the yield of a reaction usually less than 100 %?

## Self-test answers

**1.** 0.250 × 24.0 = 6.00 dm³.

**2.** 0.200 × 0.0350 = 7.00 × 10⁻³ mol.

**3.** 0.0500 mol ÷ 0.250 dm³ = 0.200 mol dm⁻³.

**4.** 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O; 3.5 × 10.0 = 35.0 cm³.

**5.** 0.501 mol Fe₂O₃ → 1.00 mol Fe; 55.9 g.

**6.** 50.2 ÷ 55.9 × 100 = 89.7 %.

**7.** n(HCl) = 3.548 × 10⁻³ mol; n(Na₂CO₃) = 1.774 × 10⁻³ mol; c = 0.0710 mol dm⁻³.

**8.** n(Zn) = 0.0765 mol needs 0.153 mol HCl; only 0.0500 mol HCl, so HCl is limiting.

**9.** n(H₂) = 0.0500 ÷ 2 = 0.0250 mol; 0.600 dm³.

**10.** CO₂ = 60 cm³, so x = 3; O₂ used = 100 cm³, 5 per volume; 3 + y/4 = 5, y = 8; C₃H₈.

**11.** Metal 0.0200 mol; H₂ 0.0300 mol; ratio 2 : 3.

**12.** Incomplete reaction, side reactions, or product lost during separation and purification.
