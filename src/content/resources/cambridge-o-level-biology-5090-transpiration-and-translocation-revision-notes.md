## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Transpiration | The loss of water vapour from the leaves of a plant |
| Transpiration pull | Pulling of the water column up the xylem as water evaporates from the leaves |
| Translocation | Movement of sucrose and amino acids in the phloem from sources to sinks |
| Source | Part of a plant that produces or releases sucrose or amino acids, for example a leaf |
| Sink | Part that uses or stores them, for example a root, fruit or growing shoot |
| Wilting | Drooping of a plant as its cells lose turgor when water loss exceeds uptake |

## The route of water loss

Xylem → mesophyll cells → **evaporates** from the cell surfaces into air spaces → **diffuses** out through the stomata as water vapour.

## Factors that change the rate

| Factor | Effect on rate | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Higher wind speed | Increases | Water vapour is carried away, keeping the gradient steep |
| Higher temperature | Increases | Faster evaporation and diffusion |
| Higher humidity | Decreases | Smaller concentration gradient |
| Higher light intensity | Increases | Stomata open wider; leaf warmer |

## Wilting

1. Water loss is faster than water uptake.
2. Cells lose water; turgor pressure falls.
3. Cells become flaccid; stems and leaves droop.
4. Closing the stomata and watering the plant lets it recover.

## Potometer

- Measures the rate of water uptake as an estimate of transpiration.
- Cut and assemble under water; seal all joints; dry the leaves.
- Measure the distance moved by the bubble in a set time.
- Rate = distance divided by time. Example: 36 mm in 6 min = 6 mm per min.
- Change **one** factor at a time; repeat and take a mean.
- Volume per minute = pi x radius squared x distance per minute. For radius 0.5 mm: 0.785 x 6 = 4.7 mm cubed per minute.

## Transpiration pull

1. Water evaporates from mesophyll cells and leaves through the stomata.
2. This pulls more water up the xylem.
3. The water molecules are held together by forces of attraction, so the water moves as a continuous column.
4. No energy is needed from the plant; the Sun's energy drives evaporation.

## Xylem

| Function | Structural feature | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Transports water and mineral ions | No cell contents | Water flows freely |
| Transport | Cells joined end to end, no cross walls | Long continuous tube |
| Support (and withstanding the pull) | Thick walls with lignin | Strong; does not collapse |

## Translocation

- Substances: **sucrose and amino acids**.
- Tissue: **phloem**.
- Direction: from **sources** to **sinks**, in either direction.
- Uses living cells and energy.
- Sources: leaves. Sinks: growing shoot tips, roots, fruits, seeds and storage organs.
- A storage organ may be a sink (when storing) and then a source (when releasing).

## Positions in transverse sections

| Organ | Xylem | Phloem | Cortex |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root | Centre of the root | Between the xylem arms | Wide band outside the vascular tissue |
| Stem | Inner part of each bundle in a ring | Outer part of each bundle | Outside the ring of bundles |

## Short model answers

**Describe transpiration.** The loss of water vapour from the leaves. Water evaporates from the mesophyll cell surfaces into the air spaces, then diffuses out through the stomata.

**Explain why transpiration is faster on a windy day.** Wind blows away the water vapour near the stomata, so the concentration gradient between the air spaces and the air stays steep, and diffusion is faster.

**Explain how water moves up the xylem.** Water evaporates from the leaves, which pulls water up the xylem. The water molecules are attracted to one another, so the whole column of water is pulled up.

**Explain why xylem vessels have no cross walls.** So that the vessels form a continuous tube and water moves without obstruction.

## Exam traps

- Transpiration is loss of water **vapour**, from the **leaves**, mainly via the **stomata**.
- Higher humidity **reduces** transpiration.
- Give an explanation, not only an effect.
- Phloem carries sucrose and amino acids, not water.
- Translocation goes from source to sink, which may be upward or downward.
- Transpiration pull uses the Sun's energy, not the plant's.
- Xylem is **inside** phloem in stem bundles.

## Self-test

2. Where does the water evaporate from inside the leaf?

4. State the effect of increasing humidity on transpiration.
5. Explain why a higher temperature increases transpiration.
6. Explain why plants wilt.
7. What does a potometer measure?
8. A bubble moves 42 mm in 7 minutes. Calculate the rate in mm per minute.
9. What holds water molecules together in the xylem?
10. Name the substances translocated in the phloem.
11. Define a sink and give an example.
12. Name two structural features of xylem and say how each helps.

### Answers

1. The loss of water vapour from the leaves of a plant.
2. From the surfaces of the mesophyll cells.
3. By diffusion through the stomata.
4. It decreases it, because the concentration gradient is less steep.
5. Evaporation and diffusion are faster at higher temperatures.
6. Water loss exceeds uptake; the cells lose turgor and become flaccid.
7. The rate of water uptake by a cut shoot, used to estimate transpiration.
8. 42 divided by 7 = 6 mm per minute.
9. Forces of attraction between the water molecules.
10. Sucrose and amino acids.
11. A part of the plant that uses or stores sucrose or amino acids, for example a root or a fruit.
12. Any two: thick lignin walls (strong, support); no cell contents (free flow); no cross walls (continuous tube).
