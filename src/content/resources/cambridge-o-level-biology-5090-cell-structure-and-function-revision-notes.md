## Structures the syllabus names

- **Animal cell (identify):** ribosomes, mitochondria, nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane.
- **Plant cell (identify):** all of the above plus chloroplasts, sap vacuole and cellulose cell wall.
- **Bacterial cell (describe):** ribosomes, circular DNA and plasmids, cytoplasm, cell membrane, cell wall.

## Key terms and functions

| Structure | Function, in exam wording |
|---|---|
| Cell membrane | Controls which substances enter and leave; partially permeable |
| Cytoplasm | Where many chemical reactions of the cell take place |
| Nucleus | Contains DNA, which controls the cell's activities |
| Ribosome | Site of protein synthesis |
| Mitochondrion | Site of aerobic respiration, which releases energy |
| Chloroplast | Contains chlorophyll; absorbs light for photosynthesis |
| Sap vacuole | Holds cell sap (water, sugars, salts); keeps the cell firm |
| Cellulose cell wall | Supports the cell, stops it bursting; fully permeable |
| Circular DNA (bacteria) | Single loop of DNA free in the cytoplasm; carries the cell's genes |
| Plasmid (bacteria) | Small extra ring of DNA with a few genes |

## Which image shows what?

| Image | Visible | Not visible |
|---|---|---|
| Diagram | Everything drawn and labelled | — |
| Photomicrograph (light microscope) | Wall, membrane edge, nucleus, cytoplasm, chloroplasts, vacuole | Ribosomes; little or no detail inside mitochondria |
| Electron micrograph | Ribosomes, mitochondria with folded inner membrane, chloroplast membranes | — |

**Recognition clues:** mitochondria are small and sausage-shaped with internal folds; chloroplasts are larger and lens-shaped with stacked membranes; ribosomes are tiny dots; the sap vacuole is a large pale space; the nucleus is the largest, most darkly stained body.

## Identifying a cell type

| Feature | Animal | Plant | Bacterial |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Yes | Yes | No |
| Cell wall | No | Yes (cellulose) | Yes (not cellulose) |
| Plasmids | No | No | Often |
| Chloroplasts | No | Some cells | No |
| Large sap vacuole | No | Yes | No |
| Mitochondria | Yes | Yes | No |

To identify a cell, pick features that rule out the other two types. A wall alone is not enough: plants and bacteria both have one. No nucleus means bacterial.

## Staining practical in five steps

1. Thin, flat specimen on a clean slide (onion epidermis, pondweed leaf, or cheek cells where allowed).
2. Add iodine solution (plant) or methylene blue (animal) for contrast.
3. Lower the cover slip at an angle with a mounted needle to avoid air bubbles.
4. Draw off excess liquid with paper towel.
5. Focus on low power first, then switch to high power and use the fine focus.

**Faults:** air bubbles (cover slip dropped flat); overlapping cells (specimen too thick); everything dark (too much stain).

**Drawing:** sharp pencil, single clear lines, no shading, label lines ruled and touching the structure, a title.

## Short model answers

**Describe two differences between a bacterial cell and a plant cell.** The bacterial cell has no nucleus; its DNA is a circular loop free in the cytoplasm. The bacterial cell wall is not made of cellulose. (Also: bacteria have plasmids; they have no chloroplasts, mitochondria or sap vacuole.)

**Explain why a plant cell does not burst when placed in water.** Water enters, and the vacuole swells and presses the cytoplasm and membrane against the cellulose cell wall. The strong wall resists further expansion, so the cell becomes firm instead of bursting.

**State where in a leaf cell light is absorbed, and name the pigment.** In the chloroplasts, by chlorophyll.

**Suggest why a muscle cell has many mitochondria.** It needs a lot of energy for contraction, and mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration, which releases that energy.

## Exam traps

- Ribosomes cannot be seen on a photomicrograph.
- Energy is released by respiration, never "made".
- Membrane: partially permeable. Wall: fully permeable.
- Bacteria have DNA but no nucleus.
- A cell wall does not prove a cell is a plant cell.
- Root and onion epidermis cells have no chloroplasts.
- Do not draw a nucleus, mitochondria or chloroplasts in a bacterial cell.
- Stains give contrast; they do not magnify.

## Self-test

1. Name the five structures you must identify in an animal cell.
2. Which three extra structures must you identify in a plant cell?
3. State the function of ribosomes.
4. Why is "mitochondria make energy" marked wrong?
5. Which structure is partially permeable, and which is fully permeable?
6. Where is the DNA in a bacterial cell?
7. What is a plasmid?
8. Why can ribosomes not be labelled on a photomicrograph?
9. A cell has a wall and plasmids but no nucleus. What type of cell is it?
10. Why is iodine solution added to onion epidermis on a slide?
11. Give one reason for lowering the cover slip at an angle.
12. Why do onion epidermis cells have no chloroplasts?

### Answers

1. Ribosomes, mitochondria, nucleus, cytoplasm, cell membrane.
2. Chloroplasts, sap vacuole, cellulose cell wall.
3. Site of protein synthesis.
4. Energy is released by aerobic respiration in mitochondria; it is not made.
5. The cell membrane is partially permeable; the cellulose cell wall is fully permeable.
6. Free in the cytoplasm, as a circular loop, with plasmids.
7. A small, separate ring of DNA in a bacterial cell, carrying a few genes.
8. They are too small to be seen with a light microscope; they appear only on electron micrographs.
9. A bacterial cell.
10. Most structures are colourless; iodine stains the nucleus (and starch) so it can be seen.
11. To avoid trapping air bubbles that hide the cells.
12. They grow underground in the onion bulb and receive no light, so they do not photosynthesise.
