## Purpose

The cash book (bank columns) and the bank statement record the same bank account from two sides. At any date their balances usually differ. A bank reconciliation statement explains the difference. Its purposes are:

- **To find items the business has not recorded**, such as bank charges, direct debits and credit transfers, so the cash book can be brought up to date.
- **To detect errors**, whether made by the business in its cash book or by the bank.
- **To detect fraud**, such as unauthorised payments or cheques altered after they were written.
- **To confirm the correct bank balance** for the statement of financial position, which is the updated cash book figure.

## Why the two records differ

| Cause | Example | Who has not recorded it yet |
|---|---|---|
| Timing: payments | Cheque sent to a supplier but not yet presented | The bank |
| Timing: receipts | Money paid in on the last day, not yet on the statement | The bank |
| Bank-initiated items | Bank charges, interest, direct debits, standing orders | The business |
| Items received direct | Credit transfers from customers | The business |
| Dishonoured cheques | A customer's cheque returned unpaid | The business |
| Errors | Wrong amount in the cash book; bank charges another customer's cheque to this account | Either |

A key fact: the bank statement shows the account from the bank's point of view. Money in the account is a **credit** on the statement (the bank owes it to the business) and a **debit** in the cash book. An overdraft is the other way round.

## The process

1. **Tick matching items.** Compare the receipts side of the cash book with the credits on the statement, and the payments side with the debits.
2. **Unticked items on the statement** (charges, direct debits, standing orders, credit transfers, interest, dishonoured cheques): enter them in the cash book.
3. **Errors in the cash book**: correct them in the cash book.
4. **Balance the updated cash book.** This is the figure for the statement of financial position.
5. **Unticked items in the cash book** (unpresented cheques, uncredited lodgements) and **bank errors**: these go on the reconciliation statement.
6. **Prepare the statement** from the updated cash book balance to the bank statement balance.

## Worked example 2: an overdraft and a bank error

At 30 September 2027 the cash book of Stanmore Removals shows an **overdraft** of 1,860 (a credit balance). The bank statement shows an overdraft of 2,395 (a debit balance on the statement). The differences are:

- Bank charges 64 and a standing order for loan interest 150, not in the cash book.
- A customer's cheque for 290 was dishonoured.
- A credit transfer of 480 from a customer, not in the cash book.
- Cheques of 725 not yet presented.
- A lodgement of 1,050 not yet credited.
- The bank charged a cheque of 186 written by a different business to Stanmore's account.

**Updated cash book.** Payments the business has not recorded increase the overdraft; receipts reduce it.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Overdraft per cash book | (1,860) |
| Less bank charges | (64) |
| Less standing order | (150) |
| Less dishonoured cheque | (290) |
| Add credit transfer | 480 |
| **Updated overdraft** | **(1,884)** |

Brackets show an overdraft. The bank error is not entered in the cash book: the business did not make it, and it tells the bank.

**Bank reconciliation statement at 30 September 2027**

| | |
|---|---:|
| Overdraft per updated cash book | (1,884) |
| Add unpresented cheques | 725 |
| | (1,159) |
| Less uncredited lodgement | (1,050) |
| | (2,209) |
| Less cheque charged in error by the bank | (186) |
| **Overdraft per bank statement** | **(2,395)** |

Think about direction with an overdraft. The unpresented cheques have not yet been taken by the bank, so the bank's overdraft is smaller than the cash book's by 725: adding 725 to a negative figure brings it closer to zero. The uncredited lodgement has not yet reduced the bank's overdraft, so it is larger. The bank's error took 186 more from the account.

## Working in the other direction

Some questions start from the bank statement balance. Reverse every adjustment: deduct unpresented cheques, add uncredited lodgements, and reverse any bank error. Linwood Gallery: 2,797 − 675 + 375 = 2,497, the updated cash book balance.

## Exam technique

- **Explain the purpose**: give a reason and a consequence: "to find bank charges not yet recorded, so the cash book balance is not overstated".
- **Process**: describe the steps in order; the key distinction is which items update the cash book and which appear only on the statement.
- **Prepare**: give a title with the date, start with a labelled balance, label every adjustment, show subtotals, and finish with a labelled final balance. Show overdrafts in brackets or with "overdrawn".

## Common errors

- Putting unpresented cheques or uncredited lodgements in the cash book.
- Starting the statement from the cash book balance before updating it.
- Adding a dishonoured cheque to the cash book. It reduces the balance.
- Entering a bank error in the cash book.
- Treating an overdraft as a positive balance and getting every sign wrong.
- Leaving out the title, date or the labels on the first and last lines.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which balance goes in the statement of financial position, the cash book or the bank statement?**
The updated cash book balance. It includes everything the business has paid or received, including items the bank has not yet processed.

**What is an unpresented cheque?**
A cheque the business has written and recorded in its cash book, but that the payee has not yet paid into the bank, so it is not on the bank statement.

**How do I handle a bank error?**
Do not enter it in the cash book. Show it as an adjustment on the reconciliation statement and tell the bank so it can correct the account.
