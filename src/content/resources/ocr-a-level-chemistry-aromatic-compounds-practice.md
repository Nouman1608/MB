## Questions

**1.** Describe the bonding in benzene according to the delocalised model, and state one way it differs from the Kekulé model. **[3]**

**2.** Hydrogenating one mole of cyclohexene releases 119 kJ. Fully hydrogenating 0.0500 mol of benzene releases 10.4 kJ.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change of hydrogenation of benzene in kJ mol⁻¹. **[1]**
**(b)** Use both values to show that benzene is more stable than the Kekulé structure predicts. Give the size of the difference. **[2]**
**(c)** State one other piece of experimental evidence for delocalisation and explain how it rules out the Kekulé model. **[2]**

**3.** Name each compound.

**(a)** Phenol with an NO₂ group on both carbons next to the C–OH and on the carbon opposite it. **[1]**
**(b)** Phenylamine with a Cl atom on the carbon opposite the C–NH₂. **[1]**
**(c)** Benzene with Cl on one carbon and NO₂ on a carbon two positions away. **[1]**

**4.** Benzene is converted into nitrobenzene.

**(a)** State the reagents and conditions. **[2]**
**(b)** Give an equation showing how the nitronium ion forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the temperature is controlled. **[1]**

**5.** Benzene reacts with bromine in the presence of aluminium bromide, AlBr₃.

**(a)** Show, with an equation, how AlBr₃ produces the electrophile. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the mechanism. Show every curly arrow and the intermediate. **[3]**
**(c)** Show how the AlBr₃ is regenerated. **[1]**

**6.** Cyclohexene decolourises bromine at room temperature. Benzene does not react unless a halogen carrier is present. Explain this difference. **[3]**

**7.** Benzene reacts with 2,2-dimethylpropanoyl chloride, (CH₃)₃CCOCl, in the presence of AlCl₃ to form 2,2-dimethyl-1-phenylpropan-1-one, C₆H₅COC(CH₃)₃.

**(a)** Name the type of reaction and write an equation to show the role of AlCl₃. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the overall equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(c)** 3.12 g of benzene reacts with excess acyl chloride. 4.73 g of the ketone is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why reactions of this type are important in organic synthesis. **[1]**

**8.** In the presence of a strong acid, benzene reacts with cyclohexene to form cyclohexylbenzene, C₆H₅C₆H₁₁. You are told that H⁺ first adds to cyclohexene to form a carbocation.

**(a)** Give an equation for the carbocation formation. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline the mechanism for its reaction with benzene. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the acid acts as a catalyst. **[1]**

**9.** Three unlabelled samples are ethanol, phenol and propanoic acid, each in aqueous solution.

**(a)** Describe how aqueous sodium carbonate and a pH test would identify all three. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of phenol with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why phenol gives no gas with sodium carbonate. **[1]**

**10.** This question is about the reactions of phenol.

**(a)** State what you would see when bromine water is added to aqueous phenol, and name the organic product. **[2]**
**(b)** State the reagent and conditions for nitrating phenol, and name the two organic products. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why phenol undergoes these substitutions more readily than benzene. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about directing effects.

**(a)** Name the main organic product when nitrobenzene is nitrated further. **[1]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of phenylamine with excess bromine water. **[2]**
**(c)** A chemist wants to make 1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene from benzene. You are told that a Cl substituent directs to the 2- and 4- positions. Give the two steps in the correct order, with reagents, and explain the order. **[3]**
**(d)** You are told that the –CHO group directs to the 3- position. Name the main product of mononitration of benzaldehyde, C₆H₅CHO. **[1]**

**12.** 4.70 g of phenol reacts with dilute nitric acid. After separation, 3.13 g of 2-nitrophenol and 2.78 g of 4-nitrophenol are collected.

**(a)** Give the equation for this nitration. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield of each isomer and the overall percentage yield. **[3]**
**(c)** Ka for phenol is 1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³. Calculate the pH of a 0.20 mol dm⁻³ solution of phenol. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why no 3-nitrophenol forms. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Each carbon has one electron in a p-orbital, above and below the ring plane [1]; the p-orbitals overlap sideways all round the ring, forming a π-system [1]; the six π-electrons are delocalised over all six carbons, whereas Kekulé has three localised C=C bonds alternating with C–C bonds [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Delocalised electrons" alone describes the result; the answer needs the sideways p-orbital overlap that produces it.

**2. (a)** 10.4 ÷ 0.0500 = 208, so ΔH = **−208 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Kekulé prediction = 3 × (−119) = −357 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]; benzene releases 149 kJ mol⁻¹ less energy, so it is **149 kJ mol⁻¹ more stable** than predicted [1]
**(c)** X-ray data show six equal ring bonds, intermediate in length between C–C and C=C [1]; the Kekulé structure needs two bond lengths, alternating round the ring [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State the conclusion in words, "more stable than predicted", rather than leaving a bare number such as 149.

**3. (a)** **2,4,6-trinitrophenol** [1]
**(b)** **4-chlorophenylamine** [1]
**(c)** **1-chloro-3-nitrobenzene** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), the locants 1 and 3 could go either way; chloro comes first alphabetically, so it takes locant 1.

**4. (a)** Concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid [1]; warm to about 50 °C [1]
**(b)** HNO₃ + H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + HSO₄⁻ + H₂O [1]
**(c)** At higher temperatures further substitution occurs, giving dinitrobenzene [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Concentrated" must appear for both acids; "nitric and sulfuric acid" alone describes a different reaction.

**5. (a)** Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ [1]
**(b)** Curly arrow from the π-system to Br⁺ [1]; intermediate with a broken horseshoe over five carbons, a + charge, and H and Br on the sixth carbon [1]; the C–H bond's electrons return to the ring by a curly arrow, giving C₆H₅Br and H⁺ [1]
**(c)** H⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ → AlBr₃ + HBr [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The first arrow must start inside the ring at the π-system, not at a single carbon or an H atom.

**6.** The C=C π-bond of cyclohexene holds its two electrons between just two atoms: a region of high electron density [1]; benzene's six π-electrons spread round the whole ring, so electron density at any bond is lower [1]; benzene cannot induce a large enough dipole in Br₂, so a halogen carrier is needed to form Br⁺ [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare electron density in both molecules; saying only that benzene is "stable" does not explain why Br₂ fails to react.

**7. (a)** Friedel–Crafts acylation (electrophilic substitution) [1]; (CH₃)₃CCOCl + AlCl₃ → (CH₃)₃CCO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ [1]
**(b)** C₆H₆ + (CH₃)₃CCOCl → C₆H₅COC(CH₃)₃ + HCl [1]
**(c)** n(benzene) = 3.12 ÷ 78.0 = 0.0400 mol; Mr(C₁₁H₁₄O) = 162.0, theoretical mass = 6.48 g [1]; yield = 4.73 ÷ 6.48 × 100 = **73.0%** [1]
**(d)** It forms a new C–C bond to the aromatic ring, extending the carbon skeleton [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep 6.48 g unrounded; rounding to 6.5 g early gives 72.8%.

**8. (a)** C₆H₁₀ + H⁺ → C₆H₁₁⁺ [1]
**(b)** Curly arrow from the π-system to the positive carbon of C₆H₁₁⁺, forming the charged intermediate [1]; C–H bond electrons move into the ring (curly arrow) and H⁺ leaves [1]
**(c)** H⁺ is used in forming the electrophile and regenerated in the final step [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Unfamiliar reactions are marked on the same pattern as nitration; reuse the three-arrow sequence rather than inventing a new one.

**9. (a)** Only propanoic acid effervesces with sodium carbonate [1]; of the other two, phenol gives a weakly acidic pH (below 7) while ethanol is neutral [1]
**(b)** C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O [1]
**(c)** Phenol is too weak an acid to release CO₂ from carbonate [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reagent and the result for each substance; NaOH alone cannot separate phenol from the acid.

**10. (a)** Bromine water is decolourised and a white precipitate forms [1]; **2,4,6-tribromophenol** [1]
**(b)** Dilute nitric acid at room temperature; **2-nitrophenol** and **4-nitrophenol** [1]
**(c)** A lone pair in an oxygen p-orbital is partly delocalised into the ring [1]; the ring's electron density rises, so it is more susceptible to electrophilic attack and can polarise Br₂ without a carrier [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep (c) to electron density; an argument based on the stability of the intermediate is outside what the specification asks for.

**11. (a)** **1,3-dinitrobenzene** [1]
**(b)** Product C₆H₂Br₃NH₂ (2,4,6-tribromophenylamine) [1]; C₆H₅NH₂ + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃NH₂ + 3HBr [1]
**(c)** Step 1: nitrate with concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ [1]; step 2: Cl₂ with a halogen carrier such as AlCl₃ [1]; NO₂ directs the chlorine to the 3- position, whereas chlorinating first would give 2- and 4- isomers [1]
**(d)** **3-nitrobenzaldehyde** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), a correct order with no reason gives only part of the credit; link the order to the directing group.

**12. (a)** C₆H₅OH + HNO₃ → C₆H₄(OH)NO₂ + H₂O [1]
**(b)** n(phenol) = 4.70 ÷ 94.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; Mr(C₆H₅NO₃) = 139.0, theoretical mass = 6.95 g [1]; 2-isomer **45.0%**, 4-isomer **40.0%**, overall **85.0%** [1]
**(c)** Ka = [H⁺]² ÷ [C₆H₅OH] [1]; [H⁺] = √(1.3 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.20) = 5.1 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **5.29** [1]
**(d)** OH is 2- and 4-directing, so substitution at position 3 does not happen [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Both isomers share the formula C₆H₅NO₃, so each yield uses the same theoretical mass; a separate Mr for each is a sign of a slip.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing p-orbital overlap without saying it is sideways and all round the ring.
- Giving a hydrogenation difference without the words "more stable".
- Omitting "concentrated" for benzene nitration, or adding H₂SO₄ to phenol nitration.
- Leaving out the halogen carrier, or the equation that regenerates it.
- Starting the first curly arrow at a carbon atom instead of the π-system.
- Explaining phenol's reactivity by intermediate stability.
- Saying phenol fizzes with carbonate.
- Ignoring directing effects when ordering synthesis steps.
- Forgetting the 3HBr in the bromination equations of phenol and phenylamine.
