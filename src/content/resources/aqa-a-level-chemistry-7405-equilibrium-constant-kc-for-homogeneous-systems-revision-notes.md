These notes condense section **3.1.6.2 Equilibrium constant Kc for homogeneous systems** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Equilibrium constant Kc study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-equilibrium-constant-kc-for-homogeneous-systems/) explains each calculation with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-equilibrium-constant-kc-for-homogeneous-systems-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- **Construct** an expression for Kc for a homogeneous system in equilibrium.
- **Calculate** a value for Kc from equilibrium concentrations at constant temperature.
- **Perform calculations** involving Kc.
- **Predict** the qualitative effect of temperature changes on Kc.
- Kc is **not affected** by changes in concentration or by a catalyst.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kc | Equilibrium constant, in terms of concentrations |
| [X] | Equilibrium concentration of X in mol dm⁻³ |
| Homogeneous | All species in the same phase |
| Position of equilibrium | The relative amounts of reactants and products |
| ICE table | A table of initial, change and equilibrium amounts |

## Writing the expression

aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD gives **Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ ÷ ([A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ)**.

- Products over reactants.
- Powers equal to the coefficients.
- Concentrations at equilibrium.
- Reverse the equation: Kc becomes 1 ÷ Kc. Double the coefficients: Kc is squared.

## Units

Substitute units into the expression and cancel.

| Reaction | Units of Kc |
|---|---|
| N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ | mol⁻² dm⁶ |
| 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃ | mol⁻¹ dm³ |
| H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI | none |
| PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂ | mol dm⁻³ |

## Method for a moles calculation

1. Write the equation and the expression for Kc.
2. Draw a table: initial, change, equilibrium (in mol).
3. Use the mole ratio to find the changes.
4. Divide by the volume (in dm³) to get concentrations. If the volume cancels, you may use moles.
5. Substitute into Kc and give the units.

## Worked reminders

**From concentrations.** [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.20, [HI] = 1.60 mol dm⁻³. Kc = 1.60² ÷ (0.20 × 0.20) = **64**, no units.

**From moles.** 1.00 mol N₂ and 3.00 mol H₂ in 2.00 dm³; 0.40 mol NH₃ at equilibrium.
Equilibrium amounts: N₂ 0.80, H₂ 2.40, NH₃ 0.40 mol. Concentrations: 0.40, 1.20, 0.20 mol dm⁻³.
Kc = 0.20² ÷ (0.40 × 1.20³) = **0.0579 mol⁻² dm⁶**.

**Decomposition.** 1.00 mol PCl₅ in 2.00 dm³, 0.40 mol left.
Equilibrium: PCl₅ 0.20, PCl₃ 0.30, Cl₂ 0.30 mol dm⁻³. Kc = 0.30 × 0.30 ÷ 0.20 = **0.45 mol dm⁻³**.

**Unknown concentration.** N₂O₄ ⇌ 2NO₂, Kc = 0.0060 mol dm⁻³, [N₂O₄] = 0.050.
[NO₂] = √(0.0060 × 0.050) = **0.0173 mol dm⁻³**.

**Ester.** Kc = 4.0, starting with 1.00 mol each of acid and ethanol: x² ÷ (1.00 − x)² = 4.0, so x ÷ (1.00 − x) = 2.0 and x = **0.667 mol**.

## What changes Kc?

| Change | Effect on Kc |
|---|---|
| Concentration | None |
| Pressure | None |
| Catalyst | None |
| Temperature | Changes Kc |

- Exothermic forward reaction: raising T **decreases** Kc.
- Endothermic forward reaction: raising T **increases** Kc.
- Reason: the position shifts in the endothermic direction on heating, changing the ratio of products to reactants.
- After a change in concentration the position shifts until the ratio equals Kc again.

## Common mistakes

- Reactants on the top of the expression.
- Forgetting powers from coefficients.
- Using initial amounts instead of equilibrium amounts.
- Using moles when the volume does not cancel.
- Missing or wrong units.
- Saying that Kc changes with pressure, concentration or a catalyst.
- Confusing the size of Kc (extent) with the rate.
- Rounding too early.

## Self-test

1. Write the expression for Kc for 2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g).
2. Give the units of Kc for 2NO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g).
3. For 2NO + O₂ ⇌ 2NO₂, [NO] = 0.020, [O₂] = 0.010 and [NO₂] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. Calculate Kc with units.
4. 2.00 mol H₂ and 2.00 mol I₂ react, and 3.00 mol HI is present at equilibrium. Calculate Kc for H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI.
5. For H₂ + I₂ ⇌ 2HI, Kc = 50. If [H₂] = [I₂] = 0.10 mol dm⁻³, calculate [HI].
6. If Kc for A ⇌ B is 0.25, what is Kc for B ⇌ A?
7. Explain why adding more reactant to an equilibrium does not change Kc.
8. State the effect of a catalyst on Kc.
9. A reaction is exothermic. What happens to Kc when the temperature rises?
10. A reaction is endothermic. What happens to Kc when the temperature rises?
11. Kc for CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O is 4.0. Starting with 2.00 mol of each reactant, calculate the amount of ester at equilibrium.
12. Does a large value of Kc mean that a reaction is fast? Explain.

### Answers

1. Kc = [N₂O₄] ÷ [NO₂]².
2. (mol dm⁻³)² ÷ ((mol dm⁻³)²(mol dm⁻³)) = **mol⁻¹ dm³**.
3. Kc = 0.040² ÷ (0.020² × 0.010) = 0.0016 ÷ 4.0 × 10⁻⁶ = **400 mol⁻¹ dm³**.
4. At equilibrium H₂ and I₂ are 0.50 mol each. Kc = 3.00² ÷ (0.50 × 0.50) = **36** (volume cancels, no units).
5. [HI]² = 50 × 0.10 × 0.10 = 0.50, so [HI] = **0.71 mol dm⁻³**.
6. 1 ÷ 0.25 = **4.0**.
7. The position shifts so that the ratio of concentrations returns to the same value.
8. None: it speeds up the forward and reverse reactions equally.
9. Kc decreases.
10. Kc increases.
11. x ÷ (2.00 − x) = 2.0, so x = **1.33 mol**.
12. No. Kc is about the extent of reaction at equilibrium, not the rate at which equilibrium is reached.
