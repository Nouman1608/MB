## What the syllabus lists

- Weathering: thermal fracture, exfoliation, salt weathering, chemical weathering, and their effects.
- Wind: corrasion/abrasion, deflation, traction, saltation, suspension.
- Water: hydrological regime, episodic rainfall, sheet and flash floods.
- Landforms: sand dunes, yardangs, zeugen, wadis, alluvial fans, arroyos, pediments, piedmont zone (bahadas, playas, salt lakes, inselbergs).
- Relative roles of aeolian and fluvial processes; Pleistocene pluvials; role of past processes.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Grus | Loose grains from granular disintegration of coarse rock |
| Tafoni | Honeycomb hollows from salt weathering |
| Erg / reg / hamada | Sand sea / stony plain / bare rock plateau |
| Desert pavement | Closely packed surface of stones |
| Angle of repose | Steepest stable angle of loose sand, about 30-34° when dry |
| Slip face | Steep lee side of a dune where sand avalanches |
| Ephemeral / exogenous | Flowing only after rain / fed from wetter areas beyond the desert |
| Transmission loss | Water lost into the channel bed during a flood |
| Fan-head trenching | Channel cut into the head of an alluvial fan |
| Piedmont angle | Sharp break of slope between mountain front and pediment |
| Bornhardt / kopje | Dome-shaped inselberg / small boulder-pile inselberg |
| Pluvial | Wetter period in the past, often in the Pleistocene |
| Relict landform | Formed under past conditions, now inactive or modified |

## Wind transport by grain size

- **Suspension:** silt and clay, finer than about 0.06 mm; travels very far.
- **Saltation:** sand about 0.06-2 mm; bounces within a metre or two of the ground.
- **Traction:** coarser grains rolled or pushed.

## Dune types

| Type | Wind | Sand | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|
| Barchan | One direction | Limited | Crescent, horns downwind |
| Transverse | One direction | Plentiful | Ridges at right angles to wind |
| Seif | Two directions | Moderate | Long ridges parallel to wind |
| Star | Many directions | Plentiful | Radiating arms, grows upward |
| Parabolic | One direction, vegetated | Moderate | U-shape, arms upwind |

## Fluvial landforms in one line

- **Wadi / arroyo:** flat-floored, steep-sided channel cut by flash floods.
- **Alluvial fan:** deposition where a channel leaves the mountain front and loses gradient and confinement.
- **Bahada:** merged fans.
- **Pediment:** gentle rock slope at the mountain foot; lateral planation, sheet floods or scarp retreat.
- **Playa / salt lake:** basin floor where floodwater evaporates.
- **Inselberg:** isolated hill; deep weathering and stripping, or scarp retreat.

## Evidence of past climates

- Palaeolake shorelines and lake sediments.
- Dry valleys too large for present rainfall.
- Vegetated, stabilised dunes.
- Fossil groundwater (Nubian Sandstone aquifer).
- Rock art of water-dependent animals (Tassili n'Ajjer).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Pediment** is erosional bedrock; **bahada** is depositional, made of merged fans.
- **Sheet flood** spreads unconfined across gentle slopes; **flash flood** is confined to a channel.
- **Yardang** is streamlined and parallel to the wind; **zeuge** is flat-topped with a resistant cap.
- **Deflation** removes loose particles; **abrasion** wears rock with moving sand.

## Mini essay plan

*"Most desert landforms are relict features of past climates." How far do you agree?*

1. **Define** relict landforms and Pleistocene pluvials.
2. **For:** palaeolake shorelines, oversized wadis, stabilised dunes, deep weathering beneath inselbergs, fossil groundwater.
3. **Against:** active dunes migrate today; flash floods still cut wadis and build fans; wind shapes yardangs now.
4. **Interaction:** present processes rework inherited forms.
5. **Judgement:** large-scale forms are often inherited; small-scale features are mostly active.

## Exam traps

- Calling every landform aeolian.
- Saltation and traction mixed up.
- Fans without loss of gradient and confinement.
- Pluvials named without evidence.
- Relict forms treated as unchanged; present processes rework them.
- Arroyo cutting blamed only on overgrazing.

## Self-test

1. What is grus and which process produces it?
2. Why is abrasion concentrated near the ground?
3. Give two explanations for desert pavements.
4. Why does a dune advance downwind?
5. Under what conditions do barchans form?
6. How do star dunes differ from seif dunes?
7. What is the difference between ephemeral and exogenous rivers?
8. Why do flash floods deposit sediment abruptly?
9. Why is coarse material found at the apex of an alluvial fan?
10. Give two explanations for pediments.
11. Give three pieces of evidence for Pleistocene pluvials.
12. Why do most geomorphologists think fluvial processes dominate many deserts?

### Answers

1. Loose mineral grains from granular disintegration, mainly by thermal fracture of coarse-grained rock.
2. Saltating sand travels mostly within a metre or two of the ground.
3. Deflation of fine material between stones; dust accumulating beneath stones, lifting them.
4. Sand moves up the windward slope and avalanches down the slip face, so the dune shifts downwind.
5. One dominant wind direction and limited sand on a hard surface.
6. Star dunes form under winds from many directions and grow upward; seifs form under two alternating directions as long ridges.
7. Ephemeral rivers flow only after rain; exogenous rivers rise in wetter areas and flow through the desert.
8. Water is lost to the bed and evaporation, and gradient falls, so the high load is dropped quickly.
9. The channel loses confinement and gradient there, so the heaviest material is dropped first.
10. Any two: lateral planation by shifting channels; sheet flood erosion; parallel retreat of the mountain front.
11. Any three: palaeolake shorelines, oversized dry valleys, stabilised dunes, fossil groundwater, rock art.
12. Fluvial landforms are widespread even in the driest deserts, and single floods move more sediment than years of wind.
