## Key results at a glance

| Idea | Rule |
|---|---|
| Gradient | Change up ÷ change across |
| As a rate | Units are y-unit per x-unit |
| Vertical intercept | Value of y when x = 0 (the starting amount) |
| Speed | distance ÷ time |
| Acceleration | change in speed ÷ time |
| m/s to km/h | × 3.6 |
| km/h to m/s | ÷ 3.6 |
| Area under a velocity-time graph (Extension only) | Distance travelled |
| Gradient of a distance-time graph | Speed |
| Gradient of a velocity-time graph | Acceleration |

## Reading real-context graphs

Method in steps:

1. Read the axis labels and units before anything else.
2. To find y from x, go up from x to the graph, then across.
3. To solve "when does the quantity equal c?", draw a horizontal line at c and read the x values where it meets the graph.
4. Where two graphs cross, the two quantities are equal at that point.
5. Give units and write "approximately" or "about".

A graph with a steep part and a shallow part shows the rate changing. A horizontal part means no change in that quantity, which in a distance-time graph means standing still.

### Worked reminder

A water bill is a fixed charge plus a cost per cubic metre. The line passes through (0, 18) and (20, 66), with cost in pounds.

```
Gradient = (66 - 18)/(20 - 0) = 48/20 = 2.4
```

The gradient means **£2.40 per cubic metre**. The intercept means a **£18 fixed charge**. The cost for 35 cubic metres is 18 + 2.4 × 35 = £102.

## Comparing two graphs

A phone plan costs £8 plus £0.50 for each gigabyte. A second plan costs £3 plus £0.80 for each gigabyte.

```
8 + 0.5g = 3 + 0.8g
5 = 0.3g
g = 16.67 (to 2 d.p.)
```

The lines cross at about 17 gigabytes. Below that the second plan is cheaper, because it starts lower. Above it the first plan is cheaper, because its gradient is smaller.

## Straight-line gradient as rate of change

- A positive gradient: quantity increasing. A negative gradient: decreasing.
- A larger gradient (in size) means a faster rate.
- Always state the meaning in context: "The tank loses 8 litres every minute."
- A straight line means the rate is constant. A curve means the rate is changing.

## Kinematics

### Distance-time graphs

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Straight upward line | Steady movement away from the start |
| Flat line | Not moving |
| Straight downward line | Constant speed back towards the start |
| Steeper line | Faster |

Average speed = total distance travelled ÷ total time. Never use displacement for this.

### Velocity-time graphs

| Feature | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Horizontal line | Constant speed, no acceleration |
| Upward slope | Speeding up |
| Downward slope | Slowing down (negative acceleration) |
| Area under the line (Extension only) | Distance travelled |

(Extension only) For a straight-line graph, find the area as a rectangle, triangle or trapezium: area = ½ × (sum of the two parallel sides) × height.

### Worked reminder (acceleration Core; distance Extension only)

A lift goes from rest to 3 m/s in 4 s, moves at 3 m/s for 10 s, then stops in 2 s.

```
Acceleration = 3/4 = 0.75 m/s²
Deceleration = 3/2 = 1.5 m/s²
Distance = ½ × (10 + 16) × 3 = 39 m
```

The parallel sides are 10 s (constant speed) and 16 s (whole journey), and the height is 3 m/s.

### Unit changes

```
54 km/h ÷ 3.6 = 15 m/s
8 m/s × 3.6 = 28.8 km/h
```

Be consistent: if speed is in km/h, convert minutes to hours first (20 min = 1/3 h).

## Curved graphs (Extension only)

| Task | Method |
|---|---|
| Gradient at a point | Draw a tangent with a ruler, then use two far-apart points on the tangent |
| Area under a curve | Split into equal strips and use trapezia |

Trapezium estimate for equal strips of width h:

```
Area ≈ (h/2) × [first + last + 2 × (sum of the middle readings)]
```

Whether the estimate is high or low depends on the curve's shape. Where the curve bends downwards, trapezia tend to give an underestimate. Where it bends upwards, they tend to give an overestimate.

### Worked reminder (Extension only): tangent

On a velocity-time graph, a tangent at t = 6 s passes through (2, 5) and (10, 11) m/s.

```
Gradient = (11 - 5)/(10 - 2) = 6/8 = 0.75
```

The acceleration at t = 6 is about **0.75 m/s²**.

### Worked reminder (Extension only): trapezia

Speed readings every 5 s are 0, 4, 6, 7 and 8 m/s from t = 0 to t = 20.

```
Area ≈ (5/2) × [0 + 8 + 2 × (4 + 6 + 7)]
     = 2.5 × [8 + 34]
     = 105 m
```

## Direct and inverse variation (Extension only)

| Words | Equation |
|---|---|
| y ∝ x | y = kx |
| y ∝ x² | y = kx² |
| y ∝ x³ | y = kx³ |
| y ∝ √x | y = k√x |
| y inversely ∝ x | y = k/x |
| y inversely ∝ x² | y = k/x² |
| y inversely ∝ √x | y = k/√x |

Method in steps:

1. Write the equation with k.
2. Substitute the known pair to find k.
3. Write the formula with that value of k.
4. Substitute the new value and solve.

### Worked reminder

y is proportional to x³, and y = 40 when x = 2. Find y when x = 3 and x when y = 320.

```
y = kx³, 40 = 8k, k = 5
x = 3: y = 5 × 27 = 135
y = 320: x³ = 64, x = 4
```

### Must-know distinctions

| Direct | Inverse |
|---|---|
| Both quantities rise together | One rises as the other falls |
| y ÷ xⁿ stays fixed | y × xⁿ stays fixed |
| Graph starts at the origin | Graph never meets either axis |
| Double x: y doubles (y = kx) | Double x: y halves (y = k/x) |

For y = kx², doubling x multiplies y by 4. For y = k/x², doubling x divides y by 4.

## Quick self-test

1. A straight line passes through (2, 5) and (10, 29). Find its gradient.
2. A tank fills at a constant rate. After 8 minutes it holds 56 litres, starting empty. What is the rate in litres per minute?
3. A walker covers 5 km in 15 minutes at steady speed. Give the speed in km/h.
4. Convert 90 km/h to m/s.
5. A scooter's speed goes from 4 m/s to 16 m/s in 6 s. Find its acceleration.
6. (Extension only) A bus starts from rest and reaches 6 m/s after 5 s, with uniform acceleration. How far does it travel in this time?
7. (Extension only) y is directly proportional to x. y = 21 when x = 7. Find y when x = 12.
8. (Extension only) y is inversely proportional to x. y = 5 when x = 8. Find y when x = 2.
9. (Extension only) y is proportional to x³ and y = 16 when x = 2. Find x when y = 54.
10. (Extension only) y is inversely proportional to √x and y = 4 when x = 9. Find x when y = 3.

### Answers

1. (29 - 5)/(10 - 2) = 24/8 = **3**
2. 56 ÷ 8 = **7 litres per minute**
3. 15 minutes = 0.25 h, so 5 ÷ 0.25 = **20 km/h**
4. 90 ÷ 3.6 = **25 m/s**
5. (16 - 4) ÷ 6 = **2 m/s²**
6. Area of a triangle = ½ × 5 × 6 = **15 m**
7. y = kx, k = 21/7 = 3, y = 3 × 12 = **36**
8. k = 5 × 8 = 40, y = 40/2 = **20**
9. y = kx³, 16 = 8k, k = 2; 54 = 2x³, x³ = 27, **x = 3**
10. y = k/√x, 4 = k/3, k = 12; 3 = 12/√x, √x = 4, **x = 16**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a gradient with no units, or with units that do not match the axes.
- Quoting the gradient as a plain number when the question asks what it represents.
- Drawing a line that cuts the curve instead of touching it when estimating a gradient (Extension only).
- Finding average speed from the distance at the end instead of the total distance travelled.
- Mixing minutes and hours, or metres and kilometres, in one calculation.
- Using the whole time as a parallel side of a trapezium when only the constant-speed part is the other side.
- Substituting the new value into the formula before finding k (Extension only).
- Treating "inversely proportional" as y = kx, or "proportional to x²" as y = kx.
- Leaving a read-off answer without saying it is approximate, or giving a value far outside a sensible reading range.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does the gradient of a real-life graph represent?**
The gradient is the rate of change of the vertical quantity per unit of the horizontal quantity, so its units are "y-unit per x-unit", such as pounds per cubic metre or litres per minute. A straight line means the rate is constant; a curve means the rate is changing.

**How do you convert between m/s and km/h?**
Multiply a speed in m/s by 3.6 to get km/h, and divide a speed in km/h by 3.6 to get m/s. For example, 90 km/h is 90 ÷ 3.6 = 25 m/s.

**Which parts of OxfordAQA 9260 graphs and variation are Extension only?**
In OxfordAQA International GCSE Mathematics (9260), reference A17 has Core and Extension content, while A18 (direct and inverse variation) is Extension only. Gradients of curves and areas under graphs, including distance from the area under a velocity-time graph, are also Extension only.

**What is the difference between direct and inverse proportion?**
In direct proportion both quantities rise together and y ÷ xⁿ stays fixed, as in y = kx. In inverse proportion one rises as the other falls and y × xⁿ stays fixed, as in y = k/x, so doubling x halves y.

**How do you work out average speed from a distance-time graph?**
Divide the total distance travelled by the total time, including any time spent stationary. Do not use the final distance from the start, because a journey that returns home has travelled further than its final displacement.
