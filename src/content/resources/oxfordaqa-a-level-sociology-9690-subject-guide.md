OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Sociology (9690) is a brand-new
specification, first taught from September 2026. It asks how societies
work, how people become who they are, and how global forces such as
development, environmental change and technology are reshaping social
life. The qualification is modular and made up of five units, each
worth 20% of the A-level. The two AS units are examined in papers of 105
minutes and 60 marks; the three A-level units in papers of 90 minutes
and 50 marks. The first AS exams are in May/June 2027 and the first
A-level exams in May/June 2028, so no one has yet sat an exam on this
specification.

## How the course is organised

The five units fall into two stages. The AS units build the foundations
of the discipline; the A-level units apply them to large-scale, often
global, questions.

**AS stage**

- **Unit 1, Introducing Sociology**: families, marriage and kinship;
  perspectives on families and their relationship with society; social
  change and changing families; and gender, age and the experience of
  family life. Section B of the unit covers research methods and theory.
- **Unit 2, Exploring Sociology**: individuals and society;
  socialisation; social identities and social change; social control;
  and deviance, harm and crime. Section B again covers research methods
  and theory.

**A-level stage**

- **Unit 3, People and Development**: the Great Divergence; what is
  meant by globalisation and who is involved in it; demographic change
  through migration and diasporas; and obstacles to progress.
- **Unit 4, People and the Environment**: the built human environment,
  human impacts on the environment, and people and animals.
- **Unit 5, People and Technology**: pre- and non-digital technologies;
  'traditional' media; the internet and social and new media; digital
  technologies; and artificial intelligence and cyborgs.

## How it's assessed

| Stage | Unit | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting (of A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS | AS Unit 1 | Families; Research Methods | 105 minutes | 60 | 20% |
| AS | AS Unit 2 | Socialisation and Social Control; Research Methods | 105 minutes | 60 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Unit 3 | People and Development | 90 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Unit 4 | People and the Environment | 90 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| A-level | A-level Unit 5 | People and Technology | 90 minutes | 50 | 20% |

Each unit is examined by an externally assessed written paper, and the
course is not tiered. Two things are worth noticing:

- **Equal weighting, unequal papers.** Every unit counts for the same
  share of the A-level, even though the AS papers are longer and carry
  more raw marks than the A-level papers.
- **Modular timing.** The AS units are examined at the end of the first
  year, from May/June 2027, and the A-level units at the end of the
  second, from May/June 2028. Check with your school how you will be
  entered.

Because this is the first cohort, there are no past papers from previous
series yet. This page follows Version 1.1 of the specification.

## How the parts fit together

The specification is designed so that the AS units equip you for the
A-level ones:

- **Research methods and theory are taught in both AS units.** Section B
  of Unit 1 and Unit 2 covers the methods and theories that you then
  apply whenever you assess evidence in Units 3, 4 and 5.
- **Socialisation and identity underpin the global units.** Ideas from
  Unit 2 about how people are shaped by society, and how identities
  change, help in analysing migration and diasporas in Unit 3, and life
  online in Unit 5.
- **Families connect to development and demography.** The Unit 1 work on
  changing families links to demographic change in Unit 3.
- **Social control and deviance link to technology and environment.**
  Questions about harm, regulation and who has power reappear in debates
  about digital technologies and human impacts on the environment.
- **Globalisation is a shared theme.** It is named in Unit 3, but it also
  frames the built environment in Unit 4 and the spread of media and
  digital technologies in Unit 5.

The skills that cross every unit are using sociological concepts and
theories accurately, applying them to evidence, and evaluating competing
explanations.

## Planning your study

The unit order is the natural teaching order:

- **First year:** Units 1 and 2. Learn research methods and theory
  alongside each topic rather than as a separate list, since both AS
  units examine them. By the end of the year you should be able to
  explain how a sociologist would study any of the AS topics.
- **End of the first year:** the first AS exam series, May/June 2027.
- **Second year:** Units 3, 4 and 5. Take the sociological theories from
  AS with you into each unit, and keep a set of notes on globalisation
  that you add to in every unit.
- **Final months:** revise the three A-level units together, ahead of
  the first A-level series in May/June 2028.

With no past papers yet available for this specification, practise with
teacher-set questions, and any sample material your school provides, as
each unit is taught. The course's exam preparation page explains how to prepare for a
new specification's papers.

## Frequently asked questions

**When are the first exams for OxfordAQA A-Level Sociology 9690?**
The first AS exams are in May/June 2027 and the first A-level exams in May/June 2028. First teaching began in September 2026.

**How many units are there and how much is each worth?**
There are five units, two at AS and three at A-level, and each is worth 20% of the A-level.

**Why are the AS papers longer than the A-level papers?**
The AS units are examined in papers of 105 minutes and 60 marks, and the A-level units in papers of 90 minutes and 50 marks, but every unit carries the same weighting.

**Where does research methods appear in 9690?**
Research methods and theory form Section B of both AS units, Unit 1 and Unit 2, and that knowledge is used throughout the A-level units.

**Are there past papers for this specification?**
Not yet. No exam has been sat on it, so practice comes from teacher-set questions and any sample material your school provides until the first series.
