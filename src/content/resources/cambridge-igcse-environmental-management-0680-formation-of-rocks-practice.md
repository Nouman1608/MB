## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by crystallisation in the rock cycle. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each of these rocks as permeable or impermeable: basalt, limestone, marble, sandstone. **[4]**

**3.** State the difference between magma and lava. **[2]**

**4.** Name the rock cycle process in which the weight of overlying layers squeezes sediment particles together. **[1]**

**5.** Name the metamorphic rock that forms from shale, and state the main condition that changes the shale. **[2]**

**6.** Limestone often contains fossils but marble does not. Explain why. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Granite and basalt are both igneous rocks.

**(a)** Describe how granite forms. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the crystals in basalt are smaller than those in granite. **[2]**

**8.** A simplified rock cycle is described by five arrows.

- Arrow A: lava from a volcano becomes basalt.
- Arrow B: a basalt cliff breaks up and the fragments are carried away by a river.
- Arrow C: the river slows down where it enters a lake and the fragments settle on the lake bed, building up layer upon layer.
- Arrow D: deeply buried layers of sand become sandstone.
- Arrow E: shale deep within a mountain belt becomes slate.

**(a)** Name the process shown by arrow A. **[1]**

**(b)** Name **two** processes shown by arrow B. **[2]**

**(c)** Name **two** processes shown by arrow C. **[2]**

**(d)** Name **two** processes shown by arrow D. **[2]**

**(e)** State the type of rock produced at arrow E and what causes the change. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the formation of limestone. **[4]**

**10.** A student measured the time taken for 50 cm³ of water to drain through blocks of four rocks of the same size. The results (invented for practice) were: sandstone 75 seconds; limestone 125 seconds; shale and granite, no water drained through after 10 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at which water drained through the sandstone, in cm³ per second. Give your answer to two decimal places. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why no water drained through the shale, even though shale is a sedimentary rock. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** improvement that would make the results more reliable. **[1]**

**11.** Explain how the permeability of rocks affects:

**(a)** where ground water can be stored underground **[2]**

**(b)** why natural gas held in shale cannot simply flow into an ordinary well. **[2]**

**12.** A student writes: "Marble is limestone that has melted and then cooled again." Explain why this statement is incorrect, and describe how marble actually forms. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The formation of crystals as magma or lava cools (to form igneous rock) [1].

**2.** Basalt: impermeable [1]. Limestone: permeable [1]. Marble: impermeable [1]. Sandstone: permeable [1].

**3.** Magma is molten rock below the Earth's surface [1]; lava is molten rock that has reached the surface [1].

**4.** Compaction [1].

**5.** Slate [1]; (high) pressure, for example from deep burial or mountain building [1]. Accept heat and pressure.

**6.** Marble forms when limestone is changed by heat (and pressure) [1]; recrystallisation of the minerals destroys the fossil remains [1].

**7. (a)** Magma rises into the crust but does not reach the surface [1]; it cools slowly because it is insulated by the surrounding rock [1]; the minerals crystallise into large, interlocking crystals, forming granite [1].

**(b)** Basalt forms from lava that cools quickly at or near the surface [1], so the crystals have little time to grow [1].

**8. (a)** Crystallisation [1].

**(b)** Any two: weathering [1]; erosion [1]; transportation [1].

**(c)** Deposition [1]; sedimentation [1].

**(d)** Compaction [1]; cementation [1].

**(e)** Metamorphic rock [1]; pressure (and heat) altering the rock without melting it [1].

**9.** Any four:
- Shells and skeletons of sea creatures, made of calcium carbonate, collect on the floor of warm, shallow seas [1].
- They build up in layers over long periods (sedimentation) [1].
- The weight of later layers compacts the material and squeezes out water [1].
- Minerals in the water seeping between the fragments cement them together [1].
- The result is a sedimentary rock that often contains fossils [1].

**10. (a)** 50 ÷ 75 [1] = 0.67 cm³ per second [1].

**(b)** Shale's particles are very fine and tightly compacted [1], so its pore spaces are tiny and not connected, which stops water passing through [1].

**(c)** Any one: repeat each test (for example three times) and calculate a mean [1]; use blocks of exactly the same size and shape [1]; time for longer than 10 minutes to see whether any water passes through [1].

**11. (a)** Permeable rocks such as sandstone and limestone have connected pore spaces that can hold and transmit water [1]; ground water collects in permeable rock lying above impermeable rock, which stops it draining further down [1].

**(b)** Shale is impermeable, so gas is trapped in tiny unconnected pore spaces [1] and cannot move through the rock towards the well unless the shale is cracked open, for example by hydraulic fracturing [1].

**12.** If limestone melted, it would become magma, and cooling magma forms igneous rock, not metamorphic rock [1]. Marble forms when limestone is changed by heat (and pressure) without melting [1], for example near a body of hot magma [1]; the calcium carbonate recrystallises into interlocking crystals, so marble is harder and impermeable [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Crystal size without cause.** Question 7(b) needs both "cooled quickly" and "at the surface" or "little time for crystals to grow"; "basalt is volcanic" alone does not explain anything.
- **Merging processes.** Arrow C in Question 8 shows two separate ideas: dropping material (deposition) and building up layers (sedimentation). Name both.
- **Calculation units.** In Question 10(a) the rate must be in cm³ per second, as the question asks, and rounded as instructed.
- **Melting.** Any answer that says metamorphic rock forms by melting loses the formation mark in Question 12.
- **Permeability explained only by rock family.** Saying "shale is impermeable because it is shale" gains nothing; refer to pore spaces and whether they are connected.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to name rocks other than the seven in the syllabus?**
No. Sub-topic 1.1 is limited to granite, basalt, limestone, sandstone, shale, marble and slate, so questions on formation use these rocks.

**How many processes should I give for "describe the formation" of a sedimentary rock?**
Give them in order: weathering and erosion, transportation, deposition and sedimentation, compaction and cementation. Match the number of steps to the marks available.
