These notes condense content item 1.5.5, External influences, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [external influences study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-external-influences/) works through a florist facing two changes at once; test yourself with the [external influences practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-external-influences-practice/). For the changes themselves, see the [technology revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-technology-and-business-revision-notes/), [legislation revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-legislation-and-business-revision-notes/) and [economy revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-the-economy-and-business-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

The importance of external influences on business: possible responses by the business to changes in technology, legislation, the economic climate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| External influence | A factor outside the business that it cannot control but that affects it |
| Internal factor | Something the business controls, such as its prices or staff |
| Response | An action a business takes because of a change |
| Threat | An external change likely to harm the business |
| Opportunity | An external change the business can benefit from |
| Economic climate | The overall state of the economy |
| Compliance | Meeting legal obligations |

## Why external influences matter

- They change revenue (customers' spending, rivals' technology).
- They change costs (laws, exchange rates, inflation, interest).
- Laws cannot be ignored; technology and economic changes affect rivals too.
- They bring opportunities as well as threats.
- Small firms have less cash to absorb shocks but can react quickly.

## Response toolkit

| Family | Examples |
|---|---|
| Price | Raise to cover costs; cut to keep customers |
| Product or range | Cheaper lines; drop weak products; add features |
| Promotion or place | Promote value; sell online; offer delivery |
| Costs | Cheaper suppliers; fewer hours; less waste |
| Invest | New technology; training; safety equipment |
| Finance | Pay off variable-rate loans; delay borrowing; build reserves |
| Markets or suppliers | Export when the pound is weak; buy locally when imports are dear |

## Typical responses by change

| Change | Possible responses |
|---|---|
| Rivals adopt new technology | Adopt it; train staff; compete on service instead |
| New payment methods expected by customers | Install card readers or online checkout |
| Higher legal minimum wage | Comply; raise prices; improve productivity; reduce hours |
| Stricter product rules | Comply; change suppliers or materials; promote higher standards |
| Falling consumer incomes | Cheaper ranges; value promotion; cut costs |
| Rising interest rates | Repay loans; delay expansion; push for faster payment from customers |
| Inflation | Review prices; find cheaper suppliers; reduce waste |
| Weaker pound | Switch to UK suppliers; target exports |
| Higher taxes on spending | Absorb some of the rise; promote value |

## Five tests for judging a response

1. Cost: can the business afford it?
2. Speed: how quickly will it work?
3. Risk: what could go wrong?
4. Customers and competitors: how will they react?
5. Objectives: does it fit the owner's aims?

## Calculations that appear here

- New cost after an exchange-rate change: foreign price ÷ new rate.
- Contribution per unit = price − variable cost per unit.
- Total contribution = units × contribution per unit.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

Example: price £20, variable cost rises from £12 to £13. Contribution falls from £8 to £7, a fall of 12.5%.

## Exam traps

- Always give a response, not just the effect of the change.
- Legal changes must be met; "ignore the law" is never a valid response.
- "Raise prices" is not the answer to everything: check competition and customers' incomes.
- Note that a change can be an opportunity.
- Evaluate: name a cost, a risk or a condition for the response you suggest.

## Sentence starter

The writer's suggestion: "In response to [change], the business could [response], which would [effect], although this depends on [condition]."

## Self-test

1. Name the three external influences in 1.5.5.
2. Define external influence.
3. Give one reason external influences are important to a small business.
4. Give one response to rivals starting to sell online.
5. Give one response to a higher legal minimum wage.
6. Why is ignoring a new law not a valid response?
7. Give one response to rising interest rates for a business with a large overdraft.
8. Give one response to a weaker pound for a business that imports materials.
9. Give one response to falling consumer incomes for a restaurant.
10. Name the five tests for judging a response.
11. Price £40, variable cost rises from £22 to £25. Calculate the percentage fall in contribution per unit.
12. Give one external change that could be an opportunity for a UK exporter.

### Answers

1. Technology, legislation, the economic climate.
2. A factor outside the business that it cannot control but that affects it.
3. Any one: they can change revenue or costs quickly; they cannot be ignored; they can bring opportunities.
4. Any one: open its own online shop; offer delivery; compete on personal service.
5. Any one: raise prices; improve productivity; reduce hours; accept lower profit (while paying the minimum).
6. The business is legally obliged to comply, and breaking the law risks fines, compensation and damage to reputation.
7. Any one: reduce the overdraft; delay borrowing; ask customers to pay more quickly.
8. Any one: switch to UK suppliers; raise prices; buy in larger amounts before further falls.
9. Any one: a cheaper set menu; value promotions; cut costs.
10. Cost, speed, risk, customers and competitors, objectives.
11. Contribution falls from £18 to £15; £3 ÷ £18 × 100 = 16.7%.
12. A fall in the value of the pound, making exports cheaper abroad.
