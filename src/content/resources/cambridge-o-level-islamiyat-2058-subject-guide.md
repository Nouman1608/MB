Cambridge O Level Islamiyat (2058) introduces students to the foundations of Islam through its two principal sources, the Qur'an and the Hadiths, and through the history of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), the first Muslim community and the Rightly Guided Caliphs, alongside the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam. Candidates sit two compulsory, externally assessed papers, Paper 1 and Paper 2, each lasting 1.5 hours and marked out of 50; because the two papers carry equal marks, each is worth 50% of the total. The course is examined in English and offered in both June and November series. This overview sets out what each paper contains and how to organise your study across them.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus labels the two papers simply Paper 1 and Paper 2. Between them they contain eight content areas, four per paper.

**Paper 1 – the Qur'an, the Prophet (pbuh) and the first community**

1. Major Themes of the Qur'an
2. The History and Importance of the Qur'an
3. The Life and Importance of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh)
4. The First Islamic Community

**Paper 2 – the Hadiths, the Caliphs and the foundations of faith**

1. Major Teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet (pbuh)
2. The History and Importance of the Hadiths
3. The Period of Rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and Their Importance as Leaders
4. The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam

Each paper therefore moves from a source of guidance (the Qur'an or the Hadiths) to the history of how that guidance was received and lived out.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is linear and not tiered. Both papers are taken at the end of the course and both are marked by Cambridge.

| Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Questions |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 1.5 hours | 50 | 50% | Five questions printed: Question 1 and Question 2 compulsory, plus two more of your choice |
| Paper 2 | 1.5 hours | 50 | 50% | Five questions printed: Question 1 and Question 2 compulsory, plus two more of your choice |

The board does not print a percentage for each paper, but with two compulsory papers of 50 marks each, the split is necessarily 50% and 50%. The question structure matters for planning: since two questions on each paper are compulsory and you choose the rest, you cannot leave any area entirely unrevised, but you do have some room to play to your strengths in the optional questions.

## How the parts fit together

The content of the two papers is best understood as one connected account rather than two separate subjects.

Paper 1's study of the Prophet's (pbuh) life (pbuh) provides the setting for Paper 2. The Hadiths preserve his words and practice, so knowing the events of his life helps you explain when and why particular teachings matter. The First Islamic Community in Paper 1 leads directly into the period of the Rightly Guided Caliphs in Paper 2, which describes how that community was led after the Prophet's (pbuh) death (pbuh).

There is also a parallel between the two "history and importance" topics. The History and Importance of the Qur'an and The History and Importance of the Hadiths both ask how a source was compiled and preserved and why Muslims value it. Comparing the two, rather than revising them in isolation, sharpens your understanding of each.

The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam tie the course together. Beliefs and practices covered on Paper 2 are rooted in the themes of the Qur'an studied on Paper 1, so cross-references between the two can strengthen an explanation.

Throughout the course, three abilities are tested again and again: accurate knowledge of the content, clear explanation of its meaning, and thoughtful comment on its importance for Muslims.

## Planning your study

A logical order is chronological. Start with the Qur'an and the Prophet's (pbuh) life (pbuh), move through the early community and the Caliphs, and study the Hadiths and the Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam alongside them.

In general terms:

- Early in the course, concentrate on the Major Themes of the Qur'an, building familiarity with the passages and what they teach, and on the history of the Qur'an.
- Next, build a clear, dated outline of the Prophet's (pbuh) life (pbuh) and the first Islamic community.
- Then study the Hadiths, their teachings and history, followed by the Rightly Guided Caliphs.
- Leave time for the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam, and finish with mixed revision across both papers.

Because Question 1 and Question 2 are compulsory on each paper, check which areas those questions draw on in recent papers and make sure they are especially secure. Begin past-paper questions on each area as it is completed, and move to full timed papers in the months before your examination. Detailed strategy for question choice and timing is on the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058?**
There are two compulsory papers, Paper 1 and Paper 2. Each lasts 1.5 hours, is marked out of 50 and is externally assessed.

**How many questions do I answer on each 2058 paper?**
Each paper prints five questions. Question 1 and Question 2 are compulsory, and you choose two of the remaining questions to answer.

**Is O Level Islamiyat 2058 examined in June and November?**
Yes. The 2026-2027 syllabus is examined in both the June and November series.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 covers the Qur'an, the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh) and the first Islamic community. Paper 2 covers the Hadiths, the Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam.

**In what language do candidates answer?**
Answers are written in English, and the syllabus is not tiered.
