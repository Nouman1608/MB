These questions cover section 4.6, Angles, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The facts are explained in the [angles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles/), and the [angles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-angles-revision-notes/) summarise them. Where a question says "give a reason", write the geometrical fact in words. Diagrams are not drawn to scale.

## Section A

**1.** Three angles at a point are 120°, 85° and x. Find x, giving a reason. **[2]**

**2.** Two angles on a straight line are 4x° and (2x + 30)°. Find x and both angles. **[3]**

**3.** Triangle PQR is isosceles with PQ = PR and angle QPR = 52°. Find angle PQR, giving reasons. **[2]**

**4.** Three angles of a quadrilateral are 85°, 97° and 110°. Find the fourth angle. **[2]**

**5.** Work out the size of an interior angle of a regular pentagon. **[2]**

**6.** AB and CD are parallel. A transversal crosses AB at P and CD at Q. A and C are on the same side of PQ, and B and D are on the other side. Angle APQ = 74°.

**(a)** Find angle PQD, giving a reason.

**(b)** Find angle PQC, giving a reason. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** DE is a straight line through A, parallel to BC. D and B are on the same side. Angle DAB = 48° and angle EAC = 67°.

**(a)** Find angle ABC and angle ACB, with reasons.

**(b)** Find angle BAC. **[4]**

**8.** Two vertically opposite angles are (4x − 15)° and (2x + 25)°. Find x and the size of each angle. **[3]**

**9.** An exterior angle of a triangle is (3x + 5)°. The two interior opposite angles are (x + 25)° and 60°. Find x and the exterior angle. **[3]**

**10.** Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°.

**(a)** Find the number of sides.

**(b)** Find the sum of the interior angles. **[3]**

**11.** The five interior angles of a pentagon are x°, (x + 10)°, (x + 20)°, (x + 30)° and (x + 40)°. Find x and the largest angle. **[3]**

**12.** ABCD is a parallelogram with angle DAB = 65°. Find angles ABC, BCD and CDA, giving a reason for each. **[3]**

**13.** In a diagram, a transversal crosses lines AB and CD. Two co-interior angles measure 118° and 62°. Show that AB is parallel to CD. **[2]**

**14.** A regular pentagon and a regular hexagon share a side. At the point where they meet outside, a third angle completes the full turn around the common vertex. Find the size of that angle. **[3]**

**15.** Three exterior angles of a quadrilateral are 80°, 95° and 100°. Find the fourth exterior angle and the fourth interior angle. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 120 + 85 + x = 360 [1]. **x = 155°** (angles at a point) [1].

**2.** 4x + 2x + 30 = 180 [1], 6x = 150 so **x = 25** [1]. The angles are **100° and 80°** [1].

**3.** Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal [1]. (180 − 52) ÷ 2 = **64°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

**4.** 360 − (85 + 97 + 110) [1] = **68°** (angle sum of a quadrilateral) [1].

**5.** Angle sum = (5 − 2) × 180 = 540° [1]. 540 ÷ 5 = **108°** [1].

**6. (a)** Angle PQD = **74°** [1] (alternate angles, AB parallel to CD) [1].

**(b)** Angle PQC = 180 − 74 = **106°** [1] (co-interior angles add up to 180°, AB parallel to CD) [1].

**7. (a)** Angle ABC = **48°** (alternate angles, DE parallel to BC) [1]. Angle ACB = **67°** (alternate angles, DE parallel to BC) [1].

**(b)** Angle BAC = 180 − 48 − 67 = **65°** (angles on a straight line) [1][1].

**8.** Vertically opposite angles are equal: 4x − 15 = 2x + 25 [1]. 2x = 40, so **x = 20** [1]. Each angle is 4(20) − 15 = **65°** [1].

**9.** Exterior angle of a triangle = sum of opposite interior angles: 3x + 5 = x + 25 + 60 [1]. 2x = 80, so **x = 40** [1]. The exterior angle is 3(40) + 5 = **125°** [1].

**10. (a)** Exterior angle = 180 − 150 = 30° [1]. Number of sides = 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1]. **(b)** (12 − 2) × 180 = **1800°** [1].

**11.** 5x + 100 = 540 [1]. x = 88 [1]. The largest angle is 88 + 40 = **128°** [1].

**12.** Angle ABC = 180 − 65 = **115°** (co-interior angles, AD parallel to BC) [1]. Angle BCD = **65°** (opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal) [1]. Angle CDA = **115°** (opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal, or co-interior angles) [1].

**13.** 118 + 62 = 180 [1]. The co-interior angles add up to 180°, so AB is parallel to CD [1].

**14.** Interior angle of a regular pentagon = 108° and of a regular hexagon = 120° [1]. Angles at a point sum to 360° [1]. 360 − 108 − 120 = **132°** [1].

**15.** Exterior angles sum to 360° [1]. 360 − (80 + 95 + 100) = **85°** [1]. The interior angle is 180 − 85 = **95°** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving no reason, or a vague reason such as "Z angles".
- Using parallel-line facts for lines that are not marked as parallel.
- Adding alternate angles to 180° or treating co-interior angles as equal.
- Using 360° for a triangle or 180° for a quadrilateral.
- Mixing up interior and exterior angles of a polygon.
