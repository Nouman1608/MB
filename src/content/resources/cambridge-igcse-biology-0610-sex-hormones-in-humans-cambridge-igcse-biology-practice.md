## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which hormone is produced by the testes? **[1]**

A: FSH

B: oestrogen

C: progesterone

D: testosterone

**2.** State **two** secondary sexual characteristics that develop in boys at puberty because of testosterone. **[2]**

**3.** Which statement describes menstruation? **[1]**

A: the release of an egg cell from an ovary

B: the breakdown and loss of the uterus lining

C: the thickening of the uterus lining

D: the fusion of a sperm nucleus with an egg nucleus

**4.** State what happens in the ovary at ovulation. **[1]**

**5.** Some secondary sexual characteristics develop in both boys and girls at puberty.

**(a)** State one such characteristic. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why these features are described as "secondary" sexual characteristics. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the gland that secretes FSH and LH. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Complete the table to describe the menstrual cycle. **[4]**

| Days | Event in the ovary | Event in the uterus lining |
|---|---|---|
| 1–5 | an egg cell starts to mature | (i) |
| 6–13 | the egg cell continues to mature | (ii) |
| about 14 | (iii) | lining is thick |
| 15–28 | (no egg cell released) | (iv) |

**8.** A student recorded the dates on which her menstruation started in 2026: 3 January, 31 January, 1 March, 29 March and 27 April.

**(a)** Calculate the mean length of her cycle, in days. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the changes in her uterus lining between 3 January and 31 January. **[3]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Complete the table to show where oestrogen and progesterone are produced. **[4]**

| Hormone | During the menstrual cycle | During late pregnancy |
|---|---|---|
| Oestrogen | (i) | (ii) |
| Progesterone | (iii) | (iv) |

**10.** *(Extended)* The table shows the concentration of progesterone in a woman's blood at different weeks of pregnancy. The units are arbitrary.

| Week of pregnancy | 4 | 8 | 12 | 20 | 36 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Progesterone | 20 | 25 | 30 | 60 | 150 |

**(a)** Describe how the progesterone concentration changes during pregnancy. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why a high progesterone concentration is important in pregnancy. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why loss of the corpus luteum in week 4 could end a pregnancy, but loss of the corpus luteum in week 20 would not. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A woman's pituitary gland stops producing LH, but her FSH and oestrogen are normal. Predict **and** explain the effects on her menstrual cycle. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Usually only one follicle develops fully in each menstrual cycle. Explain how oestrogen helps to bring this about. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]

**2.** Any two of: growth of **facial / body / pubic hair**; **voice deepens**; **muscles develop**; growth of **penis and testes**; **sperm production** starts [2].

*Examiner insight:* "Hair grows" is too vague; name where. "Gets taller" is not one of the expected answers.

**3.** **B** [1]

**4.** An **egg cell is released** from the ovary (into the oviduct) [1].

**5. (a)** Growth of **pubic hair** (or underarm hair) [1]

**(b)** They **develop at puberty**, controlled by sex hormones, rather than being present from birth like the reproductive organs [1].

**6.** **Pituitary gland** [1]

**7.** (i) lining **breaks down and is lost / menstruation** [1]; (ii) lining is **repaired and thickens** [1]; (iii) **ovulation / egg cell released** [1]; (iv) lining is **maintained / stays thick** (ready for implantation) [1].

**8. (a)** Cycle lengths: 28, 29, 28, 29 days; total 114 [1]. Mean = 114 ÷ 4 = **28.5 days** [1].

**(b)** At the start, the lining **breaks down and is lost** (menstruation, for several days) [1]. It is then **repaired and thickens** [1], and stays **thick** in the second half of the cycle until it breaks down again at the next menstruation on 31 January [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a) there are four intervals between five dates; dividing by five is a common slip.

**9.** (i) **ovaries / follicle** [1]; (ii) **placenta** [1]; (iii) **corpus luteum** in the ovary [1]; (iv) **placenta** [1].

**10. (a)** Progesterone **increases throughout** pregnancy [1], slowly at first (20 to 30 units by week 12) and then more steeply, to **150 units by week 36** [1].

**(b)** It **maintains the uterus lining**, so the pregnancy continues and there is no menstruation [1]. It **inhibits FSH and LH**, so no ovulation happens [1].

**(c)** In week 4 the **corpus luteum is the main source** of progesterone, so losing it would make progesterone fall and the lining break down [1]. By week 20 the **placenta produces progesterone**, so the level stays high without the corpus luteum [1].

**11.** There is **no LH surge**, so **no ovulation** occurs [1]. **No corpus luteum forms** [1], so **progesterone stays low** and the lining is not maintained in the usual way after day 14 [1].

**12.** As the follicle develops it secretes oestrogen, and rising oestrogen **inhibits FSH** release [1]. Without more FSH, **no further follicles are stimulated** to develop [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up menstruation and ovulation.
- Vague puberty changes such as "hair grows".
- Dividing by the number of dates instead of the number of cycles.
- (Extended) Placing FSH or LH in the ovary.
- (Extended) Forgetting that the placenta takes over hormone production in pregnancy.
- (Extended) Predicting an effect without the hormone link that explains it.
