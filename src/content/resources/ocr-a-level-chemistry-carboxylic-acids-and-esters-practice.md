These original questions cover section 6.1.3, Carboxylic acids and esters, of the OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) specification, Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The questions test every learning outcome, 6.1.3(a) to (f), from Module 6, Organic chemistry and analysis. H432 is the full A level. Questions 10 and 11 combine several outcomes with mole calculations.

## Questions

**1.** Butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH, mixes with water in all proportions.

**(a)** Describe one hydrogen bond between a butanoic acid molecule and a water molecule, stating where the lone pair and partial charges are. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why octanoic acid, CH₃(CH₂)₆COOH, is much less soluble in water than butanoic acid. **[2]**

**2.** Methanoic acid, HCOOH, reacts in aqueous conditions with each substance below.

**(a)** Calcium. Write the equation and name the salt formed. **[2]**
**(b)** Lithium carbonate. Write the equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Zinc oxide. Write the equation. **[1]**
**(d)** Potassium hydroxide. Write the ionic equation. **[1]**

**3.** Two unlabelled solutions contain propanoic acid and 3-methylphenol.

**(a)** State a reagent that identifies the propanoic acid, and what you would see with each solution. **[2]**
**(b)** 3-methylphenol is acidic. Explain why it does not give the same result as propanoic acid in (a). **[1]**

**4.** A student dissolves 2.20 g of a carboxylic acid, RCOOH, in water to make 250.0 cm³ of solution. Each 25.0 cm³ sample needs a mean of 20.0 cm³ of 0.125 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction in the titration, using RCOOH. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the acid. **[3]**
**(c)** Draw or write structural formulae for two carboxylic acids that fit your answer to (b). **[2]**

**5.** Propyl methanoate is made by heating methanoic acid and propan-1-ol with concentrated sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction, using structural formulae. **[2]**
**(b)** State the role of the sulfuric acid and explain why the yield of ester is below 100% even with no losses during purification. **[2]**

**6.** Methyl butanoate can also be made from butanoic anhydride, (CH₃CH₂CH₂CO)₂O.

**(a)** Write the equation for butanoic anhydride reacting with methanol. **[2]**
**(b)** Give two advantages of this route over heating butanoic acid with methanol and an acid catalyst. **[2]**

**7.** Pentyl propanoate, C₂H₅COOC₅H₁₁, is hydrolysed in two separate experiments.

**(a)** Write the equation for hydrolysis by heating under reflux with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the equation for hydrolysis by heating under reflux with aqueous sodium hydroxide, and name the organic products. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the reaction in (b) goes to completion but the reaction in (a) does not. **[1]**

**8.** Butanoyl chloride reacts separately with reagents A, B, C and D. The organic products are:

- A: butanoic acid
- B: methyl butanoate
- C: butanamide
- D: N-methylbutanamide

**(a)** Identify reagents A, B, C and D. **[4]**
**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction with reagent C. **[1]**
**(c)** State whether N-methylbutanamide is a primary or secondary amide, and give a reason. **[1]**

**9.** 4-methylphenyl ethanoate, CH₃COOC₆H₄CH₃, is made from 4-methylphenol, CH₃C₆H₄OH.

**(a)** Write the equation for its formation from 4-methylphenol and ethanoyl chloride. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why ethanoyl chloride is used rather than ethanoic acid with an acid catalyst. **[1]**
**(c)** State what you would see in the air above the reaction mixture. **[1]**

**10.** 2.04 g of an ester X is heated under reflux with 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ NaOH, an excess. The leftover NaOH is then neutralised by 30.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ HCl. Each mole of X reacts with one mole of NaOH.

**(a)** Write a general equation for the reaction of an ester, RCOOR', with sodium hydroxide. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of X. **[4]**
**(c)** The hydrolysis products of X are sodium methanoate and an alcohol that acidified dichromate oxidises to a ketone. Deduce the structure and name of X. **[3]**

**11.** A student makes phenyl hexanoate in two steps.

Step 1: hexanoic acid + SOCl₂ → hexanoyl chloride
Step 2: hexanoyl chloride + phenol → phenyl hexanoate

The student starts with 4.64 g of hexanoic acid. Step 1 has a yield of 80.0% and step 2 has a yield of 75.0%.

**(a)** Write the equation for step 1. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the equation for step 2. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of phenyl hexanoate, C₅H₁₁COOC₆H₅, obtained. **[4]**
**(d)** Calculate the overall percentage yield. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why the student did not make phenyl hexanoate in one step by heating hexanoic acid with phenol and concentrated sulfuric acid. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The acid's O–H hydrogen bonds to a lone pair on a water oxygen (or water's H to a lone pair on the C=O oxygen) [1]; δ+ on the H, δ− on the O with the lone pair [1]
**(b)** Octanoic acid has a much longer non-polar chain [1]; the chain cannot hydrogen bond with water, so only the –COOH end is attracted to water [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Butanoic acid is polar" is unlikely to earn credit; the answer needs the words hydrogen bond, the δ+ hydrogen and a lone pair on oxygen.

**2. (a)** 2HCOOH + Ca → (HCOO)₂Ca + H₂ [1]; **calcium methanoate** [1]
**(b)** **2HCOOH + Li₂CO₃ → 2HCOOLi + H₂O + CO₂** [1]
**(c)** **2HCOOH + ZnO → (HCOO)₂Zn + H₂O** [1]
**(d)** **HCOOH + OH⁻ → HCOO⁻ + H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check that each salt formula balances the metal's charge, (HCOO)₂Ca not HCOOCa, and keep the weak acid as HCOOH in (d) rather than H⁺.

**3. (a)** Sodium carbonate solution [1]; propanoic acid fizzes (CO₂, turns limewater milky); 3-methylphenol shows no change [1]
**(b)** Phenols are too weakly acidic to react with carbonate ions, so no CO₂ forms [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give an observation for both solutions; "fizzes" for one alone does not distinguish them.

**4. (a)** **RCOOH + NaOH → RCOONa + H₂O** [1]
**(b)** n(NaOH) = 0.0200 × 0.125 = 0.00250 mol, so n(acid) in 25.0 cm³ = 0.00250 mol [1]; n(acid) in 250.0 cm³ = 0.0250 mol [1]; Mr = 2.20 ÷ 0.0250 = **88.0** [1]
**(c)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH** (butanoic acid) [1]; **(CH₃)₂CHCOOH** (2-methylpropanoic acid) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Forgetting to scale up from 25.0 cm³ to 250.0 cm³ gives Mr = 880, which should prompt a check.

**5. (a)** HCOOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH ⇌ HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O: correct structures [1]; water as the second product and the equation balanced [1]
**(b)** Sulfuric acid is a catalyst [1]; the reaction is reversible and reaches equilibrium, so some acid and alcohol remain unreacted [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Loss on transfer" does not answer (b), since the question rules out losses; the point needed is equilibrium.

**6. (a)** (CH₃CH₂CH₂CO)₂O + CH₃OH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ + CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH: ester correct [1]; butanoic acid as the second product [1]
**(b)** Not reversible, so a higher yield [1]; no acid catalyst needed (or faster) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Water in place of butanoic acid in (a) is a common error; balance the atoms and you will see the second acyl group must leave as the acid.

**7. (a)** C₂H₅COOC₅H₁₁ + H₂O ⇌ C₂H₅COOH + C₅H₁₁OH: correct products [1]; reversible arrow and balanced [1]
**(b)** C₂H₅COOC₅H₁₁ + NaOH → C₂H₅COONa + C₅H₁₁OH [1]; **sodium propanoate** and **pentan-1-ol** [1]
**(c)** In alkali the acid is converted to its carboxylate ion, which the alcohol cannot turn back into ester, so (b) is one-way; (a) reaches equilibrium [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the organic product is the salt, not propanoic acid; naming the acid there does not match the conditions.

**8. (a)** A: **water** [1]; B: **methanol** [1]; C: **ammonia** [1]; D: **methylamine**, CH₃NH₂ [1]
**(b)** **CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂CONH₂ + NH₄Cl** [1]
**(c)** Secondary: the nitrogen carries one alkyl (methyl) group as well as the acyl group [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), one NH₃ with HCl as a product is not chemically sensible alongside excess ammonia; show two NH₃ and NH₄Cl.

**9. (a)** CH₃COCl + CH₃C₆H₄OH → CH₃COOC₆H₄CH₃ + HCl: ester correct [1]; HCl as the other product [1]
**(b)** Phenols are not readily esterified by carboxylic acids, but react readily with the more reactive acyl chloride [1]
**(c)** Misty (steamy) fumes of hydrogen chloride [1]
*Examiner insight:* With an acyl chloride the second product is HCl, never water; water here is a slip carried over from acid-catalysed esterification.

**10. (a)** **RCOOR' + NaOH → RCOONa + R'OH** [1]
**(b)** n(NaOH) added = 0.0500 × 1.00 = 0.0500 mol [1]; n(NaOH) left = n(HCl) = 0.0300 × 1.00 = 0.0300 mol [1]; n(NaOH) used = n(X) = 0.0200 mol [1]; Mr = 2.04 ÷ 0.0200 = **102** [1]
**(c)** Sodium methanoate means the acid part is HCOO– [1]; the alcohol gives a ketone, so it is secondary; HCOO– is 45, leaving 57 for C₄H₉, so the alcohol is butan-2-ol [1]; X is **HCOOCH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃, butan-2-yl methanoate** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the moles of HCl give the NaOH left over, not the NaOH that reacted; subtract before dividing into the mass.

**11. (a)** **C₅H₁₁COOH + SOCl₂ → C₅H₁₁COCl + SO₂ + HCl** [1]
**(b)** **C₅H₁₁COCl + C₆H₅OH → C₅H₁₁COOC₆H₅ + HCl** [1]
**(c)** Mr(hexanoic acid) = 116.0, so n = 4.64 ÷ 116.0 = 0.0400 mol [1]; n(hexanoyl chloride) = 0.0400 × 0.800 = 0.0320 mol [1]; n(phenyl hexanoate) = 0.0320 × 0.750 = 0.0240 mol [1]; Mr = 192.0, mass = 0.0240 × 192.0 = **4.61 g** [1]
**(d)** 0.800 × 0.750 × 100 = **60.0%** [1]
**(e)** Phenol is not readily esterified by carboxylic acids, so very little ester would form [1]
*Examiner insight:* Overall yield is the product of the step yields; 77.5% or 155% in (d) shows the yields were averaged or added.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing water as a by-product when an anhydride or acyl chloride forms an ester (questions 6 and 9).
- Giving propanoic acid rather than sodium propanoate as the product of alkaline hydrolysis.
- Using one NH₃ or one amine per acyl chloride instead of two.
- Describing solubility as "polar" without naming hydrogen bonds, the δ+ hydrogen and the oxygen lone pair.
- Treating the HCl in a back titration as the NaOH used, rather than the NaOH left over.
- Averaging step yields instead of multiplying them.
