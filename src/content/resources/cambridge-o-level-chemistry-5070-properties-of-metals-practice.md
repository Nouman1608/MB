These questions cover subtopic 9.1 Properties of metals of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028). Revise first with the [Properties of metals study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-properties-of-metals/) and the [Properties of metals revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-properties-of-metals-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain the difference between the terms malleable and ductile. **[2]**

**2.** Compare the melting points of metals and non-metals in general. **[1]**

**3.** Which equation shows the reaction of magnesium with steam?

A: Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂

B: Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂

C: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

D: Mg + H₂O → MgO + O₂

**[1]**

**4.** Name the salt formed when iron reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** observations when a piece of magnesium ribbon is added to dilute hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**6.** Name **one** metal that does not react with dilute acids, cold water or steam. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Copper is used to make saucepan bases and electrical wires.

**(a)** Describe the structure of a metal such as copper. **[2]**

**(b)** Use this structure to explain why copper is a good conductor of electricity. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why copper can be drawn into wires without breaking. **[2]**

**8.** The properties of three elements, D, E and F, are described.

- D: shiny solid; conducts electricity; melting point 650 °C; bends without breaking.
- E: yellow solid; does not conduct electricity; melting point 115 °C; crumbles when hit.
- F: black solid; conducts electricity; does not melt below 3000 °C; not malleable.

**(a)** Identify the metal. Give **two** reasons. **[3]**

**(b)** A student classifies F as a metal because it conducts electricity. Explain why this is not correct. **[2]**

**9.** Calcium and magnesium both react with water.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of calcium with cold water. **[2]**

**(b)** Magnesium reacts very slowly with cold water. Describe how magnesium can be made to react quickly with water, and name the products. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the products in (a) and (b) are different types of compound. **[1]**

**10.** A student heats small samples of magnesium, iron wool and copper foil in air.

**(a)** Describe what the student sees for magnesium and for copper. **[2]**

**(b)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction of copper with oxygen. **[1]**

**(c)** The oxide of magnesium is added to dilute hydrochloric acid. State the type of reaction and the products. **[2]**

**11.** Zinc reacts with dilute sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write the word equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation with state symbols. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe a test to identify the gas produced, including the result. **[2]**

**12.** Compare metals and non-metals in terms of **four** physical properties. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Malleable: can be hammered or pressed into shape [1]. Ductile: can be drawn out into wires [1].

**2.** Metals usually have high melting points, whereas non-metals usually have low melting points [1].

**3.** B [1].

**4.** Iron(II) chloride [1].

**5.** Any two: fizzing / bubbles; magnesium gets smaller / disappears; the solution / tube gets warm [2].

**6.** Copper / silver / gold [1].

**7. (a)** A giant lattice of positive ions [1] surrounded by a sea of delocalised electrons [1].

**(b)** The delocalised electrons can move through the structure and carry charge [1].

**(c)** The layers of ions can slide over each other [1]; the delocalised electrons still hold the ions together in their new positions, so the metal does not break [1].

**8. (a)** D [1]; any two reasons: conducts electricity; bends without breaking (malleable); shiny; fairly high melting point [2].

**(b)** Some non-metals, such as graphite, conduct electricity [1]; F is not malleable and has a very high melting point typical of a giant covalent non-metal, so the evidence overall shows a non-metal [1].

**9. (a)** Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(b)** Heat magnesium in steam [1]; magnesium oxide [1] and hydrogen [1].

**(c)** Cold water forms a hydroxide, whereas steam forms an oxide [1].
*Marking note:* in (b), "heat it" alone is not enough; the answer must say the magnesium reacts with **steam**. The usual school arrangement passes steam over heated magnesium ribbon, and the hydrogen produced can be burned at the end of the tube.

**10. (a)** Magnesium burns with a bright white flame, forming a white powder [1]. Copper does not burn; its surface turns black [1].

**(b)** 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO [1].

**(c)** Neutralisation [1]; magnesium chloride and water [1].

**11. (a)** zinc + sulfuric acid → zinc sulfate + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Zn(s) + H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g): correct balanced equation [1]; state symbols [1].

**(c)** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]; it burns with a squeaky pop [1].

**12.** Four comparisons, each naming both metals and non-metals [4]:
- Metals are good conductors of electricity; non-metals are poor conductors (except graphite) [1].
- Metals are good conductors of heat; non-metals are poor conductors [1].
- Metals are malleable and ductile; solid non-metals are brittle [1].
- Metals usually have high melting / boiling points; non-metals usually low [1].
- Metals are shiny; non-metals are usually dull [1].
*Marking note:* a statement about metals only, with no reference to non-metals, does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "salt and water" for metal + acid.
- Writing magnesium hydroxide as the product with steam.
- Leaving out "delocalised" in conductivity explanations.
- Describing the products ("magnesium oxide forms") when asked for observations; say "white powder".
- One-sided comparisons that mention only metals.
