## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Variation | Differences in traits between individuals; no two are identical in every trait |
| Species (morphological) | Group of organisms sharing traits; Linnaeus' original idea |
| Species (biological) | Group that can breed and produce fertile offspring |
| Genus | First part of a binomial name; capital letter |
| Speciation | Division of one species into two or several, usually gradual |
| Karyotyping | Preparing a karyogram of an organism's chromosomes |
| Karyogram | Chromosomes of one cell arranged in homologous pairs |
| Genome | All the genetic information of an organism |
| SNP | Single-nucleotide polymorphism: a base that differs between individuals at one position |
| Whole genome sequencing | Finding the base order of an entire genome |
| Dichotomous key (HL only) | Numbered pairs of contrasting statements leading to identification |
| eDNA (HL only) | DNA shed by organisms into water, soil or air |

## Names and species concepts (A3.1.1 to A3.1.5)

- No two individuals are identical; this variation characterises life and underlies naming and classifying.
- Binomial: *Genus species*. Genus capital, species lower case, italics in print, underlined by hand. Same genus means similar traits.
- Biological species concept: breed together and produce fertile offspring.
- Challenges: asexual organisms, fossils, populations that never meet, fertile hybrids between clearly different forms.
- Other definitions exist (morphological, ecological, shared ancestry and DNA sequence).
- Speciation is gradual, so a boundary between two diverging populations may be an arbitrary decision.

### Must-know distinctions

| Morphological concept | Biological concept |
|---|---|
| Shared observable traits | Interbreeding with fertile offspring |
| Works for fossils and preserved specimens | Needs living, sexually reproducing organisms |
| Risk: variation within a species, look-alike species | Risk: untestable where populations are isolated |

Method to answer "is it one species or two?": (1) state the definition you apply; (2) apply it to the evidence given; (3) note a limit of that definition; (4) conclude.

## Chromosomes and karyograms (A3.1.6 to A3.1.7)

- Humans 2n = 46 (23 pairs); chimpanzees 2n = 48 (24 pairs). Other values are not required.
- Diploid numbers are even because chromosomes exist as homologous pairs.
- Classify chromosomes by banding pattern, length and centromere position.
- Magnification: actual size = image size ÷ magnification. Check units (1 mm = 1000 µm).

### Method: pairing chromosomes in a karyogram

1. Find chromosomes of similar length.
2. Check the centromere position.
3. Match the banding pattern.
4. Arrange in pairs, longest first; sex chromosomes last.
5. Count: extra or missing chromosomes show as a pair with three or one.

### Chromosome 2 evidence

- Banding of human chromosome 2 matches two ape chromosomes end to end.
- Telomere-type sequence in the middle of chromosome 2.
- A remnant of a second centromere on the long arm.
- 48 − 1 fusion = 46.
- Testable: it makes predictions evidence could contradict. Non-testable: gives no prediction that data could refute, for example a claim about purpose.

*Worked reminder.* Chromosome 21 mm long at ×3000: 21 ÷ 3000 = 0.007 mm = 7.0 µm.

## Genomes (A3.1.8 to A3.1.11)

| Point | Content |
|---|---|
| Within a species | Most of the genome is shared; SNPs give diversity |
| Between species | Size and base sequence differ; much more than within a species |
| Size | Total amount of DNA, often in Mb |
| Size and complexity | No close match: large genomes in some plants, small genomes in some complex animals |
| Database task | Extract sizes for taxonomic groups, then compare with complexity |

*Worked reminder.* Roughly 1 base in 1000 differs between two people. Genome 3100 Mb: 3.1 × 10⁹ × 0.001 = 3.1 × 10⁶ differences.

*Worked reminder.* Comparing sizes: 3100 Mb ÷ 140 Mb = 22.1 times larger.

### Whole genome sequencing

| Trend | Detail |
|---|---|
| Speed | Much faster than the first human genome, which took more than a decade |
| Cost | Fallen enormously; falls are best stated as a ratio |
| Current use | Research into evolutionary relationships |
| Potential future use | Personalized medicine |

## HL only (A3.1.12 to A3.1.15)

- **A3.1.12:** The biological species concept fails for asexual species (no breeding) and bacteria (horizontal gene transfer moves genes across species).
- **A3.1.13:** Different chromosome numbers make fertile hybrids unlikely. Mule: 32 + 31 = 63, odd, pairing fails in meiosis.
- **A3.1.14:** Dichotomous key. Use clear, constant features; two contrasting statements per step; n species need n − 1 pairs; test it on someone else.
- **A3.1.15:** Barcode = short standard DNA region. eDNA method: sample, extract, amplify by PCR, sequence, match to a reference database. Fast and non-invasive. Limits: incomplete databases, DNA that has drifted from where the organism lives.

## Writing good answers on this unit

### Evaluating evidence for a hypothesis (A3.1.7)

For a command such as "evaluate the evidence", give a short list of evidence, say what each piece predicts, then weigh it.
- Evidence that supports: matching bands, telomere-like sequence in the middle of chromosome 2, a second centromere remnant.
- Weighing: independent lines of evidence agreeing make the hypothesis stronger.
- Limit: a past event cannot be observed, so the conclusion is an inference from the evidence, not a direct observation.
- Close with a clear judgement: the evidence strongly supports fusion.

### Comparing genome data (A3.1.10)

1. Read the unit and the column headings.
2. Put sizes in the same unit before dividing.
3. State the comparison as a ratio ("22 times larger") or a difference.
4. Compare with what you know of the organism's complexity.
5. Conclude carefully: a few data points show a pattern or an exception; they do not prove a rule.

### Quick HL reminders

*Dichotomous key check.* Five species need four pairs of statements. Each ending names exactly one species, and no statement uses a feature that changes with age.

*eDNA steps in order.* Collect sample, filter or extract, amplify barcode region by PCR, sequence, compare with reference database, list species present.

*Why hybrids are infertile.* Different chromosome numbers in the two parents give an unpaired set in the hybrid. Meiosis needs homologous pairs, so balanced gametes are unlikely.

### Phrases that earn credit

- "Fertile offspring" in every definition of the biological species concept.
- "Gradual" when describing speciation, with a reason: populations slowly become more different.
- "Banding pattern, length, centromere position" as the three karyogram criteria.
- "Variation within a species is small compared with variation between species."

## Quick self-test

1. Write the correct binomial name for a lion given the genus Panthera and species leo.
2. State the definition of a species in the biological species concept.
3. Give the diploid number of a chimpanzee and the number of homologous pairs.
4. Name three features used to classify chromosomes in a karyogram.
5. A chromosome is 18 mm long on a micrograph at ×1500. Calculate its actual length in µm.
6. Give one piece of evidence that human chromosome 2 formed by fusion.
7. Explain why variation between species is greater than within a species.
8. A genome of 3100 Mb has 0.1% SNP difference between two people. How many bases differ?
9. State one current and one potential future use of whole genome sequencing.
10. (HL only) A hybrid has 2n = 39. Explain why it is likely to be infertile.

### Answers

1. *Panthera leo* (genus capital, species lower case, italics).
2. A group of organisms that can breed together and produce fertile offspring.
3. 48 chromosomes, 24 pairs.
4. Any three of: banding pattern, length, centromere position.
5. 18 ÷ 1500 = 0.012 mm = **12 µm**.
6. Any one: banding matches two ape chromosomes end to end; telomere sequences in the middle; a remnant second centromere.
7. Species have accumulated separate changes over time, with no gene exchange to keep them alike; members of one species share a gene pool and so differ little.
8. 3.1 × 10⁹ × 0.001 = **3.1 × 10⁶** bases.
9. Current: research into evolutionary relationships. Future: personalized medicine.
10. 39 is odd, so chromosomes cannot all pair in meiosis, so viable gametes are unlikely.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing species names with a capital, or without italics or underlining.
- Defining a species without the word fertile.
- Saying a diploid number could be odd.
- Forgetting to convert mm to µm in magnification questions.
- Quoting a genome size with no unit, or mixing Mb and Gb in one comparison.
- Saying "chromosome 2 proves" the fusion, rather than evaluating evidence for a hypothesis.
- Answering that bigger genomes mean more complex organisms.
- Describing eDNA as "finding the animal" rather than detecting its DNA in a sample.
- Mixing up the genus and the species when explaining the name.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the biological species concept?**
In the biological species concept, a species is a group of organisms that can breed together and produce fertile offspring. Leaving out the word fertile is a common way to lose marks.

**How are chromosomes classified in a karyogram?**
Chromosomes are classified by banding pattern, length and centromere position, then arranged in homologous pairs with the longest first and the sex chromosomes last.

**What is the evidence that human chromosome 2 formed by fusion?**
The banding of human chromosome 2 matches two ape chromosomes end to end, telomere-type sequences lie in its middle, and a remnant of a second centromere lies on its long arm.

**What is a single-nucleotide polymorphism?**
A single-nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) is one base position where individuals of a species have different nucleotides; most of the genome is shared, so SNPs show both unity and diversity within a species.
