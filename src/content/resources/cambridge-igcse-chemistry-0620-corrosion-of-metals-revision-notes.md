## Rusting

- **Rusting** = corrosion of **iron or steel** only.
- Needs **water AND oxygen**.
- Product: **hydrated iron(III) oxide** (orange-brown, flaky).
- Word equation: iron + water + oxygen → hydrated iron(III) oxide.
- Rusting is **oxidation** of iron.
- Rust flakes off, exposing fresh iron, so rusting continues.

## The three-tube investigation

| Tube | Contents | Present | Rust? |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | nail + tap water, open | water + oxygen | yes |
| B | nail + **boiled** water + **oil** layer | water only | no |
| C | nail + **anhydrous calcium chloride**, **stoppered** | oxygen only (dry air) | no |

- Boiling → removes dissolved air.
- Oil layer → stops air dissolving back in.
- Calcium chloride → drying agent, absorbs water vapour.
- Stopper → keeps moist air out.

## Barrier methods (Core)

**How they work:** a layer that **excludes oxygen or water** from the iron surface.

| Method | Typical use | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Painting | car bodies, bridges, gates | covers large areas; durable on fixed surfaces |
| Greasing | chains, hinges, moving parts | paint would rub off; grease also lubricates |
| Coating with plastic | wire fences, dish racks | tough, waterproof, flexible |

**Weakness:** once scratched or worn, oxygen and water reach the iron and it rusts.

## Galvanising *(Extended)*

- Coating iron or steel with **zinc**.
- **Barrier** while intact.
- **Sacrificial protection** when scratched: the iron still does not rust.

## Sacrificial protection *(Extended)*

Two parts to a full explanation:

1. **Reactivity:** zinc is **more reactive** than iron.
2. **Electron loss:** zinc **loses electrons** more readily, Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻, so zinc is oxidised **instead of** iron. Iron does not lose electrons, so it does not rust.

Also used: **blocks** of zinc or magnesium bolted to ship hulls and buried pipes; replaced when corroded away.

**Which metals can protect iron sacrificially?** Only metals **above iron** in the reactivity series, such as zinc and magnesium. Copper (below iron) cannot.

## Answer frames

**"Suggest a barrier method for…" (2 marks):** name the method, then link it to how the object is used. "Grease, because the chain moves and paint would rub off."

**"Explain why the iron in tube X did not rust" (2 marks):** name what is missing, then say why it is missing. "There is no water, because the calcium chloride absorbs water vapour."

**"Explain why scratched galvanised iron does not rust" (3 marks, Extended):**

1. zinc is more reactive than iron;
2. zinc loses electrons more readily (Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻);
3. so zinc is oxidised instead of iron, and the iron does not rust.

**"Compare painting with galvanising" (Extended):** both are barriers; only galvanising still protects once scratched, because of sacrificial protection.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Corrosion | a metal being gradually destroyed by reacting with substances around it |
| Rusting | the corrosion of iron and steel, needing water and oxygen |
| Rust | hydrated iron(III) oxide |
| Barrier method | a coating that excludes oxygen or water from the metal |
| Galvanising *(Extended)* | coating iron or steel with zinc |
| Sacrificial protection *(Extended)* | protecting a metal by attaching a more reactive metal that corrodes instead |
| Oxidation | loss of electrons (gain of oxygen) |
| Drying agent | a substance that absorbs water, such as anhydrous calcium chloride |

## Exam traps

- **Both conditions.** "Water" alone or "air" alone is incomplete. "Oxygen" is better than "air".
- **Full name of rust:** hydrated iron(III) oxide.
- **Only iron and steel rust.** Aluminium and copper corrode but do not rust.
- **Barrier answers must say what is excluded:** oxygen and/or water.
- *(Extended)* **Galvanising is more than a barrier.** Questions about a scratched galvanised object want sacrificial protection.
- *(Extended)* **Mention electrons.** "Zinc is more reactive" alone does not get full marks.
- *(Extended)* **Zinc is oxidised, iron is not.** Do not say iron is oxidised "more slowly".

## Self-test

1. State the **two** substances that must be present for iron to rust.
2. Give the chemical name of rust.
3. In the rusting investigation, why is the water boiled?
4. Why is a layer of oil added on top of the boiled water?
5. What is the purpose of anhydrous calcium chloride in the dry-air tube?
6. Name **three** barrier methods.
7. Explain how a coat of paint prevents rusting.
8. Suggest why a bicycle chain is greased rather than painted.
9. *(Extended)* Name the metal used to galvanise steel.
10. *(Extended)* A galvanised nail is scratched through to the iron. Explain why it does not rust.
11. *(Extended)* Write the equation for zinc losing electrons.
12. *(Extended)* Explain why copper cannot be used for sacrificial protection of iron.

**Answers:**

1. Water and oxygen.
2. Hydrated iron(III) oxide.
3. To remove dissolved air (oxygen).
4. To stop air dissolving back into the water.
5. It is a drying agent; it absorbs water vapour so the air is dry.
6. Painting; greasing; coating with plastic.
7. It forms a layer that excludes oxygen and water from the iron surface.
8. The chain moves, so paint would crack or rub off; grease stays on moving parts and also lubricates them.
9. Zinc.
10. Zinc is more reactive than iron, so it loses electrons in preference to iron; the zinc is oxidised and the iron is not.
11. Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻.
12. Copper is less reactive than iron, so it does not lose electrons in preference to iron; the iron would still be oxidised.
