These notes condense section 3.8.2, Electric field strength, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), in Section 3.8 Electric fields and capacitance (International A-level only). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [electric field strength study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-field-strength-oxfordaqa/); test yourself with the [electric field strength practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-field-strength-oxfordaqa-practice/). The force between charges is in the [Coulomb's law revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-coulombs-law-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

Constants: 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻²; e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg.

## Equations

| Equation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| E = F/Q | Any field | Definition; F on a positive test charge |
| E = V/d | Uniform field between parallel plates | Not near the edges |
| Fd = QΔV | Derivation of E = V/d | Work done moving Q between plates |
| E = Q/(4πε₀r²) | Radial field of a point charge or sphere | r from the centre |
| E₂ = E₁(r₁/r₂)² | Radial field at a new distance | From the inverse-square law |

Units: N C⁻¹ = V m⁻¹ (V m⁻¹ = J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N m C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N C⁻¹).

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Electric field | A region in which a charge experiences a force |
| Electric field strength | Force per unit positive charge at a point: E = F/Q |
| Field line | Line showing the direction of the force on a positive charge |
| Uniform field | Same strength and direction everywhere: parallel, equally spaced lines |
| Radial field | Lines along radii of a point charge; strength falls as 1/r² |
| Neutral point | Point where the resultant field is zero |
| Test charge | Charge small enough not to disturb the field it measures |

## Condensed recall

- E is a vector. Away from positive charges, towards negative ones.
- Force on a negative charge is opposite to E.
- Fields from several charges add as vectors. Draw an arrow for each before adding.
- Between unlike charges both fields point towards the negative charge: add the magnitudes.
- Between like charges the fields oppose: a neutral point lies nearer the smaller charge.
- Field lines meet conductors at right angles. Edge fields of plates bulge outwards and are weaker.
- Derivation: W = Fd and W = QΔV; with F = QE, QEd = QΔV, so E = ΔV/d.

## Charged particle entering at right angles

| Direction | Force | Motion |
|---|---|---|
| Along the plates | None | Constant velocity v; time t = L/v |
| Across the plates | QE, constant | Uniform acceleration a = QE/m from zero |

- Sideways displacement y = ½at²; sideways speed on leaving at.
- Angle of the beam on leaving: tan⁻¹(at/v).
- Path between the plates: parabola, y = (QE/2mv²)x². Straight line after.
- Energy gained = charge × p.d. actually crossed (E × y), not the full plate p.d. unless it crosses the whole gap.
- Weight is negligible for subatomic particles: the electric force is many orders of magnitude larger.
- Same charge and speed: deflection ∝ 1/m.

## Field-line patterns to sketch

- Isolated positive charge: radial lines outwards. Negative: radial lines inwards.
- Parallel plates: parallel, equally spaced lines from + to −, curving at the edges.
- Two equal positive charges: lines bend away from each other; neutral point midway.
- Positive and negative pair: lines curve from + to −.

## Worked reminders

- Plates 2.5 cm apart at 400 V: E = 400/0.025 = **1.6 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹**.
- −15 nC at 0.30 m: E = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 15 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.30² = **1.5 × 10³ N C⁻¹**, towards the charge.
- Doubling r from 0.30 m to 0.60 m divides E by 4: **375 N C⁻¹**.

## Exam traps

- Subtracting fields from unlike charges at a point between them.
- Forgetting that r is squared, or using the distance to a sphere's surface.
- Using E = V/d for a point charge.
- Using the full gap as the allowed deflection when the particle enters midway.
- Drawing field lines without arrows, or crossing.
- Defining E as "force per charge" without "unit positive".

## Self-test

1. Define electric field strength.
2. Show that 1 V m⁻¹ = 1 N C⁻¹.
3. Find E between plates 4.0 mm apart with 60 V across them.
4. What force acts on an electron in a field of 2.0 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹?
5. E is 800 N C⁻¹ at 0.10 m from a point charge. Find E at 0.40 m.
6. +3.0 nC and −3.0 nC are 0.10 m apart. Find E at the midpoint.
7. +9.0 nC and +1.0 nC are 0.20 m apart. Where is the neutral point?
8. Why is the path of an electron between parallel plates a parabola?
9. An electron spends 3.0 ns between plates where its acceleration is 1.0 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻². Find its sideways displacement.
10. A proton and an electron enter the same plates at the same speed. Compare their deflections.
11. A plate p.d. is kept constant while the plate separation is halved. What happens to E?
12. Why may field lines between plates not be used to apply E = V/d near the edges?

### Answers

1. The force per unit positive charge on a small test charge placed at that point.
2. V m⁻¹ = (J C⁻¹) m⁻¹ = (N m C⁻¹) m⁻¹ = **N C⁻¹**.
3. 60/0.0040 = **1.5 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹**.
4. F = eE = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁴ = **3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ N**, opposite to the field.
5. 800 × (0.10/0.40)² = **50 N C⁻¹**.
6. Each field is 8.99 × 10⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.050² = 1.08 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹, both towards the negative charge: **2.2 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹**.
7. 3/x = 1/(0.20 − x), so **0.15 m** from the 9.0 nC charge.
8. Constant velocity along the plates and constant acceleration across them, as for a horizontally launched projectile.
9. y = ½ × 1.0 × 10¹⁵ × (3.0 × 10⁻⁹)² = **4.5 × 10⁻³ m**.
10. Same force, but the proton's mass is about 1840 times larger, so its deflection is about **1840 times smaller** and in the opposite direction.
11. E = V/d, so E **doubles**.
12. The lines curve and spread out at the edges, so the field there is weaker and not uniform.
