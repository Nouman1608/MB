## Section A

**1.** Which statement about natural selection is correct?

A: individuals change their features to suit the environment

B: better-adapted individuals are more likely to reproduce and pass on their alleles

C: the environment causes mutations that help survival

D: all offspring survive to adulthood

**[1]**

**2.** What is MRSA?

A: a virus that infects red blood cells

B: a bacterium that is resistant to several antibiotics

C: a type of antibiotic

D: a method of selective breeding

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by artificial selection. **[2]**

**4.** State **one** reason why many offspring are produced by most species. **[1]**

**5.** State **one** economically important animal or plant produced by selective breeding and the desirable feature selected. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A population of rabbits lives in a field. Some rabbits have dark fur and some have pale fur. The field has dark soil and foxes hunt the rabbits.

**(a)** Explain why there is variation in the colour of the rabbits. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how natural selection could change the proportion of dark rabbits in the population over many generations. **[5]**

**7.** A population of 800 beetles contains 600 green beetles and 200 brown beetles. Birds eat 20 per cent of the green beetles and 50 per cent of the brown beetles in one season.

**(a)** Calculate the number of green beetles and brown beetles that survive. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the surviving beetles that are green. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how this could lead to evolution in the population. **[2]**

**8.** A hospital patient has an infection caused by bacteria. The first antibiotic kills most of the bacteria but the infection returns, and a second course of the same antibiotic has no effect.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of natural selection, why the second course has no effect. **[4]**

**(b)** State **one** way in which doctors can reduce the development of resistant bacteria. **[1]**

**(c)** A single resistant bacterium divides every 20 minutes. Calculate the number of resistant bacteria after 3 hours, assuming no deaths. **[2]**

**9.** A farmer wants to produce wheat with a higher yield.

**(a)** Describe the steps that the farmer would take, using selective breeding. **[4]**

**(b)** The mean yield of the wheat was 5.0 tonnes per hectare. After several generations it was 6.5 tonnes per hectare. Calculate the percentage increase. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** disadvantage of growing only the improved variety. **[1]**

**10.** Compare natural selection with artificial selection, giving **two** differences. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why selective breeding cannot work if all the individuals in a population are genetically identical. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Selection by humans [1] of animals or plants with desirable features to breed from [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one of: many do not survive; to ensure some survive and reproduce; there will be competition so that natural selection can occur [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any suitable example with its feature, for example dairy cattle [1] with high milk yield [1]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** Variation is caused by differences in the alleles inherited (genes) [1].

**(b)** Rabbits produce many offspring, and there is competition and predation [1]. Dark rabbits are harder to see on dark soil, so foxes catch more pale rabbits [1]. More dark rabbits survive to reproduce [1] and pass on the allele for dark fur [1]. Over many generations, the proportion of dark rabbits rises [1]. **[6]**

**7.**

**(a)** Green: 600 x 0.8 = 480 [1]. Brown: 200 x 0.5 = 100 [1].

**(b)** Total = 580 [1]. Green percentage = 480 divided by 580 x 100 = 82.8 per cent [1].

**(c)** The percentage of green beetles increased (from 75 per cent) [1], and the survivors pass on their alleles, so over many generations the inherited features of the population change [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** There was variation, and some bacteria were resistant because of a mutation [1]. The antibiotic killed the non-resistant bacteria [1]. The resistant bacteria survived and reproduced [1] and passed on the resistance allele, so the population was now resistant [1].

**(b)** Any one of: prescribe antibiotics only when needed; complete the full course [1].

**(c)** 3 hours = 180 minutes, so 9 divisions [1]; 2 to the power 9 = 512 bacteria [1]. **[7]**

**9.**

**(a)** Select plants with high yield [1]. Cross them together [1]. Select the offspring with the highest yield [1]. Repeat over many generations [1].

**(b)** (6.5 - 5.0) divided by 5.0 x 100 [1] = 30 per cent [1].

**(c)** All the plants are similar, so a new disease could affect all of them (little variation) [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Any two of: in natural selection the environment selects, in artificial selection humans select [1]; natural selection selects features that help survival, artificial selection selects features useful to humans [1]; natural selection is generally slower [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Selective breeding needs differences between individuals to choose from [1]. If all are identical, there is no variation, so no individuals with better features can be selected [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying individuals adapt during their lifetime and pass this on.
- Leaving out variation or passing on alleles in a natural selection answer.
- Writing that the antibiotic causes the mutation.
- Omitting repetition over many generations in a selective breeding answer.
- Not showing the starting value as the divisor in a percentage increase.
