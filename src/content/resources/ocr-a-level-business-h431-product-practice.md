## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by a unique selling point (USP). **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by a brand. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** reason why product differentiation may help a business to charge a higher price. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**4.** Moorfield Snacks sells four products. High market growth is taken as 8% or more, and a relative market share of 1.0 or more is taken as high.

| Product | Market growth | Own share | Largest rival's share | Sales (£m) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Oat Bars | 15% | 24% | 12% | 3.0 |
| Cheese Straws | 3% | 36% | 18% | 5.5 |
| Seed Bites | 11% | 6% | 30% | 1.0 |
| Rice Crisps | −4% | 5% | 25% | 0.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the relative market share of Seed Bites. **[2]**

**(b)** Classify each of the four products on the Boston matrix. **[4]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of total sales that comes from the cash cow. **[2]**

**(d)** Recommend and justify a product mix for Moorfield Snacks. **[9]**

**5.** Peak Packs sells a rucksack for £80. The unit cost is £52, made up as follows: frame £20, waterproof cover £6, laptop pocket £9, solar charger £11, reflective strips £6. A survey found the following shares of customers rating each feature important: frame 90%, cover 70%, laptop pocket 35%, solar charger 8%, strips 45%.

**(a)** Calculate the contribution per rucksack and the margin as a percentage of the price. **[2]**

**(b)** Peak Packs removes the solar charger. Calculate the new unit cost and the new margin. **[3]**

**(c)** It sells 10,000 rucksacks a year, and removing the charger loses 4% of sales. Calculate the total contribution before and after the change. **[4]**

**(d)** Evaluate whether Peak Packs should remove the solar charger. **[6]**

**6.** Aurora Skincare has spent six years building a brand known for natural ingredients. A supermarket has offered to make a cheaper copy of its best-selling cream under the supermarket's own label. Evaluate the importance of the Aurora brand to the business. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A feature or benefit of a product [1] that sets it apart from rivals and gives customers a reason to choose it [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A name, symbol, design or reputation [1] that identifies a product and distinguishes it from competing products [1]. **[2]**

**3.** If customers see a product as different in quality, design or image [1], they may be willing to pay more for it because they cannot get the same thing from rivals [1], so demand is less sensitive to price and the business can raise price without losing many sales [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Relative share = 6 ÷ 30 [1] = **0.2** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Oat Bars: relative share 24 ÷ 12 = 2.0, growth 15%: **star** [1]. Cheese Straws: 36 ÷ 18 = 2.0, growth 3%: **cash cow** [1]. Seed Bites: 0.2, growth 11%: **question mark** [1]. Rice Crisps: 5 ÷ 25 = 0.2, growth −4%: **dog** [1]. **[4]**

**(c)** Total sales = 3.0 + 5.5 + 1.0 + 0.5 = £10.0 million [1]; 5.5 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = **55%** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** Keep and invest in Oat Bars, the star, to protect its leading share in a fast-growing market [1]. Keep Cheese Straws as the cash cow, which provides 55% of sales, and use its cash to fund Oat Bars and Seed Bites [1]. Seed Bites is a question mark: a share of 6% against a rival at 30% is low, but the market is growing at 11%, so a time-limited trial with more promotion and a target (such as a share of 12% in two years) is justified [2]. Rice Crisps is a dog with falling sales and a small share, so it should probably be dropped, unless it brings customers who buy other products [1]. This would reduce the range from four lines to three, cutting production and marketing costs and freeing money for growth [1].
Risks: 85% of sales come from two products, so Moorfield depends on them; the matrix uses only two measures and the market definition affects share; dropping a product may reduce shelf space with retailers [2]. Judgement: the mix is sensible provided the question mark is tested against a clear target and the dog is withdrawn only after checking its links with other products [1]. **[9]**

**5. (a)** £80 − £52 = **£28** [1]; 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = **35%** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** New unit cost = 52 − 11 = **£41** [1]. New contribution = 80 − 41 = £39 [1]; margin = 39 ÷ 80 × 100 = **48.75%** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** Before: 10,000 × £28 = **£280,000** [1]. After: sales 10,000 × 0.96 = 9,600 [1]; 9,600 × £39 [1] = **£374,400** [1]. **[4]**

**(d)** For: only 8% of surveyed customers value the charger, but it adds £11 to the cost [1]; total contribution rises by £94,400 (374,400 − 280,000) even after losing 4% of sales [1]; the saved cost could be used on features customers value, such as the frame [1].
Against: the survey shows stated opinions, not what buyers do, and the 4% sales loss is an estimate [1]; the charger may help Peak Packs to stand out from rivals as a USP, and without it the product may look ordinary [1].
Judgement: removing it is probably sensible, but Peak Packs should check that the loss of sales is not larger than 4%, for example by selling it as an optional extra to the minority who want it [1]. **[6]**

**6.** Importance: the brand lets Aurora charge a premium over own-label creams [1]; customers who trust natural ingredients buy again, which lowers the cost of winning sales [1]; the brand makes it easier to launch new products [1]; it protects the business against price competition, as loyal customers will not move for a small saving [1].
Limits: brand value depends on quality and reputation, and a supermarket copy of the cream could draw price-conscious customers [1]; the brand takes years to build but can be harmed quickly by one complaint or scandal [1]; a business with a strong brand but poor distribution may still lose sales [1].
Judgement: the brand is very important to Aurora because it is the main reason customers pay more than for an own-label product [1], and it should be protected by keeping quality consistent and by resisting the temptation to make own-label products that dilute its image, unless the volume is large and the products are clearly separated [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using share instead of relative share on the Boston matrix.
- Recommending a mix without a figure from the case.
- Removing a feature without checking the effect on sales.
- Describing the benefits of a brand without a limit or a judgement.
