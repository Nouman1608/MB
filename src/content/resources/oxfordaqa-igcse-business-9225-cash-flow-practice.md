All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Teal Textiles.

> **Case study: Teal Textiles**
>
> Teal Textiles weaves fabric for clothing makers, who are given 60 days
> to pay. The owner has produced a cash flow forecast for the next four
> months. Figures are in dollars.
>
> | Month | Opening balance | Cash inflows | Cash outflows | Net cash flow | Closing balance |
> |---|---|---|---|---|---|
> | May | 4,000 | 20,000 | 24,000 | (a) | (b) |
> | June | (c) | 18,000 | 22,500 | (d) | (e) |
> | July | (f) | 30,000 | 21,000 | (g) | (h) |
> | August | (i) | 25,000 | 20,000 | (j) | (k) |
>
> In June, Teal Textiles must also pay $3,000 for a service of its
> weaving machines, which is included in the cash outflows. The owner
> says that "the business is making a profit, so there is no problem".
> The bank has offered an overdraft at 16% a year.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a cash outflow? **[1]**
- A. Payment from a customer
- B. Payment of wages
- C. A loan received
- D. Sale of an old machine

**2.** Which **one** of the following correctly describes the closing balance? **[1]**
- A. Opening balance minus net cash flow
- B. Opening balance plus net cash flow
- C. Inflows plus outflows
- D. Inflows divided by outflows

**3.** Define the term "cash flow forecast". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** reasons why a business may suffer cash flow problems. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** difference between cash and profit. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the net cash flow and closing balance for May, shown as (a) and (b). **[2]**

**7.** Complete the figures for June, shown as (c), (d) and (e). **[3]**

**8.** Complete the figures for July, shown as (f), (g) and (h), and for August, shown as (i), (j) and (k). **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the interest on an overdraft equal to the shortage in June, if it is needed for 1 month at 16% a year. **[2]**

**10.** Explain **two** reasons why a negative cash balance in June may be a problem for Teal Textiles, even though the business may be profitable. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse **two** ways in which Teal Textiles could improve its cash flow. **[6]**

**12.** Evaluate whether an overdraft is the best way for Teal Textiles to deal with its cash flow problem in June. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A prediction of the cash that will flow into and out of a business [1] over a future period, usually month by month [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: customers paying late [1]; sales lower than expected [1]; costs higher than expected [1]; large one-off payments [1]; poor stock control [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Cash is the money available now, whereas profit is revenue minus costs [1]; a sale on credit counts towards profit when it is made, but the cash arrives only when the customer pays [1]. **[2]**

**6.** (a) Net cash flow = 20,000 - 24,000 = **-4,000** [1]; (b) closing balance = 4,000 - 4,000 = **0** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** (c) Opening balance = **0** [1]; (d) net cash flow = 18,000 - 22,500 = **-4,500** [1]; (e) closing balance = 0 - 4,500 = **-4,500** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** July: (f) **-4,500** [1]; (g) 30,000 - 21,000 = **9,000** [1]; (h) -4,500 + 9,000 = **4,500** [1]. August: (i) 4,500; (j) 25,000 - 20,000 = 5,000; (k) 4,500 + 5,000 = **9,500** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The overdraft needed is $4,500 [1]; interest = $4,500 x 16% x 1/12 = **$60** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** **Unable to pay bills:** with a balance of -$4,500 Teal has no cash to pay wages or suppliers [1], so staff may be unhappy or suppliers may refuse to deliver yarn [1].
**Profit does not equal cash:** the clothing makers have 60 days to pay, so the sales made earlier have not yet brought in cash [1], and the business may be profitable on paper but have to borrow to survive [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Increase inflows:** Teal could ask customers to pay within 30 days instead of 60 [1], or offer a small discount for prompt payment, which would bring cash in earlier [1].
**Reduce or delay outflows:** it could delay the $3,000 machine service until July, when the balance is positive [1], or negotiate longer credit from its suppliers, so that payments fall in a later month [1].
**Effect:** either action would reduce or remove the -$4,500 shortage [1], so Teal would need a smaller overdraft or none [1]. **[6]**

**12.** **For an overdraft:** the shortage is only $4,500 and temporary, as July's net cash flow is +$9,000 [1]; the interest of $60 for a month is small [1]; an overdraft is quick and flexible [1].
**Against:** the bank can withdraw it at short notice, and the interest rate of 16% is high if the overdraft lasts longer than planned [1]; if sales do not rise in July the shortage could continue and increase costs [1]; solving the cause, such as slow payment by customers, would help more than borrowing [1].
**Alternatives:** moving the $3,000 service to July would cut the June shortage to $1,500 [1], at no cost.
**Judgement:** an overdraft is suitable because the shortage is small and short-lived [1], but Teal should also consider moving the service payment and shortening customers' payment terms to prevent the problem returning [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the opening balance of the wrong month, instead of carrying forward the closing balance.
- Writing the net cash flow as outflows minus inflows.
- Treating a negative balance as a loss rather than a cash shortage.
- Forgetting to take only one month's interest on the overdraft.
- Recommending a solution without a drawback or without relating it to the size and cause of the shortage.
