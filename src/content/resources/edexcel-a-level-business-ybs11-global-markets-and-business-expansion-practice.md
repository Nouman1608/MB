## Sources

> **Source 1: Marlowe Textiles**
>
> Marlowe makes 200,000 jackets a year in its home country, where saturated
> demand and strong competition have squeezed profit. It is considering
> moving production to Vantara.
>
> | Per jacket | Home country | Vantara |
> |---|---|---|
> | Wage per hour | $15 | $5 |
> | Hours of labour needed | 2 | 3 |
> | Materials | $22 | $22 |
> | Transport to customers | $3 | $9 |
>
> The new factory would cost $12m, and Vantara offers a $1.5m grant.
> Vantara has a young workforce, but skilled machinists are scarce and
> its government changed recently.
>
> **Source 2: Kessel Gadgets**
>
> Kessel is looking at two overseas markets and a joint venture partner. Market A
> has disposable income of $9,000 per head, growing at 5% a year. Market B
> has $7,000 per head, growing at 9% a year. Kessel's overseas subsidiary
> earns EUR 2.4m a year in profit. The exchange rate has moved from
> $1 = EUR 0.80 to $1 = EUR 0.96. A local firm, Haldar, has offered a joint
> venture in Market B because it knows the market well and has its own
> distribution network.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "off-shoring". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate Marlowe's cost per jacket at home and in Vantara. **[4]**

**3.** Calculate the annual saving from moving production to Vantara, and the percentage fall in cost per jacket. **[4]**

**4.** Calculate the return on investment from the new factory without and with the grant, assuming the annual saving is the only gain. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** push factor that has encouraged Marlowe to look overseas. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse how the scarcity of skilled machinists could affect Marlowe's plan. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "joint venture". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the disposable income per head in Market A and Market B after five years. **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the dollar value of the subsidiary's profit before and after the exchange rate change, and the difference. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **one** reason why Kessel might benefit from a joint venture with Haldar. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss whether Kessel should enter Market A or Market B. **[8]**

**12.** Assess whether Marlowe should move production to Vantara. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Moving the business's own production or services [1] to another country, usually to cut costs [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Home: labour = 15 x 2 = $30; total = 30 + 22 + 3 = **$55** [2]. Vantara: labour = 5 x 3 = $15; total = 15 + 22 + 9 = **$46** [2]. **[4]**

**3.** Saving per jacket = 55 - 46 = $9 [1]. Annual saving = 9 x 200,000 = **$1.8m** [1]. Percentage fall = 9 / 55 x 100 [1] = **16.4%** [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Without the grant: ROI = 1.8 / 12 x 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. With the grant: net cost = 12 - 1.5 = $10.5m, so ROI = 1.8 / 10.5 x 100 [1] = **17.1%** [1]. **[4]**

**5.** The home market is saturated, so most customers already own jackets and sales growth is slow [1], which means Marlowe cannot raise profit by selling much more at home [1]. Competition is also strong, which keeps prices down [1], so cutting costs by producing abroad is one way to protect profit margins [1]. *(Competition could be developed instead.)* **[4]**

**6.** Skilled machinists are scarce, so Marlowe may have to pay higher wages than the $5 an hour assumed [1], which would raise the $15 labour cost per jacket [1]. If workers are less skilled, productivity could be lower than expected, so more than 3 hours might be needed per jacket [1], or defects might rise [1]. A rise of just 1 hour per jacket would add $5, taking the cost to $51 and cutting the saving from $9 to $4 per jacket [1]. Training would cost money and take time, which would delay the benefits of the move [1]. **[6]**

**7.** A business set up and owned jointly by two or more firms [1], which share the costs, risks and profits [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Market A: 9,000 x 1.05^5 = **$11,487** [2]. Market B: 7,000 x 1.09^5 = **$10,770** [2]. (Award 1 mark for each correct method if the arithmetic is wrong.) **[4]**

**9.** Before: 2.4m / 0.80 = **$3.0m** [1]. After: 2.4m / 0.96 = **$2.5m** [1]. Difference = 3.0 - 2.5 [1] = **$0.5m less** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Haldar knows Market B well [1], including customers' tastes, local law and business practice, so Kessel is less likely to make costly mistakes [1]. Haldar also has an existing distribution network [1], so Kessel can reach customers more quickly and cheaply than if it built its own, and shares the cost and risk of entry [1]. **[4]**

**11.** **Market A:** it has the higher income now ($9,000 against $7,000) [1], so customers can afford the product immediately [1]; it may be less risky, and Kessel's subsidiary already earns profit overseas [1].
**Market B:** income is growing faster (9% against 5%), and after five years the gap is only $717 ($11,487 compared with $10,770) [1]; if growth continues, Market B would pass Market A in about seven years [1]; there is a ready local partner in Haldar, who reduces risk [1].
**Judgement:** Market B looks the better long-term choice because the faster growth, combined with a partner that has local knowledge and distribution, outweighs the lower starting income [1]. This depends on political stability and infrastructure in Market B, which the source does not mention, and on whether Kessel's product is aimed at higher-income customers [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **For moving:** the cost per jacket falls from $55 to $46 (16.4%), saving $1.8m a year [1]; ROI of 15% (17.1% with the grant) is a good return [1]; the move addresses the saturated, competitive home market [1]; the grant lowers the risk [1].
**Against:** transport costs triple from $3 to $9 per jacket, which shows that the cost advantage is sensitive to distance [1]; skilled machinists are scarce, and a rise in labour hours per jacket to 4 would cut the saving to $4 [1]; the government has changed recently, so policies on tax, tariffs or ownership might change [1]; moving production abroad could damage the brand if quality falls or staff at home are made redundant [1].
**Judgement:** Marlowe should probably proceed, but in stages, because the return is good, the grant lowers the cost and the home market gives little room for profit growth [1]. It should first test the skills and stability risks, for example with a pilot plant or a contract with a Vantaran producer, before committing the full $12m [1]. The decision would change if productivity were much lower than 3 hours per jacket, or if the political position became less stable [1], and it would also depend on how much the home market could be improved by cheaper options such as better design [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using wages alone to compare locations and ignoring hours of labour and transport.
- Using the grant to reduce the annual saving instead of the cost of the factory.
- Converting euros to dollars by multiplying by the exchange rate.
- Treating off-shoring and outsourcing as the same thing.
- Recommending Market B or Vantara without a risk the source mentions.
