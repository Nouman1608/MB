These notes condense section 11.6, Alcohols, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All three outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [alcohols study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alcohols/); then try the [alcohols practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-alcohols-practice/).

## The three outcomes in one line each

1. Manufacture of ethanol by fermentation and by catalytic addition of steam to ethene, with advantages and disadvantages of each.
2. Combustion of alcohols.
3. Uses of ethanol: solvent and fuel.

## The two routes side by side

| | Fermentation | Steam + ethene |
|---|---|---|
| Word equation | glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide | ethene + steam → ethanol |
| Symbol equation | C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂ | C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH |
| Temperature | 25–35 °C | 300 °C |
| Pressure | atmospheric | 6000 kPa / 60 atm |
| Catalyst | yeast (its enzymes) | acid catalyst |
| Other requirement | absence of oxygen; aqueous glucose | — |
| Raw material | renewable (plants) | non-renewable (petroleum) |
| Rate | slow | fast |
| Product | dilute, impure; distil to purify | pure ethanol |
| Operation | tanks, stopped and restarted | continuous |
| Energy cost | low | high |

Memory hook for fermentation conditions: **"warm, wet, yeast, no air"**: 25–35 °C, aqueous glucose, yeast, absence of oxygen.

## Why each fermentation condition matters

- **25–35 °C**: fast enough, but not so hot that the yeast's enzymes are denatured.
- **No oxygen**: otherwise yeast respires aerobically and ethanol is not produced; oxygen could also oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid.
- **Yeast**: provides the enzymes that catalyse the reaction.
- After fermentation: filter off yeast, then fractional distillation to concentrate the ethanol.

## Choosing a method in a scenario

| Scenario clue | Better method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| plenty of crops, no oil | fermentation | renewable raw material available |
| oil refinery with cracking | steam + ethene | ethene available; fast, pure, continuous |
| need for very pure ethanol | steam + ethene | no distillation needed |
| concern about running out of fossil fuels | fermentation | renewable |
| need for low energy use | fermentation | mild conditions |

## Combustion of alcohols

| Alcohol | Complete combustion |
|---|---|
| Methanol | 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O |
| Ethanol | C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O |
| Propan-1-ol / propan-2-ol | 2C₃H₇OH + 9O₂ → 6CO₂ + 8H₂O |
| Butan-1-ol / butan-2-ol | C₄H₉OH + 6O₂ → 4CO₂ + 5H₂O |

Balancing tips:

- count **all** hydrogens, including the one in –OH
- subtract the **one oxygen** already in the alcohol before working out O₂
- double the whole equation if O₂ ends in a half

Incomplete combustion (limited air) forms carbon monoxide: C₂H₅OH + 2O₂ → 2CO + 3H₂O.

## Fermentation calculation pattern

1 mol glucose (180 g) → 2 mol ethanol (2 × 46 = 92 g) + 2 mol CO₂.

So mass of ethanol = mass of glucose × 92 ÷ 180. For 36 g glucose: 36 × 92 ÷ 180 = 18.4 g ethanol.

Percentage yield = actual mass ÷ maximum (theoretical) mass × 100. If only 12.0 g of ethanol is collected from that 36 g of glucose, the yield is 12.0 ÷ 18.4 × 100 = 65.2%.

## Uses of ethanol

| Use | Why |
|---|---|
| Solvent | dissolves many substances that do not dissolve in water; evaporates easily |
| Fuel | burns, releasing much energy; renewable if made by fermentation |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Alcohol | homologous series with the –OH functional group; general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH |
| Fermentation | conversion of glucose to ethanol and carbon dioxide by yeast without oxygen |
| Catalytic addition of steam | reaction of ethene with steam over an acid catalyst to form ethanol |
| Renewable | can be replaced as fast as it is used, e.g. crops |
| Non-renewable | finite; cannot be replaced, e.g. petroleum |
| Fractional distillation | used to separate ethanol from the fermentation mixture |
| Enzyme | biological catalyst; yeast contains the enzymes for fermentation |

## Exam traps

- Pressure is **6000 kPa** (60 atm), not 6000 atm.
- Fermentation: **absence** of oxygen.
- Fermentation gives a **dilute** solution, not pure ethanol.
- Only fermentation releases CO₂; steam addition gives ethanol alone.
- In "advantages and disadvantages" questions, cover **both** methods.
- Uses in the syllabus are **solvent** and **fuel**; learn those two.
- Combustion of any alcohol gives CO₂ and H₂O (complete) — not hydrogen.

## Self-test

1. State the temperature range for fermentation.
2. Why must oxygen be excluded during fermentation?
3. Write the symbol equation for fermentation of glucose.
4. State all the conditions for making ethanol from ethene.
5. Give **one** advantage of fermentation over the ethene route.
6. Give **one** advantage of the ethene route over fermentation.
7. How is ethanol separated from the fermentation mixture?
8. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of ethanol.
9. Write the balanced equation for the complete combustion of methanol.
10. State **two** uses of ethanol.
11. Calculate the mass of ethanol formed from 18 g of glucose.
12. Name the gaseous product of fermentation and state how it could be identified.

## Answers

1. 25–35 °C.
2. With oxygen the yeast respires aerobically, producing no ethanol (and ethanol could be oxidised to ethanoic acid).
3. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₂H₅OH + 2CO₂.
4. 300 °C, 6000 kPa (60 atm), acid catalyst.
5. Any one: renewable raw material; low temperature and pressure, so lower energy costs.
6. Any one: fast; continuous; produces pure ethanol.
7. Filter off the yeast, then fractional distillation.
8. C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O.
9. 2CH₃OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 4H₂O.
10. Solvent; fuel.
11. 18 × 92 ÷ 180 = 9.2 g.
12. Carbon dioxide; it turns limewater milky.
