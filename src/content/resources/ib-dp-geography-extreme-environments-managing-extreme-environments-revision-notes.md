Condensed recall for **3.3 Managing extreme environments** (IB DP Geography, Option C: Extreme environments). See the [Extreme environments study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-extreme-environments-study-guide/).

## Living in extreme places

- **Adaptation** using traditional knowledge: nomadic pastoralism, seasonal movement, building designs suited to climate.
- **Technology:** irrigation, engineering for frozen ground, research stations.

## Pressures

Resource extraction, tourism, irrigation agriculture, infrastructure and research stations.

## Opportunities versus risks

| Opportunities | Risks |
|---|---|
| Minerals and energy | Fragile ecosystems that recover slowly |
| Tourism | Permafrost thaw damaging infrastructure |
| Water storage in ice | Desertification |
| | Greater exposure to hazards |

## Stakeholders

Indigenous communities, governments, companies, conservation organisations, visitors. Priorities often clash, for example extraction versus conservation, or tourism income versus cultural respect.

## Strategies

- Protected areas and visitor management
- Water-efficient farming and drought-resistant crops
- Engineering that accommodates frozen ground, for example raised structures
- International agreements for shared regions

## Evaluation criteria

Environmental impact, cost, cultural respect, long-term sustainability. Ask who benefits and who bears the risk.

## Why recovery is slow

Low productivity and harsh conditions mean vegetation and soils regrow slowly, so damage can last.

## Exam traps

- Offering ideas without weighing costs, benefits and stakeholders.
- Treating indigenous knowledge as only historical rather than a working management resource.
- Ignoring that a strategy that works in one extreme environment may fail in another.
- Using unsure place details; describe the pattern in general terms instead.

## Self-test

1. Give two traditional ways of living in extreme environments.
2. Name two modern pressures.
3. Why is permafrost thaw a management problem?
4. Give two evaluation criteria for a strategy.
5. Why do extreme environments recover slowly from damage?

**Answers:** 1. For example nomadic pastoralism and climate-suited building design. 2. For example resource extraction and tourism. 3. It destabilises ground, damaging buildings and pipelines, and may release stored carbon. 4. For example cost and long-term sustainability. 5. Low productivity and harsh conditions mean slow regrowth of vegetation and soils.
