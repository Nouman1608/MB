## Formulae

| Ratio | Formula | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Stock turnover | cost of sales ÷ average stock | Times |
| Stock turnover | (average stock ÷ cost of sales) × 365 | Days |
| Debtor days | (debtors ÷ total sales revenue) × 365 | Days |
| Creditor days | (creditors ÷ cost of sales) × 365 | Days |
| Gearing ratio | (non-current liabilities ÷ capital employed) × 100 | % |

* Average stock = (opening stock + closing stock) ÷ 2.
* Capital employed = non-current liabilities + equity.
* Sales revenue goes with debtors; cost of sales goes with stock and creditors.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Efficiency ratio | Measures how well a business manages stock, credit and its long-term funding |
| Stock turnover | How often stock is sold and replaced in a year, or how long it is held |
| Debtors | Customers who owe the business money for sales on credit |
| Creditors | Suppliers the business owes money to for purchases on credit |
| Gearing | Share of capital employed that comes from long-term borrowing |
| Insolvency | Being unable to pay debts as they fall due |
| Bankruptcy | A legal status or process that can follow insolvency |

## Reading the results

| Ratio | Usually better | But watch for |
|---|---|---|
| Stock turnover (times) | Higher | Stock-outs and lost sales if too little is held |
| Stock turnover (days) | Lower | Same risk as above |
| Debtor days | Lower | Lost customers if terms are too strict |
| Creditor days | Higher, within agreed terms | Supplier relationships and lost discounts |
| Gearing | Lower if risk is the concern | Borrowing can raise owners' returns if it funds profitable assets |

**Combining the day-counts:** stock days + debtor days − creditor days gives a rough idea of how long the business funds its own trading before cash returns. A rising figure signals growing pressure on cash.

## Strategies

* **Stock turnover:** smaller, more frequent orders; just-in-time where suppliers allow; drop slow lines; promotions on old stock; better demand forecasting.
* **Debtor days:** shorter credit terms; early payment discounts; credit checks; chase late payers.
* **Creditor days:** negotiate longer terms; build supplier trust; avoid paying earlier than required.
* **Gearing:** repay loans from retained profit; issue shares and repay debt; sell unused assets.

## Insolvency versus bankruptcy

* Insolvency: a financial position; debts cannot be paid as they fall due.
* Bankruptcy: a legal status or procedure that may follow insolvency.
* A profitable business can become insolvent if cash arrives too slowly (long stock and debtor days) or leaves too quickly (short creditor days), especially with high gearing.
* Terms and procedures vary by country.

## Worked mini-example

Rowanbank Hardware (invented): sales revenue 730,000; cost of sales 438,000; opening stock 50,000; closing stock 70,000; debtors 80,000; creditors 48,000; non-current liabilities 120,000; equity 280,000.

* Average stock = 60,000.
* Stock turnover = 438,000 ÷ 60,000 = **7.3 times**, or 60,000 ÷ 438,000 × 365 = **50 days**.
* Debtor days = 80,000 ÷ 730,000 × 365 = **40 days**.
* Creditor days = 48,000 ÷ 438,000 × 365 = **40 days**.
* Gearing = 120,000 ÷ 400,000 × 100 = **30%**.

Comment: customers pay in about the same time Rowanbank takes to pay suppliers, so the stock holding period of 50 days is what the business must fund from its own cash.

## Exam traps

* Sales revenue used for creditor days or stock turnover.
* Closing stock used instead of average stock.
* "× 365" left out of a days ratio.
* Gearing calculated on total liabilities or on equity alone.
* More creditor days praised without mentioning suppliers' reaction.
* Insolvency and bankruptcy treated as the same.
* A strategy suggested without its cost or risk.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the formula for debtor days.
2. Why does creditor days use cost of sales rather than sales revenue?
3. Opening stock 40,000, closing stock 60,000, cost of sales 500,000. Find stock turnover in times.
4. Using question 3, find stock turnover in days.
5. Debtors 90,000, sales revenue 900,000. Find debtor days.
6. Creditors 60,000, cost of sales 600,000. Find creditor days.
7. Non-current liabilities 200,000, equity 600,000. Find the gearing ratio.
8. Give one strategy to reduce debtor days and one risk.
9. Why might a business choose to be highly geared?
10. Distinguish between insolvency and bankruptcy.
11. Explain how a profitable business can become insolvent.
12. Debtor days rise from 30 to 45. What does this mean for cash flow?

### Answers

1. (Debtors ÷ total sales revenue) × 365.
2. Creditors arise from purchases of stock and inputs, which are reflected in cost of sales.
3. Average stock 50,000; 500,000 ÷ 50,000 = 10 times.
4. 50,000 ÷ 500,000 × 365 = 36.5 days.
5. 90,000 ÷ 900,000 × 365 = 36.5 days.
6. 60,000 ÷ 600,000 × 365 = 36.5 days.
7. Capital employed 800,000; 200,000 ÷ 800,000 × 100 = 25%.
8. Early payment discounts; risk: lower revenue per sale (or shorter terms; risk: customers move to rivals).
9. Borrowing can fund growth without diluting ownership, and if the assets earn more than the interest, owners gain.
10. Insolvency is the position of being unable to pay debts as they fall due; bankruptcy is a legal status or process that can follow it.
11. Profit is recorded when sales are made, but if customers pay late, stock is held long and suppliers must be paid quickly, cash can run out.
12. Customers take 15 days longer to pay, so cash arrives later and the business may need more short-term finance.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is efficiency ratio analysis SL or HL?**
Section 3.6, including stock turnover, debtor days, creditor days, gearing and insolvency versus bankruptcy, is HL only in the IB DP Business Management guide, first assessment 2024.

**Is a high gearing ratio always bad?**
No. It raises risk because interest must be paid whatever profits are, but it can increase owners' returns if borrowed money earns more than it costs.
