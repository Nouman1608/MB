This guide covers sub-topic **14.3 Hormones** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core: the definition, four glands and their hormones, adrenaline's effects and the comparison with nervous control. Outcomes 5 and 6 are **Extended only**: glucagon, and adrenaline's role in metabolic activity.

The [coordination and response study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-coordination-and-response/) lists the glands and effects briefly. This page goes further: unpacking the definition, locating glands on body diagrams, explaining why each effect of adrenaline helps in danger, interpreting data, and writing comparisons that score. Afterwards, use the [hormones revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-hormones-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and the [hormones practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-hormones-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## What sub-topic 14.3 asks you to do

| Outcome | What you must be able to do | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| 14.3.1 | Describe a hormone as a chemical substance, produced by a gland and carried by the blood, which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs | Core |
| 14.3.2 | Identify the adrenal glands, pancreas, testes and ovaries, and state the hormones they secrete: adrenaline, insulin, testosterone and oestrogen | Core |
| 14.3.3 | Describe adrenaline as the hormone secreted in "fight or flight" situations and its effects: increased breathing rate, heart rate and pupil diameter | Core |
| 14.3.4 | Compare nervous and hormonal control, limited to speed of action and duration of effect | Core |
| 14.3.5 | State that glucagon is secreted by the pancreas | Extended only |
| 14.3.6 | Describe the role of adrenaline in the control of metabolic activity: increasing blood glucose concentration and heart rate | Extended only |

## 14.3.1 The definition, phrase by phrase

| Phrase | Meaning | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| "a chemical substance" | a hormone is a molecule, not an impulse | separates hormonal from nervous control |
| "produced by a gland" | made and secreted by an endocrine gland | links to outcome 2 |
| "carried by the blood" | it travels dissolved in the plasma | it reaches every part of the body |
| "alters the activity of one or more specific target organs" | only certain organs respond | explains why hormones have particular effects |

A three-mark "describe a hormone" question usually rewards: chemical produced by a gland (1), carried in the blood (1), alters the activity of target organs (1).

Hormones reach every organ in the blood, but only **target organs** respond. That is how one hormone can travel everywhere yet have specific effects.

## 14.3.2 and 14.3.5 Glands and their hormones

| Gland | Position on a body diagram | Hormone (Core) | Extra hormone (Extended) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Adrenal glands | one on top of each kidney | adrenaline | – |
| Pancreas | just below the stomach, in the loop of the small intestine | insulin | **glucagon** |
| Testes (male) | in the scrotum, outside the abdomen | testosterone | – |
| Ovaries (female) | in the lower abdomen, one on each side of the uterus | oestrogen | – |

In identification questions, the most common slip is confusing the adrenal glands with the kidneys. The adrenal glands are the small structures **on top of** the kidneys.

Insulin and glucagon are developed in [homeostasis](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-homeostasis-cambridge-igcse-biology/). Testosterone and oestrogen are developed in sex hormones (topic 16).

## 14.3.3 Adrenaline and "fight or flight" (Core)

Adrenaline is secreted by the adrenal glands in **"fight or flight" situations**: moments of danger, fear, stress or excitement when the body must prepare to act quickly. The syllabus limits its Core effects to three. Each one makes sense once you link it to the need for action.

| Effect | How it helps in a fight-or-flight situation |
|---|---|
| Increased breathing rate | more oxygen is taken in for respiration in muscles |
| Increased heart rate | blood carrying oxygen and glucose reaches the muscles faster |
| Increased pupil diameter | more light enters the eye, improving vision of the danger |

### Worked example 1: applying the effects

*A student feels nervous just before a music exam. Her heart pounds and she breathes quickly. Explain these observations.*

- The stress is a fight-or-flight situation, so her **adrenal glands secrete adrenaline**.
- Adrenaline is carried in the **blood** to its target organs.
- It **increases her heart rate**, which she feels as pounding.
- It **increases her breathing rate**.
- If asked, add that her pupils would also widen.

The danger here is not physical, but the hormone response is the same.

## 14.3.6 Adrenaline and metabolic activity (Extended only)

The Extended outcome adds two effects on metabolism:

1. **Increasing the blood glucose concentration.**
2. **Increasing heart rate.**

Together these supply muscles with more glucose and oxygen, so the rate of respiration in muscle cells can increase and more energy is released for muscle contraction. Notice that adrenaline **raises** blood glucose, the same direction as glucagon and the opposite of insulin.

### Worked example 2: interpreting data (Extended)

A doctor records a patient's heart rate and blood glucose before and after a small dose of adrenaline. (Example data written for this guide.)

| Time after dose / minutes | 0 | 2 | 5 | 10 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Heart rate / beats per minute | 72 | 96 | 104 | 98 | 80 |
| Blood glucose / arbitrary units | 5.0 | 5.4 | 6.2 | 6.8 | 6.0 |

- **Describe heart rate:** rose from 72 to a peak of 104 beats per minute at 5 minutes, then fell to 80 by 20 minutes.
- **Percentage increase in heart rate, 0 to 5 minutes:** (104 − 72) ÷ 72 × 100 = 44.44...% = **44%**.
- **Describe blood glucose:** rose from 5.0 to a peak of 6.8 at 10 minutes, then fell to 6.0.
- **Explain:** adrenaline travels in the blood to its target organs; it increases heart rate and increases blood glucose concentration, preparing the body for action.

## 14.3.4 Nervous and hormonal control compared

The syllabus limits the comparison to **speed of action** and **duration of effect**.

| | Nervous control | Hormonal control |
|---|---|---|
| Speed of action | very fast | slower |
| Duration of effect | short-lived | longer lasting |

Why? Impulses travel along neurones very quickly and stop when the impulses stop. Hormones must travel in the blood, which takes longer, and they stay in the blood for some time, so their effects last longer.

### Model answer: a compare question

**Question:** Compare nervous control with hormonal control. [2]

**Answer:** Nervous control acts more quickly than hormonal control (1). The effects of nervous control are short-lived, whereas the effects of hormones last longer (1).

**Why it scores:** each sentence covers both types of control. "Nerves are fast; hormones last a long time" makes two separate one-sided points and may score only one mark.

### Worked example 3: applying the comparison

*A pedestrian jumps back from the kerb as a bus passes close by. A minute later her heart is still beating fast. Explain these two observations.*

- Jumping back happened **within a fraction of a second**: nervous control (a reflex), which is fast.
- Her heart still racing a minute later shows **hormonal control**: adrenaline was released and its effect lasts longer.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a hormone as "a message sent along nerves".
- Saying hormones travel "through the body" instead of "in the blood".
- Saying a hormone affects "the whole body" instead of target organs.
- Placing adrenal glands in the brain or confusing them with kidneys.
- Writing "pupil size changes" without saying it **increases**.
- (Extended) Saying adrenaline **lowers** blood glucose.
- (Extended) Naming the liver as the source of glucagon. Glucagon is secreted by the pancreas.
- Comparing nervous and hormonal control on features other than speed and duration when the syllabus limits it.

## Exam technique for 14.3 command words

- **Describe** a hormone: the four-part definition.
- **Identify**: name the gland from its position; then state its hormone.
- **State**: short and exact, such as "glucagon is secreted by the pancreas".
- **Describe** adrenaline's effects: three Core effects, each with "increased".
- **Compare**: speed and duration, both types in each sentence.
