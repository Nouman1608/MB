Condensed notes on section **3.7.6 Analysing the external environment to assess opportunities and threats: social and technological** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.7 is A-level only. The pharmacy and automation examples are in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-social-technological/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-social-technological-practice/).

## Specification content

- The impact of the social and technological environment on strategic and functional decision making.
- Social changes: demographic change and population movements such as migration; changes in consumer lifestyle and buying behaviour; the growth of online businesses.
- The social environment, including CSR: reasons for and against, stakeholder versus shareholder concept, Carroll's CSR pyramid.
- Technological change: its impact on functional areas and strategy.
- The pressures for socially responsible behaviour.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demography | The size and structure of a population |
| Migration | Movement of people between areas or countries |
| CSR | A business's responsibilities to society and the environment beyond profit |
| Shareholder concept | The business exists mainly to maximise returns for its owners |
| Stakeholder concept | The business should balance the interests of all affected groups |
| Greenwashing | Making environmental claims that exceed reality |
| Automation | Using machines or software to do tasks done by people |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling over the internet |

## Social change and its effects

| Change | Opportunity | Threat |
|---|---|---|
| Ageing population | Demand for health, leisure and mobility products | Labour shortage; fewer young customers |
| Migration | Larger labour pool; new customers | Labour shortage if it falls; need to adapt products |
| Healthier lifestyles | Demand for healthy products | Decline of sugary or high-fat products |
| Convenience culture | Delivery, click-and-collect | Cost of faster service |
| Ethical consumption | Premium for fair-trade or recycled goods | Costs and scrutiny of supply chains |
| Growth of online | Wider market; lower overheads; customer data | Price comparison; pressure on high street; returns costs |

## CSR: for and against

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Reputation and brand | Cost, reducing short-term profit |
| Recruitment and retention | Shareholders' money used for causes they did not choose |
| Fewer fines and boycotts | Rivals ignoring CSR can undercut |
| Long-term security | Greenwashing risk |
| Investor interest | Benefits hard to measure |

## Shareholder against stakeholder

| | Shareholder | Stakeholder |
|---|---|---|
| Main aim | Maximise return to owners | Balance all groups |
| CSR | Only if it adds to profit | Core duty |
| Risk | Short-termism; harm to others | Conflicting interests; unclear aim |

## Carroll's pyramid (base to top)

1. **Economic**: be profitable.
2. **Legal**: obey the law.
3. **Ethical**: do what is right and fair beyond the law.
4. **Philanthropic**: contribute to society, such as donations or volunteering.

Use: judge how far a business goes. Paying only the legal minimum wage is the legal layer; paying a living wage is ethical.

## Pressures for socially responsible behaviour

Customers (boycotts); employees; pressure groups and social media; investors; government and regulation; competitors' marketing. Strength depends on visibility, ease of switching and seriousness.

## Technology by function

| Function | Impact |
|---|---|
| Operations | Automation; higher productivity and quality; lower unit costs |
| Marketing | Online advertising, social media, personalisation, data |
| Human resources | New skills; remote work; fewer routine roles; online recruitment |
| Finance | Digital payments; real-time data; investment cost |
| Strategy | New markets, products and competitors; lower entry barriers |

## Automation quick example

Machine costs £120,000, saves 3 workers at £30,000 each and costs £18,000 a year to run. Net saving = £90,000 − £18,000 = **£72,000**; payback = £120,000 ÷ £72,000 = **1.67 years**.

## Model analysis paragraph

"An ageing population is an opportunity for the pharmacy chain, because over-65s use more prescriptions. If the over-65 group in the catchment rises by 22%, demand for prescription services should rise. The chain may need longer opening hours and home delivery, and staff trained to help older customers. However, the chain also depends on young recruits, and an ageing population means fewer of them, so staffing may become harder."

## Command words

- **Analyse:** say what the change is, who it affects, and what the business should do.
- **Evaluate (CSR):** use Carroll's pyramid or shareholder against stakeholder views to structure the answer.

## Exam traps

- CSR does not always raise or always lower profit; it depends.
- Do not mix up shareholder and stakeholder concepts.
- Apply Carroll's layers to the case; do not just list them.
- Technology brings costs and disruption as well as gains.
- Link each change to this business's customers, staff or costs.

## Self-test

1. Define "demography".
2. Give two effects of an ageing population on a supermarket.
3. A town of 40,000 has 15% aged over 65. The share rises to 20%. By how many does the over-65 population rise?
4. Give one benefit and one risk to a traditional retailer of growing an online business.
5. State the four layers of Carroll's pyramid.
6. Which layer does paying a wage above the legal minimum fit best, and why?
7. Give two reasons for a business to adopt CSR.
8. Give two arguments against CSR.
9. Explain the shareholder concept.
10. Explain the stakeholder concept.
11. Give two ways technology might change human resource management.
12. Why might technological advantage be short-lived?

### Answers

1. The study of the size and structure of a population.
2. Any two: more demand for healthcare, easy-to-read labelling and home delivery; fewer young staff to recruit; need for accessible stores.
3. 40,000 × 5% = **2,000** more (from 6,000 to 8,000).
4. Benefit: wider market or lower overheads. Risk: more price competition, or delivery and returns costs.
5. Economic, legal, ethical, philanthropic.
6. Ethical: it goes beyond what the law requires because it is fair.
7. Any two: brand reputation; recruitment and retention; avoiding fines and boycotts; long-term security; investor interest.
8. Any two: cost; shareholders' money; competitive disadvantage; greenwashing risk; benefits hard to measure.
9. The business exists mainly to maximise returns for its owners (shareholders).
10. The business should balance the interests of everyone affected by its actions, such as employees, customers and the community.
11. Any two: new skills and training; remote working; fewer routine jobs; online recruitment.
12. Rivals can copy or buy similar technology, and newer technology may replace it.
