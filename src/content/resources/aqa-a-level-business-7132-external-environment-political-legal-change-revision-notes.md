Condensed notes on section **3.7.4 Analysing the external environment to assess opportunities and threats: political and legal change** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.7 is A-level only. The Larkin Care wage example is in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-political-legal-change/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-political-legal-change-practice/).

## Specification content

- The impact of changes in the political and legal environment on strategic and functional decision making.
- A broad understanding of the scope and effects of laws related to competition, the labour market and environmental legislation.
- The impact of government policy related to enterprise, the role of regulators, infrastructure, the environment and international trade.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Political environment | Government decisions, policies and stability |
| Legal environment | Laws and rules, including those set by regulators |
| Competition law | Rules against cartels, abuse of dominance and anti-competitive mergers |
| Regulator | Independent body overseeing a market and enforcing rules |
| Tariff | Tax on imports |
| Quota | Limit on the quantity of imports |
| Sanctions | Restrictions on trading with a country or group |
| Compliance cost | Cost of meeting a legal requirement |

## Areas of law and policy

| Area | Examples | Threat | Opportunity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Competition | Cartel ban; merger control; abuse of dominance | Takeover blocked; pricing limits | Dominant rivals restrained |
| Labour market | Minimum wage; working time; equality; health and safety | Higher labour cost; less flexibility | Better staff retention; new skills |
| Environment | Emission limits; packaging rules; carbon tax | Cost of compliance or redesign; fines | Demand for green products; subsidies |
| Enterprise policy | Grants; tax relief; simpler start-up rules | More new competitors | Funding for growth or innovation |
| Regulators | Price caps; quality standards; fines | Lower revenue; extra reporting | Fair access to networks; consumer trust |
| Infrastructure | Roads, rail, ports, broadband, energy | Cancelled project; poor links | New link opens markets or sites |
| International trade | Tariffs; quotas; trade deals; sanctions | Higher import cost; lost export market | Wider markets; cheaper inputs |

## Strategic or functional?

| Strategic effect | Functional effect |
|---|---|
| Which markets to enter or leave | Human resources: contracts, recruitment, training |
| Where to locate | Finance: budgets, funding, tax planning |
| Whether to merge or acquire | Operations: methods, suppliers, automation |
| What to produce | Marketing: pricing, claims, promotion |

## Assessing a change: six questions

1. **Scale**: how big is the cost or revenue effect?
2. **Timing**: sudden or phased?
3. **Certainty**: passed, proposed or reversible?
4. **Who is affected**: all rivals or only this business?
5. **Flexibility**: can prices, methods or location change?
6. **Opportunity**: can compliance become a selling point?

## Business responses

- Comply and budget for the cost.
- Pass the cost on through higher prices (easier if rivals face the same law).
- Change methods, such as automation or cleaner processes.
- Move production or sourcing.
- Lobby, alone or through trade bodies.
- Plan for scenarios, since policy can change after elections.

## Worked mini-example

A shop pays 8 staff the minimum wage for 20 hours a week. The minimum rises by £0.50 an hour. Extra annual cost = £0.50 × 20 × 52 × 8 = **£4,160**. If annual operating profit is £20,000, profit falls 20.8% unless prices rise or costs are cut. A change that looks small can dominate a thin margin.

## Exam traps

- Say how the change affects costs, revenues or choices, not only what the law is.
- Not every change is a threat; look for opportunities.
- Regulators are independent bodies, not the government.
- Link to strategic **and** functional decisions.
- Consider whether rivals are affected equally.

## Self-test

1. Define "tariff".
2. Give two examples of behaviour banned by competition law.
3. Explain one way a rise in the minimum wage could be an opportunity for a business.
4. A café pays 5 staff the minimum wage for 24 hours a week. The minimum rises by £0.60 an hour. Calculate the extra annual cost (52 weeks).
5. Give one threat and one opportunity from stricter packaging rules.
6. What is the role of a regulator?
7. Why might a price cap on a regulated industry reduce investment?
8. How could improved broadband infrastructure affect a rural business?
9. Give two ways a business might respond to a new tariff on its imports.
10. Why is a change in law that affects all rivals equally less of a threat to competitiveness?
11. Name one strategic and one functional decision affected by a new environmental law.
12. Why might a small firm find new regulation harder to deal with than a large firm?

### Answers

1. A tax on imported goods.
2. Any two: price fixing between rivals; sharing markets; abusing a dominant position; mergers that substantially reduce competition.
3. For example, higher wages for low-paid workers may raise their spending, increasing demand for the business's products; or the business may gain from lower staff turnover and training costs.
4. £0.60 × 24 × 52 × 5 = **£3,744**.
5. Threat: redesign and higher packaging cost. Opportunity: a business already using recyclable packaging gains an advantage.
6. To oversee a market, enforce rules on prices, quality and service, and protect consumers.
7. Lower allowed revenue leaves less profit to fund investment, and the return on new projects may be lower.
8. It can allow online selling, remote working and easier contact with customers, widening its market.
9. Any two: find alternative suppliers; raise prices; absorb the cost; manufacture locally; negotiate with the supplier.
10. Because rivals face the same increase, so relative costs do not change and prices can rise across the market.
11. Strategic: whether to continue making the affected product or enter a greener market. Functional: operations changes production method.
12. It has fewer staff and less money for compliance, and the fixed cost of compliance is spread over less revenue.
