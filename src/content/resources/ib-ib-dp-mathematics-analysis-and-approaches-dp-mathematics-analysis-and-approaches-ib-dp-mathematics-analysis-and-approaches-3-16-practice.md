These questions cover syllabus section 3.16 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021), which is HL only: the vector product, its properties and the geometric meaning of |v × w|. Questions are labelled "(calculator-free)" or "(calculator allowed)"; give calculator answers to 3 significant figures. Vectors are written in rows. The methods are in the [3.16 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-16/) and the [3.16 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-16-revision-notes/).

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) P(1, 0, 2), Q(3, 1, 1) and R(2, 4, 3).

**(a)** Find PQ and PR. **[2]**

**(b)** Find PQ × PR. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the area of triangle PQR. **[2]**

**(d)** Write down a unit vector perpendicular to the plane containing P, Q and R. **[1]**

**8.** (calculator-free) Vectors a and b satisfy |a| = 3, |b| = 4 and a · b = 6. Find the exact value of |a × b|. **[3]**

**9.** (calculator-free) Show that (a + b) × (a − b) = −2(a × b) for any vectors a and b. **[3]**

**10.** (calculator-free) A(0, 0, 0), B(2, 0, 0) and C(0, t, 1). The area of triangle ABC is 5. Find the possible values of t. **[4]**

**11.** (calculator-free) ABCD is a parallelogram with A(1, 1, 1), B(3, 2, 1) and D(1, 3, 4).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of C. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the exact area of ABCD. **[3]**

**12.** (calculator-free) Let u = (1, 1, 0) and v = (1, 0, 1).

**(a)** Use the vector product to find sin θ, where θ is the angle between u and v. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the scalar product to show that θ = π/3. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the vector product alone could not decide between θ = π/3 and θ = 2π/3. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (3·1 − (−2)·0, (−2)·2 − 1·1, 1·0 − 3·2) [1] = **(3, −5, −6)** [1]

**(b)** (3, −5, −6) · (1, 3, −2) = 3 − 15 + 12 = 0 and (3, −5, −6) · (2, 0, 1) = 6 + 0 − 6 = 0 [1]

**2. (a)** **k** [1]

**(b)** **−k** [1]

**(c)** **0** [1]

**3.** (2, 1, 0) × (0, 3, 1) = (1, −2, 6) [1]; area **√41** [1]

**4.** AB × AC = (1, 2, 2) × (2, −1, 2) = (6, 2, −5) [1]; magnitude √65 [1]; area **√65/2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Forgetting the ½ is the commonest error; √65 is the area of the parallelogram, not the triangle.

**5.** Components: (−4)(−9) − 6·6 = 0, 6(−3) − 2(−9) = 0, 2·6 − (−4)(−3) = 0 [1]. A zero vector product means the vectors are **parallel** [1]

**6. (a)** **(−1, 2, −3)** [1]

**(b)** **(2, −4, 6)** [1]

**7. (a)** **PQ = (2, 1, −1)** [1] and **PR = (1, 4, 1)** [1]

**(b)** (1·1 − (−1)·4, (−1)·1 − 2·1, 2·4 − 1·1) [1] = **(5, −3, 7)** [1]

**(c)** |PQ × PR| = √(25 + 9 + 49) = √83 [1]; area = √83/2 = **4.56** square units [1]

**(d)** **(5, −3, 7)/√83** (or its negative) [1]

**8.** cos θ = 6/(3 × 4) = 1/2 [1], so sin θ = √3/2 [1]. |a × b| = 3 × 4 × √3/2 = **6√3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Equivalently, |a × b|² = |a|²|b|² − (a · b)² = 144 − 36 = 108, which follows from sin²θ + cos²θ = 1.

**9.** (a + b) × (a − b) = a × a − a × b + b × a − b × b, by the distributive property [1]. a × a = b × b = 0 [1] and b × a = −a × b, so the expression is −a × b − a × b = **−2(a × b)** [1]

**10.** AB × AC = (2, 0, 0) × (0, t, 1) = (0, −2, 2t) [1]. Area = ½√(4 + 4t²) = √(1 + t²) [1]. √(1 + t²) = 5 gives t² = 24 [1], so **t = ±2√6** [1]

**11. (a)** c = b + d − a = **(3, 4, 4)** [1]

**(b)** AB = (2, 1, 0) and AD = (0, 2, 3) [1]. AB × AD = (3, −6, 4) [1]. Area = √(9 + 36 + 16) = **√61** [1]

**12. (a)** u × v = (1, −1, −1), with magnitude √3 [1]. |u||v| = 2, so **sin θ = √3/2** [1]

**(b)** u · v = 1, so cos θ = 1/2 and **θ = π/3** [1]

**(c)** sin(π/3) = sin(2π/3), so the sine is the same for both angles; only the sign of the scalar product distinguishes them [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Sign errors in the second component of the vector product.
- Leaving out the ½ when finding the area of a triangle.
- Using position vectors instead of displacement vectors from one vertex.
- Giving an area as a vector instead of a magnitude.
- Using the vector product to find an angle without checking whether it is acute or obtuse.
