Both sections assume the field is perpendicular to the current or to the velocity.

## Key definitions

- **Magnetic flux density, B:** the force on each metre of conductor per ampere of current, with the conductor lying across the field.
- **Tesla (T):** the flux density of a uniform field that exerts 1 N on 1 m of straight wire carrying 1 A and lying across the field.
- **Base units of the tesla:** 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = 1 kg s⁻² A⁻¹.
- **Cyclotron:** a device that accelerates charged particles in a spiral path using a magnetic field to bend the path and an alternating electric field in a gap to speed the particle up.

## Formulae

| Situation | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wire in a field | F = BIL | L is the length inside the field |
| Rearranged | B = F / (IL) | gives the tesla |
| Moving charge | F = BQv | Q is the magnitude of the charge |
| Circular orbit radius | r = mv / (BQ) | from BQv = mv² / r |
| Orbit period | T = 2πm / (BQ) | independent of speed |
| Orbit frequency | f = BQ / (2πm) | the cyclotron frequency |
| After accelerating through V | v = √(2QV / m) | starting from rest |
| Cyclotron maximum speed | v = BQR / m | R is the dee radius |
| Cyclotron maximum kinetic energy | (BQR)² / (2m) | equals ½mv² |

## Direction rules

Fleming's left-hand rule: thumb gives the force, first finger the field (north to south), second finger the conventional current.

| Particle | Where the second finger points |
|---|---|
| Positive charge | along the velocity |
| Negative charge (electron) | opposite to the velocity |
| Current in a wire | along the conventional current |

- The force is perpendicular to both the field and the velocity.
- The force on an electron is opposite to the force on a proton with the same velocity in the same field.
- A particle moving along the field direction feels no force.
- The force does no work, so kinetic energy and speed are unchanged.

## Method in steps: force on a wire

1. Identify L, the length of wire inside the field, in metres.
2. Check that the current and field are perpendicular.
3. Calculate F = BIL.
4. Use Fleming's left-hand rule for the direction. Reversing the current or the field reverses the force.

**Reminder.** B = 0.18 T, I = 3.5 A, L = 0.20 m gives F = 0.18 × 3.5 × 0.20 = 0.126 N, or **0.13 N** to 2 significant figures.

**Reminder.** F = 0.0420 N on a wire with I = 6.0 A and L = 0.35 m gives B = 0.0420 / (6.0 × 0.35) = **0.020 T**.

## Method in steps: current balance

1. Zero the balance with the current off.
2. Switch on the current I and record the change in mass Δm.
3. The wire's force is F = Δm g; the magnets receive the same force the other way (Newton's third law).
4. Plot Δm against I. The line is straight through the origin with gradient BL / g.
5. B = gradient × g / L.

## Method in steps: a charged particle in a circle

1. Write the centripetal force condition: BQv = mv² / r.
2. Rearrange to r = mv / (BQ).
3. If the particle was accelerated through a potential difference V, find v from QV = ½mv² first.
4. Find the period with T = 2πm / (BQ) if asked.
5. Direction of circulation: use the left-hand rule for the force at one point and sketch the centre on that side.

**Reminder.** An electron is accelerated from rest through 120 V. Then v = √(2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 120 / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = 6.5 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. In a field of 4.0 × 10⁻³ T, r = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 6.5 × 10⁶ / (4.0 × 10⁻³ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **9.2 × 10⁻³ m**.

## Cyclotron in six lines

- Two D-shaped dees in a vacuum, field at right angles to the dees.
- Electric field only in the gap; no electric field inside the dees.
- Inside a dee the particle moves in a semicircle at constant speed.
- The time per semicircle is πm / (BQ), independent of speed, so the pd frequency is fixed at f = BQ / (2πm).
- Each gap crossing adds kinetic energy QV, so the radius grows and the path spirals outwards.
- The particle leaves at radius R with speed v = BQR / m.

At speeds approaching the speed of light these simple results stop working, because the mass of the particle is no longer constant. The quantities above assume much lower speeds.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Point to remember |
|---|---|
| Force on a wire and force on a charge | F = BIL and F = BQv are the same law: IL = Qv for the charge carriers |
| Magnetic and electric force | the electric field in a gap speeds the particle up; the magnetic field only turns it |
| Radius and period | radius depends on speed; period does not |
| Proton and electron | same field and velocity, opposite force direction |
| Mass and charge | a heavier particle has a larger orbit; a larger charge gives a smaller orbit |

## Quick self-test

1. Define the tesla.
2. A wire carries 3.5 A and 0.20 m of it lies perpendicular to a field of 0.18 T. What is the force?
3. A force of 0.0420 N acts on 0.35 m of wire carrying 6.0 A at right angles to a field. What is B?
4. Take east, north and up as three perpendicular directions. A current flows north in a field pointing upward. Which way is the force?
5. An electron moves at 1.8 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a field of 0.30 T. Find the force (e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C).
6. An electron moves to the right across the page in a field directed into the page. Which way is it pushed?
7. A proton moves at 2.4 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a field of 0.50 T. Find the radius of its path. What is the radius if the field doubles? (proton mass 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)
8. A proton and an electron have equal kinetic energy and enter the same field at right angles. What is the ratio of their orbit radii?
9. Find the orbit frequency of a proton in a field of 0.65 T.
10. In a cyclotron, why can the alternating pd have a fixed frequency even though the particle speeds up?

### Answers

1. The flux density of a uniform field that exerts 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A and lying across the field.
2. F = 0.18 × 3.5 × 0.20 = 0.126 N, so **0.13 N**.
3. B = 0.0420 / (6.0 × 0.35) = **0.020 T**.
4. Current north (second finger), field up (first finger), so the thumb points **east**.
5. F = 0.30 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.8 × 10⁶ = **8.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N**.
6. Treat as conventional current to the left, field into the page. The thumb points **down the page**.
7. r = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × 2.4 × 10⁶ / (0.50 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = **0.050 m**. Doubling B halves r to **0.025 m**.
8. With equal kinetic energy, mv = √(2mEk), so r is proportional to √m. The ratio r(proton) / r(electron) = √(1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹) = **43** (the proton's orbit is larger).
9. f = BQ / (2πm) = 0.65 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ / (2π × 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷) = **9.9 × 10⁶ Hz**.
10. The time for each semicircle is πm / (BQ), which does not depend on speed. A faster particle goes round a bigger semicircle in the same time, so it always reaches the gap when the pd reverses.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the unit of B as N A m⁻¹ instead of N A⁻¹ m⁻¹, or giving the base units wrongly.
- Using the length of the whole wire rather than only the length in the field.
- Getting the direction of the force on an electron the wrong way round.
- Saying the magnetic field accelerates a particle in the cyclotron, rather than the electric field in the gap.
- Stating that the force changes the speed. It changes only the direction, because it does no work.
- Failing to show that the period is independent of speed when the question asks you to show it.
- Mixing up charge and mass, for example using the proton mass for an alpha particle.
- Giving too many or too few significant figures, or leaving out the unit.
- Describing a definition with no mention of right angles, current, length or force.
