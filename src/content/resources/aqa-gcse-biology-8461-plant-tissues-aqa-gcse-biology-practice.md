These questions cover section **4.2.3.1 Plant tissues** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.3.1 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for question 5. Measurements are invented.

Learn the content first in the [plant tissues study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-tissues-aqa-gcse-biology/) and the [plant tissues revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-tissues-aqa-gcse-biology-revision-notes/). ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the plant tissue described in each statement.

**(a)** Tall, tightly packed cells containing many chloroplasts. **[1]**
**(b)** Hollow tubes strengthened by lignin. **[1]**
**(c)** Loosely packed cells with large air spaces between them. **[1]**
**(d)** Cells at the tip of a root that divide and can differentiate into many types of cell. **[1]**

**2.** Write these leaf layers in order, starting from the upper surface: spongy mesophyll, lower epidermis, upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why it is an advantage to the plant that the cells of the upper epidermis are transparent. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how the structure of xylem is related to its function. **[3]**

**5.** A micrograph of a leaf section was taken at × 80. On the image the whole leaf is 20 mm deep and the palisade layer is 6 mm deep.

**(a)** Calculate the actual thickness of the leaf in micrometres. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the leaf's thickness made up by the palisade layer. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**6.** Leaf A is from a plant growing in a hot, dry, sunny place. It has a thick waxy covering on its epidermis. Leaf B is from a plant in a damp, shady wood and has a very thin waxy covering. Suggest which leaf loses water more slowly, and explain why this suits its habitat. **[2]**

**7.**

**(a)** State where meristem tissue is found in a plant. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how meristem tissue allows a root to keep growing throughout the plant's life. **[2]**

**8.** Water vapour is lost through stomata. Explain why a leaf still needs stomata. **[2]**

**9.** A student's drawing of a leaf section uses sketchy, broken lines, has shaded areas, and has labels written on the drawing with no label lines. Give three improvements the student should make. **[3]**

**10.** Describe the structure of phloem and explain how it suits its function. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how the tissues of a leaf are adapted to carry out photosynthesis. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why a leaf is described as an organ but the palisade mesophyll is described as a tissue. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Palisade mesophyll [1]
**(b)** Xylem [1]
**(c)** Spongy mesophyll [1]
**(d)** Meristem [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Mesophyll" alone is not enough for (a) or (c); say palisade or spongy.

**2.** Upper epidermis, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, lower epidermis [1]
*Examiner insight:* All four must be in order for the mark.

**3.** Light can pass through the upper epidermis [1] to reach the chloroplasts in the palisade mesophyll below, for photosynthesis [1]
*Examiner insight:* The second mark needs the destination of the light and its use.

**4.** Xylem is made of hollow tubes [1], so water and mineral ions can flow through them from the roots to the stems and leaves [1]; the walls are strengthened by lignin, so the tubes do not collapse and help support the plant [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give each structural feature with its effect.

**5. (a)** 20 ÷ 80 = 0.25 mm [1]; 0.25 × 1000 = **250 µm** [1]
**(b)** 6 ÷ 20 × 100 [1] = **30%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the magnification cancels out, so you can use the image measurements directly.

**6.** Leaf A loses water more slowly, because its thick waxy covering reduces evaporation from the leaf surface [1]; this helps it survive where water is scarce, whereas Leaf B's damp habitat means water loss is less of a problem [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions reward linking the structure to the conditions described.

**7. (a)** At the growing tips of shoots and roots [1]
**(b)** Meristem cells keep dividing by mitosis, producing new cells [1]; these cells can differentiate into the different types of cell the root needs, throughout the plant's life [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mention both division and differentiation.

**8.** Stomata allow carbon dioxide to diffuse into the leaf for photosynthesis [1] and oxygen to diffuse out [1]
*Examiner insight:* Guard cells can close stomata to reduce water loss when gas exchange matters less, for example at night.

**9.** Any three: use clear, continuous lines [1]; do not shade [1]; use ruled label lines that touch the structures, with labels outside the drawing [1]; also accept: draw tissue layers in correct proportion, add a title or the magnification
*Examiner insight:* Each improvement must correct a fault described in the question or follow a standard drawing rule.

**10.** Phloem is made of tubes of elongated cells with pores in the end walls [1]; cell sap containing dissolved sugars can move from one cell to the next through the pores, carrying sugars from the leaves to the rest of the plant [1]
*Examiner insight:* The detailed structure of phloem is not required; the pores in the end walls are the key feature.

**11.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer links each tissue's structure to its role in photosynthesis):
- the upper epidermis is transparent, so light reaches the palisade layer [1]
- palisade cells are tall, packed near the upper surface and contain many chloroplasts, so they absorb a lot of light [1]
- stomata in the lower epidermis let carbon dioxide into the leaf [1]
- air spaces in the spongy mesophyll let carbon dioxide diffuse to the palisade cells and give a large surface area for gas exchange [1]
- xylem in the veins brings water to the mesophyll cells [1]
- phloem carries the sugars made away from the leaf [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a structure and its effect; a list of tissue names stays in the lowest level.

**12.** A leaf is made of several different tissues working together to carry out a function, photosynthesis [1]; the palisade mesophyll is a group of similar cells with a similar structure and function [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the definitions of tissue and organ in your answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "mesophyll" without palisade or spongy.
- Giving phloem lignin, or describing phloem in detail that is not required.
- Placing meristem in the leaf.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to micrometres.
- Naming tissues in a 6-mark answer without explaining their adaptations.
