[AQA GCSE History course structure guide](/resources/gcse-history-course-structure/).

> The specific sites in scope for the Historic Environment study change by examination series
> and depend on which British depth study your school has chosen. Always confirm the current
> sites with your teacher before revising — this page covers the *skills* the study tests, which
> stay the same regardless of which sites apply to you.

## What the study tests

The Historic Environment element links a set of specified historic sites to your chosen British
depth study, and assesses your ability to:

- Explain the **significance** of features of a site (why they mattered at the time).
- Explain how a site **reflects** wider historical events, developments or ways of life.
- Use **second-order concepts** — cause, consequence, change, continuity, significance — applied to a physical place, not just a written source.

## The skill, not the fact list

Because Historic Environment questions are source- and site-based rather than pure recall, the
core skill is **connecting a specific feature of the site to the bigger picture** of your depth
study — for example, explaining what a building's layout, location or design reveals about the
society that built or used it.

## A useful structure for site-based answers

```
FEATURE   -> what is physically true of this part of the site
CONTEXT   -> what was happening in this period more broadly
LINK      -> how the feature reflects, resulted from, or shaped that context
SIGNIFICANCE -> why this connection matters / what it tells historians
```

## Exam traps

- Describing a site feature without linking it to the historical period or depth study.
- Treating the Historic Environment as a separate topic to memorise in isolation, rather than
  connecting it to the same depth study content it's drawn from.
- Relying on a previous year's list of specified sites — always confirm the current list.
- Writing only about what a site looked like, without addressing *why* that matters historically.

## Self-test

1. What four second-order concepts commonly appear in Historic Environment questions?
2. Why is it risky to revise the Historic Environment as a standalone topic?
3. What should you always confirm before starting detailed revision of this element?
4. Complete the structure: Feature → Context → Link → ____?

**Answers:** 1. Cause, consequence, change and continuity (with significance also commonly tested). 2. Because the specified sites are directly tied to your chosen British depth study — revising them in isolation misses the connections examiners specifically test. 3. The current list of specified historic sites for your examination series, since AQA refreshes this periodically. 4. Significance — explaining why the connection between the feature and its historical context matters.
