These questions cover **Section Q: Kinematics (Q1 to Q5)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. The specification assesses Section Q on Paper 2. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² and give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise. In projectiles, i is horizontal and j vertically upwards.

## Questions

**1.** A particle starts at O and moves 9 m along a straight line in the positive direction to A. It then moves 15 m in the negative direction to B. The whole journey takes 12 s.

**(a)** State the displacement of B from O and the distance travelled. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average speed and the average velocity for the journey. **[2]**

**2.** A displacement–time graph for a particle moving in a straight line is made of straight segments joining (0, 0), (4, 12), (7, 12) and (13, 0), with t in seconds and s in metres.

**(a)** Find the velocity of the particle in each of the three stages. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the average speed over the 13 seconds. **[1]**

**3.** A train starts from rest at station A and accelerates uniformly for 40 s until its speed is V m s⁻¹. It travels at this speed for 180 s, then decelerates uniformly at 0.5 m s⁻² until it stops at station B. The distance from A to B is 4.4 km.

**(a)** Use a velocity–time graph to show that V² + 200V − 4400 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** Find V and the acceleration in the first stage. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the total time for the journey. **[1]**

**4.** Starting from v = u + at and s = ut + ½at², show that s = vt − ½at² for motion with constant acceleration. **[3]**

**5.** A cyclist rides along a straight road at a constant 8 m s⁻¹. She passes a parked car at a point P. Two seconds later the car sets off from rest after her, with constant acceleration 2 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the time, measured from when the cyclist passes P, at which the car draws level with her. **[4]**
**(b)** Find how far from P this happens. **[1]**

**6.** At time t = 0 a particle is at the point with position vector (2i + 3j) m and has velocity (4i − 6j) m s⁻¹. Its acceleration is constant at (−i + 2j) m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the velocity of the particle at time t seconds. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time at which the particle is moving parallel to i. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the distance of the particle from the origin at that time. **[3]**

**7.** A particle moves along a straight line. It starts at O, and its velocity at time t seconds is v = (t² − 7t + 10) m s⁻¹ for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6.

**(a)** Find the times at which the particle is instantaneously at rest. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the acceleration of the particle when t = 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the total distance travelled in the 6 seconds. **[4]**

**8.** A particle starts from rest at O and moves along a straight line. Its acceleration at time t seconds is a = (6√t − 2) m s⁻².

**(a)** Find an expression for the velocity at time t. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the value of t > 0 at which the particle is instantaneously at rest. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the displacement of the particle from O when t = 4. **[3]**

**9.** A particle P moves in a horizontal plane. At time t seconds its acceleration is (4i − 6t j) m s⁻². At t = 0, P is at the origin O with velocity (−2i + 9j) m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the velocity of P at time t. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the time at which P is moving parallel to j, and state its velocity then. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the position vector of P when t = 2. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the speed of P when t = 2. **[2]**

**10.** A ball is struck from a point A, 1.5 m above horizontal ground, with velocity (24i + 10j) m s⁻¹. A vertical wall 4 m high stands 36 m horizontally from A, across the ball's path. Model the ball as a particle.

**(a)** Show that the ball reaches the wall 1.5 s after being struck. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the ball passes over the wall, and find by how much it clears it. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the greatest height of the ball above the ground. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the speed of the ball and its direction of motion as it passes over the wall. **[3]**
**(e)** Suggest one refinement to the model. **[1]**

**11.** A particle is projected from a point on horizontal ground with speed U m s⁻¹ at angle α above the horizontal. It lands on the ground.

**(a)** Show that its time of flight is 2U sin α / g, and hence that its range is U² sin 2α / g. **[4]**
**(b)** U = 20. Find the two possible values of α for which the range is 30 m. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Displacement = 9 − 15 = **−6 m** [1]
Distance = 9 + 15 = **24 m** [1]
**(b)** Average speed = 24 ÷ 12 = **2 m s⁻¹** [1]
Average velocity = −6 ÷ 12 = **−0.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the sign is part of a displacement or velocity; "6 m" with no direction loses the accuracy mark.

**2. (a)** Gradient of each segment [1]
0 ≤ t ≤ 4: 12 ÷ 4 = **3 m s⁻¹**; 4 ≤ t ≤ 7: **0 (at rest)** [1]
7 ≤ t ≤ 13: (0 − 12) ÷ 6 = **−2 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Distance = 12 + 12 = 24 m, average speed = 24 ÷ 13 = **1.85 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the last stage has a negative gradient; writing +2 m s⁻¹ loses the accuracy mark.

**3. (a)** Deceleration time = V ÷ 0.5 = 2V seconds [1]
Area = ½(40)V + 180V + ½(2V)V = 200V + V² [1]
200V + V² = 4400, so V² + 200V − 4400 = 0 [1]
**(b)** (V − 20)(V + 220) = 0, V > 0, so **V = 20** [1]
Acceleration = 20 ÷ 40 = **0.5 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** 40 + 180 + 40 = **260 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* convert 4.4 km to 4400 m first; a "show that" earns nothing for a fudged last line.

**4.** From v = u + at, u = v − at [1]
s = (v − at)t + ½at² [1]
= vt − at² + ½at² = vt − ½at² [1]
*Examiner insight:* each substitution must be shown; quoting the result earns no marks.

**5. (a)** Cyclist: s = 8t [1]
Car (moving for t − 2 seconds): s = ½(2)(t − 2)² = (t − 2)² [1]
(t − 2)² = 8t gives t² − 12t + 4 = 0 [1]
t = 6 ± 4√2; t = 6 − 4√2 ≈ 0.343 is before the car starts, so **t = 6 + 4√2 = 11.7 s** [1]
**(b)** s = 8 × 11.657 = **93.3 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* reject the smaller root with a reason; choosing without comment usually costs the final mark.

**6. (a)** v = u + at [1]
= **(4 − t)i + (2t − 6)j m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Parallel to i when the j-component is 0: 2t − 6 = 0 [1]
**t = 3 s**; the i-component is then 1 ≠ 0 [1]
**(c)** r = (2i + 3j) + (4i − 6j)t + ½(−i + 2j)t² [1]
At t = 3: r = (2 + 12 − 4.5)i + (3 − 18 + 9)j = 9.5i − 6j [1]
Distance = √(9.5² + 6²) = √126.25 = **11.2 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* leaving out the starting position (2i + 3j) in (c) puts the particle at the wrong point.

**7. (a)** (t − 2)(t − 5) = 0 [1]
**t = 2 s and t = 5 s** [1]
**(b)** a = dv/dt = 2t − 7 [1]
At t = 4, **a = 1 m s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** x = t³/3 − 7t²/2 + 10t (x = 0 at t = 0) [1]
x(2) = 26/3, x(5) = 25/6, x(6) = 6 [1]
Distance = 26/3 + (26/3 − 25/6) + (6 − 25/6) [1]
= **15 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* integrating v from 0 to 6 in one go gives the displacement, 6 m, not the distance.

**8. (a)** v = ∫(6t^½ − 2) dt = 4t^(3/2) − 2t + c [1]
v = 0 at t = 0, so c = 0: **v = 4t^(3/2) − 2t** [1]
**(b)** 2t(2√t − 1) = 0 [1]
√t = ½, **t = 0.25 s** [1]
**(c)** x = ∫(4t^(3/2) − 2t) dt = (8/5)t^(5/2) − t² + k [1]
x = 0 at t = 0, so k = 0 [1]
x(4) = (8/5)(32) − 16 = **35.2 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* write √t as t^½ before integrating; a wrong power carries through every later part.

**9. (a)** Integrate each component: v = 4t i − 3t² j + c [1]
c = −2i + 9j, so **v = (4t − 2)i + (9 − 3t²)j m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** 4t − 2 = 0, **t = 0.5 s** [1]
**v = 8.25j m s⁻¹**, which is non-zero [1]
**(c)** r = (2t² − 2t)i + (9t − t³)j + d [1]
r = 0 at t = 0, so d = 0 [1]
**r = 4i + 10j** m when t = 2 [1]
**(d)** v = 6i − 3j [1]
Speed = √45 = **6.71 m s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the constant of integration is a vector; adding it to one component only loses the mark in (a).

**10. (a)** Horizontal velocity is constant: 36 ÷ 24 = 1.5 s [1]
**(b)** Vertical displacement: 10(1.5) − 4.9(1.5)² = 3.975 m above A [1]
Height above ground = 3.975 + 1.5 = 5.475 m, which is more than 4 m, so it clears the wall [1]
Clearance = 5.475 − 4 = **1.475 m** [1]
**(c)** 0 = 10² − 2(9.8)h, h = 5.10 m above A [1]
Greatest height = **6.60 m** above the ground [1]
**(d)** v = 24i + (10 − 9.8 × 1.5)j = 24i − 4.7j [1]
Speed = √(24² + 4.7²) = **24.5 m s⁻¹** [1]
Angle = tan⁻¹(4.7/24) = **11.1° below the horizontal** [1]
**(e)** Any one: **include air resistance; allow for the ball's size or spin** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b), compare with 4 m and state the conclusion; a height alone is incomplete.

**11. (a)** Vertically: 0 = (U sin α)T − ½gT² [1]
T ≠ 0, so T = 2U sin α / g [1]
R = (U cos α)T = 2U² sin α cos α / g [1]
Using sin 2α = 2 sin α cos α, R = U² sin 2α / g [1]
**(b)** 30 = 400 sin 2α / 9.8, so sin 2α = 0.735 [1]
2α = 47.3° or 132.7°, so **α = 23.7° or 66.3°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "two possible values" means you need the second solution of sin 2α = 0.735.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a distance when a displacement is asked for, or dropping its sign.
- Using suvat in questions 7, 8 or 9, where the acceleration depends on t.
- Taking upwards as positive and then using a = +9.8 m s⁻².
- Dropping or mishandling a constant of integration, especially a vector one.
- Giving only the speed when the velocity or direction of motion is asked for.
