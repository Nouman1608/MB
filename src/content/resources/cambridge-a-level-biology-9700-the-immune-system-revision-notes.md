These notes condense sub-topic **11.1 The immune system** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, assumed on Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [immune system study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-immune-system/); for exam-style questions, use the [immune system practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-immune-system-practice/).

## Specific and non-specific

| | Non-specific | Specific |
|---|---|---|
| Cells | Phagocytes (neutrophils, macrophages) | Lymphocytes (B and T) |
| Targets | Any pathogen | One antigen per lymphocyte type |
| Speed | Immediate | Slow first time; fast after |
| Memory | None | Memory cells |

## Phagocytosis in five steps

1. **Chemotaxis**: move towards chemicals from pathogens and damaged cells.
2. **Bind**: receptors on the phagocyte attach to the pathogen (faster if antibodies are attached).
3. **Engulf** by endocytosis into a **phagocytic vacuole**.
4. **Lysosomes** fuse and release **hydrolytic enzymes** that digest the pathogen.
5. Neutrophil dies; macrophage **presents antigens** on its surface.

| | Neutrophil | Macrophage |
|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | Lobed | Large, rounded or kidney-shaped |
| Arrives | First, in large numbers | Later |
| Lifespan | Short | Long |
| Extra role | — | Antigen-presenting cell |

## Antigens

- **Antigen**: molecule (usually protein or glycoprotein) recognised by the immune system that stimulates an immune response.
- **Self**: on the body's own cells; lymphocytes that would react to them are removed during development.
- **Non-self**: foreign: on pathogens, transplanted tissue, mismatched blood cells, toxins → trigger a response.

## Primary immune response: cell by cell

| Order | Cell | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Macrophage | Engulfs pathogen; presents antigen |
| 2 | T-helper | Complementary receptor binds antigen (**clonal selection**) |
| 3 | T-helper | Divides by **mitosis** (**clonal expansion**); releases **cytokines** |
| 4 | B-lymphocyte | Matching B cell (has bound antigen) stimulated by cytokines; divides by mitosis |
| 5 | Plasma cell | Differentiates from B cells; **secretes antibodies** |
| 6 | T-killer | Activated by cytokines; kills infected body cells showing the antigen |
| 7 | Memory B and T cells | Formed from the clones; remain long term |

**Why slow?** Few matching cells at the start; selection, many rounds of division and differentiation take days.

**Plasma cell adaptations**: much rough ER (make antibody protein), large Golgi (package for secretion), many mitochondria (ATP).

## Secondary response and long-term immunity

| Feature | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Starting cells | Few matching lymphocytes | Many memory cells |
| Delay before antibody rises | Several days | Short |
| Peak antibody | Lower | Much higher |
| Duration | Shorter | Longer |
| Symptoms | Usually | Usually none |

Memory cells: long-lived, numerous, already selected → quickly form plasma cells and T-killer cells → pathogen destroyed before symptoms → **long-term immunity**.

### Worked reminder: comparing peaks

Primary peak 12 units on day 14; secondary peak 300 units on day 5 after re-exposure.

```
ratio of peaks = 300 ÷ 12 = 25 times higher
time to peak: 14 days vs 5 days → 9 days sooner
```

## Must-know distinctions

- **Clonal selection vs clonal expansion**: choosing the matching lymphocytes vs those lymphocytes dividing by mitosis.
- **B cell vs plasma cell**: a B cell carries antibody-like receptors on its surface; a plasma cell, formed from it, secretes free antibody.
- **T-helper vs T-killer**: helper releases cytokines and kills nothing; killer destroys infected body cells.
- **Antigen vs antibody**: antigen is the molecule recognised; antibody is the protein made in response.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Phagocyte | White blood cell that engulfs and digests pathogens |
| Chemotaxis | Movement towards a chemical stimulus |
| Phagocytic vacuole | Vesicle containing an engulfed pathogen |
| Antigen-presenting cell | Cell displaying a pathogen's antigens on its surface |
| Clonal selection | Activation of only those lymphocytes with complementary receptors |
| Clonal expansion | Division of selected lymphocytes by mitosis |
| Cytokines | Signalling molecules released by T-helper cells |
| Plasma cell | Cell derived from a B cell that secretes antibodies |
| T-killer cell | Kills body cells displaying non-self antigens |
| Memory cell | Long-lived lymphocyte giving a fast secondary response |

## Exam traps

- Phagocytes are **non-specific**; lymphocytes do not engulf.
- **Plasma cells**, not B cells in general, secrete antibodies.
- T-killer cells kill **infected body cells**, not free bacteria.
- Memory cells **recognise**; they do not "remember".
- Cytokines must be named in how T-helper cells activate others.
- An antigen is a **molecule**, not the whole pathogen.

## Self-test

1. Name the two types of phagocyte in the syllabus.
2. What is chemotaxis?
3. Which organelles fuse with a phagocytic vacuole?
4. Define antigen.
5. Why do lymphocytes not normally attack self antigens?
6. Which cell presents antigens to T-helper cells?
7. What is meant by clonal selection?
8. What do T-helper cells release to activate B cells?
9. Which cell secretes antibodies?
10. What do T-killer cells attack?
11. Give two differences between the primary and secondary response.
12. Explain why a second infection by the same pathogen usually causes no symptoms.

### Answers

1. Neutrophils and macrophages.
2. Movement towards a higher concentration of a chemical.
3. Lysosomes.
4. A molecule recognised by the immune system that stimulates an immune response.
5. Lymphocytes with receptors for self antigens are destroyed during development.
6. A macrophage.
7. Only lymphocytes with receptors complementary to the antigen are activated.
8. Cytokines.
9. Plasma cells.
10. Body cells displaying non-self antigens, such as virus-infected cells.
11. Any two: secondary is faster; produces more antibody; lasts longer; usually no symptoms.
12. Memory cells quickly produce plasma cells and antibodies, destroying the pathogen before it multiplies enough to cause symptoms.
