This guide covers sub-topic **7.3 Physical digestion** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 6 are Core and can be tested on every paper. Statement 7, the role of bile in emulsifying fats and oils, is Supplement content, so it is **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4).

The [human nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition/) summarises the statements. This page teaches them in depth: a surface-area calculation that shows why breaking food up matters, how to tell the four tooth types apart, the tissues of a tooth and what each does, churning in the stomach, and how bile speeds up fat digestion without being an enzyme. ## Physical digestion: pieces, not new molecules

**Physical digestion** is the breakdown of food into smaller pieces **without chemical change to the food molecules**. When you chew bread, the pieces get smaller, but each piece still contains starch molecules. No bonds inside the molecules are broken. Breaking those bonds is chemical digestion, which needs enzymes.

Physical digestion matters because it **increases the surface area** of the food. Enzymes can only act on the molecules at the surface of a piece of food, so more surface means more molecules exposed to enzymes at once, and chemical digestion is faster.

### Worked example: how much extra surface?

A cube of cheese has sides of 2 cm. It is cut into 1 cm cubes, then each of those into 0.5 cm cubes. Calculate the total surface area at each stage.

| Stage | Number of cubes | Area of one face / cm² | Total surface area / cm² |
|---|---|---|---|
| One 2 cm cube | 1 | 2 × 2 = 4 | 1 × 6 × 4 = **24** |
| 1 cm cubes | 8 | 1 × 1 = 1 | 8 × 6 × 1 = **48** |
| 0.5 cm cubes | 64 | 0.5 × 0.5 = 0.25 | 64 × 6 × 0.25 = **96** |

The volume of cheese never changes (8 cm³), but each halving of the side doubles the surface area. Chewing and churning do the same thing to food.

## The four types of teeth

### Identifying them

Work along a jaw from the front to the back.

| Tooth | Position | Shape clue |
|---|---|---|
| Incisor | front | flat, sharp, chisel-shaped edge; one root |
| Canine | one each side, next to the incisors | single pointed cusp; one long root |
| Premolar | behind the canines | broader crown with two cusps |
| Molar | at the back | largest, broad crown with several cusps; usually two or three roots |

### Their functions in physical digestion

| Tooth | Function |
|---|---|
| Incisors | **biting** and **cutting** pieces of food |
| Canines | **gripping** and **tearing** food |
| Premolars | **chewing** and **grinding** food |
| Molars | **chewing** and **grinding** (crushing) food |

The broad, bumpy surfaces of premolars and molars meet as the jaw moves, so food is ground into small pieces with a large surface area. Chewing also mixes food with saliva, making it easier to swallow.

## Structure of a tooth

A tooth has a **crown** above the gum and a **root** embedded in the **jaw bone** and surrounded by the **gum**.

| Part | Where | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| Enamel | outer layer of the crown | very hard covering that resists wear during biting and chewing |
| Dentine | under the enamel, forming most of the tooth | hard living tissue, softer than enamel, supporting the crown |
| Pulp (pulp cavity) | centre of the tooth | soft tissue containing the nerves and blood vessels |
| Nerves | in the pulp | detect pain, pressure and temperature |
| Blood vessels | in the pulp | supply the living cells of the tooth with oxygen and nutrients |
| Cement | covering the root | helps fix the tooth into the jaw bone |

Two details are often tested. First, enamel covers only the crown; the root is covered by cement. Second, the nerves and blood vessels enter the pulp through a small opening at the tip of the root.

### Annotated model answer

**Describe the structure of a molar tooth.** **[4]**

> The crown is covered by a hard layer of enamel [1]. Under the enamel is dentine, which forms most of the tooth [1]. In the centre is the pulp, containing nerves and blood vessels [1]. The roots are covered in cement and are embedded in the jaw bone and gums [1].

Each sentence names a tissue **and** where it is.

## The stomach in physical digestion

The stomach wall contains layers of **muscle**. These contract to **churn** the food, squeezing and mixing it with gastric juice. Churning breaks the food into smaller pieces, increasing its surface area for the enzymes in gastric juice, and turns it into a thick liquid that can pass into the duodenum. This is physical digestion; the protease in gastric juice is the chemical digestion happening alongside it.

## Bile and emulsification (Extended only)

Fats and oils do not mix with water. In the duodenum they would form large droplets with a small surface area, so lipase could only reach the fat molecules on the outside of each droplet.

**Bile** (made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder) **emulsifies** fats and oils: it breaks large droplets into many tiny droplets. This **increases the surface area** for lipase to act on, so fats are chemically digested faster.

Emulsification is physical digestion because the fat molecules are not changed: they are simply split among smaller droplets. Bile contains **no enzymes**.

### Worked example: interpreting a bile experiment

Three tubes contain milk with an alkaline pink indicator that turns colourless when fatty acids are produced. The times below are for practice only.

| Tube | Added | Time for pink colour to disappear |
|---|---|---|
| A | lipase | 9 minutes |
| B | lipase and bile | 4 minutes |
| C | bile and water | no change after 20 minutes |

- Tube C shows that bile alone does not digest fat: it produces no fatty acids, because it contains no enzyme.
- Tube B is faster than tube A: bile emulsified the fat into smaller droplets, giving a larger surface area for lipase.
- Fatty acids are made by lipase in A and B, which is why the colour changes.

## Common mistakes

- Saying physical digestion "breaks down molecules". It breaks food into smaller pieces; the molecules are unchanged.
- Saying chewing "increases the rate of absorption". It increases surface area for **enzymes**.
- Giving canines a grinding role, or molars a cutting role.
- Writing that the pulp is "where the nerve is" without mentioning blood vessels.
- Putting enamel on the root, or calling cement "the gum".
- Describing the stomach's physical role as "producing acid". Acid is chemical; churning is physical.
- (Extended) Calling bile an enzyme, or saying bile "breaks down fat into fatty acids and glycerol".

## Exam technique for 7.3

- **Describe** physical digestion: "smaller pieces" + "no chemical change to the molecules".
- **State** why it matters: "increases the surface area for enzymes".
- **Identify** teeth from a diagram: use position first, then shape.
- **Describe** tooth structure: name each tissue and its position.
- **Outline** (Extended) the role of bile: emulsifies → smaller droplets → larger surface area → faster digestion by lipase.
