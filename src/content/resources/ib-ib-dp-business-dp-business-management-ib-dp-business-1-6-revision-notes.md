These notes condense section 1.6 (Multinational companies (MNCs)) of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024, for SL and HL. The content point is the impact of MNCs on the host countries, assessed at AO3, so every answer needs a judgement. Worked examples are in the [1.6 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-business-dp-business-management-ib-dp-business-1-6/), and exam-style questions in the [1.6 practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-business-dp-business-management-ib-dp-business-1-6-practice/). Sections 1.5 and 1.6 are also summarised in the [growth and evolution and MNCs revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-growth-and-evolution-and-multinational-companies-mncs-revision-notes/). Definitions are standard textbook forms; figures are invented, in dollars.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Multinational company (MNC) | A business with operations or production facilities in more than one country |
| Host country | A country where the MNC operates, other than its home country |
| Home country | The country where the MNC is based |
| Repatriated profit | Profit sent from the host country back to the home country |
| Spillover | A benefit that spreads beyond the MNC, such as skills or technology |
| Supplier linkages | Spending by the MNC on local suppliers, creating further jobs |
| Local content | The share of an MNC's inputs bought in the host country |
| Crowding out | Local firms losing customers to the MNC and shrinking or closing |
| Footloose | Able to relocate easily when costs or incentives change |
| Incentive | A benefit a government offers to attract an MNC, such as a tax holiday or infrastructure |

## Impacts by stakeholder

| Stakeholder | Gains | Losses |
|---|---|---|
| Workers | Jobs, wages, training | Poor conditions; job losses if the MNC leaves |
| Local suppliers | Large orders, higher standards | Dependence on one buyer |
| Local competitors | Pressure to improve; trained staff | Lost customers; closure |
| Consumers | Choice, prices, quality | Less choice later if rivals close |
| Government | Tax, exports, investment | Cost of incentives; MNC influence |
| Community and environment | Infrastructure, projects | Pollution, land and water use, cultural change |

## Spillovers

- **Technology transfer**: local firms adopt better methods.
- **Skills**: trained staff move to local firms or start businesses.
- **Supplier linkages**: local purchases multiply the jobs created.

Bigger when local content is high and local staff hold skilled roles; smaller when inputs are imported and local jobs are low-skilled.

## Following the money

```
Kept in the host country = wages + local purchases + tax + reinvested profit
Leaving = repatriated profit (+ payments for imported inputs)
After-tax profit = profit × (1 − tax rate)
Repatriated = after-tax profit × share sent home
```

*Reminder.* Profit 10m, tax 20%: after-tax profit 8m. If 75% is sent home, 6m leaves and 2m is reinvested.

## Net jobs

Net jobs = jobs created by the MNC (and its local suppliers) − jobs lost in local firms that are crowded out. Then consider job **quality**: skill, pay, security.

## Time horizon

- Short run: construction spending, incentive costs, sudden competition.
- Long run: lasting jobs, tax once incentives end, skills spreading, local firms improving or closing.
- Risk: a footloose MNC may leave when a tax holiday or contract ends.

## What shapes the size of the impact

- Strength and enforcement of the host country's laws.
- Level of local content.
- Type of activity: research and design versus simple assembly.
- Host government's bargaining power.
- Share of profit reinvested; profit shifting to lower-tax countries reduces host tax.

## Evaluation method

1. Identify impacts (STEEPLE as a checklist).
2. Sort them by stakeholder.
3. Measure with the case figures.
4. Consider the time horizon.
5. Judge and give a condition.

## Exam traps

- A list with no judgement.
- Counting jobs created but not jobs lost.
- Treating repatriated profit as proof the MNC brings nothing.
- Ignoring the risk that the MNC relocates.
- Writing about the home country instead of the host country.
- Generic points not tied to the case.

## Quick self-test

1. Define host country.
2. Define repatriated profit.
3. An MNC makes a profit of 30m, pays tax at 25% and sends 60% of after-tax profit home. How much is sent home?
4. In question 3, how much after-tax profit is reinvested in the host country?
5. An MNC creates 400 jobs; 90 jobs are lost at local rivals. Find the net change in jobs.
6. Explain one way an MNC can transfer technology to a host country.
7. Why does a high level of local content increase the benefit to the host country?
8. Define crowding out.
9. What does it mean to say an MNC is footloose, and why does it matter to a host country?
10. Give one reason the tax a host government receives may be lower than expected.
11. Give one environmental cost an MNC may impose on a host country.
12. State two factors a judgement on an MNC's impact should depend on.

### Answers

1. A country, other than its home country, in which an MNC operates.
2. Profit sent from the host country back to the MNC's home country.
3. After-tax profit 30m × 0.75 = 22.5m; sent home 22.5m × 0.6 = **13.5m**.
4. 22.5m − 13.5m = **9.0m**.
5. 400 − 90 = **+310 jobs**.
6. For example: local suppliers must learn new production methods to meet the MNC's quality standards, and then use them for other customers.
7. More of the MNC's spending goes to local firms, which creates further jobs and income in the host country.
8. Local firms losing customers to an MNC, because of its lower costs or stronger brand, and shrinking or closing as a result.
9. It can relocate easily when costs rise or incentives end, so jobs and tax revenue may not last.
10. For example: a tax holiday offered as an incentive, or the MNC recording profit in a lower-tax country.
11. For example: pollution of rivers or air, or heavy use of local water.
12. Any two: enforcement of laws, the share of inputs bought locally, the skill level of jobs, how long the MNC stays, the share of profit reinvested.
