These notes condense section 3.1.5 (Gas exchange and the transport of oxygen in living organisms) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610) specification, Version 5.1, in Unit 1. For explanations and worked data, use the [gas exchange study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-gas-exchange-and-oxygen-transport/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-biology-9610-gas-exchange-and-oxygen-transport-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Surface area to volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume; falls as size increases |
| Mass transport | Bulk movement of substances over long distances in a transport system |
| Spiracle | Opening on an insect's body surface where air enters |
| Trachea (insect) | Branching air tube; the smallest branches are tracheoles |
| Stomata | Pores in a leaf, with guard cells, that exchange gases |
| Xerophyte | A plant adapted to reduce water loss in dry conditions |
| Alveolus | Air sac of the lung; the gas exchange surface |
| Tidal volume | The volume of air in one breath |
| Ventilation rate | Breaths per minute |
| FVC and FEV1 | Forced vital capacity; volume forced out in the first second |
| Haem | The iron-containing group in each globin chain |
| Affinity | How readily haemoglobin binds oxygen |

## Surface area to volume

- Surface area supplies; volume demands.
- Doubling the side of a cube multiplies the surface area by 4 and the volume by 8, so the ratio halves: 6, 3, 1.5, 0.75.
- Small organisms: diffusion alone is enough. Large organisms need body-shape adaptations, exchange surfaces and mass transport.

## Features of any good exchange surface

1. Large surface area (folds, many alveoli, many mesophyll cells).
2. Thin (one cell thick).
3. Steep gradient (ventilation, blood flow).
4. Moist so gases can dissolve.

## Three systems in one table

| | Single-celled | Insect | Dicot leaf |
|---|---|---|---|
| Route | Direct diffusion across membrane | Spiracles, tracheae, tracheoles to cells | Stomata, air spaces, mesophyll cells |
| Why it works | High ratio, short distance | Air-filled tubes reach cells; no blood needed | Large internal surface; thin leaf |
| Water loss compromise | Not a problem | Close spiracles | Close stomata |

**Xerophyte features:** thick cuticle; sunken stomata; rolled leaves; hairs; reduced leaf area. All reduce water loss and also reduce carbon dioxide uptake.

## Human gas exchange

Path: trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli.

**Alveolus adaptations:** one-cell-thick epithelium; one-cell-thick capillary wall; very many alveoli; dense capillary network keeps gradients steep; ventilation.

| | Inspiration | Expiration (quiet) |
|---|---|---|
| Diaphragm | Contracts, flattens | Relaxes, rises |
| External intercostals | Contract | Relax |
| Ribs | Move up and out | Move down and in |
| Thoracic volume | Increases | Decreases |
| Pressure | Falls below atmospheric | Rises above atmospheric |

## Lung data

- Pulmonary ventilation = tidal volume x breathing rate. Example: 0.5 dm3 x 15 = 7.5 dm3 per minute.
- FEV1 / FVC x 100: healthy about 80 %.
- Fibrosis: lungs less elastic, FVC low, ratio stays normal or high.
- Asthma and similar airway narrowing: FEV1 low, ratio low.
- Reduced gas exchange: thickened walls (a longer diffusion distance), or fewer alveoli (a smaller surface area).

## Circulation: names to know

Coronary arteries; aorta; vena cava; pulmonary artery; pulmonary vein; hepatic artery; hepatic vein; hepatic portal vein. Arteries carry blood away from the heart, capillaries exchange substances, veins return blood.

## Haemoglobin

- Four polypeptide chains, each with a haem group; each haem binds one oxygen, so up to four per molecule.
- S-shaped dissociation curve: binding one oxygen makes the next easier.
- High partial pressure of oxygen (lungs): near-saturated, loads oxygen.
- Low partial pressure (tissues): unloads oxygen.
- **Bohr effect:** more carbon dioxide shifts the curve to the right, so more oxygen is released in active tissue.
- **Left shift** = higher affinity: loads at lower partial pressure of oxygen. Suits a fetus, high altitude or burrowing animals.
- **Right shift** = lower affinity: unloads easily. Suits animals with a high metabolic rate.

## Exam traps

- "Bigger organisms have less surface area." No: less surface area in proportion to volume.
- Insect tracheae do not carry oxygen in blood.
- "Air is sucked in." Pressure in the thorax falls, and higher outside pressure pushes air in.
- Left shift and right shift reversed.
- Xerophytes do have stomata. They are positioned to reduce water loss.
- A lung disease answer must link the changed structure (thickening, scarring, narrowing) to a changed measurement.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube of side 4 cm.
2. State two ways that organisms overcome a small surface area to volume ratio.
3. Name the openings on the surface of an insect through which air enters.
4. Explain why open stomata create a problem for a plant in a dry climate.
5. Give two features of the alveolus that speed up diffusion.
6. Describe the movement of the diaphragm and ribs during inspiration.
7. Calculate the pulmonary ventilation of a person with a tidal volume of 0.45 dm3 and a rate of 12 breaths per minute.
8. A patient has an FVC of 3.5 dm3 and an FEV1 of 1.6 dm3. Calculate FEV1 / FVC as a percentage.
9. What is the effect of a high partial pressure of carbon dioxide on the dissociation curve?
10. Why is a fetal haemoglobin curve to the left of adult haemoglobin?
11. Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the gut to the liver.
12. State two xerophytic features of a leaf.

### Answers

1. Surface area 96, volume 64, ratio 1.5 : 1.
2. Any two from flattened or thin body shape, specialised exchange surfaces, mass transport system.
3. Spiracles.
4. Water vapour is lost by evaporation through the stomata, down a water potential gradient.
5. Any two from thin walls, large total surface area, dense capillary network, ventilation.
6. The diaphragm contracts and flattens; the ribs move up and out.
7. 0.45 x 12 = 5.4 dm3 per minute.
8. 1.6 / 3.5 x 100 = 45.7 %.
9. It shifts to the right, so haemoglobin releases more oxygen.
10. Fetal haemoglobin has a higher affinity for oxygen, so it can load oxygen from the mother's blood.
11. Hepatic portal vein.
12. Any two from thick cuticle, sunken stomata, rolled leaf, hairs, reduced leaf area.
