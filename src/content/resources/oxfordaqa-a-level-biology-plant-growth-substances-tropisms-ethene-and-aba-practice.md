## Questions

**1.** State three features of plant growth substances. **[3]**

**2.** **(a)** Define a tropism. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the response of a root to gravity and state its direction. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest an advantage of this response to the plant. **[1]**

**3.** A young pea plant is placed horizontally in a dark cupboard. Explain why the tip of its shoot turns upwards. **[5]**

**4.** Cress seedlings were grown for 24 hours with different concentrations of indoleacetic acid (IAA). Mean growth was measured.

| IAA (µmol dm⁻³) | 0 | 0.0002 | 0.002 | 0.02 | 0.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Root growth (mm) | 20.0 | 21.0 | 16.0 | 9.0 | 3.0 |
| Shoot growth (mm) | 10.0 | 11.0 | 14.0 | 17.0 | 18.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in root growth at 0.02 µmol dm⁻³ compared with the control. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the effect of IAA on shoot growth. **[2]**
**(c)** Contrast the effect of higher IAA concentrations on roots and shoots. **[2]**
**(d)** Use the data to explain why a horizontal root curves downwards. **[3]**

**5.** A shoot was lit from one side for 6 hours. The shaded side elongated by 3.6 mm and the lit side by 2.4 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of elongation of each side. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate by what percentage the shaded-side elongation exceeded the lit-side elongation. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the difference in elongation arises. **[3]**

**6.** A student investigates the effect of light direction on the growth of seedling shoots (Required practical 10).

**(a)** Describe a method that would give valid results. **[5]**
**(b)** Angles of curvature for five seedlings were 12°, 15°, 18°, 15° and 10°. Calculate the mean. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the purpose of a control group in even light. **[1]**
**(d)** State one limitation of the investigation. **[1]**

**7.** Explain how bananas can be picked green, shipped and then be ripe when they reach the shops. **[4]**

**8.** Green mangoes took 10 days to ripen in open air. A matched batch sealed in a bag with one ripe banana took 4 days.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage reduction in ripening time. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the result. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the bag was sealed. **[1]**

**9.** Explain how abscisic acid (ABA) causes stomata to close in a plant whose roots are deficient in water. **[5]**

**10.** Five leaves had stomatal apertures (µm) of 6.2, 5.8, 6.6, 6.0 and 6.4. After ABA was applied, the same leaves measured 2.4, 2.0, 2.8, 2.2 and 2.6. Mass lost in 2 hours by a leaf fell from 0.84 g to 0.36 g.

**(a)** Calculate the mean aperture before and after ABA. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in mean aperture. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage decrease in water loss. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain the link between your answers to (b) and (c). **[2]**

**11.** Water loss and stomatal aperture were measured in a wild-type plant and a mutant that makes very little ABA.

| Plant and treatment | Mean aperture (µm) | Water loss (g per hour) |
|---|---|---|
| Wild type, well watered | 7.0 | 0.50 |
| Wild type, water-stressed | 2.0 | 0.15 |
| Mutant, water-stressed | 6.5 | 0.46 |
| Mutant, water-stressed, sprayed with ABA | 2.2 | 0.16 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in aperture of the wild type when water-stressed. **[2]**
**(b)** Compare the response of the mutant with that of the wild type. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what the spraying result shows. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why the mutant is at a disadvantage in dry soil. **[2]**
**(e)** Suggest one cost to the wild type of closing its stomata. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** They are signal molecules produced in plants [1]; present in very low concentrations [1]; they act on target cells close to where they are made, or travel to act in other tissues [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" asks for one clear point each, so listing three short separate features is safer than one long sentence.

**2. (a)** A growth response to a directional stimulus [1].
**(b)** Positive gravitropism [1]; growth downwards [1].
**(c)** It carries the root into soil, where water and mineral ions are available (or anchors the plant) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the name and the direction; "gravitropism" alone does not say whether it is positive or negative.

**3.** IAA collects on the lower side of the horizontal shoot [1]. In a shoot a higher IAA concentration stimulates cell elongation [1]. The cells underneath lengthen more than those above [1]. The tip turns upwards [1]. This is negative gravitropism [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A chain from distribution to concentration to elongation to bending earns the marks; "IAA makes it grow up" skips the chain.

**4. (a)** (9.0 - 20.0) / 20.0 × 100 [1] = **-55 %** [1].
**(b)** Shoot growth rises as IAA concentration rises [1], from 10.0 mm to 18.0 mm, with the rise levelling off at the highest concentrations [1].
**(c)** Higher IAA concentrations inhibit root growth [1] but stimulate shoot growth [1].
**(d)** The lower side of a horizontal root has the higher IAA concentration [1], which inhibits elongation there [1]. The upper cells elongate more, so the root curves down [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and the division by the control value, so that a slip can be traced; keep the minus sign, because it shows you understand inhibition.

**5. (a)** Shaded: 3.6 / 6 = **0.60 mm per hour** [1]. Lit: 2.4 / 6 = **0.40 mm per hour** [1].
**(b)** (3.6 - 2.4) / 2.4 × 100 = **50 %** [1].
**(c)** There is more IAA on the shaded side [1], which stimulates cell elongation in the shoot [1], so the shaded cells lengthen more and the shoot tip turns to face the light [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the lit-side value when the question says the shaded side "exceeded" it; dividing by the larger figure gives a different percentage.

**6. (a)** Use seedlings of the same species, age and size [1]. Light one group from one side only [1]. Keep temperature, water supply and light intensity the same [1]. After a set time, read the angle of curvature with a protractor on each seedling or photograph [1]. Use many seedlings and calculate a mean and range [1].
**(b)** (12 + 15 + 18 + 15 + 10) / 5 = 70 / 5 = **14°** [1].
**(c)** It shows the seedlings do not curve without a directional stimulus [1].
**(d)** For example, seedlings grow at different rates, so results vary [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Fair test" is too vague; name the variable you control and say how you would measure the response.

**7.** Green bananas are firmer and are damaged less in transport [1]. Ethene is a growth substance that stimulates ripening [1], so its level is limited while the fruit is shipped [1]. At the destination the bananas are exposed to ethene so that the batch ripens together [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the practice to the role of ethene; "they ripen on the ship" alone does not explain control.

**8. (a)** (10 - 4) / 10 × 100 [1] = **60 %** [1].
**(b)** The ripe banana releases ethene [1], which stimulates ripening of the mangoes [1].
**(c)** To keep the ethene gas around the fruit rather than letting it escape [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* State that the source is the ripe banana and the effect is on the mango; a one-word "ethene" gives no explanation.

**9.** ABA acts on the guard cells [1]. It stimulates K⁺ and Cl⁻ ions to move out of the guard cells [1]. The water potential in the guard cells rises [1]. Water then moves out by osmosis [1]. The guard cells shrink and the stomata close [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The order matters: ions leave first and water follows. Reversing them contradicts the sequence.

**10. (a)** Before: 31.0 / 5 = **6.2 µm** [1]. After: 12.0 / 5 = **2.4 µm** [1].
**(b)** (6.2 - 2.4) / 6.2 × 100 = 3.8 / 6.2 × 100 [1] = **61.3 %** [1].
**(c)** (0.84 - 0.36) / 0.84 × 100 = **57.1 %** [1].
**(d)** Smaller apertures mean less water vapour leaves by transpiration [1], so less mass is lost [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Both percentages are of the starting value; quote both with units or the percent sign and compare them in words.

**11. (a)** (7.0 - 2.0) / 7.0 × 100 [1] = **71.4 %** [1].
**(b)** The wild type closed its stomata (7.0 to 2.0 µm) [1], but the mutant's aperture barely changed (7.0 to 6.5 µm) [1].
**(c)** ABA restored closure (6.5 to 2.2 µm) [1], so the mutant fails to close because it lacks ABA [1].
**(d)** Its stomata stay open, so it loses water by transpiration [1] and may wilt as the soil dries [1].
**(e)** Less carbon dioxide enters the leaf [1], so photosynthesis may fall [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* In a compare question, quote figures for both plants; a statement about one plant only gains limited credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a hormone effect without the organ: the effect of higher IAA concentration is opposite in roots and shoots.
- Omitting which side of the root or shoot has the higher IAA concentration.
- Using the new value rather than the control or starting value as the base of a percentage.
- Writing "ethene makes fruit ripe" without the link to green picking and artificial ripening.
- Reversing ions and water in the ABA sequence.
- Not saying that water potential in the guard cells rises when ions leave.
- Leaving out a control or the number of replicates in Required practical 10 answers.
- Rounding too early in multi-step calculations; round only the final value.
