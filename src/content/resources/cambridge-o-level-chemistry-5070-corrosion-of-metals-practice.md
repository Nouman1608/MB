## Section A: short questions

**1.** Give the chemical name of rust. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** substances that must be present for iron to rust. **[2]**

**3.** Which method protects iron by sacrificial protection?

A: painting it

B: coating it with plastic

C: attaching a block of magnesium to it

D: covering it with grease

**[1]**

**4.** Name **three** barrier methods used to prevent rusting. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why aluminium objects corrode but are never described as rusting. **[1]**

**6.** Write the half-equation for zinc atoms losing electrons. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student sets up three test tubes, each containing an iron nail.

- Tube 1: nail in tap water, tube open to the air.
- Tube 2: nail in water that has been boiled, with a layer of oil on top, tube stoppered.
- Tube 3: nail in air with anhydrous calcium chloride, tube stoppered.

**(a)** State in which tube(s) the nail rusts. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the water in tube 2 is boiled. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the purpose of the oil layer in tube 2. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain the purpose of the anhydrous calcium chloride in tube 3. **[1]**

**(e)** State the conclusion that can be drawn from the three tubes. **[1]**

**8.** Suggest the most suitable barrier method for each object, and give a reason.

**(a)** the chain of a bicycle **[2]**

**(b)** a steel bridge **[2]**

**(c)** a wire fence around a garden **[2]**

**9.** Ships' steel hulls sometimes have blocks of zinc attached below the waterline.

**(a)** Name this method of rust prevention. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the zinc protects the steel, referring to the reactivity series and to electrons. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why the zinc blocks must be replaced regularly. **[1]**

**10.** Steel dustbins are often galvanised.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by galvanising. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how galvanising protects the steel while the zinc layer is complete. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the steel still does not rust if the zinc layer is scratched. **[2]**

**11.** A car body is painted. After a stone chips the paint, a rust spot appears.

**(a)** Explain why rust forms at the chip. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the rust spot grows even under the surrounding paint. **[1]**

**12.** A student suggests coating iron with copper to stop it rusting, "because copper does not rust".

**(a)** Explain how a complete copper coating would protect the iron. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why, once the copper coating is scratched, the iron is not protected. **[2]**

**(c)** Name a metal that would give sacrificial protection to iron instead. **[1]**

**13.** Rusting can be summarised by a word equation.

**(a)** Write the word equation for the rusting of iron. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why rusting is an oxidation reaction. Give **two** reasons. **[2]**

**(c)** State the oxidation number of iron in iron(III) oxide. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Hydrated iron(III) oxide [1].

**2.** Water [1]; oxygen [1].

**3.** C [1].

**4.** Painting [1]; greasing / oiling [1]; coating with plastic [1].

**5.** Rusting is the term used only for iron and steel [1].

**6.** Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1].

**7. (a)** Tube 1 only [1].

**(b)** To remove dissolved oxygen / air [1].

**(c)** To stop oxygen from the air dissolving back into the water [1].

**(d)** It is a drying agent that absorbs water vapour, so the air is dry [1].

**(e)** Both water and oxygen are needed for rusting [1].

**8. (a)** Greasing / oiling [1]; the chain moves, so paint or plastic would be scraped off, and grease also lubricates it [1].

**(b)** Painting [1]; it covers a large area and can be renewed [1].

**(c)** Coating with plastic [1]; it gives a tough, waterproof layer on thin wire kept outdoors [1].

*Marking note:* galvanising is also accepted for (b) and (c), because a zinc coating is a barrier as well as sacrificial protection; the reason mark still needs a property of the method, such as keeping out oxygen and water, suiting a large structure, or surviving outdoor handling. A reason that only repeats "it stops rusting" does not score.

**9. (a)** Sacrificial protection [1].

**(b)** Zinc is more reactive than iron [1]; zinc loses electrons in preference to iron / Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ [1]; so zinc corrodes instead, and the iron does not lose electrons / rust [1].

**(c)** The zinc is used up as it corrodes [1].

**10. (a)** Coating iron or steel with zinc [1].

**(b)** The zinc acts as a barrier, excluding oxygen and water [1].

**(c)** Zinc is more reactive than iron [1], so it corrodes / loses electrons in preference to the exposed iron [1].

**11. (a)** The chip exposes the iron [1] to oxygen and water, the conditions for rusting [1].

**(b)** Rust is flaky and lets water and oxygen spread under the paint / the barrier only protects where it is intact [1].

**12. (a)** It acts as a barrier, keeping oxygen and water away from the iron [1].

**(b)** Copper is less reactive than iron [1]; so the iron loses electrons in preference to copper and rusts / copper cannot act sacrificially [1].

**(c)** Zinc or magnesium [1].

**13. (a)** iron + oxygen + water → hydrated iron(III) oxide [1].

**(b)** Iron gains oxygen [1]; iron atoms lose electrons / the oxidation number of iron increases (from 0 to +3) [1].

**(c)** +3 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "iron oxide" or "iron(II) oxide" for rust.
- Answering "air" instead of "oxygen and water".
- Describing galvanising as a barrier only.
- Saying zinc protects because it is unreactive.
- Giving "fair test" as the purpose of an experimental step.
