**These notes condense The Soviet Empire in Eastern Europe, 1953-91, the first topic of the Paper 4 International option in Cambridge 9489.** For explanation see the [Soviet Empire study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-soviet-empire-eastern-europe/); for essays and short questions see the [Soviet Empire practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-soviet-empire-eastern-europe-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 7.1 How did the Soviet Union's relationship with the satellite states develop in the period up to 1968?
- 7.2 Why did the period 1964-85 become characterised by political and economic stagnation?
- 7.3 How did Gorbachev try to respond to the crisis in the Soviet Union?
- 7.4 How and why did Soviet control of Eastern Europe collapse in 1991?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Satellite state | Nominally independent state under Soviet control |
| Warsaw Pact | Soviet-led military alliance, 1955-91 |
| COMECON | Soviet bloc economic organisation, founded 1949 |
| Stasi | East German Ministry for State Security |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | 1968 claim that a threat to socialism in one state concerned all socialist states |
| Nomenklatura | List of posts filled by party approval, and the privileged officials who held them |
| Gerontocracy | Rule by old men, as under Brezhnev, Andropov and Chernenko |
| Stagnation | The slowing of growth and reform under Brezhnev |
| Second Cold War | Renewed tension after Afghanistan, c.1979-85 |
| Glasnost | Openness: freer discussion and media |
| Perestroika | Restructuring of the economy and later the political system |
| Demokratizatsiya | Democratisation: contested elections within, and later beyond, the party |
| Sinatra Doctrine | Gorbachev's policy of letting satellites choose their own path |
| Solidarity | Polish independent trade union founded in 1980 |

## 7.1 Crises to 1968 -- compare them

| Crisis | Cause | Soviet response | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| GDR 1953 | Higher work norms | Tanks | Uprising crushed |
| Poland 1956 | De-Stalinisation, Poznań | Negotiation | Gomułka accepted |
| Hungary 1956 | Nagy's multi-party plans, Pact withdrawal | Invasion | Kádár installed; Nagy executed |
| Berlin 1961 | Mass emigration | Backed the Wall | Border sealed |
| Czechoslovakia 1968 | Dubček's reforms | Warsaw Pact invasion | Husák; normalisation |

**Pattern:** reform tolerated within limits; leaving the Pact or ending the party's monopoly brought force.

## 7.2 Stagnation -- five causes

1. **Leadership:** stability of cadres, ageing leaders.
2. **Corruption:** nomenklatura privileges and regional scandals.
3. **Economy:** diluted reforms, missed 10th Plan targets, technology gap, grain imports.
4. **Defence:** arms race costs; Afghanistan.
5. **Satellites:** Western debt, Helsinki rights, the Church and dissidents.

## 7.3 Gorbachev -- aims and outcomes

| Policy | Aim | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Perestroika | Efficient socialist economy | Disrupted planning without a market; shortages |
| Glasnost | Expose corruption, win support | Criticism of the party and the past; nationalism |
| Demokratizatsiya | Revitalise the party | Rivals such as Yeltsin gained a platform |
| New thinking abroad | Cut defence costs | INF Treaty; end of the Second Cold War |

## 7.4 Collapse -- the chain

- **Poland first:** Solidarity (1980), martial law (1981), re-legalisation and elections (1989).
- **Sinatra Doctrine:** no Soviet tanks in 1989.
- **Domino of 1989:** Hungary, the GDR and the Wall, Czechoslovakia, Romania.
- **USSR:** Baltic and other nationalisms, conservative resistance, the August 1991 coup, Yeltsin, independence of republics, resignation.

## Exam traps

- **1989 and 1991:** the satellites broke free in 1989; the USSR itself ended in 1991.
- **Brezhnev Doctrine:** announced after the invasion, as justification.
- **Gorbachev's aim:** to save socialism through reform, not to dissolve it.
- **Poland 1956 versus Hungary 1956:** different responses because Gomułka kept Poland in the Pact.
- **Martial law:** imposed by Jaruzelski, not by Soviet troops.

## Self-test

1. What triggered the East German uprising of 1953? *Increased work norms.*
2. Why did Khrushchev accept Gomułka in 1956? *Poland remained communist and in the Warsaw Pact.*
3. What did Nagy announce on 1 November 1956? *Hungary's withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact.*
4. When was the Berlin Wall begun? *13 August 1961.*
5. Which Warsaw Pact member refused to join the 1968 invasion? *Romania.*
6. What did the 10th Five Year Plan emphasise? *Quality and efficiency.*
7. Name Brezhnev's two successors before Gorbachev. *Andropov and Chernenko.*
8. What was Charter 77? *A Czechoslovak human rights group and declaration.*
9. Who led the Gdańsk strike of 1980? *Lech Wałęsa.*
10. Which disaster in 1986 widened glasnost? *Chernobyl.*
11. What was the Sinatra Doctrine? *Gorbachev's acceptance that satellite states could go their own way.*
12. What happened in August 1991? *Hardliners attempted a coup against Gorbachev, which failed within three days.*
