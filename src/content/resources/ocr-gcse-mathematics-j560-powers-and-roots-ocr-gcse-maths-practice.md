## Answers

**1. (a)** **2³ × 7²** [1]

**(b)** **64** [1]

**(c)** **6** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 4³ means 4 × 4 × 4, not 4 × 3.

**2. (a)** **3⁴** [1]

**(b)** **2⁷** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Counting up the powers of 2 (2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64, 128) shows 128 is the seventh.

**3. (a)** **1/25** [1]

**(b)** **5/2** [1]

**(c)** **1** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The index −1 just gives the reciprocal, so a fraction flips.

**4. (a)** **7/8** [1]

**(b)** **0.3** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* √0.09 is not 0.03: 0.03² = 0.0009.

**5. (a)** **6⁷** [1]

**(b)** **11⁻²** [1]

**(c)** **3¹⁰** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (b) gives a negative index because the second index is larger. 1/11² is also acceptable.

**6.** **x = 14 or x = −14** [1] **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Both roots are needed: (−14)² is also 196.

**7.** √169 = 13 and ∛(−8) = −2 [1]; 13 − (−2) = 15 [1]; 15 + 1/2 = **15 1/2** (15.5) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Subtracting a negative cube root adds 2. 2⁻¹ is one half, not −2.

**8. (a)** 16³ = (2⁴)³ = 2¹² and 8 = 2³ [1]; **2¹⁵** [1]

**(b)** 25³ = (5²)³ = 5⁶ [1]; 5⁶ ÷ 5⁴ = 5² = **25** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Change every number to the same base before applying a law of indices.

**9. (a)** 1/81 = 3⁻⁴, so **x = −4** [1]

**(b)** 32 = 2⁵ [1]; x − 1 = 5, so **x = 6** [1]

**(c)** 9ˣ = (3²)ˣ = 3²ˣ [1]; 2x = 8, so **x = 4** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Once the bases match, the indices must be equal. Write that line out.

**10. (a)** 5⁻² = 1/25, which is positive [1]

**(b)** She multiplied the bases; the base stays 2 and the indices add [1]; 2⁷ = **128** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 4⁷ = 16 384, far more than 2³ × 2⁴ = 8 × 16 = 128, which shows the claim cannot be right.

**11. (a)** ∛64 = **4** [1]

**(b)** ⁵√32 = 2 [1]; 2³ = **8** [1]

**(c)** (27/8)^(2/3) [1]; (3/2)² = **9/4** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* For a negative fractional index, flip the fraction first, then take the root, then the power.

**12. (a)** **3^(3/2)** [1]

**(b)** 3² × 3^(3/2) = **3^(7/2)** [1]

**(c)** (3³)ˣ = 3², so 3x = 2 [1]; **x = 2/3** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Hence" in part (b) means use 3^(3/2) from part (a) and add the indices.

**13. (a)** 8² = 64 and 9² = 81, and 8.5² = 72.25 < 75 [1]; **9** [1]

**(b)** ≈ √64 = **8** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 75 is between 64 and 81, but closer to 81 once you test the midpoint, so 9 is nearer than 8.

**14. (a)** √0.36 = **0.6 m** [1]

**(b)** ∛343 = **7 cm** [1]

**(c)** ∛0.064 = **0.4 m** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check part (c): 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.064. A cube root of a number with three decimal places has one.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Multiplying the base by the index.
- Giving a negative value for a negative index.
- Missing the negative solution of x² = k.
- Combining different bases into one power.
- Multiplying indices when they should be added, or the other way round.
- (Higher tier only) Forgetting the reciprocal in a negative fractional index.
- (Higher tier only) Rounding an estimate without checking which perfect square is closer.
- Taking a square root of a decimal and misplacing the point.
