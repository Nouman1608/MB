Cambridge IGCSE Accounting (0452) teaches the principles and practice
of recording financial transactions, preparing financial statements
and interpreting what they show. It begins with the purpose of
accounting and the accounting equation and builds up to the accounts
of sole traders, partnerships, limited companies, clubs and
manufacturers. Under the 2027-2029 syllabus, the qualification is
assessed by two papers: Paper 1 Multiple Choice (1 hour 30 minutes, 40
marks, 30%) and Paper 2 Structured Written Paper (1 hour 45 minutes,
100 marks, 70%).

## How the course is organised

The syllabus has seven topic areas. They follow a natural path from
recording, through checking and adjusting, to reporting and
interpreting.

**Recording**

- *The fundamentals of accounting:* the purpose of accounting and the
  accounting equation.
- *Sources and recording of data:* the double entry system of
  book-keeping, business documents and books of prime entry.

**Checking and adjusting**

- *Verification of accounting records:* the trial balance, corrections
  of errors, bank reconciliation and control accounts.
- *Accounting procedures:* capital and revenue expenditure and
  receipts; depreciation and disposal of non-current assets; other
  payables and other receivables; irrecoverable debts and the
  allowance for irrecoverable debts; and valuation of inventory.

**Reporting and interpreting**

- *Preparation of financial statements:* sole traders, partnerships,
  limited companies, manufacturing accounts, clubs and societies, and
  incomplete records.
- *Analysis and interpretation:* calculating, understanding and
  interpreting accounting ratios; inter-business comparison;
  interested parties; and the limitations of accounting statements.
- *Accounting concepts and modern practice:* accounting concepts,
  ethical considerations, and technology and sustainability.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and not tiered, so all candidates sit the
same two papers at the end of the course.

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 1 hour 30 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Structured Written Paper | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 70% |

In Paper 1 you answer all 40 multiple-choice questions. Paper 2 has
five compulsory questions of 20 marks each. Both papers are externally
assessed, and calculators are permitted in both. The 2027-2029 syllabus
is examined in 2027, 2028 and 2029, in June and November, and in March
in India.

If you are sitting the exam in November 2026, you are on the previous
edition of the syllabus, in which Paper 1 lasted 1 hour 15 minutes and
was worth 35 marks. November 2026 is the final series for that edition.

## How the parts fit together

Accounting is unusually cumulative. Every financial statement you
prepare later in the course depends on double entry and the
accounting equation from the start, and every ratio you calculate
depends on statements that have been prepared correctly. Errors early
in the chain cannot be fixed at the end.

The checking and adjusting topics are the bridge between recording
and reporting. The trial balance, corrections of errors, bank
reconciliation and control accounts confirm that the records are
reliable. Depreciation, irrecoverable debts, accruals and prepayments
(other payables and other receivables) and inventory valuation then
adjust those records so that the statements give a fair picture.

Analysis and interpretation draws the course together: once the
statements are prepared, you use ratios and comparisons to judge
performance and explain the results to interested parties, while
recognising the limitations of the statements themselves. Accounting
concepts sit behind all of this, because they explain why each
adjustment is made, and the material on ethics, technology and
sustainability places the subject in a modern business context.

The two papers test the same content in different ways. Paper 1 rewards
quick, accurate recall and short calculations across the whole
syllabus. Paper 2 asks you to work through longer structured problems,
setting out accounts and calculations clearly and adding brief written
explanations.

## Planning your study

1. **Begin with the fundamentals.** The purpose of accounting, the
   accounting equation and double entry come first, followed by
   business documents and books of prime entry.
2. **Move to verification.** The trial balance and corrections of errors
   follow naturally, with bank reconciliation and control accounts
   soon after.
3. **Prepare sole trader statements early.** Simple income statements
   and statements of financial position give purpose to the
   adjustments that follow.
4. **Add the accounting procedures.** Depreciation, irrecoverable debts,
   other payables and receivables and inventory valuation, each applied
   to sole trader statements as you learn it.
5. **Extend to other organisations.** Partnerships, limited companies,
   manufacturing accounts, clubs and societies and incomplete records.
6. **Finish with analysis and concepts.** Ratios, interpretation,
   concepts, ethics, and technology and sustainability, revisiting
   earlier topics as you go.

Practise multiple-choice and structured questions on each topic as soon
as it is taught. Full papers and the paper-by-paper exam preparation
page are most useful in the final months of the course, once all seven
topic areas are complete.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452?**
There are two. Paper 1 Multiple Choice is 1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks and 30%; Paper 2 Structured Written Paper is 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and 70%.

**Is Cambridge IGCSE Accounting tiered?**
No. The qualification is not tiered, so all candidates take the same two papers.

**Can I use a calculator in IGCSE Accounting?**
Yes. Calculators are permitted in both Paper 1 and Paper 2.

**What is in Paper 2?**
Paper 2 is a structured written paper with five compulsory questions, each worth 20 marks.

**What changed from the 2026 syllabus?**
In the 2026 edition, Paper 1 lasted 1 hour 15 minutes and was worth 35 marks. The 2027-2029 Paper 1 lasts 1 hour 30 minutes and is worth 40 marks. November 2026 is the last series for the older edition.
