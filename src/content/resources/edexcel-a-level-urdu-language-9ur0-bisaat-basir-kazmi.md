This guide is for students writing a Paper 2 Section B essay on **بساط** (*Bisaat*), the play by **باصر کاظمی** (Basir Sultan Kazmi) listed in Appendix 1 of the Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0) specification with the date 1987. It is the only play on the list, so this guide focuses on **what is different about writing an essay on drama**, and on a method you can apply to every scene. The [Bisaat revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-bisaat-revision-notes/) explain the meanings of the title, drama vocabulary and how to build a one-page note sheet. Practise with the [Bisaat practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-bisaat-basir-kazmi-practice/).

This page does not summarise the play: your evidence must come from your own reading of the text. Prescribed works can change between series, so check the current specification.

## How the essay is marked

You choose one of two questions on the play, set in Urdu, and answer in Urdu with no text and no dictionary. The specification suggests about 320–370 words, though the whole essay is marked. Three grids apply:

- **Critical and analytical response (AO4), 20 marks**: convincing interpretations justified with evidence from the work, logical arguments and linked conclusions.
- **Range of grammatical structures and vocabulary (AO3), 15 marks**: including terminology appropriate to literary analysis.
- **Accuracy of language (AO3), 10 marks.**

Responses should relate to the specification's five features: **form and technique of presentation**, **key themes, concepts and issues**, **characterisation**, **structure of the plot**, and **social and cultural setting**.

## What makes a play different

A novel can tell the reader what a character thinks. A play usually cannot: it **shows**. Almost everything reaches the audience through:

- **What characters say** (**مکالمہ**) and how they say it.
- **What they do** on stage: entrances, exits, gestures, silences.
- **Stage directions** (**اسٹیج کی ہدایات**): the playwright's notes on setting, objects, movement and tone.
- **The arrangement of scenes** (**مناظر کی ترتیب**): what we see first, what is kept from us, where the turning point comes.

So in a drama essay, the form and technique feature is about **how meaning is staged**. An examiner looking for analysis wants to see you discuss lines, actions and scenes as choices the playwright made for an audience.

## A scene-analysis method

Apply these six questions to each key scene you prepare. Write the answers in Urdu.

1. **کون، کہاں، کب؟** Who is on stage, where, and at what point in the play?
2. **مقصد** What does each character want in this scene?
3. **رکاوٹ** What, or who, stands in the way?
4. **مکالمے کا انداز** How do they speak: formally or informally, directly or evasively, at length or in short exchanges? What does this reveal?
5. **تبدیلی** What has changed by the end of the scene: a relationship, a decision, the balance of power, what the audience knows?
6. **ربط** How does the scene connect to a theme of the play and to the title?

Prepare four or five scenes this way: one near the beginning, the main turning point, the climax and the ending, plus one you find revealing. Learn a few words of dialogue from each, accurately.

## Using the title as a lens

The revision notes list the meanings of **بساط**: a spread cloth or carpet, a **chessboard**, and figuratively one's means or capacity. A title with several meanings gives you a ready-made line of argument, but only if each meaning is tested against the play. For each meaning, ask:

- Is there a scene where this meaning becomes visible, through an object, a situation, or a line?
- Which characters seem to act as players, and which as pieces moved by others?
- Does the play suggest that characters act **within their means** (**اپنی بساط کے مطابق**) or beyond them?

Then decide which meaning the play develops most fully, and say why. An essay that simply lists three meanings without evidence will stay in the middle bands of the AO4 grid.

## Worked example: an annotated plan

Question (original): **«ڈرامے "بساط" کے کردار اپنی مرضی کے مالک کم اور حالات کے مہرے زیادہ ہیں۔» اس بیان کا جائزہ لیجیے۔**

Translation: "The characters of *Bisaat* are less masters of their own will and more pawns of circumstance." Evaluate this statement.

**Key words:** **مرضی کے مالک** (masters of their own will) against **حالات کے مہرے** (pawns of circumstance). **مہرہ** is a chess piece, so the question deliberately draws on one meaning of the title.

**Plan:**

1. **Introduction**: playwright, form, and position (for example, partly agree: most characters are constrained, but at least one makes a real choice).
2. **Paragraph on constraint**: a scene where a character's choices are limited by family, society or another character; analyse the dialogue.
3. **Paragraph on agency**: a scene where a character resists or decides; show how the staging marks the moment.
4. **Paragraph on technique**: how the title and the structure of the play reinforce the chess image.
5. **Conclusion**: a judgement on how far the statement is true.

**Model introduction (with a gap for your own judgement):**

> باصر کاظمی کا ڈراما "بساط" اپنے عنوان ہی سے شطرنج کی بساط کی یاد دلاتا ہے، جہاں مہرے اپنی مرضی سے نہیں بلکہ کھیلنے والے کے اشارے پر چلتے ہیں۔ یہ سوال اسی تصور سے جڑا ہے کہ کیا ڈرامے کے کردار بھی حالات کے ہاتھوں مجبور ہیں۔ میرے خیال میں یہ بیان بڑی حد تک درست ہے، تاہم ... [the character or moment that complicates the statement]۔

(Basir Kazmi's play *Bisaat* recalls, by its very title, the chessboard, where pieces move not by their own will but at the player's signal. This question is linked to that very idea: are the play's characters also compelled by circumstance? In my view the statement is largely true; however ...)

**Annotation:** the introduction links title, question and position in three sentences; **اپنے عنوان ہی سے** uses emphatic ہی; **نہیں بلکہ** sets up a contrast; **تاہم** prepares the qualification that an evaluative answer needs. The gap must be filled with your own reading.

| داخلہ / خروج | entrance / exit |

## Common mistakes

- Writing about the play as if it were a novel, with no reference to dialogue, staging or audience.
- Interpreting the title without evidence from scenes.
- Retelling the story in order.
- Confusing the playwright with his father, the poet Nasir Kazmi.
- Pairing two films in the paper; a play and a film is allowed, two films is not.

## Exam technique

- Choose the question whose key word you can support with at least three prepared scenes.
- In every paragraph, name a scene, quote or closely describe a moment, analyse **how** it is staged, and link it to the question.
- Use the audience as a point of reference: **ناظرین کو اس لمحے احساس ہوتا ہے کہ**, **اس منظر میں ڈرامائی تناؤ اپنے عروج پر ہے**.
- Vary your structures with passives (**دکھایا گیا ہے**), conditionals and concessive clauses.
