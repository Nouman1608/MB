## Questions

**1.** A cube-shaped model organism has sides of 10 mm. Calculate its surface area, its volume and its surface area : volume ratio. **[3]**

**2.** A second model organism is a cube with sides of 20 mm. Its surface area is 2400 mm² and its volume is 8000 mm³.

**(a)** Calculate its surface area : volume ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare this ratio with your answer to question 1. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain what this tells you about oxygen supply in a very large organism. **[2]**

**3.** Two blocks of tissue each have a volume of 60 mm³. Block X is a flat slab measuring 20 mm by 6 mm by 0.5 mm. Block Y measures 6 mm by 5 mm by 2 mm. Calculate the surface area : volume ratio of each block to 2 significant figures, and state which shape is better suited to gas exchange across its surface. **[4]**

**4.** Cubes of agar containing an indicator are left in dilute acid. After a fixed time the acid has reached 3 mm in from every face of each cube. Calculate the percentage of the volume reached by the acid in a 10 mm cube and in a 20 mm cube, then explain what the results show. **[5]**

**5.** Name one substance in each case.

**(a)** A gas that diffuses from the water into the blood in a fish's gills. **[1]**
**(b)** A waste substance carried in blood plasma from the liver to the kidneys. **[1]**
**(c)** A dissolved food molecule absorbed from the human small intestine. **[1]**
**(d)** A mineral ion taken up by plant roots and used to make amino acids. **[1]**

**6.** Blood leaves the right ventricle of the heart. Describe the route it takes until it arrives at the left ventricle. **[4]**

**7.** Explain why mammals have a double circulatory system. **[4]**

**8.** This question is about the human heart.

**(a)** Name the vessel that brings oxygenated blood to the left atrium. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the valve that lets blood pass from the left atrium into the chamber below it. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the vessel that carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart. **[1]**
**(d)** Name the vessels that supply the cardiac muscle with oxygen. **[1]**
**(e)** The muscle in the wall of the left ventricle is much thicker than in the right ventricle. Explain the reason. **[2]**

**9.** An artery has a wall 1.5 mm thick and a lumen 3.0 mm across. A vein has a wall 0.2 mm thick and a lumen 5.0 mm across.

**(a)** Calculate wall thickness ÷ lumen width for each vessel. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the structure of the artery wall suits its function. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the vein contains valves. **[1]**

**10.** Data for one body: the aorta has a cross-sectional area of about 4 cm², while all the capillaries together present about 4000 cm².

**(a)** Calculate how many times larger the capillary area is. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why blood moves slowly through the capillaries and why this is useful. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** Explain how two features of a red blood cell help it to transport oxygen. **[4]**
**(b)** Name three substances, other than blood cells, carried in blood plasma. **[3]**

**12.** A student counts her pulse. At rest she counts 35 beats in 30 seconds. After exercise she counts 29 beats in 15 seconds.

**(a)** Calculate her heart rate at rest, in beats per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate her heart rate after exercise in beats per minute. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why her heart rate rises during exercise. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Surface area = 6 × 10 × 10 = 600 mm² [1]. Volume = 10 × 10 × 10 = 1000 mm³ [1]. Ratio = 600 ÷ 1000 = **0.6 : 1** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show surface area and volume before dividing. A bare ratio leaves no working to give credit to if there is a slip, and area and volume should carry units.

**2. (a)** 2400 ÷ 8000 = **0.3 : 1** [1].
**(b)** The ratio has halved (0.6 to 0.3) when the side doubled [1].
**(c)** In a large organism the surface area is small compared with the volume [1], and the distance from the surface to inner cells is long, so diffusion alone is too slow to supply them [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer to (c) ties the ratio to diffusion distance. "It is bigger so it needs more oxygen" does not explain the need for exchange surfaces.

**3.** Block X surface area = 2(120 + 3 + 10) = 266 mm² [1]; ratio = 266 ÷ 60 = **4.4 : 1** [1]. Block Y surface area = 2(30 + 12 + 10) = 104 mm²; ratio = 104 ÷ 60 = **1.7 : 1** [1]. Block X, the flat slab, is better because the larger ratio and short distance from surface to centre give faster diffusion [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Round only at the end, to the figures the question asks for. Giving the final answer without the ratio for both blocks leaves the comparison unsupported.

**4.** 10 mm cube: the unreached core is 10 − 6 = 4 mm across, so its volume is 4 × 4 × 4 = 64 mm³ [1]. Reached = (1000 − 64) ÷ 1000 × 100 = **93.6%** [1]. 20 mm cube: core is 14 mm across, so 14 × 14 × 14 = 2744 mm³ [1]. Reached = (8000 − 2744) ÷ 8000 × 100 = **65.7%** [1]. The smaller cube is reached more completely in the same time, because its larger surface area : volume ratio and shorter diffusion distance help the acid reach the centre; this models why large organisms need transport systems [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A common slip is to subtract 3 mm, not 6 mm, to find the core, because the acid enters from both faces. Show the core width.

**5. (a)** **Oxygen** [1]. **(b)** **Urea** [1]. **(c)** **Glucose** (or an amino acid) [1]. **(d)** **Nitrate** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a named substance. "Food" or "minerals" is too vague to credit, and carbon dioxide would not answer (a).

**6.** The blood goes from the right ventricle into the pulmonary artery [1]. It passes through capillaries around the alveoli in the lungs, where it picks up oxygen and loses carbon dioxide [1]. It returns in the pulmonary vein [1] to the left atrium, and then passes to the left ventricle [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the vessels and chambers in order. Writing that the blood "goes to the lungs and back" does not show the route.

**7.** Blood loses pressure as it passes through the narrow capillaries of the lungs [1]. Returning it to the heart lets it be pumped again at high pressure [1] so it reaches the body quickly [1]. It also keeps oxygenated blood apart from deoxygenated blood [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The idea of pressure loss in the lung capillaries is the key point. Saying "blood goes round twice" restates the question.

**8. (a)** **Pulmonary vein** [1]. **(b)** **Bicuspid valve** (mitral valve) [1]. **(c)** **Pulmonary artery** [1]. **(d)** **Coronary arteries** [1]. **(e)** The left ventricle sends blood round the whole body [1], needing a higher pressure than the short trip to the lungs, so more muscle is needed [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Thicker to pump harder" is incomplete. Say where each ventricle sends blood.

**9. (a)** Artery: 1.5 ÷ 3.0 = **0.5** [1]. Vein: 0.2 ÷ 5.0 = **0.04** [1].
**(b)** The thick wall contains muscle and elastic fibres which stretch as blood surges through [1] and recoil, so the wall withstands high pressure and helps move blood on [1].
**(c)** Venous blood is under low pressure, so valves are needed to prevent it flowing backwards [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Do not write that the artery is "stronger" without saying what makes it so. Name the elastic fibres and link them to pressure.

**10. (a)** 4000 ÷ 4 = **1000 times** [1].
**(b)** The same flow of blood is spread across a very large total area [1], so it moves slowly even though each capillary is narrow [1]. This gives time for oxygen and food molecules to diffuse out and wastes to diffuse in [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Examiners look for total cross-sectional area. A reply based on narrow diameter alone points the wrong way, since narrow tubes by themselves would not slow the blood.

**11. (a)** Biconcave shape gives a large surface area for fast diffusion of oxygen [1] and flexibility to squeeze through capillaries [1]. No nucleus leaves more room for haemoglobin [1], which binds oxygen at the lungs and gives it up in the tissues [1]. (Any two features with their explanations earn full marks.)
**(b)** Any three from: carbon dioxide, glucose, amino acids, mineral ions, urea, hormones [3]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* For each feature, give the feature and then its effect. Naming a feature alone shows recall, so the explanation is what answers "how does it help".

**12. (a)** 35 × 2 = **70 beats per minute** [1][1]. **(b)** 29 × 4 = **116 beats per minute** [1][1]. **(c)** Muscles respire faster during exercise [1] and need more oxygen and glucose [1]. The heart beats faster to pump blood to them more quickly [1] and carry carbon dioxide away to the lungs for removal [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert to a full minute first. Comparing 35 and 29 directly gives the wrong impression, because the times differ.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the surface area : volume ratio rises as an organism gets bigger.
- Giving a ratio with no surface area or volume shown.
- Mixing up which pulmonary vessel carries oxygenated blood.
- Writing "valve" without naming bicuspid, tricuspid or semilunar.
- Saying the left ventricle is thicker "because it is stronger" with no reference to the body.
- Explaining slow capillary flow by narrowness alone.
- Naming a red blood cell feature with no effect on function.
- Omitting units or the time conversion in rate questions.
