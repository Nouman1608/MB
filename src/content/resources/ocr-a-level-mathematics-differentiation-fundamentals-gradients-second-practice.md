## Questions

**1.** Use differentiation from first principles to show that the derivative of f(x) = 2x³ + x is 6x² + 1. **[4]**

**2.** The curve y = 2x² − x passes through A(1, 1). The point B on the curve has x-coordinate 1 + h.

**(a)** Find the gradient of the chord AB, in terms of h, in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Use your answer to find the gradient of the curve at A, and say what happens to B in the limit. **[1]**

**3.** Work this out by hand.

**(a)** Find dy/dx when y = 6x^(5/2) − 4/x³ + 9/√x. **[3]**
**(b)** Find g'(x) when g(x) = (2x − 1)(x + 4)/x. **[3]**

**4.** **A Level only.** Differentiate f(x) = 5e^(−3x) + 2 × 7^(2x) + 4 tan(x/2) + 6 ln x. **[4]**

**5.** **A Level only.** Given y = 3 sin 2x − 8 cos(x/2), with x in radians:

**(a)** find dy/dx **[2]**
**(b)** find d²y/dx². **[2]**

**6.** A cubic curve y = f(x) has a positive coefficient of x³, a local maximum at x = −2 and a local minimum at x = 4.

**(a)** Describe the graph of y = f'(x), giving its shape and where it meets the x-axis. **[2]**
**(b)** State the x-coordinate of the lowest point of y = f'(x), with a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** State the value of f''(x) at that point. **[1]**

**7.** **A Level only.** f(x) = x⁴ − 6x² + x.

**(a)** Find f''(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the intervals on which f is convex and on which it is concave. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that f has points of inflection at x = 1 and x = −1, and give their coordinates. **[2]**

**8.** **A Level only.** A cup of tea cools so that its temperature after t minutes is T = 20 + 30e^(−t/4) degrees Celsius.

**(a)** Find dT/dt. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the rate of change of temperature when t = 2, to 3 significant figures, with units. **[1]**
**(c)** Find d²T/dt². **[1]**
**(d)** Explain what the sign of d²T/dt² tells you about the cooling. **[1]**

**9.** **A Level only.** Prove from first principles that the derivative of cos x is −sin x. You may use that, in radians, sin h / h → 1 and (cos h − 1)/h → 0 as h → 0. **[5]**

**10.** **A Level only.** g(x) = e^(2x) − 4e^x.

**(a)** Find g'(x) and g''(x). **[2]**
**(b)** Show that g''(x) = 0 only when x = 0. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine where g is concave and where it is convex, and give the coordinates of the point of inflection. **[4]**
**(d)** Find the gradient of the curve at the point of inflection. **[1]**

**11.** **A Level only.** The height of the tide above a marker is h = 6 + 2.5 sin(πt/6) metres, where t is in hours and the angle is in radians.

**(a)** Find dh/dt. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact rate of change at t = 2, then give it to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Find d²h/dt². **[2]**
**(d)** At t = 3, state dh/dt, and use d²h/dt² to say what the height is doing, giving the height. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** f(x + h) − f(x) = 2(3x²h + 3xh² + h³) + h [1]. Divide by h: 6x² + 6xh + 2h² + 1 [1]. Write the limit as h → 0 [1]. The terms in h vanish, so f'(x) = **6x² + 1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" answer needs the expansion and the limit visible. Writing only the final 6x² + 1 shows none of the reasoning.

**2. (a)** y at B = 2(1 + h)² − (1 + h) = 1 + 3h + 2h² [1]. Gradient AB = (3h + 2h²)/h = **3 + 2h** [1]
**(b)** As h → 0, B moves along the curve towards A and the gradient is **3** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Cancel the h before taking the limit. Putting h = 0 into the unsimplified fraction gives 0/0, which tells you nothing about the gradient.

**3. (a)** 15x^(3/2) [1]; +12x⁻⁴ [1]; −(9/2)x^(−3/2) [1]
**(b)** Expand the numerator: 2x² + 7x − 4 [1]. Divide by x: g(x) = 2x + 7 − 4x⁻¹ [1]. g'(x) = **2 + 4/x²** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Rewrite 4/x³ as 4x⁻³ before differentiating. Applying the power rule to the fraction as printed is a common source of lost marks.

**4.** −15e^(−3x) [1]; 4 ln 7 × 7^(2x) [1]; 2 sec²(x/2) [1]; 6/x [1]. So f'(x) = **−15e^(−3x) + 4 ln 7 · 7^(2x) + 2 sec²(x/2) + 6/x** **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The factor ln 7 and the factor 2 are separate pieces of working. Show both so a slip on one does not hide the other.

**5. (a)** dy/dx = 6 cos 2x [1] + 4 sin(x/2) [1]
**(b)** d²y/dx² = −12 sin 2x [1] + 2 cos(x/2) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The minus in front of the cosine term combines with the minus from differentiating cos, giving a plus. Write the sign of each term explicitly.

**6. (a)** The graph is a parabola (a quadratic) [1] that is U-shaped and meets the x-axis at x = −2 and x = 4 [1]
**(b)** The lowest point is at x = **1**, midway between the roots, since a parabola is symmetrical [1]
**(c)** f''(1) = **0**, as the gradient of f' is zero at its lowest point [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the shape and both intercepts. A sketch with no scale or labels cannot show where the roots lie.

**7. (a)** f'(x) = 4x³ − 12x + 1 [1]; f''(x) = **12x² − 12** [1]
**(b)** f'' = 12(x − 1)(x + 1) > 0 for x < −1 and x > 1, so f is **convex** there [1]. f'' < 0 for −1 < x < 1, so f is **concave** there [1]
**(c)** f'' = 0 at x = ±1 and its sign changes at each, so both are points of inflection [1]. f(1) = −4 and f(−1) = −6, so the points are **(1, −4)** and **(−1, −6)** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that f'' changes sign. Showing only f'' = 0 does not distinguish an inflection from, say, y = x⁴ at the origin.

**8. (a)** dT/dt = 30 × (−1/4)e^(−t/4) [1] = **−7.5e^(−t/4)** [1]
**(b)** At t = 2, dT/dt = −7.5e^(−0.5) = **−4.55 °C per minute** [1]
**(c)** d²T/dt² = **1.875e^(−t/4)** [1]
**(d)** It is positive, so the rate of change is increasing from a negative value towards 0: the tea cools more slowly as time passes [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give units in (b), and say in (d) what the sign means for the situation. A bare "it is positive" earns less than a sentence in context.

**9.** The quotient is [cos(x + h) − cos x]/h, and cos(x + h) = cos x cos h − sin x sin h [1]. The numerator becomes cos x (cos h − 1) − sin x sin h [1]. Divide by h: cos x · (cos h − 1)/h − sin x · (sin h / h) [1]. As h → 0, the first fraction tends to 0 and the second to 1 [1]. So the derivative is **−sin x** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the limits you use and keep the lim notation until the final line. A jump straight from the expansion to the answer is unlikely to show the reasoning a proof needs.

**10. (a)** g'(x) = **2e^(2x) − 4e^x** [1]; g''(x) = **4e^(2x) − 4e^x** [1]
**(b)** g'' = 4e^x(e^x − 1) [1]. Since e^x > 0, g'' = 0 only when e^x = 1, so x = **0** [1]
**(c)** For x < 0, e^x < 1 so g'' < 0: **concave** [1]. For x > 0, g'' > 0: **convex** [1]. The sign changes at x = 0, so it is a point of inflection [1]. g(0) = 1 − 4 = −3, so the point is **(0, −3)** [1]
**(d)** g'(0) = 2 − 4 = **−2** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Factorise g'' so that the sign on each side of x = 0 is easy to state. Remember e^x is never zero, which is why the single root is enough.

**11. (a)** dh/dt = 2.5 × (π/6) cos(πt/6) [1] = **(5π/12) cos(πt/6)** [1]
**(b)** At t = 2: (5π/12) cos(π/3) = **5π/24** m/h [1] = **0.654** m/h [1]
**(c)** d²h/dt² = (5π/12)(−π/6) sin(πt/6) [1] = **−(5π²/72) sin(πt/6)** [1]
**(d)** At t = 3, cos(π/2) = 0, so dh/dt = **0** [1]. d²h/dt² = −5π²/72 < 0, so the gradient is decreasing: the height is at a **maximum**, 6 + 2.5 = **8.5 m** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the exact value alongside the decimal, and keep the angle in radians throughout. The factor π/6 appears once in the first derivative and again in the second.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Skipping the expansion in a first-principles answer, or dropping the limit notation.
- Putting h = 0 before cancelling the h.
- Differentiating a fraction or root without first writing it as a power.
- Missing ln a in the derivative of a^kx, or the factor k in an exponential or trigonometric derivative.
- Sign errors on cos kx and on the second derivative of sin kx.
- Calling a point an inflection from f'' = 0 without testing the sign either side.
- Describing f' as having the shape of f, rather than one degree lower.
- Giving a rate of change with no units, or an interpretation with no context.
- Using degrees when the standard results need radians.
