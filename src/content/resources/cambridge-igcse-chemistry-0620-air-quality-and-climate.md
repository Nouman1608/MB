## Clean, dry air

Clean, dry air is approximately:

- **78 % nitrogen**, N₂;
- **21 % oxygen**, O₂;
- the remainder a mixture of **noble gases** (mainly argon) and **carbon dioxide**, CO₂.

"Dry" matters because the amount of water vapour in air varies, so it is left out of the standard composition.

### Worked example: finding the percentage of oxygen

A common experiment passes a measured volume of air backwards and forwards over heated copper. The copper reacts with the oxygen (2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO, turning black), so the volume of gas falls.

*The readings below are invented for practice.*

```
volume of air at start             = 100 cm³
volume of gas at end (after cooling) = 79 cm³
volume of oxygen removed           = 100 − 79 = 21 cm³
percentage of oxygen               = 21 ÷ 100 × 100 = 21 %
```

The gas is allowed to cool before the final reading because hot gas has a larger volume.

## Pollutants: sources and effects

Grouping the pollutants by where they come from makes them easier to learn.

| Group | Pollutant | Source | Adverse effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Burning carbon fuels | carbon dioxide | **complete** combustion of carbon-containing fuels | higher levels lead to increased global warming, which leads to climate change |
| | carbon monoxide | **incomplete** combustion of carbon-containing fuels | toxic gas |
| | particulates | **incomplete** combustion of carbon-containing fuels | increased risk of respiratory problems and cancer |
| Biological | methane | decomposition of vegetation; waste gases from digestion in animals | higher levels lead to increased global warming, which leads to climate change |
| High temperature in engines | oxides of nitrogen | car engines | acid rain, photochemical smog and respiratory problems |
| Sulfur in fuel | sulfur dioxide | combustion of fossil fuels which contain sulfur compounds | acid rain |

**Complete or incomplete?** The same fuel can give different pollutants. With plenty of oxygen, the carbon in the fuel becomes carbon dioxide. With too little oxygen, some becomes carbon monoxide or carbon particles (soot). For example, methane burning:

```
complete:     CH4 + 2O2 → CO2 + 2H2O
incomplete:   2CH4 + 3O2 → 2CO + 4H2O
```

**Linking effects to pollutants.** Two pollutants cause acid rain (sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen); two cause global warming (carbon dioxide and methane). Carbon monoxide is the one that is toxic; particulates are the one linked to cancer.

## Strategies and the chemistry behind them

The syllabus asks you to **state and explain** each strategy, so learn the reason with the strategy.

### Reducing climate change

| Strategy | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Planting trees | trees remove carbon dioxide from the air by photosynthesis |
| Reduction in livestock farming | fewer animals produce less methane from digestion |
| Decreasing use of fossil fuels | less carbon dioxide is produced by combustion |
| Increasing use of hydrogen | hydrogen burns to form only water: 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, so no carbon dioxide is released |
| Increasing use of renewable energy, e.g. wind, solar | electricity is generated without burning fuels |

### Reducing acid rain

| Strategy | Why it helps |
|---|---|
| Catalytic converters in vehicles | convert oxides of nitrogen into nitrogen |
| Low-sulfur fuels | less sulfur in the fuel means less sulfur dioxide when it burns |
| Flue gas desulfurisation with calcium oxide | calcium oxide, a basic oxide, reacts with the acidic sulfur dioxide in waste gases from power stations before they are released: CaO + SO₂ → CaSO₃ |

Flue gas desulfurisation is an acid–base reaction, which links it to subtopic 7.2 Oxides.

## Photosynthesis

**Photosynthesis** is the reaction between **carbon dioxide** and **water** to produce **glucose** and **oxygen**, in the presence of **chlorophyll** and using **energy from light**.

```
word equation (Core):        carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen
symbol equation (Extended):  6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2
```

Chlorophyll and light are conditions, not reactants, so they are written above the arrow if at all, never as reactants.

## How greenhouse gases cause global warming (Supplement)

The syllabus limits this to the absorption, reflection and emission of thermal energy, and the reduction of thermal energy loss to space. Build the answer as a sequence:

1. Energy from the Sun reaches the Earth; some is **reflected** back to space.
2. The rest is **absorbed** by the Earth's surface, which warms up.
3. The warm surface **emits thermal energy** (infrared radiation).
4. Carbon dioxide and methane in the atmosphere **absorb** some of this thermal energy.
5. They **re-emit** it in all directions, including back towards the Earth.
6. This **reduces thermal energy loss to space**, so the Earth warms. More greenhouse gas means more absorption and more warming.

## Oxides of nitrogen and catalytic converters (Supplement)

**Formation.** Air drawn into a car engine is mostly nitrogen and oxygen. Nitrogen is normally very unreactive, but the very **high temperature** when fuel burns inside the engine is enough for nitrogen and oxygen to react, forming nitrogen monoxide:

```
N2 + O2 → 2NO
```

Nitrogen monoxide can react further with oxygen in the air to form nitrogen dioxide; together these are called oxides of nitrogen.

**Removal.** A catalytic converter in the exhaust contains a catalyst that speeds up the reaction between two pollutants:

```
2CO + 2NO → 2CO2 + N2
```

In this reaction nitrogen monoxide **loses oxygen**, so it is **reduced** to nitrogen; carbon monoxide **gains oxygen**, so it is **oxidised** to carbon dioxide. Two harmful gases are converted into nitrogen, which is harmless, and carbon dioxide, which is not toxic but is still a greenhouse gas.

## Common mistakes

- **Giving the composition as "78 % nitrogen, 21 % oxygen, 1 % carbon dioxide".** The remainder is mostly noble gases; carbon dioxide is only a small part of it.
- **Saying carbon dioxide causes acid rain.** In this syllabus its listed effect is global warming leading to climate change.
- **Saying cars produce methane.** Methane comes from decomposing vegetation and animal digestion.
- **Writing that catalytic converters remove carbon dioxide.** They produce it.
- **Explaining the greenhouse effect as gases "trapping heat".** Use absorption, re-emission and reduced loss to space.
- **Stating a strategy without a reason.** "Plant trees" needs "because they absorb carbon dioxide for photosynthesis".
- **Treating chlorophyll as a reactant in photosynthesis.**

## Exam technique for 10.3 command words

- **State:** the percentage, source or effect in the syllabus wording.
- **Explain** a strategy: strategy, then the chemical reason.
- **Describe** photosynthesis: reactants, products, chlorophyll and light energy.
- **Describe** global warming (Extended): the six-step sequence, using "absorb", "emit" and "loss to space".
- **Explain** NOx formation (Extended): high temperature, nitrogen and oxygen from the air.
