These notes condense section **9.4 Statistical charts and diagrams** from Topic 9 Statistics of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. You must "Draw and interpret" (a) bar charts, including composite (stacked) and dual (side-by-side) bar charts, (b) pie charts, (c) pictograms, (d) stem-and-leaf diagrams, which "should have ordered data with a key", and (e) simple frequency distributions. C9.4 and E9.4 are identical, so every line here is for Core and Extended candidates.

The full teaching, with longer worked examples, is in the [statistical charts and diagrams study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-statistical-charts-and-diagrams/). Test yourself afterwards with the [statistical charts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-statistical-charts-and-diagrams-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Frequency distribution | A list or table of each value (or class) with its frequency |
| Bar chart | Bars of equal width whose heights (or lengths) show frequency |
| Composite (stacked) bar chart | One bar per group, split into sections; the full bar is the group total |
| Dual (side-by-side) bar chart | Bars for two or more groups drawn next to each other for each category |
| Pie chart | A circle split into sectors; each angle is proportional to frequency |
| Sector | One "slice" of a pie chart |
| Pictogram | Symbols, each standing for a fixed number of items, shown in a key |
| Stem-and-leaf diagram | Data split into a stem (leading digits) and a leaf (last digit), in order, with a key |
| Key | The statement of what a symbol, colour or stem-and-leaf entry means |

## Choosing a diagram

| Data or purpose | Good choice |
|---|---|
| Qualitative or discrete data, comparing frequencies | Bar chart (equal gaps between bars) |
| One category across several groups | Dual bar chart |
| Group totals and how each total is made up | Composite bar chart |
| How a whole is shared out (proportions) | Pie chart |
| A simple, eye-catching display for a general reader | Pictogram |
| Numerical data where the individual values must stay visible | Stem-and-leaf diagram |

## (a) Bar charts

- Label both axes; frequency axis starts at **0** with an **even** scale.
- Every bar the **same width**; equal gaps for categories or discrete values.
- **Composite:** a section's value = top of the section − bottom of the section.
- **Dual:** every bar starts at 0, with a key for the groups.

**Small reminder.** On a stacked bar, a section runs from 35 to 60. It shows 60 − 35 = **25**, not 60.

## (b) Pie charts

```
Angle for one item = 360° ÷ total frequency
Sector angle       = frequency × angle for one item
                   = frequency ÷ total × 360°
Frequency          = sector angle ÷ 360° × total
```

**Drawing:** work out every angle, check they add to 360°, draw a radius, measure each angle from the last line, label each sector.

**Small reminder.** 45 students choose a sport. 360 ÷ 45 = 8° per student. A sport chosen by 14 students gets 14 × 8 = **112°**.

**Reading back:** if a sector of 54° stands for 24 people, 1° stands for 24 ÷ 54 = 4/9 of a person, so the total is 360 × 4/9 = **160** people.

**Unknown angles:** angles of 100°, 140°, x and 2x make 360°, so 3x = 120 and **x = 40°**.

A pie chart shows **proportions**. Two pie charts with different totals cannot compare numbers by angle alone.

## (c) Pictograms

- The **key** gives what one symbol stands for.
- Part-symbols stand for the same fraction of the key value.
- Every symbol the same size, lined up in columns.

**Small reminder.** Key: one symbol = 6 cars. 2½ symbols = 2.5 × 6 = **15** cars. To show 10 cars with a key of 4, draw 10 ÷ 4 = **2½** symbols.

## (d) Stem-and-leaf diagrams

- Stems in order down the left; leaves **in order** along each row, one digit each.
- Always give a **key**, for example 2 | 3 means 23.
- Leaves are evenly spaced so the rows act as a bar chart turned on its side.

**Small reminder.** 23, 31, 27, 45, 38, 31, 42, 29, 35, 40:

```
2 | 3 7 9
3 | 1 1 5 8
4 | 0 2 5
Key: 2 | 3 means 23
```

Because the values are in order you can read off: smallest 23, largest 45, range 22, mode 31, and with 10 values the median is (31 + 35) ÷ 2 = 33.

## (e) Simple frequency distributions

- A frequency table lists each value once with its frequency.
- Total frequency = number of items, **not** the sum of the values.
- Sum of all the values = total of (value × frequency).

**Small reminder.** Values 0, 1, 2 with frequencies 3, 5, 2: there are 10 items, and their sum is 0 × 3 + 1 × 5 + 2 × 2 = 9.

## Exam traps

- Reading the top of a stacked section instead of top minus bottom.
- Pie chart angles that do not add to 360° (usually a rounding or arithmetic slip; check the total).
- Sectors labelled with angles only, not with the category.
- Comparing two pie charts' angles as numbers of people when the totals differ.
- Pictograms read by counting symbols without using the key.
- Stem-and-leaf diagrams with unordered leaves, leaves like "15" in one row, or no key.
- Bars of different widths, or a frequency axis that does not start at 0.
- Adding the values column instead of the frequency column to find how many items there are.

## Quick self-test

1. A pie chart shows 60 people. What angle represents 13 people?
2. A sector of 54° represents 24 people. How many people does the whole pie chart represent?
3. Pie chart angles are 100°, 140°, x and 2x. Find x.
4. A pictogram key is one symbol = 6 cars. How many cars do 3½ symbols show?
5. Why must a stem-and-leaf diagram have a key?
6. Write down the median of the data in the stem-and-leaf diagram above.
7. On a stacked bar chart, a section runs from 35 to 60. What value does it show?
8. When is a dual bar chart better than a composite bar chart?
9. Should the bars on a bar chart of favourite fruits touch? Explain.
10. A frequency table has values 1, 2, 3 with frequencies 4, 7, 9. How many items are there, and what is their sum?
11. School P (300 students) and School Q (900 students) each have a 90° sector for "walks to school". Do the same number walk?
12. In a stem-and-leaf diagram with key 5 | 2 = 5.2, what value does the entry 6 | 8 stand for?

### Answers

1. 13 ÷ 60 × 360 = **78°**.
2. 24 ÷ 54 × 360 = **160**.
3. 240 + 3x = 360, so **x = 40°**.
4. 3.5 × 6 = **21** cars.
5. Without a key, a reader cannot tell whether 2 | 3 means 23, 2.3 or 230.
6. **33.**
7. **25.**
8. When you want to compare **one category across groups**, because every bar starts at 0.
9. **No**: the data are qualitative, so the bars are separate with equal gaps.
10. 4 + 7 + 9 = **20 items**; sum = 4 + 14 + 27 = **45**.
11. **No**: 90° is a quarter of each school, so **75** walk at P and **225** at Q.
12. **6.8.**
