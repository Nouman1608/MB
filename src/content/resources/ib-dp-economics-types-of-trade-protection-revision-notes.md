## Definitions to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods. Specific: a fixed sum per unit. Ad valorem: a share of the import's value |
| Quota | A legal ceiling on the units of a good allowed in |
| Quota rent | The price gap (domestic price minus world price) times the quota quantity, collected by licence holders |
| Subsidy | A government payment made to producers, usually per unit of output |
| Export subsidy | A payment per unit sold abroad |
| Administrative barrier | A standard, regulation, licence or procedure that makes importing harder or dearer |
| Welfare loss | The triangles of surplus that nobody gains when protection distorts output and consumption |

## The depth the guide sets

- Tariffs, quotas and subsidies: AO3 and AO4. You evaluate and you use diagram skills.
- Administrative barriers (standards and regulations): AO3.
- Stakeholder calculations from a diagram: AO4, HL only.
- Command terms such as "draw", "label", "show that" and "calculate" belong to the skills objective. "Calculate" means a numerical answer with the working shown.

## Diagram checklist

Every diagram needs the following. Mark each as you draw.

1. Axes: price (vertical) and quantity (horizontal).
2. Domestic demand D and domestic supply S, labelled.
3. World price Pw as a horizontal line.
4. The policy line or shifted curve: Pw + tariff, quota price, or Pw + subsidy.
5. Four quantities for a tariff (Qs and Qd, before and after). Name them.
6. Shaded and labelled areas for consumer loss, producer gain, revenue and welfare loss.
7. A short note stating what happened to price, production, consumption and imports.

## Methods in steps

**Tariff or quota (HL only calculation)**

1. Find Qd and Qs at Pw. Imports = Qd − Qs.
2. Find the new domestic price: Pw + t for a tariff; for a quota, solve Qd − Qs = quota.
3. Find Qd and Qs at the new price. New imports = Qd − Qs.
4. Revenue (tariff) = t × new imports. Quota rent = (new price − Pw) × quota.
5. Consumer loss = ½ × (old Qd + new Qd) × price rise.
6. Producer gain = ½ × (old Qs + new Qs) × price rise.
7. Welfare loss = ½ × price rise × (new Qs − old Qs) + ½ × price rise × (old Qd − new Qd).
8. Check: consumer loss = producer gain + revenue + welfare loss.

**Producer subsidy, import-competing market (steps 3 to 5 are HL only calculations)**

1. Producers receive Pw + s. Find the new Qs. Consumers still pay Pw, so Qd does not change.
2. New imports = Qd − new Qs.
3. Government cost = s × new Qs.
4. Producer gain = ½ × (old Qs + new Qs) × s.
5. Net welfare change = producer gain − cost, which equals −½ × s × (new Qs − old Qs).

**Export subsidy (steps 3 and 4 are HL only calculations)**

1. Domestic price rises to Pw + s.
2. New Qd and Qs at that price. Exports = Qs − Qd.
3. Cost = s × exports. Consumer loss and producer gain are trapezoids over the price rise s.
4. Net = producer gain − consumer loss − cost.

## Worked reminder: Lorvan

Fictional market for headphones in Lorvan. Demand Qd = 140 − 2P, supply Qs = 3P − 10, in thousands. Pw = 20.

At Pw: Qd = 100, Qs = 50, imports = **50**.

**Tariff of 4 (HL only)**

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| New price | 20 + 4 | 24 |
| New quantities | Qd = 140 − 48; Qs = 72 − 10 | 92 and 62 |
| New imports | 92 − 62 | 30 |
| Revenue | 4 × 30 | 120 |
| Consumer loss | ½ × (100 + 92) × 4 | 384 |
| Producer gain | ½ × (50 + 62) × 4 | 224 |
| Welfare loss | ½ × 4 × 12 + ½ × 4 × 8 = 24 + 16 | 40 |
| Check | 224 + 120 + 40 | 384 |

**Quota of 30 (rent is an HL only calculation):** solve 140 − 2P − (3P − 10) = 30, so 150 − 5P = 30 and **P = 24**. Same quantities as the tariff. Quota rent = 4 × 30 = **120**.

**Producer subsidy of 4 (cost and welfare are HL only):** producers receive 24, so Qs = 62. Consumers pay 20 and buy 100. Imports = **38**. Cost = 4 × 62 = **248**. Producer gain = ½ × (50 + 62) × 4 = **224**. Net = 224 − 248 = **−24**.

**Export subsidy (HL only):** fictional tea market in Sunmar. Qd = 120 − 2P, Qs = 3P − 30, Pw = 36. Free trade: Qd = 48, Qs = 78, exports **30**. A subsidy of 3 per unit exported lifts the domestic price to 39. Then Qd = 42, Qs = 87 and exports are **45**. Cost = 3 × 45 = **135**. Consumer loss = ½ × (48 + 42) × 3 = **135**. Producer gain = ½ × (78 + 87) × 3 = **247.5**. Net = 247.5 − 135 − 135 = **−22.5**.

## Who gains and loses from a tariff

| Stakeholder | Effect | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Domestic consumers | Lose | Higher price, lower consumption |
| Domestic producers | Gain | Higher price, higher output |
| Government | Gains revenue | Tax on each import |
| Foreign producers | Lose | Sell fewer units to this market |
| The country as a whole | Net loss in this model | Two triangles are lost |

Whether the net loss is the whole story is a question for [arguments for and against protection](/resources/ib-dp-economics-arguments-for-and-against-protection/), where jobs, security and infant industries are weighed.

## Must-know distinctions

| Compare | Tariff | Quota |
|---|---|---|
| What is fixed | The price gap | The import quantity |
| Who gets area d | Government | Licence holders |
| If demand rises | Imports rise | Imports stay capped |

| Compare | Tariff | Producer subsidy |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer price | Rises | Unchanged |
| Who pays | Consumers | Taxpayers |
| Government budget | Gains revenue | Pays out |

| Compare | Tariff | Administrative barrier |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | Collected by government | Nobody collects it |
| Cost to society | Triangles of lost surplus | Triangles plus resources spent on compliance |
| Evaluation point | Clearly a trade tool | May also serve safety or consumer protection |

## Evaluating administrative barriers

- A rule can protect health, safety or consumers, so removing it is not automatically a gain.
- The same rule may be written so that only foreign producers struggle to meet it.
- Ask whether a less restrictive rule would reach the same goal.
- Ask who pays: buyers through higher prices, importers through compliance costs.
- Government revenue is absent, unlike a tariff.

## Turning a question into a diagram

- "Explain the effect of a tariff": draw the tariff diagram, then describe price, production, consumption and imports in words. The diagram alone is not an explanation.
- "Calculate": work from the quantities and prices given. Show every line, with units.
- "Evaluate" or "discuss" a tool: give effects on each stakeholder, then weigh them. Consumers, producers, government and foreign sellers all appear.
- A percentage tariff: convert it to a per-unit sum first, using the world price.
- A quota stated in units: solve for the price that makes imports equal the quota.

## Quick self-test

Use the Lorvan market: Qd = 140 − 2P, Qs = 3P − 10, Pw = 20, quantities in thousands.

1. Define an import quota.
2. A tariff of 6 is imposed. What is the new domestic price?
3. State domestic production and consumption at that price.
4. How many units are imported?
5. Calculate government revenue. (HL only)
6. Calculate the fall in consumer surplus. (HL only)
7. Calculate the gain in producer surplus. (HL only)
8. Calculate the net change in welfare. (HL only)
9. A 15% ad valorem tariff applies to a good whose world price is 30. State the tariff per unit and the domestic price.
10. State why an administrative barrier raises no government revenue.
11. A quota of 30 is set. State the domestic price and the quota rent. (HL only)

### Answers

1. A legal ceiling on the units of a good allowed in during a period.
2. 20 + 6 = **26**.
3. Qs = 78 − 10 = **68**. Qd = 140 − 52 = **88**.
4. 88 − 68 = **20**.
5. 6 × 20 = **120**.
6. ½ × (100 + 88) × 6 = **564**.
7. ½ × (50 + 68) × 6 = **354**.
8. Welfare loss = ½ × 6 × 18 + ½ × 6 × 12 = 54 + 36 = 90. Check: 354 + 120 − 564 = **−90**.
9. 15% × 30 = **4.5** per unit. Domestic price = **34.5**.
10. The cost is spent on compliance, testing or delay, and not paid to government.
11. Solve 140 − 2P − (3P − 10) = 30, giving P = **24**. Rent = (24 − 20) × 30 = **120**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating revenue with the free-trade import figure instead of the smaller figure after the tariff.
- Calling the revenue rectangle a triangle, or leaving it out of the welfare sum.
- Giving a quota price effect but forgetting to say who gets the rent.
- Describing a producer subsidy as raising the consumer price.
- Stating that "everyone gains" from protection. Consumers and taxpayers lose, and a good answer names them.
- Drawing the diagram without Pw, the new price line or the quantities.
- Using the trapezoid formula with the wrong two quantities. Take the old and new values on the same curve.
- Treating a standard as protectionist without considering any safety purpose it may serve.
- Writing "calculate" answers without showing the working.
