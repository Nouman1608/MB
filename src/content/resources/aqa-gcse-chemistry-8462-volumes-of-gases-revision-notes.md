These notes condense **4.3.5 Use of amount of substance in relation to volumes of gases (chemistry only) (HT
only)** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching from September 2016, version 1.2). The
whole subsection is **Higher Tier only**. It is Paper 1 content. For full explanations, see the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-volumes-of-gases/); then practise with the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-volumes-of-gases-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Equal amounts in moles of gases occupy the same volume** under the same conditions of temperature and
  pressure.
- The volume of **one mole of any gas** at **room temperature and pressure (20 °C and 1 atmosphere)** is
  **24 dm³**.
- Volumes of gaseous reactants and products can be calculated from the **balanced equation**.

## Equations

```
volume (dm3) = moles x 24             moles = volume (dm3) / 24
moles = mass / Mr                     mass = moles x Mr
1 dm3 = 1000 cm3
```

## Two routes

| Starting point | Method |
|---|---|
| Mass of a gas | mass ÷ Mr = moles; moles × 24 = volume |
| Volume of a gas in an equation | Use the coefficients as a volume ratio |
| Mass of a solid that makes a gas | mass ÷ Mr; equation ratio; × 24 |
| Volume of a gas to mass | volume ÷ 24; × Mr |

## Worked examples

| Question | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Volume of 4.4 g CO₂ | 4.4 ÷ 44 = 0.10 mol; × 24 | 2.4 dm³ |
| Volume of 1.0 g H₂ | 1.0 ÷ 2 = 0.50 mol; × 24 | 12 dm³ |
| Mass of 4.8 dm³ O₂ | 4.8 ÷ 24 = 0.20 mol; × 32 | 6.4 g |
| 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O with 10 dm³ H₂ | Ratio 2 : 1 : 2 | 5 dm³ O₂; 10 dm³ steam |
| CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O with 3.0 dm³ CH₄ | Ratio 1 : 2 : 1 | 6.0 dm³ O₂; 3.0 dm³ CO₂ |
| CO₂ from 5.0 g CaCO₃ | 0.050 mol × 24 | 1.2 dm³ (1200 cm³) |
| Mr of a gas: 0.60 dm³ weighs 1.1 g | 0.025 mol; 1.1 ÷ 0.025 | 44 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Molar volume | The volume of one mole of gas |
| RTP | Room temperature and pressure: 20 °C and 1 atmosphere |
| dm³ | Cubic decimetre, equal to 1000 cm³ |
| Gaseous volume ratio | Ratio of volumes of gases in an equation |
| Mole ratio | Ratio of balancing numbers |
| Diatomic | Made of two atoms per molecule, such as H₂ and O₂ |

## Checking answers

- 24 dm³ is 24 000 cm³.
- The volume ratio applies only to gases at the same temperature and pressure.
- H₂ has Mr 2, O₂ has Mr 32, N₂ has Mr 28, Cl₂ has Mr 71.
- If the mass of gas is small, the volume in dm³ is small, unless the gas is very light (hydrogen).

## Practical link (HT only)

Gas volumes from a reaction can be measured with a gas syringe or by collecting over water, usually in cm³.

| Step | Example |
|---|---|
| Moles of reactant | 0.12 g Mg ÷ 24 = 0.0050 mol |
| Moles of gas | 0.0050 mol H₂ (ratio 1 : 1) |
| Theoretical volume | 0.0050 × 24 = 0.12 dm³ = 120 cm³ |
| Compare | 118 ÷ 120 × 100 = 98.3% collected |

## Same volume, different mass

At room temperature and pressure, 24 dm³ of any gas contains one mole, but its mass is the Mr in grams.

| Gas | Mass of one mole |
|---|---|
| Hydrogen | 2 g |
| Oxygen | 32 g |
| Carbon dioxide | 44 g |

## Method summary for each question type (HT only)

| Question asks for | Steps |
|---|---|
| Volume of a given mass of gas | Moles = mass ÷ Mr; volume = moles × 24 |
| Mass of a given volume of gas | Moles = volume ÷ 24; mass = moles × Mr |
| Volume of one gas from another | Use the coefficient ratio directly |
| Volume of gas from a solid reactant | Moles of solid; ratio; × 24 |
| Mr of a gas | Moles = volume ÷ 24; Mr = mass ÷ moles |

State the conditions (room temperature and pressure) when you quote 24 dm³ per mole, and give the unit with every
answer.

## Exam traps

- **Using 24 with cm³** without converting.
- **Applying 24 dm³ at the wrong temperature.**
- **Using atomic mass for a diatomic gas.**
- **Using the mass ratio instead of the mole or volume ratio.**
- **Applying the volume ratio to liquids and solids.**
- **Forgetting the unit.**

## Quick self-test (all HT only)

1. State the volume of one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure.
2. Calculate the volume of 0.25 mol of gas at RTP.
3. Calculate the moles in 6.0 dm³ of gas at RTP.
4. Calculate the volume of 3.2 g of oxygen (Mr 32) at RTP.
5. Calculate the volume of 11.0 g of carbon dioxide at RTP.
6. N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃. What volume of hydrogen reacts with 20 dm³ of nitrogen?
7. H₂ + Cl₂ → 2HCl. What volume of hydrogen chloride forms from 100 cm³ of hydrogen?
8. Calculate the mass of 6.0 dm³ of nitrogen (Mr 28) at RTP.
9. Convert 24 dm³ to cm³.
10. State the conditions under which 24 dm³ per mole applies.

### Answers

1. 24 dm³.
2. 0.25 × 24 = 6.0 dm³.
3. 6.0 ÷ 24 = 0.25 mol.
4. 3.2 ÷ 32 = 0.10 mol; × 24 = 2.4 dm³.
5. 11.0 ÷ 44 = 0.25 mol; × 24 = 6.0 dm³.
6. 60 dm³.
7. 200 cm³.
8. 0.25 mol × 28 = 7.0 g.
9. 24 000 cm³.
10. Room temperature and pressure (20 °C and 1 atmosphere).
