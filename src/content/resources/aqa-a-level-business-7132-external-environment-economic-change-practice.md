These questions cover section 3.7.5 economic change of the AQA A-level Business (7132) specification. All businesses and figures are invented. Revise first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-economic-change/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-external-environment-economic-change-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to benefit a UK exporter selling in euros? **[1]**

A: The pound strengthens against the euro

B: The pound weakens against the euro

C: The euro zone enters a recession

D: UK interest rates rise sharply

**2.** Which **one** of the following describes expansionary fiscal policy? **[1]**

A: The central bank raises interest rates

B: The government cuts income tax

C: The government raises VAT

D: The central bank sells government bonds

**3.** A consumer price index rises from 105.2 to 110.5. Calculate the rate of inflation, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**4.** The demand for a supermarket's own-brand budget range falls 6% when incomes rise 3%. Calculate the income elasticity of demand and state what it shows. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a rise in interest rates might reduce sales of new cars. **[3]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**6.** Perrin Ltd imports €90,000 of materials each month from the euro zone.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly sterling cost when £1 = €1.17. **[1]**

**(b)** The pound falls to £1 = €1.08. Calculate the new monthly sterling cost and the change. **[3]**

**7.** Perrin Ltd has a £500,000 variable-rate loan. The interest rate rises from 3.5% to 5.5%. Calculate the annual increase in interest cost and explain **one** way Perrin could respond. **[4]**

**8.** Perrin Ltd gives staff a 4% pay rise at a time when inflation is 6.5%. Explain **two** possible effects of this on the business. **[4]**

**9.** Real GDP in a country falls for three consecutive quarters. Analyse how this might affect a luxury holiday company and a discount clothing retailer differently. **[6]**

**10.** The government announces a cut in corporation tax and increased spending on road building. Analyse the likely effects on a construction company. **[6]**

**11.** A UK furniture maker buys 70% of its timber from abroad and sells 80% of its products in the UK. The pound is expected to fall in value over the next two years. Assess the likely impact on the business. **[9]**

**12.** A government is considering protecting a domestic steel industry with tariffs. Evaluate the possible effects on a UK car manufacturer that buys steel. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. A weaker pound makes UK goods cheaper in euro, or increases the sterling value of euro revenue. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Cutting taxes is a fiscal action that increases spending power. **[1]**

**3.** (110.5 − 105.2) ÷ 105.2 × 100 [1] = **5.0%** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** YED = −6% ÷ 3% [1] = **−2** [1]. It shows an inferior good: demand falls as incomes rise [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Many people buy new cars on credit [1], so higher interest rates make the monthly payments dearer [1]. This reduces demand, so sales fall, especially for expensive models [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** €90,000 ÷ 1.17 = **£76,923** [1]. **(b)** €90,000 ÷ 1.08 = **£83,333** [1]; increase = £83,333 − £76,923 = **£6,410** per month [1] (about 8.3%) [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Old interest = 3.5% × £500,000 = £17,500; new = 5.5% × £500,000 = £27,500 [1]; increase = **£10,000** a year [1]. Response, for example: repay part of the loan from cash to reduce interest [1], or switch to a fixed-rate loan, which guards against further rises but may cost more now [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Effect 1: real pay falls by about 2.3% [1] (1.04 ÷ 1.065 − 1), so staff may feel worse off, causing lower motivation or leaving [1]. Effect 2: the lower wage rise keeps costs down [1], protecting profit, though higher turnover may add recruitment and training costs [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Luxury holiday company: holidays are a luxury with high income elasticity [1], so demand falls sharply when incomes fall and people cut back [1], leading to unsold capacity and price cuts [1]. Discount retailer: customers trade down to cheaper clothes [1], so demand may hold or rise [1], though falling spending overall may limit growth [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Corporation tax cut: raises after-tax profit [1], which can fund investment in machinery or expansion [1]. Road building: more government contracts [1], increasing demand and revenue [1], but the company may face competition for the contracts, and costs of labour and materials may rise with demand [1]. Overall, both changes are likely to be opportunities, though the benefit depends on winning the contracts [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Costs: a weaker pound makes imported timber dearer in sterling [1]. As 70% of timber is imported, material costs may rise significantly [1], reducing profit unless prices rise [1]. Revenue: 80% of sales are in the UK, so the firm gains little from cheaper exports [1]; it might also face more competition from UK makers using home timber [1]. Competing imports of finished furniture also become dearer, which may help the business in its home market [1]. Mitigation: buying currency in advance or finding UK suppliers [1]. Judgement: the impact is probably a threat, since the business is a net importer [1], but its size depends on how far it can pass costs on to customers and how long the fall lasts [1]. **[9]**

**12.** For the manufacturer, the effect is mainly a cost threat: tariffs on imported steel raise the price of steel [1], increasing production costs [1], reducing margins or forcing price rises that could lose sales [1]. Foreign rivals who buy steel at world prices could gain an advantage [1], and trade partners might retaliate against UK car exports [1]. There are also some possible benefits: a stronger domestic steel industry might provide secure supply [1], shorter supply chains [1], and local jobs and demand [1]. Factors: how much of the cost is steel [1], whether alternative materials exist [1], and how competitive the domestic steel is on quality and delivery. Judgement: the car manufacturer is likely to be harmed in the short term [1], unless it can pass the cost on or the tariffs lead to better local supply [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* The best answers deal with a mix of effects, pick the dominant one for the business in the case, and justify the choice.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the economic change without saying what it does to demand or costs.
- Assuming a weaker pound is always good for UK firms.
- Treating every business as affected in the same way by a recession.
- Confusing fiscal and monetary policy.
- Mixing up percentages and percentage points.
