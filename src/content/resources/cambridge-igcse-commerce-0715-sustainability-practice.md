## Section A - short questions

**1.** A shop lets customers sell their used goods through its website. This is: **[1]**
A water stewardship   B a digital resale marketplace   C a free repair service   D reduced packaging

**2.** Which action helps sustainability by making products last longer? **[1]**
A free repair services   B bigger packaging   C air freight   D more returns

**3.** The purpose of sustainability in the supply chain is to make commercial processes: **[1]**
A as cheap as possible   B eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations   C as fast as possible   D free of all rules

**4.** State **two** actions that improve sustainability. **[2]**

**5.** Define recycling. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** ways in which a business benefits from reducing packaging. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *A furniture maker plans to use timber from sustainably managed forests.*
(a) Identify the action from the syllabus that this describes. **[1]**
(b) Explain **one** environmental benefit of this action. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** possible disadvantage for the furniture maker. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which a delivery company could make its logistics more sustainable. **[4]**

**9.** Explain why reducing the need for returns and exchanges can make an online shop more sustainable. **[4]**

**10.** *A drinks company sells 80,000 bottles a year. Each bottle has a label and sleeve weighing 50 g. A redesign cuts this to 35 g. The material costs $4.00 per kilogram.*
(a) Calculate the saving in grams per bottle. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the total material saved in kilograms. **[2]**
(c) Calculate the money saved each year. **[1]**
(d) Explain **one** other benefit of this change. **[2]**

**11.** *A clothing shop sends out 3,000 orders a month. 10% are returned. Each return costs $5 in postage and handling. The shop adds a size guide and returns fall to 6%.*
(a) Calculate the monthly cost of returns before the change. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the monthly cost after the change. **[2]**
(c) Calculate the monthly saving. **[1]**

**12.** Discuss whether a small factory should spend money on water stewardship. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Customers resell used goods through the firm's own platform. **[1]**

**2.** A. Repairing products keeps them in use for longer. **[1]**

**3.** B. The syllabus describes the purpose as making processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: using sustainable materials [1]; sustainable logistics [1]; reducing packaging [1]; selling eco-friendly products [1]; recycling and reducing waste [1]; reducing returns and exchanges [1]; digital resale marketplaces [1]; free repair services [1]; water stewardship [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** Collecting used materials [1] and reprocessing them into new products or materials [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: lower material costs [1]; less waste to dispose of [1]; lighter deliveries, so lower transport costs [1]; better image with eco-conscious customers [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Using sustainable materials in the production process [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any one: forests are managed so that trees are replanted [1], which protects wildlife and keeps resources for future generations [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Any one: the timber may cost more than other timber [1], which could raise prices or lower profit [1]; or supply may be limited [1], which could cause delays [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Any two, 2 marks each: using fuller loads and planned routes [1], so fewer journeys and less fuel are used [1]; using electric or low-emission vehicles [1], which reduces air pollution [1]; moving long-distance freight by rail instead of road [1], which uses less fuel per tonne [1]; using efficient warehouses powered by renewable energy [1], reducing energy use [1]. **[max 4]**

**9.** Each return needs an extra delivery to the customer's home and another back to the shop [1], so more fuel is burned and more packaging is used [1]. Returned goods may also be damaged and thrown away [1]. Reducing returns, for example with accurate size guides, means fewer wasted journeys and less waste [1]. **[4]**

**10.** (a) 50 - 35 = **15 g** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) 15 x 80,000 = 1,200,000 g [1] = **1,200 kg** [1]. (2 marks)
(c) 1,200 x 4.00 = **$4,800** [1]. (1 mark)
(d) Any one: less waste to dispose of [1], which is better for the environment [1]; or lighter parcels [1], so lower transport emissions [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**11.** (a) Returns: 3,000 x 10 / 100 = 300 [1]. Cost: 300 x 5 = **$1,500** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Returns: 3,000 x 6 / 100 = 180 [1]. Cost: 180 x 5 = **$900** [1]. (2 marks)
(c) 1,500 - 900 = **$600** [1]. (1 mark) **[5]**

**12.** Up to 2 for reasons for, up to 2 for reasons against, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
For: protects a scarce resource for the local community and future generations [1]; lower water bills and less cost of treating waste water [1]; avoids fines or damage to the firm's image if it pollutes [1].
Against: equipment and monitoring cost money, which is a bigger burden for a small firm [1]; benefits may take years to repay [1]; the firm may not use much water, so the gain is small [1].
Conclusion: for example, if the factory uses a lot of water or is in a dry area, the spending is justified, but if it uses little, simple low-cost measures are enough [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an action with no environmental benefit explained.
- Confusing reducing waste with recycling.
- Writing "it is good for the planet" with no link to the business in the question.
- Forgetting to convert grams to kilograms in calculations.
- Giving only advantages in a "discuss" question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many actions to improve sustainability does 0715 list?** Nine, from sustainable materials to water stewardship.

**What is the key idea in the purpose of sustainability?** Making commercial processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations.
