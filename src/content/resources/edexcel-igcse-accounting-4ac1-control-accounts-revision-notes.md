## What you must be able to do

- (a) Explain the purpose of control accounts.
- (b) Prepare a trade receivables control account and a trade payables control account.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Control account | Summary account for a whole ledger, built from book totals |
| Receivables ledger | Individual accounts of credit customers |
| Payables ledger | Individual accounts of credit suppliers |
| Contra (set-off) | Cancelling a customer's debt against the amount owed to the same person as a supplier |
| Dishonoured cheque | A customer's cheque the bank refuses to pay; the debt is restored |
| Credit balance in receivables ledger | A customer who has overpaid; the business owes them |
| Debit balance in payables ledger | A supplier who has been overpaid |

## Four purposes

1. Check the arithmetical accuracy of the ledger.
2. Locate errors to one ledger.
3. Discourage fraud through separation of duties.
4. Give quick totals of receivables and payables.

## Trade receivables control account

| Debit | Source | Credit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening debit balances | Previous period | Opening credit balances | Previous period |
| Credit sales | Sales day book | Receipts from customers | Cash book |
| Dishonoured cheques | Cash book | Discount allowed | Cash book |
| Refunds to customers | Cash book | Returns inwards | Sales returns book |
| Closing credit balances c/d | Ledger list | Irrecoverable debts | Journal |
| | | Contra | Journal |
| | | Closing debit balances c/d | Balancing figure |

## Trade payables control account

| Debit | Source | Credit | Source |
|---|---|---|---|
| Opening debit balances | Previous period | Opening credit balances | Previous period |
| Payments to suppliers | Cash book | Credit purchases | Purchases day book |
| Discount received | Cash book | Refunds from suppliers | Cash book |
| Returns outwards | Purchases returns book | Closing debit balances c/d | Ledger list |
| Contra | Journal | | |
| Closing credit balances c/d | Balancing figure | | |

## Keep out

- Cash sales and cash purchases.
- Non-current assets bought on credit.
- Provision for irrecoverable debts (only write-offs go in).
- Discount received in the receivables account; discount allowed in the payables account.
- Expenses, depreciation, drawings.

## Mini-check

Receivables: opening 12,000; credit sales 45,500; receipts 41,300; discount allowed 600; returns inwards 900; contra 350. Closing balance: 57,500 − 43,150 = 14,350 debit.

## Reconciling with the ledger list

| Type of error | What to correct |
|---|---|
| Day book or returns book total wrong | Control account only |
| Item posted to wrong side of an individual account | List: adjust by twice the amount |
| Item posted with the wrong amount to an individual account | List: adjust by the difference |
| Balance omitted from or miscopied on the list | List only |
| Item omitted from the books completely | Both (control and the individual account) |

## Exam traps

- Including cash sales or the provision.
- Dishonoured cheque on the credit side.
- Netting opening debit and credit balances into one figure.
- Contra entered on only one control account.
- Opening payables balance on the debit side.
- Correcting the wrong record when reconciling.
- Forgetting the closing credit balances in a receivables control account.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two purposes of control accounts.
2. Which book provides the credit sales figure?
3. On which side of the receivables control account is discount allowed?
4. On which side of the payables control account are returns outwards?
5. Why is the provision for irrecoverable debts excluded?
6. Explain a contra entry and show where it appears in both control accounts.
7. A customer's cheque for 520 is dishonoured. Where is it entered?
8. Payables: opening 7,400; credit purchases 31,800; payments 29,500; discount received 420; returns outwards 640. Calculate the closing balance.
9. The sales day book was undercast by 200. Which record needs correcting, and how?
10. A credit sale of 85 was posted to the credit of the customer's account. How is the list adjusted?
11. Why can a receivables control account have a credit balance carried down?
12. Who should prepare the control account to help prevent fraud?

### Answers

1. Any two: check the ledger; locate errors; discourage fraud; give quick totals.
2. The sales day book.
3. Credit side.
4. Debit side.
5. It is an estimate; no customer account changes.
6. A debt owed by a person who is also a supplier is set off against the amount owed to them: credit the receivables control account and debit the payables control account.
7. Debit side of the receivables control account (and the customer's account).
8. 39,200 − 30,560 = 8,640 credit.
9. The control account: increase credit sales by 200 (debit side).
10. The customer's balance is understated by 2 × 85 = 170, so add 170 to the list.
11. Some customers have overpaid, so the business owes them money.
12. Someone other than the person who keeps the individual ledger accounts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to show opening credit balances separately?**
Yes. Show debit and credit opening balances on their own sides; do not net them off.

**Is a control account part of the double entry?**
It can be, with the ledger accounts kept as memorandum records, or it can be a memorandum check itself. Questions usually ask you to prepare it as a summary, so focus on the entries and balance.
