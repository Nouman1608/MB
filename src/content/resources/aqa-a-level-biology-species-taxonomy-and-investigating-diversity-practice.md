## Questions

**1.** Two populations of a grass are crossed. The hybrid plants grow well and flower, but they produce no viable seeds. Explain whether the two populations belong to the same species. **[3]**

**2.** Two cricket species live in one meadow. Males of each produce a different chirp pattern, and a female approaches only the pattern of her own species.
**(a)** Explain how this courtship behaviour helps a female. **[2]**
**(b)** Females of species 1 are played both songs. They approach one recording and ignore the other. Explain what this shows about courtship. **[2]**

**3.** The table shows part of the classification of three mammals.

| Taxon | Red fox | Grey wolf | Eurasian otter |
|---|---|---|---|
| Order | Carnivora | Carnivora | Carnivora |
| Family | Canidae | Canidae | Mustelidae |
| Genus | *Vulpes* | *Canis* | *Lutra* |

**(a)** Which two species are most closely related? Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the lowest taxon shared by all three species in the table. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the binomial for the grey wolf (species name *lupus*) in the correct form for handwriting. **[1]**
**(d)** A new species is placed in the genus *Vulpes*. State the taxa in the table it must share with the red fox. **[2]**

**4.** A student writes the name of the cabbage as: Brassica Oleracea.
**(a)** Identify two errors in how the name is written. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one advantage of using binomials rather than common names. **[1]**

**5.** An antibody was made against serum albumin from species P. Samples of albumin from four species were mixed with the antibody. The relative amount of precipitate formed was: P 100, Q 94, R 58, S 22 (arbitrary units).
**(a)** Explain how the results show that R is more closely related to P than S is. **[2]**
**(b)** Place Q, R and S in order, closest relative of P first. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the antibody reacts less with the albumin of a distant relative. **[2]**

**6.** Describe how investigating genetic diversity has changed, and give two advantages of the newer method. **[4]**

**7.** A fragment of 40 aligned bases from the same gene was compared in four species. The numbers of bases that differ are shown.

| | W | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | -- | 2 | 6 | 14 |
| X | | -- | 7 | 15 |
| Y | | | -- | 13 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference between W and Z. **[1]**
**(b)** State which pair is most similar and give the percentage difference. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how Y and Z are related to W and X. **[2]**
**(d)** Give two limitations of this conclusion. **[2]**

**8.** The template strand of DNA for individual 1 reads TAC TTT GGG CCA ACT. Codon data: AUG Met, AAA Lys, AAG Lys, CCC Pro, UCC Ser, GGU Gly, UGA stop.
**(a)** Give the mRNA sequence. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the amino acid sequence. **[1]**
**(c)** Individual 2 has TTC in place of TTT. Explain the effect on the amino acid sequence. **[2]**
**(d)** Individual 3 has AGG in place of GGG. Give the amino acid sequence. **[1]**
**(e)** Compared with individual 1, state the number of differences in the template DNA and in the amino acid sequence for individuals 2 and 3. **[2]**

**9.** In random samples of 120 plants from two meadows, meadow A had 36 pink-flowered plants and meadow B had 78.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage with pink flowers in each meadow. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest what this shows about the two populations. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two reasons why this evidence is limited. **[2]**

**10.** A privet hedge is 2 m tall. Describe how to take random samples of 30 leaves from the top 50 cm and the bottom 50 cm, to compare leaf length. **[5]**

**11.** The tail length of 40 lizards was measured on each of three islands.

| Island | Mean / mm | Standard deviation / mm |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 62.0 | 3.1 |
| 2 | 65.5 | 3.4 |
| 3 | 62.4 | 8.2 |

**(a)** State which island shows most variation and explain how you know. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the range from one standard deviation below to one above the mean for islands 1 and 2. **[2]**
**(c)** Comment on whether the difference between the means of islands 1 and 2 is likely to be real. **[2]**

**12.** The first 12 amino acids of a protein were compared (one letter per amino acid).

```
W  GKVDLRSTAPEM
X  GKVDLRSTGPEM
Y  GKADLRNTGPEM
Z  GRADIRNSGQEL
```

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference between X and Z, and between Y and Z. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the sequences to suggest the order of relatedness of the four species, with reasons. **[3]**
**(c)** The DNA coding for W and X differs at 4 of 36 bases. Explain why the DNA percentage difference is greater than the amino acid percentage difference. **[2]**
**(d)** Give two reasons why conclusions from these amino acid data are tentative. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A species is a group able to produce fertile offspring [1]. The hybrids make no viable seeds, so they are not fertile [1]. The populations therefore fail the test and are not the same species [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the word "fertile" links the hybrid evidence to the definition.

**2. (a)** She recognises males of her own species by the chirp pattern [1], so she does not waste gametes on a mating that gives no offspring or sterile hybrids [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Females respond only to their own species' song [1], so courtship is species specific and allows species recognition before mating [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* name "species recognition" and give its consequence for mating.

**3. (a)** Red fox and grey wolf [1], because they share the family Canidae whereas the otter is in a different family [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Order Carnivora [1] **[1]**
**(c)** *Canis lupus*, with each word underlined separately, a capital C and a lower case l [1] **[1]**
**(d)** It must share the genus *Vulpes* [1] and every higher taxon in the table: the family Canidae and the order Carnivora [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* name the shared taxon when explaining why species are related.

**4. (a)** The species word *oleracea* should not have a capital letter [1]. The name should be in italics or underlined [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It is universal, so scientists worldwide use the same name for the same species [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* common names vary by language and place, so say "the same name worldwide".

**5. (a)** The antibody binds proteins similar to the one it was made against [1]. R gives more precipitate (58) than S (22), so R's albumin is more similar to P's [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Q, then R, then S [1] **[1]**
**(c)** A distant relative's albumin has more amino acid differences, so its shape differs from the antigen [1]. More differences have built up since the common ancestor [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* quote the table, then state what it means for relatedness.

**6.** Before, differences in DNA were inferred from observable or measurable characteristics [1]. Now the base sequences of DNA (or of mRNA, or the amino acid sequence of proteins) are compared directly [1]. Any two advantages from: DNA is not changed by the environment, but characteristics can be; changes with no visible effect can be detected; many genes can be compared; the comparison is objective. [2] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* give the old and new methods as two separate points.

**7. (a)** 14 / 40 × 100 = **35.0 %** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** W and X, 2 / 40 × 100 = **5.0 %** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Y is closer to W and X (15.0 % and 17.5 %) than Z is (35.0 % and 37.5 %) [1]. Z is the most distant relative, sharing an ancestor with the others longest ago [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** The fragment is only 40 bases from one gene [1]. Counting differences is a simple measure, as a site may have changed more than once [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* give the percentage for each pair and express each relationship in terms of common ancestry.

**8. (a)** AUG AAA CCC GGU UGA [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Met-Lys-Pro-Gly [1], with the stop codon not coding for an amino acid **[1]**
**(c)** The mRNA codon becomes AAG [1]. AAG and AAA both code for Lys, because the code is degenerate, so the amino acid sequence is unchanged [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** AUG AAA UCC GGU UGA gives Met-Lys-Ser-Gly [1] **[1]**
**(e)** Individual 2: one DNA difference, no amino acid difference [1]. Individual 3: one of each [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* write out the mRNA codons so each change is visible.

**9. (a)** Meadow A: 36 / 120 × 100 = **30.0 %** [1]. Meadow B: 78 / 120 × 100 = **65.0 %** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The populations may differ genetically, as pink is more frequent in B [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Flower colour can be influenced by the environment, such as soil [1]. One characteristic reflects only a small part of the genome [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* use "may" or "suggests" for an inference from characteristics.

**10.** Number every leaf in the top 50 cm [1]. Generate random numbers [1]. Select the leaf with each number, replacing repeats until there are 30 [1]. Repeat for the bottom 50 cm [1] and measure all leaves the same way [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* state how chance is used; "choose leaves randomly" repeats the question.

**11. (a)** Island 3 [1] has the largest standard deviation (8.2 mm), so its tail lengths are the most spread out [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Island 1: 62.0 − 3.1 = 58.9 to 62.0 + 3.1 = **65.1 mm** [1]. Island 2: 65.5 − 3.4 = 62.1 to 65.5 + 3.4 = **68.9 mm** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The ranges overlap, from 62.1 to 65.1 mm, so the difference may be due to chance [1]. A statistical test is needed to decide [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* refer to both the means and the spread.

**12. (a)** X and Z differ at 7 of 12 positions: 7 / 12 × 100 = **58.3 %** [1]. Y and Z differ at 5 of 12: 5 / 12 × 100 = **41.7 %** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** W and X are most similar, with 1 difference (8.3 %) [1]. Y is next, with 2 differences from X and 3 from W [1]. Z is most different from all, so it is the most distant relative [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** 4 / 36 × 100 = 11.1 %, compared with 1 / 12 × 100 = 8.3 % for the protein [1]. The genetic code is degenerate, so some base changes do not change the amino acid and appear only in the DNA [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Only 12 amino acids are compared, so one change alters the percentage greatly [1]. A single protein reflects only a little of the genome [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* give a number for each step and say what it means for common ancestry.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that species "can breed" instead of "produce fertile offspring".
- Describing courtship without mentioning species recognition.
- Errors in writing binomials.
- Quoting data without saying what it means for relationships.
- Concluding relatedness from appearance alone.
- Saying "randomly" with no method that uses chance.
- Ignoring standard deviations when comparing means.
