## Water cycle (3.1.1)

**Stores**

- Salt water: **oceans**.
- Fresh water: **ice sheets and glaciers**, **ground water**, **atmosphere**, **lakes and rivers**.

**Transfers**

| Transfer | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Evaporation | Liquid water to vapour, mainly from oceans |
| Transpiration | Water vapour lost from plant leaves |
| Condensation | Vapour cools to droplets, forming clouds |
| Precipitation | Rain, snow, sleet or hail falling from clouds |
| Interception | Leaves and branches catch precipitation |
| Infiltration | Water soaks into the soil surface |
| Surface run-off | Water flows over the ground |
| Through-flow | Water moves sideways through soil |
| Ground water flow | Water moves slowly through permeable rock |

**Arrow positions on diagrams:** up from sea or plants = evaporation or transpiration; clouds forming = condensation; down from clouds = precipitation; over the surface = run-off; into the ground = infiltration; within soil = through-flow; deep in rock = ground water flow.

## Sources of fresh water (3.1.2)

- **Atmosphere**: rain and snow.
- **Surface water**: rivers, lakes, reservoirs.
- **Ground water**: aquifers and wells.
- **Oceans**: desalination plants.

**Aquifer**: permeable rock (such as sandstone or limestone) that stores ground water above impermeable rock.

## Potable water and treatment (3.1.4 and 3.1.5)

**Potable water**: water that is safe to drink.

| Stage | What it removes | How |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Screening | Large debris: sticks, leaves, plastic | Mesh screens |
| 2 Sedimentation | Heavier suspended particles | Water held still in tanks; particles settle |
| 3 Filtration | Smaller particles | Beds of sand and gravel |
| 4 Chlorination | Disease-causing microorganisms | Chlorine added; some remains to protect water in pipes |

Memory aid: **large → heavy → small → living**.

## Availability of potable water (3.1.6)

| Comparison | Better access | Poorer access |
|---|---|---|
| Water-rich vs water-poor | High, reliable rainfall; large rivers, lakes, aquifers | Low or unreliable rainfall; high evaporation; few permanent rivers |
| Urban vs rural | Cities: treatment works and pipes serve many people | Rural: wells, rivers, rainwater, often untreated; long walks to collect water |
| Global inequalities | Wealthier countries: reservoirs, treatment, sewage works | Lower-income countries: fewer piped supplies; untreated sewage may enter rivers |

**Exception to note:** fast-growing cities may outgrow their infrastructure, leaving informal settlements without safe water.

## Desalination (3.1.7 and 3.1.8)

**Distillation**: heat water until it boils → salt remains in the liquid → steam is pure water → cool and condense the steam to form potable water.

**Reverse osmosis**: water under high pressure → passed through a membrane → water molecules pass through → most ions and other molecules are stopped.

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Ocean is an almost unlimited source | High energy use and cost; carbon dioxide if fossil-fuelled |
| Independent of rainfall; reliable in drought | Brine waste can harm marine life |
| Suits dry coastal countries | Only practical near the coast |
| Eases pressure on rivers and aquifers | High building cost |

## Multipurpose dams (3.1.9 and 3.1.10)

**Eight uses:** flood control, hydro-electric power, irrigation, storage of water, transport, recreation, tourism, fish farming.

| Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Several uses from one project | Very high cost |
| Renewable electricity | People displaced; farmland and habitats flooded |
| Reliable irrigation; higher yields | Silt trapped; reservoir fills; downstream land loses fertile deposits |
| Flood protection downstream | Fish migration blocked; river ecosystems changed |
| Jobs in tourism and fishing | Evaporation losses; disputes with countries downstream |

## Calculation to practise

**Days of supply** = volume stored ÷ daily use.

Quick check: 9 million m³ ÷ 30,000 m³ a day = 300 days. If use rises by 20% to 36,000 m³ a day: 9,000,000 ÷ 36,000 = 250 days.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Store | Place where water is held |
| Transfer | Process that moves water between stores |
| Aquifer | Permeable rock holding ground water |
| Potable water | Water that is safe to drink |
| Brine | Very salty waste water from desalination |
| Reservoir | Artificial lake behind a dam |

## Exam traps

- Infiltration is **into** the soil; through-flow is **through** it.
- Ice sheets are a fresh water store but not an accessible source.
- Filtration removes particles; **chlorination** kills microorganisms.
- Reverse osmosis needs **high pressure** and a **membrane**.
- Use "whereas" in compare questions.
- Discuss questions need benefits **and** limitations, then a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Name the four fresh water stores in the syllabus.
2. What is the difference between interception and infiltration?
3. Name the transfer in which water moves sideways through soil.
4. Name the five oceans.
5. Define potable water.
6. List the four water treatment stages in order.
7. What is the purpose of chlorination?
8. Describe reverse osmosis.
9. Give one environmental limitation of desalination.
10. Give four uses of a multipurpose dam.
11. Explain why rural areas often have less access to potable water than urban areas.
12. A reservoir holds 6 million m³ and a city uses 25,000 m³ a day. How many days would it last without rain?

## Answers

1. Ice sheets and glaciers, ground water, the atmosphere, lakes and rivers.
2. Interception is precipitation caught by vegetation; infiltration is water soaking into the soil.
3. Through-flow.
4. Pacific, Atlantic, Indian, Southern, Arctic.
5. Water that is safe to drink.
6. Screening, sedimentation, filtration, chlorination.
7. To kill disease-causing microorganisms and keep the water safe in the pipes.
8. Water is put under high pressure and passed through a membrane that lets water molecules through but stops most ions and other molecules.
9. Brine returned to the sea harms marine life, or carbon dioxide from the energy used.
10. Any four: flood control, hydro-electric power, irrigation, storage of water, transport, recreation, tourism, fish farming.
11. People are spread out, so piped supplies and treatment works are costly to build; many rely on untreated wells or rivers.
12. 6,000,000 ÷ 25,000 = 240 days.
