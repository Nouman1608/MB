Condensed recall for section 2.3.5 of Unit 2. For explanations and worked examples, use the [External influences study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-external-influences/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-external-influences-practice/). Figures are invented.

## The two-part answer

Every external influence needs an **effect** (on costs, revenue, demand or risk) and a **response** (what the business can do). Always say who is affected most: exporters, importers, borrowers, luxury sellers, small firms.

## Formula sheet

```
Cost in home currency = foreign price / exchange rate (foreign units per $1)
Interest cost         = loan x interest rate
Percentage change in demand = YED x percentage change in income
Tax payable           = profit x tax rate
```

## Economic influences at a glance

| Influence | Main effect on business | Possible responses |
|---|---|---|
| Higher inflation | Costs rise; wage claims; planning harder | Raise prices; fixed-price contracts; improve productivity |
| Currency appreciation | Exports dearer abroad; imports cheaper | Cut costs; buy more imports; focus on quality |
| Currency depreciation | Exports cheaper abroad; imports dearer | Push exports; use home suppliers; hedge |
| Higher interest rates | Loan costs rise; consumer spending falls | Fix rates; repay debt; delay investment |
| Higher taxation | Lower profit after tax or lower consumer spending | Adjust prices; seek efficiency; plan investment |
| Higher government spending | More demand for some industries | Bid for contracts |
| Boom | High demand, rising costs, stretched capacity | Expand carefully; build reserves |
| Recession | Falling demand, especially for luxuries | Lower-priced ranges; cut fixed costs; diversify |

**Income elasticity:** a positive YED above 1 means a luxury product (sales rise faster than income); between 0 and 1 a necessity; negative means an inferior good (sales fall when incomes rise).

## Exchange rates in one line each

- **Appreciation:** the currency buys more foreign currency. Foreign buyers pay more for your exports. Your imports cost less.
- **Depreciation:** the currency buys less. Exports are cheaper abroad. Imports cost more.
- A business that both exports and imports may be partly protected.
- Responses: invoice in the home currency, forward contracts, local sourcing, production in the market sold to.

## Legislation

| Law area | Typical requirement | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Consumer protection | Goods as described, safe, honest pricing | Testing, labelling, returns costs; reputation |
| Employee protection | Minimum wage, fair contracts, non-discrimination | Higher labour costs; better treatment |
| Environmental protection | Limits on emissions and waste | Investment in cleaner methods; fines if breached |
| Competition policy | No cartels; scrutiny of mergers | Limits on price fixing and takeovers |
| Health and safety | Safe equipment and training | Compliance costs; fewer accidents |
| Intellectual property | Copyright, patents, trademarks | Protects ideas and brands; costs to register and defend |

- **Patent:** protects an invention.
- **Trademark:** protects a name, logo or sign.
- **Copyright:** protects original written, musical and artistic work.
- Compliance costs weigh more heavily on small firms.

## Competitive environment

| Feature | High pressure | Lower pressure |
|---|---|---|
| Numbers | Many rivals | Few rivals |
| Size | Large rivals with scale economies | Rivals of similar or smaller size |
| Behaviour | Price wars, heavy promotion, takeovers | Stable pricing, little promotion |

**Ways for a small business to compete:** niche focus, personal service, quality or design differentiation, local reputation, flexibility and speed, low-cost online selling, partnerships or buying groups.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inflation | Sustained rise in the general price level |
| Appreciation | Rise in the value of a currency |
| Depreciation | Fall in the value of a currency |
| Business cycle | Pattern of boom, slowdown, recession and recovery |
| Income elasticity of demand | Responsiveness of demand to a change in income |
| Patent | Legal protection for an invention |
| Competition policy | Laws that prevent anti-competitive behaviour |

## Exam traps

- Appreciation does not help exporters. It makes their goods dearer abroad.
- Higher interest rates affect borrowers and customers; they can help savers.
- Percentages of a small profit can be large. A 6% cost rise can wipe out a quarter of profit.
- Write "reduces profit" only after saying how: higher cost, lower sales or both.
- A response must fit the business: a firm in a niche can often raise prices, a firm in a crowded market cannot.
- For **assess**, reach a judgement about which influence is most significant, and say what would change it.

## Self-test

1. Define "depreciation" of a currency.
2. Income rises by 6% and the YED of a product is +1.5. Calculate the change in demand.
3. A $150,000 loan has its rate raised from 5% to 7%. Calculate the extra yearly interest.
4. A firm buys EUR 72,000 of parts. Calculate the dollar cost at $1 = EUR 0.90 and at $1 = EUR 0.75, and the difference.
5. A firm's costs total $300,000 and rise by 4%. Calculate the extra cost.
6. Profit is $50,000 and tax is 20%. Calculate profit after tax.
7. Name two intellectual property rights.
8. Give one way a small business can compete with a large rival.
9. State one effect of a recession on a luxury-goods business.
10. Which feature of competition is described by "rivals start a price war"?

### Answers

1. A fall in the value of a currency, so it buys less foreign currency.
2. 6 x 1.5 = **9% increase** in demand.
3. 7% x 150,000 = $10,500 and 5% x 150,000 = $7,500, so the extra is **$3,000**.
4. At 0.90: 72,000 / 0.90 = **$80,000**. At 0.75: 72,000 / 0.75 = **$96,000**. Difference = **$16,000** more.
5. 4% x 300,000 = **$12,000**.
6. Tax = 20% x 50,000 = $10,000, so profit after tax = **$40,000**.
7. Any two of: copyright, patents, trademarks.
8. Any one: niche market, personal service, differentiation, local reputation, flexibility, low-cost online selling.
9. Demand falls more than for necessities because luxuries have a high income elasticity, so sales and profit fall.
10. Competitor behaviour.

## Answer frames

- **Explain an effect:** name the influence, say what changes (cost, price, demand), then give the result for profit or sales.
- **Explain a response:** say what the business could do and why it suits this business, then state one limit on the response.
- **Analyse:** use a chain such as "rates rise, so repayments rise, so profit after interest falls, so there is less to reinvest".
- **Assess:** compare two influences by size of effect, how soon it hits and whether the business can respond; then judge.
- **Use numbers:** a calculated change (for example 24% of profit) is stronger evidence than the word "significant".
