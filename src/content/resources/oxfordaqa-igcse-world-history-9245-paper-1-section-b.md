**Section B of Paper 1** in OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245) is a knowledge-based depth
study. You study one of four options, and the questions test what you know (AO1) and how well you
explain and judge causes and consequences (AO2). The
[International Relations Depth Studies overview](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-world-history-international-relations-depth-studies/)
introduces the paper; this guide sets out the question types and the core knowledge for each
option, with emphasis on the explanations examiners ask for.

## The four options

1. The origins of the First World War, c1890-1915
2. Hitler's foreign policy and the origins of the Second World War, 1933-1941
3. The origins and development of the Cold War, 1945-1960
4. Détente and the collapse of communism, c1969-1991

Your centre will choose one. There are no prohibited combinations with your Section A option.

## The four questions

| Question | Marks | AOs | Task |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 3 | AO1 | Show knowledge of a given issue |
| 2 | 6 | AO1, AO2 | Explain **one** way in which an issue developed (cause or consequence) |
| 3 | 9 | AO1, AO2 | Explain **two** ways or reasons why an issue developed |
| 4 | 12 | AO1, AO2 | Explain a given cause, consequence or change and at least one other, in a line of reasoning, reaching a judgement on their relative importance |

The 3-mark question rewards relevant points. The 6- and 9-mark questions reward explanation: each
way or reason needs precise knowledge and a clear link showing **how** it caused or affected the
development. The 12-mark question needs a line of reasoning across the whole answer and a judgement
that compares factors, not just a list.

## Option 1: The origins of the First World War, c1890-1915

- **Alliances**: the Triple Alliance of Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy (1882); the
  Franco-Russian Alliance (1894); the Entente Cordiale between Britain and France (1904); the
  Anglo-Russian Agreement (1907). Britain left "Splendid Isolation" as rivalry with Germany grew.
- **Kaiser Wilhelm II**: *Weltpolitik* and the demand for "a place in the sun"; fear of
  encirclement; the naval race after the launch of HMS *Dreadnought* (1906).
- **Crises**: Morocco in 1905-06 and 1911 (Agadir), both of which drew Britain and France closer;
  Austria-Hungary's annexation of Bosnia (1908-09), which angered Serbia and Russia; the Balkan Wars
  (1912-13), which enlarged Serbia.
- **The July Crisis**: Archduke Franz Ferdinand was assassinated in Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by
  Gavrilo Princip, linked to the Black Hand; Austria-Hungary's ultimatum to Serbia was only partly
  accepted; Austria-Hungary declared war on Serbia on 28 July; Russia mobilised; Germany declared
  war on Russia and France.
- **The Schlieffen Plan** sent German troops through Belgium; Britain, a guarantor of Belgian
  neutrality under the 1839 Treaty of London, declared war on 4 August. The Ottoman Empire joined
  Germany later in 1914 and Italy joined the Allies in 1915.

## Option 2: Hitler's foreign policy and the origins of the Second World War, 1933-1941

- **Aims**: overturning Versailles, uniting German-speaking peoples, and *Lebensraum* in the east.
- **1933-35**: Germany left the Disarmament Conference and the League (1933); the non-aggression
  pact with Poland (1934); the Saar voted to return to Germany (1935); conscription was
  reintroduced (1935); Britain, France and Italy formed the Stresa Front (1935), but Britain then
  signed the Anglo-German Naval Agreement (1935).
- **1936-38**: Rhineland remilitarised (March 1936); the Rome-Berlin Axis and the Anti-Comintern
  Pact (1936); German aid to Franco in the Spanish Civil War; the *Anschluss* (March 1938); the
  Munich Agreement over the Sudetenland (September 1938).
- **1939**: the occupation of the rest of Czechoslovakia (March) ended appeasement; Britain and
  France guaranteed Poland; the Pact of Steel with Italy (May); the Nazi-Soviet Pact (23 August);
  the invasion of Poland (1 September); Britain and France declared war (3 September).
- **To 1941**: the war widened with Germany's invasion of the USSR (June 1941) and Japan's attack on
  Pearl Harbor (December 1941).

## Option 3: The origins and development of the Cold War, 1945-1960

- **1945**: the Yalta (February) and Potsdam (July-August) conferences; Truman replaced Roosevelt
  and Attlee replaced Churchill; atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki; Germany and Berlin divided
  into four zones.
- **The Iron Curtain**: Soviet control over Eastern Europe; the communist takeover in
  Czechoslovakia (1948); the Truman Doctrine (1947), offering support to Greece and Turkey and
  setting out containment; Marshall Aid (1947), rejected by the USSR, which created Cominform and
  Comecon; Tito's Yugoslavia broke with Stalin in 1948.
- **Germany**: the Berlin Blockade and Airlift (1948-49); the Federal Republic and the GDR (1949);
  NATO (1949); the first Soviet atomic test (1949).
- **Asia**: the communist victory in China (1949); the Korean War (1950-53), with UN forces under
  MacArthur, Chinese intervention, stalemate and an armistice in 1953; the Geneva Agreement on
  Vietnam (1954).
- **The "Thaw"**: Stalin died in 1953; Austria became neutral (1955); Khrushchev spoke of peaceful
  co-existence; the Warsaw Pact formed (1955); the Hungarian rising of 1956, crushed when Nagy's
  government announced it would leave the Warsaw Pact; Sputnik 1 (1957).

## Option 4: Détente and the collapse of communism, c1969-1991

- **Détente**: motives included the cost of the arms race, the Sino-Soviet split and West German
  *Ostpolitik*; the Anti-Ballistic Missile Treaty and SALT I (1972); Nixon-Brezhnev summits; the
  Helsinki Accords (1975).
- **Collapse of détente**: the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (December 1979); Carter's response,
  including the failure of SALT II to be ratified; the US-led boycott of the 1980 Moscow Olympics
  and the Soviet boycott of 1984; Reagan's renewed Cold War, the Strategic Defence Initiative
  (1983) and new missiles in Europe.
- **Second détente**: Gorbachev (from 1985), the Reykjavik Summit (1986), and the START treaty
  (1991).
- **Collapse**: the costly war in Afghanistan, with Soviet withdrawal completed in 1989; Solidarity
  in Poland under Lech Wałęsa (1980-81), suppressed by martial law, then winning elections in 1989;
  glasnost and perestroika; the opening of the Berlin Wall (November 1989); the Velvet Revolution
  in Czechoslovakia; the end of communist rule in East Germany and Hungary; the collapse of the USSR
  in December 1991.

## Worked example: a 9-mark answer

**Question shape (original):** Explain two reasons why tension between the USA and the USSR
increased in the years 1945-49.

> **Reason one: the division of Germany and the Berlin Blockade.** The zones agreed at Potsdam
> became the front line of the Cold War. In 1948 the Western powers introduced a new currency in
> their zones, and Stalin responded by blockading West Berlin. The USA and Britain supplied the
> city by air until the blockade was lifted in 1949, and the crisis increased tension because both
> sides now saw Germany as a test of strength; it led directly to the creation of NATO in 1949 and
> two separate German states.
>
> **Reason two: the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Aid.** In 1947 Truman promised support to
> countries resisting communism, beginning with Greece and Turkey, and Marshall Aid offered money
> to rebuild Europe. Stalin saw the aid as an attempt to spread American influence, refused it for
> the Eastern bloc and set up Cominform and Comecon. Tension rose because Europe was now divided
> economically as well as politically.

Each reason is named, supported with precise detail and linked to *why* tension increased.

## Worked example: planning a 12-mark answer

**Question shape (original):** "The alliance system was the main reason a regional conflict in the
Balkans became a world war in 1914." How far do you agree?

- **Given factor**: alliances turned the Austro-Serbian war into a European war as Russia, Germany
  and France were drawn in.
- **Other factors**: the Schlieffen Plan, which required an attack on France through Belgium and
  brought Britain in; the naval race and imperial rivalry, which made Britain see Germany as a
  threat; nationalism in the Balkans; decisions by leaders, such as Germany's support for
  Austria-Hungary.
- **Judgement**: the alliances created the framework, but military planning made the escalation
  rapid, and the invasion of Belgium decided Britain's entry; the alliances alone did not make war
  inevitable, since Italy did not join its partners in 1914.

## Common mistakes

- **3-mark questions answered with explanation** instead of three relevant points.
- **Two reasons blended together** in the 9-mark question; separate them clearly.
- **Narrative**: telling the story of events without explaining *how* they caused the development.
- **12-mark answers with no comparison**: explain why one factor matters more than another.
- **Vague detail**: dates, names, treaties and figures turn description into evidence.

## Exam technique

Section B is 30 marks, so allow about 50 minutes of the 1 hour 45 minute paper, with roughly 18
minutes for the 12-mark question. Plan the 12-mark answer before writing.
