These notes condense subtopic **16.2 Asexual and sexual reproduction** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the definitions of the two types of reproduction, examples of asexual reproduction, and the advantages and disadvantages of each. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [16.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-asexual-and-sexual-reproduction/). Then try the [16.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-asexual-and-sexual-reproduction-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Asexual reproduction | A process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent |
| Sexual reproduction | The fusion of haploid nuclei (fertilisation) to form a diploid zygote, producing genetically different offspring |
| Clone | A genetically identical copy of an organism |
| Gamete | A haploid sex cell (such as sperm or egg) |
| Zygote | The diploid cell formed when gametes fuse |

## Examples of asexual reproduction

| Organism | Method |
|---|---|
| Bacteria | Binary fission |
| Yeast | Budding |
| Strawberry | Runners |
| Potato | Tubers |
| Onion, daffodil | Bulbs |
| Many plants | Cuttings (taken by gardeners) |

## Comparison

| Feature | Asexual | Sexual |
|---|---|---|
| Number of parents | One | Usually two |
| Gametes | No | Yes (haploid) |
| Fertilisation | No | Yes |
| Nuclear division | Mitosis | Meiosis to make gametes |
| Offspring | Identical | Different |
| Variation | None | Yes |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Asexual | One parent only; fast; many offspring; no need for a mate; good features are kept | No variation; the whole population is at risk from one disease or change; slow to adapt |
| Sexual | Variation; some may survive a change or disease; natural selection can act; new varieties are possible | Needs two parents and a mate; slower; uses more energy; fewer offspring |

## Linking to natural selection

- Natural selection needs variation.
- Sexual reproduction creates variation; asexual reproduction does not.
- In a changing environment, a varied population is more likely to contain survivors.
- In a stable environment, a population of well-adapted clones does well.

## Which type? Quick decision guide

| Clue in the question | Type |
|---|---|
| One parent only | Asexual |
| Offspring identical to the parent | Asexual |
| Gametes, fertilisation, zygote | Sexual |
| Offspring show variation | Sexual |
| Bud, runner, tuber, bulb, binary fission | Asexual |
| Pollen and ovule | Sexual (plants) |

## Chromosome numbers (human example)

| Stage | Chromosomes |
|---|---|
| Body cell (diploid) | 46 |
| Gamete (haploid) | 23 |
| Zygote (diploid) | 46 |
| Offspring body cells from asexual reproduction | Same as parent |

## Using the ideas in a farming or gardening context

- Cuttings, tubers, bulbs and runners give plants identical to a parent that is known to be good.
- Growing only clones is risky if a disease appears, because there is no variation.
- Plants from seed vary, so some may resist disease or cope with a change in weather.
- Plant breeders use sexual reproduction to produce new varieties, then use asexual methods to copy the best one.

## Worked calculation

*Yeast reproduces by budding. One cell makes a new cell every 90 minutes. How many cells are there after 9 hours, assuming that each cell buds at this rate?*

1. 9 hours = 540 minutes. Number of buddings = 540 divided by 90 = 6.
2. Number of cells = 2 to the power 6 = 64.

All the cells are genetically identical to the original unless a mutation has occurred.

## Short model answers

**Describe asexual reproduction.** One parent produces genetically identical offspring by mitosis; no gametes or fertilisation are involved.

**Describe sexual reproduction.** Haploid gametes from two parents fuse at fertilisation to form a diploid zygote, and the offspring are genetically different.

**State one advantage of asexual reproduction.** Only one parent is needed, so it is fast and uses little energy.

**State one disadvantage of asexual reproduction.** There is no variation, so all the offspring are equally affected by a disease.

**State one advantage of sexual reproduction.** The offspring are genetically different, so some may survive a change in the environment.

## Exam traps

- Offspring of asexual reproduction are **genetically identical**, not "similar".
- Asexual reproduction uses **mitosis**; gametes come from **meiosis**.
- The zygote is **diploid**; the gametes are **haploid**.
- "Discuss" needs advantages **and** disadvantages.
- Variation is an **advantage** in a changing environment.
- A single parent can reproduce sexually in some organisms, but at this level sexual reproduction involves fusion of gametes.
- Gardeners use cuttings to keep the features of a good plant.
- Do not write "cloning" without saying genetically identical.

## Self-test

1. Define asexual reproduction.
2. Define sexual reproduction.
3. How many parents are needed for asexual reproduction?
4. Name the type of nuclear division involved in asexual reproduction.
5. Give two examples of asexual reproduction.
6. Are the gametes haploid or diploid?
7. What is formed when the nuclei of two gametes fuse?
8. State one advantage of asexual reproduction.
9. State one disadvantage of asexual reproduction.
10. State one advantage of sexual reproduction.
11. State one disadvantage of sexual reproduction.
12. Why is variation useful if the environment changes?

### Answers

1. A process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.
2. The fusion of haploid nuclei (fertilisation) to form a diploid zygote, producing genetically different offspring.
3. One.
4. Mitosis.
5. Any two of: binary fission in bacteria; budding in yeast; runners in strawberry; tubers in potato.
6. Haploid.
7. A diploid zygote.
8. It needs only one parent, or it is fast.
9. There is no variation.
10. The offspring are genetically different, giving variation.
11. It needs two parents and is slower.
12. Some individuals may have features that let them survive, so the species is more likely to survive.
