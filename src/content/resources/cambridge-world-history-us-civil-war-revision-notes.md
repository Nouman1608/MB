**Key question 1.4 in Cambridge IGCSE 0470 and O Level 2147 asks why
the United States fought a civil war and what it changed.** You need a
secure 1820–77 timeline, a weighed answer on slavery as a cause, clear
reasons for the North's victory, and a judgement on Reconstruction.
For the full explanation, use the
[US Civil War study guide](/resources/cambridge-world-history-us-civil-war-1820-1877/).

## Timeline to learn

| Year | Event | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| 1820 | Missouri Compromise | Slave/free balance kept; slavery banned north of 36°30′ |
| 1831 | Garrison's *The Liberator* | Start of a radical abolitionist movement |
| 1832–33 | Nullification Crisis | South Carolina challenged federal tariffs: states' rights |
| 1850 | Compromise of 1850 | California free; tougher Fugitive Slave Act angers the North |
| 1852 | *Uncle Tom's Cabin* | Spread anti-slavery feeling in the North |
| 1854 | Kansas–Nebraska Act | Popular sovereignty; "Bleeding Kansas"; Republican Party founded |
| 1857 | Dred Scott decision | Congress cannot ban slavery in the territories |
| 1859 | John Brown's raid, Harpers Ferry | Southern fear of slave risings |
| Nov 1860 | Lincoln elected | Won with no Southern state |
| Dec 1860 | South Carolina secedes | Seven states form the Confederacy by Feb 1861 |
| Apr 1861 | Fort Sumter | War begins; eleven states in the Confederacy |
| 1863 | Emancipation Proclamation; Gettysburg and Vicksburg | Turning points |
| Apr 1865 | Appomattox (9 April); Lincoln assassinated (14 April) | War ends |
| 1865–70 | 13th, 14th, 15th Amendments | Freedom, citizenship, vote |
| 1877 | Compromise of 1877 | Federal troops leave the South: Reconstruction ends |

## Causes: the weighing you need

- **Slavery** — the issue behind almost every crisis, above all its
  spread into the West.
- **States' rights** — mainly the right to protect slavery.
- **Economic differences** — industrial North, cotton South; tariffs.
- **Political breakdown** — the Democrats split in 1860; the new
  Republican Party was purely Northern.
- **Trigger** — Lincoln's election and the secession that followed.

**Line of argument:** slavery was the underlying cause; the other
factors were largely expressions of it, and Lincoln's election was the
trigger.

## Why the North won

| Factor | Key detail |
|---|---|
| Population | About 22 million vs about 9 million (over a third enslaved) |
| Industry and railways | Most factories and track were in the North |
| Navy and blockade | "Anaconda Plan" choked Southern trade |
| Diplomacy | "King Cotton" failed; Britain and France never recognised the Confederacy |
| Emancipation | Changed war aims; about 180,000 Black soldiers joined the Union army |
| Leadership | Lincoln, Grant, Sherman (Atlanta, March to the Sea, 1864) |

**South's strengths:** defending its own ground; Lee and Jackson; high
morale early on. They were not enough in a long war of resources.

## Reconstruction: success or failure?

- **Successes:** slavery abolished (13th); citizenship and equal
  protection (14th); race no bar to voting (15th); Freedmen's Bureau
  schools; Black office-holders such as Senator Hiram Revels.
- **Failures:** Black Codes; Ku Klux Klan violence; most freed people
  became sharecroppers with no land; Northern interest faded; troops
  withdrawn in 1877; voting rights later removed in practice.
- **Politics:** President Andrew Johnson against the Radical
  Republicans; Reconstruction Acts 1867; Johnson impeached 1868 and
  acquitted by one vote.

## Exam traps

- Writing "the war was about states' rights" without saying states'
  rights to do what.
- Dating the Emancipation Proclamation to 1865 — it took effect on
  1 January 1863; the 13th Amendment abolished slavery in 1865.
- Saying the Proclamation freed every enslaved person — it applied to
  areas in rebellion, not to the loyal border states.
- Describing battles in order instead of explaining why the North won.
- Ending Reconstruction in 1865: it runs to 1877.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does "popular sovereignty" mean?** Letting the settlers of a
territory vote on whether to allow slavery, as in the Kansas–Nebraska
Act of 1854.

**Which states were the border states?** Delaware, Maryland, Kentucky
and Missouri: slave states that stayed in the Union.

**When did Reconstruction end?** In 1877, when the last federal troops
were withdrawn from the South after the disputed 1876 election.

## Self-test

1. What did the Missouri Compromise decide?
2. Name two events of the 1850s that increased tension over slavery.
3. Why did Southern states secede after November 1860?
4. Give three reasons why the North won.
5. Name the three Reconstruction amendments and what each did.

**Answers:** 1. Missouri slave, Maine free, no slavery north of 36°30′
in the Louisiana Purchase. 2. Any two: Fugitive Slave Act (1850),
*Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1852), Kansas–Nebraska Act (1854), Dred Scott
(1857), John Brown's raid (1859). 3. Lincoln, opposed to slavery's
spread, won without Southern votes, so they believed slavery was
doomed in the Union. 4. Any three from the table above. 5. 13th
abolished slavery; 14th gave citizenship and equal protection; 15th
banned racial bars to voting.
