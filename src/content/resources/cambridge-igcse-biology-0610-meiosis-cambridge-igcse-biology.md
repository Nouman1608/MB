This guide covers sub-topic **17.3 Meiosis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Both outcomes are Supplement content, so the whole page is **Extended only**: it is tested on Papers 2 and 4, and Core candidates do not need it.

The [inheritance study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-inheritance/) states the two outcomes and gives a short comparison with mitosis. This page goes further: what "reduction division" means for each pair of chromosomes, where meiosis happens in animals and plants, how chromosome numbers change around a life cycle, and why meiosis produces genetically different cells. ## What the syllabus asks for

| Outcome | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| 17.3.1 | State that meiosis is involved in the production of gametes |
| 17.3.2 | Describe meiosis as a reduction division in which the chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid, resulting in genetically different cells (details of the stages of meiosis are not required) |

The outcomes are short, but they connect to haploid and diploid nuclei (17.1), sexual reproduction (16.2) and genetic variation (18.1). Most marks for meiosis come from making those connections clearly.

## Meiosis and gametes

**Meiosis is involved in the production of gametes.** In humans it takes place in the **testes**, where sperm are made, and in the **ovaries**, where egg cells are made. In flowering plants it takes place in the **anthers**, which produce pollen grains, and in the **ovules**.

Meiosis does not take place in the gametes themselves: it is the division that **produces** them. Body cells such as skin, liver and root cells divide by mitosis, not meiosis.

## A reduction division

> Meiosis is a **reduction division** in which the **chromosome number is halved from diploid to haploid**, resulting in **genetically different cells**.

Recall the two terms from 17.1:

- **Diploid**: two sets of chromosomes, a pair of each type. A human diploid cell has 23 pairs, 46 chromosomes.
- **Haploid**: a single set of chromosomes. A human gamete has 23.

"Halved" has a precise meaning. A haploid cell does not get just any half of the chromosomes; it gets **one chromosome from each pair**, so it has one complete set.

### Worked example 1: one of each pair

*A cell of an animal has six chromosomes in three pairs: a long pair, a medium pair and a short pair. Describe the chromosomes in a gamete made from this cell by meiosis.*

- Diploid number = 6; haploid number = 6 ÷ 2 = **3**.
- The gamete contains **one long, one medium and one short chromosome**: one from each pair.
- A gamete with two long chromosomes and one short one would be missing the medium chromosome and the genes it carries, so that is not what meiosis produces.

This is why every gamete carries one allele of each gene: it has one chromosome from each pair, and each chromosome of a pair carries one allele of the genes on it.

## Why the chromosome number must be halved

Fertilisation joins two gametes. If gametes were diploid, the zygote would have four sets, and the chromosome number would double every generation. Halving in meiosis and joining at fertilisation balance each other, so the chromosome number of the species stays the same.

### Worked example 2: a human life cycle

```
adult body cells    46 (diploid)
     │ meiosis (in testes or ovaries)
gametes             23 (haploid)
     │ fertilisation: 23 + 23
zygote              46 (diploid)
     │ mitosis (growth)
adult body cells    46 (diploid)
```

The same pattern applies to any organism that reproduces sexually. For a garden pea, with 14 chromosomes in each body cell: meiosis in the anthers and ovules gives haploid nuclei with 7; fertilisation gives a zygote with 14; mitosis grows the zygote into a new plant with 14 in every body cell.

## Why the cells are genetically different

Meiosis produces cells that are **genetically different** from each other, and from the parent cell. Each gamete receives one chromosome from each pair, and **which chromosome of each pair goes into a particular gamete varies** from one gamete to the next. The two chromosomes of a pair can carry different alleles, so different gametes carry different combinations of alleles.

This is one reason why brothers and sisters differ. Sub-topic 18.1 lists **meiosis**, together with mutation, random mating and random fertilisation, as a source of genetic variation in populations.

## Mitosis and meiosis compared

| Feature | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Type of division | nuclear division | nuclear division (reduction division) |
| Chromosome number in new cells | same as parent (diploid stays diploid) | halved (diploid to haploid) |
| Genetic make-up of new cells | identical | different |
| Where in humans | throughout the body | testes and ovaries |
| Where in flowering plants | for example, root and shoot tips | anthers and ovules |
| Role | growth, repair, replacement, asexual reproduction | production of gametes |

### Worked example 3: deciding which division

| Situation | Division | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Cells in an anther producing pollen grains | meiosis | produces haploid gametes |
| A cut on the hand healing | mitosis | repair of damaged tissues |
| A zygote becoming an embryo | mitosis | growth |
| Egg cells forming in an ovary | meiosis | produces haploid gametes |

### Annotated model answer

*Explain why gametes are produced by meiosis and not by mitosis.* **[3]**

> Meiosis halves the chromosome number from diploid to haploid, but mitosis keeps the chromosome number the same [1]. At fertilisation two gametes fuse, so if gametes were diploid the zygote would have twice the normal number of chromosomes [1]. Haploid gametes give a diploid zygote, so the chromosome number stays constant from generation to generation [1].

A strong answer adds that meiosis gives genetically different gametes, leading to variation in the offspring; this would earn credit if the question had a fourth mark.

## Common mistakes

- Saying meiosis happens in body cells such as skin cells, or "in the gametes".
- Calling gametes diploid, or the zygote haploid.
- Describing "halved" as each chromosome being cut in half. The **number** of chromosomes halves; each gamete receives one **whole** chromosome from each pair.
- Saying meiosis produces genetically identical cells. That is mitosis.
- Mixing up mitosis and meiosis in the answer to a "which division?" question. Ask whether the new cells are gametes: if they are, it is meiosis.
- Writing out the stages of meiosis. They are not required.

## Exam technique

- **State** the role: "meiosis is involved in the production of gametes".
- **Describe** meiosis: reduction division, diploid to haploid, chromosome number halved, genetically different cells. Each idea can be a separate mark.
- **Calculate**: halve the diploid number for a gamete; double the haploid number for a body cell.
- **Explain** its importance: link halving to fertilisation and a constant chromosome number; link genetic differences to variation.
- **Compare** with mitosis: one point about chromosome number, one about genetic make-up, one about role.
