## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which substance is broken down by pectinase?

A: a protein in cell membranes

B: a substance in plant cell walls

C: starch in plant cells

D: the sugar in milk

**[1]**

**2.** State the useful product of anaerobic respiration in yeast during the production of biofuel. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** reasons why bread dough is left in a warm place before baking. **[2]**

**4.** Match each stain to the enzyme in a biological washing powder that digests it: (i) olive oil, (ii) egg white, (iii) mashed potato. **[3]**

**5.** **(Extended)** Name the products formed when lactase acts on lactose. **[2]**

**6.** **(Extended)** Which row is correct?

A: insulin, made by a fungus

B: penicillin, made by bacteria

C: mycoprotein, made by a fungus

D: mycoprotein, made by bacteria

**[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Describe the role of anaerobic respiration in yeast during the production of ethanol for biofuels. **[3]**

**8.** A student investigated the effect of temperature on the rise of bread dough. Equal masses of dough were put in measuring cylinders in water baths for 30 minutes.

| Temperature / °C | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rise in volume / cm³ | 4 | 12 | 24 | 30 | 14 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of rise at 40 °C. Give the unit. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the results. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the result at 60 °C. **[2]**

**(d)** State **two** variables the student should keep constant. **[2]**

**9.** A student tested the effect of pectinase on juice from crushed plums. Three repeats were done with pectinase and three without. The volumes of juice collected after 15 minutes are shown.

| | Repeat 1 | Repeat 2 | Repeat 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Without pectinase / cm³ | 10.0 | 11.0 | 10.5 |
| With pectinase / cm³ | 18.5 | 12.0 | 19.5 |

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean volume with pectinase, leaving out the anomalous result. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean volume when pectinase was used. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why more juice is collected with pectinase. **[2]**

**10.** A company advertises that its biological washing powder "cleans at 30 °C". Explain why the powder works well at 30 °C but would work no better than a non-biological powder at 80 °C, and give **one** advantage of washing at 30 °C. **[4]**

**11.** **(Extended)** Explain why lactase is used to produce lactose-free milk. **[3]**

**12.** **(Extended)** A fermenter is used to grow the fungus *Penicillium*.

**(a)** Explain why the fermenter has a water jacket. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the pH is monitored and controlled. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a source of nitrogen is added to the nutrients. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain why the fermenter and nutrients are sterilised before use. **[2]**

**13.** **(Extended)** In a fermenter, the mass of mycoprotein rose from 150 kg to 420 kg between hour 10 and hour 28.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of production. **[2]**

**(b)** At hour 28, the air supply failed. Predict and explain the effect on the rate of production. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The substance is pectin; option A describes cell membranes, which is the most common wrong idea.

**2.** Ethanol (alcohol) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Warmth speeds up the enzyme-controlled reactions of respiration in the yeast [1]; so more carbon dioxide is produced and the dough rises faster [1]. **[2]**

**4.** (i) Lipase [1]; (ii) protease [1]; (iii) amylase [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Glucose [1]; galactose [1]. **[2]**

**6.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Insulin is made by genetically modified bacteria and penicillin by a fungus.

**7.** Yeast is given sugar from plant material and kept without oxygen [1]; it respires anaerobically, converting the sugar (glucose) into ethanol and carbon dioxide [1]; the ethanol is separated and used as a fuel [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The answer must name ethanol as the product of respiration; "yeast makes the fuel" is too vague.

**8. (a)** 30 ÷ 30 = **1.0** [1]; **cm³ per minute** [1].

**(b)** The rise increases from 4 cm³ at 10 °C to a maximum of 30 cm³ at 40 °C [1]; then decreases to 0 cm³ at 60 °C [1].

**(c)** Yeast enzymes are denatured (active sites change shape) [1]; so anaerobic respiration stops and no carbon dioxide is produced [1].

**(d)** Any two: mass of dough [1]; mass/type of yeast [1]; (also accept volume of water, mass of sugar, time, size of measuring cylinder). **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), quoting figures and the peak temperature is what lifts a description to full marks.

**9. (a)** 12.0 cm³ (with pectinase, repeat 2) [1].

**(b)** (18.5 + 19.5) ÷ 2 = **19.0 cm³** [1].

**(c)** Mean without = (10.0 + 11.0 + 10.5) ÷ 3 = 10.5 cm³ [1]; (19.0 − 10.5) ÷ 10.5 × 100 = **81%** (80.95%) [1].

**(d)** Pectinase breaks down pectin [1]; in the cell walls, so juice is released more easily from the cells [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Allow error carried forward in (c) from a wrong mean in (b), provided the method is correct.

**10.** At 30 °C the enzymes are near their optimum and digest the stains [1]; into small soluble molecules that wash away [1]; at 80 °C the enzymes are denatured, so only the detergent works [1]; advantage: washing at a lower temperature uses less energy (or: delicate fabrics are less likely to be damaged) [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Lactase breaks down lactose [1]; into glucose and galactose [1]; some people do not produce enough lactase to digest lactose, so they can drink the treated milk [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Respiration by the fungus releases heat [1]; cold water in the jacket removes the heat [1]; which keeps the temperature near the optimum so enzymes are not denatured (and growth stays fast) [1].

**(b)** Enzymes work best at an optimum pH [1]; waste carbon dioxide can lower the pH, so acid or alkali is added to keep it near the optimum [1].

**(c)** Nitrogen is needed to make amino acids [1]; and so proteins, for growth [1].

**(d)** To kill other microorganisms [1]; which would compete for nutrients or contaminate the product [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* The fact that respiration releases heat is the key idea in (a); answers that say the jacket "heats the fermenter" earn nothing.

**13. (a)** (420 − 150) ÷ (28 − 10) = 270 ÷ 18 [1]; = **15 kg per hour** [1].

**(b)** The rate would fall [1]; because the fungus would not have oxygen for aerobic respiration [1]; so less energy would be released for growth [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the useful products of bread and biofuel production.
- Describing temperature results without quoting figures or the peak.
- Including an anomalous result in a mean.
- Writing "enzyme killed" instead of "denatured".
- Giving only one product of lactose breakdown.
- Linking the wrong organism to insulin, penicillin or mycoprotein.
- Leaving out the unit on a rate.
