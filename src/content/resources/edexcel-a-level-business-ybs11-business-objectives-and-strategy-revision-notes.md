## The chain from mission to action

| Level | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mission | Why the business exists | "Safe, affordable travel for our region" |
| Aims | Long-term goals | Be the preferred provider |
| Objectives | Measurable, time-limited targets | 15% market share within three years |
| Strategy | Long-term plan to meet the objectives | Expand into new towns |
| Tactics | Short-term actions | Three-month discount fare |

**Appraising a mission statement:** is it clear, distinctive, realistic and measurable in effect? Weak ones are vague ("be the best"), identical to rivals', unrelated to behaviour, or not understood by staff.

## Formula and method

```
Sales at a market share = share x market size
Market after growth     = market x (1 + growth rate) ^ years
Relative market share   = own share / largest rival's share
```

## Ansoff's Matrix

| | Existing products | New products |
|---|---|---|
| Existing markets | Market penetration (lowest risk) | Product development |
| New markets | Market development | Diversification (highest risk) |

- Penetration: promotion, price cuts, loyalty schemes.
- Market development: new regions, new customer groups, export.
- Product development: new or improved products for current customers.
- Diversification: spreads risk across markets but the business has the least experience.
- Choose by resources, appetite for risk and the state of the current market.

## Porter's Strategic Matrix

| | Low cost | Differentiation |
|---|---|---|
| Broad market | Cost leadership | Differentiation |
| Narrow segment | Cost focus | Differentiation focus |

- Cost leadership needs scale, efficiency and tight control.
- Differentiation needs investment in quality, design and brand, and customers willing to pay.
- "Stuck in the middle": no clear advantage in cost or distinctiveness.

## Portfolio analysis

- **Aim:** to balance the product range so cash from mature products funds new ones, and to decide which to invest in, keep, harvest or drop.
- **Boston Matrix:** market growth (high or low) against relative market share (high or low).

| Category | Growth | Relative share | Typical action |
|---|---|---|---|
| Star | High | High | Invest to keep the lead |
| Question mark | High | Low | Invest selectively or drop |
| Cash cow | Low | High | Harvest cash for others |
| Dog | Low | Low | Drop or restructure |

- **Limits:** only two factors; ignores links between products; labels can demotivate.

## Strategic or tactical?

| | Strategic | Tactical |
|---|---|---|
| Time scale | Long term | Short term |
| Scope | Whole business | A function or product |
| Resources | Major finance, physical and human commitment | Existing resources |
| Reversibility | Difficult | Easier |

## SWOT, PESTLE and five forces

| Tool | What it covers | Main limit |
|---|---|---|
| SWOT | Internal strengths and weaknesses; external opportunities and threats | A snapshot; subjective; gives no action alone |
| PESTLE | Political, economic, social, technological, legal, environmental | A list is not analysis; must prioritise |
| Five forces | Rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, supplier power, buyer power | Industry level, not firm level; changes over time |

- Place factors correctly: new competitors and tax changes are external; staff skills and cash reserves are internal.
- More intense forces mean lower industry profits.
- The changing competitive environment: technology can lower entry barriers; mergers change buyer and supplier power.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mission statement | General statement of purpose and values |
| Corporate objective | Specific, measurable target for the whole business |
| Market penetration | Selling more existing products in existing markets |
| Diversification | New products in new markets |
| Relative market share | Own share divided by the largest rival's share |
| Barriers to entry | Costs or rules that make market entry hard |
| Substitute | Different product meeting the same need |

## Exam traps

- A mission statement is not an objective.
- Market development is a new **market**; product development is a new **product**.
- Relative share is a ratio to the largest rival, not a plain percentage.
- Do not place external factors under strengths and weaknesses.
- For **assess**, judge the strategy against the firm's resources and risk, not just describe the model.
- Use the figures in the source: a growth target that is much faster than the market is a warning sign.

## Self-test

1. A market is worth $250m and grows by 4% a year. Calculate its value after two years.
2. A firm has 8% of the $250m market now. Calculate its current sales.
3. It targets 10% of the market in two years. Calculate the sales needed and the percentage increase.
4. A product has 15% share and the largest rival has 30%. Calculate relative market share.
5. Which Ansoff strategy is selling an existing product in a new country?
6. Which Ansoff strategy carries the highest risk?
7. State one limitation of the Boston Matrix.
8. Name the two parts of the "narrow segment" row of Porter's Strategic Matrix.
9. Distinguish a strategic decision from a tactical decision.
10. State the five forces in Porter's model.

### Answers

1. 250 x 1.04 x 1.04 = **$270.4m**.
2. 0.08 x 250 = **$20m**.
3. 0.10 x 270.4 = **$27.04m**; increase = (27.04 - 20) / 20 = **35.2%**.
4. 15 / 30 = **0.5**.
5. Market development.
6. Diversification.
7. Any one: only two factors; ignores links between products; labels can demotivate staff.
8. Cost focus and differentiation focus.
9. A strategic decision is long term, high risk, affects the whole business and is hard to reverse; a tactical decision is short term, uses existing resources and is easier to change.
10. Rivalry, threat of new entrants, threat of substitutes, bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers.

## Answer frames

- **Apply a model:** name the model, place the business in it with evidence, then say what it implies for the decision.
- **Analyse a strategy:** link the strategy to the objective ("market penetration raises share, which meets the 15% target") and to a cost or risk.
- **Discuss a tool:** give one benefit, one limit and a short verdict on how useful it is for this business.
- **Assess a choice between strategies:** compare risk, cost, time and fit with objectives, then judge, and say what the decision depends on.
- **Essay planning:** use the model as the structure, not the whole answer; every paragraph needs evidence from the source.
