## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Blood returns from the lungs and leaves the heart in the aorta. Name, in order, the chambers and valves it passes through. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the walls of the ventricles are thicker than the walls of the atria. **[2]**

**3.** Which vessel carries deoxygenated blood away from the heart?

A: Aorta

B: Pulmonary artery

C: Pulmonary vein

D: Vena cava **[1]**

**4.** Describe two features of capillaries and explain how each helps metabolic exchange. **[2]**

**5.** Explain the role of collagen and of elastic fibres in the wall of an artery. **[2]**

**6.** One cardiac cycle takes 0.85 s and the stroke volume is 68 cm³. Calculate the cardiac output. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** The table shows pressures and volume on the left side of a heart during one cardiac cycle of 0.75 s.

| Time (s) | Left atrium (kPa) | Left ventricle (kPa) | Aorta (kPa) | Ventricle volume (cm³) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.00 | 0.8 | 0.6 | 10.6 | 118 |
| 0.08 | 1.5 | 1.2 | 10.2 | 128 |
| 0.12 | 1.2 | 6.0 | 10.0 | 128 |
| 0.20 | 0.9 | 15.5 | 15.0 | 100 |
| 0.35 | 1.1 | 9.0 | 13.0 | 56 |
| 0.50 | 1.3 | 0.5 | 11.5 | 90 |

**(a)** Between which two times does the atrioventricular valve close? **[1]**

**(b)** At which time is the semilunar valve open? **[1]**

**(c)** At which time are both valves closed while the ventricle volume is at its lowest? **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the stroke volume. **[1]**

**(e)** Calculate the cardiac output. **[2]**

**8.** In some kidney diseases, plasma proteins are lost from the blood in the urine. Explain why this can lead to swelling of the tissues. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why tissue fluid builds up if lymph vessels in a limb are blocked. **[2]**

**10.** During exercise, a person's cardiac output rose from 5.0 dm³ per minute to 20.0 dm³ per minute, while heart rate rose from 70 to 160 beats per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the stroke volume, in cm³, before and during exercise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in stroke volume. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why the volume of blood in the ventricle stays the same at the start of ventricular contraction, even though the pressure is rising. **[3]**

**12.** Explain the advantages to a mammal of a double circulation. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how blood in the veins of the legs returns to the heart, even though pressure in these veins is low. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Left atrium → left atrioventricular valve → left ventricle [1] → semilunar valve → aorta [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Blood from the lungs arrives in the pulmonary vein and enters the left side of the heart.

**2.** The atria only pump blood into the ventricles, a short distance at low pressure [1]. The ventricles pump blood out of the heart to the lungs or the whole body, which needs a higher pressure, so more muscle [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link wall thickness to the pressure each chamber must generate.

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An artery carries blood away from the heart; the pulmonary artery carries deoxygenated blood to the lungs.

**4.** Any two, each with its explanation: wall one cell thick, so a short diffusion distance [1]; many narrow, branched capillaries, so a large surface area close to every cell [1]; narrow lumen, so red blood cells pass slowly and close to the wall; gaps between endothelial cells, so fluid and small solutes can leave. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A feature without its effect on exchange does not score.

**5.** Collagen makes the wall strong, so it withstands high pressure without bursting [1]. Elastic fibres stretch as blood is ejected and then recoil, smoothing the flow and maintaining pressure between beats [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Elastic recoil does not "pump" blood; it maintains pressure.

**6.** Heart rate = 60 ÷ 0.85 = 70.6 beats per minute [1]. Cardiac output = 70.6 × 68 [1] = **4800 cm³ per minute** (4.8 dm³ per minute) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded heart rate: 60 ÷ 0.85 × 68 is exactly 4800.

**7. (a)** Between **0.08 s and 0.12 s** [1].

**(b)** **0.20 s** [1].

**(c)** **0.35 s** [1].

**(d)** 128 − 56 = **72 cm³** [1].

**(e)** Heart rate = 60 ÷ 0.75 = 80 beats per minute [1]; cardiac output = 80 × 72 = **5760 cm³ per minute** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a), the AV valve closes when ventricular pressure rises above atrial pressure: 1.2 against 1.5 at 0.08 s, but 6.0 against 1.2 at 0.12 s. For (c), at 0.35 s ventricular pressure (9.0) is below aortic (13.0) but above atrial (1.1).

**8.** With fewer plasma proteins, the water potential of the plasma is higher (less negative) [1]. Less water returns into the capillaries by osmosis at the venule end [1]. More fluid stays in the tissues than the lymph vessels can drain, so tissue fluid accumulates and the tissues swell [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name osmosis and the venule end. Saying "less fluid goes back" without a reason earns one mark.

**9.** Not all tissue fluid returns to the capillaries; the excess normally drains into lymph vessels [1]. If they are blocked, the excess fluid cannot drain away and returns to the blood, so it accumulates in the tissues [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* This tests the final part of the specification statement: the return of tissue fluid to the circulatory system.

**10. (a)** Before: 5000 ÷ 70 = **71.4 cm³** [1]. During: 20 000 ÷ 160 = **125 cm³** [1].

**(b)** (125 − 71.4) ÷ 71.4 × 100 = **75%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert dm³ to cm³ first: 5.0 dm³ = 5000 cm³.

**11.** Ventricular pressure is above atrial pressure, so the atrioventricular valve is closed [1]. It is still below arterial pressure, so the semilunar valve is also closed [1]. With both valves closed, no blood can enter or leave, so the volume is constant while the muscle contracts and the pressure rises [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give both pressure comparisons; one valve alone does not explain a constant volume.

**12.** Blood loses pressure passing through the lung capillaries; returning it to the heart allows the pressure to be raised again before it goes to the body [1], so blood reaches the body tissues quickly, at high pressure [1]. The lungs receive blood at a lower pressure, which avoids damaging their delicate capillaries (and oxygenated and deoxygenated blood are kept separate) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Blood goes through the heart twice" describes a double circulation; it does not explain the advantage.

**13.** Veins have valves that close if blood starts to flow backwards [1]. Contraction of the skeletal muscles around the veins squeezes them and pushes blood towards the heart [1]. The wide lumen of veins offers little resistance to flow [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Valves do not push blood; they stop it flowing back. The squeezing comes from surrounding muscles.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying valves are moved by muscles rather than pressure differences.
- Comparing the wrong pressures when finding valve events.
- Forgetting to convert cycle time to heart rate before calculating cardiac output.
- Mixing up dm³ and cm³.
- Explaining tissue fluid return by hydrostatic pressure instead of osmosis.
- Describing a double circulation without explaining its advantage.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.2.6 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**How do I find valve events in a pressure table?**
Compare the pairs of pressures. The AV valve depends on atrium against ventricle; the semilunar valve depends on ventricle against artery. A valve changes state where the higher pressure switches sides.
