## Equations

| Equation | Units | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| moment = force × perpendicular distance from the pivot | N m | Core |
| k = F / x (spring constant) | N/m (or N/cm) | Extended only |
| F = m a (F is the resultant force) | N = kg × m/s² | Extended only |

## Effects of forces (1.5.1)

- Forces can change the **size and shape** of an object.
- **Extension** = stretched length − original length.
- Load–extension graph for a spring: straight line through the origin, then curves.
- *(Extended)* **Limit of proportionality:** the point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to load (the end of the straight part).
- *(Extended)* k = F/x applies only up to the limit of proportionality. Stiffer spring → larger k → steeper load–extension line.

**Resultant along a line**

- Same direction: add. Opposite directions: subtract; resultant points the way of the larger force.
- Zero resultant → object stays at rest **or** keeps moving at constant speed in a straight line.
- A resultant force can change **speed** or **direction** (or both).

**Friction and drag**

- Solid friction: between two surfaces; may impede motion; produces **heating**.
- Drag: friction acting on an object moving through a **liquid** or a **gas** (air resistance).

**F = ma (Extended only)**

1. Find the resultant force (with direction).
2. a = F/m; acceleration is in the **same direction** as the resultant force.
3. Resultant opposite to motion → deceleration.

**Circular motion (Extended only)**

- Force acts **perpendicular to the motion**, towards the centre.
- Same m and r, larger force → **larger speed**.
- Same m and speed, larger force → **smaller radius**.
- Larger m → **larger force** needed for the same speed and radius.

## Turning effect (1.5.2)

- Moment: measure of the turning effect of a force.
- Larger moment from a larger force or a larger perpendicular distance (long spanner, handle far from the hinge).
- **Principle of moments:** in equilibrium, total clockwise moment = total anticlockwise moment about the pivot.
- **Equilibrium:** no resultant force **and** no resultant moment.
- *(Extended)* With several forces on one side, **add** their moments first.
- *(Extended)* Experiment: balance a metre rule on a pivot with weights each side; calculate the clockwise and anticlockwise totals; they are equal.

### Method for moment calculations

1. Mark the pivot; measure every distance **from the pivot**.
2. Convert cm to m if you want N m.
3. List clockwise and anticlockwise moments separately.
4. Set the totals equal; solve.
5. Include the beam's weight at its centre of gravity if it is not over the pivot.

## Centre of gravity (1.5.3)

- The point through which the whole weight of an object appears to act.
- Irregular lamina: hang from a pin with a plumb line; mark the line; repeat from another hole; the lines cross at the centre of gravity.
- Topples when the vertical line through the centre of gravity passes **outside the base**.
- More stable: **lower** centre of gravity, **wider** base.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Resultant force | Single force with the same effect as all the forces acting |
| Solid friction | Force between two surfaces that may impede motion and produce heating |
| Drag | Friction on an object moving through a liquid or gas |
| Spring constant (Extended) | Force per unit extension |
| Limit of proportionality (Extended) | Point beyond which extension is no longer proportional to load |
| Moment | Force × perpendicular distance from the pivot |
| Equilibrium | No resultant force and no resultant moment |
| Centre of gravity | Point where the whole weight appears to act |

## Exam traps

- Plotting length instead of extension.
- "A moving object needs a force to keep it moving": not if there is no friction.
- Giving a resultant force with no direction.
- Moments with distances from the end of the beam, not the pivot.
- Mixing cm and m in one moment equation.
- Stating only one condition for equilibrium.
- *(Extended)* Using the driving force, not the resultant, in F = ma.
- *(Extended)* Writing "elastic limit" for the limit of proportionality.
- *(Extended)* An outward force in circular motion; the force is towards the centre.

## Quick self-test

1. A spring's length increases from 12.0 cm to 15.5 cm. Find its extension.
2. Forces of 40 N east and 65 N west act on a box. Find the resultant.
3. State what happens to an object moving at constant speed if the resultant force on it is zero.
4. Name the force that opposes a cyclist moving through air.
5. Find the moment of a 25 N force applied 0.40 m from a pivot, at right angles.
6. A 200 N weight is 1.2 m left of a pivot. What force 0.80 m to the right balances it?
7. State the two conditions for equilibrium.
8. Give two ways to make a lamp more stable.
9. *(Extended)* A 6.0 N load stretches a spring by 0.15 m. Find k.
10. *(Extended)* A 0.50 kg trolley has a resultant force of 1.5 N. Find its acceleration.
11. *(Extended)* A 2.0 N weight 30 cm left and a 3.0 N weight 10 cm left of a pivot balance a weight 40 cm right. Find it.
12. *(Extended)* A ball whirled on a string in a horizontal circle is swung faster at the same radius. What happens to the tension needed?

### Answers

1. 15.5 − 12.0 = **3.5 cm**.
2. 65 − 40 = **25 N west**.
3. It **continues at constant speed in a straight line**.
4. **Air resistance** (drag).
5. 25 × 0.40 = **10 N m**.
6. 200 × 1.2 = F × 0.80, so F = **300 N**.
7. **No resultant force** and **no resultant moment**.
8. **Lower** its centre of gravity (heavy base) and **widen** its base.
9. k = 6.0 ÷ 0.15 = **40 N/m**.
10. a = 1.5 ÷ 0.50 = **3.0 m/s²**.
11. (2.0 × 30) + (3.0 × 10) = 90 N cm = W × 40, so W = **2.25 N** (2.3 N).
12. It must **increase**: a larger speed at the same radius and mass needs a larger force towards the centre.
