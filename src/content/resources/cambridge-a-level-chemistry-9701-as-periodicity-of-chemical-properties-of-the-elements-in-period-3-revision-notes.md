These notes condense subtopic **9.2, Periodicity of chemical properties of the elements in Period 3** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Full explanations are in the [9.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-periodicity-of-chemical-properties-of-the-elements-in-period-3/). The [Periodicity Across Period 3 revision notes](/resources/as-chem-periodicity-revision-notes/) summarise the whole topic.

## The compounds the syllabus names

- With oxygen: **Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂**.
- With chlorine: **NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, SiCl₄, PCl₅**.
- With water: **Na and Mg only**.
- Oxide acid/base behaviour: Na₂O, MgO, Al₂O₃, P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃; hydroxides NaOH, Mg(OH)₂, Al(OH)₃; bases used: **sodium hydroxide only**.

## Elements with water

```
2Na + 2H2O  -> 2NaOH + H2          fizzes, melts into a ball, moves about
Mg  + 2H2O  -> Mg(OH)2 + H2        cold water: very slow
Mg  + H2O   -> MgO + H2            steam: bright white glow
```

## Oxidation numbers

```
Na2O +1   MgO +2   Al2O3 +3   P4O10 +5   SO2 +4   SO3 +6
NaCl +1   MgCl2 +2 AlCl3 +3   SiCl4 +4   PCl5 +5
```

- The Period 3 element is **less electronegative** than O or Cl, so it is positive.
- Maximum value = **number of outer-shell electrons**.
- SO₂ (+4) uses only four of sulfur's six outer electrons.

## Oxides: the three classes

| Class | Oxides | With water (pH) | Reacts with HCl? | Reacts with NaOH? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Basic (ionic) | Na₂O, MgO | NaOH (13–14); Mg(OH)₂ (9–10) | Yes | No |
| Amphoteric | Al₂O₃ | Insoluble (7) | Yes | Yes |
| Giant covalent | SiO₂ | Insoluble (7) | — | — |
| Acidic (covalent) | P₄O₁₀, SO₂, SO₃ | H₃PO₄ (0–2); H₂SO₃ (2–3); H₂SO₄ (0–1) | No | Yes |

Key equations:

```
Na2O + 2HCl  -> 2NaCl + H2O
MgO  + 2HCl  -> MgCl2 + H2O
Al2O3 + 6HCl -> 2AlCl3 + 3H2O
Al2O3 + 2NaOH + 3H2O -> 2NaAl(OH)4
Al(OH)3 + NaOH -> NaAl(OH)4
P4O10 + 12NaOH -> 4Na3PO4 + 6H2O
SO2 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO3 + H2O
SO3 + 2NaOH -> Na2SO4 + H2O
```

Why basic: O²⁻ accepts protons (O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻). Why amphoteric: small, highly charged Al³⁺ polarises O²⁻, so Al₂O₃ has considerable covalent character.

## Chlorides with water

| Chloride | Process | pH |
|---|---|---|
| NaCl | Dissolves only | 7 |
| MgCl₂ | Dissolves; very slight hydrolysis | about 6.5 |
| AlCl₃ | [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ⇌ [Al(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H⁺ | about 3 |
| SiCl₄ | SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl (white solid, misty fumes) | about 1–2 |
| PCl₅ | PCl₅ + 4H₂O → H₃PO₄ + 5HCl (misty fumes) | about 1–2 |

## The one explanation behind everything

Electronegativity of the element rises across the period → difference from O or Cl **falls** → bonding goes **ionic → ionic with covalent character → covalent** → oxides go basic → amphoteric → acidic, and chlorides go dissolving → hydrolysing.

## Bonding from observations

- High melting point, conducts when molten, neutral solution → **ionic**.
- Fumes in moist air, low boiling point, acidic solution → **simple covalent**.
- Very high melting point, insoluble, non-conducting → **giant covalent**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Basic oxide | Oxide that reacts with acids to form a salt and water |
| Acidic oxide | Oxide that reacts with bases (here, NaOH) to form a salt and water, and with water to form an acid |
| Amphoteric | Reacts with both acids and bases |
| Hydrolysis | Breaking of a compound by reaction with water |
| Polarisation | Distortion of an anion's electron cloud by a nearby cation |
| Electronegativity | The power of an atom to attract the bonding electrons in a covalent bond |
| Sodium aluminate | NaAl(OH)₄, formed when Al₂O₃ or Al(OH)₃ reacts with NaOH |

## Exam traps

- MgO instead of Mg(OH)₂ as the product with cold water.
- SO₂ given as +6.
- Al₂O₃ shown reacting with water.
- MgO given pH 13.
- "NaCl hydrolyses": it dissolves.
- Forgetting the misty HCl fumes for SiCl₄ and PCl₅.
- Showing Mg(OH)₂ reacting with NaOH.
- Unbalanced aluminate equations: check Al, Na, O and H.

## Self-test

1. Write the equation for magnesium reacting with steam.
2. State the oxidation number of phosphorus in P₄O₁₀ and of sulfur in SO₂.
3. Why is the maximum oxidation number of silicon +4?
4. Write the equation for Al(OH)₃ reacting with hydrochloric acid.
5. Write the equation for Al(OH)₃ reacting with sodium hydroxide.
6. Write the equation for SO₃ reacting with sodium hydroxide.
7. Why does MgO give a less alkaline solution than Na₂O?
8. Explain why a solution of AlCl₃ is acidic.
9. Give the equation and one observation for SiCl₄ added to water.
10. Explain, using electronegativity, why NaCl dissolves but PCl₅ is hydrolysed.
11. A white solid melts at a high temperature, conducts when molten and gives a neutral solution. Suggest its bonding.
12. How many moles of NaOH react with 0.0200 mol of SO₂ to form Na₂SO₃?

**Answers:**

1. Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂
2. P: +5; S: +4.
3. Silicon has four outer-shell electrons, and it can use all four in bonding.
4. Al(OH)₃ + 3HCl → AlCl₃ + 3H₂O
5. Al(OH)₃ + NaOH → NaAl(OH)₄
6. SO₃ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + H₂O
7. Mg(OH)₂ is only sparingly soluble, so fewer OH⁻ ions are in solution.
8. The small, highly charged Al³⁺ ion in [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ polarises the water molecules around it, so H⁺ is released: [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ⇌ [Al(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H⁺.
9. SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl; misty fumes, or a white solid forms.
10. The electronegativity difference between Na and Cl is large, so NaCl is ionic and dissolves as hydrated ions. The difference between P and Cl is small, so P–Cl bonds are covalent and are attacked directly by water.
11. Ionic.
12. 0.0400 mol (SO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + H₂O).
