These questions cover section **3.1.1.2 Mass number and isotopes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the ideas first in the [Mass number and isotopes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-mass-number-and-isotopes/) and the [Mass number and isotopes revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-mass-number-and-isotopes-revision-notes/). Questions on the wider topic are in the [Atomic Structure practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-atomic-structure-practice/). Use the Avogadro constant L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ where needed.

## Questions

**1.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in each species.

**(a)** ³⁴S²⁻ **[1]**

**(b)** ⁵²Cr³⁺ **[1]**

**(c)** ¹⁹F⁻ **[1]**

**2.** A particle contains 29 protons, 36 neutrons and 27 electrons. Write its symbol, showing the mass number and the charge. **[2]**

**3.** Carbon-12 and carbon-14 are isotopes of carbon.

**(a)** Define the term *isotopes*. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why carbon-12 and carbon-14 have the same chemical properties. **[2]**

**(c)** State one property that differs between the two isotopes. **[1]**

**4.** A sample of krypton is analysed in a time-of-flight mass spectrometer using electron impact.

**(a)** Write an equation, with state symbols, for the ionisation of a krypton atom. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the atoms must be ionised. **[1]**

**(c)** State what the acceleration stage achieves. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain how the detector produces a signal that measures abundance. **[2]**

**5.** A protein is analysed using electrospray ionisation.

**(a)** Write an equation for the ionisation of a protein molecule, X. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why electrospray, rather than electron impact, is used for a protein. **[1]**

**(c)** The main peak appears at m/z 5734. State the Mᵣ of the protein. **[1]**

**6.** The mass spectrum of an element shows three peaks: m/z 32 (relative height 47.5), m/z 33 (0.4) and m/z 34 (2.1).

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of the element. Give your answer to an appropriate number of significant figures. **[3]**

**(b)** Identify the element. **[1]**

**7.** Element Q has two isotopes, of mass 69 and 71. Its relative atomic mass is 69.8. Calculate the percentage abundance of the isotope of mass 69. **[2]**

**8.** An element has three isotopes, of mass 24, 25 and 26. The isotope of mass 25 makes up 10.0% of the atoms, and the relative atomic mass is 24.3. Calculate the percentage abundances of the other two isotopes. **[3]**

**9.** In a time-of-flight mass spectrometer, ⁸⁵Rb⁺ ions are accelerated to a kinetic energy of 4.00 × 10⁻¹⁶ J and travel along a 1.80 m flight tube. KE = ½mv², where m is in kg and v is in m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the mass, in kg, of one ⁸⁵Rb⁺ ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken for a ⁸⁵Rb⁺ ion to reach the detector. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the time taken for a ⁸⁷Rb⁺ ion under the same conditions. **[1]**

**10.** A molecule is ionised by electron impact. The molecular ion, with a kinetic energy of 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁶ J, takes 1.854 × 10⁻⁵ s to travel 1.20 m. KE = ½mv².

**(a)** Calculate the relative mass of the ion. **[4]**

**(b)** State the m/z of the main peak if the same compound were analysed using electrospray ionisation. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes: "The acceleration stage makes all the ions travel at the same speed, so they are separated by mass at the detector." Explain what is wrong with this statement and give a correct account. **[3]**

**12.** A student writes: "The mass spectrum of chlorine has a peak at m/z 35.5." Explain why this is wrong. **[2]**

**13.** A sample of neon contains the isotopes ²⁰Ne and ²²Ne. Describe how a time-of-flight mass spectrometer separates these isotopes, and explain how the spectrum is then used to find the relative atomic mass of the sample. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 16 protons, 18 neutrons, 18 electrons [1]

**(b)** 24 protons, 28 neutrons, 21 electrons [1]

**(c)** 9 protons, 10 neutrons, 10 electrons [1]

*Examiner insight:* For a negative ion, the electron count is above the proton count; subtracting the charge as a positive number gives 14 electrons for S²⁻, which is wrong.

**2.** Mass number 29 + 36 = 65, element copper (Z = 29) [1]; charge 29 − 27 = 2+, so **⁶⁵Cu²⁺** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Both the mass number and the charge must be in the correct positions on the symbol.

**3. (a)** Atoms of the same element with the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons [1]

**(b)** They have the same number of electrons and the same electron configuration [1]; chemical reactions involve electrons, so their reactions are the same [1]

**(c)** Mass (or density, or rate of diffusion) [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "Same number of protons" does not explain the chemistry; the mark is for the electron configuration.

**4. (a)** **Kr(g) → Kr⁺(g) + e⁻** [1]

**(b)** Only charged particles can be accelerated by the electric field (and detected) [1]

**(c)** All the ions are given the same kinetic energy [1]

**(d)** Positive ions gain electrons at the detector, producing a current [1]; the size of the current is proportional to the number of ions arriving [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Electrons can be written on the right as e⁻; leaving the state symbols off the atoms and ion loses the mark in (a).

**5. (a)** **X(g) + H⁺ → XH⁺(g)** [1]

**(b)** Electrospray is gentle, so the large molecule is ionised without breaking into fragments [1]

**(c)** 5734 − 1 = **5733** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The ion carries an extra proton, so the peak is one unit above Mᵣ.

**6. (a)** Total height = 47.5 + 0.4 + 2.1 = 50.0 [1]; Σ(m/z × height) = 1520 + 13.2 + 71.4 = 1604.6 [1]; Aᵣ = 1604.6 ÷ 50.0 = **32.1** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** **Sulfur** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The heights total 50, not 100. Dividing by 100 gives 16.0 and an impossible identification.

**7.** 69x + 71(1 − x) = 69.8 [1]; 2x = 1.2, x = 0.60, so **60%** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Let the abundance of ²⁴X be a, so ²⁶X is 0.900 − a [1]; 24a + 25(0.100) + 26(0.900 − a) = 24.3, so −2a = −1.6 [1]; a = 0.800: **80.0% mass 24 and 10.0% mass 26** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Writing the second unknown in terms of the first leaves one equation with one unknown. Check: 80.0 + 10.0 + 10.0 = 100.

**9. (a)** m = 85.0 ÷ 1000 ÷ 6.022 × 10²³ = **1.41 × 10⁻²⁵ kg** [1]

**(b)** v = √(2 × 4.00 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ 1.411 × 10⁻²⁵) [1] = 7.53 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹ [1]; t = 1.80 ÷ 7.53 × 10⁴ = **2.39 × 10⁻⁵ s** [1]

**(c)** 2.39 × 10⁻⁵ × √(87 ÷ 85) = **2.42 × 10⁻⁵ s** (or by repeating the full calculation) [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Leaving out the division by 1000 gives a speed √1000 times too small; check that ion speeds come out at around 10⁴ to 10⁵ m s⁻¹.

**10. (a)** v = d ÷ t = 1.20 ÷ 1.854 × 10⁻⁵ = 6.47 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹ [1]; m = 2KE ÷ v² = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁶ ÷ (6.47 × 10⁴)² = 7.64 × 10⁻²⁶ kg [1]; relative mass = 7.64 × 10⁻²⁶ × 1000 × 6.022 × 10²³ [1] = **46.0** [1]

**(b)** **47** (46 + 1, from MH⁺) [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Carry the unrounded speed into the next line; rounding it to 6.5 × 10⁴ gives 45.6.

**11.** The acceleration stage gives all the ions the same kinetic energy, not the same speed [1]. Since KE = ½mv², ions of smaller mass have greater speed [1]. They therefore cross the drift region in less time, so ions are separated by their time of flight [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Each peak represents one isotope, so peaks appear at the isotopic masses, 35 and 37 [1]. 35.5 is the weighted mean of these (the relative atomic mass), which no single atom has [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Indicative points; award one mark for each, up to 6:

- The sample is ionised, for example by electron impact, forming ²⁰Ne⁺ and ²²Ne⁺ [1]
- An electric field accelerates the ions so that all have the same kinetic energy [1]
- In the drift region, the lighter ²⁰Ne⁺ ions travel faster [1] and reach the detector first, so the isotopes arrive at different times [1]
- At the detector, ions gain electrons, and the current gives the abundance of each isotope [1]
- Aᵣ = (20 × abundance of ²⁰Ne + 22 × abundance of ²²Ne) ÷ total abundance [1]

Maximum **[6]**.

*Examiner insight:* An extended answer needs the reason as well as the stage: "lighter ions are faster because they have the same kinetic energy" scores, "they are separated" does not.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Subtracting the charge the wrong way for negative ions.
- Explaining identical chemistry by protons instead of electron configuration.
- "Same speed" in place of "same kinetic energy".
- Missing ÷ 1000 or the Avogadro constant in flight-time calculations.
- Dividing by 100 when relative heights do not total 100.
- Forgetting the extra proton in electrospray.
