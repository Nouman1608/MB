These questions cover section **3.2.5.1 General properties of transition metals** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [General properties of transition metals study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-general-properties-of-transition-metals/) and the [General properties of transition metals revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-general-properties-of-transition-metals-revision-notes/). Use proton numbers: Sc 21, Ti 22, V 23, Cr 24, Mn 25, Fe 26, Co 27, Ni 28, Cu 29, Zn 30.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a transition metal in the context of AQA A-level Chemistry. **[1]**

**2.** Write the electron configuration, in the form [Ar] ..., of each species.

**(a)** a nickel atom **[1]**

**(b)** Ni²⁺ **[1]**

**(c)** a copper atom **[1]**

**(d)** Cu²⁺ **[1]**

**3.** Explain why zinc is a d-block element but not a transition metal. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why scandium is not classed as a transition metal. **[2]**

**5.** State the four characteristic properties of transition metals and give one example of each. **[4]**

**6.** Define the terms ligand, complex and co-ordination number. **[3]**

**7.** State the co-ordination number of the central ion in each complex.

**(a)** [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ **[1]**

**(b)** [CoCl₄]²⁻ **[1]**

**(c)** [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ **[1]**

**8.** Deduce the oxidation state of the metal in each complex.

**(a)** [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ **[1]**

**(b)** [CuCl₄]²⁻ **[1]**

**(c)** [Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ **[1]**

**9.** Ammonia acts as a ligand in [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺. Explain how ammonia is able to bond to the nickel ion and state the type of bond formed. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why calcium does not show variable oxidation state but iron does. **[2]**

**11.** Manganese forms Mn²⁺. Write the electron configuration of Mn²⁺ and explain why manganese is a transition metal. **[2]**

**12.** A student writes the electron configuration of Fe³⁺ as [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s¹. Identify the error and give the correct configuration. **[2]**

**13.** Titanium forms Ti³⁺. Write its electron configuration and explain why titanium is classed as a transition metal. Proton number of titanium = 22. **[3]**

**14.** Compare vanadium and calcium in terms of the oxidation states they form and explain the difference. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** An element that forms at least one ion, or has atoms, with an incomplete d sub-level (the specification states that the characteristics of Ti to Cu arise from an incomplete d sub-level in atoms or ions) [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² [1]

**(b)** [Ar] 3d⁸ [1]

**(c)** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ [1]

**(d)** [Ar] 3d⁹ [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Zinc is in the d-block because its last electron enters the 3d sub-level [1]; Zn²⁺ has the configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰ with a full d sub-level, so it has no incomplete d sub-level in any ion [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Scandium forms only the Sc³⁺ ion [1]; Sc³⁺ has the configuration [Ar] with no d electrons, so it does not have an incomplete d sub-level [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Complex formation, for example [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ [1]; coloured ions, for example Cu²⁺(aq) is blue [1]; variable oxidation state, for example iron forms Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ [1]; catalytic activity, for example iron in the Haber process [1]. **[4]**

**6.** A ligand is a molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond to a transition metal by donating a pair of electrons [1]; a complex is a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands [1]; the co-ordination number is the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 6 [1]

**(b)** 4 [1]

**(c)** 2 [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** +3 [1]

**(b)** +2 (four Cl⁻ gives –4, so the metal is +2 to give –2 overall) [1]

**(c)** +2 (six CN⁻ gives –6, so the metal is +2 to give –4 overall) [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The nitrogen atom in ammonia has a lone pair of electrons [1]; this pair is donated to an empty orbital on the Ni²⁺ ion [1]; forming a co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Calcium loses its two 4s electrons to form Ca²⁺, and the next electron would have to come from an inner shell, which needs far too much energy, so it forms only Ca²⁺ [1]; iron's 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so it can lose different numbers of electrons and form Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Mn²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵ [1]; the d sub-level is incomplete in the atom and in the ion, so manganese is a transition metal [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The student removed electrons from 3d and left some in 4s; the 4s electrons are lost first [1]; correct configuration of Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵ [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Always remove 4s electrons before 3d electrons when writing the ion, then check the electron count.

**13.** Ti is [Ar] 3d² 4s², and Ti³⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹ [2: 1 for removing 4s first, 1 for the correct configuration]; the ion has an incomplete d sub-level (one d electron), so titanium is a transition metal [1]. **[3]**

**14.** Vanadium forms several oxidation states (+2, +3, +4 and +5) [1]; calcium forms only +2 [1]; in vanadium the 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy and can be lost in varying numbers, whereas calcium would need an inner-shell electron removed for a higher state, which needs far too much energy [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling zinc and scandium transition metals.
- Writing 4s electrons into the ion configuration.
- Giving copper as 3d⁹ 4s².
- Counting ligands rather than bonds for the co-ordination number.
- Forgetting the charges on ligands when finding oxidation states.
