## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standard electrode (reduction) potential, E⦵ | potential of a half-cell relative to the standard hydrogen electrode, under standard conditions, for the reduction half-equation |
| Standard conditions | 298 K; 1.00 mol dm⁻³ aqueous ions; 101 kPa for gases |
| Standard cell potential, E⦵<sub>cell</sub> | potential difference between two half-cells under standard conditions, with no current flowing |
| Salt bridge | filter paper soaked in saturated KNO₃(aq); lets ions move to complete the circuit without mixing the solutions |
| High-resistance voltmeter | measures the potential difference while drawing almost no current |
| Oxidising agent | species that is reduced, accepting electrons |
| Reducing agent | species that is oxidised, donating electrons |

## The standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)

- H₂(g) at 101 kPa bubbled over platinum (platinised for large surface area).
- H⁺(aq) at 1.00 mol dm⁻³.
- 298 K. Half-equation: 2H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ H₂(g), E⦵ = 0.00 V by definition.

## Measuring E⦵: three set-ups

| Half-cell type | Electrode | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| metal / metal ion (e.g. Ni²⁺/Ni) | the metal | 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Ni²⁺(aq) |
| non-metal / ion (e.g. Cl₂/Cl⁻) | platinum, with Cl₂ at 101 kPa bubbled over it | 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Cl⁻(aq) |
| two ions of one element (e.g. Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺) | platinum | 1.00 mol dm⁻³ of **each** ion (plus 1.00 mol dm⁻³ H⁺ if H⁺ is in the half-equation) |

Each is connected to the SHE by a salt bridge and a high-resistance voltmeter.

## Cell potential, polarity and electron flow

    E⦵cell = E⦵(more positive) − E⦵(less positive)

- More positive E⦵ → **positive electrode**, **reduction**.
- Less positive E⦵ → **negative electrode**, **oxidation**.
- Electrons flow in the **external wire** from negative to positive.

**Example:** Al³⁺/Al (−1.66 V) and Pb²⁺/Pb (−0.13 V).

    E⦵cell = −0.13 − (−1.66) = +1.53 V
    Al is negative; electrons flow from Al to Pb
    Overall: 2Al + 3Pb²⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3Pb     (n = 6)

## Reading a table of E⦵ values

- Most positive E⦵: left-hand species is the **strongest oxidising agent**.
- Most negative E⦵: right-hand species is the **strongest reducing agent**.
- A species on the left will oxidise a species on the right of any half-equation with a **less
  positive** E⦵.

## Feasibility routine

1. Write both half-equations as reductions with E⦵.
2. Identify which species would be reduced in the proposed reaction.
3. E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = E⦵(reduced species' half-cell) − E⦵(oxidised species' half-cell).
4. Positive → feasible under standard conditions.
5. Add caveats if asked: rate may be slow; non-standard concentrations change E.

## Constructing a redox equation

1. Reverse the half-equation for the species being oxidised (do not change E⦵ values when
   multiplying).
2. Multiply so that electrons lost = electrons gained.
3. Add; cancel electrons, H⁺ and H₂O that appear on both sides.
4. Check atoms and total charge.

**Example:** MnO₄⁻ (+1.52 V) with Fe²⁺ (Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77 V): E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = +0.75 V.

    MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O

## The Nernst equation

    E = E⦵ + (0.059 / z) log([oxidised species] / [reduced species])

- z = electrons in the half-equation.
- Solids (metals) are left out: for Cu²⁺/Cu, E = E⦵ + (0.059/2) log[Cu²⁺].
- More oxidised species → E more positive; less oxidised species → E less positive.

**Example:** Cu²⁺/Cu with [Cu²⁺] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³:

    E = 0.34 + 0.0295 × log(0.200) = 0.34 − 0.021 = +0.319 V

**Effect on a cell:** making the positive half-cell less positive, or the negative half-cell
less negative, reduces E<sub>cell</sub>.

## ΔG⦵ = −nE⦵<sub>cell</sub>F

- n = electrons transferred in the **overall** equation; F = 9.65 × 10⁴ C mol⁻¹.
- Gives J mol⁻¹; divide by 1000 for kJ mol⁻¹.
- Al/Pb example: ΔG⦵ = −6 × 1.53 × 96 500 = −886 000 J mol⁻¹ = −886 kJ mol⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- Include "relative to the standard hydrogen electrode" in the definition of E⦵.
- Never multiply E⦵ values when balancing electrons.
- Platinum electrode for gas/ion and ion/ion half-cells.
- Both ions at 1.00 mol dm⁻³ for an Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ half-cell (equal concentrations).
- Electrons flow through the wire; ions flow through the salt bridge.
- A positive E⦵<sub>cell</sub> means feasible, not fast.
- In ΔG = −nEF use the overall n; in the Nernst equation use z for the single half-equation.
- High-resistance voltmeter: so that no current flows and concentrations stay constant.

2. It is inert, conducts electricity and provides a surface for the H⁺/H₂ equilibrium.
3. Platinum electrode in a solution containing 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Fe³⁺ and 1.00 mol dm⁻³ Fe²⁺ at
