## Questions

**1.** State the word equation and the balanced chemical equation for photosynthesis. **[3]**

**2.** Chlorophyll is needed for photosynthesis.

**(a)** State where chlorophyll is found in a plant cell. **[1]**
**(b)** State the role of chlorophyll in photosynthesis. **[1]**

**3.** State how a plant uses each of these carbohydrates made in photosynthesis: starch, cellulose, glucose and sucrose. **[4]**

**4.** A plant with variegated (green and white) leaves is kept in the dark for two days, then placed in sunlight for six hours. A leaf is then tested for starch to show that chlorophyll is needed.

**(a)** Explain why the plant was kept in the dark for two days. **[2]**
**(b)** During the starch test the leaf is put into boiling water and then into hot ethanol. Explain the purpose of each step. **[2]**
**(c)** State **one** safety precaution when using ethanol, with a reason. **[1]**
**(d)** Predict the colour of the green and the white areas after iodine solution is added, and explain the results. **[3]**

**5.** Two destarched leaves on the same plant are each sealed in a flask. Flask A contains sodium hydroxide solution, which absorbs carbon dioxide; flask B contains the same volume of water. After eight hours in bright light both leaves are tested for starch.

**(a)** State why flask B is included. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the result for each leaf. **[2]**
**(c)** State the conclusion. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest **one** variable, other than light, that is kept the same for both leaves. **[1]**

**6.** Bubbles released by pondweed in one minute are counted three times at each lamp distance.

| Lamp distance / cm | Count 1 | Count 2 | Count 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 | 38 | 41 | 35 |
| 15 | 22 | 26 | 41 |
| 25 | 13 | 15 | 14 |

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result and calculate the mean number of bubbles per minute at 15 cm, leaving it out. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe and explain the effect of lamp distance on the rate of photosynthesis. **[3]**
**(c)** A beaker of water is placed between the lamp and the pondweed. Explain why. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why collecting the gas in a syringe gives more accurate results than counting bubbles. **[1]**

**7.** Pondweed is placed in different concentrations of sodium hydrogencarbonate, a source of carbon dioxide, at constant light and temperature.

| Sodium hydrogencarbonate / % | 0 | 0.25 | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of gas in 8 min / cm³ | 0.4 | 1.2 | 2.0 | 2.8 | 2.8 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of gas production at 0.5%, giving the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the change in volume between 0 and 1.0%. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the volume does not change between 1.0% and 2.0%, and suggest **one** factor that could be limiting. **[2]**

**8.** Describe and explain how the rate of photosynthesis of a plant changes as temperature rises from 10 °C to 45 °C, with light and carbon dioxide not limiting. **[5]**

**9.** Four sealed tubes contain the same volume of red hydrogencarbonate indicator. All are placed in bright light for three hours.

- Tube W: pondweed only
- Tube X: a water snail only
- Tube Y: pondweed and a water snail
- Tube Z: indicator only

**(a)** Predict the colour of the indicator in tubes W and X at the end. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain your predictions for W and X. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the purpose of tube Z. **[1]**

**10.** Leaves are adapted for photosynthesis.

**(a)** Explain how being thin and having a large surface area help a leaf to photosynthesise. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the palisade mesophyll and the spongy mesophyll with air spaces are each adapted for their role. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain the role of guard cells in photosynthesis. **[2]**

**11.** Identical seedlings grow for four weeks in a complete mineral solution, a solution lacking nitrate ions, or a solution lacking magnesium ions. Those without magnesium have yellow leaves; those without nitrate are much smaller.

**(a)** Explain why lack of magnesium ions produces yellow leaves and reduces growth. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why lack of nitrate ions reduces growth. **[2]**
**(c)** State which seedlings act as the control, and **one** variable that must be kept constant. **[2]**

**12.** A grower plots rate of photosynthesis against light intensity for tomato plants under three conditions. Each curve rises, then levels off.

- Curve P: 0.04% carbon dioxide, 15 °C; levels off at 20 arbitrary units
- Curve Q: 0.12% carbon dioxide, 15 °C; levels off at 35 arbitrary units
- Curve R: 0.12% carbon dioxide, 25 °C; levels off at 50 arbitrary units

**(a)** State the limiting factor on the rising part of all three curves. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the factor limiting the level part of curve P, and explain how the data shows this. **[2]**
**(c)** Identify the factor limiting the level part of curve Q, and explain how the data shows this. **[2]**
**(d)** The greenhouse is closed and unheated on a bright winter day. Suggest **two** changes to increase yield, and explain when each would work. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen [1]; correct formulae: CO₂ + H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + O₂ [1]; balanced: **6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂** (light and chlorophyll by the arrow) [1].
*Examiner insight:* the balancing mark needs the 6s on all three; light written as a reactant on the left side is not credited.

**2. (a)** In **chloroplasts** [1].
**(b)** It **transfers light energy into chemical energy** for making glucose [1].
*Examiner insight:* "absorbs light" alone is usually not enough; the mark is for transferring light energy into chemical energy.

**3.** Starch: **energy store** [1]. Cellulose: **builds cell walls** [1]. Glucose: **respiration to provide energy** [1]. Sucrose: **transport through the plant** [1].
*Examiner insight:* one mark per carbohydrate, so a use given for the wrong molecule (for example "starch for transport") loses that mark.

**4. (a)** To **remove starch** from the leaves (destarch) [1], so any starch found must have been **made during the experiment** [1].
**(b)** Boiling water **kills the cells / breaks membranes** so iodine can enter [1]; ethanol **removes chlorophyll** so the colour change can be seen [1].
**(c)** Heat the ethanol in a water bath with **no naked flame**, because ethanol is **flammable** [1].
**(d)** Green areas **blue-black**; white areas **orange-brown** [1]. Only green areas contain chlorophyll [1], so only they photosynthesised and made starch; **chlorophyll is needed** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (d) the colour mark needs both areas correct; "black" alone is not accepted for blue-black.

**5. (a)** As a **control**, to show any difference is caused by the lack of carbon dioxide [1].
**(b)** Leaf A: **no starch** (orange-brown) [1]. Leaf B: **starch present** (blue-black) [1].
**(c)** **Carbon dioxide is needed** for photosynthesis [1].
**(d)** Any one: same temperature, same leaf size or age, same time in light, same plant [1].
*Examiner insight:* "to compare" is too vague for a control mark; say what the control shows.

**6. (a)** Anomaly: **41** at 15 cm [1]. Mean = (22 + 26) ÷ 2 = **24 bubbles per minute** [1].
**(b)** As lamp distance increases, the number of bubbles (rate) **decreases** [1]. Further away means **lower light intensity** [1], so **less light energy** is transferred by chlorophyll and light is the **limiting factor** [1].
**(c)** To **absorb heat** from the lamp so temperature stays constant [1].
**(d)** Bubbles **vary in size**, so volume measures the gas released more accurately [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (a) the mean is credited only if the anomaly is excluded; including it gives 29.7, which scores the anomaly mark only.

**7. (a)** 2.0 ÷ 8 [1] = **0.25 cm³ per minute** [1].
**(b)** More sodium hydrogencarbonate gives **more carbon dioxide** [1]; carbon dioxide is a **raw material / limiting factor**, so the rate of photosynthesis rises and more oxygen is released [1].
**(c)** Carbon dioxide is **no longer the limiting factor** [1]; **light intensity** or **temperature** is now limiting [1].
*Examiner insight:* the unit is part of the accuracy mark in (a); 0.25 with no unit, or "cm³", does not get it.

**8.** Rate **increases** from 10 °C to an **optimum** [1]; molecules gain **kinetic energy** so there are **more effective collisions** between enzymes and substrates [1]. Above the optimum the rate **falls sharply** [1]; enzymes are **denatured** [1], the **active site changes shape** so the substrate no longer fits [1].
*Examiner insight:* "enzymes are killed" is not credited; denaturation and the change in active-site shape earn separate marks.

**9. (a)** W: **purple** (magenta accepted) [1]. X: **yellow** [1].
**(b)** W: in bright light photosynthesis is **faster than respiration** [1], so carbon dioxide concentration **falls** [1]. X: the snail only **respires**, so carbon dioxide concentration **rises** [1].
**(c)** **Control**: shows light alone does not change the indicator colour [1].
*Examiner insight:* each explanation must link the process to a change in carbon dioxide concentration; a colour with "because of photosynthesis" alone is not enough.

**10. (a)** Large surface area **absorbs more light** [1]; thin gives a **short diffusion distance** for carbon dioxide [1].
**(b)** Palisade: **many chloroplasts** [1], tightly packed **near the upper surface** where light is strongest [1]. Spongy and air spaces: loosely packed with a **large surface area** for gas exchange [1]; air spaces let **carbon dioxide diffuse** to the mesophyll cells [1].
**(c)** Guard cells **open the stomata** [1] so carbon dioxide can **diffuse in** (and oxygen out) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" in (b) needs feature **and** effect; "has chloroplasts" alone scores one of the two palisade marks.

**11. (a)** Magnesium is needed to **make chlorophyll** [1]; less chlorophyll, so leaves look yellow and **absorb less light** [1], so **less photosynthesis** and less carbohydrate for growth [1].
**(b)** Nitrate is needed to make **amino acids** [1], which make **proteins** for growth [1].
**(c)** The seedlings in the **complete solution** [1]; any one: same volume of solution, same light, same temperature, same seedling type or age [1].
*Examiner insight:* "magnesium makes leaves green" does not score; the mark is for linking magnesium to chlorophyll.

**12. (a)** **Light intensity** [1].
**(b)** **Carbon dioxide concentration** [1]; curve Q at higher carbon dioxide but the same temperature levels off higher [1].
**(c)** **Temperature** [1]; curve R at the same carbon dioxide but 25 °C levels off higher [1].
**(d)** **Heat** the greenhouse [1]; **add carbon dioxide** (for example with a burner or supply) [1]; each helps only while it is the limiting factor; once another factor is in shortest supply, raising it has no effect [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b) and (c) the explanation mark needs a comparison of two named curves in which only one factor differs.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving destarching out of experiment descriptions, or not saying why it is done.
- Giving a colour for the starch test without both outcomes (blue-black and orange-brown).
- Naming the x-axis factor as limiting on the level part of a curve.
- Calculating a mean that includes an obvious anomaly.
- Missing units on rates (cm³ per minute, bubbles per minute).
- Linking indicator colours to "oxygen" instead of carbon dioxide concentration.
