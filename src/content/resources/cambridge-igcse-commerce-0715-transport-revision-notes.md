Condensed notes for section 4.1 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, a worked cost comparison and an annotated answer, use the [transport study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-transport/).

## The syllabus in five lines

Section 4.1 asks for:

1. The **three functions** of transport in the supply chain: time, place and quantity.
2. The **types and methods**: road, rail, air, sea and pipeline.
3. The **advantages and disadvantages** of the different methods.
4. The **appropriateness of the factors** that affect the choice of method in given situations.
5. The **features, advantages and disadvantages of containerisation**, including transshipment.

## Three functions: time, place, quantity

| Function | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Time | Goods arrive when needed |
| Place | Goods move from where they are made to where they are wanted |
| Quantity | Goods move in the amounts the buyer needs |

## Methods to recall

| Type | Methods | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Delivery vans, trucks, couriers | Short and medium distances; door to door; small loads |
| Rail | Freight trains | Heavy, bulky goods over long land distances |
| Air | Freight planes | Urgent, valuable or perishable goods over long distances |
| Sea | Ferry, tanker, container ship, cargo liner | Very large loads, overseas trade, low cost per tonne |
| Pipeline | Fixed pipes | Liquids and gases (oil, gas, water) |

Short definitions:

- **Courier:** collects and delivers parcels quickly, usually door to door.
- **Tanker:** ship (or truck) built to carry liquids in bulk.
- **Container ship:** carries standard metal containers.
- **Cargo liner:** sails a fixed route on a timetable with mixed cargo.
- **Ferry:** short sea crossing for vehicles and passengers.

## Advantages and disadvantages at a glance

| Method | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Road | Door to door; flexible; good for short trips | Small loads; traffic delay; costly per tonne over long distances; pollution |
| Rail | Cheap for heavy loads; timetable; lower pollution than road | Fixed track; road needed at each end; not flexible |
| Air | Fastest; secure; suits perishable and high-value goods | Most expensive; limited load size; airport transfers needed |
| Sea | Huge loads; cheap per tonne; global reach | Slow; ports needed; weather risk; inland transfer needed |
| Pipeline | Cheap to run; continuous flow; low theft risk | Only liquids and gases; costly to build; fixed route; leaks harm the environment |

## Six choice factors

Mnemonic **"Never Use Cheap Shaky Delivery Routes"**:

| Letter | Factor | Think about |
|---|---|---|
| N | Nature of goods | Perishable, fragile, heavy, dangerous, liquid |
| U | Urgency | Deadline and shelf life |
| C | Cost | Budget; value of the goods compared with transport cost |
| S | Security | Value and theft risk |
| D | Distance | Short or long; across sea or land |
| R | Reliability of transporter | Record for on-time, undamaged delivery |

How to apply: pick the **one or two** factors that matter most in the case, say why, then name the method. Fresh fish going overseas: nature of goods and urgency lead, so air freight; cost is accepted. Coal going to a power station 400 km away: cost and nature of goods lead, so rail.

## Containerisation

**Features:**

- Standard-size metal boxes.
- Goods are packed once and sealed.
- Cranes move the box between ship, train and truck.
- Goods are handled as one unit.
- Special boxes exist (for example refrigerated).

**Transshipment:** moving a container from one vehicle or ship to another during the journey. The goods stay inside the container.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Protection from damage, weather and theft | High cost of cranes, ports and equipment |
| Fast loading and unloading | Empty containers must be returned |
| Lower handling costs and labour | Small firms may not fill a container |
| Easy change between road, rail and sea | Port congestion; smuggling risk |
| Lower insurance cost | Needs standard-size infrastructure everywhere |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transport | Moving goods from one place to another |
| Supply chain | The linked steps that take a product from producer to consumer |
| Freight | Goods carried by road, rail, air or sea |
| Courier | Company that collects and delivers parcels quickly |
| Pipeline | Pipes that carry liquids or gases over long distances |
| Containerisation | Moving goods in standard-size sealed metal boxes |
| Transshipment | Transfer of a container from one vehicle or ship to another during the journey |
| Perishable | Goods that spoil quickly |
| Reliability | Delivering on time and without damage |

## Exam traps

- Link each answer to **this** business's goods, not to transport in general.
- Do not say a method is "best" without naming the factor that makes it best here.
- Remember that air, sea and rail usually need a **road leg**.
- **Transshipment** is the change of vehicle; **containerisation** is the use of boxes.
- Time, place and quantity are the **functions**; the six choice factors are a different list.
- In "assess" questions, give a decision, not only a list of points.

## Self-test

1. State the three functions of transport in the supply chain.
2. Name **two** methods of road transport listed in the syllabus.
3. Which type of transport carries oil and gas directly from source to destination?
4. Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of rail freight.
5. Why is air freight suitable for fresh flowers?
6. State **two** disadvantages of sea transport.
7. List the six factors that affect the choice of transport.
8. Why might a business choose a more expensive but more reliable carrier?
9. What is a cargo liner?
10. Define containerisation.
11. What is transshipment?
12. State **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of containerisation.
13. A firm sends 15 tonnes by road at $80 per tonne or by sea at $52 per tonne plus a $150 handling fee. Which is cheaper and by how much?

**Answers**

1. Time, place and quantity.
2. Any two: delivery vans, trucks, couriers.
3. Pipeline.
4. Advantage: cheap for heavy loads over long distances (or lower pollution). Disadvantage: fixed track, so road transport is needed at each end.
5. It is the fastest method over long distances, so the flowers stay fresh.
6. Any two: slow; ports needed; weather delays; risk of loss or damage at sea; inland transfer needed.
7. Nature of goods, urgency, cost, security, distance, reliability of transporter.
8. Late or damaged goods can cost more in lost sales and customers than the extra price of a dependable carrier.
9. A ship that sails a fixed route on a timetable and carries mixed cargo.
10. Using standard-size sealed metal boxes to move goods by road, rail and sea.
11. Transferring a container from one ship, train or truck to another during its journey.
12. Advantages (any two): protection from damage and theft; fast handling; lower handling costs; easy change between road, rail and sea. Disadvantage (any one): high cost of equipment; empty containers to return.
13. Road: 15 x 80 = $1,200. Sea: 15 x 52 = 780, plus 150 = $930. Sea is cheaper by $270.
