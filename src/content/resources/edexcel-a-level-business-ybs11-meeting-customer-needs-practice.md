Every question below uses one of these words at its published tariff. All businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: Lumora Skincare**
>
> Lumora makes fragrance-free skincare for people with sensitive skin and
> sells only through its own website. Last year its sales were $1.05m in a
> sensitive-skincare market worth $15m. This year sales reached $1.26m and
> the market was worth $18m. Several new online brands have appeared, and
> customers often compare ingredients and prices on review sites before
> buying. Lumora is planning a men's range and has a database of 12,000
> customers: 7,200 aged under 35, 3,600 aged 35 to 54 and 1,200 aged 55 or
> over.
>
> **Source 2: Castellan Bikes**
>
> Castellan designs premium folding electric bikes for commuters, selling
> about 4,000 a year at $2,400 each through specialist cycle shops. The
> electric bike market is growing quickly as battery technology improves.
> A large manufacturer has just launched a folding electric bike at $1,100,
> sold through supermarkets and online. Castellan's directors are debating
> whether to launch a cheaper model for the mass market, and whether to
> research demand for an electric cargo bike for parents and delivery
> riders.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "consumer panel". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate Lumora's market share in each year and the percentage growth of both Lumora's sales and the market. **[4]**

**3.** Using the database figures, calculate how many customers from each age group Lumora should include in a stratified sample of 240. **[4]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why Lumora might choose a stratified sample rather than a quota sample for its men's range research. **[4]**

**5.** Explain how online retailing has affected competition in Lumora's market. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse **two** ways Lumora could add value to its skincare products. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 11 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "market mapping". **[2]**

**8.** Explain how innovation can lead to growth in the electric bike market. **[4]**

**9.** Analyse the likely effects on Castellan of the large manufacturer's new $1,100 folding bike. **[6]**

**10.** Discuss whether Castellan should use primary or secondary research to decide whether to launch an electric cargo bike. **[8]**

**11.** Assess whether Castellan should launch a cheaper model for the mass market. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A group of customers [1] who are consulted repeatedly over a period of time about products or buying habits, so a business can track changes in their views [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Last year: 1.05 ÷ 15 × 100 = **7%** [1]. This year: 1.26 ÷ 18 × 100 = **7%** [1]. Lumora's sales growth = (1.26 − 1.05) ÷ 1.05 × 100 = **20%** [1]. Market growth = (18 − 15) ÷ 15 × 100 = **20%** [1]. (Sales grew strongly, but no faster than the market, so share is unchanged.) **[4]**

**3.** Under 35: 7,200 ÷ 12,000 × 240 = **144** [1]. Aged 35 to 54: 3,600 ÷ 12,000 × 240 = **72** [1]. Aged 55 or over: 1,200 ÷ 12,000 × 240 = **24** [1]. Check: 144 + 72 + 24 = 240, each group in proportion to its share of the database [1]. **[4]**

**4.** In a stratified sample, people are chosen at random within each age group [1], whereas in a quota sample the researcher chooses who fills each quota, which can introduce bias [1]. Lumora already has a database with ages, so it has the population proportions it needs for stratification [1]. Since skin concerns differ with age, a sample that mirrors the 60/30/10 age split gives more reliable evidence for designing the men's range [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Online retailing has lowered the cost of entering the market, since a brand does not need shops [1], which is why several new online brands have appeared [1]. Customers compare ingredients and prices on review sites [1], so Lumora faces more direct comparison and less freedom to charge a high price unless it stands out [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Any two developed chains, 3 marks each.
**Brand and reputation:** Lumora could build a brand around dermatological trust [1], for example by publishing test results on its website [1], so customers with sensitive skin feel safer and will pay more than for an unknown rival [1].
**Service and convenience:** a subscription that delivers replacements automatically [1] saves customers time and avoids running out [1], so they value the product more and are less likely to switch to the new online brands [1].
*(Also creditable: packaging design, personalised product recommendations, refill packs that appeal to environmentally aware buyers.)* **[6]**

**7.** Plotting brands or products on a diagram against two features customers consider important, such as price and quality [1], to show how each is positioned relative to rivals and where gaps may exist [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Innovation such as better batteries gives electric bikes a longer range and lower weight [1], so they become practical for more journeys and more types of customer [1]. New customers enter the market rather than firms just taking customers from one another [1], so total market size rises, as Source 2 says is happening [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The rival's bike costs less than half of Castellan's $2,400 [1], so price-sensitive commuters may switch, reducing Castellan's sales from about 4,000 a year [1]. Supermarket and online distribution reaches far more buyers than specialist shops [1], so the rival may become the best-known folding brand, weakening Castellan's visibility [1]. However, Castellan's buyers chose a premium product, so if its quality and specialist-shop service are clearly better [1], many may stay loyal and the main loss may be to future first-time buyers rather than existing customers [1]. **[6]**

**10.** **Primary research**, such as focus groups with parents and delivery riders or a product trial, gives information specific to a product that does not yet exist [1]; it can reveal what load, range and price these new customer groups need [1]. However, it is slower and more expensive [1], and focus groups are small, so results may not represent all potential buyers [1]. **Secondary research**, such as industry reports and databases, can quickly show whether cargo bike sales are growing overall [1], but it will not tell Castellan whether customers would buy *its* cargo bike at its likely premium price [1]. **Judgement:** Castellan should start with secondary research to size the market cheaply, then use primary research before committing to design and production [1], because a new segment with new customers is where Castellan's existing knowledge is weakest [1]. **[8]**

**11.** **For:** the electric bike market is growing quickly, so a mass-market model could add large volumes [1]; higher output could spread design and fixed costs and lower unit costs [1]; a cheaper model would compete directly with the $1,100 rival rather than conceding that market [1]. A wider range could also reduce dependence on one small premium segment [1].
**Against:** a large manufacturer already has economies of scale and supermarket distribution, so Castellan would struggle to match its price [1]; selling a cheap model could damage the premium brand that justifies the $2,400 price [1]; specialist shops may not want a cheap model, and Castellan has no supermarket or online channel yet [1]; margins in mass markets are thin, so profit may fall even if volume rises [1].
**Judgement:** Castellan should probably stay in its premium niche and strengthen its differentiation [1], because its advantage is design and service rather than cost. This depends on how many existing buyers the rival actually takes; if sales fall sharply, a separately branded mid-price model might be worth testing [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating share for one year only, or forgetting that equal growth rates leave share unchanged.
- Rounding stratified sample numbers so they no longer add up to the sample size.
- Answering "add value" questions with price cuts, which reduce value rather than add it.
- Writing a generic answer about "competition" without using the $1,100 price or the distribution channels in Source 2.
- Ending a discuss or assess answer without a judgement that names what it depends on.
