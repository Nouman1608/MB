Condensed recall for sub-topic 6.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029). For explanations, a run-off calculation and a model answer, use the [Flooding study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-environmental-management-0680-flooding/).

## The core idea

Floods happen when **water arrives faster than the channel or the ground can take it**. Every cause either:
1. **increases surface run-off** (less infiltration or interception), or
2. **reduces channel capacity** (silting), or
3. **brings sea water onto land** (surge, tsunami, sea-level rise).

Label each cause with 1, 2 or 3 and the explanation follows.

## Causes (6.3.1)

| Cause | Mechanism | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Land relief: steep slopes | Water flows quickly downhill into valleys | 1 |
| Land relief: low-lying land | Water collects and drains slowly | 1 and 3 |
| Saturated soil | Pores already full; rain cannot infiltrate | 1 |
| Compacted soil | Pores squeezed by machinery or animals; slow infiltration | 1 |
| Soil erosion | Soil settles in rivers; shallower channel | 2 |
| Deforestation | No interception or uptake by trees | 1 (and 2 through erosion) |
| Agricultural practices | Ploughing downslope, overgrazing, compaction, field drains | 1 and 2 |
| Urbanisation | Impermeable surfaces; drains rush water to rivers | 1 |
| Climate change: sea-level rise | Coasts flood more easily | 3 |
| Climate change: more extreme weather | More intense rainstorms | 1 |
| Storm surges and tsunamis | Sea water pushed or thrown onto land | 3 |

## Impacts (6.3.2): nine

Damage to buildings and infrastructure · loss of crops · livestock and habitats · evacuation of people and livestock · landslides · contamination of drinking-water supplies · water-related disease · financial losses · human health.

## Benefit and limitation pairs (6.3.4)

- **Monitoring and warning**: time to move people and goods / flash floods give little warning; equipment upkeep.
- **Land use zoning**: cheap; floodplain kept for parks or farming / land scarce in cities; existing buildings stay; informal settlements.
- **Structure of buildings**: raised floors, waterproof walls, high sockets; quick recovery / costly to retrofit; roads and crops unprotected.
- **Disaster preparation**: sandbags, boats, practised plans / funding; complacency when floods are rare.
- **Evacuation**: lives saved / flooded roads; people unwilling to leave.
- **Shelters**: refuge above flood level / overcrowding and disease.
- **Rebuilding**: chance to build flood-resistant / slow, costly, may rebuild in same risky place.
- **International aid**: food, water, medical teams / late, uneven, dependency.

## Opportunities (6.3.5): two

1. **Nutrient-rich silt deposited on farmland**: water slows on the floodplain and drops fine silt, so soils are fertile.
2. **Recharge of surface water and ground water stores**: lakes, wetlands and reservoirs refill; water soaks down to aquifers.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flood | Water covering land that is normally dry |
| Infiltration | Water soaking into the soil |
| Surface run-off | Water flowing over the ground surface |
| Saturated soil | Soil whose pore spaces are full of water |
| Compacted soil | Soil whose pore spaces have been squeezed together |
| Impermeable surface | A surface water cannot pass through, such as tarmac |
| Channel capacity | The volume of water a river channel can hold |
| Floodplain | Flat land beside a river that floods naturally |
| Land relief | The shape and height of the land |
| Aquifer | Rock that stores ground water |
| Land use zoning | Controlling which land uses are allowed in which areas |

## Quick calculation pattern

Volume of rain (m³) = rainfall (m) × area (m²). Convert first: 1 mm = 0.001 m; 1 km² = 1,000,000 m². Then multiply by the fraction that runs off. Example: 20 mm on 0.5 km² = 0.020 × 500,000 = 10,000 m³; if 60% runs off, 6,000 m³ reaches the river.

## Exam traps

- "Heavy rain" given as a full explanation; say why the water could not soak in.
- Saturated and compacted soil swapped.
- Coastal causes left out.
- Flood impacts copied from the cyclone list with "flooding" in it; for floods, flooding is the event, not an impact.
- Opportunities forgotten, or described vaguely as "water for farms" without silt or recharge.
- Land use zoning described as defences; it is about where things are built.

## Self-test

1. Explain how saturated soil increases flood risk.
2. How does compaction differ from saturation?
3. Explain how soil erosion makes flooding more likely.
4. Give two agricultural practices that increase flooding.
5. Why does urbanisation increase surface run-off?
6. Name the two climate change effects in 6.3.1.
7. Name two causes of coastal flooding.
8. Give one benefit and one limitation of land use zoning.
9. Why are flash floods hard to warn people about?
10. Give two ways a house can be built to cope with flooding.
11. State the two opportunities provided by floods.
12. Calculate the volume of rain that falls when 40 mm lands on 2 km².

**Answers:** 1. Pores are already full, so rain cannot infiltrate and runs off. 2. Compacted soil has squeezed pore spaces; saturated soil has pores full of water. 3. Eroded soil settles in the river, making it shallower so it holds less water. 4. Any two: ploughing up and down slopes; overgrazing; heavy machinery; field drainage. 5. Impermeable surfaces stop infiltration, and drains carry water quickly to rivers. 6. Rise in sea level; increase in extreme weather events. 7. Storm surges; tsunamis (accept sea-level rise). 8. Keeps people off the floodplain cheaply; hard where land is scarce. 9. Water rises very quickly after intense rain, leaving little time. 10. Any two: raised floors or stilts; waterproof walls; high electrical sockets; door barriers. 11. Deposition of nutrient-rich silt on farmland; recharge of surface and ground water stores. 12. 0.040 × 2,000,000 = 80,000 m³.
