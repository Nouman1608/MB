Section A is short recall; Section B is data response and extended writing. All data are invented.

## Section A

**1.** Define *invasive species*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a direct driver and an indirect driver of biodiversity loss, using an example of each. **[3]**

**3.** State the meaning of the unit E/MSY. **[1]**

**4.** Outline why an oceanic island is likely to lose a higher proportion of its species than a continental area of the same size when a predator is introduced. **[3]**

**5.** Define *extinction debt*. **[2]**

**6.** Outline how bycatch threatens a marine mammal species that you have studied. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** (Data response) A fictional survey records extinctions in two groups of freshwater animals over the 120 years since monitoring began.

| Group | Number of species | Recorded extinctions |
|---|---|---|
| Freshwater fish | 2500 | 18 |
| Freshwater molluscs | 1200 | 27 |

**(a)** Calculate the extinction rate for each group in extinctions per million species-years. **[4]**
**(b)** Assuming a background rate of 1 E/MSY, state how many times the background rate the mollusc rate is. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest two reasons why the true extinction rates may be higher than these figures. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how the fragmentation of a forest by a new road can reduce genetic diversity in a population of a forest mammal. **[4]**

**9.** (Data response) A fictional forest fragment of 40 ha has been isolated by farmland since 1980. Bird surveys recorded the number of forest-interior species.

| Year | 1980 | 1990 | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Interior bird species | 32 | 27 | 23 | 21 | 20 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of interior species lost between 1980 and 2020. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the pattern of species loss over time. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this pattern supports the idea of extinction debt. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why tropical rainforest and coral reef ecosystems are especially vulnerable to human activity. **[4]**

**11.** Using a named species that has become extinct as a result of human activity, evaluate the relative importance of the factors that caused its extinction. **[6]**

**12.** "Climate change is now the greatest threat to global biodiversity." To what extent do you agree with this statement? **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** A non-native species introduced by human action [1] that spreads and causes harm to native species, ecosystems or the economy [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both parts are needed; "a species not from the area" describes any non-native species, not an invasive one.

**2.** A direct driver physically affects organisms or habitats, e.g. clearing forest for farmland [1]. An indirect driver is the social, economic or political cause behind it, e.g. rising global demand for beef or soy [1]. The direct driver is the immediate cause of loss, while the indirect driver explains why that activity happens [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The third mark is for a direct comparison sentence, not a third definition.

**3.** Extinctions per million species-years: the number of extinctions expected if one million species were observed for one year [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Island species often evolved without mammalian predators [1], so they lack defensive behaviour (they may be flightless, ground-nesting or tame) [1]. Many are endemic and populations are small, with no nearby source to recolonise from, so local loss is global extinction [1]. **[3]**

**5.** The future extinctions already made inevitable by past habitat loss or fragmentation [1], affecting species still present but whose remaining habitat is too small or isolated to support them in the long term [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Example, vaquita: it is caught in gillnets set for fish in the northern Gulf of California, including the illegal totoaba fishery [1]. Trapped animals drown because they cannot surface to breathe [1]. Its range is tiny and its population very small, and it reproduces slowly, so even a few deaths a year prevent recovery [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit goes to the mechanism and the link to vulnerability; the name alone earns nothing.

**7. (a)** Fish: 18 ÷ (2500 × 120) = 18 ÷ 300 000 [1] × 1 000 000 = **60 E/MSY** [1]. Molluscs: 27 ÷ (1200 × 120) = 27 ÷ 144 000 [1] × 1 000 000 = **187.5 E/MSY** (accept 188) [1].
**(b)** 187.5 ÷ 1 = about **188 times** the background rate [1].
**(c)** Any two: many species in the groups are undescribed, so their extinctions go unrecorded [1]; species are declared extinct only after long searches, so recent losses are not yet counted [1]; extinction debt means losses already committed have not yet happened; some regions are poorly surveyed. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show species × years before dividing; a correct final value with no working risks losing the method marks.

**8.** The road acts as a barrier, so individuals cannot move between the two parts of the forest [1]. Gene flow between the separated populations stops [1]. Each population is smaller, so inbreeding increases and alleles are lost by genetic drift [1]. Lower genetic diversity reduces the ability of the population to adapt to disease or environmental change, raising extinction risk [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** (32 − 20) ÷ 32 × 100 [1] = **37.5%** [1].
**(b)** Species numbers fell throughout, from 32 to 20 [1]; the loss was fastest in the first decade (5 species from 1980 to 1990) and slowed to 1 species from 2010 to 2020 [1].
**(c)** Species continued to disappear for decades after isolation in 1980, even though no further habitat was lost [1]; this shows the fragment was too small to support all the species it held, so their extinctions were committed at the time of isolation [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Rainforest: most nutrients are held in the living biomass rather than the soil [1], so clearance removes the nutrient store and the forest recovers very slowly [1]. Coral reefs: corals tolerate only a narrow range of temperature and water chemistry [1], so small amounts of warming or pollution cause bleaching and death of the reef-building species on which the community depends [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Accept high endemism or very high species diversity for either ecosystem as an alternative point, provided it is linked to vulnerability.

**11.** Indicative content for the passenger pigeon, one mark per point:
- Commercial hunting killed very large numbers for cheap meat [1].
- Railways and the telegraph allowed hunters to find nesting colonies and ship birds to cities quickly [1].
- Clearance of eastern deciduous forest removed nesting habitat and food [1].
- The species bred in dense colonies, so small scattered groups may have failed to breed [1].
- Weighing: hunting caused the rapid collapse, but habitat loss meant numbers could not recover [1].
- Judgement: the factors acted together; hunting was the most immediate cause, while the species' social breeding made it unable to survive at low numbers [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate the relative importance" requires an explicit ranking or weighing; a list of causes reaches about half marks.

**12.** Indicative content. In the real exam, extended answers are marked holistically against the IB's assessment criteria, so treat these points as a guide:
- Climate change shifts temperature and rainfall faster than many species can move or adapt [1].
- It already causes coral bleaching and shifts in species ranges towards the poles and uphill [1].
- It interacts with fragmentation: species cannot move through cleared land to suitable climates [1].
- However, the 2019 IPBES assessment ranked land and sea-use change first and direct exploitation second, with climate change third [1].
- In the oceans, direct exploitation through fishing has had the greatest impact so far [1].
- Habitat loss is the main driver in tropical forests, where most terrestrial species live [1].
- Climate change is projected to grow in importance as warming continues [1].
- The drivers are linked, and indirect drivers such as consumption lie behind all of them [1].
- Judgement: the statement is not yet true globally, but climate change is a rising threat that worsens the effects of the larger drivers [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* "To what extent" needs both sides and a conclusion that answers the question; top answers also refer to specific ecosystems.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the "× 1 000 000" step, or dividing extinctions by years only.
- Describing a data trend without quoting figures and years from the table.
- Naming a case study without the mechanism.
- Treating every non-native species as invasive.
- Ending an evaluation without ranking the factors.
