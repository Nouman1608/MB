Revise first with the [nature of the economic problem study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-nature-of-the-economic-problem/) and the [nature of the economic problem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-the-nature-of-the-economic-problem-revision-notes/). All people, places and figures are invented.

## Section A

**1.** What is the economic problem? **[1]**

A: Some countries are poorer than others.

B: Resources are finite and wants are unlimited.

C: Some goods are rare and expensive.

D: Prices rise when supply falls.

**2.** Which of the following is a free good? **[1]**

A: Bottled spring water

B: Sunlight on a beach

C: A vaccination given at no charge

D: A free newspaper handed out at a station

**3.** Which statement about scarcity is correct? **[1]**

A: It affects only low-income countries.

B: It disappears when prices fall.

C: It applies to all people and all countries.

D: It applies only to goods that have a price.

**4.** Which is the main limited resource that forces a worker to choose between overtime and leisure? **[1]**

A: Time

B: Land

C: Capital

D: Enterprise

**5.** Define the term *economic good*. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a need and a want. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Kofi has $80 to spend this month. He wants a jacket costing $55, a football costing $25 and a phone game costing $15.

**(a)** Calculate how much more money Kofi would need to buy all three items. **[2]**

**(b)** Using this example, explain the economic problem facing a consumer. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how the economic problem affects a producer. Use an example in your answer. **[4]**

**9.** The government of Country Z (invented) has a budget of $90 billion. Its ministries ask for $45 billion for health, $35 billion for education and $25 billion for transport.

**(a)** Calculate the total requested and the gap between the requests and the budget. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** ways the government could respond to the gap. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the economic problem would still exist even if the government's budget doubled. **[3]**

**10.** A mobile app is downloaded at no charge. Explain why it is an economic good rather than a free good. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how clean air could change from a free good to an economic good. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether scarcity is a problem for rich countries as well as poor countries. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. The economic problem is that resources are finite while wants are unlimited.

**2.** **B** [1]. Sunlight is not scarce and using it deprives no one. The others all use scarce resources to supply, even when the user pays nothing.

**3.** **C** [1]. Scarcity is universal, because resources are always limited relative to wants.

**4.** **A** [1]. A worker has a fixed number of hours, which must be divided between work and leisure.

**5.** A good that is scarce, so producing it uses resources that have alternative uses [1]; it therefore has an opportunity cost [1].

**6.** A need is something essential for survival, such as food or shelter [1]. A want is anything a person would like to have, which goes beyond needs and is unlimited [1].

**7. (a)** Total = 55 + 25 + 15 = $95 [1]. Extra needed = 95 − 80 = **$15** [1].

**(b)** Kofi's income of $80 is a finite resource [1], but his wants total $95 and will keep growing [1], so he must choose which item to go without, for example the phone game [1]. **[5]**

**8.** A producer has limited land, labour, capital and finance [1], but wants to produce more output and make more profit [1]. For example, a bakery with one oven must choose between baking bread and cakes during the same hours [1]; producing more of one means producing less of the other [1].

**9. (a)** Total requested = 45 + 35 + 25 = $105 billion [1]. Gap = 105 − 90 = **$15 billion** [1].

**(b)** Any two: reduce one or more ministry's spending [1]; raise taxes [1]; borrow, which must be repaid later; delay some projects.

**(c)** Wants are unlimited, so with a larger budget ministries would ask for more [1], for example new hospitals or roads that were not requested before [1]. Resources such as workers and materials would also still be limited, so choices would still be needed [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Programmers, designers and servers were needed to create and run the app [1]. These are scarce resources that could have been used to produce something else, so the app has an opportunity cost [1]. The cost is paid by someone other than the user, such as advertisers or the developer [1].

**11.** In an area with little pollution, clean air is plentiful and breathing it deprives no one, so it is a free good [1]. If traffic or industry pollutes the air, clean air becomes scarce [1]. People then use resources to obtain it, such as buying air purifiers or masks [1], and the government spends on pollution controls, so clean air now has an opportunity cost and is an economic good [1].

**12.** Rich countries have more resources, so they can satisfy more needs, such as food and housing, than poor countries [1]. In that sense, the effects of scarcity are more severe in poor countries, where some people's basic needs are not met [1]. However, wants are unlimited everywhere: as incomes rise, people want better healthcare, education and new products [1]. Rich governments still have to choose between spending on, for example, healthcare for an ageing population and defence [1], and rich consumers still face limits of time [1]. Conclusion: scarcity is a problem for all countries, because resources are always finite relative to wants, but the choices in poor countries involve basic needs, while in rich countries they mostly involve wants beyond needs [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only half of the definition of the economic problem.
- Choosing a multiple-choice answer because a good has no price to the user.
- Writing a general definition when the question asks about a specific decision maker.
- Forgetting to show the addition before the subtraction in calculations.
- In a "discuss" answer, giving only one side or no conclusion.
