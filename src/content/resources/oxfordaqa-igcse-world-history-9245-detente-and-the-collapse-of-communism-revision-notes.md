**Condensed notes on the Section B option "Détente and the collapse of communism, c1969-1991".** The specification names two parts: "The problem of maintaining détente" and "The end of the Cold War in the USSR, Central and Eastern Europe". The full explanation is in the [détente and the collapse of communism study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-detente-and-the-collapse-of-communism/); the question types are in the [Paper 1 Section B revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-world-history-9245-paper-1-section-b-revision-notes/).

## Reasons for détente

| State | Motive |
|---|---|
| USSR | Cost of the arms race; trade and technology; recognition of frontiers; fear of China |
| USA | End Vietnam; reduce costs; use the Sino-Soviet split |
| China | Fear of the USSR |
| West Germany | **Ostpolitik** under Brandt: treaties with the USSR, Poland and East Germany |

## Progress

- **ABM Treaty (1972):** limits on defensive missile systems.
- **SALT I (1972):** freeze on the number of strategic missile launchers for five years; warheads not limited.
- **Summits:** Moscow (1972), Washington (1973), Moscow (1974).
- **Helsinki Accords (1975):** 35 states; frontiers recognised (the Soviet aim); human rights promised (the western aim).

## The collapse of détente

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| Afghanistan (December 1979) | USSR supports a communist government against the Mujahideen |
| Carter's response | SALT II withdrawn from the Senate; grain embargo; boycott of the 1980 Olympics; aid to the Mujahideen |
| Soviet response | Boycott of the 1984 Olympics |
| Reagan | "Evil empire"; higher defence spending; **SDI** (1983) |
| Missiles in Europe | Soviet SS-20s; American Pershing II and cruise missiles; protests |

## The re-emergence of détente

- **Gorbachev** (1985): needed economic reform and lower arms costs.
- Geneva (1985); **Reykjavik** (1986): close to deep cuts; SDI blocked a deal.
- **INF Treaty** (1987): removed intermediate-range missiles.
- **START** (1991): cut strategic warheads.

## The end of the Cold War in Eastern Europe

| Topic | Key points |
|---|---|
| Afghanistan | Costly and unpopular; troops withdrawn 1988-89 |
| Solidarity | Founded 1980 under **Walesa**; about ten million members; suppressed by martial law in 1981; won the 1989 elections |
| Gorbachev | **Glasnost** (openness), **perestroika** (restructuring); gave up the Brezhnev Doctrine |
| Poland, 1989 | Semi-free elections; non-communist government |
| Hungary, 1989 | Multi-party system; border with Austria opened |
| East Germany, 1989 | Protests and exodus; Honecker resigns; Wall opens 9 November |
| Czechoslovakia, 1989 | **Velvet Revolution**; Havel |
| USSR, 1991 | Failed coup; republics independent; USSR dissolved |
| USA | The only superpower |

## Writing good answers

- **3 marks:** three separate, precise points.
- **6 marks:** one way, with knowledge, an explanation and a consequence.
- **9 marks:** two separate ways or reasons, each explained.
- **12 marks:** weigh the given factor against others and reach a judgement.
- Name treaties and dates (SALT I, 1972; Helsinki, 1975; INF, 1987; START, 1991).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Détente | A relaxing of tension between East and West |
| Ostpolitik | West Germany's policy of better relations with the East |
| SALT | Strategic Arms Limitation Talks |
| ABM | Anti-ballistic missile |
| SDI | Strategic Defence Initiative, a planned missile defence system |
| Mujahideen | Muslim guerrillas who fought the Soviets in Afghanistan |
| Solidarity | The Polish independent trade union formed in 1980 |
| Glasnost | Openness |
| Perestroika | Restructuring of the economy |
| Velvet Revolution | The peaceful end of communist rule in Czechoslovakia |

## Exam traps

- SALT I limited launchers, not warheads.
- The Helsinki Accords included human rights, which the USSR agreed to.
- Détente collapsed after Afghanistan, but it had already faced strains.
- The Berlin Wall opened on 9 November 1989, not in 1991.
- Solidarity was suppressed in 1981 and returned in 1989.
- Glasnost is openness; perestroika is restructuring.
- In 12-mark answers, weigh Gorbachev against other factors.

## Self-test

1. Give two reasons why the USSR wanted détente.
2. What was Ostpolitik?
3. What did SALT I do?
4. Why did the USSR want the Helsinki Accords?
5. Why did the USSR invade Afghanistan?
6. Name two ways in which Carter reacted.
7. What was SDI?
8. What was agreed in the INF Treaty?
9. Who led Solidarity?
10. What are glasnost and perestroika?
11. What happened on 9 November 1989?
12. Who resigned on 25 December 1991?

## Answers

1. Any two: the cost of the arms race; the need for trade and technology; recognition of frontiers; fear of China.
2. West Germany's policy of improving relations with the Soviet bloc, under Willy Brandt.
3. It froze the number of strategic missile launchers for five years and was the first of the arms limitation agreements.
4. It recognised the post-war frontiers in Europe.
5. To support a communist government and prevent the loss of an ally on its border.
6. Any two: withdrew SALT II from the Senate; a grain embargo; boycott of the Moscow Olympics; aid to the Mujahideen.
7. Reagan's planned space-based missile defence, "Star Wars".
8. The removal of all intermediate-range missiles in Europe.
9. Lech Walesa.
10. Glasnost is openness, and perestroika is the restructuring of the economy.
11. The Berlin Wall was opened.
12. Mikhail Gorbachev.
