This guide covers **Part 1** of the optional **Component 5 Speaking** test of Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language 0539. The syllabus describes Part 1 as "a two- to three-minute presentation by the candidate on a chosen topic". It is worth **20 marks** of the 60 for Speaking and tests the speaking objectives **S1, S2, S3, S4 and S5**. For the whole component, see the [Component 5 Speaking study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-urdu-component-5-speaking/).

## Where Part 1 sits

Speaking is optional and **separately endorsed**: it does not count towards the overall qualification grade, and certificates record a separate grade from 1 (high) to 5 (low). The test lasts approximately 10 to 12 minutes, is conducted and assessed in Urdu by a teacher or examiner at your centre, and must be recorded. Dictionaries may not be taken in. Part 1 is the only part you can prepare in advance, so it is the best chance to secure a strong start.

| Part | What happens | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Presentation | 2 to 3 minutes, on a topic you choose | 20 |
| 2 Topic conversation | 3 to 4 minutes of spontaneous questions about the presentation | 20 |
| 3 General conversation | 3 to 4 minutes on two or three general topics | 20 |

## What the syllabus requires

- The candidate **chooses** the topic.
- The topic "must reflect an aspect, or aspects, of life in an Urdu-speaking community or Urdu-speaking culture".
- **A written copy of the presentation is not allowed.**
- A candidate may bring a single **cue card** in Urdu, **no larger than a postcard**, with a **maximum of five headings** to remind them of the points to make.
- A candidate may also bring in a limited quantity of **illustrative material**, no more than three or four items, which may include maps, diagrams, statistics and pictures, but which **must not provide additional written support** for the presentation.

| Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|
| One cue card, postcard size or smaller | A script or full sentences to read |
| Up to five headings on the card | More than five headings |
| Three or four items such as a map, chart or photograph | Items with long paragraphs of written notes |
| Speaking from memory and from headings | Reading aloud from a page |

## Choosing a topic

A good topic meets four tests.

1. **Culture test.** It reflects life in an Urdu-speaking community or culture: a festival, a food, a craft, a poet, a tradition, a family custom, a city, a form of music, or a role such as a shopkeeper in a bazaar.
2. **Interest test.** You genuinely care about it, so you can speak with feeling.
3. **Depth test.** It gives you enough to say for two to three minutes, and enough more to talk about in Part 2.
4. **Language test.** You can talk about it in Urdu you are able to control.

Part 3 topics must not overlap with your presentation, so choose a topic that is cultural and specific, not a general subject such as "my school".

| Weak topic | Why | Stronger version |
|---|---|---|
| میرا اسکول | too general, not cultural | میرے دادا کے گاؤں کا میلہ |
| مشہور لوگ | too broad | علامہ اقبال اور اردو شاعری |
| انٹرنیٹ | not about Urdu culture | اردو سوشل میڈیا اور نوجوان |

## Building the presentation

A strong presentation has five parts. They match the five headings that fit on a cue card.

| Part | Content | Useful Urdu |
|---|---|---|
| 1 Opening | greet, say the topic and why you chose it | السلام علیکم، آج میں آپ کو ... کے بارے میں بتاؤں گا |
| 2 Background | what it is, where it comes from | یہ ایک ایسی ... ہے جس میں ... |
| 3 Detail | two or three facts, with an example | مثال کے طور پر ... |
| 4 Opinion | what you think and why | میرے خیال میں ... کیونکہ ... |
| 5 Close | a short summary or hope | آخر میں میں یہ کہوں گا کہ ... |

Include at least one **opinion** and one **reason**. The assessment grid for Part 1 rewards "thorough coverage of the topic", "ideas well sequenced and linked", and a range of "factual information, ideas and opinions".

## Worked example: the mushaira

The topic is *مشاعرہ* (a poetry gathering). The cue card has five headings only.

```
1  تعارف
2  مشاعرہ کیا ہے
3  مشہور شاعر
4  ایک پسندیدہ شعر
5  میری رائے
```

Illustrative items: a photograph of a poetry gathering and a picture of a poet.

A model presentation is below. It is an example of structure and language, not a script to memorise, because a written copy is not allowed.

> السلام علیکم! میرا نام ... ہے۔ آج میں آپ کو مشاعرے کے بارے میں بتاؤں گا۔
>
> مشاعرہ ایک ایسی محفل ہے جس میں شاعر باری باری اپنا کلام سناتے ہیں اور سننے والے داد دیتے ہیں۔ اردو بولنے والے معاشرے میں یہ روایت بہت پرانی ہے۔ جب کوئی شعر پسند آتا ہے تو لوگ "واہ واہ" کہتے ہیں، اور اگر بہت اچھا لگے تو "مکرر ارشاد" کہہ کر شاعر سے دوبارہ سنانے کی فرمائش کرتے ہیں۔
>
> اردو کے کئی مشہور شاعر ہیں، جیسے مرزا غالب، علامہ اقبال اور فیض احمد فیض۔ مجھے علامہ اقبال کا یہ شعر بہت پسند ہے: "ستاروں سے آگے جہاں اور بھی ہیں، ابھی عشق کے امتحاں اور بھی ہیں۔" میرے نزدیک اس کا مطلب یہ ہے کہ ہمیں ہمیشہ آگے بڑھنے کی کوشش کرنی چاہیے۔
>
> میرے خیال میں مشاعرہ آج بھی اہم ہے، کیونکہ اس سے لوگ زبان اور ادب سے جڑے رہتے ہیں۔ لیکن آج کل نوجوان کم آتے ہیں، اس لیے اسکولوں میں بھی مشاعرے ہونے چاہییں۔ آخر میں میں یہ کہوں گا کہ مشاعرہ ہماری ثقافت کا ایک خوبصورت حصہ ہے۔ شکریہ!

### What makes it effective

| Feature | Where |
|---|---|
| Clear order | greeting, what it is, poets, a favourite verse, opinion, close |
| Linking | *جیسے*، *کیونکہ*، *لیکن*، *اس لیے*، *آخر میں* |
| Facts | audience says *واہ واہ*; poets named |
| Ideas | the meaning of the couplet in the speaker's words |
| Opinion with a reason | *میرے خیال میں ... کیونکہ ...* |
| Variety of structures | *جب ... تو*، *اگر ... تو*، *جس میں* |

When you speak, add your own details and an example from your life, for instance a poetry evening in your community or a poem you learned. The model is shorter than the time available, so do not rush: slow, clear delivery with pauses is better than a fast, unclear one.

## The assessment criteria for Part 1

The syllabus marks Part 1 on two criteria, each out of 10.

| Criterion | What is assessed |
|---|---|
| Content and presentation | communication of facts, ability to express opinion and raise issues for discussion; coverage, sequencing and linking, and how well the presentation holds interest |
| Quality of language | vocabulary, pronunciation and intonation, and structures |

Marks are given in bands: very good (9 to 10), good (7 to 8), satisfactory (5 to 6), weak (3 to 4) and poor (1 to 2). Examiners work upwards from the bottom of the grid and reward what a candidate can do. In broad terms:

- the top band has a thorough, well-sequenced and engaging presentation, a wide range of precise vocabulary, clear pronunciation and intonation, and complex structures used generally accurately;
- the middle band has satisfactory coverage, some attempts at linking, simple structures used accurately, and a presentation that may be slow or stilted;
- the lower bands have limited coverage, little sequencing, very simple language and frequent errors.

## Delivery

- **Speak to the examiner**, not to the cue card. Glance at a heading and then look up.
- **Pronounce carefully.** Practise the sounds that are hard for you, such as *ق، خ، غ* and the retroflex consonants *ٹ، ڈ، ڑ*.
- **Use intonation.** Raise your voice slightly for an important point and drop it at the end of a sentence.
- **Pause** between ideas instead of using fillers.
- **Time yourself.** Practise aloud with a timer until you land in the range of two to three minutes without a script.
- **Record yourself** and listen back for hesitation and mistakes.

## Common mistakes

- **Reading or memorising a script**, which sounds flat and is not allowed in written form.
- **A topic that is not cultural**, or is so broad that it cannot be covered.
- **Too short**: fewer than two minutes may give too little to credit.
- **Facts without opinion**, so the presentation raises no issue for discussion.
- **A cue card with more than five headings**, or full sentences on it.
- **Illustrative material with written notes** that act as extra support.
- **No end**, so the presentation stops suddenly.

## Preparing step by step

1. Choose the topic and check it against the four tests.
2. Collect facts and two or three opinions with reasons.
3. Write five headings for the cue card.
4. Choose up to three or four illustrative items.
5. Practise aloud, record, listen, and improve.
6. Practise follow-up questions, because Part 2 is a conversation about the presentation.
