This guide goes deeper into section 3.6.4 Molar concentrations (statements a to d) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The course is untiered. It includes the required practical: *establish the concentration of an unknown strong acid through titration with a strong base*. ## What the specification asks

| Statement | Content |
|---|---|
| 3.6.4 a | Concentration (mol/dm³) = number of moles ÷ volume of solution (dm³) |
| 3.6.4 b | Titration with a suitable indicator measures the volumes of acid and alkali that react; strong acids (sulfuric, hydrochloric, nitric) and strong alkalis only; required practical |
| 3.6.4 c | Use titration results to find an unknown concentration, in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³ |
| 3.6.4 d | The molar gas volume at room temperature and pressure is 24 dm³; calculate moles or volume of gas in a reaction |

## Concentration and its units (3.6.4 a)

**concentration (mol/dm³) = moles ÷ volume (dm³)**

The unit mol/dm³ can also be written mol dm⁻³. Volumes measured in cm³ must be converted: **1 dm³ = 1000 cm³**, so divide cm³ by 1000.

To change between the two concentration units:

**concentration (g/dm³) = concentration (mol/dm³) × Mᵣ**

### Worked example 1: making up a solution

*4.00 g of sodium hydroxide, NaOH, is dissolved in water and made up to 250 cm³. Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³ and in g/dm³.* (Mᵣ NaOH = 40)

1. Moles = 4.00 ÷ 40 = 0.100 mol.
2. Volume = 250 ÷ 1000 = 0.250 dm³.
3. Concentration = 0.100 ÷ 0.250 = **0.400 mol/dm³**.
4. In g/dm³: 0.400 × 40 = **16.0 g/dm³** (or 4.00 g ÷ 0.250 dm³ directly).

### Worked example 2: moles in a measured volume

*How many moles of sulfuric acid are in 18.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol/dm³ solution?*

moles = concentration × volume = 0.250 × (18.0 ÷ 1000) = **0.00450 mol** (4.50 × 10⁻³ mol).

## The titration required practical (3.6.4 b)

**Aim:** find the concentration of a strong acid using an alkali of known concentration.

**Method**

1. Rinse a pipette with the alkali, then use it with a pipette filler to transfer 25.0 cm³ of alkali into a conical flask.
2. Add a few drops of a suitable indicator (one with a sharp colour change, such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange). Universal indicator is not used, because its gradual colour change makes the end-point unclear.
3. Rinse a burette with the acid, fill it, and record the starting volume, reading the bottom of the meniscus at eye level.
4. Add acid while swirling the flask. Do a quick **rough** titration first to find the approximate end-point.
5. Repeat, adding acid drop by drop near the end-point, until the indicator **just** changes colour permanently. Record the final volume.
6. Repeat until you have **concordant** titres (within 0.10 cm³ of each other).

**Why each detail matters**

| Detail | Reason |
|---|---|
| Rinse burette with acid, pipette with alkali | Water left inside would dilute the solution |
| White tile under the flask | Makes the colour change easier to see |
| Swirl continuously | Mixes the acid and alkali so the end-point is not overshot |
| Drop by drop near the end | One drop can change the colour; adding faster overshoots |
| Repeat to concordance | Improves confidence that the results are repeatable |
| Ignore the rough titre | It is deliberately overshot, so it is not accurate |

**Hazards:** acids and alkalis are irritant or corrosive at higher concentrations; wear eye protection.

## Titration calculations (3.6.4 c)

Every titration calculation follows four steps:

1. **Moles of the known solution** = concentration × volume (dm³).
2. **Mole ratio** from the balanced equation.
3. **Moles of the unknown** solution.
4. **Concentration of the unknown** = moles ÷ volume (dm³). Multiply by Mᵣ for g/dm³ if asked.

### Worked example 3: nitric acid and sodium hydroxide (1 : 1)

25.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide was titrated with nitric acid.

| Titration | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titre / cm³ | 23.10 | 22.45 | 22.55 | 22.50 |

**Mean titre:** use the concordant titres 1, 2 and 3: (22.45 + 22.55 + 22.50) ÷ 3 = **22.50 cm³**.

NaOH + HNO₃ → NaNO₃ + H₂O

1. Moles NaOH = 0.120 × 0.0250 = 0.00300 mol.
2. Ratio 1 : 1, so moles HNO₃ = 0.00300 mol.
3. Concentration HNO₃ = 0.00300 ÷ 0.02250 = **0.133 mol/dm³**.
4. Mᵣ HNO₃ = 1 + 14 + 48 = 63, so 0.1333 × 63 = **8.40 g/dm³**.

### Worked example 4: sulfuric acid and potassium hydroxide (1 : 2)

25.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide needed a mean titre of 18.75 cm³ of sulfuric acid.

H₂SO₄ + 2KOH → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

1. Moles KOH = 0.150 × 0.0250 = 0.00375 mol.
2. Ratio H₂SO₄ : KOH = 1 : 2, so moles H₂SO₄ = 0.00375 ÷ 2 = 0.001875 mol.
3. Concentration = 0.001875 ÷ 0.01875 = **0.100 mol/dm³**.
4. Mᵣ H₂SO₄ = 98, so **9.80 g/dm³**.

Dividing by 2 (not multiplying) is correct here because each H₂SO₄ neutralises two KOH.

## Gas volumes (3.6.4 d)

At room temperature and pressure (rtp), one mole of **any** gas occupies **24 dm³** (24 000 cm³).

**volume of gas (dm³) = moles × 24**

### Worked example 5: hydrogen from magnesium

*0.36 g of magnesium reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. Calculate the volume of hydrogen at rtp.* (Aᵣ: Mg = 24)

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

- Moles Mg = 0.36 ÷ 24 = 0.015 mol; ratio 1 : 1, so 0.015 mol H₂.
- Volume = 0.015 × 24 = **0.36 dm³** = 360 cm³.

### Worked example 6: gas from a solution

*40.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid reacts with excess zinc. Calculate the volume of hydrogen at rtp.*

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

- Moles HCl = 1.00 × 0.0400 = 0.0400 mol; ratio HCl : H₂ = 2 : 1, so 0.0200 mol H₂.
- Volume = 0.0200 × 24 = **0.480 dm³** = 480 cm³.

Because equal moles of gases have equal volumes, the coefficients in a gas equation also give the ratio of **volumes**: in 2H₂ + O₂ → 2H₂O, 100 cm³ of hydrogen needs 50 cm³ of oxygen.

## Common mistakes

- Using cm³ in concentration = moles ÷ volume.
- Including the rough titre, or a non-concordant titre, in the mean.
- Forgetting the 1 : 2 ratio with sulfuric acid.
- Mixing up which volume goes with which solution in step 4.
- Using 24 cm³ instead of 24 dm³ (24 000 cm³) for one mole of gas.
- Giving g/dm³ when mol/dm³ was asked for.

## Exam technique: command words

- **Describe how to carry out a titration:** use numbered steps that name the apparatus (pipette, burette, conical flask, indicator) and include concordant repeats.
- **Calculate the concentration:** set out the four steps with units on every line.
- **Explain why** a step is done: give the purpose, such as "to remove water that would dilute the acid".
- **Suggest an improvement:** for example, adding drop by drop near the end-point, or using a white tile.
