## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which vessel has a wall only one cell thick?

A: aorta

B: capillary

C: renal vein

D: vena cava

**[1]**

**2.** Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the lungs to the heart. **[1]**

**3.** Name the blood vessel that carries blood away from the kidney. **[1]**

**4.** State **one** structural feature found in veins but not in arteries. **[1]**

**5.** State the function of capillaries and name **two** substances that pass from the blood into the tissue cells. **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the blood vessel that carries blood from the small intestine to the liver. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A photomicrograph shows cross-sections of two blood vessels, A and B, next to each other. A is round with a thick wall and a small lumen. B is flattened with a thin wall and a large lumen.

**(a)** Identify vessels A and B. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **two** differences in structure between A and B, other than shape. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain how the structure of vessel A is related to the pressure of the blood it carries. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows measurements of three vessels from the same tissue.

| Vessel | Wall thickness / mm | Lumen diameter / mm |
|---|---|---|
| P | 0.001 | 0.008 |
| Q | 0.5 | 8.0 |
| R | 1.0 | 4.0 |

**(a)** Identify P, Q and R. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the ratio of wall thickness to lumen diameter for vessel R and for vessel Q. Express each ratio in the form 1 : n. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your ratios to explain which of Q and R carries blood at the higher pressure. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Explain how **three** features of capillaries are related to their function. **[3]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Blood in veins is at low pressure.

**(a)** Explain why veins have valves. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the wide lumen of a vein is an advantage. **[1]**

**(c)** A student says, "Veins have thin walls so that oxygen can diffuse out of them into the cells." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**11.** A urea molecule is made in the liver. It travels in the blood and is removed by a kidney.

**(a)** Name the vessels the blood passes through between the left ventricle and the kidney. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the vessel that carries blood out of the kidney. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Name the vessels that carry blood out of the liver, and the large vein they join. **[2]**

**(d)** *(Extended)* Blood reaching the liver arrives in two vessels. Name both and state where the blood in each has come from. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Pulmonary vein [1].

**3.** Renal vein [1].

**4.** Valves [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Thin wall" or "wide lumen" describe size, not a feature present only in veins; the question asks for something veins have and arteries lack.

**5.** Exchange of substances between the blood and tissue cells [1]. Any two of: oxygen; glucose; other nutrients such as amino acids [1] [1].

**6.** Hepatic portal vein [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Portal vein" alone is often not accepted; give the full name.

**7. (a)** A is an artery [1]; B is a vein [1].

**(b)** A has a thicker wall than B [1]; A has a narrower lumen than B [1].

**(c)** A carries blood at high pressure (from the heart) [1]; its thick, muscular, elastic wall withstands the pressure without bursting / stretches and recoils [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b) each difference must compare both vessels. In (c) the pressure must be mentioned as well as the feature.

**8. (a)** P is a capillary [1]; Q is a vein [1]; R is an artery [1].

**(b)** R: 1.0 : 4.0 = **1 : 4** [1]. Q: 0.5 : 8.0 = **1 : 16** [1].

**(c)** R has a much thicker wall relative to its lumen than Q (1 : 4 compared with 1 : 16) [1]; a relatively thicker wall withstands higher pressure without bursting, so R carries blood at the higher pressure [1].

*Examiner insight:* P is identified by its tiny size and wall, not by its ratio, which is 1 : 8; reading the units matters. In (c) quote both ratios.

**9.** Any three, each with its link to exchange:

- wall one cell thick, so there is a short diffusion distance [1]
- very many / highly branched, so there is a large surface area for exchange [1]
- every cell is close to a capillary, so diffusion distances are short throughout the tissue [1]
- narrow lumen, so red blood cells pass close to the wall / slow flow gives time for exchange [1]

*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs feature plus consequence; "thin walls" without "one cell thick" or without "short diffusion distance" does not score.

**10. (a)** Blood is at low pressure, so it could flow backwards [1]; valves close to prevent backflow, keeping flow towards the heart [1].

**(b)** It offers little resistance to flow, so low-pressure blood can still return to the heart [1].

**(c)** Exchange of substances with cells happens in capillaries, not veins [1]; a vein wall is thin because blood pressure in veins is low, not to allow diffusion [1].

**11. (a)** Aorta [1]; renal artery [1].

**(b)** Renal vein [1].

**(c)** Hepatic veins [1]; vena cava [1].

**(d)** Hepatic artery, from the aorta / heart [1]; hepatic portal vein, from the gut / small intestine [1].

*Examiner insight:* Route questions are marked vessel by vessel; spell names clearly and keep them in order.

## Where marks are usually lost

- One-sided descriptions with no comparison between vessels.
- Saying arteries have valves, or forgetting valves in veins.
- Ratio answers given the wrong way round or without the form 1 : n.
- "Portal vein" or "liver vein" instead of the full syllabus names.
- Claiming diffusion of oxygen happens through vein walls.
