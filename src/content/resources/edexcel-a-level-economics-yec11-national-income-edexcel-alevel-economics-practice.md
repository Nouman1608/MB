## Section A — Multiple choice

**1.** Which of the following is an injection into the circular flow of income?

A) Savings

B) Imports

C) Exports

D) Taxation **[1]**

**2.** In an economy, MPS = 0.1, MPT = 0.3 and MPM = 0.2. The value of the multiplier is:

A) 0.6

B) 1.67

C) 2

D) 5 **[1]**

**3.** Which of the following is an example of wealth rather than income?

A) Monthly wages

B) Dividends received this year

C) The value of a family's house

D) Interest received on savings this year **[1]**

**4.** If withdrawals from the circular flow exceed injections, national income will tend to:

A) Rise

B) Fall

C) Stay the same

D) Rise and then keep rising **[1]**

## Section B — Short-answer questions

**5.** Define "the multiplier". **[2]**

**6.** In Country J, injections total 900 billion and withdrawals are always 30% of national income. Calculate equilibrium national income, and the new equilibrium if exports fall by 24 billion. You are advised to show your working. **[4]**

**7.** Explain the distinction between injections and withdrawals in the circular flow of income. **[4]**

**8.** Analyse how a rise in the marginal propensity to save might affect the final increase in national income from a government investment programme. **[6]**

## Section C — Data question

**9.** Read the extract, then answer all parts.

*Extract (invented).* Country K is a small, open economy that imports most of its manufactured goods. Unemployment has risen to its highest level for a decade and many factories are working below capacity. The government has announced an infrastructure programme of 8 billion. Official estimates put the marginal propensity to save at 0.12, the marginal propensity to tax at 0.28 and the marginal propensity to import at 0.35.

**(a)** Define "marginal propensity to import". **[2]**

**(b)** Using the extract, calculate the multiplier and the eventual change in national income from the programme. **[4]**

**(c)** Analyse why Country K's multiplier is relatively small. **[6]**

**(d)** Examine the likely effect of the programme on equilibrium real national output and the price level in Country K. **[8]**

**(e)** Discuss the significance of the multiplier for the effect of the programme on economic activity in Country K. **[14]**

## Section D — Essay (answer ONE)

**EITHER**

**10.** Evaluate the usefulness of the multiplier in predicting the effect of a change in government expenditure on national income. **[20]**

**OR**

**11.** Evaluate the likely effects of a large fall in investment on equilibrium real national output and the price level. **[20]**

## Answers

**1. C)** Exports bring spending by foreigners into the domestic flow; the other three are withdrawals [1].

Total: 1

**2. B)** MPW = 0.1 + 0.3 + 0.2 = 0.6, and 1 ÷ 0.6 = 1.67 [1].

Total: 1

**3. C)** A house is an asset, a stock valued at a point in time; the others are flows received over a period [1].

Total: 1

**4. B)** With a net withdrawal, spending on domestic output is less than the income leaving the flow, so output and income fall [1].

Total: 1

**5.** The ratio of the final change in national income to the initial change in an injection (such as investment, government spending or exports) [1], caused by successive rounds of spending in which one person's spending becomes another's income [1].

Total: 2

**6.** At equilibrium W = J, so 0.3Y = 900 [1] and **Y = 3000 billion** [1]. Injections fall to 876, so 0.3Y = 876 [1] and **Y = 2920 billion**, a fall of 80 billion (24 ÷ 0.3) [1].

Total: 4

**7.** An injection is spending on domestic output that does not come from domestic households' current income: investment, government expenditure and exports [1]. A withdrawal is income that is not passed on as spending on domestic output: savings, taxation and imports [1]. Injections raise the level of spending and so tend to increase national income [1]; withdrawals rise with income and reduce the flow, so national income settles where withdrawals equal injections [1].

Total: 4

**8.** The investment programme is an injection that becomes income for workers and firms [1]. A higher MPS means a larger share of each round of extra income is saved instead of spent [1], so less is passed on as spending on domestic output in the next round [1]. The MPW rises, so the multiplier, 1/MPW, falls [1]. For example, if MPW rises from 0.4 to 0.5, the multiplier falls from 2.5 to 2 [1]. The AD curve shifts right by less, and the final increase in national income from the programme is smaller [1].

Total: 6

**9. (a)** The proportion of an increase in income [1] that is spent on imported goods and services, ΔM ÷ ΔY [1].

**(b)** MPW = 0.12 + 0.28 + 0.35 = 0.75 [1]; multiplier = 1 ÷ 0.75 = 1.33 [1]; change in national income = 1.33 × 8 [1] = **about 10.7 billion** [1].

**(c)** Country K imports most manufactured goods, so its MPM (0.35) is high [1]; much of each round of extra income is spent abroad and leaves the domestic flow [1]. Its MPT (0.28) is also substantial, so tax withdraws a further share [1]. Together with saving, MPW is 0.75 [1], leaving only 25% of each round to be spent on domestic output [1], so later rounds shrink quickly and the multiplier is only 1.33 [1].

**(d)** Analysis: the programme raises G, an injection, so AD shifts right [1]; through the multiplier the shift is larger than 8 billion [1]; factories are below capacity and unemployment is high, so the economy is on the flatter part of AS [1]; real output rises with little increase in the price level [1]; firms take on more workers, reducing unemployment [1]; the diagram shows AD₁ to AD₂ with a large rise in output and a small rise in the price level [1]. Evaluation (any two): the small multiplier limits the rise in output [1]; infrastructure projects take time to start, so the effect is delayed [1]; the infrastructure may also raise LRAS in the long run; leakages to imports may raise other countries' output more than Country K's.

**(e)** Analysis (up to 8, 2 marks for each developed point): each injection creates further rounds of spending, so national income rises by more than 8 billion; with a multiplier of 1.33 the total rise is about 10.7 billion; the extra income raises employment and firms' revenues, increasing economic activity; the rounds of spending raise tax revenue, offsetting part of the programme's cost. Evaluation (up to 6, 2 marks for each developed point): the multiplier is small because of high leakages, so the programme's effect on domestic activity is limited; the estimates of MPS, MPT and MPM may be wrong or may change, for example if worried households save more; the multiplier takes time to work through; spare capacity means more of the AD shift raises real output than prices; a supported judgement that the multiplier matters, but in Country K it adds only about a third to the direct effect.

Total: 34

*Examiner insight:* In part (e), use the figures from parts (b) and (c). An answer that discusses the multiplier in general without Country K's small value misses the application.

**10.** *Indicative content.*

**For** (up to 8, 2 marks each): the multiplier shows that a change in G has a larger total effect than the initial spending, which helps a government size a policy; the formula 1/MPW links the effect to measurable propensities; it explains why open economies with high MPM gain less from domestic spending; it shows that cuts in spending also reduce income by a multiple.

**Against** (up to 8, 2 marks each): the propensities are estimates and can change, for example if confidence falls and households save more; the process takes time, so the effect within a year may be smaller than the full multiplier suggests; the multiplier applies to AD, and near full capacity much of the effect is a higher price level, not real output; other changes, such as a fall in exports, may happen at the same time.

**Judgement** (4): the multiplier is a useful guide to the direction and approximate size of the effect, but its predictions are only as good as the estimated propensities, and the effect on real output depends on spare capacity and timing.

Total: 20

**11.** *Indicative content.*

**For a large fall in output** (up to 8, 2 marks each): investment is an injection, so a fall shifts AD left; the multiplier makes the fall in national income larger than the fall in investment; firms cut output and employment, and lower incomes reduce consumption further; with a net withdrawal, income falls until withdrawals have fallen to equal the lower injections.

**Qualifying points** (up to 8, 2 marks each): the price level falls or rises more slowly, though downward stickiness of wages and prices may mean output falls more than prices; the size of the effect depends on the multiplier, which is small in open economies with high MPM; other injections, such as government spending or exports, may rise to offset the fall; lower investment also reduces the future capital stock, slowing growth in LRAS.

**Judgement** (4): a large fall in investment is likely to reduce equilibrium real output by more than the initial fall and to lower inflationary pressure, but the size depends on the multiplier, any offsetting injections and the time period considered.

Total: 20

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling saving an injection or exports a withdrawal.
- Confusing income and wealth.
- Using 1/MPC instead of 1/(1 − MPC), or forgetting one of the withdrawals in the MPW.
- Assuming the full multiplier effect is on real output, whatever the spare capacity.
- Writing about the multiplier in general without using the figures given.
- Giving analysis without evaluation in examine, discuss and evaluate questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these questions from Edexcel past papers?**
No. They are original practice questions with mark schemes written for section 2.3.4 of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level Economics specification.

**Which multiplier formula should I use?**
The specification gives 1/(1 − MPC) and 1/MPW, where MPW = MPS + MPT + MPM. Use whichever matches the data given: if you are told the three withdrawal propensities, add them to find the MPW.
