This guide covers sub-topic **17.2 Mitosis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Every outcome in this sub-topic is Supplement content, so the whole page is **Extended only**: it is tested on Papers 2 and 4, and Core candidates do not need it.

The [inheritance study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-inheritance/) summarises mitosis in a few bullet points. This page explains what happens to the chromosomes before and during mitosis, tracks chromosome and DNA quantities through a division, works through each role of mitosis with examples, and links stem cells to gene expression. | Outcome | What you must be able to do |

| 17.2.2 | State the role of mitosis in growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of cells and asexual reproduction |
| 17.2.3 | State that the exact replication of chromosomes occurs before mitosis |
| 17.2.4 | State that during mitosis, the copies of chromosomes separate, maintaining the chromosome number in each daughter cell |
| 17.2.5 | Describe stem cells as unspecialised cells that divide by mitosis to produce daughter cells that can become specialised for specific functions |

## What mitosis is

> Mitosis is **nuclear division** giving rise to **genetically identical cells**.

- **Nuclear division**: it is the **nucleus** that divides. After the nucleus has divided, the rest of the cell divides too, giving two daughter cells.
- **Genetically identical**: the daughter cells have exactly the same chromosomes, and so the same genes and alleles, as each other and as the parent cell.

The syllabus states that the stages of mitosis are not required. You need to know what happens to the chromosomes, not the names of the phases.

## Before and during mitosis

Two statements explain why the daughter cells are identical.

1. **Before mitosis**, each chromosome is **copied exactly** (replicated). The two copies stay joined together until the nucleus divides.
2. **During mitosis**, the **copies of each chromosome separate**, and one copy goes to each new nucleus. Each daughter cell therefore keeps the **same chromosome number** as the parent cell.

Because the copying is exact and each daughter nucleus receives one copy of every chromosome, the daughter cells are genetically identical.

### Worked example 1: tracking the quantities

A human body cell is followed through one division. "DNA" is measured in arbitrary units; the values are chosen for this example.

| Stage | Chromosomes in the nucleus | DNA per nucleus / arbitrary units |
|---|---|---|
| Cell before replication | 46 | 6 |
| After exact replication (copies still joined) | 46, each made of two identical copies | 12 |
| Each daughter cell after mitosis | 46 | 6 |

Points examiners look for:

- The **DNA quantity doubles** before mitosis, because every chromosome is copied.
- The **chromosome number does not change**: 46 in the parent cell and 46 in each daughter cell.
- After division each daughter cell has **half the DNA of the cell just before division**, which equals the amount in the original cell.

A common trap is to say the daughter cells have "half the chromosomes". They have half the **DNA of the replicated cell**, but the **same number of chromosomes** as the original cell.

## The four roles of mitosis

The syllabus lists four roles. For each, learn what it means and an example.

| Role | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Growth** | the number of cells increases, so the organism gets bigger | a zygote dividing into an embryo; cells dividing near the tips of roots and shoots |
| **Repair of damaged tissues** | new cells fill the damaged area | skin cells dividing to close a cut; bone cells repairing a fracture |
| **Replacement of cells** | worn-out or lost cells are replaced | cells lost from the surface of the skin and the lining of the gut; red blood cells, which are made continually in bone marrow |
| **Asexual reproduction** | a new individual forms from cells of one parent | yeast budding; potato tubers; strawberry runners |

All four depend on the same property: the new cells contain **exactly the same genetic information**. A skin cell made to repair a cut must carry the same genes as the cells around it, and a plant grown from a tuber is a clone of its parent.

### Worked example 2: counting cells

*A zygote divides by mitosis. Every cell then divides again, and so on. How many cells are there after five rounds of division?*

```
1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → 32
Number of cells = 2⁵ = 32
```

Each of the 32 cells is genetically identical to the zygote, so each contains the full set of genes.

## Stem cells

> Stem cells are **unspecialised cells** that **divide by mitosis** to produce daughter cells that **can become specialised** for specific functions.

Three ideas, all needed for full marks:

1. Stem cells are **unspecialised**: they have not yet developed the features of a particular cell type.
2. They **divide by mitosis**, so their daughter cells are genetically identical to them.
3. The daughter cells **can become specialised**, for example as nerve cells, muscle cells or blood cells.

The cells of an early embryo are stem cells. Some tissues in adults also contain stem cells; for example, stem cells in bone marrow produce new blood cells.

### Linking stem cells to gene expression

A stem cell and the specialised cells made from it have **the same genes**, because mitosis gives identical cells. They become different because **different genes are expressed** in each type of specialised cell (sub-topic 17.1). A cell that becomes a red blood cell expresses the gene for haemoglobin; a cell that becomes a muscle cell does not, but expresses genes for the proteins a muscle cell needs.

### Annotated model answer

*Explain how cells produced by mitosis from stem cells in bone marrow can become different types of blood cell.* **[3]**

> Stem cells are unspecialised cells that divide by mitosis [1]. Their daughter cells are genetically identical, so they all contain the same genes [1]. Different genes are expressed in different daughter cells, so they make different proteins and become specialised as different types of blood cell [1].

## Mitosis and meiosis: do not mix them up

Meiosis (sub-topic 17.3) is the other type of nuclear division. Mitosis keeps the chromosome number the same and gives genetically identical cells; meiosis halves the chromosome number and gives genetically different cells. Questions often give a situation and ask which division is involved. If the new cells are for growth, repair, replacement or asexual reproduction, it is mitosis.

## Common mistakes

- Saying mitosis is "cell division" without "nuclear division" and "genetically identical".
- Placing chromosome replication **during** mitosis. Exact replication happens **before** mitosis.
- Saying the chromosome number halves. It is **maintained**.
- Giving only "growth" when the question asks for the roles. There are **four**.
- Saying stem cells are "specialised cells that divide". They are **unspecialised**.
- Saying specialised cells have lost genes. They contain the same genes but express different ones.
- Describing the stages of mitosis at length. They are not required and earn no extra credit.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** mitosis: "nuclear division giving rise to genetically identical cells".
- **State** a role: use the syllabus words (growth, repair of damaged tissues, replacement of cells, asexual reproduction) and give an example if the question asks for one.
- **Explain** why daughter cells are identical: exact replication before mitosis, then copies separate, one to each daughter cell.
- **Interpret** a table or graph of DNA content: doubling before division, halving at division, chromosome number unchanged.
