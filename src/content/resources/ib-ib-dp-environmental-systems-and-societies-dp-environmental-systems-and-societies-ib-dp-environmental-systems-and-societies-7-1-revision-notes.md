## Core recall

- Natural capital is a **stock**; natural income is the **flow** of goods and services it yields.
- "Renewable" depends on use: a forest, fishery or aquifer is used renewably only while the harvest stays within its natural increase.
- Some aquifers receive almost no recharge, so pumping them is effectively mining.
- Sustainable yield = the natural increase of the stock over a period.
- Under logistic growth, the increase is largest at about half the carrying capacity; that largest increase is the MSY.
- A fixed quota set at MSY becomes unsustainable if the stock falls below K ÷ 2, and the decline then accelerates.
- For non-renewable capital, reserves (economically extractable now) are much smaller than total resources and change with price, technology and discovery.
- Markets price direct use values well and regulating services and non-use values poorly.
- Hardin (1968) predicted overuse of open-access commons; Ostrom showed that communities with suitable rules can manage commons sustainably.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Natural capital | Natural resources that can produce a sustainable flow of goods or services |
| Natural income | The yield from natural capital (timber growth, fish recruited, water filtered) |
| Ecosystem services | Benefits people gain from ecosystems: provisioning, regulating, supporting, cultural |
| Sustainable yield | The increase in natural capital that can be harvested without reducing the stock |
| Maximum sustainable yield | The largest harvest that can be taken year after year; at about half of K |
| Carrying capacity (K) | The maximum population an environment can support sustainably |
| Reserve | The part of a non-renewable resource that is known and economically extractable now |
| Reserves-to-production ratio | Reserves ÷ annual production; years left at the current rate of use |
| Use value | Value from direct, indirect or possible future (option) use |
| Non-use value | Existence and bequest value |
| Intrinsic value | Value of nature in itself, independent of human use |
| Open access | No one can be excluded from using the resource |
| Individual transferable quota | A tradeable share of a total allowable catch |

## Formulas

```
Sustainable yield = stock at (t + 1) − stock at t
Logistic increase = r × N × (1 − N ÷ K)
MSY               = r × K ÷ 4, at N = K ÷ 2
Stock change      = natural increase − harvest
R/P ratio         = reserves ÷ annual production
```

## Ecosystem services: one example per category

| Category | Forest example | Coral reef example |
|---|---|---|
| Provisioning | Timber, fuelwood, wild food | Fish, shellfish |
| Regulating | Carbon storage, flood control, water filtration | Wave protection for coasts |
| Supporting | Soil formation, nutrient cycling | Nursery habitat |
| Cultural | Recreation, spiritual value | Tourism, diving |

## Management tools in one line each

- **Total allowable catch:** cap on the whole harvest; fails if stock estimates are wrong.
- **Individual transferable quotas:** tradeable shares of the cap; can concentrate ownership.
- **No-take reserves:** closed areas let stocks rebuild and spill over; need enforcement.
- **Gear and size limits:** protect young individuals; hard to police.
- **Community management:** local rules and monitoring; best with clear users and boundaries.
- **Pricing and certification:** move users and consumers towards sustainable sources; depend on demand and honest auditing.

## Ostrom's conditions (short form)

Clear boundaries; rules fitted to local conditions; users help make rules; accountable monitoring; graduated sanctions; local conflict resolution; outside recognition of the right to organise.

## Case reminder: the northern cod

Industrial trawling plus overestimated stock assessments led to collapse; Canada closed the fishery with a moratorium in 1992; recovery has been slow and incomplete. Use it for uncertainty, political pressure on quotas and time lags.

## Exam traps

- Calling the harvest the sustainable yield.
- Using MSY as a safe target at any stock size.
- Reading the R/P ratio as an exact date when a resource runs out.
- Treating the tragedy of the commons as inevitable for every shared resource.
- Naming an ecosystem service without its category or beneficiary.
- Giving only economic value in a valuation question and ignoring intrinsic value.
- Omitting units (t per year, years, Mt).

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish natural capital from natural income.
2. A forest's biomass rises from 5,000 t to 5,350 t in a year with no felling. State the sustainable yield.
3. When can groundwater be treated as non-renewable?
4. Classify each as provisioning, regulating, supporting or cultural: (a) pollination; (b) timber; (c) recreation; (d) nutrient cycling.
5. A stock has K = 80,000 t and r = 0.5 per year. Calculate the MSY.
6. For the same stock at N = 20,000 t, calculate the natural increase.
7. Why is a quota fixed at MSY dangerous if the stock falls below half of K?
8. A country has reserves of 900 Mt and produces 45 Mt a year. Calculate the R/P ratio.
9. Give two reasons why reserves can increase without new discoveries.
10. Distinguish use value from non-use value, with one example of each.
11. State two of Ostrom's conditions for successful commons management.
12. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of individual transferable quotas.

### Answers

1. Natural capital is the stock of natural resources; natural income is the yield of goods and services that the stock produces.
2. 5,350 − 5,000 = **350 t per year**.
3. When abstraction exceeds recharge, or when the aquifer receives almost no recharge today.
4. (a) Regulating; (b) provisioning; (c) cultural; (d) supporting.
5. 0.5 × 80,000 ÷ 4 = **10,000 t per year** (at N = 40,000 t).
6. 0.5 × 20,000 × (1 − 0.25) = **7,500 t per year**.
7. Below K ÷ 2 the natural increase is smaller than MSY, so the harvest exceeds the increase; the stock shrinks, the increase falls further, and decline accelerates.
8. 900 ÷ 45 = **20 years**.
9. Any two: higher prices make lower-grade deposits economic; new extraction technology; better processing that recovers more from ore.
10. Use value comes from using the resource, such as selling timber; non-use value exists without use, such as knowing a species survives (existence value).
11. Any two: clear boundaries; local rules; users help make rules; accountable monitoring; graduated sanctions; local conflict resolution; outside recognition.
12. Advantage: holders have a long-term stake, so less incentive to race for fish. Disadvantage: quotas can be bought up by large firms, squeezing out small fishers.
