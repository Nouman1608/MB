## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Indifference curve | Combinations of two goods giving the same satisfaction |
| Indifference map | A set of indifference curves for one consumer |
| Marginal rate of substitution (MRS) | Amount of Y given up for one more X while staying equally satisfied |
| Budget line | Combinations of two goods that just use up a given income at given prices |
| Price effect | Total change in quantity demanded after a price change |
| Substitution effect | Change due to relative prices alone, on the original indifference curve |
| Income effect | Change due to the change in real income |
| Inferior good | Demand falls when real income rises |
| Giffen good | Inferior good whose income effect outweighs the substitution effect |

## Indifference curve properties

- **Downward sloping:** more X needs less Y to keep satisfaction the same.
- **Convex:** MRS diminishes as the consumer has more X.
- **Higher is better:** more of at least one good, no less of the other.
- **Never cross:** a shared point cannot give two satisfaction levels.

## Budget line formulae

```
Px x X + Py x Y = M
X intercept = M / Px
Y intercept = M / Py
Slope (size) = Px / Py
Optimum: tangency, MRS = Px / Py
```

Example: M = 120, Px = 3, Py = 6. Intercepts: 40 X and 20 Y; slope 0.5.

## Shifts and pivots

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Income up or down | Parallel shift out or in |
| Price of X changes | Pivot around the Y intercept |
| Price of Y changes | Pivot around the X intercept |
| Both prices change by the same % | Parallel shift, like an income change |
| Income and all prices change by the same % | No change |

## Splitting the effects: the method

1. Original optimum A on IC1 with BL1.
2. New budget line BL2 after the price change; new optimum C.
3. Compensated line BLc: **parallel to BL2**, tangent to **IC1** at B.
4. A → B substitution effect; B → C income effect; A → C price effect.

## Direction of effects

| Good | Price fall: substitution | Price fall: income | Price fall: total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Normal | Up | Up | Up |
| Inferior | Up | Down, smaller | Up |
| Giffen | Up | Down, larger | Down |

For a **price rise**, reverse every arrow: the substitution effect always reduces quantity.

## Worked numbers to remember

Price of X rises from 4 to 5 (income 200, Py 2, half of income spent on each good): A = 25 X, B = 22.36 X, C = 20 X. Substitution −2.64, income −2.36, total −5. Normal good.

## Limitations

- Only two goods.
- Curves cannot be observed or measured.
- Assumes rational, informed consumers with stable tastes.
- Fixed prices, no discounts, no saving or borrowing, divisible goods.
- Giffen goods are rare in practice.
- Advantage over utility theory: utility only needs to be ranked, not measured.

## Exam traps

- Compensated line parallel to the **new** budget line, not the old one.
- Slope = Px ÷ Py, not Py ÷ Px.
- Not every inferior good is Giffen.
- A price change pivots; an income change shifts.
- The substitution effect never moves quantity in the same direction as price.
- Label every line, curve and point in the diagram.
