## Customer needs

| Idea | Recall |
|---|---|
| Purpose of market research | Reduce risk; understand and anticipate customer needs |
| Primary | First-hand, specific; slower and costlier |
| Secondary | Existing data; cheap and quick; may be dated |
| Quantitative | Numbers, easy to compare |
| Qualitative | Opinions and reasons; explains why |
| Confidence interval | Range likely to hold the true figure; narrower means more precise |
| Confidence level | How sure we are the range holds it (for example 95%) |
| Market mapping | Brands plotted on two features; shows rivals and gaps |

Influences on how research is done: **risk, cost, time, need for reliability**. A larger sample usually narrows the interval but costs more.

## Markets and data

**Types of market:** B2B (few, large buyers; account managers), B2C (many buyers; branding), C2C (individuals trading, often online), goods (tangible) against services (intangible; people, process and environment matter more).

**Market conditions:** degree of competition, competitive behaviour (price cutting, promotion), market growth.

| Measure | Form |
|---|---|
| Market share (%) | one firm's sales ÷ sales of whole market × 100 |
| Market growth (%) | change in size of market ÷ its original size × 100 |
| Sales growth (%) | change in sales ÷ original sales × 100 (standard form) |
| Return on marketing spend (%) | profit from marketing activities ÷ marketing spend × 100 (standard form) |
| Index number | value ÷ base value × 100 (standard form) |
| Percentage change | change ÷ original × 100 (standard form) |

Steps: write the formula, substitute with the correct year in each place, calculate, add the unit, then comment.

- Sales per region, product or store: compare using percentages or an index so size differences do not mislead.
- Volume (units) can rise while value (revenue) falls if prices fall.
- Correlation: positive, negative, strong, weak. It shows association, not cause.

**Reminder.** The market rises from £250m to £275m: growth 10%. A firm with £27.5m sales in the later year has share 27.5 ÷ 275 × 100 = 10%.

## Demand

Influences: price, incomes, tastes, substitutes, complements, advertising, number of buyers.

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| PED | percentage change in quantity ÷ percentage change in price |
| YED | percentage change in quantity ÷ percentage change in income |

- PED, disregarding sign: size above 1 elastic, below 1 inelastic.
- Elastic: a price cut raises revenue; a price rise lowers it.
- Inelastic: a price rise raises revenue; a price cut lowers it.
- YED positive: normal good (above 1, demand grows faster than income). YED negative: inferior good.

**Reminder.** Price up 5%, quantity down 2%: PED = −2 ÷ 5 = −0.4, inelastic, so revenue rises (1.05 × 0.98 = 1.029 of the original).

## Target market

- Segmentation bases: **demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural**.
- Influences on choosing and entering a target market: segment size and growth, competition, resources and brand, objectives, entry cost.
- **Niche:** small, specialised; less competition, higher prices possible; limited sales, vulnerable if the segment shrinks. **Mass:** larger sales; more rivals, less tailored.
- Adapt the whole mix to the target market so every element fits.

## Objectives and planning

- Marketing objectives: **lead generation, sales, market share, brand awareness, customer retention, engagement, conversion**.
- Influences on objectives: corporate objectives, finance, capacity, rivals, market conditions.
- Budget: influenced by revenue, profit, objectives, rivals and cash; significance is that it limits what can be done.
- Demand changes: rise (capacity, stock), fall (promotion, price, product), cyclical (follows the economy), seasonal (repeats in the year).
- Links: operations (capacity), finance (budget, cash), people (skills).
- Competitiveness: marketing decisions that make the offer more attractive or build loyalty relative to rivals.

## Marketing mix

### Product

| Tool | Recall |
|---|---|
| Product life cycle | Development, introduction, growth, maturity, decline |
| Extension strategies | Advertising, rebranding, lowering the price, adapting the product |
| Boston Matrix | Stars (high share, high growth; invest); cash cows (high share, low growth; fund others); question marks (low share, high growth; invest or withdraw); dogs (low share, low growth; consider dropping) |
| New product development | Purpose: renew range, gain advantage. Challenges: cost, time, failure |

The Boston Matrix looks at many products at one moment; the life cycle follows one product over time.

### Price

- Influences: objectives, target market, demand, PED, costs, competitors, rest of the mix.
- Impact on volume, revenue and profit margins: a cut may lift volume but lowers margin per unit.
- **Cost-based.** **Demand-based:** penetration, price skimming, dynamic pricing. **Competition-based:** premium, going rate, discount.

### Place

Direct distribution (including e-commerce): control, data, full margin; own delivery costs. Intermediaries (wholesalers, retailers): reach, storage; margin lost, less control. Multi-channel: wider reach; complexity and conflict.

### Promotional mix

| Tool | Value | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Advertising | Wide reach | Cost |
| Social media, influencers | Targeted, interactive | Control, reputation |
| Sales promotions | Short-run sales | Customers wait for deals |
| Sponsorship | Awareness, association | Cost, indirect |
| Personal selling, account managers | Relationships in B2B | Cost per contact |
| Branding, loyalty, own label | Recognition, premium price, retailer margin | Cost; limited outlets for own label |

Influences on the mix: budget, target market, product, objectives, rivals.

## Digital technology

- Uses (including AI): social media campaigns, content marketing, SEO, PPC, website cookies.
- Measures of success: search performance, click-through rates, time on site, conversion rates, users or subscribers, advertising revenue.
- CRM: customer acquisition, retention, lead generation, pipeline management.
- Data analytics: segmentation, campaign tracking, personalisation, predictions, dynamic pricing.
- Competitiveness: lower cost per customer and faster response, against set-up cost, poor data and privacy concerns.

Standard forms: click-through rate = clicks ÷ views × 100; conversion rate = buyers ÷ visitors or clicks × 100.

## Must-know distinctions

- Market growth, sales growth and market share: three different measures, so a firm can grow sales yet lose share if the market grows faster.
- Primary and secondary research; quantitative and qualitative data.
- Sales volume and sales value.
- Elastic and inelastic; PED and YED; normal and inferior goods.
- Niche and mass marketing; penetration and price skimming.
- Direct distribution and distribution through intermediaries.
- Corporate objectives and marketing objectives: the second supports the first.

## Second reminder set

- Index: a store sells 80 units in the base month and 92 now. Index = 92 ÷ 80 × 100 = 115, so sales are 15% above the base.
- Elastic case: price down 4%, quantity up 10%, PED = 10 ÷ −4 = −2.5. Revenue is 0.96 × 1.10 = 1.056 of the original, up 5.6%.
- Volume and value: units up 6%, price down 8%. Value is 1.06 × 0.92 = 0.9752 of the original, down about 2.5%.
- Digital: 50,000 views, 1,000 clicks, 50 sales. Click-through rate = 2%; conversion rate on clicks = 5%.

## Case-study technique

Quantitative questions in the assessment overview are worth 9 or 15 marks and ask you to use the result in analysis and evaluation. For any longer answer: state the point, apply it to the named business with data, explain the effect on sales, share, cost or reputation, weigh it against a limit or an alternative, then close with a judgement that names its conditions (segment size, cash or rivals).

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four segmentation bases.
2. Sales rise from £8m to £9.2m. Calculate sales growth.
3. A region's sales index is 92 against a base of 100. What does it show?
4. YED is −0.3. What does it show?
5. A campaign cost £8,000 and the profit from marketing activities was £10,000. Calculate return on marketing spend.
6. Give one value and one limit of an intermediary.
7. Name three digital success measures.
8. Why might a wide confidence interval make a launch decision risky?
9. Identify the Boston Matrix category with high share in a slow-growing market.
10. State two extension strategies.

### Answers

1. Demographic, geographic, psychographic, behavioural.
2. (9.2 − 8) ÷ 8 × 100 = **15%**.
3. Sales are 8% below the base period.
4. Inferior good: demand falls as income rises.
5. 10,000 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = **125%**.
6. Value: reach or storage. Limit: takes a margin or loses control.
7. Any three from search performance, click-through rate, time on site, conversion rate, users or subscribers, advertising revenue.
8. The true figure could lie anywhere in a wide range, including a level too low to pay.
9. Cash cow.
10. Any two from advertising, rebranding, lowering the price, adapting the product.

## Where marks are lost

- Wrong base year in percentage change, share or index.
- Quoting an elasticity value without saying what it means for revenue.
- Describing the Boston Matrix without a decision.
- Applying the mix without linking it to the target market.
- An evaluation that repeats advantages and disadvantages with no reasoned judgement.
