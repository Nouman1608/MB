## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| GMO | Genetically modified organism: one whose genetic material has been changed by genetic engineering |
| Productivity | Amount of product per unit of land, time, feed or other input |
| Quality | Properties of the product, e.g. nutritional value, appearance, storage |
| Promoter | DNA sequence where RNA polymerase binds to start transcription |
| Herbicide | Chemical that kills plants (weeds) |
| Bt toxin | Insecticidal protein coded by a gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis* |
| Refuge | Area of non-GM crop planted near a Bt crop to slow the evolution of resistance |
| Sterile | Unable to produce viable gametes / offspring |
| Gene flow | Transfer of alleles between populations, e.g. by cross-pollination |

## 19.3.1 The three examples

### GM salmon

- Atlantic salmon + **growth hormone gene from Chinook salmon** + **promoter from ocean pout**.
- Promoter keeps the gene on **all year** (normal fish grow mainly in warmer months).
- Faster growth → **market size sooner** → less feed and time per fish → more fish per tank per year.
- **Sterile females** in **land-based tanks** → cannot breed with or compete with wild salmon if they escape.

### Herbicide-resistant soybean

- Gene from a bacterium (*Agrobacterium*) for a version of an enzyme that **glyphosate does not inhibit**.
- Field sprayed during growth → weeds die, soybean survives.
- Less competition for light, water, mineral ions → **higher yield**.
- Fewer sprays; less ploughing (less soil erosion).
- **Risk:** resistant weeds evolve; possible gene flow to wild relatives.

### Insect-resistant (Bt) cotton

- Gene from *Bacillus thuringiensis* → **Bt toxin** in every plant cell.
- Activated in the alkaline gut of feeding larvae; binds gut receptors; damages gut wall → larvae die.
- Only insects that **eat the crop** affected; predators of pests spared.
- Less damage to bolls → **higher yield**; far less insecticide spraying.
- **Risk:** pests evolve resistance → plant **refuges** of non-Bt cotton.

| | Salmon | Soybean | Cotton |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source of gene | Chinook salmon (+ ocean pout promoter) | *Agrobacterium* | *Bacillus thuringiensis* |
| Product | Growth hormone all year | Glyphosate-insensitive enzyme | Bt toxin |
| Benefit | Faster growth | Weed control | Pest control |
| Containment / management | Sterile, female, land tanks | Rotate herbicides | Refuges |

## Resistance by natural selection: method in steps

1. Variation: a few weeds (or insects) carry an allele giving resistance (from random mutation).
2. Selection pressure: repeated herbicide spraying (or Bt toxin).
3. Susceptible individuals die; resistant ones survive.
4. Survivors reproduce and pass on the resistance allele.
5. Allele frequency rises over generations; the population becomes resistant.

**Refuge logic:** susceptible insects from refuges mate with resistant survivors → offspring mostly heterozygous or susceptible → resistance allele stays rare.

## 19.3.2 Ethical and social implications

| Theme | Benefits | Concerns |
|---|---|---|
| Food supply | Higher yields; more food per hectare | Benefits may not reach the poorest |
| Economics | Lower costs (fewer sprays, less feed) | Patented, expensive seed; new seed each season; corporate control |
| Environment | Less insecticide; less ploughing; contained fish farms | Resistant weeds and pests; gene flow to wild relatives; less farmland plant diversity; escaped fish |
| Health | Tested before approval; less pesticide residue | Fears of allergens or long-term effects |
| Consumers | — | Demand for labelling and choice |
| Values | Less time in captivity per fish | Objection to altering animals or moving genes between species; welfare concerns |

## Exam traps

- "Bt cotton kills all insects." Only those that eat it.
- "Herbicide-resistant crops do not need herbicide." They **can be sprayed**.
- "Herbicide causes resistance mutations." Mutations are random; herbicide **selects**.
- Promoter ≠ growth hormone gene: promoter from ocean pout, gene from Chinook salmon.
- Sterility of GM salmon is to prevent breeding with wild fish, not to improve growth.
- "Discuss" answers must include both sides.

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish productivity and quality.
2. Which species supplies the growth hormone gene in GM salmon?
3. What is the role of the ocean pout promoter?
4. Give two measures that reduce the risk of GM salmon breeding with wild salmon.
5. Why can GM soybean be sprayed with glyphosate?
6. How does herbicide resistance increase soybean yield?
7. Name the bacterium that provides the gene in insect-resistant cotton.
8. Why does Bt cotton reduce harm to insects that do not eat the crop?
9. Explain how a weed population could become resistant to glyphosate.
10. What is a refuge, and why is it used?
11. Give two economic concerns about GM crops.
12. Give two environmental benefits of Bt cotton.

### Answers

1. Productivity is the amount produced per unit input; quality is the properties of the product.
2. **Chinook salmon**.
3. Keeps the growth hormone gene switched on all year.
4. Fish are sterile (and female); farmed in land-based tanks.
5. It has a bacterial enzyme that glyphosate does not inhibit, so it keeps making the amino acids it needs.
6. Weeds are killed, so less competition for light, water and mineral ions.
7. ***Bacillus thuringiensis***.
8. The toxin is in the plant, so only insects that feed on it are exposed (no spraying).
9. A random mutation gives resistance; spraying kills non-resistant weeds; resistant weeds survive, reproduce and pass on the allele; its frequency rises.
10. An area of non-Bt crop; susceptible insects survive there and mate with resistant ones, keeping the resistance allele rare.
11. Any two: expensive patented seed; seed must be bought each season; a few companies control supply; poorer farmers excluded.
12. Any two: less insecticide spraying; less harm to non-target insects and predators; less exposure of farm workers to insecticide.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing GM organisms without linking the protein made to the effect on yield.
- Leaving natural selection out of resistance answers.
- Ethical answers that list only concerns.
