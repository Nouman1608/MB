## Section A: short questions

**1.** Here is a list of numbers: 15, 23, 27, 32, 49, 51, 64.

From the list, write down

**(a)** the prime number, **[1]**

**(b)** a cube number, **[1]**

**(c)** the two square numbers, **[1]**

**(d)** the number that is a factor of 96. **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** List all the factors of 84. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down all the common factors of 84 and 63. **[2]**

**3.** Show that 437 is not a prime number. **[2]**

**4.** Write 756 as a product of its prime factors. Give your answer in index form. **[3]**

**5.** By listing, find

**(a)** the highest common factor of 14 and 35, **[2]**

**(b)** the lowest common multiple of 14 and 35. **[2]**

**6.** You are given that 52 × 38 = 1 976. Write down the value of

**(a)** 520 × 38, **[1]**

**(b)** 5 200 × 380, **[1]**

**(c)** 19 760 ÷ 52. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.**

**(a)** 2 400 = 2ᵃ × 3ᵇ × 5ᶜ. Find the values of a, b and c. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence write 4 800 as a product of its prime factors. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your answer to part (a) to explain why 2 400 is not a square number. **[1]**

**8.** A = 2⁴ × 3² × 5 and B = 2² × 3 × 5² × 7.

**(a)** Find the highest common factor of A and B. Give your answer as an ordinary number. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the lowest common multiple of A and B. Give your answer as a product of prime factors. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the smallest whole number k such that A × k is a cube number. **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Write 126 and 120 as products of their prime factors. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence find the highest common factor of 126 and 120. **[1]**

**(c)** Hence find the lowest common multiple of 126 and 120. **[1]**

**10.** A bus on route A leaves the station every 18 minutes. A bus on route B leaves every 30 minutes. Buses on both routes leave together at 08:10.

**(a)** When do buses on both routes next leave together? **[3]**

**(b)** How many times between 08:10 and 14:00, including 08:10, do buses on both routes leave together? **[2]**

**11.** Rectangular tiles measure 16 cm by 28 cm. They are laid the same way round, with no gaps or overlaps, to make the smallest possible square. How many tiles are needed? **[4]**

**12.** A teacher has 72 pencils and 108 rulers. She puts them all into identical packs, with nothing left over, and makes as many packs as possible.

**(a)** How many packs does she make? **[2]**

**(b)** How many pencils and how many rulers are in each pack? **[2]**

**13.** Two numbers, both greater than 12, have highest common factor 12 and lowest common multiple 180. Find the two numbers. **[3]**

**14.** Sam says, "2² × 15 and 2 × 3 × 10 are two different prime factorisations of 60."

Explain why Sam is wrong, and give the prime factorisation of 60. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **23** [1]

**(b)** **27** or **64** [1]

**(c)** **49 and 64** [1]

**(d)** **32** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* 51 looks prime but 5 + 1 = 6, so it is divisible by 3: 51 = 3 × 17. Always run the digit-sum test.

**2. (a)** At least six correct factors with none wrong [1]; all twelve: **1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 12, 14, 21, 28, 42, 84** [1]

**(b)** Factors of 63 are 1, 3, 7, 9, 21, 63 [1]; common factors **1, 3, 7, 21** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Work in factor pairs (1 × 84, 2 × 42, 3 × 28, 4 × 21, 6 × 14, 7 × 12) so that no factor is missed.

**3.** Testing primes up to √437 (between 20 and 21) [1]; **437 = 19 × 23**, so it has a factor other than 1 and itself [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A single correct factor pair is a complete answer. You do not need to list every failed test once you have found one.

**4.** A correct first step, such as 756 = 2 × 378 or 4 × 189 [1]; primes 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 7 [1]; **2² × 3³ × 7** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Index form" means the repeats must be written as powers. 2 × 2 × 3 × 3 × 3 × 7 would lose the final mark.

**5. (a)** Factors of 14: 1, 2, 7, 14 and of 35: 1, 5, 7, 35 [1]; **HCF = 7** [1]

**(b)** Multiples listed: 14, 28, 42, 56, 70 and 35, 70 [1]; **LCM = 70** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The question says "by listing", so show the lists. A correct answer from prime factors alone may not earn the method mark.

**6. (a)** **19 760** [1]

**(b)** **1 976 000** [1]

**(c)** **380** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) one factor is 100 times larger and the other 10 times larger, so the product is 1 000 times larger.

**7. (a)** Correct factorisation method, such as a factor tree [1]; 2 400 = 2⁵ × 3 × 5² [1]; **a = 5, b = 1, c = 2** [1]

**(b)** 4 800 = 2 × 2 400 = **2⁶ × 3 × 5²** [1]

**(c)** In a square number every power is even; here the powers of 2 and 3 are odd (5 and 1) [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "Hence" in part (b) rewards adding one more factor of 2, not starting a new tree.

**8. (a)** Lowest powers of shared primes: 2² × 3 × 5 [1]; **60** [1]

**(b)** Highest powers of all primes [1]; **2⁴ × 3² × 5² × 7** [1]

**(c)** Powers must become multiples of 3: 2⁶ × 3³ × 5³, so k = 2² × 3 × 5² [1]; **k = 300** [1] **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (c) check the result: 2⁶ × 3³ × 5³ = (2² × 3 × 5)³ = 60³.

**9. (a)** **126 = 2 × 3² × 7** [1]; **120 = 2³ × 3 × 5** [1]

**(b)** **HCF = 2 × 3 = 6** [1]

**(c)** **LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5 × 7 = 2 520** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* A Venn diagram with 2 and 3 in the overlap, 3 and 7 for 126 only, and 2, 2 and 5 for 120 only, shows both results at once.

**10. (a)** Finding the LCM of 18 and 30 [1]; LCM = 90 minutes [1]; **09:40** [1]

**(b)** 08:10, 09:40, 11:10, 12:40 (the next, 14:10, is too late) [1]; **4 times** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Add 90 minutes as 1 hour 30 minutes. Writing 08:10 + 90 = 09:100 is a common slip.

**11.** Recognising the side of the square is the LCM of 16 and 28 [1]; 16 = 2⁴, 28 = 2² × 7, so LCM = 2⁴ × 7 = 112 cm [1]; tiles along each side: 112 ÷ 16 = 7 and 112 ÷ 28 = 4 [1]; **28 tiles** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Stopping at 112 cm answers a different question. Read the last sentence again before you finish.

**12. (a)** HCF of 72 and 108 needed, with 72 = 2³ × 3² and 108 = 2² × 3³ [1]; **36 packs** [1]

**(b)** 72 ÷ 36 = **2 pencils** [1]; 108 ÷ 36 = **3 rulers** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "As many packs as possible" with "nothing left over" is the HCF signal.

**13.** Product of the numbers = 12 × 180 = 2 160 [1]; both numbers are multiples of 12, so they are 12m and 12n with mn = 15 and m, n sharing no factor; m = 3, n = 5 (m = 1 gives 12, which is not greater than 12) [1]; **36 and 60** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check the pair: HCF(36, 60) = 12 and LCM(36, 60) = 180. Checking is the quickest way to reject a wrong guess such as 24 and 90 (HCF 6).

**14.** 15 and 10 are not prime, so neither expression is a prime factorisation [1]; the only prime factorisation is **2² × 3 × 5** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Every whole number has exactly one prime factorisation. Two "different" versions always mean one contains a composite factor.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a composite number such as 9 or 15 in a prime factorisation.
- Writing repeated factors without powers when index form is asked for.
- Missing a factor pair, so a factor list is incomplete.
- Swapping the rules: lowest powers give the HCF, highest powers give the LCM.
- Giving the side length instead of the number of tiles, or the HCF instead of the contents of each pack.
- Adding minutes to a clock time as if there were 100 minutes in an hour.
- Not showing lists when the question says "by listing".
