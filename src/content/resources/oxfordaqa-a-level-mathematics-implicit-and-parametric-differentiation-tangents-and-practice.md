These questions follow the second part of content area P2.6 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660) specification, Version 5.2: implicit and parametric differentiation, with tangents and normals. The specification places this content in International A-level (A2) Unit P2, so it is not examined at International AS. Second derivatives are not required.

## Questions

**1.** Differentiate with respect to x:

**(a)** x³y² **[2]**
**(b)** cos 2y **[1]**

**2.** The curve C has equation x³ − xy² + 2y = 8 and passes through (2, 1).

**(a)** Find dy/dx in terms of x and y. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the tangent to C at (2, 1), in the form ax + by + c = 0 with integer coefficients. **[2]**

**3.** A curve has equation xeʸ + y = 1 and passes through (1, 0).

**(a)** Find the equations of the tangent and the normal at (1, 0). **[6]**
**(b)** Explain why no point on the curve has a horizontal tangent. **[2]**

**4.** The curve D has equation 2x² + y³ = 10 and passes through P(1, 2). The tangent to D at P meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. Find the area of triangle OAB, where O is the origin. **[5]**

**5.** A curve has parametric equations x = t² − 2t, y = t³ − 12t. Find the coordinates of the points where the tangent is horizontal. **[4]**

**6.** A curve has parametric equations x = t/(1 + t), y = t², for t > 0. Find the equations of the tangent and the normal at the point where t = 1. **[6]**

**7.** A curve has parametric equations x = sin t, y = t cos t, for 0 ≤ t < π/2.

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = 1 − t tan t. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the exact gradient of the curve at t = π/4. **[1]**

**8.** A curve has parametric equations x = t³, y = 3t, for t ≠ 0.

**(a)** Show that the tangent at the point where t = p has equation x − p²y + 2p³ = 0. **[4]**
**(b)** The tangent at one point passes through (8, 6). Find the coordinates of the two possible points of contact. **[3]**

**9.** The curve C has equation y² + 2xy − x² = 7 and passes through A(1, 2).

**(a)** Show that dy/dx = (x − y)/(x + y). **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the normal to C at A. **[3]**
**(c)** The normal meets C again at B. Find the coordinates of B. **[3]**

**10.** A curve has parametric equations x = 6 cos θ, y = 2 sin θ, for 0 < θ < π/2.

**(a)** Show that the tangent at the point where θ = φ has equation x cos φ + 3y sin φ = 6. **[5]**
**(b)** This tangent meets the x-axis at A and the y-axis at B. Show that the area of triangle OAB is 12/sin 2φ. **[3]**
**(c)** Deduce the minimum area of triangle OAB and the value of φ that gives it. **[2]**

**11.** A curve has equation eˣy + y² = 5.

**(a)** Show that at the point where x = 0 and y > 0, y = (√21 − 1)/2. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the exact gradient at this point, then give it to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Product rule: 3x²y² [1] + x³ × 2y dy/dx. **Answer: 3x²y² + 2x³y dy/dx** [1]
**(b)** **−2 sin 2y × dy/dx** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* write the product rule as two separate terms. The usual way to lose credit is to differentiate y² and forget its dy/dx.

**2. (a)** 3x² − y² − 2xy dy/dx + 2 dy/dx = 0 [2] (the product rule is needed for xy²). So dy/dx (2 − 2xy) = y² − 3x², giving **dy/dx = (3x² − y²)/(2xy − 2)** [1]
**(b)** At (2, 1): (12 − 1)/(4 − 2) = 11/2 [1]. Tangent: y − 1 = (11/2)(x − 2), so **11x − 2y − 20 = 0** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the question names the form of the answer, so finish in that form. A correct line left as y = (11/2)x − 10 may not match what was asked.

**3. (a)** Differentiate: eʸ + xeʸ dy/dx + dy/dx = 0 [2]. So dy/dx = −eʸ/(xeʸ + 1) [1]. At (1, 0): −1/(1 + 1) = −1/2 [1]. Tangent: y = −(1/2)(x − 1), so **x + 2y = 1** [1]. Normal gradient 2: **y = 2x − 2** [1]
**(b)** dy/dx is a fraction with numerator −eʸ [1]. Since eʸ > 0 it is never zero, so dy/dx is never zero, **no horizontal tangent** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* an explanation needs the reason, not only the conclusion. Saying "eʸ is always positive" is the key step to state.

**4.** Differentiate: 4x + 3y² dy/dx = 0 [1]. So dy/dx = −4x/(3y²), which is −4/12 = −1/3 at P [1]. Tangent: y − 2 = −(1/3)(x − 1), so x + 3y = 7 [1]. Put y = 0: A = (7, 0). Put x = 0: B = (0, 7/3) [1]. Area = ½ × 7 × 7/3 = **49/6** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* find the axis intercepts from the simplified tangent equation. Working from y − 2 = −(1/3)(x − 1) invites sign slips.

**5.** dy/dt = 3t² − 12 [1]. Horizontal tangent where dy/dt = 0, so t = ±2 [1]. dx/dt = 2t − 2 is 2 at t = 2 and −6 at t = −2, so it is non-zero at both [1]. t = 2 gives (0, −16) and t = −2 gives (8, 16). **Points (0, −16) and (8, 16)** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* checking that dx/dt is non-zero is part of a complete answer. Quote the check, since it justifies calling the tangent horizontal.

**6.** Quotient rule: dx/dt = ((1 + t) − t)/(1 + t)² = 1/(1 + t)² [1]. dy/dt = 2t, so dy/dx = 2t(1 + t)² [1]. At t = 1: gradient 8 [1]; point (1/2, 1) [1]. Tangent: y − 1 = 8(x − 1/2), so **y = 8x − 3** [1]. Normal: y − 1 = −(1/8)(x − 1/2), so **2x + 16y = 17** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* find the point from the same value of t as the gradient. Mixing a gradient at t = 1 with a point from another value is a common slip.

**7. (a)** dx/dt = cos t [1]. dy/dt = cos t − t sin t, by the product rule [1]. dy/dx = (cos t − t sin t)/cos t = **1 − t tan t** [1]
**(b)** At t = π/4, tan t = 1, so gradient = **1 − π/4** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, write each step down, including the division by cos t. Leave the answer exact when the question asks for exact.

**8. (a)** dx/dt = 3t², dy/dt = 3, so dy/dx = 1/t² and the gradient at t = p is 1/p² [2]. Tangent: y − 3p = (1/p²)(x − p³) [1]. Multiply by p²: p²y − 3p³ = x − p³, so **x − p²y + 2p³ = 0** [1]
**(b)** Substitute (8, 6): 8 − 6p² + 2p³ = 0 [1]. This factorises as 2(p − 2)²(p + 1) = 0, so p = 2 or p = −1 [1]. The points are **(8, 6) and (−1, −3)** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* spot that p = 2 is a root because (8, 6) is itself on the curve. Using that as a trial root makes the cubic factorise quickly, and you can check the points by substitution.

**9. (a)** 2y dy/dx + 2y + 2x dy/dx − 2x = 0 [2]. Then dy/dx (y + x) = x − y, so **dy/dx = (x − y)/(x + y)** [1]
**(b)** At A: (1 − 2)/(1 + 2) = −1/3 [1]. Normal gradient 3 [1]. Normal: y − 2 = 3(x − 1), so **y = 3x − 1** [1]
**(c)** Substitute: (3x − 1)² + 2x(3x − 1) − x² = 7 gives 14x² − 8x + 1 = 7 [1]. So 7x² − 4x − 3 = 0, which is (7x + 3)(x − 1) = 0 [1]. x = 1 is A, so x = −3/7 and y = −16/7. **B = (−3/7, −16/7)** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* a quadratic for the second intersection always has the known point as a root. Use it as a check on your factorisation.

**10. (a)** dx/dθ = −6 sin θ, dy/dθ = 2 cos θ [1]. dy/dx = −cos θ/(3 sin θ) [1]. At θ = φ: y − 2 sin φ = −(cos φ/(3 sin φ))(x − 6 cos φ) [1]. Multiply by 3 sin φ: 3y sin φ − 6 sin²φ = −x cos φ + 6 cos²φ [1]. So x cos φ + 3y sin φ = 6(sin²φ + cos²φ) = **6** [1]
**(b)** y = 0 gives x = 6/cos φ; x = 0 gives y = 2/sin φ [1]. Area = ½ × (6/cos φ) × (2/sin φ) = 6/(sin φ cos φ) [1]. Since sin 2φ = 2 sin φ cos φ, area = **12/sin 2φ** [1]
**(c)** sin 2φ ≤ 1, with equality when φ = π/4 [1]. **Minimum area 12 at φ = π/4** [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* in part (a) the identity sin²φ + cos²φ = 1 is the step that reaches the printed result, so say you are using it.

**11. (a)** At x = 0: y + y² = 5, so y² + y − 5 = 0 [1]. The positive root is **y = (√21 − 1)/2** [1]
**(b)** Differentiate: eˣy + eˣ dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0 [1]. At x = 0: y + (1 + 2y) dy/dx = 0, and 1 + 2y = √21 [1]. So dy/dx = −y/√21 = **(√21 − 21)/42 ≈ −0.391** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* give the exact form first, then the rounded value if the question asks for both. Rounding too early, for example using y ≈ 1.79, can change the third significant figure.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting dy/dx on a y-term, or putting it on an x-term.
- Differentiating xy, x²y or xy² without the product rule.
- Rearranging for dy/dx with a sign error when moving terms across.
- Dividing dx/dt by dy/dt instead of dy/dt by dx/dt.
- Using the gradient from one value of the parameter and the point from another.
- Using the tangent gradient in the normal equation, or inverting without changing sign.
- Ending a "show that" question without the last step shown.
- Not giving the answer in the form asked.
- Ignoring the condition that dx/dt must be non-zero for a horizontal tangent.
