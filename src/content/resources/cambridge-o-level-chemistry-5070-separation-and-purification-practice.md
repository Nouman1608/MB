## Section A

**1.** Name the method used to separate each mixture.

**(a)** chalk powder from water **[1]**

**(b)** water from copper(II) sulfate solution **[1]**

**(c)** propanol from water (both liquids mix completely) **[1]**

**2.** Which statement about a pure substance is correct?

A: It melts over a range of temperatures.

B: It has a sharp melting point.

C: Its melting point is always 0 °C.

D: It boils over a range of temperatures.

**[1]**

**3.** State why a mixture of salt solution cannot be separated into salt and water by filtration. **[1]**

**4.** In simple distillation, state where the bulb of the thermometer should be placed. **[1]**

**5.** A sample of a compound melts at 74–79 °C. The pure compound melts at 82 °C. State **two** pieces of evidence that the sample is impure. **[2]**

**6.** State the property of the substances that fractional distillation uses to separate them. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student makes crystals of hydrated zinc sulfate from its solution.

**(a)** Describe how the student should obtain pure, dry crystals from the solution. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the student does not evaporate the solution to dryness. **[2]**

**8.** Use the data, which are invented for this question, to answer the parts below.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C | Soluble in water? | Soluble in hexane? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J | 186 | decomposes | yes | no |
| K | 80 | 218 | no | yes |
| L | 1700 | 2200 | no | no |

**(a)** Suggest how to obtain pure K from a mixture of K and L. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest how to obtain J from a mixture of J and L. **[3]**

**(c)** State the physical state of K at 100 °C. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**9.** A mixture of methanol (boiling point 65 °C) and water is separated by fractional distillation.

**(a)** State which liquid is collected first and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the thermometer reading changes during the distillation. **[2]**

**(c)** State the purpose of the fractionating column. **[1]**

**10.** A student sets up simple distillation to obtain water from ink. The cooling water enters the condenser at the top and leaves at the bottom.

**(a)** Explain why this arrangement is less effective. **[1]**

**(b)** State what is left in the flask at the end. **[1]**

**11.** Three samples of a white solid, believed to be compound M (melting point 157 °C, value invented), are tested.

| Sample | Observation |
|---|---|
| 1 | melts sharply at 157 °C |
| 2 | melts between 148 °C and 153 °C |
| 3 | melts sharply at 121 °C |

**(a)** Identify the sample that is pure M. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what the results show about sample 2. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest what the result for sample 3 shows. **[1]**

**12.** Sand, salt and water are mixed. Describe how to obtain dry sand and pure salt crystals from the mixture. **[4]**

**1. (a)** Filtration [1]

**(b)** Simple distillation [1]

**(c)** Fractional distillation [1]

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The salt is dissolved, so its particles pass through the filter paper with the water [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Level with the side arm (where vapour leaves the flask) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** It melts over a range of temperatures [1]; the melting point is lower than that of the pure compound [1]. **[2]**

- heat the solution to evaporate some of the water [1]
- until it is nearly saturated / crystals form on a cooled glass rod dipped in it [1]
- leave to cool so crystals form [1]
- filter to collect the crystals [1]
- wash with a little cold distilled water and dry between filter papers (max 4)

**(b)** The crystals would lose their water of crystallisation [1]; soluble impurities would be left in the solid instead of staying in solution [1].

**8. (a)** Add hexane and stir; K dissolves, L does not [1]. Filter off L [1]. Evaporate the hexane from the filtrate to leave K [1].

**(b)** Add water and stir; J dissolves, L does not [1]. Filter to remove L [1]. Crystallise J from the filtrate [1].

**(c)** Liquid [1]. 100 °C is above its melting point (80 °C) but below its boiling point (218 °C) [1].

**(c)** To provide a surface on which vapour repeatedly condenses and re-evaporates, so the liquids separate [1].

**12.** Add water and stir to dissolve all the salt [1]. Filter: the sand is the residue [1]; wash the sand with distilled water and dry it [1]. Crystallise the salt from the filtrate (evaporate some water, cool, filter off crystals and dry) [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using "evaporate" when the question asks for pure crystals.
- Forgetting to dissolve one solid before filtering a mixture of two solids.
- Putting the thermometer bulb in the boiling liquid.
- Claiming impurities raise the melting point.
- Choosing simple distillation for two miscible liquids.
- Answering a data question without quoting the data.
