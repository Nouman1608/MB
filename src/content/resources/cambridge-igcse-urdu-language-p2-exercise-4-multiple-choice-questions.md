This guide covers **Exercise 4 of Paper 2 Listening**, the multiple-choice questions on a discussion,
in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539). The site's
[Paper 2 Listening guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening/) gives the outline of all
four exercises. This page looks in detail at the last and most demanding one: what the questions target,
how to follow two speakers at once, the kinds of wrong option you will meet, and the Urdu that signals
agreement, disagreement and feeling.

## What the syllabus says

- Candidates hear **a discussion between two speakers**.
- They answer **multiple-choice comprehension questions**, for **8 marks**.
- It is the only exercise on the paper that assesses **all four listening objectives, L1 to L4**: select
  information; understand ideas, opinions and attitudes; understand the connections between them; and
  understand what is implied but not directly stated, such as gist, the speaker's purpose, intention and
  feelings.
- The discussion is heard **twice**, with pauses to read and answer. Questions and options are in Urdu.

The syllabus does not say how many questions there are or how many options each has, so practise with
different numbers and check the specimen paper and recent papers for your series.

## What the questions target

Because the exercise covers L1 to L4, its questions come in recognisable kinds. The question stem usually
tells you which kind you are facing:

| Objective | Typical stem in Urdu | What you need |
|---|---|---|
| L1 detail | سارہ کس دن ... ؟ / ... کہاں ہوگا؟ | one stated fact |
| L2 opinion | ... کے بارے میں حمزہ کی کیا رائے ہے؟ | one speaker's view |
| L2 objection | ... کو کس بات پر اعتراض ہے؟ | the reason for a view |
| L3 connection | دونوں کس بات پر متفق ہوتے ہیں؟ / آخر میں کیا طے پاتا ہے؟ | how the two views relate or end up |
| L4 meaning | جب وہ کہتی ہے "..." تو اس کا کیا مطلب ہے؟ | what a remark implies |
| L4 feeling | گفتگو کے آخر میں ... کیسا محسوس کرتا ہے؟ | an emotion not named directly |
| L4 gist or purpose | اس گفتگو کا مقصد کیا ہے؟ | the point of the whole exchange |

The questions normally follow the order of the discussion, with any question on the overall gist or
purpose at the end.

## Following two speakers

The commonest error in Exercise 4 is not mishearing but **mis-assigning**: knowing that something was
said, but not who said it. Before the recording, find both names in the questions and write their
initials in the margin. On the first hearing, put a quick note beside each initial when that speaker
makes a suggestion, objects or changes their mind.

Pay special attention to **turn-openers**, the first words of a reply, which tell you how the second
speaker is reacting:

| Reaction | Urdu turn-openers |
|---|---|
| full agreement | بالکل ٹھیک، میں آپ سے متفق ہوں، چلو یہی ٹھیک ہے |
| partial agreement | یہ خیال برا نہیں، مگر ... / آپ کی بات اپنی جگہ درست ہے، لیکن ... |
| disagreement | میں ایسا نہیں سمجھتا، یہ ضروری تو نہیں |
| hesitation | ہمم ... / پتا نہیں ... / دیکھو ... |
| surprise | ارے! / واقعی؟ |
| admiration | واہ! / بہت خوب |
| annoyance | اُف! / پھر وہی بات |

A speaker who begins "یہ خیال برا نہیں، مگر" is not agreeing. The option that says they "agree with the
idea" is a trap; the one about their reservation is likely correct.

## Feelings and attitudes

L4 questions often ask how someone feels, using a word the speaker never says. Learn the common feeling
words that appear in options, and the kind of evidence that points to each:

| Option word | Meaning | Evidence you might hear |
|---|---|---|
| پُرجوش | excited | اب تو میں واقعی بے تاب ہوں |
| مایوس | disappointed | میں نے اتنی امید لگائی تھی ... |
| پریشان | worried | اگر وقت پر نہ ہوا تو ...؟ |
| حیران | surprised | ارے، مجھے تو پتا ہی نہیں تھا! |
| متاثر | impressed | واہ، تم نے تو سب سوچ رکھا ہے! |
| مطمئن | satisfied, reassured | چلو، اب کوئی فکر نہیں |
| ناراض | annoyed, angry | تم نے مجھ سے پوچھا تک نہیں! |

Tone matters as much as words. واہ said warmly is admiration; said flatly after bad news it may be
sarcasm. When reading practice scripts aloud, the reader should act the tone, because that is part of
what the real recording conveys.

## The four kinds of wrong option

Multiple-choice options are written so that wrong ones sound plausible. Most fall into four types:

1. **Right words, wrong speaker.** The option describes what the *other* person said or wanted.
2. **Suggested, then rejected.** An idea is raised and dropped. If the question asks what was *decided*,
   the dropped idea is wrong.
3. **True, but not the answer.** The option states something said in the discussion, but it answers a
   different question from the one asked.
4. **The reported view.** One speaker states what the other thinks or what "people say", and the other
   corrects it.

When you choose an option, ask which of these types each of the others belongs to. If you can explain why
every other option is wrong, your choice is safe.

## A routine for the two hearings

```
In the pause:   read every stem; underline the names and the question word;
                if time, skim the options; write both speakers' initials
First hearing:  follow the order of the questions; mark a provisional choice;
                note who suggested, objected, agreed or changed their mind
Second hearing: confirm each choice; listen hardest for the turn after a
                suggestion, and for the last lines, where views often settle
After:          check every question has one answer; do not change a choice
                without a clear reason from the recording
```

## Worked example

The extract is original, written for this guide. Read it aloud with a partner, twice.

> **نادیہ:** سنا ہے تم نے گٹار سیکھنا چھوڑ دیا؟\
> **بلال:** چھوڑا نہیں، بس کچھ دن کا وقفہ لیا ہے۔ استاد صاحب دوسرے شہر چلے گئے ہیں۔\
> **نادیہ:** آن لائن کلاسیں کیوں نہیں لیتے؟\
> **بلال:** کوشش کی تھی، مگر انٹرنیٹ اتنا سست ہے کہ آدھی بات سمجھ ہی نہیں آتی تھی۔

**(a)** بلال نے گٹار کی کلاسیں کیوں روکی ہیں؟

- A. وہ گٹار سیکھنا نہیں چاہتا۔
- B. اس کے استاد دوسرے شہر چلے گئے ہیں۔
- C. اس کے پاس وقت نہیں۔

**Answer: B.** A is the **reported view**: Nadia has heard he gave up, and Bilal corrects her (چھوڑا
نہیں). C is never mentioned. This is an L1 and L2 question: a stated reason, and Bilal's attitude that he
has only paused.

**(b)** آن لائن کلاسوں کے بارے میں بلال کی کیا رائے ہے؟

- A. اس نے کبھی آن لائن کلاس لینے کی کوشش نہیں کی۔
- B. وہ آن لائن کلاسیں پسند کرتا ہے۔
- C. سست انٹرنیٹ کی وجہ سے آن لائن کلاسیں کارآمد نہیں رہیں۔

**Answer: C.** "کوشش کی تھی" rules out A. He never says the classes are useless, but "آدھی بات سمجھ ہی
نہیں آتی تھی" implies it, which is L4. B contradicts the مگر that follows his attempt.

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up the speakers**, so an option describing the other person's view is chosen.
- **Choosing a suggestion that was later dropped** when the question asks what was agreed.
- **Picking a true statement that does not answer the question.**
- **Missing the feeling** because it is shown through tone or an exclamation rather than named.
- **Ignoring the turn-opener**: "یہ خیال برا نہیں، مگر" is a reservation, not agreement.
- **Changing a good answer on the second hearing** because of a half-remembered word.
- **Leaving a question unanswered.** There is always one best option; eliminate and choose.

## Exam technique summary

Read the stems and mark both speakers' initials before the recording. Follow the questions in order and
note who suggested, objected and agreed. Listen hardest to the reply after each suggestion and to the
last lines, where decisions and feelings settle. Eliminate wrong options by type. For the whole paper, see
the [Paper 2 Listening revision notes](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening-revision-notes/)
and the full [Paper 2 listening practice set](/resources/urdu-paper-2-listening-practice/).
