Condensed recall for Cambridge International A Level Sociology (9699) sub-topic 12.1, *The
secularisation debate*, in Paper 4 Section C. The full explanation, with an annotated essay plan,
is in the [secularisation debate study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-secularisation-debate/).

## The four syllabus points

1. The secularisation thesis.
2. Evidence for a decline in religious belief.
3. Whether rationalisation is leading to a decline in religious belief.
4. Whether religion has lost its social significance.

Each Paper 4 essay is worth 35 marks and must weigh contrasting views or evidence.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Secularisation** | Process by which religious thinking, practice and institutions lose social significance (Wilson, 1966) |
| **Laicisation** | Societal-level secularisation: institutions run free of religious authority (Dobbelaere) |
| **Structural differentiation** | Specialised institutions take over functions religion once performed (Parsons) |
| **Disenchantment** | Weber's term for explaining the world by rational and natural causes rather than magic or the supernatural |
| **Rationalisation** | Spread of calculable, efficient, evidence-based thinking and organisation |
| **Religious pluralism** | Many faiths coexisting, so none holds a monopoly on truth (Berger) |
| **Generational decline** | Each generation is less religious than its parents (Bruce) |
| **Secularisation from within** | Churches stay busy but absorb secular values (Herberg on the USA) |
| **Believing without belonging** | Private belief persists while attendance falls (Davie) |
| **Vicarious religion** | An active minority practises on behalf of a wider, approving majority (Davie) |
| **Golden age** | The assumption, open to challenge, that the past was highly religious |
| **Exclusive / inclusive definition** | Religion defined by belief in the supernatural / by the functions it performs |

## 1. The thesis in one chain

Industrialisation and urbanisation → structural differentiation and loss of community → religious
and cultural diversity → rationalisation and technology → religion becomes optional, private and
less influential.

**Three levels (Dobbelaere, 1981):** society (laicisation), organisations (religious change),
individuals (religious involvement). The levels can move at different speeds, so always say
which one you mean.

## 2. Evidence of decline: what and why it is contested

- **Attendance and membership:** long-term falls in much of Western Europe. *Counter:* practice is
  not belief; churches count members differently.
- **Belief surveys:** fewer affirm a personal God. *Counter:* wording effects ("God" versus "a
  higher power").
- **Census identity:** rising "no religion". *Counter:* identity can be cultural, not devotional.
- **Rites of passage:** fewer religious weddings and baptisms. *Counter:* civil options became
  more available.
- **Golden-age critique:** the 1851 Census of Religious Worship (England and Wales) showed that
  many people did not attend even then.
- **Voas and Crockett (2005):** belief has fallen alongside belonging, "neither believing nor
  belonging", which challenges Davie.

## 3. Rationalisation

| For decline | Against decline |
|---|---|
| Weber: disenchantment; natural causes replace the supernatural | Belief persists among people who use science daily |
| Protestantism removed magic and sacraments, preparing a secular worldview (Berger, Bruce) | New spiritualities suggest change of form, not loss (12.3) |
| Technology gives practical control, so prayer is needed less (Bruce) | Explains institutional decline better than private belief |
| Pluralism creates a "crisis of credibility" (Berger, 1967) | Built on Western European history; may not transfer elsewhere |

## 4. Social significance

- **Lost:** Wilson's societal-level argument; laicisation of education, welfare and law;
  Herberg's secularisation from within.
- **Retained:** religion in politics and identity (Casanova's deprivatisation; section 11.3 case
  studies); faith schools and charities; national rituals; vicarious religion.
- **Middle position:** David Martin (1978): no single universal path; patterns depend on each
  country's church–state history.

## Exam traps

- Writing "religion is dying" with no level, place or measure stated.
- Quoting unsourced percentages. Name the type of data and its weakness instead.
- Confusing Weber's 11.3 argument about the origins of capitalism with the 12.1 argument about
  rationalisation and belief. Use the 11.3 material only to explain why rationality spread.
- Treating Davie as proof that secularisation is false; she describes a change in the form of
  religion in Europe, and her claims have been challenged.
- Leaving the conclusion unqualified. Strong conclusions specify where and in what sense the
  thesis holds.

## Linking to other sub-topics

- **11.1** gives definitions and measurement problems: use them to evaluate 12.1 evidence.
- **11.3** shows religion's political influence: use it against loss of social significance.
- **12.3** offers privatisation, spiritual shopping and resacralisation as alternative readings of
  the same trends.

## Self-test

1. State Wilson's definition of secularisation.
2. Name Dobbelaere's three levels of secularisation.
3. What did Parsons mean by structural differentiation?
4. Why does Berger link religious pluralism to a "crisis of credibility"?
5. Give one weakness of attendance figures as evidence of declining belief.
6. Explain the golden-age problem.
7. What did Voas and Crockett conclude in 2005?
8. Define vicarious religion.
9. Explain how Protestantism might have contributed to its own decline.
10. What is secularisation from within, and who argued it about the USA?
11. Give one argument that religion keeps social significance.
12. What is David Martin's contribution to the debate?

### Answers

1. The process whereby religious thinking, practice and institutions lose social significance.
2. Laicisation (societal), religious change (organisational) and religious involvement
   (individual).
3. As societies modernise, specialised institutions take over functions such as education and
   welfare, leaving religion with a narrower role.
4. When many faiths compete, none can claim unchallenged truth, so belief becomes less certain.
5. People may believe without attending, or attend for social reasons, so practice is not a valid
   measure of belief.
6. Measuring decline assumes a highly religious past; if past religiosity is overstated, the
   decline is exaggerated.
7. British survey data showed belief falling alongside attendance: "neither believing nor
   belonging".
8. A small active minority practises religion on behalf of a larger number who approve of it and
   use it at key moments.
9. By stripping magic and sacraments from faith and encouraging rational conduct, it prepared the
   way for explaining the world without religion.
10. Churches remain active but absorb secular values; Will Herberg (1955).
11. Religion shapes politics and identity in many societies (Casanova's deprivatisation; the
    Iranian revolution or the Evangelical movement in US politics from section 11.3).
12. Secularisation is not one universal process; its pattern depends on each country's history
    and church–state relations.
