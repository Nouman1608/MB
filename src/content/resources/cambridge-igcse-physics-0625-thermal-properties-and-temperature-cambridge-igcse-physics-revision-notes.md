Supplement content, marked **Extended only**, is examined on Papers 2 and 4. Explanations, the liquid experiment and worked examples are in the [study guide on thermal properties and temperature](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-thermal-properties-and-temperature-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Thermal physics revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-thermal-physics-revision-notes/) cover the whole of Topic 2.

## Thermal expansion (2.2.1)

- Heating at constant pressure → expansion; cooling → contraction.
- Mass unchanged, volume up → **density down**.
- Order for the same temperature rise: **gas > liquid > solid**.

*(Extended only)* **Why that order**

| State | Forces between particles | Effect of heating | Expansion |
|---|---|---|---|
| solid | strong; fixed positions | larger vibrations, slightly further apart | smallest |
| liquid | weaker; particles close but mobile | particles move apart more easily | medium |
| gas | very weak; particles far apart | faster particles push the boundary outwards | largest |

**Applications and consequences:** liquid-in-glass thermometers; gaps in railway lines; expansion joints in bridges; slack overhead cables; bimetallic strips in thermostats; loosening metal lids with hot water; thick glass cracking when hot liquid is poured in.

## Specific heat capacity (2.2.2)

- **Core:** temperature rise → internal energy increases.
- *(Extended only)* Temperature rise = increase in the **average kinetic energy of all the particles**.
- *(Extended only)* **Specific heat capacity:** energy required per unit mass per unit temperature increase.

| Equation (Extended only) | Units |
|---|---|
| c = ΔE / (m Δθ) | J/(kg °C) |
| ΔE = m c Δθ | J |
| Δθ = ΔE / (m c) | °C |

- Water: c ≈ 4200 J/(kg °C), high, so it warms and cools slowly.
- Metals: much lower c, so they heat up quickly.

*(Extended only)* **Experiment checklist**

- Mass by balance (subtract the container for a liquid).
- Energy by joulemeter, or E = IVt (ammeter, voltmeter, stopwatch).
- Starting temperature, then the **highest** temperature after switching off.
- Insulate; use a lid; stir liquids; oil in the thermometer hole of a solid block.
- Measured c is usually **too high**, because energy is lost to the surroundings and the container.

## Melting, boiling and evaporation (2.2.3)

- Melting and boiling: energy supplied, **temperature constant** until the change is complete.
- Water at standard atmospheric pressure: melts at **0 °C**, boils at **100 °C**.
- Condensation: gas particles slow and come together to form a liquid. Solidification: liquid particles settle into fixed positions.
- **Evaporation:** more-energetic particles escape from the **surface** of a liquid.
- **Evaporation causes cooling:** the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles falls.

*(Extended only)* **Boiling vs evaporation**

| | Evaporation | Boiling |
|---|---|---|
| where | surface only | throughout, with bubbles |
| temperature | any | boiling point only |
| energy | from the liquid (it cools) | supplied by heating |

*(Extended only)* **Faster evaporation:** higher temperature; larger surface area; more air movement.

*(Extended only)* **Cooling by contact:** the evaporating liquid cools, then energy flows from the warmer object into the liquid, so the object cools (sweating, wet cloth on a bottle).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Thermal expansion | Increase in size of a substance when its temperature rises |
| Internal energy | Total energy of the particles of an object |
| Specific heat capacity (Extended) | Energy required per unit mass per unit temperature increase |
| Melting point | Temperature at which a solid melts (0 °C for water) |
| Boiling point | Temperature at which a liquid boils (100 °C for water) |
| Evaporation | Escape of more-energetic particles from the surface of a liquid |
| Bimetallic strip | Two metals bonded together that bend when heated because they expand by different amounts |

## Exam traps

- Particles do not expand; the gaps between them grow.
- Δθ is a temperature **change**; subtract first.
- Masses in kg in ΔE = mcΔθ (250 g = 0.250 kg).
- The temperature does not rise during melting or boiling.
- Evaporation happens at any temperature, not only at the boiling point.
- "Evaporation cools because the liquid loses heat": say the most energetic particles escape, so the average kinetic energy of those left falls.
- *(Extended)* A measured c above the true value is due to energy losses, not "human error".

## Quick self-test

1. Which expands most for the same temperature rise: a solid, a liquid or a gas?
2. Why are gaps left between sections of railway track?
3. Explain how a liquid-in-glass thermometer works.
4. State what happens to the internal energy of an object when its temperature rises.
5. State the melting and boiling points of water at standard atmospheric pressure.
6. What happens to the temperature of water while it is boiling?
7. Explain why evaporation cools a liquid.
8. *(Extended)* Define specific heat capacity.
9. *(Extended)* Find the energy needed to raise the temperature of 2.0 kg of water by 15 °C (c = 4200 J/(kg °C)).
10. *(Extended)* 9000 J raises the temperature of 0.50 kg of a metal by 40 °C. Find c.
11. *(Extended)* Give **two** differences between boiling and evaporation.
12. *(Extended)* Give **three** ways to make a puddle evaporate faster.

### Answers

1. **A gas.**
2. The rails **expand** on hot days; the gaps stop them buckling.
3. The liquid **expands more than the glass** when heated, so it rises up the narrow tube; the length of liquid shows the temperature.
4. It **increases**.
5. **0 °C** and **100 °C**.
6. It **stays constant** (100 °C) while energy is supplied.
7. The **more-energetic particles escape**, so the **average kinetic energy** of the remaining particles falls.
8. The **energy required per unit mass per unit temperature increase**.
9. 2.0 × 4200 × 15 = **126 000 J**.
10. 9000 ÷ (0.50 × 40) = **450 J/(kg °C)**.
11. Any two: evaporation at the surface, boiling throughout; evaporation at any temperature, boiling only at the boiling point; evaporation cools the liquid, boiling needs energy supplied.
12. **Warm** it, **spread it out** (larger surface area), and **blow air** over it.
