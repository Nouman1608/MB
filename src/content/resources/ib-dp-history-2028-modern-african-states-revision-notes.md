**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 12 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): modern developments in Ethiopia, Niger, Somalia, Tunisia, Zambia and Zimbabwe, circa 1945 to 2020. Every Paper 3 question on this study requires reference to **one prescribed country**, so learn two or three in depth against all six inquiry topics. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-modern-african-states/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Emergence of authoritarian rule | Colonial legacies, nationalist movements, leaders' ambition, ideology |
| Move to multi-party democracy | Cold War ends, aid conditions, economic failure, unions and churches |
| Growth and development | Stability, leadership, investment, mobile technology |
| Social challenges | Disease, famine, illiteracy, youth population, gender and minorities |
| Coups and civil wars | Ethnicity, inequality, drought, weak civilian governments |
| External involvement | Cold War, neocolonialism, African Union, United Nations |

## Country timelines

| Country | Key dates |
|---|---|
| Ethiopia | 1974 revolution; Mengistu and Red Terror 1977; Ogaden War 1977 to 1978; famine 1983 to 1985; EPRDF victory 1991; constitution 1995; rapid growth 2004 to 2018; Grand Dam begun 2011 |
| Niger | Independence 1960; Diori overthrown 1974; Tuareg rebellions from the 1990s; coups 1996, 1999, 2010; national conference 1991 |
| Somalia | Independence 1960; Barre coup 1969; Ogaden War; state collapse 1991; UN and US intervention 1992 to 1995; AMISOM from 2007 |
| Tunisia | Independence 1956; Bourguiba president for life 1975; Ben Ali 1987; revolution January 2011 |
| Zambia | Independence 1964; one-party state 1972; MMD wins 1991 election; copper boom of the 2000s |
| Zimbabwe | Independence 1980; Gukurahundi 1983 to 1987; land seizures from 2000; hyperinflation 2008; coup November 2017 |

## Why authoritarian rule?

- **Colonial legacy:** extractive states, arbitrary borders, little training for self-government.
- **Independence movements:** claim to speak for the nation; opposition seen as disloyal.
- **Individuals:** Bourguiba, Kaunda, Mugabe, Barre, Mengistu.
- **Ideology:** socialism, Humanism, Marxism-Leninism, unity.

**Counter-argument:** leaders and elites chose repression, and outside powers backed them.

## Why multi-party reform?

| Factor | Examples |
|---|---|
| Foreign influence | End of the Cold War; IMF and World Bank conditions; Mitterrand's 1990 speech |
| One-party failure | Zambia's copper collapse; Ethiopia's famine and war |
| Ideas | Collapse of Marxism-Leninism; liberal democracy |
| Popular and elite support | Zambian unions and churches; Niger's national conference; Ethiopian armed opposition |

**Limits:** Ethiopia's ruling coalition and Tunisia's Ben Ali kept control; Somalia collapsed; Zimbabwe's ruling party remained dominant.

## Growth and development

- Stability and reform: Tunisia, Ethiopia (state-led model).
- Individuals: Bourguiba's education and women's rights; Meles Zenawi's planning; Somali diaspora and traders.
- Infrastructure: Addis Ababa to Djibouti railway; Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam; TAZARA.
- Technology: mobile phones and mobile money.
- Warning: Zimbabwe's early gains reversed; Niger's uranium wealth had limited effect on the poor.

## Social challenges

| Challenge | Examples |
|---|---|
| Health | HIV/AIDS in Zambia and Zimbabwe |
| Famine | Ethiopia 1983 to 1985; Niger 2005 and 2010; Somalia 1992 and 2011 |
| Education | Low literacy in Niger; collapse in Somalia; high investment in Tunisia |
| Demography | Niger's rapid population growth |
| Gender | Tunisia's 1956 Personal Status Code; early marriage in Niger |
| Marginalised groups | Tuareg, Ndebele, Oromo, minority clans in Somalia |

## Coups and civil wars

- **Ethnic tension:** Somali clans; Eritrean war in Ethiopia; Tuareg in Niger; Matabeleland in Zimbabwe.
- **Economic problems:** inequality and inflation, famine and the 1974 Ethiopian revolution.
- **Environmental:** drought in the Sahel and the Horn.
- **Civilian failure:** Diori, Somalia's 1960s government; Zimbabwe 2017.

## External involvement

- **Cold War:** the Ogaden War, with the USSR and Cuba backing Ethiopia after Somalia lost Soviet support; Zambia attacked for hosting liberation movements.
- **Neocolonialism:** French uranium and CFA franc in Niger; copper companies and debt in Zambia.
- **AU:** AMISOM; suspensions after coups.
- **UN:** Somalia 1992 to 1995; sanctions on Rhodesia.

## Arguments to practise

1. "Multi-party democracy was imposed from outside." For: aid conditions. Against: domestic movements in Zambia and Niger.
2. "Coups are caused by poverty." For: Niger. Against: political ambition, army interests.
3. "Foreign involvement made conflicts worse." For: Ogaden War and the Cold War. Against: AMISOM prevented worse outcomes.
4. "Development needs democracy." For: Zambia after 1991. Against: Ethiopia's growth under a dominant party.

## Worked example

*Question:* "Evaluate the view that external involvement was the main cause of conflict in one prescribed country." Use Somalia.

- For: Cold War arming; the Ogaden War; foreign intervention in 1992 and 2006; funding for militias.
- Against: clan divisions; Barre's repression; the collapse of the state in 1991; local warlords.
- Perspective: some scholars stress outside powers, others internal politics.
- Judgement: external involvement fed and prolonged conflict, but its roots were domestic.

## Common mistakes

- Giving a list of countries without one example in depth.
- Treating "Africa" as having a single pattern of democratisation.
- Forgetting to evaluate the AU and UN.
- Assuming all authoritarian states ended in 1990.

## Self-test

1. Who led Ethiopia's Derg after 1977?
2. Which party won Zambia's 1991 election?
3. What was Tunisia's 1956 Personal Status Code?
4. Which war in 1977 to 1978 involved Ethiopia and Somalia?
5. In what year did the African Union replace the OAU?
6. Who left office in Zimbabwe in November 2017?

**Answers:** 1. Mengistu Haile Mariam. 2. The Movement for Multiparty Democracy (MMD). 3. A law giving women rights such as a ban on polygamy. 4. The Ogaden War. 5. 2002. 6. Robert Mugabe.

## Key terms

- **Derg:** the military committee that ruled Ethiopia from 1974 to 1987.
- **Gukurahundi:** the violence in Matabeleland in the 1980s.
- **Structural adjustment:** IMF and World Bank economic reform programmes.
- **Neocolonialism:** continued outside control over a state's economy after independence.
- **Developmental state:** a state that leads economic growth through planning.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which countries pair well?** Zambia and Ethiopia contrast democratisation with state-led growth. Somalia and Niger cover conflict and climate.

**Should I memorise growth rates?** Only give figures you are sure of, and use "about".
