These notes condense **B2.3 Cell specialization** from the IB DP *Biology guide*, first assessment 2025. Statements B2.3.1 to B2.3.7 are treated as SL and HL; B2.3.8 to B2.3.10 are additional higher level. The [B2.3 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-biology-2-b2-3/) explains each idea with worked examples, and the [B2.3 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-biology-2-b2-3-practice/) test them. The previous sub-topic is in the [B2.2 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-biology-2-b2-2-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Differentiation | Development of an unspecialized cell into a specialized one |
| Gene expression | Use of a gene to make its product, such as a protein |
| Stem cell | Cell that can self-renew and differentiate |
| Stem cell niche | Microenvironment that maintains stem cells and controls their division |
| Totipotent | Can form any cell type, including extra-embryonic tissue |
| Pluripotent | Can form any body cell type, but not a whole organism |
| Multipotent | Can form a limited range of related cell types |
| Surface area to volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume; falls as size increases |
| Microvilli | Finger-like projections of the plasma membrane |
| Invagination | Infolding of the plasma membrane |
| Pneumocyte (HL) | Cell of the alveolar wall: type I or type II |
| Surfactant (HL) | Secretion that reduces surface tension in alveoli |
| Acrosome (HL) | Vesicle of enzymes in the sperm head |

## B2.3.1: differentiation

- Zygote → many unspecialized cells → differentiation.
- All cells keep the full genome; they differ in which genes are expressed.
- Concentration gradients of signals in the embryo tell cells their position, and so which genes to activate.

## B2.3.2 to B2.3.4: stem cells

**Two properties:** self-renewal; ability to differentiate.

**Niches:** bone marrow (blood cells); hair follicles (follicle and hair).

| Potency | Can make | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Totipotent | Everything, including placenta | Zygote, very early embryo |
| Pluripotent | All body cell types | Embryo a few days old |
| Multipotent | One family of cells | Adult tissues, such as bone marrow |

Potency narrows as development proceeds.

## B2.3.5: sizes to know (approximate)

| Cell | Size |
|---|---|
| Egg | About 0.1 mm |
| Sperm | About 50 µm long, head a few µm |
| Red blood cell | About 7–8 µm |
| White blood cells | Mostly 10–15 µm |
| Motor neuron | Axon up to about 1 m |
| Striated muscle fibre | Several centimetres |

## B2.3.6: surface area to volume

| Shape | Surface area | Volume | Ratio |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube, side s | 6s² | s³ | 6 ÷ s |
| Sphere, radius r | 4πr² | (4/3)πr³ | 3 ÷ r |

- Bigger cell: lower ratio.
- Low ratio: too little membrane for the volume's needs; longer diffusion distance to the centre.
- Doubling the linear size halves the ratio.

## B2.3.7: raising the ratio

| Strategy | Example | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Flattening | Red blood cell (biconcave disc) | More surface per volume; short distance to the centre |
| Microvilli | Proximal convoluted tubule cell, lumen side | Large area for reabsorption |
| Invagination | Proximal convoluted tubule cell, base | Large area for transport into tissue fluid |

## B2.3.8 to B2.3.10 (HL only)

**Pneumocytes**

- Type I: extremely thin, most of the surface, short diffusion distance.
- Type II: cuboidal, lamellar bodies, secrete surfactant to stop alveoli collapsing.

**Muscle**

- Cardiac: branched, one or two nuclei, joined end to end.
- Striated fibre: long, unbranched, multinucleate, formed by fused cells; challenges the cell definition.

**Gametes**

| Sperm | Egg |
|---|---|
| Small, flagellum | Large, not motile |
| Midpiece mitochondria for ATP | Nutrient-rich cytoplasm |
| Acrosome enzymes | Coat changes to block further sperm |

## Worked reminders

**Sphere ratio.** r = 4 µm: 3 ÷ 4 = **0.75 µm⁻¹**; r = 12 µm: **0.25 µm⁻¹**, a third as much.

**Cube ratio.** s = 3 µm: 6 ÷ 3 = **2.0 µm⁻¹**.

**Flattening.** A sphere of radius 3 µm (ratio 1.0 µm⁻¹) reshaped into a 1 µm thick disc has ratio about 2.3 µm⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- "Differentiated cells lose genes."
- Totipotent and pluripotent swapped.
- Larger cells said to have a larger ratio.
- Microvilli said to add volume.
- Type I pneumocytes said to make surfactant.
- Sperm mitochondria said to be in the head.

## Quick self-test

1. Why can cells with the same genome differentiate into different types?
2. State the two properties of stem cells.
3. Name the two stem cell niches in the guide and what each produces.
4. Distinguish totipotent from pluripotent.
5. Give the potency of bone marrow stem cells.
6. Which is larger, a human egg or a red blood cell, and by roughly how many times in diameter?
7. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a sphere of radius 6 µm.
8. Why does a low ratio limit cell size?
9. Name the three ways cells increase their ratio.
10. (HL) What do type II pneumocytes secrete, and why?
11. (HL) Why is a striated muscle fibre an unusual cell?
12. (HL) Give the function of the sperm acrosome.

### Answers

1. They express different genes.
2. Self-renewal by repeated division; ability to differentiate.
3. Bone marrow (blood cells); hair follicles (follicle and hair).
4. Totipotent cells can form every cell type including extra-embryonic tissue; pluripotent cells form all body cell types but not a whole organism.
5. Multipotent.
6. The egg: about 0.1 mm against 7–8 µm, so roughly 13 times.
7. 3 ÷ 6 = **0.5 µm⁻¹**.
8. The surface is too small to exchange materials fast enough for the volume, and diffusion distances are long.
9. Flattening, microvilli, invagination.
10. Surfactant, to reduce surface tension so alveoli do not collapse.
11. It is very long and has many nuclei within one plasma membrane, formed by fusion of cells.
12. Its enzymes digest a path through the outer layers of the egg.
