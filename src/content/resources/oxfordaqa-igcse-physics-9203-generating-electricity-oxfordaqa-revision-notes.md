These notes condense section **3.6.1 Generating electricity** (statements a to f) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The subtopic is marked P, so it is assessed in the full Physics award. For explanations and the worked graph example read the [generating electricity study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-generating-electricity-oxfordaqa/), then use the [generating electricity practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-generating-electricity-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Generator effect (a).** A pd is induced across a conductor when:

- it moves relative to a magnetic field (cutting field lines), or
- it is in a changing magnetic field.

**Coils and magnets (b).** A pd is induced across a coil when a magnet moves into or out of it, or the coil moves relative to the magnet. No relative movement → no pd.

**Current (c).** A current flows only if the conductor is part of a complete circuit. The induced current's magnetic field **opposes** the field of the permanent magnet:

- North pole pushed in → near end of coil becomes north (repels).
- North pole pulled out → near end becomes south (attracts, resisting removal).
- This is why work must be done to move the magnet; that work becomes electrical energy.

**Reversing (d).** Reverse the direction of motion **or** the magnet's polarity → the induced pd and current reverse. Reverse both → no change in direction.

**Size of induced pd (e).** Increases with:

1. speed of movement;
2. strength of magnetic field;
3. number of turns on the coil;
4. area of the coil.

**Alternator vs dynamo (e).**

| | Alternator | Dynamo |
|---|---|---|
| Output | ac | dc |
| pd–time graph | Smooth wave above and below zero | Humps all on one side of zero |
| One turn of coil | One full cycle | Two humps |
| Faster rotation | Taller peaks, shorter period | Taller humps, closer together |

pd is a maximum when the coil sides cut field lines fastest (coil plane parallel to the field) and zero when they move along the lines (coil plane perpendicular to the field).

**Power stations (f).** Turbines turn wire coils between magnets. Fuel or nuclear: heat → steam → turbine → generator. Wind and hydro: moving air or water turns the turbine directly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Generator effect | Inducing a pd by relative movement between a conductor and a magnetic field, or by a changing field |
| Induced potential difference | A pd produced across a conductor by the generator effect |
| Induced current | The current that flows when the conductor is in a complete circuit |
| Alternator | Generator that produces alternating current |
| Dynamo | Generator that produces direct current |
| Turbine | Machine turned by steam, air or water, used to drive a generator |
| Period | Time for one complete cycle of an ac output |
| Frequency | Number of cycles per second (Hz); frequency = 1 ÷ period |

## Graph skills

- One cycle of an alternator output = one rotation of the coil.
- Frequency (Hz) = rotations per second.
- Period = 1 ÷ frequency.
- Doubling the speed of rotation halves the period and increases the peak pd.

## Exam traps

- A stationary magnet inside a coil induces nothing.
- An open circuit has an induced pd but no current.
- Dynamo graphs never cross zero.
- "Use a better magnet" is too vague; say "stronger magnet".
- "Opposes" means the coil's field pushes against the magnet's movement, not that the coil and magnet always repel.
- Do not spend time on slip rings and split rings: the specification says detail of these is not required.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the effect in which moving a wire in a magnetic field induces a pd.
2. A magnet is held still inside a coil. What does a connected ammeter read?
3. Give four ways to increase the pd induced in a rotating coil.
4. A north pole is pushed into a coil. What pole forms at the near end of the coil?
5. Why must work be done to push a magnet into a coil connected in a complete circuit?
6. The magnet is reversed and also moved the opposite way. What happens to the direction of the induced current?
7. What type of current does a dynamo produce?
8. An alternator coil makes 25 turns per second. What is the frequency and period of the output?
9. The coil speed in question 8 is doubled. Describe two changes to the pd–time graph.
10. At what position of the coil is the induced pd largest?
11. Describe the energy chain in a gas-fired power station.
12. Why does a wire moving parallel to magnetic field lines have no induced pd?

### Answers

1. The generator effect.
2. Zero.
3. Faster rotation, stronger magnetic field, more turns, larger coil area.
4. A north pole, which repels the magnet.
5. The induced current's field opposes the movement, so a force must be overcome; the work done becomes electrical energy.
6. No change: the two reversals cancel.
7. Direct current (dc).
8. **25 Hz**; period = 1 ÷ 25 = **0.040 s**.
9. The peaks are taller and the waves are closer together (period halves to 0.020 s).
10. When the plane of the coil is parallel to the field, so its sides cut the lines fastest.
11. Burning gas heats water to make steam; steam turns a turbine; the turbine turns coils between magnets in the generator.
12. It does not cut any field lines.
