## Questions

**1.** Define the newton using F = ma. A net force acts on a 0.35 kg trolley and gives it an acceleration of 4.0 m s⁻². Calculate the force. **[3]**

**2.** An astronaut and suit have a combined mass of 82 kg. On Mars the gravitational field strength is 3.7 N kg⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the weight on Mars. **[2]**
**(b)** State the mass on Mars. **[1]**

**3.** A block rests on a rough ramp and does not move.

**(a)** Name the three forces on the block and give the direction of each. **[3]**
**(b)** State the net force on the block. **[1]**

**4.** A crane lifts a load of mass 350 kg using a cable. Find the tension in the cable when the load:

**(a)** accelerates upward at 0.80 m s⁻² **[2]**
**(b)** moves upward at constant speed **[1]**
**(c)** slows to rest, with a deceleration of 0.80 m s⁻² while moving upward **[2]**

**5.** A sledge of mass 24 kg is pulled along level snow by a rope at 30° above the horizontal. The tension is 80 N and friction on the sledge is 35 N.

**(a)** Calculate the horizontal component of the tension. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the sledge. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the normal contact force from the snow. **[2]**

**6.** A box of mass 7.5 kg is released from rest on a ramp at 32° to the horizontal. Friction is 12 N.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration down the ramp. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed after sliding 4.0 m. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the acceleration if friction were zero. **[1]**

**7.** A ball is hit horizontally at 22 m s⁻¹ from a height of 2.4 m. Ignore drag.

**(a)** Calculate the time to reach the ground. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the horizontal distance travelled. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed on reaching the ground. **[3]**

**8.** **(a)** State three factors that affect the drag on a car moving through air, and say how each changes the drag. **[3]**
**(b)** A steel ball bearing falls at terminal velocity in oil. Name the forces on it and state the relationship between them. **[2]**

**9.** A ball of mass 0.070 kg falls from rest through air.

**(a)** Calculate its weight. **[1]**
**(b)** State its acceleration at the instant of release. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its acceleration when the drag is 0.30 N. **[2]**
**(d)** State the drag at terminal velocity. **[1]**
**(e)** Describe the velocity–time graph for the whole fall. **[2]**

**10.** A skydiver of mass 68 kg falls at terminal velocity. She opens her parachute and the drag instantly becomes 3200 N.

**(a)** Calculate her weight. **[1]**
**(b)** State the drag on her just before the parachute opens. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate her acceleration just after it opens, including direction. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why she eventually moves at a constant, smaller speed. **[1]**

**11.** Students drop stacks of identical paper cases, each case being the same shape, and time the final 1.00 m of the fall, when the speed is constant. Here, n is the number of cases.

| n | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| time for 1.00 m / s | 0.84 | 0.59 | 0.48 | 0.42 |

**(a)** Calculate the speed for n = 3 and n = 4. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the data support v² being proportional to n. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain what this suggests about how drag depends on speed. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest one improvement to the method. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** One newton is the net force that produces an acceleration of 1 m s⁻² in a mass of 1 kg [1]. F = ma = 0.35 × 4.0 [1] = **1.4 N** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that names both the 1 kg and the 1 m s⁻² is easier to credit than "the unit of force".

**2. (a)** W = mg = 82 × 3.7 [1] = **3.0 × 10² N** (303 N) [1].
**(b)** **82 kg**, because mass does not depend on location [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep mass in kg and weight in N. Swapping them gives away an easy mark.

**3. (a)** Weight, vertically downward [1]. Normal contact force, perpendicular to the ramp surface and away from it [1]. Friction, parallel to the ramp and up the slope [1].
**(b)** **Zero**, because the block is at rest [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Direction is part of the answer: "friction" alone does not say which way it acts.

**4. (a)** T − mg = ma [1], so T = 350 × (9.81 + 0.80) = **3.7 kN** (3713 N) [1].
**(b)** a = 0, so T = mg = 350 × 9.81 = **3.4 kN** (3434 N) [1].
**(c)** The acceleration is downward: T = m(g − a) [1] = 350 × (9.81 − 0.80) = **3.2 kN** (3154 N) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing the equation with a sign convention before substituting makes the sign of a easy to check in part (c).

**5. (a)** 80 cos 30° = **69 N** (69.3 N) [1].
**(b)** Net force = 69.3 − 35 = 34.3 N [1]; a = 34.3/24 = **1.4 m s⁻²** [1].
**(c)** Vertical component of tension = 80 sin 30° = 40.0 N upward [1]. N = 24 × 9.81 − 40.0 = **195 N** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the weight and the vertical component in part (c). A bare 235 N, which is the weight alone, does not show your reasoning.

**6. (a)** Down-slope component = 7.5 × 9.81 × sin 32° = 39.0 N [1]. Net force = 39.0 − 12 = 27.0 N [1]. a = 27.0/7.5 = **3.6 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** v² = 2as = 2 × 3.6 × 4.0 [1], so v = **5.4 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(c)** a = g sin 32° = **5.2 m s⁻²** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* If you reuse your part (a) value in part (b), quote it with its units so the link is clear.

**7. (a)** s = ½gt², so t = √(2 × 2.4/9.81) [1] = **0.70 s** [1].
**(b)** x = 22 × 0.70 = **15 m** (15.4 m) [1].
**(c)** Vertical speed = 9.81 × 0.70 = 6.9 m s⁻¹ [1]. v = √(22² + 6.9²) [1] = **23 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the horizontal velocity stays constant because no horizontal force acts. A calculation without that reason leaves the physics unstated.

**8. (a)** Any three: higher speed gives greater drag [1]; larger cross-sectional area gives greater drag [1]; a less streamlined shape gives greater drag [1]; denser air gives greater drag.
**(b)** Weight downward, and upthrust and drag upward [1]. Weight = drag + upthrust, because the net force is zero [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each factor needs a direction of change. "Speed affects drag" says nothing useful.

**9. (a)** W = 0.070 × 9.81 = **0.69 N** [1].
**(b)** **9.81 m s⁻²**, because the drag is zero when the speed is zero [1].
**(c)** Net force = 0.6867 − 0.30 = 0.387 N [1]; a = 0.387/0.070 = **5.5 m s⁻²** [1].
**(d)** **0.69 N**, equal to the weight [1].
**(e)** It starts at the origin with a gradient of 9.81 m s⁻² [1], then curves with a decreasing gradient and becomes horizontal at the terminal velocity [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* For a graph description, state the starting gradient and the final gradient, not just "it curves".

**10. (a)** W = 68 × 9.81 = **667 N** [1].
**(b)** **667 N**, upward, because she is at terminal velocity [1].
**(c)** Net force = 3200 − 667 = 2533 N upward [1]; a = 2533/68 [1] = **37 m s⁻² upward** [1].
**(d)** As she slows, drag decreases until it again equals weight, so the net force is zero [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A deceleration should be labelled as an upward acceleration, because the direction carries the meaning.

**11. (a)** v = 1.00/0.48 = **2.1 m s⁻¹** [1]; v = 1.00/0.42 = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** v² for n = 1 to 4: 1.42, 2.87, 4.34, 5.67 m² s⁻² [1]. v²/n = 1.42, 1.44, 1.45, 1.42 [1]. The ratio is constant within the scatter, so v² ∝ n [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** At terminal velocity, drag equals weight, and weight is proportional to n [1]. So drag is proportional to v² [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Use more repeats for each n and average (or use light gates in place of a stopwatch) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" questions need the calculated values written out. Stating only that the ratio is constant leaves your evidence unseen.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing mass with weight, or quoting weight in kg.
- Missing the direction of a vector answer, such as the sign of an acceleration.
- Putting the normal contact force vertical on a slope.
- Forgetting upthrust in a liquid.
- Using g as the acceleration while drag acts.
- Giving a final answer with too many or too few significant figures.
- Describing a graph as "curved" without the start and end gradients.
- Not saying why terminal velocity has been reached.
