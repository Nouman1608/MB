## Section B

**5.** A chromatogram of a four-component mixture has triangular peaks.

| Peak | Base width / min | Height / arbitrary units |
|---|---|---|
| A | 0.20 | 9.0 |
| B | 0.40 | 6.0 |
| C | 0.30 | 4.0 |
| D | 0.50 | 2.4 |

**(a)** Calculate the area of each peak. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of component B in the mixture. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why using peak heights alone would give the wrong composition for this mixture. **[1]**

**6.** Pure samples of three alcohols are run under fixed conditions: methanol 1.6 min, ethanol 2.3
min, propan-1-ol 3.5 min. A mixture run under the same conditions gives peaks at 2.3 min and 3.5 min.

**(a)** Identify the alcohols probably present in the mixture. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why propan-1-ol has a longer retention time than ethanol. **[2]**

**(c)** State why the mixture must be run under the same conditions as the pure samples. **[1]**

**7.** Pentane, 2-methylbutane and 2,2-dimethylpropane all have the formula C₅H₁₂.

**(a)** Predict the order in which they reach the detector, first to last. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain your order. **[2]**

**8.** State and explain the effect on retention times of each change.

**(a)** The carrier gas flow rate is increased. **[1]**

**(b)** The column temperature is lowered. **[2]**

**9.** A 250 g sample of a fuel blend is analysed. The ethanol peak is 8.0% of the total peak area.
Calculate the mass of ethanol in the sample. **[1]**

**10.** Suggest why GLC cannot be used to separate a mixture of sodium chloride and potassium chloride.
**[1]**

**11.** Two components of a mixture give a single peak on one column. Suggest how the analyst could
show that two components are present. **[2]**

**12.** A chromatogram of a hydrocarbon mixture shows three peaks.

| Retention time / min | Peak area |
|---|---|
| 1.9 | 0.60 |
| 2.7 | 3.6 |
| 4.1 | 1.2 |

Under the same conditions, pure hexane has a retention time of 2.7 min and pure heptane 4.1 min.

**(a)** The third component is either pentane or octane. Identify it and explain your choice. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of hexane in the mixture. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** So that it stays liquid in the heated column and is not vaporised and carried away by the gas
[1].

**2.** So that it does not react with the sample or the stationary phase, letting the components reach
the detector unchanged [1].

**3.** At the maximum (top) of the peak [1].

**4.** 18.0 / 100.0 × 100 = 18.0% [1].

**5. (a)** A: ½ × 0.20 × 9.0 = 0.90; B: ½ × 0.40 × 6.0 = 1.2; C: ½ × 0.30 × 4.0 = 0.60; D: ½ × 0.50 ×
2.4 = 0.60. All four correct [2]; two or three correct [1].

**(b)** Total = 3.3, so B = 1.2 / 3.3 × 100 = 36.4% [1].

**(c)** The peaks have different widths, so height is not proportional to area (A is tallest but B has
the largest area) [1].

**6. (a)** Ethanol and propan-1-ol [1].

**(b)** Propan-1-ol has more electrons (a longer chain), so it has stronger instantaneous
dipole–induced dipole attractions to the non-polar stationary phase [1]; it dissolves more in the
stationary phase and spends longer there, so it takes longer to reach the detector [1].

**(c)** Retention time depends on conditions such as temperature, flow rate and column, so only times
measured under identical conditions can be compared [1].

**7. (a)** 2,2-dimethylpropane, then 2-methylbutane, then pentane [1].

**(b)** All three have the same number of electrons, but more branching gives a more compact molecule
with less surface contact [1]; so attractions to the stationary phase are weakest for
2,2-dimethylpropane and strongest for pentane, giving the shortest and longest retention times [1].

**8. (a)** Retention times decrease, because the components are carried through the column faster [1].

**(b)** Retention times increase [1]; at a lower temperature the components spend more time dissolved
in the stationary liquid and less time as vapour in the gas [1].

**9.** 0.080 × 250 = 20.0 g [1].

**10.** Ionic salts are not volatile, so they cannot be vaporised and carried by the gas [1].

**11.** Repeat the analysis under different conditions, for example with a different stationary phase
or column temperature [1]; if two peaks appear, or the peak's retention time no longer matches a single
reference, more than one component is present [1].

**12. (a)** Pentane [1]: its retention time (1.9 min) is shorter than hexane's, and pentane, with
fewer electrons, has weaker attractions to the stationary phase than hexane, while octane would have
a longer retention time than heptane [1].

**(b)** Total area = 0.60 + 3.6 + 1.2 = 5.4; hexane = 3.6 / 5.4 × 100 = 66.7% [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the stationary phase as a solid: in GLC it is a liquid on a solid support.
- Comparing peak heights when the peaks have different widths.
- Explaining retention time with boiling point only, without the interaction with the stationary
  phase.
- Comparing retention times measured under different conditions.
- Forgetting that a single peak could hide two components with the same retention time.
- Saying the carrier gas dissolves or reacts with the sample.
