## Paper 2 at a glance

2 hours 40 minutes, 110 marks, **30% of the A-level** — the highest raw mark total of the three papers.

| Section | Focus | Marks | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Translation into Urdu (unseen) | 20 | ~30 min |
| B | Written response, literary text | 45 | ~1hr 5min |
| C | Written response, film | 45 | ~1hr 5min |

Every candidate produces exactly **two** extended responses: either two literary-text essays (B twice, skip C), or one literary text plus one film (B once, then C).

## Section A: translation into Urdu

Marking is **points-based**: the passage is divided into 20 chunks, one mark each.

- Non-grammatical **misspellings** are tolerated if they don't create ambiguity or form a different word.
- **Verb and adjective endings must be correct** — these are explicitly *not* covered by the spelling tolerance, unlike in ordinary spelling.
- Direction is **English → Urdu** here — the reverse of Paper 1, which translates into English. Do not mix the two up under exam pressure.

## Sections B/C: works studied

Prescribed list (Pearson Edexcel): three literary texts — including an essay collection by "Patras" (Ahmad Shah Bukhari), a novel, and a play — and three films.

Marked against three levels-based criteria:

- **AO4** — critical and analytical response to the work (cultural/social insight)
- **AO3** — range of grammatical structures and vocabulary
- **AO3** — accuracy of language

Paper 2 carries **no AO1 or AO2 marks** at all — unlike Papers 1 and 3, this paper is entirely about accurate, analytical Urdu writing about a text or film, not comprehension or research skills.

## The five preparation angles

Prepare each studied work (whichever two you have) against the same five angles for every text/film: **form and technique, key themes and issues, characterisation, plot structure, social and cultural setting.**

## Worked example: structuring a works answer

**Position → Evidence → Cultural connection.** State your overall stance on the question first; support it with two or three specific scenes or lines (not full plot retelling); close by linking the writer's treatment of the issue to one of the qualification's four broader social/cultural themes. This structure rewards AO3 (accurate language throughout) and AO4 (the explicit cultural link) at the same time.

## Worked example: chunk-marking in Section A

Section A's 20 marks come from dividing the unseen passage into 20 chunks, each worth one mark for an
accurate rendering into Urdu.

```
Chunk contains a verb ending:      must be grammatically correct --
                                    no tolerance, unlike ordinary
                                    spelling
Chunk contains an everyday noun:   a non-grammatical misspelling is
                                    tolerated PROVIDED it does not
                                    create ambiguity or become a
                                    different word entirely
Chunk contains an adjective
agreeing with its noun:            gender/number agreement must be
                                    correct, treated the same way as
                                    verb endings, not as ordinary
                                    spelling
```

Working chunk by chunk rather than translating the whole passage in one pass makes it far easier to
check each mark-bearing unit individually against this rule before moving on.

## Paper 1 vs Paper 2: don't mix up the direction

| | Paper 1 | Paper 2, Section A |
|---|---|---|
| Direction | Urdu → English | English → Urdu |
| Marks | 20 (within 80 total) | 20 (within 110 total) |
| Time | Part of a longer paper | ~30 minutes |

Because both papers include a 20-mark translation section, candidates under exam pressure sometimes
default to habits from one paper while sitting the other — always confirm the direction stated on the
paper before starting.

## Exam traps

- Attempting three extended responses, or the wrong literary-text/film combination — only two total are required.
- Treating verb/adjective endings as ordinary tolerated spelling in Section A — they are not.
- Retelling plot in Sections B/C without connecting to a named social/cultural theme, weakening the AO4 mark.
- Confusing Paper 2's translation direction (into Urdu) with Paper 1's (into English).

## Timing across the paper

With 2 hours 40 minutes and three sections, keep roughly to the recommended splits — about 30 minutes
for the Section A translation, and about 1 hour 5 minutes for each extended response — rather than
letting one section overrun and squeezing the other. Since Sections B and C carry the bulk of the
marks (45 each versus 20 for Section A), a candidate who runs out of time on the second extended
response loses more marks than one who rushes the translation slightly, so timing discipline matters
more here than in a paper with more evenly distributed marks.

## Self-test

1. How many extended responses does every candidate write, and what are the two valid combinations?
2. What is explicitly excluded from Section A's spelling tolerance?
3. Which assessment objectives does Paper 2 carry, and which two does it *not* carry?
4. Name the five angles to prepare for each studied work.
5. What structure connects AO3 and AO4 in a single works answer?

**Answers:** 1. Two — either two literary-text essays, or one literary text plus one film. 2. Verb and adjective endings, which must be grammatically correct even though ordinary misspellings are tolerated. 3. AO3 (accuracy and range) and AO4 (critical/cultural response); it carries no AO1 or AO2 marks. 4. Form and technique, key themes and issues, characterisation, plot structure, social and cultural setting. 5. Position, evidence, cultural connection — stating a stance, supporting it with specific material, then linking to a broader social/cultural theme.
