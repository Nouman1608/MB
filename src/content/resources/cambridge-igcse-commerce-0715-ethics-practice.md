## Section A - short questions

**1.** A snack is advertised as "low fat" although it is high in fat. This is an example of: **[1]**
A fair trade   B misleading advertising   C harmful disposal   D sponsorship

**2.** Which is a feature of fair trade? **[1]**
A a guaranteed minimum price for producers   B the lowest possible price for buyers   C no written agreement   D sales only in the producer's country

**3.** A factory pours chemical waste into a river. This is an example of: **[1]**
A recycling   B harmful disposal   C water stewardship   D fair trade

**4.** Define ethics. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** features of fair trade. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** effects of misleading advertising on a business. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *A coffee retailer buys fair trade beans from a farming cooperative.*
(a) Identify **one** benefit of fair trade to the farmers. **[1]**
(b) Explain **one** benefit to the retailer. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** disadvantage to the retailer. **[2]**

**8.** Explain the difference between behaving legally and behaving ethically. Give an example. **[4]**

**9.** *A shop advertises "Sale: up to 70% off". Only two products out of 400 are reduced by 70%. Most are reduced by 5%.*
(a) Explain why this advert is misleading. **[2]**
(b) Explain **two** possible effects on the shop. **[4]**

**10.** *A cocoa farmer sells 3,000 kg a year. The market price is $1.60 per kg. A fair trade buyer pays a minimum of $2.00 per kg plus a premium of $0.30 per kg to a village fund.*
(a) Calculate the farmer's income at the market price. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the farmer's income at the fair trade price. **[1]**
(c) Calculate the amount of premium paid to the village fund. **[1]**
(d) Explain **one** way the village could use the premium. **[2]**

**11.** *An electronics firm sells old phones. Some are thrown into open land when they stop working.*
(a) Explain **two** harms this might cause. **[4]**
(b) Suggest **one** more ethical way of disposing of the phones. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss whether a business should always put ethics before profit. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. A false claim gives customers the wrong impression. **[1]**

**2.** A. Fair trade guarantees producers a minimum price. **[1]**

**3.** B. Dumping chemicals harms the environment and people. **[1]**

**4.** Moral principles [1] that guide what is right and wrong in how people and businesses behave, such as honesty and fairness [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: guaranteed minimum price [1]; community premium [1]; safe working conditions [1]; no child or forced labour [1]; long-term trading relationships [1]; environmentally friendly methods [1]. **[max 2]**

**6.** Any two: loss of customers' trust [1]; refunds and complaints [1]; fines [1]; legal action [1]; damaged reputation [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Any one: stable and fair income [1]; safe working conditions [1]; community premium [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any one: attracts customers who care about ethics [1], so sales may rise [1]; or improves the retailer's reputation [1], building trust and loyalty [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Any one: fair trade beans cost more [1], so the retailer must raise prices or accept lower profit [1]; or certification costs money [1], which adds to expenses [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Behaving legally means following the law [1]. Behaving ethically means doing what is fair and honest, which may go beyond the law [1]. Example, any one: a firm pays the legal minimum wage, which is legal [1], but may not be a wage workers can live on, so it may not be ethical [1]. **[4]**

**9.** (a) The words "up to 70%" suggest many items are heavily reduced [1], but only 2 of 400 are, so customers are given a false impression [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: customers feel cheated and lose trust [2]; they may complain or ask for refunds [2]; the shop's reputation falls, so future sales drop [2]; a regulator may fine the shop [2]. (max 4 marks) **[6]**

**10.** (a) 3,000 x 1.60 = **$4,800** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) 3,000 x 2.00 = **$6,000** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) 3,000 x 0.30 = **$900** [1]. (1 mark)
(d) Any one: build a school [1], so children in the village can be educated [1]; or provide clean water [1], which improves health [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**11.** (a) Any two, 2 marks each: phones contain harmful metals and chemicals [1], which can poison soil and water [1]; wildlife may be harmed [1], and people nearby may become ill [1]; the firm may face fines and clean-up costs [1], and its reputation suffers [1]. (max 4 marks)
(b) Any one: run a take-back scheme [1], so old phones are collected and recycled safely [1]; or pay a specialist firm to dismantle them [1], recovering metals and treating harmful parts [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**12.** Up to 2 for putting ethics first, up to 2 for reasons profit matters, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Ethics first: a good reputation builds trust and loyalty [1]; avoids fines and legal action [1]; staff and customers respect the firm [1].
Profit matters: a firm that makes a loss may close, and then no one benefits [1]; ethical choices can raise costs and prices, so customers may leave [1]; shareholders expect a return [1].
Conclusion: for example, a business should not break ethical standards for profit, but it must remain affordable, so it should choose the most ethical option it can sustain [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating legal and ethical as the same.
- Giving only the farmer's benefits in a question about the retailer.
- Writing "it is bad" about misleading advertising with no effect on customers or the business.
- Forgetting the premium in fair trade calculations.
- Giving a one-sided answer to a "discuss" question.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the two ethical issues named in 0715?** Misleading advertising and harmful disposal of products.

**Does fair trade cost the buyer more?** Usually yes, because the buyer pays a guaranteed minimum price and often a premium.
