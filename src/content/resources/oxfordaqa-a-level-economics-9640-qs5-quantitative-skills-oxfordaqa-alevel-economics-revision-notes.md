These notes condense quantitative skill QS5, "calculate and interpret index numbers", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification, which says the quantitative skills could be assessed in any of the assessments. The same skill supports 3.2.1.2 (index numbers, base years and weights) and 3.4.1.2 (calculating the terms of trade from export and import price indices). For full worked examples, read the [QS5 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs5-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics/); to practise, use the [QS5 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs5-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Index                      value_t / value_base × 100
Value from index           value_base × index_t / 100
Rebase to year R           old index_t / old index_R × 100
Link new series to old     new index_t × (old index in link year / 100)
Weighted index             Σ(weight × category index) / Σweights
Inflation from a CPI       (CPI_t − CPI_t−1) / CPI_t−1 × 100
Terms of trade             export price index / import price index × 100
Value index                price index × volume index / 100
Average annual change      (index_end / index_start)^(1/n) − 1
```

## Index points versus per cent

| Index moves | Points | Percentage change |
|---|---|---|
| 100 → 108 | 8 | 8% |
| 150 → 165 | 15 | 10% |
| 125 → 120 | −5 | −4% |

Points equal per cent only when the starting index is 100.

## Terms of trade

| Terms of trade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Above 100 | A unit of exports buys more imports than in the base year (improvement since then) |
| Below 100 | A unit of exports buys fewer imports than in the base year (deterioration) |
| Rising | Export prices rising faster than import prices, or falling more slowly |

An improvement can harm export sales if it reflects a higher exchange rate or higher costs. A deterioration from cheaper, more competitive exports may raise export volumes. Always state the cause.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Index number | A value expressed relative to a base year set at 100 |
| Base year | The reference year with index 100 |
| Weights | The relative importance of each item, often its share of spending |
| Rebasing | Moving the base year of a series |
| Linking (splicing) | Joining two series with different bases using a common year |
| Terms of trade | Export prices relative to import prices, as an index |
| Volume index | An index of quantities, with prices held constant |
| Value index | An index of spending or revenue, combining price and volume |

## Quick worked checks

**Rebase before comparing.** Index A (2015 = 100) is 120 in 2020 and 138 in 2024; index B (2020 = 100) is 112 in 2024. Rebased, A is 138/120 × 100 = 115 in 2024, so A grew 15% and B 12% since 2020.

**Linking.** Old index 127.1 in the link year; new index 105.6 two years later. On the old base: 105.6 × 1.271 = 134.2. The rate of change, 5.6%, is the same on either base.

**Value split.** Price index 110 and volume index 95 give a value index of 104.5: revenue up 4.5% while quantity fell 5%.

## What an index cannot tell you

- The level of the variable: equal indices in two countries do not mean equal values.
- Changes for any one household or firm: a price index is an average across a basket.
- Whether weights are still accurate: out-of-date weights can misstate inflation.
- The cause of the change.

## Exam traps

- Comparing two indices with different base years.
- Writing "up 15%" for a move from 150 to 165, which is 10%.
- Inverting the terms-of-trade formula.
- Adding rather than multiplying price and volume indices.
- Forgetting to divide by the sum of the weights when it is not 100.
- Using total change ÷ years as the average annual rate.

## Self-test

1. The base-year value is 80 and the index this year is 125. Find this year's value.
2. This year's value is 63 and the index is 105. Find the base-year value.
3. An index (2010 = 100) is 140 in 2020 and 161 in 2024. Rebase the 2024 value to 2020 = 100.
4. The export price index is 120 and the import price index is 96. Find the terms of trade.
5. A price index is 105 and the volume index is 90. Find the value index.
6. An index rises from 100 to 121 over two years. Find the average annual rate of increase.
7. An index rises from 150 to 165. State the change in points and in per cent.
8. An old index has value 130 in 2019. A new index (2019 = 100) has value 104 in 2021. Express the 2021 value on the old base.
9. The terms of trade fall from 100 to 95. Explain what this means.
10. Two categories have weights 60 and 40 and indices 110 and 105. Find the weighted index.
11. Give one thing an index number cannot show.
12. Why must two indices be rebased to a common year before they are compared?

### Answers

1. 80 × 1.25 = 100.
2. 63/1.05 = 60.
3. 161/140 × 100 = 115.
4. 120/96 × 100 = 125.
5. 105 × 90/100 = 94.5.
6. 1.21^(1/2) = 1.1, so 10% a year.
7. 15 points; 15/150 = 10%.
8. 104 × 1.30 = 135.2.
9. A unit of exports now buys 5% fewer imports than in the base year.
10. (60 × 110 + 40 × 105)/100 = (6,600 + 4,200)/100 = 108.
11. Any one: the level of the variable; the change for any one household; the cause of the change.
12. Each index measures change from its own base, so values from different bases do not measure change over the same period.
