## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Neurone | A nerve cell that carries electrical impulses |
| CNS | Central nervous system: the brain and spinal cord |
| Sensory neurone | Carries impulses from a receptor to the CNS |
| Relay neurone | Carries impulses within the CNS, from sensory to motor neurone |
| Motor neurone | Carries impulses from the CNS to an effector |
| Synapse | The gap between two neurones, crossed by a chemical that diffuses across |
| Reflex action | An automatic, rapid response not involving the conscious part of the brain |
| Accommodation | Changing the shape of the lens to focus on near or distant objects |
| Thermoregulatory centre | The part of the brain that monitors and controls body temperature |
| Vasodilation / vasoconstriction | Widening / narrowing of blood vessels near the skin |

## The pathway

stimulus → receptor → sensory neurone → CNS (relay neurone) → motor neurone → effector → response

Effectors: **muscles contract** or **glands secrete hormones**.

## Why reflexes matter

Automatic and rapid → protect the body from harm (for example, withdrawing from heat or pain) before the brain
has consciously processed the information.

## Required practical 7

| Variable | Ruler-drop example |
|---|---|
| Independent | The factor tested (caffeine, practice, distraction, hand used) |
| Dependent | Distance ruler falls (or reaction time) |
| Control | Same dropper, same hand, same starting position, same ruler |

Shorter distance = faster reaction. Repeat, remove anomalies, calculate a mean. Allow for practice effects.

## The brain (Biology only)

| Region | Function |
|---|---|
| Cerebral cortex | Consciousness, intelligence, memory, language |
| Cerebellum | Coordination of muscular activity |
| Medulla | Unconscious activities: heartbeat, breathing |

**Higher tier only:** mapped by studying patients with brain damage, electrical stimulation and MRI scanning.
Brain disorders are hard to investigate and treat because the brain is complex and delicate.

## The eye (Biology only)

| Structure | Job |
|---|---|
| Cornea | Refracts light |
| Iris | Controls pupil size |
| Lens | Focuses light on the retina |
| Ciliary muscles | Change lens shape |
| Suspensory ligaments | Hold the lens; transmit pull from the ciliary muscles |
| Retina | Light receptors |
| Optic nerve | Impulses to the brain |
| Sclera | Tough protective outer layer |

**Dim light:** pupil larger → more light enters. **Bright light:** pupil smaller → retina protected.

**Accommodation**

- Near: ciliary muscles **contract** → ligaments **loosen** → lens **thicker** → refracts **strongly**.
- Distant: ciliary muscles **relax** → ligaments **pulled tight** → lens **thin** → refracts **slightly**.

**Defects**

| Defect | Problem | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| Myopia (short sight) | Distant objects focus in front of the retina | Concave lens |
| Hyperopia (long sight) | Near objects focus behind the retina | Convex lens |

New technologies: hard and soft contact lenses; laser surgery to reshape the cornea; replacement lens.

## Body temperature (Biology only)

Receptors: in the **thermoregulatory centre** (blood temperature) and in the **skin** (impulses sent to the
centre).

| Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|
| Vasodilation | Vasoconstriction |
| Sweating | Sweating stops |
| | Shivering |

**Higher tier only:** vasodilation and sweating increase energy transfer from the skin to the environment;
vasoconstriction reduces it; shivering uses respiration, which transfers energy to warm the body.

## Reading ray diagrams of the eye (Biology only)

- Find where the rays **meet**. In front of the retina → myopia; behind the retina → hyperopia.
- A **concave** lens is thinner in the middle and spreads rays out, moving the focus back onto the retina.
- A **convex** lens is thicker in the middle and bends rays inwards, moving the focus forward onto the retina.
- In every corrected diagram, the rays must meet exactly **on** the retina.

## Exam traps

- A synapse is crossed by a chemical, not by the electrical impulse.
- Relay neurones are in the CNS, not in the arm or leg.
- Receptors detect; effectors respond.
- Ciliary muscles **contract** for **near** objects.
- Myopia → concave; hyperopia → convex.
- Blood vessels dilate or constrict; they do not move up or down in the skin.
- Cerebellum ≠ cerebral cortex.
- "Reflexes use the brain to decide" is wrong: they are automatic.

## Self-test

1. What is the CNS made of?
2. Name the three types of neurone in a reflex arc, in order.
3. How does the signal cross a synapse?
4. Why are reflex actions important?
5. In the ruler-drop test, what does a shorter distance show?
6. Which brain region controls heartbeat and breathing?
7. Which brain region coordinates muscular activity?
8. What happens to the pupil in dim light?
9. Describe what happens in the eye when focusing on a near object.
10. Which lens corrects myopia?
11. Where are the two sets of receptors that detect temperature?
12. Name three responses to the body being too cold.

### Answers

1. The brain and spinal cord.
2. Sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone.
3. A chemical diffuses across the gap and starts an impulse in the next neurone.
4. They are automatic and rapid, protecting the body from harm.
5. A faster reaction time.
6. Medulla.
7. Cerebellum.
8. It becomes larger, letting more light in.
9. Ciliary muscles contract, suspensory ligaments loosen, lens becomes thicker and refracts light strongly.
10. A concave lens.
11. In the thermoregulatory centre of the brain and in the skin.
12. Vasoconstriction; sweating stops; shivering.
