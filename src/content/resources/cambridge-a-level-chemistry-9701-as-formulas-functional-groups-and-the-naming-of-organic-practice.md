## Answers

**1.** A compound made up of carbon and hydrogen atoms only [1].

**2.** They contain only C–C and C–H single bonds; there is no reactive atom or group of atoms to define a series and its characteristic reactions [1].

**3. (a)** Carbonyl (–CHO) [1].

**(b)** Hydroxyl (–OH) [1].

**4.** CₙH₂ₙ [1].

**5. (a)** 3-methylpentane: the longest chain has five carbons and the methyl group is on carbon 3 from either end [1].

**(b)** 2-bromopentane: five-carbon chain, numbered from the end nearer the bromine [1].

**(c)** Propanal: three carbons including the CHO carbon, which is always carbon 1, so no number is written [1].

**6.** CH₃CH₂CH=CHCH₂CH₃ [1].

**7. (a)** The longest carbon chain has not been chosen: the ethyl group is part of a five-carbon chain [1].

**(b)** 3-methylpentane [1].

**8. (a)** 3-methylbutan-2-ol: four-carbon chain containing the C–OH; numbering from the left gives –OH the lower number, 2, and puts the methyl on carbon 3 [1].

**(b)** 1,3-dichlorobutane: numbering from the CH₂Cl end gives the set 1,3, which is lower than 2,4 [1].

**(c)** 3-methylbutanoic acid: the COOH carbon is carbon 1, so the methyl branch is on carbon 3 [1].

**(d)** Butanenitrile: four carbons, counting the carbon of the C≡N group [1].

**9. (a)** A: methyl propanoate [1]. B: propyl methanoate [1].

**(b)** Carboxylic acids [1]; for example butanoic acid, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH (accept 2-methylpropanoic acid) [1].

**10. (a)** Pentan-3-ol [1].

**(b)** Molecular formula C₅H₁₂O [1]; empirical formula C₅H₁₂O [1].

**(c)** Secondary: the carbon carrying the –OH is attached to two other carbon atoms [1].

**11. (a)** Molecular C₆H₁₂O₂ [1]; empirical C₃H₆O [1].

**(b)** Molecular C₆H₁₂ [1]; empirical CH₂ [1].

**12. (a)** CH₃COCH₂CH₃ [1].

**(b)** All atoms and bonds drawn: four carbons in a chain, a C=O double bond on the second carbon, and eight C–H bonds [1]. Total: 3 C–C, 8 C–H and 1 C=O, so 12 bonds (counting the double bond as one bond) [1].

**(c)** In a four-carbon chain the C=O can only be on carbon 2 (carbon 1 would make it an aldehyde), so the position is unambiguous [1].

**13.** Propanal, CH₃CH₂CHO [1 for formula, 1 for name]. Propanone, CH₃COCH₃ [1 for formula, 1 for name].

**14. (a)** 2,2-dimethylpropan-1-ol: the longest chain containing the C–OH has three carbons, with two methyl groups on carbon 2 [1].

**(b)** Primary: the carbon carrying the –OH is bonded to only one other carbon atom [1].

**(c)** A central carbon (a vertex) with four lines: three lines end at methyl carbons, and the fourth leads to a CH₂ carbon [1], from which a line leads to OH, drawn as a label [1].

**(d)** C₅H₁₂O [1]; for example pentan-1-ol (accept pentan-2-ol or pentan-3-ol) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing a chain that is not the longest one.
- Numbering from the end that gives the functional group a higher number.
- Naming esters with the acid part first.
- Writing aldehydes as –COH.
- Forgetting hydrogens implied in skeletal formulas when counting atoms.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to name branched esters or branched nitriles?**
No. The syllabus limits ester and nitrile names to straight chains. Esters can have up to six carbons in each part, and other compounds up to six carbons in total.

**Is "propylamine" or "propan-1-amine" correct?**
The syllabus table uses the form propylamine, and that style is used on this page. Follow the form your teacher and the syllabus use, and be consistent.
