## The four things section 3.4 tests

1. The purposes of purchases ledger and sales ledger control accounts.
2. The books of prime entry as sources of the entries.
3. Preparing both accounts, with every listed item and with debit and credit balances within each account.
4. The impact of digital recording on preparing and using control accounts.

Not required: reconciling control account balances with the sales ledger and purchases ledger balances (the syllabus says so).

## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger control account | One ledger account that totals all customer accounts. Normal balance: debit. |
| Purchases ledger control account | One ledger account that totals all supplier accounts. Normal balance: credit. |
| Trade receivables | Amounts owed to the business by customers for goods sold on credit. |
| Trade payables | Amounts owed by the business to suppliers for goods bought on credit. |
| Cash discount | Reduction for early payment; recorded in the cash book discount columns. |
| Trade discount | Reduction on the invoice; never entered in a control account. |
| Contra entry | Setting off what a person owes you against what you owe them, when they are both customer and supplier. No cash moves. |

## Purposes: learn four

- Gives total trade receivables or trade payables at any date.
- Provides a check, because the total is built from the books of prime entry and so is independent of the individual accounts.
- Helps control, as the control account can be kept by someone other than the person posting to individual accounts.
- Finds a missing total when the other figures are known.

## Sources

| Entry | Book of prime entry |
|---|---|
| Credit sales | Sales journal |
| Credit purchases | Purchases journal |
| Sales returns | Sales returns journal |
| Purchases returns | Purchases returns journal |
| Receipts, payments, refunds, dishonoured cheques | Cash book (bank column) |
| Cash discounts allowed and received | Cash book (discount columns) |
| Irrecoverable debts, interest on overdue accounts, contra | General journal |

## Which side? Sales ledger control account

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| Opening debit balances | Opening credit balances |
| Credit sales | Receipts from customers |
| Dishonoured cheques | Cash discounts allowed |
| Interest on overdue accounts | Sales returns |
| Refunds paid to customers | Irrecoverable debts |
| Closing credit balances c/d | Contra (to purchases ledger) |
| | Closing debit balances c/d |

## Which side? Purchases ledger control account

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| Opening debit balances | Opening credit balances |
| Payments to suppliers | Credit purchases |
| Cash discounts received | Interest charged by suppliers |
| Purchases returns | Refunds received from suppliers |
| Contra (from sales ledger) | Dishonoured cheques (our cheques) |
| Closing credit balances c/d | Closing debit balances c/d |

Memory aid: in the sales ledger control account, anything that makes customers owe more goes on the debit side. In the purchases ledger control account, anything that makes you owe suppliers more goes on the credit side. Everything that reduces the debt goes on the other side.

## Method in steps

1. Read the question and mark each item as sales ledger or purchases ledger.
2. Draw a two-sided account with an amount column on each side.
3. Place opening debit balances and opening credit balances on their own sides.
4. Enter each total from the books of prime entry on the correct side. Ignore trade discount and cash sales.
5. Put a given closing balance of the unusual kind (credit balances in the sales ledger, debit balances in the purchases ledger) on the side with the other opening items.
6. Total the side with more information. Find the balancing figure on the other side. Check that both totals agree.
7. Label every line (source or description) and bring down the balances.

## Small worked reminder

Norcott Bakery Supplies: opening debit balances 5,000; credit sales 12,000; receipts 9,500; cash discounts allowed 200; sales returns 400. No other items.

| Sales ledger control account | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Debit** | **Amount** | **Credit** | **Amount** |
| Balance b/d | 5,000 | Bank | 9,500 |
| Credit sales | 12,000 | Discount allowed | 200 |
| | | Sales returns | 400 |
| | | Balance c/d | 6,900 |
| | **17,000** | | **17,000** |

Debit side 5,000 + 12,000 = 17,000. Credit side known: 9,500 + 200 + 400 = 10,100. Balancing figure: 17,000 - 10,100 = 6,900.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Cash discount and trade discount.** Only cash discount is entered in a control account.
- **Credit sales and cash sales.** Only credit sales go into the sales ledger control account; the same applies to purchases.
- **Dishonoured cheque from a customer and one issued by you.** The first is a debit in the sales ledger control account. The second is a credit in the purchases ledger control account.
- **Interest charged to a customer and interest charged by a supplier.** The first increases what customers owe: debit, sales ledger. The second increases what you owe: credit, purchases ledger.
- **Refund paid to a customer and refund received from a supplier.** The first clears a customer's credit balance: debit, sales ledger. The second clears a supplier's debit balance: credit, purchases ledger.
- **Contra and payment.** A contra uses no cash and no bank account. It appears in both control accounts on opposite sides.

## Digital recording

- Software can total the journals and produce a control account automatically and at any date.
- Arithmetic errors in totals are less likely, so the account is quicker to prepare and can be used more often, for example for chasing overdue accounts.
- An input error (wrong amount or wrong customer) is still carried through.
- Access controls, passwords and backups protect the data the totals depend on.

## Quick self-test

1. On which side of the sales ledger control account does a customer's dishonoured cheque go?
2. Name the book of prime entry for total credit purchases.
3. A supplier charges the business 80 interest on an overdue account. Which control account, and which side?
4. What entries record a contra between the two control accounts?
5. A business pays back 120 to a customer whose account was in credit. Which account, and which side?
6. Opening debit balances 8,200; credit sales 15,000; receipts 13,100; discount allowed 300; returns 500. Find the closing debit balances.
7. Does trade discount appear in a control account?
8. Opening credit balances 6,400; credit purchases 11,800; payments 10,200; discount received 250; returns 600. Find the closing credit balances.
9. What does a debit balance on a supplier's account normally mean?
10. State one advantage and one limitation of producing control accounts from digital records.
11. Must you reconcile a control account balance with the ledger balances?

### Answers

1. Debit side.
2. Purchases journal.
3. Purchases ledger control account, credit side.
4. Debit purchases ledger control, credit sales ledger control, for the amount set off.
5. Sales ledger control account, debit side.
6. 9,300 (8,200 + 15,000 - 13,100 - 300 - 500).
7. No. Only the net invoice value goes through the journals.
8. 7,150 (6,400 + 11,800 - 10,200 - 250 - 600).
9. The supplier owes the business, for example after an overpayment.
10. Advantage: totals available quickly and at any date. Limitation: wrong data entered is still carried through.
11. No. The syllabus says you do not need to.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting a dishonoured customer cheque on the credit side.
- Using cash sales, or trade discount, as if they belonged in the account.
- Entering the contra in only one control account, or on the same side in both.
- Leaving out the opening credit balances (sales ledger) or opening debit balances (purchases ledger).
- Putting a closing credit balance (sales ledger) on the credit side, where it makes the totals disagree.
- Making a balancing error because an item was missed, so the totals do not agree.
- Unlabelled lines. Marks are usually for the item as well as the amount, so give each line a description.
- Describing digital records as removing all errors. Say that input errors remain.
- Writing "trade payables" for the sales ledger control account balance, or the reverse.
