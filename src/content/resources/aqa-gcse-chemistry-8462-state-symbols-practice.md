These questions cover section **4.2.2.2 State symbols** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for
teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher
Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content. Where a question asks you to add symbols, the
equation given is already balanced.

Learn the content first in the [state symbols study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-state-symbols/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-state-symbols-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** Give the meaning of the state symbol (aq). **[1]**
**(b)** Give the state symbol for a gas. **[1]**

**2.** Give the state symbol for each of these substances.

**(a)** Pure water. **[1]**
**(b)** Steam. **[1]**
**(c)** Copper wire. **[1]**

**3.** Hydrochloric acid is added to zinc granules. Hydrogen bubbles off and a solution of zinc chloride forms.
Add state symbols to the equation. **[3]**

Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

**4.** Magnesium ribbon burns in oxygen to form a white solid, magnesium oxide. Add state symbols to the
equation. **[2]**

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

## Section B

**5.** Dilute sulfuric acid is added to black copper(II) oxide powder and warmed until no more of the powder
dissolves. A blue solution of copper(II) sulfate forms. Add state symbols to the equation. **[4]**

CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O

**6.** Solid calcium carbonate is added to hydrochloric acid. The solid disappears and bubbles of carbon dioxide
are seen. Add state symbols to the equation and explain your choice for the symbol on water. **[5]**

CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

**7.** A student writes the equation for the reaction of potassium with water as:

2K(s) + 2H₂O(aq) → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g)

**(a)** Identify the error in the state symbols. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the error is wrong. **[1]**
**(c)** Write the corrected equation. **[1]**

**8.** Molten lead(II) bromide is electrolysed. Liquid lead forms at the negative electrode and bromine vapour
forms at the positive electrode.

**(a)** Add state symbols to the equation. **[3]**

PbBr₂ → Pb + Br₂

**(b)** Explain why the state symbol for lead(II) bromide is not (aq). **[1]**

**9.** Solutions of silver nitrate and sodium chloride are mixed. A white solid forms, and a solution of sodium
nitrate remains.

AgNO₃ + NaCl → AgCl + NaNO₃

**(a)** Add state symbols to the equation. **[4]**
**(b)** State the name given to the white solid that forms from the mixing of two solutions. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how the white solid could be separated from the mixture. **[2]**

**10.** Calcium carbonate is heated strongly in an open crucible: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

**(a)** Add state symbols to the equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the state symbols to explain why the mass of the solid in the crucible falls. **[2]**

**11.** An iron nail is left in copper(II) sulfate solution. The equation is Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu

**(a)** Add state symbols to the equation. **[3]**
**(b)** Name **one** observation that shows a solid has formed. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Aqueous**: dissolved in water [1]
**(b)** **(g)** [1]

**2. (a)** **(l)** [1]
**(b)** **(g)** [1]
**(c)** **(s)** [1]

**3.** Zn**(s)** + 2HCl**(aq)** → ZnCl₂**(aq)** + H₂**(g)**: Zn(s) and HCl(aq) [1]; ZnCl₂(aq) because a solution of the
salt forms [1]; H₂(g) because it bubbles off [1]. **[3]**

**4.** 2Mg**(s)** + O₂**(g)** → 2MgO**(s)** [1] for Mg(s) and O₂(g); [1] for MgO(s). **[2]**

**5.** CuO**(s)** + H₂SO₄**(aq)** → CuSO₄**(aq)** + H₂O**(l)** [1] each: CuO(s); H₂SO₄(aq); CuSO₄(aq) because it
forms a solution; H₂O(l). **[4]**

**6.** CaCO₃**(s)** + 2HCl**(aq)** → CaCl₂**(aq)** + H₂O**(l)** + CO₂**(g)**: CaCO₃(s) and HCl(aq) [1]; CaCl₂(aq) [1];
CO₂(g) [1]; H₂O(l) [1]; explanation: water is a liquid made in the reaction, not a substance dissolved in a
solvent, so it is not (aq) [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** The state symbol of **water** is wrong [1].
**(b)** Water is a pure liquid, not a substance dissolved in water, so it should be (l) [1].
**(c)** 2K(s) + 2H₂O**(l)** → 2KOH(aq) + H₂(g) [1]

**8. (a)** PbBr₂**(l)** → Pb**(l)** + Br₂**(g)** [1] each. **[3]**
**(b)** The compound is **molten**, not dissolved in water [1].

**9. (a)** AgNO₃**(aq)** + NaCl**(aq)** → AgCl**(s)** + NaNO₃**(aq)** [1] each. **[4]**
**(b)** A **precipitate** [1]
**(c)** **Filter** the mixture [1]; the white solid is the **residue** on the filter paper, and the sodium nitrate
solution passes through as the filtrate [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** CaCO₃**(s)** → CaO**(s)** + CO₂**(g)** [1] for the two solids; [1] for the gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Carbon dioxide is a **gas** (g) [1] and escapes from the open crucible, so its mass is no longer in the
solid left behind [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Fe**(s)** + CuSO₄**(aq)** → FeSO₄**(aq)** + Cu**(s)** [1] for Fe(s) and Cu(s); [1] for CuSO₄(aq); [1]
for FeSO₄(aq). **[3]**
**(b)** Any one: a **brown or pink-orange coating** forms on the nail; the nail changes colour [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing (aq) for pure water.
- Using (aq) for molten compounds.
- Leaving a symbol off one formula in a longer equation.
- Marking a dissolved salt (s), or a precipitate (aq).
- Writing the symbol before the formula, or using a different bracket style.
