These notes condense **4.4.1 Reactivity of metals** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for teaching
from September 2016, version 1.2). It is Paper 1 content. Sections 4.4.1.1 to 4.4.1.3 are for both tiers.
**4.4.1.4 Oxidation and reduction in terms of electrons is Higher Tier only (HT only).** For full explanations,
see the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactivity-of-metals/); then practise with the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-reactivity-of-metals-practice/).

## Core facts

- Metals react with oxygen to form **metal oxides**. These are **oxidation** reactions because the metals
  **gain oxygen**. **Reduction** is **loss of oxygen**.
- Metal atoms form **positive ions** when they react. Reactivity is related to the **tendency to form positive
  ions**.
- The **reactivity series** puts metals in order of reactivity. A **more reactive metal can displace a less
  reactive metal** from a compound.
- Unreactive metals such as gold are found **uncombined**. Metals **less reactive than carbon** can be extracted
  from their oxides by **reduction with carbon**.
- (HT only) **Oxidation is loss of electrons and reduction is gain of electrons.**

## The series

Most to least reactive: **K, Na, Li, Ca, Mg, (Al), (C), Zn, Fe, (H), Cu**. Carbon, hydrogen and aluminium are
often included; the metals in the specification are K, Na, Li, Ca, Mg, Zn, Fe and Cu.

| Metal | Water at room temperature | Dilute acid at room temperature |
|---|---|---|
| K | Violent, lilac flame | Do not try |
| Na | Vigorous, melts and fizzes | Do not try |
| Li | Steady fizzing | Do not try |
| Ca | Steady fizzing, cloudy solution | Vigorous |
| Mg | Very slow | Fast fizzing |
| Zn | None | Moderate |
| Fe | None | Slow |
| Cu | None | None |

Equations: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂; Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂; Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂; Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ +
H₂; Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂. Reactions with steam are not required.

## Displacement

| Reaction | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Mg + CuSO₄ → MgSO₄ + Cu | Occurs: Mg is more reactive than Cu |
| Zn + CuSO₄ → ZnSO₄ + Cu | Occurs |
| Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu | Occurs |
| Cu + MgSO₄ | No reaction: Cu is less reactive |

Deducing an order: the metal that displaces the most others is the most reactive.

## Extraction

| Metal oxide | Extract with carbon? | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Fe₂O₃ | Yes | Iron is less reactive than carbon |
| ZnO | Yes | Zinc is less reactive than carbon |
| CuO | Yes | Copper is less reactive than carbon |
| Al₂O₃ | No | Aluminium is more reactive than carbon |
| K₂O | No | Potassium is more reactive than carbon |

Equations: 2Fe₂O₃ + 3C → 4Fe + 3CO₂; 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂. In each, the oxide is reduced (loses oxygen) and the
carbon is oxidised (gains oxygen).

## Redox in terms of electrons (HT only)

| Idea | Example |
|---|---|
| Oxidation: loss of electrons | Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ |
| Reduction: gain of electrons | Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu |
| Ionic equation | Zn + Cu²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Cu |
| Spectator ions | Sulfate ions are unchanged and are left out |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Oxidation | Gain of oxygen (or, HT only, loss of electrons) |
| Reduction | Loss of oxygen (or, HT only, gain of electrons) |
| Reactivity series | Metals listed in order of reactivity |
| Displacement | A more reactive metal takes the place of a less reactive one |
| Ore | A rock containing a metal compound |
| Ionic equation | An equation showing only the ions that take part |
| Spectator ion | An ion present but unchanged |

## Observations to quote

| Reaction | Observations |
|---|---|
| Sodium and water | Fizzes, melts into a ball, moves on the surface, gas given off |
| Calcium and water | Fizzes steadily, solution turns cloudy |
| Magnesium and acid | Fast fizzing, tube warms, magnesium disappears |
| Zinc and acid | Moderate fizzing |
| Iron and acid | Slow fizzing |
| Magnesium and copper(II) sulfate | Blue colour fades; brown solid forms |

## Writing a method to find an order

1. Same-sized pieces of each metal.
2. Same volume and concentration of acid, same starting temperature.
3. Observe the rate of bubbling or measure temperature rise or gas volume.
4. Rank the metals: faster or bigger change means more reactive.
5. A metal with no reaction is less reactive than hydrogen's position.

## Exam traps

- **Oxidation as "gain of electrons".** It is loss.
- **Hydrogen gas bubbles with copper and acid.** Copper does not react.
- **"Iron oxide is oxidised by carbon."** It is reduced.
- **Displacement with a less reactive metal.** It does not occur.
- **Missing charges in half equations (HT only).**
- **Steam reactions.** The specification limits reactions to room temperature.

## Quick self-test

1. What is oxidation in terms of oxygen?
2. What is reduction in terms of oxygen?
3. Name the two products of sodium reacting with water.
4. Which of magnesium and copper is more reactive?
5. What does magnesium do to copper(II) sulfate solution?
6. Why can iron be extracted with carbon?
7. Why can aluminium not be extracted with carbon?
8. In 2CuO + C → 2Cu + CO₂, what is reduced?
9. (HT only) What is oxidation in terms of electrons?
10. (HT only) Write the half equation for zinc forming Zn²⁺.

### Answers

1. Gain of oxygen.
2. Loss of oxygen.
3. Sodium hydroxide and hydrogen.
4. Magnesium.
5. It displaces copper: brown copper forms and the blue colour fades.
6. Iron is less reactive than carbon.
7. Aluminium is more reactive than carbon.
8. Copper oxide.
9. Loss of electrons.
10. Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻.
