All businesses are invented. Figures are in $000 unless stated.

## Section A

**1.** Explain the meaning of *liquidity* and why it is important to a business. **[3]**

**2.** Pentire Homeware has current assets of 1,320, of which inventory is 560, and current liabilities of 800.

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the acid test ratio. **[2]**

**(c)** Comment on the acid test result. **[2]**

**3.** Pentire's revenue is 4,500, gross profit 1,620 and profit from operations 540. Its issued shares are 1,500, reserves 900 and non-current liabilities 1,200.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin and the profit margin. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ROCE and gearing. **[4]**

**4.** Pentire's cost of sales is 2,880. Inventory was 420 at the start of the year and 540 at the end. Trade receivables are 520 and credit sales are 4,000. Trade payables are 310 and credit purchases are 2,950. Calculate the rate of inventory turnover, trade receivables days and trade payables days. **[5]**

**5.** Pentire's profit for the year is 1,050 and dividends are 350. It has 7,000 thousand shares, and the market price is $2.40. Calculate the dividend yield, the dividend cover and the P/E ratio. **[5]**

## Section B

**6.** Two rival bus companies publish these results:

| | Coastway | Moorline |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 12,000 | 9,000 |
| Gross profit | 4,200 | 3,600 |
| Profit from operations | 1,080 | 1,170 |
| Capital employed | 7,200 | 6,500 |
| Non-current liabilities | 1,800 | 3,900 |

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin, profit margin, ROCE and gearing for each company. **[8]**

**(b)** An investor wants a steady return with low risk. Assess which company better suits the investor. **[8]**

**7.** Pentire Homeware's acid test ratio is below 1 : 1 and its receivables days have risen from 35 to 47.5 days. Evaluate **two** methods Pentire could use to improve its liquidity. **[10]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why a business's ROCE might fall even though its profit from operations has risen. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Liquidity is the ability of a business to pay its short-term debts as they fall due [1]. It is important because a business that cannot pay suppliers, wages or loan repayments on time may be refused credit [1] and can fail even if it is profitable [1].

**2. (a)** 1,320 ÷ 800 = **1.65 : 1** [1].

**(b)** (1,320 − 560) ÷ 800 [1] = **0.95 : 1** [1].

**(c)** Pentire has 95 cents of liquid assets for each dollar of current liabilities [1], so it depends on selling inventory to pay all its short-term debts, which may be risky if stock sells slowly [1]. **[5]**

**3. (a)** Gross profit margin = 1,620 ÷ 4,500 × 100 = **36%** [1]. Profit margin = 540 ÷ 4,500 × 100 = **12%** [1].

**(b)** Capital employed = 1,500 + 900 + 1,200 = 3,600 [1]. ROCE = 540 ÷ 3,600 × 100 = **15%** [1]. Gearing = 1,200 ÷ 3,600 × 100 [1] = **33.3%** [1]. **[6]**

**4.** Average inventory = (420 + 540) ÷ 2 = 480 [1]; inventory turnover = 2,880 ÷ 480 = **6 times** [1]. Receivables days = 520 ÷ 4,000 × 365 = **47.5 days** [1] [1]. Payables days = 310 ÷ 2,950 × 365 = **38.4 days** [1].

**5.** EPS = 1,050 ÷ 7,000 = $0.15; dividend per share = 350 ÷ 7,000 = $0.05 [1]. Dividend yield = 0.05 ÷ 2.40 × 100 = **2.1%** [1]. Dividend cover = 1,050 ÷ 350 = **3 times** [1] [1]. P/E = 2.40 ÷ 0.15 = **16** [1].

**6. (a)** Coastway: gross profit margin 35% [1]; profit margin 9% [1]; ROCE 1,080 ÷ 7,200 = 15% [1]; gearing 1,800 ÷ 7,200 = 25% [1]. Moorline: gross profit margin 40% [1]; profit margin 13% [1]; ROCE 1,170 ÷ 6,500 = 18% [1]; gearing 3,900 ÷ 6,500 = 60% [1].

**(b)** Moorline is more profitable: its ROCE of 18% is three points higher [1], and its higher profit margin shows better control of expenses [1]. However, its gearing of 60% means it relies heavily on borrowing [1], so interest is a large fixed commitment and, if passenger numbers fall, profit available to shareholders could fall sharply [1]. Coastway's gearing of 25% is lower risk [1], and it is the larger business by revenue, which may give more stable income [1]. Judgement: for an investor who wants low risk, Coastway is more suitable [1], provided its lower profitability still gives an acceptable return; the decision would also depend on dividend history and share price, which are not given [1]. **[16]**

**7.** Method 1: tighten credit control, for example by chasing overdue accounts and shortening credit terms [1]. Cutting receivables days from 47.5 back to 35 would release cash, improving the acid test [1] [1]. However, customers used to longer credit may go to rivals [1], reducing revenue. Method 2: sell surplus inventory, for example in a clearance sale [1]. This turns stock into cash, raising the acid test directly [1]; but discounts reduce the gross profit margin [1], and selling too much could cause stock-outs [1]. Judgement: credit control is the better first step [1], because the rise in receivables days is the clear cause of the problem and it does not cut margins, as long as Pentire does not lose key customers [1]. **[10]**

**8.** Reason 1: capital employed rose by a larger proportion than profit [1], for example because new loans or shares financed an investment that is not yet earning its full return [1]. Reason 2: reserves grew because profit was retained rather than paid as dividends [1], increasing capital employed so that each dollar of capital earns less unless profit rises by the same proportion [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using closing instead of average inventory.
- Writing the current or acid test ratio as a percentage.
- Forgetting non-current liabilities in capital employed.
- Comparing ratios without saying which matters most to the stakeholder in the question.
- Proposing ways to improve liquidity without the side-effects on profit or customers.
