**These notes summarise Section 4 Agricultural development of Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies
0448 Paper 2.** Learn the farming-system model, the location and needs of cotton, rice, sugar cane
and wheat, the five irrigation methods, the causes and cures of waterlogging and salinity, and the
main government measures. Each fact below is something Paper 2 can ask you to name, explain or
evaluate.

## Syllabus coverage

**0448 Paper 2, Section 4 (2026 syllabus)**
- **4(a)** Subsistence, cash crop and livestock farming as input–process–output systems
- **4(b)** Locations and uses of cotton, rice, sugar cane, wheat; livestock products; the twelve minor crops and fruits
- **4(c)** Natural and human factors; subsistence examples (traditional rice, barani wheat, karez oasis); kharif and rabi cash crops; livestock and transhumance; irrigation types; waterlogging and salinity; government action; sustainability

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Subsistence farming | Growing food mainly for the farmer's own family |
| Cash crop | A crop grown for sale, such as cotton or sugar cane |
| Kharif | Summer-sown crop harvested in autumn |
| Rabi | Winter-sown crop harvested in spring |
| Barani | Rain-fed farming without irrigation |
| Fragmented holding | A farm split into scattered small plots |
| Karez | Underground channel carrying groundwater by gravity |
| Perennial canal | Canal fed from a barrage, with water all year |
| Inundation canal | Canal that flows only when the river floods |
| Tubewell | Pump drawing water from a deep well |
| Link canal | Canal transferring water from one river to another |
| Bund | Low earth bank holding water on a field |
| Waterlogging | Soil saturated because the water table has risen near the surface |
| Salinity | Salt left on or in the soil after water evaporates |
| Transhumance | Seasonal movement of herds between summer and winter pastures |

## The four main crops

| Crop | Season | Main areas | Needs | Uses |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cotton | Kharif | Southern Punjab, central Sindh | Long, hot, sunny summer; frost-free; dry at picking; irrigation | Lint for textiles; seed for oil and cattle cake |
| Rice | Kharif | Gujranwala–Sialkot rice belt; northern Sindh | High temperature; standing water; clay soil | Food; export (basmati) |
| Sugar cane | Kharif | Central Punjab, central Sindh, Peshawar valley | Heat; long season; heavy water | Sugar, gur; molasses; bagasse fuel |
| Wheat | Rabi | Irrigated Punjab and Sindh; barani Potwar | Cool growing season, warm dry ripening | Staple flour; straw for fodder |

## Livestock

- **Buffalo and cattle:** milk, meat, hides for leather, dung for fuel and manure, draught power.
- **Goats and sheep:** meat, milk, wool and hair, skins; herded in Balochistan and the north.
- **Poultry:** eggs and meat; commercial farms near large cities.

## Irrigation at a glance

- **Canal:** inundation (flood season only) and perennial (all year, from barrages); karez (underground, Balochistan).
- **Lift:** Persian wheel (animal-powered) and tubewell (pumped).
- **Structures:** dam (store) → barrage (divert) → link canal (transfer) → distribution canal → field channel → bund.

## Waterlogging and salinity

**Causes:** seepage from unlined canals; over-irrigation; poor drainage; flat land; then high
evaporation draws salt to the surface.
**Effects:** lower yields, abandoned land, lost income.
**Restoration:** tubewells to lower the water table; surface and tile drains; flushing salts;
salt-tolerant crops.
**Prevention:** lining canals and watercourses; measured irrigation; drainage built into schemes.

## Government action

- Land reforms: ceilings on holding size and redistribution to tenants.
- Agricultural extension and training.
- Loans and subsidies for tractors, tubewells, fertiliser and pesticides.
- High-yielding seed varieties and research.
- **Evaluate:** benefits often reach larger farmers first; chemical inputs raise costs and pollute; fragmentation continues through inheritance.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why yields of rice on small, fragmented holdings are often low. **[4]**

**Model answer:** The plots are small and scattered, so time is wasted moving between them and
machinery cannot be used efficiently [1]. Farmers lack capital, so they cannot afford enough
fertiliser, pesticide or improved seed [1]. Traditional methods such as ox ploughing and hand
transplanting are slow [1]. Water supply may be unreliable at the tail end of canals, and pests and
diseases reduce the harvest [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Swapping kharif and rabi.** Wheat is the rabi crop; cotton, rice and sugar cane are kharif.
- **Saying "Punjab" for every crop.** Give a more precise area, such as the rice belt or southern Punjab for cotton.
- **Explaining salinity without waterlogging.** Salinity follows from a high water table plus evaporation; show the sequence.
- **Listing irrigation methods without assessing them.** The syllabus asks for advantages and disadvantages for both small farms and cash crops.
- **One-sided government answers.** Evaluate who benefited and what problems remain.

## How is this section examined?

This content is examined on Cambridge Paper 2, The environment of Pakistan (1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, 50% of the qualification). You answer three questions from a choice of five; each is worth 25 marks and has four parts, (a) to (d), built around resources such as maps, graphs, tables and photographs. Part (d) specifically tests analysis and evaluation, so finish every revision topic by practising a judgement: for example, how sustainable a resource or a policy is, with evidence for and against.

## Land reforms to name

| Reform | Leader | Ceiling on ownership |
|---|---|---|
| 1959 | Ayub Khan | 500 acres irrigated or 1,000 acres unirrigated |
| 1972 | Zulfikar Ali Bhutto | 150 acres irrigated or 300 acres unirrigated |
| 1977 | Zulfikar Ali Bhutto | 100 acres irrigated or 200 acres unirrigated |

Land above the ceiling was to be redistributed, but loopholes (transfers to relatives) limited the effect.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which crops are recognised from photographs?** Fields of cotton, rice, sugar cane and wheat, and
buffalo, cattle, goats, sheep and poultry.

**Why is barani farming risky?** It depends entirely on rain, so a dry year can ruin the wheat crop
and farmers cannot plan investment.

**Is the tubewell good or bad?** Both. It supplies water when needed and lowers a high water table,
but running costs are high and over-pumping can deplete groundwater.
