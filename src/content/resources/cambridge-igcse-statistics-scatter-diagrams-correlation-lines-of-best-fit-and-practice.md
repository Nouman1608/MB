## Questions

**1.** For each pair, state which variable is the independent variable.

**(a)** The speed of a car and its stopping distance. **[1]**
**(b)** The number of adverts shown and the number of sales made. **[1]**
**(c)** The number of days since a seed was planted and the height of the plant. **[1]**

**2.** The table shows the distance from a city centre and the monthly rent for six flats.

| Distance (km) | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rent (£) | 1380 | 1250 | 1090 | 960 | 790 | 680 |

**(a)** State the type of correlation. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe what this means for the flats. **[1]**
**(c)** State which variable is independent. **[1]**

**3.** State the type of correlation you would expect for each.

**(a)** The maximum daily temperature and the number of ice lollies sold at a kiosk. **[1]**
**(b)** The length of a person's surname and their height. **[1]**
**(c)** The age of a car and its value. **[1]**
**(d)** Points forming a narrow band that rises from bottom left to top right. **[1]**

**4.** A gardener gives eight identical plants different amounts of water each day (x ml) and measures the growth in a week (y mm).

| x (ml) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 | 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y (mm) | 12 | 18 | 21 | 27 | 10 | 34 | 38 | 43 |

**(a)** Plot a scatter diagram on graph paper. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify the point that does not fit the pattern. **[1]**

**5.** A courier records the distance (x km) and the delivery charge (y pounds) for several deliveries between 20 km and 80 km. A line of best fit passes through (20, 31) and (80, 73).

**(a)** Find the gradient of the line. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line in the form y = mx + c. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the charge for a delivery of 50 km. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the distance for a charge of 45 pounds. **[2]**

**6.** A kiosk records the hours of sunshine (x) and the ice lollies sold (y) on six days, in this order.

| x | 8 | 2 | 12 | 6 | 4 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 31 | 15 | 45 | 24 | 21 | 38 |

**(a)** Use the method of semi-averages to find the two points through which the line of best fit passes. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line. **[3]**
**(c)** Estimate the sales on a day with 7 hours of sunshine. **[1]**

**7.** A café uses the line y = 4x − 12 to estimate the cups of iced tea sold (y) from the midday temperature in °C (x). The line was fitted to data with x between 10 and 28.

**(a)** Estimate the sales when x = 20. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the sales when x = 35, and say whether this estimate is more or less reliable than the one in part (a). **[2]**
**(c)** Find the value of y when x = 2 and comment. **[2]**
**(d)** State one assumption made when using the line. **[1]**

**8.** The line of best fit y = 12x + 150 estimates the cost in pounds (y) of a party from the number of guests (x). The data covered 20 to 100 guests.

**(a)** Explain what the gradient 12 means here. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what the value 150 means here, and comment on it. **[2]**

**9.** A park manager records the midday temperature (x °C) and the number of visitors (y) on ten days.

| x | 10 | 3 | 15 | 7 | 2 | 13 | 8 | 16 | 5 | 11 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 64 | 48 | 74 | 56 | 46 | 70 | 57 | 76 | 53 | 66 |

**(a)** Plot a scatter diagram. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**
**(c)** Use semi-averages to find the two points on the line of best fit. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the equation of the line in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(e)** Estimate the visitors when x = 12. **[1]**
**(f)** Explain why an estimate for x = 30 would be unreliable. **[1]**

**10.** Describe the correlation in each table, where x = 1, 2, 3, ... 8 in order.

| Table | y values |
|---|---|
| P | 5, 7, 9, 12, 14, 16, 19, 21 |
| R | 16, 12, 17, 9, 15, 10, 13, 11 |
| S | 10, 4, 12, 5, 11, 3, 12, 6 |

**(a)** Table P. **[1]**
**(b)** Table R. **[1]**
**(c)** Table S. **[1]**
**(d)** State which table would give the most reliable estimates from a line of best fit, with a reason. **[1]**

**11.** A restaurant records the number of staff on duty (x) and the customers served per hour (y). The pairs were written down in this order: (6, 22), (2, 11), (9, 30), (4, 18), (1, 8), (8, 28), (3, 15), (7, 24). Sam splits them into the first four and the last four, and finds the mean point of each.

**(a)** Explain Sam's mistake. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the two correct semi-average points. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the line of best fit. **[3]**
**(d)** Estimate y when x = 10. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Speed. [1] (b) The number of adverts. [1] (c) The number of days. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the variable, not just "the first one". Say which value you would use to predict the other and the answer is usually clear.

**2. (a)** Strong negative correlation. [1]
**(b)** Flats further from the centre tend to have lower rent. [1]
**(c)** Distance from the city centre. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A description needs both direction and strength, and a context statement shows you know what the correlation means. "Negative" on its own is incomplete.

**3. (a)** Positive. [1] **(b)** No correlation. [1] **(c)** Negative. [1] **(d)** Strong positive. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "None" is a different answer from "weak". If there is no reason for the variables to be related, say no correlation.

**4. (a)** Axes labelled with units and scales that use the grid. [1] At least six points plotted accurately. [1] All eight points plotted accurately. [1]
**(b)** Positive correlation (strong apart from one point). [1]
**(c)** The point (50, 10). [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Plot with small, exact crosses and check each one against the table. Reading a scale wrongly by one square is an easy way to lose credit on a plot.

**5. (a)** Gradient = (73 − 31)/(80 − 20). [1] = 42/60 = **0.7**. [1]
**(b)** 31 = 0.7 × 20 + c, so c = 31 − 14 = 17. [1] **y = 0.7x + 17**. [1]
**(c)** y = 0.7 × 50 + 17 = **52** pounds. [1]
**(d)** 45 = 0.7x + 17, so 0.7x = 28. [1] x = **40 km**. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtraction and the substitution. Clear working for each part makes your method visible even if an earlier value is wrong.

**6. (a)** Sorted by x: (2, 15), (4, 21), (6, 24) | (8, 31), (10, 38), (12, 45). [1] Lower mean = (4, 20). [1] Upper mean = (10, 38). [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (38 − 20)/(10 − 4) = 18/6 = **3**. [1] 20 = 3 × 4 + c, so c = 8. [1] **y = 3x + 8**. [1]
**(c)** y = 3 × 7 + 8 = **29** ice lollies. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the sorted list before taking means. It shows the split is correct, and the question gives the data out of order on purpose.

**7. (a)** y = 4 × 20 − 12 = **68** cups. [1]
**(b)** y = 4 × 35 − 12 = **128** cups. [1] Less reliable, because 35 is outside the range of the data (10 to 28), so it is extrapolation. [1]
**(c)** y = 4 × 2 − 12 = **−4**. [1] Sales cannot be negative, so the line does not work outside its range. [1]
**(d)** Any one: the straight-line pattern continues; the sample represents all days; other factors such as weather are unchanged. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "It is further away" is a weaker reason than "outside the range of the data". Link the reason to the range given in the question.

**8. (a)** Each extra guest adds about 12 pounds to the cost. [1] The cost is estimated to rise by 12 pounds per guest. [1]
**(b)** 150 pounds is the estimated cost with no guests, a fixed cost. [1] No guests is outside the data (20 to 100 guests), so this value should be treated with care. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a rate with units for the gradient, and say what the intercept means in the real situation. Answers that say only "the y-intercept" give the marker nothing to credit.

**9. (a)** Axes labelled with suitable scales. [1] At least eight points correct. [1] All ten points correct. [1]
**(b)** Strong positive correlation. [1]
**(c)** Sorted by x: (2, 46), (3, 48), (5, 53), (7, 56), (8, 57) | (10, 64), (11, 66), (13, 70), (15, 74), (16, 76). [1] Lower mean: x = 25/5 = 5, y = 260/5 = 52, so (5, 52). [1] Upper mean: x = 65/5 = 13, y = 350/5 = 70, so (13, 70). [1]
**(d)** Gradient = (70 − 52)/(13 − 5) = 18/8 = **2.25**. [1] 52 = 2.25 × 5 + c, so c = 52 − 11.25 = 40.75. [1] **y = 2.25x + 40.75**. [1]
**(e)** y = 2.25 × 12 + 40.75 = 67.75, so about **68 visitors**. [1]
**(f)** 30 °C is far outside the data (2 to 16 °C), so this is extrapolation and the pattern may not continue. [1] **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* In a long question, label each part and show the sorted list and both mean points. A slip in part (c) is easier to follow when the working is laid out.

**10. (a)** Strong positive correlation. [1]
**(b)** Weak negative correlation. [1]
**(c)** No correlation. [1]
**(d)** Table P, because its points lie close to a straight line. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For tables, look at the general direction of y as x increases, then at how steady it is. Table R falls overall but jumps about, so it is weak rather than strong.

**11. (a)** The pairs must first be sorted by x, so the groups should be the four smallest and four largest x-values. [1]
**(b)** Lower group (1, 8), (2, 11), (3, 15), (4, 18): mean = (2.5, 13). [1] Upper group (6, 22), (7, 24), (8, 28), (9, 30): mean = (7.5, 26). [1]
**(c)** Gradient = (26 − 13)/(7.5 − 2.5) = 13/5 = **2.6**. [1] 13 = 2.6 × 2.5 + c, so c = 6.5. [1] **y = 2.6x + 6.5**. [1]
**(d)** y = 2.6 × 10 + 6.5 = **32.5** customers per hour. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The explanation in part (a) needs to say that the data must be ordered by the x-values, not just that Sam's groups are wrong.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only "positive" or "negative" without strength, or without context.
- Plotting points inaccurately, or using a scale that wastes the grid.
- Joining points with a zigzag instead of drawing one ruled straight line.
- Not sorting by x before splitting the data into halves.
- Subtracting in different orders in the gradient and getting the wrong sign.
- Giving an extrapolated estimate with no comment, or not noticing when it is impossible.
- Giving a gradient meaning with no units or no context.
