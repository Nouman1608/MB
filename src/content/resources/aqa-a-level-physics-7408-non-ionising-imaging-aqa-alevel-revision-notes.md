These notes condense section **3.10.4 Non-ionising imaging** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408): ultrasound, fibre optics and endoscopy, and the MR scanner. It is part of the A-level only Medical physics option. ## Equations

| Equation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Z = ρc | Acoustic impedance | kg m⁻² s⁻¹ |
| I_r/I_i = ((Z₂ − Z₁)/(Z₂ + Z₁))² | Fraction reflected at a boundary | Normal incidence; transmitted = 1 − reflected |
| depth = ct/2 | Echo time to depth | t is the round-trip time |
| λ = c/f | Wavelength in tissue | Sets the finest detail |
| sin c = n₂/n₁ | Critical angle at core–cladding boundary | n₁ core > n₂ cladding |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Acoustic impedance | Product of density and speed of sound; decides reflection at a boundary |
| Attenuation | Loss of intensity with distance by absorption and scattering; greater at higher frequency |
| Piezoelectric effect | Crystal deforms when a p.d. is applied and produces a p.d. when deformed |
| Backing material | Damps the crystal so pulses are short |
| Coupling gel | Z close to skin; removes the air layer |
| A-scan | Echo amplitude against time along one line |
| B-scan | Swept probe; echo strength shown as dot brightness to form a 2D image |
| Coherent bundle | Fibres in the same relative positions at both ends; carries the image |
| Non-coherent bundle | Fibres randomly arranged; carries light in |
| Precession | Rotation of a proton's spin axis about the magnetic field lines |
| Gradient coils | Vary the field across the patient to select the region scanned |

## Ultrasound in one list

- Large Z difference → strong echo; gas and bone reflect most of the pulse, so they block imaging beyond them.
- Soft tissue boundaries reflect about 1%: most energy continues to deeper boundaries.
- Gel replaces air (air–skin reflects about 99.9%).
- Higher f: better resolution, more attenuation, less depth. Lower f: deeper, coarser.
- Transducer: alternating p.d. burst → vibration → pulse; damping → short pulse; echoes → p.d.
- Next pulse only after the deepest echo returns.
- Advantages: non-ionising, cheap, portable, real-time. Disadvantages: lower resolution, no imaging through bone or gas, operator-dependent.

## Endoscope in one list

- Total internal reflection at the core–cladding interface keeps light in the core.
- Cladding: lower refractive index, protects the core surface, prevents leakage between fibres.
- Non-coherent bundle: light in. Coherent bundle: image out.
- Objective lens forms the image on the bundle end; eyepiece or camera views it.
- Thinner fibres → finer resolution.
- Channels for air, water and instruments → keyhole surgery: small incisions, faster recovery, lower infection risk.

## MR scanner in six steps

1. Superconducting magnet gives a strong field.
2. Protons align with spins parallel and precess about the field lines.
3. Gradient coils select the cross-section.
4. Short RF pulses excite protons in successive small regions (change of spin state).
5. Protons de-excite and emit RF signals.
6. Signals detected and processed by computer into an image.

Not examined: how the fields are produced, relaxation times.

## Comparison table (resolution, convenience, safety)

| Method | Resolution | Convenience | Safety |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ultrasound | Lower | Cheap, portable, live | Non-ionising |
| Endoscopy | Direct view of surfaces | Invasive but minor | Non-ionising; small infection risk |
| MR | High, excellent soft tissue | Expensive, slow, noisy, confined | Non-ionising; metal implants dangerous |

## Exam traps

- Depth = ct, missing the 2.
- Squaring only the numerator in the reflection formula.
- Saying gel has "low impedance"; it has impedance **close to skin**.
- Swapping the roles of the coherent and non-coherent bundles.
- Saying MR images electrons or uses X-rays.
- Saying lower frequency gives better resolution.

## Self-test

1. Calculate Z for tissue with density 1040 kg m⁻³ and speed of sound 1570 m s⁻¹.
2. Calculate the fraction reflected at a fat (Z = 1.38 × 10⁶) to blood (Z = 1.66 × 10⁶) boundary.
3. An echo returns after 130 µs (c = 1540 m s⁻¹). How deep is the boundary?
4. Calculate the wavelength of 5.0 MHz ultrasound in tissue (c = 1540 m s⁻¹).
5. Why is backing material placed behind the piezoelectric crystal?
6. State the difference between an A-scan and a B-scan.
7. Why can ultrasound not image the lungs?
8. Calculate the critical angle for a core of n = 1.52 and cladding of n = 1.46.
9. Why must the image-carrying bundle be coherent?
10. What is the role of the gradient coils in an MR scanner?
11. What do protons emit as they de-excite?
12. Give one safety disadvantage of MR scanning.

### Answers

1. 1040 × 1570 = **1.63 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹**.
2. (0.28/3.04)² = **8.5 × 10⁻³** (0.85%).
3. 1540 × 130 × 10⁻⁶/2 = **0.100 m**.
4. 1540/5.0 × 10⁶ = **3.1 × 10⁻⁴ m** (0.31 mm).
5. To **damp** the vibration so each pulse is **short**, letting close boundaries be distinguished.
6. A-scan: **amplitude against time** along one line, probe fixed. B-scan: probe swept, echoes shown as **dots of varying brightness** forming a 2D image.
7. The lungs contain **air**, whose impedance is very different from tissue, so almost all the ultrasound is reflected.
8. sin c = 1.46/1.52, so **c = 73.8°**.
9. Each fibre must arrive at the **same relative position** at the viewing end, so the pattern of light (the image) is preserved.
10. They make the field **vary across the patient**, selecting the cross-section and the small regions excited in turn.
11. **Radio-frequency (RF) signals**.
12. The strong field can move or heat **metal implants** or affect pacemakers (also: confined space).

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does higher frequency ultrasound give better images of shallow structures?**
Higher frequency means a shorter wavelength, so finer detail is resolved. Attenuation also rises with frequency, so high frequencies are used only where the structure is close to the probe.

**What is the difference between coherent and non-coherent fibre bundles?**
A coherent bundle keeps each fibre in the same relative position at both ends, so it can carry an image. A non-coherent bundle has randomly arranged fibres and only carries light into the body.
