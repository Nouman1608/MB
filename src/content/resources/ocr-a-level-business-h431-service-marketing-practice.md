## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State the three additional elements of the marketing mix used for services. **[3]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by perishability in the context of a service. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** way in which physical evidence helps a new service business to attract customers. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**4.** Quillon Dental has 40 appointment slots a day. The average fee is £70 and 70% of slots are used.

**(a)** Calculate the daily revenue. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the revenue lost each day from unused slots. **[2]**

**(c)** Quillon offers a 20% discount on afternoon slots and fills 5 extra slots. The variable cost of an appointment is £12. Calculate the extra contribution. **[3]**

**(d)** Evaluate whether off-peak discounts are a good way for Quillon Dental to deal with perishability. **[6]**

**5.** A Quillon Dental reception desk has 3 staff. Each check-in takes 8 minutes. At the busiest hour, 36 patients arrive.

**(a)** Calculate the number of check-ins the desk can complete in an hour. **[2]**

**(b)** A self-service kiosk cuts the check-in time to 2 minutes. Calculate the new capacity per hour. **[2]**

**(c)** Evaluate the importance of process to Quillon Dental. **[9]**

**6.** Quillon spends £12,000 training 20 staff in patient care. Staff turnover is expected to fall from 35% to 25%, and each leaver costs £2,500 to replace. Better service is expected to keep 4% more of its 5,000 patients, and each retained patient brings £50 contribution.

**(a)** Calculate the saving from lower staff turnover. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the extra contribution from retained patients. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the net gain from the training. **[1]**

**(d)** Evaluate the importance of people in the marketing of a service such as dental care. **[9]**

**7.** Kestrel Fitness plans to open a small, high-priced gym that offers personal training. It will use online booking and have 12 trainers. Recommend and justify a marketing mix for this service. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Process [1], people [1], physical evidence [1]. **[3]**

**2.** A service cannot be stored for later sale [1], so an unused appointment or seat is lost revenue that cannot be recovered [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Customers cannot see or try a service before buying, so they look for visible clues [1]; a clean, professional building, a good website and clear branding signal quality and reliability [1], which reduces the customer's perceived risk and builds trust before they have used the service [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** 40 × 0.70 = 28 slots [1]; 28 × 70 = **£1,960** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Unused slots = 12 [1]; 12 × 70 = **£840** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Discounted price = 70 × 0.80 = £56 [1]. Contribution per appointment = 56 − 12 = £44 [1]. Extra contribution = 5 × 44 = **£220** [1]. **[3]**

**(d)** For: unused slots earn nothing, so any price above the variable cost of £12 adds profit [1]; the £220 daily gain is certain once patients book [1]; the discount can be limited to quiet afternoons, which protects full-price slots [1].
Against: regular patients may move to the cheaper slots, reducing the gain [1]; frequent discounts may weaken the image of quality, which matters for a health service [1].
Judgement: an off-peak discount is a sensible tool if limited to certain times and new patients and monitored to check that full-price bookings do not fall [1]. **[6]**

**5. (a)** 60 ÷ 8 = 7.5 per staff member [1]; 3 × 7.5 = **22.5 check-ins** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 60 ÷ 2 = 30 per staff member [1]; 3 × 30 = **90 check-ins** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** At present capacity is 22.5 against 36 arrivals, so about 13.5 patients wait for each hour at the peak [1]; long waits cause complaints and may lead patients to change dentist [1]. A kiosk lifts capacity to 90, which removes the queue without extra staff [1]; staff can then spend time helping nervous patients [1]. Quicker check-in also helps keep appointments on time, so that the dentists' time is used well.
Limits: the kiosk costs money and may fail [1]; older patients may find it hard to use, so some still need staff help [1]; process is only one factor, since patients also care about the quality of treatment and the manner of staff [1].
Judgement: process is important at peak times because waiting is a main cause of dissatisfaction in services, but it should be balanced with the human side of care [2]. **[9]**

**6. (a)** Leavers fall from 7 (20 × 0.35) to 5 (20 × 0.25) [2: 1 for each], saving 2 × 2,500 = **£5,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** 5,000 × 0.04 = 200 patients [1]; 200 × 50 = **£10,000** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** 5,000 + 10,000 − 12,000 = **£3,000** [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** People are important because the patient meets the dentist and staff, so they are part of the product [1]; friendly, skilled staff increase satisfaction, recommendations and retention [1]; consistent, trained staff reduce variability, which is a feature of services [1]; stable staff reduce recruitment costs and give continuity [1].
Limits: training costs money and results are uncertain; if retention rises by only 1 point, the benefit would be £5,000 + £2,500 = £7,500 and the training would make a loss [1]; pay, workload and morale also affect behaviour [1]; patients also value clinical quality and price [1].
Judgement: people are among the most important elements for dental care as it is personal and trust-based, but benefits depend on motivation as well as training [2]. **[9]**

**7.** Product: personal training with a defined programme and a progress guarantee [1]. Price: a premium price that signals quality, with off-peak rates to fill quiet periods [1]. Place: a convenient location with online booking [1]. Promotion: free trial sessions, success stories and social media to make the service visible [1]. Process: easy online booking, clear first-session assessment and quick handling of complaints [1]. People: careful selection and training of the 12 trainers, since they are the product [1]. Physical evidence: a clean, well-equipped gym, branded uniforms and a professional website to match the price [1].
Justification: a high price needs high quality, so people and physical evidence matter most [1]. Risk: if trainers leave, clients may follow them, so rewards and a consistent method are needed. Judgement: the seven elements must be consistent with each other, and people are the most important [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming the extra Ps without applying them to the service.
- Forgetting that unused capacity cannot be stored.
- Treating the benefit of training as certain.
- Giving a mix that leaves out process, people or physical evidence.
