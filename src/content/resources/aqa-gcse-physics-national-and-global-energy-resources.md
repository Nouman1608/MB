This guide covers sub-topic **4.1.3 National and global energy
resources**, the third of three sub-topics in **Topic 4.1 Energy**, from
the AQA GCSE Physics (8463) specification (first teaching September
2016). This sub-topic is flagged **"physics only"** in the official
specification — it is not shared with GCSE Combined Science: Trilogy.

## Before studying this

Read [Conservation and Dissipation of Energy](/resources/aqa-gcse-physics-conservation-and-dissipation-of-energy/)
first for the underlying ideas about useful and wasted energy transfer.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE PHYSICS (8463) — Sub-topic 4.1.3 (physics only)**

Students should be able to describe the main energy sources available
and to distinguish between energy resources that are renewable and
energy resources that are non-renewable. Students should be able to
describe the uses of energy resources for the generation of electricity,
for heating (including in industry, transport, and in the domestic
context of the National Grid), and understand that different energy
resources are more or less reliable than others.

Students should be able to give a broad, general description of the
main energy sources available for use on Earth, including: fossil fuels
(coal, oil and gas), nuclear fuel, bio-fuel, wind, hydro-electricity,
geothermal, the tides, the Sun, and water waves. Students should be able
to describe the main uses of these energy resources, and distinguish
between them in terms of renewable and non-renewable.

Students should be able to describe the environmental issues that may
arise from the use of different energy resources, and consider patterns
and trends in the use of energy resources — for example, that the use of
fossil fuels for the generation of electricity in the UK has been
decreasing and the use of some renewable resources for the generation of
electricity has been increasing over recent decades. Students should
also be able to describe the environmental, economic and social issues
regarding energy resources.

## Renewable vs. non-renewable resources

A **non-renewable** energy resource is one that will run out — it is
being used up faster than it can be replaced. A **renewable** energy
resource is one that is being (or can be) replenished as it is used, so
it will not run out. The main energy resources available for use on
Earth are:

- **Non-renewable**: fossil fuels (coal, oil, gas), nuclear fuel
- **Renewable**: bio-fuel, wind, hydro-electricity, geothermal, the tides, the Sun (solar), and water waves

## Uses of energy resources

Energy resources are used for **generating electricity**, for **heating**
(in industry, homes, and via district or National Grid systems), and for
**transport** (fuels for vehicles). Resources vary in their reliability —
for example, wind and solar depend on weather conditions and are less
consistently available than fossil fuels or nuclear power, which can
generate reliably on demand.

## Environmental, economic and social issues

Different energy resources bring different environmental issues: fossil
fuel combustion releases carbon dioxide (contributing to climate change)
and other pollutants; nuclear power produces radioactive waste requiring
long-term storage; large-scale hydro-electric or wind installations can
affect land use and local ecosystems. There are also economic
considerations (cost of infrastructure, fuel availability, and running
costs) and social considerations (visual impact, local opposition,
energy security, and job creation).

A key trend to know: **the use of fossil fuels for electricity generation
in the UK has been decreasing, while the use of some renewable resources
has been increasing**, over recent decades — reflecting national and
international policy responses to climate change and energy security.

**Worked example.** A power station burns coal to generate electricity.
State whether this is a renewable or non-renewable resource, and give one
environmental issue associated with its use.

Coal is a **non-renewable** resource (a fossil fuel). One environmental
issue: burning coal releases carbon dioxide into the atmosphere,
contributing to climate change (other valid answers include air
pollution or acid rain from sulfur dioxide emissions).

## Common mistakes

Confusing "renewable" with "clean" — a resource can be renewable but
still have environmental impacts (e.g. hydro-electric dams affecting
river ecosystems). Forgetting that nuclear fuel is non-renewable despite
not being a fossil fuel. Giving vague environmental answers without
naming a specific issue (e.g. "it's bad for the environment" rather than
"it releases CO2, contributing to climate change").

## Quick revision checklist

- List the main energy resources and classify each as renewable or non-renewable.
- Describe the uses of energy resources: electricity generation, heating, transport.
- Compare reliability between different energy resources.
- Describe environmental, economic and social issues linked to specific resources.
- State the UK trend: decreasing fossil fuel use, increasing renewable use.
