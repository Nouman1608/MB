## Questions

**1.** Ravi sent these numbers of texts over eight days: 14, 9, 22, 9, 17, 11, 9, 19. Work out the mean, the median, the mode and the range. **[4]**

**2.** The mean of five numbers is 12.

**(a)** Four of the numbers are 7, 15, 9 and 14. Find the fifth number. **[2]**
**(b)** A sixth number is added and the mean of the six numbers is 13. Find the sixth number. **[2]**

**3.** Waiting times at a clinic, in minutes, for 50 patients:

| Time w | 0 < w ≤ 5 | 5 < w ≤ 10 | 10 < w ≤ 15 | 15 < w ≤ 20 | 20 < w ≤ 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 9 | 18 | 14 | 7 | 2 |

**(a)** State the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Work out an estimate for the mean waiting time. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why your answer to (b) is an estimate. **[1]**
**(d)** Estimate the median waiting time. **[3]**

**4.** Cafe A has a mean of 64 customers a day and a range of 58. Cafe B has a mean of 59 customers a day and a range of 21. Compare the two cafes. **[3]**

**5.** Prices of six flats, in £1000s: 210, 225, 230, 240, 255, 820.

**(a)** Find the mean and the median. **[2]**
**(b)** Which is the better summary of a typical price? Give a reason. **[1]**

**6.** A bar chart shows visitors to a museum in January, February and March as 1950, 2000 and 2050. The vertical axis starts at 1900 and has no label.

**(a)** Give two reasons why the chart is misleading. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the percentage increase in visitors from January to March, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows minutes of screen time before bed (x) and hours of sleep (y) for eight people.

| x | 10 | 30 | 45 | 60 | 80 | 100 | 120 | 150 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 8.9 | 8.6 | 8.4 | 8.1 | 7.8 | 7.4 | 6.9 | 6.4 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation in context. **[2]**
**(b)** A line of best fit passes through (30, 8.7) and (150, 6.3). Estimate the hours of sleep for someone with 90 minutes of screen time. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the line to estimate the sleep for 300 minutes of screen time. Comment on the reliability of this estimate. **[3]**

**8.** A newspaper reports a positive correlation between daily umbrella sales and the number of road accidents. It says that carrying umbrellas causes accidents. Explain why this conclusion is not justified. **[2]**

**9.** Seven sprinters' times, in seconds, are 12.1, 12.4, 11.9, 12.6, 12.2, 1.25 and 12.3.

**(a)** Write down the outlier. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest why it may have been recorded. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the mean of the seven values as recorded, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the mean if the outlier should have been 12.5, to 2 decimal places. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** The time (t minutes) 60 students spent on homework is shown on a cumulative frequency graph. It passes through (0, 0), (20, 5), (40, 14), (60, 33), (80, 49), (100, 57) and (120, 60). Treat the graph as straight lines between points.

**(a)** Estimate the lower quartile, the median and the upper quartile, to the nearest minute. **[3]**
**(b)** Work out the interquartile range. **[1]**
**(c)** How many students spent more than 100 minutes? **[1]**
**(d)** Another class has a median of 65 minutes and an interquartile range of 24 minutes. Compare the two classes. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Times, in seconds, for nine runners in team A: 11.8, 12.1, 12.4, 12.6, 12.9, 13.1, 13.5, 14.2, 15.0. Team B has minimum 11.5, lower quartile 12.0, median 12.5, upper quartile 13.0 and maximum 14.0.

**(a)** Find the five-number summary for team A. **[3]**
**(b)** Draw a box plot for each team on the same scale. **[2]**
**(c)** Compare the teams using the median and the interquartile range. **[2]**

**12.** Distances travelled to work by 20 + x people:

| Distance d km | 0 < d ≤ 10 | 10 < d ≤ 20 | 20 < d ≤ 30 | 30 < d ≤ 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | x | 10 | 4 |

An estimate of the mean distance is 18 km. Find x. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** In order: 9, 9, 9, 11, 14, 17, 19, 22. Sum = 110 so mean = 110 ÷ 8 = **13.75** [1]. Median = (11 + 14) ÷ 2 = **12.5** [1]. Mode = **9** [1]. Range = 22 − 9 = **13** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Sort the data before taking the median. Writing the ordered list shows your method even if you slip on the arithmetic.

**2. (a)** Total = 5 × 12 = 60 [1]. Fifth = 60 − (7 + 15 + 9 + 14) = 60 − 45 = **15** [1]
**(b)** Total of six = 6 × 13 = 78 [1]. Sixth = 78 − 60 = **18** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Work from the totals (mean × count). Subtracting means directly is a common slip that gives no useful working.

**3. (a)** **5 < w ≤ 10** [1]
**(b)** Midpoints 2.5, 7.5, 12.5, 17.5, 22.5 [1]. Sum of fx = 22.5 + 135 + 175 + 122.5 + 45 = 500 [1]. Mean = 500 ÷ 50 = **10 minutes** [1]
**(c)** The exact waiting times are not known, only the class each lies in, so midpoints are used [1]
**(d)** The median is the 25th value. Running totals are 9, 27, ... so it is in 5 < w ≤ 10 [1]. It is the 16th of 18 values in that class, so 5 + (16/18) × 5 [1] = **9.4 minutes** (1 d.p.) [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the midpoints and the column of products. Divide by the total frequency, not the class count.

**4.** Cafe A has the higher mean, so it has more customers on a typical day [1]. Cafe B has the smaller range, so its daily numbers are more consistent [1]. Cafe A's customer numbers vary much more from day to day (range 58 against 21) [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs context and one statement about average and one about spread. Quoting the numbers without saying what they mean is not a comparison.

**5. (a)** Sum = 1980, mean = 1980 ÷ 6 = **£330 000** [1]. Median = (230 + 240) ÷ 2 = **£235 000** [1]
**(b)** The median, because the single price of 820 drags the mean up, so the mean is not typical of the other five [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Choose an average and give a reason that refers to the data. A bare "median" with no reason leaves the explanation unproved.

**6. (a)** The vertical axis does not start at zero, so the increase looks much bigger than it is [1]. The axis has no label (or title), so the reader cannot tell what is measured [1]
**(b)** Increase = 2050 − 1950 = 100 [1]. 100 ÷ 1950 × 100 = **5.1%** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For each feature, say what effect it has. Naming a feature alone is thinner than naming it and its effect.

**7. (a)** Negative correlation [1]. The more screen time before bed, the fewer hours of sleep [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (6.3 − 8.7) ÷ (150 − 30) = −0.02 [1]. At x = 90: 8.7 − 0.02 × 60 = **7.5 hours** [1]
**(c)** At x = 300: 8.7 − 0.02 × 270 = **3.3 hours** [1]. 300 minutes is far outside the data (10 to 150) [1], so this is extrapolation: the pattern may not carry on, and the estimate is unreliable [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the relationship in terms of the two variables, not just "negative". For reliability, say that the value is outside the range of the data.

**8.** The two variables may both depend on a third factor, such as rainy weather, which means more umbrellas and also more accidents [1]. The data show that the two quantities rise together, which does not prove that either one produces the other [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a plausible other factor. "Correlation does not mean causation" on its own does not explain this particular case.

**9. (a)** **1.25** [1]
**(b)** A decimal point was missed (12.5 recorded as 1.25), or a timer error [1]
**(c)** Sum = 74.75 [1]. Mean = 74.75 ÷ 7 = **10.68** [1]
**(d)** Sum = 74.75 − 1.25 + 12.5 = 86.0, mean = 86 ÷ 7 = **12.29** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give a reason that fits the context. Notice how far the outlier moves the mean: 10.68 against 12.29.

**10. (Higher tier only) (a)** Heights 15, 30 and 45 [1]. Lower quartile: 40 + (1/19) × 20 = 41.05, so **41** [1]. Median: 40 + (16/19) × 20 = 56.8, so **57**. Upper quartile = **75** [1]
**(b)** 75 − 41 = **34 minutes** [1]
**(c)** 60 − 57 = **3 students** [1]
**(d)** This class has the lower median (57 against 65), so students typically spent less time [1]. Its IQR is larger (34 against 24) so the times are less consistent [1]. State both in context of homework time [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Mark the reading lines on the graph and keep working visible. Readings from a drawn graph are estimates, so say that your answers are estimates and show where you read them.

**11. (Higher tier only) (a)** Minimum 11.8 and maximum 15.0 [1]. Median = 12.9 [1]. Q1 = (12.1 + 12.4) ÷ 2 = 12.25 and Q3 = (13.5 + 14.2) ÷ 2 = 13.85 [1]
**(b)** A box from 12.25 to 13.85 with the median at 12.9 and whiskers to 11.8 and 15.0 [1]. A box from 12.0 to 13.0 with the median at 12.5 and whiskers to 11.5 and 14.0, on the same scale [1]
**(c)** Team B has the lower median (12.5 against 12.9), so B is typically faster [1]. B's IQR is 1.0 against 1.6, so B's times are more consistent [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw on a labelled scale with a ruler. In the comparison, give the medians and the IQRs and say what each means.

**12.** Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 35 [1]. Sum of fx = 30 + 15x + 250 + 140 = 420 + 15x [1]. Total frequency = 20 + x [1]. (420 + 15x) ÷ (20 + x) = 18, so 420 + 15x = 360 + 18x [1]. 60 = 3x, so **x = 20** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Set up the equation carefully and keep the total frequency as 20 + x. Check by substituting: 720 ÷ 40 = 18.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the median from data that are not in order.
- Dividing a grouped total by the number of classes instead of the number of values.
- Quoting only numbers when asked to compare, with no meaning in context.
- Calling a chart misleading without saying what feature causes the effect.
- Describing correlation as "positive" or "negative" with no reference to the variables.
- Using extrapolation without noting that it is outside the data.
- Reading quartiles at the wrong heights of a cumulative frequency graph.
- Forgetting the unit or the context in a final answer.
