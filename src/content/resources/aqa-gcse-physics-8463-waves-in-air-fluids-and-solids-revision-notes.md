## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transverse wave | Oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer |
| Longitudinal wave | Oscillations parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Compression / rarefaction | Region where particles are close together / spread apart |
| Amplitude | Maximum displacement of a point from its undisturbed position |
| Wavelength | Distance from a point on one wave to the equivalent point on the next |
| Frequency | Number of waves passing a point each second (Hz) |
| Period | Time for one wave (s) |
| Wave speed | Speed at which energy is transferred through the medium |
| Ultrasound (HT) | Sound with a frequency above 20 kHz |
| P-wave / S-wave (HT) | Longitudinal / transverse seismic wave |

## Equations

| Equation | Recall or given? |
|---|---|
| v = f λ | Recall |
| T = 1 / f | Equation sheet |
| speed = distance ÷ time | Recall (s = v t) |

### Prefix conversions

```
kHz → Hz  × 1000          ms → s  ÷ 1000          μs → s  × 10⁻⁶
cm → m    ÷ 100           mm → m  ÷ 1000
```

## Wave graphs

| Graph | Gives |
|---|---|
| Displacement–distance | Wavelength (one full cycle along the x-axis) and amplitude |
| Displacement–time | Period (one full cycle along the time axis) and amplitude |

*Worked reminder.* Two full cycles take 0.010 s on a displacement–time graph: T = 0.0050 s, f = 1/0.0050 =
**200 Hz**.

## Evidence the wave travels, not the medium

- Cork on a pond bobs up and down; it is not carried with the ripples.
- Candle flame in front of a loudspeaker flickers back and forth; it is not blown away.

## Measuring wave speeds

| Wave | Method | Calculation |
|---|---|---|
| Sound in air | Two microphones a known distance apart linked to a timer | v = distance ÷ time |
| Sound in air | Echo from a large wall, timed over many claps | v = 2 × distance ÷ time |
| Ripples | Time one ripple over a measured distance | v = distance ÷ time |
| Ripples | Measure λ (over several waves) and f | v = f λ |

Improve accuracy by using electronic timing, large distances, measuring several wavelengths and repeating.

## Required practicals 8 and 9

- **RP8 ripple tank:** lamp above, measure across many waves on the projected pattern, count waves in a
  measured time; or read f from the signal generator.
- **RP8 waves in a solid:** vibration generator drives a stretched string; measure the steady pattern with a
  metre rule; read f from the signal generator.
- **RP9:** ray box rays aimed at different surfaces (reflection) and into different substances (refraction);
  mark rays, draw normals, measure angles from the normal; work in a darkened room.

## Sound changing medium (physics only)

- Frequency: **unchanged** (set by the source).
- Speed: changes (faster in water and solids than in air).
- Wavelength: changes in proportion to speed.

*Worked reminder.* 500 Hz sound: in air at 340 m/s, λ = 0.68 m; in steel at 5000 m/s, λ = **10 m**.

## Reflection, absorption, transmission (physics only)

- **Reflected**: bounces back (echoes from hard walls; mirror images).
- **Absorbed**: energy transferred to the material (soft furnishings absorb sound).
- **Transmitted**: passes through (light through glass).
- Ray diagrams: normal at 90°; angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both from the normal.

## Higher tier only: hearing

- Sound waves make the ear drum and other parts of the ear vibrate.
- Conversion works only over a limited frequency range: human hearing **20 Hz to 20 kHz**.
- Vibrating solids produce sound; sound makes solids vibrate.

## Higher tier only: detection and exploration

```
ultrasound or echo sounding:  depth = speed × time ÷ 2   (there and back)
```

- Ultrasound is **partially** reflected at each boundary; timings give depths → medical and industrial images.
- Echo sounding: high-frequency sound to find objects in deep water and measure depth.
- P-waves: longitudinal, through solids and liquids at different speeds.
- S-waves: transverse, **not** through liquids → S-wave shadow → part of the core is liquid.
- P-waves refract at the core boundary → evidence for the size of the core.

## Exam traps

- Amplitude measured crest to trough.
- Wavelength read from a time axis.
- Frequency said to change in a new medium.
- Forgetting to halve the distance for echoes.
- Swapping P- and S-wave properties.
- Leaving μs or kHz unconverted.

## Quick self-test

1. Describe the difference between transverse and longitudinal waves.
2. Give one piece of evidence that sound waves do not carry air with them.
3. A wave has a frequency of 25 Hz and a wavelength of 0.12 m. Calculate its speed.
4. Calculate the period of a 40 Hz wave.
5. On a displacement–time graph, what does one complete cycle along the time axis represent?
6. Sound of 680 Hz travels at 340 m/s in air. Calculate its wavelength.
7. The sound in question 6 enters water, where it travels at 1500 m/s. State its frequency in water.
8. Why is an electronic timer better than a stopwatch for measuring the speed of sound?
9. (Higher tier only) State the range of normal human hearing.
10. (Higher tier only) An ultrasound echo returns 40 μs after the pulse in steel, where ultrasound travels at
    6000 m/s. How deep is the boundary?
11. (Higher tier only) Which type of seismic wave cannot travel through a liquid?
12. (Higher tier only) What does the detection pattern of S-waves tell us about the Earth's core?

### Answers

1. Transverse: oscillations perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer. Longitudinal: oscillations
   parallel to it, with compressions and rarefactions.
2. A candle flame in front of a loudspeaker vibrates back and forth but is not blown away.
3. 25 × 0.12 = **3.0 m/s**.
4. 1 ÷ 40 = **0.025 s**.
5. The period.
6. 340 ÷ 680 = **0.50 m**.
7. **680 Hz** (unchanged).
8. It removes human reaction time from the timing.
9. **20 Hz to 20 kHz**.
10. 6000 × 40 × 10⁻⁶ = 0.24 m there and back; depth = **0.12 m** (12 cm).
11. S-waves.
12. S-waves are not detected on the far side of the Earth, so part of the core must be liquid.
