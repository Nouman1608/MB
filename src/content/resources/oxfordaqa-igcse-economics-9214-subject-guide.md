OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics (9214) is an introduction to how
people, firms and governments make choices about scarce resources, and to
how national economies and international trade work. It needs no prior
study of economics and is a natural starting point for students who may
go on to economics or business at a higher level. The specification was
first taught in September 2023 and first examined in May/June 2025. It is
a linear qualification assessed by two written papers, each 105 minutes
long, worth 80 marks and carrying 50% of the grade, with no coursework.

## How the course is organised

The content is split into two halves, and each half has its own paper.

**How markets work** is the microeconomic half. It moves through six
areas: economic foundations; resource allocation; how prices are
determined; production, costs, revenue and profit; competitive and
concentrated markets; and market failure. The thread running through it
is how markets decide what is produced, how and for whom, and what
happens when they do not deliver good outcomes.

**How economies work** is the macroeconomic half. It covers an
introduction to the national economy; government objectives; how
governments manage their economies; international trade and the global
economy; and the role of money and financial markets. Here the focus
moves from individual markets to the economy as a whole and its links
with the rest of the world.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | How Markets Work | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | How Economies Work | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed, untiered and sat at the end of the
course. Each combines multiple-choice questions with essay-style
questions, so within a single paper you will move from quick, precise
recall to extended written explanation and judgement.

## How the parts fit together

Although each paper examines one half of the content, the two halves
depend on each other. The supply and demand analysis in How markets work
is the toolkit you use to explain what happens in How economies work:
the effect of a tax, a change in interest rates or a shift in trade
patterns is often easiest to explain by thinking about a particular
market first. Likewise, market failure leads directly into the question
of when and how governments should intervene, which is central to
government objectives and economic management.

Two skills run across both papers. The first is accurate use of
economic terms and diagrams, which is what the multiple-choice questions
test most directly. The second is building a chain of reasoning, from
cause to effect to consequence, and then weighing it, which is what the
essay-style questions reward. Calculations, such as costs, revenue,
profit and percentage changes, also appear in both halves.

## What a linear course means for revision

Because 9214 is linear, both papers are sat in the same exam series at the
end of the course, and nothing is banked along the way. Topics taught in
the first weeks, such as opportunity cost or the factors of production,
are examined alongside the last ones you study, so the challenge is as
much about keeping knowledge fresh as about learning it in the first
place.

A simple way to manage this is a cumulative review routine. Each week,
spend a short session revisiting a topic from earlier in the course,
redrawing its key diagram from memory and writing a brief explanation of
it in your own words. Over a full course this adds up to several passes
through every topic before formal revision starts. It also helps you
notice links between the halves, for example how a change in interest
rates in the second half affects demand in individual markets from the
first.

## Planning your study

Most students find it easiest to study How markets work first, because
its ideas and diagrams are reused throughout the macroeconomic half.
Begin with economic foundations and resource allocation, then price
determination, before moving to costs and market structures and finally
market failure.

The second half can then build on that base: start with the national
economy and government objectives, then the policies governments use, and
finish with international trade and money and financial markets.
Throughout the course, collect short real-world examples, such as a price
change in a local market or a recent decision by a central bank, to use
in written answers.

Practise multiple-choice and short written questions as each area is
taught. Once a whole half is complete, try a full paper under timed
conditions. The course's exam-preparation page covers how to balance
multiple-choice and essay questions within one paper, so use it for that
technique rather than this overview.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics 9214 assessed?**
By two written papers, Paper 1 (How Markets Work) and Paper 2 (How Economies Work). Each is 105 minutes long, worth 80 marks and carries 50% of the qualification.

**Does 9214 have coursework?**
No. It is a linear qualification assessed by the two exams only.

**What types of question are in the 9214 papers?**
Both papers combine multiple-choice questions with essay-style questions, so each paper tests precise knowledge and extended reasoning.

**When was 9214 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2023 and the first exams were in May/June 2025.

**Should I study microeconomics or macroeconomics first?**
How markets work is usually the better starting point, because its supply and demand analysis is reused when explaining how economies work.
