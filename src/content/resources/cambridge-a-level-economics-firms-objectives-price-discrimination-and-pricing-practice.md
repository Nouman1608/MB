## Questions

**1.** Firm Z's weekly figures are in the table.

| Output | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Total revenue | 40 | 76 | 108 | 136 | 160 | 180 |
| Total cost | 30 | 50 | 72 | 98 | 130 | 170 |

**(a)** Calculate MR and MC for each output and state the profit-maximising output and its profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why producing 5 units is not profit-maximising. **[1]**

**2.** Explain (a) survival and (b) profit satisficing as objectives of a firm, giving one reason a firm might adopt each. **[4]**

**3.** Firm Y faces demand P = 104 - 4Q and has total cost TC = 200 + 24Q.

**(a)** Calculate the profit-maximising output, price and profit. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the output and profit if the firm maximises revenue. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason managers might prefer the output in (b). **[1]**

**4.** A consultant has four potential clients willing to pay at most 70, 58, 46 and 34 mu. MC is constant at 30 mu.

**(a)** Find the best single price and the profit it earns. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate profit under first degree price discrimination. **[2]**

**5.** A supplier charges 6 mu a unit for the first 50 units, 4 mu for the next 50 and 2.50 mu for each unit above 100.

**(a)** Calculate the total paid and the average price for a buyer of 130 units. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this is second, not third, degree price discrimination. **[2]**

**6.** A firm sells in market A (P = 60 - 2Q) and market B (P = 28 - 2Q). MC is 4 mu and the firm can stop resale between markets.

**(a)** Find the profit-maximising output and price in each market. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate PED in each market at those prices. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate total profit. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the price is higher in market A. **[1]**

**7.** "Price discrimination always harms consumers." Evaluate this statement. **[8]**

**8.** An incumbent faces market demand P = 90 - Q and has MC = AC = 30 mu. A potential entrant's lowest AC is 50 mu.

**(a)** Calculate the incumbent's price, output and profit if it ignores entry. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the profit if it sets a limit price of 49 mu. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why it might choose the limit price. **[1]**

**9.** **(a)** Explain what is meant by predatory pricing and why it may not be profitable. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain price leadership and one effect on consumers. **[3]**

**10.** The demand for a product is Q = 120 - 4P.

**(a)** Calculate PED at a price of 20 mu. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the price that maximises total revenue and state PED there. **[2]**
**(c)** The firm cuts price from 10 mu to 5 mu. Calculate the effect on total revenue and advise the firm. **[2]**

**11.** An oligopolist charges 12 mu and sells 100 units. A rise to 13.5 mu would cut sales to 80. A fall to 10.8 mu would raise sales to 104.

**(a)** Calculate PED for each price change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate total revenue after each change. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what this suggests about the price. **[1]**
**(d)** State one limitation of the kinked demand model. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** MR = 40, 36, 32, 28, 24, 20 and MC = 30, 20, 22, 26, 32, 40 [1]. MR exceeds MC up to 4 units, but at 5 units MR is 24 and MC is 32 [1]. Output is **4 units**, with profit 136 - 98 = **38 mu** [1].
**(b)** The fifth unit adds 24 to revenue but 32 to cost, so profit falls from 38 to 30 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Compare MR with MC unit by unit, and quote both numbers. Choosing the output with the highest revenue is the common wrong answer.

**2. (a)** Survival means staying in business, which needs price to cover AVC in the short run and all costs in the long run [1]. A firm may choose it in a price war or recession [1].
**(b)** Satisficing means aiming for a satisfactory profit rather than the maximum [1]. It can occur when managers differ from owners and have other aims such as an easier working life [1].
*Examiner insight:* Define each objective, then give a reason. A definition alone tends to leave the second mark unearned.

**3. (a)** MR = 104 - 8Q and MC = 24, so Q = **10** [1]. P = 104 - 40 = **64** [1]. Profit = 640 - (200 + 240) = **200 mu** [1].
**(b)** MR = 0 gives Q = **13** [1]. TR = 52 × 13 = 676 and TC = 512, so profit = **164 mu** [1].
**(c)** Managers' pay or status may depend on turnover or market share [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the MR expression before solving. Note the profit given up (36 mu) when comparing objectives.

**4. (a)** Profits at prices 70, 58, 46 and 34 are 40, 56, 48 and 16 [1]. The best price is **58 mu**, selling to 2 clients [1], profit 2 × 28 = **56 mu** [1].
**(b)** Revenue = 70 + 58 + 46 + 34 = 208 and cost = 4 × 30 = 120 [1], so profit = **88 mu** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Test each possible price in a table. Testing only the highest and lowest prices misses the maximum.

**5. (a)** Total = 50 × 6 + 50 × 4 + 30 × 2.50 = 300 + 200 + 75 = **575 mu** [1]. Average = 575/130 = **4.42 mu** [1].
**(b)** The price depends on the quantity bought, and any customer can reach any block [1]. Third degree would charge different groups of buyers with different PEDs [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say what the price depends on. Naming the degree without the basis of the price gains little.

**6. (a)** A: MR = 60 - 4Q = 4, so Q = **14** [1] and P = **32** [1]. B: MR = 28 - 4Q = 4, so Q = **6** [1] and P = **16** [1].
**(b)** PED at A = (1/2) × 32/14 = **1.14** [1]. PED at B = (1/2) × 16/6 = **1.33** [1].
**(c)** Profit = 14 × 28 + 6 × 12 = 392 + 72 = **464 mu** [1].
**(d)** Demand is less elastic in A, so a higher price loses fewer sales [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use MR = MC in each market, not the demand-curve intercept. State which market is less elastic by quoting your PED values.

**7.** Credit for balanced points, such as:
- The firm gains extra revenue and profit [1].
- The group with inelastic demand pays more and loses consumer surplus [1].
- The price-sensitive group pays less and may be served only because of discrimination [1].
- Output can rise, which improves allocative efficiency [1].
- With linear demand and constant MC output may be unchanged, so welfare can fall [1].
- Profits may fund investment or cross-subsidise a service [1].
- First degree removes all consumer surplus yet pushes output to where P = MC [1].
- Judgement: the statement is too strong; the effect depends on the degree, on whether output rises and on who pays more [1].
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a judgement that depends on a stated condition. A list of effects with no verdict is a common weakness.

**8. (a)** MR = 90 - 2Q = 30, so Q = **30** [1], P = **60** [1], profit = 30 × 30 = **900 mu** [1].
**(b)** At P = 49, Q = 41 [1] and profit = 19 × 41 = **779 mu** [1].
**(c)** It gives up 121 mu a period to keep the entrant out, which pays if entry would cut profit by more over time [1].
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two profits and say what the firm is getting in exchange. Limit pricing is not pricing below cost.

**9. (a)** Pricing below cost [1] to force out a rival or deter entry [1]. It makes losses now, so it pays only if the firm can raise prices later because barriers stop re-entry [1].
**(b)** One firm changes its price and others follow [1]. Rivals follow to avoid losing share or starting a price war [1]. Consumers may face higher prices if it becomes tacit collusion [1].
*Examiner insight:* For predatory pricing, state the cost the price is below. A price cut alone is not enough.

**10. (a)** At P = 20, Q = 40 [1]. PED = (4 × 20)/40 = **2** [1].
**(b)** TR = P(120 - 4P) is highest at P = **15** (Q = 60, TR = 900), where PED = **1** [1][1].
**(c)** Q rises from 80 to 100, so TR falls from 800 to 500 [1]. Demand is inelastic (PED = 0.5), so the firm should not cut the price [1].
*Examiner insight:* A PED value needs its price and quantity. Show the revenue figures before and after.

**11. (a)** Rise: %ΔQ = -20% and %ΔP = +12.5%, so PED = **1.6** [1]. Fall: %ΔQ = +4% and %ΔP = -10%, so PED = **0.4** [1].
**(b)** After the rise TR = 13.5 × 80 = **1,080** [1]. After the fall TR = 10.8 × 104 = **1,123.2** [1]. Both are below 1,200.
**(c)** Either change cuts revenue, so the firm leaves price at 12 [1].
**(d)** It does not explain how the price of 12 was set, or rivals may not respond as assumed [1].
*Examiner insight:* Compare each new revenue with the starting revenue. The conclusion about price stability needs both changes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the revenue-maximising output as the profit-maximising one.
- Quoting MR = MC without the numbers to show it.
- Saying satisficing means "making no profit".
- Confusing sales maximisation with revenue maximisation.
- Defining third degree discrimination as "different prices for different customers" with no mention of PED.
- Forgetting that discrimination needs separated markets.
- Describing limit pricing as pricing below cost.
- Stating PED without a formula or sign convention.
