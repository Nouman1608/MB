## The four prescribed topics at a glance

1. **Contested meanings:** development, sustainability, poverty, inequality.
2. **Interactions of political stakeholders and actors:** states and governments at every level; civil society; IGOs including international financial institutions (IFIs); marginalised, vulnerable or most affected groups; TNCs and MNCs.
3. **Nature, practice and study:** dimensions and assessment; pathways; economic, environmental, political, social and institutional factors; links between development and sustainability.
4. **Debates:** globalization and development; sustainable development; alternative views.

## Definitions to know

| Term | Recall version |
|---|---|
| Development | Improvement in people's lives across economic, political, social and institutional dimensions -- not growth alone |
| Human development | Widening people's real freedoms and capabilities (Sen) |
| Sustainability | A system or practice that can continue without destroying what it depends on; environmental, social and economic |
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without reducing future generations' ability to meet theirs (Brundtland Report, 1987) |
| Absolute poverty | Below a fixed minimum, e.g. the World Bank's dollars-a-day line |
| Relative poverty | Well below the typical standard in your own society, e.g. under 60% of median income |
| Poverty as lack of opportunities | No access to school, credit, land or political voice |
| Inequality | Unequal income, wealth, voice or status; tied to power asymmetries |
| Structural adjustment | IMF/World Bank loan conditions from the 1980s: cut deficits, privatise, liberalise trade |
| Informal economy | Unregistered, untaxed work outside state regulation |
| FDI | Foreign direct investment: a firm builds or buys a lasting stake in another country |

## Measures -- what each misses

| Measure | Captures | Misses |
|---|---|---|
| HDI | Health, education, income (geometric mean) | Inequality, environment, freedoms |
| HPI | Deprivation (replaced by the MPI in 2010) | Household-level detail |
| MPI | Ten household indicators; MPI = H × A | Income; weights are a judgement |
| Happy Planet Index | Wellbeing and life expectancy per unit of ecological footprint | Objective living standards |
| Gini index | Income spread, 0 to 1 (or 0 to 100) | Where in the distribution inequality sits |
| Corruption and trust indices | Perceived honesty of institutions | Actual corruption |
| ESI, Sustainable Society Index, Sustainable Governance Indicators | Environmental, social and governance performance | Agreement on weights |

**Method in steps -- using an index in an essay**

1. Name the index and what it measures.
2. Use it as evidence for your point.
3. State one thing it leaves out.
4. Show how a second measure changes the picture.

**Worked reminder.** If 25% of people are multidimensionally poor (H = 0.25) and the average poor person is deprived in 44% of weighted indicators (A = 0.44), MPI = 0.25 × 0.44 = 0.11.

## Actors -- one-line roles

| Actor | Role in one line |
|---|---|
| State, national and local governments and agencies | Make welfare, tax, land and environmental policy; hold sovereign control of resources |
| Civil society and grassroots initiatives | Mobilise, deliver services, hold power to account |
| World Bank | Long-term development loans and grants |
| IMF | Short-term balance-of-payments lending with conditions |
| Inter-American Development Bank | Regional development lender for Latin America and the Caribbean |
| WTO | Global trade rules and dispute settlement (since 1995) |
| Marginalised, vulnerable or most affected groups | Bear costs, often lack voice |
| TNCs and MNCs | Investment, jobs and technology; shape supply chains and standards |

**Method in steps -- analysing an actor's role**

1. State what the actor wants.
2. Identify the power it holds (money, rules, votes, mobilisation).
3. Show who it cooperates or clashes with.
4. Judge whether those affected see its role as legitimate.

## Pathways

| Pathway | Slogan | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Modernization | Follow the rich world's stages | One route for all |
| Market-led | Trade and FDI, small state | Inequality |
| State-led | Government picks and protects industries | Vested interests |
| Dependency / world-systems | Core exploits periphery | Misses success stories |
| Human development | Capabilities first | Slow growth payoff |
| Community-led / sustainable | Local control, green technology | Scale |

## Factors checklist (the guide's five groups)

- **Political:** conflict, stability, accountability, transparency, legal frameworks, aid decisions, bureaucracy, vested interests.
- **Economic:** resources, infrastructure, debt, credit, aid, trade, FDI, income distribution, informal economy.
- **Social:** values, culture, tradition, gender relations, migration.
- **Institutional:** UN, IMF, World Bank, WTO, partnerships between developing countries, national and local institutions.
- **Environmental:** geography, resource endowment, climate change impacts.

## Debates in one line each

- **Globalization:** export-led growth and poverty reduction versus inequality, a race to the bottom and lost policy space.
- **Sustainable development and the SDGs (17 goals, 2015-2030):** shared universal agenda versus growth-dependent, non-binding, internally contradictory targets.
- **De-growth:** rich states should shrink material throughput and aim for wellbeing; critics say it hurts jobs and ignores poorer states' needs.
- **Regenerative approaches:** restore ecosystems and communities, not just limit harm.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Growth vs development:** a rise in output vs a rise in capabilities and wellbeing.
- **Absolute vs relative poverty:** fixed minimum vs compared with your own society.
- **Headcount vs intensity:** how many are poor (H) vs how poor they are (A).
- **Weak vs strong sustainability:** natural capital can be replaced by human-made capital vs some natural capital cannot be replaced.
- **IMF vs World Bank:** short-term stability lending vs long-term development lending.
- **Poverty as lack of resources vs lack of opportunities:** too little income or food vs no access to school, credit, land or voice.

**Method in steps -- Paper 2 essay plan (15 marks)**

1. Define the key term in the question, and show it is contested.
2. Set out two or three claims, each with a developed example.
3. Give a counterclaim for each and weigh them.
4. Link to at least one key concept (power, sovereignty, legitimacy, interdependence).
5. Conclude with a judgement that follows from your weighing.

## Key concepts applied

- **Power:** who writes loan conditions, trade rules and aid priorities, and who is left out.
- **Sovereignty:** debt, conditional loans and investment treaties narrow a state's policy choices.
- **Legitimacy:** whether affected people accept IFI, TNC and government decisions as fair.
- **Interdependence:** supply chains, debt and climate change tie one state's development to others'.

For the concepts themselves, see the core guides on [power](/resources/ib-dp-global-politics-core-power/) and [sovereignty](/resources/ib-dp-global-politics-core-sovereignty/). ## Quick self-test

1. Name the four contested meanings in this thematic study.
2. Give two dimensions of development other than economic.
3. Which index did the MPI replace in 2010?
4. H = 0.25 and A = 0.44. Calculate the MPI.
5. A fictional country's HDI dimension indices are 0.60, 0.80 and 0.90. Calculate its HDI to 3 s.f.
6. In a fictional country the richest fifth receive 48% of income and the poorest fifth 6%. What is the ratio of the two shares?
7. State one criticism of structural adjustment.
8. What does a Gini value of 0 mean?
9. Name the two alternative views the guide lists.
10. Give one example of a partnership between developing countries.
11. Which of the guide's factor groups includes gender relations and migration?
12. State one criticism of the SDGs.

### Answers

1. Development, sustainability, poverty, inequality.
2. Any two of political, social, institutional.
3. The Human Poverty Index (HPI).
4. 0.25 × 0.44 = **0.11**.
5. Cube root of (0.60 × 0.80 × 0.90) = cube root of 0.432 ≈ **0.756**.
6. 48 ÷ 6 = **8**; the richest fifth receive eight times the share of the poorest fifth.
7. Any one: spending cuts hit health and education; conditions limited sovereignty; privatisation raised prices for poor households.
8. Perfect equality: everyone has the same income.
9. De-growth and regenerative approaches.
10. Any one: South-South cooperation; the BRICS New Development Bank.
11. Social factors.
12. Any one: they assume continued growth; targets conflict; they are not legally binding.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining development only as GDP growth and never mentioning political, social or institutional development.
- Citing an index figure with no comment on what it leaves out.
- Writing about sustainability as if it meant only the environment.
- Listing actors without showing how they interact, cooperate or clash.
- Treating IFIs, TNCs or NGOs as all good or all bad, so no diverse perspectives appear.
- Naming a case (Rana Plaza, Bolsa Família) without developing it or tying it to the question.
- Dropping the key concepts, so the answer reads as economics rather than politics.
- Ending with a conclusion that does not follow from the weighing in the body.
