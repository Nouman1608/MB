## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scalar | A quantity with magnitude only |
| Vector | A quantity with magnitude and an associated direction |
| Force | A push or pull on an object due to its interaction with another object |
| Contact force | Acts only when the objects are physically touching |
| Non-contact force | Acts when the objects are physically separated |
| Weight | The force acting on an object due to gravity |
| Centre of mass | The single point at which an object's weight may be considered to act |
| Resultant force | A single force with the same effect as all the forces acting together |
| Resolving (HT) | Replacing one force with two components at right angles that have the same effect |
| Equilibrium | The forces on an object are balanced: the resultant force is zero |

## Scalars and vectors

| Scalar | Matching vector |
|---|---|
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| mass | weight (a force) |

Other vectors: force, acceleration, momentum. Other scalars: time, energy, temperature.

An arrow represents a vector: **length** = magnitude (to scale), **direction** = direction.

## Force types

- **Contact**: friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force.
- **Non-contact**: gravitational, electrostatic, magnetic.

### Interaction pairs: the four checks

1. Same type of force.
2. Equal in size.
3. Opposite in direction.
4. On **different** objects.

| Interaction | Force on object 1 | Force on object 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Earth and a satellite | Earth pulls satellite towards Earth (gravitational) | Satellite pulls Earth towards satellite (gravitational) |
| Two north poles of magnets | Magnet A pushed away from B (magnetic) | Magnet B pushed away from A (magnetic) |
| Swimmer's hand and the water | Hand pushes water backwards (contact) | Water pushes hand forwards (contact) |

Weight of a book and the support from a table are **not** a pair: different types, same object.

## Weight

```
W = m g        recall;  g is always given
m = W / g      g = W / m
```

- Weight ∝ mass (use the symbol ∝).
- Measured with a calibrated spring-balance (newtonmeter).
- Mass stays the same everywhere; weight changes with g.

*Worked reminder.* A 2.5 kg bag where g = 9.8 N/kg: W = 2.5 × 9.8 = **24.5 N**. On a moon where its weight is
4.0 N: g = 4.0 / 2.5 = **1.6 N/kg**.

## Resultant forces in a line

```
Step 1  choose a positive direction
Step 2  add forces that way, subtract forces the other way
Step 3  state size AND direction
```

*Worked reminder.* A lift is pulled up by a cable tension of 9200 N. Its weight is 8400 N.
Resultant = 9200 − 8400 = **800 N upwards**.

## Higher tier only: diagrams and scale drawings

### Free body diagram checklist

- Draw only the object, as a box or dot.
- One arrow per force **on** the object, starting at the object.
- Longer arrow for a bigger force; equal arrows for balanced forces.
- Label each arrow with its name (and size if known).

### Resolving (scale drawing)

```
1  state a scale, e.g. 1 cm = 10 N
2  draw the force at the given angle
3  complete a right-angled triangle with horizontal and vertical sides
4  measure each side and convert with the scale
```

### Resultant of two forces at an angle

```
1  draw the first force to scale
2  draw the second starting from the tip of the first (tip to tail)
3  join the start of the first to the tip of the second: this is the resultant
4  measure its length (magnitude) and angle (direction)
```

### Equilibrium

Forces drawn tip to tail make a **closed** shape: resultant zero.

## Exam traps

- Weight is a **force** in **newtons**, not a mass in kilograms.
- A resultant with no direction is incomplete.
- Balanced forces on one object are not an interaction pair.
- Free body diagrams show forces **on** the object only.
- Scale drawings need a stated scale and a measured length; a calculation with no drawing does not meet
  "use a scale drawing".
- Angles are measured from the line the question names, usually the horizontal.

## Quick self-test

1. Is velocity a scalar or a vector? Give a reason.
2. Name two contact forces and two non-contact forces.
3. Give the four features of the two forces in an interaction pair.
4. A man pushes on a wall with a force of 120 N. Describe the other force in this interaction pair.
5. Calculate the weight of a 0.45 kg football where g = 9.8 N/kg.
6. An object weighs 33 N where g = 1.5 N/kg. Calculate its mass.
7. State the name of the point at which weight may be considered to act.
8. Two forces act on a crate: 75 N to the left and 110 N to the right. Find the resultant force.
9. Name the instrument used to measure weight.
10. (Higher tier only) Using a scale drawing, a force of 80 N at 30° above the horizontal gives a horizontal
    line 6.9 cm long at a scale of 1 cm = 10 N. State the horizontal component.
11. (Higher tier only) Forces of 24 N east and 7 N north act on an object. A scale drawing gives a resultant
    line 5.0 cm long at a scale of 1 cm = 5 N, at about 16° north of east. State the resultant.
12. (Higher tier only) What does a closed vector triangle show?

### Answers

1. Vector: it has a direction as well as a magnitude.
2. Contact: any two of friction, air resistance, tension, normal contact force. Non-contact: any two of
   gravitational, electrostatic, magnetic.
3. Same type, equal size, opposite directions, act on different objects.
4. The wall pushes on the man with a force of 120 N, in the opposite direction (a normal contact force).
5. 0.45 × 9.8 = **4.4 N** (4.41 N).
6. 33 ÷ 1.5 = **22 kg**.
7. The centre of mass.
8. 110 − 75 = **35 N to the right**.
9. A calibrated spring-balance (newtonmeter).
10. 6.9 × 10 = **69 N**.
11. 5.0 × 5 = **25 N** at about 16° north of east.
12. The forces are balanced: the resultant force is zero, so the object is in equilibrium.
