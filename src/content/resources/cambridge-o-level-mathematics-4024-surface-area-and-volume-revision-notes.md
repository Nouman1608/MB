These notes summarise section 5.4, Surface area and volume, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [surface area and volume study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume/), and exam-style questions are in the [surface area and volume practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-surface-area-and-volume-practice/).

## What is examined

Calculations and problems on the surface area and volume of a cuboid, prism, cylinder, sphere, pyramid and cone. Answers may be asked for in terms of π. A prism is any solid with a uniform cross-section, such as a cylindrical sector.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Volume | space inside a solid, in cubic units |
| Surface area | total area of all faces, in square units |
| Curved surface area | area of the curved part only |
| Cross-section | the shape you see when the solid is cut straight across |
| Uniform cross-section | the same shape all the way along (a prism) |
| Slant height | length along the sloping surface of a cone or pyramid face |
| Vertical height | perpendicular height from the base to the apex |
| Apex | the point of a cone or pyramid |

## Formulas

| Solid | Volume | Surface area |
|---|---|---|
| Prism | cross-section area × length | 2 × cross-section + perimeter × length |
| Cuboid | lwh | 2(lw + lh + wh) |
| Cylinder | πr²h | curved 2πrh; total 2πrh + 2πr² |
| Cone | 1/3 πr²h | curved πrl; total πrl + πr² |
| Sphere | 4/3 πr³ | 4πr² |
| Pyramid | 1/3 × base area × h | base + triangular faces |
| Hemisphere | 2/3 πr³ | curved 2πr²; total 3πr² |

The List of formulas gives the volume of a prism, pyramid, cylinder, cone and sphere, the curved surface area of a cylinder and cone, and the surface area of a sphere. The others are built from these.

## Pythagoras for cones and pyramids

```
cone:  l² = r² + h²
square-based pyramid, base side b:  slant² = h² + (b/2)²
```

Example: cone with r = 6 and h = 8 has l = 10. Pyramid with b = 10 and h = 12 has slant height 13.

## Method box: a solid problem

1. Name the solid and sketch it with the dimensions.
2. Decide whether you need volume, curved area or total area.
3. Find any missing height or slant height with Pythagoras.
4. Apply the formula, keeping π in the working if the answer is required in terms of π.
5. Write the unit: cm³ for volume, cm² for area.

## Method box: equal volume problems

1. Find the volume of the original solid.
2. Set it equal to the volume of the new solid or liquid.
3. Solve for the unknown dimension.
4. For counting objects, divide the volumes and round down.

```
sphere r = 6 lowered into a tank r = 20:   288π = π × 400 × h, so h = 0.72
cube 6 cm melted to spheres r = 1.5:        216 ÷ 14.14 = 15.3, so 15 spheres
```

## Values worth knowing

| Solid | Result |
|---|---|
| cylinder r = 4, h = 10 | V = 160π, total SA = 112π |
| cone r = 6, h = 8 | l = 10, V = 96π, total SA = 96π |
| sphere r = 5 | V = 500π/3 = 524, SA = 100π |
| hemisphere r = 6 | V = 144π, total SA = 108π |
| square pyramid b = 10, h = 12 | V = 400, SA = 360 |

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| Cone vs cylinder | the cone has 1/3 of the volume of the cylinder with the same base and height |
| Total vs curved | total includes the base or ends |
| Vertical vs slant | volume uses vertical; curved area uses slant |
| Hemisphere total | includes the flat face: 3πr² |
| Pyramid | volume 1/3 × base × h |

## Exam traps

- Using the slant height for volume.
- Forgetting the flat face of a hemisphere or the base of a cone.
- Forgetting to cube the radius in the sphere formula.
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Rounding too early.
- Not rounding down when counting objects.

## Self-test

1. Find the volume of a cylinder with radius 3 cm and height 10 cm, in terms of π.
2. Find the surface area of a sphere with radius 6 cm, in terms of π.
3. Find the volume of a cone with radius 3 cm and height 4 cm, in terms of π.
4. A cone has radius 3 cm and height 4 cm. Find its curved surface area in terms of π.
5. Find the volume of a pyramid with a 9 cm square base and height 5 cm.
6. Find the volume of a cuboid 6 cm by 4 cm by 2 cm, and its surface area.
7. A prism has a cross-section of area 20 cm² and length 7 cm. Find its volume.
8. Find the radius of a sphere of volume 36π cm³.
9. Find the total surface area of a cylinder with radius 2 cm and height 10 cm, in terms of π.
10. A hemisphere has radius 3 cm. Find its total surface area in terms of π.

### Answers

1. π × 9 × 10 = **90π cm³**.
2. 4π × 36 = **144π cm²**.
3. 1/3 × π × 9 × 4 = **12π cm³**.
4. l = 5, so curved surface area = π × 3 × 5 = **15π cm²**.
5. 1/3 × 81 × 5 = **135 cm³**.
6. Volume = **48 cm³**; surface area = 2(24 + 12 + 8) = **88 cm²**.
7. 20 × 7 = **140 cm³**.
8. r³ = 27, so **r = 3 cm**.
9. 2π × 2 × 10 + 2π × 4 = 40π + 8π = **48π cm²**.
10. 3π × 9 = **27π cm²**.
