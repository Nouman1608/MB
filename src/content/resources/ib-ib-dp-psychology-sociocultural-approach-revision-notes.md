> **Course note:** these notes follow IB DP Psychology for **first assessment 2019**, whose last
> examination session is November 2026. The replacement course is first assessed in May 2027.

The sociocultural approach explains behaviour through the groups people belong to and the cultures they live in. In IB DP Psychology it is one of three core approaches, examined on Paper 1 through a 9-mark short-answer question and a possible 22-mark essay, so you need each theory linked to at least one study that you can describe and evaluate.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Social identity | The part of the self-concept that comes from membership of social groups |
| Social categorisation | Sorting people into groups, including in-groups and out-groups |
| Social comparison | Comparing the in-group favourably with out-groups to raise self-esteem |
| Positive distinctiveness | The motivation to see one's group as better than others |
| Reciprocal determinism | Bandura's idea that the person, behaviour and environment influence one another |
| Stereotype | A generalised belief about members of a group |
| Illusory correlation | Perceiving a relationship between two variables where none exists or it is weaker than believed |
| Stereotype threat | Underperformance caused by fear of confirming a negative stereotype |
| Culture | Shared norms, values, beliefs and practices transmitted across generations |
| Cultural dimension | A measurable way in which cultures differ, such as individualism-collectivism |
| Enculturation | Learning the norms of one's own culture |
| Acculturation | Psychological and cultural change after contact with a different culture |

## Key studies in one line each

| Study | Aim and finding | Evaluation point |
|---|---|---|
| Tajfel et al. (1971) | Bristol schoolboys placed in "minimal groups" (e.g. a preference for Klee or Kandinsky) allocated points to favour their in-group, even at the cost of a larger joint reward | Lab setting and trivial groups: high control but low ecological validity |
| Abrams et al. (1990) | Conformity in an Asch-style task was higher when confederates were presented as in-group members | Supports SIT's link between identity and conformity |
| Bandura, Ross and Ross (1961) | Children who watched an adult model act aggressively towards a Bobo doll imitated the aggression | Demonstrates observational learning; short-term, artificial aggression |
| Hamilton and Gifford (1976) | Participants over-associated undesirable behaviours with the smaller of two groups | Shows a cognitive origin for stereotypes |
| Steele and Aronson (1995) | Black American students performed worse when a verbal test was framed as diagnostic of ability | Shows effect of stereotypes on behaviour; mostly student samples |
| Berry (1967) | Temne people (farming society) conformed more than Inuit people (hunting society) in an Asch-type task | Links economy and culture to conformity; small cultural samples |
| Hofstede | Survey of IBM employees across many countries identified dimensions such as individualism-collectivism and power distance | Large data set; risk of treating national averages as individual traits |
| Fernald and Morikawa (1993) | American mothers labelled objects more when talking to infants; Japanese mothers used more social routines | Evidence of enculturation through language |
| Berry's model | Four acculturation strategies: integration, assimilation, separation, marginalisation | Integration usually linked to better well-being; a framework, not a test |
| Lueck and Wilson (2011) | Acculturative stress among Asian Americans related to factors such as language and discrimination | Correlational survey data |

## Worked example: a 9-mark SAQ outline

**Question:** Explain the formation of stereotypes, with reference to one study.

1. **Define** stereotype and state the explanation: illusory correlation, a cognitive bias in which distinctive events (rare behaviours by a minority group) are over-remembered.
2. **Describe the study:** Hamilton and Gifford (1976) presented statements about two groups, one twice as large as the other, with the same proportion of desirable to undesirable behaviours in each.
3. **Results:** participants overestimated how many undesirable behaviours belonged to the smaller group.
4. **Link back:** shows that stereotypes can form from normal information processing, without any real group difference.

SAQs reward explanation linked to research; evaluation is not required for a top-band SAQ.

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Describing SIT without its processes.** Name categorisation, identification and comparison.
- **Confusing enculturation and acculturation.** Enculturation is learning your own culture; acculturation follows contact with another.
- **Treating Hofstede's dimensions as personal traits.** They describe national averages.
- **Evaluating in an SAQ.** "Explain" and "describe" SAQs need explanation, not critique; save evaluation for the 22-mark essay.
- **Studies without links.** Every study should end with a sentence that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the sociocultural approach examined?**
On Paper 1: Section A contains one 9-mark SAQ on each core approach, and Section B offers three 22-mark essays, of which you answer one.

**How many studies should I learn per content point?**
At least one per point, ideally two, so that you can describe one and evaluate with the other.

**What is the HL extension for this approach?**
The influence of globalization on individual attitudes, identities and behaviour.

## Self-test

1. Name the three cognitive processes in social identity theory. *Social categorisation, social identification, social comparison.*
2. What did Bandura et al. (1961) show? *Children imitate aggression they observe in an adult model.*
3. What is stereotype threat? *Underperformance caused by fear of confirming a negative stereotype.*
4. Give two of Hofstede's dimensions. *Individualism-collectivism; power distance.*
5. Which acculturation strategy combines keeping one's original culture with joining the new one? *Integration.*
