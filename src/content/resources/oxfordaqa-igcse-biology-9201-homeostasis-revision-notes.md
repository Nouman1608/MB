## The essentials

- **Automatic control systems** in the body keep conditions inside the body **relatively constant**.
- Control systems include **receptors**, **coordination centres** and **effectors**.
- Receptors **detect stimuli** (changes in the environment).
- Coordination centres **receive and process** information from receptors.
- Effectors **bring about responses**.
- Coordination centres include **the brain, the spinal cord and the pancreas**.

```
stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response
```

## Receptors and what they detect

| Organ | Detects |
|---|---|
| Eyes | Light |
| Ears | Sound; changes in position (balance) |
| Tongue and nose | Chemicals (taste and smell) |
| Skin | Touch, pressure, pain, temperature changes |
| Brain | Blood temperature; concentration of water in the blood |
| Pancreas | Concentration of glucose in the blood |

The structure and function of sense organs such as the eye and ear are **not required**.

## Why "relatively" constant

- Conditions vary a little around a normal value.
- The control system detects a change and brings about a response that limits it.
- Worked reminder: five temperature readings 36.6, 36.9, 37.1, 36.8, 37.0 °C. Mean = 184.4 ÷ 5 = **36.88 °C**.
  Range = 37.1 − 36.6 = **0.5 °C**. A small range around the mean shows the temperature is controlled.

## Building the chain

| Situation | Stimulus | Receptor | Coordination centre | Effector | Response |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hot day | Blood temperature rises | Brain (and skin) | Brain | Sweat glands | Sweating |
| Meal eaten | Blood glucose rises | Pancreas | Pancreas | Cells acted on by a hormone | Glucose level falls (see 3.4.5) |
| Light touch on the hand | Pressure | Skin | Brain and spinal cord | Muscle | Movement |

## Rate from data

```
rate = change ÷ time
```

Worked reminder: core temperature rises from 37.0 to 38.0 °C in 20 minutes.
Rate = 1.0 ÷ 20 = **0.05 °C per minute**.

## Worked reminder: spotting the parts

A person touches a hot pan and pulls their hand away. The temperature and pain receptors in the skin detect the
stimulus. The spinal cord is the coordination centre. The effector is a muscle, and the response is the arm moving
away. The same chain applies in any control system.

A second case: after a meal the blood glucose concentration rises. The receptor and the coordination centre are both
in the pancreas, and the response is the release of a hormone (see 3.4.5 for the detail).

## Model answers to learn

- **What is the job of a receptor? (1 mark):** "It detects a stimulus (a change in the environment)."
- **Name two coordination centres. (2 marks):** "The brain and the spinal cord" (or the pancreas).
- **Name the two types of effector. (2 marks):** "Muscles and glands."
- **Why is it important to keep body temperature relatively constant? (2 marks):** "Enzymes (and chemical reactions in cells) work best in a narrow range of conditions."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homeostasis | Keeping conditions inside the body relatively constant |
| Stimulus | A change in the environment |
| Receptor | Detects stimuli |
| Coordination centre | Receives and processes information from receptors |
| Effector | Brings about a response; a muscle or a gland |
| Relatively constant | Varying only slightly around a normal value |

## Exam traps

- Receptors **detect**; they do not bring about the response.
- Muscles and glands are **effectors**, not coordination centres.
- The brain has receptors for blood temperature and for the water concentration of the blood.
- The pancreas is a receptor site for blood glucose and a coordination centre.
- There are **four** controlled conditions, not two.
- "Constant" must be written as "relatively constant".
- No marks for describing the structure of the eye or ear.

## Self-test

1. What does homeostasis keep relatively constant?
2. Name the three parts of a control system.
3. What does a receptor do?
4. Name two types of effector.
5. What are the ears sensitive to? (Give two things.)
6. What are the receptors in the brain sensitive to?
7. Which organ has receptors sensitive to the concentration of glucose in the blood?
8. Name three coordination centres.
9. List the four conditions that are controlled.
10. Core temperature goes from 37.0 °C to 38.6 °C. Calculate the rise and the percentage rise (to 1 d.p.).

### Answers

1. Conditions inside the body.
2. Receptors, coordination centres and effectors.
3. Detects stimuli (changes in the environment).
4. Muscles and glands.
5. Sound, and changes in position (balance).
6. Blood temperature and the concentration of water in the blood.
7. The pancreas.
8. Brain, spinal cord, pancreas.
9. Temperature, water content, ion content, blood glucose.
10. Rise = 38.6 − 37.0 = **1.6 °C**; percentage rise = 1.6 ÷ 37.0 × 100 = **4.3 %**.
