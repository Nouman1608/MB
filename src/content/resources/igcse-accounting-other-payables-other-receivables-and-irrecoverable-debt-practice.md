## Questions

**1.** Explain why a business matches costs and revenues to the period they belong to, rather than recording cash paid and received. **[3]**

**2.** Ravenscar Books has a 30 September year end. Give the year-end journal entry for each item. **[4]**

- Electricity of $175 has been used but not billed.
- Insurance of $960 was paid on 1 July 2028 for twelve months.
- Consultancy fees of $90 are earned but unpaid.
- Rent of $300 was received for the next quarter.

**3.** Stavely Tiles has a 31 August year end. Prepare the rates account for the year, showing the transfer to the statement of profit or loss and the balance brought down. **[5]**

- On 1 September 2027, $400 of rates was prepaid.
- On 1 October 2027 the business paid $2,400 by bank for six months to 31 March 2028.
- On 1 April 2028 it paid $2,640 by bank for six months to 30 September 2028.

**4.** Sablewood Storage has a 30 April year end. On 1 May 2027 a customer owed $240 of storage fees from the previous year.
During the year $3,780 was received, including
$360 for the following year.

**(a)** Prepare the storage fees received account, bringing down the balance. **[5]**
**(b)** Give the journal entry for the closing balance. **[1]**

**5.** Underhill Garden Centre's draft profit for the year ended 31 March 2029 is $31,400. Calculate the corrected profit after these items. **[5]**

- Wages of $520 are owing but unrecorded.
- Commission of $180 is earned but unrecorded.
- The insurance expense includes $310 for next year.
- Rent received includes $400 for next year.

**6.** Teasdale Audio has a 31 October year end.

**(a)** State what is meant by an irrecoverable debt. **[1]**
**(b)** On 14 August 2028 D Okpara, who owes $460, is declared bankrupt. Give the journal entry. **[2]**
**(c)** K Zielinski's debt of $275 is written off on 22 October 2028. Prepare the irrecoverable debts account for the year, including the transfer. **[3]**

**7.** N Sandoval owed Teasdale Audio $390, written off in the year ended 31 October 2027.
On 18 September 2028 she paid $390 by cheque.

**(a)** Give the journal entries to record the recovery. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare her account for the year. **[2]**
**(c)** State where the recovery appears in the statement of profit or loss. **[1]**

**8.** Yamada Hardware has a 30 November year end.
Trade receivables are $26,800 before H Sorensen's debt of
$800 is written off.
The allowance is to be 4% of the remaining trade receivables. The opening allowance is $920.

**(a)** Calculate the allowance required. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in the allowance and give the journal entry. **[2]**
**(c)** State the net trade receivables figure for the statement of financial position. **[2]**

**9.** Kovac Pet Supplies has a 30 April year end and a 6% allowance. At 30 April 2028 it creates its first allowance, with trade receivables of $21,500 after write-offs.
At 30 April 2029 trade receivables after write-offs are $17,000.

**(a)** Prepare the allowance account for both years, bringing down the closing balance. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why the business keeps an allowance. **[2]**

**10.** Quarmby Stationers has a draft profit of $48,260 for the year ended 31 March 2029, before the following items.

1. Trade receivables are $39,340 before any write-offs.
2.
J Nordin ($900) and P Rosales
($440) are to be written off.
3. A Petrov paid $520 into the bank on 5 January 2029. It was written off in 2027, and nothing has been recorded.
4. The opening allowance is $900. It is to be 3% of remaining trade receivables.
5. Electricity of $230 is owing.
6. Insurance paid includes $360 for next year.

**(a)** Give the journal entries for items 2 and 3. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the allowance required and the change in it. **[3]**
**(c)** Prepare a statement showing the corrected profit. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Profit is income earned less the costs of earning it in the period [1]. Cash paid or received can belong to another period, such as insurance paid ahead [1]. Using cash would misstate profit and mislead users [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare definition of matching is less likely to gain credit than a statement of what goes wrong when cash is used.

**2.** Electricity: Dr Electricity 175, Cr Other payables 175 [1]. Insurance: nine months are prepaid, 9/12 x 960 = 720, so Dr Other receivables 720, Cr Insurance 720 [1]. Fees: Dr Other receivables 90, Cr Consultancy fees 90 [1]. Rent: Dr Rent received 300, Cr Other payables 300 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name both the debit and the credit account in every journal.

**3.** Closing prepayment: 1/6 x 2,640 = 440 [1].

```
Rates account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
1 Sep 27  Balance b/d               400  31 Aug 28 Profit or loss          5,000
1 Oct 27  Bank                    2,400  31 Aug 28 Balance c/d               440
1 Apr 28  Bank                    2,640
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  5,440                                    5,440
                                 ======                                   ======
1 Sep 28  Balance b/d               440
```

Opening balance and both payments [1]; balance c/d 440 on the credit side [1]; transfer 5,000 [1]; totals 5,440 and the balance brought down as a debit [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Find the closing balance from the facts first. The transfer is then the balancing figure.

**4. (a)** Income = 3,780 - 240 - 360 = 3,180.

```
Storage fees received account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
1 May 27  Balance b/d               240  Various   Bank                    3,780
30 Apr 28 Profit or loss          3,180
30 Apr 28 Balance c/d               360
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  3,780                                    3,780
                                 ======                                   ======
                                         1 May 28  Balance b/d               360
```

Opening 240 [1]; bank 3,780 [1]; balance c/d 360 on the debit side [1]; transfer 3,180 [1]; balance b/d 360 on the credit side [1].
**(b)** Dr Storage fees received 360, Cr Other payables 360 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An opening debit on an income account is accrued income, which reduces this year's income.

**5.** Wages -520 [1]; commission +180 [1]; insurance +310 [1]; rent in advance -400 [1]. Corrected profit: 31,400 - 520 + 180 + 310 - 400 = **$30,970** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each adjustment as raising or lowering profit, so your treatment is clear.

**6. (a)** Money due from a credit customer that the business has decided it will never receive [1].
**(b)** Dr Irrecoverable debts 460 [1]; Cr D Okpara 460 [1].
**(c)**

```
Irrecoverable debts account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
14 Aug 28 D Okpara                  460  31 Oct 28 Profit or loss            735
22 Oct 28 K Zielinski               275
                                 ------                                   ------
                                    735                                      735
                                 ======                                   ======
```

Both write-offs [1]; transfer 735 [1]; balanced totals [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Debit Irrecoverable debts, not Sales: the sale was genuine revenue when made.

**7. (a)** Dr N Sandoval 390 [1]; Cr Irrecoverable debts recovered 390 [1]. Dr Bank 390, Cr N Sandoval 390 [1].
**(b)**

```
N Sandoval account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
18 Sep 28 Debts recovered           390  18 Sep 28 Bank                      390
                                 ------                                   ------
                                    390                                      390
                                 ======                                   ======
```

Reinstating debit [1]; bank credit and balanced account [1].
**(c)** As income (irrecoverable debts recovered) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Reinstating the customer first makes the account tell the full story.

**8. (a)** Remaining trade receivables 26,800 - 800 = 26,000 [1]; 4% x 26,000 = **$1,040** [1].
**(b)** Increase 1,040 - 920 = **$120** [1]; Dr Statement of profit or loss 120, Cr Allowance for irrecoverable debts 120 [1].
**(c)** Trade receivables 26,000 less allowance 1,040 [1] = **$24,960** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the percentage to the receivables left after the write-off, and charge only the 120 increase.

**9. (a)** Allowances: 6% x 21,500 = 1,290 and 6% x 17,000 = 1,020, so the decrease is 270.

```
Allowance for irrecoverable debts account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
30 Apr 28 Balance c/d             1,290  30 Apr 28 Profit or loss          1,290
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,290                                    1,290
                                 ======                                   ======
30 Apr 29 Profit or loss            270  1 May 28  Balance b/d             1,290
30 Apr 29 Balance c/d             1,020
                                 ------                                   ------
                                  1,290                                    1,290
                                 ======                                   ======
                                         1 May 29  Balance b/d             1,020
```

Creation 1,290 [1]; balance carried and brought down 1,290 [1]; decrease 270 [1]; balance c/d 1,020 [1]; balance b/d 1,020 [1].
**(b)** Some trade receivables will probably not be paid [1], so receivables should not be overstated and the cost belongs to the year of sale [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A decrease goes on the debit side of the allowance account and is income.

**10. (a)** Dr Irrecoverable debts 1,340, Cr J Nordin 900 and Cr P Rosales 440 [2]. Dr A Petrov 520, Cr Irrecoverable debts recovered 520 [1]; Dr Bank 520, Cr A Petrov 520 [1].
**(b)** Remaining trade receivables 39,340 - 1,340 = 38,000 [1]; allowance 3% x 38,000 = 1,140 [1]; increase 1,140 - 900 = **$240** [1].
**(c)**

| | $ |
|---|---|
| Draft profit | 48,260 |
| Less irrecoverable debts | (1,340) |
| Add irrecoverable debts recovered | 520 |
| Less increase in allowance | (240) |
| Less electricity owing | (230) |
| Add insurance prepaid | 360 |
| Corrected profit | 47,330 |

Debts and recovery [1]; allowance [1]; electricity [1]; insurance [1]; corrected profit [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Start from the draft profit and treat every adjustment in the same layout. Include only the allowance increase.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cash paid or received as the expense or income.
- Showing prepayments or accrued incomes as credit balances.
- Calculating the allowance before writing off irrecoverable debts.
- Charging the whole allowance rather than the change.
- Treating a fall in the allowance as an expense.
- Omitting the reinstating entry for a recovered debt.
