Condensed notes on section **3.3.4 Making marketing decisions: using the marketing mix** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [marketing mix study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-using-the-marketing-mix/); exam-style questions are in the [marketing mix practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-using-the-marketing-mix-practice/).

## Specification content

- The elements of the marketing mix (7Ps); influences on and effects of changes in them; goods and services, industrial and consumer.
- Product: portfolio analysis and the Boston Matrix; product life cycle and extension strategies; new product development.
- Pricing: penetration and price skimming.
- Promotion: branding, social media, viral marketing.
- Place: multi-channel distribution.
- People, process and physical environment.
- Integrated mix and its influences; digital marketing and e-commerce.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | The combination of marketing decisions made to meet objectives in the target market |
| Boston Matrix | Classifies products by market growth and relative market share |
| Relative market share | Firm's share ÷ share of the largest rival |
| Product life cycle | Sales of a product over time: introduction, growth, maturity, decline |
| Extension strategy | Action to prolong a product's life |
| Penetration pricing | Low launch price to gain share fast |
| Price skimming | High launch price, lowered over time |
| Brand | Name, symbol or identity that makes a product recognisable |
| Viral marketing | Content that customers share themselves |
| Multi-channel distribution | Selling through several channels together |

## The 7Ps

Product, Price, Promotion, Place, **People, Process, Physical environment**. The last three are vital for services. Industrial products involve more personal selling and negotiated prices.

## Boston Matrix

| | High relative share | Low relative share |
|---|---|---|
| High growth | **Star**: invest | **Question mark**: invest or drop |
| Low growth | **Cash cow**: milk cash | **Dog**: consider dropping |

- Cash from cash cows funds question marks, which become stars, then cash cows.
- **Limits:** only two measures; market definition is a judgement; dogs may still help other sales.

## Product life cycle

| Stage | Sales | Emphasis |
|---|---|---|
| Introduction | Low | Awareness; limited distribution |
| Growth | Rising | Brand building; wider distribution; rivals enter |
| Maturity | Peak and flat | Defend share; extension strategies |
| Decline | Falling | Cut costs; withdraw or harvest |

**Extension strategies:** new uses or markets; redesign or new packaging; added features; new promotion; new channels; price changes; variants.

**NPD:** idea, screening, development, testing, launch. Value: renews portfolio, new markets. Risks: cost, time, failure rate.

## Pricing

| | Penetration | Skimming |
|---|---|---|
| Launch price | Low | High |
| Aim | Share and volume | High margin from early adopters |
| Suits | Mass markets, price-sensitive customers | Innovative or exclusive products |
| Risk | Low early profit; hard to raise price later | Attracts rivals; slow take-up |

Influences on price: costs, objectives, demand and elasticity, competitors, life cycle stage, image. Dynamic pricing uses data to change prices automatically.

## Promotion and place

- **Branding:** recognition, loyalty, higher price; slow and costly to build; fragile.
- **Social media:** cheap, targeted, direct feedback; negative comments spread; needs constant content.
- **Viral:** low cost, wide reach if it works; cannot be controlled or guaranteed.
- **Multi-channel:** wider reach and convenience; but complexity, stock control and channel conflict.
- **E-commerce/digital marketing:** national reach, 24 hours, data; needs delivery, security, and faces price comparison.

## Integrated mix: influences

Life cycle position; Boston Matrix; type of product; marketing objectives; target market; competition; positioning. Elements must fit together; changing one affects others.

## Worked examples to remember

**Boston Matrix.** A firm treats growth above 10% as high and relative share above 1.0 as high.

| Product | Growth | Own share | Largest rival | Relative share | Class |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sparkling water | 12% | 30% | 15% | 2.0 | Star |
| Still water | 2% | 24% | 12% | 2.0 | Cash cow |
| Energy drink | 14% | 6% | 20% | 0.3 | Question mark |
| Tonic | 1% | 5% | 25% | 0.2 | Dog |

**Launch pricing.** A smart speaker under skimming sells 5,000 units at £200 (revenue £1,000,000) and later 20,000 a year at £140 (£2,800,000). Under penetration it sells 25,000 units at £90 in six months (£2,250,000). Penetration wins early revenue and share; skimming earns more per unit from early adopters. The better choice depends on unit costs, speed of rivals and whether the objective is profit or share.

## How to write a good answer

- Tie every element of the mix to the target market in the case.
- Build chains: "a penetration price raises volume, which may lower unit costs through economies of scale".
- Judge which element matters most for this business and whether the elements fit together.

## Exam traps

- Relative share is **relative to the largest rival**.
- Penetration and skimming are launch pricing strategies.
- Extension strategies prolong an existing product; NPD creates a new one.
- Services need people, process and physical environment.
- A luxury position needs all the Ps to agree.

## Self-test

1. List the 7Ps.
2. A product's market share is 18%; the largest rival has 36%. Calculate relative market share.
3. Is the product in question 2, in a market growing 15% a year, a star, cash cow, question mark or dog?
4. How should a business normally treat a cash cow?
5. Name the four stages of the product life cycle.
6. Give two extension strategies.
7. Explain the difference between penetration and skimming.
8. A firm sells 4,000 units at £50 under skimming and then 12,000 units at £35. Calculate revenue in each phase.
9. Give one benefit and one risk of viral marketing.
10. Give two benefits of multi-channel distribution.
11. Why is physical environment especially important to a restaurant?
12. Name two influences on the choice of an integrated mix.

### Answers

1. Product, price, promotion, place, people, process, physical environment.
2. 18 ÷ 36 = **0.5**.
3. A question mark (high growth, low relative share).
4. Keep investment low and use the cash it generates to fund other products.
5. Introduction, growth, maturity, decline.
6. Any two: new uses or markets; redesign or new packaging; added features; new promotion; new channels; price changes.
7. Penetration starts with a low price to gain share; skimming starts with a high price and lowers it over time.
8. First phase: 4,000 × £50 = **£200,000**. Second phase: 12,000 × £35 = **£420,000**.
9. Benefit (any one): low cost; wide reach. Risk (any one): cannot be controlled; may spread for the wrong reasons.
10. Any two: wider reach; convenience for customers; protection if one channel fails.
11. Customers experience the setting directly, and it affects their enjoyment, willingness to pay and image of the business.
12. Any two: position in the life cycle; Boston Matrix; type of product; objectives; target market; competition; positioning.
