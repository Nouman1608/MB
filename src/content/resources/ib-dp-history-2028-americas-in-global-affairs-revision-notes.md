**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. The study has two halves: the United States, and **one other country** of the Americas. These notes use Canada. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-americas-in-global-affairs/) for explanations and test yourself with the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-americas-in-global-affairs-practice/).

## Key terms

- **Manifest destiny**: belief in US destiny to expand.
- **Roosevelt Corollary (1904)**: US claim to act as "police power" in the hemisphere.
- **Big stick diplomacy**: negotiating from strength, with force in reserve.
- **Dollar diplomacy**: using loans and investment to extend influence.
- **Moral diplomacy**: Wilson's stated aim of promoting democracy and law.
- **Platt Amendment (1901)**: gave the US right to intervene in Cuba.
- **Good Neighbor Policy**: Roosevelt's non-intervention policy from 1933.
- **Lend-Lease (1941)**: US supply of war goods to Allies.
- **Statute of Westminster (1931)**: gave dominions full legislative independence.
- **Union government**: Canada's coalition of 1917.

## Reasons for US expansion

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | End of the frontier; Mahan and sea power; Monroe Doctrine |
| Economic | Surplus production; markets and investment; 1893 depression; canal and bases |
| Social | Missionary zeal (Strong); yellow press; national confidence |
| Ideology | Manifest destiny, Social Darwinism, Anglo-Saxon superiority; critics in the Anti-Imperialist League |

## Impact of expansion

- 1898: Cuba (Platt Amendment), Puerto Rico, Guam, Philippines ($20 million); Philippine-American War.
- Roosevelt: Corollary, Dominican Republic customs (1905), Panama Canal.
- Taft: dollar diplomacy; Marines in Nicaragua from 1912.
- Wilson: Veracruz (1914), Haiti (from 1915), Dominican Republic (from 1916). Moral diplomacy in name, but intervention in practice.

## Changing US role

| Phase | Key points |
|---|---|
| First World War | Neutral until 1917; Lusitania, unrestricted submarine warfare, Zimmermann Telegram; about two million troops |
| Peace | Fourteen Points; Lodge and the Senate reject Versailles; no League membership |
| Interwar | Isolationism in security, active in finance and disarmament; Neutrality Acts |
| Good Neighbor Policy | Non-intervention; Haiti withdrawal 1934; Platt Amendment repealed; Mexican oil accepted 1938 |
| Second World War | Lend-Lease; Pearl Harbor; arsenal of democracy; Manhattan Project; Hiroshima and Nagasaki; Bretton Woods and UN |

## Canada's changing role

- First World War: automatic entry in 1914; about 620,000 served; Vimy Ridge (1917); separate signature at Paris and League membership (1920).
- Autonomy: Chanak crisis (1922); Statute of Westminster (1931).
- Second World War: separate declaration (10 September 1939); about one million served; Ogdensburg Agreement (1940).
- Trade: the US replaced Britain as chief partner.
- Comparisons: Brazil declared war in 1917 and 1942 and sent a force to Italy in 1944; Argentina stayed neutral until 1945.

## Domestic impact: Second World War (Canada)

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Economic and social | Munitions and Supply under C. D. Howe; wage and price controls; family allowances (1944) |
| Political | 1942 plebiscite; late conscription for overseas service |
| Women | Factory work; service divisions; pressure to leave jobs in 1945 |
| Marginalised | About 22,000 Japanese Canadians removed; Indigenous service without equal rights |

## Debates

- Economic, strategic or ideological motives for US expansion?
- Wilson: different from Roosevelt and Taft?
- Good Neighbor Policy: change of policy or of method?
- Did the wars unite or divide Canada?

## Essay skeleton

1. State the argument and your line.
2. Rank factors, with dated evidence.
3. Compare two presidents or two wars.
4. Use a perspective.
5. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was the First World War a turning point in the global role of the USA or Canada?"

1. Choose the country and state a line: for the USA, entry in 1917 was a turning point in power but not in policy; for Canada, the war was a turning point in status.
2. USA evidence: troops and finance, Wilson's Fourteen Points, the Senate's rejection of Versailles, withdrawal to a more limited role.
3. Canada evidence: Vimy Ridge, a separate seat at Paris, League membership, the conscription crisis, later autonomy (1922 and 1931).
4. Continuity: before 1914, the US already acted in the Caribbean; Canada's foreign policy was still tied to Britain until the 1920s.
5. Judgement: the war accelerated a change that was already under way.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Spanish-American War as all of US policy.
- Ignoring the second country.
- Narrative without domestic impact.

## Self-test

1. What did the Roosevelt Corollary add to the Monroe Doctrine?
2. What was dollar diplomacy?
3. Why did the US Senate reject the Treaty of Versailles?
4. What did the Statute of Westminster do?
5. Why was conscription divisive in Canada?
6. What happened to Japanese Canadians in 1942?

**Answers:** 1. A US right to act as police power in the hemisphere. 2. Using loans and investment, backed by force if needed, to stabilise countries and extend US influence. 3. Concerns about Article 10 and losing congressional control over war, and partisan opposition led by Lodge. 4. Gave the dominions, including Canada, full legislative independence. 5. English Canada largely supported it and French Canada largely opposed it. 6. About 22,000 were removed from the coast, their property sold and many sent to camps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which country should I choose as the second?**
Canada, Brazil or Argentina. Know one well.

**Is isolationism an accurate label for the 1920s?**
Only partly. The US avoided formal commitments but was active in finance and disarmament.
