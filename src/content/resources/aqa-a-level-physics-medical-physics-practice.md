These questions cover section 3.10 Medical physics (3.10.1 to 3.10.6) of the AQA AS and A-level Physics (7407/7408) specification, version 1.4 (AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). The section is **A-level only**. Sections 3.9-3.13 are options: you study one, assessed in Paper 3 Section B.

Learn the content first with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-medical-physics/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-medical-physics-revision-notes/). Course pages: [AQA A-level Physics hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/physics/), [printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/physics/) and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/).

Use: e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, I₀ = 1.0 × 10⁻¹² W m⁻². Use the "real is positive" sign convention.

## Questions

**1.** Explain why the eye has better spatial resolution at the fovea in bright light than at the edge of the retina in dim light. **[3]**

**2.** A short-sighted person has a far point of 1.6 m and a near point of 0.15 m.

**(a)** Calculate the power of the lens that corrects the far point. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the near point when this lens is worn. **[2]**

**3.**

**(a)** A long-sighted person has a near point of 1.2 m. Calculate the power of the lens needed to read at 0.25 m. **[2]**
**(b)** A prescription for an astigmatic eye contains three numbers. State what each one specifies. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how a sound wave in air is transmitted to the inner ear and turned into nerve signals. **[3]**

**5.**

**(a)** Define intensity of a sound. **[1]**
**(b)** A machine produces an intensity level of 92 dB at a worker's ear. Calculate the intensity. **[2]**
**(c)** A second, identical machine is switched on. Calculate the new intensity level. **[2]**
**(d)** State why noise at work is measured in dBA rather than dB. **[1]**

**6.** Describe how the threshold-of-hearing curve of a person with noise-induced hearing loss differs from that of an older person with age-related loss, compared with a healthy young adult. **[3]**

**7.**

**(a)** Describe a normal ECG waveform, naming each feature and stating what happens in the heart at each. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a gel is applied before the electrodes. **[1]**

**8.** Muscle has density 1075 kg m⁻³ and speed of sound 1590 m s⁻¹. Bone has Z = 7.80 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹; air has Z = 430 kg m⁻² s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the acoustic impedance of muscle. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction of the intensity reflected at a muscle-bone boundary. **[2]**
**(c)** On an A-scan, echoes from the front and back of a layer of muscle arrive 40 µs and 92 µs after the pulse. Calculate the thickness of the layer. **[2]**
**(d)** Use a calculation to explain why gel is used between the probe and skin. Take skin to have the Z of muscle. **[2]**

**9.**

**(a)** Explain why a flexible endoscope needs both a coherent and a non-coherent fibre bundle. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how an MR scanner produces signals from a cross-section of a patient. **[4]**

**10.** An X-ray tube operates at 100 kV.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum photon energy in J. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the minimum wavelength. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the X-ray spectrum has both a continuous part and sharp lines. **[3]**
**(d)** State the effect on the beam of (i) adding an aluminium filter, (ii) increasing the tube current. **[2]**

**11.** For the X-rays used, soft tissue has μ = 22 m⁻¹ and bone has μ = 110 m⁻¹ and density 1850 kg m⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the half-value thickness of soft tissue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the fraction of the beam transmitted through 0.12 m of soft tissue. **[2]**
**(c)** A second path is 0.10 m of soft tissue plus 0.02 m of bone. Calculate the fraction transmitted, and the ratio of the two transmitted intensities. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the mass attenuation coefficient of bone. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why a barium meal is given before an X-ray of the stomach. **[2]**
**(f)** Suggest why ultrasound, not CT, is used to check the growth of a fetus. **[2]**

**12.** A tracer has physical half-life 18 h and biological half-life 9.0 h. A patient is given 400 MBq.

**(a)** Calculate the effective half-life. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the activity in the patient 24 h later. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the importance of the molybdenum-technetium generator. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe how a gamma camera forms an image. **[3]**
**(e)** Describe three ways of limiting the dose to healthy cells in high-energy X-ray therapy. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** At the fovea each cone has its own nerve connection [1]; so images on neighbouring cones give separate signals [1]; at the edge, in dim light, rods are used and many share one nerve fibre, so close images merge into one signal [1].
*Examiner insight:* The mark is for linking the nerve connections to resolution; "cones are better" with no mechanism gains little.

**2. (a)** u = ∞, v = −1.6 m, so P = 1/(−1.6) [1] = **−0.63 D** [1]
**(b)** Image must be at v = −0.15 m: 1/u = −0.625 + 1/0.15 = 6.04 m⁻¹ [1]; u = **0.17 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A positive power in (a) loses the accuracy mark even if the number is right; ECF is usually allowed into (b).

**3. (a)** P = 1/0.25 + 1/(−1.2) = 4.00 − 0.83 [1] = **+3.2 D** [1]
**(b)** Sphere: spherical power for overall focus [1]; cylinder power and axis: strength of the cylindrical correction and its orientation in degrees [1].
*Examiner insight:* For (a), a power with no sign or unit is likely to lose the accuracy mark.

**4.** Eardrum vibrates; ossicles lever the vibration to the oval window [1]; its smaller area increases the pressure on the cochlear fluid [1]; the basilar membrane vibrates and hair cells produce nerve impulses [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each step needs the right structure in the right order; vague "the ear amplifies it" earns nothing.

**5. (a)** Power per unit area normal to the direction of the wave [1].
**(b)** I = 10⁻¹² × 10^9.2 [1] = **1.6 × 10⁻³ W m⁻²** [1]
**(c)** Intensity doubles: 10 log(2 × 1.58 × 10⁹) [1] = **95 dB** [1]
**(d)** dBA weights the level for the ear's frequency response, so it matches perceived loudness [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), adding 92 + 92 dB scores zero; you must add intensities.

**6.** Both curves are higher than the healthy curve where hearing is lost [1]; noise damage raises the threshold over a band of frequencies, giving a notch [1]; age raises it most at high frequencies and lowers the upper frequency limit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Comparative wording ("higher threshold at…") is needed; describing only one person caps the marks.

**7. (a)** P wave: atria depolarise and contract [1]; QRS complex: ventricles depolarise and contract [1]; T wave: ventricles repolarise [1].
**(b)** It gives good electrical contact with low resistance between skin and electrode [1].
*Examiner insight:* Naming P, QRS and T without the heart event at each earns no credit.

**8. (a)** Z = 1075 × 1590 = **1.71 × 10⁶ kg m⁻² s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** ((7.80 − 1.71)/(7.80 + 1.71))² [1] = **0.41** [1]
**(c)** d = c × Δt/2 = 1590 × 52 × 10⁻⁶/2 [1] = **0.041 m** [1]
**(d)** Air-skin: ((1.71 × 10⁶ − 430)/(1.71 × 10⁶ + 430))² = 0.999, almost all reflected [1]; gel has Z close to skin, so most ultrasound enters the body [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), use the speed in the layer itself and the time difference; forgetting to halve gives a common one-mark loss.

**9. (a)** Non-coherent bundle carries light in to illuminate the area [1]; coherent bundle has fibres in matching positions at both ends [1]; so it carries the image back without scrambling it [1].
**(b)** Strong field: protons align and precess about the field lines [1]; gradient coils select the slice [1]; short RF pulses excite protons in small regions in turn [1]; as they de-excite they emit RF signals, detected and processed by computer [1].
*Examiner insight:* Field production and relaxation times gain nothing here; spend the words on the listed steps.

**10. (a)** E = eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.00 × 10⁵ [1] = **1.60 × 10⁻¹⁴ J** [1]
**(b)** λ = hc/E = **1.24 × 10⁻¹¹ m** [1]
**(c)** Electrons decelerate in the target, losing varying energy [1], giving photons of all energies up to eV [1]; lines arise when inner-shell vacancies in target atoms are filled, giving fixed photon energies [1].
**(d)** (i) Removes low-energy photons, so lower patient dose [1]; (ii) higher intensity, same photon energies [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (d)(ii) saying the photon energy rises loses the mark; current changes only the number of photons.

**11. (a)** x½ = ln 2/22 = **0.032 m** [1]
**(b)** I/I₀ = e^(−22 × 0.12) [1] = **0.071** [1]
**(c)** e^(−(22 × 0.10 + 110 × 0.02)) = e^(−4.4) [1] = **0.012** [1]; ratio = 0.0714/0.0123 = **5.8** [1]
**(d)** μm = 110/1850 = **0.059 m² kg⁻¹** [1]
**(e)** Barium absorbs X-rays strongly [1]; so the stomach outline contrasts with the soft tissue around it [1].
**(f)** Ultrasound is non-ionising, so no risk to the fetus [1]; it is cheap and real-time, while CT gives a high dose [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c), add the μx terms in the exponent; multiplying the two exponentials separately is fine, but adding the fractions is not.

**12. (a)** 1/T_E = 1/9.0 + 1/18 [1]; **T_E = 6.0 h** [1]
**(b)** 24 h = 4 effective half-lives [1]; 400/2⁴ = **25 MBq** [1]
**(c)** Mo-99 has a longer half-life (about 66 h, against about 6 h) and decays to Tc-99m [1]; Tc-99m is eluted on site when needed, so the short-lived tracer is always available [1].
**(d)** Collimator passes only photons travelling along its holes [1]; scintillator crystal flashes for each photon, detected by photomultiplier tubes [1]; relative pulse sizes locate each flash and a computer builds the image [1].
**(e)** Any three: several beams from different directions crossing at the tumour (or rotation) [1]; beam shaped to the tumour [1]; shielding of other organs, or dose split into sessions [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using T_P instead of T_E in (b) gives a wrong answer with no accuracy credit; method marks need T_E.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Sign errors in lens power from a positive v for a virtual image.
- Adding decibels instead of intensities.
- Missing the factor of 2 for echo depth, or using the wrong time.
- Forgetting to square in the reflection equation.
- Stating that tube current changes photon energy.
- Swapping the roles of coherent and non-coherent bundles.
- Using T_P where T_E is needed.
- Giving a single vague comparison point when resolution, convenience and safety are each worth credit.
