These questions cover **3.1.10.4 Weak acids and bases; Ka for weak acids** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: constructing Ka expressions, calculations linking the pH of a weak acid to its concentration and Ka, and converting between Ka and pKa. This is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3. Give pH and pKa to 2 decimal places. The acids labelled J, K and L in Question 8 are invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the term *weak acid*. **[1]**

**2.** Butanoic acid is CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH.

**(a)** Write an expression for Ka for butanoic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the units of Ka. **[1]**

**3.** Convert:

**(a)** Ka = 2.9 × 10⁻⁸ mol dm⁻³ into pKa **[1]**

**(b)** pKa = 9.25 into Ka. **[1]**

**4.** Butanoic acid has Ka = 1.51 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³. Calculate the pH of a 0.120 mol dm⁻³ solution. **[3]**

**5.** A 0.0250 mol dm⁻³ solution of a weak monoprotic acid has pH 3.32. Calculate Ka for the acid. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid has a higher pH than 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Ammonium chloride dissolves to give ammonium ions, which act as a weak acid. Ka for NH₄⁺ is 5.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Write an expression for Ka for the ammonium ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of a 0.250 mol dm⁻³ solution of ammonium chloride. **[3]**

**(c)** Aqueous ammonia is described as a weak base. Explain what this means. **[1]**

**8.** Three weak acids have the values below.

| Acid | Data |
|---|---|
| J | Ka = 1.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ |
| K | pKa = 4.19 |
| L | Ka = 1.38 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ |

**(a)** Calculate pKa for J and for L, and list the three acids in order of strength, strongest first. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of a 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ solution of the strongest acid. **[2]**

**9.** A student dissolves 0.740 g of propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH (Mr = 74.0), in water and makes the solution up to 250 cm³. Ka = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the solution. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate its pH. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage of propanoic acid molecules that are dissociated. **[1]**

**(d)** Use your answer to (c) to comment on the approximation that the equilibrium concentration of the acid equals its starting concentration. **[1]**

**10.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ methanoic acid (Ka = 1.80 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³) is diluted with water to 500 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the pH before dilution. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH after dilution. **[2]**

**(c)** A strong acid diluted in the same way would rise by 1.30 pH units. Explain why the rise for methanoic acid is smaller. **[2]**

**11.** A technician needs 250 cm³ of ethanoic acid with pH 2.70. Ka for ethanoic acid is 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of ethanoic acid needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid that must be diluted to 250 cm³ to make this solution. **[2]**

**12.** A student claims: "A weak acid solution with a lower pH must contain the stronger acid." Ethanoic acid has pKa 4.76 and methanoic acid has pKa 3.74. Use the pH of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid and of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ methanoic acid to evaluate the claim. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** An acid that dissociates only slightly (partially) in aqueous solution [1]

**2. (a)** **Ka = [CH₃CH₂CH₂COO⁻][H⁺] / [CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH]** [1]

**(b)** **mol dm⁻³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Do not include water, and do not add numbers to an expression question.

**3. (a)** pKa = −log₁₀(2.9 × 10⁻⁸) = **7.54** [1]

**(b)** Ka = 10^(−9.25) = **5.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ mol dm⁻³** [1]

**4.** [H⁺]² = 1.51 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.120 = 1.81 × 10⁻⁶ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.35 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.87** [1]

**5.** [H⁺] = 10^(−3.32) = 4.79 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; Ka = (4.79 × 10⁻⁴)² / 0.0250 [1]; Ka = **9.16 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 0.0250 rather than by (0.0250 − 4.79 × 10⁻⁴) is the expected approximation; either gives 9.2 × 10⁻⁶ to two figures.

**6.** Ethanoic acid dissociates only slightly, whereas hydrochloric acid dissociates completely [1]; so [H⁺] is much lower in ethanoic acid and the pH is higher [1]

**7. (a)** **Ka = [NH₃][H⁺] / [NH₄⁺]** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺]² = 5.62 × 10⁻¹⁰ × 0.250 = 1.41 × 10⁻¹⁰ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.19 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **4.93** [1]

**(c)** Only a small fraction of the ammonia molecules accept a proton from water (NH₃ + H₂O ⇌ NH₄⁺ + OH⁻ lies to the left) [1]

**8. (a)** pKa(J) = **3.74** [1]; pKa(L) = **3.86** [1]; order **J > L > K** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = √(1.80 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.0500) = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.52** [1]

*Examiner insight:* K's pKa of 4.19 is the largest of the three, so K is the weakest.

**9. (a)** n = 0.740 / 74.0 = 0.0100 mol; concentration = 0.0100 / 0.250 = **0.0400 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = √(1.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0400) = 7.35 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **3.13** [1]

**(c)** 7.35 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.0400 × 100 = **1.84 %** [1]

**(d)** Under 2 % has dissociated, so the equilibrium concentration (0.0393 mol dm⁻³) is very close to 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ and the approximation is valid [1]

**10. (a)** [H⁺] = √(1.80 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.200) = 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.22** [1]

**(b)** New concentration = 0.200 × 25.0 / 500 = 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = √(1.80 × 10⁻⁶) = 1.34 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³, pH = **2.87** [1]

**(c)** For a weak acid [H⁺] = √(Ka × [HA]), so [H⁺] falls only by √20, giving a rise of about 0.65 (half of 1.30) [1]; dilution moves the equilibrium HA ⇌ H⁺ + A⁻ to the right, so a larger fraction of the acid dissociates [1]

**11. (a)** [H⁺] = 10^(−2.70) = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [HA] = (2.00 × 10⁻³)² / 1.74 × 10⁻⁵ = **0.229 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** n needed = 0.229 × 0.250 = 0.0572 mol [1]; volume = 0.0572 / 2.00 = 0.0286 dm³ = **28.6 cm³** [1]

**12.** Ethanoic acid: Ka = 1.74 × 10⁻⁵, [H⁺] = √(1.74 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.500) = 2.95 × 10⁻³, pH = **2.53** [1]; methanoic acid: Ka = 1.82 × 10⁻⁴, [H⁺] = √(1.82 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.0200) = 1.91 × 10⁻³, pH = **2.72** [1]; ethanoic acid is weaker (higher pKa) yet has the lower pH because it is far more concentrated, so the claim is wrong: pH depends on concentration as well as on Ka [1]

*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs a judgement. Two correct pH values without a conclusion score only two of the three marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the Ka expression for butanoic acid with the wrong anion formula; copy the carbon chain exactly.
- Squaring a rounded [H⁺] and losing the third significant figure.
- Forgetting to convert 250 cm³ to 0.250 dm³ before finding a concentration.
- Comparing acid strength by pH alone without fixing the concentration.
