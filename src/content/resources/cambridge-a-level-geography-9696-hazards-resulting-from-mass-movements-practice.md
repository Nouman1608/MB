## Section A

**1.** Define the term "shear strength". **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a preparatory factor and a trigger of mass movement. **[2]**

**3.** State two characteristics of a debris flow. **[2]**

**4.** Outline how a road cutting can make a slope less stable. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why rapid mass movements cause more deaths than slow ones. **[3]**

**6.** Outline two instruments used to monitor a slope and what each measures. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** On the fictional Harlow Ridge slope, engineers estimate resisting forces of 450 kN and driving forces of 375 kN per metre width of slope.

**(a)** Calculate the factor of safety. **[1]**

**(b)** After prolonged rain, resisting forces fall by 20% while driving forces stay the same. Calculate the new factor of safety and state whether the slope is expected to fail. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why prolonged rain reduces resisting forces. **[3]**

**8.** The table shows landslides recorded in the fictional hill district of Sarnat over ten monsoon seasons, grouped by 48-hour rainfall total.

| 48-hour rainfall (mm) | Number of 48-hour periods | Periods with one or more landslides |
|---|---|---|
| Under 100 | 820 | 4 |
| 100-199 | 96 | 11 |
| 200-299 | 30 | 18 |
| 300 or more | 12 | 11 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of periods with landslides when rainfall was 200-299 mm. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a rainfall threshold for a landslide warning, giving reasons. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest two limitations of using this threshold to predict landslides. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how human activities can increase the risk from mass movements on hillsides around a growing city. **[5]**

**10.** Describe the possible impacts of a large landslide that blocks a river valley. **[5]**

**11.** Evaluate two engineering methods used to reduce the hazard from rockfall and slides. **[6]**

**12.** "Hazard mapping is the most effective way to reduce the impact of mass movements." How far do you agree? **[20]**

## Answers

**1.** The resistance of slope material to movement [1], provided by friction between particles and cohesion [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A preparatory factor slowly lowers slope stability over time, such as weathering [1], whereas a trigger causes failure at a particular moment, such as an earthquake or intense rain [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A rapid flow of mixed rock, soil and water [1] that moves down channels like wet concrete, carrying large boulders [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Cutting the base of the slope removes support [1] and steepens it [1], so shear stress rises relative to shear strength and the slope above may slide [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Rapid movements give little or no warning [1], so people have no time to escape [1]. They have high energy and destroy buildings, burying those inside [1]. **[3]**

**6.** An inclinometer in a borehole [1] measures movement of the ground at depth [1]. A piezometer [1] measures pore-water pressure in the slope [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** 450 / 375 = 1.2 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 450 x 0.8 = 360 kN; 360 / 375 = 0.96 [1]. This is below 1, so the slope is expected to fail [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Water infiltrates and raises the water table [1]. Pore-water pressure rises, pushing particles apart [1], which reduces friction and cohesion between particles [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** 18 / 30 x 100 = 60% [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** About 200 mm in 48 hours [1]. Below 100 mm only about 0.5% of periods have landslides [1], while at 200-299 mm the figure rises to 60% and above 300 mm it is about 92%, so 200 mm separates low and high likelihood [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** It ignores antecedent conditions, so a smaller total on saturated ground might still cause failure [1]. Records of only ten seasons may not include rare events, and local slope or human factors are not considered [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Migrants may build informal housing on steep, unstable slopes, the only affordable land [1]. Clearing vegetation removes root cohesion and interception [1]. Cutting terraces and roads steepens and undercuts slopes [1]. Buildings add weight, increasing shear stress [1]. Poor drainage and leaking pipes raise pore-water pressure [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Primary: burial of homes, roads and farmland in the landslide area with loss of life [1]. The river is dammed, forming a lake that floods upstream settlements and fields [1]. If the dam breaches, a flood surges downstream with little warning [1]. Roads and communications are cut, delaying rescue and supplies [1]. Longer term, loss of farmland, jobs and tourism income affect the local economy [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Rock bolts and soil nails anchor unstable rock or soil to stable ground beneath, adding resisting force [1]. They are effective on specific known weaknesses [1] but are costly per square metre and need inspection, so they suit high-value sites like roads [1]. Netting and catch fences intercept falling rocks before they reach roads [1]. They are cheaper and quick to install [1], but they do not stop the slope failing and must be cleared and repaired after impacts [1]. **[6]**

**12.** The real exam marks this essay with levels of response, not point by point. The points below are indicative creditworthy content.

*Introduction:* define hazard mapping [1] and "impact" as losses of life and property [1].

*Mapping effective:* identifies high-susceptibility land using slope, geology and past events [1]. Supports land-use zoning that keeps new building off dangerous slopes [1]. Cheap compared with engineering [1]. Guides where to invest in engineering and warnings [1]. Informs insurance and planning [1].

*Mapping limited:* maps only work if planning rules are enforced [1]. Informal settlements grow regardless of zoning [1]. Maps do not protect people already living in risk zones [1]. At Oso, the risk was known but people stayed [1].

*Other approaches:* engineering such as drainage and retaining walls reduces risk on existing slopes [1]. Hong Kong combines slope registration, upgrading and warnings [1]. Rainfall thresholds and warnings save lives during events [1]. Afforestation addresses causes [1].

*Interaction:* mapping is a foundation that other measures depend on [1]. Its value is greatest for planning new development [1].

*Judgement:* mapping is effective in the long term only with enforcement [1]; for existing settlements engineering and warnings matter more [1]; a reasoned final view on "how far" with named examples [1]. **[20]**
