OCR A Level Physics A (H556) is a linear A Level that takes students from the measurement and
mechanics they met at GCSE through electricity, waves and quantum ideas to fields, nuclear and
particle physics, astrophysics and medical imaging. The course is organised into six modules and
examined by three written papers at the end of the course: Modelling physics and Exploring physics,
each 100 marks over 135 minutes and worth 37%, and Unified physics, 70 marks over 90 minutes and
worth 26%. Alongside the exams, students complete a Practical Endorsement that is reported
separately.

## How the course is organised

The six modules build on one another, and they divide fairly cleanly into foundation material, the
first-year content and the second-year content.

**Module 1, Development of practical skills in physics**, is not taught as a block. It covers the
practical skills assessed in the written examinations and those assessed through the Practical
Endorsement, and it runs through the whole course.

**Module 2, Foundations of physics**, covers physical quantities and units, making measurements and
analysing data, and the nature of quantities. It is the toolkit every other module uses.

**Module 3, Forces and motion**, covers motion, forces in action, work, energy and power, materials,
and Newton's laws of motion and momentum.

**Module 4, Electrons, waves and photons**, covers charge and current; energy, power and resistance;
electrical circuits; waves; and quantum physics.

**Module 5, Newtonian world and astrophysics**, covers thermal physics, circular motion,
oscillations, gravitational fields, and astrophysics and cosmology.

**Module 6, Particles and medical physics**, covers capacitors, electric fields, electromagnetism,
nuclear and particle physics, and medical imaging.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| H556/01 | Paper 1: Modelling physics | 135 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H556/02 | Paper 2: Exploring physics | 135 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H556/03 | Paper 3: Unified physics | 90 minutes | 70 | 26% |

The three papers together carry 270 marks, and that total decides the overall A Level grade. The
qualification is linear and untiered, with all papers sat in the same series; it has been examined
since 2017. OCR describes Paper 3 as synoptic: it draws on all the modules rather than a subset.

The **Practical Endorsement** is a non-exam assessment, internally assessed, in which students carry
out a minimum of 12 practical activities. It is reported separately as Pass or Not Classified and
contributes no marks to the 270-mark total, which is why it does not appear in the table. Practical
skills still matter for the grade, though, because Module 1 skills are also assessed in the written
papers.

## How the parts fit together

H556 is a course where later topics reuse earlier ones almost wholesale. Module 2's units, data
analysis and treatment of uncertainty appear in every question that asks for a calculation or a
graph. Module 3's ideas of force, energy and momentum return in circular motion, oscillations and
gravitational fields in Module 5. Module 4's work on charge, current and potential difference is the
basis for capacitors, electric fields and electromagnetism in Module 6.

There are also strong parallels between the field topics. Gravitational fields and electric fields
share the same mathematical shape, so learning them side by side helps. Exponential behaviour turns
up in capacitor discharge and in radioactive decay, and wave ideas from Module 4 support the medical
imaging techniques in Module 6.

Paper 3's synoptic design rewards exactly these connections. A single question might combine
mechanics, energy and fields, so revising modules in isolation leaves a gap. The practical thread
from Module 1 ties everything together: planning measurements, handling uncertainty and evaluating
results are skills the written papers test across every module.

## Planning your study

A typical first year covers Modules 2, 3 and 4, with Module 1 skills developed through
practical work from the start. Begin the Practical Endorsement activities early and keep your
records organised, because they build up across both years.

In the second year, move on to Modules 5 and 6. A sensible order is thermal physics, circular
motion and oscillations first, as they extend first-year mechanics, then gravitational fields and
astrophysics, followed by capacitors, electric fields and electromagnetism, and finally nuclear and
particle physics and medical imaging.

Revisit first-year modules regularly during the second year rather than leaving them until the
spring. Once all six modules have been taught, start full timed papers, paying particular attention
to Paper 3 because it mixes content from everywhere. The exam-preparation page sets out what each
paper asks for and how to approach it.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exam papers are there in OCR A Level Physics A?**
Three: Paper 1 Modelling physics and Paper 2 Exploring physics, each 135 minutes, 100 marks and 37%, and Paper 3 Unified physics, 90 minutes, 70 marks and 26%.

**What is the Practical Endorsement in H556?**
It is an internally assessed non-exam assessment of at least 12 practical activities, reported separately as Pass or Not Classified. It does not add marks to the A Level grade.

**What does the synoptic Paper 3 cover?**
Paper 3, Unified physics, draws on all the modules, so it expects you to combine ideas from different parts of the course.

**How many modules does OCR A Level Physics A have?**
Six: practical skills, foundations of physics, forces and motion, electrons, waves and photons, Newtonian world and astrophysics, and particles and medical physics.

**Are practical skills only assessed through the Practical Endorsement?**
No. Module 1 includes practical skills assessed in the written examinations as well as those assessed through the endorsement.
