OxfordAQA International A-Level English Language (9670) is a modular qualification of four
compulsory-shape units, with a route choice on the last one. AS Paper 1 (Language and Context) and AS
Paper 2 (Language and Society) are each 2 hours, 50 marks, 20% of the full A-level. A-level Paper 1
(Language Variation) is compulsory for every candidate: 2 hours, 50 marks, 30%. The final 30% is then
either Route A -- A-level Paper 2, an exam on Language Exploration (2 hours 30 minutes, 50 marks) -- or
Route B -- a non-exam assessment language investigation, internally assessed and externally moderated,
also worth 50 marks. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Unit 1](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-english-language-unit-1/) and
[Unit 2](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-english-language-unit-2/).

## Confirm your route well before the final unit begins

Route A and Route B lead to completely different preparation: an exam under timed conditions on unseen
material, versus a coursework investigation with its own drafting and moderation timeline set by your
school. **Exam-preparation priority**: confirm with your teacher which route your cohort is entered for
as early as possible -- Route B in particular needs a research question settled and drafting time
planned well ahead of any exam-focused revision.

## Three of the four units share close textual analysis as a core skill

Language and Context, Language and Society, and Language Variation all require identifying and
explaining specific linguistic features in unseen or provided material, not just describing content.
**Exam-preparation priority**: treat close analytical annotation as the one technique that transfers
across all three exam papers, and drill it against material from each unit's own theme in turn.

## Language Variation is compulsory and worth the most of the three fixed units

At 30% against the AS papers' 20% each, A-level Paper 1 carries proportionally more weight and covers
regional, social and historical variation in English. **Exam-preparation priority**: once AS-level
analytical technique is secure, prioritise Language Variation's specific content -- variation is not
simply an extension of the AS units' themes and needs its own dedicated revision.

## Route A's Language Exploration paper is the longest single sitting

At 2 hours 30 minutes, it is 30 minutes longer than any other paper in the qualification.
**Exam-preparation priority**: for Route A candidates, practise a full-length timed sitting specifically
-- sustaining close analytical focus for the extra half hour is a trainable skill, not a given.

## Worked routine: annotating an unseen text for language features

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - read once for overall purpose, audience and mode:
Before hunting for individual features, know what the text is for.

Step 2 - reread and mark features by level:
Lexis, grammar, phonology, graphology, discourse -- label each
example with which level it belongs to.

Step 3 - group marked features by the effect they create:
Several small features often combine towards one larger effect --
group before writing, don't analyse in isolation.

Step 4 - select a linguistic framework or concept for each group:
Name the concept precisely; a labelled feature without a named
framework scores lower than one that is properly framed.

Step 5 - link every group back to purpose, audience or mode from
Step 1:
This is what turns feature-spotting into analysis.
```

Step 5 is the step that most distinguishes a strong answer from a feature-list: analysis is explaining
why a feature serves the text's purpose, not simply naming that the feature is present.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: confirm your Route A/Route B entry early, since preparation differs completely;
  drill close textual analysis across all three exam units; give Language Variation's specific content
  dedicated revision beyond AS-level technique.
- **During any paper**: read once for overall purpose before annotating features in detail.
- **In every analytical answer**: group features by effect, name the linguistic level and framework
  precisely, and link every point back to purpose, audience or mode.
