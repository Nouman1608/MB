These notes cover subtopic **1.4 Scalars and vectors** of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702, 2025–2027 series.

## Definitions

- **Scalar**: magnitude only.
- **Vector**: magnitude and direction.
- **Coplanar**: lying in the same plane.
- **Resultant**: the single vector with the same effect as two or more vectors combined.
- **Component**: one of two perpendicular vectors that together are equivalent to the original.

## Classify quickly

| Scalar | Vector |
|---|---|
| distance | displacement |
| speed | velocity |
| mass | weight (a force) |
| time, temperature | acceleration |
| energy, work, power | force, momentum |
| pressure, density | electric field strength, gravitational field strength |
| charge, p.d., resistance, current | |

**Pairs to keep apart:** distance and displacement; speed and velocity; mass and weight. Each pair has one scalar and one vector.

## Adding two vectors: which method?

| Situation | Method | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Perpendicular | Pythagoras + tan | R = √(a² + b²), θ = tan⁻¹(b/a) |
| Angle θ between them (tails together) | Cosine rule | R² = a² + b² + 2ab cos θ |
| Same thing, angle φ inside the triangle | Cosine rule | R² = a² + b² − 2ab cos φ, with φ = 180° − θ |
| Three or more vectors | Components | Rx = Σ x-parts, Ry = Σ y-parts |
| Any, if asked to draw | Scale diagram | Measure R and its angle |

**Recall example:** 7.0 N and 9.0 N with 120° between them: R² = 49 + 81 + 2(7.0)(9.0) cos 120° = 67; R = 8.2 N, smaller than the 9.0 N force alone, because at 120° the two forces partly oppose.

## Subtracting vectors

**a − b = a + (−b)**: reverse b, then add tip to tail.

- **Change in velocity** Δv = v − u always means final minus initial, as vectors.
- In one dimension, use signs: +5.0 m s⁻¹ then −3.0 m s⁻¹ gives Δv = −8.0 m s⁻¹.
- In two dimensions, draw v and −u tip to tail. A car turning from 20 m s⁻¹ north to 20 m s⁻¹ east at constant speed has |Δv| = √(20² + 20²) = 28 m s⁻¹, directed south-east.

## Resolving

```
along the reference direction:   F cos(theta)
perpendicular to it:             F sin(theta)
(theta measured FROM the reference direction)
```

- On a slope of angle θ: weight component **down the slope = mg sin θ**; **into the slope = mg cos θ**.
- For a rope pulling at θ above the horizontal: horizontal = T cos θ; vertical = T sin θ (this reduces the normal contact force).
- Components replace the vector: never use both in the same equation.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Tip to tail | Start each vector where the last one ends |
| Resultant direction | Always quote from a stated reference ("18° to the 5.0 kN force") |
| Negative of a vector | Same magnitude, opposite direction |
| Perpendicular components | Independent: motion along one does not affect the other |

## Exam traps

- Adding magnitudes of non-parallel vectors.
- Subtracting speeds to get a change in velocity.
- Using cos for the component opposite the given angle.
- Using the angle between the vectors in the "minus" form of the cosine rule.
- Giving a resultant without direction, or a direction without a reference.
- Listing pressure, energy or current as vectors.
- Drawing a vector triangle with arrows meeting head to head.

## Self-test

1. Define a vector quantity.
2. Classify: momentum, power, weight, density.
3. Forces of 12 N and 5.0 N act at right angles. Find the resultant.
4. A plane heads north at 120 m s⁻¹ in a wind blowing towards the east at 30 m s⁻¹. Find its resultant velocity.
5. Explain why 3 N and 4 N cannot have a resultant of 8 N.
6. A ball hits a wall at 8.0 m s⁻¹ and rebounds along the same line at 6.0 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of the change in velocity.
7. Resolve 80 N acting at 30° above the horizontal into horizontal and vertical components.
8. A 50 N box is on a 30° slope. Find the component of its weight down the slope.
9. Find a − b, where a = 6.0 m s⁻¹ east and b = 8.0 m s⁻¹ north.
10. Why is current treated as a scalar?
11. What is the largest and smallest possible resultant of 5 N and 9 N?
12. A walker goes 300 m north, then 400 m at 60° east of north. Find the magnitude of the displacement.

### Answers

1. A quantity with both magnitude and direction.
2. Momentum and weight are vectors; power and density are scalars.
3. √(144 + 25) = 13 N, at tan⁻¹(5.0/12) = 23° to the 12 N force.
4. √(120² + 30²) = 124 m s⁻¹ at tan⁻¹(30/120) = 14° east of north.
5. The largest possible resultant is 3 + 4 = 7 N, when the forces are parallel.
6. 8.0 + 6.0 = 14 m s⁻¹.
7. 80 cos 30° = 69 N horizontal; 80 sin 30° = 40 N vertical.
8. 50 sin 30° = 25 N.
9. a + (−b) = 6.0 east and 8.0 south: 10 m s⁻¹ at tan⁻¹(8.0/6.0) = 53° south of east.
10. Currents meeting at a junction add arithmetically, not by vector addition.
11. Largest 14 N (same direction); smallest 4 N (opposite directions).
12. East part 400 sin 60° = 346 m; north part 300 + 400 cos 60° = 500 m; magnitude √(346² + 500²) = 608 m ≈ 610 m.
