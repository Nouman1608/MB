These questions cover every focus point of Cambridge O Level History 2147 Option B key question 6, **How secure was the USSR's control over Eastern Europe, 1948-c.1989?** Each Paper 1 question is worth 20 marks: (a) 4 marks for describing, (b) 6 marks for explaining, (c) 10 marks for a supported judgement.

## Question 1: Hungary and Czechoslovakia

**(a)** Describe the reforms introduced in Czechoslovakia during the Prague Spring of 1968. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the USSR invaded Hungary in November 1956. **[6]**

**(c)** "Events in Hungary in 1956 and Czechoslovakia in 1968 were more similar than different." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** One mark per point: Dubček became party leader in January 1968; censorship of the press was relaxed or lifted; open criticism of the government was allowed; the Action Programme (April 1968) planned economic reform with some market elements; more freedom to travel was promised; the power of the secret police was reduced; he called it "socialism with a human face".

**(b)** Strong answers explain: Nagy's announcement on 1 November that Hungary would **leave the Warsaw Pact** threatened the USSR's buffer zone and military security; promises of **free elections** threatened one-party rule, which could spread to other satellites such as Poland; Khrushchev needed to show hardliners in Moscow that de-Stalinisation did not mean weakness; and the West, distracted by Suez, was unlikely to intervene.

**(c)** *Similar:* both were attempts to loosen Soviet control led by communist reformers (Nagy, Dubček); both were crushed by Soviet-led force; both leaders were removed; the West did nothing in either case. *Different:* Hungary aimed at neutrality and leaving the Warsaw Pact, Czechoslovakia promised to stay in it; Hungarians fought in the streets and thousands died, Czechs and Slovaks mostly resisted passively; 1956 was a Soviet action, 1968 a Warsaw Pact invasion followed by the Brezhnev Doctrine. *Judgement:* the outcome was the same, but the aims and methods differed sharply, so "more different in how they happened, similar in how they ended" is a defensible conclusion.

## Question 2: The Berlin Wall and Solidarity

**(a)** What was Solidarity? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Berlin Wall was built in 1961. **[6]**

**(c)** "Solidarity was the most important challenge to Soviet control in Eastern Europe before 1985." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** An independent trade union in Poland; formed after strikes at the Gdańsk shipyard in August 1980; led by Lech Wałęsa; it had around ten million members; supported by the Catholic Church; banned under martial law in December 1981 but survived underground.

**(b)** About three million East Germans had escaped through West Berlin since 1949, including many skilled young workers, damaging the economy; the flight embarrassed communism by showing people preferred the West; West Berlin's prosperity was a visible contrast; Khrushchev's attempts to force the Western powers out of Berlin had failed. Building the wall on 13 August 1961 sealed the escape route.

**(c)** *For:* Solidarity was a mass movement, not a reform from inside the party, and it lasted; it combined workers with the Church; even martial law could not destroy it, and it won the elections of 1989. *Against:* Hungary 1956 was the most direct challenge, aiming to leave the Warsaw Pact; the Prague Spring showed reform inside a communist party could spread; the flight of refugees before 1961 was a daily challenge that forced the wall. *Judgement:* earlier challenges were more dramatic, but Solidarity was more important because the regime could not crush it and it showed others a way forward.

## Question 3: Gorbachev and 1989

**(a)** Describe what happened to the Berlin Wall in November 1989. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Gorbachev introduced reforms after 1985. **[6]**

**(c)** "Gorbachev was personally responsible for the collapse of Soviet control over Eastern Europe." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** On 9 November 1989 the East German government announced that citizens could cross the border; crowds gathered at the checkpoints and guards opened them; Berliners climbed on and began breaking down the wall; thousands crossed into West Berlin; it became the symbol of communism's collapse, and Germany was reunified in October 1990.

**(b)** The Soviet economy was stagnating, with shortages and falling growth; the war in Afghanistan was costly; the arms race with the USA, including Reagan's spending, was unaffordable; and Gorbachev believed openness (glasnost) and restructuring (perestroika) would make communism more efficient and popular, not end it.

**(c)** *For:* he ended the Brezhnev Doctrine and refused to send troops in 1989; glasnost encouraged opposition; his troop cuts showed the USSR would not defend the regimes. *Against:* long-term economic weakness, Solidarity's success in Poland, nationalism, the Church and Western influence; communist regimes had lacked popular support since 1956 and 1968; ordinary people in Leipzig, Prague and Berlin made the revolutions. *Judgement:* Gorbachev made collapse possible by removing the threat of force, but the speed of collapse shows deep weaknesses already existed.

## Paper 2 style practice: sources (invented)

**Source A** *(invented for practice)* -- A Hungarian student leaflet, October 1956: "We demand the withdrawal of Soviet troops and free elections. Hungary belongs to Hungarians."

**Source B** *(invented for practice)* -- A Soviet newspaper report, November 1956: "Counter-revolutionary bands, armed by the West, attacked workers' homes. The Soviet army answered the Hungarian government's appeal for help."

**Question:** Why do Sources A and B give such different accounts of events in Hungary? Explain your answer using details of the sources and your own knowledge. **[8]**

**Guidance:** compare the content (popular national demands against a Western-backed counter-revolution), then explain the purpose and origin of each: A wants to win support for the rising; B justifies the invasion to Soviet readers. Own knowledge: Nagy's government had in fact asked the Soviets to leave and declared neutrality; the "appeal" came from Kádár's new government. A strong answer concludes that B is propaganda, but that A also gives only one side.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing Hungary (left the Warsaw Pact) with Czechoslovakia (stayed in).
- Giving a (c) answer with no counter-argument.
- Writing about 1989 without explaining Gorbachev's policies.
- Treating sources as true without considering who produced them and why.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many focus points does key question 2.6 have?**
Five: the 1956 and 1968 risings and Soviet reactions; their similarities; the Berlin Wall; Solidarity; Gorbachev's responsibility.

**Do I need numbers?**
Approximate figures (about 200,000 Hungarians fled; about three million East Germans left before 1961; ten million Solidarity members) strengthen explanations.
