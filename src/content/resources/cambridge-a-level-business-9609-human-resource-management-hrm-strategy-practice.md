All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define *soft HRM*. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** reason why a business competing mainly on low price might adopt hard HRM. **[3]**

**3.** Explain **one** advantage to a business of compressed working hours. **[2]**

**4.** Wexcombe Engineering had 2,450 working days lost to absence last year. It had an average of 70 employees, each with 250 working days available. Calculate the absenteeism rate. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** possible causes of poor employee performance, other than low motivation. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by an objective being "agreed" in Management by Objectives (MBO), and why this matters. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** way in which AI might change workforce planning. **[3]**

## Section B

**8.** Ferncastle Hotel needs extra housekeeping staff for a 12-week summer season: 5 staff working 32 hours a week. An agency charges $17.50 an hour. Alternatively, the hotel could employ its own temporary staff at $12.50 an hour, with recruitment costs of $300 per person and 8 hours of paid training each.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of each option and the difference between them. **[5]**

**(b)** Evaluate which option Ferncastle should choose. **[8]**

**9.** Ormsby Electronics has two assembly lines. Line A: 18 workers, output 43,200 units a year, reject rate 6%. Line B: 15 workers, output 40,500 units a year, reject rate 2%.

**(a)** Calculate output per worker on each line. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of rejected units on each line. **[2]**

**(c)** Analyse **two** possible causes of the weaker performance of Line A. **[6]**

**10.** Hartwell Accountancy employs 60 qualified accountants. Clients value continuity, and qualified staff are hard to recruit. A new finance director proposes replacing 15 permanent posts with gig-economy accountants paid per task, saving $180,000 a year.

Evaluate whether Hartwell should adopt this proposal. **[12]**

**11.** Penwood Retail plans to introduce MBO for its 40 store managers, linking a bonus to agreed sales objectives.

**(a)** Explain **two** steps in implementing MBO at Penwood. **[4]**

**(b)** Assess the usefulness of MBO for Penwood. **[8]**

**12.** Discuss the extent to which IT and AI will reduce the need for human resource staff in large businesses. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** An approach to managing people that treats employees as an asset to be developed [1], through training, consultation and long-term commitment [1].

**2.** Low prices require low unit costs, and labour is often a large cost [1]. Hard HRM uses flexible contracts so the business pays only for the hours it needs [1], keeping labour cost per unit low enough to protect profit at low prices [1].

**3.** Staff work full-time hours in fewer days, so the business can operate longer each day without overtime [1], or attract staff who value fewer travel days, helping recruitment [1].

**4.** Days available = 70 × 250 = 17,500 [1]. Absenteeism = 2,450 ÷ 17,500 × 100 = **14%** [1].

**5.** Any two: lack of skills or training [1]; poor equipment or systems [1]; unclear objectives; weak leadership; poor selection; health or personal problems.

**6.** The manager and employee discuss and settle the objective together rather than it being imposed [1]. This matters because the employee is more committed to a target they helped set [1] and can raise whether it is achievable with the resources available [1].

**7.** AI can analyse past data on labour turnover, absence and demand [1] to forecast how many staff and which skills will be needed [1], so recruitment and training can start earlier and gaps are avoided [1].

**8. (a)** Hours = 5 × 12 × 32 = 1,920 [1]. Agency = 1,920 × 17.50 = $33,600 [1]. Own staff wages = 1,920 × 12.50 = $24,000 [1]; recruitment 5 × 300 = $1,500; training 5 × 8 × 12.50 = $500; total $26,000 [1]. Difference = 33,600 − 26,000 = **$7,600** cheaper to use own staff [1].

**(b)** For own staff: saves $7,600, about 23% of the agency cost [1]; staff hired directly may return next summer, reducing future recruitment and training [1]; the hotel controls selection, so standards may be higher [1]. For the agency: quick to arrange and replaces absent staff at once [1], which matters because rooms must be ready daily [1]; no recruitment effort for managers in a busy season [1]. Judgement: own staff if there is time to recruit before the season [1], because the saving is large and returning staff build quality over the years; the agency only as backup for absences [1]. **[13]**

**9. (a)** Line A: 43,200 ÷ 18 = **2,400** units per worker [1]. Line B: 40,500 ÷ 15 = **2,700** units per worker [1].

**(b)** Line A: 43,200 × 0.06 = **2,592** [1]. Line B: 40,500 × 0.02 = **810** [1].

**(c)** Cause 1: a skills gap, for example newer or less trained workers on Line A [1], leading to slower work and more mistakes [1], which explains both lower output per worker and the higher reject rate [1]. Cause 2: poorer equipment on Line A [1], with breakdowns or worn tools [1] causing both lost time and faulty units [1]. **[10]**

**10.** For: saves $180,000 a year [1]; Hartwell pays only for tasks done, so cost falls in quiet periods [1]. Against: clients value continuity, but gig workers change from task to task [1], so client relationships and loyalty may suffer and fees may be lost [1]. Gig accountants have little loyalty and may favour other clients at busy times such as tax deadlines [1]. Remaining permanent staff may fear for their jobs, lowering morale and raising turnover [1], and qualified staff are hard to recruit, so losing them is costly [1]. Quality control over gig workers' work is harder, risking errors and reputational damage [1]. The firm's strategy appears to be quality and continuity, which suits soft HRM [1]. Possible compromise: use gig accountants only for peak-season overflow work [1]. Judgement: Hartwell should reject the full proposal, because lost client fees could exceed the $180,000 saving [1]; the limited use of gig workers for peaks is reasonable if quality checks are in place [1]. **[12]**

**11. (a)** Step 1: set Penwood's overall sales objective and divide it between stores [1], so each store objective supports the company target [1]. Step 2: agree each manager's objective in a meeting, with the resources needed [1], then review progress regularly and reward results [1].

**(b)** Useful: managers know what is expected and feel involved [1], and the bonus links their effort to company goals [1]. Results can be compared across 40 stores, helping identify best practice [1]. Limits: sales may depend on local factors managers cannot control, such as a new competitor [1], so the bonus may feel unfair [1]. Managers may push sales at the expense of customer service or staff welfare, which is harder to measure [1]. Objectives set early in the year may go out of date [1]. Judgement: useful if objectives allow for local conditions and include a service measure as well as sales [1]. **[12]**

**12.** IT and AI automate routine HR work: payroll, records, scheduling and answering standard queries [1], so fewer administrative HR staff are needed [1]. AI screening reduces the time spent reading applications [1]. However, HR work increasingly involves judgement: grievances, discipline, developing managers and planning skills [1], which needs human understanding and trust [1]. AI decisions must be checked for bias and comply with data-protection rules [1], which creates new HR tasks [1]. Staff may resist being managed by systems, so human contact remains important for morale [1]. Judgement: IT and AI will reduce the number of HR administrative roles [1], but the need for skilled HR professionals may stay the same or change rather than fall, depending on how much the business values employee relations [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting recruitment or training costs when comparing contract options.
- Calculating absenteeism without first finding the days available.
- Giving causes of poor performance that do not fit the evidence in the case.
- Evaluating gig work without considering the business's competitive strategy.
- Describing MBO steps without assessing usefulness for this workforce.
