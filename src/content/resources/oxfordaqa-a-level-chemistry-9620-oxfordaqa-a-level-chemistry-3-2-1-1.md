This guide goes deeper into **3.2.1.1 Classification**, the first point of section 3.2.1 Periodicity in the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification. The point has a single statement: "An element is classified as s, p, d or f block according to its position in the Periodic Table, which is determined by its proton number." It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 and available again in the synoptic Unit 5.

The statement is short, but it links three things you must handle confidently: proton number, electron configuration (from 3.1.1.3, for atoms and ions up to Z = 36) and position in the table. The [periodicity study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-periodicity/) introduces the blocks alongside the Period 3 trends. This page works through the reasoning step by step and deals with the cases that catch students out. Afterwards, use the [classification revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-1-1-revision-notes/) and the [classification practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-2-1-1-practice/).

## From proton number to block

The chain of reasoning is always the same:

1. **Proton number (Z)** fixes the number of electrons in a neutral atom.
2. Electrons fill sub-shells in order of energy: 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p, and so on.
3. The elements are arranged in order of proton number, in rows (**periods**) and columns (**groups**), so that each region of the table corresponds to one type of sub-shell being filled.
4. That region is the element's **block**.

So an element's block is a property of its **position**, and its position is a consequence of its **proton number**.

## Why the blocks have the widths they do

Each block is as wide as the number of electrons its sub-shell can hold.

| Block | Sub-shell being filled | Orbitals in that sub-shell | Electrons it holds | Columns in the block |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| s | s | 1 | 2 | 2 (Groups 1 and 2, plus helium) |
| p | p | 3 | 6 | 6 (Groups 3 to 0, or 13 to 18) |
| d | d | 5 | 10 | 10 (between Groups 2 and 3) |
| f | f | 7 | 14 | 14 (the lanthanide and actinide rows) |

This is why the table has its familiar shape. Each new period begins when a new principal shell starts to fill with an s electron.

## Period and group from the configuration

- The **period** is the number of the outermost occupied shell. Sodium (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s¹) has its outer electron in shell 3, so it is in Period 3.
- For the s and p blocks, the **group** number (1 to 7, then 0) equals the number of electrons in the outer shell. Chlorine, 3s² 3p⁵, has seven outer electrons: Group 7.

### Worked example 1: classify from proton number

Classify the elements with Z = 20, 26 and 33.

```
Z = 20   1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s²              outer shell 4, filling 4s    -> Period 4, s block (calcium)
Z = 26   1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁶ 4s²          filling 3d                    -> Period 4, d block (iron)
Z = 33   1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³     filling 4p                    -> Period 4, p block, Group 5 (arsenic)
```

For iron, 4s fills before 3d, but the electrons that distinguish iron from calcium and the elements in between are in 3d. For arsenic, the 3d sub-shell is already full, so the electrons being added are in 4p.

### Worked example 2: classify from a description of position

(a) An element is in Period 3, Group 2. State its block and write its configuration.
Groups 1 and 2 are the s block. Period 3 means the outer shell is n = 3, and Group 2 means two outer electrons: **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s²** (magnesium), **s block**.

(b) An element is in Period 4 and is the fourth element of the d block. State its configuration.
The d block in Period 4 starts at scandium (3d¹ 4s²). The fourth element has four 3d electrons in the usual filling pattern, but chromium is one of the two exceptions: **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁵ 4s¹**. It is still in the **d block**, because the position, not the exact arrangement of the last few electrons, decides the block.

## The cases that need care

### Hydrogen and helium

Hydrogen (1s¹) and helium (1s²) both have electrons only in an s sub-shell, so both are **s block**. Helium is placed above the noble gases in Group 0 because its chemistry matches theirs: its outer shell is full. In a "which block?" question, helium is s block even though it sits on the right-hand side of the table.

### Chromium and copper

In Period 4 the d block runs from scandium (Z = 21) to zinc (Z = 30). Two elements do not follow the simple filling order:

```
Cr (Z = 24)   [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹      (not 3d⁴ 4s²)
Cu (Z = 29)   [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹     (not 3d⁹ 4s²)
```

Both are still d-block elements. Here [Ar] is shorthand for 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶.

### Zinc

Zinc, [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s², is a **d-block** element: it sits in the d block and its 3d sub-shell fills during that row. It is not, however, counted as a transition metal in this specification, which describes transition metal properties for Ti to Cu (3.2.5.1). "d block" and "transition metal" are not the same thing.

### Ions

The block belongs to the **element**, not to an ion of it. Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵, and Na⁺ has the same configuration as neon, but iron is still a d-block element and sodium is still s block. When writing configurations of d-block ions, remember the 4s electrons are lost before the 3d electrons: Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, not [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s².

### The f block

The f-block elements are the lanthanides and actinides, usually printed as two rows below the main table. Configurations beyond Z = 36 are not required by this specification, so f-block questions are about **position**: an element in one of those two detached rows is f block. Periodic tables differ slightly in how they place the elements at either end of those rows, so use the table you are given.

## Period 3 and Period 4 at a glance

| Period | s block | d block | p block |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | Na, Mg | none | Al, Si, P, S, Cl, Ar |
| 4 | K, Ca | Sc to Zn | Ga, Ge, As, Se, Br, Kr |

Period 3 has no d block because the 3d sub-shell is not filled until after 4s, in Period 4. Aluminium is the first p-block element of Period 3: its outer configuration is 3s² 3p¹.

## Common mistakes

- Filling 3d before 4s, and so placing potassium or calcium in the d block.
- Calling helium p block because it is in Group 0.
- Calling zinc a transition metal because it is in the d block.
- Assigning a block to an ion using the ion's configuration.
- Placing aluminium in the s block because it is a metal.
- Writing chromium as 3d⁴ 4s² or copper as 3d⁹ 4s² in an atom.

## Exam technique

- **"State the block"**: one letter is enough, but make sure it is the element, not the ion, being classified.
- **"Explain why element X is in the p block"**: say that its outer (highest-energy) electrons are in a p sub-shell, or that the p sub-shell is being filled in that part of the table.
- **"Deduce the position"**: write the full configuration first; the outer shell number gives the period and, for s and p blocks, the number of outer electrons gives the group.
- **"Write the electron configuration"**: either full or [Ar] shorthand is accepted unless the question says otherwise, but give sub-shells in a consistent order.
