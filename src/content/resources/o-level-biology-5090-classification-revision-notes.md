## 2.1 Classification systems

### Definitions to learn word for word

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Classification | Sorting organisms into groups by the features they share |
| Species | A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring |
| Binomial system | An internationally agreed system in which the scientific name of an organism is made up of two parts showing the genus and species |
| Dichotomous key | A key in which each step splits the organisms into two groups using one identifiable feature |

### Binomial names: four rules

1. Genus first, with a capital letter: *Panthera*.
2. Species second, all lower case: *leo*.
3. Printed in italics; **underlined** when handwritten.
4. Same genus means more closely related than different genera.

Worked reminder: *Oryza sativa* (Asian rice) and *Oryza glaberrima* (African rice) share the genus *Oryza*, so they are more closely related to each other than either is to maize, *Zea mays*.

### Method in steps: constructing a dichotomous key

```
1. List the organisms and the visible features of each.
2. Choose ONE feature that splits the list into two groups.
3. Write two opposite statements about it (1a / 1b).
4. Repeat for each group until every organism stands alone.
5. Check: n organisms -> n - 1 couplets; each name appears once.
6. Test the key by running every organism through it.
```

Good features: number of legs, wings present or absent, leaf divided or not, veins parallel or branching, shell present or absent.
Poor features: colour, size, "lives in ponds", "fast-moving".

### Method in steps: using a key

```
1. Start at couplet 1. Read BOTH statements before choosing.
2. Follow the one that fits the specimen to the next number.
3. Write down the route (e.g. 1b -> 5a) if asked.
4. Stop when you reach a name.
```

### Small worked reminder: a four-animal key

Four vertebrates: a pigeon, a shark, a toad and a lizard. Four organisms need 4 − 1 = 3 couplets.

```
1 a  Has feathers ............................ pigeon
  b  No feathers ............................. go to 2
2 a  Has fins ................................ shark
  b  No fins ................................. go to 3
3 a  Dry skin covered in scales .............. lizard
  b  Moist skin with no scales ............... toad
```

Check it: each couplet asks about one feature, and each animal appears once. Notice that the key uses features straight from the vertebrate table below. That is a quick way to build a key in the exam: pick features the syllabus already lists for each group.

## 2.2 Features of organisms

### Classifying in steps (2.2.4)

```
1. Kingdom first: nucleus? cell wall? chloroplasts? hyphae?
2. If Animal: backbone (vertebrate) or jointed legs + exoskeleton (arthropod)?
3. Then the group: use the tables below.
4. Quote one listed feature for EACH step you name.
```

Worked reminder: an organism has a nucleus, a cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts, flowers, and leaves with a branching network of veins. Plant kingdom (cellulose wall, chloroplasts), flowering plant (flowers), dicotyledon (network veins).

### Five kingdoms at a glance

| Kingdom | Nucleus? | Cell wall? | Chloroplasts? | Cells | Other main features |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Animal | Yes | No | No | Many | Feed on organic substances made by other organisms |
| Plant | Yes | Yes, cellulose | Yes (in many cells) | Many | Photosynthesis |
| Fungus | Yes | Yes, not cellulose | No | Usually many (yeast: one) | Hyphae; secrete enzymes and absorb food |
| Prokaryote | **No** | Yes | No | One | Circular DNA free in cytoplasm; plasmids; ribosomes |
| Protoctist | Yes | Some | Some | Mostly one | A mixed group: some plant-like, some animal-like |

### Vertebrates

| Group | Skin covering | Eggs / young | Other |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mammals | Hair or fur | Most give birth to live young | External ears (pinnae); mammary glands |
| Birds | Feathers | Hard-shelled eggs | Beak; wings |
| Reptiles | Dry scales | Rubbery or leathery-shelled eggs, laid on land | |
| Amphibians | Moist, no scales | Soft eggs without shells, laid in water | Larvae (tadpoles) with gills; adults with lungs |
| Fish | Wet scales | | Fins; gills; lateral line |

### Arthropods

All arthropods: **jointed legs, segmented body, exoskeleton**.

| Group | Body sections | Pairs of legs | Pairs of antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insects | 3 | 3 | 1 |
| Arachnids | 2 | 4 | 0 |
| Crustaceans | Often 2 | More than 4 | 2 |
| Myriapods | Many segments | 1 or 2 per segment | 1 |

Memory hook: count the **legs first**, then the **antennae**. Three pairs means insect; four pairs means arachnid.

Worked reminder: a small animal has jointed legs, a body in two sections, five pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae. Jointed legs and an exoskeleton make it an arthropod. More than four pairs of legs and two pairs of antennae make it a crustacean. Watch whether a question gives legs or **pairs** of legs: ten legs is five pairs, not ten pairs.

### Viruses

- Features (syllabus limit): **protein coat** and **genetic material**.
- Not cells; not in any of the five kingdoms.
- Can only **replicate inside living cells**, using the host cell.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Prokaryote vs other kingdoms.** Prokaryotes have DNA but no nucleus. All the other kingdoms have a nucleus.
- **Plant vs Fungus.** Both have cell walls; only plants have cellulose walls and chloroplasts.
- **Reptile vs amphibian.** Dry scales and shelled eggs on land, against moist scaleless skin and soft eggs in water.
- **Insect vs arachnid.** Three pairs of legs and antennae, against four pairs of legs and no antennae.
- **Crustacean vs myriapod.** Two pairs of antennae, against one pair with legs on every segment.
- **Fern vs flowering plant.** Spores against seeds; no flowers against flowers.
- **Virus vs prokaryote.** A prokaryote is a cell with a membrane, cytoplasm and ribosomes. A virus is a protein coat around genetic material.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the definition of a species.
2. Write the binomial name of the lion correctly as you would by hand.
3. Two organisms are *Musca domestica* and *Musca autumnalis*. What does the shared first word tell you?
4. How many couplets does a dichotomous key need to separate nine organisms?
5. Why should colour not be used in a key?
6. Which kingdom has cells with no nucleus?
7. State two features that place a mushroom in the Fungus kingdom.
8. An animal has moist skin with no scales and lays eggs in water. Which vertebrate group?
9. An arthropod has three body sections and one pair of antennae. How many pairs of legs would you expect?
10. A leaf has parallel veins. Monocotyledon or dicotyledon?
11. How do ferns reproduce?
12. Why can a virus not be grown in a flask of nutrients with no cells in it?

### Answers

1. A group of organisms that can reproduce to produce fertile offspring.
2. Panthera leo, with each word underlined, capital P, lower-case l.
3. They are in the same genus (*Musca*), so they are closely related but different species.
4. 9 − 1 = **8** couplets.
5. Colour varies between individuals of the same species, so it is not reliable.
6. Prokaryote.
7. Any two: cell wall not made of cellulose; no chloroplasts; body of hyphae; secretes enzymes and absorbs the products.
8. Amphibians.
9. Three pairs (it is an insect).
10. Monocotyledon.
11. By spores, formed on the underside of the fronds.
12. A virus can only replicate inside a living cell; it has only a protein coat and genetic material, so it cannot copy itself on its own.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "can reproduce" for a species without "to produce **fertile** offspring": the key word earns the mark.
- Capitalising the species name or leaving a handwritten binomial name without underlining.
- Constructing a key where one couplet compares two different features, such as "has wings" against "has six legs".
- Using colour, size or habitat in a key, or leaving one organism unidentified at the end.
- Saying prokaryotes "have no DNA" or "no genetic material" instead of "no nucleus".
- Stating that fungi photosynthesise, or that fungal cell walls are made of cellulose.
- Mixing up arthropod counts: giving antennae to arachnids, or four pairs of legs to insects.
- Giving vague features such as "it has skin" or "it has legs", which apply to many groups and earn no credit.
- Calling a virus a cell or putting it in the Prokaryote kingdom.
