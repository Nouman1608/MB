## Section A

**1.** Define density and write the equation that links density, mass and volume. **[2]**

**2.** Convert **(a)** 7.8 g/cm³ into kg/m³ and **(b)** 1250 kg/m³ into g/cm³. **[2]**

**3.** A block measures 6.0 cm × 3.0 cm × 2.0 cm and has a mass of 97.2 g.

**(a)** Calculate its volume. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its density in g/cm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Express the density in kg/m³. **[1]**

**4.** Sea water has a density of 1030 kg/m³. Calculate the mass of 2.0 m³ of sea water. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** A metal rod has a diameter of 1.2 cm, a length of 8.0 cm and a mass of 70.6 g.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of the rod. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the density of the metal in g/cm³. **[2]**

**6.** An empty measuring cylinder has a mass of 62.4 g. When it contains 60.0 cm³ of a liquid, its mass is 138.0 g. Calculate the density of the liquid. **[3]**

**7.** A student lowers a stone of mass 98.9 g into a measuring cylinder. The water level rises from 30.0 cm³ to 41.5 cm³. Calculate the density of the stone. **[3]**

**8.** A piece of copper has a mass of 0.445 kg. The density of copper is 8900 kg/m³. Calculate its volume in m³ and in cm³. **[3]**

## Section C

**9.** Describe how to determine the density of an irregularly shaped stone that sinks in water. Include the apparatus, the measurements and the calculation. **[5]**

**10.** In the experiment in Question 9, explain the effect on the calculated density of:

**(a)** air bubbles clinging to the stone **[2]**
**(b)** weighing the stone after it has been in the water **[2]**

**11.** A student measures the mass of different volumes of a liquid.

| Volume / cm³ | 20.0 | 40.0 | 60.0 | 80.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mass of liquid / g | 18.4 | 36.6 | 55.2 | 73.6 |

**(a)** Describe the graph of mass against volume. **[1]**
**(b)** Use the gradient to find the density of the liquid. **[2]**

**12.** Two blocks each have a mass of 270 g. Block A has a volume of 100 cm³ and block B has a volume of 34.6 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the density of each block. **[2]**
**(b)** Aluminium has a density of 2.7 g/cm³. State which block could be aluminium and why the answer is "could be" rather than "is". **[2]**

**13.** A large block of wax is cut into two unequal pieces. Explain why both pieces have the same density as the original block. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Density is **mass per unit volume** [1]; ρ = m ÷ V [1].

**2. (a)** 7.8 × 1000 = **7800 kg/m³** [1]. **(b)** 1250 ÷ 1000 = **1.25 g/cm³** [1].

**3. (a)** V = 6.0 × 3.0 × 2.0 = **36 cm³** [1].
**(b)** ρ = 97.2 ÷ 36 [1] = **2.7 g/cm³** [1].
**(c)** **2700 kg/m³** [1].

**4.** m = ρV = 1030 × 2.0 [1] = **2060 kg** [1].

**5. (a)** r = 0.60 cm; V = π × 0.60² × 8.0 [1] = **9.05 cm³** [1].
**(b)** ρ = 70.6 ÷ 9.05 [1] = **7.80 g/cm³** [1].

**6.** Mass of liquid = 138.0 − 62.4 = 75.6 g [1]; ρ = 75.6 ÷ 60.0 [1] = **1.26 g/cm³** (1260 kg/m³) [1].

**7.** V = 41.5 − 30.0 = 11.5 cm³ [1]; ρ = 98.9 ÷ 11.5 [1] = **8.6 g/cm³** [1].

**8.** V = m ÷ ρ = 0.445 ÷ 8900 [1] = **5.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³** [1] = **50 cm³** [1].

**9.** Measure the mass of the **dry** stone on an electronic balance [1]. Part-fill a measuring cylinder with water and record the volume, reading the bottom of the meniscus at eye level [1]. Lower the stone **gently on a thread** until fully submerged [1] and record the new volume [1]. Volume of stone = final − initial reading; density = mass ÷ volume [1].

**10. (a)** Bubbles add to the volume displaced, so the **measured volume is too large** [1] and the calculated density is **too small** [1].
**(b)** Water clinging to the stone adds to its measured mass, so the **mass is too large** [1] and the calculated density is **too large** [1].

**11. (a)** A straight line through the origin [1].
**(b)** Gradient = 73.6 ÷ 80.0 [1] = **0.92 g/cm³** (920 kg/m³) [1].

**12. (a)** A: 270 ÷ 100 = **2.7 g/cm³** [1]; B: 270 ÷ 34.6 = **7.8 g/cm³** [1].
**(b)** **Block A** has a density matching aluminium [1]; other materials could have a similar density, and the measurement has some uncertainty, so a matching density is consistent with aluminium but does not prove it [1].

**13.** Density depends only on the **material**, not the amount [1]. Each piece's mass and volume are reduced in the **same proportion**, so mass ÷ volume is unchanged [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the diameter instead of the radius in πr²l.
- Giving the final displacement reading as the volume.
- Forgetting to subtract the empty cylinder's mass.
- Dividing by 1000 when converting g/cm³ to kg/m³.
- Describing the displacement method without saying the stone is weighed dry and lowered gently.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these density questions from past papers?**
No. They are original practice questions for subtopic 1.4 and do not copy the exam's structure, question count or mark tariffs.

**Should density answers be in g/cm³ or kg/m³?**
Either is correct unless the question specifies one. Use the unit that matches your data, and convert only if asked.

**Why is the gradient method better than one reading?**
It uses several measurements, so one poor reading has less effect, and it reveals any reading that does not fit the line.
