These notes condense section 13.2, International debt and international aid, of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Geography (9696) syllabus for 2025 and 2026 (Paper 4, Topic 13 option). For full explanations and a worked calculation, use the [13.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-international-debt-and-international-aid/); then test yourself with the [13.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-international-debt-and-international-aid-practice/).

## The syllabus statements

1. The causes, nature and problems of debt for countries.
2. The international debt crisis and debt relief.
3. Types of aid and donors: relief, development, tied, bilateral, multilateral.
4. Critically evaluate the impacts of international aid on receiving countries.

## Debt: nature and measures

- **Creditors:** bilateral (governments), multilateral (World Bank, IMF, regional banks), commercial (banks, bondholders).
- **Debt-to-GDP** = debt ÷ GDP × 100.
- **Debt service** = interest + principal repayments due in a year.
- **Debt service ratio** = debt service ÷ export earnings × 100.
- Most external debt is in foreign currency, so depreciation raises its local cost.

## Causes chain

Borrowing → spent on projects that do not raise exports or tax → commodity prices fall or interest rates rise or currency falls → debt service ratio rises → more borrowing or default.

Other causes: trade deficits, shocks (drought, conflict, pandemic), corruption and capital flight, "odious debt".

## Problems

- Crowding out of health, education and infrastructure.
- Austerity and other conditions attached to rescue loans.
- Default: loss of credit and higher borrowing costs.
- Currency crises and inflation.
- Loss of policy control to creditors.

## Crisis and relief timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1970s | Oil exporters' deposits lent to developing countries |
| 1982 | Mexico cannot service its debt; crisis spreads |
| 1987 | First debt-for-nature swap, Bolivia |
| 1989 | Brady Plan: bank loans exchanged for reduced-value bonds |
| 1996, 1999 | HIPC Initiative launched and enhanced |
| Late 1990s | Jubilee 2000 campaign for cancellation |
| 2005 | Multilateral Debt Relief Initiative |
| Ongoing | Paris Club of creditor governments coordinates rescheduling |

**Evaluation:** relief freed money for social spending but treated the stock, not the causes; debt can rebuild through new lenders and bonds.

## Aid types as three dimensions

| Dimension | Types |
|---|---|
| Purpose | Relief (short-term) or development (long-term) |
| Conditions | Tied or untied |
| Channel | Bilateral or multilateral |

Example: a government grant for a road built by its own firms = bilateral, tied, development aid.

**Donors:** governments (official development assistance), multilateral agencies, NGOs and charities, private foundations, partnerships such as Gavi (founded 2000).

## Impacts of aid

- **Positive:** lives saved in emergencies; disease reduced (smallpox eradicated in 1980 through a WHO-led campaign); infrastructure and education; pooled expertise; community-level NGO projects.
- **Negative:** food aid undercuts farmers; dependency; tied aid benefits donors; conditions override local priorities; corruption; loans add to debt.

## The aid debate

- **Jeffrey Sachs**, *The End of Poverty* (2005): more, well-targeted aid can break poverty traps.
- **William Easterly**, *The White Man's Burden* (2006): critic of large top-down plans; favours small, accountable projects.
- **Dambisa Moyo**, *Dead Aid* (2009): large government-to-government aid harmed African development.

**Evaluation structure:** type, channel, scale, timescale, context (governance).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| External debt | Money owed to foreign creditors |
| Debt service ratio | Debt service as a percentage of export earnings |
| Default | Failure to make a scheduled debt payment |
| Rescheduling | Extending the period for repayment |
| Structural adjustment | Policy conditions attached to IMF and World Bank loans |
| Debt relief | Reduction or cancellation of debt |
| Debt-for-nature swap | Debt cancelled in return for conservation spending |
| Official development assistance | Concessional aid from governments |
| Concessional loan | Loan below market interest rates, with long repayment periods |
| Tied aid | Aid that must be spent on donor goods or services |
| Multilateral aid | Aid pooled through international organisations |
| Dependency | Reliance on aid that weakens local effort or revenue |

## Exam traps

- Debt is the stock; debt service is the yearly flow.
- Aid types overlap: classify each flow on all three dimensions.
- Not all aid is a grant; loans add to debt.
- Debt relief did not end debt problems.
- Evaluate aid by type and context, not as one thing.
- Use named viewpoints for "critically evaluate".

## Self-test

1. Name the three types of creditor.
2. A country's debt service is US$3 billion and export earnings US$12 billion. Calculate the debt service ratio.
3. Why does a fall in the currency raise the burden of external debt?
4. Give two problems caused by high debt.
5. What happened in Mexico in 1982?
6. What did the Brady Plan do?
7. What is a debt-for-nature swap, and where was the first?
8. Classify: an NGO provides tents and water after a flood.
9. Classify: a donor government lends money for a dam built by its own firms.
10. Give two positive and two negative impacts of aid.
11. What did Dambisa Moyo argue?
12. Name five criteria for evaluating aid.

### Answers

1. Bilateral, multilateral, commercial.
2. 3 ÷ 12 × 100 = 25%.
3. Debt is in foreign currency, so more local currency is needed to pay it.
4. Any two: crowding out, austerity conditions, loss of credit, currency crisis, loss of policy control.
5. It announced it could not service its debt, starting the crisis.
6. Exchanged bank loans for bonds of lower value, reducing debts.
7. Debt cancelled in return for conservation spending; Bolivia, 1987.
8. Relief aid from an NGO (untied).
9. Bilateral, tied, development aid (and a loan, so it adds to debt).
10. Positive: lives saved, disease reduced, infrastructure, education. Negative: dependency, tied benefits, food aid undercutting farmers, corruption.
11. Large government-to-government aid harmed African development through dependency and corruption.
12. Type, channel, scale, timescale, context.
