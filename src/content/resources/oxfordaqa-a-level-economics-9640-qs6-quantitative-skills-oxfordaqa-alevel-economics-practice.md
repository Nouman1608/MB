## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define average revenue and explain why it equals price when every unit is sold at the same price. **[2]**

**2.** A tutoring firm has fixed costs of 1,800 a month. It gives 120 lessons a month at an average variable cost of 14 per lesson. Calculate total cost and average cost. **[2]**

**3.** A juice bar sells 640 drinks in a week at 3.50 each. Its total cost for the week is 1,920. Calculate total revenue, profit and profit per drink. **[3]**

**4.** A firm's average fixed cost is 5.50 when it produces 80 units. Calculate its fixed cost and its average fixed cost at 110 units. **[2]**

**5.** (A2) A firm's total cost is 3,100 at 200 units and 3,300 at 220 units. Calculate marginal cost per unit over this range, and use it to explain what happens to average cost. **[3]**

**6.** (A2) A firm sells 50 units at 20 each. To sell 60 units it must cut its price to 18.50 on every unit. Calculate marginal revenue per unit and explain why it is below the new price. **[3]**

**7.** A firm's total cost includes normal profit. Which statement is correct when its total revenue equals total cost? **[1]**

A: The firm is making a loss.

B: The firm is earning normal profit.

C: The firm is earning supernormal profit.

D: Average revenue is greater than average cost.

### Section B

**8.** A ceramics workshop has fixed costs of 300 a week. The table gives one figure at each output.

| Output (pieces) | Given figure |
|---|---|
| 10 | TC = 500 |
| 20 | AVC = 18 |
| 30 | AC = 26 |
| 40 | VC = 800 |

**(a)** Calculate total cost, variable cost and average cost at each output. **[4]**

**(b)** State the output with the lowest average cost. **[1]**

**(c)** (A2) Calculate marginal cost per piece for the steps from 20 to 30 and from 30 to 40 pieces, and use them to explain why average cost rises after 30 pieces. **[3]**

**9.** (A2) A bike-hire stall has fixed costs of 80 a day. The table shows the marginal cost of each extra group hire and the price at which each number of hires can be sold.

| Hires | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Price | 70 | 64 | 58 | 52 | 46 | 40 |
| MC | 30 | 22 | 20 | 26 | 36 | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate total revenue and marginal revenue at each output. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate total cost and profit at each output. **[2]**

**(c)** Identify the profit-maximising number of hires and use marginal values to explain why. **[2]**

**10.** A theatre company has fixed costs of 18,000 for a run of performances and a variable cost of 4 per ticket. Option 1 is a price of 25 with 1,200 tickets sold. Option 2 is a price of 34 with 860 tickets sold.

**(a)** Calculate total revenue, total cost and profit for each option. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate average cost for each option, to two decimal places. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why option 2 earns more profit even though its revenue is lower. **[2]**

**11.** The owner of a garden-design business could earn 30,000 a year working for another firm. This year the business has revenue of 140,000 and other costs of 104,000.

**(a)** Calculate supernormal profit, treating normal profit as a cost. **[2]**

**(b)** Next year other costs rise to 112,000 with revenue unchanged. Explain what this means for the owner's decision to stay in the industry. **[2]**

**12.** A manufacturer reports: "Our average cost fell by 10% this year, so the business is now more profitable." In year 1 it sold 5,000 units at 15 with an average cost of 12. In year 2 it cut its price to 12.80, sold 6,500 units, and average cost was 10.80. Using the data, assess the manufacturer's claim. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Average revenue is total revenue divided by the quantity sold, or revenue per unit [1]. If every unit sells at the same price, TR = P x Q, so TR / Q = P [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the algebra in one line; it is quicker and clearer than a paragraph.

**2.** TC = 1,800 + (14 x 120) = 1,800 + 1,680 = **3,480** [1]. AC = 3,480 / 120 = **29** per lesson [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Turn AVC into total variable cost before adding fixed cost; adding 14 to 1,800 is a common slip.

**3.** TR = 640 x 3.50 = **2,240** [1]. Profit = 2,240 - 1,920 = **320** [1]. Profit per drink = 320 / 640 = **0.50** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Label which figure is total profit and which is per unit; they are easy to confuse in a hurry.

**4.** FC = 5.50 x 80 = **440** [1]. AFC at 110 units = 440 / 110 = **4.00** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Fixed cost is unchanged at the new output; only its average falls.

**5.** MC = (3,300 - 3,100) / (220 - 200) = 200 / 20 = **10** per unit [1]. AC at 200 units is 15.50 and the extra units cost only 10 each, which is below AC [1], so AC falls, to 3,300 / 220 = 15.00 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the 20-unit step; quoting 200 as the marginal cost loses the mark.

**6.** TR rises from 50 x 20 = 1,000 to 60 x 18.50 = 1,110 [1]. MR = 110 / 10 = **11** per unit [1]. MR is below price because the lower price applies to the first 50 units as well, so 1.50 is lost on each of them [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the loss on existing sales; that is why MR and price differ.

**7.** **B** [1]. When normal profit is included in costs, TR = TC means the firm earns exactly normal profit. **[1]**

**8. (a)** One mark per correct row [4]:

| Output | TC | VC | AC |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 500 | 200 | 50.00 |
| 20 | 660 | 360 | 33.00 |
| 30 | 780 | 480 | 26.00 |
| 40 | 1,100 | 800 | 27.50 |

**(b)** **30 pieces** [1].

**(c)** 20 to 30: (780 - 660) / 10 = **12** per piece [1]. 30 to 40: (1,100 - 780) / 10 = **32** per piece [1]. The pieces after 30 each cost 32, more than the AC of 26, so they pull the average up [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* State the rule (MC above AC raises AC) with the two figures side by side.

**9. (a)** TR: 70, 128, 174, 208, 230, 240. MR: 70, 58, 46, 34, 22, 10 [1 for each row correct]. 

**(b)** TC: 110, 132, 152, 178, 214, 264. Profit: -40, -4, 22, 30, 16, -24 [1 for each row correct].

**(c)** **4 hires**, with profit of 30 [1]. The fourth hire has MR 34 above MC 26, so it adds 8 to profit; the fifth has MR 22 below MC 36, so it would cut profit by 14 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Build TC by adding each MC to the previous total, starting from fixed cost of 80.

**10. (a)** Option 1: TR = 25 x 1,200 = 30,000; TC = 18,000 + 4,800 = 22,800; profit = **7,200** [1] [1]. Option 2: TR = 34 x 860 = 29,240; TC = 18,000 + 3,440 = 21,440; profit = **7,800** [1] [1].

**(b)** Option 1: 22,800 / 1,200 = **19.00** [1]. Option 2: 21,440 / 860 = **24.93** [1].

**(c)** Revenue falls by 760 [1], but 340 fewer tickets saves 340 x 4 = 1,360 of variable cost, so profit rises by 600 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the change in revenue with the change in cost; average cost alone would point to the wrong option.

**11. (a)** 140,000 - 104,000 = 36,000 accounting profit [1]; 36,000 - 30,000 = **6,000** supernormal profit [1].

**(b)** Profit after other costs is 28,000, which is 2,000 less than normal profit [1]. The owner earns less than the best alternative, so in the long run has an incentive to leave the industry unless costs fall or revenue rises [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Make the link to the decision explicit; normal profit matters because of what the owner gives up.

**12.** Year 1: TR = 75,000, TC = 12 x 5,000 = 60,000, profit = 15,000 [1]. Year 2: TR = 12.80 x 6,500 = 83,200, TC = 10.80 x 6,500 = 70,200, profit = 13,000 [1]. AC did fall by 10%, from 12 to 10.80 [1], but profit fell by 2,000, because profit per unit fell from 3.00 to 2.00 and the extra 1,500 units did not make up for it [1]. The claim confuses lower cost per unit with higher profit [1]. Judgement: on these figures the claim is wrong, although the larger output may raise market share and future profit if the lower price is temporary [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculate profit for both years before judging; the data contradict the claim, and saying so with figures earns the evaluation marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving profit per unit where total profit was asked.
- Adding AVC to FC without multiplying by output.
- Not dividing by the size of the output step for MC or MR.
- Leaving fixed cost out when building TC from MC.
- Choosing an option on revenue alone.
- Rounding average cost too early.
