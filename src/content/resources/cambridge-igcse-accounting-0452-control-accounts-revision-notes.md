Condensed notes for sub-topic **3.4 Control accounts**. Fully worked sales and purchases ledger control accounts are in the [3.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-control-accounts/); test yourself with the [3.4 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-control-accounts-practice/).

## What 3.4 asks for

- Purposes of purchases ledger and sales ledger control accounts.
- Books of prime entry as the sources of the entries.
- Preparing both accounts with credit sales and purchases, receipts and payments, cash discounts, returns, irrecoverable debts, dishonoured cheques, interest on overdue accounts, contra entries, refunds, and opening and closing debit and credit balances.
- The impact of digital recording.
- Not required: reconciling control balances with lists of ledger balances.

## Purposes

1. Arithmetic check on a ledger, so errors can be located.
2. Quick totals of trade receivables and trade payables.
3. Control over staff: a second person keeps the control account, discouraging fraud.
4. Summary information for credit control.

## Sales ledger control account

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (amounts owed by customers) | Balance b/d (credit balances: customers overpaid) |
| Credit sales (sales journal) | Sales returns (sales returns journal) |
| Dishonoured cheques (cash book) | Receipts from customers (cash book) |
| Refunds paid to customers (cash book) | Discount allowed (cash book) |
| Interest charged on overdue accounts (general journal) | Irrecoverable debts (general journal) |
| Balance c/d (credit balances at the end) | Contra with purchases ledger (general journal) |
| | Balance c/d (debit balances at the end) |

## Purchases ledger control account

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Balance b/d (debit balances: suppliers overpaid) | Balance b/d (amounts owed to suppliers) |
| Purchases returns (purchases returns journal) | Credit purchases (purchases journal) |
| Payments to suppliers (cash book) | Refunds received from suppliers (cash book) |
| Discount received (cash book) | Interest charged by suppliers (general journal) |
| Contra with sales ledger (general journal) | Dishonoured cheques paid to suppliers (cash book) |
| Balance c/d (credit balances at the end) | Balance c/d (debit balances at the end) |

**Pattern:** each account mirrors the individual customer or supplier accounts it summarises. If an item increases what customers owe, debit sales control; if it increases what is owed to suppliers, credit purchases control.

## Never in a control account

- Cash sales and cash purchases.
- Trade discount.
- The allowance for irrecoverable debts or changes in it.

## Missing figures

The account must balance, so one unknown can be found:

**Example:** opening debit balances 9,200; credit sales ?; receipts 31,500; discount allowed 600; returns 1,100; closing debit balance 10,400. Credit side total = 31,500 + 600 + 1,100 + 10,400 = 43,600. Credit sales = 43,600 - 9,200 = 34,400.

## Digital recording

| Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|
| Control accounts update automatically and are always current | Built from the same postings as the ledgers, so no longer an independent check |
| No arithmetic differences between control and ledgers | Wrong account or wrong amount at input is not revealed |
| Instant totals for credit control reports | Relies on system security and access controls |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Control account | A summary account holding the totals of all entries in one ledger |
| Contra entry | Setting off an amount owed by a customer against an amount owed to the same business as a supplier |
| Dishonoured cheque | A cheque the bank refuses to pay, so the debt is reinstated |
| Refund | Money returned to a customer or received from a supplier after an overpayment |
| Interest on overdue accounts | A charge added to a debt not paid on time |
| Irrecoverable debt | An amount owed that will not be received and is written off |

## Exam traps

- Dishonoured customer cheque: **debit** sales control.
- Contra: **credit** sales control, **debit** purchases control.
- Refund received from a supplier: **credit** purchases control.
- Bring down **both** balances when there are debit and credit balances at the end.
- Discount allowed belongs only in sales control; discount received only in purchases control.

## Self-test

**1.** State two purposes of a sales ledger control account.

**2.** What is the source of the credit sales figure?

**3.** Which side of the sales ledger control account records a dishonoured cheque?

**4.** A customer owes 400 and is also owed 250 as a supplier. A contra is made. State the entries in both control accounts.

**5.** Where do refunds received from suppliers go?

**6.** Why are cash sales excluded?

**7.** Interest of 30 is charged to a customer on an overdue account. Where is it entered?

**8.** Opening credit balance 8,700; credit purchases 22,100; payments 20,300; discount received 450; returns 600. Calculate the closing credit balance (no debit balances).

**9.** Name the book of prime entry for an irrecoverable debt.

**10.** Is the allowance for irrecoverable debts entered in the control account?

**11.** Give one reason a digital control account is a weaker check than a manual one.

**12.** A sales ledger control account has a closing credit balance of 75. What does it represent?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Any two: arithmetic check on the sales ledger; quick total of trade receivables; discourages fraud through separation of duties; information for credit control.

**2.** The sales journal total.

**3.** The debit side.

**4.** Contra of 250: credit sales ledger control 250; debit purchases ledger control 250.

**5.** The credit side of the purchases ledger control account.

**6.** No customer account is involved, so they never enter the sales ledger.

**7.** Debit side of the sales ledger control account (from the general journal).

**8.** 8,700 + 22,100 - 20,300 - 450 - 600 = 9,450.

**9.** The general journal.

**10.** No; only debts actually written off are entered.

**11.** It is produced from the same postings as the individual accounts, so input errors affect both and are not revealed.

**12.** Customers who have overpaid; the business owes them 75.
