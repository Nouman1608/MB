Carboxylic acids.

## Three ways to make a carboxylic acid

| Starting material | Reagents and conditions | Carbon count | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary alcohol or aldehyde | acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ or KMnO₄, heat under **reflux** | unchanged | butan-1-ol + 2[O] → butanoic acid + H₂O |
| Nitrile | dilute acid, heat; or dilute alkali, heat, **then acidify** | unchanged from the nitrile (one more than the halogenoalkane used to make it) | CH₃CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃COOH + NH₄Cl |
| Ester | dilute acid, heat; or dilute alkali, heat, **then acidify** | acid part of the ester | HCOOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O ⇌ HCOOH + CH₃CH₂OH |

Observations: dichromate **orange → green**; manganate(VII) **purple → colourless**; alkaline nitrile
hydrolysis gives off **ammonia** (turns damp red litmus blue).

## Reactions of carboxylic acids

| Reagent | Type | Products | Observation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reactive metal (Na, Mg) | redox | salt + H₂ | effervescence; metal dissolves |
| Alkali (NaOH, KOH) | neutralisation | salt + H₂O | no visible change (temperature rises) |
| Carbonate or hydrogencarbonate | acid–base | salt + H₂O + CO₂ | effervescence; limewater turns milky |
| Alcohol, concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat | esterification (condensation) | ester + H₂O (reversible) | sweet, fruity smell of ester |
| LiAlH₄ in dry ether | reduction | primary alcohol + H₂O | — |

Equations to know with butanoic acid:

    2CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + Mg → (CH₃CH₂CH₂COO)₂Mg + H₂
    CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NaOH → CH₃CH₂CH₂COONa + H₂O
    2CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃CH₂CH₂COOK + H₂O + CO₂
    CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ + H₂O
    CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH + H₂O

## Mole ratios that catch people out

| Reaction | Acid : gas |
|---|---|
| acid + NaHCO₃ | 1 : 1 CO₂ |
| acid + Na₂CO₃ | 2 : 1 CO₂ |
| acid + Na or Mg | 2 : 1 H₂ |

## Distinguishing tests

| Test | Carboxylic acid | Alcohol | Aldehyde |
|---|---|---|---|
| Na₂CO₃ or NaHCO₃ | CO₂ fizz | no reaction | no reaction |
| Sodium metal | H₂ fizz | H₂ fizz | no reaction |
| Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, warm | stays orange | primary/secondary: orange → green | orange → green |

## Route map in one line each

- Primary alcohol → (acidified K₂Cr₂O₇, reflux) → carboxylic acid, same number of carbons.
- Halogenoalkane → (KCN, ethanol, heat) → nitrile → (dilute acid, heat) → carboxylic acid with one extra
  carbon.
- Ester → (dilute NaOH, heat) → carboxylate salt + alcohol → (dilute HCl) → carboxylic acid.
- Carboxylic acid → (LiAlH₄) → primary alcohol; → (alcohol, concentrated H₂SO₄) → ester.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carboxyl group | –COOH, the functional group of carboxylic acids |
| Carboxylate ion | RCOO⁻, formed when the acid loses H⁺ |
| Reflux | heating with a vertical condenser so that volatile substances condense and return to the flask |
| Hydrolysis | breaking a bond by reaction with water |
| Acidification | adding a strong acid to convert a carboxylate salt into the free acid |
| [O] and [H] | oxygen from an oxidising agent and hydrogen from a reducing agent, in balanced organic equations |

## Exam traps

- Oxidation to the acid uses **reflux**; distillation gives the aldehyde.
- After alkaline hydrolysis, the product is the **salt** until you acidify.
- Mg²⁺ and Ca²⁺ salts need **two** carboxylate ions.
- **LiAlH₄**, not NaBH₄, reduces carboxylic acids.
- Esterification is **reversible** and needs **concentrated** H₂SO₄ as catalyst.
- The carboxyl carbon is C1 and counts in the name.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions to make butanoic acid from butan-1-ol.
2. Why is reflux used rather than distillation in question 1?
3. Write the equation for the hydrolysis of ethanenitrile with dilute hydrochloric acid.
4. What gas is given off when a nitrile is heated with dilute NaOH, and how would you test for it?
5. Write the equation for propanoic acid reacting with calcium.
6. Name the salt formed from methanoic acid and sodium hydroxide.
7. How many moles of CO₂ form from 0.10 mol of ethanoic acid and excess Na₂CO₃?
8. Name the ester formed from butanoic acid and ethanol.
9. Give the reagent to reduce propanoic acid to propan-1-ol.
10. Write the equation for the reduction in question 9 using [H].
11. Describe a test to show that a liquid is a carboxylic acid and not an alcohol.
12. Which starting halogenoalkane would you convert via a nitrile into butanoic acid?

**Answers:**

1. Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (or acidified KMnO₄), heat under reflux.
2. Reflux keeps the intermediate aldehyde in the flask so it is oxidised to the acid; distillation would
   remove it.
3. CH₃CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃COOH + NH₄Cl.
4. Ammonia; it turns damp red litmus paper blue.
5. 2CH₃CH₂COOH + Ca → (CH₃CH₂COO)₂Ca + H₂.
6. Sodium methanoate, HCOONa.
7. 0.050 mol (2 acid : 1 CO₂).
8. Ethyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃.
9. LiAlH₄ (in dry ether).
10. CH₃CH₂COOH + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂OH + H₂O.
11. Add sodium carbonate or sodium hydrogencarbonate: effervescence of CO₂ (limewater milky) shows a
    carboxylic acid; an alcohol gives no gas.
12. 1-bromopropane (or 1-chloropropane): KCN in ethanol gives butanenitrile, then hydrolysis.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to explain why carboxylic acids are acidic for AS 9701?**
18.1 asks you to describe their acid reactions. Comparing the acidity of carboxylic acids with phenols
and alcohols is A Level content.

**Can I use KMnO₄ instead of K₂Cr₂O₇ for the oxidation?**
Yes. The syllabus names both acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ and acidified KMnO₄ with refluxing.
