These questions cover section 9.1, Classifying statistical data, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. All data sets are invented. The methods are in the [classifying statistical data study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-classifying-statistical-data/), and the [classifying statistical data revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-classifying-statistical-data-revision-notes/) list the key rules. Show your working.

## Section A

**1.** State whether each of these is categorical, discrete or continuous.

**(a)** The number of goals scored in a match.

**(b)** The time taken to run 400 metres.

**(c)** The colour of a car. **[3]**

**2.** Ayesha says that age is discrete data because her survey recorded ages in whole years. Explain why she is wrong. **[2]**

**3.** The numbers of emails received by 25 people in one hour were:

```
2 0 3 1 4 2 2 5 1 0 3 2 1 4 2 3 0 1 2 2 3 1 5 2 4
```

Organise the data into a frequency table. **[3]**

**4.** A student records lengths in centimetres using the classes 0–10, 10–20 and 20–30. Give two problems with these classes and write better ones. **[3]**

**5.** A table uses the classes 10 ≤ x < 15, 15 ≤ x < 20, 20 ≤ x < 25 and 25 ≤ x < 30. State the class that contains each of 15, 19.99 and 25. **[2]**

**6.** A frequency table for 30 items has frequencies 5, 8, 11 and 6, and a total written as 31. What should you conclude, and what should you do? **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** There are 60 pupils in Years 10 and 11 of a small school. 28 are in Year 10. Each pupil travels to school by one of cycle, walk or bus. 15 pupils walk, and 9 of these are in Year 11. 12 Year 10 pupils take the bus. 20 pupils cycle.

**(a)** Draw and complete a two-way table.

**(b)** What fraction of the Year 11 pupils take the bus?

**(c)** What fraction of the cyclists are in Year 10? Give your answer in its simplest form. **[7]**

**8.** Complete this two-way table of vehicles in a car park.

| | Red | Blue | Green | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cars | 14 | | 9 | 41 |
| Vans | | 6 | | |
| Total | 22 | | 15 | 61 |

**[4]**

**9.** The masses, in grams, of 24 apples are:

```
112.5 134.0 128.3 141.7 119.9 156.2 120.0 138.8
147.5 125.4 160.0 133.3 109.6 144.1 151.0 129.9
140.0 122.7 136.4 115.8 158.3 131.2 145.6 127.0
```

**(a)** Organise the data into a grouped frequency table with classes 100 ≤ m < 120, 120 ≤ m < 140, 140 ≤ m < 160 and 160 ≤ m < 180.

**(b)** Using only your table, can you say how many apples weigh more than 150 g? Explain. **[6]**

**10.** The numbers of words in 20 sentences are:

```
7 12 4 9 15 6 11 3 8 14 10 5 13 9 6 2 12 8 17 7
```

Organise them into a grouped frequency table with classes 1–5, 6–10, 11–15 and 16–20, and explain why classes written this way are acceptable for this data. **[4]**

**11.** In a survey of 200 adults, 45% are men. Each person either goes to a gym, runs, or does neither. 30% of the men go to a gym. 60 people go to a gym altogether. 50 people do neither, and 18 of these are men. 45 women run.

**(a)** Complete a two-way table.

**(b)** What percentage of the runners are women? **[6]**

**12.** A teacher wants to record, for each of 90 students, their year group (Year 9, 10 or 11) and whether they have a library card (yes or no). Design a suitable two-way table, showing what goes in the headings and totals. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **discrete** [1] **(b)** **continuous** [1] **(c)** **categorical** [1]

**2.** Age is measured time, so it can take any value in a range [1]; recording it in whole years rounds a continuous quantity, but does not make it discrete [1].

**3.** Values 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5 with frequencies **3, 5, 8, 4, 3, 2** [2] (one mark for at least four correct). Total 25 [1].

*Examiner insight:* A total that matches the number of people is the check examiners expect you to make.

**4.** The classes overlap at 10 and 20, so those values have two possible classes [1]; and it is not clear what happens to a length such as 30 or more [1]. Better: **0 ≤ l < 10, 10 ≤ l < 20, 20 ≤ l < 30** (with a further class if needed) [1].

**5.** 15 is in **15 ≤ x < 20**; 19.99 is in **15 ≤ x < 20** [1]; 25 is in **25 ≤ x < 30** [1].

**6.** 5 + 8 + 11 + 6 = 30, so the **total was added wrongly** [1]; correct it to 30 (and, if the frequencies did not add to 30, recount the tally) [1].

**7. (a)** Year 11 total 60 − 28 = 32 [1]; Year 10 walk 15 − 9 = 6 [1]; Year 10 cycle 28 − 6 − 12 = 10 [1]; Year 11 cycle 20 − 10 = 10 and bus total 60 − 15 − 20 = 25 [1]; Year 11 bus 25 − 12 = 13 [1].

| | Cycle | Walk | Bus | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Year 10 | 10 | 6 | 12 | 28 |
| Year 11 | 10 | 9 | 13 | 32 |
| Total | 20 | 15 | 25 | 60 |

**(b)** **13/32** [1].

**(c)** 10/20 = **1/2** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Check the Year 11 row: 10 + 9 + 13 = 32.

**8.** Cars, blue: 41 − 14 − 9 = **18** [1]. Vans, red: 22 − 14 = **8**; vans, green: 15 − 9 = **6** [1]. Vans total: 8 + 6 + 6 = **20** [1]. Blue total: 18 + 6 = **24** [1]. Check: 22 + 24 + 15 = 61 and 41 + 20 = 61.

**9. (a)** Frequencies **4, 11, 8, 1** [3] (one mark off for each error), total 24 [1].

**(b)** **No** [1]: the class 140 ≤ m < 160 holds 8 apples, but the table does not show how many of them are above 150 g [1].

*Examiner insight:* 120.0, 140.0 and 160.0 go in the higher classes. These boundary values are where most marks are lost.

**10.** Frequencies **4, 9, 6, 1** [2] (one mark for three correct), total 20 [1]. The data is discrete (whole numbers of words), so no value falls between 5 and 6 and there is no gap [1].

**11. (a)** Men 0.45 × 200 = 90 and women 110 [1]. Men, gym: 0.3 × 90 = 27, so women, gym 60 − 27 = 33 [1]. Women, neither: 50 − 18 = 32 [1]. Men, run: 90 − 27 − 18 = 45 [1]. Run total: 45 + 45 = 90 [1].

| | Gym | Run | Neither | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 27 | 45 | 18 | 90 |
| Women | 33 | 45 | 32 | 110 |
| Total | 60 | 90 | 50 | 200 |

**(b)** 45/90 = **50%** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Convert every percentage into a number of people first; the table must hold frequencies, not percentages.

**12.** Rows (or columns) for Year 9, Year 10, Year 11 and Total, and columns for Yes, No and Total [1]; a grand total cell that should equal 90 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating measured quantities recorded to the nearest unit as discrete.
- Overlapping or gapped classes for continuous data.
- Putting boundary values in the lower class.
- Totals that are not checked against the number of items.
- Using the grand total instead of a row or column total for a fraction.
- Entering percentages in a two-way table instead of frequencies.
