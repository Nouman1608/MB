## US presidents and their policies

| President | Policy |
|---|---|
| Truman | Funded the French war effort |
| Eisenhower | Domino Theory; backed Diem; advisers |
| Kennedy | More advisers (about 16,000 by 1963); Strategic Hamlets |
| Johnson | Tonkin Resolution; Rolling Thunder; ground war |
| Nixon | Vietnamisation; wider war in Cambodia and Laos; Paris Accords |
| Ford | No further US intervention; Saigon falls |

## Tactics compared

| US and ARVN | NLF and North Vietnam |
|---|---|
| Bombing (Rolling Thunder) | Tunnels and underground bases |
| Search and Destroy, helicopters, body counts | Ambushes, booby traps, hit-and-run |
| Defoliation, napalm | Supply via the Ho Chi Minh Trail |
| Strategic Hamlets, Hearts and Minds | Support from villagers; political cadres |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Vietminh | Communist-led independence movement (to 1954) |
| Domino Theory | Belief that one country falling to communism would cause others to fall |
| NLF / Viet Cong | Communist-led front fighting the South Vietnamese government |
| ARVN | Army of the Republic of (South) Vietnam |
| Search and Destroy | Missions to find and kill enemy units |
| Body count | Number of enemy dead used to measure progress |
| Vietnamisation | Handing the fighting to South Vietnam as US troops left |
| Credibility gap | Gap between official claims and what the public saw |

## Worked example: describe two features

*Describe two features of the Tet Offensive (1968).* (6 marks)

1. A **surprise, coordinated attack** during the Vietnamese New Year truce on more than a hundred towns
   and cities in the South, including the US embassy in Saigon.
2. **Heavy fighting and losses**, especially in Hue, which took about a month to recapture; the
   communists lost many fighters, but the offensive destroyed American belief that the war was nearly
   won.

## Why did the USA fail? Factors to weigh

| Factor | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Guerrilla warfare | Tunnels, ambushes, NLF support among villagers |
| Supply from the North and allies | Ho Chi Minh Trail; Soviet and Chinese weapons |
| Weak South Vietnam | Diem's corruption and persecution of Buddhists; coups after 1963 |
| US tactics | Bombing, defoliation and My Lai turned civilians against the USA |
| Opinion at home | Tet coverage, protest, Kent State, the credibility gap |
| Political limits | Congress refused more aid in 1975 |

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Assuming the USA fought France.** It funded France's war; it fought the North and NLF later.
- **Calling Tet a communist military victory.** It was a military defeat but a political success.
- **Ignoring South Vietnam's weaknesses.** Diem's unpopularity matters to every explanation of US
  failure.
- **Treating media sources as neutral.** TV coverage was selective; government statements were
  optimistic. Use provenance in cross-referencing.
- **Forgetting 1973–75.** The investigation ends with the fall of Saigon, not with US withdrawal.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did France lose?**
Giap's tactics, Chinese supplies, popular support for the Vietminh and the
disaster at Dien Bien Phu.

**Why was Diem overthrown?**
Corruption, nepotism and persecution of Buddhists made him a liability; the
USA did not prevent the generals' coup.

**How did the war affect American society?**
It divided the country, fuelled protest and damaged trust
in government.
