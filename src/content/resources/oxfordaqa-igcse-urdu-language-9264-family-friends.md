This guide goes deeper on the first topic of **Theme 1 Identity and Culture** in OxfordAQA
International GCSE Urdu (9264): **Me, my family, friends and people I know**. The
[Identity and Culture study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-identity-and-culture/) gives
the overview of the whole theme. This page concentrates on the language you need to describe
people, explain how you get on with them and write about relationships with accuracy and range.

## What this topic covers

The specification divides the topic into two sub-topics:

- **Relationships with family** — the members of your family, what they are like, how you get
  on, and family life at home.
- **Relationships with friends and people I know** — friendship, how you met, what makes a good
  friend, and other people in your life such as neighbours and classmates.

Like every topic in 9264, it has no paper of its own. It supplies the subject matter for texts in
**Paper 1 (Reading)**, which also includes translation from English into Urdu, and for tasks in
**Paper 2 (Writing)**. Because it is the most personal topic in the course, it is also where
examiners expect your grammar to be most secure: you are writing about things you know well, so
agreement slips are hard to excuse.

## Vocabulary you need to produce, not just recognise

**Family members.** Urdu distinguishes relatives on the father's side from those on the mother's
side, and translation tasks can test this.

| Urdu | Meaning | Note |
|---|---|---|
| والد / والدہ | father / mother | formal; ابو and امی are everyday |
| والدین | parents | takes plural agreement |
| دادا / دادی | father's father / father's mother | |
| نانا / نانی | mother's father / mother's mother | |
| چچا / پھوپھی | father's brother / father's sister | |
| ماموں / خالہ | mother's brother / mother's sister | |
| رشتہ دار | relative | |
| مشترکہ خاندان | joint (extended) family | |
| اکلوتا / اکلوتی | only (child) | میں اکلوتا بیٹا ہوں |

**Friends and people you know.** دوست (friend), سہیلی (a girl's female friend), ہم جماعت
(classmate), پڑوسی (neighbour), جان پہچان والے (acquaintances), بچپن کا دوست (childhood friend).

**Character.** مہربان (kind), ملنسار (sociable), خوش مزاج (cheerful), سمجھ دار (sensible),
ایمان دار (honest), مددگار (helpful), سخت (strict), شرمیلا (shy), باتونی (talkative), غصے والا
(short-tempered).

**Getting on and falling out.** These verb phrases are worth more than any single noun, because
they let you explain a relationship rather than list people:

- میری اپنی بہن سے بہت بنتی ہے — I get on very well with my sister.
- ہماری آپس میں نہیں بنتی — we do not get on.
- کبھی کبھی ہمارا جھگڑا ہو جاتا ہے — sometimes we argue.
- ہم جلد ہی صلح کر لیتے ہیں — we soon make up.
- میں اس پر بھروسا کرتا ہوں — I trust him.
- وہ میرا بہت خیال رکھتی ہیں — she takes great care of me.

## Grammar that carries this topic

**1. Possessives agree with the thing possessed, not the owner.** میرا بھائی, میری بہن, میرے
والدین. A girl and a boy both say میرا بھائی, because بھائی is masculine. The same rule applies
to کا / کی / کے: میرے دوست کی بہن (my friend's sister) uses کی because بہن is feminine.

**2. Respect for elders changes the verb.** When you talk about a parent, grandparent or teacher,
use the plural form of the verb as a mark of respect, even for one person: میرے دادا بہت مہربان
ہیں, میری والدہ ڈاکٹر ہیں, میری نانی ہمیں کہانیاں سناتی ہیں. Writing ہے here is not a spelling
error but it sounds disrespectful, and an examiner reading for accuracy and appropriateness will
notice it.

**3. Marked and unmarked adjectives.** Adjectives ending in ا change for gender and number:
شرمیلا لڑکا, شرمیلی لڑکی, شرمیلے بچے. Most others do not change: مہربان استاد, مہربان استانی.
Learn each new adjective together with whether it changes.

**4. Describing appearance with possession.** Urdu says "his hair is long" rather than "he has
long hair": اس کے بال لمبے ہیں, اس کی آنکھیں بھوری ہیں. Note that بال is masculine plural, so
the adjective is لمبے.

**5. ملنا is intransitive.** "I met my friend" is میں اپنے دوست سے ملا, with سے and no نے. A very
common error is میں نے اپنے دوست کو ملا. For a first meeting, an alternative is ہماری ملاقات
اسکول میں ہوئی (we met at school).

**6. Past habitual for childhood memories.** بچپن میں ہم ہر گرمی میں نانی کے گھر جاتے تھے (as
children we used to go to grandmother's house every summer). This is the easiest way to add a
second time frame to a description of your family.

**7. Opinion with "should".** میرے خیال میں ایک اچھے دوست کو ایمان دار ہونا چاہیے (in my view a
good friend should be honest). The person who "should" takes کو, and the oblique اچھے دوست follows
from that.

## Worked example 1: translation into Urdu

English: *My older brother is very kind, but sometimes we argue. My grandmother lives with us.*

```
Step 1  Find the verb and put it last.
        "is kind" -> مہربان ہے ; "argue" -> جھگڑا ہو جاتا ہے ; "lives" -> رہتی ہیں

Step 2  Check agreement and respect.
        بھائی is masculine -> میرا بڑا بھائی
        grandmother is an elder -> رہتی ہیں (plural of respect), not رہتی ہے

Step 3  Translate the sense, not each word.
        "we argue" is more natural as ہمارا جھگڑا ہو جاتا ہے than a word-for-word verb
```

Answer: میرا بڑا بھائی بہت مہربان ہے، لیکن کبھی کبھی ہمارا جھگڑا ہو جاتا ہے۔ میری دادی ہمارے
ساتھ رہتی ہیں۔

If the English had said "my mother's mother", you would need نانی, not دادی. Read family words in
a translation task twice.

## Worked example 2: a short piece of writing

Task (original): *Describe your best friend, explain how you met and say what you will do
together in the future.*

> میری سب سے اچھی سہیلی کا نام عائشہ ہے۔ ہماری ملاقات تین سال پہلے اسکول میں ہوئی۔ وہ بہت خوش
> مزاج اور مددگار ہے۔ جب مجھے ریاضی کا کوئی سوال سمجھ میں نہیں آتا تو وہ میری مدد کرتی ہے۔ میرے
> خیال میں سچا دوست وہی ہے جو مشکل وقت میں ساتھ دے۔ اگلی گرمیوں میں ہم دونوں ایک ساتھ کمپیوٹر
> کورس کریں گی۔

What makes it work:

- **Three time frames**: present description (ہے), a past event (ملاقات ہوئی) and a future plan
  (کریں گی, feminine plural because the two friends are girls).
- **A complex sentence** with جب ... تو (when ... then).
- **A justified opinion** that goes beyond "she is nice": سچا دوست وہی ہے جو ... (a true friend is
  the one who ...).
- **Accurate agreement** throughout: سہیلی is feminine, so the verbs are ہے, کرتی ہے and کریں گی.

## Common mistakes

- Writing ہے instead of ہیں for parents, grandparents and teachers.
- Matching میرا / میری to the writer's gender instead of the noun's gender.
- میں نے اس کو ملا instead of میں اس سے ملا.
- Confusing paternal and maternal relatives (دادی and نانی, چچا and ماموں) in translation.
- Listing family members ("I have two brothers and one sister") with no description, opinion or
  reason.
- Forgetting that marked adjectives change: شرمیلا بھائی but شرمیلی بہن.

## Exam technique

**Reading questions.** Learn the Urdu question words so that you know exactly what is being asked:
کون (who), کیا (what), کب (when), کہاں (where), کیوں (why), کیسے (how), کتنے (how many). A کیوں
question needs a reason, often found after کیونکہ or اس لیے in the text. Where a question asks how
someone **feels** about a relative or friend, look for evaluative words such as اچھا لگتا ہے, پسند
نہیں, or فخر ہے, not just facts about what the person does.

**Translation.** Every English sentence must be rendered in full. Check, in this order: verb at
the end, gender agreement, the plural of respect, and family words on the correct side of the
family.

**Writing.** Cover every point the task sets. For this topic, a reliable plan is: who the person
is, what they are like (with an example), how you get on, a past memory, and a future plan or a
view on what makes a good relationship. The specification states that, provided the tasks set are
completed, the number of words is not important, so spend time on accuracy rather than padding.
