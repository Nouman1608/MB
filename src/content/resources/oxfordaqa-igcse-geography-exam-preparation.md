OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) is a linear, untiered qualification with no coursework,
assessed by three written papers of unequal weight. Paper 1 (Living with the Physical Environment) and
Paper 2 (Challenges in the Human Environment) are each 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks and 36% of the
qualification. Paper 3 (Geographical and Fieldwork Skills) is shorter -- 1 hour 15 minutes, 60 marks
and 28%. Examinations for this specification now take place in May/June only, with no November series
from 2026. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Living With the Physical Environment](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/)
and [Urban Issues and Challenges](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-urban-issues-and-challenges/).

## Paper 3 is smaller but not optional -- and it cannot be revised the same way

At 28% Paper 3 is the lightest of the three, but it tests a different kind of knowledge: map reading,
graphical and statistical skills, and the application of fieldwork enquiry methods, often using
unfamiliar resources supplied in the paper. **Exam-preparation priority**: do not leave Paper 3 to
general revision. Practise the specific skills -- calculating gradient from a contour map, describing
distribution from a choropleth map, interpreting a scattergraph, evaluating a sampling method -- since
these are technique questions rather than content-recall questions and improve only with deliberate
practice.

## Papers 1 and 2 together are nearly three-quarters of the grade

At 36% each, Papers 1 and 2 carry the larger share between them. **Exam-preparation priority**: weight
revision time towards physical and human processes accordingly, while still protecting dedicated time
for Paper 3's skills work -- a well-prepared candidate on Papers 1 and 2 can still lose a large share of
marks on Paper 3 if its specific techniques were never practised.

## Case studies need to be named, dated and specific

Geography at this level rewards named examples used with real detail: a specific place, a specific
event, specific figures. A generalised answer about "a coastal management scheme" scores far below one
naming the scheme, its location and its actual cost or effect. **Exam-preparation priority**: reduce
each case study to a short card -- place, date, two or three quantified details, and the process it
illustrates -- and revise the cards rather than the original notes.

## Command words determine how much explanation a mark wants

"State", "describe", "explain" and "evaluate" are not interchangeable in geography either.
**Exam-preparation priority**: check every practice answer against its command word. A describe
question wants the pattern with figures; an explain question wants the process causing it; an
evaluate question wants a judgement weighing more than one factor. Confusing these is one of the most
common ways marks are lost even when the content itself is correct.

## Graphs, maps and diagrams need labels and calculations, not description alone

Where Paper 3 supplies a resource, marks come from working with it precisely -- correct grid
references, correct units, a shown calculation -- not from a general commentary. **Exam-preparation
priority**: practise map skills to fluency: four- and six-figure grid references, scale and distance,
contour interpretation, and cross-sections. These are mechanical skills that either work or do not,
and they are worth rehearsing until automatic.

## Worked routine: a data-response question using supplied statistics

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper, resource booklet or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - read the units and scale of the data before anything else:
Misreading a scale (thousands vs millions) invalidates the whole
answer.

Step 2 - identify the command word and answer only what it asks:
DESCRIBE -> the pattern, with quoted figures.
EXPLAIN  -> the process causing it.
SUGGEST  -> a plausible reason, specific to this context.

Step 3 - if describing, quote at least one specific figure:
"The population rose from 2.1 million to 3.4 million between 2000
and 2020" is creditable; "the population increased" is not.

Step 4 - if a calculation is required, show the working:
State the formula or method, then the substitution, then the
result with units.

Step 5 - link back to the named case study where relevant:
A general statistical trend, connected to a specific named place
you have studied, is stronger than either alone.
```

Step 3 is the single most reliable way to lift a description answer: a quoted figure demonstrates
precision that a general statement cannot.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: weight revision towards Papers 1 and 2's 72% combined share while protecting
  dedicated time for Paper 3's specific skills; reduce each case study to a card with place, date and
  quantified detail; drill map skills (grid references, scale, contours) to fluency; check practice
  answers against their command word before checking content.
- **During any paper**: read the mark allocation before the question and match answer length to it.
- **On data-response questions**: read units and scale first, answer only what the command word asks,
  quote specific figures, and show any calculation's working.
- **In every case-study answer**: name the place and date, and give at least two specific quantified
  details.

## Self-test

1. What is the weighting of the three papers, and what follows for revision time?
2. Why can Paper 3 not be revised the same way as Papers 1 and 2?
3. What is the single most reliable way to improve a "describe" answer?
4. Why does confusing "explain" with "describe" cost marks even when the content is correct?

**Answers:** 1. Papers 1 and 2 are 36% each and Paper 3 is 28%; revision time should weight towards
Papers 1 and 2 while still protecting dedicated time for Paper 3's distinct skills. 2. Because it
tests technique -- map reading, graphical and statistical skills, fieldwork methods -- rather than
content recall, and technique only improves with deliberate practice on unfamiliar resources.
3. Quoting a specific figure from the data, with its unit, rather than describing the pattern in
general terms. 4. Because each command word specifies a different task -- describing the pattern
versus explaining the process behind it -- and an answer that does the wrong task does not address
what was actually asked, however accurate the geography in it is.
