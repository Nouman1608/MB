## Questions

**1.** Deduce the oxidation state of:

**(a)** phosphorus in P₄O₁₀ **[1]**
**(b)** oxygen in Na₂O₂ **[1]**
**(c)** chlorine in HClO₄ **[1]**
**(d)** Name Fe₂(SO₄)₃ and PbO₂, including oxidation numbers. **[2]**

**2.** Copper(II) oxide is reduced when heated in ammonia gas:

3CuO(s) + 2NH₃(g) → 3Cu(s) + N₂(g) + 3H₂O(g)

**(a)** Give the change in oxidation state of copper and of nitrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the oxidizing agent and the reducing agent. **[2]**

**3.** In acidic solution, bromate(V) ions, BrO₃⁻, oxidize iron(II) ions to iron(III) ions and are reduced to bromine, Br₂.

**(a)** Deduce the reduction half-equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the oxidation half-equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the overall ionic equation. **[2]**

**4.** Aluminium foil is placed in aqueous nickel(II) sulfate and becomes coated with nickel. Write the two half-equations and the overall ionic equation. **[3]**

**5.** Four metals were each placed in solutions of the other three metals' 2+ ions. The results are shown (✓ = metal deposit forms, ✗ = no change).

| Metal | Mn²⁺(aq) | Fe²⁺(aq) | Sn²⁺(aq) | Cu²⁺(aq) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mn | – | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| Fe | ✗ | – | ✓ | ✓ |
| Sn | ✗ | ✗ | – | ✓ |
| Cu | ✗ | ✗ | ✗ | – |

**(a)** List the metals in order of decreasing ease of oxidation, and explain how the data show this. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction of iron with Sn²⁺(aq), and identify the reducing agent. **[2]**

**6.** Bromine water is added separately to aqueous potassium chloride and aqueous potassium iodide.

**(a)** Describe what is seen in each case. **[2]**
**(b)** Give an ionic equation for the one reaction that takes place. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why bromine is reduced less easily than chlorine. **[2]**

**7.** A 0.365 g piece of magnesium ribbon reacts completely with excess dilute hydrochloric acid.

**(a)** Give a balanced equation including state symbols. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the ionic equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen formed at STP, in cm³. **[3]**

**8.** A voltaic cell is built from a magnesium half-cell and a tin half-cell, joined by a wire and a salt bridge of potassium nitrate. Magnesium is oxidized more easily than tin.

**(a)** Identify the anode and give its sign. **[2]**
**(b)** Give the anode and cathode half-equations. **[2]**
**(c)** State the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe the movement of ions in the salt bridge and explain why it is needed. **[2]**

**9.** A nickel–metal hydride cell is rechargeable. During discharge:

- negative electrode: MH + OH⁻ → M + H₂O + e⁻ (M is a metal alloy that stores hydrogen)
- positive electrode: NiO(OH) + H₂O + e⁻ → Ni(OH)₂ + OH⁻

**(a)** Deduce the reaction at each electrode during charging. **[2]**
**(b)** State which electrode acts as the anode during charging. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the overall equation for discharge. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one advantage of this cell over a primary cell, and one disadvantage of a hydrogen fuel cell compared with this cell. **[2]**

**10.** Molten strontium bromide is electrolysed using graphite electrodes.

**(a)** Explain why solid strontium bromide does not conduct but molten strontium bromide does. **[2]**
**(b)** For each electrode, give its name and its half-equation. **[2]**
**(c)** State how current is carried in the external circuit and in the electrolyte. **[1]**
**(d)** State the energy change in this cell, and how it differs from a voltaic cell. **[1]**

**11.** 2,2-Dimethylpropan-1-ol, (CH₃)₃CCH₂OH, is a primary alcohol. It can be oxidized to an aldehyde or to a carboxylic acid.

**(a)** Describe the set-up used to obtain the aldehyde, and explain why it works. **[2]**
**(b)** Using [O], give the equation in which the aldehyde forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Using [O], give one equation for the conversion of the alcohol straight to the carboxylic acid. **[1]**
**(d)** A student oxidizes 4.41 g of the alcohol under reflux and obtains 3.83 g of 2,2-dimethylpropanoic acid. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**
**(e)** Name the product when heptan-4-ol is oxidized, and explain why 2-methylpropan-2-ol is not oxidized under the same conditions. **[2]**

**12.** This question is about reduction reactions.

**(a)** Write two equations, using [H], for the reduction of propanoic acid, CH₃CH₂COOH, to propan-1-ol. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the product of reducing hexan-2-one, and write the equation using [H]. **[2]**
**(c)** State the role of the hydride ion in these reductions. **[1]**
**(d)** Hex-1-yne, CH≡CCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃, is fully hydrogenated over a nickel catalyst. Calculate the volume of hydrogen at STP that reacts with 4.11 g of hex-1-yne. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 4x + 10(−2) = 0, so **+5** [1]
**(b)** 2(+1) + 2x = 0, so **−1** (a peroxide) [1]
**(c)** +1 + x + 4(−2) = 0, so **+7** [1]
**(d)** **iron(III) sulfate** [1]; **lead(IV) oxide** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write out the sum you set up, so that a slip in the arithmetic is easy to spot when you check.

**2. (a)** Copper: **+2 → 0** [1]; nitrogen: **−3 → 0** [1]
**(b)** Oxidizing agent: **CuO** (or Cu²⁺) [1]; reducing agent: **NH₃** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the whole species, NH₃, as the agent; "nitrogen" alone is ambiguous because N₂ is a product.

**3. (a)** 2BrO₃⁻ → Br₂ with 6H₂O added to the right and 12H⁺ to the left [1]; **2BrO₃⁻ + 12H⁺ + 10e⁻ → Br₂ + 6H₂O** [1]
**(b)** **Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ + e⁻** [1]
**(c)** Multiply (b) by 10 to match electrons [1]; **2BrO₃⁻ + 12H⁺ + 10Fe²⁺ → Br₂ + 6H₂O + 10Fe³⁺** (charge +30 on each side) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check charge as well as atoms on both sides of the final equation; an equation that balances atoms only is not fully balanced.

**4.** **Al → Al³⁺ + 3e⁻** [1]; **Ni²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Ni** [1]; **2Al + 3Ni²⁺ → 2Al³⁺ + 3Ni** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The six electrons must cancel exactly; writing Al + Ni²⁺ → Al³⁺ + Ni leaves the charges unbalanced.

**5. (a)** **Mn > Fe > Sn > Cu** [1]; Mn reduces all three other ions and Cu none: the more ions a metal reduces, the more easily it is oxidized [1]
**(b)** **Fe + Sn²⁺ → Fe²⁺ + Sn** [1]; reducing agent: **Fe** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An explanation that refers to the data ("reacts with three solutions") earns more than a memorized reactivity series.

**6. (a)** With KCl(aq): **no change**, the solution stays yellow-orange [1]; with KI(aq): the solution turns **brown** [1]
**(b)** **Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂** [1]
**(c)** A bromine atom is larger, so the incoming electron is further from the nucleus [1]; with more shielding, it is attracted less strongly, so bromine gains an electron less readily [1]
*Examiner insight:* Refer to the incoming electron being attracted to the nucleus; "bromine is less reactive" restates the question.

**7. (a)** **Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)** [1]
**(b)** **Mg(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂(g)** [1]
**(c)** n(Mg) = 0.365 ÷ 24.31 = 0.01501 mol [1]; n(H₂) = n(Mg) = 0.01501 mol [1]; V = 0.01501 × 22.7 = 0.341 dm³ = **341 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the unit asked for: cm³ here, not dm³.

**8. (a)** **Magnesium** is the anode [1]; it is **negative** [1]
**(b)** Anode: **Mg → Mg²⁺ + 2e⁻** [1]; cathode: **Sn²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Sn** [1]
**(c)** **From magnesium to tin** through the wire [1]
**(d)** NO₃⁻ moves toward the magnesium half-cell and K⁺ toward the tin half-cell [1]; this keeps both solutions neutral as Mg²⁺ forms and Sn²⁺ is removed, and completes the circuit [1]
*Examiner insight:* State that ions, not electrons, move in the salt bridge; answers suggesting electrons cross it lose credit.

**9. (a)** Negative electrode: **M + H₂O + e⁻ → MH + OH⁻** [1]; positive electrode: **Ni(OH)₂ + OH⁻ → NiO(OH) + H₂O + e⁻** [1]
**(b)** The **positive electrode** (oxidation happens there on charging) [1]
**(c)** **MH + NiO(OH) → M + Ni(OH)₂** [1]
**(d)** Advantage: it can be **recharged many times**, so less waste and lower long-term cost [1]; fuel cell disadvantage: **hydrogen is hard to store** or catalysts are expensive [1]
*Examiner insight:* A charging reaction should be the exact reverse of the discharge reaction, with every species and the electron moved across the arrow.

**10. (a)** Solid: the ions sit in a rigid lattice and cannot move [1]; molten: ions are free to move and carry charge [1]
**(b)** Cathode: **Sr²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Sr** [1]; anode: **2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻** [1]
**(c)** **Electrons** in the external circuit and **ions** in the electrolyte [1]
**(d)** **Electrical to chemical** energy (non-spontaneous reaction); a voltaic cell does the reverse [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Free electrons in the melt" is wrong for an ionic compound; the explanation needs mobile ions.

**11. (a)** Heat with the oxidizing agent and **distil off** the aldehyde as it forms [1]; with no hydrogen bonding between its molecules it boils below the alcohol, so it leaves before further oxidation [1]
**(b)** **(CH₃)₃CCH₂OH + [O] → (CH₃)₃CCHO + H₂O** [1]
**(c)** **(CH₃)₃CCH₂OH + 2[O] → (CH₃)₃CCOOH + H₂O** [1]
**(d)** M(C₅H₁₂O) = 88.17 g mol⁻¹, so n = 4.41 ÷ 88.17 = 0.05002 mol [1]; M(acid) = 102.15 g mol⁻¹, so theoretical mass = 0.05002 × 102.15 = 5.109 g [1]; yield = 3.83 ÷ 5.109 × 100 = **75.0%** [1]
**(e)** **Heptan-4-one** [1]; in 2-methylpropan-2-ol the carbon bonded to OH carries no hydrogen atom, so it cannot be oxidized this way [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded intermediate values until the last line; rounding the moles early can move the final yield.

**12. (a)** **CH₃CH₂COOH + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CHO + H₂O** [1]; **CH₃CH₂CHO + 2[H] → CH₃CH₂CH₂OH** [1]
**(b)** **Hexan-2-ol** [1]; **CH₃COC₄H₉ + 2[H] → CH₃CH(OH)C₄H₉** [1]
**(c)** It acts as a source of hydrogen with two electrons, adding to the δ+ carbonyl carbon (it is the reducing species) [1]
**(d)** M(C₆H₁₀) = 82.16 g mol⁻¹, so n = 4.11 ÷ 82.16 = 0.05002 mol [1]; two H₂ per molecule, so n(H₂) = 0.1000 mol [1]; V = 0.1000 × 22.7 = **2.27 dm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A triple bond takes two H₂ to reach the alkane; using a 1 : 1 ratio halves the answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving oxidation states without a sign, or as a total for several atoms.
- Calling the species that is oxidized the oxidizing agent.
- Balancing atoms but not charge in redox equations.
- Saying electrons move through the salt bridge or the molten electrolyte.
- Writing a charging reaction that is not the full reverse of discharge.
- Using reflux when the aldehyde is the target product.
- Using one mole of H₂ per mole of alkyne when the alkane is formed.
