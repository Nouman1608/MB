## Questions

**1.** Write a balanced equation for the complete combustion of each substance.

**(a)** sodium, forming sodium oxide **[1]**
**(b)** aluminium **[1]**
**(c)** heptane, C₇H₁₆ **[1]**

**2.** Pentan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₂CH₃, is being tested as a fuel additive.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation, with state symbols, for its complete combustion. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of oxygen needed to burn 10.0 g of pentan-2-ol completely. **[3]**
**(c)** Pentan-2-ol does not catch fire when stored in air at room temperature. Suggest why. **[1]**

**3.** Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, is a component of kerosene.

**(a)** Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of dodecane forming carbon monoxide and water. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the incomplete combustion of dodecane forming carbon and water. **[2]**
**(c)** State one observation you would expect when kerosene burns in a limited supply of air. **[1]**

**4.** In a poorly ventilated room, a spirit stove burns ethanol to give equal amounts, in mol, of carbon monoxide and carbon dioxide, plus water.

**(a)** Deduce the balanced equation for this reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this situation is a health risk. **[2]**

**5.** Methane, CH₄, and icosane, C₂₀H₄₂, are both alkanes. Using calculations where helpful, explain why icosane has a greater tendency than methane to undergo incomplete combustion. **[3]**

**6.** Use these values for the enthalpy of combustion.

| Fuel | Formula | ΔHc⦵ / kJ mol⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|
| propane | C₃H₈ | −2219 |
| hexadecane | C₁₆H₃₄ | −10 700 |
| ethanol | C₂H₅OH | −1367 |

**(a)** Calculate the energy released per gram of each fuel. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of CO₂ released per MJ of energy for each fuel. **[3]**
**(c)** Ethanol and hexadecane release similar masses of CO₂ per MJ. Explain why bioethanol may still add less CO₂ to the atmosphere than hexadecane from crude oil. **[2]**
**(d)** State one advantage and one disadvantage of natural gas compared with coal as a fuel for power stations. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how an increase in the concentration of carbon dioxide in the atmosphere leads to a rise in the Earth's average surface temperature. **[4]**

**8.** Biodiesel is made from plant oils; diesel is made from crude oil.

**(a)** Write the equation for photosynthesis. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why biodiesel is described as renewable but diesel is not. **[2]**

**9.** A city council must choose a fuel for its bus fleet. Option A is biogas made from food waste and sewage. Option B is biodiesel made from palm oil grown on land that was cleared tropical forest. Evaluate the two options and recommend one. **[6]**

**10.** A hydrogen fuel cell has an alkaline electrolyte.

**(a)** Write the half-equations at the anode and the cathode. **[2]**
**(b)** State which electrode is the negative electrode. **[1]**
**(c)** State two differences between this fuel cell and a primary cell. **[2]**

**11.** A portable generator uses a methanol fuel cell with an acidic electrolyte.

**(a)** Deduce the half-equation at the anode. **[2]**
**(b)** State the cathode half-equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Combine the half-equations to give the overall equation. **[1]**
**(d)** The cell uses 64.1 g of methanol. Calculate the amount, in mol, of electrons transferred and the mass of CO₂ released. **[3]**
**(e)** Give one advantage and one disadvantage of methanol compared with hydrogen as a fuel-cell fuel. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O** [1]
**(b)** **4Al + 3O₂ → 2Al₂O₃** [1]
**(c)** **C₇H₁₆ + 11O₂ → 7CO₂ + 8H₂O** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), an equation with NaO or Na₂O₂ as the product is wrong however well it is balanced; work out the oxide formula from the ion charges first.

**2. (a)** Correct products CO₂ and H₂O with C₅H₁₁OH [1]; **2C₅H₁₁OH(l) + 15O₂(g) → 10CO₂(g) + 12H₂O(l)** with state symbols [1]
**(b)** M = 88.17 g mol⁻¹, so n = 10.0 ÷ 88.17 = 0.1134 mol [1]; n(O₂) = 0.1134 × 15/2 = 0.851 mol [1]; mass = 0.851 × 32.00 = **27.2 g** [1]
**(c)** The reaction has a **high activation energy**, so heating or a spark is needed to start it [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), the key step is using the 15 : 2 mole ratio from your equation; assuming 1 : 1 makes every later number wrong.

**3. (a)** CO and H₂O as the only products [1]; **2C₁₂H₂₆ + 25O₂ → 24CO + 26H₂O** [1]
**(b)** C and H₂O as the only products [1]; **2C₁₂H₂₆ + 13O₂ → 24C + 26H₂O** [1]
**(c)** A **yellow, smoky flame** or **black soot** deposits [1]
*Examiner insight:* An equation that shows CO₂ as well as CO does not answer (a), which asks for one specific carbon product.

**4. (a)** Products CO, CO₂ and H₂O in a 1 : 1 ratio of CO to CO₂ [1]; **2C₂H₅OH + 5O₂ → 2CO + 2CO₂ + 6H₂O** [1]
**(b)** Carbon monoxide **binds to haemoglobin** more strongly than oxygen [1], so the blood delivers less oxygen to tissues; with no colour or smell, a build-up goes undetected [1]
*Examiner insight:* "CO is poisonous" restates the risk; credit comes from the mechanism, its binding to haemoglobin.

**5.** Methane needs 2 mol O₂ per mol for complete combustion; icosane needs 20 + 42/4 = 30½ mol O₂ per mol, so far more oxygen must reach each molecule [1]. Icosane is 85.0% carbon by mass compared with 74.8% for methane, so there is more carbon to oxidise per gram [1]. Icosane has stronger London forces, so it is a solid that does not vaporise easily and mixes poorly with air [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give three separate reasons; repeating "it is a bigger molecule" in different words adds no new point.

**6. (a)** Propane: 2219 ÷ 44.11 = **50.3 kJ g⁻¹** [1]; hexadecane: 10 700 ÷ 226.50 = **47.2 kJ g⁻¹** [1]; ethanol: 1367 ÷ 46.08 = **29.7 kJ g⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Propane: 3 × 44.01 × 1000 ÷ 2219 = **59.5 g** [1]; hexadecane: 16 × 44.01 × 1000 ÷ 10 700 = **65.8 g** [1]; ethanol: 2 × 44.01 × 1000 ÷ 1367 = **64.4 g** [1]
**(c)** The carbon in bioethanol was **removed from the air by photosynthesis** recently, so burning it returns that carbon [1]; the carbon in hexadecane was locked underground for millions of years and is new to the atmosphere [1]
**(d)** Advantage: less CO₂ per unit energy, or more energy per gram, or less soot and SO₂ [1]. Disadvantage: needs pipelines or pressurised storage, or methane leaks are explosive and a greenhouse gas [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), forgetting to multiply by the number of carbon atoms in each fuel gives answers that are too small for propane and hexadecane; show the carbon count in your working so the method is visible.

**7.** The Earth's surface absorbs sunlight and emits **infrared radiation** [1]. CO₂ molecules **absorb infrared**, causing their bonds to vibrate [1]. The energy is **re-emitted in all directions**, including back towards the surface [1]. More CO₂ means more infrared is absorbed and returned, so less energy escapes to space and the average temperature rises (an enhanced greenhouse effect) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Saying CO₂ "reflects" heat, or confusing this with ozone depletion, loses the absorption and re-emission marks.

**8. (a)** Correct formulas of reactants and products [1]; **6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂** balanced [1]
**(b)** Biodiesel crops can be **regrown each season**, so the source is replaced as fast as it is used [1]; crude oil takes **millions of years** to form, so it is used much faster than it is replaced [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Renewable means it will not run out" is too vague; tie the answer to the rate of replacement compared with the rate of use.

**9.** Any six points, at least one for each side, with a justified recommendation:
- A uses waste that would otherwise rot and release methane, so it reduces emissions [1]
- A needs no farmland, so it does not compete with food crops [1]
- A may be limited by how much waste the city produces, so it may not fuel the whole fleet [1]
- Biogas needs pressurised tanks and new refuelling equipment [1]
- B can be blended with diesel and used in existing engines [1]
- B's land was cleared forest, releasing stored carbon and destroying habitats, so its net CO₂ may exceed that of diesel [1]

Recommendation: **A**, because it gives lower net emissions without land-use harm, even though it costs more to set up (B is also acceptable if justified).
*Examiner insight:* Weigh at least one strength and one weakness of each option, then make the recommendation follow from that weighing; a one-sided list is description, not evaluation.

**10. (a)** Anode: **H₂ + 2OH⁻ → 2H₂O + 2e⁻** [1]; cathode: **O₂ + 2H₂O + 4e⁻ → 4OH⁻** [1]
**(b)** The **anode** [1]
**(c)** Any two: a fuel cell is supplied continuously with reactants while a primary cell contains a fixed amount [1]; reactants for a fuel cell are stored outside the cell, while a primary cell's are sealed inside it [1]; or a fuel cell keeps working while fuel is supplied, but a primary cell stops when its reactants are used
*Examiner insight:* Half-equations with H⁺ describe an acidic cell, so they do not answer (a) even if they are correctly balanced.

**11. (a)** CH₃OH and H₂O on the left, CO₂ on the right [1]; **CH₃OH + H₂O → CO₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻** [1]
**(b)** **O₂ + 4H⁺ + 4e⁻ → 2H₂O** (or 3/2 O₂ + 6H⁺ + 6e⁻ → 3H₂O) [1]
**(c)** **CH₃OH + 3/2 O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(d)** n(CH₃OH) = 64.1 ÷ 32.05 = 2.00 mol [1]; electrons = 6 × 2.00 = **12.0 mol** [1]; CO₂ = 2.00 × 44.01 = **88.0 g** [1]
**(e)** Advantage: methanol is a liquid, so it is easier and safer to store and transport [1]. Disadvantage: it releases CO₂, while hydrogen gives only water [1]
*Examiner insight:* Before adding half-equations, scale them so the electrons match; an overall equation with electrons left in it is not balanced.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the oxygen atom in an alcohol when balancing O₂.
- Including CO₂ in an equation that asks for carbon monoxide or carbon only.
- Giving wrong oxide formulas for metals, such as NaO or AlO.
- Forgetting to multiply by the number of carbon atoms when finding moles of CO₂.
- Writing "CO₂ traps heat" with no mention of infrared absorption and re-emission.
- Calling biofuels carbon neutral with no qualification.
- Using H⁺ in alkaline half-equations, or putting electrons on the wrong side.
