This guide covers subtopic **1.14 Using a calculator** in Topic 1 Number of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C1.14) and Extended (E1.14) statements are identical:

1. **Use a calculator efficiently**, for example knowing not to round values within a calculation and to round only the final answer.
2. **Enter values appropriately on a calculator**, for example entering 2 hours 30 minutes as 2.5 hours or 2° 30′ 0″.
3. **Interpret the calculator display appropriately**, for example in money 4.8 means $4.80, and in time 3.25 means 3 hours 15 minutes.

The syllabus excludes this subtopic from the non-calculator Papers 1 and 2, so it is tested on Papers 3 (Core) and 4 (Extended), where a scientific calculator is required. In practice it affects almost every question on those papers.

The [estimation and using a calculator study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-estimation-and-using-a-calculator/) sets out the basic rules. This page practises the harder keying: long expressions, nested fractions, negative numbers, chains of answers, and converting a display into hours, minutes and seconds. Afterwards, use the [using a calculator revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-using-a-calculator-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and the [using a calculator practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-using-a-calculator-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Efficient use (1.14.1)

Calculators follow the order of operations. They do exactly what you type, so the keying must show the structure of the calculation:

- A fraction bar is a pair of brackets around the numerator and around the denominator.
- A square root applies to everything under it: put that in brackets.
- A power applies only to the number just before it unless you use brackets.

### Worked example 1: one long calculation

Work out (4.7² + 3.1³) ÷ (√19.6 − 1.25). Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures.

```
Keying:   ( 4.7 x^2 + 3.1 x^3 ) ÷ ( √( 19.6 ) - 1.25 )
Display:  16.32921570...
Answer:   16.3 (3 s.f.)
```

Without the brackets, the calculator works out 4.7² + 3.1³ ÷ √19.6 − 1.25 = 27.6, a completely different value. A quick check: 4.7² is about 22, 3.1³ is about 30, √19.6 is about 4.4, so the answer is near 52 ÷ 3.2, which is about 16.

The fraction template key (often shown as a box over a box) is the safest way to enter a fraction bar, because it keeps the numerator and denominator apart for you.

### Worked example 2: a fraction inside a fraction

Work out 1 ÷ (1/3.2 + 1/4.8).

```
Keying:  1 ÷ ( 1 ÷ 3.2 + 1 ÷ 4.8 )
Display: 1.92
```

The answer is exact, so write 1.92. Do not add a zero or round it further.

### Worked example 3: negative numbers

Work out (a) (−2.5)², (b) −2.5², (c) (−0.6)³.

```
(a) ( - 2.5 ) x^2   ->  6.25
(b)   - 2.5 x^2     -> -6.25     (the calculator squares 2.5, then makes it negative)
(c) ( - 0.6 ) x^3   -> -0.216
```

When a substitution puts a negative number into a power, always type brackets round it.

### Worked example 4: carrying full values between steps

A circular lawn has radius 3.47 m. Turf costs $18.75 per square metre. Find the cost of turfing the lawn.

```
Area = π × 3.47^2 = 37.82760298...  (leave this on the display)
Cost = Ans × 18.75 = 709.2675...  = $709.27

If the area is first rounded to 37.8:
37.8 × 18.75 = 708.75  ->  $708.75   (wrong by 52 cents)
```

Use the Ans key, or store the value in memory, so that the next step uses every digit. On the calculator papers the syllabus asks for the π key or 3.142; the π key is more accurate.

## Entering values (1.14.2)

The calculator does not know your units, so you must enter every value in a consistent form.

- **Time:** minutes are sixtieths of an hour. 2 h 30 min is 2.5 h or 2° 30′ 0″ using the degrees-minutes-seconds key. Typing 2.30 is wrong.
- **Mixed units:** convert first, so that every length is in the same unit before you multiply or add.
- **Standard form:** use the ×10ˣ key (or EXP), which keeps a value such as 3.2 × 10⁻⁴ together as one number. Typing 6 ÷ 3.2 × 10 ^ −4 divides by 3.2 and then multiplies by 10⁻⁴, which is not 6 ÷ (3.2 × 10⁻⁴).
- **Fractions:** use the fraction key for exact fractional answers, such as 2/7 + 3/5 = 31/35.

### Worked example 5: a time in hours, minutes and seconds

A machine runs for 3.8625 hours. Write this time in hours, minutes and seconds.

```
Hours:   3
0.8625 × 60 = 51.75  ->  51 minutes
0.75   × 60 = 45     ->  45 seconds

Answer: 3 hours 51 minutes 45 seconds
```

The degrees-minutes-seconds key gives the same result: enter 3.8625, press the key, and the display shows 3° 51′ 45″. Read degrees as hours here.

## Interpreting the display (1.14.3)

A display is just a number. You must turn it into an answer that suits the question.

| Display | Context | Write |
|---|---|---|
| 4.8 | money in dollars | $4.80 |
| 12.0333... | money in dollars | $12.03 |
| 3.25 | time in hours | 3 hours 15 minutes |
| 0.4 | time in hours | 24 minutes |
| 4.06 × 10⁻⁰³ | any value | 4.06 × 10⁻³ (or 0.004 06) |
| 0.3333333333 | an exact answer from 1 ÷ 3 | 1/3, or 0.333 to 3 s.f. |
| 18.6 | number of coaches needed | 19 |

### Worked example 6: "write down all the figures"

(a) Work out √(12.4² − 3.7²). Write down all the figures on your calculator display.
(b) Write your answer to part (a) correct to 3 significant figures.

```
(a) √( 12.4 x^2 - 3.7 x^2 )
    11.83511724

(b) 11.8
```

In part (a) copy every digit the display shows. If you round in part (a), you lose that mark even though the value is close. Part (b) is the only place to round.

## Common mistakes

- Typing a fraction without brackets, so only part of the numerator is divided.
- Writing −2.5² when (−2.5)² was meant.
- Rounding an answer and then reusing the rounded value in a later part.
- Typing hours and minutes with a decimal point, such as 1 h 45 min as 1.45.
- Reading 5.15 hours as 5 hours 15 minutes; it is 5 hours 9 minutes.
- Copying "E−03" or "×10⁻⁰³" from the screen instead of writing proper standard form.
- Giving money as $4.8 or $709.2675.
- Leaving a calculator in the wrong mode, such as one that shows answers only as fractions, and not converting the answer to the form asked for.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Write down all the figures on your calculator display**: copy the full display; no rounding.
- **Work out** or **Calculate**: give the final answer to 3 significant figures unless it is exact or the question says otherwise. Angles in degrees go to 1 decimal place.
- **Give your answer in hours and minutes**: convert the decimal part of the hours by multiplying by 60.
- **Give your answer in standard form**: write A × 10ⁿ with 1 ⩽ A < 10, never the calculator's E notation.
