## Core recall

### Why vacancies arise

- The business is growing and needs more staff.
- An employee leaves, retires or is dismissed.
- The business needs skills it does not have, often because of new technology.

### Internal versus external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Existing employees, by promotion or transfer | People outside the business |
| Speed and cost | Usually quicker and cheaper | Usually slower and dearer (adverts, agency fees, more interviews) |
| Knowledge of candidate | Business already knows their work record | Relies on selection methods and references |
| Induction | Little needed | Full induction needed |
| New ideas and skills | Few | More |
| Knock-on effect | Leaves another vacancy to fill; colleagues who miss out may be unhappy | Existing staff may feel overlooked for promotion |

### The stages in order

1. Identify the vacancy.
2. Job analysis: what does the job involve?
3. Job description: the job's title, duties, responsibilities and who the post holder reports to.
4. Person specification: the qualifications, skills, experience and qualities of the ideal person, often split into essential and desirable.
5. Advertise internally, externally or both.
6. Receive application forms or CVs; shortlist against the person specification.
7. Select: interviews, skills tests, assessment days, references.
8. Offer the job and issue a contract.

Memory hook for the documents: the **job** description describes the **job**; the **person** specification describes the **person**.

### Matching selection methods to jobs

| Job | Sensible method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Delivery driver | Driving assessment plus licence check | Proves the skill the job depends on |
| Receptionist | Interview | Tests communication and manner with the public |
| Payroll clerk | Numeracy test | Accuracy with figures is essential |
| Chef | Practical cooking test | Shows skill, speed and hygiene |

### Benefits of an effective recruitment process

| Benefit | Chain to write |
|---|---|
| High productivity | Right skills, so more output per worker and less supervision |
| High-quality output or customer service | Fewer mistakes, fewer complaints, more repeat business |
| Staff retention | Suitable staff stay longer, so lower recruitment and training costs |

### Contracts of employment

| Contract | Feature | Main benefit to employer | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|---|
| Full-time | Standard full working week | Commitment, continuity, available all week | Paid even in quiet periods |
| Part-time | Fewer, usually set, hours | Covers busy periods; wider pool of applicants | More staff to manage and train |
| Job share | Two people split one full-time job | Keeps skilled staff who want fewer hours | Handover and communication needed |
| Zero-hour | No guaranteed hours | Pays only when work is needed | Staff may be less committed or unavailable |

Benefits for workers: full-time gives a regular, predictable income; part-time lets people combine work with study or caring.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting suitable applicants to fill a vacancy |
| Selection | Choosing the most suitable applicant |
| Internal recruitment | Filling a vacancy with someone already employed by the business |
| External recruitment | Filling a vacancy with someone from outside the business |
| Job analysis | Studying the tasks and skills a job involves |
| Job description | A document setting out the duties and responsibilities of a job |
| Person specification | A document setting out the qualities, skills and qualifications of the ideal applicant |
| Shortlisting | Selecting the applicants who best match the person specification to go forward to selection |
| Reference | A statement about an applicant from a previous employer or teacher |
| Staff retention | Keeping employees in the business rather than losing them |
| Contract of employment | A legal agreement setting out the terms and conditions of a job |
| Zero-hour contract | A contract with no minimum guaranteed hours |

## Quick calculations

Recruitment questions can include simple figures. Two common types:

- **Cost of a route.** Add every cost: advert + agency fee + management time. An agency fee of 12% on a £24,000 salary is 0.12 × £24,000 = £2,880.
- **Saving from retention.** Number of appointments avoided × cost per appointment. Cutting leavers from 9 to 5 at £1,500 each saves 4 × £1,500 = £6,000.

## Exam traps

- Swapping job description and person specification.
- Forgetting that internal recruitment creates a second vacancy.
- Writing "external recruitment is better" with no link to the skills the case business lacks.
- Treating zero-hour and part-time as the same contract.
- Choosing an interview for every job, even when a practical test would show the skill better.
- Naming a benefit of effective recruitment without its consequence for costs or sales.
- In an evaluation, ignoring the figures given in the case.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between recruitment and selection.
2. State two reasons why a business might need to recruit.
3. Give two advantages of internal recruitment.
4. Explain one disadvantage of internal recruitment.
5. What is the first stage of the recruitment process?
6. Would "must hold a food hygiene certificate" appear on a job description or a person specification?
7. State two selection methods.
8. Why might a garage use a practical test rather than only an interview to select a mechanic?
9. State the three benefits of an effective recruitment process named in the specification.
10. What is a job share?
11. Give one benefit to a business of employing part-time staff.
12. An agency charges 18% of a £20,000 salary. Calculate the fee.

### Answers

1. Recruitment is attracting applicants to a vacancy; selection is choosing the most suitable one.
2. Any two of: growth, replacing an employee who leaves or retires, needing new skills.
3. Any two of: quicker, cheaper, the business knows the candidate, less induction needed, motivates staff by showing promotion is possible.
4. For example, it leaves the promoted person's old job empty, so the business has to recruit again, adding time and cost.
5. Job analysis (after identifying the vacancy).
6. A person specification, because it describes a qualification the applicant must have.
7. Any two of: interview, skills or aptitude test, assessment day or group exercise, references.
8. A test shows whether the applicant can actually carry out repairs safely; an interview only shows how well they talk about it.
9. High productivity; high-quality production or customer service; staff retention.
10. One full-time job divided between two people, who split the hours and duties.
11. For example, the business can schedule staff for busy periods only, so it does not pay for idle hours.
12. 0.18 × £20,000 = **£3,600**.

## Frequently asked questions

**What goes on a person specification?**
The qualifications, skills, experience and personal qualities the ideal applicant should have, often divided into essential and desirable criteria.

**Which contracts do I need to know for AQA GCSE Business?**
Full-time, part-time, job share and zero-hour contracts, plus the benefits of full-time and part-time employment.

**Why might internal recruitment upset staff?**
Colleagues who applied and were not chosen may feel resentful, which can lower their motivation.
