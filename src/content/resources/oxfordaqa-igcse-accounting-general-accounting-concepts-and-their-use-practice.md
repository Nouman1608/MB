## Questions

**1.** Ulverby Joinery fits kitchens. State the concept each situation illustrates.

**(a)** The owner pays his daughter's school fees from the business bank account, and the amount is not treated as a business expense. **[1]**
**(b)** The firm's loyal group of customers is not given a value in the statement of financial position. **[1]**
**(c)** A customer places an order on 28 December. The kitchen is delivered and invoiced on 6 January, and the sale is included in the new year's revenue. **[1]**
**(d)** A lathe bought for 12,000 is shown at that cost less depreciation, although a dealer says it is now worth 15,000. **[1]**

**2.** Sunnerby Garden Centre values closing inventory line by line.

| Line | Cost | Other information |
|---|---|---|
| Lawn mowers | 2,400 | Selling price 2,600; repair needed costing 700 |
| Hedge trimmers | 1,500 | Selling price 2,100 |
| Fertiliser (damaged bags) | 900 | Can be sold for 450 in total |

**(a)** Calculate the value of closing inventory. **[3]**
**(b)** State the effect on profit of using this method instead of cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the concept that requires this method. **[1]**

**3.** Rushfield Cleaning buys a van on the first day of its financial year for 24,000. It expects to use the van for four years and then sell it for 4,000.

**(a)** Work out the yearly straight line depreciation charge. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the depreciation charge for each of the first two years using the reducing balance method at 30%, and the asset's net book value once year 2 is complete. **[3]**
**(c)** Name the concept that requires the business to use the same method every year, and explain why this is useful to the owner. **[2]**

**4.** Plumtree Bakery: wages of 1,800 were owing at the start of the year, 41,300 was paid in the year and 2,100 is owing at the year end.

**(a)** Prepare the wages account, ending with the amount moved to the income statement. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the concept that requires the 2,100 to be included, and state which statement of financial position heading includes it. **[2]**

**5.** Hollinworth Stationers received rent of 9,600, including 1,200 for next year. It received commission of 3,100 and earned a further 450 not yet received.

**(a)** Calculate the rent income and the commission income for the year. **[2]**
**(b)** State the statement of financial position heading for each of the 1,200 and the 450. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the concept behind these adjustments. **[1]**

**6.** Ulverby Joinery has trade receivables of 28,000 and writes off a debt of 1,000 as irrecoverable. It wants a provision for doubtful debts of 4% of the remaining receivables. The existing provision is 700.

**(a)** Calculate the new provision and the increase needed. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total expense in the income statement for irrecoverable debts and the change in provision. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate trade receivables as reported in the statement of financial position, and name the concept behind the provision. **[2]**

**7.** Hollinworth Stationers incurs these costs. (i) Printing machine 6,500. (ii) Installation of the machine 400. (iii) Repair to the shop shutters 350. (iv) An office calculator 11.

**(a)** Classify (i) to (iii) as capital or revenue expenditure. **[3]**
**(b)** State the amount at which the machine is recorded. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain which concept allows (iv) to be expensed, and state the effect on profit if the repair (iii) had been wrongly recorded as capital expenditure. **[2]**

**8.** Rushfield Cleaning sells a machine that cost 16,000 and has accumulated depreciation of 10,400. It receives 4,500 by bank.

**(a)** Prepare the disposal account. **[4]**
**(b)** Name the concept that explains why the machine was carried at cost less depreciation, not at market price, before the sale. **[1]**

**9.** Ulverby Joinery owns equipment that cost 20,000 and has charged year 1 depreciation at 20% reducing balance. The owner wants to switch to straight line at 10% of cost in year 2 to increase profit. The office also buys a 9 pack of paper and expenses it.

**(a)** Calculate the year 2 depreciation charge under each method. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which concept the change would breach and why it matters. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the paper can be expensed immediately. **[2]**

**10.** Sunnerby Garden Centre faces these issues.

**(a)** It receives an order for 5,000 of goods on 29 December, and delivers and invoices on 4 January. State in which year the sale is revenue and why. **[2]**
**(b)** Land bought for 50,000 is now valued at 80,000. State the amount in the statement of financial position, the concept and the difference from the valuation. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the going concern concept affects the way a business values its equipment. **[2]**

**11.** Plumtree Bakery's gross profit is 64,000. The trial balance shows rent 12,000, wages 21,500, insurance 3,200, motor expenses 2,650, trade receivables 18,000 and provision for doubtful debts 500. Adjust for: rent prepaid 2,000; wages owing 1,500; insurance prepaid 800; equipment cost 15,000, residual 3,000, four years, straight line; vehicles cost 18,000, accumulated depreciation 6,000, 25% reducing balance; irrecoverable debt 900; provision to be 5% of remaining receivables.

**(a)** Calculate the net profit. **[8]**
**(b)** Name the concept behind (i) the rent, wages and insurance adjustments and (ii) the provision. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) The business entity concept [1]. (b) Money measurement, the idea that only items with a money value are recorded [1]. (c) Realisation [1]. (d) Historic cost [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Commit to one precise concept per situation rather than listing several.

**2.** (a) Mowers: net realisable value is 2,600 - 700 = 1,900, below cost, so 1,900 [1]. Trimmers: cost 1,500 is lower than the 2,100 selling price, so 1,500 [1]. Fertiliser: 450 is below cost, so 450. Total **3,850** [1]. (b) Profit is lower by 950, because cost is 4,800 and the value used is 3,850 [1]. (c) Prudence [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Apply the rule line by line; one block total hides lines where net realisable value exceeds cost.

**3.** (a) (24,000 - 4,000) / 4 = **5,000** [1]. (b) Year 1: 30% of 24,000 = **7,200** [1]. Year 2: 30% of (24,000 - 7,200) = 30% of 16,800 = **5,040** [1]. Net book value = 24,000 - 7,200 - 5,040 = **11,760** [1]. (c) Consistency [1]; results from different years can be compared fairly because the same method is used [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State the base you use; reducing balance applies to net book value, not original cost.

**4.** (a) Wages account:

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank | 41,300 | Balance b/d | 1,800 |
| Balance c/d | 2,100 | Income statement | 41,600 |
| | 43,400 | | 43,400 |

Opening balance on the credit side [1], bank payments on the debit side [1], closing balance carried down [1], transfer of **41,600** to the income statement [1]. (b) Accruals [1]; it is an other payable in current liabilities [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An accrued expense is a credit balance brought down; the wrong side gives a wrong transfer figure.

**5.** (a) Rent income = 9,600 - 1,200 = **8,400** [1]. Commission income = 3,100 + 450 = **3,550** [1]. (b) The 1,200 is income received in advance, shown in current liabilities [1]. The 450 is income due, shown in current assets [1]. (c) Accruals [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the figure and where it sits; income due is an asset, not a liability.

**6.** (a) Remaining receivables = 28,000 - 1,000 = 27,000 [1]. New provision = 4% of 27,000 = **1,080** [1]. Increase = 1,080 - 700 = **380** [1]. (b) Expense = 1,000 + 380 = **1,380** [1]. (c) Receivables = 27,000 - 1,080 = **25,920** [1]; prudence [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Deduct the irrecoverable debt before taking the percentage.

**7.** (a) (i) Capital [1]. (ii) Capital [1]. (iii) Revenue [1]. (b) 6,500 + 400 = **6,900** [1]. (c) Materiality: the amount is too small to affect decisions, so it is expensed [1]. Profit would be overstated by 350, before any depreciation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to the size of the amount and its effect on readers of the statements, not just that it is cheap.

**8.** (a) Disposal account:

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Machinery (cost) | 16,000 | Provision for depreciation | 10,400 |
| | | Bank | 4,500 |
| | | Income statement (loss) | 1,100 |
| | 16,000 | | 16,000 |

Asset at cost on the debit side [1], depreciation to date on the credit side [1], sale proceeds on the credit side [1], balancing loss of **1,100** moved to the income statement [1]. (b) Historic cost [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check net book value (5,600) against proceeds (4,500) to confirm a loss, not a profit.

**9.** (a) Reducing balance: net book value after year 1 = 20,000 - 4,000 = 16,000, and 20% of 16,000 = **3,200** [1]. Straight line: 10% of 20,000 = **2,000** [1]. (b) Consistency [1]; changing method only to raise profit would make year-to-year comparison unreliable [1]. (c) Materiality [1]; the amount is too small to affect the decisions of anyone reading the statements, so recording it as inventory would be unnecessary [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that wanting more profit is not a valid reason for changing method.

**10.** (a) The new year [1], because delivery and invoicing happen on 4 January (realisation) [1]. (b) The land stays at **50,000** [1] under historic cost, 30,000 below the valuation [1]. (c) The business is assumed to continue, so equipment is valued as an asset being used, not at forced-sale prices [1], and its cost is spread over its life by depreciation [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) needs the reason as well as the year.

**11.** (a) Rent = 12,000 - 2,000 = 10,000 [1]. Wages = 21,500 + 1,500 = 23,000 [1]. Insurance = 3,200 - 800 = 2,400 [1]. Equipment depreciation = (15,000 - 3,000) / 4 = 3,000 [1]. Vehicle net book value = 18,000 - 6,000 = 12,000, so 25% gives 3,000 [1]. Irrecoverable debt 900 [1]. Provision = 5% of (18,000 - 900) = 855; increase = 855 - 500 = 355 [1]. Total expenses = 10,000 + 23,000 + 2,400 + 2,650 + 3,000 + 3,000 + 900 + 355 = 45,305. Net profit = 64,000 - 45,305 = **18,695** [1]. (b) (i) Accruals [1]. (ii) Prudence [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each adjusted expense on its own line and keep a total so one slip is easy to trace.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a concept but not applying it to the facts given.
- Naming accruals when the situation calls for prudence, and the reverse.
- Valuing inventory as one total, or forgetting selling and repair costs.
- Taking the reducing balance percentage of original cost each year.
- Putting an accrued expense on the wrong side of the ledger account.
- Taking the provision percentage before deducting irrecoverable debts.
- Describing income received in advance as an asset.
