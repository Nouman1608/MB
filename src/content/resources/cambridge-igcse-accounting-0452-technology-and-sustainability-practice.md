## Short-answer questions

Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** A business keeps its accounting records on a provider's remote servers, which it reaches through the internet. Which type of storage system is this?

A: Manual

B: Data storage device

C: Cloud service

D: Other digital service

**[1]**

**2.** Which of the following is a risk of **not** storing accounting data safely?

A: Higher use of paper

B: Unauthorised access to customer details

C: Electronic waste from old devices

D: Lower electricity use

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why a business needs to keep its accounting data. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by storing accounting data sustainably. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of storing accounting records on an external hard drive kept in the office. **[2]**

**6.** A business prints 2 copies of each of 150 purchase orders a month, using 1 sheet per copy. It decides to print 1 copy and keep the other digitally.

**(a)** Calculate the number of sheets used a year before the change. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of sheets saved a year. **[1]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Sandown Florists keeps its sales journal and ledgers on a spreadsheet, but its cash book and petty cash book are handwritten.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by using digital applications for part of the accounting records, using Sandown as an example. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** benefits Sandown would gain from keeping its cash book digitally as well. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **three** risks to a business of keeping its only copy of its accounting records on a laptop that the owner carries between home and work. **[6]**

**9.** Compare manual storage with cloud services for a small market stall that has no internet connection at its pitch. **[4]**

**10.** Ridgeway Motors receives its bank statements through an online banking service and its supplier invoices by email. It does not save copies elsewhere.

**(a)** Identify the type of storage system involved. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages of relying on this type of storage alone. **[4]**

**11.** Pembury Traders posts statements of account to 320 customers every month. Each uses 3 sheets of paper. Postage is $0.75 per statement.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly saving in paper and in postage if the statements are emailed instead. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **one** new risk the business must manage after the change. **[2]**

**12.** Nadia runs a small tutoring agency and keeps all her accounting records in paper files in her office. She is considering moving everything to a cloud service. Advise Nadia whether she should do so. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Data on remote servers reached through the internet is a cloud service. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. A and C relate to sustainability rather than safety; D is not a risk. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: to prepare the financial statements [1]; to collect debts and pay suppliers [1]; to support decisions; as evidence for tax authorities or in disputes; to check records such as the bank statement. **[2]**

**4.** Keeping records in a way that limits harm to the environment and avoids wasting resources [1], for example printing less, using energy sensibly and disposing of old devices responsibly [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Advantage, any one: fast access; large capacity; the business controls its own data; no continuing charge [1]. Disadvantage, any one: the drive may fail, be stolen or be destroyed in a fire at the office together with the computer [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 2 × 150 × 12 = **3,600 sheets** [1].

**(b)** 1 × 150 × 12 = **1,800 sheets** saved [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Some of the records are kept using a digital application and others are kept by hand [1]: Sandown's sales journal and ledgers are on a spreadsheet, but its cash book and petty cash book are handwritten [1].

**(b)** Any two benefits, each with one mark for the benefit and one for applying it.

- The bank and cash balances would be calculated automatically [1], avoiding adding-up errors in the cash book [1].
- Entries could be posted to the ledgers without re-keying [1], saving time and avoiding transfer errors between the paper book and the spreadsheet [1].
- Also creditable: up-to-date bank balance for decisions; easier back-up of the cash book. **[6]**

**8.** Each risk: one mark for the cause, one for the consequence.

- The laptop could be lost or stolen while being carried [1], so all the records would be lost and the statements could not be prepared [1].
- The laptop could fail or the files be deleted by accident [1]; with no back-up, recent entries could not be recovered [1].
- Someone could gain unauthorised access to the laptop or it could be hacked [1], exposing confidential customer or payroll details or allowing records to be altered [1].
- Also creditable: damage from being dropped; no records available for the tax authority. **[6]**

**9.** Manual storage needs no internet or power, so the stall can record sales at its pitch [1], but paper records are easily damaged by weather or lost, and are slow to search [1]. A cloud service would keep data safely off-site and accessible from home [1], but without internet at the pitch the stall could not reach it while trading and would pay a continuing charge [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Other digital services [1].

**(b)** Any two, each with one mark for the point and one for development.

- The services may keep records only for a limited time [1], so older statements or invoices may become unavailable when needed for the year-end accounts or the tax authority [1].
- The data is spread across different services [1], so it is slow to gather and some items may be missed [1].
- Also creditable: the business has less control over access and format; a service outage or closure could cut off access. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Paper: 320 × 3 × 12 = **11,520 sheets** [1]. Postage: 320 × 0.75 × 12 = **$2,880** [1].

**(b)** The statements are now stored digitally, so they could be lost through computer failure or accessed without permission [1]; the business must make regular back-ups kept elsewhere and protect the files with passwords [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Context (no separate mark): paper files in one office could be destroyed by fire or flood, and are slow to search.

Advantages of the cloud: records are held off-site, so they survive a disaster at the office [1]; Nadia could reach them from anywhere and share them with her accountant [1]; less paper would be used, which is more sustainable [1].

Disadvantages: she would pay a continuing charge and need a reliable internet connection [1], and she would depend on the provider to keep her clients' details confidential [1].

Advice: she should move to the cloud service, because her records would be safer and easier to use, but keep a back-up copy on a device stored separately [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming software products instead of describing what the application does.
- Mixing up safety risks with sustainability issues.
- Giving advantages only when the question asks you to compare or advise.
- Stating a risk without its consequence for the business.
- Recommending nothing in an Advise question.
