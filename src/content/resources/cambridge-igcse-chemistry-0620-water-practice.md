## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which reagent turns from blue to pink when water is added?

A: anhydrous cobalt(II) chloride

B: anhydrous copper(II) sulfate

C: limewater

D: universal indicator **[1]**

**2.** Describe a chemical test, using anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, to show that a liquid contains water. **[2]**

**3.** State the boiling point of pure water at standard atmospheric pressure. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** substances found in natural water that come from fertilisers. **[2]**

**5.** State **one** substance in natural water that comes from detergents. **[1]**

**6.** State why dissolved oxygen in river water is beneficial. **[1]**

**7.** Some metal compounds in natural water are beneficial and some are harmful. Explain this statement. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** A student tests three colourless liquids.

| Liquid | Effect on anhydrous copper(II) sulfate | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | turns blue | 0 | 100 |
| Y | turns blue | −3 | 101 to 104 |
| Z | no change | −114 | 78 |

**(a)** Identify which liquid contains no water. Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify which liquid is pure water. Explain how the data show this. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the copper(II) sulfate test alone cannot show that liquid X is pure. **[1]**

**9.** A student makes up a solution to test for chloride ions using acidified silver nitrate. She uses tap water instead of distilled water. The test gives a white precipitate even though her sample contained no chloride.

**(a)** Suggest why a white precipitate formed. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why distilled water should be used in practical chemistry. **[2]**

**10.** The table lists stages in the treatment of the domestic water supply.

| Stage | Purpose |
|---|---|
| sedimentation and filtration | ........ |
| treatment with carbon | ........ |
| chlorination | ........ |

**(a)** Complete the purpose of each stage. **[3]**

**(b)** A student says, "Water from the treatment works is pure water." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**11.** Water from a river near farmland contains nitrates, phosphates and sewage.

**(a)** State the effect of high levels of nitrates and phosphates on the river. **[2]**

**(b)** State why sewage in drinking water is dangerous. **[1]**

**(c)** Name the treatment stage that makes water containing microbes safe to drink. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how you would show that a sample of water from a laboratory still is pure. Include the results you would expect. **[3]**

**13.** Cobalt(II) chloride paper is used to test for water.

**(a)** State the colour of the dry paper. **[1]**

**(b)** The paper is pressed against a drop of liquid from a fruit. State the colour change if the liquid contains water. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how a used piece of the paper could be made ready to use again. **[2]**

**14.** A sample of river water is passed through filter paper. The filtrate looks clear.

**(a)** Explain why the clear filtrate may still be unsafe to drink. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the treatment stage that makes it safe from this hazard. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **A** [1].

**2.** Add the liquid to white anhydrous copper(II) sulfate [1]; it turns blue if water is present [1].

**3.** 100 °C [1].

**4.** Nitrates [1]; phosphates [1].

**5.** Phosphates [1].

**6.** Fish and other aquatic life need it (to respire) [1].

**7.** Some metal compounds provide essential minerals for life [1]; other metal compounds are toxic [1].

**8. (a)** Z [1]: it does not turn anhydrous copper(II) sulfate blue (and its melting and boiling points are not those of water) [1].

**(b)** X [1]: it melts at exactly 0 °C and boils at exactly 100 °C, the values for pure water [1].

**(c)** The test shows only that water is present; dissolved impurities would not change the colour result [1].

**9. (a)** The tap water contained chloride ions [1].

**(b)** Tap water contains dissolved chemical impurities [1]; these could react with the chemicals or give false test results, whereas distilled water has fewer impurities [1].

**10. (a)** Sedimentation and filtration: remove (insoluble) solids [1]. Carbon: removes tastes and odours [1]. Chlorination: kills microbes [1].

**(b)** Treatment does not remove dissolved substances / salts [1], and chlorine is added during treatment, so the water still contains other substances [1].

**11. (a)** They lead to deoxygenation of the water [1] and damage to aquatic life [1].

**(b)** It contains harmful microbes that cause disease [1].

**(c)** Chlorination [1].

**12.** Measure the melting point and/or the boiling point of the sample [1]. Pure water melts at 0 °C [1] and boils at 100 °C, sharply, at one temperature [1].

**13. (a)** Blue [1].

**(b)** Blue to pink [1].

**(c)** Heat the paper [1]; this drives off the water, so the paper turns blue again (the change is reversible) [1].

**14. (a)** Filtration removes only insoluble solids [1]; harmful microbes (and dissolved substances) can pass through and may cause disease [1].

**(b)** Chlorination [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a colour test to answer a purity question.
- Reversing the cobalt(II) chloride colours.
- Saying impurities lower the boiling point.
- Mixing up the jobs of carbon and chlorination.
- Writing a long eutrophication sequence instead of "deoxygenation and damage to aquatic life".
- Forgetting that detergents are a source of phosphates.
- Saying distilled water is used because it is "cleaner" without naming dissolved impurities and their effect.
