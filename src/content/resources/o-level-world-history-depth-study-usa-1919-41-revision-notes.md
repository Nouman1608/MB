**These notes condense Depth Study D of Cambridge O Level History 2147, The United States 1919-41.** They cover the four key questions -- the boom, social change, the Wall Street Crash and the New Deal -- in tables and lists for quick review before Paper 1 Section B (one question: 4 + 6 + 10 marks). For full explanations see the [Depth Study D study guide](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-usa-1919-41/); for practice see [Depth Study D practice questions](/resources/o-level-world-history-depth-study-usa-1919-41-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL HISTORY (2147), 2024-2026 -- DEPTH STUDY D**

1. How far did the US economy boom in the 1920s?
2. How far did US society change in the 1920s?
3. What were the causes and consequences of the Wall Street Crash?
4. How successful was the New Deal?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Laissez-faire | Government leaving business alone |
| Protective tariff | Tax on imports to protect US industry |
| Mass production | Making large numbers of identical goods on assembly lines |
| Hire purchase | Paying for goods in instalments |
| Buying on the margin | Buying shares with a small deposit and borrowed money |
| Speculation | Buying shares to sell quickly for profit |
| Isolationism | Staying out of foreign affairs |
| Hooverville | Shanty town of the homeless |
| Alphabet agencies | New Deal bodies known by initials |
| Rugged individualism | Hoover's belief in self-reliance rather than government help |

## Key question 1: winners and losers of the boom

| Winners | Losers |
|---|---|
| Car makers and suppliers (steel, rubber, glass, oil) | Farmers: over-production, low prices, debt |
| Electrical and consumer-goods firms | Coal, textiles, railways |
| Shareholders and the urban middle class | Black Americans, especially Southern sharecroppers |
| Advertising, construction, cinema | Native Americans and many new immigrants |

**Late-1920s weaknesses:** over-production, unequal incomes, rising debt, weak banks, falling exports.

## Key question 2: society -- change vs continuity

- **Change:** jazz, cinema, radio, cars; flappers; women voting and working in offices.
- **Continuity/intolerance:** Red Scare; quotas; Sacco and Vanzetti; Scopes Trial; Ku Klux Klan; segregation.
- **Prohibition:** *in* (1920) -- temperance groups, churches, anti-German feeling, belief alcohol caused crime; *out* (1933) -- unenforceable, gangsters, corruption, lost taxes, Depression.

## Key question 3: the Crash

**Short-term trigger:** speculation and margin buying; panic selling on Black Thursday (24 October) and Black Tuesday (29 October 1929).

**Long-term causes:** over-production; unequal wealth; weak banks; farm crisis; tariffs reducing trade.

**Consequences:** bank failures; about 13 million unemployed by 1933; Hoovervilles; Dust Bowl farmers; Hoover blamed.

**1932 election:**

| Hoover | Roosevelt |
|---|---|
| Recovery "just around the corner" | "A new deal for the American people" |
| Limited federal action; RFC | Active government; relief for the unemployed |
| Bonus Army cleared by troops | Energetic record as Governor of New York |

## Key question 4: the New Deal

| First New Deal (1933) | Second New Deal (1935) |
|---|---|
| Emergency Banking Act | WPA -- public works jobs |
| CCC -- young men's work camps | Social Security Act -- pensions, unemployment insurance |
| AAA -- farm prices | Wagner Act -- union rights |
| TVA -- dams, electricity | |
| NRA -- industry codes; PWA -- big public projects | |

**Opponents:** Republicans; the rich; business (Liberty League); the Supreme Court (NRA, AAA struck down); Huey Long ("Share Our Wealth").

**Success:** banks saved; relief and jobs; Social Security; TVA; faith in democracy. **Failure:** unemployment stayed high; 1937-38 recession; limited help for Black Americans.

## Worked example: a (c) judgement sentence

*"The New Deal was a success." How far do you agree?* **[10]**

Strong conclusion: "The New Deal succeeded in relief and reform -- saving the banks and creating Social Security -- but it did not achieve full recovery, since unemployment remained high until war production began; it was therefore a partial success."

## Exam traps

- "All Americans were rich in the 1920s."
- Speculation as the *only* cause of the Crash.
- Putting the WPA or Social Security in 1933.
- Forgetting that the Supreme Court is a named source of opposition.
- Describing alphabet agencies without saying what each achieved.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why did agriculture decline?**
Prices fell after wartime demand ended, while machinery increased output -- too much food, too little demand.

**What was the Bonus Army?**
First World War veterans who camped in Washington in 1932 to demand early payment of a promised bonus; Hoover had the army remove them.

**Who was Huey Long?**
A Louisiana senator who said the New Deal did not go far enough and proposed redistributing the wealth of the rich; he was assassinated in 1935.

## Self-test

1. What did the Volstead Act do? *Defined and enforced Prohibition from 1920.*
2. Which two days saw the worst falls in October 1929? *24 and 29 October.*
3. What did the TVA do? *Built dams for electricity and flood control in the Tennessee Valley.*
4. Which court case struck down the NRA? *The Supreme Court ruled it unconstitutional in 1935.*
5. Which Act created pensions? *The Social Security Act, 1935.*
