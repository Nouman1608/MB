## 3.1.1 The definition, word by word

**Diffusion is the net movement of particles from a region of their higher concentration to a region of their lower concentration (down a concentration gradient), as a result of their random movement.**

- **Net:** particles move in both directions all the time, but more move from high to low concentration than the other way. "Net" means the overall result.
- **Particles:** molecules or ions, such as oxygen molecules or sodium ions.
- **Higher to lower concentration:** the direction. A **concentration gradient** is the difference in concentration between two regions; diffusion goes **down** it.
- **Random movement:** particles are not "trying" to spread out. They move randomly and collide; the spreading happens because there are more particles on the crowded side to move across.

When the concentration becomes the same everywhere, particles still move, but there is no **net** movement.

## 3.1.2 Where the energy comes from

The energy for diffusion comes from the **kinetic energy of the random movement** of molecules and ions. The cell does **not** supply energy from respiration. That is why diffusion is described as a passive process, and it is the key difference from active transport (sub-topic 3.3).

## 3.1.3 Diffusion through the cell membrane

Some substances move into and out of cells by diffusion **through the cell membrane**. Small molecules such as oxygen and carbon dioxide pass through easily. Which way they go depends only on which side has the higher concentration.

## 3.1.4 Why diffusion matters in organisms

| Where | What diffuses | Direction and reason |
|---|---|---|
| Alveoli in the lungs | Oxygen | From air in the alveoli into the blood, where oxygen concentration is lower |
| Alveoli in the lungs | Carbon dioxide | From the blood into the alveoli, where its concentration is lower |
| Respiring body cells | Oxygen and carbon dioxide | Oxygen from blood into cells, which use it up; carbon dioxide from cells, which make it, into blood |
| Leaves | Carbon dioxide | From the air spaces into photosynthesising cells, which use it up |
| Leaves | Oxygen and water vapour | Out of the leaf through the stomata |
| Small intestine | Products of digestion, such as glucose | From the gut into the blood, while their concentration in the gut is higher |
| Single-celled organisms | Oxygen and carbon dioxide | Directly across the cell membrane, because the distance to every part of the cell is short |

**Gases** and **solutes** (substances dissolved in water) both diffuse. A strong answer names the substance, the place, and the direction down the concentration gradient.

## 3.1.5 Investigating the four factors

| Factor | Effect on rate of diffusion | Particle explanation |
|---|---|---|
| Surface area | Larger area → faster | More particles can cross at the same time |
| Temperature | Higher → faster | Particles have more kinetic energy and move faster |
| Concentration gradient | Steeper → faster | A bigger difference means more particles move down the gradient than back |
| Distance | Shorter → faster | Particles reach the other side sooner |

### A standard method: coloured agar cubes

Agar jelly is made containing an alkali and phenolphthalein indicator, so it is pink. Cubes are cut and placed in dilute acid. As the acid diffuses in, it neutralises the alkali and the pink colour disappears. The **time** for a cube to turn completely colourless measures how quickly acid diffuses to its centre.

- **Surface area:** compare cubes of different sizes (see the worked example).
- **Temperature:** same size cubes in acid at different temperatures, using water baths.
- **Concentration gradient:** same size cubes in acid of different concentrations.
- **Distance:** a bigger cube means the acid travels further to reach the centre.

**Variables for a fair test:**

- Independent: the one factor being changed.
- Dependent: time for the cube to turn colourless.
- Controlled: everything else, such as acid concentration, volume of acid, temperature and cube size, whichever are not being tested.
- Repeats: three cubes per condition, then a mean.

**Safety:** wear eye protection; acid and alkali are irritants. Cut cubes on a tile, away from the body.

### Worked example 1: surface area and volume of cubes

| Cube side / cm | Surface area / cm² | Volume / cm³ | Surface area : volume |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 6 × 1 × 1 = 6 | 1 × 1 × 1 = 1 | 6 : 1 |
| 2 | 6 × 2 × 2 = 24 | 2 × 2 × 2 = 8 | 3 : 1 |
| 3 | 6 × 3 × 3 = 54 | 3 × 3 × 3 = 27 | 2 : 1 |

As cubes get bigger, the surface area per unit volume falls, and the distance to the centre rises. Both slow the time for the whole cube to change colour. The same idea explains why a single-celled organism can rely on diffusion across its surface, but a large animal needs gas exchange organs and a transport system.

### Worked example 2: interpreting results (illustrative data)

| Cube side / cm | Mean time to turn colourless / s |
|---|---|
| 1 | 120 |
| 2 | 300 |
| 3 | 540 |

- **Describe:** as the cube side increases from 1 cm to 3 cm, the time increases from 120 s to 540 s.
- **Explain:** larger cubes have a smaller surface area to volume ratio and a greater distance from the surface to the centre, so acid takes longer to diffuse to the centre.
- **Rate:** a rate can be calculated as 1000 ÷ time, giving 8.33, 3.33 and 1.85 for the three cubes (arbitrary units), which shows the rate falls as size increases.

### Other simple investigations

- A crystal of a coloured substance, such as potassium manganate(VII), in water at different temperatures: compare the time for the colour to spread a set distance.
- Ammonia diffusing along a tube with damp red litmus paper at intervals: time how long each paper takes to turn blue. This shows the effect of distance.

## Common errors

- Leaving out **net** or **random movement** from the definition.
- Writing "diffusion is when particles spread out": add the direction, from **higher to lower** concentration.
- Saying diffusion uses energy from respiration. It uses the kinetic energy of the particles.
- Saying particles stop moving at equilibrium. They keep moving, but there is no net movement.
- Giving a factor without a particle explanation, for example "temperature increases diffusion" without "particles have more kinetic energy".
- Changing two variables at once in a plan.

## Exam technique for Cambridge command words

- **Describe** (the process): use the full definition.
- **State** (the energy source): "kinetic energy of random movement of molecules and ions".
- **Describe the importance:** substance, place, direction and purpose.
- **Explain** (a result): link the factor to particle movement.
- **Plan / investigate:** independent, dependent and controlled variables, repeats and safety.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does diffusion need energy from the cell?** No. The energy comes from the kinetic energy of the particles' random movement.

**What does "net movement" mean?** The overall movement after particles moving in both directions are taken into account.

**Is any of 3.1 Extended only?** No. All of sub-topic 3.1 is Core.
