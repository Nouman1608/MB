These questions cover section **3.3.13.2 Proteins** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Proteins study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-proteins/) and the [Amino acids, proteins and DNA revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-amino-acids-proteins-and-dna-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0. Relative molecular masses: glycine 75.0, alanine 89.0, serine 105.0.

## Questions

**1.** Draw the structure of the peptide link and name the small molecule lost when it forms. **[2]**

**2.** Draw the structure of the dipeptide formed when glycine, H₂N–CH₂–COOH, reacts with serine, H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH, with the glycine as the amino end of the chain. **[2]**

**3.** Draw the structure of the tripeptide alanine–glycine–serine, starting with the free amino group of alanine. **[3]**

**4.** A protein chain contains 120 amino acids. State the number of peptide links in the chain. **[1]**

**5.** The tripeptide in question 3 is heated with aqueous hydrochloric acid for several hours.

**(a)** State what happens to the peptide links. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the structures of the products. **[3]**

**6.** State what is meant by the primary structure of a protein. **[1]**

**7.** Describe the secondary structures called the α-helix and the β-pleated sheet, and state what holds each in shape. **[4]**

**8.** Name two types of interaction that maintain the tertiary structure of a protein, and explain how one of them forms from the amino acid cysteine. **[3]**

**9.** The Mᵣ of the tripeptide glycine–glycine–alanine is required. Calculate it. **[2]**

**10.** A mixture of amino acids is separated by thin-layer chromatography.

**(a)** State why the baseline is drawn in pencil. **[1]**

**(b)** State why a lid is placed on the tank. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the plate is sprayed with ninhydrin. **[1]**

**(d)** State an alternative method of locating the spots. **[1]**

**11.** On a chromatogram the solvent front is 9.0 cm from the baseline. Two spots, X and Y, are 2.7 cm and 5.4 cm from the baseline. Calculate the Rf value of each. **[2]**

**12.** In a TLC experiment under the same conditions, the Rf values of three known amino acids are: serine 0.30, alanine 0.42 and leucine 0.74. A hydrolysed peptide gives two spots at 3.8 cm and 6.7 cm from the baseline, with the solvent front at 9.0 cm. Calculate the Rf values and identify the amino acids. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why the products of the hydrolysis of a peptide in hydrochloric acid are present as ⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COOH. **[2]**

**14.** Describe how the amino acids in a sample of a protein could be identified. Include the hydrolysis and the analysis. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** –CO–NH– drawn in full, with C=O and N–H [1]; water is lost [1]. **[2]**

**2.** H₂N–CH₂–CO–NH–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH [1]; with the peptide link between the glycine carbonyl carbon and the serine nitrogen, and the free –COOH on the serine end [1]. **[2]**

**3.** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–CO–NH–CH₂–CO–NH–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH [3] (1 for the correct order, 1 for two peptide links, 1 for the free ends). **[3]**

**4.** 119 [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** The peptide links are hydrolysed (broken by reaction with water) [1]

**(b)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₃)–COOH [1]; ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH [1]; ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH [1] (the neutral amino acids, written as H₂N–CHR–COOH, are also acceptable). **[4]**

**6.** The sequence (order) of amino acids in the polypeptide chain [1]. **[1]**

**7.** α-helix: the chain coils into a spiral [1]; held by hydrogen bonds between N–H and C=O groups of peptide links in different turns of the coil [1]. β-pleated sheet: chains (or sections of chain) lie side by side in a zigzag, forming a sheet [1]; held by hydrogen bonds between N–H and C=O groups on neighbouring chains [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Hydrogen bonding between side chains [1]; S–S (disulfide) bonds [1]; cysteine has an –SH group, and two –SH groups from different parts of the chain are oxidised to form an S–S covalent bond [1]. **[3]**

**9.** 75.0 + 75.0 + 89.0 = 239.0 [1]; minus 2 × 18.0 for two waters = **203.0** [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** Ink would dissolve in the solvent and run (pencil does not) [1]

**(b)** To keep the atmosphere saturated with solvent vapour so the solvent does not evaporate from the plate [1]

**(c)** The amino acids are colourless, and ninhydrin reacts with them to give purple (coloured) spots [1]

**(d)** Viewing under ultraviolet light, if the plate has a fluorescent compound [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Rf(X) = 2.7 ÷ 9.0 = **0.30** [1]; Rf(Y) = 5.4 ÷ 9.0 = **0.60** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Rf of the first spot = 3.8 ÷ 9.0 = 0.42 [1]; Rf of the second spot = 6.7 ÷ 9.0 = 0.74 [1]; the first is alanine [1]; the second is leucine [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Measure from the baseline to the centre of each spot, not from the bottom of the plate.

**13.** In acid solution there is an excess of H⁺ [1]; the amino group is basic, so it accepts H⁺ to become –NH₃⁺, and the carboxyl group stays as –COOH [1]. **[2]**

**14.** Heat the protein with aqueous acid (for example hydrochloric acid) to hydrolyse the peptide links to amino acids [1]; spot the mixture onto the baseline of a TLC plate, with known amino acids alongside [1]; develop the plate in a suitable solvent [1]; locate the spots with ninhydrin or ultraviolet light [1]; calculate Rf values and compare with those of the known amino acids [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the direction of the peptide link or losing the C=O.
- Missing the free –NH₂ and –COOH at the ends of the chain.
- Writing the wrong number of peptide links.
- Giving covalent bonds as the force in the α-helix.
- Describing S–S bonds as hydrogen bonds.
- Using ink for the baseline, or measuring Rf from the wrong place.
