## What a partnership is

A **partnership** is a business owned by two or more people who share the
profits and losses. Like a sole trader, it can trade in goods, provide a
service (an accountancy or dental practice), manufacture, or combine these.
Its statement of profit or loss is prepared exactly as for a sole trader of
the same type; the differences come after profit for the year.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| More capital than one owner can provide | Profits are shared |
| Skills and workload are shared | Partners may disagree, slowing decisions |
| Losses are shared | Each partner is bound by the others' business decisions |
| Cover for illness or holidays | Unlimited liability for the debts of the business (for an ordinary partnership) |
| Accounts remain private | Capital is still limited compared with a company |

## The partnership agreement

A **partnership agreement** sets out the partners' rights and duties. It
matters because it settles in advance the questions most likely to cause
disputes, especially how profit is shared. Typical contents:

- the capital each partner contributes
- the ratio in which profits and losses are shared
- interest on capital, and its rate
- interest on drawings, and how it is calculated
- partners' salaries
- interest on any loans from partners
- limits on drawings
- the duties of each partner and how disputes are settled
- arrangements for a partner joining or leaving.

## Where each item goes

| Item | Where it is recorded | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on a partner's **loan** | Expense in the statement of profit or loss; credited to the partner's current account if unpaid | The loan is a liability of the business, like any other loan |
| Interest on **capital** | Appropriation account (deducted); credited to current account | A share of profit that rewards capital invested |
| **Salary** to a partner | Appropriation account (deducted); credited to current account | A share of profit that rewards extra work |
| Interest on **drawings** | Appropriation account (added); debited to current account | Discourages early or large drawings |
| **Residual profit** | Appropriation account, shared in the agreed ratio; credited to current accounts | The balance left after the above |
| **Drawings** | Debited to current accounts | Withdrawals reduce what each partner is owed |

The **appropriation account** shows how the profit for the year is divided
between the partners. It follows the statement of profit or loss.

## Capital accounts and current accounts

Partners usually keep **fixed capital accounts**: the balance changes only
when a partner introduces or permanently withdraws capital. Everything that
changes from year to year (interest, salaries, shares of profit, drawings)
goes through a **current account**.

| Capital account | Current account |
|---|---|
| Records the long-term investment | Records the yearly shares of profit and drawings |
| Usually a fixed credit balance | Balance changes every year |
| Basis for calculating interest on capital | Can be a credit or a **debit** balance |

A **debit balance** on a current account means the partner has withdrawn
more than his or her share of profit to date; in effect the partner owes the
business. It is deducted in the statement of financial position.

## Worked example: Amina and Bilal

Amina and Bilal share residual profits and losses in the ratio 3 : 2. Their
agreement provides:

- interest on capital at 5% per year
- a salary for Bilal of $12,000 per year
- interest on drawings: Amina $300, Bilal $450 for the year
- interest at 6% per year on Amina's loan to the firm of $10,000.

Fixed capitals are Amina $60,000 and Bilal $40,000. On 1 January 2027 Amina's
current account had a credit balance of $2,400 and Bilal's a debit balance of
$800. Drawings for 2027 were Amina $21,000 and Bilal $25,600. The loan
interest has not been paid. Profit for the year ended 31 December 2027,
**after** charging the loan interest of $600, was $46,750.

shown with other loans, under non-current liabilities if it is not due for
repayment within a year.

## Common mistakes

- Putting interest on a partner's loan in the appropriation account instead
of the statement of profit or loss.
- Adding interest on capital, or deducting interest on drawings, in the
appropriation account (both the wrong way round).
- Sharing the whole profit in the ratio before deducting salaries and
interest.
- Crediting drawings to the current account.
- Ignoring an opening debit balance on a current account.
- Showing a debit balance on a current account as a positive figure.
- Recording salaries as wages in the statement of profit or loss.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** the appropriation account directly below profit for the
year, with each partner named on every line.
- In current accounts use **columnar** form: one column per partner, both
sides.
- **Explain** the difference between capital and current accounts with two
contrasts: what each records and how its balance behaves.
- **Advise** questions on forming a partnership should weigh the extra
capital and skills against shared profit and unlimited liability for the
named people.
