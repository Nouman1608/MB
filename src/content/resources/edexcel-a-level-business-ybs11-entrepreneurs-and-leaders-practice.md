Each question uses one of the specification's command words at its published International AS tariff: **define** (2), **calculate** (4), **explain** (4), **analyse** (6), **discuss** (8) and **assess** (10). All people, businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: Second Rise Bakery**
>
> Nadia Okafor left her job as a hospital pharmacist, where she earned
> $52,000 a year, to open Second Rise, a bakery that trains and employs
> people who have been homeless. She invested $40,000 of savings that had
> been earning 3% interest a year. Two banks refused her a loan because
> she had no experience of running a business. In its first year Second
> Rise made a profit of $34,000, most of which Nadia plans to spend on
> training places. She says: "I wanted to run something that reflects
> what I believe, and to make my own decisions."
>
> **Source 2: Halvard Outdoor**
>
> Halvard Outdoor is an established maker of outdoor clothing. It lets its
> designers spend one day in ten on their own projects, which costs about
> $310,000 a year in staff time. One designer used this time to create a
> jacket repair service that now earns steady revenue and has attracted
> positive press coverage. Halvard sells 30,000 of its main jacket a year
> at $150, with variable costs of $90 a jacket. To raise market share, the
> marketing director wants to cut the price to $135, expecting sales to
> rise to 36,000 a year. The finance director wants to keep profit as the
> main objective.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "social entrepreneurship". **[2]**

**2.** Calculate Nadia's opportunity cost of running Second Rise for its first year, and compare it with the bakery's profit. **[4]**

**3.** Explain **one** barrier to entrepreneurship that Nadia faced. **[4]**

**4.** Explain **one** characteristic Nadia is likely to need as an entrepreneur. **[4]**

**5.** Explain why survival is likely to be an important objective for Second Rise in its early years. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse **two** non-financial motives Nadia had for setting up Second Rise. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "intrapreneurship". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the contribution from Halvard's main jacket before and after the proposed price cut. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the trade-off Halvard faces in deciding whether to cut the price of its main jacket. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse **two** benefits to Halvard of encouraging intrapreneurship. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss whether Halvard should continue to give designers one day in ten for their own projects. **[8]**

**12.** Assess whether Halvard should cut the price of its main jacket to pursue market share rather than profit. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Setting up and running a business whose main aim is to tackle a social or environmental problem [1], with most of any surplus reinvested in that aim rather than paid to owners [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Salary given up = $52,000 [1]. Interest given up = 3% × $40,000 = $1,200 [1]. Opportunity cost = **$53,200** [1]. Profit of $34,000 is $19,200 less than her best alternative, so in money terms Nadia is worse off, though she gains non-financial benefits [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Lack of access to finance [1]: two banks refused her a loan [1] because she had no experience of running a business [1], so she had to rely on her own $40,000, which limited how much she could invest and put her personal savings at risk [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Resilience [1]: Nadia was refused by two banks and is working with staff who may need extra support [1], so she will face setbacks such as staff turnover or disappointing sales [1]. Resilience lets her keep going and adapt rather than give up when early problems arise [1]. *(Also creditable: determination, communication, willingness to take risks, applied to the source.)* **[4]**

**5.** A new business has no customer base or track record [1] and Nadia could not get a bank loan, so Second Rise has limited cash to absorb losses [1]. It must cover its costs and pay suppliers and staff on time [1], because if it fails, the training places it exists to provide disappear too [1]. **[4]**

**6.** **Ethical stance** [1]: Nadia says she wanted to run something that reflects what she believes [1], so she chose a business model that trains and employs people who have been homeless, even though it may cost more than hiring experienced bakers [1].
**Independence** [1]: she wanted to make her own decisions [1], which a salaried hospital job did not allow, so she accepted lower income in return for control over what the business does [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Innovation by employees within an existing business [1], who act like entrepreneurs by developing new products or processes using the firm's resources [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Contribution per jacket now = $150 − $90 = $60, so total = 30,000 × $60 = **$1,800,000** [1] [1]. After: $135 − $90 = $45, so total = 36,000 × $45 = **$1,620,000** [1] [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Cutting the price raises expected sales by 6,000 jackets, increasing market share [1], but contribution falls by $180,000 a year [1]. Halvard cannot have both higher share and higher short-term profit from this decision [1], so it must decide whether the share gained is worth the profit given up [1]. **[4]**

**10.** **New revenue streams** [1]: the repair service was created by a designer and now earns steady revenue [1], so Halvard gains a new product without the cost of hiring outside specialists [1].
**Reputation and retention** [1]: the service attracted positive press coverage, which strengthens the brand [1], and creative staff who can pursue their own ideas are less likely to leave to start rival businesses [1]. **[6]**

**11.** **For continuing:** the repair service shows the scheme can create successful products [1]; it may help Halvard retain designers who might otherwise become competitors [1]; and the press coverage supports the premium brand [1].
**Against:** the scheme costs about $310,000 a year in staff time [1], and the opportunity cost is the main-range design work not done in that time [1]; many projects will fail, so the return is uncertain [1].
**Judgement:** Halvard should continue, because one successful project can repay the cost over several years and the scheme builds a culture of innovation [1]; this depends on whether it reviews projects so that weak ones are stopped early [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **For the price cut:** sales are expected to rise by 20%, raising market share [1]; a larger share may bring economies of scale that lower the $90 variable cost later [1]; higher volume could strengthen Halvard's position against rivals [1]; more customers owning Halvard jackets also widens the market for the repair service [1].
**Against:** contribution falls from $1.8m to $1.62m, a loss of $180,000 a year [1]; the forecast of 36,000 sales may be optimistic, which would make the loss larger [1]; a lower price could weaken Halvard's premium image, especially as the repair service supports a quality reputation [1]; once cut, prices are hard to raise again [1].
**Judgement:** Halvard should not cut the price across the board, because the profit sacrificed is certain while the share gain is only a forecast [1]. A trial in one market, or promotion that adds value without cutting price, would test demand with less risk; the decision depends on how price elastic demand for the jacket really is [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the interest forgone when calculating the opportunity cost of starting a business.
- Calling Nadia a profit maximiser when the source shows she plans to reinvest profit in training places.
- Explaining a barrier in general terms without using the bank refusals in the source.
- Comparing sales volumes but not contribution when judging a price cut.
- Giving a judgement without stating what it depends on.
