Condensed notes for subtopic **7.3 Preparation of salts**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. Items marked *(Extended)* are Supplement content. Full method descriptions are in the [preparation of salts study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-preparation-of-salts/); exam-style questions are in the [preparation of salts practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-preparation-of-salts-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Salt | Compound formed when the H⁺ of an acid is replaced by a metal ion or ammonium ion |
| Excess | More than enough to react with all of the other reactant |
| Filtrate | The solution that passes through the filter paper |
| Residue | The solid left in the filter paper |
| Saturated solution | Contains the maximum concentration of solute at that temperature |
| Hydrated | Chemically combined with water |
| Anhydrous | Containing no water |
| Water of crystallisation *(Extended)* | Water molecules present in hydrated crystals, e.g. CuSO₄·5H₂O, CoCl₂·6H₂O |
| Precipitate *(Extended)* | Insoluble solid formed when two solutions are mixed |

## Solubility rules

| Soluble | Insoluble (exceptions) |
|---|---|
| All sodium, potassium, ammonium salts | — |
| All nitrates | — |
| Chlorides | Lead and silver chlorides |
| Sulfates | Barium, calcium and lead sulfates |
| Sodium, potassium, ammonium carbonates | All other carbonates |
| Sodium, potassium, ammonium hydroxides; calcium hydroxide (partially) | All other hydroxides |

## Choosing a method

| Salt | Other reactant | Method |
|---|---|---|
| Soluble | Alkali (soluble) | Titration |
| Soluble | Suitable metal (e.g. Mg, Zn, Fe) | Excess metal, filter, crystallise |
| Soluble | Insoluble base (e.g. CuO, MgO) | Excess base, filter, crystallise |
| Soluble | Insoluble carbonate (e.g. CuCO₃, ZnCO₃) | Excess carbonate, filter, crystallise |
| Insoluble | Two soluble salts | Precipitation *(Extended)* |

## Excess-solid method: steps and purposes

| Step | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Warm the acid | Speeds up the reaction |
| Add solid until some remains | Ensures all the acid reacts |
| Filter | Removes the excess solid |
| Heat filtrate until saturated (crystals on a cooled glass rod) | Concentrates the solution |
| Leave to cool | Crystals form as solubility falls |
| Filter, wash with a little cold distilled water | Removes the remaining solution |
| Dry between filter papers / in a low oven | Removes surface water without losing water of crystallisation |

End-point signs: metal, **fizzing stops**; carbonate, **effervescence stops**; base, **solid stops dissolving**.

## Titration method

1. Pipette a fixed volume of alkali into a conical flask; add indicator.
2. Add acid from a burette until the colour just changes; note the volume.
3. Repeat with the same volumes **without indicator**.
4. Evaporate to saturation, cool, crystallise, filter, dry.

## Precipitation *(Extended)*

1. Mix a solution containing the cation with a solution containing the anion.
2. Filter to collect the precipitate.
3. Wash with distilled water.
4. Dry.

Choosing reactants: use the **metal nitrate** for the cation and the **sodium or potassium salt** for the anion.

| Target salt | Reactants |
|---|---|
| BaSO₄ | Barium chloride or nitrate + sodium sulfate |
| PbI₂ (yellow) | Lead(II) nitrate + potassium iodide |
| AgCl (white) | Silver nitrate + sodium chloride |
| CaCO₃ | Calcium nitrate + sodium carbonate |

## Picking the acid and the solid

The acid fixes the anion; the solid (or alkali) fixes the cation.

| Acid | Salt ending | Example target | Suitable second reactant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hydrochloric | Chloride | Zinc chloride | Excess Zn, ZnO or ZnCO₃ |
| Sulfuric | Sulfate | Copper(II) sulfate | Excess CuO or CuCO₃ (not Cu) |
| Nitric | Nitrate | Magnesium nitrate | Excess Mg, MgO or MgCO₃ |
| Sulfuric | Sulfate | Potassium sulfate | Potassium hydroxide (titration) |

Check every plan against two questions: is the target salt soluble, and can the excess be filtered off?

## Equations to know

```
Zn + H2SO4 → ZnSO4 + H2
CuO + H2SO4 → CuSO4 + H2O
ZnCO3 + 2HCl → ZnCl2 + H2O + CO2
KOH + HNO3 → KNO3 + H2O
Pb(NO3)2 + 2NaCl → PbCl2 + 2NaNO3
```

## Exam traps

- Say **excess** and say **why** (all the acid reacts).
- Filter off the excess **before** crystallising.
- Don't evaporate to **dryness**; heat until saturated, then cool.
- Titration: repeat **without indicator**.
- Precipitation: **no** evaporation; wash and dry the residue.
- Copper does not react with dilute acids; sodium and potassium are too dangerous.
- Hydrated CuSO₄ is **blue**; anhydrous is **white**. Hydrated CoCl₂ is **pink**; anhydrous is **blue**.

## Self-test

1. Which method would you use to make potassium chloride? Why?
2. Why is copper(II) oxide added in excess when making copper(II) sulfate?
3. How do you know when enough magnesium carbonate has been added to hydrochloric acid?
4. Why is the filtrate not evaporated to dryness?
5. How do you test whether a solution is ready to crystallise?
6. Why is the titration repeated without indicator?
7. Is lead(II) sulfate soluble or insoluble?
8. Name a metal that is unsuitable for making a salt with dilute acid, and explain why.
9. Define a hydrated substance.
10. *(Extended)* Name two solutions you could mix to make barium sulfate.
11. *(Extended)* Why is a precipitate washed with distilled water?
12. *(Extended)* Define water of crystallisation.

**Answers**

1. Titration (potassium hydroxide + hydrochloric acid); both reactants and the salt are soluble.
2. To make sure all the acid reacts; the excess is filtered off.
3. Effervescence stops and solid remains.
4. It could decompose the salt or drive off water of crystallisation; crystals are purer when formed on cooling.
5. Dip in a glass rod; crystals form on it as it cools.
6. The indicator would contaminate the salt.
7. Insoluble.
8. Copper (does not react) or sodium/potassium (reacts dangerously).
9. A substance that is chemically combined with water.
10. Barium chloride (or barium nitrate) and sodium sulfate.
11. To remove soluble impurities (the other product).
12. The water molecules present in hydrated crystals.
