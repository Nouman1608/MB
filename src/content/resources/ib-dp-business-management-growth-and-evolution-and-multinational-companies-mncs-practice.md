## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between internal growth and external growth. **[2]**

**2.** Outline three reasons why a business might choose to stay small. **[3]**

**3.** Explain one internal economy of scale and one external economy of scale. **[4]**

**4.** Nolgarth Glassworks produces bottles. Its total cost is 85,000 at 10,000 bottles, 150,000 at 25,000 bottles and 288,000 at 40,000 bottles.

**(a)** Calculate the average cost at 10,000, 25,000 and 40,000 bottles. **[3]**
**(b)** State the range of output over which diseconomies of scale appear. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain one possible cause of these diseconomies. **[2]**

**5.** Veltane Burgers franchises six outlets, each with annual sales of 280,000. The royalty is 7% of sales. One franchisee has cost of sales equal to 55% of sales and other costs of 95,000, apart from the royalty.

**(a)** Calculate the franchisor's annual royalty income. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate one franchisee's annual profit. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain one drawback of franchising for a franchisee. **[2]**

**6.** Zandrel Dairy has a market share of 18% in a market worth 250m. It takes over Torrowick Creamery, which has a market share of 7%.

**(a)** Calculate the combined market share and the combined sales revenue. **[3]**
**(b)** Distinguish between a takeover and a merger. **[2]**

**7.** Distinguish between a joint venture and a strategic alliance. **[3]**

**8.** Explain two ways in which a multinational company (MNC) can benefit a host country. **[4]**

**9.** Kellarow Electronics, an MNC, earns a profit of 40m in its host country. The host government taxes profit at 20%, and the MNC sends 75% of after-tax profit to its home country.

**(a)** Calculate the amount sent to the home country. **[3]**
**(b)** Comment on what this result suggests about the effect of the MNC on the host country. **[2]**

**10.** Ardenvale Textiles has sales revenue of 15.0m. It can grow organically by 5% a year or take over Isenhall Looms, which has sales revenue of 6.0m, for 9.0m. The takeover would save 0.6m a year in costs. Evaluate which growth method Ardenvale should use. **[8]**

**11.** Darrowmere Mining opens a mine in Casterland. It employs 900 local workers at 8,000 a year each. Annual profit is 24m, taxed at 25%, and 60% of after-tax profit is sent abroad. The clean-up of river damage is estimated at 3.0m, paid by the host government. Evaluate the impact of Darrowmere Mining on Casterland. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Internal growth uses the business's own resources, such as new products or outlets [1]. External growth involves another business, such as an acquisition or a joint venture [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "distinguish" answer tends to earn credit when both sides are covered and contrasted, not when one is simply defined.

**2.** Any three, each outlined: keep control and personal service [1]; serve a niche too small for large firms [1]; avoid debt, risk or diseconomies of scale [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" wants a short statement of each point, so three distinct reasons beat one reason developed at length.

**3.** Internal, for example purchasing: bulk orders win supplier discounts [1], which lowers cost per unit [1]. External, for example a skilled labor pool in an industrial cluster [1], which cuts recruitment and training cost for every firm in the area [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" calls for a cause and a consequence, and answers lose credit when the type is named without saying why unit cost falls.

**4. (a)** 85,000 / 10,000 = **8.50** [1]; 150,000 / 25,000 = **6.00** [1]; 288,000 / 40,000 = **7.20** [1].
**(b)** Between 25,000 and 40,000 bottles [1].
**(c)** Coordination across a larger operation becomes harder [1], so mistakes and waste raise cost per bottle [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Showing the division for each output gives a marker something to credit even if one value slips.

**5. (a)** 6 x 280,000 x 7% [1] = **117,600** [1].
**(b)** Gross profit: 280,000 x 45% = 126,000 [1]; royalty: 280,000 x 7% = 19,600 [1]; profit: 126,000 - 19,600 - 95,000 = **11,400** [1].
**(c)** The franchisee pays the royalty whatever the profit [1], so a weak year leaves very little for the owner [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Written working for a "calculate" question matters: with only an answer, a slip leaves nothing for a marker to reward.

**6. (a)** Combined share 18% + 7% = **25%** [1]; sales revenue of Zandrel is 250 x 18% = 45.0m and of Torrowick 250 x 7% = 17.5m [1]; combined **62.5m** [1].
**(b)** A takeover gains control of a company, which may be against the board's wishes [1]; a merger is an agreed combination of two firms into one new business [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the definitions are the standard forms if the stimulus gives none, and keep the contrast explicit.

**7.** A joint venture creates a new, separate business [1] that the partners own together and share risk and profit from [1]. A strategic alliance is an agreement to cooperate in which the firms stay separate and form no new entity [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The deciding contrast is whether a new entity is formed, so state it directly.

**8.** Jobs and wages: workers gain income [1], and spending supports local shops [1]. Tax revenue: the government can fund services [1] from profits earned in the country [1]. (Other valid benefits include skills transfer and technology.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers often gain from linking each benefit to the host country by name rather than to "the economy" in general.

**9. (a)** Tax: 40m x 20% = 8m [1]; after-tax profit 32m [1]; sent abroad 32m x 75% = **24m** [1].
**(b)** Of the 40m profit, 24m leaves the host country [1], while 8m tax and 8m retained stay, so the benefit is real but smaller than headline profit suggests [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Comment" asks for a judgement based on the calculated result, so refer to your figures in the sentence.

**10.** Indicative content, one mark per developed point, up to 8.
- Takeover raises sales revenue to 21.0m, growth of 40% at once [1].
- Organic growth at 5% gives 15.75m after a year; reaching 21.0m takes about 6.9 years [1].
- Cost savings of 0.6m a year; price of 9.0m repaid in 15 years [1], which is slow [1].
- Takeover risks: overpaying, culture clash, staff losses [1].
- Organic growth is lower risk and preserves culture, but is slower and may lose market share to rivals [1].
- Takeover brings a ready-made customer base and larger scale [1].
- Judgement with criterion, for example: choose organic growth unless speed is vital, because a 15-year recovery is long and the extra revenue may not arrive [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer tends to score higher when it weighs both methods using the stimulus numbers and ends with a justified choice.

**11.** Indicative content, up to 9.
- Wages: 900 x 8,000 = 7.2m [1] gives local income and spending [1].
- Tax: 24m x 25% = 6.0m [1]; after-tax profit 18.0m [1].
- Repatriated: 18.0m x 60% = 10.8m leaves; 7.2m is reinvested [1].
- Clean-up cost of 3.0m falls on the host government, which uses up half of the tax paid [1].
- Long-term effects: skills and supplier links versus job loss when the mine closes [1].
- Both sides weighed, with 20.4m staying in Casterland against 10.8m leaving [1].
- Judgement with criterion, for example: the impact is net positive financially, but depends on whether the damage can be repaired [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculations are most useful in an "evaluate" answer when each figure is tied to a party affected, such as workers or the government.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an economy of scale without saying why unit cost falls.
- Stating a number without showing how it was found.
- Mixing up a joint venture with a strategic alliance.
- Discussing franchising from the franchisor's side only.
- Writing a list for "evaluate" with no judgement or criterion.
- Using only positive or only negative effects for an MNC.
- Ignoring figures in the stem, or quoting them without saying what they show.
- Treating repatriated profit as a loss to the host country in full, when wages and tax remain.
