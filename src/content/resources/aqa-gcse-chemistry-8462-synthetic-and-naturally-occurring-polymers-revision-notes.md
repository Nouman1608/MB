## Core facts

- **Monomers** are small molecules that join to make a **polymer**, a very large molecule.
- **Addition polymers** are made from monomers with **C=C**. The double bond opens.
- Addition: **no other molecule** is formed, so the **repeating unit has the same atoms as the monomer**.
- Name: poly(monomer), for example **poly(ethene)**, **poly(propene)**.
- **Condensation polymers** (Higher tier only) lose a **small molecule**, usually **water**.
- Natural polymers: **DNA, proteins, starch, cellulose**.

## Addition polymers

| Monomer | Polymer | Repeating unit |
|---|---|---|
| Ethene, CH₂=CH₂ | Poly(ethene) | –CH₂–CH₂– |
| Propene, CH₃CH=CH₂ | Poly(propene) | –CH(CH₃)–CH₂– |
| Chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl | Poly(chloroethene) | –CHCl–CH₂– |

Writing the equation: **n CH₂=CH₂ → ( –CH₂–CH₂– )ₙ**

Drawing checklist:

1. Draw the monomer with C=C.
2. Change C=C to **C–C**.
3. Show **bonds extending** out of the ends of the repeating unit.
4. Put the unit in **brackets** with **n** at the bottom right.

Finding the monomer: take the repeating unit, **put C=C back**, remove the outer bonds, then name it.

## Condensation polymerisation (Higher tier only)

- Monomers have **two functional groups** each.
- Simplest case: **two different monomers**, each with **two of the same group**.
- Example: **ethanediol** (two –OH) + **hexanedioic acid** (two –COOH) → **polyester** + water.
- Each **ester link** formed gives off **one water molecule**.
- The repeating unit contains **both monomers**.

| | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer feature | C=C | Two functional groups |
| Small molecule lost | No | Yes (water) |
| Number of different monomers | Usually one | Usually two |
| Example | Poly(ethene) | Polyester |

## Amino acids and polypeptides (Higher tier only)

- An amino acid has **–NH₂** and **–COOH** in the same molecule.
- Glycine: **H₂N–CH₂–COOH**.
- Amino acids join by **condensation polymerisation**, losing water, to form **polypeptides**.
- **Peptide link: –CO–NH–**.
- Different amino acids in one chain make **proteins**.

n H₂N–CH₂–COOH → ( –NH–CH₂–CO– )ₙ + n H₂O

Mass check: two glycine molecules (75 each) lose one H₂O (18): 150 − 18 = **132**.

## Natural polymers

| Polymer | Monomer | Note |
|---|---|---|
| DNA | Nucleotides (four different) | Two polymer chains, double helix; carries genetic instructions |
| Proteins | Amino acids | Many different amino acid types |
| Starch | Sugars (glucose) | Energy store in plants |
| Cellulose | Sugars (glucose) | Structural material in plant cell walls |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Monomer | Small molecule that joins with others to make a polymer |
| Polymer | Very large molecule made from many repeating units |
| Repeating unit | The part of the chain that repeats |
| Addition polymerisation | Monomers with C=C join with no other product |
| Condensation polymerisation | Monomers join, losing small molecules |
| Nucleotide | Monomer of DNA |
| Peptide link | The –CO–NH– link in proteins |

## Exam traps

- **C=C left in the polymer.** The double bond opens, so the polymer has none.
- **No brackets or n.** A repeating unit needs both.
- **Wrong monomer.** Restore the double bond and keep the side groups.
- **No water in condensation.** A small molecule is always lost.
- **Mixed-up monomers.** Proteins are from amino acids, starch and cellulose from sugars, DNA from nucleotides.
- **Addition polymers losing atoms.** None are lost.
- **Saying DNA is one chain.** Most DNA molecules have two chains.

## Quick self-test

1. What is a monomer?
2. What feature must a monomer for addition polymerisation contain?
3. Name the polymer made from propene.
4. Why is the repeating unit of an addition polymer the same as the monomer in terms of atoms?
5. A polymer has repeating unit –CH₂–CHCl–. Name the monomer.
6. What are the monomers of DNA called?
7. Name the monomer of a protein.
8. Name the monomer of starch.
9. (Higher tier only) What small molecule is lost in forming a polyester?
10. (Higher tier only) What two functional groups does an amino acid have?
11. (Higher tier only) Calculate the relative formula mass of the molecule formed when two glycine molecules (relative formula mass 75) join.
12. (Higher tier only) Give one similarity and one difference between addition and condensation polymerisation.

### Answers

1. A small molecule that can join with many others to form a polymer.
2. A C=C double bond.
3. Poly(propene).
4. No other molecule is formed, so no atoms are lost.
5. Chloroethene.
6. Nucleotides.
7. Amino acids.
8. Sugars (glucose).
9. Water.
10. An amino group (–NH₂) and a carboxyl group (–COOH).
11. 2 × 75 − 18 = 132.
12. Similarity: monomers join to make a long-chain polymer. Difference: condensation loses a small molecule, but addition does not.

## Worked answers to model

**Draw the repeating unit of poly(propene).** Draw two carbon atoms joined by a single bond. One carbon has an H
and a CH₃ group, the other has two H atoms. Extend a bond from each end through square brackets and write n.

**Name the monomer for –CH₂–CH(CH₃)–.** Restore the double bond: CH₂=CH–CH₃, which is propene.

**Explain what is meant by condensation polymerisation (Higher tier only).** "Monomers with two functional groups
join together, and a small molecule such as water is lost each time two monomers join."

## Quick comparison: natural polymers

| Polymer | Monomer | Function |
|---|---|---|

| Protein | Amino acids | Enzymes, muscle, structure |
| Starch | Glucose | Energy store in plants |

## Common calculation

Number of water molecules lost when n amino acids join in a single chain: n − 1. For 50 glycine units,
**49** molecules of water are lost.
