These questions cover section 1.3, Mass and weight, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Take g = 9.8 N/kg on the Earth and g = 1.6 N/kg on the Moon unless a question gives another value.

> **Tier note:** questions 1 to 9 use Core content only (1.3 Core 1–4), and every candidate should do them. Questions 10 to 12, marked *(Extended)*, use Supplement content (1.3 Supplement 5), examined on Papers 2 and 4 only. The marks shown are indicative only.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** This question is about mass and weight.

**(a)** State what is meant by mass. **[1]**
**(b)** State what is meant by weight. **[1]**
**(c)** State the unit of each quantity. **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the weight on the Earth of a person of mass 65 kg. **[2]**

**3.** A bag of flour has a weight of 34.3 N on the Earth. Calculate its mass. **[2]**

**4.** Name an instrument that:

**(a)** measures weight directly **[1]**
**(b)** compares the mass of an object with standard masses. **[1]**

**5.** An apple has a mass of 250 g. Calculate its weight on the Earth. **[2]**

**6.** This question is about g.

**(a)** Define gravitational field strength. **[1]**
**(b)** The gravitational field strength near the Earth is 9.8 N/kg. State the acceleration of free fall near the Earth, with its unit. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** An astronaut has a mass of 80 kg.

**(a)** Calculate her weight on the Earth. **[2]**
**(b)** State her mass on the Moon. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate her weight on the Moon. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why she finds it easier to lift a box of rock samples on the Moon than on the Earth. **[1]**

**8.** A student uses a newton meter and a set of slotted masses to find g. The results are shown.

| Mass / g | 100 | 200 | 300 | 400 | 500 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weight / N | 0.98 | 1.95 | 2.95 | 3.90 | 4.90 |

**(a)** Describe how the student obtained these results. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how a graph of these results can be used to find g. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the last pair of readings to calculate g. **[1]**

**9.** An object of mass 1.2 kg is weighed on the Earth and then on the Moon.

**(a)** On the Earth, a beam balance balances when 1.2 kg of standard masses are in the other pan. State the standard masses needed on the Moon. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain your answer to (a). **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the reading on a newton meter holding the object (i) on the Earth and (ii) on the Moon. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A space probe of mass 350 kg visits three planets. The gravitational field strengths at their surfaces are: planet P 3.7 N/kg, planet Q 8.9 N/kg, planet R 24.8 N/kg.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the probe on planet R. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why planet R has the strongest field at its surface. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe weight in terms of a gravitational field. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the probe's weight changes between planets but its mass does not. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* On a moon with no atmosphere and g = 1.6 N/kg, an astronaut drops a hammer of mass 1.3 kg and a feather of mass 0.030 kg from the same height at the same time.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of each object. **[2]**
**(b)** State the acceleration of each object. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the objects have the same acceleration although their weights are very different. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A satellite of mass 500 kg has a weight of 4900 N on the launch pad. In orbit, the Earth's gravitational force on it is 4350 N.

**(a)** Calculate g at the launch pad. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gravitational field strength at the height of the orbit. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the field strength is smaller in orbit. **[1]**
**(d)** A student says the satellite is "weightless, so there is no gravity in orbit". Comment on this statement. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A measure of the quantity of matter in an object (at rest relative to the observer) [1]
**(b)** A gravitational force on an object that has mass [1]
**(c)** Mass: kg; weight: N (both needed) [1]

**2.** W = mg = 65 × 9.8 [1] = **637 N** (640 N) [1]

**3.** m = W/g = 34.3 ÷ 9.8 [1] = **3.5 kg** [1]

**4. (a)** A newton meter (spring balance) [1]
**(b)** A beam balance [1]

**5.** 250 g = 0.250 kg [1]; W = 0.250 × 9.8 = **2.45 N** (2.5 N) [1]
*Examiner insight:* 250 × 9.8 = 2450 N loses the conversion mark and gives an absurd weight for an apple.

**6. (a)** Force per unit mass [1]
**(b)** **9.8 m/s²** [1]

**7. (a)** 80 × 9.8 [1] = **784 N** [1]
**(b)** **80 kg** [1]
**(c)** 80 × 1.6 [1] = **128 N** [1]
**(d)** The box's weight is smaller on the Moon (g is smaller), so a smaller force is needed to lift it [1].

**8. (a)** Hang the newton meter from a clamp and check it reads zero with no load [1]; hang each mass in turn and read the weight with the eye level with the pointer [1]; convert each mass into kilograms [1].
**(b)** Plot weight (y-axis) against mass in kg (x-axis) and draw the straight line of best fit through the origin [1]; the gradient of the line is g [1].
**(c)** g = 4.90 ÷ 0.500 = **9.8 N/kg** [1]

**9. (a)** **1.2 kg** (the same) [1]
**(b)** Both pans are in the same gravitational field [1]; both weights are reduced by the same factor, so they still balance when the masses are equal [1].
**(c)** (i) 1.2 × 9.8 = **11.8 N** (11.76 N) [1]; (ii) 1.2 × 1.6 = **1.9 N** (1.92 N) [1]

**10. (a)** W = 350 × 24.8 [1] = **8680 N** (8700 N) [1]
**(b)** It has the greatest mass [1].
**(c)** Weight is the effect of a gravitational field on a mass [1].
**(d)** Mass is the quantity of matter, which does not depend on location [1]; weight = mg, and g is different at each planet, so the force on the same mass is different [1].

**11. (a)** Hammer: 1.3 × 1.6 = **2.1 N** (2.08 N); feather: 0.030 × 1.6 = **0.048 N** [1 each]
**(b)** **1.6 m/s²** for both [1]
**(c)** The field exerts the same force per kilogram (1.6 N/kg) on every mass [1]; with no air resistance, weight is the only force, so acceleration = weight ÷ mass = g for both [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Because there is no air" is only half the answer; the same force per unit mass is the key point.

**12. (a)** 4900 ÷ 500 = **9.8 N/kg** [1]
**(b)** g = 4350 ÷ 500 [1] = **8.7 N/kg** [1]
**(c)** The field strength decreases with distance from the Earth (the satellite is further from the Earth's centre) [1].
**(d)** Wrong: the Earth's field still acts and the satellite still has weight (4350 N) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving weight in kg or mass in N.
- Using grams in W = mg.
- Saying mass changes when an object moves to the Moon.
- Saying a beam balance gives a different reading on the Moon.
- *(Extended)* Explaining equal acceleration only by "no air resistance", without the equal force per kilogram.
