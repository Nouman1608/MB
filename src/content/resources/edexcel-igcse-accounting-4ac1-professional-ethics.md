## What section 1.4 asks you to do

| Point | Specification wording | What it means in an answer |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4 (a) | Describe the principles of professional ethics | Say clearly what each principle requires |
| 1.4 (b) | Apply the principles of professional ethics to accounting roles and functions | Use a principle on a given situation and say what the accountant should do |
| 1.4 (c) | Explain the concept of public interest and how it is applied to accounting roles and functions | Say who the public is, why they rely on accountants and how that shapes a decision |

The specification does not name the principles. The five fundamental principles in the international code of ethics for professional accountants are the widely used set, and they are the ones this page uses.

## The five principles in working detail

### Integrity

Integrity means being honest and straightforward in all professional and business relationships. An accountant must not knowingly be associated with a record, statement or report that is false, that leaves out something needed to make it accurate, or that is written to mislead. The test is simple: would a reader of this figure be deceived? Integrity is tested whenever someone asks for an entry to be backdated, invented, hidden or "tidied" without evidence.

### Objectivity

Objectivity means not letting bias, a conflict of interest or the undue influence of others override professional judgement. A conflict of interest exists when the accountant, or someone close to them, gains from the decision being made. Gifts, family links with a supplier or customer, pressure from a manager, and bonuses tied to reported profit are all common threats. The usual safeguard is to disclose the conflict and let someone independent take the decision.

### Professional competence and due care

This principle has two halves. Competence means having, and keeping up to date, the knowledge and skill the work needs. Due care means working carefully, thoroughly and on time, in line with the relevant standards. An accountant who takes on work they do not understand, or who rushes a task and skips the checks, breaks this principle even if nobody intended harm. The right response to an unfamiliar task is to say so and to seek training, supervision or help.

### Confidentiality

Confidentiality means not disclosing information gained through professional work without proper authority, and not using it for personal advantage or for the advantage of anyone else. It covers payroll details, customer balances, supplier prices, profit figures and future plans. It continues after the accountant leaves the job. Confidentiality is not absolute: disclosure can be right where the law requires it, or where the client or employer gives permission.

### Professional behaviour

Professional behaviour means complying with laws and regulations and avoiding any action that discredits the profession. Examples of breaches are exaggerating qualifications, criticising another accountant's work without grounds, and posting comments about clients online.

## A method for "apply" questions

1. **Find the fact that causes the problem.** Quote or paraphrase the part of the scenario that creates the risk.
2. **Name the principle.** Choose the one most clearly at risk. Add a second only if the facts support it.
3. **Explain why it is at risk.** Link the fact to what the principle requires and say who could be misled or harmed.
4. **State the action.** Say what the accountant should do: refuse, disclose, correct, seek help or pass the decision to someone independent.

### Worked example 1: a conflict of interest

Priya Osei is the purchase ledger clerk at Fernhill Plumbing. She is asked to choose a new supplier of pipe fittings. One of the three suppliers quoting is owned by her brother. His quote is 6,250 for the first order; the other two quotes are 5,980 and 6,400.

*Annotated answer.* The principle at risk is objectivity (step 2) because Priya's brother would gain if she chose his firm (step 1), so her judgement could be influenced by a family interest rather than by price and quality (step 3). Her brother's quote is 270 higher than the cheapest (6,250 − 5,980), so choosing it without a good reason would cost the business money. She should tell her manager about the relationship and ask for someone else to make the decision (step 4).

*Why it scores well:* every sentence is tied to the facts, the figure shows the effect, and the action is specific. "She should be ethical" would earn nothing.

### Worked example 2: competence and due care

Callum Reyes, a bookkeeper at Westgate Signs, is asked to prepare the year's depreciation schedule. He has only ever used straight line, but the business uses reducing balance. He is told the schedule must be ready by the next morning and decides to apply straight line "because it is close enough".

*Annotated answer.* Professional competence and due care is broken. Callum lacks the knowledge for the task and is not working carefully, so the depreciation charge, profit and the carrying value of non-current assets could all be wrong. The owner and anyone who reads the statements would rely on incorrect figures. He should explain that he needs guidance on the reducing balance method, ask for help or more time, and not submit work he knows is unreliable.

## The public interest

The public interest is the collective wellbeing of the community of people and organisations that a professional accountant serves. In accounting, that means everyone who relies on financial information without having prepared it: lenders, investors, suppliers, customers, employees, tax authorities and the wider public. An accountant's responsibility is therefore not only to meet the needs of the employer or client who pays them.

How it applies to roles and functions:

| Role or function | How the public interest applies |
|---|---|
| Bookkeeper recording transactions | Records must be complete and accurate because others will rely on statements built from them |
| Payroll clerk | Tax and other deductions from wages must be calculated correctly and passed to the authority, not kept by the business |
| Accountant preparing financial statements | Statements must not mislead lenders, suppliers or investors who use them to make decisions |
| Auditor | Reports honestly on the statements, because outsiders rely on that report |
| Adviser | Must not help a client hide income from the tax authority |

### Worked example 3: when the employer and the public disagree

Rosa Lindqvist prepares the accounts of Harlow Market Stores. The owner asks her to leave 15,000 of cash sales out of the records so that taxable profit is lower. Who is affected, and what should Rosa do?

*Annotated answer.* Leaving the sales out breaks integrity because the records would be false. It also goes against the public interest: the tax authority would collect less than the law requires, and other taxpayers bear the cost. Anyone lending to the business would also see revenue understated by 15,000. Rosa should refuse, record all sales and explain to the owner why. Her duty to the public outweighs the owner's wish to pay less tax.

## Exam technique: command words

- **State** or **identify**: give the principle by name, with no explanation.
- **Describe**: say what the principle requires, in one or two clear sentences. Describing integrity as "being honest" alone is thin; add "and not being associated with misleading information".
- **Explain**: give the point and the reason or consequence. Use "because" or "so".
- **Apply**: use the scenario's facts. An answer that could be pasted into any question has not applied anything.
- **Evaluate** or **assess**, where used: weigh both sides, such as loyalty to an employer against the public interest, and finish with a justified conclusion.

## Common errors

- Naming a principle that is not tested by the facts, such as confidentiality when nothing is disclosed.
- Mixing up objectivity and integrity. Integrity is about whether the record is true; objectivity is about whether judgement is free from bias.
- Treating confidentiality as absolute, so that an accountant would hide wrongdoing to "keep a secret".
- Defining the public interest as the employer's interest, or as "the interest of the public in the news".
- Giving no action, or a vague one such as "do the right thing".
- Writing about ethics in general without linking to an accounting role.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the principles of professional ethics for Edexcel IGCSE Accounting?**
The 4AC1 specification does not list them, so answers usually use the five principles of the international code: integrity, objectivity, professional competence and due care, confidentiality and professional behaviour.

**What is the difference between integrity and objectivity?**
Integrity is about honesty: not being linked with false or misleading information. Objectivity is about judgement: not letting bias, a conflict of interest or pressure from others decide the outcome.

**Can an accountant ever disclose confidential information?**
Yes, where the law requires it or where the client or employer gives permission. Confidentiality protects legitimate private information; it does not require an accountant to help hide wrongdoing.

**How is the public interest applied to accounting roles?**
Each role produces or checks information that outsiders rely on, so it must be accurate and honest. When an employer's wishes would mislead lenders, investors or the tax authority, the accountant should not follow them.
