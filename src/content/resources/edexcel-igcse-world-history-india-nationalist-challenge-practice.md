## Question 1 — 1919 and the rise of Gandhi

**Extract A** (invented for this exercise).

> "Within a single year Britain had offered India a little power with one hand and taken away its
> liberties with the other. Amritsar settled the argument: for millions, the promise of 1917 now
> looked like a lie."

**1(a)** What impression does the author give about British policy in India in 1919? You must use
Extract A to explain your answer. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** effects of the Amritsar Massacre (1919). **[8]**

**1(c)** "Gandhi's methods were the main reason for the growth of Indian nationalism in the years
1919–27." How far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Non-Cooperation Movement
- the Rowlatt Acts

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 2 — key developments, 1927–42

**Extract B** (invented for this exercise).

> "The walk to Dandi achieved nothing in law and almost everything in spirit. The salt tax

**2(a)** What impression does the author give about the Salt March? You must use Extract B to

**2(b)** Explain **two** effects of the Government of India Act 1935. **[8]**

**2(c)** "The Second World War was the main reason the Muslim League grew stronger in the years

You may use the following in your answer:
- the 1937 elections
- the Quit India campaign

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Question 3 — independence and partition, 1945–47

**3(b)** Explain **two** effects of Direct Action Day (16 August 1946). **[8]**

**3(c)** "Mountbatten was mainly responsible for the partition of India in 1947." How far do you

You may use the following in your answer:

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

The author presents British policy as **contradictory and ultimately discredited**. The image of
offering power "with one hand" and taking liberties "with the other" points to the 1919 Act and
the Rowlatt Acts pulling in opposite directions. "Settled the argument" and "looked like a lie"
suggest Amritsar ended Indian trust in British promises. Strong answers explain the effect of
these word choices; brief own knowledge (dyarchy, Rowlatt) can support the reading.

### 1(b) [8]

- **Moderates turned against British rule:** Gandhi, previously willing to cooperate, launched the
  Non-Cooperation Movement in 1920; Tagore renounced his knighthood.
- **Division between Britain and India widened:** the Hunter Committee censured Dyer, but support for
  him in Britain convinced many Indians that British justice did not apply to them.

### 1(c) [16]

**Agree:** satyagraha and non-cooperation (1920–22) turned Congress into a mass movement reaching
peasants; boycotts of cloth and *swadeshi* gave ordinary people a way to take part; the alliance
with the Khilafat campaign briefly united Hindus and Muslims.

**Other factors:** the First World War's sacrifices and raised expectations; the Rowlatt Acts and
Amritsar; the limits of the 1919 Act; economic hardship. Gandhi's methods also had limits —
non-cooperation ended after Chauri Chaura, and Hindu–Muslim unity collapsed in the mid-1920s.

**Judgement:** British actions created the anger; Gandhi's methods gave it mass form.

### 2(a) [6]

The author suggests the Salt March was a **symbolic triumph rather than a practical one**. The
balanced phrasing — "nothing in law" against "almost everything in spirit" — sets limited results
against huge moral impact; "in the eyes of the world" points to international publicity.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Provincial self-government:** elected Indian ministries controlled the provinces, leading to the
  1937 elections and Congress governments in most provinces.
- **Unresolved central question:** the proposed federation depended on the princes, who did not
  join, so Britain kept control at the centre, leaving nationalists dissatisfied.

### 2(c) [16]

**Agree:** Congress ministries resigned in 1939 while the League cooperated with the British; the
Lahore Resolution (1940) gave the League a clear goal; Congress leaders were imprisoned after Quit
India (1942), leaving the League free to organise.

**Other factors:** the League's poor 1937 result and Congress's refusal to share power in provinces,
which pushed Muslims towards the League; Jinnah's leadership and the "Day of Deliverance" in 1939;
fears of Hindu domination.

**Judgement:** the war accelerated a shift that began with the experience of Congress rule after
1937.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Communal violence spread:** the Great Calcutta Killings were followed by violence in Noakhali
  and Bihar, making a united India seem impossible.
- **Partition became likely:** British and Congress leaders increasingly accepted that the League's
  demand for Pakistan could not be ignored, which shaped Mountbatten's plan in 1947.

### 3(c) [16]

**Agree:** Mountbatten concluded quickly that partition was the only solution, persuaded Nehru and
Jinnah to accept the 3 June 1947 plan and brought independence forward to August 1947, leaving
little time to draw boundaries or prevent violence.

**Disagree:** the demand for Pakistan dated from 1940 and was confirmed by the League's success in
the 1945–46 elections; the Cabinet Mission had already failed; Direct Action and communal violence
made unity unworkable; Attlee had set the deadline of June 1948; long-term causes include separate
electorates and the 1937 ministries.

**Judgement:** Mountbatten decided the timing and method, not the fact, of partition.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), quoting without explaining.
- In (b), giving causes of the event instead of its effects.
- In (c), discussing only the stated factor, or listing factors without weighing them.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is each Paper 1 depth study question structured?**
Each 4HI1 Paper 1 question has three parts: (a) what impression an extract gives (6 marks), (b) explain two effects (8 marks) and (c) a 'how far do you agree' essay (16 marks), with bullet prompts and your own knowledge.

**Which events do these questions cover?**
Amritsar and the rise of Gandhi in 1919, the Salt March and other developments of 1927–42, and independence and partition in 1945–47.

**What is a common part (b) mistake?**
Giving the causes of an event when the question asks for its effects.
