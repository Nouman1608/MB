Revision notes for the focused study **Political and economic transitions** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Two examples, both required: **the Meiji Restoration (1853 to 1894)** and **the Russian Federation (1985 to 1999)**. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-political-economic-transitions/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-political-economic-transitions-practice/).

## Paper 1 in one box

| Question | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| First | Explain how the content of sources A and B can be used to answer the inquiry question | 6 |
| Second | Analyse how the context of source C influences how it can be used | 6 |
| Third | Examine how the perspectives in all the sources can be used | 12 |

Total 24 marks in 1 hour 15 minutes. You use the grounding content to understand the sources.

## The three inquiry questions

1. What caused the transition to take place?
2. How was the transition achieved?
3. What challenges did society face after the transition?

## The Meiji Restoration (1853 to 1894)

**1. Causes**

- Decline of the Tokugawa Shogunate: costly and poor samurai, peasant unrest, merchants with wealth but no power.
- Stagnation and demands for rapid modernisation after the arrival of Perry (1853); unequal treaties (1858) seen as national humiliation.
- Influence of the United States and European countries, as models and as threats.

**2. Achievement**

- Roles of the Meiji oligarchy (Ōkubo, Kido, Itō, Yamagata) and Emperor Mutsuhito, whose name gave legitimacy.
- Land reform (1873 land tax in cash), industrialisation (railways, telegraph, model factories) and trade.
- The Constitution of 1889: a Diet, but wide imperial powers and a limited vote.

**3. Challenges**

- Peasant unrest over taxes and conscription.
- The Satsuma Rebellion (1877) led by Saigō Takamori, defeated by the conscript army.
- Foreign relations and imperialism: treaty revision, Korea (1876), war with China (1894 to 1895).

## The Russian Federation (1985 to 1999)

**1. Causes**

- Role of the Communist Party: monopoly of power, resistance to change.
- Stagnation and failed economic reform: slow growth, shortages, military cost; reforms under Gorbachev (from 1985) did not deliver.
- Challenges to communism in Eastern Europe: 1989 revolutions, nationalism in the republics.

**2. Achievement**

- Roles of Gorbachev (glasnost, perestroika, resignation on 25 December 1991) and Yeltsin (president of Russia, 1991 to 1999).
- Privatisation (vouchers, then loans for shares), market liberalisation (price freeing, January 1992) and tax reforms.
- The Constitution of 1993: a strong presidency, approved by referendum.

**3. Challenges**

- The coup attempt of August 1991 and the constitutional crisis of September and October 1993.
- Labour strikes, protests and organised crime.
- The First Chechen War (1994 to 1996): ended by ceasefire and Russian withdrawal.

## Comparing the two examples

| Point | Meiji Japan | Russia |
|---|---|---|
| Starting point | Feudal-style shogunate under foreign pressure | Centrally planned economy and one-party state |
| Who led | Small oligarchy in the emperor's name | Gorbachev, then Yeltsin |
| Economic route | State-led industrialisation, then private sale | Rapid liberalisation and privatisation |
| Political outcome | Limited constitutional monarchy (1889) | Strong presidential republic (1993) |
| Main challenge | Rebellion and unrest; expansion abroad | Economic hardship, crime, war in Chechnya |
| Pace | Over decades | Within a few years |

## Skills checklist

**Question 1.** Use both sources. Pick specific points from each and link them to the inquiry question. A single source caps the mark at 3.

**Question 2.** Use the origin, date, author, audience and purpose of source C and link each to its use.

**Question 3.** Give each source's perspective, compare them and combine them. All three sources are needed for the top band. Two sources cap the mark at 9.

## Worked example: a question 1 answer

Inquiry question: What challenges did society face after the transition?

Source A (constructed for practice): a Japanese newspaper of 1873 reports that farmers in one province have destroyed the offices that collect the new land tax and resisted conscription. Source B (constructed for practice): a Russian miners' strike committee statement of the 1990s says wages have not been paid for months.

"Source A shows that the new land tax and conscription led to protest, as farmers attacked tax offices, which shows that the reforms created a burden for the peasantry and that the government had to deal with unrest. Source B shows that unpaid wages led to a strike by miners, which indicates that economic reform brought hardship for workers and that labour protest was a challenge after the transition. Both sources show that ordinary people paid costs of state-led change, which is one answer to the question."

## Linking to concepts

- **Cause and consequence:** actors and conditions in each transition.
- **Continuity and change:** fast political change with continuing elites.
- **Perspectives:** reformers, the old elite, peasants, workers, foreign observers.
- **Significance:** why these cases matter for modernisation and the end of the Cold War.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing the 1991 coup attempt with the 1993 crisis.
- Saying the Meiji reforms restored direct imperial rule.
- Writing a narrative of events instead of using sources.
- Giving context without linking it to use.

## Markbands at a glance

| Question | Lower bands | Top band |
|---|---|---|
| First (6 marks) | Describes the sources, with little explanation of how the content answers the inquiry question | Explains how a range of specific content from both sources can be used, with specific and developed connections |
| Second (6 marks) | Describes the context with minimal analysis | Analyses how the context influences how the source can be used, supported by relevant references to the context |
| Third (12 marks) | Outlines perspectives briefly, with minimal links to the question | Insightful understanding of the perspectives of all the sources, effectively examining similarities and differences, with relevant references |

A useful routine is to spend about 10 minutes on the first question, 10 on the second and 20 to 25 on the third, leaving a little time to check that every answer refers back to the inquiry question.

## Self-test

1. Who arrived in Edo Bay in 1853?
2. Name two Meiji oligarchs.
3. What happened in 1877?
4. What did the 1889 constitution create?
5. What was glasnost?
6. When was the USSR dissolved?
7. What were the two main events of 1991 and 1993 in Moscow?
8. How did the First Chechen War end?

**Answers.**

1. Commodore Matthew Perry and an American squadron.
2. Any two: Ōkubo Toshimichi, Kido Takayoshi, Itō Hirobumi, Yamagata Aritomo.
3. The Satsuma Rebellion, led by Saigō Takamori, was defeated.
4. A Diet with an elected lower house, under wide imperial powers.
5. Openness: Gorbachev's policy of greater freedom of speech and information.
6. December 1991 (Gorbachev resigned on 25 December).
7. The August 1991 coup attempt and the September to October 1993 constitutional crisis.
8. With a ceasefire and Russian withdrawal in 1996.
