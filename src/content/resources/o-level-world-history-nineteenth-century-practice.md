## Section A

**1.** State the aims of the Congress of Vienna (1815). **[3]**

**2.** Define nationalism and liberalism as nineteenth-century political forces. **[2]**

## Section B

**3.** Explain how the settlement of 1815 attempted to prevent future French aggression, giving **three** measures. **[6]**

**4.** Explain **three** obstacles to Italian unification before 1859. **[9]**

**5.** Explain the role of Cavour and of Garibaldi in the unification of Italy, and explain why Cavour's diplomacy was as important as Garibaldi's campaigns. **[10]**

**6.** Explain the "Eastern Question" and explain **two** reasons why the great powers were concerned with the decline of the Ottoman Empire. **[8]**

**7.** Explain **three** motives for European imperialism in Africa in the later nineteenth century. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** To **restore legitimate rulers deposed by Napoleon** [1]; to **contain France and prevent any future bid for domination**, partly by strengthening the states on her borders [1]; to establish a **balance of power** among the great powers so that no one state could dominate Europe [1].

**2. Nationalism** — the belief that people sharing a **common language, culture and history should govern themselves in their own state** [1]. **Liberalism** — the demand for **constitutional government, the rule of law, and civil freedoms such as the freedom of the press**, usually with a limited franchise [1].

**3.** France was **surrounded by strengthened neighbours**: the **Netherlands was enlarged by the addition of Belgium** [1] [1]; **Prussia was given the Rhineland**, placing a major power on France's eastern frontier [1] [1]; **Piedmont was strengthened by Genoa** in the south [1]. The powers also agreed the **Quadruple Alliance and the Congress System** to meet regularly and act together against renewed French aggression [1].

**4.** Any three, 3 marks each: **Austrian domination** — Austria held Lombardy and Venetia directly and controlled the rulers of the central duchies, and **Metternich was determined to suppress any nationalist movement** [1] [1] [1]. **Division among the nationalists** — Mazzini wanted a **united republic**, Gioberti a **federation under the Pope**, and others a **kingdom under Piedmont**, so they could not agree on a goal [1] [1] [1]. **The Papacy** — the Pope ruled the Papal States across the centre of Italy and **would not surrender temporal power**, and Catholic France would defend him [1] [1] [1]. **Lack of popular support and of a common language** — most Italians were illiterate peasants with little interest in unification, and few spoke standard Italian [1] [1] [1].

**5. Cavour** was Prime Minister of Piedmont and pursued unification through **diplomacy and economic modernisation** [1]. He **built up Piedmont's army, railways and finances** and sent troops to the Crimean War to earn a hearing at the peace conference [1]. At **Plombières (1858)** he secured a **secret alliance with Napoleon III**, promising Nice and Savoy in return for French help against Austria [1] [1]. **Garibaldi** led the **Expedition of the Thousand** in 1860, conquering Sicily and then Naples, and **handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel** rather than declaring a republic [1] [1].
Cavour's diplomacy was as important because **Piedmont alone could never have defeated Austria** — French military support was indispensable [1] [1]; and because Cavour **managed the great powers so that they did not intervene** as the kingdom expanded, and moved Piedmontese troops south to ensure Garibaldi did not attack Rome and provoke France [1] [1].

**6.** The **Eastern Question** was the problem of **what would happen to the territories of the declining Ottoman Empire**, and which powers would gain from its collapse [1] [1].
Reasons for concern, any two, 3 marks each: **Russia** sought **access to the Mediterranean through the Straits** and posed as the protector of Ottoman Christians, so Ottoman collapse would give her a decisive strategic gain [1] [1] [1]. **Britain** wanted to **prevent Russian expansion towards the Mediterranean and the route to India**, so she generally propped up the Ottoman Empire despite its misgovernment [1] [1] [1]. **Austria-Hungary** feared that **Balkan nationalism, encouraged by Ottoman retreat, would spread to her own Slav subjects** and break up the Habsburg Empire [1] [1] [1].

**7.** Any three, 2 marks each: **economic motives** — the search for **raw materials such as rubber, cotton and minerals, and for markets** for manufactured goods [1] [1]. **Strategic motives** — control of routes such as the **Suez Canal and the Cape**, and of coaling stations for naval power [1] [1]. **National prestige and rivalry** — colonies were a **mark of great-power status**, and each power seized territory to deny it to a rival, accelerating the scramble [1] [1]. **Ideological and religious motives** — the **"civilising mission" and missionary activity**, which offered a moral justification for conquest [1] [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the Vienna settlement without linking measures to containing France.
- Giving Garibaldi sole credit for Italian unification.
- Explaining the Eastern Question without the strategic interests of the powers.
- Listing imperial motives without explaining the mechanism.
