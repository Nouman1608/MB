These questions cover **3.2.5.1 General properties of transition metals** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: the incomplete d sub-level of Ti to Cu, the four characteristic properties, and the definitions of ligand, complex and co-ordination number. This is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the term *co-ordination number*. **[1]**

**2.** Define the term *ligand*. **[2]**

**3.** Write the electron configuration of:

**(a)** a titanium atom **[1]**

**(b)** an Fe³⁺ ion **[1]**

**(c)** a Cu⁺ ion. **[1]**

**4.** List the four characteristic properties of transition metals. **[2]**

**5.** Explain what is meant by a co-ordinate bond. **[1]**

**6.** Identify the donor atom in an ammonia ligand and in a water ligand. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Give the co-ordination number of the metal and its oxidation state in each complex.

**(a)** [V(H₂O)₆]³⁺ **[2]**

**(b)** [CuCl₄]²⁻ **[2]**

**(c)** [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ **[2]**

**8.** The salt Na₂[CoCl₄] contains a complex ion.

**(a)** State the charge on the complex ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the oxidation state of cobalt. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the electron configuration of the cobalt ion. **[1]**

**9.** Explain why copper is classed as a transition metal but zinc is not. **[3]**

**10.** Titanium(IV) oxide, TiO₂, is white, but titanium(III) compounds are coloured. Use electron configurations to explain this difference. **[3]**

**11.** Iron(III) forms a complex with four water ligands and two chloride ligands. Write the formula of the complex, including its charge, and state its co-ordination number. **[2]**

**12.** Manganese shows variable oxidation state. Deduce the oxidation state of manganese in MnO₄⁻, MnO₂ and Mn²⁺. **[3]**

**13.** Chromium has the atomic configuration [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.

**(a)** Write the configuration of the Cr³⁺ ion. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain whether Cr³⁺ would be expected to show transition metal properties. **[1]**

**(c)** [Cr(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is a complex. Identify the ligand, the donor atom and the co-ordination number. **[2]**

**14.** A student writes: "In [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ the co-ordination number is 2, because there are two different ligands, and cobalt is +2 because the complex is 2+." Identify the two errors and give the correct values. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion [1]

**2.** A molecule or ion that donates a pair of electrons [1]; to form a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal ion [1]

**3. (a)** **[Ar] 3d² 4s²** (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d² 4s²) [1]

**(b)** **[Ar] 3d⁵** [1]

**(c)** **[Ar] 3d¹⁰** [1]

*Examiner insight:* For Fe³⁺, both 4s electrons go first, then one 3d electron. [Ar] 3d³ 4s² is a common wrong answer.

**4.** Complex formation and formation of coloured ions [1]; variable oxidation state and catalytic activity [1]

**5.** A covalent bond in which both electrons in the shared pair come from the same atom [1]

**6.** Nitrogen in NH₃ and oxygen in H₂O [1]

**7. (a)** Co-ordination number **6** [1]; vanadium **+3** [1]

**(b)** Co-ordination number **4** [1]; copper **+2** [1]

**(c)** Co-ordination number **6** [1]; x + 4(0) + 2(−1) = +1, so cobalt is **+3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* In (c) there are six ligands of two kinds. Count every co-ordinate bond, then subtract the ligand charges from the overall charge.

**8. (a)** **2−** (two Na⁺ ions balance it) [1]

**(b)** x + 4(−1) = −2, so cobalt is **+2** [1]

**(c)** Co²⁺: **[Ar] 3d⁷** [1]

**9.** Transition metal characteristics arise from an incomplete d sub-level in the atom or an ion [1]; copper forms Cu²⁺, [Ar] 3d⁹, which has an incomplete d sub-level [1]; zinc forms only Zn²⁺, [Ar] 3d¹⁰, and the atom is also 3d¹⁰, so zinc never has an incomplete d sub-level [1]

*Examiner insight:* The definition says "atoms or ions". Quote the Cu²⁺ configuration; an answer based only on the copper atom argues the wrong way.

**10.** Ti⁴⁺ is [Ar] with no 3d electrons (3d⁰) [1]; Ti³⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹, an incomplete d sub-level [1]; with no d electrons there are none to absorb visible light and move to a higher level, so Ti(IV) compounds are white; the single d electron in Ti³⁺ can be excited by visible light, so its compounds are coloured [1]

*Examiner insight:* Ti⁴⁺ loses both 4s electrons and both 3d electrons. Showing the configurations of both ions is what earns the first two marks.

**11.** **[Fe(H₂O)₄Cl₂]⁺** (charge: +3 + 0 + 2(−1) = +1) [1]; co-ordination number **6** [1]

**12.** MnO₄⁻: x + 4(−2) = −1, so **+7** [1]; MnO₂: x + 2(−2) = 0, so **+4** [1]; Mn²⁺: **+2** [1]

**13. (a)** **[Ar] 3d³** (the 4s electron and two 3d electrons are lost) [1]

**(b)** Yes: 3d³ is an incomplete d sub-level, so Cr³⁺ forms coloured complexes and other transition metal properties are expected [1]

**(c)** Ligand **H₂O**, donor atom **oxygen** [1]; co-ordination number **6** [1]

**14.** Co-ordination number counts co-ordinate bonds, not types of ligand [1]; five NH₃ and one Cl⁻ give **6** [1]; the oxidation state must allow for ligand charges: x + 5(0) + (−1) = +2 [1]; so cobalt is **+3** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Both errors come from reading the formula too quickly. Write the charge sum out every time, even when it looks obvious.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Removing 3d electrons before 4s electrons.
- Using the copper atom (3d¹⁰) to argue that copper is not a transition metal.
- Forgetting counter-ions when working out the charge on a complex in a salt.
- Writing a ligand definition that leaves out the electron pair.
