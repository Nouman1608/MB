These questions cover topic 1.7 Urbanisation in Theme 1 (Population and settlement) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. All countries and settlements in the data questions are fictional and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Urbanisation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-geography-0460-urbanisation/) and the [Urbanisation revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-urbanisation-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term "urbanisation". **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "rural depopulation". **[1]**

**3.** Give one physical, one economic and one social factor that can cause people to leave rural areas. **[3]**

**4.** Give three characteristics of squatter settlements. **[3]**

**5.** Apart from rural-urban migration, state one other cause of rapid urban growth. **[1]**

**6.** Define the term "megacity". **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the percentage of the population living in urban areas in three fictional countries.

| Country | 1975 (%) | 2000 (%) | 2025 (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kessa | 18 | 31 | 52 |
| Lorne | 72 | 78 | 83 |
| Mavi | 9 | 14 | 21 |

**(a)** Calculate the change in Kessa's urban population percentage between 1975 and 2025. **[1]**
**(b)** Compare the urbanisation of the three countries between 1975 and 2025. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why the urban percentage in Lorne rose slowly. **[2]**

**8.** Read the fictional description.

> Riverbank is a settlement of about 30,000 people on the flood plain beside a river on the edge of a fast-growing city. Most homes are made of corrugated iron and plastic sheeting. There is one water tap for every 200 people and no sewers. The land is owned by the government.

Suggest the problems faced by the people living in Riverbank. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the effects of rapid urban growth on the natural environment. **[4]**

**10.** Describe and evaluate one strategy used to improve squatter settlements. **[5]**

**11.** For a named rapidly growing urban area in a developing country, explain why people have migrated to it and describe the impacts of its growth. **[7]**

**12.** "Developing rural areas is a better way to reduce the problems of rapid urban growth than improving cities." To what extent do you agree? **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** An increase in the proportion (percentage) of a country's population [1] living in urban areas [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Towns getting bigger" describes urban growth; urbanisation is about the share of the population that is urban.

**2.** A fall in the population of rural areas, as people (mainly young adults) move away [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Physical: drought, floods or soil erosion destroying crops [1]. Economic: low farm incomes or plots too small to support a family [1]. Social: a lack of schools, hospitals or electricity [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Built on land the residents do not own, often hazardous land such as steep slopes or flood plains [1]. Homes made of makeshift materials such as corrugated iron, wood or plastic [1]. Few services, such as shared water taps and no sewers [1]. Also credit very high density or risk of eviction. **[3]**

**5.** Natural increase in the city, because many migrants are young adults who have children there [1]. **[1]**

**6.** An urban area with a population of more than 10 million [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** 52 − 18 = 34 percentage points [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** All three countries became more urban between 1975 and 2025 [1]. Kessa urbanised fastest, rising by 34 percentage points to 52%, whereas Lorne rose by only 11 points (72% to 83%) [1]. Lorne was the most urban throughout, while Mavi stayed the least urban, at only 21% in 2025 [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** Lorne was already highly urbanised in 1975 (72%), so there were fewer rural people left to move [1]. It may be a high-income country where some people move out of cities into rural areas (counter-urbanisation) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs comparative words and figures from at least two countries in the same sentence.

**8.** Flooding is likely because homes are on the flood plain, and makeshift materials offer little protection [1]. Sharing one tap between 200 people means long queues and too little clean water [1]. With no sewers, waste contaminates water and spreads diseases such as cholera and diarrhoea [1]. Residents do not own the land, so they risk eviction and have little reason to invest in better homes [1]. Also credit fire risk or overcrowding. **[4]**

**9.** Air pollution increases from more vehicles, factories and burning waste or fuel [1]. Untreated sewage and industrial waste pollute rivers, killing fish and harming wildlife [1]. Farmland, wetlands and forest on the edge of the city are built over, destroying habitats [1]. Uncollected rubbish blocks drains, and paved surfaces increase runoff, so flooding becomes more frequent [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Description: in a self-help scheme, residents are given building materials, low-interest loans or legal title to the land [1], and improve their own homes, for example rebuilding in brick and adding toilets [1]. Evaluation: it is cheap for the government and keeps communities and businesses together [1]. Evaluation: giving legal title encourages residents to invest because they no longer fear eviction [1]. Evaluation: progress is slow and depends on residents' incomes, and it does not solve overcrowding or hazards such as flood risk [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Accept any named strategy, such as site-and-service or upgrading in place; the marks are for describing how it works and judging its strengths and limits.

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content, for example for Dhaka:

Named city and scale: Dhaka, a megacity of more than 20 million [1]. Push: rural flooding, river erosion and cyclones destroy homes and land [1]. Push: landholdings too small to support families [1]. Pull: jobs in the garment industry and construction [1]. Impact: a large share of people live in informal settlements such as Korail, with poor services and fire risk [1]. Impact: severe traffic congestion and air pollution, including from brick kilns [1]. Impact: river pollution from sewage and tanneries [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate the two parts of the question clearly; marks for "why people migrated" cannot be gained from a description of the city's problems.

**12.** Indicative content:

Agree: rural development reduces the push factors, so fewer people move and cities grow more slowly [1]. Agree: irrigation, microfinance and rural services improve lives in the countryside as well as easing urban pressure [1]. Agree: improving cities can increase the pull of the city and attract more migrants [1]. Disagree: much urban growth comes from natural increase in the city, which rural development does not affect [1]. Disagree: millions of people already live in squatter settlements and need improvements now [1]. Disagree: rural development takes many years to have an effect [1]. Use of examples such as rural electrification or upgrading of settlements in a named city [1]. Judgement: both are needed; rural development slows growth in the long term, but city improvements are essential for the people already there [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining urbanisation as urban growth.
- Giving only economic push factors when a question asks for different types.
- Answers about squatter settlements that do not use details from the resource.
- Strategies described but not evaluated.
- Case studies of cities in high-income countries when the question asks for a developing country.
