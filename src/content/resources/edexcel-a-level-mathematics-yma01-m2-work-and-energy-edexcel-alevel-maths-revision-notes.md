These notes cover section 3 of Unit M2 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): kinetic and potential energy, work and power, the work–energy principle and the principle of conservation of mechanical energy, including motion under a constant resistance and on inclined planes. For worked examples, use the [M2 work and energy study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-work-and-energy-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## Formulae to know

```
KE = (1/2)mv^2               (learn: not in the booklet)
PE = mgh                     (learn: not in the booklet)
work = F × s × cos θ         (θ between force and motion)
power = work done / time
P = Fv                       (driving force F at speed v)
```

Units: joules (J) for work and energy, watts (W) for power. 1 kW = 1000 W.

## Work done by common forces

| Force | Work done over distance d |
|---|---|
| Constant force F along the motion | Fd |
| Force F at angle θ to the motion | Fd cos θ |
| Gravity, moving up a slope at α | −mgd sin α (work against gravity mgd sin α) |
| Friction on a slope, no other perpendicular forces | Work against friction μmg cos α × d |
| Friction on level ground | Work against friction μmgd |
| Normal reaction | 0 (perpendicular to the motion) |

## The energy equation

```
KE(start) + PE(start) + work by driving forces
   = KE(end) + PE(end) + work against resistances
```

1. Choose a zero level for PE (usually the lowest point).
2. Write each term, with h as a vertical height.
3. Put work done by driving forces on the left and work against resistances on the right.
4. Solve for the unknown.

**When to use energy:** the question mentions work, energy or the work–energy principle; the path is curved; you want a speed or distance and the time is not involved.

**When to use Newton's second law instead:** you need the acceleration or the time, or what happens at an instant.

## Conservation of mechanical energy

KE + PE is constant when only gravity does work: smooth surfaces, no air resistance, no driving force. From rest, after a vertical drop h: v = √(2gh).

## Up and down a rough slope

| Stage | Forces against motion | Energy equation |
|---|---|---|
| Up | Gravity component and friction | ½mu² = mgd sin α + Fd |
| Down | Friction only (gravity helps) | mgd sin α = ½mv² + Fd |

- It slides back only if mg sin α > μmg cos α, that is if μ < tan α.
- It always returns slower than it left.

## Power

| Situation | Equation |
|---|---|
| Driving force at speed v | F = P/v |
| Maximum speed on the level | P/v = R |
| Maximum speed up a slope | P/v = R + mg sin α |
| Maximum speed down a slope | P/v + mg sin α = R |
| Acceleration at speed v | P/v − R (− mg sin α if climbing) = ma |
| Lifting at a steady speed v | P = mgv |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Work | Energy transferred by a force moving its point of application |
| Kinetic energy | Energy due to motion, ½mv² |
| Gravitational potential energy | Energy due to height above a chosen level, mgh |
| Work–energy principle | Total work done on a body equals its change in kinetic energy |
| Conservation of mechanical energy | KE + PE stays constant when no forces other than gravity do work |
| Power | Rate of doing work |

## Exam traps

- Distance along the slope used as h.
- R = mg used on a slope, giving the wrong friction.
- Friction left out on the way down a slope.
- Work against resistance added to the wrong side.
- Kilowatts not converted to watts.
- P = Rv used when the vehicle is accelerating; that only holds at maximum speed.

## Self-test

1. Find the kinetic energy of a 2 kg particle moving at 5 m s⁻¹.
2. A 4 kg box is raised 2.5 m vertically. Find its gain in PE.
3. A particle moves 10 m up a slope inclined at 30°. How high does it rise?
4. A 60 N force acts at 60° to the direction of motion over 8 m. Find the work done.
5. Why does the normal reaction do no work?
6. A ball is dropped from 20 m. Ignoring air resistance, find its speed on hitting the ground.
7. State the condition for a particle at rest on a rough slope to slide back down.
8. A car's engine works at 20 kW and the car moves at 25 m s⁻¹. Find the driving force.
9. A van has power 36 kW and constant resistance 1200 N. Find its maximum speed on level ground.
10. A 1000 kg car at 20 m s⁻¹ has driving force 1500 N and resistance 500 N on level ground. Find its acceleration.
11. A pump raises 50 kg of water per second through 6 m. Find the power needed, ignoring losses.
12. When must you use energy methods rather than suvat?

**Answers:**

1. ½ × 2 × 25 = 25 J.
2. 4 × 9.8 × 2.5 = 98 J.
3. 10 sin 30° = 5 m.
4. 60 × 8 × cos 60° = 240 J.
5. It is perpendicular to the direction of motion, so cos 90° = 0.
6. v = √(2 × 9.8 × 20) = √392 = 19.8 m s⁻¹.
7. μ < tan α, so that mg sin α is greater than the greatest friction μmg cos α.
8. F = 20000/25 = 800 N.
9. v = 36000/1200 = 30 m s⁻¹.
10. 1500 − 500 = 1000a, so a = 1 m s⁻².
11. Work per second = 50 × 9.8 × 6 = 2940 J, so the power is 2940 W (2.94 kW).
12. When the path is curved or the force is not constant along it, because the acceleration is then not constant.
