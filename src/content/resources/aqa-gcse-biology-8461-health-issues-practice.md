These questions cover section **4.2.2.5 Health issues** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. The content is assessed on Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and nothing in 4.2.2.5 is marked (HT only). A calculator is useful for questions 5, 6 and 7. All data are invented.

Learn the content first in the [health issues study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-health-issues/) and the [health issues revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-health-issues-revision-notes/). These questions are different from those in the [health issues, lifestyle and cancer practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-health-lifestyle-and-cancer-practice/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain why a person who has no disease may still not be healthy. **[2]**

**2.** State whether each disease is communicable or non-communicable.

**(a)** Measles **[1]**
**(b)** Coronary heart disease **[1]**
**(c)** HIV **[1]**
**(d)** Lung cancer **[1]**

**3.**

**(a)** Suggest why a person whose immune system is defective is more likely to catch a cold from a classmate. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how severe physical ill health can lead to mental illness. **[2]**

**4.** Suggest why vaccinating young people against a virus that lives in cells could reduce the number of cases of a cancer in later life. **[2]**

**5.** A clinic records the ages of 18 patients newly diagnosed with a skin allergy:

3, 5, 8, 11, 14, 17, 19, 23, 26, 29, 33, 36, 44, 48, 55, 61, 66, 72

**(a)** Construct a frequency table using the classes 0 to under 20, 20 to under 40, 40 to under 60 and 60 to under 80. **[3]**
**(b)** Name the type of chart that should be used to display these data, and give a reason. **[2]**

### Section B: data and longer questions

**6.** A survey asked adults in Region K whether they had experienced poor mental health in the past year.

| Group | People surveyed | Reported poor mental health |
|---|---|---|
| Employed | 1500 | 120 |
| Unemployed | 300 | 54 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each group reporting poor mental health. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest an explanation for the difference, using a factor named in the specification. **[2]**

**7.** A graph shows that a disease has an incidence of 8 new cases per 1000 people per year.

**(a)** Calculate the number of new cases expected each year in a town of 45 000 people. **[2]**
**(b)** The town actually records 520 new cases. Suggest one reason for the difference. **[1]**

**8.** A scatter diagram plots daily hours of screen time against a well-being score for 60 teenagers. The points fall from left to right and are widely spread.

**(a)** Describe the correlation fully. **[2]**
**(b)** A newspaper headline says "Screens destroy teenagers' well-being". Explain why the data do not support this claim. **[2]**

**9.** A researcher wants to find out how common high blood pressure is among adults in a city. He measures the blood pressure of 50 people at one gym on a Saturday morning.

**(a)** Evaluate his sampling method. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how he could improve it. **[2]**

**10.** Describe the relationship between health and disease, and explain how different types of disease can interact. **[6]**

**11.** A patient takes drugs that suppress the immune system after an organ transplant. Over the next year she has four chest infections and becomes very low in mood. Use your knowledge of disease interactions to explain both problems. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Health is the state of physical and mental well-being [1]; a person can be free of disease but still in poor health because of factors such as a poor diet, stress or difficult life situations [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming one of diet, stress or life situations gains the second mark.

**2. (a)** Communicable [1]
**(b)** Non-communicable [1]
**(c)** Communicable [1]
**(d)** Non-communicable [1]
*Examiner insight:* Lung cancer is not caused by a pathogen and cannot be passed on, even though smoking is a risk factor.

**3. (a)** The immune system is less able to destroy the cold virus [1]; so the virus can reproduce in the body and cause illness more easily [1]
**(b)** Long-term pain, disability or loss of independence can affect the person's life [1]; for example stopping work or activities, which can lead to depression [1]
*Examiner insight:* A chain of cause and effect is needed in both parts; naming the interaction alone earns one mark at most.

**4.** Viruses living in cells can be the trigger for cancers [1]; vaccination prevents infection with the virus, so fewer people's cells are changed by it and fewer cancers develop [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link vaccination to preventing the infection that triggers the cancer.

**5. (a)** 0 to under 20: 7 [1]; 20 to under 40: 5 [1]; 40 to under 60: 3 and 60 to under 80: 3 [1]
**(b)** A histogram [1], because age is continuous data grouped into classes, so the bars should touch [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check the frequencies total 18 before moving on.

**6. (a)** Employed: 120 ÷ 1500 × 100 = **8%** [1]; unemployed: 54 ÷ 300 × 100 = **18%** [1]
**(b)** Life situations (or stress) affect mental health [1]; unemployment may bring money worries and loss of routine, raising stress and making poor mental health more likely [1]
*Examiner insight:* Comparing percentages rather than raw numbers is essential: more employed people reported poor mental health, but a smaller proportion.

**7. (a)** 45 000 ÷ 1000 = 45 [1]; 45 × 8 = **360 cases** [1]
**(b)** Any one: the town's population may differ from the population used for the graph (for example more older people); the graph may show an average over many places; the town's figure may be for a year with an outbreak or a change in lifestyle [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" rewards any sensible reason linked to the data, not one fixed answer.

**8. (a)** Negative correlation [1]; weak, because the points are widely spread [1]
**(b)** A correlation shows the variables are linked but does not prove that screen time causes lower well-being [1]; another factor, such as stress or lack of sleep, could affect both, and the correlation is weak [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Fully" means direction and strength.

**9. (a)** Any three: 50 people is a small sample [1]; people at a gym are likely to be fitter than average, so the sample is not representative [1]; the people were not chosen at random [1]; a single place and time gives a biased picture
**(b)** Use a much larger sample [1], chosen at random from across the whole city, including all age groups and backgrounds [1]
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation means saying why each feature weakens the conclusion, not only listing it.

**10.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer defines health and explains at least two interactions):
- health is the state of physical and mental well-being [1]
- diseases, communicable and non-communicable, are major causes of ill health [1]
- other factors such as diet, stress and life situations also affect physical and mental health [1]
- defects in the immune system make a person more likely to suffer from infectious diseases [1]
- viruses living in cells can trigger cancers, and immune reactions first caused by a pathogen can trigger allergies such as asthma [1]
- severe physical ill health can lead to depression or other mental illness [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each interaction should be explained, not just listed, to reach the top level.

**11.** The drugs suppress her immune system, so fewer pathogens are destroyed [1]; this is a defect in the immune system, which makes infectious diseases such as chest infections more likely [1]. Repeated severe physical illness can affect her life and well-being [1]; this can lead to depression, an example of physical ill health leading to mental illness [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks you to use the specification's interactions: name each one and apply it to her situation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving mental well-being out of the definition of health.
- Comparing raw numbers instead of percentages or rates.
- Drawing a bar chart with gaps for continuous data.
- Saying a correlation proves cause.
- Listing sampling problems without explaining their effect on the conclusion.
