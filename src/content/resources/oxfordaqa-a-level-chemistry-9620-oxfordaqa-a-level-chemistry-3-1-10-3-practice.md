These questions cover **3.1.10.3 The ionic product of water, Kw** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3. Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 25 °C unless another value is given, and give pH to 2 decimal places. Treat the hydroxides as strong bases.

## Section A

**1.** Write an expression for Kw. **[1]**

**2.** State the units of Kw. **[1]**

**3.** Explain how the expression for Kw is derived from the equilibrium constant for the dissociation of water. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by a neutral solution. **[1]**

**5.** A solution has pH 3.20 at 25 °C. Calculate the concentration of hydroxide ions. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the pH at 25 °C of:

**(a)** 0.0750 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide **[2]**

**(b)** 0.0180 mol dm⁻³ barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂ **[2]**

**7.** 1.12 g of potassium hydroxide (Mr = 56.1) is dissolved in water and made up to 400 cm³. Calculate the pH of the solution at 25 °C. **[3]**

**8.** Values of Kw at three temperatures are given:

| Temperature / °C | 0 | 25 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Kw / mol² dm⁻⁶ | 1.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ | 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ | 9.61 × 10⁻¹⁴ |

**(a)** Deduce whether the dissociation of water is exothermic or endothermic. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH of pure water at 60 °C. **[2]**

**(c)** State whether pure water at 60 °C is acidic, neutral or alkaline, and give a reason. **[1]**

**9.** This question uses the data in Question 8.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH at 0 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** The same solution has pH 12.30 at 25 °C. Explain why the pH values differ even though [OH⁻] is the same. **[1]**

**10.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is mixed with 15.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ HCl at 25 °C. Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[4]**

**11.** 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH is diluted by a factor of 1000 at 25 °C.

**(a)** Give the pH before and after dilution. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why further dilution would make the pH approach, but never fall below, 7. **[1]**

**12.** Calculate the concentration of strontium hydroxide, Sr(OH)₂, that has a pH of 12.00 at 25 °C. **[2]**

**13.** One dm³ of water contains 55.6 mol of H₂O. Use this and Kw at 25 °C to calculate Kc for H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] [1]

**2.** mol² dm⁻⁶ [1]

**3.** Kc = [H⁺][OH⁻] / [H₂O] [1]; so little water dissociates that [H₂O] is effectively constant, so it is combined with Kc to give Kw = Kc × [H₂O] = [H⁺][OH⁻] [1]

**4.** A solution in which [H⁺] = [OH⁻] [1]

**5.** [H⁺] = 10^(−3.20) = 6.31 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 6.31 × 10⁻⁴ = **1.58 × 10⁻¹¹ mol dm⁻³** [1]

**6. (a)** [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0750 = 1.33 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.88** [1]

**(b)** [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.0180 = 0.0360 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 2.78 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³, pH = **12.56** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Without doubling, the answer is 12.26. Each Ba(OH)₂ releases two OH⁻.

**7.** n(KOH) = 1.12 ÷ 56.1 = 0.0200 mol, so [OH⁻] = 0.0200 ÷ 0.400 = 0.0499 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0499 = 2.00 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.70** [1]

**8. (a)** Kw increases as temperature increases, so the equilibrium H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻ moves to the right on heating [1]; heating favours the endothermic direction, so the dissociation is **endothermic** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = √(9.61 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 3.10 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **6.51** [1]

**(c)** **Neutral**, because [H⁺] = [OH⁻] [1]

**9. (a)** [H⁺] = 1.14 × 10⁻¹⁵ ÷ 0.0200 = 5.70 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **13.24** [1]

**(b)** Kw is smaller at 0 °C, so for the same [OH⁻], [H⁺] = Kw/[OH⁻] is smaller and the pH is higher [1]

**10.** n(OH⁻) = 0.150 × 0.0250 = 3.75 × 10⁻³ mol; n(H⁺) = 0.200 × 0.0150 = 3.00 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; excess OH⁻ = 7.5 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 40.0 cm³, so [OH⁻] = 0.0188 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.01875 = 5.33 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.27** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 25.0 cm³ rather than the total 40.0 cm³ gives 12.48.

**11. (a)** Before: [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹³, pH = **13.00** [1]; after: [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻⁴, so pH = **10.00** [1]

**(b)** As the base becomes very dilute, the H⁺ and OH⁻ from water itself dominate, so the solution approaches neutral (pH 7 at 25 °C); diluting an alkali cannot make it acidic [1]

**12.** [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹², so [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 1.00 × 10⁻¹² = 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [Sr(OH)₂] = 0.0100 ÷ 2 = **5.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³** [1]

**13.** Kc = Kw ÷ [H₂O] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 55.6 = **1.80 × 10⁻¹⁶ mol dm⁻³** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting the factor of 2 for Ba(OH)₂ and Sr(OH)₂.
- Using 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ when a different Kw is given for another temperature.
- Calling pure water above 25 °C acidic.
- Using one solution's volume, not the total, after mixing.
- Reporting −log₁₀[OH⁻] as the pH.
