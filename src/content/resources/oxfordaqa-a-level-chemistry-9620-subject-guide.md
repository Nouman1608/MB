OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) is a modular course covering the three
traditional branches of chemistry, physical, inorganic and organic, across an AS stage and an A2
stage. It is aimed at students heading towards chemistry, medicine, pharmacy, engineering or other
sciences. The full A-level is assessed by five written papers: two AS papers of 90 minutes and 70
marks, each worth 20%; two A2 papers of 90 minutes and 80 marks, each worth 21%; and a Practical and
Synoptic Paper of 85 minutes and 60 marks, worth 18%. Ten required practicals are assessed through
the written papers rather than a hands-on exam.

## How the course is organised

The specification lists thirty-four topics. They sort naturally into the three branches, and within
each branch the AS topics come first.

**Physical chemistry.** At AS: atomic structure; amount of substance; bonding; energetics;
oxidation, reduction and redox equations; kinetics; and chemical equilibria, Le Chatelier's
principle and Kc. At A2: thermodynamics, including Born–Haber cycles, Gibbs free-energy change and
entropy change; electrode potentials and electrochemical cells; acids and bases; rate equations;
and the equilibrium constant Kp for homogeneous systems.

**Inorganic chemistry.** At AS: periodicity; Group 2, the alkaline earth metals; and Group 7(17),
the halogens. At A2: properties of Period 3 elements and their oxides and chlorides; transition
metals; and reactions of ions in aqueous solution.

**Organic chemistry.** At AS: introduction to organic chemistry (nomenclature, reaction mechanisms
and isomerism); alkanes; halogenoalkanes; alkenes; alcohols; and organic analysis, including mass
spectrometry and infrared spectroscopy. At A2: optical isomerism; aldehydes and ketones; carboxylic
acids and derivatives; aromatic chemistry; amines; polymers; amino acids and proteins; organic
synthesis; nuclear magnetic resonance spectroscopy; and chromatography.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Unit | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Unit 1: Inorganic 1 and Physical 1 | 90 minutes | 70 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Unit 2: Organic 1 and Physical 1 | 90 minutes | 70 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Unit 3: Inorganic 2 and Physical 2 | 90 minutes | 80 | 21% |
| A-level Paper 2 | Unit 4: Organic 2 and Physical 2 | 90 minutes | 80 | 21% |
| A-level Paper 3 | Unit 5: Practical and Synoptic Paper | 85 minutes | 60 | 18% |

The qualification is modular and untiered, and all papers are externally assessed written exams.
The ten required practicals are assessed through the written papers, and Unit 5 is a dedicated
practical and synoptic paper. First teaching was in September 2019, with first AS and A-level exams
in May/June 2020.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles show that physical chemistry appears on all four unit papers: Physical 1 is paired
with inorganic content on AS Paper 1 and with organic content on AS Paper 2, and Physical 2 is
paired the same way at A2. Physical chemistry is therefore the backbone of the course, and weak
skills in moles, energetics or equilibria will cost marks on every paper.

Many links run between branches. Bonding and energetics explain the trends in periodicity and in
Groups 2 and 7. Redox at AS leads to electrode potentials at A2 and supports the variable oxidation
states of transition metals. Kinetics at AS becomes rate equations at A2, and Kc at AS becomes Kp.
In organic chemistry, reaction mechanisms introduced at AS reappear in every functional group that
follows, and the analytical techniques of organic analysis extend into NMR spectroscopy and
chromatography, which come together in organic synthesis.

The Practical and Synoptic Paper rewards exactly this kind of joined-up understanding, alongside the
experimental skills built through the required practicals.

## Planning your study

In the first year, secure the physical foundations early: atomic structure, amount of substance and
bonding underpin almost everything. Energetics, kinetics, redox and equilibria can then run
alongside the AS inorganic topics and the introduction to organic chemistry. Carry out the relevant
required practicals as each topic is taught and record method, results and evaluation carefully.

In the second year, work through the A2 physical topics, the A2 inorganic topics and the extended
organic sequence. Organic synthesis works best near the end, once the functional groups are
familiar, and it is a good point to build a reaction map that links every conversion you know.
Revisit AS content regularly, because the A2 papers and the synoptic paper assume it.

Begin timed practice on each unit paper once its content has been taught, then add mixed practice
for the synoptic paper. The exam-preparation page covers each paper in detail, and past papers are a
good test of readiness late in each stage.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in OxfordAQA International A-level Chemistry?**
Five: two AS papers (Units 1 and 2), two A2 papers (Units 3 and 4) and the Unit 5 Practical and Synoptic Paper.

**How are the 9620 papers weighted?**
The AS papers are 20% of the A-level each, the A2 unit papers 21% each, and the Practical and Synoptic Paper 18%, making 100% in total.

**Is there a practical exam in 9620?**
No hands-on exam. Ten required practicals are assessed through the written papers, including the dedicated Practical and Synoptic Paper.

**Which topics are AS and which are A2?**
AS covers the earlier physical topics, periodicity, Groups 2 and 7, and the organic topics up to organic analysis. A2 covers thermodynamics onwards in physical chemistry, Period 3 compounds, transition metals and aqueous ions, and optical isomerism onwards in organic chemistry.

**When was 9620 first examined?**
First teaching was in September 2019, and the first AS and A-level exams were in May/June 2020.
