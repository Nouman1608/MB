Revision notes for the thematic study **Authoritarian rule (from 1750 CE onwards)** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. Four inquiry questions, sixteen lines of inquiry, no prescribed examples. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-thematic-authoritarian-rule/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-thematic-authoritarian-rule-practice/).

## Paper 2 in one box

| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Section A | Analyse a concept, illustrated by an example from the thematic study | 6 |
| Section B (a) | Short response on one example | 4 |
| Section B (b) | Essay using examples from at least two regions | 15 |

Total 25 marks, 1 hour 45 minutes.

## Definition and types

Authoritarian rule: the concentration of political power within a small group or an individual. It lies on a spectrum with democratic processes. Types in the guide: monarchies; oligarchies; colonial governments; dictatorships or totalitarian states; military regimes. Examples may be hybrid.

## The four inquiry questions and lines of inquiry

| Inquiry question | Lines of inquiry |
|---|---|
| Why did authoritarian rule emerge? | Role of ideas; social factors; role of conflict; economic factors |
| How was authoritarian rule maintained? | Legal methods; use of force; propaganda; popular support |
| How did authoritarian rule affect people's lives? | Economic effects; social effects; experiences of women; experiences of marginalised groups |
| How was authoritarian rule challenged? | Internal opposition; popular resistance; impact of policies; external threats |

| Example | Key facts |
|---|---|

| Stalin's USSR | Five-Year Plans from 1928; collectivisation and the famine of 1932 to 1933; Great Terror 1937 to 1938 |
| Mao's China | Great Leap Forward (1958 to 1962) and famine; Cultural Revolution (1966 to 1976) |
| Pinochet's Chile | Coup 11 September 1973; DINA; constitution of 1980; plebiscite of 1988; left power in 1990 |

## Quick evidence by line of inquiry

**Emergence**

- Ideas: fascism, communism, racial hierarchy in empire.
- Social: fear of disorder and revolution; middle-class support for the Chilean coup.
- Conflict: the First World War and the Russian Civil War; conquest in empire.
- Economic: the Depression in Germany; crisis in Chile in 1973.

**Maintenance**

- Legal: the Enabling Act; Nuremberg Laws; Pinochet's 1980 constitution.
- Force: Gestapo; NKVD; DINA; Force Publique.
- Propaganda: Goebbels' ministry; Mao's cult of personality.
- Popular support: early economic recovery in Germany; industrialisation and pride in the USSR.

**Effects on lives**

- Economic: Five-Year Plans and famine; the Great Leap Forward famine (estimates vary widely).
- Social: youth organisations; informers.
- Women: Nazi motherhood ideal; Soviet women in industry with the double burden; relatives of the disappeared in Chile.
- Marginalised groups: Jews, Roma and others in Nazi Germany; African workers in the Congo.

**Challenge**

- Internal: July 1944 plot in Germany; disputes inside the Chinese Communist Party.
- Popular resistance: Salt March; protests and the "No" campaign in Chile; small groups such as the White Rose.
- Impact of policies: famine; abuses in the Congo leading to reform campaigns.
- External threats: defeat in war in 1945; international pressure and the end of the Cold War.

## Section A and B(a)

**Section A.** One-line definition of the concept, one specific example, analysis, judgement. For cause and consequence, separate actors from conditions; for perspectives, name who held each view; for significance, state criteria.

**Section B (a).** One example: identify, describe, then explain how it relates to the question.

## Section B (b): the essay

1. Line of argument in the opening.
2. Examples from at least two regions.
3. Organise by argument.
4. Compare examples explicitly.
5. Reasoned judgement.

## Worked example: a short plan

Question: "To what extent was force the main method by which authoritarian rule was maintained?"

- Thesis: force was essential but rarely sufficient; regimes also needed propaganda, legal cover and some consent.
- Paragraph 1 (Europe): Nazi Germany and the Soviet Union: the Gestapo, SS and NKVD; terror against opponents.
- Paragraph 2 (Asia): Mao's China: campaigns and the cult of personality alongside coercion.
- Paragraph 3 (Africa): the Congo Free State relied heavily on force with little attempt at consent, an extreme case.
- Conclusion: force was the main method where regimes lacked support, while elsewhere other methods carried more weight.

## Markbands at a glance

| Part | Lower bands | Top band |
|---|---|---|
| Section A (6) | Describes the concept with minimal analysis; limited reference to an example | Clearly and accurately analyses the concept, effectively supported by a relevant, specific example |
| Section B (a) (4) | Identifies (1) or describes (2) a relevant example | Effectively explains a relevant example (4); partial explanation scores 3 |
| Section B (b) (15) | Unsubstantiated assertions or description, with irrelevant or vague examples and no connections | Consistently analytical, with a reasoned judgement, consistently relevant examples and well-developed connections integrated into the analysis |

Use the time sensibly. Section A is worth 6 marks and section B part (a) 4 marks, so keep them brief, and save most of the 1 hour 45 minutes for the 15-mark essay. Plan the essay in a few minutes before writing.

## Common mistakes

- Treating all regimes as the same.
- Ignoring popular support.
- Giving mass-death figures with no care.
- Using two European examples.

## Self-test

1. Define authoritarian rule using the guide.
2. Name three types of authoritarian rule from the guide.
3. What did the Enabling Act of 1933 allow?
4. Which regime used the DINA?
5. Name one line of inquiry under "How was authoritarian rule challenged?"
6. Why does the guide say examples may be "hybrid"?

**Answers.**

1. The concentration of political power within a small group or an individual.
2. Any three of: monarchies; oligarchies; colonial governments; dictatorships or totalitarian states; military regimes.
3. Hitler's government to make laws without the Reichstag.
4. Pinochet's Chile.
5. Any of: internal opposition; popular resistance; impact of policies; external threats.
6. Because some regimes had non-authoritarian elements, such as elections or assemblies.
