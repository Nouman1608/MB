These notes condense the examination route of Unit 4 (4a) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Literature (9675) specification, Version 5.3. For a full explanation and two worked examples with original texts, use the [Unit 4a study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4a-examination/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4a-examination-practice/). The other route is in the [Unit 4b revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4b-non-exam-assessment-revision-notes/).

## The paper in brief

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Time | 2 hours 30 minutes |
| Marks | 50 |
| Weight | 30% of the International A-level |
| Questions | Two, both compulsory, on unseen texts |
| Section A | Prose (25 marks); can include literary non-fiction |
| Section B | Poetry (25 marks) |
| Texts | More than one unseen text may be offered in each question |
| Focus | The representation of an aspect of one of four areas. The two questions use different areas |
| Support | The question guides you to specific elements; it is not a completely unaided exercise |

## The four areas

1. Representations of childhood.
2. Representations of war.
3. Representations of women and men.
4. Representations of race, class and culture.

These are **not prescribed topics**. You are **not expected to use extensive contextual knowledge** or wider reading.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Representation | The process of showing a view of the world, not the world itself |
| Significance | Weighing up how the text is constructed, relevant contexts, and potential meanings and interpretations |
| Authorial methods | The choices a writer makes: voice, language, structure, form, setting |
| Interpretation | A reading of the text that others might support or challenge |
| Literary non-fiction | Non-fiction prose with literary qualities, which can appear in Section A |

## Assessment objectives (equal weighting)

| AO | Wording in brief | What the answer shows |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | How readers find meanings; genre, contexts, interpretations | Different readings; links between genre, context and meaning |
| AO2 | How meanings are shaped, with close attention to authorial methods | Close analysis of methods |
| AO3 | Informed, personal, argued response; concepts, terminology; coherent, accurate writing | A clear argument in precise language |

About a third of the marks are for each.

## Method for each question

1. Read the question and underline: the area, the aspect, the instruction.
2. Read the text twice.
3. Find the shape: opening, turn, ending.
4. Choose three or four lines of argument.
5. Write an introduction that answers the question.
6. For each argument: point, short quotation, method, effect on the representation, interpretation.
7. Conclude with a judgement on the significance.

### Methods checklist

| Group | Look at |
|---|---|
| Voice and viewpoint | Narrator, speaker, person, whose view is missing |
| Language | Word choice, imagery, tone, contrast, repetition |
| Structure and form | Opening, ending, sentence or line length, rhyme, stanza, enjambment |
| Setting and symbol | Objects and places that carry meaning |

## Paragraph pattern

**Claim, quotation, method, effect, other reading, link.**

Example sentence frames:

- "The writer represents [area] as [claim] through [method], as in [quotation]."
- "This could be read as [reading 1], but it may also suggest [reading 2]."
- "The significance of this is [link to the question]."

## Timing (suggestion)

| Stage | Minutes |
|---|---|
| Reading and planning | 10 |
| Section A, prose | 65 |
| Section B, poetry | 65 |
| Checking | 10 |

The total is 150 minutes. Each question is worth the same number of marks, so give them equal time.

## Exam traps

- Retelling the plot or paraphrasing the poem.
- Naming a method with no effect.
- Importing prepared context that the text does not support.
- Using the same argument in both answers when the areas differ.
- Not saying anything about the other readings.
- Failing to look at the last line or sentence.
- Not finishing the second answer.

## Question stems and what they ask

| Stem (illustrative) | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Explore the significance of the representation of childhood" | Weigh up what the representation means, using methods and more than one reading |
| "Write an analysis of this poem in which you explore the representation of class and culture" | Analyse methods, and link every point to class and culture |
| "Analyse how the writer presents war in this extract" | Focus on methods; do not retell the extract |

In every stem, the area gives you the topic, the aspect narrows it, and the verb tells you the skill. "Significance" signals that you must weigh up construction, context and interpretation, which the specification describes as the three parts of significance.

## Sentence frames for fast, controlled writing

- Opening: "The writer represents [area] as [claim], largely through [method]."
- Evidence: "This is clear when [short quotation] ..."
- Method and effect: "The [method] makes the reader feel / see / question ..."
- Other reading: "However, a reader could see this as ..."
- Closing a paragraph: "This shapes the representation of [area] because ..."

## Points to check before you finish each answer

- The first sentence answers the question.
- Every paragraph names a method and explains its effect.
- At least one place where you allow another reading.
- Short quotations, correctly copied.
- The last line of the text has been considered.
- No retelling of the plot or paraphrase of the poem.

## Quick self-test

1. How long is Unit 4a, and how many marks?
2. How many questions must you answer?
3. What are the two sections?
4. Can the prose be non-fiction?
5. Name the four areas.
6. Do the two questions use the same area?
7. Are you expected to use detailed context?
8. What does "representation" emphasise?
9. What fraction of the marks does each assessment objective carry?
10. Which objective is about close attention to authorial methods?
11. Give two things to underline in the question.
12. Why is the last line of a poem worth discussing?

### Answers

1. 2 hours 30 minutes; 50 marks.
2. Two, both compulsory.
3. Section A: prose; Section B: poetry.
4. Yes, it can include literary non-fiction.
5. Childhood; war; women and men; race, class and culture.
6. No. They refer to different areas.
7. No. You are not expected to use extensive contextual knowledge.
8. The process of showing a view of the world, not the world itself.
9. About a third.
10. AO2.
11. Any two from the area, the aspect, the instruction.
12. Endings often change, complicate or confirm the representation.
