## Questions

**1.** (Extension only) A and B are independent events. P(A) = 0.3 and P(B) = 0.8. Work out P(A ∩ B). **[2]**

**2.** (Extension only) Decide whether each pair of events is independent. Show your working.

**(a)** P(C) = 0.5, P(D) = 0.36 and P(C ∩ D) = 0.18. **[2]**
**(b)** P(E) = 0.6, P(F) = 0.5 and P(E ∩ F) = 0.25. **[2]**

**3.** (Extension only) A cyclist meets two sets of traffic lights. The lights work independently. The probability of a green light is 0.7 at the first set and 0.6 at the second.

**(a)** Find the probability that both lights are green. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly one light is green. **[3]**

**4.** (Extension only) A bag holds 4 black and 6 white counters. A counter is taken at random, replaced, and a second counter is taken.

**(a)** Write down the probability of black and the probability of white at each stage. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the probability that both counters are black. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out the probability of getting one counter of each colour. **[2]**

**5.** (Extension only) A box holds 8 raffle tickets: 3 are winning tickets and 5 are not. Two tickets are drawn one after the other and not returned.

**(a)** Find the probability that both are winning tickets. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly one is a winning ticket. **[3]**

**6.** (Extension only) A component passes three independent checks with probabilities 0.95, 0.9 and 0.98.

**(a)** Find the probability that the component passes all three checks. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that it fails at least one check. **[2]**

**7.** (Extension only) The table shows the 80 members of a leisure club.

| | Adult | Junior |
|---|---|---|
| Gym | 14 | 6 |
| Swimming | 12 | 18 |
| Yoga | 20 | 10 |

One member is picked at random.

**(a)** Work out the probability of picking a junior. **[1]**
**(b)** Given that the member is a junior, find the probability that they swim. **[2]**
**(c)** Given that the member does yoga, find the probability that they are a junior. **[2]**

**8.** (Extension only) The probability of frost on a morning is 0.25. If there is frost, a bus is late with probability 0.6. If there is no frost, the bus is late with probability 0.15.

**(a)** Find the probability that the bus is late. **[3]**
**(b)** Given that the bus is late, find the probability that there was frost. **[3]**

**9.** (Extension only) A bag holds 4 red and 5 blue counters. Two counters are removed one at a time and not put back.

**(a)** Work out the probability that the second counter removed is red. **[3]**
**(b)** Given that the second counter is red, find the probability that the first counter was red. **[2]**

**10.** (Extension only) 2% of the devices made in a factory are faulty. A test flags a faulty device with probability 0.95. The test also flags a device that is not faulty with probability 0.04.

**(a)** Draw a tree diagram for a device being faulty or not faulty and then flagged or not flagged. Write a probability beside each branch. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that a device chosen at random is flagged. **[3]**
**(c)** Given that a device is flagged, find the probability that it is faulty. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**11.** (Extension only) A and B are independent events. P(A) = 0.4 and the probability that neither A nor B happens is 0.3.

**(a)** Find P(B). **[4]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly one of A and B happens. **[2]**

**12.** (Extension only) A bag holds 6 mint and 4 toffee sweets. Three sweets are removed one at a time and eaten.

**(a)** Find the probability that all three are mint. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the probability that the three include a toffee. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that exactly one is toffee. **[3]**
**(d)** Given that at least one is toffee, find the probability that exactly one is toffee. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The events are independent, so multiply: P(A) × P(B) [1] = 0.3 × 0.8 = **0.24** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication and the rule you are using; a bare decimal leaves nothing to reward if the arithmetic slips.

**2. (a)** P(C) × P(D) = 0.5 × 0.36 = 0.18 [1]. This equals P(C ∩ D), so **C and D are independent** [1]
**(b)** P(E) × P(F) = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3 [1]. This is not 0.25, so **E and F are not independent** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion should come with the product and a clear comparison; saying only "yes" or "no" does not show that you tested the rule.

**3. (a)** P(both green) = 0.7 × 0.6 [1] = **0.42** [1]
**(b)** P(green then not green) = 0.7 × 0.4 = 0.28 [1]; P(not green then green) = 0.3 × 0.6 = 0.18 [1]; total = 0.28 + 0.18 = **0.46** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Exactly one" has two separate cases, and answers that include only one case are a common way to lose marks.

**4. (a)** Each stage: P(black) = 4/10 = 2/5 and P(white) = 6/10 = 3/5 [1]
**(b)** P(black, black) = 2/5 × 2/5 [1] = **4/25** [1]
**(c)** P(black, white) = 2/5 × 3/5 = 6/25 and P(white, black) = 3/5 × 2/5 = 6/25 [1]; P(different) = 6/25 + 6/25 = **12/25** [1]
*Examiner insight:* With replacement, both stages carry the same probabilities, so a different set of numbers at stage two suggests you have treated it as without replacement.

**5. (a)** P(win, win) = 3/8 × 2/7 [1] = 6/56 = **3/28** [1]
**(b)** P(win, lose) = 3/8 × 5/7 = 15/56 [1]; P(lose, win) = 5/8 × 3/7 = 15/56 [1]; total = 30/56 = **15/28** [1]
*Examiner insight:* After the first pick the total drops to 7, and the second-stage numerator also changes if the first ticket is of the same type.

**6. (a)** P(passes all three) = 0.95 × 0.9 × 0.98 [1] = **0.8379** [1]
**(b)** "Fails at least one" is the complement of "passes all" [1], so 1 − 0.8379 = **0.1621** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the complement saves listing seven paths; if you do list them, write each one so a slip can still be followed.

**7. (a)** Juniors = 6 + 18 + 10 = 34, so P(junior) = 34/80 = **17/40** [1]
**(b)** Given junior, the total is 34 [1], so P(swim | junior) = 18/34 = **9/17** [1]
**(c)** Given yoga, the total is 30 [1], so P(junior | yoga) = 10/30 = **1/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Reading the denominator from the "given" row or column is the key step; dividing by 80 in parts (b) or (c) is the usual error.

**8. (a)** Frost and late: 0.25 × 0.6 = 0.15 [1]; no frost and late: 0.75 × 0.15 = 0.1125 [1]; P(late) = 0.15 + 0.1125 = **0.2625** [1]
**(b)** P(frost | late) = P(frost and late) ÷ P(late) [1] = 0.15 ÷ 0.2625 [1] = **4/7** (0.571 to 3 significant figures) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The denominator in part (b) is the answer to part (a), so check part (a) carefully; show each path so your method can be followed.

**9. (a)** P(red, red) = 4/9 × 3/8 = 12/72 [1]; P(blue, red) = 5/9 × 4/8 = 20/72 [1]; P(second red) = 32/72 = **4/9** [1]
**(b)** P(first red | second red) = (12/72) ÷ (32/72) [1] = 12/32 = **3/8** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Second counter" does not mean "after the first is known"; both first-stage colours must be included in part (a).

**10. (a)** First stage: faulty 0.02, not faulty 0.98 [1]. Second stage after faulty: flagged 0.95, not flagged 0.05; after not faulty: flagged 0.04, not flagged 0.96 [1]
**(b)** Faulty and flagged: 0.02 × 0.95 = 0.019 [1]; not faulty and flagged: 0.98 × 0.04 = 0.0392 [1]; P(flagged) = 0.019 + 0.0392 = **0.0582** [1]
**(c)** P(faulty | flagged) = 0.019 ÷ 0.0582 [1] = 0.32646... [1] = **0.326** (3 significant figures) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Label each branch and check that each pair adds to 1; if the question specifies 3 significant figures, round only on the final line.

**11. (a)** Let P(B) = b. Independence means P(not A and not B) = P(not A) × P(not B) [1]; P(not A) = 0.6 [1]; 0.6 × (1 − b) = 0.3 [1]; 1 − b = 0.5, so b = **0.5** [1]
**(b)** P(A only) = 0.4 × 0.5 = 0.2 and P(B only) = 0.6 × 0.5 = 0.3 [1]; total = **0.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Turn the words "neither A nor B" into a product of complements before you try to solve; unclear working here is hard to credit.

**12. (a)** P(all mint) = 6/10 × 5/9 × 4/8 [1] = 120/720 = **1/6** [1]
**(b)** P(at least one toffee) = 1 − 1/6 = **5/6** [1]
**(c)** One path, toffee then mint then mint: 4/10 × 6/9 × 5/8 = 120/720 [1]; there are three equally likely orders for the single toffee [1]; 3 × 120/720 = 360/720 = **1/2** [1]
**(d)** P(exactly one | at least one) = (1/2) ÷ (5/6) [1] = 6/10 = **3/5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Exactly one toffee can occur in three positions, so state the number of orders you are counting.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the original total at the second stage when counters or tickets are not replaced.
- Adding probabilities along a branch instead of multiplying.
- Multiplying across separate paths instead of adding them.
- Counting only one of the cases in "exactly one" questions.
- Answering with the wrong conditional direction: P(A | B) instead of P(B | A).
- Using the grand total as the denominator when a conditional probability needs a reduced total.
- Stating "independent" without showing the product comparison.
- Rounding part-way through a calculation, then missing the required accuracy at the end.
- Leaving branches that do not sum to 1 and not noticing.
- Listing every path for "at least one" when 1 − P(none) was shorter and safer.
