## Definitions (3.7.1 and 3.7.3)

| Term | Definition | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Fixed cost (FC) | Cost that stays the same whatever the output | Given, or TC − VC |
| Variable cost (VC) | Cost that changes with output | Given, or TC − FC |
| Total cost (TC) | All costs of producing a given output | FC + VC |
| Average fixed cost (AFC) | Fixed cost per unit | FC ÷ output |
| Average variable cost (AVC) | Variable cost per unit | VC ÷ output |
| Average total cost (ATC) | Total cost per unit | TC ÷ output, or AFC + AVC |
| Total revenue (TR) | Sum taken in from sales | Price × quantity sold |
| Average revenue (AR) | Revenue per unit sold | TR ÷ quantity sold |
| Profit | What is left after costs | TR − TC |

Examples of fixed costs: rent, insurance, a loan repayment. Examples of variable costs: raw materials, packaging, power used by machines, pay that depends on units made. Some costs are mixed, so read the scenario to see whether a cost moves with output.

## Method in steps: a cost table (3.7.2)

1. Write FC as a constant for every output.
2. VC is usually given. TC = FC + VC.
3. Divide each total by that row's output to get AFC, AVC and ATC.
4. Check every row: AFC + AVC must equal ATC.
5. Find the lowest ATC. That is the output where each unit costs least.

### Worked table: a juice bar (invented)

Fixed cost is 1,200 a month.

| Output | VC | TC | AFC | AVC | ATC | TR at 7.50 | Profit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 200 | 1,000 | 2,200 | 6.00 | 5.00 | 11.00 | 1,500 | -700 |
| 400 | 1,800 | 3,000 | 3.00 | 4.50 | 7.50 | 3,000 | 0 |
| 600 | 2,400 | 3,600 | 2.00 | 4.00 | 6.00 | 4,500 | 900 |
| 800 | 3,800 | 5,000 | 1.50 | 4.75 | 6.25 | 6,000 | 1,000 |

Working for 600 drinks: TC = 1,200 + 2,400 = 3,600. AFC = 1,200 ÷ 600 = 2. AVC = 2,400 ÷ 600 = 4. ATC = 3,600 ÷ 600 = 6, and 2 + 4 = 6. TR = 7.50 × 600 = 4,500, so profit = 4,500 − 3,600 = 900.

Reading the table:

- ATC is lowest at 600 drinks (6.00), but profit is highest at 800 (1,000). Lowest ATC is not the same as highest profit.
- At 400 drinks, TR equals TC, so the bar earns exactly enough to cover all costs and profit is zero.
- At 200 drinks, TR is below TC, so there is a loss of 700.

### Reverse calculations

| Given | Find | Method |
|---|---|---|
| ATC 14 at 250 units | TC | 14 × 250 = 3,500 |
| AFC 3 at 300 units | FC | 3 × 300 = 900 |
| TC 5,200 and FC 2,000 | VC | 5,200 − 2,000 = 3,200 |

## Cost diagram summary (3.7.2)

Axes: output on the horizontal axis, cost (or cost per unit) on the vertical axis.

| Curve | What it looks like | Why |
|---|---|---|
| FC | Flat line | Does not change with output |
| VC | Begins at the origin and climbs | No output means no variable cost |
| TC | Begins at the FC level, always FC above VC | TC − VC is always FC |
| AFC | Slopes down, never touches the axis | Same FC shared over more units |
| AVC | U-shaped | Falls with efficiency gains, rises as the firm becomes crowded or over-used |
| ATC | U-shaped, above AVC | The gap between ATC and AVC is AFC, so it narrows |

In a full diagram, the lowest ATC is usually further right than the lowest AVC, because AFC is still falling when AVC begins to rise. In the juice bar table both are lowest at 600 among the four outputs shown, but four rows cannot show the exact turning points, so say "lowest of the outputs given" if you are reading from a table.

### Command words for this unit

- Define: give the meaning, for example "fixed cost is a cost that stays the same as output changes".
- Calculate: show the formula, the substitution and the answer with units.
- Explain: give a reason and a link, for example why AFC falls as output rises.
- Identify: pick the objective or cost from the scenario and quote the evidence.
- Evaluate: weigh both sides, then reach a judgement that uses the firm's facts.

## Revenue and sales (3.7.4)

- Constant price: if sales rise from 400 to 600 at 7.50, TR rises from 3,000 to 4,500.
- AR equals the price when all units sell at one price.
- If a firm must cut price to sell more, TR can rise, stay flat or fall. Always calculate price × quantity at each price and compare.
- Revenue is not profit. A rise in sales raises profit only if the extra TR is more than the extra cost of producing them.

### Worked reminder: price cut

A greengrocer (invented) sells 500 kg of apples at 3.00 a kilo. TR = 1,500 and AR = 3.00. After a cut to 2.60 it sells 600 kg. TR = 2.60 × 600 = 1,560, a rise of 60. The cut raised revenue, but you cannot say whether profit rose until the cost of the extra 100 kg is known.

## Objectives of firms (3.7.5)

| Objective | Meaning | Typical firm | Typical behaviour |
|---|---|---|---|
| Survival | Stay in business | New or struggling firm | Cut prices, cut costs, accept low profit |
| Profit maximisation | Largest TR − TC | Owner-run or shareholder firm | Raise efficiency, charge what customers will pay |
| Growth | Become larger | Expanding firm | Advertise, open outlets, accept low profit now |
| Social welfare | Benefit the community | Charity, social enterprise, state-owned firm | Low prices, jobs for the disadvantaged, break even |

### Evaluation checklist

- Link the objective to the firm: age, size, owner, competition.
- Say what the firm gives up. Growth may reduce short-run profit; social goals may reduce profit; high profit may need high prices.
- Say how objectives overlap. Survival needs TR to cover TC, so even a social enterprise cannot ignore revenue.
- Give a judgement: which is most likely for this firm, and why?

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| FC and VC | FC is the same at every output; VC changes |
| TC and ATC | TC is a total; ATC is per unit |
| AFC + AVC | Adds to ATC, not to TC |
| Revenue and profit | Profit is revenue minus total cost |
| Lowest ATC and highest profit | Different questions: one uses costs only, the other uses costs and revenue |
| Survival and profit maximisation | Survival needs costs covered; maximisation seeks the largest gap |

## Quick self-test

1. Fixed cost is 900 and output is 300. Find AFC.
2. Variable cost is 2,400 and output is 300. Find AVC.
3. Use your answers to questions 1 and 2 to find ATC, then check by finding TC first.
4. A firm has ATC of 14 at 250 units. Find TC.
5. A firm sells 500 units and receives 7,500. Find AR.
6. AR is 20 and 340 units are sold. Find TR.
7. TC is 5,000 and TR is 5,600. Find profit.
8. Which of FC and VC rises when output rises?
9. Which objective is shown by a café cutting prices during a slump to avoid closing?
10. Price falls from 10 to 8 and sales rise from 130 to 150. Does TR rise or fall? Show the numbers.

### Answers

1. AFC = 900 ÷ 300 = 3.
2. AVC = 2,400 ÷ 300 = 8.
3. ATC = 3 + 8 = 11. Check: TC = 900 + 2,400 = 3,300; 3,300 ÷ 300 = 11.
4. TC = 14 × 250 = 3,500.
5. AR = 7,500 ÷ 500 = 15.
6. TR = 20 × 340 = 6,800.
7. Profit = 5,600 − 5,000 = 600.
8. VC. FC stays the same.
9. Survival.
10. TR falls: 10 × 130 = 1,300 and 8 × 150 = 1,200, a fall of 100.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing TC = AFC + AVC instead of ATC = AFC + AVC.
- Dividing TC by output when the question asks for AVC or AFC.
- Giving a number with no label or unit. Say "ATC = 11 dollars per unit".
- Leaving out the working on a calculation. Show the formula, the substitution and the answer.
- Drawing AFC with a U shape, or ATC below AVC.
- Defining fixed cost as "cost that never changes". It does not change with output over the period.
- Saying a price cut "always increases revenue" or "always decreases revenue".
- Naming an objective without applying it to the firm in the question.
- Treating profit maximisation as the only objective every firm has.
