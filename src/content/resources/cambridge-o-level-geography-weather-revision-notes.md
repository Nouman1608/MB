## The one-line summary

2.4 is about **how weather is measured and how the numbers are used**:
the Stevenson Screen, eight named instruments plus digital ones, cloud
observation, calculations and graphs.

## Stevenson Screen: feature + reason

- **White** → reflects the Sun's radiation.
- **Louvred sides** → free air flow; keeps out direct sun and rain.
- **On legs, about 1.2 m up** → avoids heat radiated from the ground.
- **Insulated or double roof** → less heating from above.
- **Door away from the Sun** → no sunlight on thermometers while reading.
- **On grass, in the open, away from buildings and trees** → no
  shelter, shade or stored heat; readings are representative.
- Holds: maximum-minimum thermometer and wet-and-dry bulb thermometer.

## Instruments

| Instrument | Measures | Unit | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rain gauge | Precipitation | mm | Sunk in ground, rim above surface to stop splash-in; open site |
| Maximum-minimum thermometer | Highest and lowest temperature | °C | Read end of marker nearest liquid; reset with magnet |
| Wet-and-dry bulb (hygrometer) | Relative humidity | % | Bigger difference = drier air; no difference = saturated |
| Sunshine recorder | Hours of sunshine | hours | Glass sphere burns a trace on card |
| Barometer | Air pressure | mb or hPa | Aneroid: sealed box expands as pressure falls |
| Anemometer | Wind speed | km/h or knots | Spinning cups; mounted high and clear |
| Wind vane | Wind direction | compass point | Points to where wind comes **from** |
| Digital station / data logger | Several variables | various | Automatic and continuous, but needs power and checking |

## Cloud

- Amount in **oktas** (eighths of the sky): 0 = clear, 8 = overcast.
- **Cirrus**: high, wispy. **Cumulus**: heaped, flat base, fair
  weather. **Cumulonimbus**: towering, thunderstorms and heavy rain.
  **Stratus**: low, grey, even sheet.

## Calculations to know

| Calculation | Method |
|---|---|
| Daily temperature range | Maximum − minimum |
| Mean daily temperature | (Maximum + minimum) ÷ 2 |
| Monthly or annual mean | Sum of values ÷ number of values |
| Annual temperature range | Hottest month mean − coolest month mean |
| Total rainfall | Add all values |

Always show working and write the unit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Weather | Short-term, day-to-day state of the atmosphere |
| Climate | Long-term average weather, usually over about 30 years |
| Relative humidity | Amount of water vapour in the air as a percentage of the most it could hold at that temperature |
| Okta | One-eighth of the sky covered by cloud |
| Prevailing wind | The most frequent wind direction at a place |
| Wind rose | Diagram showing how often the wind blows from each direction |
| Millibar (hectopascal) | Unit of air pressure |

## Exam traps

- Wind vane direction reversed.
- "White to keep it cool" with no idea of **reflection**.
- Mean temperature calculated as a range.
- Graph description without figures and units.
- Rain gauge sited under trees or next to a wall.

## Self-test

1. Which two instruments are kept inside a Stevenson Screen?
2. Why is the screen raised on legs?
3. Dry bulb 24 °C, wet bulb 24 °C. What is the relative humidity?
4. A wind vane points to the north-east. Name the wind.
5. Max 35 °C, min 21 °C. Calculate the range and the mean.
6. What does 6 oktas mean?
7. Which instrument uses a glass sphere?
8. Give one disadvantage of a digital weather station.

**Answers:** 1. Maximum-minimum thermometer; wet-and-dry bulb
thermometer. 2. So that heat radiated from the ground does not affect
the readings. 3. 100 per cent (saturated air, no evaporation). 4. A
north-easterly (blowing from the north-east). 5. Range 14 °C; mean
28 °C. 6. Six-eighths of the sky is covered by cloud. 7. Sunshine
recorder. 8. Any one: needs a power supply; can fail or record wrong
values without anyone noticing; expensive to buy or repair.
