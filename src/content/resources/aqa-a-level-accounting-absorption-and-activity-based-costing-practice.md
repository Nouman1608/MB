## Questions

**1.** Which **one** term describes charging a department's overheads to products using a
predetermined rate? **[1]**

A. Allocation
B. Apportionment
C. Absorption
D. Attribution

**2.** Which **one** is the most suitable cost driver for a materials handling cost pool? **[1]**

A. Floor area of the stores
B. Number of material movements
C. Direct labour hours
D. Units sold

**3.** Explain the difference between allocation and apportionment of overheads. **[2]**

**4.** Pebworth Plastics has two production departments, Moulding and Finishing. Budgeted overheads
for the year:

| Overhead | £ |
|---|---|
| Indirect wages: Moulding £41 000, Finishing £26 400 | 67 400 |
| Rent | 72 000 |
| Depreciation of machinery | 40 000 |
| Heating and lighting | 16 000 |

| | Moulding | Finishing |
|---|---|---|
| Floor area (m²) | 2 500 | 1 500 |
| Machinery cost (£) | 320 000 | 80 000 |
| Budgeted machine hours | 16 000 | 2 000 |
| Budgeted direct labour hours | 3 000 | 13 480 |

**(a)** Prepare an overhead analysis showing the total budgeted overheads of each department. **[5]**
**(b)** Calculate a suitable overhead absorption rate for each department. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why you chose a different basis for each department. **[2]**

**5.** Using your rates from question 4, a storage crate uses £6.30 of materials, 0.75 machine hours in
Moulding and 0.5 direct labour hours in Finishing. Direct labour is paid £14 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of one crate. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the selling price using a 25% mark-up on cost. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that a 20% margin on selling price gives the same price, and explain why. **[2]**

**6.** Actual results for Pebworth Plastics: Moulding worked 15 200 machine hours with overheads of
£124 500; Finishing worked 14 200 direct labour hours with overheads of £70 300.

**(a)** Calculate the under- or over-absorption for each department. **[4]**
**(b)** State the overall effect on profit for the year. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the Moulding result using budgeted and actual activity and spending. **[2]**

**7.** Corrin Cosmetics makes 20 000 bottles of Lotion and 5 000 bottles of Serum a year. Production
overheads are £150 000, currently absorbed at a rate per direct labour hour.

| Per bottle | Lotion | Serum |
|---|---|---|
| Direct materials | £3.20 | £9.50 |
| Direct labour at £16 an hour | 0.25 hours | 0.5 hours |
| Machine hours | 0.1 | 0.3 |

The overheads have been analysed into cost pools:

| Cost pool | £ | Cost driver | Lotion | Serum |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Machine set-ups | 48 000 | Set-ups | 8 | 24 |
| Quality testing | 36 000 | Tests | 40 | 80 |
| Purchasing | 24 000 | Purchase orders | 50 | 70 |
| Machine running | 42 000 | Machine hours | 2 000 | 1 500 |

**(a)** Calculate the total cost per bottle of each product using absorption costing. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total cost per bottle of each product using activity based costing. **[6]**
**(c)** Corrin prices at a 50% mark-up on cost. Calculate the price of each product under both
methods. **[2]**

**8.** Using question 7, evaluate whether Corrin Cosmetics should adopt activity based costing. **[6]**

**9.** Hartfell Ltd makes one product. In its first year it made 12 000 units and sold 10 500 at £45.
Variable production cost is £19 per unit. Fixed production overheads were £96 000, as budgeted, and
are absorbed on a budgeted output of 12 000 units. Selling and administration costs were £70 000.

**(a)** Prepare a statement of profit for the year using marginal costing. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare a statement of profit for the year using absorption costing. **[4]**
**(c)** Reconcile the two profit figures. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why a manager's bonus based on absorption costing profit may lead to overproduction. **[2]**

**10.** A department's overhead absorption rate is £6.50 per direct labour hour. It worked 8 400 hours
and overheads were over-absorbed by £1 250. Calculate the actual overheads. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** limitations of using a single factory-wide overhead absorption rate based on
direct labour hours. **[4]**

**12.** A business absorbs fixed production overheads at £6 per unit in both years. Inventory fell from
900 units to 400 units during the year. Marginal costing profit was £58 000. Calculate the absorption
costing profit and explain the difference. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C, Absorption** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* D (attribution) is the ABC term for charging pools to products; the two are not interchangeable.

**2.** **B, number of material movements** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A driver must cause the cost to change; floor area does not change with handling activity.

**3.** Allocation charges a whole overhead to the one cost centre that caused it [1]; apportionment
shares an overhead that several cost centres caused, using a fair basis [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a definition; an example alone is not enough.

**4. (a)**

| £ | Moulding | Finishing |
|---|---|---|
| Indirect wages (allocated) | 41 000 | 26 400 |
| Rent (2 500 : 1 500) | 45 000 | 27 000 |
| Depreciation (320 000 : 80 000) | 32 000 | 8 000 |
| Heating and lighting (2 500 : 1 500) | 10 000 | 6 000 |
| **Total** | **128 000** | **67 400** |

Indirect wages allocated [1]; rent [1]; depreciation [1]; heating and lighting [1]; totals £128 000 and
£67 400 (grand total £195 400) [1].
**(b)** Moulding 128 000 ÷ 16 000 = **£8.00 per machine hour** [1]; Finishing 67 400 ÷ 13 480 =
**£5.00 per direct labour hour** [1].
**(c)** Moulding uses far more machine hours than labour hours, so machine time drives its costs [1];
Finishing is labour-intensive, so labour hours reflect its use of resources [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Allow follow-through in (b) from an arithmetic slip in (a), not from a wrong basis.

**5. (a)** Labour 0.5 × 14 = £7.00 [1]; overheads (0.75 × 8.00) + (0.5 × 5.00) = 6.00 + 2.50 = £8.50 [1];
total 6.30 + 7.00 + 8.50 = **£21.80** [1].
**(b)** 21.80 × 1.25 = **£27.25** [1]
**(c)** 21.80 ÷ 0.8 = £27.25 [1]. Profit is £5.45 either way: 5.45 is 25% of the £21.80 cost and 20% of
the £27.25 price [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Applying each department's rate to the wrong hours loses the overhead and total marks in (a).

**6. (a)** Moulding absorbed 15 200 × 8 = £121 600 [1]; 121 600 − 124 500 = **£2 900 under-absorbed** [1].
Finishing absorbed 14 200 × 5 = £71 000 [1]; 71 000 − 70 300 = **£700 over-absorbed** [1].
**(b)** Net under-absorption 2 900 − 700 = £2 200, so **profit falls by £2 200** [1].
**(c)** Machine hours were 800 below budget, so 800 × 8 = £6 400 less was absorbed [1]; spending was
£3 500 below the £128 000 budget, offsetting part: 6 400 − 3 500 = £2 900 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Absorbed overheads must use actual hours; using budgeted hours gives zero difference from the budget and earns no credit.

**7. (a)** OAR = 150 000 ÷ (20 000 × 0.25 + 5 000 × 0.5) = 150 000 ÷ 7 500 = £20 per hour [1].
Lotion 3.20 + 4.00 + 5.00 = **£12.20** [1]; Serum 9.50 + 8.00 + 10.00 = **£27.50** [1].
**(b)** Driver rates: set-ups 48 000 ÷ 32 = £1 500; tests 36 000 ÷ 120 = £300 [1]; orders
24 000 ÷ 120 = £200; machine hours 42 000 ÷ 3 500 = £12 [1].
Lotion: 12 000 + 12 000 + 10 000 + 24 000 = £58 000 [1]; Serum: 36 000 + 24 000 + 14 000 + 18 000 =
£92 000 [1].
Per bottle: Lotion 58 000 ÷ 20 000 = £2.90, total **£10.10** [1]; Serum 92 000 ÷ 5 000 = £18.40, total
**£35.90** [1].
**(c)** Absorption: Lotion **£18.30**, Serum **£41.25** [1]. ABC: Lotion **£15.15**, Serum **£53.85** [1].
**[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each driver rate; without them, one wrong figure loses the method marks too.

**8.** For ABC: Serum's cost rises from £27.50 to £35.90, so at £41.25 its margin is much thinner than
the absorption figures suggest [1]; Lotion is over-costed by £2.10 and may be over-priced against
competitors [1]; managers could cut costs by reducing set-ups and tests on Serum [1]. Against:
setting up and running ABC costs time and money [1]; pools and drivers still need judgement, so ABC
costs are estimates too [1]. Judgement: adopt ABC, because the gap is large for both products and
pricing decisions depend on it [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation needs points on both sides using Corrin's figures, then a decision.

**9. (a)** Marginal costing:

| | £ |
|---|---|
| Revenue (10 500 × 45) | 472 500 |
| Variable cost of production (12 000 × 19) | 228 000 |
| Less closing inventory (1 500 × 19) | (28 500) |
| Variable cost of sales | 199 500 |
| Contribution | 273 000 |
| Fixed production overheads | (96 000) |
| Selling and administration | (70 000) |
| **Profit** | **107 000** |

Revenue [1]; closing inventory at £19 [1]; contribution £273 000 [1]; profit **£107 000** [1].
**(b)** OAR 96 000 ÷ 12 000 = £8, full cost £27 [1]. Cost of production 324 000, less closing
inventory 1 500 × 27 = 40 500 [1], cost of sales 283 500, gross profit 189 000 [1]; less 70 000 =
**£119 000** [1].
**(c)** Inventory rose by 1 500 units, carrying 1 500 × 8 = £12 000 of fixed overheads forward [1];
107 000 + 12 000 = £119 000 [1].
**(d)** Every extra unit made but not sold carries £8 of fixed overhead into inventory [1], so profit and
the bonus rise without any extra sales [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Charging fixed production overheads again in the absorption statement double-counts them and loses the profit mark.

**10.** Absorbed 8 400 × 6.50 = £54 600 [1]; over-absorbed means actual was lower: 54 600 − 1 250 =
**£53 350** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Adding the £1 250 is the usual sign error: over-absorbed means absorbed exceeded actual.

**11.** Many overheads, such as set-ups and inspections, are not caused by labour time [1], so products
with few labour hours but many batches are under-costed [1]. Departments differ, for example machine-
intensive and labour-intensive [1], so one rate misstates costs in both and prices may be wrong [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each limitation needs a consequence for the product cost or price to earn its second mark.

**12.** Inventory fell by 500 units [1]; 500 × 6 = £3 000 of fixed overheads brought forward are charged
this year, so absorption profit is lower [1]: 58 000 − 3 000 = **£55 000** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The direction matters: falling inventory makes absorption profit lower, and stating it higher loses the final mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Apportioning on an unsuitable basis, such as rent on machine hours.
- Using budgeted rather than actual activity for overheads absorbed.
- Getting the sign of under- and over-absorption wrong, or its effect on profit.
- Dividing an ABC pool by units made rather than total driver quantity.
- Confusing mark-up (on cost) with margin (on price).
- Charging fixed production overheads twice in an absorption profit statement.
- Evaluations with no decision or no figures from the question.
