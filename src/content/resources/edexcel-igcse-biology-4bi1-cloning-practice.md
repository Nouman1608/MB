These questions cover sub-topic 5(d), **Cloning** (specification points 5.17B to 5.20B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Every point here is bold with a **B** reference, so every question is labelled **(Paper 2 only)**. The set applies to the June and November series examined under Issue 3. A calculator may be used.

## Questions

**1.** (Paper 2 only) State what is meant by each term.

**(a)** Clone **[1]**
**(b)** Explant **[1]**
**(c)** In vitro **[1]**

**2.** (Paper 2 only) A grower uses micropropagation to produce orchid plants.

**(a)** Describe the main stages of micropropagation, starting with the parent plant. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why all the orchid plants are genetically identical to the parent. **[2]**

**3.** (Paper 2 only) One explant gives 5 shoots in each 4-week cycle. Each shoot is then used to start a new cycle.

**(a)** Calculate the number of plantlets after 4 cycles. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time taken to complete 4 cycles. **[1]**
**(c)** A grower needs at least 10 000 plantlets from one explant. Calculate the number of cycles needed. **[2]**

**4.** (Paper 2 only) Give **two** advantages and **two** disadvantages of growing crop plants by micropropagation instead of from seed. **[4]**

**5.** (Paper 2 only) Dolly the sheep was cloned from an adult sheep.

**(a)** Describe how Dolly was produced. **[5]**
**(b)** Dolly was genetically identical to only one of the three sheep involved. State which one and explain why. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the nucleus used was diploid. **[1]**

**6.** (Paper 2 only) Sheep A donates a nucleus from a body cell. Sheep B donates an unfertilised egg cell. Sheep C carries the embryo.

**(a)** Name the sheep that the lamb is genetically identical to. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the nucleus of the egg cell from sheep B was removed. **[1]**
**(c)** State the role of sheep C. **[1]**

**7.** (Paper 2 only) Scientists want sheep that produce a human protein in their milk.

**(a)** Explain how the human gene can be put into sheep. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the scientists then clone the transgenic sheep. **[3]**
**(c)** State what is meant by the term transgenic. **[1]**

**8.** (Paper 2 only) Evaluate the use of micropropagation and mammal cloning in agriculture. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** An organism (or cell) that is genetically identical to its parent [1]
**(b)** A small piece of tissue taken from a plant to start a culture [1]
**(c)** In a laboratory container (in glass), not in the whole organism [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Genetically identical" is the key phrase for a clone. "A copy" on its own is too vague.

**2.** **(a)** Select a parent plant with the desired characteristics [1]; cut small explants (for example shoot tips) [1]; sterilise the explants and equipment (aseptic technique) [1]; place them on a sterile nutrient medium with growth substances, where they grow into plantlets by mitosis [1]; transfer the plantlets to compost and allow them to grow [1]
**(b)** The new plants are produced from the parent's cells by mitosis [1]; there is no fusion of gametes, so no mixing of genes (they have the same genes as the parent) [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Sterile" or "aseptic" must be mentioned. Without it a micropropagation answer cannot score full marks.

**3.** **(a)** 5⁴ [1] = 5 × 5 × 5 × 5 = **625 plantlets** [1]
**(b)** 4 × 4 = **16 weeks** [1]
**(c)** 5⁵ = 3125 (too few) [1]; 5⁶ = 15 625 (enough), so **6 cycles** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), the answer is 5 to the power 4, not 5 × 4 = 20. Each shoot starts its own cycle.

**4.** Advantages, any two of: many plants quickly [1]; plants are identical, so good characteristics are kept [1]; can be done all year round [1]; plants may be disease-free [1]
Disadvantages, any two of: no genetic variation, so a disease could affect all plants [1]; costly equipment and trained staff [1]; contamination can ruin a batch [1] [4]
*Examiner insight:* Write four separate points. Giving two versions of the same idea scores one mark.

**5.** **(a)** A body cell was taken from an adult sheep (the donor) [1]; an egg cell was taken from a second sheep and its nucleus was removed [1]; the nucleus from the body cell was put into the empty egg cell and an electric shock made the cell fuse and divide [1]; the cell divided to form an embryo, which was put into the uterus of a surrogate mother [1]; the lamb that was born was genetically identical to the sheep that donated the nucleus [1]
**(b)** The sheep that donated the body cell nucleus [1]; because all of the lamb's genes came from that nucleus [1]
**(c)** It came from a body cell, which has two sets of chromosomes [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the three sheep apart in your answer. Many students lose marks by writing that the surrogate gave her genes.

**6.** **(a)** Sheep A [1]
**(b)** So that all the genes of the lamb come from the nucleus of sheep A (accept: so the egg's genes are not present) [1]
**(c)** It is the surrogate mother in which the embryo develops until birth [1]
*Examiner insight:* The egg cell still contributes cytoplasm, so do not write that sheep B gives "nothing".

**7.** **(a)** The human gene is cut out and inserted into a sheep cell by genetic engineering [1]; the gene is made to work in the mammary gland, so the protein appears in the milk [1]
**(b)** The genetic engineering step is difficult and does not always work [1]; cloning a cell that has the gene gives many identical animals, each with the gene [1]; so more of the protein is produced / a reliable herd is made [1]
**(c)** Containing genetic material transferred from a different species [1]
*Examiner insight:* A strong (b) explains the benefit of cloning after the difficult step, rather than repeating the description of genetic engineering.

**8.** Micropropagation: many identical plants with desirable features, quickly, all year round [1]; cloning mammals: many animals with the same high-value features or protein production, but the success rate is low and costs are high [1]; both reduce genetic variation, so a disease or change could affect all the organisms [1]; a judgement, such as "micropropagation is widely useful but mammal cloning is mainly worthwhile for high-value animals" [1]
*Examiner insight:* A conclusion must refer back to the points made. "Both are good" alone does not earn the final mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the surrogate or egg donor's genes to the clone.
- Forgetting to say that the egg nucleus is removed.
- Not stating that the explants and equipment are sterile.
- Writing 5 × 4 instead of 5⁴ in a multiplication question.
- Describing cloning as adding a new gene.
- Not mentioning loss of genetic variation in an evaluation.
