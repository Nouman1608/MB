> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **19.2 Food chains and food webs** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (pyramids of energy and energy efficiency); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [food chains and food webs study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-chains-and-food-webs/) and the [food chains and food webs revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-food-chains-and-food-webs-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which is the description of a herbivore?

A: an organism that makes its own organic nutrients

B: an animal that gets its energy by eating plants

C: an animal that gets its energy by eating other animals

D: an organism that gets its energy from dead organic material

**[1]**

**2.** In the food chain grass → grasshopper → frog → snake → hawk, what is the trophic level of the snake?

A: primary consumer

B: secondary consumer

C: tertiary consumer

D: quaternary consumer

**[1]**

**3.** State what the arrows in a food chain represent. **[1]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by:

**(a)** a producer **[1]**

**(b)** a consumer. **[1]**

**5.** One dog carries 300 fleas. Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers for this dog and its fleas, and explain why it has this shape. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State **two** advantages of using a pyramid of energy rather than a pyramid of numbers to represent a food chain. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** In a pond, algae are eaten by tadpoles and by water snails. Tadpoles are eaten by dragonfly larvae and by fish. Dragonfly larvae are eaten by fish. Water snails are eaten by ducks. Fish are eaten by herons.

**(a)** Write out a food chain from this web that contains five organisms. **[1]**

**(b)** Name **one** organism that occupies two different trophic levels, and state both levels. **[2]**

**(c)** A foreign species of fish that eats only tadpoles is introduced to the pond. Suggest and explain the effect on the dragonfly larvae and on the algae. **[4]**

**8.** A student surveyed one hedgerow. The table shows her results.

| Organism | Trophic level | Number | Dry biomass / g per m² |
|---|---|---|---|
| hawthorn bushes | producer | 20 | 3 500 |
| caterpillars | primary consumer | 4 000 | 40 |
| blue tits | secondary consumer | 12 | 6 |
| sparrowhawk | tertiary consumer | 1 | 1 |

**(a)** Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers for this hedgerow. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the pyramid of biomass has a different shape from the pyramid of numbers. **[2]**

**(c)** State **one** disadvantage of collecting data for a pyramid of biomass. **[1]**

**9.** In a cold ocean, small shrimp-like animals called krill feed on algae. Krill are eaten by fish, penguins and whales. Fish are eaten by seals and penguins. Seals are eaten by killer whales.

A fishing industry begins to harvest very large numbers of krill.

Use the food web to describe the possible effects of overharvesting krill on **two** other populations. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* In a kelp forest, producers transfer 32 000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ into new biomass. Primary consumers gain 3 520 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ and secondary consumers gain 288 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer from producers to primary consumers. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the efficiency of energy transfer from primary to secondary consumers. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a pyramid of energy for this kelp forest must be pyramid-shaped. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* One hectare of soya beans provides 3.65 × 10⁷ kJ of food energy per year if humans eat the beans. If the beans are fed to pigs instead, only 10% of this energy ends up in pork eaten by humans. One person needs 3.65 × 10⁶ kJ of food energy per year.

**(a)** Calculate the energy in the pork produced from one hectare. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many people could be fed for one year by the soya beans and by the pork. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why so much less energy reaches humans when the soya is fed to pigs. **[3]**

**12.** *(Extended)* On a grassland, lions hunt zebras.

**(a)** Explain why only a small proportion of the energy in a zebra population becomes part of the lion population. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy, why there are far fewer lions than zebras on the grassland. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Count from the producer: grass is the producer, grasshopper primary, frog secondary, snake tertiary.

**3.** The transfer of energy from one organism to the next / the direction of energy flow [1].

**4. (a)** An organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually using energy from sunlight, through photosynthesis [1].

**(b)** An organism that gets its energy by feeding on other organisms [1].

**5.** It is inverted: a narrow bar for one dog with a much wider bar for 300 fleas above it [1]; the pyramid of numbers does not take account of size, and one large dog supports many small fleas [1].

**6.** Any two from: it is never inverted; it shows the energy transferred over a period of time rather than at one moment; it allows the efficiency of energy transfer between levels to be compared [2].

**7. (a)** algae → tadpoles → dragonfly larvae → fish → herons [1].

**(b)** Fish [1]: secondary consumer (algae → tadpoles → fish) and tertiary consumer (algae → tadpoles → dragonfly larvae → fish) [1]. Or herons: tertiary and quaternary consumer.

**(c)** Dragonfly larvae decrease [1], because the new fish competes with them for tadpoles, so they have less food [1]. Algae increase [1], because fewer tadpoles are left to eat them [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each effect needs its reason. Accept "algae may decrease slightly if water snails increase", provided it is explained.

**8. (a)** Not a true pyramid [1]: the producer bar (20) is narrower than the primary consumer bar (4 000), then the bars narrow for blue tits (12) and the sparrowhawk (1) [1].

**(b)** A pyramid of biomass takes account of the size / mass of organisms [1]; each hawthorn bush has a very large mass, so the producer bar (3 500 g per m²) is the widest [1].

**(c)** Any one: organisms must be killed and dried; it is difficult to measure the mass of large organisms such as bushes; it shows the biomass at one moment only [1].

**9.** Any two populations, each with effect and reason, two marks each:

- whales decrease [1], because krill is a major food for them and there is less to eat [1]
- penguins decrease [1], because they lose krill as food and must eat more fish [1]
- fish decrease [1], because they have less krill to eat / penguins eat more fish [1]
- seals decrease [1], because there are fewer fish for them [1]
- algae increase [1], because fewer krill eat them [1]

*Examiner insight:* The effect on seals is indirect: it travels through fish. Answers that name a long chain of effects score well when every link is explained.

**10. (a)** 3 520 ÷ 32 000 × 100 [1] = 11% [1].

**(b)** 288 ÷ 3 520 × 100 [1] = 8.2% [1].

**(c)** Energy is lost at each transfer, for example as heat from respiration [1], so each trophic level receives less energy than the level below, and no bar can be wider than the one beneath it [1].

**11. (a)** 10% of 3.65 × 10⁷ = 3.65 × 10⁶ kJ [1].

**(b)** Soya: 3.65 × 10⁷ ÷ 3.65 × 10⁶ = 10 people; pork: 3.65 × 10⁶ ÷ 3.65 × 10⁶ = 1 person [2].

**(c)** Eating pork adds an extra trophic level / an extra energy transfer [1]. The pigs release energy in respiration, which is lost as heat, including for movement and keeping warm [1]. Energy is also lost in the pigs' faeces and urine / in parts of the pigs not eaten [1].

**12. (a)** Any three from:

- not all parts of the zebra are eaten, such as bones and hide [1]
- some of the eaten food is not digested and is lost in faeces [1]
- energy is lost in excretory products such as urea [1]
- zebras released much of their energy in respiration, transferred as heat, before being eaten [1]
- lions use energy for movement when hunting, which is lost as heat [1]

**(b)** Only a small proportion of the energy in the zebras is transferred to lions [1], so there is enough energy to support only a small number of lions [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a chain with the arrows pointing to the prey.
- Predicting a change in a web with no reason.
- Describing a pyramid shape without using the figures.
- Dividing by the higher level in efficiency calculations.
- Saying energy is "used up" instead of transferred as heat.
