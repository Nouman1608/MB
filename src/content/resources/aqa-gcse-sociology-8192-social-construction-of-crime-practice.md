These questions cover AQA GCSE Sociology (8192) section 3.5.1, *The social construction of crime and
deviance*: how crime and deviance are defined by society; anomie, labelling, structural, subcultural
and interactionist explanations; the key ideas of Merton and Becker; and functionalist,
interactionist, feminist and Marxist perspectives. Tariffs of 1, 2, 3, 4 and 12 follow the style of
Paper 2.

## Questions

**1.** Which term did Merton use to describe a situation in which the usual rules are abandoned in
the pursuit of success?

A. Status frustration
B. Anomie
C. Master status
D. Deviant career

**[1]**

**2.** In Becker's theory, what is a *master status*?

A. The highest social class position
B. An identity that overrides all others in how a person is seen
C. A status gained through educational qualifications
D. A role in a delinquent subculture

**[1]**

**3.** Describe what sociologists mean when they say crime and deviance are *socially constructed*.
**[3]**

**4.** Identify and explain **one** of Merton's adaptations to the strain between goals and means,
other than conformity. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** agents of social control that, according to Becker, have the power to make a
label stick. **[2]**

**6.** *Item B (invented for practice)*

*A sociologist spent six months as a volunteer youth worker in a town, observing how police and
shopkeepers responded to groups of teenagers. She found that young people from one estate were
regularly moved on and searched, while others behaving similarly elsewhere were ignored. Several
young people from the estate told her, "If they think we're trouble, we might as well be."*

- **(a)** From **Item B**, examine **one** strength of the research. **[2]**
- **(b)** Identify and explain how **Item B** supports Becker's labelling theory. **[4]**

**7.** Identify and explain **one** criticism of Merton's theory. **[4]**

**8.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that deviance is created by the reactions of others.
**[12]**

**9.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that crime is mainly caused by the structure of
society. **[12]**

## Answers and mark guidance

**1. [1]** B.

**2. [1]** B.

**3. [3]** Crime and deviance are not fixed qualities of acts (1); what counts as criminal or
deviant is defined by members of society and those with power (1), and varies between societies,
over time and between situations — for example acts accepted in one culture may be illegal in
another, or behaviour once legal may later be criminalised (1).

**4. [4]** 1 for identifying, up to 3 for explanation. For example **innovation** (1): people accept
the cultural goal of material success (1) but lack legitimate means such as qualifications (1), so
they turn to illegitimate means such as theft or fraud; Merton saw this as most likely among lower
classes (1). Accept ritualism, retreatism or rebellion explained correctly.

**5. [2]** Any two: police, courts, teachers, social workers, probation officers. 1 mark each.

**6(a). [2]** One strength examined: six months of participant observation (as a volunteer) gives
in-depth, valid data about everyday interaction (1), revealing how labels are applied in practice,
which a questionnaire would miss (1). Or: young people's own words show the meanings they give to
their behaviour.

**6(b). [4]** Identify (1): the item shows police and shopkeepers labelling teenagers from one
estate. Explain (up to 3): the reaction depends on who commits the act and where, not the act itself
— as Becker argued (1); agents of control (police) make the label stick through searches (1); the
quotation "we might as well be" shows a self-fulfilling prophecy, the start of a deviant career (1).

**7. [4]** 1 for identifying, up to 3 for explanation. For example: Merton **ignores power** (1) —
he does not ask who makes the laws and who benefits from them (1), which Marxists argue is central
(1); laws may protect the property of the ruling class (1). Or: assumes **value consensus**; is
**deterministic** — does not explain why only some people experiencing anomie turn to crime;
**exaggerates working-class crime** and underestimates white-collar crime.

**8. [12]** Level guidance: 10–12 detailed, developed, a range of perspectives, supported
conclusion; 7–9 sound with some evaluation; 4–6 limited or one-sided; 1–3 basic.

*Agree (interactionist):*
- Becker (1963): an act becomes deviant when others label it so; labels depend on who, when, where
  and who observes.
- Agents of control have the power to make labels stick; master status; self-fulfilling prophecy;
  deviant career.
- Selective policing may explain why some groups appear more often in statistics.

*Disagree:*
- Functionalists: Merton (1938) — strain between goals and means causes deviance before any
  reaction; Albert Cohen — status frustration and subcultures.
- Marxists: deviance is caused by capitalism's inequality and greed, and laws reflect ruling-class
  interests.
- Feminists: gender patterns in crime are explained by patriarchal control (Heidensohn), not just
  labels.
- Labelling does not explain why the first act happens and can ignore real victims.

*Conclusion:* reactions shape deviant identities and statistics, but other causes explain the
original behaviour.

**9. [12]** Same level guidance.

*Agree (structural):*
- Merton: the structure of society — unequal access to legitimate means — produces anomie and crime.
- Albert Cohen: working-class position and educational failure produce status frustration.
- Marxists: capitalist structure creates inequality and defines crime in ruling-class interests.
- Feminists: patriarchal structure controls women and shapes gendered patterns of crime.

*Disagree:*
- Interactionists (Becker): crime results from labelling in interaction, not just structure.
- Not everyone in the same structural position commits crime, so individual choices and meanings
  matter.
- Carlen (1988): women in her study made rational choices when the rewards of conformity failed.

*Conclusion:* structure helps explain patterns, but interaction and choice explain individual
cases.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which key sociologists does 3.5.1 name?** Merton (functionalist) and Becker (interactionist).

**How do I evaluate in 12-mark answers?** Compare at least two perspectives, use the key
sociologists' ideas and criticisms, and finish with a judgement on "how far".

**What counts as a strength of the research in Item B?** Any methodological strength linked to the
item, such as validity from long-term observation.
