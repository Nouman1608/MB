## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**
**(a)** Define a barrier to communication. **[1]**
**(b)** Define feedback. **[1]**

**2.** Give two methods of communication that use information technology. **[2]**

**3.** The warehouse manager at Dovecote Foods says: "I get 200 emails a day, so I missed the message about the urgent order." State the barrier to communication shown. **[1]**

**4.** A business sends 8,000 invoices a year. 2.5% contain errors, and each costs 18 to correct. Calculate the yearly cost of the errors. **[2]**

**5.** An instruction passes through three levels of management. At each level 15% of the detail is lost. Calculate the percentage of the detail that reaches the final receiver, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** Hartley Kitchens received a complaint from a customer whose new kitchen was fitted late. Outline one limitation of replying by letter. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Explain one problem caused by ineffective communication with suppliers. **[3]**

**8.** Altamira Hotels plc has 40 hotels in eight countries. Head office sends all instructions in one language, by email, to hotel managers, who pass them on to staff.
**(a)** State two barriers to communication that may arise. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse how one of these barriers could be removed. **[4]**

**9.** Kelso Builders' site managers phone material orders to the office. Because of misunderstandings, 5% of its 1,600 orders a year are wrong, and each wrong order costs 120 to put right. It has two options.
- Option A: an ordering app costing 3,000 a year, expected to cut errors to 1%.
- Option B: a one-off training course costing 1,000, after which managers confirm every order by email, expected to cut errors to 2%.

**(a)** Calculate the current yearly cost of wrong orders. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the net saving in the first year from each option. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify which option Kelso Builders should choose. **[6]**

**10.** Fennel and Thyme is a small restaurant with 12 staff. Analyse the importance of good communication to Fennel and Thyme. **[6]**

**11.** Brightline Insurance plc has offices in six cities. Its directors plan to replace most face-to-face meetings with video calls. Evaluate whether this is a good decision. **[8]**

**12.** Explain how a failure of technology can be a barrier to communication. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Anything that stops a message reaching the receiver or being understood as intended [1].
**(b)** The receiver's response to a message, which shows whether it was understood [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: email [1], video calls [1], the intranet, a website, social media, text messages. **[2]**

**3.** Information overload [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 2.5% × 8,000 = 200 errors [1]; 200 × 18 = **3,600** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 0.85 × 0.85 × 0.85 = 0.614 [1]; **61.4%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A letter is slow to arrive and there is no instant reply [1], so the customer, already unhappy about the delay, may feel ignored and complain further or post a bad review [1]. **[2]**

**7.** If orders sent to suppliers are unclear, the wrong materials or quantities may be delivered [1]. Production may then stop while the business waits for the right items [1], so customer orders are late and sales could be lost [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Any two of: language differences for staff whose first language is different [1]; information overload for managers receiving many emails [1]; too many levels as managers pass messages on; email misunderstood with no chance to ask questions.
**(b)** Language barrier: head office could translate key instructions into each country's main language [1], so staff understand them without relying on a manager's interpretation [1]. Using simple wording and pictures for safety instructions also helps [1]. Staff then follow procedures correctly, so guests receive the same standard of service in every hotel [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** 5% × 1,600 = 80 [1]; 80 × 120 = **9,600** [1].
**(b)** Option A: errors 16 × 120 = 1,920; saving 9,600 − 1,920 − 3,000 = **4,680** [1]. Option B: errors 32 × 120 = 3,840; saving 9,600 − 3,840 − 1,000 = **4,760** [1].
**(c)** Option B is the better choice [1]. It saves 80 more than Option A in the first year [1], and because its 1,000 cost is one-off, from the second year it saves 9,600 − 3,840 = 5,760 a year against 4,680 for the app, 1,080 more [1]. The training also leaves site managers with better habits that stay with the business [1]. Option A has the lower error rate (1% against 2%) and does not rely on managers remembering to send emails [1], so the app would be better if confirmation emails are not kept up; Kelso should check after a few months that errors really have fallen to 2% [1]. **[10]**

**10.** Staff must know which dishes are ordered and any allergies [1]; if the kitchen misunderstands an order, food is wasted and customers wait [1], and a mistake over allergies could harm a customer and damage the restaurant's reputation [1]. With only 12 staff, the owner can brief everyone face to face before each shift [1], so problems are spotted quickly and staff feel involved [1], which supports morale and good service, leading to repeat customers [1]. **[6]**

**11.** For: video calls save travel time and costs between six cities [1], so managers can meet more often and decisions are made faster [1]. They still allow visual cues, unlike email or phone [1]. Against: video calls depend on reliable connections and equipment [1], and technical failures can waste meeting time [1]. Some discussions, such as sensitive staff issues or building trust with new colleagues, work better in person [1]. Judgement: for example, a good decision for routine meetings, but Brightline should keep some face-to-face meetings for sensitive matters and team building [2]. **[8]**

**12.** Many messages depend on systems such as email or the intranet [1]. If the system fails, messages are not delivered or are delayed [1], so staff may not receive instructions in time; the business can remove this barrier with back-up methods such as telephone calls [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a barrier without how it arises or how to remove it.
- Giving a benefit of a method with no limitation, when both are asked for.
- Forgetting one-off and yearly costs behave differently over time.
- Writing "communication is important" with no effect on the business named.
- Ending "evaluate" or "justify" answers without a decision.
