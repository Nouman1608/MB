## What 3.1.7 asks of you

The specification has one content statement for this section: how to prepare the internal financial statements of limited liability companies. The additional information spells out what that means.

| Part of 3.1.7 | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| The statements | Draw up three statements -- the income statement, the statement of changes in equity, the statement of financial position -- none of them intended for publication |
| Income statement | Show three profit lines: profit from operations; profit for the year before tax; then profit for the year after tax, in that order |
| Statement of changes in equity | Start from opening balances; add share issues (any premium included) and the year's profit, or deduct a loss; deduct dividends paid; finish with closing balances |
| Statement of financial position | Group every item under five sub-headings: current assets, non-current assets, equity, non-current liabilities, current liabilities |
| Exclusions | Preference shares and general reserves are not examined. Rights issues, bonus issues and revaluation of non-current assets are not examined at AS level. |

## How the three statements connect

Think of the three statements as a chain. Each one passes a figure to the next.

1. The **income statement** ends with profit for the year after tax.
2. That figure is one line in the **statement of changes in equity**, where it is added to retained earnings and dividends paid are taken away.
3. The closing balances from the statement of changes in equity become the **equity** section of the **statement of financial position**.

So prepare them in that order.

## Equity in a limited company

Where a sole trader keeps a single capital account, a company records its owners' stake in several separate balances. Section 3.1.7 uses three of them.

- **Ordinary share capital.** The number of shares issued multiplied by their nominal value (the face value printed on the share).
- **Share premium.** The extra received when shares are sold for more than nominal value. It belongs to the shareholders, so it sits in equity.
- **Retained earnings.** The total of profits after tax that the company has kept since it started, after dividends paid.

Owners of a company are rewarded by **dividends**, not drawings. A dividend is a share of profit given to shareholders. It is not a cost of earning revenue, so it never goes in the income statement.

### Worked example 1: issuing shares at a premium

Quillmere Data Ltd issues 80,000 ordinary shares with a nominal value of 0.25 each. Investors pay 0.90 per share in full.

```
Cash received   80,000 x 0.90          = 72,000
Share capital   80,000 x 0.25          = 20,000
Share premium   80,000 x (0.90 - 0.25) = 52,000
```

The double entry is:

```
Dr Bank                    72,000
   Cr Ordinary share capital      20,000
   Cr Share premium               52,000
```

Check: 20,000 + 52,000 = 72,000. Both credits are equity. The premium is not trading income, so it stays out of the income statement.

## The income statement of a limited company

Down to gross profit, nothing changes from a sole trader. Below that, a company must show three separate profit figures.

```
Revenue
Less: Cost of sales
Gross profit
Less: Expenses (wages, directors' fees, insurance,
      depreciation, irrecoverable debts ...)
Profit from operations
Less: Finance costs (loan and debenture interest)
Profit for the year before tax
Less: Taxation
Profit for the year after tax
```

- **Profit from operations** tells you what the trading activity earned before any interest is paid on borrowing.
- **Profit for the year before tax** shows the result after the cost of finance.
- **Profit for the year after tax** is what is left for shareholders; it moves into the statement of changes in equity.

Interest on a loan or debentures is charged for the whole year (the accruals concept), even if part is unpaid; the unpaid part is a current liability. A company pays tax on its own profit, so tax is charged in its income statement, and tax still owed at the year end is a current liability.

## The statement of changes in equity

This statement shows why each part of equity moved during the year. Use a column for each component and a total column.

```
                           Ordinary    Share   Retained
                      share capital  premium   earnings   Total
Balances b/f                    ...      ...        ...     ...
Issue of ordinary shares        ...      ...                ...
Profit (loss) after tax                             ...     ...
Dividends paid                                    (...)   (...)
Balances c/f                    ...      ...        ...     ...
```

If the company made a loss, show it in brackets in the profit row; it reduces retained earnings. If shares were issued during the year and the trial balance already includes them, take the issue away from the trial balance figures to find the opening balances.

## The statement of financial position

Use the five sub-headings named in the specification. One common order is:

- **Non-current assets**, each shown at carrying amount, which is cost minus accumulated depreciation.
- **Current assets**: inventory; trade receivables net of the provision for doubtful debts; prepayments and income due (other receivables); cash and cash equivalents.
- **Equity**: ordinary share capital, share premium, retained earnings, from the closing balances of the statement of changes in equity.
- **Non-current liabilities**: loans and debentures due more than 12 months after the year end.
- **Current liabilities**: trade payables, other payables (accrued expenses and interest), taxation owing, bank overdraft, and any part of a loan due within 12 months.

The last point matters. If a loan is repaid in instalments, split it: the instalment due within the next 12 months is current, the rest is non-current.

4. Land is not depreciated. Vehicles are depreciated at 25% a year on cost (straight line). Equipment is depreciated at 20% a year (reducing balance).

## What 3.1.7 leaves out

Preference shares and general reserves sit outside the examined content, according to the specification, so equity in a 3.1.7 question uses only the three components above. Rights issues, bonus issues and the revaluation of non-current assets are not examined at AS level. They appear in 3.2.6 Accounting for limited companies, which is International A-level only, together with statements of cash flow, schedules of non-current assets and published accounts.

## Common errors

- Deducting dividends paid in the income statement. They belong only in the statement of changes in equity.
- Charging the interest actually paid rather than the full year's interest, then forgetting the amount owing under current liabilities.
- Recording the whole issue price as share capital, so share premium is missing.
- Carrying profit before tax into retained earnings instead of profit after tax.
- Leaving the tax charge out of current liabilities when it has not yet been paid.
- Treating an entire instalment loan as non-current when part is due within 12 months.
- Using trial balance share figures as opening balances when the trial balance already includes an issue made during the year.
