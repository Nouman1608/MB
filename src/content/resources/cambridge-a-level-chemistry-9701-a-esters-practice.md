## Section A

**1.** State the reagents and conditions used to make phenyl benzoate by the method in subtopic
33.2. **[2]**

**2.** Name each ester.

**(a)** CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** C₆H₅COOCH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**3.** State the inorganic product of the reaction between an acyl chloride and an alcohol, and
how its formation would be observed. **[2]**

**4.** Write an equation for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with butan-1-ol. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the reaction of an acyl chloride with an alcohol gives a higher yield of ester
than the reaction of a carboxylic acid with the same alcohol. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Ester **X** has the structural formula C₆H₅COOCH(CH₃)₂.

**(a)** Name ester **X**. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the acyl chloride and the alcohol needed to make **X** at room temperature. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the equation for this reaction. **[1]**

**(d)** **X** could also be made from a carboxylic acid. State the reagents and conditions for that
route, and give one reason why it gives a lower yield. **[3]**

**7.** Phenol reacts usefully with ethanoyl chloride but not with ethanoic acid.

**(a)** Explain why the oxygen atom of phenol is a weaker nucleophile than the oxygen atom of
ethanol. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why ethanoyl chloride is attacked more readily by nucleophiles than ethanoic acid
is. **[2]**

**(c)** Write the equation for the reaction of phenol with ethanoyl chloride and name the organic
product. **[2]**

**8.** A student reacts 3.76 g of phenol with excess ethanoyl chloride and obtains 4.62 g of phenyl
ethanoate, CH₃COOC₆H₅. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**9.** Ethyl propanoate is to be made from propanoic acid in two steps.

**(a)** State a reagent for step 1, converting propanoic acid into propanoyl chloride, and write
the equation for the reaction with your chosen reagent. **[2]**

**(b)** State the reagent and conditions for step 2. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one practical advantage of using SOCl₂ in step 1. **[1]**

**10.** Ethyl ethanoate and methyl propanoate both have the molecular formula C₄H₈O₂.

**(a)** Name the acyl chloride and alcohol needed to make each ester. **[2]**

**(b)** State the relationship between the two esters and explain your answer. **[1]**

**11.** Phenyl benzoate is heated with excess NaOH(aq) until no ester remains.

**(a)** Name the two organic products present in the alkaline solution. **[2]**

**(b)** The solution is then acidified with dilute HCl. Name the two organic products formed. **[1]**

**12.** Ethane-1,2-diol, HOCH₂CH₂OH, reacts with excess ethanoyl chloride to give a diester.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of diester that could be formed from 3.10 g of
ethane-1,2-diol. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Benzoyl chloride and phenol [1]; room temperature, no catalyst needed [1].

**2. (a)** Propyl ethanoate [1].

**(b)** Ethyl benzoate [1].

**3.** Hydrogen chloride, HCl [1]; seen as steamy (misty) fumes [1].

**4.** CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ + HCl: correct organic product (butyl
ethanoate) [1]; HCl as the other product, balanced [1].

**5.** The acyl chloride reaction is not reversible (HCl is lost and the ester does not react back)
[1]; the carboxylic acid reaction reaches an equilibrium, so it cannot go to completion [1].

**6. (a)** Propan-2-yl benzoate (accept 1-methylethyl benzoate) [1].

**(b)** Benzoyl chloride [1]; propan-2-ol [1].

**(c)** C₆H₅COCl + (CH₃)₂CHOH → C₆H₅COOCH(CH₃)₂ + HCl [1].

**(d)** Benzoic acid and propan-2-ol [1]; heat with a concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst [1]; the
reaction is reversible / reaches equilibrium, so conversion is incomplete (accept: slow reaction)
[1].

**7. (a)** A lone pair on the oxygen of phenol overlaps with the π system of the ring and is partly
delocalised into it [1], so electron density on the oxygen is lower and the lone pair is less
available to attack an electrophile [1].

**(b)** In ethanoyl chloride the carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, O and Cl
[1], so it carries a larger δ+ charge (and Cl⁻ is a good leaving group) [1].

**(c)** CH₃COCl + C₆H₅OH → CH₃COOC₆H₅ + HCl [1]; phenyl ethanoate [1].

**8.** n(phenol) = 3.76 / 94.0 = 0.0400 mol [1]; Mᵣ(C₈H₈O₂) = 136.0, so theoretical mass =
0.0400 × 136.0 = 5.44 g [1]; percentage yield = 4.62 / 5.44 × 100 = 84.9% [1].

**9. (a)** Any one of PCl₅, PCl₃ (with heat) or SOCl₂ [1], with a matching equation [1]:
CH₃CH₂COOH + PCl₅ → CH₃CH₂COCl + POCl₃ + HCl, or
3CH₃CH₂COOH + PCl₃ → 3CH₃CH₂COCl + H₃PO₃, or
CH₃CH₂COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃CH₂COCl + SO₂ + HCl.

**(b)** Ethanol at room temperature [1].

**(c)** Both by-products, SO₂ and HCl, are gases, so they escape and the acyl chloride is easier to
separate [1].

**10. (a)** Ethyl ethanoate: ethanoyl chloride and ethanol [1]; methyl propanoate: propanoyl
chloride and methanol [1].

**(b)** Structural isomers [1]: same molecular formula, different arrangement of atoms (the acyl and
alkyl parts are swapped in size).

**11. (a)** Sodium benzoate [1]; sodium phenoxide [1].

**(b)** Benzoic acid and phenol (both needed) [1].

**12. (a)** HOCH₂CH₂OH + 2CH₃COCl → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂OOCCH₃ + 2HCl: correct diester [1]; balanced with
2CH₃COCl and 2HCl [1].

**(b)** n(diol) = 3.10 / 62.0 = 0.0500 mol, and the diester forms 1 : 1 [1]; Mᵣ(C₆H₁₀O₄) = 146.0,
so mass = 0.0500 × 146.0 = 7.30 g [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding concentrated H₂SO₄ or heat to the acyl chloride route: the conditions are room temperature
  only.
- Writing water rather than HCl as the by-product.
- Naming the ester in formula order: CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ is propyl ethanoate, not "methyl butanoate".
- Using a 1 : 1 ratio for a diol: each –OH needs its own molecule of acyl chloride.
- Forgetting that phenol, being acidic, ends up as the phenoxide ion in excess NaOH(aq).
- Explaining phenol's low reactivity with "phenol is unreactive" instead of the delocalised oxygen
  lone pair.
