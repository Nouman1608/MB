Condensed notes for the poetry essay on place. For the full method and a model reading, use the
[Unit 2 Section B study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-2-section-b/).

## The essay in one box

- **One** essay on your poet's selection: **25 marks**, half of Unit 2.
- **Open book**; AO1, AO2 and AO3 **weighted equally**.
- About **an hour**. Usually two poems in depth plus brief range, unless the question says
  otherwise.

## Four poets and their places

| Poet | Typical places | Recurring concerns |
|---|---|---|
| Wordsworth | The Wye valley; the Lake District; London from a bridge; Scottish rivers | Nature, memory, the growth of the mind, rural life and loss |
| Frost | New England farms, woods, walls and snowfields | Boundaries, work, isolation, choice, nature's indifference |
| Hardy | Dorset and Cornwall; churchyards; the open sea | Memory, grief, time, chance, the modern world's arrival |
| Heaney | Rural County Derry; Irish bogs; Jutland | Childhood, language, history in the land, political violence |

The selections are 8 poems (Wordsworth), 12 (Frost), 15 (Hardy) and 15 (Heaney) in Version 5.3.

## Poems to pair by idea

- **Return and memory**: "Tintern Abbey"; Hardy's "After a Journey" and "At Castle Boterel".
- **Boundaries**: "Mending Wall"; Heaney's "Act of Union".
- **Home lost or threatened**: "Michael"; "Mid-Term Break"; "The Toome Road".
- **Nature damaged or indifferent**: "A Brook in the City"; "Desert Places"; "Channel Firing";
  "Nutting".
- **History in the land**: "Requiem for the Croppies"; "Bogland"; "The Tollund Man".
- **City and stillness**: "Composed Upon Westminster Bridge".

Choose poems that differ within a shared idea, so comparison produces an argument.

## How poems build place

1. **Naming**: place-names carry history and sound ("Anahorish", "Beeny Cliff").
2. **Speaker's position**: where and when the speaker stands.
3. **Time layered on place**: past and present tenses side by side.
4. **Sensory detail**: sound, light and texture compressing a whole landscape.
5. **Form and structure**: sonnet, blank verse, quatrains, free verse; line breaks tracing movement.
6. **Imagery and symbol**: a wall, a tree, a bog, a ship.
7. **Voice and dialect**: local words that root the poem in a community.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pastoral | Poetry idealising rural life |
| Topographical poem | A poem about a specific place or view |
| Blank verse | Unrhymed iambic pentameter ("Tintern Abbey", "Mending Wall") |
| Sonnet | Fourteen-line poem with a turn ("Westminster Bridge") |
| Enjambment | A sentence running over a line break |
| Caesura | A pause within a line |
| Elegy | A poem of mourning (Hardy's poems of 1912-13) |
| Dinnseanchas | Irish tradition of place-name lore, often linked to Heaney's place-name poems |
| Persona | The voice or speaker of a poem |
| Volta | The turn in a poem's argument |

## Context in one line each

- **Wordsworth**: Romantic belief in nature's power to shape the mind; the *Lyrical Ballads* era.
- **Frost**: New England rural life; plain speech in strict forms.
- **Hardy**: grief after Emma Hardy's death in 1912; distrust of progress; the *Titanic* (1912) in
  "The Convergence of the Twain".
- **Heaney**: rural Catholic childhood in Northern Ireland; the Troubles; P. V. Glob's book on the
  preserved bodies of Iron Age Denmark behind the bog poems.

## Essay shape

1. One-sentence answer to the question.
2. Two poems in depth, chosen because they differ within a shared idea.
3. Brief reference to a third poem that tests or extends the argument.
4. Methods analysed in each: naming, position, time, form, imagery.
5. Context only where it explains the words; a conclusion that weighs the view.

## Exam traps

- Writing on one poem when the question and selection invite range.
- Paraphrasing what the poem says instead of analysing how it says it.
- Biography with no link to the words.
- Ignoring form and sound.
- Comparison as two separate summaries.
- Copying whole stanzas from the open book.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Unit 2 Section B worth?
2. Which Wordsworth poem is a sonnet about a city at dawn?
3. In which poem by Frost do two neighbours repair a boundary each spring?
4. Which life event lies behind Hardy's poems of 1912-13?
5. Which Heaney poem builds itself around an Irish place-name?
6. Name two of Heaney's bog poems in the selection.
7. What is a topographical poem?
8. What does layering time on place mean? Give an example.
9. Give two ways form can contribute to a poem's sense of place.
10. Which Hardy poem concerns the *Titanic*?
11. Name one Section B poem that presents place as political space, and explain why.
12. What is the usual default for how many poems to discuss?

## Answers

1. 25 marks, half of the 50-mark unit.
2. "Composed Upon Westminster Bridge".
3. "Mending Wall".
4. The death of his first wife, Emma, in 1912.
5. "Anahorish".
6. Any two: "Bogland"; "The Tollund Man"; "Bog Queen"; "The Grauballe Man"; "Punishment".
7. A poem about a specific place or view.
8. A poem presents a place as it is now and as it was, so the landscape holds memory; for example,
   Wordsworth revisiting the Wye after five years, or Hardy returning to the Cornish places of his
   courtship.
9. Any two: line breaks and enjambment tracing movement or a view; rhyme and metre containing or
   resisting a landscape; stanza shape reflecting a structure such as a wall or a path; a volta
   marking a shift in how the place is seen.
10. "The Convergence of the Twain".
11. For example, "The Toome Road": armoured vehicles moving through Heaney's home ground make the
    landscape a site of military power and local resistance.
12. Two poems in depth with brief reference to a third, unless the question gives an instruction.
