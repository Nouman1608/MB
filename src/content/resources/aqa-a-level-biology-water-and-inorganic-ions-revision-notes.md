## The one idea behind 3.1.7

Water is polar: δ− oxygen, δ+ hydrogen. Neighbouring molecules are joined by hydrogen bonds. Each bond is weak, but there are huge numbers, so together they give water its biological properties.

## 3.1.7 Water: property, reason, role

| Property | Reason | Biological role |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolite | Reactant in hydrolysis; product of condensation | Builds and breaks polymers; also used in photosynthesis |
| Solvent | Polar water molecules surround ions and polar molecules | Reactions in solution; transport in blood, tissue fluid and xylem |
| Relatively high heat capacity | Energy goes into disrupting hydrogen bonds, so temperature rises slowly | Buffers temperature change in cells, blood and habitats |
| Relatively large latent heat of vaporisation | Hydrogen bonds must be broken for water to evaporate; escaping molecules take energy | Cooling with little water loss (sweating, panting, transpiration) |
| Strong cohesion | Hydrogen bonding between water molecules | Continuous columns in xylem; surface tension at an air–water boundary |

### Definitions to recite

- **Metabolite:** a substance taking part in, or made by, a metabolic reaction.
- **Heat capacity (specific):** energy needed to raise the temperature of a given mass by 1 °C.
- **Latent heat of vaporisation:** energy needed to turn a liquid into a gas at constant temperature.
- **Cohesion:** attraction between molecules of the same kind.
- **Surface tension:** the skin-like surface where water meets air, caused by cohesion.

### Explaining each property in steps

**Cooling by evaporation.**
1. Water molecules at the surface need energy to escape the liquid.
2. They must break hydrogen bonds with neighbours, which takes a lot of energy.
3. The escaping molecules carry that energy away.
4. The surface left behind is cooler, and a small mass of water has removed a large quantity of heat.

**Temperature buffering.**
1. Heat supplied first disrupts hydrogen bonds.
2. Less energy is left to increase molecular movement.
3. So temperature rises slowly (and falls slowly).
4. Enzymes in cells and aquatic organisms meet smaller temperature swings.

**Water in the xylem.**
1. Hydrogen bonds make water molecules cohesive.
2. Water leaving the leaf pulls on the column below.
3. The column stays unbroken and moves as one chain through the tube-like transport cells.

### Calculation methods

| Quantity | Relationship | Reminder |
|---|---|---|
| Heating or cooling | energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change | Match units: g with J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹ |
| Evaporation | energy = mass evaporated × latent heat of vaporisation | Same units for mass and latent heat |
| Volume in a tube | volume = π × radius² × length | Use the radius, not the diameter |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 | Divide by the starting value |

**Reminder 1.** 200 g of water absorbs 8.36 kJ. Specific heat capacity is 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.
Temperature rise = 8 360 ÷ (200 × 4.18) = 8 360 ÷ 836 = **10 °C**.

**Reminder 2.** 15 g of water evaporates. Latent heat is 2.4 kJ g⁻¹.
Energy removed = 15 × 2.4 = **36 kJ**.

**Reminder 3.** A polypeptide of 60 amino acids has 59 peptide bonds. Condensation released **59** water molecules and complete hydrolysis uses **59**.

## 3.1.8 Inorganic ions

Ions occur in solution in cytoplasm and body fluids. Some are at high concentrations, others at very low concentrations. Each type has a role that depends on its properties.

| Ion | Where | Role |
|---|---|---|
| H⁺ | Cytoplasm, body fluids | Concentration sets pH; affects R-group charges, bonding and enzyme shape |
| Fe²⁺ | Haem group of haemoglobin | Binds oxygen reversibly, one O₂ per Fe²⁺ |
| Na⁺ | Ileum epithelial cells | Co-transport of glucose and amino acids |
| Phosphate | DNA, RNA, ATP | Backbone link (phosphodiester bond); Pi released by ATP hydrolysis |

### Hydrogen ions and pH

- pH = −log₁₀[H⁺]. Low pH = high [H⁺].
- Each unit is a factor of 10. pH 6 has 100 times the H⁺ of pH 8.
- Away from the optimum pH, ionic and hydrogen bonds in the tertiary structure change, the active site changes shape, and the enzyme can denature.

### Iron ions and haemoglobin

- Four polypeptides, four haem groups, four Fe²⁺ ions.
- One haemoglobin binds up to four O₂ molecules.
- Binding is reversible: loading in the lungs, unloading in respiring tissues.
- Too little dietary iron gives too little haemoglobin, so less oxygen is carried.

### Sodium ions and co-transport (ileum)

1. Sodium-potassium pump uses ATP to remove Na⁺ from the cell into the blood.
2. Inside the cell Na⁺ concentration is low, so Na⁺ diffuses in from the lumen.
3. The co-transporter protein carries glucose (or an amino acid) in with Na⁺.
4. Glucose builds up inside the cell and leaves into the blood by facilitated diffusion.

### Phosphate ions

- DNA and RNA nucleotides each contain a phosphate group.
- A condensation reaction joins the phosphate of one nucleotide to the pentose of the next: a **phosphodiester bond**.
- ATP has ribose, adenine and three phosphate groups. ATP + H₂O → ADP + Pi (ATP hydrolase). Pi can phosphorylate other compounds, often making them more reactive.
- ATP is resynthesised by condensation of ADP and Pi.

**Reminder 4.** A single DNA strand of 15 nucleotides has 14 phosphodiester bonds, so 14 water molecules were released as it formed.

## Data reminder: reading a pH table

| pH | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Rate (units) | 14 | 33 | 38 | 12 |

- Highest rate at pH 7, so the optimum is near 7 (between 6 and 8 cannot be ruled out).
- Rate at pH 6 as a percentage of the maximum = 33 ÷ 38 × 100 = **86.8%**.
- Percentage decrease from pH 7 to pH 8 = (38 − 12) ÷ 38 × 100 = **68.4%**.
- Describe first (quote figures), then explain (H⁺, R groups, active site shape).

## Answer frames worth rehearsing

**Explain why water is useful as a coolant in organisms.** Hydrogen bonds must be broken for water to evaporate; this needs a lot of energy; escaping molecules take it away; so cooling occurs with little loss of water.

**Explain how sodium ions help glucose enter an epithelial cell.** The pump lowers Na⁺ inside the cell; Na⁺ moves in down its gradient through a co-transporter; glucose is carried in with it against its own gradient.

**Explain the importance of iron in haemoglobin.** Each haem group contains Fe²⁺; Fe²⁺ binds one O₂ reversibly; so each haemoglobin carries up to four O₂ and releases them in tissues.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Hydrolysis and condensation | Hydrolysis uses water; condensation releases water |
| Heat capacity and latent heat | Temperature rise versus change of state |
| Cohesion and surface tension | Cohesion is the attraction; surface tension is the result at a surface |
| Hydrogen bond and covalent bond | Between molecules, weak; within a molecule, strong |
| Active transport and co-transport | Active uses ATP at a carrier; co-transport uses a gradient set up by the pump |

## Quick self-test

1. What is a metabolite? Give one reaction in which water is released.
2. 120 g of water absorbs 2.508 kJ. Calculate the temperature rise (4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹).
3. Calculate the energy removed when 25 g of water evaporates (2.4 kJ g⁻¹).
4. Which has more H⁺, pH 6 or pH 8, and by what factor?
5. A sample contains 5 000 haemoglobin molecules. How many O₂ molecules can they carry at most?
6. How many phosphodiester bonds are in a single strand of 40 nucleotides?
7. Write the word equation for hydrolysis of ATP and name the enzyme.
8. Why does glucose absorption in the ileum slow if the sodium-potassium pump is inhibited?
9. Explain why a small insect can stand on water.
10. An enzyme's rate falls from 40 to 25 units when the pH changes. Calculate the percentage decrease.

### Answers

1. A substance taking part in or produced by metabolism; condensation releases water (for example forming a glycosidic bond).
2. 2 508 ÷ (120 × 4.18) = 2 508 ÷ 501.6 = **5.0 °C**.
3. 25 × 2.4 = **60 kJ**.
4. **pH 6**, with 100 times the H⁺ concentration.
5. 5 000 × 4 = **20 000** molecules.
6. 40 − 1 = **39**.
7. ATP + water → ADP + inorganic phosphate (Pi); ATP hydrolase.
8. Less Na⁺ leaves the cell, so the Na⁺ gradient is lost, so less co-transport of glucose.
9. Strong cohesion between water molecules at the surface produces surface tension, a skin that supports a light animal.
10. (40 − 25) ÷ 40 × 100 = **37.5%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "heat capacity" and giving no reason involving hydrogen bonds.
- Saying evaporation cools "because heat is lost" with no mention of the energy taken by escaping molecules.
- Forgetting that the cooling effect only occurs if the water evaporates.
- Mixing grams with kilograms or kJ with J in energy calculations.
- Using the diameter instead of the radius in a volume calculation.
- Describing pH as "acidity" without linking it to H⁺ concentration and protein shape.
- Saying iron "carries" oxygen when it is the Fe²⁺ in haem that binds it, reversibly.
- Saying co-transport uses ATP directly.
- Leaving out the pump when explaining why glucose enters against its gradient.
