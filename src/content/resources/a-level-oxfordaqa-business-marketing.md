This guide covers **Topic 2 Marketing**, the second of five
International AS topics in OxfordAQA International AS and A-level
Business (9625), first teaching September 2018. It follows directly
from Topic 1's introduction to business objectives and the external
environment, applying that context to how a business identifies and
reaches its customers.

**A note on the two codes:** this guide primarily follows **9625**,
the code current students are examined against. **9725** genuinely
changes marketing content on several points — most importantly, the
marketing mix shrinks from **7Ps to 4Ps** — flagged inline below.
Check which code applies to your own cohort before relying on the
7Ps content as exam scope.

## Where this fits in 9625

Topic 1 establishes why a business exists and who it is accountable
to. Topic 2 turns to how it competes for customers -- researching
markets, choosing who to target, and building a marketing mix around
that target. It sits alongside Operations, Human Resources and Finance
as one of the four functional AS topics, before the course moves into
whole-business strategy at A2.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL AS AND A-LEVEL BUSINESS (9625) — TOPIC 2
MARKETING**

- 3.1.2.1 Marketing objectives and plans — the importance of marketing
and how marketing decisions influence competitiveness; marketing
objectives such as sales volume and value, market share, customer
retention and repeat sales; the value of and influences on marketing
plans and budgets (objectives, strategy, tactics, budget); and
external and internal influences on marketing planning, including
resources, technology (digital marketing, social media, dynamic
pricing, e-commerce and m-commerce), ethics, and market conditions
- 3.1.2.2 Marketing data — the value of primary and secondary
marketing research, including qualitative and quantitative data,
market mapping, sales forecasts, and sampling methods (random,
stratified, quota); influences on choosing a research method (cost,
time, reliability); and the interpretation and use of marketing data,
including correlation, confidence levels and intervals, and big data
and data mining
- 3.1.2.3 Segmentation, targeting and positioning — the process and
value of segmentation by demographic, geographic, income, behavioural
and psychographic methods; influences on choosing a target market and
positioning, including niche and mass marketing; and the benefits and
potential difficulties of targeting international markets
- 3.1.2.4 The marketing mix — under **9625**, the elements of the
marketing mix are the **7Ps**: product, price, promotion, place
(distribution), **plus people, process and physical environment**,
considered across goods and services, B2B and B2C contexts, national
and international markets, and e-commerce; product (new product
development, the product life cycle and extension strategies, the
Boston Matrix, and branding); pricing (influences on price, pricing
strategies including skimming, penetration and discrimination, and
price elasticity of demand); the promotional mix, which under **9625**
is advertising, sales promotions, the sales force, public relations and
exhibitions (**9725** revises this list, so use the 9725 specification's
own list if you follow 9725);
distribution, including online and multi-channel distribution; and
influences on people, process and physical environment. **Under
9725**, the mix is simplified to the classic **4Ps** (product, price,
promotion, place) — people/process/physical environment, and the
Boston Matrix (which moves to A2 content), are dropped from this
first-year section.

## How to approach it

The marketing mix (3.1.2.4) is the largest single section and the one
most exam questions draw on, so build genuine fluency in all 7Ps
rather than only the classic 4Ps (product, price, promotion, place) --
OxfordAQA's own specification explicitly extends the mix and expects
students to apply it in e-commerce and international contexts, not
just a domestic high-street example. Price elasticity of demand
(within pricing) is a common source of calculation questions, so
practise interpreting PED data and linking it to a pricing decision,
not just defining the term. Segmentation, targeting and positioning
should be revised as a connected three-step process -- segment the
market, choose a target segment, then position the product for that
segment -- since exam questions often expect all three steps applied
to a single scenario rather than treated as separate topics.

## Common mistakes

Describing the marketing mix using only the 4Ps, missing the
additional elements the specification includes for services and
international markets. Confusing market segmentation (dividing the
market into groups) with targeting (choosing which group to serve).
Treating marketing research methods as interchangeable rather than
matching a method to the situation described in the question, using
cost, time and reliability as the deciding factors. Quoting a stage of
the product life cycle without connecting it to an appropriate
strategy, such as an extension strategy at maturity or decline.

## Worked example

A product's price elasticity of demand (PED) is calculated at -0.4.
What does this tell a business about a proposed price increase?

```
PED = -0.4 means demand is PRICE INELASTIC (the coefficient's
      magnitude is less than 1) -- quantity demanded changes
      proportionally less than price.

IMPLICATION: a price increase will reduce quantity demanded by a
             smaller percentage than the price rise itself, so total
             revenue (price x quantity) will RISE.

CAUTION: PED is not fixed -- it can change over time as competitors
         respond, substitutes emerge, or the product becomes less of
         a necessity, so a business should not treat one PED figure
         as permanent when setting long-term pricing strategy.
```

Students commonly lose marks by stating whether demand is elastic or
inelastic correctly, but failing to link that to what actually happens
to revenue -- the connection between PED and revenue is what most
exam questions are really testing.

## People, process and physical environment (9625 only)

Beyond product, price, promotion and place, 9625's extended mix
requires understanding of three further elements:

- **People** -- staff who deliver a service directly affect the
  customer's experience of it (training, attitude, appearance).
- **Process** -- the systems and procedures through which a good or
  service is delivered (how quickly an order is fulfilled, how a
  complaint is handled).
- **Physical environment** -- the tangible setting in which a service
  is delivered or a product is bought (store layout, website design,
  packaging), which shapes perceived quality even when it isn't the
  product itself.

These three matter most for services, where the customer often can't
inspect the product in advance and instead judges quality from these
surrounding cues. Under 9725, all three are dropped from this section
of the specification -- confirm your code before including them.

The specification requires calculations for named marketing
objectives, not just definitions of them:

```
Market share      = (business's sales / total market sales) x 100
                     e.g. sales of $2.4m in a $12m market
                     = (2.4 / 12) x 100 = 20%

Customer retention rate = (customers at end of period - new customers
                            gained) / customers at start of period x 100
                     e.g. started with 800 customers, gained 150 new,
                     ended with 900
                     = (900 - 150) / 800 x 100 = 93.75%

Sales per employee = total sales revenue / number of employees
                     e.g. $1.5m revenue, 25 employees
                     = $1.5m / 25 = $60,000 per employee

Sales per store    = total sales revenue / number of stores
                     e.g. $3m revenue across 12 stores
                     = $3m / 12 = $250,000 per store
```

The same logic applies to sales per product or per region -- divide
total sales by the relevant count. Examiners commonly ask students not
just to calculate the figure but to **interpret** it: a falling
customer retention rate, for instance, signals a problem worth
investigating even while overall sales volume is rising.

## Quick revision checklist

- State the difference between marketing objectives and a marketing
plan, and give one example of each.
- Compare primary and secondary marketing research, and state one
factor that influences which a business would choose.
- Explain segmentation, targeting and positioning as three connected
steps, not separate topics.
- List all 7Ps of the marketing mix under 9625 (product, price,
promotion, place, people, process, physical environment), not just
the classic 4Ps -- and know that 9725 reduces the mix back to 4Ps.
- Interpret a price elasticity of demand figure and explain its effect
on total revenue if price changes.
- Calculate and interpret market share, customer retention rate, and
sales per employee/product/region/store.
- Apply the Boston Matrix categories (cash cows, stars, dogs, problem
children) to a product portfolio scenario (9625 first-year; moves to
A2 under 9725).

## Specification transition note

9625 remains current for students already on the course. Its **final
AS exam series was June 2026, with a resit series in January 2027**;
its **final A2 exam series is June 2027, with a resit series in
January 2028** -- after which there are no further 9625 exams.
Students starting AS from **September 2026** follow the revised 9725
specification for AS (first 9725 AS exams June 2027); students
starting A2 from **September 2027** (one year later than AS) follow
9725 for A2 (first 9725 A2 exams June 2028). The core marketing
content described above is expected to carry over in substance, but
students should always confirm which specification code applies to
their own cohort before relying on any single syllabus document.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the marketing mix 4Ps or 7Ps in OxfordAQA Business?**
Under 9625 the marketing mix is the 7Ps: product, price, promotion, place, people, process and physical environment. Under 9725 it is simplified to the classic 4Ps, so check which code applies to your cohort.

**What does a PED of −0.4 mean for a price increase?**
A PED of −0.4 means demand is price inelastic, so quantity demanded falls by a smaller percentage than the price rises and total revenue rises. Linking the elasticity to what happens to revenue is what most questions test.

**How do you calculate customer retention rate?**
Customer retention rate = (customers at end of period − new customers gained) ÷ customers at start of period × 100. Examiners often ask you to interpret the figure as well as calculate it.

**When are the last 9625 Business exams?**
The final 9625 AS series was June 2026, with a resit series in January 2027, and the final A2 series is June 2027, with a resit series in January 2028. Students starting AS from September 2026 follow 9725.

**Is the Boston Matrix first-year content in OxfordAQA Business?**
Under 9625 the Boston Matrix is part of the first-year marketing mix content. Under 9725 it moves to A2 content.
