These questions cover section **4.7.5 Food production** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first
teaching from 2016. The whole section is **biology only**; every question suits both tiers. All data are invented
for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 7, 8 and 9.

Learn the content first in the [Food production study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-food-production/)

## Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** State what is meant by food security. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how a new pest can threaten food security. **[1]**

**2.** Name the organism used to produce mycoprotein, and state why mycoprotein is suitable for vegetarians.
**[2]**

**3.** Human insulin is produced by a genetically modified bacterium.

**(a)** State what the insulin is used for. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **one** advantage of producing insulin in this way. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** measures that help keep fish stocks at a sustainable level. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how the cost of agricultural inputs can threaten food security. **[2]**

**6.** State how golden rice differs from ordinary rice. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** The table shows the population and cereal production of a country.

| Year | 2000 | 2010 | 2020 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Population (millions) | 40 | 48 | 60 |
| Cereal production (million tonnes) | 16 | 18 | 21 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in population from 2000 to 2020. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in cereal production from 2000 to 2020. Give your answer to 2
significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the cereal production per person in 2000 and in 2020. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(d)** Use your answers to comment on food security in this country. **[1]**

**8.** A farmer rears two groups of calves. Each group eats 1200 kg of feed. The group kept indoors in a warm
shed with limited space gains 180 kg in mass. The group kept outdoors in fields gains 132 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the efficiency of converting feed into body mass for each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference in efficiency. **[3]**
**(c)** Give **one** reason why some people object to keeping calves indoors in this way. **[1]**

**9.** The table shows the mass of adult cod in a sea area. Fishing quotas were introduced at the start of year 3.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Adult cod stock (thousand tonnes) | 90 | 70 | 52 | 48 | 60 | 75 |

**(a)** Describe how the stock changed over the six years. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the stock increased after year 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the stock did not start to rise as soon as quotas were introduced. **[1]**

**10.** Mycoprotein is produced in large vessels.

**(a)** State what the fungus is grown on. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why air is pumped into the vessel. **[2]**
**(c)** After harvesting, the fungus is purified. Suggest why. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest **two** advantages of producing protein food from a fungus rather than from cattle. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate the use of genetically modified crops to meet the food demands of a growing human population.
**[6]**

**12.** Explain how changing diets in developed countries, and conflicts in some parts of the world, can threaten
food security. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Having enough food to feed a population [1]
**(b)** It damages or kills crops (or livestock), so less food is produced [1]

**2.** *Fusarium* (a fungus) [1]; it is protein-rich and is not made from animals [1]

**3. (a)** To treat people with diabetes [1]
**(b)** Any one: large quantities can be produced; it is human insulin; production is reliable [1]

**4.** Control of net size (larger mesh) [1]; fishing quotas [1]

**5.** Inputs such as seed, fertiliser, feed and fuel cost money [1]; farmers who cannot afford them produce less
food, so less food is available [1]

**6.** It has been genetically modified to improve its nutritional value: it contains a substance the body uses to
make vitamin A [1]

**7. (a)** (60 − 40) ÷ 40 × 100 = **50%** [1]
**(b)** (21 − 16) ÷ 16 × 100 = 31.25 = **31%** [1]
**(c)** 2000: 16 ÷ 40 = **0.40 tonnes per person** [1]; 2020: 21 ÷ 60 = **0.35 tonnes per person** [1]
**(d)** Food per person has fallen because the population grew faster than cereal production, so food security
has worsened [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Production went up, so food security improved" ignores the population; always compare per
person.

**8. (a)** Indoors: 180 ÷ 1200 × 100 = **15%** [1]; outdoors: 132 ÷ 1200 × 100 = **11%** [1]
**(b)** The indoor calves move less, so less energy is transferred for muscle contraction [1]; the warm shed means
less energy is transferred to keep warm [1]; so less glucose is used in respiration and more of the feed becomes
body mass [1]
**(c)** Any one: the animals cannot move freely or behave naturally; it is cruel to restrict them [1]

**9. (a)** The stock fell from 90 to 48 thousand tonnes between years 1 and 4 [1]; it then rose to 75 thousand
tonnes by year 6 [1]
**(b)** Quotas meant fewer fish were caught, so more adults survived [1]; these adults bred, adding young fish that
grew into adults [1]
**(c)** Young fish take time to grow to adult size and breed, so the increase in adults appears later [1]

**10. (a)** Glucose syrup [1]
**(b)** To provide oxygen, so conditions are aerobic [1]; the fungus respires aerobically, transferring the
energy it needs to grow quickly [1]
**(c)** To remove other substances, such as remaining growth medium, so it is safe and suitable to eat [1]
**(d)** Any two of the following, 1 mark each [2]: the fungus grows quickly; less land is needed; it is suitable
for vegetarians; no animal welfare concerns; less biomass is lost than when cattle convert plants into meat

**11.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer covers both sides and concludes):
- GM crops could give higher yields, so more food from the same land [1]
- they can have improved nutritional value, such as golden rice [1]
- crops modified to resist pests or disease reduce losses to new pests and pathogens [1]
- some people are concerned about effects on wild plants and insects [1]
- some people are concerned about effects on human health, which they argue are not fully known [1]
- a justified conclusion, such as GM crops can help but should be tested and monitored [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer with only benefits, or only concerns, cannot reach the top level.

**12.** Changing diets in developed countries increase demand for foods grown elsewhere [1]; so scarce food
resources are transported around the world, away from the people in the country that grew them [1]; conflicts
can stop farmers growing food or destroy crops and stores [1]; and can cut off the availability of water or food
supplies [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Concluding that food security improved because total production rose, without dividing by the population.
- Explaining intensive farming without mentioning respiration or energy transfer.
- Writing "bigger nets" instead of a larger mesh size.
- Saying mycoprotein is made by bacteria.
- Evaluations that give one side only, or no conclusion.
- Forgetting the unit when calculating food per person.
