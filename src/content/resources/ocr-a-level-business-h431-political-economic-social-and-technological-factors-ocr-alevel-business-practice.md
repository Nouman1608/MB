Every business and figure is invented. ## Section A: short questions

**1.** Distinguish between a direct tax and an indirect tax, giving one UK example of each. **[4]**

**2.** State **two** purposes of government spending. **[2]**

**3.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of political stability. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** benefit to a rural bus operator of receiving a government subsidy. **[3]**

**5.** Distinguish between monetary policy and fiscal policy. **[2]**

**6.** State the **four** phases of the business cycle. **[4]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Corran Joinery fits kitchens and staircases. It has a variable-rate loan of £240,000 and operating profit of £36,000 a year. The interest rate on the loan rises from 4% to 6%.

**(a)** Calculate the annual interest before and after the rise. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate interest cover before and after the rise. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **one** other way in which the rise in interest rates may affect Corran. **[2]**

**8.** Wrenford Ceramics sells tableware sets to the USA. Each set is priced at £40. The exchange rate falls from $1.25 to $1.15 per pound. Wrenford buys a special glaze from a US supplier, priced in dollars.

**(a)** Calculate the dollar price of a set at each exchange rate, if Wrenford keeps its sterling price. **[2]**

**(b)** Wrenford instead keeps its dollar price at the original level. Calculate its sterling revenue per set at the new exchange rate. **[2]**

**(c)** Analyse the importance of the fall in the pound for Wrenford. **[4]**

**9.** Ellery Foods imports dried fruit from a country where there has been a sudden period of political instability. Explain how this instability may affect Ellery's trade. **[4]**

**10.** Parkholme Caravans makes touring caravans, sold mostly to households who borrow to buy them. Economists forecast a downturn next year. Evaluate how Parkholme should respond. **[8]**

**11.** Seacrest Holidays sells beach holidays aimed at young families. The share of the population aged over 65 is rising. Evaluate the impact and importance of this social change for Seacrest and its stakeholders. **[8]**

**12.** Brightwater Opticians is considering an online booking system with self-check-in screens. It would cost £18,000 and save 1,200 receptionist hours a year, paid at £12.50 an hour.

**(a)** Calculate the payback period. **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether Brightwater should invest in the system. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A direct tax is paid straight to government by the person or business whose income or profit it is charged on [1], for example income tax or corporation tax [1]. An indirect tax is added to the price of goods and services and passed on to the buyer [1], for example VAT [1]. **[4]**

**2.** Any two from: to provide public services such as health and education [1]; to redistribute income [1]; to support growth and jobs; to invest in infrastructure. **[2]**

**3.** Managers can predict taxes and regulations [1], so they can plan long-term investment with less risk, and lenders may lend more cheaply [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The subsidy lowers the cost of running each route [1], so a route that would make a loss can break even [1], letting the operator keep the service, protect its revenue and keep customers who rely on it [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Monetary policy uses interest rates, set in the UK by the Bank of England [1]. Fiscal policy uses government taxation and spending [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Boom [1], downturn or recession [1], slump [1], recovery [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Before: 4% × 240,000 = **£9,600** [1]; after: 6% × 240,000 = **£14,400** [1].

**(b)** Before: 36,000 ÷ 9,600 = **3.75 times** [1]; after: 36,000 ÷ 14,400 = **2.5 times** [1].

**(c)** Households pay more on mortgages and loans, so they have less to spend [1]; kitchens are a postponable purchase, so Corran's orders may fall and revenue drop [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers notice the double effect: higher costs and lower demand at the same time.

**8. (a)** At $1.25: 40 × 1.25 = **$50** [1]; at $1.15: 40 × 1.15 = **$46** [1].

**(b)** 50 ÷ 1.15 = **£43.48** per set [1] [1].

**(c)** The weaker pound makes Wrenford's sets cheaper for US buyers, or lets it earn more pounds per set [1], so it can win orders or raise its margin [1]. However, the dollar-priced glaze now costs more in pounds [1], so the gain is reduced; the importance depends on how large the glaze cost is relative to US sales revenue [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Contracts with suppliers may not be honoured, or supplies may stop [1], so Ellery may run short of stock [1]. Ports, transport or payments may be disrupted [1], raising delivery times and costs or forcing Ellery to find a new, possibly dearer, supplier [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Caravans are a luxury, postponable purchase [1], and buyers rely on borrowing, so a downturn with falling incomes and tighter credit could cut sales sharply [1]. Parkholme could reduce output and stock to protect cash [1], delay expansion or new borrowing [1], or offer finance deals or cheaper models to keep sales moving [1], though finance deals cut margin [1]. Cutting staff saves cost but loses skills needed in the recovery [1]. Judgement: protect cash first by cutting stock and delaying investment, because forecasts can be wrong and cash lets the firm survive and expand quickly in recovery; the response depends on how severe and certain the downturn is [1]. **[8]**

**11.** Seacrest's core market of young families may grow more slowly [1], so sales may stagnate unless it adapts [1]. The change is also an opportunity: over-65s often have time and savings, so it could add quieter, off-peak holidays [1], which would use capacity outside school holidays [1]. Stakeholders: staff may need new skills, such as catering for different needs [1]; shareholders may gain if off-peak capacity is filled [1]. The change is slow and predictable, so Seacrest has time to adjust [1]. Judgement: important in the long term but not urgent, and potentially positive if Seacrest diversifies its offer [1]. **[8]**

**12. (a)** Annual saving = 1,200 × £12.50 = £15,000 [1]; payback = 18,000 ÷ 15,000 = **1.2 years** [1].

**(b)** For: payback is quick, about 14 or 15 months [1], and staff time saved could be used on customer service or sales of frames [1]. Customers can book at any time, which may win new customers [1]. Against: older customers may find self-check-in hard and feel the service is less personal [1]; the system may fail or be hacked, risking customer data [1]; reception staff may lose hours, harming morale [1]. The saving assumes all 1,200 hours really disappear [1]. Judgement: invest, because the payback is short, but keep some staffed reception for customers who need it [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling VAT a direct tax.
- Calculating interest on the wrong figure, or forgetting the "before" figure.
- Multiplying when converting pounds to a foreign currency should be dividing, or the reverse.
- Treating a weaker pound as bad for every firm.
- Evaluating with no judgement, or with a judgement that ignores the case facts.
- Ignoring the stakeholders named in the question.
