**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
33.3: preparation, five reactions, the addition–elimination mechanism, and "the relative ease of
hydrolysis of acyl chlorides, alkyl chlorides and halogenoarenes (aryl chlorides)".

## Preparation: three reagents

    RCOOH + PCl₅  → RCOCl + POCl₃ + HCl        room temperature
    3RCOOH + PCl₃ → 3RCOCl + H₃PO₃             heat
    RCOOH + SOCl₂ → RCOCl + SO₂ + HCl          gaseous by-products: easiest separation

- All need **dry** conditions: water hydrolyses both the reagents and the product.
- The –OH of the acid is replaced by –Cl. The same reagents convert R–OH (alcohols) to R–Cl.

## The five reactions (all room temperature, all give HCl)

| Reagent | General product | Example product from CH₃COCl |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | carboxylic acid RCOOH | CH₃COOH |
| alcohol R′OH | ester RCOOR′ | with ethanol: CH₃COOC₂H₅ |
| phenol C₆H₅OH | aryl ester RCOOC₆H₅ | CH₃COOC₆H₅ |
| NH₃ | primary amide RCONH₂ | CH₃CONH₂ |
| primary amine R′NH₂ | N-substituted amide RCONHR′ | with CH₃NH₂: CH₃CONHCH₃ |
| secondary amine R′₂NH | N,N-disubstituted amide RCONR′₂ | with (CH₃)₂NH: CH₃CON(CH₃)₂ |

- **Observation:** steamy (misty) fumes of HCl; reactions are fast and exothermic.
- **With excess NH₃ or amine:** HCl is neutralised, e.g. CH₃COCl + 2NH₃ → CH₃CONH₂ + NH₄Cl.
- **Tertiary amines** have no N–H and do not form amides.

## Addition–elimination: the arrow checklist

1. Carbonyl carbon is **δ+** (O and Cl both electronegative) and **planar**.
2. **Addition:** arrow from the nucleophile's **lone pair** to C; arrow from the **C=O π bond** to O.
3. **Tetrahedral intermediate** with **O⁻** and a positive charge on the attacking O or N.
4. **Elimination:** arrow from O⁻ to re-form C=O; arrow from C–Cl to Cl, releasing **Cl⁻**.
5. **H⁺ lost** from the attacking atom; H⁺ + Cl⁻ → HCl.

| Mechanism | Substrate | Steps | Group lost? |
|---|---|---|---|
| addition–elimination | acyl chloride | two: add, then eliminate | yes, Cl⁻ |
| nucleophilic addition | aldehyde or ketone | add, then protonate | no |
| nucleophilic substitution (SN1/SN2) | halogenoalkane | one (SN2) or two via carbocation (SN1) | yes, X⁻ |

## Relative ease of hydrolysis

**acyl chloride > alkyl chloride > aryl chloride**

| | Acyl chloride | Alkyl chloride | Aryl chloride |
|---|---|---|---|
| Example | CH₃COCl | CH₃CH₂Cl | C₆H₅Cl |
| Conditions to hydrolyse | cold water, at once | warm aqueous alkali or water, slowly | none at these conditions |
| With AgNO₃(aq) | white AgCl precipitate immediately | white precipitate slowly on warming | no precipitate |
| Carbon attacked | sp², strongly δ+ (O and Cl) | sp³, δ+ from Cl only | part of the ring |
| Key reason | π bond accepts nucleophile's electrons; low activation energy | must displace Cl⁻ directly | C–Cl strengthened by overlap of Cl lone pair with ring π system; ring repels nucleophile |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| acyl chloride | compound containing the –COCl group |
| acyl group | RCO– |
| nucleophile | lone-pair donor that attacks an electron-deficient carbon |
| tetrahedral intermediate | species formed when a nucleophile adds to C=O; carbon has four groups |
| leaving group | group that departs with the bonding pair (here Cl⁻) |
| hydrolysis | breaking a bond by reaction with water |
| aryl chloride | chlorine bonded directly to a benzene ring (halogenoarene) |
| N-substituted amide | amide with an alkyl or aryl group on nitrogen |

## Exam traps

- "Heat" belongs only to the **PCl₃** preparation, never to the five reactions.
- PCl₅ gives **POCl₃ + HCl**; SOCl₂ gives **SO₂ + HCl**; PCl₃ gives **H₃PO₃** and needs a 3 : 1 ratio.
- Curly arrows start from a **lone pair** or a **bond**, never from an atom symbol or a charge sign
  floating in space.
- Show the charges on the intermediate.
- Chlorobenzene **does not** hydrolyse under these conditions; do not write "slowly".
- In the ranking, give a reason for **each** compound, not just the fastest.
- A secondary amine gives an **N,N-disubstituted** amide; a primary amine gives an
  **N-substituted** amide.

## Self-test

1. Write the equation for ethanoic acid with SOCl₂.
2. Why is SOCl₂ often chosen over PCl₅?
3. Balance the reaction of propanoic acid with PCl₃.
4. Name the organic product of ethanoyl chloride with water.
5. Give the product of benzoyl chloride with ammonia.
6. Give the product of propanoyl chloride with ethylamine and name it.
7. What is seen in all five reactions of an acyl chloride?
8. In addition–elimination, where does the first curly arrow start and end?
9. Why does a tertiary amine not form an amide with an acyl chloride?
10. Place CH₃COCl, C₆H₅Cl and CH₃CH₂Cl in order of decreasing ease of hydrolysis.
11. Give one reason why chlorobenzene resists hydrolysis.
12. What would you see if ethanoyl chloride were added to AgNO₃(aq)?

**Answers:**

1. CH₃COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃COCl + SO₂ + HCl.
2. Both by-products are gases, so the acyl chloride is easily separated.
3. 3CH₃CH₂COOH + PCl₃ → 3CH₃CH₂COCl + H₃PO₃.
4. Ethanoic acid (with HCl).
5. Benzamide, C₆H₅CONH₂ (with HCl or NH₄Cl).
6. CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃, N-ethylpropanamide.
7. Steamy fumes of HCl (a fast, exothermic reaction).
8. From a lone pair on the nucleophile's O or N to the δ+ carbonyl carbon.
9. It has no hydrogen on nitrogen to lose, so it cannot form a neutral amide.
10. CH₃COCl > CH₃CH₂Cl > C₆H₅Cl.
11. A Cl lone pair overlaps with the ring π system, giving C–Cl partial double-bond character
    (stronger bond); or the electron-rich ring repels the nucleophile.
12. Steamy fumes and an immediate white precipitate of AgCl.
