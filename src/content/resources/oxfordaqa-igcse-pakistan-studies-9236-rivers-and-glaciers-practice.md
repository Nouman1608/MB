## Answers

Multiple-choice questions score 1 mark. "State" questions score 1 mark per valid point. Four-mark
questions score 1 mark per valid, linked point in a logical sequence. The 6- and 9-mark questions are
marked by levels; the level descriptions follow the answers. Credit other accurate points.

**1.** **B: Attock** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C: Suspension** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** **A: Arête** [1]. **[1]**

**4.** **B: Baltoro** [1]. The Khumbu is in Nepal, the Aletsch in the Alps and the Gangotri in India.
**[1]**

**5.** Two processes, 1 mark each: hydraulic action [1]; abrasion [1]. Also credit: attrition; solution.
**[2]**

**6.** Two landforms, 1 mark each: V-shaped valley or gorge [1]; interlocking spurs [1]. Also credit:
rapids; waterfalls. **[2]**

**7.** Two processes, 1 mark each: plucking [1]; abrasion [1]. Also credit: freeze-thaw weathering
supplying debris. **[2]**

**8.** Four linked points, 1 mark each: when a river floods, water spreads over the flat land beside
the channel [1]; outside the channel the water slows and loses energy [1]; it deposits its load of
silt across the land [1]; repeated floods build up thick, fertile layers of alluvium, as on the
plains of Punjab and Sindh [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Four linked points, 1 mark each: a glacier moves down a former river valley [1]; it erodes the
floor and sides by plucking and abrasion [1]; this widens and deepens the valley and cuts off the
ends of spurs [1]; when the ice melts, a steep-sided, flat-floored U-shaped valley remains, as in the
northern mountain valleys [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Less sediment downstream:* reservoirs such as Tarbela trap silt, so less is deposited on the lower
  flood plain and in the delta, and the delta is shrinking.
- *Less water reaching the sea:* barrages divert water into canals, so the lower Indus carries less
  flow, especially in the dry season, and seawater moves inland, damaging land and mangroves.
- *Changed flooding:* dams regulate flow and reduce some floods, but sediment builds up in reservoirs
  and can reduce storage capacity over time.
- *Channel change:* reduced flow allows deposition within the channel, encouraging braiding and
  sandbars. **[6]**

**11.** Mark by the Analysis (6) levels. Indicative content:
- *Water supply:* summer meltwater feeds the Indus when farmers need it most, filling reservoirs and
  canals for irrigation and supporting hydel power.
- *Hazards:* glacial lake outburst floods can destroy roads, bridges and homes, as when the Hassanabad
  bridge on the Karakoram Highway was destroyed in 2022.
- *Tourism and livelihoods:* glaciers and high peaks such as K2 attract climbers and trekkers,
  providing income in Gilgit-Baltistan.
- *Climate change risk:* retreating glaciers in parts of the Himalaya and Hindu Kush threaten long-term
  water supply. **[6]**

**12.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Agree:* the Indus and its tributaries built the plains of Punjab and Sindh, the doabs and the
  delta, where most people live; river gorges cut through the mountains.
- *Disagree:* glaciers carved the high landscapes of the north, with cirques, arêtes, pyramidal peaks,
  U-shaped valleys and moraines; glacial meltwater supplies the rivers that shape the lowlands.
- *Judgement:* for example, rivers have shaped the larger and more populated area, but glaciers made
  the high north and supply much of the rivers' water, so the two work as one system. **[9]**

**13.** Mark by the Evaluation (9) levels. Indicative content:
- *Benefits:* meltwater for irrigation, drinking water and hydel power; tourism and mountaineering;
  water stored as ice through the year.
- *Hazards:* GLOFs and surges; the damage at Hassanabad in 2022; floods when melting combines with
  monsoon rain; long-term risk from glacier retreat.
- *Judgement:* for example, benefits are greater because the whole Indus economy depends on
  meltwater, but hazards are growing, so warning systems and planning are needed. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a landform without the sequence of processes that forms it.
- Writing about "rivers" or "glaciers" in general with no Pakistani example.
- Confusing river and glacial erosion processes.
- In 9-mark answers, giving one side only.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to draw diagrams?**
Labelled sketches can support "explain the formation" answers, but the written sequence of processes
is what earns the marks.

**How many examples should I learn?**
At least one named place for each landform type, such as the Nanga Parbat gorge, the Punjab flood
plains, the Indus delta and the Baltoro Glacier.
