This guide covers section 5.2, Area and perimeter, in the Mensuration topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to **carry out calculations involving the perimeter and area of a rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium**. It notes that, except for the area of a triangle, formulas are not given in the examination, so you must learn the others. After this guide, use the [area and perimeter revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-area-and-perimeter-revision-notes/) and the [area and perimeter practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-area-and-perimeter-practice/).

## What section 5.2 asks for

1. Find the perimeter and area of the four shapes.
2. Work backwards: find a missing side or height from an area or perimeter.
3. Use algebra and Pythagoras' theorem to find the lengths you need.
4. Give units: cm for perimeter, cm² for area.

## Perimeter and area

The **perimeter** is the total distance around the edge of a shape. The **area** is the amount of surface inside the shape. Perimeter is a length, so it is measured in units such as cm or m. Area is measured in square units such as cm² or m².

## The formulas

| Shape | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle, length l and width w | l × w | 2(l + w) |
| Triangle, base b and perpendicular height h | 1/2 × b × h | add the three sides |
| Parallelogram, base b and perpendicular height h | b × h | 2(a + b), where a and b are the two different sides |
| Trapezium, parallel sides a and b, height h | 1/2 × (a + b) × h | add the four sides |

The only formula given in the examination is the area of a triangle, 1/2 bh. You must remember the others. The height is always measured **perpendicular** to the base. It is not the slanting side, unless the shape is a rectangle or a right-angled triangle.

### Worked example 1

A rectangle is 8.5 cm long and 4.2 cm wide. Find its area and perimeter.

```
area = 8.5 × 4.2 = 35.7 cm²
perimeter = 2 × (8.5 + 4.2) = 25.4 cm
```

### Worked example 2

A triangle has a base of 12 cm and a perpendicular height of 7.5 cm. Find its area.

```
area = 1/2 × 12 × 7.5 = 45 cm²
```

Any side can be chosen as the base, as long as the height is the perpendicular distance from that base to the opposite vertex.

### Worked example 3

A parallelogram has a base of 9 cm, a perpendicular height of 6 cm and a slanting side of 7 cm. Find its area and perimeter.

```
area = 9 × 6 = 54 cm²
perimeter = 2 × (9 + 7) = 32 cm
```

The slanting side of 7 cm is used for the perimeter. The perpendicular height of 6 cm is used for the area. Mixing them up is the classic error.

### Worked example 4

A trapezium has parallel sides of 8 cm and 14 cm and a perpendicular distance of 5 cm between them. Find the area.

```
area = 1/2 × (8 + 14) × 5 = 1/2 × 22 × 5 = 55 cm²
```

## Working backwards

If you know the area and one length, rearrange the formula.

### Worked example 5

A triangle has area 54 cm² and a base of 9 cm. Find its height.

```
1/2 × 9 × h = 54
4.5 h = 54
h = 12 cm
```

### Worked example 6

A trapezium has area 91 cm². Its parallel sides are 9 cm and 17 cm. Find the distance between them.

```
1/2 × (9 + 17) × h = 91
13 h = 91
h = 7 cm
```

### Worked example 7

A parallelogram has a base of 15 cm and area 105 cm². Find its perpendicular height.

```
15 × h = 105
h = 7 cm
```

## Using algebra

### Worked example 8

A rectangle has a length that is 3 cm longer than its width, and a perimeter of 38 cm. Find its area.

```
let the width be x cm, so the length is (x + 3) cm
perimeter: 2(x + x + 3) = 38
4x + 6 = 38
x = 8, so the width is 8 cm and the length is 11 cm
area = 8 × 11 = 88 cm²
```

### Worked example 9

A rectangle has perimeter 34 cm and area 70 cm². Find its sides.

```
l + w = 17, so w = 17 − l
l(17 − l) = 70
l² − 17l + 70 = 0
(l − 7)(l − 10) = 0
sides: 7 cm and 10 cm
```

## Using Pythagoras' theorem to find a height

Often the perpendicular height is not given. It must be found from the sides.

### Worked example 10

An isosceles triangle has sides 10 cm, 10 cm and 12 cm. Find its perimeter and area.

```
perimeter = 10 + 10 + 12 = 32 cm
the height splits the base into two halves of 6 cm
h² = 10² − 6² = 64, so h = 8 cm
area = 1/2 × 12 × 8 = 48 cm²
```

### Worked example 11

An isosceles trapezium has parallel sides of 6 cm and 14 cm, and sloping sides of 5 cm. Find its area and perimeter.

```
the difference in the parallel sides is 14 − 6 = 8, so each end has an overhang of 4 cm
h² = 5² − 4² = 9, so h = 3 cm
area = 1/2 × (6 + 14) × 3 = 30 cm²
perimeter = 6 + 14 + 5 + 5 = 30 cm
```

## Choosing different bases

A triangle has three possible bases, and each gives the same area when matched to the correct height.

### Worked example 12

A triangle has sides 13 cm, 14 cm and 15 cm. Take the 14 cm side as the base. The perpendicular from the opposite vertex splits the base into 5 cm and 9 cm. Find the height, the area, and the height when the 15 cm side is the base.

```
h² = 13² − 5² = 144, so h = 12 cm
check with the other part: 15² − 9² = 144, h = 12 cm
area = 1/2 × 14 × 12 = 84 cm²
with base 15 cm: 1/2 × 15 × h = 84, so h = 168 ÷ 15 = 11.2 cm
```

Because area = 1/2 × base × height for every choice of base, the base and the height always multiply to the same number, here 168.

## Paths and borders

For a border of equal width, find the outer area and subtract the inner area.

### Worked example 13

A rectangular lawn is 10 m by 8 m. A path 1 m wide goes all the way round the outside. Find the area of the path.

```
outer rectangle: (10 + 2) × (8 + 2) = 12 × 10 = 120 m²
lawn: 10 × 8 = 80 m²
path = 120 − 80 = 40 m²
```

The border adds the width twice to each dimension, once for each side. 

## Mixed units

A rectangle measuring 250 cm by 80 cm has area 250 × 80 = 20 000 cm². To give the area in m², divide by 10 000 to get 2 m². Alternatively convert first: 2.5 m × 0.8 m = 2 m². Use one unit for all lengths before multiplying.

## Practical applications

### Worked example 14

A room is 4.5 m by 3.2 m. Carpet costs $12.50 per square metre. Find the cost of carpeting the whole room.

```
area = 4.5 × 3.2 = 14.4 m²
cost = 14.4 × 12.50 = $180
```

### Worked example 15

A field is a rectangle 45 m by 28 m. A fence goes around the edge with a post every 2 m. How many posts are needed?

```
perimeter = 2 × (45 + 28) = 146 m
posts = 146 ÷ 2 = 73
```

## Common mistakes

- Using the slanting side as the height of a triangle or parallelogram.
- Forgetting the 1/2 in the area of a triangle or trapezium.
- Adding only two sides when finding the perimeter of a parallelogram.
- Mixing area and perimeter units, such as writing an area in cm.
- Forgetting to convert all lengths to the same unit first.
- Stopping at 2x when the problem needs the full perimeter equation.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the area: write the formula, substitute the numbers, then give the unit.
- If no height is given, look for a right-angled triangle.
- **Show that**: finish the working on the stated value.
- Check the size: a trapezium's area is between the areas of two rectangles built on its parallel sides with the same height.
