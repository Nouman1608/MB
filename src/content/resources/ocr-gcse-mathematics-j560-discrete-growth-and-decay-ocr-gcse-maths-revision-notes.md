## The big picture

"Discrete" means the change happens once per interval: per year, per month, per hour. You multiply once for each interval. There is no change part-way through an interval, so "how many intervals" answers are whole numbers.

| Specification layer | Content | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Initial learning | Simple interest, including financial contexts | Both tiers |
| Foundation column | Multipliers step by step over a given interval: compound interest, depreciation | Both tiers |
| Higher column | Write growth or decay as a formula; solve and interpret | Higher tier only |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Principal | The amount first invested or borrowed |
| Simple interest | Interest worked out on the principal only; the same each year |
| Compound interest | Interest added each interval, so later interest is also earned on earlier interest |
| Depreciation | A fall in value over time, usually a percentage per year |
| Appreciation | A rise in value over time |
| Multiplier | The single number that applies a percentage change: 1 + r/100 for growth, 1 − r/100 for decay |
| Interval | The time step for one change, such as a year or a month |
| p.a. | Per annum, meaning per year |

## Simple interest

```
one year's interest = principal × rate/100
total interest      = one year's interest × years
amount              = principal + total interest
```

Recall: £6,000 at 3.2% simple interest for 5 years earns 6000 × 0.032 × 5 = £960.

Backwards: find one year's interest, then divide by the principal for the rate, or divide the total interest by one year's interest for the time.

## Multipliers

```
increase by r%  →  × (1 + r/100)
decrease by r%  →  × (1 − r/100)
same change n times  →  × multiplierⁿ
different changes    →  multiply the different multipliers
```

- 2.5% increase: × 1.025. Two years on £2,400: 2400 × 1.025² = £2,521.50.
- 10% decrease twice on £3,200: 3200 × 0.9² = 3200 × 0.81 = £2,592 (non-calculator friendly).
- 15% depreciation for 3 years: × 0.85³ = 0.614125, so a £20,000 car is worth £12,282.50.

## Successive changes

Never add or subtract percentages applied one after another.

- Up 12% then down 12%: 1.12 × 0.88 = 0.9856, a 1.44% fall overall.
- Up 15% then down 20%: 1.15 × 0.8 = 0.92, an 8% fall overall.
- Overall percentage change = (overall multiplier − 1) × 100.

## Reversing a compound change

```
original = final ÷ multiplierⁿ
```

Recall: after 3 years of 10% growth a value is 1,331. Original = 1331 ÷ 1.1³ = 1331 ÷ 1.331 = 1,000.

## Formulae (Higher tier only)

```
A = A₀ × mⁿ
A₀ = starting value,  m = multiplier,  n = number of intervals
```

| Formula | Start | Multiplier | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| A = 100 × 1.1ⁿ | 100 | 1.1 | grows 10% per interval |
| V = 650 × 0.85ⁿ | 650 | 0.85 | falls 15% per interval |
| N = 40 × 2ⁿ | 40 | 2 | doubles each interval |

**First interval past a target.** Trial whole numbers and show the two values either side.

Recall: £500 at 8% compound. After 9 years 500 × 1.08⁹ = £999.50; after 10 years 500 × 1.08¹⁰ = £1,079.46. It first exceeds £1,000 after 10 years. Stopping at 9 because 999.50 "rounds to 1,000" is wrong.

**Interpreting.** The model only applies at whole intervals; people and items are whole numbers; a fixed percentage rarely continues for ever, so long-range predictions are less reliable.

## Exam traps

- Compound versus simple: the word in the question decides.
- A 4% decrease is × 0.96, not × 0.4 or × 0.04.
- "How much interest" means the interest only; "how much in the account" means principal plus interest.
- A rise and an equal fall do not cancel.
- Reversing: divide by the multiplier; do not take the percentage off the final amount.
- Monthly rate for 3 years means 36 intervals.
- Round money only at the end, then to the nearest penny.
- In "Show that" questions, write the unrounded value as well as the rounded one.
- A "first year" answer needs evidence for the year before as well.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the multiplier for an increase of 4.5%.
2. Write the multiplier for a decrease of 35%.
3. Find the simple interest on £1,500 at 4% per year for 6 years.
4. £2,000 earns £210 simple interest in 3 years. Find the rate.
5. £7,500 is invested at 2% compound interest. Find its value after 4 years.
6. Compare your answer to question 5 with 2% simple interest on the same amount for 4 years.
7. A price rises by 20% and then falls by 25%. Find the overall percentage change.
8. A laptop worth £180 loses 5% of its value each year. Find its value after 2 years.
9. A value grows by 10% per year for 3 years to £1,331. Find the original value.
10. (Higher tier only) State the starting value and percentage change per year in V = 2400 × 0.92ⁿ.
11. (Higher tier only) A colony of 40 cells doubles every hour. Write a formula for the number N after t hours.
12. (Higher tier only) Using your formula in question 11, find the first whole hour when N exceeds 1,000.

### Answers

1. 1.045
2. 0.65
3. 1500 × 0.04 × 6 = £360
4. 210 ÷ 3 = 70 per year; 70 ÷ 2000 = 0.035, so 3.5%
5. 7500 × 1.02⁴ = 8118.2412, so £8,118.24
6. Simple interest is 7500 × 0.02 × 4 = £600, giving £8,100. Compound gives £18.24 more.
7. 1.2 × 0.75 = 0.9, so a 10% decrease
8. 180 × 0.95² = £162.45
9. 1331 ÷ 1.331 = £1,000
10. Starting value 2,400; multiplier 0.92, so a decrease of 8% per year
11. N = 40 × 2ᵗ
12. t = 4 gives 640; t = 5 gives 1,280; so after 5 hours
