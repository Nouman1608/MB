These questions cover section 2 of Unit M2: Mechanics 2 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): centres of mass of discrete mass distributions in one and two dimensions, of uniform plane figures and simple composite figures, and simple cases of equilibrium of a plane lamina. Results from the formulae booklet may be quoted. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² where needed.

Related: [M2 centres of mass study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-centres-of-mass-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [M2 centres of mass revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-centres-of-mass-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Particles of mass 4 kg, 1 kg and 3 kg lie on the x-axis at x = −1, x = 2 and x = 5. Find the position of their centre of mass. **[2]**

**2.** Particles of mass 1 kg, 3 kg and 4 kg are at the points (2, 1), (−1, 4) and (3, −2). Find the coordinates of their centre of mass. **[3]**

**3.** A uniform triangular lamina of mass 3 kg has vertices (0, 0), (6, 0) and (0, 9). A particle of mass 1 kg is attached at (6, 0). Find the coordinates of the centre of mass of the combined body. **[3]**

**4.** A uniform lamina is a quarter of a circle of radius 8 cm, with its two straight edges along the positive x- and y-axes. Using the result for a sector, show that the centre of mass is 4r/(3π) from each straight edge, and find this distance. **[3]**

**5.** A uniform wire is bent into an arc of a circle of radius 12 cm, subtending an angle of π/2 at the centre. Find the distance of its centre of mass from the centre of the circle. **[2]**

**6.** Particles of mass 2 kg, 3 kg and 5 kg are at (0, 0), (a, 4) and (6, b). Their centre of mass is at (4, 3). Find a and b. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Particles of mass 3 kg, 2 kg and m kg are at (1, 2), (5, 6) and (a, 0). Their centre of mass is at (3, 2).

**(a)** Find m. **[3]**

**(b)** Find a. **[2]**

**8.** A uniform wire is bent into three sides of a rectangle, forming the framework ABCD with AB = 8 cm, BC = 6 cm and CD = 8 cm. There is no wire along DA.

**(a)** Taking A as the origin, AB along the x-axis and AD along the y-axis, find the coordinates of the centre of mass. **[4]**

**(b)** The framework hangs freely from A. Find the angle AB makes with the vertical. **[2]**

**9.** A uniform L-shaped lamina is made of two rectangles: one occupying 0 ≤ x ≤ 6, 0 ≤ y ≤ 2, and one occupying 0 ≤ x ≤ 2, 2 ≤ y ≤ 8.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the centre of mass of the lamina. **[4]**

**(b)** The lamina hangs freely from the point (6, 0). Find the angle between the edge along the x-axis and the vertical. **[3]**

**10.** A uniform rectangular lamina occupies 0 ≤ x ≤ 12, 0 ≤ y ≤ 8, with lengths in cm. A square hole with corners (2, 2), (6, 2), (6, 6) and (2, 6) is cut out.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the centre of mass of the remaining lamina. **[4]**

**(b)** The lamina hangs freely from the corner (0, 8). Find the angle that the top edge (along y = 8) makes with the vertical. **[3]**

**11.** A uniform square lamina ABCD has side 0.4 m and mass 2 kg. A particle of mass m kg is attached at D. The lamina hangs freely from A, and AD makes an angle of tan⁻¹(1/4) with the vertical. Find m. **[5]**

**12.** A uniform rectangular lamina ABCD has AB = 0.8 m, BC = 0.5 m and mass 4 kg. A particle of mass 1 kg is attached at C. The lamina can turn freely in a vertical plane about a smooth horizontal axis through A, perpendicular to the lamina. It is held with AB horizontal and C above B by a vertical force F N acting at B.

**(a)** Find F. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the size and direction of the force exerted by the axis on the lamina. **[2]**

**13.** A uniform triangular lamina ABC has a right angle at B, with AB = 6 cm and BC = 9 cm. It stands with AB along a line of greatest slope of a rough plane, B lower than A, and BC perpendicular to the plane. The coefficient of friction is 0.8. The angle α of the plane is slowly increased from zero.

**(a)** Write down the distances of the centre of mass from AB and from BC. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that the lamina topples before it slides, and find the angle at which it topples. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** x̄ = (4(−1) + 1(2) + 3(5))/8 [1] = 13/8, so **x = 1.625** [1].

**2.** x̄ = (2 − 3 + 12)/8 = **11/8** [1]; ȳ = (1 + 12 − 8)/8 = **5/8** [1]. Centre of mass **(1.375, 0.625)** [1].

**3.** Triangle: centre of mass at the mean of the vertices, (2, 3) [1].

x̄ = (3(2) + 1(6))/4 = **3** [1]; ȳ = (3(3) + 1(0))/4 = **2.25** [1].

**4.** Sector with 2α = π/2, so α = π/4: OG = 2r sin(π/4)/(3π/4) = 4√2 r/(3π) along the line y = x [1].

Distance from each axis = OG cos 45° = (4√2 r/(3π))(1/√2) = 4r/(3π) [1].

With r = 8: **32/(3π) = 3.40 cm** [1].

**5.** α = π/4: r sin α/α = 12(√2/2)/(π/4) [1] = 24√2/π = **10.8 cm** [1].

**6.** x: (0 + 3a + 30)/10 = 4 [1], so **a = 10/3** [1]. y: (0 + 12 + 5b)/10 = 3, so **b = 3.6** [1].

**7. (a)** ȳ: (3(2) + 2(6) + m(0))/(5 + m) = 2 [1]. 18 = 10 + 2m [1], so **m = 4** [1].

**(b)** x̄: (3 + 10 + 4a)/9 = 3 [1], so 4a = 14 and **a = 3.5** [1].

**8. (a)** Rods AB, BC, CD with lengths 8, 6, 8 and midpoints (4, 0), (8, 3), (4, 6) [1].

x̄ = (8(4) + 6(8) + 8(4))/22 [1] = 112/22 = **5.09 cm** [1].

ȳ = (8(0) + 6(3) + 8(6))/22 = 66/22 = **3 cm** [1].

**(b)** G is vertically below A. tan θ = 3/5.09 [1], so AB makes **30.5°** with the vertical [1].

**9. (a)** Areas 12 and 12, with centres (3, 1) and (1, 5) [2].

x̄ = (12(3) + 12(1))/24 = **2** [1]; ȳ = (12(1) + 12(5))/24 = **3** [1].

**(b)** G is vertically below (6, 0). From (6, 0), G is 4 to the left and 3 up [1].

The edge runs left from (6, 0), so the angle between it and the vertical is tan⁻¹(3/4) [1] = **36.9°** [1].

**10. (a)** Rectangle area 96, centre (6, 4); hole area 16, centre (4, 4) [1].

96(6) − 16(4) = 80x̄ [1], so **x̄ = 6.4 cm** [1].

The shape is symmetric about y = 4, so **ȳ = 4 cm** [1].

**(b)** From (0, 8), G is 6.4 across and 4 down [1]. The top edge runs along the x direction, so the angle is tan⁻¹(4/6.4) [1] = **32.0°** [1].

**11.** Axes: A at the origin, AB along x, AD along y. Lamina centre (0.2, 0.2), particle at (0, 0.4) [1].

x̄ = 0.4/(2 + m) and ȳ = (0.4 + 0.4m)/(2 + m) [2].

AG is vertical, so tan(angle between AD and the vertical) = x̄/ȳ = 1/(1 + m) [1].

1/(1 + m) = 1/4, so **m = 3** [1].

**12. (a)** The lamina's weight 4g acts 0.4 m horizontally from A; the particle's weight 1g acts 0.8 m from A [1].

Moments about A: 0.8F = 4g(0.4) + 1g(0.8) [2].

F = (15.68 + 7.84)/0.8 = **29.4 N** [1].

**(b)** Resolving vertically: R + 29.4 = 5g = 49 [1], so the axis exerts **19.6 N vertically upwards** [1].

**13. (a)** The centre of mass is the mean of the vertices: **3 cm from AB** and **2 cm from BC** [2].

**(b)** It topples about B when G is vertically above B, so tan α = 2/3 [1], giving α = **33.7°** [1].

It would slide when tan α = μ = 0.8 [1]. Since 2/3 < 0.8, the toppling angle is reached first, so **the lamina topples before it slides** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using areas instead of lengths for a wire framework.
- Using the whole angle of an arc or sector as 2α, or forgetting to work in radians.
- Adding the area of a hole instead of subtracting it.
- Measuring the angle with the vertical from the wrong edge.
- Not stating that G is vertically below the point of suspension.
- Comparing toppling and sliding without saying which angle is smaller.
