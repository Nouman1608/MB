These questions cover section **2.5 Proportion** of Topic 2 (Equations, formulae and identities) in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on it. Section 2.5 is **Higher tier only**, so every question here is labelled (Higher). A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, but show your working: most marks are for method.

## Questions

**1.** (Higher) y is directly proportional to x. When x = 8, y = 18.

**(a)** Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the value of y when x = 12. **[1]**

**2.** (Higher) The time, T hours, to paint a hall is inversely proportional to the number of painters, n. Four painters take 15 hours.

**(a)** Find a formula for T in terms of n. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how long six painters take. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many painters are needed to finish in 2.4 hours. **[1]**

**3.** (Higher) In a model of a falling object, the distance fallen, d metres, is directly proportional to the square of the time, t seconds. When t = 2, d = 19.6.

**(a)** Find a formula for d in terms of t. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate d when t = 5. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate t when d = 78.4. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher) y is inversely proportional to the square of x, where x > 0. When x = 5, y = 12.

**(a)** Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate y when x = 10. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate x when y = 48. **[2]**

**5.** (Higher) The brightness, I units, of a lamp seen from a distance d metres is inversely proportional to d². At 2 metres the brightness is 75 units. Calculate the brightness at 5 metres. **[3]**

**6.** (Higher) y is directly proportional to √x. When x = 36, y = 18.

**(a)** Calculate y when x = 100. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate x when y = 7.5. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher) Four graphs of y against x are described for x ≥ 0 and positive k.

- Graph A: a straight line through the origin.
- Graph B: a curve through the origin that gets steeper as x increases.
- Graph C: a curve through the origin that gets less steep as x increases.
- Graph D: a curve that falls as x increases and gets close to both axes without touching them.

**(a)** Match each graph to one of these relationships: y ∝ x², y ∝ 1/x, y ∝ x, y ∝ √x. **[4]**
**(b)** Graph A passes through (5, 8). Find the equation of graph A. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher)

**(a)** y is directly proportional to x³. x is increased by 50%. Calculate the percentage increase in y. **[2]**
**(b)** p is inversely proportional to q². q is decreased by 20%. Calculate the percentage change in p. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher) The table shows values of p and q.

| q | 2 | 3 | 6 | a |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p | 27 | 12 | 3 | 0.75 |

**(a)** Show that p is inversely proportional to q². **[2]**
**(b)** Write a formula for p in terms of q. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the value of a, given a > 0. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher) y is directly proportional to x². When x = 5, y = 50. x is inversely proportional to √z. When z = 4, x = 6. All values are positive.

**(a)** Find a formula for y in terms of x. **[2]**
**(b)** Find a formula for x in terms of z. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that y = 288/z. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate z when y = 4.5. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher) In a model, the time, t seconds, for one swing of a pendulum is directly proportional to the square root of its length, l cm. A pendulum of length 36 cm takes 1.2 seconds.

**(a)** Find a formula for t in terms of l. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate t when l = 81. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the length that gives a swing time of 2.5 seconds. **[2]**
**(d)** The length of a pendulum is increased by 44%. Calculate the percentage increase in its swing time. **[2]**
**(e)** Describe the shape of the graph of t against l. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher) A fixed mass of steel is made into identical ball bearings. The number made, N, is inversely proportional to the cube of the radius, r mm. When r = 3, N = 4000.

**(a)** Find a formula for N in terms of r. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate N when r = 6. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate r when N = 32 000. **[2]**
**(d)** State the effect on N of doubling the radius. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** y = kx and 18 = 8k [1]; k = 2.25, so **y = 2.25x** [1]
**(b)** 2.25 × 12 = **27** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The second mark in (a) needs the formula with the number in it; "k = 2.25" alone gains only the method mark.

**2. (a)** T = k/n and 15 = k/4 [1]; k = 60, so **T = 60/n** [1]
**(b)** 60/6 = **10 hours** [1]
**(c)** 2.4 = 60/n, n = 60/2.4 = **25 painters** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using T = kn here gives k = 3.75 and scores no marks; the words "inversely proportional" are where the method mark is won or lost.

**3. (a)** d = kt² and 19.6 = 4k [1]; k = 4.9, so **d = 4.9t²** [1]
**(b)** 4.9 × 5² = **122.5 m** [1]
**(c)** 78.4 = 4.9t², so t² = 16 [1]; **t = 4 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Stopping at t² = 16 earns the method mark only; the accuracy mark needs the square root taken.

**4. (a)** y = k/x² and 12 = k/25 [1]; k = 300, so **y = 300/x²** [1]
**(b)** 300/100 = **3** [1]
**(c)** 48 = 300/x², so x² = 300/48 = 6.25 [1]; **x = 2.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A common slip in (c) is x² = 48/300 = 0.16, which inverts the rearrangement and loses both marks; write x² = 300/48 before using the calculator.

**5.** I = k/d² and 75 = k/4 [1]; k = 300 [1]; I = 300/25 = **12 units** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The scale factor method also earns full marks: d is multiplied by 2.5, so I is divided by 2.5² = 6.25, giving 75 ÷ 6.25 = 12; both methods need the square to score.

**6. (a)** y = k√x and 18 = 6k [1]; k = 3 [1]; y = 3√100 = **30** [1]
**(b)** 7.5 = 3√x, so √x = 2.5 [1]; **x = 6.25** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Taking k = 18/36 = 0.5 (forgetting the square root) loses the method mark and everything that follows from it.

**7. (a)** **A: y ∝ x** [1]; **B: y ∝ x²** [1]; **C: y ∝ √x** [1]; **D: y ∝ 1/x** [1]
**(b)** y = kx and 8 = 5k [1]; **y = 1.6x** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each match is marked separately, so one swap (B and C, say) costs two marks; check "steeper" against "less steep" before you write.

**8. (a)** 1.5³ = 3.375 [1]; increase = **237.5%** [1]
**(b)** q is multiplied by 0.8, so p is multiplied by 1/0.8² [1]; 1/0.64 = 1.5625 [1]; p **increases by 56.25%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), 3.375 is the multiplier, not the answer; the accuracy mark needs it converted to a percentage increase, and "337.5%" is wrong.

**9. (a)** p × q² = 27 × 4 = 108, 12 × 9 = 108, 3 × 36 = 108 [1]; the product is constant, so p is inversely proportional to q² [1]
**(b)** **p = 108/q²** [1]
**(c)** 0.75 = 108/a², a² = 144 [1]; **a = 12** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs every product shown and a conclusion; testing only one pair of values does not show a constant and scores at most one mark.

**10. (a)** y = kx² and 50 = 25k [1]; **y = 2x²** [1]
**(b)** x = c/√z and 6 = c/2 [1]; **x = 12/√z** [1]
**(c)** y = 2 × (12/√z)² [1]; = 2 × 144/z = 288/z [1]
**(d)** 4.5 = 288/z [1]; z = 288/4.5 = **64** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the method mark is for substituting the whole expression for x and squaring it; writing y = 288/z without the line 2 × 144/z gains nothing, because the answer is given.

**11. (a)** t = k√l and 1.2 = 6k [1]; k = 0.2, so **t = 0.2√l** [1]
**(b)** 0.2 × 9 = **1.8 s** [1]
**(c)** 2.5 = 0.2√l, √l = 12.5 [1]; **l = 156.25 cm** [1]
**(d)** l multiplied by 1.44, so t multiplied by √1.44 = 1.2 [1]; **20% increase** [1]
**(e)** **A curve through the origin that gets less steep as l increases** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (d), answering 44% (as if t were directly proportional to l) gains no credit; the method mark is for applying the square root to the multiplier.

**12. (a)** N = k/r³ and 4000 = k/27 [1]; k = 108 000, so **N = 108 000/r³** [1]
**(b)** 108 000/216 = **500** [1]
**(c)** r³ = 108 000/32 000 = 3.375 [1]; **r = 1.5 mm** [1]
**(d)** **N is divided by 8** (2³ = 8) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the cube root is needed for the accuracy mark; giving r = 3.375 or using a square root (1.84) earns only the method mark for r³ = 3.375.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a constant that is added (y = x² + k) instead of multiplied (y = kx²).
- Missing "inversely" or "square" in the wording and choosing the wrong form.
- Giving the value of k when the question asks for a formula in terms of x.
- Leaving t² = 16 or √x = 2.5 without finishing the last step.
- Rearranging an inverse formula upside down, so x² = y/k instead of x² = k/y.
- Quoting a multiplier (3.375, 1.5625) instead of the percentage change asked for.
- Testing a single pair of values in a "show that" question about a table.
- Adding the percentage change instead of using a power of the multiplier.
- Describing a graph without saying whether it passes through the origin, or whether it gets steeper or less steep.
