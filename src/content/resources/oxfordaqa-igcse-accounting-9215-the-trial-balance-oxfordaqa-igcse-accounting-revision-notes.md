These notes condense "Prepare and understand the use of a trial balance" from section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification. It includes preparing a trial balance from a list of balances, correcting a trial balance, and explaining its use and limitations. All amounts are in dollars.

The full teaching, with worked examples, is in the [trial balance study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/). For marked questions, use the [trial balance practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trial balance | A list of all ledger balances at one date, in a debit column and a credit column |
| Balance c/d | Carried down: the difference written on the smaller side to make an account balance |
| Balance b/d | Brought down: the same amount on the opposite side at the start of the next period |
| Debit balance | Debits exceed credits: assets, expenses, drawings |
| Credit balance | Credits exceed debits: liabilities, capital, income |
| Balancing figure | An amount found by subtraction so that two totals agree |
| Redraft | Rewrite a trial balance with every balance in its correct column |

## Balancing off an account

```
1. Total each side.
2. Difference -> "Balance c/d" on the SMALLER side.
3. Equal totals on both sides; rule off.
4. "Balance b/d" on the OTHER side, dated the first day of the next period.
```

**Small reminder.** A supplier's account: debits 1,600 (payments and returns), credits 2,350 (purchases). Balance c/d 750 on the debit side; balance b/d 750 **credit**, so the business owes the supplier 750, a credit in the trial balance.

## Debit or credit? The memory line

```
DEBIT:  assets, expenses, drawings, purchases, sales returns, opening inventory
CREDIT: liabilities, capital, income, sales, purchases returns
```

The awkward ones:

| Debit | Credit |
|---|---|
| Discount allowed | Discount received |
| Carriage inwards and carriage outwards | Rent received, commission received |
| Irrecoverable debts | Accumulated depreciation |
| Loan interest paid | Loan, bank overdraft |

## Preparing the trial balance: method

```
1. Heading: "Trial balance of [business] at [date]".
2. Each balance in ONE column.
3. Group: non-current assets, current assets, liabilities,
   trading items, other income and expenses, drawings, capital.
4. Total both columns. Compare.
```

## Missing capital

If the question says capital is missing and the records are otherwise correct:

```
Capital = total debits − total credits (excluding capital)
```

**Small reminder.** Debits 84,600; credits without capital 61,250. Capital = **23,350**.

## Correcting a draft with items in the wrong column

- A balance in the wrong column changes the difference by **twice** its value.
- Equal numbers in the wrong columns can cancel and hide each other.
- Redraft the whole trial balance; do not just adjust the totals.

**Small reminder.** Draft: debits 40,500, credits 39,700 (difference 800). Discount received 400 was in the debit column. Moving it: debits 40,100, credits 40,100. Twice 400 = 800, the whole difference.

## Checking your own trial balance

- Count the balances in the question and the lines in your trial balance: the numbers must match.
- Tick each balance in the question as you place it, so none is used twice or missed.
- Add each column twice, once from the top and once from the bottom.
- If the totals differ, look first for a balance in the wrong column: half the difference may equal one of the balances.

## Uses and limitations

| Uses | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Checks total debits = total credits | Does not reveal commission, complete reversal, compensating, omission, original entry or principle |
| A single list of balances from which financial statements are prepared | Shows that an error exists, not where |
| Warns of addition, partial omission, transposition and unequal posting errors before statements are prepared | A balancing figure such as capital assumes no other errors |

## Exam traps

- Balance c/d placed on the larger side.
- Carriage outwards or discount allowed in the credit column.
- Accumulated depreciation in the debit column or netted off the asset.
- Bank overdraft in the debit column.
- Closing inventory included: the ledger at this stage holds opening inventory.
- No heading, or no date, on the trial balance.
- Adjusting totals instead of redrafting.

## Quick self-test

1. A bank account has debits of 7,300 and credits of 8,050. What is the balance b/d, and where does it go in the trial balance?
2. State the column for discount allowed.
3. State the column for purchases returns.
4. State the column for accumulated depreciation.
5. State the column for carriage inwards.
6. State the column for commission received.
7. Debits total 52,900; credits without capital total 37,450. Find the capital.
8. Discount allowed of 300 was put in the credit column. By how much does this make the totals differ?
9. A draft has debits 28,600 and credits 29,800. Rent received of 600 was put in the debit column. Is that the only error? Explain.
10. Give two uses of a trial balance.
11. Give one limitation of a trial balance, with an example.
12. Why should inventory at the start of the year, not the end, appear in the trial balance?

### Answers

1. Credits exceed debits by **750**: a **credit balance (overdraft)**, in the **credit** column.
2. **Debit.**
3. **Credit.**
4. **Credit.**
5. **Debit.**
6. **Credit.**
7. 52,900 − 37,450 = **15,450.**
8. Twice 300 = **600** (credits too high, debits too low).
9. Moving 600 gives debits 28,000 and credits 30,400, still 2,400 apart, so **there are other errors** to find.
10. Any two: checks that **total debits equal total credits**; provides a **list of balances** for the financial statements; **warns** of some errors.
11. For example, it **cannot reveal an error of commission**, such as a payment posted to the wrong supplier's account.
12. Closing inventory is counted at the year end and is **not yet in the ledger**; the ledger still shows the opening inventory.
