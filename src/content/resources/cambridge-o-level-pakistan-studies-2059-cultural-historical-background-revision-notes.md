**These revision notes compress Section 1 of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies 2059 Paper 1, Cultural and Historical Background to the Pakistan Movement, into recall tables.** Each row gives the person or event, the date, and why it mattered, which is what 4-mark "describe" and 7-mark "explain" questions ask for. Learn the significance column as carefully as the dates.

## Syllabus coverage

**2059 Paper 1, Section 1 (2026 syllabus):** Key Question 1 (religious thinkers), Key Question 2 (decline of the Mughal Empire), Key Question 3 (War of Independence 1857-58), Key Question 4 (Sir Syed Ahmad Khan) and Key Question 5 (Urdu and regional languages).

## Key Question 1: religious thinkers

| Person | Date | What they did | Significance |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shah Waliullah | 18th century | Translated the Qur'an into Persian; called for a return to the Qur'an and Hadith; worked for unity between schools of thought; wrote *Hujjat Allah al-Baligha* | Foundation for later reformers; his sons and students, including Shah Abdul Aziz, continued his work |
| Shah Waliullah and Ahmad Shah Abdali | 1761 | Wrote asking Abdali to defend Muslims against the Marathas; Abdali defeated them at Panipat | Marathas weakened, but Abdali returned to Afghanistan and left a power vacuum |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | 1826 | Declared jihad against the Sikhs from a base among the Pathan tribes; Mujahideen captured Peshawar in 1830 | Combined revival with political action |
| Syed Ahmad Barelvi | 1831 | Killed with Shah Ismail Shaheed at Balakot | Failed to free the Punjab and lost some tribal support, but the movement continued |
| Haji Shariatullah | 19th century | Founded the Faraizi Movement in Bengal; stressed the obligatory duties of Islam; declared India Dar-ul-Harb | Supported Muslim peasants against Hindu landlords and European indigo planters; his son Dudu Miyan organised it further |

## Key Question 2: decline of the Mughal Empire

**Internal causes:**
- Aurangzeb's long Deccan wars emptied the treasury and his religious policies alienated many subjects.
- Weak, short-lived successors after 1707 fought wars of succession.
- The empire was too large to control and governors became independent.
- Rise of the Marathas (spread across central and northern India, threatened Delhi) and the Sikhs (Banda Bahadur; Ranjit Singh's kingdom).

**External causes:**
- Foreign invasions from Persia and Afghanistan: Nadir Shah (1739, sack of Delhi) and Ahmad Shah Abdali.
- Expansion of the East India Company.

| Date | Event | Significance |
|---|---|---|
| 1757 | Battle of Plassey: Clive defeats Siraj-ud-Daulah; Mir Jafar made Nawab | Company gains Bengal, the base for later expansion |
| 1848-56 | Dalhousie's Doctrine of Lapse (Satara, Jhansi, Nagpur) | Lapsed states annexed; angered rulers |
| 1849 | Annexation of the Punjab after the Anglo-Sikh wars | Last major independent power in the north-west removed |
| 1856 | Annexation of Awadh | For alleged misgovernment, not the Doctrine of Lapse |
| 1893 | Durand Line agreed with the Amir of Afghanistan | Defensible frontier |
| 1901 | North-West Frontier Province created | Part of managing the tribes |

**Why the Company succeeded:** weak local rulers; divided Indian rulers; disciplined armies with modern weapons; Bengal revenue; subsidiary alliances; Doctrine of Lapse and annexation for "misgovernment"; defeat of the Sikhs.

**Why the British wanted a "scientific" frontier:** fear of Russian expansion; Afghanistan as a buffer state; control of passes such as the Khyber; stopping tribal raids; a defensible boundary; posts plus allowances to tribal leaders.

The syllabus says the two focus points on British expansion do not need detailed teaching.

## Key Question 3: War of Independence 1857-58

**Long-term causes:**
- Political: Doctrine of Lapse; annexation of Awadh; plan to end the Mughal title after Bahadur Shah Zafar.
- Economic: heavy land revenue; ruin of Indian crafts by British imports.
- Social and religious: missionary activity; reforms seen as interference with custom.
- Military: low pay and poor promotion for Indian soldiers; being sent overseas.

**Immediate cause:** greased cartridges for the Enfield rifle (rumoured cow and pig fat); punishment of soldiers who refused at Meerut; mutiny in May 1857 and march on Delhi.

**Consequence recorded in existing pages:** after 1857 the British blamed the Muslims for the War. (The other consequences, the course of the War and the reasons for failure are on the syllabus but are not covered here; use the syllabus and your class notes.)

## Key Question 4: Sir Syed Ahmad Khan

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Belief | Muslims needed Western education to rebuild trust with the British, to compete with Hindus for government jobs and to take part in political life |
| Religion and science | Western science not in conflict with Islam; education combined with Islamic teaching to overcome opposition from some ulama |
| Organisations | Scientific Society; Muhammadan Educational Conference (including translations into Urdu) |
| Aligarh College | Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental College; opened as a school in 1875; modelled on Oxford and Cambridge; residential; Western subjects and Islamic studies |
| Later | Became Aligarh Muslim University in 1920; trained many future leaders of the Pakistan Movement |
| Two-Nation Theory; Hindi-Urdu Controversy | On the syllabus; not covered here, use the syllabus and your class notes |

## Key Question 5: Urdu and regional languages

- Syllabus content: why Urdu was chosen as the national language; its advantages and disadvantages; promotion of Balochi, Punjabi, Pushto and Sindhi.
- Disadvantages: first language of only a small proportion of people; resentment in East Pakistan, where Bengali was the majority language; protesters killed in Dhaka, February 1952; regional cultures felt undervalued; Sindhi-Urdu tension and language riots in Sindh, 1972; English still used in government, law and higher education.
- 1956 Constitution: Urdu and Bengali national languages.

## Exam traps

- Confusing Syed Ahmad Barelvi (Jihad Movement) with Sir Syed Ahmad Khan (Aligarh Movement).
- Writing about the Faraizi Movement in an answer on the Jihad Movement.
- Saying Awadh was annexed under the Doctrine of Lapse.
- Writing only about the cartridges in a 14-mark answer on the causes of 1857-58, with no counter-argument.
- Treating the three reformers separately without comparing their contributions.
- Naming a person or event without saying why it mattered.

## How long should each Section B answer be?

The 4-mark part is a short factual paragraph; the 7-mark part needs at least two explained paragraphs; the 14-mark part is an essay of about two pages in continuous prose that compares factors and reaches a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Name three things Shah Waliullah did to revive Islam.
2. Where and when was Syed Ahmad Barelvi killed?
3. Give two internal and two external causes of Mughal decline.
4. Which battle of 1757 gave the Company control of Bengal?
5. What was the immediate cause of the War of 1857-58?
6. When did Sir Syed's college open as a school, and what did it teach?
7. Give two disadvantages of choosing Urdu as the national language.

**Answers:** 1. Translated the Qur'an into Persian; called for a return to the Qur'an and Hadith; worked for unity between schools of thought (also: wrote to Abdali, wrote *Hujjat Allah al-Baligha*). 2. At Balakot, in 1831. 3. Internal: Aurangzeb's policies, weak successors, size of the empire, Maratha and Sikh rise; external: invasions from Persia and Afghanistan, East India Company expansion. 4. Plassey. 5. The greased cartridges for the new Enfield rifle and the mutiny at Meerut in May 1857. 6. 1875; Western subjects such as science and English alongside Islamic studies. 7. Spoken first by only a small proportion of people; resentment and the language movement in East Pakistan; Sindhi-Urdu tension; English still dominant in government.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was Shah Waliullah important?**
His Persian translation of the Qur'an made it accessible to more Muslims, and he called for a return to the Qur'an and Hadith and for unity between schools of thought.

**What was the Doctrine of Lapse?**
A policy of Lord Dalhousie under which a dependent state without a natural male heir passed to the Company.
