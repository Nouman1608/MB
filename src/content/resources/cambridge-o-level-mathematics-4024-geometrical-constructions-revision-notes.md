These notes summarise section 4.2, Geometrical constructions, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [geometrical constructions study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-constructions/), and exam-style questions are in the [geometrical constructions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-constructions-practice/).

## What is examined

| Outcome | Key point |
|---|---|
| Measure and draw lines and angles | use a ruler for every straight edge |
| Construct a triangle from three sides | ruler and compasses only, construction arcs must be shown |
| Draw, use and interpret nets | cubes, cuboids, prisms and pyramids; use the net to find surface area and volume |
| Not required | constructing perpendicular bisectors and angle bisectors |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Construct | make an accurate drawing with the stated instruments |
| Construction arc | a compass arc left on the page as evidence of the method |
| Protractor | an instrument for measuring and drawing angles |
| Net | a flat pattern that folds into a solid |
| Surface area | the total area of all faces, equal to the area of the net |
| Slant height | the height of a sloping face, measured along the face |
| Vertical height | the height of a solid measured at right angles to the base |
| Reflex angle | an angle between 180° and 360° |

## Measuring and drawing angles

1. Put the centre mark of the protractor on the vertex.
2. Put the baseline along one arm.
3. Read the scale that starts at 0 on that arm.
4. Check the size is sensible: acute under 90°, obtuse over 90°.
5. Extend the arms if they are too short to reach the scale.

A reflex angle is 360° minus the angle measured on the other side.

## Method box: triangle from three sides

1. Draw the longest side as the base.
2. Set the compasses to the second side. Draw an arc from one end.
3. Set the compasses to the third side. Draw an arc from the other end.
4. Mark where the arcs cross and join with a ruler.
5. Leave all arcs.

A triangle exists only if the two shorter sides add up to more than the longest side. For 12, 5 and 6, the sum 5 + 6 = 11 is less than 12, so the arcs would not meet.

## Method box: rhombus from two triangles

1. Draw one diagonal, for example AC.
2. Use the side length as the radius to draw arcs above and below the diagonal from both ends.
3. Join the four points.

The diagonals bisect each other at 90°. If the half-diagonals are p and q and the side is s, then p² + q² = s².

## Nets and solids

| Solid | Faces in the net |
|---|---|
| Cube | 6 squares |
| Cuboid | 6 rectangles in three equal pairs |
| Triangular prism | 2 triangles and 3 rectangles |
| Pentagonal prism | 2 pentagons and 5 rectangles |
| Square-based pyramid | 1 square and 4 triangles |
| Hexagonal pyramid | 1 hexagon and 6 triangles |
| Cylinder | 2 circles and 1 rectangle of length 2πr |

## Formulas from nets

```
surface area = total area of the faces in the net
cuboid:   2(lw + lh + wh)
prism:    2 × cross-section + perimeter × length
pyramid:  base + sum of triangle faces
volume of prism   = cross-section area × length
volume of pyramid = 1/3 × base area × vertical height
```

The volume and curved surface formulas for prisms, cylinders and pyramids are on the formula list in the examination papers. The area of a rectangle, parallelogram and trapezium must be known.

## Slant height and vertical height

For a square-based pyramid with base side b and slant height l, the vertical height h satisfies

```
h² + (b/2)² = l²
```

Example: b = 8, l = 5 gives h² = 25 − 16 = 9, so h = 3.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Contrast |
|---|---|
| Net | folds into a solid; a pattern of faces, not a photo |
| Measure | read from a drawing; accuracy is limited |
| Calculate | use given dimensions; exact |
| Slant height | along the face |
| Vertical height | perpendicular to the base |

## Exam traps

- Erasing arcs. They are the evidence.
- Reading the wrong protractor scale.
- A row of six squares is not a net of a cube.
- Using slant height in a volume formula.
- Giving the area of the net without the units, or writing cm instead of cm².
- Drawing a triangle with the longest side set up as a short arc, so the arcs never meet on the page.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Construct | make an accurate drawing, showing the arcs |
| Draw | use a ruler and measure carefully |
| Measure | give a reading from the diagram |
| Calculate | work out from given facts |
| Show that | give structured evidence for a stated result |

## Self-test

1. Can a triangle with sides 12 cm, 5 cm and 6 cm be constructed? Explain.
2. A cube has edges of 5 cm. Find its surface area and volume.
3. A cuboid measures 6 cm by 4 cm by 2 cm. Find its surface area and volume.
4. A prism has a right-angled triangle with sides 5 cm, 12 cm and 13 cm as its cross-section. It is 8 cm long. Find its surface area and volume.
5. A square-based pyramid has base side 8 cm and slant height 5 cm. Find its vertical height, surface area and volume.
6. An angle of 72° is measured on the other side of a reflex angle. Find the reflex angle.
7. A rhombus has side 10 cm and one diagonal 12 cm. Find the other diagonal and the area.
8. The net of a cylinder of radius 5 cm has a rectangle. Find its length to 3 significant figures.
9. How many faces does the net of a pentagonal prism have?
10. How many triangles does the net of a hexagonal pyramid have?

### Answers

1. **No**. 5 + 6 = 11, which is less than 12, so the arcs would not meet.
2. SA = 6 × 25 = **150 cm²**; V = **125 cm³**.
3. SA = 2(24 + 8 + 12) = **88 cm²**; V = **48 cm³**.
4. Ends: 2 × 30 = 60. Strip: 30 × 8 = 240. SA = **300 cm²**. V = 30 × 8 = **240 cm³**.
5. h² = 25 − 16 = 9, so **h = 3 cm**. SA = 64 + 4 × (1/2 × 8 × 5) = **144 cm²**. V = 1/3 × 64 × 3 = **64 cm³**.
6. 360 − 72 = **288°**.
7. Half the known diagonal is 6, so the half of the other is √(100 − 36) = 8, giving **16 cm**. Area = 1/2 × 12 × 16 = **96 cm²**.
8. 2π × 5 = 31.4, so **31.4 cm**.
9. 2 pentagons plus 5 rectangles gives **7 faces**.
10. **6 triangles** (plus one hexagon).
