These notes condense **section 1.4, Powers and roots**, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. Statements 1.4 A–E are for both tiers; surds and fractional powers are **Higher tier only**. For explanations and worked examples, use the [section 1.4 study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-powers-and-roots-edexcel-igcse-maths/).

## Condensed recall: both tiers

**Squares to 15² and cubes to 5³, and 10³:** 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81, 100, 121, 144, 169, 196, 225; 1, 8, 27, 64, 125, 1000.

**Roots:** √ gives the positive root; ∛ keeps the sign, so ∛(−64) = −4.

**Index laws (same base):**

| Law | Example |
|---|---|
| aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ | 7² × 7⁻⁵ = 7⁻³ |
| aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ | 4³ ÷ 4⁻¹ = 4⁴ |
| (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ | (3⁻²)⁴ = 3⁻⁸ |
| a⁰ = 1 | 19⁰ = 1 |
| a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ | 6⁻² = 1/36 |

**Prime factorisation:** divide by primes until 1 is reached; write as powers in ascending order. 540 = 2² × 3³ × 5.

**HCF:** common primes, lowest powers. **LCM:** all primes, highest powers. HCF × LCM = product of the two numbers.

**Tests:** all powers even → square number; all powers multiples of 3 → cube number. To make n × k a square, k supplies each prime with an odd power once more.

## Condensed recall: Higher tier only

**Surd rules:** √(ab) = √a√b; √(a/b) = √a/√b; (√a)² = a.

- Simplify with the largest square factor: √72 = 6√2.
- Collect like surds: 3√5 + √20 = 3√5 + 2√5 = 5√5.
- Difference of two squares: (a + √b)(a − √b) = a² − b.
- Rationalise k/√a: × √a/√a. Rationalise k/(a + √b): × (a − √b)/(a − √b).

**Fractional and negative powers:**

- a^(1/n) = ⁿ√a; a^(m/n) = (ⁿ√a)ᵐ; a^(−n) = 1/aⁿ.
- Order of work: deal with the minus (flip), then the root (denominator), then the power (numerator).
- 125^(−2/3): flip → 1/125^(2/3); cube root 5; square 25; answer 1/25.

## Worked recall

**Mixed index work (both tiers).** (3⁴ × 3⁻¹) ÷ 3⁵ = 3³ ÷ 3⁵ = 3⁻² = 1/9.

**HCF and LCM in context.** Two bells ring every 18 and 24 minutes. 18 = 2 × 3² and 24 = 2³ × 3, so the LCM is 2³ × 3² = 72: they ring together every 72 minutes.

**Surd area (Higher).** A square has side (1 + √3) cm. Its area is (1 + √3)² = 1 + 2√3 + 3 = (4 + 2√3) cm².

**Fractional power (Higher).** (0.25)^(−1/2) = (1/4)^(−1/2) = 4^(1/2) = 2.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Square number | The result of multiplying an integer by itself |
| Cube number | The result of n × n × n for an integer n |
| Index (power, exponent) | The small raised number showing repeated multiplication |
| Base | The number being raised to a power |
| Prime factor | A prime that divides a number exactly |
| Product of powers of prime factors | A number written like 2³ × 5² × 7 |
| HCF | The largest number that is a factor of each number |
| LCM | The smallest number that is a multiple of each number |
| Surd (Higher) | An irrational root, such as √3, left in root form |
| Rationalise (Higher) | Rewrite a fraction so the denominator has no surd |

## Exam traps

- **Bases stay the same:** 5³ × 5⁴ = 5⁷, never 25⁷.
- **Negative power ≠ negative number:** 10⁻² = 0.01.
- **Zero power is 1**, whatever the base (except 0).
- **Index form in factorisation:** 2 × 2 × 2 × 3 is not the final answer; write 2³ × 3.
- **HCF vs LCM:** the HCF is no larger than the smaller number; the LCM is no smaller than the larger.
- **Roots do not add:** √36 + √64 = 14, but √100 = 10.
- **Conjugate sign (Higher):** to rationalise 1/(3 − √2), multiply by (3 + √2).
- **Fractional power order (Higher):** for 27^(2/3), root first (3), then square (9).

## Self-test

1. Write down a number between 50 and 100 that is both a square and a cube.
2. Work out ∛(−216).
3. Simplify 8⁻³ × 8⁵ ÷ 8⁻¹ as a single power of 8.
4. Write 540 as a product of powers of its prime factors.
5. Find the HCF and LCM of 540 and 126.
6. 2⁴ × 3³ × 5 × k is a square number. Find the smallest possible whole number k.
7. Explain why 2⁵ × 3² is not a square number.
8. (Higher) Simplify √98 − √32.
9. (Higher) Expand and simplify (5 + √3)(5 − √3).
10. (Higher) Rationalise 6/√12, simplifying fully.
11. (Higher) Evaluate 16^(−3/2).
12. (Higher) Find x if 9ˣ = 27.

**Answers**

1. 64 (8² and 4³).
2. −6.
3. 8^(−3 + 5 + 1) = 8³.
4. 2² × 3³ × 5.
5. 126 = 2 × 3² × 7. HCF = 2 × 3² = 18; LCM = 2² × 3³ × 5 × 7 = 3780.
6. The odd powers are 3³ and 5¹, so k = 3 × 5 = 15.
7. The power of 2 is 5, which is odd.
8. 7√2 − 4√2 = 3√2.
9. 25 − 3 = 22.
10. 6√12/12 = √12/2 = 2√3/2 = √3.
11. 1/16^(3/2) = 1/4³ = 1/64.
12. 3^(2x) = 3³, so x = 3/2.
