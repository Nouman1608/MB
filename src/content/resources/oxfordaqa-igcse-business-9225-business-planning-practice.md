All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Sunrise Smoothies.

> **Case study: Sunrise Smoothies**
>
> Dev plans to open Sunrise Smoothies, a stall selling fruit smoothies in
> a city park. His business plan says he needs $18,000 for equipment and
> a licence. He will put in $6,000 of savings and wants to borrow $12,000
> from a bank. Fixed costs will be $3,000 a month (stall rent, insurance
> and a part-time assistant's salary). Fruit and cups will cost $1.40 for
> each smoothie. He will charge $4.00 and forecasts sales of 2,500
> smoothies a month. The bank manager has asked about the risk that
> sales are lower, because it is Dev's first business and he has not
> carried out any market research.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a fixed cost? **[1]**
- A. Fruit
- B. Paper cups
- C. Insurance
- D. Sales commission

**2.** Which **one** of the following is revenue? **[1]**
- A. The money received from selling goods
- B. The cost of making goods
- C. The money left after paying costs
- D. The money invested by the owner

**3.** State **two** reasons why a business writes a business plan. **[2]**

**4.** Define the term "total costs". **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** drawback of business planning. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate Dev's forecast monthly revenue. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate Dev's forecast total monthly costs. **[3]**

**8.** Calculate Dev's forecast monthly profit. **[1]**

**9.** In the first month Dev sells only 1,200 smoothies. Calculate his profit or loss. **[3]**

**10.** State **two** sections of a business plan that would help Dev to persuade the bank to lend him $12,000. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** reasons why a bank manager might want to see market research in Dev's plan. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate whether the business plan will be useful to Dev. Use your answers to Questions 8 and 9. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **A** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any two: to help set up a new business [1]; to raise finance [1]; to set objectives [1]; to organise the functions of the business [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The sum of the fixed costs [1] and the variable costs [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A plan takes time and effort to write [1], which the owner could have spent running the business [1]. Accept forecasts may be wrong [1], so decisions based on them could be poor [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Revenue = 2,500 x $4.00 [1] = **$10,000** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Variable costs = 2,500 x $1.40 = $3,500 [1]; total costs = $3,000 + $3,500 [1] = **$6,500** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** $10,000 - $6,500 = **$3,500** [1]. **[1]**

**9.** Revenue = 1,200 x $4.00 = $4,800 [1]; total costs = $3,000 + (1,200 x $1.40 = $1,680) = $4,680 [1]; profit = $4,800 - $4,680 = **$120** profit [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Any two: the financial plan [1]; market research or the target market [1]; the marketing plan [1]; the aims and objectives [1]. **[2]**

**11.** **Evidence of demand:** the forecast of 2,500 smoothies a month is only an estimate [1], and research would show whether enough people in the park would buy at $4.00 [1].
**Reducing risk:** research on competitors and customers could show whether sales are likely to be lower [1], so the bank would know whether Dev can repay the $12,000 [1]. **[4]**

**12.** **Useful:** the plan makes Dev think about the costs and sales he needs [1]; it shows a forecast profit of $3,500 a month, which can persuade the bank [1]; comparing with the first month, when profit was only $120, shows the plan can be used to spot problems early [1]; it helps him decide how to reduce risk, for example by cutting fixed costs [1].
**Limited use:** the forecast of 2,500 was not based on market research, so it was too optimistic, because 1,200 smoothies gave only $120 profit [1]; fixed costs of $3,000 a month would still have to be paid [1]; a plan does not make customers buy [1].
**Judgement:** the plan is useful because it identifies the risk and the break-even level of sales, but only if Dev updates it using real sales figures and market research [1]. Dev should treat the forecast as a target and prepare a cautious scenario [1]. **[9]**

## Tips for the longer questions

- In Question 11, give a reason for each of the two points: say what market research shows and how that helps the bank judge risk.
- In Question 12, use both sets of figures: the forecast month (profit $3,500) and the first month (profit $120). Comparing them shows how a plan helps spot problems.
- Finish with a judgement and a condition, such as "provided Dev reviews the plan each month".
- Check each answer shows the working for revenue, then costs, then profit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using $4.00 instead of $1.40 per smoothie for variable costs.
- Forgetting to add fixed costs to variable costs to find total costs.
- Calculating profit as revenue minus variable costs only.
- Writing that a plan "makes the business successful".
- Not explaining why a bank wants market research in the plan.
