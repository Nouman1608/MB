## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Explain what "equivalence" means in MYP mathematics. **[1]**
**(b)** A student says (x + 1)² is equivalent to x² + 1. Justify whether this is true, using a value of x. **[2]**

**2.** Name the three related concepts that the guide lists for all four branches of the framework. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** Estimate 7.9 × 31.2 by rounding the first number to the nearest whole number and the second to the nearest ten. **[1]**
**(b)** The exact value is 246.48. Find the error in your estimate. **[1]**
**(c)** State whether the estimate is too high or too low, and explain why in terms of the rounding. **[2]**

**4.** The area covered by lilies on a pond grows from 0.8 m² to 2.0 m² in 4 weeks.
**(a)** Find the rate of change in m² per week. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the percentage increase. **[2]**

**5.** Figures 1, 2 and 3 of a tile pattern use 7, 11 and 15 tiles.
**(a)** Find a rule for the number of tiles in figure n. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the number of tiles in figure 12. **[1]**
**(c)** Which figure uses 87 tiles? **[2]**

**6.** A mobile plan costs C = 12 + 0.8g, where C is the monthly cost and g is the data used in GB.
**(a)** Find the cost when 7.5 GB is used. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the most data that can be used with a budget of 25. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe one way the model may not match real use. **[1]**

**7.** A relationship is given by y = 5 − 2x.
**(a)** Complete a table for x = 0, 1, 2 and 3. **[2]**
**(b)** State the gradient and the y-intercept of the graph. **[2]**
**(c)** Represent the same relationship in words. **[1]**

**8.** **(a)** Simplify (12a³b) / (8ab²). **[3]**
**(b)** A round pond is modelled as a cylinder with radius 1.5 m and depth 0.8 m. Find its volume to 3 significant figures, and name the related concept shown by treating the pond as a cylinder. **[2]**

**9.** A model of a store room is built to scale 1:25. The model measures 12 cm by 8 cm by 6 cm.
**(a)** Find the real dimensions in metres. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the volume of the model in cm³ and of the real room in m³. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that the volume of the real room is 25³ times the volume of the model. **[2]**

**10.** **(a)** Write 110101 (base 2) as a base 10 number. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 29 as a base 2 number. **[2]**

**11.** A rectangular garden has length (x + 3) m and width x m, and its area is 40 m².
**(a)** Show that x² + 3x − 40 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Solve the equation. **[2]**
**(c)** State which solution is valid, giving a reason, and write the dimensions of the garden. **[2]**

**12.** A school rain tank is monitored. Its volume V litres is recorded t hours after a leak starts.

| t (hours) | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| V (litres) | 1500 | 1380 | 1260 | 1140 |

**(a)** Find the rate of change of V. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a model for V in terms of t. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your model to find when the tank would be empty. **[1]**
**(d)** Comment on the validity of using the model for t = 40. **[2]**
**(e)** Name one related concept used in part (b) and one used in part (d). **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Equivalence means two statements, quantities or expressions are identically equal, so one can replace the other [1].
**(b)** Try x = 1: (1 + 1)² = 4 [1] but 1² + 1 = 2, so they differ and the claim is **false** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* One value that breaks the claim is a full justification. A value that works, such as x = 0, would not show equivalence.

**2.** **Equivalence [1], quantity [1] and systems [1].** **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Learn the branch table as a table, not a list. Naming a concept that appears in only some branches loses the mark.

**3. (a)** 7.9 ≈ 8 and 31.2 ≈ 30, so 8 × 30 = **240** [1].
**(b)** Error = 246.48 − 240 = **6.48** [1].
**(c)** **Too low** [1]. The 31.2 was rounded down by about 3.8%, which is more than the 1.3% by which 7.9 was rounded up, so the product falls short [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Too low" with no reason earns little. Link the direction of the error to what you rounded up or down.

**4. (a)** Change = 2.0 − 0.8 = 1.2 m² [1]. Rate = 1.2 ÷ 4 = **0.3 m² per week** [1].
**(b)** 1.2 ÷ 0.8 [1] × 100 = **150%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change is measured against the starting value. Dividing by 2.0 gives 60%, which answers a different question.

**5. (a)** The terms rise by 4, so the rule has 4n [1]. At n = 1, 4n = 4, so add 3: **4n + 3** [1].
**(b)** 4 × 12 + 3 = **51** [1].
**(c)** 4n + 3 = 87 [1], so 4n = 84 and n = **21**, so figure 21 [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the rule against all three given terms before using it. A rule that fits only the first term will fail part (b).

**6. (a)** C = 12 + 0.8 × 7.5 [1] = **18** [1].
**(b)** 12 + 0.8g ≤ 25 [1], so g ≤ 13 ÷ 0.8 = **16.25 GB** [1].
**(c)** For example, the real plan may charge extra after a data limit, or round usage up to whole GB [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A limitation must be about the situation (price changes, rounding, a cap). Saying "the model is not accurate" is too vague.

**7. (a)** x = 0, 1, 2, 3 gives y = **5, 3, 1, −1** [2].
**(b)** Gradient **−2** [1], y-intercept **5** [1].
**(c)** Start at 5 and subtract 2 for every increase of 1 in x [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Each representation should agree with the others. Check your gradient against the drop in your table.

**8. (a)** Divide by 4: (3a³b) / (2ab²) [1]. Cancel a: a³ ÷ a = a² [1]. Cancel b: b ÷ b² = 1/b, giving **3a² / (2b)** [1].
**(b)** V = π × 1.5² × 0.8 = **5.65 m³** [1]. The related concept is **simplification** (or models) [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the factor you divide by. A test value, such as a = 2 and b = 1, is a quick way to check you have not changed the expression.

**9. (a)** 12 × 25 = 300 cm = **3 m**; 8 × 25 = 200 cm = **2 m**; 6 × 25 = 150 cm = **1.5 m** [2].
**(b)** Model: 12 × 8 × 6 = **576 cm³** [1]. Real: 3 × 2 × 1.5 = **9 m³** [1].
**(c)** 25³ = 15 625 [1]. 576 × 15 625 = 9 000 000 cm³ = 9 m³, matching part (b) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep units with each line. The conversion from cm³ to m³ divides by 1 000 000, which is not the same as 100.

**10. (a)** 32 + 16 + 0 + 4 + 0 + 1 [1] = **53** [1].
**(b)** 29 = 16 + 8 + 4 + 0 + 1 [1], so the digits are **11101** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the place values above your digits (32, 16, 8, 4, 2, 1). It makes errors in position easy to spot.

**11. (a)** Area: x(x + 3) = 40 [1], so x² + 3x = 40 and x² + 3x − 40 = 0 [1].
**(b)** (x + 8)(x − 5) = 0 [1], so x = −8 or x = 5 [1].
**(c)** A width cannot be negative, so **x = 5** is valid [1]. The garden is **5 m by 8 m** (5 × 8 = 40) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" part, the final line must be the given equation. In part (c), the reason must refer to the context.

**12. (a)** Change = 1380 − 1500 = −120 in 2 hours [1], so the rate is **−60 litres per hour** [1].
**(b)** The starting value is 1500 [1], so **V = 1500 − 60t** [1].
**(c)** 1500 − 60t = 0 gives **t = 25 hours** [1].
**(d)** At t = 40 the model gives V = 1500 − 2400 = −900 [1]. A tank cannot hold a negative volume, so the model is not valid beyond t = 25 [1].
**(e)** Part (b): models, or patterns or representation. Part (d): validity [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment on validity needs a value or fact from the model and a statement about the situation. "It is wrong" alone is not enough.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a concept with its everyday meaning instead of the guide's glossary sense.
- Listing concepts for a branch from memory, and including space outside spatial reasoning.
- Testing equivalence with a single value that happens to work.
- Not saying whether an estimate is too high or too low, or not giving a reason.
- Calculating a percentage change against the final value instead of the starting value.
- Fitting a rule to one term and not testing it on the others.
- Forgetting units, or converting cm³ to m³ by the wrong factor.
- Keeping a negative length because it solves the equation.
- Commenting on a model's validity without a value, a fact or the context.
