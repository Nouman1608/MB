These revision notes are for **higher level (HL) only**. The Cold War in Asia (1949 to 2002) is a regional study examined on Paper 3, which standard level students do not sit. They condense the IB *History guide*, first assessment 2028. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-cold-war-in-asia/) first, then use the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-cold-war-in-asia-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Containment | The US policy of stopping the spread of communism |
| Domino theory | The belief that if one state fell to communism, neighbours would follow |
| 38th parallel | The line dividing Korea in 1945 |
| DMZ | The demilitarised zone set up in Korea in 1953 |
| Juche | North Korea's ideology of self-reliance |
| Geneva Accords | 1954 agreements that divided Vietnam at the 17th parallel |
| Vietcong | Communist guerrillas in South Vietnam, linked to the National Liberation Front |
| Search and destroy | US tactic of finding and eliminating enemy units and bases |
| Vietnamization | Nixon's policy of handing the fighting to South Vietnamese forces |
| Mujahideen | Afghan resistance fighters ("those who struggle") |
| Saur Revolution | The April 1978 coup that brought the PDPA to power |
| Taliban | Religious-student movement that ruled most of Afghanistan from 1996 |

## Korea: causes at three levels

| Level | Cause |
|---|---|
| Local | Division of the country; rival regimes; Rhee's and Kim's wish to reunify; border clashes |
| Regional | Communist victory in China (1949); Mao's agreement |
| Global | Cold War rivalry; Stalin's approval; US containment; Soviet atomic bomb |

## Korea: impact checklist

- **Political:** armistice and DMZ; authoritarian rule in both Koreas; Park's coup in 1961.
- **Social:** millions dead; divided families; refugees.
- **Economic and environmental:** destruction; aid-led reconstruction; the south's growth from the 1960s.
- **Regional:** China's prestige and losses; Japan's boom and the 1952 treaties.

## Vietnam: causes

1. Colonial legacy and the Japanese occupation (including the 1944 to 1945 famine).
2. First Indochina War; Điện Biên Phủ.
3. Geneva Accords; failure of the 1956 elections.
4. Leaders: Hồ Chí Minh (communist nationalist) against Diệm (Catholic anti-communist).
5. Great powers: US containment; Soviet and Chinese aid to the north.

## Vietnam: impact points to check

| Area | Detail |
|---|---|
| US tactics | Bombing, search and destroy, defoliants, napalm |
| Communist tactics | Guerrilla war, tunnels, Hồ Chí Minh Trail, Tet |
| Political | US protest, Vietnamization, 1973 accords, reunification in 1976 |
| Economic and environmental | Destroyed land; chemical damage; unexploded bombs; boat people |
| Cambodia | Secret bombing, Lon Nol, Khmer Rouge |

## Afghanistan: causes

- Weak central state and diverse society.
- PDPA coup (1978) and rapid reforms met resistance.
- Mujahideen supported by Pakistan, the USA, Saudi Arabia and China.
- Soviet intervention in December 1979 to save a client government.

## Afghanistan: impact

| Phase | Detail |
|---|---|
| 1979 to 1989 | Soviet occupation; guerrilla war; about a million Afghans killed; millions of refugees |
| 1989 to 1992 | Soviet withdrawal; Najibullah holds on; his regime falls |
| 1992 to 1996 | Civil war among mujahideen groups |
| 1996 to 2001 | Taliban rule; strict Sharia; al-Qaeda hosted |
| 2001 | US-led intervention after 11 September |

## Perspectives

- Korea: traditionalist against Cumings's civil-war thesis; archive-based work (Weathersby, Chen Jian).
- Vietnam: orthodox, revisionist and post-revisionist; Logevall.
- Afghanistan: Westad on the Soviet decision; Coll on American involvement.

## Worked example: a short plan

**Question:** "Evaluate the impact of the Vietnam conflict on Vietnam, to 1985."

1. Thesis: the impact was devastating and lasting, but political outcomes were decisive.
2. Human cost.
3. Environment and economy.
4. Politics: reunification, the one-party state, the refugee crisis.
5. Regional: Cambodia and the 1979 war with China.
6. Judgement with perspectives.

## Common mistakes

- Treating Korea, Vietnam and Afghanistan as identical.
- Ignoring the Korean and Vietnamese leaders as actors.
- Omitting the Taliban in a question on Afghanistan to 2002.
- Forgetting Cambodia.

## Self-test

1. Where was Korea divided?
2. Which Soviet policy allowed the UN to act in June 1950?
3. What did the Geneva Accords of 1954 decide?
4. What was the Tet Offensive?
5. Which communist movement took Phnom Penh in April 1975?
6. What was the Saur Revolution?
7. Which three states backed the mujahideen?
8. When did the Taliban take Kabul?

**Answers:** 1. At the 38th parallel. 2. The Soviet boycott of the Security Council. 3. Temporary division at the 17th parallel and elections in 1956 that were never held. 4. A large communist attack in January 1968 that failed militarily but changed US opinion. 5. The Khmer Rouge. 6. The April 1978 coup that brought the PDPA to power. 7. Any three from the USA, Pakistan, Saudi Arabia and China. 8. 1996.

## Frequently asked questions

**Must I study all three conflicts?**
Prepare all six inquiry topics, since each exam question may name one conflict.

**How do I compare the conflicts?**
Use a common frame: local causes, great-power roles, tactics, civilian impact, long-term effects.

**What should I do if I cannot remember a date?**
Give the year or "in the early 1950s" and keep the argument going. A clear sequence of events matters more than an exact day.
