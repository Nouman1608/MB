## Section A

**1.** Name the unit of power and give its symbol. **[2]**

**2.** Convert each value into the unit shown.

**(a)** 2.4 kJ into J **[1]**
**(b)** 350 g into kg **[1]**
**(c)** 3.0 minutes into s **[1]**

**3.** Which one of these is a unit of energy: N, W, J or m/s? **[1]**

**4.** Explain why efficiency has no unit. **[1]**

**5.** Use the equations for work done and power to show that one watt is the same as one newton metre per second. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** An electric motor lifts a 2.5 kg mass through a height of 80 cm in 4.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained by the mass. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the useful power output of the motor. **[2]**
**(c)** The motor is supplied with 50 J of electrical energy during the lift. Calculate its efficiency. **[2]**

**7.** A cyclist and bicycle have a total mass of 80 kg and travel at 36 km/h.

**(a)** Calculate their kinetic energy in kJ. **[3]**
**(b)** The brakes bring the cyclist to rest in a distance of 20 m. Calculate the average braking force, assuming all the kinetic energy is transferred by the work done by the brakes. **[2]**

**8.** A kettle has a power of 2.4 kW and is switched on for 2.5 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred by the kettle, in MJ. **[3]**
**(b)** A student gives the answer as 6.0 kJ. Explain the mistake the student has made. **[2]**

**9.** A student rearranges KE = ½ × m × v² and writes v = 2 × KE ÷ m.

**(a)** Use units to show that this rearrangement cannot be correct. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the correct rearrangement. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the speed of a 0.80 kg ball with 90 J of kinetic energy. **[2]**

**10.** A wind turbine has an electrical power output of 2.5 MW.

**(a)** Calculate the energy it transfers electrically in one hour, in joules. Give your answer in standard form. **[3]**
**(b)** Write your answer to (a) in MJ. **[1]**

**11.** A crane lifts a 1500 kg load through 12 m in 1.5 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the useful power output of the crane, in kW. **[4]**
**(b)** The crane's motor has an input power of 2.5 kW. Calculate the efficiency of the crane as a percentage. **[2]**

**12.** A student's notes contain three errors in units:

- the kinetic energy of a trolley is 450 W;
- the efficiency of a lamp is 65 J;
- the power of a motor is 3.0 kJ.

For each, state the correct unit and explain why the student's unit is wrong. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The watt [1]; W [1].

**2. (a)** 2400 J [1].
**(b)** 0.35 kg [1].
**(c)** 180 s [1].

**3.** J [1].

**4.** It is a ratio of two energies measured in the same unit (joules), so the units cancel [1].

**5.** Work done = force × distance, so 1 J = 1 N m [1]. Power = work done ÷ time, so 1 W = 1 J/s = 1 N m/s [1].

**6. (a)** h = 80 cm = 0.80 m [1]. GPE = m × g × h = 2.5 × 10 × 0.80 [1] = 20 J [1].
**(b)** P = W ÷ t = 20 ÷ 4.0 [1] = 5.0 W [1].
**(c)** efficiency = useful energy output ÷ total energy output × 100% = 20 ÷ 50 × 100% [1] = 40% (or 0.40) [1].

**7. (a)** v = 36 ÷ 3.6 = 10 m/s [1]. KE = 0.5 × 80 × 10² = 4000 J [1] = 4.0 kJ [1].
**(b)** Work done by brakes = force × distance, so F = 4000 ÷ 20 [1] = 200 N [1].

**8. (a)** P = 2400 W and t = 2.5 × 60 = 150 s [1]. E = P × t = 2400 × 150 = 360 000 J [1] = 0.36 MJ [1].
**(b)** The student multiplied 2.4 by 2.5 without converting, so used kW with minutes [1]. Power must be in W and time in s (or the minutes converted to seconds) before multiplying, which gives a value 60 times larger than 6.0 kJ [1].

**9. (a)** The units of 2 × KE ÷ m are J/kg = (kg m²/s²) ÷ kg = m²/s² [1], which is the unit of a speed squared, not of a speed (m/s) [1].
**(b)** v = √(2 × KE ÷ m) [1].
**(c)** v = √(2 × 90 ÷ 0.80) = √225 [1] = 15 m/s [1].

**10. (a)** P = 2.5 × 10⁶ W and t = 3600 s [1]. E = P × t = 2.5 × 10⁶ × 3600 [1] = 9.0 × 10⁹ J [1].
**(b)** 9000 MJ (9.0 × 10³ MJ) [1].

**11. (a)** Work done = GPE gained = m × g × h = 1500 × 10 × 12 = 180 000 J [1]. t = 1.5 × 60 = 90 s [1]. P = 180 000 ÷ 90 = 2000 W [1] = 2.0 kW [1].
**(b)** efficiency = 2.0 ÷ 2.5 × 100% [1] = 80% [1].

**12.** Kinetic energy should be in joules (J): the watt is a unit of power, a rate of energy transfer, not an amount of energy [1]. Efficiency has no unit (65% or 0.65): it is a ratio of two energies, so it is not itself an energy [1]. Power should be in watts (W, so 3.0 kW): kJ is a unit of energy, while power is energy transferred per second [1].
