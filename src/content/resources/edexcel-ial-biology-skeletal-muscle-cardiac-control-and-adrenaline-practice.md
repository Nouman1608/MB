## Questions

**1.** A footballer straightens her knee to kick a ball. The quadriceps is the extensor and the hamstring is the flexor.

**(a)** Explain how these two muscles work as an antagonistic pair during this movement. **[2]**
**(b)** State one difference in function between a tendon and a ligament. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the features of a mammalian skeletal muscle fibre that distinguish it from a typical animal cell. **[4]**

**3.** An electron micrograph of a myofibril has a magnification of ×40 000. A relaxed sarcomere measures 100 mm on the image. After contraction, the same sarcomere measures 72 mm. The A band is 1.5 µm in both states.

**(a)** Calculate the actual length of the sarcomere when relaxed and when contracted. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in sarcomere length. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the total length of the I band in the contracted sarcomere. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the A band does not change length during contraction. **[2]**

**4.** Describe how the arrival of an action potential at a skeletal muscle fibre leads to shortening of the sarcomeres. **[6]**

**5.** The table shows data supplied for two types of muscle fibre from the same muscle.

| Property | Fibre type A | Fibre type B |
|---|---|---|
| Mitochondria (% of fibre volume) | 2.5 | 7.0 |
| Myoglobin (mg per g of tissue) | 1.0 | 5.5 |
| Lactate dehydrogenase activity (arbitrary units) | 180 | 60 |
| Time to fatigue when stimulated continuously (s) | 40 | 900 |

**(a)** Identify which type is slow twitch. Use two pieces of evidence from the data. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the myoglobin content of type B is than type A. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest why type A fibres suit a short sprint. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Explain what is meant by the term myogenic. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the atrioventricular node (AVN) delays the wave of excitation. **[2]**
**(c)** State the role of the Purkyne fibres. **[1]**

**7.** An ECG trace was recorded at a paper speed of 25 mm per second. Five consecutive R-to-R intervals together measure 70 mm.

**(a)** Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute. **[3]**
**(b)** Name the condition shown by your answer to (a). **[1]**
**(c)** A second trace shows irregular R-to-R spacing and no clear P waves. Name the abnormal rhythm this suggests. **[1]**

**8.** A trained athlete has a resting heart rate of 58 beats per minute and a resting stroke volume of 95 cm³. At maximum exercise the heart rate is 185 beats per minute and the stroke volume is 150 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the resting cardiac output in dm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the cardiac output at maximum exercise in dm³ per minute. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in cardiac output. **[2]**
**(d)** An untrained person reaches a heart rate of 190 and a stroke volume of 100 cm³. State which factor accounts for the athlete's higher maximum cardiac output. **[1]**

**9.** Explain how the heart rate is increased during exercise and then returned to normal afterwards. **[6]**

**10.** **(a)** State where adrenaline is released and how it reaches its target cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how adrenaline helps the body respond to danger. **[3]**

**11.** A student analysed spirometer traces. At rest, there were 5 breaths in 20 s, the tidal volume was 0.50 dm³ and the baseline fell by 0.45 dm³ in 90 s. During exercise, there were 8 breaths in 15 s, the tidal volume was 1.6 dm³ and the baseline fell by 3.9 dm³ in 3 min.

**(a)** Calculate the resting breathing rate. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the resting respiratory minute ventilation. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the breathing rate and minute ventilation during exercise. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the oxygen consumption at rest and during exercise, in dm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why the baseline of the trace falls. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The quadriceps contracts and pulls on the lower leg to straighten the knee [1]; the hamstring relaxes (and is stretched), as muscles can only pull [1] **[2]**
**(b)** A tendon joins muscle to bone and transmits the pull of the muscle [1]; a ligament joins bone to bone and stabilises the joint [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming which muscle contracts and which relaxes is what the explanation needs; "they work together" does not say that.

**2.** Any four from: it is multinucleate (many nuclei in one cell) [1]; it is very long, with a sarcolemma that folds inwards as T tubules [1]; it has a sarcoplasmic reticulum storing calcium ions [1]; it contains myofibrils made of repeating sarcomeres, giving striations (and many mitochondria between them) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "distinguish" question rewards features that differ from an ordinary cell; naming the organelles every cell has gains nothing.

**3. (a)** Relaxed: 100 ÷ 40 000 = 0.0025 mm = **2.5 µm** [1]; contracted: 72 ÷ 40 000 = 0.0018 mm = **1.8 µm** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (2.5 − 1.8) ÷ 2.5 × 100 = **28%** [1]
**(c)** 1.8 − 1.5 = **0.3 µm** [1]
**(d)** The thick (myosin) filaments, which make up the A band, do not shorten [1]; the thin filaments slide between them, so only the I band and H zone shorten [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the conversion from mm to µm; it makes the working easy to follow, and a slip in powers of ten is easy to spot if each line is written out.

**4.** The action potential spreads along the sarcolemma and down the T tubules, and the sarcoplasmic reticulum releases calcium ions [1]; calcium ions bind to troponin, which changes shape, so tropomyosin moves and exposes the myosin-binding sites on actin [1]; myosin heads attach to actin as cross-bridges [1]; the heads tilt and pull the actin filaments inwards, releasing ADP and phosphate [1]; ATP binds, detaching the heads, and ATPase hydrolyses it to re-set the heads [1]; repeated cycles pull the thin filaments inwards, so the sarcomere shortens [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer keeps the order of events; listing the protein names without saying what each does tends to earn little.

**5. (a)** Type B is slow twitch [1]; it has more mitochondria (7.0 against 2.5) so more aerobic respiration [1]; it has higher myoglobin and a much longer time to fatigue (900 s against 40 s) [1] **[3]**
**(b)** 5.5 ÷ 1.0 = **5.5 times** [1]
**(c)** Type A has high lactate dehydrogenase activity, so it can produce ATP quickly by anaerobic glycolysis [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table with units when comparing; a bare "type B is higher" does not use the data.

**6. (a)** The cardiac muscle contracts and relaxes on its own, without a nerve impulse starting each beat [1] **[1]**
**(b)** The delay gives the atria time to finish contracting [1]; so the ventricles fill with blood before they contract [1] **[2]**
**(c)** They carry the wave of excitation up through the ventricle walls from the apex, so the ventricles contract from the bottom upwards [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b) give a purpose as well as the fact of a delay; that is the reasoning the "explain" asks for.

**7. (a)** Total time = 70 ÷ 25 = 2.8 s [1]; one interval = 2.8 ÷ 5 = 0.56 s [1]; heart rate = 60 ÷ 0.56 = **107 beats per minute** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** **Tachycardia** (rate above 100) [1]
**(c)** **Atrial fibrillation** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by five intervals before converting is the step that is often missed; write the interval time on its own line.

**8. (a)** 58 × 95 = 5510 cm³ [1] = **5.51 dm³ per minute** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** 185 × 150 = 27 750 cm³ = **27.75 dm³ per minute** [1]
**(c)** (27.75 − 5.51) ÷ 5.51 × 100 [1] = **404%** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** **Stroke volume**: the athlete's is larger, while the heart rates are similar (untrained cardiac output is 19.0 dm³ per minute) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert cm³ to dm³ once and say so; mixing units between parts is a common way to lose accuracy.

**9.** Muscles respire faster and release more carbon dioxide, so blood pH falls [1]; chemoreceptors in the carotid arteries and aorta detect this [1]; they send impulses to the cardiovascular control centre in the medulla oblongata [1]; the accelerator pathway sends impulses along sympathetic neurones to the SAN [1]; the SAN fires faster and the heart rate rises [1]; afterwards, as pH returns to normal, impulses pass along the vagus nerve (decelerator pathway) to slow the SAN [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A full answer runs stimulus, receptor, centre, effector and response in order; naming only the medulla and the SAN leaves out the middle links.

**10. (a)** Released from the adrenal glands (medulla) [1]; carried in the blood plasma [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Increases heart rate and stroke volume, so more oxygenated blood reaches muscles [1]; liver glycogen is converted to glucose, giving more respiratory substrate [1]; arterioles to muscles dilate and those to gut and skin constrict, diverting blood to muscle [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each effect earns credit when linked to its benefit; "heart beats faster" alone is only half of the point.

**11. (a)** 5 ÷ 20 × 60 = **15 breaths per minute** [1]
**(b)** 0.50 × 15 = **7.5 dm³ per minute** [1]
**(c)** Rate = 8 ÷ 15 × 60 = **32 breaths per minute** [1]; ventilation = 1.6 × 32 = **51.2 dm³ per minute** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Rest: 0.45 ÷ 1.5 = **0.30 dm³ per minute** [1]; exercise: 3.9 ÷ 3 = **1.3 dm³ per minute** [1] **[2]**
**(e)** Carbon dioxide breathed out is absorbed by soda lime [1]; oxygen is used by the volunteer, so the volume of gas in the circuit decreases [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert 90 s to 1.5 min before dividing; time units must match the per-minute unit asked for.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying muscles push, or not stating that the antagonist relaxes.
- Writing that the A band shortens in contraction.
- Skipping the source of energy: ATP detaches the myosin head and ATPase re-sets it.
- Saying calcium ions bind to actin or myosin rather than troponin.
- Saying the AVN "speeds up" the signal, or that nerves start each heartbeat.
- Not dividing by the number of intervals when finding heart rate from an ECG.
- Mixing seconds with minutes or cm³ with dm³.
- Describing the effects of adrenaline without linking them to the benefit for muscle.
