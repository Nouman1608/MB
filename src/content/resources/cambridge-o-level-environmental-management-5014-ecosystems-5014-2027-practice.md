## Section A

**1.** Define *habitat*. **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between a habitat and a niche. **[2]**

**3.** Write the word equation for photosynthesis. **[2]**

**4.** Name the **two** groups of organisms that act as decomposers. **[2]**

**5.** State the process that follows pollination and leads to the formation of seeds and fruit. **[1]**

**6.** Explain what is meant by an *invasive species*. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In a coastal lagoon, mangrove leaves are eaten by crabs and snails. Algae are eaten by snails and small fish. Crabs are eaten by large fish and herons. Small fish are eaten by large fish and herons. Large fish are eaten by herons. Nothing eats the herons.
(a) Name **one** producer in this food web. **[1]**
(b) Identify the apex predator. **[1]**
(c) Name the organism that is both a secondary and a tertiary consumer. **[1]**
(d) An invasive predator arrives and eats most of the crabs. Predict and explain the effect on the snails and on the large fish. **[3]**

**8.** Seagrass in a bay captures 54,000 kJ of energy per m² per year.
(a) Using the syllabus figure for energy transfer, calculate the energy reaching a tertiary consumer in the chain seagrass → sea urchin → crab → octopus. **[1]**
(b) A study measures 4,860 kJ per m² per year reaching the sea urchins. Calculate the percentage of energy transferred from seagrass to sea urchins. **[1]**
(c) Explain why less energy is available at each trophic level. **[2]**

**9.** A single mango tree supports 2,500 aphids. The aphids are eaten by 40 ladybirds, and the ladybirds are eaten by 3 birds.
(a) Describe the shape of the pyramid of numbers for this food chain. **[1]**
(b) Explain why it has this shape. **[1]**
(c) Explain why a pyramid of energy for the same food chain would have a different shape. **[2]**

**10.** During a hot, dry summer, the water level in a small pond falls. Explain how **two** abiotic factors in the pond could change, and the effect of each on the fish. **[4]**

**11.** The table shows the numbers of voles (prey) and owls (predators) in a woodland over six years. The figures are invented for practice.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Voles | 200 | 450 | 700 | 400 | 180 | 300 |
| Owls | 4 | 6 | 10 | 12 | 7 | 5 |

(a) Describe the relationship between the vole and owl populations. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
(b) Explain why the owl population fell after year 4. **[2]**

**12.** Carbon is stored in the wood of a tree. Describe **two** different routes by which this carbon could return to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why a decline in insect pollinators could reduce food production. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The place where an organism lives **[1]**.

**2.** A habitat is the place where an organism lives **[1]**, whereas a niche is the role of a species in the ecosystem, such as what it eats and how it interacts with other species **[1]**.

**3.** carbon dioxide + water **[1]** → glucose + oxygen **[1]**.

**4.** Bacteria **[1]**; fungi **[1]**.

**5.** Fertilisation **[1]**.

**6.** A species that has been introduced to an area where it does not occur naturally **[1]** and spreads rapidly, harming or out-competing native species **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Mangrove (leaves) or algae **[1]**.
(b) Heron **[1]**.
(c) Heron: it is a secondary consumer when it eats crabs or small fish and a tertiary consumer when it eats large fish **[1]**.
(d) Snails may increase because they no longer compete with crabs for mangrove leaves **[1]**. Large fish may decrease because they lose crabs as a food source **[1]**, although they may eat more small fish instead, which could reduce the decline **[1]**.

**8.** (a) 54,000 → 5,400 → 540 → 54 kJ per m² per year **[1]**.
(b) 4,860 ÷ 54,000 × 100 = 9% **[1]**.
(c) Energy is used in life processes such as movement, respiration, digestion and excretion, and much is lost as heat **[1]**; not all of each organism is eaten or digested, so only a small part passes to the next level **[1]**.

**9.** (a) The base is narrow (one tree), the second bar is very wide (2,500 aphids), and the bars then narrow (40, then 3), so the pyramid is inverted at the bottom **[1]**.
(b) One mango tree is very large and provides enough food for thousands of small aphids **[1]**.
(c) A pyramid of energy shows energy, not numbers, and energy is lost at every level **[1]**, so the tree's bar is the widest and each bar above is narrower, giving a true pyramid shape **[1]**.

**10.** Any two explained:
- Temperature rises in shallower water **[1]**, which may go beyond the range some fish can tolerate and increases their need for oxygen **[1]**.
- Oxygen falls as warm water holds less and decomposition of dead plants uses oxygen **[1]**, so fish are stressed or die **[1]**.

Also accept rising salinity or changing pH as water evaporates and substances become more concentrated, with an effect on fish.

**11.** (a) Both populations rise and fall, but owl numbers follow vole numbers with a delay **[1]**: voles peak at 700 in year 3, while owls peak at 12 in year 4 **[1]**.
(b) Vole numbers fell sharply from 700 to 180 between years 3 and 5, so the owls had less food **[1]**; fewer owls survived or bred successfully, so their population fell to 5 by year 6 **[1]**.

**12.** Route 1: the tree dies and decomposers break down the wood **[1]**; the decomposers respire and release carbon dioxide **[1]**. Route 2: the wood is burned as fuel **[1]**, and combustion releases carbon dioxide **[1]**. Also accept the tree respiring, or the wood being eaten by an animal that respires.

**13.** Many crop plants are pollinated by insects, which carry pollen from anthers to stigmas **[1]**. Without pollination there is no fertilisation, so fewer seeds and fruit form **[1]**. Harvests of fruit and seed crops fall, reducing the food supply **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 2**: defining niche as "where it lives in the habitat" instead of its role.
- **Question 7(d)**: predicting a change without explaining it through competition or food supply.
- **Question 8(a)**: stopping at the secondary consumer; count the steps from the producer.
- **Question 9(c)**: saying "the pyramid would be the right way up" without explaining energy loss.
- **Question 11(a)**: describing each population separately and missing the delay between the peaks.
