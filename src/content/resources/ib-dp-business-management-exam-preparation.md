The [assessment revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-assessment-revision-notes/) set
out what each component weighs at SL and HL. These notes turn that into an exam-day plan -- how to
use the pre-released Paper 1 material, what to practise for the fully unseen papers, and a worked
scenario -- alongside the
[full syllabus guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-syllabus-guide/) and
[subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-business-management-subject-guide/) already on the site.

## Paper 1: the advance-preparation paper (35% SL / 25% HL)

Paper 1 is the one component where genuine advance preparation is possible: a short statement,
released three months ahead of the exam, names a small number of research topics and gives roughly
the first 200 words of the case study. The rest of the (fictitious) 800-1,000 word case study stays
unseen until the exam itself.

**Exam-preparation priority**: treat the released statement as material to actively research and
annotate over three months, not background reading to skim once. Build short notes on each named
research topic in advance, and re-read the released extract closely enough to predict, in general
terms, what kind of organisation and issue the full case study is likely to explore -- students who
only skim the advance material consistently underperform relative to their overall preparation,
since case-study-based questions carry a substantial share of Paper 1's marks.

## Paper 2 and HL Paper 3: fully unseen stimulus material

Unlike Paper 1, Paper 2 (and HL Paper 3) present **entirely unseen** stimulus material under full
exam-time pressure -- no advance research is possible here, so preparation instead means practising
the general skill of reading and applying business tools to unfamiliar stimulus quickly and
accurately. HL Paper 3 specifically uses a **social-enterprise** stimulus, which changes how success
and strategy should be evaluated: profit maximisation is not necessarily the organisation's primary
objective, so answers need to weigh social or environmental impact alongside (or instead of)
financial performance.

**Exam-preparation priority for Paper 2/3**: practise applying the same business tools (SWOT,
Ansoff's matrix, decision trees, and so on) to a range of unfamiliar short scenarios under timed
conditions, since the tools themselves are well known but applying them quickly to genuinely new
material, without the benefit of advance research, is the specific skill being tested.

## Using the four concepts as an answer-planning tool

Change, creativity, ethics and sustainability run through every paper and the internal assessment.
Before answering a longer response question on any paper, identify which of the four concepts is
most relevant to the scenario given -- for example, a question about entering a new international
market is fundamentally about **change** -- and use that concept explicitly to frame the analysis.
This is a fast, repeatable planning step that works identically across Papers 1, 2 and 3.

## Worked practice scenario: applying a concept and a tool together

A Paper 2-style stimulus describes a mid-sized manufacturer facing rising raw-material costs and
considering whether to automate part of its production line.

```
Step 1 -- identify the concept:  This scenario is primarily about CHANGE (a shift in
                                  production method) with a CREATIVITY dimension (finding
                                  a new way to manage rising costs).

Step 2 -- select a tool:         A force-field analysis, weighing driving forces
                                  (rising costs, competitor automation) against
                                  restraining forces (redundancy costs, staff
                                  resistance, retraining time).

Step 3 -- apply, don't just
list:                            State which forces are strongest given the
                                  scenario's specific figures, not just list forces
                                  generically -- e.g. if raw-material costs have
                                  risen sharply and are described as the main
                                  driver of falling margins, that driving force
                                  likely outweighs a one-off redundancy cost.

Step 4 -- reach a judgement:     Recommend a course of action and state what it
                                  depends on (e.g. the size of the redundancy cost
                                  relative to projected savings), rather than
                                  ending on the analysis alone.
```

This concept-then-tool-then-judgement structure is the same shape "recommend and justify" and
"evaluate" questions reward across every paper, whether the stimulus is pre-released (Paper 1) or
fully unseen (Paper 2/3).

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exam**: research and annotate the Paper 1 advance material thoroughly, not just
  once; practise applying standard business tools to unfamiliar short scenarios under timed
  conditions for Paper 2 (and Paper 3 at HL); practise identifying which of the four concepts fits
  a given scenario quickly.
- **During Paper 1**: draw explicitly on research done from the advance material where relevant,
  rather than answering as if the case study were entirely unseen.
- **During Paper 2/HL Paper 3**: read the stimulus fully before selecting a tool; apply the tool to
  the scenario's actual figures and details, not generically; for HL Paper 3, remember the
  organisation's objective may be social or environmental rather than purely financial, and evaluate
  accordingly.
- **On every paper**: identify the most relevant concept before writing a longer answer, and end
  extended responses with an explicit, reasoned judgement.

## Self-test

1. What makes Paper 1 different from Paper 2 and HL Paper 3 in terms of exam preparation?
2. Why does HL Paper 3's social-enterprise focus change how "success" should be evaluated?
3. In the worked scenario, what four steps does a strong Paper 2 answer follow?
4. Why is identifying one of the four concepts a useful first step before answering a longer
   question?
5. What is a common weakness when applying a business tool such as force-field analysis?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 1's case study is preceded by a released statement three months in advance,
allowing genuine research beforehand, while Paper 2 and HL Paper 3 use entirely unseen stimulus
material under exam conditions. 2. Because a social enterprise's primary objective may be social or
environmental impact rather than profit maximisation, so success and strategy must be judged against
those aims rather than financial performance alone. 3. Identify the most relevant concept, select an
appropriate tool, apply the tool to the scenario's specific details rather than generically, and
reach an explicit, reasoned judgement. 4. Because it gives a fast, repeatable way to frame the
analysis around what the scenario is fundamentally about, rather than answering purely from
unit-specific content in isolation. 5. Listing the tool's components generically without applying
them to the scenario's actual figures and details, or stopping at the analysis without reaching a
reasoned judgement.
