## Questions

**1.** A letter is chosen at random from the word STATISTICS. Find (a) P(the letter is S or T) **[1]**, (b) P(the letter is a consonant, given that it is not S) **[2]**.

**2.** For events A and B, P(A) = 0.5, P(B) = 0.35 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.7.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(A | B). **[1]**
**(c)** Determine whether A and B are independent. **[1]**

**3.** Events A and B are independent with P(A) = 0.3 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.65.

**(a)** Find P(B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that exactly one of A and B occurs. **[2]**

**4.** Machine M makes 60% of a factory's items and machine N makes the rest. Of M's items, 3% are defective. Of N's items, 5% are defective.

**(a)** Find the probability that an item chosen at random is defective. **[2]**
**(b)** Given that it is defective, find the probability that it came from N. **[2]**
**(c)** Two items are chosen at random from N's output. Find the probability that exactly one is defective. **[2]**

**5.** A box holds 4 yellow and 3 green counters. Two are taken at random without replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that they are the same colour. **[3]**
**(b)** Given that they are the same colour, find the probability that both are green. **[2]**

**6.** Of 40 students, 18 take art, 16 take music and 14 take drama. Also 6 take art and music, 5 take art and drama, 4 take music and drama, and 2 take all three.

**(a)** Find the number of students in each of the eight regions of a three-set Venn diagram. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that a randomly chosen student takes exactly one of the three subjects. **[2]**
**(c)** Find P(art | drama) and state, with a reason, whether taking art and taking drama are independent. **[2]**

**7.** On nine days the mean outdoor temperature T (°C) and the gas used G (units) were recorded. Σ T = 76, Σ G = 380, Σ T² = 796, Σ G² = 17 616, Σ TG = 2721. Find the regression line of G on T, giving the constants to 3 significant figures. **[4]**

**8.** In question 7 the temperatures lay between 2 °C and 15 °C, and the line is G = 68.9 − 3.16T.

**(a)** Which is the explanatory variable? **[1]**
**(b)** Interpret the gradient in context. **[1]**
**(c)** Predict G when T = 9. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the line should not be used to predict G when T = 25. **[2]**
**(e)** Comment on the meaning of the constant 68.9. **[1]**

**9.** A regression line for cars of mass m kg (between 800 and 1600) and fuel use E litres per 100 km is q = 2.1 + 0.35p, where p = (m − 1000)/100 and q = E − 4.

**(a)** Express E in terms of m. **[3]**
**(b)** Estimate E when m = 1400. **[1]**
**(c)** The correlation coefficient between p and q is 0.94. State the coefficient between m and E, with a reason. **[1]**

**10.** **(a)** For a data set, Sxx = 85, Syy = 120 and Sxy = −78. Calculate r. **[2]**
**(b)** Interpret your value. **[1]**
**(c)** Nine points have x = 1, 2, …, 9 and y = (x − 5)². Show that r = 0 and comment. **[2]**

**11.** A shop records weekly advertising spend x (£100) and sales y (tens of items): (2, 14), (3, 19), (5, 22), (6, 31), (8, 35), (10, 47).

**(a)** Find Sxx, Sxy and Syy. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the regression line of y on x. **[3]**
**(c)** Find r. **[2]**
**(d)** Predict y when x = 7, and comment on a prediction for x = 20. **[2]**

**12.** A candidate has up to three attempts at a test. The probability of passing is 0.6 on the first attempt, 0.7 on the second and 0.8 on the third. Attempts stop after a pass.

**(a)** Find the probability that the candidate eventually passes. **[3]**
**(b)** Given that the candidate passes, find the probability that it was on the third attempt. **[2]**
**(c)** Two candidates take the test independently. Find P(both eventually pass) **[1]** and P(at least one passes on the first attempt) **[2]**.

## Answers

**1. (a)** S and T appear 3 times each out of 10: P = 6/10 = **3/5** [1]
**(b)** Not S leaves 7 letters, of which T, T, T, C are consonants [1]. P = **4/7** [1]
*Examiner insight:* in a conditional question, the denominator shrinks to the given condition, so state it ("7 letters") to show the method.

**2. (a)** P(A ∩ B) = 0.5 + 0.35 − 0.7 [1] = **0.15** [1]
**(b)** P(A | B) = 0.15/0.35 = **3/7** [1]
**(c)** P(A) P(B) = 0.175 ≠ 0.15 [1], so **not independent**.
*Examiner insight:* a conclusion needs both values quoted and a verdict. Writing only "no" gives little to credit.

**3. (a)** 0.3 + p − 0.3p = 0.65 [1] so 0.7p = 0.35 and **p = 0.5** [1]
**(b)** (0.3)(0.5) + (0.7)(0.5) [1] = **0.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "exactly one" needs two routes added. A common slip is giving only P(A′ ∩ B), the route that was easiest to find.

**4. (a)** (0.6)(0.03) + (0.4)(0.05) [1] = 0.018 + 0.02 = **0.038** [1]
**(b)** 0.02 / 0.038 [1] = 10/19 = **0.526** [1]
**(c)** 2 × (0.05)(0.95) [1] = **0.095** [1]
*Examiner insight:* writing the route products on the tree before adding keeps part (b) easy, because the numerator is already there.

**5. (a)** P(YY) = (4/7)(3/6) = 2/7 [1]; P(GG) = (3/7)(2/6) = 1/7 [1]; total **3/7** [1]
**(b)** P(GG | same) = (1/7)/(3/7) [1] = **1/3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* using 4/7 twice, as with replacement, is the usual error here, and it changes every later line.

**6. (a)** Three-way region: 2. Two-only regions: art and music 6 − 2 = 4, art and drama 5 − 2 = 3, music and drama 4 − 2 = 2 [1]. Only art 18 − 4 − 3 − 2 = 9, only music 16 − 4 − 2 − 2 = 8, only drama 14 − 3 − 2 − 2 = 7 [1]. Union = 9 + 8 + 7 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 2 = 35, so none = **5** [1]
**(b)** (9 + 8 + 7)/40 [1] = 24/40 = **3/5** [1]
**(c)** P(art | drama) = 5/14 [1]. P(art) P(drama) = (18/40)(14/40) = 0.1575 ≠ 5/40 = 0.125, so **not independent** [1]
*Examiner insight:* checking that all eight regions sum to 40 is a quick test that catches an early subtraction slip.

**7.** Sxx = 796 − 76²/9 = 154.2 [1]. Sxy = 2721 − (76)(380)/9 = −487.9 [1]. b = −487.9/154.2 = **−3.16** [1]. a = 380/9 − (−3.1635)(76/9) = **68.9**, so **G = 68.9 − 3.16T** [1]
*Examiner insight:* keep extra decimals until the end. Rounding b to −3.16 before finding a shifts the third significant figure of a.

**8. (a)** **Temperature** [1]
**(b)** Each 1 °C rise in temperature reduces the predicted gas use by about 3.16 units [1]
**(c)** 68.94 − 3.1635(9) = **40.5** [1]
**(d)** 25 °C is outside the observed range 2 to 15 °C, so this is extrapolation [1]. The line gives a negative value (about −10), which is impossible [1]
**(e)** It is the prediction at 0 °C, which is outside the data (2 to 15 °C), so it is an extrapolated estimate [1]
*Examiner insight:* a comment gains credit when it refers to the actual range or value in the question, rather than "extrapolation is unreliable" alone.

**9. (a)** E − 4 = 2.1 + 0.35(m − 1000)/100 [1]. E = 6.1 + 0.0035(m − 1000) [1]. **E = 2.6 + 0.0035m** [1]
**(b)** 2.6 + 0.0035(1400) = **7.5** [1]
**(c)** **0.94**, because both codings multiply by positive constants so r is unchanged [1]
*Examiner insight:* substituting both codings, not just one, is the main step to show. Check by converting one point from the coded line.

**10. (a)** r = −78 / √(85 × 120) [1] = −78/101.0 = **−0.772** [1]
**(b)** Fairly strong negative linear correlation [1]
**(c)** Σx = 45, Σy = 60, Σxy = 300, so Sxy = 300 − (45)(60)/9 = 0 [1], hence r = 0. The points fit a curve exactly, so r = 0 does not mean no relationship, only no linear one [1]
*Examiner insight:* "no correlation" and "no relationship" are not the same claim, and answers that blur them lose the point.

**11. (a)** Σx = 34, Σy = 168, Σx² = 238, Σy² = 5436, Σxy = 1131 [1]. Sxx = 238 − 34²/6 = 45.33 [1], Sxy = 1131 − (34)(168)/6 = 179 and Syy = 5436 − 168²/6 = 732 [1]
**(b)** b = 179/45.33 [1] = **3.95** (3 s.f.). x̄ = 34/6, ȳ = 28, so a = 28 − 3.9485(34/6) = 5.625 [1]. **y = 5.63 + 3.95x** [1]
**(c)** r = 179 / √(45.33 × 732) [1] = 179/182.2 = **0.983** [1]
**(d)** y = 5.625 + 3.9485(7) = **33.3** [1]. x = 20 is far outside the data (2 to 10), so extrapolation: unreliable [1]
*Examiner insight:* showing the sums and S-values separately lets a slip in one be seen and not repeated across the rest.

**12. (a)** P(pass) = 0.6 + (0.4)(0.7) + (0.4)(0.3)(0.8) [1] = 0.6 + 0.28 + 0.096 [1] = **0.976** [1]
**(b)** 0.096/0.976 [1] = 6/61 = **0.0984** [1]
**(c)** 0.976² = **0.953** [1]. P(at least one first-attempt pass) = 1 − (0.4)² [1] = **0.84** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the later probabilities here are conditional on earlier failures, so draw the tree and multiply along every branch used.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the unconditional probability where a conditional one is needed, or swapping P(A | B) and P(B | A).
- Not stating the condition's total as the denominator in a conditional probability.
- Calling events independent because they look unrelated, rather than testing P(A ∩ B) = P(A) P(B).
- Keeping the same denominators when items are not replaced.
- Adding where the product law applies, or multiplying where routes should be added.
- Leaving out an arithmetic step in Sxx or Sxy, so one slip changes everything after it.
- Rounding a and b early, then predicting with the rounded values.
- Commenting on a prediction without referring to the data range given.
- Saying "no correlation" when the pattern is a curve.
