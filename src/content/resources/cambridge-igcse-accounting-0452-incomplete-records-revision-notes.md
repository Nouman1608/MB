## Scope

Sole traders only, and not service or manufacturing businesses. Syllabus points:
why businesses keep different types of records; advantages and disadvantages of
not keeping full records; opening and closing statements of affairs; profit or
loss from changes in capital; calculating sales, purchases, gross profit, trade
receivables, trade payables and other figures; preparing statements from
incomplete records; 5.1 adjustments; mark-up, margin and inventory turnover to
find missing figures.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Incomplete records | Records that do not form a full double entry system |
| Single entry | Recording each transaction once, often only in a cash book |
| Statement of affairs | A list of assets and liabilities on a date, giving capital |
| Capital introduced | New money or assets put in by the owner during the year |
| Mark-up | Gross profit as a percentage of cost of sales |
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit as a percentage of revenue |
| Rate of inventory turnover | Cost of sales ÷ average inventory (times) |
| Average inventory | (Opening inventory + closing inventory) ÷ 2 |

## Advantages and disadvantages of incomplete records

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Cheap and quick | No trial balance to check accuracy |
| Little skill needed | Profit not known without reconstruction |
| Adequate for a small cash business | Errors, fraud and theft harder to find |
| | Weak control of receivables, payables and inventory |
| | Lenders may not accept the figures |

## Method 1: change in capital

```
Profit = closing capital − opening capital + drawings − capital introduced
```

A negative answer is a loss. Capital comes from a statement of affairs: **all**
assets (including prepayments and accrued income) less **all** liabilities
(including accruals, overdraft and loans).

## Statement of affairs layout

```
Statement of affairs at [date]
Non-current assets (at valuation or net book value)      X
Inventory, trade receivables, prepayments, bank, cash    X
Total assets                                             X
Less trade payables, accruals, overdraft, loans         (X)
Capital                                                  X
```

Prepare one at the start and one at the end of the year. If the bank balance at the
end is unknown, find it from the bank summary first.

## Method 2: rebuild the figures

```
Credit sales = received from customers + closing receivables − opening receivables
Total revenue = credit sales + cash sales
Purchases     = paid to suppliers + closing payables − opening payables
Expense       = paid + closing accrual − opening accrual
                     − closing prepayment + opening prepayment
```

If cash takings were used to pay expenses or drawings before banking, add those
back to find total cash sales.

## Ratio techniques

| Given | Find | How |
|---|---|---|
| Mark-up % and cost of sales | Revenue | cost of sales × (100 + mark-up) ÷ 100 |
| Mark-up % and revenue | Cost of sales | revenue × 100 ÷ (100 + mark-up) |
| Margin % and revenue | Gross profit | revenue × margin ÷ 100 |
| Rate of turnover and cost of sales | Average inventory | cost of sales ÷ rate |
| Average and opening inventory | Closing inventory | 2 × average − opening |

**Conversions:** mark-up 50% ↔ margin 33⅓%; mark-up 25% ↔ margin 20%; mark-up
33⅓% ↔ margin 25%; mark-up 100% ↔ margin 50%.

## Worked reminder

Revenue 56,000; mark-up 40%; rate of inventory turnover 5 times; opening inventory 7,600.

```
Cost of sales       56,000 × 100/140   = 40,000
Average inventory   40,000 ÷ 5         =  8,000
Closing inventory   16,000 − 7,600     =  8,400
Purchases           40,000 + 8,400 − 7,600 = 40,800
```

## Exam traps

- **Change in capital is not profit** until drawings are added and capital
  introduced is deducted.
- **Overdraft** is a liability in the statement of affairs.
- **Receipts are not sales;** payments are not purchases.
- **Mark-up is on cost; margin is on selling price.**
- **Average inventory**, not closing, is used in turnover.
- **5.1 adjustments** (depreciation, accruals, irrecoverable debts, goods taken)
  still apply.
- **Goods taken by the owner** increase drawings and reduce purchases.

## Self-test

1. Give one advantage of not keeping full records.
2. Opening capital 15,000; closing 18,500; drawings 7,000; capital introduced 1,000.
   Calculate profit.
3. Is a bank overdraft an asset or a liability in a statement of affairs?
4. Received from customers 22,000; receivables 1,500 at the start and 1,900 at the
   end. Calculate credit sales.
5. Paid suppliers 31,000; payables 2,600 at the start and 2,200 at the end.
   Calculate purchases.
6. Convert a margin of 25% to a mark-up.
7. Cost of sales 36,000; mark-up 50%. Calculate revenue.
8. Cost of sales 45,000; rate of inventory turnover 9. Calculate average inventory.
9. Average inventory 5,000; opening inventory 4,400. Calculate closing inventory.
10. Why is capital introduced deducted in the change-in-capital method?
11. Cash takings of 48,000 were banked after paying wages of 3,000 in cash.
    Calculate cash sales.
12. Name one disadvantage of incomplete records for a lender.

### Answers

1. For example, it is cheaper and quicker.
2. 18,500 − 15,000 + 7,000 − 1,000 = **9,500**.
3. A liability.
4. 22,000 + 1,900 − 1,500 = **22,400**.
5. 31,000 + 2,200 − 2,600 = **30,600**.
6. Profit 25, cost 75: 25 ÷ 75 = **33⅓%**.
7. 36,000 × 150/100 = **54,000**.
8. 45,000 ÷ 9 = **5,000**.
9. 10,000 − 4,400 = **5,600**.
10. It increased capital without being earned by the business.
11. 48,000 + 3,000 = **51,000**.
12. The figures are less reliable, so the lender cannot be sure the business can
    repay.
