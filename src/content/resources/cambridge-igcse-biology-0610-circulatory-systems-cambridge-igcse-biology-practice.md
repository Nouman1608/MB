> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **9.1 Circulatory systems** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (fish and mammal circulations and the advantages of a double circulation); Core candidates can skip them.

Revise first with the [circulatory systems study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-circulatory-systems-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [circulatory systems revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-circulatory-systems-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of these is the syllabus description of a circulatory system?

A: a heart that pumps blood to the lungs

B: a system of blood vessels with a pump and valves to ensure one-way flow of blood

C: a network of capillaries where substances are exchanged

D: blood, which carries oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide

**[1]**

**2.** State the function of the valves in a circulatory system. **[1]**

**3.** Name the structure that acts as the pump in the circulatory system of a mammal. **[1]**

**4.** *(Extended)* State the number of times blood passes through the heart in one complete circuit of the body in:

**(a)** a fish **[1]**

**(b)** a mammal. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Name the organ in which blood takes up oxygen in:

**(a)** the single circulation of a fish **[1]**

**(b)** the pulmonary circulation of a mammal. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A student writes the route of blood in a fish as: heart → body → gills → heart. Identify the error and write the correct route. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** *(Extended)* A diagram shows the circulation of a mammal as two loops, P and Q, both joined to the heart. Loop P passes through the lungs. Loop Q passes through the liver, kidneys and muscles.

**(a)** Name loop P and loop Q. **[2]**

**(b)** State which side of the heart pumps blood into loop Q. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the double circulation of a mammal. **[3]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A red blood cell is in a capillary in the wall of the stomach. Describe the route it takes back to the stomach, naming the chambers of the heart it passes through, and state where it picks up oxygen. **[4]**

**9.** *(Extended)* Fish and mammals both have a circulatory system with a heart.

**(a)** Describe the single circulation of a fish. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why blood flows more slowly to the body organs of a fish than to the body organs of a mammal. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain **two** advantages of a double circulation. **[4]**

**10.** A scientist studies a small invertebrate animal. Its body fluid is pumped by a muscular tube, flows through vessels, and is prevented from flowing backwards by flaps of tissue.

**(a)** Use the syllabus description to explain why this animal can be said to have a circulatory system. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest what would happen to the movement of the body fluid if the flaps of tissue were damaged. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Mammals are active animals that keep a constant body temperature. Explain how a double circulation helps to supply the high rate of respiration they need. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A describes only part of the pump's job, C describes capillaries and D describes blood; only B contains vessels, pump and valves.

**2.** To prevent backflow of blood / to ensure blood flows in one direction only [1].

*Examiner insight:* "To control blood" or "to pump blood" does not score; the mark is for the idea of direction or backflow.

**3.** The heart [1].

**4. (a)** Once [1].

**(b)** Twice [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Two" for a mammal is accepted, but answers such as "two circuits" without reference to passing through the heart can be confusing; answer exactly what is asked.

**5. (a)** The gills [1].

**(b)** The lungs [1].

**6.** The gills should come straight after the heart / blood goes to the gills before the body [1]. Correct route: heart → gills → body → heart [1].

*Examiner insight:* The first mark is for saying what is wrong, not just rewriting the route; the second needs a complete loop that starts and ends at the heart.

**7. (a)** P is the pulmonary circulation [1]; Q is the systemic circulation [1].

**(b)** The left side [1].

**(c)** Blood passes through the heart twice in each complete circuit [1]. The right side of the heart pumps blood to the lungs and it returns to the left side (pulmonary circulation) [1]. The left side pumps blood to the body organs and it returns to the right side (systemic circulation) [1].

*Examiner insight:* The count of two passes through the heart is a separate mark from the two routes; many answers describe the loops but never say "twice".

**8.** Stomach → veins → vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle [1]; → pulmonary artery → lungs, where it picks up oxygen [1]; → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle [1]; → aorta → arteries → back to the stomach capillaries [1].

*Examiner insight:* The four marks are for the right side of the heart, the lungs as the site of oxygen uptake, the left side of the heart, and the aorta back to the body. Placing the left side before the lungs loses two marks.

**9. (a)** Blood is pumped from the heart to the gills and then flows directly to the body organs before returning to the heart [1]; it passes through the heart once in each complete circuit [1].

**(b)** Blood loses pressure as it passes through the narrow gill capillaries [1]; it is not pumped again by the heart before reaching the body organs, whereas in a mammal blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again [1].

**(c)** Any two explained advantages, two marks each:

- blood returns to the heart after the lungs and is pumped again, so it reaches the body at high pressure [1]; it flows faster, so oxygen and glucose reach respiring cells more quickly [1]
- blood is sent to the lungs at lower pressure [1], so the delicate lung capillaries are not damaged / there is time for gas exchange [1]
- the two sides of the heart keep oxygenated and deoxygenated blood separate [1], so blood going to the body carries as much oxygen as possible [1]

*Examiner insight:* In (c) each advantage has a feature mark and a consequence mark; "it is more efficient" earns nothing because it does not say what is more efficient or why.

**10. (a)** It has vessels that carry the fluid [1]; it has a pump (the muscular tube) [1]; it has valves (the flaps) that ensure one-way flow [1].

**(b)** The fluid would flow backwards as well as forwards / flow would no longer be one-way [1].

**(c)** Blood is pumped a second time after collecting oxygen at the lungs, so it reaches the body at high pressure [1]; it flows quickly, so oxygen and glucose are delivered to respiring cells at a high rate [1]; oxygenated blood does not mix with deoxygenated blood, so the blood delivered to the tissues carries as much oxygen as possible [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (a) is an application question: match each part of the description to the animal. Repeating the definition without pointing to the tube and the flaps does not show you have applied it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving valves or one-way flow out of the definition.
- Forgetting to state the number of passes through the heart when describing a circulation.
- Putting the left side of the heart before the lungs when tracing a route.
- Stating "high pressure" without saying it delivers oxygen and glucose faster.
- Giving the size of the lungs, instead of delicate capillaries, as the reason for lower pulmonary pressure.
