These notes condense the non-exam assessment route of Unit 4 (4b) of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Literature (9675) specification, Version 5.3. For the full explanation and a worked paragraph, use the [Unit 4b study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4b-non-exam-assessment/); then try the [practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4b-non-exam-assessment-practice/). The other route is in the [Unit 4a revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-4a-examination-revision-notes/).

## The task in brief

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Type | Non-exam assessment; teacher assessed and externally moderated |
| Texts | One poetry text and one prose text |
| Writing | Two essays, 1,250 to 1,500 words each, separate responses |
| Marks | 25 per essay; 50 in total; 30% of the International A-level |
| Level | International A-level only. A student takes this route or the 4a exam |
| Re-sits | NEA marks can be carried forward when re-sitting a qualification |

## Rules for texts

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Poetry | Single author; number of poems equivalent to Units 2 and 3 |
| Short stories | Single author; number equivalent to Unit 3 |
| Areas | Each text linked to a different area from the four |
| Set lists | Cannot use any text from the AS or A-level exam set text lists |
| Translation | Allowed |
| Critical range | Must allow access to a range of critical views and interpretations |
| Meaning | Must maximise opportunities for writing about how meanings can be found |

### The four areas

Childhood. War. Women and men. Race, class and culture. They are not prescribed topics, and you are not expected to use extensive contextual knowledge or wider reading.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Representation | The process of showing a view of the world, rather than the world itself |
| Interpretation | A reading of the text that others might support or challenge |
| Authorial methods | The choices a writer makes in voice, language, structure, form and setting |
| Perception | Depth of understanding and sensitive response to the texts and task |
| Assuredness | Confidence and conviction in the writing |
| Coherence | Logical, consistent argument |
| Thoroughness | Careful, precise writing with detail |

## A good task title

- States a **view** that can be argued with or against.
- Names the **writer** and the **aspect** of the representation.
- Ends with an invitation to judge ("say how far you agree").

**Template:** '[Writer] presents [group or idea] as [claim].' Exploring [Writer]'s representation of [aspect], say how far you agree with this view.

**Weak titles:** "Childhood in the poems" (a topic, not an argument). "Describe the poet's life" (no representation, no judgement).

## Essay plan for about 1,400 words

| Part | Words |
|---|---|
| Introduction: the view and your line of argument | 120 |
| Section 1 | 290 |
| Section 2 | 290 |
| Section 3 | 290 |
| Section 4 | 290 |
| Conclusion: judgement | 120 |
| Total | 1,400 |

### Paragraph pattern

**Claim. Evidence. Method and effect. Context. Alternative reading. Link to the task.**

## Mark bands (out of 25 for each essay)

| Band | Marks | Words that describe it |
|---|---|---|
| 5 | 21 to 25 | Perceptive, assured |
| 4 | 16 to 20 | Coherent, thorough |
| 3 | 11 to 15 | Straightforward, relevant |
| 2 | 6 to 10 | Simple, generalised |
| 1 | 1 to 5 | Largely irrelevant, misunderstood or inaccurate |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing written or no connection to the text or task |

### Assessment objectives (equal weighting)

- **AO1:** how readers find meanings; genre, contexts, interpretations.
- **AO2:** how meanings are shaped, with close attention to authorial methods.
- **AO3:** informed, personal, argued response, with concepts, terminology and accurate writing.

### Moving up a band

| From | To | What to add |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | 3 | Focus on the task; use detail to support points |
| 3 | 4 | A logical argument across the essay; thorough explanations; precise expression |
| 4 | 5 | Sensitivity to the texts; confident conviction; interpretations that connect context, genre and methods |

## Traps

- Using a set text from any AS or A-level list.
- Two texts linked to the same area.
- Poems by different poets or stories by different authors in one text.
- A title without a view to argue.
- A word count outside 1,250 to 1,500 words.
- Retelling the plot or listing techniques.
- Only one reading offered.
- A long background section that is not linked to the writing.

## Quick self-test

1. How many essays are written, and how long is each?
2. How many marks is each essay worth?
3. What percentage of the International A-level is Unit 4?
4. Who must the author of a poetry text be?
5. Can a student choose a text from the Unit 1 set list?
6. Can the texts be in translation?
7. What must be true of the two texts' areas?
8. Complete the title: "Exploring the writer's representation of ..., say how ...".
9. What is the mark range for Band 4?
10. Which band is described as "simple and generalised"?
11. What do the three assessment objectives have in common in their weighting?
12. State one thing to add to move from Band 3 to Band 4.

### Answers

1. Two; 1,250 to 1,500 words each.
2. 25.
3. 30%.
4. A single author.
5. No. Texts cannot come from any AS or A-level exam set text list.
6. Yes.
7. Each text must be linked to a different area.
8. "... say how far you agree with this view."
9. 16 to 20.
10. Band 2.
11. They are equally weighted.
12. For example, a logical argument across the whole essay, with thorough explanations and precise expression.
