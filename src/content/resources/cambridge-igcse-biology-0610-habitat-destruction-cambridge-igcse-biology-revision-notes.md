These notes condense sub-topic **20.2 Habitat destruction** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All four outcomes are **Core**; there is no Extended content in this sub-topic.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [habitat destruction study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-habitat-destruction-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [habitat destruction practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-habitat-destruction-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [human influences on ecosystems revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-human-influences-on-ecosystems-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 20.

## Core recall: biodiversity

> Biodiversity is the **number of different species** that live in an area.

Count **species**, not individuals. A monoculture with millions of plants of one crop has very low biodiversity.

## Core recall: three reasons for habitat destruction

| Reason | Examples |
|---|---|
| (a) Increased area for **housing, crop plant production and livestock production** | felling forest for farms; draining wetlands; building towns and roads |
| (b) **Extraction of natural resources** | mining, quarrying, logging, drilling for oil and gas |
| (c) **Freshwater and marine pollution** | sewage and fertiliser in rivers; oil spills; plastic in the sea |

## Core recall: altering food webs

**Statement to learn:** through altering food webs and food chains, humans can have a negative impact on habitats.

Examples: hunting a predator lets prey overgraze plants; overfishing removes food for seabirds; introducing a species with no predators lets it replace natives; pesticides remove food for insect-eating birds.

## Core recall: five effects of deforestation as chains

| Effect | Chain |
|---|---|
| **Reducing biodiversity** | trees removed → food and shelter lost → many species die or leave → fewer species |
| **Extinction** | species found only in that forest → nowhere else to live → species lost permanently |
| **Loss of soil** | no roots to bind soil, no canopy or leaf litter to shield it → rain and wind erode the soil |
| **Flooding** | less uptake and transpiration, less interception of rain → more surface run-off → rivers overflow |
| **Increase of carbon dioxide** | less photosynthesis + combustion of felled trees + decomposition of wood → more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere |

## Calculations to expect

- **Percentage decrease = (original − new) ÷ original × 100.**
- **Mean rate of loss = area lost ÷ number of years**, with units such as "hectares per year".
- **Total species** = add the species in each group.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biodiversity | The number of different species that live in an area |
| Habitat | The place where an organism lives |
| Habitat destruction | Damage or removal of a habitat so that species can no longer live there |
| Deforestation | Clearing of forest, usually by felling or burning |
| Extinction | Permanent loss of every individual of a species |
| Natural resources | Materials taken from the environment, such as metal ores, stone, timber, oil |
| Soil erosion | Removal of soil by rain or wind |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from leaves |
| Run-off | Rainwater flowing over the surface into rivers |

## Exam traps

- **Biodiversity = number of organisms.** It is the number of different **species**.
- **"Pollution" alone.** Say freshwater or marine pollution makes the habitat unsuitable for its species.
- **Soil loss with no mechanism.** Mention roots binding soil and rain hitting bare ground.
- **Flooding with no mechanism.** Mention transpiration, interception and run-off.
- **Carbon dioxide with one reason.** Give less photosynthesis **and** combustion or decomposition.
- **Ozone.** Deforestation and the ozone layer are not linked in this sub-topic.
- **Extinction = fewer species locally.** Extinction is the loss of the species everywhere.
- **Percentage decrease.** Divide by the original value.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** reasons: reason + example.
- **State** the food-web idea in one sentence.
- **Explain** each deforestation effect: trees removed → what stops → effect.

## Self-test

1. Describe biodiversity.
2. Give the three reasons for habitat destruction named in the syllabus.
3. Give two examples of extraction of natural resources.
4. How can marine pollution destroy a habitat?
5. State how altering food webs affects habitats.
6. Explain how removing a predator by hunting could damage a habitat.
7. Explain why deforestation reduces biodiversity.
8. Explain why deforestation can cause extinction.
9. Explain how deforestation leads to loss of soil.
10. Explain how deforestation can lead to flooding.
11. Give two reasons why deforestation increases carbon dioxide in the atmosphere.
12. A forest shrinks from 5.0 to 3.5 million hectares in 15 years. Calculate the percentage decrease and the mean rate of loss.

### Answers

1. The number of different species that live in an area.
2. Increased area for housing, crop plant production and livestock production; extraction of natural resources; freshwater and marine pollution.
3. Any two: mining, quarrying, logging, drilling for oil or gas.
4. It makes the water unsuitable for the species living there, for example oil coating shorelines or plastic harming animals.
5. Through altering food webs and food chains, humans can have a negative impact on habitats.
6. The prey increase because they are not eaten, and may overgraze the plants, reducing food and shelter for other species.
7. Trees provide food and shelter for many species; without them, many species die or leave.
8. Species found only in that forest have nowhere else to live, so they die out completely.
9. Roots no longer bind the soil and rain falls directly on bare ground, so soil is washed or blown away.
10. Fewer trees absorb and transpire water and intercept rain, so more water runs off into rivers, which overflow.
11. Less carbon dioxide is removed by photosynthesis; burning or decomposing the trees releases carbon dioxide.
12. (5.0 − 3.5) ÷ 5.0 × 100 = 30%; 1.5 ÷ 15 = 0.1 million hectares per year.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing biodiversity as the number of organisms.
- Listing deforestation effects without explaining any.
- Using the new value as the denominator in a percentage decrease.
- Confusing a fall in local biodiversity with extinction.
- Forgetting combustion when explaining the rise in carbon dioxide.
