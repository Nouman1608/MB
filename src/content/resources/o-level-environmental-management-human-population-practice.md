## Section A

**1.** Define *population distribution*. **[1]**

**2.** State the age range usually used for the working population. **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by a *pro-natalist* policy. **[1]**

**4.** What do the letters LIC stand for? **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** The table shows data for two regions of the same country.

| Region | Population | Area (km²) |
|---|---|---|
| Coastal plain | 5 400 000 | 18 000 |
| Mountain plateau | 450 000 | 45 000 |

(a) Calculate the population density of each region. **[2]**
(b) Suggest **two** reasons for the difference. **[2]**

**6.** A population pyramid for Country R shows: 0-14 years, 18% of the population; 15-64 years, 60%;
65 and over, 22%. There are noticeably more women than men aged over 75.
(a) Calculate the percentage of the population that is dependent. **[1]**
(b) Describe the population structure of Country R. **[3]**
(c) Suggest whether Country R is an LIC or an HIC. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** Compare the population structure of a typical LIC with that of a typical HIC. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how improved access to healthcare can first increase and later reduce the rate of population
growth. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **two** push factors and **two** pull factors that lead to rural-urban migration. **[4]**

**10.** A city's population has grown from 6 million to 12 million in 25 years.
(a) State the term for a city with more than 10 million people. **[1]**
(b) Describe **four** environmental impacts of this growth. **[4]**
(c) Explain why traffic congestion increases as the city grows. **[2]**

**11.** Describe how access to education can reduce birth rates. **[3]**

## Section C

**12.** A country with a fast-growing population is considering an anti-natalist policy. Discuss the
benefits and limitations of this policy compared with improving access to contraception. **[6]**

**13.** An HIC with an ageing population and a falling birth rate is considering a pro-natalist policy or
encouraging immigration. Discuss which strategy would be more effective. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** How people are spread over an area. **[1]**

**2.** 15-64 years. **[1]**

**3.** A policy that encourages people to have more children. **[1]**

**4.** Low-income country. **[1]**

**5.** (a) Coastal plain: 5 400 000 / 18 000 = **300 per km²** **[1]**; mountain plateau: 450 000 / 45 000 =
**10 per km²** **[1]**.
(b) Any two: flat land on the coast is easier to build on and farm **[1]**; coasts have ports, trade and
jobs **[1]**; mountains are colder with poor soils and difficult access **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**6.** (a) 18 + 22 = **40%**. **[1]**
(b) A relatively small young population (18%) showing a low birth rate **[1]**; a large working population
(60%) **[1]**; a large elderly population (22%) with more elderly women than men, showing high life
expectancy and longer female life expectancy **[1]**.
(c) HIC **[1]**: low proportion of young people and high proportion of elderly people indicate low birth
rates and long life expectancy **[1]**.

**7.** LIC: wide base, showing a high proportion of children and a high birth rate **[1]**; narrow top, showing
lower life expectancy **[1]**. HIC: narrower base, showing a low birth rate **[1]**; wider top, showing more
elderly people and higher life expectancy **[1]**.

**8.** Healthcare reduces death rates, especially of infants, while birth rates stay high **[1]**, so
population grows faster at first **[1]**; later, as parents see that more children survive, they choose
smaller families and birth rates fall, slowing growth **[1]**.

**9.** Push, any two: lack of jobs; low farm incomes; drought or crop failure; poor schools or healthcare
**[2]**. Pull, any two: jobs in factories and services; higher wages; better schools and hospitals;
entertainment **[2]**.

**10.** (a) Megacity. **[1]**
(b) Any four: air pollution from vehicles and industry **[1]**; water pollution from untreated sewage **[1]**;
more waste than can be managed **[1]**; noise and light pollution **[1]**; loss of farmland and habitats as
the city expands **[1]**; higher energy usage **[1]**. **[max 4]**
(c) More people need to travel to work and services **[1]**, and road and public transport infrastructure
cannot expand as fast as the population **[1]**.

**11.** Girls who stay in education marry and have children later **[1]**; they are more likely to work, so the
opportunity cost of children rises **[1]**; they are more aware of contraception and family planning **[1]**.

**12.** **[6]** Anti-natalist policy benefits: can reduce the birth rate quickly through limits or incentives;
eases pressure on schools, jobs and resources. Limitations: creates an ageing population and possible
gender imbalance, as seen after China's one-child policy (1980-2015); may be unpopular or breach rights;
needs strong enforcement. Contraception benefits: voluntary, cheaper and improves women's health.
Limitations: slower; depends on supply, cost and cultural acceptance. Conclusion: improving contraception
and education is generally more acceptable and sustainable, though slower.

**13.** **[6]** Pro-natalist policy: child benefits, tax breaks or paid parental leave can raise the birth rate
and provide future workers; but they are costly, effects are often small, and new children do not join the
workforce for about 15-20 years. Immigration: provides working-age people immediately and fills labour
shortages; but can cause political tension and pressure on housing and services, and the immigrants
themselves will age. Conclusion: immigration is faster, while pro-natalist policy addresses the long-term
birth rate; many HICs combine the two.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating density without units (people per km²).
- Describing a pyramid with no figures.
- In "discuss" questions, failing to compare the two strategies the question names.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I know if a pyramid is from an LIC or an HIC?**
Look at the base and the top. A wide base and narrow
top suggest an LIC; a narrow base and wide top suggest an HIC.

**Why might healthcare increase population at first?**
Death rates fall before birth rates do, so for a time
many more people are born than die.

**What counts as an impact of population growth in 5014?**
Twelve are listed, including informal
settlements, megacities, energy usage, sanitation, water supply, waste, land, infrastructure, pollution,
traffic congestion and changing employment.
