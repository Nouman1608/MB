## Condensed recall

**Refraction (a).** Waves change speed when they enter a different medium. Unless they travel along the normal, they also change direction. Frequency stays the same; wavelength changes with speed.

**Direction rule (b).**

- Into a denser medium (slower): bends **towards** the normal.
- Into a less dense medium (faster): bends **away from** the normal.
- Along the normal: speed changes, direction does not.

**Wavefront picture (b).** The end of a wavefront that reaches the denser medium first slows first, so the wavefront pivots. Wavefronts are closer together where the wave is slower.

**Dispersion (c).** Each colour has a slightly different speed, and so a slightly different refractive index, in glass. A prism spreads white light into a spectrum: red bends least, violet most.

**Refractive index (d, e).**

| Form | When to use it |
|---|---|
| n = speed in vacuum ÷ speed in medium | Speeds given; take light in a vacuum (or air) as 3.00 × 10⁸ m/s |
| n = sin i ÷ sin r | Angles given; *i* in air, *r* in the medium |
| n = 1 ÷ sin c | Critical angle given or wanted |

n is greater than 1 and has no unit. Higher n means slower light and more bending.

**Leaving a medium.** sin(angle in air) = n × sin(angle inside). If this comes out greater than 1, the light is totally internally reflected.

**Critical angle (f).** The angle of incidence in the denser medium for which the refracted ray travels along the boundary (angle of refraction 90°). You do not need to recall values, only use the equation.

**Total internal reflection (g).** Happens only when both conditions hold:

1. Light travels from a denser medium towards a less dense one.
2. Angle of incidence is greater than the critical angle.

At exactly the critical angle the ray grazes the surface; below it, most light refracts out with a weak partial reflection.

**Optical fibres (h).** Light or infrared is totally internally reflected along a thin glass or plastic fibre, even round bends.

- Medicine: endoscopes let doctors see inside the body without major surgery.
- Communication: carry large amounts of information over long distances with little energy loss and no electrical interference.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Refraction | Change in direction caused by a change in wave speed at a boundary |
| Normal | Line at 90° to the boundary at the point where the ray meets it |
| Angle of refraction | Angle between the refracted ray and the normal |
| Optically denser | The medium in which light travels more slowly (higher n) |
| Dispersion | Splitting of white light into its colours by refraction |
| Refractive index | Ratio of the speed of light in a vacuum to its speed in the medium |
| Critical angle | Angle of incidence in the denser medium giving an angle of refraction of 90° |
| Total internal reflection | Complete reflection inside a denser medium when the angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle |

## Required practical summary

Investigate the refraction of light by different substances.

- Draw round a block (or transparent tank of liquid), draw a normal, and shine a narrow ray at a measured *i*.
- Mark the emergent ray with crosses, join entry and exit points to find the ray inside, measure *r*.
- Repeat for several *i*; plot sin *i* against sin *r*. Gradient = n.
- Uncertainty is largest at small angles, because a 1° reading error is a bigger fraction of a small angle.
- Hazard: a ray box lamp gets hot; work in a dimmed room carefully.

## Exam traps

- Writing "the light slows down because it bends". It bends because it slows down.
- Using sin r ÷ sin i by mistake for a ray entering glass.
- Saying total internal reflection happens when light enters glass.
- Stating "angle equal to the critical angle" as the condition. It must be greater.
- Forgetting that frequency does not change.
- Giving refractive index a unit.

## Quick self-test

1. What causes refraction?
2. When does a wave cross a boundary without changing direction?
3. Light enters water from air. Does it bend towards or away from the normal?
4. Light travels at 2.26 × 10⁸ m/s in a liquid. Calculate its refractive index.
5. A ray in air enters a block with i = 50° and r = 30°. Calculate n.
6. Calculate the critical angle of a material with n = 1.40.
7. A material has a critical angle of 37°. Calculate its refractive index.
8. State the two conditions for total internal reflection.
9. Why does a prism produce a spectrum?
10. Light inside glass (n = 1.50) meets the surface at 45°. What happens?
11. Give one use of optical fibres in medicine and one benefit in communication.
12. In the required practical, why are small angles of incidence less reliable?

### Answers

1. A change in the speed of the wave as it enters a different medium.
2. When it travels along the normal (at 90° to the surface).
3. Towards the normal (water is denser than air).
4. n = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 2.26 × 10⁸ = **1.33**.
5. n = sin 50° ÷ sin 30° = 0.766 ÷ 0.500 = **1.53**.
6. sin c = 1 ÷ 1.40 = 0.714, so c = **45.6°**.
7. n = 1 ÷ sin 37° = 1 ÷ 0.602 = **1.66**.
8. Light travelling from a denser to a less dense medium; angle of incidence greater than the critical angle.
9. Different colours travel at different speeds in glass, so they refract by different amounts and spread out.
10. 45° is greater than the critical angle (41.8°), so it is totally internally reflected.
11. Endoscopes to see inside the body; large amounts of information carried with little energy loss (or no electrical interference).
12. A fixed reading error of about 1° is a larger fraction of a small angle, so the ratio is less reliable.
