These notes condense **M1.1 Motion in a straight line with constant acceleration** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Mathematics (9660), version 5.2, Unit PSM1. They focus on the methods for harder, multi-step problems. For full worked examples see the [M1.1 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-motion-constant-acceleration/); to practise, use the [M1.1 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-mathematics-9660-m1-motion-constant-acceleration-practice/). Take g = 9.8 m s⁻².

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displacement s | Position relative to a fixed point, with sign; a vector |
| Distance | Total length of path travelled; a scalar, never negative |
| Velocity v | Rate of change of displacement, with sign |
| Speed | Magnitude of velocity |
| Acceleration a | Rate of change of velocity |
| Average speed | Total distance ÷ total time |
| Average velocity | Total displacement ÷ total time |
| Particle | Object modelled as a point, so its size and rotation are ignored |
| Uniform acceleration | Constant acceleration, so the suvat equations apply |

## The five equations and when to use each

| Equation | Leaves out | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| v = u + at | s | Time to reach a speed |
| s = ut + ½at² | v | Position at a given time; meeting problems |
| s = vt − ½at² | u | Final speed known, starting speed not |
| s = ½(u + v)t | a | Speeds and time known |
| v² = u² + 2as | t | Stopping distances; impact speeds |

All five come from a straight-line velocity–time graph: gradient gives v = u + at, trapezium area gives s = ½(u + v)t, and the others follow by substitution.

## Sign rules

- Fix a positive direction first and write it on the sketch.
- Points below the start are negative displacements when up is positive.
- Deceleration in the positive direction means a negative a.
- For gravity with up positive, a = −9.8 throughout the flight, including at the top.

## Graph facts

| Graph | Gradient | Area |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement–time | Velocity | No meaning |
| Velocity–time | Acceleration | Displacement (below the axis counts as negative) |

- A straight sloping velocity–time line means constant acceleration; a curved displacement–time graph goes with it.
- For distance, add the areas above and below the axis as positive amounts.

## Methods for multi-step problems

**Two particles.** Write each displacement from one common origin as a function of t, allowing for any delay in starting. Meeting: set the displacements equal. Greatest separation before one catches the other: when their velocities are equal.

**Several stages.** Sketch the velocity–time graph, find the speed at each change of stage, and treat each stage separately. An unknown time is usually fixed by a total distance (area) or total time condition.

**Three points.** Measure every displacement from the first point so the unknowns are only u and a, then solve simultaneous equations.

**From a height.** Use s = −h for the landing point below the start. A quadratic in t: reject negative roots; two positive roots are the up and down times at that level.

## Modelling assumptions

- Particle model: size ignored.
- No air resistance.
- Motion in a straight line.
- Acceleration constant; g = 9.8 m s⁻².

Removing the no-air-resistance assumption gives a lower greatest height and a smaller impact speed.

## Exam traps

- Positive displacement for a point below a cliff.
- Keeping a negative time root.
- Merging stages with different accelerations.
- Measuring two particles from different origins.
- Rounding mid-calculation, which gives the wrong third significant figure.
- Giving "deceleration of −4": a deceleration is quoted as a positive magnitude.
- Using the area under a displacement–time graph.

## Self-test

1. A cyclist accelerates uniformly from 3 m s⁻¹ to 9 m s⁻¹ in 4 s. Find the distance.
2. A car travelling at 25 m s⁻¹ stops in 62.5 m with uniform deceleration. Find the deceleration.
3. A particle finishes a 5 s stage at 12 m s⁻¹ having accelerated at 1.6 m s⁻². Find the distance in that stage.
4. A ball is dropped from rest and falls 19.6 m. Find the time taken.
5. A ball is thrown up at 19.6 m s⁻¹ from ground level. Find the greatest height.
6. For the ball in question 5, find how long it is more than 14.7 m above the ground.
7. A stone is thrown down at 5 m s⁻¹ from a bridge 30 m above water. Find its speed on hitting the water.
8. A runs at a constant 7 m s⁻¹ past O. B starts from rest at O at the same instant with acceleration 1.4 m s⁻². When does B catch A?
9. In question 8, what is the greatest gap between A and B before B catches A?
10. A particle starts from rest, accelerates at 2 m s⁻² for 5 s, then moves at constant speed for 8 s. Find the total distance.
11. Particle passes P, Q, R with PQ = 10 m in 2 s and QR = 18 m in the next 2 s. Find the acceleration.
12. State two modelling assumptions used for vertical motion under gravity.

### Answers

1. s = ½(3 + 9) × 4 = 24 m.
2. 0 = 625 − 125a gives a = 5, a deceleration of 5 m s⁻².
3. s = vt − ½at² = 60 − 20 = 40 m.
4. 19.6 = 4.9t², t = 2 s.
5. 0 = 19.6² − 19.6s, s = 19.6 m.
6. 14.7 = 19.6t − 4.9t² gives t² − 4t + 3 = 0, t = 1 or 3, so 2 s.
7. Down positive: v² = 25 + 2 × 9.8 × 30 = 613, v = 24.8 m s⁻¹.
8. 7t = 0.7t², t = 10 s.
9. Speeds equal at t = 5: gap = 35 − 17.5 = 17.5 m.
10. 25 + 10 × 8 = 105 m.
11. From P: 10 = 2u + 2a and 28 = 4u + 8a give a = 2 m s⁻².
12. Any two: particle model; no air resistance; constant g = 9.8 m s⁻²; motion in a vertical straight line.
