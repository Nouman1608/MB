## Equations

| Equation | Meaning | Units |
|---|---|---|
| P = 1/f | lens power | D (f in m) |
| 1/u + 1/v = 1/f | lens equation | m⁻¹ |
| m = v/u | magnification | none |

**Real is positive**: real object and image distances positive; virtual image distances negative; converging lens f positive; diverging lens f negative.

## Lenses: recall

| | Converging | Diverging |
|---|---|---|
| Effect on parallel rays | brings to a real focus | spreads as if from a virtual focus |
| Sign of f and P | positive | negative |
| Image of distant object | real, inverted | virtual, upright, diminished |
| Object inside f | virtual, upright, magnified | (always virtual) |

## 3.10.1.1 Physics of vision: recall

- **Cornea**: most of the refraction. **Lens**: fine adjustment by **ciliary muscles** (accommodation).
- Image on retina: **real and inverted**.
- Model the eye as one converging lens a fixed distance from the retina.
- **Far point**: furthest point in focus (normal: infinity). **Near point**: closest point in focus (normal: 0.25 m).
- **Rods**: high sensitivity, dim light, one type, no colour, absent from the fovea centre, many share one nerve fibre.
- **Cones**: bright light, three types (peaks near 420, 530 and 560 nm), colour vision, concentrated in the fovea, individual nerve connections there.
- **Spectral response**: about 400–700 nm; rods peak at a shorter wavelength than the cones' combined daylight response.

**Spatial resolution**

- Two images resolved if they fall on cones separated by at least one unstimulated cone.
- Best: fovea, bright light (cones, separate connections).
- Worse: periphery, dim light (rods, shared connections).
- Given cone spacing s and eye length L: minimum angle ≈ 2s/L.

## 3.10.1.2 Defects and correction: recall

| Defect | Problem | Image without lens | Correcting lens | Lens forms image of… |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Myopia | far point < ∞ | in front of retina | diverging (negative P) | object at ∞, at the far point |
| Hypermetropia | near point > 0.25 m | behind retina | converging (positive P) | object at 0.25 m, at the near point |
| Astigmatism | power differs between planes | lines in one orientation blurred | cylindrical | — |

**Method: myopia** — u = ∞, v = −(far point): P = −1/(far point).

**Method: hypermetropia** — u = 0.25 m, v = −(near point): P = 4.0 − 1/(near point).

**Astigmatism prescription**: spherical power (D), cylindrical power (D), axis (degrees, 0°–180°, orientation of the cylinder).

**Correcting myopia moves the near point further away**: find it with v = −(old near point) and the lens power.

## Ray diagram checklist

- Draw the eye as one converging lens with the retina a fixed distance behind it.
- **Uncorrected myopia**: parallel rays meet in front of the retina.
- **Corrected myopia**: diverging lens first; rays leave it as if from the far point and meet on the retina.
- **Uncorrected hypermetropia**: rays from a near object would meet behind the retina.
- **Corrected hypermetropia**: converging lens first; rays leave it as if from the near point and meet on the retina.
- Use a ruler, add arrows, and show where the rays would have met without the lens.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Principal focus | Point where rays parallel to the axis meet (or appear to diverge from) after the lens |
| Focal length | Distance from the lens to the principal focus |
| Power | 1/focal length, in dioptres |
| Dioptre | Unit of lens power, m⁻¹ |
| Accommodation | Change in power of the eye's lens to focus objects at different distances |
| Far point | Furthest distance at which the eye can focus |
| Near point | Closest distance at which the eye can focus |
| Spatial resolution | Ability to distinguish two close objects as separate |
| Cylindrical lens | Lens with power in one plane only |

## Exam traps

- Signs: v is negative for the virtual images that correcting lenses produce.
- Use metres: 25 cm = 0.25 m gives 4.0 D.
- "Short sight" is myopia; "long sight" is hypermetropia.
- Rods: more sensitive but worse resolution.
- Hypermetropia: the object is at 0.25 m (u), the image is at the near point (v).
- Astigmatism needs a cylindrical lens, not just a stronger spherical one.

## Self-test

**1.** Which part of the eye does most of the refraction?
**2.** Is the image on the retina real or virtual, upright or inverted?
**3.** A lens has f = −0.40 m. Find its power and state its type.
**4.** Why can't we see colour in dim light?
**5.** Why is resolution best at the fovea?
**6.** State the sign convention used in AQA lens calculations.
**7.** A myopic eye has a far point of 0.80 m. Find the correcting power.
**8.** A hypermetropic eye has a near point of 0.50 m. Find the correcting power.
**9.** Which type of lens corrects astigmatism?
**10.** Name the three parts of an astigmatism prescription.
**11.** A converging lens has f = 0.10 m. Where is the image of an object 0.15 m away?
**12.** Define accommodation.

## Self-test answers

**1.** The cornea.
**2.** Real and inverted.
**3.** −2.5 D; diverging.
**4.** Only rods work in dim light, and there is only one type of rod.
**5.** The fovea is packed with cones, each with its own nerve connection.
**6.** Real is positive: real distances positive, virtual negative; diverging lenses have negative f.
**7.** P = 1/(−0.80) = −1.25 D.
**8.** P = 4.0 − 1/0.50 = +2.0 D.
**9.** Cylindrical.
**10.** Spherical power, cylindrical power and axis.
**11.** 1/v = 10 − 6.67, so v = +0.30 m (real).
**12.** The eye's ability to change the power of its lens to focus on objects at different distances.
