> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge**, for practice on the report form in 1123
> Paper 2 Section A, Directed Writing. The two source texts are original and shorter than the real
> 400–450 words. Question 12 is a full task at the syllabus tariff of 25 marks (15 writing, 10 reading);
> the other questions practise its separate skills with practice marks.

## Section A

**1.** Write a title and a one-sentence purpose statement for a report to a head teacher on whether to extend the school day by thirty minutes. **[2]**

**2.** Rewrite in an impersonal, objective register: "I reckon loads of students would be really annoyed by this." **[2]**

**3.** Rewrite this attribution so that it signals possible bias: "Cars are now much safer." (The claim comes from a car manufacturer's advertisement.) **[1]**

**4.** Improve this recommendation: "Something should be done about the queues." **[2]**

**5.** Give two suitable headings for the findings of a report on a school's new recycling scheme. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why "Text 1 says... Text 2 says..." is a weak way to organise a report. **[1]**

## Section B

Read the two texts (original, written for this practice set).

> **Text 1: from the volunteer coordinator's notes**
>
> Twelve older students have offered to help younger children with homework at the community centre
> on Saturday mornings. The hall is free from 9 a.m. to 12 noon. Volunteers would need a short
> training session, and the centre would need someone to supervise who has had safety checks. Last
> year a similar club in the next town was so popular that it had a waiting list.
>
> **Text 2: summary of a survey of parents**
>
> Forty parents replied to the centre's survey. Thirty said they would like a homework club, and many
> said they find it hard to help with mathematics. Ten said Saturday mornings clash with sports and
> religious classes. Several parents asked whether the club would be free, and two asked whether
> volunteers would be "proper teachers".

**7.** Plan the headings for a report to the community centre committee on the proposed club, and list which source points belong under each. **[3]**

**8.** Write the findings section on benefits (about 70 words). **[3]**

**9.** Write the findings section on concerns (about 70 words), evaluating at least one source. **[4]**

**10.** Write two specific recommendations that follow from your findings. **[2]**

**11.** Calculate the percentage of parents who replied in favour of a club, and explain one limitation of the survey as evidence. **[3]**

**12.** Write a report for the community centre committee on the proposal to start a Saturday homework club, with recommendations. Use and evaluate ideas from both texts. Write about 250–350 words. **[25]**

## Answers

**1.** For example: title "Report for the Head Teacher: Extending the School Day" **[1]**; purpose "This report considers the proposal to add thirty minutes to the school day and makes a recommendation." **[1]**

**2.** For example: "Many students would be likely to object to this change." **[2]** One mark for removing personal and informal wording, one for keeping the meaning.

**3.** For example: "According to the manufacturer's advertisement, cars are now much safer." **[1]**

**4.** For example: "It is recommended that a second serving point be opened in the canteen between 12.30 and 1 p.m." **[2]** One mark for a specific action, one for detail of when or where.

**5.** Any two, one mark each: "Benefits" **[1]**; "Problems reported" **[1]**. Also creditable: "Participation so far"; "Costs"; "Practical issues".

**6.** It retells the sources in order instead of grouping evidence by theme, which makes comparison and evaluation harder **[1]**.

**7.** One mark per heading with relevant points:
- *Benefits*: twelve volunteers; free hall; parents want help with mathematics; a similar club had a waiting list **[1]**.
- *Concerns*: Saturday clashes; cost; supervision and safety checks; whether volunteers are qualified **[1]**.
- *Recommendations*: training, supervision, timing and cost decisions **[1]**.

**8.** Model:

> **Benefits**
> Twelve older students have volunteered, and the hall is free on Saturday mornings, so the club could
> start at little cost. Demand appears strong: thirty of the forty parents who replied want a club, and
> many find mathematics difficult to help with. A similar club in the next town had a waiting list,
> which suggests that interest is likely to last.

Marking: uses points from both texts **[1]**; groups them under the theme **[1]**; objective register **[1]**.

**9.** Model:

> **Concerns**
> Ten parents reported that Saturday mornings clash with sports and religious classes, so some
> children may be unable to attend. Parents also asked about cost and whether volunteers would be
> qualified. These are fair questions: the volunteers are students, not teachers, and the coordinator
> notes that they would need training. A supervisor with safety checks would also be required, which
> the centre does not yet have.

Marking: uses concerns from both texts **[1]**; evaluates the volunteers' qualifications fairly **[1]**; develops the supervision issue **[1]**; objective register **[1]**.

**10.** For example, one mark each: "Arrange a training session for volunteers and appoint a supervisor with safety checks before the club opens." **[1]** "Run the club free of charge for one term on alternate Saturday and Wednesday sessions, to test which time suits more families." **[1]**

**11.** 30 / 40 = 75% **[1]**. Limitation: only forty parents replied **[1]**, and those who answer a survey about a homework club may be the most interested, so the result may overstate demand **[1]**.

**12.** Marked out of 25: reading (R3, R5) and writing (W1–W5).
- **Reading:** uses points from both texts; develops them (mathematics support, cost to families, safety requirements); evaluates them (a small survey of forty, a self-selected response, the volunteers' status, the comparison with the next town) **[10]**.
- **Writing:** report conventions (title, purpose, headings, findings by theme, conclusion, specific recommendations); objective, impersonal register suited to a committee; varied vocabulary and sentences; accurate spelling, punctuation and grammar **[15]**.

Lower-level responses have no headings, retell the texts in order, adopt a persuasive tone, or end without recommendations.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4:** replacing one vague phrase with another ("Action should be taken").
- **Question 9:** dismissing the parents' concerns rather than weighing them.
- **Question 11:** calculating the percentage but not commenting on the sample.
- **Question 12:** writing persuasively, as if the report were a speech for the club.
