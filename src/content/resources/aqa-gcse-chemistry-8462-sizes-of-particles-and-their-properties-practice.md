These questions cover section **4.2.4.1 Sizes of particles and their properties** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on
Paper 1 at Foundation and Higher Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content. Show your working in
calculations.

Learn the content first in the [sizes of particles study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-sizes-of-particles-and-their-properties/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-sizes-of-particles-and-their-properties-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State the size range of nanoparticles in nanometres. **[1]**
**(b)** State the size range of fine particles (PM2.5) in nanometres. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Write 1 nm in metres, in standard form. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 50 nm in metres, in standard form. **[1]**

**3.** A particle has a diameter of 2 × 10⁻⁷ m.

**(a)** Give the diameter in nanometres. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether it is a nanoparticle or a fine particle. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by "coarse particles" in this context. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A cube has sides of length 4 cm.

**(a)** Calculate its surface area. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its volume. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. **[1]**

**6.** A second cube has sides of length 0.4 cm.

**(a)** Calculate its surface area to volume ratio. **[2]**
**(b)** Show how the ratio compares with that of the 4 cm cube in question 5. **[2]**

**7.** A cube-shaped nanoparticle has sides of length 5 nm.

**(a)** Calculate its surface area. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its volume. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate its surface area to volume ratio, and state the unit. **[2]**

**8.** A fine particle has a diameter of 1500 nm and a nanoparticle has a diameter of 15 nm.

**(a)** Calculate how many times larger the fine particle is. **[1]**
**(b)** Give your answer to part (a) as an order of magnitude. **[1]**

**9.** An atom has a diameter of about 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. A nanoparticle has a diameter of 4 × 10⁻⁸ m. Calculate how many
atoms would fit across the nanoparticle. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why a catalyst made of nanoparticles may need a smaller mass than the same catalyst in larger
pieces to be effective. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1 to 100 nm** [1]
**(b)** **100 to 2500 nm** [1]

**2. (a)** **1 × 10⁻⁹ m** [1]
**(b)** 50 × 10⁻⁹ = **5 × 10⁻⁸ m** [1]

**3. (a)** 2 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 10⁻⁹ = **200 nm** [1]
**(b)** A **fine particle** (it is between 100 and 2500 nm) [1]

**4.** Larger particles, often called **dust** (PM10), with diameters between about 2.5 × 10⁻⁶ m and 1 × 10⁻⁵ m [1]

**5. (a)** 6 × 4 × 4 = **96 cm²** [1]
**(b)** 4 × 4 × 4 = **64 cm³** [1]
**(c)** 96 ÷ 64 = **1.5** (per cm) [1]

**6. (a)** Surface area = 6 × 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.96 cm²; volume = 0.4 × 0.4 × 0.4 = 0.064 cm³ [1]; ratio = 0.96 ÷ 0.064 =
**15** per cm [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 15 ÷ 1.5 = **10** [1]; the side fell by a factor of 10 and the ratio rose by a factor of 10 [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 6 × 5 × 5 = **150 nm²** [1]
**(b)** 5 × 5 × 5 = **125 nm³** [1]
**(c)** 150 ÷ 125 = **1.2** [1]; unit: **per nm** (nm⁻¹) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** 1500 ÷ 15 = **100** [1]
**(b)** **10²** (about a hundred times larger) [1]

**9.** 4 × 10⁻⁸ ÷ 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ [1] = **400** atoms [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Nanoparticles have a very high surface area to volume ratio [1]; a larger proportion of the atoms are on the
surface where the reactants can reach them [1]; so a smaller mass gives the same or greater effect [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Getting the standard form of nanometres wrong (10⁻⁹ m).
- Saying the ratio decreases for smaller particles.
- Forgetting to include units, or giving area in cm rather than cm².
- Mixing up the 6 faces of a cube when calculating surface area.
- Not converting nm and m to the same unit before dividing.
