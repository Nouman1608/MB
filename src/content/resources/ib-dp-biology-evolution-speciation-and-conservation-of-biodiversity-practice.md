## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between evolution and a change that an organism acquires during its lifetime. **[2]**

**2.** Part of the same protein was sequenced in three species (hypothetical data).

| Species | Amino acid sequence |
|---|---|
| X | ACDEFGHIKL |
| Y | ACDEFGHVKL |
| Z | ACNEYGHVRL |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage difference between X and Z. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce which two species share the most recent common ancestor, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** Outline how selective breeding of domesticated animals provides evidence for evolution. **[3]**

**4.** The arm of a human and the wing of a bird have the same bones in the same arrangement. The eye of an octopus and the eye of a human both form images but develop differently.
**(a)** State the term for the structures in the arm and the wing. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the eyes of the octopus and human illustrate convergent evolution. **[3]**

**5.** A region contains 2,400 species. Over a period, 85 new species form and 140 species become extinct.
**(a)** Calculate the number of species at the end of the period. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a population of finches that slowly becomes larger-beaked over time, remaining one species, is not an example of speciation. **[2]**

**6.** Bonobos live south of the Congo River and common chimpanzees live to the north.
**(a)** Explain the role of geographical isolation in the divergence of the two species. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how differential selection contributes to the formation of separate species. **[2]**

**7.** (HL only) Two forms of a fish live in one lake. One form breeds in spring and the other in autumn.
**(a)** State the type of reproductive isolation. **[1]**
**(b)** Distinguish between sympatric and allopatric speciation. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline why courtship behaviour usually prevents hybridization in animals. **[2]**

**8.** (HL only) Plant species P has 2n = 18 and species R has 2n = 22. They hybridize, and the hybrid is sterile. A fertile offspring later arises by polyploidy.
**(a)** Calculate the chromosome number of the hybrid and of the fertile polyploid. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the hybrid is sterile and why the polyploid is fertile. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why a mule is also sterile. **[2]**

**9.** (HL only) Explain how adaptive radiation increases the biodiversity of an island archipelago with vacant niches. **[3]**

**10.** A grassland survey recorded 62 species. A repeat survey by the same method recorded 51.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in species richness. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the abundance of each species should also be recorded. **[2]**
**(c)** Evaluate the use of data collected by citizen scientists. **[3]**

**11.** Mixed dipterocarp forest in a Southeast Asian region covered 5,400 km² and, 12 years later, 4,320 km².
**(a)** Calculate the mean area lost per year. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline two human causes of the loss of this ecosystem. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline the cause of extinction of the Caribbean monk seal. **[2]**
**(d)** State the level of biodiversity that is reduced when a species goes extinct. **[1]**

**12.** A forest bird has few close relatives, and its forest is being cleared. Fewer than 200 remain.
**(a)** Explain why EDGE of Existence would prioritize this species. **[2]**
**(b)** Discuss the need for several approaches to conserving it, referring to in situ and ex situ methods. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Evolution is a genetic change in the features of a population over generations [1]; an acquired change is not genetic, so is not passed on to offspring and is not evolution [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "distinguish" answer needs both sides stated. Writing only what evolution is leaves the comparison unfinished.

**2. (a)** X and Z differ at positions 3, 5, 8 and 9, which is 4 of 10 [1]: **40%**. **[1]**
**(b)** X and Y differ by only 1 position (10%), fewer than the other pairs [1], so X and Y share the most recent common ancestor, because fewer differences mean less time since divergence [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State the counts for each pair as evidence. A conclusion without numbers is easy to lose marks on.

**3.** Breeds of one species, such as dogs, differ greatly in size, coat and behaviour [1]; the breeds and varieties arose from the original wild species in a short time [1]; this shows heritable variation exists and that selection of which individuals breed changes populations rapidly, as natural selection could [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a specific example and link it to heritable variation and selection. A statement that humans "changed" the animals is too vague.

**4. (a)** **Homologous** structures [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Both are structures used for the same function (forming images) [1]; but they have different evolutionary origins and develop differently, so are analogous [1]; similar selection pressures in unrelated lineages led to similar solutions, which is convergent evolution [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both the shared function and the separate ancestry. Convergent evolution is the process; analogous is the term for the structures.

**5. (a)** 2,400 + 85 − 140 = **2,345** species [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Speciation is the splitting of a species into two [1]; gradual change in one lineage with no split leaves a single species [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the idea of splitting. Saying "the beak is just a change" does not explain the definition.

**6. (a)** The river separated an ancestral population [1], stopping gene flow between them (reproductive isolation) [1], so mutations and selection caused allele frequencies to diverge until they were separate species [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The environments on each side differed, so different alleles were favoured [1]; over generations the populations diverged in traits and could no longer interbreed successfully [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each step to the next. Naming the river without explaining the loss of gene flow gains little.

**7. (a)** **Temporal** isolation [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Sympatric speciation happens in the same area, with behavioural or temporal isolation [1]; allopatric speciation happens when populations are geographically separated [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Courtship behaviour is specific to a species [1]; an individual does not respond to the displays of another species, so mating between species is rare [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs a difference stated for each type. Part (c) should say why behaviour is species-specific, not just that it "prevents mating".

**8. (a)** Hybrid: 9 + 11 = **20** [1]; polyploid: 2 × 20 = **40** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Chromosomes from P and R are not homologous, so they cannot pair in meiosis [1]; viable gametes are not formed [1]; doubling gives each chromosome a partner, so meiosis works and gametes form [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** A mule has 63 chromosomes, from a horse (32) and a donkey (31) [1]; they cannot all pair in meiosis, so it produces no viable gametes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the numbers in a calculation and then use them in the explanation. Unpaired chromosomes is the point; saying "hybrids are always sterile" is wrong, as polyploids show.

**9.** Adaptive radiation is the evolution of many related species from one ancestor [1]; each is adapted to a different vacant niche [1]; close relatives coexist without competing, so the number of species increases [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the vacant niches and the lack of competition. Without them the answer describes speciation only.

**10. (a)** (51 − 62) ÷ 62 × 100 [1] = **−17.7%** (a fall of 17.7%) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Richness counts species only [1]; abundance shows evenness, so changes in dominance or declining populations are detected even if the species list is unchanged [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Strength: many observers cover more habitats and years at low cost [1]; limitation: variable identification skill or uneven sampling reduces reliability [1]; judgement: useful if methods are checked and validated against expert data [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An "evaluate" answer needs both sides and a conclusion. Calculate from the earlier value, and give the sign.

**11. (a)** 5,400 − 4,320 = 1,080 km² [1]; 1,080 ÷ 12 = **90 km² per year** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two, such as logging for timber [1] and clearance for plantations such as oil palm [1]; fire is also accepted. **[2]**
**(c)** Hunted for oil and meat [1]; overfishing reduced the seals' food supply [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** **Species diversity** [1] (genetic diversity also falls). **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the cause of each loss by name. A statement that humans "destroyed" the forest is too general.

**12. (a)** It is evolutionarily distinct, with few close relatives, so its extinction would remove a large amount of unique evolutionary history [1]; it is globally endangered with a very small population [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any six of the following: no single approach is sufficient [1]; in situ protection of the remaining forest in a managed reserve [1]; reclamation or rewilding of degraded forest to enlarge habitat [1]; ex situ captive breeding in a zoo as insurance against loss [1]; storage of germ plasm in tissue banks [1]; a reasoned judgement that a combination is needed, since different species require different measures [1]. Also acceptable: control of poaching or invasive species in the reserve. Further valid points earn no marks beyond six. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer should weigh approaches and reach a conclusion. A list of methods without a link to this bird will not reach the top of the mark range.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling an acquired characteristic evolution.
- Giving conclusions about relatedness with no counts or percentages.
- Mixing up homologous and analogous.
- Counting gradual change in one lineage as speciation.
- Forgetting that geographical isolation works by stopping gene flow.
- Treating all hybrids as sterile and ignoring polyploidy.
- Dividing by the later value in percentage change.
- Describing causes of loss in general terms instead of naming the case study.
- Ignoring the ethical and economic debate in prioritizing species.
