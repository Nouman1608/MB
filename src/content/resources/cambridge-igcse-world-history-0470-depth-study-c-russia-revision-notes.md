These notes condense **Depth Study C: Russia, 1905-41** in Cambridge IGCSE History 0470. Its four key questions ask
why the Tsarist regime collapsed in 1917, how the Bolsheviks gained power and consolidated their rule, how Stalin
gained and held on to power, and what the impact of Stalin's economic policies was. For explanation and a worked (b)
answer, read the [Depth Study C study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-depth-study-russia-1905-41/); to
apply the facts, try the [Depth Study C practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-depth-study-c-russia-practice/).

Dates for 1917 are given in the western calendar, which the syllabus uses (the March and November revolutions). Russia
then used an older calendar, so many books call them the February and October revolutions.

## Key question 1: Why did the Tsarist regime collapse in 1917?

**Ruling Russia before 1914**

- **Autocracy:** Nicholas II (Tsar from 1894) believed he ruled by divine right; no elected parliament before 1906.
- **Control:** the Okhrana (secret police), censorship, the Orthodox Church and Russification of minorities.
- **Society:** a huge peasant majority short of land; a small but growing working class in crowded factory cities;
  a small middle class wanting a say in government.
- **Opponents:** liberals (later the Kadets), Socialist Revolutionaries (peasant-based) and the Marxist Social
  Democrats, split from 1903 into Bolsheviks (Lenin) and Mensheviks.

**The 1905 Revolution and survival**

- Defeat by Japan (1904-05) exposed the regime's weakness.
- **Bloody Sunday** (January 1905): troops fired on a peaceful march led by Father Gapon.
- Strikes, peasant risings, the *Potemkin* mutiny and the St Petersburg Soviet followed.
- **How the Tsar survived:** the **October Manifesto** promised a Duma and civil rights, splitting liberals from
  socialists; peace with Japan freed loyal troops; the army stayed loyal and crushed the Soviet and peasant risings.
- **Aftermath:** the Fundamental Laws (1906) kept the Tsar's power; the first two Dumas were dissolved and voting
  rules changed to favour landowners; **Stolypin** combined repression (the hangman's noose became known as
  "Stolypin's necktie") with land reform to create prosperous peasant farmers; he was assassinated in 1911. Troops
  shot striking miners at the Lena goldfields in 1912.

**The First World War**

- Defeats at Tannenberg (1914) and huge losses in 1915.
- The Tsar became commander-in-chief (September 1915), so defeats were blamed on him personally.
- The Tsarina and Rasputin influenced ministers in Petrograd; Rasputin was murdered in December 1916.
- Inflation, food and fuel shortages, and railways overloaded with military traffic.

**March 1917:** bread queues and strikes in Petrograd, starting on International Women's Day; soldiers mutinied and
joined the crowds; the Duma set up a Provisional Government; the Petrograd Soviet was formed; generals advised the
Tsar to go, and he abdicated.

## Key question 2: How did the Bolsheviks gain power and consolidate their rule?

- **Dual power:** the Provisional Government shared authority with the Petrograd Soviet, whose **Order No. 1**
  told soldiers to obey the Soviet first.
- **Provisional Government's mistakes:** kept fighting (the June Offensive failed); delayed land reform and elections
  to a Constituent Assembly.
- **Lenin's return** (April 1917): the **April Theses** -- no support for the government, "all power to the Soviets",
  "peace, bread, land".
- **July Days:** failed armed demonstrations; Lenin fled to Finland.
- **Kornilov affair** (August-September): Kerensky armed the Red Guards to stop the general's march on Petrograd,
  restoring Bolshevik prestige; the Bolsheviks won a majority in the Petrograd Soviet.
- **Seizure of power** (6-7 November): Trotsky's Military Revolutionary Committee took key points; the Winter Palace
  fell. Decrees on Peace and Land followed.
- **Consolidation:** the Cheka (December 1917); the **Constituent Assembly**, in which the Socialist Revolutionaries
  were the largest party, closed after one day (January 1918); Brest-Litovsk (March 1918) ended the war at a heavy
  price in land.
- **Civil War** (1918-21): Reds held the central core, railways and industry; Trotsky built a disciplined Red Army
  with commissars and former Tsarist officers; Whites (Kolchak, Denikin, Yudenich, Wrangel) were divided and far
  apart; foreign intervention helped Bolshevik propaganda; the Tsar's family was shot in 1918.
- **War Communism:** grain requisitioning, nationalisation, rationing; famine and unrest followed.
- **Kronstadt** (March 1921): sailors who had backed the revolution rebelled and were crushed; Lenin introduced the
  **New Economic Policy**: private trade and small businesses allowed, a tax in kind replaced requisitioning. Food
  production recovered, but "NEPmen" were resented by many communists.

## Key question 3: How did Stalin gain and hold on to power?

- **Positions:** General Secretary from 1922, controlling appointments; he placed loyal supporters in the party.
- **Lenin's Testament** criticised Stalin but was not published.
- **Trotsky's errors:** missed Lenin's funeral; seen as arrogant; his "permanent revolution" seemed less attractive
  than Stalin's "socialism in one country".
- **Shifting alliances:** with Zinoviev and Kamenev against Trotsky; with Bukharin against them; then against
  Bukharin and the Right.
- **The Purges:** Kirov's murder (December 1934); show trials of Old Bolsheviks (1936-38); the army purge
  including Marshal Tukhachevsky (1937); the NKVD; millions arrested and sent to the gulag labour camps.
- **Propaganda and culture:** cult of personality; history rewritten; **socialist realism** in art and literature.
- **The 1936 Constitution** promised rights that were not honoured.

## Key question 4: What was the impact of Stalin's economic policies?

- **Five-Year Plans** (from 1928), set by Gosplan: heavy industry, coal, steel, oil, electricity; projects such as
  Magnitogorsk and the Dnieper dam; Stakhanov's record coal shift (1935) used to push workers harder.
- **Collectivisation** (from 1929): peasant farms merged into collective farms; kulaks "liquidated as a class";
  peasants slaughtered livestock rather than hand it over; famine in 1932-33, especially in Ukraine and Kazakhstan.
- **Social groups:** skilled workers and party officials gained; peasants lost most; women entered industry in large
  numbers but kept domestic burdens; divorce was made harder and abortion banned in 1936; minorities faced
  Russification and deportation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Autocracy | Rule by one person with unlimited power |
| Duma | Russian parliament created after 1905 |
| Soviet | Council of workers' and soldiers' deputies |
| Dual power | Shared authority of the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet |
| Cheka | Bolshevik secret police founded in 1917 |
| War Communism | Bolshevik economic policy during the Civil War |
| NEP | New Economic Policy (1921), allowing limited private trade |
| Kulak | A richer peasant, the target of collectivisation |
| Collective farm | Large farm run jointly, delivering quotas to the state |
| Gulag | System of Soviet labour camps |
| Show trial | Public trial with a fixed verdict, used for propaganda |
| Socialist realism | Art showing Soviet life positively and heroically |

## Exam traps

- **Two revolutions in 1917.** March removed the Tsar; November brought the Bolsheviks to power.
- **The Provisional Government was not Bolshevik.** It was led by liberals, then Kerensky.
- **NEP was a retreat**, not a step towards communism; Lenin called it temporary.
- **Stalin was not Lenin's chosen successor.** The Testament warned against him.
- **Date range.** Depth Study C ends in 1941, before the German invasion.

## Self-test

1. What happened on Bloody Sunday? *Troops fired on a peaceful march to the Winter Palace in January 1905.*
2. How did the October Manifesto help the Tsar survive? *It split the liberals from the socialists by promising a Duma.*
3. What were Stolypin's two approaches? *Repression of opponents and land reform for peasants.*
4. Why was the Tsar's decision of September 1915 so damaging? *As commander-in-chief he was blamed for every defeat.*
5. What was Order No. 1? *The Soviet's instruction that soldiers obey it before the Provisional Government.*
6. What slogan summed up the April Theses? *"Peace, bread, land" and "all power to the Soviets".*
7. How did the Kornilov affair help the Bolsheviks? *Red Guards were armed to defend Petrograd and the Bolsheviks gained credit.*
8. Why did Lenin close the Constituent Assembly? *The Socialist Revolutionaries, not the Bolsheviks, had won most seats.*
9. Who built the Red Army? *Trotsky.*
10. What event in 1921 persuaded Lenin to introduce the NEP? *The Kronstadt Rising.*
11. Whose murder in 1934 began the Purges? *Sergei Kirov's.*
12. Why did livestock numbers fall during collectivisation? *Peasants slaughtered animals rather than hand them to collective farms.*
