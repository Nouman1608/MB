These questions cover section 4.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: cumulative frequency curves and polygons for continuous data. Revise with the [cumulative frequency graph study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-cumulative-frequency-graphical-form/) or the [cumulative frequency graph revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-cumulative-frequency-graphical-form-revision-notes/). Where a graph is needed, the answers use readings from the straight-line polygon; a curve would give slightly different estimates. All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** The heights of 50 plants, measured to the nearest centimetre, are grouped as follows.

| Height (cm) | 150–159 | 160–169 | 170–179 | 180–189 | 190–199 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 5 | 12 | 18 | 9 | 6 |

List the coordinates you would plot for a cumulative frequency polygon. **[2]**

**2.** Using your points from question 1, estimate the number of plants shorter than 175 cm. **[2]**

**3.** Using your points from question 1, estimate the height exceeded by the tallest 10 plants. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why a cumulative frequency polygon should not be drawn for the number of goals scored in matches. **[1]**

**5.** A cumulative frequency polygon passes through (0, 0), (5, 4), (10, 22), (15, 31), (20, 34) and (25, 35). Between which two values is the graph steepest, and what does this show? **[2]**

**6.** The first class boundary of a distribution is 140 cm. Explain how the horizontal axis can be drawn so that the graph fills the page. **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** The lifetimes, t hours, of 200 batteries are recorded.

| Lifetime, t hours | 0 < t ≤ 10 | 10 < t ≤ 20 | 20 < t ≤ 30 | 30 < t ≤ 40 | 40 < t ≤ 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 38 | 70 | 52 | 28 |

**(a)** Write down the coordinates for a cumulative frequency polygon. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate the number of batteries lasting more than 35 hours. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the percentage of batteries lasting more than 15 hours but not more than 45 hours. **[3]**

**(d)** Find the time by which 25% of the batteries had failed. **[1]**

**8.** Times, in seconds, for 40 boys and 60 girls to run 100 m are summarised by cumulative frequencies.

| Time (s) ≤ | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 | 18 | 20 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boys | 0 | 2 | 10 | 26 | 36 | 40 |
| Girls | 0 | 3 | 9 | 27 | 48 | 60 |

**(a)** Convert both rows to cumulative percentages. **[2]**

**(b)** Using cumulative percentage polygons, estimate the percentage of each group that ran 100 m in 15 seconds or less. **[2]**

**(c)** Which group was faster? Give a reason based on your graphs. **[1]**

**9.** Three cumulative percentage polygons, each for 100 values between 0 and 50, pass through these points at 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50.

- Graph A: 0, 40, 70, 85, 95, 100
- Graph B: 0, 5, 30, 70, 95, 100
- Graph C: 0, 20, 40, 60, 80, 100

Match each graph to a description, giving a reason: values spread evenly; values concentrated near the middle; most values small with a tail of larger values. **[3]**

**10.** A pupil draws a cumulative frequency curve for the masses of 60 parcels in classes 0 ≤ m < 2, 2 ≤ m < 4, 4 ≤ m < 6 and 6 ≤ m < 10 kg, with frequencies 9, 21, 18 and 12. The pupil plots (1, 9), (3, 30), (5, 48) and (8, 60), and draws the vertical axis with labels 0, 10, 20, 40, 60 equally spaced.

**(a)** Identify three errors. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the correct points to plot. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why readings taken from a cumulative frequency curve and from a cumulative frequency polygon for the same data may differ, and state which (if either) is exact. **[2]**

**12.** A cumulative frequency polygon for the waiting times of 160 patients passes through (0, 0), (10, 24), (20, 72), (30, 120), (40, 148) and (60, 160).

**(a)** Estimate the time within which 80% of patients were seen. **[2]**

**(b)** Estimate the number of patients who waited between 15 and 35 minutes. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe what the graph shows about waiting times between 40 and 60 minutes. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Upper boundaries 159.5, 169.5, 179.5, 189.5, 199.5 with cumulative frequencies 5, 17, 35, 44, 50 [1], plus the starting point (149.5, 0) [1]. **[2]**

**2.** 175 lies 5.5/10 of the way from 169.5 to 179.5 [1]: 17 + 0.55 × 18 = 26.9, so about 27 plants [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Read at 50 − 10 = 40 [1]. Between (179.5, 35) and (189.5, 44): 179.5 + (5/9) × 10 ≈ 185 cm [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Goals are discrete, so no values lie between whole numbers, but joining the points suggests that they do [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Between 5 and 10, where the cumulative frequency rises by 18 [1]; most values are concentrated in that interval [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Start the scale at 140 (or a convenient value below it) with a broken axis, keeping the scale linear after the break [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** (0, 0), (10, 12), (20, 50) [1], (30, 120), (40, 172), (60, 200) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** At 35: 120 + 0.5 × 52 = 146 [1]; more than 35: 200 − 146 = 54 batteries [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** At 15: 12 + 0.5 × 38 = 31 [1]. At 45: 172 + (5/20) × 28 = 179 [1]. Between: 148, which is 148/200 × 100 = 74% [1]. **[3]**

**(d)** 25% of 200 = 50, which is the plotted point at t = 20, so 20 hours [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** Boys: 0, 5, 25, 65, 90, 100 [1]. Girls: 0, 5, 15, 45, 80, 100 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Boys: halfway between 25 and 65 gives 45% [1]; girls: halfway between 15 and 45 gives 30% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The boys, because a higher percentage of boys ran in 15 s or less (45% against 30%); the boys' polygon is above and to the left of the girls' [1]. **[1]**

**9.** Graph A: most values small with a tail of larger values, because it rises steeply first (40% in the first 10) and then levels off [1]. Graph B: concentrated near the middle, because it is steepest between 20 and 30 (an S shape) [1]. Graph C: spread evenly, because it rises by 20% in every interval (a straight line) [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Any three, for [1] each: points plotted at midpoints instead of upper boundaries; no starting point at (0, 0); the vertical scale is not linear (the gap from 20 to 40 is the same as from 0 to 10); the last class runs to 10, not 8. **[3]**

**(b)** (0, 0), (2, 9), (4, 30), (6, 48), (10, 60) [1]. **[1]**

**11.** The polygon assumes values are spread evenly within each class, while the curve assumes a smooth change, so between plotted points they give different estimates [1]. Neither is exact; both are estimates, because the original values are unknown (only the plotted points are exact) [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** 80% of 160 = 128 [1]; between (30, 120) and (40, 148): 30 + (8/28) × 10 ≈ 32.9 minutes [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** At 15: 24 + 0.5 × 48 = 48; at 35: 120 + 0.5 × 28 = 134 [1]; between: 134 − 48 = 86 patients [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The graph is nearly flat: only 12 patients waited between 40 and 60 minutes, so long waits were uncommon [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting at midpoints, or omitting the starting point.
- Not subtracting from n for "more than" or "top k" questions.
- Using frequencies rather than percentages to compare groups of different size.
- Describing shape without linking steepness to concentration of values.
- Quoting estimates as exact.

## Frequently asked questions

**How accurate must my graph readings be?**
As accurate as the scale allows. Graph readings are estimates, so show ruled guide lines to make your method clear.

**Do I need to draw the graph if I can interpolate?**
If the question says "use your graph", draw it and read from it with guide lines.
