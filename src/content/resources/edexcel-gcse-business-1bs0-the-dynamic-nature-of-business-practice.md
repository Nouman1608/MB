## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a reason given in the specification for new business ideas coming about? **[1]**

A: Lower interest rates

B: Products and services becoming obsolete

C: A rise in the number of competitors

D: Government grants for start-ups

**2.** Which **one** of the following is an example of adapting an existing service? **[1]**

A: Inventing a product that has never existed

B: Closing a branch that makes a loss

C: A hairdresser offering appointments in customers' homes

D: Raising prices to cover higher costs

**3.** Define the term obsolete. **[1]**

**4.** Give **two** ways a business could adapt an existing product or service. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a change in what consumers want can lead to a new business idea. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** disadvantage to an entrepreneur of starting a business based on an original idea. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why a change in technology can be a threat to an existing small business. **[3]**

### Section B: case-study questions

**8.** *Pinfold Prints* has run a high-street shop printing photographs from memory cards since 2009. Most customers now keep photos on their phones and print very few. The owner, Imogen, has noticed that customers often ask whether she can turn phone photos into calendars, mugs and cushions.

**(a)** State the reason, from the specification, why demand for Pinfold Prints' original service has fallen. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline **one** way Imogen could adapt her business. **[2]**

**(c)** Analyse the impact on Pinfold Prints of products and services becoming obsolete. **[6]**

**9.** *Harrow Lane Bikes* repairs ordinary bicycles. Sales of electric bicycles in its town have grown each year, and two customers have asked whether it can service electric motors. Training and tools would cost £2,400.

Outline **one** reason why the growth of electric bicycles is an opportunity for Harrow Lane Bikes. **[2]**

**10.** *Corriemuir Kitchen* is a café in a market town. Its owner, Ruaridh, notices that more customers ask for plant-based dishes, and a regular customer says she now drives to the next town because Corriemuir has none on its menu.

Outline **one** risk to Corriemuir Kitchen if it does not respond to this change. **[2]**

**11.** *Brockwell Tutors* gives face-to-face maths tuition in students' homes. Its owner, Darius, is considering two options.

Option 1: adapt the service so that lessons are given by video call, allowing him to teach students across the country.

Option 2: develop an original idea, a maths practice app that sets questions and marks them automatically, which would take a year to build and use £9,000 of his savings.

Justify which option Darius should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B: Products and services becoming obsolete [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The other options may affect a business, but only obsolescence is one of the three triggers listed in 1.1.1.

**2.** C: A hairdresser offering appointments in customers' homes [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The service already exists; changing where it is delivered is an adaptation.

**3.** Out of date, so that few people still want it, usually because something newer has replaced it [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Any two from: change the design or features of the product [1]; aim it at a different group of customers [1]; change where or how it is delivered, such as online or at home; change how customers pay, such as renting or subscribing. **[2]**

**5.** Customers' wants change over time, for example towards healthier food [1]. Existing businesses may not yet supply what customers now want, so there is a gap in the market [1]. An entrepreneur who spots the gap early can set up a business to fill it and win those customers [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Nobody has sold the product before, so the entrepreneur cannot be sure that customers will want it [1]. If demand turns out to be low, sales may not cover costs [1], so the entrepreneur could lose the money invested in developing the product [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An alternative chain is that customers do not understand a new product, so the business must spend heavily on promotion to explain it, which raises costs.

**7.** New technology can allow rivals to offer a better or cheaper product [1]. Customers may switch to the rival, so the existing business's sales fall [1]. If it cannot afford to adopt the new technology itself, it may become unable to cover its costs and close [1]. **[3]**

**8. (a)** Products and services becoming obsolete (printing from memory cards is out of date) [1].

**(b)** Imogen could adapt her service to print phone photos onto calendars, mugs and cushions [1], which customers are already asking for, so she could replace lost printing sales using her existing printing skills and shop [1].

**(c)** Printing photos from memory cards has become obsolete because customers now keep photos on their phones [1], so demand for Pinfold Prints' original service has fallen [1]. Lower sales mean less revenue to cover the shop's fixed costs such as rent [1], so if nothing changes the business could make a loss and eventually close [1]. However, the need to keep and display photos still exists, as shown by customers asking for calendars and mugs [1]. If Imogen adapts to meet these requests, obsolescence becomes an opportunity, because she already has a shop and customers who trust her [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Analyse answers that show both the threat and the opportunity, each tied to Pinfold's own customers and shop, reach the top of the range more easily than general comments.

**9.** The number of electric bicycles in the town is growing, so more owners will need servicing [1]. Harrow Lane already has repair skills and customers, so £2,400 of training and tools would let it win these new repair jobs before rivals do [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Customers who want plant-based dishes may go to cafés in other towns, as one regular already does [1], so Corriemuir would lose sales and possibly the loyalty of existing customers [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Option 1 adapts a service Darius already provides, so demand is proven: he already has students who pay for maths tuition [1]. Teaching by video call widens his market from one town to the whole country [1], which could raise the number of students and his revenue [1]. It needs little money, mainly a computer and a camera, so the financial risk is low [1].

Option 2 is an original idea, so if it works he could be first to market with a product that sells to many users at once [1]. However, nobody can be sure students will pay for it [1], it takes a year before any revenue arrives, and £9,000 of his savings would be at risk if it fails [1].

Judgement: Darius should choose Option 1, because it builds on his existing customers and skills and puts far less money at risk [1]. Option 2 would only be better if market research showed strong demand for the app and he could afford to lose the £9,000 [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Justify asks for a recommendation of one of the two options using the information given. Comparing the risk of each, with the £9,000 and the year's delay quoted, gives the judgement its support.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a trigger that is not in the specification, such as interest rates.
- Explaining obsolescence without saying what replaced the old product.
- Treating an adaptation as an original idea.
- Describing a change in consumers' wants with no consequence for the business.
- In Justify answers, recommending an option without comparing it with the other.
