These notes cover section 3.1.1.2, Tectonic hazards, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3. Key ideas: "Earthquakes and volcanic eruptions are the result of physical processes"; "The effects of, and responses to, earthquakes and volcanoes vary between areas of contrasting levels of wealth"; "Management can reduce the effects of a tectonic hazard." For full explanations, read the [tectonic hazards study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-tectonic-hazards/). For the whole unit, see the [Living with the Physical Environment study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-geography-living-with-the-physical-environment/).

## Specification checklist

- **Plate tectonics theory** and the **global distribution** of earthquakes and volcanoes.
- **Processes** at constructive, destructive and conservative margins.
- **Primary and secondary effects**; **immediate and long-term responses**.
- **Two named examples** from areas of contrasting wealth.
- **Why people live** in risky areas.
- **Monitoring, prediction, protection and planning.**

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lithosphere | The rigid outer layer of the Earth, broken into plates |
| Plate margin | Boundary between plates |
| Focus | Underground starting point of an earthquake |
| Epicentre | Point on the surface above the focus |
| Subduction | A denser plate sinking beneath another |
| Composite volcano | Steep cone built of layers of ash and lava; explosive |
| Shield volcano | Wide, gentle cone built of runny lava |
| Liquefaction | Shaking turns wet soil to liquid |
| Lahar | Volcanic mudflow |
| Pyroclastic flow | Fast, hot cloud of gas, ash and rock |

## Distribution in brief

- Earthquakes and volcanoes form **belts**, not random scatter, along plate margins.
- **Ring of Fire** around the Pacific: Andes, Japan, Philippines, New Zealand, west coast of North America.
- **Mid-Atlantic Ridge** and Iceland; the Mediterranean to the Himalayas.
- Describe with: where, pattern, named places.

## The three margins

| | Constructive | Destructive | Conservative |
|---|---|---|---|
| Movement | Apart | Together; denser plate subducts | Slide past |
| Earthquakes | Small, shallow | Powerful, shallow to deep | Can be strong, shallow |
| Volcanoes | Gentle shield volcanoes, runny lava | Explosive composite volcanoes | None |
| Landform | Ridge, rift valley | Trench, fold mountains | Fault line |
| Example | Mid-Atlantic Ridge, Iceland | Nazca and South American plates, Japan | San Andreas Fault |

Why earthquakes happen: plates stick, **pressure builds**, then they **jerk free**, sending out shock waves from the focus.

## Effects

| | Primary | Secondary |
|---|---|---|
| Earthquake | Shaking, collapse of buildings, deaths, injuries | Fires, landslides, liquefaction, tsunami, disease, homelessness, job losses |
| Volcano | Lava, ash fall, pyroclastic flows, gases | Lahars, crop loss, grounded flights, loss of income |

## Responses

- **Immediate (hours to days):** search and rescue, first aid, shelter, food, water, evacuation.
- **Long-term (months to years):** rebuilding, restoring jobs and services, better building codes, reducing future risk.

## Two contrasting examples (check against your own notes)

| | Haiti 2010 (low-income) | Christchurch 2011 (high-income) |
|---|---|---|
| Magnitude, location | 7.0, near the capital, Port-au-Prince | 6.3, a few km from the city centre |
| Deaths | Estimated 100 000 to over 300 000 | 185 |
| Buildings | Weak, little reinforcement | Stricter codes; older brick buildings failed |
| Secondary effects | Camps, cholera from October 2010 | Liquefaction, rock falls |
| Immediate response | Aid delayed by damaged port and roads | Fast national and international rescue |
| Long-term | Slow; reliant on aid | Insurance, recovery agency, new rules |

The hazard was similar in scale, so human factors explain the difference: building quality, services, insurance and government resources.

## Why people stay in hazard zones

- Fertile volcanic soils.
- Geothermal energy, minerals, tourism.
- Jobs, family and community ties.
- Poverty: cannot afford to move.
- Low perceived risk; trust in prediction and protection.

## Managing tectonic hazards

| Approach | Earthquakes | Volcanoes |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring | Seismometers | Gas emissions, tiltmeters, small earthquakes, satellite data |
| Prediction | Cannot be predicted; long-term probabilities only | Often forecast days to weeks ahead |
| Protection | Steel frames, cross-bracing, base isolators, shock absorbers, automatic gas shut-off | Barriers or channels to divert lava; exclusion zones |
| Planning | Hazard maps, land-use zoning, drills, evacuation routes, emergency kits | Hazard maps, evacuation plans, education |

## Worked exam answer

**Question:** Explain how earthquakes occur at a conservative plate margin. [4 marks]

**Model answer:** "At a conservative margin two plates slide past each other, either in different directions or at different speeds. The plates do not move smoothly because friction makes them stick. Pressure builds up along the fault as the plates keep trying to move. When the pressure becomes greater than the friction, the plates suddenly jerk free. The energy is released from the focus as shock waves, which shake the ground at the surface."

How it scores: one mark each for movement, sticking, build-up of pressure and sudden release as shock waves.

## Quick recall: five ideas to remember

- No volcanoes at conservative margins; earthquakes can be strong and shallow.
- Destructive margins: subduction, powerful earthquakes, explosive composite volcanoes.
- Constructive margins: new crust, gentle shield volcanoes, small earthquakes.
- Wealth shapes effects and responses, but depth, distance and building quality matter too.
- Monitor, predict, protect, plan: volcanoes can often be forecast; earthquakes cannot.

## Exam traps

- Volcanoes do not occur at conservative margins.
- Focus is below ground; epicentre is on the surface.
- Effects (what the hazard did) are not responses (what people did).
- Earthquakes cannot be predicted; volcanoes can often be forecast.
- Name the examples, give figures or details, and link to wealth.
- Wealth explains differences in deaths and recovery, but depth, distance and time of day also matter.
- Do not write about all three margins if the question asks about one.

## Self-test

1. What causes an earthquake at a conservative margin?
2. Why are volcanoes at destructive margins more explosive than at constructive margins?
3. Distinguish between focus and epicentre.
4. Give **two** primary and **two** secondary effects of an earthquake.
5. What is liquefaction?
6. Name **two** immediate and **two** long-term responses.
7. Why did the same size of earthquake kill so many more people in Haiti than in Christchurch?
8. Give **three** reasons why people continue to live near volcanoes.
9. Why can volcanoes be predicted better than earthquakes?
10. Describe **two** ways to make a building earthquake-resistant.

**Answers**

1. Plates slide past each other but stick; pressure builds and is suddenly released.
2. The magma at destructive margins comes from melted subducted crust, is thick and sticky and holds gas, so it erupts violently; at constructive margins the lava is runny and gas escapes easily.
3. Focus: the point underground where the earthquake starts. Epicentre: the point on the surface directly above it.
4. Primary: shaking; building collapse; deaths. Secondary: fires; landslides; tsunami; disease.
5. Shaking turns waterlogged soil into a liquid, so buildings sink or tilt.
6. Immediate: search and rescue; first aid; emergency shelter. Long-term: rebuilding; restoring jobs; improved building codes.
7. Haiti had weaker buildings, higher density and poverty, fewer services and little insurance; Christchurch had strict building codes, strong emergency services and resources.
8. Any three: fertile soils; geothermal energy; tourism; jobs; family ties; cannot afford to move; low perceived risk.
9. Volcanoes give warning signs such as gas, swelling and small earthquakes; earthquakes show no reliable warning signs.
10. Any two: steel frame; cross-bracing; base isolation; shock absorbers; lighter roof.
