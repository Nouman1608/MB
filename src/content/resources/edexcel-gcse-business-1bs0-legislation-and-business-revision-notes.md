These notes condense content item 1.5.3, Legislation and business, in Topic 1.5 (Understanding external influences on business) of the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. The [legislation study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-legislation-and-business/) has worked examples on refunds and wage costs; test yourself with the [legislation practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-legislation-and-business-practice/). The wider [legislation, economy and external influences revision notes](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-legislation-the-economy-and-external-influences-revision-notes/) cover 1.5.4 and 1.5.5 as well.

The specification names principles, not particular Acts, so learn the principles and describe the law in general terms.

## What the specification lists

**The purpose of legislation:**

- principles of consumer law: quality and consumer rights
- principles of employment law: recruitment, pay, discrimination and health and safety.

**The impact of legislation on businesses:**

- cost
- consequences of meeting and not meeting these obligations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Legislation | Laws made by government |
| Obligation | Something a business is legally required to do |
| Consumer law | Laws protecting customers who buy goods and services |
| Quality (consumer law) | Goods of reasonable standard, fit for purpose and as described; services with reasonable care and skill |
| Consumer rights | Customers' entitlement to a remedy, such as repair, replacement or refund, when goods or services fall short |
| Employment law | Laws protecting people at work |
| Discrimination | Treating someone less favourably because of a characteristic such as sex, race, age, disability or religion |
| Health and safety | Protecting people at work from injury and ill health |
| Compliance | Meeting legal obligations |

## Purpose: who is protected, and why

- **Customers** know less about products than sellers → consumer law.
- **Employees** depend on employers → employment law.
- **Honest businesses** should not be undercut by rivals who cut corners → a minimum standard for all.

## What each principle means for a business

| Principle | Business action |
|---|---|
| Quality | Choose reliable suppliers; check goods; describe products accurately |
| Consumer rights | Returns and complaints process; budget for refunds; train staff |
| Recruitment | Fair adverts and selection based on ability; keep records |
| Pay | At least the legal minimum; accurate and on time |
| Discrimination | Equal treatment in hiring, pay, training and promotion |
| Health and safety | Risk assessments; safe equipment; training; protective clothing |

## Impact: cost

- One-off: equipment, premises changes, new procedures.
- Ongoing: wages, training, inspections, record keeping, management time.
- Heavier for small firms: the same task, less revenue to spread it over.

## Consequences

**Meeting obligations:** fewer accidents and complaints; good reputation; easier recruitment and retention; customer trust.

**Not meeting obligations:** fines and penalties; compensation; bad publicity and lost sales; staff leave; possibly ordered to stop an activity.

## Responses to higher compliance costs

Accept lower profit; raise prices; cut costs elsewhere; improve efficiency (for example, better equipment that reduces accidents).

## Calculations

- Wage bill = number of staff × hours × hourly rate.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- Refund cost = number of returns × price.
- Compliance cost as a percentage of revenue = cost ÷ revenue × 100.

Example: 5 staff × 30 hours × 50 weeks = 7,500 hours. A 40p rise in the hourly rate adds 7,500 × £0.40 = £3,000 a year.

## Exam traps

- Do not invent the names or dates of laws.
- Consumer law protects customers; employment law protects workers.
- Consequences include reputation, recruitment and sales, not just fines.
- Legislation brings benefits as well as costs.
- Use the context: staff numbers, products, revenue.

## Chains to learn

- Minimum wage rises → wage bill rises → profit falls unless prices rise or productivity improves.
- Quality standard not met → refunds and complaints → reputation damaged → sales fall.
- Discrimination in recruitment → legal claim and compensation → bad publicity → harder to recruit.
- Safety training provided → fewer accidents → fewer days lost → output and staff morale protected.

## Self-test

1. Define legislation.
2. Name the two principles of consumer law in the specification.
3. Name the four principles of employment law in the specification.
4. Give one thing a business must do to meet the quality principle.
5. Give two remedies a customer may be entitled to when goods are faulty.
6. Give one action a business can take to meet health and safety obligations.
7. Give one consequence of not meeting consumer law obligations.
8. Give one benefit of meeting employment law obligations.
9. Why do compliance costs weigh more heavily on small businesses?
10. A shop sells 600 kettles at £30; 5% are returned for a refund. Calculate the cost of refunds.
11. 4 staff work 20 hours a week for 50 weeks. The hourly rate rises by £0.50. Calculate the yearly increase in the wage bill.
12. Give two ways a business might respond to a rise in the legal minimum wage.

### Answers

1. Laws made by government.
2. Quality and consumer rights.
3. Recruitment, pay, discrimination, health and safety.
4. Any one: sell goods fit for purpose; describe products accurately; check goods before sale.
5. Any two: repair; replacement; refund.
6. Any one: carry out risk assessments; provide safe equipment; train staff; provide protective clothing.
7. Any one: compensation or refunds; fines; bad publicity and lost sales.
8. Any one: easier recruitment; staff stay longer; good reputation; fewer accidents or disputes.
9. Many tasks take the same time and cost whatever the size of the firm, but a small firm has less revenue to cover them.
10. 5% × 600 = 30 returns; 30 × £30 = £900.
11. 4 × 20 × 50 = 4,000 hours; 4,000 × £0.50 = £2,000.
12. Any two: raise prices; accept lower profit; reduce staff hours; find savings elsewhere.
