These questions cover "Explanation of dispositional factors affecting obedience. Adorno's theory of
the Authoritarian Personality" in **3.2.2 Social influence** from Paper 2 of OxfordAQA International
GCSE Psychology (9218).

---

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a trait of the authoritarian personality?
A Tolerance of uncertainty
B Exaggerated respect for authority
C Rejection of traditional values
D Sympathy for people who break rules **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by a dispositional explanation of obedience. **[1]**

**3.** Outline the type of upbringing Adorno linked to the authoritarian personality. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what the F-scale was designed to measure. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **one** strength and **one** limitation of using the F-scale to measure authoritarianism.
**[2]**

**6.** Marcus always follows his manager's instructions without question and is harsh with junior staff
who make mistakes. His parents were very strict and punished him severely. Use Adorno's theory to
explain Marcus's behaviour. **[3]**

## Section B — Longer questions

**7.** Describe Adorno's theory of the Authoritarian Personality. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how displacement and identification are used in Adorno's explanation. **[4]**

**9.** Outline Elms and Milgram's research and explain how it supports Adorno's theory. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why the Authoritarian Personality cannot fully explain obedience in Milgram's research.
**[3]**

**11.** Outline **one** dispositional factor affecting obedience other than the authoritarian
personality. **[2]**

**12.** Evaluate Adorno's theory of the Authoritarian Personality as an explanation of obedience.
**[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** An explanation that says people obey because of their own characteristics, such as their
personality, rather than features of the situation [1].

**3.** Strict, harsh parents who demanded obedience and used severe discipline, including physical
punishment [1], and who gave love only when the child behaved as required (conditional love) [1].

**4.** Authoritarian, or potentially fascist, attitudes [1], such as respect for authority, rigid
conventional values and hostility towards those seen as inferior [1].

**5.** Strength, any one [1]: it gives a quantitative score that allows comparison between people;
it is quick to give to large samples. Limitation, any one [1]: all statements were worded so that
agreeing showed authoritarianism, causing acquiescence bias; social desirability; it measures only
right-wing authoritarianism.

**6.** Marcus's strict, punitive upbringing fits the cause Adorno described [1]. He may have identified
with his powerful parents, so he respects and obeys authority figures such as his manager without
question (authoritarian submission) [1]. His hidden hostility is displaced onto weaker people, such as
junior staff, which explains his harshness to them (authoritarian aggression) [1].

**7.** One mark per point, up to 4:
- Some people have a personality type that makes them especially obedient to authority [1].
- Traits include submission to authority, aggression towards those of lower status, conventional
  values and rigid, black-and-white thinking [1].
- The personality develops from a strict, harsh upbringing with conditional love [1].
- Hostility towards the parents is displaced onto weaker scapegoats such as minority groups [1].
- Such people obey because they respect authority and do not question orders [1].
Maximum 4.

**8.** Identification, up to 2: the child cannot challenge their powerful, harsh parents [1], so they
identify with them and come to admire and obey authority figures in general [1]. Displacement, up to
2: the child's hostility towards the parents cannot be expressed safely [1], so it is redirected onto
weaker targets who cannot retaliate, such as minority groups [1].

**9.** Elms and Milgram followed up participants from Milgram's obedience studies, comparing those who
had obeyed fully with those who had refused [1]. The obedient participants scored higher on the
F-scale [1], supporting the idea that authoritarian personality traits are linked to obedience [1].

**10.** One mark per point, up to 3:
- In Milgram's variations, obedience changed greatly when the situation changed, for example when the
  victim was closer or the experimenter gave orders by telephone [1].
- The kinds of people taking part did not change, so personality cannot explain the change [1].
- This suggests situational factors explain much of the obedience [1].
- The baseline obedience rate was high, so it is unlikely that most participants had authoritarian
  personalities [1].
Maximum 3.

**11.** Locus of control [1]: people with an internal locus of control believe they are responsible for
their own actions and are more likely to resist orders, whereas those with an external locus are more
likely to obey [1].

**12.** Award up to 6, for balanced evaluation:
- Support: Elms and Milgram found obedient participants scored higher on the F-scale [1].
- Strength: it explains individual differences, why some people in the same situation obey and others
  refuse [1].
- Limitation: it cannot explain obedience across a whole society, because too many people obeyed to
  share one personality type [1].
- Limitation: the F-scale was open to acquiescence bias, as every statement was worded so that
  agreement showed authoritarianism [1].
- Limitation: the research was correlational, so harsh upbringing may not cause authoritarianism; a
  third factor such as education may be involved [1].
- Limitation or judgement: the sample was white, middle-class Americans; or a conclusion that
  dispositional and situational factors both contribute [1].
Maximum 6.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing the traits without the upbringing that produces them.
- Calling the theory a social or situational explanation.
- Applying the theory to a scenario without linking each point to the person described.
- Evaluation points with no explanation of why they weaken or strengthen the theory.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know other dispositional factors?**
The specification asks for an explanation of dispositional factors, naming Adorno's theory. A second
example, such as locus of control, can strengthen an answer.

**Can I use Milgram's findings to evaluate Adorno?**
Yes. Milgram's variations are strong evidence that the situation also matters.
