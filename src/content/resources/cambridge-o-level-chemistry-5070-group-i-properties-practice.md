Revise first with the [Group I properties study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-i-properties/) and the [Group I properties revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-group-i-properties-revision-notes/).

## Section A: short questions

**1.** State **one** physical property that shows the Group I metals are "relatively soft". **[1]**

**2.** Which row correctly shows the trends down Group I from lithium to potassium?

A: melting point increases; reactivity increases

B: melting point decreases; reactivity increases

C: melting point decreases; reactivity decreases

D: melting point increases; reactivity decreases

**[1]**

**3.** Name the two products formed when lithium reacts with water. **[2]**

**4.** Write a balanced symbol equation, with state symbols, for the reaction of sodium with water. **[2]**

**5.** A few drops of universal indicator are added to the water before a piece of potassium is added. State the final colour of the indicator and explain it. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why sodium, unlike most metals, must be stored under oil. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A teacher adds small pieces of lithium, sodium and potassium, one at a time, to separate troughs of cold water.

**(a)** Give **one** observation that is the same for all three metals. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **two** observations for potassium that are not seen for lithium. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how these observations show the trend in reactivity. **[2]**

**8.** The table gives data for Group I elements.

| Element | Melting point / °C | Density / g cm⁻³ |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium | 181 | 0.53 |
| Sodium | 98 | 0.97 |
| Potassium | 63 | 0.86 |
| Rubidium | 39 | 1.53 |

**(a)** Describe the general trend in density down the group. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the element that does not fit this trend. **[1]**

**(c)** Predict whether rubidium floats or sinks when added to water. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**9.** Caesium is below rubidium in Group I.

**(a)** Predict **three** properties of caesium. For each, give a reason. **[6]**

**(b)** Write the formula of caesium hydroxide. **[1]**

**10.** Sodium melts into a ball when it reacts with water, but lithium does not.

**(a)** Use the data in Question 8 to explain this difference. **[2]**

**(b)** State what this shows about whether the reaction of sodium with water is exothermic or endothermic. **[1]**

**11.** A student writes: "Potassium reacts with water to form potassium oxide and hydrogen. The potassium sinks and a yellow flame is seen."

Identify and correct **three** errors in the student's statement. **[3]**

**12.** Explain, in terms of electronic configuration, why potassium is more reactive than sodium. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** They can be cut with a knife [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3.** Lithium hydroxide [1]; hydrogen [1].

**4.** 2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g): correct formulae and balancing [1]; correct state symbols [1].

**5.** Blue / purple [1]; potassium hydroxide forms, which is an alkali / gives OH⁻ ions in solution [1].

**6.** It reacts with oxygen / water vapour in the air [1]; oil keeps air and moisture away from the surface [1].

**7. (a)** Any one: floats; fizzes / bubbles; moves on the surface; disappears / gets smaller [1].

**(b)** Any two: lilac flame; melts into a ball; reacts much faster / more violently / moves much faster; crackles or spits [2].

**(c)** The reaction becomes more vigorous from lithium to sodium to potassium [1], so reactivity increases down the group [1].

**8. (a)** Density increases down the group [1].

**(b)** Potassium (its density is lower than sodium's) [1].

**(c)** Sinks [1], because its density (1.53 g cm⁻³) is greater than that of water (1.0 g cm⁻³) [1].

**9. (a)** Any three properties, each with a reason [3 × 2]:
- Melting point lower than 39 °C / lower than rubidium [1], because melting point decreases down the group [1].
- Density greater than 1.53 g cm⁻³ / greater than rubidium [1], because density increases down the group [1].
- Reacts more violently / explosively with water than rubidium [1], because reactivity increases down the group [1].
- Forms an alkaline solution / caesium hydroxide and hydrogen with water [1], as all Group I metals do [1].
- Forms a Cs⁺ ion [1], because it has one outer electron like all Group I elements [1].
- Soft / can be cut with a knife [1], as Group I metals are soft and softness increases down the group [1].

**(b)** CsOH [1].
*Marking note:* a prediction with no reason scores only the first mark of each pair. Reasons that name the trend ("decreases down the group") or the shared group feature ("one outer electron") are both acceptable; "because it is lower down" on its own does not say which way the property changes.

**10. (a)** Sodium's melting point (98 °C) is below 100 °C / much lower than lithium's (181 °C) [1]; the heat released by the reaction is enough to melt sodium but not lithium (lithium's slower reaction also releases heat more slowly) [1].

**(b)** Exothermic (heat is released) [1].

**11.** One mark each [3]:
- Potassium hydroxide, not potassium oxide, is formed [1].
- Potassium floats, not sinks [1].
- The flame is lilac, not yellow [1].

**12.** Potassium has more electron shells than sodium (2,8,8,1 compared with 2,8,1) [1]; its outer electron is further from the nucleus / less strongly attracted [1]; so it is lost more easily in reactions [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "oxide" instead of "hydroxide" as the product with water.
- Describing what is formed ("hydrogen") when the question asks for what is **seen**.
- Repeating the same observation in different words for two marks.
- Giving a prediction without the trend that justifies it.
- Writing "sodium is more reactive because it has more shells", reversing the comparison.
