These questions cover sub-topic 3(c), The electromagnetic spectrum
(statements 3.10 to 3.13), of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics
(4PH1). The speed of electromagnetic waves in free space is
3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

## Section A

**1.** Name the two regions of the electromagnetic spectrum on either side of visible light. **[2]**

**2.** State which region of the electromagnetic spectrum has the lowest frequency. **[1]**

**3.** Put these colours in order of increasing wavelength: green, red, violet, yellow. **[2]**

**4.** State the use of x-rays named in the specification. **[1]**

**5.** State the harmful effect of excessive exposure to microwaves. **[1]**

**6.** A student says "gamma rays travel faster than radio waves because they have more energy". Correct the student's statement. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Satellite television signals are sent using microwaves of frequency 12 GHz.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the signals, in cm. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why microwaves, rather than visible light, are used to send signals to and from a satellite. **[1]**

**8.** A hospital sterilises sealed packs of surgical instruments.

**(a)** Name the radiation used. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why this radiation is suitable for sterilising instruments that are already sealed in packaging. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe two simple measures that protect the workers. **[2]**

**9.** A microwave oven cooks food.

**(a)** Explain how microwaves heat the food. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two features of the oven that protect the user, and explain how they reduce the risk. **[3]**

**10.** A search team uses night vision cameras to find a missing walker on a hillside at night.

**(a)** Name the radiation detected by the cameras. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the walker shows up clearly against the hillside. **[2]**

**11.** Ultraviolet radiation from the Sun reaches the Earth's surface.

**(a)** Describe two harmful effects of excessive exposure to ultraviolet. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two protective measures a person could take on a sunny day. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how ultraviolet radiation is used in a fluorescent lamp. **[2]**

**12.** The table gives the wavelengths of three electromagnetic waves, A, B and C.

| Wave | Wavelength / m |
|---|---|
| A | 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ |
| B | 2.0 × 10² |
| C | 1.0 × 10⁻¹² |

**(a)** Identify which wave is radio, which is visible light and which is gamma. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency of wave A. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, using the wave equation, why wave C has the highest frequency. **[2]**

**13.** Compare the uses of x-rays and visible light in medicine. In your answer, explain the property of each radiation that makes it suitable for its use. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Infrared [1] and ultraviolet [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Radio waves [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Violet, green, yellow, red. All correct [2]; one adjacent pair swapped [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Observing the internal structure of objects and materials, including for medical applications (for example imaging bones) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Internal heating of body tissue [1]. **[1]**

**6.** All electromagnetic waves, including gamma rays and radio waves, travel at the same speed in free space (3.0 × 10⁸ m/s); they differ in frequency and wavelength [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** f = 12 × 10⁹ Hz = 1.2 × 10¹⁰ Hz [1]; λ = v ÷ f = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 1.2 × 10¹⁰ = 0.025 m [1] = 2.5 cm [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Microwaves pass through the atmosphere, including cloud, whereas visible light is blocked by cloud [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** Gamma rays [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Gamma rays penetrate the packaging [1] and kill bacteria and other microorganisms on the instruments, so they are sterilised without being unpacked or heated [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any two: the source is surrounded by lead or thick concrete shielding [1]; workers stay at a distance or operate it remotely [1]; exposure time is limited [1]; workers wear badges to monitor their dose [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** Microwaves penetrate into the food and are absorbed by water molecules [1], which gain energy, so the temperature of the food rises [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two features, with explanations, up to 3 marks: a metal case [1] reflects or absorbs microwaves so they do not escape [1]; a metal mesh in the glass door [1] stops microwaves escaping while letting the user see in [1]; a cut-out switch [1] turns the oven off when the door is opened, so the user is not exposed [1]. Maximum 3. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Infrared [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The walker's body is warmer than the hillside at night [1], so it emits more infrared, which the camera detects as a brighter image [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Damage to surface cells of the skin (sunburn, increased risk of skin cancer) [1]; damage to the eyes, which can lead to blindness [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any two: use sunscreen; wear covering clothing or a hat; wear sunglasses that block UV; limit time in strong sunlight [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Ultraviolet is produced inside the tube [1]; a coating on the inside of the glass absorbs the ultraviolet and emits visible light [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** B is radio, A is visible light, C is gamma. All three correct [2]; one correct [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** f = v ÷ λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = 6.0 × 10¹⁴ Hz [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** All three travel at the same speed, so f = v ÷ λ means frequency is inversely related to wavelength [1]; C has the shortest wavelength, so it has the highest frequency [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Indicative points, one mark each:
- x-rays are used to observe the internal structure of the body, for example bones [1]
- because x-rays pass through soft tissue but are absorbed by denser materials such as bone, so a detector records an image [1]
- visible light is used in optical fibres, for example in an endoscope to see inside the body (or in photography) [1]
- because visible light is detected by the eye or a camera and can be sent along a fibre by total internal reflection [1]
