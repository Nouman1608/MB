## Answers

**1.** An object stays at rest or moves at **constant velocity unless acted on by a resultant force** [1]. The **resultant force on an object of constant mass equals mass × acceleration**, ΣF = ma, and the acceleration is in the direction of the resultant force [1]. If A exerts a force on B, **B exerts an equal and opposite force on A**, of the same type [1].

**2.** Momentum = **mass × velocity**, p = mv [1]; it is a vector, in kg m s⁻¹ [1]. In a closed system (no external resultant force) the **total momentum before an interaction equals the total momentum after** it [1].

**3. (a)** Δp = mΔv = 1200 × (0 − 20) [1] = **−24 000 kg m s⁻¹** (magnitude 24 000) [1].
**(b)** a = Δv ÷ t = 20 ÷ 4.0 = 5.0 m s⁻² [1]; F = ma = 1200 × 5.0 = **6000 N** [1]. (Equivalently, change in momentum ÷ time = 24 000 ÷ 4.0.)
**(c)** The crumple zone **deforms, increasing the time taken to stop** [1]. The **change in velocity is fixed**, so a longer time gives a **smaller deceleration** [1], and by F = ma a **smaller force** on the occupants [1].

**4. (a)** Momentum before = 0.20 × 6.0 = 1.2 kg m s⁻¹ [1]; total mass after = 0.50 kg [1]; v = 1.2 ÷ 0.50 = **2.4 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** KE before = ½ × 0.20 × 6.0² = **3.6 J** [1]. KE after = ½ × 0.50 × 2.4² = **1.44 J** [1]. Kinetic energy has **decreased** [1], so the collision is **inelastic** — momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not [1].

**5. (a)** Horizontal = 25 cos 30° = **21.7 m s⁻¹** [1]; vertical = 25 sin 30° = **12.5 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** At the highest point the vertical velocity is zero: 0 = 12.5 − 9.81t [1], so t = 1.27 s [1]; by symmetry the total time of flight = 2 × 1.27 = **2.55 s** [1].
**(c)** Range = 21.7 × 2.55 [1] = **55 m** [1].

**6.** Work done = mgh = 300 × 9.81 × 12 [1] = 35 316 J [1]; power = 35 316 ÷ 40 = **880 W** [1].

**7. (a)** Horizontal motion is at **constant velocity** (no horizontal force) [1]: distance = 15 × 2.0 = **30 m** [1].
**(b)** Vertical motion starts from rest and accelerates at g [1]: v = u + at = 0 + 9.81 × 2.0 = **19.6 m s⁻¹** (downward) [1].

**8.** efficiency = useful power output ÷ total power input, so useful output = 0.75 × 800 [1] = **600 W** [1].

**9.** Initially weight exceeds air resistance, so there is a resultant force and the diver accelerates [1]; as speed increases air resistance grows until it **equals** weight, giving **zero resultant force**, so by Newton's first law the diver continues at **constant velocity** [1].

**10. Mass** is the amount of matter in an object, measured in **kg**, and is a **scalar** [1]. **Weight** is the force of gravity acting on that mass (W = mg), measured in **N**, and is a **vector** [1].

**11. (a)** v² = u² + 2as: 16² = 4.0² + 2 × a × 300 [1], so a = (256 − 16) ÷ 600 = **0.40 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** v = u + at: t = (16 − 4.0) ÷ 0.40 [1] = **30 s** [1].
**(c)** Area under a velocity–time graph = **displacement** [1]; gradient of a displacement–time graph = **velocity** [1].

**12. (a)** W = mg = 5.0 × 9.81 = 49.05 N; component down the slope = 49.05 sin 25° [1] = **20.7 N** [1].
**(b)** Normal contact force = component perpendicular to the slope = 49.05 cos 25° [1] = **44.5 N** [1].
**(c)** **20.7 N up the slope** [1]: the box is at rest, so by Newton's first law the resultant force is zero and friction balances the weight component down the slope [1].

**13. (a)** The single point through which the whole weight of the plank can be taken to act [1].
**(b)** Take moments about the left-hand support. The plank's weight acts at its centre, 2.0 m from the left. Clockwise moments = 600 × 1.0 + 150 × 2.0 = 900 N m [1]; anticlockwise moment = R × 4.0 [1]; R = 900 ÷ 4.0 = **225 N** [1].
**(c)** Upward forces = downward forces: 750 − 225 = **525 N** [1].

**14.** ΔW = FΔs cos θ = 60 × 40 × cos 30° [1] = **2080 J** (2.1 kJ) [1]. Only the component of the force along the motion does work.

**15. (a)** Loss of gravitational potential energy = gain in kinetic energy: mgΔh = ½mv² [1]; v = √(2gΔh) = √(2 × 9.81 × 1.8) [1] = **5.9 m s⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** ΔEgrav = 0.50 × 9.81 × 1.8 = 8.83 J; Ek = ½ × 0.50 × 5.0² = 6.25 J [1]; work done against resistive forces = 8.83 − 6.25 = **2.6 J** [1].

**16.** From rest, h = ½gt² [1], so a graph of h against t² is a straight line through the origin [1] with gradient ½g, so **g = 2 × gradient** [1]. (A graph of 2h against t² has gradient g.)

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting momentum is a vector — take direction into account.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved in all collisions.
- Using the full launch speed for horizontal range instead of the horizontal component.
- Confusing work done with power.
- Using the launch/thrown speed for both horizontal and vertical motion in a projectile — the two components are independent.
- Applying total power input where useful (output) power is asked for, or vice versa, in an efficiency calculation.
- Confusing mass (a scalar, in kg) with weight (a vector force, in N).
- Taking moments about a point and forgetting the weight of the beam itself, which acts at its centre of gravity.
- Using F × s for work when the force is at an angle; the work is FΔs cos θ.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is impulse in Edexcel IAL Physics Unit 1?**
No. Unit 1 topic 1.3 defines momentum as p = mv and uses conservation of linear momentum in one dimension. The equation impulse = FΔt = Δp is in Unit 4 topic 4.3 Further Mechanics.

**Which value of g should I use?**
Use g = 9.81 m s⁻² (or N kg⁻¹), the value given in the Edexcel IAL data list, unless the question gives another value.

**How do I choose a pivot in a moments question?**
Take moments about the point where an unknown force acts, so that force has zero moment and drops out. Then use "upward forces = downward forces" to find the other unknown.

**Do I need to know the core practicals?**
Yes. Core practical 1 (determining g by free fall) belongs to this topic, and Unit 3 can ask you to plan, process or evaluate it.
