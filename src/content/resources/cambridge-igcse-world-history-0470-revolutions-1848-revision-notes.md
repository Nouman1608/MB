**Key question 1.1, Cambridge IGCSE History 0470:** "Were the revolutions of 1848 important?" You need the causes,
events and results of the revolutions in **France, Italy, Germany and the Austrian Empire**, the influence of
liberalism and nationalism, and the reasons for failure. Full explanations are in the
[1848 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-revolutions-1848/); test yourself with the
[1848 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-world-history-0470-revolutions-1848-practice/).

## Long-term and short-term causes

| Long-term | Short-term |
|---|---|
| Liberal ideas: constitutions, parliaments, a free press | Potato blight (1845) and poor harvests (1846) |
| Nationalism among Germans, Italians, Hungarians, Czechs | Commercial slump (1847): unemployment, high bread prices |
| Repression under Metternich: Carlsbad Decrees (1819) | Example of Paris, February 1848, spread by newspapers and railways |
| Growing, excluded middle class; threatened artisans | Weak or hesitant rulers who gave way at first |

## Why most revolutions failed

- **Armies stayed loyal to rulers**: Radetzky (Italy), Windischgrätz (Prague, Vienna), the Prussian army (Berlin).
- **Liberals versus radicals**: the June Days made the middle classes side with order against the workers.
- **Nationalities divided**: Croats fought Hungarians; Czechs rejected a German national state.
- **Peasants satisfied** once labour services ended in 1848.
- **Foreign intervention**: Russia in Hungary; France in Rome.
- **Frankfurt's weakness**: no army, no tax income, long debates, dependence on Prussia over Schleswig-Holstein.

## What changed

| Lasting change | Limit |
|---|---|
| Serfdom abolished in the Austrian Empire | Absolute rule restored by Franz Joseph |
| Universal male suffrage in France | Used by Napoleon III to approve his own power |
| Piedmont's Statuto survives | The only Italian constitution to survive |
| Prussia keeps a parliament | Elected on an unequal three-class system |
| Nationalism now a mass issue | Unification would need war and diplomacy |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liberalism | Belief in constitutions, elected parliaments and individual rights |
| Nationalism | Belief that a nation sharing language and culture should govern itself |
| July Monarchy | Louis-Philippe's regime in France, 1830-48, with a very narrow franchise |
| National Workshops | French scheme of public works for the unemployed, closed in June 1848 |
| June Days | Rising of Paris workers in June 1848, crushed by the army |
| Statuto | Piedmont's constitution, granted by Charles Albert in 1848 |
| Frankfurt Parliament | Elected German national assembly, 1848-49 |
| Grossdeutsch / Kleindeutsch | A "greater Germany" including Austria, or a "smaller Germany" led by Prussia |
| April Laws | Reforms of 1848 giving Hungary its own government |
| Robot | Unpaid labour peasants owed to landlords in the Austrian Empire |

## Exam traps

- **Writing one generic story.** Examiners want named, country-specific examples.
- **Saying nothing changed.** Weigh lasting changes against the restoration of the old rulers.
- **Mixing up Napoleons.** Louis Napoleon (Napoleon III) is the nephew of Napoleon I.
- **Confusing Custoza.** Piedmont lost at Custoza in 1848; Italy lost a second battle there in 1866.
- **Forgetting peasants.** Their withdrawal after the end of labour services is a reason for failure in Austria.
- **Ignoring the question's date range.** A question on "1848" may expect events of 1849, when most revolutions were
  defeated.

## One-line answers to the focus points

- **Why had liberalism and nationalism grown?** French revolutionary ideas, a larger educated middle class, and
  resentment of repression after 1815.
- **Why so many revolutions?** Economic crisis made ordinary people ready to act, and the fall of Louis-Philippe
  showed that thrones could fall.
- **Anything in common?** Liberal and national demands, urban leadership and quick early success.
- **Why did most fail?** Loyal armies, divisions among revolutionaries and between nationalities, and foreign help
  for rulers.
- **Did they change anything?** Some lasting reforms, and the lesson that unity would need force.

## Self-test

1. Where did the first revolution of 1848 break out? *Palermo, Sicily, in January 1848.*
2. What event in February 1848 triggered revolution in Paris? *The government's ban on a reform banquet.*
3. Why did the June Days happen? *The assembly closed the National Workshops, leaving Paris workers without income.*
4. Who won the French presidential election of December 1848? *Louis Napoleon Bonaparte.*
5. What happened in Milan in March 1848? *The Five Days: citizens forced Radetzky's Austrian army out of the city.*
6. Name the two battles in which Piedmont was defeated in 1848-49. *Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849).*
7. Who defended the Roman Republic, and who destroyed it? *Garibaldi defended it; French troops sent by Louis Napoleon
   took Rome.*
8. Why did the Frankfurt Parliament rely on Prussia? *It had no army, so it needed Prussian troops, for example in
   the war with Denmark over Schleswig-Holstein.*
9. What did Frederick William IV do in April 1849? *He refused the crown of a united Germany offered by Frankfurt.*
10. Which Austrian general retook Vienna in October 1848? *Windischgrätz.*
11. Which foreign power helped crush Hungary in 1849? *Russia, under Tsar Nicholas I.*
12. Give one lasting change from 1848 in the Austrian Empire. *The abolition of labour services and serfdom.*
