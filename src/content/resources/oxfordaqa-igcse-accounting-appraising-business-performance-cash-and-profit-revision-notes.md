## The nine measures in one table

The specification says the formulae are in OxfordAQA's guide "Formulae for accounting ratios". These are the standard forms. Confirm them against that guide.

| Measure | Formula | Answer as |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | gross profit / revenue x 100 | % |
| Markup | gross profit / cost of sales x 100 | % |
| Profit in relation to revenue | profit for the year / revenue x 100 | % |
| Return on capital employed | profit for the year / capital employed x 100 | % |
| Rate of inventory turnover | cost of sales / average inventory | times |
| Current ratio | current assets : current liabilities | x : 1 |
| Liquid capital ratio | (current assets - inventory) : current liabilities | x : 1 |
| Trade receivable days | trade receivables / credit sales x 365 | days |
| Trade payable days | trade payables / credit purchases x 365 | days |

Supporting definitions

- Capital employed = non-current assets + current assets - current liabilities = capital + non-current liabilities.
- Average inventory = (opening + closing) / 2.
- Working capital = current assets - current liabilities.
- Gross profit = revenue - cost of sales.
- Profit for the year = gross profit - expenses.

## Method in steps

1. Write the formula.
2. Substitute the figures from the income statement or statement of financial position.
3. Calculate and give the unit.
4. Compare with another year or business.
5. Explain the reason and give a judgement.

## Worked reminder: Aldmoor Fabrics

Revenue 200,000, cost of sales 130,000, expenses 46,000, capital employed 160,000. Opening inventory 14,000, closing 16,000. Credit sales 146,000, trade receivables 18,000, credit purchases 73,000, trade payables 9,000. Current assets 52,000 (including inventory 16,000), current liabilities 26,000.

| Measure | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | 70,000 / 200,000 x 100 | 35.0% |
| Markup | 70,000 / 130,000 x 100 | 53.8% |
| Profit in relation to revenue | 24,000 / 200,000 x 100 | 12.0% |
| ROCE | 24,000 / 160,000 x 100 | 15.0% |
| Inventory turnover | 130,000 / 15,000 | 8.67 times |
| Current ratio | 52,000 : 26,000 | 2.00 : 1 |
| Liquid capital ratio | 36,000 : 26,000 | 1.38 : 1 |
| Receivable days | 18,000 / 146,000 x 365 | 45.0 days |
| Payable days | 9,000 / 73,000 x 365 | 45.0 days |

## What a change usually means

Use these as starting points, then tie them to the facts in the question.

| Ratio moves | Possible meaning | Possible causes |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin down | Lower profit on each sale | Price cuts, discounts, dearer supplies, wastage |
| Markup down | Smaller addition on cost | Same causes as margin |
| Profit in relation to revenue down | Expenses growing faster than revenue | Wages, rent, advertising, interest |
| ROCE down | Weaker return on investment | Lower profit, or more capital added without extra profit |
| Inventory turnover down | Inventory selling more slowly | Over-buying, weak demand, obsolete lines |
| Inventory turnover up | Faster sales, less cash tied up | Better buying, strong demand (but risk of running out) |
| Current ratio down | Less cover for short-term debts | More payables or overdraft, less cash |
| Liquid capital ratio below 1 | Relying on selling inventory to pay debts | Cash used up, inventory built up |
| Receivable days up | Customers paying slower | Weak credit control, longer terms |
| Payable days up | Taking longer to pay suppliers | Cash shortage, or a negotiated deal; risks goodwill |

A higher ratio is not always better. A very high current ratio can mean idle cash or too much inventory. Very few receivable days might mean credit terms are so tight that sales are lost.

## Margin versus markup

Same gross profit, different base. If gross profit is 70,000 on revenue of 200,000 and cost of sales of 130,000, the margin is 35.0% and the markup is 53.8%. Markup is always larger than margin when the margin is below 100%. Do not mix them up.

## Cash versus profit

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profit | Revenue less expenses, with sales and costs recorded when they arise |
| Cash | Money actually received and paid, held in the bank or as cash |
| Liquidity | Ability to pay debts as they fall due; depends on cash and quickly convertible assets |

Why they differ

- Credit sales: profit rises now, cash arrives later.
- Credit purchases and accruals: costs are recorded now, cash leaves later.
- Depreciation: reduces profit, no cash leaves.
- Buying non-current assets: cash leaves, profit is not reduced by the whole cost.
- Drawings: reduce cash and capital, not profit.
- Loans and extra capital: raise cash, not profit.
- Inventory build-up: uses cash, no effect on profit until the goods are sold.

## Transaction grid

| Transaction | Profit | Cash | Current ratio (if above 1) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cash sale at a profit | Up | Up | Up |
| Credit sale at a profit | Up | No change | Up |
| Customer pays what they owe | No change | Up | No change |
| Pay a supplier from the bank | No change | Down | Up |
| Buy inventory on credit | No change | No change | Down |
| Buy non-current asset for cash | No change | Down | Down |
| Depreciation charge | Down | No change | No change |
| Drawings in cash | No change | Down | Down |
| Take out a long-term loan | No change | Up | Up |
| Pay an expense in cash | Down | Down | Down |

A transaction affects profitability if it changes revenue or expenses. It affects liquidity if it changes current assets or current liabilities.

## Quick self-test

1. Revenue is 300,000 and gross profit is 90,000. Find the gross profit margin.
2. Gross profit is 90,000 and cost of sales is 210,000. Find the markup.
3. Cost of sales is 96,000 with opening inventory 10,000 and closing inventory 14,000. Find the rate of inventory turnover.
4. Current assets are 45,000 and current liabilities are 30,000. Find the current ratio.
5. Inventory in question 4 is 15,000. Find the liquid capital ratio.
6. Trade receivables are 24,000 and credit sales 240,000. Find the receivable days to one decimal place.
7. Trade payables are 15,000 and credit purchases 120,000. Find the payable days to one decimal place.
8. Profit for the year is 18,000 and capital employed 120,000. Find ROCE.
9. A business buys inventory on credit. State the effect on profit and on the current ratio, assuming the ratio started above 1.
10. A business charges depreciation. State the effect on profit and on cash.
11. Name two reasons a profitable business may have a bank overdraft.

### Answers

1. 90,000 / 300,000 x 100 = 30.0%.
2. 90,000 / 210,000 x 100 = 42.9%.
3. Average inventory (10,000 + 14,000) / 2 = 12,000. 96,000 / 12,000 = 8 times.
4. 45,000 : 30,000 = 1.5 : 1.
5. (45,000 - 15,000) : 30,000 = 1.0 : 1.
6. 24,000 / 240,000 x 365 = 36.5 days.
7. 15,000 / 120,000 x 365 = 45.6 days.
8. 18,000 / 120,000 x 100 = 15.0%.
9. No effect on profit. The current ratio falls, because current assets and current liabilities rise by the same amount.
10. Profit falls. Cash does not change.
11. Any two: growth in receivables, growth in inventory, buying non-current assets, large drawings, loan repayments.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a number with no formula, so the method cannot be seen.
- Leaving out units: %, times, days or x : 1.
- Using total sales instead of credit sales for receivable days, or the wrong purchases figure for payable days.
- Putting markup over revenue, or margin over cost of sales.
- Describing a change ("it went up") without a reason, a comparison or a judgement.
- Saying a higher current ratio is always better, or that more profit means more cash.
- Forgetting that drawings and loan repayments change cash but not profit.
- Counting an overdraft as a current asset instead of a current liability when finding capital employed or the current ratio.
- Mixing average inventory and closing inventory between years without saying so.

## Model appraisal sentences

Use the pattern: result, comparison, reason, judgement.

- "Gross profit margin fell from 35.0% to 32.0%, so each sale earned less. Cost of sales rose faster than revenue, which suggests dearer supplies or discounting. This weakens profitability."
- "The liquid capital ratio is 0.8 : 1, below 1. Without selling inventory the business could not meet all its current liabilities, so the owner should collect receivables sooner."
- "Profit rose by 10% but the bank balance fell. Much of the profit is tied up in higher receivables and inventory, so profit and cash have moved apart."

Name the stakeholder where the question does. A lender looks first at liquidity and ROCE. An owner looks at profit in relation to revenue and ROCE. A supplier looks at payable days and the current ratio.
