## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limited liability | A shareholder's loss is limited to the amount paid or agreed to be paid for their shares |
| Equity | Total assets less total liabilities: share capital plus reserves |
| Ordinary shares | Ownership shares; variable dividend; voting rights; highest risk |
| Preference shares | Fixed-rate dividend paid before ordinary dividends; usually no vote |
| General reserve | Retained profit set aside by the directors and kept in the business |
| Retained earnings | Accumulated profit not paid as dividends or transferred to reserves |
| Debentures | Long-term loans to the company at a fixed rate of interest; loan capital |
| Issued share capital | Nominal value of the shares issued to shareholders |
| Called-up share capital | Part of the nominal value the company has asked shareholders to pay |
| Paid-up share capital | Part of the called-up amount actually received |
| Dividend | A share of profit paid to shareholders, decided by the directors |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Limited liability | Profit shared among many shareholders |
| Large capital can be raised | Formation and running costs; legal rules |
| Continuity: separate legal person | Accounts available to the public |
| Easier to borrow | Owners may have little control |

## Ordinary shares versus debentures

| | Ordinary shares | Debentures |
|---|---|---|
| Holder is | an owner | a lender |
| Return | dividend, varies, may be nil | interest, fixed, compulsory |
| Shown in | statement of changes in equity | statement of profit or loss (finance cost) |
| In the statement of financial position | equity | non-current liability (current if repayable within a year) |
| Repaid? | not while the company trades | on the agreed date |
| Voting | usually yes | no |

## The three statements

1. **Statement of profit or loss:** gross profit + other income − expenses
   = profit from operations; − finance costs (debenture interest) = profit for the year.
2. **Statement of changes in equity:** a column for each part of equity and a
   total. Rows: opening balance; shares issued; profit for the year; dividends
   paid; transfer to general reserve; closing balance.
3. **Statement of financial position:** assets as for a sole trader; equity from the
   closing row of the statement of changes in equity; debentures under
   non-current liabilities; accrued debenture interest under current liabilities.

## Worked reminders

**Dividends.** 80,000 of 5% preference shares: 4,000. 400,000 ordinary shares of
$0.50 with a dividend of $0.04 per share: 400,000 × 0.04 = 16,000. The dividend is per
**share**, not per dollar of nominal value.

**Issued, called-up, paid-up.** 600,000 shares of $0.50; $0.30 called; calls unpaid
on 12,000 shares.

```
Issued     600,000 × 0.50        = 300,000
Called-up  600,000 × 0.30        = 180,000
Paid-up    180,000 − 12,000 × 0.30 = 176,400
```

**Debenture interest accrued.** 80,000 of 9% debentures issued on 1 April; year end
31 December; paid 3,600. Charge = 80,000 × 9% × 9/12 = 5,400; accrued 1,800.

**Retained earnings column.** Opening 22,000; profit 35,400; dividends 14,000;
transfer to general reserve 6,000.

```
22,000 + 35,400 − 14,000 − 6,000 = 37,400 closing retained earnings
```

Check the statement of changes in equity by adding the closing figure of every
column: the result must equal the closing figure in the total column.

## Types of company business

A company can be **trading** (buys goods to resell), **service** (sells skills or
time), **manufacturing** (makes goods, so it prepares a manufacturing account) or a
**combination**. The equity section is the same whatever the type.

## Exam traps

- **No drawings** in a company; directors' fees are expenses.
- **Dividends** are never in the statement of profit or loss.
- **Interest on debentures** is charged for the full period even if unpaid.
- **New shares** issued before a dividend date receive that dividend.
- **Transfers to reserves** move amounts within equity; the total is unchanged.
- **Debentures** are not equity, even though they are long-term capital.
- **Retained earnings** is a reserve, not cash.

## Self-test

1. Define limited liability.
2. What is equity?
3. Name the four components of equity in the 0452 capital structure.
4. Where is debenture interest shown?
5. Where are dividends paid shown?
6. 100,000 7% preference shares of $1. Calculate the annual preference dividend.
7. 200,000 shares of $0.25 issued; $0.20 called; all paid. State issued and
   paid-up capital.
8. Retained earnings 40,000; profit 25,000; dividends 12,000; transfer to general
   reserve 8,000. Calculate closing retained earnings.
9. Give one feature of preference shares.
10. Why are debentures a non-current liability?
11. State one disadvantage of operating as a limited company.
12. Does a transfer to general reserve change total equity?

### Answers

1. Shareholders can lose only the amount they paid or agreed to pay for their shares.
2. Total assets less total liabilities, equal to share capital plus reserves.
3. Preference share capital, ordinary share capital, general reserve, retained earnings.
4. In the statement of profit or loss, as a finance cost.
5. In the statement of changes in equity.
6. **7,000**.
7. Issued **50,000**; paid-up **40,000**.
8. 40,000 + 25,000 − 12,000 − 8,000 = **45,000**.
9. A fixed rate of dividend paid before ordinary dividends.
10. They are loans repayable more than twelve months after the year end.
11. For example, profits are shared among many shareholders.
12. No: it moves profit from retained earnings to the general reserve.
