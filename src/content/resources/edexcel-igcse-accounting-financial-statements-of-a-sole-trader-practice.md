## Questions

**1.** State two differences between an income statement and a statement of financial position. **[2]**

**2.** A potter's records for the year show: sales 73,600; sales returns 2,100; opening inventories 6,300; purchases 39,200; carriage inwards 850; purchases returns 1,400; closing inventories 5,700.

**(a)** Calculate cost of sales. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate gross profit. **[2]**

**3.** Copy the table and write the correct heading from the list for each item: cost of sales; expense in the income statement; current asset; current liability; non-current liability; deduction from equity. **[6]**

| Item | Heading |
|---|---|
| Carriage inwards | |
| Carriage outwards | |
| Closing inventories | |
| Bank overdraft | |
| Loan repayable in four years | |
| Drawings | |

**4.** Kamal's capital at 1 January was 27,500. During the year he put in a further 3,000, made a profit of 11,260 and took drawings of 9,800.

**(a)** Calculate his capital at 31 December. **[3]**
**(b)** State what happens to closing capital if drawings are larger than the profit for the year. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why drawings are not shown as an expense in the income statement. **[3]**

**6.** A draft income statement contains five entries, listed below. Identify the mistake in each and say where the item should go.

- (i) Purchase of a new freezer, 4,800, listed as an expense.
- (ii) Owner's drawings, 6,000, listed as an expense.
- (iii) Carriage inwards, 380, listed as an expense.
- (iv) Closing inventories, 2,900, listed as an expense.
- (v) Repayment of 2,000 of a loan, listed as an expense. **[5]**

**7.** Sidra Naeem runs Loomhouse Rugs. For the year ended 31 July 2026 her records show: sales 96,400; sales returns 1,600; opening inventories 7,800; purchases 52,300; purchases returns 1,900; carriage inwards 900; closing inventories 8,200; discount received 410. Expenses are wages 17,500, rent 5,400, carriage outwards 1,260, advertising 2,150, general expenses 1,840, depreciation 3,000 and loan interest 450.

Prepare the income statement of Loomhouse Rugs for the year ended 31 July 2026, showing gross profit and profit for the year. **[9]**

**8.** Using your profit from question 7, prepare the statement of financial position of Loomhouse Rugs at 31 July 2026. Other balances: premises 70,000; fixtures at cost 15,000 with accumulated depreciation of 9,000 (after this year's charge); inventories 8,200; trade receivables 6,300; bank 2,750; cash 300; trade payables 5,100; loan 7,500 (repayable in 2030). Capital at 1 August 2025 was 80,000 and drawings were 11,760. **[9]**

**9.** At the year end a trader's assets total 48,300 and liabilities total 19,650. During the year the profit was 6,450 and drawings were 8,000.

**(a)** Calculate closing equity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the capital at the start of the year. **[3]**

**10.** Dar Electrics has these balances at its year end: non-current assets (carrying amount) 21,000; inventories 4,100; trade receivables 3,600; cash 90; bank overdraft 5,400; trade payables 6,200; loan repayable in six years 15,000.

**(a)** Calculate working capital. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the owner's equity at the year end. **[2]**
**(c)** State two features of the statement of financial position that suggest the business may be at risk. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two differences, one mark each:
- The income statement covers a period of time (such as a year) whereas the statement of financial position is at one date [1].
- The income statement shows revenue, expenses and profit; the statement of financial position shows assets, liabilities and equity [1].

*Examiner insight:* Two separate contrasts are needed for two marks; saying only that one is "for the year" and the other "at a date" is a single point.

**2. (a)** Opening inventories 6,300 + purchases 39,200 + carriage inwards 850 = 46,350 [1]; less purchases returns 1,400 = 44,950 [1]; less closing inventories 5,700 = **39,250** [1].
**(b)** Net revenue 73,600 - 2,100 = 71,500 [1]; gross profit 71,500 - 39,250 = **32,250** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Gross profit is taken from net revenue, not from the sales figure before returns, and writing the subtotals down lets a slip in one line be seen separately from the rest.

**3.** Carriage inwards: cost of sales [1]. Carriage outwards: expense in the income statement [1]. Closing inventories: current asset [1]. Bank overdraft: current liability [1]. Loan repayable in four years: non-current liability [1]. Drawings: deduction from equity [1].

*Examiner insight:* Inwards and outwards carriage are a favourite pair; the word tells you which side of the business the delivery is on.

**4. (a)** Opening 27,500 + capital introduced 3,000 + profit 11,260 [1] - drawings 9,800 [1] = **31,960** [1].
**(b)** Closing capital falls (equity is reduced) [1].

*Examiner insight:* A common slip is subtracting the profit; profit increases capital and drawings reduce it.

**5.** Drawings are money or goods the owner takes for personal use [1]. They are not a cost of running the business or earning revenue [1], so they do not reduce profit; instead they are deducted from the owner's capital in the statement of financial position [1].

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the effect on equity as well as saying "it is not an expense".

**6.** (i) A freezer is capital expenditure, so it is a non-current asset in the statement of financial position [1]. (ii) Drawings are deducted from equity, not shown as an expense [1]. (iii) Carriage inwards belongs in cost of sales [1]. (iv) Closing inventories are deducted in cost of sales and shown as a current asset [1]. (v) A loan repayment reduces the liability; only the interest is an expense [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each item needs both the mistake and the correct place; naming only the correct place may not show you saw the error.

*Examiner insight:* Show each subtotal. The business name, statement title and "for the year ended" wording are part of the presentation and are easy to omit.

Correct title, name and date [1]. Net assets equal closing capital, which confirms the work.

*Examiner insight:* A statement of financial position that does not balance is a signal to re-check before leaving the question; the net assets and equity totals should match.

**9. (a)** Equity = assets - liabilities [1]; 48,300 - 19,650 = **28,650** [1].
**(b)** Closing capital = opening capital + profit - drawings, so opening = closing - profit + drawings [1]; 28,650 - 6,450 + 8,000 [1] = **30,200** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Rearranging the equity roll-forward correctly is the skill being tested; show the equation before the figures.

**10. (a)** Current assets 4,100 + 3,600 + 90 = 7,790 [1]; current liabilities 5,400 + 6,200 = 11,600, so working capital is **(3,810)**, a negative figure [1].
**(b)** Net assets = 21,000 + (-3,810) - 15,000 [1] = **2,190**, which equals equity [1].
**(c)** Working capital is negative, because current liabilities exceed current assets [1]; the business has a bank overdraft and very little cash [1]. (Other valid points, such as equity being small compared with the loan, also earn credit; two points are needed.)

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs figures from the statement as evidence; a general comment such as "the business is doing badly" is not tied to the numbers.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing closing inventories into cost of sales as an addition instead of a deduction.
- Starting gross profit from sales instead of net revenue after sales returns.
- Putting carriage inwards among the expenses.
- Showing drawings, a freezer purchase or a loan repayment in the income statement.
- Forgetting accumulated depreciation, so the carrying amount is wrong.
- Mixing up the order of current assets, or placing a bank overdraft in assets.
- Adding a loss to capital or taking drawings off the wrong line.
- Leaving out the business name, statement title or the date wording.
- Giving a general comment about risk without quoting a figure.
