## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Phenol** | C₆H₅OH; also any compound with –OH bonded directly to a benzene ring |
| **Phenoxide ion** | C₆H₅O⁻, formed when phenol loses H⁺ |
| **Activated ring** | ring with increased electron density, so it reacts with electrophiles under milder conditions |
| **Diazonium salt** | e.g. benzenediazonium chloride, C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ |
| **Azo compound** | contains –N=N– linking two aromatic rings; coloured, used as dyes |
| **Naphthol** | a phenol of naphthalene, C₁₀H₇OH |

## Production in two steps

| Step | Reagents | Temperature | Product |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | phenylamine + HNO₂ (NaNO₂ + dilute HCl) | **below 10 °C** | benzenediazonium chloride |
| 2 | water | **warm** | phenol + N₂ (+ HCl) |

## Five reactions with equations

    (a) C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O
    (b) 2C₆H₅OH + 2Na → 2C₆H₅ONa + H₂
    (c) C₆H₅N₂⁺ + C₆H₅O⁻ → C₆H₅N=NC₆H₄OH        (NaOH(aq), cold)
    (d) C₆H₅OH + HNO₃ → HOC₆H₄NO₂ + H₂O           (dilute, room temp; 2- and 4-isomers)
    (e) C₆H₅OH + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃OH + 3HBr          (Br₂(aq), room temp)

| Reaction | Key observation |
|---|---|
| NaOH(aq) | phenol dissolves; add acid and it turns cloudy again |
| Na | effervescence (H₂) |
| diazonium salt | yellow-orange colour or precipitate |
| Br₂(aq) | orange colour lost; white precipitate |

## Acidity

**Order:** phenol > water > ethanol. Phenol is weaker than carboxylic acids: **no CO₂** with carbonates.

| Anion | Charge on O | Stability | Acid strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| C₆H₅O⁻ | delocalised into ring | most stable | strongest |
| OH⁻ | localised | intermediate | intermediate |
| C₂H₅O⁻ | intensified by electron-donating ethyl group | least stable | weakest |

**Logic:** more stable anion → equilibrium further right → stronger acid.

## Why milder conditions than benzene

| | Benzene | Phenol |
|---|---|---|
| Nitration | conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C | dilute HNO₃(aq), room temperature |
| Bromination | Br₂ + AlBr₃ | Br₂(aq), no catalyst |
| Number of Br added | one | three (2, 4, 6) |

Reason: lone pair on O **delocalised into the ring** → **higher electron density** → ring **activated** →
polarises Br₂ itself and reacts with a weaker nitrating mixture.

## Other phenolic compounds: the routine

1. –OH carbon = C1.
2. Activated positions: **2, 4, 6**.
3. Remove any already occupied.
4. One Br₂ per free position with bromine water.

| Phenol | Br₂ (mol) | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 2-chlorophenol | 2 | 2,4-dibromo-6-chlorophenol |
| 3-methylphenol | 3 | 2,4,6-tribromo-3-methylphenol |
| 4-nitrophenol | 2 | 2,6-dibromo-4-nitrophenol |
| 2,4,6-trimethylphenol | 0 | no substitution |
| 2,6-dimethylphenol | 1 | 4-bromo-2,6-dimethylphenol |

**Nitration of substituted phenols:** dilute HNO₃ puts one –NO₂ at a free 2- or 4-position. 4-methylphenol gives
4-methyl-2-nitrophenol only, because its 4-position is taken.

**Naphthol:** reacts with NaOH, Na and diazonium salts like phenol; gives intensely coloured azo dyes. Check
the actual ring positions, not phenol's numbering (2-naphthol couples at position 1).

## Distinguishing tests

- **Na₂CO₃(aq):** only carboxylic acids fizz.
- **Br₂(aq):** only the phenol gives a white precipitate (of the three: phenol, alcohol, acid).
- **NaOH(aq):** phenol and acids react; alcohols do not.

## Exam traps

- Two temperatures in production: **below 10 °C**, then **warm**.
- **Dilute** HNO₃ for phenol; a **mixture** of 2- and 4-nitrophenol.
- **No catalyst** for phenol bromination; **3HBr** in the equation.
- Acidity is about **anion stability**, not O–H bond strength.
- Ethyl group **destabilises** ethoxide.
- **No CO₂** from phenol with carbonates.
- Count **free** activated positions before balancing.

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions for each step in making phenol from phenylamine.
2. Write the equation for phenol reacting with sodium hydroxide.
3. What is seen when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to aqueous sodium phenoxide?
4. Explain why phenol is a stronger acid than water.
5. Explain why water is a stronger acid than ethanol.
6. Give a test that distinguishes phenol from ethanoic acid.
7. Name the products of nitrating phenol and state the conditions.
8. Why does phenol not need a halogen carrier to react with bromine?
9. How many moles of Br₂ react with one mole of 2-methylphenol, and why?
10. Name the product of 4-methylphenol with excess bromine water.
11. Write the equation for 2,6-dimethylphenol reacting with bromine water.
12. State one reaction that 2-naphthol shares with phenol, and the product type.

**Answers:**

1. NaNO₂ and dilute HCl (HNO₂), below 10 °C; then warm the diazonium salt solution with water.
2. C₆H₅OH + NaOH → C₆H₅ONa + H₂O.
3. The solution turns cloudy as phenol, which is only slightly soluble, is re-formed.
4. In the phenoxide ion the negative charge is delocalised into the ring, stabilising it; in OH⁻ the charge is
   localised on O.
5. The ethyl group is electron-donating, increasing the charge density on O in ethoxide and destabilising it.
6. Add Na₂CO₃(aq): ethanoic acid effervesces (CO₂); phenol does not. (Or Br₂(aq): phenol gives a white
   precipitate.)
7. 2-nitrophenol and 4-nitrophenol; dilute HNO₃(aq), room temperature.
8. The lone pair on O is delocalised into the ring, increasing its electron density so that it can polarise Br₂
   itself.
9. Two: the 2-position is occupied by CH₃, leaving the 4- and 6-positions free.
10. 2,6-dibromo-4-methylphenol.
11. (CH₃)₂C₆H₃OH + Br₂ → (CH₃)₂C₆H₂BrOH + HBr (product: 4-bromo-2,6-dimethylphenol).
12. Any one: dissolves in NaOH(aq) to form a salt; reacts with Na to give H₂; couples with a diazonium salt in
    NaOH(aq) to give an azo dye.
