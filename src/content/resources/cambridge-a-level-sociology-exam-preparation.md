Cambridge A Level Sociology (9699, 2024-2026 syllabus) is assessed through four externally assessed written papers: Paper 1 Socialisation, Identity and Methods of Research (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 25%), Paper 2 The Family (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 25%), Paper 3 Education (1 hour 15 minutes, 50 marks, 21%) and Paper 4 Globalisation, Media and Religion (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks, 29%). The uneven split is intentional and adds up to 100. This page explains how to plan for each paper, using topic pages such as [Globalisation, Media and Religion](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-globalisation-media-religion/), [Media Ownership, Control and the New Media](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-media-ownership-and-control/) and [Secularisation, Feminism and Post-modern Religion](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-influence-of-religion/).

## The uneven weightings

Paper 3 is 21% and Paper 4 is 29%. The syllabus records that Paper 3 moved from 20% to 21% and Paper 4 from 30% to 29% in the current version (Version 3, published April 2026), so older notes or past-paper guidance may quote 20% and 30%. Use the current figures. On the AS-only route, Papers 1 and 2 are weighted 50% each of the AS Level, and on the full A Level they are 25% each.

## Papers 1 and 2: the AS Level

Paper 1 and Paper 2 are each 90 minutes and 60 marks, 1.5 minutes per mark. Paper 1 covers socialisation, identity and methods of research, so the research methods vocabulary (sampling, reliability, validity, ethics and the strengths and limits of each method) carries across the whole course. Paper 2 is The Family. These two papers can be a standalone AS Level, or the first half of the full A Level staged over two years, or all four papers can be sat in the same series.

## Paper 3: Education (1 hour 15 minutes, 50 marks, 21%)

Paper 3 is A Level only. At 75 minutes for 50 marks, the rate is 1.5 minutes per mark, the same as the AS papers, but the shorter total time means less room to recover from a slow start. Prepare to move between theories, concepts and evidence on education quickly and to write compact, evaluated paragraphs.

## Paper 4: Globalisation, Media and Religion (1 hour 45 minutes, 70 marks, 29%)

Paper 4 is the longest and most heavily weighted paper at 105 minutes for 70 marks, 1.5 minutes per mark. It spans three areas, so an even revision split matters: the study guides on [globalisation debates and perspectives](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-globalisation-debates-and-perspectives/) and [global inequality, migration and crime](/resources/a-level-cambridge-sociology-globalisation-contemporary-issues/) help you cover the globalisation strand without neglecting media and religion.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 90 / 60 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 90 / 60 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 75 / 50 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 105 / 70 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- All four papers: 90 + 90 + 75 + 105 = 360 minutes for 60 + 60 + 50 + 70 = 240 marks.

The consistent rate makes timing simple. A 12-mark question takes about 18 minutes, and a 6-mark question about 9 minutes, including planning.

## Command words and question styles

"Describe" and "outline" want the main features or ideas without judgement. "Explain" wants reasons with development. "Assess", "evaluate" and "to what extent" want an argued judgement that weighs competing perspectives, such as functionalist, Marxist, feminist and interactionist views, against evidence. In sociology, an evaluative point is stronger when it names a perspective or study and states a specific limitation, not a vague "other sociologists disagree". Confirm exact command-word definitions in the current syllabus.

## Worked practice scenario

Original question: "Evaluate the view that the nuclear family is the dominant and most useful family form in modern societies."

Plan:

1. Claim: the nuclear family remains important but is no longer the only or clearly dominant form.
2. For: functionalists such as Parsons argued that the nuclear family fits an industrial society by performing key functions.
3. Against: later sociologists emphasised family diversity, including lone-parent, reconstituted and cohabiting households.
4. Evaluation: whether "most useful" is a claim about function or about statistics, and who benefits.
5. Conclusion: a reasoned judgement that keeps the claim, with a qualification.

Model paragraph: "Functionalists such as Parsons argued that the nuclear family suited industrial society because it was small enough to be geographically mobile and could perform core functions such as the socialisation of children. However, this view has been criticised for presenting one family form as natural and for neglecting the diversity of household types. Writers on family diversity argue that people now choose between many arrangements, which weakens the claim that one form is dominant. On balance, the nuclear family remains a common household type, but the evidence suggests it is better described as one important form among several than as the single dominant one." Notice the pattern of claim, perspective, criticism and judgement.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you sit the AS Level (Papers 1 and 2) or the full A Level.
- Use the current weightings: 25%, 25%, 21% and 29%.
- Build a perspectives grid (functionalist, Marxist, feminist, interactionist) for each area.
- Practise timed responses at 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Revise research methods thoroughly, since it supports the whole course.
- Give every evaluative point a named source of support or criticism.
- Plan longer answers in two or three minutes before writing.
- Reserve a few minutes to check you have answered each part.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Sociology 9699?**
There are four externally assessed written papers: Socialisation, Identity and Methods of Research; The Family; Education; and Globalisation, Media and Religion.

**Why are the weightings uneven?**
They add up to 100 as 25, 25, 21 and 29. The syllabus records the move of Paper 3 from 20% to 21% and Paper 4 from 30% to 29% in its current version.

**Which papers make up the AS Level?**
Papers 1 and 2, each 90 minutes and 60 marks, at 50% each of the AS Level.

**How long is each paper?**
Papers 1 and 2 are 1 hour 30 minutes, Paper 3 is 1 hour 15 minutes and Paper 4 is 1 hour 45 minutes.

**Is this syllabus still current?**
It is the 2024-2026 edition and its final examination year is 2026, so check which syllabus applies to your series.
