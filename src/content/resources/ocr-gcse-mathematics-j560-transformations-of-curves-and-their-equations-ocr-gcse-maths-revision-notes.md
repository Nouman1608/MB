These notes condense section 7.03, Transformations of curves and their equations, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026). All of 7.03 is **Higher tier only**. The specification asks you to "identify and sketch translations and reflections of a given graph (or the graph of a given equation)" and states that knowledge of function notation will not be required. Full explanations are in the [transformations of curves study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-transformations-of-curves-and-their-equations-ocr-gcse-maths/); test yourself with the [transformations of curves practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-transformations-of-curves-and-their-equations-ocr-gcse-maths-practice/). The wider topic is in the [exponential and trigonometric graphs revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-exponential-and-trigonometric-graphs-and-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Translation | Every point moves the same distance in the same direction; described by a column vector |
| Column vector (a over b) | a to the right (left if negative), b up (down if negative) |
| Reflection in the x-axis | Every point (p, q) goes to (p, −q); the graph flips upside down |
| Reflection in the y-axis | Every point (p, q) goes to (−p, q); the graph flips left to right |
| Key points | Turning points, intercepts and other labelled points to move first |
| Asymptote | A line the curve approaches but never meets; it moves with the curve |
| Invariant | Unchanged by the transformation |

## The four rules

```
y = (expression) + a        translation (0 over a)       (p, q) → (p, q + a)
x replaced by (x + a)       translation (−a over 0)      (p, q) → (p − a, q)
y = −(expression)           reflection in x-axis         (p, q) → (p, −q)
x replaced by −x            reflection in y-axis         (p, q) → (−p, q)
```

Outside the expression: acts on y, as expected. Inside, on x: the opposite way.

| Equation from y = x² | Description | Turning point |
|---|---|---|
| y = x² + 6 | Translation (0 over 6) | (0, 6) |
| y = (x + 4)² | Translation (−4 over 0) | (−4, 0) |
| y = (x − 1)² − 7 | Translation (1 over −7) | (1, −7) |
| y = −x² | Reflection in x-axis | (0, 0), now a maximum |
| y = −x² + 3 | Reflection in x-axis, then translation (0 over 3) | (0, 3), a maximum |

## Method for a sketch

```
1. Identify the base graph (x², x³, 1/x, kˣ, sin x, cos x, tan x).
2. Read off each transformation from the equation.
3. Move the key points and asymptotes using the point rules.
4. Draw the shape through the new points.
5. Label turning points, intercepts and asymptote equations.
```

## Base graphs: features to move

| Graph | Key features |
|---|---|
| y = x² | Minimum (0, 0); symmetrical about the y-axis |
| y = x³ | Passes through (0, 0), (1, 1), (−1, −1) |
| y = 1/x | Asymptotes x = 0 and y = 0; passes through (1, 1) and (−1, −1) |
| y = kˣ (k > 1) | Passes through (0, 1); asymptote y = 0 |
| y = sin x | (0, 0), (90°, 1), (180°, 0), (270°, −1), (360°, 0) |
| y = cos x | (0, 1), (90°, 0), (180°, −1), (270°, 0), (360°, 1) |
| y = tan x | (0, 0), (180°, 0); asymptotes at x = 90° and x = 270° |

## Reminders

- y = (x + 3)² − 2: translation (−3 over −2); minimum (−3, −2); y-intercept 9 − 2 = 7.
- y = 1/(x − 4): asymptotes x = 4 and y = 0.
- y = 3ˣ + 5: asymptote y = 5; y-intercept (0, 6).
- y = cos x − 2: runs from −3 to −1; starts at (0, −1).
- y = sin(x − 90°): moves 90° right; it is the graph of y = −cos x.
- Roots move with a horizontal translation: if y = (curve) has roots 1 and 4, replacing x by (x + 2) gives roots −1 and 2.
- Translation to an equation: y = x² moved by (−5 over 1) is y = (x + 5)² + 1.

## Order and brackets

- y = −x² + 4: reflect, then up 4. Maximum (0, 4).
- y = −(x² + 4) = −x² − 4: maximum (0, −4).
- Every x must be replaced: y = (x − 1)³ − (x − 1) is a translation of y = x³ − x; y = (x − 1)³ − x is not.

## Exam traps

- (x + a) moves left, not right.
- A horizontal translation changes the x-coordinates only; a vertical one changes the y-coordinates only.
- Moving the curve but not its asymptotes.
- A reflection of a graph symmetrical in the y-axis (such as y = x² or y = cos x) in the y-axis gives the same graph.
- "Describe fully" needs the type and the details: vector or mirror line.
- Leaving the turning point or asymptote unlabelled on a sketch.

## Quick self-test

1. Describe the transformation from y = x² to y = x² − 8.
2. Describe the transformation from y = x² to y = (x − 5)².
3. Write down the turning point of y = (x + 1)² + 6.
4. y = x³ is translated by (2 over 0). Write down its new equation.
5. Write down the coordinates of the image of (3, 9) on y = x² for the curve y = −x².
6. The point (2, 8) lies on y = x³. Find its image on y = (−x)³.
7. Write down the asymptotes of y = 1/(x + 3) − 2.
8. Write down the y-intercept and the asymptote of y = 2ˣ + 4.
9. Describe y = cos x + 1 and give its maximum and minimum values.
10. y = x² is translated so that its turning point is at (−2, 5). Write its equation.
11. A curve has roots x = −3 and x = 2. Where are the roots after x is replaced by x − 4?
12. Explain why y = −(x² − 1) and y = −x² − 1 are different curves.

### Answers

1. Translation by (0 over −8)
2. Translation by (5 over 0)
3. (−1, 6)
4. y = (x − 2)³
5. (3, −9)
6. (−2, 8)
7. x = −3 and y = −2
8. y-intercept (0, 5); asymptote y = 4
9. Translation by (0 over 1); maximum 2, minimum 0
10. y = (x + 2)² + 5
11. Moved right 4: x = 1 and x = 6
12. −(x² − 1) = −x² + 1, which has maximum (0, 1) and crosses the x-axis at ±1; y = −x² − 1 has maximum (0, −1) and no roots
