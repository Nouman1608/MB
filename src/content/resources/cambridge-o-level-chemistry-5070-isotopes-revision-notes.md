These notes condense section 2.3, Isotopes, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [isotopes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-isotopes/), and the [isotopes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-isotopes-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## The four outcomes

1. **Define** isotopes.
2. **State** why isotopes have the same chemical properties.
3. **Interpret and use** symbols for atoms and ions.
4. **Calculate** relative atomic mass from isotope masses and abundances.

## Definition (word for word)

**Isotopes** are different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.

| | Same in isotopes? |
|---|---|
| Protons | yes |
| Electrons (neutral atoms) | yes |
| Electronic configuration | yes |
| Neutrons | **no** |
| Mass number | **no** |
| Chemical properties | yes |
| Mass of atom | **no** |

## Same chemistry: the chain

Same number of protons → same number of electrons → **same electronic configuration** (same outer electrons) → **same chemical properties**.

Neutrons affect mass only, not reactions.

## Reading symbols

```
 mass number  →  37
                     Cl −   ←  charge (ions only)
 proton number → 17
```

- Protons = bottom number.
- Neutrons = top − bottom.
- Electrons = bottom − charge (positive ion) or bottom + charge (negative ion).
- Isotopes in a list: **same bottom number, different top number**.
- Same top number, different bottom number = different elements (e.g. argon-40 and calcium-40).

## Isotope examples worth knowing

| Element | Isotopes | Neutrons |
|---|---|---|
| Hydrogen | ¹H, ²H, ³H | 0, 1, 2 |
| Carbon | ¹²C, ¹³C, ¹⁴C | 6, 7, 8 |
| Chlorine | ³⁵Cl, ³⁷Cl | 18, 20 |
| Magnesium | ²⁴Mg, ²⁵Mg, ²⁶Mg | 12, 13, 14 |

## Relative atomic mass calculation

Ar = Σ(isotope mass × % abundance) ÷ 100

If abundances are given as relative heights that do not total 100, divide by their **total** instead.

**Check list:**
- Answer between the lightest and heaviest isotope masses.
- Answer nearest the most abundant isotope.
- Rounded only at the end, to the precision asked for.

**Reverse calculation** (two isotopes): let one abundance be *x* and the other (100 − *x*); set the weighted sum equal to Ar × 100 and solve.

Mini-example: isotopes of mass 10 and 11, Ar = 10.8. 10*x* + 11(100 − *x*) = 1080 → 1100 − *x* = 1080 → *x* = 20%. So 20% mass 10, 80% mass 11.

## Laying out an Ar calculation

Examiners award a method mark for the expression, so write it in full before any arithmetic:

```
Ar = (mass 1 × % 1) + (mass 2 × % 2) + (mass 3 × % 3)
     ------------------------------------------------
                          100
```

Then show the numerator as a single number, divide, and round only at the end. If the question says "to one decimal place", a whole-number answer loses the final mark even when the method is right.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Isotope | an atom of an element with the same proton number but a different number of neutrons from another atom of that element |
| Proton number | number of protons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Mass number | total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus |
| Abundance | the percentage (or proportion) of an element's atoms that are a particular isotope |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | average mass of the atoms of an element, weighted by isotope abundance, compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Nuclide symbol | symbol showing mass number and proton number, e.g. ¹⁴₇N |

## Exam traps

- Isotopes differ in **neutrons**; ions differ in **electrons**.
- "Same element" alone does not explain identical chemistry; give the configuration chain.
- Top number is mass number, bottom is proton number. Don't swap them.
- Ar is an average, so a value like 35.5 is expected, not an error.
- Divide by 100 (or the total abundance) at the end.
- Same mass number does not mean same element.

## Self-test

1. Define isotopes.
2. Explain why chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 react in the same way.
3. How many neutrons are in an atom of ¹⁴₆C?
4. How many electrons are in ³²₁₆S²⁻?
5. Which two of these are isotopes of the same element: ¹⁴₆C, ¹⁴₇N, ¹⁵₇N, ¹⁶₈O?
6. Write the symbol, with mass number and proton number, for a particle with 11 protons, 12 neutrons and 10 electrons.
7. Neon is 90.5% neon-20, 0.3% neon-21 and 9.2% neon-22. Calculate Ar to one decimal place.
8. Silver is 51.8% silver-107 and 48.2% silver-109. Calculate Ar to one decimal place.
9. An element has isotopes of mass 63 and 65 and Ar = 63.5. Calculate the percentage of the mass-63 isotope.
10. Why is the relative atomic mass of chlorine not a whole number?
11. Give **one** property that differs between two isotopes of the same element.
12. Bars of relative abundance 9 at mass 20 and 1 at mass 22 are shown for an element. Calculate Ar.

## Answers

1. Different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons but different numbers of neutrons.
2. Both have 17 protons, so 17 electrons and the same electronic configuration (2,8,7); chemical properties depend on electrons.
3. 14 − 6 = 8.
4. 16 + 2 = 18.
5. ¹⁴₇N and ¹⁵₇N.
6. ²³₁₁Na⁺.
7. (20 × 90.5 + 21 × 0.3 + 22 × 9.2) ÷ 100 = 2018.7 ÷ 100 = 20.187 = 20.2.
8. (107 × 51.8 + 109 × 48.2) ÷ 100 = 10 796.4 ÷ 100 = 107.964 = 108.0.
9. 63*x* + 65(100 − *x*) = 6350 → 6500 − 2*x* = 6350 → *x* = 75%.
10. It is an average of the masses of its isotopes (chlorine-35 and chlorine-37), weighted by their abundances.
11. Mass of the atom (or the number of neutrons, mass number, or a mass-dependent physical property such as density).
12. (20 × 9 + 22 × 1) ÷ 10 = 202 ÷ 10 = 20.2.
