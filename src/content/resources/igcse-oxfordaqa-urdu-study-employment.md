This guide covers **Theme 3 Current and Future Study and Employment**, the third and final theme in
OxfordAQA International GCSE Urdu (9264), for International GCSE exams May/June 2026 onwards. It
completes the theme trio alongside [Theme 1 Identity and Culture](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-identity-and-culture/)
and [Theme 2 Local, National, International and Global Areas of Interest](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-urdu-local-national-international/)
-- where those two themes look inward to personal life and outward to the wider world, Theme 3 looks
forward, to education and career.

## Where this fits in 9264

As with the other two themes, Theme 3 is examined across both Paper 1 (Reading, Grammar and Meaning)
and Paper 2 (Writing), each 1 hour 45 minutes and worth 70 marks (50% of the GCSE). There is no
separate paper for this theme -- the same two skills papers draw on content from all three themes, so
candidates cannot skip Theme 3 preparation on the assumption it is examined separately.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE URDU (9264) — THEME 3 CURRENT AND FUTURE STUDY AND EMPLOYMENT**

- 3.1 My Studies -- describing school subjects, opinions on subjects, and the school day
- 3.2 Life at School/College -- school facilities, rules, routines, and school life more broadly
- 3.3 Education Post-16 -- further education and study options after the current stage of schooling
- 3.4 Jobs, Career Choices and Ambitions -- part-time work, career aspirations, and the language of
  future plans

## Vocabulary you need in place

Theme 3 leans on future-oriented and opinion-giving language more than Theme 1's descriptive,
present-focused vocabulary:

- **School and studies** -- subject names, opinions on subjects (I like/dislike... because...),
  timetables, and the school day.
- **School life** -- facilities, uniform, rules, and comparing your own school experience with
  others.
- **Post-16 options** -- vocabulary for further study routes, qualifications, and further/higher
  education.
- **Work and careers** -- part-time jobs, workplace vocabulary, and career or profession names,
  alongside the language of future intention (I would like to..., I am planning to..., I hope to...).

Because this theme is inherently about the future, treat future-tense and conditional structures as
core vocabulary in their own right, not an optional extra layered on top of present-tense
description.

## Structures that raise the grade

- **Future tense** -- for stating study or career plans directly (I will study...).
- **Conditional and "would like to" structures** -- for more tentative or aspirational statements (I
  would like to become..., if I passed my exams, I would...).
- **Comparatives** -- comparing subjects, schools, or career paths (this subject is more useful
  than..., a stable career is more important to me than...).
- **Opinion and justification language** -- giving a preference and a reason together (my favourite
  subject is..., because...), which examiners reward far more than a bare preference alone.
- **Past tense for reflection** -- occasionally required when a task asks a candidate to reflect on
  past school experience before discussing future plans.

Because Theme 3 tasks often ask for both a present description and a future plan in the same
response (for example, describing your current studies before explaining your career ambitions),
practise moving between tenses within a single piece of writing, the same skill Theme 2 develops for
switching between description and argument.

## Approaching the tasks

For **reading and listening**, questions on this theme often test whether a candidate can distinguish
a stated fact (a specific subject or job mentioned) from an expressed opinion or aspiration (why the
candidate likes a subject, or what they hope to do in future) -- read or listen for both layers rather
than treating the passage as a flat list of facts. For **writing and speaking**, career and ambition
tasks specifically reward candidates who justify a choice, not just name it: stating a career ambition
and then explaining a reason for it, ideally referencing a personal interest or skill, is a reliably
strong structure.

## Worked example

Task: *Write about your studies and your plans for after you finish school.*

```
Weak:   I study many subjects. I want a good job.
        (lists subjects with no opinion, states an ambition with no
        justification, one tense only)

Strong: names specific subjects with an opinion and reason (present
          tense) -- my favourite subject is science because I find
          experiments interesting
        states a post-16 plan (future tense) -- after school, I will
          continue my studies at college
        gives a career ambition with justification (would like to /
          because) -- I would like to become a doctor because I want
          to help people
        adds a conditional reflection -- if I study hard, I will
          achieve my goal
```

Naming a specific subject with a justified opinion, stating a concrete post-16 plan, and closing with
a justified career ambition is a reliable structure for the strongest responses in this theme.

## Common mistakes

Naming a subject or job without ever giving an opinion or reason, missing an easy opportunity to
demonstrate justification language. Using only the present tense throughout a task that explicitly
asks about future plans, which under-demonstrates the future and conditional structures this theme is
designed to test. Confusing "life at school" (facilities, routine, rules) with "my studies" (subjects,
opinions on them) when a task asks specifically about one or the other. Losing verb agreement when
switching from present-tense description to future-tense plans within the same response.

## Quick revision checklist

- Build active vocabulary across all four sub-topics: studies, school life, post-16 options, and
  jobs/careers.
- Practise future tense and "would like to" structures for stating plans and ambitions.
- Rehearse justifying a subject preference or career ambition with a reason, not just naming it.
- Practise moving between present-tense description and future-tense plans within a single response.
- Distinguish "my studies" (subjects, opinions) from "life at school" (facilities, routine) in reading
  and listening tasks.
