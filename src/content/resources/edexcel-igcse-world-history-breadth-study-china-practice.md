These questions practise the three Breadth Study question types of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE
History 4HI1 Paper 2, Section B — two differences (6), two causes (8) and a 16-mark change-over-time
essay — across the five key topics and themes of "China: conflict, crisis and change, 1900–89".

## Set 1

**1(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the government of China in 1910 was different from the government
of China in 1920. **[6]**

**1(b)** Explain **two** causes of the CCP's victory in the Civil War (1946–49). **[8]**

**1(c)** "External influences were the main cause of conflict in China in the years 1900–49." How far do
you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Boxer Uprising
- the war with Japan (1937–45)

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 2

**2(a)** Explain **two** ways in which education in China in 1965 was different from education in 1968.
**[6]**

**2(b)** Explain **two** causes of the Great Leap Forward's failure. **[8]**

**2(c)** "The lives of Chinese peasants changed more in the years 1949–63 than in the years 1976–89." How
far do you agree? **[16]**

You may use the following in your answer:
- the Agrarian Reform Law (1950)
- Deng's reforms in agriculture

You **must** also use information of your own.

## Set 3 — extra practice

**3(a)** Explain **two** ways in which China's relationship with the Soviet Union in 1953 was different
from its relationship in 1969. **[6]**

**3(b)** Explain **two** causes of the student protests in Tiananmen Square in 1989. **[8]**

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

- **Who ruled:** in 1910 the Qing emperor (the child Puyi, with regents) ruled; by 1920 the dynasty had gone
  and a republic existed in name.
- **Unity:** in 1910 there was a single imperial government, however weak; in 1920 real power lay with
  rival warlords controlling different regions.

### 1(b) [8]

- **GMD weakness:** Japan's invasion had cost the GMD its best troops and coastal cities; hyperinflation
  and corruption destroyed middle-class and peasant support.
- **CCP strengths:** land reform won peasant loyalty; disciplined, experienced forces under Mao and Lin Biao
  won decisive battles such as Huai-Hai (1948–49), capturing weapons and absorbing defectors.

### 1(c) [16]

**External influences:** the Boxer Uprising against foreign influence and its suppression by foreign
troops; Versailles and the May Fourth Movement; Soviet backing for the United Front; Japanese invasion of
Manchuria (1931) and war from 1937, which weakened the GMD and helped the CCP.
**Internal factors:** Qing weakness and the 1911 Revolution; warlordism; the GMD–CCP rivalry, the Shanghai
Massacre and Extermination Campaigns; peasant poverty and landlord power; leadership of Chiang and Mao.
**Judgement:** external pressures repeatedly triggered and intensified conflict, but the struggle for
power inside China was its core. Range across the full period.

### 2(a) [6]

- **Schools open v closed:** in 1965 schools and universities ran normally with exams; by 1968 many had
  closed as Red Guards attacked teachers.
- **Content:** in 1965 teaching mixed academic subjects with politics; in 1968 study of Mao's thought
  dominated and young people were being sent to the countryside to learn from peasants.

### 2(b) [8]

- **Unrealistic planning:** targets for steel and grain were impossible; officials reported false figures,
  so the state requisitioned grain that did not exist.
- **Neglect of farming and poor methods:** peasants were taken from fields for backyard furnaces producing
  useless steel, and pseudo-scientific methods reduced harvests; the famine followed, worsened by the
  withdrawal of Soviet advisers in 1960.

### 2(c) [16]

**1949–63:** landlords overthrown and land redistributed (1950); then cooperatives, collectives and
communes; the Great Leap and famine; the Marriage Law for women.
**1976–89:** household responsibility system let families farm and sell surpluses; rising rural incomes;
one-child policy; rural industry; migration to cities and SEZs.
**Judgement:** 1949–63 brought more dramatic and violent change — two revolutions in landholding and a
catastrophic famine — while 1976–89 brought a steadier rise in living standards. Reward clear criteria for
"more change".

### 3(a) [6]

- In 1953 the USSR was China's main ally, providing loans and experts for the first Five-year Plan; by 1969
  relations had broken down.
- In 1953 Mao accepted Soviet leadership of world communism; in 1969 the two states fought border clashes.

### 3(b) [8]

- **Demands for political reform:** economic change had not been matched by democracy; students
  influenced by the Democracy Wall and the 1986 protests wanted freedom of speech.
- **Corruption and inflation, and Hu Yaobang's death:** rising prices and officials' corruption angered
  people, and the death of the reformer Hu in April 1989 sparked mourning that became protest.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), confusing the dates of the Great Leap and Cultural Revolution.
- In (b), describing events without explaining why they caused failure or protest.
- In (c), giving no clear criteria for comparison.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is the Paper 2 breadth study question structured?**
Part (a) asks you to explain two ways something was different at two dates (6 marks), part (b) to explain two causes (8 marks) and part (c) is a 16-mark essay on change, continuity or significance across the period.

**Who ruled China in 1910?**
The Qing dynasty, under the child emperor Puyi with regents; by 1920 the dynasty had fallen and China was a republic in name.

**Which dates are most often confused?**
The Great Leap Forward (from 1958) and the Cultural Revolution (from 1966); check them before you write.
