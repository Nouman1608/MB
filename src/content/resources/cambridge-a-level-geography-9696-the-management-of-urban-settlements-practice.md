These questions cover **6.4 The management of urban settlements** from Paper 2 Core Human Geography. Questions 1 to 6 use fictional places and data; questions 7 to 10 ask for your own case studies. Revise first with the [6.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-geography-9696-the-management-of-urban-settlements/) and the [6.4 revision notes](/resources/a-level-geography-9696-management-of-urban-settlements-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term "security of tenure". **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a site and service scheme and in-situ upgrading. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why many shanty towns are located on hazardous land. **[3]**

**4.** The table shows conditions in the fictional informal settlement of Kesu before and after an upgrading scheme.

| Indicator | 2012 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|
| Households with a piped water connection (%) | 15 | 72 |
| Households with a toilet connected to a sewer (%) | 8 | 55 |
| Lanes paved (%) | 10 | 80 |
| Households with legal title to their plot (%) | 5 | 20 |
| Child diarrhoea cases per 1 000 children per year | 240 | 110 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in child diarrhoea cases between 2012 and 2024. Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** Using evidence from the table, assess the success of the scheme. **[4]**

**5.** The table shows the average daily journey to work for residents of four districts of the fictional city of Varo.

| District | Location | Journey time (minutes) | Share of income spent on travel (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Central, high income | 20 | 4 |
| B | Inner, middle income | 35 | 8 |
| C | Hillside informal settlement | 95 | 18 |
| D | New suburb on a rail line | 40 | 9 |

**(a)** Describe how journeys to work vary between the districts. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest **two** reasons why residents of district C face the longest and most expensive journeys. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **two** challenges of supplying electricity to a rapidly growing city in a low or middle income country. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** For a shanty town you have studied in a low or middle income country, describe the challenges faced by its residents. **[6]**

**8.** Explain why community-led schemes may be more successful than clearance in improving shanty towns. **[4]**

**9.** For a city you have studied, explain the challenges of providing either power or transport infrastructure. **[5]**

**10.** With reference to your case study of power or transport for a city, evaluate the success of the attempted solutions. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** The legal right to occupy a plot or home [1] without the threat of eviction, for example through a title or long lease [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A site and service scheme provides new plots with water, drainage and roads on which residents build their own homes [1]. In-situ upgrading improves an existing settlement where it stands, adding services and paving without moving residents [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Formal developers avoid hazardous land, so it is often unused or publicly owned [1]. Migrants cannot afford land in the formal market, so they occupy the only land available, such as steep slopes, floodplains or land beside railways [1]. These sites may also be close to jobs in the city, outweighing the risk for residents [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** (240 − 110) ÷ 240 × 100 [1] = 54.2% fall [1].

**(b)** Water connections rose from 15% to 72% and sewer connections from 8% to 55%, a large improvement in services [1]. The fall in child diarrhoea, by more than half, suggests health benefits from better water and sanitation [1]. However, 45% of households still lack a sewer connection [1], and only 20% have legal title, so insecurity remains for most residents [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* "assess" needs a judgement: the scheme was successful for services and health but much less so for tenure.

**5. (a)** Journeys are shortest and cheapest in central district A (20 minutes, 4% of income) [1]. District C has by far the longest journeys, 95 minutes, almost five times A [1], and spends the highest share of income on travel, 18% [1].

**(b)** Steep slopes and narrow lanes mean buses cannot reach the settlement, so residents walk long distances or use informal transport [1]. It is far from a rail line, unlike district D, and lower incomes mean travel costs take a larger share of income [1]. **[5]**

**6.** Demand grows faster than generating capacity [1], so supply is rationed through planned power cuts that disrupt businesses and homes [1]. Many households make illegal connections [1], which are dangerous and mean the supplier loses revenue needed to invest in the network [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* also credit the cost of building new power stations or the pollution from fossil-fuel generation, if developed.

**7.** Credit: a named, located shanty town in an LIC or MIC [1]; lack of water, with detail such as shared taps or buying from vendors [1]; poor sanitation and its effect on health [1]; insecure tenure and the threat of eviction [1]; a hazard or problem of site, such as flooding or slopes [1]; specific data or place detail from the case study [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Community-led schemes keep residents in place, near their jobs and social networks [1], whereas clearance often moves people far from work [1]. Residents contribute labour and money, which lowers costs [1] and gives them ownership, so they are more likely to maintain what is built [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Credit: a named city and the type of infrastructure chosen [1]; growth of demand linked to population or car ownership [1]; physical challenges such as relief or density [1]; unequal access, with the poorest worst served [1]; cost or environmental challenges such as pollution [1]. **[5]**

**10.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. The points below are indicative creditworthy content.

*Introduction:* names the city and the scheme [1], and the challenge it aimed to solve [1].

*Description of solutions:* what was built and when [1], and who paid for it or ran it [1].

*Evidence of success:* reduced journey times or improved supply [1]; benefits for low-income groups [1]; environmental benefits, such as lower emissions [1].

*Limitations:* capacity or coverage limits [1]; costs, maintenance or unintended effects [1]; groups who did not benefit [1].

*Evaluation criteria:* reference to sustainability [1] and to whether the scheme has been copied elsewhere [1].

*Use of detail:* specific dates, figures or places from the case study [1].

*Judgement:* a clear overall view on success [1], supported by the evidence [1]. **[15]**

*Examiner insight:* for Medellín, a strong answer recognises that the Metrocable's success depended on wider investment in hillside districts, and that cable cars carry far fewer passengers than a metro line.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing a shanty town in a high income country.
- Generic answers with no named place or scheme.
- Describing what was built without evidence of its effects.
- Writing about power and transport together instead of one in depth.
- Assessing data without a final judgement.
