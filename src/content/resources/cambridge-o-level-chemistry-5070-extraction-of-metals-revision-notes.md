Condensed recall for Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 subtopic **9.6 Extraction of metals**. For explanations, redox analysis and worked calculations, use the [Extraction of metals study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-extraction-of-metals/).

## Three things to know

1. How easily a metal is extracted depends on its position in the **reactivity series**.
2. Iron: **blast furnace**, five steps with **symbol equations**.
3. Aluminium: **electrolysis** of aluminium oxide in **molten cryolite**; electrode half-equations; anodes replaced.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ore | Rock containing a metal compound from which the metal can be extracted economically |
| Hematite | Iron ore; mainly iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃ |
| Bauxite | Aluminium ore; purified to give aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃ |
| Reduction | Loss of oxygen / gain of electrons |
| Reducing agent | Substance that reduces another and is itself oxidised (CO in the blast furnace) |
| Slag | Calcium silicate, CaSiO₃, formed from limestone and sandy impurities |
| Cryolite | Compound in which aluminium oxide is dissolved to lower the operating temperature |
| Anode / cathode | Positive / negative electrode |

## Method by position

```
K Na Ca Mg Al | C | Zn Fe Cu | ... Au
electrolysis    |   carbon reduction  | found native
(above carbon)       (below carbon)
```

More reactive → harder to extract → needs electrolysis (expensive electricity).

## Blast furnace: raw materials

| Material | Job |
|---|---|
| Hematite | Provides iron(III) oxide |
| Coke | Burns for heat; forms carbon monoxide |
| Limestone | Decomposes to calcium oxide, which removes silicon dioxide |
| Hot air | Supplies oxygen to burn the coke |

## Blast furnace: the five equations

```
(a) C + O₂ → CO₂
(b) CO₂ + C → 2CO
(c) Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂
(d) CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂
(e) CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃
```

- (c): Fe₂O₃ **reduced**; CO **oxidised**; CO is the **reducing agent**.
- (d): thermal decomposition.
- (e): basic oxide + acidic oxide → slag (a salt). Slag floats on the molten iron and is run off.

## Aluminium: electrolysis

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Electrolyte | Aluminium oxide dissolved in molten cryolite |
| Role of cryolite | Lowers the operating temperature (Al₂O₃ alone melts above 2000 °C), saving energy and cost |
| Electrodes | Carbon (graphite) |
| Cathode (−) | Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al (reduction) |
| Anode (+) | 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻ (oxidation) |
| Anode problem | Hot oxygen reacts with carbon: C + O₂ → CO₂; anodes burn away and are replaced |
| Overall | 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂ |

## Calculation shortcuts

| Ore compound | Mr | Metal per 1000 kg |
|---|---|---|
| Fe₂O₃ | 160 | 1000 × 112 ÷ 160 = 700 kg |
| Al₂O₃ | 102 | 1000 × 54 ÷ 102 = 529 kg |

Method: Mr of compound, mass of metal in one formula, scale.

## Comparing the two extractions

| Feature | Iron | Aluminium |
|---|---|---|
| Position | Below carbon | Above carbon |
| Method | Reduction by carbon monoxide | Electrolysis |
| Reduction step | Fe₂O₃ loses oxygen | Al³⁺ gains electrons |
| Main cost | Coke and limestone | Electricity |
| Carbon's role | Fuel and source of reducing agent | Electrode material (anode burns away) |

## Model answers

**"Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced regularly." [3]**
Oxygen is produced at the anode [1]. At the high temperature it reacts with the carbon to form carbon dioxide (C + O₂ → CO₂) [1]. So the anodes burn away and become smaller [1].

**"State the role of cryolite." [2]**
Aluminium oxide is dissolved in molten cryolite, so the electrolyte is molten at a much lower temperature than aluminium oxide alone [1]. This saves energy and reduces cost [1].

**"Describe how the ease of extraction depends on reactivity." [2]**
The more reactive the metal, the harder it is to extract [1]. Metals above carbon need electrolysis; metals below carbon can be reduced by heating with carbon [1].

## Exam traps

- Coke is the **fuel**; carbon monoxide is the **reducing agent**.
- Limestone does **not** reduce anything; it forms calcium oxide for slag.
- Balance step (c) with **3CO** and **3CO₂**.
- Anode equation: **2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻**; cathode: **Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al**.
- Cryolite is **not a catalyst**.
- Aluminium forms at the **cathode**.
- Anodes are replaced because they **react with oxygen**, not because they dissolve.
- The syllabus spells the ore **hematite**; details of purifying bauxite are not required.

## Self-test

1. Explain why aluminium cannot be extracted by heating its oxide with carbon.
2. Name the main compound in hematite.
3. Write the equation for the burning of coke.
4. Write the equation for the reduction of carbon dioxide to carbon monoxide.
5. Write the equation for the reduction of iron(III) oxide.
6. Name the reducing agent in question 5.
7. Write the equation for the thermal decomposition of limestone.
8. Write the equation for the formation of slag.
9. State the role of cryolite.
10. Write the half-equation at the cathode in aluminium extraction.
11. Explain why the carbon anodes must be replaced.
12. Calculate the mass of iron from 320 kg of Fe₂O₃ (Fe = 56, O = 16).

**Answers:** 1. Aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so carbon cannot remove oxygen from aluminium oxide. 2. Iron(III) oxide, Fe₂O₃. 3. C + O₂ → CO₂. 4. CO₂ + C → 2CO. 5. Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. 6. Carbon monoxide. 7. CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂. 8. CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃. 9. Aluminium oxide is dissolved in it so the electrolyte is molten at a lower temperature, saving energy. 10. Al³⁺ + 3e⁻ → Al. 11. Oxygen formed at the anodes reacts with the hot carbon to form carbon dioxide, so the anodes burn away. 12. 320 × 112 ÷ 160 = 224 kg.
