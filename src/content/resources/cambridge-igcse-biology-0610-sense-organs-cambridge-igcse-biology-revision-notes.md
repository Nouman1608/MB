## Sense organs (Core)

**Definition:** groups of **receptor cells** responding to **specific stimuli**.

Five stimuli: **light, sound, touch, temperature, chemicals**.

## Eye structures and functions (Core)

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Cornea | refracts light |
| Iris | controls how much light enters the pupil |
| Pupil | opening through which light enters |
| Lens | focuses light on to the retina |
| Retina | contains light receptors, some sensitive to light of different colours |
| Optic nerve | carries impulses to the brain |
| Blind spot | no receptors, where the optic nerve leaves |

Light path: **cornea → pupil → lens → retina → (impulses) optic nerve → brain**.

## Pupil reflex

**Core:**

- Bright light → pupil diameter **decreases** → less light enters → retina protected.
- Dim light → pupil diameter **increases** → more light enters → enough light to see.

Reflex arc: retina receptors → sensory neurone → brain → motor neurone → iris muscles.

**Extended only: antagonistic iris muscles**

| | Circular | Radial | Pupil |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bright | contract | relax | constricts |
| Dim | relax | contract | dilates |

## Accommodation (Extended only)

Chain: **ciliary muscles → suspensory ligaments → lens → refraction**.

| | Near | Distant |
|---|---|---|
| Ciliary muscles | contract | relax |
| Suspensory ligaments | slacken | tighten |
| Lens | fatter | thinner |
| Refraction | more | less |

## Rods, cones and fovea (Extended only)

| | Rods | Cones |
|---|---|---|
| Where | most of retina, more at the edges; none at fovea | concentrated at fovea |
| Function | more sensitive: night vision | three kinds, different colours: colour vision |

**Fovea:** directly opposite the lens; gives the sharpest, most detailed colour image.

**Blind spot:** no rods and no cones.

## Worked reminders

**Pupil percentage change.** Diameter falls from 6.0 mm to 2.4 mm: (6.0 − 2.4) ÷ 6.0 × 100 = **60%** decrease. Divide by the starting value.

**Distant object (Extended).** Ciliary muscles relax → suspensory ligaments pulled tight → lens pulled thinner → less refraction → image focused on retina.

**Dim-light colour (Extended).** In dim light only rods respond, and rods do not give colour vision, so colours are hard to see.

**Looking to the side of a dim object (Extended).** Light falls outside the fovea, where there are rods, which are more sensitive.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Iris vs pupil:** the iris is the muscle; the pupil is the hole it controls.
- **Cornea vs lens:** both refract; only the lens changes shape to focus (Extended).
- **Ciliary muscles vs suspensory ligaments (Extended):** muscles contract or relax; ligaments only change in tension.
- **Fovea vs blind spot (Extended):** packed with cones compared with no receptors at all.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Receptor cell | Cell that detects a specific stimulus |
| Refraction | Bending of light as it passes into a different material |
| Pupil reflex | Automatic change in pupil diameter in response to light intensity |
| Antagonistic muscles | Pair of muscles with opposite effects; one contracts as the other relaxes (Extended) |
| Accommodation | Changing lens shape to focus near or distant objects (Extended) |
| Ciliary muscles | Ring of muscle that changes tension in the suspensory ligaments (Extended) |
| Suspensory ligaments | Fibres holding the lens, transmitting tension (Extended) |
| Rod | Light receptor sensitive to dim light, for night vision (Extended) |
| Cone | Light receptor for colour vision; three kinds (Extended) |
| Fovea | Part of the retina opposite the lens, packed with cones (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Only the lens refracting; the cornea refracts too.
- "The iris lets light in" (the pupil does; the iris controls its size).
- Pupil reflex without a reason.
- (Extended) Muscle actions swapped in bright and dim light.
- (Extended) "Ligaments contract."
- (Extended) Thinner lens for near objects.
- (Extended) Rods at the fovea; rods detecting colour.
- (Extended) "Three cones" without saying they absorb light of different colours.

## Command words

- **Identify**: exact structure names.
- **Describe** a function: one syllabus verb each.
- **Explain** the pupil reflex or accommodation: full chain in order, cause to effect.
- **Outline** rods and cones: one function each.

## Self-test

1. Name the five stimuli in the syllabus description of sense organs.
2. State the function of the cornea.
3. State the function of the retina.
4. Why is no image detected at the blind spot?
5. What happens to the pupil in dim light, and why is this useful?
6. Name the effector in the pupil reflex.
7. (Extended) Which iris muscles contract in bright light?
8. (Extended) Describe the ciliary muscles and suspensory ligaments when looking at a near object.
9. (Extended) What happens to the lens shape when looking at a distant object?
10. (Extended) State the function of rods.
11. (Extended) Where are cones most concentrated, and what is that area's function?
12. (Extended) How many kinds of cone are there, and how do they differ?

### Answers

1. Light, sound, touch, temperature, chemicals.
2. It refracts light.
3. It contains light receptors, some sensitive to light of different colours.
4. There are no light receptors there (it is where the optic nerve leaves).
5. It widens (diameter increases), so more light enters and enough reaches the retina to see.
6. The muscles of the iris.
7. Circular muscles (radial muscles relax).
8. Ciliary muscles contract; suspensory ligaments slacken.
9. It becomes thinner.
10. They are more sensitive to light, giving night vision.
11. In the fovea; it gives the sharpest, most detailed colour image.
12. Three; each absorbs light of different colours.
