Condensed recall for **4.3 Population-resource relationships** (food security, carrying capacity, optimum population). For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Food security | Reliable access to enough safe, nutritious food |
| Carrying capacity | Maximum population an environment can support indefinitely |
| Optimum population | Size giving the highest standard of living with available resources and technology |
| Overpopulation | More people than resources and technology can support at a good standard of living |
| Underpopulation | Fewer people than needed to use resources fully |
| Malnutrition | Poor diet: too little, or lacking nutrients |
| Famine | Extreme food shortage with high death rates |

## Causes of food shortage

- Drought, flood, pests and other climatic hazards;
- war and conflict, which disrupt farming and distribution;
- poverty and lack of access, even when food exists;
- poor infrastructure and storage losses;
- population growth outpacing production;
- soil degradation and water shortage.

## Consequences

Malnutrition, disease, higher mortality, migration, political unrest, and reduced economic output.

## Technology and innovation

Irrigation, fertilisers, improved seed varieties (green revolution), mechanisation and better storage can raise yields. Costs include environmental damage and unequal access.

## Theories

| Theory | Idea | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Malthus | Population grows geometrically, food arithmetically; checks result | Underestimated technology |
| Boserup | Pressure on resources stimulates innovation and intensification | Intensification can harm the environment |

## Exam traps

- Optimum population changes as technology and resources change.
- Overpopulation is about balance with resources, not density alone.
- Food shortage is not always a shortage of food: distribution and income matter.
- Evaluate: both Malthus and Boserup have supporting and opposing evidence.

## Self-test

1. Define carrying capacity.
2. Give two causes of food shortage.
3. Summarise Malthus.
4. Summarise Boserup.
5. What is optimum population?
6. Why can technology raise carrying capacity?

**Answers:** 1. Maximum population an environment can support indefinitely. 2. Drought, conflict, poverty, poor storage. 3. Population grows faster than food supply, so checks limit it. 4. Population pressure stimulates innovation and higher food output. 5. Number giving the highest standard of living for the resources and technology. 6. It increases output from the same resources.
