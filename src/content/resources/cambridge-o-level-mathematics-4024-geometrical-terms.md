This guide covers section 4.1, Geometrical terms, in the Geometry topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The section is about vocabulary. It asks you to **use and interpret** terms such as vertex, bisector and bearing, the names of triangles, special quadrilaterals, polygons, solids and nets, and the parts of a circle. The syllabus notes add that candidates are not expected to show that two shapes are congruent, although they need to know what the word means. A condensed list is in the [geometry revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-geometry-revision-notes/), and questions are in the [geometrical terms practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-geometrical-terms-practice/).

## Why vocabulary matters

Geometry questions rely on exact words. A question may say "the perpendicular bisector of AB" or "the tangent at T" and expect you to draw the correct picture before you start. In angle questions you must also give reasons in correct language. Learn the terms below until you can sketch each one without hesitation.

## Points, lines and planes

- A **point** has a position but no size.
- A **line** is a straight, endless path. A **line segment** is the part between two points.
- A **plane** is a flat surface that goes on forever, such as a table top extended in all directions.
- A **vertex** is a corner where two or more edges meet. The plural is vertices.
- **Parallel** lines stay the same distance apart and never meet. They are marked with matching arrows.
- **Perpendicular** lines meet at a right angle.
- A **perpendicular bisector** of a line segment cuts it in half and meets it at 90°.

## Angles

| Name | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | more than 0° and less than 90° |
| Right angle | exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | more than 90° and less than 180° |
| Straight angle | exactly 180° |
| Reflex | more than 180° and less than 360° |

An angle is named with three letters, such as angle ABC, where the middle letter is the vertex. A **bearing** is an angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures (for example 045° or 270°). Bearings are developed in the scale drawings and trigonometry sections.

In a polygon, an **interior angle** is inside the shape at a vertex. An **exterior angle** is formed outside by extending one side. At each vertex, the interior and exterior angles lie on a straight line, so they add up to 180°.

## Similar, congruent and scale factor

- **Congruent** shapes are exactly the same shape and size. One can be moved, turned or flipped onto the other.
- **Similar** shapes have the same shape but may be different sizes. All corresponding angles are equal and corresponding sides are in the same ratio.
- The **scale factor** is the ratio of corresponding lengths. If a triangle's sides are doubled, the scale factor is 2.

Congruent shapes are the special case of similar shapes where the scale factor is 1.

## Triangles

| Triangle | Property |
|---|---|
| Equilateral | three equal sides, three angles of 60° |
| Isosceles | two equal sides, and the two angles opposite them are equal |
| Scalene | no equal sides and no equal angles |
| Right-angled | one angle of 90°; the longest side is the hypotenuse |

Marks on a diagram show equal sides (small dashes) and equal angles (small arcs).

## Special quadrilaterals

A quadrilateral has four sides. The angles always sum to 360°.

| Shape | Sides | Angles | Diagonals | Line symmetry | Rotational symmetry |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal sides, opposite sides parallel | four right angles | equal, bisect each other at 90° | 4 | order 4 |
| Rectangle | opposite sides equal and parallel | four right angles | equal, bisect each other | 2 | order 2 |
| Rhombus | 4 equal sides, opposite sides parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other at 90°, bisect the angles | 2 | order 2 |
| Parallelogram | opposite sides equal and parallel | opposite angles equal | bisect each other | 0 | order 2 |
| Kite | two pairs of adjacent equal sides | one pair of opposite angles equal | meet at 90°, one bisects the other | 1 | order 1 |
| Trapezium | one pair of parallel sides | co-interior angles between the parallel sides add to 180° | no special rule | 0 (1 if isosceles) | order 1 |

Check which shape is a special case of another. A square is a rectangle, a rhombus and a parallelogram. A rectangle is a parallelogram. A rhombus is a parallelogram.

## Polygons

A **polygon** is a closed shape with straight sides. A **regular** polygon has all sides equal and all angles equal. An **irregular** polygon does not.

| Sides | Name |
|---|---|
| 5 | pentagon |
| 6 | hexagon |
| 8 | octagon |
| 10 | decagon |

The angle sum of an n-sided polygon is (n − 2) × 180°. For a regular polygon, each interior angle is that sum divided by n, and each exterior angle is 360° ÷ n.

### Worked example 1

Find the interior and exterior angles of a regular octagon.

```
angle sum = (8 − 2) × 180 = 1080°
each interior angle = 1080 ÷ 8 = 135°
each exterior angle = 360 ÷ 8 = 45°
check: 135 + 45 = 180
```

The same method gives 108° (interior) and 72° (exterior) for a pentagon, 120° and 60° for a hexagon, and 144° and 36° for a decagon.

### Worked example 2

Each exterior angle of a regular polygon is 24°. How many sides does it have?

```
number of sides = 360 ÷ 24 = 15
each interior angle = 180 − 24 = 156°
```

## Solids and nets

A **solid** is a three-dimensional shape. Its flat surfaces are **faces**. An **edge** is where two faces meet, and a corner is a vertex. The outside is its **surface**.

| Solid | Description |
|---|---|
| Cube | six square faces |
| Cuboid | six rectangular faces |
| Prism | uniform cross-section; sides are rectangles |
| Cylinder | circular cross-section, one curved surface and two circular faces |
| Pyramid | a polygon base and triangular faces meeting at one apex |
| Cone | circular base and a curved surface to an apex |
| Sphere | all points the same distance from the centre |
| Hemisphere | half a sphere |
| Frustum | a cone or pyramid with the top cut off by a parallel plane |

A **net** is a flat pattern that folds into a solid. A cube has several different nets, all made of six squares. Nets are covered further in the constructions section.

For solids with flat faces, count faces (F), vertices (V) and edges (E). They satisfy V − E + F = 2.

### Worked example 3

A triangular prism has 5 faces and 6 vertices. How many edges has it?

```
V − E + F = 2
6 − E + 5 = 2
E = 9
```

Check by counting: the two triangles contribute 3 + 3 edges, and 3 more edges join them. That is 9.

A pentagonal pyramid has 6 faces (one pentagon and five triangles) and 6 vertices, so E = 6 + 6 − 2 = 10.

## The vocabulary of a circle

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centre | the fixed point in the middle |
| Radius (plural radii) | a line from the centre to the circumference |
| Diameter | a chord through the centre, equal to twice the radius |
| Circumference | the distance around the circle, also the name of the curve |
| Semicircle | half a circle, cut by a diameter |
| Chord | a straight line joining two points on the circumference |
| Tangent | a straight line touching the circle at one point only |
| Major arc | the longer arc between two points |
| Minor arc | the shorter arc between two points |
| Sector | a region bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Segment | a region bounded by a chord and an arc |

Do not mix up a sector (a slice of pizza) with a segment (a cap cut off by a chord).

## Common mistakes

- Calling any four-sided shape with parallel sides a parallelogram without checking, or forgetting a square is a rhombus.
- Saying a trapezium needs two pairs of parallel sides. It has exactly one pair.
- Mixing up radius and diameter.
- Confusing a segment with a sector.
- Thinking congruent means "the same shape" only; it also needs the same size.
- Giving a bearing with fewer than three figures, such as 45° instead of 045°.

## Exam technique

- **Name** or **state** questions need the precise term, with no description.
- **Describe** questions need the key features, for example "a rhombus has four equal sides".
- Label diagrams as you read, so that vertices, bisectors and tangents are all visible.
