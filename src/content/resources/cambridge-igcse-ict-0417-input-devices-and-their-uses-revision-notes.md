Section 2.1 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 asks for the characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of these input devices: keyboard, numeric keypad, pointing devices, remote control, joystick/driving wheel, touch screen (as input), scanners, camera, microphone, sensors and light pen.

Condensed for revision. For the full device-by-device explanations and a model answer, use the [input devices study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-input-devices-and-their-uses/), then try the [input devices practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-input-devices-and-their-uses-practice/).

## Device summary

| Device | Typical use | One advantage | One disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keyboard | Text and data entry | Fast for trained typists | RSI with prolonged use |
| Numeric keypad | PINs, till quantities, alarms | Fast number entry in small space | PIN can be seen by others |
| Mouse | Selecting in a GUI | Fast, accurate pointing | Needs a flat surface |
| Touchpad | Pointing on a laptop | Built in, nothing to carry | Less precise than a mouse |
| Trackerball | Users with limited movement; control rooms | Stays still; less arm movement | Extra cost; needs practice |
| Remote control | TVs, projectors, air conditioning | Operate from a distance | Infrared needs line of sight |
| Joystick / driving wheel | Games, flight and driving simulators | Realistic, continuous control | Poor for selecting menu items or text |
| Touch screen (input) | Phones, kiosks, self-service tills | Intuitive; no loose parts | Dirt, germs; slow for long text |
| Scanner | Digitising photos and documents; 3D scanning objects | Avoids retyping or redrawing | Large files; quality depends on original |
| Camera / webcam | Photos, video, video-conferencing | Images transferred instantly | Large files; privacy risk with webcams |
| Microphone | Calls, recording, voice commands, speech to text | Hands-free | Background noise causes errors |
| Sensors | Monitoring and control systems | Continuous, automatic, accurate readings | Can fail or give false readings |
| Light pen | Drawing or selecting on suitable screens | More precise than a finger | Tiring; blocks view of screen |

## Sensors: match the sensor to the job

| Sensor | Example job |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Central heating, greenhouse, patient monitoring |
| Light | Automatic street lights, greenhouse lighting |
| Pressure | Burglar alarm mats, traffic counting |
| Moisture | Soil in a greenhouse |
| Humidity | Greenhouses, museums, weather stations |
| pH | Water quality, soil acidity |
| Infrared / motion | Burglar alarms, automatic doors |
| Gas | Pollution monitoring, gas leak detection |
| Sound | Noise monitoring, alarms |

Sensors only **measure**. The computer or microprocessor compares the reading with a set value and decides what to do.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Input device | Hardware that sends data or instructions into a computer |
| QWERTY | Standard keyboard layout named after the first six letter keys |
| Ergonomic | Designed to reduce strain and injury |
| Pointing device | Moves an on-screen pointer: mouse, touchpad, trackerball |
| Infrared | Invisible light used by many remote controls; needs line of sight |
| Resolution | Level of detail captured, for example by a scanner or camera |
| Webcam | Camera built into or attached to a computer, used mainly for video calls |
| Sensor | Device that measures a physical quantity and sends readings automatically |
| Speech recognition | Software that converts spoken words into text or commands |

## Exam traps

- A touch screen is input **and** output; say which role you are describing.
- A scanner produces an image, not editable text. OCR software is needed for that.
- A sensor does not switch anything on; an actuator does that after the computer decides.
- "Easy to use" is not an advantage unless you say why (for example, "no training needed because users touch what they see").
- Name the most specific device the scenario describes, such as trackerball rather than mouse.
- Microphones produce analogue signals that must be converted to digital.

## Self-test

1. Give **one** use of a numeric keypad and **one** disadvantage.
2. Name **three** pointing devices.
3. Why might a trackerball suit a user with limited arm movement?
4. Give **one** disadvantage of an infrared remote control.
5. Give **two** uses of a joystick or driving wheel.
6. Give **two** disadvantages of a touch screen at a public kiosk.
7. What does a 3D scanner capture?
8. Give **one** privacy concern about webcams.
9. Give **two** disadvantages of using a microphone for speech recognition.
10. Name the sensor used in a greenhouse to decide when to water the soil.
11. Give **two** advantages of using sensors rather than human readings.
12. Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of a light pen.

## Self-test answers

1. Use: entering a PIN, quantities at a till, alarm codes. Disadvantage: small keys cause errors, or the PIN can be seen by onlookers.
2. Mouse, touchpad, trackerball.
3. The device stays still and only the ball is moved by the fingers or thumb, so little arm movement is needed.
4. It needs a clear line of sight, so the signal can be blocked; or it is easily lost; or batteries need replacing.
5. Any two: games, flight simulators, driving simulators, controlling robotic arms or machinery.
6. Any two: dirt and fingerprints; spreads germs; limited options on screen; screen can be damaged.
7. The shape of a solid object, as digital data.
8. It could be accessed without permission, letting someone watch the user.
9. Background noise causes errors; accents or unclear speech cause errors; sound files are large.
10. Moisture sensor.
11. Any two: continuous readings; no person needs to be present; more accurate; can be used in dangerous places; no recording errors.
12. Advantage: more precise than a finger, or natural for drawing. Disadvantage: works only on suitable screens, tiring, or blocks the view.
