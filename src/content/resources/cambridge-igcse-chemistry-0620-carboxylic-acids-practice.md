## Answers

**1.** All atoms and all bonds shown, including C=O and O–H [1]:

```
    H   O
    |   ||
H — C — C — O — H
    |
    H
```

**2.** **B** [1]. Mg²⁺ needs two CH₃COO⁻ ions.

**3.** Potassium ethanoate [1]; CH₃COOK [1].
*K⁺ has a 1+ charge, so one ethanoate ion balances it and no bracket is needed.*

**4.** Any two [2]: effervescence / bubbles; the solid dissolves / gets smaller; the gas turns limewater milky.

**5.** Propanoic acid [1].

**6.** **B** [1].

**7.** Propyl ethanoate [1].
*Split at –COO–: CH₃CO– (two carbons, with C=O) is the ethanoate part; –CH₂CH₂CH₃ (three carbons) is the propyl part, from propan-1-ol.*

**8.** **(a)** Hydrogen [1]; it pops with a lighted splint [1].

**(b)** 2CH₃COOH + Zn → (CH₃COO)₂Zn + H₂. Correct formula of zinc ethanoate [1]; balanced with 2CH₃COOH [1].

**9.** **(a)** ethanoic acid + sodium carbonate → sodium ethanoate + water + carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂. Correct formulae [1]; correct balancing [1].

**10.** **(a)** The black solid dissolves / disappears [1]; a blue solution forms [1].

**(b)** Copper(II) ethanoate [1]; (CH₃COO)₂Cu [1].
*The equation is 2CH₃COOH + CuO → (CH₃COO)₂Cu + H₂O. Copper(II) oxide is a base, so the products are a salt and water only: there is no fizzing.*

**11.** **(a)** Ethanoic acid [1].

**(b)** Bacteria oxidise the ethanol [1].

**(c)** The bacterial oxidation needs oxygen from the air; in a sealed bottle no fresh air (oxygen) can get in [1].

**12.** **(a)** An acid (catalyst) [1]. *Accept concentrated sulfuric acid.*

**(b)** Methyl ethanoate [1]; CH₃COOCH₃ [1].

**(c)** ethanoic acid + methanol → methyl ethanoate + water [1].

**13.** **(a)** Ethyl methanoate [1].

**(b)** Methanoic acid [1]; ethanol [1].
*Split at –COO–: HCO– (one carbon, with C=O) is the acid part; –CH₂CH₃ (two carbons) is the alcohol part.*

**14.** **(a)** Ethanoic acid [1].

**(b)** Ethanoic acid is a weak acid, only partially dissociated in water [1], so it has a lower concentration of H⁺ ions than hydrochloric acid, which is fully dissociated, and reacts more slowly [1].
*Do not credit "ethanoic acid is more dilute": the concentrations are the same.*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing CH₃COOMg or CH₃COOZn without the bracket and the 2.
- Forgetting the 2 in front of CH₃COOH when balancing reactions with 2+ metals and with carbonates.
- Giving water instead of hydrogen as a product of the reaction with a metal.
- Leaving out "acidified" from potassium manganate(VII), or reversing the colour change.
- Naming esters backwards: CH₃COOCH₃ is methyl ethanoate, not ethyl methanoate.
- Confusing "weak" (partially dissociated) with "dilute" (low concentration).

## Method for the two most common question types

**Salt name and formula.** Name the metal, then add "ethanoate" (or methanoate, propanoate, butanoate for other acids). For the formula, write the metal ion charge: 1+ metals take one CH₃COO⁻, 2+ metals take two, written (CH₃COO)₂M. Then balance the equation by putting the matching number of acid molecules on the left.

**Ester name** *(Extended)*. If you are given the acid and alcohol, the alcohol becomes the first word ending in -yl and the acid becomes the second word ending in -oate. If you are given a formula, split it at –COO–: the fragment containing C=O, counted including that carbon, is the acid; the other fragment is the alcohol.
