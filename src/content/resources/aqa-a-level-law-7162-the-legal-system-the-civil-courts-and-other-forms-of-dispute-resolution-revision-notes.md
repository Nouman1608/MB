Condensed notes on **The legal system: the civil courts and other forms of dispute resolution** in AQA A-level Law (7162) section 3.1: civil courts, the track system and the appeal system; the tribunal structure and role; and mediation and negotiation. For full explanation and a worked scenario, see the [Civil Courts and Dispute Resolution study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-the-legal-system-the-civil-courts-and-other-forms-of-dispute-resolution/).

## First-instance civil courts

- **County Court:** most civil claims; district and circuit judges.
- **High Court:** King's Bench (contract, tort, Commercial Court), Chancery (business, property, trusts, insolvency, probate), Family.
- **Starting point:** High Court only for money claims over £100,000 (over £50,000 for personal injury); otherwise County Court.

## Stages of a claim

1. Pre-action protocol: exchange information, try to settle, consider alternatives.
2. Claim form and particulars; court fee; often online.
3. Defendant admits, defends or does nothing (default judgment).
4. Allocation to a track.
5. Case management: disclosure, witness statements, experts; trial.
6. Enforcement if unpaid.

**Framework:** Civil Procedure Rules 1998, after Woolf's *Access to Justice* (1996). **Overriding objective:** deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost.

## Tracks

| Track | Value | Key features |
|---|---|---|
| Small claims | ≤ £10,000 (personal injury generally ≤ £1,000; road traffic injury ≤ £5,000; housing disrepair ≤ £1,000) | District judge; informal; self-representation common; costs generally not recoverable; mediation service for most defended money claims |
| Fast | £10,000 – £25,000 | One-day trial; limited experts; fixed recoverable costs |
| Intermediate (from October 2023) | £25,000 – £100,000 | Up to three-day trial; fixed recoverable costs |
| Multi | > £100,000 or complex | Judge-managed; County Court or High Court |

**Allocation factors besides value:** complexity of facts and law; number of parties; amount of oral evidence; importance to non-parties; parties' views.

## Appeals

- **Permission** needed; ground: decision **wrong** or **unjust through serious procedural irregularity**; not a rehearing.
- **Routes:** district judge → circuit judge → High Court judge; High Court → Court of Appeal (Civil Division) → Supreme Court.
- **Second appeals:** important point of principle or practice, or other compelling reason.
- **Leapfrog:** High Court → Supreme Court on points of general public importance.

## Tribunals

- **Role:** specialist disputes, often individual v state (benefits, immigration, tax), or employment.
- **History:** Franks Report (1957): openness, fairness, impartiality. Leggatt Review (2001): fragmented system → Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007.
- **First-tier Tribunal chambers:** Social Entitlement; Immigration and Asylum; Tax; Property; General Regulatory; Health, Education and Social Care; War Pensions and Armed Forces Compensation.
- **Upper Tribunal chambers:** Administrative Appeals; Immigration and Asylum; Tax and Chancery; Lands. Appeals on points of law; some judicial review.
- **Then:** Court of Appeal with permission.
- **Employment:** Employment Tribunals and Employment Appeal Tribunal, outside the two tiers; ACAS early conciliation first.
- **Leadership and panels:** Senior President of Tribunals; tribunal judge, sometimes with specialist members.

| For tribunals | Against |
|---|---|
| Cheap; costs rarely awarded | Legal aid rarely available |
| Quick | Backlogs |
| Specialist | Users may still find them intimidating |
| Informal | Little binding precedent at first tier |

## Mediation

- Neutral mediator helps parties reach their **own** agreement; no imposed decision.
- *Dunnett v Railtrack* (2002): costs denied for refusing to consider mediation.
- *Halsey v Milton Keynes General NHS Trust* (2004): courts could not compel, but could penalise unreasonable refusal.
- *Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil CBC* (2023): courts **can** order non-court dispute resolution if proportionate and fair-trial rights are preserved.
- Uses: small claims mediation; neighbour, business, family (mediation information and assessment meeting before most children and finance applications).
- For: cheap, quick, private, flexible, preserves relationships. Against: may not settle; power imbalance; no precedent.

## Negotiation

- Parties or lawyers settle directly; most civil claims settle before trial.
- **Part 36 offers:** rejecting an offer then failing to beat it at trial brings costs consequences.
- For: cheapest, private, full control, any time. Against: unequal bargaining power; no neutral help.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pre-action protocol | Steps parties should take before issuing a claim |
| Allocation | Assigning a defended claim to a track |
| Overriding objective | Dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost |
| Permission to appeal | Leave required before an appeal can proceed |
| Leapfrog appeal | Appeal straight from the High Court to the Supreme Court |
| First-tier Tribunal | First-instance tribunal, organised in chambers |
| Upper Tribunal | Appellate tribunal hearing points of law |
| Mediation | Neutral-assisted settlement by the parties themselves |
| Part 36 offer | Formal settlement offer with costs consequences |

## Exam traps

- Value treated as the only allocation factor.
- High Court said to have four divisions.
- Appeal described as an automatic rehearing.
- Tribunals described as a form of mediation.
- Mediator said to decide the dispute.
- *Halsey* cited as current law on compulsion without *Churchill*.

## Self-test

1. Name the three divisions of the High Court.
2. Above what value may a non-injury money claim be started in the High Court?
3. What is the overriding objective of the Civil Procedure Rules?
4. Which track hears a £60,000 claim, and how long is its trial?
5. State three allocation factors other than value.
6. What must be shown for a civil appeal to succeed?
7. What is the test for a second appeal?
8. Which report led to the Tribunals, Courts and Enforcement Act 2007?
9. Name two chambers of the Upper Tribunal.
10. What must most employment claimants do before claiming?
11. How did *Churchill* change *Halsey*?
12. What is a Part 36 offer and why does it encourage settlement?

**Answers:** 1. King's Bench, Chancery and Family. 2. £100,000. 3. To deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost. 4. The intermediate track, if it meets the conditions; trial of up to three days. 5. Any three: complexity of facts and law; number of parties; amount of oral evidence; importance to non-parties; parties' views. 6. That the decision was wrong, or unjust because of a serious procedural irregularity. 7. An important point of principle or practice, or some other compelling reason. 8. The Leggatt Review (2001). 9. Any two: Administrative Appeals; Immigration and Asylum; Tax and Chancery; Lands. 10. Contact ACAS for early conciliation. 11. *Halsey* said courts could not compel mediation; *Churchill* held they can order non-court dispute resolution if proportionate and consistent with fair-trial rights. 12. A formal settlement offer; a party who rejects it and fails to do better at trial faces costs consequences, so parties have an incentive to accept reasonable offers.
