# Allah in Himself: passages 1 to 5 of Appendix 1

This is the first of the three groups in Appendix 1. Related pages: [Major Themes of the Qur'an study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran/) and [revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-islamiyat-major-themes-of-the-quran-revision-notes/).

## How Question 1 is set (applies to every passage below)

The syllabus (Paper 1, Question 1) says three passages are set from Appendix 1 and candidates may choose any two, for 8 marks. Section 5.1 says candidates explore (1) the major theme or themes in each passage and (2) the importance of those themes in the lives of Muslims today, and must show knowledge of the original Arabic as well as the translation. The site's own record of the 2058/11 June 2024 paper (decision log E697) gives the wording "(a) themes [4], (b) importance in a Muslim's life today [4]". The 4 and 4 split is **not re-verified against the paper in this draft**.

**Answer pattern for each passage you choose:** (1) name the theme in the passage's own terms; (2) point to the verse number or Arabic word that shows it; (3) for part (b), give one concrete way a Muslim acts or thinks differently today because of it. Do not narrate the passage line by line.

## 1. Sura 2.255

**Syllabus group:** Allah in himself (Appendix 1, passage 1)

**Themes it addresses:** God's life and self-sufficiency, His ownership of everything in the heavens and earth, the limits of intercession (only by His permission), the limits of anyone's knowledge compared with His, His throne, and His unwearied care of creation.

**Link to the group theme:** The verse describes who God is in Himself with no human action in it; every phrase is about His nature, so it fits the group directly.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** al-Hayy (the Living), al-Qayyum (the Self-subsisting), kursi (usually rendered Throne), shafa'a (intercession).

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Pick two or three ideas (for example: He never sleeps or tires; all things belong to Him; no one intercedes without His permission). Tie each to a clause in the verse. For part (b) link to trust in God and to how Muslims use the verse in daily life.

## 2. Sura 6.101-103

**Syllabus group:** Allah in himself (Appendix 1, passage 2)

**Themes it addresses:** God as Creator of all things; rejection of the idea that God has a son or consort; His full knowledge; worship belongs to Him alone; no vision can grasp Him though He grasps all vision.

**Link to the group theme:** Verses 101-103 define God's uniqueness and unlimited knowledge and power; the denial of a son and consort connects to tawhid.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** khaliq (Creator), wakil (disposer of affairs), tawhid, shirk.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today. Because it holds several distinct themes, a stronger answer separates them (creation, uniqueness, knowledge, beyond human comprehension).

**How to answer:** Give each theme its own sentence with the verse number (101, 102 or 103). In (b), connect worship of God alone and the denial of partners to a Muslim's beliefs and conduct today.

## 3. Sura 41.37

**Syllabus group:** Allah in himself (Appendix 1, passage 3)

**Themes it addresses:** The night, day, sun and moon are among God's signs; they are created and must not be worshipped; worship belongs to God who created them.

**Link to the group theme:** The syllabus places it under Allah in Himself because it separates the Creator from created things, which is tawhid applied to nature.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** aya (sign; plural ayat), sujud (prostration/worship), shams, qamar.

**Arabic (copied from the site's existing Paper 1 practice page, which records it as Tanzil text checked against the syllabus appendix; not re-checked here):**

> وَمِنْ آيَاتِهِ اللَّيْلُ وَالنَّهَارُ وَالشَّمْسُ وَالْقَمَرُ لَا تَسْجُدُوا لِلشَّمْسِ وَلَا لِلْقَمَرِ وَاسْجُدُوا لِلَّهِ الَّذِي خَلَقَهُنَّ إِن كُنتُمْ إِيَّاهُ تَعْبُدُونَ

**How the exam asks about it:** The decision log (E697) records this passage as set in the 2058/11 June 2024 paper; that is a site record, not checked against the paper for this draft. As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Name the theme (only God is to be worshipped), point to the signs the verse names, and say why creation points to the Creator and is not itself to be worshipped. In (b) say how a Muslim can value nature without worshipping it.

## 4. Sura 42.4-5

**Syllabus group:** Allah in himself (Appendix 1, passage 4)

**Themes it addresses:** Everything in the heavens and earth belongs to God; He is most high and most great; the angels praise Him and ask forgiveness for beings on earth; He is forgiving and merciful.

**Link to the group theme:** It describes God's greatness and ownership (verse 4) and His forgiveness and mercy (verse 5), which are attributes of God in Himself; the angels show the response.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** al-'Aliyy (most high), al-'Azim (most great), al-Ghafur (oft-forgiving), al-Rahim (most merciful).

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Use two themes, greatness/ownership and forgiveness/mercy, each pinned to verse 4 or 5. In (b), link forgiveness and mercy to why a Muslim keeps turning to God after wrongdoing.

## 5. Sura 112

**Syllabus group:** Allah in himself (Appendix 1, passage 5)

**Themes it addresses:** The absolute oneness of God; His eternal, absolute nature; that He neither has offspring nor is born; that nothing is like Him.

**Link to the group theme:** The standard statement of tawhid; the clearest of the five for the group theme.

**Key Arabic terms to learn (transliteration):** ahad (one), al-samad (the eternal/absolute, depended on by all), tawhid, shirk.

**How the exam asks about it:** As one of three passages printed in Arabic and English, you may choose it. Part (a) asks for its main theme or themes; part (b) asks why those themes matter to Muslims today.

**How to answer:** Take the meaning of each verse, not just its order: one, self-sufficient, no family, no equal. In (b) write about tawhid shaping worship and rejecting shirk.
