These notes condense section **14.1 Homeostasis in mammals** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. Explanations and worked examples are in the [14.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-mammals-cambridge-alevel-biology/); exam-style questions are in the [14.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-mammals-cambridge-alevel-biology-practice/).

## Core recall

### Principles

- **Homeostasis**: keeping the internal environment within narrow limits despite internal and external changes.
- Loop: stimulus → receptor → coordination (nervous or endocrine) → effector (muscle or gland) → response.
- **Negative feedback**: the response reverses the original change.
- Key variables: core temperature, blood glucose, blood water potential.

### Urea

- Made in the **liver** by **deamination** of excess amino acids.
- Carried in blood plasma to the kidneys for excretion.

### Kidney, outside to inside

Fibrous capsule → cortex → medulla → renal pelvis → ureter. Blood: branches of renal artery in, branches of renal vein out.

### Nephron route

Glomerulus in Bowman's capsule → PCT → loop of Henle → DCT → collecting duct.

- Cortex: glomeruli, Bowman's capsules, PCT, DCT.
- Medulla: loops of Henle, collecting ducts.

### Ultrafiltration

- Afferent arteriole wider than efferent → high hydrostatic pressure in glomerulus.
- Filter layers: endothelium with pores → **basement membrane (the filter)** → podocytes with filtration slits.
- Filtrate: water, glucose, amino acids, urea, ions. Not: cells, most proteins.

### Selective reabsorption (PCT)

1. Na⁺/K⁺ pumps in basal membrane remove Na⁺ from cell (ATP).
2. Na⁺ enters from lumen by cotransporters, bringing glucose or amino acids.
3. Glucose and amino acids leave into blood by facilitated diffusion.
4. Water follows by osmosis.

PCT adaptations: microvilli, many mitochondria, basal infoldings, close to capillaries.

### Osmoregulation

| Blood water potential falls | Blood water potential rises |
|---|---|
| Osmoreceptors in hypothalamus stimulated | Osmoreceptors less stimulated |
| Posterior pituitary releases more ADH | Less ADH released |
| Aquaporins inserted into collecting duct membranes | Aquaporins removed into vesicles |
| More water reabsorbed by osmosis | Less water reabsorbed |
| Small volume of concentrated urine | Large volume of dilute urine |

### Glucagon signalling (six links)

Receptor (conformational change) → G-protein → adenylyl cyclase → cAMP (second messenger) → protein kinase A → enzyme cascade (amplification by phosphorylation) → glycogen broken down.

### Blood glucose control

- **High glucose** → β cells → insulin → muscle cells take up more glucose (more GLUT4 in membrane) and make glycogen; liver cells take up glucose and make glycogen → glucose falls.
- **Low glucose** → α cells → glucagon → liver breaks down glycogen and makes glucose from other compounds → glucose rises.

### Measuring glucose

- Glucose oxidase: glucose + oxygen → product + **hydrogen peroxide**.
- Peroxidase: hydrogen peroxide + colourless chromogen → **coloured compound**.
- Test strip: colour compared with chart.
- Biosensor: immobilised glucose oxidase on electrode → current → digital reading.

### Structure to function, in one line each

| Structure | Feature | Function it serves |
|---|---|---|
| Glomerulus | Many capillaries; wide afferent, narrow efferent arteriole | Large area and high pressure for ultrafiltration |
| Bowman's capsule | Podocytes with filtration slits | Filtrate passes easily into the capsule |
| PCT | Microvilli and basal infoldings | Large area for transporters and pumps |
| PCT | Many mitochondria | ATP for Na⁺/K⁺ pumps |
| Collecting duct | Cells with ADH receptors and aquaporin vesicles | Variable water reabsorption |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Set point | The value around which a variable is kept |
| Deamination | Removal of the amino group from an amino acid |
| Ultrafiltration | Filtration under pressure that separates small molecules from large ones and cells |
| Podocyte | Capsule wall cell with extensions that leave filtration slits |
| Cotransporter | Membrane protein moving two substances together, such as Na⁺ with glucose |
| Aquaporin | Channel protein that lets water cross a membrane |
| ADH | Antidiuretic hormone, released from the posterior pituitary, increases water reabsorption |
| Second messenger | A molecule inside the cell (here cAMP) that relays a signal from a receptor |
| Amplification | Each enzyme activates many of the next, so a small signal gives a large response |

## Exam traps

- The **hypothalamus** detects water potential; the posterior pituitary releases ADH.
- ADH acts on the **collecting duct**, through aquaporins. "More permeable" alone is not enough.
- Ultrafiltration is driven by **pressure**, not ATP.
- The **basement membrane**, not the podocytes, is the main filter.
- Glucagon does **not** enter the cell; cAMP carries the message inside.
- Insulin acts on **muscle and liver** cells in this syllabus; glucagon on **liver** cells.
- Glycogen, glucagon and glucose are different things: spell them carefully.
- Glucose in urine means the PCT's capacity to reabsorb glucose was exceeded.

## Self-test

1. Define homeostasis.
2. Give one internal and one external stimulus.
3. Where and how is urea made?
4. Name the five regions of the kidney from the outside in, ending with the ureter.
5. Why is the blood pressure in the glomerulus high?
6. Which layer of the filtration barrier holds back plasma proteins?
7. Explain why the PCT cells contain many mitochondria.
8. Where are the osmoreceptors that detect blood water potential?
9. What do aquaporins do when ADH binds to collecting duct cells?
10. Name the second messenger in glucagon signalling.
11. Which enzyme produces hydrogen peroxide in a glucose test strip?
12. State one advantage of a biosensor over a test strip.

### Answers

1. The maintenance of a constant internal environment within narrow limits, despite changes inside or outside the body.
2. Internal: rise in blood glucose after a meal (or fall in blood water potential). External: fall in air temperature.
3. In the liver, by deamination of excess amino acids.
4. Fibrous capsule, cortex, medulla, renal pelvis, ureter.
5. The afferent arteriole is wider than the efferent arteriole.
6. The basement membrane.
7. To supply ATP for the sodium–potassium pumps that drive selective reabsorption.
8. In the hypothalamus.
9. Vesicles containing aquaporins fuse with the membrane facing the lumen, so more water can cross by osmosis.
10. Cyclic AMP (cAMP).
11. Glucose oxidase.
12. It gives a precise digital value of the current concentration rather than a colour range.
