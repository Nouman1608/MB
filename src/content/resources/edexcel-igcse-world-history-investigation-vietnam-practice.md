## Enquiry 1 — from Dien Bien Phu to escalation, 1954–68

**1(a)** Describe **two** features of the Strategic Hamlet Programme. **[6]**

**Source A** (invented). From a statement by a US military spokesman in Saigon, November 1967.

> "The enemy has lost the initiative. His units avoid contact and his losses far exceed his capacity
> to replace them. We can now see the light at the end of the tunnel."

**Source B** (invented). From a report by an American television correspondent, February 1968.

> "Two weeks ago we were told the enemy was beaten. Tonight, after fighting in the streets of Saigon and
> Hue, it is hard to believe that the end is anywhere in sight."

**1(b)** How far does Source A support the evidence of Source B about the progress of the war in
1967–68? **[8]**

**Extract C** (invented interpretation).

> "The United States was drawn into Vietnam step by step, by presidents who each believed they could
> not be the one to 'lose' it."

**1(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract C about why the USA became involved in
Vietnam by 1965? Use Extract C, Sources A and B and your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Enquiry 2 — withdrawal, civilians and opinion, 1968–75

**2(a)** Describe **two** features of Nixon's policy of Vietnamisation. **[6]**

**Source D** (invented). From a leaflet distributed by a student anti-war group, May 1970.

> "They promised to bring the boys home. Instead they have taken the war into Cambodia, and now
> students are shot on their own campus for saying so."

**Source E** (invented). From a letter to a newspaper by a construction worker in New York, May 1970.

> "My son is serving in Vietnam. These college kids burn the flag and insult men who are fighting for
> them. Most working people I know stand behind the President."

**2(b)** How far does Source D support the evidence of Source E about American attitudes to the war in
1970? **[8]**

**Extract F** (invented interpretation).

> "The war was lost not in the jungles of Vietnam but in American living rooms, through television."

**2(c)** How far do you agree with the interpretation in Extract F about why the USA failed in
Vietnam? Use Extract F, Sources D and E and your own knowledge. **[16]**

## Answers and mark guidance

### 1(a) [6]

Two features: from 1962 South Vietnamese peasants were moved into fortified villages surrounded by
ditches and fences, to separate them from the NLF; villagers resented leaving ancestral land and being
forced to build the hamlets, and NLF members often got inside, so the programme largely failed and was
abandoned after Diem's fall.

### 1(b) [8]

**Challenge:** A claims the enemy has "lost the initiative"; B reports a huge offensive in Saigon and Hue
that contradicts this.
**Support:** B's "we were told the enemy was beaten" confirms that official statements like A were being
made.
**Provenance and context:** A is an official claim designed to reassure; B is written immediately after
Tet, reflecting the "credibility gap". **Judgement:** little support on the war's progress, though B
confirms A's official optimism.

### 1(c) [16]

**Supporting:** Truman funded France; Eisenhower backed Diem and the Domino Theory; Kennedy increased
advisers; Johnson used the Tonkin Resolution to escalate — each step built on the last, and no
president wanted to be blamed for losing Vietnam after China in 1949.
**Other factors:** Cold War containment and the Domino Theory as a deliberate strategy; the weakness of
South Vietnam, which needed ever more support; the NLF's success after 1960; the Gulf of Tonkin
incident as a trigger. Sources A and B show the commitment had become huge by 1967–68.
**Judgement:** a gradual commitment driven by Cold War beliefs; strong answers weigh "step by step"
against key turning points such as 1964–65.

### 2(a) [6]

Two features: the South Vietnamese army (ARVN) was trained and equipped to take over the fighting; US
troop numbers fell from over half a million in 1969 to almost none by 1973; heavy US air support
continued, including attacks in Cambodia and Laos.

### 2(b) [8]

**Challenge:** D strongly opposes the war and Nixon's expansion into Cambodia; E supports the President
and criticises protesters.
**Support:** both show that opinion was deeply divided and that the war had become a bitter domestic
issue in May 1970; both refer to student protest (Kent State in D; "college kids" in E).
**Provenance:** D is campaigning material; E is a personal letter from a soldier's father, reflecting
pro-war working-class opinion. **Judgement:** they agree only that the country was divided.

### 2(c) [16]

**Supporting:** Tet coverage destroyed public confidence; images of My Lai and civilian casualties; the
credibility gap; protest pushed Johnson not to stand again and Nixon to withdraw.
**Challenging:** military and political factors in Vietnam — guerrilla tactics, the Ho Chi Minh Trail,
the weakness and unpopularity of South Vietnamese governments, US tactics that alienated civilians, and
Soviet and Chinese aid; Congress's refusal of aid in 1975; Source E shows much of the public still backed
the war.
**Judgement:** television shaped opinion, but the USA's failure began with the situation in Vietnam
itself.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating TV reports and official statements as equally reliable without discussing provenance.
- Describing Kent State without linking it to the question.
- Writing about the war's outcome in (2c) without assessing the extract's claim about television.
