## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Organelle | Discrete subunit of a cell with a specific function |
| Not organelles | Cell wall, cytoskeleton, cytoplasm |
| Compartmentalization | Separating cell regions by membranes so reactions occur apart |
| Differentiation | Development of an unspecialized cell into a specialized one by gene expression |
| Stem cell | Cell able to divide endlessly and to differentiate along different pathways |
| Niche | Microenvironment that maintains stem cells or promotes their proliferation and differentiation |
| Totipotent | Can form every cell type, including a whole organism |
| Pluripotent | Can form most body cell types, not a whole organism |
| Multipotent | Can form a limited range of related cell types |

## B2.2 Organelles: recall points

**B2.2.1.** Nuclei, vesicles, ribosomes, mitochondria and the plasma membrane are organelles. Cell fractionation uses a homogenate and an ultracentrifuge. Denser structures pellet at lower speeds.

**B2.2.2.** Eukaryote: transcription in the nucleus, then post-transcriptional modification of mRNA, then export, then translation in the cytoplasm. Prokaryote: no nucleus, so ribosomes can meet mRNA immediately and no modification is possible first.

**B2.2.3.** Advantages of compartments:

- Enzymes and substrates are concentrated.
- Each compartment can have its own conditions, such as pH.
- Incompatible reactions are separated.

Examples: **lysosomes** hold hydrolytic enzymes that would damage the cytoplasm. **Phagocytic vacuoles** hold engulfed material, then fuse with lysosomes.

### HL only: mitochondrion, chloroplast and nucleus

| Structure | Adaptation | Why it helps |
|---|---|---|
| Mitochondrion | Small intermembrane space | Protons build up quickly, so a steep gradient forms |
| Mitochondrion | Cristae | More surface for electron transport chains and ATP synthase |
| Mitochondrion | Matrix | Krebs cycle enzymes and substrates concentrated |
| Chloroplast | Thylakoid membranes | Large area carries photosystems |
| Chloroplast | Small thylakoid volume | Protons build up quickly inside |
| Chloroplast | Stroma | Calvin cycle enzymes and substrates concentrated |
| Nuclear envelope | Double membrane with pores | Controls transport of mRNA and proteins, and can break into vesicles in mitosis and meiosis |

### HL only: the protein route

1. Ribosomes make a protein, free or on the rough ER.
2. Protein from rough ER ribosomes enters the ER lumen and folds.
3. A transport vesicle carries it to the Golgi apparatus.
4. The Golgi apparatus processes and sorts the protein.
5. A vesicle carries the protein to the plasma membrane, where exocytosis releases it.

Free ribosomes make proteins kept in the cell. Ribosomes on rough ER make proteins for secretion, for lysosomes or for the membrane. Clathrin assembles into a cage that helps shape a coated vesicle.

## B2.3 Cell specialization: recall points

**B2.3.1.** All cells hold the same genome. Differentiation depends on which genes are expressed. Gradients in an early embryo mean cells at different positions receive different signals, so they express different genes.

**B2.3.2.** Two properties only: endless division and differentiation along different pathways.

**B2.3.3.** The niche either maintains stem cells or promotes their proliferation and differentiation. Two examples:

- Bone marrow: haematopoietic stem cells give blood cells.
- Hair follicle: stem cells supply cells for hair growth and renewal.

**B2.3.4.** Early embryo cells: totipotent, then soon pluripotent. Adult tissue: multipotent.

**B2.3.5.** Know the size range in humans, from smallest to largest, and link size to function:

| Cell | Size note | Link to function |
|---|---|---|
| Erythrocyte | About 7 µm across | Small and flat, so oxygen exchanges quickly |
| White blood cell | Somewhat larger | Room for a nucleus and engulfed material |
| Egg | About 0.1 mm across | Stores food for early development |
| Sperm | Small head, long tail | Motile, delivers a haploid nucleus |
| Neuron | Axon can exceed a metre | Carries signals over long distances |
| Striated muscle fibre | Up to several centimetres | Contracts along a long, multinucleate cell |

### Surface area-to-volume ratio

For a cube with side *s*:

| Quantity | Formula |
|---|---|
| Surface area | 6*s*² |
| Volume | *s*³ |
| Ratio | 6/*s* |

Exchange across the surface depends on area. The need depends on volume. As a cell grows, volume rises faster than area, so exchange cannot keep up. This sets a limit on cell size. A model with cubes is a simplified version of real cells.

**Method for a ratio question.** (1) Write the formula. (2) Substitute the side. (3) Divide. (4) State units. (5) Comment on what the number means for exchange.

**Method for magnification.** actual size = image size ÷ magnification. Convert units before you divide. 1 mm = 1000 µm.

**Reminder.** A cube of side 3 µm has area 54 µm², volume 27 µm³ and ratio 2 µm⁻¹. A cube of side 6 µm has area 216 µm², volume 216 µm³ and ratio 1 µm⁻¹. Doubling the side halves the ratio.

### HL only: specialized cells

**Method for explaining a specialized cell.** Name the feature, state what it changes, then link that to the cell's job. For example: microvilli (feature) increase surface area (change), so more solute is reabsorbed from the filtrate per unit of cell volume (job).

**Method for a protein-route data question.** Find the time when each structure holds its highest share. The order of the peaks gives the order of the route. State the first and last stage, then say whether the protein is secreted or retained.

| Cell | Key adaptations |
|---|---|
| Erythrocyte | Flattened biconcave disc gives high SA:V |
| Proximal convoluted tubule cell | Microvilli and membrane invaginations raise surface area for reabsorption |
| Type I pneumocyte | Extremely thin, so diffusion distance is short |
| Type II pneumocyte | Lamellar bodies release surfactant into the alveolar lumen |
| Cardiac muscle cell | Myofibrils, branched, usually one or two nuclei |
| Striated muscle fibre | Myofibrils, unbranched, very long, many nuclei |
| Sperm | Flagellum, mitochondria for ATP, acrosome with enzymes |
| Egg | Large, with stored food in the cytoplasm and a protective outer layer |

The alveolar epithelium shows that one tissue can contain more than one cell type, because its overall function needs different adaptations. Your answer on the muscle fibre as a cell depends on the definition you use: it is multinucleate and formed by fusion, but it has a single plasma membrane.

## Must-know distinctions

- Organelle versus non-organelle: the cell wall is outside the plasma membrane and is not an organelle.
- Totipotent versus pluripotent: only totipotent cells can form extra-embryonic tissue and a whole organism.
- Pluripotent versus multipotent: many cell types versus a limited range.
- Free versus rough ER ribosomes: where the protein ends up.
- Surface area versus SA:V ratio: area gets bigger as cells grow, but the ratio gets smaller.

## Quick self-test

1. Name two structures that are not organelles.
2. State one reason why eukaryotic mRNA is made in a separate compartment from translation.
3. A cell is modelled as a cube of side 4 µm. Find its ratio of surface area to volume.
4. A micrograph shows a nucleus 30 mm across at ×6000. Calculate its actual diameter in µm.
5. Give two advantages of compartmentalization shown by lysosomes.
6. (HL only) State why a small intermembrane space helps mitochondria make ATP.
7. (HL only) Which type of ribosome makes a protein that is secreted?
8. State one location of a stem cell niche in an adult human.
9. Distinguish pluripotent from multipotent.
10. (HL only) State the function of lamellar bodies.

### Answers

1. Any two of: cell wall, cytoskeleton, cytoplasm.
2. The mRNA can be modified after transcription, before ribosomes reach it. Prokaryotes lack this separation.
3. Area = 6 × 4² = 96 µm². Volume = 4³ = 64 µm³. Ratio = 96 ÷ 64 = **1.5 µm⁻¹**.
4. 30 mm ÷ 6000 = 0.005 mm = **5 µm**.
5. Hydrolytic enzymes are kept apart from the rest of the cell, and they can work at their own acidic pH. The enzymes are concentrated, too.
6. Protons pumped into a small space raise their concentration quickly, so a steep gradient forms to drive ATP synthase.
7. Ribosomes attached to the rough ER.
8. Bone marrow or hair follicle.
9. Pluripotent cells can give most cell types of the body. Multipotent cells give only a limited range of related types.
10. They release surfactant into the alveolar lumen, which reduces surface tension.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the cytoplasm or cell wall an organelle.
- Writing "bigger cells have a bigger ratio", when the ratio falls.
- Stating only the area or the volume change, with no link to the exchange need.
- Forgetting to convert mm to µm in magnification questions.
- Describing the mitochondrion as "having a large surface area" without saying that cristae are folds of the inner membrane.
- Saying surfactant "helps gas exchange" without stating that it reduces surface tension.
- Omitting the units on a ratio, such as µm⁻¹.
- Treating differentiation as a change in DNA, when it is a change in gene expression.
- Giving a "yes" or "no" about the muscle fibre as a cell, with no reasons.
