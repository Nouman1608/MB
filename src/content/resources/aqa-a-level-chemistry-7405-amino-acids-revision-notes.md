It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Amino acids study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amino-acids/), and exam-style questions are in the [Amino acids practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amino-acids-practice/). ## What you must be able to do

- State that amino acids have **both acidic and basic properties**, including the formation of **zwitterions**.
- Draw amino acids as **zwitterions**.
- Draw the **ions formed in acid solution and in alkaline solution**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Amino acid | Contains an –NH₂ group and a –COOH group |
| α-amino acid | Both groups on the same carbon atom |
| Amphoteric | Has both acidic and basic properties |
| Zwitterion | Ion with a positive and a negative charge and no overall charge |
| Side chain (R) | The group that differs between amino acids |
| Chiral | Has a carbon with four different groups |

## Structure

General formula: **H₂N–CH(R)–COOH**.

| Amino acid | R | Name |
|---|---|---|
| Glycine | H | 2-aminoethanoic acid |
| Alanine | CH₃ | 2-aminopropanoic acid |
| Serine | CH₂OH | 2-amino-3-hydroxypropanoic acid |

All except glycine are **chiral** and have optical isomers.

## Acidic and basic groups

- **–COOH**: acidic, donates H⁺.
- **–NH₂**: basic, accepts H⁺ using the lone pair on nitrogen.
- The molecule is **amphoteric**.

## Three forms

| Form | Structure | Charge | When |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cation | ⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COOH | 1+ | In acid solution |
| Zwitterion | ⁺H₃N–CH(R)–COO⁻ | 0 overall | Solid, near neutral |
| Anion | H₂N–CH(R)–COO⁻ | 1− | In alkaline solution |

### How to get each form

```
Zwitterion:  H₂N–CHR–COOH   →   ⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻          (proton moves inside the molecule)
In acid:     ⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻ + H⁺   →   ⁺H₃N–CHR–COOH     (–COO⁻ takes H⁺)
In alkali:   ⁺H₃N–CHR–COO⁻ + OH⁻  →   H₂N–CHR–COO⁻ + H₂O (–NH₃⁺ loses H⁺)
```

Glycine examples: ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH; ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻; H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻.

## Properties explained

| Property | Reason |
|---|---|
| High melting point | Strong ionic attractions between zwitterions in the lattice |
| Soluble in water | Charged groups interact with water molecules |
| Poor solubility in non-polar solvents | Ionic character |
| Reacts with acids and alkalis | Contains both a base (–NH₂) and an acid (–COOH) |

## Reactions

| Reagent | Group involved | Product (glycine) |
|---|---|---|
| HCl | –NH₂ | ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH Cl⁻ |
| NaOH | –COOH | H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O |
| Another amino acid | Both | Dipeptide with a peptide link (loses H₂O) |
| Alcohol with acid catalyst | –COOH | Ester |

## Drawing checklist

1. Central carbon with H, R, nitrogen group and carbon group.
2. Check charge: cation 1+, zwitterion 0 (with both + and −), anion 1−.
3. Do not put a charge on the R group.
4. Use –NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻ for the zwitterion, not –NH₂ and –COOH.

## Worked examples to copy

**Example 1.** Draw the structure of serine in alkaline solution.

1. Start with serine: H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH.
2. In alkali, the acidic –COOH loses H⁺ to hydroxide: –COO⁻.
3. The –NH₂ group stays as –NH₂.
4. Answer: H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COO⁻, overall charge 1−.

**Example 2.** Explain why glycine melts at a much higher temperature than a molecule of similar size such as propylamine.

1. Glycine is a zwitterion in the solid state.
2. The lattice is held by ionic attractions between ⁺H₃N– and –COO⁻ groups.
3. A lot of energy is needed to overcome these strong attractions.
4. Propylamine has only hydrogen bonds and London forces between its molecules.

## Comparing the three forms of alanine

| Feature | Cation | Zwitterion | Anion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Amino group | –NH₃⁺ | –NH₃⁺ | –NH₂ |
| Carboxyl group | –COOH | –COO⁻ | –COO⁻ |
| Overall charge | 1+ | 0 | 1− |
| Present in | Acid | Neutral solution, solid | Alkali |

## Quick calculations

- Amino acid reacts 1 : 1 with HCl and 1 : 1 with NaOH (one group each).
- Mᵣ of a dipeptide = Mᵣ(amino acid 1) + Mᵣ(amino acid 2) − 18.0.
- n = c × V (dm³).

Example: glycine (Mᵣ 75.0) reacting with 20.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH: n(NaOH) = 0.00400 mol, so n(glycine) = 0.00400 mol, mass = 0.00400 × 75.0 = 0.300 g.

## Exam traps

- Zwitterion drawn as H₂N–CHR–COO⁻ or ⁺H₃N–CHR–COOH.
- Missing + or − on the groups.
- Writing a charge outside the brackets of the zwitterion.
- The acid and alkali forms the wrong way round.
- Explaining the high melting point as "large molecules" instead of ionic attractions.
- Amino group placed on the wrong carbon.
- Saying "neutral" and omitting that the charges are separate.
- Naming glycine as a chiral molecule.

## Self-test

1. Give the general formula of an α-amino acid.
2. What does amphoteric mean?
3. Draw glycine as a zwitterion.
4. Why does the proton move to form a zwitterion?
5. Draw the ion formed by alanine in strongly acidic solution.
6. Draw the ion formed by alanine in strongly alkaline solution.
7. What is the overall charge of each ion in questions 5 and 6?
8. Why do amino acids have high melting points?
9. Why are they soluble in water?
10. Which amino acid is not chiral?
11. What is the Mᵣ of a dipeptide made from glycine (75.0) and alanine (89.0)?
12. What volume of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH neutralises 0.300 g of glycine (Mᵣ 75.0)?

### Answers

1. H₂N–CH(R)–COOH.
2. It has both acidic and basic properties.
3. ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻.
4. The acidic –COOH group donates H⁺ to the basic –NH₂ group in the same molecule.
5. ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₃)–COOH.
6. H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COO⁻.
7. 1+ and 1−.
8. The solid is a lattice of zwitterions held by strong ionic attractions.
9. The charged groups interact strongly with water molecules.
10. Glycine.
11. 75.0 + 89.0 − 18.0 = 146.0.
12. n = 0.300 ÷ 75.0 = 0.00400 mol; V = 0.00400 ÷ 0.100 = 0.0400 dm³ = 40.0 cm³.
