Talmora and all data about it are fictional.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Distinguish between a stock resource and a flow resource, giving an example of each. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why a country's measured reserves of a mineral may increase without any new discovery. **[2]**

**3.** Outline the concept of resource peak. **[2]**

**4.** The table shows Talmora's primary energy mix (percentage of total supply).

| Source | 2005 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 45 | 25 |
| Oil | 30 | 27 |
| Natural gas | 15 | 22 |
| Nuclear | 5 | 6 |
| Renewables | 5 | 20 |

**(a)** By how many percentage points did the share of renewables change between 2005 and 2025? **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the main changes in Talmora's energy mix. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest how these changes may have affected Talmora's energy security. **[3]**

**5.** Talmora has proven coal reserves of 1,800 million tonnes and produces 45 million tonnes a year.

**(a)** Calculate the reserves-to-production ratio. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why Talmora's coal may not run out at the end of this period. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how virtual water trade can improve water security for an arid country. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Outline the stages of an environmental impact assessment for a proposed dam. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how geology and drainage influence the supply of water in an area. **[4]**

**9.** With reference to a major energy resource development, assess its environmental impacts. **[9]**

**10.** Explain the role of transnational corporations in the production, processing and distribution of energy. **[6]**

**11.** With reference to a specified place you have studied, analyse how its physical environment affects the availability and cost of either water or energy. **[9]**

**12.** "Desalination is the solution to future water insecurity." To what extent do you agree with this view? **[12]**

## Answers

Questions 9, 11 and 12 are extended responses. The real exam marks these with levels of response, not point by point; the [1] points show the kind of content that earns credit.

**1.** A stock resource is finite on human timescales and is used up as it is exploited, such as coal or oil [1]. A flow resource is renewed continuously by natural processes, such as wind or river flow [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Higher prices can make known deposits profitable to extract [1], and new technology can lower extraction costs, so material moves from resources into reserves [1]. **[2]**

**3.** The point at which production of a finite resource reaches its maximum [1], after which output declines as the resource becomes harder or more costly to extract, or as demand shifts to substitutes [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** +15 percentage points (from 5% to 20%) [1].
**(b)** Coal's share fell sharply, from 45% to 25% [1]. Renewables quadrupled, from 5% to 20% [1]. Gas rose from 15% to 22%, while oil and nuclear changed little [1].
**(c)** The mix is more diverse, with no source above 27%, so dependence on any one fuel has fallen [1]. Domestic renewables may reduce reliance on imports, improving security [1]. However, if the extra gas is imported, Talmora may be more exposed to price shocks or supply disruption [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in (c), "suggest" allows reasoned possibilities; say what you would need to know, such as whether the gas is imported.

**5. (a)** 1,800 / 45 = 40 years [1].
**(b)** Reserves are not fixed: higher prices or new technology may turn known resources into reserves, and exploration may find more [1]. Production may also fall as Talmora shifts from coal, so the reserves last longer [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Virtual water is the water used to produce goods such as crops [1]. An arid country can import water-intensive food rather than grow it [1], saving its scarce domestic water for drinking, industry or high-value uses [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Screening decides whether a full assessment is needed, and scoping identifies the key impacts, such as on river ecology and displaced communities [1]. A baseline study records conditions before construction [1]. Impacts are predicted and mitigation is proposed, for example fish passes or resettlement plans [1]. Public consultation informs the decision, and impacts are monitored after the dam is built [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Permeable rocks such as sandstone or limestone store water in aquifers, allowing groundwater supply [1], while impermeable rocks such as granite or clay produce high surface runoff and suit reservoirs [1]. Drainage basins collect water into river systems, so supply is greatest downstream of large catchments [1]. Where population lies outside well-watered basins, water transfers are needed, adding cost [1]. **[4]**

**9.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Introduce the Alberta oil sands, Canada [1]. Open-pit mining removes boreal forest and peatland, causing habitat loss [1]. Tailings ponds store contaminated water and pose risks to wildlife and groundwater [1]. Extraction uses large volumes of water from the Athabasca River [1]. Processing oil sands produces more greenhouse gas emissions per barrel than many conventional oil sources [1]. Pipelines carry risks of leaks and spills across ecosystems [1]. Some land is reclaimed after mining, but restoration is slow [1]. Impacts fall heavily on Indigenous communities downstream [1]. Judgement: local impacts are severe and long-lasting, and the global climate impact may be the most significant [1]. **[9]**

**10.** TNCs, both private and state-owned, explore for and extract oil, gas and coal in many countries [1]. They provide the capital and technology needed for difficult projects, such as deep-water drilling [1]. They own refineries and processing plants, often located near markets rather than sources [1]. They control pipelines, tankers and liquefied natural gas terminals that distribute energy globally [1]. Their decisions on investment affect which countries develop their resources [1]. Host governments negotiate with them over taxes and production shares, which can create tension between national and corporate interests [1]. **[6]**

**11.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Locate the place precisely, for example Iceland [1]. Active volcanism along the Mid-Atlantic Ridge provides geothermal heat close to the surface [1]. Geothermal energy heats most homes through district heating [1]. Glacial meltwater and steep relief give high hydro-electric potential [1]. Almost all electricity comes from renewable sources, so domestic energy is relatively cheap [1]. Cheap electricity attracts energy-intensive industry such as aluminium smelting [1]. Physical limits: remoteness means electricity cannot easily be exported [1]. Use: abundant energy supports greenhouse farming in a cold climate [1]. Judgement: the physical environment is the main reason energy is abundant and cheap [1]. **[9]**

**12.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Define desalination and water insecurity [1]. For: it supplies water independent of rainfall, vital for arid coastal states such as Saudi Arabia and Israel [1]. Technology has reduced the energy needed per cubic metre [1]. Solar-powered plants may cut costs and emissions [1]. Against: it needs large amounts of energy, so costs and emissions depend on energy sources [1]. Brine discharge harms marine ecosystems [1]. It is only practical near coasts; inland and upland regions would need costly pumping [1]. Poorer countries may not afford the capital cost [1]. Agriculture uses most water, and desalinated water is often too expensive for irrigation [1]. Demand management, recycling and virtual water trade may be cheaper [1]. Political factors, such as transboundary agreements, also shape security [1]. Judgement: desalination is part of the solution for wealthy coastal states, but not a general solution [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing energy mix data without quoting figures or comparing years.
- Treating a reserves-to-production ratio as an exact date for exhaustion.
- Writing about EIA as a single document rather than a process.
- Extended answers with no named place or scheme.
- Evaluations without a final judgement on the question asked.
