## Section A

**1.** State the reagents and conditions needed to convert benzene into cyclohexane. **[2]**

**2.** Give the formula of the electrophile in the Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene with chloromethane. **[1]**

**3.** Name the organic product when 1,4-dimethylbenzene is heated with hot alkaline KMnO₄ and the mixture is then acidified. **[1]**

**4.** State the colour change seen when methylbenzene is heated with alkaline KMnO₄. **[1]**

**5.** Methylbenzene reacts with Br₂ in two different ways. State the condition that must be used to substitute the side-chain and not the ring. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Benzene reacts with bromine in the presence of AlBr₃.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of the electrophile. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the mechanism of the reaction, including the intermediate. **[3]**

**(c)** Write an equation to show that AlBr₃ acts as a catalyst. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why benzene does not undergo addition with bromine. **[2]**

**7.** Methylbenzene is nitrated with a mixture of concentrated nitric and sulfuric acids.

**(a)** Name the two main organic products. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the 3-isomer is formed only in very small amounts. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why the temperature is kept no higher than 60 °C. **[1]**

**8.** Compound D, C₈H₁₀, contains a benzene ring. When D is heated with alkaline KMnO₄ and then acidified, benzoic acid, C₆H₅COOH, is the only organic product.

**(a)** Deduce the structure and name of D. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the alternative isomer 1,4-dimethylbenzene cannot be D. **[1]**

**9.** Plan two-step syntheses, giving reagents and conditions for each step and naming the intermediate, from methylbenzene to:

**(a)** 4-nitrobenzoic acid **[3]**

**(b)** 3-nitrobenzoic acid **[3]**

**10.** Benzene reacts with ethanoyl chloride when heated with aluminium chloride.

**(a)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why only one acyl group is usually substituted on the ring, whereas Friedel–Crafts alkylation often gives more than one substitution. **[2]**

**11.** 3.90 g of benzene is nitrated and 4.92 g of nitrobenzene, C₆H₅NO₂, is obtained.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of benzene used. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield of nitrobenzene. **[2]**

**12.** Methylbenzene is reacted separately with chlorine under conditions X (AlCl₃, room temperature, no light) and conditions Y (ultraviolet light, no catalyst).

**(a)** Name the type of mechanism under each set of conditions. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the structure or name of one organic product under each set of conditions. **[2]**

**(c)** Under conditions Y, excess chlorine gives a product of formula C₇H₅Cl₃. Suggest its structure. **[1]**

**13.** Write a balanced equation for the Friedel–Crafts acylation of benzene with ethanoyl chloride, and identify the inorganic by-product. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** H₂ with a Pt or Ni catalyst [1]; heat [1].

**2.** CH₃⁺ [1].

**3.** Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid [1].

**4.** Purple solution to a brown precipitate [1].

**5.** Ultraviolet light, with no catalyst (halogen carrier) [1].

**6. (a)** Br₂ + AlBr₃ → Br⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ [1].

**(b)** Curly arrow from the delocalised π system of the ring to Br⁺ [1]; intermediate with the attacked carbon bearing H and Br, a partial delocalised ring and a positive charge [1]; curly arrow from the C–H bond into the ring, H⁺ lost and the ring restored, giving bromobenzene [1].

**(c)** H⁺ + AlBr₄⁻ → HBr + AlBr₃ [1].

**(d)** Addition would destroy the delocalised π system [1], losing the aromatic stabilisation; substitution restores the delocalised ring, so it is energetically favoured [1].

**7. (a)** 2-nitromethylbenzene [1] and 4-nitromethylbenzene [1].

**(b)** The –CH₃ group directs incoming electrophiles to the 2- and 4-positions [1].

**(c)** Above 60 °C, more than one nitro group is substituted onto the ring [1].

**8. (a)** Ethylbenzene, C₆H₅CH₂CH₃ [1]; it has a single side-chain, which is oxidised to one –COOH group on the ring [1].

**(b)** 1,4-dimethylbenzene has two side-chains, so oxidation would give two –COOH groups (benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid), not benzoic acid [1].

**9. (a)** Step 1: concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C [1], giving 4-nitromethylbenzene (–CH₃ directs to position 4) [1]. Step 2: hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid, oxidising –CH₃ to –COOH [1].

**(b)** Step 1: hot alkaline KMnO₄, then dilute acid, giving benzoic acid [1]. Step 2: concentrated HNO₃ and concentrated H₂SO₄ [1]; –COOH directs to position 3, giving 3-nitrobenzoic acid [1].

**10. (a)** Phenylethanone [1].

**(b)** The –COCH₃ group withdraws electron density and deactivates the ring, so the product is less reactive than benzene [1]; an alkyl group activates the ring, so the alkylated product is more reactive than benzene and reacts again [1].

**11. (a)** Mr(C₆H₆) = 78.0; n = 3.90 / 78.0 = 0.0500 mol [1].

**(b)** Mr(C₆H₅NO₂) = 123.0; theoretical mass = 0.0500 × 123.0 = 6.15 g [1]; percentage yield = 4.92 / 6.15 × 100 = 80.0% [1].

**12. (a)** X: electrophilic substitution [1]; Y: free-radical substitution [1].

**(b)** X: 2-chloromethylbenzene or 4-chloromethylbenzene [1]; Y: (chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl [1].

**(c)** C₆H₅CCl₃, all three side-chain hydrogens replaced [1].

**13.** C₆H₆ + CH₃COCl → C₆H₅COCH₃ + HCl [1]; the by-product is hydrogen chloride, formed when H⁺ from the ring combines with AlCl₄⁻, which also regenerates the AlCl₃ catalyst [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the first curly arrow from a ring carbon or from Br₂ instead of from the delocalised π system.
- Leaving out the H⁺ loss or the catalyst-regeneration equation.
- Using UV light for ring substitution, or a halogen carrier for side-chain substitution.
- Forgetting "then dilute acid" in the oxidation conditions.
- Planning a synthesis in an order that puts the second group in the wrong position.
- Using the wrong Mr for nitrobenzene in yield calculations.
