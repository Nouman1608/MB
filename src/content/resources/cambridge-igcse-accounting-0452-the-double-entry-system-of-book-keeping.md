This guide covers sub-topic **2.1 The double entry system of book-keeping**. The syllabus lists: the double entry system; preparing ledger accounts; posting transactions; calculating balances and making transfers to financial statements; interpreting ledger accounts and their balances; "ledger accounts that may be presented using a three-column running balance format"; the division of the ledger into the sales, purchases and nominal (general) ledgers; and how ledger accounts can be kept digitally. It adds: "Candidates do not need to explain or use folio columns."

The examples follow Kestrel Sports Supplies, owned by Dev Malhotra, whose financial year ends on 31 May 2027. Amounts are in dollars. ## 1. The rule behind every entry

Each transaction is recorded twice: once on the debit (left) side of one account and once on the credit (right) side of another, for the same amount. This follows from the accounting equation, which must balance after every transaction.

| Type of account | An increase is a | A decrease is a |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Debit | Credit |
| Expense (including purchases) | Debit | Credit |
| Drawings | Debit | Credit |
| Liability | Credit | Debit |
| Capital | Credit | Debit |
| Income (including sales) | Credit | Debit |

**A three-step method** for any transaction:

1. Name the two accounts affected.
2. Decide what type each account is.
3. Use the table: for example, rent paid by bank is an expense increasing (debit rent) and an asset decreasing (credit bank).

## 2. Worked example 1: deciding the entries

| Date | Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 May | Goods bought on credit from Fenwick Wholesale, 3,600 | Purchases | Fenwick Wholesale |
| 5 May | Goods sold on credit to Ashby School, 2,750 | Ashby School | Sales |
| 8 May | Goods returned to Fenwick Wholesale, 400 | Fenwick Wholesale | Purchases returns |
| 10 May | Ashby School returns goods, 250 | Sales returns | Ashby School |
| 12 May | Cash sales, 980 | Cash | Sales |
| 15 May | Rent paid by bank transfer, 1,100 | Rent | Bank |
| 18 May | Ashby School pays 1,500 by bank transfer | Bank | Ashby School |
| 22 May | Dev takes goods costing 120 for his own use | Drawings | Purchases |
| 25 May | Commission received by bank transfer, 300 | Bank | Commission received |
| 28 May | Fenwick Wholesale paid by bank transfer, 3,200 | Fenwick Wholesale | Bank |
| 30 May | Goods sold on credit to Ashby School, 1,400 | Ashby School | Sales |

Three entries need care. Returns have **their own accounts** rather than being entered on the "wrong" side of sales or purchases, so the total returned can be seen. Goods taken by the owner reduce purchases, not sales, because they were never sold. Commission received is income, so it is credited.

## 3. Worked example 2: posting and balancing

**Balancing an account** takes four steps: add each side; write the larger total on both sides; enter the difference on the smaller side as "Balance c/d"; bring it down on the opposite side, dated the next day, as "Balance b/d".

```
Ashby School account (sales ledger)
Date     Details                $  Date     Details                $
2027                               2027
5 May    Sales              2,750  10 May   Sales returns        250
30 May   Sales              1,400  18 May   Bank               1,500
                                   31 May   Balance c/d        2,400
                            -----                              -----
                            4,150                              4,150
                            =====                              =====
2027
1 Jun    Balance b/d        2,400
```

The debit balance of 2,400 means Ashby School owes Kestrel 2,400.

```
Fenwick Wholesale account (purchases ledger)
Date     Details                $  Date     Details                $
2027                               2027
8 May    Purchases returns    400  3 May    Purchases          3,600
28 May   Bank               3,200
                            -----                              -----
                            3,600                              3,600
                            =====                              =====
```

When the two sides are already equal, there is no balance to carry down: the totals are ruled off and the account is clear. Kestrel owes Fenwick nothing.

## 4. Transfers to the financial statements

At the year end each account is either closed off to a financial statement or carried forward.

| Account type | Year-end treatment |
|---|---|
| Income and expenses | Transferred to the statement of profit or loss; no balance remains |
| Assets, liabilities and capital | Balanced and carried down; shown in the statement of financial position |
| Drawings | Transferred to the capital account |

Kestrel's sales account had a credit balance of 61,870 for June 2026 to April 2027, plus May credit sales of 2,750 and 1,400 and cash sales of 980.

```
Sales account (nominal ledger)
Date     Details                     $  Date     Details                $
2027                                    2027
31 May   Statement of profit       67,000  1 May    Balance b/d       61,870
           or loss                         5 May    Ashby School       2,750
                                           12 May   Cash                 980
                                           30 May   Ashby School       1,400
                                 ------                               ------
                                 67,000                               67,000
                                 ======                               ======
```

The transfer is on the debit side, because a credit balance is removed with a debit; the matching credit is the sales figure in the statement of profit or loss. Dev's drawings account had a debit balance of 8,400 before 22 May; with the goods taken it reaches 8,520, which is transferred by debiting capital and crediting drawings.

## 5. Three-column running balance accounts

The running balance format has **Debit**, **Credit** and **Balance** columns, and the balance is recalculated after every line, marked Dr or Cr. Entries go in the same column as the side they would take in a two-sided account.

```
Ashby School account
Date     Details           Debit    Credit    Balance
2027
5 May    Sales             2,750               2,750 Dr
10 May   Sales returns                250      2,500 Dr
18 May   Bank                       1,500      1,000 Dr
30 May   Sales             1,400               2,400 Dr
```

The closing balance, 2,400 Dr, agrees with the two-sided account. The format is useful because the balance is visible at every date. A balance can change sign: if Pellow Gym owes 600 and pays 650 by mistake, its balance becomes 50 **Cr**, meaning Kestrel now owes Pellow Gym 50 until it is refunded or set against the next sale.

## 6. Interpreting a ledger account

Questions may give an account and ask what it shows. Read each line as a sentence.

```
Marsh Textiles account (purchases ledger)
Date     Details                $  Date     Details                $
2027                               2027
9 May    Purchases returns    210  1 May    Balance b/d        2,890
20 May   Bank               2,890  14 May   Purchases          1,960
31 May   Balance c/d        1,750
                            -----                              -----
                            4,850                              4,850
                            =====                              =====
```

- On 1 May Kestrel owed Marsh Textiles 2,890 (a credit balance on a supplier).
- On 14 May it bought goods on credit for 1,960.
- On 9 May it returned goods worth 210, most likely from an earlier delivery.
- On 20 May it paid the opening amount of 2,890 in full by bank.
- At 31 May it owes 1,750, which is 1,960 - 210: the May purchases less returns.

**Unusual balances are a warning.** A credit balance on an expense account or a debit balance on the sales account usually means an error. A debit balance on a supplier means the business has overpaid.

## 7. Division of the ledger and digital ledgers

| Ledger | Contains | Kestrel examples |
|---|---|---|
| Sales ledger | Credit customers' accounts | Ashby School, Pellow Gym |
| Purchases ledger | Credit suppliers' accounts | Fenwick Wholesale, Marsh Textiles |
| Nominal (general) ledger | All other accounts | Sales, Purchases, Rent, Drawings, Capital |

Cash and bank are kept in the cash book, which also acts as their ledger account. Dividing the ledger lets work be shared and lets a customer's or supplier's account be found quickly.

In a digital system the user enters each transaction once and chooses the accounts; the software posts both sides, recalculates balances immediately (often displaying them in running balance form) and produces a trial balance on request. The debit and credit rules are unchanged, so a wrong account choice is posted just as quickly as a right one.

## Common mistakes

- Debiting sales returns to the sales account, or crediting goods taken by the owner to sales.
- Writing "Balance c/d" on the larger side, or bringing the balance down on the same side.
- Treating the transfer of an income balance to the statement of profit or loss as a credit entry in the income account.
- In running balance accounts, forgetting to mark each balance Dr or Cr.
- Calling a debit balance on a customer "owed by the business".

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** means "present information in a suitable format": dates, details naming the *other* account, amounts, and totals that agree. Folio columns are not required.
- In the details column, name the account where the other half of the entry is made, for example "Bank" in Ashby School's account.
- **Explain** what a balance shows by stating who owes whom and how much, with the date.
- If a question asks for a running balance account, do not also balance it off; its final balance is the closing balance.
