Pearson Edexcel International A Level Biology (YBI11) is a modular
course that runs from biological molecules and cells to ecosystems,
immunity and gene technology. It is taken by students heading towards
medicine, life sciences or any degree that values scientific reasoning.
The qualification is made up of six externally assessed units: four
theory papers each worth 20% and two practical skills papers each worth
10%. Units 1 to 3 form the International Advanced Subsidiary (IAS)
stage and Units 4 to 6 the second (IA2) stage. First teaching was
September 2018, with first examination in January 2019, and the
current specification is Issue 2.

## How the course is organised

The specification contains eight topics, and the titles of the theory
units show how they pair up.

**IAS stage**

- **Unit 1: Molecules, Diet, Transport and Health** brings together
  Topic 1, Molecules, Transport and Health, and Topic 2, Membranes,
  Proteins, DNA and Gene Expression. It builds the molecular and
  cellular foundations used throughout the rest of the course.
- **Unit 2: Cells, Development, Biodiversity and Conservation** draws on
  Topic 3, Cell Structure, Reproduction and Development, and Topic 4,
  Plant Structure and Function, Biodiversity and Conservation.
- **Unit 3: Practical Skills in Biology I** assesses the experimental
  skills developed during the IAS topics.

**IA2 stage**

- **Unit 4: Energy, Environment, Microbiology and Immunity** covers
  Topic 5, Energy Flow, Ecosystems and the Environment, and Topic 6,
  Microbiology, Immunity and Forensics.
- **Unit 5: Respiration, Internal Environment, Coordination and Gene
  Technology** covers Topic 7, Respiration, Muscles and the Internal
  Environment, and Topic 8, Coordination, Response and Gene Technology.
- **Unit 6: Practical Skills in Biology II** assesses practical skills
  at the IA2 level.

## How it's assessed

| Unit | Paper code | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Unit 1: Molecules, Diet, Transport and Health | WBI11/01 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| Unit 2: Cells, Development, Biodiversity and Conservation | WBI12/01 | 1 hour 30 minutes | 80 | 20% |
| Unit 3: Practical Skills in Biology I | WBI13/01 | 1 hour 20 minutes | 50 | 10% |
| Unit 4: Energy, Environment, Microbiology and Immunity | WBI14/01 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 5: Respiration, Internal Environment, Coordination and Gene Technology | WBI15/01 | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 20% |
| Unit 6: Practical Skills in Biology II | WBI16/01 | 1 hour 20 minutes | 50 | 10% |

Weightings are given as a share of the full International A Level. All
six units are externally assessed. Two features are worth noting:

- **The practical units are written papers.** Units 3 and 6 test
  practical skills through questions on paper rather than through a
  separate hands-on endorsement, so laboratory experience has to be
  translated into clear written answers about method, data and
  evaluation.
- **Mathematics is built in.** Each paper includes a stated minimum of
  marks targeting mathematics at Level 2 or above; for Unit 1, for
  example, that minimum is 8 marks.

Because the course is modular, units can be taken across different
series, and the IAS can be certificated after the first three units.

## How the parts fit together

The pairing of topics into units is not arbitrary. Each IA2 unit builds
on ideas first met at IAS.

Membrane structure and gene expression from Topic 2 return in Topic 8,
where coordination depends on membrane potentials and gene technology
depends on understanding DNA and protein synthesis. Cell structure and
development from Topic 3 provide the background for microbiology and
immunity in Topic 6. The biodiversity and conservation content of
Topic 4 leads naturally into the ecosystem and environmental biology of
Topic 5. Respiration in Topic 7 depends on the molecular biology of
Topic 1.

The practical skills papers belong to their stage: Unit 3 sits
alongside the IAS theory units and Unit 6 alongside the IA2 units. The
practical work you do while learning each topic is the preparation for
them, so theory and practical skills are best studied together rather
than one after the other. Mathematical skills such as
handling data, calculating rates and interpreting graphs are tested
in every paper.

## Planning your study

A sensible plan takes the topics in specification order, since each
builds on the one before.

- **First year (IAS):** work through Topics 1 and 2, then Topics 3 and
  4. Alongside each topic, carry out the practical work and practise
  writing up methods, recording data and evaluating results, which is
  what Unit 3 assesses.
- **Second year (IA2):** move through Topics 5 and 6, then 7 and 8,
  reviewing the IAS content each new topic depends on. Keep linking
  practical work to the written style of Unit 6.
- **Maths throughout:** practise calculations and graph work as part of
  every topic rather than as a separate task.

The exact series in which each unit is sat depends on your school's
entry plan, so confirm it early. Begin past papers for a unit as soon as
its topics are complete, and use the exam-preparation page for unit-by-
unit advice on timing and question styles.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many units are in Edexcel International A Level Biology?**
Six. Units 1 to 3 make up the IAS and Units 4 to 6 the IA2. Units 1, 2, 4 and 5 are each worth 20% and Units 3 and 6 are each worth 10% of the full qualification.

**Is there a hands-on practical exam in IAL Biology?**
No. Units 3 and 6, Practical Skills in Biology I and II, are written papers of 1 hour 20 minutes and 50 marks each that test practical skills on paper.

**How long are the IAL Biology theory papers?**
Units 1 and 2 are 1 hour 30 minutes for 80 marks. Units 4 and 5 are 1 hour 45 minutes for 90 marks.

**How much maths is in IAL Biology?**
Every paper includes a stated minimum of marks targeting mathematics at Level 2 or above. In Unit 1, for example, at least 8 marks do so.

**When was the current IAL Biology specification first examined?**
First teaching was September 2018 and first examination was January 2019. The current version is Specification Issue 2.
