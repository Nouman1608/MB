## Questions

**1.** Units.

**(a)** State the units of a rate of reaction measured as change in concentration per second. **[1]**
**(b)** A rate equation is rate = k[X]²[Y]. Give the units of k. **[1]**
**(c)** Convert 18.6 cm³ into dm³. **[1]**

**2.** 2.145 g of ethanedioic acid dihydrate (Mr = 126.0) is dissolved and made up to 250.0 cm³ in a volumetric flask. Calculate the concentration, giving your answer to an appropriate number of significant figures. **[2]**

**3.** In a titration, the initial burette reading is 1.35 cm³ and the final reading is 19.85 cm³. Each reading has an uncertainty of ±0.05 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the titre and its percentage uncertainty. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest one change to the procedure that would halve this percentage uncertainty. **[1]**

**4.** A student investigates how temperature affects the time taken for acidified potassium manganate(VII) to be decolourised by excess ethanedioic acid.

**(a)** Identify the independent and dependent variables. **[1]**
**(b)** State three variables that must be controlled, and how each would be controlled. **[3]**

**5.** A student records this table while collecting gas.

| Time (seconds) | Volume |
|---|---|
| 30 | 12 cm³ |
| 60 | 21.5 cm³ |
| 90 | 28 cm³ |

State three ways the table should be improved. **[3]**

**6.** A white solid Z is tested.
Test 1: warming with NaOH(aq) gives a gas that turns damp red litmus blue.
Test 2: dilute nitric acid gives no effervescence.
Test 3: Ba(NO₃)₂(aq) gives a white precipitate.

**(a)** Identify Z, explaining your reasoning from each test. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the test for sulfate is carried out before any test for halide ions. **[1]**

**7.** Titres (cm³): rough 22.90, then 22.65, 22.30, 22.70, 22.60. Choose the titres to use and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**

**8.** The initial rate of a reaction is measured at different concentrations of reactant A.

| [A] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.020 | 0.040 | 0.060 | 0.080 | 0.100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ | 6.20 × 10⁻⁵ | 1.25 × 10⁻⁴ | 1.60 × 10⁻⁴ | 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ | 3.11 × 10⁻⁴ |

A graph of initial rate against [A] gives a straight line through the origin.

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result and explain your choice. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the rate constant k from the gradient of the line. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the units of k. **[1]**
**(d)** State what the shape of the graph shows about the order with respect to A. **[1]**

**9.** On a concentration–time curve for a reactant, a tangent drawn at t = 60 s passes through (0 s, 0.150 mol dm⁻³) and (150 s, 0.000 mol dm⁻³).

**(a)** Calculate the rate of reaction at 60 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the tangent should be extended as far as possible before its gradient is measured. **[1]**

**10.** A student finds the volume of hydrogen from 0.060 g of magnesium ribbon with excess hydrochloric acid. She removes the bung of the flask, drops in the ribbon, replaces the bung and collects the gas over water in a 100 cm³ measuring cylinder. She collects 52 cm³. Use Ar(Mg) = 24.3 and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen expected. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest two limitations of the procedure that explain the low volume, and an improvement for each. **[4]**

**11.** A student heats hydrated barium chloride, BaCl₂·xH₂O, in a crucible. Each balance reading is ±0.001 g. Use Mr(BaCl₂) = 208.3 and Mr(H₂O) = 18.0.

| Reading | Mass / g |
|---|---|
| empty crucible | 18.462 |
| crucible + hydrated salt | 20.904 |
| crucible + residue after heating | 20.560 |

**(a)** Calculate x to two decimal places. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mass of water lost. **[1]**
**(c)** The accepted formula is BaCl₂·2H₂O. Calculate the percentage error in your value of x. **[1]**
**(d)** Evaluate the result, suggesting the main cause of the difference and an improvement. **[3]**

**12.** Each iron tablet is thought to contain about 65 mg of iron as Fe²⁺. You have 0.00500 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄(aq), dilute sulfuric acid and standard laboratory glassware. Use Ar(Fe) = 55.8 and:

MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 5Fe³⁺ + 4H₂O

Plan a titration to find the mass of iron in one tablet. Include quantities, show that your titre will be a suitable size, and explain how the result is calculated. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** k = rate ÷ ([X]²[Y]) = (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³, so **dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** 18.6 ÷ 1000 = **0.0186 dm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Build units of k from the rate equation each time; a memorised unit for the wrong overall order is unlikely to gain credit.

**2.** n = 2.145 ÷ 126.0 = 0.017024 mol; c = 0.017024 ÷ 0.2500 [1] = **0.06810 mol dm⁻³** (4 s.f., matching the data) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The data are to 4 significant figures, so 0.068 or 0.0681 shows less precision than the measurements allow.

**3. (a)** Titre = 19.85 − 1.35 = **18.50 cm³** [1]; uncertainty = 2 × 0.05 = ±0.10 cm³, so 0.10 ÷ 18.50 × 100 = **0.54%** [1]
**(b)** Double the titre, for example by using a sample of twice the volume or concentration [1]
*Examiner insight:* Writing the titre as 18.5 cm³ hides the burette's resolution; record and quote it to two decimal places.

**4. (a)** Independent: temperature; dependent: time taken to decolourise (both needed) [1]
**(b)** Any three, each with a method: volume and concentration of KMnO₄(aq), by pipette from one stock solution [1]; volume and concentration of ethanedioic acid, likewise [1]; volume and concentration of sulfuric acid, likewise [1]; total volume of the mixture
*Examiner insight:* "Same amount" alone is too vague; name the quantity and say how you would keep it constant.

**5.** Units belong in the headings, as volume / cm³, not in the cells [1]; time heading should be "time / s" [1]; values in a column need the same number of decimal places, for example 12.0, 21.5, 28.0 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Three different faults are needed; repeating "units are wrong" twice is unlikely to earn two marks.

**6. (a)** Test 1: ammonia released, so NH₄⁺ present [1]. Test 2: no CO₂, so carbonate absent [1]. Test 3: white BaSO₄, so sulfate present; Z is **(NH₄)₂SO₄**, ammonium sulfate [1]
**(b)** Sulfate ions could precipitate with Ag⁺ and be mistaken for a silver halide [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link each observation to an ion; a correct formula with no reasoning does not answer an "explain" question.

**7.** Concordant titres 22.65, 22.70 and 22.60; 22.30 and the rough titre are left out [1]; mean = 67.95 ÷ 3 = **22.65 cm³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Averaging every value, including the rough titre, gives a mean that does not reflect the concordant results.

**8. (a)** **[A] = 0.060 mol dm⁻³** [1]; the others give rate ÷ [A] ≈ 3.1 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, this one 2.67 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹, so it lies below the line [1]
**(b)** Gradient from (0, 0) and (0.100, 3.11 × 10⁻⁴) on the line [1]: k = 3.11 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.100 = **3.11 × 10⁻³** [1]
**(c)** **s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Rate is directly proportional to [A], so the reaction is **first order** with respect to A [1]
*Examiner insight:* Take gradient points from the drawn line, well apart; using the anomalous point pulls k down.

**9. (a)** Gradient = (0.000 − 0.150) ÷ (150 − 0) = −1.00 × 10⁻³ [1]; rate = **1.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** A longer line gives larger Δy and Δx, so reading errors are a smaller fraction of each [1]
*Examiner insight:* Rate is quoted as a positive value even though the gradient for a reactant is negative.

**10. (a)** n(Mg) = 0.060 ÷ 24.3 = 2.47 × 10⁻³ mol = n(H₂) [1]; V = 2.47 × 10⁻³ × 24 000 = **59 cm³** [1]
**(b)** Hydrogen escapes while the bung is off [1]; improvement: hang the ribbon in a small tube inside the sealed flask, or attach it by thread, and release it after sealing [1]. Oxide coating on the ribbon means less magnesium reacts [1]; improvement: clean the ribbon with emery paper before weighing [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each improvement must address the limitation you named; "repeat the experiment" does not fix a systematic loss.

**11. (a)** Mass of hydrated salt 2.442 g; anhydrous 2.098 g; water 0.344 g [1]; n(BaCl₂) = 2.098 ÷ 208.3 = 0.01007 mol; n(H₂O) = 0.344 ÷ 18.0 = 0.01911 mol [1]; x = 0.01911 ÷ 0.01007 = **1.90** [1]
**(b)** Two readings, so ±0.002 g: 0.002 ÷ 0.344 × 100 = **0.58%** [1]
**(c)** (2 − 1.90) ÷ 2 × 100 = **5.0%** [1]
**(d)** 5.0% is much larger than the measurement uncertainty (about 0.7% in total), so the error is systematic [1]. The value is low because not all the water was driven off [1]. Heat, cool and reweigh repeatedly until the mass is constant [1]
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs the comparison stated; giving 5.0% and 0.58% without saying what they show does not complete it.

**12.** Weigh five tablets (about 0.33 g of iron) and crush them [1]. Dissolve in dilute sulfuric acid, which supplies the H⁺ the equation needs [1]. Transfer with washings to a 250.0 cm³ volumetric flask, make up to the mark and invert to mix [1]. Pipette 25.0 cm³ into a conical flask [1]. Titrate with KMnO₄ from a burette until the first permanent pale pink colour [1]. Repeat to concordant titres within 0.10 cm³ and average them [1]. Estimate: 5 × 0.065 = 0.325 g, or 5.82 × 10⁻³ mol Fe²⁺; 5.82 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 25.0 cm³; MnO₄⁻ = 1.16 × 10⁻⁴ mol, a titre of about **23 cm³**, suitable for a 50 cm³ burette [1]. Mass of Fe per tablet = mean titre (dm³) × 0.00500 × 5 × 10 × 55.8 ÷ 5 [1]
*Examiner insight:* Planning answers gain credit for stated quantities and a reason for each; "add some acid" or "use a titration" alone is too vague.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Controlled variables named without saying how they are kept constant.
- Units in table cells, or headings with no unit.
- ±0.05 cm³ used for a titre from two readings.
- Rounding below the precision of the data.
- The rough or a non-concordant titre included in a mean.
- A gradient from an anomalous point, or from close points.
- A negative rate quoted from a reactant's tangent.
- Limitations with no matching improvement.
- Percentage error and uncertainty calculated but not compared.
- Plans with no quantities, or an unsuitable titre size.
