These questions cover section **4.5.4 Plant hormones** (4.5.4.1 and 4.5.4.2, with required practical activity 8)
of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for first teaching from 2016. The section is Biology only. Questions
or parts marked **Higher tier only** cover gibberellins, ethene and the uses of plant hormones. All data are
invented for practice. A calculator is useful for questions 7 and 11.

## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.**

**(a)** Name the plant hormone that controls the bending of shoots towards light. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the scientific name for a growth response to gravity. **[1]**

**2.** State the effect of auxin on:

**(a)** cells in a shoot **[1]**
**(b)** cells in a root **[1]**

**3.** Explain why it is useful to a plant for its roots to grow downwards. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) Name the plant hormone used for each purpose.

**(a)** Ending seed dormancy **[1]**
**(b)** Rooting powder for cuttings **[1]**
**(c)** Controlling the ripening of fruit during transport **[1]**

**5.** (Higher tier only)

**(a)** Name the plant hormone important in initiating seed germination. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **two** processes controlled by ethene. **[1]**

### Section B: longer and structured questions

**6.** A student germinates bean seeds on damp cotton wool in a dish, standing the dish on its edge in the dark.
When the roots and shoots are 10 mm long, she turns the dish through 90 degrees so that the seedlings lie on
their sides. Two days later the roots have curved downwards and the shoots have curved upwards.

**(a)** Explain why the dish was kept in the dark. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the roots curved downwards. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the shoots curved upwards. **[2]**

**7.** A student grows cress seedlings in three boxes for five days, then measures five shoots in each box.

| Box | Shoot lengths (mm) | Appearance |
|---|---|---|
| A: light from one side | 21, 23, 22, 35, 24 | Bent towards the light |
| B: light from above | 20, 22, 21, 23, 19 | Straight |
| C: no light | 37, 40, 39, 38, 41 | Straight, pale |

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result in box A. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean shoot length in box A without the anomalous result. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the shoots in box A bent towards the light. **[3]**
**(d)** Give **two** variables the student should control. **[2]**
**(e)** State the other way, besides length measurements, that the specification says results should be recorded. **[1]**

**8.** A seedling is placed on a turntable that rotates slowly. A lamp shines on it from one side. The shoot grows
straight up. Use your knowledge of auxin to suggest why. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A farmer sprays a weedkiller containing auxin on a field of wheat. The weeds die but the
wheat is not harmed.

**(a)** Explain how the weedkiller kills the weeds. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how regular use of weedkillers can affect biodiversity. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A supermarket tests storage of bananas picked before they are ripe.

| Storage condition | Mean days until ripe |
|---|---|
| Air only | 12 |
| Air with added ethene | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate how many days sooner the bananas ripened with ethene. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why fruit is picked unripe and then ripened with ethene close to the time of sale. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A grower sprays grape vines with gibberellins. The mean mass of a grape from sprayed
vines is 4.2 g; from unsprayed vines it is 2.8 g.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in mean grape mass. **[2]**
**(b)** Give **one** other use of gibberellins. **[1]**

**12.** Explain how auxin causes the shoot of a seedling to grow towards light, and its root to grow downwards. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Auxin [1]
**(b)** Gravitropism (geotropism) [1]

**2. (a)** Stimulates growth (cell elongation) [1]
**(b)** Inhibits growth [1]

**3.** Roots reach water and mineral ions in the soil [1]; and anchor the plant [1]

**4. (a)** Gibberellins [1]
**(b)** Auxins [1]
**(c)** Ethene [1]

**5. (a)** Gibberellins [1]
**(b)** Cell division and fruit ripening (both needed) [1]

**6. (a)** To make sure the growth response was to gravity, not to light [1]
**(b)** Auxin collected on the lower side of the root [1]; in roots auxin inhibits growth, so the lower side grew more slowly [1]; the upper side grew faster, so the root curved downwards [1]
**(c)** Auxin collected on the lower side of the shoot [1]; in shoots auxin stimulates growth, so the lower side grew faster and the shoot curved upwards [1]

**7. (a)** 35 mm [1]
**(b)** (21 + 23 + 22 + 24) ÷ 4 = **22.5 mm** [1]
**(c)** Auxin moved to the shaded side of the shoot [1]; cells on the shaded side elongated faster than on the lit side [1]; so the shoot bent towards the light [1]
**(d)** Any two: type of seedling; number of seeds; temperature; volume of water; time grown [2]
**(e)** Labelled biological drawings [1]
*Examiner insight:* One mark for each control variable in (d). "Light" is not a control variable here; it is the independent variable.

**8.** Each side of the shoot faces the lamp in turn, so all sides receive the same amount of light [1]; auxin is spread evenly, so all sides grow at the same rate and the shoot grows straight [1]

**9. (a)** The auxin makes the weeds grow too fast [1]; so they die, while the wheat is not affected by the weedkiller [1]
**(b)** Weedkillers remove many wild plant species [1]; animals such as insects that feed or shelter on these plants lose food and habitat [1]; so the number of different species in the area falls [1]

**10. (a)** 12 − 4 = **8 days** [1]
**(b)** Unripe fruit is firmer, so it is less damaged during storage and transport [1]; ethene then ripens it when it is needed, so it is ready to eat on sale [1]

**11. (a)** (4.2 − 2.8) ÷ 2.8 × 100 [1] = **50%** [1]
**(b)** Any one: ending seed dormancy; promoting flowering [1]

**12.** One mark for each point:
- auxin causes unequal growth when it is unevenly distributed [1]
- in a shoot lit from one side, auxin moves to the shaded side [1]
- in shoots, auxin stimulates cell elongation, so the shaded side grows faster and the shoot bends towards the light [1]
- in a root, auxin collects on the lower side because of gravity [1]
- in roots, auxin inhibits growth, so the lower side grows more slowly [1]
- the upper side grows faster, so the root bends downwards [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the shoot rule for the root (auxin making the root grow faster) reverses the explanation and loses the root marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying auxin speeds up root growth.
- Forgetting to say which side auxin collects on.
- Naming light as a control variable in a light experiment.
- Confusing ethene (ripening) with gibberellins (fruit size).
- Percentage increase divided by the new value instead of the original.
