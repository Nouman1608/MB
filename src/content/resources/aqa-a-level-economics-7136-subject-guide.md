AQA A-Level Economics (7136) studies how individuals, firms and governments make choices about scarce resources,
and how whole economies grow, fluctuate and trade with one another. It is a strong choice for students interested
in economics, politics, business, finance or law at university, and it rewards both clear diagrammatic reasoning
and well-structured written argument. The qualification is linear and not tiered. It is assessed by three
compulsory written papers at the end of the course, each lasting 2 hours and worth 80 marks, with each paper
carrying a third of the A-level.

## How the course is organised

The content is in two large parts, often described as microeconomics and macroeconomics.

**Individuals, firms, markets and market failure.** This half begins with economic methodology and the economic
problem, then covers individual economic decision making, price determination in a competitive market, and
production, costs and revenue. It goes on to perfect competition, imperfectly competitive markets and monopoly,
the labour market, the distribution of income and wealth (including poverty and inequality), and finally the
market mechanism, market failure and government intervention in markets.

**The national and international economy.** This half covers the measurement of macroeconomic performance, how
the macroeconomy works (the circular flow of income, aggregate demand and aggregate supply analysis and related
concepts), economic performance, financial markets and monetary policy, fiscal policy and supply-side policies,
and the international economy.

AQA numbers the content sections from 1 to 14: the eight microeconomic areas are sections 1 to 8, and the six
macroeconomic areas are sections 9 to 14.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Content |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 7136/1 Paper 1: Markets and market failure | 2 hours | 80 | 33.33% | Sections 1-8 |
| 7136/2 Paper 2: National and international economy | 2 hours | 80 | 33.33% | Sections 9-14 |
| 7136/3 Paper 3: Economic principles and issues | 2 hours | 80 | 33.34% | Sections 1-14 |

Papers 1 and 2 share a format. Section A is a data-response question, with a choice of one of two contexts, worth
40 marks. Section B is an essay, chosen from three, also worth 40 marks.

Paper 3 is the synoptic paper and can draw on all the content. Section A is 30 marks of multiple-choice questions
and Section B is 50 marks of case-study questions. Each paper is a third of the A-level; the weightings are
recorded as 33.33%, 33.33% and 33.34% so that they total exactly 100. The qualification is out of 240 marks. It
was first taught in September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in June 2017.

## How the parts fit together

The first two papers follow the two halves of the content, but the course is designed to be understood as a
whole, and Paper 3 tests exactly that. Its case study can combine a market-level issue with its macroeconomic
consequences, and its multiple-choice section can range across every section.

The links between the halves are worth tracing deliberately. Market failure and government intervention lead
naturally into fiscal and supply-side policy. The labour market connects to unemployment and economic
performance. Financial markets and monetary policy depend on the same supply-and-demand reasoning used in
price determination. The distribution of income and wealth is both a microeconomic question about markets and
a macroeconomic question about policy.

Two skills run through all three papers: using diagrams and data accurately, and evaluating. Data-response
questions reward applying theory to unfamiliar figures; essays and case studies reward weighing up arguments and
reaching a supported judgement. Both should be practised throughout the course, not only near the exams.

## Planning your study

The two halves can be studied in parallel, but a sequential route also works well:

1. **Begin with methodology, the economic problem and price determination.** Supply and demand analysis is the
   basic tool for the whole course.
2. **Continue with decision making, costs and revenue, and market structures**, then the labour market,
   distribution and market failure.
3. **Introduce the macroeconomy with measurement and the circular flow**, then AD/AS analysis, so later policy
   topics have a framework.
4. **Complete economic performance, monetary, fiscal and supply-side policy, and the international economy.**

Keep a set of core diagrams that you can draw quickly and accurately, and a notebook of current economic examples
filed by section. Short data-response and multiple-choice practice can start as soon as a section is taught.
Full Paper 1 and Paper 2 practice is most useful once each half is complete, and Paper 3 practice once both are.
Paper-specific timing and technique are covered on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in AQA A-Level Economics?**
Three compulsory written papers, each lasting 2 hours and worth 80 marks. Each counts for a third of the A-level, and the total is 240 marks.

**What is on each AQA A-Level Economics paper?**
Paper 1 covers markets and market failure (sections 1-8), Paper 2 covers the national and international economy (sections 9-14), and Paper 3 covers economic principles and issues across all 14 sections.

**What question types are in Papers 1 and 2?**
Each has a 40-mark data-response question, chosen from two contexts, and a 40-mark essay chosen from three.

**What is different about Paper 3?**
It is synoptic. Section A is 30 marks of multiple-choice questions and Section B is 50 marks of case-study questions, drawing on all the content.

**Is AQA A-Level Economics modular?**
No. It is linear and not tiered, so all three papers are taken at the end of the course.
