These questions cover section 3.1.1 Monomers and polymers of the AQA AS and A-level Biology specification (7401/7402), first teaching 2015. They focus on the general chemistry of condensation and hydrolysis across all three polymer families, which the [carbohydrates-focused practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-monomers-polymers-and-carbohydrates-practice/) does not test in depth. ## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *monomer*. **[1]**

**2.** Define the term *hydrolysis reaction*. **[2]**

**3.** Copy and complete the table. **[3]**

| Monomer | Polymer | Bond between monomers |
|---|---|---|
| Amino acid | A | Peptide |
| B | Polysaccharide | Glycosidic |
| Nucleotide | Polynucleotide | C |

**4.** A student writes: "Triglycerides are polymers because they are formed by condensation reactions." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**5.** A linear polypeptide contains 340 amino acids.

**(a)** State how many water molecules were released when it was made. **[1]**

**(b)** State how many water molecules are needed to hydrolyse it completely to amino acids. **[1]**

**6.** A circular DNA molecule in a bacterium contains 9,600 nucleotides. State the number of phosphodiester bonds it contains and explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** A protein is made of two separate polypeptide chains of 141 and 146 amino acids. Calculate the total number of peptide bonds in the protein. **[1]**

**8.** Explain why the total mass of the products is greater than the mass of a polymer after complete hydrolysis. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**9.** A storage polysaccharide is a branched chain of 2,500 glucose units with 210 branch points. Take Mr of glucose as 180 and water as 18.

**(a)** State the number of glycosidic bonds in the molecule. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the molecule. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in mass when the molecule is fully hydrolysed. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**10.** A scientist discovers a new polymer in a deep-sea bacterium. The diagram shows that it is made of repeating units, each joined to the next by an oxygen atom between two carbon atoms. When the polymer is treated with an enzyme from the same bacterium, the mixture's mass increases and a single type of small molecule is produced.

**(a)** Suggest which group of biological molecules the polymer belongs to. Give one reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest the type of reaction the enzyme catalyses. Use evidence from the description. **[2]**

**(c)** The scientist finds that the polymer has the same number of bonds as monomers. Suggest what this tells you about its shape. **[1]**

**11.** Describe how a peptide bond is formed between two amino acids, naming the groups involved. **[3]**

**12.** In a cell, glycogen is broken down when glucose is needed for respiration, and amino acids are joined to form proteins at ribosomes.

**(a)** Name the type of reaction involved in each process. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why both processes depend on water in the cell. **[2]**

**13.** The biochemical basis of life is similar for all living things. Explain how the content of 3.1.1 supports this statement, and suggest why it is useful when studying unfamiliar organisms. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A smaller unit from which larger molecules are made [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A reaction that breaks a chemical bond between two molecules [1] and involves the use of a water molecule [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Both parts are needed. "Splitting a molecule with water" without naming the bond is often given only partial credit.

**3.** A: polypeptide [1]. B: monosaccharide [1]. C: phosphodiester [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Triglycerides are formed from one glycerol and three fatty acids [1], so they are not made of a large number of repeating monomers; being formed by condensation does not make a molecule a polymer [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 339 [1].

**(b)** 339 [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 9,600 bonds [1], because in a circular molecule the two ends are joined, so there is one bond for every nucleotide rather than one fewer [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The word "circular" is the cue. Writing 9,599 is the most common wrong answer.

**7.** 140 + 145 = **285** [1]. **[1]**

**8.** Each bond broken uses one water molecule [1], and the atoms of that water (–OH and –H) become part of the products, so their total mass includes the added water [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** 2,499 [1]. Each glucose unit added, whether in the main chain or at a branch, forms one glycosidic bond, so a single molecule of n units has n − 1 bonds regardless of branching [1].

**(b)** (2,500 × 180) − (2,499 × 18) = 450,000 − 44,982 [1] = **405,018** [1].

**(c)** 44,982 ÷ 405,018 × 100 [1] = **11.1%** [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* The 210 branch points are a distractor. Writing the general rule first protects you from being led to subtract them.

**10. (a)** Carbohydrate (polysaccharide) [1], because units joined by an oxygen between two carbons describes a glycosidic bond (C–O–C) [1].

**(b)** Hydrolysis [1]; the mass increases because water is added, and the polymer is broken into its monomer [1].

**(c)** It is circular: its ends are joined, so there is one bond per monomer [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" questions reward applying principles to new material. Use the clues given, mass increase and the C–O–C link, rather than general statements.

**11.** The carboxyl group (–COOH) of one amino acid reacts with the amine group (–NH₂) of the other [1]; an –OH from the carboxyl group and an –H from the amine group are removed as a molecule of water [1]; a peptide bond forms between the carbon of the first amino acid and the nitrogen of the second [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Glycogen breakdown: hydrolysis [1]. Joining amino acids: condensation [1].

**(b)** Hydrolysis uses water molecules to break each glycosidic bond [1]. Condensation releases water, so it takes place in the watery cytoplasm, where water is the solvent in which the reactions occur [1]. **[4]**

**13.** All organisms build their large molecules from the same few monomer families: monosaccharides, amino acids and nucleotides [1]. In every case the polymers are made by condensation and broken down by hydrolysis [1], so the same reactions and bond types (glycosidic, peptide, phosphodiester) occur across all life [1]. This means principles learned in one organism, such as how polymers are digested or built, can be applied to an unfamiliar organism [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers name the shared monomers and reactions. A general sentence that "all cells are similar" repeats the stem without evidence.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining condensation or hydrolysis without mentioning water.
- Saying hydrolysis releases water.
- Calling triglycerides or disaccharides polymers.
- Subtracting one bond for a circular molecule, or subtracting branch points.
- Forgetting that each of two separate chains loses one bond from its count.
- Naming the wrong bond for the monomer family.
