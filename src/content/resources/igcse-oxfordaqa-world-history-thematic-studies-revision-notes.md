**These notes condense Section B of OxfordAQA International GCSE History (9245) Paper 2: the thematic study of Technology and change or Medicine and change, c800 to the present.** Each option is summarised as a "people and developments" table for its six periods, followed by the factors that drive change -- the key to questions 3 and 4. ## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA 9245, VERSION 1.1 -- 3.2.2 SECTION B** -- choose one: Technology and change, c800-present day; Medicine and change, c800-present day. Each has six periods. Questions: 3, 6, 9 and 12 marks (30 in total).

## Factors that drive change (use in questions 3 and 4)

| Factor | Technology example | Medicine example |
|---|---|---|
| Individuals | Watt's condenser | Pasteur's germ theory |
| Science and technology | Transistors making computers small | Microscopes; X-rays |
| War | Turing's codebreaking | Mass-produced penicillin in the Second World War |
| Government | Regulation of radio and cars | Public health laws; the WHO |
| Communication | Printing spreading ideas | Translation Movement; journals |
| Religion and beliefs | Reformation pamphlets | Religious explanations of plague |
| Economy and trade | Oil and the car industry | Pharmaceutical companies |

## Turning points to argue about

- **Technology:** the House of Wisdom's translations (knowledge preserved and passed to Europe); Gutenberg's press (ideas spread faster than ever before); Watt's condenser (power no longer tied to rivers); the telegraph (communication faster than transport); the assembly line (mass ownership of machines); the Web (global, instant information).
- **Medicine:** the Translation Movement (Greek medicine preserved and extended); the Black Death (old explanations failed); germ theory in the 1860s (the biggest change in ideas about cause); penicillin (the first widely effective antibiotic); X-rays and transplants (seeing and replacing parts of the body); the WHO (disease tackled globally).

For each turning point, be ready to say what changed, why it happened then, and who it affected. A strong question 4 answer often compares two turning points from different periods.

## Change and continuity in one line per option

- **Technology:** inventions became faster to spread and more global, but every period built on earlier knowledge -- from Greek texts in Baghdad to Babbage's ideas in the computers of the twentieth century.
- **Medicine:** ideas about cause moved from imbalance, miasma and religion to germs and genes, while practical public health measures such as clean water and quarantine recur across the whole period.

## Worked example: question 4 structure

*"War was the most important factor in the development of medicine in the 20th century." How far do you agree?* **[12]**

- **War:** penicillin mass-produced in the USA for the armed forces; blood storage and transfusion; national health systems influenced by war.
- **Other factor -- science and individuals:** Fleming's discovery (1928) and Florey and Chain's research came before or apart from war needs; Hodgkin's work on molecular structure.
- **Other factor -- government and industry:** pharmaceutical companies, national health systems.
- **Judgement:** war speeded up development, but science and individuals made the discoveries.

## Exam traps

- Listing names without the change they caused.
- Using only European examples -- the specification is global.
- Mixing up periods (for example, putting Lister in the twentieth century).
- In question 4, not explaining the "other" factor in enough depth.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I revise six periods?**
Learn each period's two or three key individuals, their contribution and one consequence.

**Is continuity examined?**
Questions focus on cause, consequence and change; showing what stayed the same can strengthen a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Who designed the separate condenser? *James Watt.*
2. What was the House of Wisdom? *A centre of learning in Baghdad where ancient texts were translated.*
3. Who linked cholera to a water pump? *John Snow, 1854.*
4. Who wrote early programs for Babbage's Analytical Engine? *Ada Lovelace.*
5. When was the WHO founded? *1948.*
