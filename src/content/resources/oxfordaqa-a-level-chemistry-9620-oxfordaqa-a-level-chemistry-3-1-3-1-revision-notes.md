## The definition

**Ionic bonding is the electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in a lattice.**

- Ions form by electron transfer, from metal atoms to non-metal atoms.
- The attraction acts in all directions, between each ion and all its oppositely charged neighbours.
- There are no molecules: the formula is the simplest ratio of ions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ion | Atom or group of atoms with an overall charge |
| Cation | Positive ion, formed by losing electrons |
| Anion | Negative ion, formed by gaining electrons |
| Compound ion | Group of covalently bonded atoms with an overall charge, such as SO₄²⁻ |
| Lattice | Regular three-dimensional arrangement of particles repeating throughout a crystal |
| Electrostatic attraction | Force between opposite charges |
| Formula unit | Simplest ratio of ions in an ionic compound, as given by its formula |

## Charges from the Periodic Table

| Group | 1 | 2 | 13 | 15 | 16 | 17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ion charge | 1+ | 2+ | 3+ | 3− | 2− | 1− |
| Example | K⁺ | Ca²⁺ | Al³⁺ | N³⁻ | S²⁻ | Br⁻ |

- Metals (left) lose electrons; non-metals (right) gain them, reaching a noble gas configuration.
- Group 14 and Group 18: no simple ions in ordinary compounds.
- d-block: charge from the Roman numeral, such as iron(III) = Fe³⁺; also Ag⁺, Zn²⁺.

## Compound ions to know

| Ion | Formula | Ion | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sulfate | SO₄²⁻ | Carbonate | CO₃²⁻ |
| Hydroxide | OH⁻ | Ammonium | NH₄⁺ |
| Nitrate | NO₃⁻ | | |

Others that may be given in a question: hydrogencarbonate HCO₃⁻, phosphate PO₄³⁻.

## Writing formulas

1. Write both ions with charges.
2. Balance the charges using the lowest numbers.
3. Bracket a compound ion when more than one is needed.

| Compound | Ions | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Lithium oxide | Li⁺, O²⁻ | Li₂O |
| Aluminium sulfate | Al³⁺, SO₄²⁻ | Al₂(SO₄)₃ |
| Magnesium nitride | Mg²⁺, N³⁻ | Mg₃N₂ |
| Ammonium carbonate | NH₄⁺, CO₃²⁻ | (NH₄)₂CO₃ |
| Iron(III) hydroxide | Fe³⁺, OH⁻ | Fe(OH)₃ |
| Calcium phosphate | Ca²⁺, PO₄³⁻ | Ca₃(PO₄)₂ |

## Dot-and-cross checklist

- Each ion drawn separately, in square brackets.
- Charge outside the bracket, top right.
- Transferred electrons shown with a different symbol.
- Correct number of each ion (for example two Cl⁻ for CaCl₂).
- No lines or shared pairs between ions.

## Naming ionic compounds

- Metal first, then the non-metal or compound ion: magnesium chloride, potassium nitrate.
- A simple anion ends in **-ide**: oxide O²⁻, sulfide S²⁻, nitride N³⁻, chloride Cl⁻. Hydroxide OH⁻ is the compound-ion exception that also ends in -ide.
- An anion containing oxygen often ends in **-ate**: sulfate, nitrate, carbonate, phosphate.
- A metal that forms more than one ion takes a Roman numeral equal to its charge: copper(I) oxide is Cu₂O, copper(II) oxide is CuO.
- Ammonium compounds behave like metal compounds in naming: ammonium chloride is NH₄Cl.

## Dot-and-cross reminders

| Compound | What the diagram shows |
|---|---|
| MgO | One [Mg]²⁺ and one [O]²⁻; oxide shows eight outer electrons, two as crosses |
| CaCl₂ | One [Ca]²⁺ and two separate [Cl]⁻; each chloride has eight outer electrons, one a cross |
| Na₂O | Two separate [Na]⁺ and one [O]²⁻; oxide has eight outer electrons, two as crosses |
| AlF₃ | One [Al]³⁺ and three separate [F]⁻; each fluoride has one cross among eight electrons |

Check that the total charge shown on all the ions in the diagram is zero.

## Exam traps

- "Shared electrons" is covalent; ionic bonding is attraction between ions.
- Electron transfer forms the ions; it is not itself the bond.
- No "molecules" of ionic compounds.
- Brackets around compound ions: Ca(OH)₂, not CaOH₂.
- No charges in a compound's formula.
- Nitrate NO₃⁻ is not nitride N³⁻; sulfate SO₄²⁻ is not sulfide S²⁻.
- Do not use group number for transition metal charges.

## Self-test

1. Define ionic bonding.
2. Predict the charge on a barium ion and explain your prediction.
3. Predict the charge on a phosphide ion.
4. Give the formula of the hydroxide ion and the ammonium ion.
5. Write the formula of potassium sulfate.
6. Write the formula of magnesium nitrate.
7. Write the formula of aluminium carbonate.
8. Write the formula of copper(II) hydroxide.
9. Write the formula of ammonium nitrate.
10. Explain why the formula of calcium fluoride is CaF₂.
11. Why is sodium chloride described as a lattice rather than as molecules?
12. Name the compound with formula Fe₂(SO₄)₃.

### Answers

1. Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions in a lattice.
2. 2+, because barium is in Group 2 and loses two electrons to reach a noble gas configuration.
3. 3− (P³⁻), because phosphorus is in Group 15.
4. OH⁻ and NH₄⁺.
5. K₂SO₄
6. Mg(NO₃)₂
7. Al₂(CO₃)₃
8. Cu(OH)₂
9. NH₄NO₃
10. Ca²⁺ has a 2+ charge and F⁻ has a 1− charge, so two fluoride ions balance one calcium ion.
11. Each ion is attracted to all the oppositely charged ions around it in a giant regular arrangement; there are no separate units.
12. Iron(III) sulfate.
