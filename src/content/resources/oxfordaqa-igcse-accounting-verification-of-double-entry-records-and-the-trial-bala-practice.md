## Questions

**1.** State whether each balance appears in the debit column or the credit column of a trial balance: **(a)** returns outwards **[1]**; **(b)** drawings **[1]**; **(c)** bank overdraft **[1]**; **(d)** inventory at the start of the year **[1]**.

**2.** Prepare the trial balance of Cinderfell Print Studio at 31 March from these balances, which are in no order: machinery 24,500; sales 58,900; wages 12,450; trade payables 4,365; inventory at the start of the year 3,820; capital 32,700; trade receivables 5,640; purchases 36,200; bank (in funds) 2,915; returns outwards 1,100; electricity 2,180; drawings 8,400; returns inwards 960. **[8]**

**3.** A trial balance agrees. Explain why this does not prove that the accounts are free from error. **[3]**

**4.** Name the error in each case. In every case the account balances were later listed on a trial balance.

**(a)** Cash of 740 from a customer, Mr Dunmore, is debited to his account and credited to bank. **[1]**
**(b)** A supplier invoice for 1,150 for goods bought on credit is lost and never recorded. **[1]**
**(c)** The rent account is overadded by 300 and the interest received account is overadded by 300. **[1]**
**(d)** A credit sale of 520 to Mr Brasher is debited to the account of Mr Brashear. **[1]**
**(e)** A computer costing 1,400 is debited to the office expenses account. **[1]**
**(f)** A supplier invoice for 2,050 is read as 2,500 and recorded as 2,500 in purchases and in the supplier's account. **[1]**
**(g)** State what the six cases have in common in their effect on the trial balance. **[1]**

**5.** Name each error, then state which trial balance column total is higher and by how much.

**(a)** The sales account is overcast (added up too high) by 400. **[2]**
**(b)** A cash purchase of goods for 275 is debited to purchases, but the credit to cash is forgotten. **[2]**
**(c)** A receipt of 3,810 is debited to bank correctly but credited to the customer's account as 3,180. **[2]**
**(d)** Rent of 900 is paid by cheque. Rent is debited with 900 and bank is credited with 950. **[2]**

**6.** A trial balance has a debit total of 70,415 and a credit total of 70,235.

**(a)** Explain why the difference suggests a transposition. **[2]**
**(b)** The ledger balance on the wages account is 14,350 but the trial balance lists it as 14,530. Show that this explains the difference and state the corrected debit total. **[3]**

**7.** A draft trial balance for Tormund Laundry shows a debit total of 88,150 and a credit total of 99,200. Three slips are found: drawings of 5,000 were listed in the credit column; wages of 21,450 were listed as 21,540; insurance of 1,140 was left off the list. Calculate the corrected totals and show that they agree. **[7]**

**8.** An owner says, "Our trial balance agrees, so I can sign the accounts as correct." Evaluate this statement. **[6]**

**9.** The draft trial balance of Dunsmoor Stationers shows a debit total of 78,650 and a credit total of 73,950.

**(a)** Calculate the difference. **[1]**
**(b)** The difference is exactly twice the bank overdraft of 2,350, which was listed in the debit column. Explain why this causes a difference twice the size of the balance. **[3]**
**(c)** State the corrected totals. **[2]**
**(d)** After correction the trial balance agrees. Two further errors are then found. For each, name the error and state whether the trial balance would have shown it: **(i)** a supplier invoice of 1,800 was never entered anywhere; **(ii)** a cheque for 640 for repairs to a machine was debited to the machinery account. **[4]**

**10.** Distinguish between an error of omission and a partial omission, and state the effect of each on the trial balance. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) Credit [1]. (b) Debit [1]. (c) Credit, because an overdraft is owed to the bank [1]. (d) Debit [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that give a reason, as in part (c), show the rule is understood, but a correct column alone is what each part asks for.

**2.**
Trial balance of Cinderfell Print Studio at 31 March [1]

| Account | Debit | Credit |
|---|---:|---:|
| Machinery | 24,500 | |
| Inventory at start of year | 3,820 | |
| Trade receivables | 5,640 | |
| Bank | 2,915 | |
| Purchases | 36,200 | |
| Wages | 12,450 | |
| Electricity | 2,180 | |
| Drawings | 8,400 | |
| Returns inwards | 960 | |
| Sales | | 58,900 |
| Trade payables | | 4,365 |
| Returns outwards | | 1,100 |
| Capital | | 32,700 |
| Totals | 97,065 [1] | 97,065 [1] |

All nine debit items in the debit column [2], all four credit items in the credit column [2], and a statement that the totals agree [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Heading, both totals and a visible total line are easy to omit, and a column that is added wrongly makes a correct list look wrong, so add each column twice.

**3.** The trial balance only checks that the debit total equals the credit total [1]. Some errors affect debits and credits equally or leave both sides complete, such as an omission or a compensating error [1], so they are present while the totals agree. For example, a purchase of 1,150 that is never recorded changes neither total relative to the other [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A general statement about "accuracy" tends to earn less than naming an error that leaves the totals equal and saying why.

**4.** (a) Complete reversal [1]. (b) Omission [1]. (c) Compensating [1]. (d) Commission [1]. (e) Principle [1]. (f) Original entry [1]. (g) In each case the debit total still equals the credit total, so the trial balance agrees [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Look for what is wrong: the sides (reversal), the amount (original entry), the account (commission), the class (principle), a missing transaction (omission) or two errors (compensating).

**5.** (a) Addition error [1]; credit column higher by 400 [1]. (b) Partial omission [1]; debit column higher by 275 [1]. (c) Transposition [1]; debit column higher by 630, since 3,810 − 3,180 = 630 [1]. (d) Unequal posting [1]; credit column higher by 50, since 950 − 900 = 50 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the error and the direction of the difference are separate points, so both are worth stating clearly.

**6.** (a) Swapping two digits changes a number by a multiple of 9 [1]; 70,415 − 70,235 = 180 and 180 = 9 × 20, which fits [1]. (b) 14,530 − 14,350 = 180, which equals the difference [1]; the debit column is overstated [1]; corrected debit total = 70,415 − 180 = **70,235**, which equals the credit total [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Explaining why the figure divides by 9 usually earns more than saying "it is a transposition" without evidence.

**7.** Draft difference = 99,200 − 88,150 = 11,050 (credit higher). Drawings: debit column 5,000 too low and credit column 5,000 too high [1]. Wages: debit column 21,540 − 21,450 = 90 too high [1]. Insurance: debit column 1,140 too low [1]. Corrected debit total = 88,150 + 5,000 − 90 + 1,140 [1] = **94,200** [1]. Corrected credit total = 99,200 − 5,000 = **94,200** [1]. The totals agree [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A separate line for each correction makes it easier to follow your work, and credit is more likely when working is visible.

**8.** The trial balance does show that total debits equal total credits [1], and it supplies the balances for the financial statements [1]. However, it does not reveal errors such as omission, compensating errors, commission, complete reversal, original entry and principle [1]. For example, a transaction never recorded leaves both totals equal while the accounts are wrong [1]. Other checks, such as control accounts and bank reconciliation, test different parts of the records [1]. Judgement: the owner is wrong to treat agreement as proof; the trial balance is a useful first check, not a guarantee [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation question tends to reward a balanced answer that ends with a reasoned judgement, not a list of facts.

**9.** (a) 78,650 − 73,950 = **4,700** (debit higher) [1]. (b) The overdraft is a credit balance, so it should be in the credit column [1]. Putting it in the debit column adds 2,350 to the debit total [1] and leaves 2,350 out of the credit total, so the gap is 2 × 2,350 = 4,700 [1]. (c) Corrected debit total = 78,650 − 2,350 = **76,300** [1]; corrected credit total = 73,950 + 2,350 = **76,300** [1]. (d)(i) Omission [1]; the trial balance would not show it [1]. (d)(ii) Principle, since repairs are an expense and not an asset [1]; the trial balance would not show it [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the movement on both columns, because moving a balance changes two totals and answers that adjust only one are often incomplete.

**10.** Omission: a whole transaction is left out of the books [1], so debits and credits stay equal and the trial balance agrees [1]. Partial omission: only one side of the transaction is recorded [1], so the totals differ and the trial balance shows an error [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The comparison earns marks only when each term is defined and tied to its effect on the totals.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing an agreeing trial balance as proof of correct records.
- Putting an overdraft, loan or trade payables in the debit column.
- Moving a balance to the right column on one side only and not adjusting both totals.
- Naming an error without saying which total is higher.
- Mixing up omission and partial omission.
- Calling commission an error of principle, or the reverse.
- Showing totals without the adding, so one slip hides the method.
- Forcing totals to agree by changing a figure.
