These notes condense section 3.10.2, Moving charges in a magnetic field, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), in Section 3.10 Magnetic fields (International A-level only). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [moving charges study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moving-charges-in-a-magnetic-field-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics/); test yourself with the [moving charges practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-moving-charges-in-a-magnetic-field-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-practice/). The force on a current-carrying wire is in the [magnetic flux density revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-magnetic-flux-density-oxfordaqa-alevel-physics-revision-notes/).

Data: e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C; electron 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg; proton 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg; alpha particle 6.64 × 10⁻²⁷ kg, charge +2e.

## Equations

| Equation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| F = BQv | Force on a moving charge | v perpendicular to B |
| BQv = mv²/r | Circular motion | Magnetic force is the centripetal force |
| r = mv/(BQ) = p/(BQ) | Radius | Larger for faster or heavier particles |
| T = 2πm/(BQ) | Period | Independent of speed and radius |
| f = BQ/(2πm) | Orbit frequency; cyclotron p.d. frequency | Independent of speed |
| e/m = 2V/(B²r²) | Specific charge from a beam accelerated through V | Combines eV = ½mv² and r = mv/(eB) |
| v = E/B | Speed passed undeflected by crossed E and B fields | Independent of Q and m |
| Ek(max) = B²Q²R²/(2m) | Cyclotron of dee radius R | Independent of the gap p.d. |

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Magnetic force on a charge | BQv, perpendicular to both v and B |
| Centripetal force | Resultant force towards the centre of a circular path |
| Specific charge | Charge per unit mass, Q/m, in C kg⁻¹ |
| Cyclotron | Accelerator: two dees in a uniform magnetic field, with an alternating p.d. across the gap |
| Dee | Hollow D-shaped electrode; no electric field inside, so the path is a semicircle |

## Condensed recall

- No force if the charge is at rest or moving along the field.
- Fleming's left-hand rule: second finger along the velocity for a positive charge, opposite it for a negative charge.
- The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work: speed and kinetic energy stay constant.
- A constant-size force perpendicular to motion gives a circle.
- The period does not depend on speed: faster particles move in bigger circles in the same time.
- Cyclotron: semicircles in the dees, acceleration only in the gap, fixed frequency, radius grows each crossing.
- Cyclotron energy is limited by B and the dee radius, not by the gap p.d.

## Comparing two particles

| Shared quantity | Radius proportional to |
|---|---|
| Speed v | m/Q |
| Momentum p | 1/Q |
| Kinetic energy | √m / Q |
| Accelerating p.d. | √(m/Q) |

Alpha against proton: same speed, alpha radius about twice; same kinetic energy, radii almost equal.

## Worked reminders

- Proton at 4.0 × 10⁵ m s⁻¹ in 0.25 T: F = **1.6 × 10⁻¹⁴ N**.
- Electron at 2.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹ in 5.0 mT: r = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × 2.0 × 10⁷ / (1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 5.0 × 10⁻³) = **0.023 m**.
- Proton cyclotron, B = 0.60 T, R = 0.35 m: f = **9.1 MHz**, maximum energy **2.1 MeV**.

## Exam traps

- Pointing the second finger along an electron's velocity.
- Saying the magnetic force speeds a particle up or slows it down.
- Using e for an alpha particle; its charge is 2e.
- Claiming faster particles orbit more often.
- Stating that a larger gap p.d. gives a higher final energy in a cyclotron.
- Forgetting that the radius is squared in e/m = 2V/(B²r²), so its percentage uncertainty doubles.

## Self-test

1. Why does a magnetic field not change the speed of a charged particle?
2. Find the force on an alpha particle at 1.5 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 0.40 T field.
3. An electron moves east; the field points vertically upwards. Which way is the force?
4. Derive r = mv/(BQ).
5. Show that the period of the orbit does not depend on the speed.
6. A particle's speed is doubled in the same field. What happens to its radius and its period?
7. A proton and an alpha particle move at the same speed in the same field. Compare their radii.
8. Electrons accelerated through 180 V move in a circle of radius 5.0 cm in a 0.90 mT field. Find e/m.
9. Crossed fields: E = 2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ and B = 0.010 T. Which speed is passed undeflected?
10. Why is there no electric field inside a dee?
11. Why can the alternating p.d. in a cyclotron have a fixed frequency?
12. A cyclotron's dee radius is doubled with the same field. By what factor does the maximum kinetic energy change?

### Answers

1. The force is perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work on the particle.
2. F = 0.40 × 3.20 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.5 × 10⁶ = **1.9 × 10⁻¹³ N**.
3. Treat it as conventional current west: the force is **north**.
4. BQv = mv²/r, so r = mv/(BQ).
5. T = 2πr/v = 2π(mv/BQ)/v = 2πm/(BQ): v cancels.
6. Radius **doubles**; period **unchanged**.
7. r ∝ m/Q: the alpha has about 4 times the mass and 2 times the charge, so its radius is about **twice** as large.
8. e/m = 2 × 180 / ((0.90 × 10⁻³)² × 0.050²) = **1.8 × 10¹¹ C kg⁻¹**.
9. v = E/B = **2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹**.
10. The dee is a hollow conductor; the p.d. is applied between the dees, so the field is in the gap only.
11. The time for each semicircle, πm/(BQ), is the same at every speed.
12. Ek ∝ R², so it rises by a factor of **4**.
