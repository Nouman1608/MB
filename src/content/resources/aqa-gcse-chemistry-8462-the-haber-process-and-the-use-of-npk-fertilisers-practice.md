These questions cover section **4.10.4 The Haber process and the use of NPK fertilisers (chemistry only)** of
the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on
Paper 2 in the separate chemistry qualification. Questions 6, 7 and 8 test the equilibrium and trade-off
statements that the specification marks (HT only), and are labelled **Higher tier only**. The data in
Question 7 are invented for practice.

Learn the content first in the
[study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-the-haber-process-and-the-use-of-npk-fertilisers/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-the-haber-process-and-the-use-of-npk-fertilisers-revision-notes/).

Relative atomic masses: H = 1, N = 14, O = 16, S = 32.

## Questions

**1.** The Haber process makes ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen.

**(a)** Write the balanced symbol equation for the reaction. Use the symbol for a reversible reaction. **[2]**
**(b)** State a source of the nitrogen and a source of the hydrogen. **[2]**
**(c)** State the catalyst, the temperature and the pressure used. **[3]**

**2.** The gases leaving the reactor are cooled.

**(a)** Explain how cooling separates the ammonia from nitrogen and hydrogen. **[2]**
**(b)** State what happens to the unreacted nitrogen and hydrogen and why. **[2]**

**3.** 250 dm³ of nitrogen reacts completely with hydrogen at constant temperature and pressure.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of hydrogen needed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume of ammonia formed. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the total volume of gas decreases during the reaction. **[1]**

**4.** NPK fertilisers contain compounds of three elements.

**(a)** Name the three elements. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why farmers use NPK fertilisers. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why NPK fertilisers are described as formulations. **[1]**

**5.** Ammonia is used to make ammonium nitrate and ammonium sulfate.

**(a)** Name the acid used to make each salt from ammonia. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation for ammonia reacting with nitric acid. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄. **[3]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) The percentage yield of ammonia at equilibrium falls as the temperature rises.

**(a)** Deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the temperature used in industry is about 450 °C and not a much lower temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** State the effect of the iron catalyst on the position of equilibrium and on the rate. **[2]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) The table shows the percentage of ammonia at equilibrium at different temperatures and pressures.

| Temperature / °C | 100 atm | 200 atm | 300 atm |
|---|---|---|---|
| 350 | 40 | 55 | 63 |
| 450 | 16 | 28 | 38 |
| 550 | 6 | 11 | 17 |

**(a)** State the effect of increasing the pressure at 450 °C and explain it in terms of the equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the conditions that give the highest percentage of ammonia and explain why they are not used. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the increase in percentage of ammonia when the pressure is raised from 100 to 200 atm at 450 °C. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Explain how the commercial conditions of about 450 °C, 200 atmospheres and an iron catalyst are related to the cost of energy, the cost of equipment, the rate and the equilibrium position. **[6]**

**9.** Phosphate rock is mined and used to make fertilisers.

**(a)** Explain why phosphate rock cannot be used directly as a fertiliser. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the products of treating phosphate rock with nitric acid. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the products of treating phosphate rock with sulfuric acid. **[2]**

**10.** A student makes ammonium sulfate in the laboratory and compares it with industrial production.

**(a)** Describe how the student could make crystals of ammonium sulfate from ammonia solution and dilute sulfuric acid. **[4]**
**(b)** Give **two** differences between the laboratory and industrial production. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ [formulae and balance 1; reversible symbol 1] **[2]**
**(b)** Nitrogen: the **air** [1]; hydrogen: **natural gas** (methane) [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **Iron** catalyst [1]; about **450 °C** [1]; about **200 atmospheres** [1] **[3]**

**2. (a)** Ammonia has a **higher boiling point** than nitrogen and hydrogen [1], so it **liquefies** on cooling while the others stay as gases [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** They are **recycled** (returned to the reactor) [1], so they are not wasted and the overall conversion is high [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 3 × 250 = **750 dm³** [1]
**(b)** 2 × 250 = **500 dm³** [1]
**(c)** 4 volumes of reactants become 2 volumes of product (fewer gas molecules) [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** **Nitrogen, phosphorus, potassium** [1] [1] [1] **[3]**
**(b)** They provide the elements plants need [1], which improves **growth and crop yield / agricultural productivity** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** They are **mixtures** of salts [1] in carefully chosen proportions. **[1]**

**5. (a)** Ammonium nitrate: **nitric acid** [1]; ammonium sulfate: **sulfuric acid** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** NH₃ + HNO₃ → NH₄NO₃ [1]
**(c)** Mr = 2 × (14 + 4) + 32 + 64 = 132 [1]; mass of N = 28 [1]; 28 ÷ 132 × 100 = **21.2%** [1] **[3]**

**6. (a)** **Exothermic** [1]; increasing the temperature favours the endothermic (reverse) direction, so the yield falls [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At a much lower temperature the **rate is too slow** [1], so a compromise of reasonable yield and speed is chosen [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** No effect on the position of equilibrium [1]; it increases the rate [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** The percentage of ammonia **increases** (16 to 28 to 38) [1]; the forward reaction produces **fewer gas molecules** (4 to 2), so the position shifts to the right [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **350 °C and 300 atm** (63%) [1]; at 350 °C the **rate is too slow** [1] and 300 atm needs **very strong, costly equipment and more energy** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** 28 − 16 = **12 percentage points** [1] **[1]**

**8.** Up to six, one mark per point:
- High pressure (200 atm) gives a higher yield as there are fewer gas molecules on the right [1].
- High pressure also increases the rate [1].
- Very high pressure needs strong, expensive pipes and vessels and much energy for compression [1].
- Temperature: the forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature gives a higher yield [1].
- But a low temperature makes the rate too slow, so 450 °C is a compromise between rate and yield [1].
- The iron catalyst speeds up the reaction without changing the equilibrium position, allowing a lower temperature [1].
- Unreacted gases are recycled, so a modest yield per pass is acceptable [1].
(maximum **[6]**)

**9. (a)** It is **insoluble**, so plants cannot absorb it [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **Phosphoric acid** [1] and **calcium nitrate** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **Calcium phosphate** [1] and **calcium sulfate** [1] **[2]**

**10. (a)** Add a measured volume of ammonia solution to a flask with indicator [1]; add sulfuric acid from a burette until the indicator changes colour (neutralisation) [1]; repeat without indicator using the same volumes [1]; **evaporate** some of the water and leave to **crystallise**, then filter and dry [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: laboratory is **small scale**, industry very large; laboratory is a **batch** process, industry **continuous**; industry uses **integrated processes** and raw materials made on site; industry recovers and reuses heat. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the catalyst increases the yield.
- Saying a high temperature increases the yield of ammonia.
- Forgetting to mention that unreacted gases are recycled.
- Only quoting yield effects without the effect on rate.
- Writing fertiliser formulae without checking the Mr.
