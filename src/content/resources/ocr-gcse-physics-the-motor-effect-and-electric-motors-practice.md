## Questions

**1.** State what the first finger, the second finger and the thumb of the left hand represent in Fleming's left-hand rule. **[3]**

**2.** A wire of length 0.15 m lies at right angles to a uniform magnetic field of flux density 0.35 T. The current in the wire is 4.0 A. Calculate the force on the wire. **[3]**

**3.** A straight conductor with 0.30 m inside a uniform 0.20 T field, perpendicular to it, feels a force of 0.90 N. Calculate the current in the conductor. **[3]**

**4.** A short wire rests across two metal rails between the poles of a horseshoe magnet. When a switch is closed, the wire jumps out of the gap. Explain why the wire moves. **[4]**

**5.** A wire carries a current that flows out of the page towards you. It is in a uniform field that points from left to right across the page. State the direction of the force on the wire and explain how you worked it out. **[3]**

**6.** Explain how a d.c. motor keeps a coil turning in the same direction. **[5]**

**7.** A wire 2.5 cm long carries a current of 800 mA at right angles to a field of 0.60 T. Calculate the force on the wire. **[3]**

**8.** A strong magnet sits on a top-pan balance. A rigid wire is held in a fixed clamp so that 0.050 m of it lies at right angles to the field between the poles. The flux density is 0.12 T. A current of 5.0 A is switched on.

**(a)** Calculate the force on the wire. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the reading on the balance changes. **[1]**
**(c)** The force on the magnet is equal to your answer to part (a). Use g = 10 N/kg to calculate the change in the balance reading, in grams. **[2]**

**9.** A student measures the force on a wire with 0.10 m in a uniform field for four values of current.

| Current (A) | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Force (N) | 0.040 | 0.081 | 0.119 | 0.160 |

**(a)** State what the data show about how force depends on current, and justify your answer with calculations. **[2]**
**(b)** Use the data for a current of 3.0 A to calculate the magnetic flux density. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the graph of force against current. **[1]**

**10.** A wire in a magnetic field is pushed upwards by the motor effect. State the direction of the force after each change below, taking each change from the starting situation.

**(a)** The current is reversed. **[1]**
**(b)** The magnet is turned over so its poles swap. **[1]**
**(c)** The current is reversed and the magnet is turned over. **[1]**
**(d)** The wire is turned to lie along the field lines. **[1]**

**11.** A rectangular coil in a uniform field of 0.25 T has two opposite sides, each with 0.060 m of wire at right angles to the field. The current in the coil is 1.8 A.

**(a)** Calculate the force on one of these sides. **[3]**
**(b)** State the direction of the force on the other side compared with the first, and explain how the two forces make the coil turn. **[2]**
**(c)** The current is increased to 3.6 A. State the new force on one side. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the current in the coil must be reversed every half turn for the coil to keep turning. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** First finger: the magnetic field (north to south) [1]. Second finger: the current (positive to negative) [1]. Thumb: the force or motion [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Listing "field, current, motion" in the wrong order can lose credit, so match each digit to its quantity explicitly.

**2.** F = B I l [1]. F = 0.35 × 4.0 × 0.15 [1]. **F = 0.21 N** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the equation and the substitution. A clear substitution lets a reader follow your method and spot a slip.

**3.** I = F / (B l) [1]. I = 0.90 / (0.20 × 0.30) = 0.90 / 0.060 [1]. **I = 15 A** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Rearrange first, then substitute. Substituting into F = B I l and solving afterwards is fine, but keep each line clear.

**4.** There is a current in the wire, and so the wire has its own magnetic field [1]. This interacts with the field of the magnet [1]. The result is a force on the wire [1]. The force is at right angles to both the field and the current, so the wire is pushed out of the gap [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A four-mark "explain" needs a chain of linked reasons. "It is pushed by the magnet" gives only one point.

**5.** Field points right: first finger right [1]. Current is towards you: second finger towards you [1]. The thumb points **up the page**, so the force is upwards [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State both input directions before the result. A correct direction with no reasoning may not gain all the marks on an "explain how" question.

**6.** The coil carries a current in a magnetic field, so a force acts on each side [1]. The currents in the two sides are in opposite directions, so the forces are in opposite directions [1]. These forces turn the coil [1]. After half a turn, the sides have swapped places, so the forces would turn the coil back [1]. Reversing the current every half turn keeps the forces turning the coil the same way [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The step linking half a turn to the need for a current reversal is the one most often missing. Name the component only if you are sure, because the specification does not require the structure.

**7.** Convert: l = 0.025 m and I = 0.800 A [1]. F = 0.60 × 0.800 × 0.025 [1]. **F = 0.012 N** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Unconverted values give an answer out by a factor of 100,000. Write the converted values on their own line.

**8.** **(a)** F = B I l [1]. F = 0.12 × 5.0 × 0.050 [1]. **F = 0.030 N** [1].
**(b)** The wire exerts an equal and opposite force on the magnet (Newton's third law), so the force on the magnet changes the balance reading [1].
**(c)** Mass change = F / g = 0.030 / 10 = 0.0030 kg [1]. **Change = 3.0 g** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the balance reading to the third law pair explicitly. Convert kilograms to grams at the end and show the line.

**9.** **(a)** Force divided by current is almost constant: 0.040, 0.0405, 0.0397, 0.040 N per A [1]. So force is directly proportional to current [1].
**(b)** B = F / (I l) [1]. B = 0.119 / (3.0 × 0.10) [1]. **B = 0.40 T (to 2 significant figures)** [1].
**(c)** A straight line through the origin [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Proportional" needs evidence, such as a constant ratio or a line through the origin. Saying "it goes up" is not enough.

**10.** **(a)** The force is **downwards** [1]. **(b)** The force is **downwards** [1]. **(c)** The force is **upwards** (two reversals cancel) [1]. **(d)** There is **no force** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Parts (a) and (b) each reverse one quantity. Do not carry a change from an earlier part into the next one.

**11.** **(a)** F = B I l [1]. F = 0.25 × 1.8 × 0.060 [1]. **F = 0.027 N** [1].
**(b)** The force on the other side is the same size but in the **opposite direction** [1]. Opposite forces on opposite sides of the axle give a turning effect on the coil [1].
**(c)** **0.054 N** (force is proportional to current) [1].
**(d)** After half a turn the sides have swapped positions, so without a reversal the forces would push the coil back towards where it came from [1]. Reversing the current keeps each force turning the coil in the same direction [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the forces act in opposite directions on opposite sides. "A force turns the coil" alone does not explain why it turns.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the right hand, or mixing up first finger and second finger.
- Using electron flow instead of conventional current.
- Forgetting to convert cm to m or mA to A.
- Missing the unit on a force, or writing T for a force.
- Giving a direction without the reasoning that earned it.
- Not saying the force is at right angles to both the field and the current.
- Reversing the force when both the current and the field are reversed.
- Describing a motor without saying why the current must be reversed every half turn.
- Writing that doubling the current doubles the flux density. The field is the magnet's, so B is unchanged.
