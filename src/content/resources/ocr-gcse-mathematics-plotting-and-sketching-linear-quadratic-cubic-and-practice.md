These questions follow sections 7.01a to 7.01c of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560 specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): coordinates, plotting from tables, and recognising and sketching graphs. Questions 1 to 8 and 12 are Foundation and Higher content. Questions 9 to 11 are **(Higher tier only)**. Learn the methods first from the [study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-plotting-and-sketching-linear-quadratic-cubic-and/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-plotting-and-sketching-linear-quadratic-cubic-and-revision-notes/). Use squared paper for the plotting questions, and draw sketches freehand.

## Questions

**1.** P(−4, 3), Q(2, 3) and R(2, −1) are three vertices of rectangle PQRS.

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of S. **[1]**
**(b)** In which quadrant does P lie? **[1]**
**(c)** Work out the area of the rectangle. **[1]**

**2.** A table of values is made for y = 6 − 4x. The values at x = −2, 0 and 3 are 14, 6 and −6.

**(a)** Work out y when x = −1, 1 and 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Plot the graph of y = 6 − 4x for x from −2 to 3. **[2]**
**(c)** Use your graph to estimate the value of x when y = 0. **[1]**

**3.** The equation of a line is 5x + 2y = 20. Work out y when x = 0, 2 and 4, then draw the line. **[3]**

**4.** A table of values is made for y = x² − 5x + 4. The values at x = −1, 1 and 6 are 10, 0 and 10.

**(a)** Work out y when x = 0, 2, 3, 4 and 5. **[2]**
**(b)** Plot the graph of y = x² − 5x + 4 for x from −1 to 6. **[2]**
**(c)** Use symmetry to find the coordinates of the turning point. **[2]**
**(d)** Write down the roots of x² − 5x + 4 = 0 from your graph. **[1]**

**5.** A table of values is made for y = x³ + x² − 2x, with y = 0 when x = −2.

**(a)** Work out y when x = −1, 1 and 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Plot the graph of y = x³ + x² − 2x for x from −2 to 2, and read off where it crosses the x-axis. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the y-intercept. **[1]**

**6.** Write down the equation of each graph.

**(a)** A horizontal line through (0, −4). **[1]**
**(b)** A vertical line through (5, 0). **[1]**
**(c)** A straight line through the origin and through (1, −3). **[1]**
**(d)** A U-shaped curve through the origin, with its lowest point at the origin and passing through (2, 4). **[1]**
**(e)** A smooth curve through the origin and (1, 1), rising throughout, in quadrants 1 and 3. **[1]**
**(f)** Two separate branches in quadrants 1 and 3, through (1, 1), never touching either axis. **[1]**

**7.** A table of values is made for y = x + 9/x. The values at x = 1 and x = 3 are 10 and 6.

**(a)** Work out y when x = 2, 4 and 6. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out y when x = −3. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the graph has no point at x = 0. **[1]**

**8.** A curve has equation y = x² − 6x − 16.

**(a)** Solve x² − 6x − 16 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the y-intercept. **[1]**
**(c)** Use symmetry to find the turning point. **[2]**
**(d)** Sketch the curve, labelling the intercepts and the turning point. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Solve x² − 8x + 5 = 0. Give both solutions to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A curve has equation y = x² + 10x + 14.

**(a)** Write x² + 10x + 14 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the turning point. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the exact roots of x² + 10x + 14 = 0. **[2]**
**(d)** Sketch the curve, labelling the turning point and the y-intercept. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) A curve has equation y = 5 − 4x − x².

**(a)** Write the equation in the form y = a − (x + p)². **[2]**
**(b)** State the turning point and whether it is a maximum or a minimum. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the roots of 5 − 4x − x² = 0. **[2]**
**(d)** Sketch the curve, labelling the turning point and the intercepts. **[2]**

**12.** The curve y = x² + bx + c crosses the x-axis at x = −5 and x = 1.

**(a)** Find the values of b and c. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the turning point. **[2]**
**(c)** Sketch the curve, labelling the intercepts and the turning point. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** S has the x-value of P and the y-value of R: **(−4, −1)**. [1]
**(b)** x is negative and y is positive: **second quadrant**. [1]
**(c)** Width 6, height 4, so area **24 square units**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* write the coordinates in brackets in (x, y) order. A bare pair of numbers is easy to read as the wrong way round.

**2. (a)** x = −1: 6 − 4(−1) = 10 [1]. x = 1 gives 2 and x = 2 gives −2. [1]
**(b)** Plot (−2, 14), (−1, 10), (0, 6), (1, 2), (2, −2), (3, −6) [1]. Join with a ruler in one straight line [1].
**(c)** The line crosses the x-axis at **x = 1.5** (reading from the graph). [1]
*Examiner insight:* a plotted point that is a small square out of place often shows an arithmetic slip, so check any point that breaks the straight-line pattern.

**3.** x = 0 gives y = 10, x = 2 gives y = 5, x = 4 gives y = 0. [1] for the first value, [1] for the other two. The line passes through (0, 10), (2, 5) and (4, 0) and is drawn with a ruler. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* when the equation has x and y on the same side, rearrange to y = (20 − 5x)/2 and show this step, so a reader can follow your method.

**4. (a)** Substitute each value, for example x = 3: 9 − 15 + 4 = −2. [1] The values are 4, −2, −2, 0, 4 for x = 0, 2, 3, 4, 5. [1]
**(b)** All eight points plotted correctly. [1] A smooth parabola through them with no straight segments. [1]
**(c)** The curve is symmetrical, with equal y-values at x = 2 and x = 3, so the turning point has x = 2.5. [1] y = 6.25 − 12.5 + 4 = −2.25, so **(2.5, −2.25)**. [1]
**(d)** The curve crosses the x-axis at **x = 1 and x = 4**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* the turning point is often between two plotted points, so state both coordinates and not only the x-value.

**5. (a)** x = −1: −1 + 1 + 2 = 2 [1]. x = 1 gives 0 and x = 2 gives 8. [1]
**(b)** Plot (−2, 0), (−1, 2), (0, 0), (1, 0), (2, 8). [1] Join with one smooth curve that rises, falls, then rises, crossing the x-axis at **−2, 0 and 1**. [1]
**(c)** At x = 0, y = 0, so the y-intercept is **(0, 0)**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* with so few points, the dip between x = 0 and x = 1 is easy to flatten. Add a point such as x = 0.5 (giving −0.625) to place the turn.

**6.** **(a)** y = −4 [1]. **(b)** x = 5 [1]. **(c)** y = −3x [1]. **(d)** y = x² [1]. **(e)** y = x³ [1]. **(f)** y = 1/x [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* y = −4 and x = 5 are different kinds of line. Horizontal lines are y = a number; vertical lines are x = a number.

**7. (a)** x = 2: 2 + 9/2 = 6.5 [1]. x = 4 gives 6.25 and x = 6 gives 7.5. [1]
**(b)** −3 + 9/(−3) = −3 − 3 = **−6**. [1]
**(c)** Putting x = 0 means dividing by 0, which has no value, so no point can be plotted. [1]
*Examiner insight:* in a table with negative x, put the negative number in brackets and divide with care. The sign of 9/(−3) is a common source of error.

**8. (a)** (x − 8)(x + 2) = 0 [1], so **x = 8 or x = −2**. [1]
**(b)** At x = 0, y = **−16**, so the point is (0, −16). [1]
**(c)** The mean of the roots is (8 − 2)/2 = 3 [1]. y = 9 − 18 − 16 = −25, so **(3, −25)**. [1]
**(d)** A U-shaped parabola with labelled axes, crossing at (−2, 0), (8, 0) and (0, −16), with minimum (3, −25). [1]
*Examiner insight:* a sketch that shows the labelled points in the correct order along the x-axis, with the minimum under the midpoint of the roots, is easy to check.

**9.** x = (8 ± √(64 − 20))/2 [1] = (8 ± √44)/2 = 4 ± √11 [1]. **x = 7.32 or x = 0.68** [1]
*Examiner insight:* show the substitution into the formula before simplifying. Keep the exact form until the final rounding.

**10. (a)** (x + 5)² − 25 + 14 [1] = **(x + 5)² − 11**. [1]
**(b)** Turning point **(−5, −11)**. [1]
**(c)** (x + 5)² = 11 [1], so **x = −5 ± √11**. [1]
**(d)** A U-shaped curve with minimum (−5, −11) below the x-axis, crossing the y-axis at (0, 14) [1] and the x-axis twice, at about −8.32 and −1.68. [1]
*Examiner insight:* write the turning point as (−5, −11), not (5, −11): the sign flips because the bracket contains x + 5.

**11. (a)** 5 − 4x − x² = −(x² + 4x) + 5 = −[(x + 2)² − 4] + 5 [1] = **9 − (x + 2)²**. [1]
**(b)** Turning point **(−2, 9)**, a **maximum**. [1]
**(c)** (x + 2)² = 9 [1], so x + 2 = ±3 and **x = 1 or x = −5**. [1]
**(d)** An upside-down parabola with its maximum at (−2, 9) [1], crossing the x-axis at −5 and 1 and the y-axis at (0, 5). [1]
*Examiner insight:* for a negative x² term, take the minus sign outside the bracket first. It is a common source of wrong signs in the constant.

**12. (a)** (x + 5)(x − 1) [1] = x² + 4x − 5, so **b = 4 and c = −5**. [1]
**(b)** Mean of roots (−5 + 1)/2 = −2 [1]. y = 4 − 8 − 5 = −9, so **(−2, −9)**. [1]
**(c)** U-shaped parabola crossing the x-axis at (−5, 0) and (1, 0) and the y-axis at (0, −5) [1], with minimum (−2, −9) below the midpoint of the roots. [1]
*Examiner insight:* working backwards from roots to an equation is checked by substitution: put x = 1 into your answer and confirm it gives 0.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing coordinates as (y, x).
- Squaring a negative as a negative number when completing a table.
- Joining plotted curve points with straight lines or a pointed bottom.
- Joining the two branches of a reciprocal graph.
- Giving the x-value of a turning point and forgetting the y-value.
- Forgetting the y-intercept, which is the constant term.
- Using (p, q) instead of (−p, q) as the turning point.
- Leaving a decimal when an exact answer was requested.
- Drawing a U-shape for a negative x² coefficient.
