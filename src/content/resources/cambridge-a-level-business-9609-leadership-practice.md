All businesses are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** Define *leadership*. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** difference between a leadership theory and a management style. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the role of a supervisor as a leader in a business. **[3]**

**4.** Identify **two** of Goleman's competencies of emotional intelligence that relate to other people rather than to the leader themselves. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a business might prefer a leader with expert power to one who relies on coercive power. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **one** limitation of trait theory for a business choosing a new manager. **[2]**

**7.** Distinguish between transformational and transactional leadership. **[4]**

## Section B

**8.** Kestrel Bus Company's depot manager, Mr Haddad, was promoted from driver. Drivers like and respect him, and they follow his instructions willingly. He has no control over pay, which is set by head office.

**(a)** Identify **two** sources of power Mr Haddad holds. **[2]**

**(b)** Analyse how his sources of power might affect the performance of the depot. **[6]**

**9.** Larchmont Labs, a small research business, has 14 highly qualified scientists. The founder, Dr Iqbal, checks every experiment personally and sets daily tasks. Two scientists have left in a year, and others say they "have no freedom to think".

**(a)** Using the Blake and Mouton grid, explain where Dr Iqbal's leadership might be placed. **[4]**

**(b)** Using contingency theory, evaluate whether Dr Iqbal should change her approach. **[10]**

**10.** Halberton Fashion's operations director, Mrs Linde, is technically brilliant. In meetings she interrupts colleagues, does not notice when staff are upset, and becomes angry when plans are questioned. Staff turnover in her division rose from 12% to 21% in a year.

**(a)** Analyse which **two** EQ competencies Mrs Linde lacks, using evidence from the case. **[6]**

**(b)** Assess whether improving her emotional intelligence would solve the turnover problem. **[8]**

**11.** Evaluate the importance of the qualities of the leader in the success of a business that is going through major change. **[12]**

**12.** The workforce at Dunmore Cement has elected a worker representative, Ms Varga, to sit on a consultation committee with directors. Explain how Ms Varga can act as a leader even though she has no formal authority over other employees. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Leadership is influencing and directing people [1] so that they work willingly towards the objectives of the business [1].

**2.** A management style describes how decisions are made, for example autocratic or democratic [1], whereas a leadership theory tries to explain what makes a leader effective, such as trait or contingency theory [1].

**3.** A supervisor leads a front-line team in its daily work [1], setting an example and keeping tasks on track [1], and passes information between managers and staff so problems are spotted early [1].

**4.** Social awareness [1] and social skills [1].

**5.** Expert power comes from knowledge that staff respect [1], so staff follow willingly and are committed to the work [1]. Coercive power relies on the threat of punishment [1], which produces compliance only and may raise labour turnover, so performance falls when the threat is removed [1].

**6.** No agreed list of traits predicts success in every situation [1], so a candidate with the "right" traits may still fail in this business's circumstances, and able candidates without them may be overlooked [1].

**7.** Transformational leaders inspire followers to commit to a shared vision [1], for example through a compelling purpose and attention to each person's development [1]. Transactional leaders exchange rewards and penalties for meeting set targets [1], which suits stable, routine work but rarely creates commitment beyond the reward [1].

**8. (a)** Referent power, as drivers like and respect him [1]; legitimate power from his position as depot manager [1]. (Expert power from his experience as a driver is also acceptable.)

**(b)** Referent power means drivers follow him willingly [1], so they are likely to cooperate with rota changes and stay longer, cutting recruitment costs [1]. His driving experience gives expert credibility [1], so his advice on routes and safety is accepted, which may improve punctuality [1]. However, he lacks reward power because pay is set by head office [1], so if drivers become dissatisfied with pay he cannot respond, and his referent power may not be enough to prevent absence or turnover [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** Dr Iqbal shows a high concern for the task, checking every experiment and setting daily tasks [1] [1], but a low concern for people, as staff feel they have no freedom [1]. This places her near 9,1, authority-compliance [1].

**(b)** Contingency theory says the effective approach depends on the situation [1]. Here the followers are highly qualified scientists who expect autonomy [1], and the task, research, is unstructured and creative [1], so close control is a poor fit, as shown by two leavers out of 14 [1] and complaints about lack of freedom [1]. A more consultative or delegating approach would use their expertise and may improve innovation and retention [1]. Against change: as founder, Dr Iqbal may hold expertise the business relies on [1], and close checking may protect quality in experiments where errors are costly [1]. Judgement: she should change her approach for most work, delegating experimental design while keeping checks on high-risk work [1], because the loss of skilled scientists threatens the business more than occasional errors [1]. **[14]**

**10. (a)** Self-management: she becomes angry when plans are questioned [1], showing she cannot control her emotions under pressure [1]. Social awareness: she does not notice when staff are upset [1], so she misses signs of stress and resentment [1]. Either competency weakens the division: staff may stop raising problems [1], and feeling ignored may push them to leave, as the rise in turnover suggests [1]. (Social skills, shown by interrupting colleagues, is an acceptable alternative.)

**(b)** Improving EQ could help: if staff leave because of how they are treated, better self-management and social awareness would address the cause [1] and could bring turnover back towards 12%, cutting recruitment and training costs [1]. However, the case does not prove that her behaviour is the only cause [1]; pay, workload or competitors' offers may also matter [1]. EQ is also hard to improve quickly [1], so turnover may stay high in the short term [1]. Judgement: improving her EQ is necessary but may not be sufficient [1]; the business should also use exit interviews to confirm why staff leave [1]. **[14]**

**11.** During major change, staff face uncertainty, so leadership is critical [1]. Qualities such as vision and communication help staff understand why change is needed [1], reducing resistance [1]. Integrity builds the trust needed for staff to accept short-term costs [1]. Decisiveness keeps the change moving [1]. However, other factors matter: finance to fund the change [1], the skills of the workforce [1], market conditions [1], and the structure and systems that support new ways of working [1]. A strong leader with poor finance may still fail [1]. Judgement: the leader's qualities are very important because change depends on people's willing support, but they are a necessary rather than sufficient condition [1]; their importance is greatest where the change is large and staff are sceptical [1]. **[12]**

**12.** Ms Varga can influence colleagues through the trust they placed in her by electing her [1], a form of referent power [1]. She can lead by explaining directors' decisions to staff and representing staff views to directors [1], shaping opinion and helping the workforce reach a shared position [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a source of power without quoting the evidence from the case.
- Placing a leader on the grid without reference to both task and people.
- Applying contingency theory without naming the features of the situation.
- Listing EQ competencies with no consequence for staff or the business.
- Concluding that one factor alone explains success or failure.
