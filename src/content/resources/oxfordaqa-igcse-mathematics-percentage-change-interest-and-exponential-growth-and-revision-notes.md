## Formulas and results

| Idea | Result | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Multiplier, increase of r% | 1 + r/100 | Core |
| Multiplier, decrease of r% | 1 − r/100 | Core |
| New amount | original × multiplier | Core |
| Percentage change | (change ÷ original) × 100 | Core |
| Simple interest | P × r/100 × t | Core |
| Compound interest, working year by year | multiply by the yearly multiplier once per year | Core |
| Repeated change | multiply the multipliers together | Core |
| Original amount (reverse percentage) | new amount ÷ multiplier | Extension only |
| Compound interest formula | value = P(1 + r/100)ⁿ | Extension only |
| Exponential growth and decay | amount = start × kᵗ | Extension only |

In the formula, P is the amount invested, r is the percentage rate of interest and n is the number of years of compound interest.

## Method boxes

**Percentage change**

1. Find the change: new − old.
2. Divide by the original (the starting value).
3. Multiply by 100 and say whether it is an increase or a decrease.

**Simple versus compound**

1. Simple: the interest is the same every year, so interest = P × r/100 × t.
2. Compound: the balance is multiplied by 1 + r/100 each year, so the interest grows.
3. After one year the two are the same. They differ from year 2 onwards.

**Reverse percentage (Extension only)**

1. Write the multiplier for the change that happened.
2. Set up: original × multiplier = new amount.
3. Divide the new amount by the multiplier.
4. Check by multiplying forwards.

**Growth or decay (Extension only)**

1. Write k = 1 + r/100 for growth, or k = 1 − r/100 for decay.
2. Write the model: amount = start × kᵗ.
3. To find a time, try whole values of t until the amount crosses the target. State the first value that works.

## Small worked reminders

**Percentage change.** A jacket is £380 and is reduced by 15%.

```
380 × 0.85 = £323
```

A bike is £70 in a shop and £91 online. The online price is a **30% increase**, because (91 − 70)/70 × 100 = 30.

**Simple interest.** £1,200 at 4% a year for 3 years.

```
1200 × 0.04 × 3 = £144
```

**Compound interest (Core).** £2,000 at 5% a year for 4 years.

```
2000 × 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05 × 1.05 = £2,431.01
```

**Compound interest formula (Extension only).** The same sum is 2000 × 1.05⁴ = £2,431.01 (nearest penny).

**Reverse percentage (Extension only).** A shop price after a 16% increase is £1,566.

```
1566 ÷ 1.16 = £1,350
```

A price after a 28% decrease is £396.

```
396 ÷ 0.72 = £550
```

**Changing rate (Core).** £800 earns 2% in year 1 and 3% in year 2.

```
800 × 1.02 × 1.03 = £840.48
```

**Finding the rate (Extension only).** £4,000 becomes £4,368.10 after 2 years of compound interest.

```
(1 + r/100)² = 4368.10 ÷ 4000 = 1.09203 (rounded)
1 + r/100 = √1.09203 = 1.045
r = 4.5
```

The rate is 4.5% a year.

**Two reverse steps (Extension only).** A price is cut by 10%, and the reduced price is cut by a further 20%. The final price is £144.

```
Overall multiplier = 0.9 × 0.8 = 0.72
Original = 144 ÷ 0.72 = £200
```

**Trial for time (Extension only).** A 250 g sample loses 20% of its mass each hour. When does it first fall below 100 g?

```
Mass after t hours = 250 × 0.8ᵗ
t = 4: 102.4 g   (not yet)
t = 5: 81.92 g   (below 100 g)
```

It first falls below 100 g during the **5th hour**, so the answer is 5 hours.

**Repeated change (Core).** A quantity rises by 10% and then falls by 10%.

```
1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99
```

The overall change is a **1% decrease**.

**Decay (Extension only).** A tank of 6,400 litres loses 10% of its contents each day. After 3 days:

```
6400 × 0.9³ = 4,665.6 litres
```

**Growth (Extension only).** A population of 150 grows by 20% a year. After 5 years:

```
150 × 1.2⁵ = 373 (nearest whole number)
```

## Reading the wording

| The question says | What to do |
|---|---|
| "Increase by 8%" or "decrease by 8%" | Multiply by 1.08 or by 0.92 |
| "Find the percentage change" | (new − old) ÷ old × 100, then state increase or decrease |
| "Simple interest" | Same interest each year, P × r/100 × t |
| "Compound interest" | Multiply by 1 + r/100 once for every year |
| "Before the increase" or "original price" | Divide by the multiplier (Extension only) |
| "After t years" with a repeated percentage | Start × kᵗ (Extension only) |
| "How many years until it exceeds…" | Trial of whole values of t (Extension only) |
| "Overall change" after two or more changes | Multiply all the multipliers, then convert back to a percentage |

To convert an overall multiplier back to a percentage, subtract 1 and multiply by 100. A multiplier of 1.07016 is a 7.016% increase. A multiplier of 0.83 is a 17% decrease.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Simple and compound.** Simple interest uses the original sum every year. Compound interest uses the balance at the start of each year.
- **Original and new.** Percentage change is divided by the original. A reverse percentage divides the final amount by the multiplier.
- **Adding and multiplying.** A rise of 10% then a fall of 10% is 1.1 × 0.9, not zero change.
- **Growth and decay.** Growth has k above 1. Decay has k between 0 and 1.
- **Interest earned and total value.** "Find the interest" means subtract the starting sum. "Find the value" means the whole amount.
- **Percentage points and percent.** A rate moving from 3% to 4% is a rise of one percentage point, but a rise of 33.3% in the rate itself. Say which one you mean.
- **Core method and Extension formula.** The formula P(1 + r/100)ⁿ is Extension only. At Core, show each year's multiplication.

## Quick self-test

1. Increase £520 by 18%.
2. Decrease £520 by 18%.
3. A price goes from £40 to £46. Find the percentage increase.
4. Find the simple interest on £900 at 3% a year for 6 years.
5. Find the total multiplier for three years of compound interest at 7% a year. (Extension only)
6. Find the multiplier for two successive 12% decreases. (Core)
7. After a 15% increase a bill is £345. Find the original bill. (Extension only)
8. Find the value of £2,500 after 2 years at 4% compound interest, using the formula. (Extension only)
9. A radioactive sample of 60 g halves every day. Find the mass after 3 days. (Extension only)
10. A 10% rise followed by a 10% fall: state the single overall percentage change. (Core)

### Answers

1. 520 × 1.18 = **£613.60**
2. 520 × 0.82 = **£426.40**
3. 6/40 × 100 = **15%** increase
4. 900 × 0.03 × 6 = **£162**
5. 1.07³ = **1.225043**
6. 0.88 × 0.88 = **0.7744**
7. 345 ÷ 1.15 = **£300**
8. 2500 × 1.04² = **£2,704**
9. 60 × 0.5³ = **7.5 g**
10. 1.1 × 0.9 = 0.99, so a **1% decrease**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing the finishing value as the divisor in a percentage change. The starting value is the one to use.
- Using 1.5 instead of 1.05 for a 5% increase, or 0.5 instead of 0.95 for a 5% decrease.
- Taking a percentage of the final amount when the question asks for the starting amount. Divide by the multiplier.
- Answering with the total when the interest was asked for, or with the interest when the total was asked for.
- Adding percentages in repeated change problems instead of multiplying the multipliers.
- Rounding intermediate values, which can move the final answer in the third significant figure or the last penny.
- Leaving the answer to a money question as £2431.0125 instead of rounding to the nearest penny.
- Counting the wrong number of years: interest for 3 years means n = 3.
- Stopping a trial-and-improvement search at the first value that looks close, without checking the value either side of the target.
- Writing a decrease as a negative multiplier or forgetting to say "increase" or "decrease".
