## Section A

**1.** Which organ makes urea?

A: kidney

B: liver

C: lung

D: pancreas

**[1]**

**2.** Which is an example of excretion?

A: a person breathing out carbon dioxide

B: a person secreting saliva

C: a person egesting faeces

D: a person releasing hormones

**[1]**

**3.** Define excretion. **[2]**

**4.** State the organ through which carbon dioxide is excreted. **[1]**

**5.** State the substance from which urea is made. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain the difference between excretion and egestion, using an example of each. **[4]**

**7.** Describe how carbon dioxide made in a muscle cell leaves the body. **[4]**

**8.** The table shows the concentration of urea in blood vessels of a person after a meal rich in protein.

| Blood vessel | Urea (mmol per dm cubed) |
|---|---|
| Hepatic portal vein | 2.5 |
| Hepatic vein | 6.0 |

**(a)** State the function of the hepatic portal vein. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the concentration of urea is in the hepatic vein. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the difference in urea concentration. **[3]**

**(d)** Predict how the difference would change after a meal containing very little protein, and explain your prediction. **[2]**

**9.** Explain why a person who eats a lot of protein produces more urea than a person who eats little. **[3]**

**10.** Urea is described as a toxic waste product.

**(a)** Explain what is meant by toxic. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how urea is carried from the liver to the organs that remove it. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why carbon dioxide must be excreted. **[2]**

**12.** A person eats a large steak, which is rich in protein, and a bowl of sugar. Explain which of the two will lead to more urea being made, and why. **[3]**

**13.** Explain what happens to the part of an amino acid that is left after the nitrogen-containing part has been removed in the liver. **[2]**

**14.** The breathing rate of a runner was 15 breaths per minute at rest and 40 breaths per minute during a race.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in breathing rate. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how this helps the body to excrete carbon dioxide. **[3]**

**15.** Plants also produce carbon dioxide in respiration. Suggest why a plant in bright light does not need to excrete much carbon dioxide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The removal of toxic materials [1] and the waste products of metabolism from organisms [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The lungs [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Excess amino acids [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Excretion is the removal of toxic materials and waste products of metabolism [1], for example carbon dioxide or urea [1]. Egestion is the removal of undigested food from the gut [1], for example faeces [1]. **[4]**

**7.** The carbon dioxide is made in respiration and diffuses from the muscle cell into the blood [1]. It is carried in the blood plasma to the lungs [1], diffuses from the blood into the air in the alveoli [1], and is breathed out [1]. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** The vein that carries blood from the small intestine to the liver [1].

**(b)** 6.0 divided by 2.5 = 2.4 times [1].

**(c)** Excess amino acids are broken down in the liver [1], which makes urea [1], and the urea passes into the blood leaving the liver [1].

**(d)** The difference would be smaller [1], because fewer excess amino acids would reach the liver to be broken down [1]. **[7]**

**9.** Protein is digested to amino acids that are absorbed [1]. The body cannot store amino acids, so the excess is broken down in the liver [1], and more excess amino acids means more urea [1]. **[3]**

**10.**

**(a)** It is poisonous and harms the body if it builds up [1].

**(b)** It dissolves in the blood plasma [1] and is carried in the blood to the kidneys [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Carbon dioxide forms an acid in water, which would change the pH of the blood [1] and upset the working of enzymes (or the body cells) [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The steak will lead to more urea [1]. Protein is digested to amino acids, and the excess cannot be stored, so it is broken down in the liver [1]. Sugar is a carbohydrate and contains no nitrogen, so it does not make urea [1]. **[3]**

**13.** It can be used in respiration to release energy [1], or converted into carbohydrate or fat for storage [1]. **[2]**

**14.**

**(a)** Increase = 40 - 15 = 25 [1]. 25 divided by 15 x 100 = 167 per cent [1].

**(b)** During the race the muscles respire faster and make more carbon dioxide [1]. Faster breathing brings more air to the alveoli and keeps the concentration gradient steep [1], so carbon dioxide is removed from the blood more quickly [1]. **[5]**

**15.** In bright light the plant uses the carbon dioxide from respiration in photosynthesis [1], so little is left to be excreted (and the plant may take in more from the air) [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not mentioning **metabolic** waste in the definition of excretion.
- Giving faeces as an example of excretion.
- Saying that urea is made in the kidneys.
- Saying that urea is made from carbohydrates or fats.
- Forgetting that excess amino acids cannot be stored.
