Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) is a two-year course for
students who want a full qualification in chemistry. It covers the
principles that explain how substances behave, the chemistry of particular
groups of elements and compounds, the physical chemistry of reactions, and
organic chemistry. The whole qualification is assessed by two untiered
written papers sat in the same series: Paper 1 (120 minutes, 110 marks,
61.1%) and Paper 2 (75 minutes, 70 marks, 38.9%). The specification was
first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in June 2019.

## How the course is organised

The content sits in four numbered areas. Each one builds on the vocabulary
and models introduced before it, so the order matters more than it might
first appear.

- **1 Principles of chemistry** is the foundation of the course. It runs
  from states of matter and the difference between elements, compounds and
  mixtures, through atomic structure and the Periodic Table, to chemical
  formulae, equations and calculations. It then explains ionic, covalent and
  metallic bonding and finishes with electrolysis, which draws on ideas about
  ions and bonding at the same time.
- **2 Inorganic chemistry** applies those principles to real substances:
  Group 1 (the alkali metals) and Group 7 (the halogens), gases in the
  atmosphere, the reactivity series, the extraction and uses of metals,
  acids, alkalis and titrations, the preparation of salts from acids and
  bases, and a set of chemical tests.
- **3 Physical chemistry** looks at how and why reactions happen as they do,
  treating reactions as processes that can be measured and compared rather
  than just described.
- **4 Organic chemistry** introduces carbon compounds and the way families
  of related compounds behave in similar ways.

The first two areas are by far the most detailed in terms of named
subtopics, and they supply the language that the later areas assume.

## How it's assessed

4CH1 is a linear qualification, so both papers are taken at the end of the
course in a single series. There is one route for everyone: no tiers and no
optional components.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4CH1/1C | Chemistry Paper 1 | 120 minutes (2h) | 110 | 61.1% |
| 4CH1/2C | Chemistry Paper 2 | 75 minutes (1h15) | 70 | 38.9% |

Both papers are written exams and both are externally assessed. The
difference between them is the content they can draw on. The specification
marks some statements in bold. Paper 1 assesses only the core content, the
statements that are not bold-referenced, while Paper 2 assesses all of the
content, including the bold-referenced material. This is the same pattern
Pearson uses for its International GCSE Biology and Physics papers.

## How the parts fit together

The split between core and bold-referenced content is the most useful thing
to understand about this course. Because Paper 2 can test everything, the
core content effectively counts twice: it is the whole of Paper 1 and part
of Paper 2. A secure grasp of the core is therefore the base of the grade,
and the bold-referenced statements add depth on top of it rather than
forming a separate course.

The content areas are also tightly linked. Chemical formulae, equations and
calculations from area 1 reappear in titrations, salt preparations and
metal extraction in area 2, and again whenever physical chemistry asks you
to compare amounts or rates. Bonding explains the properties of the Group 1
and Group 7 elements, and the reactivity series connects directly to how
metals are extracted. Electrolysis sits at the meeting point of bonding and
the extraction of metals. Students who treat each subtopic as a separate
box of facts usually find the later areas harder than those who keep asking
which earlier idea explains what they are learning now.

Two skills run through the whole course: writing and balancing equations,
and carrying out quantitative calculations with clear working. Both are
tested on both papers, so they are worth practising little and often from
the first term.

## Planning your study

A sensible route follows the specification order. In the first year, work
through Principles of chemistry thoroughly, giving extra time to formulae,
equations and calculations and to the three types of bonding, since almost
everything later depends on them. Begin Inorganic chemistry towards the end
of the first year, starting with Groups 1 and 7 and the reactivity series.

In the second year, complete Inorganic chemistry (acids, alkalis, titrations,
salt preparations and chemical tests), then move on to Physical chemistry and
Organic chemistry. Keep a running list of which statements in each area are
bold-referenced, so that you know which material belongs only to Paper 2.

Start timed past-paper practice once all four areas have been taught, and
use the months before the exam series to revisit the core content from area
1, which supports both papers. For how to approach each paper in the exam
itself, use the dedicated exam-preparation page rather than this overview.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Chemistry 4CH1?**
There are two written papers. Paper 1 lasts 120 minutes and carries 110 marks (61.1%), and Paper 2 lasts 75 minutes and carries 70 marks (38.9%). Both are externally assessed and sat in the same series.

**Is IGCSE Chemistry 4CH1 tiered?**
No. Both papers are untiered, so every candidate sits the same two papers.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 assesses only the core content, the statements that are not bold-referenced in the specification. Paper 2 assesses all of the content, including the bold-referenced material.

**What are the main content areas of 4CH1?**
The course has four areas: Principles of chemistry, Inorganic chemistry, Physical chemistry and Organic chemistry.

**When was 4CH1 first examined?**
The specification was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in June 2019.
