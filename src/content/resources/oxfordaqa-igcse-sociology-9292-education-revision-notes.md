Condensed for the final weeks. For the full explanation, use the [education study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-education/). Education is **Section B of Paper 1**, worth 22 marks.

## Education and schooling (3.1.2.1)

- **Formal** (planned, in institutions) v **informal** (everyday learning).
- **Official curriculum** (subjects taught) v **hidden curriculum** (values taught through routines, such as punctuality and obedience).
- **Levels**: nursery or pre-school, primary, secondary, tertiary.
- **School types**: state or public, private, selective, non-selective, faith, international, boarding, progressive, vocational.
- **Online learning**: wider access but needs devices and connection.

## Perspectives (3.1.2.2)

| Perspective | Key ideas | Sociologists |
|---|---|---|
| Functionalism | Shared values; role allocation; human capital; bridge from particularistic to universalistic standards | Durkheim, Parsons, Davis and Moore |
| Marxism | Schooling differs by class; reproduces and legitimises class; prepares workers; myth of meritocracy | Bowles and Gintis, Bourdieu (cultural capital) |
| Radical critics | Schools create dependence; "banking" education | Illich, Freire |
| Feminisms | Schools shape gender roles and expectations | Sharpe |

## Relationships and processes (3.1.2.3)

- **Interactionism**: micro-level classroom interaction.
- **Labelling** → **self-fulfilling prophecy**. **Becker**: the **ideal pupil**. **Rosenthal and Jacobson**: "spurters" field experiment.
- **Stereotyping**; **halo effect** (one good impression colours all judgements).
- **Sanctions**: positive (praise, rewards) and negative (detention, exclusion).
- **Subcultures**: pro-school and anti-school (**Willis**, "the lads").
- **Ethos and curricula**: ethnocentrism, vocational routes, unequal access to knowledge; racism and sexism.

## Attainment (3.1.2.4)

- **Measuring**: exams, IQ tests (cultural bias). **Credentialism**: rising demand for qualifications.
- **Material**: money for resources, space, tuition.
- **Cultural**: cultural capital, language, attitudes.
- **Home and community**: parental support and expectations.
- **In-school**: labelling, setting, subcultures, curriculum.
- Class, gender and ethnicity **intersect**.

## Global education (3.1.2.5)

Literacy rates; differences in provision (free and compulsory years, class sizes, trained teachers, buildings); poverty (child labour, fees, uniforms); gender and other barriers (early marriage, disability, rural location, conflict); the digital divide. Example: Malala Yousafzai and girls' education in Pakistan.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Role allocation | Sorting people into jobs by ability |
| Meritocracy | A system where success depends on ability and effort |
| Cultural capital | Knowledge, language and tastes valued by schools |
| Ideal pupil | Teachers' image of the perfect pupil |
| Self-fulfilling prophecy | A prediction that comes true because it was made |
| Credentialism | Increasing demand for formal qualifications |
| Ethnocentric curriculum | One that centres one culture and marginalises others |

## Exam traps

- Hidden curriculum ≠ informal education.
- Cultural capital is not money.
- Labels can be rejected, so the prophecy is not automatic.
- One-factor explanations of attainment gaps.
- Global education points with no example from a named society, when the question asks for one.

## Self-test

1. What did Parsons mean by school as a "bridge"?
2. What is the myth of meritocracy?
3. What did Rosenthal and Jacobson find?
4. Why did Willis's lads reject school?
5. Give one material and one cultural explanation of class differences in attainment.
6. What is credentialism?

**Answers:** 1. School moves the child from the family's particularistic standards to society's universalistic standards. 2. The Marxist claim that success appears to be based on merit but is really shaped by class. 3. Pupils whom teachers expected to "spurt" often made greater gains, showing the power of expectations. 4. They saw schoolwork as pointless for the manual jobs they expected and valued having a laugh. 5. Material: lack of money for books, devices or study space. Cultural: less cultural capital valued by school. 6. The growing demand for formal qualifications for jobs.
