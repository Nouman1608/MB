Condensed notes on Article 11.1. For full explanation and a worked scenario, use the
[Article 11 study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-article-11-of-the-european-convention-on-human-rights-1953/).

## The text in summary

Everyone has the right to **freedom of peaceful assembly** and to **freedom of association with
others**, including the right to **form and join trade unions** for the protection of their interests.
Qualified right: restrictions in 11.2. Closely linked with Article 10, since protest is usually
expression.

Answer order: **which freedom** (assembly or association) → **is it peaceful / is it an association** →
**interference or failure of positive duty** → **11.2** → **conclusion**.

## Peaceful assembly: core rules

| Rule | Authority |
|---|---|
| Only peaceful assemblies are protected; violent intention removes protection | Kudrevičius v Lithuania (2015) |
| A peaceful individual keeps protection despite violence by others | Ezelin v France (1991): lawyer disciplined for attending; breach |
| Disruption alone does not make an assembly non-peaceful, but deliberate serious disruption is not at the core | Kudrevičius: road blockades by farmers; convictions justified |
| No general right to protest on private land where alternatives exist | Appleby v UK (2003): shopping centre; no breach |
| States must take reasonable and appropriate measures to protect lawful demonstrations | Plattform "Ärzte für das Leben" v Austria (1988) |

## English cases on protest

- **DPP v Jones (1999)**: peaceful, non-obstructive assembly on the highway can be a reasonable use, not
  automatically trespass.
- **R (Laporte) v Chief Constable of Gloucestershire (2006)**: turning back coaches of protesters
  unlawful; breach of the peace not imminent; disproportionate.
- **DPP v Ziegler (2021)**: "lawful excuse" for obstructing the highway requires a proportionality
  assessment under Articles 10 and 11; acquittals restored.

- **Kay v MPC (2008)**: Critical Mass cycle ride with no fixed route was "commonly or customarily held",
  so no advance notice was needed under the Public Order Act 1986 s11.

## Linking Article 11 with Article 10

A protest is both an assembly and a form of expression. The Court usually treats Article 11 as the
**main right** where the complaint concerns a gathering, reading it **in the light of Article 10**. In an
answer, name both, then analyse under Article 11 with Article 10 support. Where the complaint is about
leaflets, banners or speeches rather than the gathering itself, Article 10 may be the better lead right.

## Freedom of association

| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| Young, James and Webster v UK (1981) | Dismissal for refusing to join a union under a closed shop: breach; negative freedom |
| ASLEF v UK (2007) | Union entitled to expel a BNP member; UK law preventing this: breach |
| RMT v UK (2014) | Ban on secondary industrial action: no breach; wide margin |
| Redfearn v UK (2012) | Dismissal for BNP membership with no unfair dismissal remedy: breach of positive obligation |
| United Communist Party of Turkey v Turkey (1998) | Dissolution of a party: breach |
| Refah Partisi v Turkey (2003) | Dissolution justified; aims incompatible with democracy |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Peaceful assembly | A gathering of people whose organisers and participants do not intend violence |
| Association | A voluntary grouping for a common goal, such as a union, party or campaign group |
| Negative freedom of association | Freedom not to be compelled to join an association |
| Positive obligation | Duty on the state to take reasonable steps to protect the exercise of the right |
| Closed shop | Agreement requiring employees to be members of a particular union |
| Secondary action | Industrial action against an employer that is not party to the dispute |
| Imminence | The requirement that a breach of the peace be about to happen before preventive powers are used (Laporte) |

## Exam traps

- Disruptive does not mean non-peaceful.
- Violence by others does not remove a peaceful protester's protection.
- Article 11 is not a right to protest anywhere; Appleby.
- Remember positive obligations: the police may need to protect, not just tolerate, a lawful protest.
- ASLEF protects the **union's** choice of members; Redfearn protects the **individual's** association.
- Do not forget that the association half carries marks too.

## Self-test

1. What two freedoms are protected by Article 11.1?
2. Which group does Article 11.1 expressly mention as an association a person may form and join?
3. What makes an assembly fall outside Article 11?
4. What was decided in Ezelin v France?
5. What did the Grand Chamber decide in Kudrevičius v Lithuania?
6. Why was there no breach in Appleby v UK?
7. What positive obligation did Plattform "Ärzte für das Leben" v Austria recognise?
8. What did DPP v Jones decide about assemblies on the highway?
9. Why was the police action in Laporte unlawful?
10. What principle did Young, James and Webster v UK establish?
11. Contrast ASLEF v UK with Redfearn v UK.
12. What did DPP v Ziegler require courts to do?

**Answers:** 1. Peaceful assembly and association with others. 2. Trade unions. 3. Violent intentions
on the part of the organisers or participants. 4. A peaceful participant cannot be punished for taking
part in a demonstration where others commit offences, if they commit no reprehensible act themselves.
5. Road blockades were a peaceful assembly, though deliberate serious disruption is not at the core of
the right; the convictions were justified. 6. The campaigners had other effective ways to reach the
public, so being excluded from a private shopping centre did not breach Articles 10 or 11. 7. States must
take reasonable and appropriate measures to enable lawful demonstrations to proceed peacefully. 8. A
peaceful, non-obstructive assembly can be a reasonable use of the highway and is not automatically a
trespass. 9. A breach of the peace was not imminent and turning back everyone was disproportionate.
10. Article 11 includes a negative freedom not to be compelled to join a union. 11. ASLEF upheld a
union's right to expel a member for his politics; Redfearn upheld an individual's right to protection
from dismissal for political membership. 12. Assess proportionality under Articles 10 and 11 when
deciding whether protesters had a lawful excuse for obstructing the highway.
