**Population and employment is Section 8, the final section of Paper 2 in Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan
Studies 0448.** It covers Pakistan's population structure and growth, the causes and effects of
migration, the distribution and density of population, and the make-up of the workforce, including
unemployment, underemployment and the importance of education. Many questions give a population
pyramid, graph or table to interpret, so data skills matter as much as recall.

## Where does this section fit in 0448 Paper 2?

Population ties the whole paper together. Where people live depends on the land and climate of
Section 1 and on water from Section 2. The jobs they do are in agriculture (Section 4), industry
(Section 5) and services, including trade and transport (Sections 6 and 7). Rapid population growth
puts pressure on every resource in the syllabus, which is why the sustainability theme is strongest
here.

## What does Pakistan's population pyramid show?

Pakistan's pyramid has a **wide base** and **narrow top**: a large share of the population is
under 15, and a small share is over 65. This shows a high birth rate and a falling but still
moderate death rate. Over time the base has begun to narrow slightly as birth rates fall, while
the middle widens as large younger groups grow up.

**Effects on development:** a young population means high dependency, with heavy demand for schools,
health care and food, and a fast-growing labour force that needs jobs. If those jobs are created, a
large working-age population can boost output; if not, unemployment rises.

## Why is Pakistan's population growing quickly?

The 2023 census recorded about 241 million people, among the largest populations in the world, with
a rapid growth rate. Natural increase is the birth rate minus the death rate.

| Factor | How it raises growth |
|---|---|
| Social | Early marriage; preference for large families and for sons; children as support in old age |
| Educational | Low female literacy, linked to higher fertility; limited awareness of family planning |
| Economic | Children work on farms and in the informal sector, adding family income |
| Political | Family planning programmes have had uneven funding and reach |
| Health | Better medical care and vaccination have lowered the death rate, especially among infants |

**Problems:** pressure on food, water, housing, schools, hospitals and jobs; growth of slums;
environmental damage. **Solutions:** female education, family planning services, better health
care to reduce infant deaths, and economic development that reduces the need for large families.

## Why do people migrate, and what are the effects?

| Type | Push factors | Pull factors |
|---|---|---|
| Rural–urban | Few jobs, low wages, landlessness, poor services, floods | Jobs in industry and services, schools, hospitals, bright lights |
| Seasonal | Little farm work in the slack season; winter snow in the mountains | Harvest work on the plains; urban jobs in winter |
| Emigration | Unemployment, low pay | High wages in the Gulf states, the UK and elsewhere |
| Immigration and refugees | Conflict in neighbouring countries | Safety, family links |

Pakistan has hosted millions of Afghan refugees since the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979.

**Effects in cities:** rapid growth of shanty settlements, known as katchi abadis, with poor
housing, little clean water or sanitation, congestion and pollution. Tent cities form when refugees
arrive or after disasters such as floods and earthquakes. **Effects in rural areas:**
depopulation, loss of young workers, but income from remittances.

**Solutions:** self-help schemes where residents build their own sewers and improve homes, such
as the Orangi Pilot Project in Karachi; provision of standpipes, clean water and services;
upgrading katchi abadis rather than demolishing them. Evaluate them: upgrading can encourage more
migration, and poorly planned schemes may still leave bad housing.

## What is the difference between distribution and density?

**Distribution** is the pattern of where people live. **Density** is the number of people per
square kilometre. Punjab has the largest share of the population and high densities on the
irrigated plain; Balochistan is the largest province by area but has the smallest share of the
population and very low density. Within provinces, densities are high in cities such as Karachi
and Lahore and in fertile river valleys, and low in mountains and deserts.

**Factors:** physical (flat land, fertile soil, water, mild climate versus mountains, deserts and
extreme cold); economic (jobs, industry, irrigation, trade); social (services, family ties);
political (capital city functions, government investment, security).

## How is the workforce employed?

- **Primary** occupations (farming, fishing, mining) still employ the largest single share of workers, though the share is falling.
- **Secondary** occupations (manufacturing, construction) have grown with industry.
- **Tertiary** occupations (trade, transport, government, education, health) are growing fastest in the cities.

**Unemployment** means having no job; **underemployment** means not having enough work, such as
farm labourers idle outside the planting and harvest seasons, or street vendors earning very
little. Both reduce GNP and GDP because people produce less than they could. Pakistan has plenty
of **manual labour** but a shortage of **skilled labour**, so raising literacy, technical training
and female education is central to development.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a dependent population?**
People under 15 and over 65 who mostly rely on those of working
age. Pakistan's dependency is high because so many people are young.

**What is the Orangi Pilot Project?**
A self-help scheme begun in Karachi's Orangi area in 1980,
where residents organised and paid for their own lane sewers with technical guidance.

**Why is female education stressed?**
Educated women tend to marry later and have fewer children,
and their skills raise family income and national output.
