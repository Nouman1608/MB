## Section A

**1.** Which component of blood carries oxygen?

A: platelets

B: phagocytes

C: plasma

D: red blood cells

**[1]**

**2.** Which change takes place when blood clots?

A: fibrin is converted to fibrinogen

B: fibrinogen is converted to fibrin

C: haemoglobin is converted to fibrin

D: platelets are converted to red cells

**[1]**

**3.** State the function of a phagocyte. **[1]**

**4.** List the four components of blood. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** substances, other than blood cells, that are transported in plasma. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A blood smear shows four cell types, W, X, Y and Z.

| Cell | Description |
|---|---|
| W | small, circular, pink-red, pale centre, no nucleus |
| X | large cell with a lobed nucleus and granular cytoplasm |
| Y | cell with a large round nucleus filling most of the cell |
| Z | tiny piece of a cell with no nucleus |

**(a)** Identify W, X, Y and Z. **[4]**

**(b)** State the function of cell X. **[1]**

**(c)** State the function of cell Y. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain how the shape of cell W is adapted to its function. **[2]**

**7.** Describe how a cut in the skin stops bleeding and why this is important. **[4]**

**8.** Tissue fluid is formed from blood plasma.

**(a)** Explain how tissue fluid forms at a capillary. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why red blood cells do not move into tissue fluid. **[1]**

**(c)** State **two** substances that diffuse from tissue fluid into cells and **two** that diffuse from cells into tissue fluid. **[4]**

**(d)** Explain why oxygen diffuses from tissue fluid into cells. **[2]**

**9.** A photomicrograph shows a red blood cell with an image diameter of 35 mm. The actual diameter of the cell is 7 micrometres.

**(a)** Convert 7 micrometres to millimetres. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the magnification of the photomicrograph. **[2]**

**10.** A blood sample has 4.8 million red blood cells and 8000 white blood cells in each cubic millimetre. Calculate how many times more red blood cells than white blood cells there are. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why a person with a low number of lymphocytes is more likely to become ill. **[2]**

**12.** Describe how you would identify a lymphocyte on a prepared slide of blood. **[2]**

**13.** A person has a diet lacking iron. Explain how this affects their blood and why they feel tired. **[3]**

**14.** Explain why a cut capillary in the skin does not usually lead to a lot of blood loss in a healthy person. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** D [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** To engulf (and digest) pathogens [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Red blood cells and white blood cells [1]; platelets and plasma [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: ions; glucose; amino acids; hormones; carbon dioxide; urea; vitamins; plasma proteins [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.**

**(a)** W: red blood cell [1]. X: phagocyte [1]. Y: lymphocyte [1]. Z: platelet [1].

**(b)** To engulf and digest pathogens [1].

**(c)** To produce antibodies [1].

**(d)** The cell is a biconcave disc, giving a large surface area to volume ratio, so oxygen diffuses in and out quickly [1]. It has no nucleus, so there is more room for haemoglobin to carry oxygen [1]. **[8]**

**7.** Platelets help to start clotting [1]. Soluble fibrinogen is converted into insoluble fibrin [1], which forms a mesh that traps red blood cells and forms a clot [1]. The clot prevents blood loss and stops pathogens entering the wound [1]. **[4]**

**8.**

**(a)** Blood in the capillary is under pressure [1]. Plasma is forced out through the gaps in the thin capillary wall [1], and the fluid contains dissolved substances such as oxygen and glucose [1].

**(b)** They are too large to pass through the gaps in the capillary wall [1].

**(c)** Into cells, any two of: oxygen; glucose; amino acids [2]. Out of cells, any two of: carbon dioxide; urea; other waste [2].

**(d)** Cells use oxygen in respiration, so the concentration inside is lower than in the tissue fluid [1], and oxygen diffuses down the concentration gradient [1]. **[10]**

**9.**

**(a)** 7 divided by 1000 = 0.007 mm [1].

**(b)** Magnification = 35 divided by 0.007 [1] = x5000 [1]. **[3]**

**10.** 4 800 000 divided by 8000 [1] = 600 times [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Lymphocytes produce antibodies [1], so with fewer lymphocytes fewer antibodies are produced and pathogens are destroyed less effectively [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Look for a cell with a large round nucleus that fills most of the cell [1] and has very little cytoplasm, and which is slightly larger than a red blood cell [1]. **[2]**

**13.** Iron is needed to make haemoglobin [1]. With less haemoglobin, the red blood cells carry less oxygen [1], so the cells release less energy by aerobic respiration and the person feels tired [1]. **[3]**

**14.** Platelets help clotting, so fibrinogen is converted into fibrin [1], which forms a clot that seals the cut [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that white blood cells carry oxygen.
- Giving a nucleus to red blood cells or leaving it out for lymphocytes.
- Reversing fibrinogen and fibrin.
- Describing clotting as only preventing blood loss.
- Forgetting to convert micrometres to millimetres before using the magnification formula.
