## Questions

**1.** State three properties that are common to all electromagnetic waves. **[3]**

**2.** Name the region of the electromagnetic spectrum that is most likely for each wavelength: **(a)** 2 × 10⁻² m, **(b)** 5 × 10⁻⁷ m, **(c)** 1 × 10⁻¹⁰ m. **[3]**

**3.** A wireless network uses radiation of frequency 5.0 GHz. An ultraviolet source emits radiation of wavelength 120 nm.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the network radiation. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency of the ultraviolet radiation. **[2]**
**(c)** Name the region of the spectrum for each radiation. **[1]**

**4.** A lamp is viewed through two polarising filters. One filter is fixed and the other is rotated slowly through 360° about the line of sight.

**(a)** Describe what is observed. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why this shows that light is a transverse wave. **[2]**

**5.** A microwave transmitter gives plane polarised waves with the electric field vertical. A receiver aligned with the field is placed in line with it. A metal grille of parallel horizontal rods is placed between them.

**(a)** Predict and explain the receiver signal. **[2]**
**(b)** The grille is now turned through 90° in its own plane. State and explain the change. **[2]**

**6.** Light passes from air into glass of refractive index 1.58. The angle of incidence is 52.0°.

**(a)** Calculate the angle of refraction. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of light in the glass. **[2]**

**7.** A ray in air strikes the surface of a liquid with an angle of incidence of 65.0°. The angle of refraction is 36.0°. Calculate the refractive index of the liquid and the speed of light in it. **[4]**

**8.** A student investigates refraction through a rectangular acrylic block using a ray box. The readings are shown.

| i / ° | 15.0 | 25.0 | 35.0 | 45.0 | 55.0 | 65.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r / ° | 10.0 | 16.5 | 22.5 | 28.5 | 33.5 | 37.5 |

**(a)** Calculate sin i and sin r for the readings at 15.0° and 65.0°. **[2]**
**(b)** Use these two points to find the gradient of a graph of sin i against sin r, and state the refractive index of the acrylic. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why a best-fit line through all the points is preferable to using two readings. **[1]**

**9.** The refractive index of a glass is 1.62.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle for a glass-air boundary. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the critical angle for a boundary between this glass and water of refractive index 1.33. **[3]**

**10.** A student uses a ray box and a semi-circular glass block to find the critical angle.

**(a)** Explain why the ray is aimed at the centre of the flat face through the curved surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe what is seen at the flat face as the angle of incidence is increased from 0° to beyond the critical angle. **[3]**

**11.** A small lamp lies at the bottom of a tank holding oil of refractive index 1.47 to a depth of 0.50 m.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle for the oil-air surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the radius of the circle on the surface through which light from the lamp can emerge. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what happens to light that reaches the surface outside this circle. **[2]**
**(d)** The oil is replaced by water of refractive index 1.33 at the same depth. Calculate the new radius. **[2]**

**12.** Red light of wavelength 650 nm in air enters glass of refractive index 1.60.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the light. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the speed of light in the glass. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the wavelength in the glass. **[2]**
**(d)** State what happens to the frequency of the light on entering the glass. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three from: they are transverse waves [1]; they travel through a vacuum, needing no medium [1]; in a vacuum they move at 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, or they transfer energy [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* One property per mark; "they are waves" is too vague, and "travel at the same speed" needs "in a vacuum" to be secure.

**2.** (a) microwave [1]; (b) visible light [1]; (c) X-ray [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Match on the order of magnitude, using a reliable table; gamma rays overlap with X-rays at about 10⁻¹¹ m, so 10⁻¹⁰ m is the safer X-ray answer.

**3.** (a) λ = c/f = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (5.0 × 10⁹) [1] = **0.060 m** [1]
(b) f = c/λ = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (120 × 10⁻⁹) [1] = **2.5 × 10¹⁵ Hz** [1]
(c) microwave (network) and ultraviolet [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert GHz and nm to base units before substituting; a power-of-ten slip here is the commonest way to lose both marks.

**4.** (a) The light is bright, then dims to darkness [1]; it brightens again [1]; two bright and two dark positions occur in 360° [1]. (b) Only a transverse wave can be plane polarised, with oscillation in one plane at right angles to its direction of travel [1]; the first filter transmits one plane, and the second blocks it when crossed, which longitudinal oscillations could not do [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that the filters are "crossed" at darkness; and an explanation of why it proves transverse needs the idea of oscillation perpendicular to the direction of travel.

**5.** (a) The signal is a maximum, or almost unaffected [1], as the rods are at right angles to the electric field so electrons are not driven along them and the wave passes [1].
(b) The signal falls to a minimum [1], since the rods are now parallel to the electric field; electrons are driven along them, so energy is absorbed or reflected [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the orientation of the rods to the electric field direction in each case; many answers state only "it blocks the waves".

**6.** (a) 1.00 × sin 52.0° = 1.58 × sin r [1]; sin r = 0.7880 / 1.58 = 0.4987 [1]; r = **29.9°** [1]
(b) v = c/n = (3.00 × 10⁸) / 1.58 [1] = **1.90 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substituted Snell's law line; give the angle to 3 significant figures, as the data are given to that precision.

**7.** n = sin 65.0° / sin 36.0° = 0.9063 / 0.5878 [1] = **1.54** [1]; v = c/n = (3.00 × 10⁸) / 1.54 [1] = **1.95 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Air is medium 1 here, so n is sin i divided by sin r; inverting the ratio gives n below 1, which should alert you to a mistake.

**8.** (a) sin 15.0° = 0.259 and sin 10.0° = 0.174 [1]; sin 65.0° = 0.906 and sin 37.5° = 0.609 [1]
(b) gradient = Δ(sin i) / Δ(sin r) = (0.906 − 0.259) / (0.609 − 0.174) [1] = 0.647 / 0.435 [1] = 1.49, so **n = 1.49** [1]
(c) A best-fit line averages out random errors in the readings. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A gradient needs both axes' changes shown; remember the graph is sin i (y) against sin r (x), so the gradient is n directly.

**9.** (a) sin C = 1/n = 1/1.62 = 0.6173 [1]; C = **38.1°** [1]
(b) sin C = n₂/n₁ = 1.33/1.62 [1] = 0.8210 [1]; C = **55.2°** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* For a glass-water boundary the ratio is n₂/n₁ with the larger index on the bottom; using 1/n would give the glass-air value.

**10.** (a) The ray enters along a radius, so it meets the curved surface along the normal [1] and is not refracted there, so the only bending occurs at the flat face [1].
(b) At small angles a refracted ray leaves the flat face, bent away from the normal, with a weak reflected ray [1]. At the critical angle the refracted ray travels along the flat face [1]. Beyond it there is no refracted ray and all the light is reflected back into the glass: total internal reflection [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the normal when explaining why the ray is not deviated; the critical angle is an angle of incidence, not an angle of refraction.

**11.** (a) sin C = 1/1.47 = 0.6803 [1]; C = **42.9°** [1]
(b) radius = depth × tan C = 0.50 × tan 42.9° [1] = **0.46 m** [1]
(c) The angle of incidence exceeds the critical angle [1], and the light is totally internally reflected back into the oil [1].
(d) sin C = 1/1.33, so C = 48.8° [1]; radius = 0.50 × tan 48.8° = **0.57 m** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw the right-angled triangle with the depth as the vertical side; and state both conditions for TIR in (c), not only that the angle is large.

**12.** (a) f = c/λ = (3.00 × 10⁸) / (650 × 10⁻⁹) [1] = **4.62 × 10¹⁴ Hz** [1]
(b) v = c/n = (3.00 × 10⁸) / 1.60 [1] = **1.88 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹** [1]
(c) λ = v/f = (1.875 × 10⁸) / (4.615 × 10¹⁴) [1] = **4.06 × 10⁻⁷ m** (406 nm) [1]
(d) It is unchanged. [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded values in a multi-step calculation; a quick check is that the wavelength in the glass equals 650 nm divided by 1.60.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Measuring angles from the surface rather than the normal.
- Not converting GHz, MHz or nm before using c = fλ.
- Writing that the wave "bends towards the normal because it slows down" without saying the second medium has the higher refractive index.
- Describing polarisation as "waves vibrating up and down" and not as oscillation in a single plane.
- Reversing the effect of a metal grille on microwaves.
- Using sin C = 1/n for a boundary between two materials.
- Omitting one of the two conditions for total internal reflection.
- Saying the frequency changes when light enters glass.
- Plotting i against r and quoting a gradient as n.
