## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Name the two glands associated with the digestive system in the specification, and state where the secretion of each enters the gut. **[2]**

**2.** Name the type of bond hydrolysed in each of these molecules.

**(a)** Maltose **[1]**

**(b)** A triglyceride **[1]**

**(c)** A dipeptide **[1]**

**3.** Which enzyme acts while bound to the cell-surface membrane of ileum epithelial cells?

A: Salivary amylase

B: Pancreatic lipase

C: Sucrase

D: An endopeptidase in the stomach **[1]**

**4.** Explain why emulsification by bile salts is described as a physical process rather than digestion. **[2]**

**5.** An unbranched amylose chain contains 400 glucose units. Amylase converts it entirely to maltose.

**(a)** How many glycosidic bonds are in the original chain? **[1]**

**(b)** How many maltose molecules are produced? **[1]**

**(c)** How many water molecules does amylase use? **[1]**

**6.** Explain why ileum epithelial cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A lipid droplet of diameter 0.5 mm is emulsified into droplets of diameter 0.005 mm.

**(a)** Calculate how many small droplets are produced. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the factor by which the total surface area increases. Show your working. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the effect of this change on the rate of lipid digestion. **[1]**

**8.** The uptake of substance X by pieces of ileum was measured in four conditions (arbitrary units).

| Condition | Uptake of X |
|---|---|
| Normal saline, oxygen supplied | 36 |
| Normal saline, respiratory inhibitor added | 34 |
| Sodium-free saline, oxygen supplied | 35 |
| Concentration of X in the lumen lower than inside the cells | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in uptake when the respiratory inhibitor is added. **[1]**

**(b)** What do the data suggest about how X is absorbed? Use the data to explain your answer. **[3]**

**9.** Describe and explain how glucose is absorbed from the ileum when its concentration in the lumen is lower than its concentration in the epithelial cells. **[5]**

**10.** A drug inhibits pancreatic lipase.

**(a)** Suggest how the drug affects the lipid content of a person's faeces. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest the effect of the drug on the number of chylomicrons entering the lacteals. **[1]**

**11.** Explain why micelles are needed for the absorption of lipids, and describe what happens to the products of lipid digestion inside an epithelial cell. **[5]**

**12.** Some people's ileum epithelial cells produce very little lactase. Suggest why drinking milk can cause diarrhoea in these people. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how endopeptidases, exopeptidases and dipeptidases together digest a protein more quickly than exopeptidases could alone. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Salivary glands, into the mouth [1]. Pancreas, into the duodenum [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The specification limits the glands to these two. The liver is not required.

**2. (a)** Glycosidic [1].

**(b)** Ester [1].

**(c)** Peptide [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Ester bond" is the answer for lipids; "glycerol bond" or "fatty acid bond" do not score.

**3.** **C** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Disaccharidases and dipeptidases are the membrane-bound enzymes in the specification.

**4.** Bile salts break large lipid droplets into smaller droplets [1], but they do not hydrolyse any bonds, so no new smaller molecules are produced [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the answer to the definition of digestion: hydrolysis of large molecules into smaller ones.

**5. (a)** 400 − 1 = **399** [1].

**(b)** 400 ÷ 2 = **200** [1].

**(c)** 399 − 200 = **199** (each maltose still contains one bond) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A common error is to answer 399 in (c). Amylase stops at maltose, so the last 200 bonds are left for maltase.

**6.** Mitochondria are the site of aerobic respiration, producing ATP [1]. ATP is needed by the sodium-potassium pump (active transport), which maintains the sodium gradient for co-transport of glucose and amino acids [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the process that uses the ATP. "For energy" alone is too vague.

**7. (a)** (0.5 ÷ 0.005)³ = 100³ = **1 000 000** droplets [1].

**(b)** Each small droplet has (1/100)² = 1/10 000 of the original area [1]; total = 1 000 000 ÷ 10 000 = **100 times** the original [1].

**(c)** Lipase acts at the droplet surface, so a larger total surface area increases the rate of hydrolysis [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Volume scales with diameter cubed and area with diameter squared. Writing both scalings earns the method mark.

**8. (a)** (34 − 36) ÷ 36 × 100 = **−5.6%** (a decrease of 5.6%) [1].

**(b)** X is absorbed by diffusion, probably facilitated diffusion [1]. The respiratory inhibitor and the absence of sodium ions have almost no effect on uptake (34 and 35 against 36), so absorption does not depend on ATP or on co-transport with sodium ions [1]. There is no uptake when the lumen concentration is lower than the cell concentration, so X only moves down its concentration gradient [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the figures. A small difference such as 36 to 34 is not evidence of an active process.

**9.** The sodium-potassium pump in the basal membrane uses ATP to move sodium ions out of the epithelial cell into the blood [1]. This keeps the sodium ion concentration in the cell lower than in the lumen [1]. Sodium ions diffuse into the cell through a co-transporter protein in the membrane facing the lumen [1], carrying glucose with them against the glucose concentration gradient [1]. Glucose then leaves the cell into the blood by facilitated diffusion [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the order: pump, gradient, co-transporter, glucose. Credit is lost if ATP is said to drive the co-transporter directly.

**10. (a)** Lipid content increases [1], because fewer triglycerides are hydrolysed to monoglycerides and fatty acids, and undigested triglycerides cannot be absorbed, so they are egested [1].

**(b)** Fewer chylomicrons [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Chylomicrons are made from the absorbed products of lipid digestion, so less digestion means fewer chylomicrons.

**11.** Monoglycerides and fatty acids are insoluble in water [1], so they combine with bile salts to form micelles, which carry them through the watery lumen to the epithelial membrane [1]. There they are released and diffuse across the membrane because they are lipid-soluble [1]. Inside the cell they are recombined into triglycerides (in the endoplasmic reticulum) [1] and packaged with protein into chylomicrons, which leave by exocytosis into the lacteal [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Micelles carry fats into the cell" is wrong. The micelle delivers its contents to the membrane; it does not cross it.

**12.** Lactose is not hydrolysed, so it stays in the lumen of the gut [1]. This lowers the water potential of the gut contents [1]. Water moves from the tissue into the lumen by osmosis, producing watery faeces [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* This applies the same water-potential reasoning used for cholera. Use "water potential", not "concentration of water".

**13.** Endopeptidases hydrolyse peptide bonds within the polypeptide chain [1], producing many shorter chains and so many more ends [1]. Exopeptidases act only at the ends, so with more ends they release amino acids and dipeptides faster, and membrane-bound dipeptidases then hydrolyse the dipeptides to amino acids [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The key idea is the number of ends. Saying "the enzymes work together" without explaining why scores nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming carbohydrate rather than the specific substrate and product.
- Treating bile salts as enzymes.
- Counting water molecules as equal to monomers rather than bonds.
- Missing the sodium-potassium pump in co-transport answers.
- Calling small differences in uptake data evidence of active transport.
- Saying micelles cross the membrane.
- Using "concentration" where water potential is needed.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions written for section 3.2.2 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the board's past papers.

**How do I decide whether data show co-transport?**
Look for a large fall in uptake when sodium ions are removed and when ATP production is stopped, and for uptake that continues against a concentration gradient.
