These notes condense sub-topic 1(c) Atomic structure (points 1.14 to 1.17) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). None of these points has a C reference, so all can appear on Paper 1 and Paper 2. Full explanations and six worked examples are in the [atomic structure study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-atomic-structure/); test yourself afterwards with the [atomic structure practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-atomic-structure-practice/).

## Key terms (1.14, 1.16)

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Atom | The smallest particle of an element that has the chemical properties of that element |
| Molecule | Two or more atoms chemically bonded together |
| Nucleus | The tiny, positive centre of the atom, containing protons and neutrons |
| Atomic number | The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Mass number | The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Isotopes | Atoms of the same element with the same atomic number but different mass numbers (different numbers of neutrons) |
| Relative atomic mass, Ar | The weighted mean mass of the atoms of an element compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |

## Sub-atomic particles (1.15)

| Particle | Position | Relative mass | Relative charge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | Nucleus | 1 | +1 |
| Neutron | Nucleus | 1 | 0 |
| Electron | Shells | 1/1840 | −1 |

- Mass is concentrated in the nucleus.
- Nucleus is positive; atom is neutral (protons = electrons).
- Most of the atom's volume is occupied by electrons; the nucleus is tiny.

## Counting particles

```
protons   = atomic number (bottom left)
neutrons  = mass number (top left) - atomic number
electrons = protons - ion charge
```

| Species | p | n | e |
|---|---|---|---|
| ²⁷₁₃Al | 13 | 14 | 13 |
| ¹⁶₈O²⁻ | 8 | 8 | 10 |
| ⁶⁵₂₉Cu²⁺ | 29 | 36 | 27 |
| ³⁷₁₇Cl⁻ | 17 | 20 | 18 |

Working backwards: protons give the element; protons + neutrons give the mass number; protons − electrons give the charge.

## Isotopes

- Same protons, same electrons, same electron arrangement → **same chemical properties**.
- Different neutrons → different mass number and slightly different mass.
- Most elements exist naturally as a mixture of isotopes, which is why Ar is rarely a whole number.

## Relative atomic mass (1.17)

```
Ar = sum of (mass number x abundance) / total abundance
```

Steps:

1. Multiply each mass number by its abundance.
2. Add the products.
3. Divide by 100 (percentages) or by the sum of the abundances (relative amounts or ratios).
4. Round as instructed; no units.
5. Check the answer lies between the lightest and heaviest isotopes, nearer the most abundant.

### Working backwards from Ar

For two isotopes with mass numbers a and b and Ar known:

```
a x + b (100 - x) = Ar x 100
solve for x = percentage of isotope a
```

Reminder: lithium has Ar 6.9 with lithium-6 and lithium-7. 6x + 7(100 − x) = 690 → 700 − x = 690 → x = 10, so 10% lithium-6 and 90% lithium-7.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Atomic number vs mass number | Protons only vs protons + neutrons |
| Mass number vs Ar | Whole number for one isotope vs weighted mean for the element |
| Atom vs ion | Same nucleus; ion has gained or lost electrons |
| Atom vs molecule | Single particle of an element vs bonded group of atoms |
| Isotope vs different element | Same protons vs different protons |

## Exam traps

- Electron relative mass written as 0 instead of 1/1840 (negligible).
- "Isotopes have different numbers of neutrons" with no mention of the same element: incomplete.
- "Isotopes have different chemical properties": wrong.
- Positive ion: electrons = protons **minus** charge.
- Ar given in grams, or rounded to a whole number when 1 d.p. is required.
- Dividing by 100 for abundances such as 3 : 1.
- Missing the final H when counting atoms in C₂H₅OH.

## Self-test

1. State the relative mass and relative charge of an electron.
2. Explain why an atom has no overall charge.
3. How many atoms are in one molecule of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆?
4. State what is meant by mass number.
5. Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in ²⁴₁₂Mg²⁺.
6. A particle has 16 protons, 18 neutrons and 18 electrons. Write its symbol with mass number, atomic number and charge.
7. Explain why chlorine-35 and chlorine-37 react in the same way.
8. Gallium is 60% gallium-69 and 40% gallium-71. Calculate Ar.
9. Neon-20, neon-21 and neon-22 are present in the relative amounts 90.5 : 0.3 : 9.2. Calculate Ar to 1 decimal place.
10. Rubidium has Ar 85.5 and isotopes rubidium-85 and rubidium-87. Calculate the percentage of rubidium-85.
11. Why does Ar have no units?
12. Explain why the Ar of chlorine is not a whole number.

### Answers

1. Relative mass 1/1840 (negligible); relative charge −1.
2. It has equal numbers of protons and electrons, whose charges are equal and opposite.
3. 6 + 12 + 6 = 24 atoms.
4. The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
5. 12 protons, 12 neutrons, 10 electrons.
6. ³⁴₁₆S²⁻ (16 protons is sulfur; 16 + 18 = 34; 16 − 18 = −2).
7. Same number of protons, so the same number and arrangement of electrons; reactions involve electrons.
8. (69 × 60 + 71 × 40) ÷ 100 = (4140 + 2840) ÷ 100 = 69.8.
9. Total = 100. (20 × 90.5 + 21 × 0.3 + 22 × 9.2) ÷ 100 = (1810 + 6.3 + 202.4) ÷ 100 = 20.187 = 20.2.
10. 85x + 87(100 − x) = 8550 → 8700 − 2x = 8550 → x = 75%.
11. It is a ratio: a mass compared with 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom.
12. Chlorine is a mixture of isotopes (chlorine-35 and chlorine-37), and Ar is a weighted mean of their masses.
