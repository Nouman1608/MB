These notes cover section 3 of Unit M1 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): motion in a straight line with constant acceleration, including displacement–time, velocity–time, speed–time and acceleration–time graphs. For worked examples, use the [M1 kinematics study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m1-kinematics-straight-line-edexcel-alevel-maths/).

## The formulae (learn them; they are not in the booklet)

| Formula | Missing quantity |
|---|---|
| v = u + at | s |
| s = ut + ½at² | v |
| s = vt − ½at² | u |
| v² = u² + 2as | t |
| s = ½(u + v)t | a |

**Method:** write s, u, v, a, t in a column, fill in what you know, mark what you want, and pick the formula without the remaining unknown.

## Sign conventions

- Choose a positive direction and write it down.
- Every vector (s, u, v, a) takes a sign; t never does.
- Vertical motion, upwards positive: a = −9.8 m s⁻².
- An object thrown up from a height h and landing below the start has s = −h.
- "Decelerates at 3 m s⁻²" means a = −3 if the motion is in the positive direction.

## Graphs

| Graph | Gradient | Area under it | Shape for constant a |
|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement–time | Velocity | — | Parabola |
| Velocity–time | Acceleration | Displacement | Straight line |
| Speed–time | Size of acceleration | Distance | Straight lines, never below the axis |
| Acceleration–time | — | Change in velocity | Horizontal line |

- Velocity–time area **below** the axis: subtract for displacement, add for distance.
- A velocity–time line crossing the axis means the particle is instantaneously at rest and turns round.
- Speed–time graphs show that part reflected upwards, giving a V shape.
- Acceleration–time graphs for a multi-stage journey are separate horizontal segments, with jumps between them.

## Multi-stage journeys

1. Sketch the velocity–time graph and label every known speed and time.
2. Write each unknown as a letter (V, T).
3. Use gradients for accelerations and areas for distances.
4. If the total time and total distance are both given, you get two equations. These may combine into a quadratic.
5. Check each root against the situation, for example that every time is positive.

**Common area shapes:** triangle ½ × base × height; rectangle; trapezium ½(a + b)h.

## Two-interval problems

A particle passes A, B, C with constant acceleration, with known times and distances AB and BC. Use u as the speed at A and write s = ut + ½at² for AB and for AC (not BC). Solve the two equations together.

## Vertical motion: key results

| Situation | Result |
|---|---|
| Greatest height | v = 0, so s = u²/(2g) |
| Time to greatest height | t = u/g |
| Returns to the start | s = 0, so t = 2u/g |
| Time above height h | Solve h = ut − 4.9t² and subtract the roots |
| Two objects meet | Equal heights at the same time |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Position relative to the start, with direction |
| Distance | Total length of path travelled; never negative |
| Velocity | Rate of change of displacement |
| Speed | Magnitude of velocity |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity |
| Deceleration | Acceleration opposite to the direction of motion |
| Instantaneously at rest | v = 0 at one instant, as at the top of a throw |

## Exam traps

- A missing or switched sign for g.
- Using s = ½(u + v)t across two stages with different accelerations.
- Giving displacement when distance is asked for, or the reverse.
- Acceleration–time graphs drawn as joined sloping lines.
- Average speed taken as the mean of the start and end speeds over a journey that is not one constant acceleration stage.
- Forgetting to subtract the two roots in a "time above" question.

## Self-test

1. Which formula has no u in it?
2. A car at 25 m s⁻¹ brakes uniformly to rest in 50 m. Find its deceleration.
3. A particle at 6 m s⁻¹ accelerates at 1.5 m s⁻² for 8 s. How far does it go?
4. A stone is dropped from rest and hits water after 2.5 s. Find the height it fell, to 3 s.f.
5. What does the gradient of a displacement–time graph represent?
6. A velocity–time graph is a straight line from (0, 12) to (8, −4). Find the acceleration.
7. For the graph in question 6, find the displacement and the distance travelled over 8 s.
8. How does a speed–time graph differ from a velocity–time graph for a ball thrown up and caught?
9. What does the acceleration–time graph look like for a stage of constant acceleration?
10. A ball is thrown up at 24.5 m s⁻¹. Find its greatest height.
11. For the ball in question 10, for how long is it at least 29.4 m above the start?
12. A train has speed V for the middle stage of a journey. Why might a quadratic in V have one root rejected?

**Answers:**

1. s = vt − ½at².
2. 0 = 625 + 2a(50), so a = −6.25; the deceleration is 6.25 m s⁻².
3. s = 6(8) + ½(1.5)(64) = 48 + 48 = 96 m.
4. s = ½(9.8)(6.25) = 30.6 m.
5. Velocity.
6. (−4 − 12)/8 = −2 m s⁻².
7. At rest at t = 6. Area above: ½(6)(12) = 36; below: ½(2)(4) = 4. Displacement 32 m; distance 40 m.
8. The part where the velocity is negative is reflected above the axis, so the speed–time graph is V-shaped.
9. A horizontal line segment.
10. s = 24.5²/19.6 = 30.6 m (30.625 m).
11. 29.4 = 24.5t − 4.9t² gives t² − 5t + 6 = 0, so t = 2 or 3; the ball is above for 1 s.
12. The other root can give a negative time for another stage, which is impossible.
