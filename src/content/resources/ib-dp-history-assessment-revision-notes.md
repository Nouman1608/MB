IB DP History, in the course first assessed in 2017 and examined until
2027, is assessed by **Paper 1** (sources), **Paper 2** (two essays),
**HL Paper 3** (three regional essays) and the **historical
investigation**. These notes condense the format, marks and weightings
for quick recall. For the full overview see the
[subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-history-subject-guide/); for an
exam-day plan see the
[exam preparation guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-exam-preparation/).

## What are the papers and weightings?

| Component | Content | Time | Marks | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 4 questions on 4 sources from one prescribed subject | 1 h | 24 | 30% | 20% |
| Paper 2 | 2 essays, each from a different world history topic | 1 h 30 min | 30 | 45% | 25% |
| Paper 3 (HL) | 3 essays on one regional option | 2 h 30 min | 45 | — | 35% |
| Historical investigation | Up to 2,200 words, 3 sections | 20 h | 25 | 25% | 20% |

Check: SL 30 + 45 + 25 = 100%; HL 20 + 25 + 35 + 20 = 100%.
External assessment is 75% at SL and 80% at HL.

## How does Paper 1 work?

| Question | Marks | Skill |
|---|---|---|
| 1(a) | 3 | Comprehension: what a source says about a stated issue |
| 1(b) | 2 | Comprehension: the message of a source, often a visual one |
| 2 | 4 | Value and limitations of one source, with reference to its origin, purpose and content |
| 3 | 6 | Compare and contrast what two sources reveal |
| 4 | 9 | Using the sources and your own knowledge, an argued answer |

Total 3 + 2 + 4 + 6 + 9 = 24. At 60 minutes for 24 marks, budget about
2.5 minutes per mark, so Question 4 needs about 22 minutes.

## How do Paper 2 and Paper 3 work?

- **Paper 2:** 12 world history topics, two questions on each (24 in
  all). Answer two, each from a different topic. Many questions ask
  you to compare examples "each chosen from a different region".
  About 45 minutes per essay.
- **Paper 3 (HL):** for your region, two questions on each of 18
  sections (36 in all). Answer any three. About 50 minutes per essay.
- Both papers use the same five essay markbands, 0–15 per essay: the
  top band (13–15) needs a clearly focused, well-structured answer,
  accurate and relevant knowledge, evaluation of different
  perspectives and a consistently argued conclusion.

## What are the six key concepts?

**Change, continuity, causation, consequence, significance,
perspectives.** They are the lenses questions are set through: "Examine
the consequences…", "Evaluate the significance…", "To what extent did
… change…". See the
[key concepts revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-history-four-concepts-revision-notes/).

## How is the historical investigation structured?

| Section | Focus | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Identification and evaluation of sources | State the question; explain the relevance of two sources; analyse their value and limitations with reference to origin, purpose and content | 6 |
| 2. Investigation | Critical analysis focused on the question, using a range of evidence, reaching a reasoned conclusion | 15 |
| 3. Reflection | What the investigation showed about the methods historians use and the challenges they face | 4 |

Total 25. A bibliography is required. The teacher marks it and the IB
moderates a sample.

## What do the assessment objectives look like in an answer?

| Objective | What examiners look for |
|---|---|
| AO1 Knowledge and understanding | Accurate, relevant, specific detail: names, dates, figures you are sure of |
| AO2 Application and analysis | Knowledge used to support an argument, not listed for its own sake |
| AO3 Synthesis and evaluation | Weighing perspectives and evidence; judging value and limitations of sources |
| AO4 Use of appropriate skills | Structured essays; in the IA, a focused question and reflection on methods |

Paper 1 leans on source skills (AO2 and AO3), Papers 2 and 3 on
argued essays using all four objectives, and the IA adds the research
and reflection skills of AO4.

## Exam traps

- Bringing own knowledge into Paper 1 Question 1, which only asks what
  the source says.
- Describing provenance in Question 2 without linking it to value and
  limitations for the named historian's topic.
- Writing two Paper 2 essays from the same world history topic: they
  must be from different topics.
- Choosing a Paper 2 comparison question but using two examples from
  the same region when the question requires different regions.
- At HL, revising only one or two of your three Paper 3 sections,
  which removes question choice.
- Leaving the IA reflection to the last minute: it is a separate,
  marked section.

## Self-test

1. What is Paper 2 worth at SL and at HL?
2. How many questions does Paper 3 set for each region, and how many do
   you answer?
3. How many marks is Paper 1 Question 4 worth?
4. What is the IA word limit, and how many marks is it out of?
5. Name the six key concepts.
6. Which IA section carries the most marks?

**Answers:** 1. 45% at SL, 25% at HL. 2. 36 (two on each of 18
sections); three. 3. 9. 4. 2,200 words; 25 marks. 5. Change,
continuity, causation, consequence, significance, perspectives.
6. Section 2, the investigation (15 marks).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do SL and HL students sit the same Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Yes. The papers and marks are identical; only the weighting differs
(Paper 1 30% SL and 20% HL; Paper 2 45% SL and 25% HL).

**How long should I spend on each essay?**
About 45 minutes for each of the two Paper 2 essays and about 50
minutes for each of the three Paper 3 essays.

**Is the IA reflection section really assessed?**
Yes. Section 3 is worth 4 of the 25 marks and asks what the
investigation taught you about historians' methods.
