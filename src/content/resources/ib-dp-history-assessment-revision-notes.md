[IB DP History subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-history-subject-guide/). This covers the
current course, first examined 2028.

## Four inquiry contexts as revision structure

Focused study, thematic study, regional study and historical investigation are not just labels for the four assessed components -- they describe genuinely different ways of engaging with historical evidence (Paper 1's close source analysis of one focused topic, Paper 2's broad thematic comparison, HL Paper 3's regional depth study, and the IA's independent inquiry), so a revision plan built around "what skill does this paper actually test" is more effective than one built purely around memorised content for each period.

## SL vs HL weightings

| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (source-based, 1 focused-study option) | 30% | 20% |
| Paper 2 (concept response + essay, 1 thematic-study option) | 40% | 25% |
| Paper 3 (HL only: two essays, 1 regional study) | — | 35% |
| Historical investigation (internal) | 30% | 20% |

## Four inquiry contexts, four concepts

**Contexts:** focused study, thematic study, regional study, historical investigation.
**Concepts:** cause and consequence, continuity and change, perspectives, significance.

These concepts run through **all three papers and the IA** — a question on "significance" might
appear in Paper 1's source analysis, Paper 2's thematic essay, or Paper 3's regional essay.

## The historical investigation

- Student formulates their **own inquiry question**.
- Selects and evaluates **sources**.
- Externally moderated by the IB.

## The historical investigation in more depth

The investigation is graded against criteria covering: identifying a focused, historically valid research question; the range and quality of sources selected and evaluated, including their origin, purpose, value and limitation; the organisation and analysis of evidence in relation to the research question; and a critical reflection on what the investigation reveals about the methods and challenges facing the historian more broadly. A common weakness is a source evaluation that describes a source's origin and content without genuinely assessing its value and limitation for answering the specific research question -- examiners consistently reward evaluation tied directly back to the question being investigated, not a generic commentary on the source in isolation.

## Command terms across the papers

Paper 1's source-based questions use command terms specific to working with historical evidence -- **compare and contrast** two sources, **assess the value and limitations** of a source given its origin and purpose, and **using the sources and your own knowledge** to construct an argument that integrates both. Paper 2 and Paper 3's essay questions use the broader command-term hierarchy shared across individuals-and-societies subjects -- **examine**, **evaluate**, **discuss**, **to what extent** -- but always in relation to a named period, region or theme, meaning a strong essay must be built from specific, dated evidence rather than general narrative description.

## Why the four concepts recur across all three papers

Cause and consequence, continuity and change, perspectives, and significance are not confined to any single paper or unit -- they are the analytical lens the specification expects applied to every historical scenario, whether that is a Paper 1 source extract, a Paper 2 thematic essay, or a Paper 3 regional-study essay. A strong Paper 1 answer, for instance, might use "perspectives" to explain why two sources disagree about a historical event, given their different origins; a strong Paper 2 essay might use "continuity and change" to structure a comparison across a defined time period. Revising the four concepts as transferable analytical tools, rather than as abstract theory to define once, is what makes them useful across all three papers.

## How SL and HL differ beyond the headline weightings

Beyond the shift in Paper 1 (30% SL, 20% HL) and Paper 2 (40% SL, 25% HL) weightings, HL students sit an entirely additional paper (Paper 3, worth 35%) built around a regional depth study not examined at SL at all -- this is new content layered on top of the shared SL core, not merely a longer or harder version of Papers 1-2. HL students should treat Paper 3's regional study as requiring genuinely separate revision time proportionate to its 35% weighting, rather than assuming strong Paper 1/2 preparation will transfer automatically.

## Exam traps

- Revising only "content" for a period without practising the four concepts (cause/consequence, continuity/change, perspectives, significance) that actually structure how questions are asked.
- Confusing focused study (Paper 1), thematic study (Paper 2) and regional study (Paper 3, HL only) — know which options your course covers for each.
- Underweighting source-evaluation skill for Paper 1, which is source-based rather than pure essay-writing.

## Self-test

1. Name the four historical concepts that run through the whole course.
2. What is Paper 3, and who sits it?
3. What must the historical investigation include, beyond the write-up itself?
4. What are Paper 1 and Paper 2 weighted at, at SL?
5. Name one criterion the historical investigation is graded against, beyond the research question itself.
6. What distinguishes HL Paper 3 from Papers 1 and 2 in terms of content?

**Answers:** 1. Cause and consequence, continuity and change, perspectives, significance. 2. Two essays on a chosen regional study, sat only by HL students, worth 35%. 3. A student-formulated inquiry question and the selection and evaluation of sources. 4. Paper 1: 30%, Paper 2: 40%. 5. Any of: the range and quality of sources evaluated (origin, purpose, value, limitation), the organisation and analysis of evidence in relation to the question, or critical reflection on method. 6. Paper 3 is built around an entirely additional regional depth study not examined at SL at all, rather than a harder version of the SL-shared content in Papers 1-2.
