## Questions

**1.** (a) Convert 8.2 m³ to litres. **[1]**
**(b)** Convert 450 cm² to m². **[1]**
**(c)** Convert 90 km/h to m/s. **[2]**

**2.** A coach leaves at 20:50 and the journey takes 5 h 45 min. It travels 391 km.
**(a)** Write the arrival time in 24 hour form. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the average speed in km/h. **[2]**
**(c)** Convert your answer to part (b) into m/s, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**

**3.** A metal block measures 9 cm by 4 cm by 2.5 cm and has mass 0.774 kg.
**(a)** Calculate its density in g/cm³. **[3]**
**(b)** Write its density in kg/m³. **[1]**

**4.** (a) A trapezium has parallel sides 9.2 cm and 14.8 cm, which are 7.5 cm apart. Find its area. **[2]**
**(b)** A parallelogram has area 117 cm² and base 13 cm. Find its perpendicular height. **[2]**

**5.** A tank is a prism. Its cross-section is a trapezium with parallel sides 1.2 m and 2.0 m, which are 0.9 m apart. The tank is 3 m long.
**(a)** Calculate the volume of the tank in m³. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the capacity in litres. **[1]**
**(c)** Water enters at 36 litres per minute. How many hours does it take to fill the tank? **[2]**

**6.** (a) A circle has diameter 14 cm. Write its circumference and area as multiples of π. **[3]**
**(b)** A different circle has circumference 40 cm. Calculate its area to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**7.** A rectangle is 14 cm by 8 cm. A semicircle of diameter 8 cm is cut out of one of the 8 cm ends, so that the shape has a curved notch.
**(a)** Find the perimeter of the shape, in terms of π. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the area of the shape, in terms of π. **[3]**

**8.** A circular running track has outer radius 25 m. The track is 4 m wide.
**(a)** Find the area of the track, in terms of π. **[3]**
**(b)** Surfacing costs £12 per m². Find the cost, to the nearest pound. **[2]**

**9.** (Extension only) A solid hemisphere has radius 6 cm.
**(a)** Find its volume in terms of π. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its total surface area in terms of π. **[2]**

**10.** (Extension only) A cone has base radius 6 cm and perpendicular height 8 cm.
**(a)** Find the slant height. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the volume, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the curved surface area, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** (Extension only) A solid cone has height 24 cm and base radius 9 cm. A cut parallel to the base removes a smaller cone of height 10 cm from the top, leaving a frustum.
**(a)** Find the radius of the cone that was removed. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the volume of the frustum, to 3 significant figures. **[4]**
**(c)** The frustum is made from a material of density 2.4 g/cm³. Find its mass in kg, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**12.** (Extension only) A solid is made from a cube of edge 10 cm with a square-based pyramid fixed on top. The base of the pyramid is the top face of the cube. The pyramid has perpendicular height 12 cm.
**(a)** Find the volume of the solid. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the total surface area of the solid. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 8.2 × 1000 = **8200 litres** [1]
**(b)** 450 ÷ 10 000 = **0.045 m²** [1]
**(c)** 90 × 1000 ÷ 3600 [1] = **25 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the conversion factor you are using. For an area, the factor is squared, so it is 100 × 100 rather than 100.

**2. (a)** 20:50 + 5 h 45 min = 26:35 [1], which is **02:35** [1]
**(b)** 5 h 45 min = 5.75 h [1]; 391 ÷ 5.75 = **68 km/h** [1]
**(c)** 68 ÷ 3.6 = **18.9 m/s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Turning 5 h 45 min into 5.45 h is a frequent slip. Dividing the minutes by 60 and showing it makes your method clear even if you slip later.

**3. (a)** Volume = 9 × 4 × 2.5 = 90 cm³ [1]; mass = 774 g [1]; density = 774 ÷ 90 = **8.6 g/cm³** [1]
**(b)** 8.6 × 1000 = **8600 kg/m³** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert the mass to grams before dividing. A density with mixed units, such as kg/cm³, is not what the question asked for.

**4. (a)** ½ × (9.2 + 14.8) × 7.5 [1] = **90 cm²** [1]
**(b)** Height = 117 ÷ 13 [1] = **9 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the formula with numbers substituted before evaluating. For part (b), rearranging first (height = area ÷ base) avoids multiplying instead of dividing.

**5. (a)** Cross-section = ½ × (1.2 + 2.0) × 0.9 [1] = 1.44 m² [1]; V = 1.44 × 3 = **4.32 m³** [1]
**(b)** 4.32 × 1000 = **4320 litres** [1]
**(c)** 4320 ÷ 36 = 120 minutes [1] = **2 hours** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The length of 3 m is the prism's length, not part of the trapezium. Check the final unit: the question asks for hours, not minutes.

**6. (a)** Circumference = π × 14 = **14π cm** [1]; radius = 7 [1]; area = **49π cm²** [1]
**(b)** 2πr = 40, so r = 20/π [1]; A = π × (20/π)² [1] = 400/π = **127 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using the radius 7 rather than the diameter 14 in πr² is the usual error. In part (b), keep r as 20/π rather than a rounded decimal, then round only the final answer.

**7. (a)** Straight edges: 14 + 14 + 8 [1]; arc = ½ × π × 8 = 4π [1]; perimeter = **36 + 4π cm** [1]
**(b)** Rectangle = 14 × 8 = 112 [1]; semicircle = ½ × π × 4² = 8π [1]; area = **112 − 8π cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The notch replaces one 8 cm edge, so the straight 8 cm edge is not part of the outside. Subtract in part (b) because the semicircle is removed.

**8. (a)** Inner radius = 25 − 4 = 21 m [1]; area = π × 25² − π × 21² [1] = 625π − 441π = **184π m²** [1]
**(b)** 184π × 12 = 2208π = 6936.6… [1]; cost = **£6937** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The width of the track is the difference between the radii, not an inner radius of its own. Use the exact value 184π in part (b) rather than a rounded area.

**9. (a)** V = ½ × (4/3) × π × 6³ [1] = **144π cm³** [1]
**(b)** Curved = ½ × 4π × 6² = 72π, flat face = π × 6² = 36π [1]; total = **108π cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A solid hemisphere has a flat circular face as well as a curved surface. Leaving it out is the most common way to lose marks on total surface area.

**10. (a)** l = √(6² + 8²) = √100 = **10 cm** [1]
**(b)** V = (1/3) × π × 6² × 8 [1] = 96π = **302 cm³** [1]
**(c)** Curved area = π × 6 × 10 [1] = 60π = **188 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Volume uses the perpendicular height 8, whereas the curved area uses the slant height 10. Write down which height you are using for each formula.

**11. (a)** Scale factor = 10/24 [1]; radius = 9 × 10/24 = **3.75 cm** [1]
**(b)** Large cone = (1/3) × π × 9² × 24 = 648π [1]; small cone = (1/3) × π × 3.75² × 10 = 46.875π [1]; frustum = 601.125π [1] = **1890 cm³** [1]
**(c)** Mass = 1888.49 × 2.4 = 4532.4 g [1] = **4.53 kg** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The removed cone is an enlargement scaled down by 10/24, so radius and height must be in the same ratio. Do not pair the radius 9 with the height 10.

**12. (a)** Cube = 10³ = 1000 [1]; pyramid = (1/3) × 100 × 12 = 400 [1]; total = **1400 cm³** [1]
**(b)** Slant height s: s² = 12² + 5² [1], so s = 13 [1]; four triangles = 4 × ½ × 10 × 13 = 260 [1]; five cube faces = 500, so total = **760 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The pyramid covers the top face of the cube, so only five cube faces are on the outside. The slant height uses half the base, 5, not the full 10.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the plain length factor on areas and volumes, instead of its square or cube.
- Writing minutes as a decimal of an hour without dividing by 60.
- Using the diameter in πr², or the radius in a formula that needs the diameter.
- Using the sloping side of a parallelogram or trapezium as the height.
- Giving a rounded decimal where an exact multiple of π was requested, or the reverse.
- Counting an internal join in a composite perimeter, or forgetting a curved edge.
- Rounding in the middle of a multi-step question.
- Omitting units, or giving cm where cm² or cm³ is needed.
- Mixing up the two heights of a cone: slant for curved area, perpendicular for volume.
- Counting a face that is hidden inside a composite solid.
