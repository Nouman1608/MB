## 3.12.3.1 The Michelson-Morley experiment

In the nineteenth century, light was understood as a wave, and waves were thought to need a medium. The proposed medium for light, the **ether**, was assumed to be at absolute rest. The Earth moves through space, so the Earth should move through the ether, and the speed of light measured on Earth should depend on its direction relative to that motion. Detecting this would reveal the Earth's **absolute motion**.

**The interferometer.** Light from a monochromatic source strikes a **semi-silvered glass plate** at 45°, which splits it into two beams at right angles. Each beam travels along an arm to a mirror and is reflected back to the plate, where the beams recombine and travel to a viewing telescope. Because the beams have travelled different optical paths, they produce an **interference pattern** of bright and dark fringes. A compensator plate equalises the glass each beam passes through.

**The expected result.** If the apparatus moved through the ether along one arm, light would take a different time to travel along and back that arm than across the other. Rotating the whole apparatus through 90° swaps the roles of the arms, so the time difference would reverse and the **fringes should shift** by a predictable amount. The apparatus was sensitive enough to detect the expected shift easily, and the observations were repeated at different times of day and year, when the Earth's motion has different directions.

**The result: no shift was detected** (within experimental error).

**Significance:**

- **Absolute motion cannot be detected**; there is no measurable ether.
- The speed of light is the same along both arms whatever the motion of the apparatus: the **invariance of the speed of light**.

This null result was a turning point because classical physics could not explain it without extra assumptions; Einstein's theory made it a starting point instead.

## 3.12.3.2 Einstein's theory of special relativity

An **inertial frame of reference** is one in which Newton's first law holds: an object with no resultant force on it stays at rest or moves with constant velocity. Inertial frames move at constant velocity relative to each other; an accelerating or rotating frame is not inertial.

Einstein's two postulates:

1. **Physical laws have the same form in all inertial frames.** No experiment inside a closed laboratory moving at constant velocity can show whether it is "really" moving.
2. **The speed of light in free space is invariant**: every inertial observer measures the same value, c, whatever the motion of the source or observer.

These lead directly to time dilation and length contraction: if c is fixed for everyone, then time intervals and lengths must differ between frames.

## 3.12.3.3 Time dilation

**Proper time** t₀ is the time interval between two events measured by an observer for whom the events happen **at the same place** (at rest relative to the clock). An observer moving at speed v relative to that clock measures a **longer** interval:

**t = t₀/√(1 − v²/c²)**

The factor 1/√(1 − v²/c²) is always at least 1, and becomes very large as v approaches c.

**Evidence from muon decay.** Muons are produced high in the atmosphere by cosmic rays, travelling close to c. Their half-life at rest is about 1.5 µs. Detectors at the top and bottom of a mountain (or at known heights) count muons with a selected speed. Far more reach the lower detector than classical physics predicts.

**Worked example 1.** Muons travel 10 km vertically at 0.998c. Proper half-life 1.5 µs. Compare the predicted surviving fractions.

```
time in Earth frame = 10 000/(0.998 × 3.00 × 10⁸) = 33.4 µs
1/√(1 − 0.998²) = 15.8
dilated half-life = 15.8 × 1.5 = 23.7 µs
fraction surviving (relativity) = (1/2)^(33.4/23.7) = 0.38
fraction surviving (no dilation) = (1/2)^(33.4/1.5) = 2 × 10⁻⁷
```

Measured counts agree with the relativistic prediction.

## 3.12.3.4 Length contraction

An object of length l₀ measured at rest (its **proper length**) has a shorter length along its direction of motion when measured by an observer it moves past at speed v:

**l = l₀√(1 − v²/c²)**

Lengths perpendicular to the motion are unchanged.

**Worked example 2.** In the muon's own frame, the 10 km of atmosphere moves past it at 0.998c, so it is contracted to 10 000/15.8 = 632 m. The time to cross it at 0.998c is 632/(0.998 × 3.00 × 10⁸) = 2.11 µs, which is 1.41 half-lives, giving (1/2)^1.41 = 0.38 surviving. Both frames predict the same number of muons: Earth observers explain it with time dilation, the muon with length contraction.

## 3.12.3.5 Mass and energy

**E = mc²**: mass and energy are equivalent. The relativistic mass of an object moving at v is m = m₀/√(1 − v²/c²), where m₀ is the rest mass, so

**E = m₀c²/√(1 − v²/c²)**

The rest energy is m₀c², and the **kinetic energy** is E_k = mc² − m₀c².

**Worked example 3.** Rest energies:

```
electron: 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ J = 0.512 MeV
proton:   1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ × (3.00 × 10⁸)² = 1.50 × 10⁻¹⁰ J = 939 MeV
```

At v = 0.866c, 1/√(1 − v²/c²) = 2.00: the total energy is twice the rest energy, so the kinetic energy equals the rest energy.

**Graphs** (the specification asks for these):

- **Mass against speed**: starts at m₀ at v = 0, stays nearly constant at low speed, then rises steeply and tends to infinity as v approaches c.
- **Kinetic energy against speed**: follows the Newtonian curve ½m₀v² at low speeds, then rises far above it, tending to infinity as v approaches c. The Newtonian curve crosses v = c at a finite energy; the relativistic curve never reaches c.

**Bertozzi's experiment** gave direct evidence for the variation of kinetic energy with speed. Electrons were accelerated to a range of known kinetic energies and sent along a measured flight path; their time of flight was measured electronically, giving their speed. The kinetic energy was checked independently from the heating of the target the electrons struck. As the kinetic energy increased, the speed approached c but never exceeded it, matching the relativistic prediction and not the Newtonian one.

**Worked example 4.** An electron has kinetic energy 3.0 MeV. Find its speed and its time to cover 6.0 m.

```
total energy = 3.0 + 0.512 = 3.51 MeV;  factor = 3.51/0.512 = 6.85
v = c√(1 − 1/6.85²) = 0.989c = 2.97 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹
time = 6.0/2.97 × 10⁸ = 20.2 ns
Newtonian: v = √(2E_k/m₀) = 1.03 × 10⁹ m s⁻¹ = 3.4c (impossible)
```

## Common mistakes

- Using the dilated time where the proper time belongs, or vice versa: proper time is measured where the events happen at the same place, and is the shorter.
- Contracting lengths perpendicular to the motion.
- Writing ½mv² for fast particles.
- Saying the Michelson-Morley experiment measured the speed of the ether.
- Forgetting that both postulates refer to **inertial** frames.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** proper time and proper length in words before using either formula.
- **Describe** the Michelson-Morley experiment in three parts: apparatus, expected shift on rotation, null result and its meaning.
- **Sketch** relativistic graphs with an asymptote at v = c and the Newtonian curve for comparison.
