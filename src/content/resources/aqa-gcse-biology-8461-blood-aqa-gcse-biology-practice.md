## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.**

**(a)** State what plasma is. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the component of blood that is a fragment of a cell. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why blood is described as a tissue. **[2]**

**3.** Name the blood component that:

**(a)** carries urea from the liver to the kidneys **[1]**
**(b)** produces antitoxins **[1]**
**(c)** contains haemoglobin. **[1]**

**4.** A student describes two cells seen in a blood micrograph. Cell X is large and has a darkly stained, lobed nucleus. Cell Y is small, pale in the centre and has no nucleus. Identify cell X and cell Y. **[2]**

**5.** A red blood cell measures 2.8 mm across in a micrograph at × 400. Calculate its actual diameter in micrometres. **[3]**

**6.** A student counts red blood cells in five fields of view of the same blood smear: 312, 298, 305, 320 and 290.

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of red blood cells per field of view. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the student counted five fields of view rather than one. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Explain how the shape of a red blood cell and its lack of a nucleus help it to carry out its function. **[4]**

**8.** A patient has very few platelets in their blood.

**(a)** Suggest why the patient bleeds for longer than usual after a small cut. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why the cut is also more likely to become infected. **[1]**

**9.** A patient has a very low number of white blood cells. Explain why this patient is more likely to suffer from infectious diseases. **[3]**

**10.** People who live at high altitude, where each breath contains less oxygen, have more red blood cells per cm³ of blood than people living at sea level. Suggest why this is an advantage. **[2]**

**11.** Describe how a student could observe and draw blood cells using a light microscope. **[4]**

**12.** A patient with a very low platelet count needs an operation. Doctors suggest a transfusion of platelets donated by other people. Evaluate the use of this blood product for the patient. **[6]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The (pale yellow) liquid part of the blood, in which the cells and platelets are suspended [1]
**(b)** Platelet [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Water" alone is not enough for (a); plasma is the liquid in which the other components are suspended.

**2.** It is made of cells (and platelets) suspended in plasma [1], which have a shared function: transport around the body [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the tissue definition: cells working together with a similar function.

**3. (a)** Plasma [1]
**(b)** White blood cells [1]
**(c)** Red blood cells [1]
*Examiner insight:* Haemoglobin is a substance inside red blood cells, so "haemoglobin" is not a blood component.

**4.** X: white blood cell [1]; Y: red blood cell [1]
*Examiner insight:* The presence of a nucleus is the deciding feature.

**5.** Actual size = image size ÷ magnification = 2.8 ÷ 400 [1] = 0.007 mm [1] = **7 µm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The third mark is for converting to micrometres; 0.007 mm alone earns two.

**6. (a)** (312 + 298 + 305 + 320 + 290) ÷ 5 = 1525 ÷ 5 [1] = **305** [1]
**(b)** One field of view may not be representative; a mean of several gives a more reliable value [1]
*Examiner insight:* Words like "representative" and "reliable" earn the mark; "to be accurate" alone is too vague.

**7.** The biconcave (dented) shape gives a large surface area [1], so oxygen diffuses into and out of the cell quickly [1]. Having no nucleus leaves more space inside the cell [1], so it can hold more haemoglobin and carry more oxygen [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each feature needs its effect; the haemoglobin link is the usual missing mark.

**8. (a)** Platelets help the blood to clot [1]; with few platelets a clot forms more slowly, so blood keeps flowing from the wound [1]
**(b)** Without a clot to seal the wound, pathogens can enter more easily [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) is about the clot as a barrier, not about white blood cells.

**9.** White blood cells are part of the immune system [1]; with fewer of them, fewer pathogens are engulfed and fewer antibodies or antitoxins are produced [1]; so pathogens can reproduce and cause disease more easily [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name at least one way white blood cells work; "they fight infection" is too general.

**10.** More red blood cells means more haemoglobin, so the blood can carry more oxygen [1]; this helps body cells receive enough oxygen for respiration even though each breath contains less [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the extra cells to oxygen delivery and respiration.

**11.** Use a prepared, stained blood smear on the microscope stage [1]; focus first on the lowest-power objective, then move to a higher power and use the fine focus [1]; draw a few cells of each type in pencil with clear, unshaded outlines, keeping sizes in proportion [1]; label red cells, white cells and platelets with ruled lines and record the magnification [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each mark needs a specific action; "look at it under a microscope" scores nothing.

**12.** Marking points (levels-marked; a top answer gives both sides and a justified conclusion):
- the donated platelets help the patient's blood to clot [1]
- so the patient is less likely to lose a dangerous amount of blood during the operation [1]
- risk: a pathogen could be passed on in the donated product [1]
- this risk is reduced because donors are questioned and donations are tested [1]
- risk: the patient's immune system may react to the donated product, so it must be matched; supply also depends on donors [1]
- conclusion: the transfusion should go ahead because the risk of serious bleeding outweighs the small, controlled risks [1]
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation that gives only risks, or only benefits, cannot reach the top level.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling haemoglobin or plasma a "cell".
- Leaving out haemoglobin when explaining why red cells lack a nucleus.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to micrometres.
- Writing "white blood cells fight germs" without saying how.
- Ending an evaluation without a judgement.
