These notes condense section 11.8, Polymers, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All twelve outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [polymers study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-polymers/); then try the [polymers practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-polymers-practice/).

## The twelve outcomes, grouped

**Definitions and structure**

1. Polymers are large molecules built up from many smaller molecules called monomers.
2. Identify repeat units and linkages in addition and condensation polymers.
3. Deduce an addition polymer from an alkene, and the alkene from the polymer.
4. Deduce a polyamide (dicarboxylic acid + diamine) or polyester (dicarboxylic acid + diol) from monomers, and the monomers from the polymer.
5. Describe the differences between addition and condensation polymerisation.

**Plastics**

6. Plastics are made from polymers.
7. Properties of plastics have implications for their disposal.
8. Environmental challenges: landfill, accumulation in oceans, toxic gases from burning.

**Named polymers**

9. Describe and draw nylon (a polyamide) and PET (a polyester).
10. PET can be converted back into monomers and re-polymerised.
11. Proteins are natural polyamides formed from amino acids, H₂N–CH(R)–COOH.
12. Describe and draw the structure of proteins.

## Addition polymerisation

| Monomer | Formula | Polymer | Repeat unit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ethene | CH₂=CH₂ | poly(ethene) | –[CH₂–CH₂]– |
| Propene | CH₂=CHCH₃ | poly(propene) | –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]– |
| Chloroethene | CH₂=CHCl | poly(chloroethene) | –[CH₂–CHCl]– |

Monomer → repeat unit: C=C becomes C–C; add extension bonds through brackets; write *n*. Side groups (CH₃, Cl) hang off the chain.

Repeat unit → monomer: take the two main-chain carbons; make the bond between them C=C; remove extension bonds.

## Condensation polymerisation

| Polymer type | Monomer 1 | Monomer 2 | Linkage | Small molecule lost | Example |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Polyamide | dicarboxylic acid HOOC–▭–COOH | diamine H₂N–▬–NH₂ | amide –CO–NH– | water | nylon |
| Polyester | dicarboxylic acid HOOC–▭–COOH | diol HO–▬–OH | ester –CO–O– | water | PET |

Repeat units:

- polyamide: –CO–▭–CO–NH–▬–NH–
- polyester: –CO–▭–CO–O–▬–O–

Polymer → monomers: break each linkage between the C=O carbon and the N or O; add –OH to the C=O end and –H to the N or O end.

One water molecule is released for every linkage formed.

Worked check: a polyester section containing three dicarboxylic acid units and three diol units in one chain has five ester linkages joining six monomer units, so five water molecules were released. Count the linkages, not the monomers.

## Addition against condensation

| | Addition | Condensation |
|---|---|---|
| Monomer feature | C=C | two functional groups per monomer |
| Number of monomer types (at this level) | one | two |
| By-product | none | water |
| Main chain | carbon atoms only | contains N or O in linkages |

## Proteins

- Monomers: amino acids, H₂N–CH(R)–COOH; R is a side-chain that varies.
- Linkage: amide, the same as in nylon.
- Chain: –NH–CH(R)–CO–NH–CH(R')–CO–
- Proteins are **natural polyamides**.

## Plastics and disposal

| Property | Consequence |
|---|---|
| unreactive, non-biodegradable | stays in landfill sites for a very long time |
| durable, many float | accumulates in oceans, harming aquatic life |
| contains carbon (and sometimes other elements) | burning can release toxic gases |

PET: can be converted back into monomers and re-polymerised to make new PET.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Polymer | large molecule built up from many smaller molecules (monomers) |
| Monomer | small molecule that joins with others to form a polymer |
| Repeat unit | smallest section that repeats to make the polymer chain |
| Addition polymerisation | alkene monomers join with no other product |
| Condensation polymerisation | monomers join with loss of a small molecule such as water |
| Amide linkage | –CO–NH–, found in polyamides and proteins |
| Ester linkage | –CO–O–, found in polyesters |
| Amino acid | monomer of proteins, with an amine group and a carboxylic acid group |
| Non-biodegradable | not broken down by microorganisms |

## Exam traps

- Poly(propene) main chain has **two** carbons per repeat unit; CH₃ is a side group.
- Addition repeat units contain **no** C=C.
- Condensation releases **water**; addition releases nothing.
- Nylon and proteins are **polyamides**; PET is a **polyester**.
- Break a polyester between C=O carbon and O, not between O and the block.
- PET recycling point: converted back into **monomers**, then re-polymerised.
- Toxic gases come from **burning** plastics, not from landfill.
- An addition repeat unit has the same molecular formula as its monomer; a condensation repeat unit has fewer atoms than its two monomers combined, because water is lost.

## Self-test

1. Define *polymer*.
2. Draw the repeat unit of poly(ethene) in condensed form.
3. Give the monomer of the polymer with repeat unit –[CH₂–CHCl]–.
4. Name the two types of monomer used to make a polyamide.
5. Name the linkage in a polyester.
6. Name the small molecule released in condensation polymerisation.
7. State **two** differences between addition and condensation polymerisation.
8. What type of polymer is a protein?
9. Write the general structure of an amino acid.
10. State what can be done to PET that allows it to be recycled into new PET.
11. Give **two** environmental challenges caused by plastics.
12. Why do plastics remain in landfill sites for a very long time?

## Answers

1. A large molecule built up from many smaller molecules called monomers.
2. –[CH₂–CH₂]–.
3. Chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl.
4. A dicarboxylic acid and a diamine.
5. Ester linkage, –CO–O–.
6. Water.
7. Any two: addition uses alkene (C=C) monomers, condensation uses monomers with two functional groups; addition gives only the polymer, condensation also gives water; addition uses one type of monomer here, condensation uses two; condensation polymers have amide or ester linkages.
8. A natural polyamide.
9. H₂N–CH(R)–COOH.
10. It can be converted back into its monomers and re-polymerised.
11. Any two: disposal in landfill sites; accumulation in oceans; toxic gases from burning.
12. They are unreactive and non-biodegradable, so they do not decompose.
