This guide is for students writing a Paper 2 Section B essay on **پطرس کے مضامین** by **احمد شاہ پطرس بخاری**, listed in Appendix 1 of the Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0) specification with the date 2011. Only **five essays** are prescribed:

1. **سویرے جو کل آنکھ میری کھُلی**
2. **اُردو کی آخری کتاب**
3. **مُرید پُور کا پیر**
4. **مرحُوم کی یاد میں**
5. **لاہور کا جغرافیہ**

The [Patras revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-patras-ke-mazameen-revision-notes/) summarise each essay and list the main techniques. This guide shows how to **analyse** humour in an exam essay, how to compare essays, and how the 45 marks are earned. Practise with the [Patras practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-patras-ke-mazameen-ahmed-shah-patras-bukhari-practice/). Prescribed works can change between series; check the current specification.

## How the essay is marked

You choose one of two questions on the essays, set in Urdu, and answer in Urdu without the text or a dictionary. The specification suggests about 320–370 words. Three grids apply:

- **Critical and analytical response (AO4), 20 marks**: interpretations justified with appropriately selected evidence, logical arguments, linked conclusions, relevance throughout.
- **Range of grammatical structures and vocabulary (AO3), 15 marks**: including terminology for literary analysis.
- **Accuracy of language (AO3), 10 marks.**

The specification's five features (form and technique; themes, concepts and issues; characterisation; plot structure; social and cultural setting) apply to essays as well as to fiction, but you must adapt them. For a humorous essay, **form and technique** is usually the richest feature; **characterisation** means the narrator and the comic figures around him; **plot structure** means how each essay builds and ends.

## Analysing humour: a method

The commonest weakness in Patras essays is the sentence "this is very funny". Replace it with a four-step analysis: **technique, example, effect, purpose**.

| Step | Question to ask | Urdu frame |
|---|---|---|
| Technique | What device creates the humour? | مصنف یہاں ... کا سہارا لیتے ہیں |
| Example | Where exactly? (a short reference) | مثلاً ... |
| Effect | Why does the reader laugh? | اس سے قاری کو ہنسی اس لیے آتی ہے کہ ... |
| Purpose | What is being exposed or questioned? | دراصل اس کے پیچھے ... پر طنز ہے |

Techniques to name, beyond those in the revision notes:

- **تضاد** (incongruity): a gap between style and subject, or between what a character believes and what is true.
- **خود تمسخری** (self-mockery): the narrator exposes his own vanity, laziness or excuses.
- **مزاحیہ مبالغہ** (comic exaggeration): a small event inflated to epic scale.
- **تحریف** (parody): imitation of a serious form, as in the school-book essays.
- **مضحکہ خیز صورتِ حال** (comic situation): events that escalate beyond the narrator's control.
- **ستم ظریفی** (irony): saying one thing while meaning another; the reader understands more than the narrator admits.
- **زبان کا کھیل** (wordplay): play on idioms, formal phrases and the language of textbooks.

## The essays as a set

Examiners reward answers that compare. Group the five essays by what they share:

**The narrator as target.** In **سویرے جو کل آنکھ میری کھُلی** and **مرحُوم کی یاد میں**, the first-person narrator's own weaknesses are central. The reader laughs at a man who deceives himself, and laughs with him because the weaknesses are familiar.

**Parody of form.** **اُردو کی آخری کتاب** imitates a children's primer; its title plays on the familiar name of beginners' readers, **اُردو کی پہلی کتاب**. **لاہور کا جغرافیہ** imitates a geography textbook. In both, the joke depends on the reader recognising the serious format, which links the essays to education and colonial-era schooling.

**Satire of public life.** **مُرید پُور کا پیر** turns on public reverence for a supposed leader and on the emptiness of speech-making; its humour carries the sharpest social point.

**Mock-solemnity.** **مرحُوم کی یاد میں** applies the language of mourning to a bicycle; the title itself (**مرحوم**, the late, the deceased) signals the joke.

A good comparative sentence: **جس طرح "اُردو کی آخری کتاب" میں درسی کتاب کے انداز کی نقل کی گئی ہے، اسی طرح "لاہور کا جغرافیہ" میں جغرافیے کی کتاب کا سنجیدہ اسلوب ایک شہر کی خامیوں پر ہنسنے کے لیے استعمال ہوا ہے۔**

## Social and cultural setting

The essays were written in the first half of the twentieth century. Bukhari taught at Government College Lahore, and Lahore itself is the subject of one essay. The world they show is that of educated, middle-class men in colonial north India: students, teachers, neighbours, meetings and textbooks. Ask what the humour reveals about this world: attitudes to education and rote learning, the gap between public speeches and private behaviour, and the habits of a growing city.

Connect the setting to the qualification's themes only where it is genuine; for example, the school-book parodies can be linked to education (Theme 1) and the satire of speeches to public and political life.

## Worked example: an analytical paragraph

Question (original): **«پطرس کا مزاح دوسروں سے زیادہ اپنی ذات پر ہنسنے سے پیدا ہوتا ہے۔» اس بیان کا جائزہ لیجیے۔**

> پطرس کے کئی مضامین میں راوی خود اپنی کمزوریوں کا سب سے بڑا نشانہ ہے۔ "سویرے جو کل آنکھ میری کھُلی" میں وہ صبح سویرے اٹھنے کا پختہ ارادہ کرتا ہے، مگر ہر بار کوئی نہ کوئی بہانہ اس کے ارادے پر غالب آ جاتا ہے۔ قاری کو ہنسی اس لیے آتی ہے کہ راوی اپنی سستی کو بڑی سنجیدگی سے جائز ثابت کرنے کی کوشش کرتا ہے، اور یہی خود تمسخری ہمیں اپنی عادتوں کا آئینہ دکھاتی ہے۔ تاہم یہ کہنا درست نہیں کہ پطرس صرف اپنے آپ پر ہنستے ہیں؛ "مُرید پُور کا پیر" میں طنز کا رخ عوام کی اس عادت کی طرف ہے جو کسی کو بھی رہنما مان لیتی ہے۔

(In many of Patras's essays, the narrator himself is the main target of his weaknesses. In *Sawere Jo Kal Aankh Meri Khuli*, he makes a firm resolution to rise early, but each time some excuse or other overcomes his resolve. The reader laughs because the narrator tries, with great seriousness, to justify his own laziness, and it is this self-mockery that shows us a mirror of our own habits. However, it is not true that Patras laughs only at himself; in *Mureedpur Ka Peer*, the satire is aimed at the public habit of accepting anyone as a leader.)

**Why it works:** technique (**خود تمسخری**), example, effect (**قاری کو ہنسی اس لیے آتی ہے کہ**) and purpose (**آئینہ دکھاتی ہے**) appear in order; **تاہم** turns to a second essay to qualify the statement, which is what **جائزہ لیجیے** requires. In your own answer, add a short phrase from the essay that you have checked.

## Common mistakes

- Writing about essays outside the five prescribed.
- Summarising each essay in turn without a line of argument.
- Treating the narrator's opinions as the author's sincere views; the narrator is often a comic mask.
- Explaining a joke at such length that the analysis becomes a retelling.
- Forgetting the respectful plural when referring to the author (**پطرس لکھتے ہیں**), or switching between singular and plural.

## Exam technique

- Prepare at least **three** essays in detail, so that you can answer either question and compare.
- For each prepared essay, memorise two or three short phrases and one key moment you can describe precisely.
- Open with a clear position; make each paragraph follow technique, example, effect, purpose; end with a judgement.
- Show range with literary terms and with structures such as **جس طرح ... اسی طرح**, **نہ صرف ... بلکہ**, and the passive **پیش کیا گیا ہے**.
