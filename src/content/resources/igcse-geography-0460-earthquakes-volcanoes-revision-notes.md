## Structure of the Earth

From the surface inwards: **crust**, **mantle**, **outer core** (liquid), **inner core** (solid).
The crust is broken into tectonic plates that move slowly.

- **Oceanic crust** is thinner and denser; **continental crust** is thicker and less dense. This
  difference explains why oceanic crust is the one that sinks at a destructive boundary.
- Plates move a few centimetres a year, carried by **convection currents** in the mantle: heat from
  the core makes mantle rock rise, spread and sink again, dragging the plates above.

## Plate boundaries

| Boundary | Plate movement | Hazards |
|---|---|---|
| **Constructive (divergent)** | Plates move apart; magma rises and forms new crust, for example mid-ocean ridges | Volcanic activity |
| **Destructive (convergent)** | Plates move together; oceanic plate sinks beneath another (**subduction**); trenches and fold mountains | Powerful earthquakes and explosive volcanoes |
| **Conservative** | Plates slide past each other; pressure builds and is released suddenly | Earthquakes, no volcanoes |

No magma is made at a conservative boundary, so there are no volcanoes there.

**Process at a destructive boundary, step by step:** the denser oceanic plate is forced down
beneath the lighter plate; friction makes the plates stick, pressure builds and is released as an
earthquake; deeper down the sinking plate melts, and the new magma, being less dense, rises through
cracks to form explosive volcanoes. The bending crust forms a deep ocean **trench** and the edge of
the upper plate crumples into **fold mountains**.

**Process at a constructive boundary:** as plates separate, pressure falls and magma rises to fill
the gap. The runny basalt lava erupts gently and builds new ocean floor, a mid-ocean ridge and, where
the ridge rises above the sea as in **Iceland**, shield-type volcanoes.

## Volcano and earthquake terms

- **Volcano features:** crater, vent, magma chamber.
- **Strato (composite) volcano:** steep sided, explosive, typical of destructive boundaries.
- **Shield volcano:** gentle slopes, runny lava, typical of constructive boundaries.
- **Focus:** the point underground where the earthquake starts. **Epicentre:** the point on the
  surface directly above it. **Magnitude:** the size, recorded by a seismometer on the Richter or
  moment magnitude scale.
- **Active / dormant / extinct:** erupted recently / not erupted for a long time but may again /
  not expected to erupt again.
- **Seismic waves** spread out from the focus; shaking is usually strongest near the epicentre and
  weakens with distance.
- Each whole number on the magnitude scale is a large step up in energy, not a small one.

**Why composite volcanoes are steep and explosive:** their magma is thick (viscous) and full of gas,
so it does not flow far before cooling, building steep slopes of alternating ash and lava. Gas
cannot escape easily, so pressure builds until it erupts violently.

## Distribution: describe then explain

Earthquake and volcano locations trace the plate boundaries, most clearly around the Pacific
**Ring of Fire**. Name the plate pair at each place:

- **Chile:** Nazca Plate subducted beneath the South American Plate.
- **Indonesia (Sumatra and Java):** Indo-Australian Plate subducted beneath the Sunda Plate.
- **Japan:** subduction of the Pacific Plate.
- **California, San Andreas Fault:** a conservative boundary, with earthquakes but no volcanoes.

**Describing a distribution map:** say they form **long, narrow belts**, give a named belt (the
Ring of Fire, the mid-Atlantic ridge), and note exceptions such as volcanoes in mid-plate locations
like **Hawaii**, which sit over a hot spot rather than a boundary.

## Hazards, opportunities and management

| Volcanoes | Earthquakes |
|---|---|
| Hazards: lava flows, ash fall, pyroclastic flows | Hazards: ground shaking, collapsed buildings, fires, ruptured services |
| Opportunities: fertile soils, geothermal energy, minerals, tourism | Reducing impacts: earthquake-resistant buildings, public education and drills, emergency plans, land-use planning |
| Reducing impacts: monitoring, warnings, evacuation planning | |

For volcanoes you must cover **both hazards and opportunities**; a one-sided answer loses marks.

### Why people live near volcanoes

- **Fertile soils:** weathered volcanic ash and lava produce rich soils for crops.
- **Geothermal energy:** hot rock heats water for electricity and heating, as in Iceland.
- **Minerals and building stone** come from volcanic rock.
- **Tourism:** visitors come to see eruptions, craters and hot springs.
- Family ties, a lack of alternative land, or a belief that the volcano will not erupt in their
  lifetime also keep people there.

### How earthquake damage is reduced

- **Buildings:** deep foundations, steel frames that bend rather than snap, rubber shock absorbers in
  the foundations, cross-bracing and lightweight roofs.
- **Preparation:** drills in schools and workplaces, emergency kits, trained rescue services.
- **Warning systems** can detect the first, faster waves and give a few seconds' warning, enough to
  stop trains and shut gas supplies.
- **Planning:** keep hospitals and schools off unstable ground and enforce building codes.

Earthquakes **cannot be predicted** precisely, but volcanoes often give warning signs: small
earthquakes, swelling of the ground and changes in gas emissions.

### Primary and secondary effects

- **Primary:** caused directly by the event -- buildings collapse, people are killed by falling
  debris, lava destroys property.
- **Secondary:** follow on from it -- fires from broken gas pipes, tsunami, landslides, disease
  from damaged water supplies, loss of jobs and income.

## Case studies

The syllabus requires **a named earthquake and a named volcano**. For each, prepare: location, date,
magnitude or type, primary and secondary effects, and responses. Do not rely on generic description.

**Earthquake: Tohoku, Japan, 11 March 2011.** A magnitude 9 earthquake off the north-east coast,
where the Pacific Plate subducts beneath the plate carrying northern Japan. Its biggest impact came
from the **tsunami**, which overtopped sea walls and flooded coastal towns; around 18 000 people were
killed or went missing. The tsunami caused a meltdown at the **Fukushima Daiichi nuclear power
station**, leading to a long-term evacuation zone. Japan's preparation -- strict building codes,
regular drills and an early-warning system that stopped high-speed trains -- limited damage from the
shaking itself, but showed that defences could be overwhelmed.

**Volcano: Soufrière Hills, Montserrat (Caribbean), from 1995.** A composite volcano on a destructive
boundary where Atlantic oceanic crust subducts beneath the Caribbean Plate. Eruptions from 1995
produced **pyroclastic flows** and ash; in **1997** pyroclastic flows killed 19 people, and the
capital **Plymouth** was buried and abandoned. The south of the island became an exclusion zone, and
about two-thirds of the population left the island. The **Montserrat Volcano Observatory** now
monitors the volcano, and new settlement has been built in the safer north.

## Exam traps

- Confusing **focus** with **epicentre**.
- Writing only hazards (or only opportunities) for volcanoes.
- Describing a distribution map without explaining it with plate boundaries.
- Saying "the Pacific plate" for every Ring of Fire location.
- Putting volcanoes on a conservative boundary.
- Mixing primary and secondary effects.
- Claiming earthquakes can be predicted.

## Self-test

1. Which boundary type has earthquakes but no volcanoes?
2. Distinguish focus from epicentre.
3. What happens at a destructive boundary?
4. Name two opportunities volcanoes offer.
5. Give two ways to reduce earthquake impacts.
6. Why does oceanic crust sink beneath continental crust?
7. Why are composite volcanoes more explosive than shield volcanoes?
8. Give one primary and one secondary effect of an earthquake.
9. Name a volcano that is not on a plate boundary and say why it exists.
10. Give two warning signs that a volcano may erupt.
11. Why did the 2011 Tohoku earthquake cause so many deaths despite Japan's preparation?

**Answers:** 1. Conservative. 2. The focus is underground where it starts; the epicentre is the
surface point directly above. 3. An oceanic plate is subducted, giving earthquakes and explosive
volcanoes. 4. For example fertile soils and geothermal energy. 5. For example earthquake-resistant
buildings and public drills. 6. It is denser. 7. Their magma is thick and gas-rich, so pressure builds
until it is released violently. 8. Primary: buildings collapse; secondary: fires from broken gas
pipes. 9. For example Hawaii, which sits over a hot spot in the middle of the Pacific Plate. 10. Small
earthquakes and swelling of the ground (or changing gas emissions). 11. The tsunami overtopped sea
walls and flooded coastal towns.
