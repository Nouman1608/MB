These questions cover sections 1.6 (Percentages) and 1.7 (Ratio and proportion) of topic 1, Numbers and the number system, in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017), for the January and June series examined on it. Questions 1–10 are for both tiers, except part 7(a), which is marked **(Higher)** because writing a proportion formula (statement 2.5A) is Higher tier only. Questions 11 and 12 are **(Higher)**: they use repeated percentage change and compound interest problem-solving, which the specification lists for the Higher tier only. A calculator may be used on every 4MA1 paper, but show your working: most marks are for method.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-percentages-ratio-and-proportion/) and the [revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-percentages-ratio-and-proportion-revision-notes/). ## Questions

**1.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** Write 42% as a decimal. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 42% as a fraction in its simplest form. **[1]**
**(c)** Increase £240 by 17.5%. **[2]**

**2.** (Both tiers) Express 270 g as a percentage of 1.8 kg. **[2]**

**3.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** Write the ratio 1.2 m : 45 cm : 75 cm in its simplest form. **[2]**
**(b)** Write the ratio 5 : 18 in the form 1 : n. **[1]**

**4.** (Both tiers) Ali, Bea and Chen share some money in the ratio 2 : 5 : 6. Chen receives £84 more than Ali.

**(a)** Calculate how much Bea receives. **[3]**
**(b)** What fraction of the money does Bea receive? **[1]**

**5.** (Both tiers) The price of a laptop falls from £640 to £552. Calculate the percentage decrease. **[3]**

**6.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** In a sale, all prices are reduced by 15%. A jacket costs £57.80 in the sale. Calculate the price of the jacket before the sale. **[3]**
**(b)** A restaurant bill is £81 after a 12.5% service charge has been added. Calculate the service charge. **[3]**

**7.** w varies directly as d. When d = 6, w = 16.8.

**(a)** (Higher) Find a formula for w in terms of d. **[2]**
**(b)** Complete the table. **[2]**

| d | 6 | 15 | ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| w | 16.8 | ? | 9.8 |

**8.** (Both tiers) A map has a scale of 1 : 40 000.

**(a)** Two farms are 7.5 cm apart on the map. Calculate the real distance between them in kilometres. **[2]**
**(b)** A cycle path is 5.2 km long. Calculate its length on the map in centimetres. **[2]**

**9.** (Both tiers)

**(a)** A recipe for 6 people uses 450 g of flour. Calculate how much flour is needed for 10 people. **[2]**
**(b)** Pack A has 6 cans of juice for £4.32. Pack B has 15 cans of the same juice for £10.50. Which pack is better value? Show your working. **[3]**

**10.** (Both tiers) Kofi and Lena share £14 000 in the ratio 3 : 4. Kofi invests his share at 3.5% per year compound interest. Lena invests her share at 2.5% per year compound interest. Both invest for 3 years.

**(a)** Show that Kofi invests £6000. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the value of Kofi's investment after 3 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Who earns more interest, and by how much? **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the total interest earned by Kofi and Lena together as a percentage of £14 000. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**11.** (Higher) The price of a bicycle is increased by 20%. The new price is then increased by 15%. This price is then reduced by 25% in a sale. Calculate the overall percentage change in the price. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher)

**(a)** A van costs £12 000 when new. Its value depreciates by 14% per year. Find the number of whole years after which its value is first less than half of its value when new. **[3]**
**(b)** An investment grows from £5000 to £5618 in 2 years at a fixed rate of compound interest. Find the rate of interest. **[3]**
**(c)** After 3 years at 5% per year compound interest, an account holds £9261. Calculate the amount first invested. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 42 ÷ 100 = **0.42** [1]
**(b)** 42/100 = **21/50** [1]
**(c)** Multiplier 1.175 [1]; 240 × 1.175 = **£282** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) 42/100 is not in its simplest form, so it does not earn the mark; in (c) finding 17.5% of 240 (42) and stopping scores the method mark only.

**2.** 1.8 kg = 1800 g and 270/1800 [1]; 270/1800 × 100 = **15%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 270/1.8 × 100 = 15 000% shows no unit conversion and earns nothing; the method mark needs both quantities in the same units.

**3. (a)** 120 : 45 : 75 in cm [1]; divide by 15: **8 : 3 : 5** [1]
**(b)** 18 ÷ 5 = 3.6, so **1 : 3.6** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A correct but not fully simplified ratio such as 24 : 9 : 15 earns the method mark only; the accuracy mark needs the simplest form.

**4. (a)** Chen − Ali = 6 − 2 = 4 parts = £84 [1]; one part = 84 ÷ 4 = £21 [1]; Bea = 5 × 21 = **£105** [1]
**(b)** Total parts = 13, so **5/13** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing £84 by 13 (the total parts) earns nothing, because £84 is the difference between two shares, not the total.

**5.** Decrease = 640 − 552 = 88 [1]; 88/640 × 100 [1] = **13.75%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 552 (the new price) loses both the second method mark and the accuracy mark; the original value must be the denominator.

**6. (a)** Sale price is 85% of the original, multiplier 0.85 [1]; 57.80 ÷ 0.85 [1] = **£68** [1]
**(b)** Bill is 112.5% of the cost before the charge, so 81 ÷ 1.125 [1] = £72 [1]; service charge = 81 − 72 = **£9** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Working out 15% of £57.80 and adding it on gives £66.47 and scores 0, because the method mark is for dividing by the correct multiplier.

**7. (a)** k = 16.8 ÷ 6 = 2.8 [1]; **w = 2.8d** [1]
**(b)** d = 15: w = 2.8 × 15 = **42** [1]; w = 9.8: d = 9.8 ÷ 2.8 = **3.5** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A wrong value of k in (a) can still earn both marks in (b) on follow-through if it is used correctly, so always write your formula down.

**8. (a)** 7.5 × 40 000 = 300 000 cm [1]; = **3 km** [1]
**(b)** 5.2 km = 520 000 cm [1]; 520 000 ÷ 40 000 = **13 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* An answer of 300 000 earns the method mark only; the accuracy mark needs the conversion to kilometres the question asks for.

**9. (a)** For 1 person: 450 ÷ 6 = 75 g [1]; for 10 people: 75 × 10 = **750 g** [1]
**(b)** Pack A: 4.32 ÷ 6 = £0.72 per can [1]; Pack B: 10.50 ÷ 15 = £0.70 per can [1]; **Pack B is better value** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The final mark needs a correct conclusion supported by two comparable figures; "B" with no calculation scores 0.

**10. (a)** 14 000 ÷ 7 = 2000 per part [1]; Kofi = 3 × 2000 = 6000 [1]
**(b)** 6000 × 1.035³ [1] = **£6652.31** [1]
**(c)** Lena's value: 8000 × 1.025³ = £8615.13, so her interest is £615.13 [1]; Kofi's interest is 6652.31 − 6000 = £652.31 [1]; **Kofi earns more, by £37.18** [1]
**(d)** Total interest = 652.31 + 615.13 = £1267.44 [1]; 1267.44/14 000 × 100 = **9.05%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" in (a), the answer 6000 is given, so the marks are for the working: write 14 000 ÷ 7 and × 3 in full.

**11.** Multipliers 1.2, 1.15 and 0.75 [1]; 1.2 × 1.15 × 0.75 = 1.035 [1]; **an increase of 3.5%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Adding the percentages (20 + 15 − 25 = 10%) scores 0; the method mark is for a product of the three correct multipliers.

**12. (a)** Half of 12 000 is 6000, and the yearly multiplier is 0.86 [1]; after 4 years 12 000 × 0.86⁴ = £6564.10 and after 5 years 12 000 × 0.86⁵ = £5645.12 [1]; **5 years** [1]
**(b)** 5618 ÷ 5000 = 1.1236 = m² [1]; m = √1.1236 = 1.06 [1]; **6% per year** [1]
**(c)** 1.05³ = 1.157625 [1]; 9261 ÷ 1.157625 [1] = **£8000** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "5 years" with no values for n = 4 and n = 5 can lose the method marks, because trial and improvement must show the values either side of the target.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing the change by the new value instead of the original in a percentage change.
- Taking 15% of a sale price and adding it on, instead of dividing by 0.85.
- Treating a given difference between shares as the total when dividing in a ratio.
- Leaving ratios with mixed units (m with cm) or not fully simplified.
- Using simple interest (adding the same amount each year) when the question says compound.
- Giving the final value when the question asks for the interest, or the interest when it asks for the value.
- Rounding the value at the end of each year, so the final answer is a few pence out.
- Leaving a map length in cm when the answer must be in km.
- (Higher) Adding successive percentage changes instead of multiplying the multipliers.
- (Higher) Giving only the final number of years without the values either side of the target.
