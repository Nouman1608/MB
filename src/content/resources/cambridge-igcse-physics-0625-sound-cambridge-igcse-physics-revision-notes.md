## Numbers to know

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Audible range (humans) | 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz |
| Ultrasound | frequency above 20 kHz (20 000 Hz) |
| Speed of sound in air | about 330–350 m/s |

## Production and nature (Core)

- Sound is produced by **vibrating** sources (strings, loudspeaker cones, vocal cords, tuning forks).
- Sound is a **longitudinal** wave: particles vibrate **parallel** to the direction the wave travels.
- Particles vibrate about fixed positions; **energy** is transferred, not the air.
- A **medium** (solid, liquid or gas) is needed: sound cannot travel through a vacuum.
- Bell-in-a-jar: pump the air out and the ringing fades although the hammer is still seen moving; let air in and the sound returns.

## Extended only

- **Compression:** region where particles are pushed close together (higher pressure).
- **Rarefaction:** region where particles are spread apart (lower pressure).
- Wavelength = distance between centres of neighbouring compressions (or rarefactions).
- Speed: generally **solids > liquids > gases**.
- Typical given values: air about 340 m/s, water about 1500 m/s, steel several thousand m/s.

## Loudness and pitch (Core)

| Property of the wave | Heard as | Trace on a screen |
|---|---|---|
| Amplitude ↑ | louder | taller peaks |
| Frequency ↑ | higher pitch | waves closer together |

- The two are independent: change one without the other.

## Echoes and speed of sound (Core)

- An **echo** is sound **reflected** from a surface.
- Echo distance travelled = **2 × distance to the surface**.
- Speed = distance ÷ time.

**Two-observer method, in steps**

1. Measure a distance of at least 100 m with a tape or trundle wheel.
2. One person makes a sound with a visible action (blocks clapped together above the head, starting pistol).
3. The other starts a stopwatch on **seeing** the action and stops it on **hearing** the sound.
4. Speed = distance ÷ time; repeat and average; swap ends to reduce the effect of wind.

**Echo method, in steps**

1. Stand a measured distance d from a large wall.
2. Clap so each clap coincides with the echo of the previous one.
3. Time a large number n of intervals; one interval t = total time ÷ n.
4. Speed = 2d ÷ t.

## Ultrasound uses (Extended only)

| Use | What reflects the pulse |
|---|---|
| Non-destructive testing | Far side of a metal part, plus any crack (earlier echo) |
| Medical scanning of soft tissue | Boundaries between tissues |
| Sonar | Sea bed or shoal of fish |

**Depth or distance = (speed × time) ÷ 2**, where time is from sending the pulse to receiving the echo.

## Exam traps

- "Sound is transverse" or "air moves from the speaker to the ear": both wrong.
- Forgetting to double the distance (or halve the time) for an echo.
- Writing that ultrasound has a higher speed than ordinary sound in air: it has a higher **frequency**; the speed is the same.
- Using v = fλ for an echo calculation when you are given a distance and a time.
- Mixing up 20 Hz and 20 kHz: 20 kHz is the **upper** limit of hearing and the lower limit of ultrasound.
- Saying sound travels fastest in gases.

## Self-test

1. What do all sources of sound have in common?
2. State the audible range of frequencies for humans.
3. Why can astronauts on a spacewalk not talk to each other directly through space?
4. Two notes have the same amplitude but note B has twice the frequency of note A. Compare how they sound.
5. A student hears an echo from a cliff 1.5 s after shouting. The speed of sound is 340 m/s. How far away is the cliff?
6. In a two-observer experiment the observers are 500 m apart and the time is 1.5 s. Calculate the speed of sound.
7. Why should the two observers be far apart?
8. Define ultrasound.
9. Calculate the wavelength of a 680 Hz sound in air, where v = 340 m/s.
10. *(Extended)* What is a rarefaction?
11. *(Extended)* Put air, steel and water in order of increasing speed of sound.
12. *(Extended)* A sonar pulse returns from a shoal of fish 0.12 s after being sent. Speed of sound in sea water = 1500 m/s. How deep is the shoal?

### Answers

1. They are all **vibrating**.
2. **20 Hz to 20 000 Hz**.
3. Space is a vacuum: there is **no medium** to carry sound.
4. Same loudness; B has a **higher pitch**.
5. Distance there and back = 340 × 1.5 = 510 m, so the cliff is **255 m** away.
6. 500 ÷ 1.5 = **333 m/s** (330 m/s to 2 s.f.).
7. So the time is long compared with the observer's **reaction time**, reducing its effect on the result.
8. Sound with a frequency **higher than 20 kHz**.
9. λ = 340 ÷ 680 = **0.50 m**.
10. A region where the particles are **spread further apart** than normal (lower pressure).
11. **Air, water, steel**.
12. (1500 × 0.12) ÷ 2 = **90 m**.
