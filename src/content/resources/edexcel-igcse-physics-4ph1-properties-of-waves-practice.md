These questions cover sub-topic 3(b), Properties of waves (statements 3.2
to 3.9), of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1). Where
needed, the speed of electromagnetic waves in free space is
3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.

## Section A

**1.** Define the amplitude of a wave. **[1]**

**2.** Define the time period of a wave and state its unit. **[2]**

**3.** State what is transferred by a wave, and what is not transferred. **[2]**

**4.** Name the regions of a longitudinal wave where the particles are (a) closest together and (b) furthest apart. **[2]**

**5.** A wave has a frequency of 0.50 Hz. Calculate its time period. **[2]**

**6.** State which wave property stays the same when a wave is refracted. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student holds one end of a long slinky spring. The other end is fixed.

**(a)** Describe how she could use the slinky to produce a transverse wave and a longitudinal wave. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two waves in terms of the direction of oscillation. **[2]**
**(c)** A piece of ribbon is tied to one coil of the slinky. Describe the motion of the ribbon as a longitudinal wave passes. **[1]**

**8.** A wave on a rope has 4 complete wavelengths along a 6.0 m length. A point on the rope makes 10 oscillations in 4.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the frequency. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the wave speed. **[2]**

**9.** A ship's sonar sends a sound pulse of frequency 30 kHz down through the sea. Sound travels at 1500 m/s in sea water.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound in sea water. **[3]**
**(b)** The pulse reflects from the sea bed and returns 0.80 s after it was sent. Calculate the depth of the sea. **[3]**

**10.** Water waves in a ripple tank have a frequency of 6.0 Hz. In the deep region their wavelength is 2.0 cm. They pass into a shallow region, where their speed is 0.090 m/s.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the waves in the deep region. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the wavelength in the shallow region. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the frequency in the shallow region is still 6.0 Hz. **[1]**

**11.** A radio station broadcasts on a wavelength of 3.3 m.

**(a)** Calculate the frequency of the radio waves. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time period of the radio waves. **[2]**

**12.** A car sounds its horn, which has a constant frequency of 400 Hz, as it drives towards a stationary observer and then away.

**(a)** State how the frequency heard by the observer compares with 400 Hz while the car approaches. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of wavefronts and wavelength, why the frequency heard changes. **[3]**
**(c)** State what the observer hears if the car is stationary with its horn sounding. **[1]**

**13.** A student says: "Sound waves cannot be refracted, because only light can bend." Use your knowledge of wave properties to show that the student is wrong. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The maximum displacement of a point on the wave from its rest (undisturbed) position [1]. **[1]**

**2.** The time taken for one complete wave to pass a point (or for one complete oscillation) [1]; unit: second (s) [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Energy and information are transferred [1]; matter is not [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** Compressions [1]. **(b)** Rarefactions [1]. **[2]**

**5.** T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 0.50 [1] = 2.0 s [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Frequency [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** Transverse: move the end from side to side, at right angles to the slinky [1]. Longitudinal: push and pull the end backwards and forwards along the slinky's length [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** In the transverse wave the coils oscillate perpendicular to the direction in which the wave travels [1]; in the longitudinal wave they oscillate parallel to the direction of travel [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** It moves backwards and forwards along the length of the slinky, about a fixed position (it does not travel along with the wave) [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** λ = 6.0 ÷ 4 = 1.5 m [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** f = 10 ÷ 4.0 [1] = 2.5 Hz [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** v = f × λ = 2.5 × 1.5 [1] = 3.75 m/s, so 3.8 m/s [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** f = 30 kHz = 30 000 Hz [1]; λ = v ÷ f = 1500 ÷ 30 000 [1] = 0.050 m [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Distance travelled = speed × time = 1500 × 0.80 = 1200 m [1]; this is there and back, so depth = 1200 ÷ 2 [1] = 600 m [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** v = f × λ = 6.0 × 0.020 [1] = 0.12 m/s [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** λ = v ÷ f = 0.090 ÷ 6.0 [1] = 0.015 m (1.5 cm) [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The frequency is set by the source (the dipper), and the same number of waves per second must arrive at and leave the boundary [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** f = v ÷ λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ ÷ 3.3 [1] = 9.1 × 10⁷ Hz (91 MHz) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** T = 1 ÷ f = 1 ÷ 9.1 × 10⁷ [1] = 1.1 × 10⁻⁸ s [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** It is higher than 400 Hz [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** As the car approaches, each wave is emitted from a point closer to the observer than the last [1], so the wavefronts are bunched together and the observed wavelength is shorter [1]. Since the speed of sound is unchanged, a shorter wavelength means a higher observed frequency (v = f × λ); as the car moves away the wavefronts spread out and the frequency heard is lower [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** 400 Hz: there is no relative motion, so there is no Doppler effect [1]. **[1]**

**13.** All waves can be reflected and refracted, including sound [1]. Refraction happens because the speed of a wave changes when it passes into a different medium [1]; sound changes speed between different materials (or between air at different temperatures), so it changes direction at a boundary met at an angle, just as light does [1]. **[3]**
