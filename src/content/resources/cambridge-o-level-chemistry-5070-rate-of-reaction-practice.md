## Section A

**1.** State **three** properties of a catalyst, as described in the syllabus. **[3]**

**2.** State what is meant by an enzyme. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why increasing the pressure increases the rate of a reaction between two gases. **[2]**

**4.** 54 cm³ of oxygen is collected in the first 18 s of a reaction. Calculate the mean rate of reaction over this time, giving units. **[2]**

**5.** Excess marble chips react with dilute hydrochloric acid. Which change does **not** increase the rate of reaction?

A: warming the acid

B: using powdered marble instead of chips

C: using a larger volume of acid of the same concentration

D: using acid of a higher concentration **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the rate of a reaction decreases as the reaction proceeds. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain, using collision theory, why the rate of reaction between magnesium and hydrochloric acid increases when the acid is warmed. **[3]**

**8.** Excess marble chips are added to dilute hydrochloric acid in a flask with a cotton wool plug, standing on a balance.

| Time / s | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 | 150 | 180 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mass / g | 150.00 | 149.48 | 149.16 | 148.98 | 148.92 | 148.90 | 148.90 |

**(a)** Calculate the total mass lost.

**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of mass loss in the first 30 s.

**(c)** State when the reaction stopped and explain why it stopped.

**(d)** Calculate the total volume of carbon dioxide produced at r.t.p., in cm³. **[6]**

**9.** A student wants to follow the volume of carbon dioxide produced in a reaction. Evaluate collecting the gas over water in an upturned measuring cylinder, compared with using a gas syringe. **[3]**

**10.** 0.24 g of magnesium ribbon reacts with excess dilute hydrochloric acid at 20 °C, and the volume of hydrogen is plotted against time. Describe how the curve would differ from the original if the experiment were repeated with:

**(a)** 0.24 g of magnesium powder at 20 °C

**(b)** 0.24 g of magnesium ribbon with the acid at 10 °C

**(c)** 0.12 g of magnesium ribbon at 20 °C, considering the final volume only. **[5]**

**11.** Manganese(IV) oxide speeds up the decomposition of hydrogen peroxide: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂. Describe how a student could show that manganese(IV) oxide acts as a catalyst in this reaction. **[3]**

**12.** Explain, in terms of activation energy, how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction. **[2]**

**13.** A student investigates how the concentration of hydrochloric acid affects the rate of its reaction with magnesium ribbon. State **two** variables that must be kept constant. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** It increases the rate of a reaction [1]; it decreases the activation energy [1]; it is unchanged at the end of the reaction [1].

**2.** A biological catalyst [1].

**3.** There are more gas particles per unit volume [1], so collisions are more frequent [1].

**4.** 54 ÷ 18 = 3.0 [1]; **cm³/s** [1].

**5.** **C** [1].

**6.** The reactants are used up, so there are fewer reacting particles per unit volume (and less surface of solid) [1], so the frequency of collisions falls [1].

**7.** Particles gain kinetic energy and move faster [1], so they collide more frequently [1], and a greater proportion of collisions have energy equal to or greater than the activation energy [1].

**8. (a)** 150.00 − 148.90 = **1.10 g** [1].

**(b)** (150.00 − 149.48) ÷ 30 = 0.52 ÷ 30 = **0.017 g/s** [1].

**(c)** At 150 s [1], because the hydrochloric acid (the limiting reactant) was used up [1].

**(d)** Mr of CO₂ = 44; 1.10 ÷ 44 = 0.0250 mol [1]; 0.0250 × 24 000 = **600 cm³** [1].

**9.** Collecting over water is simple and cheap and works for gases that are insoluble in water [1]. However, carbon dioxide is slightly soluble in water, so some dissolves and the volume recorded is too low [1]. A gas syringe measures the gas directly without water, so it is the better choice for carbon dioxide [1].

**10. (a)** The curve is steeper at the start [1] and levels off sooner at the same final volume [1].

**(b)** The curve is less steep at the start [1] and levels off later at the same final volume [1].

**(c)** The final volume is half the original [1].

**11.** Weigh the manganese(IV) oxide before adding it [1]. Show that oxygen is produced faster with it than without it (for example, by comparing the volume of gas collected in a fixed time) [1]. After the reaction, filter off, wash and dry the manganese(IV) oxide and reweigh it: its mass is unchanged [1].

**12.** A catalyst lowers the activation energy [1], so a greater proportion of collisions have enough energy to react / more collisions are successful per second [1].

**13.** Any two: volume of acid [1]; mass or length of magnesium ribbon [1]; temperature; the same type of magnesium (ribbon from the same roll).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: writing "it is not used up" and "it is unchanged" as two separate properties. They are the same point.
- Question 4: giving the number without units, or dividing time by volume.
- Question 5: choosing A or B. A larger volume of the same acid has the same number of particles per unit volume, so the rate is not increased.
- Question 7: giving only "particles move faster and collide more". The activation energy point is the key mark.
- Question 8(c): saying the marble ran out. The marble was in excess, so the acid was limiting.
- Question 8(d): forgetting to convert dm³ into cm³.
- Question 10(a): increasing the final volume. Surface area changes the rate, not the amount of product.
- Question 11: showing the reaction is faster without checking that the catalyst is unchanged in mass.
