These questions cover sub-topic 5.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): describing strategies for conserving the biodiversity and genetic resources of natural ecosystems, and discussing their benefits and limitations. Data in the sources is invented for practice.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *genetic resources*. **[1]**

**2.** Name the **three** threat levels used in the syllabus to classify organisms, from least to most severe. **[1]**

**3.** Describe what a wildlife corridor is. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** methods used to make the harvesting of wild fish sustainable. **[2]**

**5.** Give **one** reason why some plant seeds cannot be kept in a seed bank. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain why seed banks are described as a way of conserving genetic resources rather than ecosystems. **[3]**

**7.** Source: *A breeding centre holds 18 individuals of a rare antelope, all descended from 6 animals caught in the wild. Its natural grassland habitat has shrunk by half in 30 years because of farming.*
(a) Using the source, explain **one** problem with the genetic variety of the captive population. **[2]**
(b) Suggest **two** conditions that should be met before the antelope are released into the wild. **[2]**

**8.** A survey found 120 of a bird species in a forest reserve. Ten years later, after illegal logging, 78 remained. Calculate the percentage decrease. Show your working. **[2]**

**9.** Describe how sustainable forestry differs from clear-felling, and give **one** benefit for biodiversity. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how international cooperation can reduce the illegal trade in animal products such as ivory. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of national parks and reserves as a strategy for conserving biodiversity. **[6]**

**12.** A small island nation wants to protect its coral reef and the fish that live on it. Discuss which **two** strategies from the syllabus would be most effective, giving benefits and limitations of each. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Genes held in wild plants and animals that people may use, for example to breed crops that resist disease **[1]**.

**2.** Threatened, endangered, extinct, in that order **[1]**.

**3.** A strip or area of habitat, such as a hedgerow, forest belt or bridge over a road **[1]**, that links separate patches of habitat so organisms can move between them **[1]**.

**4.** Any two: quotas limiting the catch **[1]**; closed seasons during breeding **[1]**; minimum size limits or net mesh sizes so young fish escape **[1]**; licences or permits **[1]**; no-take zones. **[max 2]**

**5.** Some seeds die when they are dried or frozen **[1]** (accept: some species produce few seeds, or reproduce mainly without seeds).

**6.** Seed banks store the genes of plant species as seeds **[1]**, so variety is kept for future breeding or replanting **[1]**; but they do not protect the habitat, the other species or the interactions of the wild ecosystem **[1]**.

**7.** (a) All 18 animals come from only 6 wild ancestors **[1]**, so genetic variety is low and inbreeding is likely, which can reduce health, fertility or ability to adapt **[1]**.
(b) Any two: enough suitable grassland habitat is protected, since the source shows it has halved **[1]**; the threat that caused the decline, such as farming or hunting, is controlled **[1]**; animals are prepared for the wild, for example finding food and avoiding predators **[1]**; numbers released are large enough for a breeding population **[1]**; monitoring is in place after release. **[max 2]**

**8.** Decrease = 120 − 78 = 42 **[1]**; 42 ÷ 120 × 100 = 35% **[1]**.

**9.** Sustainable forestry fells only selected mature trees **[1]** and replants or allows natural regrowth, while clear-felling removes all trees in an area **[1]**. Benefit, any one: habitat and canopy remain for wildlife; the forest keeps much of its species variety; soil and rivers are protected **[1]**.

**10.** Countries share intelligence on smuggling routes and gangs **[1]**; customs officers in exporting and importing countries are trained and equipped to detect products **[1]**; agreements ban or limit trade and countries campaign to reduce demand, so traders find it harder and less profitable to sell **[1]**.

**11.** Award up to 3 for benefits and up to 3 for limitations, with credit for a judgement within that total. Benefits: protects the whole habitat and every species in it **[1]**; protects species not yet identified or studied **[1]**; allows research, education and ecotourism income **[1]**. Limitations: needs funding for rangers and enforcement, or poaching and logging continue **[1]**; local people may lose land, hunting or forest resources, causing conflict **[1]**; parks cannot keep out pollution, invasive species or climate change, and isolated parks may be too small for wide-ranging species **[1]**. A judgement such as "most effective when linked by corridors and supported by local people" may replace one point. **[max 6]**

**12.** Award up to 3 marks for each strategy discussed (what it is, a benefit, a limitation). Example strategies: **National park or marine reserve**: what — a protected area where fishing and anchoring are banned or controlled **[1]**; benefit — protects the whole reef habitat, letting fish populations recover and spread to fished areas **[1]**; limitation — needs patrols and funding, and fishers may lose income **[1]**. **Sustainable harvesting**: what — quotas, closed seasons and size limits on reef fish **[1]**; benefit — fishers keep an income, so they support protection **[1]**; limitation — hard to monitor on a small island with limited staff **[1]**. Credit **ecotourism** (diving fees fund protection; but divers and boats can damage coral) or **regulation of trade** (bans export of rare reef species; but smuggling continues) as alternatives. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only benefits in "discuss" questions; each side earns its own marks.
- Saying zoos or seed banks protect habitats.
- Not using the source, such as the six wild ancestors or the halved habitat.
- Dividing the decrease by the final value instead of the original value.
- Naming strategies without explaining how each conserves biodiversity.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Managing biodiversity questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them with the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**How many strategies do I need to know for 5.3?**
The syllabus lists ten, from sustainable harvesting to classifying organisms by threat level. A question may name one or let you choose, so learn a benefit and a limitation for each.

**Should a "discuss" answer end with a judgement?**
Yes, where you can. A short conclusion saying when the strategy works best, or what it should be combined with, shows the structured discussion the command word asks for.
