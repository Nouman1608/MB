Cambridge 9489 Paper 4 asks you to write two 30-mark essays in 1 hour 45 minutes on your chosen option, using the command words **assess** ("make an informed judgement") or **evaluate** ("judge... the quality, importance, amount, or value"). The nine questions below cover all twelve key questions of the European option, so whichever two you are set, you have practised the skill.

## How Paper 4 essays are marked

Published 9489 Paper 4 mark schemes award 15 marks for AO1 (knowledge) and 15 for AO2 (analysis and judgement), each on five levels.

| Level | AO2 (15) | AO1 (15) |
|---|---|---|
| 5 (13-15) | Valid, wide-ranging criteria; consistently analytical; balanced, sustained argument; clear sustained judgement | Carefully selected, fully focused, wide-ranging, consistently precise and accurate |
| 4 (10-12) | Valid criteria; analytical but uneven; attempts balance; supported judgement | Appropriately selected, mostly focused, a range of points, mostly accurate |
| 3 (7-9) | Attempts criteria; some analysis with description; argument lacks balance; weakly substantiated judgement | Mostly relevant, descriptive in places, narrow range |
| 2 (4-6) | Implicit criteria; mainly descriptive; inconsistent argument; assertion | Narrative, not linked to the argument, frequent inaccuracy |
| 1 (1-3) | Addresses the topic, not the question | Limited, inaccurate or vague |

## The questions

**1.** "Mussolini became prime minister in 1922 because the liberal state was unwilling to resist him." Assess this view. **[30]** *(Key question 1.1)*

**2.** Evaluate the extent to which Mussolini's economic policies achieved their aims. **[30]** *(1.3)*

**3.** Assess the success of fascist policies towards the Church and young people in Italy. **[30]** *(1.4, 1.2)*

**4.** "Stalin's control of the party machine was the main reason he emerged as Lenin's successor." Assess this view. **[30]** *(2.1)*

**5.** Evaluate the view that the human cost of collectivisation outweighed its achievements. **[30]** *(2.3)*

**6.** Assess the reasons for the Terror and Purges of the 1930s. **[30]** *(2.2, 2.4)*

**7.** Evaluate the view that Hitler's consolidation of power in 1933-34 depended more on legal methods than on terror. **[30]** *(3.1)*

**8.** "Nazi economic policy served rearmament rather than the German people." Assess this view. **[30]** *(3.3, 3.4)*

**9.** Assess the extent of opposition to the Nazi regime in the years 1933-41. **[30]** *(3.2)*

## Indicative content

These notes show the range of material an examiner would credit. They are not model essays: a Level 5 answer selects from them, sets criteria and argues to a judgement.

### Question 1

- **For the view:** Facta's government asked the king to sign a martial-law decree on 28 October 1922, and Victor Emmanuel III refused to sign it; the army could have dispersed the poorly armed squads. Liberal leaders (Giolitti in 1921) had included fascists in the National Blocs, legitimising them; police and prefects often tolerated squad violence during the Biennio Rosso.
- **Against / other factors:** the impact of war and the "mutilated victory"; Fiume as a model of defiance; economic crisis and fear of socialism among landowners and industrialists; Mussolini's "dual policy" of threatening violence while negotiating; the PNF's growth after 1921.
- **Judgement criterion:** distinguish *preconditions* (war, crisis) from the *decisive moment* (the king's refusal). A strong answer might argue that liberal unwillingness was decisive in October 1922 but was itself a product of fear of the left.

### Question 2

- **Aims:** national prestige, self-sufficiency, a "Third Way" between capitalism and socialism, and preparing for war.
- **Successes:** Battle for Grain increased wheat output; land reclamation in the Pontine Marshes created new towns; IRI rescued banks and industries in the Depression and made the state a major industrial owner; public works reduced unemployment.
- **Failures:** "Quota 90" overvalued the lira, damaging exports; grain policy displaced more suitable crops; the Corporate State gave employers more power than workers and changed little in practice; autarky after the 1935 sanctions was never achieved; Italy entered war in 1940 with weak industrial capacity.
- **Judgement:** prestige aims partly met; economic modernisation and readiness for war were not.

### Question 3

- **Church:** early anti-clericalism abandoned; Lateran Treaties (1929) recognised Vatican City and Catholicism as the state religion -- a major gain in consent. Tensions over Catholic Action (1931) and the 1938 racial laws show limits.
- **Youth:** Gentile reform (1923), textbook changes, teachers' oath; ONB (1926) and later GIL; university fascist groups. Limits: membership was patchy in the south and countryside; the Church kept its own youth influence.
- **Judgement criterion:** success as *control* versus success as *indoctrination*; compromise with the Church bought support but conceded independence.

### Question 4

- **For:** General Secretary from 1922; Orgburo and appointments; the "Lenin enrolment" of new members loyal to the apparatus; control of delegates to congresses.
- **Other factors:** rivals' weaknesses (Trotsky's arrogance and absence from Lenin's funeral; Zinoviev and Kamenev's opposition to the October 1917 rising); suppression of Lenin's Testament (May 1924); the popular appeal of "Socialism in One Country"; Stalin's shifting alliances -- first with Zinoviev and Kamenev against Trotsky, then with the Right against the United Opposition, then against the Right in 1928-29; the cult of "Lenin's disciple".
- **Judgement:** the party machine provided the means; ideology and rivals' errors provided the opportunities.

### Question 5

- **Costs:** dekulakisation and deportations; the famine of 1932-33, including in Ukraine and Kazakhstan, with millions of deaths; huge losses of livestock as peasants slaughtered animals; low productivity on collective farms.
- **Achievements (on Stalin's terms):** the state gained control of grain procurement; labour moved from countryside to industry, supporting the Five Year Plans; by the late 1930s most households were collectivised; machine tractor stations extended state control.
- **Judgement criterion:** "achievement" for whom -- the regime's industrial and political aims, or the peasantry? Valid answers can reach either verdict if the criterion is explicit.

### Question 6

- **Reasons:** Stalin's insecurity and personality; elimination of Old Bolsheviks and potential rivals (show trials of 1936-38); the Kirov murder (December 1934) as pretext; scapegoating for economic failures ("wreckers"); fear of war and a "fifth column", including the Red Army purge of 1937; a dynamic of NKVD quotas and denunciations once the Yezhovshchina began.
- **Wider effects on society (2.4 link):** fear and conformity; the Gulag as a labour source for projects.
- **Judgement:** weigh "top-down" explanations (Stalin's intention) against "bottom-up" ones (local denunciation, institutional momentum).

### Question 7

- **Legal methods:** appointment by Hindenburg; the Reichstag Fire Decree under Article 48; the March 1933 election; the Enabling Act passed with Centre Party votes (23 March 1933); Gleichschaltung laws; Hindenburg's death and the army oath.
- **Terror:** SA auxiliary police; early camps such as Dachau (March 1933); arrest of KPD deputies before the Enabling Act vote, SA intimidation in the chamber; banning of trade unions (May 1933) and parties (July 1933); Night of the Long Knives (30 June 1934).
- **Judgement:** the "legal revolution" was legality made possible by terror; a strong answer shows how each legal step depended on coercion.

### Question 8

- **For:** Mefo bills hid rearmament spending; the New Plan and the Four Year Plan prioritised raw materials and war industry; consumer goods and food imports were squeezed ("guns or butter"); the DAF froze wages and abolished trade union bargaining.
- **Against:** unemployment fell from about six million to under half a million by 1938; KdF holidays, Beauty of Labour and the promise of the Volkswagen; farmers protected by tariffs and the Reich Food Estate.
- **Judgement criterion:** separate short-term recovery (1933-36) from the war economy after 1936; ask which groups benefited.

### Question 9

- **Evidence of opposition:** underground KPD and SPD networks; the Confessing Church and the Pastors' Emergency League (Niemöller); Bishop Galen's sermon against the euthanasia programme (August 1941), which led to the official programme being halted; youth non-conformity among the Swing movement and Edelweiss Pirates; passive resistance such as grumbling, refusing the Hitler salute or listening to foreign radio.
- **Limits:** left-wing parties banned in 1933 and their leaders arrested or exiled; the Gestapo, SD, SS, block wardens and concentration camps; propaganda (Goebbels, Nuremberg rallies, 1936 Olympics) and economic recovery producing consent; the churches mostly protested only when their own interests were threatened.
- **Judgement criterion:** define opposition -- active resistance aiming to overthrow the regime was very limited before 1941, whereas non-conformity was widespread but did not threaten Nazi control.

## Planning drill

For any of the questions above, take five minutes to write: (1) your criterion for judgement, (2) three arguments for, (3) two or three arguments against, (4) a one-line judgement. Then write for 45 minutes. Two essays in 1 hour 45 minutes gives about 52 minutes each.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need historians in Paper 4?**
No. Paper 4 tests AO1 and AO2 only; interpretations are assessed on Paper 3. Use evidence and argument instead.

**Can one essay cover two key questions?**
Yes -- questions 3, 6 and 8 above deliberately cross key questions, as real questions sometimes do.

**What makes the judgement "sustained"?**
Each paragraph ends with a mini-judgement that links back to the question, so the conclusion confirms an argument the reader has already followed.
