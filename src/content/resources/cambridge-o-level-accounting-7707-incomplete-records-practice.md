These questions cover section 5.6 Incomplete records of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for 2027 to 2029. As the syllabus requires, every business is a sole trader. Questions 8 and 9 use the same trader. Revise first with the [incomplete records study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-incomplete-records/) and the [incomplete records revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-incomplete-records-revision-notes/). All traders and figures are invented, and amounts are in dollars.

## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** reasons why a small trader might not keep a full set of double entry records. **[2]**

**2.** A trader's capital was $42,600 at the start of the year and $47,150 at the end. During the year she withdrew $21,000 in cash and took goods costing $750. She also paid in $5,000 of her savings. Calculate her profit for the year. **[3]**

**3.** Receipts from credit customers were $58,720. Trade receivables were $6,340 at the start of the year and $5,980 at the end. Calculate credit sales. **[2]**

**4.** A trader's revenue is $72,000 and she adds a mark-up of 60% to cost. Calculate her cost of sales. **[2]**

**5.** A trader's gross profit margin is 20%. Calculate the mark-up. **[2]**

**6.** A trader's capital was $25,000 at the start of the year and $28,000 at the end. Drawings were $9,000 and capital introduced was $4,000. What was the profit for the year?

A: $2,000

B: $8,000

C: $10,000

D: $16,000

**[1]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Dina runs a market stall. At 1 April 2028 her business had: motor van $9,000; inventory $6,250; trade receivables $2,840; rent prepaid $400; bank overdraft $1,120; trade payables $3,470; loan from her brother $5,000.

**(a)** Prepare Dina's statement of affairs at 1 April 2028. **[4]**

**(b)** At 31 March 2029 Dina's capital was $12,300. Her drawings for the year were $14,400 and she introduced no capital. Calculate her profit for the year. **[2]**

**8.** Kwame does not keep full records. For the year ended 31 December 2028 his bank statements show receipts from credit customers $39,450, cash sales banked $12,800 and payments to suppliers $31,260. All sales other than cash sales are on credit and all purchases are on credit.

| | 1 January 2028 | 31 December 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Trade receivables | 4,120 | 3,670 |
| Trade payables | 2,980 | 3,540 |
| Inventory | 5,600 | 6,350 |

During the year Kwame took goods costing $300 for his own use.

**(a)** Calculate credit sales. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate total sales. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate purchases. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate cost of sales and gross profit. **[3]**

**9.** Kwame's gross profit for 2028 is $21,030. His other payments during 2028 were rent $4,800, wages $6,100 and electricity $1,350. At 31 December 2028 rent of $400 was prepaid. Electricity of $150 was owing at 1 January 2028 and $220 at 31 December 2028. His equipment had a book value of $7,500 at 1 January 2028 and $6,000 at 31 December 2028, with no purchases or sales of equipment.

**(a)** Calculate the electricity expense for 2028. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate Kwame's profit for the year. **[4]**

**10.** Mira's shop had revenue of $126,000 for the year and a gross profit margin of 25%. Opening inventory was $11,200 and purchases were $97,300. At the year end she counted inventory of $12,450 and suspects some goods were stolen.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of sales. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the closing inventory that should have been held. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the value of inventory missing. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest **one** way Mira could reduce losses of inventory. **[1]**

**11.** A trader's cost of sales for the year was $105,000 and the rate of inventory turnover was 7.5 times. Closing inventory was $15,200.

**(a)** Calculate the average inventory. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the opening inventory. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the inventory turnover in days, to two decimal places. **[2]**

**12.** Leon runs a sole trader business using only bank statements and a list of unpaid invoices. His bank has asked for a full statement of profit or loss before it will lend him $15,000.

**(a)** Explain **two** advantages to Leon of keeping a full set of double entry records. **[4]**

**(b)** Advise Leon whether to change to a full double entry system. Justify your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: it takes time [1]; it costs money to employ a book-keeper [1]; the owner lacks the knowledge; the business is small and simple. **[2]**

**2.** Drawings = 21,000 + 750 = $21,750 [1]. Profit = 47,150 - 42,600 + 21,750 - 5,000 [1] = **$21,300** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Goods taken are drawings, just like cash.

**3.** 58,720 - 6,340 + 5,980 [1] = **$58,360** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 72,000 x 100/160 [1] = **$45,000** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Taking 60% of revenue as gross profit gives $28,800, which treats the mark-up as a margin.

**5.** Gross profit 20, revenue 100, so cost of sales 80 [1]. Mark-up = 20/80 x 100 = **25%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** B [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)**

```
Dina
Statement of affairs at 1 April 2028
                                                USD        USD
Assets
  Motor van                                              9,000
  Inventory                                   6,250
  Trade receivables                           2,840
  Rent prepaid                                  400
                                                         9,490
Total assets                                            18,490
Liabilities
  Bank overdraft                              1,120
  Trade payables                              3,470
  Loan from brother                           5,000
                                                         9,590
Capital                                                  8,900
```

Marks: assets including the prepayment [1]; total assets $18,490 [1]; liabilities including the overdraft and loan [1]; capital $8,900 [1].

**(b)** 12,300 - 8,900 + 14,400 [1] = **$17,800** [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** 39,450 - 4,120 + 3,670 [1] = **$39,000** [1].

**(b)** 39,000 + 12,800 = **$51,800** [1].

**(c)** 31,260 - 2,980 + 3,540 [1] = **$31,820** [1].

**(d)** Cost of sales = 5,600 + (31,820 - 300) - 6,350 [1] = **$30,770** [1]. Gross profit = 51,800 - 30,770 = **$21,030** [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* Leaving the $300 in purchases overstates cost of sales and understates gross profit by $300.

**9. (a)** 1,350 - 150 + 220 [1] = **$1,420** [1].

**(b)** Rent = 4,800 - 400 = $4,400 [1]. Depreciation = 7,500 - 6,000 = $1,500 [1]. Expenses = 4,400 + 6,100 + 1,420 + 1,500 = $13,420 [1]. Profit for the year = 21,030 - 13,420 = **$7,610** [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Gross profit = 126,000 x 25% = $31,500 [1]. Cost of sales = 126,000 - 31,500 = **$94,500** [1].

**(b)** 11,200 + 97,300 - 94,500 [1] = **$14,000** [1].

**(c)** 14,000 - 12,450 = **$1,550** [1].

**(d)** Any one: count inventory more often; lock storage areas; limit staff access; check deliveries against invoices [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** 105,000 / 7.5 = **$14,000** [1].

**(b)** Opening inventory = (14,000 x 2) - 15,200 [1] = **$12,800** [1].

**(c)** 14,000 / 105,000 x 365 [1] = **48.67 days** [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** Any two, each explained: a trial balance can be drawn up [1], so errors are found before the statements are prepared [1]; a full statement of profit or loss and statement of financial position can be prepared [1], which the bank needs before it will lend [1]; better control of receivables and payables, so Leon can chase slow customers and avoid missing supplier payments; inventory and cash losses are noticed sooner because the records show what should be held.

**(b)** Leon should change, or employ a book-keeper [1], because the $15,000 loan depends on full statements and the extra time or cost is small against the benefit of the loan (also credit a justified case for rebuilding one year's statements through an accountant instead) [1]. **[6]**
