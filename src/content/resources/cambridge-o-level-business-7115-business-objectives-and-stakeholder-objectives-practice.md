These questions cover subtopic **1.5 Business objectives and stakeholder objectives** of Cambridge O Level Business Studies 7115 (2026 series). All businesses and figures are invented. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [Business objectives and stakeholder objectives study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-business-objectives-and-stakeholder-objectives/) and the [Business objectives and stakeholder objectives revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-business-objectives-and-stakeholder-objectives-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define market share. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** internal stakeholder groups. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why it is important for a business to set objectives. **[2]**

**4.** A paint company has sales of $1.2 million in a market worth $15 million. Calculate its market share. **[2]**

**5.** Explain **one** reason why a business might change its main objective from growth to survival. **[2]**

**6.** A café is run as a social enterprise and employs people with disabilities. State **one** social objective and **one** environmental objective it might have. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Pinecrest Bakery has the objectives of growing its sales and increasing its market share.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Pinecrest sales ($) | 250,000 | 290,000 |
| Total market sales ($) | 2,000,000 | 2,500,000 |

**(a)** Calculate Pinecrest's market share in each year.

**(b)** Calculate the percentage growth in Pinecrest's sales from Year 1 to Year 2.

**(c)** Explain whether Pinecrest achieved each of its two objectives. **[6]**

**8.** Stonebridge Quarries plc plans to open a new quarry next to a village.

**(a)** Identify **two** stakeholder groups that could benefit from the new quarry and explain how each benefits.

**(b)** Explain **one** conflict between stakeholder objectives that the plan could cause. **[6]**

**9.** Fairloop is a social enterprise that collects old computers, repairs them and sells them cheaply to schools.

**(a)** Explain **one** way Fairloop's objectives differ from those of a private company that sells new computers.

**(b)** Explain why Fairloop still needs a financial objective. **[4]**

**10.** A supermarket chain is deciding whether to give its employees a 6% pay rise. Consider whether employees or shareholders should be treated as the more important stakeholders in this decision. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** On the same route, one bus service is run by a public corporation and another by a private company. Explain **two** differences between the objectives of the two organisations. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why the objectives of the managers and the shareholders of a public limited company might conflict. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The sales of one business as a percentage [1] of the total sales in the market [1].

**2.** Any two [1] [1]: owners or shareholders; managers; employees.

**3.** Any two [1] [1]: they give direction; they help managers make decisions; they allow performance to be measured; they can motivate employees; they help departments coordinate.

**4.** 1.2 ÷ 15 × 100 [1] = **8%** [1].

**5.** One reason identified and explained [1] [1]: a new competitor has opened nearby and is taking customers, so the business must protect its cash and customers before trying to expand; or an economic downturn reduces demand, so staying in business becomes the priority.

**6.** Social: for example, employ and train a set number of people with disabilities each year [1]. Environmental: for example, reduce food waste or use only recyclable packaging [1].

**7.** **(a)** Year 1: 250,000 ÷ 2,000,000 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]. Year 2: 290,000 ÷ 2,500,000 × 100 = **11.6%** [1].

**(b)** (290,000 − 250,000) ÷ 250,000 × 100 [1] = **16%** [1].

**(c)** The growth objective was achieved because sales rose by 16% [1]. The market share objective was not, because the market grew by 25%, faster than Pinecrest, so its share fell from 12.5% to 11.6% [1].

**8.** **(a)** Two groups, each identified [1] and the benefit explained [1]: shareholders, because the new quarry increases output and should raise profit and dividends; local people seeking work, because the quarry creates jobs; the government, because it gains tax revenue and lower unemployment; local suppliers and shops, because quarry workers and the company spend money locally.

**(b)** A conflict identified [1] and explained [1]: shareholders want the quarry for profit, but local residents want a quiet, clean environment and will suffer dust, noise and lorry traffic.

**9.** **(a)** Fairloop's main aim is social and environmental, such as cutting electronic waste and helping schools afford computers [1], while a private company's main aim is usually profit for its owners [1].

**(b)** Fairloop must cover its costs, such as wages and repair parts, to survive [1]; any surplus can be reinvested to repair and supply more computers, so profit supports its social purpose [1].

**10.** Employees: a pay rise improves motivation and may reduce labour turnover, cutting recruitment and training costs [1]; better-motivated staff may give better service, which keeps customers [1]. Shareholders: a 6% rise increases costs and may reduce profit and dividends [1]; shareholders own the business and may sell shares if returns fall [1]. Judgement: employees should be treated as more important here if the supermarket is losing staff or customers because of poor service, since the long-term gain in sales can protect shareholders' profit too [1]; if profit is already low and staff turnover is small, shareholders' interest may come first [1].

**11.** Two differences, each identified [1] and explained [1]: the public corporation aims to provide a service to all passengers, while the private company aims to make a profit; the public corporation may keep fares low or run quiet, unprofitable times, while the private company may run only busy times to cover its costs; the public corporation answers to the government, while the private company answers to its owners.

**12.** Managers may want the company to grow, raising their salaries, status and job security [1]. Shareholders may want the highest possible profit paid out as dividends [1]. Spending profit on growth, or on high salaries for managers, reduces the dividends shareholders receive [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using sales growth as evidence of a higher market share, as tested in question 7.
- Naming stakeholders without saying how each gains or loses.
- Treating a social enterprise as a charity that does not need to cover its costs.
- Giving a judgement in question 10 without saying what it depends on.
- Stating that the public corporation "does not care about costs".
