These notes condense section 3 of Unit P2 (Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Level Mathematics, YMA01, Issue 3): the coordinate geometry of the circle "using the equation of a circle in the form (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²", with the angle in a semicircle, the perpendicular from the centre to a chord, and the perpendicularity of radius and tangent. For full worked examples, use the [P2 circle study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p2-coordinate-geometry-edexcel-alevel-maths/), then try the [P2 circle practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-p2-coordinate-geometry-edexcel-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Core facts

- Circle centre (a, b), radius r: **(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²**.
- Centre at the origin: x² + y² = r².
- Expanded form x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0: complete the square in x and in y; the centre is (−g, −f) and r² = g² + f² − c, which must be positive.
- A point is inside, on or outside the circle as its distance from the centre is less than, equal to or greater than r.

## The three properties and what each gives you

| Property | What to do with it |
|---|---|
| Angle in a semicircle is 90° | A right angle at P means AB is a diameter: centre = midpoint of AB, r = ½AB. Conversely, if AB is a diameter, AP ⟂ BP |
| Perpendicular from centre bisects chord | The line from the centre to a chord's midpoint is perpendicular to the chord; half-chord² = r² − d² |
| Radius ⟂ tangent | Tangent gradient = −1 ÷ (radius gradient); tangent length² = CT² − r² |

## Standard routines

**Centre and radius from the expanded form.** x² + y² + 6x − 8y = 0: (x + 3)² − 9 + (y − 4)² − 16 = 0, so (x + 3)² + (y − 4)² = 25. Centre (−3, 4), radius 5.

**Circle from a diameter AB.** Centre = midpoint of AB; r = half the length of AB.

**Circle through three points.** Look for a right angle (gradients multiply to −1): the side opposite it is a diameter. Otherwise, intersect two perpendicular bisectors to find the centre.

**Tangent at P on the circle.**

1. Gradient of CP.
2. Negative reciprocal.
3. y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) through P.

**Chord with known midpoint M.** Chord gradient = −1 ÷ (gradient of CM). Half-length = √(r² − CM²).

**Tangent length from external T.** √(CT² − r²).

**Line and circle.** Substitute the line into the circle and look at b² − 4ac of the resulting quadratic: positive (two points), zero (tangent), negative (no intersection).

**Circle touching an axis.** Touches the x-axis: r = |b|. Touches the y-axis: r = |a|.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centre | The fixed point (a, b) every point of the circle is equidistant from |
| Radius | The distance r from the centre to the circle |
| Diameter | A chord through the centre; length 2r |
| Chord | A line segment joining two points on the circle |
| Tangent | A line that touches the circle at exactly one point |
| Normal | The line perpendicular to the tangent at the point of contact; it passes through the centre |
| Perpendicular bisector | The line through a segment's midpoint at right angles to it; for a chord it passes through the centre |
| Completing the square | Rewriting x² + px as (x + p/2)² − (p/2)² to reveal the centre |

## Exam traps

- **Signs:** (x − 4)² + (y + 2)² = 9 has centre (4, −2).
- **r versus r²:** the right-hand side is r²; take the square root, leaving surds exact.
- **Coefficient of x² not 1:** divide every term first.
- **Negative right-hand side:** the equation does not represent a circle.
- **Radius gradient used for the tangent:** always take the negative reciprocal.
- **Half-chord:** Pythagoras gives half the chord; double it.
- **Vertical radius:** if the radius is vertical the tangent is horizontal (y = constant), and vice versa; the gradient rule needs care when a gradient is zero or undefined.

## Self-test

**1.** State the centre and radius of (x − 6)² + (y + 1)² = 49.

**2.** Find the centre and radius of x² + y² − 2x + 8y − 8 = 0.

**3.** A circle has centre (0, 0) and passes through (5, 12). Write down its equation.

**4.** Is the point (1, 2) inside, on or outside the circle (x − 3)² + y² = 9?

**5.** The radius to a point P has gradient ¾. State the gradient of the tangent at P.

**6.** A circle has radius 13. A chord is 5 units from the centre. Find the chord's length.

**7.** T is 17 units from the centre of a circle of radius 8. Find the length of a tangent from T.

**8.** A(2, 0) and B(0, 4) are the ends of a diameter. Find the circle's equation.

**9.** For what values of k does x² + y² + 4x + k = 0 represent a circle?

**10.** Find the tangent to x² + y² = 25 at (3, 4).

**11.** A circle with centre (2, 3) touches the y-axis. State its radius.

### Answers

**1.** Centre (6, −1), radius 7.

**2.** (x − 1)² + (y + 4)² = 25: centre (1, −4), radius 5.

**3.** x² + y² = 169.

**4.** (1 − 3)² + 2² = 8 < 9, so inside.

**5.** −4/3.

**6.** Half-chord = √(169 − 25) = 12, so the chord is 24.

**7.** √(289 − 64) = √225 = 15.

**8.** Centre (1, 2), r² = 1 + 4 = 5: (x − 1)² + (y − 2)² = 5.

**9.** (x + 2)² + y² = 4 − k, so k < 4.

**10.** Radius gradient 4/3, tangent gradient −3/4: y − 4 = −¾(x − 3), i.e. 3x + 4y = 25.

**11.** 2, the distance from the centre to the y-axis.
