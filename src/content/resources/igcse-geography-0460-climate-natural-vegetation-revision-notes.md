## Weather and climate

**Weather** is the day-to-day state of the atmosphere. **Climate** is the long-term average pattern
of weather. Describe a climate with mean temperature of the hottest and coolest months, the annual
range, and rainfall amount and seasonal distribution.

## The two climates compared

| | Equatorial | Hot desert |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Hot all year; very small annual range | Very hot by day, cooler at night; large daily range |
| Rainfall | High (well over 2000 mm in the study guide's example), fairly even through the year | Low (typically under 250 mm), unreliable and often in a few events |
| Main rainfall type | Convectional | No regular pattern |

## Factors that shape climate

- **Latitude:** angle of the sun; low latitude means consistently hot.
- **Pressure systems:** rising air cools and condenses (equatorial low pressure, rain); sinking air
  warms and its relative humidity falls, suppressing rain (many hot deserts under high pressure).
- **Winds:** bring or block moisture.
- **Distance from the sea:** moisture supply.
- **Altitude:** temperature falls with height.
- **Ocean currents:** warm currents warm coasts; cold currents cool them and can reduce rain.

## Tropical rainforest

- **Layers:** emergent, canopy, understorey, forest floor. Most life is in the dense canopy.
- **Adaptations:** drip tips shed heavy rain, buttress roots support tall trees, plants compete for
  light.
- Dense, layered and highly biodiverse, sustained by constant warmth and plentiful rain.

## Hot desert

- Sparse vegetation adapted to water scarcity and temperature swings.
- **Adaptations:** long or wide shallow roots, small or waxy leaves, spines, water-storing stems --
  each reduces water loss.

## Deforestation

| Causes | Effects |
|---|---|
| Logging, farming and cattle ranching, mining, road building, settlement | Loss of biodiversity; soil erosion; flooding; less transpiration and local rainfall; more carbon dioxide in the atmosphere |

Weigh the benefits that motivated clearing (income, farmland, jobs) against these costs.

## Case studies

The syllabus requires **an area of tropical rainforest and an area of hot desert**. Prepare a named
location for each, with climate, ecosystem features and a threat or use.

## Exam traps

- Describing a climate without explaining it, or naming a factor without linking it to rainfall.
- Saying sinking air "has no moisture" -- the point is that its relative humidity falls.
- Giving only the total rainfall and ignoring its seasonal distribution.
- Naming a plant adaptation without the climate condition it answers.
- A one-sided deforestation answer that omits either benefits or costs.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish weather from climate.
2. Why is the annual temperature range so small in an equatorial climate?
3. How does high pressure help create a desert?
4. Explain one adaptation of a desert plant.
5. Give two causes and two effects of rainforest deforestation.

**Answers:** 1. Weather is the day-to-day state; climate is the long-term average. 2. Near the
equator the sun's angle is consistently high, so temperatures stay high all year. 3. Sinking air warms
and its relative humidity falls, which suppresses cloud and rain. 4. For example small waxy leaves
reduce water loss. 5. Causes: logging and farming; effects: loss of biodiversity and soil erosion.
