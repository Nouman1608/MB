Condensed recall for section 4.7 (Geometrical reasoning) of the 4MA1 specification. Foundation candidates must give informal reasons for angle calculations based on lines (including chords and tangents), triangles and polygons. Higher candidates must give standard geometrical statements in any context with lines, polygons and circles. Full model answers are in the [Geometrical reasoning study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-geometrical-reasoning-edexcel-igcse-maths/); exam-style practice is in the [Geometrical reasoning practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-geometrical-reasoning-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transversal | A line crossing two (or more) parallel lines |
| Alternate angles | Z shape between parallel lines: equal |
| Corresponding angles | F shape, same position at each crossing: equal |
| Co-interior (allied) angles | C shape between parallel lines, same side: add to 180° |
| Vertically opposite | Opposite angles where two lines cross: equal |
| Exterior angle (triangle) | Angle between one side and the extension of the next |
| Exterior angle (polygon) | 180° minus the interior angle |
| Standard geometrical statement | The accepted full wording of an angle fact |

## Reasons to learn word for word

**Lines**

- Angles on a straight line add up to 180°.
- Angles at a point add up to 360°.
- Vertically opposite angles are equal.
- Alternate angles are equal.
- Corresponding angles are equal.
- Co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180°.

**Triangles and polygons**

- Angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
- The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
- Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal.
- Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°.
- Interior angles of an n-sided polygon add up to (n − 2) × 180°, which is (2n − 4) right angles.
- Exterior angles of a polygon add up to 360°.
- Opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal.

**Circles (both tiers)**

- Radii are equal (so the triangle is isosceles).
- A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.
- Tangents from an external point are equal in length.
- The perpendicular from the centre to a chord bisects the chord.

**Circles (Higher tier only)**

- The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference.
- The angle in a semicircle is 90°.
- Angles in the same segment are equal.
- Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral add up to 180°.
- The alternate segment theorem.

## Answer layout

```
angle XYZ = value   (reason)
angle ...  = value   (reason)
answer: angle asked for = value
```

One step, one reason. Use three-letter angle names. Finish "show that" questions with a sentence linking the result to what you were asked to prove.

## Using a fact backwards

The converse facts let you **prove** lines are parallel:

- If alternate angles are equal, the lines are parallel.
- If corresponding angles are equal, the lines are parallel.
- If co-interior angles add up to 180°, the lines are parallel.

## Spotting the shape between parallel lines

| Shape you can trace | Angle pair | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Z (or backwards Z) | Alternate | Equal |
| F (in any orientation) | Corresponding | Equal |
| C or U | Co-interior | Add to 180° |
| Arrowhead pointing between the lines | Split with an extra parallel line | Two alternate pairs |

## Polygon quick values

| Regular polygon | Exterior | Interior | Sum of interior angles |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pentagon | 72° | 108° | 540° |
| Hexagon | 60° | 120° | 720° |
| Octagon | 45° | 135° | 1080° |
| Decagon | 36° | 144° | 1440° |

## Exam traps

- "Z angles", "F angles", "C angles", "X angles": not standard statements.
- Calling co-interior angles equal.
- One reason for several steps.
- Missing the isosceles reason when two equal sides or two radii are used.
- Starting from the given answer in a "show that" question.
- Measuring an angle on a diagram that is not accurately drawn.
- Higher: "circle theorem" instead of naming the theorem.

## Self-test

1. Give the reason: two angles in a C shape between parallel lines total 180°.
2. Give the reason: an angle and the angle opposite it where two lines cross are equal.
3. An exterior angle of a triangle is 131°; one interior opposite angle is 58°. Find the other, with a reason.
4. Angles round a point are 95°, 140° and x°. Find x, with a reason.
5. Find the sum of the interior angles of a heptagon (7 sides).
6. Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 140°. How many sides? Give the reason you use.
7. The apex angle of an isosceles triangle is 52°. Find a base angle.
8. Two angles at a transversal are corresponding and both are 68°. What can you conclude?
9. OA and OB are radii; angle AOB = 100°. Find angle OAB and give both reasons.
10. Why is "angles in parallel lines" not an acceptable reason?
11. (Higher) A triangle stands on a diameter with one angle 33° at an end of the diameter. Find the third angle.
12. (Higher) The angle between a tangent and a chord is 47°. State the angle in the alternate segment and the reason.

### Answers

1. Co-interior (allied) angles add up to 180°.
2. Vertically opposite angles are equal.
3. 131° − 58° = 73°; the exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.
4. 360 − 95 − 140 = 125; angles at a point add up to 360°.
5. (7 − 2) × 180° = 900°.
6. Exterior 40°, so 360 ÷ 40 = 9 sides; exterior angles of a polygon add up to 360°.
7. (180° − 52°) ÷ 2 = 64°.
8. The lines are parallel (corresponding angles are equal).
9. 40°; radii are equal so the triangle is isosceles (base angles equal), and angles in a triangle add up to 180°.
10. It does not say which pair or which rule; name alternate, corresponding or co-interior angles.
11. 90° − 33° = 57° (the angle in a semicircle is 90°).
12. 47°, by the alternate segment theorem.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many reasons do I need in a multi-step angle question?**
One for each step. If you find three angles on the way to the answer, each needs its own standard statement.

**Is "allied angles" the same as "co-interior angles"?**
Yes. The 4MA1 specification uses "allied", and both names describe angles between parallel lines on the same side of a transversal, which add up to 180°.
