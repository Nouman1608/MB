Condensed recall for **4.2 Demographic transition** (the model, its stages and its limits). For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demographic transition | Shift from high to low birth and death rates as a country develops |
| Youthful population | Large share of children |
| Ageing population | Large share of elderly people |
| Life expectancy | Average expected years of life |
| Natural decrease | Deaths exceed births |

## The five stages

| Stage | Birth rate | Death rate | Natural increase | Population |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | High | High | Low | Small, stable or fluctuating |
| 2 | High | Falls rapidly | Rising to high | Grows quickly, youthful |
| 3 | Falls | Low, falling | Slowing | Still growing |
| 4 | Low | Low | Low | Large, stable |
| 5 | Very low | Low | Zero or negative | Ageing, may shrink |

## Reasons for change

- **Death rate falls:** better food supply, clean water, sanitation, medicine and vaccination, public health.
- **Birth rate falls:** lower infant mortality, education and employment of women, contraception, urbanisation, rising cost of children.
- **Stage 5:** low fertility, high old-age dependency, possible reliance on immigration.

## Links with development

As infant mortality falls and life expectancy rises, a country moves along the model. These changes accompany economic and social development, though the relationship is not exact.

## Critical appreciation

- Based on European experience; **later transitions** (many LICs) can be faster, because medical technology arrives from outside.
- Does not include migration.
- Assumes a single path; some countries show fertility falling before economic growth, others stall.
- Stage 5 is a later addition, not part of the original four-stage model.
- Government policy and HIV/AIDS and conflict can distort the path.

## Exam traps

- In stage 2 the death rate falls first; the birth rate stays high.
- Natural increase is highest in stage 2, not stage 3, but population keeps growing in stage 3.
- Do not call the model a prediction; it is a generalisation.

## Self-test

1. Which stage has the highest natural increase?
2. Why does the death rate fall in stage 2?
3. Describe stage 4.
4. What is distinctive about stage 5?
5. Give one limitation of the model.
6. Why do many LICs transition faster?

**Answers:** 1. Stage 2. 2. Better food, sanitation, medicine and public health. 3. Low birth and death rates, large stable population. 4. Birth rate below death rate, ageing population, natural decrease. 5. European basis; ignores migration; assumes one path. 6. Imported medical technology cut death rates quickly.
