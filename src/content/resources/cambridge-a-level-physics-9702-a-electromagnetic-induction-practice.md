## Section A

**1.** Define magnetic flux. **[2]**

**2.** State Faraday's law and Lenz's law of electromagnetic induction. **[2]**

**3.** A square loop of side 4.0 cm is placed with its plane perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.25 T. Calculate the magnetic flux through the loop. **[2]**

**4.** The loop in Question 3 is turned so that its plane is parallel to the field. State the flux through it now. **[1]**

**5.** In which situation is an e.m.f. induced in a coil?

A: the coil is at rest in a constant uniform field

B: the coil moves without rotating through a uniform field, staying entirely inside it

C: the current in a nearby coil is being switched off

D: a magnet is held at rest inside the coil

**[1]**

**6.** A bar magnet is held at rest inside a coil connected to a sensitive meter. Explain why the meter reads zero even though magnetic flux passes through the coil. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A rectangular coil of 200 turns measures 5.0 cm by 8.0 cm. It is placed with its plane perpendicular to a uniform field of 0.35 T.

**(a)** Calculate the flux linkage. **[2]**

**(b)** The coil is rotated through 90° in 0.12 s, so that its plane becomes parallel to the field. Calculate the average induced e.m.f. **[2]**

**(c)** Instead, the coil is rotated through 180° from its starting position in 0.12 s. Calculate the average induced e.m.f. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the flux linkage when the plane of the coil makes an angle of 30° with the field. **[1]**

**8.** A metal rod of length 0.15 m slides at a constant 4.0 m s⁻¹ along two parallel rails joined at one end. A uniform field of 0.60 T acts at right angles to the plane of the rails. The total resistance of the circuit is 0.90 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the induced e.m.f. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the current. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the magnetic force on the rod. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the power needed to keep the rod moving at constant speed, and show that it equals the electrical power dissipated. **[2]**

**(e)** Use Lenz's law to explain why an external force is needed to keep the rod moving. **[2]**

**9.** The flux linkage of a coil increases steadily from 0 to 0.060 Wb in the first 0.030 s, stays constant until t = 0.050 s, then decreases steadily to zero at t = 0.060 s.

**(a)** Calculate the induced e.m.f. during each of the three stages. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the graph of induced e.m.f. against time for 0 to 0.060 s. **[2]**

**10.** An aircraft with a wingspan of 30 m flies horizontally at 250 m s⁻¹. The vertical component of the Earth's magnetic field is 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ T.

**(a)** Calculate the e.m.f. induced between the wingtips. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why there is no continuous current along the wing as a result of this e.m.f. **[1]**

**11.** A bar magnet is dropped vertically through a coil connected to a data-logger that records the induced e.m.f. against time. The magnet passes completely through the coil.

**(a)** Explain why the trace shows two pulses of opposite sign. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the second pulse has a larger peak value but a shorter duration than the first. **[2]**

**(c)** State what can be said about the areas under the two pulses, with a reason. **[1]**

**12.** A flat square coil of 50 turns and side 5.0 cm moves at a steady 0.10 m s⁻¹ into a region of uniform field of 0.20 T. The plane of the coil is perpendicular to the field, and one side of the coil is parallel to the edge of the field region.

**(a)** Calculate the e.m.f. induced while the coil is entering the field. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time for which this e.m.f. is induced. **[1]**

**(c)** State and explain the e.m.f. when the coil is entirely inside the field. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The product of the magnetic flux density [1] and the cross-sectional area perpendicular to the direction of the field [1].

**2.** Faraday: the induced e.m.f. is proportional to the rate of change of magnetic flux linkage [1]. Lenz: the induced e.m.f. is in a direction that opposes the change producing it [1].

**3.** A = 0.040² = 1.6 × 10⁻³ m² [1]; Φ = BA = 0.25 × 1.6 × 10⁻³ = 4.0 × 10⁻⁴ Wb [1].

**4.** Zero [1].

**5.** C [1].

**6.** The induced e.m.f. depends on the rate of change of flux linkage, not on the flux itself [1]. With the magnet at rest the flux linkage is constant, so no e.m.f. is induced [1].

**7. (a)** A = 0.050 × 0.080 = 4.0 × 10⁻³ m² [1]; NΦ = 200 × 0.35 × 4.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.28 Wb [1].

**(b)** Change in flux linkage = 0.28 Wb [1]; E = 0.28 ÷ 0.12 = 2.3 V [1].

**(c)** Change = 2 × 0.28 = 0.56 Wb; E = 0.56 ÷ 0.12 = 4.7 V [1].

**(d)** Perpendicular area = A sin 30°: NΦ = 0.28 × 0.50 = 0.14 Wb [1].

**8. (a)** E = BLv = 0.60 × 0.15 × 4.0 = 0.36 V [1].

**(b)** I = E/R = 0.36 ÷ 0.90 = 0.40 A [1].

**(c)** F = BIL = 0.60 × 0.40 × 0.15 = 0.036 N [1].

**(d)** Mechanical power = Fv = 0.036 × 4.0 = 0.144 W ≈ 0.14 W [1]; electrical power = EI = 0.36 × 0.40 = 0.144 W, the same [1].

**(e)** The induced current in the rod flows in a direction that opposes the change producing it [1], so the force on the rod (from the induced current in the field) acts against its motion; without an external force the rod would slow down [1].

**9. (a)** 0 to 0.030 s: E = 0.060 ÷ 0.030 = 2.0 V [1]. 0.030 s to 0.050 s: zero [1]. 0.050 s to 0.060 s: E = 0.060 ÷ 0.010 = 6.0 V, of opposite sign [1].

**(b)** A constant e.m.f. of 2.0 V from 0 to 0.030 s, zero from 0.030 s to 0.050 s [1], then a constant e.m.f. of 6.0 V of the opposite sign from 0.050 s to 0.060 s [1].

**10. (a)** In 1 s the wing sweeps an area of 30 × 250 m², so E = BLv [1] = 4.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 30 × 250 = 0.30 V [1].

**(b)** The wing is not part of a complete circuit, so although an e.m.f. is induced, there is no closed path for a continuous current [1].

**11. (a)** As the magnet enters, the flux linkage increases; as it leaves, the flux linkage decreases [1]. The e.m.f. is in the direction that opposes the change, so the two changes give e.m.f.s of opposite sign [1].

**(b)** The magnet accelerates as it falls, so it is moving faster as it leaves [1]; the flux linkage changes at a greater rate (larger e.m.f.) over a shorter time [1].

**(c)** The areas are equal, because each equals the same total change in flux linkage (from zero to maximum and back) [1].

**12. (a)** E = NBLv = 50 × 0.20 × 0.050 × 0.10 [1] = 0.050 V [1].

**(b)** t = 0.050 ÷ 0.10 = 0.50 s [1].

**(c)** Zero: the flux linkage is no longer changing once the whole coil is in the uniform field [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole area of a coil whose plane is tilted to the field.
- Forgetting the number of turns.
- Treating a 180° rotation as a zero change in flux linkage.
- Explaining induction in terms of flux rather than its rate of change.
- Converting cm² to m² incorrectly.
- In Lenz's law answers, not saying what the induced effect opposes.
