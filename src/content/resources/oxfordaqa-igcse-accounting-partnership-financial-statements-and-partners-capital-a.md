## 1. Three statements, one flow

A partnership has two or more owners who share the profit. Its accounts follow the same chain as a sole trader's, with one extra step.

1. The **income statement** finds the profit for the year (or loss).
2. The **appropriation account** shows how that profit is shared between the partners.
3. The **statement of financial position** shows the assets, liabilities and the partners' equity at the year end.

Every figure you need comes from the question: the partnership agreement is given as rates, amounts and a ratio. Use only the items the question mentions. Do not add interest or salaries that are not stated.

## 2. The income statement

The income statement is prepared exactly as for a sole trader: revenue, cost of sales, gross profit, expenses, profit for the year. Two points are specific to partnerships.

- **Interest on a partner's loan** is an expense. A loan from a partner is a liability of the firm, so its interest is charged in the income statement before the profit for the year is reached. It does not go in the appropriation account.
- **Partners' salaries and drawings are not expenses.** They are shares of profit, so they appear in the appropriation account and in the partners' accounts, never in the income statement.

## 3. The appropriation account

The appropriation account starts with the profit for the year and divides it. Work down the following items in this order.

| Item | Effect on the amount shared | Credited to the partner's current account? |
|---|---|---|
| Interest on drawings | Added to profit (the firm earns it) | No, it is debited to the partner |
| Interest on capital | Deducted | Yes |
| Partnership salaries | Deducted | Yes |
| Residual profit (or deficit) | Shared in the profit sharing ratio | Yes (a deficit is debited) |

The residual profit is what remains after interest on capital and salaries. It is the figure that is shared in the ratio. Each partner's total share is their interest on capital plus their salary plus their part of the residual, less their interest on drawings.

Why each item exists: interest on capital rewards a partner who invests more; salaries reward a partner who works more in the business; interest on drawings discourages large early withdrawals; the ratio shares what is left.

## 4. Capital and current accounts

Each partner has two accounts. The two serve different purposes.

| | Capital account | Current account |
|---|---|---|
| What it records | The long-term investment the partner has put in | The yearly flow of profit and drawings |
| Normal entries | The agreed capital, which changes only by agreement | Interest on capital, salary, share of profit (credit); drawings and interest on drawings (debit) |
| Usual balance | Credit | Credit if the firm owes the partner; debit if the partner has taken out more than earned |
| Where it appears | Statement of financial position, equity | Statement of financial position, equity |

Keeping the two apart means the capital, which decides the interest on capital, stays fixed. Day-to-day movements go through the current account. In this guide the capital accounts do not change during the year.

A **debit balance** on a current account means the partner has taken out more than the profit allocated. It is shown in brackets in equity and reduces the total.

## Example 1: Kellmoor Bicycle Works

Kellmoor Bicycle Works is owned by Anwen, Bashir and Chidi. The year ended 30 June 2026. The partnership agreement gives:

- capital: Anwen 60,000; Bashir 40,000; Chidi 20,000
- interest on capital 5% per year
- salaries: Bashir 12,000; Chidi 6,000
- interest on drawings 2% of each partner's drawings for the year
- residual profit shared 3 : 2 : 1
- Bashir has lent the firm 10,000 at 6% per year, paid in full during the year

Other figures: revenue 214,000; opening inventory 16,500; purchases 118,300; closing inventory 19,400; wages 36,400; rent and rates 11,600; general expenses 8,570; depreciation 6,300. These expenses are already adjusted for accruals and prepayments. Drawings: Anwen 10,000; Bashir 11,000; Chidi 9,000. Opening current accounts: Anwen credit 1,800; Bashir credit 900; Chidi debit 400.

**Income statement for the year ended 30 June 2026**

```

Opening inventory                    16,500
Purchases                           118,300
                                    134,800
Less closing inventory               19,400

Interest on partner's loan              600      63,470

```

Loan interest is 10,000 x 6% = 600.

**Appropriation account**

```

                                                          35,730

   Chidi 1,000                                  6,000

Residual profit                                           11,730
Shared 3:2:1: Anwen 5,865, Bashir 3,910, Chidi 1,955      11,730
```

Interest on drawings: 2% of 10,000, 11,000 and 9,000. The residual is divided into six parts of 1,955.


Chidi's debit balance of 625 is the balancing figure on the credit side. Chidi drew 9,180 in cash and interest against an entitlement of 8,955, plus the opening debit of 400.

## Example 2: Fernhaven Print Studio

Fernhaven Print Studio is a service business owned by Dara (capital 50,000) and Eilidh (capital 30,000). The agreement gives 4% interest on capital, a salary of 9,000 to Eilidh, and residual profit or loss shared 2 : 1. There are no drawings in this extract. The table shows two possible years.

| | Profit of 11,000 | Loss of 4,000 |
|---|---|---|
| Profit or (loss) for the year | 11,000 | (4,000) |
| Interest on capital: Dara 2,000, Eilidh 1,200 | 3,200 | 3,200 |
| Salary: Eilidh | 9,000 | 9,000 |
| Total taken before the ratio | 12,200 | 12,200 |
| Residual (deficit) | (1,200) | (16,200) |
| Dara 2/3 | (800) | (10,800) |
| Eilidh 1/3 | (400) | (5,400) |
| Dara credited in total | 1,200 | (8,800) |
| Eilidh credited in total | 9,800 | 4,800 |

Even when the firm makes a loss, the agreed interest and salary are still credited, so the loss plus those items becomes a larger deficit. The deficit is shared in the ratio and debited to the partners. Dara's account is debited by 8,800 in total in the second year, which is worth a comment: Dara bears most of the loss but is paid no salary.

## 5. Commenting on partnership statements

The specification asks you to comment as well as prepare. A good comment states a figure, says what it means and links it to the partners or the business. Useful points:

- **Overdrawn current account.** Chidi's debit balance means a partner has taken out more than has been allocated. The firm's funds are in effect financing that partner. A sensible suggestion is to agree a drawings limit or raise the interest rate on drawings.
- **Salary and interest take most of the profit.** In Example 1, 24,000 of 35,730 is allocated before the ratio. The ratio then affects only a third of the profit, so it matters less to the partners than the salaries.
- **Loan versus capital.** Bashir's loan earns a fixed 600 whatever the profit, which is taken before profit is shared. Interest on capital is not an expense: it is an appropriation, so in a poor year it adds to the deficit that the partners share.
- **Liquidity.** Bank is 1,580 against current liabilities of 12,950. The partnership depends on its receivables and inventory to meet its payables.
- **Fairness.** A partner with a large capital but no salary (Dara in Example 2) may feel the agreement is not fair in a poor year.

## Common errors

- Putting salaries or drawings in the income statement.
- Putting interest on a partner's loan in the appropriation account.
- Deducting interest on drawings from profit instead of adding it.
- Sharing the profit for the year in the ratio before deducting interest on capital and salaries.
- Dropping the interest and salaries in a loss year.
- Forgetting that a debit balance on a current account is deducted in equity.
- Adding the current accounts to the capital accounts and then adding the loan again.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is a partnership appropriation account?**
The appropriation account follows the income statement and shows how the profit for the year is shared between the partners. Interest on drawings is added, then interest on capital and partnership salaries are deducted, and the residual profit is split in the profit sharing ratio.

**Is interest on a partner's loan an expense or an appropriation of profit?**
Interest on a partner's loan is an expense, so it goes in the income statement before the profit for the year is found. Interest on capital and partnership salaries are appropriations, so they belong in the appropriation account instead.

**What is the difference between a partner's capital account and current account?**
The capital account holds the fixed amount each partner has invested, which usually stays the same from year to year. The current account records the partner's share of profit, interest and salary, less drawings and interest on drawings, so it changes every year.

**What does a debit balance on a partner's current account mean?**
A debit balance means the partner has withdrawn more than the profit, interest and salary credited to them. It is shown as a deduction within the current accounts on the statement of financial position and is worth commenting on as a sign the partner is overdrawing.

**Are admission and retirement of partners examined in OxfordAQA IGCSE Accounting 9215?**
No. The 9215 specification states that admission, retirement of a partner, dissolution of partnerships and changes to the profit sharing ratio will not be examined.
