Condensed notes for section 1.2, Accounting concepts, Topic 1 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1), Issue 1, October 2016: "understand the significance of" consistency, prudence, accruals, materiality, money measurement and business entity. Amounts are in dollars, written without a currency sign.

## The six concepts: meaning, effect, significance

| Concept | Meaning | Where you see it in the records | Significance for users |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consistency | Same methods and classifications each period | Depreciation method, inventory valuation, where costs are classified | Years can be compared fairly |
| Prudence | Do not overstate profit or assets; recognise expected losses promptly | Provision for irrecoverable debts; inventory at lower of cost and net realisable value | Profit and assets can be relied on |
| Accruals | Record income and expenses in the period they belong to | Other payables and other receivables | Profit reflects the period's activity, not payment timing |
| Materiality | Detailed treatment only where an item could affect decisions | Small items expensed instead of capitalised | Statements are clear and economical to prepare |
| Money measurement | Record only items measurable in money | Skills and reputation left out | Statements are not a full valuation of the business |
| Business entity | Business recorded separately from the owner | Capital and drawings | Profit is not distorted by private items |

## Double entries driven by business entity

| Event | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays more money into the business bank | Bank | Capital |
| Owner pays a business supplier from a personal account | Trade payables | Capital |
| Owner takes cash for private use | Drawings | Cash or bank |
| Owner takes goods for private use | Drawings | Purchases |
| Business pays the owner's private bill | Drawings | Bank |

## Quick calculations

- **Prudence.** Receivables 30,000; a 2,000 debt is irrecoverable; provision 4% on the rest. Write off 2,000; provision 28,000 × 4% = 1,120; receivables shown 26,880; profit lower by 3,120 (no earlier provision).
- **Accruals.** December rent received in January, 700: other receivable 700, included in this year's income. December wages unpaid at year end, 1,300: other payable 1,300, included in this year's wages.
- **Consistency.** Moving 1,500 of carriage inwards from cost of sales to expenses raises gross profit by 1,500; profit for the year is unchanged; year-on-year gross profit comparison is distorted.
- **Materiality.** Policy: capitalise items of 400 or more. A 95 stapler is an expense; a 1,600 printer is a non-current asset.

## When concepts conflict

| Pair | The tension |
|---|---|
| Prudence and accruals | Accruals matches to the period; prudence records an expected loss before it happens |
| Prudence and consistency | A more cautious method may be tempting, but changing methods breaks comparability |
| Materiality and accuracy | Exact treatment of tiny items costs more than it is worth |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concept | An agreed rule underlying how transactions are recorded and reported |
| Comparability | Users can compare one period's figures with another's |
| Realised | Turned into cash or a right to receive cash |
| Net realisable value | Expected selling price less costs of selling |
| Irrecoverable debt | An amount owed that will not be received |
| Provision for irrecoverable debts | An estimate of receivables that may not be collected |
| Other payable | An expense owed at the year end |
| Other receivable | Income owed to the business, or an expense paid in advance, at the year end |
| Capital | The owner's investment in the business |
| Drawings | Resources taken by the owner for private use |

## Exam traps

- Naming a concept without saying what it does to the figures.
- "Prudence means always choosing the lowest profit": it means not overstating.
- "Accruals means recording cash": it is the opposite.
- "Materiality means anything over a fixed amount": it depends on the size of the business.
- "Money measurement means current value": it means only what can be measured in money is recorded.
- Treating the owner's private bill as a business expense.
- Forgetting that changing classification, not just method, breaks consistency.

## Self-test

**1.** Which concept explains why the owner's private car is not in the business's records?
**2.** Which concept explains why the skill of the staff is not shown as an asset?
**3.** State the significance of the consistency concept.
**4.** A customer owing 900 has gone out of business. Which concept requires the debt to be written off now?
**5.** December electricity of 320 is paid in January. Which concept applies, and what is recorded at the year end?
**6.** Give the double entry when the owner takes goods costing 180 for private use.
**7.** Give the double entry when the owner pays a business supplier 450 from a personal account.
**8.** Why might 300 be material to one business and not to another?
**9.** What does prudence say about gains that are expected but not yet realised?
**10.** Give one way prudence and accruals can pull in different directions.
**11.** Why can figures from years far apart not be compared exactly, under money measurement?
**12.** A business moves delivery costs on purchases from cost of sales to expenses. What happens to gross profit and to profit for the year?

### Answers

**1.** Business entity.
**2.** Money measurement.
**3.** Using the same methods each period lets users compare years fairly, so changes in profit reflect performance, not a change of method.
**4.** Prudence.
**5.** Accruals: an other payable of 320, and 320 included in this year's electricity expense.
**6.** Debit drawings 180; credit purchases 180.
**7.** Debit trade payables 450; credit capital 450.
**8.** Materiality is relative: 300 may be large compared with a small business's profit but trivial for a large one.
**9.** They are not recorded until they are realised.
**10.** Prudence records an expected loss (such as a provision) before it occurs, rather than waiting to match it to the period when it happens.
**11.** The value of money changes, so identical items bought in different years are recorded at different amounts.
**12.** Gross profit rises by the amount moved; profit for the year is unchanged.
