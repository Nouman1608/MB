## Paper 1 at a glance

| Section | Focus | Structure |
|---|---|---|
| A | Shakespeare play | Set extract, then whole-text essay |
| B | 19th-century novel | Set extract, then whole-text essay |

1 hour 45 minutes, **closed book**, 64 marks = 40% of the GCSE. (Paper 2: 2h15, 96 marks, 60%.)

## Closed book means...

No text in the exam room — quotation banks must be **memorised**. Build a compact bank per text
covering: 2-3 major characters, 2-3 central themes, and a handful of pivotal moments — rather
than attempting to memorise the whole play or novel.

## Shakespeare section

- Treat the play as **drama**, not prose: comment on staging, performance choices and how a line
  might be delivered, not just what it means on the page.
- Both the extract and the whole-text question expect you to move fluently between the printed
  extract and your knowledge of the play as a whole.

## Novel section

- Track how a **theme or character develops across the whole novel** — a single-scene answer
  underperforms one that shows development.
- Integrate historical/social **context** into the analysis itself, not as a separate paragraph —
  AQA's mark scheme explicitly credits this integration.

## Exam traps

- Treating Shakespeare purely as text, ignoring performance and staging.
- Writing "context paragraphs" that are disconnected from the actual argument.
- Spending equal time on Paper 1 and Paper 2 revision, when Paper 2 is worth 50% more (96 vs 64 marks).
- Attempting to memorise entire texts instead of a focused, well-chosen quotation bank.

## Self-test

1. Why does "closed book" change how you should revise?
2. What two elements does each of Sections A and B contain?
3. Give one context-integration tip specific to Shakespeare.
4. How should Paper 1 revision time compare with Paper 2, given the mark split?

**Answers:** 1. Because no text is available in the exam, quotations must be memorised — making a focused, well-chosen quotation bank essential rather than optional. 2. A set extract question and a whole-text essay question, once for the Shakespeare play and once for the novel. 3. Keep performance and staging in view alongside language, since how a line is staged or spoken is often as significant as what it says. 4. Paper 1 (64 marks, 40%) should get proportionally less revision time than Paper 2 (96 marks, 60%), including dedicated unseen-poetry practice for Paper 2.
