## Section A

**1.** Give three reasons for Kennedy's victory in the 1960 election. **[3]**

**2.** Identify three measures of the Great Society other than civil rights laws. **[3]**

**3.** Explain two consequences of the Bay of Pigs invasion. **[4]**

**4.** Identify three options the ExComm considered in October 1962. **[3]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why US tactics in South Vietnam, 1965-68, were ineffective. **[4]**

**6.** Identify three methods used by Southern officials and citizens to resist civil rights. **[3]**

**7.** Give three aims or activities of the Black Panther Party. **[3]**

**8.** Identify three stages by which the Watergate cover-up was exposed. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "Kennedy's domestic achievements were limited." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the reasons why the USA withdrew from Vietnam. **[30]**

**11.** Assess the importance of Martin Luther King in the progress of civil rights, 1961-68. **[30]**

**12.** "Opposition to the Vietnam War was the main reason for challenges to authority in the USA, 1961-74." Assess this view. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: Kennedy's youth and performance in the televised debates (1); the promise of a New Frontier and "getting the country moving again" (1); an efficient, well-funded campaign organisation (1); Lyndon Johnson as running mate helping win Texas and parts of the South (1); his call to Coretta Scott King, which helped win African American votes (1); Nixon's association with a recession and the U-2 incident (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: the Economic Opportunity Act and War on Poverty (Job Corps, VISTA, Head Start) (1); Medicare and Medicaid (1965) (1); the Elementary and Secondary Education Act (1965) (1); the Higher Education Act (1965) (1); the Department of Housing and Urban Development and Model Cities (1); the Immigration and Nationality Act (1965) (1); the Wilderness Act (1964) and air and water pollution laws (1). **[3]**

**3.** Two consequences, 2 marks each: it humiliated Kennedy and made him determined to appear tough, leading to covert efforts against Castro (Operation Mongoose) (2); it drove Castro closer to the USSR, encouraging Khrushchev to place missiles in Cuba to defend it (2); it led Kennedy to distrust CIA and military advice, which shaped his caution in the missile crisis (2). **[4]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: a surgical air strike on the missile sites (1); a full invasion of Cuba (1); a naval blockade or quarantine (1); diplomatic pressure through the UN or direct negotiation with Khrushchev (1); doing nothing (1). **[3]**

**5.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: search-and-destroy missions and body counts measured success by enemy dead, but the Viet Cong could avoid battle and replace losses, so territory was not secured (2); bombing and defoliants destroyed villages and crops, alienating the peasants whose support was needed (2); supplies continued down the Ho Chi Minh trail through Laos and Cambodia, which US ground forces were not allowed to cut (2); the South Vietnamese government remained unstable and unpopular (2). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: literacy tests and obstructive registrars to stop voter registration (1); police violence, as under Bull Connor in Birmingham (1); Governor Wallace blocking integration at the University of Alabama (1); filibusters by Southern Congressmen (1); economic pressure by White Citizens' Councils, such as sacking those who registered (1); Klan bombings and murders (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: armed patrols to monitor police behaviour in Black neighbourhoods (1); the Ten-Point Programme demanding housing, jobs, education and an end to police brutality (1); free breakfast programmes for children (1); health clinics (1); Black pride and self-defence (1); political campaigning, such as Seale's run for mayor of Oakland (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: reporting by Woodward and Bernstein in the *Washington Post* (1); the trial of the burglars and McCord's letter alleging pressure to stay silent (1); the Senate Watergate Committee hearings (1973) (1); the revelation of the White House taping system (July 1973) (1); the Saturday Night Massacre (October 1973) (1); *United States v. Nixon* forcing release of the tapes (July 1974) (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For the view:** Medicare and federal aid to education were blocked; the civil rights bill and tax cut passed only after his death; the conservative coalition of Republicans and Southern Democrats controlled key committees; his narrow mandate limited his leverage. **Against:** the minimum wage rise, Area Redevelopment Act, Housing Act (1961), Manpower Development and Training Act (1962) and Equal Pay Act (1963); the Commission on the Status of Women; using marshals and troops to enforce integration (Ole Miss, 1962); the June 1963 civil rights address set the agenda that Johnson completed. **Criterion:** achievements measured by laws passed, or by agenda-setting. **Judgement:** limited in legislation, but more significant in shaping the reforms of 1964-65. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Military:** the failure of attrition; Tet (1968) showed that victory was not near despite official optimism. **Domestic opinion:** television coverage, rising casualties, My Lai, the draft and campus protest; Cronkite's doubts after Tet; Johnson's withdrawal from the election. **Political:** Nixon's promise of "peace with honour" and Vietnamisation; the 1970 Cambodia invasion and Kent State deepened opposition; Congress restricted funding. **Economic:** the war's cost fuelled inflation and undermined the Great Society. **International:** détente with the USSR and China (1972) reduced the domino logic. **Judgement:** weigh military stalemate (the underlying cause) against domestic opinion (which made continuing impossible); strong answers explain how Tet linked the two. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **King's importance:** leadership of SCLC; non-violent strategy designed to provoke federal action; Birmingham (1963) and the Letter from Birmingham Jail; the March on Washington speech; Selma (1965) leading to the Voting Rights Act; national and international profile (Nobel Peace Prize, 1964). **Other factors:** SNCC, CORE and local activists ran the Freedom Rides and Freedom Summer; federal action by Kennedy and Johnson; the Supreme Court; television coverage of Southern violence; the Chicago campaign (1966) shows King's limits outside the South; Black Power groups challenged his methods. **Judgement:** King was the movement's most effective national spokesman and strategist, but progress depended on grassroots organisations and on federal willingness to act. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **For:** the draft and casualties; teach-ins and marches; the 1968 Chicago convention; Kent State (1970); the war's impact on trust in government, deepened by the Pentagon Papers. **Other causes:** the slow pace of racial change and urban poverty (Watts, 1965; the riots of 1968); Black Power; feminism (Friedan, NOW, *Roe v. Wade*); gay rights after Stonewall; Native American activism (AIM, Wounded Knee); a youth counter-culture rooted in affluence and education; Watergate. **Judgement:** Vietnam intensified and linked many protests but did not cause most of them; strong answers distinguish anti-war protest from identity-based movements with older roots. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
