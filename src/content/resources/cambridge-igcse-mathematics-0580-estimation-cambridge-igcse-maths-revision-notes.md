These notes condense subtopic **1.9 Estimation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core and Extended candidates learn the same three statements, and estimation is examined on both the calculator and the non-calculator papers. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [estimation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-estimation-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [estimation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-estimation-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## The three statements

1. Round values to a specified degree of accuracy (decimal places and significant figures).
2. Make estimates for calculations involving numbers, quantities and measurements.
3. Round answers to a reasonable degree of accuracy in the context of a given problem.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Degree of accuracy | How precisely a value is given: nearest 10, 100, 1000, or a number of d.p. or s.f. |
| Decimal places (d.p.) | Digits counted after the decimal point |
| Significant figures (s.f.) | Digits counted from the first non-zero digit |
| Estimate | An approximate answer from rounded values, usually each to 1 s.f. |
| Overestimate | An estimate larger than the exact value |
| Underestimate | An estimate smaller than the exact value |
| Reasonable accuracy | An accuracy that suits the context and is no more precise than the data |

## Rounding with carries

A carry can change several digits. Always check that the accuracy asked for is still shown.

```
0.0396 to 2 s.f.    -> 0.040    (keep the final 0)
6.995  to 2 d.p.    -> 7.00
95 300 to 1 s.f.    -> 100 000
5764 to nearest 1000 -> 6000    (syllabus example)
```

## Estimating: the three-line layout

```
Line 1:  every number rounded to 1 s.f.
Line 2:  the rounded calculation, simplified step by step
Line 3:  the estimate
```

- A fraction bar acts as brackets: finish the numerator and the denominator first.
- Round the numbers **inside** a root or power, then evaluate.
- Remove decimals from a division by multiplying top and bottom by the same power of 10: 60 ÷ 0.3 = 600 ÷ 3 = 200.
- Keep the rounded values even if the arithmetic is awkward. 50 ÷ 3 = 16.7 is a valid estimate.

**Fast facts for the non-calculator papers**

| Operation | Same as |
|---|---|
| × 0.05 | ÷ 20 |
| ÷ 0.05 | × 20 |
| × 0.2 | ÷ 5 |
| ÷ 0.04 | × 25 |
| ÷ 0.3 | × 10, then ÷ 3 |

## Over or under?

| Calculation | Estimate is larger if you... |
|---|---|
| a × b or a + b | round a or b up |
| a ÷ b or a − b | round a up, or round b down |

- All roundings push the same way: you can state "overestimate" or "underestimate".
- Roundings push opposite ways: you cannot tell without the exact value.
- Example: 52.3 ÷ 2.86 ≈ 50 ÷ 3. The numerator went down and the denominator went up, so both make the estimate **smaller**: an underestimate.

## Estimating quantities

- Round each measurement to 1 s.f., then calculate.
- Interpret the answer: tins, tiles, coaches and people are whole numbers.
- "How many are needed?" means round up. "How many can be filled or bought?" means round down.
- Check the units: an area estimate is in square units, a volume estimate in cubic units.

## Reasonable accuracy: decision order

1. Follow any instruction in the question ("to the nearest metre", "to 2 decimal places").
2. Otherwise, on Papers 3 and 4, give non-exact answers to 3 s.f. (angles in degrees to 1 d.p.).
3. Money: to the nearest cent, written with two decimal places, such as $7.30.
4. Do not give more accuracy than the data supports: a mean of lengths measured to the nearest centimetre is given to the nearest centimetre.
5. Exact answers stay exact: 2.5 is not written as 2.50.

## Exam traps

- Rounding 0.765 to 0.7 instead of 0.8 because only the first digit was looked at.
- Writing 9.79 as 9.8 when 1 s.f. (10) was required.
- Missing out line 1 of the layout, so no method mark can be given.
- Working out the exact answer on a calculator paper and calling it the estimate.
- Calling an estimate an overestimate when the roundings pulled in opposite directions.
- Giving an answer such as 1.683 333 m from data measured to the nearest centimetre.
- Leaving a context answer as a decimal: 9.14 tins.

## Self-test

1. Round 0.007 083 to 2 significant figures.
2. Round 29.96 to 1 decimal place.
3. Write 47 512 correct to the nearest thousand.
4. By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate (8.93 × 31.2) ÷ 0.48.
5. By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate 5.08³.
6. By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate (612 + 389) ÷ 0.192.
7. By writing each number correct to 1 significant figure, estimate 7.92 ÷ 0.0386.
8. Estimate 2.86 × 6.71. Is your estimate larger or smaller than the exact value? Explain.
9. Estimate 52.3 ÷ 2.86 to 1 s.f. values. Is it an overestimate or an underestimate? Explain.
10. A machine fills cans holding 0.33 litres from a tank of 500 litres. How many full cans can it fill?
11. Taking π as 3 and rounding the radius to 1 significant figure, estimate the area of a circle of radius 4.9 cm.

### Answers

1. **0.0071**
2. **30.0** (the 9s carry; the 0 after the point shows 1 d.p.)
3. **48 000**
4. (9 × 30) ÷ 0.5 = 270 ÷ 0.5 = **540** (exact 580.45)
5. 5³ = **125** (exact 131.1 to 4 s.f.)
6. (600 + 400) ÷ 0.2 = 1000 ÷ 0.2 = **5000** (exact 5214 to 4 s.f.)
7. 8 ÷ 0.04 = 800 ÷ 4 = **200** (exact 205.2 to 4 s.f.)
8. 3 × 7 = **21**. Both numbers were rounded up, so the estimate is **larger** (exact 19.19 to 4 s.f.).
9. 50 ÷ 3 = **16.7** (or 17). The numerator was rounded down and the denominator up, and both make the result smaller, so it is an **underestimate** (exact 18.3 to 3 s.f.).
10. 500 ÷ 0.33 = 1515.15..., so **1515 full cans** (round down).
11. 3 × 5² = **75 cm²**
