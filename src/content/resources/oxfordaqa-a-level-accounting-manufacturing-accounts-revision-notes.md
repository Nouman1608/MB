## The checklist from the specification

Section 3.2.7 asks you to **prepare and comment on** the financial statements of manufacturers:

- the manufacturing account;
- the income statement;
- the statement of financial position.

Preparing them involves calculating and recording:

1. prime costs;
2. overhead cost;
3. factory cost of finished goods;
4. profit on manufacture;
5. provisions for unrealised profit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct costs | Costs traced to each unit made: direct materials, direct labour, direct expenses |
| Prime cost | Total of the direct costs |
| Factory overheads | Indirect production costs: supervision, factory power, factory rent, machine depreciation |
| Work in progress | Partly finished goods at the year end |
| Factory cost of finished goods | Prime cost + overheads + opening WIP − closing WIP |
| Transfer price | Value at which completed goods leave the factory: cost plus a mark-up, or market value |
| Profit on manufacture | Transfer price − factory cost of finished goods |
| Provision for unrealised profit (PUP) | Profit on manufacture still held in unsold finished goods |

## Formulas

| What you need | Formula |
|---|---|
| Direct materials consumed | Opening raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards − returns outwards − closing raw materials |
| Prime cost | Direct materials consumed + direct labour + direct expenses |
| Factory cost of finished goods | Prime cost + factory overheads + opening WIP − closing WIP |
| Profit on manufacture (mark-up m%) | Factory cost of finished goods × m/100 |
| PUP (mark-up m% on cost) | Finished goods at transfer price × m/(100 + m) |
| PUP (margin p% of transfer price) | Finished goods at transfer price × p/100 |
| Change in PUP to income statement | Closing PUP − opening PUP |
| Finished goods in statement of financial position | Transfer price − closing PUP |

Converting: a mark-up of m% equals a margin of m/(100 + m). A 30% mark-up is a margin of 30/130,
about 23.1%. A 20% margin is a mark-up of 20/80 = 25%.

## Method in steps: the manufacturing account

1. Direct materials consumed (raw materials only; carriage inwards in, returns outwards out).
2. Add direct labour and direct expenses: **prime cost**.
3. List factory overheads, using only the factory share of shared costs: **overhead cost**.
4. Add opening WIP, deduct closing WIP: **factory cost of finished goods**.
5. Add profit on manufacture to reach the **transfer to the income statement**.

Skeleton to copy in the exam room:

```
Manufacturing account for the year ended ...
Inventory of raw materials (opening)                    x
Purchases of raw materials + carriage inwards
  − returns outwards                                    x
Less inventory of raw materials (closing)              (x)
Direct materials consumed                               x
Direct labour + direct expenses                         x
PRIME COST                                              x
Factory overheads (listed)                              x
Add opening work in progress                            x
Less closing work in progress                          (x)
FACTORY COST OF FINISHED GOODS                          x
Profit on manufacture                                   x
Transfer to income statement                            x
```

## Method in steps: the income statement

1. Revenue.
2. Cost of sales: opening finished goods + transfer from manufacturing account + any purchases of
   finished goods − closing finished goods (both inventories at the values given, usually transfer price).
3. Gross profit.
4. Add profit on manufacture; deduct an increase in PUP, or add a decrease.
5. Deduct office, selling and finance expenses (including the office share of shared costs).
6. Sole trader: profit for the year. Limited company: profit from operations, then profit for the year
   before and after tax.

## Method in steps: the statement of financial position

Under current assets list inventories of raw materials, work in progress, and finished goods **less**
the closing PUP. The provision is never a liability.

## Recording the provision

The provision for unrealised profit has its own ledger account, with a credit balance.

- Increase: debit income statement, credit provision.
- Decrease: debit provision, credit income statement.

## Small worked reminders

These use **Quillby Glassware**, which transfers at factory cost plus 30%.

*Prime cost.* Raw materials: opening 7,250, purchases 63,900, carriage inwards 1,350, returns outwards
900, closing 8,100. Consumed = 7,250 + 63,900 + 1,350 − 900 − 8,100 = **63,500**. With direct wages
48,700 and direct expenses 2,300, prime cost = **114,500**.

*Factory cost.* Prime cost and overheads total 171,000; opening WIP 6,400, closing WIP 5,100. Factory
cost of finished goods = 171,000 + 6,400 − 5,100 = **172,300**.

*Profit on manufacture.* 30% × 172,300 = **51,690**, so the transfer is **223,990**.

*Apportionment.* Rent of 27,200 split 5 : 3 between factory and office puts **17,000** in the
manufacturing account and 10,200 in the income statement.

*Provision over two years.* Closing finished goods at transfer price are 24,700 in year 1 and 21,320
in year 2. The provision is 5,700 then 4,920. In year 2 the provision account is debited with 780 and
the income statement credited, so profit on manufacture is shown **plus** 780.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Direct v indirect labour.** Operators who make the product are direct; supervisors, cleaners and
  maintenance staff are factory overheads.
- **Carriage inwards v carriage outwards.** Inwards on raw materials goes in prime cost; outwards is a
  distribution expense.
- **Factory v office share.** Only the factory share of a shared cost is a production cost.
- **Mark-up v margin.** A mark-up is a percentage of cost; a margin is a percentage of the transfer price.
- **Provision v change in provision.** The full provision reduces finished goods in the statement of
  financial position; only the change affects profit.
- **Profit on manufacture v profit for the year.** Profit on manufacture moves profit between factory
  and selling; it does not create profit on unsold goods, because the PUP removes it.

## Commenting: points to reach for

- Factory cost per unit against the price an outside supplier would charge.
- Whether the mark-up flatters the factory compared with market value.
- Which costs would stop if production ended (make-or-buy), and which would continue.
- Non-financial factors: quality control, reliability of supply, staff, flexibility.
- Movements in raw materials, WIP and finished goods inventories and what they suggest about cash and
  sales.
- Finish with a decision that answers the question set.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three kinds of inventory a manufacturer holds.
2. Is a royalty paid on each unit made a prime cost or an overhead?
3. Raw materials: opening 5,480, purchases 41,250, carriage inwards 870, returns outwards 600, closing
   6,000. Find direct materials consumed.
4. Prime cost and overheads total 96,300, opening WIP 3,950, closing WIP 4,250. Find the factory cost of
   finished goods.
5. Goods costing 96,000 are transferred at cost plus 30%. Find the profit on manufacture.
6. Closing finished goods at transfer price are 18,850 (mark-up 30%). Find the PUP.
7. Show finished goods in the statement of financial position for question 6.
8. A year later finished goods at transfer price are 16,120 (mark-up still 30%). Find the change in PUP
   and say how it is treated.
9. Goods are transferred at a price giving a margin of one fifth. Closing finished goods at transfer
   price are 22,800. Find the PUP.
10. Which two accounting concepts require a provision for unrealised profit?
11. Where does depreciation of delivery vans appear?
12. Insurance of 14,400 is shared 7 : 2 between factory and office. How much goes in the manufacturing account?

### Answers

1. Raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
2. Prime cost: it is a direct expense, traced to each unit made.
3. 5,480 + 41,250 + 870 − 600 − 6,000 = **41,000**.
4. 96,300 + 3,950 − 4,250 = **96,000**.
5. 96,000 × 30% = **28,800**.
6. 18,850 × 30/130 = **4,350**.
7. 18,850 − 4,350 = **14,500** (cost).
8. 16,120 × 30/130 = 3,720; the provision falls by **630**, which is added to profit on manufacture.
9. 22,800 × 1/5 = **4,560**.
10. Realisation and prudence.
11. Income statement, as a selling and distribution expense.
12. 14,400 × 7/9 = **11,200**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking 30% of the transfer price as the PUP when the 30% is a mark-up on cost.
- Charging the full closing provision to profit instead of the movement.
- Adding a decrease in the provision the wrong way, so profit falls.
- Leaving finished goods at transfer price in the statement of financial position.
- Placing the WIP adjustment above prime cost.
- Applying the mark-up to total factory cost before the WIP adjustment.
- Mislabelling totals: write "prime cost" and "factory cost of finished goods" in full.
- Comments that never use a cost per unit or a market price, or never reach a decision.
