These notes condense subtopic **4.1 Geometrical terms** from Topic 4 of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Both tiers must use and interpret the listed terms and the vocabulary of triangles, special quadrilaterals, polygons, nets, solids and circles. Terms only in the Extended list are marked *(Extended)*. You are not expected to show that two shapes are congruent. For explanations and worked examples, read the [geometrical terms study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-terms-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [geometrical terms practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-geometrical-terms-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Points, lines and angles

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point | A position, usually labelled with a capital letter |
| Vertex (plural vertices) | A corner, where lines or edges meet |
| Line | Straight; a line segment has two end points |
| *(Extended)* Plane | A flat surface extending in all directions |
| Parallel | Always the same distance apart; never meet |
| Perpendicular | At right angles (90°) |
| *(Extended)* Perpendicular bisector | Line through the midpoint of a segment at 90° to it |
| Bearing | Angle measured clockwise from north, written with three figures |
| Interior angle | Inside a polygon at a vertex |
| Exterior angle | Between a side and the extension of the next side; interior + exterior = 180° |

```
acute    0 < angle < 90
right    angle = 90
obtuse   90 < angle < 180
reflex   180 < angle < 360
reflex angle = 360 - the angle on the other side
```

Angle ABC: the middle letter, B, is the vertex.

## Triangles

| Name | Sides | Angles |
|---|---|---|
| Equilateral | 3 equal | all 60° |
| Isosceles | 2 equal | 2 equal (opposite the equal sides) |
| Scalene | none equal | none equal |
| Right-angled | any | one 90° |

A right-angled triangle can also be isosceles (45°, 45°, 90°) or scalene.

## Special quadrilaterals

| Name | Defining features |
|---|---|
| Square | 4 equal sides, 4 right angles |
| Rectangle | 4 right angles, opposite sides equal |
| Rhombus | 4 equal sides, opposite sides parallel |
| Parallelogram | 2 pairs of parallel sides, opposite sides equal |
| Trapezium | 1 pair of parallel sides |
| Kite | 2 pairs of equal adjacent sides |

Family: every square is a rectangle and a rhombus; every rectangle and rhombus is a parallelogram. Always give the **most precise** name.

## Polygons

```
pentagon 5   hexagon 6   octagon 8   decagon 10
regular   = all sides equal AND all angles equal
irregular = anything else
```

## Solids

| Solid | Key feature |
|---|---|
| Cube | 6 square faces |
| Cuboid | 6 rectangular faces |
| Prism | same cross-section throughout |
| Cylinder | circular cross-section; 2 flat faces, 1 curved surface |
| Pyramid | polygon base, triangular faces meeting at one vertex |
| Cone | circular base, curved surface meeting at one vertex |
| Sphere | every point the same distance from the centre |
| *(Extended)* Hemisphere | half a sphere |
| *(Extended)* Frustum | cone or pyramid with the top cut off parallel to the base |

Face = flat surface. Edge = where two faces meet. Surface = the outside, flat or curved. A **net** is a flat pattern that folds into a solid.

Counting for prisms with an n-sided cross-section: n + 2 faces, 3n edges, 2n vertices. For pyramids with an n-sided base: n + 1 faces, 2n edges, n + 1 vertices.

## Circles

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Centre | The middle point |
| Radius (plural radii) | Centre to circumference |
| Diameter | Chord through the centre; 2 × radius |
| Circumference | Distance round the circle |
| Semicircle | Half a circle |
| Chord | Line joining two points on the circle |
| Tangent | Line touching the circle at one point |
| Arc | Part of the circumference; *(Extended)* minor (shorter) and major (longer) |
| Sector | Region bounded by two radii and an arc |
| Segment | Region bounded by a chord and an arc |

## Similar, congruent, scale factor

- **Congruent**: identical shape and size.
- **Similar**: same shape; lengths in the same ratio; angles equal.
- **Scale factor** = new length ÷ original length; greater than 1 enlarges, less than 1 reduces.

## Exam traps

- Calling an angle over 180° obtuse.
- Calling a rhombus regular (its angles need not be equal).
- Writing "rectangle" for a square when asked for the precise name.
- Swapping sector and segment.
- Calling a cylinder a prism or a cone a pyramid when the exact name is required.
- Using the wrong letter as the vertex in angle ABC.
- Writing a bearing as two figures.

## Self-test

1. Classify an angle of 179°.
2. Find the reflex angle that goes with 65°.
3. An exterior angle of a polygon is 72°. Find the interior angle at that vertex.
4. Name a triangle with angles 50°, 50° and 80°.
5. Name the quadrilateral with four equal sides and no right angles.
6. Is a rectangle with sides 3 cm and 5 cm a regular polygon? Why?
7. How many faces, edges and vertices does an octagonal prism have?
8. How many edges does a hexagonal pyramid have?
9. Name the region between a chord and the minor arc.
10. Triangle P has sides 5, 8, 9 cm; triangle Q has sides 7.5, 12, 13.5 cm. Are they similar? If so, give the scale factor from P to Q.
11. What is the name of the longest chord in a circle?
12. *(Extended)* Name the solid made by cutting the top off a cone parallel to its base.

### Answers

1. **Obtuse**
2. 360 – 65 = **295°**
3. 180 – 72 = **108°**
4. **Isosceles** (two equal angles)
5. **Rhombus**
6. **No**: its angles are equal but its sides are not
7. **10 faces, 24 edges, 16 vertices**
8. **12 edges** (6 base + 6 sloping)
9. **Minor segment** (a segment)
10. 7.5/5 = 12/8 = 13.5/9 = 1.5: **similar, scale factor 1.5**
11. **Diameter**
12. **Frustum**
