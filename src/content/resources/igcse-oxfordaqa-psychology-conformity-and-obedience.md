This guide covers the conformity and obedience content of **3.2.2
Social Influence**, from OxfordAQA International GCSE Psychology
(9218), Version 1.1, for International GCSE exams May/June 2025
onwards. Section 3.2.2 is a single, flat block of content in the
specification rather than a set of numbered sub-headings.

## Scope of this guide

3.2.2 Social Influence covers four strands: conformity to majority
influence, obedience, bystander intervention, and prosocial/antisocial
behaviour in crowds. This resource focuses on the first two strands —
conformity and obedience — since each carries enough named research and
theory to need proper depth. Bystander intervention (Piliavin, Latane
and Darley, Levine) and collective crowd behaviour are left for a
separate resource.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE PSYCHOLOGY (9218) — 3.2.2 SOCIAL INFLUENCE (CONFORMITY AND OBEDIENCE)**

- Identification and explanation of how social factors (group size,
anonymity and task difficulty) and dispositional factors (personality,
expertise) affect conformity to majority influence
- Conformity as investigated by Asch
- Obedience as investigated by Milgram
- Milgram's Agency theory of social factors affecting obedience:
agency, authority, culture and proximity
- Explanation of dispositional factors affecting obedience
- Adorno's theory of the Authoritarian Personality

## How to approach it

Keep conformity and obedience conceptually distinct even though they
are often taught together: conformity is changing behaviour to match a
group's norms, typically without direct instruction, while obedience is
changing behaviour in direct response to an order from a figure of
authority. Asch's research is the reference study for conformity;
Milgram's is the reference study for obedience — be able to describe
the basic procedure of each and at least one key finding, since
questions frequently ask for a named study rather than a general
description of the phenomenon.

For obedience, keep the social and dispositional explanations clearly
separated. Agency theory (social/situational) explains obedience
through the agentic state — the idea that a person passes moral
responsibility to the authority figure they believe is directing them —
alongside factors like proximity to the victim and legitimacy of the
authority. Adorno's Authoritarian Personality theory (dispositional)
instead explains obedience through an individual's personality traits,
often linked to a strict upbringing. A common exam question asks
candidates to compare a situational and a dispositional explanation of
the same behaviour, so preparing this contrast explicitly pays off.

Task difficulty and anonymity are the other two social factors named
in the specification alongside group size. Task difficulty affects
conformity because ambiguous or difficult tasks make individuals less
confident in their own judgement, increasing reliance on the group's
answer; anonymity tends to reduce conformity, because being identifiable
increases the social pressure to publicly agree with the majority even
when privately disagreeing. Keep these three social factors — group
size, task difficulty and anonymity — ready as a set, since a question
may ask for any one of them specifically.

## Worked example: social vs dispositional factors in conformity

A question asks candidates to explain how one social factor and one
dispositional factor affect conformity to majority influence.

```
Social factor:         group size — conformity tends to increase as the
                        size of the majority group increases, up to a
                        point, because a larger unanimous group creates
                        stronger normative pressure to agree
Dispositional factor:  personality — individuals with lower
                        self-esteem or a higher need for social
                        approval are more likely to conform, because
                        they are more sensitive to the social
                        consequences of disagreeing with the majority
```

Answering with one clearly labelled social factor and one clearly
labelled dispositional factor, each with a brief explanation of the
mechanism, is exactly the structure this content is assessed against.

## Common mistakes

Treating conformity and obedience as the same phenomenon rather than
distinguishing normative group pressure from direct authority. Naming
Asch or Milgram without being able to describe what their research
actually involved. Confusing Agency theory (a social/situational
explanation) with the Authoritarian Personality (a dispositional
explanation) when asked to compare explanation types. Listing factors
affecting conformity or obedience without explaining the mechanism by
which each factor has its effect.

## Key terms to define precisely

**Conformity** — a change in behaviour or belief as a result of real or
imagined group pressure, without a direct order to do so. **Obedience**
— a change in behaviour in response to a direct command from a
perceived authority figure. **Normative social influence** — conforming
in order to be liked or accepted by a group, rather than because the
group is believed to be correct. **Agentic state** — the psychological
state, central to Milgram's Agency theory, in which a person sees
themselves as an instrument carrying out another person's wishes and so
no longer feels personally responsible for their actions. **Legitimate
authority** — an authority figure whose right to give instructions is
accepted because of their recognised social position, such as a
scientist, teacher or police officer, which increases obedience compared
to an authority figure without recognised legitimacy.

Being able to give a precise, one-sentence definition of each of these
terms — rather than a vague paraphrase — is often what separates a
mid-band answer from a top-band one, since exam mark schemes frequently
reward accurate use of specification-specific vocabulary over general
description.

## Quick revision checklist

- Learn the basic procedure and one key finding of Asch's conformity
research and Milgram's obedience research.
- Separate social/situational factors from dispositional/personality
factors for both conformity and obedience.
- Know the four elements of Milgram's Agency theory: agency, authority,
culture and proximity.
- Be able to summarise Adorno's Authoritarian Personality theory as a
dispositional explanation of obedience.
- Practise answers that name one social and one dispositional factor
side by side, as this comparison structure is commonly tested.
