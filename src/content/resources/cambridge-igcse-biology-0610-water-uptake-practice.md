These questions cover sub-topic **8.2 Water uptake** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All of 8.2 is Core, so every question suits both Core and Extended candidates. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Measurements are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [water uptake study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-water-uptake/) and the [water uptake revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-water-uptake-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the functions of root hair cells. **[2]**

**2.** Which is the correct pathway of water through a plant?

A: root cortex cells → root hair cells → xylem → mesophyll cells

B: root hair cells → xylem → root cortex cells → mesophyll cells

C: root hair cells → root cortex cells → xylem → mesophyll cells

D: mesophyll cells → xylem → root cortex cells → root hair cells **[1]**

**3.** Name the process by which water moves from the soil into a root hair cell. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** features you would use to identify a root hair cell in a micrograph of a root. **[2]**

**5.** State how the large surface area of root hairs affects the plant. **[1]**

**6.** Suggest why a root hair cell does not contain chloroplasts. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student measures the surface area of a 1 cm length of root before and after its root hairs are removed.

| Root | Surface area / mm² |
|---|---|
| With root hairs | 42.0 |
| Root hairs removed | 3.5 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the surface area is with root hairs.

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in surface area when the root hairs are removed.

**(c)** Explain how the root hairs help the plant. **[6]**

**8.** Outline the pathway taken by water from the soil to a palisade cell in a leaf. Name each type of cell or tissue the water passes through. **[4]**

**9.** A student wants to show the pathway of water through the above-ground parts of a plant.

**(a)** Describe a method she could use. Include the materials, the conditions and how she would observe the results.

**(b)** State the result she would expect when she looks at a cross-section of the stem.

**(c)** State **one** safety precaution. **[7]**

**10.** Two identical celery stalks were placed in the same dye solution for 2 hours. Stalk X was in a warm room with a fan blowing air over it. Stalk Y was in a cool room with still air. Each stalk was then cut into 1 cm slices from the base upwards to find the highest slice containing dye.

| Stalk | Height of highest slice with dye / cm |
|---|---|
| X | 14 |
| Y | 5 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of movement of the dye up stalk X, in cm per hour.

**(b)** Suggest why the dye travelled further in stalk X.

**(c)** State **two** variables that were kept the same. **[5]**

**11.** A gardener moves young lettuce plants from a seed tray into her garden. She pulls some out of the tray by their leaves, and lifts others with a block of soil around their roots. A day later, the pulled plants have wilted.

**(a)** Suggest why the pulled plants wilted.

**(b)** Explain why lifting the plants with soil prevented wilting. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why a plant needs to take up both water and mineral ions through its root hair cells. Refer to photosynthesis and to one named mineral ion. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Uptake of water [1]; uptake of mineral ions [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "Absorbs nutrients" does not score; name water and mineral ions.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Osmosis [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Any two from: long, thin extension growing out from the cell; in the outer layer of the root; large vacuole; no chloroplasts [2]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** It increases the uptake of water and mineral ions [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**6.** Root hairs are underground, where there is no light for photosynthesis [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**7. (a)** 42.0 ÷ 3.5 [1] = **12 times** [1].

**(b)** (42.0 − 3.5) ÷ 42.0 × 100 [1] = 38.5 ÷ 42.0 × 100 = **91.7%** [1].

**(c)** Root hairs give a large surface area in contact with soil water [1], so more water and mineral ions are taken up at the same time [1]. Total: 6 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the decrease is divided by the **original** value (42.0); dividing by 3.5 gives 1100%, which is the percentage increase, not the decrease.

**8.** Water enters root hair cells from the soil [1], passes through the root cortex cells [1], enters the xylem and is carried up the stem [1], and moves from the xylem in the leaf into the mesophyll cells, including the palisade cell [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**9. (a)** Stand a freshly cut leafy stalk of celery, or a white-flowered shoot, in water containing a dye [1]. Leave it for a few hours in warm, bright conditions [1]. Cut thin cross-sections of the stalk with a scalpel [1] and examine them with a hand lens or microscope [1].

**(b)** Coloured spots in a ring near the edge of the stem, showing the xylem [2]; "the stem is coloured" alone [1 only].

**(c)** Cut away from the fingers on a tile / wear gloves because the dye stains [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**10. (a)** 14 ÷ 2 = **7 cm per hour** [1].

**(b)** Higher temperature and moving air increased the rate of transpiration from stalk X [1], so water containing dye was drawn up the xylem faster [1].

**(c)** Any two from: dye concentration; time; type or length of stalk; number of leaves [2]. Total: 5 marks.

**11. (a)** Pulling the plants tore off many root hairs [1], so the surface area for water uptake was reduced and less water was taken up [1].

**(b)** The soil protected the root hairs, so they were not damaged [1], and the plants could keep taking up water as fast as they lost it [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**12.** Water is a raw material for photosynthesis [1]; it travels from root hair cells to the mesophyll cells in the xylem [1]. Mineral ions such as nitrate ions are needed to make amino acids for growth, or magnesium ions to make chlorophyll [1]; they also enter through the root hair cells [1]. Total: 4 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "nutrients" instead of water and mineral ions.
- Leaving out the root cortex or the mesophyll cells from the pathway.
- Describing the stain result without naming the xylem.
- Dividing by the wrong value in percentage-change questions.
- Giving a variable such as "same conditions" instead of a named, measurable factor.
- Saying root hairs are separate cells from the root hair cell.
