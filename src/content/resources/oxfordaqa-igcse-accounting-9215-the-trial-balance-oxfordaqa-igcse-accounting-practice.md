These questions cover "Prepare and understand the use of a trial balance" from section 3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Accounting (9215) specification, version 1.2: preparing a trial balance from a list of balances, correcting a trial balance, and its use and limitations. All amounts are in dollars. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [trial balance study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting/) and the [trial balance revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-9215-the-trial-balance-oxfordaqa-igcse-accounting-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State whether each balance belongs in the debit or the credit column of a trial balance: carriage outwards, discount received, accumulated depreciation, loan interest. **[2]**

**2.** A supplier's account has debit entries of 1,800 (bank) and 240 (purchases returns), and credit entries of 950 (balance b/d) and 2,600 (purchases).

**(a)** Calculate the balance to be carried down. **[1]**

**(b)** State whether the balance brought down is a debit or a credit, and what it represents. **[1]**

**3.** Which one of these statements about a trial balance is correct?

A: If it agrees, the ledger contains no errors.

B: It shows which account contains an error.

C: It checks that total debit balances equal total credit balances.

D: It includes the closing inventory counted at the year end.

**[1]**

**4.** A trial balance has total debits of 96,250. The credit balances, excluding capital, total 71,880. The owner's capital is missing. Calculate the capital. **[2]**

**5.** Carriage inwards of 450 was entered in the credit column of a trial balance. All other balances were correct.

**(a)** State which column total will be higher. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference between the totals. **[1]**

**6.** State two uses of a trial balance. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** The balances of Ayodele Print Shop at 30 June are: machinery 28,000; sales 72,300; office furniture 6,500; purchases 31,200; inventory at 1 July of the previous year 3,900; trade receivables 5,640; bank 2,180 (in funds); sales returns 760; purchases returns 640; carriage inwards 420; carriage outwards 590; discount allowed 380; discount received 470; wages 15,600; rent paid 7,200; rent received 2,400; electricity 1,940; irrecoverable debts 260; trade payables 4,830; accumulated depreciation of machinery 8,400; loan 10,000; drawings 8,400; capital 13,930.

Prepare the trial balance of Ayodele Print Shop at 30 June. **[8]**

**8.** Prepare the following accounts for May, balance them at 31 May and bring the balances down.

**(a)** The account of the customer T Ndlovu: balance at 1 May 1,200 owed by T Ndlovu; 9 May credit sales 2,450; 14 May sales returns 300; 20 May received by bank 1,900, with discount allowed of 50. **[3]**

**(b)** State the column in which T Ndlovu's balance appears in the trial balance at 31 May. **[1]**

**9.** A trainee has prepared this trial balance for Brennan Sports at 31 December.

```
                          Debit     Credit
Equipment                20,000
Inventory at 1 January    3,500
Trade receivables         4,200
Purchases                38,000
Wages                    12,600
Rent paid                 6,000
Drawings                  7,000
Discount received           350
Bank overdraft            1,150
Sales                               68,900
Trade payables                       3,700
Capital                             18,300
Carriage outwards                      480
Sales returns                          620
Totals                   92,800     92,000
```

**(a)** Explain why the totals do not agree. **[2]**

**(b)** Redraft the trial balance correctly. **[4]**

**10.** The balances of Kasprzak Garage include a bank overdraft of 3,700. The other balances are: premises 45,000; vehicle 12,000; inventory 4,300; trade receivables 3,800; purchases 26,500; wages 9,100; drawings 6,200; cash 700; sales 61,400; trade payables 2,900; loan 5,000. Capital is missing.

**(a)** Calculate the capital. **[2]**

**(b)** A student treats the overdraft as a debit balance. Calculate the capital the student would find. **[1]**

**11.** "Our trial balance agrees, so the accounts are correct." Explain why this statement may be wrong, using two named types of error with an example of each. **[4]**

**12.** A draft trial balance has debits that exceed credits by 1,040. The only error is one balance placed in the wrong column. The balances include rent received 520, wages 1,040, discount allowed 260 and carriage inwards 520. Identify the balance that was misplaced and explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Carriage outwards **debit** and discount received **credit** [1]; accumulated depreciation **credit** and loan interest **debit** [1]

**2. (a)** Credits 3,550 − debits 2,040 = **1,510** (carried down on the debit side) [1]

**(b)** **Credit balance b/d of 1,510**: the amount the business owes the supplier [1] **[2]**

**3.** **C** [1]

**4.** 96,250 − 71,880 [1] = **24,370** [1]

**5. (a)** The **credit** column [1]

**(b)** 2 × 450 = **900** [1] **[2]**

**6.** Any two [1] [1]: it checks that total debits equal total credits; it provides a list of balances for preparing the financial statements; a disagreement warns that errors exist before the financial statements are prepared

**7.** Heading "Trial balance of Ayodele Print Shop at 30 June" [1]

Debit column: machinery 28,000; office furniture 6,500; inventory 3,900; trade receivables 5,640; bank 2,180 [1]; purchases 31,200; sales returns 760; carriage inwards 420; carriage outwards 590 [1]; discount allowed 380; wages 15,600; rent paid 7,200 [1]; electricity 1,940; irrecoverable debts 260; drawings 8,400 [1]

Credit column: sales 72,300; purchases returns 640; discount received 470; rent received 2,400 [1]; trade payables 4,830; accumulated depreciation 8,400; loan 10,000; capital 13,930 [1]

Totals **112,970** on both sides [1]

*Examiner insight:* the carriage accounts are both debits, and rent paid and rent received go on opposite sides; these are the balances most often misplaced.

**8. (a)**

```
Dr                    T Ndlovu                          Cr
 1 May  Balance b/d   1,200  | 14 May  Sales returns       300
 9 May  Sales         2,450  | 20 May  Bank              1,900
                             | 20 May  Discount allowed     50
                             | 31 May  Balance c/d       1,400
                      3,650  |                           3,650
 1 Jun  Balance b/d   1,400  |
```

Entries on the correct sides [1]; balance c/d 1,400 and totals 3,650 [1]; balance b/d 1,400 on the debit side on 1 June [1]

**(b)** **Debit** column (T Ndlovu owes the business, so it is part of trade receivables) [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** Four balances are in the wrong column: discount received and bank overdraft (credit balances) are in the debit column, and carriage outwards and sales returns (debit balances) are in the credit column [1]; the net effect, 2 × (350 + 1,150 − 480 − 620) = 800, is the difference between the totals [1]

**(b)** Debit column: equipment, inventory, trade receivables, purchases, wages, rent paid, drawings as before [1]; carriage outwards 480 and sales returns 620 in the debit column [1]; discount received 350 and bank overdraft 1,150 in the credit column [1]; totals **92,400** on both sides [1] **[6]**

**10. (a)** Debits = 45,000 + 12,000 + 4,300 + 3,800 + 26,500 + 9,100 + 6,200 + 700 = 107,600; credits = 61,400 + 2,900 + 3,700 + 5,000 = 73,000 [1]; capital = **34,600** [1]

**(b)** Debits 111,300 − credits 69,300 = **42,000** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* misplacing the overdraft changes the capital by twice 3,700, which is why the overdraft is so often tested.

**11.** A trial balance only checks that total debits equal total credits [1]; for example, an **error of commission**, such as a receipt from L Shah credited to L Shaw's account, keeps both totals equal [1]; an **error of principle**, such as a new computer debited to office expenses, also keeps both totals equal [1]; so the accounts can contain errors even when the trial balance agrees [1]

**12.** **Rent received 520** [1]; it is a credit balance, and putting it in the debit column makes debits exceed credits by twice 520 = 1,040 [1]

*Examiner insight:* carriage inwards 520 in the wrong column would make the credits higher, not the debits, because it belongs in the debit column.

## Where marks are usually lost

- No heading or date on a trial balance.
- Carriage outwards, discount allowed or sales returns placed as credits.
- Bank overdraft or accumulated depreciation placed as debits.
- Adjusting the totals instead of redrafting every balance.
- Balance c/d placed on the larger side of an account.
