## Section B — Unseen passages

**4.** Read this **original** prose passage.

> The bus was late, and then it was later, and then the timetable on the shelter wall stopped meaning
> anything at all. Grace counted the cars. She counted the pigeons. She counted the coins in her
> pocket, which did not take long. When the bus finally came round the corner, lit up like a
> birthday, she found she did not want to get on.

**(a)** Explain how the first sentence presents the passing of time. **(b)** Explain the effect of the
repeated "She counted". **[5]**

**5.** In the passage in Question 4, explain how the final sentence changes the reader's understanding of
Grace. **[4]**

**6.** Read this **original** poem.

> **Ferry**
>
> We cross at dusk. The engine's even drum
> is all the conversation that we make.
> Behind us, the town folds up its lights; ahead,
> the island we were born on, and won't take
>
> back. You hold the rail. I hold my breath.
> The gulls keep pace, then leave us to the dark.

Explain how the poet uses **form and line endings** to present the speakers' feelings about returning
home. **[6]**

**7.** In "Ferry", explain the effect of the **contrast** in "You hold the rail. I hold my breath." **[3]**

**8.** Read this **original** drama extract.

> *(A hospital corridor. Two plastic chairs. JOE sits. His sister MAYA enters with two coffees, gives
> him one. She does not sit.)*
>
> JOE: What did they say?
>
> MAYA: They said we can see her soon.
>
> JOE: That's not what I asked.
>
> *(MAYA looks at the closed door. A long pause.)*
>
> MAYA: Drink your coffee, Joe.

Explain how the dramatist uses **stage directions** to create tension in this extract. **[5]**

**9.** In the extract in Question 8, explain how the **dialogue** shows the difference between the two
characters. **[5]**

**10.** A candidate's essay on the extract in Question 8 says: "Their mother is probably dying of an
illness and Maya knows the doctors cannot save her." Explain the problem with this comment, and how the
candidate could make the same point more securely. **[3]**

**11.** Write a one-sentence **thesis** for an essay on the poem "Ferry" answering the question
"Discuss the presentation of homecoming in the poem." Then explain what makes your thesis effective.
**[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: the candidate answers **one question from a choice of two** [1]; the passages cover **two
of prose, poetry and drama** [1]; passages are from works **originally written in English**; **authors
and dates are not given**; knowledge of **background is not expected**. (Maximum 2.)

**2.** Authors and dates are **deliberately not given**, and background knowledge **is not expected**,
so guesses about period earn no credit [1]. The opening also delays the **analysis of language, form and
structure** that the question rewards [1].

**3.** The best choice depends on whether you can **answer the question's specific focus**, not just on
which passage you understand [1]; reading both lets you choose the passage that gives you an **argument**
to make [1].

**4. (a)** The repetition of "and then" **stretches the sentence**, so the delay is felt as it grows [1].
The escalation from "late" to "later" to the timetable meaning nothing presents time becoming
**meaningless** through waiting [1]. **(b)** The **anaphora** of "She counted" creates a mechanical
rhythm [1], showing Grace filling empty time with **trivial tasks** [1]; the list ends on the coins
"which did not take long", hinting at her **poverty** with dry understatement [1].

**5.** The passage builds an expectation that the bus is wanted, so her reluctance is a **reversal** [1].
The simile "lit up like a birthday" makes the bus festive [1], which **contrasts** with her refusal [1],
suggesting her waiting was really **avoidance** of where the bus would take her [1].

**6.** The **regular, steady metre** of the opening lines echoes the "even drum" of the engine, creating a
controlled surface over the feeling [1] [1]. The enjambment across the stanza break ("won't take // back")
**separates "back" from its verb**, so the rejection by the island is delayed and lands hard [1] [1]. The
**rhyme** of "make" and "take" binds the silence between the speakers to the fear of not being accepted
[1]. The final line ends with "the dark", leaving the arrival **unresolved** [1].

**7.** The parallel structure sets **two reactions side by side** [1]: one physical and steadying, one
anxious and suspended [1], so the speakers' **different ways of facing the return** are shown without
explanation [1].

**8.** Maya **"does not sit"**, so she stays standing while Joe sits, suggesting restlessness or
readiness to leave [1] [1]. Her look at **"the closed door"** directs the audience's attention to what
lies beyond it [1]. The **"long pause"** stages her reluctance to answer [1], so the audience senses bad
news before anything is said [1].

**9.** Joe's lines are **direct questions** ("What did they say?"), showing his need for the truth [1]
[1]. Maya **evades** with a partial answer [1] and then changes the subject ("Drink your coffee"),
protecting him or herself [1]. Her use of his name gives the line a **gentle, parental** tone, reversing
any expectation that the questioner controls the scene [1].

**10.** The comment **invents detail** the extract does not give: we do not know who is behind the door
or what is wrong [1] [1]. A more secure version analyses the evidence: Maya's evasion and the long pause
**suggest** she has been told something serious that she does not want to share [1].

**11.** A suitable thesis, for example: "The poem presents homecoming as a crossing into uncertainty,
using the steady rhythm of the ferry to hold back a fear of rejection that breaks through only at the
end." [1] [1] Effective because it **answers the question directly** [1] and names a **method** (rhythm,
structure) as well as a meaning [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Guessing at the author, period or context of the passage.
- Inventing events before or after the extract.
- Listing techniques without effects or argument.
- Treating a poem or drama extract as if it were prose narrative.
