Condensed recall notes for **Paper 2 Listening** of Cambridge IGCSE
Urdu as a Second Language (0539), 2025–2027 series. Use these alongside
the full study guide on Paper 2 for explanation of exam technique —
these notes are for fast recall of the exam format in the final stretch
of revision.

## Paper format at a glance

- Written paper, approximately **35–45 minutes**, **30 marks total**.
- Weighted **33%** of the overall qualification (Paper 1 Reading and
  Writing carries the remaining 67%).
- All questions and responses are in **Urdu**.
- Candidates attempt **all questions** — there is no choice of
  exercise.
- Answers are written directly into the spaces provided in the
  question paper booklet.
- **Dictionaries are not permitted** in the exam.
- Each spoken text is heard **twice**, with pauses built into the
  recording to give candidates time to read the questions and write
  answers.

## Exercise 1 — Short answer questions (8 marks)

- Candidates hear a series of **short spoken texts** — for example a
  travel announcement, a recorded phone message, or a brief dialogue.
- Candidates write **short answers** in response to eight separate
  questions.
- Tests assessment objectives **L1 and L2** (basic comprehension of
  explicit and implicit meaning).

## Exercise 2 — Gap-fill sentences (8 marks)

- Candidates hear a **longer spoken text** — a conversation, an
  interview, a monologue or a formal talk.
- Candidates complete gaps in **notes or sentences printed on the
  question paper** as they listen.
- Also tests **L1 and L2** — this exercise rewards catching specific
  factual detail rather than general gist.

## Exercise 3 — Multiple matching (6 marks)

- Candidates hear **six short extracts**, each from a different
  speaker.
- Each speaker must be **matched to the appropriate content** option.
- Tests **L3 and L4** — a step up from Exercises 1–2, since this
  requires distinguishing between similar speakers or viewpoints, not
  just extracting one fact.

## Exercise 4 — Multiple-choice questions (8 marks)

- Candidates hear a **discussion between two speakers**.
- Candidates answer **multiple-choice comprehension questions** on
  what was said.
- Tests **L1, L2, L3 and L4 together** — the most demanding exercise
  on the paper, since it draws on every listening skill assessed
  across the whole component.

## What the material sounds like

The syllabus specifies the kinds of spoken material used across the
four exercises: **dialogues, interviews, conversations, news items,
public announcements, and talks**. Exam preparation should include
exposure to all of these formats, not just conversational Urdu, since
Exercise 1 in particular draws on more formal registers such as
announcements and phone messages.

## Common pitfalls

- **Writing a full sentence when only a short answer is required** in
  Exercise 1 — this wastes time relative to the mark allocation and
  does not earn extra credit.
- **Guessing before the second playing** in Exercise 2's gap-fill —
  use the first playing purely to orient yourself to the structure of
  the text, and commit answers only once you have heard it a second
  time.
- **Losing track of which speaker said what** in Exercise 3's multiple
  matching, especially when two speakers express similar opinions —
  note one distinguishing detail per speaker, not just their general
  topic.
- **Second-guessing a first instinct** on Exercise 4's multiple-choice
  questions — because this exercise draws on all four listening
  objectives at once, the option that first seems correct after a
  careful first playing is usually right; changing answers based on a
  half-remembered detail from the second playing is a common source of
  lost marks.

## Practical exam-day reminders

- Centres must check the **Cambridge Handbook** for the specific
  examination series for details on accessing audio materials.
- No dictionaries at any point in the exam.
- Because each recording plays only **twice**, use the first playing to
  get overall gist and the second to fill in specific detail — do not
  try to write a complete answer on the first playing alone.

## Command word and instruction reminders

Because every question and response on this paper is in Urdu, read the
instruction line at the top of each exercise carefully before the
recording starts — it tells you the exact response format expected
(a single word, a short phrase, or a completed sentence), and losing
marks for writing in the wrong format is avoidable if this line is
read in the pause before playback begins. For gap-fill (Exercise 2)
specifically, write exactly what is heard rather than a paraphrase;
markers are checking for the precise word or phrase from the recording,
not an equivalent one.

## Quick recall checklist

- Can I name all four exercises in order and their mark values (8, 8,
  6, 8 = 30 total)?
- Do I know which assessment objectives (L1–L4) apply to which
  exercise, and that Exercise 4 draws on all of them?
- Can I list the six types of spoken material the syllabus names?
- Do I remember that dictionaries are banned and each recording plays
  exactly twice?

## How to approach it

Because Exercises 1 and 2 test the same objectives (L1, L2) but differ
in format — short isolated texts versus one longer text with gap-fill
— practise both formats separately rather than treating "short answer
practice" as covering gap-fill technique too. For Exercise 3's
multiple matching, the biggest single skill to drill is note-taking
speed: since six speakers are heard in quick succession, practise
jotting a one- or two-word cue for each speaker during the first
playing, then matching against the answer options on the second.
