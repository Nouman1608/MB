This page is a guide to OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) past papers from June 2023 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from OCR's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners reported for June 2023, June 2024 and June 2025, written by us from OCR's examiners' reports. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is available from OCR, and the official links are below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) qualification page, with the specification](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/chemistry-a-h032-h432-from-2015/)
- [OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) assessment page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/as-and-a-level/chemistry-a-h032-h432-from-2015/assessment/)
- [OCR past paper finder](https://www.ocr.org.uk/qualifications/past-paper-finder/)
- [OCR grade boundaries page](https://www.ocr.org.uk/administration/grade-boundaries/)

OCR publishes papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder, grade boundaries on its grade-boundaries page, and examiners' reports through its Teach Cambridge site for registered centres. Where a document is not public to everyone, ask your teacher or exams officer.

Use the paper, mark scheme and examiner report together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly against the mark scheme, which tells you what each mark needs. Then read the examiner report for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. The H432 course is assessed through three written papers at the end of the two-year course, and the three papers add up to 270 marks (H432/01 and H432/02 are 100 marks each, H432/03 is 70). The Practical Endorsement is reported separately as Pass or Not Classified and carries no marks towards the grade. The current specification is Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. The 2023 to 2025 examiners' reports describe the same three-paper structure, but the paper's front cover and the specification version in force that year are the sure guide, so check them before you rely on a question from an older paper.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports describe three papers that rewarded exact recall and tidy method on the first two papers and flexible application on the third. Paper 1 covered periodic trends, structure and bonding, equilibrium and Kp, acids and buffers, electrode potentials and transition-metal complexes. Paper 2 was organic: mechanisms, synthesis routes, analysis and a final structure-deduction question. Paper 3 was the synoptic paper, with mass spectrometry and NMR, titration and energy-change calculations, and unfamiliar reactions. Candidates who did well on Paper 1 explained melting-point trends from structure and bonding, described buffer action fully, linked a change in Kp to a shift in equilibrium and temperature, and defined a monodentate ligand in full. On Paper 2 the strongest scripts used skeletal formulae confidently. Across the series, the common weakness was unstructured calculations and unfamiliar contexts.

**Where marks were lost**

- H432/01: three-molecule shape questions where shapes or reasons were wrong; calculations not set out clearly or not given to the stated significant figures.
- H432/01: buffer questions, where candidates could not work out the mass needed or describe how a buffer acts; standard electrode potential diagrams that were inaccurate or incomplete.
- H432/01: electron configurations of transition-metal ions, where the atom's configuration was written instead; coordination number and number of ligands mixed up even when an empirical formula was right.
- H432/02: miscounted hydrogens or carbons in skeletal formulae, which carried through into wrong equations and wrong relative molecular masses; structures drawn in pencil and rubbed out so that the scan was hard to read.
- H432/02: functional groups not identified, and reagents or conditions for interconversions and identification tests not recalled; the final analysis question answered in part but not drawn together into a feasible structure.
- H432/03: unfamiliar atomic-structure and mass-spectrometry contexts (the number of electrons in a particle, the pattern of peaks for a dibromo compound); equal-height peaks assumed.
- H432/03: NMR splitting where protons in the same environment were assumed to split one another; equations for unfamiliar reactions not written from the information supplied; mole calculations scattered across the page with unlabelled numbers. The report says this last point was also raised in 2023 and 2024.

## June 2024

The June 2024 papers leaned on clear working and on applying known chemistry to unfamiliar compounds. Paper 1 included rate-determining steps and rate equations, enthalpy profiles, standard electrode potentials, polarity and two extended-response questions. Paper 2 ran across organic mechanisms, synthesis routes, balancing equations and practical organic techniques, finishing with a structure-deduction question. Paper 3 drew on pH calculations, bond enthalpies, complex ions, titration analysis and organic tests. Better answers set out calculations in steps, gave concise extended responses and wrote formulae for unfamiliar complex ions. Weaker answers were unstructured, used the wrong significant figures or units, and struggled to apply earlier learning to unfamiliar situations.

**Where marks were lost**

- H432/01: extended-response answers that lacked depth or used the wrong terminology; calculations laid out so that working could not be credited, answers not to the stated significant figures and missing unit conversions in a titration-style calculation.
- H432/01: balanced and ionic equations for Group 2 and Group 7 reactions; enthalpy profiles for exothermic or endothermic reactions drawn inaccurately; incomplete labelled diagram for measuring a standard electrode potential.
- H432/02: hydrogens miscounted in skeletal formulae and so molar masses wrong; poor connectivity in drawing alcohol (O–H) groups; reagents and conditions confused, sometimes with a contradictory reagent written alongside the right one.
- H432/02: the closing analysis question attempted in part but not completed into a feasible structure.
- H432/03: equations for unfamiliar reactions not written from the information supplied; weak knowledge of common formulae and oxidation numbers; titration-analysis calculation not laid out in a logical order.
- H432/03: a strong-base pH answer that came out acidic without the candidate noticing, and confusion over which type of pH calculation a question needed.

## June 2023

The June 2023 reports describe a series where method and communication separated grades more than recall did. Paper 1 covered ionisation-energy trends, equilibrium, buffers, Group 2 chemistry, electrode potentials and two extended-response questions. Paper 2 tested organic mechanisms, synthesis, calculations and explanations. Paper 3 was synoptic, with reactions of functional groups, enthalpy and titration calculations, Kc and formula work. Better answers set out calculations clearly, gave the right number of significant figures, converted units, and explained equilibrium changes and buffer behaviour. Weaker answers gave rambling or contradictory explanations and did not use the information in unfamiliar questions.

**Where marks were lost**

- H432/01: extended-response answers that lacked depth or contained inaccurate definitions; ionisation-energy trends described without clear, concise reasoning.
- H432/01: calculations with unclear working, wrong significant figures or missing unit conversions; balanced and ionic equations for Group 2 metals and their compounds.
- H432/02: multiple-choice answers left blank; skeletal formulae misread so that hydrogens were miscounted and molar masses were wrong, with extra or missing atoms in drawn structures.
- H432/02: long explanations that contradicted themselves; calculations with illogical steps and rounding of intermediate values.
- H432/03: unfamiliar-reaction equations not written from the information given; the whole of the question on identifying elements from a table, which showed weak basic knowledge of the periodic table.
- H432/03: titration and Kc calculations with numbers scattered and unlabelled.

## What to take from the series

Across the three years of reports the same habits keep appearing. Calculations need to be laid out in steps, with units and the stated number of significant figures. Skeletal formulae need hydrogens counted carefully, because a miscount carries into the molar mass and then the answer. Reagents and conditions for organic interconversions should be recalled exactly, without adding a contradictory alternative. Equations for unfamiliar reactions should be built from the information the question gives. Our revision notes cover several of these areas: [acids, bases and buffers](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-acids-bases-and-buffers-revision-notes/), [alcohols](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-alcohols-revision-notes/), [organic synthesis](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-6-2-5-organic-synthesis-revision-notes/) and [qualitative analysis](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-5-3-2-qualitative-analysis-revision-notes/).

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432) past papers?**
OCR publishes papers and mark schemes through its past paper finder, and grade boundaries on its grade-boundaries page. Examiners' reports are held on Teach Cambridge for registered centres.

**How many marks is H432 out of?**
The three written papers total 270 marks: H432/01 and H432/02 are 100 marks each and H432/03 is 70. The Practical Endorsement is reported separately and carries no marks towards the grade.

**Which series does this page cover?**
The June 2023, June 2024, June 2025 and June 2026 series. Examiner notes cover the first three, and June 2026 has thresholds only.

**Which specification were older H432 papers set on?**
The current specification is Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017. Check the front cover of any older paper and the specification version in force before relying on a question.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
