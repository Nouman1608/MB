## Questions

**1.** State two differences between sexual and asexual reproduction. **[2]**

**2.** Describe what happens from fertilisation in a human to the formation of an embryo. **[3]**

**3.** The table describes two flowers, P and Q.

| Feature | Flower P | Flower Q |
|---|---|---|
| Petals | Large and red | None |
| Nectar | Present | Absent |
| Anthers | Inside the flower | Hanging outside on long filaments |
| Stigma | Small and sticky, inside the flower | Feathery, outside the flower |

**(a)** Identify the wind-pollinated flower. Give two pieces of evidence from the table. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why flower Q produces far more pollen than flower P. **[2]**

**4.** A pollen grain lands on the stigma of a flower of the same species.

**(a)** Describe how the male nucleus reaches and fertilises the female nucleus. **[3]**
**(b)** State what each of these structures develops into after fertilisation: the ovule; the ovary. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator allowed) A student put 50 mung bean seeds on damp cotton wool in each of four dishes and kept each dish at a different temperature. After four days she counted the seeds that had germinated.

| Temperature (°C) | 5 | 15 | 25 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Seeds germinated | 0 | 21 | 46 | 43 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage germination at 15 °C and at 25 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Give two variables the student should have kept the same. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why no seeds germinated at 5 °C. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest how the student could make her results more reliable. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) The dry mass of 10 germinating maize seedlings was measured every two days.

| Day | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dry mass (g) | 2.40 | 2.25 | 2.05 | 1.92 | 2.10 | 2.46 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in dry mass between day 0 and day 6. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the dry mass decreased between day 0 and day 6. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the dry mass increased after day 6. **[2]**

**7.** (calculator allowed) A gardener took 80 cuttings from one geranium plant. She dipped 40 in rooting powder and left 40 untreated. After three weeks, 34 treated and 18 untreated cuttings had grown roots.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of cuttings that grew roots in each group. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between the two groups. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the gardener removed the lower leaves from each cutting. **[1]**
**(d)** Strawberry plants reproduce by runners. Give one similarity and one difference between reproduction by runners and by cuttings. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how each structure is adapted for its function.

**(a)** The testes are held in the scrotum. **[2]**
**(b)** Glands add fluid to sperm. **[1]**
**(c)** The oviduct. **[2]**
**(d)** The cervix. **[1]**

**9.** This question is about the hormones oestrogen and progesterone.

**(a)** Describe the role of oestrogen in the menstrual cycle. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why menstruation begins when the progesterone concentration falls. **[2]**
**(c)** State the structure in the ovary that makes progesterone after ovulation. **[1]**
**(d)** Give one secondary sexual characteristic caused by oestrogen and one caused by testosterone. **[2]**

**10.** (Paper 2 only) FSH and LH control the ovary.

**(a)** Name the gland that secretes FSH and LH. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe two roles of FSH. **[2]**
**(c)** The LH concentration of a woman peaks on day 13 of her cycle. State what happens next and why. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why a second egg does not normally mature in the same cycle. **[2]**

**11.** An embryo develops in the uterus, attached to the placenta and surrounded by amniotic fluid.

**(a)** Describe how the embryo obtains glucose and amino acids from its mother. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two features of the placenta that make exchange efficient. **[4]**
**(c)** Explain how amniotic fluid protects the embryo. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: sexual needs two parents, asexual one [1]; sexual involves gametes and fertilisation, asexual does not / sexual produces variation, asexual produces genetically identical offspring [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each point must state both sides, or be clearly comparative; "asexual has one parent" alone may not get the mark if the other half is missing.

**2.** The nucleus of a sperm fuses with the nucleus of an egg [1]; forming a zygote [1]; the zygote divides repeatedly by mitosis to form a ball of cells that develops into the embryo [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Sperm joins egg" is often too vague; the fusion of nuclei is the creditworthy idea.

**3. (a)** Q [1]; any two from: no petals / no nectar / anthers outside the flower on long filaments / feathery stigma outside the flower [1] [1]
**(b)** Pollen is carried randomly by the wind, so most never reaches a stigma [1]; producing large amounts increases the chance that some pollen lands on a stigma [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), the identification mark stands alone, but evidence must come from the table; general facts about wind pollination not in the table are not credited.

**4. (a)** A pollen tube grows from the pollen grain down through the style [1]; into the ovary and into an ovule [1]; the male nucleus travels down the tube and fuses with the female nucleus [1]
**(b)** Ovule → seed [1]; ovary → fruit [1]
*Examiner insight:* Reversing ovule and ovary loses both marks in (b), so learn the pair as a fixed link.

**5. (a)** 15 °C: (21 ÷ 50) × 100 = **42%** [1]; 25 °C: (46 ÷ 50) × 100 = **92%** [1]
**(b)** Any two: volume of water / species or age of seeds / number of seeds / light conditions / time before counting [1] [1]
**(c)** Enzymes in the seed work very slowly at low temperature [1]; so stored food is not broken down or respired fast enough to release energy for growth [1]
**(d)** Repeat each temperature several times and calculate a mean [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b), "the same amount of seeds" is accepted, but vague answers such as "same conditions" score zero; name the variable.

**6. (a)** Decrease = 2.40 − 1.92 = 0.48 g [1]; (0.48 ÷ 2.40) × 100 = **20%** [1]
**(b)** Stored starch is broken down by amylase to glucose [1]; glucose is used in respiration [1]; the seedling has no leaves yet / cannot photosynthesise, so the food used is not replaced [1]
**(c)** Leaves have grown and begun photosynthesis [1]; glucose is made faster than it is used in respiration, so new tissue is built [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), dividing by the final value (1.92) gives 25% and loses the accuracy mark; the denominator is always the starting value.

**7. (a)** Treated: (34 ÷ 40) × 100 = **85%** [1]; untreated: (18 ÷ 40) × 100 = **45%** [1]
**(b)** Rooting powder contains plant hormones [1]; which stimulate root growth, so more treated cuttings formed roots [1]
**(c)** To reduce water loss by transpiration [1]
**(d)** Similarity: both are asexual / both give genetically identical plants [1]; difference: runners are a natural method, cuttings an artificial method done by people [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b), quoting the 40-percentage-point difference supports the answer but does not replace the explanation; the hormone idea is required.

**8. (a)** The scrotum holds the testes outside the body, where the temperature is slightly lower [1]; which suits sperm production [1]
**(b)** The fluid provides nutrients for the sperm and a liquid to swim in [1]
**(c)** It is lined with cilia [1]; which sweep the egg towards the uterus, and it is where fertilisation happens [1]
**(d)** It is a ring of muscle that keeps the fetus in the uterus during pregnancy and widens at birth [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Explain how adapted" questions need the feature and its link to the function; naming the structure's job alone scores at most half.

**9. (a)** Oestrogen causes the uterus lining to repair [1]; and thicken after menstruation [1]
**(b)** Progesterone maintains the uterus lining [1]; when it falls, the lining is no longer maintained and breaks down, leaving the body as menstruation [1]
**(c)** The corpus luteum [1]
**(d)** Oestrogen: breasts develop / hips widen / menstrual cycle begins [1]; testosterone: facial hair / deeper voice / muscle development / sperm production [1]
*Examiner insight:* Swapping the roles of oestrogen (repair, thicken) and progesterone (maintain) is penalised, so use those exact verbs.

**10. (a)** The pituitary gland [1]
**(b)** Causes an egg to mature in a follicle in the ovary [1]; stimulates the ovary to produce oestrogen [1]
**(c)** Ovulation, the release of an egg from the ovary [1]; because the LH surge triggers it [1]
**(d)** Oestrogen (and later progesterone) inhibits FSH secretion [1]; without FSH, no further eggs are stimulated to mature [1]
*Examiner insight:* "The brain" is not accepted for (a); the named gland is needed.

**11. (a)** The mother's blood brings glucose and amino acids to the placenta [1]; they diffuse across the thin barrier into the embryo's blood [1]; and are carried to the embryo in the umbilical cord [1]
**(b)** Villi give a large surface area [1]; so more diffusion can happen at once [1]; the barrier between the two bloods is thin [1]; so the diffusion distance is short [1] (also accept as a feature-and-explanation pair: rich blood supply on both sides, keeping a steep concentration gradient)
**(c)** The fluid acts as a shock absorber [1]; cushioning the embryo against knocks and pressure [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (a), saying the blood of mother and embryo mixes is a contradiction that loses credit; the substances cross by diffusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing fertilisation as "sperm meets egg" without fusion of nuclei.
- Confusing pollination with fertilisation in flower questions.
- Answering flower questions with general knowledge rather than evidence from the table.
- Writing "ovary becomes seed".
- Dividing by the final value in a percentage-change calculation.
- Missing respiration when explaining a fall in dry mass.
- Writing "same conditions" instead of naming control variables.
- Stating a structure's job without its adaptation.
- Swapping the roles of oestrogen and progesterone.
- Putting the source of FSH and LH in the ovary.
