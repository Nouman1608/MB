For full explanations and worked examples, read the [features of organisms study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-features-of-organisms/) first. These notes cover sub-topic 1.3 of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 (animal and plant kingdoms, vertebrates, arthropods) are **Core**. Outcomes 4 to 7 (five kingdoms, ferns and flowering plants, viruses) are **Extended only**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Kingdom | One of the largest groups into which organisms are classified |
| Vertebrate | An animal with a backbone |
| Arthropod | An animal with jointed legs, a segmented body and an exoskeleton |
| Exoskeleton | A hard outer skeleton |
| Mammary glands | Glands that produce milk for the young |
| Hyphae (Extended) | Thread-like structures that make up the body of most fungi |
| Prokaryote (Extended) | A single-celled organism with no nucleus, such as a bacterium |
| Frond (Extended) | The leaf of a fern |
| Cotyledon (Extended) | A seed leaf inside the seed of a flowering plant |

## Core: animal or plant

| | Animal | Plant |
|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | No | Yes, cellulose |
| Chloroplasts | No | Yes |
| Nutrition | Eats organic substances made by others | Photosynthesis |
| Store | Glycogen | Starch or sucrose |

## Core: vertebrates in one line each

- **Mammals:** fur or hair; mammary glands; external ears.
- **Birds:** feathers; beak; wings; hard-shelled eggs.
- **Reptiles:** dry, scaly skin; leathery eggs laid on land.
- **Amphibians:** moist skin, no scales; eggs without shells in water; tadpoles with gills.
- **Fish:** scales; fins; gills.

**Order to check:** feathers? → fur or hair? → dry scales? → moist skin? → fins and gills?

## Core: arthropods by counting

All: jointed legs, segmented body, exoskeleton.

| Group | Legs | Body parts | Antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insects | 6 | 3 | 1 pair |
| Arachnids | 8 | 2 | 0 |
| Crustaceans | More than 8 | Fused head and thorax | 2 pairs |
| Myriapods | 1 or 2 pairs per segment | Many segments | 1 pair |

## Near-misses to learn

| Organism | Looks like | Actually | Deciding feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Whale | Fish | Mammal | Mammary glands; lungs |
| Bat | Bird | Mammal | Fur; no feathers |
| Penguin | Not a bird (cannot fly) | Bird | Feathers; beak |
| Newt | Reptile | Amphibian | Moist skin, no scales |
| Scorpion | Insect | Arachnid | 8 legs; no antennae |
| Woodlouse | Insect | Crustacean | 14 legs; 2 pairs of antennae |
| Centipede | Insect | Myriapod | Legs on every segment |

## Answer frame: classify and justify

1. Name the larger group first (vertebrate or arthropod; or, for Extended, the kingdom).
2. Name the smaller group.
3. Quote **two** features from the description or drawing that match that group.
4. If asked why it is not in a similar group, name the feature that rules that group out (for example, "not an insect, because it has 4 pairs of legs, not 3").

## Extended: five kingdoms

| Kingdom | Deciding features |
|---|---|
| Animal | Multicellular; no cell wall; no chloroplasts |
| Plant | Multicellular; cellulose wall; chloroplasts |
| Fungus | Hyphae (yeast single-celled); non-cellulose (chitin) wall; no chloroplasts; feeds by secreting enzymes and absorbing |
| Prokaryote | No nucleus; circular DNA; plasmids; single-celled |
| Protoctist | Nucleus; mostly single-celled; some plant-like, some animal-like |

## Extended: plant groups

- **Ferns:** fronds; no flowers; spores.
- **Flowering plants:** flowers; seeds.

| | Monocotyledon | Dicotyledon |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons | 1 | 2 |
| Leaf veins | Parallel | Branching network |
| Flower parts | Threes | Fours or fives |

## Extended: viruses

Protein coat + genetic material. Nothing more is required. No cells, so no kingdom.

## Exam traps

- "Lays eggs" does not identify a vertebrate group; name the egg type or another feature.
- Count legs in **pairs** and in total: 3 pairs = 6 legs.
- Spiders are not insects; woodlice are not insects.
- (Extended) Fungi are not plants: no chloroplasts and no cellulose.
- (Extended) Prokaryotes have **no nucleus**; protoctists **do**.
- (Extended) Ferns have no flowers and no seeds.
- (Extended) Do not give viruses a membrane, cytoplasm or ribosomes.

## Quick self-test

1. State two features that place an organism in the plant kingdom.
2. State the deciding feature of birds.
3. A vertebrate has moist skin without scales and lays eggs in water. Name its group.
4. Why is a bat classified as a mammal?
5. State three features shared by all arthropods.
6. An arthropod has 8 legs and no antennae. Name its group.
7. An arthropod has 3 body parts and 1 pair of antennae. Name its group.
8. (Extended) Name the kingdom of organisms that have no nucleus.
9. (Extended) Give two features of fungi.
10. (Extended) How do ferns reproduce?
11. (Extended) A plant has parallel leaf veins. Is it a monocotyledon or a dicotyledon?
12. (Extended) State the features of a virus required by the syllabus.

### Answers

1. Any two: cellulose cell walls; chloroplasts; photosynthesis; multicellular.
2. Feathers.
3. Amphibians.
4. It has fur and mammary glands (it does not have feathers).
5. Jointed legs, a segmented body and an exoskeleton.
6. Arachnids.
7. Insects.
8. Prokaryotes.
9. Any two: made of hyphae (except single-celled yeast); cell walls not made of cellulose; no chloroplasts; feed by secreting enzymes and absorbing the products.
10. By spores.
11. A monocotyledon.
12. A protein coat and genetic material.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are the five kingdoms Core or Extended?**
Extended only. Core candidates need the animal and plant kingdoms and the vertebrate and arthropod groups.

**Do I need to name examples of each group?**
Knowing a few examples helps you check your answers, but marks come from stating features and using them to classify.
