**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The key practices in Islam: "the role and importance of the Five Pillars" and "the meaning and importance of Amr bil maroof (commanding good) and Nahi annil munkar (forbidding evil)".** The prescribed passage is the Hadith of Jibril in Sahih al-Bukhari 50, Book 2, Hadith 43. The later topics (3.1.2.2 to 3.1.2.8) cover individual practices in detail. This page gives the overview: what the pillars are, how they work together, and the duty to encourage good and discourage harm.

## Beliefs and practices

Islam joins belief and action. The key beliefs of 3.1.1 are what a Muslim holds in the heart, and the key practices are what a Muslim does. The Qur'an often joins them: "Those who believe and do righteous deeds" is one of its most repeated phrases. A practice without belief would be empty, and belief without practice would be incomplete.

## The Five Pillars

The Prophet (pbuh) described the structure of Islam in a Hadith narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim): "Islam is built upon five: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing prayer, paying Zakah, the pilgrimage to the House (Hajj) and fasting in Ramadhan." A building rests on its pillars, so they are the supports of a Muslim's religious life.

| Pillar | What it is | What it trains | Link in the specification |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Shahadah** | Declaring that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad (pbuh) is His messenger | Faith and identity | 3.1.2.2 |
| **Salah** | Five daily prayers at fixed times | Discipline, remembrance, humility | 3.1.2.3 |
| **Sawm** | Fasting from dawn to sunset in Ramadhan | Self-control, empathy, gratitude | 3.1.2.4 |
| **Zakah** | Giving a fixed share of wealth to those in need | Generosity, justice | 3.1.2.5 |
| **Hajj** | Pilgrimage to Makkah once for those able | Unity, equality, submission | 3.1.2.6 |

### The role of each pillar

1. **Shahadah: belief declared.** It is the doorway into Islam and the statement of Tawhid and prophethood. Everything else depends on it.
2. **Salah: worship in daily life.** Five prayers divide the day and keep Allah in mind. Prayer is a direct link between a person and Allah.
3. **Sawm: worship through restraint.** A person gives up lawful food and drink for the sake of Allah, which trains self-control and shows solidarity with the hungry.
4. **Zakah: worship through wealth.** It purifies wealth and supports the poor. The Qur'an lists those who may receive it (9:60).
5. **Hajj: worship through journey.** Pilgrims from every background dress alike and follow the same rites, showing the unity of the Ummah. It is required of those who are able (3:97).

### Why the pillars are important

- **They give Muslims a shared identity.** Muslims all over the world follow the same five acts.
- **They organise time and money.** Prayer structures the day, Ramadhan the year, Zakah income, and Hajj a lifetime.
- **They balance the personal and the social.** Salah and Sawm are personal worship, while Zakah and Hajj bring social and community effects.
- **They teach equality.** Rich and poor stand in the same rows, fast the same fast, and wear the same Ihram.
- **They bring spiritual growth.** Each practice builds taqwa, the awareness of Allah that the Qur'an links with success.
- **They are a form of obedience.** Muslims perform them because Allah commands them, even when the reason is not fully understood.

### Differences between Muslims

Sunni Muslims speak of the Five Pillars. Twelver Shi'a Muslims commonly teach ten practices of religion (*Furu' ad-Din*), which include prayer, fasting, Zakah and Hajj and also Khums (a one-fifth contribution), Jihad, enjoining good, forbidding evil, love for the Prophet's family and disassociation from their enemies. The practices overlap strongly. Mention the difference only if asked.

## Amr bil maroof and Nahi annil munkar

*Amr bil maroof* means "commanding what is good" and *Nahi annil munkar* means "forbidding what is wrong". *Ma'ruf* is what is known to be good and accepted in Islamic teaching and by sound human nature, such as honesty and kindness to parents. *Munkar* is what is known to be wrong, such as theft and cruelty.

The duty appears in the Qur'an in several forms:

- "Let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong, and those will be the successful" (3:104).
- "You are the best nation produced [as an example] for mankind. You enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong and believe in Allah" (3:110).
- "The believing men and believing women are allies of one another. They enjoin what is right and forbid what is wrong" (9:71).

### How the duty is to be carried out

The Prophet (pbuh) said, "Whoever among you sees a wrong, let him change it with his hand; if he is not able, then with his tongue; and if he is not able, then with his heart, and that is the weakest of faith" (Sahih Muslim). The three levels give practical limits.

| Level | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| By the hand | Acting to stop harm, when one has the right and ability | A parent stopping a child from hurting a sibling; a teacher stopping bullying |
| By the tongue | Speaking out, advising | Advising a friend to pray; explaining why gossip is wrong |
| By the heart | Disliking the wrong and praying for change | Disapproving when you cannot do anything more |

**Rules for doing it well:**

1. **Knowledge:** know what is right and wrong before advising others.
2. **Gentleness:** "Invite to the way of your Lord with wisdom and good instruction" (16:125).
3. **Starting with oneself:** the Qur'an criticises those who tell others to do right and forget themselves (2:44).
4. **Avoiding greater harm:** a person must not cause worse harm by trying to prevent a wrong.
5. **Respect for law:** in a modern state, "by the hand" is carried out through proper channels, and not by taking the law into one's own hands.

### Why it is important

- **It protects the community.** The Prophet (pbuh) compared society to a ship where some people lived on the upper deck and some below. If those below made a hole to get water without troubling those above, and the others did not stop them, all would drown; if they stopped them, all would be saved (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- **It shows care.** Advising someone is a form of love, since it wishes them good.
- **It makes Muslims active.** Faith is not private only; it includes responsibility for others.
- **It strengthens the Pillars.** A community that reminds one another to pray, give Zakah and fast will find the pillars easier to keep.

## The prescribed passage: the Hadith of Jibril

The Hadith in Sahih al-Bukhari 50 tells how Jibril (AS), in the form of a man, asked the Prophet (pbuh) about faith, Islam and excellence. For this topic, the second answer matters most: Islam is "to worship Allah alone and none else, to offer prayers perfectly, to pay the compulsory charity (Zakah) and to observe fasts during the month of Ramadhan."

**Points to draw out:**

- The Hadith defines Islam through actions, which supports the idea that practice is central.
- The list in this report does not mention the Shahadah and Hajj in the same words; the Shahadah is implied by "worship Allah alone", and other reports list the five fully. The Hadith of Ibn Umar above gives all five.
- It places Islam beside faith (*iman*) and excellence (*ihsan*): "to worship Allah as if you see Him." This links the pillars to sincerity.
- The Prophet (pbuh) said at the end that Jibril "came to teach the people their religion", which shows that these three levels summarise the religion.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the Five Pillars are important to Muslims. **[4]**

> They are important because they give Muslims a shared identity and unity [1]. Muslims all over the world pray, fast and give in the same way, so they feel part of one Ummah [1]. They are also important because they build taqwa [1]; for example, fasting teaches self-control and prayer reminds a Muslim of Allah five times a day [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Amr bil maroof and Nahi annil munkar are important. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> It is important because it protects the community from harm [1]. If people ignore wrongdoing, it spreads and affects everyone, so Muslims have a duty to speak out [1]. It is also important because it shows care for others [1], since encouraging someone to do good is a way of wishing them success in this life and the next [1]. The Prophet (pbuh) taught that a person who sees wrong should change it by hand, tongue or heart [2], which shows that everyone has some way to take part.

## Common mistakes

- **Listing the pillars without roles.** Add what each pillar achieves.
- **Saying Zakah and Hajj are for everyone.** Zakah depends on reaching the *nisab*, and Hajj depends on ability.
- **Treating "forbidding evil" as permission for force.** The Hadith allows the hand only where one has the right and ability, and teaching stresses gentleness.
- **Forgetting that the pillars are acts of worship.** They are done to please Allah, not only to help society.
- **Missing the passage.** Use the Hadith's definition of Islam and the idea of ihsan.
