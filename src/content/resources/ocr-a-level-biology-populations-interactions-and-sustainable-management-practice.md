## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a limiting factor, and name two density-dependent limiting factors for a population of woodland birds. **[3]**

**2.** A population of 1250 lizards lives on an island. In one year there were 210 births, 95 deaths, 12 immigrants and 37 emigrants.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in the population. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest why the percentage change is likely to fall as the population grows. **[1]**

**3.** A yeast culture was sampled every 2 hours. The table shows log10 of the number of cells per cm3.

| Time / h | 0 | 2 | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 | 16 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| log10 cells per cm3 | 4.00 | 4.00 | 4.30 | 4.60 | 4.90 | 5.20 | 5.40 | 5.45 | 5.45 |

**(a)** Describe the growth of the culture from 0 to 16 hours. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of cells per cm3 at 10 hours. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the doubling time between 2 and 10 hours. The log10 of 2 is 0.30. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the change in growth after 10 hours. **[2]**

**4.** Wood mice and their predators, kestrels, were surveyed for eight years.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mice per hectare | 60 | 150 | 280 | 190 | 70 | 110 | 240 | 170 |
| Kestrel pairs per 10 km2 | 4 | 5 | 8 | 11 | 7 | 4 | 6 | 9 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between the two populations. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in the mouse population from year 3 to year 5. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the kestrel peak follows the mouse peak. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why these data alone do not show that kestrels control the mouse population. **[1]**

**5.** A plant was sown at three densities. Mean dry mass per plant at harvest was 12.0 g at 5 plants per m2, 7.5 g at 20 per m2 and 3.0 g at 80 per m2.

**(a)** Calculate the total dry mass per m2 at each density. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the mass per plant falls as density rises. **[2]**
**(c)** Name this type of competition and state how it differs from interspecific competition. **[1]**

**6.** Distinguish between conservation and preservation, and give an example of each. **[4]**

**7.** A coastal village depends on a mangrove forest. Give one economic, one social and one ethical reason for conserving the forest. **[6]**

**8.** A 90 ha wood is coppiced on a 15-year rotation with equal areas cut each year. Each hectare yields 38 m3 of poles when cut.

**(a)** Calculate the area cut each year. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the volume harvested each year. **[1]**
**(c)** The owner proposes a 10-year rotation, with each hectare then yielding 24 m3. Calculate the new yearly volume and the percentage change. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how coppicing on a rotation can increase the biodiversity of a wood. **[2]**

**9.** A fish stock is 35 000 tonnes. It adds 5600 tonnes of new growth each year. A fixed quota of 7000 tonnes per year is proposed.

**(a)** Express the quota as a percentage of the stock. **[1]**
**(b)** Show that the quota is not sustainable. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the stock after 3 years if the quota is taken each year and growth stays at 5600 tonnes. **[2]**
**(d)** State the largest percentage of the current stock that could be taken sustainably. **[1]**
**(e)** Describe one measure, other than a quota, that helps maintain the stock. **[1]**

**10.** A bay of 320 km2 supports a fishing village. Annual catches over five years were 900, 860, 790, 710 and 650 tonnes. A plan would make 25% of the bay a no-take zone. In a similar bay with a no-take zone, fish biomass was 380 tonnes per km2 inside it and 160 tonnes per km2 outside.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in catch over the five years. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the area of the proposed no-take zone. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times greater the biomass is inside the no-take zone than outside, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(d)** Evaluate the plan. **[6]**

**11.** Ground-nesting seabirds breed on an island. Before rats arrived there were 1200 breeding pairs and 72% of nests produced a chick. Five years after rats arrived there were 780 pairs and 31% of nests produced a chick. After trapping there were 1010 pairs.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in breeding pairs after rats arrived. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage rise after trapping, from the 780 pairs. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how rats reduced the seabird population. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest two reasons why the population has not returned to 1200 pairs. **[2]**
**(e)** State one ethical issue raised by killing rats. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A limiting factor is a variable in short supply [1] that keeps the population below the size it could otherwise reach. Two density-dependent factors, for example competition for food or nest sites [1] and disease or predation [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name factors whose effect grows with density; frost or flood would be density-independent.

**2. (a)** Net change = (210 + 12) - (95 + 37) = 90 [1]. Percentage = 90 / 1250 x 100 [1] = **7.2%** increase [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Competition for food or space rises, so births fall or deaths rise [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the births, immigrants, deaths and emigrants in one line of working, so the logic is visible.

**3. (a)** No growth from 0 to 2 hours (lag phase) [1]. Steady increase on the log scale from 2 to 10 hours, which is exponential growth [1]. Growth slows after 10 hours and levels off at 5.45 from 14 hours [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** 10^5.20 [1] = **1.6 x 10^5 cells per cm3** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Rise in log10 = 5.20 - 4.00 = 1.20 over 8 hours, so 0.15 per hour [1]. Doubling time = 0.30 / 0.15 = **2.0 hours** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Limiting factors act, such as nutrients running low [1]. Births (cell division) fall to equal deaths, so numbers level off at the carrying capacity [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The axis is a logarithm. Quoting a log value as if it were a cell count loses credit in part (b).

**4. (a)** Both populations rise and fall in repeated cycles [1]. The kestrel peaks come one year after the mouse peaks [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** (70 - 280) / 280 x 100 = **-75%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** A larger mouse population feeds more kestrels, which survive and breed [1]. Kestrels need time to find prey and raise young, so their peak is delayed [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Mouse numbers may also depend on their own food supply or weather, so the data show a correlation only [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* State the lag as a number of years, not just that the graphs are similar.

**5. (a)** 5 x 12.0 = 60 g; 20 x 7.5 = 150 g [1]; 80 x 3.0 = 240 g [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Each plant competes with more neighbours for light, water and ions [1]. Each gets a smaller share of the resources, so it grows less [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Intraspecific competition, between plants of the same species; interspecific is between different species [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data in part (b): density rose 16 times while total mass rose 4 times.

**6.** Conservation is the maintenance of ecosystems and biodiversity through management [1], allowing sustainable human use [1]. Preservation is protection of an area or species as it is, with human interference minimised or excluded [1]. Example: coppicing a woodland (conservation) and a no-take marine zone (preservation) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The terms differ in what human activity is allowed, so say that explicitly.

**7.** Economic: the forest supplies timber and fish nursery areas for income, or attracts tourists [2]. Social: it protects the village from storm waves and supports livelihoods and cultural traditions [2]. Ethical: other species have a right to exist, and people have a duty to future generations [2]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each type of reason to the mangrove context supplied; generic statements about biodiversity are less specific.

**8. (a)** 90 / 15 = **6 ha** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 6 x 38 = **228 m3** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** New area = 90 / 10 = 9 ha, volume = 9 x 24 = 216 m3 [1]. Change = (216 - 228) / 228 x 100 = **-5.3%** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Sections are cut in turn, so parts are at different stages of regrowth [1]. Light reaches the woodland floor after cutting, so ground plants and their insects increase, giving a range of habitats [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c) give the new volume and the percentage, with its sign.

**9. (a)** 7000 / 35 000 x 100 = **20%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The quota of 7000 exceeds the growth of 5600 by 1400 tonnes per year, so the stock falls [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Each year the stock changes by 5600 - 7000 = -1400 [1]. After 3 years: 35 000 - 4200 = **30 800 tonnes** [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 5600 / 35 000 x 100 = **16%** [1]. **[1]**
**(e)** Any one: larger net mesh so that young fish escape, closed season or no-take zone to protect spawning, or limits on fishing effort [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For "show that", compare the quota with the growth and state the difference.

**10. (a)** (650 - 900) / 900 x 100 = **-27.8%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 0.25 x 320 = **80 km2** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** 380 / 160 = 2.375, which is **2.4 times** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Protected fish can reach breeding size and spawn [1]. Biomass is higher in a no-take zone, which suggests stocks can recover [1]. Fish may spread into fished areas, which could help catches [1]. Fishers lose 80 km2 of fishing area and income at first [1]. The catch is already falling, so doing nothing may cost more in the long term [1]. Judgement: the plan is justified because the stock is declining, though compensation may be needed, and the evidence is from a similar bay and not this one [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation gains from a judgement that weighs short-term against long-term effects.

**11. (a)** (780 - 1200) / 1200 x 100 = **-35%** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** (1010 - 780) / 780 x 100 = **29.5%** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Rats take eggs and chicks from nests on the ground [1]. Nest success fell from 72% to 31%, so fewer chicks reached breeding age [1]. Fewer young replaced the adults that died [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** Some rats may remain [1]. Chicks take years to reach breeding age, so recovery is slow [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** Animals are killed to protect another species, which some people think unacceptable [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (c), connect the nest-success figures to numbers of young and then to the population.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting a logarithmic value as if it were a count.
- Describing a predator-prey graph without the lag in years.
- Naming a density-independent factor when asked for density-dependent.
- Omitting units, or not saying whether a percentage change is a rise or a fall.
- Giving general reasons for conservation that ignore the context.
- Treating conservation and preservation as the same thing.
- Evaluating with no judgement.
