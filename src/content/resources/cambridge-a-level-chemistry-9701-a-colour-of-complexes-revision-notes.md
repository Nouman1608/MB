## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Degenerate orbitals** | orbitals with the same energy (all five 3d orbitals in an isolated ion) |
| **Non-degenerate orbitals** | orbitals of the same sub-shell that now have different energies, because ligands repel them unequally |
| **ΔE** | the energy gap between the two non-degenerate sets of d orbitals |
| **d–d transition** | promotion of an electron from the lower set to the higher set by absorbing a photon |
| **Complementary colour** | the colour seen when a particular colour is removed from white light |
| **Ligand exchange** | replacement of one ligand in a complex by another |

## Splitting patterns

Octahedral (six ligands on the axes):

    higher set:  dx²−y²   dz²                 (2 orbitals; lobes point at ligands)
                     ↑ ΔE (larger)
    lower set:   dxy   dxz   dyz              (3 orbitals; lobes between ligands)

Tetrahedral (four ligands between the axes):

    higher set:  dxy   dxz   dyz              (3 orbitals)
                     ↑ ΔE (smaller)
    lower set:   dx²−y²   dz²                 (2 orbitals)

**Memory hook:** octahedral "2 over 3", tetrahedral "3 over 2", and the tetrahedral gap is smaller.

## Why colour happens: four marks, four links

1. Ligands **split** the d orbitals into two non-degenerate sets (gap ΔE).
2. A d electron **absorbs** visible light and is **promoted** from the lower to the higher set.
3. Frequency absorbed: **ΔE = hf** (and f = c/λ).
4. The colour **seen** is the **complementary** colour of the light absorbed.

| Bigger ΔE means... | Smaller ΔE means... |
|---|---|
| higher frequency absorbed | lower frequency absorbed |
| shorter wavelength absorbed | longer wavelength absorbed |
| absorption moves towards the violet end | absorption moves towards the red end |

## Which ions can show d–d colour?

| Ion | 3d electrons | d–d transition possible? |
|---|---|---|
| Sc³⁺, Ti⁴⁺ | 3d⁰ | no: no electron to promote |
| Ti³⁺ | 3d¹ | yes |
| Fe³⁺ | 3d⁵ | yes |
| Cu²⁺ | 3d⁹ | yes: one vacancy in the upper set |
| Zn²⁺, Cu⁺ | 3d¹⁰ | no: no vacancy to promote into |

To count d electrons: write the atom's configuration, then remove the **4s electrons first**.
Example: Co is [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s², so Co²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁷.

## Complementary colour pairs

    red ↔ green-blue      orange ↔ blue      yellow ↔ violet

Absorbs orange → looks blue. Absorbs yellow → looks violet. Absorbs blue → looks orange.

## What changes ΔE

- **Ligand identity:** for copper(II), NH₃ gives a larger ΔE than H₂O.
- **Shape and coordination number:** tetrahedral splitting is smaller than octahedral.
- **Metal oxidation state:** a different charge on the ion gives a different ΔE.

## Syllabus colour changes

| Starting ion | Reagent | New species | Colour change | Shape |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | OH⁻(aq) or a little NH₃(aq) | [Cu(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] | pale blue solution → pale blue precipitate | octahedral |
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | excess NH₃(aq) | [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ | pale blue → deep blue solution | octahedral |
| [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | concentrated HCl | [CuCl₄]²⁻ | pale blue → yellow-green | tetrahedral |
| [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | OH⁻(aq) or a little NH₃(aq) | [Co(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] | pink solution → blue precipitate | octahedral |
| [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | excess NH₃(aq) | [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ | pink → pale straw, darkening in air | octahedral |
| [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | concentrated HCl | [CoCl₄]²⁻ | pink → blue | tetrahedral |

Ammonia first acts as a **base** (making OH⁻, so the hydroxide precipitates), then in excess as a **ligand**
(the precipitate dissolves to give the ammine complex).

Chloride exchanges are **reversible**: adding water shifts the equilibrium back to the aqua ion and its colour.

## Calculation pattern

    f  = c / λ                      (λ in metres: nm × 10⁻⁹)
    ΔE = h f                        (J per ion)
    ΔE × L ÷ 1000                   (kJ per mole)

Quick check: absorption at 600 nm gives f = 5.00 × 10¹⁴ Hz, ΔE = 3.32 × 10⁻¹⁹ J, about 200 kJ mol⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- **Absorbed, not emitted.** Electrons are promoted; nothing "falls back and emits" in this explanation.
- **Seen ≠ absorbed.** The observed colour is the complementary one.
- **Do not attach colour to a ligand** ("ammonia is blue"). The complex absorbs.
- **Remove 4s before 3d** when counting d electrons in an ion.
- **Shape matters.** [CoCl₄]²⁻ is tetrahedral; that alone makes ΔE smaller.
- **Name the new complex** in a colour-change answer; marks are often given for the formula.
- **d⁰ and d¹⁰** have no d–d transition: no electron, or no vacancy.

## Self-test

1. Define *degenerate orbitals*.
2. Why are the 3d orbitals of a gaseous Fe²⁺ ion degenerate, but not those of [Fe(H₂O)₆]²⁺?
3. Which two d orbitals form the higher set in an octahedral complex?
4. State the splitting pattern in a tetrahedral complex.
5. Explain why a solution of a titanium(IV) compound is colourless.
6. A complex absorbs mainly yellow light. What colour is it?
7. Complex A absorbs at 450 nm and complex B at 650 nm. Which has the larger ΔE?
8. Calculate ΔE in J for one ion that absorbs light of frequency 5.50 × 10¹⁴ Hz (h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s).
9. Give the formula and colour of the copper species formed with excess concentrated hydrochloric acid.
10. Give two reasons why ΔE for [CoCl₄]²⁻ differs from ΔE for [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺.
11. Write the equation for the formation of the deep blue copper complex from the aqua ion.
12. Describe what you see when aqueous ammonia is added dropwise, then in excess, to aqueous cobalt(II) ions.

**Answers:**

1. Orbitals of equal energy.
2. In the free ion no ligands are present, so nothing repels the d orbitals unequally; in the complex, the six
   water ligands repel the d orbitals by different amounts, splitting them into two sets.
3. dx²−y² and dz².
4. Three orbitals in the higher set and two in the lower set; smaller ΔE than octahedral.
5. Ti⁴⁺ is 3d⁰, so there is no d electron to promote and no visible light is absorbed.
6. Violet.
7. A: a shorter wavelength means a higher frequency, so a larger ΔE.
8. ΔE = hf = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 5.50 × 10¹⁴ = 3.65 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.
9. [CuCl₄]²⁻, yellow-green.
10. The ligand is different (Cl⁻ instead of H₂O) and the shape is different (tetrahedral instead of
    octahedral, coordination number 4 instead of 6).
11. [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4NH₃ → [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ + 4H₂O.
12. A blue precipitate of [Co(OH)₂(H₂O)₄] forms first; in excess ammonia it dissolves to give a pale straw
    solution of [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺, which darkens on standing in air.
