## The specification points

1. Evaluate the impact and importance of a marketing strategy to a business and its stakeholders.
2. Recommend and justify a marketing strategy for a business.

## Core recall

### Contents of a marketing strategy

| Part | Content |
|---|---|
| Objectives | Measurable targets with dates |
| Analysis | SWOT, market research, share, growth, competitors, external influences |
| Target markets | Which segments to serve |
| Positioning | How the product is seen against rivals |
| Mix | Product, price, place, promotion (plus process, people, physical evidence for services) |
| Budget and resources | Money, people, time |
| Timetable and responsibilities | Who does what and when |
| Monitoring and control | Measures and corrective action |

A campaign is part of a strategy, not the whole.

### Return on spending

- Extra contribution = extra sales × margin.
- Net gain = extra contribution − extra spend.
- Return = net gain ÷ extra spend × 100.

### Monitoring

- Variance = actual − target (adverse if below target).
- Achievement = actual ÷ target × 100.
- A small shortfall in sales can cut the return sharply, because the margin is small compared with the sales target.

### Comparing options with a weighted table

1. List criteria (fit with objectives, cost, risk, speed, brand fit).
2. Weight them (total 100).
3. Score each option from 1 to 5.
4. Weighted score = sum of weight × score ÷ 100.
5. Check by changing the weights. Say that scores are judgements.

### Impact and importance

| For the business | For stakeholders |
|---|---|
| Aligns the mix behind one aim | Shareholders: profit if it works, loss if it fails |
| Directs scarce resources | Employees: growth and training, or pressure and redundancy |
| Gives standards to measure progress | Customers: better products or loss of a product |
| Supports other functions | Suppliers: larger or smaller orders |
| Prepares for competition and change | Lenders: confidence in repayment |

Most important when entering a new market, launching a product, facing new competition or with limited resources. Limits: relies on forecasts; costs time and money; may become out of date; rigid if followed blindly; useless if not communicated.

### Recommending a strategy

1. Diagnose with data.
2. State the objective (number and date).
3. Choose target market and positioning.
4. Outline the mix; check it is consistent.
5. Cost it and give the expected return.
6. Justify against an alternative.
7. State the main risk and how progress will be monitored.

## Quick worked reminder

Wren Outdoor (invented): sales £3.0m; target +20% = £3.6m. Extra spend £60,000. Extra contribution at 30% = £600,000 × 0.30 = £180,000. Net £120,000, so return 200%. Halfway: target £1.8m, actual £1.62m; variance −£0.18m; 90% achieved. If the year ends at £3.24m, extra contribution is £72,000, net £12,000 and the return falls to 20%.

Weighted table (weights 30, 20, 20, 15, 15): A scores 4.15, B 3.40, C 2.70. With weights 50, 10, 10, 15, 15: A 4.15, B 3.90, C 3.20. Recommend A.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing strategy | A long-term plan to achieve marketing objectives |
| Positioning | How a product is seen against rivals |
| Target market | The segments the business chooses to serve |
| Variance | The difference between actual and target |
| Adverse variance | Actual result worse than target |
| Return on spending | Net gain divided by the spending, as a percentage |
| Weighted scoring | Scoring options against weighted criteria |

## Evaluation prompts

- Does the strategy link to the business objectives and use evidence from the analysis?
- Is the mix consistent, and affordable with the resources?
- What is the expected return, and how sensitive is it to the sales forecast?
- Who gains and who loses among the stakeholders?
- What was rejected and why?
- Finish with a judgement and the main risk.

## Exam traps

- A list of activities is not a strategy.
- Give numbers: target, cost, return.
- Compare with at least one alternative.
- Different stakeholders are affected differently.
- Mention monitoring and what would trigger a change.

## Self-test

1. Define a marketing strategy.
2. Name four parts of a marketing strategy.
3. Distinguish a strategy from a campaign.
4. Extra sales £400,000 at 25% margin, extra spend £50,000. Find the net gain.
5. Find the return on spending in question 4.
6. Target £2.0m; actual £1.7m. Find the variance and percentage achieved.
7. Option scores: weight 60 score 4, weight 40 score 3. Find the weighted score.
8. Give one benefit of a marketing strategy to employees.
9. Give one limit of a marketing strategy.
10. Why check a weighted table by changing the weights?
11. Why must a strategy be communicated to staff?
12. Give two things to include when justifying a recommended strategy.

### Answers

1. A long-term plan for achieving marketing objectives.
2. Any four: objectives; analysis; target markets; positioning; mix; budget; timetable; monitoring.
3. A strategy is the long-term overall plan; a campaign is one activity within it.
4. 400,000 × 0.25 = 100,000; less 50,000 = **£50,000**.
5. 50,000 ÷ 50,000 × 100 = **100%**.
6. Variance = **−£0.3m** (adverse); 1.7 ÷ 2.0 = **85%**.
7. (60×4 + 40×3) ÷ 100 = (240 + 120) ÷ 100 = **3.6**.
8. Any one: clear targets; training; job security if growth.
9. Any one: relies on forecasts; costly; can become out of date; rigid.
10. To see whether the ranking depends on the weights chosen.
11. Staff carry out the plan, so they need to know the aims and their part in it.
12. Any two: evidence from the case; expected return; comparison with an alternative; risk and how to monitor.
