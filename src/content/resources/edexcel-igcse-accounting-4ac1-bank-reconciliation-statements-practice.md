## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain two purposes of a bank reconciliation statement. **[4]**

**2.** State whether each item should be entered in the cash book or shown only on the bank reconciliation statement.

**(a)** Bank charges. **[1]**
**(b)** A cheque paid to a supplier that has not yet been presented. **[1]**
**(c)** A credit transfer from a customer. **[1]**
**(d)** Cash paid in on the last day of the month, not yet on the statement. **[1]**
**(e)** A standing order for insurance. **[1]**
**(f)** A cheque from another business wrongly charged to the account by the bank. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why a dishonoured cheque reduces the cash book balance. **[2]**

**4.** The updated cash book shows 1,870. Unpresented cheques total 640 and there is an uncredited lodgement of 1,120. Calculate the balance on the bank statement. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why unpresented cheques cause a difference between the cash book and the bank statement. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** At 31 August 2027 the cash book of Ferndale Bakery showed a debit balance of 3,260. A comparison with the bank statement revealed:

- bank charges of 42 and a direct debit for electricity of 236, not in the cash book
- a credit transfer of 615 from a customer and bank interest received of 24, not in the cash book
- a customer's cheque for 310 had been dishonoured
- a cheque payment of 572 had been entered in the cash book as 527
- cheques of 488 and 1,140 had not been presented
- a lodgement of 930 had not been credited.

**(a)** Prepare the updated cash book (bank column), showing the balance carried down. **[6]**
**(b)** Prepare the bank reconciliation statement at 31 August 2027. **[4]**

**8.** At 31 October 2027 Haddon Signs' cash book shows an overdraft of 2,140. The following are not in the cash book: bank interest charged 85; a direct debit of 160; a credit transfer from a customer of 300. Cheques of 410 have not been presented, and a lodgement of 760 has not been credited.

**(a)** Calculate the updated cash book balance. **[3]**
**(b)** Prepare the bank reconciliation statement, starting with the updated cash book balance. **[3]**

**9.** The bank statement of Elder Street Books shows a balance of 5,210 in funds. Cheques of 1,370 have not been presented and a lodgement of 845 has not been credited. The bank has wrongly credited the account with 200 that belonged to another customer.

Calculate the updated cash book balance, starting from the bank statement balance. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why:

**(a)** unpresented cheques are not entered in the cash book **[2]**
**(b)** bank charges are entered in the cash book. **[2]**

**11.** Explain, with an example, how preparing a bank reconciliation statement each month can help a business detect fraud. **[3]**

**12.** The owner of Haddon Signs says that monthly bank reconciliations are unnecessary because the online banking app shows the balance at any time. Evaluate this view. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, each explained. Unrecorded items [1]: bank charges, direct debits and credit transfers are found and entered, so the cash book is complete [1]. Errors [1]: mistakes by the business or the bank are found by comparing the two records line by line [1]. Also acceptable: detecting fraud; confirming the correct bank balance for the statement of financial position. **[4]**

**2.** (a) Cash book [1]. (b) Statement only [1]. (c) Cash book [1]. (d) Statement only [1]. (e) Cash book [1]. (f) Statement only [1]. **[6]**

**3.** The cheque was recorded as a receipt when it was paid in [1], but the bank has refused to pay it, so the money has not been received and the receipt must be reversed by a payment entry [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 1,870 + 640 − 1,120 [1] = **1,390** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The business records a cheque in the cash book when it is written [1], but the bank only records it when the payee pays it in and it is presented, which may be days later [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Start: 5,210 [1]. Less unpresented cheques 1,370 [1]. Add uncredited lodgement 845. Less bank error 200, because the bank balance is overstated by money that is not the business's [1]. Updated cash book = **4,485** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** The cash book already records the cheque correctly, on the date it was written [1]; the difference is only timing, which disappears when the bank processes it [1].
**(b)** The bank has already taken the charges from the account [1], so the business's record is incomplete and its balance overstated until they are entered [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Comparing every payment on the statement with the cash book shows any payment the business did not authorise [1]. For example, a cheque altered from 150 to 1,500 after it was signed would show 1,500 on the statement but 150 in the cash book [1], so the difference would be noticed and investigated within the month, before larger losses occur [1]. **[3]**

**12.**
- The app shows the bank's balance, which is useful for day-to-day cash management [1].
- But it does not show unpresented cheques or uncredited lodgements, so it is not the business's true position [1].
- Without a reconciliation, unrecorded charges, bank errors and fraudulent payments may go unnoticed, and the cash book (used for the financial statements) may be wrong [1].
- Conclusion: the app is a helpful check, but a monthly reconciliation is still needed to keep the cash book correct and detect errors and fraud [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Entering unpresented cheques or uncredited lodgements in the cash book.
- Adding a dishonoured cheque in the cash book.
- Starting the reconciliation from the cash book balance before updating it.
- Reversing signs when the account is overdrawn.
- Entering a bank error in the cash book.
- Missing titles, dates or labels on the first and last lines.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I check that my reconciliation is right?**
The final line must equal the closing balance on the bank statement given in the question. If it does not, look for an item placed in the cash book that belongs on the statement, or the reverse.

**What happens to a cash book error found during reconciliation?**
It is corrected in the cash book before the statement is prepared, so the updated balance is right.
