## Questions

**1.** Biodiversity can be described at three levels. Name each level and give an example of what is measured at that level. **[3]**

**2.** Two invented woodland sites were surveyed.

| Species | Site 1 | Site 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Oak | 22 | 70 |
| Birch | 26 | 10 |
| Hazel | 24 | 12 |
| Holly | 28 | 8 |

**(a)** State the species richness of each site. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce which site has the higher biodiversity, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how the loss of an ecosystem can lead to a loss of genetic diversity in a surviving species. **[2]**

**4.** Compare the causes of past mass extinctions with the causes of the present loss of biodiversity. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the Caribbean monk seal was vulnerable to human activity and how humans caused its extinction. **[3]**

**6.** Outline the causes of the loss of mixed dipterocarp forest in Southeast Asia, and one consequence for biodiversity. **[3]**

**7.** Two surveys of dragonflies at a lake gave these invented results.

| Survey | Year | Length of shore searched / m | Number of visits | Species recorded |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 2010 | 2000 | 12 | 18 |
| B | 2025 | 800 | 4 | 11 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in species recorded. **[1]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether these data show a real decline in dragonfly diversity. **[2]**

**8.** Many records of species come from citizen scientists. State one advantage and one disadvantage of using these records as evidence of a biodiversity crisis. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how the introduction of a predatory mammal to an island could cause the extinction of a native ground-nesting bird. **[2]**

**10.** A rare plant grows only in one valley, which will be flooded by a new dam. Its seeds remain viable for many years when dried. Discuss which conservation approaches should be used. **[3]**

**11.** Four invented species are being considered for a conservation programme.

| Species | Close living relatives | Threat status |
|---|---|---|
| E | Many | Critically endangered |
| F | None; only member of its family | Critically endangered |
| G | None; only member of its family | Least concern |
| H | Few | Vulnerable |

**(a)** Using the EDGE approach, deduce which species should be the highest priority and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest one non-scientific factor that might also affect the choice. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why conservation of biodiversity requires several different approaches. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Ecosystem diversity: the variety of habitats or communities in an area [1]. Species diversity: the number of species and their relative abundance [1]. Genetic diversity: the variety of alleles within a species [1] **[3]**

**2. (a)** Both sites have 4 species [1]

**(b)** Site 1 [1]: richness is the same, but its species have similar abundances (high evenness), whereas Site 2 is dominated by oak [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* With equal richness, evenness decides; quote the figures, such as 70 of 100 trees being oak at Site 2.

**3.** Loss of habitat leaves only a small population of the species [1]; a small population carries fewer alleles, and more are lost by chance and inbreeding, so genetic diversity falls [1] **[2]**

**4.** Past mass extinctions had natural causes such as volcanic activity or an asteroid impact [1], whereas the present loss is driven mainly by human activity, such as habitat destruction and overexploitation, linked to human population growth [1] **[2]**

**5.** It bred and rested on beaches and reefs that people could reach easily [1]. It was hunted for its oil and meat [1], and overfishing reduced its food supply; it was last confirmed in 1952 and declared extinct in 2008 [1] **[3]**

**6.** Logging of large timber trees [1]; clearance for plantations such as oil palm, and fire [1]. Consequence: species that depend on the forest lose their habitat and decline or become extinct [1] **[3]**

**7. (a)** (11 − 18) ÷ 18 × 100 = **−39%** [1]

**(b)** Survey B searched 40% of the shore length with a third of the visits, so fewer species would be found even if none had been lost [1]; the data may suggest a decline but cannot show one, because the method was not standardised [1] **[3]**

**8.** Advantage: many observers give wide coverage over large areas and long periods [1]. Disadvantage: identification skills and recording effort vary, so records may be inaccurate or inconsistent [1] **[2]**

**9.** The native bird evolved without such predators, so it has no defensive behaviour and its nests on the ground are easy to reach [1]; the predator eats eggs, chicks and adults faster than the population can reproduce, so numbers fall to extinction [1] **[2]**

**10.** In situ conservation is not possible because the habitat will be destroyed [1]. Seeds can be stored in a seed bank, which is suitable because they stay viable when dried [1]. Plants could also be grown in a botanic garden or moved to a similar protected site, so that a living population is kept as well as stored seed [1] **[3]**

**11. (a)** Species F [1]: it is both evolutionarily distinct (no close relatives) and critically endangered, so its loss would remove a large unique branch of evolutionary history [1]

**(b)** Any one: cost; likelihood of success; cultural or economic value; public appeal; needs of local people [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Species G is just as distinct, but it is not threatened; EDGE needs both criteria.

**12.** Species differ in their needs: some can be protected in their habitat, while others need captive breeding or stored germ plasm because their habitat is lost or their numbers are tiny [1]; using several approaches together gives a back-up if one fails [1] **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining biodiversity as species number only.
- Judging diversity on richness alone when evenness differs.
- Accepting a decline from surveys with different effort.
- Giving a case study without vulnerability, cause and outcome.
- Choosing a species for EDGE on only one of the two criteria.
- Recommending in situ conservation for a habitat that is being destroyed.
