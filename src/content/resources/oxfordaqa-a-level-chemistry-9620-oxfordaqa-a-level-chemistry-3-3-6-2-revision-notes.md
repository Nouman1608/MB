## Specification in brief

- Mass spectrometry can be used to determine the **molecular formula** of a compound.
- Use **precise atomic masses** and the **precise molecular mass** to do this.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Relative isotopic mass | Mass of an atom of an isotope relative to 1/12 of the mass of a carbon-12 atom |
| Precise atomic mass | Relative isotopic mass to four decimal places (for example ¹H = 1.0078) |
| Precise molecular mass | Sum of the precise masses of the atoms in one molecule |
| Molecular ion, M⁺ | Ion formed when a molecule loses one electron; its m/z equals the molecular mass |
| m/z | Mass-to-charge ratio; for a 1+ ion it equals the ion's mass |
| High-resolution mass spectrometer | Instrument measuring m/z to four decimal places |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule |

## Recall: precise masses

| Isotope | Precise relative mass | Compared with whole number |
|---|---|---|
| ¹²C | 12.0000 | exact (by definition) |
| ¹H | 1.0078 | slightly above |
| ¹⁴N | 14.0031 | slightly above |
| ¹⁶O | 15.9949 | slightly below |

**Consequence:** for formulae with the same whole-number Mr, more H and N push the precise mass up; more O pulls it down.

## Recall: the method

1. List candidates with the same whole-number Mr.
2. Calculate each precise mass, atom by atom, to four decimal places.
3. Match to the measured m/z of the molecular ion.
4. Show the rejected totals as well.

## Recall: useful sets with the same nominal mass

| Nominal Mr | Formula: precise mass |
|---|---|
| 28 | CO 27.9949; N₂ 28.0062; C₂H₄ 28.0312 |
| 44 | CO₂ 43.9898; N₂O 44.0011; C₂H₄O 44.0261; CH₄N₂ 44.0374; C₃H₈ 44.0624 |
| 60 | C₂H₄O₂ 60.0210; CH₄N₂O 60.0323; C₃H₈O 60.0573; C₂H₈N₂ 60.0686 |
| 100 | C₅H₈O₂ 100.0522; C₆H₁₂O 100.0885; C₅H₁₂N₂ 100.0998; C₇H₁₆ 100.1248 |

Look at the pattern within each row: replacing CH₄ by O lowers the precise mass by 0.0363, so formulae differing in this way are easy to separate.

## Recall: combining with other data

- **Percentage composition → empirical formula**; whole-number Mr from M⁺ → multiplier → molecular formula; precise mass confirms it.
- **Mass spectrometry gives the formula only.** Use test tube reactions or infrared spectra to decide between isomers.

### Worked reminder

M⁺ at m/z = 60.0573, compound contains C, H and O only. Candidates with Mr 60: C₂H₄O₂ (60.0210) and C₃H₈O (60.0573). Formula: **C₃H₈O**, which could be propan-1-ol, propan-2-ol or methoxyethane. Acidified dichromate, oxidation products and infrared evidence would then separate these.

### Worked reminder: nitrogen-containing candidates

M⁺ at m/z = 74.0479. Candidates with Mr 74:

- C₃H₆O₂: 3(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = 74.0366
- C₂H₆N₂O: 2(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 2(14.0031) + 15.9949 = 74.0479
- C₃H₁₀N₂: 3(12.0000) + 10(1.0078) + 2(14.0031) = 74.0842

The formula is **C₂H₆N₂O**; the other two differ from the measured value in the second decimal place.

## Exam traps

- Rounded Ar values (12, 1, 16) cannot separate candidates; use the precise ones.
- Keep **four decimal places** throughout.
- Write every multiplication: 8(1.0078), not "8 H".
- The molecular ion is the main peak at **highest m/z**, not the tallest peak.
- The answer is a **molecular formula**, not a structure.
- Use the precise masses **given in the question**.
- A candidate with a **different whole-number Mr** can be rejected before any precise calculation.
- Count atoms from the formula twice: a single missed H changes the total by 1.0078 and gives a false match or mismatch.

## Self-test

1. What does a high-resolution mass spectrometer measure that a low-resolution one does not?
2. Why is the precise relative mass of ¹²C exactly 12.0000?
3. Why does m/z of M⁺ equal the molecular mass?
4. Calculate the precise molecular mass of C₂H₆O.
5. Calculate the precise molecular mass of CH₂O₂.
6. A molecular ion has m/z = 28.0062. Which of CO, N₂ and C₂H₄ is it?
7. Why do CO₂ and C₃H₈ have different precise masses even though both have Mr 44?
8. A compound of C, H and O gives M⁺ at 72.0573. Is it C₄H₈O (72.0573) or C₃H₄O₂ (72.0210)?
9. Name two isomers that share the molecular formula found in question 8.
10. Compound Q has empirical formula CH₂O and M⁺ at m/z 60.0210. Give its molecular formula.
11. Explain why rounding intermediate totals to one decimal place is a problem.
12. State one thing mass spectrometry does not tell you in this subtopic.

### Answers

1. **m/z to four decimal places**, so it gives the **precise** molecular mass.
2. Carbon-12 is the **standard**: relative masses are defined relative to it.
3. M⁺ has a **1+ charge**, so m/z = mass ÷ 1.
4. 2(12.0000) + 6(1.0078) + 15.9949 = **46.0417**.
5. 12.0000 + 2(1.0078) + 2(15.9949) = **46.0054**.
6. **N₂.**
7. Isotopic masses of H and O are **not whole numbers**: C₃H₈ (44.0624) has many H atoms above whole number; CO₂ (43.9898) has O atoms below.
8. **C₄H₈O.**
9. Any two of: **butanal, butanone, 2-methylpropanal** (or an unsaturated alcohol such as but-3-en-1-ol).
10. (CH₂O)₂ = **C₂H₄O₂** (precise mass 60.0210 confirms).
11. Candidates can differ only in the **third or fourth decimal place**, so rounding can make them **look equal**.
12. The **structure** (or which isomer, or which functional groups).
