These questions cover syllabus section 3.6 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (first assessment 2021): the Pythagorean identity, the double angle identities for sine and cosine, and the relationship between the ratios. All questions are SL and HL, and all are calculator-free, so give exact answers. Learn the methods first in the [3.6 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-6/) and the [3.6 revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-6-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Given that sin θ = 2/3 and θ is obtuse, find the exact value of:

**(a)** cos θ **[2]**

**(b)** sin 2θ **[1]**

**(c)** cos 2θ **[1]**

**2.** Given that cos x = −2/7, find the exact value of cos 2x. **[2]**

**3.** Given that tan A = 5/12 and π < A < 3π/2, find the exact values of sin A and cos A. **[3]**

**4.** Write each expression as a single trigonometric function.

**(a)** 1 − 2sin²3x **[1]**

**(b)** 8 sin x cos x cos 2x **[2]**

**5.** Given that sin θ = k, where 0 < θ < π/2, write each of the following in terms of k.

**(a)** cos θ **[1]**

**(b)** sin 2θ **[1]**

**(c)** cos 2θ **[1]**

**(d)** tan θ **[1]**

**6.** Show that (1 − cos 2x)/(1 + cos 2x) = tan²x, for cos x ≠ 0. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Show that cos 2x + 3 sin x − 2 can be written as −2sin²x + 3 sin x − 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence solve cos 2x + 3 sin x − 2 = 0 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2π. **[4]**

**8.** Let f(x) = (sin x + cos x)².

**(a)** Show that f(x) = 1 + sin 2x. **[2]**

**(b)** Hence write down the range of f. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the period of f. **[1]**

**9.** Given that cos 2θ = 1/8 and π/2 < θ < π, find the exact value of:

**(a)** cos θ **[3]**

**(b)** sin θ **[2]**

**(c)** sin 2θ **[1]**

**10.** **(a)** Show that cos⁴θ − sin⁴θ = cos 2θ for all θ. **[3]**

**(b)** Hence find the exact value of cos⁴(π/12) − sin⁴(π/12). **[2]**

**11.** An isosceles triangle has two sides of length 10 cm, and the angle between them is 2θ, where 0 < θ < π/2.

**(a)** Show that the area of the triangle is 100 sin θ cos θ cm². **[2]**

**(b)** Given that cos θ = 0.8, find the area. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the value of θ for which the area is greatest, and write down the greatest area. **[2]**

**12.** Given that sin x = 1/3 and x is acute, find the exact value of:

**(a)** cos 2x **[1]**

**(b)** sin 2x **[2]**

**(c)** cos 4x **[2]**

**(d)** sin 4x **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** cos²θ = 1 − 4/9 = 5/9 [1]; θ is obtuse so cos θ < 0: **cos θ = −√5/3** [1]

**(b)** sin 2θ = 2(2/3)(−√5/3) = **−4√5/9** [1]

**(c)** cos 2θ = 1 − 2(4/9) = **1/9** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The positive root in (a) loses the accuracy mark there and is followed through in (b); (c) does not depend on the sign.

**2.** cos 2x = 2cos²x − 1 = 2(4/49) − 1 [1] = **−41/49** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Choosing 2cos²x − 1 avoids finding sin x at all, and no quadrant is needed.

**3.** The hypotenuse of the 5, 12 triangle is 13 [1]. A is in the third quadrant, where sin and cos are both negative: **sin A = −5/13** [1] and **cos A = −12/13** [1]

**4. (a)** **cos 6x** [1]

**(b)** 8 sin x cos x = 4 sin 2x [1], so the expression is 4 sin 2x cos 2x = **2 sin 4x** [1]

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the double angle identity is used twice, once with angle x and once with angle 2x.

**5. (a)** θ is acute, so cos θ = **√(1 − k²)** [1]

**(b)** **2k√(1 − k²)** [1]

**(c)** **1 − 2k²** [1]

**(d)** **k/√(1 − k²)** [1]

**6.** 1 − cos 2x = 2sin²x [1] and 1 + cos 2x = 2cos²x [1], so the left-hand side is 2sin²x/2cos²x = sin²x/cos²x = **tan²x** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Picking the form of cos 2x that cancels the 1 in each bracket is the step being rewarded; any other form makes the algebra much longer.

**7. (a)** cos 2x = 1 − 2sin²x [1], so cos 2x + 3 sin x − 2 = 1 − 2sin²x + 3 sin x − 2 = **−2sin²x + 3 sin x − 1** [1]

**(b)** −2sin²x + 3 sin x − 1 = 0 gives 2sin²x − 3 sin x + 1 = 0 [1], so (2 sin x − 1)(sin x − 1) = 0 [1]. sin x = 1/2 gives **x = π/6, 5π/6** [1]; sin x = 1 gives **x = π/2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Each root of the quadratic carries its own accuracy mark; x = π/2 is the one most often forgotten.

**8. (a)** (sin x + cos x)² = sin²x + 2 sin x cos x + cos²x [1] = 1 + sin 2x, using cos²x + sin²x = 1 and sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x [1]

**(b)** −1 ≤ sin 2x ≤ 1 [1], so **0 ≤ f(x) ≤ 2** [1]

**(c)** **π** [1]

*Examiner insight:* The period in (c) is that of sin 2x, which is 2π/2; the answer 2π is a common slip.

**9. (a)** 2cos²θ − 1 = 1/8 [1], so cos²θ = 9/16 [1]; θ is in the second quadrant: **cos θ = −3/4** [1]

**(b)** sin²θ = 1 − 9/16 = 7/16 [1]; sin is positive in the second quadrant: **sin θ = √7/4** [1]

**(c)** sin 2θ = 2(√7/4)(−3/4) = **−3√7/8** [1]

**10. (a)** cos⁴θ − sin⁴θ = (cos²θ − sin²θ)(cos²θ + sin²θ) [1] = (cos²θ − sin²θ) × 1 [1] = **cos 2θ** [1]

**(b)** By (a), the expression equals cos(2 × π/12) = cos(π/6) [1] = **√3/2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* "Hence" in (b) means using (a); expanding fourth powers of cos(π/12) by another route scores no method marks.

**11. (a)** Area = ½ × 10 × 10 × sin 2θ = 50 sin 2θ [1] = 50 × 2 sin θ cos θ = **100 sin θ cos θ** [1]

**(b)** θ is acute, so sin θ = 0.6 [1]; area = 100(0.6)(0.8) = **48 cm²** [1]

**(c)** Area = 50 sin 2θ is greatest when 2θ = π/2, so **θ = π/4** [1], and the greatest area is **50 cm²** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Writing the area as 50 sin 2θ makes (c) immediate; trying to maximise sin θ cos θ without the identity needs calculus.

**12. (a)** cos 2x = 1 − 2(1/9) = **7/9** [1]

**(b)** cos x = √(1 − 1/9) = 2√2/3 [1], so sin 2x = 2(1/3)(2√2/3) = **4√2/9** [1]

**(c)** cos 4x = 2cos²2x − 1 = 2(49/81) − 1 [1] = **17/81** [1]

**(d)** sin 4x = 2 sin 2x cos 2x = 2(4√2/9)(7/9) [1] = **56√2/81** [1]

*Examiner insight:* A quick check is (56√2/81)² + (17/81)² = (6272 + 289)/6561 = 1, so (c) and (d) are consistent.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the positive square root when the quadrant makes the ratio negative, as in questions 1, 3 and 9.
- Writing sin 2θ = 2 sin θ or cos 2θ = 2 cos θ.
- Choosing a form of cos 2θ that does not cancel, which turns a three-line proof into a page.
- Losing a root of a quadratic in sin x, such as sin x = 1 in question 7.
- Decimal answers where exact values are required.
