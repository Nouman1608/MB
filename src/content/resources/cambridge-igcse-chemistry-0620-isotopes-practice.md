## Section A

**1.** Define the term *isotopes*. **[2]**

**2.** Which pair of atoms are isotopes of each other?

A: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar and ⁴⁰₂₀Ca

B: ²⁰₁₀Ne and ²²₁₀Ne

C: ²³₁₁Na and ²³₁₁Na⁺

D: ¹⁴₆C and ¹⁴₇N

**[1]**

**3.** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in:

**(a)** an atom of ³²₁₆S **[3]**

**(b)** an atom of ³⁴₁₆S. **[3]**

**4.** A particle has 12 protons, 13 neutrons and 10 electrons. Write its symbol, showing its mass number, proton number and charge. Magnesium has proton number 12. **[2]**

**5.** State the difference in the nuclei of ²⁰₁₀Ne and ²²₁₀Ne. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended only)* Explain why lithium-6 and lithium-7 react with water in the same way. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Extended only)* Rubidium is approximately 72.2% rubidium-85 and 27.8% rubidium-87. Calculate the relative atomic mass of rubidium to one decimal place. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended only)* Sulfur is approximately 95.0% sulfur-32, 0.8% sulfur-33 and 4.2% sulfur-34.

**(a)** Show that the percentages add up to 100. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of sulfur to one decimal place. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended only)* A sample of element Q contains 250 atoms: 190 atoms of Q-120 and 60 atoms of Q-122. Calculate the relative atomic mass of Q to one decimal place. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows the particles in four atoms or ions, A to D.

| Particle | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 8 | 8 | 8 |
| B | 8 | 10 | 8 |
| C | 8 | 8 | 10 |
| D | 9 | 10 | 9 |

**(a)** Which two particles are atoms that are isotopes of each other? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Which particle is an ion? Give its charge. **[2]**

**(c)** State the mass number of D. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended only)* Potassium is approximately 93.3% potassium-39 and 6.7% potassium-41. A student calculates its relative atomic mass as (39 + 41) ÷ 2 = 40.0.

**(a)** Explain the student's mistake. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct relative atomic mass to one decimal place. **[2]**

**12.** A student says: "⁴⁰₁₈Ar and ⁴⁰₂₀Ca are isotopes because they have the same mass number." Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**13.** Hydrogen has three isotopes: ¹₁H, ²₁H and ³₁H. For each isotope, state the number of protons, neutrons and electrons in one atom. **[3]**

**14.** *(Extended only)* An invented element X has two isotopes, X-6 and X-7. A sample contains 2 atoms of X-6 for every 3 atoms of X-7. Calculate the relative atomic mass of X. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Different atoms of the same element that have the same number of protons [1] but different numbers of neutrons [1].

**2.** B [1].

**3. (a)** Protons 16 [1]; neutrons 32 − 16 = 16 [1]; electrons 16 [1].

**(b)** Protons 16 [1]; neutrons 34 − 16 = 18 [1]; electrons 16 [1]. **[6]**

**4.** ²⁵₁₂Mg²⁺: mass number 25 and proton number 12 [1]; charge 2+ [1].

**5.** ²²₁₀Ne has two more neutrons (12) than ²⁰₁₀Ne (10); the number of protons is the same [1].

**6.** Both isotopes have the same number of electrons (three), so they have the same electronic configuration, 2,1 [1]. Chemical reactions involve the electrons, so they have the same chemical properties [1].

**7.** (85 × 72.2 + 87 × 27.8) ÷ 100 = (6137 + 2418.6) ÷ 100 [1] = 85.556 = **85.6** [1].

**8. (a)** 95.0 + 0.8 + 4.2 = 100.0 [1].

**(b)** (32 × 95.0 + 33 × 0.8 + 34 × 4.2) ÷ 100 = (3040 + 26.4 + 142.8) ÷ 100 [1] = 32.092 = **32.1** [1]. **[3]**

**9.** (120 × 190 + 122 × 60) [1] = 22 800 + 7320 = 30 120 [1]; 30 120 ÷ 250 = 120.48 = **120.5** [1].

**10. (a)** A and B [1]: both have 8 protons, so they are the same element, but they have different numbers of neutrons (8 and 10) [1].

**(b)** C [1]; it has two more electrons than protons, so its charge is 2− [1].

**(c)** 9 + 10 = **19** [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** The student took a simple average and ignored the abundances; potassium-39 is far more common [1].

**(b)** (39 × 93.3 + 41 × 6.7) ÷ 100 = (3638.7 + 274.7) ÷ 100 [1] = 39.134 = **39.1** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Isotopes must be atoms of the same element, with the same number of protons [1]. Argon has 18 protons and calcium has 20, so they are different elements; having the same mass number does not make them isotopes [1].

**13.** ¹₁H: 1 proton, 0 neutrons, 1 electron [1]. ²₁H: 1 proton, 1 neutron, 1 electron [1]. ³₁H: 1 proton, 2 neutrons, 1 electron [1].

**14.** (6 × 2 + 7 × 3) ÷ (2 + 3) [1] = 33 ÷ 5 = **6.6** [1].

*Examiner insight:* with a ratio, divide by the sum of the ratio numbers, not by 100. Check that the answer lies nearer 7, the more common isotope.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Incomplete definitions that do not mention the same element or the same number of protons.
- Saying isotopes have different numbers of electrons.
- Treating an ion as an isotope.
- Dividing by 100 when abundance is given as numbers of atoms.
- Taking a simple average instead of weighting by abundance.
