AQA GCSE Business (8132) is assessed through two written papers, not tiered: Paper 1 (Influences on
operations and HRM on business activity, 1h45, 90 marks, 50%) and Paper 2 (Influences of marketing and
finance on business activity, 1h45, 90 marks, 50%). These notes turn that structure into an exam-day
plan, alongside the
[Business in the Real World study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-business-in-the-real-world/) and
[Technology and Ethical Practice revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-business-technology-ethical-revision-notes/)
already on the site.

## Know which topics sit under each paper before you build a revision plan

Paper 1 focuses on operations and human resources influences on business activity; Paper 2 focuses on
marketing and finance influences. **Exam-preparation priority**: map your studied topics to Paper 1 or
Paper 2 explicitly before building a revision timetable, since business topics can sound similar
across papers (for example, cash flow appears under Paper 2's finance content, while workforce
planning appears under Paper 1's HRM content) and misallocating revision time to the wrong paper is a
preventable error.

## AO1, AO2 and AO3: know which skill a question is testing

Both papers are marked against roughly the same three assessment objectives: AO1 (knowledge and
understanding, ~35%), AO2 (application of business concepts to a given context, ~35%), and AO3
(analysis and evaluation of business issues and decisions, ~30%). **Exam-preparation priority**:
because AO2 and AO3 together outweigh AO1, practise applying business concepts to an unfamiliar
case-study business specifically, not just defining the concepts in the abstract.

## Case-study data-response questions reward context, not generic answers

Each paper is built around case-study material, and higher-tariff questions expect an answer that
engages directly with the specific business described, not a generic, could-apply-to-any-business
answer. **Exam-preparation priority**: when practising extended-response questions, deliberately name
details from the case study (the business's product, market, size, or stated problem) within the
answer itself, rather than writing an answer that reads as pre-learned and unconnected to the specific
scenario given.

## Worked practice scenario: a context-specific extended-response plan

Question: "A small, local bakery is considering whether to expand by opening a second shop in a
nearby town. Recommend whether the bakery should expand, justifying your answer."

```
Step 1 - identify a relevant business concept:
Expansion carries both benefits (economies of scale, increased market
reach) and risks (overstretching cash flow, losing the personal,
local-community identity that may be part of the bakery's appeal).

Step 2 - apply it explicitly to THIS business's context:
Because the business is described as small and local, its appeal may
depend partly on personal service and community reputation -- a
second shop in a different town would not automatically carry that
same reputation, unlike a national chain opening a new branch.

Step 3 - consider a specific risk named in the context:
A small bakery expanding into a second location also risks stretching
its cash flow, since a new shop requires upfront investment before it
generates its own revenue.

Step 4 - reach a context-justified recommendation:
Given the bakery's size and reliance on local reputation, expansion
could be justified IF it first tests demand in the new town (e.g.
through a market stall or pop-up) before committing to a full second
shop -- reducing the cash-flow risk while assessing whether the
reputation transfers.
```

Every step is explicitly tied back to the described business (small, local, bakery) rather than
generic expansion theory that could apply to any business -- exactly what case-study-based questions
reward.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exam**: map studied topics to Paper 1 or Paper 2 explicitly; practise applying business
  concepts to unfamiliar case-study businesses, not just defining them; practise naming specific case-
  study details (product, market, size, stated problem) within extended-response answers.
- **During Paper 1**: check whether a question is drawing on operations or HRM content, since both
  are assessed together on this paper.
- **During Paper 2**: check whether a question is drawing on marketing or finance content, since both
  are assessed together on this paper.
- **On every paper**: for extended-response questions, name a specific detail from the case study
  within your answer, not just the underlying business theory.

## Self-test

1. Why is it worth mapping studied topics to Paper 1 or Paper 2 before revising?
2. Which two assessment objectives, combined, roughly match or slightly outweigh AO1?
3. Why do case-study data-response questions reward context-specific answers over generic ones?
4. In the worked scenario, why is the recommendation to test demand first, rather than committing
   immediately to a full second shop?

**Answers:** 1. Because business topics can sound similar across papers (for example, cash flow under
Paper 2's finance content versus workforce planning under Paper 1's HRM content), so misallocating
revision time to the wrong paper is a preventable, avoidable error. 2. AO2 (application) and AO3
(analysis and evaluation), together roughly 65% of the marks against AO1's roughly 35%. 3. Because
each paper is built around specific case-study material, and higher-tariff questions expect an answer
that engages directly with the described business's context, not a generic answer that could apply to
any business. 4. Because the business is described as small and local, its appeal may depend partly on
reputation that doesn't automatically transfer to a new town -- testing demand first (e.g. via a
market stall) reduces the cash-flow risk of a full second shop while checking whether that reputation
does transfer, a more context-justified recommendation than committing immediately.
