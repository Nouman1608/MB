These notes condense section 3.1.1, Solids, liquids and gases, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [solids, liquids and gases study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-solids-liquids-and-gases/); then try the [solids, liquids and gases practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-solids-liquids-and-gases-practice/).

## What the specification asks

- Classify matter into the three states.
- Name each inter-conversion process.
- Describe and explain inter-conversions using particle arrangement and movement.
- Understand the energy changes that accompany changes of state.
- Know simple diffusion experiments as evidence for particles: Br₂/air, NH₃/HCl, KMnO₄/water.

## The three states

| | Solid | Liquid | Gas |
|---|---|---|---|
| Arrangement | regular, touching | random, touching | random, far apart |
| Movement | vibrate in place | slide past each other | fast, in all directions |
| Shape | fixed | takes shape of container | fills container |
| Volume | fixed | fixed | fills container |
| Compressible? | no | no (barely) | yes |

## Changes of state

```
           melting →          boiling / evaporating →
   SOLID ─────────────► LIQUID ──────────────────────► GAS
         ◄─────────────        ◄──────────────────────
           ← freezing                 ← condensing

   SOLID ──────────── sublimation ────────────────────► GAS
```

- Energy **taken in**: melting, boiling, evaporating, sublimation.
- Energy **given out**: freezing, condensing.
- Melting point = freezing point. Boiling point = condensing point.
- **Evaporation** happens at the surface below the boiling point; **boiling** happens throughout the liquid at the boiling point.

## Explaining a change: energy, forces, particles

Use all three in an "explain" answer.

| Change | Energy | Forces | Particles afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|
| Melting | taken in | some overcome | move around each other, still close |
| Boiling | taken in | almost all overcome | far apart, moving randomly |
| Condensing | given out | particles pulled together | close, moving around each other |
| Freezing | given out | particles held in place | fixed positions, vibrating |

Stronger forces between particles → more energy needed → higher melting and boiling points.

## Heating curve

1. Temperature rises (solid).
2. **Flat** at melting point: energy overcomes forces.
3. Temperature rises (liquid).
4. **Flat** at boiling point.
5. Temperature rises (gas).

Flat sections: two states present; temperature constant.

## Predicting state from data

- Temperature < melting point → **solid**.
- Melting point < temperature < boiling point → **liquid**.
- Temperature > boiling point → **gas**.

Example: a substance melts at −39 °C and boils at 357 °C. At 20 °C it is a **liquid**.

## Diffusion evidence

| Experiment | Observation | Conclusion |
|---|---|---|
| Bromine / air | brown colour spreads through the jar without stirring | gas particles move randomly |
| KMnO₄ / water | purple colour spreads slowly through still water | liquid particles move; slower than gases |
| NH₃ / HCl in a tube | white ring of ammonium chloride, nearer the HCl end | lighter NH₃ (Mr 17) moves faster than HCl (Mr 36.5) |

Diffusion: net movement of particles from high to low concentration, caused by random motion. It does not happen through a solid, because the particles cannot move from place to place.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Melting point | temperature at which a solid melts (and the liquid freezes) |
| Boiling point | temperature at which a liquid boils (and the gas condenses) |
| Evaporation | liquid turning to gas at the surface, below the boiling point |
| Sublimation | solid turning directly into gas |
| Diffusion | spreading of particles from high to low concentration by random movement |
| Forces between particles | attractions that hold particles together; overcome during melting and boiling |
| Particle model | the idea that all matter is made of tiny moving particles |

## Exam traps

- Particles do not expand, melt or change colour; the substance does.
- Solid particles **vibrate**; they do not stay still.
- Temperature stays **constant** during a change of state.
- Boiling a simple molecular substance overcomes forces **between** molecules, not covalent bonds within them.
- In the NH₃/HCl tube, the ring forms **nearer the HCl**, because HCl is heavier and slower.
- Name the change *and* say whether energy is taken in or given out.

## Self-test

1. Describe the arrangement and movement of particles in a liquid.
2. Name the change from gas to liquid.
3. Is energy taken in or given out when a liquid freezes?
4. Why does the temperature stay constant while ice melts?
5. A substance melts at 44 °C and boils at 280 °C. State its state at 20 °C and at 100 °C.
6. What is sublimation?
7. Why can a gas be compressed but a liquid cannot?
8. Give one difference between evaporation and boiling.
9. What does the spreading of a purple colour through still water show?
10. In the NH₃/HCl experiment, why does the white ring not form in the middle of the tube?
11. Why is diffusion slower in a liquid than in a gas?
12. Substance P has a higher boiling point than substance Q. What does this suggest about the forces between their particles?

## Answers

1. Close together in a random arrangement; they move around and past each other.
2. Condensing.
3. Given out.
4. The energy is used to overcome the forces between particles, not to raise the temperature.
5. Solid at 20 °C; liquid at 100 °C.
6. A solid changing directly into a gas without becoming a liquid.
7. Gas particles are far apart with spaces between them; liquid particles are already touching.
8. Evaporation happens only at the surface and below the boiling point; boiling happens throughout the liquid at the boiling point.
9. Particles in a liquid move randomly and mix (diffusion).
10. Ammonia particles are lighter and move faster, so ammonia travels further before the gases meet.
11. Liquid particles are close together and collide constantly, so they spread out more slowly; gas particles move freely and quickly.
12. The forces between the particles of P are stronger than those between the particles of Q.
