This guide covers subtopic **11.2 Heart** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to identify the structures of the mammalian heart (the muscular wall, the septum, the left and right ventricles and atria, the atrioventricular and semilunar valves and the coronary arteries), to explain the relative thickness of the muscle walls, to describe the heartbeat in terms of muscle contraction and the action of the valves, to state that blood leaves the heart in arteries and returns in veins, to state how heart activity may be monitored, to investigate and explain the effect of physical activity on heart rate, to describe coronary heart disease and its risk factors, and to discuss the role of diet and exercise in reducing the risk.

## Structure of the heart

The heart is a hollow organ made of **cardiac muscle**. It has four chambers.

| Structure | Description and function |
|---|---|
| Muscular wall | Cardiac muscle that contracts to pump blood |
| Septum | Wall of muscle between the left and right sides; stops oxygenated and deoxygenated blood mixing |
| Right atrium | Receives deoxygenated blood from the body (via the vena cava) |
| Right ventricle | Pumps deoxygenated blood to the lungs (via the pulmonary artery) |
| Left atrium | Receives oxygenated blood from the lungs (via the pulmonary vein) |
| Left ventricle | Pumps oxygenated blood to the body (via the aorta) |
| Atrioventricular (AV) valves | Between each atrium and ventricle; stop blood flowing back into the atria |
| Semilunar valves | At the start of the pulmonary artery and aorta; stop blood flowing back into the ventricles |
| Coronary arteries | Run over the surface of the heart; supply the heart muscle with oxygen and glucose |

The left side of a diagram appears on the right of the page, because you view the heart as if facing the person. Always label from the person's point of view.

### Why the heart needs its own blood supply

The heart muscle works all the time and needs a continuous supply of oxygen and glucose for aerobic respiration. The blood inside the chambers does not supply the muscle; the **coronary arteries** do. If a coronary artery is blocked, part of the muscle is starved of oxygen.

## Thickness of the muscle walls

The thickness of a chamber wall is related to the **pressure** that the chamber must generate.

| Comparison | Explanation |
|---|---|
| **Left ventricle** thicker than right ventricle | The left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body, a long way, and must produce a high pressure. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the nearby lungs, where a high pressure could damage the capillaries. |
| **Ventricle walls** thicker than atrium walls | The ventricles pump blood out of the heart, to the lungs or to the whole body. The atria only push blood a short distance into the ventricles, so they need less force. |

A good answer states the pressure, the distance and the destination. "It pumps blood further" on its own does not explain the need for pressure.

## The heartbeat

One heartbeat has a repeating sequence. The valves open and close because of differences in pressure, not because of muscles in the valves.

1. **Atria contract.** Blood is squeezed from the atria into the ventricles. The AV valves are pushed open.
2. **Ventricles contract.** The pressure in the ventricles rises above the pressure in the atria, so the **AV valves shut** (making the first heart sound, "lub"). This stops blood flowing back into the atria.
3. The pressure in the ventricles rises above that in the arteries, so the **semilunar valves open**, and the blood is forced out into the pulmonary artery and aorta.
4. **Ventricles relax.** The pressure falls below that in the arteries, so the **semilunar valves shut** (the second sound, "dub"). This stops blood from flowing back into the ventricles.
5. The atria fill with blood again, the AV valves open, and the cycle repeats.

**Blood leaves the heart in arteries and returns to the heart in veins.**

## Monitoring the heart

Heart activity may be monitored in three ways.

- **ECG (electrocardiogram)**: electrodes on the skin detect the electrical activity of the heart, which is shown as a trace.
- **Pulse rate**: the pulse is felt at the wrist or neck. Each pulse corresponds to a heartbeat, so the number of pulses per minute is the heart rate.
- **Listening to the sounds of the valves closing**, using a stethoscope.

### Worked example: pulse rate

*A student counted 19 pulses in 15 seconds. Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute.* There are 60 seconds in a minute, so 60 divided by 15 = 4. Then 19 x 4 = **76 beats per minute**. Counting for a longer time and repeating gives a more reliable result.

## The effect of physical activity on heart rate

**Investigation**: measure the resting pulse rate, then ask the person to exercise for a set time, such as 2 minutes, and measure the pulse rate straight afterwards and every minute until it returns to the resting value.

**Result**: the heart rate rises during exercise and falls gradually afterwards.

**Explanation**: during exercise, the muscles respire faster, so they need more oxygen and glucose, and produce more carbon dioxide. A faster heart rate pumps blood more quickly, delivering oxygen and glucose faster and removing carbon dioxide and heat. After exercise, the heart rate stays high for a while to remove lactic acid (see subtopic 10.3).

### Worked example: output of the heart

*At rest, the heart beats 70 times per minute and pumps 75 cm cubed of blood per beat. During hard exercise, it beats 150 times per minute and pumps 100 cm cubed of blood per beat.*

1. At rest: 70 x 75 = 5250 cm cubed per minute = **5.25 dm cubed per minute**.
2. During exercise: 150 x 100 = 15 000 cm cubed per minute = **15.0 dm cubed per minute**.
3. Ratio = 15.0 divided by 5.25 = **2.9 times** as much.

(The volume per beat and the amount pumped per minute are not named in the syllabus, but this type of calculation tests data skills.)

## Coronary heart disease

**Coronary heart disease (CHD)** is caused by the **blockage of the coronary arteries**. Fatty deposits (containing cholesterol) build up on the inside of the arteries and make them narrower. Blood may clot at the narrowed place and block it completely. The part of the heart muscle that the artery supplies receives too little oxygen and glucose, so it cannot respire aerobically, and may be damaged or die. This is a **heart attack**.

### Risk factors

| Risk factor | Why it raises the risk |
|---|---|
| Diet high in saturated fat | Fatty deposits build up in the arteries |
| Sedentary (inactive) lifestyle | Weaker heart, higher blood pressure, and a tendency to gain mass |
| Stress | Raises blood pressure and heart rate |
| Smoking | Carbon monoxide and nicotine damage the arteries and raise blood pressure |
| Genetic predisposition | Some people inherit a greater tendency |
| Age | Risk increases as the arteries become narrower with age |
| Gender | Men have a higher risk at a younger age than women |

### Reducing the risk: diet and exercise

- **Diet**: eat less saturated fat and salt, and more fruit, vegetables and fibre. This reduces the fatty deposits and lowers the blood pressure.
- **Exercise**: regular exercise strengthens the heart muscle, lowers blood pressure, helps to control body mass and reduces stress.
- Not smoking and managing stress also help.

**Discuss questions** ask for balance. Describe the role of diet and exercise, say that they reduce but do not remove the risk, and note that age, gender and genes cannot be changed.

## Common mistakes

- Labelling the left ventricle on the left of the page.
- Saying that the right ventricle is thinner "because it is smaller". The reason is the lower pressure needed.
- Saying that the valves are closed by muscles.
- Saying that the AV valves stop blood leaving the heart in the arteries.
- Writing that the coronary arteries carry blood into the chambers of the heart.
- Saying that CHD is a disease of the veins.
- Saying that exercise "makes the heart tired" and so raises the rate.

## Command words

- **Identify**: name the chambers, valves and vessels on a diagram.
- **Explain**: give the reason for the wall thickness or the change in heart rate.
- **Describe**: give the sequence of a heartbeat or the cause of CHD.
- **Discuss**: give the role of diet and exercise, with balanced points.
