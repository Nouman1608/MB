These questions cover sub-topic **10.2 Antibiotics** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027 (AS Level, Paper 1 and Paper 2 style). The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. All data are invented for practice. Calculators are allowed.

Revise first with the [antibiotics study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-antibiotics/) and the [antibiotics revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-antibiotics-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Which process does penicillin directly prevent?

A: breaking of bonds in peptidoglycan

B: formation of cross-links in peptidoglycan

C: protein synthesis on 70S ribosomes

D: replication of plasmid DNA **[1]**

**2.** Why do antibiotics have no effect on HIV?

A: HIV has a peptidoglycan wall that antibiotics cannot cross

B: HIV is too small to absorb the antibiotic

C: HIV lacks the structures and metabolic processes that antibiotics target

D: HIV is destroyed by the immune system before antibiotics can act **[1]**

**3.** State why penicillin does not harm human cells. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which a resistance gene can be passed on in a bacterial population. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a doctor would not prescribe an antibiotic for a common cold. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Outline how penicillin kills bacteria. **[5]**

**7.** A culture of bacteria was divided in two. Penicillin was added to one half while the bacteria were dividing rapidly, and to the other half after the bacteria had stopped growing. Predict and explain the result in each half. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how a population of bacteria can become resistant to an antibiotic. **[5]**

**9.** A hospital recorded the percentage of *Staphylococcus aureus* samples resistant to one antibiotic, and the amount of that antibiotic used per year.

| Year | Antibiotic used / kg | Samples resistant / % |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 40 | 8 |
| 3 | 55 | 14 |
| 5 | 70 | 23 |
| 7 | 36 | 19 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in resistance between year 1 and year 5.

**(b)** Describe the relationship between antibiotic use and resistance shown by the data.

**(c)** Suggest why resistance fell only slightly after year 5 even though use fell sharply. **[6]**

**10.** Discuss the consequences of antibiotic resistance for health care. **[4]**

**11.** Discs containing four antibiotics, each 6 mm in diameter, were placed on a lawn of bacteria. After incubation the diameters of the clear zones were measured.

| Antibiotic | W | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Clear zone diameter / mm | 18 | 30 | 6 | 22 |

**(a)** Calculate the area of the clear zone around X.

**(b)** State which antibiotic the bacteria are resistant to, and explain your answer.

**(c)** Suggest why the antibiotic with the largest clear zone may not be the best one to prescribe. **[5]**

**12.** Explain how each of the following reduces the impact of antibiotic resistance.

**(a)** Testing which antibiotic a bacterium is sensitive to before prescribing.

**(b)** Reducing the use of antibiotics to promote growth in farm animals.

**(c)** Isolating patients infected with resistant bacteria in hospital. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Human cells have no cell wall (no peptidoglycan) [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Vertically, by binary fission to offspring [1]; horizontally, on plasmids passed to other bacteria (conjugation) [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** A cold is caused by a virus [1]; antibiotics do not affect viruses, and unnecessary use selects for resistant bacteria [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Bacterial cell walls are made of peptidoglycan with peptide cross-links [1]; as bacteria grow, enzymes break bonds in the wall and new cross-links are formed by transpeptidases [1]; penicillin inhibits these enzymes, so cross-links do not form [1]; the wall is weakened [1]; water enters by osmosis and the cell bursts (osmotic lysis) [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**7.** Rapidly dividing half: many bacteria killed [1]; because they are making new wall, which cannot be cross-linked, so they burst [1]. Non-growing half: few or no bacteria killed [1]; because they are not making new wall, so penicillin has nothing to act on [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**8.** A random mutation produces an allele giving resistance (e.g. a gene for penicillinase) [1]; when the antibiotic is used, susceptible bacteria die [1]; resistant bacteria survive and reproduce by binary fission, passing on the allele [1]; resistance genes on plasmids can pass to other bacteria [1]; over time the proportion of resistant bacteria in the population increases [1]. Total: 5 marks.

*Examiner insight:* "the bacteria get used to the antibiotic" or "the antibiotic causes a mutation" scores zero; the mutation is already present before treatment.

**9. (a)** (23 − 8) ÷ 8 × 100 [1] = **187.5 %** (accept 188 %) [1].

**(b)** As antibiotic use increased from year 1 to year 5, the percentage resistant increased (positive correlation) [1]; when use fell in year 7, resistance fell [1].

**(c)** Any two from: resistant bacteria are still present and continue to reproduce and spread; other antibiotics may also select for the same resistance genes; resistance genes on plasmids remain in the population; carrying the resistance gene may be of little disadvantage, so it is not quickly lost [2]. Total: 6 marks.

**10.** Any four, each linked to its significance: infections harder or impossible to treat, so more deaths; longer hospital stays, increasing cost and spread; need for newer drugs, which are more expensive or have more side effects; surgery, cancer treatment or care of premature babies become riskier; TB and other diseases harder to control [4]. Total: 4 marks.

**11. (a)** r = 30 ÷ 2 = 15 mm [1]; area = π × 15² = **707 mm²** [1].

**(b)** Y [1]; the clear zone is the same diameter as the disc, so there is no inhibition of growth [1].

**(c)** Any one: a large zone may reflect faster diffusion through agar rather than greater effectiveness; it may have side effects or be harmful to the patient; it may be a broad-spectrum antibiotic, increasing selection for resistance in other bacteria; it may not reach the site of infection in the body [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**12. (a)** An effective, narrow-spectrum antibiotic can be chosen, so fewer other bacteria are exposed and selection for resistance is reduced [1].

**(b)** Less selection pressure in animal bacteria, so fewer resistant bacteria (or resistance genes) pass to humans [1].

**(c)** Prevents resistant bacteria spreading to other patients [1]. Total: 3 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- "Penicillin breaks down the wall" instead of "prevents cross-links".
- Lysis without osmosis.
- Antibiotics causing mutation, or bacteria "becoming immune".
- Using the diameter as the radius.
- Listing steps without how each reduces selection or spread.
