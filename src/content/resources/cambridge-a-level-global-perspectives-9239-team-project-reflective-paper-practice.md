These questions practise the reflective paper in Component 3, the Team Project, of Cambridge International AS & A Level Global Perspectives & Research (9239), 2026-2028, which focuses on "how effectively the team worked together, what could be improved, and any changes to their personal views shaped by the collaborative experience". For the component as a whole, see the [Team Project study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-global-perspectives-team-project/).

> **These are original questions written for Marlbridge.** The reflective paper is coursework, so these questions practise reflective writing skills; your own paper must reflect your own team's experience. All teams, names and events below are invented. Marks are for practice only.

Revise first with the [reflective paper study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-global-perspectives-9239-team-project-reflective-paper/) and the [reflective paper revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-global-perspectives-9239-team-project-reflective-paper-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the maximum length of the reflective paper and the marks it carries. **[2]**

**2.** Name the assessment objective the paper is assessed on and the **two** strands it assesses. **[3]**

**3.** Name the **three** areas the syllabus says a candidate's learning may be about. **[3]**

## Section B

**4.** A candidate writes: "Our team was great. Everyone got on and we finished on time. I think we could have communicated better. I learned how to research and present."

**(a)** Identify **two** weaknesses in this reflection. **[2]**
**(b)** Rewrite it as effective reflection on collaboration, inventing reasonable details. **[4]**

**5.** Classify each sentence as description, evaluation or improvement. **[3]**

- **(i)** "We met every Tuesday lunchtime in the library."
- **(ii)** "Meeting weekly kept everyone's research on track, so no one fell more than a week behind."
- **(iii)** "Next time we should end each meeting by writing down who will do what before the next one."

**6.** A candidate identifies this weakness: "Decisions were usually made by the two most confident members, and Leo's ideas about local interviews were not discussed." Write an improvement that answers this weakness. **[2]**

**7.** A team spent two weeks disagreeing about its problem statement, but the final statement let each of its four members argue a clearly distinct perspective. Evaluate how effective this part of the collaboration was. **[4]**

**8.** Rewrite this sentence so that it evaluates fairly: "Our project was nearly ruined because Sana never did any work." **[3]**

**9.** A candidate's draft reflective paper is 1,050 words. Explain the problem and suggest **two** ways to cut it without losing reflection. **[3]**

**10.** A candidate's learning section lists eight skills in one paragraph. Explain why this is weaker than depth, and develop one of the skills into a reflective sentence or two. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how working with a teammate's different perspective could change a candidate's personal view, and write an example. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate this paragraph as reflection on collaboration. **[6]**

> "At first our team divided the work by topic, with each member taking one perspective. This worked well for research because everyone knew their area. However, we did not see each other's presentations until the day before submission, so I could not use feedback from the others. I felt stressed about this. Overall our collaboration was good."

## Answers

**1.** Maximum 800 words [1]; 10 marks [1]. **[2]**

**2.** AO2, Reflection [1]. Reflection on the effectiveness of the team collaboration [1]; reflection on the learning [1]. **[3]**

**3.** The problem itself [1]; the research skills developed [1]; presentation skills [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** Any two: general claims with no evidence [1]; no evaluation in relation to the team's goal [1]; "communicated better" is a vague improvement that does not identify the weakness [1]; learning is listed, not explained [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Credit a rewrite that includes a specific event [1], an evaluation of its effect on the team's goal [1], a weakness identified precisely [1] and a specific improvement that answers it [1]. For example: "Setting a shared deadline for first findings in week four kept all four of us on schedule, so each presentation was ready to rehearse a week early. Our weakness was that findings were shared only at meetings, so when Kofi found useful council data on Monday, the rest of us did not see it until Friday. A shared folder with a note for each new source would have made findings available the same day." **[4]**

**5.** (i) description [1]; (ii) evaluation [1]; (iii) improvement [1]. **[3]**

**6.** For example: "At each meeting we could take turns to speak first, and record every suggestion in the notes before deciding [1], so that ideas such as Leo's interviews are discussed rather than lost [1]." **[2]**

**7.** The disagreement cost two weeks, which reduced research time [1]. However, the team's goal at this stage was a statement that supported distinct perspectives, and it achieved this [1], which made each member's individual research and solution easier later [1]. Judgement: effective in outcome but inefficient in process; a time limit or agreed test for the statement could have reached the same result faster [1]. **[4]**

**8.** For example: "Sana's research on local costs did not arrive before our rehearsal [1], which meant two of us had to work without it [1]. We had not agreed a deadline for that section or checked progress mid-week, so I share responsibility for not noticing the problem sooner [1]." **[3]**

**9.** The paper exceeds the 800-word maximum, so it must be cut to meet the requirement [1]. Ways to cut, any two: shorten the description of the research and the problem, which is not assessed here [1]; reduce the number of collaboration aspects to two or three and go deeper on those [1]; remove repeated general statements such as "we worked well" [1]. **[3]**

**10.** A list shows no evaluation of how or why each skill developed or why it matters [1]. Depth on one or two skills shows real reflection on learning [1]. For example: "I learned to check who produced a source before using it: my first draft relied on a figure from a company's advertising, and replacing it with council data made my solution more credible." [1] **[3]**

**11.** A teammate arguing from another perspective can expose an assumption the candidate had not questioned, and so change how the candidate understands the problem or which solution they prefer [1] [1]. Example: "I assumed residents opposed the new bus lane because they disliked change, but Priya's interviews showed most were worried about deliveries to their shops, which changed my view of what a fair solution would need to include." [1] **[3]**

**12.** Indicative content:
- Strength: describes a specific approach and evaluates its effect on research [1].
- Strength: identifies a real weakness and its consequence for the candidate's work [1].
- Weakness: "I felt stressed" describes a feeling without reflecting on its significance [1].
- Weakness: no improvement is suggested, although the weakness points directly to one, such as an earlier rehearsal [1].
- Weakness: the final judgement "good" is not reasoned and does not weigh the strength against the weakness in relation to the team's goal [1].
- Judgement: a promising start that evaluates one strength and one weakness, but incomplete reflection without an improvement or a weighed conclusion [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rewrites that add detail but still contain no evaluation.
- Treating feelings as reflection.
- Improvements that do not answer the weakness described.
- Blame without evidence or without the writer's own role.
- Learning sections that list skills instead of explaining one change in depth.
