These notes condense section **3.12.3 Special relativity** of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408), part of the A-level only Turning points in physics option. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [special relativity study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-special-relativity/); test yourself with the [special relativity practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-special-relativity-practice/).

## Equations

| Equation | Meaning |
|---|---|
| t = t₀/√(1 − v²/c²) | Time dilation; t₀ is the proper time |
| l = l₀√(1 − v²/c²) | Length contraction; l₀ is the proper length |
| E = mc² | Mass-energy equivalence |
| m = m₀/√(1 − v²/c²) | Relativistic mass |
| E = m₀c²/√(1 − v²/c²) | Total energy of a moving particle |
| E_k = mc² − m₀c² | Relativistic kinetic energy |

Useful values: electron rest energy about 0.51 MeV (8.20 × 10⁻¹⁴ J); proton rest energy about 940 MeV. The factor 1/√(1 − v²/c²) is 1.25 at 0.60c, 1.67 at 0.80c, 2.00 at 0.866c, 2.29 at 0.90c.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ether | Hypothetical medium for light, assumed at absolute rest |
| Absolute motion | Motion relative to the ether; shown to be undetectable |
| Inertial frame | Frame in which Newton's first law holds (not accelerating) |
| Invariance of c | Every inertial observer measures the same speed of light in free space |
| Proper time | Interval measured by an observer for whom both events happen at the same place |
| Proper length | Length measured in the object's rest frame |
| Rest mass | Mass measured when the object is at rest |
| Rest energy | m₀c² |

## Michelson-Morley in four lines

1. Semi-silvered plate splits light into two perpendicular beams; mirrors return them; recombined beams give fringes.
2. Motion through the ether should make the times along the arms differ.
3. Rotating the apparatus through 90° should shift the fringes.
4. No shift: absolute motion undetectable, speed of light invariant.

## Postulates

1. Physical laws have the same form in all inertial frames.
2. The speed of light in free space is invariant.

## Muon evidence

- Muons made high in the atmosphere move close to c; proper half-life about 1.5 µs.
- Classically almost none should survive a journey of many kilometres.
- Far more are detected at ground level: their half-life is dilated in the Earth frame.
- In the muon's frame, the atmosphere's thickness is contracted: same prediction.

## Method: a relativity calculation

1. Decide which observer sees both events at the same place: their time is t₀.
2. Decide in which frame the object is at rest: its length there is l₀.
3. Calculate the factor 1/√(1 − v²/c²) once and reuse it.
4. Check the answer: dilated times are longer, contracted lengths shorter.

## Graphs

- Mass–speed: flat at m₀, then rising to infinity as v → c.
- Kinetic energy–speed: matches ½m₀v² at low v, then rises steeply to infinity as v → c (asymptote at c).

## Bertozzi

- Electrons accelerated to known energies; time of flight over a known distance gave speed.
- Energy checked by heating of the target.
- Speed approached but never exceeded c: direct evidence that E_k varies with speed as relativity predicts.

## Exam traps

- Swapping t and t₀: proper time is always the **shorter** interval.
- Swapping l and l₀: proper length is always the **longer** length.
- Contracting dimensions perpendicular to the motion.
- Using ½mv² for relativistic particles.
- Saying Michelson-Morley "detected the ether wind" or measured c.
- Forgetting the 90° rotation in the description.
- Giving E_k = mc² instead of mc² − m₀c².

## Self-test

1. What did the Michelson-Morley experiment expect to see when the apparatus was rotated?
2. State the two postulates of special relativity.
3. Define an inertial frame of reference.
4. Calculate 1/√(1 − v²/c²) for v = 0.60c.
5. A clock on a spacecraft at 0.60c ticks once per second in its own frame. What interval does an observer on Earth measure?
6. A 2.0 m rod moves along its length at 0.80c. What length is measured?
7. A particle with a proper half-life of 20 ns moves at 0.95c. Find its half-life in the laboratory frame.
8. Find the total energy and kinetic energy of an electron at 0.90c (rest energy 0.512 MeV).
9. Find the kinetic energy of a proton at 0.50c (rest energy 939 MeV).
10. Describe the shape of the kinetic energy–speed graph for relativistic particles.
11. Why do more muons reach the ground than classical physics predicts?
12. What did Bertozzi's experiment show?

### Answers

1. A **shift in the interference fringes**.
2. Physical laws have the **same form in all inertial frames**; the speed of light in free space is **invariant**.
3. A frame in which **Newton's first law holds** (moving at constant velocity, not accelerating).
4. 1/√(1 − 0.36) = **1.25**.
5. 1.25 × 1.0 = **1.25 s**.
6. 2.0 × √(1 − 0.64) = **1.2 m**.
7. 1/√(1 − 0.9025) = 3.20, so 20 × 3.20 = **64 ns**.
8. Factor 2.29: total = 2.29 × 0.512 = **1.17 MeV**; E_k = 1.17 − 0.512 = **0.66 MeV**.
9. Factor 1.155: E_k = 0.155 × 939 = **145 MeV**.
10. Close to ½m₀v² at low speeds, then rises **ever more steeply**, tending to infinity as v approaches c.
11. **Time dilation**: in the Earth frame their half-life is many times longer, so far more survive the journey.
12. As kinetic energy increased, electron speed **approached but never exceeded c**, matching the relativistic prediction.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which time is the proper time in a time dilation question?**
The proper time is measured by the observer for whom both events happen at the same place, usually the clock moving with the object. It is always the shorter of the two times.

**Why was the Michelson-Morley null result important?**
It showed that absolute motion through an ether cannot be detected and that the speed of light is the same in every direction regardless of the Earth's motion. Einstein made this invariance a postulate of special relativity.
