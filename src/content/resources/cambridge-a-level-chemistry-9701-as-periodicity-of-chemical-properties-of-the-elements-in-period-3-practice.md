## Section B

**7.** Aluminium oxide is described as amphoteric.

**(a)** State what is meant by *amphoteric*. **[1]**

**(b)** Write equations for the reactions of aluminium oxide with hydrochloric acid and with aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the amphoteric behaviour of aluminium oxide in terms of its bonding. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why sodium oxide is a basic oxide. Include an ionic equation in your answer. **[2]**

**9.** Describe and explain the difference in what happens when sodium chloride and silicon tetrachloride are separately added to water. Include an equation and the likely pH in each case. **[5]**

**10.** A solution of aluminium chloride in water has a pH of about 3.

**(a)** Write an equation to show how the hydrated aluminium ion releases H⁺ ions. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why magnesium chloride solution is much less acidic. **[2]**

**11.** 3.40 g of SiCl₄ is added to an excess of water.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of SiCl₄. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount of HCl formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of SiO₂ formed. **[1]**

**12.** 1.24 g of Na₂O is dissolved in water. Calculate the volume of 0.500 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid needed to neutralise the solution. **[3]**

**13.** Chloride X is a colourless liquid at room temperature. It fumes in moist air, and its reaction with water gives an acidic solution and a white solid that has a very high melting point.

**(a)** Suggest the type of bonding in X, and in the white solid. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂ [1]. Any two of: floats; melts into a ball; fizzes (bubbles of gas); moves about on the surface; disappears [2].

**2. (a)** Mg + 2H₂O → Mg(OH)₂ + H₂ [1].

**(b)** Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂ [1].

**3.** AlCl₃ +3; P₄O₁₀ +5; SO₂ +4; SiCl₄ +4 [2 for all four, 1 for two or three].

**4.** Sulfur has six outer-shell (valence) electrons [1]. In SO₃ all six are used in bonding to the more electronegative oxygen atoms, giving +6 [1].

**5.** Na₂O: about 13–14 [1]. Al₂O₃: 7, because it is insoluble [1]. P₄O₁₀: about 0–2 [1].

**6.** SO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₃ + H₂O [1].

**7. (a)** It reacts with both acids and bases [1].

**(b)** Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O [1]. Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH + 3H₂O → 2NaAl(OH)₄ (accept Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O) [1].

**(c)** Al³⁺ is small and highly charged, so it polarises the oxide ion [1]. The bonding is ionic with considerable covalent character, intermediate between the basic ionic oxides and the acidic covalent oxides [1].

**8.** Sodium oxide is ionic and contains the oxide ion, which accepts protons (acts as a base) [1]. O²⁻ + H₂O → 2OH⁻, or O²⁻ + 2H⁺ → H₂O [1].

**9.** NaCl is ionic [1] and simply dissolves to give hydrated Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; no reaction, pH 7 [1]. SiCl₄ is covalent [1] and is hydrolysed completely: SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl [1]. The solution has a pH of about 1–2, and misty fumes of HCl are seen [1].

**10. (a)** [Al(H₂O)₆]³⁺ ⇌ [Al(H₂O)₅(OH)]²⁺ + H⁺ [1].

**(b)** Mg²⁺ has a lower charge and a larger radius than Al³⁺ [1], so it polarises the surrounding water molecules much less and releases very few H⁺ ions [1].

**11. (a)** Mᵣ(SiCl₄) = 28.1 + 4(35.5) = 170.1; amount = 3.40 ÷ 170.1 = 0.0200 mol [1].

**(b)** 4 × 0.0200 = 0.0800 mol [1].

**(c)** Mᵣ(SiO₂) = 60.1; mass = 0.0200 × 60.1 = 1.20 g [1].

**12.** Amount of Na₂O = 1.24 ÷ 62.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]. Na₂O + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O, so HCl needed = 0.0400 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0400 ÷ 0.500 = 0.0800 dm³ = 80.0 cm³ [1].

**13. (a)** X: simple molecular covalent [1]. White solid: giant covalent [1].

**(b)** Silicon tetrachloride, SiCl₄ [1]. SiCl₄ + 2H₂O → SiO₂ + 4HCl [1].

**14. (a)** Add each solid to water and test with universal indicator [1]. MgO gives a weakly alkaline solution (about pH 9–10, blue); P₄O₁₀ gives a strongly acidic solution (about pH 0–2, red) [1].

**(b)** P₄O₁₀ + 12NaOH → 4Na₃PO₄ + 6H₂O [1].

**(c)** MgO is ionic; its oxide ions accept protons, so it is basic [1]. P₄O₁₀ is a covalent (simple molecular) oxide of a non-metal that reacts with water to form an acid, so it is acidic [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving MgO rather than Mg(OH)₂ for cold water.
- Showing aluminium oxide dissolving in water.
- Giving an oxidation number of +6 for sulfur in SO₂.
- Describing sodium chloride as hydrolysed.
- Using a 1 : 1 ratio for Na₂O and HCl in neutralisation calculations.
