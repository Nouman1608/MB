## Section A

**1.** A car travels forwards along a straight road while its brakes are applied. Which statement is correct?

A: The resultant force and the acceleration are both forwards.

B: The resultant force is backwards and the acceleration is forwards.

C: The resultant force and the acceleration are both backwards.

D: The resultant force is zero because the car is still moving forwards. **[1]**

**2.** Which is **not** a correct unit for momentum?

A: kg m s⁻¹

B: N s

C: N s⁻¹

D: J s m⁻¹ **[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by the statement that mass is the property of an object that resists change in motion. **[1]**

**4.** State Newton's third law of motion. **[2]**

**5.** A 1200 kg car accelerates uniformly from rest to 27 m s⁻¹ in 9.0 s. Calculate the resultant force on the car using force = rate of change of momentum. **[2]**

**6.** A robotic rover has a mass of 15 kg. On Mars the acceleration of free fall is about 3.7 m s⁻². Calculate the rover's weight on Mars and state its mass there. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A child on a sledge has a total mass of 40 kg. The sledge is pulled with a horizontal force of 90 N along level snow. The friction force on the sledge is 30 N.

**(a)** Draw a labelled free-body diagram showing the horizontal forces on the sledge. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the acceleration of the sledge. **[2]**

**(c)** The pulling force is reduced so that the sledge moves at constant velocity. State the new pulling force and explain your answer using Newton's first law. **[2]**

**8.** A 72 kg student stands on bathroom scales in a lift.

**(a)** The scales read 790 N. Calculate the magnitude and direction of the lift's acceleration. **[3]**

**(b)** Later the scales read 600 N. Describe **two** different motions of the lift that would give this reading. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the student's weight does not change during the journey. **[1]**

**9.** A locomotive of mass 50 000 kg pulls two carriages, each of mass 20 000 kg, along a level track. The resultant forward force on the whole train is 45 000 N.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration of the train. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the tension in the coupling between the locomotive and the first carriage. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the tension in the coupling between the two carriages. **[1]**

**10.** A cricket ball of mass 0.16 kg moving at 25 m s⁻¹ is hit straight back along its path at 35 m s⁻¹. The ball is in contact with the bat for 2.0 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the change in momentum of the ball. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the average force exerted by the bat on the ball. **[2]**

**(c)** State the average force exerted by the ball on the bat, and name the law you used. **[1]**

**11.** Water leaves a hose at 15 m s⁻¹ at a rate of 0.80 kg s⁻¹ and hits a vertical wall horizontally.

**(a)** The water stops on hitting the wall. Calculate the force exerted on the wall. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the force would be larger if the water bounced back from the wall. **[2]**

**12.** A book rests on a table. A student says: "The weight of the book and the upward push of the table on the book form a Newton's third law pair."

**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the third-law partner of the book's weight. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **C** [1]. *(J s m⁻¹ = kg m² s⁻² × s ÷ m = kg m s⁻¹, so D is correct.)* **[1]**

**3.** A larger mass needs a larger resultant force to produce the same change in velocity in the same time (it has greater inertia) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** If body A exerts a force on body B, then body B exerts a force on body A [1] that is equal in magnitude and opposite in direction (and of the same type) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Δp = 1200 × 27 − 0 = 32 400 kg m s⁻¹ [1]; F = 32 400 ÷ 9.0 = **3600 N** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** W = 15 × 3.7 = **56 N** (55.5 N) [1]; the mass is still **15 kg** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Diagram: 90 N arrow forwards and 30 N arrow backwards, both acting on the sledge and labelled [1].

**(b)** Resultant = 90 − 30 = 60 N [1]; a = 60 ÷ 40 = **1.5 m s⁻²** [1].

**(c)** **30 N** [1]: constant velocity means zero acceleration, so by the first law the resultant force must be zero, and the pull must balance the friction [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Weight = 72 × 9.81 = 706 N [1]; resultant = 790 − 706 = 84 N upwards [1]; a = 84 ÷ 72 = **1.2 m s⁻² upwards** [1].

**(b)** The acceleration is downwards (a = (706 − 600) ÷ 72 = 1.5 m s⁻²), so either the lift is moving down and speeding up [1], or it is moving up and slowing down [1].

**(c)** Weight is mg, which depends only on the mass and the gravitational field, and neither changes; the scales measure the contact force, not the weight [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** a = 45 000 ÷ 90 000 = **0.50 m s⁻²** [1].

**(b)** The coupling pulls both carriages: mass 40 000 kg [1]; T = 40 000 × 0.50 = **20 000 N** [1].

**(c)** The second coupling pulls only the last carriage: T = 20 000 × 0.50 = **10 000 N** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Taking the final direction as positive: Δp = 0.16 × 35 − 0.16 × (−25) [1] = **9.6 kg m s⁻¹** in the direction of the final velocity [1].

**(b)** F = 9.6 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻³ [1] = **4800 N** [1].

**(c)** 4800 N in the opposite direction, by Newton's third law [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Momentum destroyed per second = 0.80 × 15 [1] = 12 kg m s⁻²; force = **12 N** [1].

**(b)** If the water rebounds, its change in velocity each second is larger (for example from +15 to −5 m s⁻¹, a change of 20 m s⁻¹, rather than 15 m s⁻¹) [1], so the rate of change of momentum, and therefore the force, is larger (16 N in this example) [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** The two forces act on the same body (the book) [1], and they are different types of force (gravitational and contact); a third-law pair acts on two different bodies and is of the same type [1].

**(b)** The gravitational force of the book on the Earth (upwards) [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a single force rather than the resultant force in F = ma.
- Assuming the acceleration is in the direction of motion.
- Forgetting the sign change when a ball reverses direction, giving 1.6 kg m s⁻¹ instead of 9.6 kg m s⁻¹.
- Using the mass of the whole train to find the tension in a coupling.
- Choosing weight and the normal contact force as a third-law pair.
