These questions cover section **3.1.2.4 Empirical and molecular formula** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the method in the [Empirical and molecular formula study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-empirical-and-molecular-formula/) and the [Empirical and molecular formula revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-empirical-and-molecular-formula-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Mg = 24.3, S = 32.1, Cl = 35.5, Cu = 63.5, and R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Define each term.

**(a)** empirical formula **[1]**

**(b)** molecular formula **[1]**

**2.** A hydrocarbon contains 92.3% carbon by mass. Its Mᵣ is 78.0. Calculate its empirical formula and deduce its molecular formula. **[3]**

**3.** A compound contains 24.3% carbon, 4.1% hydrogen and 71.6% chlorine by mass. Its Mᵣ is 99.0. Calculate its empirical formula and deduce its molecular formula. **[3]**

**4.** A compound contains 26.7% carbon and 2.2% hydrogen, and the rest is oxygen. Its Mᵣ is 90.0. Calculate its empirical formula and deduce its molecular formula. **[3]**

**5.** An oxide of nitrogen contains 63.6% nitrogen by mass. Calculate its empirical formula. **[2]**

**6.** 1.20 g of magnesium is heated in air until the mass is constant. The oxide formed has a mass of 2.00 g. Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide. **[2]**

**7.** A student heats copper in a stream of oxygen and records these masses.

| Measurement | Mass / g |
|---|---|
| Empty boat | 25.00 |
| Boat and copper | 26.27 |
| Boat and copper oxide | 26.43 |

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide. **[3]**

**(b)** State how the student could show that the reaction was complete. **[1]**

**8.** A hydrocarbon contains 90.6% carbon. Its Mᵣ is 106.0. Calculate its empirical formula and deduce its molecular formula. **[3]**

**9.** A student analyses a hydrocarbon containing 81.8% carbon and 18.2% hydrogen. The student finds a ratio C : H of 1 : 2.67, rounds it to 1 : 3 and gives the empirical formula as CH₃.

**(a)** Explain why the student's answer is wrong. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the correct empirical formula. **[1]**

**10.** 2.46 g of hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, is heated until all the water is removed. The anhydrous MgSO₄ (Mᵣ = 120.4) left has a mass of 1.20 g. Calculate the value of x. **[3]**

**11.** A gaseous hydrocarbon contains 85.7% carbon by mass. A 0.420 g sample of the gas occupies 250 cm³ at 300 K and 100 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of the hydrocarbon. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mᵣ of the hydrocarbon using pV = nRT. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the molecular formula. **[1]**

**12.** A liquid contains 52.2% carbon, 13.0% hydrogen and 34.8% oxygen by mass. The molecular ion peak in its mass spectrum (electron impact) is at m/z 46.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula, showing your reasoning. **[2]**

**13.** Ethanoic acid (C₂H₄O₂) and glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆) have the same empirical formula. Give the empirical formula and explain how two compounds can share one empirical formula. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The simplest whole number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound [1]

**(b)** The actual number of atoms of each element in (one molecule of) a compound [1]. **[2]**

**2.** H = 7.7%; C 92.3 ÷ 12.0 = 7.69, H 7.7 ÷ 1.0 = 7.7 [1]; ratio 1 : 1, empirical formula CH [1]; CH = 13.0, n = 78.0 ÷ 13.0 = 6, so **C₆H₆** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** C 24.3 ÷ 12.0 = 2.025, H 4.1, Cl 71.6 ÷ 35.5 = 2.017 [1]; ratio 1 : 2 : 1, empirical formula CH₂Cl [1]; mass 49.5, n = 2, so **C₂H₄Cl₂** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** O = 71.1%; C 2.225, H 2.2, O 4.444 [1]; ratio 1 : 1 : 2, empirical formula CHO₂ [1]; mass 45.0, n = 2, so **C₂H₂O₄** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Missing the oxygen by difference makes the formula CH and the molecular formula impossible to find.

**5.** O = 36.4%; N 63.6 ÷ 14.0 = 4.543, O 36.4 ÷ 16.0 = 2.275 [1]; ratio 2.00 : 1, so **N₂O** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Mg 1.20 ÷ 24.3 = 0.0494; O (2.00 − 1.20) ÷ 16.0 = 0.0500 [1]; ratio 1 : 1.01, so **MgO** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Cu = 1.27 g, O = 0.16 g [1]; Cu 1.27 ÷ 63.5 = 0.0200, O 0.16 ÷ 16.0 = 0.0100 [1]; ratio 2 : 1, so **Cu₂O** [1]

**(b)** Reheat and reweigh until the mass is constant [1]. **[4]**

**8.** H = 9.4%; C 90.6 ÷ 12.0 = 7.55, H 9.4 [1]; ratio 1 : 1.245, × 4 gives 4 : 4.98, empirical formula C₄H₅ [1]; mass 53.0, n = 2, so **C₈H₁₀** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 1.245 is not within 0.1 of a whole number; it is 1¼, so multiply by 4.

**9. (a)** 2.67 is not close to a whole number; it is 2⅔, so every value must be multiplied by 3 rather than rounded [1]

**(b)** **C₃H₈** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Mass of water = 2.46 − 1.20 = 1.26 g, n = 1.26 ÷ 18.0 = 0.0700 mol [1]; n(MgSO₄) = 1.20 ÷ 120.4 = 0.00997 mol [1]; ratio 0.0700 ÷ 0.00997 = 7.02, so **x = 7** [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** C 85.7 ÷ 12.0 = 7.14, H 14.3 ÷ 1.0 = 14.3 [1]; ratio 1 : 2.00, empirical formula **CH₂** [1]

**(b)** Mᵣ = mRT ÷ pV = (0.420 × 8.31 × 300) ÷ (1.00 × 10⁵ × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = **41.9** [1]

**(c)** n = 41.9 ÷ 14.0 = 2.99 ≈ 3, so **C₃H₆** [1]. **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* n should come out close to a whole number. A value such as 2.5 means an error in the Mᵣ (often a unit not converted).

**12. (a)** C 4.35, H 13.0, O 2.175 [1]; ratio 2 : 6 : 1, empirical formula **C₂H₆O** [1]

**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 24.0 + 6.0 + 16.0 = 46.0 [1]; the molecular ion gives Mᵣ = 46, so n = 1 and the molecular formula is **C₂H₆O** [1]. **[4]**

**13.** **CH₂O** [1]. The empirical formula gives only the simplest ratio of atoms; the molecules contain different whole-number multiples of it (2 and 6) [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out an element given by difference.
- Rounding a ratio such as 1.25, 1.33 or 2.67 instead of multiplying.
- Dividing by 32 for oxygen.
- Forgetting to subtract to find the mass of oxygen or water.
- Giving an empirical formula when the molecular formula is asked for.
