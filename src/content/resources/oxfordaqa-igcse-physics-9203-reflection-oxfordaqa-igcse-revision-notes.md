These notes condense section **3.3.4 Reflection** (statements a to c) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. For full explanations and worked examples read the [reflection study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-reflection-oxfordaqa-igcse/), then test yourself with the [reflection practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-reflection-oxfordaqa-igcse-practice/).

## The three statements in one line each

- **3.3.4 a** When waves are reflected, the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
- **3.3.4 b** The normal is a construction line perpendicular to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence.
- **3.3.4 c** The image in a plane mirror is virtual, upright and laterally inverted.
- **Skill** Construct ray diagrams showing the changing path of reflected rays.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Incident ray | The ray travelling towards the surface |
| Reflected ray | The ray travelling away from the surface after reflection |
| Point of incidence | Where the incident ray meets the surface |
| Normal | Construction line at 90° to the surface at the point of incidence |
| Angle of incidence, *i* | Angle between the incident ray and the normal |
| Angle of reflection, *r* | Angle between the reflected ray and the normal |
| Virtual image | Rays only appear to come from it; it cannot be formed on a screen |
| Upright | The same way up as the object |
| Laterally inverted | Left and right reversed |

## Condensed recall

**The law applies to all waves.** Light at a mirror, water waves at a barrier in a ripple tank, sound echoing from a wall and microwaves at a metal sheet all reflect with *i* = *r*.

**What reflection does not change.** Speed, frequency and wavelength stay the same, because the wave is still in the same medium. Only the direction changes.

**Surface angle conversion.** Angle with the normal = 90° − angle with the surface. Always convert before using *i* = *r*.

**Special case.** A ray along the normal (*i* = 0°) reflects straight back along its own path.

**Angle between incident and reflected rays** = *i* + *r* = 2*i*.

**Turning a mirror.** If a mirror rotates by an angle θ while the incident ray stays fixed, the reflected ray turns by 2θ. You can always reach this from 2*i* before and after.

**Plane mirror image facts.**

- Virtual, upright, laterally inverted (the specification's three words).
- Same size as the object.
- As far behind the mirror as the object is in front, on the perpendicular line through the object.

**Rough and smooth surfaces.** On a smooth surface all normals are parallel, so a parallel beam stays parallel and an image forms. On a rough surface the normals point in many directions, so reflected rays scatter. Each ray still obeys *i* = *r*.

## Ray diagram rules

1. Ruler for every line.
2. Normal drawn dashed, at 90° to the surface, at the point of incidence.
3. Angles measured from the normal with a protractor.
4. Arrows on every real ray.
5. Rays extended behind a mirror drawn dashed.
6. Image located where two dashed extensions meet.

## Useful set-ups

| Set-up | Key result |
|---|---|
| Periscope: two parallel mirrors at 45° | Each mirror turns the ray through 90°; the image is upright and not reversed |
| Two mirrors at 90° | The ray leaves parallel to its original direction, travelling back the way it came |
| Ray along the normal | Returns along its own path |
| Mirror rotated by θ | Reflected ray turns by 2θ |

## Exam traps

- "Angle of incidence" in a question that shows only the angle to the surface: subtract from 90° first.
- Calling the image real, or inverted. It is virtual and upright.
- Confusing lateral inversion (left-right) with being upside down.
- Drawing extensions behind the mirror as solid lines, or forgetting arrows.
- Putting the image on the mirror rather than behind it.
- Writing "reflection changes the wavelength". It does not.
- Defining the normal without "perpendicular" or without "at the point of incidence".

## Quick self-test

1. State the law of reflection.
2. Define the normal.
3. A ray makes 28° with a mirror surface. What is the angle of reflection?
4. A ray reflects with an angle of incidence of 35°. What is the angle between the incident and reflected rays?
5. Give the three words the specification uses to describe a plane mirror image.
6. Why can a plane mirror image not be caught on a screen?
7. A pupil stands 2.4 m in front of a plane mirror. How far is the pupil from the image?
8. A mirror is rotated by 15° while the incident ray stays fixed. How far does the reflected ray turn?
9. Does reflection change the frequency of a wave? Explain briefly.
10. Explain why paper does not produce a clear image even though each ray obeys the law of reflection.
11. A ray strikes a mirror along the normal. Describe the reflected ray.
12. In a periscope with two parallel mirrors at 45°, through what angle does each mirror turn the ray?

### Answers

1. The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection.
2. A construction line drawn perpendicular to the reflecting surface at the point of incidence.
3. 90° − 28° = 62°, so the angle of reflection is **62°**.
4. 35° + 35° = **70°**.
5. Virtual, upright, laterally inverted.
6. No light passes through the image position; the reflected rays only appear to come from it.
7. The image is 2.4 m behind the mirror, so 2.4 + 2.4 = **4.8 m**.
8. 2 × 15° = **30°**.
9. No. The wave stays in the same medium, so its speed is unchanged, and the frequency is set by the source.
10. Its surface is rough, so the normals point in many directions and the reflected rays leave in many directions instead of as an ordered beam.
11. It travels straight back along the same path (angle of reflection 0°).
12. **90°** each (45° + 45°).
