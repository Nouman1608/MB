## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** In a germinating barley grain, where is amylase produced?

A: Embryo

B: Endosperm

C: Aleurone layer

D: Seed coat **[1]**

**2.** State how many times the sensitive hairs of a Venus fly trap must be stimulated to close the trap, and suggest one advantage of this. **[2]**

**3.** What is the direct effect of auxin on cells in the zone of elongation?

A: It hydrolyses cellulose in the cell wall.

B: It stimulates proton pumps that move H⁺ into the cell wall.

C: It pumps water into the cell.

D: It causes DELLA proteins to be made. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why lowering the pH of a cell wall allows a plant cell to elongate. **[2]**

**5.** A coleoptile segment grows from 15.0 mm to 17.7 mm in auxin solution. Calculate the percentage increase in length. **[2]**

**6.** State two ways in which the closure of a Venus fly trap differs from a response produced by a mammal's nervous system. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** Describe and explain how stimulation of the sensitive hairs leads to closure of a Venus fly trap. **[5]**

**8.** Coleoptile segments were placed in unbuffered solutions, with or without auxin, and the pH of the solution was measured.

| Time / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pH with auxin | 6.0 | 5.6 | 5.2 | 5.0 |
| pH without auxin | 6.0 | 6.0 | 5.9 | 5.9 |

**(a)** Describe the effect of auxin on the pH of the solution. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how auxin causes this change and how the change leads to cell elongation. **[3]**

**(c)** Predict and explain the effect on elongation if the auxin solution were strongly buffered at pH 7. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the role of gibberellin in the germination of a barley grain. **[5]**

**10.** Isolated aleurone layers were incubated with different concentrations of gibberellin, and amylase activity was measured.

| Gibberellin / µmol dm⁻³ | 0 | 0.01 | 0.1 | 1.0 | 1.0 + inhibitor of transcription |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Amylase activity / arbitrary units | 2 | 10 | 35 | 60 | 3 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between gibberellin concentration and amylase activity. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the result with the inhibitor of transcription. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the ways in which auxin and gibberellin bring about their effects on plant cells. **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** Suggest an advantage to the Venus fly trap of closing in a fraction of a second. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the closure of the trap depends on a supply of ATP. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why the trap continues to tighten after it has closed when an insect is caught, but reopens if the trap closed on a small stone. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]

**2.** Twice, within a short time (the same hair twice or two hairs) [1]. This stops the trap closing when touched once by rain or debris, so energy is not wasted on false closures [1].

**3.** B [1]

**4.** Low pH activates expansins, which loosen the links between cellulose microfibrils [1]. Water enters the cell by osmosis and turgor pressure stretches the loosened wall, so the cell elongates [1].

**5.** (17.7 − 15.0) ÷ 15.0 × 100 [1] = **18 %** [1]

**6.** Any two: no neurones, the signal passes between ordinary cells [1]; no muscles, movement results from changes in cell water content and cell walls [1]; the response needs two stimulations within a short time (accept).

**7.** Bending a hair opens ion channels in cells at its base, causing a receptor potential [1]. At threshold an action potential is generated, and a second stimulation within a short time is needed [1]. Action potentials spread across the lobes from cell to cell [1]. Hydrogen ions are pumped into the walls of cells in the outer layers, and water rapidly enters these cells by osmosis, so they expand [1]. The lobes flip from a convex to a concave shape and the trap snaps shut [1].

**8.**

**(a)** With auxin, pH falls from 6.0 to 5.0 in 90 minutes [1]. Without auxin, pH hardly changes (6.0 to 5.9) [1].

**(b)** Auxin binds to receptors and stimulates proton pumps, which use ATP to move H⁺ out of the cytoplasm into the cell wall and surrounding solution [1]. The low pH activates expansins that loosen the links between cellulose microfibrils [1]. Water enters by osmosis and turgor pressure stretches the walls, so the cells elongate [1].

**(c)** Elongation would be reduced, close to that without auxin [1], because the buffer prevents the cell walls from becoming acidic, so expansins are not activated and the walls are not loosened [1].

**9.** The grain absorbs water, which activates the embryo [1]. The embryo produces gibberellin, which diffuses to the aleurone layer [1]. Gibberellin causes the breakdown of DELLA proteins, which normally inhibit a factor needed for transcription [1]. The amylase gene is transcribed and amylase is secreted into the endosperm [1]. Amylase hydrolyses starch to maltose, and the sugars are used by the embryo for respiration and growth [1].

**10.**

**(a)** As gibberellin concentration increases, amylase activity increases [1]. The increase is not proportional: a tenfold rise from 0.1 to 1.0 µmol dm⁻³ less than doubles activity (35 to 60) [1].

**(b)** Gibberellin acts by causing transcription of the amylase gene, after DELLA proteins are broken down [1]. If transcription is blocked, no amylase mRNA is made, so no new amylase is produced (activity 3, close to the 0 value) [1].

**11.** Both are plant hormones that bind to receptors in target cells [1]. Auxin acts quickly on the cell wall by stimulating proton pumps, without needing new gene expression in this mechanism [1]. Gibberellin acts on gene expression, causing DELLA protein breakdown so that the amylase gene is transcribed [1].

**12.**

**(a)** The insect has no time to escape before the lobes close [1].

**(b)** Proton pumps use ATP to move H⁺ into cell walls [1]. Without this, the walls would not loosen and cells would not take in water rapidly, so the lobes would not flip [1].

**(c)** A live insect keeps stimulating the hairs, triggering further action potentials, while a stone does not move, so no further stimulation occurs [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that muscles close the trap.
- Saying protons are pumped into the cell rather than into the wall.
- Saying gibberellin itself digests starch.
- Placing amylase production in the embryo.
- Forgetting that water enters by osmosis and turgor stretches the wall.
- In "compare" answers, giving no similarity.
