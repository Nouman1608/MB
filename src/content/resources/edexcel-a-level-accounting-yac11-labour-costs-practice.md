These questions cover outcomes 1.4.4 to 1.4.6 (labour productivity, methods of remuneration, employee earnings and employer cost) in Unit 1 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11). All amounts are in dollars and all businesses are invented. Tax and pension rates are invented for practice.

## Section A

**1.** Define labour productivity. **[2]**

**2.** A factory made 7,200 units in 900 labour hours. Labour is paid 12 an hour. Calculate the output per labour hour and the labour cost per unit. **[3]**

**3.** An employee works 38 hours at a basic rate of 15 an hour and 7 hours of overtime at time and a half. Calculate gross pay. **[3]**

**4.** A piece rate of 0.75 per unit is paid, with a guaranteed minimum of 300 a week. Calculate the pay for a week in which the employee makes (a) 380 units and (b) 460 units. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** disadvantages of piecework for an employer. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** An employee at Kestrel Fabrics worked 38 hours at 16 an hour and 6 hours of overtime at 24 an hour. Income tax is deducted at 20% of gross pay and the employee's pension contribution is 5% of gross pay. The employer pays social security of 10% of gross pay and a pension contribution of 5% of gross pay.

**(a)** Calculate the employee's gross pay and net pay. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the total cost of the employee to Kestrel Fabrics. **[2]**

**7.** In a bonus scheme, the allowed time is 0.25 hours per unit. A worker making 240 units took 50 hours at 16 an hour. The bonus is 50% of the value of the time saved at the hourly rate. Calculate the time saved, the bonus and the worker's total earnings. **[4]**

**8.** A group of six workers has a target of 4,000 units a week. The group made 4,900 units. A group bonus of 0.15 is paid for each unit above target and is shared equally. Calculate the bonus for each worker. **[3]**

**9.** Hale Plastics pays a worker 10 an hour for a 40-hour week under day work. An alternative is piecework at 0.80 per unit with a guaranteed minimum of 400 a week.

**(a)** Calculate the labour cost per unit under each method when output is 450 units and when output is 600 units. **[4]**

**(b)** Recommend which method Hale Plastics should use, giving **two** reasons. **[4]**

**10.** Evaluate the use of a group bonus scheme compared with an individual bonus scheme. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The amount of output produced [1] for each unit of labour input, usually per labour hour or per employee [1].

**2.** Output per hour 7,200 ÷ 900 = **8 units** [1]; labour cost 900 × 12 = 10,800 [1]; cost per unit 10,800 ÷ 7,200 = **1.50** [1].

**3.** Basic pay 38 × 15 = 570 [1]; overtime rate 15 × 1.5 = 22.50, so 7 × 22.50 = 157.50 [1]; gross pay 570 + 157.50 = **727.50** [1].

**4. (a)** 380 × 0.75 = 285, which is below the guarantee, so pay is **300** [2]

**(b)** 460 × 0.75 = **345** [1]

**5.** Any two: quality may fall as workers rush [1]; the employer may need extra inspection [1]; pay is uncertain for workers, which may reduce morale [1]; a guaranteed minimum means the employer pays even when output is low [1]; workers may resist new methods that change the piece rate [1].

**6. (a)** Basic pay 38 × 16 = 608 [1]; overtime 6 × 24 = 144 [1]; gross pay **752**, tax 150.40 and pension 37.60 give net pay 752 − 150.40 − 37.60 = **564.00** [2].

**(b)** Employer's social security 75.20 and pension 37.60 [1]; total cost 752 + 75.20 + 37.60 = **864.80** [1].

**7.** Allowed time 240 × 0.25 = 60 hours [1]; time saved 60 − 50 = **10 hours** [1]; bonus 10 × 16 × 50% = **80** [1]; total earnings 50 × 16 + 80 = 800 + 80 = **880** [1].

**8.** Units above target 4,900 − 4,000 = 900 [1]; bonus pool 900 × 0.15 = 135 [1]; each worker 135 ÷ 6 = **22.50** [1].

**9. (a)**

| Output | Day work | Piecework |
|---|---:|---:|
| 450 units | 400 ÷ 450 = **0.89** | 450 × 0.80 = 360, so the guarantee of 400 applies: 400 ÷ 450 = **0.89** |
| 600 units | 400 ÷ 600 = **0.67** | 600 × 0.80 = 480; 480 ÷ 600 = **0.80** |

Day work cost 400 at both outputs [1]; piecework cost 400 at 450 units [1]; piecework cost 480 at 600 units [1]; cost per unit values [1].

**(b)** Reasons for day work: labour cost per unit falls as output rises, giving 0.67 at 600 units compared with 0.80 under piecework [1]; the employer does not pay more when output is high [1]. Reasons for piecework: it rewards effort and may motivate workers to produce more [1]; the cost per unit is fixed at 0.80 once output passes 500 units [1]. Any recommendation with two supported reasons earns 4 marks; a recommendation without a reason earns none.

**10.** For a group scheme: it encourages co-operation and helps where individual output cannot be measured [1]; it avoids disputes over who contributed to a team job [1]. Against: a hard-working worker may gain little if others do not work [1]; individual schemes link reward to effort more directly [1]; group schemes may encourage group pressure on weaker workers [1].
Judgement: the choice depends on the work; a group scheme suits work done by a team, while an individual scheme suits separate tasks where time can be measured [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Paying overtime at the basic rate (Question 3).
- Ignoring the guaranteed minimum (Questions 4 and 9).
- Giving the whole value of the time saved to the worker instead of 50% (Question 7).
- Treating the employee's tax as an extra cost to the employer (Question 6).
- Dividing a group bonus by the wrong number of workers.
- Writing a recommendation with no supporting figures.

## Approaching these questions

Build pay in stages: basic, overtime, bonus, gross, deductions, net. For employer cost, start from gross pay and add only the employer's own contributions. For comparisons, calculate the cost per unit at each output and then make a decision using the numbers.
