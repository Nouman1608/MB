## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it (question 5: four marks for a developed explanation; question 6: two differences, each identified and explained). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** A barrier; the stage between death and resurrection [1].

**2.** Salat al-Janazah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: people are raised and gathered; the book of deeds is given; deeds are weighed on the scale; the body testifies; people are sent to Jannah or Jahannam; the Prophet (pbuh) intercedes with Allah's permission.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: gardens with rivers flowing beneath; rivers of water, milk, wine and honey; peace and freedom from sorrow; reunion with righteous loved ones; nearness to Allah.

**5.** Washing (ghusl) is done to purify the body before it meets Allah [1], usually by relatives of the same gender, as a last service and mark of respect [1]. The plain white kafan shows that all are equal before Allah, rich and poor [1], and avoids display, reminding the living that worldly status ends at death [1].

**6.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Takbirs** [1]: Sunni Muslims say four takbirs in Salat al-Janazah, while Shi'a Muslims say five, following different traditions about how the Prophet (pbuh) prayed [1].
- **Gatherings** [1]: some communities hold gatherings on set days after a death to read the Qur'an, while others regard these as cultural customs and not part of the Sunnah [1].
- **Expression of grief** [1]: weeping is accepted, but customs on loud mourning vary, and Hadith discourage wailing [1].
- **Graves** [1]: some Muslims visit and pray at graves regularly, while others limit this to simple visits to remember death [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Accountability** [1]: every action will be recorded and weighed, so Muslims take their choices seriously [1].
- **Justice** [1]: wrongs that are not punished in this world will be put right, which gives hope to the oppressed [1].
- **Motivation for good** [1]: the hope of Jannah encourages prayer, charity and honesty [1].
- **Warning** [1]: the fear of Jahannam discourages cruelty and injustice [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "We place the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be treated unjustly at all" (Qur'an 21:47) [2].
- "Whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it" (99:7) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Honesty in private** [1]: believing that deeds are recorded, a Muslim tries to be truthful and fair when no one is watching [1].
- **Charity** [1]: they give Zakah and sadaqah because the reward continues after death [1].
- **Patience in loss** [1]: bereaved Muslims are comforted by the hope of reunion in Paradise [1].
- **Perspective** [1]: knowing that wealth ends at death, they avoid greed and pride [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your [full] compensation on the Day of Resurrection" (Qur'an 3:185) [2].
- The Hadith that a person's deeds end at death except ongoing charity, knowledge that benefits others and a righteous child who prays for them (Sahih Muslim) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The passage shows the frightening side: people flee from their own families, which shows the seriousness of judgement.
- It also shows hope: "bright, laughing, rejoicing" faces belong to those who are successful.
- Fear (*khawf*) and hope (*raja'*) are both parts of faith.
- **Against the statement:** many Muslims say love of Allah, gratitude, following the Prophet (pbuh) and care for others are equally strong reasons; the Qur'an stresses Allah's mercy as much as His justice; good conduct is also a duty in itself and is valued in this world.
- **Judgement:** fear of judgement is important, but most Muslims would say it is balanced by hope and love, so it is one reason among several and not the main one.

*Use of the passage*

- "flee from his own siblings ... his mother and his father ... his wife and his children" shows that each person is accountable alone.
- "a matter adequate for him" shows that each person is occupied with their own record.
- "bright ... laughing, rejoicing at good news" gives hope.
- "dust ... blackness" gives a warning.
- "Those are the disbelievers, the wicked ones" shows that outcomes depend on faith and actions.
