Spoken language in AQA GCSE English Language (8700) is a **compulsory** non-exam assessment reported as
a **separate endorsement**: you give a prepared, formal presentation on a topic agreed with your teacher
(as a guide, no more than ten minutes), then respond to questions and feedback. It carries **0% of the
GCSE mark** and is graded Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified against AO7, AO8 and AO9. For the
full skills guide, use the
[Spoken language study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-english-language-spoken-language-study-guide/).

## What the specification content says (3.1.3)

| Subject content (8700, 3.1.3) | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| **Presenting information and ideas** | Selecting and organising information and ideas effectively and persuasively for prepared spoken presentations; planning for different purposes and audiences; making presentations and speeches |
| **Responding to spoken language** | Listening to and responding appropriately to any questions and feedback |
| **Spoken Standard English** | Expressing ideas using Standard English whenever and wherever appropriate |

## The three spoken-language assessment objectives

| AO | Wording |
|---|---|
| AO7 | Demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting |
| AO8 | Listen and respond appropriately to spoken language, including to questions and feedback on presentations |
| AO9 | Use spoken Standard English effectively in speeches and presentations |

AO1–AO6 belong to the two written papers; AO7–AO9 belong only to the endorsement.

## Key requirements of the presentation

- **Prepared and formal**, on a specific topic; the form can vary: talks, debates, speeches and dialogues.
- **Topic agreed in advance** with your teacher.
- **Planned and organised**: AQA warns that lack of preparation is likely to prevent access to the higher
  grades.
- **Length:** as a guide, no more than ten minutes.
- **Notes and slides allowed** (pre-prepared notes, PowerPoint), but not required.
- **Questions and feedback:** as part of, or after, the presentation you must listen and respond.
- **Teacher-only audience:** allowed, but the presentation must be designed for a potentially wider
  audience, for example replicating a television interview.

## How grading works: the competency rule

No marks are given. The grade is holistic and on a **competency basis**: you must meet **every** criterion
in a grade before you can move to the next. Students who do not reach Pass are recorded as Not Classified.

### General criteria (all grades)

- Be **audible**.
- Use **spoken Standard English**: be intelligible and generally use language appropriate to the formal
  setting.

### Grade criteria compared

| | Pass | Merit | Distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Content | Straightforward ideas/information/feelings | Challenging ideas/information/feelings, with a range of vocabulary | Sophisticated ideas/information/feelings, with a sophisticated repertoire of vocabulary |
| Structure | Attempts to organise and structure | Organised and structured clearly and appropriately for the audience | Organised and structured using an effective range of strategies to engage the audience |
| Audience | Attempts to meet the needs of the audience | (within structure, above) | (within structure, above) |
| Purpose | — | Achieves the purpose | Achieves the purpose |
| Questions | Appropriate response in a straightforward manner | Responds formally and in some detail | Responds perceptively and, if appropriate, elaborates with further ideas and information |

## Moderation and recording

- Your school records a **sample** of presentations on audio-visual media, complete and unedited, including
  the questions and feedback.
- 30 or fewer students: all recorded; over 30: 30 recorded, with at least 10 at each grade (D, M, P) where
  available. Not Classified students are not included.
- Malpractice rules apply: work must be your own, and sources must be acknowledged.
- A student who does not attempt the presentation receives Not Classified.

## Planning checklist

| Stage | Action |
|---|---|
| Topic | Choose one that lets you express **challenging** or **sophisticated** ideas, not just facts |
| Purpose | Decide: inform, persuade, argue, explain? Write it down |
| Audience | Who are they, what do they know, what will engage them? |
| Structure | Opening hook → clear sections with signposting → memorable close |
| Strategies | Anecdote, rhetorical question, statistic, visual, audience participation |
| Language | Formal Standard English; subject vocabulary explained where needed |
| Questions | Predict likely questions; prepare evidence and examples |
| Rehearsal | Time it to stay within about ten minutes |

## Worked example: Standard English in speech

| Non-standard / informal | Spoken Standard English |
|---|---|
| "So basically we was thinking that, like, homework is pointless." | "My argument today is that much homework does not improve learning." |
| "There's loads of kids what don't have a quiet place to work." | "Many students do not have a quiet place to work at home." |
| "Yeah, nah, I don't get your question." | "Could you clarify what you mean by 'fair'?" |

Standard English is not the same as using long words: it means grammatically standard forms (for
example "we were", not "we was") and a register suited to a formal setting.

## Common mistakes / traps

- **Treating it as optional.** It is compulsory for 8700, even though it does not count towards the grade.
- **Choosing a list topic.** "My hobbies" makes Merit and Distinction hard to reach.
- **Ignoring purpose.** Merit and Distinction both require the presentation to achieve its purpose.
- **Reading slides aloud.** Slides support, but strategies to engage the audience are what Distinction
  rewards.
- **One-word answers.** Responding "in some detail" (Merit) or "perceptively" with elaboration
  (Distinction) is part of the grade.
- **Slipping into informal speech under pressure**, especially in the question stage.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the spoken language endorsement affect my GCSE English Language grade?**
No. It has 0% weighting; it is reported as a separate grade on your certificate alongside the overall GCSE
grade.

**How long should the presentation be?**
AQA's guide is no more than ten minutes. The exact duration is at your teacher's discretion.

**Can I use PowerPoint or notes?**
Yes. Pre-prepared notes and slides are allowed but not required.

**What happens if I meet most Distinction criteria but not all?**
You are awarded Merit. The competency rule means every criterion in a grade must be met.

## Self-test

1. What weighting does spoken language carry in the GCSE? (0%; reported separately.)
2. Name the three spoken-language AOs. (AO7 presenting; AO8 listening and responding; AO9 spoken Standard English.)
3. What is the competency rule? (All criteria in a grade must be met to achieve it.)
4. Who must agree the topic? (The student and the teacher, in advance.)
5. What must a teacher-only presentation be designed for? (A potentially wider audience.)
