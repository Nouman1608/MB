Subtopic 20.2 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the force that might act on a current-carrying conductor in a magnetic field, the equation F = BIL sin θ with directions from Fleming's left-hand rule, and the definition of magnetic flux density. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Force on a current-carrying conductor study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-force-on-a-current-carrying-conductor/); to apply the ideas, try the [Force on a current-carrying conductor practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-force-on-a-current-carrying-conductor-practice/).

## The equation

```
F = BIL sin θ
F = NBIL sin θ        (N turns of a coil, each side in the field)
B = F / (IL)          (wire at right angles to the field)
```

- L = length **in the field**, in m.
- θ = angle between the **current** and the **field**.
- F is perpendicular to both I and B.

## When is there a force?

| Arrangement | θ | Force |
|---|---|---|
| Current parallel to field | 0° | none |
| Current antiparallel to field | 180° | none |
| Current at angle θ | θ | BIL sin θ |
| Current perpendicular to field | 90° | maximum, BIL |
| No current | — | none (for a non-magnetic wire) |

## Fleming's left-hand rule

- **F**irst finger → **F**ield (N to S)
- Se**C**ond finger → **C**urrent (conventional, + to −)
- Thu**M**b → **M**otion (force)

Reverse current or field → force reverses. Reverse both → force unchanged.

| Current | Field | Force |
|---|---|---|
| Right | Into page | Up the page |
| Right | Out of page | Down the page |
| Up the page | Into page | Left |
| Into page | Left to right (N on left) | Down the page |

## Defining the tesla

**Magnetic flux density** is the force acting per unit current per unit length on a wire placed at right angles to the magnetic field.

```
1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = 1 kg s⁻² A⁻¹
```

## The balance method

1. Magnet on a top-pan balance; wire clamped through the field, perpendicular to it.
2. Switch on the current; reading changes by Δm.
3. Force on wire = Δm g (Newton's third law: equal and opposite to the force on the magnet).
4. Reading up → magnet pushed down → wire pushed up.
5. Plot F against I: straight line through the origin, gradient = BL.

## Coils and couples

- In a rectangular coil with its plane parallel to the field, the two sides perpendicular to the field carry opposite currents: equal and opposite forces → a couple.
- Sides parallel to the field: no force.
- Torque = force on one side × perpendicular distance between the sides.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetic flux density B | Force per unit current per unit length on a wire at right angles to the field |
| Tesla (T) | N A⁻¹ m⁻¹; base units kg s⁻² A⁻¹ |
| Catapult field | Resultant of the wire's own field and the external field; crowded on one side, pushing the wire to the other |
| Fleming's left-hand rule | Gives the direction of force on a current in a field |
| Conventional current | Direction of flow of positive charge; opposite to electron flow |

## Exam traps

- Using the full length of a wire instead of the length in the field.
- Taking θ as the angle between the wire and the pole face, or between the force and the field.
- Using the right hand for the force rule.
- Missing "at right angles" from the definition of B.
- Forgetting the number of turns N in a coil.
- Thinking the force on a coil in a uniform field gives a resultant push; it gives a couple.
- Treating electron flow as conventional current.

## Self-test

1. Define magnetic flux density.
2. Express the tesla in SI base units.
3. A wire 0.30 m long carries 4.0 A at right angles to a field of 0.15 T. Calculate the force.
4. The wire in Question 3 is turned so that it makes 20° with the field. Calculate the new force.
5. State the force on a current-carrying wire lying along the field lines.
6. Current flows up the page in a field directed into the page. Give the direction of the force.
7. Both the current and the field are reversed. What happens to the force?
8. A balance reading rises by 1.8 g when 2.0 A flows in 4.0 cm of wire perpendicular to the field. Find B.
9. In Question 8, which way does the force on the wire act?
10. A coil of 80 turns has a side of 5.0 cm perpendicular to a 0.10 T field. The current is 0.25 A. Find the force on that side.
11. A graph of force F against current I for a fixed wire in a fixed field has gradient 0.012 N A⁻¹. The length in the field is 6.0 cm. Find B.
12. Why does a rectangular coil in a uniform field experience no resultant force?

**Answers:** 1. Force per unit current per unit length on a wire placed at right angles to the field. 2. kg s⁻² A⁻¹. 3. 0.15 × 4.0 × 0.30 = 0.18 N. 4. 0.18 × sin 20° = 0.062 N. 5. Zero. 6. To the left. 7. It is unchanged. 8. F = 1.8 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 = 0.0177 N; B = 0.0177 ÷ (2.0 × 0.040) = 0.22 T. 9. Upwards (the magnet is pushed down). 10. 80 × 0.10 × 0.25 × 0.050 = 0.10 N. 11. B = gradient ÷ L = 0.012 ÷ 0.060 = 0.20 T. 12. Opposite sides carry current in opposite directions, so the forces on them are equal and opposite and cancel (they may form a couple).
