## 4.5.1 Electromagnetic induction

- e.m.f. induced when a conductor **moves across** a field or the **field linking it changes**.
- Experiment: move a magnet in and out of a coil joined to a sensitive meter; deflection while moving, zero when still, opposite deflection when removed.
- Bigger e.m.f.: **faster** movement, **stronger** magnet, **more turns**.
- **Extended only:** the induced e.m.f. **opposes the change** causing it (N pole pushed in → near end of coil becomes N).
- **Extended only:** Fleming's **right-hand** rule: thumb = motion, first finger = field, second finger = induced current.

## 4.5.2 a.c. generator (Extended only)

- Rotating coil in a field (or rotating magnet in a coil).
- **Slip rings and brushes** connect the rotating coil to the circuit.
- Coil plane **parallel** to field → e.m.f. **maximum**.
- Coil plane **perpendicular** to field → e.m.f. **zero**.
- e.m.f. reverses every half turn. Faster → higher peaks and higher frequency.

## 4.5.3 Magnetic effect of a current

- Straight wire: **concentric circles**; right-hand grip rule (thumb = current).
- Solenoid: like a bar magnet outside; strong, nearly uniform inside.
- Experiment: wire or solenoid through card; iron filings for pattern, plotting compasses for direction.
- **Relay:** coil current → electromagnet → attracts armature → closes second circuit.
- **Loudspeaker:** varying current in a coil in a magnet's field → coil and cone vibrate → sound.
- **Extended only:** field weaker further from a wire; more current → stronger field; reversed current → reversed field.

## 4.5.4 Force on a conductor

- Current at right angles to a field → force at right angles to both.
- Reverse current **or** field → force reverses; reverse both → unchanged.
- **Extended only:** Fleming's **left-hand** rule: first finger = field, second finger = current, thumb = force.
- **Extended only:** charged beams: positive particles, current along their motion; electrons, current **opposite** to their motion.

## 4.5.5 d.c. motor

- Coil in a field with a current experiences a **turning effect**.
- Bigger turning effect: **more turns**, **larger current**, **stronger field**.
- **Extended only:** forces on the two sides are opposite; **split-ring commutator** reverses the coil current every half turn; **brushes** make sliding contact.

## 4.5.6 Transformer

- Two coils (**primary**, **secondary**) on a **soft-iron core**.
- **Step-up:** Ns > Np, Vs > Vp. **Step-down:** Ns < Np, Vs < Vp.
- Transmission: step up at the power station, step down near users.
- Advantages of high voltage: **smaller current**, **less energy lost** heating cables, **thinner** cables.
- **Extended only:** a.c. in primary → **changing** field in core → alternating e.m.f. induced in secondary. No output with d.c.
- **Extended only:** higher voltage → lower current → I²R loss falls with the square of the current.

## Generator or motor?

| | Motor effect | Induction (generator) |
|---|---|---|
| Input | Current in a field | Motion in a field |
| Output | Force, motion | Induced e.m.f., current |
| Hand rule (Extended only) | Left | Right |
| Connection to rotating coil (Extended only) | Split-ring commutator | Slip rings (for a.c.) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic induction | Production of an e.m.f. by relative movement or a changing field |
| Solenoid | Long coil of wire |
| Relay | Switch operated by an electromagnet |
| Primary coil | Input coil of a transformer |
| Secondary coil | Output coil of a transformer |
| Step-up transformer | Raises the voltage (more secondary turns) |
| Split-ring commutator (Extended only) | Reverses the current in a motor coil every half turn |
| Slip rings (Extended only) | Keep a generator coil connected without twisting the wires |

## Exam traps

- Left hand for the motor effect; right hand for induction.
- No e.m.f. when a wire moves parallel to the field, or when nothing moves.
- Transformers need **a.c.**
- Generator peak is when the coil plane is **parallel** to the field.
- For electrons, reverse the direction of motion before using the left-hand rule.
- Cable loss uses the cable current in P = I²R.

## Self-test

1. State three ways to increase the e.m.f. induced when a magnet moves into a coil.
2. A magnet is held still inside a coil. Is an e.m.f. induced?
3. Describe the magnetic field pattern around a long straight wire.
4. State the effect of reversing both the current and the field on the force on a wire.
5. State three ways to increase the turning effect on a motor coil.
6. A transformer has 400 primary turns and 1600 secondary turns. The input is 12 V a.c. Find the output.
7. Give two advantages of transmitting electricity at high voltage.
8. *(Extended)* An N pole is pulled out of a coil. What pole forms at the near end of the coil?
9. *(Extended)* At what position of a generator coil is the e.m.f. zero?
10. *(Extended)* A 100% efficient transformer gives 12 V at 2.0 A from a 240 V supply. Find the primary current.
11. *(Extended)* Explain why a transformer does not work with d.c.
12. *(Extended)* 1.0 MW is sent through cables of resistance 2.0 Ω at 10 kV. Find the power lost.

### Answers

1. Move it **faster**, use a **stronger** magnet, use **more turns**.
2. **No**: there is no relative movement, so the field linking the coil does not change.
3. **Concentric circles** around the wire, closer together near it.
4. **No change** in direction.
5. **More turns**, **larger current**, **stronger** field.
6. Vs = 12 × 1600 / 400 = **48 V** (step-up).
7. Smaller current, so **less energy lost** in the cables; **thinner** cables can be used.
8. An **S pole** (attracting the magnet, opposing its removal).
9. When the plane of the coil is **perpendicular** to the field.
10. Ip = (12 × 2.0) / 240 = **0.10 A**.
11. d.c. gives a **steady** field in the core, so there is no changing field to induce an e.m.f. in the secondary.
12. I = 1.0 × 10⁶ / 10 × 10³ = 100 A; P = 100² × 2.0 = **2.0 × 10⁴ W** (20 kW).
