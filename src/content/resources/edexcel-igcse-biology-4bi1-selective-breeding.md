## The idea behind selective breeding

**Selective breeding** (artificial selection) is the process in which humans choose which organisms breed together, so that useful characteristics are passed on and become more common over many generations.

It works because of three facts:

1. Individuals of a species show **variation**. Some wheat plants give more grain than others, and some cows give more milk.
2. Much of this variation is caused by **genes**, which are passed from parents to offspring.
3. If the organisms with the best characteristic are bred together, their offspring are more likely to inherit the genes for it.

In natural selection, the environment decides which organisms survive and reproduce. In selective breeding, **humans decide**. The characteristics chosen are those useful to humans, and not necessarily those that help the organism survive in the wild.

## The steps of selective breeding

The same four steps apply to plants and animals.

1. **Decide** the characteristic you want, for example a high milk yield or resistance to a disease.
2. **Select** the individuals from the population that show the characteristic most strongly, to be the parents.
3. **Breed** the selected parents together.
4. From the offspring, **select** the ones that show the characteristic best, and breed them together. **Repeat** this for many generations.

Over time, the average value of the characteristic in the population shifts in the chosen direction.

### Worked example 1: the steps for plants

*A farmer has a field of wheat. Some plants have heavy ears of grain and others are shorter and fall over in the wind. Describe how the farmer can develop a variety with high yield and strong stems.*

1. Choose the plants with the heaviest ears and the strongest stems.
2. Collect pollen from one and place it on the stigma of another, or let only these plants breed together. This is **cross-pollination** under control.
3. Collect the seeds produced. Grow them in the next season.
4. From these plants, choose the best again and breed them together.
5. Repeat for many seasons. Eventually most plants have heavy ears and strong stems.

Notice that the answer names the characteristic, the selection, the breeding and the repetition. Those four ideas earn the marks.

## 5.10 Plants with desired characteristics

Plants are selectively bred to improve:

| Characteristic | Example |
|---|---|
| Higher yield | Wheat or maize with more grain per plant |
| Disease resistance | Crop varieties that resist a fungal disease |
| Shorter stems | Cereals less likely to be flattened by wind or rain |
| Faster ripening or larger fruit | Larger tomatoes or apples |
| Better taste, colour or flavour | Sweeter fruits, brighter flowers |
| Tolerance of drought or salty soil | Crops that grow in difficult areas |

A second approach is to cross two varieties that each have a useful feature, for example one with high yield and one with disease resistance, then select the offspring that combine both.

Because plants can reproduce asexually, a plant with excellent characteristics can be copied by taking cuttings, so its good features are kept without further breeding.

## 5.11 Animals with desired characteristics

Animals are selectively bred for:

| Characteristic | Example |
|---|---|
| High milk yield | Dairy cattle |
| Lean meat, fast growth | Beef cattle, pigs, chickens |
| Many eggs | Hens |
| Good wool quality | Sheep |
| Disease resistance, calm behaviour | Many farm animals |
| Speed, strength, appearance | Horses, dogs |

### Worked example 2: dairy cattle

*A farmer wants cows that produce more milk. Describe how this can be achieved.*

1. Choose the cows with the highest milk yield, and bulls whose mothers had a high milk yield.
2. Breed them together.
3. Measure the milk yield of the female calves when they grow up.
4. Breed the best calves together.
5. Repeat for many generations.

The characteristic of a bull cannot be seen directly, so farmers use the performance of his daughters or mother as evidence.

### Worked example 3: calculating improvement

*The mean milk yield per cow in a herd was 5000 litres per year. After selective breeding over three generations it was 5300, then 5620, then 5960 litres. Calculate the percentage increase from the first measurement to the last, and the mean increase per generation.*

```
total increase = 5960 − 5000 = 960 litres
percentage increase = 960 ÷ 5000 × 100 = 19.2%
mean increase per generation = 960 ÷ 3 = 320 litres
```

The herd's mean yield rose by 19.2% in three generations, an average of 320 litres per generation. Divide by the original value for a percentage change, and by the number of generations for the mean change.

## Advantages and disadvantages

### Advantages

- Higher yield, so more food from the same area or number of animals.
- Better quality, for example more tender meat, better flavour or more nutritious crops.
- Disease resistance, which lowers losses and use of chemicals.
- Can be done with no special equipment, simply by choosing parents.

### Disadvantages

- It takes many **generations**, so it is slow, especially in animals that mature slowly.
- It **reduces genetic variation**. If only a few organisms are chosen as parents, the population has fewer different alleles.
- With less variation, the whole population may be affected by a new disease or a change in conditions. This is a serious risk in a crop grown as the same variety everywhere.
- Breeding closely related individuals (**inbreeding**) can increase the chance of harmful recessive alleles showing up, which may cause health problems.
- Selecting for one characteristic may accidentally reduce another, for example a very high milk yield can make animals more prone to illness.

### Worked example 4: evaluating a decision

*A farmer decides to plant only one variety of wheat that has the highest yield. Evaluate this decision.*

**Benefit:** the high yield gives the greatest harvest in a normal year.

**Risk:** the plants are very similar genetically. If a new disease appears, and the variety is not resistant, all of the crop could be lost.

**Conclusion:** the decision is good for yield in the short term but risky. Growing more than one variety, or keeping some variation, would reduce the risk.

## Comparing selective breeding with natural selection

| Feature | Natural selection | Selective breeding |
|---|---|---|
| Who selects? | The environment | Humans |
| Characteristic favoured | Helps survival and reproduction | Useful to humans |
| Speed | Slow, over very many generations | Can be quicker but still many generations |
| Result | Better adapted to the environment | Better for human use, not always for wild survival |

## Common mistakes

- Saying that the organisms "change" to get the characteristic. The population changes over generations because the parents with the best genes are chosen. Individuals do not change.
- Leaving out the repeat of the process over many generations.
- Describing selective breeding as the same as genetic engineering. In selective breeding, no genes are moved from one species to another.
- Writing "the best animals" without saying what the characteristic is.
- Forgetting that the breeders choose the **parents**, not the offspring after they are born.
- Giving only advantages when the question asks for an evaluation.
- Saying that inbreeding always creates new diseases. It makes harmful recessive alleles more likely to appear in a pair.

## Self-check

1. Define selective breeding.
2. Give two characteristics that farmers select in cattle.
3. Why must the process be repeated for many generations?
4. Explain one disadvantage of selective breeding.

**Answers:** (1) Humans choosing which organisms breed so that useful characteristics are passed on. (2) Any two of: high milk yield, lean meat, fast growth, disease resistance. (3) Each generation only slightly increases how common the useful alleles are. (4) Reduced genetic variation, so a new disease could affect the whole population; or inbreeding causes harmful alleles to appear.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is selective breeding the same as genetic engineering?**
No. Selective breeding chooses parents from the same species. Genetic engineering moves genes directly, sometimes between species.

**Why is selective breeding slow?**
It needs many generations of breeding and selecting before the characteristic becomes common.

**Is selective breeding the same as natural selection?**
No. In selective breeding humans choose the parents. In natural selection the environment does.
