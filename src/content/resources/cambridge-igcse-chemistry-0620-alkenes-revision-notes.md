Condensed recall for **11.5 Alkenes**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). Full explanations and worked examples are in the [alkenes study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alkenes/); exam-style questions are in the [alkenes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-alkenes-practice/).

> **Tier note:** outcomes 1–4 (bonding, cracking, reasons for cracking, bromine test) are Core. Outcomes 5–6 (addition reactions and drawing their products) are Supplement, marked *(Extended)*.

## Core facts

- Alkenes contain a **C=C double covalent bond**.
- Alkenes are **unsaturated hydrocarbons**.
- General formula **CₙH₂ₙ**; first member **ethene, C₂H₄**.
- Alkenes are made by **cracking** larger alkanes.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Unsaturated | one or more carbon–carbon bonds are not single bonds |
| Cracking | breaking larger alkane molecules into smaller alkanes, alkenes and hydrogen using a high temperature and a catalyst |
| Aqueous bromine | bromine water, orange; used to test for C=C |
| Addition reaction *(Extended)* | a reaction in which only one product is formed |
| Hydrogenation *(Extended)* | addition of hydrogen across C=C, nickel catalyst |
| Hydration *(Extended)* | addition of steam across C=C, acid catalyst |

## Cracking

| Reactant | Conditions | Products |
|---|---|---|
| larger alkane | **high temperature** + **catalyst** | shorter alkanes, **alkenes**, **hydrogen** |

Examples (check: atoms balance on each side):

```
C₁₀H₂₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₂H₄
C₁₀H₂₂ → C₇H₁₆ + C₃H₆
C₁₂H₂₆ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₂H₄
C₂H₆  → C₂H₄ + H₂
```

Completing an equation: subtract the carbons and hydrogens of the known product(s) from the reactant; classify what is left using CₙH₂ₙ₊₂ (alkane) or CₙH₂ₙ (alkene).

### Reasons for cracking

1. **Demand** for short-chain fractions (such as gasoline) is greater than the **supply** from fractional distillation; long-chain fractions are in surplus.
2. Cracking produces **alkenes**, used to make **polymers** and other chemicals such as ethanol; hydrogen is also produced.

Cracking is **chemical** (bonds break); fractional distillation is **physical** (no bonds break).

## The bromine water test

| | Alkene (unsaturated) | Alkane (saturated) |
|---|---|---|
| Add aqueous bromine and shake | **orange → colourless** | **stays orange** |

## Addition reactions *(Extended)*

| Reagent | Condition | Ethene gives | Propene gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Br₂ or aqueous bromine | room temperature | CH₂BrCH₂Br | CH₂BrCHBrCH₃ |
| H₂ | **nickel catalyst** | CH₃CH₃ (ethane) | CH₃CH₂CH₃ (propane) |
| H₂O (steam) | **acid catalyst** (300 °C, 6000 kPa for ethene) | CH₃CH₂OH (ethanol) | an alcohol, C₃H₇OH |

Rule for drawing: only the **two carbons of the C=C** gain atoms, one atom or group each; the double bond becomes **single**.

```
C₂H₄ + Br₂  → C₂H₄Br₂
C₂H₄ + H₂   → C₂H₆
C₂H₄ + H₂O  → C₂H₅OH
```

## Cracking vs fractional distillation

| | Fractional distillation (11.3) | Cracking (11.5) |
|---|---|---|
| Type of change | physical | chemical |
| Bonds inside molecules | not broken | broken |
| Conditions | heating; temperature gradient in a column | high temperature + catalyst |
| What comes out | fractions already present in petroleum | new, smaller molecules, including alkenes |

## Displayed formulae to know

Ethene, CH₂=CH₂: two carbons joined by **one double bond**, each carbon also bonded to **two** hydrogens.

Products of ethene *(Extended)*:

- with Br₂: each carbon has two H and one Br, joined by a **single** bond;
- with H₂: ethane, six H in total;
- with H₂O: ethanol, with –OH on one carbon and an extra H on the other.

Rule for any addition: the number of atoms in the product = atoms in the alkene + atoms in the reagent. Nothing is lost.

## Isomers *(Extended)*

But-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃, and but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃, are **structural isomers**: same molecular formula, C₄H₈, different position of the C=C. Both give **butane** with hydrogen, but they give **different** dibromo products with bromine (CH₂BrCHBrCH₂CH₃ and CH₃CHBrCHBrCH₃).

## Answer frames

- **Describe cracking:** "Large alkane molecules are heated to a high temperature and passed over a catalyst; they break down into smaller alkanes, alkenes and hydrogen."
- **Describe the bromine test:** "Shake with aqueous bromine: an alkene turns it from orange to colourless; an alkane leaves it orange."
- **Draw an addition product** *(Extended)*: copy the carbon chain, turn C=C into C–C, add one new atom or group to each of those two carbons.

## Exam traps

- Say **colourless**, never "clear"; give **both** colours.
- Cracking needs a **catalyst** and a **high** temperature: both are mark points.
- Reasons for cracking must say **why**, not just "it makes smaller molecules".
- Count H atoms in cracking equations; one missing H is a lost mark.
- Hydrogen addition uses **nickel**; steam addition uses an **acid** catalyst. *(Extended)*
- Do not leave a C=C in an addition product. *(Extended)*
- Addition gives **one** product only; substitution (alkanes) gives two. *(Extended)*

## Self-test

1. State the bond present in all alkenes that is not in alkanes.
2. Give the general formula of the alkenes.
3. State the two conditions used for cracking.
4. Name the three types of product that can form when alkanes are cracked.
5. Complete: C₁₄H₃₀ → C₁₀H₂₂ + ......
6. Give two reasons why long-chain alkanes are cracked.
7. Describe the result when aqueous bromine is shaken with ethene.
8. Is cracking a physical or chemical process? Explain.
9. *(Extended)* State what is meant by an addition reaction.
10. *(Extended)* Name the catalyst used when ethene reacts with hydrogen.
11. *(Extended)* Give the structural formula of the product of ethene with bromine.
12. *(Extended)* Name the product when steam is added to ethene.

**Answers:**

1. A carbon–carbon double bond (C=C).
2. CₙH₂ₙ.
3. High temperature; a catalyst.
4. Shorter-chain alkanes, alkenes and hydrogen.
5. C₄H₈ (or 2C₂H₄): 14 − 10 = 4 C and 30 − 22 = 8 H.
6. Demand for short-chain fractions exceeds supply; cracking produces alkenes needed to make polymers and other chemicals.
7. The aqueous bromine turns from orange to colourless.
8. Chemical: covalent bonds within the molecules are broken and new substances form.
9. A reaction in which only one product is formed.
10. Nickel.
11. CH₂BrCH₂Br.
12. Ethanol.
