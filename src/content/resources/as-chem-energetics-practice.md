## Questions

**1.** Define standard enthalpy of formation. **[2]**

**2.** State Hess's law. **[1]**

**3.** Explain why bond enthalpy values give only approximate results. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate ΔH for `C₂H₄ + H₂ → C₂H₆` using these mean bond enthalpies (kJ mol⁻¹):
C=C 612, C–C 348, C–H 412, H–H 436. **[4]**

**5.** 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid is added to 50.0 cm³ of 1.00 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide. The temperature rises by 6.8 °C.
(c = 4.18 J g⁻¹ K⁻¹; assume density 1.00 g cm⁻³)

**(a)** Calculate the heat energy released. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the moles of water formed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the enthalpy of neutralisation. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain why the answer is negative. **[1]**

**6.** Give **three** reasons why an experimental enthalpy of combustion is usually less exothermic than the data book value, and **two** specific improvements. **[5]**

---

## Answers

**1.** The enthalpy change when **one mole** of a compound is formed from its **elements in their standard states** [1] under standard conditions [1].

**2.** The total enthalpy change is **independent of the route taken**, provided the initial and final conditions are the same [1].

**3.** Bond enthalpies are **mean values averaged across many compounds** [1] and apply only to species in the **gaseous** state [1].

**4.** Bonds broken: C=C + 4(C–H) + H–H = 612 + 1648 + 436 = 2696 [1]
Bonds made: C–C + 6(C–H) = 348 + 2472 = 2820 [1]
ΔH = 2696 − 2820 [1] = **−124 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].

**5. (a)** Total mass = 100 g [1]
q = mcΔT = 100 × 4.18 × 6.8 = **2842 J** [1].

**(b)** n(HCl) = 1.00 × 0.0500 = 0.0500 mol [1]; 1:1 ratio, so n(H₂O) = **0.0500 mol** [1].

**(c)** ΔH = −2842 ÷ 0.0500 [1] = **−56 840 J mol⁻¹ = −56.8 kJ mol⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** The reaction is **exothermic** — energy is released to the surroundings [1].

**6.** Any three reasons: **heat loss to the surroundings** [1]; **incomplete combustion** producing carbon monoxide or soot [1]; **evaporation of the fuel** from the wick [1]; heat absorbed by the apparatus; non-standard conditions.
Two improvements: use a **draught shield** to reduce heat loss [1]; use a **copper calorimeter** rather than glass, since it conducts heat to the water more efficiently, or **reduce the distance** between flame and container [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the mass of fuel rather than the total mass of solution in q = mcΔT.
- Reversing bonds broken and bonds made.
- Omitting the minus sign in ΔH = −q/n.
- Vague improvements such as "be more accurate".
