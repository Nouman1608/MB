## Section A

**1.** Define magnetic flux density. **[2]**

**2.** Show that the tesla is equivalent to kg s⁻² A⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A straight wire carries a current in a uniform magnetic field. State the angle between the wire and the field for which the force on the wire is **(a)** zero and **(b)** a maximum. **[2]**

**4.** A wire carries a current from left to right across the page in a uniform field directed out of the page. State the direction of the force on the wire. **[1]**

**5.** A 6.0 cm length of wire carrying 3.5 A lies in a uniform field of 0.40 T.

**(a)** Calculate the force on it when it is perpendicular to the field. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the force when the wire makes an angle of 25° with the field. **[1]**

**6.** Explain, in terms of magnetic fields, why a force acts on a current-carrying wire placed between the poles of a magnet. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A U-shaped magnet rests on a top-pan balance. A rigid horizontal wire passes between its poles, perpendicular to the field, and is clamped so that it cannot move. The length of wire in the field is 8.0 cm. When a current of 2.5 A is switched on, the balance reading decreases by 3.6 g.

**(a)** Calculate the force exerted on the magnet by the wire. **[1]**

**(b)** State the size and direction of the force exerted on the wire by the magnet, and name the law you used. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the flux density between the poles. **[2]**

**(d)** The current is reversed. State the new change in balance reading. **[1]**

**8.** A metal rod of mass 80 g and length 0.25 m rests horizontally on two horizontal rails, at right angles to them. A uniform vertical magnetic field acts over the region. A current is passed along the rod.

**(a)** Explain why the rod tends to roll along the rails rather than lift off them. **[2]**

**(b)** The field is now made horizontal and perpendicular to the rod, with flux density 0.40 T. Calculate the current needed for the magnetic force to support the rod's weight. **[2]**

**9.** A rectangular coil of 120 turns is placed in a uniform field of 0.15 T. Two sides of the coil, each 3.0 cm long, are perpendicular to the field and 2.0 cm apart. The plane of the coil is parallel to the field. The current in the coil is 0.40 A.

**(a)** Calculate the force on each 3.0 cm side. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why there is no resultant force on the coil. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the torque on the coil. **[2]**

**(d)** State the force on each of the other two sides, and explain your answer. **[1]**

**10.** A student investigates how the force on a wire depends on the current. She keeps 5.0 cm of wire perpendicular to a uniform field and obtains the readings below.

| I / A | 1.0 | 2.0 | 3.0 | 4.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| F / mN | 6.1 | 11.9 | 18.2 | 24.0 |

**(a)** State the relationship between F and I suggested by the data, with a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Use the data to determine the flux density. **[2]**

**11.** A light, flexible wire of length 0.10 m and mass 20 g hangs vertically from a fixed point, with its lower end connected through a loose lead so that it can swing. It is in a horizontal uniform field of 0.30 T perpendicular to the wire. A current of 5.0 A is passed through it.

**(a)** Calculate the magnetic force on the wire when it is vertical. **[1]**

**(b)** The wire swings to a new position and settles with the force balanced by the weight and the tension. Assuming the magnetic force stays horizontal and equal to your answer to (a), calculate the angle the wire makes with the vertical. **[2]**

**12.** An overhead cable carries a direct current of 400 A horizontally from east to west. At this place the Earth's magnetic field has a horizontal component of 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ T pointing north, and a vertical component of 4.5 × 10⁻⁵ T pointing downwards.

**(a)** Calculate the force on a 100 m length of cable due to the horizontal component of the field, and state its direction. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the force due to the vertical component, and state its direction. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The force acting per unit current per unit length [1] on a wire placed at right angles to the magnetic field [1].

**2.** B = F/(IL), so 1 T = 1 N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ [1]. 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻², so 1 T = kg m s⁻² A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = kg s⁻² A⁻¹ [1].

**3. (a)** 0° (or 180°): wire parallel to the field [1].

**(b)** 90°: wire perpendicular to the field [1].

**4.** Down the page [1].

**5. (a)** F = BIL = 0.40 × 3.5 × 0.060 = 0.084 N [1].

**(b)** F = 0.084 × sin 25° = 0.036 N [1].

**6.** The current produces its own magnetic field around the wire, which combines with the field of the magnet [1]. The resultant field is stronger on one side of the wire than the other, and the wire is pushed towards the weaker side [1].

**7. (a)** F = Δm g = 3.6 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 = 0.035 N, upwards on the magnet [1].

**(b)** 0.035 N [1] downwards on the wire, by Newton's third law [1].

**(c)** B = F/(IL) = 0.0353 ÷ (2.5 × 0.080) [1] = 0.18 T [1].

**(d)** The reading increases by 3.6 g [1].

**8. (a)** The field is vertical and the current horizontal, so the force is perpendicular to both: horizontal [1], along the rails, so it pushes the rod along them and has no vertical component to lift it [1].

**(b)** BIL = mg, so I = mg/(BL) = 0.080 × 9.81 ÷ (0.40 × 0.25) [1] = 7.8 A [1].

**9. (a)** F = NBIL = 120 × 0.15 × 0.40 × 0.030 [1] = 0.22 N [1].

**(b)** The currents in the two sides are in opposite directions, so the forces are equal and opposite [1].

**(c)** Torque = F × separation = 0.216 × 0.020 [1] = 4.3 × 10⁻³ N m [1].

**(d)** Zero, because the current in those sides is parallel (or antiparallel) to the field, so sin θ = 0 [1].

**10. (a)** F is proportional to I [1]: F/I is constant at about 6.0 mN A⁻¹ (doubling I doubles F) [1].

**(b)** Gradient (or mean F/I) = 6.0 × 10⁻³ N A⁻¹ = BL [1]; B = 6.0 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.050 = 0.12 T [1].

**11. (a)** F = BIL = 0.30 × 5.0 × 0.10 = 0.15 N [1].

**(b)** tan θ = F/(mg) = 0.15 ÷ (0.020 × 9.81) = 0.76 [1]; θ = 37° [1].

**12. (a)** Current west, field north: F = BIL = 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 400 × 100 = 0.80 N [1], directed vertically downwards [1].

**(b)** F = 4.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 400 × 100 = 1.8 N [1], directed horizontally towards the south [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "at right angles to the field" in the definition of B.
- Using the angle between the wire and something other than the field.
- Forgetting the number of turns for a coil.
- In balance questions, not using Newton's third law to connect the force on the magnet with the force on the wire.
- Giving directions as "sideways" or "outwards" instead of a definite direction.
- Not converting grams to kilograms or centimetres to metres.
