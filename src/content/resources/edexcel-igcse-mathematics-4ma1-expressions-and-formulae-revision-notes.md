Quick-recall notes for section 2.3, Expressions and formulae, in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1). Foundation statements A to F apply to both tiers. Higher statement A (the subject appearing twice, or as a power or root) is Higher tier only. For full explanations, read the [expressions and formulae study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-expressions-and-formulae/); for exam-style questions, use the [expressions and formulae practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-expressions-and-formulae-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unknown | A letter standing for one particular number to be found |
| Variable | A letter that can take a range of values |
| Expression | Terms with no equals sign, such as 8 + 2.5h |
| Formula | A rule linking quantities, with a subject, such as C = 8 + 2.5h |
| Subject | The letter on its own on one side of a formula |
| Substitute | Replace letters by given values |
| Derive | Build a formula or expression yourself from words or a diagram |
| Rearrange | Change which letter is the subject |

## Notation in one table

| Conventional | Means |
|---|---|
| 5xy | 5 × x × y |
| x²y | x × x × y |
| 3(a + b) | 3 × (a + b) |
| a/3 | a ÷ 3 |
| ab² | a × b × b (only b is squared) |
| (ab)² | a²b² |
| −x | −1 × x |

## Substitution rules

- Brackets round every substituted value: v = −6 gives v² = (−6)² = 36.
- Keep fractions exact: 1/2 − (−1/3) = 5/6.
- Work out the numerator and the denominator separately, then divide.
- Follow the order of operations: in ½mv², square v before multiplying.

## From words to a formula

```
fixed amount + rate × quantity          C = 8 + 2.5h
total shared equally                    each = T/n
"is 3 more than twice"                  y = 2x + 3
"is 3 less than twice"                  y = 2x − 3
"is twice 3 more than x"                y = 2(x + 3)
```

Always define your letters with units, and always start with the subject ("C = ...").

## Deriving from a diagram

- Label every edge on a sketch, including edges the diagram does not mark, in terms of the letters.
- Perimeter: add every outside edge. Area: split into rectangles, or take a whole rectangle and subtract the missing part.
- Removing a square from a corner leaves the perimeter unchanged; cutting a square notch from the middle of an edge increases the perimeter by two sides of the square.
- Simplify the final expression and state the units (cm, cm²).

## Changing the subject

**Subject appears once (both tiers):** undo the operations in reverse order.

```
v = u + at        →   t = (v − u)/a
K = (a − 3b)/4    →   b = (a − 4K)/3
A = πr²           →   r = √(A/π)
P = 2(l + w)      →   w = P/2 − l     or   w = (P − 2l)/2
```

**Subject twice (Higher):** clear fractions, expand, collect subject terms on one side, factorise, divide.

```
2k + 7 = (3 − k)/w
2kw + 7w = 3 − k
2kw + k = 3 − 7w
k(2w + 1) = 3 − 7w
k = (3 − 7w)/(2w + 1)
```

**Power or root of the subject (Higher):** isolate it, then square, cube, square-root or cube-root last.

```
T = 2π√(l/g)    →   l = gT²/(4π²)
E = ½mv²        →   v = √(2E/m)
V = (4/3)πr³    →   r = ∛(3V/(4π))
```

## Exam traps

- **Dividing one term only.** From 4K = a − 3b you need b = (a − 4K)/3, with the whole of a − 4K divided by 3.
- **Root of a sum.** √(x² + 9) is not x + 3.
- **Squaring a coefficient.** (2π)² = 4π², so T² gives 4π² l/g.
- **Subject on both sides.** If the final line still has k on the right, it is not finished.
- **Negative substitution.** −x² with x = −3 is −9, but (−x)² is 9.
- **Rounded values.** When a later part uses your rearranged formula, round only the final answer.

## A quick check for any rearrangement

Pick easy numbers, find the value of the original subject, then put everything into your new formula and see if you get back the letter you started with.

```
v = u + at with u = 2, a = 3, t = 4 gives v = 14
t = (v − u)/a = (14 − 2)/3 = 4                      correct
```

## Self-test

1. Write a × 4 × b × b in conventional notation.
2. E = ½mv². Find E when m = 0.4 and v = −6.
3. T = (a − b)/(ab). Find T when a = 1/2 and b = −1/3.
4. A plumber charges £35 call-out plus £28 per hour (prices invented). Write a formula for the charge £P for h hours.
5. Make t the subject of v = u + at.
6. Make b the subject of K = (a − 3b)/4.
7. Make r the subject of A = πr², where r > 0.
8. Make w the subject of P = 2(l + w).
9. A rectangle x cm by (x + 4) cm has a 2 cm square removed from a corner. Write expressions for its area and perimeter.
10. (Higher) Make k the subject of 2k + 7 = (3 − k)/w.
11. (Higher) Make l the subject of T = 2π√(l/g).
12. (Higher) Make v the subject of E = ½mv², where v > 0.

### Answers

1. **4ab²**.
2. 0.2 × 36 = **7.2**.
3. (5/6) ÷ (−1/6) = **−5**.
4. **P = 35 + 28h**.
5. **t = (v − u)/a**.
6. **b = (a − 4K)/3**.
7. **r = √(A/π)**.
8. **w = P/2 − l** (or w = (P − 2l)/2).
9. Area **x² + 4x − 4** cm²; perimeter **4x + 8** cm (the same as the full rectangle).
10. **k = (3 − 7w)/(2w + 1)**.
11. **l = gT²/(4π²)**.
12. **v = √(2E/m)**.
