## Section A

**1.** Give three reasons why the Warsaw Pact was formed in 1955. **[3]**

**2.** Identify three methods used by communist governments to control the population of the satellite states. **[3]**

**3.** Explain two differences between the crises in Poland and Hungary in 1956. **[4]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why the Berlin Wall was built in 1961. **[4]**

**5.** Identify three features of the nomenklatura system or the gerontocracy under Brezhnev. **[3]**

**6.** Give three effects of the war in Afghanistan on the Soviet Union. **[3]**

**7.** Identify three problems facing the Soviet economy when Gorbachev took power in 1985. **[3]**

**8.** State three of the demands made by the Gdańsk strikers in August 1980. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**9.** "The Prague Spring was a greater threat to Soviet control than the Hungarian uprising." Assess this view. **[30]**

**10.** Evaluate the reasons why the Soviet economy stagnated in the years 1964-85. **[30]**

**11.** Assess the extent to which glasnost, rather than perestroika, undermined the Soviet system. **[30]**

**12.** "Solidarity was the most important factor in the collapse of communism in Eastern Europe." Assess this view. **[30]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: as a response to West Germany joining NATO in May 1955 (1); to give a legal basis for stationing Soviet troops in member states (1); to create a unified military command under Soviet control (1); to tie the satellite states more firmly to the USSR (1); as a bargaining counter against NATO in negotiations (1). **[3]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: censorship of press, radio and publishing (1); secret police and informer networks such as the Stasi (1); restrictions on emigration and travel to the West (1); pressure on churches, such as the internment of Cardinal Wyszyński (1); one-party control of elections, unions and youth groups (1); show trials and imprisonment of opponents (1). **[3]**

**3.** Two differences, 2 marks each: Gomułka kept Poland in the Warsaw Pact and the party's monopoly, whereas Nagy allowed other parties and announced withdrawal from the Pact (2); Khrushchev negotiated a settlement in Poland, but in Hungary Soviet forces invaded on 4 November (2); the Polish crisis ended with a reforming communist accepted by Moscow, while the Hungarian rising ended with Kádár installed and Nagy later executed (2). **[4]**

**4.** Two reasons, 2 marks each: mass emigration of East Germans through West Berlin, many of them young and skilled, threatened the GDR's economy and legitimacy (2); Khrushchev's demand that the West leave Berlin (1958) had failed, so sealing the border was an alternative way to stabilise the GDR (2); West Berlin was a showcase of Western prosperity and a base for espionage (2). **[4]**

**5.** Any three, 1 mark each: posts filled from party-approved lists (1); officials enjoyed privileges such as special shops, housing and holidays (1); "stability of cadres" meant officials kept posts for decades (1); corruption scandals in republics such as Uzbekistan (1); the Politburo's average age rose and leaders were often ill (1); Andropov and Chernenko died within two years of taking office (1). **[3]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: thousands of Soviet troops were killed and many more wounded (1); high financial cost at a time of economic weakness (1); damage to army morale and public trust (1); international condemnation, the US grain embargo and the Olympic boycott of 1980 (1); the end of détente and the start of the Second Cold War (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: stagnant growth and outdated technology (1); food shortages and rationing in some areas (1); a growing budget deficit (1); absenteeism and alcoholism among workers (1); poor housing and living conditions (1); the burden of defence spending (1). **[3]**

**8.** Any three, 1 mark each: free trade unions independent of the party (1); the right to strike (1); freedom of speech and publication (1); release of political prisoners (1); broadcast of Sunday Mass on the radio (1); wage rises and improved supplies (1). **[3]**

**9.** Indicative content. **For:** the Prague Spring developed inside the party leadership itself, so it threatened to show that communism could be reformed from within; the end of censorship risked spreading ideas to Poland, the GDR and Ukraine; neighbouring leaders such as Ulbricht and Gomułka pressed for action, and the response required a multinational invasion and a new doctrine. **Against:** Hungary in 1956 involved armed fighting, the formation of other parties and an announced exit from the Warsaw Pact -- a direct challenge to the bloc; it required a large military operation with heavy casualties; Dubček insisted that Czechoslovakia would stay in the Pact. **Criterion:** threat measured by the challenge to the party's monopoly, to the Pact, and the risk of spread. **Judgement:** Hungary was the more immediate military threat; the Prague Spring was the more dangerous ideological one, which is why the Brezhnev Doctrine followed. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**10.** Indicative content. **Political causes:** stability of cadres, the nomenklatura's resistance to change, corruption and an ageing leadership. **Economic system:** central planning rewarded quantity over quality; the Kosygin reforms were diluted; the 10th Five Year Plan missed key targets; technology lagged; collective farms remained unproductive, requiring grain imports. **Defence:** the arms race and Afghanistan diverted resources. **Masking factors:** oil and gas revenue in the 1970s delayed reform; when prices fell in the 1980s problems became visible. **Judgement:** the planned system was the root cause, but political leadership determined that it was not reformed; strong answers show how each reinforced the other. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**11.** Indicative content. **Glasnost:** after Chernobyl, freedom of expression exposed corruption, Stalin's crimes and the Nazi-Soviet Pact's secret protocol, destroying the party's claim to legitimacy; it allowed nationalist movements in the Baltic states and elsewhere to organise; it gave reformers like Yeltsin a platform. **Perestroika:** partial market reforms (enterprise law, cooperatives) disrupted supplies, increased shortages and inflation, and caused fragmentation; economic failure turned people against Gorbachev. **Demokratizatsiya:** elections in 1989-90 and the end of the party's monopoly gave rivals power bases in the republics. **Judgement:** many answers argue glasnost was decisive because it turned economic failure into political rejection; others argue that without perestroika's economic collapse glasnost would not have produced revolt. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**

**12.** Indicative content. **For Solidarity:** the first independent mass movement in the bloc; ten million members showed the party had lost the working class; martial law could not destroy it; round-table talks and the June 1989 election made Poland the first to transfer power, setting an example for the region. **Other factors:** Gorbachev's refusal to use force (the Sinatra Doctrine) was decisive; economic decline and debt across the bloc; Hungary's opening of the Austrian border, which triggered the East German exodus; the Church and the Polish Pope; Western influence and the Helsinki rights; nationalism. **Judgement:** Solidarity was the pioneer but succeeded only when Soviet policy changed; strong answers explain how Gorbachev's choices turned Polish change into a regional collapse. AO1 15 + AO2 15 = **[30]**
