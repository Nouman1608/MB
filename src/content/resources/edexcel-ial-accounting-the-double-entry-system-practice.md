## Questions

**1.** Sandpiper Cycles records the following. State the account to be debited and the account to be credited for each.

**(a)** Display racks costing 2,400 are bought, paid from the bank. **[2]**
**(b)** Bicycles are sold on credit to Skerry Cycling Club for 1,850. **[2]**
**(c)** The owner takes a bicycle costing 420 for personal use. **[2]**

**2.** Cobalt Bay Divers has these balances at 30 June: bank 7,350 (debit); equipment 22,000; purchases 14,600; rent 4,400; wages 6,900; drawings 4,200; trade receivables 5,400; sales returns 450; capital 30,000; sales 31,900; trade payables 3,100; purchases returns 300. Prepare the trial balance. **[7]**

**3.** Greywell Print's sales day book for May shows invoices of 3,150, 2,480 and 1,970. Its sales returns day book shows credit notes of 260 and 140.

**(a)** Calculate the total of the sales day book and state how it is posted to the general ledger. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the total of the sales returns day book and state how it is posted. **[2]**

**4.** Corriman Pottery has a bank balance of 3,600 (debit) on 1 September. During the month it: receives 1,940 from customer A after allowing discount of 60; pays supplier B 2,750 after receiving discount of 50; banks cash sales of 920; pays rent of 650 by bank; receives 1,175 from customer C after allowing discount of 25; and takes drawings of 400 from the bank.

**(a)** Prepare the bank account with discount columns, balanced. **[6]**
**(b)** State the ledger entries for the totals of the two discount columns. **[2]**

**5.** Nethercote Books uses an imprest petty cash system with a float of 180. Payments in the month are stationery 46, travel 38, cleaning 22 and refreshments 15.

**(a)** Calculate the total paid and the reimbursement needed. **[2]**
**(b)** State the double entry for the reimbursement and the postings from the analysis columns. **[2]**

**6.** Prepare journal entries, with narratives, for Hawksmoor Candles.

**(a)** Machinery costing 5,800 is bought on credit from Tarrant Tools. **[2]**
**(b)** An irrecoverable debt of 740 owed by Daventry Garden Services is written off. **[2]**
**(c)** The owner takes goods that cost 310 for personal use. **[2]**

**7.** Sandpiper Cycles has a year end of 30 September. Electricity paid during the year is 4,180; 520 was owing at 1 October and 690 is owing at 30 September. On 1 July it paid insurance of 3,240 for twelve months; 600 was prepaid at 1 October.

**(a)** Prepare the electricity account, balanced and with the transfer to the statement of profit or loss. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the insurance prepaid at 30 September and the insurance charge for the year. **[4]**
**(c)** State where the two year-end balances appear in the statement of financial position. **[1]**

**8.** Greywell Print has opening inventory of 6,150, purchases of 52,800, purchases returns of 1,350 and closing inventory of 7,200. Drawings for the year are 21,000.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of sales. **[2]**
**(b)** State the double entry that records closing inventory at the year end. **[2]**
**(c)** State where the drawings are transferred and explain why they are not in the statement of profit or loss. **[2]**

**9.** Bexford Garden Centre has trade receivables of 71,400 at 31 December before writing off irrecoverable debts of 1,400. The remaining receivables are analysed: not yet due 44,000 (1.5%); up to 30 days overdue 15,000 (5%); 31 to 90 days overdue 7,500 (12%); over 90 days overdue 3,500 (40%). The percentages are the allowance rates. The existing allowance is 3,150. During the year 300 was received from a customer whose debt was written off earlier.

**(a)** Calculate the required allowance. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare the allowance for irrecoverable debts account. **[3]**
**(c)** Show the trade receivables line in the statement of financial position. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the net charge to the statement of profit or loss for irrecoverable debts, the allowance change and the recovery. **[1]**

**10.** Hawksmoor Candles has replaced its manual ledgers with accounting software.

**(a)** Explain **two** ways in which the software helps with recording transactions and preparing the trial balance. **[4]**
**(b)** A credit purchase invoice for 3,870 is keyed as 3,780. Explain why the trial balance still agrees and state the effect on the figure for purchases. **[4]**

**11.** Marlowe Optics has a provisional profit for the year of 42,300. These items have not yet been recorded: rent of 900 is accrued; insurance of 650 is prepaid; an irrecoverable debt of 480 must be written off; commission of 350 is accrued income. Trade receivables are 24,480 before the write-off. The allowance is to be 3% of the remaining receivables and the existing allowance is 540.

**(a)** Calculate the revised profit for the year. **[7]**
**(b)** State the statement of financial position item and classification for each of the rent, insurance and commission adjustments. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Debit Display racks, a non-current asset increasing [1]; credit Bank [1].
**(b)** Debit Skerry Cycling Club (a trade receivable) [1]; credit Sales [1].
**(c)** Debit Drawings [1]; credit Purchases [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name the account on each side, and say what type it is, so the choice of side follows visibly from the rule.

**2.** Debit column: bank, equipment and trade receivables [1]; purchases, rent and wages [1]; drawings and sales returns [1]. Credit column: capital and sales [1]; trade payables and purchases returns [1]. Total debits 65,300 [1]; total credits 65,300 [1].

*Examiner insight:* Sales returns are a debit and purchases returns a credit. Showing both column totals makes the statement complete.

**3. (a)** 3,150 + 2,480 + 1,970 = 7,600 [1]. Debit the customers' personal accounts for each invoice [1]; credit Sales with the total 7,600 [1].
**(b)** 260 + 140 = 400 [1]. Debit Sales returns 400 and credit the customers' accounts [1].
*Examiner insight:* State both that personal accounts are posted individually and that the nominal account is posted from the total.

**4. (a)**

| Details | Discount allowed | Bank | Details | Discount received | Bank |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Balance b/d | | 3,600 | Supplier B | 50 | 2,750 |
| Customer A | 60 | 1,940 | Rent | | 650 |
| Cash sales | | 920 | Drawings | | 400 |
| Customer C | 25 | 1,175 | Balance c/d | | 3,835 |
| | 85 | 7,635 | | 50 | 7,635 |

Opening balance [1]; receipts with discounts allowed [1]; supplier B payment with discount received [1]; rent and drawings [1]; balance c/d 3,835 with totals 7,635 [1]; discount totals 85 and 50 [1].
**(b)** Debit Discount allowed 85 [1]; credit Discount received 50 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Discount columns are memorandum columns, not part of the bank balance. Keep each discount on the same side as its receipt or payment.

**5. (a)** 46 + 38 + 22 + 15 = 121 [1]; the reimbursement equals the amount spent, 121 [1].
**(b)** Debit Stationery 46, Travel 38, Cleaning 22 and Refreshments 15 from the analysis columns [1]; credit Bank 121 for the reimbursement [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say that the float is restored to its fixed amount; reimbursing the whole float does not fit the imprest idea.

**6. (a)** Debit Machinery 5,800; credit Tarrant Tools 5,800 [1]. Narrative: machinery bought on credit [1].
**(b)** Debit Irrecoverable debts 740; credit Daventry Garden Services 740 [1]. Narrative: debt written off as irrecoverable [1].
**(c)** Debit Drawings 310; credit Purchases 310 [1]. Narrative: goods taken by the owner for personal use [1].
*Examiner insight:* A journal entry needs a narrative as well as accounts and amounts.

**7. (a)**

| Electricity | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bank | 4,180 | Balance b/d | 520 |
| Balance c/d | 690 | Statement of profit or loss | 4,350 |
| | 4,870 | | 4,870 |

Bank and opening balance on the correct sides [1]; closing accrual 690 [1]; transfer 4,350 with totals 4,870 [1].
**(b)** Nine months of cover remain, 3,240 × 9/12 [1] = 2,430 prepaid [1]. Charge = 600 + 3,240 − 2,430 [1] = 1,410 [1].
**(c)** The electricity accrual is an other payable (current liability) and the insurance prepayment is an other receivable (current asset) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the opening balance in the formula for the charge; using only cash paid misses last year's accrual or prepayment.

**8. (a)** 6,150 + 52,800 − 1,350 − 7,200 [1] = 50,400 [1].
**(b)** Debit Inventory 7,200 [1]; credit the statement of profit or loss 7,200 [1].
**(c)** Drawings are closed to capital [1]. They are the owner taking money out, not a cost of running the business (business entity), so they do not reduce profit [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason as well as the treatment: the owner and the business are separate.

**9. (a)** After write-off, 71,400 − 1,400 = 70,000 [1]. Bands: 660 and 750 [1]; 900 and 1,400 [1]. Total allowance 3,710 [1].
**(b)**

| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | | | |
|---|---|---|---|
| Balance c/d | 3,710 | Balance b/d | 3,150 |
| | | Statement of profit or loss | 560 |
| | 3,710 | | 3,710 |

Opening balance 3,150 on the credit side [1]; increase of 560 [1]; closing 3,710 and totals [1].
**(c)** Trade receivables 70,000 less allowance 3,710 [1] = 66,290 [1].
**(d)** 1,400 + 560 − 300 = 1,660 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write off first, then apply the rates. Showing each band separately lets a reader follow your method.

**10. (a)** Posting once completes both sides of the double entry [1], giving faster entry with fewer posting slips [1]. The trial balance is produced directly from the ledger balances [1], with no hand listing or adding [1].
**(b)** The software posts 3,780 to both Purchases and the supplier [1], so total debits still equal total credits [1]. Purchases is understated by 3,870 − 3,780 = 90 [1]. Only comparing the invoice with the entry would find it [1].
*Examiner insight:* Say what the software does and what it cannot do: accurate processing of wrong input still gives a wrong result.

**11. (a)**

Start with 42,300 [1]; rent decreases profit [1]; insurance prepaid increases it [1]; irrecoverable debt decreases it [1]. After write-off receivables are 24,000, so the allowance is 720 [1]; the increase on 540 is 180, decreasing profit [1]. Commission accrued increases profit, giving 41,740 [1].
**(b)** Accrued rent: other payable, a current liability [1]. Prepaid insurance: other receivable, a current asset [1]. Accrued commission: other receivable (accrued income), a current asset [1].
*Examiner insight:* State whether each adjustment raises or lowers profit. For the allowance, only the change in it, not its full amount, affects profit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting returns accounts on the wrong side of the trial balance.
- Treating discount columns as part of the bank balance.
- Reimbursing the whole petty cash float rather than the amount spent.
- Ignoring the opening accrual or prepayment in an expense charge.
- Applying allowance rates before writing off irrecoverable debts, or putting the whole allowance rather than the change through the statement of profit or loss.
- Treating drawings as an expense.
- Claiming ICT removes errors without saying which it cannot detect.
