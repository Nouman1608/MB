This guide covers subtopic **1.16 Money** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. The syllabus lists two outcomes: calculate with money, and convert from one currency to another. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page covers bills, unit costs, comparing deals, currency conversion in one or two steps, and exchange fees. The exchange rates in the examples are invented for practice and are not real rates.

Companion pages: the [Money revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-money-revision-notes/) and the [Money practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-money-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

1. Calculate with money.
2. Convert from one currency to another.

Money problems use all the other number skills: the four operations, decimals, percentages, ratio and rates. The aim in this subtopic is to apply them accurately in a financial setting and to present the answers in the correct form. Questions on money appear on both papers, and the mark scheme usually rewards clear working.

## Writing money correctly

Money amounts are written with two decimal places when the amount has cents (or pence, or the equivalent). The calculator display 4.8 means $4.80, and 12.5 means $12.50. Round money to the nearest cent unless the question tells you otherwise.

```
23.075 → $23.08       (round the third decimal place)
33.9285... → $33.93
```

Keep the full calculator value during the working, and round only at the end.

## Bills and change

Work out each line of a bill separately, add them, and then find the change with a subtraction.

**Worked example.** A shopper buys 4 notebooks at $2.35 each, 2.5 kg of apples at $3.60 per kg and 6 pens at $0.45 each. She pays with a $50 note. Find the total cost and the change.

```
notebooks: 4 x 2.35  = $9.40
apples:    2.5 x 3.60 = $9.00
pens:      6 x 0.45  = $2.70

total = 9.40 + 9.00 + 2.70 = $21.10
change = 50 - 21.10 = $28.90
```

A good habit is to set the lines out in a column so that the decimal points line up.

## Unit costs and best buys

The cost of a quantity is the unit cost multiplied by the quantity.

```
cost of 2.4 m of cloth at $7.50 per metre = 2.4 x 7.50 = $18.00
cost of 35 litres of fuel at $1.38 per litre = 35 x 1.38 = $48.30
```

To compare two offers, find the cost of the same quantity, or the unit cost.

**Worked example.** A 1.5 litre bottle of juice costs $2.64. A 2 litre bottle costs $3.40. Which is better value?

```
1.5 L: 2.64 ÷ 1.5 = $1.76 per litre
2 L:   3.40 ÷ 2   = $1.70 per litre
```

The 2 litre bottle is cheaper per litre.

## Sharing a cost

To share a cost equally, divide by the number of people and round to the nearest cent.

```
$184.60 shared between 8 people: 184.60 ÷ 8 = 23.075 → $23.08 each
```

Because of the rounding, 8 payments of $23.08 total $184.64, which is 4 cents more. This is normal, and a question will only ask for the share.

## Wages, pay and deductions

Pay is rate × time. Deductions such as tax are percentages of the pay.

**Worked example.** A worker earns $14.20 per hour for 40 hours. 12% is taken off for tax and other deductions. Find his pay after deductions.

```
gross pay = 14.20 x 40 = $568.00
deduction = 0.12 x 568 = $68.16
net pay = 568 - 68.16 = $499.84
```

An alternative is to multiply by 0.88 directly: 568 × 0.88 = $499.84.

## Hire purchase and extra cost

Buying in instalments often costs more than paying cash. Work out the total paid, then compare it with the cash price.

**Worked example.** A sofa has a cash price of $620. Hire purchase terms are a deposit of $240 and 12 monthly payments of $38.50. Find the total paid and the extra cost as a percentage of the cash price.

```
total paid = 240 + 12 x 38.50 = 240 + 462 = $702
extra = 702 - 620 = $82
percentage extra = 82 ÷ 620 x 100 = 13.2% (3 s.f.)
```

## Profit and savings

**Worked example.** A trader buys 12 items at $18.50 each and sells all of them at $24.90 each. Find the profit.

```
cost = 12 x 18.50 = $222.00
income = 12 x 24.90 = $298.80
profit = 298.80 - 222.00 = $76.80
```

**Worked example.** Rui saves $85 each month for 18 months and receives a gift of $120. How much does he have?

```
85 x 18 = 1530,    1530 + 120 = $1 650
```

## Phone plans and fixed charges

Many costs have a fixed charge plus a charge per unit.

**Worked example.** A phone plan costs $12 per month plus $0.15 per minute of calls. Find the cost for a month with 140 minutes of calls.

```
cost = 12 + 0.15 x 140 = 12 + 21 = $33
```

## Converting currencies

An exchange rate tells you how much of one currency you get for one unit of another. Write down the rate in words and decide the direction before you calculate.

**Worked example.** Take $1 = €0.92 (invented rate).

- To change dollars to euros, multiply by 0.92.
- To change euros to dollars, divide by 0.92.

```
€460 into dollars:  460 ÷ 0.92 = $500
$500 into euros:    500 x 0.92 = €460
```

A sense check: the euro is worth more than a dollar in this example, so there are fewer euros than dollars for the same value. The answer of $500 is more than 460, as it should be.

### Converting through a third currency

If you are given rates relative to the dollar for two currencies, convert in two steps.

**Worked example.** $1 = €0.92 and $1 = £0.79 (invented rates). Change €460 into pounds.

```
step 1: €460 → dollars:   460 ÷ 0.92 = $500
step 2: dollars → pounds: 500 x 0.79 = £395
```

## Exchange fees and commission

A bureau may charge a fee, either as a fixed amount or a percentage. Decide whether the fee is taken before or after the exchange, as the question states.

**Worked example.** A traveller exchanges $800 into euros at $1 = €0.92. The bureau takes a 2% commission from the euros. How many euros does she receive?

```
exchange: 800 x 0.92 = €736
commission: 2% of 736 = €14.72
received: 736 - 14.72 = €721.28
```

Using the multiplier method, 736 × 0.98 = €721.28.

**Worked example.** Bureau A offers $1 = €0.92 but charges a fixed fee of $4. Bureau B offers $1 = €0.90 with no fee. Which bureau gives more euros for $300?

```
A: (300 - 4) x 0.92 = 296 x 0.92 = €272.32
B: 300 x 0.90 = €270.00
```

Bureau A gives more, by €2.32.

### Changing back

When money is exchanged and then changed back, the traveller usually loses money because the buying and selling rates differ.

**Worked example.** A tourist changes £400 into dollars at £1 = $1.25. She spends $380. She changes the rest back into pounds at $1 = £0.78. How many pounds does she receive?

```
400 x 1.25 = $500
500 - 380 = $120 left
120 x 0.78 = £93.60
```

## Exam technique

- State the exchange rate you are using and show the multiplication or division.
- Sense check the direction: a currency with a smaller value per unit gives a larger number.
- Write money answers with the currency sign and two decimal places.
- Keep full values until the end, then round once.
- For a multi-step problem, label each stage (cost, income, change, fee).
- Check whether a percentage applies to the original or the new amount.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying when you should divide in a currency question (or the reverse).
- Writing $4.8 instead of $4.80.
- Rounding intermediate values.
- Applying commission to the wrong amount.
- Forgetting the deposit when finding the total hire purchase cost.
- Comparing the cost of different quantities instead of the cost per unit.

## Where this leads

Money questions bring together percentages, rates and ratio. They also prepare you for compound interest and exponential growth, such as the value of an investment or a loan.
