## Questions

**1.** (a) Write 160° in radians, in terms of π. **[1]**
**(b)** Write 11π/12 radians in degrees. **[1]**
**(c)** Write 0.9 radians in degrees, to 1 decimal place. **[1]**

**2.** In triangle PQR, angle P = 54°, angle Q = 67° and the side opposite P has length 12.6 cm. Find the lengths of the other two sides. **[4]**

**3.** In triangle ABC, AB = 5.8 cm, BC = 9.1 cm and angle ABC = 112°.
**(a)** Find the length of AC. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**4.** A triangle has sides of length 6 cm, 7 cm and 9 cm. Find the size of its largest angle, and the area of the triangle. **[5]**

**5.** A sector of a circle has radius 9.5 cm and angle 0.8 radians. Find its arc length, its area and its perimeter. **[4]**

**6.** A circular sector has radius 5 cm, and its curved edge is 8.5 cm long. Find the angle of the sector in radians and the area of the sector. **[3]**

**7.** A sector of a circle has perimeter 24 cm and area 36 cm². Find the radius and the angle of the sector. **[5]**

**8.** A glass panel is cut from a circle of radius 12 cm by a straight cut AB. The cut AB subtends an angle of 2π/3 at the centre O. The panel is the segment between AB and the minor arc.
**(a)** Find the exact area of the panel. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the exact perimeter of the panel. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** State the period of y = tan x and the equations of its asymptotes for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°. **[2]**
**(b)** For y = 3 sin 2x, state the period, the maximum value, and the smallest positive value of x at which the maximum occurs. **[3]**

**10.** It is given that sin 38° = 0.6157, cos 38° = 0.7880 and tan 38° = 0.7813, all to 4 decimal places. Write down the value of each of the following. **[5]**
sin 142°, cos 142°, sin 218°, cos 322°, tan 218°

**11.** A walker leaves a camp at P and goes 6.5 km on a bearing of 070° to a lake at Q. She then walks 9 km on a bearing of 175° to a hut at R.
**(a)** Show that angle PQR = 75°. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the straight-line distance PR. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the bearing of R from P. **[3]**

**12.** A sector of a circle has centre O and radius 10 cm. The chord joining the two ends of the arc has length 16 cm.
**(a)** Use the cosine rule to find the angle of the sector, in radians to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the perimeter of the sector. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the area of the region enclosed by the chord and the arc. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 160 × π/180 = 8π/9 [1]
**(b)** 11π/12 × 180/π = **165°** [1]
**(c)** 0.9 × 180/π = **51.6°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication by π/180 or 180/π, because a bare answer to an angle conversion gives little to credit if a digit is wrong, and an exact answer in terms of π is usually the form asked for.

**2.** Angle R = 180° − 54° − 67° = 59° [1]
q / sin 67° = 12.6 / sin 54° [1]
q = 12.6 × sin 67° ÷ sin 54° = **14.3 cm** [1]
r = 12.6 × sin 59° ÷ sin 54° = **13.3 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* When two angles are given, find the third before using the sine rule; setting up the ratio with the correct matching pair is the step most likely to be credited even if the value is slightly off.

**3. (a)** AC² = 5.8² + 9.1² − 2 × 5.8 × 9.1 × cos 112° [1]
= 33.64 + 82.81 + 39.5... = 156.0... [1]
AC = **12.5 cm** [1]
**(b)** Area = ½ × 5.8 × 9.1 × sin 112° [1]
= **24.5 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Cos 112° is negative, so the two products add. Writing the substitution line before evaluating lets the method be seen even if a sign slips.

**4.** Largest angle is opposite the 9 cm side. cos θ = (6² + 7² − 9²) / (2 × 6 × 7) [1]
= 4/84 = 0.0476... [1]
θ = **87.3°** [1]
Area = ½ × 6 × 7 × sin 87.3° [1]
= **21.0 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Use the unrounded angle in the area, since a rounded angle can shift the third significant figure; identify why you are choosing the 9 cm side so the working is easy to follow.

**5.** Arc = 9.5 × 0.8 = **7.6 cm** [1]
Area = ½ × 9.5² × 0.8 = **36.1 cm²** [1]
Perimeter = 2 × 9.5 + 7.6 [1]
= **26.6 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A common loss is giving the arc length as the perimeter. Writing 2r + arc makes it clear the radii were included.

**6.** θ = 8.5/5 = **1.7 rad** [1]
Area = ½ × 5² × 1.7, or ½ × 5 × 8.5 [1]
= **21.25 cm²**, which is 21.3 cm² to 3 s.f. [1]
*Examiner insight:* The shortcut A = ½rl is a valid alternative, but quote the formula you use so the method is clear.

**7.** Perimeter: 2r + rθ = 24, so rθ = 24 − 2r [1]
Area: ½r(rθ) = 36 [1]
½r(24 − 2r) = 36, so 12r − r² = 36 [1]
r² − 12r + 36 = 0, so (r − 6)² = 0 and **r = 6 cm** [1]
θ = (24 − 12)/6 = **2 rad** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A repeated root means only one sector fits, so do not hunt for a second solution. Substitute back to check that both the perimeter and the area are satisfied.

**8. (a)** Sector area = ½ × 12² × 2π/3 = 48π [1]
Triangle area = ½ × 12² × sin(2π/3) [1]
= 72 × √3/2 = 36√3 [1]
Panel = **48π − 36√3 cm²** [1]
**(b)** Arc = 12 × 2π/3 = 8π [1]
Chord² = 12² + 12² − 2 × 12 × 12 × cos(2π/3) = 288 + 144 = 432, so the chord is 12√3. Perimeter = **8π + 12√3 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Exact answers are expected, so keep π and √3 in the working. A decimal such as 88.4 can be given as a check but should not replace the exact form.

**9. (a)** Period is **180°** (π radians) [1]
Asymptotes at **x = 90° and x = 270°** [1]
**(b)** Period = 360° ÷ 2 = **180°** [1]
Maximum value **3** [1]
Maximum when 2x = 90°, so **x = 45°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Say how you reached each value, for example dividing 360° by 2, so that a slip in one part does not hide a correct idea in the others.

**10.** sin 142° = sin(180° − 38°) = **0.6157** [1]
cos 142° = −cos 38° = **−0.7880** [1]
sin 218° = sin(180° + 38°) = **−0.6157** [1]
cos 322° = cos(360° − 38°) = **0.7880** [1]
tan 218° = tan(180° + 38°) = **0.7813** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A rough sketch of the graph or the quadrant diagram prevents sign errors. Questions like this ask for values from the given data, not new evaluations.

**11. (a)** The bearing of P from Q is 070° + 180° = 250° [1]
Angle PQR = 250° − 175° = **75°** [1]
**(b)** PR² = 6.5² + 9² − 2 × 6.5 × 9 × cos 75° [1]
= 42.25 + 81 − 30.28... = 92.97... [1]
PR = **9.64 km** [1]
**(c)** sin(QPR) / 9 = sin 75° / 9.642... [1]
Angle QPR = **64.4°** [1]
R lies clockwise from Q as seen from P, so the bearing is 70° + 64.4° = **134.4°**, which is 134° to 3 s.f. [1]
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" the working must reach the stated value from first principles, so write the back bearing explicitly. In part (c), a sketch showing which side of PQ the point R lies on supports adding rather than subtracting.

**12. (a)** cos θ = (10² + 10² − 16²) / (2 × 10 × 10) [1]
= −56/200 = −0.28 [1]
θ = **1.85 rad** [1]
**(b)** Arc = 10 × 1.8546 = 18.55 [1]
Perimeter = 20 + 18.55 = **38.5 cm** [1]
**(c)** Sector = ½ × 100 × 1.8546 = 92.73 [1]
Triangle = ½ × 100 × sin θ = 48 (since sin θ = 0.96) [1]
Segment = 92.73 − 48 = **44.7 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A negative cosine gives an angle above π/2, which is correct here. Use the unrounded angle in later parts, since 1.85 alone would give 92.5 for the sector and shift the final digits.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating an angle such as 0.9 as degrees when it is in radians, which gives a very different value.
- Choosing the wrong pair in the sine rule, so that a side is divided by the sine of a non-matching angle.
- Dropping the minus sign when the cosine of an obtuse angle appears in the cosine rule.
- Giving the arc length alone when the question asks for the perimeter of a sector.
- Treating a segment as sector minus sector instead of sector minus triangle.
- Giving a decimal for an answer requested in terms of π or in surd form.
- Missing the obtuse second answer when an area or sine rule equation has two solutions.
- Mixing up bearings: adding or subtracting 180° wrongly, or measuring the angle between legs from the wrong side.
- Giving the period of y = sin 2x as 720° or 2π, instead of 180° or π.
