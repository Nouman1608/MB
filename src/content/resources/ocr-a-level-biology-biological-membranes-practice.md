## Questions

**1.** Give **three** roles of membranes within and at the surface of cells, with an example of each. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why the model of membrane structure is called the *fluid mosaic* model. **[2]**

**3.** State the role of each of the following in a cell-surface membrane: **(a)** cholesterol; **(b)** glycoproteins;
**(c)** channel proteins. **[3]**

**4.** Many drugs are designed to bind to membrane-bound receptors. Explain why a drug of this kind affects only
some cells in the body. **[2]**

**5.** A student placed beetroot pieces in water at 20, 40, 60 and 80 degrees Celsius for 30 minutes and measured
the absorbance of the water with a colorimeter.

**(a)** Explain why the pieces were washed in water before the experiment. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict and explain the results. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest how the experiment could be changed to investigate the effect of a solvent. **[1]**

**6.** Agar cubes containing an indicator were placed in acid. The pink colour disappears as acid diffuses in.

| Cube side (cm) | Surface area (cm squared) | Volume (cm cubed) |
|---|---|---|
| 0.5 | | |
| 1.0 | 6.0 | 1.0 |
| 3.0 | | |

**(a)** Complete the table and calculate the surface area to volume ratio for the 0.5 cm and 3.0 cm cubes. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain which cube becomes colourless first. **[2]**

**7.** Compare diffusion and facilitated diffusion. **[3]**

**8.** In a 4-minute period, acid diffused 3.4 mm into an agar block. Calculate the rate of diffusion in mm per
minute. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how a cell releases a protein hormone by exocytosis, and explain why this process needs ATP. **[3]**

**10.** Potato cylinders were placed in sucrose solutions. One cylinder's mass increased from 2.40 g to 2.58 g.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mass. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain this change in terms of water potential. **[2]**

**11.** Describe and explain what happens to a red blood cell and to a plant cell placed in pure water. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why a plant cell becomes plasmolysed in a concentrated salt solution. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three [1 each, with example]: partially permeable barrier between the cell and its environment
(cell-surface membrane); barrier between organelles and cytoplasm (for example nuclear envelope, lysosome
membrane); compartments within organelles (for example thylakoids, cristae); site of chemical reactions (for example
inner mitochondrial membrane in respiration); site of cell signalling (for example hormone receptors).

**2.** Fluid: phospholipids and proteins can move sideways within the bilayer [1]. Mosaic: proteins are scattered
through the phospholipid bilayer in a pattern [1].

**3. (a)** Regulates fluidity / stabilises the membrane [1]. **(b)** Act as receptors / antigens for cell recognition
/ cell attachment [1]. **(c)** Allow specific ions or polar molecules to cross through a hydrophilic pore [1].

**4.** The drug binds only to receptors with a complementary binding site [1]; only cells with that receptor on
their membrane respond [1].

**5. (a)** To remove pigment released from cells damaged by cutting, so only pigment leaking through membranes is
measured [1].
**(b)** Absorbance increases as temperature increases [1], because phospholipids gain kinetic energy and the
membrane becomes more fluid and permeable [1]; above about 40 to 60 degrees Celsius it rises sharply because membrane
proteins denature, leaving gaps [1].
**(c)** Keep the temperature constant and use different concentrations of ethanol (or another solvent) [1].

**6. (a)** 0.5 cm cube: surface area = 6 x 0.5 x 0.5 = 1.5 cm squared; volume = 0.125 cm cubed; ratio = **12 : 1**
[1]. 3.0 cm cube: surface area = 6 x 3 x 3 = 54 cm squared; volume = 27 cm cubed [1]; ratio = **2 : 1** [1].
**(b)** The 0.5 cm cube [1]: it has the largest surface area to volume ratio and the shortest distance to the
centre, so acid reaches the centre fastest [1].

**7.** Both are passive and move substances down a concentration gradient [1]. Diffusion is directly through the
phospholipid bilayer, for small non-polar molecules [1]. Facilitated diffusion uses channel or carrier proteins,
for ions and polar molecules, and its rate is limited by the number of proteins [1].

**8.** 3.4 / 4 = **0.85 mm per minute** [1].

**9.** The hormone is packaged into vesicles by the Golgi apparatus [1]; vesicles move to and fuse with the
cell-surface membrane, releasing the hormone outside the cell [1]. ATP provides the energy to move the vesicles and
for the membrane to fuse / change shape [1].

**10. (a)** (2.58 - 2.40) / 2.40 x 100 = **+7.5%** [1].
**(b)** The solution had a higher water potential than the potato cells [1], so there was a net movement of water
into the cells by osmosis through the partially permeable membranes [1].

**11.** Red blood cell: water enters by osmosis because pure water has a higher water potential than the cell [1];
there is no cell wall, so it swells and bursts (haemolysis) [1]. Plant cell: water enters by osmosis [1]; the cell
wall resists expansion, so the cell becomes turgid and does not burst [1].

**12.** The solution has a lower water potential than the cell, so water leaves by osmosis [1]; the protoplast /
vacuole shrinks and the cell-surface membrane pulls away from the cell wall [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the membrane "semi-permeable" without explaining which substances cross.
- Missing that endocytosis and exocytosis need ATP.
- Getting the surface area of a cube wrong: it is 6 x side squared.
- Saying plant cells burst in pure water.
- Forgetting the plus sign for a percentage increase.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why does a smaller agar cube lose its colour sooner?**
It has a larger surface area to volume ratio and a shorter distance from the surface to the centre, so the acid
reaches every part of it sooner.

**How do I describe absorbance results?**
More pigment in the solution means higher absorbance, which shows the membranes were more permeable.

**Which processes need ATP?**
Active transport, endocytosis and exocytosis. Diffusion, facilitated diffusion and osmosis are passive.
