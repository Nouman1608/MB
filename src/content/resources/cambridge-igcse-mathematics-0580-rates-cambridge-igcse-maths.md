This guide covers subtopic **1.12 Rates** in Topic 1 Number of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C1.12) and Extended (E1.12) statements are identical:

1. **Use common measures of rate**, for example hourly rates of pay, exchange rates between currencies, flow rates and fuel consumption.
2. **Apply other measures of rate**, for example pressure, density and population density. The syllabus says the required formulas will be given in the question.
3. **Solve problems involving average speed.** You must know the speed, distance and time formula; it will not be given.

The broader [rates, time and money study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-rates-time-and-money/) introduces each kind of rate. This page goes deeper into the skill that links them all: reading a compound unit, rearranging the rate relationship, and converting between units. Afterwards, use the [rates revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-rates-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and the [rates practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-rates-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## One idea behind every rate

A rate tells you how much of one quantity there is **per one unit** of another. Its unit says exactly how it was calculated:

| Rate | Unit | Calculated as |
|---|---|---|
| Rate of pay | $ per hour | pay ÷ hours |
| Flow rate | litres per minute | volume ÷ time |
| Fuel consumption | km per litre, or litres per 100 km | distance ÷ fuel, or fuel ÷ (distance ÷ 100) |
| Density | g/cm³ | mass ÷ volume |
| Pressure | N/m² | force ÷ area |
| Population density | people per km² | population ÷ area |
| Speed | km/h, m/s | distance ÷ time |

Every one of these has the same structure, **rate = A ÷ B**, so it can be rearranged in the same two ways:

```
rate = A ÷ B        A = rate × B        B = A ÷ rate
```

Read "per" as "divided by". If you forget which way round a formula goes, look at the unit: g/cm³ means grams divided by cubic centimetres.

## Common measures of rate (1.12.1)

### Worked example 1: rate of pay (non-calculator)

Nadia is paid $12.40 per hour. One week she earns $458.80. How many hours did she work?

```
hours = pay ÷ rate = 458.80 ÷ 12.40 = 37 hours
```

Without a calculator, check by multiplying: 37 × 12.40 = 370 + 88.80 = 458.80.

### Worked example 2: flow rate with a leak (calculator)

An empty tank holds 2.4 m³. Water flows in at 15 litres per minute, but it leaks out at 3 litres per minute. How long does the tank take to fill? (1 m³ = 1000 litres.)

```
Capacity = 2.4 × 1000 = 2400 litres
Net flow = 15 - 3 = 12 litres per minute
Time = 2400 ÷ 12 = 200 minutes = 3 hours 20 minutes
```

Two rates acting at once combine before you divide. Also convert the volume to the unit used in the rate (litres) first.

### Worked example 3: two forms of fuel consumption (calculator)

A car uses 7.5 litres of fuel per 100 km. (a) Write this in km per litre. (b) How much fuel is used on a 340 km journey?

```
(a) 100 km on 7.5 litres:  100 ÷ 7.5 = 13.3 km per litre (3 s.f.)

(b) 340 km is 3.4 lots of 100 km:  3.4 × 7.5 = 25.5 litres
```

The two forms run in opposite directions. A **higher** km per litre means a more efficient car; a **higher** litres per 100 km means a less efficient one.

Exchange rates belong to this statement too: an exchange rate such as $1 = 0.92 euros is a rate of euros per dollar. Converting currencies is covered fully in the [money study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-money-cambridge-igcse-maths/).

## Other measures of rate (1.12.2)

For pressure, density and population density the formula is given, so the marks come from substituting correctly and handling units.

### Worked example 4: density and a unit conversion (calculator)

A metal has density 19.3 g/cm³. Density = mass ÷ volume.

(a) Find the mass of a cuboid of the metal measuring 2 cm by 3 cm by 5 cm.
(b) Write the density in kg/m³.

```
(a) Volume = 2 × 3 × 5 = 30 cm^3
    mass = density × volume = 19.3 × 30 = 579 g

(b) 1 m^3 = 100 × 100 × 100 = 1 000 000 cm^3
    19.3 g/cm^3 = 19.3 × 1 000 000 g per m^3 = 19 300 000 g/m^3
    ÷ 1000 to change g to kg:  19 300 kg/m^3
```

Convert one half of a compound unit at a time. A cubic metre contains a million cubic centimetres, not a hundred.

### Worked example 5: pressure (non-calculator)

A box with weight 45 N rests on a face measuring 0.3 m by 0.5 m. Pressure = force ÷ area. Find the pressure on the floor.

```
Area = 0.3 × 0.5 = 0.15 m^2
Pressure = 45 ÷ 0.15 = 4500 ÷ 15 = 300 N/m^2
```

Standing the same box on a smaller face would increase the pressure, because the same force is spread over less area.

### Worked example 6: population density (non-calculator)

An island has an area of 1250 km² and a population of 387 500. Find its population density.

```
387 500 ÷ 1250 = 310 people per km^2
```

To divide without a calculator, simplify first: 387 500 ÷ 1250 = 3100 ÷ 10 = 310 (dividing both numbers by 125).

## Average speed (1.12.3)

```
average speed = total distance ÷ total time
```

The syllabus example: a cyclist travels 45 km in 3 hours 45 minutes. Find the average speed.

```
3 hours 45 minutes = 3 + 45/60 = 3.75 hours
45 ÷ 3.75 = 12 km/h
```

Writing 3 h 45 min as 3.45 hours would give 13.0 km/h, which is wrong.

### Converting speed units

```
km/h -> m/s:   × 1000 ÷ 3600   (the same as ÷ 3.6)      90 km/h = 25 m/s
m/s -> km/h:   × 3600 ÷ 1000   (the same as × 3.6)      15 m/s = 54 km/h
```

The shortcut works because 1 km/h = 1000 m ÷ 3600 s.

### Worked example 7: the average of two speeds (non-calculator)

A driver travels 60 km at 40 km/h and then another 60 km at 60 km/h. Find the average speed for the whole journey.

```
Time for first part  = 60 ÷ 40 = 1.5 hours
Time for second part = 60 ÷ 60 = 1 hour
Average speed = 120 ÷ 2.5 = 48 km/h
```

The answer is not (40 + 60) ÷ 2 = 50 km/h, because more time is spent at the slower speed.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing the wrong way round: the unit tells you what to divide by what.
- Converting only one half of a compound unit, or using 1 m³ = 100 cm³.
- Typing hours and minutes as a decimal, such as 3 h 45 min as 3.45.
- Taking the mean of two speeds instead of total distance ÷ total time.
- Mixing units: a flow in litres per minute with a volume in m³ or cm³.
- Leaving out the unit of a rate, or writing it incorrectly (such as km/h for a time).

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Calculate** or **Work out**: write the rate relationship you are using, such as "time = volume ÷ flow rate", before substituting.
- **Give your answer in** a stated unit: convert before you calculate, or convert the final answer, but say which.
- **Show (that)**: in a given-answer unit conversion, show each half of the compound unit being converted.
- **Explain**: when comparing two rates, such as fuel efficiency, refer to the values and say which is better and why.
