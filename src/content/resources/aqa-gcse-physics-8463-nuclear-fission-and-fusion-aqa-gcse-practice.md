## Section A

**1.** Define nuclear fission. **[1]**

**2.** State what must usually happen to a uranium-235 nucleus before it undergoes fission. **[1]**

**3.** Define nuclear fusion. **[2]**

**4.** State whether each of these involves fission or fusion.

**(a)** The energy released in the core of a main sequence star such as the Sun. **[1]**
**(b)** The energy released in a nuclear power station operating today. **[1]**

**5.** Uranium-235 undergoes fission:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁴₅₆Ba + ⁸⁹₃₆Kr + x ¹₀n

Calculate x. Show your working. **[2]**

**6.** Energy is released when a nucleus undergoes fission. Give two ways in which this energy is carried
away from the point where the nucleus split. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student writes this description of fission:

"A uranium atom splits by itself into one large nucleus and several tiny pieces, and gives out one
electron."

Identify four errors in the description and correct each one. **[4]**

**8.** In a sample of uranium, each fission releases 2 neutrons.

**(a)** Assume every released neutron causes another fission and that one fission starts the process.
Calculate the number of fissions in the sixth generation. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the total number of fissions in the first six generations. **[2]**
**(c)** In a nuclear reactor, the number of fissions per generation stays the same. Explain what must happen
to the neutrons released by each fission. **[2]**

**9.** Plutonium-239 (atomic number 94) can undergo fission:

²³⁹₉₄Pu + ¹₀n → ¹³⁴₅₄Xe + ¹⁰³₄₀Zr + x ¹₀n

**(a)** Calculate x, and check that the atomic numbers balance. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this reaction is fission and not fusion. **[2]**
**(c)** State what may happen to the neutrons released by this reaction. **[1]**

**10.** A diagram of a chain reaction shows the following.

- A neutron is absorbed by nucleus A, which splits and releases three neutrons.
- The first of these is absorbed by nucleus B, which splits. The second leaves the edge of the diagram.
  The third is absorbed by nucleus C, which does not split.
- Nucleus B releases two neutrons. They are absorbed by nuclei D and E, which both split.

**(a)** State the number of fissions in each of the three generations shown. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many of the neutrons released by A do not continue the chain. **[1]**
**(c)** Use your answer to (a) to state whether the chain reaction is steady or growing between the second
and third generations, and what would happen if this continued. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the chain reaction in a nuclear reactor with the chain reaction in a nuclear weapon. **[4]**

**12.** Describe what happens when a uranium-235 nucleus undergoes fission, and explain how this can lead to
a chain reaction. **[6]**

**13.** Two hydrogen-2 nuclei can join:

²₁H + ²₁H → ³₂He + ¹₀n

**(a)** Explain why this reaction is an example of fusion. **[2]**
**(b)** The total mass of the products is slightly less than the total mass of the two hydrogen-2 nuclei.
State what has happened to the missing mass. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one difference between this reaction and the fission of uranium-235, other than the size of
the nuclei involved. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The splitting of a large, unstable nucleus (such as uranium or plutonium) [1]

**2.** It must absorb a neutron [1]

**3.** The joining of two light nuclei [1]; to form a heavier nucleus [1]

**4. (a)** Fusion [1]
**(b)** Fission [1]

**5.** Mass numbers: 235 + 1 = 236 and 144 + 89 = 233 [1]; x = 236 − 233 = **3** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Forgetting the absorbed neutron on the left gives x = 2 and loses the answer mark.

**6.** Kinetic energy of the fission products (the two smaller nuclei and the neutrons) [1]; gamma rays [1]

**7.** Each error identified and corrected:
- "Atom" should be **nucleus**: fission is the splitting of the nucleus [1]
- "By itself": spontaneous fission is rare; the nucleus usually **absorbs a neutron** first [1]
- "One large nucleus and several tiny pieces": it splits into **two smaller nuclei of roughly equal size** [1]
- "One electron": it emits **two or three neutrons** (and gamma rays) [1]

**8. (a)** 1, 2, 4, 8, 16, **32** [1]
**(b)** 1 + 2 + 4 + 8 + 16 + 32 [1]; = **63** fissions [1]
**(c)** On average, only one of the two neutrons from each fission goes on to cause another fission [1]; the
other must be absorbed (or escape) without causing fission [1]

**9. (a)** 239 + 1 = 240; 134 + 103 = 237; x = 240 − 237 = **3** [1]; atomic numbers: 94 = 54 + 40 ✓ [1]
**(b)** A large (heavy) nucleus [1]; splits into two smaller nuclei, which is fission; fusion would join light
nuclei into a heavier one [1]
**(c)** They may be absorbed by other plutonium nuclei and cause further fissions, starting a chain reaction
[1]

**10. (a)** First generation: 1 (A); second: 1 (B) [1]; third: 2 (D and E) [1]
**(b)** **2** (one leaves the diagram; one is absorbed by C without fission) [1]
**(c)** Growing, because the number of fissions doubles from 1 to 2 [1]; if this continued, the number of
fissions and the energy released would grow rapidly, as in an uncontrolled chain reaction [1]
*Examiner insight:* A neutron absorbed by a nucleus that does not split does not count as a fission.

**11.** Reactor: controlled; weapon: uncontrolled [1]; in a reactor, on average one neutron from each fission
causes another fission, whereas in a weapon more than one does [1]; so the number of fissions per second is
steady in a reactor but grows rapidly in a weapon [1]; a reactor releases energy at a steady rate, whereas a
weapon releases a huge amount very quickly, causing an explosion [1]

**12.** The uranium-235 nucleus absorbs a neutron [1]; it becomes unstable and splits into two smaller nuclei
of roughly equal size [1]; it emits two or three neutrons [1]; and gamma rays, and energy is released, with
all the fission products having kinetic energy [1]; the released neutrons can be absorbed by other
uranium-235 nuclei, causing them to undergo fission [1]; each of these releases more neutrons, so the
process repeats: a chain reaction [1]
*Examiner insight:* AQA marks 6-mark answers by level. Top-level answers give the steps in order and link
the released neutrons to further fissions.

**13. (a)** Two light nuclei [1]; join to form a heavier nucleus (helium-3) [1]
**(b)** It has been converted into the energy of radiation [1]
**(c)** Any one: no neutron needs to be absorbed first; one heavier nucleus is formed rather than two
smaller ones [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "atom" instead of "nucleus".
- Leaving out neutron absorption at the start of fission.
- Giving one neutron, or forgetting the gamma rays.
- Leaving the absorbed neutron out of an equation.
- Counting a nucleus that absorbs a neutron but does not split as a fission.
- Writing two separate descriptions in a "compare" question.
