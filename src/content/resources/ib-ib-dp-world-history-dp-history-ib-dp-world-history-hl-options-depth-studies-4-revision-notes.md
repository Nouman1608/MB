## The French Revolution and Napoleon, 1774-1815

### Chronology

- 1774: Louis XVI becomes king.
- 1778-83: French support for the American War of Independence deepens royal debt.
- 1787: Assembly of Notables rejects tax reform.
- 5 May 1789: Estates-General.
- June 1789: National Assembly; Tennis Court Oath.
- 14 July 1789: Bastille.
- August 1789: August Decrees; Declaration of the Rights of Man.
- October 1789: women's march to Versailles; royal family brought to Paris.
- July 1790: Civil Constitution of the Clergy.
- June 1791: Flight to Varennes.
- April 1792: war with Austria.
- 10 August 1792: Tuileries stormed; September massacres.
- 21 January 1793: Louis XVI executed.
- March 1793: Vendée rising.
- 1793-94: the Terror; Committee of Public Safety; Law of Suspects.
- 27 July 1794: fall of Robespierre (9 Thermidor).
- 1795-99: the Directory.
- November 1799: Brumaire coup; the Consulate.
- 1801: Concordat; 1804: Civil Code; December 1804: Napoleon crowned emperor.
- 1805: Austerlitz; 1806: Continental System.
- 1808-14: Peninsular War; 1812: invasion of Russia; 1813: Leipzig.
- April 1814: first abdication; 18 June 1815: Waterloo.

### Causes of the Revolution, sorted

| Type | Factors |
|---|---|
| Financial | Debt; failed reform attempts by Calonne and others; tax exemptions |
| Social | Privileges of nobility and clergy; ambitions of the bourgeoisie; peasant burdens |
| Intellectual | Enlightenment ideas of rights, sovereignty and reason |
| Economic | Poor harvest of 1788; bread prices; unemployment |
| Political | Weak leadership of Louis XVI; the deadlock over voting in the Estates-General |

## Italian and German unification

| Step | Italy | Germany |
|---|---|---|
| Economic base | Piedmont's railways and trade under Cavour | Zollverein (1834); industrial and railway growth |
| Failed revolution | 1848-49 revolutions | Frankfurt Parliament (1848-49) |
| Leader | Cavour (prime minister from 1852) | Bismarck (minister-president from 1862) |
| Foreign help or neutrality | France in 1859; Prussia in 1866; French withdrawal from Rome in 1870 | Russian neutrality; French isolation in 1870 |
| Wars | 1859 against Austria; Garibaldi's campaign (1860) | 1864 Denmark; 1866 Austria; 1870-71 France |
| Outcome | Kingdom of Italy (March 1861); Venetia (1866); Rome (1870) | German Empire (18 January 1871) |
| Popular involvement | Garibaldi's volunteers; plebiscites | Limited; unification from above |

**Key people:** Mazzini (republican nationalist), Garibaldi, Cavour, Victor Emmanuel II, Napoleon III; Bismarck, William I, Roon, Moltke.

## Central and Eastern Europe, 1945-1991

- 1945-48: communist takeovers; "salami tactics".
- February 1948: Czech coup.
- 1948: Tito's split with Stalin.
- 1949: Comecon founded.
- June 1953: East German uprising.
- 1955: Warsaw Pact.
- 1956: Polish October (Gomułka); Hungarian revolution crushed (November).
- 1961: Berlin Wall.
- 1968: Prague Spring; invasion 20-21 August; Brezhnev Doctrine.
- 1975: Helsinki Final Act; human rights groups such as Charter 77 (1977) in Czechoslovakia.
- 1978: a Polish pope, John Paul II; visit to Poland 1979.
- August 1980: Solidarity founded.
- December 1981: martial law in Poland.
- 1985: Gorbachev in power.
- 1989: Polish Round Table and elections (June); Hungary opens the border; Berlin Wall opens (9 November); Velvet Revolution; Romanian revolution.
- 1990: German reunification (October).
- 1991: Comecon and the Warsaw Pact dissolved; Yugoslav wars begin; USSR dissolved (December).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ancien régime | France's social and political order before 1789 |
| Sans-culottes | Radical Parisian working people |
| Jacobins | Radical political club associated with Robespierre |
| *Levée en masse* | Mass conscription decreed in August 1793 |
| *Risorgimento* | The movement for Italian unification |
| *Realpolitik* | Politics based on power and practical interest rather than ideals |
| Salami tactics | Gradual removal of non-communist rivals in coalitions |
| Brezhnev Doctrine | Soviet claim to intervene to protect socialism in its bloc |

## Exam traps

- **French Revolution essays that stop in 1789** when the question runs to 1794 or 1799.
- **Napoleon as purely a reformer or purely a tyrant.** Show both and judge.
- **Unification as the work of one man.** Weigh leaders against economic and international factors.
- **1989 explained by one cause.** Combine Gorbachev, economic failure, popular movements and the domino effect.
- **Outside the section's dates.** Read the question's date range twice.

## Self-test

1. Why did the Assembly of Notables matter in 1787? *Its rejection of tax reform forced the king to call the Estates-General.*
2. What was the Tennis Court Oath? *The National Assembly's vow (20 June 1789) not to separate until France had a constitution.*
3. Why did the Flight to Varennes matter? *It destroyed trust in the king and strengthened republicanism.*
4. What was the *levée en masse*? *Mass conscription of the French population for war, decreed in August 1793.*
5. Name two of Napoleon's lasting reforms. *Any two: Civil Code, Concordat, prefects, Bank of France, lycées.*
6. What did Cavour gain from the Crimean War? *A seat at the peace conference and French goodwill.*
7. What did Plombières (1858) agree? *French help against Austria in return for Nice and Savoy.*
8. What were the three wars of German unification? *Against Denmark (1864), Austria (1866) and France (1870-71).*
9. What was the Ems Telegram? *Bismarck's edited account of a meeting between William I and the French ambassador, published to provoke France.*
10. Why did Hungary in 1956 lead to invasion while Poland did not? *Nagy announced withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact; Gomułka kept Poland in the Pact and the party in control.*
11. What was Solidarity? *An independent Polish trade union and mass movement founded in 1980, led by Lech Wałęsa.*
12. Why did the Soviet Union not intervene in 1989? *Gorbachev had abandoned the Brezhnev Doctrine and refused to use force, partly because of economic crisis.*
