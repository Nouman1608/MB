Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics (0580) is tiered. Core candidates sit Paper 1 (Non-calculator, 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 50%) and Paper 3 (Calculator, 1 hour 30 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). Extended candidates sit Paper 2 (Non-calculator, 2 hours, 100 marks, 50%) and Paper 4 (Calculator, 2 hours, 100 marks, 50%). Good preparation pairs the pacing below with topic work such as [coordinates and linear graphs](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-coordinates-and-drawing-linear-graphs/), [circle theorems](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-ii/) and [cumulative frequency diagrams](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-cumulative-frequency-diagrams/).

## Choosing your tier

Your tier decides which two papers you sit. According to the verified record, Core candidates (Papers 1 and 3) are eligible for grades C to G, and Extended candidates (Papers 2 and 4) for grades A* to E. Decide the tier with your teacher well before the entry deadline, using timed mock papers rather than impressions. A candidate who is secure on Core content and is also working towards a higher grade should weigh the risk: a Core entry caps the grade range, while an Extended entry demands fluency in more advanced algebra and geometry.

Both tiers cover the same nine areas of content: number, algebra and graphs, coordinate geometry, geometry, mensuration, trigonometry, transformations and vectors, probability and statistics. The tier changes the depth and the demand, not the list of headings.

## The Core papers: Paper 1 and Paper 3

Paper 1 forbids a calculator, and Paper 3 permits one; each lasts 90 minutes, carries 80 marks and is worth 50%. Use Paper 1 to build secure mental and written arithmetic: fractions, percentages, ratio, simple algebra and estimation. Use Paper 3 to practise efficient calculator work, including brackets, powers, and rounding only at the end of a calculation. The page on [estimation and using a calculator](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-estimation-and-using-a-calculator/) is directly relevant to the difference between the two papers.

## The Extended papers: Paper 2 and Paper 4

Paper 2 is non-calculator and Paper 4 is calculator, each 2 hours, 100 marks and 50%. The Extended papers are longer and carry more marks, so stamina matters. Rehearse two-hour sittings in the weeks before the exam. On the non-calculator paper, expect to leave answers in exact form where appropriate (surds, fractions, multiples of pi), and revise [surds and exponential growth and decay](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-exponential-growth-decay-and-surds/) accordingly.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Core Paper 1 and Paper 3: 90 minutes for 80 marks is 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark. A 3-mark question deserves about 3.4 minutes.
- Extended Paper 2 and Paper 4: 120 minutes for 100 marks is 120 / 100 = 1.2 minutes per mark. A 5-mark question deserves about 6 minutes.

Each paper is worth 50%, so the non-calculator and calculator papers are equally important; a tier's two papers together make 50% + 50% = 100%. Because the pace is only slightly more than one minute per mark, there is little room for long hesitation. If a part is not progressing after about two minutes, mark it, move on and return.

## Command words and question styles

Common instruction words in mathematics include calculate, work out, solve, show that, prove, sketch, draw and explain. Show that means the answer is given and every step of the reasoning must be visible, without working backwards from the stated result. Calculate and work out usually expect method to be written down. Give your answer to a stated accuracy only at the final step, and include units whenever the question gives them.

## Worked practice scenario

Question (Extended, non-calculator): Solve 2x squared - 5x - 12 = 0, and state the sum of the two solutions.

**Model answer.**

1. To factorise, look for two numbers that multiply to 2 x (-12) = -24 and add to -5. These are -8 and +3.
2. Split the middle term: 2x squared - 8x + 3x - 12 = 0.
3. Group: 2x(x - 4) + 3(x - 4) = 0, so (2x + 3)(x - 4) = 0.
4. Hence 2x + 3 = 0 gives x = -1.5, and x - 4 = 0 gives x = 4.
5. Check by substitution: for x = 4, 2(16) - 20 - 12 = 0. For x = -1.5, 2(2.25) + 7.5 - 12 = 4.5 + 7.5 - 12 = 0. Both solutions are correct.
6. The sum is 4 + (-1.5) = 2.5. As a check, the sum of roots of ax squared + bx + c = 0 is -b / a = 5 / 2 = 2.5.

The method matters as much as the answer: showing the factorisation and the check protects marks if there is a slip in the arithmetic.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm your tier and which two paper codes you are sitting.
- Practise at least one full timed paper of each type: non-calculator and calculator.
- Check you have a suitable calculator, a ruler, a protractor and compasses, and that you know how to use the calculator's fraction and bracket keys.
- During non-calculator papers, write intermediate steps instead of attempting mental shortcuts.
- During calculator papers, keep full calculator values until the last step and then round.
- Show working on every multi-mark question.
- Label graphs and diagrams clearly and use a sharp pencil.
- Spend the last five minutes checking units, rounding and sign errors.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does a candidate take in Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580?**
Two. Core candidates take Papers 1 and 3, and Extended candidates take Papers 2 and 4.

**Which papers allow a calculator?**
Paper 3 (Core) and Paper 4 (Extended) are the calculator papers. Paper 1 (Core) and Paper 2 (Extended) are non-calculator.

**How long and how many marks are the papers?**
Papers 1 and 3 are each 1 hour 30 minutes and 80 marks, and Papers 2 and 4 are each 2 hours and 100 marks. Every paper is worth 50%.

**What grades are available on each tier?**
According to the verified record, Core is eligible for grades C to G and Extended for grades A* to E.

**Which examination years does this syllabus cover?**
The examination-year window is 2025-2027, and Cambridge does not give a separate first-teaching date.
