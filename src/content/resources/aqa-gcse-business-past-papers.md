This page is a guide to AQA GCSE Business (8132) past papers for June 2024 and June 2025. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published one, a short account of what examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA GCSE Business (8132) specification page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/gcse/business-8132)
- [AQA GCSE Business (8132) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/business/gcse/business-8132/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs and how the levels-of-response questions are banded. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still stopped at the lower levels. The notes below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. GCSE Business (8132) is a linear, untiered specification for first teaching from September 2017, first examined in 2019. Both series covered here were set on that specification, so the paper structure below applies to them. For any older paper, confirm the specification and the paper structure on the front cover before you rely on it.

### Exam structure

Both papers are written exams. Section A is multiple choice and short answer, and the later sections are built on case-study or data-response material. All assessment is at the end of the course.

## June 2025

This series tested operations and human resources on Paper 1 and marketing and finance on Paper 2. Examiners on both papers describe students who knew the content and used the case-study data reasonably, but who too often copied from the material or stopped at a generic chain such as "more sales, so more profit". Short-answer marks went to students who developed one point rather than listing several. In the longer questions the common limit was analysis tied to the business in the case study, and a final decision that added something new.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (8132/1), Section A: opportunity cost was understood only as a loose definition and sometimes confused with business risk. Decentralisation was the weakest question in the section, with many students confusing it with delay or not describing decision-making between head office and branches. Training for existing staff was answered as induction, and recruitment documents were mixed up with processes such as shortlisting.
- Paper 1, Section B: a benefit of lean production was named but not explained as a result of how the method works. Describing a trend from exchange-rate data needed figures from the table, and students mixed up imports and exports. Span of control and organisational structure were muddled, for example narrower spans described as a flatter structure. Job-sharing was often assumed to mean higher pay or shifts.
- Paper 1, Section C: percentage and commission calculations lost marks through rounding and by adding the percentage to the base. Sustainability was explained by its benefits, not by how a business achieves it. Digital communication answers drifted into social media and e-commerce. The final judgement did not always say which option mattered most.
- Paper 2 (8132/2), Section A: sales promotion and the average rate of return formula were the weakest multiple-choice topics. Margin of safety was often not calculated, and answers without units lost marks. Answers about newspaper advertising were generic rather than specific to the method.
- Paper 2, Section B: stating that interest rates raise costs or prices without saying why. Students recommended nothing on market research, and several treated cash as a method of payment, not as working capital. Misreading which months made up the season cost marks on a calculation.
- Paper 2, Section C: telesales was confused with television advertising or e-commerce. Diseconomies of scale were poorly known. Segmentation was defined but its benefit was not explained. The last question was answered with case-study facts repeated, not linked to competitiveness.

**What went well**

- Identifying factors such as price, quality and location when choosing a supplier, and reasons for becoming an entrepreneur.
- Environmental impact of business activity, retained profit as a source of finance, and extension strategies.
- Using data from the case studies to support points, and the discount and cost calculations in Section C of Paper 2.

**Technique the examiners repeated:** plan 9- and 12-mark answers, make fewer but better-developed points, build the counter-argument into an evaluative second paragraph and keep to the space given. A conclusion should add new evidence for the judgement. Use the stem to focus a 6- or 9-mark answer on the most important factor.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find the June 2025 8132 papers and mark schemes?**
On AQA's assessment-resources page for 8132, linked above. Question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series.

**Are the grade thresholds the same every year?**
No. AQA sets them after each series, so the same raw mark can give a different grade from one year to the next. Compare the two tables above for an example, and never assume a future series will match.

**Is 8132 tiered?**
No. Every student sits the same two papers, and grades 9 to 1 are available to all.

**What are notional component boundaries?**
They are AQA's illustrative boundaries for individual papers. The grade is awarded on the total mark, so treat the paper figures as a guide to how a total might split.

**Why is there no examiner report for June 2024 here?**
AQA's assessment-resources page lists none for that series. If AQA adds one we will summarise it.
