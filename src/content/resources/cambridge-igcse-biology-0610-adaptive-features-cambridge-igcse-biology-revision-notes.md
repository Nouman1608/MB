These notes condense sub-topic **18.2 Adaptive features** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 and 2 are Core. Outcome 3 (hydrophytes and xerophytes) is **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [adaptive features study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-adaptive-features-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [adaptive features practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-adaptive-features-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [variation and selection revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-variation-and-selection-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 18.

## Core recall: the definition

> An adaptive feature is an **inherited** feature that helps an organism to **survive and reproduce** in its environment.

Three checks for any feature in a question:

- Is it **inherited**? (If it was gained during life, it is not an adaptive feature.)
- Does it help **survival or reproduction**?
- Does it fit **this environment**?

## Core recall: the answer chain

> **Feature → what it does → how it helps survival or reproduction here.**

One mark is usually for the feature with its function, the next for the link to the environment. Before writing, pick out the conditions in the stem: temperature, water, predators, food, light.

## Core recall: functions to link features to

| Feature type | Typical function | Habitat link |
|---|---|---|
| Thick fur, fat layer | insulation, reduces heat loss | cold climates |
| Small ears and limbs | small surface area, less heat lost | cold climates |
| Large ears with many blood vessels | large surface area, more heat lost | hot climates |
| Colour matching surroundings | camouflage from predators or prey | any habitat |
| Broad feet | spread mass, do not sink | snow or sand |
| Bright, scented petals | attract insect pollinators | reproduction |
| Closable nostrils, long eyelashes | keep out sand | deserts |

## Extended recall: hydrophytes

Problem: getting **light and gases**, staying near the surface. Water loss is no problem.

- **Large air spaces** → buoyancy, leaves float in bright light; store gases; oxygen reaches roots.
- **Stomata on upper surface** → lower surface is in water; gas exchange with the air.
- **Thin or no cuticle** → water loss is not a risk.
- **Little xylem** → water absorbed across the surface; little transport needed.
- **Little support tissue** → water supports the plant.
- **Large flat floating leaves** → absorb maximum light.
- **Thin or finely divided submerged leaves** → large surface for absorbing carbon dioxide and ions.

## Extended recall: xerophytes

Problem: **losing water faster than it is absorbed**.

- **Thick waxy cuticle** → less evaporation from the surface.
- **Sunken stomata** → humid air trapped → smaller water vapour gradient → less diffusion out.
- **Hairs** → trap moist air; reduce air movement.
- **Rolled leaves** → humid air inside; less surface exposed to wind.
- **Spines or small leaves** → small surface area for water loss.
- **Fewer stomata** → fewer pores for water vapour.
- **Fleshy stems or leaves** → store water.
- **Deep roots** → reach deep water; **shallow widespread roots** → absorb rain quickly.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Adaptive feature | An inherited feature that helps an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment |
| Acquired feature | A feature gained during life; not inherited, so not adaptive |
| Camouflage | Colouring or pattern that matches the surroundings |
| Insulation | A layer, such as fur or fat, that reduces heat transfer |
| Hydrophyte (Extended) | A plant adapted to live in water |
| Xerophyte (Extended) | A plant adapted to live in dry conditions |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from leaves, mainly through stomata |
| Water vapour gradient | Difference in water vapour concentration between the leaf and the air |
| Cuticle | Waxy, waterproof layer on the leaf surface |

## Exam traps

- **Acquired features.** Scars, trained muscles and sun tans are not adaptive features.
- **Half a definition.** "Helps it survive" without "and reproduce" or "inherited" loses marks.
- **Lists without functions.** Naming "thick fur" scores nothing until you add "insulates, reducing heat loss".
- **"Needs less water."** A xerophyte loses less water; it still needs water.
- **"Stops water loss."** Features reduce water loss; they rarely stop it.
- **Stomata on the wrong side.** Floating leaves have stomata on the upper surface.
- **Camel's hump.** It stores fat, not water.
- **Ignoring the stem.** If the question gives data, quote a figure.

## Command-word reminders

- **Describe** an adaptive feature: say what it is and what it does.
- **Explain**: add "so" and the effect on survival, water loss, light or gases.
- **Compare** a hydrophyte and xerophyte: one sentence covering both, joined by "whereas".

## Self-test

1. Describe what is meant by an adaptive feature.
2. A deer has a scar from a fight. Explain why this is not an adaptive feature.
3. Explain how large ears help a desert fox survive.
4. Explain how white fur helps an Arctic predator to catch prey.
5. How does a brightly coloured petal help a plant to reproduce?
6. Why do broad feet help an animal living on soft sand?
7. (Extended) Define a xerophyte.
8. (Extended) Explain why sunken stomata reduce water loss.
9. (Extended) Why are the stomata of a water lily leaf on the upper surface?
10. (Extended) Explain the advantage of large air spaces in a hydrophyte.
11. (Extended) Explain why a cactus has spines instead of broad leaves.
12. (Extended) Give one way in which roots of xerophytes are adapted, and explain it.

### Answers

1. An inherited feature that helps an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment.
2. It was acquired during the deer's life, so it is not inherited.
3. They have a large surface area, so more heat is lost, which helps keep the body cool.
4. It is camouflage against snow, so prey are less likely to see it approaching.
5. It attracts insects, which carry pollen between flowers, so pollination happens.
6. They spread the body mass over a larger area, so the animal does not sink.
7. A plant adapted to living in dry conditions.
8. Humid air is trapped near the stoma, so the water vapour gradient is smaller and less water vapour diffuses out.
9. The lower surface is in contact with water, so gas exchange can only happen with the air above.
10. The leaves float near the surface where light is brightest; air spaces store gases and supply oxygen to roots.
11. Spines have a small surface area, so less water is lost by transpiration.
12. Deep roots reach water far underground, or shallow widespread roots absorb rain quickly over a large area.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a feature with no function.
- Describing how a feature helps without linking it to the environment in the question.
- Giving an acquired feature as an example.
- Explaining xerophyte features without mentioning water.
- Forgetting light and gas exchange when explaining hydrophyte features.
