**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level**
subtopic 26.2: homogeneous and heterogeneous catalysts; heterogeneous mode of action (iron in the
Haber process; Pd, Pt and Rh in catalytic converters); homogeneous mode of action (oxides of
nitrogen with SO₂; Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ with I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | increases the rate by providing an alternative pathway with lower activation energy; not used up overall |
| Homogeneous catalyst | in the same phase as the reactants |
| Heterogeneous catalyst | in a different phase from the reactants (usually a solid with gases or solutions) |
| Active site | place on a catalyst surface where reactant molecules adsorb |
| Adsorption | reactant molecules form weak bonds to the catalyst surface |
| Desorption | product molecules break away from the surface |
| Intermediate | species formed in one step and used in a later step |

## What a catalyst does and does not change

| Changes | Does not change |
|---|---|
| activation energy (lower) | ΔH of the reaction |
| reaction pathway | position of equilibrium (both directions speed up equally) |
| rate (and k) | overall equation |

## Heterogeneous catalysis: say all three stages

1. **Adsorption** of reactants onto active sites.
2. **Bond weakening** within the adsorbed molecules (and reactants held close together in a
   suitable orientation).
3. **Desorption** of products, freeing the active sites.

Large surface area matters: finely divided iron; thin metal coating on a ceramic honeycomb.

**Iron, Haber process:** N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃. N₂ and H₂ adsorb; N≡N and H–H weakened; NH₃
desorbs.

**Pt, Pd, Rh, catalytic converter:**

    2CO + 2NO  → 2CO₂ + N₂
    4CO + 2NO₂ → 4CO₂ + N₂

NO/NO₂ and CO adsorb; N–O bonds weakened; N₂ and CO₂ form and desorb.

## Homogeneous catalysis: used, then reformed

**Oxides of nitrogen in the atmosphere:**

    step 1:  SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO        (catalyst used)
    step 2:  NO + ½O₂ → NO₂              (catalyst reformed)
    overall: SO₂ + ½O₂ → SO₃

Catalyst: NO₂. Intermediate: NO. Consequence: SO₃ + H₂O → H₂SO₄ in acid rain.

**Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺ with iodide and peroxodisulfate:**

    overall: S₂O₈²⁻ + 2I⁻ → 2SO₄²⁻ + I₂
    step 1:  S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺
    step 2:  2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂

- Uncatalysed: two **negative** ions repel → high Eₐ → slow.
- Catalysed: each step is between **oppositely charged** ions → faster.
- Fe³⁺ works too: step 2 first, then step 1.

## E⦵ test for a possible homogeneous catalyst

Both steps must be feasible, so E⦵(catalyst pair) must lie between the two reactant half-cells:

    I₂/I⁻ = +0.54 V    <    E⦵(catalyst)    <    S₂O₈²⁻/SO₄²⁻ = +2.01 V

| Pair | E⦵ / V | Can catalyse? |
|---|---|---|
| Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ | +0.77 | yes |
| Co³⁺/Co²⁺ | +1.82 | yes, on energetic grounds |
| Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ | +0.15 | no: Sn⁴⁺ cannot oxidise I⁻ |
| V³⁺/V²⁺ | −0.26 | no: V³⁺ cannot oxidise I⁻ |

## Energy profiles

- Uncatalysed: one high peak.
- Heterogeneous or homogeneous: lower peak(s); same reactant and product levels.
- Two-step homogeneous route: two lower peaks with an intermediate in the dip between them.

## Classifying examples

| Catalyst | Reaction phase | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Fe(s) | gases | heterogeneous |
| Pt/Pd/Rh(s) | gases | heterogeneous |
| NO₂(g) | gases | homogeneous |
| Fe²⁺(aq) | aqueous | homogeneous |
| H⁺(aq) in propanone iodination | aqueous | homogeneous |

## Exam traps

- Homogeneous means same phase as the **reactants**, not the products.
- Heterogeneous answers need **adsorption, bond weakening, desorption** in that order.
- Use "adsorb", not "absorb".
- Balance the iron steps: 2Fe²⁺ per S₂O₈²⁻, 2Fe³⁺ per 2I⁻.
- Name NO₂ (not NO) as the catalyst in SO₂ oxidation; NO is the intermediate.
- A catalyst does not shift equilibrium or change ΔH.
- Explain the slowness of the uncatalysed I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction by **repulsion** between anions.

## Self-test

1. Define a homogeneous catalyst.
2. Is iron in the Haber process homogeneous or heterogeneous? Explain.
3. List the three stages of heterogeneous catalysis.
4. Which bond in nitrogen is weakened on the iron surface, and why does this matter?
5. Name the three metals used in catalytic converters to remove oxides of nitrogen.
6. Write an equation for the removal of NO by CO in a converter.
7. Write the two steps by which NO₂ catalyses the oxidation of SO₂.
8. In Question 7, identify the intermediate.
9. Why is the uncatalysed reaction between I⁻ and S₂O₈²⁻ slow?
10. Write the two steps when Fe³⁺ is used as the catalyst, in the order they occur.
11. Explain why Sn⁴⁺/Sn²⁺ (+0.15 V) cannot catalyse the I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction.
12. Why are heterogeneous catalysts used with a large surface area?

**Answers**

1. A catalyst in the same phase as the reactants.
2. Heterogeneous: solid iron with gaseous nitrogen and hydrogen.
3. Adsorption of reactants; bond weakening; desorption of products.
4. The N≡N triple bond; it is very strong, which is why the uncatalysed activation energy is so
   high.
5. Platinum, palladium and rhodium.
6. 2CO + 2NO → 2CO₂ + N₂.
7. SO₂ + NO₂ → SO₃ + NO; NO + ½O₂ → NO₂.
8. NO.
9. Both reactants are negative ions, which repel, so the activation energy is high.
10. 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂, then S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺.
11. Sn⁴⁺ cannot oxidise I⁻ (E⦵<sub>cell</sub> = 0.15 − 0.54 = −0.39 V), so the catalyst would
    not be regenerated.
12. Reaction happens only at the surface, so more surface means more active sites and a faster
    rate.
