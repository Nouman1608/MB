**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** content:
subtopic 29.2 Characteristic organic reactions: understand and use the terms **electrophilic substitution**
and **addition–elimination**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Electrophile** | electron-pair acceptor; attracted to electron-rich regions |
| **Nucleophile** | electron-pair donor (has a lone pair); attracted to δ+ atoms |
| **Substitution** | one atom or group replaced by another |
| **Addition** | two reactants combine into one product, using a π bond |
| **Elimination** | a small molecule or ion removed |
| **Electrophilic substitution** | an electrophile attacks an electron-rich centre (a benzene ring) and replaces an atom, usually H |
| **Addition–elimination** | a nucleophile adds to a C=O carbon, then a group is eliminated and C=O re-forms |
| **Leaving group** | the atom or ion that departs, taking the bonding pair (Cl⁻ from acyl chlorides) |

## Electrophilic substitution in three stages

    1. generate electrophile        e.g.  CH₃Cl + AlCl₃ → CH₃⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
    2. ring π electrons attack E⁺   → positive intermediate, delocalisation partly broken
    3. lose H⁺                      → ring restored;  H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → HCl + AlCl₃

**Why substitution, not addition:** keeping the delocalised ring keeps its stability.

| A Level example | Electrophile | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| nitration of benzene | NO₂⁺ | conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C |
| bromination of benzene | Br⁺ (Br₂ polarised by AlBr₃) | Br₂, AlBr₃ |
| Friedel–Crafts alkylation | CH₃⁺ | CH₃Cl, AlCl₃, heat |
| Friedel–Crafts acylation | CH₃CO⁺ | CH₃COCl, AlCl₃, heat |
| bromination of phenol | Br₂ polarised by the activated ring | Br₂(aq), room temperature |
| nitration of phenol | from dilute HNO₃ | dilute HNO₃(aq), room temperature |
| azo coupling | diazonium ion, C₆H₅N₂⁺ | phenol in NaOH(aq), below 10 °C |

## Addition–elimination in two steps

    1. addition:     Nu: lone pair → δ+ carbonyl C;  C=O π electrons → O⁻
                     (intermediate: O⁻, Nu and Cl all on one carbon)
    2. elimination:  O⁻ lone pair re-forms C=O;  C–Cl electrons → Cl⁻ leaves;  H⁺ lost from Nu

**Why acyl chlorides react this way:** carbonyl C bonded to both O and Cl, so strongly δ+; C=O can accept the
pair; Cl⁻ is a good leaving group.

| Nucleophile (room temperature) | Product with CH₃COCl | By-product |
|---|---|---|
| H₂O | CH₃COOH, ethanoic acid | HCl |
| CH₃CH₂OH | CH₃COOCH₂CH₃, ethyl ethanoate | HCl |
| C₆H₅OH | CH₃COOC₆H₅, phenyl ethanoate | HCl |
| NH₃ | CH₃CONH₂, ethanamide | HCl |
| CH₃NH₂ | CH₃CONHCH₃, N-methylethanamide | HCl |

## Classification table

| Substrate | Attacked by | Overall | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| arene / phenol ring | electrophile | H replaced | electrophilic substitution |
| acyl chloride C=O | nucleophile | Cl replaced via intermediate | addition–elimination |
| alkene C=C | electrophile | added across | electrophilic addition |
| aldehyde / ketone C=O | nucleophile (CN⁻) | added across | nucleophilic addition |
| halogenoalkane sp³ C | nucleophile | X replaced | nucleophilic substitution |
| alkane, UV | radical | H replaced | free-radical substitution |

The same reagent can be involved in different mechanisms: NH₃ takes part in nucleophilic substitution with
bromoethane but in addition–elimination with ethanoyl chloride. Classify by the **substrate**, not the reagent.

Test: **Is there a C=O with a leaving group?** If so, think addition–elimination. **Is a benzene ring kept in
the product?** If so, think electrophilic substitution.

## Exam traps

- Acyl chloride + nucleophile is **addition–elimination**, not "nucleophilic substitution".
- Arene + electrophile is **substitution**, not addition: the ring survives.
- Give **both words** of a mechanism name.
- Electrophile = **acceptor**; nucleophile = **donor**. Do not swap.
- Show **H⁺ loss** (electrophilic substitution) and **C=O re-forming** with **Cl⁻ leaving** (addition–elimination).
- Curly arrows start from a **bond or lone pair**, never an atom or a positive charge.
- In azo coupling the **diazonium ion is the electrophile** and the phenoxide ion's ring is attacked.

## Self-test

1. Define *electrophilic substitution*.
2. Define *addition–elimination*.
3. Give the electrophile in the Friedel–Crafts alkylation of benzene with CH₃Cl.
4. Write the equation showing how AlCl₃ is regenerated in a Friedel–Crafts reaction.
5. Explain why ethanoyl chloride is attacked by nucleophiles more readily than chloroethane.
6. Name the intermediate type formed in step 1 of the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with water.
7. Classify: C₆H₆ + Cl₂ → C₆H₅Cl + HCl (AlCl₃ catalyst).
8. Classify: CH₃CH₂COCl + CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃ + HCl.
9. Classify: CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃.
10. What is eliminated in step 2 of addition–elimination with an acyl chloride?
11. In the coupling of benzenediazonium ions with phenol, which species is the electrophile?
12. Why does benzene not undergo addition with bromine under the conditions used for alkenes?

**Answers:**

1. A reaction in which an electrophile attacks an electron-rich centre, such as a benzene ring, and replaces an
   atom (usually H).
2. A nucleophile adds to a carbonyl carbon to form an intermediate; then a group is eliminated and the C=O
   bond re-forms, so one group on the carbonyl carbon is replaced by another.
3. CH₃⁺.
4. H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → HCl + AlCl₃.
5. Its carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms (O and Cl), so it is more strongly δ+, and the
   C=O π bond can accept the electron pair in an addition step.
6. A tetrahedral intermediate carrying O⁻ (and an –OH₂⁺ group) on the former carbonyl carbon.
7. Electrophilic substitution.
8. Addition–elimination.
9. Electrophilic addition.
10. Cl⁻ (followed by loss of H⁺ from the nucleophile, giving HCl overall).
11. The benzenediazonium ion, C₆H₅N₂⁺.
12. Addition would destroy the stable delocalised π system; plain Br₂ is not a strong enough electrophile to
    disrupt it without a halogen carrier, and when it does react (with AlBr₃) it substitutes.
