These questions cover section 3.10.3, Magnetic flux and flux linkage, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [flux and flux linkage study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-magnetic-flux-and-flux-linkage/) or the [flux and flux linkage revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-magnetic-flux-and-flux-linkage-revision-notes/) first.

All fields are uniform unless stated.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define magnetic flux and state its unit. **[2]**

**2.** Show that the weber is equivalent to kg m² s⁻² A⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A flat coil is placed so that its plane lies parallel to the field lines. Which statement is correct?

A: The flux linkage is BAN.

B: The flux linkage is zero.

C: The flux linkage is BAN/2.

D: The flux linkage depends on the current in the coil.

**[1]**

**4.** A rectangular coil of 120 turns measures 0.080 m by 0.050 m. It is perpendicular to a field of 0.25 T.

**(a)** Calculate the flux through one turn. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the flux linkage. **[1]**

**5.** A coil of 50 turns and area 3.0 × 10⁻³ m² is in a field of 0.18 T. The field makes an angle of 40° with the plane of the coil. Calculate the flux linkage. **[2]**

**6.** A coil of 300 turns has an area of 12 cm² and is perpendicular to a field of 65 mT. Calculate its flux linkage. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** A rectangular coil of 80 turns measures 0.040 m by 0.060 m and sits in a field of 0.30 T. It can rotate about an axis perpendicular to the field.

**(a)** Calculate the flux linkage when the angle between the field and the normal to the coil is 0°, 60° and 90°. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the change in flux linkage when the coil turns from 0° to 180°. **[2]**

**8.** A square coil of side 0.050 m and 50 turns moves at a constant 0.020 m s⁻¹ through a region 0.15 m wide containing a field of 0.40 T perpendicular to the coil. There is no field outside the region. At t = 0 the leading edge of the coil reaches the field.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum flux linkage. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the times at which the coil becomes fully inside the field, at which it begins to leave, and at which it has fully left. **[3]**

**(c)** Describe the shape of the graph of flux linkage against time from t = 0 until the coil has left. **[2]**

**9.** A long solenoid has a cross-sectional area of 2.0 × 10⁻³ m² and a field of 0.030 T inside it. A coil of 150 turns and area 5.0 × 10⁻³ m² is wound tightly round the middle of the solenoid.

**(a)** Calculate the flux linkage of the coil. **[2]**

**(b)** The current in the solenoid is reversed, reversing the field. Calculate the change in flux linkage of the coil. **[1]**

**10.** A coil with maximum flux linkage BAN = 0.024 Wb rotates at 25 revolutions per second about an axis perpendicular to a uniform field. At t = 0 the normal to the coil is along the field.

**(a)** Write an equation for the flux linkage at time t, with the value of ω. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the flux linkage at t = 5.0 ms. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the first time after t = 0 at which the flux linkage is zero. **[1]**

**11.** A student says that a coil moved sideways at a steady speed inside a large uniform field must have a changing flux linkage, because it is moving. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**12.** A coil of 100 turns and area 2.0 × 10⁻³ m² is in a field of 0.50 T. Its flux linkage is 0.050 Wb. Calculate the angle between the field and the plane of the coil. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Magnetic flux is the product of the flux density and the area perpendicular to the field, Φ = BA [1]; unit: the **weber** (Wb), equal to T m² [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Wb = T m² [1]; T = N A⁻¹ m⁻¹ = kg s⁻² A⁻¹, so Wb = kg s⁻² A⁻¹ × m² = **kg m² s⁻² A⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** A = 0.080 × 0.050 = 4.0 × 10⁻³ m² [1]; Φ = BA = 0.25 × 4.0 × 10⁻³ = **1.0 × 10⁻³ Wb** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** NΦ = 120 × 1.0 × 10⁻³ = **0.12 Wb** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Angle to the normal = 50°, so NΦ = BAN cos 50° (= BAN sin 40°) [1] = 0.18 × 3.0 × 10⁻³ × 50 × 0.643 = **1.7 × 10⁻² Wb** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A = 12 × 10⁻⁴ m² [1]; NΦ = 0.065 × 12 × 10⁻⁴ × 300 = **2.3 × 10⁻² Wb** [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** BAN = 0.30 × 2.4 × 10⁻³ × 80 = **0.058 Wb** at 0° [1]; × cos 60° = **0.029 Wb** at 60° [1]; **zero** at 90° [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** NΦ goes from +0.0576 Wb to −0.0576 Wb [1]; change = **0.12 Wb** (2BAN) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** BAN = 0.40 × 0.050² × 50 = **0.050 Wb** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Fully inside after 0.050 / 0.020 = **2.5 s** [1]; begins to leave when the leading edge reaches the far side, 0.15 / 0.020 = **7.5 s** [1]; fully left at **10.0 s** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** A straight line rising from 0 to 0.050 Wb between 0 and 2.5 s, horizontal at 0.050 Wb from 2.5 s to 7.5 s [1], then a straight line falling to zero between 7.5 s and 10.0 s (a trapezium shape) [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** The field exists only inside the solenoid, so use its area [1]: NΦ = 150 × 0.030 × 2.0 × 10⁻³ = **9.0 × 10⁻³ Wb** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** From +9.0 × 10⁻³ to −9.0 × 10⁻³ Wb: change = **1.8 × 10⁻² Wb** [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** ω = 2π × 25 = 157 rad s⁻¹ [1]; NΦ = 0.024 cos(157t) Wb [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** ωt = 157 × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = 0.785 rad (45°), so NΦ = 0.024 × cos 45° = **0.017 Wb** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** A quarter of a period: T = 1/25 = 0.040 s, so t = **0.010 s** [1]. **[1]**

**11.** Flux linkage depends on B, the area in the field and the angle [1]. Moving sideways inside a uniform field changes none of these, so the flux linkage stays constant (and no emf is induced) [1]. **[2]**

**12.** BAN = 0.50 × 2.0 × 10⁻³ × 100 = 0.10 Wb, so cos θ = 0.050 / 0.10 = 0.50 and the angle to the normal is 60° [1]; angle to the plane = **30°** [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cos of the angle to the plane rather than to the normal.
- Forgetting the reversal when a coil turns through 180°.
- Leaving cm² or mT unconverted.
- Using the coil area when the field fills only part of it.
- Drawing a flux linkage graph that changes while the coil is wholly inside a uniform field.
