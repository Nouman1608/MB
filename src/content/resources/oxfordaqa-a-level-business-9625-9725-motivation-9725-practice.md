This set tests **3.2.2.4 Motivation** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). Section A has short-answer and calculation questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. Every business and figure is invented. Revise first with the [motivation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-motivation-9725/) and the [motivation revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-motivation-9725-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term job enrichment. **[2]**

**2.** State **two** hygiene factors in Herzberg's two-factor theory. **[2]**

**3.** A machinist is paid $0.80 for each garment she finishes. Last week she finished 520 garments. Calculate her pay for the week. **[2]**

**4.** A kitchen showroom pays its designers a basic salary of $1,500 a month plus 4% commission on the value of kitchens they sell. Calculate the monthly pay of a designer who sells kitchens worth $55,000. **[3]**

**5.** A worker at Tolland Components is paid $14 an hour for a 38-hour week and produces 760 units. The firm is considering piece rate pay of $0.65 per unit, which it expects would raise the worker's output to 900 units a week.

**(a)** Calculate the labour cost per unit under the hourly wage. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the worker's weekly pay and the labour cost per unit under piece rate. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **one** difference between Maslow's and Herzberg's views of the role of pay in motivation. **[3]**

**7.** Explain **one** benefit to a supermarket of using job rotation for its shop-floor staff. **[3]**

**8.** State **two** examples of fringe benefits. **[2]**

## Section B

**9.** Brightcode Software pays its engineers well above the industry average, yet labour turnover is high and many engineers say their work is repetitive. Using Maslow's hierarchy of needs, analyse why a further pay rise may not improve the engineers' motivation. **[6]**

**10.** Halford Motors sells new and used cars.

**(a)** Explain **two** reasons why commission may be a suitable way to pay Halford's sales staff. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain **one** drawback for Halford of paying its sales staff mainly by commission. **[2]**

**11.** A call centre has empowered its advisers to give refunds of up to $50 without a manager's approval. Explain **two** ways the call centre could assess whether this has been effective. **[4]**

**12.** Penhallow Foods makes ready meals in a factory with 180 production workers on hourly wages. Over the past year labour productivity has fallen by 6%, absence has risen and retailers have complained about inconsistent quality. Workers each do one short task on the line for the whole shift. The operations director wants to introduce piece rate pay. The HR director prefers to organise workers into teams responsible for a whole product, including quality checks. Evaluate which approach Penhallow should choose to improve motivation. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Giving employees more responsibility and more challenging tasks [1], such as planning their own work or checking its quality, so that the job uses more of their abilities [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: pay [1]; working conditions [1]; supervision; company policy; relationships at work. **[2]**

**3.** 0.80 × 520 [1] = **$416** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Commission = 0.04 × 55,000 = $2,200 [1]. Total pay = 1,500 + 2,200 [1] = **$3,700** [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** Weekly pay = 14 × 38 = $532; labour cost per unit = 532 ÷ 760 [1] = **$0.70** [1].

**(b)** Pay = 0.65 × 900 = **$585** [1]; labour cost per unit = 585 ÷ 900 = **$0.65** [1]. **[4]**

**6.** For Maslow, pay helps meet lower-level needs, basic and safety, so it can motivate until those needs are met [1]. For Herzberg, pay is a hygiene factor [1]: poor pay causes dissatisfaction, but raising it removes dissatisfaction without creating lasting motivation [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Staff move between tasks such as stocking shelves, working on the tills and serving at the deli counter [1], which reduces boredom from doing one task all day [1]. The supermarket also gains staff who can cover several roles, so absences or a sudden queue can be handled without extra hiring [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Any two of: company car [1]; health insurance [1]; staff discount; pension contributions; free meals; gym membership. **[2]**

**9.** The engineers are already paid well, so their basic and safety needs are likely to be met [1]. According to Maslow, once a need is satisfied it no longer motivates [1], so more pay adds little [1]. Repetitive work suggests their esteem and self-actualisation needs are not being met [1]. High turnover may mean engineers leave for jobs offering more challenge and recognition [1]. Brightcode would do better to offer varied projects, training and recognition for achievements, which meet those higher needs [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Each sale is worth a large amount, so commission links pay directly to the revenue each person brings in [1], encouraging staff to work hard to close sales [1]. Labour costs then rise only when sales rise [1], which protects Halford's cash flow in months when car sales are low [1].

**(b)** Staff may use high-pressure selling to earn commission [1], which could upset customers and damage Halford's reputation, reducing repeat business [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Compare customer satisfaction scores before and after the change [1]: faster resolution of complaints should raise satisfaction if empowerment is working [1]. Track the average time taken to resolve calls and the total value of refunds given [1], checking that faster service has not led to excessive refunds that outweigh the benefit [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Credit chains of reasoning and a judgement, for example:

- Piece rate links pay to output, so, following Taylor, workers have a financial reason to work faster [1], which could reverse the 6% fall in productivity [1].
- Output on a production line is easy to count, so piece rate is practical to run [1].
- But retailers already complain about quality; paying by output may encourage rushing and make quality worse [1].
- One short task per shift is repetitive; piece rate does nothing about boredom, which may explain rising absence [1].
- Teams responsible for a whole product enrich the job, adding variety and responsibility [1]: in Herzberg's terms, these are motivators [1].
- Teamwork also meets social needs (Maslow), which may reduce absence [1].
- Giving teams responsibility for quality checks directly addresses retailers' complaints [1].
- However, teamworking needs training and reorganising the line, so it costs more and takes longer to show results [1].
- Penhallow could combine the approaches with a team bonus for output that meets quality targets [1].
- Judgement: Penhallow should choose teamworking, because the evidence of boredom, absence and poor quality points to job design rather than pay, but it should measure productivity, absence and complaints before and after to assess whether it works [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling pay a motivator in an answer that uses Herzberg.
- Describing Maslow's hierarchy without saying which level the workers in the case have not met.
- Calculating labour cost per unit using the wrong output figure.
- Recommending piece rate where quality is the main problem.
- Not saying how the business will know whether a method has worked.
