This guide covers **4.10.2 Life cycle assessment and recycling** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. It sits in topic 4.10, Using resources, and is assessed on
Paper 2 at both Foundation and Higher Tier. It is not chemistry only, and there is no Higher tier only
statement. Much of the assessment is **interpreting and evaluating information**, so practise with data tables.

## 4.10.2.1 Life cycle assessment

A **life cycle assessment (LCA)** assesses the **environmental impact of a product** at every stage of its life.
There are four stages:

1. **Extracting and processing raw materials**
2. **Manufacturing and packaging**
3. **Use and operation during its lifetime**
4. **Disposal at the end of its useful life**, including **transport and distribution at each stage**.

### What can be measured

- The use of **water**, **resources** and **energy sources** and the production of some **wastes** can be fairly
  easily **quantified** (given a number).
- **Pollutant effects** are harder. Allocating a numerical value to the damage caused by a pollutant needs
  **value judgements**, so **LCA is not a purely objective process**. Two people may give different weight to,
  say, a lost habitat compared with a carbon dioxide emission.

### Comparative LCAs

LCAs should be done as a **comparison** of the impact on the environment of the stages in the life of a product,
and **quantified only where data is readily available** for energy, water, resources and wastes. You must be able
to **carry out simple comparative LCAs for shopping bags made from plastic and paper**, and to **interpret LCAs**
of other materials or products given information.

### Worked example 1: plastic and paper shopping bags

The table gives illustrative energy use per bag (the numbers are invented to practise the method).

| Stage | Plastic bag / MJ | Paper bag / MJ |
|---|---|---|
| Extracting and processing raw materials | 0.9 | 1.2 |
| Manufacturing and packaging | 0.6 | 1.1 |
| Transport and distribution | 0.1 | 0.3 |
| Disposal | 0.1 | 0.2 |
| **Total** | **1.7** | **2.8** |

1. Add each column: plastic 0.9 + 0.6 + 0.1 + 0.1 = 1.7 MJ; paper 1.2 + 1.1 + 0.3 + 0.2 = 2.8 MJ.
2. On energy alone, the plastic bag has the lower impact **per bag**.
3. The **use** stage matters. If a plastic bag is used 3 times and a paper bag only once, the energy **per use**
   is 1.7 ÷ 3 = **0.57 MJ** for plastic and 2.8 MJ for paper.
4. Other data is also needed: water use, waste, and what happens at disposal (a plastic bag may last for
   decades in the environment, paper decomposes more quickly).

A good comparison uses **both** the numbers and the points that cannot be easily measured, and reaches a
conclusion with a reason.

### Misuse of LCAs

**Selective or abbreviated LCAs** can be devised to evaluate a product, but they can be **misused to reach
pre-determined conclusions**, for example in support of **claims for advertising**. A company might quote only the
stage where its product performs best and leave out stages where it performs badly. When you evaluate an LCA
claim, ask: Which stages are included? Who paid for it? Are the numbers shown? What is left out?

## 4.10.2.2 Ways of reducing the use of resources

The **reduction in use, reuse and recycling** of materials by end users **reduces**:

- the use of limited resources
- the use of energy sources
- waste
- environmental impacts.

**Metals, glass, building materials, clay ceramics and most plastics** are made from **limited raw materials**,
and **much of the energy** for their production comes from limited resources. **Quarrying and mining** cause
**environmental impacts**: loss of habitat, noise, dust, spoil heaps and carbon dioxide emissions from machinery
and transport.

### Reuse and recycling

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Reuse** | The product is used again for the same purpose without remaking | **Glass bottles** are washed and refilled |
| **Recycling** | The material is remade into new products | **Glass bottles** can be **crushed and melted** to make different glass products |

Some products **cannot be reused**, so they are **recycled for a different use**. **Metals** can be recycled by
**melting and recasting** or **reforming** into different products. The amount of **separation** needed depends
on the material and the properties needed in the final product. For example, some **scrap steel** can be
**added to iron from a blast furnace** to reduce the amount of iron that needs to be extracted from iron ore.

### Worked example 2: energy saved

*Making a metal from ore needs 200 MJ per kilogram. Making it from scrap needs 10 MJ per kilogram. Calculate the
percentage of energy saved by recycling.*

```
saving = 200 - 10 = 190 MJ per kg
percentage = 190 / 200 x 100 = 95 %
```

(The values are illustrative; real figures vary by metal.) Recycling also saves the ore, reduces mining and
reduces the waste sent to landfill.

### Evaluating ways to reduce the use of limited resources

| Way | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Reduce** use (use less, repair, share) | Saves all the energy and raw materials | Needs changes in behaviour |
| **Reuse** (refill bottles) | Little energy needed; no reprocessing | Needs washing and collection; bottles must be returned |
| **Recycle** (melt and reform) | Saves ore and energy; less waste | Collection and sorting cost; some materials degrade; energy still needed |

Questions give you data on cost, energy and waste, so your answer should refer to numbers and reach a conclusion
(for example, "reuse is better than recycling for glass bottles because it needs no melting").

## Exam technique

- Name the **four stages** of an LCA when asked.
- When asked to compare products, **total the data** for each stage, then comment on **use**, **disposal**, and
  what **cannot be measured**.
- State clearly that LCAs involve **value judgements** when you discuss pollutant effects.
- For recycling, give a **resource saved** (ore, energy) and a **problem** (cost of collecting and sorting).
- Use words such as **finite**, **landfill**, **quarrying** and **sustainable**.

## Common mistakes

- Saying an LCA only looks at the manufacture of a product.
- Saying LCAs are fully objective because they use numbers.
- Treating recycling as free of energy use.
- Mixing up reuse (same item used again) and recycling (material remade).
- Ignoring the use stage when comparing products.
- Drawing a conclusion without using the data.
