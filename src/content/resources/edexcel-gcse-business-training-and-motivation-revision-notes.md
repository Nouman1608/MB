## Key terms

| Term | Short definition |
|---|---|
| Training | Teaching an employee the skills needed to do a job well |
| Development | Preparing an employee for more responsibility over time (standard knowledge: the specification does not define it) |
| Formal training | Planned, structured, often away from the work area, may give a qualification |
| Informal training | Learning during normal work, such as shadowing or coaching |
| Self-learning | The employee takes charge of their own learning |
| Target | A goal to be reached by a set time; good targets are clear, measurable, achievable and dated (standard knowledge, SMART-style) |
| Performance review | A meeting to assess work against targets and agree next steps |
| Retention | Keeping employees rather than losing them |
| Motivation | The desire to work hard and do the job well |
| Productivity | Output for a given input, usually per worker (standard knowledge, not given in the specification: output ÷ number of employees) |
| Remuneration | Payment for work: a wage (often hourly or weekly) or a salary (fixed annual amount); the wage and salary split is standard knowledge |
| Bonus | An extra payment for meeting a target or for good results |
| Commission | Pay linked to sales made, usually a percentage of sales |
| Promotion | Moving to a higher post with more responsibility, usually with more pay |
| Fringe benefits | Extras given on top of pay |
| Job rotation | Moving between tasks or jobs at planned times |
| Job enrichment | Adding more challenging, responsible tasks to a job |
| Autonomy | Freedom to decide how to do the work |

## 2.5.3 Training and development

### Ways to train and develop

| Way | Example | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Formal training | Outside course, college day release | Consistent content; qualification | Cost; time away from work |
| Informal training | Shadowing, coaching at the workstation | Cheap; fits the real job | Depends on the teacher; slows the teacher |
| Self-learning | Online module, manual, practice | Own pace; low cost | Needs discipline; hard to check |
| Ongoing training for all employees | Regular updates for new and long-serving staff | Skills stay current | Repeated cost and time |
| Target setting | A stated output or quality goal with a deadline | Clear expectations | Unrealistic targets cause stress |
| Performance reviews | Yearly or half-yearly meeting with a manager | Feedback; finds training needs | Takes manager time |

### Why train and develop

- Link: training can build confidence and satisfaction, which can raise motivation, which can improve retention.
- Retention saves recruitment costs: advertising, interviewing and lost output while a newcomer learns.
- Retraining for new technology: employees learn the new equipment or software so the investment is used well.
- Risk to weigh: a trained employee may leave for a rival.

### Method in steps: recommending a training method

1. Identify the need (new skill, new technology, weak performance, new starter).
2. Match a method to it (for example, a supplier course for new software, shadowing for local knowledge).
3. State the cost, including time away from work.
4. Say how success will be checked (a target and a review).
5. Reach a judgement and give a reason.

## 2.5.4 Motivation

### Why it matters

| Reason | Think |
|---|---|
| Attracting employees | Better applicants; wider choice |
| Retaining employees | Fewer leavers; lower recruitment cost; experience kept |
| Productivity | More output per worker; fewer errors |

### Financial methods

| Method | One-line point | Typical drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Remuneration | Wage or salary | Pay alone may not hold people |
| Bonus | Extra for target or results | Quality may suffer if only the target matters |
| Commission | Percentage of sales | Pay uncertain; pushy selling |
| Promotion | Higher post and usually more pay | Few posts; disappointment |
| Fringe benefits | Non-pay extras | Cost; valued differently |

### Non-financial methods

| Method | One-line point | Typical drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Job rotation | Variety; wider skills; cover for absence | Extra training; slower at first |
| Job enrichment | More challenge and responsibility | May expect more pay; needs training |
| Autonomy | Trust and freedom to decide | Poor decisions; some want guidance |

## Worked reminders

**Commission.** A sales assistant earns a basic £1,400 a month plus 5% commission. Sales are £24,000. Commission = 0.05 × 24,000 = £1,200. Total = £1,400 + £1,200 = £2,600.

**Bonus.** A workshop pays £2.50 for every unit above a target of 120 units. An employee makes 164. Extra units = 164 − 120 = 44. Bonus = 44 × 2.50 = £110.

**Productivity.** A workshop makes 3,150 items with 7 employees. Productivity = 3,150 ÷ 7 = 450 items per employee.

**Percentage change.** Leavers fall from 12 to 9. Change = (9 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 = −25%, a fall of 25%. Divide by the starting figure.

**Mean.** Five employees had 2, 5, 3, 6 and 4 days of training. Mean = (2 + 5 + 3 + 6 + 4) ÷ 5 = 20 ÷ 5 = 4 days.

## Applying it to a case

| If the case says... | Link to make |
|---|---|
| New software or machines are being introduced | Retraining so the investment is used well; fewer mistakes; less anxiety |
| Many staff are leaving | Retention: training, promotion prospects, pay, fringe benefits; recruitment costs avoided |
| Output per worker is low | Productivity: targets, bonus or commission, job enrichment, autonomy |
| Jobs are repetitive | Job rotation or job enrichment to reduce boredom |
| The business struggles to fill vacancies | Attracting employees: competitive remuneration, benefits, a reputation for training |
| The business has little spare cash | Low-cost methods: informal training, self-learning, autonomy |

### Method in steps: an evaluation of a motivation method

1. Name the method and say what it is.
2. Explain how it affects attracting employees, retaining them or productivity.
3. Give a cost or drawback, using figures from the case if there are any.
4. Compare with an alternative.
5. Decide, and give a reason that refers to the business.

## Must-know distinctions

- Training teaches skills for the present job. Development prepares for the future (standard knowledge).
- Formal training is planned and structured. Informal training happens during work.
- A bonus is linked to a target or results. Commission is linked to sales made.
- Remuneration is payment for work. Fringe benefits are extras beyond that pay.
- Job rotation changes the task. Job enrichment makes the task more responsible.
- Autonomy is about deciding how to work. Job enrichment is about what the work contains.
- Motivation is the desire to work well. Productivity is the measured output.
- Retention keeps existing employees. Attracting brings in new ones.

## Quick self-test

1. Give one example of informal training.
2. State what a performance review is.
3. Explain one reason why a business might retrain staff when new technology arrives.
4. A team leader earns £1,400 plus 5% commission on sales of £24,000. Calculate total pay.
5. Seven employees make 3,150 items. Calculate productivity.
6. Which is a financial method: job enrichment, autonomy or a bonus?
7. Staff leavers fall from 12 to 9. Calculate the percentage change.
8. Explain the difference between job rotation and job enrichment.
9. Six employees each go on a £250 course. Calculate the total course fee.
10. Of 24 employees, 18 had a review this year. Calculate the percentage reviewed.
11. Give two ways in which motivation can help a business.
12. Calculate the mean number of training days for 2, 5, 3, 6 and 4 days.

### Answers

1. Shadowing a colleague, or being coached at the workstation.
2. A meeting where a manager and employee assess work against targets, give feedback and agree next steps.
3. Employees may not know how to use the new equipment, so retraining lets them use it correctly. This means the investment is used well and employees feel less anxious about their jobs.
4. £1,400 + (0.05 × 24,000) = £1,400 + £1,200 = £2,600.
5. 3,150 ÷ 7 = 450 items per employee.
6. A bonus.
7. (9 − 12) ÷ 12 × 100 = −25%, a fall of 25%.
8. Job rotation moves employees between tasks, giving variety. Job enrichment adds more challenging, responsible tasks to a job.
9. 6 × £250 = £1,500.
10. 18 ÷ 24 × 100 = 75%.
11. Any two of: attracting employees, retaining employees, raising productivity.
12. (2 + 5 + 3 + 6 + 4) ÷ 5 = 4 days.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a method with no explanation of how it motivates or trains.
- Mixing up a bonus and commission, or rotation and enrichment.
- Writing about motivation in general when the question names a business and its situation.
- Dividing a percentage change by the new figure instead of the original.
- Giving only the advantages of a method and no drawback.
- Forgetting the cost of training, including time away from work.
- Ending an "evaluate" or "justify" answer without a clear decision.
- Claiming that money is the only motivator.
