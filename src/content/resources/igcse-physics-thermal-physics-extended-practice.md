## Questions

**1.** *(Extended)* A bicycle pump is sealed at the end and contains 60 cm³ of air at a pressure of 100 kPa. The handle is pushed in slowly, so the temperature stays the same, until the volume of the air is 24 cm³. Calculate the new pressure of the air. **[2]**

**2.** *(Extended)* A fixed mass of gas in a sealed cylinder is slowly compressed so that its temperature stays the same. Explain, in terms of the particles and the forces they exert, why the pressure of the gas increases. **[3]**

**3.** *(Extended)* A 60 W electric heater is placed in 0.80 kg of water. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg °C). Assuming all the energy from the heater goes into the water, calculate the time needed to raise the temperature of the water by 15 °C. **[3]**

**4.** *(Extended)* State **two** differences between boiling and evaporation. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Explain, in terms of particles, why thermal conduction in a gas is much poorer than in a solid. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A metal plate left in sunlight absorbs energy at a rate of 30 W and, at its present temperature, transfers energy away at a rate of 22 W. State and explain what happens to the temperature of the plate over the next few minutes. **[2]**

**7.** *(Extended)* A sealed syringe holds 36 cm³ of gas at a pressure of 120 kPa. The plunger is pulled out slowly, at constant temperature, until the gas occupies 90 cm³.
(a) Calculate the new pressure of the gas. **[2]**
(b) Explain, in terms of collisions between the particles and the walls, why the pressure has changed in this way. **[2]**

**8.** *(Extended)* A 2.5 kg block of aluminium is heated from 20 °C to 80 °C by an electric heater rated at 500 W. The specific heat capacity of aluminium is 900 J/(kg °C).
(a) Calculate the energy needed to raise the temperature of the block. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the time the heater would take if no energy were lost. **[1]**
(c) In practice the heater is on for 360 s. Calculate the energy wasted. **[2]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A student heats a 0.50 kg block of metal with a 40 W heater for 300 s. The temperature of the block rises from 12 °C to 36 °C.
(a) Calculate the specific heat capacity of the metal. **[4]**
(b) Some energy escapes to the surroundings during the experiment. State whether your value is larger or smaller than the true value, and why. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A shallow dish of water is left in a warm room.
(a) Explain, in terms of particles, why the water in the dish becomes slightly cooler as it evaporates. **[3]**
(b) State **one** change that would make the water evaporate faster. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A kettle is rated at 2000 W. It contains 1.2 kg of water at 20 °C. The specific heat capacity of water is 4200 J/(kg °C).
(a) Calculate the energy needed to raise the temperature of the water to its boiling point of 100 °C. **[2]**
(b) The kettle takes 224 s to bring the water to 100 °C. Calculate the energy supplied by the heater and the energy wasted. **[2]**
(c) Calculate the efficiency of the kettle, as a percentage. **[2]**
(d) Suggest **two** places the wasted energy goes. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Two plates of the same size and material are placed in strong sunlight. One has a matt black surface and the other a shiny silver surface. After 30 minutes the black plate is at 58 °C and the silver plate is at 41 °C.
(a) Explain why the black plate is hotter. **[2]**
(b) Explain, in terms of the rates at which energy is absorbed and emitted, why the temperature of the black plate stops rising. **[3]**
(c) A cloud covers the Sun. Describe and explain what happens to the temperature of the black plate. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** *(Extended)* p₁V₁ = p₂V₂, so 100 × 60 = p₂ × 24 [1]; p₂ = 6000 ÷ 24 = **250 kPa** [1].

*Examiner insight:* multiplying by the volume ratio the wrong way round is the usual slip. A smaller volume must give a larger pressure, so check that your answer is bigger than 100 kPa.

**2.** *(Extended)* The gas particles collide with the walls, and each collision exerts a **force** on the wall [1]. With a smaller volume the particles hit the walls **more often** (more collisions per second on each unit of area) [1], so the total force per unit area, which is the **pressure**, is greater [1].

*Examiner insight:* saying the particles move faster loses the marks. At constant temperature their average speed does not change; the increase comes from more frequent collisions.

**3.** *(Extended)* ΔE = mcΔθ = 0.80 × 4200 × 15 = **50 400 J** [1]. t = ΔE ÷ P [1] = 50 400 ÷ 60 = **840 s** (14 minutes) [1].

*Examiner insight:* candidates who stop at the energy lose the last two marks. The question asks for the time, so the energy must then be divided by the power.

**4.** *(Extended)* Boiling happens at **one fixed temperature** (the boiling point), but evaporation happens at **any temperature** [1]; boiling happens **throughout the liquid** with bubbles forming, but evaporation happens **only at the surface** [1].

*Examiner insight:* each difference needs both sides stated, for example "at any temperature" against "only at the boiling point". Describing only one process earns nothing.

**5.** *(Extended)* In a gas the particles are **far apart** [1], so they collide with each other only rarely and energy is passed on from particle to particle **much more slowly** than through the closely packed, vibrating particles of a solid [1].

*Examiner insight:* saying gas particles "do not move" or "cannot vibrate" is wrong. They move quickly; the problem is the large spacing between them.

**6.** *(Extended)* The temperature of the plate **rises** [1], because it receives energy faster than it transfers energy away (30 W > 22 W); as it gets hotter it transfers energy away faster, until the two rates are equal and the temperature stays **constant** [1].

*Examiner insight:* "it keeps getting hotter for ever" is the common error. A hotter object transfers energy away faster, so it reaches a new steady temperature.

**7.** *(Extended)*
(a) p₁V₁ = p₂V₂, so 120 × 36 = p₂ × 90 [1]; p₂ = 4320 ÷ 90 = **48 kPa** [1].
(b) The same particles now have a larger volume, so they hit the walls **less often** [1]; the force per unit area on the walls is smaller, so the pressure falls [1].

*Examiner insight:* in part (b), link the fewer collisions to force on a unit area of wall. "Less pressure because fewer collisions" with no mention of force gets one mark at best.

**8.** *(Extended)*
(a) ΔE = mcΔθ = 2.5 × 900 × 60 [1] = **135 000 J** [1].
(b) t = E ÷ P = 135 000 ÷ 500 = **270 s** [1].
(c) Energy supplied = 500 × 360 = 180 000 J [1]; wasted = 180 000 − 135 000 = **45 000 J** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the temperature change is 80 − 20 = 60 °C, not 80 °C. Using the final temperature in mcΔθ is the most common way to lose the method mark in part (a).

**9.** *(Extended)*
(a) E = Pt = 40 × 300 = 12 000 J [1]; Δθ = 36 − 12 = 24 °C [1]; c = E ÷ (mΔθ) [1] = 12 000 ÷ (0.50 × 24) = **1000 J/(kg °C)** [1].
(b) **Larger** than the true value [1], because less energy than 12 000 J actually stayed in the block, so the true energy per kg per °C is smaller.

*Examiner insight:* in (b) the direction alone earns nothing without the reason. Say that the energy used is greater than the energy gained.

**10.** *(Extended)*
(a) The particles with the most kinetic energy are the ones that escape from the surface [1]; the particles left behind have a lower average kinetic energy [1]; a lower average kinetic energy means a lower temperature, so the water cools [1].
(b) Any one of: warmer water or air; larger surface area; moving air across the surface (a draught) [1].

*Examiner insight:* "the fast particles escape" must be followed by what remains. Without the lower average energy of the particles left behind, the cooling is not explained.

**11.** *(Extended)*
(a) ΔE = mcΔθ = 1.2 × 4200 × 80 [1] = **403 200 J** [1].
(b) Energy supplied = Pt = 2000 × 224 = **448 000 J** [1]; wasted = 448 000 − 403 200 = **44 800 J** [1].
(c) Efficiency = useful energy ÷ total energy × 100% [1] = 403 200 ÷ 448 000 × 100 = **90%** [1].
(d) Any two of: the body of the kettle is warmed; the surrounding air is warmed; the surface or stand under the kettle is warmed [2].

*Examiner insight:* the temperature rise is 100 − 20 = 80 °C. In (c), efficiency is useful energy over total energy supplied, not over the wasted energy.

**12.** *(Extended)*
(a) A matt black surface is a **good absorber** of radiation [1], so the black plate absorbs energy from the Sun at a greater rate than the shiny plate [1].
(b) At first the plate absorbs energy faster than it emits it, so its temperature rises [1]; the hotter the plate, the faster it emits radiation [1]; when the rate of emission equals the rate of absorption, the temperature stays constant [1].
(c) The rate of absorption falls, so it is now less than the rate of emission [1]; the temperature of the plate falls [1], until the plate emits at the same (lower) rate as it now absorbs and the temperature becomes steady again at a lower value [1].

*Examiner insight:* answers that stay at "black absorbs more" lose the second mark. The question asks about rates of energy, so write "rate" and compare the two.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Turning the pV = constant calculation upside down. Check whether the pressure should rise or fall before you write the answer.
- Explaining gas pressure without mentioning force, or saying particles speed up when the temperature is constant.
- Using the final temperature instead of the temperature change in mcΔθ.
- Stopping part-way through a two-step specific heat capacity calculation, such as finding energy but not time.
- Describing boiling and evaporation separately instead of making direct comparisons.
- Explaining evaporative cooling without saying that the remaining particles have a lower average kinetic energy.
- Forgetting that an object reaches a constant temperature when it gains and loses energy at the same rate.
- Calculating efficiency from the wasted energy instead of the useful energy.
