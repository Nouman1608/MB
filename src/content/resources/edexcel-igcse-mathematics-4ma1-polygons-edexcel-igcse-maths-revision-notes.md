Quick-recall notes for section 4.2, Polygons, in Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1). The section is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also covers, so it applies to both tiers. For explanations and worked examples, read the [polygons study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-polygons-edexcel-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [polygons practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-polygons-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Polygon | A closed shape with straight sides |
| Quadrilateral | A four-sided polygon |
| Regular polygon | All sides equal and all angles equal |
| Interior angle | Angle inside the polygon at a vertex |
| Exterior angle | Angle between one side and the extension of the next |
| Diagonal | A line joining two vertices that are not next to each other |
| Bisect | Cut into two equal parts |
| Congruent | Exactly the same shape and size |

## Names to know

triangle 3 · quadrilateral 4 · pentagon 5 · hexagon 6 · octagon 8 · decagon 10

## The angle formulae

```
quadrilateral angle sum          360°
polygon angle sum (n sides)      (n − 2) × 180°  =  (2n − 4) right angles
exterior angles of any polygon   add up to 360°
interior + exterior at a vertex  180°
regular polygon exterior         360° ÷ n
regular polygon interior         180° − 360° ÷ n
number of sides (regular)        n = 360° ÷ exterior angle
```

| Polygon | n | Angle sum | Regular exterior | Regular interior |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Triangle | 3 | 180° | 120° | 60° |
| Quadrilateral | 4 | 360° | 90° | 90° |
| Pentagon | 5 | 540° | 72° | 108° |
| Hexagon | 6 | 720° | 60° | 120° |
| Octagon | 8 | 1080° | 45° | 135° |
| Decagon | 10 | 1440° | 36° | 144° |
| 12-sided | 12 | 1800° | 30° | 150° |

## Special quadrilaterals

| Shape | Key facts |
|---|---|
| Parallelogram | opposite sides equal and parallel; opposite angles equal; neighbouring angles add to 180°; diagonals bisect each other |
| Rectangle | all angles 90°; diagonals equal and bisect each other |
| Rhombus | all sides equal; diagonals bisect each other at 90° and bisect the angles |
| Square | all sides equal, all angles 90°; diagonals equal, at 90°, bisecting each other and the angles |
| Trapezium | exactly one pair of parallel sides; same-side angles between them add to 180° |
| Kite | two pairs of equal neighbouring sides; one pair of equal opposite angles (between unequal sides); diagonals at 90° |

Family tree: square ⊂ rhombus ⊂ parallelogram; square ⊂ rectangle ⊂ parallelogram.

## Identifying a quadrilateral from clues

| Clue | Shape |
|---|---|
| diagonals equal and bisect each other at 90° | square |
| diagonals bisect each other at 90°, not equal | rhombus |
| diagonals equal and bisect each other, not at 90° | rectangle |
| diagonals bisect each other only | parallelogram |
| only one pair of parallel sides | trapezium |
| diagonals at 90°, only one bisected | kite |

## Can a regular polygon have this angle?

Find the exterior angle (180 − interior). It is possible only if 360 ÷ exterior is a whole number of at least 3.

```
interior 150°  → exterior 30°  → 360 ÷ 30 = 12      yes
interior 130°  → exterior 50°  → 360 ÷ 50 = 7.2     no
```

## Combining facts

- Two sides of a regular polygon meeting at a vertex are equal, so joining their far ends makes an isosceles triangle: in a regular hexagon, the angle between a side and the short diagonal is (180 − 120) ÷ 2 = 30°.
- Polygons meeting at a point: the angles at that point add up to 360°.
- An unknown angle in an irregular polygon: angle sum minus the known angles.

## Congruence

- Congruent = same shape and same size; reflections and rotations of a shape are congruent to it.
- A diagonal of a parallelogram makes two congruent triangles.
- Similar shapes (same shape, different size) are not congruent.

## Exam traps

- **360 ÷ n is the exterior angle**, not the interior angle.
- **(n − 2), not n.** A hexagon's angles add up to 720°, not 1080°.
- **Regular means sides and angles.** A rhombus is not regular; a rectangle is not regular unless it is a square.
- **Kite angles.** The equal pair is between the unequal sides.
- **Trapezium.** Only one pair of parallel sides; do not assume equal sides.
- **Give reasons.** "Angles in a quadrilateral add up to 360°", "opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal".

## Self-test

1. How many sides has an octagon?
2. Three angles of a quadrilateral are 92°, 78° and 115°. Find the fourth.
3. Find the sum of the interior angles of a decagon.
4. Write (2n − 4) right angles for n = 7 in degrees.
5. Find the interior angle of a regular 12-sided polygon.
6. A regular polygon has exterior angles of 20°. How many sides has it?
7. Can a regular polygon have interior angles of 125°? Explain.
8. In a parallelogram, one angle is 68°. Find the other three.
9. Which quadrilateral has diagonals that are equal and bisect each other, but not at right angles?
10. Kite PQRS has PQ = PS and RQ = RS. Angle QPS = 80° and angle QRS = 60°. Find angle PQR.
11. Is a rhombus a regular polygon? Explain.
12. A shape is enlarged by scale factor 2. Is the image congruent to the original?

### Answers

1. **8**.
2. 360 − 285 = **75°**.
3. 8 × 180 = **1440°**.
4. 10 right angles = **900°**.
5. 180 − 30 = **150°**.
6. 360 ÷ 20 = **18**.
7. Exterior 55°; 360 ÷ 55 = 6.54..., not a whole number, so **no**.
8. **112°, 68° and 112°**.
9. **Rectangle**.
10. (360 − 80 − 60) ÷ 2 = **110°**.
11. **Not in general**: its sides are equal but its angles need not be.
12. **No**: the size has changed.
