## Core recall

### What the specification lists

- Methods of good service: product knowledge; customer engagement (creating a positive experience for the customer); post-sales services (for example user training, help lines, servicing).
- Benefits: increase in customer satisfaction; customer loyalty; increased spend; profitability.
- Dangers of poor service: dissatisfied customers; poor reputation via word of mouth; reduction in revenue.
- Understand the sales process; analyse techniques businesses use.
- ICT developments: websites; e-commerce; social media.

### The sales process

1. Before the sale: information is easy to find.
2. Finding out needs: ask and listen.
3. Helping the customer choose: product knowledge.
4. Making the sale: quick, accurate, convenient.
5. After the sale: delivery, set-up, help, repairs, complaints.

### Methods with examples

| Method | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product knowledge | Staff can explain products and match them to needs | A bike shop worker advising on frame size |
| Customer engagement | A positive experience: friendly, attentive, no pressure | A café remembering a regular's order |
| User training | Showing customers how to use the product | A shop setting up a new laptop |
| Help lines | Phone, chat or email support | A broadband company's 24-hour line |
| Servicing | Maintenance and repairs after purchase | An annual service for a boiler or car |

### How businesses achieve good service

Training; recruiting the right people; enough staff at busy times; empowering staff to solve problems; collecting and acting on feedback.

### The two chains

- Good service: satisfaction → loyalty → increased spend → profitability.
- Poor service: dissatisfied customers → poor reputation via word of mouth → reduction in revenue.

### ICT and customer service

| | Improvement | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| Websites | Information, FAQs and order tracking at any time | Out-of-date or confusing information loses sales |
| E-commerce | Buy anywhere, any time; preferences remembered | Expectations of fast delivery and easy returns |
| Social media | Fast two-way contact; public replies | Complaints and poor replies seen by many |

### When good service matters most

- Expensive or complex products: post-sales support reassures buyers.
- Markets with many similar rivals: service is the difference.
- Businesses relying on repeat customers: loyalty drives revenue.

### Quick worked reminder

Figures invented. A garage has 900 customers who each spend £300 a year on servicing. Losing 5% through poor service means 45 customers × £300 = £13,500 of revenue lost each year.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Customer service | How a business looks after customers before, during and after a sale |
| Sales process | The stages a customer goes through when buying, and the business's role at each |
| Product knowledge | Staff understanding of products' features, uses and differences |
| Customer engagement | Creating a positive experience for the customer |
| Post-sales service | Support given after the purchase |
| Customer satisfaction | How well a business meets customers' expectations |
| Customer loyalty | Customers choosing to return to the same business |
| Word of mouth | Customers telling others about their experiences |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling online |
| Social media | Online platforms where users share content and interact |

## Exam traps

- **"Good service is being polite."** Name specific methods from the specification.
- **Forgetting after-sales.** Post-sales services are one of the three methods.
- **Benefits as a list.** Show how satisfaction leads to loyalty, spend and profit.
- **ICT as all positive.** Social media spreads complaints quickly.
- **Ignoring cost.** Training, extra staff and help lines cost money.
- **No context.** Choose methods that suit the product: servicing for cars, not for sandwiches.

## Self-test

1. Name the three methods of good service in the specification.
2. Give two examples of post-sales services.
3. What does the specification say customer engagement means?
4. State the four benefits of good customer service in the specification.
5. State the three dangers of poor customer service.
6. Why does product knowledge reduce returns?
7. Name the three ICT developments in the specification.
8. Give one way social media can improve customer service and one risk.
9. Why are post-sales services especially important for expensive, complex products?
10. A gym has 1,200 members paying £35 a month. Poor service causes 50 to leave. Calculate the lost revenue per month.
11. Give two ways a business can improve the product knowledge of its staff.
12. Why might good customer service increase profit even though it costs money?

### Answers

1. Product knowledge, customer engagement, post-sales services.
2. Any two: user training, help lines, servicing.
3. Creating a positive experience for the customer.
4. Increase in customer satisfaction, customer loyalty, increased spend, profitability.
5. Dissatisfied customers, poor reputation via word of mouth, reduction in revenue.
6. Staff help customers choose the right product first time, so fewer are unhappy with what they bought.
7. Websites, e-commerce, social media.
8. Improvement: quick two-way contact and public answers. Risk: complaints and poor replies are seen by many people.
9. Customers worry about using them and what happens if they go wrong, so support reassures them and encourages the purchase.
10. 50 × £35 = £1,750 a month.
11. Any two: training sessions, product guides, letting staff try products, briefings on new products.
12. Loyal customers return and spend more, and recommend the business, so revenue rises by more than the cost, without as much spending on advertising.
