Condensed recall for **3.2.3 Energy resources** in OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203), for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Energy resources study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-resources-oxfordaqa-igcse/); exam-style questions are in the [Energy resources practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-resources-oxfordaqa-igcse-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fuel | A store of energy that can be released, usually by burning |
| Energy content | Energy released per kilogram of fuel, often in MJ/kg |
| Renewable resource | One that is replenished as it is used and will not run out |
| Non-renewable resource | One being used up faster than it can be replaced: coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear fuel |
| Fossil fuel | Coal, oil or natural gas, formed from ancient remains of living things |
| Greenhouse gas | A gas such as carbon dioxide that traps heat in the atmosphere, linked to global warming |
| Acid rain | Rain made acidic by gases such as sulfur dioxide from burning fuels, especially coal |
| Reliability | Whether a source can supply energy whenever it is needed |
| Geothermal energy | Energy from hot rocks beneath the Earth's surface |

## Condensed recall

**Choosing a fuel (a).** Consider ease of storage, energy content and safety, plus any other factors the question gives (cost, availability, waste).

**Fuel efficiency (b).**
- Energy released = mass × energy content.
- Useful energy = efficiency × energy released.
- The rest heats the surroundings.
- A more efficient heater or engine gives more useful energy from the same fuel.

**Sources and society (c).** Judge each source on renewability and on environmental impacts of **extraction, use and disposal**.

**Renewable technologies (d).** Wave, solar, geothermal named in the specification; also wind, hydroelectric, tidal, biofuel.

## Non-renewable sources at a glance

| Source | Main advantage | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | Reliable; large output on demand | Carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide; mining damage |
| Oil | High energy content; easy to transport as a liquid | Carbon dioxide; spills harm wildlife |
| Natural gas | Quick start-up; less carbon dioxide than coal for the same energy | Still releases carbon dioxide |
| Nuclear | No carbon dioxide during generation; reliable | Radioactive waste stored for a very long time |

## Renewable sources at a glance

| Source | Main advantage | Main drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Wave | No fuel; no carbon dioxide in operation | Unreliable; coastal habitats |
| Solar | No fuel; good for remote sites | None at night; less in cloud |
| Geothermal | Reliable, continuous | Only where hot rocks are near the surface |
| Wind | No fuel; no carbon dioxide in operation | None in calm weather; noise and visual impact |
| Hydroelectric | Reliable; fast start-up | Flooding destroys habitats |
| Tidal | Predictable | Few sites; estuary habitats |
| Biofuel | Renewable if replanted | Needs land that could grow food |

## Evaluation frame

1. Advantage linked to the context.
2. Drawback linked to the context.
3. Comparison with an alternative.
4. Conclusion with a reason.

## Fuel calculations in two lines

```
energy released = mass of fuel x energy content
useful energy = efficiency x energy released
```

Worked check (invented figures): 1.5 kg of a fuel with energy content 42 MJ/kg releases 63 MJ. In a boiler of efficiency 0.75, the water gains 47.25 MJ, about 47 MJ; the other 15.75 MJ heats the surroundings.

To compare two fuels for the same job, find the mass of each needed for the same energy: mass = energy needed ÷ energy content. The fuel with the higher energy content needs less mass.

## Extraction, use and disposal: one example each

| Stage | Fossil fuels | Nuclear fuel | Renewables |
|---|---|---|---|
| Extraction | Mining or drilling damages land; oil spills | Uranium mining | Building dams, turbines or panels uses land and materials |
| Use | Carbon dioxide; sulfur dioxide from coal | No carbon dioxide during generation | No fuel burned; habitats near the site may be affected |
| Disposal | Ash and waste gases | Long-lived radioactive waste | Old panels and turbine blades must be dealt with at the end of their life |

## Exam traps

- **Nuclear is non-renewable.** Its fuel (uranium) is mined and will run out.
- **Renewable is not the same as harmless.** Building dams or turbines still affects the environment.
- **Reliability matters.** Wind, wave and solar vary; geothermal, hydroelectric and fossil fuels can supply steadily.
- **Be specific.** "Bad for the environment" earns little; name the gas or the habitat affected.
- **Use the data.** If a table is given, quote figures in your answer.
- **Energy content versus total energy.** Multiply by the mass to get the total.

## Self-test

**1.** Name the three fuel-selection factors in the specification.

**2.** Why is a high energy content important for an aircraft fuel?

**3.** A fuel has an energy content of 30 MJ/kg. Find the energy released by 2.5 kg.

**4.** A boiler is 0.90 efficient and the fuel releases 50 MJ. How much energy heats the water?

**5.** Name two non-renewable energy resources.

**6.** Give one environmental impact of the disposal stage of nuclear power.

**7.** Which gas from burning coal contributes to acid rain?

**8.** Give one advantage and one drawback of geothermal power.

**9.** Why is solar power not reliable on its own?

**10.** Give one environmental drawback of a hydroelectric dam.

**11.** Which renewable source is the most predictable: wind, wave or tidal?

**12.** Why is biofuel described as renewable?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Ease of storage, energy content and safety.

**2.** The aircraft must carry its fuel, so more energy per kilogram means less mass to lift for the same journey.

**3.** 2.5 × 30 = 75 MJ.

**4.** 0.90 × 50 = 45 MJ.

**5.** Any two: coal, oil, natural gas, nuclear fuel.

**6.** Radioactive waste must be stored safely for a very long time.

**7.** Sulfur dioxide.

**8.** Advantage: reliable, continuous supply (no fuel needed). Drawback: only possible where hot rocks are near the surface.

**9.** It produces no electricity at night and less on cloudy days.

**10.** Flooding the valley destroys habitats and land.

**11.** Tidal.

**12.** The crops can be regrown, replacing what is used.
