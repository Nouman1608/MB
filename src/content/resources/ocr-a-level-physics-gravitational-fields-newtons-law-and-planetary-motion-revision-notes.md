## Definitions and key facts

| Term | Meaning (statement) |
|---|---|
| Gravitational field | Region where a mass experiences a force due to other mass (5.4.1(a)) |
| Gravitational field strength, g | Force each kilogram of a small test mass feels, g = F/m, in N kg⁻¹; a vector pointing to the source (5.4.1(d)) |
| Point-mass model | A sphere acts as if all its mass is at its centre (5.4.1(b)) |
| Field line | Gives the direction a small mass is pulled; closer lines mean a stronger field (5.4.1(c)) |
| Uniform field | Parallel, equally spaced lines; a good model close to the Earth's surface (5.4.2(c)) |
| Geostationary orbit | Stays above one point on the equator, period 24 h (5.4.3(e)) |

## Formulae

| Relationship | Notes |
|---|---|
| g = F/m | N kg⁻¹ is equivalent to m s⁻² |
| F = −GMm/r² | Negative sign: force on m is towards M. Use the magnitude and state the direction |
| g = −GM/r² | Depends on M and r only, not on the test mass |
| v = 2πr/T | Circular orbit speed from period |
| T² = (4π²/GM) r³ | Gradient of T² against r³ is 4π²/GM |
| T₂ = T₁ (r₂/r₁)^(3/2) | Same central body only |
| v₁r₁ = v₂r₂ | Only at the two points where velocity is perpendicular to the line to the centre |

## Field lines

- Single sphere: radial lines pointing inwards, closer together near the surface.
- Near the surface: parallel, equally spaced, vertical lines.
- Lines never cross. Arrows point towards the mass.
- Link to section 6.2: electric field strength is force per unit charge, E = F/Q. Both fields are radial for a point source and weaken with distance. Gravity only attracts. Electric forces can attract or repel.

## Method boxes

**Force or field strength from Newton's law**

1. Find r from the centre of the body, not the surface. Add the planet radius to any height.
2. Convert to metres and kilograms.
3. Substitute into GMm/r² (force) or GM/r² (field strength).
4. Give the answer with units and direction (towards the centre).

**Deriving T² = (4π²/GM) r³**

1. Gravitational force provides the centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r.
2. Cancel m and one r: GM/r = v².
3. Substitute v = 2πr/T: GM/r = 4π²r²/T².
4. Rearrange: T² = (4π²/GM) r³.

**Kepler's three laws**

1. Orbits are ellipses with the Sun at one focus.
2. The line from the Sun to a planet sweeps equal areas in equal times.
3. T² ∝ r³.

**Geostationary orbit: conditions and radius**

1. Above the equator, moving west to east, circular, period 24 h.
2. Set T = 86 400 s in r³ = GMT²/4π².
3. For the Earth, r ≈ 4.22 × 10⁷ m, which is about 3.59 × 10⁷ m above the surface.

## Small worked reminders

Triton has mass 2.14 × 10²² kg and radius 1.35 × 10⁶ m. A probe is 1.5 × 10⁶ m above its surface.

```
r = 1.35e6 + 1.5e6 = 2.85 × 10⁶ m
g = GM/r² = 6.67e-11 × 2.14e22 / (2.85e6)² = 0.176 N kg⁻¹
Surface value: 6.67e-11 × 2.14e22 / (1.35e6)² = 0.783 N kg⁻¹
```

The distance from the centre is 2.11 times the surface radius, so g drops by 2.11² = 4.46 times: 0.783 / 4.46 = 0.176.

A moon orbits a planet at radius 1.8 × 10⁸ m with period 1.1 days. Find the planet's mass.

```
T = 1.1 × 86 400 = 9.50e4 s
M = 4π²r³ / (G T²) = 4π² × (1.8e8)³ / (6.67e-11 × (9.50e4)²)
  = 3.8 × 10²⁶ kg
```

Check the steps in this order: convert the period to seconds, cube the radius, square the period, then divide. The moon's own mass is not needed, and a graph of T² against r³ for several moons would give a straight line through the origin.

## Must-know distinctions

- **g and G.** g is field strength at a place and varies with r. G is a universal constant.
- **r and h.** r is distance from the centre. h is height above the surface. For a satellite, r = R + h.
- **Force and field strength.** Force F depends on both masses and is measured in N. Field strength g depends only on the source mass and distance, and is measured in N kg⁻¹.
- **Kepler's second and third laws.** The second compares speeds at different points on one orbit. The third compares whole orbits of different bodies around the same central mass.
- **Mass of the orbiting body and mass of the central body.** Only the central body appears in T² = (4π²/GM) r³.
- **Geostationary and geosynchronous.** Geostationary also needs an equatorial orbit so that the satellite stays above one point.
- **Uses of geostationary satellites.** Television and telephone communications and weather observation of a fixed region. The disadvantages are a signal delay of about 0.24 s and poor coverage near the poles.

## Quick self-test

1. Define gravitational field strength.
2. Two masses of 1500 kg and 800 kg are 20 m apart. Calculate the force between them, using G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻².
3. A moon has mass 1.35 × 10²³ kg and radius 2.57 × 10⁶ m. Calculate g at its surface.
4. The surface field strength of a planet is 12.0 N kg⁻¹. State g at a distance of three radii from its centre.
5. A satellite orbits the Earth at radius 1.0 × 10⁷ m. Calculate its period (Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg).
6. A planet orbits at four times the radius of another planet around the same star. State the ratio of the periods.
7. State why the mass of a satellite does not appear in the formula for its orbital period.
8. A comet is 1.5 × 10¹¹ m from the Sun at speed 3.6 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹. Calculate its speed at 4.5 × 10¹¹ m, at the other end of its orbit.
9. A graph of T² against r³ for moons of a planet has gradient 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ s² m⁻³. Find the planet's mass.
10. State three conditions a satellite must meet to be geostationary.

### Answers

1. The force per unit mass experienced by a small test mass at that point; g = F/m.
2. F = GMm/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1500 × 800 / 20² = **2.0 × 10⁻⁷ N**.
3. g = GM/R² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.35 × 10²³ / (2.57 × 10⁶)² = **1.36 N kg⁻¹**.
4. g ∝ 1/r², so g = 12.0 / 3² = **1.33 N kg⁻¹**.
5. T = 2π √(r³/GM) = 2π √((1.0e7)³ / (6.67e-11 × 5.97e24)) = **9.96 × 10³ s** (about 2.8 hours).
6. T ∝ r^(3/2), so the ratio is 4^1.5 = **8**: the outer planet's period is 8 times longer.
7. The satellite's mass appears in both the gravitational force and the centripetal force expression, so it cancels.
8. v₂ = v₁r₁/r₂ = 3.6e4 × 1.5e11 / 4.5e11 = **1.2 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹**.
9. Gradient = 4π²/GM, so M = 4π² / (G × gradient) = 39.5 / (6.67e-11 × 1.0e-14) = **5.9 × 10²⁵ kg**.
10. Orbit above the equator, move in the same direction as the Earth rotates, and have a period of 24 hours. It must also orbit at the radius that gives this period.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the height above the planet into GM/r² where the distance from its centre belongs.
- Squaring the coefficient but not the power of ten in r².
- Writing the minus sign in F = −GMm/r² in an answer and then giving the magnitude as negative with no direction.
- Giving the period in days when the formula needs seconds.
- A derivation that does not state that gravity provides the centripetal force, or that does not show where v = 2πr/T comes from.
- Saying a geostationary satellite is "stationary", or giving a polar orbit as geostationary.
- Cancelling the central mass instead of the satellite mass.
- Drawing field lines that cross, or without arrows, or equally spaced for a point mass.
- Unit slips: N kg⁻¹ for field strength, N for force.
