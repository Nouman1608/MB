Aids to trade are the support services without which commerce would struggle: advertising tells buyers
about goods, banks handle payments and lend money, insurers protect against loss, and finance pays for
the business itself. In Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100, 2026-2028) Topic 5 has four sections - 5.1
Advertising, 5.2 Banking, 5.3 Insurance and 5.4 Sources of finance and commercial performance - and it
contains most of the syllabus's calculations.

For full explanations use the
[aids to trade study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-commerce-aids-to-trade-that-support-commerce-study-guide/);
practise with the [aids to trade practice questions](/resources/o-level-commerce-aids-to-trade-practice/).

## 5.1 Advertising: why, what, where, how

- **Why (purposes)**: to inform customers, persuade them to buy, launch new products, build a brand and
  increase sales.
- **What (types)**: informative; persuasive; collective/generic (a whole industry advertises together);
  competitive (compares with rivals). Informative adverts help customers decide but can be dull;
  persuasive adverts raise sales but can mislead; collective adverts share cost but do not promote one
  brand; competitive adverts win customers but can provoke rivals.
- **Where (media)**: traditional - TV, radio, cinema, newspapers, magazines and trade journals, leaflets,
  packaging, direct mail, sponsorship, catalogues, posters, transport vehicles, window and point of sale
  displays, signboards, word of mouth. Digital - social media and internet advertising (websites, search
  engines), display and video adverts, email marketing, digital billboards, influencers.
- **How (appeals)**: emotion, fear, humour, rational, ambition, social acceptance, romantic, adventure,
  music, celebrity and influencer endorsement.

**Choosing media - match the medium to the situation**

| Business | Sensible media | Factor used |
|---|---|---|
| Village bakery | Leaflets, window display, word of mouth | Local coverage, low cost |
| Machine-tool maker | Trade journals, trade websites | Specialist audience |
| International sportswear brand | TV, social media, influencers, digital billboards | International coverage, multi-channel |
| Seasonal ice-cream kiosk | Signboards, social media posts in summer | Timing, flexibility to change |

**Sales promotion** = short-term incentives to increase sales, chosen according to business type,
competitors, cost, nature of product, whether the product is new or old, and timing. Methods: point of
sale, special offers, sponsorship, displays, competitions, coupons, sales periods, loss leaders (sold below
cost to attract customers), free gifts, free samples.

## 5.2 Banking: services for every need

| Need | Service |
|---|---|
| Keep money safe and make daily payments | Current account |
| Earn interest on spare cash | Deposit/savings account |
| Deposit takings after closing time | Night safe |
| Pay for an overseas trip or import | Foreign currency; documentary credit |
| Pay rent of the same amount monthly | Standing order |
| Pay a bill that varies each month | Direct debit |
| Pay a supplier directly from the account | Credit transfer or electronic transfer |
| Buy now, pay later | Credit card; store card |
| Short-term shortage of cash | Overdraft |
| Buy premises | Mortgage |
| Buy equipment in instalments | Hire purchase; loan |
| Get advice | Financial advice; telephone banking |

**Digital banking**: internet and mobile banking. For - convenient, 24/7, fast and cheap. Against - fraud
and hacking risk, needs devices and network, can exclude some customers. Impact - branch closures, more
digital-only banks, less use of cash.

## 5.3 Insurance: how it works

1. Business completes a **proposal form**, disclosing all relevant facts.
2. Insurer may issue a **cover note** for temporary cover.
3. The **policy** sets out the full contract.
4. The insurer **pools risk**: premiums from many cover the losses of a few.
5. An **insurance broker** can advise on and arrange the best cover.

**Purposes**: risk reduction, compensation, financial protection, business confidence, investment.
**Risks**: consequential loss, employer's liability, fire, motor, premises, public liability, theft.

**Premium example.** A van worth $30 000 is insured at 4% a year. Premium = 30 000 x 4 / 100 = **$1 200**.

## 5.4 Finance and commercial performance

- **Entrepreneurship**: identifying an opportunity and taking the risk of starting a business.
- **Short-term**: trade credit, debt factoring, hire purchase, leasing, sale and leaseback, overdraft,
  selling assets, owner's savings, retained profit.
- **Long-term**: ordinary and preference shares, loans, debentures, venture capital, crowdfunding.

**Calculations - worked from one set of figures**

A shop sells 1 200 lamps at $40. Each lamp cost $28. Expenses are $9 000. Current assets $18 000; current
liabilities $11 000.

| Measure | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 1 200 x 40 | $48 000 |
| Gross profit | 48 000 - (1 200 x 28 = 33 600) | $14 400 |
| Gross profit margin | 14 400 / 48 000 x 100 | 30% |
| Profit for the year | 14 400 - 9 000 | $5 400 |
| Working capital | 18 000 - 11 000 | $7 000 |
| Mark-up | (40 - 28) / 28 x 100 | 42.9% |

**Improving performance**: cut expenses, raise mark-up, change prices, advertise better, launch new
products.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Using revenue instead of cost as the base for mark-up.
- Saying an overdraft is long-term finance.
- Confusing a cover note with a proposal form.
- Recommending a medium without linking it to the audience or cost in the scenario.
- Calling a loss leader a free gift - it is sold, but below cost.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between ordinary and preference shares?** Preference shareholders receive a fixed
dividend before ordinary shareholders; ordinary shareholders usually have voting rights and variable
dividends.

**Why might a business choose leasing instead of buying?** Leasing avoids a large upfront payment, although
the business does not own the asset and may pay more overall.

**What does "working capital" measure?** Current assets minus current liabilities: the money available to
pay short-term debts and run the business day to day.
