These questions cover section E9.7, Histograms, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus lists C9.7 as "Extended content only", so every question here is **(Extended)** and suits Papers 2 and 4. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Histograms are described by their bars (class and height) so you can sketch them. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured ones.

Revise first with the [histograms study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-histograms/) and the [histograms revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-histograms-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The class 30 < x ≤ 50 has frequency 26. Find its frequency density. **[1]**

**2.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A histogram bar for the class 75 < x ≤ 100 has frequency density 0.4. Find the frequency of the class. **[1]**

**3.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write down the class widths of 0 < t ≤ 2.5 and 2.5 < t ≤ 4. **[1]**

**4.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The table shows the lengths of some leaves.

```
Length l (cm)   0 < l ≤ 4   4 < l ≤ 6   6 < l ≤ 12
Frequency           10          9          24
```

**(a)** Find the frequency density of each class. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**

**5.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Which label should be on the vertical axis of a histogram with unequal class widths?

A: Frequency

B: Cumulative frequency

C: Frequency density

D: Class width

**[1]**

**6.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A histogram bar for 10 < x ≤ 30 has frequency density 2.5. Estimate the number of values between 10 and 16. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** (Extended) (calculator) The ages of 80 people at a concert are shown.

```
Age a (years)   10 < a ≤ 15   15 < a ≤ 20   20 < a ≤ 30   30 < a ≤ 50   50 < a ≤ 70
Frequency            8            22            26            18             6
```

**(a)** Calculate the frequency density for each class, ready to draw a histogram. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**

**(c)** Estimate the number of people aged over 40. **[2]**

**8.** (Extended) (calculator) A histogram shows the times, t minutes, taken to complete a task. The bars are:

```
Class         0 < t ≤ 10   10 < t ≤ 15   15 < t ≤ 20   20 < t ≤ 30   30 < t ≤ 50
Bar height        1.5           4.8            6            2.2           0.45
```

**(a)** Find the frequency of each class. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the total number of people. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate an estimate of the mean time. **[3]**

**9.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A histogram of the masses, m kg, of some sacks has no numbers on its vertical axis. The bar for 20 < m ≤ 30 represents 35 sacks and is 7 cm tall. The other bars are:

```
0 < m ≤ 20:   2 cm tall
30 < m ≤ 35:  8 cm tall
35 < m ≤ 50:  4 cm tall
```

**(a)** Find the frequency density shown by 1 cm of height. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the number of sacks in each of the other three classes. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) (non-calculator) The table and histogram show the same data, but each is incomplete.

```
Class          Frequency   Bar height (frequency density)
0 < x ≤ 4          12             ?
4 < x ≤ 6           ?             8
6 < x ≤ 10         20             ?
10 < x ≤ 20         ?             0.9
```

**(a)** Find the two missing bar heights. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the two missing frequencies. **[2]**

**11.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Zara draws a diagram for the data in question 7 with "Frequency" on the vertical axis. Each bar's height is the class frequency, and the bars touch.

**(a)** Explain why her diagram is misleading. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the classes 20 < a ≤ 30 and 30 < a ≤ 50 to illustrate your answer. **[1]**

**12.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A histogram has bars for 20 < x ≤ 30 with height 3 and 30 < x ≤ 40 with height 1.2.

**(a)** Estimate the number of values greater than 25 and not more than 40. **[2]**

**(b)** Leila says "the bar for 30 < x ≤ 40 shows 1.2 values". Explain her mistake. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Width 20: 26 ÷ 20 = **1.3** [1]

**2.** Width 25: 0.4 × 25 = **10** [1]

**3.** **2.5** and **1.5** [1]

**4. (a)** 10 ÷ 4 = **2.5**, 9 ÷ 2 = **4.5**, 24 ÷ 6 = **4** [1]

**(b)** **4 < l ≤ 6** (highest frequency density) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 6 < l ≤ 12 has the highest frequency, but it is three times as wide; the modal class is the tallest bar.

**5.** **C** [1]

**6.** The part from 10 to 16 has width 6 [1]; 2.5 × 6 = **15** [1]

**7. (a)** 8 ÷ 5 = **1.6** and 22 ÷ 5 = **4.4** [1]; 26 ÷ 10 = **2.6** [1]; 18 ÷ 20 = **0.9** and 6 ÷ 20 = **0.3** [1]

**(b)** **15 < a ≤ 20** [1]

**(c)** From 40 to 50 is half of the class 30 < a ≤ 50: 0.9 × 10 = 9 [1]; 9 + 6 = **15** people [1] **[6]**

**8. (a)** 1.5 × 10 = **15** and 4.8 × 5 = **24** [1]; 6 × 5 = **30** and 2.2 × 10 = **22** [1]; 0.45 × 20 = **9** [1]

**(b)** 15 + 24 + 30 + 22 + 9 = **100** [1]

**(c)** Midpoints 5, 12.5, 17.5, 25, 40 [1]; Σf × midpoint = 75 + 300 + 525 + 550 + 360 = 1810 [1]; 1810 ÷ 100 = **18.1 minutes** [1] **[7]**

**9. (a)** Density of the known bar = 35 ÷ 10 = 3.5, shown by 7 cm, so 1 cm = **0.5** [1]

**(b)** 0 < m ≤ 20: 2 × 0.5 = 1, 1 × 20 = **20** [1]; 30 < m ≤ 35: 8 × 0.5 = 4, 4 × 5 = **20** [1]; 35 < m ≤ 50: 4 × 0.5 = 2, 2 × 15 = **30** [1] **[4]**

**10. (a)** 12 ÷ 4 = **3** [1]; 20 ÷ 4 = **5** [1]

**(b)** 8 × 2 = **16** [1]; 0.9 × 10 = **9** [1] **[4]**

**11. (a)** The classes have **different widths**, so bar height (or area) does not show how crowded each class is; wide classes look too important [1]

**(b)** On Zara's diagram the 30 < a ≤ 50 bar (height 18, width 20) covers more area than the 20 < a ≤ 30 bar (height 26, width 10), suggesting more people, although it has fewer (18 against 26) and its frequency density (0.9) is about a third of 2.6 [1] **[2]**

**12. (a)** 25 to 30: 3 × 5 = 15 [1]; 30 to 40: 1.2 × 10 = 12; total **27** [1]

**(b)** 1.2 is the **frequency density**; the frequency is 1.2 × 10 = **12** [1] **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Plotting frequency instead of frequency density.
- Wrong class widths, especially for classes such as 30 < a ≤ 50.
- Giving the class with the highest frequency as the modal class.
- Reading a bar's height as its frequency.
- Splitting a class by a fraction of its height instead of the area of the part needed.
