Use ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F m⁻¹. All data are invented for practice.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define capacitance and state its SI unit. **[2]**

**2.** Which change doubles the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor?

A: doubling the plate separation

B: doubling both the plate area and the plate separation

C: halving the plate separation

D: replacing the dielectric with one of half the relative permittivity

**[1]**

**3.** A 47 μF capacitor is charged to 12 V. Calculate the charge on each plate and the energy stored. **[3]**

**4.** A capacitor has a capacitance of 220 pF with air between its plates and 990 pF when a slab of plastic fills the gap.

**(a)** Calculate the relative permittivity of the plastic. **[1]**

**(b)** State what is meant by the dielectric constant of the plastic. **[1]**

**5.** A parallel plate capacitor has rectangular plates 0.15 m by 0.10 m, separated by a 0.50 mm sheet of material of relative permittivity 3.0. Calculate its capacitance. **[2]**

**6.** A capacitor has 3.0 mC on each plate and a capacitance of 50 μF. Calculate the energy stored. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Describe and explain how a dielectric made of polar molecules increases the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor. **[5]**

**8.** Explain, with reference to a graph of charge against p.d., why the energy stored in a capacitor is ½QV. **[3]**

**9.** A 470 μF capacitor is charged from 5.0 V to 15 V.

**(a)** Calculate the energy added. **[2]**

**(b)** A student calculates ½ × 470 × 10⁻⁶ × 10² and gets 0.024 J. Explain the error. **[1]**

**10.** A 9.0 V battery is used to charge an uncharged 100 μF capacitor fully through a resistor.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the battery. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy stored in the capacitor. **[1]**

**(c)** Account for the difference. **[1]**

**11.** An air-filled parallel plate capacitor is charged and then disconnected from the supply. Its plates are then pulled apart until the separation is three times as large.

**(a)** State and explain what happens to the charge, the capacitance and the p.d. **[3]**

**(b)** State the factor by which the stored energy changes, and explain where the energy comes from. **[2]**

**12.** A student charges a capacitor to different p.d.s and records the charge.

| V / V | 2.0 | 4.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q / μC | 66 | 132 | 198 | 264 |

**(a)** Use the data to find the capacitance. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy stored at 8.0 V. **[2]**

**13.** A capacitor stays connected to a 50 V supply while a dielectric of relative permittivity 2.5 is slid in to fill the gap. Its capacitance was 400 pF before. Calculate the charge that flows from the supply as the dielectric is inserted. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Charge stored per unit p.d. across the capacitor, C = Q/V [1]; the **farad** (F), equal to one coulomb per volt [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Q = CV = 47 × 10⁻⁶ × 12 [1] = **5.6 × 10⁻⁴ C** [1]; E = ½CV² = ½ × 47 × 10⁻⁶ × 12² = **3.4 × 10⁻³ J** [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** εr = 990/220 = **4.5** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The factor by which the capacitance increases when the plastic fills the gap, compared with a vacuum (its relative permittivity) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** A = 0.15 × 0.10 = 0.015 m² [1]; C = 0.015 × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 3.0 / 0.50 × 10⁻³ = **8.0 × 10⁻¹⁰ F** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** E = ½Q²/C = (3.0 × 10⁻³)² / (2 × 50 × 10⁻⁶) [1] = **0.090 J** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Polar molecules have permanently charged ends and are randomly oriented with no field [1]. The field exerts equal and opposite forces on the ends, forming a couple that rotates each molecule [1]. The molecules align, so negative ends face the positive plate and positive ends face the negative plate, leaving layers of charge at the surfaces [1]. These produce a field opposing the applied field, reducing the resultant field between the plates [1]. For the same charge the p.d. is smaller, so C = Q/V is larger [1]. **[5]**

**8.** Adding a small charge ΔQ at p.d. V requires work VΔQ, the area of a thin strip on the graph [1]. The p.d. rises in proportion to Q, from 0 to V, so the strips build up a triangle [1]. The total area, and so the energy, is ½ × Q × V [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** ΔE = ½C(V₂² − V₁²) = ½ × 470 × 10⁻⁶ × (15² − 5.0²) [1] = **0.047 J** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Energy depends on V², so the energy added is the difference of the two ½CV² values, not ½C(ΔV)², which assumes the capacitor started uncharged [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** Q = CV = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ C [1]; work = QV = 9.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.0 = **8.1 × 10⁻³ J** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** ½QV = **4.1 × 10⁻³ J** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** Half the work done by the battery is dissipated as thermal energy in the resistance of the circuit while the capacitor charges [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** The charge stays the same, because the capacitor is isolated and charge has nowhere to go [1]. C ∝ 1/d, so the capacitance falls to one third [1]. V = Q/C, so the p.d. triples [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Energy ½Q²/C **triples** [1]; the extra energy comes from the work done pulling the plates apart against their mutual attraction [1]. **[2]**

**12. (a)** Q/V is constant: 66 μC / 2.0 V [1] = **33 μF** (gradient of the Q–V graph) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** E = ½QV = ½ × 264 × 10⁻⁶ × 8.0 [1] = **1.1 × 10⁻³ J** [1]. **[2]**

**13.** New capacitance = 2.5 × 400 = 1000 pF [1]. Charge rises from 400 × 10⁻¹² × 50 = 2.0 × 10⁻⁸ C to 1000 × 10⁻¹² × 50 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁸ C [1]. Charge from the supply = **3.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving capacitance in μF or pF, or separation in mm, when substituting.
- Using ½C(ΔV)² for a change of p.d.
- Describing dielectric action without the couple, the rotation or the opposing field.
- Assuming the charge is constant when the capacitor is still connected, or the p.d. constant when it is isolated.
- Stating that energy "triples" without giving where the energy comes from.
