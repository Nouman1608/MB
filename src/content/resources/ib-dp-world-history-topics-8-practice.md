## Section A

**1.** Identify **two** methods used by the Indian National Congress between 1920 and 1942. **[2]**

**2.** State the dates on which India and Ghana became independent. **[2]**

**3.** Outline **three** reasons why France fought to keep Algeria. **[3]**

**4.** Define "satyagraha". **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** ways in which the Second World War weakened colonial rule in Asia. **[2]**

**6.** Outline **three** challenges faced by Algeria in the years immediately after independence. **[3]**

---

## Section B

**7.** **(a)** Explain why the Indian independence movement gained mass support after 1919. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain **two** divisions within the Indian independence movement before 1947. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Explain how Nkrumah's Convention People's Party achieved independence for the Gold Coast. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why Nkrumah's government became increasingly authoritarian after 1957. **[4]**

**9.** Explain the importance of Napoleon's invasion of Spain in 1808 for independence movements in Spanish America. **[4]**

**10.** Compare and contrast the methods used by the Indian and Algerian independence movements. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate the success of **one** newly independent government in responding to the challenges it faced in its first ten years. **[8]**

**12.** "External factors were more important than internal factors in the success of independence movements." With reference to **two** independence movements, each chosen from a different region, to what extent do you agree with this statement? **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: non-cooperation, including boycotts of British goods, courts and schools (1920-22); civil disobedience such as the Salt March (1930); contesting elections under the 1935 Act and forming provincial ministries (1937); the Quit India campaign (1942); negotiation with the British. **[2]**

**2.** 1 mark each: India, 15 August 1947; Ghana, 6 March 1957. **[2]**

**3.** 1 mark each, any three: Algeria was legally part of France (organized as French departments), not a colony in law; around a million European settlers (*pieds-noirs*) lived there and had political influence in France; after defeat in Indochina (1954), the army and politicians were determined not to lose again; Algeria had economic value, including oil and gas discoveries in the Sahara in the 1950s; national prestige and fear of wider loss of empire. **[3]**

**4.** 1 mark: Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance, meaning "truth force" or "holding to truth". 1 mark: it involved accepting punishment without retaliation in order to expose injustice and change the opponent's mind, for example through civil disobedience. **[2]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: Japanese victories (Singapore, 1942) destroyed European prestige; Japanese occupation removed colonial administrations, as in Indochina and the Dutch East Indies; the war exhausted Britain, France and the Netherlands economically; the Atlantic Charter (1941) spread the language of self-determination; nationalist movements gained arms and organization during the war. **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: the departure of most European settlers removed skilled workers and administrators; economic damage from eight years of war; violence against the *harkis*; rivalry within the FLN leadership; Ben Bella's overthrow by Boumediene in 1965; dependence on French economic links and oil revenue. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** Up to 4 marks for developed explanation: anger after the Amritsar massacre (1919) and disappointment with limited reform (1); Gandhi's leadership and simple, inclusive methods such as spinning and boycotts that ordinary people could join (1); linking Congress to the Khilafat movement brought Muslim support in 1920-22 (1); campaigns on everyday grievances, such as the salt tax in 1930, connected nationalism to peasants' lives (1). **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed division: between Congress and the Muslim League, which demanded Pakistan from 1940; between Gandhi's non-violence and those who favoured armed struggle, such as Subhas Chandra Bose and the Indian National Army; between moderates and radicals, or left and right, within Congress. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** 1 mark each: the CPP (1949) built a mass party with a clear demand for "self-government now"; Positive Action (1950) strikes and boycotts pressured the British; the CPP won the 1951 election, so Britain released Nkrumah to lead the government; further election victories (1954, 1956) and co-operation with the British led to independence in 1957. **[4]**
**(b)** 2 marks for each developed reason: opposition from regional and ethnic movements, especially in Ashanti, led Nkrumah to see opponents as a threat to national unity, so he passed the Preventive Detention Act (1958); his ambitious development plans and falling cocoa prices produced economic strain and discontent; his belief in a single national party culminated in the one-party state (1964). **[4]**

**9.** Up to 4 marks: Napoleon removed the Spanish king and put his brother on the throne (1); this created a crisis of legitimacy, since colonial elites rejected French rule (1); juntas formed in American cities to govern in the king's name, gaining experience of self-rule (1); when Ferdinand VII returned in 1814 and tried to restore control, many creoles moved to full independence under leaders such as Bolívar and San Martín (1). **[4]**

**10.** Up to 6 marks; both similarities and differences are needed. Similarities: both used mass political organization; both combined internal pressure with diplomacy and international opinion; both movements had internal rivals (the Muslim League; the MNA in Algeria). Differences: Congress relied mainly on non-violent mass protest, the FLN on armed struggle, guerrilla warfare and urban terrorism from 1954; India negotiated a transfer of power with a willing Labour government, while Algeria won after a war that brought down the Fourth Republic; the cost in lives in the Algerian war was far greater, though partition brought mass violence in India. The final mark requires a judgement on why methods differed, such as the colonial power's response. **[6]**

**11.** Up to 8 marks: 2 marks for criteria for success (for example stability, unity, economic growth, democracy); up to 4 marks for developed evaluation of at least two challenges and responses; 2 marks for a supported overall judgement. *India as an example:* partition violence and refugees were managed only with great loss of life; the princely states were mostly integrated by 1949, though Kashmir remained contested; the constitution (1950) and elections (1951-52) established a durable democracy; linguistic demands were met by states reorganization (1956); Five-Year Plans brought industrial growth but limited progress on rural poverty. Judgement: largely successful in political terms, less so in economic terms. **[8]**

**12.** Marked holistically using the 15-mark markbands; the indicative content is a guide, not a checklist. *India and Algeria, for example.* External factors: the Second World War's effect on British power, US pressure and the Atlantic Charter; for Algeria, French defeat in Indochina, Cold War diplomacy, UN debates and support from Arab states and the non-aligned movement. Internal factors: Congress's mass organization and Gandhi's leadership; FLN organization, armed struggle and the political effect of the war in France. The colonial power's own politics, such as Labour's victory in 1945 and de Gaulle's change of policy, can be treated as a link between external and internal. A strong answer defines "success", treats both cases in each paragraph, and judges, for example, that external factors determined the timing of independence while internal movements determined that it happened and on whose terms. **[15]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Can I use a Latin American movement from the early 1800s?**
Yes, if you studied it: the topic runs from 1800, and the Americas is a separate IB region.

**Must I write about the period after independence?**
Only when the question asks for it, but revise it, because questions on post-independence challenges are a regular part of this topic.
