## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between share capital and loan capital. **[2]**

**2.** State the sub-heading of the statement of financial position in which each item appears. **[5]**

(i) bank overdraft; (ii) 7% loan repayable in 2031; (iii) retained earnings; (iv) insurance paid in advance; (v) the net book value of plant.

**3.** At 31 December 2025 a company's loan of 30,000 was repayable on 31 October 2027. At 31 December 2026 it is still unpaid. Explain how its classification changes in the statement of financial position at 31 December 2026. **[3]**

**4.** Brexley Games Ltd has 400,000 ordinary shares. Retained earnings at the start of the year were 62,000 and profit for the year was 48,500. A dividend of 6 cents a share was paid.

**(a)** Calculate the total dividend paid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate retained earnings at the end of the year. **[2]**

**5.** A company has an 8% loan of 25,000 for the whole year. Only 1,000 of interest was paid during the year. State the interest to be shown in the income statement and the amount owing, and say where the amount owing appears in the statement of financial position. **[3]**

**6.** State three items that the specification says will not be examined for limited companies. **[3]**

**7.** Give two advantages and two disadvantages of trading as a limited company rather than as a sole trader. **[4]**

**8.** Two companies sell similar goods. Compare their results.

| | Hartlow Bakeries Ltd | Fenwith Tools Ltd |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 500,000 | 520,000 |
| Gross profit | 200,000 | 208,000 |
| Profit for the year | 60,000 | 31,200 |
| Current assets | 90,000 | 96,000 |
| Current liabilities | 60,000 | 40,000 |
| Loan capital | 0 | 250,000 |
| Equity | 400,000 | 300,000 |

Calculate the gross profit margin, the profit for the year as a percentage of revenue and the current ratio for each company, and comment on the position of each. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Share capital is money put in by the owners (shareholders), who receive a dividend that depends on profit [1]. Loan capital is borrowed from lenders, who are paid fixed interest and repaid on an agreed date [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both sides compared. Describing only shares, or only loans, leaves the contrast unsupported.

**2.** (i) Current liabilities [1]. (ii) Non-current liabilities [1]. (iii) Equity [1]. (iv) Current assets [1]. (v) Non-current assets [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the exact sub-heading names from the specification. A heading such as "fixed assets" or "long-term" may not be accepted in place of the board's wording.

**3.** At 2025 the loan was due after more than one year, so it was a non-current liability [1]. At 31 December 2026 it falls due within the next year (October 2027) [1]. It is therefore moved into current liabilities, which increases current liabilities and reduces non-current liabilities by 30,000 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason as well as the move. The test is the time left until repayment, not the size of the loan.

**4.** **(a)** 400,000 x 0.06 [1] = **24,000** [1].
**(b)** 62,000 + 48,500 [1] - 24,000 = **86,500** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working lines for the roll-forward. Dividends are subtracted from retained earnings, and deducting them from profit for the year in the income statement expenses is a common slip.

**5.** Interest for the year is 8% of 25,000 = **2,000** shown in the income statement [1]. Owing = 2,000 - 1,000 = **1,000** [1]. The amount owing is an accrual in current liabilities [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* State the full-year charge, not only the amount paid. Writing "accrual" without the sub-heading is less complete than naming current liabilities.

**6.** Any three of: statement of changes in equity [1]; preference shares [1]; general reserves [1]; rights issues; bonus issues; revaluation of non-current assets (one mark for each of any three). **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give three separate items. Repeating one item in different words, such as "bonus shares" and "bonus issues", will not add a second point.

**7.** Advantages (any two from): limited liability, so owners cannot lose more than their shares; can raise more capital by selling shares; the business continues if one owner leaves [1] [1]. Disadvantages (any two from): more rules to follow than a sole trader; owners share control with other shareholders; profits are shared as dividends [1] [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write each point as a full statement that compares with a sole trader. A bare word such as "regulation" is easy to lose credit on.

**8.** Gross profit margin: Hartlow 200,000 / 500,000 = 40.0% [1]; Fenwith 208,000 / 520,000 = 40.0%. Profit for the year as a percentage of revenue: Hartlow 60,000 / 500,000 = 12.0%; Fenwith 31,200 / 520,000 = 6.0% [1]. Current ratio: Hartlow 90,000 / 60,000 = 1.5; Fenwith 96,000 / 40,000 = 2.4 [1].
Comments: The two make the same margin on goods sold, but Fenwith keeps half as much of each dollar of revenue as profit, which may be because of its large loan interest [1]. Fenwith is more liquid, and its current assets cover its short-term debts 2.4 times [1]. Fenwith relies on loan capital for 45.5% of its finance (250,000 out of 550,000) while Hartlow has none, so Fenwith carries more risk [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A comment gains credit when it names the figure, compares and gives a reason. Say "may be due to" when the data does not prove the cause.

**10.** Profit for the year would be overstated by 4,000 (59,300 instead of 55,300) [1]. Current liabilities would be understated by 4,000 [1]. Retained earnings, and so equity, would be overstated by 4,000 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the direction and the amount for each effect. An omitted expense raises profit, and the matching liability is missing from the statement of financial position.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing dividends in the income statement expenses instead of in the retained earnings working.
- Treating interest paid as the full charge when some is still owing.
- Showing a loan repayable in several years as a current liability, or leaving a loan due within a year among non-current liabilities.
- Depreciating land, or applying the reducing balance rate to cost instead of net book value.
- Using the opening inventory figure in current assets.
- Omitting accumulated depreciation from the non-current asset table.
- Giving ratios without a comment, or a comment without any figure.
- Putting an item under the wrong sub-heading even though the total is right.
- Using retained earnings at the start of the year in the equity section.
