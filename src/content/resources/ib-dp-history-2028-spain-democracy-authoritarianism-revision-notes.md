**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 10, Democracy, authoritarianism and conflict in Spain (circa 1920 to 2020), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-spain-democracy-authoritarianism/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for in-depth understanding of the argument, a thorough and balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement. Questions may cover one period or link the Civil War, Franco and democracy.

## 1920s and early 1930s

- **Weaknesses:** rigged elections; Church, army and landowners against reform; regional nationalism; strong anarchist and socialist unions.
- **Primo de Rivera:** coup 1923; ended Rif War 1925; public works; alienated officers and Catalans; resigned 1930.
- **Republic:** constitution of 1931; Azaña's reforms on army, Church, land and Catalonia; Sanjurjo coup (1932).
- **Polarization:** right wins 1933; Asturias 1934; Popular Front 1936; centre collapses.

## Causes of the Civil War: immediate

| Factor | Points |
|---|---|
| Election | Popular Front narrow win in February 1936 |
| Policies | Prisoners freed, Catalan autonomy restored, land seizures |
| Violence | Falange against the left; the murder of Calvo Sotelo (13 July) |
| Coup | Mola, Sanjurjo and Franco; rising begins 17 July; fails in major cities |
| Franco | Chosen leader, September to October 1936; party merger in 1937 |

## Why the Nationalists won

- **Foreign aid:** Germany (Condor Legion), Italy (troops and aircraft) against the USSR (arms, advisers) and International Brigades; non-intervention hurt the Republic.
- **Military:** Army of Africa; unified command; Republic's militias; Ebro and Catalonia fell.
- **Economic:** Republic lost the north in 1937; Nationalists held farmland and credit.
- **Individuals:** Franco's discipline; Republican infighting (Barcelona, May 1937).

## Franco's regime

| Area | Points |
|---|---|
| Political | Caudillo; single party; Cortes (1942); Law of Succession (1947) |
| Opposition | Executions, prisons, exile, bans on unions and parties |
| Economic | Autarky and hunger in the 1940s; 1959 Stabilization Plan; growth and tourism |
| Social and cultural | National Catholicism; women's roles restricted; censorship; regional languages suppressed; ETA founded 1959 |

## Transition and democracy

- **Juan Carlos:** named successor in 1969; backed reform after 1975; appointed Suárez in 1976.
- **Individuals:** Suárez (negotiator); Fraga (reform from within); the legalisation of the Communist Party (1977).
- **Steps:** Law for Political Reform (1976); elections (1977); Moncloa Pacts; constitution (1978).
- **External support and social factors:** European and US encouragement; middle class and secularisation; fear of repeating the Civil War.
- **Tests:** ETA violence; 23 February 1981.

## 1982 to 2020

| Parties | González (PSOE, 1982 to 1996); Aznar (PP, 1996 to 2004); Zapatero (PSOE, 2004 to 2011) |
| Economy and society | Growth, property boom, 2008 crisis, 15-M, Podemos and Vox |

## Historians' views

- Civil War: class conflict, Catholic crusade, or a failure of democracy?
- Foreign involvement against internal division as the key to Nationalist victory.
- Transition: a model of peaceful change or an elite deal that avoided justice ("pact of forgetting")?

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was foreign intervention the main reason for Nationalist victory?"

1. **Thesis:** Foreign aid was vital, but Nationalist unity and the Republic's divisions were also decisive.
2. **Paragraph 1, foreign aid:** Germany and Italy against the USSR; non-intervention.
3. **Paragraph 2, unity:** Franco's command and party merger against anarchist and communist conflict.
4. **Paragraph 3, military and economic:** Army of Africa, loss of the north, Ebro.
5. **Perspective:** scholars who stress outside intervention against those who stress internal factors.
6. **Judgement:** foreign aid made victory possible; internal factors determined how.

## Common mistakes

- Treating the Civil War as simply left against right.
- Ignoring the Republic's divisions.
- Giving casualty figures as exact.
- Skipping the post-1982 period when the question covers it.
- Forgetting regional nationalism.

## Self-test

1. Which general led the 1923 coup?
2. What was the Popular Front?
3. Name two foreign sources of aid to the Nationalists.
4. What was the Stabilization Plan of 1959?
5. What did the Law for Political Reform do?
6. When did Spain join the EEC?

**Answers:** 1. Primo de Rivera. 2. The left coalition that won in February 1936. 3. Germany and Italy. 4. A plan to open the economy to trade, tourism and foreign investment. 5. It opened the way to democratic elections. 6. 1 January 1986.
