## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to be an objective of employees? **[1]**

A: Low prices for products

B: Job security

C: Higher tax revenue

D: Prompt payment of invoices

**2.** Define the term stakeholder. **[2]**

**3.** Which **two** of the following are stakeholder groups named in the specification? **[2]**

A: Competitors

B: Pressure groups

C: Suppliers

D: Celebrities

E: Television viewers

**4.** Give **one** objective of the local community as a stakeholder. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** way suppliers can impact the activity of a business. **[3]**

**6.** Explain **one** possible conflict between managers and the local community. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Bellweather Bakery Ltd has 4 shareholders and made a profit of £64,000 last year. It shares out 75% of its profit equally between the shareholders.

**(a)** Calculate the amount each shareholder receives. **[2]**

**(b)** The bakery gives its 16 employees a pay rise costing £400 each per year, paid for from profit. Calculate the new amount each shareholder receives, assuming the same 75% share-out. **[3]**

**8.** Outline **one** way customers could be affected if Bellweather Bakery reduced the number of staff in its shops. **[2]**

**9.** Lockwood Quarry wants to extend its working hours to include Saturdays. Outline **one** way the local community could impact this decision. **[2]**

**10.** Seren runs Seren's Fashion, a clothing shop. A pressure group has posted online that one of her suppliers uses unsafe factories. Analyse the possible impact of the pressure group on Seren's Fashion. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** reason why a business may not be able to meet the objectives of all its stakeholders. **[3]**

**12.** Marsh Lane Pet Supplies is a successful local shop. A national pet-store chain has offered to supply it with products at 15% lower prices if it stops buying from three small local suppliers. The owner's objective is to increase profit, but the shop's customers value its "local and independent" image. Evaluate whether the owner should accept the chain's offer. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Any individual or group with an interest in a business [1] who is affected by, or can affect, its activities [1]. **[2]**

**3.** B [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any one from: local jobs; little noise, traffic or pollution; support for local projects [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Suppliers set the price of the materials or stock a business buys [1]. If a supplier raises its prices, the business's costs increase [1], which reduces profit unless the business can raise its own prices or find a cheaper supplier [1]. (Alternatively: late deliveries can stop production and lose sales.) **[3]**

**6.** Managers may want to expand the business to increase sales and their own status [1]. Expansion could mean a larger site, longer hours or more deliveries [1], which brings more noise and traffic, conflicting with the local community's wish for a quiet area [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** 75% × £64,000 = £48,000 [1]. £48,000 ÷ 4 = **£12,000** [1]

**(b)** Pay rise = 16 × £400 = £6,400 [1]. New profit = £64,000 − £6,400 = £57,600; 75% = £43,200 [1]. £43,200 ÷ 4 = **£10,800** each [1] **[5]**

**8.** Fewer staff could mean longer queues in Bellweather's shops at busy times [1], so customers wait longer for their bread and may go to a rival bakery instead [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Residents could complain to the council about noise and lorries on Saturdays [1], which might lead the council to refuse the change or limit the hours, so Lockwood Quarry cannot produce as much as it planned [1]. **[2]**

**10.** The post could reach many of Seren's customers [1], and some may stop buying from her because they do not want to support unsafe factories [1], reducing her sales revenue [1]. To protect her image Seren might switch to a supplier with safer factories [1], but this supplier may charge more, increasing her costs and cutting profit [1]. On the other hand, if Seren acts quickly and tells customers about the change, she could gain trust and loyalty from customers who care about the issue [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Different stakeholders want things that compete for the same money [1]. For example, employees want higher pay while customers want lower prices and shareholders want higher profit [1]; raising pay increases costs, so the business would need either higher prices or lower profit, which means at least one group's objective is not met [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Accepting the offer would cut the cost of stock by 15% [1], which fits the owner's objective of increasing profit; for example, if the shop spends £100,000 a year on stock, it would save £15,000 [1]. The chain is a large supplier and may deliver reliably [1].

However, the three local suppliers would lose orders, which may damage their businesses [1], and customers who value the shop's "local and independent" image may stop shopping there if they learn about the change [1]. Buying from a rival chain also makes the shop dependent on a competitor, which could later raise prices or stop supplying [1]. Lower sales could cancel out the cost saving [1].

Judgement: the owner should not accept the full offer, because the shop's local image is its point of difference and losing loyal customers could reduce profit more than the 15% saving increases it [1]. The decision depends on how many customers notice and care; a compromise could be to buy only some products from the chain while keeping local suppliers for the products customers see most [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The £100,000 figure is an illustration because the source gives no stock value; saying so is fine. Strong answers weigh the owner's profit objective against customers and suppliers, then reach a conclusion with a condition.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing stakeholders with shareholders in definitions.
- Choosing competitors as a stakeholder group in Question 3; they are not one of the eight listed.
- Calculating the pay rise per employee but forgetting to multiply by 16.
- Writing about pressure groups in general in Question 10 instead of Seren's suppliers and customers.
- Giving a one-sided answer in Question 12.
