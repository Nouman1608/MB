first teaching 2016, examined on Paper 2. Questions or parts marked **Higher tier only** test the

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which statement is correct? Give the letter. **[1]**

A. Only objects hotter than their surroundings emit infrared radiation.
B. All objects emit and absorb infrared radiation, whatever their temperature.
C. Objects below 0 °C emit no radiation.
D. Objects emit radiation only when they glow.

**2.** Define a perfect black body. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why a perfect black body is the best possible emitter of radiation. **[2]**

**4.** A block of metal is heated from room temperature until it glows. State what happens to the amount of
radiation it emits each second. **[1]**

**5.** A student says: "A perfect black body would always look black." Explain why this is not always true. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) A rock in a desert has a constant temperature at midday. State what this tells you about
the radiation it absorbs and emits. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A graph shows intensity against wavelength for the radiation emitted by the same object at two
temperatures, curve P and curve Q. Curve Q is higher than curve P at every wavelength.

**(a)** State which curve shows the higher temperature. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe how the peak of curve Q differs from the peak of curve P. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the graph shows that the object emits more radiation in total at the higher temperature. **[2]**

**8.** A steel poker is placed in a fire. Its colour changes from no visible glow, to dull red, to orange and then
to yellow-white.

**(a)** Explain why the poker emits infrared radiation even before it glows. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain the order of the colour changes in terms of the wavelength distribution of the emitted radiation. **[3]**

**9.** Two identical metal cans, X and Y, hold the same volume of hot water at the same starting temperature. Can
X is painted matt black. Can Y is shiny silver. They are left to cool in the same room.

**(a)** Predict which can cools faster. Explain your answer. **[3]**
**(b)** The cans are emptied, filled with cold water and placed in bright sunlight. Predict which can warms
faster. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) A solar panel on a spacecraft absorbs radiation from the Sun at a rate of 620 W. At one
moment it emits radiation at 540 W.

**(a)** Calculate the net energy gained by the panel each second. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe and explain how the temperature of the panel changes over the following minutes. Your answer
should say what happens to the emission rate. **[4]**

**11.** The average temperature of the Earth depends on the balance of radiation.

**(a)** (Higher tier only) Give three factors that determine the temperature of the Earth. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the radiation emitted by the Earth's surface has longer wavelengths than most of the radiation
arriving from the Sun. **[2]**
**(c)** (Higher tier only) The amount of cloud and ice covering the Earth increases, while everything else stays the same. Explain the
effect on the Earth's temperature. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) Some gases in the atmosphere absorb infrared radiation emitted by the Earth's surface.
Explain how an increase in the amount of these gases could lead to a higher Earth temperature that then stays
constant. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Options A, C and D are all versions of the belief that cold objects do not radiate.

**2.** An object that absorbs all of the radiation incident on it [1]; it does not reflect or transmit any radiation [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Absorbs all radiation" alone is one mark; the second mark needs "reflects none" or "transmits none".

**3.** A good absorber is also a good emitter [1]; a perfect black body is the best possible absorber, so it is the best possible emitter [1]
*Examiner insight:* Restating the definition without the absorber–emitter link earns no marks here.

**4.** It increases [1]

**5.** "Black" describes that it absorbs all incident radiation and reflects none [1]; a perfect black body that is hot enough emits visible light, so it would glow and not look black [1]

**6.** It absorbs and emits radiation at the same rate [1]
*Examiner insight:* "It is not absorbing or emitting" is wrong and scores zero.

**7. (a)** **Q** [1]
**(b)** The peak of Q is higher [1]; and at a shorter wavelength [1]
**(c)** Curve Q is higher at every wavelength [1]; so the total radiation emitted per second (the area under the curve) is greater [1] **[5]**

**8. (a)** All bodies emit infrared radiation whatever their temperature [1]
**(b)** As the temperature rises, the intensity increases and the peak moves to shorter wavelengths [1]; the emission first reaches the longest visible wavelength, red [1]; at higher temperatures more of the shorter visible wavelengths are emitted, so it appears orange and then yellow-white [1] **[4]**

**9. (a)** Can X [1]; matt black is a better emitter than shiny silver [1]; so X emits radiation at a greater rate at the same temperature and loses energy faster [1]
**(b)** Can X [1]; matt black is a better absorber, while shiny silver reflects most of the incident radiation [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a) the reason must be about emitting; in (b) it must be about absorbing. Swapping them loses a mark each time.

**10. (a)** 620 − 540 = **80 J** (80 W) [1]
**(b)** The panel absorbs radiation faster than it emits [1]; so its temperature rises [1]; as it gets hotter, the rate at which it emits radiation increases [1]; when the emission rate reaches 620 W the rates are equal and the temperature stays constant at a higher value [1] **[5]**

**11. (a)** The rate at which the Earth absorbs radiation [1]; the rate at which it emits radiation [1]; the amount of radiation reflected back into space [1]
**(b)** The Earth's surface is much cooler than the Sun [1]; a cooler body emits with its peak at a longer wavelength [1]
**(c)** More of the incoming radiation is reflected into space [1]; so less is absorbed while emission is at first unchanged, and the Earth emits faster than it absorbs [1]; the temperature falls until the emission rate drops to equal the lower absorption rate, at a lower constant temperature [1] **[8]**

**12.** Marked by levels; a top-level answer gives a linked chain like this:
- The surface absorbs radiation from the Sun and emits infrared radiation [1]
- More of the gases absorb more of this outgoing infrared [1]
- The gases emit radiation in all directions, some back towards the surface [1]
- So less radiation escapes into space each second [1]
- The Earth now absorbs radiation faster than it emits it, so its temperature rises [1]
- As it warms it emits at a greater rate, until emitting equals absorbing again and the temperature stays constant at the new, higher value [1]

*Examiner insight:* Answers that say "the gases trap heat" without any mention of rates of absorption and emission stay in the lowest level.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying cold objects do not emit radiation.
- Defining a black body by its colour instead of by absorbing all incident radiation.
- Explaining the best-emitter point without "a good absorber is a good emitter".
- Describing a hotter curve as "moving to the left" without saying it is also higher.
- Giving an absorbing reason for a cooling question, or an emitting reason for a warming question.
- (Higher tier only) Stopping at "the temperature rises" and not explaining why it then becomes constant.
- (Higher tier only) Leaving reflected radiation out of the Earth's balance.
