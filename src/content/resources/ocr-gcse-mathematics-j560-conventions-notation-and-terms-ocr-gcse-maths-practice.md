These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 8.01, Conventions, notation and terms. The specification says this content is assessed at **both tiers**, so every question here is for both tiers. No diagrams are given: draw your own sketch from each description, which is itself a skill in 8.01e. You need a ruler, a protractor and compasses for Question 11. Vertices of a polygon are always listed in order round the shape.

Learn the conventions first in the [conventions, notation and terms study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-conventions-notation-and-terms-ocr-gcse-maths/) or the [conventions, notation and terms revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-conventions-notation-and-terms-ocr-gcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Write down the type of each angle.

**(a)** 95° **[1]**

**(b)** 205° **[1]**

**(c)** 89° **[1]**

**2.** Lines OA, OB and OC start at the point O, with B between A and C. Angle AOB = 48° and angle AOC = 115°.

**(a)** Find angle BOC. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the reflex angle AOC. **[1]**

**3.** In triangle DEF:

**(a)** which side is labelled e? **[1]**

**(b)** which angle is opposite the side DE? **[1]**

**4.** WXYZ is a quadrilateral.

**(a)** Name its two diagonals. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the side opposite WX. **[1]**

**5.** Write down the mathematical name of each shape.

**(a)** A quadrilateral with exactly one pair of parallel sides. **[1]**

**(b)** A triangle with angles 40°, 70° and 70°. **[1]**

**(c)** A regular quadrilateral. **[1]**

**6.** **(a)** Write down the number of faces, edges and vertices of a pentagonal prism. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the solid that has one curved surface and no edges or vertices. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Say whether each statement is true or false. Give a reason for each answer.

**(a)** Every square is a rhombus. **[1]**

**(b)** Every rhombus is a square. **[1]**

**(c)** A rectangle that is not a square is a regular polygon. **[1]**

**(d)** Every rhombus is a kite. **[1]**

**8.** P is (−2, 1), Q is (4, 1) and R is (4, 5).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of S so that PQRS is a rectangle. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the lengths of PQ and QR. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the midpoint of the diagonal PR. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why PQRS is not a square. **[1]**

**9.** A is (0, 0), B is (5, 0), C is (8, 4) and D is (3, 4).

**(a)** Show that AB and DC are parallel and equal in length. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that AD = 5 units, and use this to explain why ABCD is a rhombus. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why ABCD is not a square. **[1]**

**10.** Triangle ABC has AB = AC = 7 cm and angle BAC = 40°. M is the midpoint of BC.

**(a)** Write down the mathematical name for triangle ABC. **[1]**

**(b)** Find angle ABC. **[2]**

**(c)** Find angle AMB. Give a reason for your answer. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Describe how to construct, with a ruler and compasses, a triangle with sides 5 cm, 6 cm and 7 cm. **[3]**

**(b)** Which angle of the triangle is the largest, and which instrument would you use to measure it? **[1]**

**12.** Triangle T has vertices (2, 1), (5, 1) and (2, 3).

**(a)** Write down the vertices of the image of T after a reflection in the y-axis. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the vertices of the image of T after a reflection in the line y = x. **[1]**

**(c)** Write down the vertices of the image of T after a translation by the vector (−3 over 4). **[1]**

**(d)** Name two properties of T that are the same in all three images. **[1]**

**13.** ABCD is a square. E is a point outside the square so that triangle BCE is equilateral.

**(a)** Find angle ABE. **[2]**

**(b)** Find angle BAE. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Obtuse** [1]

**(b)** **Reflex** [1]

**(c)** **Acute** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 89° is just under a right angle, so it is acute. Do not round an angle to decide its type.

**2. (a)** 115° − 48° = **67°** [1]

**(b)** 360° − 115° = **245°** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* With three arms at O there are three different angles, which is why each needs three letters.

**3. (a)** e is opposite angle E, so **e = DF** [1]

**(b)** **Angle F** (angle DFE) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* A side and the angle opposite it share a letter: lower case for the side, capital for the angle.

**4. (a)** **WY and XZ** [1]

**(b)** **ZY** (or YZ) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The letters go in order round the shape, so W is next to X and Z, and opposite Y.

**5. (a)** **Trapezium** [1]

**(b)** **Isosceles** triangle [1]

**(c)** **Square** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In part (b), two equal angles mean two equal sides. A regular quadrilateral needs equal sides and equal angles, which only a square has.

**6. (a)** 7 faces and 15 edges [1]; 10 vertices [1]

**(b)** **Sphere** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A prism with an n-sided cross-section has n + 2 faces, 3n edges and 2n vertices. For n = 5 that is 7, 15 and 10.

**7. (a)** **True**: a square has four equal sides, which is the property of a rhombus [1]

**(b)** **False**: a rhombus need not have right angles [1]

**(c)** **False**: its angles are equal but its sides are not all equal [1]

**(d)** **True**: its four equal sides give two pairs of equal adjacent sides [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* "True" or "false" alone earns nothing when a reason is asked for. Quote the defining property.

**8. (a)** **S = (−2, 5)** [1]

**(b)** **PQ = 6 units** [1]; **QR = 4 units** [1]

**(c)** ((−2 + 4)/2, (1 + 5)/2) = **(1, 3)** [1]

**(d)** Its sides are not all equal: PQ = 6 but QR = 4 [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* PQ is horizontal, so its length is the difference in x-coordinates: 4 − (−2) = 6. Watch the double negative.

**9. (a)** AB goes 5 right and 0 up; DC goes from (3, 4) to (8, 4), also 5 right and 0 up [1]; equal steps mean they are parallel and both have length 5 [1]

**(b)** AD goes 3 right and 4 up, so AD² = 3² + 4² = 25 and AD = 5 [1]; AD = AB, and opposite sides of a parallelogram are equal, so all four sides are 5 units, which makes ABCD a rhombus [1]

**(c)** AD is not vertical while AB is horizontal, so angle DAB is not a right angle [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Part (a) shows ABCD is a parallelogram. One extra fact, two adjacent sides equal, upgrades it to a rhombus.

**10. (a)** **Isosceles** triangle [1]

**(b)** The base angles are equal: (180° − 40°)/2 [1]; **70°** [1]

**(c)** **90°** [1]; AM joins the apex to the midpoint of the base, which is the line of symmetry of the isosceles triangle, so it is perpendicular to BC [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* Sketch the triangle with the dashes on AB and AC first. The symmetry is much easier to see once the equal sides are marked.

**11. (a)** Draw a 7 cm line with a ruler [1]; set the compasses to 5 cm and draw an arc from one end, then to 6 cm and draw an arc from the other end [1]; join both ends to the point where the arcs cross, leaving the arcs visible [1]

**(b)** The angle **opposite the 7 cm side**, measured with a **protractor** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* The largest angle is always opposite the longest side. Leaving the construction arcs on the diagram is part of the method.

**12. (a)** **(−2, 1), (−5, 1), (−2, 3)** [1]

**(b)** **(1, 2), (1, 5), (3, 2)** [1]

**(c)** **(−1, 5), (2, 5), (−1, 7)** [1]

**(d)** Any two of: side lengths, angles (including the right angle), area, shape [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Reflection in y = x swaps the coordinates. A translation adds the vector to every vertex.

**13. (a)** 90° + 60° [1]; **150°** [1]

**(b)** AB = BC = BE, so triangle ABE is isosceles with AB = BE [1]; (180° − 150°)/2 = **15°** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Mark the equal sides on your sketch: the square's sides and the equilateral triangle's sides are all the same length, which is the key to part (b).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an angle with the vertex letter in the wrong place.
- Reading vertices out of order, so a diagonal is treated as a side.
- Giving true or false without a reason.
- Calling a rhombus or rectangle regular.
- Writing coordinates the wrong way round, or losing a negative sign when finding a length.
- Rubbing out construction arcs.
- Not drawing a sketch from the description, and so missing equal sides.
