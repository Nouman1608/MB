## Questions

**1.** (a) Define simple harmonic motion. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the minus sign means in a = −ω²x. **[1]**

**2.** A piston completes 90 oscillations in 45.0 s. Calculate its period, frequency and angular frequency. **[3]**

**3.** Two identical pendulums have period 2.0 s. One passes through equilibrium 0.50 s after the other, moving the same way. Calculate the phase difference in radians. **[2]**

**4.** An oscillator has amplitude 0.12 m and angular frequency 5.0 rad s⁻¹. Calculate **(a)** its maximum speed **[1]**, **(b)** its maximum acceleration **[1]**, **(c)** its speed when the displacement is 0.090 m **[2]**, and **(d)** its acceleration at that displacement **[1]**.

**5.** A mass oscillates with displacement x = 0.035 sin(18t) in SI units.
**(a)** State the amplitude and calculate the period. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate x when t = 0.25 s. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the first time at which x = +0.0175 m. **[2]**

**6.** The acceleration-displacement graph of an oscillator is a line through the origin that passes through x = 0.050 m, a = −12.8 m s⁻². **(a)** Show that the motion is SHM and find ω. **[2]** **(b)** Calculate the period. **[1]** **(c)** The amplitude is 0.050 m. Calculate the maximum speed. **[1]** **(d)** State the phase relation between v and x. **[1]**

**7.** A student times a trolley on springs. Twenty oscillations take 31.8 s, with an uncertainty of ±0.2 s for the timing.
**(a)** Describe how to time the oscillations as accurately as you can. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the period and its absolute uncertainty. **[2]**

**8.** A 0.25 kg mass hangs from a spring of constant 49 N m⁻¹.
**(a)** Show that the resultant force gives a = −(k/m)x and calculate ω and T. **[3]**
**(b)** The amplitude is increased from 2.0 cm to 6.0 cm. State what happens to T, the maximum speed and the total energy, with figures. **[3]**

**9.** A 0.40 kg oscillator has ω = 7.0 rad s⁻¹ and amplitude 0.10 m.
**(a)** Calculate the total energy. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the kinetic and potential energy at x = 0.060 m. **[3]**
**(c)** Describe the energy-displacement graphs. **[2]**

**10.** A light-damped pendulum has amplitude 9.0 cm, falling to 4.5 cm after some cycles.
**(a)** Calculate the percentage of its energy lost. **[2]**
**(b)** State where the energy goes. **[1]**
**(c)** Compare light, critical and heavy damping. **[3]**
**(d)** Give one use of critical damping. **[1]**

**11.** A 0.20 kg mass on a spring of constant 18 N m⁻¹ is driven by a vibrator whose frequency is slowly increased from 0.5 Hz to 3.0 Hz.
**(a)** Calculate the natural frequency. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe and explain how the amplitude changes. **[4]**
**(c)** State how the amplitude-driving frequency graph changes if a card is attached to the mass. **[2]**
**(d)** Give an example of unwanted resonance and one way to reduce it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The acceleration is proportional to the distance from equilibrium [1] and points back towards equilibrium [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The acceleration is opposite in direction to the displacement [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition needs proportionality and direction; "back and forth" is too vague to define SHM.

**2.** T = 45.0/90 = **0.500 s** [1]; f = 1/0.500 = **2.00 Hz** [1]; ω = 2π × 2.00 = **12.6 rad s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep to the units asked for; rad s⁻¹ and Hz are different.

**3.** Phase difference = 2π × 0.50/2.0 [1] = **π/2 rad (1.57 rad)** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the fraction of a period used, which makes your method clear to the reader.

**4. (a)** vmax = ωA = 5.0 × 0.12 = **0.60 m s⁻¹** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** amax = ω²A = 25 × 0.12 = **3.0 m s⁻²** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** v = 5.0 × √(0.12² − 0.090²) [1] = 5.0 × 0.0794 = **0.40 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** a = ω²x = 25 × 0.090 = 2.25 = **2.3 m s⁻²** (2 s.f.) towards equilibrium [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A direction for a in (d) is part of the answer; a bare magnitude leaves the answer incomplete.

**5. (a)** Amplitude **0.035 m** [1]; T = 2π/18 = **0.35 s** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** x = 0.035 sin(4.5) = **−0.034 m** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** sin 18t = 0.5, so 18t = π/6 [1], t = **0.029 s** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use radians for the angle; the same expression in degrees gives a different number, so work in radians throughout.

**6. (a)** a is proportional to −x, with gradient −12.8/0.050 = −256 s⁻² [1], so a = −ω²x with ω² = 256 and ω = **16 rad s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** T = 2π/16 = **0.39 s** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** vmax = 16 × 0.050 = **0.80 m s⁻¹** [1] **[1]**
**(d)** v leads x by **π/2** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "line through the origin with negative gradient" to show it is SHM, not just state it.

**7. (a)** Use a fiducial marker at the equilibrium position and start and stop the timer as the trolley passes it [1]. Time many (20) oscillations and divide [1]. Repeat and take a mean, with a small steady amplitude [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** T = 31.8/20 = **1.59 s** [1]; uncertainty = 0.2/20 = **±0.01 s** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide the absolute uncertainty by 20 as well as the time.

**8. (a)** The resultant force is F = −kx [1], so a = F/m = −(k/m)x with ω² = 49/0.25 = 196 s⁻², ω = **14 rad s⁻¹** [1] and T = 2π/14 = **0.45 s** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** T stays **0.45 s** because the oscillator is isochronous [1]; vmax = 14 × 0.060 = **0.84 m s⁻¹** (three times larger) [1]; energy ½kA² rises from 0.0098 J to **0.088 J** (nine times) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Nine times" must come from energy being proportional to A², not A.

**9. (a)** E = ½mω²A² = ½ × 0.40 × 49 × 0.010 [1] = **0.098 J** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Ek = ½ × 0.40 × 49 × (0.010 − 0.0036) [1] = **0.063 J** [1]; Ep = 0.098 − 0.063 = **0.035 J** [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Ep against x is a parabola with minimum at x = 0 and Ek against x is an inverted parabola with maximum at x = 0 [1]; they sum to a horizontal total-energy line at 0.098 J, crossing at x = ±0.071 m [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that Ek and Ep add to E; it is a quick test of the arithmetic.

**10. (a)** Energy is proportional to A², so energy left is (4.5/9.0)² = 0.25 [1]; energy lost is **75%** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It is transferred as thermal energy to the surroundings (through friction or drag) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Light damping: oscillations continue with decaying amplitude [1]. Critical damping: settles at equilibrium in the least time with no oscillation [1]. Heavy damping: no oscillation and a slow return [1]. **[3]**
**(d)** For example a car suspension, a door closer or a meter needle [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare all three types in terms of oscillation and return time, not just by name.

**11. (a)** ω = √(k/m) = √(18/0.20) = 9.49 rad s⁻¹ [1]; f = ω/2π [1] = **1.5 Hz** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The amplitude is small at 0.5 Hz [1], rises as the driving frequency approaches 1.5 Hz [1], reaches a maximum at resonance, where the driving frequency has become equal to the natural frequency, because energy transfer is greatest [1], then falls again towards 3.0 Hz [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** The peak is lower [1] and broader (the maximum moves to slightly lower frequency) [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** For example a washing machine drum shaking at one spin speed [1]; add damping or change the natural frequency [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name driving and natural frequency in the same sentence; "frequencies match" is too loose.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the direction (towards equilibrium) in a = −ω²x.
- Using degrees for the angle in x = A sin ωt.
- Taking amplitude as peak-to-peak distance.
- Forgetting 2π when changing between f and ω.
- Saying energy rises with A, not A².
- Describing a damped oscillation without the loss of energy as thermal energy.
- Leaving the peak position unchanged but taller when damping is added.
- Writing "frequencies match" at resonance without naming driving and natural.
