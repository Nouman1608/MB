## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following statements matches Piaget's view? Shade one box. **[1]**

- A Language determines how we think
- B Thought develops first and language reflects it
- C Language and thought develop completely separately
- D People cannot think about things they have no words for

**2.** Identify **two** functions of animal communication. **[2]**

**3.** In Von Frisch's study, what information did the **angle** of the waggle dance give other
bees? **[1]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by **displacement** in human language. **[2]**

**5.** Define **postural echo**. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Outline the difference between the **strong** and **weak** versions of the Sapir-Whorf
hypothesis. **[2]**

**7.** Describe Von Frisch's bee study. **[4]**

**8.** Read the scenario.

*At a job interview, Ellie sits with her arms folded and looks at the floor while answering
questions. The interviewer, who sits leaning forward with arms open, finds Ellie hard to read.*

Using your knowledge of non-verbal communication, explain why the interviewer might form a negative
impression of Ellie. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which personal space can differ between people. **[4]**

**10.** Describe and evaluate Darwin's evolutionary explanation of non-verbal behaviour. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss the relationship between language and thought. Refer to Piaget's theory and the
Sapir-Whorf hypothesis in your answer. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** Any two: survival (for example, alarm calls) [1]; reproduction (courtship) [1]; territory [1];
food [1].

**3.** The **direction** of the food source relative to the sun [1].

**4.** The ability to communicate about things that are not present here and now [1], such as past
or future events, distant places or imaginary ideas [1].

**5.** Unconsciously mirroring or copying another person's posture [1], which signals rapport or
agreement and tends to increase liking [1].

**6.** The **strong** version says language **determines** thought, so we cannot think about
something we have no words for [1]. The **weak** version says language only **influences** thought,
making some things easier to notice or remember [1].

**7.** Up to 4 marks. Von Frisch marked honeybees and placed food at different distances and
directions from the hive, then observed the returning bees [1]. When food was near, they performed a
**round dance**, telling other bees to search nearby [1]. When food was further away, they performed a
**waggle dance** [1]: the angle of the waggle run showed direction relative to the sun and its
length showed distance, and other bees flew to the food [1].

**8.** Up to 4 marks: identify the behaviour [1] and explain what it communicates in context [1], for
two behaviours.
- **Closed posture** [1]: Ellie's folded arms may signal defensiveness or lack of openness, so the
  interviewer may think she is unfriendly or uninterested [1].
- **Lack of eye contact** [1]: looking at the floor may signal low confidence or lack of interest,
  and makes turn-taking harder, so the interviewer finds her hard to read [1].
- *Also creditable:* **no postural echo** [1]: Ellie does not mirror the interviewer's open,
  forward-leaning posture, so there is less sign of rapport [1].

**9.** Two from, 2 marks each (identify [1], explain [1]):
- **Culture** [1]: some cultures stand closer during conversation than others, so normal
  conversational distance differs [1].
- **Status** [1]: people tend to keep more distance from someone of higher status, such as a head
  teacher [1].
- **Gender** [1]: personal space preferences can differ between males and females, and between
  same-sex and mixed-sex pairs [1].

**10.** Level-based answer, up to 6 marks.
- *Description:* NVC such as facial expressions is **innate**; it evolved through natural selection
  because it helped survival, for example a fear expression warns others of danger and bared teeth
  signal a threat, avoiding a fight.
- *Evaluation for:* expressions appear very early in babies; children born blind produce similar
  expressions; Ekman found basic emotional expressions recognised in an isolated culture.
- *Evaluation against:* **Yuki (2007)** found Japanese participants judged emotion more from the eyes
  and Americans more from the mouth, suggesting interpretation is learned through culture; gestures
  and personal space vary between cultures.
- *Conclusion:* basic expressions may be innate, but culture shapes their display and interpretation.
**5--6 marks:** accurate description and well-explained evaluation on both sides. **3--4:**
reasonable description, some evaluation. **1--2:** limited.

**11.** Level-based answer, up to 9 marks.
- **Piaget:** thought comes first; children form concepts (for example, object permanence) before
  they have words for them; language reflects thinking. *Evidence:* Sinclair-de-Zwart -- teaching
  non-conserving children the vocabulary of conservers made little difference to their conservation.
- **Sapir-Whorf:** language shapes thought. Strong version (determinism) and weak version
  (relativity). *Evidence:* Berinmo speakers recognised and remembered colours more easily across
  their own language's colour boundaries; Carmichael's study showed that labels changed how
  ambiguous drawings were recalled.
- **Discussion:** the two are opposite claims; the strong version is largely rejected because people
  can perceive and think about things they cannot name, and new words are created for new ideas;
  the weak version has support; Piaget's evidence comes mainly from young children. A strong
  conclusion: thought may come first in early development, but language then influences how we
  categorise and remember.
**7--9 marks:** accurate, detailed knowledge of both, effective discussion with evidence and a
reasoned conclusion. **4--6:** both described with some discussion. **1--3:** limited or confused
(for example, the theories reversed).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing Piaget and Sapir-Whorf.
- Mixing up which feature of the waggle dance shows direction and which shows distance.
- Naming a non-verbal behaviour without saying what it communicates in the scenario.
- Using Yuki as support for Darwin rather than as evidence of learning.
