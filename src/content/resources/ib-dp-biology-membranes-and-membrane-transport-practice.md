## Questions

**1.** Describe how phospholipid molecules are arranged when they form a bilayer in water, and explain why they arrange themselves in this way. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why oxygen can cross a membrane without help from a protein, whereas a sodium ion cannot. **[3]**

**3.** Distinguish between integral and peripheral proteins in a membrane, and give one function of a membrane protein. **[3]**

**4.** Using the idea that particles move randomly, explain why water shows net movement from a dilute solution to a more concentrated solution across a membrane that is impermeable to the solute. **[4]**

**5.** Cylinders of beetroot were weighed, left in sucrose solutions for one hour, blotted dry and weighed again.

| Sucrose (mol dm⁻³) | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial mass (g) | 3.20 | 3.15 | 3.18 | 3.22 |
| Final mass (g) | 3.52 | 3.21 | 3.04 | 2.90 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in mass at 0.20 mol dm⁻³. **[2]**
**(b)** Using a straight line between 0.20 and 0.30 mol dm⁻³, estimate the concentration giving zero change in mass. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the loss of mass at 0.40 mol dm⁻³. **[2]**

**6.** Compare facilitated diffusion with active transport. **[4]**

**7.** An electron micrograph shows a membrane. The width of the membrane on the image is 21 mm and the magnification is ×3 000 000. Calculate the actual width of the membrane in nanometres. **[3]**

**8.** Outline the structure of the fluid mosaic model of the plasma membrane, as you would show it in a labelled two-dimensional drawing. **[5]**

**9.** State where the carbohydrate part of a glycoprotein is found in a plasma membrane, and give two roles of glycoproteins and glycolipids. **[3]**

**10.** (HL only) The percentage of unsaturated fatty acid chains in a fish's membrane phospholipids was measured after acclimation at four temperatures.

| Temperature (°C) | 5 | 15 | 25 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Unsaturated chains (%) | 62 | 55 | 47 | 38 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in unsaturated chains from 5 °C to 35 °C, relative to the value at 5 °C. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the advantage of the pattern shown, in terms of membrane fluidity. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain the role of cholesterol in animal cell membranes at high and low temperatures. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) A nerve cell has a sodium-potassium pump that completes 2.4 × 10⁶ cycles per minute.

**(a)** Calculate the net number of positive charges moved out of the cell per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of ATP molecules used per second, assuming one ATP per cycle. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the pump helps to produce a membrane potential. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) Epithelial cells of the small intestine absorb glucose using a sodium-dependent glucose cotransporter.

**(a)** Explain why this is indirect active transport. **[3]**
**(b)** The transporter carries 2 sodium ions with each glucose molecule. 600 glucose molecules enter. Calculate the ATP needed by sodium-potassium pumps to remove the sodium, assuming one ATP per cycle. **[3]**
**(c)** State the type of gating of the nicotinic acetylcholine receptor and of the sodium channels in an axon. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Phospholipids form two layers, with the hydrophilic heads facing the water on the outside [1] and the hydrophobic fatty acid tails pointing inwards, facing each other [1]. The tails are shielded from water, which makes the arrangement stable [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer names both the arrangement and the reason. Saying only "heads out, tails in" describes without explaining, and explanation is what the command word asks for.

**2.** Oxygen is small and non-polar [1], so it dissolves in the hydrophobic hydrocarbon core and passes between phospholipids [1]. A sodium ion is charged and hydrophilic, so the core repels it and it cannot cross without a protein [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to both particles. An answer about oxygen alone leaves the contrast unexplained.

**3.** Integral proteins lie within one lipid layer or span both [1]; peripheral proteins are attached to a surface and do not enter the core [1]. Any one function, for example a channel, a pump, a receptor or an enzyme [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Integral proteins go through the membrane" is incomplete, since some are embedded in one layer only. Describe the position, not just the span.

**4.** Water molecules move randomly in both directions across the membrane [1]. The membrane is impermeable to the solute, so only water crosses [1]. The more concentrated side has a lower proportion of water molecules, so fewer cross from it than arrive from the dilute side [1]. Net movement is therefore towards the higher solute concentration [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the direction using solute concentration. "Towards more water" or "towards less water" is ambiguous and tends to lose credit.

**5. (a)** Change = 3.21 − 3.15 = 0.06 g [1]. 0.06 ÷ 3.15 × 100 = **+1.9%** [1]
**(b)** The percentage change at 0.30 is (3.04 − 3.18) ÷ 3.18 × 100 = −4.4% [1]. Concentration = 0.20 + 0.10 × 1.9 ÷ (1.9 + 4.4) = **0.23 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(c)** The solution outside has a higher solute concentration than the cell contents [1], so there is net movement of water out of the cells by osmosis [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out both the subtraction and the division. A percentage change needs its sign, and a bare mass change in grams does not answer a question asking for percentage.

**6.** Both move specific particles through a membrane protein [1] and are selective [1]. Facilitated diffusion moves particles down a concentration gradient using no ATP, through a channel [1], while active transport uses ATP in a pump and can move particles against a gradient [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs at least one shared feature and one difference, each stated outright. Describing each process in separate paragraphs without linking them does not compare.

**7.** Actual size = image size ÷ magnification [1]. 21 mm = 21 × 10⁶ nm, so 21 × 10⁶ ÷ 3 000 000 [1] = **7 nm** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert units before dividing. Dividing 21 by 3 000 000 without converting units gives a meaningless number, and the unit must be written.

**8.** Two layers of phospholipids with hydrophilic heads outwards and hydrophobic tails inwards [1]. Integral proteins embedded in or across the bilayer [1]. Peripheral proteins on a surface [1]. Cholesterol among the phospholipids and glycoproteins with carbohydrate chains on the outer surface [1]. Hydrophilic and hydrophobic regions labelled [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw tails as two lines from each head and keep carbohydrate chains on the outside. Unlabelled drawings are hard to credit.

**9.** The carbohydrate is on the extracellular (outer) side of the membrane [1]. Roles: cell recognition [1] and cell adhesion [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Cell communication" is vaguer than recognition. Use the terms in the syllabus statement.

**10. (a)** Decrease = 62 − 38 = 24 percentage points [1]. 24 ÷ 62 × 100 = **38.7%** [1]
**(b)** At low temperature, more unsaturated chains are present [1]. Their kinks prevent tight packing, so the bilayer stays fluid and flexible [1]. At higher temperatures, more saturated chains make the membrane stronger [1]
**(c)** At high temperatures cholesterol restricts the movement of phospholipids, which stabilises the membrane [1]; at low temperatures it prevents the tails packing closely, so the membrane does not stiffen [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* In data questions, use the numbers and link them to the mechanism. For cholesterol, state what it does at both ends of the temperature range.

**11. (a)** Each cycle moves 3 Na⁺ out and 2 K⁺ in, a net of one positive charge out [1]. 2.4 × 10⁶ cycles give **2.4 × 10⁶ net positive charges per minute** [1]
**(b)** 2.4 × 10⁶ ÷ 60 [1] = **4.0 × 10⁴ ATP per second** [1]
**(c)** More positive charge leaves than enters, so the inside becomes negative relative to the outside [1], and the pump also keeps sodium low and potassium high inside, producing concentration gradients for ions [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention the unequal ratio when explaining membrane potential. Saying only "it pumps ions" does not link to charge.

**12. (a)** Sodium ions move into the cell down their gradient through the cotransporter [1]. Glucose is carried in with them even against its gradient [1]. The ATP is used by a different pump, which keeps the sodium gradient, so ATP is not used directly by the cotransporter [1]
**(b)** Sodium = 2 × 600 = 1200 ions [1]. Each cycle removes 3, so 1200 ÷ 3 [1] = **400 ATP** [1]
**(c)** The nicotinic acetylcholine receptor is neurotransmitter-gated [1]; the axon sodium channels are voltage-gated [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For indirect active transport, say where the energy comes from. "Uses sodium" alone does not say that ATP was spent elsewhere.

## Where marks are usually lost
- Describing an arrangement without giving the reason when "explain" is the command word.
- Saying osmosis moves water "to where there is less water" instead of towards the higher solute concentration.
- Leaving out the fact that the membrane is impermeable to the solute.
- Omitting the sign or the unit in a percentage change or magnification answer.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to nanometres before dividing.
- Writing that facilitated diffusion needs ATP.
- Putting the carbohydrate of a glycoprotein on the cytoplasmic side.
- Treating cholesterol as only a stiffener (HL only).
- Giving the sodium-potassium ratio as 2 out, 3 in (HL only).
