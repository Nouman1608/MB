Condensed notes for subtopic **8.4 Transition elements**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620, 2026–2028. Items marked *(Extended)* are Supplement content. For the full explanation, data and a worked example, see the [transition elements study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-transition-elements/); for exam-style questions, use the [transition elements practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-transition-elements-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transition elements | Metals in the central block of the Periodic Table, between Groups II and III |
| Density | Mass per unit volume |
| Catalyst | Increases the rate of a reaction and is unchanged at the end |
| Oxidation number *(Extended)* | Shown by the Roman numeral, e.g. iron(III) = +3 |
| Variable oxidation numbers *(Extended)* | An element forms ions with different charges, e.g. Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ |

## The four Core properties

Transition elements are metals that:

1. have **high densities**
2. have **high melting points**
3. form **coloured compounds**
4. often act as **catalysts**, both as **elements** and in **compounds**

Note: it is the **compounds** that are coloured, not the metals.

## Evidence: compared with Group I

| Property | Transition elements | Group I metals |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | High (Fe 1538 °C, Cu 1085 °C) | Low (Na 98 °C, K 63 °C) |
| Density | High (Fe 7.87 g/cm³, Cu 8.96 g/cm³) | Low (Na 0.97 g/cm³; float on water) |
| Hardness | Hard, strong | Soft; cut with a knife |
| Compounds | Often coloured | Typically white, e.g. NaCl, KNO₃ |
| Catalytic use | Common (Fe, Ni, V₂O₅) | Not used as catalysts |
| Ions | Variable charge *(Extended)* | Always 1+ |
| Reaction with cold water | Slow or none | Vigorous |

## Coloured compounds to quote

| Substance | Colour |
|---|---|
| CuSO₄·5H₂O / Cu²⁺(aq) | Blue |
| CuCO₃ | Green |
| CoCl₂·6H₂O | Pink |
| Fe(OH)₂ precipitate | Green |
| Fe(OH)₃ precipitate | Red-brown |
| KMnO₄ solution | Purple |

## Catalysts to quote

| Catalyst | Element / compound | Use |
|---|---|---|
| Iron | Element | Haber process (ammonia) |
| Nickel | Element | Hydrogenation of alkenes |
| Vanadium(V) oxide | Compound | Contact process (SO₂ → SO₃) |

Always name the **process** with the catalyst.

## Variable oxidation numbers *(Extended)*

| | Iron(II) | Iron(III) |
|---|---|---|
| Ion | Fe²⁺ | Fe³⁺ |
| Oxidation number | +2 | +3 |
| Chloride | FeCl₂ | FeCl₃ |
| Oxide | FeO | Fe₂O₃ |
| Sulfate | FeSO₄ | Fe₂(SO₄)₃ |
| With NaOH(aq) | Green precipitate | Red-brown precipitate |

```
Fe + 2HCl → FeCl2 + H2          (iron(II))
2Fe + 3Cl2 → 2FeCl3             (iron(III))
Fe2+ → Fe3+ + e-                (oxidation)
```

- Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ is **oxidation** (oxidation number +2 → +3; loses one electron).
- Fe²⁺ decolourises acidified KMnO₄ (it is a reducing agent).
- Copper also varies: Cu₂O (copper(I)) and CuO (copper(II)).

## Model answers

*Describe the properties of transition elements.* (4 marks)

> Transition elements are metals with high densities and high melting points. They form coloured compounds, such as blue copper(II) sulfate, and they often act as catalysts, for example iron in the Haber process.

*(Extended) Explain how iron shows a property that Group I metals do not have.* (2 marks)

> Iron forms ions with variable oxidation numbers, Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ (as in FeCl₂ and FeCl₃). Group I metals form only 1+ ions.

## Building a formula from the name

1. Read the oxidation number from the Roman numeral.
2. Find the charge on the anion (Cl⁻, O²⁻, SO₄²⁻, OH⁻).
3. Balance charges: Fe³⁺ with O²⁻ → 2 × (+3) = 3 × (−2) → Fe₂O₃.

## Exam traps

- The compounds are coloured, not the metals.
- "Conducts electricity" and "shiny" are true of all metals; they are not the syllabus transition-element properties.
- Catalyst answers need both the **catalyst** and the **process**.
- The syllabus wants "as elements **and** in compounds": give one of each.
- Always include the Roman numeral: iron(II) chloride, not iron chloride.
- FeO is iron(II) oxide; Fe₂O₃ is iron(III) oxide.
- Zinc oxide is white: don't use zinc for colour examples.
- In "compare" questions, state the property for **both** metals with a comparative word: "iron has a higher melting point than sodium".
- A catalyst is unchanged at the end of the reaction; don't say it is "used up".

## Self-test

1. State **four** properties of transition elements.
2. Give **two** differences between iron and sodium.
3. Name a transition element that acts as a catalyst as an element, and the process.
4. Name a transition element compound that acts as a catalyst, and the process.
5. What colour is aqueous copper(II) sulfate?
6. What colour is hydrated cobalt(II) chloride?
7. *(Extended)* Explain what is meant by "variable oxidation numbers".
8. *(Extended)* Give the formula of iron(III) sulfate.
9. *(Extended)* Name FeO.
10. *(Extended)* What forms when iron reacts with dilute hydrochloric acid?
11. *(Extended)* Describe a test to show a solution contains Fe³⁺ rather than Fe²⁺.
12. *(Extended)* Is Fe²⁺ → Fe³⁺ oxidation or reduction? Explain.

**Answers**

1. High density; high melting point; form coloured compounds; act as catalysts.
2. Iron has a much higher melting point and higher density (also harder, forms coloured compounds, less reactive with water).
3. Iron, Haber process (or nickel, hydrogenation of alkenes).
4. Vanadium(V) oxide, Contact process.
5. Blue.
6. Pink.
7. The element forms ions with different charges / oxidation numbers, e.g. Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺.
8. Fe₂(SO₄)₃.
9. Iron(II) oxide.
10. Iron(II) chloride and hydrogen.
11. Add aqueous sodium hydroxide: Fe³⁺ gives a red-brown precipitate (Fe²⁺ gives green).
12. Oxidation; the oxidation number increases (+2 → +3) and an electron is lost.
