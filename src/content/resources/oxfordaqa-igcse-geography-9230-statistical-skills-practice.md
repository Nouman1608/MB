## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the median. **[1]**

**2.** Find the mode of: 4, 7, 7, 9, 12, 7, 5. **[1]**

**3.** A set of values has a lower quartile of 14 and an upper quartile of 39. Calculate the inter-quartile range. **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by interpolation. **[1]**

**5.** Give **one** reason why a graph with a vertical axis that does not start at zero can be misleading. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The numbers of cars parked in a beach car park in each of 12 hours on one day were:

3, 5, 5, 6, 8, 9, 9, 9, 12, 14, 16, 40

**(a)** State the median. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** State the mode and the range. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the inter-quartile range. **[3]**

**(e)** Explain which average is most suitable to describe the typical number of cars parked. **[2]**

**7.** A council reports that the number of visitors to a beach fell from 8 200 in one year to 6 970 in the next.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change. **[2]**

**(b)** The council says that "visitor numbers have plunged this year". Suggest **one** reason why this statement could be misleading. **[1]**

**8.** A student measures the height of 40 sand dunes in a desert area. The results are grouped in the table.

| Height (m) | Frequency |
|---|---|
| 0 to under 2 | 3 |
| 2 to under 4 | 9 |
| 4 to under 6 | 15 |
| 6 to under 8 | 8 |
| 8 to under 10 | 5 |

**(a)** State the modal class. **[1]**

**(b)** Complete a cumulative frequency table for the data. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the median height, using a cumulative frequency graph or calculation. **[3]**

**(d)** Calculate an estimate of the mean height, using the class midpoints. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows the distance from the sea and the percentage vegetation cover at six sites on a sand dune system.

| Distance from sea (m) | 10 | 30 | 50 | 70 | 90 | 110 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Vegetation cover (%) | 5 | 12 | 21 | 35 | 44 | 52 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship shown by the data. **[2]**

**(b)** Use a line of best fit to estimate the vegetation cover at 60 m from the sea. **[2]**

**(c)** A student uses the line to predict the cover at 130 m. Explain why this prediction is less reliable than the one in part (b). **[2]**

**10.** A newspaper graph shows the number of burglaries in a town. The vertical axis starts at 480. The bars are 490 for last year and 500 for this year, and this year's bar appears twice as tall.

Explain **two** weaknesses in the way the data are presented. **[4]**

**11.** Suggest how a pupil could improve the reliability of the results when measuring pebble sizes on a beach, and justify your answer. **[3]**

**12.** "The mean is always the best way to summarise a set of geographical data." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The middle value when the data are placed in order [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 7 [1]. **[1]**

**3.** 39 - 14 = 25 [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Estimating a value that lies within the range of the data you have (for example from a line of best fit) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** It exaggerates small differences between bars, making them look larger than they are [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** The values are already in order. Median = (9 + 9) ÷ 2 = 9 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** The total is 136 [1]. Mean = 136 ÷ 12 = 11.3 [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Mode = 9 [1]. Range = 40 - 3 = 37 [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** Lower half: 3, 5, 5, 6, 8, 9. Lower quartile = (5 + 6) ÷ 2 = 5.5 [1]. Upper half: 9, 9, 12, 14, 16, 40. Upper quartile = (12 + 14) ÷ 2 = 13 [1]. IQR = 13 - 5.5 = 7.5 [1]. **[3]**

**(e)** The median (9) is most suitable [1], because the 40 is an extreme value (anomaly) which pulls the mean up to 11.3, higher than almost all the hourly counts, whereas the median is not affected [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** (6 970 - 8 200) ÷ 8 200 × 100 [1] = -15%, so a 15% decrease [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Any one from: it is based on only two years, which may not show the long-term trend; the previous year may have been unusually high; weather may have affected one day or season; the sample may not include all visitors [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** 4 to under 6 m [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** Cumulative frequencies: 3, 12, 27, 35, 40 [2 marks for all five correct; 1 mark for at least three correct]. **[2]**

**(c)** The median is at 40 ÷ 2 = 20th value [1]. Cumulative frequency is 12 at 4 m and 27 at 6 m [1]. Median = 4 + (20 - 12) ÷ (27 - 12) × 2 = about 5.1 m [1] (accept 5.0 to 5.2 from a graph). **[3]**

**(d)** Midpoints: 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 [1]. Sum of midpoint × frequency = 3 + 27 + 75 + 56 + 45 = 206 [1]. Mean = 206 ÷ 40 = 5.15, so about 5.2 m [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** There is a strong positive relationship [1]. As distance from the sea increases, vegetation cover increases, from 5% to 52% [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** A line of best fit is drawn through the centre of the points [1]. At 60 m, the cover is about 28% (accept 26% to 30%) [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** 130 m is outside the range of the data (10 m to 110 m), so it is extrapolation [1]. The relationship may not continue in the same way, for example the cover cannot rise beyond 100%, and other factors such as soil or human trampling may change it [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Truncated axis: the vertical axis starts at 480, not zero, so a small difference looks very large [1]. Effect: the real increase is only 10 burglaries, or about 2%, but the bar appears twice as tall [1]. A second weakness, such as only two years are shown [1], so no trend can be seen and there is no context such as population or earlier years [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Measure a larger sample, for example 30 pebbles instead of 10 [1]. Use random sampling, such as selecting pebbles at set intervals along a line or with random numbers, to avoid bias [1]. Repeat measurements at several sites or on different days, and calculate averages [1]. (Accept: use the same method and equipment each time.) **[3]**

**12.** Indicative content:
- Agree: the mean uses every value, so it reflects all the data [1].
- Agree: it is simple to calculate and compare, and is useful for symmetrical data [1].
- Disagree: outliers pull the mean up or down, so it can be unrepresentative [1].
- Disagree: the median is better for skewed data, and the mode is better for categories [1].
- Disagree: the mean says nothing about spread, so range or IQR is also needed [1].
- Judgement: the mean is useful but should be used with a measure of spread and compared with the median; the best average depends on the data; a justified conclusion is needed [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not ordering the data before finding the median or quartiles.
- Giving an answer with no working or no unit.
- Dividing by the new value in a percentage change.
- Joining plotted points instead of drawing a line of best fit.
- Failing to say that extrapolation is less reliable.
- Saying a graph is "misleading" without saying how.
