These questions cover section 9.2 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: lines of best fit, the method of semi-averages, the equation y = mx + c, and the use and limitations of a line of best fit in prediction. Recall the methods from the [lines of best fit study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-lines-of-best-fit/) or the [lines of best fit revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-lines-of-best-fit-revision-notes/). All data are invented. Show your working and give answers to 3 significant figures unless a question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** Find the mean point of the pairs (2, 7), (5, 12), (6, 15), (8, 18) and (9, 23). **[2]**

**2.** A line of best fit passes through (5, 40) and (25, 10). Find its equation in the form y = mx + c. **[3]**

**3.** For parcels with masses from 1 kg to 20 kg, a courier's charge y ($) is related to the mass x (kg) by the line y = 12 + 0.5x. Interpret the gradient and the intercept in context. **[2]**

**4.** Explain the difference between interpolation and extrapolation. **[2]**

**5.** The semi-average points of a data set are (3, 16) and (11, 40).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line of best fit. **[2]**

**(b)** Use it to estimate y when x = 7. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A driver records the distance x (km) and the time y (minutes) for eight deliveries.

| Distance x | 12 | 5 | 9 | 15 | 3 | 7 | 11 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time y | 52 | 30 | 41 | 60 | 24 | 37 | 48 | 56 |

**(a)** Using the method of semi-averages, find the two semi-average points. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the equation of the line of best fit. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the mean point of all eight deliveries and show that it lies on your line. **[2]**

**(d)** Estimate the time for a 10 km delivery, and interpret the gradient in context. **[2]**

**7.** The table shows the years of experience x and the hourly pay y ($) of seven workers.

| x | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 | 9 | 11 | 13 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 10 | 13 | 16 | 22 | 26 | 28 | 33 |

**(a)** In the method of semi-averages, state which pair is left out and why. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the two semi-average points. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the equation of the line of best fit. **[2]**

**(d)** Use the line to estimate the hourly pay of a worker with 30 years of experience, and comment on the reliability of your estimate. **[2]**

**8.** On a scatter diagram the x-axis starts at 40. A line of best fit passes through (40, 18) and (80, 38). A student says the intercept is 18.

**(a)** Explain the student's error. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the correct equation of the line. **[3]**

**9.** A line of best fit was found from 30 points showing strong positive correlation, with x values from 10 to 50. It is used to estimate y when x = 30 and when x = 70. Compare the reliability of the two estimates. **[4]**

**10.** For batteries aged 2 to 24 months, the percentage y of original battery life remaining after x months is modelled by y = 95 − 3x.

**(a)** Estimate the percentage remaining after 15 months. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the model to estimate the percentage remaining after 40 months. **[1]**

**(c)** Comment on your answer to (b). **[2]**

**(d)** Interpret the gradient in context. **[1]**

**11.** Six pairs of data have mean point (8, 30). Five of the y values are 22, 25, 28, 34 and 37.

**(a)** Find the sixth y value. **[2]**

**(b)** A line of best fit is drawn through the mean point and the point (12, 40). Find its equation. **[3]**

**12.** A data set has an even number, 2k, of pairs. They are ordered by x and split into two halves of k pairs each.

**(a)** Give one advantage of the method of semi-averages over drawing a line by eye. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that the midpoint of the two semi-average points is the overall mean point. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** x̄ = 30 ÷ 5 = 6 [1], ȳ = 75 ÷ 5 = 15, so the mean point is **(6, 15)** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** m = (10 − 40) ÷ (25 − 5) = −1.5 [1]. c = 40 − (−1.5 × 5) = 47.5 [1]. **y = 47.5 − 1.5x** [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Gradient: each extra kilogram adds about **$0.50** to the charge [1]. Intercept: a fixed charge of about **$12** on top of the charge per kilogram; this is sensible because 0 kg is close to the smallest mass of 1 kg [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Interpolation is estimating a value **inside** the range of the data [1]; extrapolation is estimating **outside** that range, where the pattern may not continue [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** m = (40 − 16) ÷ (11 − 3) = 3, c = 16 − 9 = 7 [1], so **y = 3x + 7** [1].

**(b)** 3 × 7 + 7 = **28** [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** Ordered by x, lower half: (3, 24), (5, 30), (7, 37), (9, 41); upper half: (11, 48), (12, 52), (14, 56), (15, 60) [1]. Lower point: (24 ÷ 4, 132 ÷ 4) = **(6, 33)** [1] [1]. Upper point: (52 ÷ 4, 216 ÷ 4) = **(13, 54)** [1].

**(b)** m = (54 − 33) ÷ (13 − 6) = 21 ÷ 7 = 3 [1]. c = 33 − 3 × 6 = 15 [1]. **y = 3x + 15** [1].

**(c)** Mean point (76 ÷ 8, 348 ÷ 8) = (9.5, 43.5) [1]. 3 × 9.5 + 15 = 43.5, so it lies on the line [1].

**(d)** 3 × 10 + 15 = **45 minutes** [1]. Each extra kilometre adds about 3 minutes to the delivery time [1]. **[11]**
*Examiner insight:* Splitting the table in the order printed, before sorting, is the most expensive error here; every later mark depends on the halves.

**7. (a)** **(7, 22)** is left out [1], because there are seven pairs and each half must contain the same number, three [1].

**(b)** Lower: (9 ÷ 3, 39 ÷ 3) = **(3, 13)** [1]. Upper: (33 ÷ 3, 87 ÷ 3) = **(11, 29)** [1].

**(c)** m = 16 ÷ 8 = 2 [1], c = 13 − 6 = 7, so **y = 2x + 7** [1].

**(d)** 2 × 30 + 7 = **$67** [1]. Unreliable, because 30 years is far outside the data range of 1 to 13 years, so pay may not keep rising at the same rate [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** The axis does not start at zero, so the height of the line at the left edge (x = 40) is not the value of y when x = 0 [1].

**(b)** m = (38 − 18) ÷ (80 − 40) = 0.5 [1]. c = 18 − 0.5 × 40 = −2 [1]. **y = 0.5x − 2** [1]. **[4]**

**9.** The estimate at x = 30 is interpolation, inside the range 10 to 50 [1], and with strong correlation and 30 points it should be reasonably reliable [1]. The estimate at x = 70 is extrapolation [1], so it is less reliable because the relationship may change outside the observed range [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 95 − 45 = **50%** [1].

**(b)** 95 − 120 = **−25%** [1].

**(c)** A negative percentage is impossible [1]. 40 months is outside the range of 2 to 24 months, so this is extrapolation and the model does not apply [1].

**(d)** About **3 percentage points** of battery life are lost each month [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Total of y = 6 × 30 = 180 [1]. Sixth value = 180 − 146 = **34** [1].

**(b)** m = (40 − 30) ÷ (12 − 8) = 2.5 [1]. c = 30 − 2.5 × 8 = 10 [1]. **y = 2.5x + 10** [1]. **[5]**

**12. (a)** It gives the same line for everyone using the same data, whereas lines by eye vary between people [1].

**(b)** The semi-average x values are (sum of lower x) ÷ k and (sum of upper x) ÷ k [1]. Their mean is (sum of lower x + sum of upper x) ÷ 2k [1] = Σx ÷ 2k = x̄, and the same argument for y gives ȳ [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forming the halves before sorting by x.
- Including the middle pair when the number of pairs is odd.
- Finding the gradient from data points rather than from points on the line.
- Reading the intercept from an axis that does not start at zero.
- Interpreting the gradient without units.
- Calling an extrapolated or impossible estimate reliable.
