These notes condense sub-topic **3.2 Variations in economic activity: aggregate demand and aggregate supply** from the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022). It is SL and HL content. For full explanations and a worked equilibrium read the [3.2 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-2/); then test yourself with the [3.2 practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-3-2-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aggregate demand (AD) | Total planned spending on final output at each price level: C + I + G + (X - M) |
| Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) | Real output firms supply at each price level with resource prices fixed |
| Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) | Output when all prices and wages have adjusted; vertical at potential output in the monetarist/new classical view |
| Keynesian AS curve | AS curve with horizontal, upward-sloping and vertical sections |
| Potential output | Output when resources are fully employed at the natural rate of unemployment |
| Short-run equilibrium | Where AD meets SRAS |
| Long-run equilibrium | Where AD, SRAS and LRAS meet, at potential output |
| Recessionary (deflationary) gap | Equilibrium output below potential output |
| Inflationary gap | Equilibrium output above potential output |
| Supply shock | A sudden change in costs that shifts SRAS |

## AD in brief

- **Slope:** wealth effect, interest rate effect, international trade effect.
- **Movement along:** change in the price level only.
- **Shifts:** any change in C, I, G or (X - M) at a given price level.

| Component | Rightward shift triggers |
|---|---|
| C | Higher income, wealth or confidence; lower interest rates or income tax |
| I | Lower interest rates; higher business confidence; new technology; lower corporate tax |
| G | Government decision to spend more |
| X - M | Higher foreign incomes; depreciation; lower trade barriers abroad |

## SRAS in brief

- Slopes upwards because wages and resource prices are fixed in the short run.
- **Shifts left:** higher wages, energy or raw material prices; higher indirect taxes; lower subsidies; negative supply shocks.
- **Shifts right:** the reverse.

## Two views of the long run

| | Monetarist/new classical | Keynesian |
|---|---|---|
| Wages and prices | Flexible in the long run | Sticky downwards |
| Long-run AS | Vertical at Yp | Horizontal, rising, then vertical |
| Recessionary gap | Self-corrects: wages fall, SRAS shifts right | Can persist; AD must rise |
| Role for AD policy | Limited; affects only prices in the long run | Important when there is spare capacity |

**LRAS shifts right** with more or better factors of production, better technology and improved institutions.

## Method: equilibrium from a table

1. Find the price level where AD equals SRAS: that is short-run equilibrium.
2. Compare equilibrium output with potential output to name the gap.
3. For a shift, add or subtract the change at every price level and repeat step 1.
4. Report the change in the price level as a percentage and the new gap.

**Worked reminder.**

| Price level | AD | SRAS | AD after a fall in spending |
|---|---|---|---|
| 90 | 300 | 250 | 275 |
| 95 | 290 | 265 | 265 |
| 100 | 280 | 280 | 255 |
| 105 | 270 | 295 | 245 |
| 110 | 260 | 310 | 235 |

Initial equilibrium: price level 100, output 280. With potential output of 270, there is an **inflationary gap of 10**. After AD falls by 25, equilibrium is at price level 95 and output 265: the price level falls by 5%, and there is now a **recessionary gap of 5**.

## Diagram checklist

- Axes: **price level** and **real output** (or real GDP).
- Curves labelled AD1, AD2, SRAS1, SRAS2, LRAS (or Keynesian AS).
- Yp or Yf marked; P1, P2, Y1, Y2 with dotted lines.
- Gap bracketed and named.
- Arrows showing the direction of each shift.

## Exam traps

- Axes labelled "price" and "quantity".
- Shifting AD because the price level changed.
- Treating a wage rise as an AD shift (it shifts SRAS).
- Saying a recessionary gap always self-corrects: that is only the monetarist/new classical view.
- Drawing a Keynesian AS curve but describing self-correction.
- Forgetting that AD policy on the vertical section only raises prices.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the AD equation.
2. Give the three reasons AD slopes downwards.
3. What happens to AD if business confidence falls?
4. What happens to SRAS if oil prices rise sharply?
5. Why is LRAS vertical in the monetarist/new classical view?
6. Describe the three sections of the Keynesian AS curve.
7. Define a recessionary gap.
8. Explain how a recessionary gap closes in the monetarist/new classical view.
9. Why might a Keynesian expect a recessionary gap to persist?
10. Give two factors that shift LRAS to the right.
11. In the worked reminder, what would happen to the gap if AD had fallen by only 10?
12. On the vertical section of the Keynesian AS curve, what happens if AD rises?

### Answers

1. AD = C + I + G + (X - M).
2. Wealth effect; interest rate effect; international trade effect.
3. Investment falls, so AD shifts left.
4. Firms' costs rise, so SRAS shifts left (a negative supply shock).
5. In the long run wages and prices adjust fully, so output is set by resources and technology, not the price level.
6. Horizontal (spare capacity, output rises without price rises); upward-sloping (bottlenecks, prices rise with output); vertical (full capacity, only prices rise).
7. Equilibrium real output below potential output, with unemployment above its natural rate.
8. Unemployment pushes money wages and resource prices down, SRAS shifts right, and output returns to potential at a lower price level.
9. Money wages are sticky downwards, so SRAS does not shift right and output stays below full employment.
10. Any two: more labour; better education and training; more capital; better technology; improved institutions.
11. New AD: 290, 280, 270, 260, 250. AD = SRAS lies between price levels 95 and 100; treating the schedules as straight lines, it is at a price level of about 98 and output of about 274. Output stays above 270, so a smaller inflationary gap of about 4 remains.
12. Only the price level rises; real output does not change.
