This guide covers **6.1, Communication**, the first subtopic of Topic
6, ICT applications, in Cambridge IGCSE Information and Communication
Technology 0417. It follows [Topic 5, The effects of using
IT](/resources/igcse-ict-effects-of-using-it/), and moves from IT's
general social effects into the specific communication media and
devices used to reach an audience.

## Communication media

You need to know the characteristics and uses of a named set of
communication media: newsletters, posters, websites, multimedia
presentations, audio, video, media streaming, and ePublications. These
fall into two useful groups when revising. **Static/print-style media**
-- newsletters and posters -- suit delivering a fixed message to a
defined audience without needing a device to view it (a poster on a
notice board, a newsletter distributed by email or print), but cannot
be updated once distributed and offer no interactivity. **Digital/
interactive media** -- websites, multimedia presentations, audio,
video, media streaming and ePublications -- can combine text, images,
sound and moving image, can typically be updated after publication
(unlike a printed poster), and often allow the audience some degree of
interaction (navigating a website, pausing a video), but require a
suitable device and, for streaming specifically, a working internet
connection to access at all.

A key distinction worth learning precisely: **media streaming** delivers
audio or video content continuously over the internet without
requiring the full file to be downloaded first, whereas an
**ePublication** (an electronic book, magazine or document) is
typically downloaded as a complete file and can then be accessed
offline. This affects a genuinely examinable trade-off: streaming
needs an ongoing internet connection but no local storage space for
the full file, while an ePublication needs storage space but, once
downloaded, no ongoing connection.

## A worked comparison: newsletter vs website vs multimedia presentation

A school wants to communicate its termly achievements to parents.
Choosing between the three media illustrates the trade-offs above. A
**newsletter** reaches parents without internet access or a
particular device, but is fixed at the moment of distribution and
cannot be corrected or updated afterward, and printing and
distributing it has an ongoing material cost. A **website** can be
updated at any time, can include far more content than fits on a
printed page, and can link out to further detail, but only reaches
parents who have a suitable device and internet access, and requires
someone to actively visit it rather than having it arrive directly.
A **multimedia presentation** (combining text, images, audio and
video) suits a scenario where the school wants to deliver a richer,
more engaging summary at an in-person event such as a parents'
evening, but is far less practical for parents to access
independently afterward compared with a website. None of the three is
unconditionally best -- the right choice depends on the audience's
access to technology, whether the content needs to be updated later,
and whether delivery is passive (arrives to the audience) or active
(the audience must seek it out).

## Mobile communication

You need to know the use of mobile devices for communication,
covering SMS messaging, phone calls, Voice over Internet Protocol
(VoIP), video calls, and accessing the internet. SMS messaging sends
short text messages over a mobile network. Traditional phone calls use
the mobile network's voice channel directly. **VoIP** is distinct from
a traditional phone call in a specific, examinable way: it carries
voice communication as data over the internet rather than over a
dedicated voice channel, which is why VoIP calls typically require a
working internet or wi-fi connection and can be significantly cheaper
than a standard call, especially internationally, but depend on
connection quality in a way a traditional voice call over a mobile
network does not. Video calls extend this same internet-based
approach to include a live video feed alongside audio.

## How to approach it

When comparing communication media, always weigh a specific trade-off
rather than declaring one medium simply "better" -- a poster's key
advantage (no device or internet connection needed to view it) is also
exactly why it cannot be updated once printed, while a website's
updatability is only available to someone with a suitable device and
connection. For the streaming-versus-ePublication distinction, anchor
your answer to what happens to the data: streaming plays data as it
arrives without storing the whole file, while an ePublication is
downloaded and stored in full before, or as, it is used -- this single
fact answers most "characteristics and uses" questions on the pair.
For mobile communication, be ready to explain specifically why VoIP
differs from a standard phone call (data over the internet rather than
a dedicated voice channel), since this distinction, not just naming
VoIP as an option, is what exam questions in this subtopic typically
test.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is an ePublication?**
In Cambridge IGCSE ICT (0417), an ePublication is an electronic book, magazine or document that is typically downloaded as a complete file and can then be read offline. It needs storage space but no ongoing internet connection once downloaded.

**Do you need an internet connection to stream media?**
Yes. Media streaming delivers audio or video continuously over the internet without downloading the full file first, so it needs an ongoing connection but no local storage for the whole file.

**What is VoIP?**
VoIP, Voice over Internet Protocol, carries voice communication as data over the internet rather than over a dedicated voice channel. It needs a working internet or wi-fi connection and depends on connection quality.

**What are the disadvantages of a printed newsletter?**
A printed newsletter is fixed once distributed, so it cannot be corrected or updated, and printing and distributing it has an ongoing material cost. Its advantage is that readers need no device or internet access.
