These questions cover section **3.3.11.3 Nucleophilic properties** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Nucleophilic properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amine-nucleophilic-properties/) and the [Amines revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-amines-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Br = 79.9.

## Questions

**1.** Explain why ammonia and amines act as nucleophiles. **[2]**

**2.** Bromoethane reacts with ammonia.

**(a)** Write an equation for the formation of ethylamine, using 2 moles of ammonia. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of ethylamine with a second molecule of bromoethane to form diethylamine and HBr. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this second reaction can occur. **[1]**

**3.** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of ammonia with 1-bromopropane to form propylamine. **[4]**

**4.** Triethylamine, (C₂H₅)₃N, reacts with excess bromoethane.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the type of product formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this product does not react with any more bromoethane. **[1]**

**5.** State the conditions that favour formation of the primary amine when a halogenoalkane reacts with ammonia, and the conditions that favour a quaternary ammonium salt. **[2]**

**6.** The compound CH₃(CH₂)₁₅N⁺(CH₃)₃ Br⁻ is used in fabric conditioners.

**(a)** State the class of compound. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how its structure makes it effective as a cationic surfactant. **[3]**

**7.** Ethanoyl chloride reacts with methylamine.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why two moles of methylamine are used for each mole of ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**8.** Outline the mechanism for the reaction of ethanoyl chloride with ethylamine, CH₃CH₂NH₂. **[5]**

**9.** The amide formed in question 8 does not react with another molecule of ethanoyl chloride under the same conditions, but the amine formed from a halogenoalkane and ammonia does react with another halogenoalkane. Explain the difference. **[2]**

**10.** 2.02 g of triethylamine (Mᵣ = 101.0) reacts completely with excess bromoethane to form tetraethylammonium bromide (Mᵣ = 209.9). Calculate the mass of salt formed. **[3]**

**11.** 4.50 g of ethylamine (Mᵣ = 45.0) reacts with excess ethanoyl chloride to form N-ethylethanamide (Mᵣ = 87.0). The mass obtained is 6.09 g. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why the reaction of a halogenoalkane with ammonia gives a mixture of products, and suggest how the proportion of primary amine can be increased. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The nitrogen atom has a lone pair of electrons [1]; the lone pair can be donated to an electron-deficient (δ+) carbon atom to form a new covalent bond [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** CH₃CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br [1]

**(b)** CH₃CH₂NH₂ + CH₃CH₂Br → (CH₃CH₂)₂NH + HBr [1]

**(c)** The primary amine still has a lone pair on nitrogen, so it is also a nucleophile [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Arrow from the lone pair on N of NH₃ to the δ+ carbon [1]; arrow from the C–Br bond to Br [1]; intermediate CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺ and Br⁻ shown [1]; a second NH₃ removes H⁺ giving CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ and NH₄⁺ [1]. **[4]**

**4. (a)** (CH₃CH₂)₃N + CH₃CH₂Br → (CH₃CH₂)₄N⁺Br⁻ [1]

**(b)** A quaternary ammonium salt [1]

**(c)** The nitrogen has no lone pair, because all four of its bonds are used, so it cannot act as a nucleophile [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A large excess of ammonia favours the primary amine [1]; an excess of halogenoalkane favours the quaternary ammonium salt [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** A quaternary ammonium salt [1]

**(b)** The ion has a positively charged nitrogen head, which is hydrophilic and attracted to negatively charged fabric surfaces [1]; the long hydrocarbon chain is non-polar and hydrophobic [1]; the tails stick out from the surface, giving a soft feel, and the positive head makes it a cationic surfactant [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** CH₃COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]

**(b)** N-methylethanamide [1]

**(c)** One molecule acts as the nucleophile and the second removes H⁺ from the intermediate [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Arrow from the lone pair on N to the δ+ carbon of C=O [1]; arrow from the C=O bond onto O [1]; intermediate with O⁻ and N⁺H₂CH₂CH₃ on the same carbon as CH₃ and Cl [1]; arrow from the lone pair on O⁻ to reform C=O, and arrow from C–Cl onto Cl, giving Cl⁻ [1]; loss of H⁺ from N, giving CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ [1]. **[5]**

**9.** In the amide, the nitrogen lone pair is drawn towards the C=O group, so it is less available [1]; the amide is therefore a much weaker nucleophile than an amine [1]. **[2]**

**10.** n(triethylamine) = 2.02 ÷ 101.0 = 0.0200 mol [1]; n(salt) = 0.0200 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.0200 × 209.9 = **4.20 g** [1]. **[3]**

**11.** n(ethylamine) = 4.50 ÷ 45.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.100 × 87.0 = 8.70 g [1]; percentage yield = 6.09 ÷ 8.70 × 100 = **70.0 %** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The primary amine formed is also a nucleophile and can react with more halogenoalkane [1]; so secondary and tertiary amines and a quaternary ammonium salt can also form [1]; using a large excess of ammonia makes it more likely that the halogenoalkane meets ammonia rather than an amine [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* "Mixture of products" needs the reason: each product still has a lone pair on nitrogen, so it can keep reacting.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Starting the curly arrow from the N atom rather than from its lone pair.
- Drawing a lone pair on the quaternary ammonium ion.
- Forgetting the second mole of amine in acylation reactions.
- Describing the surfactant without the long non-polar tail.
- Missing the N- prefix in the amide name.
- Not saying why the substitution continues beyond the primary amine.
