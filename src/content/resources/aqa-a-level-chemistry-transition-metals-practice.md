## Questions

**1.** Define the terms *ligand*, *complex* and *co-ordination number*. **[3]**

**2.** Use electron configurations to explain why copper shows transition metal properties but zinc does not. **[2]**

**3.** This question is about ligand substitution.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction of [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with excess ammonia, and explain why the co-ordination number does not change. **[2]**
**(b)** Write an equation for the reaction of [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ with concentrated hydrochloric acid. State the change in shape and explain the change in co-ordination number. **[3]**
**(c)** With excess aqueous ammonia, [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ forms [Cu(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺. State its co-ordination number and why this is called incomplete substitution. **[2]**

**4.** EDTA⁴⁻ reacts with hexaaquacopper(II) ions:

[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + EDTA⁴⁻ → [Cu(EDTA)]²⁻ + 6H₂O

For this question take ΔH = −5.0 kJ mol⁻¹ and ΔS = +150 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Explain the signs of ΔH and ΔS, calculate ΔG at 298 K, and state what it shows. **[5]**

**5.** This question is about shapes of complexes.

**(a)** [Co(NH₃)₄Cl₂]⁺ is octahedral. Name the type of isomerism it shows and give the Cl–Co–Cl angle in each isomer. **[2]**
**(b)** State the co-ordination number of [Ni(H₂NCH₂CH₂NH₂)₃]²⁺ and the type of stereoisomerism it shows. **[2]**
**(c)** State the shape and bond angle of the silver complex in Tollens' reagent. **[1]**

**6.** Haemoglobin contains haem.

**(a)** Name the type of bond between oxygen and iron in haemoglobin, and give the oxidation state of iron. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why carbon monoxide is toxic. **[1]**

**7.** A complex ion absorbs light most strongly at 510 nm. h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹, L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate ΔE for one ion. **[2]**
**(b)** Convert your answer to kJ mol⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why [Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ and [CoCl₄]²⁻ have different colours. **[2]**

**8.** A student finds the concentration of copper(II) ions by colorimetry. Standards give a straight line through the origin; a 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ standard has absorbance 0.150.

**(a)** State how the student should choose the filter. **[1]**
**(b)** Why is the colorimeter zeroed with a blank? **[1]**
**(c)** The unknown has absorbance 0.390. Calculate its concentration. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why the unknown must be treated with the same reagents as the standards. **[1]**

**9.** Zinc and dilute acid are added to a solution of ammonium vanadate(V).

**(a)** Give the formula and colour of the vanadium species in oxidation states +5 and +2. **[2]**
**(b)** Write a half-equation for the reduction of VO₂⁺ to VO²⁺, and an overall equation for its reduction by zinc. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why the flask is loosely stoppered to keep air out once V²⁺ forms. **[1]**

**10.** 2.016 g of hydrated ethanedioic acid, H₂C₂O₄·xH₂O, is dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions are acidified, warmed and titrated with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ KMnO₄. The mean titre is 32.00 cm³. Mr of H₂C₂O₄ = 90.0; Mr of H₂O = 18.0.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why no indicator is added. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the value of x. **[5]**
**(d)** Explain why hydrochloric acid is not used to acidify the mixture. **[1]**

**11.** This question is about catalysis by transition metals.

**(a)** Distinguish between heterogeneous and homogeneous catalysts. **[2]**
**(b)** Write two equations to show how V₂O₅ acts as a catalyst in the Contact process, and explain the role of variable oxidation state. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the uncatalysed reaction of S₂O₈²⁻ with I⁻ is slow, and write two equations to show how Fe²⁺ catalyses it. **[3]**
**(d)** In the reaction of C₂O₄²⁻ with acidified MnO₄⁻, identify the autocatalyst and describe how the rate changes from the start of the reaction. **[2]**

**12.** Heterogeneous catalysts are often spread on a support and can be poisoned.

**(a)** Give the purpose of a support medium. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how poisoning affects a catalyst and why it matters to a manufacturer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Ligand: a molecule or ion that donates a pair of electrons to form a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal [1]. Complex: a central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands [1]. Co-ordination number: the number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Number of ligands" scores nothing for co-ordination number, because bidentate ligands make two bonds each.

**2.** Cu²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁹, an incomplete d sub-level [1]; Zn²⁺ is [Ar] 3d¹⁰ (and Zn is 3d¹⁰ 4s²), so no atom or ion has an incomplete d sub-level [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* You must refer to the ions; quoting only the atoms' configurations does not explain copper's behaviour.

**3. (a)** **[Co(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 6NH₃ → [Co(NH₃)₆]²⁺ + 6H₂O** [1]; NH₃ and H₂O are similar in size and both uncharged [1]
**(b)** **[Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ + 4Cl⁻ → [CuCl₄]²⁻ + 6H₂O** [1]; octahedral to tetrahedral [1]; Cl⁻ is larger, so only four fit, and the co-ordination number falls from 6 to 4 [1]
**(c)** **6** [1]; only four of the six water ligands are replaced [1]
*Examiner insight:* Check that the complex charge balances in each equation; a wrong charge on [CuCl₄]²⁻ normally loses the equation mark.

**4.** Particles increase from 2 to 7 [1], so ΔS is positive [1]. ΔH is small because similar numbers of similar co-ordinate bonds are broken and made [1]. ΔG = −5.0 − (298 × 0.150) = **−49.7 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. ΔG is negative, so the reaction is feasible: the chelate effect is entropy-driven [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Forgetting to divide ΔS by 1000 gives a huge ΔG; the method mark may still be allowed, but the answer mark is lost.

**5. (a)** cis–trans isomerism [1]; **90° (cis) and 180° (trans)** [1]
**(b)** **6** [1]; optical isomerism [1]
**(c)** **Linear, 180°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (b) "3" is wrong: en is bidentate, so count six co-ordinate bonds.

**6. (a)** Co-ordinate (dative covalent) bond [1]; **+2** [1]
**(b)** CO replaces the oxygen co-ordinately bonded to Fe(II), so less oxygen is transported [1]
*Examiner insight:* "CO binds to haemoglobin" alone is not enough; the mark needs the idea of replacing O₂ at Fe(II).

**7. (a)** ΔE = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸)/(510 × 10⁻⁹) [1] = **3.90 × 10⁻¹⁹ J** [1]
**(b)** 3.90 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ = **235 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Different ligand and co-ordination number change ΔE between d-electron ground and excited states [1], so a different wavelength is absorbed and different light is transmitted [1]
*Examiner insight:* An error carried from (a) into (b) is normally allowed as error carried forward, so show the conversion clearly.

**8. (a)** Use the colour of light the solution absorbs most [1]
**(b)** So that absorbance due to the solvent and cuvette is removed, measuring only the copper(II) ions [1]
**(c)** Gradient = 0.150/0.0200 = 7.50 [1]; c = 0.390/7.50 = **0.0520 mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(d)** The coloured species and its absorbance depend on the ligand, so it must match the calibration [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the unit mol dm⁻³ is needed with the number for the final mark.

**9. (a)** **VO₂⁺, yellow** (+5) [1]; **V²⁺, violet** (+2) [1]
**(b)** VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → VO²⁺ + H₂O [1]; **2VO₂⁺ + 4H⁺ + Zn → 2VO²⁺ + Zn²⁺ + 2H₂O** [1]
**(c)** V²⁺ is readily oxidised by oxygen in air back to higher oxidation states [1]
*Examiner insight:* VO₂⁺ and VO²⁺ are easily swapped; a wrong formula loses the mark even if the colour is right.

**10. (a)** **2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂** [1]
**(b)** MnO₄⁻ is purple and Mn²⁺ almost colourless, so the first permanent pink shows the end point [1]
**(c)** n(MnO₄⁻) = 0.0200 × 32.00/1000 = 6.40 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; n(C₂O₄²⁻) = 6.40 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.60 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; in 250 cm³: 1.60 × 10⁻² mol [1]; Mr = 2.016/0.0160 = 126.0 [1]; x = (126.0 − 90.0)/18.0 = **2** [1]
**(d)** MnO₄⁻ would oxidise Cl⁻ to chlorine, so the titre would be too large [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using a 1 : 5 or 5 : 2 ratio here is a common slip; examiners can still credit later steps by error carried forward, but not the ratio mark.

**11. (a)** Heterogeneous: different phase from reactants, reaction at active sites on the surface [1]. Homogeneous: same phase, reaction through an intermediate species [1]
**(b)** SO₂ + V₂O₅ → SO₃ + V₂O₄ [1]; V₂O₄ + ½O₂ → V₂O₅ [1]; V is reduced from +5 to +4 and reoxidised, so the catalyst is regenerated [1]
**(c)** Both ions are negative and repel, so activation energy is high [1]; S₂O₈²⁻ + 2Fe²⁺ → 2SO₄²⁻ + 2Fe³⁺ [1]; 2Fe³⁺ + 2I⁻ → 2Fe²⁺ + I₂ [1]
**(d)** **Mn²⁺** [1]; slow at first, speeds up as Mn²⁺ forms, then slows as reactants are used up [1]
*Examiner insight:* For (b) and (c) the two equations must add up to the overall reaction; if the catalyst does not cancel, expect to lose a mark.

**12. (a)** Maximises the surface area of catalyst and minimises the amount (cost) needed [1]
**(b)** Impurities block the active sites, reducing efficiency [1]; the catalyst must be cleaned or replaced, or the feed purified, which adds cost [1]
*Examiner insight:* "Poison stops the catalyst" is too vague; name the active sites being blocked.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting ligands instead of co-ordinate bonds when asked for co-ordination number.
- Writing tetrahedral complexes with six Cl⁻ ligands, or missing the overall charge.
- Explaining the chelate effect with enthalpy alone.
- Giving cis and trans angles the wrong way round.
- Using λ in nm in hc/λ.
- Mixing up the MnO₄⁻ ratios: 1 : 5 for Fe²⁺, 2 : 5 for C₂O₄²⁻ and H₂O₂.
- Forgetting to scale from the titrated portion to the full volumetric flask.
- Writing catalyst equations in which the catalyst is not regenerated.
