Quick recall for section 1.6, Government objectives and policies, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [government objectives and policies study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-government-objectives-and-policies/); exam-style questions are in the [government objectives and policies practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-government-objectives-and-policies-practice/). Definitions are standard ones; the specification lists these points without defining them. Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists

- 1.6.1 Government spending: to provide public service; taxation and constraints on public spending.
- 1.6.2 How governments can affect business activity: infrastructure provision; legislation; trade policy (membership of trading blocs, tariffs).
- 1.6.3 The effect of interest rates on businesses and on consumer spending.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Public services | Services paid for from public funds, such as health, education and policing |
| Taxation | Money the government raises from businesses and individuals |
| Constraints on public spending | Limits on what a government can afford: tax revenue, borrowing, competing demands |
| Infrastructure | Roads, railways, ports, airports, energy, water and communications networks |
| Legislation | Laws passed by the government |
| Trading bloc | Countries that trade with each other on easier terms |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |
| Interest rate | The cost of borrowing and the reward for saving |

## Effects on a business: the four channels

Every answer should land on at least one of these: **costs**, **revenue or demand**, **prices**, **plans** (investment, location, hiring).

| Policy | Typical effect |
|---|---|
| More public spending | More orders for suppliers of public services |
| Spending cuts | Lost contracts; demand falls in affected sectors |
| Higher profit tax | Less retained profit for investment or dividends |
| Higher income tax | Customers have less to spend |
| Higher sales tax | Higher prices to customers; fewer units sold |
| Better infrastructure | Lower transport costs; wider market; site may become attractive |
| New legislation | Compliance costs; product or process changes; fairer competition |
| Tariff on imports | Domestic rivals of importers gain; importers of parts face higher costs |
| Joining a trading bloc | Tariff-free access to a larger market; more competition at home |
| Higher interest rates | Dearer loans and overdrafts; investment delayed; credit-financed spending falls |

## Reminders

- Profit 200,000; profit tax 20%: tax 40,000; 160,000 left.
- Compliance cost 12,000 a year: 4.00 per unit at 3,000 units; 0.04 at 300,000 units.
- Loan 80,000; rate rises from 5% to 7%: interest rises from 4,000 to 5,600, up 1,600 (a 40% rise).
- Imported part 50; tariff 10%: 55.

## Tariff winners and losers

- Win: domestic producers that compete with imports; the government (revenue).
- Lose: businesses importing materials or parts; consumers paying higher prices; exporters if other countries respond with tariffs.

## Constraints on public spending

1. Tax revenue is limited and falls when the economy weakens.
2. Higher taxes are unpopular.
3. Borrowing must be repaid with interest.
4. Opportunity cost: spending on one service means less for another.

## Who is hurt most by higher interest rates?

- Businesses with large loans or overdrafts.
- Businesses planning to borrow to invest.
- Sellers of goods often bought on credit: cars, furniture, holidays, houses.
- Least affected: businesses with savings and little debt; sellers of everyday necessities.

## Small and large businesses

- Paper 1: overdraft interest, local infrastructure, compliance costs spread over few sales, public contracts from local services.
- Paper 2: tariffs and trading blocs, profit tax on large profits, national infrastructure, effect of rates on mass-market demand.

## Exam traps

- "A tariff is a tax on exports."
- "Tariffs harm all domestic businesses."
- Describing a policy with no effect on the business named.
- Percentage points confused with percentage change (5% to 7% is 2 points, but a 40% rise in interest).
- Assuming every business suffers equally from higher rates.
- Forgetting the government can be a business's customer.
- Naming specific laws instead of describing legislation in general terms.

## Self-test

1. Give two public services a government provides.
2. Name one constraint on public spending.
3. A business makes a profit of 90,000 and pays tax at 25%. Calculate profit after tax.
4. How can a tax on workers' incomes affect a shop?
5. Define infrastructure.
6. Give one benefit to a business of a new railway line.
7. Why can a new law cost a small business more per unit than a large one?
8. Define tariff.
9. Which business gains from a tariff on imported shoes: a domestic shoe maker or a shoe importer?
10. Give one benefit and one drawback to a business of its country joining a trading bloc.
11. A loan of 60,000 has interest at 6%. The rate rises to 8%. Calculate the increase in yearly interest.
12. Explain why a car dealer may be hit harder by rising interest rates than a supermarket.

### Answers

1. Any two of: health care, education, policing, roads, defence.
2. Limited tax revenue (or borrowing must be repaid; or opportunity cost).
3. 90,000 × 75% = 67,500.
4. Customers have less income to spend, so the shop's sales may fall.
5. The roads, railways, ports, airports, energy, water and communications networks businesses rely on.
6. Lower transport costs or faster deliveries (or access to more customers or workers).
7. Compliance costs are similar for any size, so they are spread over fewer units.
8. A tax on imported goods.
9. The domestic shoe maker.
10. Benefit: tariff-free access to more customers. Drawback: more competition from firms in member countries.
11. 60,000 × 2% = 1,200.
12. Many people buy cars on credit, which becomes dearer, so car sales fall; food is a necessity, so supermarket spending changes little.
