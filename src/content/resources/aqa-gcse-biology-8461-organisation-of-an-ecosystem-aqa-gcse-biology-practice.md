These questions cover section **4.7.2 Organisation of an ecosystem** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461)
specification, for first teaching from 2016. Questions 4, 6, 10 and 11 draw on 4.7.2.3, which is biology only.
Question 13 is **Higher tier only** (4.7.2.4). All data are invented for practice. A calculator is useful for
questions 3, 7, 10 and 11.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A food chain in the Southern Ocean is: phytoplankton → krill → penguin → leopard seal.

**(a)** Name the producer and the secondary consumer. **[2]**
**(b)** State what the arrows in a food chain represent. **[1]**

**2.** Explain the difference between the abundance and the distribution of a species, and name the sampling
technique used to study each. **[2]**

**3.** A student counts clover plants in eight quadrats: 2, 5, 0, 3, 5, 7, 5, 1.

Calculate the mean, and state the median and the mode. **[3]**

**4.** Name the gas produced when material decays anaerobically, and give **one** use of it. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how water that evaporates from the sea can become available to plants on land. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why turning a compost heap regularly speeds up decay. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student estimates the number of plantain plants on a playing field 50 m long and 30 m wide. She uses 15
quadrats, each 0.5 m × 0.5 m, and counts a total of 42 plantains.

**(a)** Estimate the number of plantains on the field. Show your working. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe how she should decide where to place the quadrats. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **one** reason her estimate may not be accurate. **[1]**

**8.** A transect runs up a rocky shore from the low-tide line. At each point a student records the percentage
cover of a seaweed and the time the rock is out of the water each day.

| Distance up shore (m) | 0 | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time out of water (hours per day) | 2 | 5 | 9 | 14 | 20 |
| Seaweed cover (%) | 85 | 60 | 30 | 10 | 0 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between time out of water and seaweed cover. Use data in your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest an explanation for the pattern. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the student could estimate the percentage cover in a quadrat. **[1]**

**9.** A gardener counts aphids and the ladybirds that eat them on rose plants each week.

| Week | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aphids per plant | 20 | 60 | 140 | 90 | 30 | 25 | 70 | 120 |
| Ladybirds per 10 plants | 2 | 3 | 6 | 12 | 9 | 4 | 3 | 6 |

**(a)** Give the week in which each population first peaks. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why ladybird numbers rose between weeks 2 and 4. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why aphid numbers fell between weeks 3 and 5. **[1]**
**(d)** Predict how the ladybird population will change after week 8, and give a reason. **[1]**

**10.** Four mesh bags, each holding 50 g of dead leaves, are left for 10 weeks in different conditions.

| Bag | Conditions | Mass after 10 weeks (g) |
|---|---|---|
| A | Warm, moist, with oxygen | 18 |
| B | Warm, dry, with oxygen | 41 |
| C | Cold, moist, with oxygen | 36 |
| D | Warm, waterlogged, no oxygen | 39 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of decay in bag A. Give the unit. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why bag A decayed faster than bag B and faster than bag D. **[4]**

**11.** In Required practical 10, a student times how long milk with lipase and phenolphthalein takes to turn
from pink to colourless at different temperatures.

| Temperature (°C) | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Time (s) | 600 | 300 | 150 | 100 | 400 |

**(a)** Calculate the rate at 30 °C using rate = 1 ÷ time. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the trend in time from 10 °C to 40 °C. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the result at 50 °C. **[2]**
**(d)** Give **one** variable the student must keep the same. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how a carbon atom in carbon dioxide in the air could become part of a rabbit, and then be returned
to the air. **[6]**

**13.** (Higher tier only) Over 50 years, the lowest altitude at which a mountain flower grows rose from 600 m
to 850 m. Mean summer temperature in the area rose by 1.1 °C. Over the same period, sheep grazing on the lower
slopes increased. Evaluate the claim that the rise in temperature caused the change in the flower's
distribution. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Producer: phytoplankton [1]; secondary consumer: penguin [1]
**(b)** The direction in which biomass (food) passes: "is eaten by" [1]

**2.** Abundance is how many individuals there are, studied with randomly placed quadrats [1]; distribution is
where the species is found or how it changes across a habitat, studied with a transect [1]

**3.** Mean = 28 ÷ 8 = **3.5** [1]; median: in order 0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 5, 5, 7, so (3 + 5) ÷ 2 = **4** [1];
mode = **5** [1]

**4.** Methane [1]; used as a fuel (biogas) [1]

**5.** Water vapour rises and cools [1]; it condenses to form clouds [1]; it falls as precipitation (rain) on
land, where plants absorb it from the soil [1]

**6.** Turning mixes in air, so more oxygen is available [1]; so decomposers respire aerobically and break down
the material faster [1]

**7. (a)** Mean = 42 ÷ 15 = 2.8 per quadrat [1]; quadrat area 0.25 m², so 2.8 ÷ 0.25 = 11.2 per m² [1];
field area 1500 m², so 11.2 × 1500 = **16 800 plantains** [1]
**(b)** Lay two tapes at right angles along the edges of the field [1]; use a random number generator to give
coordinates for each quadrat [1]
**(c)** Any one: 15 quadrats is a small sample; plantains may be clumped; counting errors at quadrat edges [1]
*Examiner insight:* Forgetting to convert from per quadrat to per m² gives 4200, a common wrong answer.

**8. (a)** As the time out of water increases, seaweed cover decreases [1]; from 85% at 2 hours to 0% at 20
hours [1]
**(b)** The seaweed loses water and dries out when exposed to air [1]; so it cannot survive where it is out of
the water for long periods [1]
**(c)** Use a quadrat divided into 100 squares and count the squares more than half covered by seaweed [1]

**9. (a)** Aphids week 3; ladybirds week 4 [1]
**(b)** There were more aphids, so more food for the ladybirds [1]; so more ladybirds survived and reproduced [1]
**(c)** More ladybirds were eating the aphids [1]
**(d)** Ladybird numbers will increase, because aphid numbers are rising again so there is more food [1]

**10. (a)** (50 − 18) ÷ 10 [1] = **3.2 g per week** [1]
**(b)** A has water but B is dry [1]; microorganisms need water to grow and for their enzymes to work, so decay is
slower in B [1]; A has oxygen but D has none [1]; so microorganisms in A can respire aerobically and break down
material faster [1]

**11. (a)** 1 ÷ 150 [1] = **0.0067 per second** [1]
**(b)** As temperature rises the time falls, so the rate increases [1]; because lipase molecules move faster and
collide with fat more often, producing fatty acids faster [1]
**(c)** At 50 °C the lipase has started to denature [1]; the active site changes shape so fewer fat molecules
are broken down, and the reaction is slower [1]
**(d)** Any one: volume of milk; volume or concentration of lipase; volume of sodium carbonate solution; amount of
indicator [1]

**12.** Marking points (AQA marks 6-mark questions by levels; a top answer gives a full sequence):
- carbon dioxide is absorbed by a plant (grass) for photosynthesis [1]
- it becomes part of glucose, then other carbon compounds such as starch, cellulose or proteins [1]
- the rabbit eats the grass and digests and absorbs the carbon compounds [1]
- the carbon is built into the rabbit's body (biomass) [1]
- the rabbit respires, releasing carbon dioxide; or the rabbit dies or produces waste [1]
- microorganisms decay the body or waste and respire, releasing carbon dioxide into the air [1]

**13.** Marking points, 1 mark each [4]:
- for: the flower moved to higher altitudes as the temperature rose, and a warmer climate may make the lower
  slopes too warm for it to survive or compete
- against: more sheep grazing on the lower slopes could also have removed the flower there
- against: this is a correlation, from one area, so it does not prove cause
- a judgement: temperature may be one cause, but grazing could also explain the change, so the claim is not
  proven without more evidence (such as fencing out sheep from some plots)
*Examiner insight:* A top answer uses the grazing information as an alternative explanation and reaches a
conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Food chain arrows drawn from predator to prey.
- Not converting a mean per quadrat into a number per m² before scaling up.
- Describing a trend without quoting figures.
- Saying decay is faster "because it is warm" without linking to enzymes or microorganisms.
- Carbon cycle answers that stop at the rabbit, with no respiration or decay.
- Higher tier evaluations with no alternative explanation or conclusion.
