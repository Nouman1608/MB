These questions cover **3.1.2.5 Balanced equations and associated calculations**, including required practical 1, of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, drawn on by Units 2 and 5). Ar values: H 1.0, C 12.0, N 14.0, O 16.0, Na 23.0, Cl 35.5, Ca 40.1, Fe 55.8, Zn 65.4. Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹. Revise with the [balanced equations and associated calculations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-5/) or the [revision notes for this subtopic](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-5-revision-notes/) first if you need to.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Balance the equation for the complete combustion of pentane: C₅H₁₂ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O **[1]**

**2.** Balance these equations for unfamiliar reactions.

**(a)** Al + H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + H₂ **[1]**

**(b)** Pb(NO₃)₂ → PbO + NO₂ + O₂ **[1]**

**3.** Solid calcium carbonate reacts with hydrochloric acid. Write the ionic equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**4.** Aqueous sodium hydroxide is added to aqueous copper(II) sulfate and a blue precipitate of copper(II) hydroxide forms.

**(a)** Write the ionic equation, with state symbols. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the spectator ions. **[1]**

**5.** Iron is extracted by the reaction Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂. Calculate the maximum mass of iron obtainable from 40.0 g of Fe₂O₃. **[2]**

**6.** Calculate the percentage atom economy for producing iron by the reaction in Question 5. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the volume of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂, needed to neutralise 20.0 cm³ of 0.120 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[3]**

### Section B

**8.** 3.27 g of zinc is added to 40.0 cm³ of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂

**(a)** Show by calculation which reagent is limiting. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of hydrogen produced at 293 K and 101 kPa. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of zinc left unreacted. **[2]**

**9.** Ammonia can be made industrially, N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, or in the laboratory: 2NH₄Cl + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCl₂ + 2NH₃ + 2H₂O (Mr: NH₄Cl 53.5, Ca(OH)₂ 74.1, NH₃ 17.0)

**(a)** State the percentage atom economy of the industrial reaction and explain your answer. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage atom economy of the laboratory reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** In the laboratory method, 10.7 g of NH₄Cl reacts with excess Ca(OH)₂ and 2.55 g of NH₃ is collected. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**(d)** Give one economic and one environmental advantage of choosing a process with a high atom economy. **[2]**

**10.** In required practical 1, a student makes 250 cm³ of standard sodium carbonate solution from 1.33 g of anhydrous Na₂CO₃ (Mr 106.0). 25.0 cm³ portions are titrated with hydrochloric acid using methyl orange. Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂. Titres: 24.60 cm³ (rough), 24.20 cm³, 24.10 cm³, 24.15 cm³.

**(a)** Describe how the student should transfer the dissolved solid into the volumetric flask and make the solution up to the mark. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean titre. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the amount of Na₂CO₃ in each 25.0 cm³ portion. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the concentration of the hydrochloric acid. **[2]**

**11.** Sodium hydrogencarbonate decomposes on heating: 2NaHCO₃ → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂. A student heats 4.20 g of NaHCO₃ (Mr 84.0).

**(a)** Calculate the volume, in cm³, of CO₂ formed, measured at 298 K and 101 kPa. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of Na₂CO₃ (Mr 106.0) left at the end. **[1]**

**(c)** The student finds a residue of 2.81 g. Suggest why it is greater than the value in (b). **[1]**

**12.** 40 cm³ of ethane is burned in 200 cm³ of oxygen: 2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O. All volumes are measured at the same room temperature and pressure, at which water is a liquid.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen that reacts. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the total volume of gas left after the reaction. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** C₅H₁₂ + 8O₂ → 5CO₂ + 6H₂O [1] **[1]**

**2.**

**(a)** 2Al + 3H₂SO₄ → Al₂(SO₄)₃ + 3H₂ [1]

**(b)** 2Pb(NO₃)₂ → 2PbO + 4NO₂ + O₂ [1] **[2]**

**3.** CaCO₃(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Ca²⁺(aq) + CO₂(g) + H₂O(l): correct species [1]; balanced with state symbols [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The carbonate is a solid, so it is not split into ions.

**4.**

**(a)** Cu²⁺(aq) + 2OH⁻(aq) → Cu(OH)₂(s): species [1]; balancing and state symbols [1]

**(b)** Na⁺ and SO₄²⁻ [1] **[3]**

**5.** n(Fe₂O₃) = 40.0 ÷ 159.6 = 0.251 mol, so n(Fe) = 0.501 mol [1]; mass = 0.501 × 55.8 = **28.0 g** [1] **[2]**

**6.** (2 × 55.8) ÷ (159.6 + 3 × 28.0) × 100 [1] = 111.6 ÷ 243.6 × 100 = **45.8%** [1] **[2]**

**7.** n(HCl) = 0.120 × 0.0200 = 2.40 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; Ba(OH)₂ + 2HCl, so n(Ba(OH)₂) = 1.20 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; V = 1.20 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0500 = 0.0240 dm³ = **24.0 cm³** [1] **[3]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** n(Zn) = 3.27 ÷ 65.4 = 0.0500 mol; n(HCl) = 2.00 × 0.0400 = 0.0800 mol [1]; 0.0800 mol HCl reacts with only 0.0400 mol Zn, so **HCl is limiting** [1]

**(b)** n(H₂) = 0.0800 ÷ 2 = 0.0400 mol [1]; V = (0.0400 × 8.31 × 293) ÷ 101 000 = 9.64 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = **964 cm³** [1]

**(c)** Zinc left = 0.0500 − 0.0400 = 0.0100 mol [1]; mass = 0.0100 × 65.4 = **0.654 g** [1] **[6]**

**9.**

**(a)** **100%**, because ammonia is the only product [1]

**(b)** (2 × 17.0) ÷ (2 × 53.5 + 74.1) × 100 [1] = 34.0 ÷ 181.1 × 100 = **18.8%** [1]

**(c)** n(NH₄Cl) = 10.7 ÷ 53.5 = 0.200 mol, so theoretical n(NH₃) = 0.200 mol [1]; theoretical mass = 0.200 × 17.0 = 3.40 g [1]; yield = 2.55 ÷ 3.40 × 100 = **75.0%** [1]

**(d)** Economic: less raw material is needed per tonne of product, or less waste to treat [1]; environmental: less waste is produced, or finite resources are used more efficiently [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the desired product's mass is 2 × 17.0 because the equation gives two ammonia molecules.

**10.**

**(a)** Pour the solution into the volumetric flask and rinse the beaker (and glass rod) with distilled water, adding the washings to the flask [1]; add distilled water until the bottom of the meniscus is on the mark, then stopper and invert to mix [1]

**(b)** (24.20 + 24.10 + 24.15) ÷ 3 = **24.15 cm³** [1]

**(c)** n in 250 cm³ = 1.33 ÷ 106.0 = 0.01255 mol [1]; in 25.0 cm³ = **1.25 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]

**(d)** n(HCl) = 2 × 1.255 × 10⁻³ = 2.51 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; c = 2.51 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.02415 = **0.104 mol dm⁻³** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* The rough titre is excluded; the other three are concordant because they lie within 0.10 cm³ of each other.

**11.**

**(a)** n(CO₂) = (4.20 ÷ 84.0) ÷ 2 = 0.0250 mol [1]; V = (0.0250 × 8.31 × 298) ÷ 101 000 = 6.13 × 10⁻⁴ m³ = **613 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 0.0250 × 106.0 = **2.65 g** [1]

**(c)** The decomposition was incomplete, so some NaHCO₃ remained [1] **[4]**

**12.**

**(a)** 40 × 7 ÷ 2 = **140 cm³** [1]

**(b)** 40 × 4 ÷ 2 = **80 cm³** [1]

**(c)** Oxygen left = 200 − 140 = 60 cm³ [1]; total gas = 60 + 80 = **140 cm³** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* At the same temperature and pressure, volume ratios of gases equal mole ratios; liquid water is not counted.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the reagent in excess to find the product.
- Splitting solids, such as CaCO₃, into ions in ionic equations.
- Leaving coefficients out of atom economy calculations.
- Including the rough titre in the mean.
