## Simplifying ratios (1.7 A)

A ratio compares quantities. Divide every part by a common factor, just like simplifying a fraction, until no common factor is left.

- **Units first:** 2.5 kg : 750 g = 2500 : 750 = 10 : 3.
- **Decimals:** multiply to remove them. 0.6 : 1.5 = 6 : 15 = 2 : 5.
- **Fractions:** multiply by the common denominator. 1/2 : 2/3 = 3/6 : 4/6 = 3 : 4.

A simplified ratio has whole numbers and no units.

## The form 1 : n and n : 1

Divide both parts by the first part to get 1 : n, or by the second part to get n : 1. Here n need not be whole.

- 8 : 30 = 1 : 3.75 (÷ 8)
- 12 : 5 = 2.4 : 1 (÷ 5)

This form is used to compare ratios quickly. If one map's scale is 1 : 25 000 and another's is 1 : 50 000, the first shows more detail.

## Ratios and fractions

In a ratio a : b, the whole is a + b parts.

- The first quantity is a/(a + b) of the total. If boys : girls = 4 : 7, then 7/11 of the group are girls.
- The first quantity is a/b **of the second**. Boys are 4/7 of the number of girls.

Mixing these two up is one of the commonest errors in the topic.

## Dividing in a ratio (1.7 B)

The method is always the same: find the value of **one part**, then multiply.

**Worked example 1.** Share £720 in the ratio 7 : 5 : 3.

```
total parts = 7 + 5 + 3 = 15
one part    = 720 ÷ 15 = £48
shares      = 7 × 48, 5 × 48, 3 × 48 = £336, £240, £144
check       = 336 + 240 + 144 = £720
```

**When one share is given.** Money is shared between Tom and Uma in the ratio 3 : 8. Uma gets £96. Then 8 parts = £96, so one part = £12; Tom gets 3 × 12 = £36 and the total is 11 × 12 = £132.

**When the difference is given.** In a ratio 5 : 2 the difference is 3 parts. If the difference is 54, one part is 18 and the quantities are 90 and 36.

Decide first what the given number represents: the total, one share or the difference. That decides what you divide by.

## Combining and changing ratios

**Combining two ratios.** If cats : dogs = 3 : 4 and dogs : rabbits = 6 : 5, make the shared quantity (dogs) the same in both. The LCM of 4 and 6 is 12:

```
cats : dogs     = 3 : 4  = 9 : 12
dogs : rabbits  = 6 : 5  = 12 : 10
cats : dogs : rabbits = 9 : 12 : 10
```

**A ratio that changes.**

**Worked example 2.** A bag contains red and blue counters in the ratio 3 : 5. Six red counters are added, and the ratio becomes 1 : 1. How many counters of each colour were there at the start?

```
start: red = 3k, blue = 5k
after: 3k + 6 = 5k
       6 = 2k, so k = 3
start: 9 red and 15 blue
check: 15 : 15 = 1 : 1
```

Using a letter for "one part" turns the problem into a simple linear equation.

## Proportion (1.7 C, 1.7 D)

Two quantities are in **direct proportion** when doubling one doubles the other: their ratio stays the same.

**The unitary method.** Find the value for one unit, then scale. If 5 notebooks cost £7.15, one costs £1.43 and 8 cost £11.44.

**Scaling without finding one.** If 8 litres of paint cover 52 m², then 2 litres (a quarter) cover 13 m², and 18 litres (2.25 times as much) cover 117 m².

**Missing values in a table.** The specification's example is "s varies directly as t: find the missing value in a table". Find the constant ratio s ÷ t from a complete pair, then use it.

**Worked example 3.** s varies directly as t. Complete the table.

| t | 3 | 7.5 | ? |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | 10.2 | ? | 51 |

```
s ÷ t = 10.2 ÷ 3 = 3.4, so s = 3.4 × t
t = 7.5:  s = 3.4 × 7.5 = 25.5
s = 51:   t = 51 ÷ 3.4 = 15
```

**Best buys and recipes** are proportion problems too. In a recipe, the ingredient that runs out first limits how much you can make: divide what you have by the amount one recipe needs, for each ingredient, and take the smallest result.

## Maps and scale diagrams (1.7 E)

A map scale 1 : 200 000 means 1 cm on the map is 200 000 cm in reality. Convert with the scale, then change units.

- Map to real: 4.7 cm × 200 000 = 940 000 cm = 9.4 km.
- Real to map: 13 km = 1 300 000 cm, and 1 300 000 ÷ 200 000 = 6.5 cm.

Useful conversions: 100 cm = 1 m, 100 000 cm = 1 km. A scale diagram of a room drawn at 1 : 50 shows a 3.6 m wall as 360 ÷ 50 = 7.2 cm.

## Common mistakes

- Dividing by one of the ratio numbers instead of the total number of parts.
- Simplifying a ratio without converting to the same units first.
- Writing the shares in the wrong order, or not checking that they add to the total.
- Confusing "fraction of the total" with "fraction of the other quantity".
- In combined ratios, scaling only one ratio, so the shared quantity does not match.
- Map scales: converting cm to km by dividing by 1000 instead of 100 000.

## Exam technique

- **Write in its simplest form** or **in the form 1 : n**: follow the form exactly; 1 : 3.75 is acceptable, 4 : 15 is not, when 1 : n is asked.
- **Show your working** for sharing: "15 parts, 1 part = £48" earns method marks even if a multiplication slips.
- **Which is better value?** Compare the same quantity (cost per item or per 100 g) for each option, then state the conclusion.
- **Explain** in context: "The butter runs out first, so the greatest number of biscuits is 32."
- Check answers: shares add up to the total, and scaled quantities move in the right direction.
