## The four named uses

| Metal | Use | Property | Why the property matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| Aluminium | aircraft | **low density** | lighter aircraft, so less fuel needed |
| Aluminium | overhead electrical cables | **low density** + **good electrical conductivity** | light enough to hang between pylons; carries current well |
| Aluminium | food containers | **resistance to corrosion** | not attacked by moist or acidic food; container does not weaken |
| Copper | electrical wiring | **good electrical conductivity** + **ductility** | carries current well; can be drawn into thin wires |

**Memory hook:** aluminium is the "low density" metal; copper is the "wire" metal.

## Key comparisons

**Aluminium or copper for overhead cables?**

- Copper is the better conductor.
- Copper is over three times as dense (about 9.0 g/cm³ compared with about 2.7 g/cm³).
- For the same mass, aluminium carries current better, and lighter cables need less support.
- So aluminium is used overhead.

**Aluminium or copper for house wiring?**

- Mass does not matter inside walls.
- Copper conducts better and is ductile.
- So copper is used.

**Ductile or malleable?**

- Ductile: drawn into **wires** (wiring, cables).
- Malleable: hammered or rolled into **sheets** (foil, panels).

## Answering data questions

When a table gives several metals and asks which to use:

1. Decide which properties the job needs (for example, a cable needs conductivity; an aircraft part needs low density).
2. Find the metal that best matches **those** columns.
3. Rule out metals that fail an essential property, such as corrosion for a food container.
4. Quote the data in your reason.

**Density calculation:** mass = density × volume. Example: 50 cm³ of aluminium at 2.70 g/cm³ has a mass of 2.70 × 50 = 135 g.

## Applying the same reasoning to unfamiliar uses

Questions may describe a job you have not met and ask you to suggest a suitable property. Match the job to the property:

| Job described in the question | Property to look for |
|---|---|
| something that is carried, lifted or flown | low density |
| something that carries an electric current | good electrical conductivity |
| something made into thin wires | ductility |
| something pressed or rolled into sheets or shapes | malleability |
| something that heats food or transfers heat | good thermal conductivity |
| something that must not melt when heated strongly | high melting point |
| something in contact with water, air or food for a long time | resistance to corrosion |

**Answer frame:** "[Metal] is used for [job] because it has [property], so [consequence for the job]." The consequence is what turns a "state" answer into an "explain" answer, for example "…low density, so the aircraft needs less fuel".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Density | mass per unit volume, in g/cm³ |
| Low density | light for its size |
| Electrical conductivity | how easily a material lets electric current pass |
| Ductile | can be drawn out into wires |
| Malleable | can be hammered or rolled into shape |
| Corrosion | a metal reacting with substances around it, such as air, water or acids, so that the metal is gradually worn away |
| Resistance to corrosion | the metal is not easily attacked by air, water or food |

## Exam traps

- **A use alone scores nothing.** Always add "because it has…".
- **Two-property uses need both properties.** Overhead cables: low density **and** conductivity. Copper wiring: conductivity **and** ductility.
- **"Light" is weaker than "low density".** Use the scientific term.
- **Do not write "aluminium is unreactive".** It is above zinc and iron in the reactivity series; the correct property is **resistance to corrosion**.
- **Do not use copper for overhead cables** in your answer just because it conducts better.
- **Ductile is for wires.** "Malleable" is the wrong property for wiring.
- **Read the job carefully in data questions.** A table may include strength, melting point or cost; use only the columns the job needs.

## Self-test

1. State the property of aluminium that makes it suitable for aircraft.
2. State **two** properties of aluminium that make it suitable for overhead cables.
3. Why is aluminium used for food containers?
4. State **two** properties that make copper suitable for electrical wiring.
5. Copper is a better electrical conductor than aluminium. Why is copper not used for overhead cables?
6. Define *ductile*.
7. Why is malleability not the best answer for why copper is used in wires?
8. Calculate the mass of 40 cm³ of copper, density 8.96 g/cm³.
9. A metal has low density but corrodes quickly in water. Suggest why it would not be suitable for a drink can.
10. Why does copper's high density not prevent its use for wiring inside buildings?
11. Name the metal used for each: (a) aircraft bodies; (b) household wiring.
12. Suggest the property a metal needs for the base of a saucepan, and why.

**Answers:**

1. Low density.
2. Low density; good electrical conductivity.
3. It resists corrosion.
4. Good electrical conductivity; ductility.
5. Copper has a much higher density, so cables would be too heavy for the pylons to support.
6. Can be drawn out into a wire.
7. Wires are made by drawing the metal out, which is ductility; malleability is about hammering into shape.
8. 8.96 × 40 = 358.4 g, or 358 g to 3 significant figures.
9. The can would corrode, weaken and could contaminate the drink.
10. The wiring is supported by the building, so its mass does not matter.
11. (a) Aluminium; (b) copper.
12. Good thermal conductivity, so heat passes quickly from the cooker to the food (a high melting point also helps).
