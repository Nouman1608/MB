These questions cover section 4 of Unit M2: Mechanics 2 in Pearson Edexcel International A Level Mathematics (YMA01): momentum and impulse as vectors, conservation of linear momentum, direct impact with Newton's law of restitution, loss of mechanical energy, and successive impacts. All surfaces are smooth and all impacts are direct. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² where needed.

Related: [M2 collisions study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-collisions-edexcel-alevel-maths/) and [M2 collisions revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-mathematics-yma01-m2-collisions-edexcel-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A particle of mass 0.2 kg hits a fixed smooth vertical wall at right angles with speed 8 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of restitution between the particle and the wall is 0.6. Find the rebound speed and the magnitude of the impulse on the particle. **[3]**

**2.** A particle of mass 0.4 kg moving with velocity (5i − 2j) m s⁻¹ receives an impulse of (−2i + 3.6j) N s. Find its velocity and speed immediately afterwards. **[3]**

**3.** A ball of mass 0.25 kg changes velocity from (6i + 2j) m s⁻¹ to (−2i + 8j) m s⁻¹ when it is struck. Find the impulse and its magnitude. **[3]**

**4.** State what happens in a direct collision between two particles when **(a)** e = 0 and **(b)** e = 1. **[2]**

**5.** Particle A, of mass 3 kg, moves at 4 m s⁻¹ and hits particle B, of mass 1 kg, which is at rest. The coefficient of restitution is 0.6. Find the velocities of A and B after the impact. **[4]**

**6.** A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 2.5 m onto a horizontal floor. The coefficient of restitution is 0.4. Find the height to which it first rebounds. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Particle A, of mass 4 kg, moves at 5 m s⁻¹ towards particle B, of mass 2 kg, which moves at 4 m s⁻¹ towards A along the same line. The coefficient of restitution is 0.25.

**(a)** Find the velocities of A and B after the impact. **[5]**

**(b)** Find the kinetic energy lost in the impact. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the magnitude of the impulse on B. **[2]**

**8.** Particle P, of mass 2m, moves with speed 2u and hits particle Q, of mass m, which is at rest. The coefficient of restitution is e.

**(a)** Show that the speed of Q after the impact is 4(1 + e)u/3, and find the velocity of P. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain why P continues in its original direction for every possible value of e. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that the kinetic energy lost is (4/3)mu²(1 − e²). **[4]**

**9.** Particle A, of mass m, moves with speed u towards particle B, of mass 4m, which is at rest. Beyond B is a fixed smooth vertical wall, perpendicular to the line of motion. The coefficient of restitution between A and B is e.

**(a)** Show that after the impact B moves with speed (1 + e)u/5, and find the velocity of A. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the range of values of e for which A rebounds. **[2]**

**(c)** Take e = ½. B hits the wall and rebounds. The coefficient of restitution between B and the wall is f. Find the range of values of f for which B collides with A again. **[3]**

**10.** Three particles A, B and C, of masses 1 kg, 2 kg and 3 kg, lie at rest in that order on a straight line. A is projected towards B with speed 6 m s⁻¹. The coefficient of restitution for every impact is 0.5.

**(a)** Find the velocities of A and B after the first impact. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the velocities of B and C after B hits C. **[4]**

**(c)** Explain why there are no further impacts. **[2]**

**11.** A ball is dropped from rest from a height of 10 m onto a horizontal floor. The coefficient of restitution between the ball and the floor is 0.5.

**(a)** Find the speed of the ball just after its first bounce. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the height of the first rebound. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the time from the release of the ball until its second impact with the floor. **[3]**

**12.** Particle A, of mass 2 kg, has velocity (3i − j) m s⁻¹. Particle B, of mass 1 kg, has velocity (−i + 2j) m s⁻¹. They collide and move together after the impact.

**(a)** Find their common velocity after the impact. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the magnitude of the impulse on B. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Rebound speed = 0.6 × 8 = **4.8 m s⁻¹** [1].

Away from the wall positive: I = 0.2(4.8) − 0.2(−8) [1] = **2.56 N s** [1].

**2.** v = (5i − 2j) + (−2i + 3.6j)/0.4 [1] = (5i − 2j) + (−5i + 9j) = **7j m s⁻¹** [1]. Speed **7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**3.** I = 0.25[(−2i + 8j) − (6i + 2j)] [1] = **(−2i + 1.5j) N s** [1]. Magnitude = √(4 + 2.25) = **2.5 N s** [1].

**4. (a)** The particles move with the same velocity after impact (they do not separate) [1].

**(b)** The collision is perfectly elastic, so no kinetic energy is lost [1].

**5.** Momentum: 3(4) = 3v_A + v_B [1]. Restitution: v_B − v_A = 0.6(4) = 2.4 [1].

12 = 3v_A + v_A + 2.4, so **v_A = 2.4 m s⁻¹** [1] and **v_B = 4.8 m s⁻¹** [1], both in A's original direction.

**6.** Speed before impact = √(2 × 9.8 × 2.5) = 7 m s⁻¹ [1]. Speed after = 0.4 × 7 = 2.8 m s⁻¹ [1].

Height = 2.8²/19.6 = **0.4 m** [1].

**7. (a)** A's direction positive. Momentum: 4(5) + 2(−4) = 4v_A + 2v_B, so 4v_A + 2v_B = 12 [2].

Restitution: v_B − v_A = 0.25(5 + 4) = 2.25 [1].

4v_A + 2(v_A + 2.25) = 12, so **v_A = 1.25 m s⁻¹** [1] and **v_B = 3.5 m s⁻¹** [1], both in A's original direction.

**(b)** KE before = ½(4)(25) + ½(2)(16) = 66 J [1]. KE after = ½(4)(1.5625) + ½(2)(12.25) = 15.375 J [1].

KE lost = **50.6 J** (50.625 J) [1].

**(c)** I = 2(3.5 − (−4)) [1] = **15 N s** [1].

**8. (a)** Momentum: 2m(2u) = 2mv_P + mv_Q, so 4u = 2v_P + v_Q [1].

Restitution: v_Q − v_P = 2eu [1].

Subtracting: 4u − 2eu = 3v_P, so **v_P = 2(2 − e)u/3** [1].

v_Q = v_P + 2eu = (4 − 2e + 6e)u/3 [1] = **4(1 + e)u/3** as required [1].

**(b)** e ≤ 1, so 2 − e > 0 and v_P > 0 [1].

**(c)** KE before = ½(2m)(2u)² = 4mu² [1].

KE after = ½(2m)(4(2 − e)²u²/9) + ½m(16(1 + e)²u²/9) = (4mu²/9)[(2 − e)² + 2(1 + e)²] [1].

(2 − e)² + 2(1 + e)² = 6 + 3e², so KE after = (4mu²/3)(2 + e²) [1].

Loss = 4mu² − (4mu²/3)(2 + e²) = **(4/3)mu²(1 − e²)** [1].

**9. (a)** Positive towards the wall. Momentum: u = v_A + 4v_B [1]. Restitution: v_B − v_A = eu [1].

Adding: 5v_B = (1 + e)u, so **v_B = (1 + e)u/5** [1].

**v_A = (1 − 4e)u/5** [1].

**(b)** A rebounds when 1 − 4e < 0, so e > ¼ [1]. With e ≤ 1: **¼ < e ≤ 1** [1].

**(c)** e = ½: v_A = −u/5, so A moves away from the wall at u/5; v_B = 3u/10 [1].

B rebounds at 3fu/10, moving in the same direction as A. It catches A if 3fu/10 > u/5 [1].

So f > 2/3, and with f ≤ 1: **2/3 < f ≤ 1** [1].

**10. (a)** Momentum: 1(6) = v_A + 2v_B [1]. Restitution: v_B − v_A = 0.5(6) = 3 [1].

6 = (v_B − 3) + 2v_B, so **v_B = 3 m s⁻¹** [1] and **v_A = 0** [1].

**(b)** Momentum: 2(3) = 2w_B + 3w_C [1]. Restitution: w_C − w_B = 0.5(3) = 1.5 [1].

6 = 2w_B + 3(w_B + 1.5), so **w_B = 0.3 m s⁻¹** [1] and **w_C = 1.8 m s⁻¹** [1], both towards C's side.

**(c)** A is at rest and B moves away from A, so A and B do not meet again [1]. C moves faster than B in the same direction, so B and C do not meet again [1].

**11. (a)** Speed before = √(2 × 9.8 × 10) = 14 m s⁻¹ [1]. Speed after = 0.5 × 14 = **7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Height = 7²/19.6 [1] = **2.5 m** [1].

**(c)** Time to fall 10 m: 14/9.8 = 1.429 s [1]. Time up and down after the bounce: 2 × 7/9.8 = 1.429 s [1].

Total = **2.86 s** [1].

**12. (a)** Momentum: 2(3i − j) + 1(−i + 2j) = 3v [1], so 3v = 5i and **v = (5/3)i m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** I on B = 1[(5/3)i − (−i + 2j)] = (8/3)i − 2j [1].

Magnitude = √(64/9 + 4) = √(100/9) [1] = **10/3 N s** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a speed instead of a negative velocity for a particle moving the other way.
- Writing restitution with the subtractions in different orders on the two sides.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved when e is less than 1.
- Adding an impulse vector to a velocity without dividing by the mass.
- Not combining an inequality for e or f with the bound 0 ≤ e ≤ 1.
- Giving the time of flight after the bounce but forgetting the first fall.
