These questions cover sections 5.4.1 to 5.4.3 of the OCR Advanced GCE in Physics A specification (H556), Version 3.0 (March 2026), for first assessment in 2017. H556 is the full A level. Use G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻² and, where needed, Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg and Earth radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m.

## Questions

**1.**
**(a)** State the property of an object that gives rise to a gravitational field. **[1]**
**(b)** Define gravitational field strength. **[2]**

**2.** Four moons orbit a planet. Their orbital radii r and periods T are given in the table.

| r / 10⁸ m | 1.2 | 2.0 | 3.5 | 5.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| T / days | 0.676 | 1.45 | 3.37 | 5.75 |

**(a)** Show that T² ∝ r³ holds for the moons at 1.2 × 10⁸ m and 3.5 × 10⁸ m. **[3]**
**(b)** Use your values to calculate the mass of the planet. **[2]**

**3.**
**(a)** Describe the field lines around an isolated spherical planet and what their spacing shows. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the field lines close to the planet's surface and explain why the field there is treated as uniform. **[2]**

**4.** Two uniform spheres of mass 6.0 kg and 4.0 kg have their centres 0.30 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational force between them. **[2]**
**(b)** State the force when the centres are 0.60 m apart. **[1]**
**(c)** State the distance used in your calculation and why it is valid for spheres. **[1]**

**5.** Jupiter has mass 1.90 × 10²⁷ kg and radius 7.15 × 10⁷ m.

**(a)** Calculate g at the planet's surface. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the field strength at a point 2.0 radii above the surface. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the gravitational force on a 3.0 kg probe at that point. **[1]**

**6.** A summit is 2000 m above sea level.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational field strength at the summit, and the percentage decrease from the sea-level value. **[2]**
**(b)** State what this shows about treating g as uniform near the surface. **[1]**

**7.** Derive the equation T² = (4π²/GM) r³ for a planet of mass m in a circular orbit of radius r around a star of mass M. **[4]**

**8.**
**(a)** State Kepler's three laws of planetary motion. **[3]**
**(b)** An asteroid orbits the Sun. Its closest approach is 2.2 × 10¹¹ m, where its speed is 2.1 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹, and its furthest distance is 4.4 × 10¹¹ m. Calculate its speed at the furthest point. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the area swept out per second by the line from the Sun to the asteroid at closest approach. **[1]**

**9.** A satellite orbits the Earth at a height of 800 km.

**(a)** State the orbital radius. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its orbital period. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate its orbital speed. **[2]**

**10.** A star has two planets in circular orbits. The inner planet has orbital radius 2.0 × 10¹⁰ m and period 20 days. The outer planet has orbital radius 5.0 × 10¹⁰ m.

**(a)** Calculate the period of the outer planet. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of the star. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the masses of the planets are not needed in part (b). **[1]**
**(d)** State the condition that must be met for the comparison in part (a) to be valid. **[1]**

**11.** Saturn has mass 5.68 × 10²⁶ kg, equatorial radius 6.03 × 10⁷ m and a rotation period of 10.7 hours. A satellite is to stay above one point on Saturn's equator.

**(a)** State two orbit conditions. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the orbital radius needed. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the height of the orbit above the surface. **[1]**
**(d)** State one use and one disadvantage of geostationary satellites at the Earth. **[2]**

**12.** The Sun and the Earth have masses 1.99 × 10³⁰ kg and 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg. Their centres are 1.50 × 10¹¹ m apart. A point on the line between them has zero resultant gravitational field strength.

**(a)** State the condition for the resultant field to be zero. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the distance of this point from the centre of the Earth. **[4]**
**(c)** Calculate the magnitude of the Earth's field strength at this point. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Mass [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The force per unit mass [1] on a small test mass at that point, g = F/m [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Define it with both "force" and "per unit mass".

**2. (a)** For 1.2 × 10⁸ m: T = 5.84 × 10⁴ s, T²/r³ = 3.41 × 10⁹ / 1.728 × 10²⁴ = 1.97 × 10⁻¹⁵ s² m⁻³ [1]. For 3.5 × 10⁸ m: T = 2.91 × 10⁵ s, T²/r³ = 8.48 × 10¹⁰ / 4.2875 × 10²⁵ = 1.98 × 10⁻¹⁵ s² m⁻³ [1]. The ratios agree to within rounding, so T² ∝ r³ [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** T²/r³ = 4π²/GM, so M = 4π² / (G × 1.98 × 10⁻¹⁵) [1] = **3.0 × 10²⁶ kg** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert days to seconds before squaring. One ratio alone proves nothing, so show both.

**3. (a)** Straight lines pointing radially inwards towards the centre, with arrows [1]. They are closer together nearer the planet, showing the field is stronger there [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The lines are parallel and equally spaced, vertically downwards [1], because over a small height range the distance from the centre barely changes, so g barely changes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both direction and spacing, and say what the spacing means.

**4. (a)** F = GMm/r² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6.0 × 4.0 / 0.30² [1] = **1.8 × 10⁻⁸ N** (1.78 × 10⁻⁸ N) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Distance doubled, so force is a quarter: **4.4 × 10⁻⁹ N** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The distance between the centres, 0.30 m [1]: a uniform sphere acts as if its mass is at its centre. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the power of ten with the unit throughout. Forces between small objects are tiny, so a large answer signals an error.

**5. (a)** g = GM/R² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 1.90 × 10²⁷ / (7.15 × 10⁷)² [1] = **24.8 N kg⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The distance from the centre is R + 2.0R = 3.0R = 2.145 × 10⁸ m [1]. g = 24.8 / 3² = **2.75 N kg⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** F = mg = 3.0 × 2.754 = **8.3 N** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* "2.0 radii above the surface" means r = 3.0 R. State the distance you use at the start.

**6. (a)** r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 2000 = 6.372 × 10⁶ m [1]. g = GM/r² = 9.807 N kg⁻¹ against 9.813 N kg⁻¹ at sea level, a decrease of **0.063 %** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The change is under 0.1 % over 2000 m, so g is effectively uniform near the surface [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the percentage, then a comparison. A bare number does not answer "what does this show".

**7.** The gravitational force provides the centripetal force [1]: GMm/r² = mv²/r [1]. Substitute v = 2πr/T [1]: GM/r² = 4π²r/T². Rearranging: **T² = (4π²/GM) r³** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the starting statement in words and show m cancelling. A derivation that jumps to the answer leaves nothing to credit.

**8. (a)** Planets move in ellipses with the Sun at one focus [1]. The line joining a planet to the Sun sweeps equal areas in equal times [1]. T² is proportional to r³ [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Equal areas in equal times at the closest and furthest points gives v₁r₁ = v₂r₂ [1]. v₂ = 2.1 × 10⁴ × 2.2 × 10¹¹ / 4.4 × 10¹¹ = **1.05 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Area per second = ½rv = ½ × 2.2 × 10¹¹ × 2.1 × 10⁴ = **2.3 × 10¹⁵ m² s⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the relation holds at these two points because velocity is perpendicular to the radius there.

**9. (a)** r = 6.37 × 10⁶ + 8.0 × 10⁵ = **7.17 × 10⁶ m** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** T = 2π √(r³/GM) [1] = 2π √((7.17 × 10⁶)³ / (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴)) = **6.05 × 10³ s** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** v = 2πr/T [1] = 2π × 7.17 × 10⁶ / 6045 = **7.45 × 10³ m s⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing "r = R + h" first avoids using the height as r. Cross-check with v = √(GM/r).

**10. (a)** T ∝ r^(3/2) so T = 20 × (5.0 / 2.0)^1.5 [1] = **79 days** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** T = 20 × 86 400 = 1.728 × 10⁶ s [1]. M = 4π²r³/(GT²) = 4π² × (2.0 × 10¹⁰)³ / (6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × (1.728 × 10⁶)²) [1] = **1.6 × 10³⁰ kg** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** The planet's mass appears in both the gravitational force and the centripetal force, so it cancels [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Both planets must orbit the same central star [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* The ratio in part (a) needs no unit conversion, but part (b) needs seconds.

**11. (a)** Any two of: above the equator; same direction as Saturn rotates; circular; period equal to its rotation period [1] [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** T = 10.7 × 3600 = 3.852 × 10⁴ s [1]. r³ = GMT²/4π² [1]. r = **1.13 × 10⁸ m** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** h = 1.125 × 10⁸ − 6.03 × 10⁷ = **5.2 × 10⁷ m** [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Use: television or telephone communications, or weather observation of a fixed region [1]. Disadvantage: a signal delay of about 0.24 s, or poor coverage at high latitudes [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Geostationary" needs "above the equator" as well as "24 hours". Missing the equator is the usual lost point.

**12. (a)** The fields from the two bodies are equal in magnitude and opposite in direction [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Let the distance from the Earth be x. GM_E/x² = GM_S/(d − x)² [1]. x/(d − x) = √(M_E/M_S) = 1.73 × 10⁻³ [1]. x = d × 1.73 × 10⁻³ / (1 + 1.73 × 10⁻³) [1] = **2.6 × 10⁸ m** (2.59 × 10⁸ m) [1]. **[4]**
**(c)** g = GM_E/x² = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴ / (2.59 × 10⁸)² [1] = **5.9 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the square root of both sides early to avoid a quadratic, then check that the point is much nearer the Earth.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Substituting the height above the planet into GM/r² instead of the distance to its centre.
- Writing "2.0 radii above the surface" as r = 2.0R.
- Not squaring the power of ten when squaring r.
- Starting a derivation without saying gravity provides the centripetal force.
- Applying v₁r₁ = v₂r₂ away from the two closest and furthest points.
- Leaving out "above the equator" for a geostationary orbit.
