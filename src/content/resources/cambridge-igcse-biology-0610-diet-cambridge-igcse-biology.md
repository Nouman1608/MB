This guide covers sub-topic **7.1 Diet** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. All three statements are Core and there is no Supplement content, so every candidate needs all of it and it can appear on any paper.

The [human nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition/) lists the statements for the whole of topic 7. This page teaches 7.1 in full: a definition of a balanced diet that scores, a source and an importance for every nutrient group, the two deficiency diseases, and how to answer questions about a person's diet. Condense it with the [diet revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [diet practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-diet-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/).

## What 7.1 asks you to do

| Statement | Command word | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 7.1.1 | Describe | What is meant by a balanced diet |
| 7.1.2 | State; describe | The principal dietary sources and the importance of carbohydrates, fats and oils, proteins, vitamins (C and D only), mineral ions (calcium and iron only), fibre (roughage) and water |
| 7.1.3 | State | The causes of scurvy and rickets |

The limits matter. Only two vitamins and two mineral ions are named, so questions will be built around these four.

## What a balanced diet is

A **balanced diet** contains all the nutrients the body needs (carbohydrates, fats and oils, proteins, vitamins, mineral ions, fibre and water) **in the correct proportions** to stay healthy.

Two ideas earn the marks: **all** the nutrient groups, and the **right amounts or proportions**. A diet can contain every nutrient and still be unbalanced. Too much fat, or too little protein, upsets the balance even if nothing is missing.

The amounts that are "correct" are not the same for everyone. A very active person needs more energy from food than an inactive one, and a growing child needs relatively more protein for new tissue. Questions sometimes ask you to apply this to a named person, so link the need to what the nutrient does.

## The seven nutrient groups

"State the principal dietary source" needs a named food. "Describe the importance" needs what the nutrient does in the body.

| Nutrient | Principal dietary sources | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | bread, rice, pasta, potatoes, cereals | main source of energy; glucose is used in respiration |
| Fats and oils | butter, cooking oil, cheese, nuts | source of energy and a store of energy; insulation under the skin |
| Proteins | meat, fish, eggs, beans and lentils, milk | growth and repair of cells and tissues; making enzymes and antibodies |
| Vitamin C | citrus fruits such as oranges and lemons; fresh fruit and vegetables | keeps skin, gums and blood vessel walls healthy; helps wounds heal |
| Vitamin D | oily fish, egg yolk, fortified milk; also made in the skin in sunlight | needed to absorb calcium, so needed for strong bones and teeth |
| Calcium | milk, cheese, yoghurt; some green vegetables | strong bones and teeth; needed for blood clotting |
| Iron | red meat, liver, beans, dark green leafy vegetables such as spinach | making haemoglobin in red blood cells, which carries oxygen |
| Fibre (roughage) | wholemeal bread, whole-grain cereals, fruit, vegetables | adds bulk, so the gut muscles can push food along; prevents constipation |
| Water | drinks, and most foods, especially fruit and vegetables | solvent in which the body's reactions happen; transports substances in blood plasma; removes waste in urine; cooling by sweating |

### Points that separate good answers

- **Energy.** Carbohydrates, fats and oils, and proteins all contain energy, but carbohydrates are the main everyday source. Fat contains about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate, which is why it suits storage.
- **Protein.** The key word is **growth** (new cells) and **repair**. "For muscles" is too vague on its own.
- **Fibre** is not digested and is not absorbed. Its value comes from staying in the gut.
- **Vitamin D and calcium** work as a pair. Vitamin D lets the body absorb calcium from food; calcium is what actually strengthens the bones and teeth.
- **Iron** links to oxygen transport. A shortage means less haemoglobin, so less oxygen reaches the cells and the person feels tired.
- **Water** is a nutrient in its own right: the body cannot store much of it, so it must be taken in every day.

## Scurvy and rickets (7.1.3)

| Disease | Cause | What you might notice |
|---|---|---|
| Scurvy | lack of **vitamin C** in the diet | bleeding gums, wounds that heal slowly |
| Rickets | lack of **vitamin D** (or calcium) in the diet | soft, weak bones; bowed legs in children |

The statement asks for **causes**, so the essential answer is the missing nutrient. Rickets is also more likely if a child spends very little time in sunlight, because less vitamin D is made in the skin.

Vitamin C is easily destroyed by heating, which is why a diet with no fresh fruit or vegetables, only cooked or preserved food, can lead to scurvy. This links to the DCPIP results in the [biological molecules study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-biological-molecules/), where boiled juice needed more volume to decolourise DCPIP.

## Worked example 1: diagnosing a diet

A sailor on a long voyage eats dry biscuits, salted meat and water for several months. His gums begin to bleed. **Explain the cause of his symptoms.** **[3]**

- His diet contains no fresh fruit or vegetables [1].
- So it lacks vitamin C [1].
- Lack of vitamin C causes scurvy, which causes bleeding gums [1].

The mark scheme rewards the chain: missing food → missing nutrient → named disease.

## Worked example 2: reading food data

The table gives values per 100 g for two breakfast foods. The values are for practice only.

| Per 100 g | Food A | Food B |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrate / g | 68 | 25 |
| Fat / g | 2 | 32 |
| Protein / g | 10 | 13 |
| Fibre / g | 9 | 0 |
| Calcium / mg | 40 | 60 |

**(a) Which food would better prevent constipation? Explain.** Food A, because it contains 9 g of fibre per 100 g and Food B contains none; fibre adds bulk so food is pushed along the gut.

**(b) Suggest why Food B may supply more energy per 100 g than Food A, even though it has much less carbohydrate.** It contains sixteen times as much fat (32 g compared with 2 g), and fat contains about twice as much energy per gram as carbohydrate.

**(c) A child eats only Food A for breakfast and avoids dairy foods. Suggest one nutrient she may be short of, and its effect.** Calcium: Food A is low in calcium, and calcium is needed for strong bones and teeth.

Notice that every answer quotes the data and then gives the biology.

## Common mistakes

- "A balanced diet is eating equal amounts of every food." It is the correct proportions of all nutrients.
- "Vitamins and minerals give us energy." They do not release energy.
- Giving "vegetables" as the source for everything. Name a specific food where you can.
- Linking iron to bones or calcium to blood. Iron is for haemoglobin; calcium is for bones and teeth.
- Saying rickets is caused by lack of vitamin C, or scurvy by lack of calcium.
- Writing that fibre is digested to give energy.
- Forgetting water when asked to list the nutrient groups.

## Exam technique for 7.1

- **Describe** a balanced diet: name "all nutrients" and "correct proportions" in the same sentence.
- **State** a source: one food is enough, but it must be a good source.
- **Describe the importance**: say what the nutrient does, not just that it is "healthy".
- **Explain** a deficiency: food missing → nutrient missing → effect or disease.
- **Suggest** a dietary change: name a food that supplies the missing nutrient.
