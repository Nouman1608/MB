## Definitions to know word for word

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary sector | Firms that extract or grow natural resources |
| Secondary sector | Firms that manufacture or construct |
| Tertiary sector | Firms that provide services |
| Private sector | Firms owned by individuals or groups, usually seeking profit |
| Public sector | Firms owned by the state |
| Turnover | Sales revenue over a period |
| Market share | One firm's slice of total market sales, as a percentage |
| Internal (organic) growth | Expansion the firm funds and runs itself, such as extra sales or branches |
| External growth | Growth by joining with another firm, such as a merger |
| Merger | Two firms agreeing to become one |
| Economies of scale | Average cost per unit falls when output increases |
| Diseconomies of scale | Average cost per unit rises when output increases |

## Formulas

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Market share | firm's sales / total market sales x 100 |
| Growth rate | (new - old) / old x 100 |
| Average cost | total cost / output |
| Turnover per employee | turnover / number of employees |
| Profit | total revenue - total cost |
| Profit margin | profit / revenue x 100 |

## 3.5.1 Classification

Ask three questions about any firm.

1. What does it mainly do? Takes from nature (primary), makes or builds (secondary), serves (tertiary).
2. Who owns it? Private owners or the government.
3. How big is it? Say which measure: employees, turnover, output, capital employed or market share.

Size measures can disagree. A highly automated plant may have few employees and large turnover. You are not required to know detailed types of firm structure.

**Worked reminder (illustrative).** A market stall has weekly revenue of 2,100 and costs of 1,650.

```
Profit = 2,100 - 1,650 = 450
Margin = 450 / 2,100 x 100 = 21.4%
```

## 3.5.2 Small firms

| Advantages | Disadvantages and challenges |
|---|---|
| Personal service | Higher unit costs |
| Fast decisions | Hard to raise finance |
| Owner motivated | Little bargaining power with suppliers |
| Low start-up cost | Hard to compete on price with large rivals |
| Fills a niche | Few reserves in a downturn |

**Reasons small firms exist** (remember SPIN): **S**mall market, **P**ersonal service wanted, **I**ndustry with few scale economies, **N**iche or owner's choice to stay small. Also add that some small firms supply large firms.

**Method for a "why do small firms survive" answer**

1. Name the reason.
2. Link it to customers or costs.
3. Give a short example.

Example: "Customers want a made-to-measure product. A large firm that mass produces cannot offer it, so a small workshop keeps its customers even at a higher price."

## 3.5.3 Growth

**Reasons for growth**: more profit, more power over price, lower costs, spreading risk, survival, and the aims of owners and managers.

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Example | Higher market share, new branch | Merger |
| Pace | Slower | Faster |
| Cost | Spread out | Large purchase cost |
| Main risk | Slow to build | Culture clash, redundancies |

**Method: did the firm gain market share?**

1. Work out share = firm sales / market sales x 100 for each year.
2. Compare the two shares, not the sales figures.

**Worked reminder (illustrative).** Sales of 9.0m in a market of 60m, then 10.8m in a market of 72m.

```
Share, year 1 = 9.0 / 60 x 100  = 15%
Share, year 2 = 10.8 / 72 x 100 = 15%
Sales growth  = (10.8 / 9.0 - 1) x 100 = 20%
Market growth = (72 / 60 - 1) x 100    = 20%
```

Sales grew 20% but share did not change, because the market also grew 20%.

## 3.5.4 Mergers

| Type | Joins | Key advantage | Key disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Horizontal | Same industry, same stage | Scale economies, bigger share | Less competition, job losses |
| Vertical backward | Firm and its supplier | Secure supply, supplier's margin | Unfamiliar stage, inefficiency kept |
| Vertical forward | Firm and its retailer or customer | Control of sales outlets | Unfamiliar stage, rivals may refuse to deal |
| Conglomerate | Different industries | Spreads risk | Little scale saving, managers lack expertise |

**Memory aid.** Horizontal means "side by side" (same stage). Vertical means "up or down the supply chain". Conglomerate means "unrelated".

**Examples (illustrative).** Two regional airlines merge: horizontal. A bakery chain buys a wheat farm: vertical, backward. A brewer buys a chain of pubs: vertical, forward. A mining group buys a television channel: conglomerate.

**Evaluation checklist** for "should this merger go ahead?"

- Who gains: owners, consumers, workers?
- Do cost savings reach consumers as lower prices?
- How many competitors remain?
- Are there barriers that stop new firms entering?
- Short run job losses against long run efficiency.

## 3.5.5 Scale: economies and diseconomies

| Type | Where it comes from | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Internal economies | The firm's own size | Technical, purchasing, financial, managerial, marketing, risk-bearing |
| Internal diseconomies | The firm being too big | Poor communication, low morale, weak coordination |
| External economies | The industry's size, usually in one area | Skilled labour pool, specialist suppliers, better transport |
| External diseconomies | The industry becoming crowded | Congestion, higher rents, shortage of workers |

**Shape of the pattern.** Average cost first falls (economies), may stay flat at its lowest level, then climbs (diseconomies).

**Worked reminder (illustrative).** A firm makes 12,000 units at a total cost of 84,000 and 25,000 units at 150,000.

```
Average cost at 12,000 = 84,000 / 12,000  = 7.00
Average cost at 25,000 = 150,000 / 25,000 = 6.00
Fall = (7.00 - 6.00) / 7.00 x 100 = 14.3%
```

This shows economies of scale over that range.

**Chain of reasoning, internal economy.** A large firm orders more materials, so the supplier gives a discount, so the cost of each unit falls, so average cost falls, so the firm can lower price or raise profit.

**Chain of reasoning, external diseconomy.** More firms crowd into one area, so competition for workshop space rises, so rents rise, so every firm's costs increase, even small ones.

## Method: naming a merger

1. Are the two firms in the same industry? If not, it is conglomerate.
2. If yes, are they at the same stage of production? If yes, horizontal.
3. If at different stages, is the buyer moving towards its supplier (backward) or towards its customer (forward)? Both are vertical.

**Worked reminder (illustrative).** A tile maker uses 5,000 sacks of cement a year at 6.00 each. It buys a cement plant whose cost is 4.80 a sack.

```
Cost before = 5,000 x 6.00 = 30,000
Cost after  = 5,000 x 4.80 = 24,000
Saving      = 6,000, or 1.20 a sack (20%)
```

The saving is the plant's margin, so the type is vertical, backward. Always set such a saving against the price paid for the plant.

## Using a source in a case question

Pick out the figure, say what it shows, and link it to the concept. For example: "The firm's market share fell from 15% to 12%, so although sales rose it grew more slowly than the market." Quote the numbers, then explain.

## Must-know distinctions

- Internal economies belong to one firm. External economies help every firm in the industry.
- Horizontal firms are at the same stage. Vertical firms are at different stages.
- Market share is a proportion. Sales are an amount.
- Large means "large on a stated measure".
- Economies of scale lower average cost. They do not by themselves raise profit.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the sector of an oil well, a car factory and a hotel.
2. A firm has sales of 9.0m in a market of 60m. Calculate its market share.
3. Sales rise from 9.0m to 10.8m while the market rises from 60m to 72m. Has market share changed?
4. Firms with market shares of 18% and 9% merge. State the new share and the type of merger.
5. A farm owner buys the mill that processes the crop. State the type of merger.
6. Total cost is 84,000 for 12,000 units and 150,000 for 25,000 units. Calculate each average cost.
7. Unit cost falls from 12.50 to 11.00. Calculate the percentage fall.
8. Give one internal and one external economy of scale.
9. Give two reasons why small firms survive.
10. State one disadvantage of a conglomerate merger.

### Answers

1. Primary, secondary, tertiary.
2. 9.0 / 60 x 100 = 15%.
3. No. 10.8 / 72 x 100 = 15%, the same as before.
4. 18 + 9 = 27%. Horizontal.
5. Vertical (backward).
6. 7.00 and 6.00. Average cost falls, so there are economies of scale.
7. (12.50 - 11.00) / 12.50 x 100 = 12%.
8. For example bulk buying (internal) and a pool of skilled local workers (external).
9. For example a small local market and customers wanting personal service.
10. Managers may lack knowledge of the new industry, and there are few savings from scale.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a list of features without explaining why they matter.
- Calling vertical mergers horizontal, or reversing forward and backward.
- Comparing sales instead of market shares.
- Writing "large" without a measure of size.
- Saying a merger always reduces costs, with no mention of diseconomies.
- Giving external economies as if they belonged to one firm.
- Evaluating with no conclusion, or a conclusion that ignores the context.
- Missing units or percentage signs in calculations, or rounding too early.
