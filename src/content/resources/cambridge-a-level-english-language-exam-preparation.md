Cambridge A-Level English Language 9093 has four externally assessed papers, each 50 marks and 25% of the A Level: Paper 1 Reading (2 hours 15 minutes), Paper 2 Writing (2 hours), Paper 3 Language Analysis (2 hours 15 minutes) and Paper 4 Language Topics (2 hours 15 minutes). This is the 2024-2026 syllabus edition, whose final examination year is 2026. Our pages on [Paper 1 Reading](/resources/a-level-cambridge-english-language-paper-1-reading/), [Paper 2 Writing](/resources/a-level-english-language-paper-2-writing/) and [Paper 3 Language Analysis](/resources/a-level-english-language-paper-3-language-analysis/) cover each paper in detail.

## AS Level and A Level routes

Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level, each 50% of the AS, and can be a standalone qualification. Papers 3 and 4 complete the full A Level and count only towards it, each 25% of the A Level. The AS can be staged over two years, carrying forward the AS result, or all four papers can be taken together in one series.

## Paper 1: Reading

Paper 1 is 135 minutes and 50 marks, 25% of the A Level. **How to spend the time:** 135 / 50 = 2.7 minutes per mark. A long paper with a modest mark total suits careful reading, so spend the first part of the time reading the material twice, then plan each response against the marks available. Support every point with a short quotation and a comment on its effect.

## Paper 2: Writing

Paper 2 is 120 minutes and 50 marks, 25% of the A Level. **How to spend the time:** 120 / 50 = 2.4 minutes per mark. Writing rewards control of purpose, audience and style, so spend time planning and leave a final stretch for proofreading, since accuracy of expression is part of what is judged. Practise producing polished, shaped writing within the time rather than drafting at length.

## Paper 3: Language Analysis

Paper 3 is 135 minutes and 50 marks, 25% of the A Level, and counts only towards the full A Level. **How to spend the time:** 135 / 50 = 2.7 minutes per mark. Analysis means connecting the features of a text to its purpose, context and audience, so build a vocabulary of linguistic frameworks, and always move from feature to effect to meaning.

## Paper 4: Language Topics

Paper 4 is 135 minutes and 50 marks, 25% of the A Level, and also counts only towards the full A Level. **How to spend the time:** 2.7 minutes per mark again. Prepare the language topics your centre has taught in depth, with a stock of concepts, research findings and examples that you can deploy flexibly.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

| Paper | Minutes | Marks | Minutes per mark |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 135 | 50 | 2.7 |
| 2 | 120 | 50 | 2.4 |
| 3 | 135 | 50 | 2.7 |
| 4 | 135 | 50 | 2.7 |

Weightings: 25 x 4 = 100. Total marks: 4 x 50 = 200. Because the rate is generous, protect planning and checking time rather than writing for the full period without a structure.

## Command words and question styles

Expect verbs such as "analyse", "compare", "discuss" and "evaluate". "Analyse" asks you to examine how language works, "compare" asks for similarities and differences kept in balance, and "discuss" asks for a developed, reasoned view. In every case, give specific evidence from the text, name the feature precisely, and explain its effect on the reader.

## Worked practice scenario: analysing a short original extract

**Extract (original):** "Residents are reminded that the north car park will close on Friday. We know this is inconvenient, and we thank you for your patience."

**Plan:**

```
Purpose  : to inform and to maintain goodwill
Features : passive/reminder framing; first-person plural "we"; direct address "you"
Effect   : institutional voice softened by apology and gratitude
Context  : a public notice from an organisation to a community
```

**Model paragraph:** The notice opens with the formal verb phrase "are reminded", which sets an institutional tone and presents the closure as an official matter. The shift to the first-person plural in "We know this is inconvenient" then humanises the organisation, positioning it as sympathetic rather than distant. The closing "thank you for your patience" uses direct address to create a sense of relationship with the reader, though it also presumes compliance, since patience is thanked before it has been given. Together these choices show a text balancing authority with courtesy.

## Planning your final weeks

Give each paper its own routine. For Papers 1 and 3, read a varied range of short texts and practise annotating them for purpose, audience and language choices, then writing one developed response against the clock. For Paper 2, collect models of well-shaped writing, and practise producing a plan, a draft and a proofread version within 120 minutes. For Paper 4, rehearse the topics you have studied by writing short summaries from memory and linking each to examples. Because three of the four papers run to 2 hours 15 minutes, practise sustained concentration by sitting at least one full-length paper before the examination.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm which route you are entered for.
- Check with your teacher whether your series is the final one for this syllabus edition.
- Practise each paper in full, in timed conditions.
- Learn a short list of linguistic terms and use them accurately.
- Plan writing before drafting.
- Use short, embedded quotations.
- Link each feature to an effect and a meaning.
- Leave time to proofread.
