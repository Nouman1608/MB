## Section A: short questions

**1.** Define *dramatic monologue* and explain why it matters to distinguish the poet from the speaker
in one. **[2]**

**2.** Identify the method in these original lines and explain its effect: *"and still the river ran
/ beneath the bridge they burned / as if it had not heard."* **[2]**

**3.** Explain the effect of the full stop in the middle of this original line: *"a child has chalked a
door. That's all."* **[2]**

**4.** What is a *volta*? Explain one effect it can have in a poem. **[2]**

**5.** Improve this comparison so that it would earn credit for AO2: *"Both poems are about power and
they both use imagery."* **[3]**

**6.** Give three reasons for choosing a particular second poem from your cluster in the exam. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

Read the two original poems, then answer Questions 7 and 8.

> *Original poem 1, "The Statue"*
> They raised him in the square in bronze,
> one hand held out to bless the crowd;
> for fifty years the pigeons came
> and no one said his name aloud.
> Then came the night of ropes and shouts,
> the crane, the cheering, and the fall.
> This morning, on the empty plinth,
> a child has chalked a door. That's all.

> *Original poem 2, "Weeds"*
> Between the paving slabs
> the dandelions
> are planning something.
> Nobody gave them permission.
> Nobody ever does.
> Next spring
> the car park
> will be theirs.

**7.** Compare how the poets present power in "The Statue" and "Weeds". **[12]**

**8.** Write a comparative opening paragraph for Question 7 that states one similarity and one
difference in method. **[4]**

**9.** For your own studied cluster, build a mini comparison grid for one poem of your choice: list
three other poems you could compare it with and, for each, one shared idea and one difference in
method. **[6]**

**10.** Using your own cluster, choose one poem as if it were the named poem on the paper. *Compare
how poets present the effects of power or conflict, or ideas about identity, in your chosen poem and
in one other poem from your cluster.* **[30]**

**11.** Using your own cluster, choose a different poem as the named poem. *Compare how poets present
memory or the past in your chosen poem and in one other poem from your cluster.* **[30]**

## Answers

**1.** A dramatic monologue is a poem in which one character speaks, usually to a silent listener,
often revealing more about themselves than they intend [1]. The speaker is a persona whose views the
poet may criticise, so treating them as the poet's own views can lead to a misreading [1].

**2.** Enjambment across the line breaks [1]. The sentence keeps flowing as the river does,
suggesting nature continues indifferently despite human destruction [1].

**3.** The full stop creates a caesura that cuts the line short [1]. "That's all" lands abruptly,
making the change seem small and understated while hinting that something new, a door, has replaced
the old power [1].

**4.** A volta is a turn in thought, tone or argument, often found in sonnets [1]. It can show a
change of attitude, such as a speaker moving from doubt to certainty, which shapes the poem's
meaning [1].

**5.** Example: *"Both poets present power as temporary, but whereas 'The Statue' uses a regular,
rhymed form that collapses into a short, flat ending, 'Weeds' uses free verse and short lines to
suggest a quiet, growing force."* [1] It names a specific method in each poem [1] and compares the
effects, not just the subject [1].

**6.** A clear link to the idea in the question [1]; a meaningful similarity and difference in
method to explore, not just shared subject matter [1]; quotations you can recall accurately under
exam conditions [1].

**7.** Award one mark per developed comparative point, up to 12:

- Both poems present power as something that changes hands over time [1].
- "The Statue" presents human, state power as imposing: bronze and a blessing gesture suggest
  authority and permanence [1].
- "Weeds" presents power in tiny, overlooked things: dandelions "between the paving slabs" [1].
- The pigeons and the silence ("no one said his name aloud") show the statue's power already fading
  before it falls [1].
- The personification of dandelions "planning something" gives nature intention and quiet menace,
  while the statue, ironically, has no agency at all [1].
- "The Statue" uses regular quatrains and an ABCB rhyme scheme, suggesting order, which is then
  broken by the event of the fall [1].
- "Weeds" uses free verse and very short lines, mimicking slow, irregular growth [1].
- The list "the crane, the cheering, and the fall" accelerates the poem towards a sudden, violent
  change [1].
- The repetition of "Nobody" in "Weeds" mocks the idea of permission, presenting human rules as
  powerless against nature [1].
- The endings differ: "That's all" is understated and open, while "will be theirs" is confident and
  final [1].
- The chalked door may symbolise a new beginning made by the powerless, which links to the
  dandelions as another small force that wins [1].
- Overall judgement: "The Statue" shows power overthrown suddenly by people; "Weeds" shows power
  gained slowly and inevitably, perhaps suggesting nature's power outlasts human authority [1].

**8.** Award marks as follows:

- A clear statement of the shared idea about power across both poems [1].
- One similarity in method, with brief evidence [1].
- One difference in method, with brief evidence [1].
- Comparative connectives used to keep both poems together [1].

**9.** Award marks as follows:

- Three relevant partner poems from the same cluster [1].
- A shared idea for each partner that is specific, not a general label [2].
- A difference in method for each partner, such as form, voice, structure or imagery [2].
- At least one short quotation or precise reference attached to the grid [1].

**10.** AQA marks this holistically against level descriptors; the weighting is a guide only.

- **AO1 [12]:** a comparative argument from the opening, with apt references from both poems; a
  personal judgement about how the poets' attitudes compare.
- **AO2 [12]:** comparison of voice, form, structure and language in both poems, with effects
  explained and terminology used precisely; balanced coverage of the remembered poem.
- **AO3 [6]:** relevant context for each poem, used to explain a similarity or difference.

**11.** As for Question 10, marked holistically:

- **AO1 [12]:** a clear comparison of how each poem presents memory or the past, with references
  from both and a judgement about the poets' attitudes.
- **AO2 [12]:** methods compared side by side, such as tense shifts, imagery of objects or places,
  stanza structure, and endings that return to or move beyond the past.
- **AO3 [6]:** context, such as the period or circumstances of each poem, used where it explains
  why memory is presented as it is.

## Using these questions well

Answer Questions 1 to 8 using the original poems to practise comparative writing without the pressure
of recall. Then use Question 9 to test your grid. Write Question 10 or 11 in about 45 minutes,
printing or copying only your chosen "named" poem and writing the second from memory, just as in the
exam.
