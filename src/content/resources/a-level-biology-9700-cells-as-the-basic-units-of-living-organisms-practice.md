## Questions

**1.** Name the structure described in each case.

**(a)** A dense region inside the nucleus, with no membrane, that makes ribosomal RNA. **[1]**
**(b)** The partially permeable membrane around the large permanent vacuole of a plant cell. **[1]**
**(c)** Narrow channels through the walls of neighbouring plant cells that link their cytoplasm. **[1]**

**2.** Name the two organelles of a eukaryotic cell that contain 70S ribosomes, and state one feature of their genetic material that they share with a typical bacterium. **[3]**

**3.** A muscle cell has many mitochondria.

**(a)** State the process in mitochondria that provides the cell with ATP. **[1]**
**(b)** Name two energy-requiring processes in cells that use this ATP. **[2]**

**4.** All viruses are described as non-cellular.

**(a)** Name the two structures found in every virus. **[2]**
**(b)** Some viruses have an extra outer layer. Name this layer and the type of molecule it is made of. **[2]**
**(c)** State two structural features of a typical bacterium that a virus does not have. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator allowed) A transmission electron micrograph shows a rod-shaped cell. A scale bar labelled 0.5 µm measures 8 mm. The cell measures 52 mm long.

**(a)** Calculate the magnification of the micrograph. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the actual length of the cell in µm. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says the structure could be a mitochondrion. State two features visible in the micrograph that would show it is a bacterium. **[2]**

**6.** Compare the structure and function of cilia and microvilli. **[4]**

**7.** Animal cells contain centrioles and microtubules.

**(a)** Describe the structure of a centriole. **[2]**
**(b)** State two functions of microtubules in a cell. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) A student counted the mitochondria visible in electron micrograph sections of five cells of each of two types. Cell type A comes from a tissue that absorbs substances by active transport. Cell type B is a fat-storage cell. The results are hypothetical.

| Cell | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type A | 24 | 31 | 27 | 29 | 34 |
| Type B | 9 | 12 | 8 | 11 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean number of mitochondria for each cell type. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the mean for type A is greater than the mean for type B. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest an explanation for the difference. **[2]**

**9.** The Golgi body and lysosomes are found in animal cells.

**(a)** Describe the appearance of the Golgi body in a transmission electron micrograph. **[2]**
**(b)** Outline how lysosomes are formed and what they do. **[2]**

**10.** A typical bacterium, a liver cell and a palisade mesophyll cell are compared.

**(a)** Copy and complete the table with a tick (present) or a cross (absent) for each cell. **[5]**

| Feature | Bacterium | Liver cell | Palisade cell |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cell wall | | | |
| Nucleus with nuclear envelope | | | |
| 80S ribosomes | | | |
| Circular DNA | | | |
| Chloroplasts | | | |

**(b)** A student sees 70S ribosomes in an electron micrograph and concludes that the cell must be prokaryotic. Explain why this conclusion is not valid. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A transmission electron micrograph at × 12 000 shows a cell from the lining of a mammalian organ. It shows:

- a nucleus with an image diameter of 84 mm, containing a dark, dense region
- many parallel flattened membrane sacs covered with small dots
- a large curved stack of membrane sacs, with many vesicles near it and near the cell surface
- many oval organelles with two membranes, the inner one folded
- on one surface, a row of small finger-like folds of the membrane with no microtubules inside
- no cell wall.

**(a)** Calculate the actual diameter of the nucleus in µm. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the finger-like folds and state their function. **[2]**
**(c)** Give two pieces of evidence from the description that this is an animal cell, not a plant cell. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest, with a reason, what this cell produces in large amounts. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Nucleolus** [1]
**(b)** **Tonoplast** [1]
**(c)** **Plasmodesmata** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Naming questions reward the exact syllabus term; "vacuole membrane" for (b) or "pores" for (c) is unlikely to be accepted, and a misspelling that could be another structure (for example "nucleus" for nucleolus) scores zero.

**2.** **Mitochondria** [1]; **chloroplasts** [1]. Both contain **small circular DNA**, like a bacterium [1].
*Examiner insight:* "DNA" alone does not earn the third mark: the shared feature the syllabus names is that the DNA is small and circular, so the shape word must be there.

**3. (a)** **Aerobic respiration** [1]
**(b)** Any two, one mark each: protein synthesis; active transport; muscle contraction; cell division; movement of vesicles. **Muscle contraction** [1]; **protein synthesis** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Avoid writing that respiration "makes energy" or "produces energy": energy is released and transferred to ATP, and wording that says energy is created is usually not credited.

**4. (a)** A **nucleic acid core** (DNA or RNA) [1]; a **protein capsid** [1].
**(b)** An **envelope** [1] made of **phospholipids** [1].
**(c)** Any two, one mark each: cell wall of peptidoglycan; ribosomes (70S); cytoplasm; cell surface membrane. **Peptidoglycan cell wall** [1]; **70S ribosomes** [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (a), "DNA and RNA" as the core is wrong: a virus has one or the other, so write "DNA or RNA" to keep the mark.

**5. (a)** 8 mm = 8000 µm; magnification = 8000 ÷ 0.5 [1] = **× 16 000** [1]
**(b)** Length = 52 mm ÷ 16 000 = 52 000 µm ÷ 16 000 [1] = **3.25 µm** [1]
**(c)** Any two, one mark each: a cell wall outside the membrane; no double membrane (envelope) around it; no cristae; ribosomes free in the cytoplasm; DNA free in the cytoplasm. **Cell wall present** [1]; **no double membrane or cristae** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Magnification has no units, so "× 16 000 µm" can lose the accuracy mark; allow error carried forward in (b) if a wrong magnification from (a) is used correctly.

**6.** Both are extensions of the cell surface covered by the cell surface membrane [1]. Cilia contain a **9 + 2 arrangement of microtubules**, while microvilli have no microtubule core [1]. Cilia grow from a **basal body**, and microvilli do not; cilia are also much longer [1]. Cilia **beat to move fluid or mucus** across a surface, while microvilli **increase the surface area for absorption** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Compare" needs both structures named in each point; separate descriptions of cilia, then of microvilli, usually score fewer marks than matched statements.

**7. (a)** A short hollow **cylinder** [1] made of **nine triplets of microtubules** arranged in a ring; centrioles occur in pairs at right angles [1].
**(b)** Any two, one mark each: form the spindle in cell division; form the cytoskeleton that supports the cell; move vesicles or organelles; form the core of cilia. **Spindle formation** [1]; **cytoskeleton / moving organelles** [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Nine microtubules" without "triplets" or "groups of three" is an incomplete structural point and may not get the second mark in (a).

**8. (a)** Type A: (24 + 31 + 27 + 29 + 34) ÷ 5 = **29** [1]; type B: (9 + 12 + 8 + 11 + 10) ÷ 5 = **10** [1]
**(b)** (29 − 10) ÷ 10 × 100 [1] = **190%** [1]
**(c)** Active transport is an energy-requiring process that uses ATP [1]; type A cells need more ATP, which is made by aerobic respiration in mitochondria [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b) the change is divided by the smaller value (type B); dividing by 29 gives about 66% and loses the accuracy mark, though the method mark may still be given if the subtraction is shown.

**9. (a)** A stack of **flattened, curved membrane sacs** [1], with **vesicles** at the edges [1].
**(b)** Lysosomes **bud off from the Golgi body** as vesicles [1]; they contain **hydrolytic enzymes** that digest worn-out organelles or material taken into the cell [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mentioning ribosomes on the Golgi body is a contradiction that cancels the mark: the dots belong to the RER, not the Golgi.

**10. (a)** One mark per correct row:
Cell wall: ✓ ✗ ✓ [1]
Nucleus with nuclear envelope: ✗ ✓ ✓ [1]
80S ribosomes: ✗ ✓ ✓ [1]
Circular DNA: ✓ ✓ ✓ (in mitochondria, and in chloroplasts for the palisade cell) [1]
Chloroplasts: ✗ ✗ ✓ [1]
**(b)** Eukaryotic cells also contain **70S ribosomes inside mitochondria and chloroplasts** [1], so the cell is only prokaryotic if it also has **no nucleus and no double-membrane organelles** [1].
*Examiner insight:* The circular DNA row is the one most often marked wrong: the syllabus states that mitochondria and chloroplasts contain small circular DNA, so all three cells get a tick.

**11. (a)** 84 mm = 84 000 µm; 84 000 ÷ 12 000 [1] = **7 µm** [1]
**(b)** **Microvilli** [1]; they **increase the surface area for absorption** [1].
**(c)** Any two, one mark each: no cell wall; no chloroplasts; no large permanent vacuole; microvilli present. **No cell wall** [1]; **microvilli present / no chloroplasts or large vacuole** [1].
**(d)** **Protein** (for secretion) [1]; there is a lot of rough endoplasmic reticulum and a large Golgi body with many vesicles near the cell surface [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the evidence must come from the description given; "it has centrioles" is not credited because the micrograph description does not mention them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "DNA" instead of "small circular DNA" for mitochondria and chloroplasts.
- Treating 70S ribosomes as proof that a cell is prokaryotic.
- Giving units with a magnification, or not converting mm to µm before dividing.
- Dividing by the wrong value in a percentage change calculation.
- Writing separate descriptions in a "compare" question instead of matched points.
- Saying a virus core is "DNA and RNA".
- Describing a centriole as nine single microtubules rather than nine triplets.
- Placing ribosomes on the Golgi body or on the SER.
- Using evidence that is not in the micrograph or description given.
