## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by an insurance **premium**. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** purposes of insurance. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the type of insurance that would pay compensation to a customer who slips on a wet floor in a shop. **[1]**

**4.** Identify the type of insurance that covers an employee injured by faulty machinery. **[1]**

**5.** State the correct order of these documents: policy, proposal form, cover note. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A factory is valued at $250,000. The fire insurance rate is 0.36%.

**(a)** Calculate the annual premium. **[2]**

**(b)** The factory has a 25% no-claims discount. Calculate the premium after the discount. **[2]**

**7.** An insurer covers 2,000 shops, each paying a premium of $250. In a year it expects 15 claims averaging $24,000.

**(a)** Calculate the total premiums received. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the expected claims. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the amount left for running costs and profit. **[1]**

**8.** Explain what is meant by **pooling of risk**. **[3]**

**9.** A restaurant is closed for six weeks after a kitchen fire. Explain **two** types of insurance that would help the owner. **[4]**

**10.** Describe the purpose of a **proposal form**. **[2]**

**11.** Explain **two** services provided by an insurance broker. **[4]**

**12.** A delivery company has an excess of $200 on its motor policy. A van is damaged and the repair costs $1,350. Calculate how much the insurer pays and explain what excess means. **[3]**

**13.** Discuss whether a small business with a tight budget should buy insurance. **[5]**

**14.** A delivery firm insures 5 vans. The basic premium is $480 for each van. The insurer adds a 10% loading because the vans travel through a busy city.

**(a)** Calculate the total basic premium. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the loading and the total premium the firm pays. **[2]**

**15.** Name the type of insurance that is most suitable in each case. **[3]**

**(a)** A customer is hurt by a falling shelf in a shop.

**(b)** A burglar steals stock from a warehouse.

**(c)** A factory loses its profits for two months while repairs are carried out after a flood.

## Answers

**1.** The payment made to an insurance company in return for cover [1].

**2.** Any two from: risk reduction; compensation; financial protection; business confidence; investment [1 each].

**3.** Public liability [1].

**4.** Employer's liability [1].

**5.** Proposal form, cover note, policy [1].

**6.** **(a)** 0.36% × 250,000 [1] = $900 [1].

**(b)** Discount = 25% × 900 = $225 [1]. Premium = 900 − 225 = $675 [1].

**7.** **(a)** 2,000 × 250 = $500,000 [1].

**(b)** 15 × 24,000 = $360,000 [1].

**(c)** 500,000 − 360,000 = $140,000 [1].

**8.** Many businesses or people each pay a small premium into a common fund [1]. Only a few suffer a loss in any year [1], and the fund pays compensation to them, so the cost of the loss is shared [1].

**9.** Fire insurance [1] pays for repairs to the kitchen and replacement of damaged equipment [1]. Consequential loss insurance [1] pays for the lost profit and extra costs while the restaurant is shut, so wages and bills can be paid [1].

**10.** It is completed by the person wanting insurance, giving details of the risk [1], so the insurer can decide whether to accept it and what premium to charge [1].

**11.** Any two developed: compares policies from several insurers [1] to find suitable cover at a fair price [1]; gives expert advice on the type and amount of cover [1] so the customer does not under-insure [1]; helps with forms and claims [1], saving time [1]. Maximum 4.

**12.** The excess is the amount the insured pays towards each claim [1]. The insurer pays 1,350 − 200 [1] = $1,150 [1].

**13.** Any five from: insurance protects against large losses that could close the business [1]; compensation helps recovery after a fire or theft [1]; may be needed by law (employer's liability) [1] or by lenders [1]; but premiums are an extra cost [1]; some risks are unlikely, so money may seem wasted [1]; the business can reduce cost by choosing only important cover [1]; a justified conclusion, for example buying only essential cover [1]. Maximum 5.

**14.** **(a)** 5 × 480 = $2,400 [1].

**(b)** Loading = 10% × 2,400 = $240 [1]. Total premium = 2,400 + 240 = $2,640 [1].

**15.** **(a)** Public liability [1]. **(b)** Theft [1]. **(c)** Consequential loss [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 3: choosing employer's liability for a customer.
- Question 6: applying the discount to the sum insured instead of the premium.
- Question 7: forgetting to subtract the claims from the premiums.
- Question 9: naming only fire insurance.
- Question 12: adding the excess instead of subtracting it.
- Question 13: no judgement.

## Quick reference for checking your answers

| Item | Key point |
|---|---|
| Premium | Sum insured × rate |
| Pooling | Many pay, few claim |
| Documents | Proposal, cover note, policy |
| Public liability | Harm to the public |
| Employer's liability | Harm to employees |
