## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify **two** non-wage factors that may influence a person's choice of occupation. **[2]**

**2.** Which change would shift the demand for car-factory workers to the right?

A: a fall in demand for new cars

B: a rise in the productivity of car-factory workers

C: a rise in the number of people trained to work in car factories

D: a fall in the price of robots that can do the same tasks

**[1]**

**3.** Define division of labour. **[2]**

**4.** A minimum wage is set below the equilibrium wage in a labour market. State the effect on employment. **[1]**

**5.** Which of the following is most likely to explain why unskilled workers earn less than skilled workers?

A: the supply of unskilled workers is small

B: the demand for unskilled workers is high

C: the supply of unskilled workers is large

D: unskilled workers have strong bargaining power

**[1]**

**6.** A builder earns 1,350 for a 45-hour week. A teacher earns 1,200 for a 37.5-hour week. Calculate the hourly pay of each. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** The table shows the invented labour market for hotel workers in a tourist town.

| Wage per hour | Workers demanded | Workers supplied |
|---|---|---|
| 8 | 900 | 500 |
| 9 | 800 | 600 |
| 10 | 700 | 700 |
| 11 | 600 | 800 |
| 12 | 500 | 900 |

**(a)** State the equilibrium wage and employment. **[2]**

**(b)** The government sets a minimum wage of 11 an hour. Calculate the surplus of workers that results. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **one** benefit and **one** cost of this minimum wage for hotel workers. **[4]**

**8.** Using a labour-market diagram, explain the effect on the wage of nurses if the length of nurse training is increased from three years to four. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** reasons why a person might choose a job in the public sector that pays less than a similar private-sector job. **[4]**

**10.** A furniture firm reorganises production so that each of its 10 workers performs one task. Weekly output rises from 50 chairs to 75 chairs.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in output per worker. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** disadvantages of division of labour for the firm. **[4]**

**11.** Analyse why workers in the tertiary sector of a developed economy often earn more than workers in the primary sector of a low-income economy. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether a national minimum wage is the best way to raise the earnings of low-paid workers. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: working hours or holidays [1]; job security [1]; promotion prospects; training; working conditions; fringe benefits; job satisfaction; location. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* More productive workers are worth more to the firm, so it demands more of them at each wage. C shifts supply, not demand.

**3.** Splitting a production process into separate tasks [1]; with each worker specialising in one task [1]. **[2]**

**4.** No change in employment [1], because the market wage is already above the minimum. **[1]**

**5.** C [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Builder: 1,350 / 45 = **30** an hour [1]; teacher: 1,200 / 37.5 = **32** an hour [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Wage **10** an hour [1]; employment **700** workers [1].

**(b)** Supplied 800 - demanded 600 = **200** workers [1].

**(c)** Benefit: the 600 workers who keep jobs earn 11 instead of 10, a 10% rise [1], raising their living standards [1]. Cost: employment falls from 700 to 600 [1], so 100 workers who had jobs lose them and earn nothing from the hotels [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Draw wage rate on the vertical axis and quantity of nurses on the horizontal axis, with D and S crossing at W and Q [1]. Longer training makes entry slower and more costly, so fewer people are willing and able to become nurses [1]. The supply curve shifts left to S1 [1]. The equilibrium wage rises from W to W1 and the number of nurses employed falls from Q to Q1 [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Greater job security: public-sector jobs may be less likely to be cut when demand falls [1], so the worker values the reduced risk of unemployment above the extra pay [1]. Better fringe benefits such as a pension [1], which may be worth more over a lifetime than the pay difference [1]. (Accept job satisfaction from serving the public, or regular hours, developed.) **[4]**

**10. (a)** Before: 50 / 10 = 5 chairs per worker; after: 75 / 10 = 7.5 [1]; change = (7.5 - 5) / 5 x 100 = **50%** [1].

**(b)** If one worker is absent, the stage they perform stops [1], so the whole production line halts and output is lost [1]. Workers may become bored with repetitive tasks [1], leading to mistakes, poor quality and higher staff turnover [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Tertiary-sector workers in a developed economy are often skilled, so the supply of such labour is limited [1]. Their output, such as financial or medical services, has a high value, so demand for their labour is high [1]. In a low-income economy many people seek farm work that needs few skills, so the supply of primary-sector labour is large [1]. Productivity per farm worker is often low because of little capital, so demand for labour is weak [1]. High demand and limited supply give a high wage in the first case; low demand and plentiful supply give a low wage in the second [1]. **[5]**

**12.** For: a minimum wage directly raises the pay of the lowest-paid workers who keep their jobs [1]; it is quick to introduce and can reduce poverty among working households [1].
Against: if set above the market wage, firms may employ fewer workers, so some low-paid workers lose jobs [1]; firms may pass higher costs on as higher prices [1]. Education and training raise workers' productivity and skills, which raises demand for their labour and their wages in the long run [1].
Judgement: a minimum wage helps quickly if set close to the market wage, but training is a better long-term way to raise earnings because it does not risk job losses; the two work best together [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Shifting labour demand when the cause affects supply, such as training length.
- Leaving out axis labels or the new equilibrium on a labour-market diagram.
- Ignoring workers who lose jobs when explaining a minimum wage above equilibrium.
- Comparing pay per week when hours differ.
- Giving advantages of division of labour when the question asks for disadvantages.
- Ending a "discuss" answer without a judgement.
