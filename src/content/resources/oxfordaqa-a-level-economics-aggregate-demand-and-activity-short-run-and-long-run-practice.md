## Questions

**1.** Explain what economists mean by the multiplier. **[2]**

**2.** In Country B the marginal propensity to save is 0.07, to tax 0.18 and to import 0.25. Investment rises by 45m. Find the value of the multiplier and the resulting change in income. **[3]**

**3.** In Country C, of each extra 100 units of income, 62 are spent on domestically produced goods. Exports fall by 19m. Calculate the change in national income. **[3]**

**4.** Country D has normal capacity output of 3,500m and actual output of 3,360m. Its government believes the multiplier is 3.5.

**(a)** Calculate the injection needed to close the gap. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one reason why the rise in income might be smaller than planned. **[2]**

**5.** Country E receives an injection of 50m. In every round, 70% of new income is spent on domestic output.

**(a)** Calculate the new spending in each of the first three rounds. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total change in income once all rounds are complete. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the full effect may take a long time to appear. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between moving along SRAS and shifting it, giving one cause of each. **[4]**

**7.** Classify each event below as a movement along SRAS or a shift of SRAS, and give its direction. **[5]**
(i) A fall in raw material prices.
(ii) A rise in AD that raises the price level.
(iii) A rise in indirect taxes.
(iv) A fall in AD that lowers the price level.
(v) A rise in productivity.

**8.** A firm pays 18 an hour for output of 36 units an hour. Money wages rise 8% and productivity rises 3%. Calculate the percentage change in labour cost per unit and state the effect on SRAS. **[4]**

**9.** Country F has 6.4m workers, each producing 90,000 a year.

**(a)** Calculate normal capacity output in billions. **[1]**
**(b)** The workforce rises 1.5% and output per worker 2.5%. Calculate the new capacity, its percentage growth, and state what happens to LRAS. **[3]**

**10.** Explain how any two determinants of LRAS named in the specification can shift LRAS. **[6]**

**11.** Analyse why an economy may stay below its normal capacity level of output for many years. **[8]**

**12.** Country G has capacity output of 5,000m and actual output of 4,700m. Its multiplier is thought to be 3. Evaluate whether raising AD or raising LRAS is the better response. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** It shows how many times larger the final change in income is than the first injection [1]. The effect exceeds the injection because each recipient re-spends part of what they receive, creating further income [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain the re-spending mechanism as well as the size effect; a sentence that only says "income rises by more" leaves out the reason.

**2.** MPW = 0.07 + 0.18 + 0.25 = 0.50 [1]. k = 1 ÷ 0.50 = 2 [1]. Change in income = 45 × 2 = **90m** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write down the sum of the withdrawals before dividing; showing each step lets a reader follow your reasoning even if the arithmetic slips.

**3.** k = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.62) = 1 ÷ 0.38 = 2.63 [1]. Change = −19 × 2.63 [1] = **−50m** (income falls by 50m) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The sign matters: a fall in an injection must give a fall in income, so state the direction as well as the size.

**4. (a)** Gap = 3,500 − 3,360 = 140m [1]. Injection = 140 ÷ 3.5 = **40m** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Leakages may be larger than assumed, for example a higher marginal propensity to import [1]. The multiplier is then smaller; if k were 2.8, 40m would raise income by only 112m [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation needs the link from the reason to the size of k; naming "leakages" alone gives less than showing how they change the result.

**5. (a)** Round 1 = 50m [1]. Round 2 = 35m, round 3 = 24.5m [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** k = 1 ÷ (1 − 0.7) = 3.33 [1]. Total = 50 × 3.33 = **166.7m** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Each round needs time for people to receive income and then decide to spend it [1]. So the later rounds spread over many months [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* In a table or list of rounds, label which round each figure belongs to, so the pattern of 70% falling away each round is clear.

**6.** The economy moves along SRAS when the price level alters [1], for example after a rise in AD that lifts prices and output [1]. The curve itself shifts when production costs alter [1], for example higher money wage rates moving SRAS to the left [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Pairing each cause with its direction of effect usually earns more than listing causes without saying what they do to the curve.

**7.** (i) Shift right [1]. (ii) Movement up along SRAS [1]. (iii) Shift left [1]. (iv) Movement down along SRAS [1]. (v) Shift right [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write both parts, the type and the direction, for every item; answers that give only "shift" leave out half of what is asked.

**8.** Starting cost = 18 ÷ 36 = 0.50 per unit [1]. New cost = (18 × 1.08) ÷ (36 × 1.03) = 19.44 ÷ 37.08 = 0.524 [1]. Change = **+4.9%** [1]. Costs per unit rise, so SRAS shifts left [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare cost per unit, not wages alone; a wage rise matched by productivity growth would leave SRAS where it was.

**9. (a)** 6.4m × 90,000 = **576bn** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** New capacity = 576 × 1.015 × 1.025 = **599.3bn** [1]. Growth = **4.04%**, which is more than 1.5% + 2.5% because the two compound [1]. LRAS shifts right, which is underlying economic growth [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Multiply the growth factors rather than adding the percentages; adding gives 4.0% and misses the compounding.

**10.** Any two, 3 marks each. Example 1: capital stock rises [1]; each worker has more equipment, so more output per worker [1]; normal capacity rises and LRAS shifts right [1]. Example 2: working population rises, for example through higher participation [1]; more labour is available at normal capacity [1]; LRAS shifts right [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each determinant should be followed through to normal capacity output; a list of eight determinants with no explanation earns little.

**11.** AD falls, for example because of lower confidence [1]. Firms cut output and jobs, so unemployment rises [1]. Wages and prices are slow to fall because workers resist pay cuts and firms are cautious [1]. So costs fall slowly and SRAS shifts right only slowly [1]. Weak incomes and low confidence keep consumption and investment low, so AD stays low [1]. The economy settles at an equilibrium with output below LRAS [1]. This can continue for many years [1]. The Keynesian view is therefore that a rise in AD may be needed [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Build a chain of linked steps, each starting from the previous one; stating the end result without the chain is a conclusion, not an analysis.

**12.** Gap = 4,700 − 5,000 = −300m, or −6.0% [1]. Output is below capacity, so the main problem is deficient demand [1]. With k = 3, an injection of 300 ÷ 3 = 100m would close the gap [1]. If k is only 2, 150m is needed, so the estimate is uncertain [1]. Raising AD can use spare resources quickly without much inflation [1]. Raising LRAS lifts capacity but takes years [1], and if AD does not also rise, the gap widens because capacity is larger [1]. If capacity were overestimated, a stimulus could raise prices rather than output [1]. A rise in AD cannot raise normal capacity permanently, so long-term growth needs LRAS [1]. Judgement: with a large gap, raise AD first, then LRAS, because it matches the problem and its timescale [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation earns more when it reaches a supported judgement about this country's gap, not when it lists advantages of both options without weighing them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by MPC instead of by 1 minus MPC, or by MPW.
- Leaving out tax or imports when adding the withdrawals.
- Losing the negative sign when an injection falls.
- Treating the multiplier as the change in income.
- Shifting SRAS when the price level changes.
- Adding growth rates instead of compounding them.
- Naming a determinant of LRAS without explaining the link to capacity.
- Writing an evaluation that lists points without a judgement.
