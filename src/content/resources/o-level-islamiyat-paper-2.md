This guide covers **Paper 2**, one of two compulsory papers for
Cambridge O Level Islamiyat 2058, 2024-2025 series. As confirmed
against 2058's own specification PDF, it shares an identical Paper 1
/ Paper 2 four-topic structure with Cambridge IGCSE Islamiyat 0493.

## Where this fits in 2058

Paper 1 (not covered in this guide) covers the Qur'an and the life of
the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh). Paper 2 moves to the Hadiths, the period
of rule of the four Rightly Guided Caliphs, and the Articles of Faith
and Pillars of Islam. Together the two papers form the whole of 2058
-- there is no separate topic list beyond these four sub-topics per
paper.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL ISLAMIYAT 2058 — PAPER 2**

- 2.1 Major Teachings in the Hadiths of the Prophet — the central
  teachings recorded in the sayings and practice of the Prophet
  Muhammad (pbuh)
- 2.2 The History and Importance of the Hadiths — how the Hadiths were
  preserved, compiled and authenticated, and their significance
  alongside the Qur'an
- 2.3 The Period of Rule of the Rightly Guided Caliphs and Their
  Importance as Leaders — the leadership of Abu Bakr, Umar, Uthman and
  Ali, and their significance for the early Muslim community
- 2.4 The Articles of Faith and the Pillars of Islam — the six
  Articles of Faith and the five Pillars of Islam, and their meaning
  and practice

## How to approach it

As with Paper 1, each paper mixes a compulsory question with a choice
from the remainder, so even coverage across all four sub-topics
matters more than deep preparation on only one or two. 2.4 is the
sub-topic most likely to be under-revised because it feels familiar
from general knowledge -- treat the Articles of Faith and Pillars of
Islam with the same factual rigour as the rest of the paper, naming
each one precisely and explaining its significance rather than
assuming it needs no revision. For 2.3, build a simple timeline of
the four Caliphs in order, with one or two defining events and
challenges for each reign, since questions often ask candidates to
compare leadership across the period rather than describe one Caliph
in isolation. As with 2.2, be ready to explain *why* the preservation
of the Hadiths mattered, not only how it happened.

## The Hadiths: teaching and preservation

The Hadiths are the recorded sayings, actions and approvals of the
Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), transmitted alongside the Qur'an as a second
source of guidance for Muslims. Where the Qur'an sets out core
principles, the Hadiths often provide the practical detail of how
those principles were lived out day to day. Their preservation relied
on a chain of transmission (isnad) linking each reported saying back
to a witness of the Prophet's life, and later scholars developed
rigorous methods to assess the reliability of each chain -- this
process of authentication is central to why the Hadiths are treated
as a trustworthy record.

## The Rightly Guided Caliphs

Following the death of the Prophet Muhammad (pbuh), the Muslim
community was led in succession by four companions known as the
Rightly Guided Caliphs (al-Khulafa al-Rashidun): Abu Bakr, Umar,
Uthman and Ali.

| Caliph | Notable for |
|---|---|
| Abu Bakr | Consolidating the community after the Prophet's death |
| Umar | Territorial expansion and administrative organisation |
| Uthman | Standardising the compilation of the Qur'an |
| Ali | Leadership during a period of internal division |

Each reign is examined both for its specific achievements and for
the qualities of leadership it demonstrated, which the syllabus
frames as a model for the early Muslim community.

## The Articles of Faith and Pillars of Islam

The six Articles of Faith are the core beliefs of Islam: belief in
Allah, His angels, His revealed books, His prophets, the Day of
Judgement, and divine decree (qadar). The five Pillars of Islam are
the framework of practice built on that belief: the declaration of
faith (Shahada), prayer (Salah), almsgiving (Zakat), fasting during
Ramadan (Sawm), and pilgrimage to Makkah (Hajj, for those who are
able).

## Worked example

A question asks candidates to explain the importance of the isnad
(chain of transmission) in establishing the reliability of a Hadith.
A strong answer would state that the isnad names every person in the
chain from the Prophet (pbuh) to the person recording the Hadith,
that scholars checked each narrator's character and memory, and that
this scrutiny is why some Hadiths are classified as more reliable
than others -- linking the *mechanism* of preservation to *why* it
matters is what separates a strong answer from a purely descriptive
one.

## Common mistakes

Listing the Articles of Faith or Pillars of Islam without explaining
their meaning or significance, on the assumption that naming them is
sufficient. Describing the Rightly Guided Caliphs in isolation rather
than comparing their leadership across the period. Treating the
Hadiths as identical in status to the Qur'an rather than explaining
their distinct but complementary role. Under-revising 2.4 because it
feels more familiar than the historical sub-topics.

## Quick revision checklist

- Explain the major teachings recorded in the Hadiths and how they
  complement the Qur'an.
- Describe how the Hadiths were preserved and authenticated, and why
  that process matters.
- Sequence the four Rightly Guided Caliphs and give one defining
  feature of each reign.
- Name and explain the significance of each of the six Articles of
  Faith.
- Name and explain the practice and significance of each of the five
  Pillars of Islam.
