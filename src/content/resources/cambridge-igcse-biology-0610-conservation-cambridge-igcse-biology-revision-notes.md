These notes condense sub-topic **20.4 Conservation** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core. Outcomes 5 to 9 (forests, fish stocks, reasons for conservation, AI and IVF, and genetic variation) are **Extended only**.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [conservation study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-conservation-cambridge-igcse-biology/) first, then test yourself with the [conservation practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-conservation-cambridge-igcse-biology-practice/). The [human influences on ecosystems revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-human-influences-on-ecosystems-revision-notes/) cover the rest of topic 20.

## Core recall: sustainable resources

> A sustainable resource is one which is **produced as rapidly as it is removed** from the environment **so that it does not run out**.

- Resources that can be conserved and managed sustainably (syllabus list): **forests** and **fish stocks**.
- **Sustainable when:** amount removed ≤ amount produced by growth and reproduction.

## Core recall: six causes of endangerment and extinction

| Cause | Effect on population |
|---|---|
| Climate change | habitat or food changes faster than the species can move or adapt |
| Habitat destruction | food, shelter and breeding sites removed |
| Hunting | adults killed faster than replaced |
| Overharvesting | resource species removed faster than it reproduces |
| Pollution | organisms killed or breeding reduced |
| Introduced species | new predators, competitors or diseases |

Finish each with "so deaths exceed births and numbers fall".

## Core recall: four conservation methods

- **Monitoring and protecting species and habitats:** population counts; reserves; laws against hunting and trade.
- **Education:** people understand and avoid harm.
- **Captive breeding programmes:** breed in safety; release offspring into protected habitats.
- **Seed banks:** seeds dried and stored cold; stay alive for years; regrow lost populations.

## Extended recall: forests

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Education | demand for sustainable timber; people value forests |
| Protected areas | logging banned or limited |
| Quotas | felling limited to below regrowth |
| Replanting | felled trees replaced |

## Extended recall: fish stocks

| Method | How it helps |
|---|---|
| Education | consumers choose sustainable fish |
| Closed seasons | adults breed undisturbed |
| Protected areas | breeding and nursery grounds safe |
| Net types and mesh size | young fish escape and survive to breed |
| Quotas | catch kept below rate of reproduction |
| Monitoring | stock counts used to adjust quotas |

## Extended recall: reasons, AI and IVF, genetic variation

**Four reasons for conservation programmes:** maintain or increase biodiversity; reduce extinction; protect vulnerable ecosystems; maintain ecosystem functions (nutrient cycling; resource provision: food, drugs, fuel, genes).

- **AI:** sperm collected from a male, inserted into a female; frozen sperm transported, so animals need not move.
- **IVF:** eggs collected and fertilised outside the body; embryos placed into a female to develop.
- Both allow **unrelated parents** to be chosen.

**Small population risks:** reduced genetic variation → fewer individuals with alleles to survive a new disease or environmental change; more inbreeding, so harmful recessive alleles are expressed more often → higher risk of extinction.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sustainable resource | Produced as rapidly as it is removed, so it does not run out |
| Endangered species | A species at risk of extinction because its numbers are very low |
| Extinction | Permanent loss of every individual of a species |
| Captive breeding | Breeding endangered animals in zoos or centres, then releasing offspring |
| Seed bank | A store of dried seeds kept cold to preserve plant species |
| Quota | A limit on the amount of a resource that can be taken |
| Closed season (Extended) | A period, usually the breeding season, when fishing is banned |
| Artificial insemination (Extended) | Placing collected sperm into a female |
| In vitro fertilisation (Extended) | Fertilising eggs outside the body, then placing embryos in a female |
| Genetic variation | Differences in alleles between individuals of a population |

## Exam traps

- **Half a definition** of a sustainable resource.
- **Fossil fuels as sustainable.** They are not.
- **Causes without effects.** Say how each cause raises deaths or reduces births.
- **Captive breeding without release.** The aim is to return animals to the wild.
- **(Extended) "Bigger mesh catches fewer fish".** Say young fish escape and survive to breed.
- **(Extended) AI and IVF swapped.** AI: sperm into female. IVF: fertilisation in the laboratory.
- **(Extended) Genetic drift.** Not required; explain using reduced variation.

## Self-test

1. Describe a sustainable resource.
2. Name the two resources the syllabus says can be managed sustainably.
3. Explain how introduced species can make a native species endangered.
4. Explain how hunting can lead to extinction.
5. Describe how a seed bank conserves a plant species.
6. Describe what happens in a captive breeding programme.
7. (Extended) Explain how quotas help conserve forests.
8. (Extended) Explain how closed seasons help conserve fish stocks.
9. (Extended) Give the four reasons for conservation programmes.
10. (Extended) Describe artificial insemination in captive breeding.
11. (Extended) Describe in vitro fertilisation in captive breeding.
12. (Extended) Explain one risk to a species whose population is very small.

### Answers

1. One which is produced as rapidly as it is removed from the environment so that it does not run out.
2. Forests and fish stocks.
3. The introduced species may eat the native species, compete with it for food, or bring disease, so deaths exceed births.
4. Adults are killed faster than they can be replaced by reproduction, so numbers fall until none are left.
5. Seeds are dried and stored at low temperature so they stay alive; they can be grown to replace lost wild populations.
6. Animals are bred in zoos or centres where they are safe, and offspring are released into protected habitats.
7. They limit the number of trees felled, keeping removal below the rate of regrowth.
8. Fishing is banned during the breeding season, so adults can reproduce and replace the fish caught.
9. Maintain or increase biodiversity; reduce extinction; protect vulnerable ecosystems; maintain ecosystem functions (nutrient cycling and resource provision).
10. Sperm is collected from a male and inserted into a female; frozen sperm can be transported between zoos.
11. Eggs are collected and fertilised by sperm outside the body; the embryos are placed into a female to develop.
12. Reduced genetic variation means few individuals have alleles to survive a new disease or environmental change, so the species may become extinct.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "will not run out" without "produced as rapidly as it is removed".
- Naming a cause of endangerment with no effect on the population.
- Describing seed banks without saying the seeds are stored so they can be grown later.
- (Extended) Giving conservation methods for forests when fish are asked for, or the reverse.
- (Extended) Explaining small-population risk with no mention of alleles or variation.
