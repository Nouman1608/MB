## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Composite demand | A good demanded for two or more different uses |
| Joint supply | Producing one good automatically yields another |
| Competitive supply | Resources used for one good cannot be used for another |
| Ceteris paribus | All other things held constant |
| Disequilibrium | Quantity demanded and supplied differ at the current price |
| Consumer surplus | The difference between what buyers are willing to pay and what they pay |
| Producer surplus | The difference between the price received and the minimum price sellers would accept |
| Marginal utility | The extra satisfaction from consuming one more unit |
| Marginal external benefit | The benefit to third parties from one more unit |
| Moral hazard | Riskier behaviour after the cost of risk is passed to someone else |
| Quasi-public good | A good with some but not all public good characteristics |
| Government failure | Intervention that leads to a less efficient outcome than before |

## Core formulae

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| PED | % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in price |
| YED | % change in quantity demanded ÷ % change in income |
| XED | % change in quantity demanded of good A ÷ % change in price of good B |
| PES | % change in quantity supplied ÷ % change in price |
| Point PED (straight line) | (change in Q ÷ change in P) × P ÷ Q |
| Consumer surplus (straight line) | ½ × quantity × (price where demand meets the axis − market price) |
| Welfare loss (externality) | ½ × gap between market and optimum quantity × external cost or benefit per unit |
| Marginal value | Change in total ÷ change in units |

## Quick worked chain (invented)

Demand Qd = 600 − 2P; supply Qs = 3P − 150 (P in cu, Q in thousands).

- Equilibrium: 600 − 2P = 3P − 150, so **P = 150**, **Q = 300**.
- Point PED = −2 × 150 ÷ 300 = **−1.0**; PES = 3 × 150 ÷ 300 = **1.5**.
- Consumer surplus: demand meets the axis at P = 300, so ½ × 300 × 150 = **22,500 thousand cu**.
- A tax of 25 cu per unit shifts supply to Qs = 3(P − 25) − 150 = 3P − 225. New equilibrium: 600 − 2P = 3P − 225, so **P = 165**, **Q = 270**. Consumers pay 15 of the tax (60%), producers 10 (40%). Tax revenue = 25 × 270 = **6,750 thousand cu**.

## Related markets: direction of effects

| Change in market A | Link | Effect on market B |
|---|---|---|
| Price of A rises | Joint demand (complements) | Demand for B falls |
| Price of A rises | Competitive demand (substitutes) | Demand for B rises |
| Demand for A rises | Joint supply | Supply of B rises |
| Demand for A rises | Competitive supply | Supply of B falls |
| Demand for one use rises | Composite demand | Less available for other uses; price rises for all |

## Market failure checklist

- **Negative externality:** MSC > MPC, so the market over-produces. Tools: tax, regulation, permits.
- **Positive externality:** MSB > MPB, so the market under-consumes. Tools: subsidy, provision, information.
- **Information failure:** merit goods under-consumed, demerit goods over-consumed; asymmetric information and moral hazard distort choices.
- **Public goods:** free-riding means the market may provide none.

## Evaluating intervention

| Ask | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Can the external effect be measured? | A wrongly sized tax or subsidy creates a new welfare loss |
| What is the opportunity cost? | Public money could fund other services |
| How elastic are demand and supply? | A tax on an inelastic good raises revenue but changes behaviour little |
| Are there unintended consequences? | Price controls cause shortages or surpluses; high taxes encourage evasion |
| Is a cheaper tool available? | Information provision may fix an information failure at low cost |

## Exam traps

- Shifting supply when demand changed (that is an extension of supply).
- Calculating point PED away from the stated price and quantity.
- Using price, not the external value per unit, as the height of the welfare loss triangle.
- Confusing composite demand with joint demand.
- Calling any good provided by the state a public good.
- Assuming intervention always improves on the market.

## Self-test

1. Why does barter limit trade?
2. Define composite demand and give an example.
3. Distinguish an extension of demand from an increase in demand.
4. Demand is Qd = 400 − 4P and supply is Qs = 6P − 100. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.
5. Using question 4, calculate point PED at equilibrium.
6. A 10% rise in income raises quantity demanded by 3%. Calculate YED and classify the good.
7. Total utility from three units is 20, 35 and 45. State the marginal utility of the third unit.
8. State the condition for the socially optimal output.
9. The market output is 80 thousand and the optimum 60 thousand; the external cost is 5 cu per unit. Calculate the welfare loss.
10. Give two characteristics of a public good.
11. Define moral hazard.
12. Give one cause of government failure.

### Answers

1. Each trade needs a double coincidence of wants, which is costly to find.
2. A good demanded for several uses; for example, milk used for drinking, cheese and butter.
3. An extension is a movement along the demand curve caused by a fall in the good's own price; an increase is a rightward shift caused by another factor, such as higher income.
4. 400 − 4P = 6P − 100, so **P = 50**, **Q = 200**.
5. −4 × 50 ÷ 200 = **−1.0**.
6. 3 ÷ 10 = **+0.3**: a normal good, income inelastic (a necessity).
7. 45 − 35 = **10** units of utility.
8. Marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost.
9. ½ × 20 × 5 = **50 thousand cu**.
10. Any two of: non-excludable; non-rival (non-diminishable); non-rejectable; zero marginal cost.
11. Riskier behaviour by an agent once the cost of that risk falls on someone else.
12. Any one of: imperfect information; unintended consequences; administrative costs; political pressures.
