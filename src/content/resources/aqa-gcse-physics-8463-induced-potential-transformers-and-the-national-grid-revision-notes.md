## 4.7.3.1 The generator effect

A p.d. is induced across a conductor when:

- it **moves relative to a magnetic field** (cuts field lines), or
- the **magnetic field around it changes**.

Complete circuit → **induced current**.

| Bigger induced p.d. | Reversed induced p.d. |
|---|---|
| Faster movement / faster change of field | Movement reversed |
| Stronger magnetic field | Field reversed |
| More turns on the coil | |

- No change (magnet held still, or wire moving along the field lines) → **no** induced p.d.
- The induced current makes a field that **opposes the change** that caused it.

**Method: predicting the pole on a coil**

```
Step 1  identify the change (pole pushed in, or pulled out)
Step 2  pushed in → facing end of coil = SAME pole (repels)
        pulled out → facing end = OPPOSITE pole (attracts back)
Step 3  say "the induced field opposes the movement"
```

## 4.7.3.2 Alternators and dynamos

| | Alternator | Dynamo |
|---|---|---|
| Output | a.c. | d.c. |
| Connection | Slip rings and brushes | Split-ring commutator |
| p.d.–time graph | Wave above and below the axis | Humps all on one side of the axis |

Faster rotation → **higher peak p.d.** and **more cycles per second** (shorter time per cycle).
One full rotation = one cycle: rotations per second = 1 ÷ time for one cycle.

## 4.7.3.3 Microphones

```
sound waves → diaphragm vibrates → coil moves in permanent magnet's field
→ p.d. induced (generator effect) → current varies with the sound's pressure variations
```

Microphone = generator effect. Loudspeaker = motor effect. Same structure, opposite job.

## 4.7.3.4 Transformers

Structure: primary coil, secondary coil, **iron core** (easily magnetised).

```
a.c. in primary → changing field in core → field passes through secondary
→ alternating p.d. induced in secondary
```

d.c. → constant field → nothing induced.

### Equations (on the Physics equation sheet)

```
Vp / Vs = np / ns            step-up: ns > np, Vs > Vp
                             step-down: ns < np, Vs < Vp
Vs × Is = Vp × Ip            (100% efficient: power out = power in)
```

Mini-examples:

```
Vp = 230 V, np = 1000, ns = 50   →  Vs = 230 × 50 / 1000 = 11.5 V
Vs = 11.5 V, Is = 2.0 A          →  Ip = 11.5 × 2.0 / 230 = 0.10 A
```

Rule of thumb: p.d. stepped down by a factor → current stepped up by the same factor (100% efficient).

## Transmission and the National Grid

- National Grid: system of cables and transformers linking power stations to consumers.
- **Step-up** transformers: raise p.d. for the transmission cables.
- **Step-down** transformers: lower p.d. to a much lower, safer value for homes.

**Why high p.d.?**

```
same power, higher V → smaller I       (P = V I)
cable heating loss = I² R              → falls with the SQUARE of I
10 × V  → I ÷ 10  → loss ÷ 100
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Generator effect | Inducing a p.d. by relative movement of conductor and field, or a changing field |
| Induced current | Current driven by an induced p.d. in a complete circuit |
| Alternator | Generator producing a.c., using slip rings |
| Dynamo | Generator producing d.c., using a split-ring commutator |
| Primary coil | Transformer input coil |
| Secondary coil | Transformer output coil |
| Step-up transformer | Increases p.d.; more turns on the secondary |
| Step-down transformer | Decreases p.d.; fewer turns on the secondary |

## Exam traps

- "A strong magnet inside a coil induces a current": only if it **moves** or the field **changes**.
- Induced current said to assist the motion. It **opposes** it.
- Slip rings and commutator swapped.
- Faster rotation: forgetting the shorter time per cycle on the graph.
- Transformers on d.c.
- "High voltage reduces resistance": it reduces **current**.
- Putting Vs over Vp but np over ns in the same equation.

## Quick self-test

1. State two ways to induce a p.d. across a wire.
2. Give three factors that increase the size of an induced p.d.
3. A magnet is held still inside a coil. What is the induced p.d.?
4. The N pole of a magnet is pushed into a coil. Which pole forms at the near end of the coil?
5. What part makes an alternator produce a.c.?
6. Describe the p.d.–time graph of a dynamo.
7. A coil rotates faster. Give two changes to the alternator's graph.
8. Which effect does a microphone use?
9. Why is a transformer core made of iron?
10. Vp = 230 V, np = 4600, ns = 240. Calculate Vs.
11. A 100% efficient transformer gives 12 V at 3.0 A from 230 V mains. Calculate Ip.
12. The transmission p.d. is multiplied by 5 for the same power. By what factor does the cable heating loss change?

### Answers

1. Move it relative to a magnetic field; change the magnetic field around it.
2. Faster movement, stronger field, more turns.
3. Zero.
4. An N pole (it repels the incoming N pole).
5. Slip rings.
6. Humps all on the same side of the time axis.
7. Higher peak p.d.; shorter time per cycle (more cycles per second).
8. The generator effect.
9. Iron is easily magnetised.
10. Vs = 230 × 240 / 4600 = **12 V**.
11. Ip = 12 × 3.0 / 230 = **0.16 A** (2 s.f.).
12. Current ÷ 5, so loss ÷ 25 (one twenty-fifth).
