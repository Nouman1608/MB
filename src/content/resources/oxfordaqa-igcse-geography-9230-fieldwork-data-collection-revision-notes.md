These notes cover strand 2 of geographical enquiry, "Selecting, measuring and recording data appropriate to the chosen enquiry", in section 3.3.2 of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Geography (9230) specification, Version 4.3, with the numerical skill to "design fieldwork data collection sheets and collect data with an understanding of accuracy, sample size and procedures, control groups and reliability". For the whole unit, see the [Geographical and fieldwork skills study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-geographical-and-fieldwork-skills/); for full explanations, read the [fieldwork data collection study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-geography-9230-fieldwork-data-collection/).

## Specification checklist (strand 2)

- Difference between primary and secondary data.
- Identification and selection of appropriate physical and human data.
- Measuring and recording data using different sampling methods.
- Description and justification of data collection methods.
- Alternative methods must go beyond pre-processed data: selecting internet data, raw teacher data, interviews in school or at home, selecting from photographs and maps.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary data | Collected first-hand |
| Secondary data | Collected by someone else |
| Quantitative | Numerical |
| Qualitative | Descriptive: words, images |
| Sample | The part of a population or area actually measured |
| Point / line / area sampling | At points / along a transect / within quadrats |
| Random sampling | Equal chance for every point |
| Systematic sampling | Regular intervals |
| Stratified sampling | Groups sampled in proportion |
| Accuracy | Closeness to the true value |
| Reliability | Repeating gives similar results |
| Procedure | The exact steps followed |
| Control group | Comparison without the factor being tested |
| Cross-sectional area | Width × mean depth |
| Discharge | Cross-sectional area × velocity |

## Physical methods

| Variable | Equipment | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| Width | Tape | Bank to bank at water surface, at right angles to flow |
| Depth | Metre rule | Equal intervals across channel; mean |
| Velocity | Float, stopwatch, tape (or flow meter) | 10 m, three repeats, mean |
| Sediment size | Ruler or callipers | Longest axis of a sample of pebbles |
| Roundness | Roundness chart | Match to standard shapes |
| Beach profile | Ranging poles, clinometer, tape | Angle at each break of slope |
| Vegetation | Quadrat | Percentage cover or species count |
| Microclimate | Thermometer, anemometer | Same height at every site |

## Human methods

| Variable | Key point |
|---|---|
| Pedestrian count | Fixed time (e.g. five minutes), same time of day at each site |
| Traffic count | Fixed time; tally by vehicle type |
| Land use | Ground-floor use in set categories |
| Environmental quality | Score criteria on a scale, e.g. -2 to +2 |
| Questionnaire | Same questions; mostly closed, some open |
| Interview | Few people, depth of opinion |
| Photographs, field sketches | Qualitative record of each site |

## Sampling choices

| Method | Example | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Random | Random numbers pick quadrat coordinates | Avoids bias | May miss areas |
| Systematic | Every 500 m along a river | Even coverage, repeatable | May miss features between sites |
| Stratified | 7 quadrats on grass, 3 in woodland for a 70:30 park | Every group represented | Needs prior information |

## Recording sheet must-haves

Title, date, time, weather; site and location; one column per variable **with units**; space for repeats and means; notes column.

## Improving data quality

- **Accuracy:** precise equipment; careful reading; taut tapes.
- **Reliability:** repeats; means; more than one day.
- **Procedures:** same method, equipment, time and person at every site.
- **Sample size:** enough readings to dilute unusual ones.
- **Control:** compare with a site lacking the factor tested.

Consistent but wrong readings are **reliable but not accurate**.

## Describe versus justify

- **Describe:** what, with what equipment, where, how many, how often, so it could be repeated.
- **Justify:** why it suited the hypothesis, and how it improved reliability, accuracy or fairness.

## Condensed example

**Describe and justify a sampling method for a pedestrian count:** "We counted pedestrians for five minutes at sites every 100 m along the main road from the town centre, all between 11 am and noon on one weekday. Systematic sampling gave even coverage of the route, so the change with distance could be seen clearly, and counting at the same time at every site made comparisons fair."

## Exam traps

- Methods with no equipment, units or location.
- Methods that do not test the hypothesis.
- Sampling named but not explained.
- Accuracy and reliability confused.
- Recording sheets without units or repeat columns.
- "It was easy" as the only justification.
- Pre-processed data passed off as primary data.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish primary from secondary data.
2. How should river depth be measured at a site?
3. How can velocity be measured without a flow meter?
4. How is discharge calculated?
5. Describe how to carry out a pedestrian count fairly.
6. What is an environmental quality survey?
7. Distinguish point, line and area sampling.
8. A field is 60% grass and 40% bare soil. How would you place ten quadrats using stratified sampling?
9. Give one strength and one limitation of systematic sampling.
10. List four features of a good recording sheet.
11. Distinguish accuracy from reliability.
12. What is a control group in a microclimate enquiry?

**Answers**

1. Primary is collected first-hand; secondary is collected by someone else.
2. With a metre rule at equal intervals across the channel, then calculate the mean.
3. Time a float over a measured distance, such as 10 m, three times, and calculate the mean.
4. Cross-sectional area (width × mean depth) multiplied by velocity.
5. Tally people passing a fixed point for a fixed time, at the same time of day at each site.
6. A survey scoring criteria such as litter, noise and greenery on a scale at each site.
7. Point: at specific points. Line: along a transect. Area: within areas such as quadrats.
8. Six quadrats on grass and four on bare soil.
9. Strength: even coverage and easy to repeat. Limitation: may miss features between sites.
10. Any four: title; date, time and weather; site and location; variable columns with units; space for repeats and means; notes column.
11. Accuracy is closeness to the true value; reliability is getting similar results when the method is repeated.
12. A comparison site without the factor being tested, such as grass when testing whether tarmac is warmer.
