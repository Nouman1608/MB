## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State how many covalent bonds each carbon atom forms in graphite. **[1]**
**(b)** State the shape of the rings in the layers of graphite. **[1]**

**2.** Graphite is used in pencils.

**(a)** State the property of graphite that makes it suitable for this use. **[1]**
**(b)** State whether there are covalent bonds between the layers of graphite. **[1]**

**3.** State whether graphite conducts electricity. **[1]**

**4.** A section of a graphite layer contains 60 hexagons. Each carbon atom is shared by three hexagons and
each hexagon has six carbon atoms at its corners. Calculate the number of carbon atoms in the section. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** Explain why graphite conducts electricity. **[3]**

**6.** Explain why graphite is soft and can be used as a lubricant. **[3]**

**7.** Graphite is a non-metal that conducts electricity.

**(a)** Name the particles that carry the charge in graphite. **[1]**
**(b)** State **one** similarity and **one** difference between how graphite and a metal conduct electricity.
**[2]**

**8.** A student writes: "Graphite has a low melting point because there are no bonds between the layers."

**(a)** Explain why the student is wrong. **[3]**
**(b)** Write a correct statement about the melting point of graphite. **[1]**

**9.** The table compares diamond and graphite.

| Property | Diamond | Graphite |
|---|---|---|
| Covalent bonds per carbon atom | 4 | 3 |
| Conducts electricity | No | Yes |
| Hardness | Very hard | Soft |

**(a)** Explain why diamond does not conduct but graphite does. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why diamond is very hard but graphite is soft. **[3]**

**10.** Describe the structure and bonding of graphite and explain how they give rise to its electrical
conductivity, its softness and its high melting point. **[6]**

**11.** Graphite powder is sometimes used to lubricate door locks.

**(a)** Give **one** property of graphite that makes it suitable for this use and explain it. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** disadvantage of using graphite in electrical equipment. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Three** [1]
**(b)** **Hexagonal** [1]

**2. (a)** It is **soft** (layers rub off onto paper) [1]
**(b)** **No** [1]

**3.** **Yes** [1]

**4.** Atoms = 60 × 6 ÷ 3 [1] = **120** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Each carbon atom uses three of its four outer electrons in bonding [1]; the fourth electron is
delocalised and free to move [1]; the delocalised electrons carry charge through the graphite [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Graphite has layers of carbon atoms with **no covalent bonds between the layers** [1]; the layers can
**slide over each other** easily [1]; this makes it soft and slippery, so it works as a lubricant [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** **Delocalised electrons** [1]
**(b)** Similarity: in both, the delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge [1]. Difference: in graphite
there is one delocalised electron per carbon atom and it moves along the layers, whereas in a metal the
electrons move through the whole structure of positive ions [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** The student is wrong in three ways: melting graphite needs the **covalent bonds within the layers** to be
broken [1]; there are many of these and they are strong [1]; so a very large amount of energy is needed and the
melting point is high, not low [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Graphite has a **very high melting point** [1]

**9. (a)** In diamond all four outer electrons of each carbon are used in covalent bonds, so there are no
delocalised electrons or ions [1] and it cannot conduct [1]; in graphite one electron per atom is delocalised and
free to move and carry charge [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Diamond has a rigid 3D network of strong covalent bonds [1] so atoms cannot slide [1]; graphite has layers
with no covalent bonds between them, so the layers can slide [1]. **[3]**

**10.** Indicative content, one mark per point, up to 6:
- Each carbon atom forms three covalent bonds with three other carbon atoms [1].
- The atoms form layers of hexagonal rings [1].
- One electron from each carbon atom is delocalised, so graphite conducts electricity [1].
- There are no covalent bonds between the layers, so they slide over each other [1] and graphite is soft [1].
- The covalent bonds within the layers are strong and must be broken to melt it, so it has a high melting
  point [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** The layers can **slide over each other** easily [1] because there are **no covalent bonds between the
layers** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** It **conducts electricity**, so it could cause a short circuit [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "weak covalent bonds between layers" rather than "no covalent bonds".
- Linking softness to weak covalent bonds inside the layers.
- Forgetting the delocalised electron when explaining conduction.
- Mixing up diamond (four bonds) and graphite (three bonds).
- Saying graphite has a low melting point because the layers can slide.
