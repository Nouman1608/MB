These notes condense section **3.1.5.5 Catalysts** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2 (and Paper 3). The [Catalysts study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts/) explains each idea with worked examples, and the [Catalysts practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-catalysts-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- A catalyst **increases the rate** of a reaction **without being changed in chemical composition or amount**.
- Catalysts work by providing an **alternative route of lower activation energy**.
- Use a **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** to explain how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction involving a gas.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Catalyst | A substance that increases the rate without being changed in chemical composition or amount |
| Activation energy | Minimum energy for a successful collision |
| Alternative route | A different pathway with a different mechanism |
| Homogeneous catalyst | In the same phase as the reactants |
| Heterogeneous catalyst | In a different phase from the reactants |
| Adsorption | Reactants held on a catalyst surface |

## How a catalyst works

1. It provides an **alternative route** for the reaction.
2. The alternative route has a **lower activation energy**.
3. A greater proportion of collisions have energy at least equal to the lower Eₐ.
4. More collisions are successful per second, so the rate increases.

## Reaction profile

| Feature | With a catalyst |
|---|---|
| Reactants level | Same |
| Products level | Same |
| ΔH | Unchanged |
| Peak height | Lower |
| Eₐ | Lower |
| Peaks | One lower peak, or two or more small peaks |

## Maxwell–Boltzmann diagram

- Draw **one** curve. The temperature has not changed.
- Mark the original Eₐ.
- Mark the lower Eₐ(catalysed) to the **left** of it.
- Shade the area to the right of each line. The area beyond the lower Eₐ is larger.
- The curve itself does not move.

## What a catalyst does not do

| Not changed | Reason |
|---|---|
| ΔH | Same reactants and products |
| Position of equilibrium | Speeds up forward and reverse reactions equally |
| Yield at equilibrium | Same composition is reached, only faster |
| Energy of the particles | Same temperature, same distribution |
| Eₐ of the uncatalysed route | The alternative route is used in addition |
| Amount of catalyst | It is regenerated |

## Industrial benefits

- Lower operating temperature, so less energy and lower fuel costs.
- Lower carbon dioxide emissions, because less fuel is burned for heating.
- Faster reaction, so more product per hour.
- Possibly higher selectivity and less waste.
- Reusable, though it can be poisoned by impurities.

| Process | Catalyst |
|---|---|
| Haber process | Iron |
| Contact process | Vanadium(V) oxide |
| Hydrogenation of alkenes | Nickel |
| Catalytic converter | Platinum and rhodium |
| Decomposition of H₂O₂ | Manganese(IV) oxide |

## Worked reminders

**Define a catalyst.** "A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction without being changed in chemical composition or amount."

**Explain how a catalyst speeds up a reaction between gases.** It provides an alternative route with lower Eₐ. The Maxwell–Boltzmann curve is the same, but the lower Eₐ lies where the area under the curve is bigger. A greater proportion of molecules has energy at least equal to Eₐ, so more collisions are successful.

**Effect on equilibrium.** None. Both rates increase equally. Equilibrium is reached sooner, but the composition is the same.

**Does the catalyst change ΔH?** No. The profile starts and finishes at the same levels.

**Why is a catalyst "not used up"?** It may react in one step of the mechanism but is reformed in a later step.

## Common mistakes

- Omitting "alternative route".
- Saying that particles gain energy.
- Different start and end levels on a profile.
- Shifting the whole distribution curve.
- Saying the yield increases.
- Saying that the catalyst is consumed.
- Drawing the catalysed Eₐ to the right.
- Saying that a catalyst changes the temperature.

## Self-test

1. Define a catalyst.
2. Explain how a catalyst increases the rate of reaction.
3. Sketch a reaction profile for a catalysed and uncatalysed exothermic reaction.
4. State the effect of a catalyst on ΔH.
5. State the effect of a catalyst on the position of an equilibrium, and explain.
6. Explain, with a Maxwell–Boltzmann diagram, why a catalyst increases the rate of a gas-phase reaction.
7. Name the catalyst used in the Haber process.
8. Give two benefits of using a catalyst in industry.
9. Explain why a catalyst lets a process be run at a lower temperature.
10. A student draws a distribution curve for the catalysed reaction that is shifted to the right of the uncatalysed one. What is wrong?
11. Why is a catalyst said to be "unchanged in amount"?
12. Name the catalyst in the catalytic converter of a car.

### Answers

1. A substance that increases the rate of a reaction without being changed in chemical composition or amount.
2. It provides an alternative route with a lower activation energy, so a greater proportion of collisions are successful.
3. Same reactant and product levels; the catalysed curve has a lower peak.
4. No effect.
5. No effect on the position: it increases the rates of the forward and reverse reactions equally, so equilibrium is reached more quickly.
6. One curve; Eₐ(catalysed) lies to the left of Eₐ(uncatalysed); the area to the right of the lower Eₐ is greater, so more molecules have enough energy and more collisions succeed.
7. Iron.
8. Any two of: lower temperature (less energy used), lower emissions, faster production, less waste, reusable.
9. The lower activation energy means a high enough rate can be reached at a lower temperature, saving energy.
10. The temperature, and hence the distribution, has not changed, so the curve should be the same. Only the activation energy changes.
11. It is regenerated at the end of the reaction, so the amount at the end is the same as at the start.
12. Platinum and rhodium.
