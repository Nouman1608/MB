These notes condense sub-topic **11.2 Antibodies and vaccination** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, assumed on Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [antibodies and vaccination study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-antibodies-and-vaccination/); for exam-style questions, use the [antibodies and vaccination practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-antibodies-and-vaccination-practice/).

## Antibody: structure → function

- **Glycoprotein**, quaternary structure, Y-shaped.
- **2 heavy + 2 light chains**, joined by **disulfide bonds** → stable.
- **Variable regions** (arm tips): differ between antibodies → **antigen-binding sites** complementary to one antigen.
- **Two binding sites** → link pathogens together (**agglutination**).
- **Hinge** → flexible arms reach antigens at different spacings.
- **Constant region** → binds receptors on phagocytes → easier engulfing.

**Actions**: agglutination; neutralising toxins; blocking viral attachment sites; marking pathogens for phagocytes.

## Hybridoma method in five steps

| Step | Why |
|---|---|
| 1. Inject mouse with antigen | Stimulates B cells making the wanted antibody |
| 2. Take B-lymphocytes from spleen | Spleen holds many activated B cells |
| 3. Fuse with myeloma cells → hybridoma | Plasma cells cannot divide; myeloma cells divide indefinitely |
| 4. Screen and select | Only some hybridomas make the right antibody |
| 5. Clone and culture; collect antibody | Large amounts of identical antibody |

## Using monoclonal antibodies

**Principle**: binds **only one specific antigen**.

**Diagnosis** (antibody + label):
- Pregnancy strip: mobile labelled anti-hCG antibodies bind hCG; fixed antibodies at the test line catch the complex → coloured line; control line catches mobile antibodies → shows the test worked.
- Detecting pathogens in samples; locating tumours or blood clots with labelled antibodies.

**Treatment**:
- Block a receptor or neutralise a molecule (e.g. trastuzumab binds HER2 on some breast cancer cells).
- Mark target cells for the immune system.
- Deliver an attached drug or toxin only to target cells.
- Humanised to reduce an immune response against the antibody.

## Four types of immunity

| | Natural | Artificial |
|---|---|---|
| Active | Infection | Vaccination |
| Passive | Antibodies across placenta or in breast milk | Injected antibodies |

| | Active | Passive |
|---|---|---|
| Who makes antibodies | Own plasma cells | Supplied from outside |
| Memory cells | Yes | No |
| Onset | Slow | Immediate |
| Lasts | Long-term | Weeks to months |

## Vaccines

- Contain **antigens**: attenuated live pathogen, killed pathogen, toxoid, or isolated antigens.
- Cause a **primary response** without the disease → **memory cells**.
- Real infection later → fast **secondary response** → no symptoms.
- **Booster** → more memory cells, longer protection.

### Worked reminder: effect of a booster

Antibody after first dose peaks at 20 units; after booster peaks at 160 units.

```
160 ÷ 20 = 8 times higher after the booster
```

## Vaccination programmes

- **Herd immunity**: enough immune people → transmission chains break → unvaccinated people protected.
- **Ring vaccination**: vaccinate contacts around each case.
- **Smallpox** (eradication declared 1980): stable antigens, humans only, easy to recognise.
- **Difficulties**: antigenic variation (influenza); many strains; intracellular pathogens (*Plasmodium*, HIV); poor response in malnourished or HIV-positive people; cold storage; remote areas; low uptake.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Antibody | Glycoprotein secreted by plasma cells that binds a specific antigen |
| Variable region | Part of an antibody forming the antigen-binding site |
| Constant region | Part of an antibody that is the same within a class; binds phagocytes |
| Agglutination | Clumping of pathogens by antibodies |
| Monoclonal antibody | Identical antibodies from one clone of cells |
| Hybridoma | Cell formed by fusing a B-lymphocyte with a myeloma cell |
| Active immunity | Immunity from one's own immune response; memory cells form |
| Passive immunity | Immunity from antibodies supplied from outside; no memory cells |
| Vaccine | Preparation containing antigens that stimulates an immune response |
| Herd immunity | Protection of a population when most individuals are immune |
| Antigenic variation | Change in a pathogen's surface antigens |

## Exam traps

- Vaccines contain **antigens**, not antibodies.
- Placental or milk antibodies are **natural passive**, not artificial.
- Passive immunity forms **no** memory cells.
- Myeloma cells are used because they **divide indefinitely**.
- Monoclonal antibody answers must say **specific** or **complementary**.
- Herd immunity is about breaking transmission, not vaccinating everyone.

## Self-test

1. How many polypeptide chains does an antibody have?
2. Which bonds hold the chains together?
3. Which region of an antibody binds the antigen?
4. Explain how two binding sites help destroy bacteria.
5. Why are B-lymphocytes fused with myeloma cells?
6. Why must hybridomas be screened?
7. State the principle behind all uses of monoclonal antibodies.
8. In a pregnancy test, what does the control line show?
9. Classify immunity from antibodies in breast milk.
10. Why does passive immunity not last?
11. Name two forms in which antigens may be present in a vaccine.
12. Explain why influenza vaccines must be changed regularly.

### Answers

1. Four (two heavy, two light).
2. Disulfide bonds.
3. The variable region.
4. They bind two bacteria at once, causing agglutination, so bacteria are engulfed by phagocytes in groups.
5. B cells make the antibody but cannot divide; myeloma cells divide indefinitely; hybridomas do both.
6. Only some fused cells produce the antibody that binds the target antigen.
7. The antibody binds only to one specific antigen.
8. That the urine has moved along the strip and the test has worked.
9. Natural passive.
10. No memory cells are made, and the antibodies are broken down.
11. Any two: attenuated live pathogen; killed pathogen; toxoid; isolated antigen molecules.
12. The virus changes its surface antigens, so memory cells from earlier vaccines no longer match.
