## Questions

**1.** **(a)** State the name of the single long fibre that projects from a neuron cell body. **[1]**

**(b)** State the name of the several shorter fibres. **[1]**

**(c)** State where the nucleus of a neuron is found. **[1]**

**2.** Explain how the sodium-potassium pump helps to produce a negative resting potential. **[4]**

**3.** An impulse travels 0.72 m along a myelinated fibre in 9.0 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the impulse in m s⁻¹. **[2]**

**(b)** A non-myelinated fibre conducts at 1.2 m s⁻¹. Calculate the time in ms for an impulse to travel the same distance. **[2]**

**4.** A student measured the reflex delay in fibres of different diameter (fictional data).

| Fibre diameter (µm) | 4 | 6 | 8 | 10 | 12 | 14 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reflex delay (ms) | 19 | 16 | 13 | 12 | 9 | 8 |

The correlation coefficient r for these data is -0.988.

**(a)** Describe the correlation. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate R², to 2 significant figures, and state what it shows. **[3]**

**(c)** State why the data do not prove that diameter causes the change in delay. **[1]**

**5.** Explain how the arrival of an action potential at a presynaptic membrane causes neurotransmitter to enter the synaptic cleft. **[4]**

**6.** Acetylcholine is released at a neuromuscular junction.

**(a)** Explain how acetylcholine produces an excitatory postsynaptic potential. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why a signal passes in one direction only across a synapse. **[2]**

**7.** **(HL only)** An oscilloscope trace from an axon shows a resting potential of -70 mV. There are four identical peaks, each reaching +30 mV, at 3, 11, 19 and 27 ms.

**(a)** Calculate the amplitude of one action potential. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of impulses per second. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the events at the membrane during the rising phase of a peak. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain the events during the falling phase. **[2]**

**8.** **(HL only)** **(a)** Explain how an action potential at one point on an axon causes an action potential in the next section. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why impulses travel faster in myelinated fibres. **[2]**

**9.** **(HL only)** **(a)** Explain how a neonicotinoid pesticide affects synaptic transmission in an insect. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how cocaine increases stimulation of postsynaptic neurons. **[3]**

**10.** **(HL only)** A neuron has a resting potential of -70 mV and a threshold of -55 mV. Neurons A, B and C each give an excitatory postsynaptic potential of +5, +7 and +4 mV. Neuron D gives an inhibitory postsynaptic potential of -6 mV.

**(a)** Determine the membrane potential when A, B and C fire together and state whether an action potential results. **[2]**

**(b)** Determine the potential when A, B, C and D all fire and state the outcome. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain what happens at the postsynaptic membrane to produce an inhibitory postsynaptic potential. **[2]**

**11.** **(HL only)** **(a)** Outline how capsaicin from chilli peppers causes the sensation of pain. **[3]**

**(b)** State what is meant by an emergent property, using consciousness as the example. **[1]**

**12.** **(HL only)** Axons from the same animal were treated with two toxins (fictional data).

| Treatment | Resting potential (mV) | Highest potential reached (mV) | Time to repolarize (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Control | -70 | +32 | 1.5 |
| Toxin A | -70 | -58 | not applicable |
| Toxin B | -70 | +32 | 4.0 |

Toxin A blocks voltage-gated sodium channels. Toxin B blocks voltage-gated potassium channels.

**(a)** Compare the control with toxin A. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the result for toxin A. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in repolarization time caused by toxin B. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain the result for toxin B. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The axon [1]
**(b)** Dendrites [1]
**(c)** In the cell body (with the cytoplasm) [1]
**[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A state question needs only the name; adding extra description gains nothing, so give the term and move on.

**2.** The pump uses energy from ATP [1]; it moves 3 sodium ions out and 2 potassium ions in each cycle [1]; so more positive charge leaves than enters [1]; this establishes concentration gradients (high sodium outside, high potassium inside) that, with potassium leaking out through open channels, make the inside negative [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers usually earn credit when the 3 to 2 ratio and the direction of each ion are both stated; saying only that the pump "creates a gradient" is too vague.

**3. (a)** Speed = distance ÷ time = 0.72 ÷ 0.0090 [1]; **80 m s⁻¹** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Time = 0.72 ÷ 1.2 = 0.60 s [1]; **600 ms** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A frequent loss is leaving the time in ms during the first division; convert to seconds before dividing and give units on each answer.

**4. (a)** Strong negative correlation [1] **[1]**
**(b)** R² = (-0.988)² [1]; = **0.98** (to 2 significant figures) [1]; about 98% of the variation in reflex delay is explained by the variation in diameter [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Correlation shows an association only; other variables (for example temperature or myelination) could also affect delay [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Stating R² as a number without saying what it explains about the dependent variable usually leaves the final point unearned.

**5.** The action potential depolarizes the presynaptic membrane [1]; voltage-gated calcium channels open and Ca²⁺ diffuses in [1]; calcium causes vesicles containing neurotransmitter to fuse with the membrane [1]; the neurotransmitter is released by exocytosis into the cleft [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that skip calcium and jump from depolarization to release tend to lose the linking steps that an explain question rewards.

**6. (a)** Acetylcholine diffuses across the synaptic cleft [1]; it binds to transmembrane receptors on the postsynaptic membrane, opening channels [1]; positive ions (mainly sodium) enter and depolarize the membrane slightly, giving an EPSP [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Neurotransmitter is released only from the presynaptic membrane [1]; receptors are located only on the postsynaptic membrane [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Receptors are on one side only" earns the second point only if the answer also says release happens from the other side.

**7. (a)** 30 - (-70) = **100 mV** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** Interval = 8 ms = 0.008 s [1]; 1 ÷ 0.008 = **125 impulses per second** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Voltage-gated sodium channels open once threshold is reached [1]; sodium ions diffuse in, making the inside positive [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Sodium channels close [1]; voltage-gated potassium channels open and potassium diffuses out, restoring the negative inside [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the ion, the channel type and the direction of movement for each phase is the pattern that attracts credit on trace-interpretation questions.

**8. (a)** Sodium ions entering at one point spread sideways, forming local currents inside and outside the axon [1]; this depolarizes the next section of membrane [1]; when it reaches threshold, its voltage-gated sodium channels open and a new action potential starts [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Myelin insulates the axon, so ion movement happens only at the nodes of Ranvier, where pumps and channels are clustered [1]; the action potential jumps from node to node, which is faster than depolarizing the whole length [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say only "myelin speeds up the impulse" restate the question; the node-to-node jump is the idea that explains the speed.

**9. (a)** The pesticide binds to acetylcholine receptors on the postsynaptic membrane [1]; this blocks normal synaptic transmission, disrupting the insect's nervous system [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Cocaine blocks reuptake of dopamine into the presynaptic neuron [1]; dopamine stays in the synaptic cleft for longer [1]; so receptors on the postsynaptic membrane are stimulated for longer [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the molecule that is blocked (the receptor or the reuptake transporter) separates the two chemicals; answers that treat them as the same lose the contrast.

**10. (a)** -70 + 5 + 7 + 4 = -54 mV [1]; this is above the -55 mV threshold, so an **action potential results** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** -54 - 6 = **-60 mV** [1]; below threshold, so **no action potential** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The inhibitory neurotransmitter opens channels that make the inside more negative [1]; the postsynaptic membrane is hyperpolarized [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the running total and the comparison with threshold explicitly; an examiner can then follow your reasoning even if one addition slips.

**11. (a)** Free nerve endings in the skin have cation channels that open in response to capsaicin [1]; positive ions enter and threshold is reached [1]; impulses pass to the brain, where pain is perceived [1] **[3]**
**(b)** A property that arises from the interaction of many parts (here, neurons) and is not found in any single part [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Pain is perceived in the brain, not at the skin; answers that place perception at the nerve ending usually lose this point.

**12. (a)** Resting potential is the same (-70 mV) [1]; toxin A reaches only -58 mV, but the control reaches +32 mV [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Toxin A blocks voltage-gated sodium channels, so sodium cannot enter [1]; there is no regenerative depolarization, so the potential stays below the -55 mV threshold [1]; no action potential is propagated [1] **[3]**
**(c)** (4.0 - 1.5) ÷ 1.5 × 100 [1]; **167%** [1] **[2]**
**(d)** Toxin B blocks voltage-gated potassium channels, so less potassium leaves [1]; repolarization is slower [1]; the peak is unchanged because sodium channels still open normally [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the numbers from the table in a comparison, and link each toxin to the channel it blocks; answers that quote only one column usually leave marks unearned.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the ratio of pump movements the wrong way round.
- Omitting calcium from release of neurotransmitter.
- Writing that sodium "enters the axon" without naming the voltage-gated channel.
- Confusing depolarization (sodium in) with repolarization (potassium out).
- Forgetting to convert ms to s in speed calculations.
- Quoting R² with no meaning for the variables.
- Treating correlation as proof of cause.
- Leaving out the comparison with threshold in summation questions.
- Treating hyperpolarization as depolarization.
