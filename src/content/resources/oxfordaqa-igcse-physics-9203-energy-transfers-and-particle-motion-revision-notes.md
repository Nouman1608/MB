The qualification is untiered. For the full particle explanations read the [energy transfers study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-and-particle-motion/), then test yourself with the [energy transfers practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-energy-transfers-and-particle-motion-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Conduction (a).**

- Hot particles vibrate more and pass vibrations to neighbouring particles.
- Metals also have **free electrons** that gain kinetic energy, move through the metal and collide with particles elsewhere. This makes metals good conductors.
- Insulators have no free electrons. Gases are very poor conductors because their particles are far apart. Trapped air makes good insulation.

**Convection (a).** Only in fluids. Heated fluid: particles move faster and further apart → fluid expands → less dense → rises. Cooler, denser fluid sinks → convection current.

**Evaporation (b).** The most energetic particles at the surface escape. The average kinetic energy of those left falls, so the liquid cools.

**Rate of evaporation increases with:** higher temperature, larger surface area, air flow across the surface, drier air.

**Condensation (b).** Gas particles cool on a cold surface, slow down and form a liquid, releasing energy to the surface.

**Rate of energy transfer by heating (c).** Depends on:

- surface area and volume (larger surface area to volume ratio → faster);
- the material of the object;
- the nature of the surface it is in contact with.

**Temperature difference (d).** Bigger difference → faster rate of energy transfer.

**Expansion (e).** Heated particles move more and spread out, so most substances expand. Particles do not grow.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conduction | Energy transfer through a material by particle vibrations (and free electrons in metals) |
| Free electrons | Electrons in a metal that can move through the structure |
| Conductor | Material that transfers energy quickly by conduction |
| Insulator | Material that transfers energy slowly by conduction |
| Convection | Energy transfer by the movement of a fluid, as less dense fluid rises |
| Convection current | The circulation of fluid set up by convection |
| Evaporation | Particles escaping from the surface of a liquid below its boiling point |
| Condensation | Gas changing to liquid as its particles lose energy |
| Surface area to volume ratio | Surface area divided by volume; higher means faster energy transfer relative to energy stored |
| Bi-metallic strip | Two bonded metals that expand differently, so the strip bends when heated |

## Applications to recognise

| Example | Physics |
|---|---|
| Cooling fins on an engine or processor | Increase surface area for little extra volume |
| Large ears of desert animals | Large surface area for losing energy |
| Small ears and rounded bodies in cold climates | Reduce surface area through which energy is lost |
| Heater near the floor | Warm air rises, setting up a convection current |
| Sweating | Evaporation of sweat cools the skin |
| Hot pan on a wooden board | Wood conducts poorly, so the pan cools more slowly than on metal |
| Expansion gaps in bridges and rails | Prevent buckling when they expand in heat |
| Thermostat | Bi-metallic strip bends and breaks the circuit at a set temperature |

## Surface area to volume ratio reminder

- Cube of side s: area 6s², volume s³, ratio 6 ÷ s.
- Halving the side doubles the ratio.
- Flattening a shape of fixed volume increases its surface area and ratio.

## Exam traps

- "Heat rises" scores nothing; say less dense warm fluid rises.
- Convection does not happen in solids.
- Evaporation can happen at any temperature, not only at the boiling point.
- The cooling effect needs "the most energetic particles escape", not just "particles escape".
- Particles do not expand; the spaces between them increase.
- In a bi-metallic strip, the metal that expands more is on the **outside** of the curve.

## Quick self-test

1. Why are metals better conductors than plastics?
2. Why is a layer of trapped air a good insulator?
3. Explain why warm air rises.
4. Can convection happen in a solid? Why?
5. Explain why evaporation cools a liquid.
6. Give three factors that increase the rate of evaporation.
7. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a 2 cm cube.
8. Why do cooling fins work?
9. Why do desert animals tend to have large ears?
10. Why does a hot pan cool faster on a metal worktop than on a wooden board?
11. Why do bridges have expansion gaps?
12. In a bi-metallic strip of brass and iron, brass expands more. Which metal is on the outside of the bend when heated?

### Answers

1. Metals have free electrons that carry energy quickly through the metal; plastics do not.
2. Air is a poor conductor and, when trapped, cannot move to transfer energy by convection.
3. Its particles move faster and further apart, so it expands and becomes less dense than the cooler air around it.
4. No. The particles in a solid cannot move from place to place, so no current can form.
5. The most energetic particles escape, so the average kinetic energy of the remaining particles, and so the temperature, falls.
6. Any three: higher temperature, larger surface area, air moving over the surface, drier air.
7. Area 24 cm², volume 8 cm³, so ratio **3 per cm**.
8. They give a large surface area for a small extra volume, so energy is transferred to the surroundings faster.
9. Large ears give a large surface area, so they lose energy to the surroundings faster and help keep the animal cool.
10. Metal is a better conductor, so it conducts energy away from the pan faster.
11. So that the bridge can expand on hot days without buckling or cracking.
12. **Brass**.
