## Condensed recall

### Supply

- **Capture fisheries**: wild harvest. Production has levelled off since the 1990s.
- **Aquaculture**: farming fish, crustaceans, molluscs, aquatic plants. Source of nearly all growth in supply since then.
- Productive zones: continental shelves, upwelling zones, estuaries, reefs, kelp. Unproductive: stratified open-ocean gyres.
- Long open-ocean food chains mean little producer energy reaches large fish.

### Surplus yield and MSY

```
surplus yield = r × N × (1 − N ÷ K)
```

- Maximum surplus (MSY) at N = K ÷ 2: MSY = r × K ÷ 4.
- Short reminder: K = 60 000 t, r = 0.5 → MSY = 0.5 × 60 000 ÷ 4 = **7 500 t per year**, at N = 30 000 t.
- The same surplus occurs at a small and a large stock; the large stock is safer.
- Catch above the surplus → stock shrinks → surplus falls further (unless N is above K ÷ 2).
- Limits: K and r uncertain and changing; natural fluctuations; single-species view; precautionary catches below MSY.

### Food-web effects

- **Fishing down the food web**: average trophic level of catches falls as large predators are depleted (Pauly and colleagues, late 1990s).
- **Trophic cascade**: removing predators lets prey increase, affecting lower levels.
- **Shifting baseline syndrome**: each generation judges against what it first saw, so long-term decline is missed.
- **Size selection**: removing large spawners can favour early maturity at smaller size.

### Grand Banks cod (Newfoundland)

- Factory trawlers and sonar after the Second World War; catches peaked in the late 1960s.
- 200-nautical-mile limit (1977), then domestic fleet expansion and optimistic quotas.
- Technology kept catch rates up while the stock fell.
- 1992 moratorium; tens of thousands of jobs lost; slow recovery.

### Management beyond basic rules

| Approach | How it works | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| ITQs (catch shares) | Total catch divided into tradable shares | Ends the race to fish; owners gain from a healthy stock | Shares concentrate in large firms |
| Ecosystem-based management | Catches set with predators, prey and habitat in mind | Protects food-web roles | Needs much more data |
| MSC certification | Label for fisheries meeting standards | Market reward for good practice | Depends on consumer and retailer choice |

### Aquaculture options

- **Lower trophic levels**: seaweed, mussels, oysters need no feed; filter feeders remove nutrients.
- **IMTA**: fish with seaweed and shellfish that use fish waste.
- **Recirculating systems**: land-based tanks, filtered water reused; no escapes; high energy use.
- **Feed substitution**: plant, algal or insect protein instead of fishmeal.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capture fishery | Harvest of wild aquatic organisms |
| Aquaculture | Farming of aquatic organisms |
| Surplus yield | Annual growth of a stock that can be removed without reducing it |
| Maximum sustainable yield | The largest surplus yield, at about half the carrying capacity |
| Precautionary approach | Setting catches below MSY to allow for uncertainty |
| Fishing down the food web | Shift of catches to lower trophic levels as predators decline |
| Shifting baseline syndrome | Judging change against a recent, already degraded state |
| Moratorium | A temporary ban, here on commercial fishing |
| ITQ | Individual transferable quota, a tradable share of the total catch |
| IMTA | Integrated multi-trophic aquaculture |
| Forage fish | Small fish, such as anchovies, caught for fishmeal and fish oil |

## Exam traps

- MSY is sustainable only at the right stock size; it is not simply the biggest possible catch.
- Saying a steady catch proves a healthy stock. Check effort and catch per unit effort.
- Assuming aquaculture always spares wild fish: carnivorous species eat fishmeal from forage fish.
- In the surplus-yield expression, using N ÷ K as a percentage instead of a fraction.
- Writing about the cod collapse without dates, technology or the role of uncertain estimates.
- Evaluating ITQs without their social effects on small-scale fishers.

## Self-test

1. Name two highly productive marine zones and explain why one of them is productive.
2. Why is the open ocean far less productive per unit area than continental shelves?
3. Write the surplus-yield expression and define each symbol.
4. Calculate the surplus yield of a stock with K = 200 000 t and r = 0.3 when N = 150 000 t.
5. At what stock size is MSY obtained, and what is MSY for the stock in question 4?
6. Give two reasons why managers often set catches below MSY.
7. What does "fishing down the food web" mean?
8. Define shifting baseline syndrome.
9. In which year was the northern cod moratorium declared?
10. Explain one way ITQs encourage conservation.
11. How does integrated multi-trophic aquaculture reduce pollution?
12. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of recirculating aquaculture systems.

### Answers

1. Any two of continental shelves, upwelling zones, estuaries, reefs, kelp forests; e.g. upwelling brings deep nutrients to the sunlit surface.
2. Strong stratification keeps nutrients in deep water, away from the sunlit surface where phytoplankton grow.
3. Surplus yield = r × N × (1 − N ÷ K); r = maximum growth rate, N = stock size, K = carrying capacity.
4. 0.3 × 150 000 × (1 − 0.75) = **11 250 t per year**.
5. At N = K ÷ 2 = 100 000 t; MSY = 0.3 × 200 000 ÷ 4 = **15 000 t per year**.
6. Any two: K and r are uncertain; stocks fluctuate naturally; to protect the species' role in the food web; to keep catch per unit effort high.
7. As large predators are depleted, catches shift to species at lower trophic levels, so the average trophic level of landings falls.
8. Each generation judges a stock or ecosystem against the state it first knew, so long-term decline is underestimated.
9. 1992.
10. A share is worth more when the stock is healthy, so owners gain from supporting conservative catches; the race to fish ends.
11. Seaweed and shellfish take up dissolved nutrients and particles released by the fish.
12. Advantage: no escapes or direct pollution of open water. Disadvantage: high energy and building costs.
