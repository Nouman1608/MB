These questions cover section 4.7, Circle theorems I, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The theorems are explained in the [circle theorems I study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i/), and the [circle theorems I revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-i-revision-notes/) summarise them. In every question, O is the centre of the circle. Give a geometrical reason for each angle you find. Diagrams are not drawn to scale.

## Section A

**1.** AB is a diameter of a circle. C is a point on the circumference and angle ABC = 63°. Find angle BAC, giving reasons. **[2]**

**2.** PT is a tangent to a circle at T, and angle TOP = 70°. Find angle TPO, giving a reason. **[2]**

**3.** A, B and C are on a circle and angle AOB = 112°, where the angle stands on the minor arc AB and C is on the major arc. Find angle ACB, giving a reason. **[2]**

**4.** A, B, C and D are on a circle, with C and D on the same side of the chord AB. Angle ACB = 37°. Find angle ADB, giving a reason. **[2]**

**5.** ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral with angle DAB = 108°. Find angle BCD, giving a reason. **[2]**

**6.** A tangent touches a circle at T. A chord TA makes an angle of 52° with the tangent. Find the angle in the alternate segment, giving a reason. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A, B and C are on a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = (6x + 10)° and angle ACB = (2x + 15)°, with C on the major arc. Find x, angle ACB and angle AOB. **[4]**

**8.** A, B and C are on a circle with centre O, and angle AOB = 132°.

**(a)** Find angle OAB, giving reasons.

**(b)** Find angle ACB, where C is on the major arc, giving a reason. **[4]**

**9.** AC is a diameter of a circle, and B and D are on the circumference on opposite sides of AC. Angle CAD = 34°.

**(a)** Find angle ADC.

**(b)** Find angle ACD.

**(c)** Find angle ABC.

Give a reason for each answer. **[4]**

**10.** AB is a diameter of a circle with centre O. C is on the circumference and angle CAB = 58°. The tangent at C meets BA extended at D.

**(a)** Find angle ACB.

**(b)** Find angle ABC.

**(c)** Find angle ACD.

**(d)** Find angle CDA. **[5]**

**11.** A, B, C and D lie in this order on a circle. Angle DBC = 48° and angle BCA = 35°.

**(a)** Find angle DAC, giving a reason.

**(b)** Find angle ADB, giving a reason. **[4]**

**12.** The opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral are (4x + 10)° and (5x − 1)°. Find x and the size of each of the two angles. **[3]**

**13.** Explain why a parallelogram can be a cyclic quadrilateral only if it is a rectangle. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Angle ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle) [1]. Angle BAC = 180 − 90 − 63 = **27°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

**2.** Angle OTP = 90° (angle between a tangent and a radius) [1]. Angle TPO = 180 − 90 − 70 = **20°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

**3.** Angle ACB = 112 ÷ 2 = **56°** [1] (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference) [1].

**4.** Angle ADB = **37°** [1] (angles in the same segment are equal) [1].

**5.** Angle BCD = 180 − 108 = **72°** [1] (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°) [1].

**6.** The angle is **52°** [1] (angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment) [1].

**7.** Angle AOB = 2 × angle ACB, so 6x + 10 = 2(2x + 15) [1]. 6x + 10 = 4x + 30 [1], so **x = 10** [1]. Angle ACB = **35°** and angle AOB = **70°** [1].

**8. (a)** OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles [1]. Angle OAB = (180 − 132) ÷ 2 = **24°** [1].

**(b)** Angle ACB = 132 ÷ 2 = **66°** (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference) [1][1].

**9. (a)** Angle ADC = **90°** (angle in a semicircle) [1].

**(b)** Angle ACD = 180 − 90 − 34 = **56°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

**(c)** Angle ABC = **90°** (angle in a semicircle) [1][1].

**10. (a)** Angle ACB = **90°** (angle in a semicircle) [1].

**(b)** Angle ABC = 180 − 90 − 58 = **32°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

**(c)** Angle ACD = **32°** (angle between a tangent and a chord equals the angle in the alternate segment, angle ABC) [1].

**(d)** Angle CAD = 180 − 58 = 122° (angles on a straight line) [1]. Angle CDA = 180 − 122 − 32 = **26°** (angle sum of a triangle) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Check (d) another way. Angle AOC = 2 × 32 = 64° (angle at the centre), and angle OCD = 90°, so angle CDA = 180 − 90 − 64 = 26°.

**11. (a)** Angle DAC = angle DBC = **48°** (angles in the same segment are equal) [1][1].

**(b)** Angle ADB = angle ACB = **35°** (angles in the same segment are equal) [1][1].

**12.** (4x + 10) + (5x − 1) = 180 [1]. 9x + 9 = 180, so **x = 19** [1]. The angles are **86° and 94°** [1].

**13.** The opposite angles of a parallelogram are equal [1]. For a cyclic quadrilateral they must also sum to 180°, so each is 90°, and a parallelogram with right angles is a rectangle [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a theorem without the angle it applies to, or giving a reason from the wrong theorem.
- Using the angle-at-centre theorem on angles that stand on different arcs.
- Pairing adjacent angles of a cyclic quadrilateral instead of opposite angles.
- Choosing the wrong segment in the alternate segment theorem.
- Not drawing the radii that make isosceles triangles.
