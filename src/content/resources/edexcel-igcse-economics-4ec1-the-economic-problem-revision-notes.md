These notes condense section 1.1.1, The economic problem, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, February 2026, examined in Paper 1. There is a single tier of entry. All figures are invented. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [economic problem study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-the-economic-problem/); then test yourself with the [economic problem practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-the-economic-problem-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Scarcity | Unlimited wants but finite resources, so choices must be made |
| Wants | Goods and services people would like to have; unlimited |
| Resources | Land, labour, capital and enterprise; finite at any time |
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative forgone when a choice is made |
| Economic agents | Consumers, producers and government |
| Production possibility frontier (PPF) | Curve showing the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with all resources fully employed |
| Positive economic growth | An increase in productive potential; PPF shifts outwards |
| Negative economic growth | A fall in productive potential; PPF shifts inwards |
| Unobtainable production | A combination outside the PPF |

## Scarcity in four links

Unlimited wants → finite resources → not all wants can be met → choices must be made.

Scarcity is permanent and affects rich and poor economies alike. It applies to time as well as goods.

## Opportunity cost by agent

| Agent | Example (invented) | Opportunity cost | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consumer | Spends 30 on a concert ticket rather than a pair of trainers | The trainers | Weighs what is given up, not only price |
| Producer | Uses a workshop to make tables rather than chairs | The chairs (or the profit from them) | Moves resources to the best-paying use |
| Government | Builds a road rather than a hospital | The hospital | Every project means another is not funded, or taxes rise |

**Rule:** opportunity cost is **one** option, the next best, not the total of all options.

## PPF: what each feature shows

| Feature | What it shows |
|---|---|
| Point on the curve | Maximum productive potential; resources fully employed |
| Point inside the curve | Unemployed (or under-used) resources; possible but inefficient |
| Point outside the curve | Unobtainable with current resources |
| Movement along the curve | Opportunity cost: more of one good means less of the other |
| Outward shift | Positive economic growth |
| Inward shift | Negative economic growth |
| Pivot on one axis | Change affecting only one good |
| Bowed-out shape | Increasing opportunity cost |
| Straight line | Constant opportunity cost |

## Calculating opportunity cost from a PPF

Opportunity cost per unit = units of good B given up ÷ units of good A gained.

*Example:* moving from 20 to 30 units of A, B falls from 70 to 46. Cost = (70 − 46) ÷ (30 − 20) = 24 ÷ 10 = **2.4 units of B per unit of A**.

## Drawing checklist

- Both axes labelled with the goods.
- Curve labelled PPF (PPF1 and PPF2 for a shift).
- Points plotted from the data; intercepts on each axis.
- Arrow showing the direction of any shift.
- Any named points marked and labelled.

## Causes of growth

| Positive (outward) | Negative (inward) |
|---|---|
| Investment in capital goods | War or natural disaster destroying capital |
| Education and training | Emigration of skilled workers |
| New technology | Depletion of natural resources |
| Discovery of new resources | Loss of land, for example to flooding |
| Immigration of workers | |

**Chain for any cause:** cause → change in quantity or quality of resources → change in productive potential → PPF shifts.

**Link to remember:** producing more capital goods now costs consumer goods today, but can shift the PPF outwards later.

## Exam traps

- "Inside the PPF" means unemployed resources, **not** unobtainable.
- A move from inside to the curve is **not** growth.
- Opportunity cost is the **next best** alternative only.
- Scarcity is not poverty or lack of money.
- Unlabelled axes lose marks even with a correct curve.
- Divide by the units gained when working out opportunity cost per unit.
- One-part definitions where two parts are needed.
- Giving all forgone options as the opportunity cost.
- Confusing unemployed resources with unobtainable output.
- Drawing a shift as a movement along the curve.
- Stating a cause of growth with no link to resources or the PPF.

## Command words

- **Define / What is meant by:** two separate parts.
- **Calculate:** show working.
- **Draw:** labelled diagram with the points asked for.
- **Explain:** statement plus two developments.
- **Analyse, Assess, Evaluate:** extended answers; assess and evaluate need a judgement.

## Quick self-test

1. What is meant by scarcity?
2. Define opportunity cost.
3. Name the three economic agents in the specification.
4. A consumer can spend 20 on a meal, a book or a cinema trip, ranked in that order, and buys the meal. What is the opportunity cost?
5. What does a point inside the PPF show?
6. What does a point outside the PPF show?
7. How is positive economic growth shown on a PPF diagram?
8. Give two causes of negative economic growth.
9. Why is a PPF usually bowed outwards?
10. Moving from 4 to 8 units of food, clothing falls from 60 to 48. Calculate the opportunity cost of one unit of food.
11. A government spends 50 million on defence rather than schools. State the opportunity cost.
12. Explain why investment in capital goods can cause economic growth.

### Answers

1. Unlimited wants and finite resources, so choices have to be made.
2. The next best alternative forgone when a choice is made.
3. Consumers, producers and government.
4. The book.
5. Unemployed or under-used resources: the economy is producing below its potential.
6. Unobtainable production with current resources.
7. An outward shift of the whole curve.
8. Any two: war, natural disasters, emigration of skilled workers, depletion of resources.
9. Resources are not equally suited to both goods, so the opportunity cost rises as more of one good is produced.
10. (60 − 48) ÷ (8 − 4) = 12 ÷ 4 = **3 units of clothing**.
11. The schools that could have been built with the 50 million.
12. More capital goods increase the quantity of capital, so more can be produced; productive potential rises and the PPF shifts outwards.
