## Questions

**1.** In a diagram of water molecules, the oxygen atoms are labelled δ− and the hydrogen atoms δ+.

**(a)** Explain why these partial charges arise. **[2]**

**(b)** State where hydrogen bonds should be drawn in the diagram. **[1]**

**2.** Distinguish between cohesion and adhesion. **[2]**

**3.** Pond skaters are insects that stand and move on the surface of still water. Explain how the properties of water make the surface a habitat for them. **[3]**

**4.** Glass tubes of different internal diameter were stood upright in water, and the height the water rose was measured.

| Internal diameter / mm | 0.5 | 1.0 | 2.0 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Height of water / mm | 60 | 30 | 15 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between diameter and height. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why water rises in the tubes. **[2]**

**5.** Water in xylem vessels is under tension. Suggest why xylem vessel walls are thickened with lignin. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Classify each substance as hydrophilic or hydrophobic: glycine (an amino acid), potassium ions, a steroid hormone, nitrogen gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why potassium chloride dissolves in water. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why water's solvent properties make it a suitable medium for metabolism. **[2]**

**8.** The black-throated loon feeds by diving for fish. Its legs are set far back on its body, and its bones contain less air than those of most flying birds. Suggest how each feature is an adaptation to life in water, and one disadvantage of the leg position. **[3]**

**9.** A pond contains 2.0 × 10⁶ g of water. On a sunny day it absorbs 8.36 × 10⁷ J of energy. Use the supplied values: specific heat capacity of water 4.18 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹, of air 1.0 J g⁻¹ °C⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the rise in temperature of the pond. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the rise in temperature if the same energy were absorbed by the same mass of air. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the significance of your answers for organisms living in the pond. **[2]**

**10.** Suggest why the largest living animals are found in water rather than on land. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) Earth has kept large amounts of water for billions of years. Outline the reasons why. **[2]**

**12.** (HL only) Four invented planets orbit one star. Each has an atmosphere at about Earth's surface pressure.

| Planet | Distance from star / million km | Mean surface temperature / °C |
|---|---|---|
| W | 60 | 310 |
| X | 115 | 72 |
| Y | 160 | 8 |
| Z | 400 | −120 |

**(a)** Deduce which planets lie in the Goldilocks zone. **[2]**

**(b)** A news report says planet Y "must have life". Evaluate this claim. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Oxygen attracts the shared electrons in each O–H bond more strongly than hydrogen, so electrons are shared unequally [1]; the molecule is bent, so the partial charges do not cancel [1]

**(b)** As dashed lines between a δ+ hydrogen of one molecule and the δ− oxygen of a neighbouring molecule [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* A hydrogen bond drawn between the O and H inside one molecule shows a covalent bond, not a hydrogen bond.

**2.** Cohesion is attraction between water molecules, by hydrogen bonding [1], whereas adhesion is attraction between water and a different polar or charged substance [1] **[2]**

**3.** Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together (cohesion) [1]. Surface molecules are pulled inwards and sideways but not upwards, so the surface resists breaking: surface tension [1]. The light insect's weight is spread so it does not break the surface, so it can live on it [1] **[3]**

**4. (a)** Height increases as diameter decreases [1]; halving the diameter doubles the height (height is inversely proportional to diameter) [1]

**(b)** Water adheres to the polar glass surface, drawing it up the walls [1]; cohesion pulls more water up behind it, and in a narrower tube a greater proportion of the water is in contact with the wall [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Quote the data pattern precisely; "it goes up" earns less than the doubling relationship.

**5.** The column is pulled from above, so the walls are drawn inwards [1]; lignin strengthens the walls so the vessels do not collapse [1] **[2]**

**6. (a)** Hydrophilic: glycine, potassium ions [1]. Hydrophobic: steroid hormone, nitrogen gas [1]

**(b)** Potassium chloride is made of charged ions [1]; polar water molecules are attracted to them and surround each ion, separating them [1] **[4]**

**7.** Enzymes and substrates dissolve, so they move freely and collide [1]; dissolved substrates can reach enzyme active sites, so reactions happen in the cytoplasm [1] **[2]**

**8.** Legs far back give strong propulsion when swimming underwater [1]. Less air in the bones reduces buoyancy, so diving takes less effort [1]. Disadvantage: the bird walks poorly on land [1] **[3]**

**9. (a)** 8.36 × 10⁷ ÷ (2.0 × 10⁶ × 4.18) = **10 °C** [1]

**(b)** 8.36 × 10⁷ ÷ (2.0 × 10⁶ × 1.0) = **41.8 °C** [1]

**(c)** Water's high specific heat capacity means its temperature changes much less for the same energy input [1], so pond organisms live in a thermally stable habitat and avoid extreme temperature swings [1] **[4]**

**10.** Water is dense, so buoyancy supports much of an animal's weight [1]; a large aquatic animal needs less skeletal support against gravity than a land animal of the same mass [1] **[2]**

**11.** Earth's gravity is strong enough to hold an atmosphere, so water vapour does not escape [1]; surface temperatures allow water vapour to condense into liquid oceans [1] **[2]**

**12. (a)** Liquid water at about Earth's pressure needs 0 °C to 100 °C [1]; X and Y are in the zone, W is too hot and Z too cold [1]

**(b)** Liquid water is necessary for life as we know it, so Y is a reasonable candidate [1]; but it is not sufficient, and there is no direct evidence of life, so "must" is not justified [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs both a point in favour and a limitation; agreeing or disagreeing alone earns one mark at most.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing hydrogen bonds inside a water molecule.
- Calling water-to-cellulose attraction cohesion.
- Explaining capillary action with adhesion only.
- Saying a substance dissolves because it is "small" rather than polar or charged.
- Confusing specific heat capacity with thermal conductivity.
- Treating the Goldilocks zone as proof of life.
