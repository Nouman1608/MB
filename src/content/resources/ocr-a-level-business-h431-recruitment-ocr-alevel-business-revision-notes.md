## What the specification lists

- What is meant by recruitment; the reasons for recruitment.
- Content and purpose of a job description, a person specification and a job advertisement.
- Internal and external recruitment.
- Impact and importance of recruitment; usefulness of documentation and methods.
- Recommend and justify methods of recruitment.

## Core recall

### Recruitment against selection

- **Recruitment:** attracting suitable candidates to apply. Ends with a pool of applicants.
- **Selection:** choosing between the applicants (next stage; see the selection notes).

### Reasons for recruitment

Replace leavers; growth; new skills; changes in demand; technology or law; diversity; restructuring.

### The three documents

| Document | Content | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| Job description | Title, department, reporting line, duties, responsibilities, place and hours, pay band | Tells applicants what the job is; basis for contract and appraisal |
| Person specification | Qualifications, experience, skills, knowledge, personal qualities; essential and desirable | Describes the person needed; checklist for shortlisting |
| Job advertisement | Title, main duties, key requirements, location, pay, how to apply, closing date, about the business | Attracts suitable applicants |

- Job description = what the job is. Person specification = who can do it. Advertisement uses both.

### Internal against external

| | Internal | External |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Low | Higher |
| Speed | Quick | Slower |
| Knowledge of the business | High | Needs induction |
| Risk | Known candidate | Unknown until employed |
| Motivation | Promotion route motivates | May demotivate staff passed over |
| New ideas | Few | New skills and ideas |
| Side effect | Creates another vacancy | None |
| Pool | Limited | Wide |

### External methods

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Own website and social media | Cheap; interested audience | Needs a strong employer profile |
| Job boards | Wide reach | Many unsuitable applicants |
| Newspapers and trade journals | Targeted | Costly; narrower reach |
| Job centres | Low cost; local | Less specialised |
| Agencies and headhunters | Save time; find specialists | Fee, often a percentage of pay |
| Schools, colleges, universities | Suit trainees and graduates | Limited to early career |
| Word of mouth, staff referrals | Cheap, quick | Narrow; can limit diversity |

### Impact of good and poor recruitment

- Good: strong pool; fewer later costs; skills for strategy; employer reputation; legal compliance.
- Poor: wasted cost; vacancies stay open; overworked staff; wrong hires; high turnover.

## Worked reminder

Trevelyan Hotels (invented), receptionist post paying £26,000, managers' time £30 an hour.

- Internal promotion: £180 + £800 training + £1,100 to fill the vacancy created = **£2,080**.
- External, own advertising: £1,200 + £540 + £1,000 induction + £1,000 slow start = **£3,740**.
- External, agency at 18%: £4,680 + £180 + £1,000 + £1,000 = **£6,860**.
- Internal is £1,660 cheaper than own external and £4,780 cheaper than the agency, but needs a suitable internal candidate.

## Recommending a method

| Situation | Likely best method |
|---|---|
| Promotion-type post with suitable staff | Internal advertisement first |
| Routine local post | Own website, job centre, word of mouth |
| Specialist or senior post | Agency or specialist journal |
| Graduate or trainee post | Universities, colleges, apprenticeships |
| Urgent post | Agency or immediate internal move |

State the cost, the risk and what it depends on.

## Quick case practice

A firm (invented) must recruit an accountant on £30,000. An agency charges 20% of the first year's salary. Advertising it itself would cost £1,500 plus 12 hours of managers' time at £30 an hour.

- Agency fee = 20% × £30,000 = **£6,000**.
- Own recruitment = £1,500 + 12 × £30 = **£1,860**.
- Difference = £6,000 − £1,860 = **£4,140**.

Model response outline: advertising directly is much cheaper, but takes managers' time and may not reach specialists. An agency may be justified if the post is urgent or hard to fill. Consider an internal candidate first, which saves cost but creates another vacancy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Recruitment | Attracting candidates to apply |
| Vacancy | A post to be filled |
| Headhunter | A specialist who approaches suitable people directly |
| Essential criteria | Requirements every candidate must meet |
| Desirable criteria | Extra qualities that favour a candidate |
| Induction | Introduction of new staff to the business |
| Chain effect | Internal promotion creating another vacancy |

## Exam traps

- Recruitment is not selection.
- Do not mix up the job description (the job) and the person specification (the person).
- Internal recruitment creates another vacancy; do not say it is free.
- Match the method to the post, the budget and the urgency.
- Advertisements must not discriminate.
- Quote costs from the case when comparing methods.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between recruitment and selection.
2. Name two things found in a job description.
3. Name two things found in a person specification.
4. What is the difference between essential and desirable criteria?
5. Give two advantages of internal recruitment.
6. Give one disadvantage of internal recruitment.
7. A recruitment agency charges 15% of a £28,000 salary. Calculate the fee.
8. A manager spends 10 hours at £30 an hour on a recruitment. Calculate the cost of time.
9. Give one reason for using a trade journal rather than a national newspaper.
10. Give two reasons why a business might recruit.

### Answers

1. Recruitment attracts a pool of applicants; selection chooses among them.
2. Any two: job title; duties; reporting line; place and hours of work; pay band.
3. Any two: qualifications; experience; skills; personal qualities.
4. Essential criteria must be met by every candidate; desirable criteria are extra qualities that favour a candidate.
5. Any two: lower cost; quicker; candidate knows the business; motivates staff.
6. Any one: limited pool; creates another vacancy; may demotivate staff who are passed over.
7. 15% × £28,000 = **£4,200**.
8. 10 × £30 = **£300**.
9. It reaches people in a specific profession, so applicants are more likely to be suitable.
10. Any two: replacing a leaver; growth; new skills; seasonal demand; restructuring.
