## Section A - short questions

**1.** A customer is injured when a shelf falls in a shop. Which insurance would pay the compensation? **[1]**
A employer's liability   B public liability   C motor   D consequential loss

**2.** Which document gives temporary proof of insurance until the policy is ready? **[1]**
A proposal form   B policy   C cover note   D invoice

**3.** What is the price paid for insurance called? **[1]**
A a claim   B a premium   C a commission   D a sum insured

**4.** State **two** types of insurance risk named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**5.** Define an insurance broker. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** purposes of insurance. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Mina owns a clothes shop. A fire closes the shop for six weeks while it is repaired.*
(a) Identify the type of insurance that would pay for Mina's lost profit during the closure. **[1]**
(b) Explain **one** reason why Mina would also need premises insurance. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** reason why this insurance gives Mina confidence in running her business. **[2]**

**8.** Explain how pooling of risk allows an insurer to pay claims. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the purpose of a proposal form and explain why the person completing it should be honest. **[4]**

**10.** *A warehouse owner insures stock worth $80,000. The fire rate is 0.4% of the value insured and the theft rate is 0.6%. The insurer gives a 15% discount on the total because the warehouse has sprinklers and an alarm.*
(a) Calculate the premium for fire and the premium for theft. **[2]**
(b) Calculate the total premium after the discount. **[2]**
(c) Explain **two** reasons why an insurer would give a discount. **[4]**

**11.** *An insurer covers 5,000 homes. From past records it expects 50 claims a year, each of $8,000 on average. It adds 25% to cover costs and profit.*
(a) Calculate the expected total of claims. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the premium per home. **[3]**
(c) Explain why the insurer needs many customers to predict claims. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss whether a small business should use a broker to arrange its insurance. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. Public liability covers injury to customers and the public. **[1]**

**2.** C. A cover note gives temporary cover. **[1]**

**3.** B. The premium is the price of the cover. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: consequential loss [1]; employer's liability [1]; fire [1]; motor [1]; premises [1]; public liability [1]; theft [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** An independent agent [1] who arranges insurance between customers and insurers, advises on cover and is paid commission [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: risk reduction [1]; compensation [1]; financial protection [1]; business confidence [1]; investment [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Consequential loss insurance [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Fire and storms can damage the building and fixtures [1], and premises insurance pays for the repairs, so Mina does not need to find the money herself [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Insurance pays for large losses [1], so Mina can keep trading, buy stock and employ staff without fear of being ruined by one event [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Many people or firms pay premiums into one fund [1]. Only a few suffer a loss in a given year [1]. The insurer pays those claims from the fund [1], so the cost of each loss is shared among all contributors [1]. **[4]**

**9.** A proposal form is completed by the person asking for insurance [1] and gives details of what is to be insured, its value and past claims, so the insurer can decide whether to offer cover and at what price [1]. The person should be honest because false or hidden information can lead the insurer to refuse a claim [1], leaving the person without cover when a loss happens [1]. **[4]**

**10.** (a) Fire: 80,000 x 0.4 / 100 = **$320** [1]. Theft: 80,000 x 0.6 / 100 = **$480** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Total before discount: 320 + 480 = $800 [1]. After 15% discount: 800 x 0.85 = **$680** [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Any two, 2 marks each: sprinklers reduce the chance and size of a fire loss, so the insurer expects to pay fewer claims [2]; an alarm makes theft less likely, so the risk to the insurer is lower [2]; the discount encourages other customers to install safety measures, which reduces claims in the pool [2]. (max 4 marks) **[8]**

**11.** (a) 50 x 8,000 = **$400,000** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Cost per home: 400,000 / 5,000 = $80 [1]. Add 25%: 80 x 1.25 [1] = **$100** [1]. (3 marks)
(c) With a large number of customers the insurer can use past records to predict how many claims will occur [1], whereas it cannot predict whether one particular home will have a loss, so a small group would make the premium unreliable [1]. (2 marks) **[6]**

**12.** Up to 2 for reasons for, up to 2 for reasons against, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
For: a broker gives expert advice on the type and amount of cover [1]; compares policies from several insurers and saves time [1]; helps to complete forms and make claims [1].
Against: the broker is paid commission, so it may recommend a policy that pays a higher commission [1]; the commission may be built into the cost [1]; a small business with simple needs may find cover directly [1].
Conclusion: for example, a broker is useful where the business needs several types of cover and has little knowledge, but a business with one simple risk can deal with an insurer directly [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that insurance stops fires and theft, instead of paying for the loss.
- Confusing employer's liability with public liability.
- Calculating the premium on the wrong value, or applying the discount as a subtraction of the percentage figure (for example, subtracting 15 instead of 15%).
- Putting the documents in the wrong order.
- Explaining pooling without saying that only a few contributors claim.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the three insurance documents in 0715?** The proposal form, the cover note and the policy.

**Does a broker work for the insurer?** A broker is an independent agent, paid by commission from the insurer.
