Cambridge O Level Commerce 7100 is assessed by two compulsory, externally assessed papers: Paper 1
Multiple Choice (1 hour, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 2 Written (2 hours, 80 marks, 70%), made up of
structured questions of varying length. The syllabus is not tiered and grades run A*-E. These notes
build a preparation plan on top of the site's content guides for
[Commerce and Production](/resources/o-level-cambridge-commerce-commerce-and-production/) and
[Commercial Operations](/resources/o-level-cambridge-commerce-commercial-operations/).

## Paper 1 allows 90 seconds per question -- and some of them are calculations

Forty multiple-choice questions in 60 minutes gives an average of 90 seconds each, and the syllabus
notes that Paper 1 questions may involve simple calculations. That matters, because a calculation
question takes longer than a definition question, and the time has to come from somewhere. **Exam-
preparation priority**: practise the routine commercial calculations -- percentages, simple interest,
discounts, profit and loss figures, insurance premiums -- to the point where they are quick rather
than merely possible. The aim is not to be able to do them; it is to be able to do them in under two
minutes without a second attempt.

## Paper 2 carries more than twice the weight and rewards applied answers

At 80 marks and 70%, Paper 2 determines the grade. Its questions are structured and of varying
length, which means the same paper asks for one-line recall and for developed explanation within a
few minutes of each other. **Exam-preparation priority**: rehearse switching register. A two-mark
part wants two crisp points, not a paragraph; an extended part wants development and application to
the business or situation described. Candidates who write at one consistent length across the whole
paper either waste time on low-tariff parts or under-develop high-tariff ones.

## The mark tariff, not the answer space, tells you how much to write

The most reliable in-exam signal on a structured paper is the number of marks. **Exam-preparation
priority**: build the habit of reading the tariff before reading the question, and of translating it
into a plan -- four marks usually means two developed points or four brief ones, depending on the
command word. Paper 2's 120 minutes across 80 marks is 1.5 minutes per mark, so a four-mark part is a
six-minute answer including thinking time.

## Commerce answers earn marks for application, not for definitions alone

Commerce is a subject about how trade actually works, and its higher-tariff questions typically
present a specific trader, retailer or transaction and ask you to reason about it. A correct textbook
definition that is never connected to the scenario earns knowledge marks and stops there. **Exam-
preparation priority**: when revising each topic, write one sentence that applies the concept to a
concrete situation -- not "define warehousing" but "why this wholesaler's warehouse reduces this
retailer's stockholding costs". That sentence is the shape of an exam answer; the definition alone
is not.

## Command words are the difference between a full and a half answer

"State", "explain" and "discuss" ask for materially different things: a fact, a fact plus its reason,
and a fact plus reasons on more than one side plus a judgement. **Exam-preparation priority**: check
every practice answer against its command word before checking it against the content. A great many
lost marks on Paper 2 are not knowledge failures at all -- they are answers that explained when the
question asked them to evaluate.

## Worked practice scenario: an applied Paper 2-style answer

Question (original, structured-question style): "A small retailer buys stock from a wholesaler rather
than direct from the manufacturer. Explain two benefits to the retailer of buying through a
wholesaler."

```
Point 1 - breaking bulk:
A wholesaler buys in large quantities from the manufacturer and
sells on in smaller quantities.
Development: the retailer can therefore buy the quantity it can
actually sell, which reduces the capital tied up in stock and the
risk of unsold goods -- a manufacturer's minimum order would often
be larger than a small retailer needs.

Point 2 - variety from a single source:
A wholesaler stocks goods from many manufacturers.
Development: the retailer can obtain a wide range of products in
one transaction, reducing the time and transport cost of dealing
with several manufacturers separately.
```

Each point has two layers: the fact, then why it benefits *this* retailer. On an "explain" question,
the second layer is where the second mark of each pair sits -- listing four benefits without
development typically scores less than developing two.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: drill the routine commercial calculations for speed, not just accuracy;
  weight revision towards Paper 2's applied and extended questions; write one applied sentence per
  topic rather than re-reading definitions; check practice answers against their command word first.
- **During Paper 1**: answer recall questions immediately and bank the time for the calculation
  questions; attempt every question, since there is no penalty for a wrong answer.
- **During Paper 2**: read the mark tariff before the question and plan to it; budget roughly 1.5
  minutes per mark; on any question that names a specific business or transaction, make sure at least
  one clause of each point refers to that scenario rather than to commerce in general.

## Self-test

1. Why does Paper 1 need calculation practice specifically, rather than general revision?
2. What does a four-mark "explain" part typically require?
3. Why does a correct definition alone score poorly on a higher-tariff Paper 2 question?
4. In the worked scenario, what is the second layer of each point doing?

**Answers:** 1. Because Paper 1 averages 90 seconds per question and its questions may involve simple
calculations -- so the calculations must be fast, not merely achievable, or they consume time budgeted
for other questions. 2. Usually two developed points -- each a fact plus the reason it matters --
rather than four unexplained ones, though the exact shape depends on the command word. 3. Because the
higher-tariff questions present a specific trader or transaction and award marks for reasoning about
it; an unapplied definition earns the knowledge mark and stops. 4. It explains why the fact benefits
that particular retailer, which is where the explanation mark sits -- the fact alone is only half of
each point.
