These notes condense **section 12.3 Social networking services/platforms** (Topic 12 IT in society) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. Full explanations are in the [social networking study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-social-networking-services-platforms/); test yourself with the [social networking practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-social-networking-services-platforms-practice/).

## The four strands

1. Types of platform.
2. Uses by individuals, businesses, organisations and governments, and in education, finance, healthcare and news.
3. Impact on each of those groups.
4. Advantages and disadvantages of each type.

## Types: recall

| Type | One-line description | Key feature |
|---|---|---|
| Chat room | Many users talking live in a shared space | Real time, often on a topic |
| Instant messaging | Private live messages | Delivery notices; groups; calls |
| Email | Messages stored on a server | Formal record; attachments |
| Forum | Threads of posts and replies | Searchable and lasting |
| Blog | Dated posts by one author, with comments | Long-form writing |
| Microblog | Very short public posts | Fast, widely shared |

## Uses: recall

| User | Typical uses |
|---|---|
| Individuals | Keep in touch; meet people; interest groups; exchange ideas |
| Businesses | Advertising; product and safety information; customer feedback |
| Organisations | Spread information; weather warnings; campaigns; fundraising |
| Governments | Useful information; updating regulations; advice and discussion; news; citizen feedback |

By field: **education** (discussion, resources), **finance** (company announcements, customer contact), **healthcare** (advice, support groups), **news** (stories and eyewitness reports).

## Impact on individuals

| Positive | Negative |
|---|---|
| Meet new people | Intellectual isolation (echo chambers) |
| Keep in contact with family and friends | Ideological polarisation |
| Common interest groups | Stereotyping |
| Exchange ideas | Physical and mental health problems that differ by age group |
| | Cognitive issues: shorter attention span, distraction |

**Health by age (examples).** Teenagers: comparison with others, bullying, less sleep. Adults: stress from constant contact, less exercise. Older people: less loneliness, but greater risk from scams.

## Impact on others

- **Businesses:** targeted advertising; product information; safety information; customer feedback; risk of damage from bad reviews.
- **Organisations:** quick spreading of information, such as weather warnings; risk of rumours and unapproved posts.
- **Governments:** distribute useful information; update regulations; advice and discussion on issues; government news; feedback from citizens. Risks: **censorship**, **false or distorted information**.

## Advantages and disadvantages of types

| Type | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Chat room | Live, many people | Anonymous users; abuse; no record |
| Instant messaging | Fast, private | Interruptions; privacy of stored messages |
| Email | Formal; attachments; works everywhere | Slow; spam and phishing |
| Forum | Lasting, searchable, moderated | Slow replies; wrong advice stays |
| Blog | Detailed; owner controls content | Time consuming; needs promotion |
| Microblog | Rapid; wide reach | No detail; rumours spread |

## Quick example: campaign measures

A club has 8,000 followers. A post receives 320 likes and comments. Engagement rate = 320 ÷ 8,000 = **4%**. It spends £90 on promotion, which brings 450 visits and 18 sign-ups. Cost per visit = 90 ÷ 450 = **£0.20**. Conversion = 18 ÷ 450 = **4%**. Cost per sign-up = 90 ÷ 18 = **£5**.

## Exam traps

- Treating every platform alike.
- Listing only advantages when the question says "discuss".
- Vague health claims without an age group or feature.
- Mixing up forum and chat room.
- Not naming the group (individuals, business, organisation or government) in an impact answer.
- Forgetting censorship and false information when writing about governments.

## Self-test

1. Name four types of social networking service listed in the syllabus.
2. What is a microblog?
3. How does a forum differ from a chat room?
4. Give two uses of social networking by a business.
5. Give one use by an organisation.
6. Give two uses by a government.
7. What is an echo chamber?
8. Explain ideological polarisation.
9. State one effect of social networking on mental health for young people.
10. Give one risk for a government using social media.
11. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of email as a social networking service.
12. A post has 600 likes and comments and the account has 15,000 followers. What is the engagement rate?

### Answers

1. Any four: chat room, instant messaging, email, forum, blog, microblog.
2. A service for very short public posts that are easily shared.
3. A forum keeps threads of posts and replies that can be read later; a chat room is live conversation, and nothing may be kept.
4. Any two: advertising, product information, safety information, customer feedback.
5. Any one: spreading information quickly, weather warnings, campaigns, fundraising.
6. Any two: distributing useful information, updating regulations, advice and discussions, government news, citizen feedback.
7. A situation where a person only sees views like their own, so they are rarely challenged.
8. Groups of like-minded people strengthen each other's opinions and move to more extreme positions.
9. Any one: comparing themselves with others, bullying, loss of sleep.
10. Any one: censorship, spread of false or distorted information, people without access missing messages.
11. Advantage: formal record or attachments. Disadvantage: slower than messaging, or spam and phishing.
12. 600 ÷ 15,000 = 4%.
