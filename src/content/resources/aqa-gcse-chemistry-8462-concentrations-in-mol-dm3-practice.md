These questions cover section **4.3.4 Using concentrations of solutions in mol/dm³** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry
(8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The whole subsection is marked
(HT only), so every question is labelled **Higher tier only**. Relative atomic masses: H 1, C 12, O 16, Na 23,
Cl 35.5, K 39. Show your working.

Learn the content first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-concentrations-in-mol-dm3/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-concentrations-in-mol-dm3-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (Higher tier only) 0.30 mol of a solute is dissolved in water to make 600 cm³ of solution. Calculate the
concentration in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**2.** (Higher tier only) Calculate the number of moles of sulfuric acid in 40.0 cm³ of a solution of
concentration 0.250 mol/dm³. **[2]**

**3.** (Higher tier only) A solution of sodium hydroxide has a concentration of 0.50 mol/dm³.

**(a)** Calculate its concentration in g/dm³. (Mr of NaOH = 40) **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of sodium hydroxide in 250 cm³ of this solution. **[2]**

**4.** (Higher tier only) A solution of hydrochloric acid contains 73 g of hydrogen chloride per dm³ of solution.
Calculate its concentration in mol/dm³. (Mr of HCl = 36.5) **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** (Higher tier only) A student dissolves 5.85 g of sodium chloride (Mr = 58.5) in water and makes the volume
up to 500 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of sodium chloride. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the concentration would change if the same mass were dissolved to make 250 cm³. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher tier only) 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution is exactly neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of
0.100 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O
Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³. **[4]**

**7.** (Higher tier only) 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution is exactly neutralised by 12.5 cm³ of
0.200 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid. H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide in mol/dm³. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate its concentration in g/dm³. **[1]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) 25.0 cm³ of a 0.100 mol/dm³ solution of sodium carbonate is titrated with
0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. Na₂CO₃ + 2HCl → 2NaCl + H₂O + CO₂
Calculate the volume of hydrochloric acid needed. **[4]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) A technician needs 500 cm³ of a 0.200 mol/dm³ solution of sodium chloride (Mr 58.5).

**(a)** Calculate the mass of sodium chloride required. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how the technician should prepare the solution. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) 25.0 cm³ of a 0.400 mol/dm³ solution of copper(II) sulfate is diluted with water to
100 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of copper(II) sulfate in the 25.0 cm³. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the diluted solution. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the number of moles does not change when water is added. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Volume = 600 ÷ 1000 = 0.600 dm³ [1]; concentration = 0.30 ÷ 0.600 = **0.50 mol/dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Volume = 0.0400 dm³ [1]; moles = 0.250 × 0.0400 = **0.0100 mol** [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 0.50 × 40 = **20 g/dm³** [1]
**(b)** 20 × 0.250 = **5.0 g** [2] (1 for converting 250 cm³ to 0.250 dm³; 1 for the answer). **[2]**

**4.** 73 ÷ 36.5 [1] = **2.0 mol/dm³** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** 5.85 ÷ 58.5 = **0.100 mol** [1]
**(b)** 500 cm³ = 0.500 dm³ [1]; 0.100 ÷ 0.500 = **0.200 mol/dm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The concentration would be **doubled** (0.400 mol/dm³) [1] because the same amount of solute is in half
the volume (concentration is inversely proportional to volume) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Moles HCl = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 0.00200 mol [1]; ratio NaOH : HCl = 1 : 1, so 0.00200 mol NaOH [1];
volume = 0.0250 dm³ [1]; concentration = 0.00200 ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0800 mol/dm³** [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Moles H₂SO₄ = 0.200 × 0.0125 = 0.00250 mol [1]; ratio H₂SO₄ : NaOH = 1 : 2, so 0.00500 mol NaOH [1];
volume 0.0250 dm³ [1]; concentration = 0.00500 ÷ 0.0250 = **0.200 mol/dm³** [1]. **[4]**
**(b)** 0.200 × 40 = **8.0 g/dm³** [1]

**8.** Moles Na₂CO₃ = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol [1]; ratio Na₂CO₃ : HCl = 1 : 2, so 0.00500 mol HCl [1];
volume = 0.00500 ÷ 0.200 = 0.0250 dm³ [1] = **25.0 cm³** [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Moles = 0.200 × 0.500 = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 58.5 = **5.85 g** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Weigh 5.85 g of sodium chloride accurately [1]; dissolve it in some water in a beaker and transfer the
solution to a 500 cm³ volumetric flask [1]; add water up to the 500 cm³ mark and mix [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 0.400 × 0.0250 [1] = **0.0100 mol** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 100 cm³ = 0.100 dm³ [1]; 0.0100 ÷ 0.100 = **0.100 mol/dm³** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Only water is added, so the amount of solute is unchanged [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using cm³ instead of dm³.
- Inverting the mole ratio in titrations.
- Forgetting to multiply or divide by Mr when changing between g/dm³ and mol/dm³.
- Calculating moles of the wrong solution.
- Not showing the intermediate steps.
