Condensed notes for sub-topic **2.2 Business documents**. Worked invoice, credit note and statement calculations are in the [2.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-business-documents/); exam-style questions are in the [2.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-business-documents-practice/).

## What 2.2 asks for

- Eight documents: invoice, debit note, credit note, cheque counterfoil, paying-in slip, receipt, bank statement, statement of account.
- How they are used as sources of information.
- That they can be produced and recorded manually or digitally.

## Document by document

**Invoice.** Issued by the seller for a credit sale. Shows quantities, list prices, trade discount, the net amount owed, the date and the credit terms (including any cash discount offered). Seller: sales journal. Buyer: purchases journal.

**Debit note.** Sent by the buyer to the seller about goods returned or an overcharge, asking for a reduction. It leads the seller to issue a credit note.

**Credit note.** Issued by the seller, reducing what the buyer owes. Must deduct the same trade discount as the original invoice. Seller: sales returns journal. Buyer: purchases returns journal.

**Cheque counterfoil.** The stub kept by the business that writes a cheque. Source for a payment on the credit side of the cash book, bank column.

**Paying-in slip.** Completed when cash and cheques are paid into the bank; the stamped counterfoil is kept. Source for the debit side of the cash book, bank column. Banking cash from the till is a contra entry.

**Receipt.** Given by the person who receives money to the person who pays. The payer keeps it as evidence; the issuer's copy supports cash received.

**Bank statement.** The bank's copy of the business's account. Used to find and record items not yet in the cash book (bank charges, interest, direct debits, standing orders, credit transfers) and to prepare a bank reconciliation statement.

**Statement of account.** Sent by a seller to each credit customer, usually monthly: opening balance, invoices, credit notes, payments, closing balance. The customer checks it against its own records. It is **not** recorded again.

## Who sends what

| Direction | Documents |
|---|---|
| Seller to buyer | Invoice, credit note, statement of account, receipt |
| Buyer to seller | Debit note, payment (cheque or transfer) |
| Kept by the business itself | Cheque counterfoil, paying-in slip counterfoil |
| Bank to business | Bank statement |

## Discounts on documents

| | Trade discount | Cash discount |
|---|---|---|
| Purpose | Lower price for trade or bulk buyers | Reward for paying early |
| Where shown | Deducted on the invoice | Offered in the terms on the invoice |
| Recorded in the books? | Never; only the net amount is recorded | Yes, when payment is made within the terms, in the cash book discount columns |
| Applies to credit notes? | Yes, deduct it in the same way | Not applicable |

**Quick example:** list price 900, trade discount 20% = 180, invoice 720. Cash discount 5% for payment within 7 days = 36, so a prompt payment is 684. The sales journal shows 720.

## Manual and digital

| Benefits of digital documents | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Sent and received instantly | Need equipment, power and internet |
| Can be imported into software without retyping | Risk of hacking, viruses or deletion |
| Easy to store, search and copy | Duplicated documents can be recorded twice |
| Less paper and printing | Staff need training; systems cost money |

Bank transfers and card payments are increasingly replacing cheques and paying-in slips, and online statements replace printed ones. The entries made are the same.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Source document | The original evidence from which a transaction is recorded |
| List price | The catalogue price before trade discount |
| Trade discount | A reduction in list price given to trade customers |
| Cash discount | A reduction offered for prompt payment |
| Credit terms | The period allowed for payment and any cash discount |
| Payee | The person to whom a cheque or payment is made |
| Drawer | The person or business that writes a cheque |
| Contra entry | An entry on both sides of the cash book, such as banking cash |

## Exam traps

- Credit notes come from the **seller**; debit notes from the **buyer**.
- A credit note for returned goods must deduct **trade discount**.
- Cash discount is only allowed if payment is made **within the stated period**.
- The statement of account is a **check**, not a new entry.
- A bank statement shows the account from the **bank's** side, so money in is a credit on it; in the cash book it is a debit.
- A receipt is evidence for the **payer**; a counterfoil is the payer's own record.

## Self-test

**1.** Which document does a seller send when goods are returned?

**2.** Which document does a buyer send to report an overcharge?

**3.** Goods with a list price of 600 are sold with 25% trade discount. What amount is entered in the sales journal?

**4.** Why is trade discount not recorded in the ledger?

**5.** Which book of prime entry is written up from cheque counterfoils?

**6.** What does the paying-in slip counterfoil prove?

**7.** Name two items a business might first discover from its bank statement.

**8.** Why does a customer check a statement of account?

**9.** Goods invoiced at 400 after 20% trade discount are returned in full. What is the credit note value?

**10.** State one benefit and one limitation of emailed invoices.

**11.** Who keeps the receipt when a cash payment is made?

**12.** An invoice for 1,000 offers 3% cash discount within 10 days. The customer pays on day 20. How much should it pay?

## Self-test answers

**1.** A credit note.

**2.** A debit note.

**3.** 600 - 150 = 450.

**4.** It is a reduction in price, not a transaction; only the net amount is owed.

**5.** The cash book (credit side, bank column).

**6.** That the stated cash and cheques were paid into the bank on that date.

**7.** Any two: bank charges, interest, direct debits, standing orders, credit transfers.

**8.** To make sure it agrees with the customer's own record of the supplier's account before paying.

**9.** 400 (the net amount already invoiced).

**10.** Benefit: arrives instantly, can be imported into software, easy to store. Limitation: needs equipment and internet, risk of loss or hacking, could be recorded twice.

**11.** The person who paid.

**12.** 1,000: the payment is outside the discount period.
