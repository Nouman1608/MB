The syllabus asks you to explain the role of yeast in the production of bread and ethanol; to understand why bacteria are useful in biotechnology and genetic modification (rapid reproduction and the ability to make complex molecules); to discuss why they are useful in terms of ethical concerns and plasmids; to describe how fermenters are used for large-scale production, including the conditions that need to be controlled (temperature, pH, oxygen, nutrient supply and waste products); and to describe the use of enzymes in biological washing powders, pectinase in fruit juice production and lactase for lactose-free milk.

## What is biotechnology?

**Biotechnology** is the use of living organisms, or substances made by them (such as enzymes), to make useful products or to carry out useful processes. People have used it for thousands of years to make bread, beer and cheese. Modern biotechnology also uses bacteria and fungi grown on a large scale to make medicines and foods.

## Yeast

Yeast is a single-celled fungus. When yeast is given sugar and there is little or no oxygen, it carries out **anaerobic respiration**, also called **fermentation**. The word equation is:

glucose gives ethanol + carbon dioxide (and a small amount of energy)

Balanced chemical equation: C6H12O6 → 2C2H5OH + 2CO2.

Check the atoms: left side has 6 carbon, 12 hydrogen and 6 oxygen. Right side has 4 carbon + 2 carbon = 6 carbon in total (2 ethanol give 4 carbon, 2 carbon dioxide give 2), hydrogen is 2 x 6 = 12, and oxygen is 2 (from ethanol) + 4 (from carbon dioxide) = 6. The equation balances.

### Bread

1. Yeast is mixed with flour, sugar and water to make dough, and left in a warm place.
2. The yeast ferments the sugar and releases **carbon dioxide**.
3. The carbon dioxide forms bubbles that get trapped in the sticky dough and make it **rise**.
4. When the dough is baked, the heat expands the gas and kills the yeast. The dough is set as a light, spongy bread.
5. The ethanol evaporates during baking.

The product used in bread making is the **carbon dioxide**.

### Ethanol (alcoholic drinks and fuel)

1. Yeast is mixed with a sugar source, such as fruit juice or sugar cane juice, in water.
2. It is kept in conditions with no oxygen, so the yeast ferments the sugar.
3. The product wanted is **ethanol**. The carbon dioxide is released.
4. The concentration of ethanol is limited because high concentrations of ethanol kill the yeast.

Ethanol can also be used as a fuel, often mixed with petrol.

### Worked example 1: yeast investigation

*A student mixed 5 g of yeast with sugar solution at different temperatures and counted the bubbles of carbon dioxide produced in 2 minutes.*

| Temperature (degrees Celsius) | Bubbles in 2 minutes |
|---|---|
| 10 | 12 |
| 25 | 46 |
| 40 | 88 |
| 70 | 0 |

The rate of fermentation increases as temperature rises, because the enzymes work faster. At 40 degrees Celsius the rate is 88 divided by 2 = 44 bubbles per minute. At 70 degrees Celsius there are no bubbles because the enzymes in the yeast were denatured. The percentage increase from 10 to 40 degrees Celsius is (88 - 12) divided by 12 x 100 = 633 per cent.

## Bacteria

Bacteria are single-celled organisms without a nucleus. They are widely used in biotechnology and genetic modification for these reasons.

- **Rapid reproduction.** Bacteria reproduce by binary fission, which can take as little as 20 minutes in good conditions, so a large quantity of product is made in a short time.
- **They make complex molecules.** Bacteria can make complex molecules such as proteins (for example insulin, when they contain the human gene).
- **No ethical concerns.** Bacteria are single-celled and few people believe that they have the same rights as animals, so there is little ethical objection to growing and manipulating them. Using animals raises ethical concerns about their welfare.
- **They contain plasmids.** A **plasmid** is a small ring of DNA, in addition to the main chromosome. A gene from another organism can be inserted into a plasmid, and the bacteria will copy it and make its protein. Plasmids are covered further in the genetic modification subtopic.

When asked to discuss, link each reason to the benefit: for example, rapid reproduction means a quick, cheap supply of the product.

## Fermenters

A **fermenter** is a large container in which microorganisms such as bacteria or fungi are grown to make a useful product on a large scale. Examples of products are antibiotics (from fungi), insulin (from genetically modified bacteria) and mycoprotein for food.

The conditions inside the fermenter must be controlled so the organisms grow and make the product as quickly as possible.

| Condition | Why it is controlled |
|---|---|
| Temperature | Enzymes work best at an optimum; too hot denatures them, too cold slows them. The fermenter is kept at the optimum, using a water jacket to remove the heat the organisms release |
| pH | Enzymes have an optimum pH; the pH is monitored and kept at the optimum by adding acid or alkali |
| Oxygen | Many organisms need oxygen for aerobic respiration to release energy for growth; air is bubbled in. (For ethanol production oxygen is excluded) |
| Nutrient supply | Organisms need sugars, amino acids and minerals to grow and make the product; these are added continuously or in batches |
| Waste products | Waste products such as carbon dioxide and heat can slow growth or be toxic, so they are removed |

A paddle or stirrer keeps the contents mixed, so that the organisms have an even supply of nutrients and oxygen and the temperature is the same throughout. The fermenter is sterilised first so that unwanted microorganisms do not contaminate the culture.

## Enzymes in industry

Enzymes made by microorganisms are used because they work at moderate temperatures and are specific.

### Biological washing powders

Biological washing powders contain enzymes, mainly **proteases** (digest proteins) and **lipases** (digest fats). They break down stains such as blood, egg and grease into small, soluble molecules that can be washed away. The enzymes work at lower temperatures (for example 30 to 40 degrees Celsius) than are needed for non-biological powders, which saves energy. They are denatured by very hot water.

### Pectinase in fruit juice

Pectin is a substance in plant cell walls and in the fruit that holds cells together. **Pectinase** breaks down pectin. When it is added to crushed fruit, it:

- breaks down the cell walls, so more juice is released, which **increases the yield**;
- makes the juice **clearer**, because it breaks down the pectin that makes juice cloudy.

### Worked example 2: pectinase

*1.0 kg of crushed apples yields 520 cm3 of juice without pectinase and 680 cm3 with pectinase. Calculate the percentage increase in yield.*

1. Increase = 680 - 520 = 160 cm3.
2. Percentage = 160 divided by 520 x 100 = 30.8 per cent (to one decimal place).

### Lactase and lactose-free milk

**Lactase** breaks down the sugar **lactose** in milk into glucose and galactose. Some people cannot digest lactose (they are lactose intolerant) and feel ill after drinking ordinary milk. Lactase is added to milk (or the enzyme is immobilised in a column and the milk passed through), producing lactose-free milk. The glucose and galactose taste sweeter than lactose, so less sugar needs to be added to some products.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that yeast uses oxygen to make ethanol. Ethanol is produced in anaerobic conditions.
- Writing that bread rises because of the ethanol. It rises because of carbon dioxide.
- Giving only "they are small" as a reason for using bacteria. Give rapid reproduction, ability to make complex molecules, no ethical concerns and plasmids.
- Saying that all fermenters need oxygen. This depends on the organism and the product.
- Forgetting that temperature in the fermenter needs cooling as well as heating.
- Saying that pectinase "adds" juice. It breaks down cell walls so that more juice is released.
- Saying that enzymes in washing powder work at 100 degrees Celsius. They are denatured.

## Command words

- **Explain**: give the role of yeast, with the product and the reason.
- **Discuss**: give reasons for using bacteria, with the benefit of each.
- **Describe**: give the conditions controlled in a fermenter and why.
