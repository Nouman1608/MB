These questions cover sub-topic 4.10, Economic growth and/or economic development strategies, from the IB Diploma Programme *Economics guide* (first assessment 2022). It is SL and HL content. Countries and figures are invented, and the [1] points in the answers are a revision aid, not IB marks. Revise first with the [4.10 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-4-10/) or the [4.10 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-economics-dp-economics-ib-dp-economics-4-10-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define *export promotion*. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** limitation of import substitution as a growth strategy. **[2]**

**3.** Distinguish between Official Development Assistance and aid provided by non-governmental organisations. **[2]**

**4.** Explain how diversification reduces an economy's vulnerability. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the World Bank and IMF attach conditions to some loans, and **one** criticism of this. **[2]**

**6.** Explain how secure land rights can promote development. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A multinational firm builds a textile factory in the fictional country of Ranmor. It employs 2,400 workers at 5,500 rans a year each, buys 15 million rans of local cotton and services, and earns a profit of 30 million rans, of which 60% is sent to its parent company abroad. Ranmor gave the firm a tax holiday worth 4 million rans a year.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual gain to Ranmor. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain **two** benefits of the FDI not shown in your calculation. **[4]**

**8.** Ranmor's exports are 8.0 billion rans and its annual debt service is 2.0 billion. Creditors agree to debt relief that cuts debt service to 1.2 billion.

**(a)** Calculate the debt service ratio before and after relief. **[2]**

**(b)** The freed revenue is added to an education budget of 3.2 billion rans. Calculate the percentage increase in that budget. **[1]**

**9.** A farmer in Ranmor borrows 400 rans from a microfinance institution at 15% a year to buy a solar water pump, which raises income by 50 rans a month. Calculate the amount left after one year once interest and the loan are repaid, and explain **one** limitation of microfinance as a development strategy. **[3]**

**10.** Ranmor and the fictional country of Tessel report the share of the population with access to electricity (an indicator for Goal 7 of the Sustainable Development Goals). Ranmor rose from 52% to 70% over ten years; Tessel rose from 58% to 66%. Compare their progress. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how privatisation of a state-owned electricity company could affect economic development. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that market-oriented strategies are more effective than interventionist strategies in promoting economic development. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A trade strategy that supports domestic firms in selling goods and services abroad [1], for example through subsidies, trade fairs or a competitive exchange rate, so that growth is led by exports [1].

**2.** Protected firms face little competition, so they may stay inefficient and never become internationally competitive [1]; consumers pay higher prices and have less choice (or trading partners may retaliate) [1].

**3.** ODA is aid given by governments to developing countries [1]; NGO aid comes from non-profit organisations, which often run projects directly with local communities [1].

**4.** With production and exports spread across many goods and markets, a fall in the price of one product [1] has a smaller effect on total export earnings, tax revenue and employment [1].

**5.** Lenders want policies they believe will restore stability and repayment, such as lower budget deficits [1]. Critics argue the conditions can force cuts in health and education spending and limit a government's own choices [1].

**6.** Secure title lets people use land as collateral to borrow for investment [1] and gives them an incentive to improve it, raising productivity and incomes [1].

**7. (a)** Wages = 2,400 × 5,500 = 13.2 million [1]. Profit kept locally = 0.4 × 30 = 12 million [1]. Net gain = 13.2 + 15 + 12 - 4 = **36.2 million rans a year** [1].

**(b)** Skills transfer: workers and managers gain skills and experience they can later use in local firms [1], raising human capital and productivity [1]. Technology transfer: local suppliers adopt better methods to meet the firm's standards [1], improving their competitiveness and possibly their access to export markets [1]. (Credit infrastructure improvements or extra foreign exchange, similarly explained.)

**8. (a)** Before: 2.0 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = **25%**. After: 1.2 ÷ 8.0 × 100 = **15%** [1] [1].

**(b)** Freed revenue = 0.8 billion; 0.8 ÷ 3.2 × 100 = **25%** [1].

**9.** Interest = 0.15 × 400 = 60; extra income = 50 × 12 = 600; amount left = 600 - 60 - 400 = **140 rans** [1] [1]. Limitation: loans are small and suit small trading or farming activities, so microfinance does little to build larger-scale industry (or high interest rates, or repayments starting before income rises) [1].

**10.** Ranmor improved by (70 - 52) ÷ 10 = 1.8 percentage points a year; Tessel by (66 - 58) ÷ 10 = 0.8 [1]. Ranmor has overtaken Tessel despite starting lower [1]. However, the indicator does not show the reliability or cost of supply, and further gains may be harder as remaining households are more remote [1].

**11.** Private owners may run the company more efficiently, cutting costs and investing to reduce power cuts [1], which lowers firms' costs and supports growth [1]. However, a private monopoly may raise prices or neglect unprofitable rural connections [1], so poorer households may lose access, which harms development unless the state regulates prices and coverage [1].

**12.** For market-oriented strategies: competition and private ownership raise efficiency [1]; trade liberalisation gives access to cheaper inputs and larger markets [1]; attracting FDI brings capital, skills and technology without heavy public spending [1]. Limitations: markets under-provide merit goods such as education and health, which are central to development [1]; gains may be unevenly shared, widening inequality [1]; privatised utilities may neglect poorer areas [1]. Interventionist strategies can supply these goods and reduce inequality, but face high costs and the risk of government failure and corruption [1]. Judgement: development usually needs both, with market-oriented policies working best where institutions are strong and the state providing human capital and infrastructure, so the claim is only partly true [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting all of a multinational's profit as a gain to the host country.
- Forgetting to subtract the loan itself when working out a microfinance return.
- Comparing SDG indicator levels without comparing rates of change.
- Evaluating strategies without linking them to a specific barrier or country condition.
