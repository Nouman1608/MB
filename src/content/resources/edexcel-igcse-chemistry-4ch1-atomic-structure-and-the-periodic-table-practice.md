These questions cover sub-topics 1(c) Atomic structure and 1(d) The Periodic Table (points 1.14–1.24) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Chemistry (4CH1) specification, Issue 3 (September 2024). The qualification is untiered and none of these points has a "C" reference, so all of the questions suit both Paper 1 and Paper 2. A calculator may be used on both papers.

## Questions

**1.** Copy and complete the table for the three sub-atomic particles. **[3]**

| Particle | Relative mass | Relative charge |
|---|---|---|
| Proton | | |
| Neutron | | |
| Electron | | |

**2.** This question is about atoms and molecules.

**(a)** State what is meant by a molecule. **[1]**
**(b)** Helium, nitrogen, ammonia and argon are all gases at room temperature. State which **two** of these substances are made of molecules. **[1]**
**(c)** One of your answers to (b) is an element. Name it and give a reason. **[1]**

**3.** Copy and complete the table to show the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in each particle. **[4]**

| Particle | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| ¹⁸₈O | | | |
| ⁴⁰₂₀Ca | | | |
| ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺ | | | |
| ²³₁₁Na⁺ | | | |

**4.** A sample of silicon contains 92.2% silicon-28, 4.7% silicon-29 and 3.1% silicon-30. Calculate the relative atomic mass of silicon. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**5.** This question is about how the Periodic Table is arranged.

**(a)** State the property used to put the elements in order. **[1]**
**(b)** Argon has a relative atomic mass of 39.9 and potassium has a relative atomic mass of 39.1. Explain why argon is placed before potassium in the Periodic Table. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the atoms of all the elements in one period have in common. **[1]**

**6.** Give the electronic configuration of the element found in each position. **[3]**

**(a)** Period 2, Group 7
**(b)** Period 3, Group 3
**(c)** Period 4, Group 1

**7.** An atom of element Z has the electronic configuration 2,8,2.

**(a)** State the group and the period of Z. **[2]**
**(b)** Name element Z. **[1]**
**(c)** Name **one** other element with similar chemical properties to Z and explain your choice. **[2]**

**8.** A student tests four elements, W, X, Y and Z. The results are shown below.

| Element | Does the solid conduct electricity? | Test on the oxide |
|---|---|---|
| W | Yes | Dissolves in water; universal indicator turns purple |
| X | No | Dissolves in water; universal indicator turns red |
| Y | Yes | Insoluble in water; reacts with dilute sulfuric acid to form a salt and water |
| Z | Yes | A gas that dissolves in water to give a solution of pH 5 |

**(a)** Classify each element as a metal or a non-metal. Give a reason for each, using the oxide result. **[4]**
**(b)** Element Z is a non-metal. Suggest the identity of Z and explain why the conductivity result alone would have misled the student. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe how the student could show that the oxide of Y is basic, and state the result. **[2]**

**9.** Explain each of the following in terms of electrons.

**(a)** Lithium and potassium have similar chemical properties. **[2]**
**(b)** Neon does not readily react. **[2]**
**(c)** Helium is placed in Group 0 even though its atoms have only two electrons. **[1]**

**10.** Chlorine has two isotopes, chlorine-35 and chlorine-37. Its relative atomic mass is 35.5.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage abundance of chlorine-35. **[3]**
**(b)** Give the electronic configuration of a chlorine atom (atomic number 17). **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how this configuration shows the group and the period of chlorine. **[2]**
**(d)** An oxide of chlorine dissolves in water. Predict whether the solution has a pH above or below 7, and explain your prediction using the position of chlorine in the Periodic Table. **[2]**

**11.** A sample of potassium contains 93.3% potassium-39 and 6.7% potassium-41. Potassium has atomic number 19.

**(a)** Calculate the relative atomic mass of potassium. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**
**(b)** State the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in an atom of potassium-41. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the electronic configuration of potassium and state its group and period. **[2]**
**(d)** Describe a test on potassium oxide that shows potassium is a metal. Give the expected result. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why potassium reacts in a similar way to sodium. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Proton: relative mass 1, relative charge +1 [1]. Neutron: relative mass 1, relative charge 0 [1]. Electron: relative mass about **1/1840** (negligible), relative charge **−1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* each row is usually marked as a unit, so one wrong value in a row loses that row's mark; write "0" for the neutron's charge rather than leaving the cell blank.

**2. (a)** Two or more atoms chemically (covalently) bonded together [1].
**(b)** **Nitrogen and ammonia** [1].
**(c)** **Nitrogen**: its molecules contain only one type of atom (N₂) [1].
*Examiner insight:* "a group of atoms" without the idea of bonding is too vague for the definition mark; the word "bonded" or "joined chemically" is what earns it.

**3.** ¹⁸O: 8, 10, 8 [1]. ⁴⁰Ca: 20, 20, 20 [1]. ²⁷Al³⁺: 13, 14, **10** [1]. ²³Na⁺: 11, 12, **10** [1].
*Examiner insight:* a mark per row means one slip costs only that row; check each ion separately by subtracting the charge from the proton number, not from the mass number.

**4.** Ar = (28 × 92.2 + 29 × 4.7 + 30 × 3.1) ÷ 100 = (2581.6 + 136.3 + 93.0) ÷ 100 [1]
= 28.109 = **28.1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* a correct final answer normally earns both marks even with no working, but a wrong answer with no working earns nothing; showing the products gives you a route to the method mark.

**5. (a)** **Atomic number** (proton number) [1].
**(b)** Elements are arranged in order of atomic number, not relative atomic mass [1]. Argon has atomic number 18 and potassium 19, so argon comes first (and this keeps each in a group with similar elements) [1].
**(c)** The same number of occupied **electron shells** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the second mark needs the comparison of atomic numbers; repeating the Ar values from the stem earns nothing.

**6. (a)** **2,7** [1]
**(b)** **2,8,3** [1]
**(c)** **2,8,8,1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* configurations must be written in shell order from the nucleus outwards; 1,8,8,2 or 8,8,2,1 style reversals are not credited.

**7. (a)** Group **2** [1]; Period **3** [1].
**(b)** **Magnesium** [1].
**(c)** **Beryllium** or **calcium** [1]. Both have 2 electrons in the outer shell, and reactions involve the outer-shell electrons [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (c) "in the same group" alone usually earns no explanation mark, because it restates the question; the mark is for the same number of outer-shell electrons.

**8. (a)** W: **metal**; the oxide gives an alkaline solution, so it is a basic oxide [1].
X: **non-metal**; the oxide gives an acidic solution, so it is an acidic oxide [1].
Y: **metal**; the oxide reacts with an acid to form a salt and water, so it is basic [1].
Z: **non-metal**; the oxide gives an acidic solution (pH below 7) [1].
**(b)** Z is **carbon (graphite)**, a non-metal that conducts electricity, so conductivity alone would suggest a metal [1].
**(c)** Add the oxide of Y to dilute acid (and warm) [1]. The oxide reacts and dissolves, neutralising the acid and forming a salt and water (the pH of the mixture rises) [1].
*Examiner insight:* each classification in (a) needs its reason; "metal" with no reference to the oxide or its pH scores nothing for that element.

**9. (a)** Both atoms have **one electron in the outer shell** [1]. Chemical reactions involve the outer-shell electrons, so they react in similar ways [1].
**(b)** Neon has a **full outer shell** (2,8) [1]. This is a stable arrangement, so it has no tendency to lose, gain or share electrons [1].
**(c)** Its outer shell (the first shell) is **full** with two electrons [1].
*Examiner insight:* "stable" on its own is not enough in (b); the second mark needs the idea that the atom does not need to lose, gain or share electrons.

**10. (a)** Let the percentage of chlorine-35 be x, so chlorine-37 is (100 − x).
(35x + 37(100 − x)) ÷ 100 = 35.5 [1]
3700 − 2x = 3550 [1]
x = **75%** [1].
**(b)** **2,8,7** [1].
**(c)** Three occupied shells, so Period **3** [1]. Seven electrons in the outer shell, so Group **7** [1].
**(d)** **Below 7** (acidic) [1]. Chlorine is on the right of the Periodic Table, so it is a non-metal, and non-metal oxides are acidic [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (a) setting up the weighted-mean equation earns the first mark even if the algebra later goes wrong; a guess of "75%" justified only by "35.5 is nearer 35" earns at most the final mark.

**11. (a)** Ar = (39 × 93.3 + 41 × 6.7) ÷ 100 = (3638.7 + 274.7) ÷ 100 [1]
= 39.134 = **39.1** [1].
**(b)** 19 protons and 19 electrons [1]; **22 neutrons** [1].
**(c)** **2,8,8,1** [1]; Group **1**, Period **4** [1].
**(d)** Add potassium oxide to water containing universal indicator [1]. The indicator turns blue or purple: the solution is alkaline (pH above 7), so the oxide is basic, which shows a metal [1].
**(e)** Both have **one electron in the outer shell** [1], and chemical reactions involve the outer-shell electrons [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (a) the instruction "to 1 decimal place" is part of the accuracy mark, so 39.13 or 39 can lose it even when the working is correct; Ar also has no units.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the electron's relative mass as 0 instead of a very small value such as 1/1840.
- Counting electrons in a positive ion as more than the protons.
- Using mass number instead of atomic number when counting electrons.
- Dividing by 100 when the abundances are a ratio, or forgetting to divide at all.
- Rounding Ar to a whole number when 1 decimal place is asked for.
- Writing 2,8,9 for potassium: the third shell holds 8 for the first 20 elements.
- Explaining similar properties with "same group" instead of "same number of outer-shell electrons".
- Saying noble gases have "8 outer electrons" without allowing for helium's full shell of 2.
- Classifying an element without using the oxide evidence the question gives.
