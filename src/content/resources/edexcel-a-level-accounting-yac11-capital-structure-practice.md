## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between a capital reserve and a revenue reserve, and give one example of each. **[3]**

**2.** A company is authorised to issue 500,000 $1 ordinary shares. It has issued 360,000 and has called up 80 cents per share. State the authorised, issued and called-up share capital. **[3]**

**3.** A company with 300,000 $1 ordinary shares makes a 1 for 3 bonus issue out of retained earnings. Prepare the journal entry. **[3]**

**4.** A company has 200,000 $1 ordinary shares. It makes a 1 for 4 rights issue at $2.40. The market price before the issue is $3.00. Calculate the number of new shares, the cash raised, the amount credited to share capital and to share premium, and the theoretical ex-rights price. **[5]**

**5.** State **two** differences between ordinary shares and preference shares. **[2]**

**6.** A company has equity of 540,000, 6% debentures of 135,000 and 8% preference shares of 125,000. Calculate the capital gearing as non-current liabilities, including preference shares, divided by equity plus non-current liabilities. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Kestrel Ltd issues 60,000 $1 ordinary shares at $1.80. The amount is payable: 0.50 on application; 0.90 on allotment (including the whole premium); and 0.40 on the first and final call. All shares are taken up and all money is received.

**(a)** Calculate the amount received at each stage. **[3]**

**(b)** State the amount credited to share capital and to share premium at each stage. **[3]**

**(c)** Prepare the journal entries for the allotment stage. **[2]**

**8.** Kestrel Ltd redeems 40,000 $1 redeemable preference shares at par, using the bank balance. It has no other source of new capital, so the redemption is financed from retained earnings. Prepare the journal entries. **[4]**

**9.** Neva Ltd has equity of 600,000 and 8% debentures of 100,000. It needs 400,000 for expansion. It can either issue 200,000 $1 ordinary shares at $2, or issue more 8% debentures. Calculate the gearing under each option and evaluate which is better for the existing shareholders. **[8]**

**10.** A customer has made a legal claim of 25,000 against a company. The directors believe that the company will probably lose.

**(a)** Explain whether a provision is needed. **[2]**

**(b)** Prepare the journal entry. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A capital reserve arises from capital events, not trading, and cannot be paid as a dividend [1]. A revenue reserve arises from trading profit and can be distributed [1]. One correct example of each, such as share premium and retained earnings [1].

**2.** Authorised **500,000** [1]; issued **360,000** [1]; called up 360,000 × 0.80 = **288,000** [1].

**3.** Bonus shares 300,000 ÷ 3 = 100,000 [1]. Dr retained earnings 100,000 [1], Cr ordinary share capital 100,000 [1].

**4.** New shares 200,000 ÷ 4 = **50,000** [1]; cash 50,000 × 2.40 = **120,000** [1]; share capital **50,000** and share premium 50,000 × 1.40 = **70,000** [1]; ex-rights price (4 × 3.00 + 1 × 2.40) ÷ 5 [1] = **2.88** [1].

**5.** Any two: ordinary shares receive a dividend that varies with profit, preference shares usually receive a fixed rate [1]; preference dividends are paid before ordinary dividends [1]; ordinary shareholders normally have voting rights, preference shareholders normally do not [1]; preference shares may be redeemable or cumulative [1].

**6.** Non-current liabilities including preference shares 135,000 + 125,000 = 260,000 [1]; equity plus those = 540,000 + 260,000 = 800,000 [1]; gearing 260,000 ÷ 800,000 × 100 = **32.5%** [1].

**7. (a)** Application 60,000 × 0.50 = **30,000**; allotment 60,000 × 0.90 = **54,000**; call 60,000 × 0.40 = **24,000** [3]

**(b)** Application: share capital 30,000 [1]. Allotment: share capital 6,000 (60,000 × 0.10) and share premium 48,000 (60,000 × 0.80) [1]. Call: share capital 24,000 [1]. Totals: share capital 60,000 and premium 48,000, with bank 108,000.

**(c)** Dr application and allotment account 54,000, Cr share capital 6,000, Cr share premium 48,000 [1]; Dr bank 54,000, Cr application and allotment account 54,000 [1].

**8.** Dr redeemable preference share capital 40,000, Cr bank 40,000 [2]; Dr retained earnings 40,000, Cr capital redemption reserve 40,000 [2]. The second entry keeps the capital protected for creditors.

**9.** Option 1, shares: equity 600,000 + 400,000 = 1,000,000, debentures 100,000; gearing 100,000 ÷ 1,100,000 × 100 = **9.1%** [2]. Option 2, debentures: debentures 500,000, equity 600,000; gearing 500,000 ÷ 1,100,000 × 100 = **45.5%** [2]. Evaluation: debentures cost an extra 32,000 in interest each year (8% × 400,000) but no dividend or loss of control [1]; if profits grow strongly, existing shareholders gain more because there are no new shares to share profit with [1]; but risk is higher, since interest must be paid even if profits fall [1]; issuing shares brings no fixed cost but dilutes existing holders (200,000 new shares) [1]. Judgement: if profits are stable and expected to rise, debentures may suit existing holders; if profits are uncertain, shares are safer [1]. (Maximum 8 marks.)

**10. (a)** A provision is needed because there is a present obligation from a past event [1], loss is probable, and the amount can be estimated reliably (25,000) [1].

**(b)** Dr statement of comprehensive income (legal expense) 25,000 [1]; Cr provision for legal claim 25,000 [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating a bonus issue as bringing in cash (Question 3).
- Forgetting the share premium on a rights issue (Question 4).
- Adding preference shares to equity, rather than to the liabilities, when using the gearing formula given (Question 6).
- Crediting the whole premium to share capital (Question 7).
- Omitting the second entry for the capital redemption reserve (Question 8).
- Describing gearing without referring to the profits available to ordinary shareholders (Question 9).

## Approaching these questions

For each issue, split the amount between nominal value and premium first. Write the journal in full, with narrative. For gearing, state the formula, calculate, and then say what the percentage implies for risk and returns. For a provision, check the three conditions before recording it.
