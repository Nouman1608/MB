## Unit 2 at a glance

1 hour 30 minutes, 90 marks, single exam, every topic compulsory in its own 30-mark section — 3.2.1 Biopsychology, 3.2.2 Cognitive development, 3.2.3 Research methods 1.

## Biopsychology — key structure

**Nervous system:** central (brain, spinal cord) vs peripheral (somatic, autonomic) — the autonomic system is a *division of* the peripheral system, not a separate category.

**Neuron → synapse:** sensory, relay and motor neurons; synaptic transmission via neurotransmitters, with excitation and inhibition.

**Brain localisation:** motor, somatosensory, visual, auditory and language centres; Broca's and Wernicke's areas; split-brain research; plasticity and functional recovery after trauma.

Revise with **labelled diagrams** (nervous system divisions, a neuron with synapse, localised brain regions) rather than prose alone — this content rewards precise anatomical vocabulary.

## Cognitive development — Piaget vs Vygotsky

| | Piaget | Vygotsky |
|---|---|---|
| View | Internal, stage-based development | Socially and culturally driven |
| Key ideas | Schemas, assimilation, accommodation, equilibration; object permanence, conservation, egocentrism, class inclusion | Zone of proximal development, scaffolding |

Also: Baillargeon's violation-of-expectation research (challenges Piaget on object permanence timing); social cognition — theory of mind, Sally-Anne studies, mirror neuron system.

Exam questions frequently ask you to **compare** Piaget and Vygotsky directly, not describe either alone.

## Research methods 1 — cumulative foundations

Experimental methods (lab, field, natural); observation types (naturalistic, controlled, covert/overt, participant/non-participant); self-report (questionnaires, interviews); correlations; sampling and its implications; pilot studies; experimental designs (repeated measures, independent groups, matched pairs); random allocation, counterbalancing, demand characteristics, ethics; descriptive statistics (mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation); normal/skewed distributions.

Everything here is assumed knowledge for Research Methods 2 at A2 — build it securely now.

## Worked example: interpreting a correlation

A study finds r = −0.7 between pre-bed screen time and sleep duration.

```
Coefficient:   -0.7 = strong NEGATIVE correlation -- more screen
               time associates with less sleep
Not shown:     correlation ≠ causation; a third variable (e.g.
               stress) could plausibly cause both
Evaluation:    efficient and ethical, no variable manipulation, but
               cannot support causal claims and is vulnerable to
               unmeasured third variables
```

Always name a specific, plausible third variable rather than gesturing at "other factors."

## The endocrine system and fight-or-flight

The endocrine system communicates via **hormones** released by glands directly into the bloodstream,
which is slower but longer-lasting than the electrical signalling of the nervous system. The
**fight-or-flight response** is the specification's named example: a perceived threat triggers the
hypothalamus, which activates the sympathetic nervous system and the adrenal medulla to release
**adrenaline**, producing physiological changes (increased heart rate, blood diverted to muscles,
pupil dilation) that prepare the body for rapid action. Learn this as a single connected sequence
(threat → hypothalamus → sympathetic activation → adrenaline → physiological changes) rather than as
a list of disconnected facts, since exam questions often ask you to explain the *sequence*, not just
name the hormone involved.

## Split-brain research and lateralisation

**Hemispheric lateralisation** refers to the idea that some functions are dominated by one hemisphere
of the brain rather than shared equally — language, for most people, is left-hemisphere dominant
(Broca's area for speech production, Wernicke's area for language comprehension). **Split-brain
research** studies patients whose corpus callosum (the structure connecting the two hemispheres) has
been surgically severed, typically as an epilepsy treatment, allowing researchers to present
information to just one hemisphere at a time and observe what the patient can and cannot report or
act on. This research is the specification's key evidence for lateralisation and for **plasticity and
functional recovery** — the brain's capacity to reorganise itself and, to some extent, compensate for
damage by having other areas take over lost functions after injury.

## Exam traps

- Treating the autonomic nervous system as separate from the peripheral system.
- Naming a Piaget stage without its defining feature (e.g. "concrete operational" without conservation).
- Calling a correlation a type of experiment — it is a distinct, non-experimental method.
- Confusing a population with a sample, or naming a sampling technique with no stated implication.

## Theory of mind and the Sally-Anne studies

**Theory of mind** is the ability to understand that other people hold beliefs, desires and
perspectives that may differ from one's own. The **Sally-Anne studies** test this directly: a child
watches Sally hide an object then leave the room, after which Anne moves the object; the child is
asked where Sally will look for it on her return. A child who has not yet developed theory of mind
tends to answer with the object's *actual* new location, since they cannot yet represent that Sally
holds a false belief about where it is. Answering correctly (that Sally will look where she left it)
demonstrates the child can represent another person's mental state as distinct from reality. The
**mirror neuron system** is cited as a possible biological basis for this and related social-cognitive
abilities, since these neurons fire both when performing an action and when observing someone else
perform it.

## Self-test

1. Which two systems compose the peripheral nervous system?
2. What does Baillargeon's research challenge in Piaget's account?
3. Give one strength and one limitation of a correlational study.
4. What is the central executive... no — what is scaffolding, in Vygotsky's theory?
5. Name the three self-report/observation categories examined in Research Methods 1.

**Answers:** 1. The somatic and autonomic nervous systems. 2. The timing of when object permanence develops in infants, via violation-of-expectation methodology. 3. Strength: can efficiently and ethically identify relationships without manipulating variables; limitation: cannot establish causation and can be affected by unmeasured third variables. 4. Support given by a more knowledgeable other, adjusted and withdrawn as the learner's competence grows, within their zone of proximal development. 5. Observation techniques, self-report techniques, and experimental/correlational methods.
