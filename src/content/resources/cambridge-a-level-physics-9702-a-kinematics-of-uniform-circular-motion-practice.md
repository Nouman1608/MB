## Section A

**1.** Define the radian. **[1]**

**2.** Convert 45° into radians, and 2.5 rad into degrees. **[2]**

**3.** Define angular speed and state its SI unit. **[2]**

**4.** A wheel completes 4.5 revolutions. State its angular displacement in radians. **[1]**

**5.** A ceiling fan rotates at 90 revolutions per minute. The tips of its blades are 0.60 m from the axis. Calculate the angular speed of the fan and the linear speed of a blade tip. **[3]**

**6.** Starting from the definition of the radian, show that the linear speed v of an object moving in a circle of radius r with angular speed ω is given by v = rω. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A record turntable rotates at a constant 33.3 revolutions per minute.

**(a)** Calculate its angular speed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its period of rotation. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the linear speed of a point 0.15 m from the centre. **[1]**

**(d)** State the angular speed and the linear speed of a point 0.075 m from the centre, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**8.** A car travels at a constant 18 m s⁻¹. Its wheels have radius 0.32 m and roll without slipping.

**(a)** Calculate the angular speed of each wheel. **[2]**

**(b)** Express this angular speed in revolutions per minute. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the number of revolutions each wheel makes while the car travels 2.0 km. **[2]**

**9.** The Earth orbits the Sun in an approximately circular path of radius 1.5 × 10¹¹ m, taking 365 days for one orbit.

**(a)** Calculate the angular speed of the Earth in its orbit. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the Earth's orbital speed. **[2]**

**10.** A belt that does not slip connects pulley A, of radius 0.050 m, to pulley B, of radius 0.15 m. Pulley A rotates at 30 rad s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the linear speed of the belt. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the angular speed of pulley B. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the period of rotation of pulley B. **[1]**

**11.** A fairground ride has seats at 4.0 m and 8.0 m from its vertical axis. It completes one revolution every 6.0 s.

**(a)** Calculate the angular speed of the ride. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the linear speed of each seat. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the angle, in radians and in degrees, through which the ride turns in 2.0 s. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the distance travelled by the outer seat in 2.0 s. **[1]**

**12.** A satellite moves in a circular orbit of radius 6.7 × 10⁶ m and takes 90 minutes to complete one orbit.

**(a)** Calculate its angular speed. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate its linear speed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the angle through which it moves in 10 minutes, in degrees. **[1]**

**(d)** The satellite's speed is constant. Explain why its velocity is not constant. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The angle subtended at the centre of a circle by an arc equal in length to the radius of the circle [1].

**2.** 45° × π/180 = 0.785 rad (π/4) [1]; 2.5 × 180/π = 143° [1].

**3.** The rate of change of angular displacement (angle turned through per unit time) [1]; rad s⁻¹ [1].

**4.** 4.5 × 2π = 9π = 28.3 rad [1].

**5.** f = 90 ÷ 60 = 1.5 rev s⁻¹ [1]; ω = 2π × 1.5 = 9.4 rad s⁻¹ [1]; v = rω = 0.60 × 9.42 = 5.7 m s⁻¹ [1].

**6.** From the definition, arc length s = rθ (θ in radians) [1]. Dividing by the time t: s/t = r(θ/t), and s/t = v while θ/t = ω, so v = rω [1].

**7. (a)** f = 33.3 ÷ 60 = 0.555 rev s⁻¹ [1]; ω = 2π × 0.555 = 3.49 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** T = 2π ÷ 3.49 = 1.80 s [1].

**(c)** v = rω = 0.15 × 3.49 = 0.52 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(d)** Angular speed 3.49 rad s⁻¹ (unchanged) and linear speed 0.26 m s⁻¹ [1]; every point on the rigid turntable turns through the same angle in the same time, so ω is the same, and v = rω is halved because r is halved [1].

**8. (a)** ω = v ÷ r = 18 ÷ 0.32 [1] = 56 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** 56.25 ÷ 2π × 60 = 537 rpm (about 540 rpm) [1].

**(c)** Distance per revolution = 2π × 0.32 = 2.01 m [1]; revolutions = 2000 ÷ 2.01 = 995 (about 990–1000) [1].

**9. (a)** T = 365 × 24 × 3600 = 3.15 × 10⁷ s [1]; ω = 2π ÷ 3.15 × 10⁷ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁷ rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** v = rω = 1.5 × 10¹¹ × 1.99 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = 3.0 × 10⁴ m s⁻¹ [1].

**10. (a)** v = r_A ω_A = 0.050 × 30 = 1.5 m s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** The rim of B moves at the belt speed [1], so ω_B = 1.5 ÷ 0.15 = 10 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** T = 2π ÷ 10 = 0.63 s [1].

**11. (a)** ω = 2π ÷ 6.0 = 1.05 rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** Inner: 4.0 × 1.05 = 4.2 m s⁻¹; outer: 8.0 × 1.05 = 8.4 m s⁻¹ [1 each].

**(c)** θ = ωt = 1.05 × 2.0 = 2.09 rad = 120° [1].

**(d)** s = rθ = 8.0 × 2.09 = 16.8 m [1].

**12. (a)** T = 90 × 60 = 5400 s [1]; ω = 2π ÷ 5400 = 1.16 × 10⁻³ rad s⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** v = rω = 6.7 × 10⁶ × 1.16 × 10⁻³ = 7.8 × 10³ m s⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** 10 minutes is 1/9 of the period, so the angle is 360° ÷ 9 = 40° [1].

**(d)** Velocity is a vector; its direction, along the tangent to the orbit, changes continuously [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using degrees instead of radians in s = rθ, ω = θ/t or v = rω.
- Not converting rpm, minutes or days to seconds and radians.
- Giving pulleys linked by a belt the same angular speed.
- Giving points on the same rotating object different angular speeds.
- Defining angular speed as "speed in a circle" without mentioning angle.
- Saying a satellite moving at constant speed has constant velocity.
