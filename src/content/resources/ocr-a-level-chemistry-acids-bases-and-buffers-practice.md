This set covers section 5.1.3, Acids, bases and buffers, from the H432 specification for OCR A Level Chemistry A (Version 3.1, May 2026; first assessment 2017). The questions reach every outcome from 5.1.3(a) to (o) in Module 5 of the full H432 A level. Questions 11 and 12 are extended problems.

If you get stuck, see the [study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-acids-bases-and-buffers/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-acids-bases-and-buffers-revision-notes/).

Take Kw as 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶. Every solution is at 298 K unless a question gives another temperature.

## Questions

**1.** Methanoic acid reacts with hydrogenphosphate ions in this equilibrium:
HCOOH + HPO₄²⁻ ⇌ HCOO⁻ + H₂PO₄⁻

**(a)** Define the term Brønsted–Lowry acid. **[1]**
**(b)** Identify the two conjugate acid–base pairs, labelling the acid in each pair. **[2]**

**2.** State the basicity of each acid.

**(a)** H₃PO₄ **[1]**
**(b)** ethanedioic acid, HOOC–COOH **[1]**
**(c)** methanoic acid, HCOOH **[1]**

**3.** Dilute hydrochloric acid is added separately to four substances. For each one, give the ionic equation, including state symbols.

**(a)** iron filings, forming Fe²⁺ ions **[1]**
**(b)** aqueous potassium carbonate **[1]**
**(c)** solid magnesium oxide **[1]**
**(d)** aqueous lithium hydroxide **[1]**

**4.** A technician pipettes 20.0 cm³ of 0.240 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid into a 500 cm³ volumetric flask and fills it to the mark using distilled water. Calculate the pH of the solution now in the flask. **[3]**

**5.** **(a)** Calculate the pH of 0.0315 mol dm⁻³ barium hydroxide, Ba(OH)₂. **[3]**
**(b)** At 40 °C, Kw = 2.92 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶. Find the pH of a sample of pure water held at 40 °C, and explain whether the sample is acidic, neutral or alkaline. **[3]**

**6.** Benzoic acid, C₆H₅COOH, is a weak monobasic acid with Ka = 6.46 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of 0.0380 mol dm⁻³ benzoic acid. **[3]**
**(b)** State the two approximations you used. **[2]**

**7.** A weak monobasic acid HR is made up at 0.160 mol dm⁻³, and the solution has pH 2.84. Calculate Ka and pKa for HR. **[4]**

**8.** A weak monobasic acid HW has Ka = 3.3 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³. A solution of HW has concentration 0.0200 mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Use the usual weak acid approximations to calculate the pH. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of HW that your answer to (a) implies has dissociated. Use it to explain why the approximation is unreliable for this acid. **[3]**
**(c)** State whether the true pH is higher or lower than your answer to (a). **[1]**

**9.** A student adds 35.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide to 60.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ propanoic acid (Ka = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³).

**(a)** Calculate the pH of the buffer solution formed. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how this buffer minimises the pH change when a small amount of dilute hydrochloric acid is added. **[2]**

**10.** A buffer of pH 5.15 is wanted. Sodium butanoate, C₃H₇COONa (M = 110.0 g mol⁻¹), is to be dissolved in 250 cm³ of 0.120 mol dm⁻³ butanoic acid (Ka = 1.48 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³). Assume the volume does not change. Calculate the mass of sodium butanoate needed. **[4]**

**11.** A pH probe follows the titration of 30.0 cm³ of 0.0960 mol dm⁻³ butanoic acid (Ka = 1.48 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³) with 0.160 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.

| Indicator | pH range | Colour in acid | Colour in alkali |
|---|---|---|---|
| methyl orange | 3.1–4.4 | red | yellow |
| phenol red | 6.8–8.4 | yellow | red |
| phenolphthalein | 8.3–10.0 | colourless | pink |

**(a)** Calculate the volume of NaOH needed to reach the equivalence point. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the pH before any NaOH is added. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the pH after 9.00 cm³ of NaOH has been added. **[2]**
**(d)** Choose the most suitable indicator from the table and explain your choice. **[2]**
**(e)** Treating the indicator as a weak acid, HIn, explain the colour change at the end point. **[2]**
**(f)** The NaOH is replaced by aqueous ammonia of the same concentration. Describe how the curve changes after equivalence, and explain why no indicator in the table is then suitable. **[2]**

**12.** Blood plasma contains the carbonic acid–hydrogencarbonate buffer. For H₂CO₃, take Ka = 7.9 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate [H⁺] in blood plasma at pH 7.40. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio [HCO₃⁻] / [H₂CO₃] at pH 7.40. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how this buffer responds if a little base enters the plasma. **[2]**
**(d)** A student measures the pH of a plasma sample with a pH meter. Describe how she should calibrate the meter and take a reliable reading. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A proton (H⁺) donor [1]
**(b)** HCOOH (acid) and HCOO⁻ (base) [1]; H₂PO₄⁻ (acid) and HPO₄²⁻ (base) [1]
*Examiner insight:* each pair must differ by exactly one H⁺; pairing the two reactants loses both marks.

**2. (a)** **tribasic** [1]
**(b)** **dibasic** [1]
**(c)** **monobasic** [1]
*Examiner insight:* basicity counts donatable protons, not all hydrogen atoms; counting the C–H hydrogen in methanoic acid gives the wrong answer.

**3. (a)** Fe(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Fe²⁺(aq) + H₂(g) [1]
**(b)** CO₃²⁻(aq) + 2H⁺(aq) → CO₂(g) + H₂O(l) [1]
**(c)** MgO(s) + 2H⁺(aq) → Mg²⁺(aq) + H₂O(l) [1]
**(d)** H⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq) → H₂O(l) [1]
*Examiner insight:* leave out spectator ions (K⁺, Li⁺, Cl⁻) and keep MgO(s) as a solid; an equation whose charges do not balance should not be expected to gain credit.

**4.** New concentration = 0.240 × 20.0 / 500 = 0.00960 mol dm⁻³ [1]
HNO₃ is a strong acid, so [H⁺] = 0.00960 mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = –log(0.00960) = **2.02** [1]
*Examiner insight:* a pH worked out from the undiluted 0.240 mol dm⁻³ cannot be right.

**5. (a)** [OH⁻] = 2 × 0.0315 = 0.0630 mol dm⁻³ [1]
[H⁺] = (1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴) / 0.0630 = 1.59 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **12.80** [1]
**(b)** [H⁺] = √(2.92 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 1.71 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **6.77** [1]
**Neutral**, because [H⁺] = [OH⁻] in pure water [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (b), "acidic because pH is below 7" is wrong; link neutrality to [H⁺] = [OH⁻].

**6. (a)** Ka = [H⁺]² / [C₆H₅COOH] [1]
[H⁺] = √(6.46 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0380) = 1.57 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **2.80** [1]
**(b)** [H⁺] = [C₆H₅COO⁻], because the dissociation of water is negligible [1]
[C₆H₅COOH]equilibrium ≈ 0.0380 mol dm⁻³, because dissociation is so small [1]
*Examiner insight:* "the acid is weak" is not an approximation; say what is assumed equal to what.

**7.** [H⁺] = 10⁻²·⁸⁴ = 1.45 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]
Ka = (1.45 × 10⁻³)² / 0.160 [1]
Ka = **1.31 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³** [1]
pKa = –log(1.31 × 10⁻⁵) = **4.88** [1]
*Examiner insight:* give Ka its units, mol dm⁻³, and give pKa none.

**8. (a)** [H⁺] = √(3.3 × 10⁻³ × 0.0200) = 8.12 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **2.09** [1]
**(b)** Percentage = (8.12 × 10⁻³ / 0.0200) × 100 = **41%** [1]
The approximation assumes [HW]equilibrium ≈ 0.0200 mol dm⁻³, i.e. very little dissociation [1]
But 41% has dissociated, so [HW]equilibrium is much less than 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ and the assumption fails [1]
**(c)** **Higher** (the approximation overestimates [H⁺]) [1]
*Examiner insight:* name the specific approximation that fails; "approximations are inaccurate" earns little.

**9. (a)** n(propanoic acid) = 0.0600 × 0.150 = 0.00900 mol; n(OH⁻) = 0.0350 × 0.100 = 0.00350 mol [1]
n(propanoate) = 0.00350 mol; n(acid) left = 0.00900 – 0.00350 = 0.00550 mol [1]
[H⁺] = 1.35 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.00550 / 0.00350 = 2.12 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **4.67** [1]
**(b)** Added H⁺ reacts with the conjugate base: CH₃CH₂COO⁻ + H⁺ → CH₃CH₂COOH [1]
The equilibrium CH₃CH₂COOH ⇌ H⁺ + CH₃CH₂COO⁻ shifts left, removing most of the added H⁺, so [H⁺] and pH change very little [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (a), subtract the OH⁻ from the acid; in (b), name the species that removes H⁺ and the direction of shift.

**10.** [H⁺] = 10⁻⁵·¹⁵ = 7.08 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³ [1]
[C₃H₇COO⁻] = Ka × [HA] / [H⁺] = 1.48 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.120 / (7.08 × 10⁻⁶) = 0.251 mol dm⁻³ [1]
n = 0.251 × 0.250 = 0.0627 mol [1]
mass = 0.0627 × 110.0 = **6.90 g** [1]
*Examiner insight:* write the rearranged Ka expression before substituting; forgetting to convert 250 cm³ to 0.250 dm³ gives a mass 1000 times too large.

**11. (a)** n(acid) = 0.0300 × 0.0960 = 2.88 × 10⁻³ mol; V = 2.88 × 10⁻³ / 0.160 = **18.0 cm³** [1]
**(b)** [H⁺] = √(1.48 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0960) = 1.19 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]
pH = **2.92** [1]
**(c)** 9.00 cm³ is half the equivalence volume, so [HA] = [A⁻] and [H⁺] = Ka [1]
pH = pKa = –log(1.48 × 10⁻⁵) = **4.83** [1]
**(d)** **Phenolphthalein** [1]
Its range, 8.3–10.0, lies inside the steep section (about pH 7–11); the other two change colour before equivalence [1]
**(e)** HIn ⇌ H⁺ + In⁻; HIn is colourless and In⁻ is pink [1]
Added OH⁻ removes H⁺, so the equilibrium shifts right and the pink In⁻ form appears at the end point [1]
**(f)** After equivalence the pH rises only slowly and levels off lower, so there is no sharp steep section [1]
An indicator needs a sharp pH change across its range at equivalence, so none is suitable for a weak acid–weak base titration [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (d), a choice without a reason linking its range to the steep section is unlikely to gain the second mark.

**12. (a)** [H⁺] = 10⁻⁷·⁴⁰ = **3.98 × 10⁻⁸ mol dm⁻³** [1]
**(b)** Ka = [H⁺][HCO₃⁻] / [H₂CO₃], so [HCO₃⁻] / [H₂CO₃] = Ka / [H⁺] [1]
= (7.9 × 10⁻⁷) / (3.98 × 10⁻⁸) = **20** (to 2 s.f.) [1]
**(c)** OH⁻ from the base reacts with H⁺ to form water, lowering [H⁺] [1]
H₂CO₃ dissociates, so H₂CO₃ ⇌ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻ shifts right, replacing the H⁺ and keeping blood pH within 7.35–7.45 [1]
**(d)** Calibrate with buffer solutions of known pH, e.g. pH 4, 7 and 10 [1]
Rinse the electrode with deionised water between solutions and blot dry [1]
Immerse the bulb, stir gently and record the value only when the reading is steady, at constant temperature [1]
*Examiner insight:* in (d), each mark needs a separate practical point.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using –log[OH⁻] as the pH of a strong base instead of going through Kw.
- Not doubling [OH⁻] for Ba(OH)₂ and other group 2 hydroxides.
- Stating "the acid is weak" instead of the two specific approximations.
- Describing buffer action without naming the reacting species and the direction of shift.
- Picking an indicator that changes colour before or after the steep part of the curve.
- Giving units for pH or pKa, or leaving them off Ka.
