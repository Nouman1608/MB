## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain the purpose of appraisal. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between self-assessment and 360 degree assessment. **[3]**

**3.** Calculate the statutory holiday entitlement, in days, of an employee who works 3 days a week. The statutory minimum is 5.6 times the number of days worked in a week. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** problem with using target setting for appraisal. **[2]**

**5.** Which **one** of the following measures the proportion of employees who leave in a year?

A: Absenteeism.

B: Productivity.

C: Labour turnover.

D: Lateness.

**[1]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Rosewell Dairy has 80 staff, each expected to work 240 days a year. Last year 1,152 days were lost to absence and 14 staff left. The average number employed was 80.

**(a)** Calculate the absence rate. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate labour turnover. **[2]**

**(c)** Output last year was 96,000 crates; this year it was 104,000 crates from the same 80 staff. Calculate productivity for each year and the percentage change, to one decimal place. **[4]**

**(d)** Evaluate the usefulness of these measures to Rosewell Dairy. **[8]**

**7.** Rosewell Dairy plans performance related pay of £1.50 for each crate an employee handles above 1,200 a year. Every employee handles 1,300 crates. Each extra crate earns a contribution of £2.20.

**(a)** Calculate the bonus per employee and the total bonus for 80 employees. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether Rosewell Dairy should introduce performance related pay. **[9]**

**8.** Recommend an appraisal method for the delivery drivers and a method for the supervisors at Rosewell Dairy. Justify your choices. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Appraisal reviews an employee's performance against agreed standards [1] so that strengths, weaknesses and training needs can be identified and objectives set for the next period [1]. Accept: to decide pay or promotion. **[2]**

**2.** In self-assessment the employee evaluates their own performance [1], whereas in 360 degree assessment feedback is collected from several sources, such as the manager, peers, subordinates and customers [1]. 360 degree gives a wider view but takes more time [1]. **[3]**

**3.** 5.6 × 3 [1] = **16.8 days** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Targets may be set too high or too low [1], so staff either give up or hit them without effort; and targets ignore tasks that are not measured, such as helping colleagues [1]. **[2]**

**5.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Total working days = 80 × 240 = 19,200 [1]. Absence rate = 1,152 ÷ 19,200 [1] × 100 = **6.0%** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** 14 ÷ 80 [1] × 100 = **17.5%** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Last year: 96,000 ÷ 80 = **1,200** crates per employee [1]. This year: 104,000 ÷ 80 = **1,300** [1]. Change = (1,300 − 1,200) ÷ 1,200 × 100 [1] = **8.3%** [1]. **[4]**

**(d)** Absence of 6.0% means 1,152 days lost; if each lost day costs £90 in sick pay and cover, this is 1,152 × £90 = £103,680 a year, so the measure shows a cost worth reducing [1]. Productivity has risen by 8.3% with no extra staff, which is positive [1]. Turnover of 17.5% is useful when compared with the sector and with past years [1].
Limits: none of the measures says why. Absence may be due to illness, low morale or a bad shift pattern [1]. Productivity could be higher because of new equipment or longer hours, not better effort [1]. High productivity does not show whether quality has fallen [1]. Used alone, a measure can be gamed [1].
Judgement: the measures are useful as a warning, but Rosewell needs to follow up with staff surveys and exit interviews to find the causes before acting [1]. **[8]**

**7. (a)** Bonus per employee = (1,300 − 1,200) × £1.50 = **£150** [1]. Total = 80 × £150 [1] = **£12,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Extra crates = 100 × 80 = 8,000; extra contribution = 8,000 × £2.20 = £17,600 [1]. Net gain = £17,600 − £12,000 = £5,600 [1]. The scheme pays for itself on these figures [1].
But the 8,000 extra crates may have happened anyway, since staff already handle 1,300; if so, the bonus is a pure cost [1]. If the target of 1,200 is too low, the business pays for output it would have had without PRP [1].
Staff may rush to raise the count and make more errors or break more crates [1], and teamwork may suffer if staff compete [1]. Employees on PRP have uncertain income, which may reduce loyalty.
Benefits: staff who work hard are rewarded fairly, and motivation may rise [1].
Judgement: Rosewell should introduce PRP with a higher target and a quality condition (for example, damaged crates reduce the bonus), because the gain is small and depends on the target being right [1]. **[9]**

**8.** Delivery drivers: target setting with a manager's review [1], because deliveries completed, on-time rate and damaged goods can all be measured [1]. Add a quality target so that speed does not harm care of goods [1]. Supervisors: 360 degree assessment [1], because their performance depends on how drivers, managers and customers see them [1]. It is costly and time-consuming, so it should be done once a year [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing absence by the number of staff instead of the days available.
- Using leavers over total staff at the end of the year rather than the average.
- Forgetting that part-time holiday entitlement is pro rata.
- Treating a measure as an explanation of the cause.
- Assuming PRP always motivates.
