All businesses and figures are invented. Section B questions refer to the case study about Daleford Kitchens.

> **Case study: Daleford Kitchens**
>
> Daleford Kitchens designs and fits kitchens. It sells about 150 premium
> kitchens a year at an average of $8,000 and 60 budget kitchens at
> $3,500. Many customers pay using bank loans. Daleford has a
> variable-rate loan of $120,000 at 6% for its showroom and an average
> overdraft of $30,000 at 9%. Its profit before interest is $48,000 a
> year. Interest rates are expected to rise by 2 percentage points. The
> town's largest employer has announced 400 job cuts. The owner, who
> avoids interest for religious reasons in his personal life, wants to
> obtain three new vans through an ijara agreement.

## Section A

**1.** Which **one** of the following is most likely to cost a business more immediately after a rise in interest rates? **[1]**
- A. A fixed-rate loan taken out last year
- B. An overdraft
- C. The owner's capital
- D. Retained profit

**2.** Which **one** of the following best describes Sharia compliant finance? **[1]**
- A. A loan charging a lower rate of interest
- B. Finance arranged so that no interest is charged or paid
- C. A grant from the government
- D. An overdraft with no limit

**3.** Define the term "variable interest rate". **[2]**

**4.** State **two** ways in which a fall in the level of employment could affect a business. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a rise in interest rates can reduce consumer spending. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Calculate the extra yearly interest Daleford would pay if the rates on its loan and overdraft both rise by 2 percentage points. **[3]**

**7.** Calculate Daleford's profit after interest before and after the rise in rates. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which a rise in interest rates could reduce Daleford's sales. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how Daleford could obtain its new vans through an ijara agreement, and **one** benefit of doing so. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse how the 400 job cuts at the town's largest employer could affect Daleford Kitchens. **[6]**

**11.** The owner expects sales of premium kitchens to fall by 20% and sales of budget kitchens to rise by 10%. Calculate the overall change in Daleford's yearly revenue. **[3]**

**12.** Evaluate whether rising interest rates or the local job cuts pose the bigger threat to Daleford Kitchens. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** An interest rate on borrowing that can change during the agreement [1], rising or falling as market interest rates change [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two: fewer people in work means lower household incomes, so demand falls [1]; recruitment becomes easier because more people are looking for work [1]; less pressure to raise wages; low-price businesses may gain customers who trade down. **[2]**

**5.** Households pay more on their mortgages, loans and credit cards [1], so they have less income left to spend on goods and services [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Loan: $120,000 × 2% = $2,400 [1]. Overdraft: $30,000 × 2% = $600 [1]. Total extra interest = **$3,000** a year [1]. **[3]**

**7.** Before: interest = (120,000 × 6%) + (30,000 × 9%) = $7,200 + $2,700 = $9,900, so profit = $48,000 − $9,900 = **$38,100** [1]. After: interest = $12,900, so profit = **$35,100** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** **Customers' loans:** many customers pay for kitchens with bank loans [1], so higher rates make a new kitchen more expensive overall, and some will delay buying [1].
**Mortgage payments:** homeowners with mortgages face higher monthly repayments [1], leaving less money for large home improvements such as a premium kitchen [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Under ijara, a financier would buy the three vans and lease them to Daleford for an agreed rent over an agreed period [1], so Daleford uses the vans without borrowing money at interest [1]. Benefit: the rent is agreed at the start, so the cost of the vans does not rise if interest rates rise [1], which makes Daleford's costs easier to plan and fits the owner's wish to avoid interest [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The job cuts mean 400 households in the town lose wages [1], so they are likely to cancel or delay large purchases such as a new kitchen [1]. Other households may fear losing their jobs too, so they become cautious and spend less [1]. Premium kitchens at $8,000 are likely to be hit hardest because they are a luxury that can be postponed [1]. On the other hand, Daleford may find it easier to recruit fitters [1], and some customers may switch to its $3,500 budget range, which would soften the fall in revenue [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Premium: 150 × 0.8 = 120 kitchens; revenue falls from $1,200,000 to $960,000, a fall of $240,000 [1]. Budget: 60 × 1.1 = 66 kitchens; revenue rises from $210,000 to $231,000, a rise of $21,000 [1]. Overall change = −$240,000 + $21,000 = **−$219,000** a year [1]. **[3]**

**12.** **Interest rates:** they raise Daleford's own interest by $3,000 a year, cutting profit to $35,100 [1]; this effect is certain and starts at once because both its loan and overdraft are variable [1]; they also make customers' kitchen loans dearer, cutting demand across the whole area, not just one town [1].
**Job cuts:** 400 lost jobs reduce local spending directly [1], and the forecast fall of $219,000 in revenue is far larger than the extra interest [1]; fear of further job losses may spread caution through the town [1].
**Judgement:** the job cuts are likely to be the bigger threat, because a large fall in revenue matters more than a $3,000 rise in costs [1]. However, the two work together: higher rates and lower local incomes both hit premium kitchens [1]. The answer depends on how many of Daleford's customers come from this town; if it sells across a wider region, interest rates may matter more [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Applying a 2% rise to the old interest bill instead of to the amount borrowed.
- Forgetting the overdraft when calculating extra interest.
- Writing about interest rates without linking them to Daleford's customers, who often borrow to buy.
- Describing ijara as a loan with low interest.
- Comparing threats without using the figures from Questions 6 and 11.
