## Definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Demand | Amount buyers want and can afford at each price during a stated period |
| Demand curve | Plots quantity demanded against price |
| Movement along the curve | Triggered by the good's own price alone (contraction or extension) |
| Shift of the curve | Triggered by any other influence (increase or decrease in demand) |
| Normal good | Demand rises when income rises (YED positive) |
| Inferior good | Demand falls when income rises (YED negative) |
| Substitutes | Alternatives for each other (XED positive) |
| Complements | Used together (XED negative) |
| Total revenue | Price × quantity sold, which equals consumers' total expenditure |

## Shifts of demand: what to remember

| Influence | Shifts demand right when... | Shifts demand left when... |
|---|---|---|
| Income | Income rises (normal good) | Income rises (inferior good), or falls (normal good) |
| Wealth | Wealth rises | Wealth falls |
| Price of a substitute | The substitute gets dearer | The substitute gets cheaper |
| Price of a complement | The complement gets cheaper | The complement gets dearer |
| Preferences | Tastes move towards the good | Tastes move away from it |
| Social and emotional factors | Fashion, status or mood favour the good | They turn against it |

Picture two parallel downward-sloping lines, the second to the right of the first: at the same price, a bigger quantity is wanted.

## The three elasticity formulas

| Measure | Formula | Sign and size tell you |
|---|---|---|
| PED | percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in own price | Negative; size gives elastic (above 1), inelastic (below 1) or unit |
| YED | percentage change in quantity demanded ÷ percentage change in income | Positive means normal; negative means inferior; above 1 is income elastic |
| XED | percentage change in quantity demanded of X ÷ percentage change in price of Y | Positive means substitutes; negative means complements |

Percentage change = (new − original) ÷ original × 100.

### Method in steps

1. Find the percentage change in the price or income (the cause).
2. Work out the percentage change in quantity (the effect).
3. Divide effect by cause. Keep the sign.
4. State what the number means in words and in the context given.

## Worked reminders

### PED and revenue

In Country B (invented) a price falls from 50 to 45 and quantity rises from 800 to 920.

- % change in price = −5 ÷ 50 × 100 = −10%
- % change in quantity = +120 ÷ 800 × 100 = +15%
- PED = 15 ÷ −10 = −1.5, so demand is elastic.
- Revenue: 50 × 800 = 40,000 before; 45 × 920 = 41,400 after. Revenue rises 3.5%.

### YED

Income rises from 2,000 to 2,100 (+5%). Demand for a food staple rises from 400 to 404 (+1%). YED = 1 ÷ 5 = +0.2. It is normal and income inelastic.

### XED

A 12% rise in the price of good Y cuts demand for good X by 6%. XED = −6 ÷ 12 = −0.5. The goods are weak complements.

### Working backwards from a given value

- PED = −0.4 and price rises 5%: quantity falls 0.4 × 5 = 2%. Revenue index is 1.05 × 0.98 = 1.029, so revenue rises 2.9%.
- YED = +2.0 and income falls 3%: quantity falls 2.0 × 3 = 6%.

### Watch-out: large price changes

A price rises from 20 to 25 (+25%) and quantity falls from 300 to 240 (−20%). PED = −0.8, which suggests revenue should rise. But revenue is 20 × 300 = 6,000 before and 25 × 240 = 6,000 after. It is unchanged. When the price change is large, work out price × quantity directly rather than relying on the rule.

## Price elasticity and total revenue

| Demand is... | If price goes up | If price comes down |
|---|---|---|
| Elastic (size above 1) | Takings drop | Takings grow |
| Inelastic (size below 1) | Takings grow | Takings drop |
| Unit elastic (size equal to 1) | Takings unchanged | Takings unchanged |

On a straight-line demand curve, demand is elastic towards the top left, inelastic towards the bottom right, and unit elastic at the midpoint, where revenue is at its maximum. PED therefore varies along a straight-line curve even though the slope stays constant.

## Determinants of the elasticities

| Measure | Higher size when... | Lower size when... |
|---|---|---|
| PED | Many close substitutes; luxury; large share of income; long time period; narrowly defined good | Few substitutes; necessity; small share of income; short period; broadly defined good; habit |
| YED | The good is a luxury or an experience that people buy more of as they grow richer | The good is a basic item, or is replaced as incomes rise (negative) |
| XED | Goods are close substitutes or tightly linked complements | Goods are only loosely related |

## Must-know distinctions

- Sliding along one curve (own price) versus the curve itself moving (everything else).
- Price elastic (size above 1) versus income elastic (YED above 1). They are different measures.
- Normal good (positive YED) versus inferior good (negative YED). Inferior does not mean poor quality.
- Substitute (positive XED) versus complement (negative XED).
- Elasticity (proportional response) versus slope (absolute response).

## Chains of reasoning to practise

- A rise in the price of a substitute shifts demand right, because buyers switch, so quantity at every price is higher.
- A firm with inelastic demand that raises its price sees quantity fall by a smaller percentage, so revenue rises. Whether profit rises depends on costs.
- Rising incomes shift demand right for normal goods and left for inferior goods. The size of the shift depends on YED.
- Over time, PED tends to become more elastic, because consumers find alternatives, so a revenue gain from a price rise may fade.

## Quick self-test

1. Distinguish a contraction in quantity demanded from a decrease in demand.
2. A price rises from 8 to 10 and quantity falls from 500 to 420. Calculate PED.
3. For question 2, what happens to revenue, and does it match the PED?
4. Income rises from 3,000 to 3,150 and demand for a product rises from 60 to 69. Calculate YED and classify the good.
5. Income goes up from 1,000 to 1,100 and demand falls from 50 to 45. Calculate YED and classify the good.
6. A 5% rise in the price of good Y lowers demand for good X by 3.5%. Calculate XED and state the relationship.
7. PED is −1.8. The firm cuts price by 5%. Find the percentage change in quantity and the revenue index.
8. PED is −0.5. A price rises from 40 to 46 with 1,000 units sold before. Find the new quantity and the revenue change.
9. State two factors that make demand for a product more price elastic.
10. Why is a straight-line demand curve not equally elastic along its length?

### Answers

1. A contraction is a movement along the curve caused by a rise in the good's own price. A decrease in demand is a leftward shift caused by another influence.
2. % change in price = 2 ÷ 8 × 100 = +25%. % change in quantity = −80 ÷ 500 × 100 = −16%. PED = −16 ÷ 25 = −0.64, so inelastic.
3. Revenue rises from 4,000 to 4,200 (+5%), which matches inelastic demand.
4. % change in income = +5%; % change in quantity = +15%. YED = +3.0. A normal, income elastic good.
5. % change in income = +10%; % change in quantity = −10%. YED = −1.0. An inferior good.
6. XED = −3.5 ÷ 5 = −0.7. The goods are complements.
7. Quantity changes by 1.8 × 5 = +9%. Revenue index = 0.95 × 1.09 = 1.0355, so revenue rises about 3.6%.
8. Price change = +15%, so quantity falls 7.5% to 925. Revenue goes from 40,000 to 42,550, a rise of about 6.4%.
9. Any two of: more or closer substitutes, a luxury rather than a necessity, a large proportion of income, a longer time period, a narrower definition of the good.
10. The percentage changes differ at each point. A given price change is a small percentage at a high price and a large percentage at a low price, and the opposite holds for quantity.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating elasticity from absolute changes, not percentage changes.
- Dropping the sign on YED and XED, and so missing the classification.
- Saying a price change shifts the demand curve.
- Calling demand "elastic" when the size is between 0 and 1.
- Stating the revenue rule without saying whether price rose or fell.
- Giving a definition without applying it to the numbers or context in the question.
- Leaving an evaluation point undeveloped, for example "it depends on costs" with no reason.
- Treating a substitute as a complement, or the reverse, when reading a sign.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between a contraction in quantity demanded and a decrease in demand?**
A contraction is a movement along the demand curve caused by a rise in the good's own price. A decrease in demand is a leftward shift of the whole curve caused by another influence, such as a fall in income for a normal good.

**What does a negative income elasticity of demand mean?**
A negative YED means the good is an inferior good: demand falls as income rises. Inferior does not mean poor quality; it describes how demand responds to income.

**What is the difference between being price elastic and income elastic?**
They are different measures. Price elastic means PED is above 1 in size, so quantity responds more than proportionally to the good's own price; income elastic means YED is above 1, so demand rises more than proportionally to income.

**What makes demand for a product more price elastic?**
Demand is more price elastic when there are many close substitutes, the good is a luxury, it takes a large share of income, the time period is long, or the good is narrowly defined, such as a single brand.

**Which OxfordAQA 9640 unit examines demand and elasticity?**
