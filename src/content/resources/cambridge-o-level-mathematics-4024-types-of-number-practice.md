## Section B

**8.** **(a)** Express 2205 and 1050 as products of prime factors. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the highest common factor of 2205 and 1050. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the lowest common multiple of 2205 and 1050. **[2]**

**9.** The number 588 is multiplied by the smallest positive integer k so that the result is a perfect square.

**(a)** Find k. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the square root of the result. **[1]**

**10.** Find the smallest positive integer n such that 252n is a perfect cube. **[3]**

**11.** Two lights flash at regular intervals. One flashes every 12 seconds and the other every 20 seconds. They flash together at the start.

**(a)** After how many seconds do they next flash together? **[2]**

**(b)** How many times do they flash together in the first 5 minutes after the start, counting a flash at exactly 5 minutes? **[2]**

**12.** A shop has 84 pencils and 126 erasers. They are shared into identical packs with nothing left over. Each pack has pencils and erasers. The shop makes as many packs as possible.

**(a)** How many packs are made? **[2]**

**(b)** How many pencils and how many erasers are in each pack? **[2]**

**13.** Find the smallest positive integer that is divisible by 6, 8 and 15. **[3]**

**14.** The highest common factor of two positive integers is 6. Their lowest common multiple is 180. One of the integers is 36.

**(a)** Find the other integer. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that your answer is correct by finding the HCF and LCM of the two integers. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** forty thousand, three hundred and five **[1]**

**(b)** 3 012 000 **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* The groups are three million, then zero hundreds of thousands, then twelve thousand. A zero is needed to hold the hundred-thousands place.

**2.** 97 **[1]** and 101 **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 91 = 7 × 13, 93 = 3 × 31, 99 = 9 × 11 and 105 = 5 × 21. Test 7 as well as 2, 3 and 5.

**3.** 540 ÷ 2 = 270, 270 ÷ 2 = 135, 135 ÷ 3 = 45, 45 ÷ 3 = 15, 15 ÷ 3 = 5 **[1]**; prime factors 2, 2, 3, 3, 3, 5 **[1]**; answer **2² × 3³ × 5** **[1]** **[3]**

**4.** √18 **[1]** and π/3 **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* √25 = 5, 7/9 is a fraction and 0.8 = 4/5, so these are rational. 18 is not a square number, so its root is irrational.

**5. (a)** **9/7** **[1]**

**(b)** 0.4 = 2/5, so the reciprocal is **5/2** (or 2.5) **[1]**

**(c)** -1 1/4 = -5/4, so the reciprocal is **-4/5** **[1]** **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The sign stays negative. Change the mixed number to an improper fraction before turning it over.

**6.** 6³ = 216 **[1]** and 7³ = 343 **[1]** **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 5³ = 125 is too small and 8³ = 512 is too large.

**7. (a)** **2** **[1]**

**(b)** Any pair that sums to a rational number, for example √2 + (-√2) = **0** **[2]**: a correct pair **[1]**, and the sum shown to be rational **[1]** **[3]**

**8. (a)** 2205 = **3² × 5 × 7²** **[1]**; 1050 = **2 × 3 × 5² × 7** **[1]**

**(b)** Common primes with the smaller index: 3, 5, 7 **[1]**; HCF = 3 × 5 × 7 = **105** **[1]**

**(c)** All primes with the larger index: 2 × 3² × 5² × 7² **[1]**; LCM = **22 050** **[1]** **[6]**

*Check:* 105 × 22 050 = 2 315 250 = 2205 × 1050.

**9. (a)** 588 = 2² × 3 × 7² **[1]**; the index of 3 is odd, so **k = 3** **[1]**

**(b)** 588 × 3 = 1764 = (2 × 3 × 7)², so the square root is **42** **[1]** **[3]**

**10.** 252 = 2² × 3² × 7 **[1]**; each index must reach a multiple of 3, so multiply by 2 × 3 × 7² **[1]**; **n = 294** **[1]** **[3]**

*Check:* 252 × 294 = 74 088 = 42³.

**11. (a)** LCM of 12 and 20 **[1]** = **60 seconds** **[1]**

**(b)** 5 minutes = 300 seconds, and 300 ÷ 60 = 5 **[1]**; **5 times** **[1]** **[4]**

**12. (a)** 84 = 2² × 3 × 7 and 126 = 2 × 3² × 7, so HCF = 2 × 3 × 7 **[1]** = **42 packs** **[1]**

**(b)** 84 ÷ 42 = **2 pencils** **[1]** and 126 ÷ 42 = **3 erasers** **[1]** **[4]**

**13.** 6 = 2 × 3, 8 = 2³, 15 = 3 × 5 **[1]**; LCM uses 2³, 3 and 5 **[1]**; **120** **[1]** **[3]**

**14. (a)** HCF × LCM = product of the numbers, so 6 × 180 = 1080 **[1]**; 1080 ÷ 36 = **30** **[1]**

**(b)** 36 = 2² × 3² and 30 = 2 × 3 × 5; HCF = 2 × 3 = 6 **[1]**; LCM = 2² × 3² × 5 = 180 **[1]** **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the zero that holds a place in Question 1(b).
- Treating 91 as prime in Question 2 because it is not divisible by 2, 3 or 5.
- Writing the factor tree but not the final product in index form in Question 3.
- Treating any square root as irrational in Question 4.
- Dropping the negative sign in Question 5(c).
- Taking the smaller index for the LCM in Question 8(c).
- Giving k = 14 or another multiple, rather than the smallest value, in Questions 9 and 10.
- Counting the flash at the start as one of the five in Question 11(b).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is 1 a prime number?**
No. A prime has exactly two different factors, 1 and itself. The number 1 has only one factor.

**Is zero a natural number?**
Books differ, and the syllabus wording does not say. If a question depends on it, the question will normally make the intention clear. Use the word integer when you need to include zero.

**How can I tell the HCF from the LCM in a word problem?**
If things repeat and you want the first time they match, use the LCM. If you are splitting or cutting into equal parts, use the HCF.

**Can a decimal be irrational?**
Only a decimal that never ends and never repeats. Every terminating or recurring decimal is rational.
