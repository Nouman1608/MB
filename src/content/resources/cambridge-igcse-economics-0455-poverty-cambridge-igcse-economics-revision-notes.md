These notes condense section 5.2 of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: 5.2.1 absolute and relative poverty, 5.2.2 causes of poverty and 5.2.3 policies to alleviate poverty and redistribute income. All figures are invented. Full explanations and a model answer are in the [poverty study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-poverty-cambridge-igcse-economics/); test yourself with the [poverty practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-poverty-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/). ## 5.2.1 Core recall

- **Absolute poverty:** income too low to buy the basic necessities of life (food, clean water, shelter, clothing).
- **Relative poverty:** income well below that of most people in the same society, often measured as below a percentage (such as 50% or 60%) of median income.
- The absolute line is fixed in real terms. The relative line rises when median income rises.
- Growth can cut absolute poverty while relative poverty stays the same or rises.

### Methods

**Absolute line for a household:** cost of basics per person × household size.

```
Basics cost 1,800 per person; household of 4 earns 6,500
Needs = 4 x 1,800 = 7,200 -> 700 short -> absolute poverty
```

**Relative line:** find the median (middle value of ranked incomes; average the middle two if the number is even), then take the stated percentage.

```
Incomes: 2,000  5,000  7,000  9,000  12,000  20,000
Median = (7,000 + 9,000) / 2 = 8,000
Line at 60% = 4,800 -> one household of six is below -> 16.7%
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Absolute poverty | Unable to afford the basic necessities of life |
| Relative poverty | Income well below the typical income in that society |
| Median income | The middle income when all incomes are ranked |
| Redistribution of income | Moving income from richer to poorer people, usually through taxes and benefits |
| Progressive tax | A tax that takes a higher proportion of income as income rises |
| State benefits | Payments by the government to people such as the unemployed, sick, elderly or low-paid |
| National minimum wage | The lowest hourly pay employers may legally pay |
| Dependants | People, such as children or elderly relatives, who rely on another's income |

## 5.2.2 Causes as chains

- **Unemployment:** no wage → income falls to savings or benefits → long spells erode skills → harder to find work.
- **Low wages:** low skills or weak bargaining power → low pay or part-time work → income below needs, worse with dependants.
- **Illness:** less or no work plus treatment costs → income falls as spending rises.
- **Age:** children cannot earn; elderly people may have small or no pensions.
- Causes reinforce each other: illness → job loss → lower future wages.

## 5.2.3 Policy grid

| Policy | Mechanism | Helps most | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Economic growth | More jobs, higher incomes, more tax revenue | Unemployed | Gains may go to the better-off |
| Improved education | Higher skills → higher productivity → higher wages | Low-paid, future workers | Slow and costly |
| More generous state benefits | Direct income support | Sick, elderly, unemployed, families with children | Cost; may reduce work incentive |
| Progressive taxation | Takes a higher share from high earners; funds spending | Narrows inequality | Raises no one's income unless the revenue is spent on the poor |
| National minimum wage | Raises the lowest pay | Low-paid workers in jobs | No help to those out of work; may cut jobs if set too high |

**Speed:** benefits work fastest, education slowest.
**Targeting:** benefits and a minimum wage target particular groups; growth is untargeted.
**Cost to government:** benefits and education cost money; a minimum wage costs the government little directly.

## Evaluation phrases that score

- "This helps those in work but not the elderly or sick, so..."
- "The effect depends on how far above the market wage the minimum is set."
- "Progressive tax narrows the gap but lifts no one out of poverty unless the revenue funds benefits or services."
- "Education tackles the cause but takes years to raise incomes."

## Exam traps

- Defining relative poverty as "less serious" than absolute poverty.
- Saying progressive tax means the rich pay "more tax". They pay a higher proportion.
- Using the mean instead of the median for a relative poverty line.
- Forgetting household size when judging absolute poverty.
- Listing policies with no limitation or no link to a cause.
- Claiming growth always reduces relative poverty.

## Quick self-test

1. Define absolute poverty.
2. Define relative poverty.
3. A basic basket costs 2,500 per person. A household of 3 earns 7,000. Is it in absolute poverty?
4. Find the median of 4,000; 6,000; 10,000; 15,000; 30,000.
5. Using your answer to question 4, find a relative poverty line at 50% of the median.
6. Explain why relative poverty can rise when everyone's income rises.
7. Give two ways illness can cause poverty.
8. Why are elderly people at risk of poverty?
9. Which policy works fastest to raise the income of a sick person who cannot work?
10. Why does progressive taxation alone not raise the incomes of the poor?
11. Give one reason a national minimum wage might increase poverty.
12. Why is improved education described as tackling a cause of poverty?

### Answers

1. Not having enough income to buy the basic necessities of life, such as food, water, shelter and clothing.
2. Having income well below that of most others in the same society.
3. Needs = 3 × 2,500 = 7,500, so 7,000 is 500 short: yes.
4. 10,000 (the middle of five ranked values).
5. 50% × 10,000 = 5,000.
6. If higher incomes rise faster, the median and the line rise more than the incomes of the poorest.
7. It reduces hours worked or stops work, and treatment costs raise spending.
8. They may have no earnings and only a small pension or savings.
9. More generous state benefits.
10. It takes more from the rich but gives nothing to the poor unless the revenue is spent on benefits or services for them.
11. If set well above the market wage, firms may employ fewer workers, so some lose their jobs.
12. It raises skills and productivity, so people can earn higher wages and are less likely to be unemployed.
