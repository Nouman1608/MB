## Section A

**1.** Identify which credited area each feature most clearly shows, and explain briefly: (a) a manager addressing staff by first name while being addressed as "Mrs Ahmed"; (b) friends calling each other "legend" after a failed exam; (c) a forum post signed off "keep the rubber side down" by a motorcycling club member. **[6]**

**2.** Explain the difference between positive face and negative face, with an example of a politeness strategy for each. **[4]**

**3.** Explain what is meant by convergence and divergence in communication accommodation theory. **[4]**

**4.** Explain why banter between close friends should not be analysed as hostility. **[3]**

## Section B

**Text A** (transcript, original, invented). A job interview for a part-time café role. INT is the manager; CAND is the candidate, aged 17.

```
INT:   so (.) tell me why you want to work here
CAND:  um (.) well I've always really liked this café (.) I come here with my friends
       quite a lot actually (.) and I think I'd be good with customers
INT:   okay (.) have you got any experience
CAND:  not in a café exactly (.) but I volunteer at the library on Saturdays so
INT:   // right (.) and you can do weekends
CAND:  yes (.) definitely (.) any time really
INT:   good (.) we'll be in touch
```

**5.** Analyse how the manager's language asserts power and status in Text A. **[6]**

**6.** Analyse how the candidate uses language to present her identity and manage the relationship. **[6]**

**Text B** (written, original). A post on a running club's social media page.

> *RUN CLUB FAM! Huge shout-out to everyone who braved the monsoon on Sunday. Special mention to Dev, who has now
> officially lost more water bottles than he has run miles. Same time next week, rain or shine. Who's in? #SoggySocks*

**7.** Analyse how Text B creates group rapport and shared perspectives. **[6]**

**8.** Analyse the creative play and performance in Text B. **[5]**

**9.** Compare the ways Text A and Text B construct relationships between participants. **[8]**

**10.** Explain how the power relationship in Text A might be described using Wareing's distinction between instrumental and influential power. **[4]**

**11.** Identify **two** observations from your analysis of Texts A and B that could support a Section B essay on the claim that "language reflects the groups we belong to", and explain how each would work as evidence. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** (a) **Assertion of power and status** [1]: **asymmetrical address** marks the manager's higher position [1]. (b) **Creative play and performance**, also rapport [1]: "legend" is used **ironically** after failure, a playful inversion that consoles [1]. (c) **Group rapport / group membership** [1]: a **shared in-group catchphrase** of motorcyclists signals membership and shared perspective [1].

**2.** **Positive face** is the wish to be liked and approved of [1]; it is attended to by **compliments, inclusive language or expressions of interest** ("Great idea, we should all try it") [1]. **Negative face** is the wish not to be imposed on [1]; it is attended to by **hedges, apologies and indirect requests** ("Sorry to bother you, but could you possibly...") [1].

**3.** **Convergence** is a speaker adjusting their speech towards that of the listener [1], often to gain approval or build rapport [1]. **Divergence** is adjusting speech away from the listener's [1], to emphasise difference or group identity [1].

**4.** Between close friends, **mock impoliteness signals solidarity**: only people secure in their relationship can risk apparent insults [1]. Context, laughter and reciprocity show the **intended meaning differs from the literal one** [1]. Reading it as hostility misses its function of **building rapport and identity** [1].

**5.** The manager **controls the topic and the turns**: every question is hers, and the candidate only answers [1] [1]. She **interrupts** the candidate's explanation ("// right") and redirects to her own priority, weekend availability [1]. Her **minimal evaluative feedback** ("okay", "good") withholds approval, keeping the candidate uncertain [1]. She **closes the interaction** unilaterally with "we'll be in touch", a formulaic close only the more powerful participant can make [1]. Her **imperative "tell me"** sets the agenda from the start [1].

**6.** The candidate **presents herself as a loyal customer** ("I come here with my friends quite a lot"), building a connection with the business [1] [1]. **Hedges and fillers** ("um", "well", "I think") show deference to the interviewer's authority, attending to negative face, but may undercut confidence [1] [1]. She **reframes limited experience positively** ("not in a café exactly (.) but I volunteer") to present herself as reliable [1], and her **emphatic agreement** ("yes (.) definitely (.) any time really") signals eagerness and accommodation to the manager's needs [1].

**7.** The **collective address "RUN CLUB FAM"** uses in-group slang ("fam") to construct the club as a family [1] [1]. **Shared experience is presupposed** ("braved the monsoon on Sunday"), positioning readers as fellow participants [1]. The **question "Who's in?"** invites participation and agreement [1]. The **hashtag #SoggySocks** creates a shared label for the experience [1]; **"rain or shine"** expresses a shared value of commitment [1].

**8.** **Hyperbole**: "the monsoon" exaggerates ordinary rain for comic effect [1]. **Teasing a named member** ("lost more water bottles than he has run miles") is affectionate mock criticism performed publicly [1] [1]. The **comparative structure** gives the joke a punchline [1]. The **alliterative hashtag #SoggySocks** is playful wordplay [1].

**9.** Text A constructs an **unequal, institutional relationship**: asymmetrical turns, interruption and formulaic closing [1] [1]. Text B constructs an **equal, affectionate group relationship**: inclusive address and in-group slang [1] [1]. Both involve **evaluation of individuals**: the manager's withheld judgement versus Text B's public teasing [1] [1]. Mode matters: A is **spontaneous speech** with hesitation showing pressure; B is **written but speech-like, multimodal social media**, designed to perform belonging publicly [1] [1].

**10.** The manager holds **instrumental power**: authority from her role allows her to set the agenda and decide the outcome [1] [1]. The candidate can only use **influential power**, trying to persuade through self-presentation [1], which explains her positive framing and eagerness [1].

**11.** Observation 1: Text B's in-group lexis ("fam", the hashtag) shows **language marking group membership**, supporting the claim [1] [1]. Observation 2: the candidate in Text A **adapts her language to an unfamiliar institutional group**, showing language can reflect the group a speaker wants to join, not only belongs to [1], which allows the essay to qualify the claim [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Labelling a credited area without quoting the features that create it.
- Missing that the interviewer's minimal feedback is itself a power move.
- Reading teasing in Text B as unkindness.
- Comparing the texts' topics rather than their relationships.
- Applying frameworks without linking them to a specific moment.
