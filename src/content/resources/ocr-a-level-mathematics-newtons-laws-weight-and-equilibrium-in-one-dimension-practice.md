## Questions

**1.** A parcel of mass 6.5 kg rests on a horizontal shelf.

**(a)** Find its weight. **[1]**
**(b)** State the normal reaction on the parcel and the Newton's third law partner of that reaction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the weight would be different on another planet while the mass is not. **[1]**

**2.** A trolley of mass 15 kg is on a smooth horizontal track. A force of 80 N pushes it forwards and a force of 35 N acts backwards. It starts from rest. Find its acceleration and its speed after 6 s. **[3]**

**3.** A stone of mass 0.5 kg is dropped from rest. Air resistance is a constant 0.9 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the stone. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its speed after falling 5 m. **[2]**

**4.** A passenger of mass 64 kg stands on the floor of a lift.

**(a)** Find the force from the floor when the lift accelerates upwards at 0.9 m s⁻². **[3]**
**(b)** Find the force from the floor when the lift accelerates downwards at 2.1 m s⁻². **[2]**

**5.** A lift of mass 250 kg carries a passenger of mass 80 kg. The lift cable has tension T and the lift accelerates upwards at 0.5 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find T. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the force the passenger exerts on the floor of the lift. **[2]**
**(c)** State why the lift and passenger can be treated as one particle in part (a). **[1]**

**6.** Only two forces, (12i − 9j) N and (−2i − 15j) N, act on a particle of mass 2 kg.

**(a)** Find the acceleration as a vector. **[2]**
**(b)** Find its magnitude and the angle it makes with i. **[2]**

**7.** A 3 kg particle is acted on by (2i + 5j) N and by a second force F N. Its acceleration is (4i − j) m s⁻².

**(a)** Find F in vector form. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the magnitude of F. **[1]**

**8.** (A Level only) A body of mass 6 kg is pulled along a smooth horizontal surface by a rope at 40° above the horizontal with tension 45 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the normal reaction. **[3]**
**(c)** The tension is increased, the angle stays at 40°. Find the tension at which contact is lost. **[2]**

**9.** (A Level only) A body of mass 4 kg slides down a line of greatest slope of a smooth plane inclined at 24° to the horizontal.

**(a)** Find its acceleration. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the normal reaction. **[2]**

**10.** A lift of total mass 600 kg moves between two floors. It accelerates from rest at 1.5 m s⁻² for 2 s, travels at constant speed for 6 s, then decelerates uniformly at 1 m s⁻² to rest. The cable is light and inextensible.

**(a)** Find the tension during the first 2 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the tension during the constant-speed stage. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the time taken to decelerate, and the tension during that stage. **[3]**
**(d)** Find the total distance travelled. **[4]**

**11.** A particle of mass 1.8 kg is raised vertically on a light inextensible string. The string breaks if the tension exceeds 36 N.

**(a)** Find the greatest upward acceleration the string allows. **[3]**
**(b)** The particle moves upwards at 6 m s⁻¹ when the string is cut. Find the greatest height it reaches above that point, and the time for it to return to that point. **[5]**
**(c)** State one way in which the string model is unrealistic. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** W = 6.5 × 9.8 = **63.7 N** [1]
**(b)** At rest, R = W, so R = **63.7 N** upwards [1]. The partner is the force of the parcel on the shelf, **63.7 N** downwards [1].
**(c)** W = mg and g depends on location, but the mass does not change [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b) name the body each force acts on. "Weight" alone is not the third-law partner of R, because both act on the parcel.

**2.** Resultant = 80 − 35 = 45 N forwards [1]. 45 = 15a, so **a = 3 m s⁻²** [1]. v = 0 + 3 × 6 = **18 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Writing the resultant as a single signed expression makes your method easy to follow and easy to check.

**3. (a)** Downwards positive: 0.5 × 9.8 − 0.9 = 0.5a [1]; 4.9 − 0.9 = 4, so **a = 8 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** v² = 0 + 2 × 8 × 5 = 80 [1]; **v = 8.94 m s⁻¹** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (b) needs the acceleration from (a), not g. Using 9.8 here changes the answer.

**4. (a)** Upwards positive: R − 64 × 9.8 = 64 × 0.9 [1]; R = 627.2 + 57.6 [1]; **R = 685 N** [1].
**(b)** Downwards positive: 64 × 9.8 − R = 64 × 2.1 [1]; R = 627.2 − 134.4 = **493 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* State your positive direction. The two parts have opposite sign patterns, and a clear convention shows why.

**5. (a)** Total mass 330 kg [1]. T − 330 × 9.8 = 330 × 0.5 [1]; **T = 3399 N** [1].
**(b)** Passenger: R − 80 × 9.8 = 80 × 0.5 [1]; R = 824 N, so by the third law the passenger pushes the floor with **824 N** downwards [1].
**(c)** There is no relative motion between them, so they share the same acceleration [1].
*Examiner insight:* Use the third law explicitly in (b). The force on the passenger and the force on the floor are equal but act on different bodies.

**6. (a)** Resultant = (10i − 24j) N [1]; a = (10i − 24j) ÷ 2 = **(5i − 12j) m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** |a| = √(25 + 144) = **13 m s⁻²** [1]; tan⁻¹(12 ÷ 5) gives **67.4° below i** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Divide the resultant, not one force, by the mass. Say which side of i the acceleration points; a bare 67.4° does not say.

**7. (a)** ma = 3(4i − j) = 12i − 3j [1]; F = ma − (2i + 5j) [1]; **F = (10i − 8j) N** [1].
**(b)** |F| = √(100 + 64) = √164 = **12.8 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Subtract the known force from the resultant, not from the acceleration. Check by adding F back.

**8. (a)** 45 cos 40° = 6a [1]; **a = 5.75 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** R + 45 sin 40° = 6 × 9.8 [1]; 45 sin 40° = 28.93 [1]; **R = 29.9 N** [1].
**(c)** Contact is lost when R = 0 [1]; T sin 40° = 58.8, so **T = 91.5 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Resolve the pull both ways. A common slip is to take R = 58.8, which ignores the lifting effect of the rope.

**9. (a)** Along the slope: 4a = 4 × 9.8 × sin 24° [1]; mass cancels [1]; **a = 3.99 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** Perpendicular to the slope there is no acceleration: R = 4 × 9.8 × cos 24° [1]; **R = 35.8 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show which component of the weight you use. Swapping sin and cos is the usual error.

**10. (a)** T − 600 × 9.8 = 600 × 1.5 [1]; **T = 6780 N** [1].
**(b)** Constant speed gives zero resultant, so **T = 5880 N** [1].
**(c)** Speed reached = 1.5 × 2 = 3 m s⁻¹, so time = 3 ÷ 1 = **3 s** [1]; 5880 − T = 600 × 1 [1]; **T = 5280 N** [1].
**(d)** First stage: ½ × 1.5 × 2² = 3 m [1]. Middle: 3 × 6 = 18 m [1]. Last: 3² ÷ (2 × 1) = 4.5 m [1]. Total **25.5 m** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Multi-stage questions reward a clear table of stages. The tension in stage three is below the weight because the acceleration is downward.

**11. (a)** T − 1.8 × 9.8 = 1.8a [1]; 36 − 17.64 = 18.36 [1]; **a = 10.2 m s⁻²** [1].
**(b)** After cutting, only weight acts, so a = 9.8 m s⁻² downwards [1]. 0 = 6² − 2 × 9.8 × h [1]; **h = 1.84 m** [1]. Time up = 6 ÷ 9.8, so total time = 2 × 6 ÷ 9.8 [1]; **1.22 s** [1].
**(c)** A real string has mass and stretches (or can only be approximately light and inextensible) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Cutting the string removes the tension but not the velocity. Use 6 m s⁻¹ as the starting speed with acceleration downwards.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving the weight out of a vertical equation of motion.
- Using R = mg for a body in an accelerating lift.
- Dividing a single force, not the resultant, by the mass.
- Taking the wrong sign for acceleration when the positive direction is not stated.
- Treating weight and R as a third-law pair.
- Swapping sin and cos when resolving along or across a slope.
- Rounding g to 10 or rounding early, so the final digits are wrong.
- Leaving out the model statement when asked, such as light string or no relative motion.
