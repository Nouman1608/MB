These questions cover section **3.7 Vectors** of Pure Mathematics 3 (Paper 3) in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Mathematics 9709 syllabus for 2026 and 2027. The origin is O. Give angles in degrees to 1 decimal place. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question.

Related: the [3.7 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-vectors-cambridge-alevel-maths/) and the [3.7 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-vectors-cambridge-alevel-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** The vector a is 2i − j + 2k. Find |a| and the unit vector in the direction of a. **[2]**

**2.** The points A and B have coordinates (3, −1, 2) and (5, 3, −2). Find |AB| and the position vector of the midpoint M of AB. **[3]**

**3.** OABC is a parallelogram. The points A and C have position vectors 2i + j + 3k and −i + 4j + k.

**(a)** Find the position vector of B.

**(b)** Find angle AOC. **[4]**

**4.** The line l passes through A(1, 0, −2) and is parallel to 3i + j − k. Show that the point B(7, 2, −4) lies on l. **[2]**

**5.** Find the value of p for which the vectors 2i + pj − k and pi + 3j + 4k are perpendicular. **[2]**

**6.** The lines l₁ and l₂ have equations r = (i + 2j) + s(2i − 4j + 6k) and r = (j + k) + t(−i + 2j − 3k). Show that l₁ and l₂ are parallel and distinct. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** The lines l₁ and l₂ have equations r = (i − j + 2k) + s(2i + j − k) and r = (4i − 2j + 3k) + t(i − 2j + 2k).

**(a)** Show that l₁ and l₂ intersect, and find the position vector of the point of intersection.

**(b)** Find the acute angle between l₁ and l₂. **[7]**

**8.** The lines l₁ and l₂ have equations r = (i + j) + s(i + 2k) and r = (2i − j + 3k) + t(j − k).

**(a)** Show that l₁ and l₂ are not parallel.

**(b)** Show that l₁ and l₂ are skew.

**(c)** Find the acute angle between the directions of l₁ and l₂. **[6]**

**9.** The point P has coordinates (5, 5, −1) and the line l has equation r = (i + 2j + 3k) + t(i + 2j − 2k).

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the foot F of the perpendicular from P to l.

**(b)** Find the perpendicular distance from P to l.

**(c)** Find the coordinates of the reflection of P in l. **[7]**

**10.** The tetrahedron OABC has vertices O(0, 0, 0), A(4, 0, 0), B(0, 4, 0) and C(0, 0, 6). The point M is the midpoint of AB.

**(a)** Find angle ACB.

**(b)** Show that CM is perpendicular to AB.

**(c)** Find the exact area of triangle ABC. **[7]**

**11.** A cuboid OABCDEFG has O at the origin, A(6, 0, 0), C(0, 4, 0) and D(0, 0, 3), so that B is (6, 4, 0), F is (6, 4, 3) and G is (0, 4, 3). The point M is the midpoint of FG.

**(a)** Find a vector equation of the line AM.

**(b)** Find the angle between AM and AB. **[5]**

**12.** The lines l₁ and l₂ have equations r = (i + 2j + 3k) + s(2i − j + k) and r = (4i + aj + 2k) + t(i + j − 2k), where a is a constant. The lines intersect.

**(a)** Find the value of a.

**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point of intersection. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** |a| = √(4 + 1 + 4) = **3** [1]. Unit vector **⅓(2i − j + 2k)** [1]

**2.** AB = 2i + 4j − 4k [1]. |AB| = √(4 + 16 + 16) = **6** [1]. OM = ½(OA + OB) = **4i + j** [1]

**3.** **(a)** OB = OA + OC = **i + 5j + 4k** [1]

**(b)** OA.OC = −2 + 4 + 3 = 5 [1]. |OA| = √14, |OC| = √18 [1]. cos AOC = 5/√252, so **angle AOC = 71.6°** [1]

**4.** r = (1 + 3t, t, −2 − t). From the x-component, 1 + 3t = 7 gives t = 2 [1]. Then y = 2 and z = −4, which match B, so B lies on l [1]

**5.** 2p + 3p − 4 = 0 [1]. **p = 4/5** [1]

**6.** 2i − 4j + 6k = −2(−i + 2j − 3k), so the lines are parallel [1]. For (0, 1, 1) to lie on l₁: 1 + 2s = 0 gives s = −½, but then y = 2 + 2 = 4 ≠ 1 [1]. So the point is not on l₁, and the lines are parallel and distinct [1]

**7.** **(a)** x: 1 + 2s = 4 + t; y: −1 + s = −2 − 2t [1]. From y, s = −1 − 2t, so 1 − 2 − 4t = 4 + t and **t = −1, s = 1** [1]. z: l₁ gives 2 − 1 = 1 and l₂ gives 3 − 2 = 1, which agree, so the lines intersect [1]. Point: **3i + k**, that is (3, 0, 1) [1]

**(b)** (2, 1, −1).(1, −2, 2) = 2 − 2 − 2 = −2 [1]. |d₁| = √6, |d₂| = 3, so cos θ = −2/(3√6) and θ = 105.8° [1]. Acute angle **74.2°** [1]

**8.** **(a)** (1, 0, 2) is not a multiple of (0, 1, −1): the x-components are 1 and 0 [1]

**(b)** x: 1 + s = 2, so s = 1 [1]. y: 1 = −1 + t, so t = 2 [1]. z: l₁ gives 2s = 2 but l₂ gives 3 − t = 1. These differ, so the lines do not meet; with (a), they are skew [1]

**(c)** |(1, 0, 2).(0, 1, −1)| = 2; cos θ = 2/(√5 × √2) = 2/√10 [1]. **θ = 50.8°** [1]

**9.** **(a)** F = (1 + t, 2 + 2t, 3 − 2t) [1]. PF = (t − 4, 2t − 3, 4 − 2t) [1]. PF.(1, 2, −2) = t − 4 + 4t − 6 − 8 + 4t = 9t − 18 = 0, so t = 2 [1]. **F = (3, 6, −1)** [1]

**(b)** PF = (−2, 1, 0), so the distance is **√5** [1]

**(c)** P′ = 2F − P [1] = **(1, 7, −1)** [1]

**10.** **(a)** CA = (4, 0, −6) and CB = (0, 4, −6) [1]. CA.CB = 36 and |CA| = |CB| = √52, so cos ACB = 36/52 = 9/13 [1]. **Angle ACB = 46.2°** [1]

**(b)** M = (2, 2, 0), so CM = (2, 2, −6) and AB = (−4, 4, 0) [1]. CM.AB = −8 + 8 + 0 = 0, so CM is perpendicular to AB [1]

**(c)** |AB| = 4√2 and |CM| = √44 = 2√11 [1]. Area = ½ × 4√2 × 2√11 = **4√22** [1]

*Examiner insight:* triangle ABC is isosceles with CA = CB, which is why the median CM is also the perpendicular height.

**11.** **(a)** M = (3, 4, 3), so AM = −3i + 4j + 3k [1]. **r = 6i + t(−3i + 4j + 3k)** [1]

**(b)** AB = 4j [1]. cos θ = 16/(4 × √34) = 4/√34 [1]. **θ = 46.7°** [1]

**12.** **(a)** x: 1 + 2s = 4 + t; z: 3 + s = 2 − 2t [1]. From z, s = −1 − 2t, so 1 − 2 − 4t = 4 + t, giving t = −1 and s = 1 [1]. y: 2 − s = a + t [1], so 1 = a − 1 and **a = 2** [1]

**(b)** s = 1 in l₁: **(3, 1, 4)** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the unknown a appears only in the y-component, so solve the other two components first.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using points instead of direction vectors to find the angle between lines.
- Not checking the third component before claiming lines intersect.
- Proving skew lines without stating that they are not parallel.
- Giving the obtuse angle when the acute angle between lines is asked for.
- Writing a wrong vertex of a cuboid because the coordinates were not listed first.
- Giving a position vector when coordinates were asked for, or the other way round.
