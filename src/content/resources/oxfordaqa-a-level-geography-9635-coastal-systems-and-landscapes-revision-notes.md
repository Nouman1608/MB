Condensed recall for **3.1.3 Section C: Coastal systems and landscapes** (OxfordAQA International AS
and A-level Geography 9635, Unit 1, option 1b). For the full explanation, read the
[Physical Geography 1 study guide](/resources/a-level-oxfordaqa-geography-physical-geography-1/).

## Erosion, transport and waves

- **Erosion:** hydraulic action (water compresses air in cracks), abrasion (rock fragments scour),
  attrition (fragments wear each other down), solution (chemical dissolving of soluble rock).
- **Longshore drift:** waves approach at an angle, swash carries sediment obliquely up the beach,
  backwash returns it straight down under gravity, giving net movement along the coast.

| | Constructive waves | Destructive waves |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Low, long wavelength | High, short wavelength |
| Swash and backwash | Swash stronger | Backwash stronger |
| Effect | Build beaches | Erode beaches |

## Landforms

**Erosional**
- **Headlands and bays:** differential erosion of alternating resistant and less resistant rock; soft
  rock becomes bays, hard rock remains as headlands.
- **Cave → arch → stack → stump:** a line of weakness becomes a cave; the cave is cut through to an
  arch; the roof collapses to leave a stack; the base erodes to a stump.
- **Wave-cut platform:** a gently sloping surface left at the base of a retreating cliff.

**Depositional**
- **Spit:** deposition builds out into open water where the coastline changes direction.
- **Tombolo:** a spit that joins the mainland to an offshore island.

## Sediment cells

A **sediment cell** is a largely closed system of **sources, transfers and sinks** of sediment.
Management in one place affects others. **Groynes** trap sediment moving by longshore drift and
starve the beach further along the drift direction, which becomes more vulnerable to erosion. This
knock-on effect is the key evaluation point.

## Management

| Hard engineering | Soft engineering |
|---|---|
| Sea walls, groynes, rip-rap | Beach nourishment, dune stabilisation, managed retreat |
| Protect land directly; costly; can move the problem along the coast | Work with natural processes; cheaper and more sustainable; less immediate protection |

**Managed retreat** lets the sea reclaim land instead of defending it. It is favoured on cost and
sustainability but is socially contentious, because it means abandoning land and property.

## Case studies

A local-scale coastal environment (processes, landscape outcomes and sustainable management
challenges) and a contrasting coastal landscape (risks and opportunities for human occupation, and
human responses of resilience, mitigation and adaptation). Name places and use only figures you are
sure of.

## Exam traps

- Describing the cave-to-stump sequence without the process behind each step.
- Swapping constructive and destructive wave features.
- Evaluating management without the sediment cell knock-on effect.
- Treating hard engineering as always better or always worse.
- Writing about management in general when a named coast is required.

## Self-test

1. Describe longshore drift.
2. How do headlands and bays form?
3. Distinguish a spit from a tombolo.
4. What is a sediment cell?
5. Why can groynes cause erosion elsewhere?

**Answers:** 1. Waves arrive at an angle, swash moves sediment obliquely up the beach, backwash
returns it straight down, so sediment moves along the coast. 2. Differential erosion of hard and soft
rock. 3. A spit is a ridge built out from the coast; a tombolo is a spit that links the mainland to an
island. 4. A largely closed system of sediment sources, transfers and sinks. 5. They trap sediment
and starve beaches further along the drift direction.
