## Section A - short questions

**1.** Egg producers across a country pay together for adverts saying "Eggs are good for you". This is: **[1]**
A competitive advertising   B collective advertising   C informative advertising   D sales promotion

**2.** Which is a digital advertising medium? **[1]**
A window display   B signboard   C email marketing   D catalogue

**3.** A shop sells one product at less than cost to attract customers. This is a: **[1]**
A free sample   B coupon   C loss leader   D competition

**4.** State **two** purposes of advertising. **[2]**

**5.** Define a method of appeal in advertising. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** methods of sales promotion. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *A bank advert shows a family losing their home after a fire because they had no insurance cover.*
(a) Identify the method of appeal used. **[1]**
(b) Explain **one** reason why this appeal suits the product. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** risk of using this appeal. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** advantages of using social media influencers to advertise a new sports drink. **[4]**

**9.** Describe the difference between informative and persuasive advertising. Give an example of each. **[4]**

**10.** *A poster campaign costs $600 and is seen by 50,000 people. A magazine advert costs $1,800 and is seen by 90,000 people.*
(a) Calculate the cost per 1,000 people reached for each medium. **[2]**
(b) State which is cheaper per person and by how much. **[1]**
(c) Explain **two** other factors, apart from cost, that the business should consider before choosing. **[4]**

**11.** *A shop plans to sell a jacket usually priced at $60 with a 20% discount in an end-of-season sale. The jacket costs the shop $40.*
(a) Calculate the sale price. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the profit per jacket at the sale price. **[1]**
(c) Explain **two** reasons why the shop might hold the sale despite the lower profit. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether a small local bakery should use television advertising. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. A group of producers promotes a whole product type. **[1]**

**2.** C. Email marketing is one of the digital media in the syllabus. **[1]**

**3.** C. A loss leader is sold at or below cost to attract buyers. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: to inform customers [1]; to persuade customers to buy [1]; to remind customers of a product [1]; to increase sales [1]; to build the brand image [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** The emotional or logical reason [1] that an advert gives for buying the product, such as fear, humour or a rational argument [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: point of sale [1]; special offers [1]; sponsorship [1]; displays [1]; competitions [1]; coupons [1]; sales periods [1]; loss leaders [1]; free gifts [1]; free samples [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Fear [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any one, 2 marks: insurance is bought to protect against loss [1], so showing the harm of having no cover makes the need clear [1]; or customers delay buying insurance, so a strong message pushes them to act [2]. (2 marks)
(c) Any one, 2 marks: the advert may upset or frighten viewers [1], who may then dislike the brand or switch off [1]; or the message may seem to exaggerate the danger [1], so people may not trust it [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Any two, 2 marks each: followers trust the influencer, so the endorsement is more believable than an advert [2]; influencers reach a specific young, sporty audience that matches the drink's buyers [2]; posting costs less than television, so a new brand with a small budget can reach many people [2]; the influencer can show the drink being used in real life, which makes the product feel attractive [2]. **[max 4]**

**9.** Informative advertising gives facts such as features, price or where to buy [1]; example: a notice of new opening times or a new bus timetable [1]. Persuasive advertising tries to change opinions and make people want the product, using attractive images or emotional appeals [1]; example: a sports star wearing a brand of trainers [1]. **[4]**

**10.** (a) Poster: 600 / 50,000 x 1,000 = **$12** [1]. Magazine: 1,800 / 90,000 x 1,000 = **$20** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Posters are cheaper per 1,000 people by 20 - 12 = **$8** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) Any two, 2 marks each: audience, because the magazine may reach readers who match the target customers better [2]; cost of the campaign in total, because the poster campaign is cheaper overall [2]; timing, because a monthly magazine has a fixed publication date [2]; type of product, because a product needing detailed explanation suits a magazine more than a poster [2]; flexibility, because posters are harder to change once they are printed [2]. (max 4 marks) **[7]**

**11.** (a) 60 x 0.80 = **$48** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) 48 - 40 = **$8** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) Any two, 2 marks each: to clear last season's stock before new stock arrives [2]; to attract new customers who may buy other items at full price [2]; to increase sales and cash flow, because extra jackets sold can make up for lower profit on each [2]; to compete with other shops that are also holding sales [2]. (max 4 marks) **[6]**

**12.** Up to 2 for reasons for, up to 2 for reasons against, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
For: television reaches a large audience [1]; sight and sound can make the bread look appealing [1].
Against: very costly compared with the bakery's size [1]; most viewers live too far away to visit a local bakery, so most of the cost would be wasted [1]; cheaper options such as local radio, flyers, window displays and social media reach the local area [1].
Conclusion: for example, television is not appropriate for a small local business, which should use local media, because the cost is high and the reach is mostly outside its area [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing advertising with sales promotion.
- Writing "advertising informs people" without naming the type.
- Giving a general benefit of a medium with no link to the product or audience in the question.
- Writing "emotion" for every appeal instead of naming fear, humour or the right one.
- In percentage questions, subtracting the discount percentage from the price (60 - 20) instead of finding 80% of the price.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four types of advertising in 0715?** Informative, persuasive, collective (generic) and competitive.

**Is a free sample advertising?** No. It is a method of sales promotion.
