## The industrial system

- **Inputs**: raw materials or components, energy, labour, capital,
  land, research.
- **Processes**: refining, smelting, assembling, testing, packaging.
- **Outputs**: products, by-products, **waste** (gases, heat, waste
  water, scrap), profit.
- **Feedback**: profit reinvested.
- Waste links 3.3 to 3.7 (pollution).

## Types of industry

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Processing | Raw material made more useful | Oil refining, sugar refining, smelting, flour milling |
| Manufacturing | Making goods from materials | Steel products, textiles, furniture |
| Assembly | Components put together | Cars, televisions, mobile phones |
| High technology | Research-based advanced products | Microchips, software, pharmaceuticals, aerospace |

## Seven location factors: what to say

| Factor | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Land | Flat, cheap, room to expand; greenfield v brownfield |
| Labour | Cheap and plentiful (assembly, clothing) or highly skilled (high tech) |
| Raw materials | Heavy, bulky, weight-losing or perishable → locate at source |
| Fuel and power | Once coalfields; now national grid → footloose; aluminium seeks cheap HEP |
| Transport | Ports for imports (break of bulk), motorways, rail, airports for light high-value goods |
| Markets | Weight-gaining, fragile, perishable or customer-facing → near market |
| Political | Grants, tax breaks, export processing zones, special economic zones; trade barriers |

Plus **agglomeration**: firms cluster near suppliers, skilled workers
and related firms.

## Matching factor to industry

- Iron and steel, sugar refining → raw materials.
- Soft-drink bottling, bakery → market.
- Clothing, electronics assembly → cheap labour.
- Microchips, software → skilled labour, universities, capital.
- Aluminium smelting → cheap electricity.

## Case study outline: Silicon Valley, California (USA)

- Southern San Francisco Bay Area: Palo Alto, Mountain View,
  Cupertino, Santa Clara, San Jose.
- High technology: Intel (Santa Clara), Apple (Cupertino), Google
  (Mountain View); Hewlett-Packard founded in Palo Alto in 1939.
- Factors: Stanford University (graduates and research); venture
  capital; agglomeration; airports at San Francisco and San Jose;
  pleasant climate attracts workers.
- Problems: very high housing costs, congestion, inequality.
- Alternative: Bengaluru, India (Infosys and Wipro headquarters,
  Electronic City, Indian Institute of Science).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Footloose industry | Industry not tied to raw materials or power, free to locate widely |
| Greenfield site | Undeveloped land, often on the edge of a town |
| Brownfield site | Previously developed land, often old industrial land |
| Agglomeration | Clustering of related firms that benefit from being close |
| Break-of-bulk point | Place where goods are transferred between transport modes, such as a port |
| Export processing zone | Area offering tax and customs advantages to firms that make goods for export |
| Science park | Planned site for high-technology firms, often linked to a university |

## Self-test

1. What output of an industrial system is often forgotten?
2. Classify: (a) a car plant using parts from many suppliers, (b) an
   oil refinery.
3. Why do iron and steel works often locate near raw materials?
4. What is a footloose industry?
5. Give two reasons for the growth of Silicon Valley.
6. Why might a government create an export processing zone?

**Answers:** 1. Waste. 2. (a) Assembly. (b) Processing. 3. The raw
materials are heavy and bulky and lose weight in processing, so
locating near them cuts transport costs. 4. An industry that can
locate almost anywhere because it is not tied to raw materials or a
power source. 5. Any two: Stanford University providing graduates and
research; venture capital; clustering of related firms; good airports;
attractive climate. 6. To attract foreign investment, creating jobs
and export earnings.
