This guide covers **Paper 2: Translation into Urdu and Written Response
to Works**, the second of three papers in Pearson Edexcel GCE A-Level
Urdu (9UR0). Where Paper 1 tests translation *into* English alongside
independent research, Paper 2 reverses the translation direction and
adds the qualification's literature and film component -- knowledge of
the prescribed works is assessed here and nowhere else in the
qualification.

## Where this fits in 9UR0

Paper 2 is worth 30% of the A-level, but carries the highest raw mark
total of the three papers: 110 marks over 2 hours 40 minutes, compared
with 80 marks for Paper 1 and 60 for Paper 3. This reflects the
weighting of two substantial extended-response essays sitting alongside
the translation section. For the qualification's structure as a whole
and Paper 1 detail, see the
[Paper 1 study guide](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-reading/).

## Syllabus coverage

**PEARSON EDEXCEL A-LEVEL URDU (9UR0) — PAPER 2 (2 HOURS 40 MINUTES, 110 MARKS, 30% OF A-LEVEL)**

- **Section A: Translation into Urdu (20 marks, recommended 30 minutes)**
-- candidates translate one unseen passage from English into Urdu. As
in Paper 1's translation section, marking is points-based: the passage
is divided into 20 "chunks", each worth one mark, with non-grammatical
misspellings tolerated as long as they do not create ambiguity or
belong to a different word. Verb and adjective endings, however, must
be correct and are not treated as tolerable spelling variation.

- **Section B: Written response to works — literary texts (45 marks,
recommended 1 hour 5 minutes per response)** -- candidates write an
extended response to one or two of the prescribed literary texts, from
a choice of two questions per text.

- **Section C: Written response to works — films (45 marks, recommended
1 hour 5 minutes)** -- candidates who answered only one literary-text
question in Section B write a second extended response on one of the
prescribed films, from a choice of two questions.

In total, every candidate produces **two** extended responses to works:
either two literary-text essays (completing Section B twice and
skipping Section C), or one literary-text essay plus one film essay.
The prescribed list, set by Pearson Edexcel, currently comprises three
literary texts -- including a well-known essay collection by the Urdu
humorist Ahmad Shah Bukhari ("Patras"), alongside a novel and a play --
and three films. Candidates study either **one literary text and one
film**, or **two literary texts**.

Sections B and C are each marked against three levels-based criteria:
critical and analytical response to the work (AO4), range of
grammatical structures and vocabulary (AO3), and accuracy of language
(AO3). Across the whole paper, the assessment objective weighting is
AO3 (accuracy and range, 20%) and AO4 (critical/cultural response, 10%)
-- Paper 2 carries no AO1 or AO2 marks at all, unlike Papers 1 and 3.

## How to approach it

Because Section A is marked chunk by chunk, the same discipline that
helps Paper 1's translation applies here in reverse: work through the
passage systematically, and prioritise getting verb and adjective
endings grammatically correct, since these are explicitly excluded from
the tolerance given to ordinary misspellings. For the extended
responses in Sections B and C, prepare each of your two chosen works
(whether two literary texts, or one literary text and one film) against
the same five angles Pearson's guidance for teachers highlights: form
and technique, key themes and issues, characterisation, plot structure,
and social and cultural setting. Because AO4 (critical and cultural
response) is assessed here just as it is in Paper 1's research
question, revise your works with an eye to their connection to the
qualification's four broader social and cultural themes, not just plot
recall -- examiners reward responses that place a text or film within
its wider social or historical context, not only ones that summarise
what happens in it.

## Worked example: structuring a works response

A strong Section B or C response does not move through a work
chronologically retelling events; it argues a position in response to
the set question and supports that position with specific, well-chosen
material. For example, if a question asks how a prescribed play
presents changing attitudes towards a social institution, an effective
answer would open with a brief statement of the writer's overall
stance, then work through two or three specific scenes or exchanges
that best evidence that stance, referring to particular lines or
moments rather than paraphrasing whole scenes, and close by connecting
the writer's treatment of the issue to the wider social theme it
belongs to within the specification's four themes. This structure --
position, evidence, cultural connection -- rewards both AO3 (through
precise, accurate language while discussing the text) and AO4 (through
the explicit link to social and cultural context), rather than treating
the two objectives as separate paragraphs bolted together.

## Common mistakes

Attempting three extended responses (or a literary-text response and a
film response when the specification only requires two works in total)
instead of the correct combination of two. Treating verb and adjective
endings as ordinary spelling in the Section A translation, when the
mark scheme explicitly excludes them from that tolerance. Writing about
a prescribed work's plot in isolation without connecting it to the
qualification's cultural and social themes, which weakens the AO4
component of the mark. Confusing Section A's translation direction with
Paper 1's -- Paper 1 translates into English, Paper 2 translates into
Urdu.

## Quick revision checklist

- Confirm with your teacher which two works you are studying: either
two literary texts, or one literary text and one film.
- Practise chunk-by-chunk translation into Urdu, with particular care
over verb and adjective endings.
- Prepare each studied work against form/technique, themes, characters,
plot and social/cultural setting.
- Connect each work's content to the qualification's broader social and
cultural themes for stronger AO4 marks.
- Know the paper's timing allocation (30 minutes for translation, roughly
1 hour 5 minutes per extended response) and rehearse it under timed
conditions.
