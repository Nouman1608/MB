Condensed notes for the section 3.3 row **Occupiers' liability**: "Liability in respect of visitors (Occupiers' Liability Act 1957)" and "Liability in respect of trespassers (Occupiers' Liability Act 1984)". For explanation and a worked problem, use the [occupiers' liability study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-law-7162-occupiers-liability/).

## Five-step route

```
1 Occupier?      control of premises (Wheat v Lacon, 1966)
2 Premises?      land, buildings, fixed or movable structures (s1(3)(a) 1957)
3 Status?        visitor (permission) -> 1957 Act | trespasser -> 1984 Act
4 Duty/breach?   1957: common duty of care s2(2) | 1984: s1(3) conditions, s1(4) duty
5 Damage?        1957: injury + property | 1984: injury only (s1(8))
```

## Occupier, premises and status

| Point | Authority |
|---|---|
| Occupier = sufficient degree of control; can be more than one | *Wheat v E Lacon & Co* (1966) |
| Premises include vessels, vehicles, aircraft | s1(3)(a) OLA 1957 |
| Implied permission from long acquiescence | *Lowery v Walker* (1911) |
| Exceeding permission makes a trespasser | *The Calgarth* (1927): "slide down the banisters" |
| Visitor can become trespasser by prohibited activity | *Tomlinson v Congleton BC* (2003) |
| Entry under a right conferred by law treated as visitor | s2(6) OLA 1957 |

## OLA 1957: visitors

**s2(2) common duty of care:** reasonable care to see the visitor is reasonably safe in using the premises for the permitted purposes.

| Provision | Rule | Cases |
|---|---|---|
| s2(3)(a) children | Expect children to be less careful; allurements | *Glasgow Corp v Taylor* (1922); *Jolley v Sutton LBC* (2000) |
| Very young children | Parents share responsibility | *Phipps v Rochester Corp* (1955) |
| s2(3)(b) specialists | Expect them to guard against risks of their calling | *Roles v Nathan* (1963) |
| s2(4)(a) warnings | Must enable the visitor to be reasonably safe; none needed for obvious risks | *Staples v West Dorset DC* (1995); *Darby v National Trust* (2001) |
| s2(4)(b) contractors | Not liable if reasonable to entrust, competence checked, work checked where possible | *Haseldine v Daw* (1941); *Woodward v Mayor of Hastings* (1945); *Bottomley v Todmorden CC* (2003) |
| s2(1) exclusion | Possible by notice or agreement, subject to UCTA s2(1) and CRA s65 | |

## OLA 1984: trespassers

**Background:** *BRB v Herrington* (1972), "common duty of humanity".

**s1(3): duty arises only if all three:**
1. aware of the danger, or reasonable grounds to believe it exists;
2. knows, or has reasonable grounds to believe, the trespasser is or may come into the vicinity;
3. risk is one against which the occupier may reasonably be expected to offer some protection.

**s1(4):** duty to take reasonable care to see the trespasser does not suffer injury by reason of the danger.
**s1(5):** discharged by reasonable steps to warn or discourage.
**s1(8):** no liability for property damage.

| Case | Point |
|---|---|
| *Donoghue v Folkestone Properties* (2003) | Midnight winter swim: no reason to expect anyone there |
| *Rhind v Astbury Water Park* (2004) | Hidden object unknown to occupier: no awareness |
| *Keown v Coventry Healthcare NHS Trust* (2006) | Danger from the child's own activity, not the premises |
| *Ratcliff v McConnell* (1999) | Obvious risk voluntarily taken by an adult |
| *Tomlinson v Congleton BC* (2003) | No duty to protect against obvious risks of swimming |

## 1957 and 1984 compared

| | 1957 Act | 1984 Act |
|---|---|---|
| Claimant | Visitor | Non-visitor (trespasser) |
| When duty arises | Automatically | Only if s1(3) satisfied |
| Standard | Common duty of care | Reasonable care against the specific danger |
| Damage | Injury and property | Injury only |
| Warnings | s2(4)(a) | s1(5) |

## Evaluation points

- **Clarity:** statutory wording gives a clear structure, and section numbers make answers precise.
- **Personal responsibility:** *Tomlinson* stresses that adults must accept obvious risks, limiting a "compensation culture".
- **Children:** the 1957 Act is generous to children (*Glasgow Corp*, *Jolley*), while *Phipps* places responsibility on parents; trespassing children rely on the narrower 1984 Act.
- **Uncertainty:** the s1(3) conditions turn on what an occupier "has reasonable grounds to believe", which makes outcomes hard to predict.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Occupier | Person with a sufficient degree of control over premises |
| Visitor | Person with express or implied permission to enter |
| Trespasser | Person on premises without permission |
| Common duty of care | The s2(2) duty owed to visitors |
| Allurement | Something attractive and dangerous to children |
| Independent contractor | Person engaged to do work but not an employee |

## Exam traps

- Classify status at the moment of injury, not on entry.
- A warning sign is not automatically enough for children or hidden dangers.
- The 1984 Act duty is about the danger due to the state of the premises.
- No property damage claim for trespassers.
- Distinguish *Haseldine* (technical work) from *Woodward* (non-technical).

## Self-test

1. What test identifies an occupier? *Sufficient degree of control (Wheat v Lacon).*
2. What did *Lowery v Walker* decide? *Long acquiescence can give implied permission.*
3. What is the s2(2) duty? *To take reasonable care to see that the visitor will be reasonably safe for the permitted purposes.*
4. Why was the occupier not liable in *Phipps*? *Parents of very young children are expected to supervise them.*
5. Which section covers specialists and which case illustrates it? *s2(3)(b); Roles v Nathan.*
6. Why was no warning needed in *Staples*? *The danger was obvious.*
7. What three steps protect an occupier under s2(4)(b)? *Reasonable to entrust; checked competence; checked work where possible.*
8. Why was the occupier liable in *Woodward*? *Checking a cleaner's work needed no expertise.*
9. State the three s1(3) conditions. *Aware of danger; knows trespasser may be nearby; risk one against which protection is reasonably expected.*
10. Why did *Donoghue v Folkestone* fail? *No reason to believe anyone would be swimming there at midnight in winter.*
11. What is excluded by s1(8)? *Liability for damage to property.*
12. What did *Keown* decide? *The danger arose from the boy's activity, not the state of the premises.*
