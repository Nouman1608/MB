> Original questions written for Marlbridge, not past-paper questions. Read each passage in the syllabus Appendix 1 (Arabic and translation) before answering.

**How to use this set.** Question 1 is compulsory: three passages set, two answered, 8 marks in all, each answer covering themes and importance today. This set drills the skills one at a time. Marks are for practice and follow the syllabus levels loosely.

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Name the three groups into which the Appendix 1 passages are divided. **[3]**

**2.** State the group of each passage: (a) Sura 41.37, (b) Sura 6.75-79, (c) Sura 114. **[3]**

**3.** Sura 112 states Allah's oneness. Give two of the four things it says about Allah. **[2]**

**4.** In Sura 41.37 the sun and the moon are mentioned. Explain what the passage says Muslims should and should not do about them. **[3]**

**5.** Sura 99 mentions an "atom's weight" of good and of wrong. Explain what this teaches about deeds. **[3]**

**6.** Sura 96.1-5 is linked to a particular event in the Prophet's (pbuh) life. Name the event and the year. **[2]**

## Section B: Exam-style (passage given)

**7.** Sura 2.255. (a) Explain the main theme or themes of the passage. **[4]** (b) Explain its importance in the life of a Muslim today. **[4]**

**8.** Sura 6.75-79. (a) Explain the main theme or themes of the passage. **[4]** (b) Explain its importance in the life of a Muslim today. **[4]**

**9.** Sura 93. (a) Explain the main theme or themes of the passage. **[4]** (b) Explain its importance in the life of a Muslim today. **[4]**

## Section C: Evaluate

**10.** "Learning only five of the fifteen passages is enough for Question 1." Explain why a candidate should not rely on this. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Allah in himself [1]; Allah's relationship with the created world [1]; Allah's Messengers [1].

**2.** (a) Allah in himself [1]; (b) Allah's Messengers [1]; (c) Allah's relationship with the created world [1].

**3.** Any two: Allah is one [1]; He is eternal and absolute [1]; He neither begets nor is begotten [1]; nothing is like Him [1].

**4.** Do not prostrate to the sun or moon [1], which are signs of Allah [1]; worship Allah, who created them [1].

**5.** Every deed, however small, is counted [1]; good will be seen and so will wrong [1]; people are accountable on the Last Day [1].

**6.** The first revelation to the Prophet (pbuh) [1] in 610 [1].

**7.** (a) Allah is living and self-subsisting and never tires or sleeps [1]; He owns all things and none intercedes except by His permission [1]; His knowledge is unlimited and no one comprehends any of it except as He wills [1]; His Throne extends over heavens and earth, and He guards them without fatigue [1]. (b) Muslims recite it for protection and after prayer, trusting that Allah is always watching over them [up to 2]; it removes fear of other powers, since only Allah is the Most High [up to 2]. Credit any other reasoned application.

**8.** (a) Ibrahim observes a star, the moon and the sun, each of which sets [1]; he concludes that what sets cannot be his Lord [1]; only the Creator of the heavens and earth deserves worship [1]; he asks for guidance, showing dependence on Allah [1]. (b) Reflection on creation leads to tawhid [up to 2]; created things and idols are rejected as objects of worship, and a Muslim learns to reason from creation to the Creator [up to 2].

**9.** (a) Allah has not left the Prophet (pbuh) [1]; the hereafter will be better than the present [1]; Allah recalls past favours: shelter as an orphan, guidance and provision [1]; duties follow: do not oppress the orphan, do not turn away the one who asks, speak of Allah's blessings [1]. (b) Comfort for those in hard times [up to 2]; care for orphans and the needy [up to 2].

**10.** The three passages set may come from any group [1]; if the learned passages are all from one group, none of the set passages may be one the candidate knows [1]; the syllabus says candidates should study the whole of the syllabus [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing what a passage mentions instead of the teaching it gives.
- A part (b) with no concrete action.
- Forgetting the Arabic: be ready to recognise the title or key words.
