## Section A

**1.** Write the equation for the reaction of propanoic acid with PCl₅ and name the two inorganic
products. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the apparatus must be dry when an acyl chloride is prepared. **[1]**

**3.** Dimethylamine, (CH₃)₂NH, is added to ethanoyl chloride.

**(a)** Classify dimethylamine as a primary, secondary or tertiary amine. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the structural formula of the organic product. **[1]**

**4.** Name the mechanism by which acyl chlorides react with nucleophiles, and state what feature a
species must have to act as the nucleophile. **[2]**

**5.** Place C₆H₅Cl, CH₃COCl and CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl in order of increasing ease of hydrolysis. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Ethanoyl chloride reacts with ethanol at room temperature.

**(a)** Explain why the carbonyl carbon atom of ethanoyl chloride is attacked by ethanol. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the mechanism of this reaction, referring to each curly arrow and to the
intermediate formed. **[4]**

**7.** A student places 2 cm³ of aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol into each of three test tubes,
then adds a few drops of ethanoyl chloride to tube A, 1-chloropropane to tube B and chlorobenzene
to tube C. All three tubes are then warmed.

**(a)** Describe what the student sees in each tube. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the reaction in tube A is the fastest. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the result in tube C. **[2]**

**8.** Compound **Y** reacts with cold water to give 2-methylpropanoic acid, (CH₃)₂CHCOOH, and HCl.

**(a)** Give the structural formula of **Y**. **[1]**

**(b)** Name **Y**. **[1]**

**(c)** Give the structural formula of the organic product of **Y** with phenol. **[1]**

**(d)** Write the equation for the reaction of **Y** with excess ammonia. **[2]**

**9.** Benzoyl chloride is made by reacting benzoic acid with PCl₅.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest how the benzoyl chloride could be separated from the other liquid product. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest a different reagent that would make separation simpler, and explain why. **[2]**

**10.** N-phenylethanamide, CH₃CONHC₆H₅, can be made from ethanoic acid in two steps.

**(a)** State a reagent for step 1, which converts ethanoic acid into ethanoyl chloride. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the equation for step 2, using phenylamine, C₆H₅NH₂. **[1]**

**(c)** In practice two moles of phenylamine are used for each mole of ethanoyl chloride. Suggest
why, and give the formula of the salt formed. **[2]**

**11.** 1.57 g of ethanoyl chloride is added to excess water. The resulting solution is titrated with
0.500 mol dm⁻³ NaOH(aq). Calculate the volume of NaOH(aq) needed to neutralise all the acid
present. **[3]**

**12.** The reaction between ethanoyl chloride and ammonia can be described both as a
**condensation** reaction and as a nucleophilic **addition–elimination** reaction. Explain why each
description is correct. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** CH₃CH₂COOH + PCl₅ → CH₃CH₂COCl + POCl₃ + HCl [1]; phosphorus oxychloride (phosphoryl
chloride), POCl₃, and hydrogen chloride [1].

**2.** Water would hydrolyse the acyl chloride (and the chlorinating reagent) back to the carboxylic
acid / HCl [1].

**3. (a)** Secondary [1].

**(b)** CH₃CON(CH₃)₂, N,N-dimethylethanamide [1].

**4.** Addition–elimination (nucleophilic addition–elimination) [1]; the nucleophile must have a
lone pair of electrons that it can donate to the δ+ carbon [1].

**5.** C₆H₅Cl < CH₃CH₂CH₂Cl < CH₃COCl [1].

**6. (a)** The carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, O and Cl, so it carries a
large δ+ charge [1].

**(b)** Curly arrow from a lone pair on the O of ethanol to the carbonyl carbon, and from the C=O π
bond to O [1]; tetrahedral intermediate with O⁻ and a positively charged O (bearing H and C₂H₅)
[1]; curly arrow from O⁻ re-forming C=O and from the C–Cl bond to Cl, releasing Cl⁻ [1]; loss of
H⁺ from the positive oxygen to give ethyl ethanoate, with H⁺ + Cl⁻ forming HCl [1].

**7. (a)** Tube A: white precipitate forms immediately (with steamy fumes) [1]; tube B: white
precipitate forms slowly on warming [1]; tube C: no precipitate [1].

**(b)** The carbonyl carbon is strongly δ+ because of both O and Cl [1], and the C=O π bond can
accept the electron pair from the attacking water molecule, so addition is easy and the activation
energy is low [1].

**(c)** A lone pair on chlorine overlaps with the delocalised π system of the ring, so the C–Cl bond
has partial double-bond character and is stronger [1]; the electron-rich ring also repels the
nucleophile, so no hydrolysis occurs and no Cl⁻ is released to precipitate [1].

**8. (a)** (CH₃)₂CHCOCl [1].

**(b)** 2-methylpropanoyl chloride [1].

**(c)** (CH₃)₂CHCOOC₆H₅ [1].

**(d)** (CH₃)₂CHCOCl + 2NH₃ → (CH₃)₂CHCONH₂ + NH₄Cl: correct amide [1]; NH₄Cl with 2NH₃ balanced
[1]. (Accept the 1 : 1 equation giving HCl for 1 mark only, since excess ammonia is stated.)

**9. (a)** C₆H₅COOH + PCl₅ → C₆H₅COCl + POCl₃ + HCl [1].

**(b)** Fractional distillation (the two liquids have different boiling points) [1].

**(c)** SOCl₂ [1]; both by-products, SO₂ and HCl, are gases and leave the mixture [1].

**10. (a)** Any one of PCl₅, SOCl₂, or PCl₃ with heat [1].

**(b)** CH₃COCl + C₆H₅NH₂ → CH₃CONHC₆H₅ + HCl [1].

**(c)** The second mole of phenylamine acts as a base and reacts with the HCl formed [1];
phenylammonium chloride, C₆H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1].

**11.** n(CH₃COCl) = 1.57 / 78.5 = 0.0200 mol [1]; hydrolysis gives 0.0200 mol CH₃COOH and 0.0200
mol HCl, so 0.0400 mol NaOH is needed [1]; volume = 0.0400 / 0.500 = 0.0800 dm³ = 80.0 cm³ [1].

**12.** Condensation: two molecules join with the loss of a small molecule, HCl [1].
Addition–elimination: the nucleophile (NH₃) first adds to the C=O group, then Cl⁻ is eliminated and
C=O re-forms [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving "heat" as a condition for the room-temperature reactions of acyl chlorides.
- Missing POCl₃ or HCl from the PCl₅ equation, or using a 1 : 1 ratio with PCl₃.
- Drawing curly arrows from atoms rather than lone pairs, or leaving charges off the intermediate.
- Saying chlorobenzene "reacts slowly"; under these conditions it does not react.
- Counting only the ethanoic acid in Question 11: hydrolysis also produces HCl, which uses up NaOH.
- Writing a 1 : 1 equation with HCl gas when the question says ammonia or amine is in excess.
