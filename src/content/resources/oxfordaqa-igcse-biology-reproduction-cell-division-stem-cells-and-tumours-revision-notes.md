## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sexual reproduction | Joining (fusion) of male and female gametes; mixed genetic information from two parents leads to variety |
| Asexual reproduction | No fusion of gametes; one parent; no mixing, so no genetic variation |
| Clone | Genetically identical individual made by asexual reproduction |
| Chromosome | Found in the nucleus; carries many genes |
| Gene | Controls a characteristic of the body; many genes per chromosome |
| Allele | A different form of a gene; may produce a different characteristic |
| Mitosis | Division of a body cell into two genetically identical body cells |
| Meiosis | Division of a cell in a reproductive organ into four gametes |
| Gamete | Sex cell with a single set of chromosomes |
| Fertilisation | Gametes join to form a single body cell with new pairs of chromosomes |
| Differentiation | Cells become specialised for different jobs |
| Stem cell | Cell (from embryos or adult bone marrow) that can be made to differentiate into many cell types |
| Therapeutic cloning | Embryo carrying the patient's genes; its stem cells are not rejected |
| Tumour | Abnormal, uncontrolled growth of cells |
| Carcinogen | Agent that can cause tumours, such as chemicals in tobacco smoke or asbestos, or ionising radiation |

## Sexual versus asexual (3.5.1 a)

| | Sexual | Asexual |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | Two | One |
| Gametes fuse | Yes | No |
| Variation in offspring | Yes | None genetically |
| Offspring called | Offspring | Clones |
| Share of genes from each parent | Half | All from one |

## Chromosomes, genes and alleles (3.5.2 a-c)

- Chromosomes are in the nucleus and carry genes.
- A chromosome carries a large number of genes.
- Alleles are different forms of the same gene.
- Body cells have chromosomes in pairs. Gametes have one from each pair.
- Number in a body cell, then pairs, then gamete: 46, 23, 23 for a human.

Rule: pairs = body number ÷ 2, gamete number = body number ÷ 2, fertilised egg = body number.

## Mitosis and meiosis side by side (3.5.2 d-h)

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Cell type | Body cell | Cell in reproductive organ |
| Genetic material copied first | Yes | Yes |
| Divisions | 1 | 2 |
| Cells made | 2 | 4 |
| Chromosomes in each new cell | Full set, in pairs | Single set |
| Purpose | Growth, replacement | Gametes |

### Method: counting cells and chromosomes

1. Decide whether the question is about mitosis or meiosis.
2. Mitosis: cells after n rounds = 2ⁿ, each with the full number.
3. Meiosis: one cell gives 4 gametes, each with half the body number.
4. Multiply by the number of starting cells if there are several.

Worked reminder: a pea body cell has 14 chromosomes. A cell undergoing meiosis gives 4 gametes with 14 ÷ 2 = 7 chromosomes each. After 5 rounds of mitosis one cell becomes 2⁵ = 32 cells.

## Fertilisation, differentiation and plants (3.5.2 i-j)

Order of events:

1. Gametes fuse at fertilisation, forming a body cell with new pairs.
2. That cell divides by mitosis many times.
3. Cells differentiate into different kinds of cell as the organism develops.

| | Animals | Plants |
|---|---|---|
| Differentiation | Most cell types specialise early | Many cells can still specialise at any age |
| Division in adults | Largely repair and replacement | Cells can still differentiate, so new tissues can form |

## Stem cells and therapeutic cloning (3.5.2 k-m)

- Sources: human embryos and adult bone marrow.
- They can be made to differentiate into many types of human cell.
- Possible use: conditions such as paralysis.
- Therapeutic cloning: the embryo carries the patient's genes, so the cells are not rejected.
- Knowledge of stem cell techniques is not required.

### Ethical judgements

Use the information given. Cover:

1. Benefit to patients (treating illness).
2. Concern about using embryos, which are not allowed to develop.
3. Concern about the donor for bone marrow cells.
4. Different views among people, including religious views.
5. A reasoned conclusion that follows from your points.

## Tumours and carcinogens (3.5.2 n-o)

| | Benign | Malignant |
|---|---|---|
| Cause | Abnormal, uncontrolled cell growth | Abnormal, uncontrolled cell growth |
| Invades healthy tissue | No | Yes |
| Cells enter bloodstream | No | Some may |
| Secondary tumours | No | Can form |

Causes: chemical carcinogens (tobacco smoke, asbestos); ionising radiation (UV, X-rays).

## Must-know distinctions

- Gene versus allele: a gene is a section of instructions for one characteristic; alleles are its forms.
- Chromosome versus gene: a chromosome carries many genes.
- Mitosis gives identical body cells; meiosis gives gametes.
- Benign tumours do not invade; malignant ones do.
- Gametes have one set; body cells have pairs.

## Worked reminders

### Percentage change and rate

1. Find the change: final value minus starting value.
2. Divide by the **starting** value and multiply by 100.
3. For a rate, divide the change by the time and give the unit.

Example: a tumour grows from 6 mm to 15 mm in 9 months. Change = 9 mm. Percentage increase = 9 ÷ 6 × 100 = 150%. Mean rate = 9 ÷ 9 = 1 mm per month.

### Doubling cultures

A stem cell culture of 300 cells doubles every 12 hours. After 48 hours there are 48 ÷ 12 = 4 doublings, so 300 × 2⁴ = 4800 cells. If you are given a table, check that each row is double the one before before you extend the pattern.

### Counting gametes and chromosomes

A maize body cell has 20 chromosomes. One cell undergoing meiosis makes 4 gametes with 10 chromosomes each. Two gametes fusing give 20 again. A zygote that undergoes 3 rounds of mitosis becomes 2³ = 8 cells, each with 20 chromosomes.

## Facts to link together

- Asexual reproduction needs one parent, so the offspring are clones, and any differences between them come from the environment.
- Sexual reproduction mixes genetic information, because each parent supplies one set of chromosomes through a gamete.
- Fertilisation restores the pairs, mitosis builds the organism, and differentiation makes it specialised.
- Stem cells are valuable because they can be made to differentiate into many types of human cell.
- Therapeutic cloning matches the genes of the stem cells to the patient.
- A tumour is uncontrolled cell division. Whether it is benign or malignant depends on whether its cells invade healthy tissue.
- Carcinogens and ionising radiation raise the chance that cells divide without control.

## Exam technique for this unit

- **Explain** questions need a cause and a result, joined by "because" or "so".
- **Calculate** questions need working. Write the sum before the answer.
- **Evaluate or make a judgement** questions need points on both sides and a conclusion.
- **State** questions need a short answer without explanation.
- When asked about chromosomes, give numbers whenever the question supplies a number.

## Quick self-test

1. A chimpanzee has 48 chromosomes in a body cell. How many pairs?
2. How many chromosomes are in a chimpanzee sperm?
3. How many cells result from one round of mitosis in one cell?
4. How many gametes result from one cell dividing by meiosis?
5. A zygote divides by mitosis 5 times, with all cells dividing together. How many cells?
6. Name two sources of stem cells that the specification mentions.
7. Why are stem cells from therapeutic cloning not rejected?
8. State one difference between benign and malignant tumours.
9. A tumour has a mass of 2.5 g and grows to 4.0 g. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. Name two chemical carcinogens or sources of carcinogens.
11. Why can a plant cutting grow roots, when a mature animal muscle cell does not form a new animal?
12. Give the term for genetically identical offspring.

### Answers

1. 48 ÷ 2 = **24 pairs**.
2. **24**.
3. **Two**.
4. **Four**.
5. 2⁵ = **32 cells**.
6. **Human embryos and adult bone marrow**.
7. The embryo carries the patient's genes, so the immune system does not see the cells as foreign.
8. Malignant tumour cells invade healthy tissue; benign tumours do not.
9. (4.0 − 2.5) ÷ 2.5 × 100 = **60%**.
10. Any two from tobacco smoke and asbestos.
11. Plant cells can still differentiate at any age; most animal cells specialise early and later divide mainly for repair and replacement.
12. **Clones**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "half the chromosomes" for a body cell instead of working out the number from the data.
- Mixing up the number of cells: mitosis makes two, meiosis makes four.
- Saying gametes are "identical" to each other when comparing with mitosis.
- Calling a benign tumour "not cancerous" without saying it does not invade other tissues.
- Giving an ethical answer that lists only advantages, or only objections.
- Forgetting the percentage change formula: change ÷ original × 100.
- Writing "the embryo is the patient" instead of saying it carries the patient's genes.
- Forgetting units in rate answers, such as mm per month.
