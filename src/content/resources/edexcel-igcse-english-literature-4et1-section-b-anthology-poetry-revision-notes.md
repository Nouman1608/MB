## The task in one box

| Feature | Section B |
|---|---|
| Texts | The sixteen poems in Part 3 of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE English Anthology |
| Choice | One question from two; each compares two poems (one may let you choose the second) |
| Marks | 30 |
| Assessment objectives | AO2 (language, form, structure) and AO3 (comparison) |
| Recommended time | 40 minutes |

The anthology list changes only when Pearson revises the specification; check it for your issue.

## The poems by form

| Form | Poem(s) | Comparison hook |
|---|---|---|
| Shakespearean sonnet | *Sonnet 116* | Argument resolved in a final couplet |
| Petrarchan sonnet | *Remember* | Turn after line 8 changes the request |
| Villanelle | *Do not go gentle into that good night* | Two refrains: insistence, urgency |
| Dramatic monologue | *My Last Duchess* | Speaker reveals more than he intends |
| Ballad | *La Belle Dame sans Merci* | Story told through question and answer |
| Rhyming couplets with repeated questions | *The Tyger* | Awe and unease built through questions |
| A single extended sentence of conditions | *If* | Advice held back until the end |
| Free or broken form | *Half-caste*, *Search For My Tongue* | Layout and language as part of the argument |

## Pairing grid (shared focus, different method)

- *Piano* + *Poem at Thirty-Nine*: an adult remembering a parent; nostalgia versus admiration.
- *Half-past Two* + *Hide and Seek*: a child's perspective; time and confusion versus isolation.
- *Sonnet 116* + *Remember*: lasting love; certainty versus selfless release.
- *My Last Duchess* + *La Belle Dame sans Merci*: power in relationships; control versus enchantment.
- *Search For My Tongue* + *Half-caste*: identity and language; loss and regrowth versus challenge.
- *Do not go gentle into that good night* + *Remember*: facing death; resistance versus acceptance.
- *War Photographer* + *Blessing*: suffering seen from outside; guilt and indifference versus joy amid hardship.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AO3 | Exploring links and connections between texts: in Section B, sustained comparison |
| Comparative overview | An opening that states the main similarity and difference in one or two sentences |
| Point-by-point structure | Each paragraph compares both poems on one idea or method |
| Volta | A turn in thought, especially in a sonnet |
| Refrain | A repeated line, central to the villanelle |
| Dramatic monologue | A single character speaking to a silent listener |
| Comparative connective | Whereas, similarly, in contrast, both, unlike, however |

## Sentence frames

- "Both poets present X, but where Poet A uses ..., Poet B ..."
- "Unlike the controlled form of ..., the looser structure of ... suggests ..."
- "Similarly, ... yet the effect is different because ..."

## Traps

- Two separate mini-essays with a comparison added at the end.
- Comparing only content, not methods and effects.
- Choosing a weakly connected second poem.
- Retelling the story of narrative poems.
- Running beyond 40 minutes because the poems feel familiar.

## Self-test

1. What do the two assessment objectives in Section B require?
2. Name the form of *Do not go gentle into that good night* and one feature of it.
3. Where does the turn come in a Petrarchan sonnet such as *Remember*?
4. What three questions should you ask when choosing your own second poem?
5. What should the final paragraph do?

**Answers:** 1. AO2: analyse language, form and structure and their effects; AO3: compare the poems,
exploring links and differences. 2. A villanelle: nineteen lines with two repeated refrains. 3. After
the eighth line, between the octave and the sestet. 4. Does it share the focus, does it differ enough
in method, and do I know it well enough to quote quickly? 5. Make a comparative judgement (which
presentation is more hopeful, unsettling or convincing, and why) rather than repeat earlier points.
