## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Photon | A packet of electromagnetic energy; its energy rises with frequency |
| Excited state | Any arrangement where an electron sits above its lowest available level |
| Continuous spectrum | All wavelengths in a range, no gaps |
| Line spectrum | Only certain wavelengths, seen as separate lines |
| Main energy level | Shell numbered n = 1, 2, 3 ...; holds up to 2n² electrons |
| Sublevel | s, p, d or f division of a main level |
| Orbital | Region around the nucleus where an electron is likely to be; holds two electrons of opposite spin |
| Convergence limit | Point where spectral lines merge at high frequency (HL: corresponds to ionization) |
| First ionization energy | (HL) Energy per mole to take one electron from each gaseous ground-state atom |

## 1.3.1 Spectra in four lines

- Higher frequency = higher energy = shorter wavelength.
- Increasing energy: radio, microwave, infrared, visible, ultraviolet, X-ray, gamma.
- Visible light: red is lowest energy, violet is highest.
- Electrons fall from excited states to lower levels and each fall emits one photon. Gap size fixes the photon's frequency, so a fixed set of gaps gives a fixed set of lines.

**Distinction:** a rainbow or white light through a prism is continuous. A gas discharge tube gives a line spectrum unique to the element.

## 1.3.2 Hydrogen emission spectrum

| Electron falls to | Energy released | Region |
|---|---|---|
| n = 1 | Largest | Ultraviolet |
| n = 2 | Middle | Visible |
| n = 3 | Smallest of the three | Infrared |

- Lines in each family crowd together towards **higher** frequency, because the levels get closer together as n increases.
- The merging point is the convergence limit.
- The names of the series are not needed.

## 1.3.3 to 1.3.5 Levels, sublevels and orbitals

| n | Sublevels | Orbitals (n²) | Max electrons (2n²) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1s | 1 | 2 |
| 2 | 2s, 2p | 4 | 8 |
| 3 | 3s, 3p, 3d | 9 | 18 |
| 4 | 4s, 4p, 4d, 4f | 16 | 32 |

Per sublevel: s has 1 orbital, p has 3, d has 5, f has 7.

**Shapes:** s is a sphere centred on the nucleus. Each p orbital is a dumbbell; the three (px, py, pz) lie along the x, y and z axes at 90° to each other.

### The three rules

| Rule | In one line |
|---|---|
| Aufbau principle | Fill the lowest-energy orbital first: 1s 2s 2p 3s 3p 4s 3d 4p |
| Pauli exclusion principle | Two electrons at most per orbital, with opposite spins |
| Hund's rule | Spread out singly with parallel spins across equal-energy orbitals before pairing |

### Method in steps: configuration of an atom

1. Read off Z; a neutral atom has Z electrons.
2. Fill sublevels in the Aufbau order until all electrons are placed.
3. Check for Cr (Z = 24) and Cu (Z = 29): move one 4s electron into 3d.
4. For a condensed form, replace the inner part with the previous noble gas in square brackets.
5. Check that the superscripts add up to Z.

### Method in steps: configuration of an ion

1. Write the atom first.
2. Positive ion: remove electrons from the highest main level first. For first-row transition elements that means **4s before 3d**.
3. Negative ion: add electrons to the next free orbital in the Aufbau order.
4. Check the total equals Z minus the charge.

### Small worked reminders

```
Ni (Z = 28):    [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s²
Ni²⁺:           [Ar] 3d⁸            (4s emptied first)
3d  [↑↓][↑↓][↑↓][↑ ][↑ ]            2 unpaired electrons

Cu⁺ (Z = 29):   [Ar] 3d¹⁰           (the single 4s electron goes)
S²⁻ (Z = 16):   1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ (same as argon)
```

### Cr and Cu exceptions

| Atom | Expected | Actual | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cr, Z = 24 | [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ | Half-filled 3d gives lower overall energy |
| Cu, Z = 29 | [Ar] 3d⁹ 4s² | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ | Filled 3d gives lower overall energy |

## 1.3.6 HL only: convergence limit and first IE

**Formulas:** E = hf and c = λf, so E = hc/λ. Molar energy = E × L, where L is the Avogadro constant.

### Method in steps

1. Convert the wavelength to metres if needed (1 nm = 10⁻⁹ m).
2. Find the energy of one photon with E = hf or E = hc/λ.
3. Multiply by L to get J mol⁻¹.
4. Divide by 1000 for kJ mol⁻¹ and give 3 significant figures.

**Worked reminder.** The convergence limit for lithium's ground-state lines is at a frequency of 1.30 × 10¹⁵ Hz. Taking L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ and h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s:

```
E = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 1.30 × 10¹⁵ = 8.62 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
First IE = 8.62 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ = 5.19 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ = 519 kJ mol⁻¹
```

### First IE trends

| Pattern | Cause |
|---|---|
| Rises across a period | Nuclear charge increases; electron removed from the same main level with similar shielding |
| Falls down a group | Outer electron in a higher main level, further away and more shielded |
| Drop from group 2 to group 13 | Electron removed from a p sublevel, higher in energy than s |
| Drop from group 15 to group 16 | Electron removed from a doubly occupied p orbital; pair repulsion |

## 1.3.7 HL only: successive ionization energies

- Every value is larger than the one before: the ion left behind is more positive each time.
- Look for the biggest **increase**, not the biggest value.
- Electrons removed before that jump = outer electrons = group (add 10 for groups 13 to 18).

**Worked reminder.** Boron, kJ mol⁻¹ (use these values): 801, 2427, 3660, 25 026. Increases: +1626, +1233, +21 366. The jump comes after the third value, so boron has three outer electrons and is in **group 13**.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Frequency vs wavelength**: energy rises with frequency and falls with wavelength.
- **Main level vs sublevel vs orbital**: n = 3 is a main level; 3p is a sublevel; 3px is one orbital.
- **Filling order vs loss order**: 4s fills before 3d but empties before 3d.
- **Full vs condensed**: full lists every sublevel from 1s; condensed starts from a noble gas core.
- **Convergence (HL)**: the convergence limit is at high frequency, not low.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing [Ar] 4s² 3d⁴ or [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s² for chromium, or the 3d⁹ form for copper.
- Removing 3d electrons before the 4s pair for ions such as Ni²⁺ or Co²⁺.
- Drawing two electrons in one box with the same spin, breaking the Pauli principle.
- Pairing up in a p or d sublevel before every orbital holds one electron.
- Saying an s orbital is circular rather than spherical, or showing p orbitals not at 90°.
- Mixing up which hydrogen lines are ultraviolet (drops to n = 1) and which are visible (drops to n = 2).
- (HL) Using nm directly in E = hc/λ, or giving the energy of one atom when a molar value is asked for.
- (HL) Explaining the group 15 to 16 drop as "extra stability" with no mention of paired-electron repulsion.

## Quick self-test

1. Which photon has more energy: 450 nm or 650 nm light?
2. State one difference between a continuous spectrum and a line spectrum.
3. In which region are hydrogen lines produced by electrons falling to n = 3?
4. What is the maximum number of electrons in the third main energy level?
5. How many orbitals are in a d sublevel?
6. Give the condensed configuration of Ni²⁺.
7. Write the full electron configuration of copper.
8. Which 3+ ion with Z ≤ 36 has the configuration [Ar] 3d³?
9. How many unpaired electrons are in an Fe³⁺ ion?
10. (HL) Explain why the first IE of oxygen is lower than that of nitrogen.
11. (HL) The first IE of caesium is 376 kJ mol⁻¹. Calculate the frequency at the convergence limit. Take L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ and h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s.
12. (HL) Successive IEs of an element (kJ mol⁻¹): 419, 3052, 4420, 5877. Deduce its group.

### Answers

1. 450 nm: shorter wavelength, higher frequency, higher energy.
2. A continuous spectrum contains all wavelengths in a range; a line spectrum contains only specific wavelengths.
3. Infrared.
4. 18 (2 × 3²).
5. Five.
6. [Ar] 3d⁸.
7. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s¹.
8. Cr³⁺ (chromium, Z = 24: 24 − 3 = 21 electrons).
9. Five. Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵, one electron in each 3d orbital.
10. Oxygen's fourth 2p electron shares an orbital with another electron. Repulsion between the pair makes it easier to remove.
11. Energy per atom = 376 000 ÷ (6.02 × 10²³) = 6.246 × 10⁻¹⁹ J; f = E ÷ h = **9.42 × 10¹⁴ Hz**.
12. The large jump is between the first and second values, so one outer electron: **group 1**.
