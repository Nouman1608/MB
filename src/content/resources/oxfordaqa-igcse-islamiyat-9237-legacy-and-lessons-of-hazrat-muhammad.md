**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.10 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The legacy and lessons of Hazrat Muhammad's (pbuh) life.** The specification asks for "the key teachings from his final sermon" and "key teachings from Hazrat Muhammad's life including: treatment of parents, the importance of justice, honesty and patience." The prescribed passage is Qur'an 5:3.

This is the point where the life story turns into guidance. Examiners are not asking you to retell events. They want to know what a Muslim should take from them, and how you can show it with a teaching from the Qur'an or Hadith and an example from his life.

## What do we mean by legacy?

A legacy is what a person leaves behind. For the Prophet (pbuh) it has four parts:

- **A complete message.** The Qur'an was revealed and preserved, and the religion was declared complete.
- **A living example.** His Sunnah shows how to apply the Qur'an in daily life.
- **A community.** Tribes that were once at war became one Ummah, bound by faith rather than by lineage.
- **A set of values.** Justice, honesty, mercy, patience and respect for family run through everything he taught.

In the Final Sermon he told the pilgrims to hold on to the first two: the Book of Allah and his Sunnah. That is why the legacy is also the bridge to the next content area, sources of authority.

## The Final Sermon

**Setting.** In Dhul-Hijjah 10 AH (632 CE) the Prophet (pbuh) led the only Hajj of his later life. On the ninth day he spoke to the very large crowd gathered at Arafat. He told them that he might not meet them again in the same place, and he asked those present to pass his words to those who were absent. Within three months he had died in Madinah.

The sermon survives in several reports with slightly different wording, so write "the sermon reports that..." and keep to the main teachings.

| Teaching | What he said (in summary) | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Sanctity of life, property and honour | They are as sacred as the day, the month and the city | Protects every person; the base of law and security |
| Trusts | Return what you have been trusted with to its owner | Honesty and Amanat in everyday dealings |
| Interest and revenge | Interest and the blood feuds of the Jahiliyyah are cancelled, starting with his own family's | Justice begins with the leader's own household |
| Women | Treat women well and respect their rights; they have rights over you as you have over them | Reverses pre-Islamic treatment of women |
| Equality | No Arab is better than a non-Arab, or a white person than a black person, except by taqwa | Ends racial and tribal pride |
| Brotherhood | Every Muslim is a brother to every other Muslim | Unity of the Ummah |
| Guidance | He left the Book of Allah, and, in other reports, his Sunnah; hold fast to it and you will not go astray | The two sources of authority |
| Worship | Pray five times, fast Ramadhan, pay Zakah, perform Hajj | The Five Pillars as the shape of life |
| Passing on the message | Let the present tell the absent | Every Muslim shares the duty to teach |

The picture is of a community built on rights and duties. Use the table as a bank of points: any one of these can be developed into an explained reason.

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 5:3

The passage begins with a list of forbidden foods: dead animals, blood, pork, and food dedicated to anyone other than Allah. Then comes the verse that matters for this content area: "This day those who disbelieve have despaired of [defeating] your religion; so fear them not, but fear Me. This day I have perfected for you your religion and completed My favour upon you and have approved for you Islam as religion." The verse ends with a mercy: whoever is forced by severe hunger, with no inclination to sin, will find that "Allah is Forgiving and Merciful."

Tradition connects the verse with Arafat during the Farewell Pilgrimage, which is why it belongs to the legacy.

- "**Despaired of defeating your religion**": the opposition that began in Makkah had ended; the community was secure.
- "**Fear Me**": strength is no reason for pride; accountability is to Allah.
- "**I have perfected for you your religion**": the guidance is complete, so Muslims look to the Qur'an and Sunnah rather than invent new teachings.
- "**Completed My favour upon you**": Islam is a blessing to be thankful for.
- "**Forced by severe hunger**": even firm rules have mercy built into them, which shows balance.

## Lesson 1: treatment of parents

**Teaching.** The Qur'an places kindness to parents straight after worship of Allah: Your Lord has decreed that none but Him is to be worshipped and that parents are to be treated with good treatment (17:23). It says that, even in old age, a child must not say "uff", the smallest sign of impatience, and must speak to them with respect and "lower to them the wing of humility out of mercy" (17:23-24).

**Hadith.** A man asked who most deserved his good company. The Prophet (pbuh) answered "your mother" three times and then "your father" (reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim). The repetition shows the special honour of mothers, who carry and nurse a child.

**His example.** The Prophet (pbuh) lost his father before birth and his mother when he was about six. He remembered both with love, and he showed courtesy to those connected with them. He taught that kindness to parents continues after their death, through prayer for them and honour for their friends.

**Practical meaning.** Care in their old age, obedience in what is right, patience, and not causing them distress. The limit is that obedience does not extend to disobeying Allah (Qur'an 31:15), but even then the child must "accompany them in this world with appropriate kindness".

## Lesson 2: the importance of justice

**Teaching.** "O you who have believed, be persistently standing firm in justice, as witnesses for Allah, even if it be against yourselves or parents and relatives" (Qur'an 4:135). Hatred of a people must not push anyone away from fairness (5:8).

**His example.** A woman from a respected tribe was found guilty of theft. Some companions asked for the punishment to be waived because of her family's standing. The Prophet (pbuh) refused and said that earlier nations had been destroyed because they let the powerful go free and punished the weak. He added that if his own daughter Fatima stole, he would apply the same rule.

**In the Final Sermon.** Cancelling his own relatives' debts and blood feuds first shows that a just leader begins with his own family. At Makkah, the amnesty shows justice joined to mercy.

## Lesson 3: honesty

**Teaching.** "Truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise" (reported by al-Bukhari and Muslim). The Qur'an commands believers to be with the truthful (9:119).

**His example.** Before prophethood the people of Makkah called him *al-Amin*, the trustworthy, and *al-Sadiq*, the truthful. His enemies left valuables with him even as they opposed him. On the night of the Hijrah he asked Hazrat Ali (RA) to stay behind in Makkah to return those deposits to their owners. This is trust (Amanat) in practice and links to the Final Sermon's command to return trusts.

## Lesson 4: patience

**Teaching.** "Indeed, Allah is with the patient" (Qur'an 2:153). "the patient will be given their reward without account" (39:10). A Hadith says that all the believer's affairs are good: thankful in ease and patient in hardship (Muslim).

**His example.** He bore years of insult and boycott in Makkah, was driven out of Ta'if with stones, lost his wife Khadijah (RA) and his uncle Abu Talib in the same year, and lost his infant son Ibrahim. Yet he did not curse his persecutors, and at Makkah he forgave. His patience was not silence: he worked, planned and prayed.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** key teachings from the Final Sermon. **[4]**

> One teaching is that people are equal in the sight of Allah [1]: an Arab has no superiority over a non-Arab, or a white person over a black person, except through taqwa, so tribal and racial pride has no place in Islam [1]. A second teaching is that life, property and honour are sacred [1], so Muslims must not harm one another and must return trusts to their owners, which protects society and builds trust [1].

## Using Qur'an 5:3 in a 12-mark answer

A statement such as "The most important part of the Prophet's (pbuh) legacy is that the religion was completed" can be examined with the passage. Agree by quoting "I have perfected for you your religion" and showing that the Qur'an and Sunnah guide every area of life. Challenge by showing that behaviour, such as justice, honesty and kindness to parents, is also a legacy and that guidance means little if it is not lived. Then reach a judgement: the completed message and the lived example belong together.

## Common mistakes

- **Describing the sermon as a list of rules only.** Say why each teaching matters.
- **Quoting the sermon word for word.** Summarise the content; the wording varies between reports.
- **Forgetting that parents, justice, honesty and patience are named.** Be ready with a teaching and an example for each.
- **Treating 5:3 as only about food.** The key phrase is the completion of the religion.
- **Giving an example without saying what it teaches.** Always link the event to the lesson.
