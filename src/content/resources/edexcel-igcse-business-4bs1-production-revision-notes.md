Quick recall for section 5.2 Production of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, points 5.2.1 to 5.2.3. Worked comparisons of the three processes, productivity, JIT savings and a robot decision are in the [production study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-production/); questions are in the [production practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-production-practice/). Figures are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The specification wording

- **5.2.1 Production processes:** job, batch, flow; labour and capital intensive production; the impact of different types of production; calculation of productivity and the impact of productivity improvements.
- **5.2.2 Lean production:** just-in-time (JIT); Kaizen; the importance of using resources effectively.
- **5.2.3 Technology in production:** use of robotics; balancing cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Job production | Making single, often unique, items to a customer's order |
| Batch production | Making a set quantity of identical items, then switching to a different item |
| Flow production | Continuous production of identical items, often on an assembly line |
| Labour intensive | Using a high proportion of labour relative to machinery |
| Capital intensive | Using a high proportion of machinery and technology relative to labour |
| Productivity | Output per unit of input, usually output per worker or per worker-hour |
| Lean production | Producing with as little waste of time, materials, space and money as possible |
| Just-in-time (JIT) | Receiving materials only when needed, so little inventory is held |
| Kaizen | Continuous improvement through many small changes, often suggested by workers |
| Robotics | Programmable machines carrying out tasks such as welding, picking or packing |
| Flexibility | The ability to change products, volumes or tasks easily |

## 3. The three processes compared

| | Job | Batch | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|
| Volume | Low | Medium | High |
| Variety | High | Some | Low |
| Unit cost | High | Medium | Low at high volume |
| Capital needed | Low | Medium | High |
| Flexibility | High | Medium (changeover time) | Low |
| Worker experience | Skilled, varied | Semi-skilled | Repetitive |
| Example | Wedding cake, bespoke suit | Bakery runs of bread types | Bottled drinks line |

**Decision rule:** match the process to demand. High, steady demand for one design suits flow; several designs in moderate numbers suit batch; one-off orders suit job.

**Cost per unit with set-up costs:** cost per unit = variable cost per unit + (set-up or fixed cost ÷ number of units). A big fixed cost only becomes cheap per unit at high volume.

## 4. Productivity

- **Labour productivity = output ÷ number of workers** (or ÷ worker-hours). Standard form; the specification gives no formula.
- Percentage change = (new − old) ÷ old × 100.
- Labour cost per unit = wage rate ÷ output per worker (for the same period).

**Worked reminder.** 12 workers make 1,440 units a day: 1,440 ÷ 12 = **120** per worker. After training they make 1,800: 1,800 ÷ 12 = **150** per worker, an increase of (150 − 120) ÷ 120 × 100 = **25%**.

**Impact of improvement:** lower labour cost per unit → lower prices or higher profit; more output without more staff. Check quality has not fallen.

**Ways to raise productivity:** training, better equipment, Kaizen, better layout, motivation methods.

## 5. Lean production

| Method | Benefit | Risk or requirement |
|---|---|---|
| JIT | Less money tied up in stock; lower storage and damage costs; less waste | Late delivery stops production; needs reliable suppliers and accurate forecasts |
| Kaizen | Steady gains in efficiency and quality; involves staff | Needs time and supportive managers; each gain is small |
| Using resources effectively | Lower unit costs; frees cash; less environmental harm | Needs monitoring and staff commitment |

**JIT reminder.** Holding cost 15% a year: stock of 15,000 costs 2,250 a year; with JIT stock of 3,000 it costs **450**, a saving of 1,800, plus 12,000 of cash released.

## 6. Robotics: the four-way balance

| Factor | For robots | Against robots |
|---|---|---|
| Cost | Lower labour cost per unit | High purchase, set-up and maintenance cost |
| Productivity | Fast; can run long hours | Breakdowns stop output |
| Quality | Consistent; fewer errors | A programming fault repeats on every unit |
| Flexibility | Can be reprogrammed | Less adaptable than people for varied or custom work |

Also consider the effect on workers: redundancy, retraining, morale.

## 7. Exam traps

- Saying job production is cheap. It has the highest unit cost.
- Forgetting changeover time is the main drawback of batch production.
- Dividing output by wages or cost to find productivity.
- Giving productivity without units or a time period.
- Writing "JIT means no stock".
- Describing Kaizen as one major reorganisation.
- Recommending robots on cost alone without quality and flexibility.
- Choosing flow production for a business with low or varied demand.

## 8. Answer frames

- **Explain** one impact of flow production: low cost per unit → lower prices → higher sales, or repetitive work → lower motivation → more errors.
- **Analyse** JIT for a case: saving on stock → risk from suppliers in the case → effect on production and customers.
- **Justify** a process: demand level and variety in the case → cost per unit at that volume → flexibility and risk → decision.

## Quick self-test

1. Which process suits a tailor making suits to measure?
2. Give one disadvantage of batch production.
3. Give one advantage of flow production.
4. 20 workers make 3,000 units a week. Calculate productivity.
5. After changes, the same 20 workers make 3,300 units. Calculate the percentage increase in productivity.
6. Define just-in-time.
7. Give one risk of JIT.
8. What is Kaizen?
9. Give one reason why using resources effectively matters.
10. Name the four factors the specification says must be balanced when using technology.
11. A set-up cost of 1,500 is spread over 300 units with a variable cost of 9 per unit. Calculate cost per unit.
12. Why might a business with many product designs avoid flow production?

### Answers

1. Job production.
2. Time and cost lost when changing equipment between runs, or stock building up between runs.
3. Lowest cost per unit at high volume, so prices can be low.
4. 3,000 ÷ 20 = 150 units per worker per week.
5. New productivity 3,300 ÷ 20 = 165; (165 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 = 10%.
6. A system in which materials arrive just when they are needed for production, so little inventory is held.
7. A late delivery can stop production (or a sudden rise in demand cannot be met from stock).
8. Continuous improvement through many small changes, often suggested by workers.
9. Waste raises cost per unit, so using resources well keeps costs and prices down (or frees cash, or reduces environmental harm).
10. Cost, productivity, quality and flexibility.
11. 9 + 1,500 ÷ 300 = 9 + 5 = 14.
12. A flow line is costly to change between products, so frequent switching would waste time and money.
