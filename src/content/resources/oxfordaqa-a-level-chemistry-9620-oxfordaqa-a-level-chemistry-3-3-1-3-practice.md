These questions cover **3.3.1.3 Isomerism** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. Section A has short recall and application questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. ## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Which compound shows E–Z isomerism?

A: but-1-ene

B: 2-methylpropene

C: pent-2-ene

D: 2-methylbut-2-ene **[1]**

**2.** Define the term stereoisomers. **[1]**

**3.** Name the type of structural isomerism shown by each pair.

**(a)** pentan-2-one and pentan-3-one **[1]**

**(b)** hexane and 2,3-dimethylbutane **[1]**

**(c)** but-2-ene and cyclobutane **[1]**

**4.** Explain why 1,1-dichloroethene, CH₂=CCl₂, does not show E–Z isomerism. **[1]**

**5.** Use the CIP rules to state which group has the higher priority in each pair.

**(a)** –CH₂OH and –CH₂CH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** –Cl and –CH₂Br **[1]**

**6.** Give the structural formula of a position isomer of pentan-2-ol. **[1]**

### Section B

**7.** There are five structural isomers of C₅H₁₀ that are alkenes.

**(a)** Name the two isomers that have an unbranched carbon chain. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the three isomers that have a branched carbon chain. **[3]**

**(c)** One of the five isomers shows E–Z isomerism. Draw its E and Z isomers and label each. **[2]**

**8.** 3-Methylpent-2-ene has the structural formula CH₃CH=C(CH₃)CH₂CH₃.

**(a)** Explain why it shows E–Z isomerism. **[1]**

**(b)** Use the CIP rules to identify the higher-priority group on C2 and on C3. Explain your choice for C3. **[2]**

**(c)** Draw the Z isomer. **[1]**

**9.** An alkene has this arrangement of groups around its C=C double bond.

```
      Cl          CH(CH₃)₂
        \        /
         C ==== C
        /        \
  CH₃CH₂          CH₂OH
```

**(a)** Identify the higher-priority group on the left-hand carbon. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the higher-priority group on the right-hand carbon, and explain why it has priority although the other group contains more carbon atoms. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce whether this is the E or the Z isomer. **[1]**

**10.** Four alcohols have the molecular formula C₄H₁₀O.

**(a)** Name the four alcohols. **[4]**

**(b)** From your answer, give one pair of chain isomers and one pair of position isomers. **[2]**

**11.** This question is about why E–Z isomerism exists.

**(a)** Explain why the two isomers of 1,2-dichloroethene cannot be converted into each other at room temperature. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why 1,2-dichloroethane, CH₂ClCH₂Cl, does not exist as two stereoisomers of this kind. **[1]**

**12.** Compounds R, S and T all have the molecular formula C₄H₈O. R is a ketone. S and T are aldehydes; S has a branched chain and T does not.

**(a)** Name R. **[1]**

**(b)** Name S and T, and state the type of isomerism they show. **[2]**

**(c)** State the type of isomerism shown by R and T. **[1]**

**(d)** Compound U, also C₄H₈O, is an unbranched alcohol with a C=C bond between C2 and C3 and the –OH group on C1. Give its structural formula and explain why it has E–Z isomers. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** C [1]

**2.** Compounds with the same structural formula but a different arrangement of atoms in space [1]

**3.**

**(a)** Position isomerism [1]

**(b)** Chain isomerism [1]

**(c)** Functional group isomerism [1]

**Total: 3**

**4.** One carbon of the double bond carries two identical atoms (two H on one carbon, or two Cl on the other), so swapping groups does not give a different compound [1]

**5.**

**(a)** –CH₂OH: the first atoms are both C, and at the next position O (8) beats C (6) [1]

**(b)** –Cl: Cl (17) beats C (6) at the first atom; the Br further out does not count [1]

**Total: 2**

*Examiner insight:* In (b) the comparison stops at the first point of difference, which is the attached atom itself.

**6.** CH₃CH₂CH(OH)CH₂CH₃ (pentan-3-ol) or CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂OH (pentan-1-ol) [1]

### Section B

**7.**

**(a)** Pent-1-ene [1]; pent-2-ene [1]

**(b)** 2-methylbut-1-ene [1]; 3-methylbut-1-ene [1]; 2-methylbut-2-ene [1]

**(c)** Pent-2-ene drawn with CH₃ and CH₂CH₃ on opposite sides of C=C, labelled E [1]; with CH₃ and CH₂CH₃ on the same side, labelled Z [1]

**Total: 7**

*Examiner insight:* "Methylbutene" without locants scores nothing in (b): there are three different methylbutenes.

**8.**

**(a)** Rotation about C=C is restricted, and each carbon of the double bond carries two different groups (C2: CH₃ and H; C3: CH₃ and CH₂CH₃) [1]

**(b)** C2: CH₃ has priority over H [1]; C3: CH₂CH₃ has priority over CH₃, because both attach through C, and at the next position (C, H, H) beats (H, H, H) [1]

**(c)** CH₃ on C2 and CH₂CH₃ on C3 on the same side of the double bond [1]

**Total: 4**

**9.**

**(a)** Cl (atomic number 17 beats 6) [1]

**(b)** CH₂OH [1]; both groups attach through carbon, and at the next position CH₂OH has (O, H, H) while CH(CH₃)₂ has (C, C, H); O (8) beats C (6) at the first difference, so the number of carbons does not matter [1]

**(c)** Cl is on the upper side and CH₂OH on the lower side, so they are on opposite sides: E [1]

**Total: 4**

**10.**

**(a)** Butan-1-ol [1]; butan-2-ol [1]; 2-methylpropan-1-ol [1]; 2-methylpropan-2-ol [1]

**(b)** Chain: butan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-1-ol (or butan-2-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol) [1]; position: butan-1-ol and butan-2-ol (or 2-methylpropan-1-ol and 2-methylpropan-2-ol) [1]

**Total: 6**

**11.**

**(a)** Rotation about the C=C bond is restricted [1]; turning one carbon would break the π bond (the sideways overlap of p orbitals), which needs much more energy than is available at room temperature [1]

**(b)** There is free rotation about the C–C single bond, so all arrangements interconvert and are the same compound [1]

**Total: 3**

**12.**

**(a)** Butanone [1]

**(b)** S: 2-methylpropanal; T: butanal [1]; chain isomerism [1]

**(c)** Functional group isomerism [1]

**(d)** CH₃CH=CHCH₂OH (but-2-en-1-ol) [1]; C2 carries CH₃ and H, and C3 carries CH₂OH and H, so each carbon of the restricted C=C has two different groups [1]

**Total: 6**

*Examiner insight:* For an E–Z explanation, name the two different groups on each carbon. "It has a double bond" alone does not score.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions without "molecular formula" or "structural formula".
- Names without locants, so different isomers cannot be told apart.
- Ranking CIP groups by size or by the number of carbons.
- Drawing E–Z isomers with unclear geometry.
- Forgetting cyclic and branched isomers in "all isomers" questions.
