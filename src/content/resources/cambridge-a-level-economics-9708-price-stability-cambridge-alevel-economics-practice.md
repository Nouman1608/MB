## Section A

**1.** The inflation rate in a country falls from 6% to 3%. What has happened?

A: Deflation: the price level has fallen

B: Disinflation: the price level is rising more slowly

C: The price level has halved

D: Real incomes must have fallen

**[1]**

**2.** Which is most likely to cause cost-push inflation?

A: A cut in income tax

B: A rise in consumer confidence

C: A rise in the world price of imported fuel

D: An increase in government spending on roads

**[1]**

**3.** Define deflation. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the categories in a consumer price index are given weights. **[2]**

**5.** Distinguish between nominal and real values. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why borrowers with fixed repayments may gain from unanticipated inflation. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Country T's CPI (Year 0 = 100) is shown below.

| Year 0 | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | Year 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 100.0 | 104.0 | 106.6 | 107.1 | 106.0 |

**(a)** Calculate the inflation rate in Year 2 and in Year 4, to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the year or years of disinflation and the year of deflation, explaining your answer. **[3]**

**8.** In an invented CPI, the weights and price changes over a year are as follows.

| Category | Weight | Price change |
|---|---|---|
| Housing | 0.30 | +4% |
| Food | 0.25 | +10% |
| Transport | 0.20 | -3% |
| Other | 0.25 | +2% |

**(a)** Calculate the rate of inflation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the 10% rise in food prices raised the index by much less than 10%. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a low-income household might experience a higher inflation rate than the CPI shows. **[2]**

**9.** A worker's nominal weekly wage rises from 600 cu to 630 cu while the CPI rises from 100 to 107.

**(a)** Calculate the real wage in the second year, in base-year prices, and the percentage change in the real wage. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what has happened to the worker's purchasing power. **[1]**

**10.** *Original extract:* "Country V's currency lost 20% of its value against the US dollar. Prices of imported machinery parts and fuel rose sharply. Factory output fell for three quarters in a row, yet consumer prices rose 7%."

**(a)** Identify the type of inflation and explain your answer using evidence from the extract. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain, using AD/AS analysis, why output fell while prices rose. **[3]**

**11.** Explain two consequences of high inflation for firms. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why the harm caused by inflation depends on whether it is anticipated. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. The inflation rate is still positive, so prices rise, but more slowly.

**2.** **C** [1]. Dearer fuel raises firms' costs and shifts SRAS left; the other options raise AD.

**3.** A **sustained fall** [1] in the **general price level**, so that the inflation rate is negative [1].

**4.** Households spend **different shares** of their income on different items [1], so each category must affect the index **in proportion to its importance** in spending; a price change in rent matters more than one in matches [1].

**5.** **Nominal** values are measured in the **prices of the time** [1]; **real** values are **adjusted for changes in the price level**, so they show changes in quantity or purchasing power [1].

**6.** Their repayments are **fixed in money terms** [1], so when prices and usually incomes rise, the **real value of the debt and of each repayment falls** [1].

**7. (a)** Year 2: (106.6 - 104.0) / 104.0 x 100 = **2.5%** [1]. Year 4: (106.0 - 107.1) / 107.1 x 100 = **-1.0%** [1].

**(b)** Year 1 inflation is 4.0%, Year 2 is 2.5% and Year 3 is (107.1 - 106.6) / 106.6 x 100 = 0.5% [1]. **Years 2 and 3 show disinflation**: inflation fell but stayed positive, so prices still rose [1]. **Year 4 shows deflation**: the index fell, so the inflation rate is negative [1].

**8. (a)** 0.30 x 4 + 0.25 x 10 + 0.20 x (-3) + 0.25 x 2 = 1.2 + 2.5 - 0.6 + 0.5 [1] = **3.6%** [1].

**(b)** Food has a **weight of 0.25**, so its price rise contributes only 0.25 x 10 = 2.5 percentage points [1]. Other categories rose less or fell, so the **weighted average** is much lower than 10% [1].

**(c)** A low-income household spends a **larger share of its income on food** than the weight in the index [1], so the 10% food price rise affects it more and its personal inflation rate is above the CPI average [1].

**9. (a)** Real wage = 630 / 107 x 100 = **588.8 cu** [1]. Change = (588.8 - 600) / 600 x 100 = **-1.9%** [1].

**(b)** Purchasing power has **fallen**: prices rose by 7%, faster than the 5% rise in the nominal wage, so the wage buys less [1].

**10. (a)** **Cost-push inflation** [1]. The fall in the currency raised the **prices of imported inputs** such as machinery parts and fuel, increasing firms' costs [1]. Output **fell** while prices rose, which matches cost-push rather than demand-pull [1].

**(b)** Higher costs shift the **short-run aggregate supply curve to the left** [1]. With AD unchanged, the new equilibrium has a **higher price level** [1] and a **lower level of real output**, so employment also falls [1].

**11.** **Menu costs** [1]: firms must spend time and money changing price lists, catalogues and systems more often [1]. **Uncertainty** [1]: when future costs and prices are hard to predict, firms may delay or cancel investment projects, reducing future capacity [1]. (Also creditworthy: loss of international competitiveness for exporters if domestic inflation is higher than abroad.)

**12.** If inflation is **anticipated**, wages, pensions and interest rates can be **adjusted in advance** to keep their real value [1], so little purchasing power is redistributed [1]. If it is **unanticipated**, contracts are based on wrong expectations, so savers, lenders and fixed-income earners lose and borrowers gain unexpectedly, and planning becomes harder [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling Year 2 or Year 3 in Question 7 "deflation" because the rate fell.
- Forgetting to weight each category in Question 8.
- Treating the nominal rise in Question 9 as a rise in living standards.
- Shifting AD instead of SRAS in Question 10(b).
