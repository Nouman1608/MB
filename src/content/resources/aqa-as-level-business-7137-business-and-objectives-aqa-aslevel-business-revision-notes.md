Condensed notes on section **3.1.1 Business and objectives** of AQA AS Business (7137), specification Version 1.1 (November 2025), for first teaching from September 2026. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [business and objectives study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-7137-business-and-objectives-aqa-aslevel-business/); exam-style questions are in the [business and objectives practice set](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-7137-business-and-objectives-aqa-aslevel-business-practice/).

## The three content areas

1. **Entrepreneurs**: reasons for setting up; characteristics; challenges; changing role as the business grows.
2. **Business planning and decisions**: business plans; competitiveness; influences on decisions; risks and rewards; ethical dilemmas.
3. **Business objectives**: purpose and value; SMART.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Entrepreneur | Person who identifies an opportunity and takes the risk of setting up a business |
| Financial reason | A money motive for starting a business, such as higher income |
| Non-financial reason | A motive not based on money, such as independence or passion |
| Business plan | Document setting out the business idea, market, strategy and financial forecasts |
| Competitiveness | Ability of a business to win and keep customers against rivals |
| Opportunity cost | The next best alternative given up when a decision is made |
| Risk | The chance that an outcome is worse than expected |
| Reward | The potential gain from a decision |
| Ethical dilemma | A choice between what is profitable and what is morally right |
| SMART | Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific |

## Entrepreneurs

**Reasons:** financial (income, building a saleable asset, profit, escaping insecure work); non-financial (independence, passion, flexibility, solving a problem).

**The six characteristics in the specification:** risk taker, resilient, focused, passionate, innovative, adaptable. Each helps with a challenge; each can become a weakness if taken too far.

**Challenges:** raising finance with no record; winning customers from known brands; cash flow; skill gaps; workload and uncertain income.

**Changing role:** doing everything → managing and delegating → leading and setting strategy → possibly stepping back. The risk is failing to delegate.

## Business plans

- **Purpose:** test the idea; persuade lenders and investors; set objectives and a benchmark.
- **Value is limited by:** guesswork in forecasts for new businesses; plans going out of date; false confidence.
- Most useful when reviewed and updated.

## Competitiveness

- **Price** competitiveness: lower costs allow lower prices.
- **Non-price** competitiveness: quality, design, service, brand, convenience.
- **Importance:** sales, margins, growth and survival.

## Influences on decisions: one question each

| Influence | Ask |
|---|---|
| Objectives | Does it fit the aim? |
| Risk | What could go wrong, how likely, how serious? |
| Reward | What is the potential gain? |
| Resources | Is there enough finance, staff, skill, time? |
| Market conditions | Is demand growing; how strong are rivals? |
| Ethics | Is it right; how will stakeholders react? |
| Opportunity cost | What is given up? |

**Risk and reward:** higher reward usually means higher risk; attitude to risk depends on the decision maker, the money at stake and the consequences of failure.

**Profit versus ethics:** ethical choices may raise short-term costs but protect reputation, loyalty and legal standing.

## SMART objectives

| Letter | Meaning | Check |
|---|---|---|
| S | Specific | Is it clear exactly what is to be achieved? |
| M | Measurable | Is there a number to check against? |
| A | Accountable | Is someone responsible for it? |
| R | Realistic | Can it be done with the resources and market? |
| T | Time specific | Is there a deadline? |

**Value of objectives:** direction, coordination, motivation, measurement of performance, a basis for decisions.

## Command words and tariffs (AS)

Define 2; Explain 4; Analyse 6; Assess 9; Evaluate 15, as listed in the specification.

## Exam traps

- In AQA's SMART, **A = Accountable**, not Achievable.
- "Risk taker" alone is not an explanation: link it to the case.
- A business plan does not guarantee success.
- Opportunity cost is the **next best** alternative, not everything given up.
- Assess and Evaluate answers need a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Give two financial and two non-financial reasons for starting a business.
2. Name the six characteristics of entrepreneurs listed in the specification.
3. How can being a risk taker become a weakness?
4. Describe how an entrepreneur's role changes as the business grows.
5. Give two purposes of a business plan.
6. Give one limitation of a business plan for a new business.
7. Define competitiveness.
8. A business chooses to spend £30,000 on advertising instead of a new machine. What is the opportunity cost?
9. List the seven influences on business decisions named in the specification.
10. Give an example of a profit versus ethics dilemma.
11. What does each letter of SMART stand for in AQA's specification?
12. Make this objective SMART: "We want happier customers."

### Answers

1. Financial: any two of higher income, building a saleable asset, profit, escaping insecure work. Non-financial: any two of independence, passion, flexibility, solving a problem.
2. Risk taker, resilient, focused, passionate, innovative, adaptable.
3. Taking on too much borrowing or too many uncertain projects can lead to debts the business cannot repay.
4. From doing every task, to managing and delegating, to leading and setting strategy, and possibly stepping back.
5. Any two: testing the idea; persuading lenders and investors; setting objectives and a benchmark.
6. Sales forecasts are often guesses, so the plan may be inaccurate (or it goes out of date quickly).
7. The ability of a business to win and keep customers against its rivals.
8. The new machine and the benefits it would have brought.
9. Objectives, risk, reward, resources, market conditions, ethics, opportunity costs.
10. For example, buying from a cheaper supplier that uses poor working conditions.
11. Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific.
12. For example: "The customer service manager will raise the average satisfaction score from 3.8 to 4.3 out of 5 by 31 December."
