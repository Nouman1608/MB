**These notes condense Section 8 Population and employment of Cambridge IGCSE Pakistan Studies 0448
Paper 2.** Learn the definitions, how to read a population pyramid and growth graph, the push and
pull factors for each type of migration, why density varies, and the causes and effects of
unemployment and underemployment.

## Syllabus coverage

**0448 Paper 2, Section 8 (2026 syllabus)**
- **8(a)** Population pyramids; effects of structure; birth, death and natural increase data; factors in growth; problems and solutions
- **8(b)** Rural–urban, seasonal, emigration, immigration and refugees; shanty towns, tent cities, rural depopulation; self-help and service provision
- **8(c)** Distribution versus density; variations between and within provinces; physical, economic, social and political factors
- **8(d)** Primary, secondary, tertiary jobs; unemployment and underemployment; skilled and manual labour; GNP and GDP; literacy, education and training

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Birth rate | Live births per 1,000 people per year |
| Death rate | Deaths per 1,000 people per year |
| Natural increase | Birth rate minus death rate |
| Population pyramid | Bar chart of population by age group and sex |
| Dependency | Young (under 15) and old (over 65) people supported by the working-age group |
| Migration | Movement of people from one place to live in another |
| Push factor | Something that makes people want to leave |
| Pull factor | Something that attracts people to a place |
| Seasonal migration | Temporary movement for part of the year |
| Emigration / immigration | Leaving / entering a country to live |
| Refugee | Person forced to leave their country by war or persecution |
| Katchi abadi | Unplanned shanty settlement in a Pakistani city |
| Distribution | Pattern of where people live |
| Density | People per square kilometre |
| Underemployment | Having some work but not enough to be fully employed |
| Remittances | Money sent home by workers abroad |

## Reading a pyramid: three-step method

1. **Describe the shape:** wide base, narrow top = young, fast-growing population.
2. **Quote data:** state a percentage or bar value for an age group.
3. **Explain and link:** high birth rate, falling death rate; then the effect, such as the need for schools and jobs.

## Factors in population growth

- **Social:** early marriage, large families valued, son preference.
- **Educational:** low female literacy; low awareness of family planning.
- **Economic:** children as labour and old-age security.
- **Political:** uneven family planning programmes.
- **Health:** vaccination and medical care reduce deaths, especially of infants.

**Solutions:** female education, family planning, mother and child health care, development.

## Migration summary

| Type | Push | Pull | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rural–urban | Poverty, few jobs, poor services | Jobs, schools, hospitals | Katchi abadis; rural depopulation |
| Seasonal | Slack farm season, winter cold | Harvest jobs, urban winter work | Temporary labour supply |
| Emigration | Unemployment | High wages abroad (Gulf, UK) | Remittances; loss of skilled workers |
| Refugees | War (Afghanistan since 1979) | Safety | Tent cities; pressure on services |

**Self-help example:** Orangi Pilot Project, Karachi, where residents built their own sewers.

## Distribution and density

- Punjab: largest population; high density on irrigated plains.
- Sindh: very high density in Karachi and along the Indus; low in the Thar.
- Khyber Pakhtunkhwa: dense in the Peshawar valley; sparse in mountains.
- Balochistan: largest area, smallest population share, lowest density.
- Northern areas: people concentrated in valleys; mountains almost empty.

**Factors:** physical (relief, water, soil, climate), economic (jobs, irrigation, industry), social
(services), political (government investment, security).

## Employment

- **Primary:** largest single share, falling.
- **Secondary:** manufacturing and construction, growing.
- **Tertiary:** services, growing fastest in cities.
- **Rural underemployment:** seasonal farm work, small holdings, mechanisation.
- **Urban unemployment:** migrants outnumber jobs; lack of skills.
- **Effects:** lower GNP and GDP, poverty, crime, emigration of skilled workers.
- **Skills gap:** surplus manual labour, shortage of technicians, managers and professionals.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain why underemployment is common in rural areas of Pakistan. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Farm work is seasonal, so labourers are busy at sowing and harvest but idle at
other times [1]. Holdings are small and fragmented, so a family has more workers than the land
needs [1]. Mechanisation, such as tractors and threshers, has reduced the need for labour [1]. Few
non-farm jobs exist in villages because there is little industry [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing up distribution and density.** Distribution is pattern; density is a number per km².
- **Describing a pyramid without data.** Quote figures from the resource when asked to describe.
- **Treating all migration as rural–urban.** Learn push and pull factors for each type.
- **Forgetting adverse outcomes.** The syllabus asks you to evaluate solutions, including poor housing.
- **Saying unemployment and underemployment are the same.** Underemployed people have some work.

## How is this section examined?

This content is examined on Cambridge Paper 2, The environment of Pakistan (1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, 50% of the qualification). You answer three questions from a choice of five; each is worth 25 marks and has four parts, (a) to (d), built around resources such as maps, graphs, tables and photographs. Part (d) specifically tests analysis and evaluation, so finish every revision topic by practising a judgement: for example, how sustainable a resource or a policy is, with evidence for and against.

## Frequently asked questions

**What does a wide-based pyramid mean for Pakistan?** A large young population, high dependency and
heavy demand for schools, health care and future jobs.

**Why does Balochistan have such low density?** Its plateaux, mountains and deserts, low rainfall
and limited water make farming and settlement difficult.
