Quick-recall notes for **3.2.4 Research methods** in Paper 2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE
Psychology (9218). The specification gives this topic the same content as Paper 1's 3.1.4, including
data handling. These notes condense non-experimental methods and data handling; experiments,
sampling and controls are condensed in the
[Research Methods (Paper 1) revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-research-methods-revision-notes/).
For full explanations, see the
[Research Methods (Paper 2) study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-research-methods-paper2/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Closed question** | Fixed-choice answer; gives quantitative data |
| **Open question** | Answered in the respondent's own words; gives qualitative data |
| **Structured interview** | Same questions in the same order for everyone |
| **Unstructured interview** | Conversation-style; follow-up questions allowed |
| **Social desirability bias** | Answering to look good rather than truthfully |
| **Case study** | In-depth study of one person or small group |
| **Behaviour categories** | Clear, observable actions used to record behaviour |
| **Inter-observer reliability** | The extent to which two observers' records agree |
| **Co-variables** | The two variables measured in a correlation |
| **Primary data** | Collected first-hand by the researcher |
| **Secondary data** | Collected by someone else, used again |
| **Normal distribution** | Symmetrical bell curve; mean, median and mode in the centre |

## Methods at a glance

| Method | Best for | Main strength | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Large samples; attitudes | Quick; easy to compare | Social desirability; little detail |
| Structured interview | Comparing many people | Easy to repeat | Less flexible |
| Unstructured interview | Personal experiences | Rich detail | Hard to compare; time-consuming |
| Case study | Rare or unusual cases | Depth | Cannot generalise; subjective |
| Observation | What people actually do | Real behaviour | People change if watched; reasons unseen |
| Correlation | Variables that cannot be manipulated | Shows relationships | No cause and effect |

## Inter-observer reliability in four steps

1. Agree clear behaviour categories.
2. Two observers watch the same behaviour at the same time.
3. Each records independently.
4. Compare tallies; high agreement = reliable.

## Correlation

- **Positive:** both rise together. **Negative:** one rises as the other falls. **None:** no pattern.
- Plot on a **scatter diagram**, one point per person.
- Always mention the possibility of a **third variable** when evaluating.

## Quantitative versus qualitative

| | Quantitative | Qualitative |
|---|---|---|
| Form | Numbers | Words, descriptions |
| From | Closed questions, tallies, scores | Open questions, interviews, case notes |
| Strength | Easy to analyse; more reliable | Detailed; often more valid |
| Weakness | Lacks depth | Hard to analyse; subjective |

## Data handling formulas and rules

| Measure | How | When to use |
|---|---|---|
| Mean | Add all scores, divide by number of scores | Data without extreme scores |
| Median | Middle score after ordering (average the two middle scores if even) | Data with extreme scores |
| Mode | Most frequent score | Categories, or to show the most common response |
| Range | Highest minus lowest | Quick measure of spread |

- **Percentage** = (part ÷ whole) × 100.
- **Ratio:** simplify by dividing both sides by the same number (12:8 = 3:2).
- **Significant figures:** round from the first non-zero digit (0.04672 to two significant figures is
  0.047).

## Choosing a method for a Paper 2 aim

| Research aim | Suitable method | Why |
|---|---|---|
| How common feelings of isolation are among teenagers | Questionnaire | Large sample, quick, anonymous |
| What it is like to live with a rare disorder | Case study or unstructured interview | Depth and detail |
| How often passers-by help someone who drops their shopping | Observation (with categories) | Records actual behaviour |
| Whether stress scores rise with hours of work | Correlation | Variables cannot be manipulated ethically |

## Exam traps

- Correlation does **not** mean causation.
- A behaviour category must be **observable**: "smiles" works, "feels happy" does not.
- **Order** the scores before finding the median.
- A histogram has **no gaps**; a bar chart has gaps.
- When asked for a strength **in this study**, link it to the scenario's topic.
- Secondary data is not "worse": it is quicker and cheaper, but may not fit the aim.

## Self-test

1. What kind of data do closed questions produce?
2. Give one weakness of unstructured interviews.
3. Why are case studies suitable for studying rare disorders?
4. Write one observable behaviour category for a study of helping in a street.
5. How is inter-observer reliability checked?
6. What type of correlation is shown if anxiety falls as hours of sleep rise?
7. Give one reason a correlation cannot show cause and effect.
8. Find the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 5.
9. Find the mean and range of 12, 15, 9, 14.
10. Which graph should show the number of people choosing each of four categories?
11. Give two features of a normal distribution.
12. Give one advantage of secondary data.

**Answers:**
1. Quantitative data.
2. Answers are hard to compare between participants (accept: time-consuming; interviewer may
   influence answers).
3. They allow in-depth study of cases that are too rare to find in large numbers and could not be
   created ethically.
4. For example, "stops walking", "picks up the dropped item" or "speaks to the person".
5. Two observers record the same behaviour independently using the same categories and compare
   their records for agreement.
6. Negative correlation.
7. A third variable could cause both (accept: we do not know which variable affects which).
8. Ordered: 2, 4, 5, 7, 9; median = 5.
9. Mean = 50 ÷ 4 = 12.5; range = 15 − 9 = 6.
10. A bar chart.
11. Any two: symmetrical; bell-shaped; most scores near the middle; mean, median and mode at the
    centre.
12. It is quick and cheap to obtain (accept: may cover a large population).
