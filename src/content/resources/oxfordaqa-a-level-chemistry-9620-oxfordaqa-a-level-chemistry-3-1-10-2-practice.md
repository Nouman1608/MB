These questions cover **3.1.10.2 Definition and determination of pH** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3. Give pH values to 2 decimal places. Treat HCl, HBr, HI and HNO₃ as completely dissociated.

## Section A

**1.** Define pH. **[1]**

**2.** Explain why a logarithmic scale is used to measure acidity. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the pH of a solution in which [H⁺] = 8.2 × 10⁻⁶ mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**4.** Calculate [H⁺] in a solution of pH 3.85. **[1]**

**5.** Calculate the pH of 0.150 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**6.** State how many times greater [H⁺] is in a solution of pH 1.50 than in a solution of pH 4.50. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** 0.959 g of hydrogen iodide (Mr = 127.9) is dissolved in water and made up to 150 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of HI. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of H⁺ ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the pH. **[1]**

**8.** 10.0 cm³ of 0.800 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid is diluted.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of the acid before dilution. **[1]**

**(b)** The acid is made up to 500 cm³ with water. Calculate the new pH. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of water that should be added to the original 10.0 cm³ to give a solution of pH 2.10. **[2]**

**9.** 40.0 cm³ of 0.250 mol dm⁻³ HCl is mixed with 60.0 cm³ of 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ HBr.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of the mixture. **[3]**

**(b)** A student instead averages the pH values of the two acids. Explain why this method is wrong. **[1]**

**10.** A technician needs 250 cm³ of hydrochloric acid of pH 1.85 and has a stock solution of 2.00 mol dm⁻³ HCl.

**(a)** Calculate [H⁺] in the required solution. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of HCl needed. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the volume of stock solution that must be diluted to 250 cm³. **[1]**

**11.** A question tells you to assume that sulfuric acid is completely dissociated.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of 0.0300 mol dm⁻³ H₂SO₄ on this assumption. **[2]**

**(b)** The measured pH is slightly higher. Suggest why. **[1]**

**12.** Explain why a pH meter, rather than universal indicator, is used when pH values to 2 decimal places are needed, and state what must be done before using the meter. **[2]**

**13.** A student calculates that 1.0 × 10⁻⁹ mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid has pH 9.00. Explain why this answer must be wrong and state what the pH would be close to. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** pH = −log₁₀[H⁺], where [H⁺] is the hydrogen ion concentration in mol dm⁻³ [1]

**2.** [H⁺] varies over a very wide range (many powers of ten), so a log scale gives manageable numbers [1]

**3.** pH = −log₁₀(8.2 × 10⁻⁶) = **5.09** [1]

**4.** [H⁺] = 10^(−3.85) = **1.41 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³** [1]

**5.** [H⁺] = 0.150 mol dm⁻³; pH = **0.82** [1]

**6.** **1000 times** (three pH units, 10³) [1]

**7. (a)** n = 0.959 ÷ 127.9 = **7.50 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = 7.50 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.150 = **0.0500 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(c)** pH = −log₁₀(0.0500) = **1.30** [1]

**8. (a)** pH = −log₁₀(0.800) = **0.10** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = 0.800 × 10.0 ÷ 500 = 0.0160 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.80** [1]

**(c)** Required [H⁺] = 10^(−2.10) = 7.94 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³; total volume = (0.800 × 10.0) ÷ 7.94 × 10⁻³ = 1.01 × 10³ cm³ [1]; water to add = 1007 − 10 ≈ **997 cm³** (about 1.00 dm³) [1]
*Examiner insight:* The question asks for the water added, not the final volume. Subtract the 10.0 cm³ of acid.

**9. (a)** n(H⁺) = (0.250 × 0.0400) + (0.0400 × 0.0600) = 0.0100 + 0.00240 = 0.0124 mol [1]; [H⁺] = 0.0124 ÷ 0.100 = 0.124 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **0.91** [1]

**(b)** pH is a logarithmic quantity, so it does not add or average like amounts; the moles of H⁺ must be combined (averaging gives 1.00, which is wrong) [1]

**10. (a)** [H⁺] = 10^(−1.85) = **1.41 × 10⁻² mol dm⁻³** [1]

**(b)** n = 1.41 × 10⁻² × 0.250 = **3.53 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]

**(c)** V = 3.53 × 10⁻³ ÷ 2.00 = 1.77 × 10⁻³ dm³ = **1.77 cm³** [1]

**11. (a)** [H⁺] = 2 × 0.0300 = 0.0600 mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **1.22** [1]

**(b)** The second proton transfer (from HSO₄⁻) is not complete, so [H⁺] is less than 0.0600 mol dm⁻³ [1]

**12.** Universal indicator gives only an approximate pH from a colour, while a meter gives a numerical reading to 2 decimal places [1]; the meter must be calibrated with buffer solutions of known pH first [1]

**13.** Adding acid to water cannot make the solution alkaline; water already supplies about 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ H⁺, far more than the acid adds [1]; so the pH is **just below 7** (very close to 7.00) [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using ln or forgetting the minus sign.
- Averaging pH values after mixing.
- Using one volume instead of the total volume.
- Reporting the final volume when the volume of water added was asked for.
- Doubling [H⁺] for sulfuric acid without the instruction to do so.
