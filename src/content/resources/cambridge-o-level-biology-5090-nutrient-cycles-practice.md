**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the original mass lost at 20 degrees Celsius. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the effect of temperature on the rate of decay. **[3]**

**(d)** State **one** variable that should be kept constant in this investigation. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The change of nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds that can be used by plants [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Fungi [1] and bacteria [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Denitrification [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Plants take in carbon dioxide from the air in photosynthesis to make carbohydrates [1]. Carbon passes to animals by feeding [1]. Plants, animals and decomposers release carbon dioxide by respiration [1]. When organisms die, decomposers break them down and release carbon dioxide [1]. Over millions of years, some dead organisms form fossil fuels, which store carbon [1]. Combustion of fossil fuels or wood releases carbon dioxide into the air [1]. **[6]**

**7.**

**(a)** Denitrification [1].

**(b)** Denitrifying bacteria change nitrate ions in the soil into nitrogen gas [1]. There are fewer nitrate ions for the plants to absorb [1], so the plants cannot make enough amino acids and protein [1].

**(c)** The fertiliser provides nitrate ions [1], so the plants can absorb them and make amino acids and protein for growth [1]. **[6]**

**8.** The bacteria in the roots change nitrogen gas from the air into nitrogen compounds [1]. The plant can use these to make amino acids and proteins [1], so it does not depend on nitrate ions in the soil [1]. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** 50 - 12 = 38 g [1].

**(b)** 50 - 31 = 19 g lost [1]; 19 divided by 50 x 100 = 38 per cent [1].

**(c)** The rate of decay increases as the temperature rises [1], because the enzymes of the fungi and bacteria work faster [1], so they digest and respire the leaves more quickly [1].

**(d)** Any one of: mass of leaves; moisture; type of leaf; time [1]. **[7]**

**10.** Fungi and bacteria release enzymes onto dead matter and waste [1]. The enzymes digest it into soluble molecules [1], which the microorganisms absorb [1]. They respire and release carbon dioxide, and release ammonium ions into the soil so the nutrients are recycled [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Photosynthesis removes carbon dioxide at about the same rate as respiration and decomposition add it [1], so the amounts are balanced [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Decomposers break down the protein and release ammonium ions [1]. Nitrifying bacteria change the ammonium ions into nitrate ions [1]. The nitrate ions are absorbed by plant roots [1] and used to make amino acids and then protein [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that plants take up nitrogen gas.
- Mixing up nitrification and denitrification.
- Forgetting that decomposers respire and release carbon dioxide.
- Missing the role of enzymes in decomposition.
- Naming a process without saying which compound is formed.

## Quick checks before you hand in

1. Follow each arrow in a cycle diagram and name the process on it. Decay, nitrification, absorption, feeding and respiration are different processes, so one word is not enough.
2. When a question asks you to explain a change in nitrate or carbon dioxide levels, say what makes the level rise and what makes it fall. A one-sided answer usually earns half the marks.
3. Use the right form of the element: nitrogen gas in the air, ammonium ions from decay, nitrate ions taken up by roots, and carbon dioxide in the air. Marks are often lost for naming the wrong form.
4. Link every process to the organisms that carry it out, such as decomposers, nitrifying bacteria, denitrifying bacteria, plants and animals.
5. Check that your answer gives as many separate points as there are marks.
