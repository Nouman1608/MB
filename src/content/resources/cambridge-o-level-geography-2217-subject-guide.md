Cambridge O Level Geography (2217) studies the relationship between people and the physical world through three themes, assessed entirely by written examination. There are three compulsory, externally assessed papers: Paper 1, Geographical Themes (1 hour 45 minutes, 75 raw marks weighted to 100, 45%), Paper 2, Geographical Skills (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%) and Paper 3, Geographical Investigations (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%). There is no coursework and no choice of route, and this syllabus is examined in the November series only. The sections below describe how the content is grouped, how the papers relate to one another and how to spread the work across the course.

## How the course is organised

The subject content is arranged in three themes. Each theme has its own set of topics, but they share a common approach: understand the process, know the pattern it creates, and support explanations with real examples.

**Theme 1 – Population and Settlement** covers Population Dynamics, Migration, Population Structure, Population Density and Distribution, Settlements (Rural and Urban) and Service Provision, Urban Settlements, and Urbanisation. The theme builds from the size and make-up of populations to the places people live and the pressures created as cities grow.

**Theme 2 – The Natural Environment** covers Earthquakes and Volcanoes, Rivers, Coasts, Weather, and Climate and Natural Vegetation. It is the physical side of the course, concerned with landforms, atmospheric processes and the way ecosystems reflect climate.

**Theme 3 – Economic Development** covers Development, Food Production, Industry, Tourism, Energy, Water, and Environmental Risks of Economic Development. Here the focus shifts to how people use resources and environments, and what that use costs.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is linear: all three papers are taken at the end of the course in the same series. The syllabus is not tiered.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Geographical Themes | 1 hour 45 minutes | 75 (weighted to 100) | 45% |
| Paper 2 | Geographical Skills | 1 hour 30 minutes | 60 | 27.5% |
| Paper 3 | Geographical Investigations | 1 hour 30 minutes | 60 | 27.5% |

According to the syllabus, Paper 1 asks for three questions, one from each section; Paper 2 requires every question to be answered; and Paper 3 contains two compulsory questions. All three papers are marked by Cambridge.

Two features set 2217 apart from the closely related Cambridge IGCSE syllabus (0460). First, Paper 3 is compulsory for everyone, whereas the IGCSE offers a coursework option alongside its written alternative. Second, 2217 is examined in November only, so there is no June sitting. The record used here covers the 2026 examination window (Version 3 of the syllabus), first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

It helps to think of the three papers as testing knowledge, skills and method in turn, all drawn from the same three themes.

Paper 1 is the knowledge paper. It expects you to explain processes, describe patterns and use case studies from Population and Settlement, The Natural Environment and Economic Development. Because you answer one question from each section, every theme needs to be secure; there is no safe topic to drop.

Paper 2 is the skills paper. Map reading, interpretation of photographs, graphs and statistics, and the ability to describe what a resource shows are all practised here. These skills appear throughout the themes, so they are best learned alongside the content rather than as a separate unit at the end.

Paper 3 is the investigation paper. It asks you to think like a geographer carrying out an enquiry: how a hypothesis is set up, how data might be collected, how results are presented, and how far conclusions can be trusted. Its two compulsory questions draw on familiar physical and human contexts, which is why fieldwork methods should be linked to topics such as Rivers, Coasts, Urban Settlements or Service Provision as you study them.

Seen this way, a single topic like Coasts can feed all three papers: the processes and landforms for Paper 1, a map or profile for Paper 2, and a beach survey method for Paper 3.

## Planning your study

Because all three papers are sat together in November, the course works best as a steady build rather than a series of separate blocks. A practical sequence is to begin with Population and Settlement or The Natural Environment, depending on your school's scheme, and then work through Economic Development in the later part of the course.

In general terms:

- In the first stage, cover the opening theme and start a case-study file, noting for each example what it shows and which topic it supports.
- In the middle stage, complete the second theme and begin practising investigation methods alongside it, so that Paper 3 thinking is not left until the end.
- In the final stage, work through Economic Development, finishing with Environmental Risks of Economic Development, then turn to mixed revision and full papers.

Short skills exercises should run through every stage. Begin topic-level exam questions as soon as each theme is finished and move to complete past papers in the months before November. For detailed advice on each paper, use the separate exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Geography 2217?**
There are three compulsory papers: Paper 1 Geographical Themes, Paper 2 Geographical Skills and Paper 3 Geographical Investigations. All three are externally assessed.

**Does O Level Geography 2217 have coursework?**
No. Unlike the IGCSE syllabus 0460, which offers a coursework option, 2217 is assessed only by written papers, with Paper 3 Geographical Investigations compulsory for everyone.

**When is Cambridge O Level Geography 2217 examined?**
This syllabus is examined in the November series only; there is no June series for 2217.

**What are the weightings of the 2217 papers?**
Paper 1 carries 45% (75 raw marks weighted to 100), while Paper 2 and Paper 3 each carry 27.5% and are marked out of 60.

**Which themes does the course cover?**
Theme 1 Population and Settlement, Theme 2 The Natural Environment and Theme 3 Economic Development.
