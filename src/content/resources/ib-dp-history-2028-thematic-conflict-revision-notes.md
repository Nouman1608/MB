Revision notes for the thematic study **Conflict (from 750 CE onwards)** in IB DP History (first assessment 2028), for SL and HL. The study has four inquiry questions and sixteen lines of inquiry, with no prescribed examples. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-thematic-conflict/) and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-thematic-conflict-practice/).

## Paper 2 in one box

| Part | Task | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| Section A | Analyse a concept, illustrated by an example from the thematic study (choose one of two questions) | 6 |
| Section B (a) | Short response on one example | 4 |
| Section B (b) | Essay using examples from at least two regions | 15 |

Total 25 marks in 1 hour 45 minutes. Section B has two questions per study; you choose one and answer both parts.

## Definition

Conflict: a situation where two or more groups use violence to resolve disputes. It lies on a spectrum with peaceful cooperation, and relations can contain both.

## The four inquiry questions and sixteen lines of inquiry

| Inquiry question | Lines of inquiry |
|---|---|
| Why did conflict emerge? | Economic factors; political factors; social factors; environmental factors |
| What determined the outcome of the conflict? | Role of leadership; strategies and tactics; mobilisation of resources; role of technology |
| How did the conflict affect people's lives? | Economic impact; social impact; experiences of women; experiences of marginalised groups |
| How was peace established? | Military outcome; political decision-making; social factors; post-conflict peace-building |

Each line needs at least two examples from more than one region.

## Example bank (illustrative)

| Example | Key facts |
|---|---|
| American Civil War (1861 to 1865) | Lincoln elected 1860; secession; Union blockade; Grant; Appomattox April 1865; emancipation; Reconstruction ended 1877 |
| Second World War (1939 to 1945) | Total war; mobilisation of women; Lend-Lease; radar and atomic bombs; Holocaust; Marshall Plan; occupation of Japan |
| Algerian War (1954 to 1962) | FLN revolt November 1954; guerrilla war; Battle of Algiers 1957; Evian Accords March 1962; harkis |
| Chinese Civil War (1927 to 1949) | Mao and the Communist army; peasant support; Communist victory in 1949 |
| Crusades (1095 to 1291) | Religious and political motives; capture of Jerusalem in 1099; recapture by Saladin in 1187; effects on civilians |

## Quick evidence by line of inquiry

**Why did conflict emerge?**

- Economic: slave-based cotton economy versus industrialising North; resource needs in 1930s Japan.
- Political: Lincoln's election; exclusion of Algeria's Muslim majority from power.
- Social: racist ideology and nationalism; religious identity.
- Environmental: competition for land and resources, as a condition not the sole cause.

**What determined the outcome?**

- Leadership: Lincoln and Grant; Mao; Churchill, Roosevelt and Stalin.
- Strategies: blockade and Sherman's march; guerrilla war and urban attacks in Algeria.
- Resources: Union industry and railways; American production and Soviet relocation of factories.
- Technology: railways, telegraph, rifled muskets; radar, tanks and atomic bombs.

**How did it affect lives?**

- Economic: destroyed cities and industry; emancipation reshaped the Southern economy.
- Social: deaths, displacement, memory.
- Women: factory work, nursing, combat roles in the USSR, FLN couriers and fighters.
- Marginalised groups: Black soldiers in the Union army; victims of the Holocaust; harkis.

**How was peace established?**

- Military outcome: Appomattox; unconditional surrender in 1945.
- Political decisions: Evian Accords; Yalta and Potsdam.
- Social factors: war weariness in France.
- Peace-building: Reconstruction amendments; Marshall Plan; Japanese constitution of 1947.

## Section A: how to answer

Typical concepts: cause and consequence, continuity and change, perspectives, significance.

1. Define the concept in a line.
2. Choose one example from the thematic study and give precise detail.
3. Analyse: tie each point to the concept.
4. Judge briefly.

**Markband (6 marks).** 1 to 2: describes the concept with minimal analysis. 3 to 4: partial analysis with a relevant example. 5 to 6: clear and accurate analysis, effectively supported by a specific example.

## Section B (a): how to answer (4 marks)

One example, explained, not just described. Aim for a clear identification, accurate detail and an explanation of how it relates to the question. The bands run from identifying (1) to describing (2) to partially explaining (3) to effectively explaining (4).

## Section B (b): how to answer (15 marks)

1. Open with a brief line of argument that answers the question.
2. Use at least two examples from at least two regions.
3. Organise by argument or factor, not by example.
4. In each paragraph, compare examples: similarity, difference, pattern or exception.
5. Finish with a reasoned judgement.

**Top band (13 to 15).** Consistently analytical, a reasoned judgement consistent with the analysis, examples consistently appropriate and relevant, well-developed connections integrated into the argument.

## Worked example: a plan for an essay

Question: "To what extent was leadership the main factor in determining the outcome of conflicts?"

- Thesis: leadership mattered, but resources and strategy usually set the limits of what leaders could do.
- Paragraph 1: Leadership (Lincoln and Grant in the Civil War, Americas; Mao in China, Asia): both leaders made strategic choices that mattered.
- Paragraph 2: Resources (Union industry; the war economies in the Second World War): the stronger economy usually prevailed, which limited the role of leaders.
- Paragraph 3: Exception (Algeria, Africa): the weaker side won by political and guerrilla strategy and by the war's cost to France.
- Conclusion: leadership was important where it shaped strategy, but it was rarely sufficient alone.

## Common mistakes

- A narrative of one war.
- Both examples from one region in the essay.
- No connections between the examples.
- A conclusion that repeats the question without a judgement.

## Self-test

1. Give the guide's definition of conflict.
2. How many regions are there, and how many must an essay use?
3. How many marks is Section B part (b)?
4. Name two lines of inquiry under "How was peace established?"
5. Name two types of conflict listed in the guide.
6. Give one example of a conflict won by the weaker side, and say why.

**Answers.**

1. A situation where two or more groups use violence to resolve disputes.
2. Four regions; at least two.
3. 15 marks.
4. Any two of: military outcome; political decision-making; social factors; post-conflict peace-building.
5. Any two of: civil wars, interstate wars, wars of independence, revolutions, terrorist movements, total wars, world wars, proxy wars, the Cold War.
6. For example, Algeria: the FLN combined guerrilla tactics and political pressure, and France judged the cost of staying too high.
