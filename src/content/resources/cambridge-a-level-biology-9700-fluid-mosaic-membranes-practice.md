## Section A: Short-answer questions

**1.** Which membrane component has carbohydrate chains on the outer surface and acts in cell recognition? **[1]**

A: Cholesterol

B: Channel protein

C: Glycoprotein

D: Phospholipid

**2.** Which change would make a membrane more fluid? **[1]**

A: A fall in temperature

B: A higher proportion of unsaturated fatty acid tails

C: A higher proportion of saturated fatty acid tails

D: Cooling the membrane to 4 °C

**3.** Explain why phospholipids form a bilayer, rather than a single layer, between the cytoplasm and tissue fluid. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the arrangement of cholesterol in a cell surface membrane. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the term "mosaic" is used to describe the membrane. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why sodium ions cannot diffuse directly through the phospholipid bilayer. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** A transmembrane protein has three regions: one exposed to the cytoplasm, one within the bilayer and one exposed to tissue fluid.

**(a)** Describe the R groups you would expect in the region within the bilayer, and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why this protein can move sideways in the membrane but does not leave it. **[2]**

**8.** Explain the roles of cholesterol in a cell surface membrane with reference to fluidity, stability and permeability. **[4]**

**9.** A student placed beetroot discs in water at different temperatures for 30 minutes and measured the absorbance of the water (invented data).

| Temperature / °C | 25 | 35 | 45 | 55 | 65 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Absorbance | 0.07 | 0.09 | 0.18 | 0.56 | 0.80 |

**(a)** Calculate the increase in absorbance between 45 °C and 55 °C as a percentage of the value at 45 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the results between 45 °C and 65 °C. **[3]**

**(c)** State two variables the student should control. **[2]**

**10.** Ethanol dissolves lipids. Suggest and explain the effect of placing beetroot discs in a 40% ethanol solution compared with water at the same temperature. **[2]**

**11.** Outline the three stages of cell signalling that lead to a specific response, using a hormone as the example. **[3]**

**12.** Two cell types, X and Y, are exposed to the same ligand. Only X responds.

**(a)** Explain why only X responds. **[2]**

**(b)** After X is treated with an enzyme that digests proteins on its outer surface, it no longer responds. Explain this result. **[2]**

**13.** Describe the roles of glycolipids and glycoproteins in the cell surface membrane. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Cholesterol and phospholipids have no carbohydrate chains; channel proteins are for transport. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. Kinks in unsaturated tails stop close packing. A, C and D all reduce fluidity. **[1]**

**3.** There is water on both sides of the membrane, so heads must face water on both sides [1]. Two layers allow the hydrophobic tails of both layers to point inwards, away from water, forming a core [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Cholesterol molecules lie between phospholipids in both layers of the bilayer [1], with the –OH group near the phosphate heads and the rest among the fatty acid tails [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Proteins are scattered irregularly through the phospholipid bilayer, like the pieces of a mosaic [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Sodium ions are charged and surrounded by water molecules [1], so they cannot pass through the hydrophobic core of fatty acid tails [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Hydrophobic (non-polar) R groups [1], because they are in contact with the hydrophobic fatty acid tails and are stable there [1].

**(b)** Sideways movement keeps the hydrophobic region within the core, so it is allowed by the fluid bilayer [1]; leaving would expose the hydrophobic region to water, which is unstable [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Fluidity: cholesterol regulates fluidity, reducing movement of phospholipids at higher temperatures [1] and preventing them packing tightly and becoming rigid at low temperatures [1]. Stability: it packs between phospholipids, making the membrane more stable [1]. Permeability: it reduces the permeability of the bilayer to ions and polar molecules [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** Increase = 0.56 − 0.18 = 0.38 [1]; 0.38 ÷ 0.18 × 100 = **211%** [1].

**(b)** Above 45 °C membrane proteins denature, leaving gaps in the membrane [1]; the phospholipids gain kinetic energy and the bilayer becomes much more fluid [1]; so more pigment leaks from the vacuoles and cells, giving higher absorbance [1].

**(c)** Any two: size and number of discs [1]; volume of water [1]; time in water; washing method. **[7]**

**10.** Absorbance would be higher in ethanol [1], because ethanol dissolves lipids in the cell surface and vacuole membranes, so the membranes become more permeable and more pigment leaks out [1]. **[2]**

**11.** A gland cell secretes the hormone (the ligand) [1]. The hormone is transported in the blood to target cells [1]. It binds to cell surface receptors on target cells, which have a complementary binding site, triggering a response [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Only X has receptors on its cell surface membrane [1] with a binding site complementary in shape to the ligand [1].

**(b)** The receptor is a protein on the outer surface [1]; once digested, its binding site is lost, so the ligand cannot bind and no response is triggered [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Their carbohydrate chains act as cell surface antigens, allowing cell recognition [1]. They form hydrogen bonds with water around the cell, stabilising the membrane [1]. Some glycoproteins act as receptors in cell signalling [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In question 9(b), credit is given for both proteins and phospholipids. Answers that say "phospholipids denature" lose the mark: only proteins denature.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying heads face "outwards" without saying they face water.
- Forgetting that cholesterol is in both layers.
- Describing cholesterol as simply making the membrane rigid.
- Leaving out the transport stage of cell signalling.
- Using the change in absorbance rather than the percentage change when asked for a percentage.
