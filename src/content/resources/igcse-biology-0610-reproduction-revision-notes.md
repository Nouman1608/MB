These notes condense topic 16 (Reproduction) of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028, sections 16.1 to 16.6. Core content is tested on Papers 1 and 3 and is needed by everyone; content marked "Extended only" is Supplement, tested on Papers 2 and 4. Section 16.6 has no Supplement content. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Reproduction study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-reproduction/).

## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Asexual reproduction:** a process resulting in the production of genetically identical offspring from one parent.
- **Sexual reproduction:** a process involving the fusion of the nuclei of two gametes to form a zygote and the production of offspring that are genetically different from each other.
- **Fertilisation:** the fusion of the nuclei of gametes.
- **Pollination:** the transfer of pollen grains from an anther to a stigma.
- **Self-pollination (Extended only):** transfer from an anther to the stigma of the same flower or a different flower on the same plant.
- **Cross-pollination (Extended only):** transfer from an anther to the stigma of a flower on a different plant of the same species.
- **STI:** an infection transmitted through sexual contact.

## 16.1 and 16.2 -- asexual vs sexual (must-know distinction)

| | Asexual | Sexual |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | One | Usually two |
| Gametes and fertilisation | None | Yes |
| Offspring | Genetically identical | Genetically different |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| Variation | None | Yes |

**Extended only:** gamete nuclei are **haploid**; the zygote nucleus is **diploid**.

**Extended only -- the "discuss" frame.** For wild populations, weigh speed and not needing a mate (asexual) against variation and the ability to adapt when the environment changes (sexual). For crops, weigh uniform, predictable plants with the grower's chosen features (asexual) against the risk that one disease destroys a genetically identical crop, and the chance of new, improved varieties (sexual). A "discuss" answer needs points on **both** sides.

## 16.3 -- flowers

**Male:** stamen = **anther** (makes pollen) + **filament** (holds anther up).
**Female:** carpel = **stigma** (receives pollen) + **style** (holds stigma up) + **ovary** (contains ovules).
**Ovules** contain the female gamete. **Sepals** protect the bud. **Petals** attract insects.

| | Insect-pollinated | Wind-pollinated |
|---|---|---|
| Petals | Large, bright, scented | Small, dull or absent |
| Anthers | Inside flower | Hanging outside, long filaments |
| Stigmas | Inside, sticky | Feathery, hanging outside |
| Pollen | Larger, sticky or spiky, fewer grains | Smaller, lighter, smooth, huge numbers |

**Extended only -- effects on a population.**

| | Self-pollination | Cross-pollination |
|---|---|---|
| Variation | Less | More |
| Capacity to respond to environmental change | Lower | Higher |
| Reliance on pollinators | Lower | Higher |

**Fertilisation in plants -- method in steps**

1. Pollen lands on the stigma (pollination).
2. *(Extended only)* A pollen tube grows from the grain down through the style.
3. *(Extended only)* The tube enters the ovule through the micropyle.
4. The pollen nucleus fuses with the nucleus in the ovule (fertilisation).

**Germination needs:** water, oxygen, suitable temperature. Not light. In an investigation, change one condition per tube and compare with a control that has all three.

## 16.4 -- humans

| Structure | Function |
|---|---|
| Testes | Make sperm |
| Scrotum | Holds testes outside body (cooler) |
| Sperm ducts | Carry sperm to urethra |
| Prostate gland | Adds fluid for sperm to swim in |
| Urethra | Carries semen out through penis |
| Penis | Places semen in vagina |
| Ovaries | Make eggs |
| Oviducts | Carry egg; site of fertilisation |
| Uterus | Site of implantation; fetus develops here |
| Cervix | Holds fetus in; widens at birth |
| Vagina | Receives sperm; birth canal |

**Gametes compared**

| | Sperm | Egg cell |
|---|---|---|
| Size | Much smaller | Much larger |
| Structure | Head with acrosome, flagellum, many mitochondria | Large cytoplasm with energy stores, jelly coat |
| Motility | Swims (motile) | Not motile |
| Numbers | Very large numbers | Few, usually one per cycle |

**Adaptations -- feature plus job:** flagellum → swimming; mitochondria → energy for swimming; acrosome enzymes → digest a way into the egg; energy stores → feed the early embryo; jelly coat changes at fertilisation → only one sperm enters.

**Development:** zygote → embryo (ball of cells) → implants in uterus lining. Placenta = exchange; umbilical cord = links fetus to placenta; amniotic sac + fluid = protect and cushion.

**Extended only:** at the placenta, oxygen, glucose, amino acids and other dissolved nutrients pass from mother's blood to fetal blood; carbon dioxide and urea pass from fetal to mother's blood. Some pathogens and toxins can cross and affect the fetus.

## 16.5 -- hormones

**Puberty:** testosterone (testes) → facial and body hair, deeper voice, muscle growth. Oestrogen (ovaries) → breasts develop, hips widen, menstrual cycle starts.

**Menstrual cycle (about 28 days):** days 1–5 lining lost → lining rebuilds and thickens → egg matures in a follicle in the ovary → ovulation about day 14 → lining kept thick → if no implantation, lining is lost and the cycle repeats.

**Must-know distinction -- ovary vs uterus in the cycle**

| Stage | In the ovary | In the uterus lining |
|---|---|---|
| Days 1–5 | A new follicle starts to develop | Lining breaks down and is lost |
| Days 6–13 | Egg matures inside the follicle | Lining repairs and thickens |
| About day 14 | Ovulation: egg released | Lining thick |
| Days 15–28 | Empty follicle becomes the corpus luteum (Extended detail) | Lining kept thick, ready for implantation |

Questions often give a graph of lining thickness against days. Read the day numbers from the axis, and say "lining" or "endometrium" -- not "uterus wall breaks down".

**Must-know distinction -- the three support structures:** the **placenta** exchanges substances, the **umbilical cord** carries the fetus's blood to and from the placenta, and the **amniotic fluid** inside the **amniotic sac** protects the fetus from knocks. Do not give the placenta's job to the fluid, or the other way round.

**Extended only -- hormone table**

| Hormone | Source | Key effect |
|---|---|---|
| FSH | Pituitary gland | Follicle develops; oestrogen made |
| Oestrogen | Ovary (follicle); placenta in pregnancy | Lining thickens; inhibits FSH; triggers LH surge |
| LH | Pituitary gland | Ovulation; corpus luteum forms |
| Progesterone | Corpus luteum; placenta in pregnancy | Maintains lining; inhibits FSH and LH |

**Sequence to learn:** FSH → oestrogen rises → LH surge → ovulation → progesterone from corpus luteum → no pregnancy → corpus luteum breaks down → progesterone falls → menstruation → FSH rises.

## 16.6 -- STIs (all Core)

- HIV is a pathogen that causes an STI; HIV infection may lead to AIDS.
- **Transmission:** unprotected sex; sharing needles; infected blood transfusion; mother to baby (placenta, birth, breast milk).
- **Control:** condoms; fewer partners; testing, treating and informing partners; screening blood; not sharing needles; education.

## Quick self-test

1. A dog's body cells contain 78 chromosomes. How many are in a dog sperm cell, and in a dog zygote? *(Extended)*
2. Name the part of a flower that receives pollen.
3. Give two ways the anthers of a wind-pollinated flower differ from those of an insect-pollinated flower.
4. State the three conditions a seed needs to germinate.
5. In a germination test, 42 out of 50 seeds germinated. Calculate the percentage germination.
6. Give one structural difference between an egg cell and a sperm cell, other than size.
7. State the function of the scrotum.
8. What happens to the embryo after it forms from the zygote?
9. A woman's regular 28-day cycle begins on 10 June. On about which date will she ovulate?
10. Name the structure that produces progesterone in the second half of the menstrual cycle. *(Extended)*
11. Explain why a woman does not ovulate during pregnancy. *(Extended)*
12. State two ways HIV can be transmitted other than by sexual contact.

### Answers

1. Sperm cell **39**; zygote **78**.
2. The **stigma**.
3. Any two: they hang **outside** the flower; they are on **long filaments**; they are loosely attached so the wind shakes pollen out.
4. **Water, oxygen** and a **suitable temperature**.
5. (42 ÷ 50) × 100 = **84 %**.
6. Any one: the egg has a **jelly coat** / energy stores in the cytoplasm; the sperm has a **flagellum** / acrosome / many mitochondria.
7. Holds the **testes outside the body**, where it is cooler.
8. It **implants** into the lining of the uterus.
9. 10 June is day 1, so day 14 is **23 June**.
10. The **corpus luteum** (in the ovary).
11. **Progesterone** stays high (from the corpus luteum, then the placenta), which **inhibits FSH and LH**, so no follicle matures and no LH surge triggers ovulation.
12. Any two: **sharing needles**; transfusion of **infected blood**; from **mother to baby** across the placenta, at birth or in breast milk.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "fusion of gametes" instead of **fusion of the nuclei** of gametes.
- Treating pollination and fertilisation as the same event.
- Adding light, or carbon dioxide, to the list of germination conditions.
- Giving a feature of a gamete without saying how it helps (for example "mitochondria" with no mention of energy for swimming).
- Saying the mother's and fetus's blood mix in the placenta.
- On an Extended "discuss" question, giving advantages only and no disadvantages.
- Saying FSH or LH is made by the ovary.
- Writing that progesterone "causes" menstruation. It is the **fall** in progesterone that leads to the lining breaking down.
- Stating a control method for STIs without explaining how it reduces spread.
