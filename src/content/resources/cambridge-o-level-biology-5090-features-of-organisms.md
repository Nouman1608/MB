This guide covers subtopic **2.2 Features of organisms** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to state the main features used to place organisms into one of the five kingdoms (animal, plant, fungus, prokaryote, protoctist); into the groups of vertebrates (mammals, birds, reptiles, amphibians, fish) and arthropods (myriapods, insects, arachnids, crustaceans); into ferns and flowering plants (dicotyledons and monocotyledons); to classify organisms using those features; and to state the main features of viruses and understand that they replicate only in living cells.

The [Classification topic guide](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-classification/) covers the whole topic. This page gives the fine detail, a method for classifying an unknown organism, and the points that cost marks. Revise with the [2.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-features-of-organisms-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [2.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-features-of-organisms-practice/).

## The five kingdoms

Every organism is placed in one of five kingdoms. The features used are about cell type, how many cells make up the body, and how the organism gets its food.

| Kingdom | Cells and body | Cell wall | Nutrition | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Animal | Many cells, with a nucleus | None | Feed on other organisms (take in food); no chlorophyll | Cat, earthworm, fish |
| Plant | Many cells, with a nucleus | Cellulose | Make their own food by photosynthesis, using chlorophyll in chloroplasts | Fern, maize, bean |
| Fungus | Some single-celled (yeast); others many-celled, made of threads called hyphae; nucleus present | Not cellulose (made of chitin) | Feed on dead or living matter by releasing enzymes and absorbing the products; no chlorophyll | Mushroom, mould, yeast |
| Prokaryote | Single cell, **no nucleus**; circular DNA, sometimes plasmids | Present, not cellulose | Various | Bacteria |
| Protoctist | Mostly single-celled, with a nucleus | Variable | Some photosynthesise, some feed on other organisms | Amoeba, *Chlorella*, *Plasmodium* |

Two features do most of the sorting.

1. **Does the cell have a nucleus?** No nucleus means prokaryote. All the others have a nucleus.
2. **Is the body many-celled or single-celled, and how is food obtained?** Many-celled organisms that photosynthesise are plants. Many-celled organisms that take in food are animals. Fungi do not photosynthesise and do not take in food as a whole; they absorb it after digesting it outside their bodies.

Protoctists are the group that does not fit elsewhere. They have a nucleus, so they are not prokaryotes, and they are mostly single cells, so they are neither plants nor animals.

## The vertebrates

**Vertebrates** are animals with a backbone. The syllabus lists five groups. Learn the features that **separate** one group from the others.

| Group | Body covering | Reproduction | Other features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mammals | Hair or fur | Give birth to live young; feed young on milk from mammary glands | External ears (pinnae) in most; body temperature kept constant |
| Birds | Feathers; scaly legs | Lay eggs with hard shells | Wings, beak, no teeth; body temperature kept constant |
| Reptiles | Dry scales | Lay eggs with soft, leathery shells on land | Body temperature varies with surroundings |
| Amphibians | Moist, smooth skin | Lay jelly-covered eggs in water; young (tadpoles) live in water | Adults live on land and in water; body temperature varies |
| Fish | Wet scales | Lay eggs in water | Gills, fins; body temperature varies |

Mark-winning phrases include "hair or fur" for mammals (not just "skin"), "feathers" for birds, "dry scales" for reptiles, "moist skin" for amphibians and "gills" for fish.

## The arthropods

**Arthropods** are invertebrates with jointed legs, a hard outer skeleton (exoskeleton) and a body divided into segments. They are the largest group of animals. The syllabus names four groups.

| Group | Body | Legs | Antennae | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Myriapods | Many similar segments | Many legs, one or two pairs per segment | One pair | Centipede, millipede |
| Insects | Three parts: head, thorax, abdomen | Six | One pair; usually wings | Housefly, bee |
| Arachnids | Two parts: head-thorax joined, and abdomen | Eight | None | Spider, scorpion |
| Crustaceans | Hard shell; body often in two parts | Five or more pairs | Two pairs | Crab, woodlouse, shrimp |

The fastest tests are leg count (six, eight or many) and the number of antennae (none, one pair, two pairs).

## Ferns and flowering plants

Plants are separated by how they reproduce.

- **Ferns** have leaves called **fronds**, and they reproduce by **spores** rather than seeds. They do not have flowers.
- **Flowering plants** reproduce using **flowers** that make **seeds**. They are split into two groups.

| Feature | Dicotyledons | Monocotyledons |
|---|---|---|
| Seed leaves (cotyledons) | Two | One |
| Leaf veins | Branching network | Parallel |
| Leaf shape | Usually broad | Usually long and narrow |
| Flower parts | In fours or fives (or multiples) | In threes (or multiples) |
| Vascular bundles in stem | Arranged in a ring | Scattered |
| Examples | Bean, rose, oak | Maize, grass, lily |

When you are asked to identify the group from a drawing, use the veins first: network means dicotyledon, parallel lines mean monocotyledon.

## Viruses

Viruses are very small and are not made of cells. Their main features, as the syllabus lists them, are:

- a **protein coat**
- **genetic material** inside the coat, which is either DNA or RNA

A virus has no nucleus, no cytoplasm, no cell membrane and no ribosomes. It cannot carry out its own life processes. **Viruses can only replicate inside living cells.** A virus enters a host cell, and the host cell's own chemical machinery is used to make new virus particles. Because they are not made of cells and cannot reproduce on their own, viruses do not fit into any of the five kingdoms.

## Worked example: classifying an organism

*An organism is many-celled, has cell walls made of chitin, has no chlorophyll, and feeds by releasing enzymes onto dead wood. Place it in a kingdom and give three reasons.*

1. **Cells**: many-celled, so it is not a prokaryote. A prokaryote is single-celled.
2. **Wall and chlorophyll**: the wall is not cellulose and there is no chlorophyll, so it is not a plant.
3. **Nutrition**: it releases enzymes and absorbs the digested food. It does not take in whole food and has no mouth, so it is not an animal.
4. **Conclusion**: it is a **fungus**, because it has chitin walls, no chlorophyll and feeds by extracellular digestion and absorption.

*A second organism has eight legs, two body parts and no antennae. Classify it as far as you can.* It is an animal with jointed legs and a segmented body, so an arthropod. Eight legs, two body parts and no antennae identify an **arachnid**.

## Telling similar groups apart

Exam questions often give two groups that look alike and ask for a difference. Prepare a pair of contrasts for each.

- **Mammals and birds**: both keep a constant body temperature, so this cannot be the answer. Use hair or fur against feathers, live young against hard-shelled eggs, and milk from mammary glands.
- **Reptiles and amphibians**: dry scales against moist skin, and soft-shelled eggs laid on land against jelly-covered eggs laid in water. Amphibian young (tadpoles) have gills and live in water, while reptile young hatch looking like small adults.
- **Reptiles and fish**: both have scales, so look at gills and the habitat of the eggs. Fish have gills and fins and lay their eggs in water.
- **Insects and arachnids**: six legs and three body parts against eight legs and two body parts. Insects have antennae and usually wings; arachnids have neither.
- **Myriapods and crustaceans**: myriapods have one pair of antennae and a long body of similar segments, while crustaceans have two pairs of antennae.
- **Ferns and flowering plants**: both are plants with leaves, roots and vascular tissue, but ferns have no flowers or seeds and reproduce by spores.

Always give a feature of **both** groups when you are asked for a difference. "Birds have feathers" is half an answer unless you also say that mammals have hair or fur.

## Why a table of features is a classification tool

A table that lists features against groups is the quickest way to revise, and it is also how you work out an unknown organism in the examination. Cover the group names and try to name each group from its features alone. Then cover the features and try to write at least three for each group. Practising in both directions helps you to use features to classify an unfamiliar organism, which is the skill the syllabus statement "classify organisms using the features" expects.

## Common mistakes

- Saying a virus is a cell or placing it in a kingdom. Viruses are not made of cells.
- Describing bacteria as having "no DNA". They have DNA, but no nucleus.
- Writing that amphibians "live in water". Adults live on land and in water, and the young live in water.
- Describing all arthropods as insects. Insects are only one of four groups.
- Using "has eyes" or "has legs" as the feature that separates groups. Use specific counts and structures.
- Saying fish have "skin" when the marking point is "scales and gills".
- Mixing up the monocotyledon and dicotyledon leaf veins.

## Command words

- **State**: give the feature briefly, for example one feature of birds.
- **Describe**: give the main features of a group.
- **Classify**: place the organism in a group and use the features given to justify it.
- **Compare**: give similarities as well as differences, for example monocotyledons and dicotyledons.
