**These notes condense content area 3.1.1.2 of 9237: "the meaning and importance of the key beliefs for Muslims, including Tawhid, Malaikah, Kutub, Risalah, Yaum e Akhirah, Al-Qadr and all good and bad is from Allah".** The full explanation is in the [Key beliefs study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-key-beliefs/).

## The list

| # | Arabic | English | One-line meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Tawhid | Oneness of Allah | One God, no partner |
| 2 | Malaikah | Angels | Beings of light who obey Allah |
| 3 | Kutub | Books | Revealed scriptures, ending in the Qur'an |
| 4 | Risalah | Messengers | Prophets sent to guide, ending with Muhammad (pbuh) |
| 5 | Yaum e Akhirah | The Last Day | Resurrection, judgement, Paradise or Hell |
| 6 | Al-Qadr | The decree | Allah's knowledge and will over all things |

**Plus:** all good and bad is from Allah (part of Al-Qadr).

**Memory aid:** use the chain. *Allah* sends *angels* with *books* to *messengers*, who warn of the *Last Day*, and all is by *decree*.

## Where the list comes from

| Source | What it gives |
|---|---|
| Qur'an 2:285 | Allah, angels, books, messengers |
| Qur'an 4:136 | Adds the Last Day |
| Qur'an 54:49 | "All things We created with predestination" |
| Hadith of Jibril (Sahih Muslim) | All six, including the decree, "its good and its bad" |

Note: the version of the Hadith of Jibril in Sahih al-Bukhari 50 (used for the Five Pillars passage) has a shorter list of beliefs. Use the Muslim report or the verses for the full six.

## Why each belief matters

- **Tawhid:** basis of the Shahadah; equality, sincerity, no fear of created things.
- **Malaikah:** reminds Muslims of the unseen and of recording angels, so behaviour is better in private.
- **Kutub:** guidance and law; the Qur'an is the last, preserved word (15:9).
- **Risalah:** shows how to live; "no distinction between any of His messengers" (2:285).
- **Yaum e Akhirah:** accountability and hope of justice.
- **Al-Qadr:** patience, gratitude, trust; no despair.

## "All good and bad is from Allah"

- Nothing happens outside Allah's knowledge and permission (64:11).
- It does **not** mean Allah loves or commands evil.
- Humans choose and are responsible (4:79).
- Hardship is a test (2:155), and patience is rewarded.
- Examples: illness, loss and exam failure are hard events, within the decree; cruelty and theft are human choices to be judged.

**Contrast to avoid:** "Allah makes people do bad things" (wrong) against "Allah allows people to choose, and knows what they will choose" (right).

## The passage: Qur'an 2:285-286

| Phrase | Use for |
|---|---|
| "believed in Allah and His angels and His books and His messengers" | The key beliefs in one sentence |
| "We make no distinction between any of His messengers" | Risalah, respect for all prophets |
| "We hear and we obey" | Belief leads to action |
| "to You is the [final] destination" | Yaum e Akhirah |
| "Allah does not require of any soul more than what it can afford" | Al-Qadr, fairness, mercy |
| "All good will be for its own benefit, and all evil will be to its own loss" | Responsibility and accountability |
| The closing prayer | Humility, asking for forgiveness and help |

## Differences between Muslims

- **Sunni:** six key beliefs.
- **Twelver Shi'a:** five roots (*usul ad-din*): Tawhid, 'Adl (justice), Nubuwwah, Imamah, Ma'ad.
- **Common ground:** One God, prophets, scripture and the Last Day.

## Exam traps

- Count them: **six** beliefs plus the "good and bad" statement.
- Do not confuse key beliefs (iman) with the Five Pillars (practices).
- Do not write that Allah is responsible for sin in a way that removes human responsibility.
- Do not forget that belief leads to action: link each belief to behaviour.
- Spell the Arabic terms as they appear in the specification.

## Model answer shapes

- **2 marks "Give two key beliefs":** belief in angels; belief in the Last Day.
- **4 marks "Explain two reasons why belief in the books is important":** The books are Allah's guidance, so they tell people how to worship and live [1 and 1]. The Qur'an is believed to be preserved, so Muslims can rely on it for law and teaching in every age [1 and 1].
- **6 marks:** two reasons, each explained, plus one source used and applied.
- **12 marks:** choose three beliefs, tie each to a phrase of the passage, consider whether one belief is more important, and judge.

## Linking beliefs to behaviour: a quick table

| Belief | Example of behaviour | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Tawhid | Prays only to Allah; refuses to follow superstition | Allah alone is Lord and worshipped |
| Malaikah | Speaks the truth even when alone | Two angels record every action |
| Kutub | Reads and studies the Qur'an | It is Allah's final, preserved guidance |
| Risalah | Follows the Sunnah, such as honest trade | Muhammad (pbuh) is the model |
| Yaum e Akhirah | Gives to charity in secret | Deeds are weighed on the Last Day |
| Al-Qadr | Says "Alhamdulillah" in ease and stays patient in difficulty | Everything is within Allah's knowledge |

Use a row like these as the "explain" half of a 4-mark answer: the belief gives the identification mark, and the behaviour with its reason gives the development mark.

## A model 6-mark paragraph

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which belief in the Last Day and belief in the books affect a Muslim. Apply **one** teaching. **[6]**

Belief in the Last Day makes a Muslim think about accountability, because every action will be judged, so they try to be honest in private as well as in public [1 and 1]. Belief in the books means a Muslim treats the Qur'an as the final guidance of Allah and studies it to know how to pray, give and behave [1 and 1]. The passage says "We hear and we obey" [1], which shows that belief is meant to lead to action, for example by following what the Qur'an commands once it has been studied [1].

## Self-test

1. List the six key beliefs by their Arabic names.
2. Which verse lists belief in the Last Day alongside Allah, angels, books and messengers?
3. What does "no distinction between any of His messengers" mean?
4. Name the five books or scrolls Muslims believe were revealed.
5. What does the Hadith of Jibril add to the list in 2:285?
6. Explain in one sentence why "all good and bad is from Allah" does not remove human responsibility.
7. Give two ways belief in the Last Day changes behaviour.
8. Which key belief brings patience in hardship?
9. What is the Shi'a list of "roots of religion" called?
10. How are the key beliefs different from the Five Pillars?

**Answers:** 1. Tawhid, Malaikah, Kutub, Risalah, Yaum e Akhirah, Al-Qadr. 2. Qur'an 4:136. 3. Muslims honour every prophet and do not accept one while rejecting another. 4. Suhuf of Ibraheem, Tawrat, Zabur, Injil, Qur'an. 5. The Last Day and the decree, with its good and bad. 6. Everything happens within Allah's knowledge, but people choose their actions and are judged for them. 7. Honesty when unobserved; kindness and charity because deeds are recorded and will be weighed. 8. Al-Qadr. 9. Usul ad-din. 10. Key beliefs are what Muslims accept (faith); the Five Pillars are what they do (practice).
