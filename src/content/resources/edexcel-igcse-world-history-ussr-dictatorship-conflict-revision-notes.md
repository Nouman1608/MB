## Stalin v Trotsky

| | Stalin | Trotsky |
|---|---|---|
| Strengths | General Secretary; controlled appointments; seemed moderate; patient tactician | Red Army founder; brilliant speaker and thinker |
| Weaknesses | Criticised in Lenin's Testament; dull speaker | Arrogant; late Bolshevik (1917); missed Lenin's funeral; poor at building alliances |
| Policy | Socialism in One Country | Permanent Revolution |

## Successes and failures to weigh

| Policy | Successes | Failures |
|---|---|---|
| Five-year Plans | Rapid growth of coal, steel, electricity; new industrial cities; base for war production | Poor quality, waste, few consumer goods, harsh discipline, forced labour |
| Collectivisation | State control of grain; mechanisation; funds and labour for industry | Famine of 1932–33; loss of livestock; kulaks destroyed; low productivity |
| Purges | Removed rivals; total obedience | Lost experts and officers; fear; weakened army in 1941 |

## Worked example: two effects

*Explain two effects of the purges on the Soviet Union.*

1. **Weakened armed forces:** Tukhachevsky and many senior officers were executed in 1937–38, so the
   Red Army lacked experienced commanders — one reason for the disasters of 1941.
2. **Fear and obedience:** mass arrests by the NKVD meant no one dared to criticise Stalin, giving him
   unchallenged power but discouraging initiative in the economy.

## Essay plan in outline

*"Stalin's control of the party machine was the main reason he became leader by 1929." How far do
you agree?*

- **Paragraph 1 (stated factor):** as General Secretary he appointed loyal officials who voted at
  party congresses; the "Lenin Enrolment" brought in new members grateful to him.
- **Paragraph 2 (other factors):** alliance tactics — first with Zinoviev and Kamenev against
  Trotsky, then with Bukharin against them, then against Bukharin himself.
- **Paragraph 3 (rivals' weaknesses):** Trotsky's arrogance, his absence from Lenin's funeral and
  the suppression of Lenin's Testament.
- **Conclusion:** the party machine gave Stalin the votes; tactics decided when to use them.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Dating collectivisation.** The specification refers to collectivisation from 1928; the main
  drive began in late 1929. Avoid saying it began in 1932.
- **Mixing up the factions.** Zinoviev and Kamenev were the Left with Trotsky (by 1926); Bukharin led
  the Right.
- **Treating the purges as one event.** Separate the party (show trials), the army and the general
  population.
- **Only negative judgements.** Essays on the Plans must weigh real industrial growth against the
  human cost.
- **Ignoring the post-war years.** Key topic 5 runs to 1953; know the Fourth Plan and post-war purges.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why was Kirov's murder significant?**
It gave Stalin a pretext for arrests and new laws that led to
the show trials and the Great Terror.

**Why did the USSR survive 1941?**
Distance and winter, the defence of Moscow, the evacuation of
industry, war production, Allied aid and enormous sacrifices.

**What does "the revision of history" mean?**
Rewriting Soviet history to glorify Stalin and remove
his enemies, as in the 1938 *Short Course*.
