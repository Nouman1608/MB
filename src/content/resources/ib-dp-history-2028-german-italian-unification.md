**This page is for HL only.** Regional study 5, Unification of Germany and Italy (1815 to 1871), is one of twelve studies in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3, where you write two essays from different regional studies. The guide has six inquiry topics: the impact of the Congress System on Germany and Italy; the growth of nationalism and liberalism in Italy (1815 to 1848); the same for Germany; the impact of the 1848 revolutions; the unification of Italy (1849 to 1871); and the unification of Germany and the German Empire (1849 to 1871). Unlike some studies, this one names both countries, so you need to know both.

## Impact of the Congress System

The **Congress of Vienna** (1814 to 1815) met after the defeat of Napoleon to restore order. Its leading figure was the Austrian foreign minister **Klemens von Metternich**, who valued stability, monarchy and balance of power. Its decisions affected both countries.

- In Italy, Austria gained Lombardy and Venetia directly and had influence over the other states, which were ruled by Bourbon, Habsburg and papal rulers. Piedmont-Sardinia, in the north-west, was strengthened as a buffer against France.
- In Germany, the 300 or so states of the old Holy Roman Empire had been reduced to 39 in a loose **German Confederation**, with a Diet at Frankfurt presided over by Austria. Prussia was enlarged with lands in the west, including the Rhineland.

*Dominance of Austria.* Austria was strong in both regions, with armies in Italy and the chair of the German Diet. It had good reason to oppose nationalism, because a multinational empire might be pulled apart by it.

*Metternich.* He used the Holy Alliance (1815, Russia, Austria and Prussia) and the Congress System of meetings among the great powers to put down revolutions. When revolts broke out in Naples and Piedmont in 1820 and 1821, Austrian troops crushed them. In Germany the **Carlsbad Decrees** (1819) imposed censorship and controls on universities after a nationalist student killed a writer. Historians note that Metternich did not stop change forever; he delayed it, and his system depended on great-power agreement that weakened by the 1820s.

## Growth of nationalism and liberalism in Italy (1815 to 1848)

*Intellectual origins.* Napoleon had united much of Italy under French influence, and this left memories of better administration and a shared identity. Writers and poets promoted Italian culture and language.

*Attempted revolutions.* Secret societies, such as the **Carbonari**, led revolts in 1820 to 1821 and 1831, mainly among army officers, nobles and professionals. They failed because they lacked mass support and because Austria intervened.

*Mazzini and Gioberti.* **Giuseppe Mazzini** founded Young Italy in 1831, wanting a united republic achieved by popular action. His 1830s and 1840s plots failed, such as the Bandiera brothers' attempt in 1844, but he built the idea of national unity. **Vincenzo Gioberti**, in 1843, proposed a federation of Italian states under the pope. This moderate plan appealed to the educated and the Church.

*The papacy.* Pope Pius IX, elected in 1846, seemed liberal at first, with an amnesty for political prisoners. His withdrawal in 1848 from support for the war against Austria showed that a pope could not lead a national cause against Catholic Austria, which damaged Gioberti's plan.

## Growth of nationalism and liberalism in Germany (1815 to 1848)

*Intellectual origins.* German romantic writers valued language and folk culture. Resistance to Napoleon encouraged national feeling. Student societies (*Burschenschaften*) and festivals, such as Hambach in 1832, called for unity and liberty.

*Monarchy in the Vormärz.* The period before March 1848 was marked by conservative rule. Rulers resisted constitutions, controlled the press and relied on censorship. Some southern states had constitutions, but real power stayed with princes.

*The rise of Prussia.* Prussia had a strong army and an effective bureaucracy, and it grew economically in the Rhineland and Ruhr.

*Zollverein.* A customs union begun by Prussia in 1818 spread, and by 1834 the Zollverein included most German states, but not Austria. It removed internal tariffs, encouraged trade and railways, and built Prussian economic leadership. Historians debate whether it caused political unification or only made it easier.

## Impact of the 1848 revolutions

In 1848 revolutions spread across Europe, starting in France. In Italy, rebellions broke out in Sicily, Milan and Venice, and **Charles Albert** of Piedmont declared war on Austria. He was defeated at Custoza (July 1848) and Novara (March 1849). A Roman Republic led by Mazzini and defended by **Garibaldi** fell to French troops, who restored the pope in 1849.

In Germany, revolts in Berlin and Vienna in March 1848 led to the **Frankfurt Parliament**, which met in May to draw up a constitution. It split over whether to include Austria (the Greater Germany solution) or exclude it (the Lesser Germany solution). In 1849 it offered the crown to Frederick William IV of Prussia, who refused. The liberal revolution failed.

*Role of the middle classes and intellectuals.* The middle classes and intellectuals led the revolts and the Frankfurt Parliament, but they were divided and had no control over armies. Peasants and workers had different aims.

*Political impact.* Autocracy was re-established. But some changes lasted: Piedmont kept its constitution (the *Statuto*), and Prussia had a constitution (1850) and a parliament, though with a voting system that favoured the rich.

*Social and cultural impact.* Many liberals went into exile, and nationalism moved from poets to politicians. A famous judgement, that 1848 was a "turning point at which history failed to turn", is often debated. A fair view is that the revolutions failed immediately but changed how people thought about unity.

## Unification of Italy (1849 to 1871)

*Role of Cavour.* **Camillo Benso di Cavour**, prime minister of Piedmont from 1852, modernised its economy and army and wanted to expand Piedmont, not necessarily to create a united Italy. He took Piedmont into the Crimean War (1855) to win the attention of the great powers. At Plombières (1858) he agreed an alliance with Napoleon III against Austria. In 1859 the allies won at Magenta and Solferino, but Napoleon made a separate armistice at Villafranca. Piedmont gained Lombardy, and in 1860 plebiscites in central Italy brought Tuscany, Parma, Modena and Romagna into Piedmont; France received Nice and Savoy.

*Role of Garibaldi.* In May 1860 **Giuseppe Garibaldi** landed in Sicily with about a thousand volunteers (the Red Shirts) and, helped by local revolt and Bourbon weakness, took Sicily and then Naples. Cavour feared he would attack Rome and bring in the French, so he sent Piedmontese troops through the Papal States. Garibaldi handed his conquests to Victor Emmanuel II. The Kingdom of Italy was proclaimed in March 1861, and Cavour died in June.

*Foreign influence.* Napoleon III's help was vital in 1859. In 1866 Italy joined Prussia against Austria and gained Venetia. In 1870, when France withdrew its garrison during the Franco-Prussian War, Italian troops entered Rome, which became the capital in 1871.

*Social and economic factors.* Piedmont's economy and army gave it strength. But the new state was divided: the south was poorer, brigandage was crushed by force, and few Italians spoke standard Italian or voted. Massimo d'Azeglio's line, that Italy was made and now Italians must be made, captures the problem.

## Unification of Germany (1849 to 1871)

*Role of Bismarck.* After Prussia's humiliation at Olmütz (1850), when it gave way to Austria, Prussia strengthened its economy and army. **Otto von Bismarck** became minister-president in 1862 during a conflict between King William I and the liberal parliament over army reform. He ruled without a legal budget, saying that the great questions of the day would be settled by "iron and blood".

*Military reorganization.* Army minister Roon and chief of staff Moltke expanded and modernised the army, using railways and the telegraph, and Prussian breech-loading rifles.

*Wars of unification.* The war against Denmark over Schleswig-Holstein (1864) was fought jointly with Austria. Disagreement over the duchies led to the Austro-Prussian War of 1866, won decisively at Königgrätz. Austria was excluded from Germany, and the North German Confederation formed under Prussia in 1867. Bismarck then used the Ems telegram of July 1870 to provoke France into declaring war. The southern states joined Prussia, France was defeated at Sedan (September 1870), and the German Empire was proclaimed at Versailles on 18 January 1871. France lost Alsace and most of Lorraine.

## Perspectives to use

Older Prussian-centred historians such as Treitschke saw unification as Prussia's destiny. A.J.P. Taylor argued that Bismarck was an opportunist who improvised. Others, such as Wehler, stressed deeper social and economic forces. For Italy, Mack Smith stressed Cavour's calculation, while Gramsci called the process a passive revolution led from above. A good essay weighs individuals against structures.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need both Germany and Italy?**
Yes, the guide names both, and questions may be on either.

**Was unification inevitable?**
Not as it happened. Foreign wars, diplomacy and accident shaped the timing and form.
