**Yield** means the mass of crop (or product) harvested from a given area or in a given time, for example tonnes per hectare.

## 5.1 and 5.2 Glasshouses and polythene tunnels

A **glasshouse** has glass walls and roof. A **polythene tunnel** is a cheaper structure of plastic sheet over hoops. Both create a controlled environment around the crop.

How they raise yield:

- They trap heat from the Sun, so the temperature stays higher than outdoors.
- They protect crops from wind, heavy rain, frost and many pests.
- They let growers control other conditions, such as light, water and carbon dioxide.
- They extend the growing season, so crops can be grown earlier or later in the year.

### Carbon dioxide and temperature

Photosynthesis makes the glucose that the plant uses to grow, so a faster rate of photosynthesis gives more growth and a higher yield.

- **Carbon dioxide:** it is a raw material of photosynthesis. In a glasshouse with enough light and a suitable temperature, adding carbon dioxide raises the rate of photosynthesis, until another factor becomes limiting.
- **Temperature:** photosynthesis depends on enzymes. A rise in temperature increases the rate up to the optimum, because enzyme and substrate molecules collide more often. Above the optimum, enzymes begin to denature and the rate falls.

A grower therefore controls temperature at a suitable level, often with heating, ventilation and shading, and balances the cost of heating and gas against the extra income from a larger yield.

### Worked example 1: cost and benefit

*A glasshouse holds 1000 tomato plants. Without extra carbon dioxide each plant gives 3.2 kg. With extra carbon dioxide and a controlled temperature each gives 4.8 kg. The tomatoes sell at £1.20 per kg and the extra carbon dioxide and heating cost £600 per season. Is it worth it?*

```
extra yield per plant = 4.8 − 3.2 = 1.6 kg
percentage increase = 1.6 ÷ 3.2 × 100 = 50%
extra yield = 1.6 × 1000 = 1600 kg
extra income = 1600 × 1.20 = £1920
net gain = 1920 − 600 = £1320
```

The grower gains £1320 per season, so the extra investment is worth it. Always compare the extra income with the extra cost.

## 5.3 Fertiliser

Crops take minerals from the soil. When a crop is harvested, the minerals in it are removed, so soil becomes less fertile. A **fertiliser** is a substance added to soil to supply the minerals plants need.

- **Nitrates** are used to make amino acids and proteins, so they are needed for growth.
- **Phosphates** and **potassium** compounds are needed for healthy growth, roots and other processes.

With more minerals available, plants grow larger, so the yield is higher. Too much fertiliser can be washed into streams and lakes and cause eutrophication (see sub-topic 4(d)), so the amount and timing matter.

## 5.4 Pest control

A **pest** is an organism that damages crops or reduces the yield. Examples are insects that eat leaves and fruit, and aphids that suck sap and spread viral diseases. Pests reduce yield and the quality of the crop, so growers control them.

### Pesticides

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Kill pests quickly | May kill harmless or useful insects such as bees and pollinators |
| Easy to apply to large areas | Can build up in food chains and harm other animals |
| Increase yield | Pests may become resistant, so the chemical stops working |
| Reasonably cheap | Can wash into water and cause pollution; may be harmful to workers |

### Biological control

**Biological control** uses another living organism to reduce the pest. Examples are ladybirds that eat aphids, and a parasitic wasp that kills whitefly in glasshouses.

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| No chemicals, so no residue on the food | Slow to work and may not remove all the pests |
| Often specific to the pest | The control organism may become a pest or may not stay in the crop area |
| Pests do not usually become resistant | Depends on temperature and other conditions |
| Can last for a long time | May need to be reintroduced each season |

### Worked example 2: choosing a method

*A grower has a glasshouse of tomatoes attacked by whitefly. Suggest and justify a method.*

Use **biological control**, by introducing a parasitic wasp that attacks whitefly. A glasshouse is an enclosed space, so the wasps stay with the crop. There are no chemical residues on the tomatoes. The disadvantage is that it works more slowly than spraying.

## 5.5 and 5.6 Yeast, bread and anaerobic respiration

**Yeast** is a single-celled fungus. In bread making, yeast is mixed with flour, water and sugar.

- Yeast respires the sugar. In the dough there is little oxygen, so it carries out **anaerobic respiration**:

```
glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide + (a little) energy
```

- The carbon dioxide forms bubbles in the dough, which stretches and **rises**.
- When the bread is baked, the heat kills the yeast and makes the gas bubbles expand, which gives the bread a light texture. The ethanol evaporates.

### Practical: yeast in different conditions (5.6)

**Aim:** to investigate how a condition such as temperature affects the rate of anaerobic respiration in yeast.

1. Mix yeast with a glucose solution in a boiling tube.
2. Add a layer of oil to keep oxygen out, so the respiration is anaerobic.
3. Place the tube in a water bath at a chosen temperature.
4. Measure the rate: count the bubbles of gas per minute, or collect the gas in a gas syringe, or use limewater to show the gas is carbon dioxide (it turns cloudy).
5. Repeat at several temperatures with the same mass of yeast and the same volume and concentration of glucose.

**Variable to control:** the volume and concentration of glucose, the mass of yeast, the time and the pH. **Expected result:** the rate rises with temperature up to about the optimum for yeast, then falls as enzymes denature.

### Worked example 3: reading results

*Bubbles per minute at 15 °C, 25 °C, 35 °C and 45 °C were 8, 20, 46 and 12. Describe the pattern and calculate the percentage increase from 15 °C to 25 °C.*

```
percentage increase = (20 − 8) ÷ 8 × 100 = 150%
```

The rate rises to a peak at 35 °C, because enzymes work faster, then drops at 45 °C because the enzymes begin to denature. The peak lies near 35 °C, but with only four temperatures the exact optimum is not shown.

## 5.7 Bacteria and yoghurt

Yoghurt is made from milk by the bacterium *Lactobacillus*.

1. The milk is heated to kill unwanted microorganisms, then cooled to a warm temperature (about 40 to 45 °C).
2. *Lactobacillus* is added.
3. The mixture is kept warm for several hours.
4. The bacteria respire the milk sugar, **lactose**, and produce **lactic acid**.
5. The lactic acid lowers the pH, which makes the milk proteins clump together (curdle), so the milk thickens and tastes sour. The low pH also stops the growth of many other bacteria.

Heating before adding the bacteria ensures that only the wanted bacteria grow. The warm temperature gives the bacterial enzymes a good rate.

## 5.8 The industrial fermenter

A **fermenter** is a large vessel in which microorganisms are grown in large numbers to make a product such as an antibiotic, a protein or a medicine. Conditions inside are controlled.

| Condition | Why it is needed |
|---|---|
| **Aseptic precautions** (sterilise the vessel and nutrient medium) | Prevent contaminating microorganisms from competing for nutrients or spoiling the product |
| **Nutrients** (sugar for energy, nitrogen source, minerals) | Supply energy and materials for growth and reproduction |
| **Optimum temperature** | Enzymes work at their fastest. Respiration produces heat, so a cooling water jacket is used |
| **Optimum pH** | Enzymes work best at a certain pH. The pH is monitored and adjusted |
| **Oxygenation** (sterile air bubbled in) | Aerobic microorganisms need oxygen for respiration |
| **Agitation** (stirring paddles) | Mixes nutrients, oxygen and heat evenly through the liquid and brings microorganisms in contact with food |

### Worked example 4: population growth in a fermenter

*A fermenter starts with 1000 bacteria. The population doubles every 30 minutes. How many bacteria are there after 4 hours?*

```
4 hours = 8 half-hours = 8 doublings
1000 × 2⁸ = 1000 × 256 = 256 000
```

The fast doubling shows why good conditions are important: nutrients and oxygen are used up quickly.

## 5.9B Fish farming (Paper 2 only)

Fish farms raise large numbers of fish as a source of protein. The methods deal with the problems of keeping many fish in one place.

| Problem | Method used |
|---|---|
| **Water quality** (oxygen, temperature, pH, ammonia) | Monitor and aerate the water, filter it, and keep the right stocking density |
| **Intraspecific predation** (fish eating their own species) | Sort the fish by size and keep fish of similar size together |
| **Interspecific predation** (other species eating the fish) | Use nets and covers to keep out birds and other predators |
| **Disease** | Keep stocking density lower, use vaccines and treatments, remove sick fish |
| **Waste products** (faeces, uneaten food) | Remove waste, change water or filter it, so ammonia does not build up |
| **Quality and frequency of feeding** | Use a high-protein diet at controlled times and amounts, so fish grow without waste |
| **Selective breeding** | Breed fish that grow fast, resist disease and convert food efficiently |

## Common mistakes

- Saying more carbon dioxide always raises the yield. Another factor, such as light or temperature, can become limiting.
- Saying that high temperature increases photosynthesis without limit. Enzymes denature above the optimum.
- Writing that yeast produces oxygen or that bread rises because of "heat". The gas is carbon dioxide from anaerobic respiration.
- Saying that the oil layer in the yeast practical "provides" something. It keeps oxygen out.
- Saying that *Lactobacillus* turns lactose into alcohol. It produces lactic acid.
- Writing "to make the fermenter hot" for agitation. Stirring mixes the contents.
- Forgetting the disadvantage of pesticides, such as resistance.

## Self-check

1. Give two ways a glasshouse increases crop yield.
2. Name the gas that makes bread dough rise.
3. State one advantage and one disadvantage of biological control.
4. Why are aseptic precautions used in a fermenter?
5. (Paper 2 only) Why do fish farmers sort fish by size?

**Answers:** (1) Any two of: higher temperature, extra carbon dioxide, protection from pests and weather, longer growing season. (2) Carbon dioxide. (3) Advantage: no chemical residues (or specific to the pest). Disadvantage: slow (or may not remove all pests). (4) To stop other microorganisms competing for nutrients or spoiling the product. (5) To reduce larger fish eating smaller ones of the same species.

**Is the yeast practical examined?**
The specification asks you to investigate anaerobic respiration by yeast. Questions can test the method, variables and results.

**What does a fermenter do?**
It provides controlled conditions for growing microorganisms in large numbers to make a product.
