**How to use this set.** Question 1 of Paper 1 prints three of the fifteen set passages in Arabic and
English; you choose two and answer part (a), the themes, worth 4 marks, and part (b), their importance
today, worth 4 marks. Section A checks your knowledge of individual passages. Section B gives full
Question 1-style tasks on pairs of passages. Keep the English translations from the syllabus appendix
open while you practise.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** Name the **three** groups into which the set passages are divided. **[3]**

**2.** Sura 41.37 tells people not to adore the sun and the moon. State what they should do instead,
and the reason the verse gives. **[2]**

**3.** Give **two** things Sura 42.5 says the angels do. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what the Arabic phrase *al-Hayy al-Qayyum* in Sura 2.255 teaches about God. **[2]**

**5.** In Sura 2.30–37, describe what happened when the angels were told to bow down to Adam, and
explain what this teaches Muslims about pride. **[3]**

**6.** Name **three** miracles of Isa mentioned in Sura 5.110, and explain the importance of the
repeated words "by my leave". **[4]**

**7.** Sura 93.6–8 reminds the Prophet (pbuh) of three ways God cared for him. Name them, and state
**two** duties that verses 9–11 draw from them. **[4]**

For each question, write about **both** passages.

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Cambridge marks Question 1 by levels, and the two passages are read
together for one mark in each part. The guide below breaks that into checkable points: in part (a),
for each passage, the theme correctly identified [1] and explained with reference to the wording [1];
in part (b), for each passage, one reasoned application to life today [2]. Other valid points of the
same quality earn the same credit.

**1.** God in Himself [1]; God's relationship with the created world [1]; God's Messengers [1].

**2.** They should adore (worship) God alone [1], because He is the one who created the sun and the
moon, which are only His signs [1].

**3.** They celebrate the praises of their Lord [1]; they pray for forgiveness for beings on
earth [1].

**4.** *Al-Hayy* means the Living: God is alive in a way that has no beginning or end [1]. *Al-Qayyum*
means the Self-Subsisting: He depends on nothing, while everything depends on Him to exist and
continue [1].

**5.** All the angels bowed down except Iblis [1], who refused and was haughty, so he became one of
those who reject faith [1]. Pride led directly to disobedience, so Muslims should be humble and
accept God's commands even when they do not understand them fully [1].

**6.** Any three, [1] each: he spoke to people in childhood (in the cradle) and in maturity; he made
the figure of a bird from clay and it became a bird; he healed those born blind; he healed lepers;
he brought forth the dead. Maximum 3. The words "by my leave" show that the miracles were God's power
working through Isa, not Isa's own, so he is a prophet and not divine, which upholds tawhid [1].

**7.** He found him an orphan and gave him shelter; wandering and gave him guidance; in need and made
him independent [2 for all three, 1 for two]. Any two duties, [1] each: do not treat the orphan
harshly; do not drive away the beggar (one who asks); tell of the bounty of your Lord. Maximum 4.

**8. (a)** Sura 2.255: God in Himself, His unique, living and all-powerful nature [1]; He is "the
living, the self-subsisting", never overtaken by slumber or sleep, owns all in the heavens and
earth, allows intercession only by His permission and knows everything, while people know only
what He wills [1]. Sura 6.101–103: God as Creator with no partner or son [1]; He originated the
heavens and earth, "how can He have a son when He has no consort?", He created and knows all
things, and "no vision can grasp Him", yet He grasps all vision [1].
**(b)** Sura 2.255: Muslims recite it for protection, for example before sleep, because they trust a
Guardian who never tires; knowing He sees and knows everything encourages honesty in private [2].
Sura 6.101–103: Muslims reject the idea that God has offspring and do not make images of Him; they
worship Him directly, without intermediaries, because He alone "has power to dispose of all
affairs" [2].

**9. (a)** Sura 1: God's relationship with creation as merciful Lord, Judge and Guide [1]; He is
"cherisher and sustainer of the worlds", "most gracious, most merciful" and "Master of the day of
judgement"; people worship Him alone, seek His help and ask for the straight way [1]. Sura 99: the
Day of Judgement and accountability [1]; the earth will be shaken, throw up its burdens and
declare what happened on it, and people will be shown their deeds down to "an atom's weight" of
good or evil [1].
**(b)** Sura 1: recited in every unit of every prayer, so several times a day Muslims renew their
dependence on God and ask for guidance in daily choices [2]. Sura 99: because even tiny deeds are
recorded, Muslims do small kindnesses, such as smiling or removing litter, and avoid small wrongs
such as minor lies [2].

**10. (a)** Sura 96.1–5: God as Creator and Teacher of humanity [1]; the command "Read! in the name of
your Lord, who created" links knowledge to God, who created man from a clot and "taught by the pen"
what man did not know [1]. Sura 114: God as Protector of mankind [1]; people seek refuge with the
Lord, King and God of mankind from the whisperer who withdraws and whispers into hearts, whether
jinn or human [1].
**(b)** Sura 96.1–5: Muslims value education for boys and girls and begin study in God's name, seeing
learning as worship; scholars and teachers are respected [2]. Sura 114: when tempted to do wrong or
troubled by fear, Muslims turn to God, reciting the sura, for example before sleep, rather than
relying on charms or superstition [2].

**11. (a)** Sura 42.4–5: God's greatness and mercy [1]; all in the heavens and earth belong to Him, He is
"most high, most great", the heavens are almost rent asunder, the angels praise Him and pray for
people, and He is "oft-forgiving, most merciful" [1]. Sura 93: God's care for His Messenger [1]; His
Lord has not forsaken him, the Hereafter will be better, and as God sheltered, guided and enriched
him, he must care for orphans and those who ask [1].
**(b)** Sura 42.4–5: Muslims who have sinned should not despair but repent, knowing God forgives and
even angels pray for them; awe of His greatness keeps them humble [2]. Sura 93: in times of
hardship Muslims trust that God has not abandoned them; they support orphans, for example by
sponsoring an orphan, and give to those who ask [2].

**12. (a)** Sura 2.30–37: God's Messengers, beginning with Adam as khalifa (vicegerent) [1]; God taught
Adam the names, the angels bowed except proud Iblis, Adam and his wife slipped, then Adam learned
words of repentance and God turned to him, "often-returning, most merciful" [1]. Sura 41.37: God in
Himself, the Creator above His creation [1]; night, day, sun and moon are His signs, and worship is
for the One who created them, not for created things [1].
**(b)** Sura 2.30–37: humans are responsible for looking after the earth, for example avoiding waste and
pollution; when they sin they repent sincerely, trusting God's forgiveness as Adam did [2]. Sura
41.37: Muslims do not worship or seek luck from stars, the sun or any created thing, and they
reflect on nature as evidence of God; the prostration of salat is for God alone [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- In Section B, writing about only one passage: each passage carries half of each part.
- Giving the same application, "believe in God", for every passage instead of one tied to its wording.
- Describing the story in Sura 2.30–37 without stating the theme of khalifa and repentance.
- Forgetting the Arabic: a key term such as *khalifa* or *al-Samad*, explained, strengthens part (a).

## Frequently asked questions

**How long should a Question 1 answer be?**
About a short paragraph per passage for each part: one stating the theme with evidence, one giving a
specific application.

**Are these questions from Cambridge papers?**
No. They are original questions in the Question 1 format.
