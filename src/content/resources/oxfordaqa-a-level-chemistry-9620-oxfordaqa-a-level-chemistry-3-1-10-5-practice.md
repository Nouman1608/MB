These questions cover **3.1.10.5 pH curves, titrations and indicators** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, including required practical 7. This is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 3, with practical skills also tested in Unit 5. Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 25 °C and give pH to 2 decimal places. The titration data are invented for practice.

Indicator ranges for this page: methyl orange 3.1–4.4; methyl red 4.4–6.2; bromothymol blue 6.0–7.6; phenolphthalein 8.3–10.0.

## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between the *end point* and the *equivalence point* of a titration. **[2]**

**2.** 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution is titrated with 0.0850 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid. The titres are 21.45 cm³ (rough), 20.95 cm³, 21.05 cm³ and 21.00 cm³. Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide. **[3]**

**3.** Propanoic acid is titrated with sodium hydroxide. State whether the pH at the equivalence point is below, equal to or above 7, and explain why. **[2]**

**4.** Choose the most suitable indicator for titrating hydrochloric acid with aqueous ammonia, and justify your choice. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why no indicator is suitable for titrating ethanoic acid with aqueous ammonia. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why the pH curve for 0.100 mol dm⁻³ ethanoic acid titrated with NaOH starts at a higher pH than the curve for 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A 1.50 g sample of impure benzoic acid, C₆H₅COOH (Mr = 122.0), is dissolved and made up to 250 cm³. 25.0 cm³ portions are titrated with 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. The mean concordant titre is 23.40 cm³. The impurities do not react with NaOH. Calculate the percentage purity of the benzoic acid. **[4]**

**8.** 20.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid is titrated with 0.200 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide at 25 °C.

**(a)** State the volume of NaOH needed to reach the equivalence point. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH before any NaOH is added. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the pH after 19.0 cm³ of NaOH has been added. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the pH after 21.0 cm³ of NaOH has been added. **[3]**

**(e)** Explain why the pH changes so sharply between 19.9 cm³ and 20.1 cm³. **[1]**

**9.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.0800 mol dm⁻³ benzoic acid (Ka = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³) is titrated with 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of NaOH at the equivalence point. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the pH before any NaOH is added. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the pH after 10.0 cm³ of NaOH has been added. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate the pH after 15.0 cm³ of NaOH has been added. **[2]**

**(e)** Explain why phenolphthalein is suitable for this titration but methyl orange is not. **[2]**

**10.** 0.100 mol dm⁻³ aqueous ammonia is added from a burette to 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. Describe the pH curve you would expect, giving the starting pH, the equivalence volume, the approximate position of the vertical section, the pH at equivalence and how the final pH compares with that for NaOH of the same concentration. **[4]**

**11.** In required practical 7 a student titrates 25.0 cm³ of an unknown weak monoprotic acid with NaOH. The equivalence point is at 24.40 cm³ and the pH at 12.20 cm³ is 4.87.

**(a)** The acid is one of: methanoic acid (pKa 3.74), ethanoic acid (pKa 4.76) or propanoic acid (pKa 4.87). Identify it and explain your reasoning. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest two ways the student could make sure the vertical section of the curve is well defined. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the pH meter is calibrated before use. **[1]**

**12.** The titration in Question 8 is repeated with 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ nitric acid and 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. State two ways in which the pH curve differs from the original and one way in which it is the same. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The equivalence point is where the amounts of acid and base exactly react according to the equation [1]; the end point is where the indicator changes colour [1]

**2.** Mean of concordant titres = (20.95 + 21.05 + 21.00) / 3 = 21.00 cm³ [1]; n(HNO₃) = 0.0850 × 21.00 / 1000 = 1.785 × 10⁻³ mol = n(KOH) [1]; [KOH] = 1.785 × 10⁻³ / 0.0250 = **0.0714 mol dm⁻³** [1]

**3.** **Above 7** [1]; the solution contains propanoate ions, which accept protons from water, CH₃CH₂COO⁻ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOH + OH⁻ [1]

**4.** **Methyl red** (methyl orange also accepted) [1]; its whole range (4.4–6.2) lies inside the vertical section, which is in the acidic region (about pH 3 to 7) for a strong acid–weak base titration [1]

**5.** The curve has no vertical section, so the colour would change gradually over a large volume [1]

**6.** Ethanoic acid is only slightly dissociated, so [H⁺] is much lower than in hydrochloric acid of the same concentration [1]

**7.** n(NaOH) = 0.0500 × 23.40 / 1000 = 1.17 × 10⁻³ mol = n(acid) in 25.0 cm³ [1]; n in 250 cm³ = 1.17 × 10⁻² mol [1]; mass = 1.17 × 10⁻² × 122.0 = 1.427 g [1]; purity = 1.427 / 1.50 × 100 = **95.2 %** [1]

**8. (a)** **20.0 cm³** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³; pH = **0.70** [1]

**(c)** Excess H⁺ = 4.00 × 10⁻³ − 3.80 × 10⁻³ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; [H⁺] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.0390 = 5.13 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³, pH = **2.29** [1]

**(d)** Excess OH⁻ = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol [1]; [OH⁻] = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ / 0.0410 = 4.88 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ / 4.88 × 10⁻³ = 2.05 × 10⁻¹², pH = **11.69** [1]

**(e)** Almost all the H⁺ has reacted, so a tiny volume of NaOH changes [H⁺] (or [OH⁻]) by a very large factor, and pH is a logarithmic scale (pH 3.30 at 19.9 cm³ and 10.70 at 20.1 cm³) [1]

*Examiner insight:* In (c) and (d) the total volume is 39.0 cm³ or 41.0 cm³. Dividing by 20.0 cm³ is the most common error.

**9. (a)** n(acid) = 0.0800 × 0.0250 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol; V = 2.00 × 10⁻³ / 0.100 = **20.0 cm³** [1]

**(b)** [H⁺] = √(6.3 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.0800) = 2.24 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **2.65** [1]

**(c)** 10.0 cm³ is half the equivalence volume, so pH = pKa = −log₁₀(6.3 × 10⁻⁵) = **4.20** [1]

**(d)** n(A⁻) = 1.50 × 10⁻³ mol; n(HA) = 0.50 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; [H⁺] = 6.3 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.50 / 1.50 = 2.1 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³, pH = **4.68** [1]

**(e)** Phenolphthalein's whole range (8.3–10.0) lies inside the vertical section, which is in the alkaline region [1]; methyl orange (3.1–4.4) would change in the buffer region, long before the equivalence point [1]

**10.** Starts at pH 1 (strong acid fully dissociated) [1]; equivalence at 25.0 cm³ [1]; vertical section roughly pH 3 to 7, with the equivalence pH below 7 [1]; levels off at a lower pH (about 9 to 10) than with NaOH, because ammonia is a weak base [1]

**11. (a)** **Propanoic acid** [1]; 12.20 cm³ is half the equivalence volume, where pH = pKa, and 4.87 matches propanoic acid [1]

**(b)** Add the NaOH in small portions (for example 0.10–0.20 cm³) near the equivalence point [1]; stir and wait for a steady reading before recording each pH [1]

**(c)** So that the meter gives accurate pH readings, by checking it against buffer solutions of known pH [1]

**12.** Starts at a higher pH (1.70 instead of 0.70) [1]; the vertical section is shorter, and the final pH is lower [1]; the equivalence volume (20.0 cm³) and equivalence pH (7.00) are the same [1]

*Examiner insight:* "State" needs only the differences, but each must be a feature you could see on the graph.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Including a rough or non-concordant titre in the mean.
- Forgetting the factor of 10 when a portion of a 250 cm³ solution was titrated.
- Giving pH = pKa at the equivalence point instead of at half-equivalence.
- Justifying an indicator without stating that its whole range lies within the vertical section.
