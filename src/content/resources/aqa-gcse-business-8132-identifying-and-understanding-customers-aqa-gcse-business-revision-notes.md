## Core recall

### What customers need

| Need | What the customer is looking for | Example of a business meeting it |
|---|---|---|
| Price or value for money | A price that feels fair for what they get | A supermarket's own-brand range |
| Quality | A product that works, lasts and meets expectations | A long guarantee on a washing machine |
| Design and features | Appearance and functions that suit them | A phone case in many colours |
| Convenience | Easy to find and buy, at a suitable time and place | A late-opening pharmacy |
| Choice | Options to suit different tastes | A café with dairy-free milks |
| Service | Helpful staff, fast delivery, easy returns | Free next-day delivery |

**Identifying** customers = knowing who they are. **Understanding** customers = knowing which needs matter most to them and why.

Needs differ between customers and change over time, for example with income, technology or attitudes to the environment.

### The five reasons in the specification

Memory hook: **B**usinesses **S**ell **M**ore, **M**inus **C**ostly errors.

| Reason | Chain to write |
|---|---|
| **B**: provide a product or service customers will **buy** | Built around real needs → more likely to sell → revenue earned on the investment |
| **S**: increase **sales** | Needs met → more customers buy and return → recommendations bring new customers |
| **M**: select the correct **marketing mix** | Price, product, promotion and place fitted to the target customer → less wasted spending |
| **M**: avoid costly **mistakes** | Find out before spending on development, stock and promotion → money not lost on a product nobody wants |
| **C**: be **competitive** | Offer what customers value more than rivals do → customers choose this business |

### Where the methods sit

- How to group customers: segmentation (3.5.2).
- How to find out what they want: market research (3.5.3).
- How to act on it: the marketing mix (3.5.4).

## Quick calculations

Questions on this section sometimes use simple figures to show the effect of meeting or missing a need.

- **Extra revenue:** (new sales − old sales) × price. Sales rise from 800 to 950 units at £6: 150 × £6 = £900.
- **Percentage increase:** change ÷ original × 100. £900 ÷ (800 × £6) × 100 = £900 ÷ £4,800 × 100 = 18.75%.
- **Money at risk:** add development, stock and promotion costs. £15,000 + £8,000 + £6,000 = £29,000.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Customer | A person or organisation that buys a product or service |
| Consumer | The person who uses the product (not always the buyer, as with a parent buying toys) |
| Customer needs | What customers want from a product or service before they will buy it |
| Target market | The group of customers a business aims its product at |
| Marketing mix | The combination of price, product, promotion and place |
| Competitive advantage | Something that makes customers choose one business over its rivals |
| Customer satisfaction | How far a product or service meets or beats what customers expected |
| Repeat custom | Customers who come back to buy again |

## Exam traps

- Listing the five reasons instead of explaining one in context.
- Writing "to make a profit": the specification's reasons are the five above.
- Treating every customer as having the same needs.
- Describing a product feature ("it has a big screen") instead of the need it meets ("customers want to watch films while travelling").
- Forgetting that needs change, so research must be repeated.
- Giving a reason with no consequence for the business.

## Self-test

1. State the five reasons the specification gives for identifying and satisfying customer needs.
2. Give three types of customer need.
3. What is the difference between a customer and a consumer?
4. Explain how understanding customers helps a business choose its marketing mix.
5. Why might a business that understood its customers five years ago no longer understand them?
6. Give an example of a business meeting a need for convenience.
7. Explain how satisfied customers can increase sales without extra promotion spending.
8. A gift shop plans a range that will cost £4,000 to develop, £6,500 to stock and £1,500 to promote. How much money is at risk if the range does not sell?
9. Sales rise from 2,000 to 2,300 a month at £4 each. Calculate the increase in monthly revenue.
10. How can understanding customers make a business more competitive without cutting prices?
11. Which section of the specification covers how businesses group customers?

### Answers

1. To provide a product or service customers will buy; to increase sales; to select the correct marketing mix; to avoid costly mistakes; to be competitive.
2. Any three of: price or value for money, quality, design and features, convenience, choice, service.
3. The customer buys the product; the consumer uses it. They can be different people.
4. Each of price, product, promotion and place can be chosen to suit the target customers, for example a low price and social media for students, so less money is wasted.
5. Needs change with income, technology and attitudes, so old knowledge may be out of date.
6. For example, a bakery offering phone pre-orders, or a shop opening late.
7. They come back and recommend the business to others, bringing in new customers at no extra cost.
8. £4,000 + £6,500 + £1,500 = **£12,000**.
9. 300 × £4 = **£1,200** a month.
10. It can offer something customers value that rivals do not, such as longer opening hours, better service or a better-suited design.
11. Segmentation, section 3.5.2.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is this a large section of the AQA GCSE Business specification?**
It is short, but it underpins the rest of marketing, so its ideas appear in questions on segmentation, research and the marketing mix.

**How should I explain a reason for identifying customer needs?**
Name one reason, apply it to the business in the question and finish with a consequence, such as higher sales or avoided costs.
