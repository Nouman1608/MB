Cambridge International A-Level Law (9084) is a staged qualification of four papers, each worth exactly
25% of the full A Level. Paper 1 (English Legal System) is 1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks. Paper 2
(Criminal Law) is 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks. Together, Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level. Paper 3 (Law
of Contract) and Paper 4 (Law of Tort) are each 1 hour 30 minutes, 75 marks, and complete the full A
Level. These notes complement the site's guides to
[The English Legal System](/resources/a-level-cambridge-law-english-legal-system/) and
[Criminal Law](/resources/a-level-cambridge-law-criminal-law/).

## Equal weighting means no paper can be safely deprioritised

Unlike qualifications where later papers carry more marks, all four of 9084's papers are worth exactly
25% each. **Exam-preparation priority**: revise all four substantive areas -- legal system, criminal,
contract, tort -- to a comparable depth, since underinvesting in any one paper costs exactly as much as
underinvesting in any other.

## Paper 2's marks are concentrated differently from Paper 1's

Paper 1 spreads 75 marks across legal-system topics (courts, judiciary, juries, legal personnel); Paper
2 concentrates 60 marks specifically on criminal law doctrine and application. **Exam-preparation
priority**: because Paper 2 has fewer marks over the same 1 hour 30 minutes as Paper 1, expect more time
per mark on Paper 2's problem questions -- plan accordingly rather than assuming identical pacing across
both AS papers.

## Contract and Tort both reward the same applied-problem technique

Papers 3 and 4 are both problem-based: applying legal rules to a fact scenario rather than describing
law in the abstract. **Exam-preparation priority**: the analytical structure that works for Contract
problem questions transfers directly to Tort -- practise the technique once, thoroughly, and apply it
across both papers rather than treating them as needing separate approaches.

## Case citation precision is examined across all four papers

Marks are available specifically for correctly naming and applying relevant case law, not just stating
legal principles in general terms. **Exam-preparation priority**: build a compact bank of named cases per
topic and drill recalling which principle each case establishes, since a legal principle stated without
its supporting authority scores lower than the same point made with a named case.

## AS and A Level content build on the same core legal skills

Because Papers 1-2 (AS) and Papers 3-4 (A Level) all reward the same underlying skills -- rule
identification, case citation, and applying law to facts -- technique practised early in the course
carries forward rather than needing to be relearned for the A Level-only papers. **Exam-preparation
priority**: treat AS-level answer technique as the foundation for Contract and Tort, not as a separate
skill set to be built from scratch once A Level-only teaching begins.

## Worked routine: structuring a problem-question answer

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - identify the specific legal issue raised by the facts:
State it as a question the law must answer, not a general topic
area.

Step 2 - state the relevant rule, with a named case as authority:
The rule alone is not enough -- name the case that establishes it.

Step 3 - apply the rule to the facts given, not to facts in
general:
Reference specific details from the scenario directly.

Step 4 - consider a plausible counter-argument or distinguishing
case:
Shows the rule is understood, not just recited.

Step 5 - reach a conclusion that follows from the application:
Not a restatement of the rule -- state what it means for the
parties in this scenario.
```

Step 3 is where marks are most often lost: a technically correct rule stated without being applied to
the scenario's own specific facts earns far less credit than the same rule applied directly to them.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise all four papers to comparable depth, since none outweighs another; build
  a named-case bank per topic; practise the problem-question structure once and apply it consistently
  across Contract and Tort.
- **During any paper**: identify the precise legal issue before stating any rule.
- **In every problem answer**: name a case as authority for every rule, apply it to the scenario's own
  facts, and reach a conclusion specific to those facts.

## Self-test

1. How are the four papers weighted, and what does that imply for revision priority?
2. Why might Paper 2 need different pacing from Paper 1, despite the same time allowance?
3. What technique transfers directly between Papers 3 and 4?
4. What is the most common way marks are lost in a problem-question answer?

**Answers:** 1. All four papers are weighted 25% each, so no paper can be safely deprioritised relative
to the others. 2. Paper 2 has fewer marks (60) than Paper 1 (75) over the same 1 hour 30 minutes, so more
time is available per mark, changing the pacing calculation. 3. The applied-problem-question structure
-- identifying the issue, stating the rule with case authority, applying it to the facts, and reaching a
conclusion -- transfers directly from Contract to Tort. 4. Stating a legal rule correctly but failing to
apply it to the scenario's own specific facts, rather than to the topic in general.
