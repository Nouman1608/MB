These notes condense section 11.7, Carboxylic acids, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All three outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [carboxylic acids study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-carboxylic-acids/); then try the [carboxylic acids practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-carboxylic-acids-practice/).

## The three outcomes in one line each

1. Reactions of carboxylic acids with metals, bases and carbonates, with names and formulae of the salts.
2. Ethanoic acid from ethanol: acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII), or bacterial oxidation in vinegar production.
3. Carboxylic acid + alcohol, with an acid catalyst, gives an ester.

## The acids and their ions

| Acid | Formula | Salt ion | Example salt |
|---|---|---|---|
| Methanoic | HCOOH | methanoate, HCOO⁻ | sodium methanoate, HCOONa |
| Ethanoic | CH₃COOH | ethanoate, CH₃COO⁻ | magnesium ethanoate, (CH₃COO)₂Mg |
| Propanoic | CH₃CH₂COOH | propanoate, CH₃CH₂COO⁻ | calcium propanoate, (CH₃CH₂COO)₂Ca |
| Butanoic | CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH | butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COO⁻ | potassium butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOK |

All carboxylate ions are 1−. Metal ions: Na⁺, K⁺ (1+, one ion each); Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺, Zn²⁺, Cu²⁺ (2+, two carboxylate ions, use brackets).

## Reaction patterns

| Reacts with | General equation | Example | Observations |
|---|---|---|---|
| Metal | acid + metal → salt + hydrogen | 2CH₃COOH + Mg → (CH₃COO)₂Mg + H₂ | fizzing; metal dissolves; gas pops with lighted splint |
| Base | acid + base → salt + water | CH₃COOH + NaOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O | no visible change with an alkali |
| Carbonate | acid + carbonate → salt + water + carbon dioxide | 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂ | fizzing; gas turns limewater milky |

Carboxylic acids are **weak** acids (partially dissociated), so they react more slowly than strong acids of the same concentration, but give the same amount of product.

### Building a salt equation in three steps

1. Write the salt formula from the ion charges, e.g. zinc ethanoate is (CH₃COO)₂Zn.
2. Count how many acid molecules the salt needs: two CH₃COO⁻ ions means 2CH₃COOH.
3. Add the other products for the reaction type and balance: 2CH₃COOH + ZnO → (CH₃COO)₂Zn + H₂O.

## Making ethanoic acid from ethanol

| Method | Reagent | Equation | Sign |
|---|---|---|---|
| Laboratory | acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII), warm | CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O | purple → colourless |
| Vinegar production | bacteria, oxygen from air | C₂H₅OH + O₂ → CH₃COOH + H₂O | wine or cider turns sour |

Oxidation here: ethanol **gains oxygen and loses hydrogen**. It is not combustion; no CO₂ forms.

## Esterification

```
carboxylic acid + alcohol --acid catalyst--> ester + water
```

| Acid | Alcohol | Ester |
|---|---|---|
| ethanoic | ethanol | ethyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ |
| ethanoic | methanol | methyl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH₃ |
| propanoic | methanol | methyl propanoate, CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ |
| methanoic | propan-1-ol | propyl methanoate, HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| butanoic | ethanol | ethyl butanoate, CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₂CH₃ |

Water forms from the –OH of the acid and the H of the alcohol's –OH.

## Telling ethanol and ethanoic acid apart

| Test | Ethanol | Ethanoic acid |
|---|---|---|
| Add sodium carbonate | no fizzing | fizzing; CO₂ turns limewater milky |
| Add magnesium | no reaction | fizzing; H₂ pops |
| Warm with acidified KMnO₄ | purple → colourless | stays purple |
| Litmus | no change | blue litmus turns red |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Carboxylic acid | homologous series with the –COOH functional group; CₙH₂ₙ₊₁COOH |
| Weak acid | acid that is partially dissociated in aqueous solution |
| Carboxylate salt | salt of a carboxylic acid, named -oate |
| Oxidation | gain of oxygen (here also loss of hydrogen) |
| Oxidising agent | substance that oxidises another, e.g. acidified potassium manganate(VII) |
| Vinegar | dilute aqueous ethanoic acid, made by bacterial oxidation of ethanol |
| Esterification | reaction of a carboxylic acid with an alcohol to form an ester and water |
| Ester | compound containing the –COO– linkage between two carbon groups |

## Exam traps

- Salts end **-oate**: sodium ethanoate, not sodium ethanoic.
- Brackets for 2+ metals: (CH₃COO)₂Ca.
- Metal → **hydrogen**; carbonate → **carbon dioxide**; base → only salt and water.
- Manganate(VII) goes **purple → colourless**.
- Bacterial oxidation needs **oxygen** from the air.
- Esterification needs an **acid catalyst** and gives **water** as well as the ester.
- Ester name: alcohol part first.
- Weak acid means partially dissociated, not dilute.
- Oxidation of ethanol gives ethanoic acid and water; combustion gives carbon dioxide and water. Do not mix the two equations.

## Self-test

1. Name the salt formed from propanoic acid and sodium hydroxide.
2. Write the formula of magnesium methanoate.
3. Write the equation for ethanoic acid reacting with calcium carbonate.
4. Name the gas produced when ethanoic acid reacts with zinc.
5. Name the reagent used to oxidise ethanol to ethanoic acid in the laboratory.
6. State the colour change seen in question 5.
7. Name the process in which bacteria convert ethanol to ethanoic acid.
8. Name the ester made from methanol and butanoic acid.
9. State the condition needed for esterification.
10. Why does ethanoic acid react more slowly with magnesium than hydrochloric acid of the same concentration?
11. Describe a test to show that a liquid is ethanoic acid and not ethanol.
12. Write the equation for making ethyl ethanoate.

## Answers

1. Sodium propanoate.
2. (HCOO)₂Mg.
3. 2CH₃COOH + CaCO₃ → (CH₃COO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂.
4. Hydrogen.
5. Acidified aqueous potassium manganate(VII).
6. Purple to colourless.
7. Vinegar production (bacterial oxidation).
8. Methyl butanoate.
9. An acid catalyst.
10. It is a weak acid, only partially dissociated, so there is a lower concentration of H⁺ ions.
11. Add sodium carbonate: ethanoic acid fizzes and the gas turns limewater milky; ethanol does not fizz.
12. CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH → CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O.
