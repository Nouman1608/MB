This guide covers subtopic **13.1 Excretion** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to describe excretion as the removal of toxic materials and the waste products of metabolism from organisms, to state that carbon dioxide is a waste product of respiration which is excreted through the lungs, and to state that urea is a toxic waste product produced in the liver from the breakdown of excess amino acids.

The [Excretion guide](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-excretion/) covers the whole topic. This subtopic is short, so this page spends its time on precise definitions, on what makes each waste product toxic, on how urea is made and moved, and on the distinctions that examiners test. Use the [13.1 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-excretion-revision-notes/) for recall and the [13.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-excretion-practice/) to test yourself.

## What excretion is

**Excretion is the removal of toxic materials and the waste products of metabolism from organisms.**

**Metabolism** is the sum of all the chemical reactions in an organism. Many of these reactions make substances that are not needed, or that are harmful if they build up. The cells and the body must get rid of them.

Look at the two parts of the definition.

1. **Waste products of metabolism**: substances made by the cell's own chemical reactions, such as carbon dioxide from respiration and urea from the breakdown of amino acids.
2. **Toxic materials**: poisonous substances, including some taken in from outside, which have to be removed.

### Excretion, egestion and secretion

These three words are easily mixed up.

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Excretion** | Removal of toxic materials and waste products of metabolism | Carbon dioxide breathed out; urea in urine |
| **Egestion** | Removal of undigested food (faeces) from the gut | Faeces leaving through the anus |
| **Secretion** | Release of a useful substance from a cell or gland | Saliva, enzymes, hormones |

Faeces are mostly undigested food (fibre), dead cells and bacteria. They never entered the body's cells and were not made by metabolism, so removing them is **egestion**, not excretion.

## Carbon dioxide

**Carbon dioxide is a waste product of respiration. It is excreted through the lungs.**

Every living cell respires, and aerobic respiration produces carbon dioxide (subtopic 10.2). The carbon dioxide:

1. diffuses out of the respiring cells into the tissue fluid and the blood;
2. is carried in the blood plasma to the lungs;
3. diffuses from the blood into the air in the alveoli;
4. is breathed out.

### Why carbon dioxide must be removed

Carbon dioxide dissolves in water to form a weak acid. If it built up in the blood, the pH would fall, and enzyme-controlled reactions in the body would be disturbed. A rising carbon dioxide level is also detected by the brain, which makes breathing deeper and faster (see subtopic 9.1). Carbon dioxide is therefore a waste product that the body must control.

*Fact check:* inspired air has about 0.04% carbon dioxide and expired air about 4%, which is about 100 times as much.

## Urea

**Urea is a toxic waste product produced in the liver from the breakdown of excess amino acids.**

### Why excess amino acids?

Proteins are digested to amino acids, which are absorbed and used to make new proteins (assimilation, subtopic 8.3). The body cannot store amino acids. If a person eats more protein than they need, the extra amino acids cannot be kept.

### How urea is made

1. The excess amino acids travel in the blood to the **liver**.
2. In the liver, the amino acids are broken down. The part of each molecule that contains nitrogen is removed. This is **deamination**.
3. The nitrogen-containing part is converted into **urea**.
4. The rest of the amino acid can be used in respiration to release energy, or converted into carbohydrate or fat for storage.
5. Urea passes into the blood plasma, which carries it to the **kidneys**, where it is removed in the urine (subtopic 13.2).

Urea is **toxic**, so it must be removed from the blood. The body cannot let it build up.

*Why is the nitrogen part removed?* Carbohydrates and fats contain no nitrogen. The nitrogen-containing group cannot be stored or used in respiration, so it has to be converted into a substance that can be excreted safely. Urea is much less harmful than the alternative product of the first breakdown step, so it can be carried in the blood to the kidneys.

### Worked example: tracing a data question

*A scientist measured urea in blood vessels near the liver, after a meal rich in protein (illustrative units, mmol per dm cubed).*

| Blood vessel | Urea concentration |
|---|---|
| Hepatic portal vein (to the liver) | 2.0 |
| Hepatic vein (from the liver) | 5.0 |

1. The concentration is higher in the hepatic vein. The difference is 5.0 - 2.0 = **3.0 mmol per dm cubed**.
2. The ratio is 5.0 divided by 2.0 = **2.5 times** higher.
3. **Explanation**: the liver breaks down excess amino acids and makes urea, which is released into the blood leaving the liver.
4. **Prediction**: after a meal very low in protein, the difference would be smaller, because fewer excess amino acids would reach the liver.

## Other excretory products

The syllabus names carbon dioxide and urea. Other substances are excreted too, for example excess water and salts in urine (subtopic 13.2) and water in sweat. The key examination idea is that excretion removes **metabolic waste**, not undigested food.

## Excretion in other organisms

All living organisms excrete, not just humans. Plants excrete carbon dioxide from respiration, and in the light they use this carbon dioxide in photosynthesis, so the gas exchange runs in the opposite direction. Single-celled organisms excrete across their cell surface by diffusion. Larger animals need specialised organs: the lungs for carbon dioxide and the kidneys for urea.

Excretion draws together several parts of the course, and questions often cross the boundaries.

| Topic | Link to excretion |
|---|---|
| Respiration (10) | Aerobic respiration produces carbon dioxide, the waste product excreted by the lungs |
| Human gas exchange (9) | The alveoli are where carbon dioxide leaves the blood and is breathed out |
| Biological molecules (4) | Proteins are made of amino acids, which contain nitrogen; carbohydrates and fats do not |
| Human nutrition (8) | Excess protein in the diet leads to excess amino acids and so more urea |
| Transport in humans (11) | The plasma carries both carbon dioxide and urea |

### How the body controls carbon dioxide

The level of carbon dioxide in the blood is monitored by the brain. When a person exercises, the muscles respire faster and release more carbon dioxide into the blood. The rise is detected, and the breathing rate and depth increase, so that more carbon dioxide is removed at the lungs. When the blood level falls back, breathing returns to normal. This is an example of the body controlling the level of a waste product, and it shows that excretion is closely tied to respiration and breathing.

### An exam-style long answer

*Describe how the body deals with the waste products carbon dioxide and urea.* A complete answer has four parts.

1. Carbon dioxide is produced in all respiring cells and diffuses into the blood.
2. It is carried in the plasma to the lungs, where it diffuses into the alveoli and is breathed out.
3. Urea is produced in the liver from the breakdown of excess amino acids, which cannot be stored.
4. It passes into the plasma, which carries it to the kidneys, to be removed in the urine.

Each part names a **place**, a **process** and a **route**. Writing these three things for each waste product earns the marks.

## Common mistakes

- Defining excretion as "getting rid of waste" with no mention of **metabolic** waste or **toxic** materials.
- Counting faeces as excreted. They are egested.
- Saying that urea is made in the kidneys. It is made in the **liver** and removed by the kidneys.
- Saying that urea comes from the breakdown of carbohydrates or fats.
- Saying that carbon dioxide is excreted through the kidneys or skin. It is excreted through the **lungs**.
- Saying that oxygen is a waste product of respiration.
- Forgetting that excess amino acids cannot be stored.
- Saying that sweat or tears are the main route for urea.

## Command words

- **Describe**: give the definition of excretion.
- **State**: name the waste product, where it is made and how it is excreted.
- **Explain**: give the reason, for example why the liver is needed to make urea.
- **Distinguish**: give the difference between excretion and egestion.
