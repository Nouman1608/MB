## Section A

**1.** Which layer of a leaf contains the most chloroplasts?

A: lower epidermis

B: palisade mesophyll

C: spongy mesophyll

D: upper epidermis

**[1]**

**2.** Which structure controls the size of a stoma?

A: air space

B: guard cell

C: palisade cell

D: xylem vessel

**[1]**

**3.** State **two** reasons why a leaf is thin. **[2]**

**4.** State the function of the waxy cuticle. **[1]**

**5.** Describe the function of the xylem and the phloem in a leaf. **[3]**

## Section B

**6.** A diagram of a section through a dicotyledonous leaf shows five labelled parts: P, a waterproof layer on the outer surface; Q, tall cells just under the upper surface; R, loosely arranged cells with large gaps between them; S, a pair of cells forming a pore in the lower surface; T, a tube carrying water into the leaf.

**(a)** Name the parts P, Q, R, S and T. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain how the structure of Q is adapted for photosynthesis. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain the function of the gaps in R. **[2]**

**7.** The upper epidermis is transparent and has no chloroplasts, but the guard cells in the lower epidermis do contain chloroplasts.

**(a)** Explain why it is an advantage for the upper epidermis to be transparent. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how guard cells open a stoma. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why a stoma is closed at night. **[2]**

**8.** A photomicrograph of a leaf section is at a magnification of x250. The distance across the leaf, from the upper surface to the lower surface, measures 40 mm on the photomicrograph.

**(a)** Calculate the actual thickness of the leaf in millimetres. **[2]**

**(b)** Convert your answer to micrometres. **[1]**

**9.** Compare the palisade mesophyll with the spongy mesophyll. **[4]**

**10.** Explain, by following the route of carbon dioxide, how a molecule of carbon dioxide reaches a palisade cell from the air outside the leaf. **[4]**

**11.** Most stomata are on the lower surface of the leaf. Suggest an advantage of this. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why the leaf of a plant growing in a dry environment might have a thicker cuticle than a leaf of a plant growing in a pond. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Light can reach all the cells inside the leaf [1]. Gases have a short distance to diffuse to reach the cells [1]. **[2]**

**4.** It reduces water loss from the surface of the leaf [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Xylem carries water and mineral ions into the leaf [1]. Phloem carries sucrose and amino acids away from the leaf to other parts of the plant [1]. Xylem also gives support [1]. **[3]**

**6.**

**(a)** P: cuticle [1]. Q: palisade mesophyll [1]. R: spongy mesophyll [1]. S: guard cells [1]. T: xylem [1].

**(b)** It contains many chloroplasts to absorb light [1]. It is tall and narrow, so light passes through few cell walls and many cells can fit close to the surface [1]. It is placed just under the upper epidermis, where light is brightest [1].

**(c)** The air spaces let carbon dioxide diffuse to the cells that photosynthesise [1] and oxygen diffuse out [1]. **[10]**

**7.**

**(a)** Light passes through the upper epidermis [1] to reach the chloroplasts in the palisade cells for photosynthesis [1].

**(b)** Water enters the guard cells and they become turgid [1], and their shape changes so that the pore opens [1].

**(c)** There is no light, so no photosynthesis and no need for carbon dioxide [1]. Closing the stoma reduces water loss [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** Actual size = image size divided by magnification [1]. 40 divided by 250 = 0.16 mm [1].

**(b)** 0.16 x 1000 = 160 micrometres [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Any four of: palisade cells are tall and narrow but spongy cells are rounded [1]; palisade cells are tightly packed but spongy cells are loosely arranged with air spaces [1]; palisade cells have more chloroplasts [1]; palisade cells are nearer the upper surface and spongy cells nearer the lower surface [1]; both contain chloroplasts and photosynthesise [1]; both are covered with a film of water in which gases dissolve [1]. **[4]**

**10.** It diffuses in through an open stoma [1], into the air spaces in the spongy mesophyll [1], then dissolves in the film of water on the surface of the cells [1], and diffuses into the palisade cell down a concentration gradient [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The lower surface is cooler and not in direct sunlight [1], so less water evaporates through the stomata [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The cuticle is waterproof and reduces water loss from the leaf surface [1]. In a dry environment, water is scarce and evaporation is high, so a thick cuticle reduces water loss more [1]. In a pond, water is plentiful, so there is less need to reduce water loss [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "palisade cells absorb light" without naming the chloroplasts.
- Naming the gaps as holes in cells rather than **air spaces between cells**.
- Forgetting the unit when converting from millimetres to micrometres.
- Saying the whole epidermis photosynthesises.
- Mixing up xylem (water and ions) and phloem (sucrose).
