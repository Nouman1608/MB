Condensed notes on section **3.7.3 Analysing the existing internal position of a business to assess strengths and weaknesses: overall performance** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.7 is A-level only. The worked Thornby Logistics example is in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-internal-position-overall-performance/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-internal-position-overall-performance-practice/).

## Specification content

- How to analyse data other than financial statements to assess strengths and weaknesses: operations, human resource and marketing data, over time or in comparison with other businesses.
- The importance of core competences.
- Assessing short- and long-term performance.
- The value of different measures of assessing business performance, including Elkington's Triple Bottom Line (Profit, People, Planet).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Productivity | Output per unit of input, such as output per employee |
| Capacity utilisation | Actual output ÷ maximum output × 100 |
| Labour turnover | Leavers in a year ÷ average staff × 100 |
| Absenteeism | Days lost through absence ÷ days available × 100 |
| Market share | Firm's sales ÷ total market sales × 100 |
| Customer retention | Customers kept ÷ customers at the start × 100 |
| Core competence | A hard-to-copy bundle of skills and processes that gives customers value and can be used across products or markets |
| Triple Bottom Line | Judging a business on profit, people and planet |

## Data to use

| Area | Measures | Early warning of |
|---|---|---|
| Operations | Productivity, capacity utilisation, unit cost, defect rate, on-time delivery | Rising costs, quality problems, late deliveries |
| Human resources | Labour turnover, absenteeism, training hours, staff survey | Low morale, skills gaps, high recruitment cost |
| Marketing | Market share, sales growth, satisfaction, complaints, retention, brand awareness | Loss of customers, weakening brand |

**Rule:** analyse over time (direction) and against rivals or industry norms (relative position). Check that measures are defined in the same way.

## Reading capacity utilisation

- **Low:** unused resources; high fixed cost per unit.
- **High:** fixed costs spread over many units; but no room for extra demand, maintenance or error.

## Reading labour turnover

- **High:** cost of recruitment and training; inexperienced staff; possible low morale. Normal in some seasonal industries.
- **Low:** stable experienced team; but may mean lack of fresh ideas.

## Reading market share

- Rising share: gaining ground.
- Sales may grow while share falls, if the market grows faster.
- Share bought through price cuts may harm profit.

## Core competences

**Test:** valued by customers; hard for rivals to copy; usable in several products or markets.

**Value:** a base for choosing markets and products; supports competitive advantage; guides what to keep in house and what to outsource; warns against moving into areas with no advantage.

**Limits:** can become outdated; over-reliance on one competence; identifying it is a judgement.

## Short and long term

| | Short term | Long term |
|---|---|---|
| Measures | Profit, cash flow, quarterly sales | Market share, brand, innovation, staff capability |
| Danger of focusing only on it | Cuts to training or research to lift profit | Ignoring cash needed to survive now |

Ask: was the short-term result achieved in a way that protects the future?

## Measures of performance

| Measure | Value | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Profit | Clear, comparable | Ignores timing, risk and social impact |
| ROCE | Relates profit to funds used | Accounting values |
| Market share | Competitive standing | Can be bought with losses |
| Customer satisfaction | Predicts repeat business | Survey bias |
| Employee data | Early warning | Hard to link to profit |

## Triple Bottom Line (Elkington)

- **Profit**: economic return.
- **People**: employees, customers, communities, fair treatment.
- **Planet**: energy, emissions, waste, resources.
- **Value:** wider view of success; protects reputation; meets stakeholder pressure.
- **Limits:** people and planet hard to measure and compare; the three can conflict; selective reporting; survival still needs profit.

## Model analysis paragraph

"Labour turnover rose from 12% to 20% while on-time delivery fell from 96% to 91%. The two are probably linked: new staff are less experienced, so deliveries are late. Late deliveries harm customer satisfaction and may explain the fall in market share. The profit figures may not yet show this, so non-financial data give an early warning. The firm should find out why staff are leaving before it affects profit."

## Command words

- **Analyse data:** quote figures, calculate the change, explain cause and effect.
- **Assess performance:** use financial and non-financial measures, short and long term.

## Exam traps

- Interpret the figures; do not just describe them.
- Sales growth with falling market share is not unqualified success.
- A core competence is a capability, not a product.
- The Triple Bottom Line adds to profit; it does not replace it.
- Use both financial and non-financial evidence when asked to assess performance.

## Self-test

1. Write the formula for labour turnover.
2. A firm lost 24 of its average 300 staff in a year. Calculate labour turnover.
3. A factory made 18,000 units with a maximum capacity of 24,000. Calculate capacity utilisation.
4. A firm has sales of £4.5m in a market worth £60m. Calculate market share.
5. Give two possible causes of high labour turnover.
6. Why might sales grow while market share falls?
7. State two features of a core competence.
8. Give two reasons why identifying core competences helps strategic decisions.
9. Why can focusing only on short-term profit damage performance?
10. State the three parts of Elkington's Triple Bottom Line.
11. Give one value and one limit of the Triple Bottom Line.
12. Why is it useful to compare performance data with those of a competitor?

### Answers

1. Leavers in a year ÷ average number of employees × 100.
2. 24 ÷ 300 × 100 = **8%**.
3. 18,000 ÷ 24,000 × 100 = **75%**.
4. 4.5 ÷ 60 × 100 = **7.5%**.
5. Any two: low pay; poor management; lack of promotion; poor working conditions; strong job market elsewhere.
6. The market is growing faster than the firm's sales.
7. Any two: valued by customers; difficult to imitate; usable across several products or markets.
8. Any two: it shows which markets to enter; it supports competitive advantage; it shows what to outsource; it warns against diversifying into unrelated areas.
9. Cuts to training, research or maintenance raise profit now but reduce future competitiveness.
10. Profit, people, planet.
11. Value: widens the view of success and protects reputation. Limit: people and planet are hard to measure and compare.
12. It shows whether performance is good or poor relative to the industry, and which areas to improve.
