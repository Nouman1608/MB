## Questions

**1.** Outline two properties of carbon atoms that allow the formation of a very large number of different compounds. **[2]**

**2.** An electron micrograph shows a cell surface membrane. The membrane measures 28 mm across on the image and the magnification is ×4 000 000.

**(a)** Calculate the actual thickness of the membrane in nanometres. **[2]**
**(b)** Express your answer to (a) in micrometres. **[1]**

**3.** Glucose and fructose (both C6H12O6, relative molecular mass 180.16) join to form sucrose, and the water released has a relative molecular mass of 18.02.

**(a)** Name the type of reaction and the bond formed. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of sucrose. **[2]**
**(c)** Outline what happens to the water molecule when sucrose is digested. **[2]**

**4.** Molecule X has a ring made of four carbon atoms and one oxygen atom, with a fifth carbon atom attached to the ring as a side group. Molecule Y has five carbons and an oxygen in its ring, plus a sixth carbon as a side group.

**(a)** Classify X and Y as pentose or hexose. **[2]**
**(b)** State one example of each. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how four properties of glucose make it suitable as the main carbohydrate fuel carried in the blood. **[4]**

**6.** Explain how the structure of glycogen makes it suitable as an energy store in animals. **[4]**

**7.** Cellulose and starch are both polymers of glucose. Explain how the structure of cellulose suits its function in the plant cell wall. **[4]**

**8.** Red blood cells carry ABO antigens on their surface.

**(a)** State the type of molecule that carries these antigens. **[1]**
**(b)** A patient with blood group A is mistakenly given group B blood. Explain why this is dangerous. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why blood group O red cells can be given to patients of other groups in an emergency. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows melting points of four fatty acids, each with 18 carbon atoms. The values are supplied for this question.

| Fatty acid | C=C bonds | Melting point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| Stearic | 0 | 69 |
| Oleic | 1 | 13 |
| Linoleic | 2 | −5 |
| Linolenic | 3 | −11 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between the number of C=C bonds and melting point. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the relationship. **[3]**
**(c)** State whether oleic acid is solid or liquid at a human body temperature of 37 °C. **[1]**
**(d)** Suggest why many plant seeds store energy as oils rich in unsaturated fatty acids, while mammals store fats richer in saturated fatty acids. **[2]**

**10.** A hibernating mammal stores 400 g of triglyceride. Its energy use is 150 kJ per day. Lipid yields 37 kJ per gram and carbohydrate 17 kJ per gram (data supplied).

**(a)** Calculate the energy stored in the fat. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many days the fat store lasts, to the nearest day. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of carbohydrate needed to store the same energy, to the nearest gram. **[2]**
**(d)** Give two properties of triglycerides, other than energy density, that suit long-term storage. **[2]**
**(e)** Explain why a thick layer of fat is more useful for an Arctic seal than for a tropical frog. **[2]**

**11.** Phospholipids and triglycerides are both made from glycerol and fatty acids.

**(a)** State the number of water molecules released when one triglyceride is formed from glycerol and fatty acids, and when one phospholipid is formed from glycerol, two fatty acids and a phosphate group. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, using the term amphipathic, why phospholipids form a bilayer in water. **[3]**
**(c)** The table shows how easily three substances cross an artificial phospholipid bilayer (illustrative values, arbitrary units).

| Substance | Relative rate of crossing |
|---|---|
| Testosterone | 95 |
| Glucose | 0.2 |
| Sodium ions | 0.001 |

Explain the differences in rate. **[3]**
**(d)** Testosterone is a steroid. State how a steroid can be recognised from its molecular diagram. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A carbon atom forms up to four covalent bonds [1]. It can bond to other carbon atoms in chains and rings, and to other non-metals such as hydrogen, oxygen and nitrogen, with single or double bonds [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare "carbon can form four bonds" gives only one idea; the second point needs the bonding to other carbon atoms or the variety of skeletons.

**2. (a)** Actual size = image size ÷ magnification [1]. 28 mm ÷ 4 000 000 = 7 × 10⁻⁶ mm = **7 nm** [1].
**(b)** 7 nm ÷ 1000 = **0.007 µm** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the rearranged formula and convert mm to nm explicitly; a correct final value is easier to credit when the working is visible.

**3. (a)** Condensation [1]; glycosidic bond [1].
**(b)** 180.16 + 180.16 − 18.02 [1] = **342.30** [1].
**(c)** A water molecule is split [1] into -H and -OH, which are added to the two monosaccharides (hydrolysis) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers on hydrolysis gain credit when they say water is split and the -H and -OH are incorporated; "water is used" is incomplete.

**4. (a)** X is a pentose [1] (five carbons in total); Y is a hexose [1] (six carbons in total).
**(b)** X: ribose or deoxyribose [1]. Y: glucose, fructose or galactose [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Count every carbon, including the one outside the ring; counting only ring carbons reverses both answers.

**5.** Soluble, because the many -OH groups form hydrogen bonds with water, so it dissolves in plasma [1]. Transportable, since it is dissolved and small, so it moves in the blood and into cells [1]. Chemically stable, so it is not altered while being carried [1]. Oxidation in respiration releases energy for ATP production [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each property needs its link to the function; a list of four property names without the consequence tends to earn little.

**6.** Glycogen is a polymer of alpha-glucose that is coiled and highly branched, which makes it compact [1]. Its large molecular size makes it relatively insoluble, so it does not affect the water balance of the cell by osmosis [1]. Glucose units are added by condensation and removed by hydrolysis [1]. The many branch ends allow many units to be mobilised at once for rapid energy release [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention of branching earns most credit when tied to either compactness or the number of free ends.

**7.** Cellulose is made of beta-glucose, with alternate units inverted [1], giving straight, unbranched chains [1]. Chains lie side by side in bundles cross-linked by hydrogen bonds [1]. Many hydrogen bonds together give high tensile strength to the wall, which resists the cell bursting [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Hydrogen bonds between chains" is the key link; stating that the bonds are individually weak but numerous shows understanding rather than recall.

**8. (a)** Glycoprotein [1].
**(b)** The patient has antibodies against B antigens [1]. These bind the B antigens on the donated cells [1] and cause clumping of the red blood cells, which can block blood vessels [1].
**(c)** Group O cells lack A and B antigens [1], so the recipient's antibodies have nothing to bind to [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State who has the antibodies and what they bind; answers that blame "the wrong blood type" without naming antigens and antibodies often do not progress.

**9. (a)** As the number of C=C bonds increases, the melting point decreases [1]. Quote data, for example stearic acid with none melts at 69 °C but linolenic acid with three melts at −11 °C [1].
**(b)** C=C bonds put bends in the hydrocarbon chain [1], so molecules cannot pack closely [1] so the molecules separate with a smaller energy input [1].
**(c)** Liquid, because its melting point (13 °C) is below 37 °C [1].
**(d)** Plant oils stay liquid at ambient temperatures so can be mobilised in a seed [1]. Mammals' body temperature is high enough for fats richer in saturated acids to remain firm in adipose tissue [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "describe" question, a trend plus data quoted from the table usually gains more than a trend alone.

**10. (a)** 400 × 37 = **14 800 kJ** [1].
**(b)** 14 800 ÷ 150 [1] = 98.7, so **99 days** [1].
**(c)** 14 800 ÷ 17 [1] = **871 g** [1].
**(d)** Insoluble, so no osmotic effect [1]; stable over long periods [1]. (Compact storage in adipose tissue is also acceptable.)
**(e)** Fat conducts heat poorly, so it reduces heat loss [1]. The seal is an endotherm in cold water and needs to retain body heat, while a frog in a warm habitat is an ectotherm that does not maintain a high body temperature, so insulation gives little benefit [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Include units with every calculated value and relate the insulation point to both body temperature and habitat, as the question asks you to compare.

**11. (a)** Triglyceride: 3 waters [1]. Phospholipid: 3 waters (two from the fatty acids, one from the phosphate) [1].
**(b)** Phospholipids are amphipathic, with a hydrophilic phosphate head and hydrophobic fatty acid tails [1]. In water the heads point towards the surrounding water while the tails turn inwards, away from it [1], so two layers form with tails inside, a bilayer [1].
**(c)** Testosterone is non-polar, so it dissolves in the hydrophobic core and crosses quickly [1]. Glucose is polar and crosses slowly [1]. Sodium ions are charged and are almost blocked by the core [1].
**(d)** Four fused carbon rings [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Use comparative language tied to the table values, and link each substance to polarity rather than only to size.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying condensation "removes" or hydrolysis "adds" water without naming the -H and -OH groups.
- Counting only the ring carbons when classifying a sugar.
- Missing the unit, or converting between nm, µm and mm with the wrong factor.
- Describing starch as insoluble with no link to osmosis or compact storage.
- Giving cellulose strength as "tough" without alternating beta-glucose and hydrogen bonds.
- Confusing which antigens and which antibodies a person has.
- Describing unsaturated fatty acids without mentioning C=C bonds and chain bends.
- Calling the phosphate head hydrophobic, or the tails attracted to water.
- Giving insulation as "keeps warm" with no reference to endotherms or habitat.
