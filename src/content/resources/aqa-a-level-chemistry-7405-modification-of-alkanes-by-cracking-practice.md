These questions cover section **3.3.2.2 Modification of alkanes by cracking** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Modification of alkanes by cracking study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-modification-of-alkanes-by-cracking/) and the [Modification of alkanes by cracking revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-modification-of-alkanes-by-cracking-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values C = 12.0 and H = 1.0, and a molar gas volume of 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by cracking and state the type of bond that breaks. **[2]**

**2.** State the conditions used in thermal cracking and name the type of product formed in high percentage. **[3]**

**3.** State the conditions and the catalyst used in catalytic cracking, and name two types of product that are mainly formed. **[4]**

**4.** Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, is cracked to form C₄H₁₀ and one other product only. Deduce the molecular formula of the other product and state its homologous series. **[2]**

**5.** Hexadecane, C₁₆H₃₄, is cracked to form octane and ethene only. Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**6.** Pentadecane, C₁₅H₃₂, is cracked to form octane and one alkene only. Write the equation and give the name of the alkene. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why each cracking reaction must produce at least one alkene. **[2]**

**8.** Explain the economic reasons for cracking alkanes. **[4]**

**9.** Compare thermal cracking and catalytic cracking in terms of energy costs. **[2]**

**10.** 22.6 g of hexadecane (Mᵣ = 226.0) is cracked completely: C₁₆H₃₄ → C₈H₁₈ + 2C₄H₈. Calculate the mass and the volume of butene produced. **[4]**

**11.** Tetradecane is cracked: C₁₄H₃₀ → C₁₀H₂₂ + 2C₂H₄. Calculate the atom economy for the production of ethene. **[2]**

**12.** Explain why fractional distillation of crude oil is not enough to meet the demand for petrol, and how cracking helps. **[3]**

**13.** A student says that "cracking is a physical process like fractional distillation". Identify the error and describe the difference between the two processes. **[3]**

**14.** Dodecane, C₁₂H₂₆, is cracked and the products include propene and one other alkene. Write an equation for cracking dodecane to give 2 molecules of propene and one molecule of an alkane, and name or give the formula of the alkane. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Cracking is the breaking of long-chain alkanes into shorter molecules [1]; C–C (carbon–carbon) bonds are broken [1]. **[2]**

**2.** High temperature [1] and high pressure [1]; a high percentage of alkenes [1]. **[3]**

**3.** A slight pressure [1] and a high temperature [1], with a zeolite catalyst [1]; motor fuels (branched and cyclic alkanes) and aromatic hydrocarbons [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Carbon: 12 – 4 = 8, hydrogen: 26 – 10 = 16, so the other product is C₈H₁₆ [1]; it is an alkene (CₙH₂ₙ) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** C₁₆H₃₄ → C₈H₁₈ + 4C₂H₄ [2: 1 for the correct formulae, 1 for balancing]. (Check: carbon 16 = 8 + 8, hydrogen 34 = 18 + 16.) **[2]**

**6.** C₁₅H₃₂ → C₈H₁₈ + C₇H₁₄ [2]; the alkene is heptene (hept-1-ene or another heptene) [1]. **[3]**

**7.** The molecule being cracked is saturated [1]; when it splits, the hydrogen atoms cannot all be accommodated in saturated products, so at least one product must have a C=C double bond [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Crude oil contains more long-chain hydrocarbons than the market needs, while demand for short-chain fuels such as petrol is high [1]; cracking turns the less valuable surplus into more valuable products [1]; the alkenes formed are the starting materials (feedstock) for making polymers and other chemicals [1]; catalytic cracking forms branched and aromatic hydrocarbons that make better fuels [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Catalytic cracking uses a lower temperature and a much lower pressure than thermal cracking [1]; so it needs less energy and has lower energy costs (thermal cracking is more expensive to run) [1]. **[2]**

**10.** n(C₁₆H₃₄) = 22.6 ÷ 226.0 = 0.100 mol [1]; n(C₄H₈) = 2 × 0.100 = 0.200 mol [1]; mass = 0.200 × 56.0 = **11.2 g** [1]; volume = 0.200 × 24.0 = **4.80 dm³** [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Mᵣ of C₁₀H₂₂ = 142.0 and Mᵣ of 2C₂H₄ = 56.0, so the total = 198.0 [1]; atom economy = 56.0 ÷ 198.0 × 100 = **28.3%** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Fractional distillation only separates the hydrocarbons already in crude oil, and crude oil contains too little of the short-chain petrol fraction to meet the demand [1]; cracking breaks the surplus long-chain molecules into shorter ones [1]; the shorter molecules are suitable for use in petrol [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Cracking is a chemical process in which C–C bonds break, forming new smaller molecules [1]; fractional distillation is a physical process that separates molecules by boiling point and breaks no covalent bonds [1]; fractional distillation uses differences in London forces, but cracking changes the molecules [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Check every equation by counting carbon and hydrogen on each side, and make sure one product is an alkene.

**14.** C₁₂H₂₆ → 2C₃H₆ + C₆H₁₄ [1]; the alkane is hexane, C₆H₁₄ (carbon: 12 = 6 + 6; hydrogen: 26 = 12 + 14) [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing cracking equations that do not balance.
- Mixing up the pressures of thermal and catalytic cracking.
- Not naming the zeolite catalyst.
- Missing the supply-and-demand reason.
- Treating cracking as a physical separation.
