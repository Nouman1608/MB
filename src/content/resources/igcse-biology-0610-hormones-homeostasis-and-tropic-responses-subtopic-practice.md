## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by a hormone. **[3]**

**2.** An outline diagram of the human body shows four glands, labelled A to D.

- A: on top of each kidney.
- B: just below the stomach.
- C: in a female, one on each side of the uterus.
- D: in the scrotum of a male.

**(a)** Name each gland, A to D, and the hormone it secretes. **[4]**
**(b)** *(Extended)* Gland B secretes a second hormone. Name it. **[1]**

**3.** A cyclist swerves to avoid a car that pulls out in front of her. Her adrenal glands secrete adrenaline.

**(a)** State the name given to situations like this, in which adrenaline is secreted. **[1]**
**(b)** State three effects of adrenaline on her body. **[3]**
**(c)** *(Extended)* Describe the role of adrenaline in the control of metabolic activity, and explain how this helps her muscles. **[3]**

**4.** The cyclist in Question 3 squeezed her brakes within a fraction of a second of seeing the car. Ten minutes later her heart was still beating fast. Use this example to compare nervous and hormonal control. **[2]**

**5.**

**(a)** Describe what is meant by homeostasis. **[1]**
**(b)** State the effect of insulin on the blood glucose concentration. **[1]**
**(c)** *(Extended)* The body has a set point for its core temperature. Explain the concept of homeostatic control by negative feedback, with reference to this set point. **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Two adults, A and B, each drank the same glucose solution after fasting overnight.

| Time after drink / min | 0 | 30 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A, glucose / mmol per dm³ | 4.4 | 7.7 | 6.6 | 5.3 | 4.5 |
| B, glucose / mmol per dm³ | 6.4 | 10.8 | 13.2 | 14.4 | 14.2 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in person A's blood glucose concentration between 0 and 30 minutes. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe how person A's pancreas and liver return the blood glucose concentration towards normal between 30 and 120 minutes. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in person B's blood glucose concentration between 0 and 90 minutes. **[2]**
**(d)** Person B has Type 1 diabetes and has not taken any treatment that morning. Explain why person B's blood glucose concentration stays high. **[1]**
**(e)** Outline the treatment of Type 1 diabetes. **[3]**

**7.** *(Extended)* Two hours into a marathon, a runner's blood glucose concentration falls below its set point.

**(a)** Explain how the pancreas and liver bring the blood glucose concentration back up. **[4]**
**(b)** Name one other hormone that increases the blood glucose concentration. **[1]**

**8.** *(Extended)* Identify each structure seen in a diagram of a section through human skin.

**(a)** A coiled tube deep in the skin, with a duct that opens onto the skin surface. **[1]**
**(b)** A small muscle attached to the base of a hair. **[1]**
**(c)** A layer of cells filled with lipid, below the deeper layer of the skin. **[1]**
**(d)** Nerve endings that detect a rise or fall in temperature. **[1]**
**(e)** Cells that carry impulses from these nerve endings to the central nervous system. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended)* A cyclist rides hard for 1.5 hours on a hot day. Her body mass falls by 1.35 kg, almost all of it as sweat.

**(a)** Calculate her mean rate of mass loss in grams per minute. Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how sweating, and the arterioles supplying her skin surface capillaries, help to stop her body temperature rising. **[4]**
**(c)** Later, in a cold wind, her core temperature starts to fall. Describe three ways her body responds. **[3]**

**10.** A student placed five bean seedlings in a box lit through a slit on one side. After three days every shoot had bent towards the slit, by these angles from vertical: 34°, 29°, 38°, 31° and 28°.

**(a)** Name this response. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean angle of bending. **[1]**
**(c)** Describe a suitable control for this investigation. **[1]**
**(d)** State two variables the student should keep the same. **[2]**
**(e)** In a second investigation she laid germinating bean seeds on their sides on damp paper in the dark. Describe the direction in which the root and the shoot will grow. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A young shoot is lit from one side only.

**(a)** Explain how auxin causes the shoot to bend towards the light. **[4]**
**(b)** In an investigation, 63% of the auxin collected below a shoot tip lit from one side came from the shaded side and 37% from the lit side. Calculate the ratio of auxin on the shaded side to auxin on the lit side. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** The shoot tip of a second seedling is cut off before it is lit from one side. Predict what happens to this shoot, and explain your prediction. **[2]**
**(d)** Phototropism and gravitropism of a shoot are described as examples of the chemical control of plant growth. State why. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** A chemical substance produced by a gland [1], carried by the blood [1], which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs [1].

*Examiner insight:* The three marks match the three parts of the syllabus description; "a chemical messenger" alone earns at most one.

**2. (a)** A: adrenal gland, adrenaline [1]. B: pancreas, insulin [1]. C: ovary, oestrogen [1]. D: testis, testosterone [1].

**(b)** **Glucagon** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each mark in (a) needs both gland and hormone correct; "glycogen" written for glucagon in (b) scores nothing.

**3. (a)** **Fight or flight** [1].

**(b)** Increased breathing rate [1]; increased heart rate [1]; increased pupil diameter (wider pupils) [1].

**(c)** Adrenaline increases the blood glucose concentration [1] and increases heart rate [1], so more glucose and oxygen are delivered to her muscles for (faster) respiration / energy release [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Effects on the heart" or "the eyes change" is too vague; each mark needs the direction of change (increased) and the specific feature, such as pupil diameter.

**4.** Nervous control is faster: the braking response started in a fraction of a second [1]. Hormonal control lasts longer: her heart rate was still raised ten minutes later [1].

*Examiner insight:* Each point must be comparative ("faster", "longer lasting") and about speed or duration, the only two features the syllabus asks for.

**5. (a)** The maintenance of a constant internal environment [1].

**(b)** Insulin decreases the blood glucose concentration [1].

**(c)** A rise above the set point is detected [1] and responses bring it back down; a fall below it is reversed in the same way [1]. The response always reverses (opposes) the change, which is why it is called negative feedback [1].

*Examiner insight:* Describing only the response to overheating, with no set point and no reversal of the change, usually scores one mark.

**6. (a)** (7.7 − 4.4) ÷ 4.4 × 100 [1] = **75%** [1].

**(b)** The pancreas detects the rise and secretes insulin [1]. Insulin causes the liver to take up glucose [1] and convert it to glycogen, which is stored [1].

**(c)** (14.4 − 6.4) ÷ 6.4 × 100 [1] = **125%** [1].

**(d)** Person B's pancreas secretes little or no insulin, so the liver does not convert glucose to glycogen [1].

**(e)** Injections of insulin [1]; regular monitoring of the blood glucose concentration [1]; controlling the diet, especially carbohydrate intake, and planning exercise [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a) and (c), divide by the starting value; dividing by the final value loses the method mark.

**7. (a)** The pancreas secretes glucagon [1], carried in the blood to the liver [1]. The liver converts glycogen into glucose [1] and releases it into the blood, so the concentration rises towards the set point [1].

**(b)** **Adrenaline** [1].

*Examiner insight:* "Glucagon is converted into glucose" loses the conversion mark; it is glycogen that the liver converts.

**8. (a)** **Sweat gland** [1].

**(b)** **Hair erector muscle** [1].

**(c)** **Fatty tissue** [1].

**(d)** **Receptors** (temperature receptors) [1].

**(e)** **Sensory neurones** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Use the syllabus names; "nerve" for (e) is not specific enough when the question asks which cells carry impulses to the central nervous system.

**9. (a)** 1.35 kg = 1350 g; 1.5 hours = 90 minutes [1]. 1350 ÷ 90 = **15 g per minute** [1].

**(b)** Sweat glands release sweat onto the skin [1]; evaporation of sweat removes heat [1]. Arterioles supplying skin surface capillaries widen (vasodilation) [1], so more blood flows near the surface and more heat is lost [1].

**(c)** Any three of: vasoconstriction of arterioles, so less blood flows near the skin surface; shivering, as muscle contractions release heat from respiration; hair erector muscles contract so hairs trap insulating air; fatty tissue insulates. One mark each [1] [1] [1].

*Examiner insight:* Capillaries that "dilate" or "move nearer the surface" earn nothing; the marks are for arterioles widening or narrowing.

**10. (a)** **Phototropism** [1].

**(b)** (34 + 29 + 38 + 31 + 28) ÷ 5 = **32°** [1].

**(c)** An identical set of seedlings given light from all sides (or evenly from above) [1].

**(d)** Any two of: temperature; volume of water; type and age of seedling; light intensity [1] [1].

**(e)** The root grows downwards, towards gravity [1]; the shoot grows upwards, away from gravity [1].

*Examiner insight:* "A box with no light" changes light intensity as well as direction, so it does not earn the control mark.

**11. (a)** Auxin is made in the shoot tip [1]. It diffuses through the plant from the tip [1]. More auxin collects on the shaded side than the lit side [1]. Auxin stimulates cell elongation, so cells on the shaded side elongate more and the shoot bends towards the light [1].

**(b)** 63 ÷ 37 = **1.7** (shaded : lit = 1.7 : 1) [1].

**(c)** The shoot does not bend (and grows very little) [1], because auxin is made in the shoot tip, so none is produced [1].

**(d)** The growth response is controlled by a chemical, auxin [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), "light destroys auxin" is not credited; the safe wording is that more auxin is found on the shaded side.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a hormone without all three parts: made by a gland, carried by the blood, acting on target organs.
- Comparing nervous and hormonal control on anything other than speed of action and duration of effect.
- Mixing up glucose, glycogen and glucagon, or saying a hormone does the converting instead of the liver.
- Explaining negative feedback with no mention of a set point.
- Writing that capillaries dilate, constrict or move, instead of arterioles widening or narrowing.
- Not converting hours to minutes or kilograms to grams.
- Saying auxin moves towards the light; it collects on the shaded side.
