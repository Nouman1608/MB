Condensed notes for sub-topic **2.1 The double entry system of book-keeping**. Worked ledger accounts are in the [2.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-double-entry-system-of-book-keeping/); exam-style questions are in the [2.1 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-the-double-entry-system-of-book-keeping-practice/).

## What 2.1 asks for

- The double entry system; preparing ledger accounts; posting transactions.
- Calculating balances and making transfers to financial statements.
- Interpreting ledger accounts and their balances.
- Three-column running balance accounts.
- Division of the ledger: sales, purchases and nominal (general) ledgers.
- How ledger accounts can be kept digitally.
- Folio columns are **not** required.

## The rules

**Debit** to increase: assets, expenses, drawings. **Credit** to increase: liabilities, capital, income. Decreases go on the opposite side.

| Transaction | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Owner pays capital into the bank | Bank | Capital |
| Credit purchase of goods for resale | Purchases | Supplier |
| Credit sale | Customer | Sales |
| Goods returned by a customer | Sales returns | Customer |
| Goods returned to a supplier | Supplier | Purchases returns |
| Cash sale | Cash | Sales |
| Expense paid by bank | Expense | Bank |
| Customer pays by bank | Bank | Customer |
| Supplier paid by bank | Supplier | Bank |
| Owner takes cash | Drawings | Cash |
| Owner takes goods | Drawings | Purchases |
| Loan received | Bank | Loan |
| Non-current asset bought by bank | Asset account (for example equipment) | Bank |
| Income received, such as rent or commission | Bank | Rent received / Commission received |

**Details column:** always name the account holding the other half of the entry.

## Balancing in four steps

1. Add both sides.
2. Write the larger total on both sides, level with each other.
3. Enter the difference on the smaller side as **Balance c/d**.
4. Bring it down on the **opposite** side the next day as **Balance b/d**.

If both sides are equal, rule off the totals; there is no balance.

## What a balance means

| Account | Debit balance | Credit balance |
|---|---|---|
| Customer | Customer owes the business | Customer has overpaid; business owes them |
| Supplier | Business has overpaid the supplier | Business owes the supplier |
| Asset | Value of the asset held | Unusual: check for an error |
| Expense | Cost so far this period | Unusual: check for an error |
| Income | Unusual: check for an error | Income earned so far |
| Capital | Unusual (owner's equity negative) | Owner's equity |

## Year-end transfers

- **Income and expense accounts** close to the statement of profit or loss. Income: debit the income account, credit the statement. Expense: debit the statement, credit the expense account.
- **Assets, liabilities and capital** are balanced and carried down to the next year; they appear in the statement of financial position.
- **Drawings** close to capital: debit capital, credit drawings.

## Running balance format

- Columns: Date, Details, Debit, Credit, Balance.
- New balance after every line; mark it **Dr** or **Cr**.
- Entries use the same side as in a two-sided account.
- No balancing off: the last balance is the closing balance.
- A balance can move from Dr to Cr when more is credited than the debit balance, for example an overpayment.

**Example:** a supplier with an opening balance of 800 Cr; purchases of 1,200 give 2,000 Cr; a return of 150 gives 1,850 Cr; a payment of 800 gives 1,050 Cr.

## Division of the ledger

| Ledger | Holds |
|---|---|
| Sales ledger | Credit customers |
| Purchases ledger | Credit suppliers |
| Nominal (general) ledger | Every other account: assets, liabilities, capital, drawings, income, expenses |

The cash book is the ledger account for cash and bank.

**Digital ledgers:** one entry posts both sides; balances update instantly; reports are produced quickly; fewer arithmetic errors. But the same rules apply, choosing the wrong account still causes an error, and the system needs equipment, training and security.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Double entry | Recording each transaction as a debit in one account and a credit of the same amount in another |
| Ledger | The book or file that holds the accounts |
| Posting | Entering a transaction in the ledger accounts |
| Balance c/d | Balance carried down to close the period |
| Balance b/d | Balance brought down to start the next period |
| Running balance account | A three-column account showing the balance after every entry |
| Nominal ledger | The ledger for all accounts other than customers and suppliers |
| Transfer | Closing an account by moving its balance to a financial statement or to capital |

## Exam traps

- Returns go to **returns accounts**, never to the opposite side of sales or purchases.
- Goods taken by the owner are credited to **purchases**, not sales.
- The c/d and b/d entries are on **opposite** sides.
- Transfers of income balances are **debits** in the income account.
- Do not add folio columns or lose time on them.
- In a running balance account, an unlabelled balance can lose the mark.

## Self-test

**1.** Which side records an increase in an expense?

**2.** Give the double entry for goods sold on credit to J Rana for 600.

**3.** Give the double entry for goods returned to a supplier, B Cole, worth 90.

**4.** The owner takes goods costing 75 for private use. Give the double entry.

**5.** A customer's account shows debits of 3,100 and credits of 2,450. State the balance and what it means.

**6.** On which side is the balance brought down if the credit side total is larger?

**7.** How is the balance of the rent payable account dealt with at the year end?

**8.** Which ledger holds the account of a credit supplier?

**9.** A customer's running balance is 400 Dr. She pays 520. What is the new balance?

**10.** State two advantages of keeping the ledger digitally.

**11.** Where is the drawings balance transferred at the year end?

**12.** A sales account has a debit balance. What does this suggest?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Debit.

**2.** Debit J Rana 600; credit Sales 600.

**3.** Debit B Cole 90; credit Purchases returns 90.

**4.** Debit Drawings 75; credit Purchases 75.

**5.** 650 debit balance (3,100 - 2,450): the customer owes the business 650.

**6.** On the credit side (balance c/d on the debit side, b/d on the credit side).

**7.** It is transferred to the statement of profit or loss: debit the statement, credit rent payable.

**8.** The purchases ledger.

**9.** 120 Cr: the business owes the customer 120.

**10.** Any two: both sides posted from one entry; balances always up to date; fewer arithmetic errors; reports such as a trial balance produced quickly; easy searching.

**11.** To the capital account (debit capital, credit drawings).

**12.** An error, since sales normally has a credit balance.
