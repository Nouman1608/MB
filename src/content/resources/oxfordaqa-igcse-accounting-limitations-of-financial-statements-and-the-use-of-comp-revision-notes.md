## The big idea

A statement or ratio is a summary of the past, in money, at a date or over a period. It says what happened. It does not say why, what comes next, or what the numbers leave out.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limitation | A reason a statement or ratio cannot give a full or reliable picture of performance |
| Financial factor | A limit that comes from how the figures are produced or used |
| Non-financial factor | A real influence on the business that does not appear in the statements |
| Historical cost | Assets recorded at what was paid, not at today's value |
| Snapshot | The statement of financial position shows one date only |
| Computerised software | Programs that record transactions, post to ledgers and produce reports |
| Manual methods | Handwritten books and ledgers |

## Financial limitations at a glance

| Limitation | One-line explanation | Quick example |
|---|---|---|
| Past, not future | Statements record what already happened | A strong year before the main customer leaves |
| Snapshot | One date may be unrepresentative | Inventory low at a quiet season end |
| Historical cost | Assets understated after price rises | A building bought long ago shows a low figure |
| Estimates and policies | Depreciation and inventory choices change profit | Two shops, same trade, different profit |
| Needs comparison | A ratio alone means little | A current ratio of 1.8 with no benchmark |
| Different definitions | Other businesses may calculate differently | Different year ends or different margins in the trade |
| No reasons | A ratio shows change, not cause | Margin falls: price cuts or dearer purchases? |
| Averages hide detail | One figure covers many items | One slow customer inside receivable days |
| Profit is not cash | Profit can exist without cash | Profit made, but customers pay late |

## Non-financial factors at a glance

- Management quality and staff skills
- Staff morale and industrial relations
- Reputation and customer service
- Competitors and market conditions
- The wider economy (interest rates, spending, exchange rates)
- Changes in law
- Environmental and ethical conduct
- Location and technology
- Future plans such as a new product or closure

Memory aid: people, market, law, planet, plans.

## Method in steps: appraise with limits

1. Calculate the ratios asked for, using the OxfordAQA formulas.
2. Compare with a previous year, a budget or a similar business.
3. Say what the ratios suggest about profitability or liquidity.
4. State one or two limitations that apply to this case.
5. Name what extra information you would want.
6. Give a conclusion in one sentence.

## Worked reminders

### Snapshot effect

Current assets 60,000 (cash 24,000), current liabilities 40,000, so current ratio 1.50. Paying 20,000 of payables from cash gives 40,000 / 20,000 = 2.00. Trading is unchanged, but the ratio looks better.

### Policy effect

Machine cost 40,000, 50,000 profit before depreciation, revenue 200,000. Straight line at 10% charges 4,000, giving profit 46,000 and a margin of 23.0%. Reducing balance at 20% charges 8,000 in year 1, giving 42,000 and 21.0%.

### Seasonal inventory

Cost of sales 360,000. Closing inventory 30,000 gives 12 times. Opening 90,000 with closing 30,000 averages 60,000, which gives 6 times. The year-end figure alone flatters the business.

### Historical cost effect

Profit is 30,000. Capital employed on historical cost is 150,000, so the return is 20.0%. If the same assets were restated to today's values, capital employed would be 240,000 and the return 12.5%. The business has not changed, only the basis of the figures.

## Sentence frames for written answers

Use these to turn a fact into a point that earns credit.

- Limitation: "[Factor] limits the usefulness of the ratio because it means the figure may not show [what the user wants to know]."
- Link to the case: "In this business, the year-end inventory is high, so the rate of inventory turnover may not be typical of the whole year."
- Extra information: "I would also want to know the previous year's figures and the reason for the change."
- Computers, advantage: "Entering a sale once updates the ledger accounts automatically, which saves time and avoids copying errors."
- Computers, disadvantage: "Hardware and training are costs, which a small business with few transactions may not recover."
- Conclusion: "The ratios suggest profitability has fallen, but because they do not explain why, the owner should investigate before acting."

## Command words

| Command word | What to do here |
|---|---|
| State | Give the point briefly, with no explanation |
| Explain | Give the point and the reason it matters |
| Calculate | Show the formula or working and the answer with units or percentage sign |
| Suggest | Give a sensible reason that fits the case |
| Evaluate or assess | Give points for and against, then a judgement |

## Computers: advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Fast posting and totals | Cost of hardware, software, updates, training |
| Arithmetic is automatic | Hacking, viruses and fraud risk |
| One entry updates many records | Power failure or system crash |
| Trial balance and statements on demand | Wrong data still posted wrongly |
| Easy storage, search and back-up | Staff need training and may resist |
| Several users, controlled by passwords | Back-ups must be made and kept |

Manual methods: low cost, no power needed, suit few transactions. Slow, error-prone, hard to search.

## Must-know distinctions

- Limitation of a statement (historical cost, snapshot) versus limitation of a ratio (needs comparison, no reasons). Many points apply to both.
- Financial factor versus non-financial factor. Staff morale is non-financial.
- Profit versus cash. A profitable business can have no cash.
- Software removes arithmetic slips. It does not remove input errors or errors of principle.
- A limit is not a reason to ignore the figures. Say what they suggest first.

## Quick self-test

1. State two non-financial factors that a income statement does not show.
2. Why does historical cost limit a return on capital employed comparison?
3. A business has current assets of 90,000 and current liabilities of 60,000. Calculate the current ratio.
4. It uses 30,000 of its cash to pay trade payables. Calculate the new current ratio.
5. Give two reasons why comparing your ratios with a rival's might mislead.
6. Cost of sales is 270,000, opening inventory 45,000 and closing inventory 15,000. Calculate the rate of inventory turnover on average inventory.
7. Give one financial reason and one non-financial reason a business could report profit and still be in difficulty.
8. State two advantages of computerised software over manual methods.
9. State two disadvantages of computerised software.
10. A shop pays 1,500 a year for software that saves 200 hours at 11 an hour. What is the yearly net saving?
11. True or false: a computerised system cannot post a wrong amount.

### Answers

1. Any two from staff morale, reputation, competitors, market conditions, law, management quality.
2. Assets are shown at cost less depreciation, so capital employed is below current value and two businesses that bought at different times look different.
3. 90,000 / 60,000 = 1.50.
4. Current assets 60,000 and current liabilities 30,000, so 2.00.
5. Different accounting policies or definitions; different size, products or year end.
6. Average inventory = (45,000 + 15,000) / 2 = 30,000. 270,000 / 30,000 = 9 times.
7. Financial: customers pay late so cash is short. Non-financial: a key customer leaving or a new competitor.
8. Any two from speed, automatic arithmetic, one entry updating several records, quick reports, easy storage.
9. Any two from cost, security risk, system failure, staff training, wrong data still posted.
10. 200 x 11 = 2,200, less 1,500 = 700.
11. False. It posts whatever is entered, so wrong amounts are posted wrongly.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "ratios are inaccurate" instead of saying what they leave out.
- Giving a limitation with no link to the business in the question.
- Calling a non-financial factor, such as reputation, a financial one.
- Quoting a ratio and concluding nothing from it.
- Giving only advantages of computers, or only disadvantages.
- Saying software stops all errors or that manual methods are always cheaper.
- Comparing ratios across businesses without saying the definitions and sizes may differ.
- Using year-end figures without noting that they may be unrepresentative.
