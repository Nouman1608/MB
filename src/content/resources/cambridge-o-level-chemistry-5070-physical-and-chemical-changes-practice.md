## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a chemical change. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each change as physical or chemical: evaporating ethanol; burning ethanol; dissolving copper(II) sulfate in water; heating copper(II) carbonate until it turns black. **[4]**

**3.** Describe **two** differences between a physical change and a chemical change. **[2]**

**4.** Iodine crystals are warmed gently and turn into a purple vapour, which forms crystals again on a cold surface. Identify the type of change and explain your answer. **[2]**

**5.** 5.0 g of sodium chloride dissolves completely in 100.0 g of water. State the mass of the solution and explain your answer. **[2]**

**6.** Which observation, on its own, does **not** prove that a chemical change has taken place?

A: a precipitate forms when two clear solutions are mixed

B: bubbles form when a liquid is heated

C: a gas is produced that turns limewater milky

D: a solid that was magnetic forms a product that is not magnetic **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Iron filings are mixed with powdered sulfur. The mixture is then heated strongly.

**(a)** Describe a test showing that the iron and sulfur are only mixed, not combined, before heating.

**(b)** When heated, the mixture glows, and continues to glow after the heat is removed. A black solid forms. Identify the type of change and give **two** pieces of evidence.

**(c)** Write the word equation for the reaction. **[6]**

**8.** When blue hydrated copper(II) sulfate is heated, it turns white. Adding water to the white powder turns it blue again. A student says this must be a physical change because it can be reversed. Evaluate the student's statement. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why melting solid lead(II) bromide is a physical change, but passing an electric current through molten lead(II) bromide is a chemical change. **[3]**

**10.** A student makes zinc chloride crystals. She adds zinc carbonate to dilute hydrochloric acid until no more fizzing occurs, filters the mixture, evaporates the filtrate and leaves it to crystallise. Identify each of the four steps as physical or chemical. **[4]**

**11.** Marble chips react with dilute hydrochloric acid in an open flask standing on a balance. The reading falls by 0.44 g.

**(a)** Explain why the reading falls, even though mass is conserved in chemical reactions.

**(b)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of carbon dioxide that escaped.

**(c)** Suggest how the experiment could be changed to show that mass is conserved. **[4]**

**12.** Describe the physical and chemical changes that take place when a candle burns. **[3]**

**13.** Explain, in terms of particles and bonds, the difference between boiling water and electrolysing water. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A change in which at least one new substance is formed [1].

**2.** Evaporating ethanol: physical [1]. Burning ethanol: chemical [1]. Dissolving copper(II) sulfate: physical [1]. Heating copper(II) carbonate: chemical [1].

**3.** A chemical change forms a new substance; a physical change does not [1]. Physical changes are usually easily reversed by physical means such as cooling; chemical changes usually are not / chemical changes rearrange atoms into new particles, physical changes keep the same particles [1].

**4.** Physical change [1]. The vapour is still iodine and turns back into iodine crystals; no new substance forms [1].

**5.** 105.0 g [1]. No particles are gained or lost on dissolving; the sodium chloride is still present, spread through the water [1].

**6.** **B** [1].

**7. (a)** Hold a magnet near the mixture [1]; the iron filings are attracted and can be separated from the sulfur [1].

**(b)** Chemical change [1]. Evidence (any two): a black solid forms that was not present before [1]; the glowing continues without external heating, so energy is released by the reaction [1]; the product is not attracted to a magnet.

**(c)** iron + sulfur → iron(II) sulfide [1].

**8.** The student is wrong [1]. Hydrated copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄·5H₂O, and anhydrous copper(II) sulfate, CuSO₄, are different substances with different formulae, so new substances form in each direction [1]. Some chemical changes are reversible, so reversibility does not show a change is physical [1].

**9.** Melting: the ions become free to move, but they are still Pb²⁺ and Br⁻ ions; no new substance forms [1]. Electrolysis: the ions are turned into lead and bromine [1], which are new substances, so the change is chemical [1].

**10.** Adding zinc carbonate to acid: chemical (zinc chloride, water and carbon dioxide form) [1]. Filtering: physical [1]. Evaporating: physical [1]. Crystallising: physical [1].

**11. (a)** Carbon dioxide gas is produced [1] and escapes from the open flask, so its mass is no longer recorded on the balance [1].

**(b)** Mr of CO₂ = 44; 0.44 ÷ 44 = **0.0100 mol** [1].

**(c)** Carry out the reaction in a sealed container, or fit a balloon over the flask, so that no gas escapes [1].

**12.** The solid wax melts to a liquid, which is a physical change [1]. The wax vapour reacts with oxygen (burns), which is a chemical change [1], forming new substances: carbon dioxide and water [1].

**13.** Boiling: the forces between water molecules are overcome so the molecules separate, but the bonds inside each molecule are not broken [1]. Electrolysis: the bonds inside water molecules are broken [1] and new bonds form to make hydrogen and oxygen, which are new substances [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: classifying dissolving copper(II) sulfate as chemical because the solution is blue. The ions are unchanged.
- Question 3: writing two versions of the same difference, such as "new substance formed" and "new product made".
- Question 5: giving 100.0 g because the salt "disappears".
- Question 7(b): giving "it gets hot" as evidence. The heat came from the Bunsen burner; the evidence is that glowing continues after the burner is removed.
- Question 8: agreeing with the student. Reversibility is a clue, not the test.
- Question 9: saying melting is physical because "no bonds are broken". Ionic bonds are overcome on melting; the reason is that no new substance forms.
- Question 11(a): stating that mass is lost in the reaction. The mass is still there, in the escaped gas.
- Question 13: describing both changes as "breaking bonds" without distinguishing forces between molecules from bonds within them.
