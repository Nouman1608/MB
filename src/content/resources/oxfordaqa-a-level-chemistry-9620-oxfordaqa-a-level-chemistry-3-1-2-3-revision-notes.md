These notes condense **3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1, drawn on by Unit 2 and able to reappear in Unit 5. The [ideal gas equation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-3/) has fully worked examples; the [ideal gas equation practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-3-practice/) test them. R is given in exams; these notes use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹.

## The equation

**pV = nRT**, with every variable in SI units.

| Rearranged for | Form |
|---|---|
| amount | n = pV ÷ RT |
| volume | V = nRT ÷ p |
| pressure | p = nRT ÷ V |
| temperature | T = pV ÷ nR |
| relative molecular mass | Mr = mRT ÷ pV |

## Key terms and units

| Term | Meaning | SI unit |
|---|---|---|
| Pressure, p | Force per unit area exerted by the gas | Pa |
| Volume, V | Space occupied by the gas | m³ |
| Amount, n | Number of moles of gas | mol |
| Gas constant, R | Constant linking the variables, 8.31 | J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |
| Absolute temperature, T | Temperature on the kelvin scale | K |
| Volatile liquid | Liquid that vaporises easily, so its vapour can be measured | not applicable |

## Conversion card

| Given | Convert to SI |
|---|---|
| 101 kPa | 101 × 10³ = 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa |
| 2.00 MPa | 2.00 × 10⁶ Pa |
| 250 cm³ | 250 × 10⁻⁶ = 2.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³ |
| 3.50 dm³ | 3.50 × 10⁻³ m³ |
| 27 °C | 27 + 273 = 300 K |
| −23 °C | −23 + 273 = 250 K |

Remember: 1 m³ = 10³ dm³ = 10⁶ cm³.

## Method box

1. List p, V, n and T with their given units.
2. Convert each to SI.
3. Rearrange pV = nRT for the unknown.
4. Substitute and calculate.
5. Convert the answer to the unit asked for (cm³, dm³, kPa or °C) and give three significant figures.

## Reminders with numbers

- **Pressure.** 2.00 g of N₂ (Mr 28.0) in 1.50 dm³ at 27 °C: n = 0.0714 mol; p = (0.0714 × 8.31 × 300) ÷ (1.50 × 10⁻³) = 1.19 × 10⁵ Pa = 119 kPa.
- **Amount.** 500 cm³ at 100 kPa and 300 K: n = (100 000 × 5.00 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 300) = 0.0201 mol.
- **Mr.** 0.346 g of vapour occupies 200 cm³ at 50 °C and 98.0 kPa: n = (98 000 × 2.00 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 323) = 7.30 × 10⁻³ mol; Mr = 0.346 ÷ 7.30 × 10⁻³ = 47.4.

## Useful consequences

- At fixed p and T, the volume of a gas is proportional to n, whichever gas it is.
- Near room temperature and atmospheric pressure, one mole of any gas occupies about 24 dm³. Use this only as a sense check, not in place of pV = nRT.
- Gas density = pMr ÷ RT, so at the same p and T a gas with larger Mr is denser.

## Exam traps

- kPa left unconverted: answers wrong by a factor of 1000.
- cm³ divided by 1000 instead of 10⁶: answers wrong by a factor of 1000.
- °C used instead of K.
- Mass used in place of moles.
- Final answer left in m³ or Pa when cm³ or kPa was asked for.
- Rounding n early when it will be used to find Mr.
- R and the Avogadro constant are given on the paper; do not spend time memorising them, but do learn the units of R.

## Self-test

1. Convert 85.0 kPa to Pa.
2. Convert 450 cm³ to m³.
3. Convert 120 °C to K.
4. Rearrange pV = nRT to give T.
5. Calculate the pressure, in kPa, of 0.500 mol of gas in 10.0 dm³ at 400 K.
6. Calculate the volume, in cm³, of 3.20 g of O₂ (Mr 32.0) at 20 °C and 100 kPa.
7. At what temperature, in K, does 0.250 mol of gas occupy 6.00 dm³ at 110 kPa?
8. Show that Pa m³ is equivalent to J.
9. Write an expression for Mr in terms of m, p, V, R and T.
10. How many molecules are in 1.00 cm³ of gas at 101 kPa and 298 K? (L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹)
11. Why must T be in kelvin rather than °C?
12. Two flasks of equal volume at the same temperature and pressure contain hydrogen and carbon dioxide. Compare the amounts of gas and the masses.

### Answers

1. 85 000 Pa (8.50 × 10⁴ Pa)
2. 4.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³
3. 393 K
4. T = pV ÷ nR
5. p = (0.500 × 8.31 × 400) ÷ (1.00 × 10⁻²) = 166 000 Pa = 166 kPa
6. n = 0.100 mol; V = (0.100 × 8.31 × 293) ÷ 100 000 = 2.43 × 10⁻³ m³ = 2430 cm³
7. T = (110 000 × 6.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (0.250 × 8.31) = 318 K
8. Pa = N m⁻², so Pa × m³ = N m, which is a joule.
9. Mr = mRT ÷ pV
10. n = (101 000 × 1.00 × 10⁻⁶) ÷ (8.31 × 298) = 4.08 × 10⁻⁵ mol; × L = 2.46 × 10¹⁹ molecules
11. The equation needs temperature measured from absolute zero. The zero of the Celsius scale is arbitrary, so values in °C give wrong, even zero or negative, results.
12. Equal amounts in moles; the carbon dioxide has 22 times the mass (Mr 44.0 against 2.0).
