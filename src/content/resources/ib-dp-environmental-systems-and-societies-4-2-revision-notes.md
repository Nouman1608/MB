## Condensed recall

### Use

- **Withdrawal**: water taken from a source, returned or not.
- **Consumption**: the part not returned to the source (evaporated, transpired, built into products).
- Cooling water: high withdrawal, low consumption. Irrigation: high withdrawal, high consumption.
- Agriculture: about 70% of global freshwater withdrawals; industry and domestic use follow. Industry's share is larger in many high-income countries.

### Indicators

| Indicator | Formula | Shows | Hides |
|---|---|---|---|
| Availability per person | renewable freshwater ÷ population | How much exists for each person | Access, quality, seasons |
| Stress ratio | withdrawals ÷ renewable freshwater × 100 | How hard supply is worked | Regional and seasonal differences, quality |

Short reminder: withdrawals of 18 km³ from 45 km³ of renewable supply give 18 ÷ 45 × 100 = **40%**.

### Footprints and virtual water

- **Blue footprint**: surface and groundwater consumed.
- **Green footprint**: soil rainwater used by crops.
- **Grey footprint**: freshwater needed to dilute pollution to acceptable quality.
- **Virtual water**: water embedded in traded goods. Volume = mass traded × water per unit mass.
- Unit check: 1 km³ = 10⁹ m³.
- Importing virtual water saves domestic water but ties food security to world markets and moves pressure to exporters.

### Rights and equity

- 2010: UN General Assembly recognised safe drinking water and sanitation as a human right.
- **SDG 6**: clean water and sanitation for all by 2030.
- Collection burden falls most on women and girls; unsafe water spreads diseases such as cholera.
- **Rising block tariff**: cheap basic allowance, higher prices for heavier use; balances the right to water with pricing for conservation.

### Cases

- **Nile**: Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam on the Blue Nile; Ethiopia wants hydroelectric power for development; Egypt and Sudan fear reduced flow, especially during filling in dry years.
- **Aral Sea**: Soviet diversion of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya for cotton from the 1960s; sea shrank and split; salinity rose; fishing collapsed; toxic dust storms; a 2005 dam partly restored the northern part only.

### Management

- **IWRM**: manage the whole basin as one system with all users.
- **Environmental flows**: water deliberately left in rivers for ecosystems.
- **Managed aquifer recharge**: infiltrating surplus or treated water into aquifers for later use.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Withdrawal | Water removed from a source |
| Consumption | Withdrawn water not returned to its source |
| Water-stress ratio | Withdrawals as a percentage of renewable freshwater |
| Water footprint | All freshwater used to produce what is consumed |
| Grey water footprint | Freshwater volume needed to dilute pollutants |
| Virtual water | Water embedded in traded goods |
| Water security | Reliable access to enough safe water, at acceptable risk |
| Transboundary water | A river or aquifer shared by more than one state |
| Environmental flow | Water allocated to keep a river ecosystem functioning |
| IWRM | Integrated water resources management across a whole basin |
| Rising block tariff | Water pricing that increases per unit as use rises |
| Managed aquifer recharge | Deliberate infiltration of water to refill an aquifer |

## Exam traps

- Withdrawal and consumption are different; check which one data refer to.
- Mixing km³ and m³ in one calculation.
- Grey water footprint is a dilution volume, not wastewater from homes.
- Virtual water imports are not cost-free: mention food security and exporting regions.
- The Aral Sea shrank because of irrigation diversions, not natural climate change.
- One-size-fits-all strategies: desalination needs energy and money; rainwater harvesting needs rain.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between water withdrawal and water consumption.
2. Why does a thermal power station have high withdrawal but low consumption?
3. Which sector accounts for most global freshwater withdrawals?
4. A country withdraws 12 km³ from 30 km³ of renewable freshwater. Calculate the stress ratio.
5. Name the three components of a water footprint.
6. A country imports 0.5 million tonnes of rice. Assuming 2500 m³ of water per tonne, calculate the virtual water imported in km³.
7. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of relying on virtual water imports.
8. In which year did the UN General Assembly recognise the human right to water and sanitation?
9. What is a rising block tariff, and why is it used?
10. State two environmental consequences of the shrinking of the Aral Sea.
11. Why do downstream states object to large dams upstream?
12. What is an environmental flow?

### Answers

1. Withdrawal is water taken from a source; consumption is the part not returned to it.
2. It takes water for cooling and returns most of it to the river, only warmer.
3. Agriculture (about 70%).
4. 12 ÷ 30 × 100 = **40%**.
5. Blue, green and grey.
6. 0.5 × 10⁶ × 2500 = 1.25 × 10⁹ m³ = **1.25 km³**.
7. Advantage: saves scarce domestic water. Disadvantage: food security depends on world prices and other countries' harvests.
8. 2010.
9. A basic allowance at low cost with higher prices per unit for greater use; it protects access for poor households while discouraging waste.
10. Any two: rising salinity; collapse of fish populations; exposed seabed producing salt and chemical-laden dust storms; loss of wetland habitat.
11. Dams can reduce or alter the flow and sediment reaching them, especially when reservoirs fill, threatening their water supply and farming.
12. Water deliberately kept in a river to sustain its ecosystems.
