## Definitions

| Term | Meaning | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Productive efficiency | Output produced at lowest possible average cost | MC = AC, the lowest point of AC; on the PPF |
| Allocative efficiency | Resources go to the goods society values most | P = MC; with externalities, MSB = MSC |
| Economic efficiency | Best use of scarce resources overall | Productive and allocative together |
| Static efficiency | Efficiency at one point in time | Given resources and technology |
| Dynamic efficiency | Efficiency over time through investment and innovation | Cost curves shift down |
| X-inefficiency | Cost above the lowest possible for the output | Actual AC above efficient AC |
| Market failure | Free market fails to allocate efficiently | MSB not equal to MSC |

## Method boxes

**Find the productively efficient output**
1. Write AC = TC ÷ Q for each output.
2. Find the lowest AC.
3. Check MC = AC there. MC is below AC while AC falls and above it while AC rises.

**Find the allocatively efficient output**
1. Set price (demand) equal to MC.
2. For a monopolist also set MR = MC, to find its actual output.
3. Welfare loss = ½ × (output difference) × (price minus MC at the monopoly output).

**Solve an externality diagram**
1. Market output: MPB = MPC.
2. Add the external element: MSC = MPC + MEC, or MSB = MPB + MEB.
3. Social output: MSB = MSC.
4. Welfare change = ½ × (output difference) × (gap between MSB and MSC at the market output).

## Worked reminders

**Productive and allocative efficiency together.** A small firm in Country J (illustrative) has TC = 32 + 6Q + 2Q², with Q in thousands and TC in £ thousand. Then AC = 32/Q + 6 + 2Q and MC = 6 + 4Q.

| Q | AC | MC |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 22.67 | 18 |
| 4 | 22.00 | 22 |
| 5 | 22.40 | 26 |
| 6 | 23.33 | 30 |

- Productive efficiency: lowest AC is £22 at Q = 4, where MC = AC.
- If the price is £30, P = MC gives 30 = 6 + 4Q, so Q = 6. The firm is allocatively efficient there.
- At Q = 6, AC = £23.33, so it is not productively efficient. Profit = (30 − 23.33) × 6 = **£40 thousand** supernormal.
- If entry pushes the price down to £22, Q = 4 and both conditions hold with zero supernormal profit.

**X-inefficiency and dynamic efficiency.** If the same firm's actual unit cost at Q = 4 were £25 instead of £22, the excess is 3 ÷ 22 = 13.6% above the efficient level. Its cost curve sits above the efficient one. Dynamic efficiency would be an investment that moves the whole curve down in later years. For example, a firm in Country K (illustrative) spends £300,000 on new equipment, cutting unit cost from £12 to £10.50 on 80,000 units a year. Saving = 1.50 × 80,000 = £120,000 a year, so payback = 300,000 ÷ 120,000 = **2.5 years**. The gain is real but arrives late and is uncertain, which is why dynamic efficiency is harder to weigh against a certain static loss.

**Negative externality of consumption.** In a market in Country G (illustrative), MPB = 66 − 2Q, MPC = 6 + 2Q and the external cost of each unit consumed is £12, so MSB = 54 − 2Q.

- Market: 66 − 2Q = 6 + 2Q, so Q = 15 and P = £36.
- Social: 54 − 2Q = 6 + 2Q, so Q = 12 and P = £30.
- At Q = 15, MSC = 36 and MSB = 24, a gap of 12. Welfare loss = ½ × 3 × 12 = **£18 thousand**.

The market over-consumes by 3 thousand units.

**Positive externality of production.** In Country H (illustrative), MPB = 64 − 2Q, MPC = 28 + 2Q and external benefit of production is £8 per unit, so MSC = 20 + 2Q.

- Market: 64 − 2Q = 28 + 2Q, so Q = 9 and P = £46.
- Social: 64 − 2Q = 20 + 2Q, so Q = 11 and P = £42.
- At Q = 9, MSB = 46 and MSC = 38, a gap of 8. Welfare gain = ½ × 2 × 8 = **£8 thousand**.

The market under-produces by 2 thousand units.

## Four externality cases at a glance

| Case | Curve shift | Market output | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Negative, production | MSC above MPC | Too high | Factory noise or waste |
| Negative, consumption | MSB below MPB | Too high | Congestion, noise |
| Positive, consumption | MSB above MPB | Too low | Education, vaccination |
| Positive, production | MSC below MPC | Too low | Staff training that spreads |

Market failure arises in each because price reflects only private costs and benefits.

## Market failure: what to say

1. State what the market does: output is set where private benefit equals private cost.
2. State what is missing: a cost or benefit to a third party, or a missing market.
3. State the result: MSB differs from MSC, so output is too high or too low and resources are misallocated.
4. Name the consequence: a welfare loss, the triangle between MSB and MSC, which is allocative inefficiency.

## Efficiency by market structure

| Structure | Allocative | Productive |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect competition | Yes (P = MC) | Yes in the long run |
| Monopolistic competition | No | No in the long run |
| Oligopoly | Usually not | Depends on scale and rivalry |
| Monopoly | No | Often not, though scale economies may help |

Evaluation hooks: perfect competition is a benchmark, not a description; scale economies can make a large firm more efficient; contestability disciplines price; dynamic gains may offset a static loss.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Productive and allocative:** the first is about cost, the second is about what is produced.
- **Static and dynamic:** a point in time against change over time.
- **X-inefficiency and allocative inefficiency:** x-inefficiency is cost above the efficient curve; allocative inefficiency is price above MC.
- **MPC, MEC, MSC:** private, external, then their sum. The same for benefits.
- **Over- and under-production:** negative externalities give too much; positive give too little.

## Effects on markets: chains of reasoning

- **Education:** skills benefit others, so MSB exceeds MPB. Individuals buy too little. Evaluate by size of the gap and how well benefits can be measured.
- **Health:** vaccination lowers infection risk for others, so there is under-consumption. Smoking harms others, so there is over-consumption.
- **Transport:** each driver slows others, so MSC exceeds MPC. Evaluate by timing, and by whether alternatives exist.
- **Environment:** firm pollution raises MSC above MPC. The damage may be long-run and hard to value.

For any of these, finish with a judgement. Say whether the externality is large or small, whether it is certain or uncertain, and whether it falls on many people or few. A large, certain and widespread externality supports a firm conclusion that the market fails badly. A small or uncertain one supports a more cautious view.

## Quick self-test

1. A firm's total cost for 10 units is £300. Calculate AC.
2. Total cost rises from £300 to £318 for one more unit. State MC.
3. MPC is £14 and MEC is £6. Calculate MSC.
4. MPB is £25 and MEB is £9. Calculate MSB.
5. In a market the price is £15 and MC is £11. State whether output is too high or too low, and why.
6. A firm has unit cost £26. An efficient rival has £20. Calculate the excess cost as a percentage of the efficient level.
7. MSC lies below MPC. Name the externality and state the market outcome.
8. A monopolist produces 8 units at price £40 where MC is £28. Allocative efficiency would be at 13 units. Calculate the welfare loss.
9. Classify as static or dynamic: a firm cuts its price to equal MC.
10. A market produces 14 units, the social optimum is 10 and the gap between MSC and MSB at 14 is £6. Calculate the welfare loss.

### Answers

1. 300 ÷ 10 = **£30**.
2. 318 − 300 = **£18**.
3. 14 + 6 = **£20**.
4. 25 + 9 = **£34**.
5. P > MC, so buyers would pay more for another unit than it costs to make: output is **too low**.
6. (26 − 20) ÷ 20 × 100 = **30%**.
7. A **positive externality of production**; the market **under-produces**.
8. ½ × (13 − 8) × (40 − 28) = ½ × 5 × 12 = **£30**.
9. **Static**: it concerns efficiency at a point in time.
10. ½ × 4 × 6 = **£12**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying "P = MC" for allocative efficiency when an externality is present. Use MSB = MSC.
- Defining productive efficiency as "low cost" without naming the lowest point of AC.
- Mixing up the curves: MSC is MPC plus MEC, so a negative externality of production puts MSC above MPC.
- Describing the welfare loss triangle without saying why it is a loss: units where MSC exceeds MSB are produced.
- Stating that externalities cause market failure with no chain: private decision, ignored third party, wrong output.
- Evaluation that lists points without a judgement or without weighing size, timing and measurement.
- Calling a monopoly inefficient with no mention of scale economies or dynamic gains.
