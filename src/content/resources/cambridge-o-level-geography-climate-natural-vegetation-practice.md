> These are original practice questions written in the style of Cambridge O Level
> Geography (2217) assessment objectives. They are not taken from any past paper
> and are not endorsed by Cambridge International. Climate data are illustrative,
> not from real stations.

Use these questions alongside the [climate and natural vegetation study
guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-climate-natural-vegetation/)
and the [climate and natural vegetation revision

## Section A

**1.** State the meaning of *annual temperature range*. **[1]**

**2.** Name two factors, other than latitude, that influence climate.
**[2]**

**3.** Name the four layers of a tropical rainforest, from top to
bottom. **[2]**

**4.** State two ways in which hot desert plants reduce water loss.
**[2]**

**5.** On one day in a hot desert the maximum temperature is 40 °C and
the minimum is 18 °C. Calculate the diurnal range. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows climate data for two stations, A and B.

| | J | F | M | A | M | J | J | A | S | O | N | D |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A temp (°C) | 26 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 26 | 26 | 26 | 27 | 27 | 27 | 26 |
| A rain (mm) | 220 | 230 | 260 | 280 | 250 | 170 | 130 | 110 | 150 | 200 | 220 | 230 |
| B temp (°C) | 13 | 15 | 19 | 24 | 29 | 33 | 34 | 33 | 30 | 25 | 19 | 14 |
| B rain (mm) | 3 | 2 | 2 | 1 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |

**(a)** Calculate the annual temperature range for each station. **[2]**
**(b)** Station A receives 2,450 mm of rain a year. Calculate the total
annual rainfall for station B. **[1]**
**(c)** Identify which station has an equatorial climate. Give two
pieces of evidence from the table. **[3]**
**(d)** Explain why station A receives rain in every month. **[3]**

**7.** Many hot deserts lie on the west coasts of continents beside
cold ocean currents.

**(a)** Explain how a cold ocean current makes a coastal area dry. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why hot deserts are found near the Tropics. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how tropical rainforest vegetation is adapted to its
climate. Refer to three adaptations. **[6]**

**9.** Explain why rainforest soils are nutrient-poor even though the
forest grows so well. **[3]**

**10.** For a named area of tropical rainforest, describe the causes
and effects of deforestation. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** The difference between the mean temperature of the hottest month
and the mean temperature of the coolest month [1].

**2.** Any two [2]: pressure systems; winds; distance from the sea;
altitude; ocean currents.

**3.** Emergents, canopy, understorey, forest floor (all four in the
correct order for 2 marks; two or three in order for 1 mark) [2].

**4.** Any two [2]: small leaves; waxy leaves; leaves reduced to spines;
fewer stomata or stomata closed by day; water stored in thick stems.

**5.** 40 − 18 = 22 °C [1].

**6. (a)** A: 27 − 26 = 1 °C [1]. B: 34 − 13 = 21 °C [1].
**(b)** 14 mm [1].
**(c)** Station A [1]. Any two pieces of evidence, with figures [2]:
temperature between 26 and 27 °C all year; very small annual range of
1 °C; heavy rainfall totalling 2,450 mm; every month receives at least
110 mm.
**(d)** Up to 3 marks: the Sun is high overhead all year, so the
ground is strongly heated [1]; hot air rises, giving low pressure [1];
rising air cools and water vapour condenses to form cumulonimbus
clouds and heavy convection rain, usually in the afternoon [1].

**7. (a)** Up to 3 marks: the cold current cools the air above it [1];
cool air can hold little water vapour, so there is little moisture
available for rain [1]; the cooled air is stable and does not rise, so
rain clouds do not form, although fog may [1].
**(b)** Up to 3 marks: air that rises at the Equator moves towards the
Tropics and sinks [1], forming the subtropical high-pressure belt [1];
sinking air warms and holds its moisture, so clouds and rain rarely
form, giving clear skies [1].

**8.** Three adaptations, each identified [1] and explained [1] [6]:
drip tips, which shed heavy rain quickly so fungi and algae do not
grow on leaves; buttress roots, which support very tall trees in
shallow soil; lianas and epiphytes, which reach light in the canopy
without growing their own trunk; tall trees with branches only at the
top, to reach light above the canopy; thin, smooth bark, as there is
no cold season to protect against.

**9.** Up to 3 marks: heavy rain leaches nutrients down through the
soil, out of reach of roots [1]; most nutrients are stored in the
vegetation rather than the soil [1]; leaf litter decomposes rapidly
in the hot, wet conditions and nutrients are quickly taken back up by
shallow roots, so few remain in the soil [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking.
*Level 1 (1–3 marks)*: simple causes or effects listed with no named
place, for example "trees are cut down for farming".
*Level 2 (4–5 marks)*: causes and effects developed, with a named
area.
*Level 3 (6–7 marks)*: causes and effects developed and linked, with
place-specific detail.
Indicative content for the Amazon: causes include cattle ranching,
soya farming, logging, mining such as the Carajás iron-ore mine, road
building such as the Trans-Amazonian Highway, and dams such as Belo
Monte on the Xingu. Effects include soil erosion and leaching once the
canopy is removed; loss of fertility; silting of rivers and increased
flooding; loss of habitats and species; reduced transpiration and
local rainfall; release of carbon dioxide; and loss of land and
culture for indigenous peoples. Credit balanced points such as jobs
and export income.

## A note on exam technique for this subtopic

Use figures with months and units whenever you describe a climate,
and give a process, not just a factor, when you explain one ("air
rises, cools and condenses" rather than "it is low pressure"). For
adaptations, the mark is usually for the *reason*, so finish each
point with "so that" or "because".
