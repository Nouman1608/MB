## Section A

**1.** A particle accelerates uniformly from rest to 15 m s⁻¹ in 6 s. Find its acceleration and the distance it travels in this time. **[3]**

**2.** A train decelerates uniformly from 30 m s⁻¹ to 12 m s⁻¹ while travelling 420 m. Find the deceleration and the time taken. **[4]**

**3.** A particle moves in a straight line with initial velocity 2 m s⁻¹ and constant acceleration 3 m s⁻². Find the distance it travels during the fifth second of its motion. **[3]**

**4.** A ball is dropped from rest from a window and reaches the ground 1.5 s later. Find the height of the window above the ground and the ball's speed on reaching the ground. **[3]**

**5.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards and caught at the point of release 3 s later. Explain why its average velocity over the flight is zero but its average speed is not. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 12.6 m s⁻¹ from a point 2.5 m above horizontal ground.

**(a)** Find the greatest height of the ball above the ground. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the time from release until the ball hits the ground. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the total distance the ball travels before hitting the ground. **[2]**

**7.** Particle A is projected vertically upwards from ground level at 24.5 m s⁻¹. At the same instant particle B is released from rest at a point 49 m vertically above A's starting point.

**(a)** Show that the particles meet 2 s after they start moving. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the height above the ground at which they meet. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the velocity of each particle when they meet. **[2]**

**8.** Two particles, P and Q, are 100 m apart on a straight track. At time t = 0, P starts from rest and moves towards Q with constant acceleration 2 m s⁻². At the same instant, Q moves towards P at a constant 10 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Find the time at which the particles meet. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the distance P has travelled when they meet. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the speed of P when they meet. **[1]**

**9.** A train starts from rest and accelerates uniformly to V m s⁻¹ in 40 s. It travels at V m s⁻¹ for 120 s and then decelerates uniformly to rest in 30 s. The total distance travelled is 3100 m.

**(a)** Sketch a velocity–time graph and use it to find V. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the acceleration and the deceleration. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the average speed for the journey. **[1]**

**10.** A particle moves along a straight line with constant acceleration. It passes point A, then point B 3 s later, then point C a further 2 s later. AB = 21 m and BC = 25 m.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particle and its speed at A. **[4]**

**(b)** Find its speed at C. **[1]**

**11.** A driver travelling at 20 m s⁻¹ sees an obstruction 60 m ahead. After a reaction time of 0.7 s, during which the car keeps its speed, the driver brakes with a constant deceleration of 4.5 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the distance travelled during the reaction time. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the braking distance and state whether the car stops before the obstruction. **[3]**

**(c)** Find the greatest initial speed from which the car could stop within 60 m, with the same reaction time and deceleration. **[3]**

**12.** A walker moves along a straight east–west path. Her displacement–time graph, with east positive and displacement measured from her starting point, consists of three straight line segments: from (0, 0) to (120, 240), from (120, 240) to (180, 240), and from (180, 240) to (300, −60), with time in seconds and displacement in metres.

**(a)** Find her velocity in each stage. **[3]**

**(b)** Find her average speed and her average velocity for the whole 300 s. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** a = 15 ÷ 6 = **2.5 m s⁻²** [1]. s = ½(u + v)t = ½ × 15 × 6 [1] = **45 m** [1].

**2.** v² = u² + 2as: 144 = 900 + 840a [1], so a = −0.9, a **deceleration of 0.9 m s⁻²** [1]. v = u + at: 12 = 30 − 0.9t [1], so **t = 20 s** [1].

**3.** Distance in the fifth second = s(5) − s(4) [1]. s(5) = 2 × 5 + 1.5 × 25 = 47.5 m and s(4) = 2 × 4 + 1.5 × 16 = 32 m [1]. Distance = **15.5 m** [1].

**4.** Down positive, u = 0, a = 9.8, t = 1.5. s = ½ × 9.8 × 1.5² = 11.025, so **11.0 m** [1] [1]. v = 9.8 × 1.5 = **14.7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**5.** The ball returns to its starting point, so its displacement is zero and average velocity = 0 ÷ 3 = 0 [1]. It still travels a positive distance (up and back down), so average speed = distance ÷ time is greater than zero [1].

**6. (a)** Up positive, v = 0 at the top: 0 = 12.6² − 19.6s [1], s = 158.76 ÷ 19.6 = 8.1 m above the release point [1]. Height above ground = 8.1 + 2.5 = **10.6 m** [1].

**(b)** Ground is 2.5 m below release, so s = −2.5: −2.5 = 12.6t − 4.9t² [1], giving 4.9t² − 12.6t − 2.5 = 0 [1]. t = (12.6 + √207.76) ÷ 9.8 = **2.76 s** (the negative root is rejected) [1].

**(c)** Up 8.1 m, then down 10.6 m [1]. Total = **18.7 m** [1].

**7. (a)** Up positive from the ground. A: h = 24.5t − 4.9t² [1]. B: h = 49 − 4.9t² [1]. Equal when 24.5t = 49, so **t = 2 s** [1].

**(b)** h = 49 − 4.9 × 4 = **29.4 m** [1].

**(c)** A: v = 24.5 − 9.8 × 2 = **4.9 m s⁻¹ upwards** [1]. B: v = 9.8 × 2 = **19.6 m s⁻¹ downwards** [1].

**8. (a)** P travels t² metres and Q travels 10t metres [1]. They meet when t² + 10t = 100 [1], so t² + 10t − 100 = 0 and t = (−10 + √500) ÷ 2 [1] = **6.18 s** [1].

**(b)** t² = **38.2 m** [1].

**(c)** v = 2t = **12.4 m s⁻¹** [1].

**9. (a)** Trapezium-shaped graph rising over 40 s, flat for 120 s, falling over 30 s [1]. Area = ½ × 40V + 120V + ½ × 30V = 155V [1]. 155V = 3100, so **V = 20** [1].

**(b)** Acceleration = 20 ÷ 40 = **0.5 m s⁻²** [1]. Deceleration = 20 ÷ 30 = **0.667 m s⁻²** [1].

**(c)** 3100 ÷ 190 = **16.3 m s⁻¹** [1].

**10. (a)** From A, with u the speed at A: to B, 21 = 3u + 4.5a [1]; to C (t = 5, s = 46), 46 = 5u + 12.5a [1]. Solving: 15a = 33 [1], so **a = 2.2 m s⁻²** and **u = 3.7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** v = 3.7 + 2.2 × 5 = **14.7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**11. (a)** 20 × 0.7 = **14 m** [1].

**(b)** 0 = 400 − 9s [1], so s = **44.4 m** [1]. Total = 58.4 m, which is less than 60 m, so the car **stops before the obstruction** [1].

**(c)** 0.7u + u² ÷ 9 = 60 [1], so u² + 6.3u − 540 = 0 [1]. u = (−6.3 + √2199.69) ÷ 2 = **20.3 m s⁻¹** [1].

**12. (a)** Stage 1: 240 ÷ 120 = **2 m s⁻¹ east** [1]. Stage 2: **0** (at rest) [1]. Stage 3: (−60 − 240) ÷ 120 = **−2.5 m s⁻¹, so 2.5 m s⁻¹ west** [1].

**(b)** Distance = 240 + 300 = 540 m, so average speed = 540 ÷ 300 = **1.8 m s⁻¹** [1]. Displacement = −60 m, so average velocity = **−0.2 m s⁻¹ (0.2 m s⁻¹ west)** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using s = +2.5 or +39.2 for a point below the release point.
- Keeping the negative root of a quadratic in t.
- In meeting problems, using one particle's displacement for both, or forgetting that particles moving towards each other share the gap between them.
- Finding the distance travelled in the fifth second as s(5) instead of s(5) − s(4).
- Treating the reaction time as part of the braking stage.
- Giving average velocity as a distance, with no sign or direction.
