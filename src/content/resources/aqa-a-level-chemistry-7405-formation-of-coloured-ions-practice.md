These questions cover section **3.2.5.4 Formation of coloured ions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Formation of coloured ions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-formation-of-coloured-ions/) and the [Formation of coloured ions revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-formation-of-coloured-ions-revision-notes/). Use h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s, c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹ and L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Explain why an aqueous solution of copper(II) sulfate is blue. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why aqueous solutions of zinc(II) ions and scandium(III) ions are colourless. **[2]**

**3.** Write the equation that links the energy difference between d orbitals with the wavelength of light absorbed, and state what each symbol represents. **[2]**

**4.** A complex ion absorbs light of wavelength 650 nm. Calculate the energy difference ΔE for one ion. **[2]**

**5.** A complex ion absorbs light of wavelength 480 nm. Calculate ΔE in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**

**6.** The energy gap between two d orbitals in a complex ion is 3.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ J.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of light absorbed, in nm. **[2]**

**(b)** The light absorbed is red. State the colour in which the solution appears. **[1]**

**7.** Calculate the frequency of light of wavelength 520 nm. **[2]**

**8.** State and explain what happens to the colour when [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ reacts with excess concentrated hydrochloric acid. Refer to the energy gap in your answer. **[3]**

**9.** The colour of iron changes from pale green to yellow-brown when Fe²⁺(aq) is oxidised to Fe³⁺(aq). Explain why. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how a colorimeter is used to find the concentration of copper(II) ions in a solution. **[4]**

**11.** The absorbances of five standard solutions of a coloured complex are in direct proportion to concentration: 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ gives an absorbance of 0.550. A 5.00 cm³ sample of an industrial solution is diluted to 50.0 cm³ and has an absorbance of 0.275. Calculate the concentration of the original solution. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why copper(I) compounds are usually white but copper(II) compounds are blue or green. **[2]**

**13.** A student writes: "The colour of a transition metal ion is due to d electrons falling to a lower energy level and giving out coloured light." Identify the error and correct it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The Cu²⁺ ion has an incomplete d sub-level (3d⁹), and the ligands split the d orbitals into groups of different energy [1]; some wavelengths of visible light are absorbed as d electrons move to a higher energy d orbital (an excited state) [1]; the remaining light is transmitted, which gives the complementary colour, blue [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Zn²⁺ has a full 3d¹⁰ sub-level, so no d electron can be excited to a vacant d orbital [1]; Sc³⁺ has no d electrons, so none can be excited [1]; so neither absorbs visible light. **[2]**

**3.** ΔE = hν = hc/λ [1]; ΔE is the energy difference, h is Planck's constant, ν is frequency, c is the speed of light and λ is the wavelength [1]. **[2]**

**4.** λ = 650 × 10⁻⁹ m [1]; ΔE = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 6.50 × 10⁻⁷ = **3.06 × 10⁻¹⁹ J** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** ΔE per ion = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 4.80 × 10⁻⁷ = 4.14 × 10⁻¹⁹ J [1]; multiply by 6.022 × 10²³ to give 2.50 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ [1]; = **250 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** λ = hc/ΔE = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ 3.00 × 10⁻¹⁹ [1] = 6.63 × 10⁻⁷ m = **663 nm** [1]

**(b)** Blue-green (the complementary colour of red) [1]. **[3]**

**7.** ν = c/λ = 3.00 × 10⁸ ÷ 5.20 × 10⁻⁷ [1] = **5.77 × 10¹⁴ Hz** (s⁻¹) [1]. **[2]**

**8.** The solution changes from blue to yellow-green [1]; water ligands are replaced by chloride ligands, and the co-ordination number changes from 6 to 4 [1]; this changes the energy gap ΔE between the d orbitals, so a different wavelength of light is absorbed [1]. **[3]**

**9.** The oxidation state of iron changes from +2 to +3 [1]; this changes the energy gap ΔE between the d orbitals, so different wavelengths are absorbed [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Select a filter that is the complementary colour of the solution (so it passes light that the solution absorbs strongly) [1]; measure the absorbance of a series of standard solutions of known concentration [1]; plot a calibration graph of absorbance against concentration [1]; measure the absorbance of the unknown and read its concentration from the graph [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Gradient = 0.550 ÷ 0.0100 = 55.0 dm³ mol⁻¹, so c(diluted) = 0.275 ÷ 55.0 = 0.00500 mol dm⁻³ [1]; dilution factor = 50.0 ÷ 5.00 = 10 [1]; c(original) = 0.00500 × 10 = **0.0500 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Cu⁺ has a full 3d¹⁰ sub-level, so d electrons cannot be excited and no visible light is absorbed [1]; Cu²⁺ has 3d⁹, an incomplete sub-level, so d electrons can absorb visible light and the compound is coloured [1]. **[2]**

**13.** The colour is due to absorption, not emission: a d electron absorbs energy and moves to a higher energy d orbital [1]; the colour seen is the light that is not absorbed (transmitted), the complementary colour [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* "Absorbs some wavelengths and transmits the rest" is the heart of every colour answer.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that the electrons emit light.
- Forgetting to convert nm to m.
- Giving ΔE per ion when kJ mol⁻¹ was asked.
- Saying "the ligand changes the colour" without referring to the energy gap ΔE.
- Using the same colour for the filter as the solution.
