## Questions

**1.** Name the function of money shown in each case.
**(a)** A tenant agrees in January to pay February's rent at the end of February. **[1]**
**(b)** A shopkeeper marks every item with a price in the local currency. **[1]**
**(c)** A worker pays a bus fare with a travel card. **[1]**
**(d)** A farmer puts the harvest income into a bank account to spend next year. **[1]**

**2.** Country B's money (illustrative, bn units): banknotes and coins 15, current account balances 45, savings deposits 90.
**(a)** Calculate the broad total. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate banknotes and coins as a percentage of the total. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why money is defined as more than banknotes and coins. **[2]**

**3.** In a barter economy there are 6 goods.
**(a)** Calculate the number of exchange rates needed, and the number of prices if money is used. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which function of money this shows and why it helps traders. **[2]**

**4.** A household in Country C holds 2,400 units. Prices rise 6% over the year.
**(a)** Calculate the spending power of 2,400 units held as cash, in today's prices, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** The household instead saves at 4% interest. Calculate the balance after one year and its value in today's prices, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(c)** State what this shows about money as a store of value. **[1]**

**5.** Explain two roles of a central bank. **[4]**

**6.** A household in Country D has a variable-rate loan of 90,000 units at 7%. The central bank raises its rate by 1 percentage point and the bank passes on the full rise.
**(a)** Work out how much more interest the household pays over a year, and the monthly equivalent. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the rise could affect spending in the economy. **[4]**

**7.** A commercial bank in Country E holds deposits of 600 mn units, pays savers 1.5%, and has lent 450 mn at 5%.
**(a)** Calculate the gross interest margin. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how the bank serves both savers and borrowers. **[4]**

**8.** A bank in Country F has loans of 400 mn units, own funds of 20 mn and deposits of 300 mn, of which 36 mn is held in cash.
**(a)** 6% of the loans are not repaid. Calculate the loss and say whether own funds cover it. **[3]**
**(b)** 20% of depositors withdraw at once. Calculate the cash shortfall. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the central bank could help to protect the stability of the system. **[3]**

**9.** An insurance company in Country G has 2,000 policyholders paying 45 units a year each. On average 1 in 40 makes a claim of 1,500 units.
**(a)** Calculate premiums, expected claims and the amount left. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how insurers and other institutions help households and firms. **[3]**

**10.** A firm in Country H wants to borrow 200,000 units to build a workshop. Its bank charges 6%. The central bank then cuts its rate and the bank lowers the loan rate to 5%. Evaluate whether the cut will lead the firm to invest. **[8]**

**11.** Evaluate whether commercial banks are more important than the central bank for the success of Country J's economy. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Means of deferred payment. [1]
**(b)** Unit of account. [1]
**(c)** Medium of exchange. [1]
**(d)** Store of value. [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match the function to what the money is doing in the sentence; a price tag measures value and is not itself the payment.

**2. (a)** 15 + 45 + 90 = **150 bn units** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 15 ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Balances in current and savings accounts are accepted for payment or can be turned into spending money [1]. Most payments are made by card or transfer, so notes and coins alone would leave out 90% of the money [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Support the definition with the figures from the table; a bare statement that money is "more than cash" tends to gain less than one that uses the data.

**3. (a)** Barter: 6 × 5 ÷ 2 [1] = **15 exchange rates**. Money: **6 prices** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Unit of account [1]. Every good is priced in one unit, so traders compare goods easily and need only 6 prices instead of 15 rates [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the function and then say what it changes; naming alone is a recall point only.

**4. (a)** 2,400 ÷ 1.06 [1] = **2,264.15 units** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Balance = 2,400 × 1.04 = **2,496 units** [1]. In today's prices 2,496 ÷ 1.06 = **2,354.72 units** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** When prices rise, cash loses spending power, and the interest earned only partly makes up the loss [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by 1.06 for the real value; subtracting 6% of the balance, or comparing 2,496 with 2,400, does not allow for prices.

**5.** Influencing interest rates: the central bank sets a policy rate that guides the rates banks charge and pay [1], so it can make borrowing dearer or cheaper and change spending and investment [1]. Ensuring stability: it supervises banks and can lend to one that is short of cash [1], which protects deposits and confidence so that lending can continue [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each role needs a development to reach full credit; naming a role alone leaves the explanation incomplete.

**6. (a)** 90,000 × 0.07 = 6,300 and 90,000 × 0.08 = 7,200, so extra = **900 units a year** [1], which is 900 ÷ 12 = **75 units a month** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Higher interest makes loans dearer, so borrowers have less income left after repayments [1]. Consumers cut spending on credit-financed goods [1]. Saving becomes more attractive, which also reduces spending [1]. Firms find investment more costly and may postpone it, so aggregate demand falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the cost of the loan to behaviour and then to aggregate demand; stopping at "people have less money" leaves the chain unfinished.

**7. (a)** Interest received = 450 × 0.05 = 22.5 mn [1]. Interest paid = 600 × 0.015 = 9 mn [1]. Margin = 22.5 − 9 = **13.5 mn units** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Savers get a safe place to keep money [1] and earn interest on deposits [1]. Borrowers get loans for homes or business without having saved the full amount [1]. The bank pools many savers' deposits to lend, and earns from the gap between the rates [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the interest figures from part (a) in part (b); work out interest received on the amount lent and interest paid on all deposits, then subtract; do not apply either rate to the wrong amount.

**8. (a)** Loss = 400 × 0.06 = **24 mn units** [1]. Own funds are 20 mn [1], so they fall short by **4 mn** and do not cover the loss [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Withdrawals = 300 × 0.20 = 60 mn [1]. Shortfall = 60 − 36 = **24 mn units** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** It could set rules that require banks to hold more own funds and cash [1]. It could lend to the bank so that withdrawals can be paid [1]. This stops a loss of confidence from spreading to other banks [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A bank run is about liquidity (cash) and a loan loss is about solvency (own funds); keeping the two apart makes each answer more precise.

**9. (a)** Premiums = 2,000 × 45 = **90,000** [1]. Claims = 2,000 ÷ 40 = 50, and 50 × 1,500 = **75,000** [1]. Left = **15,000 units** for costs and profit [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Insurance pools small payments from many, so each household or firm pays a known amount rather than risk a large loss [1]. This lets them buy homes or start businesses with less fear [1]. Other institutions such as pension funds or stock markets move savings to those who can use them, for example by letting firms sell shares to raise funds [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Name a specific institution and say what it does; general statements that "it helps people" are too vague to develop.

**10.** Interest at 6% = 200,000 × 0.06 = 12,000 a year; at 5% = 10,000, so the saving is 2,000 a year [1]. Lower borrowing costs raise the expected profit from the workshop, so more projects now earn more than they cost [1]. The firm may borrow and invest, adding to capacity and demand for builders and equipment [1]. However, a 1 point cut only saves 2,000 a year [1]. If demand for the firm's products is weak or the owner is pessimistic, a lower rate may not persuade the firm to invest [1]. The bank may also refuse the loan if it judges the firm risky [1]. Judgement: the cut helps most for firms with sound projects and stable demand; it is not enough alone when confidence is low [1]. The effect also builds up over time, as the firm decides and builds [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the data (a 2,000 saving on a 200,000 loan) to judge size; arguments that restate "lower rates encourage investment" without testing it stay at assertion.

**11.** For commercial banks: they take deposits and lend to firms and households, turning saving into investment [1]. Without loans, a bakery cannot buy an oven or a family a home, so output and jobs grow more slowly [1]. They provide accounts and payment services used by everyone [1]. For the central bank: it influences interest rates, which affect borrowing across the whole economy [1]. It supports stability, and a failure of the banking system would stop lending and cause a fall in output [1]. Commercial banks depend on the central bank's rules and support in a crisis [1]. Judgement: in normal times commercial banks fund growth day to day, but they rely on a stable system that only the central bank can protect, so both are needed; the central bank matters more when stability is at risk [1]. The answer depends on whether the economy is stable or in difficulty [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement that says when each institution matters more, and why, is more convincing than "both are important" with no reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining money as banknotes and coins only.
- Naming a function but not applying it to the scenario.
- Dividing by the wrong number when finding a real value.
- Mixing up the central bank and a commercial bank.
- Forgetting to link a change in interest rates to spending and then aggregate demand.
- Confusing a loan loss with a shortage of cash.
- Treating deposits as the bank's own money.
- Giving a one-sided answer to an evaluate question without a judgement.
