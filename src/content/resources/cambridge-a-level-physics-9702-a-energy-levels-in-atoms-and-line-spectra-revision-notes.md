Subtopic 22.4 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers discrete electron energy levels in isolated atoms, the appearance and formation of emission and absorption line spectra, and the equation hf = E₁ − E₂. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Energy levels and line spectra study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-energy-levels-in-atoms-and-line-spectra/); to apply the ideas, try the [Energy levels and line spectra practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-energy-levels-in-atoms-and-line-spectra-practice/).

## Core ideas

- Electrons in an **isolated** atom (for example a low-pressure gas) can only have certain **discrete** energies.
- Each change of level involves **one photon**, with energy equal to the **difference** between the two levels.
- Only certain differences exist, so only certain wavelengths appear: a **line** spectrum.

```
hf = E₁ − E₂        λ = hc / (E₁ − E₂)
1 eV = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
```

## The energy level diagram

| Feature | What to say |
|---|---|
| Zero of energy | A free electron, just removed from the atom |
| Negative values | Bound electrons have less energy than a free electron |
| Ground state | Lowest level; where the atom normally sits |
| Excited states | All higher levels; unstable |
| Spacing | Levels get closer together towards zero |
| Ionisation energy | Energy to take an electron from the ground state to zero |

**Atomic hydrogen:** n = 1: −13.6 eV; n = 2: −3.40 eV; n = 3: −1.51 eV; n = 4: −0.85 eV. Ionisation energy 13.6 eV.

## Emission versus absorption

| | Emission line spectrum | Absorption line spectrum |
|---|---|---|
| Appearance | Bright lines on a dark background | Dark lines on a continuous spectrum |
| Source | Hot or electrically excited low-pressure gas | White light passing through a cooler gas |
| Electron movement | Falls to a lower level | Rises to a higher level |
| Photon | Emitted, energy = gap | Absorbed only if energy exactly = gap |
| Number of lines | Many: any pair of levels | Fewer: mostly from the ground state |
| Wavelengths | Characteristic of the element | Same wavelengths as some emission lines |

**Why the absorption lines are dark:** absorbed photons are re-emitted in **all directions**, so fewer reach the observer along the original beam.

**Why hot solids give continuous spectra:** atoms are close together, so their levels interact and spread out; they are not isolated.

## Method for transition questions

1. Read off the two level energies, keeping the signs.
2. ΔE = higher − lower (always positive for the photon).
3. Convert eV to J: multiply by 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹.
4. f = ΔE/h, or λ = hc/ΔE.
5. Check the region: visible light is about 400–700 nm, which is roughly 1.8–3.1 eV.

**Number of possible emission lines** from level n down: n(n − 1)/2.

## Quick calculations (hydrogen)

| Transition | ΔE / eV | λ / nm | Region |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2 → 1 | 10.2 | 122 | Ultraviolet |
| 3 → 2 | 1.89 | 658 | Visible (red) |
| 4 → 2 | 2.55 | 488 | Visible (blue-green) |
| 4 → 3 | 0.66 | 1880 | Infrared |
| Ionisation from n = 1 | 13.6 | 91.4 | Ultraviolet |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Energy level | An allowed energy of an electron in an atom |
| Ground state | The lowest energy level |
| Excitation | Raising an electron to a higher level |
| De-excitation | An electron falling to a lower level, emitting a photon |
| Ionisation | Removing an electron completely from the atom |
| Line spectrum | Spectrum of separate, sharp wavelengths |
| Continuous spectrum | Spectrum with all wavelengths over a range |

## Exam traps

- Subtracting signed numbers wrongly: −1.51 − (−3.40) = +1.89, not −4.91.
- Using one level's energy as the photon energy.
- Forgetting to convert eV to J before using h in J s.
- Saying the absorption lines are dark because photons are "destroyed" or "used up" without mentioning re-emission in all directions.
- Claiming an atom absorbs any photon with **at least** the gap energy. It must be an exact match (unless the photon ionises the atom).
- Describing emission lines as "bands" or "a rainbow".
- Forgetting that some hydrogen lines lie outside the visible region.

## Self-test

1. What does "discrete" mean when describing energy levels?
2. Why are the energy levels of an atom given negative values?
3. Describe the appearance of an emission line spectrum.
4. Describe the appearance of an absorption line spectrum.
5. Why do absorption lines appear dark even though excited atoms re-emit photons?
6. Write the equation linking photon frequency to two energy levels.
7. Calculate the photon energy, in J, for a hydrogen transition from n = 4 to n = 2.
8. Calculate the wavelength of that photon.
9. How many emission lines can be produced by electrons excited to n = 5?
10. Why does a cool gas show fewer absorption lines than the same gas shows emission lines?
11. A photon of energy 11.0 eV passes through hydrogen in the ground state. Is it absorbed? Explain.
12. Why does a hot filament lamp produce a continuous spectrum?
