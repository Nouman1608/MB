## Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** What is the meaning of the term Haram? **[1]**

**2.** Name **one** food or drink that Muslims are forbidden to consume. **[1]**

**3.** Give **two** conditions for the halal slaughter of an animal. **[2]**

**4.** Muslims are asked to dress modestly.

**(a)** State **one** rule of modesty in dress for men. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain **one** reason why modest dress is important for Muslim women. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **two** ways in which parents give their children a religious upbringing. **[4]**

**6.** Explain **two** reasons why Muslims try to earn a halal living. **[4]**

## Section B: longer-answer questions

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why Muslims follow dietary laws.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which parents can help their children to develop good morals and character.
Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority in your answer. **[6]**

**9.**

> **Sahih al-Bukhari 5027**
>
> The Prophet (pbuh) said, "The best among you (Muslims) are those who learn the Qur'an and teach it."

'The most important duty of Muslim parents is to teach their children the Qur'an.'

Examine why this statement is significant for Muslims.

In your answer you should:

- use the given passage
- provide supporting evidence and reasoning. **[12]**

## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

**1.** Forbidden or unlawful [1].

**2.** Any one [1]: pork; alcohol and other intoxicants; blood; dead animals; food dedicated to other than Allah.

**3.** Any two, [1] each: the animal is alive and healthy; the name of Allah is said; the throat is cut quickly with a very sharp knife; the blood is allowed to drain.

**4. (a)** Any one [1]: cover at least from the navel to the knees; do not wear silk or gold jewellery; avoid pride in clothing; lower the gaze.

**(b)** One reason identified [1] and explained [2].

- **Obedience to Allah** [1]: the Qur'an tells believing women to draw their head coverings over their chests (24:31) [1], so modest dress is a way of obeying a clear command [1].
- **Dignity and respect** [1]: modest dress asks others to judge a woman by her character [1], and it protects her from harassment (33:59) [1].
- **Identity** [1]: it shows that she is a Muslim [1], and reminds her and others of her values [1].

**5.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Teaching prayer** [1]: parents encourage children to pray from about the age of seven, so it becomes a habit [1].
- **Teaching the Qur'an** [1]: children learn to read, recite and understand it, so they know their source of guidance [1].
- **Setting an example** [1]: children copy what they see, so parents who pray and are honest teach by their actions [1].
- **Taking them to the mosque** [1]: children join the community and see worship in practice [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Obedience to Allah** [1]: the Qur'an forbids interest, theft and fraud, so honest earnings are part of obedience [1].
- **Purity of worship** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said that Allah is pure and accepts only what is pure [1].
- **Fairness to others** [1]: honest earnings do not harm customers, employees or the poor [1].
- **Blessing** [1]: Muslims believe that halal earnings bring blessing to the family [1].

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Obedience** [1]: the foods are forbidden by commands of Allah [1].
- **Discipline** [1]: giving up certain foods trains self-control [1].
- **Kindness to animals** [1]: halal slaughter requires a sharp knife and a quick cut to avoid suffering [1].
- **Health and purity** [1]: the rules protect people from what is harmful or impure [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- Qur'an 5:3: dead animals, blood, pork and food dedicated to other than Allah are forbidden, with an exception for severe hunger [2].
- Qur'an 5:90: intoxicants are "defilement from the work of Satan" [2].
- The Hadith that Allah has prescribed excellence in everything, so slaughter well and spare suffering [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Setting an example** [1]: children learn honesty and kindness from what they see at home [1].
- **Teaching manners and values** [1]: parents explain respect, sharing and gratitude [1].
- **Giving advice** [1]: Luqman advised his son to worship Allah, be patient and avoid arrogance, a model for parents [1].
- **Fair treatment** [1]: treating children justly teaches fairness [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "The best of you are those best in character" (Bukhari) [2].
- Qur'an 31:13-19, the advice of Luqman to his son [2].
- Qur'an 66:6: "protect yourselves and your families from a Fire" [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the Qur'an is the first source of Shariah; learning it gives children belief, prayer and morals; the Hadith calls those who learn and teach it the best; children who know it can then follow the rest of Shariah.
- **Against the statement:** good character and example may matter as much as teaching; the Qur'an teaches parents to protect the family in many ways, such as justice, kindness, good food and health; a child who learns the words without seeing them lived may not be guided; the Hadith praises both learning and teaching, not only parents.
- **Balanced view:** teaching the Qur'an is central, but it must be joined with example and care.

*Use of the passage*

- "The best among you" shows that excellence is in knowledge of the Qur'an.
- "Learn the Qur'an" shows the first step.
- "And teach it" shows that the duty is to pass it on, as parents do.
- Credit candidates who link the Hadith to the duty of parents as "shepherds" of the household.

Level 4 answers use the passage directly, argue both sides with examples, and reach a reasoned judgement.
