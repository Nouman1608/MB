All data are invented. Revise first with the [economic methodology study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-economic-methodology-cambridge-alevel-economics/) and the [economic methodology revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-economic-methodology-cambridge-alevel-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following is a normative statement? **[1]**

A: The unemployment rate rose from 5% to 7% last year

B: A higher minimum wage would reduce employment of young workers

C: The government ought to give priority to reducing unemployment

D: Most voters think unemployment is the main economic problem

**2.** Which **one** of the following is a positive statement? **[1]**

A: Petrol taxes are unfairly high

B: Petrol prices should be controlled by the government

C: A 10% rise in petrol taxes would raise tax revenue by 6%

D: Cheaper petrol would be better for families

**3.** What is meant by the very long run? **[1]**

A: A period of more than ten years

B: A period in which all factors of production can be varied but technology is fixed

C: A period in which technology and the quality of factors can change as well as their quantity

D: A period in which at least one factor of production is fixed

**4.** Explain why economics is described as a social science. **[3]**

**5.** Explain why a statement can be positive even if it is untrue. **[2]**

**6.** State what is meant by ceteris paribus, and give an example of its use in drawing a demand curve. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured questions

**7.** Classify each statement as positive or normative, giving a reason in each case.

**(a)** "Food prices in the country rose by 9% last year." **[1]**

**(b)** "Food prices rose by 9% last year, so the poor should receive food vouchers." **[1]**

**(c)** "Giving food vouchers to the poor would increase their food consumption." **[1]**

**(d)** "Food vouchers are a fairer policy than a cash payment." **[1]**

**8.** In an invented town, the price of cinema tickets rises by 5% over a year, and over the same year a large new housing estate is built nearby. The number of tickets sold rises by 3%.

**(a)** A local newspaper concludes that higher prices make people go to the cinema more often. Explain why this conclusion is unreliable. **[3]**

**(b)** Separate evidence suggests that, ceteris paribus, the price rise alone would have reduced sales by 4%. Estimate the effect of the new estate on its own. **[2]**

**9.** A clothing manufacturer faces a large, permanent rise in demand for its jackets.

**(a)** Explain how it could respond in the short run. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how it could respond in the long run. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how its response could differ in the very long run. **[2]**

**10.** After a 25% rise in electricity prices in an invented country, household electricity use falls 2% in the first year and 10% after four years. Explain why the response is larger over the longer period. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **two** reasons why economists find it harder than natural scientists to test their theories. **[4]**

**12.** Two economists agree that a tax on sugary drinks would reduce consumption by 15% and raise 50 million cu a year. One supports the tax and the other opposes it. Assess how far the positive and normative distinction helps to explain their disagreement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. "Ought to" signals a value judgement. D is positive: whether most voters think this can be checked by a survey. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. It is a testable prediction. A, B and D contain value judgements ("unfairly", "should", "better"). **[1]**

**3.** C [1]. B describes the long run and D the short run; time periods are not defined by a number of years. **[1]**

**4.** Economics studies human behaviour: how individuals, firms and governments make choices about scarce resources [1]. It uses a scientific method, building models and hypotheses and testing them against evidence [1]. But it is a social rather than a natural science because it studies people, who can change their behaviour, and controlled experiments are rarely possible [1]. **[3]**

**5.** A positive statement is one that can be tested against evidence [1]. The test may show it to be false, but it remains positive because it contains no value judgement and evidence can settle it [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Ceteris paribus means other things being equal: all other influences are held constant [1]. A demand curve shows how quantity demanded changes with price, assuming income, tastes and the prices of other goods do not change [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Positive: it can be checked against price data [1].

**(b)** Normative: it contains a fact but the conclusion ("should") is a value judgement [1].

**(c)** Positive: it is a testable prediction about behaviour [1].

**(d)** Normative: "fairer" is a value judgement [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Two things changed at the same time: the price and the number of people living nearby [1]. The new estate would increase demand for tickets, which could outweigh the effect of the price rise [1]. Without holding other things constant, the newspaper cannot separate the two effects, so the data do not show that higher prices cause higher attendance [1].

**(b)** Combined change = price effect + estate effect, so 3 = −4 + estate effect [1], giving an estate effect of about **+7%** [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** In the short run its factory and machinery are fixed [1], so it can raise output only by hiring more workers, adding overtime or extra shifts, and buying more materials [1].

**(b)** In the long run all factors are variable [1], so it can buy more machines or open a larger factory using existing technology [1].

**(c)** In the very long run technology and the quality of factors can change [1], so it could adopt new production methods, such as automated cutting, or develop new fabrics that raise output from the same inputs [1]. **[6]**

**10.** In the first year households can change little: their appliances and heating systems are already installed [1], so they can only use them less, for example by switching off lights [1]. Over four years they can replace appliances with more efficient ones, insulate homes or switch to other energy sources [1]. More substitutes become available with time, so the fall in use (10%) is five times the first-year response (2%) [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Reason 1: controlled experiments are rarely possible [1], because many influences change at once in the real economy, so the effect of one variable is hard to isolate [1]. Reason 2: people learn and change their behaviour [1], sometimes in response to the predictions themselves, so a relationship observed in the past may not hold in future [1]. Other valid reasons: data are imprecise and often revised; values influence which questions are studied. **[4]**

**12.** Award up to 8 marks across knowledge, analysis and evaluation.
- The economists agree on the positive analysis: the predicted fall in consumption and the revenue raised [1].
- Their disagreement must therefore rest on normative judgements [1].
- One may value the health benefits of lower sugar consumption more highly [1].
- The other may value consumer freedom more, or object that the tax takes a larger share of poorer households' income [1].
- So the distinction explains why agreement on facts does not produce agreement on policy [1].
- But they may also disagree on other positive questions not mentioned, such as the size of long-run health gains or whether consumers switch to other unhealthy products [1].
- Positive evidence on these could narrow the disagreement, though not remove the value judgements [1].
- Judgement: the distinction largely explains the disagreement, but differences in unstated positive predictions may also play a part, with a reasoned conclusion [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Judging positive statements by whether they are true.
- Missing the "should" at the end of a statement that starts with facts.
- Writing "ceteris paribus" without saying what is held constant.
- Defining time periods in months or years.
- Describing a short-run response that changes the size of the factory.
- Ending an assess question without a judgement.
