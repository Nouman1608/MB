## Questions

**1.** In a closed cylinder, 120 J of heat enters a gas and a piston does 45 J of work on it. Find ΔU. **[2]**

**2.** (a) State what is meant by a closed system and by an isolated system. **[2]**
(b) State the change in internal energy of an isolated sample of ideal gas, and give a reason. **[1]**

**3.** A gas at a constant pressure of 2.4 × 10⁵ Pa expands from 1.5 × 10⁻³ m³ to 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³ while absorbing 1.50 kJ of heat.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in internal energy. **[2]**

**4.** A rigid container holds 3.0 mol of gas. The gas is heated from 280 K to 350 K. Determine the change in internal energy, the work done by the gas and the heat supplied. **[3]**

**5.** 0.25 mol of gas at 310 K expands isothermally while absorbing 540 J of heat.

**(a)** State the change in internal energy and the work done by the gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of the particles, why the pressure of the gas falls. **[2]**

**6.** A gas changes from 3.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 5.0 × 10⁻³ m³ along a straight line on a P-V diagram.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in internal energy. **[2]**
**(c)** Determine the heat transferred to the gas. **[1]**

**7.** A gas at 1.2 × 10⁵ Pa and 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ expands adiabatically to 3.0 × 10⁻³ m³.

**(a)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[3]**
**(c)** State whether the temperature of the gas rises or falls. **[1]**

**8.** A gas in an insulated cylinder is compressed quickly by a piston.

**(a)** State the heat transferred to the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** State the sign of the work W and of the change in internal energy under the Clausius convention. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain, using the particle model, why the temperature rises. **[2]**

**9.** 0.40 mol of gas starts at A with T = 300 K and V = 5.0 × 10⁻³ m³. It is heated at constant volume to B, where the pressure has doubled. It then expands at constant pressure to C, where the volume has doubled.

**(a)** Determine the temperatures at B and at C. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total change in internal energy from A to C. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the total work done by the gas. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the total heat supplied. **[2]**

**10.** 0.050 mol of gas at 290 K and 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa is compressed adiabatically to one eighth of its volume.

**(a)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the final temperature is 1160 K (to 3 s.f.). **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the change in internal energy. **[2]**
**(d)** State the work done by the gas. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why the gas ends up hotter even though no heat enters it. **[1]**

**11.** A gas is taken from state 1 (3.0 × 10⁵ Pa, 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³) to state 2 (3.0 × 10⁵ Pa, 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³), then to state 3 (1.5 × 10⁵ Pa, 4.0 × 10⁻³ m³).

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas from 1 to 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in internal energy and the heat supplied from 1 to 2. **[3]**
**(c)** For the change from 2 to 3, state the work done and determine ΔU and Q. **[3]**
**(d)** Show that the change from 1 to 3 has ΔU = 0, and state what this tells you about the temperatures of states 1 and 3. **[1]**
**(e)** The gas could instead go from 1 to 3 by a different route. State whether ΔU, Q and W would each be the same or could differ. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** W = −45 J because work is done on the gas [1]. ΔU = Q − W = 120 − (−45) = **165 J** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A bare 75 J (from subtracting the wrong way round) shows a sign slip; writing W = −45 J explicitly makes your intention clear.

**2. (a)** Closed: matter cannot cross the boundary, but energy can cross it as heat or work [1]. Isolated: the boundary lets through neither matter nor energy [1]
**(b)** **ΔU = 0**, because Q = 0 and W = 0 for an isolated system [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say a closed system "cannot exchange anything" lose the idea that energy still crosses its boundary.

**3. (a)** W = PΔV = 2.4 × 10⁵ × (4.0 − 1.5) × 10⁻³ [1] = **600 J** [1]
**(b)** ΔU = Q − W = 1500 − 600 [1] = **900 J** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert kJ to J before subtracting; mixing 1.50 kJ with 600 J is a common slip that loses the final answer.

**4.** Volume is fixed, so **W = 0** [1]. ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = 1.5 × 3.0 × 8.31 × 70 [1] = **2.62 kJ**, and Q = ΔU + W = **2.62 kJ** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Justify W = 0 by saying the volume does not change; stating the value alone gives no reasoning to credit.

**5. (a)** ΔU = 0 as the temperature is constant [1]; so W = Q = **540 J** [1]
**(b)** Temperature is constant, so the mean kinetic energy and speed of the particles are unchanged [1]. The volume is larger, so collisions with a given area of wall are less frequent and the pressure is lower [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explanations that mention only "the gas has more room" miss the link to collision rate and are not enough on their own for an explain question.

**6. (a)** Area under the line is a trapezoid: average pressure = 2.0 × 10⁵ Pa [1]; W = 2.0 × 10⁵ × 3.0 × 10⁻³ = **600 J** [1]
**(b)** P₁V₁ = 600 J and P₂V₂ = 500 J [1]; ΔU = 3/2 × (500 − 600) = **−150 J** [1]
**(c)** Q = ΔU + W = −150 + 600 = **450 J** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* ΔU can be found from the end points without n or T; candidates who try to find a temperature first often introduce rounding errors.

**7. (a)** P₂ = P₁(V₁/V₂)^(5/3) = 1.2 × 10⁵ × (1/3)^(5/3) [1] = **1.92 × 10⁴ Pa** [1]
**(b)** Q = 0 so W = −ΔU [1]; ΔU = 3/2 × (1.923 × 10⁴ × 3.0 × 10⁻³ − 1.2 × 10⁵ × 1.0 × 10⁻³) = 3/2 × (57.7 − 120) = −93.5 J [1]; W = **+93.5 J** [1]
**(c)** The temperature **falls**, since ΔU is negative [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded intermediate values for the pressure; rounding to 1.9 × 10⁴ Pa before using it changes the third significant figure of the work.

**8. (a)** **Q = 0** [1]
**(b)** W is **negative** (work is done on the gas) [1]; ΔU is **positive** [1]
**(c)** The piston does work on the gas and no heat escapes, so the random kinetic energy of the particles increases [1]; temperature measures the mean kinetic energy so it rises [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both parts of the chain: energy transferred by work, then the effect on particle kinetic energy. "Compressing makes it hot" alone is a restatement, not an explanation.

**9. (a)** At constant volume P ∝ T, so T_B = 2 × 300 = **600 K** [1]. At constant pressure V ∝ T, so T_C = 2 × 600 = **1200 K** [1]
**(b)** ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = 1.5 × 0.40 × 8.31 × 900 [1] = **4.49 kJ** [1]
**(c)** A to B: W = 0. P_B = 2nRT_A/V_A = 3.99 × 10⁵ Pa [1]; B to C: W = 3.99 × 10⁵ × 5.0 × 10⁻³ = **1.99 kJ** [1]
**(d)** Q = ΔU + W = 4.49 + 1.99 [1] = **6.48 kJ** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Work is only done on the isobaric leg. Adding a PΔV term for the vertical leg gives a wrong total work, and the heat total then inherits the error.

**10. (a)** P₂ = P₁ × 8^(5/3) [1] = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 32 = **3.2 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]
**(b)** T₂/T₁ = (P₂V₂)/(P₁V₁) = 32 × (1/8) = 4 [1]; T₂ = 4 × 290 = **1160 K** [1]
**(c)** ΔU = 3/2 nRΔT = 1.5 × 0.050 × 8.31 × 870 [1] = **542 J** [1]
**(d)** W = −ΔU = **−542 J** [1]
**(e)** Work is done on the gas, which raises its internal energy, and Q = 0 means none of it is lost as heat [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that" question, state the intermediate ratio before the final value; going straight to 1160 K with no working does not demonstrate the result.

**11. (a)** W = PΔV = 3.0 × 10⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻³ [1] = **600 J** [1]
**(b)** P₁V₁ = 600 J, P₂V₂ = 1200 J [1]; ΔU = 3/2 × 600 = **900 J** [1]; Q = 900 + 600 = **1500 J** [1]
**(c)** Volume is fixed, so **W = 0** [1]; P₃V₃ = 600 J, so ΔU = 3/2 × (600 − 1200) = **−900 J** [1], and Q = **−900 J** [1]
**(d)** P₃V₃ = P₁V₁ = 600 J, so ΔU = 900 − 900 = 0 and states 1 and 3 are at the **same temperature** [1]
**(e)** ΔU would be the **same** as it depends only on the end states, while Q and W **could differ** because they depend on the path [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (e) needs both halves: that ΔU is fixed by the end states and that Q and W are route dependent. Naming only one earns less than the full mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating compression work as positive, which flips the sign of ΔU or Q.
- Forgetting that W = 0 on a vertical P-V line.
- Mixing kJ with J, or cm³ and litres with m³.
- Using Celsius temperatures in an equation that needs T in kelvin.
- Writing ΔU = 0 for an adiabatic change instead of Q = 0.
- Using PV^(5/3) = constant for an isothermal change, or PV = constant for an adiabatic change.
- Rounding intermediate values and then losing the third significant figure.
- Explaining a temperature change without naming the energy transfer (work or heat) that causes it.
- Saying Q and W are the same for any route between two states.
