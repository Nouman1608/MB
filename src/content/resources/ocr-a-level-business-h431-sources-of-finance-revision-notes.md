Condensed notes on the **Sources of finance** content (6.2 on this site) in the Area of Study **Accounting and finance within a business environment**, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and two worked examples are in the [sources of finance study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-sources-of-finance/); test yourself with the [sources of finance practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-sources-of-finance-practice/). The whole topic is in the [accounting and financial considerations revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-accounting-and-financial-considerations-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

- Explain sources of finance.
- Distinguish internal from external, and short-term from long-term.
- Explain how these factors affect the choice: time, legal structure, quantitative factors, qualitative factors, external influences.
- Evaluate sources of finance for a business and its stakeholders.

## Core recall

### The sources at a glance

| Source | Internal or external | Term | Main advantage | Main disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Retained profit | Internal | Long | Cheap, keeps control | Slow; not for new firms |
| Sale of assets | Internal | Long | Quick cash | Less capacity |
| Collecting receivables faster | Internal | Short | Frees cash | May upset customers |
| Owner's capital | Internal | Long | No interest | Personal risk |
| Overdraft | External | Short | Flexible, quick | High interest; can be withdrawn |
| Trade credit | External | Short | Free if on time | Late payment harms relations |
| Factoring | External | Short | Cash from invoices at once | Fee; may upset customers |
| Bank loan | External | Long | Certain amount and repayment | Fixed interest; security |
| Leasing | External | Long | No big outlay | No ownership; higher total cost |
| Share issue | External | Long | No repayment | Dilutes control; dividends |
| Venture capital | External | Long | Expertise and funds | Loss of control; wants an exit |
| Debentures | External | Long | Fixed rate | Interest must be paid |
| Grants | External | Varies | Often not repayable | Conditions |

### Classifying

- **Internal:** from within the business. **External:** from outside.
- **Short-term:** up to about a year. **Long-term:** over a year.

### The five factors

| Factor | Effect on the choice |
|---|---|
| Time | Match finance to how long it is needed; consider speed |
| Legal structure | Sole traders and partnerships cannot issue shares; private companies sell shares privately; public limited companies can sell to the public |
| Quantitative | Amount, interest rate, total cost, gearing, ability to repay, security |
| Qualitative | Control, attitude to risk, relationships, flexibility |
| External influences | Interest rates, the economy, availability of credit, the stock market |

### Loan against shares

| | Loan | Share issue |
|---|---|---|
| Repayment | Required | None |
| Interest or dividends | Interest due whatever the profit | Dividends optional |
| Gearing | Rises | Falls |
| Control | Unchanged | Diluted |
| Risk | Higher in bad years | Lower |

### Evaluation checklist

Cost, risk, control, speed, fit with purpose and time, effect on stakeholders. Then a judgement.

## Quick worked reminder

Kingsley Components (invented) needs £400,000. A loan at 7% adds £28,000 of interest and raises gearing from 25% to 40%; earnings per share rise to 43.5p. A share issue of 100,000 shares at £4 leaves gearing at 20%, gives earnings per share of 40.0p and cuts the owners' holding to 83.3%. Dalby Joinery: factoring £90,000 of invoices costs £1,800; an overdraft of £72,000 for 60 days at 10% costs £1,184.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Overdraft | Bank borrowing up to an agreed limit |
| Factoring | Selling invoices to a finance company for cash now |
| Leasing | Renting an asset for regular payments |
| Venture capital | Funds from investors in a high-growth business in return for shares |
| Dilution | A fall in each owner's percentage holding when new shares are issued |
| Gearing | Non-current liabilities as a percentage of capital employed |
| Security | An asset a lender can claim if a loan is not repaid |
| Debenture | A long-term loan certificate, usually fixed-rate |

## Exam traps

- Overdrafts are short-term; they are not suitable for buying long-lived assets.
- A share issue is not free: it dilutes ownership and may lead to dividends.
- Sole traders cannot issue shares.
- A cheaper source may be riskier; weigh both.
- Link the source to the purpose and the time the money is needed.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between internal and external sources of finance.
2. Give two examples of internal sources.
3. Give two examples of short-term external sources.
4. Which source can a sole trader not use?
5. Calculate overdraft interest on £30,000 at 12% a year for 90 days (to the nearest £).
6. A machine costs £60,000. Leasing costs £1,500 a month for 48 months. How much more does leasing cost?
7. State one advantage of a share issue over a loan.
8. State one qualitative factor affecting the choice of finance.
9. Why does the matching principle matter?
10. Explain one effect of high interest rates on the choice of finance.
11. A shareholder owns 60,000 of 100,000 shares. The company issues 50,000 new shares. What percentage does she now hold?
12. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of venture capital.

### Answers

1. Internal sources come from within the business (for example retained profit); external sources come from outside (for example a bank loan).
2. Any two: retained profit, sale of assets, owner's savings, faster collection of receivables.
3. Any two: overdraft, trade credit, factoring.
4. A share issue.
5. £30,000 × 12% × 90 ÷ 365 = **£888** (887.67).
6. 48 × £1,500 = £72,000; £72,000 − £60,000 = **£12,000** more.
7. There is no repayment or fixed interest, so gearing and risk are lower.
8. Any one: wish to keep control, attitude to risk, relationship with the lender, flexibility of terms.
9. Finance should last as long as the need, so a long-lived asset is not funded by money that may be withdrawn quickly.
10. Loans cost more, so the business may prefer retained profit or shares, or delay the project.
11. 60,000 ÷ 150,000 × 100 = **40%**, so she loses majority control.
12. Advantage: expertise and a large sum with no repayment. Disadvantage: loss of some control or pressure to sell.
