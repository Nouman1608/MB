These questions cover the standard level and higher level understandings of D.2, Electric and magnetic fields, in the IB *Physics guide* (first assessment 2025). The higher level potential content is not included. Take k as 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻², e as 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C and g as 9.81 m s⁻².

## Questions

**1.** A small sphere carrying −12 nC hangs near a rod carrying +30 nC. State the direction of the force on the sphere, the direction of the force on the rod, and compare the sizes of the two forces. **[3]**

**2.** Two small spheres carry +12 nC and +30 nC. Their centres are 5.0 cm apart.

**(a)** Calculate the electric force on the 12 nC sphere and state its direction. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the separation at which the force would be four times as large. **[2]**

**3.** Three identical conducting spheres A, B and C are on insulating stands. A carries +9.0 µC, B is neutral and C carries −3.0 µC. A touches B and they are separated. Then B touches C and they are separated. Find the final charge on each sphere. **[4]**

**4.** In a Millikan-type experiment a negatively charged oil drop of mass 8.16 × 10⁻¹⁵ kg is held stationary between two horizontal plates 1.20 cm apart. The potential difference across the plates is 1.50 kV.

**(a)** State which plate is positive, with a reason. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the charge on the drop. **[3]**
**(c)** State the number of elementary charges on the drop. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how results from many drops provide evidence that charge is quantized. **[2]**

**5.** You have a negatively charged rod and a neutral metal sphere on an insulating stand. Describe how to give the sphere a positive charge without the rod touching it. **[4]**

**6.** An isolated point charge of −4.8 nC sits in air.

**(a)** Find the magnitude and direction of the field strength 0.25 m from it. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the force on a proton placed at that point. **[2]**

**7.** Two parallel metal plates 2.5 cm apart have a potential difference of 1.8 kV.

**(a)** Calculate E in the central region of the gap. **[2]**
**(b)** A particle of charge +3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ C is in this region. Calculate the force on it and state its direction. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the field lines between and around the edges of the plates. **[2]**

**8.** Three point charges lie on a straight line: A (+3.0 nC) at x = 0, B (−2.0 nC) at x = 0.10 m and C (+5.0 nC) at x = 0.30 m. Calculate the resultant force on B, giving its direction. **[6]**

**9.** **(a)** Describe the electric field inside and outside an isolated charged conducting sphere. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how field lines show the way the field strength of a point charge changes with distance. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why two electric field lines never cross. **[1]**

**10.** **(a)** A long straight wire carries a current into the page. State the direction of the magnetic field at a point P to the right of the wire, in the plane of the page. **[2]**
**(b)** Looking at the left end of an air-core solenoid, the current circulates clockwise. State which end is the north pole. **[2]**
**(c)** State one way in which the field of a solenoid resembles that of a bar magnet. **[1]**

**11.** Two identical small spheres, each of mass 1.5 g, hang from insulating threads 0.50 m long attached to the same point. They carry equal charges and come to rest 4.0 cm apart.

**(a)** Identify the three forces on one sphere. **[2]**
**(b)** Determine the electrostatic force between the spheres. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the charge on each sphere. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The sphere is pulled towards the rod, since the charges are unlike [1]. The rod is pulled towards the sphere [1]. The forces are equal in magnitude, even though the charges differ, as they form a Newton's third law pair [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Comparing the forces needs a reason, and a statement that the larger charge gives a larger force is the common wrong answer here.

**2. (a)** F = (8.99 × 10⁹ × 12 × 10⁻⁹ × 30 × 10⁻⁹) / 0.050² [1]. F = **1.29 × 10⁻³ N**, repulsive, directed away from the other sphere [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** F is proportional to 1/r², so four times the force needs half the distance [1]. The separation is **2.5 cm** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the unit conversions in your substitution so that a slip is visible.

**3.** After A touches B: each has 9.0 / 2 = +4.5 µC [1]. B and C have a total of 4.5 + (−3.0) = +1.5 µC [1]. Shared equally, B and C each have +0.75 µC [1]. Final charges: **A = +4.5 µC, B = +0.75 µC, C = +0.75 µC**; the total is +6.0 µC, which equals the starting total [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Checking that the final total equals the initial total is a quick way to catch an error and shows conservation of charge explicitly.

**4. (a)** The drop is negative and the electric force must act upwards, so the field must point downwards [1]. Field lines run from positive to negative, so the **top plate is positive** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** E = V/d = 1500 / 0.0120 = 1.25 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹ [1]. qE = mg [1]. q = (8.16 × 10⁻¹⁵ × 9.81) / 1.25 × 10⁵ = **6.40 × 10⁻¹⁹ C** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** 6.40 × 10⁻¹⁹ / 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ = **4** elementary charges [1]. **[1]**
**(d)** Different drops carry different charges, but every measured value is a whole-number multiple of one smallest value [1]. No fractions of that unit appear, so charge comes in discrete units and is not continuous [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For an explain question about evidence, say what the data showed and what conclusion follows; stating only that charge is quantized does not answer how the results support it.

**5.** Bring the rod near the sphere without touching, so electrons in the sphere are repelled to the far side [1]. Connect the sphere to earth, so electrons flow from the sphere to earth [1]. Disconnect the earth connection while the rod is still in place [1]. Remove the rod, and the remaining positive charge spreads over the sphere [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The order of removing the earth and the rod is the point of the question, so state the order explicitly.

**6. (a)** E = kQ/r² = (8.99 × 10⁹ × 4.8 × 10⁻⁹) / 0.25² [1]. E = **6.90 × 10² N C⁻¹** [1], directed towards the charge, because the charge is negative [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** F = eE = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.90 × 10² [1]. F = **1.10 × 10⁻¹⁶ N**, directed towards the charge [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A direction is part of the answer for a vector quantity, and a negative source charge reverses the direction you might assume.

**7. (a)** E = V/d = 1800 / 0.025 [1] = **7.2 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** F = qE = 3.2 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 7.2 × 10⁴ [1] = **2.3 × 10⁻¹⁴ N**, directed from the positive plate towards the negative plate [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Between the plates the lines are straight, parallel and evenly spaced, running from the positive plate to the negative plate [1]. Near the edges the lines curve outwards, so the field is not uniform there [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert cm to m before dividing; the answer in V m⁻¹ will otherwise be a hundred times too small.

**8.** Force from A on B: F = (8.99 × 10⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁹) / 0.10² = 5.39 × 10⁻⁶ N [1], attractive, so towards A [1]. Force from C on B: the distance is 0.20 m, so F = (8.99 × 10⁹ × 5.0 × 10⁻⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁹) / 0.20² = 2.25 × 10⁻⁶ N [1], attractive, so towards C [1]. The two forces are in opposite directions, so subtract [1]. Resultant = **3.1 × 10⁻⁶ N towards A** (3.15 × 10⁻⁶ N before rounding) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw both force arrows first; using 0.30 m for the force from C is a common slip.

**9. (a)** The charge sits on the surface of the sphere [1]. The field inside is zero, so there are no field lines inside [1]. Outside, the field lines are radial and meet the surface at right angles, as for a point charge at the centre [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Where the lines are closer, the field is stronger [1]. The radial lines spread apart as the distance from the charge increases, so the field gets weaker further away [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The field at a point has only one direction, and a crossing would give two directions at that point [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A description of the inside of a conductor should say zero field, not merely weak field.

**10. (a)** Using the right-hand grip rule with the thumb into the page, the field circles are clockwise [1]. At P, on the right of the wire, the field points **down the page** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** The fingers curl clockwise as seen from the left end, so the thumb points away from the viewer, to the right [1]. The field leaves the solenoid at the right end, so the **right end is the north pole** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Outside the solenoid the field lines form loops running from the north end to the south end, like those of a bar magnet [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention conventional current and the grip rule you used; a bare direction with no reasoning leaves nothing to credit if it is wrong.

**11. (a)** The weight, acting downwards [1]. The tension in the thread, along the thread, and the horizontal electrostatic repulsion from the other sphere [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** sin θ = 0.020 / 0.50, so θ = 2.29° [1]. In equilibrium, F = mg tan θ [1]. F = 1.5 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 × tan 2.29° = **5.9 × 10⁻⁴ N** [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** F = kq²/r² with equal charges, so q = √(F r² / k) [1]. q = √(5.89 × 10⁻⁴ × 0.040² / 8.99 × 10⁹) [1]. q = **1.0 × 10⁻⁸ C** on each sphere [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the full separation of 0.040 m, not half of it, in Coulomb's law.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving nC, µC or cm unconverted in a formula.
- Using the wrong distance, for example the radius of a sphere or half a separation, in Coulomb's law.
- Giving a size without the direction, or the force on a negative charge along the field.
- Explaining Millikan's results without stating the whole-number pattern.
- Describing induction with the steps in the wrong order, or missing the earth connection.
- Field lines lacking arrows, intersecting, or ending away from any charge.
- Using the direction of electron flow in a right-hand grip rule.
