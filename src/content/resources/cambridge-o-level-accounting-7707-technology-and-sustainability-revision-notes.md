Quick recall for section **7.3 Technology and sustainability** of the Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029, which lists it as new content in this edition. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Technology and sustainability study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-technology-and-sustainability/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [Technology and sustainability practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-accounting-7707-technology-and-sustainability-practice/). The wider Topic 7 picture is in the [concepts, ethics, technology and sustainability revision notes](/resources/igcse-accounting-accounting-concepts-ethics-technology-and-sustainabilit-revision-notes/). Figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## The five points in 7.3

1. Digital applications for **all or part** of the accounting records.
2. The **need** to store accounting data safely and sustainably.
3. The **risks** of not storing it safely and sustainably.
4. The **four types** of storage system: manual, data storage devices, cloud services, other digital services.
5. The **advantages and disadvantages** of each type.

No specific accounting software is required. Describe functions, never brand names.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to recall |
|---|---|
| Digital application | A computer program used to record, process or report accounting data |
| All or part of the records | Every book kept digitally, or only some (for example, the sales ledger digital and the cash book handwritten) |
| Safe storage | Records kept complete, accurate, available and confidential |
| Sustainable storage | Records kept with as little waste of paper, energy and equipment as possible, while staying usable |
| Back-up | A second copy of data, kept so that the records can be restored if the first copy is lost |
| Off-site copy | A copy held away from the premises, so one fire, flood or theft cannot destroy every copy |
| Unauthorised access | Someone without permission seeing or changing the records |
| Data storage device | Equipment the business holds: internal drive, external hard drive, USB flash drive, memory card |
| Cloud service | Data held on a provider's servers and reached over the internet |
| Other digital services | Systems that hold records as a by-product of another service: online banking, emailed invoices, online payment records |
| Electronic waste | Discarded computers and devices, which should be wiped of data and recycled |

## What digital applications change

| Better than manual | Not solved, or made worse |
|---|---|
| One input posts both sides of the entry | Errors of original entry are copied to every record that uses the figure |
| Totals and balances calculated automatically | Errors of principle and omission still occur |
| Reports produced quickly | Cost of equipment, set-up and training |
| Records easy to search and copy | Business depends on the system and on power |

**Recall example:** a selling price keyed as 4.00 instead of 40.00 on 75 credit sales understates revenue by 75 x 36.00 = 2,700. The trial balance still agrees, because both sides were posted from the same wrong figure.

## Why store safely, why store sustainably

**Safely**, because the records are needed again: to prepare statements, chase debts, answer suppliers, support loan applications, answer tax enquiries and provide evidence in disputes. Safe storage also protects customers' private details, which links to confidentiality in 7.2.

**Sustainably**, because storage uses paper, ink, space, electricity (including the servers behind cloud services) and equipment that eventually becomes waste. Remedies: print only what is needed, avoid duplicate copies, send documents electronically, keep readable formats, wipe and recycle old devices.

## Risks of poor storage

- Loss from fire, flood or theft, with no copy.
- Device failure, corruption or accidental deletion.
- Hacking or unauthorised access, exposing private data.
- Fraud: figures altered to hide a theft.
- Records cannot be produced when the tax authority or a lender asks.
- Cost and time of rebuilding records.
- Loss of trust from customers, suppliers and lenders.
- Environmental harm from wasted paper and discarded devices.

## The four storage systems compared

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Manual | No power, internet or equipment needed; little training; cannot be hacked remotely | Destroyed with the premises; bulky; slow to search, total and copy; uses paper |
| Data storage devices | Fast; large capacity; business controls its data; works without internet | Small devices lost or stolen; devices fail; back-ups rely on someone doing them; e-waste |
| Cloud services | Access anywhere with internet; held off-site; provider maintains equipment; shared working | Needs internet; continuing charge; depends on a third party; must control access |
| Other digital services | Records created automatically; less paper; independent evidence | Data spread across systems; may be kept for a limited time; format controlled by others |

**A mix is strongest:** working copy on a device, second copy in a cloud service, key originals on paper.

## Answer pattern for 7.3 questions

1. Name the point (storage type, risk, advantage).
2. Explain it: why or how.
3. Apply it to the business in the question.
4. For discuss, evaluate or advise: give the other side, then a judgement with a reason.

**Back-up frequency** matters as much as having a back-up. Weekly back-ups risk up to a week of entries; daily back-ups limit the loss to one day.

## Exam traps

- Naming a software package.
- Claiming digital records are "error-free".
- Mixing up safe (protects data) and sustainable (limits waste and energy).
- Saying "the cloud is not secure" with no reason.
- Listing only advantages in a "discuss" answer.
- Leaving "other digital services" out of the four types.
- Writing generic points with no link to the business described.
- Forgetting that a device kept in the same office is lost in the same fire.

## Self-test

1. What does "part of the accounting records" mean in 7.3?
2. Name the four types of storage system.
3. Give two reasons a business needs its old records.
4. State the four qualities of safely stored data.
5. Give two environmental costs of storing records.
6. Why does a digital application not reveal an error of original entry in the trial balance?
7. Give one advantage of manual storage over a cloud service.
8. Give one disadvantage of a USB flash drive as the only copy of the records.
9. Give an example of an "other digital service".
10. A business backs up every Monday evening. Its computer fails on Friday evening. How many days of entries could be lost if it trades Tuesday to Friday?
11. Which ethical principle from 7.2 is most directly linked to preventing unauthorised access?
12. Why is a combination of storage systems safer than one system?

### Answers

1. Some books are kept digitally and others manually.
2. Manual, data storage devices, cloud services, other digital services.
3. Any two: preparing statements; chasing debts; answering suppliers; loan applications; tax enquiries; evidence in disputes.
4. Complete, accurate, available, confidential.
5. Any two: paper and ink; electricity for devices and servers; electronic waste; storage space.
6. Both sides are posted from the same wrong figure, so debits still equal credits.
7. For example, it needs no internet connection or continuing charge, or cannot be hacked remotely.
8. For example, it is easily lost or stolen, or it can fail, so the records could not be recovered.
9. For example, online banking statements, invoices sent by email, or records of an online payment service.
10. Four days (Tuesday, Wednesday, Thursday, Friday).
11. Confidentiality.
12. One copy can be lost, damaged or unavailable while another survives.
