## Questions

### Section A: short answer

**1.** Define the term **market research**. **[2]**

**2.** Identify two methods of primary research. **[2]**

**3.** State two advantages of secondary research. **[2]**

**4.** Define the term **sample** in market research. **[2]**

**5.** In a survey, 63 out of 180 people said they would try a new brand of yoghurt. Calculate the percentage who said they would try it. **[2]**

**6.** State one difference between an open question and a closed question. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Harrowgate Pet Supplies surveyed 200 of its customers to ask whether they would use a home-delivery service.

| Age group | Number asked | Would use delivery |
|---|---|---|
| Under 30 | 50 | 21 |
| 30 to 59 | 90 | 45 |
| 60 and over | 60 | 36 |
| Total | 200 | 102 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of each age group who would use the delivery service. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of all customers asked who would use the service. **[1]**

**(c)** Harrowgate has 3,000 regular customers. Estimate how many might use the service. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain one reason why the estimate in part (c) may be inaccurate. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why sampling is useful to a business carrying out market research. **[3]**

**9.** Lansdowne Hotels plans to open a hotel in a city it does not know well. Explain how two different secondary research sources could help it decide. **[4]**

**10.** Pemrose Gifts wants to know whether customers would buy a new range of scented candles. The owner asked 15 friends, at her birthday party, the question: "Wouldn't you love to buy my beautiful new candles?" All 15 said yes.

**(a)** Identify two factors that make this research inaccurate. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how the owner could improve the accuracy of her research. **[4]**

**11.** Ivelle Fragrances is about to launch a new perfume. It is deciding whether to use a focus group or a questionnaire to test customer reactions.

Consider the advantages and disadvantages of each method for Ivelle Fragrances. Justify which method it should use. **[6]**

**12.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of primary research for a small business. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Collecting and analysing information [1] about customers, competitors or the market [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Any two from: questionnaires or surveys; interviews; focus groups; observation. One mark for each of two different methods [1] [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Usually cheaper than primary research [1]; quicker to obtain because the data already exists [1]. Also accept: gives a wide view of the whole market. **[2]**

**4.** A smaller group of people chosen from the whole target population [1] whose answers are used to represent that population [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 63 / 180 x 100 [1] = **35%** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** A closed question gives a fixed set of answers to choose from [1], while an open question lets people answer in their own words [1]. **[2]**

**7.** **(a)** Under 30: 21 / 50 x 100 = **42%** [1]. 30 to 59: 45 / 90 x 100 = **50%** [1]. 60 and over: 36 / 60 x 100 = **60%** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** 102 / 200 x 100 = **51%** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 51 / 100 x 3,000 = **1,530** customers [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** People who say they would use a service do not always do so [1], so actual use may be lower than 1,530, especially if Harrowgate charges for delivery and the survey did not mention a price [1]. Also accept: the 200 surveyed may not represent all 3,000 customers, with a reason. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The percentages show interest rising with age. A good follow-on comment is that Harrowgate should aim early promotion of the service at older customers.

**8.** It is too expensive and slow to ask every customer [1], so a sample gives results more quickly and at lower cost [1]. If the sample is representative, the results still give a reasonable guide to what the whole market thinks [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Government sources [1] could show the number of tourists or business visitors to the city each year, helping Lansdowne judge whether demand is high enough [1]. Competitor websites [1] would show the prices and facilities of other hotels, helping Lansdowne set a competitive price and offer something different [1]. Also accept market reports or trade magazines with a relevant use. **[4]**

**10.** **(a)** The sample is too small (15 people) [1]. The question is leading [1]. Also accept: the sample is biased because friends are likely to support her. **[2]**

**(b)** Ask a larger number of people, such as 100 or more [1], chosen from her actual target customers rather than friends, so the sample is representative [1]. Use neutral questions, such as asking people to rate the candles from 1 to 5 [1], and include questions about price and how often they would buy, so she learns whether they would actually spend money [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Focus group: lets Ivelle hear *why* people like or dislike the scent and packaging, which is important for a product judged on smell and image [1]; but the group is small and may not represent all its customers, and strong members may sway others [1]. Questionnaire: can reach many more people, giving figures such as the percentage who would buy [1]; but it cannot capture detailed reactions to a scent, and people would need to smell the perfume to answer, which limits how it can be sent out [1]. Judgement: Ivelle should start with a focus group [1], because for a perfume the reasons behind reactions matter more than numbers at this stage, and it could follow with a questionnaire once the scent is chosen [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Advantage: the data is collected for the business's own question, such as what its local customers want [1], so it is directly useful for the decision it faces [1]. Disadvantage: it takes time and money to design, carry out and analyse [1], which a small business with a limited budget and few staff may struggle to afford [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing raw numbers between groups of different sizes.
- Naming a factor affecting accuracy without saying how it distorts the results.
- Recommending a method without linking it to what the business needs to find out.
- Treating stated intentions as guaranteed sales.
- Describing secondary research as free.
