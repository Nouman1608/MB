These questions cover sub-topic 7.1 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): population density and distribution, population pyramids, World Bank income groups and the comparison of LIC and HIC population structures. All countries and data are invented.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *population distribution*. **[1]**

**2.** State the unit usually used to measure population density. **[1]**

**3.** Name the age group described as the elderly dependent population. **[1]**

**4.** State what the World Bank uses to classify countries into income groups. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** physical features of places that are usually sparsely populated. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows data for three invented provinces.

| Province | Population | Area (km²) |
|---|---|---|
| Arlen | 1,800,000 | 9,000 |
| Bexa | 350,000 | 35,000 |
| Corin | 4,200,000 | 3,500 |

(a) Calculate the population density of each province. Show your working. **[3]**
(b) Using your answers, explain why Corin is described as densely populated. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why two countries with the same population density can have very different population distributions. **[2]**

**8.** A population pyramid for the invented country of Talvia shows these percentages of the total population:

| Age group | Male | Female |
|---|---|---|
| 0 to 14 | 20% | 19% |
| 15 to 64 | 28% | 29% |
| 65 and over | 2% | 2% |

(a) Calculate the percentage of the population that is young dependent. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the number of dependants per 100 people of working age, to one decimal place. **[2]**
(c) Suggest whether Talvia is more likely to be an LIC or an HIC, giving **two** reasons from the data. **[3]**

**9.** Describe **three** features you would expect to see on the population pyramid of an HIC. **[3]**

**10.** Suggest **two** reasons why the oldest age groups of an HIC pyramid contain more females than males. **[2]**

**11.** Compare the population structure of a typical LIC with that of a typical HIC. **[4]**

**12.** A government sees from its pyramid that the young dependent population is very large. Explain **three** pressures this structure may place on the country over the next 20 years. **[3]**

---

## Answers

**1.** How people are spread over an area **[1]**.

**2.** People per km² (people per square kilometre) **[1]**.

**3.** People aged 65 and over **[1]**.

**4.** Income per person, or gross national income per capita **[1]**.

**5.** Any two: very hot or very cold climate (deserts, polar areas) **[1]**; high, steep mountains **[1]**; dense forest **[1]**; poor, thin or infertile soils **[1]**; lack of reliable water. **[max 2]**

**6.** (a) Arlen: 1,800,000 ÷ 9,000 = 200 people per km² **[1]**; Bexa: 350,000 ÷ 35,000 = 10 people per km² **[1]**; Corin: 4,200,000 ÷ 3,500 = 1,200 people per km² **[1]**.
(b) Corin has the highest density, 1,200 people per km², many people living in a small area, six times Arlen's figure **[1]**.

**7.** Density is an average for the whole area **[1]**, so in one country people may be spread evenly while in the other they may be clustered in a few areas, such as along a coast or river, leaving other areas almost empty **[1]**.

**8.** (a) 20 + 19 = 39% **[1]**.
(b) Dependants = 39 + 4 = 43% and working population = 28 + 29 = 57% **[1]**; 43 ÷ 57 × 100 = 75.4 **[1]**.
(c) LIC **[1]**; any two reasons: a large young dependent population of 39% shows a high birth rate **[1]**; only 4% are aged 65 and over, suggesting a short life expectancy **[1]**; most dependants are young rather than elderly. **[max 3]**

**9.** Any three: narrow base, showing a low birth rate **[1]**; fairly straight sides, as most people survive to old age **[1]**; wide top with a large elderly population, showing long life expectancy **[1]**; more females than males in the oldest groups **[1]**; a column or rectangular shape overall. **[max 3]**

**10.** Any two: women tend to have a longer life expectancy than men **[1]**; men may have worked in more dangerous jobs or had higher rates of some diseases linked to lifestyle **[1]**; past wars killed more men than women. **[max 2]**

**11.** Any four comparative points: the LIC has a wider base than the HIC, showing a higher proportion of children **[1]**; the HIC has a wider top than the LIC, showing a higher proportion of elderly people **[1]**; the LIC's sides narrow quickly while the HIC's are fairly straight, so more people survive into old age in the HIC **[1]**; dependants in the LIC are mainly young, while in the HIC they are increasingly elderly **[1]**; both have a working population, but in the HIC it forms a larger share of the total **[1]**; the HIC has more women than men in the oldest groups, while the LIC is more even **[1]**. **[max 4]**

**12.** Any three explained: more schools and teachers are needed for the large number of children now **[1]**; when these children reach working age, many new jobs will be needed or unemployment will rise **[1]**; demand for housing, food and water will increase as they form households, and as they have children of their own the population may keep growing **[1]**; healthcare for children and mothers must expand. **[max 3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing area by population, or leaving out "per km²".
- Forgetting to add the male and female bars when a pyramid shows percentages.
- Saying "the LIC has a wide base" and "the HIC has a narrow base" in separate sentences when asked to compare.
- Calling a country "developed" or "poor" instead of using the World Bank income groups.
- Giving reasons from general knowledge when the question asks for reasons from the data.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these population questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Do I need to learn the World Bank income limits?**
No. The limits change every year. Learn the three groups the syllabus names and that they are based on income per person.

**What age groups count as dependants?**
On most pyramids, the young dependent population is 0 to 14 and the elderly dependent population is 65 and over; the working population is 15 to 64.
