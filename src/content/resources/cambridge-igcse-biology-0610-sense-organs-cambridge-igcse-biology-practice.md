## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which part of the eye carries impulses to the brain?

A: cornea

B: lens

C: optic nerve

D: retina

**[1]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by a sense organ. **[2]**

**3.** State the function of the iris. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why light falling on the blind spot is not detected. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the function of the fovea. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the function of rods. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A diagram of the eye shows structures labelled A to E. A is the clear curved front surface. B is the coloured ring of muscle. C is the gap in the centre of B. D is the transparent structure behind C. E lines the back of the eye.

**(a)** Identify A to E. **[5]**

**(b)** Name **two** structures in the list that refract light. **[2]**

**8.** A student shines a torch into a partner's eye in a dimly lit room.

**(a)** Describe the change in the pupil. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the advantage of this change. **[1]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain how the iris muscles bring about this change. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows pupil diameter at different light intensities.

| Light intensity / arbitrary units | 5 | 10 | 20 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pupil diameter / mm | 6.4 | 5.2 | 3.6 | 2.8 |

**(a)** Describe the relationship between light intensity and pupil diameter. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in pupil diameter between light intensity 5 and light intensity 40. Give your answer to the nearest whole number. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the pupil reflex is described as a reflex action. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A student is reading a book and then looks up at a clock on the far wall.

**(a)** Describe the changes in the ciliary muscles, the suspensory ligaments and the lens as she looks from the book to the clock. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why these changes are needed for a clear image of the clock. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)*

**(a)** Describe the distribution of rods and cones in the human retina. **[2]**

**(b)** Outline the function of cones. **[2]**

**(c)** A person walking at night finds it hard to tell the colour of parked cars. Explain why. **[2]**

**12.** The skin contains receptors.

**(a)** Name **two** stimuli detected by receptors in the skin. **[2]**

**(b)** The tongue contains receptors. Name the type of stimulus they detect. **[1]**

**13.** Light from a tree enters a person's eye and the person sees the tree. Describe the route of the light through the eye and how information about the image reaches the brain, naming the structures involved. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** A group of receptor cells [1] responding to a specific stimulus (such as light, sound, touch, temperature or chemicals) [1].

**3.** It controls how much light enters the pupil / the eye [1].

**4.** There are no light receptors (rods or cones) at the blind spot [1].

**5.** It gives the sharpest / most detailed (colour) image [1].

**6.** Night vision / they are more sensitive to (dim) light [1].

**7. (a)** A cornea [1]; B iris [1]; C pupil [1]; D lens [1]; E retina [1].

**(b)** Cornea [1]; lens [1].

*Examiner insight:* Many answers give only the lens. The cornea does much of the refraction and is named in the syllabus as the structure that refracts light.

**8. (a)** The pupil becomes smaller / constricts / its diameter decreases [1].

**(b)** Less light enters the eye, which protects the retina from damage [1].

**(c)** The circular muscles contract [1]; the radial muscles relax [1]; the muscles work antagonistically, so the pupil constricts [1].

**9. (a)** As light intensity increases, pupil diameter decreases [1].

**(b)** (6.4 − 2.8) ÷ 6.4 × 100 [1] = 56.25% = **56%** [1].

**(c)** It is automatic / happens without conscious thought [1] and is rapid [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide by the starting value (6.4), not the final value. Dividing by 2.8 gives a value over 100%, which should warn you that something is wrong.

**10. (a)** The ciliary muscles relax [1]; the suspensory ligaments are pulled tight / tension increases [1]; the lens becomes thinner [1].

**(b)** Light from a distant object needs to be refracted less [1], so the thinner lens focuses the light on the retina [1].

*Examiner insight:* Ligaments do not contract or relax; describe them in terms of tension.

**11. (a)** Cones are concentrated in the fovea [1]; rods are spread across the rest of the retina, more towards the edges, with none at the fovea [1].

**(b)** Colour vision [1]; there are three kinds of cone, each absorbing light of different colours [1].

**(c)** In dim light, the cones do not respond / need brighter light [1]; vision depends on rods, which do not give colour vision [1].

**12. (a)** Touch [1]; temperature [1].

**(b)** Chemicals [1].

**13.** Light passes through the cornea, which refracts it [1]; it enters through the pupil [1]; the lens focuses the light on to the retina [1]; light receptors in the retina are stimulated and impulses travel along the optic nerve to the brain [1].

*Examiner insight:* Keep the structures in order and give each its job. Saying "the eye sends the image to the brain" scores nothing: impulses, not images, travel along the optic nerve.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming only the lens as refracting light.
- Pupil reflex answers that describe the change but not the reason.
- Dividing by the wrong value in percentage-change calculations.
- (Extended) Circular and radial muscle actions swapped.
- (Extended) Saying suspensory ligaments contract or relax.
- (Extended) Saying rods give colour vision.
