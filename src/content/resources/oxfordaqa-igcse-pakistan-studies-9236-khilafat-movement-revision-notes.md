These notes condense one content point from key development 3.1.1.2 of the 9236 specification: "the
Khilafat movement and its importance for Muslim-Hindu relations", with the linked point on
"non-cooperation and the growth of communalism 1920-27". For explanation and a worked answer, read
the [Khilafat movement study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-pakistan-studies-9236-khilafat-movement/);
for the Pakistan movement as a whole, the
[History and Culture of Pakistan revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-history-and-culture-revision-notes/).

## Timeline

| Date | Event |
|---|---|
| 1914 | Ottoman Empire enters the First World War against Britain |
| 1919 | Khilafat Committee formed |
| 1920 | Delegation to London under Muhammad Ali fails; Treaty of Sèvres divides Ottoman lands |
| 1920 | Non-Cooperation launched with Gandhi; Hijrat to Afghanistan; Jinnah leaves Congress |
| 1921 | Moplah uprising in Malabar; Ali brothers imprisoned |
| Feb 1922 | Chauri Chaura; Gandhi calls off Non-Cooperation |
| 1922 | Gandhi arrested; Turkish nationalists abolish the sultanate |
| Mar 1924 | Mustafa Kemal abolishes the caliphate; movement collapses |

## Leaders and aims

- **Leaders:** Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar and Maulana Shaukat Ali (the Ali brothers), Maulana Abul
  Kalam Azad, Hakim Ajmal Khan; allied with Gandhi.
- **Aims:** keep the Ottoman Sultan as Khalifa; keep the holy places under Muslim control; stop the
  break-up of the Ottoman Empire.

## Non-Cooperation methods

- Return of British titles and honours.
- Boycott of government schools, colleges and courts.
- Boycott of council elections.
- Boycott of British goods; use of home-spun cloth.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Khalifa (caliph) | Leader of the Muslim community; title held by the Ottoman Sultan |
| Khilafat | The caliphate, the institution of the Khalifa |
| Non-Cooperation | Gandhi's campaign of peaceful refusal to work with British institutions |
| Hijrat | Migration for the sake of faith; here, to Afghanistan in 1920 |
| Dar-ul-harb | A land where Muslims cannot freely practise their faith |
| Moplah (Mappila) | Muslim community of Malabar; rose in 1921 |
| Shuddhi | Hindu reconversion campaign of the 1920s |
| Sangathan | Hindu organisation and self-defence campaign |
| Tabligh and tanzim | Muslim missionary and organising responses |
| Communalism | Political loyalty to a religious community, often hostile to others |

## Effects on Muslim-Hindu relations

| Short term (1920-21) | Longer term (1922-27) |
|---|---|
| Joint campaign against British rule | Muslims felt betrayed when Non-Cooperation was called off |
| Hindu support for a Muslim cause | Moplah violence hardened attitudes |
| Shared protests and imprisonment | Shuddhi, sangathan and Muslim responses; more riots |
| Gandhi accepted as leader by many Muslims | Many Muslims looked to their own political organisation |

**Wider importance:** brought ordinary Muslims and ulema into mass politics; gave experience of
organising; showed the limits of relying on Congress.

## Why it failed

- Gandhi called off Non-Cooperation after Chauri Chaura (1922).
- The Hijrat ended in hardship and discredited parts of the leadership.
- Leaders were imprisoned.
- Moplah violence divided supporters.
- The Turkish government itself abolished the caliphate (1924).

## Key people

| Person | Role |
|---|---|
| Muhammad Ali Jauhar | Khilafat leader; led the 1920 delegation to London; imprisoned 1921 |
| Shaukat Ali | His brother and fellow leader; imprisoned 1921 |
| Abul Kalam Azad | Religious scholar and Khilafat campaigner, later a Congress leader |
| Hakim Ajmal Khan | Physician and Khilafat leader in Delhi |
| Gandhi | Linked Khilafat to Non-Cooperation; called it off in 1922 |
| Jinnah | Opposed mass non-cooperation; left Congress in 1920 |
| Mustafa Kemal | Turkish leader who abolished the caliphate in 1924 |

## Was it a failure? Aims against effects

- **Aims:** not achieved; the caliphate ended and Ottoman lands were lost.
- **Effects:** mass political experience for Muslims; ulema drawn into politics; lessons about
  relying on Congress, later reflected in demands for written safeguards such as the Fourteen Points
  (1929).
- **Judgement tip:** separate what the movement wanted from what it caused.

## Exam traps

- Saying Britain, not Turkey, abolished the caliphate.
- Treating the Hijrat as a separate movement unrelated to Khilafat.
- Dating Chauri Chaura to 1921.
- Writing only about unity when asked about importance.
- Claiming Jinnah led the Khilafat movement; he opposed the non-cooperation method.

## Self-test

1. What was the Khalifa, and who held the title in 1919?
2. Name the two brothers who led the Khilafat movement.
3. State two aims of the movement.
4. Name two Non-Cooperation methods.
5. Why did Muslims migrate to Afghanistan in 1920?
6. What happened to the Hijrat migrants?
7. Where and when did the Moplah uprising take place?
8. What happened at Chauri Chaura, and what did Gandhi do as a result?
9. Who abolished the caliphate, and when?
10. Give one long-term effect of the movement on Muslim-Hindu relations.
11. Why did Jinnah leave Congress in 1920?
12. Name one Hindu revival movement of the 1920s.

## Answers

1. The leader of the Muslim community; the Ottoman Sultan.
2. Maulana Muhammad Ali Jauhar and Maulana Shaukat Ali.
3. Any two: keep the Sultan as Khalifa; keep the holy places under Muslim control; prevent the
   break-up of the Ottoman Empire.
4. Any two: returning titles; boycotting schools, courts or councils; boycotting British goods.
5. Some religious leaders declared British India *dar-ul-harb* and urged migration to a Muslim country.
6. Afghanistan closed the border; many died or returned destitute.
7. Malabar, in 1921.
8. A crowd set fire to a police station, killing the policemen inside (February 1922); Gandhi called
   off Non-Cooperation.
9. Mustafa Kemal's Turkish government, in March 1924.
10. Any one: Muslim distrust of Congress; more communal riots; growth of rival religious movements;
    Muslims seeking their own political organisation.
11. He opposed mass non-cooperation as a method likely to cause disorder.
12. Shuddhi or sangathan.
