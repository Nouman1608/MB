Cambridge O Level Biology (5090) is not tiered. Paper 1 is Multiple Choice (1 hour, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 2 is Theory (1 hour 45 minutes, 80 marks, 50%). The practical component is a choice: Paper 3 Practical Test (1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks, 20%) or Paper 4 Alternative to Practical (1 hour, 40 marks, 20%). Both are externally assessed. Build your plan around topics such as [cell structure and organisation](/resources/cell-structure-and-organisation/), [biological molecules](/resources/biological-molecules/) and [enzymes](/resources/enzymes/), and the later topics like [biotechnology and genetic modification](/resources/o-level-biology-5090-biotechnology-and-genetic-modification/).

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice

Paper 1 lasts one hour for 40 marks and is worth 30%. That gives 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per question, a more generous pace than many multiple choice papers. Use it: read the stem, think of the answer, then evaluate every option, because distractors are often partly true statements that do not answer the question asked.

The paper samples the whole syllabus, which spans nineteen topics from cells and classification to inheritance and the relationships of organisms with their environment. Prepare with a coverage grid rather than relying on favourite topics. Questions that include a diagram, table or graph reward careful reading of axes, units and labels, so practise extracting a single piece of information from a data item before looking at the options.

## Paper 2: Theory

Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes for 80 marks and is worth 50%, the largest share. That is 105 / 80 = 1.3125 minutes per mark. A 6-mark question therefore deserves a little under 8 minutes. Answers are expected in biological terms: use the correct vocabulary (for example, diffusion, osmosis, active transport, denaturation) and link cause to effect.

Practise three kinds of response. Recall answers state or define. Application answers use knowledge in a new context, such as a patient or an ecosystem. Data answers interpret graphs and tables. For the topics where candidates often lose marks, such as enzyme action, rehearse a complete chain of reasoning: what changes, how that affects the active site or the rate, and what the consequence is. The [enzymes revision notes](/resources/enzymes-revision-notes/) are a good place to practise this.

## Paper 3 or Paper 4: the practical component

According to the verified record, a candidate takes either Paper 3, the Practical Test (1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks, 20%), or Paper 4, the Alternative to Practical (1 hour, 40 marks, 20%). They are alternatives, so only one counts. Your school will confirm which you sit.

The skills overlap heavily: following or planning a method, recording results in a table, drawing graphs or charts, making observations, calculating and interpreting results, and evaluating the experiment. If you take the Alternative to Practical, practise from your own laboratory experience and from written descriptions of standard investigations, because you cannot perform the work in the exam. If you take the Practical Test, practise setting up and handling apparatus under time pressure and write observations as you go.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 105 / 80 = 1.3125 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes per mark.
- Paper 4: 60 / 40 = 1.5 minutes per mark.

The weightings 30% + 50% + 20% sum to 100% for the papers you sit. Theory is the dominant component, but the practical share equals two-thirds of Paper 1's weighting, so deserves proper preparation rather than being left until the final week.

## Command words and question styles

State and name need single facts. Describe asks what happens, or what a graph shows. Explain asks for a reason. Compare requires similarities or differences about both items. Suggest means applying knowledge to an unfamiliar situation. Calculate needs working and units. Where a question says use the information in the table, your answer must refer to figures from it, not only to general knowledge.

## Worked practice scenario

An investigation places cylinders of potato in a sugar solution. A cylinder has an initial mass of 3.20 g and, after 40 minutes, a final mass of 3.56 g. (a) Calculate the percentage change in mass. (b) Explain the result using osmosis.

**Model answer.**

(a) Change in mass = 3.56 - 3.20 = +0.36 g.
Percentage change = (change / initial mass) x 100 = (0.36 / 3.20) x 100 = 11.25%, so an increase of 11.3% to 3 significant figures.

(b) The mass increased, so water entered the potato cells. The solution outside was more dilute than the cell sap, meaning it had a higher water potential. Water therefore moved by osmosis, through the partially permeable cell membranes, from the region of higher water potential to lower water potential. The cells became turgid.

An important practical point is that percentage change allows fair comparison between cylinders of different starting mass, so always divide by the initial mass, not the final one.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm whether you are entered for Paper 3 or Paper 4.
- Make a coverage grid of the nineteen syllabus topics and rate yourself on each.
- Practise multiple choice at 1.5 minutes per question.
- Learn the key terms precisely and practise writing them in full sentences.
- For data questions, use figures from the table or graph in your answer.
- Show working for every calculation and include units.
- In practical work, draw tables with headings and units before recording results.
- Leave time at the end to check units, labels and any unanswered parts.

## Self-test

1. How many marks and minutes is Paper 2?
2. How many minutes per mark does Paper 3 allow?
3. Which practical papers are alternatives to each other?
4. What is the combined weighting of Papers 1 and 2?
5. In the worked scenario, why divide by the initial mass?

**Answers:** 1. 80 marks and 1 hour 45 minutes. 2. 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes per mark. 3. Paper 3 and Paper 4. 4. 30% + 50% = 80%. 5. So the change is expressed relative to the starting mass, allowing fair comparison between samples.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers does a candidate sit for O Level Biology 5090?**
Three: Paper 1 Multiple Choice, Paper 2 Theory, and one practical paper, either Paper 3 or Paper 4.

**What are the marks and weightings?**
Paper 1 is 40 marks and 30%, Paper 2 is 80 marks and 50%, and the practical component (Paper 3 or Paper 4) is 40 marks and 20%.

**What is the difference between Paper 3 and Paper 4?**
Paper 3 is a Practical Test of 1 hour 30 minutes, and Paper 4 is an Alternative to Practical of 1 hour. A candidate takes one of them.

**Is O Level Biology tiered?**
No. The syllabus is not tiered, so all candidates take the same papers.

**Which series does this syllabus cover?**
It is examined in the June and November series from 2026 to 2028, and Cambridge does not give a separate first-teaching date.
