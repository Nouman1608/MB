Condensed notes for **2.2 Traditional costing methods** (2.2.1 to 2.2.4), Cambridge AS & A Level Accounting 9706, 2026–2028 series. AS Level content. Figures are invented; amounts in dollars.

## Costing applications (2.2.1)

| Method | Use when | Cost per item |
|---|---|---|
| Unit costing | Identical output or a repeated service | Total cost ÷ units |
| Job costing | Work done to one customer's order | Direct costs of the job + overheads absorbed |
| Batch costing | Identical items made together as a group | Cost of the group ÷ items in it |

- Works in services too: a law firm's case, a garage's repair, an architect's design are jobs.
- **Mark-up** = profit as a % of cost. **Margin** = profit as a % of selling price.
- Cost 80 with 25% mark-up: price 100. Cost 80 with 25% margin: price 80 ÷ 0.75 = 106.67.

## Absorption costing (2.2.2)

**Sequence:** allocate → apportion → reapportion service centres → calculate OAR → absorb into cost units → compare absorbed with actual.

| Overhead | Typical apportionment basis |
|---|---|
| Rent, rates, heating | Floor area |
| Machine insurance, depreciation | Value of machinery |
| Canteen, personnel | Number of employees |
| Power | Machine hours or kilowatt hours |
| Stores (service centre) | Number of requisitions |
| Maintenance (service centre) | Maintenance hours |

**OAR** = budgeted overhead ÷ budgeted activity.

| Department type | Usual basis |
|---|---|
| Machine-intensive | Machine hours |
| Labour-intensive | Direct labour hours |
| One identical product | Units |

**Absorbed** = OAR × **actual** activity.

- Absorbed > actual: **over-absorbed**, deduct from cost of sales, profit rises.
- Absorbed < actual: **under-absorbed**, add to cost of sales, profit falls.
- Causes: actual activity differs from budget, actual spending differs from budget, or both.

**Cost centre** = a location, function or item of equipment for which costs are collected. **Cost unit** = the unit of output to which costs are charged.

## Marginal costing (2.2.3)

| Formula | |
|---|---|
| Contribution per unit | Selling price − variable cost per unit |
| Break-even units | Fixed costs ÷ contribution per unit (round **up**) |
| C/S ratio | Contribution ÷ sales |
| Break-even revenue | Fixed costs ÷ C/S ratio |
| Units for target profit | (Fixed costs + target profit) ÷ contribution per unit |
| Margin of safety | Budgeted sales − break-even sales (units, revenue or %) |

**Reading a break-even chart** (interpret only; you will not prepare one): fixed cost line horizontal; total cost line starts at fixed cost; revenue line starts at the origin; break-even where revenue meets total cost; the vertical gap gives profit or loss; the horizontal gap from break-even to planned output is the margin of safety.

**Profit statements:**

- Absorption: inventory at full production cost; fixed production overhead absorbed per unit; adjust for under- or over-absorption.
- Marginal: inventory at variable production cost; all fixed costs charged in the period.

**Reconciliation:** marginal profit + fixed overhead in closing inventory − fixed overhead in opening inventory = absorption profit.

| Inventory | Higher profit |
|---|---|
| Rising | Absorption |
| Falling | Marginal |
| Unchanged | Same |

## Decision rules

| Decision | Relevant comparison |
|---|---|
| Make or buy | Variable cost + avoidable fixed cost of making vs purchase price |
| Special order, spare capacity | Price vs variable cost + any extra costs |
| Special order, no spare capacity | Order contribution vs contribution from normal sales lost |
| Closure of a unit | Contribution lost vs avoidable fixed costs saved |
| Limiting factor | Rank by contribution per unit of the scarce resource |
| Target profit | (Fixed + target) ÷ contribution |

Non-financial factors to weigh: quality and reliability of an outside supplier, reaction of existing customers to a lower price, staff morale and redundancies, reputation, loss of skills, links between products (complementary sales).

## Cost–volume–profit analysis (2.2.4)

| Advantages | Limitations |
|---|---|
| Quick to calculate and easy to present | Assumes constant price and variable cost per unit |
| Shows the effect of price, cost or volume changes | Assumes fixed costs stay fixed at all outputs |
| Supports target-profit planning and risk (margin of safety) | Assumes a single product or constant sales mix |
| | Assumes sales equal production; ignores non-financial factors |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Allocate | Charge an overhead wholly to the cost centre that caused it |
| Apportion | Share an overhead between cost centres on a fair basis |
| Reapportion | Recharge service centre overheads to production centres |
| OAR | Predetermined rate for charging overheads to cost units |
| Contribution | Selling price less variable cost |
| Period cost | A cost charged in full to the period in which it is incurred |
| Margin of safety | Amount by which sales can fall before a loss is made |
| Avoidable cost | A cost that would be saved by a decision |

## Exam traps

- Using actual activity to calculate the OAR: it is set in advance from budgets.
- Using budgeted activity to calculate overhead absorbed: absorbed uses actual activity.
- Rounding break-even units down.
- Dividing fixed costs by selling price instead of contribution.
- Forgetting that a full-capacity special order displaces normal sales.
- Treating apportioned head-office costs as savings on closure.
- Ending a decision question without a recommendation or non-financial factors.

## Self-test

**1.** Define a cost unit.
**2.** Which basis suits apportioning rent?
**3.** Budgeted overhead 72,000; budgeted labour hours 9,000. Find the OAR.
**4.** Using Question 3, actual hours were 9,400 and actual overhead 74,000. Is overhead under- or over-absorbed, and by how much?
**5.** Price 45, variable cost 27, fixed costs 54,000. Find the break-even point in units.
**6.** Using Question 5, find the C/S ratio.
**7.** Using Question 5, how many units give a profit of 18,000?
**8.** Budgeted sales are 4,000 units and break-even is 3,000 units. Give the margin of safety as a percentage.
**9.** Inventory rose by 500 units; fixed overhead is 6 a unit. Which method shows higher profit, and by how much?
**10.** A cost of 60 is priced with a 20% margin. What is the price?
**11.** A unit shows a loss after apportioned costs but has positive contribution and no avoidable fixed costs. Should it close on financial grounds?
**12.** Name two non-financial factors in a make-or-buy decision.

### Answers

**1.** The unit of product or service to which costs are charged.
**2.** Floor area.
**3.** 72,000 ÷ 9,000 = 8.00 per labour hour.
**4.** Absorbed 9,400 × 8.00 = 75,200, more than 74,000: over-absorbed by 1,200.
**5.** Contribution 18; 54,000 ÷ 18 = 3,000 units.
**6.** 18 ÷ 45 = 40%.
**7.** (54,000 + 18,000) ÷ 18 = 4,000 units.
**8.** 1,000 ÷ 4,000 = 25%.
**9.** Absorption, by 500 × 6 = 3,000.
**10.** 60 ÷ 0.80 = 75.
**11.** No: closing loses its contribution and saves nothing.
**12.** Any two: supplier reliability, quality control, loss of in-house skills, effect on staff, confidentiality of designs.
