## What the specification lists

- Explain what a statement of financial position is and what it contains.
- Complete, interpret and analyse one (non-current assets, inventory, receivables, payables, working capital, non-current liabilities, net assets, retained earnings, total shareholders' equity).
- Evaluate how assets may be valued: non-current assets, investments, patents, goodwill, receivables, bad debts, inventories.
- Depreciation: meaning, need for the provision, straight line and reducing balance, impact.
- Evaluate the usefulness of the statement.

## Core recall

### What it shows

- A **snapshot** of what the business owns, owes and is worth on one date.
- **Net assets = equity**: assets − liabilities = shareholders' equity.

### Layout

| Section | Items |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Property, machinery, vehicles, patents, goodwill, long-term investments |
| Current assets | Inventory, receivables, cash |
| Current liabilities | Payables, overdraft, tax due (within a year) |
| Working capital | Current assets − current liabilities |
| Total assets less current liabilities | Non-current assets + working capital |
| Non-current liabilities | Long-term loans, debentures |
| Net assets | Total assets less current liabilities − non-current liabilities |
| Equity | Share capital + retained earnings |

Check: net assets must equal total shareholders' equity.

### Valuing assets

| Asset | Basis | Issue |
|---|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Cost less accumulated depreciation | May differ from market value |
| Investments | Cost or market value | Market value changes |
| Patents | Cost written off over legal life | Hard to value |
| Goodwill | Price paid − fair value of net assets (on purchase) | Intangible; can be overstated |
| Receivables | Amount owed less provision for doubtful debts | Estimate; bad debts written off |
| Inventories | Lower of cost and net realisable value | Obsolete stock written down |

### Depreciation

- Spreads the cost of a non-current asset over its useful life (accruals).
- Avoids overstating profit and assets.
- Does not involve cash leaving the business.

| Method | Formula | Pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Straight line | (Cost − residual value) ÷ life | Equal charge each year |
| Reducing balance | Rate × net book value at start of year | Higher charge early, lower later |

Net book value (NBV) = cost − accumulated depreciation. Use one method consistently.

### Impact of depreciation

- Lowers profit and net assets; no effect on cash.
- Higher early charge (reducing balance) lowers early profit and ROCE.
- Managers could change method or life to flatter profit; consistency prevents this.

### Usefulness

- Strengths: shows financial strength, funding, liquidity and gearing; comparison over time.
- Limits: snapshot (timing can be manipulated); assets at cost not market value; intangibles hard to value; omits skills and brand; says nothing about the future.

## Quick worked reminder

Alder Engineering plc (invented): non-current assets £450,000 (cost £640,000 less £190,000); current assets £215,000; current liabilities £90,000; working capital £125,000; total assets less current liabilities £575,000; loan £180,000; net assets £395,000 = share capital £150,000 + retained earnings £245,000. A £90,000 machine with £10,000 residual and five years: straight line £16,000 a year; reducing balance at 30%: £27,000, £18,900, £13,230 (NBV £63,000, £44,100, £30,870).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Non-current asset | An asset kept for more than a year |
| Current asset | An asset expected to become cash within a year |
| Net assets | Assets less liabilities |
| Retained earnings | Profits kept in the business |
| Equity | The owners' stake: share capital plus retained earnings |
| Net book value | Cost less accumulated depreciation |
| Residual value | Expected value of an asset at the end of its life |
| Goodwill | The excess of the price paid for a business over the fair value of its net assets |
| Bad debt | A debt that will not be paid |
| Provision | An amount set aside for an expected loss |

## Exam traps

- Net assets must equal equity; use retained earnings as the balancing figure if told.
- Reducing balance uses NBV, not cost.
- Depreciation does not reduce cash.
- Goodwill arises only on purchase of a business.
- A write-down of stock reduces profit and net assets.

## Self-test

1. What does a statement of financial position show?
2. Write the formula for working capital.
3. Non-current assets £300,000; current assets £100,000; current liabilities £50,000; non-current liabilities £100,000. Calculate net assets.
4. Share capital is £120,000. Calculate retained earnings.
5. Distinguish between a non-current and a current asset.
6. A van costs £36,000, residual value £6,000, life 5 years. Calculate straight line depreciation.
7. Calculate year 1 reducing balance depreciation at 25%.
8. Calculate the NBV after year 1 (reducing balance).
9. Buying a firm for £150,000 when its net assets are worth £118,000 creates what, and how much?
10. Why is stock shown at the lower of cost and NRV?
11. Does depreciation reduce cash? Explain.
12. Give one limitation of the statement of financial position.

### Answers

1. What the business owns (assets), owes (liabilities) and is worth (equity) on a particular date.
2. Working capital = current assets − current liabilities.
3. £300,000 + (£100,000 − £50,000) − £100,000 = **£250,000**.
4. Net assets equal equity, so retained earnings = £250,000 − £120,000 = **£130,000**.
5. A non-current asset is kept for more than a year; a current asset is expected to become cash within a year.
6. (£36,000 − £6,000) ÷ 5 = **£6,000** a year.
7. 25% × £36,000 = **£9,000**.
8. £36,000 − £9,000 = **£27,000**.
9. Goodwill of £150,000 − £118,000 = **£32,000**.
10. Prudence: stock must not be valued above what it can be sold for.
11. No. It is an accounting charge; the cash was spent when the asset was bought.
12. Any one: it is a snapshot; assets at cost not market value; intangibles hard to value; it omits skills and brand.
