## Questions

**1.** A discrete variable X has the distribution below. Find k, then find P(X ≥ 2).

| x | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| p(x) | 0.10 | 0.30 | k | 0.35 |

**[3]**

**2.** The variable X takes the values 0, 1, 2, 3 with p(x) = k(x + 1).

**(a)** Find k. **[2]**
**(b)** Find E(X). **[2]**

**3.** A drawer holds 4 black socks and 6 white socks. Two are taken at random without replacement. X is the number of black socks taken.

**(a)** Form the probability distribution of X. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**

**4.** A survey of 60 households records the number of cars each owns.

| Cars | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Households | 9 | 27 | 18 | 6 |

**(a)** Use the data to estimate the probability distribution of the number of cars, X. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what E(X) tells you, given that no household owns 1.35 cars. **[1]**

**5.** A variable X takes the values 1, 2, 3, 4 with p(1) = 0.15, p(2) = a, p(3) = b and p(4) = 0.25. Given that E(X) = 2.7, find a and b. **[4]**

**6.** The variable X takes the values 1, 2, 3 with probabilities 0.4, 0.35 and 0.25.

**(a)** Find E(X). **[2]**
**(b)** Find E(X²). **[2]**
**(c)** Find E(3X − 1). **[2]**

**7.** A game costs Rs 3 to play. A fair spinner is numbered 1 to 5. A score of 5 pays Rs 10, a score of 4 pays Rs 4, and any other score pays nothing.

**(a)** Write down the distribution of the net gain. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the expected net gain per play. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the expected total net gain over 150 plays. **[1]**
**(d)** Is the game fair? Give a reason. **[1]**

**8.** A raffle sells 500 tickets. There is one prize of Rs 200, four prizes of Rs 50 and ten prizes of Rs 10.

**(a)** Find the ticket price that makes the raffle fair. **[3]**
**(b)** The organiser sells every ticket at Rs 1.50. Find the organiser's expected profit. **[2]**

**9.** To play a game you pay Rs 4 and take one card at random from 4 cards. One card wins Rs w, and the other three win nothing. Find w for the game to be fair. **[4]**

**10.** A fair coin is tossed up to three times. Tossing stops as soon as a head appears, or after the third toss. X is the number of tosses made.

**(a)** Find the distribution of X. **[2]**
**(b)** Find E(X). **[2]**
**(c)** A player pays Rs 2 and receives Rs 1 if the game stops on toss 1, Rs 3 if it stops on toss 2, and Rs 8 if it stops on toss 3. Find the expected net gain. **[2]**
**(d)** The organiser changes only the Rs 8 prize so that the game is fair. Find the new prize. **[2]**

**11.** Two fair dice are rolled. Y is the positive difference between the two scores (0 if they match).

**(a)** Show that the distribution of Y is as follows. **[3]**

| y | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p(y) | 6/36 | 10/36 | 8/36 | 6/36 | 4/36 | 2/36 |

**(b)** Calculate E(Y). **[2]**
**(c)** A player pays Rs 2 to roll and receives Rs y. Find the expected net gain per play. **[1]**
**(d)** Find the expected total net gain over 360 plays and say who benefits. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Probabilities sum to 1: k = 1 − (0.10 + 0.30 + 0.35) [1] = 0.25 [1]. P(X ≥ 2) = 0.25 + 0.35 = **0.6** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the sum equal to 1 before the value of k; a bare k with no equation gives no way to see where a slip happened.

**2. (a)** The probabilities are k, 2k, 3k, 4k, so 10k = 1 [1], giving **k = 1/10** [1].
**(b)** E(X) = 0 + 1 × 0.2 + 2 × 0.3 + 3 × 0.4 [1] = 0.2 + 0.6 + 1.2 = **2** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing each product x p(x) separately shows the method, so one slip does not hide the rest of your working.

**3. (a)** p(0) = 6/10 × 5/9 = 1/3 [1]. p(1) = 2 × (4/10 × 6/9) = 8/15 [1]. p(2) = 4/10 × 3/9 = 2/15 [1].
**(b)** E(X) = 8/15 + 2 × 2/15 [1] = 12/15 = **4/5** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A value reachable in two orders needs both branches added; a total of 1 in your table is the quickest check that you did this.

**4. (a)** Divide by 60: p(0) = 0.15, p(1) = 0.45 [1], p(2) = 0.30, p(3) = 0.10 [1].
**(b)** E(X) = 0.45 + 0.60 + 0.30 [1] = **1.35** [1]
**(c)** In the long run the average number of cars per household is 1.35; it need not be a possible value [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* An interpretation needs the context (cars per household) and the idea of an average, not a statement that a household owns 1.35 cars.

**5.** Total: 0.15 + a + b + 0.25 = 1, so a + b = 0.6 [1]. Expectation: 0.15 + 2a + 3b + 1.0 = 2.7, so 2a + 3b = 1.55 [1]. Substitute a = 0.6 − b: 1.2 + b = 1.55 [1]. **b = 0.35, a = 0.25** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use both conditions: the total of 1 and the given expectation; using only one leaves two unknowns and cannot succeed.

**6. (a)** E(X) = 0.4 + 0.70 + 0.75 [1] = **1.85** [1]
**(b)** E(X²) = 1 × 0.4 + 4 × 0.35 + 9 × 0.25 [1] = **4.05** [1]
**(c)** E(3X − 1) = 3 × 1.85 − 1 [1] = **4.55** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Squaring 1.85 gives 3.4225, not 4.05; state clearly that you are squaring each value first.

**7. (a)** Net gain 7 with probability 1/5, net gain 1 with probability 1/5, net gain −3 with probability 3/5 [1] [1]
**(b)** E = (7 + 1 − 9)/5 = **−0.2**, a loss of Rs 0.20 per play [1]
**(c)** 150 × (−0.2) = **−Rs 30**, an expected loss of Rs 30 [1]
**(d)** **No**, the expected net gain is not zero [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the sign and the unit, and say whether it is a gain or a loss for the player; a bare negative number is easy to misread.

**8. (a)** Total prizes = 200 + 4 × 50 + 10 × 10 [1] = 500 [1]. Expected prize per ticket = 500/500, so the fair price is **Rs 1** [1].
**(b)** Expected profit per ticket = 1.50 − 1 = Rs 0.50 [1]. For 500 tickets: **Rs 250** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain that a fair price equals the expected prize per ticket; the figure alone does not show you understand why.

**9.** Net gain is w − 4 with probability 1/4 and −4 with probability 3/4 [1]. Expected net gain = (w − 4)/4 − 12/4 [1]. Fair, so (w − 4)/4 − 3 = 0 [1]. **w = 16** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Set the expectation equal to zero explicitly; the answer 16 then follows by checking that 16 × 1/4 equals the stake.

**10. (a)** p(1) = 1/2, p(2) = 1/4 [1], p(3) = 1/4 (two tails, then stop whatever the third toss shows) [1].
**(b)** E(X) = 1/2 + 2/4 + 3/4 [1] = **7/4 = 1.75** [1]
**(c)** E(received) = 1 × 1/2 + 3 × 1/4 + 8 × 1/4 = 3.25 [1]; net gain = 3.25 − 2 = **Rs 1.25 in the player's favour** [1]
**(d)** Let the prize be w: 1/2 + 3/4 + w/4 = 2 [1], so **w = 3**, a prize of Rs 3 [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* The stopping rule means the probability of 3 tosses is 1/4, not 1/8; check the three probabilities add to 1 before going on.

**11. (a)** There are 36 equally likely outcomes [1]. Count those with each difference: 0 occurs 6 ways, 1 occurs 10 ways, 2 occurs 8 ways, 3 occurs 6 ways, 4 occurs 4 ways, 5 occurs 2 ways [1]. The counts total 36, so each probability is the count over 36 [1].
**(b)** E(Y) = (0 + 10 + 16 + 18 + 16 + 10)/36 [1] = 70/36 = **35/18 ≈ 1.94** [1]
**(c)** Expected net gain = 35/18 − 2 = **−1/18 ≈ −Rs 0.06** per play [1]
**(d)** 360 × (−1/18) = **−Rs 20**, an expected loss of Rs 20 for the player, so the organiser benefits [1] [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs the counting displayed, for example a grid of differences; stating the final table alone gains little.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Not checking that the probabilities total 1.
* Missing the second order when a value can occur in two ways.
* Using the with-replacement fractions when the question says without replacement.
* Dividing Σ x p(x) by the number of values.
* Writing E(X²) as the square of E(X).
* Treating the prize as the net gain instead of subtracting the stake.
* Writing an expected loss with no sign, no unit or no statement of who loses.
* Calling a game fair or unfair with no calculation of the expectation.
* Reading an expectation as the result of the next single play.
