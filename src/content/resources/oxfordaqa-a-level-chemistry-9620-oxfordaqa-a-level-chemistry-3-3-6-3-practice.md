This set practises **3.3.6.3 Infrared spectroscopy** from section 3.3.6 Organic analysis of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 2 and in the synoptic Unit 5. Revise first with the [infrared spectroscopy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-3/) or the [infrared spectroscopy revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-6-3-revision-notes/).

Spectra are described in words. Use these ranges (cm⁻¹): O–H alcohols 3230–3550; N–H 3300–3500; C–H 2850–3300; O–H acids 2500–3000; C≡N 2220–2260; C=O 1680–1750; C=C 1620–1680; C–O 1000–1300.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A spectrum has a sharp absorption at 2240 cm⁻¹. Which bond causes it?

A: C=O

B: C≡N

C: O–H

D: C=C **[1]**

**2.** State what is plotted on each axis of an infrared spectrum. **[2]**

**3.** Explain how the infrared spectra of butan-1-ol and butanoic acid differ. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by the fingerprint region and how it is used. **[2]**

**5.** Name the bond in methane that absorbs infrared radiation. **[1]**

**6.** Why is the absorption at 2850–3300 cm⁻¹ rarely useful for identifying a functional group? **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** Compound F has the formula C₃H₆O. Its infrared spectrum shows a strong absorption at 1715 cm⁻¹ and C–H absorptions just below 3000 cm⁻¹. There is no absorption between 3230 and 3550 cm⁻¹ or between 1620 and 1680 cm⁻¹. F gives no silver mirror with Tollens' reagent. Identify F, explaining each piece of evidence. **[4]**

**8.** A student tries to make butanal by oxidising butan-1-ol. The infrared spectrum of the product shows a strong absorption at 1725 cm⁻¹ and a very broad absorption across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹. There is no separate broad absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the impurity and explain how the spectrum shows it. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest how the method should be changed to avoid this impurity. **[1]**

**9.** 2-Methylbutan-2-ol is heated with concentrated phosphoric acid and the product is distilled. The spectrum of the distillate shows a medium absorption at 1665 cm⁻¹ and no absorption between 3230 and 3550 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Explain what the spectrum shows about the product. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the spectrum shows about the purity of the product. **[1]**

**10.** Explain how the absorption of infrared radiation by carbon dioxide, methane and water vapour is linked to global warming. **[4]**

**11.** Compound G has the molecular formula C₄H₈O₂. Its spectrum has a very broad absorption across 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and a strong absorption at 1710 cm⁻¹.

**(a)** Identify the functional group in G. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the names of the two possible structures of G with this functional group. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how infrared spectroscopy could be used to decide which structure G has. **[1]**

**12.** A bottle is labelled "pentan-3-one", but a technician suspects it is pentan-2-one. Explain why the C=O absorption cannot settle this, and how the infrared spectrum can. **[2]**

**13.** 1-Bromobutane is heated under reflux with potassium cyanide. State **one** absorption that would appear in the product's spectrum, and explain what it shows. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2.** Vertical axis: transmittance (%) [1]; horizontal axis: wavenumber (cm⁻¹), decreasing from left to right [1]

**3.** Butan-1-ol: broad O–H absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹ and no C=O [1]; butanoic acid: very broad O–H absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ and a C=O absorption at 1680–1750 cm⁻¹ [1]

**4.** The region below about 1500 cm⁻¹, with a complex pattern of absorptions unique to each compound [1]; it is compared with reference spectra to identify the compound [1]

**5.** C–H [1]

**6.** Almost all organic compounds contain C–H bonds, so the band is nearly always present [1]

**7.** 1715 cm⁻¹ is within 1680–1750, so F contains C=O [1]; no absorption at 3230–3550 shows no alcohol O–H, and none at 1620–1680 shows no C=C [1]; so F is an aldehyde or a ketone, and no silver mirror shows it is not an aldehyde [1]; F is **propanone**, CH₃COCH₃ [1] **[4]**

**8. (a)** The impurity is **butanoic acid** [1]; the very broad absorption at 2500–3000 cm⁻¹ is acid O–H, which butanal cannot produce [1]

**(b)** Use the alcohol in excess and distil off the butanal as it forms, so it is not oxidised further [1]

**9. (a)** 1665 cm⁻¹ is within 1620–1680, so the product contains C=C [1]; an alkene has formed (2-methylbut-1-ene and/or 2-methylbut-2-ene) [1]

**(b)** No absorption at 3230–3550 cm⁻¹, so no significant amount of unreacted alcohol (or water) is detected [1]

**10.** The Earth's surface, warmed by the Sun, emits infrared radiation [1]; the C=O bonds in CO₂, C–H bonds in CH₄ and O–H bonds in H₂O absorb some of this infrared [1]; the energy is re-emitted in all directions, so some returns towards the Earth instead of escaping to space [1]; as concentrations of these gases increase, more infrared is absorbed and re-emitted, contributing to global warming [1] **[4]**

**11. (a)** Carboxylic acid [1]

**(b)** **Butanoic acid** [1]; **2-methylpropanoic acid** [1]

**(c)** Compare the fingerprint region of G with reference spectra of the two acids; the one that matches identifies G [1]

**12.** Both are ketones with the same bonds, so both show C=O in the same range [1]; compare the fingerprint region (below about 1500 cm⁻¹) with reference spectra of both ketones to see which matches [1]

**13.** A sharp absorption at 2220–2260 cm⁻¹ [1]; it shows a C≡N bond, so the nitrile (pentanenitrile) has formed; 1-bromobutane has no C≡N [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting a bond without the wavenumber evidence.
- Mixing up the alcohol and acid O–H ranges.
- Ignoring the absence of bands, which is often the deciding evidence.
- Using C=O alone to choose between an aldehyde and a ketone.
- Global warming answers that do not mention bonds absorbing infrared.
