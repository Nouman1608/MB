These questions cover **2.10 Graphs of functions** and **2.11 Sketching curves** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027 (June and November series, and March in India). Questions 1–5 use Core content (C2.10, C2.11), examined on Paper 1 (non-calculator) and Paper 3 (calculator). Questions 6–11 are marked **(Extended)**: they use E2.10 and E2.11 content examined on Paper 2 (non-calculator) and Paper 4 (calculator). Where a question asks you to draw a graph, use graph paper; readings from a graph are accepted within a small tolerance.

They differ from the [Algebra and graphs practice questions](/resources/igcse-mathematics-algebra-and-graphs-practice/) and the [Extended algebra practice](/resources/igcse-mathematics-algebra-extended-practice/). For teaching and worked examples, read the [graphs of functions and sketching curves study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-graphs-of-functions-and-sketching-curves/) first.

## Questions

**1.** (non-calculator) The table is for y = x² + x − 6.

```
x   -4   -3   -2   -1    0    1    2    3
y    6    ..   -4   ..   -6   ..    0   ..
```

**(a)** Complete the table (each .. is a missing value). **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the roots of x² + x − 6 = 0. **[1]**
**(c)** Write down the equation of the line of symmetry of the graph. **[1]**

**2.** (calculator) The table is for y = 8/x.

```
x   -4   -2   -1   -0.5   0.5   1    2    4
y   -2   ..   -8   ..     16    ..   4    ..
```

**(a)** Complete the table. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the table has no value for x = 0. **[1]**
**(c)** The graph of y = 8/x is drawn. Write down the equation of the line you would draw to solve 8/x = 2x, and give the solutions. **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator) Sketch the graph of y = 12 + x − x². Show the coordinates of the points where it crosses the axes and the line of symmetry. **[4]**

**4.** (non-calculator) Match each equation to one description.

Equations: A y = 4 − 2x, B y = 6 − x², C y = x² − 3x, D y = 7/x.

Descriptions: P a U-shaped curve through the origin; Q two separate branches in the first and third quadrants; R a straight line with negative gradient; S an n-shaped curve crossing the y-axis at (0, 6). **[3]**

**5.** (calculator) The graph of y = x² − 3x − 2 has been drawn for −1 ≤ x ≤ 5.

**(a)** Find the equation of the straight line you need to draw on the same grid to solve x² − 4x + 1 = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the line and give the solutions to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended, non-calculator) Sketch y = 3/x − 2. Show both asymptotes and the coordinates of any point where the curve crosses an axis. **[4]**

**7.** (Extended, non-calculator)

**(a)** Write x² − 6x + 11 in the form (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the turning point of y = x² − 6x + 11 and say whether it is a maximum or a minimum. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how your answer to (a) shows that x² − 6x + 11 = 0 has no real roots. **[1]**
**(d)** Sketch y = x² − 6x + 11, showing the y-intercept and the turning point. **[2]**

**8.** (Extended, calculator) The mass of a medicine in a patient's blood, M mg, t hours after a dose, is modelled by M = 200 × 0.8ᵗ.

**(a)** Complete the table, giving values to the nearest whole number.

```
t   0     1     2     3     4     5     6
M   200   160   ..    102   ..    ..    52
```
**[2]**
**(b)** Draw the graph for 0 ≤ t ≤ 6 and use it to estimate when M = 100. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the model shows growth or decay, and describe what happens to M as t keeps increasing. **[2]**

**9.** (Extended, calculator) The table is for y = x³ − 4x + 2.

```
x   -3   -2   -1    0    1    2    3
y   ..    2    ..   2    ..   2    ..
```

**(a)** Complete the table. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the graph for −3 ≤ x ≤ 3. State how many roots x³ − 4x + 2 = 0 has, and estimate each one to 1 decimal place. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the straight line you would draw to solve x³ − 5x + 1 = 0, and state how many solutions this equation has for −3 ≤ x ≤ 3. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended, non-calculator)

**(a)** Factorise x³ − x² − 6x completely. Hence sketch y = x³ − x² − 6x, labelling the points where it crosses the axes. **[4]**
**(b)** Sketch y = −2x³ + 16, labelling the points where it crosses the axes. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended, calculator) The graph of y = x² + 4/x is to be drawn for 0.5 ≤ x ≤ 3.

```
x   0.5    1    1.5    2    2.5    3
y   8.25   ..   4.92   ..   7.85   10.33
```

**(a)** Complete the table. **[2]**
**(b)** Draw the graph and use it to solve x² + 4/x = 6. **[2]**
**(c)** Write down the equation of the vertical asymptote of y = x² + 4/x. **[1]**
**(d)** Use your graph to explain why x² + 4/x = 4 has no solution for x > 0. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** x = −3: 9 − 3 − 6 = 0; x = −1: 1 − 1 − 6 = −6 [1]. x = 1: 1 + 1 − 6 = −4; x = 3: 9 + 3 − 6 = 6 [1]. Row: **6, 0, −4, −6, −6, −4, 0, 6**.
**(b)** Where y = 0: **x = −3 and x = 2** [1].
**(c)** Halfway between the roots: **x = −0.5** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (a), (−3)² must be worked as +9; a value that breaks the table's symmetry usually loses the accuracy mark and then the plotting marks that follow from it.

**2. (a)** Missing values: x = −2: **−4**; x = −0.5: **−16**; x = 1: **8**; x = 4: **2**. [1] for any two correct, [1] for all four correct.
**(b)** 8 ÷ 0 cannot be calculated, so y = 8/x has no value at x = 0 [1].
**(c)** Draw **y = 2x** [1]. The line meets both branches: **x = −2 and x = 2** [1] (check: 8/2 = 4 = 2 × 2).
*Examiner insight:* when asked for the line, an equation such as y = 2x is needed; writing "the line 2x" or giving only the solutions does not earn the method mark.

**3.** y = −(x² − x − 12) = −(x − 4)(x + 3) [1]. Roots **(−3, 0) and (4, 0)** [1]. The x² coefficient is negative, so an **n-shape** through **(0, 12)** [1]. Symmetrical about **x = 0.5** [1].
*Examiner insight:* sketch marks are for the shape and the labelled intercepts; an n-shape drawn as a U loses the shape mark even if every intercept is correct.

**4.** **A → R** and **D → Q** [1]. **B → S** [1]. **C → P** [1] (x² − 3x = x(x − 3) passes through the origin).
*Examiner insight:* each mark depends on one feature (sign of x², constant term, a/x form); check that feature rather than guessing.

**5. (a)** x² − 4x + 1 = 0 becomes x² − 3x − 2 = x − 3 (add x − 3 to both sides) [1]. Draw **y = x − 3** [1].
**(b)** From an accurate graph, **x ≈ 0.3 and x ≈ 3.7** [1] [1] (exact values 2 ± √3 = 0.268 and 3.73).
*Examiner insight:* check your line by subtracting it from the curve's equation; if the result is not the equation you were given, the method mark is lost and both solutions with it.

**6.** Vertical asymptote **x = 0** [1]. Horizontal asymptote **y = −2** [1]. Root: 3/x = 2, so **x = 1.5**, giving **(1.5, 0)** [1]. Two branches, top-right and bottom-left of the point (0, −2), each approaching both asymptotes without touching them [1].
*Examiner insight:* asymptotes should be drawn as dashed lines and labelled with their equations; a curve that crosses y = −2 loses the shape mark.

**7. (a)** x² − 6x + 11 = (x − 3)² − 9 + 11 [1] = **(x − 3)² + 2** [1].
**(b)** **(3, 2), a minimum** [1].
**(c)** (x − 3)² ≥ 0, so (x − 3)² + 2 ≥ 2 and can never equal 0 [1].
**(d)** U-shape crossing the y-axis at **(0, 11)** [1], lowest point at (3, 2) and entirely above the x-axis [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the sign of p flips: (x − 3)² gives x = 3, and a turning point written as (−3, 2) earns no credit.

**8. (a)** 200 × 0.8² = **128**, 200 × 0.8⁴ = 81.92 = **82**, 200 × 0.8⁵ = 65.536 = **66** [1] for two correct, [1] for all three.
**(b)** Smooth decreasing curve through the plotted points [1]. Read across from M = 100: **t ≈ 3.1 hours** [1] (accept 3.0 to 3.2).
**(c)** **Decay**, because the multiplier 0.8 is less than 1 [1]. M keeps decreasing towards 0 but never reaches it; the curve gets closer and closer to the t-axis [1].
*Examiner insight:* a reading from a graph is credited within a tolerance; show your reading line on the graph so the method can be seen if your value is slightly outside it.

**9. (a)** x = −3: −27 + 12 + 2 = −13; x = −1: −1 + 4 + 2 = 5 [1]. x = 1: 1 − 4 + 2 = −1; x = 3: 27 − 12 + 2 = 17 [1]. Row: **−13, 2, 5, 2, −1, 2, 17**.
**(b)** The curve crosses the x-axis **three** times [1]. Roots **x ≈ −2.2** [1], **x ≈ 0.5 and x ≈ 1.7** [1] (accurate values −2.21, 0.539, 1.68).
**(c)** x³ − 5x + 1 = 0 becomes x³ − 4x + 2 = x + 1 [1]. Line **y = x + 1** [1]. It meets the curve **three** times, so **3 solutions** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the sign changes in the table (−13 to 2, 2 to −1, −1 to 2) tell you where the three roots are before you draw; a root missing from one of those intervals shows a plotting error.

**10. (a)** x(x² − x − 6) [1] = **x(x − 3)(x + 2)** [1]. Roots **(−2, 0), (0, 0), (3, 0)**, and the curve crosses the y-axis at the origin [1]. Positive x³ term: rises from bottom left, up through −2, turns, down through 0, turns, up through 3 [1].
**(b)** y-intercept **(0, 16)** [1]. Root: 2x³ = 16, x³ = 8, **x = 2**, giving **(2, 0)** [1]. Negative x³ term: the curve falls from top left to bottom right with the cubic's flattened S-shape, flattest where it crosses the y-axis [1].
*Examiner insight:* "factorise completely" needs all three factors including x; stopping at x(x² − x − 6) earns only the first mark.

**11. (a)** x = 1: 1 + 4 = **5** [1]. x = 2: 4 + 2 = **6** [1].
**(b)** Draw y = 6 [1]. It meets the curve at **x ≈ 0.7 and x = 2** [1] (exact values −1 + √3 = 0.732 and 2).
**(c)** **x = 0** [1].
**(d)** The lowest point of the curve for x > 0 is about y = 4.8 (near x = 1.3), so the line y = 4 does not meet the curve [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (d) the explanation must refer to the graph (the curve stays above y = 4); solving algebraically or saying "it doesn't work" earns nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Squaring negatives wrongly in tables, so one plotted point is out of line with the rest.
- Ruled segments between plotted points on a curve instead of one smooth curve.
- Joining the two branches of a reciprocal graph through x = 0.
- Choosing the wrong line for a graphical solution because the given equation was not rearranged to "curve = line".
- Giving a y-value when the question asks for the x-value of an intersection.
- Unlabelled intercepts or asymptotes on a sketch.
- (Extended) Sign error in the turning point from completing the square.
- (Extended) An exponential or reciprocal curve drawn crossing its asymptote.
