specification (for teaching from September 2016). Paper 2 content for both tiers; nothing is Higher tier only.
For explanations use the
[life cycle assessment and recycling study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-life-cycle-assessment-and-recycling/);
to test yourself use the
[practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-life-cycle-assessment-and-recycling-practice/).

## Core facts

- An **LCA** assesses the **environmental impact** of a product at each stage of its life.
- Four stages: **raw materials**, **manufacture and packaging**, **use and operation**, **disposal**
  (including **transport and distribution** at each stage).
- Easy to quantify: **water, resources, energy sources, some wastes**.
- Hard to quantify: **pollutant effects**, which need **value judgements**, so an LCA is **not purely
  objective**.
- **Selective or abbreviated LCAs** can be **misused**, for example in **advertising**.

## LCA stages

| Stage | Examples of impact |
|---|---|
| Extracting and processing raw materials | Mining, quarrying, energy, water, habitat loss |
| Manufacturing and packaging | Energy, waste, pollution, packaging materials |
| Use and operation | Energy or water used during life, number of uses |
| Disposal | Landfill, incineration, recycling, how long it lasts in the environment |
| Transport (at every stage) | Fuel used, emissions |

## Comparing shopping bags (method)

1. **Total the data** in each column.
2. Divide by **number of uses** to get a fair value per use.
3. Comment on **disposal** and what **cannot be easily measured**.
4. Give a **conclusion** using the data.

Example: plastic 1.7 MJ per bag and paper 2.8 MJ per bag. If plastic is used 3 times and paper once: plastic
1.7 ÷ 3 = **0.57 MJ per use**, paper **2.8 MJ per use**.

## Asking questions about an LCA claim

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Which stages are included? | Missing stages may hide a weakness |
| Are numbers shown? | Claims without data are weak |
| Who produced it? | An advertiser may be biased |
| Is it comparative? | Fair comparison needs the same method for both |

## Reducing the use of resources

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Reduce** use | Less raw material, energy and waste |
| **Reuse** | Same item used again (glass bottles washed and refilled) |
| **Recycle** | Material remade into new products |

- Quarrying and mining cause **environmental impacts**.
- Metals, glass, building materials, clay ceramics and most plastics come from **limited raw materials**.
- Much of the energy for processes comes from **limited resources**.

## Recycling examples

| Material | Method |
|---|---|
| Glass bottles | Reused, or **crushed and melted** to make different glass products |
| Metals | **Melted and recast** or reformed into different products |
| Steel | Scrap steel added to iron from the **blast furnace**, reducing the iron extracted from ore |

The amount of **separation** needed depends on the material and the properties required of the final product.

Energy saving: (200 − 10) ÷ 200 × 100 = **95%** (illustrative metal needing 200 MJ/kg from ore and 10 MJ/kg from
scrap).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Life cycle assessment | Assessment of environmental impact at every stage of a product's life |
| Finite resource | Resource that will run out |
| Quarrying | Digging rock from the ground |
| Reuse | Using an item again without remaking |
| Recycle | Reprocessing a material into new products |
| Landfill | Burying waste in the ground |
| Value judgement | A decision based on opinion about what matters most |

## Exam traps

- **Only manufacture counted.** Include all four stages.
- **LCAs are objective.** Not for pollutant effects.
- **Reuse = recycle.** They differ.
- **Recycling uses no energy.** It uses less, not none.
- **Ignoring uses.** Compare per use.
- **Advertising claim accepted.** Question which stages were left out.
- **No data in a "compare" answer.** Quote figures.

## Quick self-test

1. List the four stages of an LCA.
2. Which environmental impacts of an LCA can be easily quantified?
3. Why is an LCA not purely objective?
4. How can an LCA be misused?
5. Define reuse and give an example.
6. Define recycling and give an example for glass.
7. Describe how scrap steel is recycled in iron making.
8. Name two environmental impacts of quarrying.
9. A bag has total energy 3.0 MJ and is used 5 times. Calculate the energy per use.
10. A metal needs 160 MJ/kg from ore and 8 MJ/kg from scrap. Calculate the percentage saved by recycling.
11. Explain why separation matters in recycling.
12. Suggest why a company might publish an abbreviated LCA.

### Answers

1. Extracting and processing raw materials; manufacturing and packaging; use and operation; disposal.
2. Use of water, resources and energy, and production of some wastes.
3. Allocating values to pollutant effects needs value judgements.
4. Selective or abbreviated LCAs can be used to reach a pre-determined conclusion, such as in advertising.
5. Using an item again, for example refilling glass bottles.
6. Remaking a material into new products; glass crushed and melted into new glass products.
7. It is added to iron from the blast furnace, reducing the iron needed from ore.
8. Any two: habitat loss, noise, dust, spoil heaps, emissions from machines and transport.
9. 3.0 ÷ 5 = 0.6 MJ per use.
10. (160 − 8) ÷ 160 × 100 = 95%.
11. Mixed materials may give a recycled product with the wrong properties; the separation needed depends on the properties required.
12. To make the product look good by leaving out stages where it performs badly.

## Worked answers to model

**Explain why LCAs are not purely objective.** "Energy, water and waste can be measured, but the effects of
pollutants need value judgements about how serious each is, so people can reach different conclusions."

**Suggest why a company's LCA might be misleading.** "It may only include the stage where the product performs
best, such as use, and leave out manufacture or disposal."

**Evaluate recycling aluminium cans.** "Recycling uses much less energy than extracting aluminium from its ore,
and it saves ore and reduces landfill. But cans must be collected and sorted, which costs money and uses fuel."

## Exam answer frame for comparing two products

1. State the stage totals for each product.
2. State which is lower in total and per use.
3. Mention what the data does not show (pollution, litter, habitats).
4. Reach a conclusion.
