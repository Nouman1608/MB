This set covers **section 1.4, Powers and roots**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. **Tiers:** questions marked **(Higher tier only)** use surds and fractional or negative powers, which the specification lists for the Higher Tier only. ## Section A

**1.** Here is a list of numbers: 1, 8, 16, 27, 45, 64, 100. From the list, write down

**(a)** all the square numbers **[1]**

**(b)** all the cube numbers. **[1]**

**2.** Work out

**(a)** √196 **[1]**

**(b)** ∛343 **[1]**

**(c)** (−3)³ **[1]**

**3.** Write each as a single power.

**(a)** 6³ × 6⁵ **[1]**

**(b)** 9⁴ ÷ 9⁻² **[1]**

**(c)** (5²)⁻³ **[1]**

**4.** Work out 3⁻² + 3⁰, giving your answer as a fraction. **[2]**

**5.** Write 2100 as a product of powers of its prime factors. **[2]**

**6.** 360 = 2³ × 3² × 5. Find the HCF and the LCM of 2100 and 360. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** 3136 = 2⁶ × 7².

**(a)** Use this to find √3136, without a calculator. Show your method. **[2]**

**(b)** Is 3136 a cube number? Give a reason. **[1]**

**8.** A = 2³ × 3² × 5 and B = 2 × 3⁴ × 7.

**(a)** Find the HCF of A and B. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the LCM of A and B, as an ordinary number. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the smallest whole number n such that A × n is a square number. **[2]**

**9.** A rectangular floor measures 168 cm by 120 cm. It is to be covered exactly with identical square tiles, with no cutting. Find the side of the largest tile that can be used, and the number of these tiles needed. **[3]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Give each answer in its simplest form.

**(a)** Simplify √75 − √12. **[2]**

**(b)** Expand and simplify (3 − √2)². **[2]**

**(c)** Rationalise the denominator of 12/√6. **[2]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Show that 5/(√7 + √2) = √7 − √2. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher tier only)** Show your working.

**(a)** Evaluate 64^(−2/3). **[2]**

**(b)** Evaluate (27/8)^(−1/3). **[2]**

**(c)** Find x if 4ˣ = 1/8. **[2]**

**13.** n is a positive integer and 2ⁿ × 2³ = 128. Find the value of n. **[2]**

**14.** **(Higher tier only)** Show that (√12 + √3)² = 27. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1, 16, 64, 100** [1]

**(b)** **1, 8, 27, 64** [1]

**2. (a)** **14** [1]

**(b)** **7** [1]

**(c)** **−27** [1]

**3. (a)** **6⁸** [1]

**(b)** **9⁶** [1]

**(c)** **5⁻⁶** [1]

**4.** 1/9 + 1 [1]; = **10/9** (or 1 1/9) [1]

**5.** Correct method, such as a factor tree with primes 2, 2, 3, 5, 5, 7 [1]; **2² × 3 × 5² × 7** [1]

**6.** HCF = 2² × 3 × 5 [1] = **60** [1]; LCM = 2³ × 3² × 5² × 7 = **12 600** [1]

*Examiner insight:* Writing both factorisations one above the other, with the same primes lined up, makes it easy to pick the lower power (HCF) and higher power (LCM) of each prime.

**7. (a)** Halve each power: √3136 = 2³ × 7 [1] = **56** [1]

**(b)** **No**: the power of 7 is 2, which is not a multiple of 3 [1]

**8. (a)** 2 × 3² = **18** [1]

**(b)** 2³ × 3⁴ × 5 × 7 [1] = **22 680** [1]

**(c)** The powers of 2 and 5 in A are odd [1], so **n = 2 × 5 = 10** [1]

**9.** 168 = 2³ × 3 × 7 and 120 = 2³ × 3 × 5, so HCF = 24 [1]; largest tile **24 cm** [1]; 168 ÷ 24 = 7 and 120 ÷ 24 = 5, so **35 tiles** [1]

**10. (a)** 5√3 − 2√3 [1] = **3√3** [1]

**(b)** 9 − 3√2 − 3√2 + 2 [1] = **11 − 6√2** [1]

**(c)** 12√6/6 [1] = **2√6** [1]

**11.** Multiply by (√7 − √2)/(√7 − √2) [1]; denominator 7 − 2 = 5 [1]; 5(√7 − √2)/5 = √7 − √2 [1]

**12. (a)** 1/64^(2/3) [1]; ∛64 = 4, 4² = 16, so **1/16** [1]

**(b)** (8/27)^(1/3) [1] = **2/3** [1]

**(c)** 2^(2x) = 2⁻³ [1], so 2x = −3 and **x = −3/2** [1]

**13.** 128 = 2⁷ [1]; n + 3 = 7, so **n = 4** [1]

**14.** √12 = 2√3, so √12 + √3 = 3√3 [1]; (3√3)² = 9 × 3 = 27 [1]

*Examiner insight:* Expanding the bracket also works: 12 + 2√36 + 3 = 12 + 12 + 3 = 27. Simplifying the surds first is shorter.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing 45 as a square number in question 1, or missing that 1 and 64 are in both lists.
- Writing 9² for question 3(b) by subtracting 4 − 2 instead of 4 − (−2).
- Writing 3⁻² as −9 in question 4.
- Taking the higher powers for the HCF in questions 6 and 8.
- Giving n = 2 in question 8(c), which fixes only the power of 2.
- Multiplying by (√7 + √2) in question 11, which does not remove the surd.
