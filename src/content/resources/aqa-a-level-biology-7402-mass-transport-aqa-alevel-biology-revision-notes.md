## Haemoglobin

- Quaternary protein: four polypeptides, each with a haem group (iron) binding one O₂.
- Found in red blood cells; haemoglobins are a group of chemically similar molecules found in many organisms.
- **Loads** O₂ at high partial pressure (lungs); **unloads** at low partial pressure (respiring tissues).
- **Cooperative binding:** first O₂ binds with difficulty → shape changes → next O₂ binds more easily → S-shaped (sigmoid) curve.
- **Bohr effect:** more CO₂ → lower pH → haemoglobin shape changes → lower affinity → curve shifts **right** → more unloading.

| Curve position | Affinity | Suits |
|---|---|---|
| Left | Higher; loads at low partial pressure | Low-oxygen environments; fetus |
| Right | Lower; unloads readily | Active animals; small mammals with high metabolic rate per gram |

**Unloading calculation:** % saturation leaving lungs − % saturation at tissue = % of capacity unloaded.

## Circulation

- Double circulation: pulmonary (heart–lungs) and systemic (heart–body).
- Coronary arteries supply heart muscle.

| Vessel | Carries |
|---|---|
| Vena cava | Deoxygenated blood from body to right atrium |
| Pulmonary artery | Deoxygenated blood from right ventricle to lungs |
| Pulmonary vein | Oxygenated blood from lungs to left atrium |
| Aorta | Oxygenated blood from left ventricle to body |
| Renal artery / vein | Blood to / from kidneys |

## The heart and cardiac cycle

- Atria above ventricles; atrioventricular valves between; semilunar valves in aorta and pulmonary artery.
- Left ventricle wall thicker: higher pressure to pump blood around the body.
- Valves move only because of pressure differences.

| Stage | Atria | Ventricles | AV valves | Semilunar valves |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Atrial systole | Contract | Relaxed | Open | Closed |
| Ventricular systole | Relaxed | Contract | Close | Open (once ventricle pressure > artery) |
| Diastole | Relaxed | Relaxed | Open (once ventricle pressure < atrium) | Close |

**Graph rules:** ventricle > atrium → AV shuts; ventricle > aorta → semilunar opens; ventricle < aorta → semilunar shuts; ventricle < atrium → AV opens.

**Heart rate** = 60 ÷ time for one cycle in seconds.

## Blood vessels

| Vessel | Key structures | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Artery | Thick muscle, elastic tissue, folded endothelium | Withstand and smooth high pressure |
| Arteriole | Muscle in wall | Control flow to capillaries |
| Vein | Wide lumen, valves | Low pressure, prevent backflow |
| Capillary | One-cell-thick endothelium, narrow, branched beds | Short distance, large surface area for exchange |

## Tissue fluid

1. Arteriole end: high hydrostatic pressure forces water and small solutes out; proteins stay in.
2. Venule end: lower hydrostatic pressure; plasma proteins give blood lower water potential; water returns by osmosis.
3. Excess drains into the lymph and returns to the blood.

Build-up of tissue fluid: high blood pressure, or low plasma protein.

## Plants

**Xylem: cohesion-tension**

- Transpiration: water evaporates from leaf cells, diffuses out of stomata.
- Lower water potential in leaf → water drawn from xylem.
- Cohesion (hydrogen bonds) → continuous column pulled up under tension.
- Adhesion to xylem walls helps.
- Evidence: trunk diameter shrinks in the day; cut xylem draws in air.

**Phloem: mass flow**

- Source: sucrose actively loaded (companion cells) → low water potential → water in by osmosis → high pressure.
- Sink: sucrose used or stored → water out → lower pressure.
- Solution flows source → sink down the pressure gradient.

| For mass flow | Against |
|---|---|
| Sap under pressure when phloem cut | Solutes move at different speeds |
| Higher sucrose at source than sink | Movement in different directions at once |
| ATP inhibitors stop translocation | Sieve plates would hinder flow |

**Tracers:** radioactive carbon in CO₂ → labelled sugars → autoradiograph shows them in phloem.
**Ringing:** remove bark and phloem → swelling with sugars above ring.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Affinity | How readily haemoglobin binds oxygen |
| Partial pressure of oxygen | Measure of oxygen concentration |
| Bohr effect | Higher CO₂ lowers haemoglobin's affinity for O₂ |
| Systole / diastole | Contraction / relaxation of heart chambers |
| Tissue fluid | Fluid bathing cells, formed from plasma |
| Cohesion | Attraction between water molecules by hydrogen bonds |
| Translocation | Movement of organic substances in the phloem |
| Source / sink | Where assimilates are made / where they are used or stored |

## Exam traps

- Bohr effect = shift to the **right**.
- Valves are not moved by muscles.
- Left ventricle: higher pressure, not more blood.
- Plasma proteins are too big to leave capillaries; they lower blood water potential.
- Sucrose travels in phloem, water in xylem.
- In ringing experiments, the xylem is left intact.

## Quick self-test

1. Explain why the dissociation curve is S-shaped.
2. Which way does the curve shift with more CO₂?
3. Why does fetal haemoglobin have a higher affinity than adult haemoglobin?
4. Name the vessel carrying blood from the left ventricle.
5. What supplies the heart muscle with blood?
6. What makes the atrioventricular valve close?
7. One cardiac cycle lasts 0.75 s. Calculate heart rate.
8. Why do veins have valves?
9. Why does water return to capillaries at the venule end?
10. What is the role of cohesion in xylem transport?
11. Describe what happens at a sink in the mass flow hypothesis.
12. Give one piece of evidence against mass flow.

### Answers

1. Cooperative binding: the first O₂ changes haemoglobin's shape, making further binding easier.
2. To the right.
3. So it can load oxygen at the lower partial pressure of the mother's blood in the placenta.
4. Aorta.
5. Coronary arteries.
6. Ventricular pressure rising above atrial pressure.
7. 60 ÷ 0.75 = **80 beats per minute**.
8. Blood pressure is low, so valves prevent backflow.
9. Hydrostatic pressure is lower and plasma proteins give the blood a lower water potential, so water enters by osmosis.
10. Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together, so a continuous column is pulled up under tension.
11. Sucrose is removed and used or stored, water potential rises, water leaves by osmosis, pressure falls.
12. Any one: solutes move at different speeds; movement in different directions at once; sieve plates would hinder flow.
