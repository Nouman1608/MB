These notes condense **3.1.6.2 Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 2. For explanations and worked examples, read the [Maxwell–Boltzmann study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-6-2/); for exam-style questions, use the [Maxwell–Boltzmann practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-6-2-practice/).

## Core ideas

- The **Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution** shows the spread of **molecular energies in a gas** at a fixed temperature.
- **Area under the curve** = total number of molecules.
- **Area to the right of Ea** = molecules with energy ≥ Ea (able to react on collision).
- You must **draw** and **interpret** curves for **different temperatures**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution | Graph of number of molecules against energy for a gas at one temperature |
| Most probable energy | Energy at the peak: the energy held by the most molecules |
| Mean energy | Average energy; to the right of the peak because of the tail |
| Tail | The long high-energy part of the curve that approaches the axis without reaching it |
| Activation energy, Ea | Minimum collision energy for reaction; a vertical line in the tail |

## Shape: feature and reason

| Feature | Why |
|---|---|
| Starts at (0, 0) | No molecules have zero energy |
| Single peak | Most molecules have moderate energies |
| Asymmetric, skewed right | Energies cannot be below zero, but have no upper limit |
| Never meets the energy axis | Some molecules always have very high energies |
| Fixed area | Number of molecules is fixed |

## Axis labels

- x-axis: **energy**.
- y-axis: **number of molecules** (or fraction of molecules) with that energy.

## Two temperatures, one sample

| | Lower temperature | Higher temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Peak position | Further left | Further right |
| Peak height | Higher | Lower |
| Width | Narrower | Broader |
| Area | Same | Same |
| Area beyond Ea | Smaller | Larger |
| Above the other curve | At low energies | At high energies (including at Ea) |

The curves cross once.

## Other changes

| Change | Effect on curve |
|---|---|
| More molecules, same temperature | Same shape and peak energy; higher at every energy; larger total area; same fraction ≥ Ea |
| Fewer molecules, same temperature | Same shape and peak energy; lower everywhere; smaller area |
| Heating | Peak moves right and down; same area; larger fraction ≥ Ea |
| Cooling | Peak moves left and up; same area; smaller fraction ≥ Ea |

Ea itself does **not** change with temperature or number of molecules.

## Sketch-checking list

- Starts at the origin.
- Long tail on the right, above the axis.
- Hotter curve: lower, right-shifted peak; curves cross; equal areas.
- Ea marked on the energy axis, well to the right of the peak.
- Both axes labelled; each curve labelled with its temperature.

## Reading areas: a worked reminder

A curve at T₁ encloses 1500 small squares; 12 lie beyond Ea. At a higher temperature T₂ the same sample has 33 squares beyond Ea.

- Fraction ≥ Ea at T₁ = 12 ÷ 1500 = 0.8 %; at T₂ = 33 ÷ 1500 = 2.2 %.
- The fraction rises by 33 ÷ 12 = 2.75 times.
- The total area is still 1500 squares at T₂, because the number of molecules is unchanged.

Statements about the curve should always refer to **areas** (numbers of molecules), not to the height of the curve at one energy.

## Exam traps

- y-axis labelled "energy" or "kinetic energy".
- Bell-shaped, symmetrical curve.
- Tail touching or crossing the axis.
- Taller peak drawn for the higher temperature.
- Hotter curve above the cooler curve everywhere (more area).
- Ea moved when temperature changes.
- Interpreting from curve height at Ea rather than the **area** beyond it.

## Self-test

1. What is plotted on each axis of a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution?
2. What does the total area under the curve represent?
3. Why does the curve start at the origin?
4. Why does the tail never touch the energy axis?
5. Where is the mean energy compared with the most probable energy?
6. State three ways the curve changes when the same gas is heated.
7. Why is the total area unchanged on heating?
8. What does the area to the right of Ea represent?
9. A curve at T₂ has more area beyond Ea than the curve at T₁ for the same gas. Which temperature is higher?
10. More of the same gas is added at the same temperature. Does the peak move?
11. Does activation energy change when the temperature rises?
12. Why must two curves for the same sample at different temperatures cross?

### Answers

1. x-axis: energy; y-axis: number (or fraction) of molecules with that energy.
2. The total number of molecules in the sample.
3. No molecules have zero energy.
4. There is no maximum energy; a few molecules always have very high energy.
5. To the right (higher), because of the long high-energy tail.
6. Any three: peak moves to higher energy; peak is lower; curve is broader; more area beyond Ea.
7. The number of molecules has not changed.
8. The number of molecules with energy equal to or greater than the activation energy.
9. T₂.
10. No: the peak stays at the same energy; the curve is higher everywhere.
11. No.
12. Their areas are equal, so if the hotter curve is higher at high energies it must be lower at low energies.
