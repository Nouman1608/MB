> These are original practice questions written in the style of Cambridge O Level
> Geography (2217) assessment objectives. They are not taken from any past paper
> and are not endorsed by Cambridge International. Country data are illustrative;
> use your own learned case study where a question asks for a named example.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *life expectancy*. **[1]**

**2.** Classify each job into its sector of production: (a) bank
clerk, (b) coal miner, (c) software researcher, (d) car assembly
worker. **[4]**

**3.** Name the three components combined in the Human Development
Index. **[3]**

**4.** Define *globalisation*. **[2]**

**5.** State two reasons why GNP per capita on its own may not show
the quality of life of most people in a country. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows employment by sector for three illustrative
countries.

| Country | Primary (%) | Secondary (%) | Tertiary and quaternary (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 65 | 10 | 25 |
| Y | 30 | 32 | 38 |
| Z | 2 | 18 | 80 |

**(a)** Which country is likely to be the most developed? Give a reason
from the data. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times greater the tertiary and quaternary
share is in country Z than in country X. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the proportion employed in the secondary sector in
country Y is higher than in country X. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest why the secondary share in Z is lower than in Y. **[2]**

**7.** Suggest reasons why some countries remain at a low level of
development. Refer to physical, economic and political factors. **[6]**

**8.** Many countries have large differences in wealth between regions.

**(a)** Describe what is meant by a core–periphery pattern. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the migration of young adults can make regional
inequality worse. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how improvements in transport and communications have
encouraged globalisation. **[4]**

**10.** For a named transnational corporation, describe its global
links and assess the advantages and disadvantages of its operations
for a country in which it has invested. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** The average number of years a person born in a country can
expect to live [1].

**2.** (a) Tertiary [1]. (b) Primary [1]. (c) Quaternary [1].
(d) Secondary [1].

**3.** Life expectancy (health) [1]; education, measured by years of
schooling [1]; income per person (GNI per capita) [1].

**4.** The increasing connection and interdependence of countries [1]
through trade, investment, the movement of people, culture and
information [1].

**5.** Any two [2]: it is an average, so it hides inequality between
rich and poor; it does not include the informal economy or subsistence
production; it does not measure health, education or living
conditions; prices differ between countries, so the same income buys
different amounts.

**6. (a)** Country Z [1]: it has the smallest primary share (2 per
cent) and the largest tertiary and quaternary share (80 per cent) [1].
**(b)** 80 ÷ 25 = 3.2 times [1].
**(c)** Up to 3 marks: Y is industrialising, so factories have been
built, often with investment from TNCs [1]; farming has been
mechanised, so fewer workers are needed on the land [1]; factory work
pays more than farming, so workers move from the primary to the
secondary sector [1].
**(d)** Any two [2]: manufacturing has been automated so fewer workers
are needed; factories have relocated to countries with lower labour
costs (deindustrialisation); rising incomes have increased demand for
services, so more people work in the tertiary sector.

**7.** Levels-style marking. Up to 2 marks for simple reasons; 3–4
marks for developed reasons from two types of factor; 5–6 marks for
developed reasons covering physical, economic and political factors.
Indicative content: physical — landlocked, so trade is costly;
frequent droughts or tropical storms destroy crops and infrastructure;
few natural resources. Economic — dependence on exporting a few
primary products with unstable prices; debt repayments reduce money
for schools and hospitals; little capital for investment. Political —
conflict destroys infrastructure and drives away investors; corruption
diverts funds; a colonial history that left an economy based on raw
material exports.

**8. (a)** A wealthy, well-developed central region, often the capital
or main port [1], contrasted with poorer, less developed outer regions
[1].
**(b)** Up to 3 marks: young adults leave the periphery for jobs in the
core [1]; the periphery loses its most productive workers and an
ageing population remains, so the local economy declines further [1];
the core gains workers and spending, attracting yet more investment,
so the gap widens [1].

**9.** Up to 4 marks: container ships and larger aircraft have cut the
cost and time of moving goods [1], so it is cheaper to make products in
one country and sell them in another [1]; the internet and mobile
communications allow a company to manage factories and offices
worldwide from one headquarters [1]; services such as call centres and
software can be supplied from another country instantly [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking.
*Level 1 (1–3 marks)*: general statements about TNCs, no named
company or places.
*Level 2 (4–5 marks)*: named TNC with some global links and some
advantages or disadvantages.
*Level 3 (6–7 marks)*: named TNC with specific places, both advantages
and disadvantages developed, and an assessment.
Indicative content (Toyota example): headquarters in Toyota City,
Japan; car plant at Burnaston near Derby and engine plant at Deeside
in the UK; plant at Georgetown, Kentucky, USA. Advantages for the host:
direct jobs, further jobs in component suppliers, training, exports.
Disadvantages: major decisions made in Japan; profits return to the
home country; jobs at risk if production is cut. An assessment might
conclude that the benefits are large while the plant operates, but
the host has little control over its future.

## A note on exam technique for this subtopic

When data are given, use them: quote figures, calculate differences
and make comparative statements. When reasons are asked for, vary the
type of reason and develop each one with a consequence. A TNC answer
without named places stays in the lowest level.
