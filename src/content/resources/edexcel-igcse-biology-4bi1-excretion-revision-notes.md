## Key definitions

- **Excretion:** removal of the waste products of metabolism (chemical reactions in cells) from the body.
- **Egestion:** removal of undigested food as faeces. Not excretion.
- **Osmoregulation:** control of the water content (and ion content) of the blood.
- **Urea:** a nitrogen-containing waste made in the liver from excess amino acids, carried in plasma.
- **Urine:** the liquid made by the kidneys -- water, urea and ions.
- **Ultrafiltration:** filtration of small molecules out of the blood under high pressure, in the glomerulus.
- **Selective reabsorption:** taking particular useful substances, such as glucose, back into the blood from the nephron.
- **Glomerular filtrate:** the liquid that enters Bowman's capsule.
- **ADH:** antidiuretic hormone, released by the pituitary gland; makes the collecting duct more permeable to water.

## 2.70 Plants: waste gases

| Gas | Waste product of | When it leaves the leaf | Route out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Respiration | In the dark (or dim light), when respiration is faster than photosynthesis | Diffusion through stomata |
| Oxygen | Photosynthesis | In bright light, when photosynthesis is faster than respiration | Diffusion through stomata |

Respiration never stops. In bright light its carbon dioxide is used in photosynthesis instead of leaving.

## 2.73B Urinary system (Paper 2 only)

Renal artery → **kidney** → **ureter** → **bladder** (stores urine) → **urethra** → outside.
Cleaned blood leaves the kidney in the renal vein.

Memory hook: the ureter comes first (kidney to bladder); the urethra is the exit.

## 2.74B Nephron structure and jobs (Paper 2 only)

| Part | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Glomerulus | Cortex, inside the capsule | Knot of capillaries; blood at high pressure |
| Bowman's capsule | Cortex | Collects glomerular filtrate (ultrafiltration) |
| Proximal convoluted tubule | Cortex | Reabsorbs all glucose, plus some water and ions |
| Loop of Henle | Dips into medulla | Makes medulla tissue concentrated (high ion concentration) |
| Distal convoluted tubule | Cortex | Adjusts ions reabsorbed |
| Collecting duct | Through the medulla | Water reabsorbed by osmosis; controlled by ADH |

## 2.75B Ultrafiltration in steps (Paper 2 only)

1. Vessel into the glomerulus is wider than the vessel out.
2. So blood pressure in the glomerulus is high.
3. Small molecules are forced out through the capillary wall and capsule wall.
4. They form the glomerular filtrate in Bowman's capsule.

| In the filtrate | Not in the filtrate (too large) |
|---|---|
| Water, glucose, urea, ions, amino acids | Red and white blood cells, platelets, plasma proteins |

## 2.77B Glucose reabsorption (Paper 2 only)

- **Why:** glucose is needed for respiration, so the body must not lose it.
- **Where:** proximal convoluted tubule. All of it, normally.
- **How:** including active transport, using energy from respiration -- so the tubule cells have many mitochondria (and microvilli for surface area).
- Glucose in urine means the filtrate held more glucose than the tubule could take back, for example when blood glucose is very high in untreated diabetes.

## 2.76B and 2.78B Water reabsorption and ADH (Paper 2 only)

Method in steps -- "explain how water is reabsorbed":

1. Loop of Henle makes the medulla tissue concentrated.
2. Collecting duct passes through the medulla.
3. Water leaves the duct by **osmosis** into the tissue, then into the blood.
4. ADH sets how permeable the duct walls are, and so how much water leaves.

| Blood water content | ADH | Collecting duct walls | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Low (sweating, not drinking, salty food) | More | More permeable | Less volume, more concentrated |
| High (drinking a lot) | Less | Less permeable | More volume, more dilute |

This is negative feedback, and body water content is an example of homeostasis (2.81).

## 2.72B and 2.79B Summary (Paper 2 only)

- **Excretion role:** removes urea (filtered, not reabsorbed).
- **Osmoregulation role:** controls blood water content by varying water reabsorption at the collecting duct.
- **Urine contains:** water, urea, ions. Healthy urine has no glucose and no protein.

## Two calculations to know

**Concentration factor:** concentration in urine ÷ concentration in filtrate. It is bigger than 1 for urea because water is reabsorbed but urea is not.

**Percentage of filtrate reabsorbed:** (volume of filtrate − volume of urine) ÷ volume of filtrate × 100. Use the same units and the same time period for both volumes.

Small reminder: if filtrate is formed at 110 cm³ per minute and urine at 2.2 cm³ per minute, the percentage reabsorbed is (110 − 2.2) ÷ 110 × 100 = 98%.

## Method in steps: reading a plasma, filtrate and urine table

1. **Same in plasma and filtrate?** The substance is small enough to be filtered.
2. **Zero in filtrate?** Too large to pass out of the glomerulus (proteins, cells).
3. **In filtrate, zero in urine?** It is completely reabsorbed (glucose, at the proximal convoluted tubule).
4. **Higher in urine than filtrate?** Water has been reabsorbed but the substance has not (urea).
5. Quote figures from the table with units when you describe a change, and give a cause for each difference.

## Answer frame: "Explain how the kidney produces urine"

Use the order of the nephron and give one cause for each step:

- high pressure in the glomerulus → ultrafiltration into Bowman's capsule;
- filtrate contains water, glucose, urea, ions -- no proteins or cells;
- all glucose reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule, by active transport;
- water reabsorbed by osmosis from the collecting duct, more when ADH is high;
- urea stays in the tubule, so urine holds water, urea and ions;
- urine passes along the ureter to the bladder.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Excretion vs egestion:** waste of metabolism vs undigested food.
- **Urea vs urine:** the waste chemical vs the whole liquid.
- **Ureter vs urethra:** kidney to bladder vs bladder to outside.
- **Ultrafiltration vs selective reabsorption:** non-selective, by pressure, at the glomerulus vs chosen substances back into blood, at the tubules.
- **Proximal vs distal convoluted tubule:** first coil (glucose back) vs second coil (ions adjusted).
- **Oxygen vs carbon dioxide in plants:** waste of photosynthesis vs waste of respiration.
- **More ADH vs less ADH:** less, concentrated urine vs more, dilute urine.

### Answers

1. Respiration.
2. Stomata (by diffusion).
3. Any two of: water, ions (salts), urea.
4. Urethra.
5. Glomerulus / Bowman's capsule.
6. Any two of: plasma proteins, red blood cells, white blood cells, platelets.
7. It is needed for respiration (as an energy source).
8. Osmosis.
9. Pituitary gland.
10. ADH rises; urine volume falls (urine is more concentrated).
11. (130 − 1.3) ÷ 130 × 100 = **99.0%**.
12. 16 ÷ 0.25 = **64 times**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying oxygen is "made by respiration" or that plants only respire at night.
- Writing "the stomata excrete" without saying the gas diffuses out through them.
- Listing faeces or bile as excretory products of the organs in 2.71.
- Swapping ureter and urethra, or writing "urea" when the question asks about urine.
- Saying ultrafiltration "selects" urea: all small molecules enter the filtrate.
- Giving "glucose is filtered" as the reason there is no glucose in urine -- the mark is for reabsorption in the proximal convoluted tubule.
- Saying ADH "reabsorbs water" -- ADH changes the permeability of the collecting duct; water moves by osmosis.
- Saying "more ADH, more urine" -- it is the reverse.
- Dividing by the urine volume instead of the filtrate volume in a percentage reabsorbed calculation.
- Calling the urea concentration rise "urea being added": urea is concentrated because water is removed.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between excretion and egestion?**
Excretion is the removal of the waste products of metabolism, such as urea and carbon dioxide. Egestion is the removal of undigested food as faeces.

**What is the difference between a ureter and a urethra?**
A ureter carries urine from each kidney to the bladder. The urethra carries urine from the bladder out of the body.

**How does ultrafiltration happen?**
The vessel into the glomerulus is wider than the vessel leaving it, so blood pressure is high and small molecules are forced out through the capillary walls into the Bowman's capsule, forming glomerular filtrate.

**Where is glucose reabsorbed?**
All the glucose is reabsorbed in the proximal convoluted tubule, partly by active transport, because it is needed for respiration.

**Does more ADH mean more urine?**
No. More ADH makes the collecting duct more permeable, so more water is reabsorbed and a smaller volume of concentrated urine is produced.
