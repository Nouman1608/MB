## Section A

**1.** For a regular pentagon, write down

**(a)** the number of lines of symmetry, **[1]**

**(b)** the order of rotational symmetry. **[1]**

**2.** Name a quadrilateral that has exactly two lines of symmetry and rotational symmetry of order 2, and whose diagonals are **not** lines of symmetry. **[1]**

**3.** Name a type of triangle that has exactly one line of symmetry. **[1]**

**4.** A shape maps onto itself when it is turned through 72° about its centre, and through no smaller angle. Write down its order of rotational symmetry. **[1]**

**5.** A regular polygon has 12 lines of symmetry.

**(a)** Write down the number of sides of the polygon. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out the size of each exterior angle. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the size of each interior angle. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why a diagonal of a rectangle measuring 6 cm by 4 cm is not a line of symmetry. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A quadrilateral has vertices P(0, 0), Q(4, 0), R(5, 3) and S(1, 3).

**(a)** Write down the number of lines of symmetry of PQRS. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the order of rotational symmetry of PQRS. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the coordinates of the centre of rotational symmetry. **[2]**

**8.** The line x = 5 is a line of symmetry of a shape. Half of the shape is drawn: it starts on the mirror line at (5, 0), goes to (2, 0), (2, 2) and (3, 4), and returns to the mirror line at (5, 4).

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the three vertices that complete the shape. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the order of rotational symmetry of the completed shape. Give a reason. **[2]**

**9.** In a 4 by 4 grid of squares, squares A1 and B2 are shaded.

**(a)** Shade exactly two more squares so that the pattern has rotational symmetry of order 2 about the centre of the grid. Write down the two squares. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the number of lines of symmetry of the completed pattern. **[1]**

**10.** Each interior angle of a regular polygon is 150°.

**(a)** Work out the number of sides. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the number of lines of symmetry and the order of rotational symmetry. **[1]**

**11.** Sam says, "Every shape with rotational symmetry of order 2 also has two lines of symmetry."

Give an example to show that Sam is wrong, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**12.** Triangle ABC is isosceles with AB = AC. Its line of symmetry passes through A and meets BC at M. Angle BAC = 48°.

**(a)** Work out the size of angle ABC. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the size of angle AMB, giving a reason based on symmetry. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 5 [1].

**(b)** 5 [1].

**2.** Rectangle [1]. (A rhombus has diagonals as its lines of symmetry, so it does not fit.)

**3.** Isosceles triangle [1].

**4.** 360 ÷ 72 = 5, so order **5** [1].

**5. (a)** 12 [1].

**(b)** 360° ÷ 12 = **30°** [1].

**(c)** 180° − 30° = **150°** [1].

**6.** Reflecting a corner in the diagonal does not land on another corner of the rectangle [1], because the two sides meeting at that corner are different lengths (6 cm and 4 cm), so the two halves are not mirror images [1].

**7. (a)** 0 [1] (PQRS is a parallelogram: PQ = 4 but PS = √10, so it is not a rhombus, and its angles are not right angles).

**(b)** 2 [1].

**(c)** Midpoint of a diagonal, e.g. PR: ((0 + 5)/2, (0 + 3)/2) [1] = **(2.5, 1.5)** [1]. (QS gives ((4 + 1)/2, (0 + 3)/2), the same point.)

**8. (a)** Images in x = 5 use (x, y) → (10 − x, y): (2, 0) → **(8, 0)**, (2, 2) → **(8, 2)**, (3, 4) → **(7, 4)**. All three correct [2] (1 mark for two correct).

**(b)** Order **1** [1]. A half turn about the centre (5, 2) would send (2, 0) to (8, 4), which is not a vertex, so the shape does not fit onto itself before a full turn [1].

**9. (a)** A half turn swaps A ↔ D, B ↔ C, 1 ↔ 4 and 2 ↔ 3. A1 → **D4** [1]; B2 → **C3** [1].

**(b)** **2** [1]. The shaded squares A1, B2, C3 and D4 lie along one diagonal of the grid, which is a line of symmetry, and the other diagonal also reflects the pattern onto itself (A1 ↔ D4, B2 ↔ C3). This agrees with order 2.

**10. (a)** Exterior angle = 180° − 150° = 30° [1]; number of sides = 360 ÷ 30 = **12** [1].

**(b)** 12 lines of symmetry and order 12 [1].

**11.** A parallelogram (that is not a rectangle or rhombus) [1] has rotational symmetry of order 2 but no lines of symmetry, so Sam's statement is false [1].

**12. (a)** By symmetry, angle ABC = angle ACB [1]. (180° − 48°) ÷ 2 = **66°** [1].

**(b)** **90°** [1]. Reflection in AM maps B onto C, so the line of symmetry AM is the perpendicular bisector of BC.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: answering "rhombus". Its diagonals are its lines of symmetry, which the question rules out.
- Question 4: giving 72 (the angle) instead of 5 (the order).
- Question 6: saying only "it doesn't fold". The mark needs the reason: the sides at a corner are unequal, so a corner does not reflect onto a corner.
- Question 7: counting the diagonals of the parallelogram as lines of symmetry.
- Question 8(a): reflecting in x = 5 with x − 5 instead of 10 − x, or leaving out (8, 0).
- Question 10: dividing 360 by 150 instead of finding the exterior angle first.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you find the centre of rotational symmetry of a parallelogram?**
Find the midpoint of either diagonal. The diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, so both midpoints are the same point, and a half turn about it maps the shape onto itself.

**How do you complete a shape so that a given line is a line of symmetry?**
Reflect each vertex in the line: for a vertical line x = a, the point (x, y) goes to (2a − x, y). Points on the line stay where they are, and you join the images in the same order.
