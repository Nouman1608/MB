This practice set is for IB MYP Individuals and Societies and follows the IB *MYP Individuals and societies guide* (for use from September 2014/January 2015; published May 2014, updated April 2021). It covers the six global contexts, statements of inquiry and how a context frames an investigation, and suits MYP years 4 and 5 (no SL/HL split). MYP has no prescribed content list -- schools design their own units -- so your teacher will share the task-specific clarifications.

Each question is labelled with the criterion it trains: A Knowing and understanding, B Investigating, C Communicating, D Thinking critically. Real MYP work is judged against criterion level descriptors (1–8, in four bands), so the [1] points here are a revision aid, not IB marks. The town and data in question 8 are fictional.

See the [study guide](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-global-contexts/), the [revision notes](/resources/ib-myp-individuals-and-societies-global-contexts-revision-notes/), the [MYP Individuals and Societies hub](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-individuals-and-societies/) and the [printable checklist](/checklists/ib/ib-myp/myp-individuals-and-societies/).

## Questions

**1.** (Criterion A) List the six MYP global contexts. **[3]**

**2.** (Criterion A) State what the guide requires a teacher to do about a global context for each unit, and name the context it says many I&S inquiries focus on. **[2]**

**3.** (Criterion A) Explain the difference between the key concept *time, place and space* and the global context *orientation in space and time*. **[3]**

**4.** (Criterion A) Suggest the best-fitting global context for each unit outline.

**(i)** How a powerful banking family used paintings and buildings to display its status.
**(ii)** Why life expectancy differs between the richest and poorest regions of one country.
**(iii)** How the arrival of railways changed where people could live and work.
**(iv)** How a coffee chain sources beans from three continents, and the effect on forests.
**(v)** How the street plan of one city changed between 1800 and 1900. **[5]**

**5.** (Criterion D) A school plans a unit on the building of a large dam on a river. Explain how the unit would differ if it were framed by *fairness and development* rather than *globalization and sustainability*. Give one inquiry question for each. **[4]**

**6.** (Criterion B) A unit on the global supply chain of cheap clothing uses the key concept *systems* and the global context *globalization and sustainability*. Write a statement of inquiry for the unit and explain why it is transferable. **[3]**

**7.** (Criterion B) Three draft research questions are shown.

- Q1: "Was the Industrial Revolution good?"
- Q2: "How have the number and type of shops on one high street in my town changed between 2015 and 2025?"
- Q3: "What exactly did farm labourers in my region eat each day in 1347?"

Evaluate the three questions, choose the best, and improve Q1. **[5]**

**8.** (Criterion B) A student investigates access to home broadband in Brennick, a fictional town, under the global context *fairness and development*. She surveys 240 households.

| District | Households surveyed | Households with broadband |
|---|---|---|
| North | 80 | 68 |
| South | 90 | 45 |
| East | 70 | 56 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of households with broadband in each district and in the whole sample. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the gap, in percentage points, between the highest and lowest district. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the percentage of all households without broadband that are in the South district. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how the global context should shape the student's next step. **[2]**

**9.** (Criterion B) Write an action plan for this research question, set in the global context *scientific and technical innovation*: "How has the use of contactless card payments changed the way stallholders at one weekly market take payment since 2020?" **[6]**

**10.** (Criterion C) Plan a one-page infographic for Year 7 students that explains what a global context is, using palm oil in Indonesia as the example. **[5]**

**11.** (Criterion D) "Orientation in space and time is the only global context a history unit really needs." To what extent do you agree? **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Identities and relationships; orientation in space and time. [1] Personal and cultural expression; scientific and technical innovation. [1] Globalization and sustainability; fairness and development. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs two correctly worded contexts; "time and space orientation" or "sustainability and globalization" may not be credited.

**2.** The teacher must identify a global context for the unit, or develop an additional one, to show why the inquiry matters. [1] Many I&S inquiries focus on orientation in space and time. [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" needs the fact only, but both parts must be there; the word "must" (a requirement) is the point being tested.

**3.** *Time, place and space* is a key concept -- a big idea about the position of people, objects and ideas, used across subjects. [1] *Orientation in space and time* is a global context -- the real-world setting that shows why a unit matters. [1] A unit can use both: the concept is the lens for thinking, the context is the reason the inquiry is relevant. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain the difference" needs both sides defined and a link between them; describing only one earns at most one point.

**4. (i)** Personal and cultural expression. [1]
**(ii)** Fairness and development. [1]
**(iii)** Scientific and technical innovation. [1]
**(iv)** Globalization and sustainability. [1]
**(v)** Orientation in space and time. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Another context can earn the point with a reason (for example, (iii) as a turning point), so justify any less obvious choice.

**5.** Fairness and development focuses on who gains and who loses -- displaced villagers, farmers downstream, city electricity users. [1] Question: "Who benefited from the dam, and were those displaced fairly compensated?" [1] Globalization and sustainability focuses on long-term environmental effects and wider links, such as river flow, sediment and power exported elsewhere. [1] Question: "Can the dam meet energy needs without lasting harm to the river system?" [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The points come from showing the *focus* changes; two questions with no explanation of the shift earn half at most.

**6.** A statement that links systems to globalization and sustainability without naming the topic, such as: "When one part of a global production system cuts costs, the effects spread to workers and environments elsewhere." [1] It uses the key concept (connected parts) and the context (global links, long-term effects). [1] It is transferable: it could also guide a unit on smartphones or food. [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A statement that names a brand, country or date is a fact about one case and loses the transferability point.

**7.** Q1 is too broad and vague: "good" for whom, where and when? It is not manageable or assessable in the time allowed. [1] Q3 is focused but unanswerable: records that detailed for that year are unlikely to exist. [1] Q2 is best: one place, a fixed period, and evidence from business directories and a street survey. [1] Q2 also has a clear context link -- orientation in space and time, or globalization if the focus is chain stores. [1] Improved Q1: "How did the opening of a cotton mill change working hours and wages in one named town between 1800 and 1840?" [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Credit comes from using the guide's points (relevance, manageability, available resources); "Q2 is more specific" alone earns one point at most.

**8. (a)** North 68 ÷ 80 × 100 = **85.0%**; South 45 ÷ 90 × 100 = **50.0%**; East 56 ÷ 70 × 100 = **80.0%**. [1] Whole sample: (68 + 45 + 56) ÷ 240 × 100 = 169 ÷ 240 × 100 = **70.4%** (3 s.f.). [1]
**(b)** 85.0 − 50.0 = **35.0 percentage points**. [1]
**(c)** Without broadband: 240 − 169 = 71 households; South 90 − 45 = 45. 45 ÷ 71 × 100 = **63.4%** (3 s.f.). [1]
**(d)** Fairness and development asks *why* access is unequal, so the next step should explain the gap, not just record it. [1] For example, compare income or housing type by district, or interview South households about cost and availability of a connection. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The overall figure must come from the totals (169 ÷ 240), not the mean of three percentages (71.7%); averaging percentages from unequal groups is a common slip.

**9.** Aim and context: to find out who gains and who loses when payment technology changes. [1] Methods: structured interviews with about 10 stallholders across stall types. [1] Add observation: count card and cash payments at a few stalls during set periods on two market days. [1] Recording: a tally table and interview notes in a fixed template. [1] Timeline: a schedule with deadlines for permission, data collection, analysis and writing up; ask the market organiser before starting. [1] Evaluation built in: note limits such as a small sample and unreliable memories of 2020. [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* An action plan needs order and timing as well as methods; a list of methods with no sequence or deadlines usually limits strand ii.

**10.** Title and one-line definition in plain words: "A global context is the reason a topic matters." [1] A central map of Sumatra and Borneo, labelled with areas of rainforest cleared for oil palm plantations. [1] Two or three panels showing the same topic through different contexts, for example globalization and sustainability (long-term damage) and fairness and development (whose forest land and livelihoods were lost). [1] Short sentences, simple words and colour coding per context for Year 7. [1] Sources listed in a recognised convention at the bottom. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Criterion C rewards style matched to audience and format, plus documented sources; a dense paragraph for 11-year-olds loses the audience point.

**11.** Introduction defining a global context, with a clear line of argument. [1] Agree: the guide notes many I&S inquiries focus on orientation in space and time, and history studies turning points. [1] Agree, with example: the first US transcontinental railroad (1869) as a turning point that shrank journeys across a continent. [1] Disagree: the guide encourages other contexts, and one context narrows the questions asked. [1] Example: Britain's 1807 and 1833 Acts studied through fairness and development ask whose rights were denied. [1] Example: the transatlantic telegraph cable (1866) through scientific and technical innovation asks who gained from faster news. [1] Example: German unification (1871) through identities and relationships asks how a nation was imagined. [1] Evaluation: time and place runs through all history, but as a key concept, not necessarily the context. [1] Weighs the view that one context gives a course consistency. [1] A justified conclusion that answers "to what extent". [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Criterion D rewards a judgement built from evidence on both sides; examples must show what a *different context* changes, not just name more history topics.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the key concept *time, place and space* with the context *orientation in space and time*.
- Writing a statement of inquiry that names a place, person or date.
- Research questions that are too broad (Q1 type) or impossible to research (Q3 type).
- Averaging percentages from groups of different sizes instead of using totals.
- Action plans with methods but no order, timing or permissions.
- Extended answers that list topics but never show how the context changes the inquiry.
