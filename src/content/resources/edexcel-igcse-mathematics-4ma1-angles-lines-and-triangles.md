## Types of angle (4.1 A)

| Type | Size |
|---|---|
| Acute | more than 0° and less than 90° |
| Right | exactly 90° |
| Obtuse | more than 90° and less than 180° |
| Reflex | more than 180° and less than 360° |

Where two lines meet there are two angles, one on each side; if one is 130°, the other, measured the long way round, is the reflex angle 230°.

## Angle facts for lines (4.1 B)

- **Angles on a straight line** add up to 180°.
- **Angles at a point** add up to 360°.
- **Vertically opposite angles**, formed where two straight lines cross, are equal.

When a straight line (a transversal) crosses two parallel lines:

| Pair | Picture | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| Corresponding angles | in matching positions at each crossing ("F" shape) | equal |
| Alternate angles | on opposite sides of the transversal, between the parallels ("Z" shape) | equal |
| Allied (co-interior) angles | on the same side of the transversal, between the parallels ("C" shape) | add up to 180° |

The letter shapes help you spot the pairs, but in an exam reason write the proper names, not "Z angles".

**Worked example 1 (angles at a point).** Four angles meet at a point: 3x°, (2x + 15)°, 115° and (x + 50)°. Find x.

```
3x + 2x + 15 + 115 + x + 50 = 360      angles at a point add up to 360°
6x + 180 = 360
x = 30
```

**Worked example 2 (a point between parallel lines).** Two parallel lines run left to right. A point E lies between them. A segment from E meets the upper line, making an angle of 38° with it on the side facing E; another segment from E meets the lower line, making an angle of 47° with it on the side facing E. Find the angle at E between the two segments.

Draw a third line through E, parallel to the other two. It splits the angle at E into two parts.

```
upper part = 38°    alternate angles are equal (upper line and the new line)
lower part = 47°    alternate angles are equal (lower line and the new line)
angle at E = 38° + 47° = 85°
```

Adding a parallel line through a point is the standard way to unlock a "bent" angle between parallels.

## Triangle facts (4.1 C)

### Angle sum

The angles in a triangle add up to 180°. Why: through one vertex, draw a line parallel to the opposite side. The two angles of the triangle at the other vertices are equal to two angles on this new line, by alternate angles. Those two angles and the third angle of the triangle sit together on a straight line, so they add up to 180°.

### Exterior angle

Extend one side of a triangle. The angle between the extension and the next side is an exterior angle. **The exterior angle equals the sum of the two interior opposite angles.** Why: the exterior angle and the interior angle next to it add up to 180° (straight line); the three interior angles also add up to 180°; so the exterior angle equals the other two interior angles together.

**Worked example 3.** In a triangle, the exterior angle at C is (5x − 10)°. The interior angles at A and B are (2x + 15)° and (x + 25)°. Find x and the exterior angle.

```
5x − 10 = (2x + 15) + (x + 25)     exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum
5x − 10 = 3x + 40                   of the interior opposite angles
2x = 50,  x = 25
exterior angle = 5(25) − 10 = 115°
```

## Special triangles (4.1 D)

| Triangle | Sides | Angles |
|---|---|---|
| Isosceles | two sides equal | the two base angles (opposite the equal sides) are equal |
| Equilateral | all three sides equal | every angle is 60° |
| Right-angled | one angle is 90° | the other two add up to 90° |

Equal sides are shown by matching dashes on a diagram; equal angles are opposite equal sides.

**Worked example 4 (a chain of reasons).** ABC is an equilateral triangle. BC is extended to D so that CD = CA. Find angle BAD.

```
angle ACB = 60°                 angles in an equilateral triangle are 60°
angle ACD = 180° − 60° = 120°   angles on a straight line add up to 180°
triangle ACD is isosceles (CA = CD), so angle CAD = angle CDA
angle CAD = (180° − 120°) ÷ 2 = 30°   angle sum of a triangle; base angles equal
angle BAD = 60° + 30° = 90°
```

Each line has its value and its reason. This layout is what "Give reasons for your answer" asks for.

## Writing reasons

Use the standard wording:

- "Angles on a straight line add up to 180°."
- "Angles at a point add up to 360°."
- "Vertically opposite angles are equal."
- "Alternate angles are equal." / "Corresponding angles are equal." / "Allied angles add up to 180°."
- "Angles in a triangle add up to 180°."
- "The exterior angle of a triangle equals the sum of the interior opposite angles."
- "Base angles of an isosceles triangle are equal."

Name the angle you are finding with three letters (angle ABC means the angle at B), and give a reason for each step, not just the last one.

## Common mistakes

- Calling a 180° turn or a 95° angle "reflex"; reflex angles are more than 180°.
- Assuming lines are parallel because they look parallel. Parallel lines are marked with arrows, or the question says so.
- Mixing up alternate (equal) and allied (add to 180°) angles.
- Making the wrong pair equal in an isosceles triangle: the equal angles are at the ends of the base, opposite the equal sides.
- Writing "Z angles" or "F angles" as a reason.
- Finding x and stopping when the question asks for an angle.

## Exam technique

- **"Work out the size of angle ..."**: a numerical answer, usually with working.
- **"Give a reason for your answer"** or **"Give reasons for each stage of your working"**: one reason per step, in full sentences using the names above.
- **"Show that"**: start from the given facts and reach the given value; every step needs a reason.
- **"Diagram NOT accurately drawn"**: never measure with a protractor; calculate.
- Mark each angle you find on the diagram as you go; it shows the examiner your route.
