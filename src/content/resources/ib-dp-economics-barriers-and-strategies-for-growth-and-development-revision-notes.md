## Poverty cycle in one line

```text
Low income -> low saving and low tax revenue -> low investment in capital,
health and education -> low productivity -> low income
```

Any linked set of factors is acceptable. Explain every arrow. Break the cycle with a large push at one point.

## Barriers: economic

| Barrier | One-line effect | Good link to make |
|---|---|---|
| Rising inequality | The poor cannot afford education or start firms | Weak demand, social tension |
| Lack of infrastructure and appropriate technology | Higher costs, cut-off farmers | Technology must suit local skills |
| Low human capital | Low output per worker | Health and education reinforce each other |
| Primary sector dependence | Volatile prices, low value added | Export earnings swing |
| No access to international markets | Cannot reach higher prices or scale | Trade barriers, poor transport |
| Informal economy | Outside tax and legal protection | Lost revenue, small firms |
| Capital flight | Funds leave the country | Political risk, weak currency |
| Indebtedness | Debt service crowds out spending | Debt service ratio |
| Geography, including landlocked | Trade passes through neighbours | Higher transport cost |
| Tropical climate, endemic disease | Poor health, lower yields | Health spending, lost days |

## Barriers: political and social

| Barrier | One-line effect |
|---|---|
| Weak legal system | Contracts hard to enforce, so less investment |
| Ineffective taxation | Low revenue, few public goods |
| Weak banking system | Savings not turned into loans |
| Insecure property rights | No collateral, little incentive to improve |
| Gender inequality | Talent wasted, lower family investment in girls |
| Lack of good governance, corruption | Funds diverted, investors deterred |
| Unequal political power and status | Policy serves a narrow group |

## Strategies

| Strategy | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Import substitution | Builds industries, saves foreign exchange | Inefficiency, higher prices |
| Export promotion | Foreign exchange, scale | Relies on foreign demand, costly |
| Economic integration | Larger market, competition | Some industries lose |
| Diversification | Less exposure to one price | Slow, needs skills and capital |
| Social enterprise | Reaches neglected groups | Small, short of finance |
| Trade liberalisation | Cheaper inputs, competition | Weak firms exposed |
| Privatisation | Efficiency, cash for the state | Monopoly risk, job losses |
| Deregulation | Easier to start firms | Weaker protection |
| Redistribution (taxes, transfers, minimum wages) | Lower inequality, more human capital | Cost, incentives, possible job losses |
| Merit goods (education, health, infrastructure) | Productivity, long-run gains | Slow returns, high cost |
| Inward FDI | Jobs, capital, technology | Profit outflows, dependence |
| Foreign aid (humanitarian, development, ODA, NGOs, debt relief) | Funds projects, frees budgets | Tied, dependence, corruption |
| World Bank and IMF | Finance and advice | Conditions on loans |
| Institutional change | Lower risk, more investment | Slow, hard to enforce |

Institutional change covers: improved access to banking (microfinance, mobile banking), women's empowerment, reducing corruption, property rights and land rights. Infrastructure covers: energy, transport, telecommunications, clean water and sanitation.

## Key definitions

- **ODA:** government aid flows to developing countries.
- **Humanitarian aid:** short-term help in emergencies. **Development aid:** long-term project funding.
- **Debt relief:** cancelling or reducing debt.
- **Microfinance:** small loans and savings services for people excluded from formal banks.
- **Mobile banking:** banking and payments by phone.
- **Appropriate technology:** fits local skills, costs and conditions.
- **Debt service ratio:** debt repayments (interest plus capital) divided by export earnings, as a percentage.

## Method boxes

**Percentage fall in export earnings when one product's price changes** (volume unchanged)
1. Find that product's share of total exports.
2. Multiply the share by the percentage price fall.

Example: copper is 60% of exports and its price falls 40%. Earnings fall by 0.60 x 40 = 24%.

**Debt service ratio**
1. Divide debt service by export earnings.
2. Multiply by 100.

Example: debt service 0.5 billion and exports 2.0 billion gives 25%. After relief cuts debt service to 0.3 billion the ratio is 0.3/2.0 = 15%.

**Revenue forgone through the informal economy**
1. Find informal output as a percentage of GDP: 8/32 = 25%.
2. Apply the average tax rate: 8 x 0.20 = 1.6 billion.

State the assumption that the same rate would apply.

**Evaluating a barrier or strategy**
1. Explain the effect with a chain of reasoning.
2. Give a strength and a limitation.
3. Add a condition: size, time, country context or interaction.
4. Reach a justified judgement.

## Significance, intervention and the SDGs

- **Significance of barriers:** judge by size (how many people or how much output), root or symptom, country context and interaction with other barriers. Landlocked geography matters more in Halvern than in an island country.
- **Intervention against markets:** markets give efficiency, competition and FDI but under-supply merit goods and ignore equity. Governments can fund health, education and infrastructure but may lack capacity or face corruption. The best mix depends on the country's institutions.
- **Sustainable Development Goals:** compare progress toward selected goals in two or more countries. State which goals, which indicators and the starting points.

## Worked reminders

**FDI.** A plant costing 800 million employs 2,000 workers at 5,000 each: wage bill 2,000 x 5,000 = 10 million a year. Profit of 90 million with 70% sent abroad gives 90 x 0.7 = 63 million leaving, 27 million staying.

**Microloan.** A loan of 300 at 12% a year gives interest 300 x 0.12 = 36. Extra income of 40 a month is 480 a year. After interest and repaying the 300, 480 - 36 - 300 = 144 remains.

## Must-know distinctions

- Growth means more output. Development means better lives, including health, education and freedom. One barrier can hurt growth more than development, or the reverse.
- Poverty cycle (the loop) against poverty trap (the situation that is hard to leave).
- Humanitarian aid (emergencies) against development aid (long-term).
- World Bank (development lending and projects) against IMF (lending for balance of payments problems, with advice).
- Import substitution (replace imports) against export promotion (sell abroad).
- Market-based policy (liberalise, privatise, deregulate) against interventionist policy (taxes, transfers, merit goods).
- Symptom against root cause: corruption can be a symptom of weak institutions.

## Quick self-test

1. State two political barriers to development.
2. Halvern exports 8.0 billion; mining is 5.0 billion. Mining prices fall 20%, volume unchanged. Find the fall in total export earnings as a percentage.
3. A country has debt service of 1.2 billion and exports of 4.0 billion. Calculate the debt service ratio.
4. Define Official Development Assistance.
5. Explain one way capital flight lowers growth.
6. Distinguish import substitution from export promotion.
7. State one strength and one limitation of privatisation.
8. Explain why a landlocked country may face higher trade costs.
9. Give two examples of infrastructure named in the guide.
10. State one limitation of foreign aid.
11. A firm in the informal sector makes 6 billion of output in an economy of 24 billion. Find the informal share.
12. Explain how secure land rights might promote development.

### Answers

1. Any two: weak institutional framework, gender inequality, lack of good governance or corruption, unequal political power and status.
2. Mining share is 5.0/8.0 = 62.5%. Fall in earnings is 62.5% x 20% = 12.5%. (Check: 5.0 x 0.8 = 4.0, total becomes 7.0, a fall of 1.0/8.0 = 12.5%.)
3. 1.2/4.0 = 30%.
4. Government aid flows to developing countries to promote their development.
5. Savings leave, so domestic investment falls, capital stock grows slowly and productivity and income grow slowly.
6. Import substitution protects and supports domestic producers so they replace imports; export promotion supports firms that sell abroad to earn foreign exchange.
7. Strength: efficiency gains. Limitation: private monopoly or job losses.
8. Goods must cross neighbours' territory, with delay, fees and dependence on their ports and roads.
9. Any two of energy, transport, telecommunications, clean water, sanitation.
10. It may be tied to donor goods, create dependence or be lost to corruption.
11. 6/24 = 25%.
12. Owners can use land as collateral for loans and gain reason to invest, raising output and incomes.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a barrier without explaining the chain to lower growth or development.
- A poverty cycle drawn as a list of factors with no arrows or explanations.
- Mixing up the debt service ratio with total debt.
- Leaving a calculation without working, or without a stated unit or percentage.
- Treating aid, FDI or privatisation as simply good or simply bad.
- Offering strategies in part (b) of an extended answer with no judgement of which works best and under what conditions.
- Writing a real-world example without explaining how it relates to the question.
- Forgetting to link a strategy to the barrier it addresses.
