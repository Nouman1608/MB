This is A Level content, examined in Paper 4. All data are invented for the exercise. Revise first with the [14.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-plants-cambridge-alevel-biology/) and the [14.2 revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-homeostasis-in-plants-cambridge-alevel-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which event happens first when a stoma opens?

A: Potassium ions leave the guard cells.

B: Hydrogen ions are pumped out of the guard cells.

C: Water is actively transported into the guard cells.

D: Calcium ions enter the cytoplasm of the guard cells. **[1]**

**2.** State two environmental conditions that cause stomata to close. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why the regulation of stomatal aperture is described as a balance. **[2]**

**4.** Which acts as the second messenger when abscisic acid causes stomatal closure?

A: Cyclic AMP

B: Potassium ions

C: Calcium ions

D: Hydrogen ions **[1]**

**5.** Explain how the arrangement of cellulose microfibrils in guard cell walls helps the stoma to open. **[2]**

**6.** A guard cell has a water potential of −900 kPa and its neighbouring epidermal cell −1200 kPa. After the guard cell takes up ions, its water potential is −1500 kPa. State the direction of net water movement before and after ion uptake. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A plant was kept in normal days and nights, then moved to constant darkness at 18:00 on day 1. Stomatal aperture was measured.

| Time | Day 2, 06:00 | Day 2, 12:00 | Day 2, 00:00 | Day 3, 06:00 | Day 3, 12:00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Aperture / µm | 3.0 | 6.5 | 1.0 | 2.5 | 5.5 |

**(a)** Describe the pattern shown by the data. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what the data suggest about the control of stomatal opening. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest an advantage to a plant of opening its stomata before dawn. **[1]**

**8.** Describe and explain how guard cells open a stoma in the light. **[5]**

**9.** Leaf strips were placed in solutions of abscisic acid (ABA) for 30 minutes and stomatal apertures were measured.

| ABA concentration / µmol dm⁻³ | 0 | 1 | 10 | 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Mean aperture / µm | 9.0 | 7.2 | 4.5 | 2.7 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in aperture at 100 µmol dm⁻³ ABA compared with 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how ABA causes the stomata to close. **[4]**

**10.** On a bright, hot, windy day, a plant growing in dry soil closes its stomata around midday. Suggest and explain why. **[3]**

**11.** Leaf strips were treated with ABA in a solution containing a substance that removes free calcium ions from the solution outside the cells. Stomatal closure was much smaller than with ABA alone. Explain this result. **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** Explain why guard cells contain many mitochondria. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why loss of potassium ions from guard cells makes them flaccid. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** Any two from: darkness [1]; water stress or dry soil [1]; high carbon dioxide concentration inside the leaf (accept). Two correct conditions earn both marks.

**3.** Open stomata are needed so that carbon dioxide can diffuse in for photosynthesis [1]. But open stomata also allow water vapour to diffuse out by transpiration, so aperture must be adjusted to limit water loss [1].

**4.** C [1]

**5.** The microfibrils run radially, so the guard cells cannot easily widen when they take in water [1]. Instead they lengthen and, with the thicker inner wall, bow apart, opening the pore [1].

**6.** Before: from the guard cell to the epidermal cell (from −900 kPa to −1200 kPa) [1]. After: from the epidermal cell into the guard cell (from −1200 kPa to −1500 kPa) [1].

**7.**

**(a)** Aperture rises to a peak around midday (6.5 µm, then 5.5 µm) and falls to a minimum at night (1.0 µm) [1]. The cycle repeats about every 24 hours, with slightly smaller peaks on day 3 [1].

**(b)** Stomata continue to open and close in constant darkness, so the rhythm is not caused by light itself [1]. It must be controlled by an internal timing mechanism with a period of about 24 hours [1].

**(c)** Stomata are already open when light arrives, so CO₂ can enter and photosynthesis can start immediately [1].

**8.** Proton pumps in the guard cell membranes actively transport H⁺ out of the cells, using ATP [1]. The inside becomes more negative, so K⁺ channels open and K⁺ diffuses into the guard cells [1]. The guard cells' water potential falls below that of neighbouring cells [1]. Water enters the guard cells by osmosis and they become turgid [1]. The thicker inner wall and radial microfibrils make the cells bow apart, so the stoma opens [1].

**9.**

**(a)** (9.0 − 2.7) ÷ 9.0 × 100 [1] = **70 %** [1]

**(b)** ABA binds to receptors on the guard cell surface membrane [1]. Calcium ions enter the cytoplasm and act as a second messenger [1]. Channels open so K⁺ and anions leave, and the proton pumps are inhibited [1]. The guard cells' water potential rises, water leaves by osmosis, the cells become flaccid and the stoma closes [1].

**10.** High temperature, wind and dry soil mean water is lost by transpiration faster than it can be replaced from the soil [1]. The plant is under water stress, so ABA is produced [1]. Closing the stomata reduces water loss and prevents wilting, even though it limits CO₂ uptake and photosynthesis [1].

**11.** ABA normally causes calcium ions to enter the cytoplasm, partly from outside the cell [1]. Removing external Ca²⁺ means less Ca²⁺ enters, so the second messenger signal is weaker [1]. Fewer ion channels open, so fewer ions leave and less water leaves by osmosis; some closure still happens because Ca²⁺ is also released from internal stores such as the vacuole [1].

**12.**

**(a)** Proton pumps use ATP to move H⁺ out of the cells [1]. Mitochondria supply this ATP by aerobic respiration [1].

**(b)** Losing ions raises the water potential of the guard cells above that of neighbouring cells [1]. Water leaves by osmosis, so the cells lose turgor and become flaccid [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that water is actively transported into guard cells.
- Saying K⁺ is pumped out when stomata open.
- Naming ABA as the second messenger.
- Giving only one side of the CO₂ and water trade-off.
- Describing a rhythm in constant darkness without saying it shows internal control.
- Calculating percentage change from the wrong starting value.
