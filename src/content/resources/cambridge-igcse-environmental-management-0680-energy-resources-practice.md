## Section A

**1.** Classify each energy resource as renewable or non-renewable: nuclear power, biogas, natural gas, tidal power. **[4]**

**2.** Explain why coal is classified as a finite resource. **[2]**

**3.** State what biogas is produced from and how it is produced. **[2]**

**4.** Name the device used to generate electricity directly from sunlight, and state how it differs from the way most power stations generate electricity. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** factors that cause energy demand to rise in a country that is industrialising. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the formation of coal. **[4]**

**7.** Describe how oil and natural gas form and how they come to be trapped in a reservoir underground. **[5]**

**8.** Nuclear power provides electricity in many countries.

**(a)** Describe how a nuclear power station generates electricity. **[3]**

**(b)** Give **one** benefit and **one** limitation of nuclear power. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows electricity generation in Country X in two years. The data are invented for practice.

| Resource | Year 1 (TWh) | Year 10 (TWh) |
|---|---|---|
| Coal | 120 | 80 |
| Natural gas | 40 | 60 |
| HEP | 30 | 30 |
| Wind | 10 | 50 |
| Solar | 0 | 20 |
| **Total** | **200** | **240** |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of electricity generated from renewable resources in Year 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of electricity generated from renewable resources in Year 10. Give your answer to one decimal place. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the change in electricity generated from coal between Year 1 and Year 10. Use figures in your answer. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest **two** reasons why total electricity generation increased. **[2]**

**10.** Compare the ways in which HEP and tidal power generate electricity. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how disruption to supply and scarcity of resources can each affect the demand for an energy resource. **[4]**

**12.** A small island lies in a tectonically active area, has strong winds for most of the year and imports all of its oil by ship. Discuss the benefits and limitations of geothermal power and wind power as ways for the island to generate its electricity. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Nuclear power: non-renewable [1]. Biogas: renewable [1]. Natural gas: non-renewable [1]. Tidal power: renewable [1].

**2.** Coal formed from plant remains over millions of years [1], but it is being used up much faster than it is replaced [1].

**3.** From animal manure, food waste or sewage [1], broken down by bacteria in the absence of oxygen (anaerobic digestion) [1].

**4.** Photovoltaic (solar) cells [1]; they produce electricity directly from light, without a turbine and generator [1].

**5.** Any two: growth of industry, such as manufacturing [1]; rising personal and national wealth [1]; more transport [1]; population growth [1].

**6.** Any four, in a logical sequence:
- Plants grew in warm, swampy forests [1].
- When they died, they fell into waterlogged ground with little oxygen, so they did not fully decay [1].
- Layers of partly decayed plant material built up as peat [1].
- The peat was buried under sediment and compressed [1].
- Heat and pressure over millions of years drove out water and gases, raising the carbon content and forming coal [1].

**7.** Any five:
- Tiny marine organisms (plankton) died and sank to the sea floor [1].
- They were buried in mud with little oxygen, so they did not fully decay [1].
- More sediment was deposited on top, increasing heat and pressure [1].
- Over millions of years, the organic remains changed into oil and natural gas [1].
- Oil and gas are less dense than water, so they moved upwards through permeable rock [1].
- They were trapped below a layer of impermeable rock, collecting in the permeable rock as a reservoir, with gas above the oil [1].

**8. (a)** Uranium fuel undergoes nuclear fission in a reactor, releasing heat [1]; the heat turns water into steam [1]; the steam turns a turbine connected to a generator [1].

**(b)** Benefit, any one: very little carbon dioxide released while operating; large, continuous output [1]. Limitation, any one: radioactive waste stays dangerous for a very long time; high cost of building and decommissioning; risk of accidents; uranium is finite [1].

**9. (a)** (30 + 10 + 0) ÷ 200 × 100 [1] = 20% [1].

**(b)** (30 + 50 + 20) ÷ 240 × 100 = 100 ÷ 240 × 100 [1] = 41.7% [1].

**(c)** Coal generation fell [1] from 120 TWh to 80 TWh, a fall of 40 TWh (one third) [1].

**(d)** Any two: population growth [1]; rising wealth, so more appliances and air conditioning [1]; growth of industry [1]; more electric vehicles or electrified transport [1].

**10.** Similarity: both use moving water to turn turbines connected to generators [1]. Differences, any two: HEP uses water stored behind a dam in a reservoir and released downhill, whereas tidal power uses the rise and fall of the sea [1]; HEP output can be controlled by releasing water when needed, whereas tidal output follows the timing of the tides [1]; HEP is built on rivers inland, tidal power in estuaries or coastal waters [1].

**11.** Disruption to supply: an event such as a war, natural disaster or strike cuts the supply [1], prices rise and consumers are forced to use less of the resource or switch to another [1]. Scarcity of resources: as a resource runs low, it becomes more expensive to obtain [1], so demand for it falls and demand for alternatives, such as renewables, rises [1].

**12.** Levels-based. Credit up to 6 from:
- **Geothermal benefits**: the tectonically active location means hot rocks are close to the surface [1]; continuous output day and night, unlike wind [1]; ends dependence on imported oil, improving energy security [1].
- **Geothermal limitations**: high cost of drilling and building a power station for a small island [1]; risk from earthquakes or eruptions damaging the plant [1].
- **Wind benefits**: strong winds for most of the year give high output [1]; turbines are quicker and cheaper to install in small numbers [1].
- **Wind limitations**: intermittent, so back-up or battery storage is needed on calm days [1]; visual and noise impacts on a small island, and possible storm damage [1].
- **Judgement**: for example, geothermal for steady supply with wind as a supplement, linked to the island's features [1].

Maximum 6; an answer that discusses only one resource scores no more than 3.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 1**: nuclear power classified as renewable because it emits little carbon dioxide.
- **Question 6**: omitting the waterlogged, low-oxygen conditions, which explain why the plants did not decay.
- **Question 7**: describing formation but not the trapping, which needs both permeable and impermeable rock.
- **Question 9(b)**: forgetting solar, or rounding to 42% when one decimal place was asked for.
- **Question 10**: describing each resource separately without a comparison word such as "whereas" or "both".
