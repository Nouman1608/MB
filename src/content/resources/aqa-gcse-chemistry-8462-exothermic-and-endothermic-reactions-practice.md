These questions cover section **4.5.1 Exothermic and endothermic reactions** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 1. Questions 8, 9 and 10 use
bond energies, which the specification marks (HT only), and are labelled **Higher tier only**. Question 6 is
based on required practical 4.

Learn the content first in the
[exothermic and endothermic reactions study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-exothermic-and-endothermic-reactions/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-exothermic-and-endothermic-reactions-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Three reactions are carried out in test tubes. The temperature of the mixture is measured before and after.

| Reaction | Start / °C | End / °C |
|---|---|---|
| A: zinc with copper sulfate solution | 20.5 | 28.0 |
| B: citric acid with sodium hydrogencarbonate solution | 21.0 | 14.5 |
| C: sodium hydroxide with hydrochloric acid | 20.0 | 26.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the temperature change for reaction B. **[1]**
**(b)** State which reactions are exothermic and explain how you know. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe what happens to energy in reaction B. **[2]**

**2.** Give **one** example of each of the following.

**(a)** An exothermic reaction **[1]**
**(b)** An endothermic reaction **[1]**
**(c)** An everyday use of an exothermic reaction **[1]**

**3.** A sports injury pack contains substances that react when the pack is squeezed. The pack gets cold.

**(a)** State the type of reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of this kind of pack compared with an ice pack from a freezer. **[2]**

**4.** A reaction profile for an exothermic reaction has reactants at 120 kJ/mol, a peak at 200 kJ/mol and products at 70 kJ/mol.

**(a)** Calculate the activation energy. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the overall energy change, with a sign. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how you would draw the reaction profile, including labels. **[3]**

**5.** A reaction needs a flame to start but then keeps going and gives out heat. Explain, using activation energy, why it needs a flame to start. State whether it is exothermic or endothermic. **[3]**

**6.** A student investigates the temperature change when magnesium powder is added to copper sulfate solution.

**(a)** Describe a method. Include how to reduce heat loss. **[4]**
**(b)** Identify the independent variable, the dependent variable and **two** control variables for an investigation into how concentration affects the temperature change. **[3]**
**(c)** The student gets a rise of 31.5 °C in one run and 29.0 °C in a repeat. Suggest why the results differ. **[1]**

**7.** Hand warmers contain iron powder that reacts with oxygen to form iron oxide.

**(a)** Explain why the warmer gets hot. **[1]**
**(b)** Evaluate the use of a disposable hand warmer on a winter walk, compared with a reusable warmer that is heated in boiling water. **[4]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Ammonia is made by the reaction N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃.
Bond energies in kJ/mol: N≡N 945, H–H 436, N–H 391.

**(a)** Calculate the total energy needed to break the bonds in the reactants. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total energy released when the bonds in the products form. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the overall energy change and state whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Ethene reacts with hydrogen: H₂C=CH₂ + H–H → CH₃–CH₃.
Bond energies in kJ/mol: C=C 614, C–C 347, H–H 436, C–H 413.
Calculate the overall energy change and state what it shows about the reaction. **[4]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Water can be split by electrical energy: 2H₂O → 2H₂ + O₂.
Bond energies in kJ/mol: O–H 464, H–H 436, O=O 498.

**(a)** Calculate the overall energy change for the reaction. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain, in terms of bond breaking and bond making, why the reaction is endothermic. **[2]**

**11.** A student draws a reaction profile for an endothermic reaction with the products lower than the reactants. A teacher says the diagram is wrong.

**(a)** Explain the error. **[1]**
**(b)** Describe the changes needed to make the diagram correct. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the student should add to show the activation energy. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 14.5 − 21.0 = **−6.5 °C** (a fall of 6.5 °C) [1]
**(b)** **A and C** [1], because the **temperature of the surroundings rises** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Energy is **taken in from the surroundings** [1], so the products have **more energy** than the reactants / the temperature falls [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** Any one: combustion; neutralisation; oxidation of a metal; displacement of copper by zinc [1]
**(b)** Any one: thermal decomposition of calcium carbonate; citric acid with sodium hydrogencarbonate [1]
**(c)** Any one: self-heating can; hand warmer; fuel burning in a heater [1]

**3. (a)** **Endothermic** [1]
**(b)** Advantage: works instantly without needing a freezer / portable [1]. Disadvantage: single use (cannot be refrozen) / cools for a limited time / creates waste [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** 200 − 120 = **80 kJ/mol** [1]
**(b)** 70 − 120 = [1] **−50 kJ/mol** (exothermic) [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Any three [1] each: **axes** labelled energy and progress of reaction; reactants level at 120 and products lower at 70; a **smooth curve** over a peak at 200; arrow for **activation energy** from reactants up to the peak; arrow for **overall energy change** from reactants down to products. **[3]**

**5.** The particles need a minimum energy (the **activation energy**) to react when they collide [1]; the flame provides the **energy to get over** the activation energy [1]. The reaction is **exothermic**, because it gives out heat [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Needing a flame does not mean a reaction is endothermic. The overall energy change depends on the products' energy compared with the reactants.

**6. (a)** Any four [1] each: measure a fixed volume of copper sulfate solution into a **polystyrene cup**; record the **start temperature**; add a measured mass of magnesium powder; **stir**; record the **highest temperature**; use a **lid** and place the cup in a beaker to reduce heat loss; **calculate the change**. **[4]**
**(b)** Independent: **concentration** of copper sulfate [1]. Dependent: **temperature change** [1]. Any two controls [1]: volume of solution, mass of magnesium, same type of container, same start temperature, particle size of magnesium. **[3]**
**(c)** Heat losses differed / measurement error / the powder did not react completely each time / reading the thermometer inaccurately [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** The reaction is **exothermic**: it transfers energy to the surroundings [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Any four [1] each. Disposable: no heating needed so convenient on a walk; works immediately; single use so wasteful; costs more over time. Reusable: needs a source of boiling water, which is not available outdoors; can be used many times so less waste; takes time to recharge. A judgement is needed, such as disposable for a one-off walk and reusable for regular use. **[4]**

**8. (a)** N≡N: 945; 3 H–H: 3 × 436 = 1308 [1]. Total = **2253 kJ/mol** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** 2NH₃ contains 6 N–H bonds: 6 × 391 [1] = **2346 kJ/mol** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** 2253 − 2346 = **−93 kJ/mol** [1]; **exothermic** as more energy is released than is taken in [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each NH₃ has three N–H bonds, so two molecules have six.

**9.** Broken: C=C + H–H = 614 + 436 = 1050 [1]. Formed: C–C + 2 C–H = 347 + 826 = 1173 [1]. Energy change = 1050 − 1173 = **−123 kJ/mol** [1]. The reaction is **exothermic** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** Broken: 4 O–H = 4 × 464 = 1856 [1]. Formed: 2 H–H + O=O = 872 + 498 = 1370 [1]. Energy change = 1856 − 1370 = **+486 kJ/mol** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** More energy is needed to **break** the bonds in the reactants (1856) than is released on **forming** the bonds in the products (1370) [1], so energy is **taken in** overall [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** For an endothermic reaction the products must be **higher** than the reactants [1].
**(b)** Raise the products' level above the reactants [1]; make the overall energy change arrow point **upwards** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** An arrow from the reactants' level up to the **peak** of the curve [1]. **[1]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing exothermic and endothermic the wrong way round, or describing the energy of the reaction rather than of the surroundings.
- Drawing the activation energy arrow from the products, or leaving it out.
- Reversing the subtraction in bond energy sums, so the sign is wrong.
- Counting too few bonds in molecules such as ammonia and water.
- Leaving out a judgement in an evaluation question.
