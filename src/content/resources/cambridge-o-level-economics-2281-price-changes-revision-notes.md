Quick recall for section 2.6 **Price changes** of the Cambridge O Level Economics 2281 syllabus for examination in 2026: "changing market conditions as causes of price changes" and their "consequences for equilibrium price and sales", shown on demand and supply diagrams. Full explanations are in the [price changes study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-price-changes/); test yourself with the [price changes practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-economics-2281-price-changes-practice/). All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market conditions | The conditions of demand and supply, other than the product's own price |
| Increase in demand or supply | A shift of the curve to the right: more at every price |
| Decrease in demand or supply | A shift of the curve to the left: less at every price |
| Extension / contraction | A movement along a curve caused by a change in the product's own price |
| New equilibrium | Where the shifted curve meets the curve that did not move |
| Substitute | A product used instead of another; a price rise for one raises demand for the other |
| Complement | A product used with another; a price rise for one lowers demand for the other |
| Derived demand | Demand for a factor, such as labour, that comes from demand for the product it makes |

## The routine

1. Name the market.
2. Buyers or sellers affected? Demand or supply shifts.
3. Direction: right (increase) or left (decrease).
4. At the old price: shortage (price rises) or surplus (price falls).
5. State new price **and** new sales; describe the adjustment along the curve that did not move.

## Four single shifts

| Shift | At old price | Price | Sales |
|---|---|---|---|
| Demand right | Shortage | Up | Up |
| Demand left | Surplus | Down | Down |
| Supply right | Surplus | Down | Up |
| Supply left | Shortage | Up | Down |

## Two shifts at once

| Demand | Supply | Certain | Uncertain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Right | Right | Sales up | Price |
| Left | Left | Sales down | Price |
| Right | Left | Price up | Sales |
| Left | Right | Price down | Sales |

Equal shifts in the same direction leave the price unchanged.

## Tricky causes

- **Wages**: a cost to the seller (supply shifts) or income for the buyer (demand shifts)? Decide which group earns them.
- **Taxes per unit on a product**: supply of that product decreases; demand for its complements falls.
- **Price of a related good**: changes demand (substitutes, complements) or supply (alternative products a firm could make).
- **Weather**: shifts supply of crops; may also shift demand for products such as umbrellas or ice cream.

## Worked reminder

Market for cooking oil: Qd = 240 − 20P and Qs = 40P − 120.

- Original: 360 = 60P, so P = $6 and Q = 120 litres.
- A rise in seed costs cuts supply by 60 at every price: Qs = 40P − 180.
- New: 420 = 60P, so P = $7 and Q = 240 − 140 = 100 litres.
- Price change: 1 ÷ 6 × 100 = +16.7%. Sales change: −20 ÷ 120 × 100 = −16.7%.
- Spending: 6 × 120 = $720 before; 7 × 100 = $700 after.

## Consequences beyond price and sales

- Consumers: pay more or less; some leave or join the market.
- Producers: revenue and profit change; profit attracts entry, losses cause exit.
- Workers: derived demand, so jobs follow sales.
- Related markets: substitutes and complements move.
- Government: revenue from per-unit taxes follows sales.
- Resources: move towards products whose prices rise.

## Exam traps

- Shifting supply for a buyer-side event, or demand for a seller-side event.
- Shifting a curve because the product's own price changed.
- Claiming a definite price effect when both curves move the same way with no sizes given.
- Forgetting to state the effect on sales.
- Omitting the shortage or surplus at the old price.
- Analysing the market where the event happened rather than the market the question names.

## Quick self-test

1. What is meant by "changing market conditions" in this section?
2. A disease kills many chickens. What happens to the price and sales of eggs?
3. The price of tea rises. What happens to the demand for coffee and to its equilibrium price?
4. The price of printers falls. What happens to the equilibrium price of ink cartridges?
5. Demand rises and supply falls. Which of price and sales is certain?
6. Demand and supply both rise by the same amount at every price. What happens to price?
7. A rise in incomes increases demand for holidays. At the old price, is there a shortage or a surplus?
8. Using the worked reminder, calculate the percentage fall in sales.
9. Why does a fall in demand for furniture affect carpenters' jobs?
10. Higher wages for factory workers in the shoe industry: which curve shifts in the shoe market?
11. State one consequence for the government of a fall in sales of a taxed product.

### Answers

1. Changes in the conditions of demand or supply other than the product's own price, such as incomes, tastes, costs or technology.
2. Supply of eggs decreases (shifts left), so price rises and sales fall.
3. Demand for coffee (a substitute) increases, so its equilibrium price rises and sales rise.
4. Demand for ink (a complement) increases, so its price rises.
5. Price: it must rise. The effect on sales depends on which shift is larger.
6. It stays the same, while sales rise.
7. A shortage, so the price rises.
8. (120 − 100) ÷ 120 × 100 = 16.7%.
9. Demand for carpenters is derived from demand for furniture, so fewer carpenters are needed.
10. Supply of shoes shifts left, because wages are a cost to shoe makers.
11. Revenue from the tax falls, because fewer units are taxed.
