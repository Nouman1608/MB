> rebuilt course (launched February 2025): the four options described below
> are replaced by four contexts studied at both SL and HL (one of which
> absorbs what was the abnormal psychology option -- the IB itself
> describes abnormal psychology as "renamed" to health and well-being);

> first-assessment-2027 course does not yet exist on this site.

At the core of IB Diploma Programme Psychology (first assessment 2019)
is an introduction to three different approaches to understanding
behaviour: the biological, cognitive and sociocultural approaches.
Students study and critically evaluate the knowledge, concepts,
theories and research that have developed understanding in these
fields. The IB's 2019 subject brief describes the interaction of these
approaches as the basis of a holistic and integrated approach to
understanding mental processes and behaviour, understood through four
options in applied psychology (abnormal psychology, developmental
psychology, health psychology and the psychology of relationships).
The course also promotes an understanding of qualitative and
quantitative research methods, used both to reflect critically on
evidence and to design, implement, analyse and evaluate students' own
investigations.

## Aims

The course aims for students to:

- develop an understanding of the biological, cognitive and
  sociocultural factors affecting mental processes and behaviour
- apply an understanding of the biological, cognitive and
  sociocultural factors affecting mental processes and behaviour to
  at least one applied area of study
- understand diverse methods of inquiry
- understand the importance of ethical practice in psychological
  research in general and observe ethical practice in their own
  inquiries
- ensure that ethical practices are upheld in all psychological
  inquiry and discussion
- develop an awareness of how psychological research can be applied
  to address real-world problems and promote positive change.

## How it's assessed

DP Psychology is assessed differently at SL and HL. At SL, students sit two
external papers -- Paper 1 (2 hours: three short-answer questions on the
core approaches to understanding behaviour, plus one essay from a choice of
three) worth 50% of the final grade, and Paper 2 (1 hour: one essay-style
question on a single option, chosen from abnormal, developmental, health or
relationships psychology) worth 25%. External assessment totals 75% of the
SL grade. At HL, students sit the same Paper 1 (2 hours, reweighted to
40%), but a substantially longer Paper 2: because HL students study *two*
options rather than one, HL Paper 2 runs 2 hours and requires two
essay-style questions -- one from each of the two studied options --
rather than SL's single essay on a single option, and is worth 20%. HL
students also sit a third paper (1 hour: three short-answer questions on
research methodology) worth 20%, bringing external assessment to 80% of
the HL grade across three papers totalling 5 hours, compared with SL's two
papers totalling 3 hours.

The remaining 25% (SL) or 20% (HL) is internal assessment: an experimental
study, in which students design, carry out (usually in a small group),
analyse and individually write up a simple experiment, then evaluate it and
suggest modifications. This component is externally moderated by IB
examiners rather than marked solely by the student's own teacher.

The four options -- abnormal psychology, developmental psychology, health
psychology, and psychology of human relationships -- give students a
chance to apply what they have learned about the biological, cognitive
and sociocultural approaches to a specific area of applied psychology.
Across all assessment components, students are expected to progress
through four assessment objectives: knowledge and comprehension of
specified content; application and analysis of psychological theories and
research; synthesis and evaluation of that research; and selection and use
of skills appropriate to psychology, including the design, conduct and
ethical practice of their own investigations.

At HL, students are additionally expected to analyse qualitative and
quantitative research methods and to evaluate research scenarios from a
methodological and ethical perspective -- reflecting the extra breadth
that comes with the HL-only Paper 3 on approaches to research.

## The three core approaches

Every DP Psychology student studies three core approaches to
understanding behaviour, and they are not alternatives to choose
between: the biological approach, examining the relationship between
the brain and behaviour, hormones and pheromones and their effects on
behaviour, the relationship between genetics and behaviour and, at HL
only, the role of animal research in understanding human behaviour; the
cognitive approach, examining how mental processes such as memory,
perception and thinking shape behaviour; and the sociocultural
approach, examining how social and cultural context shapes both
behaviour and mental processes. Many topics within the core are
deliberately revisited from more than one approach -- memory, for
example, can be examined biologically through brain structures,
cognitively through information-processing models, and socioculturally
through the influence of schema and culture on recall -- and the
course expects students to recognise a given topic from multiple
angles rather than filing it under a single approach only. Approaches
to researching behaviour is taught alongside these three content
approaches rather than as a separate block, covering the qualitative
and quantitative methods psychologists use to test their ideas, and
is examined specifically in Paper 3 at HL, separately from the content
approaches tested in Paper 1.

## The four options

Unlike the compulsory core, students study only one option at SL and
two options at HL, chosen from abnormal psychology (the definition,
explanation and treatment of psychological disorders), developmental
psychology (how cognition, behaviour and identity change across the
lifespan), health psychology (the psychological factors influencing
health, illness and healthcare behaviour), and psychology of human
relationships (how relationships form, function and sometimes end).
Because HL Paper 2 requires one essay question from each of the two
studied options, HL students should confirm with their teacher exactly
which two options their own course has covered, since this genuinely
differs between schools and the exam paper's structure follows that
difference directly rather than allowing a choice on the day.

## Preparing studies for evaluation, not just recall

Across all three core approaches, Paper 1's essay question specifically
rewards evaluation rather than accurate description alone, so
preparation for any given study should include not just what it found
but its methodology and at least one genuine evaluative point -- a
strength or limitation of how it was conducted. This applies just as
much to the chosen option as to the compulsory core: because only one
option is studied at SL, the studies and theories within it require
the same depth of evaluative preparation as the core, rather than a
lighter treatment on the assumption that a single option needs less
rigour than three compulsory approaches.

## The internal assessment and research methodology

The experimental study that makes up the internal assessment is most
often let down not by weak content knowledge but by insufficient
ethical planning and an under-justified choice of experimental design,
so the research-methodology content covered within "approaches to
researching behaviour" applies directly to a student's own
investigation rather than functioning only as separate exam content.
Students who can explain why they chose their specific experimental
design, and what its ethical considerations were, with the same rigour
they would apply to evaluating a published study, tend to score more
highly than those who describe their procedure without justifying the
choices behind it.
