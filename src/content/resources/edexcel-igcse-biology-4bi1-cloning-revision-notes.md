These revision notes cover sub-topic 5(d), **Cloning** (specification points 5.17B to 5.20B), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. ## Key definitions

- **Clone:** an organism or cell that is genetically identical to its parent.
- **Explant:** a small piece of tissue taken from a plant to start a culture.
- **In vitro:** in a laboratory container, not in the whole organism.
- **Micropropagation (tissue culture):** making many plants from small pieces of tissue grown on sterile nutrient medium.
- **Aseptic technique:** working so that no unwanted microorganisms enter.
- **Enucleated:** having had the nucleus removed.
- **Diploid:** having two sets of chromosomes, as in body cells.
- **Transgenic:** containing genes from a different species.
- **Surrogate mother:** an animal in which the embryo develops.

## 5.17B Micropropagation: the stages

1. Choose a parent plant with the wanted characteristics.
2. Cut small explants (often from a shoot tip).
3. Sterilise the explants and the equipment.
4. Place the explants on sterile nutrient medium with growth substances.
5. Cells divide by mitosis; shoots and roots form (plantlets).
6. Move the plantlets to compost and acclimatise them.

## 5.18B Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Many plants quickly from little tissue | No variation, so one disease can affect all plants |
| Genetically identical, so useful features are kept | Costly: sterile lab, trained staff |
| Possible all year round | Contamination can ruin a whole batch |
| Can give disease-free plants | |
| Saves rare species | |

## 5.19B Cloning a mammal (Dolly)

1. Take a **body cell** (diploid nucleus) from the donor sheep.
2. Take an **egg cell** from another sheep and **remove its nucleus**.
3. Put the donor nucleus into the empty egg cell; an electric shock fuses them and starts division.
4. The cell divides by mitosis to form an **embryo**.
5. Implant the embryo into a **surrogate mother**.
6. The lamb is born **genetically identical to the nucleus donor**.

Three sheep are involved: the **nucleus donor** (the clone looks like this one), the **egg donor** (gives only the egg cytoplasm) and the **surrogate** (carries the embryo).

## 5.20B Cloned transgenic animals

1. A human gene is put into an animal cell by genetic engineering.
2. The modified nucleus is used to clone animals (as for Dolly).
3. Each animal carries the human gene and makes the **human protein**, usually in its milk.
4. The protein is extracted and purified for use as a medicine.
5. Cloning gives a herd of identical animals, so the supply is large and reliable.

## Two calculations to know

**Multiplication in micropropagation:** number after n cycles = (shoots per cycle)ⁿ.
Example: 3 shoots per cycle for 4 cycles gives 3⁴ = 81.

**Time needed:** cycles × weeks per cycle.
Example: 4 cycles of 3 weeks take 12 weeks.

## Method in steps: describing how Dolly was produced

1. Name the donor of the nucleus and say the nucleus is diploid.
2. Say that the egg cell's own nucleus was removed.
3. Say the nucleus was put into the empty egg and the cell was stimulated to divide.
4. Say the embryo was implanted in a surrogate mother.
5. Say the lamb is genetically identical to the nucleus donor.

## Answer frame: "Explain why cloned plants are genetically identical"

They are produced from the parent's cells by **mitosis** only, with no fusion of gametes, so there is no mixing of genes. All the cells have the same genes as the parent.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Cloning vs selective breeding:** one parent's genes copied vs two parents' genes mixed.
- **Cloning vs genetic modification:** no new gene added vs a gene added.
- **Nucleus donor vs surrogate:** genes come from the donor, development happens in the surrogate.
- **Explant vs plantlet:** the starting piece vs the small plant that grows from it.
- **Haploid vs diploid:** one set of chromosomes (gametes) vs two sets (body cells).

## Quick self-test

1. What is meant by a clone?
2. Why is aseptic technique used in micropropagation?
3. What type of cell division produces the plantlets?
4. Give one advantage of micropropagation.
5. Give one disadvantage of micropropagation.
6. Why was the nucleus removed from the egg cell in the Dolly method?
7. Is the donor nucleus haploid or diploid?
8. Which sheep was Dolly genetically identical to?
9. Why are cloned transgenic animals useful for making human proteins?
10. One explant gives 3 shoots per 3 weeks. Calculate the plantlets after 4 cycles.

### Answers

1. An organism genetically identical to its parent.
2. To stop bacteria and fungi growing on the nutrient medium.
3. Mitosis.
4. Any one of: many plants quickly, identical plants, all year round, disease-free plants.
5. Any one of: no variation, costly, contamination risk.
6. So that all the genes come from the donor nucleus.
7. Diploid.
8. The sheep that donated the nucleus (the Finn Dorset sheep).
9. The animals make the protein in their milk, and cloning gives many identical animals.
10. 3⁴ = **81 plantlets**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that Dolly's genes came from the surrogate or the egg donor.
- Forgetting that the egg cell's nucleus is removed.
- Calling the nucleus haploid.
- Missing out aseptic technique.
- Describing cloning as adding a gene.
- Not writing that the new plants are genetically identical.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a clone exactly the same as the parent?**
It has the same genes, but environment can still affect features such as size.

**Why is micropropagation used for orchids?**
Orchid seeds are difficult to grow. Micropropagation produces many identical plants from small pieces of tissue.

**Is cloning a mammal efficient?**
No. Many embryos fail, so the success rate is low.
