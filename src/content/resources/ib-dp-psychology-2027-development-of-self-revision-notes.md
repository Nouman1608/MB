These notes condense the **development of self** strand of the human development context (SL and HL, first assessment 2027). The guide lists four areas: attachment, enculturation of social norms, peer influence and childhood experiences. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-development-of-self/), then try the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-development-of-self-practice/).

## The four areas at a glance

| Area | Learning objective (summarised) | Key idea | Example evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Attachment | Role in the development of self | Internal working model; secure and insecure types | Harlow; Ainsworth's Strange Situation; Minnesota longitudinal study |
| Enculturation of social norms | Role in the development of self | Learning your own culture's norms shapes self-concept | Markus and Kitayama (1991) |
| Peer influence | Role in the development of self | Comparison, conformity, social learning | Gardner and Steinberg (2005); group socialisation theory |
| Childhood experiences | Role in the development of self | Adversity, deprivation, protective factors, self-control | Felitti et al. (1998); Mischel and later replication |

## Key definitions

- **Self-concept:** beliefs a person holds about themselves.
- **Self-esteem:** how positively a person evaluates themselves.
- **Identity:** the roles, values and group memberships a person identifies with.
- **Attachment:** a strong emotional bond between infant and caregiver.
- **Internal working model:** expectations about relationships formed from early experience.
- **Enculturation:** learning the norms and values of your own culture.
- **Self-construal:** how a person sees the self in relation to others (independent or interdependent).
- **Social comparison:** judging yourself against others.
- **Normative influence:** conforming to be accepted.
- **Resilience:** positive adaptation despite adversity.
- **Retrospective data:** information recalled about the past.

## Attachment

- **Bowlby:** infants are prepared to attach; the internal working model carries into later relationships.
- **Harlow:** infant monkeys preferred a cloth mother to a wire mother that supplied milk, so contact comfort matters. Animal study with ethical problems.
- **Ainsworth:** Strange Situation types: secure, insecure-avoidant, insecure-resistant.
- **Link to self:** secure attachment supports "I am worth caring for; others are reliable."
- **Evaluate:** artificial procedure; temperament and culture affect classification; mostly American samples; determinism is too strong because people change.
- **Concepts:** causality, measurement, bias, change.

## Enculturation of social norms

- **Mechanisms:** observation, reinforcement, instruction, language, school.
- **Markus and Kitayama (1991):** independent self in many Western settings; interdependent self in many East Asian settings.
- **Example:** praise for individual achievement versus praise for helping the group builds different self-concepts.
- **Evaluate:** helpful for explaining variation; the contrast is a simplification; individuals vary; globalisation mixes messages; etic questionnaires can impose Western meanings.
- **Do not confuse** with acculturation (change after contact between cultures).
- **Concepts:** perspective, bias, measurement.

## Peer influence

| Process | How it shapes the self |
|---|---|
| Social comparison | Judging abilities and appearance against friends |
| Conformity | Adopting group norms to belong |
| Social learning | Copying peers' behaviour and attitudes |
| Looking-glass effect | Seeing yourself as others see you |

- **Gardner and Steinberg (2005):** adolescents took more risks in a driving simulation when friends were present.
- **Group socialisation theory:** peers matter at least as much as parents for behaviour; controversial.
- **Evaluate:** experiments show a causal effect; selection effects (we choose similar friends); Western samples; lab tasks may not predict real behaviour.
- **Concepts:** causality, change, responsibility.

## Childhood experiences

- **Adversity (Felitti et al., 1998):** more adverse childhood experiences linked to more health and emotional difficulties. Retrospective and correlational.
- **Deprivation:** prolonged early deprivation linked to lasting problems, but many children recovered.
- **Self-control (Mischel):** longer waiting at age four linked to later outcomes; the later large study by Watts, Duncan and Quan (2018) found a much weaker link after controlling for background.
- **Protective factors:** a supportive adult, good friends and opportunities reduce harm.
- **Evaluate:** longitudinal designs are strong; confounds such as income and genes; memory bias; ethics of researching distress.
- **Concepts:** causality, change, responsibility, measurement.

## Linking the four areas

Secure attachment encourages exploration and peer relationships; culture decides which behaviours are valued; peers give feedback that confirms or changes the self-concept; overall experience, including adversity or support, shapes the result. The four interact, and each is moderated by temperament and later chances to change.

## Concept links to prepare

- **Causality:** correlational designs with confounds; selection effects in peer studies.
- **Change:** can early patterns change? What produces resilience?
- **Measurement:** the Strange Situation, self-report scales of self-esteem, retrospective surveys.
- **Bias:** cultural bias in attachment and self-concept measures; Western samples.
- **Perspective:** biological (temperament), cognitive (internal working model, schemas) and sociocultural (enculturation, peers).
- **Responsibility:** ethics of animal and child research; stigma attached to labels such as "insecure".

## A model answer plan (Section B style)

Scenario: a 13-year-old moves schools, is mocked by new classmates and starts to describe herself as "weird".
1. Name the areas: peer influence (social comparison, looking-glass effect).
2. Apply to details, such as her changing self-description.
3. Add a second area: earlier secure attachment may buffer the effect (childhood experiences).
4. Evaluate: self-reports at one point cannot show whether the change lasts.

## Common mistakes

- Presenting attachment type as a fixed fate.
- Mixing up enculturation and acculturation.
- Treating cultural dimensions as facts about every person.
- Describing a study but not linking it to the self.
- Using only one of the four areas in a "development of self" question.

## Self-test

1. What is an internal working model?
2. What did Harlow's monkey experiment suggest about attachment?
3. Define enculturation.
4. Give one limitation of the Strange Situation.
5. What did Gardner and Steinberg (2005) find about adolescents and peers?
6. Why is Felitti et al. (1998) open to memory bias?
7. What did the later large study of delay of gratification suggest about the earlier findings?

**Answers:** 1. A set of expectations about relationships and the worth of the self, formed from early caregiving. 2. Comfort from contact with a caregiver is more important than food in forming attachment. 3. Learning the norms, values and practices of your own culture, mainly in childhood. 4. It is an artificial, short procedure, and classification may reflect temperament or cultural norms. 5. Adolescents took more risks when friends were present, while adults changed less. 6. Adults recalled childhood experiences retrospectively, and memories may be inaccurate. 7. The link was much weaker after family background and early ability were controlled, so waiting may partly reflect circumstances.
