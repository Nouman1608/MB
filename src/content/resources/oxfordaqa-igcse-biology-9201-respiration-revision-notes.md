Version 5.2, for exams in May/June 2018 onwards. The qualification is untiered. This section includes the
required practical on the effects of exercise on the human body.

## The essentials

- Respiration in cells **transfers energy**, aerobically (using oxygen) or anaerobically (without oxygen).
- Aerobic respiration takes place **continuously** in plants and animals.
- **Most** aerobic reactions take place inside **mitochondria**.

## Equations

```
aerobic:              glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water
                      C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O
anaerobic (muscle):   glucose → lactic acid
                      C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃
anaerobic (plants, some microorganisms):  glucose → ethanol + carbon dioxide
```

## Aerobic vs anaerobic

| | Aerobic | Anaerobic in muscle | Anaerobic in plants and some microorganisms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oxygen | Needed | Not needed | Not needed |
| Products | CO₂ + water | Lactic acid | Ethanol + CO₂ |
| Glucose breakdown | Complete | Incomplete | Incomplete |
| Energy transferred | Much more | Much less | Much less |

## Uses of the energy

1. Building larger molecules from smaller ones.
2. In animals, muscle contraction.
3. In mammals and birds, keeping a steady body temperature in colder surroundings.
4. In plants, building sugars, nitrates and other nutrients into amino acids, then proteins.

## Exercise

| Change | Effect |
|---|---|
| Heart rate increases | More blood flow to the muscles |
| Rate and depth of breathing increase | More oxygen into the blood, more CO₂ removed |
| Muscle glycogen converted back to glucose | More glucose available for respiration |

**Overall**: more glucose and oxygen supplied to the muscles; carbon dioxide removed faster.

## Oxygen debt and fatigue

```
not enough oxygen to muscles → anaerobic respiration → lactic acid builds up
→ oxygen debt → after exercise, extra oxygen oxidises lactic acid to CO₂ and water
```

- Long vigorous activity → muscles **fatigued**, stop contracting efficiently.
- One cause: **lactic acid** build-up.
- **Blood flow** through the muscles eventually removes the lactic acid.

## Plants respire all the time

| Condition | Net gas exchange |
|---|---|
| Dark | O₂ in, CO₂ out (respiration only) |
| Bright light | CO₂ in, O₂ out (photosynthesis faster than respiration) |

Waterlogged soil → little oxygen for roots → anaerobic respiration → ethanol, less energy.

## Required practical: effects of exercise

- Independent variable: exercise (type, time or intensity).
- Dependent variables: pulse rate, breathing rate.
- Control: same person, same exercise, same measuring method and timing.
- **Recovery time**: time for the pulse to return to the resting value.
- Repeat, calculate means; safety: stop if unwell.

Worked reminder: resting pulse 64, after exercise 112. Increase = 48; percentage increase = 48 ÷ 64 × 100 =
**75%**.

## Evidence that living things respire

Germinating seeds in a vacuum flask warm up; boiled (dead) seeds in a second flask do not. The dead seeds are the
**control**, and disinfecting both sets stops microorganisms on the seeds adding heat of their own.

## Model answers to learn

- **Why does heart rate increase during exercise? (2 marks):** "More blood flows to the muscles, supplying more
  oxygen and glucose for respiration and removing carbon dioxide faster."
- **Why do muscles become fatigued in a long race? (2 marks):** "Not enough oxygen reaches them, so they respire
  anaerobically and lactic acid builds up; this stops them contracting efficiently."
- **Why is heat released by a mammal useful in cold weather? (1 mark):** "It keeps the body temperature steady."

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aerobic respiration | Respiration using oxygen; glucose completely broken down |
| Anaerobic respiration | Respiration without oxygen; glucose incompletely broken down |
| Mitochondria | Organelles where most aerobic respiration reactions happen |
| Glycogen | Storage carbohydrate in muscles, converted back to glucose in exercise |
| Lactic acid | Product of anaerobic respiration in muscles |
| Oxygen debt | Oxygen needed after exercise to oxidise lactic acid |
| Muscle fatigue | Muscles no longer contracting efficiently after long vigorous activity |
| Ethanol | Product, with carbon dioxide, of anaerobic respiration in plants and some microorganisms |
| Recovery time | Time for pulse to return to resting rate after exercise |
| Oxidise | Combine with oxygen; here, lactic acid to CO₂ and water |

## Exam traps

- Energy is **transferred**, never "made".
- Plants respire **all the time**, not only at night.
- Muscles make **lactic acid**; plants and yeast make **ethanol and carbon dioxide**.
- Anaerobic respiration transfers **much less** energy, not none.
- Exercise changes must be linked to **oxygen, glucose and carbon dioxide**.
- Oxygen debt answers need **lactic acid** and **oxidised**.

## Self-test

1. What does respiration do?
2. Write the word equation for aerobic respiration.
3. Where do most aerobic respiration reactions take place?
4. Give the four uses of energy from respiration in the specification.
5. Name three changes in the body during exercise.
6. What is glycogen converted into during exercise?
7. When do muscles respire anaerobically?
8. Write the balanced symbol equation for anaerobic respiration in muscles.
9. Why does anaerobic respiration transfer less energy?
10. What is an oxygen debt?
11. Name the products of anaerobic respiration in yeast.
12. A pulse rises from 72 to 126 beats per minute. Calculate the percentage increase.

### Answers

1. Transfers energy from glucose for the cell's processes.
2. Glucose + oxygen → carbon dioxide + water.
3. Mitochondria.
4. Building larger molecules; muscle contraction (animals); keeping a steady body temperature (mammals and
   birds); building amino acids and proteins (plants).
5. Heart rate increases; rate and depth of breathing increase; muscle glycogen converted to glucose.
6. Glucose.
7. When insufficient oxygen reaches them.
8. C₆H₁₂O₆ → 2C₃H₆O₃.
9. The breakdown of glucose is incomplete.
10. The oxygen needed after exercise to oxidise lactic acid to carbon dioxide and water.
11. Ethanol and carbon dioxide.
12. (126 − 72) ÷ 72 × 100 = **75%**.
