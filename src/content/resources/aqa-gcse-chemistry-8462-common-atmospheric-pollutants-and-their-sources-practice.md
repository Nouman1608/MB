These questions cover section **4.9.3 Common atmospheric pollutants and their sources** of the AQA GCSE
Chemistry (8462) specification, for teaching from September 2016. The content is assessed on Paper 2 at both
Foundation and Higher Tier. The specification marks nothing in this section (HT only), so no question is
labelled Higher tier only.

Learn the content first in the
[common atmospheric pollutants study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-common-atmospheric-pollutants-and-their-sources/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-common-atmospheric-pollutants-and-their-sources-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Petrol is a mixture of hydrocarbons.

**(a)** Name the **two** products of complete combustion of a hydrocarbon. **[2]**
**(b)** Name **two** other pollutants that may be released from a car engine. **[2]**

**2.** Carbon monoxide can form when a gas fire burns methane.

**(a)** Explain why carbon monoxide forms. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the formation of carbon monoxide and water from methane and oxygen. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why carbon monoxide is especially dangerous in a home. **[2]**

**3.** A candle flame produces a black deposit on a cold spoon held in the flame.

**(a)** Name the black solid. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the solid forms. **[1]**
**(c)** Name the problem caused by large amounts of solid particles in the air. **[2]**

**4.** Coal contains carbon and a small proportion of sulfur. A power station burns coal.

**(a)** Name the gas formed from the sulfur. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation for its formation. **[1]**
**(c)** State **two** environmental problems caused by this gas. **[2]**

**5.** Oxides of nitrogen are found in vehicle exhaust gases, but petrol does not contain nitrogen.

**(a)** Explain how oxides of nitrogen are formed in a car engine. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the formation of nitrogen monoxide, NO, from its elements. **[1]**
**(c)** State **two** effects of oxides of nitrogen. **[2]**

**6.** Predict the products of each fuel burning under the conditions given.

**(a)** Hexane (C₆H₁₄) in a plentiful supply of oxygen **[2]**
**(b)** Hexane in a very limited supply of oxygen **[2]**
**(c)** Coal (carbon with sulfur impurities) in a plentiful supply of oxygen **[3]**

**7.** Acid rain damages limestone statues.

**(a)** Name the **two** types of pollutant gas that cause acid rain. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how acid rain damages a limestone statue. **[2]**
**(c)** State **one** other effect of acid rain on the environment. **[1]**

**8.** A school uses a gas boiler. A technician says that regular servicing is important.

Explain, using the chemistry of combustion, why servicing reduces the risk of carbon monoxide poisoning. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows data on the pollutants from the burning of 1 kg of three fuels.

| Fuel | Carbon dioxide / kg | Sulfur dioxide / g | Particulates / g |
|---|---|---|---|
| Natural gas | 2.8 | 0 | 0 |
| Fuel oil | 3.2 | 10 | 2 |
| Coal | 2.5 | 20 | 8 |

**(a)** State which fuel produces the most sulfur dioxide and suggest why. **[2]**
**(b)** State which fuel produces the most particulates and name the problem this causes. **[2]**
**(c)** A student says that coal is the cleanest fuel because it gives the least carbon dioxide. Evaluate this claim. **[3]**

**10.** Describe and explain the problems caused by an increase in each pollutant in the air.

**(a)** Carbon monoxide **[2]**
**(b)** Particulates **[2]**
**(c)** Sulfur dioxide **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Carbon dioxide** [1] and **water** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Any two [1] each: carbon monoxide; oxides of nitrogen; soot / particulates; unburned hydrocarbons; sulfur dioxide (if the fuel contains sulfur). **[2]**

**2. (a)** **Incomplete combustion**, because there is not enough oxygen [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O [formulae 1; balanced 1] **[2]**
**(c)** Carbon monoxide is **toxic** [1] and is **colourless and odourless**, so people cannot detect it [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** **Carbon** (soot) [1]
**(b)** **Incomplete combustion** of the wax gives carbon [1].
**(c)** Particulates cause **global dimming** [1] and **health problems** (lung / breathing problems) [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** **Sulfur dioxide** [1]
**(b)** S + O₂ → SO₂ [1]
**(c)** **Acid rain** [1] and **respiratory problems** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** The **nitrogen and oxygen in the air** [1] react together [1] at the **high temperature** in the engine [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** N₂ + O₂ → 2NO [1]
**(c)** Respiratory problems [1] and acid rain [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** **Carbon dioxide** and **water** [1] [1] **[2]**
**(b)** **Carbon (soot)** and/or **carbon monoxide** [1], and **water** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** **Carbon dioxide** [1], **sulfur dioxide** [1] and possibly oxides of nitrogen / particulates [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** **Sulfur dioxide** [1] and **oxides of nitrogen** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** The gases dissolve in rain to form **acidic solutions** [1]; limestone (calcium carbonate) **reacts with the acid**, so the surface is worn away [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: damages trees and plants; makes lakes too acidic for fish. [1] **[1]**

**8.** A poorly serviced boiler may have **blocked air vents or burners** [1], so there is **limited oxygen** [1]; this causes **incomplete combustion**, producing **carbon monoxide** (a toxic gas that is hard to detect); servicing keeps the oxygen supply and burners working for complete combustion [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** **Coal** produces the most (20 g) [1]; coal contains **more sulfur** impurities, which burn to form sulfur dioxide [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **Coal** (8 g) [1]; particulates cause **global dimming** and health problems [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Coal gives the least carbon dioxide per kilogram (2.5 kg) [1], but it gives the **most sulfur dioxide and particulates** [1], which cause acid rain and health problems, so it is not the cleanest fuel overall [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** Carbon monoxide is **toxic** [1]; it is colourless and odourless so is not easily detected / reduces the oxygen carried by blood [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Particulates cause **global dimming** [1] and **health problems** in the lungs [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Sulfur dioxide causes **respiratory problems** [1] and **acid rain** [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying nitrogen oxides come from the fuel rather than the air.
- Forgetting to link the pollutant to the conditions (limited oxygen, high temperature, sulfur in fuel).
- Writing that carbon monoxide has a smell.
- Missing a balancing step in combustion equations.
- Judging a fuel by only one pollutant.
