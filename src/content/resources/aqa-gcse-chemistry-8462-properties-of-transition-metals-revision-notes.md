## The six elements to name

**Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu** -- chromium, manganese, iron, cobalt, nickel, copper. These are the only transition metals the specification asks you to use as examples. They sit in the central block of the periodic table, between Group 2 and Group 3.

Learn the spellings. "Maganese" and "chromeium" cost marks on recall questions.

## Key definitions

- **Transition elements**: metals in the central block of the periodic table, with similar properties to each other that are different from those of Group 1.
- **Ion**: a charged particle formed when an atom loses or gains electrons. A Roman numeral in a name gives the metal ion's charge: iron(III) = Fe³⁺.
- **Catalyst**: a substance that changes the rate of a reaction but is not used up. It does not appear in the chemical equation.
- **Density**: mass per unit volume, in g/cm³. Use "denser", not "heavier".

## 4.1.3.1 Transition metals compared with Group 1

| Property | Group 1 (Li, Na, K) | Transition metals (Cr–Cu) |
|---|---|---|
| Melting point | low: all below 200 °C | high: all six above 1000 °C |
| Density | low: Li, Na, K float on water | high: about 7–9 g/cm³ |
| Strength | weak | strong |
| Hardness | soft, cut with a knife | hard |
| Reaction with oxygen | tarnish fast in air; stored under oil | slow, or only when heated |
| Reaction with water | react with cold water, fizz, give H₂ and an alkali | Cu: no reaction; Fe: very slow (rusting needs air too) |
| Reaction with halogens | vigorous, e.g. Na burns in Cl₂ | less vigorous, usually need heating |

Useful data (given in questions, not to memorise):

| | Li | Na | K | Cr | Fe | Cu |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Melting point / °C | 181 | 98 | 64 | 1907 | 1538 | 1085 |
| Density / g/cm³ | 0.53 | 0.97 | 0.89 | 7.15 | 7.87 | 8.96 |

### Equations to have ready

| Reaction | Group 1 | Transition metal |
|---|---|---|
| with oxygen | 4Na + O₂ → 2Na₂O | 2Cu + O₂ → 2CuO (black, on heating) |
| with water | 2K + 2H₂O → 2KOH + H₂ | copper: no reaction |
| with chlorine | 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl (white) | 2Fe + 3Cl₂ → 2FeCl₃ (brown, iron heated) |

### Method: answering a "compare" question

1. Pick one property at a time.
2. Name both metals, or use a comparative word (higher, denser, less reactive).
3. If data are given, quote a figure for each metal or work out a difference or ratio.
4. Move to the next property. Do not repeat the same property in different words.

**Small reminder.** "Compare chromium and potassium (melting point)." Chromium's melting point is far higher: 1907 − 64 = 1843 °C higher. That is one property, compared and backed by data.

## 4.1.3.2 Typical properties

Many transition elements:

1. have **ions with different charges**
2. form **coloured compounds**
3. are **useful as catalysts**.

"Many", not "all". Group 1 metals form only 1+ ions, and their compounds are white or colourless.

### Ions with different charges

| Element | Ions | Example names and formulae |
|---|---|---|
| Fe | Fe²⁺, Fe³⁺ | iron(II) sulfate FeSO₄, iron(III) chloride FeCl₃ |
| Cu | Cu⁺, Cu²⁺ | copper(I) oxide Cu₂O, copper(II) oxide CuO |
| Cr | Cr²⁺, Cr³⁺ | chromium(III) oxide Cr₂O₃ |
| Co | Co²⁺, Co³⁺ | cobalt(II) chloride CoCl₂ |
| Mn | Mn²⁺ and others | manganese(IV) oxide MnO₂ |

**Method: formula from a Roman numeral**

1. The numeral gives the metal ion's charge.
2. Write the charge of the other ion (Cl⁻, O²⁻, SO₄²⁻).
3. Use enough of each ion to make total + equal total −.
4. Put brackets round a group ion if you need more than one: Fe₂(SO₄)₃.

### Coloured compounds

| Compound | Colour |
|---|---|
| copper(II) sulfate solution | blue |
| copper(II) carbonate | green |
| copper(II) oxide | black |
| iron(II) compounds | pale green |
| iron(III) compounds | orange-brown |
| chromium(III) oxide | green |
| potassium dichromate | orange |
| potassium manganate(VII) | purple |
| hydrated cobalt(II) chloride | pink |
| nickel(II) sulfate | blue-green |

Link to chemical analysis (4.8.3.2): with sodium hydroxide solution, copper(II) gives a **blue** precipitate, iron(II) **green**, iron(III) **brown**. The colour tells you the charge on the iron ion. ### Catalysts

| Catalyst | Reaction |
|---|---|
| iron | Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃ |
| manganese(IV) oxide | decomposition of hydrogen peroxide: 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ |

Iron in the Haber process is the catalyst the specification names. How a catalyst works (lower activation energy) is Topic 6: see the [rate and extent of chemical change revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-the-rate-and-extent-of-chemical-change-revision-notes/).

**How to recognise a catalyst in data:** the reaction is faster with it, the same mass is left at the end, and it is not in the equation.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Less reactive** (transition metals) vs **more reactive** (Group 1). Transition metals are not unreactive: iron rusts, and heated iron burns in oxygen.
- **Dense** vs **heavy**. Density compares equal volumes.
- **Hard** (resists scratching and denting) vs **strong** (resists breaking under force). The specification lists both, so name both.
- **Iron(II)** (Fe²⁺, green) vs **iron(III)** (Fe³⁺, orange-brown). Iron(II) does not mean "two iron atoms".
- **Catalyst** (not used up, not in the equation) vs **reactant** (used up, in the equation).

## Linking a property to a use

Exam questions often give a use and ask which property explains it. Match the use to the property, then say why Group 1 would fail.

| Use | Property that matters | Why a Group 1 metal would not work |
|---|---|---|
| iron (as steel) in bridges and tools | strong, hard | Group 1 metals are soft and weak |
| copper water pipes | does not react with water | Group 1 metals react with cold water |
| iron catalyst in the Haber process | catalytic activity; stays solid at the process temperature of about 450 °C | sodium melts at 98 °C, so it would be liquid |
| coloured glazes and pigments | coloured compounds | Group 1 compounds are white |

**Small reminder (catalyst data).** A powder speeds up a reaction, and 1.0 g is put in and 1.0 g is recovered. It is a catalyst: faster reaction **and** mass unchanged. If only 0.6 g were recovered, it would be acting as a reactant, because it was used up.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the six transition metals the specification uses as examples.
2. State **two** physical properties in which iron differs from sodium.
3. Using the data table above, how many times as dense as potassium is copper? Give your answer to 2 significant figures.
4. Write the formula of copper(I) chloride.
5. Write the formula of chromium(III) chloride.
6. Write the formula of iron(III) sulfate.
7. Give the colour of potassium manganate(VII).
8. A solution gives a brown precipitate with sodium hydroxide solution. Which ion is present?
9. Balance: Fe + Br₂ → FeBr₃.
10. Name the catalyst used in the Haber process.
11. Why is copper, not potassium, used for hot water pipes? Give **two** reasons.
12. True or false: every transition metal compound is coloured and every transition metal is a catalyst.

### Answers

1. Chromium, manganese, iron, cobalt, nickel, copper.
2. Any two: iron has a higher melting point; is denser; is harder; is stronger.
3. 8.96 ÷ 0.89 = 10.07, so about **10 times**.
4. Cu⁺ and Cl⁻: **CuCl**.
5. Cr³⁺ and three Cl⁻: **CrCl₃**.
6. Two Fe³⁺ (6+) and three SO₄²⁻ (6−): **Fe₂(SO₄)₃**.
7. **Purple**.
8. **Iron(III)**, Fe³⁺.
9. **2Fe + 3Br₂ → 2FeBr₃**.
10. **Iron**.
11. Copper does not react with water; potassium reacts vigorously with water. Copper has a high melting point and is strong; potassium is soft and melts at 64 °C.
12. **False**: the specification says **many** transition elements have these properties, not all.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying transition metals are "more reactive" than Group 1, or calling them "unreactive".
- Writing "heavier" instead of "denser".
- Describing one metal only in a "compare" question.
- Giving an example metal outside Cr, Mn, Fe, Co, Ni, Cu (or a Group 2 metal by mistake).
- Leaving the Roman numeral off iron and copper compound names.
- Swapping iron(II) green and iron(III) orange-brown.
- Writing a catalyst as a reactant in the equation.
- Claiming that **all** transition elements are catalysts or form coloured compounds.
- Forgetting brackets in formulae such as Fe₂(SO₄)₃.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which transition metals do you need to know for AQA GCSE Chemistry 8462?**
The specification uses chromium, manganese, iron, cobalt, nickel and copper as examples. Giving a metal outside this list, or a Group 2 metal, is a common error.

**What does the Roman numeral in iron(III) chloride mean?**
The Roman numeral gives the charge on the metal ion, so iron(III) means Fe³⁺. Leaving the numeral out of iron and copper compound names loses marks.

**What colours are iron(II) and iron(III) compounds?**
Iron(II) compounds are typically green and iron(III) compounds are orange-brown. Swapping these colours is a frequent mistake.

**Do all transition metals form coloured compounds and act as catalysts?**
No. The specification says many transition metals have these properties, not all. Claiming that all of them do is marked as an error.
