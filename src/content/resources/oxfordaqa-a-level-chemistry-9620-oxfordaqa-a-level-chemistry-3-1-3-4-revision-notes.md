These notes condense **3.1.3.4 Bonding and physical properties** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International AS content, examined in Unit 1; Unit 2 may draw on bonding principles. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [bonding and physical properties study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-3-4/); to test yourself, use the [bonding and physical properties practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-3-4-practice/).

## The subtopic in brief

- Four crystal types: **ionic, metallic, macromolecular (giant covalent), molecular**.
- Six named examples: **sodium chloride** (ionic), **magnesium** (metallic), **diamond** and **graphite** (macromolecular), **iodine** and **ice** (molecular).
- Melting point depends on what must be overcome: strong bonds throughout a giant structure, or only weak forces between molecules.
- Conductivity needs charged particles that are **free to move**: delocalised electrons or mobile ions.
- Changes of state: melting and boiling are endothermic; freezing and condensing are exothermic.
- Diagrams must show the right arrangement and the number of particles asked for.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Crystal lattice | A regular, repeating three-dimensional arrangement of particles |
| Ionic lattice | Oppositely charged ions held by electrostatic attraction in all directions |
| Metallic lattice | Positive ions attracted to a sea of delocalised electrons |
| Macromolecular (giant covalent) | Atoms joined by covalent bonds throughout the structure; no separate molecules |
| Molecular crystal | Separate molecules held in a lattice by intermolecular forces |
| Delocalised electron | An electron not tied to one atom or bond, free to move through the structure |
| Sublimation | Change from solid directly to gas (graphite and iodine on heating) |
| Endothermic change of state | Melting or boiling: energy taken in to overcome forces between particles |

## Structure to property in one table

| | Sodium chloride | Magnesium | Diamond | Graphite | Iodine | Ice |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Ionic | Metallic | Macromolecular | Macromolecular | Molecular | Molecular |
| Melting point | High (801 °C) | High (650 °C) | Very high | Very high | Low (114 °C) | Low (0 °C) |
| Overcome on melting | Ionic attractions | Metallic bonding | Covalent bonds | Covalent bonds | Van der Waals forces between I₂ | Hydrogen bonds between H₂O |
| Conducts as solid? | No | Yes | No | Yes, along layers | No | No |
| Conducts when liquid? | Yes (ions move) | Yes | — | — | No | No |

## Explaining melting points: the three-link chain

1. **Name the structure** (for example, giant ionic lattice).
2. **Name what is overcome** (for example, strong electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions).
3. **Link to energy**: many strong attractions, so a lot of energy is needed, so a high melting point.

For molecular crystals, step 2 must say **intermolecular forces**, and the covalent bonds inside molecules stay intact.

## Conductivity rules

- Metals: delocalised electrons carry charge in solid and liquid.
- Graphite: one delocalised electron per carbon; conducts along the layers.
- Ionic solids: no (ions fixed). Molten or aqueous: yes, because the **ions** move.
- Diamond: no; every outer electron is in a localised covalent bond.
- Molecular substances: no; the molecules are neutral.

## Changes of state

- Heating curve: temperature rises, then stays **constant** during melting, rises again, then stays constant during boiling.
- During a plateau, energy goes into overcoming forces between particles, not into kinetic energy.
- Boiling needs much more energy than melting. For water: about +6.0 kJ mol⁻¹ to melt and +40.7 kJ mol⁻¹ to boil, because boiling separates molecules completely.

## Drawing checklist

- Sodium chloride: alternate Na⁺ and Cl⁻; each ion has 6 opposite neighbours in the full lattice.
- Magnesium: Mg²⁺ ions in rows with delocalised electrons between them; two electrons per ion.
- Diamond: tetrahedral, four bonds per carbon.
- Graphite: hexagonal layers, three bonds per carbon, gap between layers.
- Iodine: I₂ pairs (covalent bond within) arranged regularly, separate from neighbouring pairs.
- Ice: each H₂O hydrogen bonded to four others; dashed hydrogen bonds from δ+ H to a lone pair on O.

## Exam traps

- "Covalent bonds break when iodine melts": no, only van der Waals forces between I₂ molecules.
- "Molten sodium chloride conducts because of free electrons": no, mobile ions.
- "Graphite conducts because its layers slide": no; sliding layers explain softness.
- Calling diamond a molecule; it is macromolecular.
- Forgetting "in all directions" and "many" for giant lattices.
- Drawing the wrong number of particles, or leaving them unlabelled.
- Saying temperature rises while a solid melts.

## Self-test

1. Name the four types of crystal structure.
2. Which two named examples are molecular?
3. What particles are present in solid magnesium?
4. Why does solid sodium chloride not conduct electricity?
5. Why does molten sodium chloride conduct?
6. How many carbon atoms is each carbon bonded to in graphite, and in diamond?
7. Why is graphite soft?
8. What is overcome when iodine melts?
9. Why does the temperature of a melting solid stay constant?
10. Is condensation endothermic or exothermic? Explain.
11. A solid melts at 2050 °C and conducts only when molten. Identify its structure type.
12. Using +40.7 kJ mol⁻¹, calculate the energy needed to boil 9.0 g of water at 100 °C.

### Answers

1. Ionic, metallic, macromolecular (giant covalent) and molecular.
2. Iodine and ice.
3. Mg²⁺ ions and delocalised electrons.
4. Its ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice, so they cannot move.
5. The ions are free to move and carry charge.
6. Graphite: three. Diamond: four.
7. The layers are held together only by weak forces, so they slide over each other.
8. The van der Waals (induced dipole–dipole) forces between I₂ molecules.
9. Energy supplied is used to overcome forces between particles, not to raise their kinetic energy.
10. Exothermic: attractions between particles form, releasing energy.
11. Ionic (high melting point; conducts when molten because ions can move, but not as a solid).
12. 9.0 ÷ 18.0 = 0.50 mol; 0.50 × 40.7 = **20.4 kJ** (20 kJ to 2 significant figures).
