## Definitions

- **Radian:** the angle at the centre of a circle subtended by an arc equal in length to the radius. 2π rad = 360°, so π rad = 180°.
- **Angle of elevation:** measured upwards from the horizontal to the line of sight.
- **Angle of depression:** measured downwards from the horizontal to the line of sight.
- **Bearing:** a three-figure angle measured clockwise from north (for example 007°, 245°).
- **Angle between a line and a plane:** the angle between the line and its projection onto the plane.
- **Right pyramid / right cone:** the apex is directly above the centre of the base.
- **Slant height l of a cone:** the distance from the apex to the edge of the base; l² = r² + h².

## Method in steps

### Choosing a triangle rule

```
Right angle in the triangle?         → SOH CAH TOA or Pythagoras
Know a side AND its opposite angle?  → sine rule
Know two sides and included angle?   → cosine rule for the third side
Know all three sides?                → cosine rule for an angle
Want the area?                       → ½ab sin C (angle between a and b)
```

### Angle between a line and a plane (3D solid)

1. Mark the point where the line meets the plane.
2. From another point on the line, drop a perpendicular to the plane.
3. Join the foot of the perpendicular to the meeting point. This is the projection.
4. You now have a right-angled triangle. Use tan, sin or cos.

### Combined solid

1. Split into named solids (cone, hemisphere, pyramid, sphere).
2. Volume: add the parts.
3. Surface area: list each outside face. Leave out faces that are joined.
4. Give exact answers in π on calculator-free questions.

### Elevation from two observation points

1. Sketch the tower and both points on one horizontal line.
2. At the nearer point, the angle inside the triangle is 180° minus its angle of elevation.
3. The angle at the top is the difference between the two angles of elevation.
4. Use the sine rule in the non-right-angled triangle to find the sloping distance from the nearer point.
5. Finish in the right-angled triangle: height = sloping distance × sin(nearer elevation).

### Segment problems

1. Find the sector area ½r²θ with θ in radians.
2. Find the triangle area ½r² sin θ.
3. Segment area = sector − triangle.
4. For the segment perimeter, find the chord with the cosine rule and add the arc rθ.

### Bearings problem

1. Draw a north line at every point used.
2. Mark each bearing clockwise from north.
3. Use back bearing = bearing ± 180° to find angles inside the triangle.
4. Solve the triangle, then convert back to a bearing from north.

## Small worked reminders

- A(0, 3, −1), B(4, 5, 3): AB = √(16 + 4 + 16) = √36 = 6; midpoint (2, 4, 1).
- 135° = 135 × π/180 = 3π/4; 1 rad = 180/π ≈ 57.3°.
- Sector with r = 5 and θ = 1.2: arc = 6, area = ½ × 25 × 1.2 = 15.
- Cone with r = 5, h = 12: l = 13, curved surface = 65π, V = 100π.

- Chord for r = 9, θ = 1.8: AB² = 81 + 81 − 162 cos 1.8, so AB = 14.1; with arc 16.2 the segment perimeter is 30.3.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | The difference |
|---|---|
| Degrees vs radians | Arc and sector formulas need radians. Exam papers assume radians unless told otherwise (guide). |
| Arc length vs sector perimeter | Perimeter = rθ + 2r. The arc alone is rθ. |
| Height vs slant height | V = (1/3)πr²h uses h; curved area πrl uses l. |
| Sphere vs hemisphere surface | 4πr² vs 2πr² curved, 3πr² with the flat face. |
| Elevation vs depression | Both measured from the horizontal; up vs down. They are equal for the same two points. |
| Sine rule vs cosine rule | Sine rule needs a matching side–angle pair; cosine rule does not. |
| Sine rule for an angle | Gives an acute answer. Safe if the angle is opposite the shorter known side. The ambiguous case is in section 3.5, not here. |
| SL vs HL 3D questions | The guide says SL examinations set only right-angled trigonometry on 3D shapes. |

## Calculator-free vs calculator allowed

Paper 1 (SL and HL) allows no technology. Paper 2 requires it, as does HL Paper 3. On Paper 1 expect exact answers: surds, multiples of π, fractions. On Paper 2, give answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise, and check your angle mode.

## Quick self-test

1. Find the distance between (1, 2, −2) and (3, −4, 1).
2. Find the midpoint of (−3, 5, 2) and (7, 1, −6).
3. Write 225° in radians as a multiple of π.
4. Write 2 radians in degrees, to 3 significant figures.
5. A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 0.7 rad. Find the arc length.
6. A sector has radius 4 cm and angle 3π/8. Find its exact area.
7. Find the exact volume of a sphere of radius 3 cm.
8. Find the exact total surface area of a solid hemisphere of radius 5 cm.
9. A right cone has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its exact curved surface area and volume.
10. Find the area of a triangle with sides 7 cm and 10 cm and included angle 30°, given sin 30° = ½.
11. A triangle has sides 3, 5 and 7. Find its largest angle (calculator allowed).
12. A sector has radius 8 cm and arc length 12 cm. Find the angle in radians.

### Answers

1. √(2² + 6² + 3²) = √49 = **7**
2. **(2, 3, −2)**
3. 225 × π/180 = **5π/4**
4. 2 × 180/π = 114.59... = **115°**
5. l = 10 × 0.7 = **7 cm**
6. ½ × 16 × 3π/8 = **3π cm²**
7. (4/3)π × 27 = **36π cm³**
8. 3π × 25 = **75π cm²**
9. l = √(25 + 144) = 13; curved area = π × 5 × 13 = **65π cm²**; V = (1/3)π × 25 × 12 = **100π cm³**
10. ½ × 7 × 10 × ½ = **17.5 cm²**
11. cos C = (9 + 25 − 49)/30 = −½, so C = **120°**
12. θ = 12/8 = **1.5 rad**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using l = rθ or ½r²θ with θ in degrees, or leaving the GDC in degree mode on a radian question.
- Giving a sector's perimeter as rθ and forgetting the two radii.
- Using vertical height in πrl, or slant height in (1/3)πr²h.
- Counting a joined face in the surface area of a combined solid.
- Measuring the angle between a line and a plane against an edge of the plane instead of the projection.
- Bearings not written with three figures, or measured anticlockwise.
- Rounding a side to 3 s.f. and carrying it into the next step, which shifts the final answer.
- Using the sine rule for the largest angle of a triangle when it may be obtuse.
- Decimal answers on Paper 1 questions that ask for exact values.
- No labelled diagram, so the angle used in the working is not clear to the examiner.
