## Questions

**1.** Define globalisation. **[2]**

**2.** Explain two causes of globalisation. **[4]**

**3.** Distinguish a free trade area from a customs union. **[3]**

**4.** Name the form of economic integration described in each case. **[4]**

**(a)** Members remove all tariffs between themselves and each keeps its own tariffs on outsiders.
**(b)** Members share a currency and a central bank but do not coordinate tax and spending.
**(c)** Members remove internal tariffs and apply one common tariff to outsiders.
**(d)** Members share a currency and central bank and also harmonise fiscal and other policies.

**5.** Country M makes sandals at a cost of 50 cu a pair. Partner P can supply them at 44 cu and outside Country Z at 40 cu. Country M charges a 30% tariff on all imports. Demand in M is Q = 700 − 10P, and domestic firms supply at a constant cost.

**(a)** Show that M buys from domestic firms before it joins a customs union with P. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the welfare gain to M after the union is formed. **[3]**

**6.** Country M also imports cloth. Outside Country Z supplies it at 8 cu a metre and partner P at 10 cu. M's tariff on all imports is 50%. Demand is Q = 390 − 20P.

**(a)** Explain why M's imports of cloth switch to P after the customs union is formed. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the net effect on M, showing the consumer gain, the lost tariff revenue and the extra resource cost. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why a customs union needs a common external tariff, whereas a free trade area does not need one but does need rules of origin. **[4]**

**8.** Explain one advantage and one disadvantage for a member of joining a monetary union. **[4]**

**9.** Total imports into a bloc were 400 units before a customs union and 460 units after. Imports from members were 120 units before and 180 units after.

**(a)** Calculate the change in the intra-bloc share of imports. **[2]**
**(b)** Assess whether the data show trade creation or trade diversion. **[3]**

**10.** A member of a bloc finds that trade creation on one good replaces 120 units of higher-cost output with a saving of 3 cu per unit, while trade diversion on another good costs 80 units at 4 cu each of extra resource cost. Calculate the net resource effect and say what it implies for the bloc. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss whether globalisation benefits a developing country. **[8]**

**12.** Evaluate whether joining a customs union is likely to benefit a country. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Globalisation is the growing integration and interdependence of national economies [1] through rising cross-border flows of goods, services, capital, labour, technology and information [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition that mentions only trade between countries is incomplete; naming a second flow (capital, labour, technology or production) shows you see the wider process.

**2.** Any two causes, each with a developed link. For example: lower transport costs [1] mean goods and components can be moved cheaply, so production can be split across countries [1]. Information and communication technology [1] lets firms manage suppliers and customers abroad and trade services at low cost [1]. Others that earn credit with an explanation: trade liberalisation, capital mobility, multinational companies. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Explain how each named cause lowers cost or risk; two explained causes show more understanding than a list of four labels.

**3.** Both remove tariffs between members [1]. A free trade area lets each member keep its own tariffs on non-members [1], whereas a customs union applies a common external tariff to all non-members [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction needs both sides; saying what a customs union has without saying what a free trade area lacks leaves the contrast incomplete.

**4.** (a) Free trade area [1]. (b) Monetary union [1]. (c) Customs union [1]. (d) Full economic union [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Take care with (b) and (d): the difference is whether wider policies, such as fiscal policy, are harmonised.

**5. (a)** Landed price from Z = 40 × 1.30 = 52 cu and from P = 44 × 1.30 = 57.2 cu [1]. Domestic cost is 50 cu, below both, so M buys 200 units (700 − 500) from its own firms [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** After the union P's price is 44 cu, below domestic 50 cu and Z's landed 52 cu, so M imports from P; Q = 700 − 440 = 260 [1]. Production effect = 200 × (50 − 44) = 1,200 cu [1]. Consumption effect = ½ × 6 × 60 = 180 cu, so the total gain is **1,380 cu** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the landed-price comparison before the union, because it is what proves the switch is trade creation and not just assertion; give the unit with the final figure.

**6. (a)** Before the union the landed price from Z is 8 × 1.5 = 12 cu and from P is 10 × 1.5 = 15 cu, so M imports from Z [1]. After the union P's goods are tariff-free at 10 cu, while Z's landed price remains 12 cu [1]. M switches to P because it is now cheaper for buyers, even though Z has the lower production cost: this is trade diversion [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Q before = 390 − 240 = 150; Q after = 390 − 200 = 190. Consumer gain = (12 − 10) × (150 + 190) ÷ 2 = 340 cu [1]. Lost tariff revenue = 150 × (12 − 8) = 600 cu [1]. Net effect = 340 − 600 = **−260 cu** (made up of a consumption gain of 40 cu less an extra resource cost of 150 × 2 = 300 cu) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The net figure is negative because of the resource cost of buying from the dearer producer; explaining that in words alongside the number shows you know why diversion is a loss.

**7.** Without a common external tariff, outsiders can send goods through the member with the lowest tariff [1], then move them tariff-free to other members; this is trade deflection [1]. A customs union removes the incentive because every member applies the same tariff [1]. A free trade area leaves members free to set their own tariffs, so it must use rules of origin to check where goods were made [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Connect the cause (different tariffs) to the consequence (goods re-routed); a bare statement that the common external tariff "stops cheating" earns little.

**8.** Advantage: no currency conversion costs or exchange rate risk among members [1], which encourages trade and investment [1]. Disadvantage: one interest rate has to suit every member [1], so a country hit by a shock cannot cut its own rate or devalue, and must adjust through wages, prices or labour mobility [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answer with one advantage and one disadvantage as asked; extra points beyond the two requested rarely add credit.

**9. (a)** Share before = 120 ÷ 400 = 30% [1]. Share after = 180 ÷ 460 = 39.1%, a rise of **9.1 percentage points** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Imports from outside the bloc were 280 units before (400 − 120) and 280 after (460 − 180) [1], so there is no evidence of switching away from outsiders, which is what diversion would show [1]. Total imports rose by 60 units, all from members, which is consistent with trade creation, but growth in demand could also explain the rise, so the data are not conclusive [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A rising share alone proves nothing; the stronger answers compare imports from outside, and finish with a limit on the data.

**10.** Gain from creation = 120 × 3 = 360 cu [1]. Loss from diversion = 80 × 4 = 320 cu [1]. Net effect = 360 − 320 = **40 cu gain**; creation outweighs diversion, so the bloc is a net gain in resource terms, though a narrow one [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both products before subtracting, and state what the sign of the result means.

**11.** Gains, up to 3 marks. Access to larger export markets lets firms exploit economies of scale and raises growth [1]; inward investment by multinational companies brings capital, jobs and technology [1]; cheaper imported inputs and consumer goods lower costs and prices [1]. Costs, up to 3 marks. Domestic firms in declining industries face competition and close, causing structural unemployment [1]; reliance on a few exports exposes the country to world price shocks [1]; multinational companies may move profits abroad or leave if costs change [1]. Evaluation, 2 marks. The outcome depends on the country's comparative advantage, skills and labour flexibility, and on policies such as retraining [1]. Gains are likely to be larger in the long run than the short run, so a supported judgement is needed [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Essays that list gains then costs and stop earn less than those that weigh them; a conditional judgement, such as "provided the country can diversify its exports", is the clearest form of evaluation.

**12.** Analysis, up to 6 marks. A customs union removes tariffs on partner goods and applies a common external tariff [1]. Trade creation: domestic output replaced by cheaper partner imports gives production and consumption gains [1]. Trade diversion: imports switch from cheaper outsiders to dearer partners, causing a resource loss and lost tariff revenue [1]. A before and after comparison of landed prices (cost × (1 + tariff)) as illustration [1]. Dynamic gains: a bigger market gives scale economies, competition and investment [1]. Loss of control of trade policy to a common external tariff [1]. Evaluation, up to 6 marks. Net benefit depends on the balance of creation and diversion [1]; on how far partner costs exceed world costs [1]; on whether the common external tariff is lower than old tariffs, which reduces diversion [1]; on price elasticity of demand, which affects the consumption gain [1]; on the time period, as dynamic gains take time [1]. A judgement stating likely benefit if partners are efficient and close to world costs, and likely loss if trade is mainly diverted from low-cost outsiders [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer uses figures or a before and after comparison of landed prices to support the claim, then ends with a judgement tied to named conditions rather than a general statement that "it depends".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "customs union" when the description has no common external tariff.
- Defining globalisation as trade only.
- Comparing supplier costs without adding the tariff, so the cheapest source is misjudged.
- Calling every rise in intra-bloc trade trade creation, without checking imports from outside.
- Giving the net effect of trade diversion without saying why it is a loss.
- Stopping an essay at costs and gains with no weighing of which matters more and why.
