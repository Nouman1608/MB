Section 2.2 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 asks for the characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of seven direct data entry devices: magnetic stripe reader, chip and PIN reader, RFID reader, OMR, OCR, bar code reader and QR scanner.

## What makes it "direct"

Data is read straight from a card, tag, form or label, with no typing. So it is **faster**, **more accurate** (no transcription errors) and needs **fewer staff**. The data must already be in machine-readable form.

## The seven devices

**Magnetic stripe reader**

- Card swiped; stripe holds a small amount of data.
- Uses: hotel key cards, membership and library cards, older bank cards.
- For: fast, cheap cards, works when wet. Against: damaged by magnets and wear, little data, easily cloned.

**Chip and PIN reader**

- Card inserted; chip read; PIN typed on a keypad and checked.
- Uses: shop payments, cash machines.
- For: chip hard to copy, PIN needed, more data, customer keeps card. Against: shoulder surfing, forgotten PINs, slower than contactless.

**RFID reader**

- Radio waves read a tag (microchip and antenna). Passive tags: no battery, short range. Active tags: battery, longer range.
- Uses: stock and parcel tracking, luggage, contactless cards, pets and livestock, access cards, road tolls.
- For: no line of sight, read through packaging, many tags at once, reads moving items, robust. Against: more expensive than bar codes, metal and liquids interfere, may be read by unauthorised readers.

**OMR**

- Detects marks in fixed positions on a designed form.
- Uses: multiple-choice answer sheets, lottery tickets, surveys, registers.
- For: very fast for large numbers of forms, accurate. Against: forms must be designed and printed precisely; faint, wrong or misplaced marks rejected; only choices, not written answers.

**OCR**

- Software that converts an image of text into editable text (after scanning or photographing).
- Uses: digitising printed documents, number plate recognition, passports, forms in block capitals.
- For: much faster than retyping; text editable and searchable. Against: errors with poor originals, unusual fonts and handwriting; output must be checked.

**Bar code reader**

- Light reflected from lines of varying width; gives a number used to look up a database record.
- Uses: checkouts and stock control, libraries, parcels, wristbands, tickets.
- For: fast, accurate, very cheap labels, automatic stock updates; number can be typed if unreadable. Against: needs line of sight; damaged codes fail; holds only a short number; codes can be swapped.

**QR scanner**

- Two-dimensional square pattern; read by scanners or phone cameras.
- Uses: web links on posters and menus, e-tickets and boarding passes, payments, contact details.
- For: much more data than a bar code, any angle, readable if partly damaged, phones read it. Against: needs a camera device; fake codes can lead to harmful websites; destination unknown before scanning.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct data entry | Input read straight from the source without typing |
| Transcription error | Mistake made when copying data by hand |
| PIN | Personal Identification Number, typed to confirm the cardholder |
| RFID tag | Microchip with an antenna that sends data by radio waves |
| Passive tag | RFID tag without a battery; powered by the reader |
| Active tag | RFID tag with its own battery; longer range |
| Line of sight | A clear straight path between reader and code |
| Cloning | Copying a card's data to make a fake card |
| QR code | Quick Response code: a 2D pattern of squares |

## Exam traps

- **OMR = marks; OCR = characters.** OCR is software; a scanner captures the image first.
- RFID does **not** need line of sight. Bar codes and QR codes do.
- A bar code normally stores a product number, not the price.
- Contactless payment uses RFID; chip and PIN requires insertion and a PIN.
- Magnetic stripes are less secure than chips because they are easier to clone.
- In a comparison, name the scenario's need (moving items, hidden tags, large volumes of forms) and match it to the device feature.

## Self-test

1. Define *direct data entry*.
2. Give **two** uses of magnetic stripe cards.
3. Why is chip and PIN more secure than a magnetic stripe?
4. State the difference between a passive and an active RFID tag.
5. Give **two** advantages of RFID over bar codes for tracking luggage.
6. Give **one** disadvantage of RFID tags.
7. Give **two** uses of OMR.
8. State the difference between OMR and OCR.
9. Give **one** reason OCR output must be checked.
10. Give **one** advantage of a QR code over a bar code.
11. Describe **one** security risk of scanning QR codes.
12. What happens at a checkout if a bar code cannot be read?

## Self-test answers

1. Entering data directly from its source, such as a card, tag or form, without typing it.
2. Any two: hotel room keys, membership or library cards, older bank cards, some travel tickets.
3. The chip is harder to copy than a stripe, and the PIN must be known to complete the payment.
4. A passive tag has no battery and is powered by the reader, so it works at short range; an active tag has its own battery and longer range.
5. Any two: no line of sight needed; can be read through the bag; many tags read at once; read while moving on a conveyor.
6. Any one: more expensive than bar codes; affected by metal or liquids; may be read by unauthorised readers.
7. Any two: multiple-choice answer sheets, lottery tickets, surveys, registers.
8. OMR detects whether marks are present in fixed positions; OCR recognises each character and converts an image into editable text.
9. Poor-quality originals, unusual fonts or handwriting may be misread.
10. It stores much more data, can be read from any angle, or still reads if partly damaged.
11. A fake code placed over a real one could take the user to a harmful website.
12. The number printed under the bar code is typed in by hand.
