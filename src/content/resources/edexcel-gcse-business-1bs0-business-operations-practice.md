## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term flow production. **[2]**

**2.** A bakery makes 300 white loaves, then cleans its mixers and makes 200 brown loaves. Which **one** production process is this? **[1]**

A: Job production

B: Batch production

C: Flow production

D: Just in time

**3.** Which **two** of the following are features of job production? **[2]**

A: Each product is unique

B: Low cost per unit

C: Skilled, flexible workers

D: A continuous production line

E: Large numbers of identical products

**4.** Hensley Joinery has 6 workers who make 210 window frames a month. Calculate labour productivity per month. **[2]**

**5.** Morland Mugs has monthly costs of £18,600 and produces 6,000 mugs a month. After installing a new kiln, it produces 8,000 mugs a month and its monthly costs rise to £20,800.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per mug before the new kiln. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost per mug after the new kiln. **[2]**

**6.** Vale Tailors measures each customer and cuts and sews a suit to fit that person only. State the production process used by Vale Tailors. **[1]**

**7.** Explain **one** advantage to a business of using flow production. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Quill Signs makes shop signs designed with each customer. It is considering a machine that cuts standard letter shapes very quickly. Outline **one** way this technology could reduce Quill's flexibility. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** reason why a business providing a service may find it harder to match its operations to demand than a business producing goods. **[3]**

**10.** Ridgeway Ice Cream makes twelve flavours in small groups of tubs, changing and cleaning its equipment between flavours. A supermarket has offered a large, regular order for vanilla only. To meet it, Ridgeway would have to install a continuous production line for vanilla. Analyse the possible impact on Ridgeway Ice Cream of introducing flow production for vanilla. **[6]**

**11.** Penhallow Pottery employs 4 potters at £24,000 a year each, producing 12,000 hand-glazed mugs a year. It wants to raise output and has two options.

Option 1: employ a fifth potter at £24,000 a year. Output would rise to 15,000 mugs.

Option 2: lease an automated glazing machine for £18,000 a year, keeping 4 potters. Output would rise to 16,500 mugs.

Justify which option Penhallow Pottery should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Swiftclean washes cars by hand. Six staff, each paid £22,000 a year, wash 15,600 cars a year at £12 each. The owner is considering an automatic car-wash tunnel leased for £40,000 a year, which would need only 2 staff on the same pay. With the tunnel, she would cut the price to £8 and forecasts 24,000 cars a year. Some regular customers say machines miss dirt and can scratch paintwork.

Evaluate whether Swiftclean should replace hand washing with the tunnel. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Continuous production of identical products [1] along a production line, often using specialised machinery [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 210 ÷ 6 [1] = **35 frames per worker per month** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** £18,600 ÷ 6,000 [1] = **£3.10** [1]

**(b)** £20,800 ÷ 8,000 [1] = **£2.60** [1] **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Total costs rose, but cost per mug fell because output rose by more. Always divide by the matching output figure.

**6.** Job production [1]. **[1]**

**7.** Flow production makes very large numbers of identical products with specialised machines [1], so fixed costs and labour costs are spread across many units [1]. This gives a low cost per unit, which allows the business to charge competitive prices and still make a profit [1]. **[3]**

**8.** The machine is designed to cut standard letter shapes quickly [1], so unusual designs or hand-drawn lettering that customers ask for might not suit it, making it harder for Quill to offer the made-to-order signs its customers expect [1]. **[2]**

**9.** A service, such as a haircut, is usually provided and consumed at the same time [1], so it cannot be made in advance and stored for busy periods [1]. The business must plan staff numbers carefully: too few at busy times means long waits and lost customers, and too many at quiet times means paying staff with little to do [1]. **[3]**

**10.** A flow line for vanilla would produce large numbers of identical tubs continuously, raising productivity [1] and spreading costs over many more tubs, lowering cost per tub [1]. This would let Ridgeway offer the supermarket a competitive price and gain a large, regular order, helping its sales grow [1]. However, the production line is a large investment, and if the supermarket cancels the order Ridgeway may be left with equipment it cannot use for its other eleven flavours [1]. Ridgeway would also depend heavily on one powerful customer that may push its price down over time [1]. Its small groups of other flavours could continue alongside, keeping the variety that its existing customers value [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* An Analyse answer follows the chain from process to productivity, cost and price, then uses the case to show a risk.

**11.** Current cost per mug (labour) = £96,000 ÷ 12,000 = £8.00 [1]. Option 1: £120,000 ÷ 15,000 = **£8.00** per mug [1]. Option 2: (£96,000 + £18,000) ÷ 16,500 = £114,000 ÷ 16,500 = **£6.91** per mug [1]. Labour productivity stays at 3,000 mugs per potter in Option 1 but rises to 4,125 in Option 2 [1]. Option 2 gives 1,500 more mugs than Option 1 at £6,000 a year less cost [1]. The machine should also give a consistent glaze, improving quality [1]. However, Penhallow sells hand-glazed mugs; machine glazing may lose the hand-made appeal that justifies its prices [1]. A fifth potter keeps flexibility for different designs and avoids the risk of a breakdown stopping glazing [1]. **Judgement:** choose Option 2, because it lowers cost per mug by over £1 and raises productivity, unless customers buy mainly because the mugs are hand-glazed; Penhallow could use the machine for its standard range and keep hand-glazing for premium designs [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong Justify answers weigh the figures against quality and flexibility, the factors the specification asks you to balance.

**12.** Hand washing: revenue 15,600 × £12 = £187,200; staff cost 6 × £22,000 = £132,000; left over £55,200 [1]. Tunnel: revenue 24,000 × £8 = £192,000; costs £40,000 + (2 × £22,000) = £84,000; left over **£108,000**, almost double [1][1]. Cost per car falls from about £8.46 to £3.50 [1]. For: the tunnel raises productivity and allows a competitive £8 price, which may attract many new customers [1]. Against: the forecast depends on 8,400 more cars a year, and if only 15,600 came at £8, revenue would be £124,800, leaving £40,800, less than now [1]. Quality concerns from regulars suggest some will leave, and the tunnel offers less flexibility for large or unusual vehicles [1]. Four staff would lose their jobs, which costs redundancy pay and may harm Swiftclean's local reputation [1]. **Conclusion:** the tunnel is worthwhile only if the lower price brings in many more cars; Swiftclean could keep a hand-wash service at a premium price for customers who want it, gaining the tunnel's low cost while protecting quality-conscious regulars [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Testing the forecast, here by working out the result if the number of cars did not rise, gives an Evaluate answer a firm basis.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing total costs by the wrong output figure.
- Confusing productivity with total output.
- Assuming technology improves every factor; flexibility and quality can suffer.
- Describing job, batch and flow without linking them to cost and price.
- Ignoring the customers' view of quality when recommending automation.
