## What section 2.1 asks you to do

| Point | Specification wording |
|---|---|
| 2.1 (a) | Explain the purpose of business documents |
| 2.1 (b) | Prepare: purchase order, purchase invoice, sales invoice, credit note, statement of account, remittance advice, petty cash voucher |
| 2.1 (c) | Cash receipt |

"Prepare" means producing the document with a correct layout and correct figures. Point (c) has no verb in the specification, so be ready both to explain a cash receipt and to prepare one.

## Why businesses use documents

A business document is the written evidence of a transaction. Its purposes are:

- **Evidence.** It proves that a transaction happened, on what date and for how much.
- **Source for the records.** Each entry in the books of original entry is made from a document, so the document number is written beside the entry.
- **Checking.** Documents are compared with each other before anything is recorded or paid, so mistakes and overcharges are found.
- **Authorisation and control.** Orders and vouchers are signed by someone with authority, which prevents unapproved spending.
- **Settling disputes.** If buyer and seller disagree, the documents show what was agreed.
- **An audit trail.** Numbered documents let any figure in the accounts be traced back to its origin.

## What each document must show

| Document | Essential contents |
|---|---|
| Purchase order | Order number, date, buyer and supplier names, description and quantity of goods, agreed price, authorising signature |
| Sales invoice / purchase invoice | Invoice number, date, buyer and seller, order reference, quantity, description, unit price, total at list price, trade discount, net amount due, payment terms |
| Credit note | Credit note number, date, invoice it relates to, reason (returns or overcharge), quantity, price, trade discount, amount credited |
| Statement of account | Date, customer name, opening balance, each invoice, credit note and payment with dates, running balance, amount now due |
| Remittance advice | Date, payer and payee, invoices paid, credit notes deducted, discount taken, amount of payment |
| Petty cash voucher | Voucher number, date, what was bought, amount, signature of the person paid and of the person authorising |
| Cash receipt | Receipt number, date, amount received, what it was for, from whom, signature or stamp of the receiver |

## Discounts on documents

**Trade discount** is a reduction from list price given to trade customers, often for buying in bulk. It is deducted on the invoice itself, so only the net figure is recorded in the books.

**Cash discount** is an offer printed in the payment terms, such as "2% if paid within 14 days". It is not deducted on the invoice, because nobody knows yet whether the customer will pay early. It is taken only when payment is made, and it is shown on the remittance advice and the statement.

## Worked example 1: preparing a sales invoice

Thornbury Textiles supplies Quayside Upholstery on 4 March 2027, against order Q-560. Trade discount is 20%. Terms: 2% cash discount if paid within 14 days, otherwise 30 days.

```
SALES INVOICE No. 2207                       4 March 2027
Thornbury Textiles to Quayside Upholstery    Order ref: Q-560
Qty   Description          Unit price      Total
30 m  Velvet                    18.00     540.00
12    Cushion pads               7.50      90.00
25 m  Lining                     4.20     105.00
      Total at list price                 735.00
      Less trade discount 20%             147.00
      Amount due                          588.00
Terms: 2% cash discount if paid within 14 days; net 30 days
```

Check the working: 30 × 18.00 = 540.00, 12 × 7.50 = 90.00, 25 × 4.20 = 105.00; total 735.00; 20% is 147.00; 735.00 − 147.00 = 588.00. Thornbury enters 588.00 in its sales day book. Quayside keeps the same document as a purchase invoice and enters 588.00 in its purchases day book, after checking it against order Q-560.

## Worked example 2: a credit note for returns and an overcharge

On 9 March Quayside returns 4 damaged cushion pads. It also points out that the lining was agreed at 3.80 a metre, not 4.20. Thornbury issues one credit note covering both.

```
CREDIT NOTE No. 118                          9 March 2027
Thornbury Textiles to Quayside Upholstery    Re: invoice 2207
4 cushion pads returned, damaged   4 x 7.50      30.00
Overcharge on lining          25 m x 0.40        10.00
                                                 40.00
Less trade discount 20%                           8.00
Amount credited                                  32.00
```

The overcharge is 4.20 − 3.80 = 0.40 a metre. The credit note uses the same 20% trade discount as the invoice, otherwise the credit would be too large. Quayside now owes 588.00 − 32.00 = 556.00 on this invoice.

## Worked example 3: remittance advice with cash discount

Quayside pays on 17 March, within 14 days, and takes the cash discount on the amount it owes after the credit note.

```
REMITTANCE ADVICE                           17 March 2027
Quayside Upholstery to Thornbury Textiles
Invoice 2207                                     588.00
Less credit note 118                              32.00
                                                 556.00
Less cash discount 2%                             11.12
Payment enclosed (bank transfer)                 544.88
```

Two per cent of 556.00 is 11.12, so 544.88 is paid. The remittance advice lets Thornbury match the money to the right invoice and credit note.

## Worked example 4: statement of account

Quayside owed 415.00 at 1 March for invoice 2150 (26 February). It paid that invoice on 10 March, taking 2% discount of 8.30. A new invoice, 2231 for 312.00, was sent on 22 March.

```
STATEMENT OF ACCOUNT          Thornbury Textiles to Quayside Upholstery
31 March 2027                          Debit      Credit     Balance
1 Mar   Balance b/d                                           415.00
4 Mar   Invoice 2207                  588.00                1,003.00
9 Mar   Credit note 118                            32.00      971.00
10 Mar  Bank                                      406.70
        Discount                                    8.30      556.00
17 Mar  Bank                                      544.88
        Discount                                   11.12        0.00
22 Mar  Invoice 2231                  312.00                  312.00
Amount now due                                                312.00
```

Debits increase what the customer owes; credits reduce it. Quayside compares this statement with Thornbury's account in its own payables ledger. If the balances differ, one side has an error or an item in transit.

## Worked example 5: petty cash voucher and cash receipt

```
PETTY CASH VOUCHER No. 41                   12 March 2027
Taxi to deliver fabric samples                    14.60
Received by: L. Brandt     Authorised by: Office Manager
Taxi receipt attached
```

```
CASH RECEIPT No. 0093                       14 March 2027
Received from: Mr D. Kaur
The sum of: thirty-six dollars and fifty cents     36.50
For: fabric offcuts, cash sale
Signed: for Thornbury Textiles
```

The voucher is the petty cashier's evidence for paying out 14.60; the receipt is the customer's proof of payment and Thornbury keeps a copy as evidence of a cash sale.

## Which document feeds which book

| Document | Book of original entry |
|---|---|
| Sales invoice (issued) | Sales day book (seller) |
| Purchase invoice (received) | Purchases day book (buyer) |
| Credit note issued | Sales returns book |
| Credit note received | Purchases returns book |
| Cash receipt, remittance advice with payment | Cash book |
| Petty cash voucher | Petty cash book |
| Purchase order, statement of account | No entry: used for checking only |

A purchase order records no transaction, because nothing is owed until goods are supplied. A statement summarises entries already made.

## Exam technique: command words

- **Explain the purpose**: say what the document does and why that matters, for example "the remittance advice shows which invoices the payment covers, so the supplier can allocate it to the correct invoices".
- **Prepare**: give the title, number, date, both names, each line with quantity and price, the trade discount and the final amount, labelled. Missing headings and dates cost presentation marks.
- **Calculate**: show each step. Apply trade discount before cash discount.

## Common errors

- Deducting cash discount on the invoice instead of when payment is made.
- Calculating a credit note at list price, ignoring the trade discount on the invoice.
- Calculating cash discount on the invoice total rather than on the amount owed after credit notes.
- Treating a purchase order as a record of a transaction.
- Showing payments on the debit side of a statement of account.
- Leaving out the authorising signature on a petty cash voucher.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is trade discount shown on an invoice but cash discount is not deducted?**
Trade discount is certain when the invoice is raised, so it is deducted there. Cash discount depends on the customer paying early, so it is only taken when payment is made.

**Is a purchase invoice a different document from a sales invoice?**
No. It is the same invoice: the seller calls it a sales invoice and the buyer, who receives it, calls it a purchase invoice.

**What is the difference between a cash receipt and a remittance advice?**
A cash receipt is given by the person receiving money as proof of payment. A remittance advice is sent by the payer to explain which invoices a payment covers.
