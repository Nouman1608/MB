This guide goes deeper into subtopic **9.6 Extraction of metals** of the Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 syllabus for 2026–2028. The broader [Alloys and Extraction of Metals](/resources/alloys-and-extraction-of-metals/) guide lists the blast furnace equations and the electrode reactions for aluminium. This page explains what each raw material is for, follows the redox in each step, shows how slag formation is an acid–base reaction, and works through simple-proportion calculations. | 1 | Core | Describe the ease in obtaining metals from their ores, related to the position of the metal in the reactivity series |
| 2 | Core | Describe the extraction of iron from hematite in the blast furnace, in words (symbol equations are not required at Core) |
| 3 | Core | State that the main ore of aluminium is bauxite and that aluminium is extracted by electrolysis |
| 4 | Supplement | State the symbol equations for the extraction of iron from hematite |
| 5 | Supplement | Describe the extraction of aluminium from purified bauxite (aluminium oxide): the role of cryolite, why the carbon anodes need replacing, and the electrode reactions with ionic half-equations |

The purification of bauxite is not required.

## Reactivity decides the method

An **ore** is a rock containing a metal compound, often an oxide, from which the metal can be obtained. Extracting the metal means **reducing** the compound: removing oxygen from it, or adding electrons to the metal ions.

The more reactive a metal, the more strongly it holds on to oxygen in its compounds, and the harder it is to extract.

| Position in the series | Examples | How the metal is obtained |
|---|---|---|
| Above carbon | potassium, sodium, calcium, magnesium, aluminium | electrolysis of a molten compound; carbon is not reactive enough to remove the oxygen |
| Below carbon | zinc, iron, copper | heating the oxide with carbon (or carbon monoxide), which removes the oxygen |
| Very unreactive | gold (and sometimes silver) | can be found as the uncombined metal, so little or no chemical extraction is needed |

**Why carbon is the dividing line:** carbon can take oxygen only from the oxide of a metal that is *less* reactive than carbon. Electrolysis is more expensive because it uses large amounts of electricity, so it is used only when carbon cannot do the job.

## Iron: the blast furnace

### The raw materials and their jobs

| Raw material | What it is | Its job |
|---|---|---|
| Hematite | iron ore, mainly iron(III) oxide | the source of iron |
| Coke | almost pure carbon | burns to provide heat; makes the reducing agent, carbon monoxide |
| Limestone | calcium carbonate | removes the main impurity, silicon(IV) oxide (sand), as slag |
| Hot air | blown in near the bottom | supplies the oxygen for the coke to burn |

### The five stages

| Stage | Core description | Supplement equation |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Coke burns in the hot air, giving out heat and producing carbon dioxide | C + O₂ → CO₂ |
| 2 | Carbon dioxide is reduced by more coke to carbon monoxide | C + CO₂ → 2CO |
| 3 | Carbon monoxide reduces iron(III) oxide to iron | Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂ |
| 4 | Limestone is thermally decomposed to calcium oxide | CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂ |
| 5 | Calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide to form slag | CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃ |

**Following the redox.** In stage 3, iron(III) oxide **loses oxygen**, so it is **reduced**. Carbon monoxide **gains oxygen** to become carbon dioxide, so it is **oxidised**, and it is the **reducing agent**. In stage 2, carbon dioxide loses oxygen (reduced) and carbon gains it (oxidised).

**Slag is an acid–base reaction.** Calcium oxide is a **basic** oxide (a metal oxide); silicon(IV) oxide is an **acidic** oxide (a non-metal oxide). They neutralise each other to form calcium silicate, the slag. This links 9.6 to subtopic 7.2 Oxides.

**What comes out.** Molten iron collects at the bottom of the furnace and is run off. Molten slag is less dense, so it floats on top of the iron and is run off separately. Waste gases leave from the top.

## Aluminium: electrolysis

At Core level you need two facts: the main ore of aluminium is **bauxite**, and aluminium is extracted by **electrolysis**. Aluminium is above carbon in the series, so carbon cannot reduce its oxide.

### The cell in detail (Supplement)

- **Electrolyte:** purified bauxite, which is aluminium oxide, Al₂O₃, **dissolved in molten cryolite**.
- **Electrodes:** both are carbon (graphite). The cathode is the carbon lining of the cell; the anodes are carbon blocks dipping into the electrolyte.

**The role of cryolite.** Pure aluminium oxide has a melting point above 2000 °C. Dissolving it in molten cryolite gives an electrolyte that is liquid at a much lower temperature. Less energy is needed to keep the electrolyte molten, so the process is cheaper. The ions must be free to move for electrolysis to take place, which is why the electrolyte must be molten (or dissolved).

**At the cathode (negative electrode): reduction.** Aluminium ions gain electrons:

```
Al3+ + 3e− → Al
```

Molten aluminium collects at the bottom of the cell and is tapped off.

**At the anode (positive electrode): oxidation.** Oxide ions lose electrons:

```
2O2− → O2 + 4e−
```

**Why the anodes must be replaced.** The anodes are carbon, and the cell is very hot. The oxygen produced at the anode reacts with the carbon to form carbon dioxide:

```
C + O2 → CO2
```

So the anodes are gradually burned away and must be replaced regularly.

**Balancing the electrons.** To combine the half-equations, multiply so both involve 12 electrons: 4Al³⁺ + 12e⁻ → 4Al and 6O²⁻ → 3O₂ + 12e⁻. The overall change is 2Al₂O₃ → 4Al + 3O₂.

## Worked example: simple proportion

**Question.** Use relative formula masses to calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be obtained from 800 tonnes of pure iron(III) oxide. (Ar: Fe = 56, O = 16) [3]

```
Mr of Fe2O3 = (2 × 56) + (3 × 16) = 160             [1]
160 t of Fe2O3 contains (2 × 56) = 112 t of iron    [1]
800 t of Fe2O3 contains 112 × 800/160 = 560 t       [1]
```

*This uses reacting masses in simple proportion, which the syllabus lists as Core. Real ore is not pure iron(III) oxide, so the actual yield from the ore is lower.*

## Common mistakes

- **Saying carbon reduces iron(III) oxide directly.** In the furnace it is **carbon monoxide** that reduces iron(III) oxide.
- **Saying limestone removes iron impurities.** It removes **silicon(IV) oxide (sand)** as slag.
- **Mixing up the electrodes.** Aluminium forms at the **cathode** (negative); oxygen forms at the **anode** (positive).
- **Writing that cryolite is a catalyst.** It is a solvent that lowers the operating temperature.
- **Saying the anodes "dissolve".** They react with oxygen to form carbon dioxide.
- **Explaining the method by cost alone.** Link it to position relative to carbon.
- **Writing the anode half-equation as O²⁻ → O + 2e⁻.** Oxygen forms O₂ molecules: 2O²⁻ → O₂ + 4e⁻.

## Exam technique for 9.6 command words

- **Describe** (Core): give the stages in order in words, naming the substances and what each does.
- **State** (Supplement): write balanced symbol equations exactly; check atoms balance.
- **Explain:** link to reactivity ("aluminium is more reactive than carbon, so…") or to the chemistry ("oxygen reacts with the hot carbon anode…").
- **Identify:** say which substance is oxidised, reduced, or the reducing agent, using gain or loss of oxygen or electrons.
- **Calculate:** show the relative formula mass, then the proportion, with units.
