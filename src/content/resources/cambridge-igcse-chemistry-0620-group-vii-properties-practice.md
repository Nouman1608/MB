## Section B

**6.** Chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium bromide.

**(a)** Describe the colour change. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the balanced symbol equation. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the reaction takes place. **[2]**

**7.** A student adds bromine water to aqueous sodium chloride and to aqueous sodium iodide in separate test tubes.

**(a)** State what happens in each test tube. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the ionic equation for the reaction that occurs. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain the different results. **[2]**

**8.** A student is given three unlabelled colourless solutions: potassium chloride, potassium bromide and potassium iodide. Describe how chlorine water could be used to identify which is which. **[3]**

**9.** The table shows information about the halogens.

| Halogen | Melting point / °C | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Fluorine | −220 | Pale yellow |
| Chlorine | −101 | Pale yellow-green |
| Bromine | −7 | Red-brown |
| Iodine | 114 | Grey-black |
| Astatine | ? | ? |

**(a)** Predict the melting point of astatine. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the state and colour of astatine at r.t.p. **[2]**

**(c)** Predict whether astatine would displace iodine from aqueous potassium iodide. Explain. **[2]**

**10.** Fluorine is above chlorine in Group VII.

**(a)** Predict the reactivity of fluorine compared with chlorine. **[1]**

**(b)** Predict the products when fluorine reacts with aqueous potassium chloride. **[2]**

**11.** Explain why bromine is a liquid at r.t.p. while chlorine is a gas, using the trend in the group. **[1]**

**12.** Chlorine is used to treat water. A student says chlorine and chloride are the same substance. Give **two** differences between chlorine and chloride ions. **[2]**

**13.** Three halogens, labelled P, R and T, were each added to solutions of the halides of the other two. The letters are not chemical symbols.

| Halogen added | Halide of P | Halide of R | Halide of T |
|---|---|---|---|
| P | — | Colour change | Colour change |
| R | No change | — | No change |
| T | No change | Colour change | — |

**(a)** Put the three halogens in order of reactivity, most reactive first. **[1]**

**(b)** The three halogens are chlorine, bromine and iodine. Identify P, R and T. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how the results for halogen T support your order. **[2]**

**14.** Describe the test for chlorine gas and give the result. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Pale yellow-green gas [1].

**(b)** Red-brown liquid [1].

**(c)** Grey-black solid [1].

**2.** Each molecule contains two (chlorine) atoms, Cl₂ [1].

**3.** **C** [1].

**4. (a)** Increases [1].

**(b)** Decreases [1].

**5.** Br⁻ (1−) [1].

**6. (a)** From colourless [1] to orange [1].

**(b)** Cl₂ + 2KBr → 2KCl + Br₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Chlorine is more reactive than bromine [1]; so it displaces bromine from the bromide (taking electrons from the bromide ions) [1].

**7. (a)** Sodium chloride: no change / no reaction [1]; sodium iodide: turns brown [1].

**(b)** Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂: correct species [1]; balanced [1].

**(c)** Bromine is more reactive than iodine, so it displaces iodine [1]; bromine is less reactive than chlorine, so it cannot displace chlorine [1].

**8.** Add chlorine water to a sample of each solution [1]; the one that turns orange contains bromide; the one that turns brown contains iodide [1]; the one with no colour change is potassium chloride [1].

**9. (a)** Any value above 114 °C, e.g. 250–350 °C [1].

**(b)** Solid [1]; black / very dark (darker than iodine) [1].

**(c)** No [1]; astatine is less reactive than iodine, so it cannot displace it [1].

**10. (a)** More reactive [1].

**(b)** Potassium fluoride [1]; chlorine [1].

**11.** Melting and boiling points increase down the group, so bromine (below chlorine) has a higher boiling point, above room temperature [1].

**12.** Any two from: chlorine is an element made of Cl₂ molecules, chloride is an ion (Cl⁻) [1]; chlorine is a pale yellow-green gas, chloride ions are colourless in solution [1]; chlorine bleaches damp litmus paper, chloride ions do not.

**13. (a)** P > T > R [1].

**(b)** P is chlorine [1]; T is bromine and R is iodine [1].

**(c)** T displaces R from its halide, so T is more reactive than R [1]; T does not displace P, so T is less reactive than P [1].

**14.** Hold damp litmus paper in the gas [1]; it is bleached (turns white) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "chlorine" when the question is about chloride ions, or the reverse.
- Using single-atom formulae (Cl, Br, I) for the elements.
- Describing bromine as a gas, or iodine as purple at r.t.p.
- Giving a colour change without the starting colour.
- Explaining displacement without comparing the reactivity of the two halogens involved.
- Predicting astatine as more reactive than iodine.
