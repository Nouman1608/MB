These questions cover section **4.2.3.3 Graphene and fullerenes** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462)
specification, for teaching from September 2016 onwards (version 1.2). The content is examined on Paper 1 at
Foundation and Higher Tier, and no question here tests Higher-tier-only content. Diagrams are described in
words.

Learn the content first in the [graphene and fullerenes study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-graphene-and-fullerenes/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-graphene-and-fullerenes-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** **(a)** State what graphene is. **[1]**
**(b)** State the formula of Buckminsterfullerene. **[1]**

**2.** Carbon nanotubes are cylindrical fullerenes.

**(a)** State what is meant by a "very high length to diameter ratio". **[1]**
**(b)** Give **one** use of carbon nanotubes. **[1]**

**3.** A fullerene molecule has a hollow, spherical shape and contains 60 carbon atoms. Each carbon atom is
bonded to three others.

**(a)** Name this fullerene. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its relative formula mass. (Relative atomic mass: C = 12.) **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of covalent connections between atoms, if each connection joins two atoms. **[2]**

**4.** Choose the correct statement. **[1]**

- A Graphene has four covalent bonds per carbon atom.
- B Graphene is a single layer of graphite.
- C Graphene is a hollow sphere.
- D Graphene has no delocalised electrons.

## Section B

**5.** Explain why graphene conducts electricity. **[3]**

**6.** Graphene is very strong although it is only one atom thick. Explain why. **[2]**

**7.** Two diagrams are described.

- Diagram P: rings of six carbon atoms only, arranged in a flat sheet.
- Diagram Q: rings of five and six carbon atoms joined to make a hollow ball.

**(a)** Identify the structure in each diagram. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the rings in diagram Q can form a hollow shape. **[1]**

**8.** A company wants a lightweight, strong material for a tennis racket frame.

**(a)** Suggest a carbon material that could be added to the frame. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why it is suitable. **[2]**
**(c)** State **one** other use of the material in your answer to (a). **[1]**

**9.** The table compares forms of carbon.

| Form | Bonds per carbon atom | Particles | Conducts electricity? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diamond | 4 | Giant covalent | No |
| Graphite | 3 | Giant covalent | Yes |
| Graphene | 3 | Giant covalent (one layer) | Yes |
| C₆₀ | 3 | Molecules | Not covered here |

**(a)** Explain why diamond does not conduct but graphene does. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe **two** ways in which C₆₀ differs in structure from graphene. **[2]**

**10.** Compare the structure and bonding of graphite, graphene and a carbon nanotube. **[5]**

**11.** A company adds carbon nanotubes to a bicycle frame to make it stronger.

**(a)** Explain why carbon nanotubes make the frame stronger. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **one** reason why some people are concerned about the use of nanotubes. **[1]**

## Answers
## Answers

**1. (a)** A **single layer of graphite** [1]
**(b)** **C₆₀** [1]

**2. (a)** The tube is **very long compared with its width** [1]
**(b)** Any one: strengthening materials; electronics; nanotechnology [1]

**3. (a)** **Buckminsterfullerene** (C₆₀) [1]
**(b)** 60 × 12 = **720** [1]
**(c)** 60 × 3 ÷ 2 [1] = **90** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1]

**5.** Each carbon atom is bonded to three others, using three outer electrons [1]; the fourth electron is
delocalised and free to move [1]; the delocalised electrons carry charge across the sheet [1]. **[3]**

**6.** All the atoms are joined by **strong covalent bonds** across the sheet [1]; a very large force is needed to
break these many bonds [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** P: **graphene** (or a layer of graphite) [1]; Q: a **fullerene** (such as C₆₀) [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Rings with **five** carbon atoms (as well as hexagons) make the structure curve [1]

**8. (a)** **Carbon nanotubes** (or graphene) [1]
**(b)** They are very strong because of strong covalent bonds [1]; only a small quantity is needed, so the frame
stays light [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Any one: electronics; nanotechnology; composites [1]

**9. (a)** In diamond all four outer electrons are used in covalent bonds, so there are no delocalised electrons
[1]; in graphene one electron per atom is delocalised [1] and free to move and carry charge [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Any two [1] [1]: C₆₀ is a **molecule** and graphene is a giant structure; C₆₀ is **hollow and spherical**
whereas graphene is a **flat sheet**; C₆₀ has rings of **five** carbon atoms as well as six. **[2]**

**10.** Marking points, up to 5:
- All three are made of carbon atoms with each atom forming three covalent bonds [1].
- All three are based on hexagonal rings of carbon atoms [1].
- Graphite has many layers with no covalent bonds between the layers [1].
- Graphene is a single layer of graphite [1].
- A carbon nanotube is a cylindrical fullerene, a hollow tube with a very high length to diameter ratio [1].
- Each has one delocalised electron per carbon atom [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** Carbon nanotubes contain **strong covalent bonds** throughout the structure [1]; a very large force is
needed to break them, and only a small mass is needed, so the frame is strong but light [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Any one: very small particles may be **harmful to health** if they get into the body; the **long-term
effects** are not fully known [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that graphene has multiple layers.
- Calling C₆₀ a giant covalent structure.
- Leaving out the delocalised electron in a conduction explanation.
- Forgetting that fullerenes can contain rings of five or seven atoms.
- Not linking a use to a property.
