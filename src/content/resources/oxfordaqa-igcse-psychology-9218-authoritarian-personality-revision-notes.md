Quick-recall notes for "Explanation of dispositional factors affecting obedience. Adorno's theory of
the Authoritarian Personality", part of **3.2.2 Social influence** in OxfordAQA International GCSE
Psychology (9218). For full explanations and a comparison answer, use the
[Authoritarian Personality study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-authoritarian-personality/);
for Milgram's research and Agency theory, see the
[Conformity and Obedience revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-conformity-obedience-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Dispositional explanation** | Behaviour explained by the person's characteristics, such as personality |
| **Situational (social) explanation** | Behaviour explained by features of the situation |
| **Authoritarian Personality** | Personality type marked by submission to authority and hostility to those of lower status |
| **F-scale** | Adorno's questionnaire measuring authoritarian (potentially fascist) attitudes |
| **Authoritarian submission** | Exaggerated respect for and obedience to authority |
| **Authoritarian aggression** | Harshness towards people seen as inferior or rule-breakers |
| **Conventionalism** | Rigid commitment to traditional values |
| **Conditional love** | Affection given only when the child behaves as required |
| **Displacement** | Redirecting hostility from its real target onto a safer one |
| **Scapegoat** | A weaker person or group blamed and targeted for others' frustrations |
| **Acquiescence bias** | Tendency to agree with statements whatever their content |
| **Locus of control** | Belief about whether events are controlled by oneself (internal) or outside forces (external) |

## The theory in five steps

1. **Strict, harsh parenting:** severe discipline, demands for obedience, conditional love.
2. Child feels **hostility** to parents but cannot express it.
3. Child **identifies** with the powerful parent and learns to respect authority.
4. Hostility is **displaced** onto weaker groups (scapegoats).
5. Adult has an **authoritarian personality**: obeys those above, is harsh to those below.

## Traits to name

Authoritarian submission · authoritarian aggression · conventionalism · rigid, black-and-white
thinking · prejudice towards out-groups.

## Why authoritarians obey

- Respect authority for its own sake.
- Do not question orders (rigid thinking).
- May see a weak or different victim as deserving punishment.

## The research

- **Who:** Adorno, Frenkel-Brunswik, Levinson and Sanford (1950).
- **Sample:** over two thousand white, middle-class Americans.
- **Method:** questionnaires including the F-scale, plus in-depth interviews with high and low scorers.
- **Finding:** high scorers tended to report strict, punitive upbringings and showed authoritarian
  traits and prejudice.

## Supporting study: Elms and Milgram (1966)

Followed up Milgram's participants: those who had **obeyed fully** scored **higher** on the F-scale than
those who **defied** the experimenter.

## Evaluation summary

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| Elms and Milgram link F-scale scores to obedience | Cannot explain obedience of whole societies |
| Explains why some people in the same situation obey and others refuse | Milgram's variations show obedience changes with the situation |
| Mixed quantitative and qualitative data | F-scale open to acquiescence bias (all items worded one way) |
| | Correlational: upbringing may not cause the personality; education may be a third factor |
| | White, middle-class American sample |
| | Measures right-wing authoritarianism only |

## Second dispositional factor: locus of control

- **Internal:** "I control what happens to me" → more likely to resist unjust orders.
- **External:** "outside forces control what happens" → more likely to obey.

## Comparing with Agency theory

| | Agency theory | Authoritarian Personality |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Social (situational) | Dispositional |
| Why people obey | Agentic state; responsibility passed to authority | Personality formed by harsh upbringing |
| Who obeys | Most people, in the right situation | Mainly people with authoritarian traits |
| Key evidence | Milgram's variations | Elms and Milgram's F-scale follow-up |

## Exam traps

- The Authoritarian Personality is **dispositional**; Agency theory is **social**.
- Include the **upbringing**, not just the traits.
- The F-scale's "F" stands for **fascism**.
- "It cannot explain Nazi Germany" needs the reason: **too many people obeyed** for one personality type.
- Acquiescence bias is about **wording**: agreeing always scored as authoritarian.

## Self-test

1. What is a dispositional explanation of obedience?
2. Who proposed the Authoritarian Personality, and when was it published?
3. What did the F-scale measure?
4. Name three traits of the authoritarian personality.
5. Describe the type of upbringing Adorno linked to authoritarianism.
6. Explain the role of displacement in the theory.
7. Give two reasons an authoritarian person is likely to obey.
8. What did Elms and Milgram find?
9. Why is the F-scale open to acquiescence bias?
10. Why does the theory struggle to explain obedience across a whole society?
11. Explain why the research is correlational and why that matters.
12. How might locus of control affect obedience?

**Answers:**
1. An explanation based on the person's own characteristics, such as personality, rather than the
   situation.
2. Adorno and colleagues (Frenkel-Brunswik, Levinson and Sanford); 1950.
3. Authoritarian or potentially fascist attitudes.
4. Any three: authoritarian submission; authoritarian aggression; conventionalism; rigid,
   black-and-white thinking; prejudice towards out-groups.
5. Strict, harsh parenting with severe discipline, demands for obedience and conditional love.
6. Hostility felt towards the parents cannot be expressed, so it is redirected onto weaker groups,
   who become scapegoats.
7. Any two: they respect authority for its own sake; they do not question orders; they may see the
   victim as deserving punishment.
8. Fully obedient participants from Milgram's studies scored higher on the F-scale than defiant ones.
9. All its statements were worded so that agreeing showed authoritarianism, so people who tend to agree
   with anything would score highly.
10. So many people obeyed that they could not all have had the same personality and upbringing;
    situational factors explain this better.
11. It shows a link between upbringing and authoritarian scores, not that one causes the other; a third
    factor such as education could affect both.
12. People with an internal locus are more likely to resist orders; people with an external locus are
    more likely to obey.
