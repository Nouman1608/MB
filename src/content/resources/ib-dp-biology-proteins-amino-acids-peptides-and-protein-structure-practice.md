## Questions

**1.** Sketch a generalized amino acid. Label the alpha carbon and the three groups and one atom attached to it. **[3]**

**2.** A dipeptide is made from two amino acids.

**(a)** Write the word equation for this reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Name the bond formed and state which two parts are removed to make the small molecule. **[2]**
**(c)** State how many water molecules are produced when a tetrapeptide forms. **[1]**

**3.** A polypeptide contains 240 amino acids in one unbranched chain. The average mass of an amino acid unit in the chain, after water loss, is 110 daltons.

**(a)** State the number of peptide bonds. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of water molecules released during its formation. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of the polypeptide. **[2]**

**4.** Rice protein is short of essential amino acid L. Lentil protein is short of essential amino acid M.

**(a)** Define "essential amino acid". **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a vegan diet needs attention to protein sources. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest, with reasoning, how a cook could use both foods. **[2]**

**5.** Peptide chains can be built from the 20 amino acids coded for in the genetic code.

**(a)** State how many different tripeptides are possible. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many different chains of eight amino acids are possible. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the number of different proteins is far greater than 20. **[2]**

**6.** The table shows the activity of a bacterial enzyme after incubation at different temperatures. Activity is in arbitrary units.

| Temperature (degrees Celsius) | 25 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 70 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Activity | 40 | 92 | 68 | 15 | 3 |

**(a)** Identify the optimum temperature from these data. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in activity between 40 and 60 degrees Celsius. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the fall in activity above the optimum. **[3]**

**7.** Outline how you would test whether pH changes the rate at which a protease digests a protein. Include the variable you change, one way to measure the response, and two variables to keep constant. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only) Four R-groups are described: a hydrocarbon chain; a hydroxyl group; a carboxyl group; an amine group. For each, state whether the R-group is hydrophobic or hydrophilic and, if charged, whether it is acidic or basic. **[4]**

**9.** (HL only) In an enzyme, cysteine at position 12 and cysteine at position 87 form a disulfide bond. A mutation replaces the cysteine at 87 with alanine, an amino acid with a non-polar R-group.

**(a)** Explain the effect on the tertiary structure. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why a change in primary structure can change function. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) A region of a protein contains 63 amino acids in an alpha helix. There are 3.6 amino acids per turn and each adds 0.15 nm to the length (data supplied).

**(a)** Calculate the number of turns. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the length of the helix in nm. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the helix is stabilized. **[2]**

**11.** (HL only) Soluble globular proteins and integral membrane proteins both contain hydrophobic amino acids.

**(a)** Describe where the hydrophobic amino acids are in a soluble globular protein and why. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe their role in an integral protein. **[2]**
**(c)** A basic R-group and an acidic R-group form an ionic bond. Explain how a large drop in pH could break it. **[3]**

**12.** (HL only) Insulin consists of two polypeptides of 21 and 30 amino acids. Collagen consists of three polypeptides wound together. Haemoglobin has four polypeptides each with a haem group.

**(a)** Distinguish between conjugated and non-conjugated proteins, classifying all three. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of peptide bonds in insulin. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the shape of insulin and collagen suits the function of each. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest how cryogenic electron microscopy has advanced knowledge of protein structure. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Central carbon drawn and labelled alpha carbon [1]; amine group NH2 and carboxyl group COOH both bonded to it [1]; hydrogen atom and R-group both bonded to it [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Diagram questions usually reward correct attachment, so check that all four labels join the same carbon and that the R-group is not on the nitrogen.

**2. (a)** Amino acid + amino acid → dipeptide + water [1]
**(b)** Peptide bond [1]; OH from the carboxyl group of one and H from the amine group of the other [1].
**(c)** Three [1], because four units have three bonds. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the bond and also the atoms removed shows you understand condensation. A bare "water" does not say where it came from.

**3. (a)** 239 [1]
**(b)** 239 [1]
**(c)** 240 × 110 [1] = **26 400 daltons** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the multiplication. A common slip is 240 bonds, from forgetting that n units have n - 1 links.

**4. (a)** An amino acid the body cannot make and must obtain from food [1].
**(b)** Some plant proteins are short of one or more essential amino acids [1].
**(c)** Combine rice and lentils in the same day [1], because each supplies the amino acid the other lacks, giving all essential amino acids [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Suggest" wants a reason linked to the data in the stem, so name both missing amino acids rather than only saying "balanced diet".

**5. (a)** 20^3 = **8000** [1]
**(b)** 20^8 [1] = **2.56 × 10^10** (25 600 000 000) [1]
**(c)** Chains can be any length from a few to thousands of units [1]; the 20 amino acids can be arranged in any order [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both length and order. Saying only "many combinations" without these gains little.

**6. (a)** 40 degrees Celsius (the highest activity tested) [1]
**(b)** (92 - 15) / 92 × 100 [1] = **83.7 %** decrease [1]
**(c)** Higher temperature gives more kinetic energy [1]; hydrogen and ionic bonds maintaining the folded shape break, so the enzyme unfolds (denaturation) [1]; the active site changes shape so substrate no longer binds, and activity falls [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* State the denominator in a percentage change. Answers that say peptide bonds break lose credit because denaturation leaves them intact.

**7.** Independent variable: different buffer pH values, for example pH 3 to 11 [1]; measure the response such as time for the protein substrate to clear or the mass of product formed [1]; keep two of temperature, enzyme concentration, substrate concentration and volume constant [1]; repeat each pH and take a mean [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" needs each element named. Listing "keep everything the same" earns nothing without two specific variables.

**8.** Hydrocarbon chain: hydrophobic (non-polar) [1]; hydroxyl group: hydrophilic, polar and uncharged [1]; carboxyl group: hydrophilic and acidic, negatively charged after losing H+ [1]; amine group: hydrophilic and basic, positively charged after gaining H+ [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the charge change as well as the label. "Acidic" alone does not show why a carboxyl group can join an ionic bond.

**9. (a)** The disulfide bond between positions 12 and 87 can no longer form [1]; this covalent link held two parts of the chain together, so the folding changes [1]; alanine is hydrophobic, so new hydrophobic interactions may form that alter the shape [1].
**(b)** The sequence decides which R-groups interact [1], so a change alters the 3D shape and the active site or binding site no longer fits [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the lost bond to the whole shape and then to function. Stopping after "the bond is lost" is incomplete.

**10. (a)** 63 / 3.6 [1] = **17.5 turns** [1]
**(b)** 63 × 0.15 [1] = **9.45 nm** [1]
**(c)** Hydrogen bonds [1] between groups of the backbone in regular positions along the chain [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the division or multiplication and give units. Say the bonds are in the backbone, not between R-groups.

**11. (a)** Hydrophobic amino acids gather in the centre of the molecule [1], away from the surrounding water, with polar ones outside [1].
**(b)** The hydrophobic regions are in the part within the bilayer's hydrophobic core [1], which helps the protein embed in the membrane [1].
**(c)** At low pH the acidic group gains H+ and loses its negative charge [1]; the attraction with the positive basic group is lost, so the ionic bond breaks [1]; the tertiary structure then changes [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A good pH answer follows the hydrogen ions: which group gains or loses them and what charge results.

**12. (a)** A conjugated protein contains a non-protein part [1]; insulin and collagen are non-conjugated [1]; haemoglobin is conjugated, with haem [1].
**(b)** (21 - 1) + (30 - 1) = **49** [1]
**(c)** Insulin is compact and globular, soluble in blood, and its shape fits the receptor [1]; collagen is long and fibrous, with three chains wound together [1], giving strength and insolubility for support [1].
**(d)** Cryo-electron microscopy can image single protein molecules [1] and their interactions with other molecules, which cannot be seen unaided [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* For form and function, name the shape feature and the property it gives. Naming the function alone does not link the two.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Attaching the R-group to the wrong atom in a diagram.
- Writing n bonds for n amino acids instead of n - 1.
- Saying denaturation breaks peptide bonds.
- Giving the optimum as a value between measured points without saying it is only the best tested.
- Not stating the denominator in a percentage change.
- Describing a hydrophobic effect as "bonds" without saying the groups cluster away from water.
- Confusing hydrogen bonds in secondary structure with those between R-groups in tertiary structure.
- Naming a function without linking it to shape.
- Leaving out units on lengths and masses.
