These notes are for **HL only**. The culture extension is assessed in Paper 3 (1 hour 45 minutes, 30 marks), which SL students do not sit. They condense the IB Psychology guide for first assessment 2027. Read the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-hl-extension-culture/) for detail and test yourself with the [practice set](/resources/ib-dp-psychology-2027-hl-extension-culture-practice/).

## Core ideas

- Culture explains differences in behaviour across populations.
- Three levels to keep in mind: **local culture**, the culture you are **enculturated** into, and **global culture**.
- **Cultural diffusion** (and globalisation) raises questions about the effect on mental and physical health, identity and relationships.
- The extension is applied **inside** the four contexts; Paper 3 sources will fit one extension within one context.
- Related concept: **perspective** (including Indigenous perspectives), and also bias, measurement and responsibility.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ecological fallacy | Assuming an individual shares the norms of their cultural group |
| Imposed etic bias | Applying findings or measures from one culture to another |
| Etic | Outsider approach comparing cultures with the same measures |
| Emic | Insider approach studying a culture in its own terms |
| Enculturation | Learning the norms of one's own culture |
| Acculturation | Adapting to a new culture; Berry's strategies are integration, assimilation, separation, marginalisation |
| Cultural dimensions | Ways in which cultures differ, such as individualism-collectivism and power distance (Hofstede) |
| Indigenous psychology | Psychology developed from within Indigenous communities and their perspectives |
| Transferability | How far qualitative findings can apply to another context |

## Areas of inquiry (from the guide)

- **Health and well-being:** culture in health and well-being; diagnosing and treating mental health issues; cross-cultural prevalence.
- **Human development:** cultural dimensions in children's social and cognitive development; Western models and Indigenous cultures; culture and the self.
- **Human relationships:** cross-cultural relationship strategies; cross-cultural communication.
- **Learning and cognition:** culture and learning and cognition; bilingualism and other cognitive processes; similarity and difference across cultures.

## Example evidence (examples only)

- **Hofstede dimensions:** national differences on, for example, individualism-collectivism. Strength: allows comparison. Limit: averages, ecological fallacy.
- **Markus and Kitayama (1991):** independent versus interdependent self-construals. Useful for the self in development.
- **Berry's acculturation model:** integration is often linked with better mental health outcomes than marginalisation. Limit: strategies are not always chosen freely.
- **Durie's Te Whare Tapa Whā:** a Māori model of well-being with four connected parts: spiritual, mental, family and physical. An Indigenous perspective on health.
- **Becker et al. (Fiji):** disordered eating attitudes among adolescent girls rose after the introduction of television. Correlational; cannot isolate television.
- **van IJzendoorn and Kroonenberg (1988):** secure attachment was the most common type in all countries studied, with variation in the other types.
- **Masuda and Nisbett (2001):** holistic versus analytic attention.
- **Bilingualism:** advantage in executive control reported in some studies, mixed in others.

## Research problems checklist

| Problem | What to say |
|---|---|
| Operationalising culture | "Culture" is not a variable; use country, language or a dimension, and say the limitation |
| Ecological fallacy | Group means do not describe individuals |
| Imposed etic | A measure from one culture may not be valid in another; translation matters |
| Sampling | Students or urban samples do not represent a nation |
| Quasi-experimental | Culture cannot be randomly assigned; other differences confound |
| Researcher and cultural bias | Researcher's own culture shapes questions and interpretation; use reflexivity and local researchers |
| Transferability | Describe context in detail for qualitative studies |
| Responsibility | Avoid stereotypes and stigma; report uncertainty |

## Paper 3 technique

The paper has four source-based questions. Know the markbands.

| Question | Skill | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Explain one issue that limits the interpretation of information from a source | 3 |
| 2 | Analyse the findings from one source and state a conclusion | 6 |
| 3 | Credibility, bias or transferability in one source | 6 |
| 4 | Synthesis of at least three sources to discuss the validity of a claim | 15 |

For culture:
- **Question 1:** a typical limit is that the mean hides variation, or the sample is not representative of the whole culture.
- **Question 2:** quote values from the graph or table, state the difference, then a conclusion that links to the finding and does not go beyond it.
- **Question 3:** use explicit evidence from the source. "To what extent are the findings transferable?" needs a decision.
- **Question 4:** interpret three or more sources, bring your own knowledge of the extension, present different points of view and reach a reasoned conclusion on the claim.

## Common mistakes

- Treating a culture as uniform.
- Using Western studies as if universal.
- Ignoring the limits of cross-cultural quasi-experiments.
- Not referring to the sources in Paper 3.

## Self-test

1. Define the ecological fallacy.
2. Give the guide's own example of imposed etic bias.
3. Name the four acculturation strategies in Berry's model.
4. Why is a cross-cultural comparison a quasi-experiment?
5. State one reason why prevalence rates of a disorder might differ between countries, other than real differences in how common it is.
6. What does transferability mean?
7. In Paper 3, how many marks is the synthesis question?

**Answers:** 1. Assuming that because someone belongs to a cultural group they share its norms. 2. Applying findings about modesty in one African culture to a South American culture because both are agricultural societies. 3. Integration, assimilation, separation, marginalisation. 4. Participants are not randomly assigned to a culture, so other differences may explain results. 5. Differences in diagnosis, help-seeking, stigma or measures. 6. How far qualitative findings can be applied to other populations or contexts. 7. 15.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does the guide name studies for the culture extension?**
No. It prints areas of inquiry in each context, so choose examples you know well.

**Is the culture extension only about other countries?**
No. It also covers local culture, enculturation and the effects of global culture on your own community.

**Do SL students study it?**
No. The extensions are HL only. (Data analysis and interpretation is also studied at SL but is not assessed there.)
