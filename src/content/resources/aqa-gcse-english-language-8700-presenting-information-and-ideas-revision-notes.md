These notes condense **presenting information and ideas**, the first strand of 3.1.3 Spoken language in AQA GCSE
English Language (8700): selecting and organising information and ideas effectively and persuasively for prepared
spoken presentations; planning for different purposes and audiences; making presentations and speeches. It is
assessed by **AO7**: demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting.

## The strand in one paragraph

Choose a topic with enough depth for challenging ideas, select only the information your listeners need, organise it
in a visible shape with spoken signposts, adapt it to the audience and purpose, and prepare thoroughly: the
specification warns that lack of preparation is likely to block the higher grades.

## Requirements to remember

| Requirement | Detail |
|---|---|
| Status | Compulsory; reported separately; 0% of the GCSE grade |
| Grades | Pass, Merit, Distinction or Not Classified |
| Topic | Specific, identified in advance and agreed with the teacher |
| Length | As a guide, no more than ten minutes |
| Register | Formal |
| Forms allowed | Talks, debates, speeches, dialogues and others |
| Aids | Notes or slides allowed, not required |
| Teacher-only audience | Must be designed for a potentially wider audience, for example a television interview |
| Questions | Responding to questions and feedback is part of the assessment |

## The criteria for presenting

General criteria for every grade: be audible; use spoken Standard English (be intelligible and generally use language
appropriate to the formal setting).

| | Pass | Merit | Distinction |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ideas | Straightforward | Challenging, with a range of vocabulary | Sophisticated, with a sophisticated repertoire of vocabulary |
| Structure | An attempt to organise and structure | Clear and appropriate to the audience's needs | An effective range of strategies to engage the audience |
| Audience and purpose | An attempt to meet the audience's needs | Achieves the purpose | Achieves the purpose |

**Competency basis:** every criterion in a grade must be met for that grade to be awarded.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| AO7 | Demonstrate presentation skills in a formal setting |
| Formal setting | Speaking to an audience beyond close friends, with a serious purpose |
| Signposting | Spoken phrases that show the structure ("my second point...") |
| Hook | An opening that wins attention |
| Cue cards | Small cards with keywords only, not a script |
| Purpose | Inform, explain, argue or persuade |
| Holistic assessment | One overall grade, with no marks |
| Competency | All criteria for a grade must be met |

## Turning a subject into a topic with depth

- Make it a **question** ("Should...?", "Does...?", "What can...?").
- Make it **specific** to a place, group or decision.
- Make sure it has **more than one side** or something to explain beyond the obvious.

## Selection test for every piece of information

1. Does it serve the purpose?
2. Will the audience understand it on one hearing?
3. Is it memorable when heard, not read?

## Spoken structures

| Shape | Best for |
|---|---|
| Problem and solution | Proposals and campaigns |
| Claim, reasons, counter-argument | Persuasive speeches and debates |
| Past, present, future | Explaining change |
| Two options compared | Weighing a decision |
| Myth-busting | Correcting common misunderstandings |

## Making structure audible

- Preview: "I'll cover three things."
- Transition: "That brings me to..."
- Recap: "So far we've seen..."
- Close: return to the opening image or question.

## Written style versus spoken style

- Shorter sentences, one idea at a time.
- More repetition of key ideas.
- Pauses in place of parenthesis.
- Formal, but natural: not an essay read aloud.

## Visual aids: use or lose

- **Use** a slide for one image, a single key figure or a short quotation the audience needs to see.
- **Lose** slides that repeat your words: listeners read them instead of listening.
- **Notes:** keywords on cue cards, numbered, so that a dropped card does not derail the talk.

## Traps

- A flat, list-like topic that limits ideas to "straightforward".
- Reading a full script.
- No signposts, so listeners get lost.
- Slides full of text.
- Too much material for the time.
- Treating a teacher-only audience as an audience of one.
- Little rehearsal, which the specification links to lower grades.

## Quick self-test

1. Which assessment objective covers presenting?
2. As a guide, what is the maximum length of the presentation?
3. Name three forms the presentation may take.
4. Who must agree the topic?
5. What are the two general criteria every grade requires?
6. What word describes ideas at Merit, and at Distinction?
7. What does "competency basis" mean?
8. How should a teacher-only presentation be designed?
9. Give two signposting phrases.
10. Why does spoken structure need to be more visible than written structure?
11. Turn "Pets" into a topic with depth.
12. Are slides required?

### Answers

1. AO7.
2. No more than ten minutes.
3. Any three: talks, debates, speeches, dialogues.
4. The teacher, in advance, with the student.
5. Being audible, and using spoken Standard English (intelligible, generally appropriate to the formal setting).
6. Merit: challenging. Distinction: sophisticated.
7. A student must meet every criterion for a grade to be awarded it.
8. So that it could have a wider audience, for example as a television interview.
9. For example: "My first point is..."; "That brings me to..."; "To sum up...".
10. Listeners cannot go back and reread, so they rely on spoken signals to follow the shape.
11. For example: "Should landlords be allowed to ban pets from rented homes?"
12. No. Notes or slides may be used, but they are not a requirement.
