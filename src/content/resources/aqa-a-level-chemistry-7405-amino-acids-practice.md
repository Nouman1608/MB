These questions cover section **3.3.13.1 Amino acids** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Amino acids study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amino-acids/) and the [Amino acids revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-amino-acids-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** Draw the structural formula of the amino acid alanine, H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COOH, and name the two functional groups. **[3]**

**2.** Explain why amino acids are described as amphoteric. **[2]**

**3.** Draw the structure of glycine as a zwitterion and explain how it forms. **[3]**

**4.** Draw the structure of the species formed by alanine in:

**(a)** strongly acidic solution **[1]**

**(b)** strongly alkaline solution **[1]**

**5.** State the overall charge on each species in question 4. **[1]**

**6.** Glycine is a white solid with a melting point (with decomposition) of about 230 °C, whereas propylamine, which has a similar relative molecular mass, is a liquid at room temperature. Explain the difference. **[3]**

**7.** Explain why glycine is soluble in water. **[2]**

**8.** Write equations for the reaction of glycine with:

**(a)** hydrochloric acid **[1]**

**(b)** sodium hydroxide solution **[1]**

**9.** The structure of serine is H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COOH. Draw the zwitterion of serine and the ion formed in alkaline solution. **[2]**

**10.** Which of glycine and alanine can show optical isomerism? Explain your answer. **[2]**

**11.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid exactly reacts with a solution containing alanine (Mᵣ = 89.0).

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in mol, of hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of alanine in the solution. **[2]**

**12.** Glycine (Mᵣ = 75.0) and alanine (Mᵣ = 89.0) react to form a dipeptide.

**(a)** Name the small molecule lost. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the dipeptide. **[2]**

**13.** A student claims that the zwitterion of an amino acid is uncharged. Explain why this is only partly correct. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COOH drawn with the central carbon bonded to H, CH₃, NH₂ and COOH [1]; amino group (–NH₂) [1]; carboxyl group (–COOH) [1]. **[3]**

**2.** They contain a basic group (–NH₂) and an acidic group (–COOH) [1]; so they can react with both acids and alkalis [1]. **[2]**

**3.** ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻ [1]; the acidic –COOH group donates a proton [1]; to the basic –NH₂ group in the same molecule [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₃)–COOH [1]

**(b)** H₂N–CH(CH₃)–COO⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**5.** (a) 1+ and (b) 1− [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Glycine exists as zwitterions, ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COO⁻, in the solid [1]; the zwitterions are held in a lattice by strong ionic attractions [1]; propylamine has only weaker hydrogen bonds and London forces between molecules, so it is a liquid with a low boiling point [1]. **[3]**

**7.** The glycine zwitterion has charged groups, –NH₃⁺ and –COO⁻ [1]; which form strong ion–dipole attractions with water molecules [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** H₂N–CH₂–COOH + HCl → ⁺H₃N–CH₂–COOH Cl⁻ [1]

**(b)** H₂N–CH₂–COOH + NaOH → H₂N–CH₂–COO⁻Na⁺ + H₂O [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Zwitterion: ⁺H₃N–CH(CH₂OH)–COO⁻ [1]; alkaline: H₂N–CH(CH₂OH)–COO⁻ [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Alanine can show optical isomerism [1]; its central carbon has four different groups (H, CH₃, NH₂, COOH), whereas glycine has two hydrogen atoms on that carbon [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** n(HCl) = 0.0250 × 0.100 = **0.00250 mol** [1]

**(b)** n(alanine) = 0.00250 mol (1 : 1) [1]; mass = 0.00250 × 89.0 = **0.2225 g** [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Water [1]

**(b)** Mᵣ = 75.0 + 89.0 − 18.0 [1] = **146.0** [1]. **[3]**

**13.** The zwitterion has no overall charge because the +1 and −1 charges cancel [1]; but it contains a positively charged group (–NH₃⁺) and a negatively charged group (–COO⁻), so it is not uncharged within the molecule [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Always write the charges on the groups, and do not put an overall charge on the zwitterion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing the zwitterion as H₂N–CHR–COO⁻ or ⁺H₃N–CHR–COOH.
- Omitting the charges on the groups.
- Giving the acid and alkaline forms the wrong way round.
- Explaining the high melting point in terms of molecular size.
- Describing glycine as chiral.
- Using the wrong ratio in the titration calculation.
