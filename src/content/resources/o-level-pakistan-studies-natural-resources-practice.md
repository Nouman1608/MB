## Section A

**1.** Name the three major dams identified in this section. **[3]**

**2.** State the difference between a plantation and afforestation, as
defined in this syllabus. **[2]**

**3.** Name three effects of deforestation. **[3]**

**4.** State the difference between marine and inland fishing
locations in Pakistan. **[2]**

**5.** Name one metallic and one non-metallic mineral resource
covered in this section. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The Indus Water Treaty is one of the most exam-significant
items in this section.

**(a)** Explain one reason the Indus Water Treaty was needed. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one consequence that has followed from the treaty. **[2]**

**7.** A factory near a river discharges untreated waste into the
water, which is also used for irrigation downstream.

**(a)** Explain the effect this pollution has on water availability
for farmers downstream. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest one solution to this water supply problem. **[2]**

**8.** A region plants new forest in the Indus Plain for timber
production, while a different region plants trees on steep mountain
slopes.

**(a)** Identify which activity is a plantation and which is
afforestation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the different purpose behind each activity. **[2]**

**9.** A mining company extracts a mineral resource for export, but
local communities report damage to nearby land and water sources.

**(a)** Suggest one benefit this extraction could bring to Pakistan's
economy. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest one environmental problem the extraction could be
causing. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why an answer evaluating this situation should pair a
named benefit with a named environmental cost, rather than describing
only one side. **[2]**

**10.** A fishing community reports improved catch sizes after
adopting new fishing equipment, but is also concerned about
overfishing in local waters.

**(a)** Suggest one improvement in fishing methods or processing that
could explain the better catch sizes. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why an answer describing this improvement is
incomplete without also addressing the sustainability concern raised. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Mangla, Tarbela and Warsak [3].

**2.** A plantation is new forest planted in the Indus Plain for
commercial or resource use; afforestation is planting on mountain
slopes and plateaux, typically to prevent soil erosion and manage
water flow [2].

**3.** Any three of: soil erosion, silting, flooding [3].

**4.** Marine fishing takes place along the Balochistan and Sindh
coasts; inland fishing takes place in rivers, lakes and fish farms
[2].

**5.** Any correctly identified metallic mineral (for example, from
Pakistan's metallic mineral resources) and any correctly identified
non-metallic mineral, such as limestone, gypsum or rock salt [2].

**6. (a)** The treaty was needed to divide and manage the shared use
of the Indus river system's waters between the countries through
which it flows, since competing demands on a shared water source can
otherwise lead to disputes over supply [2].
**(b)** Any reasonable consequence, for example: it established
agreed rights to specific rivers for each country, which has shaped
how water infrastructure (such as dams and irrigation systems) has
since been planned and built [2].

**7. (a)** The pollution contaminates the water, reducing the amount
of clean water available for irrigation downstream and potentially
harming crops or requiring costly treatment before the water can be
used safely [2].
**(b)** Stricter enforcement of industrial waste-treatment regulations
before discharge is permitted, alongside investment in water-treatment
infrastructure [2].

**8. (a)** Planting new forest in the Indus Plain for timber
production is a plantation; planting trees on steep mountain slopes is
afforestation [2].
**(b)** The plantation is aimed at commercial or resource use, such as
producing timber for sale [1]; the afforestation is aimed at
preventing soil erosion and managing water flow on steep terrain,
rather than primarily for commercial production [1].

**9. (a)** For example, export revenue or providing raw material
input for industry [1].
**(b)** For example, damage to land through extraction activity, or
contamination of nearby water sources [1].
**(c)** Because an "evaluate" question is specifically asking for a
weighed judgement, not a one-sided description; pairing a named
benefit with a named environmental cost demonstrates the ability to
assess sustainability, which is what this type of question rewards
over listing benefits or problems in isolation [2].

**10. (a)** For example, adopting improved nets, boats, or fish
processing techniques that increase catch efficiency or reduce
spoilage [1].
**(b)** Because the syllabus explicitly pairs improvements in fishing
methods and processing with the problems facing the fishing industry;
an answer that only describes the improvement without addressing
sustainability concerns such as overfishing does not engage with the
full scope of what the question is asking about the industry's
future development [2].

## A note on exam technique for this topic

Across all four sub-sections of this topic — water, forests, minerals
and fish — the strongest answers follow the same underlying shape:
name a specific resource or feature, state a specific cause or use,
and pair it with a specific consequence or solution, rather than
describing any one resource in general terms. The Indus Water Treaty
in particular should always be treated as something to explain (its
reasons and consequences), not simply a fact to state existed. Keep
Section 1's map knowledge (dams, rivers, coastal regions) close at
hand while revising this section, since nearly every sub-topic here
asks you to locate a feature before analysing it.
