**Key question 1.1 in Cambridge O Level History 2147 asks whether the revolutions of 1848 were
important.** You need the causes, events and results in four places (France, Italy, Germany and the
Austrian Empire), the influence of liberalism and nationalism, and a judgement on why they failed and
what they changed. For the full explanation, use the
[Revolutions of 1848 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-revolutions-1848/).
Test yourself with the [Revolutions of 1848 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-revolutions-1848-practice/).

## Why had liberalism and nationalism grown?

- Vienna settlement (1815) ignored nationality; Metternich's censorship.
- Education, newspapers, student societies spread ideas.
- Memory of the French Revolution.
- Mazzini's Young Italy (1831).
- Zollverein (1834) showed German economic unity.

## Why so many revolutions in 1848?

**E**conomic crisis (potato blight, poor harvests, slump, unemployment) · **L**ong-standing grievances
· **F**rench example spreading quickly · **W**eak rulers giving way.
Hook: **ELF-W**.

## What the revolutions had in common, and how they differed

| Common | Different |
|---|---|
| Liberal demands: constitutions, parliaments, free press | France: form of government and workers' demands |
| Middle-class and student leadership, worker support | Italy and Germany: unification |
| Economic hardship | Austrian Empire: rights or independence for nationalities |
| Broke out within weeks of Paris | Outcomes: France changed regime; elsewhere old rulers returned |

## Why most failed

1. **Divisions**: liberals against radicals (June Days); nationalities against each other (Croats against
   Hungarians); German debates over including Austria.
2. **Loyal armies**: Radetzky, Windischgrätz, the Prussian army.
3. **Rulers recovered** and withdrew March concessions.
4. **Peasants satisfied** once serfdom ended.
5. **Foreign intervention**: Russia in Hungary; France in Rome.
6. **Parliaments without power**: Frankfurt had no army.

**Line of argument:** divisions weakened the revolutions, but loyal armies decided the outcome.

## What changed

- Serfdom abolished in the Austrian Empire (not restored).
- Piedmont kept the Statuto: leader of Italian nationalism.
- Prussia kept a (limited) constitution.
- France kept universal male suffrage; Second Republic, then Second Empire.
- Metternich's system ended.
- Lesson: unification would need a strong state's army and diplomacy.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Liberalism | Belief in constitutions, elected parliaments and individual rights |
| Nationalism | Belief that a people sharing language and culture should govern themselves |
| Constitution | Set of rules defining how a state is governed and citizens' rights |
| Universal male suffrage | Vote for all adult men |
| National Workshops | French public-works scheme for the unemployed, 1848 |
| Serfdom | Peasants bound to work for landlords |
| *Grossdeutschland* | A united Germany including Austria |
| *Kleindeutschland* | A united Germany without Austria, likely led by Prussia |
| Statuto | Piedmont's 1848 constitution |

## Exam traps

- Treating 1848 as one revolution with one cause.
- Calling France's revolution nationalist; its main issues were the form of government and workers'
  demands.
- Confusing Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849).
- Writing about Cavour or Bismarck in detail; they belong to key questions 1.2 and 1.3.
- "How far" answers with no judgement.

## Self-test

1. Who resigned as Austrian chancellor in March 1848?
2. Which French king abdicated in February 1848?
3. What were the National Workshops, and why did their closure matter?
4. Name the Hungarian nationalist leader of 1848.
5. Why did the Croats support the emperor?
6. What was the Statuto?
7. Why did Pius IX's decision in April 1848 weaken the Italian cause?
8. Name the battles in which Radetzky defeated Piedmont in 1848 and 1849.
9. Who ended the Roman Republic in 1849?
10. Give two reasons the Frankfurt Parliament failed.
11. Which change in the Austrian Empire in 1848 was not reversed?
12. Give one way 1848 influenced later Italian or German unification.

### Answers

1. Metternich.
2. Louis-Philippe.
3. Public-works schemes for the unemployed in Paris; their closure caused the June Days rising, which
   split workers from the middle classes.
4. Kossuth.
5. They feared rule by Hungary more than rule by Vienna.
6. Piedmont's constitution, granted in March 1848 and kept after 1849.
7. He refused to fight Catholic Austria, so many Italians would not follow a war against it.
8. Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849).
9. French troops sent by Louis Napoleon.
10. Any two: no army of its own; divided over including Austria; dependent on Prussia (Malmö);
    Frederick William IV refused the crown.
11. The abolition of serfdom.
12. Piedmont's constitution made it the leader of Italian nationalism; nationalists learned that a strong
    state's army was needed.
