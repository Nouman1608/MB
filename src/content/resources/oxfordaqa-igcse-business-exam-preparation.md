OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225) is linear and untiered, assessed entirely by two written
papers with no coursework. Paper 1 (Influences of Operations and Human Resources on Business Activity)
and Paper 2 (Influences of Marketing and Finance on Business Activity) each run 2 hours, carry 90
marks and are worth 50%. First teaching was September 2020 and the qualification was first examined in
May/June 2022. These notes complement the site's guides to
[Business in the Real World](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-business-business-in-the-real-world/) and
[Influences on Business](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-business-influences-on-business/).

## The paper titles tell you the content split precisely -- use them as a revision checklist

Paper 1 is operations and human resources; Paper 2 is marketing and finance. That is an unusually
clean division. **Exam-preparation priority**: organise revision under those two headings from the
start, and audit for topics that have drifted into the wrong pile. Because the split is so explicit,
a gap is easy to find -- but only if you are looking for it in these terms rather than working
through notes in teaching order.

## Two hours for 90 marks is 1.3 minutes per mark

That is a reasonable budget, but 90 marks is a substantial paper and the extended-response questions
sit at the end. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise complete papers rather than question sets, and
specifically practise arriving at the final extended question with the time it needs. A rushed final
answer costs more than the extra development it funded earlier.

## Every question is anchored to a business -- generic answers are marked as generic

Business questions on this specification are set in a described context. **Exam-preparation
priority**: note three things about the business before answering -- what it does, its scale, and the
pressure it faces -- and refer to at least one of them in every developed point. A textbook-correct
statement about motivation or cash flow that never touches the business in front of you has answered a
different question.

## Finance questions ask for a calculation and then for its meaning

Paper 2's finance content produces questions where the arithmetic is the first half only. **Exam-
preparation priority**: practise following each calculation with a sentence on what the figure means
for this business's decision. The interpretation is usually where the additional marks sit and is the
part most often omitted.

## Operations and HR answers reward trade-off thinking

Paper 1's content is full of genuine trade-offs: quality against cost, flexibility against efficiency,
motivation against wage bill. **Exam-preparation priority**: for each topic, rehearse the trade-off
rather than the definition. Questions at the top of the mark range are almost always asking you to
weigh one against another rather than to explain either.

## The mark tariff, not the answer space, tells you how much to write

Structured papers of this kind put a one-mark identification and an extended evaluation within a few
lines of each other, and the printed answer space is a poor guide to either. **Exam-preparation
priority**: read the mark allocation before reading the question, and translate it into a plan -- a
two-mark part wants a point and a brief reason, a six-mark part wants two developed points applied to
the business. Writing at one consistent length across the paper either wastes time on low-tariff parts
or under-develops high-tariff ones, and both are avoidable.

## Worked routine: a recommendation answer

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper, case study or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - state the recommendation in the first sentence:
Do not build to it. A clear position lets everything after it
function as support.

Step 2 - give the main reason, tied to the business:
"...because its main constraint is cash flow rather than
capacity, as shown by [fact from the case]."

Step 3 - give the strongest argument against:
Taken seriously. A dismissed counter-argument earns nothing.

Step 4 - say what the decision depends on:
Scale of demand, availability of finance, how quickly staff can
be trained -- name the factor, do not gesture at "circumstances".

Step 5 - close by confirming or qualifying the recommendation:
With the reason, referring back to step 1.
```

Step 4 is the clearest signal of evaluative thinking at this level. A recommendation that identifies
the condition it depends on demonstrates judgement rather than preference.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: organise revision under the two paper titles and audit for misplaced topics;
  practise complete 2-hour papers; rehearse trade-offs rather than definitions for operations and HR;
  practise calculation followed immediately by interpretation for finance.
- **During either paper**: protect time for the final extended-response question.
- **On every case**: note what the business does, its scale and its main pressure, and use one of them
  in each developed point.
- **In every recommendation**: state it first, ground the reason in the case, take the counter-case
  seriously, name the deciding factor, and close.

## Self-test

1. How is content divided between the two papers?
2. What time budget does each paper allow, and where is the risk?
3. What is usually missing from an incomplete finance answer?
4. What kind of thinking do the highest-tariff Paper 1 questions ask for?

**Answers:** 1. Paper 1 covers operations and human resources; Paper 2 covers marketing and finance --
an unusually explicit split that works well as a revision checklist. 2. Two hours for 90 marks, about
1.3 minutes per mark; the risk is over-developing earlier answers and reaching the final
extended-response question without adequate time. 3. The interpretation -- what the calculated figure
means for this business's decision, rather than the figure alone. 4. Trade-off thinking: weighing
quality against cost, flexibility against efficiency, or motivation against wage bill, rather than
explaining either side alone.
