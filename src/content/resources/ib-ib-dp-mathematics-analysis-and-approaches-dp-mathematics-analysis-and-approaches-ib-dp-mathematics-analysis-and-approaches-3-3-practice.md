This set covers **section 3.3** (SL and HL) of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: applications of right and non-right-angled trigonometry, including Pythagoras's theorem, elevation, depression, bearings and labelled diagrams. All questions allow a calculator except question 6. Draw a labelled diagram for every question. Give answers to 3 significant figures and bearings to 0.1°. The methods are in the [applications study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-3/) and the [applications revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-3-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** From the top of a lighthouse 45 m high, the angle of depression of a boat is 9°.

**(a)** Find the horizontal distance from the foot of the lighthouse to the boat. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the direct distance from the top of the lighthouse to the boat. **[1]**

**2.** A straight ramp rises 1.2 m over a horizontal distance of 9.5 m.

**(a)** Find the angle the ramp makes with the horizontal. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length of the ramp. **[1]**

**3.** B is 10 km from A on a bearing of 112°. C is due east of A and due north of B.

**(a)** Write down the bearing of A from B. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the distances AC and BC. **[2]**

**4.** A ship sails 8 km on a bearing of 090°, then 6 km on a bearing of 180°.

**(a)** Find its distance from the starting point. **[1]**
**(b)** Find its bearing from the starting point. **[2]**

**5.** From a point A on level ground the angle of elevation of the top of a tree is 40°. From B, 15 m further from the tree on the same straight line, the angle of elevation is 28°. Find the height of the tree. **[4]**

**6.** (calculator-free) B is 7 km from A on a bearing of 040°. C is 7 km from B on a bearing of 160°.

**(a)** Show that triangle ABC is equilateral. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the bearing of C from A. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A ship leaves port P and sails 14 km on a bearing of 035° to Q. It then sails 9 km on a bearing of 110° to R.

**(a)** Show that angle PQR = 105°. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the distance PR. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the bearing of R from P. **[3]**

**8.** Two observers A and B stand 200 m apart on level ground, on opposite sides of a point directly below a hot-air balloon and in line with it. The angles of elevation of the balloon from A and B are 52° and 37°.

**(a)** Find the height of the balloon. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the direct distance from A to the balloon. **[2]**

**9.** A vertical mast stands with its foot F on level ground. Point A is 60 m due west of F, and the angle of elevation of the top of the mast from A is 24°. Point B is due south of F, and the angle of elevation of the top from B is 30°.

**(a)** Find the height of the mast. **[2]**
**(b)** Find FB. **[2]**
**(c)** Find AB. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the bearing of B from A. **[2]**

**10.** An aircraft flies at a height of 2000 m. Directly ahead of it, in the same vertical plane, are two ships. The angles of depression of the ships are 32° and 19°. Find the distance between the ships. **[4]**

**11.** Town Y is 25 km from town X on a bearing of 300°. Town Z is 18 km from X on a bearing of 050°.

**(a)** Find angle YXZ. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the distance YZ. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the bearing of Z from Y. **[3]**

**12.** A straight road runs due east from a junction O. A farmhouse H is 3.4 km from O on a bearing of 058°.

**(a)** Find the shortest distance from H to the road. **[2]**
**(b)** A point P on the road is 2.5 km from H. Find the two possible distances OP. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The angle of elevation of the top from the boat is 9° (alternate angles) [1]. Distance = 45/tan 9° = 284 m [1]
**(b)** 45/sin 9° = 288 m [1]

**2. (a)** tan θ = 1.2/9.5 [1], θ = 7.20° [1]
**(b)** √(1.2² + 9.5²) = 9.58 m [1]

**3. (a)** 112° + 180° = 292° [1]
**(b)** Angle CAB = 112° − 90° = 22°, with a right angle at C. AC = 10 cos 22° = 9.27 km [1]; BC = 10 sin 22° = 3.75 km [1]

**4. (a)** √(8² + 6²) = 10 km [1]
**(b)** Angle south of east = tan⁻¹(6/8) = 36.9° [1], so the bearing is 090° + 36.9° = 126.9° [1]

**5.** In triangle ABT (T the top), angle ATB = 40° − 28° = 12° [1]. AT/sin 28° = 15/sin 12° [1], so AT = 33.87 m [1]. Height = AT sin 40° = 21.8 m [1]
*Examiner insight:* The exterior angle of triangle ABT at A is 40°, so the angle at T is the difference of the two elevations.

**6. (a)** At B the bearing back to A is 220° [1]. Angle ABC = 220° − 160° = 60° [1]. AB = BC with included angle 60°, so the base angles are (180° − 60°)/2 = 60° and the triangle is equilateral [1]
**(b)** Angle BAC = 60°, and C is clockwise from B as seen from A [1], so the bearing is 040° + 60° = 100° [1]

**7. (a)** At Q the bearing back to P is 035° + 180° = 215° [1]. Angle PQR = 215° − 110° = 105° [1]
**(b)** PR² = 14² + 9² − 2(14)(9) cos 105° [1], PR = 18.5 km [1]
**(c)** sin QPR = 9 sin 105°/18.499… [1], QPR = 28.0° [1]. Bearing = 035° + 28.0° = 063.0° [1]

**8. (a)** Let the height be h. Then h/tan 52° + h/tan 37° = 200 [2]. h(0.7813 + 1.3270) = 200 [1], so h = 94.9 m [1]
**(b)** h/sin 52° [1] = 120 m [1]

**9. (a)** h = 60 tan 24° [1] = 26.7 m [1]
**(b)** FB = h/tan 30° [1] = 46.3 m [1]
**(c)** Angle AFB = 90° [1], so AB = √(60² + 46.27²) = 75.8 m [1]
**(d)** From A, F is due east and B is south of F, so the angle between east and AB is tan⁻¹(46.27/60) = 37.6° [1]. Bearing 090° + 37.6° = 127.6° [1]

**10.** The angles of elevation from the ships equal the depression angles [1]. Horizontal distances: 2000/tan 32° = 3200.7 m [1] and 2000/tan 19° = 5808.4 m [1]. Distance between ships = 2610 m (3 s.f.) [1]

**11. (a)** From 300° clockwise to 050° is 60° + 50° = 110° [1]
**(b)** YZ² = 25² + 18² − 2(25)(18) cos 110° [1], YZ = 35.5 km [1]
**(c)** sin XYZ = 18 sin 110°/35.45… [1], XYZ = 28.5° [1]. From Y, X is on a bearing of 120°, and Z is 28.5° anticlockwise of X, so the bearing is 120° − 28.5° = 091.5° [1]

**12. (a)** H is 90° − 58° = 32° north of the road's direction [1]. Distance = 3.4 sin 32° = 1.80 km [1]
**(b)** The foot N of the perpendicular is 3.4 cos 32° = 2.883 km from O [1]. NP = √(2.5² − 1.8017²) = 1.733 km [1]. OP = 2.883 ± 1.733 = 1.15 km or 4.62 km [1]
*Examiner insight:* A circle of radius 2.5 km about H cuts the road twice, which is why there are two answers. The cosine rule with OP as the unknown gives the same quadratic.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Depression angles placed at the wrong vertex.
- Bearings not written with three figures, or measured from the wrong point.
- Interior angles at a turning point found without the back bearing.
- Rounding intermediate lengths before the final step.
- Giving one value when the geometry allows two.
