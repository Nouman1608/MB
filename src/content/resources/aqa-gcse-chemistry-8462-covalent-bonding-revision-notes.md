These notes condense **4.2.1.4 Covalent bonding** of the AQA GCSE Chemistry (8462) specification (for
teaching from September 2016, version 1.1). It is Paper 1 content for both tiers, with no Higher-only
statements and no required practical. For full explanations, see the
[covalent bonding study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-covalent-bonding/); then practise with the
[covalent bonding practice questions](/resources/aqa-gcse-chemistry-8462-covalent-bonding-practice/).

## Core facts

- When atoms **share pairs of electrons**, they form **covalent bonds**. These bonds are **strong**.
- The shared pair is attracted to the nuclei of both atoms.
- Covalent substances come in three types: **small molecules**, **very large molecules (polymers)** and
  **giant covalent structures** such as diamond and silicon dioxide.
- One line = one shared pair (single bond); two lines = double bond; three lines = triple bond.

## Bonds each atom forms

| Atom | Bonds | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| H | 1 | Needs 1 electron to reach 2 |
| Cl | 1 | 7 outer electrons, needs 1 |
| O | 2 | 6 outer electrons, needs 2 |
| N | 3 | 5 outer electrons, needs 3 |
| C | 4 | 4 outer electrons, needs 4 |

## The eight required dot and cross molecules

| Molecule | Bonds | Total outer electrons | Lone pairs |
|---|---|---|---|
| H₂ | H–H | 2 | 0 |
| Cl₂ | Cl–Cl | 14 | 3 on each Cl |
| O₂ | O=O | 12 | 2 on each O |
| N₂ | N≡N | 10 | 1 on each N |
| HCl | H–Cl | 8 | 3 on Cl |
| H₂O | two O–H | 8 | 2 on O |
| NH₃ | three N–H | 8 | 1 on N |
| CH₄ | four C–H | 8 | 0 |

Self-check every diagram: right total of electrons; 2 around each H and 8 around every other atom; each
shared pair in an overlap with one dot and one cross.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Covalent bond | A shared pair of electrons between two atoms |
| Double bond | Two shared pairs between the same two atoms |
| Lone pair | A pair of outer electrons not shared with another atom |
| Small molecule | A substance made of separate molecules containing a few atoms |
| Polymer | A very large molecule made of many repeating units |
| Repeat unit | The section of a polymer drawn in brackets with *n* |
| Giant covalent structure | A network of atoms joined by covalent bonds, continuing in all directions |
| Molecular formula | The actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule |

## Classifying covalent substances

| Clue | Type |
|---|---|
| Separate identical groups of a few atoms | Small molecules |
| Brackets, bond lines through them, and *n* | Polymer |
| A repeating network with no ends, bonds running off the edge | Giant covalent |
| Formula of non-metals with small numbers (e.g. HBr, SO₂, C₂H₆) | Usually small molecules |
| Diamond or silicon dioxide | Giant covalent (from the specification) |

## Molecular formula from a model

1. Count each type of atom (use the key).
2. Write the counts as subscripts; carbon compounds usually C, then H, then others.
3. **Do not simplify.** C₂H₄ stays C₂H₄; H₂O₂ stays H₂O₂.
4. Check each atom has its usual number of bonds.

## Limitations of each representation

- **Dot and cross:** no 3D shape.
- **Displayed formula:** no lone pairs, no 3D shape.
- **Ball and stick:** atom sizes not to scale; sticks are not real rods.
- **Space-filling:** bonds hidden.
- **2D drawing of a giant structure:** cannot show all bonds in three dimensions; only a small part shown.

## Exam traps

- **N₂ is a triple bond.** A single or double bond leaves nitrogen short of 8 electrons.
- **O₂ is a double bond.** One shared pair is not enough.
- **Short formula, giant structure.** SiO₂ looks like a small molecule but is giant covalent.
- **Simplifying a molecular formula.** That turns it into an empirical formula and loses the mark.
- **Two dots in a shared pair.** Each pair in these molecules has one electron from each atom.
- **Polymer repeat units.** Forgetting the bond lines through the brackets or the *n*.

## Quick self-test

1. What is a covalent bond?
2. Why is a covalent bond strong?
3. How many bonds does an oxygen atom usually form?
4. How many shared pairs are there in a nitrogen molecule?
5. How many lone pairs does the nitrogen atom in ammonia have?
6. How many outer electrons should a dot and cross diagram of chlorine show in total?
7. Name the three types of covalent substance.
8. Give the two giant covalent structures named in this subsection of the specification.
9. A model shows 4 carbon atoms and 10 hydrogen atoms. Give the molecular formula.
10. Why must you not simplify C₂H₆ to CH₃?
11. State one limitation of a dot and cross diagram of water.
12. What does *n* mean in a polymer repeat unit?

### Answers

1. A shared pair of electrons between two atoms.
2. There is strong electrostatic attraction between the shared pair and the nuclei of both atoms.
3. Two.
4. Three (a triple bond).
5. One.
6. 14 (7 from each atom).
7. Small molecules, polymers (very large molecules) and giant covalent structures.
8. Diamond and silicon dioxide.
9. C₄H₁₀.
10. A molecular formula gives the actual number of atoms in one molecule, which is 2 C and 6 H.
11. It does not show the 3D shape of the molecule.
12. A large number of repeat units joined in the chain.
