This set covers **section 1.1, Integers**, of the Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2. All of it is Foundation Tier content that the Higher Tier also assumes, so every question is for both tiers. Show your working.

## Section A

**1.**

**(a)** Write down the value of the 6 in the number 4 365 210. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the number 7 040 090 in words. **[1]**

**2.** Work out

**(a)** −7 + 12 **[1]**

**(b)** −4 − (−9) **[1]**

**(c)** (−6) × (−3) × (−2) **[1]**

**(d)** −72 ÷ 8 **[1]**

**3.** Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first: −14, 3, −2, 0, −20, 9 **[1]**

**4.** Work out (5 − 3 × 4)² − 2 × (−6). Show your working. **[2]**

**5.** Here is a list of numbers: 1, 2, 9, 15, 17, 21, 28, 31, 36. From the list, write down

**(a)** all the prime numbers **[1]**

**(b)** the odd numbers that are multiples of 3 **[1]**

**(c)** the numbers that are factors of 72. **[1]**

**6.** Hassan says 187 is a prime number. Is he correct? Show how you decide. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Nadia's bank account is overdrawn by £38.

**(a)** She pays in £150. Work out her new balance. **[1]**

**(b)** She then pays two bills, of £64 and £95. Work out her balance after paying both bills. **[2]**

**(c)** How much must she then pay in so that her balance is £20? **[1]**

**8.** A diver is at a depth of 24 m below sea level. A seabird is flying 37 m above sea level, directly above her.

**(a)** Work out the vertical distance between the diver and the bird. **[1]**

**(b)** The diver rises 9 m and then dives down 15 m. Work out her new position relative to sea level. **[2]**

**9.** The midday temperatures in five cities on one day were:

| City | Oslo | Cairo | Moscow | Toronto | Lima |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Temperature (°C) | −7 | 23 | −15 | −2 | 18 |

**(a)** Work out the difference between the highest and lowest temperatures. **[1]**

**(b)** Which two cities had temperatures exactly 8 degrees apart? **[1]**

**(c)** Work out the mean of the five temperatures. **[2]**

**10.**

**(a)** Copy 8 − 3 × 2 + 4 = 14 and insert one pair of brackets to make it correct. **[1]**

**(b)** Copy 8 − 3 × 2 + 4 = −10 and insert one pair of brackets to make it correct. **[1]**

**(c)** Work out (36 − 4²) ÷ (2 − 7). **[2]**

**11.** Two runners start together at the start line of a circular track. Kemi completes a lap every 10 minutes and Josh every 15 minutes.

**(a)** List the first five multiples of 10 and of 15. **[1]**

**(b)** After how many minutes are they next at the start line together, and how many laps has each completed? **[2]**

**(c)** Write down the prime factors of 90. **[1]**

**12.**

**(a)** n is an odd number. Is n² + n odd or even? Give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Priya says, "The sum of two prime numbers is always even." Give an example to show she is wrong. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the product of any two consecutive integers is even. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **60 000** (sixty thousand) [1]

**(b)** **Seven million, forty thousand and ninety** [1]

**2. (a)** **5** [1]

**(b)** −4 + 9 = **5** [1]

**(c)** Three negative signs, so negative: **−36** [1]

**(d)** **−9** [1]

**3.** **−20, −14, −2, 0, 3, 9** [1]

**4.** 5 − 12 = −7 and (−7)² = 49 [1]; 49 − (−12) = **61** [1]

**5. (a)** **2, 17, 31** [1]

**(b)** **9, 15, 21** [1]

**(c)** **1, 2, 9, 36** [1]

**6.** Test primes up to √187 ≈ 13.7: 187 ÷ 11 = 17 [1]. 187 = 11 × 17, so it has more than two factors and **Hassan is wrong** [1]

**7. (a)** −38 + 150 = **£112** [1]

**(b)** 64 + 95 = 159 [1]; 112 − 159 = **−£47** (overdrawn by £47) [1]

**(c)** 20 − (−47) = **£67** [1]

**8. (a)** 37 − (−24) = **61 m** [1]

**(b)** −24 + 9 = −15 [1]; −15 − 15 = **−30 m**, so 30 m below sea level [1]

**9. (a)** 23 − (−15) = **38 degrees** [1]

**(b)** **Oslo and Moscow**: −7 − (−15) = 8 [1]

**(c)** −7 + 23 − 15 − 2 + 18 = 17 [1]; 17 ÷ 5 = **3.4 °C** [1]

**10. (a)** **(8 − 3) × 2 + 4 = 14** [1]

**(b)** **8 − 3 × (2 + 4) = −10** [1]

**(c)** 36 − 16 = 20 and 2 − 7 = −5 [1]; 20 ÷ (−5) = **−4** [1]

**11. (a)** 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 15, 30, 45, 60, 75 [1]

**(b)** First common multiple is **30 minutes** [1]; Kemi has run **3 laps** and Josh **2 laps** [1]

**(c)** **2, 3 and 5** [1]

**12. (a)** n² = n × n is odd × odd, so odd [1]; odd + odd is even, so **n² + n is even** [1]

**(b)** For example **2 + 3 = 5**, which is odd [1]

**(c)** Of two consecutive integers, one must be even, and even × any integer is even [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading "overdrawn by £38" as +38 in question 7.
- Adding the depths in question 8(a) as 37 − 24 = 13 instead of finding the gap across zero.
- Doing 5 − 3 first in question 4, ignoring that multiplication comes before subtraction.
- Including 1 or 9 as primes in question 5(a), or 28 as a factor of 72 in question 5(c).
- Stopping the prime test at 7 in question 6 without reaching √187.
- Giving the mean in question 9(c) without the negative temperatures subtracted.
