Cambridge O Level History 2147 examines Depth Study C in **Paper 1, Section B**, where you choose one of two questions on it. Each is worth 20 marks: (a) 4, (b) 6, (c) 10. The four questions below cover all four key questions of the Depth Study -- more than one exam would set, so you can practise every area.

## Question 1: The fall of the Tsar (key question 1)

**(a)** Describe the events of Bloody Sunday, January 1905. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Tsar survived the 1905 Revolution. **[6]**

**(c)** "The First World War was the main reason for the collapse of the Tsarist regime." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** A peaceful march of workers and their families led by Father Gapon; they marched to the Winter Palace in St Petersburg; they carried a petition and icons and portraits of the Tsar; they asked for better conditions and an elected assembly; troops opened fire; many were killed; it destroyed trust in the Tsar as the "Little Father".

**(b)** The October Manifesto promised a Duma and civil rights, satisfying liberals and splitting them from socialists; peace with Japan (1905) allowed loyal troops to return and crush the St Petersburg Soviet and the Moscow rising; the opposition had no single leader or aim; and Stolypin's harsh repression afterwards ended peasant revolts.

**(c)** *War:* defeats and massive losses turned soldiers against the regime; the Tsar became personally responsible after 1915; shortages and inflation in the cities caused the protests of March 1917; the army's refusal to fire was decisive. *Other causes:* the autocracy's refusal to reform (dissolving Dumas); poverty and repression; the Rasputin scandal; the growth of revolutionary parties; discontent shown in 1905 and the Lena strikes of 1912. *Judgement:* the regime was already weak, but the war turned weakness into collapse by destroying the army's loyalty.

## Question 2: The Bolsheviks (key question 2)

**(a)** What was the Kornilov affair? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why the Kronstadt Rising led to the New Economic Policy. **[6]**

**(c)** "The weaknesses of the Whites were the main reason why the Bolsheviks won the Civil War." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** In September 1917 General Kornilov, commander-in-chief, appeared to move troops towards Petrograd; Kerensky feared a military coup; he released Bolsheviks from prison and armed the Red Guards to defend the city; Kornilov's troops were persuaded not to fight; the Bolsheviks were seen as defenders of the revolution and their support grew.

**(b)** The Kronstadt sailors had been strong Bolshevik supporters in 1917, so their revolt in March 1921 showed how unpopular War Communism had become; they demanded free elections to the soviets and an end to grain requisitioning; at the same time peasant risings and famine showed the economy was collapsing; Lenin realised that he needed to win back peasant support, so he replaced requisitioning with a tax in kind and allowed private trade.

**(c)** *White weaknesses:* divided leaders (Kolchak, Denikin, Yudenich) who never co-ordinated; different aims, from restoring the Tsar to a democratic republic; long supply lines; brutality and links with landlords lost peasant support; foreign help let the Reds call them puppets of foreigners. *Red strengths:* Trotsky's Red Army and use of political commissars; control of the central industrial area and railways; War Communism to supply troops; propaganda and Cheka terror; unity under Lenin. *Judgement:* Red strengths and White weaknesses worked together; the Reds' central position meant they could exploit White divisions.

## Question 3: Stalin's power (key question 3)

**(a)** Describe Stalin's position in the Communist Party when Lenin died. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Stalin launched the Purges. **[6]**

**(c)** "Terror was the most important method Stalin used to control the Soviet Union." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** He was General Secretary from 1922; he controlled appointments and promotions, so many officials owed him their jobs; he was a member of the Politburo; he had a reputation as a dull administrator, so rivals underestimated him; Lenin's Testament had criticised him and suggested his removal.

**(b)** To remove possible rivals such as the old Bolsheviks Zinoviev, Kamenev and Bukharin; Kirov's murder (1934) gave him an excuse and suggested a plot; he feared the army leadership might oppose him; economic failures needed scapegoats ("wreckers"); and fear made the whole population obedient during rapid change.

**(c)** *Terror:* millions arrested; show trials; the Gulag; the army purged; denunciations made everyone afraid. *Other methods:* the cult of personality in posters, statues and renamed towns; censorship and rewritten history; socialist realism in the arts; education and youth organisations; genuine belief among some workers that Stalin was building a modern socialist state. *Judgement:* terror removed opposition, but propaganda gave the regime support; most answers argue terror was essential but not sufficient.

## Question 4: The economy (key question 4)

**(a)** What was collectivisation? **[4]**

**(b)** Explain why Stalin introduced the Five-Year Plans. **[6]**

**(c)** "Stalin's economic policies were a success." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

### Worked answer

**(a)** The joining together of individual peasant farms into large collective farms (kolkhozes); land, animals and tools were shared; the state set grain quotas; begun on a mass scale in 1929; kulaks were removed; machinery was supplied through Machine Tractor Stations.

**(b)** To modernise the USSR rapidly and catch up with the West; to build heavy industry to defend against attack from capitalist countries; to create a larger working class, which communists saw as the base of socialism; to end the NEP's private trade; and to strengthen Stalin's own authority as leader of a great project.

**(c)** *Success:* coal, steel, oil and electricity output rose dramatically; new cities, dams and canals were built; unemployment ended; by 1941 the USSR had the industrial base to fight Germany. *Failure:* famine in 1932-33 killed millions; agriculture recovered only slowly and livestock numbers collapsed; consumer goods and housing were poor; forced labour and terror; quality often low. *Judgement:* a success in Stalin's aim of rapid industrial power, a failure measured by human cost and living standards.

## Where marks are usually lost

- In (a), giving one detailed point instead of several separate ones.
- In (b), explaining what happened rather than why.
- In (c), failing to define "success" or "main reason" before judging.

## Frequently asked questions

**Should I learn production figures?**
A few approximate comparisons (for example, coal and steel output rising several times over between 1928 and 1940) help, but explanation matters more.

**Which questions overlap with Germany?**
Methods of control (terror and propaganda) appear in both Depth Studies B and C, but answer with Soviet evidence here.
