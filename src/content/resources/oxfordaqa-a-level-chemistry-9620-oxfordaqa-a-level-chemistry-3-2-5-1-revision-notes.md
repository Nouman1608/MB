These notes condense **3.2.5.1 General properties of transition metals** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification: the incomplete sub-level that gives Ti to Cu their characteristics, the four characteristic properties, and the definitions of ligand, complex and co-ordination number. ## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Transition metal (Ti to Cu) | Element whose characteristics arise from an incomplete d sub-level in its atoms or ions |
| Ligand | A molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal ion by donating a pair of electrons |
| Complex | A central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands |
| Co-ordination number | The number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion |
| Co-ordinate bond | A covalent bond in which both shared electrons come from one atom |
| Donor atom | The atom in a ligand that supplies the lone pair (O in H₂O, N in NH₃) |

## The four characteristic properties

1. Complex formation
2. Formation of coloured ions
3. Variable oxidation state
4. Catalytic activity

One example each: [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ (complex); blue Cu²⁺(aq) (colour); Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺ (oxidation states); Fe in the Haber process (catalyst).

## Configurations to know

| Atom | Configuration | Ion | Configuration |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ti | [Ar] 3d² 4s² | Ti³⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹ |
| V | [Ar] 3d³ 4s² | V²⁺ | [Ar] 3d³ |
| Cr | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ | Cr³⁺ | [Ar] 3d³ |
| Mn | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² | Mn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁵ |
| Fe | [Ar] 3d⁶ 4s² | Fe²⁺ / Fe³⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁶ / [Ar] 3d⁵ |
| Co | [Ar] 3d⁷ 4s² | Co²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁷ |
| Ni | [Ar] 3d⁸ 4s² | Ni²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁸ |
| Cu | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ | Cu²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁹ |

Rules: Cr and Cu are the exceptions in the atom; 4s electrons are lost first when ions form.

## Boundary cases

- **Copper**: atom 3d¹⁰ is full, but Cu²⁺ (3d⁹) is incomplete, so copper counts.
- **Zinc**: only Zn²⁺ (3d¹⁰); no incomplete d sub-level in atom or ion, so not a transition metal; compounds white.

## Analysing a complex: the four checks

1. Ligand(s) and donor atom(s).
2. Co-ordination number = number of co-ordinate bonds.
3. Oxidation state of the metal = overall charge − sum of ligand charges.
4. Configuration of the metal ion, to confirm the d sub-level is incomplete.

Ligand charges: H₂O and NH₃ are 0; Cl⁻ is −1.

**Mini examples**
- [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺: co-ordination number 6; Fe +3.
- [CuCl₄]²⁻: co-ordination number 4; Cu +2.
- [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺: co-ordination number 6; Co +3.
- K₂[NiCl₄]: complex is [NiCl₄]²⁻; Ni +2.

## Is the d sub-level incomplete? Quick checks

| Species | Configuration | Incomplete 3d? | Consequence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cu⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ | no | compounds typically white or colourless |
| Cu²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁹ | yes | coloured complexes, such as blue [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ |
| Ti⁴⁺ | [Ar] (3d⁰) | no | TiO₂ is white |
| Ti³⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹ | yes | coloured compounds |
| Zn²⁺ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ | no | white compounds; zinc is not a transition metal |
| Fe³⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁵ | yes | coloured complexes |

Method: remove the 4s electrons first, then 3d electrons, until the charge is reached; then count the 3d electrons. Between 1 and 9 means incomplete.

## Exam traps

- Removing 3d electrons before 4s.
- Forgetting the Cr and Cu exceptions.
- Ligand definition with no mention of donating an electron pair.
- Counting ligands rather than bonds for co-ordination number.
- Ignoring the counter-ions when finding the charge of a complex in a salt.
- Saying zinc is a transition metal because it is in the d block.
- Calling a co-ordinate bond "ionic".

9. Explain why zinc is not a transition metal.
10. Give the co-ordination number and oxidation state of nickel in [Ni(NH₃)₆]²⁺.

### Answers

1. An **incomplete d sub-level** in their atoms or ions.
2. Complex formation; formation of coloured ions; variable oxidation state; catalytic activity.
3. A molecule or ion that forms a co-ordinate bond with a transition metal ion by donating a pair of electrons.
4. A central metal atom or ion surrounded by ligands.
5. The number of co-ordinate bonds to the central metal atom or ion.
6. **[Ar] 3d⁵**
7. **[Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹**
8. Its Cu²⁺ ion is [Ar] 3d⁹, an incomplete d sub-level.
9. Its only ion, Zn²⁺, is [Ar] 3d¹⁰, and the atom is also 3d¹⁰: neither has an incomplete d sub-level.
10. Co-ordination number **6**; nickel **+2**.
11. Co-ordination number **6**; x + 0 + (−1) = +2, so iron is **+3**.
12. A shared pair of electrons in which both electrons are donated by the same atom.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are 4s electrons lost before 3d electrons?**
Once the 3d sub-level is occupied, the 4s electrons are the outermost and are removed first when the atom forms a positive ion. So Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶, not [Ar] 3d⁴ 4s².

**Does co-ordination number equal the number of ligands?**
Only for monodentate ligands. It is always the number of co-ordinate bonds to the metal.
