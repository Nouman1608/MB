Condensed recall for **12.4 Separation and purification**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 (2026–2028). All of 12.4 is **Core**. Worked examples are in the [separation and purification study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-separation-and-purification/); exam-style questions are in the [separation and purification practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-separation-and-purification-practice/).

## The three outcomes

1. Describe **and explain** separation using: a suitable solvent, filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation, fractional distillation.
2. **Suggest** a technique from information about the substances.
3. **Identify** substances and **assess purity** from melting and boiling points.

## Methods at a glance

| Method | Separates | Works because | Key apparatus |
|---|---|---|---|
| suitable solvent | a soluble from an insoluble component | different solubility | beaker, stirring rod |
| filtration | insoluble solid from liquid | solid particles cannot pass through filter paper | filter funnel, filter paper |
| crystallisation | soluble solid from its solution | solubility falls as solution cools | evaporating basin, heat, glass rod |
| simple distillation | solvent from a solution | solvent boils, solute does not | flask, thermometer at side arm, condenser |
| fractional distillation | miscible liquids | different boiling points | fractionating column, thermometer, condenser |

## Crystallisation steps

1. Heat to evaporate **some** of the solvent.
2. Test: crystals form on a cooled **glass rod** → stop heating.
3. **Cool** → crystals form.
4. **Filter** off the crystals.
5. **Wash** with a little **cold** distilled water.
6. **Dry** between filter papers / in a warm oven.

Do not evaporate to dryness: soluble impurities stay mixed in, and some salts decompose or lose water of crystallisation.

## Distillation details

- **Thermometer bulb** level with the **side arm** (measures vapour temperature).
- **Condenser water** in at the **bottom**, out at the **top** (condenser stays full and cold).
- Simple distillation: distillate = pure solvent; solid stays in the flask.
- Fractional distillation: the lower-boiling liquid distils first; thermometer **steady** at its boiling point; a **rise** means the next liquid is starting.
- Example: ethanol (78 °C) distils before water (100 °C).

## Choosing a method

| You are told... | Use |
|---|---|
| "insoluble", "suspended", "precipitate" | filtration |
| "crystals of the salt" | crystallisation |
| "pure water from seawater" | simple distillation |
| "two liquids that mix", "different boiling points" | fractional distillation |
| "one dissolves in ... the other does not" | suitable solvent + filtration |
| "coloured dyes", "inks" | chromatography (12.3) |

Two-step separations are common: **dissolve → filter → crystallise**.

## Purity

| | Pure | Impure |
|---|---|---|
| melting point | sharp, fixed | lower, over a range |
| boiling point | sharp, fixed | higher, over a range |

- Identify a substance by matching its melting or boiling point to a data value.
- Water: melts at 0 °C, boils at 100 °C.

## Three-component example

Mixture: sand, salt and water, and you want all three.

1. **Filter** → sand (residue), salt water (filtrate). Wash and dry the sand.
2. **Simple distillation** of the filtrate → pure water (distillate); salt stays in the flask.
   Or, to get salt crystals instead of collecting the water: **crystallise** the filtrate.

The choice in step 2 depends on which product the question asks for.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Residue | substance that remains after evaporation, distillation, filtration or a similar process |
| Filtrate | liquid or solution that has passed through a filter |
| Distillate | liquid collected after condensing the vapour |
| Saturated solution | maximum concentration of solute dissolved at a specified temperature |
| Miscible | liquids that mix completely |
| Crystallisation point | when a solution is concentrated enough for crystals to form on cooling |

## Exam traps

- Crystallisation is **not** "evaporate to dryness".
- Wash crystals with **cold** water and only **a little**.
- Thermometer goes at the **side arm**, not in the liquid.
- Filtration cannot separate a **dissolved** solid.
- Simple distillation cannot separate two liquids with close boiling points; use **fractional**.
- Impurities **lower** the melting point and **raise** the boiling point; both become a **range**.
- "Explain" needs the **property**: solubility, particle size or boiling point.
- A fraction or distillate collected over a **temperature range** is not a single pure liquid.

## Self-test

1. Name the method used to separate chalk from water.
2. Name the method used to obtain pure water from seawater.
3. Name the method used to separate ethanol from water.
4. Where should the thermometer bulb be placed during distillation?
5. Why is the solution not evaporated to dryness when making crystals?
6. How can you tell when a solution is ready to leave to crystallise?
7. Why are crystals washed with cold, not warm, water?
8. Describe how to obtain salt from a mixture of salt and sand.
9. A sample melts between 76 °C and 80 °C. The data value is 82 °C. What does this show?
10. Why does salt water boil above 100 °C?
11. Which liquid distils first from a mixture of ethanol and water, and why?
12. Explain why filtration separates sand from water.

**Answers:**

1. Filtration.
2. Simple distillation.
3. Fractional distillation.
4. Level with the side arm of the flask.
5. Soluble impurities would remain in the solid / some salts decompose or lose water of crystallisation.
6. Crystals form on a glass rod dipped in and allowed to cool.
7. Less of the crystals dissolves in cold water.
8. Add water and stir; filter off the sand; heat the filtrate to the crystallisation point, cool, filter and dry the crystals.
9. The sample is impure: it melts below the data value and over a range.
10. The dissolved salt is an impurity, which raises the boiling point.
11. Ethanol, because it has the lower boiling point (78 °C compared with 100 °C).
12. Sand particles are insoluble and too large to pass through the filter paper; water passes through.
