## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Conjugate base | species formed when an acid loses H⁺ |
| Conjugate acid | species formed when a base gains H⁺ |
| Conjugate acid–base pair | two species differing by one H⁺ |
| pH | −log₁₀[H⁺(aq)] |
| Ka | [H⁺][A⁻] ÷ [HA]; mol dm⁻³ |
| pKa | −log₁₀ Ka; smaller pKa = stronger acid |
| Kw | [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 298 K |
| Buffer solution | resists changes in pH when small amounts of acid or alkali are added |
| Ksp | product of ion concentrations in a saturated solution of a sparingly soluble salt, each raised to its coefficient |
| Common ion effect | lower solubility of a salt in a solution already containing one of its ions |

## pH routines

| Solution | Steps |
|---|---|
| Strong acid HX | [H⁺] = c; pH = −log c |
| Strong alkali MOH | [OH⁻] = c; [H⁺] = Kw ÷ [OH⁻] |
| Strong alkali M(OH)₂ | [OH⁻] = 2c, then as above |
| Weak acid HA | [H⁺] = √(Ka × c) |
| Ka from pH | [H⁺] = 10^(−pH); Ka = [H⁺]² ÷ c |
| Buffer | [H⁺] = Ka × [HA] ÷ [A⁻] |

Quick checks *(values supplied)*:

    0.00250 mol dm⁻³ HNO₃:  pH = 2.60
    0.00800 mol dm⁻³ KOH:   [H⁺] = 1.25 × 10⁻¹² ; pH = 11.90
    0.100 mol dm⁻³ HA, Ka = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵:  [H⁺] = 1.58 × 10⁻³ ; pH = 2.80

**Weak-acid assumptions:** [H⁺] = [A⁻] (water's ionisation ignored); [HA] at equilibrium ≈
initial concentration (dissociation small).

## Buffers

**Making one:**

- weak acid + salt of its conjugate base (CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa);
- excess weak acid + some strong base (partial neutralisation).

**Controlling pH (equations needed):**

    added H⁺:   A⁻ + H⁺ → HA
    added OH⁻:  HA + OH⁻ → A⁻ + H₂O

Large reservoirs of HA and A⁻ mean the ratio [HA] : [A⁻] barely changes.

**Blood:** H₂CO₃/HCO₃⁻ keeps pH near 7.4; HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂CO₃ removes excess acid;
H₂CO₃ + OH⁻ → HCO₃⁻ + H₂O removes excess alkali.

**Other uses:** calibrating pH meters; keeping pH constant for enzyme reactions and in some
foods, medicines and shampoos.

**Buffer calculations** *(Ka values supplied)*:

    0.300 mol dm⁻³ HA + 0.150 mol dm⁻³ A⁻, Ka = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵:
    [H⁺] = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 2 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ ; pH = 4.30

    4.10 g CH₃COONa (M = 82.0) dissolved in 500 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH, Ka = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵:
    mol A⁻ = 0.0500 → [A⁻] = 0.100 ; [HA] = 0.200
    [H⁺] = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 2 = 3.6 × 10⁻⁵ ; pH = 4.44

**Moles method for mixtures:** work out moles of HA and A⁻ after any reaction; the volume is
common, so use the mole ratio. When [HA] = [A⁻], pH = pKa.

## Ksp

| Salt | Equilibrium | Ksp | In terms of s | Units |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AgBr | AgBr ⇌ Ag⁺ + Br⁻ | [Ag⁺][Br⁻] | s² | mol² dm⁻⁶ |
| BaSO₄ | BaSO₄ ⇌ Ba²⁺ + SO₄²⁻ | [Ba²⁺][SO₄²⁻] | s² | mol² dm⁻⁶ |
| Ag₂CrO₄ | Ag₂CrO₄ ⇌ 2Ag⁺ + CrO₄²⁻ | [Ag⁺]²[CrO₄²⁻] | 4s³ | mol³ dm⁻⁹ |
| PbCl₂ | PbCl₂ ⇌ Pb²⁺ + 2Cl⁻ | [Pb²⁺][Cl⁻]² | 4s³ | mol³ dm⁻⁹ |

**Precipitation test:** calculate concentrations **after mixing** (halve them for equal
volumes), find the ionic product, compare with Ksp. Greater than Ksp → precipitate.

*Example (values supplied):* 50 cm³ of 1.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ Pb(NO₃)₂ + 50 cm³ of
2.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ NaCl; Ksp(PbCl₂) = 1.7 × 10⁻⁵ mol³ dm⁻⁹.

    [Pb²⁺] = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ ; [Cl⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻³
    ionic product = 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ × (1.0 × 10⁻³)² = 5.0 × 10⁻¹⁰  <  Ksp  → no precipitate

**Common ion:** use the added ion's concentration for that ion; solve for the other.
AgBr (Ksp = 5.0 × 10⁻¹³, supplied) in 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ NaBr: [Ag⁺] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ mol dm⁻³.

## Exam traps

- A conjugate pair differs by **one** H⁺ only.
- HCO₃⁻ and H₂O can act as acid or base; decide from the reaction.
- Double [OH⁻] for Ba(OH)₂ and Ca(OH)₂.
- Use the unrounded [H⁺] for the log; give pH to two decimal places.
- Buffer: [A⁻] comes from the salt or from the NaOH added, not from HA's dissociation.
- Halve concentrations when mixing equal volumes before using Ksp.
- Ksp units depend on the formula; state them.
- A common ion lowers solubility, not Ksp; temperature alone changes Ksp.

## Self-test

2. Define Kw and give its value at 298 K.
3. Calculate the pH of 0.0150 mol dm⁻³ Ca(OH)₂.
4. A 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ weak acid has pH 2.85. Calculate Ka.
5. State the two approximations used in Question 4.
6. Explain, with an equation, how an ethanoic acid / sodium ethanoate buffer removes added OH⁻.
7. 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³ HA (Ka = 2.5 × 10⁻⁵) is mixed with 10.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol dm⁻³
   NaOH. Calculate the pH.
8. Write the Ksp expression and units for Ag₂CrO₄.
9. The solubility of AgBr is 7.1 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³. Calculate Ksp.
10. Calculate the solubility of AgBr (Ksp = 5.0 × 10⁻¹³) in 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ NaBr.
11. Equal volumes of 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ AgNO₃ and 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ NaBr are mixed. Does
    AgBr precipitate?
12. Why does HCO₃⁻ matter in blood?

**Answers**

1. HCO₃⁻ / CO₃²⁻ (acid / conjugate base); H₂O / OH⁻ (conjugate acid / base).
2. Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶.
3. [OH⁻] = 0.0300; [H⁺] = 3.33 × 10⁻¹³; pH = **12.48**.
4. [H⁺] = 1.41 × 10⁻³; Ka = (1.41 × 10⁻³)² ÷ 0.0500 = **4.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³**.
5. [H⁺] = [A⁻]; [HA] at equilibrium equals 0.0500 mol dm⁻³.
6. CH₃COOH + OH⁻ → CH₃COO⁻ + H₂O; the large reservoir of acid removes OH⁻, so the ratio and pH
   hardly change.
7. HA: 5.00 × 10⁻³ mol; OH⁻: 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol → HA 3.00 × 10⁻³, A⁻ 2.00 × 10⁻³; [H⁺] =
   2.5 × 10⁻⁵ × 1.5 = 3.75 × 10⁻⁵; pH = **4.43**.
8. Ksp = [Ag⁺]²[CrO₄²⁻]; mol³ dm⁻⁹.
9. (7.1 × 10⁻⁷)² = **5.0 × 10⁻¹³ mol² dm⁻⁶**.
10. **1.0 × 10⁻¹¹ mol dm⁻³**.
11. After mixing, each is 1.0 × 10⁻⁴; product = 1.0 × 10⁻⁸ > 5.0 × 10⁻¹³, so **yes**.
12. It removes excess H⁺ (HCO₃⁻ + H⁺ → H₂CO₃), keeping blood pH close to 7.4.
