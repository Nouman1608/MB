**These are two Paper 2 style questions on Section 6 Trade, each worth 25 marks in parts (a) to
(d).** Question 1 tests reading export and import data and the meaning of GDP, GNP and the balance
of trade; Question 2 tests trading partners, blocs, barriers and exchange rates. All figures in
the resources are illustrative and rounded, not official statistics.

## Question 1: Exports, imports and the balance of trade

**Fig. 1.1** (illustrative): value of Pakistan's goods trade, US$ billion.

| Year | Exports | Imports |
|---|---|---|
| Year A | 24 | 46 |
| Year B | 25 | 56 |
| Year C | 32 | 80 |
| Year D | 28 | 55 |
| Year E | 31 | 55 |

**Fig. 1.2** (illustrative): share of exports by group in Year E — textile group 55%, food group
(mainly rice) 19%, other manufactures (surgical, sports goods, leather, chemicals, cement) 19%,
other 7%.

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 1.1, calculate the balance of trade in Year C. **[1]**

**(ii)** Using Fig. 1.2, state the share of exports **not** from the textile group. **[1]**

**(iii)** Using Fig. 1.1, describe the changes in imports from Year A to Year E. **[3]**

**(b) (i)** Define *gross domestic product* (GDP). **[1]**

**(ii)** Explain the difference between GDP and GNP. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** reasons why imports fell between Year C and Year D. **[4]**

**(d)** Explain how a continuing trade deficit affects Pakistan's economy and development, and
evaluate **two** ways of reducing it. **[12]**

## Question 2: Partners and the conditions of trade

**Fig. 2.1** (illustrative): Pakistan's five largest export markets in a recent year — United
States 18%, China 8%, United Kingdom 7%, United Arab Emirates 6%, Germany 6%; all others 55%.

**(a) (i)** Using Fig. 2.1, name the largest export market. **[1]**

**(ii)** Name **two** goods Pakistan exports to the market you named in (i). **[2]**

**(iii)** Name the country from which Pakistan buys most of its palm oil. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what is meant by a *trading bloc* and give **one** example of a trading arrangement
that includes Pakistan. **[3]**

**(c) (i)** Explain how the EU's GSP+ scheme helps Pakistani exporters. **[3]**

**(ii)** Explain how a fall in the value of the rupee affects Pakistan's exports **and** imports.
**[4]**

**(d)** "It is very difficult for a developing country such as Pakistan to increase its share of
world trade." How far do you agree? Give reasons for your answer. **[11]**

## Mark scheme: Question 1

**(a) (i)** A deficit of US$48 billion (32 − 80) [1]. Must indicate deficit or show a minus sign.

**(ii)** 45% [1].

**(iii)** Any three: imports rose overall from US$46 billion to US$55 billion [1]; they rose
steeply to a peak of US$80 billion in Year C [1]; then fell sharply to US$55 billion in Year D [1];
and stayed at US$55 billion in Year E [1].

**(b) (i)** The total value of goods and services produced within a country in a year [1].

**(ii)** GDP counts output produced inside Pakistan regardless of who owns it [1]; GNP counts
output by Pakistan's residents and firms wherever they are [1]; so GNP = GDP plus income earned
abroad by Pakistanis minus income paid to foreign owners in Pakistan [1].

**(c)** Two reasons, each developed for 2 marks: world oil and gas prices fell, cutting the fuel
import bill [2]; the government restricted imports, for example limits on letters of credit, to
protect foreign-exchange reserves [2]; a sharp fall in the rupee made imports more expensive, so
fewer were bought [2]; slower economic growth reduced demand for machinery and raw materials [2].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (9–12) explains effects and evaluates two methods with a judgement;
Level 2 (5–8); Level 1 (1–4).

**Effects:** reserves fall; borrowing from the IMF and other lenders rises; debt repayments take
money from schools, hospitals and infrastructure; the rupee weakens, raising fuel and food prices;
industry cannot import machinery and inputs, slowing growth and jobs.

**Ways to reduce it, with evaluation:**
- *Raise value-added exports* (garments rather than yarn; branded sports goods): earns more per
  unit, but needs investment, skills and cheaper energy, and takes years.
- *Diversify exports* (IT services, surgical instruments, seafood, fruit): less reliance on
  textiles, but quality standards abroad are strict.
- *Cut fuel imports* by using Thar coal, hydel and solar: reduces the biggest import, but large
  projects are slow and costly.
- *Restrict imports* with tariffs: quick, but raises costs for exporters who rely on imported
  inputs and can breach trade agreements.

A Level 3 judgement explains which method is most sustainable, for example that diversifying and
adding value tackle the cause, while restrictions only treat the symptom.

## Mark scheme: Question 2

**(a) (i)** United States [1].

**(ii)** Any two of bed linen, towels, knitwear, ready-made garments, denim, leather goods,
surgical instruments [2].

**(iii)** Indonesia (accept Malaysia) [1].

**(b)** A group of countries that agree to reduce or remove barriers such as tariffs on trade
between them [2]; example: SAFTA, ECO, or the China–Pakistan Free Trade Agreement as a bilateral
arrangement [1].

**(c) (i)** It removes or cuts EU tariffs on most Pakistani goods [1], so they are cheaper for EU
buyers than goods from non-qualifying countries [1], which has helped textile and leather exports
grow [1]. Credit: access is conditional on human-rights and labour standards [1]. Maximum 3.

**(ii)** Exports become cheaper in foreign currency, so demand may rise [1] and exporters earn
more rupees per dollar [1]; imports become more expensive in rupees [1], so fuel, machinery and
inputs cost more, raising inflation and exporters' own costs [1].

**(d)** Levels: Level 3 (8–11); Level 2 (4–7); Level 1 (1–3).

**Agree:** narrow export base in textiles and rice; strong competitors (Bangladesh, Vietnam,
India); tariffs, quotas and quality standards in richer markets; high energy costs; small firms
lacking technology and branding; political tension limiting regional trade; Pakistan's share of
world exports has stayed well below one per cent.

**Disagree, or partly:** GSP+ and the China FTA give preferential access; location near the Gulf
and on CPEC routes; a young workforce and growing IT services; record rice exports show sales can
rise when conditions allow. A balanced judgement, for example "difficult but possible if Pakistan
diversifies and cuts production costs", reaches Level 3.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I show a deficit in a calculation answer?**
Write it as a negative number or use the word "deficit", and always include the unit, for example
"a deficit of US$48 billion".

**What counts as a developed point in a 2-mark "suggest" answer?**
A reason plus its effect, for example "oil prices fell, so the same volume of fuel cost less to
import".

**Can I use real figures I remember in a data question?**
Use the resource first. A remembered figure can support a point in part (d), but always date it and
mark it as approximate.
