## Three formulae

- **Mᵣ** = sum of Aᵣ of all atoms in the formula (3.6.2 a).
- **% by mass** of an element = (Aᵣ × number of its atoms) ÷ Mᵣ × 100 (3.6.2 b).
- **Empirical formula**: divide mass (or %) by Aᵣ, divide by the smallest, make whole numbers (3.6.2 c).
- **Molecular formula** = empirical formula × (Mᵣ ÷ empirical formula mass).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Relative atomic mass, Aᵣ | Mass of an atom of an element on the scale used in the periodic table |
| Relative formula mass, Mᵣ | Sum of the Aᵣ values of the atoms in a formula; no units |
| Percentage by mass | Fraction of a compound's mass due to one element, as a percentage |
| Empirical formula | Simplest whole-number ratio of atoms in a compound |
| Molecular formula | Actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule |
| Hydrated | Contains water of crystallisation, shown after a dot |
| Anhydrous | Without water of crystallisation |

## Mᵣ reminders

| Formula | Mᵣ | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|
| Na₂CO₃ | 2(23) + 12 + 3(16) = 106 | the 2 on sodium |
| Fe(OH)₃ | 56 + 3(16 + 1) = 107 | bracket multiplies O and H |
| Pb(NO₃)₂ | 207 + 2(14 + 48) = 331 | bracket multiplies N and O |
| K₂SO₄ | 2(39) + 32 + 4(16) = 174 | |
| CuSO₄·5H₂O | 159.5 + 5(18) = 249.5 | Cu = 63.5 in the specification table |

## Percentage by mass at a glance

| Compound | Element | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| CaCO₃ | Ca | 40 ÷ 100 × 100 | 40.0% |
| CuO | Cu | 63.5 ÷ 79.5 × 100 | 79.9% |
| CO(NH₂)₂ (urea) | N | 28 ÷ 60 × 100 | 46.7% |
| NH₄NO₃ | N | 28 ÷ 80 × 100 | 35.0% |

Mass of element in a sample = % by mass × mass of sample ÷ 100.

## Empirical formula ratios that need scaling

| Ratio after dividing by smallest | Multiply all by | Example |
|---|---|---|
| about 1.5 or 2.5 | 2 | 1 : 1.5 → 2 : 3 |
| about 1.33 or 1.67 | 3 | 1 : 1.33 → 3 : 4 |
| about 1.25 or 1.75 | 4 | 1 : 1.25 → 4 : 5 |

Values such as 1.98 or 3.02 come from rounding in the data: round to the whole number.

## Mini-examples

- 2.4 g carbon + 0.6 g hydrogen: C 0.2, H 0.6 → **CH₃**. With Mᵣ = 30, molecular formula **C₂H₆**.
- Phosphorus oxide, 43.7% P and 56.3% O: P 1.41, O 3.52 → 1 : 2.5 → **P₂O₅**. With Mᵣ = 284, 284 ÷ 142 = 2 → **P₄O₁₀**.
- 1.27 g copper forms 1.59 g oxide: Cu 0.0200, O 0.0200 → **CuO**.

## Exam traps

- Adding the atomic number instead of Aᵣ.
- Treating MgSO₄·7H₂O as MgSO₄ + 7H + 2O.
- Forgetting there are two nitrogen atoms in NH₄NO₃.
- Rounding 2.49 down to 2.
- Leaving the empirical formula as a decimal ratio such as CH₂.₅.
- Dividing the Mᵣ by the wrong empirical mass when finding the molecular formula.
- Giving Mᵣ units of grams.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate Mᵣ of Mg(OH)₂. (Aᵣ: Mg = 24, O = 16, H = 1)
2. Calculate Mᵣ of (NH₄)₃PO₄. (Aᵣ: N = 14, H = 1, P = 31, O = 16)
3. Calculate the percentage by mass of oxygen in water.
4. Calculate the percentage by mass of sulfur in SO₃. (Aᵣ: S = 32, O = 16)
5. What mass of calcium is in 250 g of calcium carbonate?
6. Define empirical formula.
7. A compound contains 0.10 mol of sulfur atoms and 0.20 mol of oxygen atoms. Give its empirical formula.
8. An oxide of iron contains 2.24 g of iron and 0.96 g of oxygen. Find its empirical formula. (Aᵣ: Fe = 56, O = 16)
9. A hydrocarbon has empirical formula CH₂ and Mᵣ 56. Give its molecular formula.
10. After dividing by the smallest, a student gets C 1, H 1.33. What should the student do next?
11. Which contains the higher percentage of nitrogen, NH₃ or NH₄Cl? (Aᵣ: N = 14, H = 1, Cl = 35.5)
12. 5.00 g of hydrated copper sulfate loses 1.80 g of water on heating. What mass of anhydrous copper sulfate remains?

### Answers

1. 24 + 2 × 17 = 58
2. 3 × 18 + 31 + 64 = 149
3. 16 ÷ 18 × 100 = 88.9%
4. 32 ÷ 80 × 100 = 40.0%
5. 40% of 250 g = 100 g
6. The simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound.
7. SO₂
8. Fe 2.24 ÷ 56 = 0.040; O 0.96 ÷ 16 = 0.060; ratio 1 : 1.5 → Fe₂O₃.
9. 56 ÷ 14 = 4, so C₄H₈.
10. Multiply both by 3 to get C₃H₄.
11. NH₃: 14 ÷ 17 = 82.4%; NH₄Cl: 14 ÷ 53.5 = 26.2%. NH₃ is higher.
12. 5.00 − 1.80 = 3.20 g
