AQA AS Level Business 7137 is a linear qualification, not tiered, assessed by two compulsory written papers: Paper 1 (What is business? Managing marketing and finance) and Paper 2 (Managing people and operations), each 1 hour 45 minutes (105 minutes), 80 marks and 50% of the AS, 160 marks in total. Both papers are required for the award. It is first taught from September 2026, with the first AS exams in June 2027. Use it with our topic pages on [what is business](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-what-is-business/), [marketing management](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-marketing-management/) and [financial management](/resources/aqa-as-level-business-financial-management/).

## Paper 1: What is business? Managing marketing and finance

Paper 1 covers Unit 3.1. It is 105 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the AS. The format is two case studies, each followed by six compulsory questions worth 40 marks in total, so each case-study block accounts for half the paper. **How to spend the time:** 105 minutes across two blocks leaves 52.5 minutes each. Read the case study in the first few minutes of each block, mark the numbers you may need, and move through the six questions in proportion to their marks.

## Paper 2: Managing people and operations

Paper 2 covers Unit 3.2, with links to Unit 3.1. It is also 105 minutes, 80 marks and 50% of the AS, with the same two-case-study format. **How to spend the time:** the same budget applies, about 52.5 minutes per case-study block. Because Paper 2 has links to Unit 3.1, practise using marketing and finance ideas, such as costs, cash flow and profit, inside people and operations answers.

## The outgoing specification, 7131

If you meet older materials, they may refer to AQA AS Business 7131. That specification ran two papers of 90 minutes and 80 marks each, 160 marks in total, with Paper 1 (Business 1) and Paper 2 (Business 2) each 50%. Its first assessment was June 2024 and its final AS assessment was June 2026. Past-paper timings and mark patterns from 7131 do not match 7137, so adjust your practice to the 105-minute, case-study format.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- 7137, each paper: 105 minutes / 80 marks = 1.3125 minutes per mark, or about 1.3.
- A 40-mark case-study block: 40 x 1.3125 = 52.5 minutes.
- Check: 52.5 x 2 = 105 minutes.
- For comparison, 7131 papers: 90 / 80 = 1.125 minutes per mark, so 7137 is more generous per mark.
- Reading the case study should be taken from the block's budget, not added to it.

## Command words and question styles

Business questions build in demand. "Identify" or "State" needs a short answer. "Explain" needs a point plus a reason or consequence linked to the case. "Analyse" requires a developed chain of reasoning. "Evaluate" or "Justify" requires you to weigh up options and reach a supported decision. Always use the business, the figures and the circumstances in the case study, because answers that could apply to any firm are less convincing.

## Worked practice scenario: labour turnover and productivity

**Question:** Harbour Tools Ltd employs an average of 80 staff and 12 left during the year. Output was 24,000 units from 40 production workers. After introducing training, output rose to 27,300 units with 42 production workers. (a) Calculate the labour turnover rate. (b) Calculate labour productivity before and after training. (c) Calculate the percentage change in productivity and comment.

**Model answer:**

```
(a) Labour turnover = leavers / average staff x 100
                    = 12 / 80 x 100 = 15%

(b) Productivity = output / number of workers
    Before: 24,000 / 40 = 600 units per worker
    After : 27,300 / 42 = 650 units per worker

(c) Change = (650 - 600) / 600 x 100 = 8.33% (2 d.p.)
```

Comment: productivity rose by about 8.3%, so the training appears to have helped, but the data shows one year only and other factors, such as new machinery, could have contributed. A turnover rate of 15% may be a concern if the leavers were skilled, since recruitment and retraining costs add to unit costs. A fuller judgement would compare the cost of training with the value of the extra output.

## Planning your final weeks

Because both papers are compulsory and equally weighted at 50% each, divide revision evenly between Unit 3.1 and Unit 3.2 rather than favouring the unit you enjoy. A useful rhythm is to read one case study, answer its six questions against the clock at about 52.5 minutes, then mark your own work by asking whether each answer used a figure or fact from the case. Keep a short list of the numerical measures that recur, such as margins, break-even, productivity and turnover, and rehearse them until the method is automatic. Two timed full papers in the last fortnight, one for each unit, are better than many untimed fragments.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm you are on 7137 and not the outgoing 7131.
- Time yourself at 52.5 minutes per case-study block.
- Learn the key formulae for margins, break-even, productivity and turnover.
- Skim all six questions in a block before answering the first.
- Annotate the case study with the figures you may use.
- Show your working and units for every calculation.
- Link each answer back to the named business.
- Leave a short buffer at the end of each paper to check numbers.
