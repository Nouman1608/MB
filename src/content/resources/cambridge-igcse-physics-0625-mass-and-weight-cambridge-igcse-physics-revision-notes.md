These notes condense section 1.3, Mass and weight, of the Cambridge IGCSE Physics 0625 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Supplement content, marked **Extended only**, is examined on Papers 2 and 4. Explanations and worked examples are in the [study guide on mass and weight](/resources/cambridge-igcse-physics-0625-mass-and-weight-cambridge-igcse-physics/); the [Motion, forces and energy revision notes](/resources/igcse-physics-motion-forces-and-energy-revision-notes/) cover the whole of Topic 1.

## Definitions to learn word for word

- **Mass:** a measure of the quantity of matter in an object at rest relative to the observer.
- **Weight:** a gravitational force on an object that has mass.
- **Gravitational field strength:** force per unit mass.
- *(Extended only)* **Weight** is the effect of a gravitational field on a mass.

## The equation

| Form | Use it to find |
|---|---|
| g = W / m | field strength from a weighing |
| W = m g | weight from mass |
| m = W / g | mass from a weight reading |

Units: W in **newtons (N)**, m in **kilograms (kg)**, g in **N/kg**.

g is **equivalent to the acceleration of free fall**: 9.8 N/kg = 9.8 m/s² near the Earth, because 1 N/kg = 1 m/s².

## Mass versus weight

- Mass: kg, scalar, the same everywhere.
- Weight: N, vector (towards the centre of the planet), changes with g.
- A 60 kg student has a weight of 60 × 9.8 = 588 N on the Earth and 60 × 1.6 = 96 N on the Moon. Her mass is 60 kg in both places.

## Typical field strengths

| Place | g / N/kg (approx.) | Weight of 1.0 kg |
|---|---|---|
| Earth's surface | 9.8 | 9.8 N |
| Moon's surface | 1.6 | 1.6 N |
| Mars's surface | 3.7 | 3.7 N |

Questions give the value of g for anywhere other than the Earth; use the value given.

## Balances

| Instrument | What it really responds to | Reading on the Moon for an object of mass 3.0 kg |
|---|---|---|
| Beam balance | compares two weights | balances against 3.0 kg of standard masses, as on Earth |
| Spring balance (newton meter) | stretch caused by the weight | 3.0 × 1.6 = 4.8 N (Earth: 29.4 N) |
| Electronic top-pan balance | force on the pan, shown as a mass using Earth's g | about 0.49 kg (one sixth of the true mass) |

Why a beam balance still works on the Moon: both pans are in the **same** field, so equal weights still mean equal masses.

## Gravitational fields (Extended only)

- A gravitational field is a region in which a mass experiences a force.
- Every mass at the same place feels the same force **per kilogram**, so all fall with the same acceleration (ignoring air resistance).
- g at a surface is larger for a body with more mass.
- g decreases with distance from the body; a spacecraft far from the Earth weighs less, but its mass is unchanged.
- Weight acts along the field direction, towards the centre of the body.

## Finding g by experiment

- Newton meter in a clamp; hang 100 g to 500 g in turn.
- Convert grams to kilograms; record each weight.
- Plot W (y-axis) against m (x-axis): straight line through the origin.
- Gradient = g.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mass | Quantity of matter in an object; kg |
| Weight | Gravitational force on an object that has mass; N |
| Gravitational field strength | Force per unit mass; N/kg |
| Acceleration of free fall | Acceleration of an object falling with only its weight acting; m/s² |
| Newton meter | Spring balance calibrated in newtons |
| Beam balance | Balance that compares the weights on two pans |
| Gravitational field (Extended) | Region in which a mass experiences a force |

## Exam traps

- **Units swapped.** "Weight = 50 kg" is wrong. Weight is in N.
- **Grams.** 450 g is 0.45 kg before using W = mg.
- **"Gravity" for weight.** Name the force: weight, or the gravitational force of the Earth on the object.
- **Mass changing.** Moving an object never changes its mass in this syllabus.
- **Rearranging.** m = W ÷ g, not W × g.
- **Vague definitions.** "Weight is how much something weighs" earns nothing.
- *(Extended)* **"No gravity in space."** Fields weaken with distance but do not vanish.
- *(Extended)* **Heavier falls faster.** In the same field, all masses have the same acceleration of free fall.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is meant by mass.
2. State what is meant by weight.
3. Define gravitational field strength.
4. Find the weight on the Earth of a 0.75 kg book (g = 9.8 N/kg).
5. A box weighs 196 N on the Earth. Find its mass.
6. A 5.0 kg mass weighs 18.5 N on a planet. Find g there.
7. State the unit of g in two equivalent forms.
8. Why does a beam balance give the same result on the Moon as on the Earth?
9. A spring balance reads 2.94 N with an object on Earth. What does it read on the Moon (g = 1.6 N/kg)?
10. A student says, "On the Moon my mass is less, so I can jump higher." Correct the statement.
11. *(Extended)* Describe weight in terms of a gravitational field.
12. *(Extended)* Explain why a 1 kg and a 5 kg ball dropped together in a vacuum land together.

### Answers

1. A measure of the **quantity of matter** in an object (at rest relative to the observer).
2. A **gravitational force** on an object that has mass.
3. **Force per unit mass.**
4. 0.75 × 9.8 = **7.35 N** (7.4 N).
5. 196 ÷ 9.8 = **20 kg**.
6. 18.5 ÷ 5.0 = **3.7 N/kg**.
7. **N/kg** and **m/s²**.
8. Both pans are in the **same gravitational field**, so when the weights balance, the masses are equal.
9. Mass = 2.94 ÷ 9.8 = 0.30 kg; Moon weight = 0.30 × 1.6 = **0.48 N**.
10. Her **mass is unchanged**; her **weight** is smaller because g is smaller on the Moon.
11. Weight is the **effect of a gravitational field on a mass**: the field exerts a force mg on any mass in it.
12. The field exerts the **same force per kilogram** on each ball, so both have the **same acceleration** g.
