## Section A: short questions

**1.** State why alkenes are described as unsaturated. **[1]**

**2.** Which compound is an alkene?

A: C₃H₈

B: C₄H₈

C: C₄H₁₀

D: C₂H₅OH

**[1]**

**3.** State the **two** conditions needed to crack large alkane molecules. **[2]**

**4.** Aqueous bromine is added to two hydrocarbons, X and Y, and shaken. The mixture with X stays orange. The mixture with Y becomes colourless.

**(a)** State which hydrocarbon is unsaturated. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest the name of the homologous series to which X belongs. **[1]**

**5.** Complete the equation for the cracking of nonane.

C₉H₂₀ → C₆H₁₄ + ...... **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Name the catalyst used when hydrogen is added to an alkene. **[1]**

**7.** *(Extended)* State what is meant by an addition reaction. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**8.** Fractional distillation of petroleum produces more long-chain hydrocarbons than are needed.

**(a)** Explain why the oil industry cracks long-chain alkanes. Give **two** reasons. **[2]**

**(b)** Tetradecane, C₁₄H₃₀, is cracked to give decane, C₁₀H₂₂, and **two** molecules of one other product only. Write the balanced equation and name the other product. **[2]**

**(c)** State **one** difference between cracking and fractional distillation. **[1]**

**9.** Ethane can be cracked to form ethene and one other product.

**(a)** Write the equation for this reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the displayed formula of ethene. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* But-1-ene and but-2-ene both have the molecular formula C₄H₈.

**(a)** Write the structural formula of each. **[2]**

**(b)** Both compounds react with hydrogen. Name the product, which is the same for both. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Ethene reacts with three different reagents.

**(a)** Complete the table. **[3]**

| Reagent | Condition | Name of product |
|---|---|---|
| bromine | room temperature | ............ |
| hydrogen | ............ | ethane |
| steam | acid catalyst | ............ |

**(b)** Write the balanced equation for the reaction of ethene with steam. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* Propene, CH₂=CHCH₃, reacts with aqueous bromine.

**(a)** Draw the displayed formula of the product. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why only one product is formed in this reaction. **[1]**

**13.** A student is given two unlabelled bottles. One contains hexane, the other hex-1-ene. Describe a chemical test that would show which is which, including the result for each. **[3]**

**14.** A sample of decane, C₁₀H₂₂, is cracked in the laboratory by passing its vapour over a heated catalyst. The gas produced is collected over water.

**(a)** One reaction that occurs is C₁₀H₂₂ → C₇H₁₆ + X. Give the molecular formula of X and name the homologous series it belongs to. **[2]**

**(b)** The collected gas is shaken with aqueous bromine. Predict the result and explain it. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the delivery tube must be removed from the water before the heating is stopped. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** They contain a carbon–carbon double bond / not all carbon–carbon bonds are single bonds [1].

**2.** **B** [1]. C₄H₈ fits CₙH₂ₙ. A and C are alkanes (CₙH₂ₙ₊₂); D is an alcohol.

**3.** High temperature [1]; catalyst [1].

**4.** **(a)** Y [1].

**(b)** Alkanes [1].

**5.** C₃H₆ [1]. *(9 − 6 = 3 C; 20 − 14 = 6 H.)*

**6.** Nickel [1].

**7.** A reaction in which only one product is formed [1].

**8.** **(a)** Any two of [2]:

- demand for short-chain fractions (such as gasoline/petrol) is greater than the supply from fractional distillation;
- long-chain fractions are in surplus / less useful, so they are converted into more useful products;
- cracking produces alkenes, which are used to make polymers / other chemicals such as ethanol;
- cracking produces hydrogen, which is useful.

*Do not credit "to make smaller molecules" alone.*

**(b)** C₁₄H₃₀ → C₁₀H₂₂ + 2C₂H₄ [1]; ethene [1].
*Check: C 10 + 4 = 14; H 22 + 8 = 30.*

**(c)** Cracking is a chemical reaction that breaks covalent bonds in molecules / makes new substances, whereas fractional distillation is a physical separation of substances already present by boiling point [1]. *Accept: cracking needs a catalyst; fractional distillation does not.*

**9.** **(a)** C₂H₆ → C₂H₄ + H₂ [1].

**(b)** Two carbon atoms joined by a double bond, each carbon also bonded to two hydrogen atoms, all bonds shown [1]:

```
H       H
 \     /
  C = C
 /     \
H       H
```

**10.** **(a)** But-1-ene: CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ [1]; but-2-ene: CH₃CH=CHCH₃ [1].

**(b)** Butane [1].

**11.** **(a)** 1,2-dibromoethane [1]; nickel catalyst [1]; ethanol [1].
*Accept "dibromoethane" for the first product.*

**(b)** C₂H₄ + H₂O → C₂H₅OH [1]. *Accept CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH₂OH.*

**12.** **(a)** Correct three-carbon chain with all single bonds, all atoms and bonds shown [1]; one bromine atom on each of the first two carbons, no bromine on the CH₃ carbon [1]:

```
    H   H   H
    |   |   |
H — C — C — C — H
    |   |   |
    Br  Br  H
```

**(b)** The bromine molecule adds across the double bond, so all the atoms join to form a single molecule; nothing is removed or replaced [1].

**13.** Add aqueous bromine (bromine water) to a sample of each and shake [1]. Hex-1-ene: turns from orange to colourless [1]. Hexane: no change / stays orange [1].
*Do not credit "goes clear" for the alkene result.*

**14.** **(a)** C₃H₆ [1]; alkenes [1].
*10 − 7 = 3 C; 22 − 16 = 6 H. C₃H₆ fits CₙH₂ₙ (propene).*

**(b)** The aqueous bromine turns from orange to colourless [1]; the gas contains an alkene, which is unsaturated, so bromine adds across its C=C bond [1].

**(c)** Otherwise, as the apparatus cools, water is sucked back into the hot tube, which could crack the glass [1]. *Accept "to prevent suck-back".*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "clear" instead of "colourless" in the bromine test, or giving only the final colour.
- Giving "to make small molecules" as a reason for cracking, which describes the process rather than explaining why it is done.
- Miscounting hydrogen atoms when completing cracking equations, or forgetting a multiplier such as 2C₂H₄.
- Confusing the catalysts: nickel for hydrogen, an acid for steam.
- Drawing addition products with a double bond still present, or with atoms added to a carbon that was not part of the C=C.
- Describing cracking as a separation technique.
