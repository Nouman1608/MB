## Section A - short questions

**1.** A seller collects a batch of unsafe toys from customers. This is a: **[1]**
A refund   B recall   C credit note   D discount

**2.** Which is the best first step for a buyer with a faulty product? **[1]**
A go to court   B contact the seller with proof of purchase   C throw the product away   D post an angry review only

**3.** A shop gives a customer a voucher to spend in the shop instead of money back. This is a: **[1]**
A credit note   B recall   C mediation   D deposit

**4.** State **two** reasons why consumers need protection. **[2]**

**5.** Define a refund. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** actions a seller can take to resolve a complaint. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Imran bought a pair of headphones online. The description said they were waterproof, but they stopped working after he wore them in the rain.*
(a) Identify the type of complaint. **[1]**
(b) Explain **two** actions Imran could take to resolve it. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** reasons why buyers of online goods may need more protection than buyers in a shop. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** ways in which consumer protection also benefits honest sellers. **[4]**

**10.** *A customer returns a $120 oven that has stopped working. The seller can refund $120, repair it for $35, or replace it with an oven that costs the seller $70.*
(a) Calculate how much cheaper repair is than replacement. **[1]**
(b) Calculate how much cheaper repair is than a refund. **[1]**
(c) Recommend which remedy the seller should offer if the customer still wants an oven. Give **two** reasons. **[3]**

**11.** *A company sells 900 hair dryers with a fault that can cause overheating. A recall costs $16 per dryer for collection and handling, and a safe replacement costs $25 per dryer.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of the recall. **[3]**
(b) Explain **two** reasons why the company should recall the dryers even though the cost is high. **[4]**

**12.** Discuss whether it is better for a buyer to settle a complaint directly with the seller or to go to a consumer agency. **[6]**

---

## Answers

**1.** B. A recall is a request to return unsafe goods. **[1]**

**2.** B. The buyer should contact the seller and show proof of purchase. **[1]**

**3.** A. A credit note is a voucher to spend with the same seller. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: safety [1]; quality [1]; honest information [1]; fair prices [1]; unequal power between buyer and seller [1]; protection of vulnerable groups [1]; online selling [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** Money paid back to the buyer [1] when goods are faulty or not wanted, or a service has not been provided [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: listen and apologise [1]; investigate [1]; offer a refund, repair or replacement [1]; act quickly [1]; recall unsafe goods [1]; train staff [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Goods not as described [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: contact the seller in writing with the order number and photographs [1], asking for a refund or replacement and giving a deadline [1]; ask the bank or card company for a refund [1], because the goods were not as described [1]; contact a consumer agency [1], which can give advice or take up the case [1]; leave an honest review [1], which warns other buyers [1]. (max 4 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Any two, 2 marks each: buyers cannot see or test the goods before buying [2]; the goods may look different from the photograph [2]; the seller may be in another country, which makes it harder to return goods or complain [2]; payment is made before the buyer sees the goods [2]. **[max 4]**

**9.** Any two, 2 marks each: customers trust the market and so buy more [2]; honest sellers are not undercut by dishonest sellers who sell unsafe or falsely described goods [2]; clear rules show what is expected, so sellers can plan [2]; a good complaints process helps sellers keep customers [2]. **[max 4]**

**10.** (a) 70 - 35 = **$35** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) 120 - 35 = **$85** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) Repair [1]. Reasons, any two: it costs the seller least ($35) [1]; the customer keeps the oven they wanted [1]; it avoids a lost sale [1]. (3 marks) **[5]**

**11.** (a) Cost per dryer: 16 + 25 = $41 [1]. Total: 900 x 41 [1] = **$36,900** [1]. (3 marks)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: overheating dryers could cause fires and injuries, so safety must come first [2]; compensation and legal costs after an accident could be higher than the recall [2]; failing to recall would badly damage the company's reputation [2]; the company may be fined or banned from selling [2]. (max 4 marks) **[7]**

**12.** Up to 2 for settling directly, up to 2 for going to an agency, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Direct: usually quicker and cheaper [1]; the seller wants to keep the customer and may offer a remedy at once [1]; keeps the relationship [1].
Agency: independent and has more power than a single buyer [1]; helpful when the seller refuses or ignores the buyer [1]; may bring wider action against the seller [1].
Conclusion: for example, the buyer should try the seller first with written evidence, and go to an agency only if the seller refuses [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a reason such as "sellers cheat" with no explanation of the risk to buyers.
- Listing buyer actions in the wrong order, for example court first.
- Forgetting that sellers also take actions.
- Mixing up a refund with a credit note.
- Not multiplying the unit cost by the number of items in a recall.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the two statements in 0715 section 6.3?** The reasons for consumer protection, and the actions taken by buyers and sellers to resolve complaints.

**Do the same consumer laws apply in every country?** No. Use general principles unless a question names a rule.
