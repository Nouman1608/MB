These notes condense section 2.10, Graphs of functions, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Every candidate studies this content. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [graphs of functions study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-graphs-of-functions/); to practise exam-style questions, use the [graphs of functions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-graphs-of-functions-practice/).

## The four outcomes in one line each

1. Tables of values and graphs for sums of up to three terms axⁿ, and for abˣ + c.
2. Solving equations from a graph, including where a line crosses a curve.
3. Drawing and reading exponential growth and decay graphs.
4. Estimating a gradient by drawing a tangent.

## Allowed functions

- **Powers:** n = −2, −1, −½, 0, ½, 1, 2, 3, with a rational. So y = x⁴ or y = 1/x³ are outside the list.
- **Exponential:** abˣ + c with b a positive integer; in growth and decay contexts the multiplier may be a decimal such as 1.2 or 0.8.
- **Combinations:** no more than three axⁿ terms, for example y = x³ − 3x + 1 or y = x + 4/x.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Table of values | x-values chosen by the question, with y calculated for each |
| Root | an x-value where the curve meets the x-axis (y = 0) |
| Intersection | a point where two graphs meet; its x-coordinate solves "curve = line" |
| Asymptote | a line a curve approaches but does not reach, such as y = c for abˣ + c |
| Tangent | a straight line touching the curve at one point with the same direction there |
| Chord | a line joining two points on a curve; its gradient is not the gradient at a point |
| Growth factor | the multiplier b; greater than 1 means growth, between 0 and 1 means decay |

## Shapes to expect

- **x², x³:** defined everywhere; a cubic with positive x³ coefficient ends high on the right and low on the left.
- **1/x, 1/x²:** no value at x = 0. For 1/x the two branches are in opposite quadrants; for 1/x² both branches are above the x-axis.
- **√x and 1/√x:** only for x ≥ 0 (or x > 0 for 1/√x).
- **2ˣ:** passes through (0, 1), increases ever more steeply, and approaches 0 for large negative x.
- **abˣ + c:** y-intercept a + c and horizontal asymptote y = c.

## Method: completing a table

1. Write each term as its own row.
2. Use brackets for negative x on the calculator: (−2)² = 4, (−2)³ = −8.
3. Add the rows to get y, rounding as the table does.
4. Check one value the question already gives, to confirm your method.

Example: y = 2√x − x gives y = 0, 1, 0 and −3 at x = 0, 1, 4 and 9.

## Method: solving equations from a curve

1. Write the equation you need to solve.
2. Rearrange it so one side matches the curve already drawn exactly.
3. The other side is the line to draw.
4. Read the x-coordinates of the intersections, to the accuracy of the grid.

| Curve drawn | Equation to solve | Rearranged | Line to draw |
|---|---|---|---|
| y = x + 4/x | x² − 5x + 4 = 0 | x + 4/x = 5 (divide by x) | y = 5 |
| y = x³ − 3x + 1 | x³ − 5x + 2 = 0 | x³ − 3x + 1 = 2x − 1 | y = 2x − 1 |
| y = 2ˣ | 2ˣ − x − 3 = 0 | 2ˣ = x + 3 | y = x + 3 |

Check a rearrangement by moving everything back to one side: you must recover the original equation.

## Method: gradient from a tangent

1. Draw a tangent touching the curve at the point, with the curve on one side of it near the point.
2. Extend it well beyond the point.
3. Choose two points on the line that are far apart and easy to read.
4. Gradient = change in y ÷ change in x, with units "y-units per x-unit".

A tangent through (1, 2) and (4, 11) has gradient 9 ÷ 3 = 3.

## Exponential models

- y = abˣ: a is the starting value (x = 0), b the multiplier for each step of 1 in x.
- y = abˣ + c: the curve levels off at y = c.
- Growth by 20% per year means b = 1.2; decay by 20% per year means b = 0.8.
- To find when a value is reached, draw a horizontal line at that value and read across to the curve, then down.

## Exam traps

- Plotting at x = 0 for a 1/x or 1/x² term, or joining across the gap.
- Drawing the wrong line because of a sign error in the rearrangement.
- Calculating a gradient from two points on the curve (a chord).
- Reading only one intersection when the line meets the curve two or three times in the given range.
- Giving a calculator solution when the question says "use your graph"; your graph's reading is what earns credit.
- Forgetting units or sign meaning when a gradient is a rate in context.

## Self-test

1. Which of these are of a form named in section 2.10: y = √x + 2, y = 5x⁴, y = 3 × 2ˣ + 1, y = 1/x³?
2. For y = x³ − 3x + 1, find y when x = −2.
3. Why does a table for y = x + 4/x never include x = 0?
4. For y = 2√x − x, find y when x = 9.
5. The graph of y = x + 4/x is drawn. Which line solves x² − 5x + 4 = 0, and what are the solutions?
6. The graph of y = x³ − 3x + 1 is drawn. Which line solves x³ − 5x + 2 = 0?
7. State the y-intercept and the horizontal asymptote of y = 3 × 2ˣ − 4.
8. A tangent passes through (1, 2) and (4, 11). Find its gradient.
9. A population is modelled by P = 200 × 1.5ᵗ. Find P when t = 2.
10. A machine's value is V = 640 × 0.75ᵗ dollars after t years. Find V when t = 2, and the percentage lost each year.
11. On a graph of temperature (°C) against time (minutes), a tangent has gradient −1.5. Interpret this.
12. A student joins (2, 3) and (4, 11) on a curve and calls 4 "the gradient at x = 3". What is wrong?

### Answers

1. y = √x + 2 (powers ½ and 0) and y = 3 × 2ˣ + 1 (abˣ + c). Powers 4 and −3 are not in the list.
2. (−2)³ − 3(−2) + 1 = −8 + 6 + 1 = **−1**.
3. 4/x is undefined when x = 0, so the function has no value there.
4. 2 × 3 − 9 = **−3**.
5. Divide by x: x + 4/x = 5, so draw **y = 5**. Solutions **x = 1 and x = 4**.
6. x³ − 3x + 1 = 2x − 1, so draw **y = 2x − 1**.
7. y-intercept 3 × 1 − 4 = **−1**; asymptote **y = −4**.
8. (11 − 2) ÷ (4 − 1) = **3**.
9. 200 × 2.25 = **450**.
10. 640 × 0.5625 = **360 dollars**. The multiplier 0.75 means **25%** is lost each year.
11. At that moment the temperature is **falling at 1.5 °C per minute**.
12. That is the gradient of a chord, not of the curve at a point. A tangent at x = 3 is needed.
