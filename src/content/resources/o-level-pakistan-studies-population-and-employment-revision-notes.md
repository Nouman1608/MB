**These notes condense Section 8 Population and employment of Cambridge O Level Pakistan Studies
2059 Paper 2.** The section is data-heavy: practise reading pyramids and rate graphs, learn a few
dated census figures, and prepare balanced judgements on population policy, migration schemes and
education. Full explanations are in the
[Population and Employment study guide](/resources/o-level-pakistan-studies-population-and-employment/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Birth rate | Live births per 1,000 people per year |
| Death rate | Deaths per 1,000 people per year |
| Natural increase | Birth rate minus death rate |
| Population pyramid | Bar chart of population by age group and sex |
| Dependency ratio | Dependants (under 15 and over 65) compared with people aged 15–64 |
| Push factor | Something that drives people away from a place |
| Pull factor | Something that attracts people to a place |
| Katchi abadi | Unplanned shanty settlement in a Pakistani city |
| Tent city | Temporary camp for refugees or disaster victims |
| Distribution | The pattern of where people live |
| Density | People per square kilometre |
| Underemployment | Working less, or less productively, than one could |

## Reading a pyramid: four-step method

1. **Base:** wide means high birth rate; narrowing means births are falling.
2. **Middle:** a bulge shows a large working-age or youth group.
3. **Top:** narrow means low life expectancy; widening means people are living longer.
4. **Sides:** compare male and female bars for imbalances.

Then link each feature to an effect: wide base → schools and child health; youth bulge → jobs;
widening top → pensions and care.

## Reading a birth- and death-rate graph (worked example)

*Fig.* (illustrative): birth rate 45 per 1,000 falling to 27; death rate 23 falling to 7.

> **Trend:** both rates fell. The death rate fell by 16 points to 7 per 1,000, a drop of about
> 70%, while the birth rate fell by 18 points to 27 per 1,000, a drop of only 40%, so births stayed
> high. **Natural increase:** 45 − 23 = 22 per
> 1,000 at the start and 27 − 7 = 20 per 1,000 at the end, so growth stayed high for decades.
> **Reason:** medical advances cut deaths quickly, while social attitudes to family size change
> slowly.

## Migration grid

| Type | Main push | Main pull | Effect |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rural–urban | Landlessness, floods, low wages | Jobs, schools, hospitals | Katchi abadis, congestion; rural ageing |
| Seasonal | Slack season, snow, Thar drought | Harvest and picking work | Temporary camps; extra income |
| Emigration | Unemployment, low pay | Gulf and European wages | Remittances; loss of skilled workers |
| Refugees | War in neighbouring countries | Safety, kin | Camps and tent cities; pressure on services |

**Measures:** self-help housing (e.g. incremental plots in Khuda ki Basti, Hyderabad), community
sanitation, regularising katchi abadis, standpipes and clean water. **Adverse outcomes:** upgrading
can pull in more migrants; some schemes leave overcrowded, poor housing far from jobs.

## Density: between and within

- **Between:** Punjab most populous and dense; Balochistan largest by area and least dense;
  Islamabad Capital Territory most dense.
- **Within:** dense in Lahore–Gujranwala–Faisalabad, Karachi, the Peshawar valley, Quetta;
  sparse in Cholistan, Thal, Thar, Kirthar, Chitral, and Gilgit-Baltistan's high ground.
- **Factors:** physical (relief, water, soil, climate); economic (jobs, irrigation, ports);
  social (services, kinship); political (capital city, investment, security).

## Exam traps

- **Writing "density" when the question asks for "distribution".** Distribution is about pattern;
  density is a number per km².
- **Forgetting the Northern Area.** The syllabus names it in both distribution and density.
- **Treating unemployment and underemployment as the same.** The underemployed have some work.
- **Calling all migration "bad".** Remittances and urban jobs bring real benefits.
- **Giving a trend with no figures.** Quote start and end values with units.
- **One-sided solutions.** Evaluate cost, time and cultural acceptance.

## Self-test

1. Define natural increase.
2. Which province has the lowest population density?
3. Name two push factors for rural–urban migration.
4. What is a tent city, and when have tent cities appeared in Pakistan?
5. Give one reason skilled labour is scarce in Pakistan.
6. How do unemployment and underemployment affect GDP?

**Answers:** 1. Birth rate minus death rate. 2. Balochistan. 3. Any two of landlessness, low
wages, few services, floods or drought. 4. A temporary camp for refugees or disaster victims, for
example after the 2005 earthquake or the 2010 and 2022 floods, or for Afghan refugees. 5. Low
literacy and training, or emigration of qualified people. 6. People produce less than they could,
so national output is lower.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to memorise census figures?**
Learn a few headline figures with their year, such as 241.5 million in 2023, but in data questions
always use the figures given in the resource.

**Why is female education linked to lower population growth?**
Educated women tend to marry later, know more about health and family planning, and have more
earning options, so they usually have fewer children.

**What does "Northern Area" refer to in the syllabus?**
The mountainous region now administered as Gilgit-Baltistan, where people live mainly on narrow
valley floors.
