Condensed recall for **3.2.3 Research methods 1** (OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Psychology 9685, Unit 2, Section C). For explanation and a worked study, read the [research methods 1 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-psychology-9685-research-methods-1/); then try the [research methods 1 practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-psychology-9685-research-methods-1-practice/).

## What the specification lists

- **Methods:** laboratory, field and natural experiments; naturalistic and controlled, covert and overt, participant and non-participant observation; questionnaires and interviews; correlations.
- **Scientific processes:** aims and hypotheses (directional and non-directional); population and sample, random and opportunity sampling, bias and generalisation; pilot studies; repeated measures, independent groups and matched pairs; random allocation and counterbalancing; independent, dependent and extraneous variables, manipulation, operationalisation and control; demand characteristics; behavioural categories; open and closed questions; structured and unstructured interviews; ethics (confidentiality, consent, deception, debrief, right to withdraw, protection from harm, privacy).
- **Data handling:** quantitative and qualitative data; primary and secondary data, including meta-analysis; mean, median, mode, range and standard deviation; fractions and percentages; positive, negative and zero correlations; tables, bar charts, line graphs and scattergrams; normal and skewed distributions.

## Methods at a glance

| Method | Strength | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Laboratory experiment | High control; cause and effect | Artificial; demand characteristics |
| Field experiment | Natural behaviour | Less control; consent issues |
| Natural experiment | Studies variables that cannot be manipulated | No random allocation |
| Naturalistic observation | Realistic behaviour | Little control; hard to replicate |
| Controlled observation | Replicable | Behaviour may be less natural |
| Covert observation | Less reactivity | Ethical issues of consent |
| Participant observation | Insight into the group | Loss of objectivity |
| Questionnaire | Large samples, quick | Social desirability bias |
| Structured interview | Comparable answers | Less depth |
| Unstructured interview | Rich detail | Hard to compare; interviewer effects |
| Correlation | Studies relationships that cannot be manipulated | No cause and effect |

## Designs at a glance

| Design | Strength | Limitation | Remedy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Independent groups | No order effects | Participant variables | Random allocation |
| Repeated measures | Controls participant variables | Order effects | Counterbalancing |
| Matched pairs | Reduces participant variables without order effects | Time-consuming; matching imperfect | Match on the most relevant variable |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Aim | General statement of what is being investigated |
| Directional hypothesis | Predicts the direction of a difference or relationship |
| Non-directional hypothesis | Predicts a difference or relationship, not its direction |
| Population | The group the researcher wants to study |
| Sample | The people who actually take part |
| Random sampling | Every member of the population has an equal chance of selection |
| Opportunity sampling | Using whoever is available at the time |
| Generalisation | Applying findings from the sample to the population |
| Pilot study | Small trial run to check procedures |
| Random allocation | Assigning participants to conditions by chance |
| Counterbalancing | Varying the order of conditions to spread order effects |
| Operationalisation | Defining variables in measurable terms |
| Extraneous variable | Any variable other than the IV that may affect the DV |
| Demand characteristics | Cues that reveal the aim and change participants' behaviour |
| Behavioural categories | Observable, measurable units of a target behaviour |
| Meta-analysis | Combining results of many studies on one topic |
| Co-variables | The two variables measured in a correlation |

## Calculations

```
Mean          = sum of scores / number of scores
Median        = middle score when ordered (mean of the two middle scores if n is even)
Mode          = most frequent score
Range         = highest score - lowest score
Percentage    = part / whole x 100
Percentage change = (new - old) / old x 100
```

The specification names the calculation of the mean, median, mode and range; standard deviation is listed as a measure of dispersion, so be ready to interpret it: a larger standard deviation means scores are more spread around the mean.

## Distributions

- **Normal:** symmetrical bell shape; mean = median = mode; most scores near the middle.
- **Positive skew:** most scores low, a tail of high scores; mean > median > mode.
- **Negative skew:** most scores high, a tail of low scores; mean < median < mode.

## Graphs

- **Table:** summary of raw data or descriptive statistics.
- **Bar chart:** separate categories or condition means; gaps between bars.
- **Line graph:** change across a continuous variable, such as time.
- **Scattergram:** relationship between two co-variables.

## Ethics in one line each

Consent (informed agreement) -- deception (avoid unless necessary) -- debrief (reveal the aim, reassure) -- right to withdraw (at any time) -- protection from harm (no more risk than everyday life) -- confidentiality (no identifiable data) -- privacy (observe only in public places).

## Exam traps

- Writing a hypothesis without operationalised variables or conditions.
- Confusing random sampling with random allocation.
- Identifying the design as "repeated measures" when different people did each condition.
- Stating a limitation without explaining how it affects the study in the question.
- Saying a correlation shows one variable causes another.
- Reversing the direction of skew.
- Forgetting to put scores in order before finding the median.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish a directional from a non-directional hypothesis.
2. Give one implication of using an opportunity sample.
3. What is the purpose of a pilot study?
4. Which design controls participant variables but risks order effects?
5. How does counterbalancing work?
6. Operationalise "aggression" for an observation.
7. Give one advantage of a closed question.
8. What is a natural experiment?
9. Find the median of 4, 9, 2, 7, 5, 8.
10. A set of scores has a mean of 20 and a median of 15. What kind of distribution is it?
11. What is a meta-analysis?
12. Name the seven ethical issues in the specification.

**Answers:** 1. A directional hypothesis states the direction of the effect; a non-directional one predicts an effect without its direction. 2. The sample may be biased towards people available at that time and place, so findings may not generalise. 3. To check procedures, instructions and timings before the main study. 4. Repeated measures. 5. Half the participants do condition A then B, the other half B then A, so order effects are spread across conditions. 6. For example the number of times a child hits, pushes or shouts at another child in ten minutes. 7. It gives quantitative data that are easy to analyse and compare. 8. A study in which the IV varies naturally rather than being manipulated by the researcher. 9. Ordered: 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9; median = (5 + 7) / 2 = 6. 10. Positively skewed. 11. A combination of the results of many studies on the same topic. 12. Confidentiality, consent, deception, debrief, right to withdraw, protection from harm, privacy.
