> These are original practice questions written in the style of Cambridge O Level
> Geography (2217) assessment objectives. They are not taken from any past paper
> and are not endorsed by Cambridge International. All data about the fictional
> island of Pelora is illustrative. Where a question asks for a named example,
> use your own learned case study.

Use these questions alongside the [Tourism study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-tourism/)
and the [Tourism revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-tourism-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term *receiving area*. **[1]**

**2.** State two physical attractions and two human attractions that
might draw tourists to a mountain region. **[4]**

**3.** Give two reasons why the number of international tourists has
grown in recent decades. **[2]**

**4.** Explain what is meant by *leakage* in tourism. **[2]**

**5.** Table 1 shows tourist arrivals on the fictional island of Pelora.

| Year | 2010 | 2014 | 2018 | 2022 | 2024 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Arrivals (thousands) | 120 | 150 | 205 | 190 | 240 |

**(a)** Describe the trend in arrivals between 2010 and 2024. Use
figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in arrivals from 2010 to
2024. Show your working. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** In one year, tourists spent 50 million dollars on Pelora. Of this,
30 million dollars left the island as profits to foreign-owned hotels
and payments for imported food and fuel.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of tourist spending that leaked out of
the island. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest two ways the government of Pelora could reduce this
leakage. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how keeping more spending on the island could benefit
local people through the multiplier effect. **[3]**

**7.** Table 2 shows the number of hotel workers employed on Pelora in
different months of one year.

| Month | Jan | Apr | Jul | Oct |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hotel workers | 3,800 | 2,100 | 1,400 | 3,500 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between the highest and lowest
employment. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain one problem for local people caused by this pattern. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one way the island could reduce the problem. **[2]**

**8.** A coral reef near Pelora has been damaged by large numbers of
divers and boat anchors.

**(a)** Suggest two management measures that could protect the reef. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why protecting the reef is important for the future of
tourism on the island. **[2]**

**9.** Evaluate the view that tourism brings more benefits than
disadvantages to people living in a receiving area. **[6]**

**10.** For a named area where tourism is important, describe its main
attractions and explain how tourism there is being managed to make it
more sustainable. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** The place or area that tourists travel to and visit [1].

**2.** Physical: any two of snow for skiing, scenery, lakes, waterfalls,
wildlife, clean air or cool climate for walking [2]. Human: any two of
ski lifts and resorts, hotels, historic villages, festivals, mountain
railways, restaurants [2].

**3.** Any two of: higher incomes; more paid holiday; cheaper air travel
including budget airlines; faster long-haul flights; internet booking
and social media; government promotion of tourism [1 each].

**4.** Money spent by tourists that does not stay in the receiving area
[1], for example profits sent abroad by foreign-owned hotel chains or
money paid for imported food and drink [1].

**5. (a)** Arrivals increased overall, from 120 thousand in 2010 to 240
thousand in 2024 [1]. The growth was fastest between 2014 and 2018 (150
to 205 thousand) [1]. There was a fall to 190 thousand in 2022 before a
recovery to the highest figure in 2024 [1].
**(b)** (240 - 120) / 120 x 100 [1] = **100%** increase (arrivals
doubled) [1].

**6. (a)** 30 / 50 x 100 = **60%** [1].
**(b)** Any two of: encourage or require hotels to buy local food and
crafts; support locally owned hotels and guesthouses; train local people
for skilled and management jobs; tax foreign-owned companies' profits;
develop local farming and fishing to replace imports [1 each].
**(c)** Money retained pays wages to local workers and payments to local
suppliers such as farmers and fishers [1]. They spend this money in
local shops and services [1], which creates further jobs and income, so
the original spending has a bigger total effect on the island's economy
[1].

**7. (a)** 3,800 - 1,400 = **2,400** workers [1].
**(b)** Many jobs are seasonal [1], so workers lose income in the low
season (July here) and may struggle to support families or need to
migrate for work [1].
**(c)** Any one, explained: promote off-season attractions such as
festivals or conferences; offer cheaper off-season prices; develop other
industries for the low season [1 for measure, 1 for how it helps].

**8. (a)** Any two of: limit the number of dive permits; fix permanent
mooring buoys so boats do not drop anchors; create a marine protected
zone where diving is banned; train guides and brief divers not to touch
coral; charge a reef fee to pay for monitoring [1 each].
**(b)** The reef is a main attraction [1]; if it is destroyed, visitors
will go elsewhere and jobs and income from diving tourism will be lost,
so protection keeps tourism sustainable [1].

**9.** Levels-style marking.
- Benefits [up to 3]: jobs and income; foreign currency and taxes that
  fund services; new infrastructure such as roads, water and
  electricity used by residents; money for conservation or heritage.
- Disadvantages [up to 3]: seasonal, low-paid jobs; leakage to foreign
  companies; higher house and land prices; crowding and loss of
  traditional culture; environmental damage such as litter, sewage and
  erosion.
- Judgement [required for full marks]: for example, "tourism brings more
  benefits only where it is locally controlled and managed; where
  leakage is high and the environment is damaged, local people gain
  little". Answers with no judgement are capped at 4.

**10.** Answers depend on the case study. Credit:
- Named area with accurate location [1].
- Attractions, both physical and human, with specific detail [up to 2].
- Management measures named and specific to the area [up to 2].
- Explanation of how each measure makes tourism more sustainable [up to
  2].

Example using the Maldives: coral islands in the Indian Ocean with
beaches, reefs and warm clear water; luxury resorts. Management: resorts
built on separate, uninhabited islands ("one island, one resort") limits
contact between large visitor numbers and local communities; waste is
collected and taken off resort islands, though dumping at Thilafushi
shows the limits of this. A full answer judges how far the measures
work, for example noting that sea-level rise and reef damage remain
serious threats.
