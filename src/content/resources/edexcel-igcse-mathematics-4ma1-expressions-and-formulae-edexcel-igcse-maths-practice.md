## Section A

**1.** Write each of these in conventional algebraic notation.

**(a)** y × 5 × x **[1]**

**(b)** a × a × a ÷ 2 **[1]**

**(c)** (m + n) × 3 **[1]**

**2.** Work out the value of 4a − b² when a = −1/2 and b = −3. **[2]**

**3.** v = u + at. Work out v when u = 12, a = −3.5 and t = 4. **[2]**

**4.** Make w the subject of P = 2l + 2w. **[2]**

**5.** Make x the subject of y = x/3 − 8. **[2]**

**6.** Q = (p + q)/(pq). Work out Q when p = 2/3 and q = 3/4. Give your answer as a fraction. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A window cleaner charges a fixed £12 per visit plus £1.75 for each window.

**(a)** Write a formula for the charge, £C, for cleaning n windows on one visit. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out the charge for 18 windows. **[1]**

**(c)** Make n the subject of your formula. **[2]**

**(d)** One visit cost £40. How many windows were cleaned? **[1]**

**8.** A rectangular garden is (3x + 2) m long and (x + 4) m wide. It contains a square pond of side x m. The rest of the garden is lawn.

**(a)** Show that the area of the lawn is (2x² + 14x + 8) m². **[3]**

**(b)** Derive an expression, in its simplest form, for the perimeter of the garden. **[2]**

**9.** A = πr² + 20.

**(a)** Make r the subject of the formula, where r > 0. **[3]**

**(b)** Work out r when A = 100. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher tier only)** Make x the subject of 5x − a = bx + 2. **[3]**

**11.** **(Higher tier only)** Make m the subject of a = (m + 2)/(3 − m). **[4]**

**12.**

**(a)** The volume of a cone is V = (1/3)πr²h. Make r the subject, where r > 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Work out r when V = 300 and h = 12. Give your answer correct to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**(c)** E = ½mv². Make v the subject, where v > 0, and find v when E = 90 and m = 5. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **5xy** [1].

**(b)** **a³/2** (or ½a³) [1].

**(c)** **3(m + n)** [1].

**2.** 4 × (−1/2) = −2 and (−3)² = 9 [1]; −2 − 9 = **−11** [1].

**3.** v = 12 + (−3.5) × 4 [1] = 12 − 14 = **−2** [1].

**4.** P − 2l = 2w [1]; **w = (P − 2l)/2** (or w = P/2 − l) [1].

**5.** y + 8 = x/3 [1]; **x = 3(y + 8)** (or 3y + 24) [1].

**6.** p + q = 2/3 + 3/4 = 17/12 and pq = 1/2 [1]; Q = (17/12) ÷ (1/2) = **17/6** [1].

**7. (a)** C = 12 + 1.75n: 1 mark for 1.75n with any constant, 1 mark for the fully correct formula starting "C =" [2].

**(b)** 12 + 1.75 × 18 = **£43.50** [1].

**(c)** C − 12 = 1.75n [1]; **n = (C − 12)/1.75** [1].

**(d)** (40 − 12)/1.75 = 28/1.75 = **16 windows** [1].

**8. (a)** Garden area = (3x + 2)(x + 4) [1] = 3x² + 12x + 2x + 8 = 3x² + 14x + 8 [1]; lawn = 3x² + 14x + 8 − x² = 2x² + 14x + 8, as required [1].

**(b)** 2(3x + 2) + 2(x + 4) [1] = **8x + 12** m [1].

**9. (a)** A − 20 = πr² [1]; (A − 20)/π = r² [1]; **r = √((A − 20)/π)** [1].

**(b)** √(80/π) = 5.0462... = **5.05** [1].

**10.** 5x − bx = 2 + a [1]; x(5 − b) = a + 2 [1]; **x = (a + 2)/(5 − b)** [1].

**11.** a(3 − m) = m + 2 [1]; 3a − am = m + 2 [1]; 3a − 2 = m + am = m(1 + a) [1]; **m = (3a − 2)/(1 + a)** [1].

**12. (a)** 3V = πr²h, so r² = 3V/(πh) [1]; **r = √(3V/(πh))** [1].

**(b)** √(900/(12π)) = √23.873... = 4.886... = **4.89** [1].

**(c)** 2E = mv², so v² = 2E/m [1]; **v = √(2E/m)** [1]; v = √(180/5) = √36 = **6** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1(b): writing 3a/2 instead of a³/2. Repeated multiplication is a power, not a coefficient.
- Question 2: working out b² as −9. The bracket (−3)² gives +9, so the expression subtracts 9.
- Question 4: writing w = P − 2l/2, which divides only 2l by 2.
- Question 5: multiplying only x/3 by 3 and leaving x = 3y + 8.
- Question 7(a): writing "12 + 1.75n" without "C =", or 1.75 + 12n with the rates swapped.
- Question 8(a): forgetting to subtract the pond, or subtracting x instead of x².
- Question 9(a): taking the square root before dividing by π, or subtracting 20 after the root.
- Questions 10 and 11: collecting terms but not factorising, so the subject still appears twice.
- Question 12(a): writing r = √(3V/π) × h, which leaves h outside the root.

## Method reminder for questions 10 and 11

Both questions follow the same five steps. Clear any fraction by multiplying both sides by the denominator; expand any bracket; move every term containing the new subject to one side and every other term to the other; factorise the subject out as a common factor; divide both sides by the bracket. In question 11 it is neater to collect the m terms on the right, where they are both positive: 3a − 2 = m + am. Either side is acceptable, but if you collect them on the left you will get m = (2 − 3a)/(−1 − a), which is the same answer with both signs changed. Check by choosing a value, for example m = 1 gives a = 3/2 in the original, and (3 × 1.5 − 2)/(1 + 1.5) = 2.5/2.5 = 1.
