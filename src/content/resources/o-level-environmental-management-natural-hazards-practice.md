These questions practise every learning outcome in Topic 6, Natural hazards, of the 2027-2029 Cambridge
O Level Environmental Management (5014) syllabus. The contexts are an island volcano, a coastal city hit
by a tsunami, a river floodplain and a farming region in drought.

## Section A

**1.** Name the continent that lies over the South Pole. **[1]**

**2.** State the type of plate boundary where plates slide past each other. **[1]**

**3.** State the minimum ocean depth needed for a tropical cyclone to form. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** gases released in a volcanic eruption. **[2]**

## Section B

**5.** An island volcano lies in the middle of a plate, far from any plate boundary.
(a) Explain how the volcano formed. **[2]**
(b) Describe what a pyroclastic flow is and why it is dangerous. **[2]**
(c) Explain how the volcano could cause acid rain. **[2]**

**6.** Describe what happens at a divergent (constructive) plate boundary. **[3]**

**7.** An undersea earthquake triggers a tsunami that strikes a coastal city.
(a) Distinguish between the focus and the epicentre. **[2]**
(b) Describe **four** impacts of the tsunami on people. **[4]**
(c) Explain why water-related diseases may spread after the event. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways volcanoes provide economic opportunities for people. **[4]**

**9.** A region lies at 18° N. Its sea temperature is 29 °C in September and 25 °C in February.
Explain in which month a tropical cyclone is more likely to form. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how cyclone shelters and improved building structure reduce the impacts of tropical
cyclones. **[4]**

**11.** A town on a river floodplain has grown rapidly and nearby hills have been cleared for farming.
(a) Explain **three** reasons why flood risk has increased. **[3]**
(b) Describe **two** opportunities that floods provide to farmers on the floodplain. **[2]**

**12.** Explain how climate change can cause both more flooding and more drought. **[3]**

**13.** Describe **four** strategies to increase water supply in a region affected by drought. **[4]**

## Section C

**14.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of land use zoning and monitoring and warning as strategies for
managing flooding. **[6]**

**15.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of international aid in managing the impacts of a drought. **[6]**

**16.** "Building design is the most important way to reduce deaths from earthquakes." Discuss. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Antarctica. **[1]**

**2.** Conservative. **[1]**

**3.** 60 m. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: water vapour; carbon dioxide; sulfur dioxide. **[2]**

**5.** (a) It formed over a hotspot **[1]**, where hot magma rises from the mantle through the plate to the
surface **[1]**.
(b) A fast-moving cloud of hot gas, ash and rock **[1]**; it moves too fast to escape and burns or buries
everything in its path **[1]**.
(c) The eruption releases sulfur dioxide **[1]**, which reacts with oxygen and water vapour in the atmosphere
to form acid rain **[1]**.

**6.** The plates move apart **[1]**, driven by convection currents in the mantle **[1]**; magma rises into the
gap, causing volcanic activity and earthquakes **[1]**.

**7.** (a) The focus is the point underground where energy is released **[1]**; the epicentre is the point on
the surface directly above it **[1]**.
(b) Any four: deaths and injuries **[1]**; buildings and infrastructure destroyed **[1]**; evacuation and
homelessness **[1]**; drinking water contaminated by sea water and sewage **[1]**; fires **[1]**; financial
losses from damaged businesses **[1]**. **[max 4]**
(c) Sewage systems are damaged and drinking water is contaminated **[1]**, so bacteria that cause diseases
such as cholera spread **[1]**.

**8.** Any two explained: fertile soils from weathered volcanic rock give high crop yields **[2]**; tourism to
see the volcano brings income and jobs **[2]**; geothermal power provides cheap, renewable electricity **[2]**;
minerals and building materials can be extracted and sold **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**9.** September **[1]**, because the sea temperature (29 °C) is above the 27 °C needed, whereas in February
(25 °C) it is too cool **[1]**.

**10.** Shelters: strong, raised buildings where people can stay during the storm **[1]**, protecting them from
wind and flooding **[1]**. Building structure: reinforced roofs fixed to walls and buildings on stilts **[1]**
resist high winds and floodwater, so fewer collapse **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Urban growth adds impermeable surfaces, so less infiltration **[1]**; deforestation on the hills
reduces interception, increasing surface run-off **[1]**; eroded soil silts the river, reducing its capacity
**[1]**.
(b) Deposition of nutrient-rich silt **[1]**; recharge of surface and ground water stores for irrigation **[1]**.

**12.** Higher global temperatures cause more extreme weather events **[1]**: some places receive more intense
rainfall, causing floods **[1]**, while others have longer dry periods and more evaporation, causing drought
**[1]**.

**13.** Any four: dams and reservoirs **[1]**; use of aquifers and wells **[1]**; desalination **[1]**; rainwater
harvesting **[1]**; emergency water supplies brought by tanker. **[max 4]**

**14.** **[6]** Land use zoning: keeps homes and important buildings off the highest-risk land and allows
parkland or farms there; but land is scarce in growing towns, rules are hard to enforce, and existing
buildings remain. Monitoring and warning: river gauges and forecasts give time to evacuate and move
property; but warnings must reach everyone and give no protection to property that cannot be moved.
Conclusion: zoning reduces long-term risk; warning reduces immediate deaths; both are needed.

**15.** **[6]** Benefits: food, water and medical aid prevent famine and death when local resources fail;
funding can support wells or reservoirs. Limitations: aid may arrive late because droughts develop slowly
and attention comes late; distribution problems in remote areas; dependency; food aid can lower local
prices. Conclusion: aid saves lives in severe drought but works best alongside monitoring and water
conservation.

**16.** **[6]** For: most earthquake deaths come from collapsing buildings, so cross-bracing, flexible
foundations and strong materials save many lives; buildings protect people at all times. Against: costly
for low-income countries and for existing buildings; land use zoning, disaster preparation and drills,
monitoring for tsunamis, and rescue teams also save lives; fire and landslides can kill even where
buildings stand. Conclusion: building design is the single most important but is not enough alone.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not using the figures in the source, for example the sea temperatures in question 9.
- Describing a strategy without saying how it reduces impacts.
- Ignoring the "before, during, after" framing in management questions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn the seven continents?**
Yes. Syllabus outcome 6.1.2 asks you to identify Africa,
Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania and South America.

**Is the Richter scale used in 5014?**
The syllabus names the Moment Magnitude Scale for earthquake
magnitude, so use that term.

**What are the opportunities provided by volcanoes?**
Fertile soils, extraction of minerals, building
materials, geothermal power and tourism.
