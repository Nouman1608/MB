## Questions

**1.** Calculate the relative formula mass, Mr, of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. **[2]**

**2.** *(Supplement)* State the volume, in dm³, occupied by one mole of any gas at r.t.p. **[1]**

**3.** *(Supplement)* A sample of ammonia gas, NH₃, has a mass of 8.5 g.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of ammonia in the sample. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of the sample at r.t.p., in dm³. **[1]**

**4.** *(Supplement)* Magnesium reacts with hydrochloric acid: Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

Calculate the volume of hydrogen, in cm³, formed at r.t.p. when 1.2 g of magnesium reacts completely. **[3]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* 4.0 g of sodium hydroxide, NaOH, is dissolved in water to make 250 cm³ of solution.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the solution in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the solution in g/dm³. **[1]**

**6.** *(Supplement)* Calcium carbonate decomposes on heating: CaCO₃ → CaO + CO₂

Calculate the mass of calcium oxide formed when 25 g of calcium carbonate decomposes completely. **[3]**

**7.** *(Supplement)* A compound contains only aluminium and oxygen. A 10.2 g sample contains 5.4 g of aluminium and 4.8 g of oxygen. Calculate the empirical formula of the compound. **[3]**

**8.** *(Supplement)* Nitrogen-containing fertilisers are compared by the percentage of nitrogen they contain by mass.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in ammonium nitrate, NH₄NO₃. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in urea, CO(NH₂)₂. **[2]**

**(c)** Compare the two fertilisers in terms of the mass of nitrogen supplied by 100 g of each. **[1]**

**9.** *(Supplement)* Magnesium burns in oxygen: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO

3.6 g of magnesium is burned and 5.1 g of magnesium oxide is collected. Calculate the percentage yield of magnesium oxide. **[3]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* In a titration, 20.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid is exactly neutralised by 32.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution. H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

Calculate the concentration of the sodium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³. **[4]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* 2.00 g of calcium carbonate is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.500 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid. CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of each reactant and state which reactant is limiting. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed at r.t.p., in cm³. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of calcium chloride formed. **[2]**

**12.** *(Supplement)* A student says that 32 g of oxygen gas, O₂, and 32 g of sulfur dioxide gas, SO₂, contain the same number of molecules. Explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

## Answers

Marking key: **M** = method mark, **A** = answer mark. A correct final answer with no working scores full marks unless the question says "show". Where a first-stage answer is wrong, a later method mark can still be awarded for correct use of that wrong value (error carried forward).

**1.** Mr = 40 + 2 × (16 + 1) [M1]; = **74** [A1].

**2.** **24 dm³** [1].

**3.**

**(a)** Mr of NH₃ = 14 + 3 = 17 [M1]; n = 8.5 ÷ 17 = **0.50 mol** [A1].

**(b)** V = 0.50 × 24 = **12 dm³** [1].

**4.** n(Mg) = 1.2 ÷ 24 = 0.050 mol [M1]; ratio Mg : H₂ = 1 : 1, so n(H₂) = 0.050 mol [M1]; V = 0.050 × 24 000 = **1200 cm³** [A1] (accept 1.2 dm³ only if the unit is given as dm³).

**5.**

**(a)** Mr of NaOH = 23 + 16 + 1 = 40; n = 4.0 ÷ 40 = 0.10 mol [M1]; c = 0.10 ÷ 0.250 = **0.40 mol/dm³** [A1].

**(b)** 4.0 ÷ 0.250 = **16 g/dm³** [1] (or 0.40 × 40 = 16).

**6.** Mr of CaCO₃ = 40 + 12 + 48 = 100; n = 25 ÷ 100 = 0.25 mol [M1]; ratio CaCO₃ : CaO = 1 : 1, so n(CaO) = 0.25 mol [M1]; mass = 0.25 × 56 = **14 g** [A1] (Mr of CaO = 56).

**7.** Al: 5.4 ÷ 27 = 0.20 mol; O: 4.8 ÷ 16 = 0.30 mol [M1]; ratio 0.20 : 0.30 = 2 : 3 [M1]; empirical formula **Al₂O₃** [A1].

**8.**

**(a)** Mr = 28 + 4 + 48 = 80 [M1]; % N = 28 ÷ 80 × 100 = **35.0%** [A1].

**(b)** Mr = 12 + 16 + 2 × (14 + 2) = 60 [M1]; % N = 28 ÷ 60 × 100 = **46.7%** [A1].

**(c)** 100 g of urea supplies more nitrogen (46.7 g) than 100 g of ammonium nitrate (35.0 g) [1].

**9.** n(Mg) = 3.6 ÷ 24 = 0.15 mol; ratio Mg : MgO = 1 : 1, so n(MgO) = 0.15 mol [M1]; theoretical mass = 0.15 × 40 = 6.0 g [M1]; yield = 5.1 ÷ 6.0 × 100 = **85%** [A1].

**10.** n(H₂SO₄) = 0.100 × 0.0200 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; ratio H₂SO₄ : NaOH = 1 : 2, so n(NaOH) = 4.00 × 10⁻³ mol [M1]; volume of NaOH = 0.0320 dm³ [M1]; c = 4.00 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0320 = **0.125 mol/dm³** [A1].

**11.**

**(a)** n(CaCO₃) = 2.00 ÷ 100 = 0.0200 mol [1]; n(HCl) = 0.500 × 0.0500 = 0.0250 mol [1]; ratio 1 : 2, so 0.0200 mol of CaCO₃ needs 0.0400 mol of HCl, which is more than is present, so **hydrochloric acid is limiting** [1].

**(b)** n(CO₂) = 0.0250 ÷ 2 = 0.0125 mol; V = 0.0125 × 24 000 = **300 cm³** [1].

**(c)** n(CaCl₂) = 0.0125 mol [M1]; Mr = 40 + 71 = 111; mass = 0.0125 × 111 = 1.3875, so **1.39 g** [A1] (accept 1.4 g).

**12.** Mr of O₂ = 32, so 32 g is 1.0 mol; Mr of SO₂ = 64, so 32 g is 0.50 mol [1]; equal numbers of molecules need equal amounts in moles, and the SO₂ sample has half as many molecules [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³ before using concentration × volume.
- Not using the mole ratio from the balanced equation, especially where it is 1 : 2.
- Choosing the limiting reactant by comparing masses instead of moles.
- Using 16 instead of 32 for the Mr of oxygen gas, or giving the Mr of CaCl₂ as 75.5 instead of 111.
- Giving a volume in dm³ when the question asks for cm³, or the reverse.
- Turning the percentage-yield fraction upside down.
