Quick-recall notes for **sub-topic 2.1 Commercial enterprises** in Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100). The [commercial enterprises study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-commerce-7100-commercial-enterprises/) explains each point with examples and an annotated answer.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Private sector | Enterprises owned by private individuals or investors |
| Public sector | Enterprises owned and controlled by the government for the public |
| Unlimited liability | Owners' personal possessions can be used to pay business debts |
| Limited liability | Owners can lose only the money they invested in shares |
| Separate legal entity | A company exists in law apart from its owners and continues if an owner leaves or dies |
| Cooperative | Business owned and controlled by its members for their benefit, usually one member one vote |
| Partnership agreement | Document setting out partners' capital, profit shares and duties |
| Public corporation | State-owned enterprise run by a government-appointed board |
| Multinational company (MNC) | Company with headquarters in one country and operations in others |
| Host country | The country where an MNC sets up operations |
| Franchisor | Business that owns the brand and sells the right to use it |
| Franchisee | Business that buys the right to trade under the brand |
| Royalty | Ongoing payment from franchisee to franchisor, often a percentage of sales |

## Ownership forms: one-line summaries

- **Sole trader**: one owner, full control, keeps all profit, unlimited liability, limited capital.
- **Partnership**: two or more owners, shared capital and skills, usually unlimited liability, risk of disagreement.
- **Cooperative**: member-owned, one member one vote, profits shared among members, aims to benefit members.
- **Private limited company**: limited liability, shares sold privately, accounts must be filed, cannot offer shares to the general public.
- **Public limited company**: limited liability, shares offered to the public, largest capital, accounts published, risk of takeover, owners often not managers.

## Public corporations

- **Features**: state-owned; board appointed by government; financed by government and customers' payments; accountable to government; service objective.
- **Reasons**: essential services at affordable prices; strategic industries under national control; services in loss-making areas; prevent private monopolies; very large projects; protect jobs.

## MNCs

**Reasons they exist:** new markets; cheaper labour, land or materials; avoid tariffs by producing inside a market; lower transport costs; spread risk; economies of scale; host government incentives.

| Positive for host | Negative for host |
|---|---|
| Jobs and training | Local firms closed by competition |
| New technology and skills | Profits sent abroad |
| Investment and infrastructure | Low wages or poor conditions |
| Tax revenue | Environmental damage |
| Exports and consumer choice | Political influence; may leave suddenly |

## Franchises

- **Features**: brand, logo, products and methods supplied by franchisor; initial fee plus royalties; franchisee follows the franchisor's rules; franchisor gives training and national advertising.
- **Franchisee**: lower risk, known brand, support / fees, royalties, little freedom.
- **Franchisor**: rapid growth with others' capital, fee income / less control, brand risk from poor outlets.

## Location factors

- **Physical (9)**: labour, markets, transport, power, raw materials, competition, suppliers, communication, government influence.
- **Ecommerce (4)**: wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists, logistics.
- Heavy raw materials → near the source. Perishable or service products → near customers. Online seller → connectivity, warehouse and delivery links.

## Exam traps

- **Public limited company is private sector.** "Public" refers to who can buy the shares, not who owns the business.
- **Liability is about debts, not profits.** Do not write "limited liability means limited profit".
- **Cooperative members are not partners**; a cooperative is a separate form.
- **Two sides for MNCs and franchises**: questions often ask for positives and negatives, or for franchisee and franchisor.
- **Location answers must be selective**: the most relevant factors for the business, with a reason.

## Self-test

1. What decides whether an enterprise is in the private or public sector?
2. Define unlimited liability.
3. Give two features of a cooperative.
4. State one difference between a private limited company and a public limited company.
5. Why is a company called a separate legal entity?
6. Give three reasons why a government may run a public corporation.
7. Define a multinational company.
8. State two reasons why an MNC may set up a factory in another country.
9. Give one positive and one negative impact of an MNC on a host country.
10. What is a royalty in a franchise?
11. Give one disadvantage of franchising for the franchisor.
12. Name the four ecommerce location factors.

## Self-test answers

1. Who owns it: private individuals or investors (private sector) or the government (public sector).
2. The owner's personal possessions can be used to pay the business's debts.
3. Any two: owned by members; usually one member one vote; profits shared among members or used for their benefit; aims to serve members.
4. A public limited company can offer shares to the general public, often on a stock exchange; a private limited company can sell shares only privately.
5. It exists in law separately from its owners: it can own property, make contracts and be sued in its own name, and it continues if an owner leaves.
6. Any three: essential services at affordable prices; strategic control; services in loss-making areas; preventing private monopoly; very large projects; protecting jobs.
7. A company with its headquarters in one country and production or service operations in at least one other country.
8. Any two: new markets; cheaper labour or materials; avoid tariffs; lower transport costs; spread risk; government incentives.
9. Positive, for example jobs and training; negative, for example profits sent back to the home country.
10. An ongoing payment from the franchisee to the franchisor, often a percentage of sales.
11. Less direct control over quality, so a poor outlet can damage the whole brand.
12. Wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists and logistics.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know the legal registration documents for companies?** The 7100 content asks for features of each form, not the names of registration documents, so concentrate on ownership, liability, control, profit and capital.

**How many location factors should an answer use?** Usually two or three, chosen for the business in the question and each explained.
