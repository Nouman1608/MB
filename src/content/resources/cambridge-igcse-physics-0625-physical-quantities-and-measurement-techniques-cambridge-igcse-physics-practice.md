## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** A student measures the length of a metal rod with a wooden metre rule. The zero end of the rule is worn.

**(a)** Describe how the student should use the rule to measure the length of the rod. **[2]**
**(b)** State how the student should position their eye when taking each reading, and why. **[2]**

**2.** A student needs to measure about 70 cm³ of water. Three measuring cylinders are available: 10 cm³ (divisions of 0.2 cm³), 100 cm³ (divisions of 1 cm³) and 1000 cm³ (divisions of 10 cm³).

**(a)** State which cylinder the student should use, and explain why. **[2]**
**(b)** State which part of the water surface the student should read. **[1]**

**3.** Suggest a suitable instrument for timing each of these, and give a reason for one of your choices.

**(a)** the time for an ice cube to melt in a glass of water **[1]**
**(b)** the time for a trolley to travel 10 cm along a bench at high speed **[1]**
**(c)** reason **[1]**

**4.** A stack of 25 identical metal washers is 41.0 mm tall.

**(a)** Calculate the thickness of one washer. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why this method gives a more accurate thickness than measuring one washer with a ruler. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* This question is about scalars and vectors.

**(a)** State whether each of these quantities is a scalar or a vector: (i) mass (ii) weight (iii) gravitational field strength. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the difference between speed and velocity. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Two forces act on a small object: 9.0 N towards the east and 12.0 N towards the north.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the resultant and the 9.0 N force. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student investigates a simple pendulum.

**(a)** Define the period of a pendulum. **[1]**
**(b)** The student first times single oscillations and records 1.2 s, 1.6 s and 1.3 s. Explain why these readings are unreliable. **[2]**
**(c)** The student then times 20 oscillations three times and records 27.9 s, 28.4 s and 28.3 s. Calculate the period. **[2]**
**(d)** State one technique that helps the student start and stop the stopwatch at the right moments. **[1]**

**8.** A student wants to find the diameter of a thin copper wire using a ruler and a pencil.

**(a)** Describe how the student can find the diameter. **[3]**
**(b)** 40 turns of the wire cover 22.0 mm of the pencil. Calculate the diameter of the wire. **[2]**
**(c)** In a second attempt the turns are not quite touching. State the effect on the calculated diameter. **[1]**

**9.** A student finds the volume of one drop of water from a dropper. The water level in a 10 cm³ measuring cylinder is 5.0 cm³. After 60 drops are added, the level is 8.0 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of one drop. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the student did not measure the volume of one drop directly. **[1]**
**(c)** State why the cylinder must stand on a level bench. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A swimmer swims straight across a river at 1.5 m/s relative to the water. The river flows at 0.80 m/s parallel to the banks.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the swimmer's resultant velocity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the resultant velocity and the direction straight across the river. **[2]**
**(c)** The river is 30 m wide. Calculate the time taken to cross and how far downstream the swimmer lands. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Two tugs pull a ship. One pulls with a force of 12 kN due east and the other with 5.0 kN due south.

**(a)** Describe how to find the resultant force by scale drawing. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the magnitude of the resultant force. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate its direction, measured from due east. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A student says: "The resultant of a 3.0 N force and a 4.0 N force is always 7.0 N."

**(a)** State the resultant when the forces act in the same direction along a straight line. **[1]**
**(b)** State the resultant when they act in opposite directions along a straight line. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the resultant when they act at right angles. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why force is a vector but energy is a scalar. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Place one end of the rod against a clear mark away from the worn end (for example 10.0 cm) [1]; read the other end and subtract the starting reading [1].
**(b)** Directly above the mark, at right angles to the scale [1]; to avoid parallax error [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Make sure the ruler is straight" does not answer (a); the worn end is the point of the question.

**2. (a)** The 100 cm³ cylinder [1]; the 10 cm³ cylinder is too small and the 1000 cm³ cylinder's 10 cm³ divisions are too coarse to read 70 cm³ precisely [1].
**(b)** The bottom of the meniscus [1].

**3. (a)** A clock or stopwatch [1]
**(b)** A digital timer with light gates [1]
**(c)** The trolley takes only a fraction of a second, comparable to human reaction time, so hand timing would have a large error; or, the melting takes minutes, so reaction time is negligible [1].

**4. (a)** 41.0 ÷ 25 [1] = **1.64 mm** [1]
**(b)** The uncertainty in reading the ruler is shared between 25 washers, so it is a much smaller fraction of each washer's thickness [1].
*Examiner insight:* An answer of 1.6 mm is acceptable; 1.64 mm shows the benefit of the method.

**5. (a)** (i) scalar [1] (ii) vector [1] (iii) vector [1]
**(b)** Speed has magnitude only; velocity is speed in a given direction (magnitude and direction) [1].

**6. (a)** R = √(9.0² + 12.0²) = √225 [1] = **15 N** [1]
**(b)** tan θ = 12.0 ÷ 9.0 [1]; θ = **53°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* 9.0 + 12.0 = 21 N scores zero in (a).

**7. (a)** The time for one complete oscillation (there and back) [1].
**(b)** One oscillation lasts about the same time as human reaction time [1], so reaction-time errors are a large fraction of each reading (and the readings vary a lot) [1].
**(c)** Mean = (27.9 + 28.4 + 28.3) ÷ 3 = 28.2 s [1]; period = 28.2 ÷ 20 = **1.41 s** [1]
**(d)** Any one: use a fixed marker at the centre of the swing; time as the bob passes the centre (where it moves fastest); count "zero" as the stopwatch starts; count passes in the same direction [1].

**8. (a)** Wind the wire tightly round the pencil with the turns touching [1]; count a large number of turns, for example 40 [1]; measure the length of the coil with the ruler and divide by the number of turns [1].
**(b)** 22.0 ÷ 40 [1] = **0.55 mm** [1]
**(c)** The calculated diameter would be too large (the gaps add to the measured length) [1].

**9. (a)** Volume of 60 drops = 8.0 − 5.0 = 3.0 cm³ [1]; one drop = 3.0 ÷ 60 = **0.050 cm³** [1]
**(b)** One drop is too small to read on the cylinder's scale (the reading uncertainty would be larger than the volume) [1].
**(c)** If the cylinder tilts, the liquid level is not at the true mark, so the reading is wrong [1].

**10. (a)** v = √(1.5² + 0.80²) = √2.89 [1] = **1.7 m/s** [1]
**(b)** tan θ = 0.80 ÷ 1.5 [1]; θ = **28°** (downstream of straight across) [1]
**(c)** t = 30 ÷ 1.5 = **20 s** [1]; distance downstream = 0.80 × 20 = **16 m** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the crossing time uses only the across component of velocity; using 1.7 m/s and 30 m is a common error.

**11. (a)** Choose a scale, for example 1 cm to 1 kN [1]; draw a 12 cm arrow east and, from its tip, a 5.0 cm arrow south at 90° [1]; draw the resultant from the start of the first arrow to the end of the second, measure its length and convert, and measure the angle with a protractor [1].
**(b)** √(12² + 5.0²) = √169 [1] = **13 kN** [1]
**(c)** tan θ = 5.0 ÷ 12, θ = **23° south of east** [1]

**12. (a)** **7.0 N** [1]
**(b)** **1.0 N** (in the direction of the 4.0 N force) [1]
**(c)** √(3.0² + 4.0²) = **5.0 N** [1]
**(d)** A force acts in a particular direction (it has magnitude and direction); energy has magnitude only [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the wrong number of oscillations, turns or intervals.
- Writing "read the meniscus" without saying the bottom of it.
- Choosing a cylinder or ruler without saying why (range, or size of scale divisions).
- *(Extended)* Adding perpendicular vectors as plain numbers.
- *(Extended)* Giving an angle without saying what it is measured from.
