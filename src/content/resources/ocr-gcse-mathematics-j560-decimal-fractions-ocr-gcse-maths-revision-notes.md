These notes condense section 2.02, Decimal fractions, of OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026): 2.02a decimals and fractions, 2.02b addition, subtraction and multiplication of decimals, and 2.02c division of decimals. Everything is for **both tiers** except converting recurring decimals to exact fractions and back, which is **Higher tier only**.

The [2.02 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-decimal-fractions-ocr-gcse-maths/) explains each method with worked examples; the [2.02 practice questions](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-decimal-fractions-ocr-gcse-maths-practice/) give exam-style questions with mark schemes. For decimals with fractions, see the [fractions and decimal fractions revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-fractions-and-decimal-fractions-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Decimal fraction | A fraction written with place value after a decimal point | 0.65 = 65/100 |
| Terminating decimal | Stops after a finite number of digits | 3/8 = 0.375 |
| Recurring decimal | A digit or block repeats for ever | 7/12 = 0.58333... |
| Recurring block | The digits that repeat | 571428 in 4/7 |
| Place value | The value of a digit from its position | In 2.074, the 7 is 7 hundredths |
| Dividend, divisor | Number divided, number divided by | In 0.63 ÷ 0.009, 0.63 and 0.009 |
| Exact | Not rounded; often a fraction | 4/11 rather than 0.364 |

## Conversions

```
terminating decimal → fraction:  over 10, 100, 1 000 …; simplify
                                 0.36 = 36/100 = 9/25
fraction → decimal (friendly):   scale the denominator to 10, 100 or 1 000
                                 13/20 = 65/100 = 0.65
fraction → decimal (any):        short division; watch for a repeating remainder
                                 7/12 = 0.58333...
terminates?                      only if the simplified denominator has
                                 prime factors 2 and 5 alone
```

## Recurring to fraction (Higher tier only)

```
1. Let x = the decimal.
2. Multiply by 10ⁿ, where n = length of the recurring block.
3. If there are non-repeating digits first, also multiply so that
   both lines have identical decimal parts.
4. Subtract and solve for x; simplify.
```

| Decimal | Lines to subtract | Fraction |
|---|---|---|
| 0.777... | 10x − x = 7 | 7/9 |
| 0.3636... | 100x − x = 36 | 36/99 = 4/11 |
| 0.8333... | 100x − 10x = 75 | 75/90 = 5/6 |
| 0.234234... | 1 000x − x = 234 | 234/999 = 26/111 |

Shortcut check for a pure block: the block over the same number of 9s.

## Arithmetic methods

**Add or subtract.** Line up the points; fill gaps with zeros. With mixed signs, subtract the sizes and keep the sign of the larger size: −4.6 + 1.85 = −(4.60 − 1.85) = −2.75.

**Multiply.** Ignore the points, multiply, then give the answer as many decimal places as the two numbers have together. 0.9 × 0.04: 9 × 4 = 36, three places, 0.036.

**Divide by a whole number.** Short division with the point kept in line: −6.72 ÷ 8 = −0.84.

**Divide by a decimal.** Multiply both by the power of 10 that makes the divisor whole: 0.63 ÷ 0.009 = 630 ÷ 9 = 70.

**Use a known fact.** From 36 × 47 = 1 692: count how many times each number has been divided or multiplied by 10, then apply the same to the answer.

## Estimating to check

Round each number to 1 significant figure before calculating exactly. The estimate tells you where the decimal point belongs.

| Calculation | Estimate | Exact |
|---|---|---|
| 2.7 × 1.8 | 3 × 2 = 6 | 4.86 |
| 0.9 × 0.04 | 1 × 0.04 = 0.04 | 0.036 |
| 6.72 ÷ 8 | 7 ÷ 8 ≈ 0.9 | 0.84 |
| 2.85 ÷ 0.15 | 3 ÷ 0.15 = 20 | 19 |

**Multiplying by a number between 0 and 1** makes the answer smaller; **dividing** by one makes it larger. If your answer moves the wrong way, the point is misplaced.

**Money and measures.** Give money to 2 decimal places (£4.50, not £4.5). Convert units before calculating: 450 g × 1.2 is 540 g, or 0.54 kg.

## Exam traps

- **Digits copied across:** 3/25 is 0.12, not 0.325.
- **Too few repeats written:** show enough digits to make the pattern clear, or use dot notation.
- **(Higher tier only) Wrong multiplier:** a two-digit block needs 100x, a three-digit block 1 000x.
- **(Higher tier only) Non-repeating start ignored:** 0.8333... is not 83/99.
- **Points not aligned** in addition or subtraction.
- **Decimal places counted in the answer** rather than the question when multiplying.
- **Only the divisor scaled** when dividing by a decimal.
- **Sign lost** when the negative number is the larger size.
- **Expecting a smaller answer** after dividing by a number less than 1: 0.63 ÷ 0.009 = 70, much larger than 0.63.

## Quick self-test

1. Write 0.36 as a fraction in its simplest form.
2. Write 13/20 as a decimal.
3. Use division to write 3/8 as a decimal.
4. Use division to write 7/12 as a decimal.
5. (Higher tier only) Write 0.777... as a fraction.
6. (Higher tier only) Write 0.3636... as a fraction in its simplest form.
7. (Higher tier only) Write 0.8333... as a fraction in its simplest form.
8. Work out −4.6 + 1.85.
9. Work out 0.9 × 0.04.
10. Work out 2.7 × 1.8.
11. Work out −6.72 ÷ 8.
12. Work out 0.63 ÷ 0.009.

### Answers

1. 36/100 = **9/25**.
2. 65/100 = **0.65**.
3. 30 ÷ 8 = 3 r 6; 60 ÷ 8 = 7 r 4; 40 ÷ 8 = 5. **0.375**.
4. **0.58333...** (the 3 recurs).
5. 10x − x = 7, so **7/9**.
6. 100x − x = 36, so 36/99 = **4/11**.
7. 100x − 10x = 83.333... − 8.333... = 75, so 75/90 = **5/6**.
8. **−2.75**.
9. **0.036**.
10. 27 × 18 = 486, two decimal places: **4.86**.
11. **−0.84**.
12. 630 ÷ 9 = **70**.
