Condensed notes for sub-topic **8(a) Units** of Pearson Edexcel
International GCSE Physics (4PH1). Statement 8.1: use kilogram (kg),
metre (m), metre/second (m/s), metre/second² (m/s²), newton (N), second
(s) and newton/kilogram (N/kg). Core content. Worked examples are in the
[Units in astrophysics study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-units-astrophysics/).

## The seven units

| Unit | Quantity |
|---|---|
| kg | Mass |
| m | Distance, orbital radius |
| m/s | Speed: orbital speed, galaxy velocity, speed of light |
| m/s² | Acceleration |
| N | Force, including weight |
| s | Time, orbital period |
| N/kg | Gravitational field strength g |

## Mass, weight and g

- Mass (kg): same everywhere.
- Weight (N): gravitational force; changes with location.
- **W = m × g**: N = kg × N/kg.
- g differs on the Moon and other planets (statement 8.3). Typical values: Earth close to 10 N/kg; Moon about 1.6 N/kg. Use the value the question gives.
- **1 N/kg = 1 m/s²**. g on a planet = acceleration of a freely falling object there (no air resistance).

## Orbital speed units

```
v = 2πr / T
r in m, T in s → v in m/s
```

**Conversions**

```
1 km  = 1000 m
1 h   = 3600 s
1 day = 86 400 s
1 year ≈ 3.15 × 10⁷ s
```

- Orbital radius is measured from the **centre** of the body orbited: r = planet radius + height above the surface.
- Rearranged: T = 2πr / v and r = vT / 2π.

## Red-shift units

```
Δλ / λ₀ = v / c
```

- Both ratios are unitless.
- Δλ and λ₀ must be in the same unit as each other (nm is fine).
- With c = 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s, v comes out in m/s.
- Δλ = observed wavelength − reference wavelength.

## Unit checks

- v = 2πr / T → m ÷ s = m/s.
- W = m × g → kg × N/kg = N.
- T = 2πr / v → m ÷ (m/s) = s.
- If the unit that comes out is wrong, a conversion was missed.

## Standard form and estimates

- Earth's orbital radius ≈ 1.5 × 10¹¹ m; speed of light 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.
- Use the calculator's ×10ˣ key.
- Check: 2π ≈ 6; does the answer have a sensible power of ten?

## Quick worked example

*Illustrative figures.* A 900 kg rover on Mars, where g = 3.7 N/kg.

- Mass on Mars: 900 kg.
- Weight on Mars: 900 × 3.7 = 3330 N ≈ 3300 N (2 s.f.).
- If dropped from rest (no air), its speed increases by 3.7 m/s every second.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mass | Amount of matter in an object, in kg |
| Weight | Gravitational force on an object, in N |
| Gravitational field strength | Force per kilogram, in N/kg |
| Orbital radius | Distance from the centre of the orbited body to the orbiting object, in m |
| Time period | Time for one complete orbit, in s |
| Orbital speed | Speed of an orbiting object, in m/s |
| Reference wavelength | Wavelength of a spectral line from a stationary source |

## Exam traps

- Weight in kg, or mass in N.
- "Mass is smaller on the Moon": no, only weight is.
- Using km and hours in v = 2πr / T and calling the result m/s.
- Forgetting to add the planet's radius to a satellite's height.
- Using the observed wavelength as λ₀ in the red-shift ratio.
- Leaving answers unrounded: give 2 or 3 significant figures, matching the data.

## Self-test

1. State the units of mass, weight and gravitational field strength.
2. Show that 1 N/kg is the same as 1 m/s².
3. A 60 kg student stands on the Moon (g = 1.6 N/kg). Find her weight.
4. A lander weighs 3000 N on Earth (g = 10 N/kg). Find its mass.
5. Convert 3.6 × 10⁴ km into metres.
6. Convert 5.0 days into seconds.
7. A satellite orbits 600 km above a planet of radius 6400 km. What is r in metres?
8. Which units must r and T be in to give v in m/s?
9. In Δλ / λ₀ = v / c, what unit does the left-hand side have?
10. An object dropped near the surface of a planet accelerates at 8.9 m/s². What is g there, in N/kg?
11. A moon has r = 2.0 × 10⁸ m and T = 4.0 × 10⁵ s. Calculate v.
12. Why is a speed of 3.0 × 10⁸ km/s wrong for light?

## Answers

1. Mass: kg; weight: N; gravitational field strength: N/kg.
2. 1 N is the force that gives 1 kg an acceleration of 1 m/s², so 1 N/kg = 1 (kg m/s²)/kg = 1 m/s².
3. W = 60 × 1.6 = 96 N.
4. m = W / g = 3000 / 10 = 300 kg.
5. 3.6 × 10⁴ × 1000 = 3.6 × 10⁷ m.
6. 5.0 × 86 400 = 432 000 s = 4.32 × 10⁵ s.
7. 6400 + 600 = 7000 km = 7.0 × 10⁶ m.
8. r in metres and T in seconds.
9. None: it is a ratio of two wavelengths.
10. 8.9 N/kg.
11. v = 2π × 2.0 × 10⁸ / 4.0 × 10⁵ = 3.1 × 10³ m/s (about 3140 m/s).
12. The speed of light is 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s; 3.0 × 10⁸ km/s is 1000 times too large.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are any Topic 8 unit statements Physics-only?**
No. Statement 8.1 is core, although the units are also used in the Physics-only cosmology calculations.

**Why is N/kg used for g rather than m/s²?**
Both are correct and equivalent; the specification lists both units. N/kg fits weight calculations, and m/s² fits acceleration.
