These notes cover **Exercise 3, Note-making**, of 3248 Paper 1: "candidates read a longer text printed
in the question paper and make brief notes under a number of supplied headings". For the whole paper
see the [O Level Urdu Paper 1 study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-and-second-language/);
for the full method and a worked example see the
[Exercise 3 Note-making study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-urdu-language-3248-exercise-3-note-making/).

## Task facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Marks | 9 |
| Objectives | R1, R2, R3, R4 |
| Text | the longest on the paper; also the source for Exercise 4 |
| Task | brief notes under supplied headings |
| Language | questions and answers in Urdu |
| Dictionaries | not allowed |
| Paper | 1 hour 45 minutes, 50 marks, five compulsory exercises |

## Text types to expect

The syllabus's reading content lists blogs, brochures, emails, forms, imaginative writing, letters,
magazines, newspapers and web pages, on subjects of contemporary interest such as
conservation and environment, culture and history, science and technology, music and arts, and human
achievement. That list is not exhaustive.

## The objectives in one line each

- **R1**: find the information that belongs under each heading.
- **R2**: recognise ideas, opinions and attitudes, including a view the writer quotes.
- **R3**: connect details across paragraphs to the right heading.
- **R4**: supply what is implied when the heading needs it.

## A good note is

1. **brief** – three to eight words;
2. **single** – one point per line;
3. **specific** – names the actual thing, not a category;
4. **meaningful alone** – an examiner can understand it without the text;
5. **placed correctly** – it answers the heading it sits under.

## Urdu note style

Use noun phrases, often with a verbal noun or *میں کمی / میں اضافہ*:

| Full sentence in text | Note |
|---|---|
| بازار کا خرچ کم ہوا ہے | خرچ میں کمی |
| پڑوسیوں سے ملاقات ہو جاتی ہے | پڑوسیوں سے میل جول |
| کئی پودے تیز دھوپ میں جھلس گئے | دھوپ سے پودوں کا جھلسنا |
| پہلے سال صرف آسان پودے لگائیں | آسان پودوں سے آغاز |

Brackets for supporting examples are fine: *آسان پودے (پودینہ، ہری مرچ)*.

## Heading-mapping routine

1. Headings first.
2. Whole text once.
3. Second reading: margin letters (a, b, c) beside each relevant detail.
4. Remove overlaps: each point under one heading.
5. Match the number of points to the printed lines, if any.

## Link to Exercise 4

The summary (no more than 100 words, own words as far as possible) is on an aspect or aspects of
this text. Accurate notes give you most of its content; turn the noun phrases back into short,
linked sentences.
Read the Exercise 4 question before you finish Exercise 3: if it names the aspect you will summarise,
you can make sure the notes under that heading are complete and precisely worded.

## Mini example

Text (original): *میلے میں اس بار بچوں کے لیے الگ کھیل کا میدان بنایا گیا۔ کھانے کے اسٹال صاف ستھرے
تھے، مگر پارکنگ کی جگہ بہت کم تھی اور لوگ گھنٹوں گاڑیوں میں پھنسے رہے۔*

Heading: *میلے کی خوبیاں*

- Good: *بچوں کے لیے الگ کھیل کا میدان*
- Good: *صاف ستھرے کھانے کے اسٹال*
- Wrong heading: *پارکنگ کی کمی* (a drawback, not a strength)

## Timing

Paper 1 gives 1 hour 45 minutes for 50 marks, about two minutes a mark. For 9 marks, allow roughly
18 to 20 minutes including the reading of the longest text, knowing that the reading also serves
Exercise 4.

## Exam traps

- Copying sentences in full.
- Two or three points squeezed into one note.
- A category word as a note (*فوائد*، *مسائل*).
- The same point twice in different words.
- A correct point under the wrong heading.
- Ignoring points inside quotations from people in the text.
- Missing qualifiers that change meaning (*کچھ*، *شروع میں*، *اکثر*).
- Using knowledge from outside the text.

## Self-test

1. How many marks is Exercise 3 worth, and which objectives does it test?
2. Which later exercise uses the same text?
3. Must notes be in full sentences?
4. Turn into a note: *ماہرین کے مطابق چھت پر پودے عمارت کو ٹھنڈا رکھتے ہیں۔*
5. Why might *سستی اور صحت مند سبزیاں* earn only one mark?
6. Under which heading would a fear that the roof might be damaged belong: benefits, difficulties or
   advice?
7. What should you do if a point seems to fit two headings?
8. What does the number of printed bullet lines under a heading tell you?
9. Give two text types the syllabus lists for reading.
10. Why is *دھوپ* alone a weak note?
11. Which objective covers recognising connections between ideas across paragraphs?
12. Are dictionaries allowed?

**Answers**

1. 9 marks; R1, R2, R3 and R4.
2. Exercise 4, the summary.
3. No: the syllabus asks for brief notes.
4. *عمارت کا ٹھنڈا رہنا* (or *گرمی میں کمی*).
5. It combines two separate points (cheapness, healthiness) in one note.
6. Difficulties: a worry is a difficulty even if nothing has gone wrong yet.
7. Put it under the heading it answers more directly, and only once.
8. The number of points you are expected to give under that heading.
9. Any two of blogs, brochures, emails, forms, imaginative writing, letters, magazines, newspapers,
   web pages.
10. It does not say what the problem is; the note must show the difficulty (plants scorched by sun).
11. R3.
12. No.
