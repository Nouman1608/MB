These notes condense content item 1.3.2, Business revenues, costs and profits, from Topic 1.3 Putting a business idea into practice in the Pearson Edexcel Level 1/Level 2 GCSE (9-1) in Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022 (Theme 1, Paper 1). ## The specification in brief

- The concept and calculation of revenue, fixed and variable costs, total costs, profit and loss, interest, break even level of output and margin of safety.
- Interpretation of break even diagrams: the impact of changes in revenue and costs, break even level of output, margin of safety, profit and loss.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Revenue | Money from sales: price × quantity |
| Fixed costs | Costs that do not change with output in the short term |
| Variable costs | Costs that change directly with output |
| Total costs | Fixed costs + variable costs |
| Profit / loss | Revenue − total costs; negative means a loss |
| Interest | The cost of borrowing, the extra repaid on top of the loan |
| Contribution per unit | Price − variable cost per unit (the bottom of the break even formula) |
| Break even level of output | The output where revenue equals total costs |
| Margin of safety | Actual or budgeted sales − break even sales |

## Formulae, including rearranged forms

| You want | Use |
|---|---|
| Revenue | price × quantity |
| Total costs | TFC + TVC |
| Interest % | (total repayment − borrowed amount) ÷ borrowed amount × 100 |
| Total repayment from a rate | borrowed amount × (1 + rate ÷ 100) |
| Break even (units) | fixed costs ÷ (price − variable cost) |
| Break even (revenue) | break even units × price |
| Margin of safety | actual or budgeted sales − break even sales |
| Fixed costs from break even | break even units × (price − variable cost) |
| Profit check above break even | margin of safety (units) × contribution per unit |

The first, second, third, fifth, sixth and seventh rows are Appendix 3 formulae. The others are rearrangements.

## Worked reminder: Garnet Glassworks

Price £24, variable cost £9, fixed costs £4,500 a month, budget 420 items.

| Item | Working | Answer |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 24 × 420 | £10,080 |
| TVC | 9 × 420 | £3,780 |
| TC | 4,500 + 3,780 | £8,280 |
| Profit | 10,080 − 8,280 | £1,800 |
| Contribution | 24 − 9 | £15 |
| Break even | 4,500 ÷ 15 | 300 items |
| Break even revenue | 300 × 24 | £7,200 |
| Margin of safety | 420 − 300 | 120 items (£2,880) |
| Profit check | 120 × 15 | £1,800 |

## What each change does

| Change | Diagram | Break even | Margin of safety |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price up | Revenue line steeper | Falls | Widens (if sales hold) |
| Price down | Revenue line flatter | Rises | Narrows |
| Fixed costs up | Total costs line starts higher | Rises | Narrows |
| Variable cost up | Total costs line steeper | Rises | Narrows |
| Price and variable cost both up | Both lines steeper | Depends on contribution | Depends on contribution |

Garnet examples: fixed costs up to £5,400 → 5,400 ÷ 15 = 360 items. Variable cost up to £11 → 4,500 ÷ 13 = 346.15, so 347 items. Price up to £26 → 4,500 ÷ 17 = 264.71, so 265 items.

## Fixed or variable? Quick sort
- Fixed: rent, salaries, insurance, loan repayments, website fees.
- Variable: materials, packaging, piece-rate wages, delivery per order, card fees per sale.

## Exam traps

- Using price, not contribution, as the divisor.
- Rounding break even down.
- Mixing margin of safety in units with margin of safety in pounds.
- Dividing interest by the total repayment.
- Starting the total costs line at zero.
- Saying a price rise always lowers break even "so profit rises" without checking sales.

## Self-test

1. Define variable costs.
2. A business sells 750 items at £6. Calculate revenue.
3. Fixed costs are £2,200 and TVC is £1,900. Calculate total costs.
4. Revenue is £3,800 and total costs are £4,100. State the profit or loss.
5. A loan of £9,000 has an interest rate of 14%. Calculate the total repayment.
6. A loan of £6,000 is repaid with £6,900. Calculate the interest percentage.
7. Price £18, variable cost £10, fixed costs £2,400. Calculate break even output.
8. Using question 7, calculate break even revenue.
9. Using question 7, budgeted sales are 380. Calculate the margin of safety in units.
10. A product breaks even at 300 units with a contribution of £8. Calculate fixed costs.
11. What happens to the total costs line if variable cost per unit rises?
12. Why might a price rise not improve a business's position?

### Answers

1. Costs that change directly with the level of output, such as raw materials.
2. 750 × £6 = £4,500.
3. £2,200 + £1,900 = £4,100.
4. £3,800 − £4,100 = a loss of £300.
5. £9,000 × 1.14 = £10,260.
6. (£6,900 − £6,000) ÷ £6,000 × 100 = 15%.
7. £2,400 ÷ (£18 − £10) = 300 units.
8. 300 × £18 = £5,400.
9. 380 − 300 = 80 units.
10. 300 × £8 = £2,400.
11. It becomes steeper, so it crosses the revenue line at a higher output.
12. Customers may buy fewer units at the higher price, so actual sales could fall towards or below the new break even level.
