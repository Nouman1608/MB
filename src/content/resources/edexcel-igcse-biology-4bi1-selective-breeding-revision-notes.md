These revision notes cover sub-topic 5(b), **Selective breeding** (specification points 5.10 and 5.11), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. Both points can be examined on **either paper**. They apply to the June and November series examined under Issue 3.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [selective breeding study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-selective-breeding/) first. Then test yourself with the [selective breeding practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-selective-breeding-practice/). The course hub is [Edexcel IGCSE Biology](/boards/edexcel/igcse/biology/).

## Key definitions

- **Selective breeding (artificial selection):** humans choose which organisms breed together so that useful characteristics become more common.
- **Variation:** differences between individuals of the same species.
- **Characteristic:** a feature of an organism, for example milk yield or disease resistance.
- **Generation:** one stage of parents and offspring.
- **Inbreeding:** breeding between closely related individuals.
- **Genetic variation:** the range of different alleles in a population.

## The four steps (always in this order)

1. **Decide** the characteristic wanted.
2. **Select** parents that show it most strongly.
3. **Breed** them together.
4. **Select** the best offspring and breed again. **Repeat** for many generations.

## 5.10 Plants

| Aim | Example |
|---|---|
| Higher yield | Wheat or maize with more grain |
| Disease resistance | A variety resistant to a fungal disease |
| Shorter, stronger stems | Cereal that is not flattened by wind |
| Larger or sweeter fruit | Tomatoes, apples |
| Colour or scent | Garden flowers |
| Tolerance of poor conditions | Drought-resistant crops |

- Two varieties can be crossed to combine features, for example high yield with disease resistance.
- Good plants can be copied by cuttings to keep the feature.

## 5.11 Animals

| Aim | Example |
|---|---|
| High milk yield | Dairy cattle |
| Fast growth, lean meat | Beef cattle, pigs, chickens |
| More eggs | Hens |
| Good wool | Sheep |
| Disease resistance | Many farm animals |

- Males cannot be measured for milk yield, so evidence comes from their mother or daughters.

## Advantages and disadvantages

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| More yield or product | Slow, needs many generations |
| Better quality | Less genetic variation |
| Disease resistance | Disease can affect the whole population |
| Simple, no special equipment | Inbreeding can bring out harmful recessive alleles |
| | Selecting one feature can weaken another |

## Selective breeding vs natural selection

| | Natural selection | Selective breeding |
|---|---|---|
| Selected by | The environment | Humans |
| Favours | Survival and reproduction | Characteristics useful to humans |

## Two calculations to know

**Percentage increase** = (final − original) ÷ original × 100.
Example: yield rising from 5000 to 5960 litres gives 960 ÷ 5000 × 100 = 19.2%.

**Mean change per generation** = total change ÷ number of generations.
Example: 960 ÷ 3 = 320 litres per generation.

## Method in steps: describing selective breeding in a new organism

1. Name the characteristic you want.
2. Say that you choose the individuals that show it best.
3. Say that you breed those individuals together.
4. Say that you choose the best offspring and breed again.
5. State that the process is repeated over many generations.

## Answer frame: "Evaluate selective breeding"

Give a benefit (yield or quality), give a risk (loss of variation or disease), and then reach a judgement that depends on the circumstances.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Selective breeding vs genetic engineering:** choosing parents within a species vs moving genes directly.
- **Selective breeding vs natural selection:** human choice vs environmental pressure.
- **Variation vs inheritance:** differences between individuals vs passing genes to offspring.
- **Inbreeding vs cross-breeding:** closely related parents vs different varieties.

## Quick self-test

1. Define selective breeding.
2. Who decides which organisms breed?
3. Give two characteristics selected in crop plants.
4. Give two characteristics selected in farm animals.
5. Why are the best offspring bred again?
6. Why can a bull not be chosen for milk yield directly?
7. Give one disadvantage of selective breeding.
8. Why is a field of one wheat variety at risk of disease?
9. A hen flock laid a mean of 220 eggs per year and after selective breeding 264. Calculate the percentage increase.
10. After 4 generations a herd has gained 480 litres in mean milk yield. Calculate the mean gain per generation.

### Answers

1. Humans choose which organisms breed so that useful characteristics are passed on.
2. Humans.
3. Any two of: yield, disease resistance, shorter stems, larger fruit.
4. Any two of: milk yield, lean meat, wool quality, egg number, disease resistance.
5. So that each generation has more of the useful alleles.
6. A bull does not produce milk, so his mother or daughters' yield is used.
7. Any one of: slow, less variation, inbreeding problems.
8. The plants are genetically similar, so one disease can affect all of them.
9. (264 − 220) ÷ 220 × 100 = **20%**.
10. 480 ÷ 4 = **120 litres**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the repetition over many generations.
- Describing the organisms as "changing" to meet a need.
- Confusing selective breeding with genetic engineering.
- Giving a characteristic without saying that the best individuals are chosen as parents.
- Writing "less variation" without explaining why that is a problem.
- Using "the best" instead of naming the characteristic.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can selective breeding produce a new species?**
Not in the way it is used in farming. It changes the proportion of alleles for a characteristic within a species.

**Why is variation needed?**
Selective breeding can only choose between differences that already exist in the population.

**Is selective breeding used for pets?**
Yes. Dogs and other pets are bred for appearance and behaviour in the same way.
