**How to use this set.** In Paper 2, one or two of Questions 3–5 are set on this topic, each worth
14 marks: a knowledge part (a) worth 10 and an understanding part (b) worth 4. Section A checks
facts; Section B gives full exam-style questions.

## Section A — Short questions

**1.** List the **six** Articles of Faith. **[3]**

**2.** Name the books revealed to Musa, Dawud and Isa. **[3]**

**3.** Explain what each half of the shahada affirms. **[2]**

**4.** Describe **three** meanings of jihad. **[3]**

**5.** Name the five daily prayers in order, starting with the dawn prayer. **[2]**

**6.** Explain what a Muslim should do about a missed prayer, and how a traveller's prayers may be
changed. **[3]**

**7.** Give **three** events of the Last Day in the order they occur. **[3]**

## Section B — Exam-style questions

**8. (a)** Describe what Muslims believe about the prophets. **[10]**
**(b)** Why do Muslims believe that God needed to send prophets to humanity? **[4]**

**9. (a)** Describe Muslim belief in God's predestination and decree (al-qadr). **[10]**
**(b)** How can belief in al-qadr help a Muslim who is facing illness or failure? **[4]**

**10. (a)** Describe the preparations for salat and how a unit of prayer is performed. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain the importance of praying in congregation for the Muslim community. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Describe how Muslims observe the fast of Ramadan, including who is exempted. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain the significance of fasting for an individual Muslim. **[4]**

**12. (a)** Describe the main observances of hajj from the 8th to the 10th of Dhu al-Hijja. **[10]**
**(b)** Explain how hajj expresses the unity and equality of Muslims. **[4]**

## Answers

**How Section B is marked.** Levels of response: part (a) Level 4 = 8–10, Level 3 = 5–7, Level 2 =
3–4, Level 1 = 1–2; part (b) Level 2 = 3–4, Level 1 = 1–2. Each developed point below earns [2];
credit the best five in (a) (maximum 10) and the best two in (b) (maximum 4). Other accurate points
of the same quality earn the same credit.

**1.** God (Allah); angels; revealed books; prophets; the Last Day; God's predestination and decree.
All six [3], four or five [2], two or three [1].

**2.** Musa: the Tawrat [1]. Dawud: the Zabur [1]. Isa: the Injil [1].

**3.** "There is no god but God" affirms tawhid, that God alone is to be worshipped [1]; "Muhammad is
the Messenger of God" accepts his prophethood and the duty to follow his teaching and Sunnah [1].

**4.** Any three, [1] each: the inner, spiritual struggle against one's own wrong desires (the greater
jihad); striving with the mind and tongue, by learning, teaching and speaking the truth; striving
with wealth for good causes; physical defence of the community under proper authority and strict
limits. Maximum 3.

**5.** Fajr, Zuhr, Asr, Maghrib, Isha: all five in order [2]; three or four in order [1].

**6.** A missed prayer must be made up (qada) as soon as possible [1]. A traveller may shorten the
four-unit prayers (Zuhr, Asr, Isha) to two units [1] and may combine Zuhr with Asr and Maghrib with
Isha [1].

**7.** Any three in the correct order, [1] each: the trumpet is blown by Israfil; the world ends; the
dead are resurrected; people are judged and given their record; deeds are weighed; people cross the
sirat; they enter paradise or hell. Maximum 3.

**8. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- Prophets are human beings chosen by God to receive and deliver His message [2].
- God sent a messenger to every people (Sura 16.36); the Qur'an names twenty-five, from Adam to
  Muhammad (pbuh) [2].
- A rasul is given a revealed book or law; every rasul is a nabi, but not every nabi is a rasul [2].
- All prophets taught the same core message of tawhid and obedience to God [2].
- They were of excellent character, truthful and trustworthy, and protected from major sin [2].
- Some were given miracles by God's permission, such as Musa's staff and Isa's healing of the sick [2].
- Muslims must believe in all of them without distinction (Sura 2.285); Muhammad (pbuh) is the last,
  the Seal of the Prophets (Sura 33.40) [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- People cannot know by reason alone how God wants to be worshipped, so they need revealed
  guidance [2].
- Prophets show the message in practice, giving a human example people can follow (Sura 33.21) [2].
- They remind people when they forget or corrupt the message, so no one can say they were not
  warned [2].

**9. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- Al-qadr is one of the Articles of Faith, named in the Hadith of Jibril, including its good and its
  bad [2].
- God knows everything that has happened and will happen, before it happens [2].
- Everything is recorded in the Preserved Tablet (al-Lawh al-Mahfuz) (Sura 57.22) [2].
- Nothing happens except by God's will; He has created all things in due measure (Sura 54.49) [2].
- Humans are given free will and are responsible for their choices, so they will be judged on them [2].
- Effort is still required: the Prophet (pbuh) told a man to tie his camel and then trust in God [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- Knowing that hardship is part of God's plan gives patience and stops despair [2].
- A Muslim keeps trying, such as seeking treatment or studying again, because effort is required as
  well as trust (tawakkul) [2].
- Hardship borne patiently brings reward and forgiveness, so it can be faced with hope [2].

**10. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- The worshipper makes intention (niyya) and must be in a state of purity: wudu, or ghusl after major
  impurity, or tayammum with clean earth if there is no water [2].
- Wudu: washing the hands, rinsing mouth and nose, washing the face and arms to the elbows, wiping
  the head and ears, and washing the feet to the ankles [2].
- Body, clothes and place must be clean, the body properly covered, and the worshipper faces the
  qibla at the correct time [2].
- The prayer begins with "Allahu akbar"; standing, the worshipper recites al-Fatiha and another
  passage [2].
- Bowing (ruku'), praising God; then standing upright again [2].
- Two prostrations (sujud), with a short sitting between them, praising God as "the Most High" [2].
- After every two units, and at the end, the worshipper sits for the tashahhud, then ends with the
  greeting of peace to the right and left [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- Rich and poor stand side by side in rows, which teaches equality and humility [2].
- Meeting at the mosque five times a day, and at Jumu'a, lets Muslims know and help one another [2].
- Following one imam builds discipline and unity, and congregational prayer carries greater reward [2].

**11. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- Fasting in Ramadan, the ninth month, is obligatory (Sura 2.183); it begins with the sighting of the
  new moon [2].
- Muslims make the intention and eat suhur before dawn [2].
- From dawn to sunset they avoid food, drink and marital relations, and also lying, quarrelling and
  bad speech [2].
- They break the fast at sunset with iftar, often with dates and water as the Prophet (pbuh) did [2].
- Nights include extra prayers (tarawih) and recitation of the Qur'an; many seek Laylat al-Qadr in
  the last ten nights [2].
- Exempted: children, the sick, travellers, the elderly, pregnant or breastfeeding women, and women
  during menstruation [2].
- Missed fasts are made up later; those permanently unable feed a poor person for each day (fidya) [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- It builds self-control (taqwa), as the Qur'an says fasting was prescribed "that you may learn
  self-restraint" (Sura 2.183) [2].
- Feeling hunger builds empathy with the poor and leads to generosity [2].
- It brings forgiveness of past sins for those who fast with faith and hope of reward [2].

**12. (a)** Credit the best five, [2] each:
- Pilgrims enter ihram at the miqat, men in two white unsewn cloths, and recite the talbiya,
  "Here I am, O God, here I am" [2].
- On arrival they perform tawaf, seven circuits of the Ka'ba, and sa'y, seven times between Safa and
  Marwa, recalling Hajar's search for water [2].
- On the 8th they go to Mina [2].
- On the 9th they stand at Arafat from midday to sunset in prayer; without this the hajj is not
  valid [2].
- That night they stay at Muzdalifa and collect pebbles [2].
- On the 10th they stone the largest pillar at Mina, sacrifice an animal, and shave or cut their
  hair [2].
- They return to Makka for the tawaf al-ifada [2].
**(b)** Credit the best two, [2] each:
- All pilgrims wear the same simple ihram, so wealth and status disappear [2].
- Muslims of every nation and language perform the same rites at the same time [2].
- Standing together at Arafat reminds them that all will stand equal before God on the Day of
  Judgement [2].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing the Articles or Pillars without explanation in part (a).
- Leaving out the purity requirements when describing salat.
- Confusing the order of the hajj rites, especially Arafat and Muzdalifa.
- In part (b), describing the practice again instead of explaining its significance.
