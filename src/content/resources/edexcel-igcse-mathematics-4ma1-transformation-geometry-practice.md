## Section A

**1.** Translate the point (4, −1) by the vector (−6, 3). Write down the coordinates of the image. **[1]**

**2.** A translation maps the point (2, 5) onto the point (−1, 9).

**(a)** Write down the column vector of the translation. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the column vector of the translation that maps (−1, 9) back onto (2, 5). **[1]**

**3.** Triangle A has vertices (1, 2), (4, 2) and (4, 3). Reflect triangle A in the line x = 5. Write down the coordinates of the vertices of the image. **[2]**

**4.** Reflect the point (3, −2) in the line y = x. Write down the image. **[1]**

**5.** **(a)** Rotate the point (2, 5) through 90° clockwise about the origin. Write down the image. **[1]**

**(b)** Rotate the point (2, 5) through 180° about the point (3, 1). Write down the image. **[1]**

**6.** Name the transformations in 4MA1 that always produce an image congruent to the object, and give the property they all preserve. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Triangle B has vertices (2, 1), (4, 1) and (2, 4). Rotate triangle B through −90° about the point (1, 2). Write down the coordinates of the vertices of the image. **[3]**

**8.** Triangle C has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (1, 2). Triangle D has vertices (5, 1), (5, 3) and (4, 1). Describe fully the single transformation that maps triangle C onto triangle D. **[3]**

**9.** Triangle E has vertices (2, 3), (3, 3) and (2, 5).

**(a)** Enlarge triangle E by scale factor 3, centre (1, 2). Write down the coordinates of the image. **[3]**

**(b)** The side of E from (2, 3) to (2, 5) has length 2. Write down the length of the matching side of the image. **[1]**

**10.** Shape P has vertices (5, 7), (9, 7) and (5, 9). Shape Q has vertices (2, 3), (4, 3) and (2, 4). Describe fully the single transformation that maps P onto Q. **[3]**

**11.** Triangle T has vertices (1, 1), (3, 1) and (1, 2).

**(a)** Reflect T in the line x = 0. Label the image U and write down its vertices. **[1]**

**(b)** Reflect U in the line x = 3. Label the image V and write down its vertices. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe fully the single transformation that maps T onto V. **[2]**

**12.** **(a)** A reflection maps (−1, 7) onto (−1, 1). Write down the equation of the mirror line. **[1]**

**(b)** A reflection maps (1, 4) onto (4, 1) and (2, 6) onto (6, 2). Write down the equation of the mirror line. **[1]**

**(c)** Reflect the point (−3, 5) in the mirror line from part (b). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** (4 − 6, −1 + 3) = **(−2, 2)** [1].

**2. (a)** **(−3, 4)** [1].

**(b)** **(3, −4)** [1].

**3.** (x, y) → (10 − x, y): **(9, 2), (6, 2), (6, 3)**. All three correct [2] (1 mark for two correct).

**4.** **(−2, 3)** [1].

**5. (a)** 90° clockwise about the origin sends (x, y) to (y, −x): **(5, −2)** [1].

**(b)** (2 × 3 − 2, 2 × 1 − 5) = **(4, −3)** [1].

**6.** Rotations, reflections and translations [1]; they preserve length and angle [1].

**7.** Subtract the centre: (1, −1), (3, −1), (1, 2) [1]. Turn −90°, (x, y) → (y, −x): (−1, −1), (−1, −3), (2, −1) [1]. Add the centre back: **(0, 1), (0, −1), (3, 1)** [1].

**8.** Rotation [1], 90° anticlockwise (+90°) [1], about centre **(3, 3)** [1]. (The perpendicular bisectors x = 3 and y = −x + 6 of two object–image pairs meet at (3, 3).)

**9. (a)** P − C: (1, 1), (2, 1), (1, 3) [1]; multiplied by 3: (3, 3), (6, 3), (3, 9) [1]; image **(4, 5), (7, 5), (4, 11)** [1].

**(b)** 2 × 3 = **6** [1].

**10.** Enlargement [1], scale factor **½** [1], centre **(−1, −1)** [1].

**11. (a)** U: **(−1, 1), (−3, 1), (−1, 2)** [1].

**(b)** V: **(7, 1), (9, 1), (7, 2)** [1].

**(c)** Translation [1] by the vector **(6, 0)** [1].

**12. (a)** **y = 4** [1].

**(b)** **y = x** (accept y − x = 0) [1].

**(c)** **(5, −3)** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2(a): taking object minus image, giving (3, −4).
- Question 5(a): using the anticlockwise rule (x, y) → (−y, x).
- Question 7: rotating about the origin and forgetting to subtract and add back the centre.
- Question 8: giving the angle without a direction, or the centre as "the middle".
- Question 10: describing it as a "reduction" or leaving out the centre.
- Question 11(c): writing "two reflections" when the question asks for a single transformation.

## Frequently asked questions

**How do you find the centre of a rotation from a diagram?**
Join a point to its image and construct the perpendicular bisector; repeat for a second pair. The two bisectors cross at the centre. Tracing paper is a quick check.

**What is the single transformation equal to two reflections in parallel lines?**
A translation, perpendicular to the lines, by twice the distance between them. Reflecting in x = 0 and then x = 3 gives a translation by (6, 0).
