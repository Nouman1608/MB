Condensed notes for **1.2 Business structure** in Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609 (2026–2028). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [Business structure study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-business-structure-cambridge-alevel-business/); to test yourself, use the [Business structure practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-business-structure-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/). All businesses and figures are invented; sums are in dollars.

## What 1.2 asks you to handle

- **1.2.1**: the four economic sectors; the public and private sectors; reasons for and consequences of changes in their relative importance.
- **1.2.2**: features and appropriateness of eight forms of ownership; unlimited and limited liability; changing from one form to another.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary sector | Extracting or growing natural resources: farming, fishing, mining |
| Secondary sector | Turning materials into goods: manufacturing, construction |
| Tertiary sector | Providing services: retail, transport, banking, tourism |
| Quaternary sector | Knowledge-based activity: research, IT, data analysis |
| Public sector | Organisations owned and controlled by the state |
| Private sector | Businesses owned by individuals or shareholders |
| Unlimited liability | Owners are personally responsible for all business debts |
| Limited liability | Owners' loss is limited to the amount invested in shares |
| Separate legal identity | A company can own assets, sue and be sued in its own name |
| Franchise | A franchisee uses a franchisor's brand and system in return for fees |
| Co-operative | A business owned and run by its members for their shared benefit |
| Joint venture | Two or more businesses jointly run a project or new business |
| Social enterprise | A business that trades to meet social or environmental aims, reinvesting most profit |
| Personal guarantee | A promise by a director to repay a company loan personally if the company cannot |

## Sector change: reasons vs consequences

| Reasons | Consequences |
|---|---|
| Rising incomes shift spending to services | Growth opportunities for service firms |
| Mechanisation cuts jobs in farming and factories | Structural unemployment in declining sectors |
| Manufacturing moves to lower-cost countries | Skills shortages in growing sectors; retraining needed |
| Digital technology creates knowledge work | Regional decline where old industries were concentrated |
| Privatisation or nationalisation | Public sector shrinks or grows; objectives change |

**Remember:** a falling **share** need not mean a falling **total**; output share and employment share differ.

## Eight forms at a glance

| Form | Liability | Finance | Control | Best suited to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | Unlimited | Owner's savings, small loans | Full | Low-risk, small-scale work |
| Partnership | Unlimited (usually) | Partners' capital | Shared | Professional services pooling skills |
| Private limited company | Limited | Shares sold privately, loans | Kept by family or founders | Growing firms needing protection |
| Public limited company | Limited | Shares sold to the public | Spread among many shareholders | Large-scale expansion |
| Franchise | Depends on the franchisee's own form | Franchisee's capital; fees to franchisor | Limited by franchisor's rules | Entering a market with a proven brand |
| Co-operative | Often limited, depending on how it is registered | Members' contributions | Members, often one vote each | Member benefit rather than profit |
| Joint venture | Depends on the structure | Shared by partners | Shared | A specific project needing combined resources |
| Social enterprise | Depends on the legal form | Trading income, grants | Varies | A social or environmental mission |

## Appropriateness: five questions

Risk → finance needed → control wanted → aims → expertise. Use them to justify a recommendation.

## Liability: two exam-ready points

- Limited liability makes it easier to attract investors and protects personal assets from trade creditors.
- In small companies, **personal guarantees** on bank loans mean directors may still lose personal assets.

**Worked reminder.** A director invests $8 000 in shares and guarantees a $30 000 loan. The company fails; its assets of $12 000 go to the bank. She must pay the remaining $18 000, so her total loss is $8 000 + $18 000 = **$26 000**.

## Changing form

| Change | Main gain | Main cost |
|---|---|---|
| To partnership | Capital and skills | Shared control and liability |
| To private limited | Limited liability; separate identity | Filing accounts; less privacy |
| To public limited | Large share capital | Loss of control; takeover risk |
| Back to private | Regained control | Cost of buying back shares |

**Dilution method:** founders' share = founders' shares ÷ total shares after the issue × 100. Below 50% means loss of majority control.

**Worked reminder.** Founders hold 600 000 shares and 900 000 new shares are issued: 600 000 ÷ 1 500 000 × 100 = **40%**.

## Exam traps

- Public sector ≠ public limited company.
- Limited liability does not stop the **business** losing money.
- Recommending a form without using the case evidence.
- Mixing up reasons and consequences of sector change.
- Forgetting that franchise fees cut profit even when revenue is higher.

## Self-test

1. Classify a software research laboratory by sector.
2. Give two reasons for growth in the tertiary sector.
3. Give one consequence of a declining secondary sector for a manufacturing business.
4. Why is a plc in the private sector?
5. Define limited liability.
6. Why might a sole trader become a private limited company?
7. State one disadvantage of converting to a public limited company.
8. How does a co-operative differ from a private limited company in its aims?
9. Why might a franchisee earn little more than an independent business despite higher revenue?
10. Founders own 1 200 000 shares and the company issues 800 000 more to the public. What percentage do the founders now own?
11. Why might a bank ask for a personal guarantee?
12. Give one reason a joint venture might be chosen instead of a merger.

### Answers

1. Quaternary (knowledge-based).
2. Rising incomes increase demand for services; technology creates new service industries (or manufacturing moves abroad).
3. Domestic suppliers may close, so it relies more on imported components (or skilled workers become scarce).
4. It is owned by private shareholders, not the state.
5. Owners' losses are limited to the amount they invested in shares.
6. To gain limited liability and raise more capital from private shareholders.
7. Loss of control, takeover risk, or short-term pressure from investors.
8. A co-operative is run for the benefit of its members; a private limited company usually aims to make profit for shareholders.
9. Royalties and the initial fee absorb much of the extra revenue.
10. 1 200 000 ÷ 2 000 000 × 100 = **60%**.
11. A new company has few assets and limited liability, so the bank wants a person who will repay if the company cannot.
12. The partners share costs and risks of one project while keeping their own businesses separate.
