Condensed recall for section 4.3.2 of Unit 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [Global markets and business expansion study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-markets-and-business-expansion/), then try the [practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-business-ybs11-global-markets-and-business-expansion-practice/). Figures are invented.

## Formula sheet

```
Unit cost                = labour cost per unit + materials + transport (and any tariff)
Labour cost per unit     = hourly wage x hours per unit
Return on investment     = annual profit / cost of investment x 100
Future income            = income now x (1 + growth rate) ^ years
Home-currency value      = foreign amount / exchange rate (foreign units per $1)
```

## Push and pull factors

| Push factors (from home) | Pull factors (to overseas) |
|---|---|
| Saturated market: slow growth, most customers already own the product | Increased sales and profitability |
| Competition: price and margin pressure | Spreading risk across markets |
| | Economies of scale from larger output |

- **Off-shoring:** the firm moves its own activity abroad, usually for lower cost. Keeps control; needs investment.
- **Outsourcing:** an outside firm does the activity. Flexible and cheaper to start; less control of quality.
- **Extending the product life cycle:** a product in decline at home may be growing in a new market.
- Cost saving must allow for productivity, transport, tariffs, quality and exchange rates, not just wages.

## Assessing a country as a market

| Factor | Question |
|---|---|
| Disposable income (level and growth) | Can customers afford it now and later? |
| Ease of doing business | How hard is it to register, hire and enforce contracts? |
| Infrastructure | Can goods be delivered and sold reliably? |
| Political stability | Is there a risk of sudden policy change or conflict? |
| Exchange rates | Stable or volatile? |
| Porter's five forces | Rivalry, new entrants, substitutes, suppliers, buyers |

- Strong local rivals, powerful retail buyers and low entry barriers reduce profit potential.
- Fast income growth can outweigh a lower starting income, if the country is stable.

## Assessing a country as a production location

| Factor | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Costs of production | Wages, energy, land, taxes |
| Skills and availability of labour | Education, training, labour pool |
| Infrastructure | Ports, roads, power, broadband |
| Trade bloc membership | Tariff-free access to neighbouring markets |
| Government incentives | Grants, tax breaks |
| Ease of doing business | Regulation, legal protection |
| Political stability | Safety of assets |
| Natural resources | Materials and energy |
| Return on investment | Profit against cost |

Judge the whole package. The cheapest location can fail on skills, infrastructure or stability.

## Global mergers, takeovers and joint ventures

| Reason | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Spreading risk, economies of scale | Several markets; bigger volumes |
| Entering new markets or trading blocs | Avoids barriers; quick access |
| Acquiring brands and patents | Instant reputation or technology |
| Securing resources and supplies | Control of inputs |
| Maintaining global competitiveness | Keep up with rivals |
| Reducing competition | Fewer rivals |
| Local knowledge | Customers, law and culture |
| Government or legal requirement | Local partner may be compulsory |
| Supply chains and distribution | Ready-made networks |
| Sharing costs and risks | Joint ventures spread both |

- **Joint venture:** a separate business set up and owned by two or more partners.
- **Risks:** culture clash, disputes between partners, overpaying, loss of control.

## Uncertainty in global expansion

| Issue | Effect | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Home currency depreciates | Overseas profits worth more at home; imported inputs dearer; exports cheaper | Sell and produce in the same currency area; forward contracts |
| Home currency appreciates | Overseas profits worth less; exports dearer; imports cheaper | Cut costs; focus on quality; source abroad |
| Skill shortages | Higher wages; delays; weaker quality; lost competitiveness | Training, migration, automation, relocating |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Push factor | Reason to leave the home market |
| Pull factor | Attraction of an overseas market |
| Off-shoring | Moving the firm's own activity to another country |
| Outsourcing | Paying another firm to carry out an activity |
| Joint venture | Business set up and owned by two or more partners |
| Return on investment | Annual profit as a percentage of the cost of the investment |
| Disposable income | Income after tax and essential payments |

## Exam traps

- Off-shoring and outsourcing are different: one moves your own activity, the other pays someone else.
- Low wages do not guarantee low unit costs: productivity, transport and tariffs matter.
- A weaker home currency helps exporters but hurts importers.
- A joint venture is a new shared business, not a takeover.
- Apply the market and location factors to the country in the source; do not just list them.
- For **assess**, state which factor matters most for this firm and why, and what would change your judgement.

## Answer frames

- **Location decision:** compare two or three factors with figures, choose the best overall and say what the choice depends on.
- **Market entry:** compare exporting, a joint venture and FDI on cost, speed, risk and control.
- **Exchange rate:** state the direction of the currency movement, convert a figure, and say who gains or loses.
- **Skill shortage:** name the cost or delay it causes, and the response with its limit.

## Self-test

1. Home: wage $10, 2 hours per unit, materials $25, transport $3. Calculate unit cost.
2. Overseas: wage $5, 3 hours per unit, materials $25, transport $9. Calculate unit cost and say which location is cheaper.
3. A factory costs $5m and earns $0.6m profit a year. Calculate ROI.
4. A $0.5m grant reduces the cost of the factory. Calculate the new ROI.
5. Disposable income of $5,000 grows by 4% a year. Calculate income after three years.
6. A subsidiary earns 10 million local currency units. Convert to dollars at 20 units to $1 and at 25 units to $1, and state the change.
7. Give one push factor.
8. Give one pull factor.
9. Distinguish off-shoring from outsourcing.
10. Give one risk of a joint venture.

### Answers

1. 10 x 2 = 20; 20 + 25 + 3 = **$48**.
2. 5 x 3 = 15; 15 + 25 + 9 = **$49**. The home location is cheaper by $1 per unit.
3. 0.6 / 5 x 100 = **12%**.
4. 0.6 / 4.5 x 100 = **13.3%**.
5. 5,000 x 1.04^3 = **$5,624**.
6. 10m / 20 = **$500,000**; 10m / 25 = **$400,000**; the change is **$100,000 less** (the dollar appreciated).
7. Any one: saturated home market; strong competition.
8. Any one: higher sales and profit; risk spreading; economies of scale.
9. Off-shoring moves the firm's own activity abroad; outsourcing pays another firm to do it.
10. Any one: culture clash; disagreements between partners; loss of control; sharing profits.
