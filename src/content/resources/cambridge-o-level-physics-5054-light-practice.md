## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the *normal* at a surface, and define the *angle of refraction*. **[2]**

**2.** A ray of light strikes a plane mirror at 32° to the mirror surface.

**(a)** State the angle of incidence. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle between the incident ray and the reflected ray. **[1]**

**3.** State **three** characteristics of the image formed by a plane mirror. **[3]**

**4.** A ray of light passes from air into water at an angle of incidence of 30°. The refractive index of water is 1.33. Calculate the angle of refraction. **[2]**

**5.** List the seven colours of the visible spectrum in order of increasing wavelength, and state which colour is refracted most by a glass prism. **[2]**

**6.** Explain the difference between a real image and a virtual image. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A student uses a ray box and a semicircular glass block to show total internal reflection.

**(a)** Describe how the student carries out the experiment and what is observed as the angle of incidence at the flat face is increased. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the ray is aimed at the centre of the flat face. **[1]**

**8.** A glass prism has a refractive index of 1.52.

**(a)** Calculate the critical angle for this glass. **[2]**
**(b)** Inside the prism, a ray meets a glass–air face at an angle of incidence of 45°. State and explain what happens to the ray. **[2]**
**(c)** Another ray meets a glass–air face from inside at 30°. Calculate the angle the emerging ray makes with the normal in air. **[2]**

**9.** An object 2.0 cm tall stands upright on the principal axis, 15 cm from a thin converging lens of focal length 5.0 cm.

**(a)** Describe how you would draw a ray diagram to locate the image, naming **two** rays and stating what each does at the lens. **[3]**
**(b)** The image forms 7.5 cm from the lens and is 1.0 cm tall. Calculate the linear magnification. **[2]**
**(c)** State **two** characteristics of this image. **[2]**

**10.** Optical fibres carry telephone and internet signals.

**(a)** Explain how light travels along a fibre even when the fibre is curved. **[3]**
**(b)** State **two** advantages of optical fibres over copper cables in telecommunications. **[2]**

**11.** A student cannot see distant objects clearly but can read a book held close.

**(a)** Name this eye defect and state where light from a distant object is focused in the student's eye. **[2]**
**(b)** Name the type of lens that corrects the defect and explain how it works. **[2]**
**(c)** The student's grandfather is long-sighted. State where light from a near object is focused in his eye and the type of correcting lens. **[2]**

**12.** A narrow beam of white light passes through a glass prism onto a white screen.

**(a)** Describe what is seen on the screen. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why violet light is deviated more than red light. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the frequency of red light changes as it enters the glass. **[1]**

**13.** A converging lens is used as a magnifying glass to view a stamp 1.2 cm tall. The image seen is 4.8 cm tall.

**(a)** Calculate the linear magnification. **[1]**
**(b)** State where the stamp must be placed relative to the lens. **[1]**
**(c)** State **two** characteristics of the image. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The normal is a line drawn **at right angles (90°) to the surface** at the point where the ray strikes it [1]. The angle of refraction is the angle **between the refracted ray and the normal** [1].

**2. (a)** i = 90° − 32° = **58°** [1].
**(b)** r = 58°, so the angle between the rays = 58° + 58° = **116°** [1].

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: **virtual** (cannot be formed on a screen); **upright**; **same size as the object**; **same distance behind the mirror as the object is in front**.

**4.** sin r = sin i ÷ n = sin 30° ÷ 1.33 = 0.500 ÷ 1.33 = 0.376 [1]; r = **22°** (22.1°) [1].

**5.** **Violet, indigo, blue, green, yellow, orange, red** [1]. **Violet** is refracted most [1].

**6.** A real image is formed where **rays of light actually meet (converge)** and can be **caught on a screen** [1]. A virtual image is where **diverging rays only appear to come from**; it cannot be caught on a screen [1].

**7. (a)** Shine a ray from the ray box **through the curved face towards the centre of the flat face** [1]. Gradually **increase the angle of incidence** at the flat face, measured from the normal with a protractor [1]. At first there is a **refracted ray in air bending away from the normal and a weak reflected ray** [1]. At the **critical angle the refracted ray travels along the flat face**; above it, the refracted ray disappears and **all the light is reflected inside the block** (total internal reflection) [1].
**(b)** The ray then **crosses the curved face along a normal (radius)**, so it is **not refracted on entry** and the only refraction studied is at the flat face [1].

**8. (a)** sin c = 1 ÷ n = 1 ÷ 1.52 = 0.658 [1]; c = **41°** (41.1°) [1].
**(b)** 45° is **greater than the critical angle** [1], so the ray is **totally internally reflected**, leaving the face at 45° to the normal inside the glass [1].
**(c)** sin (angle in air) = n × sin (angle in glass) = 1.52 × sin 30° = 0.760 [1]; angle = **49°** (49.5°) [1].

**9. (a)** A ray from the **top of the object parallel to the principal axis, refracted through the principal focus** on the far side [1]. A ray from the top of the object **through the centre of the lens, passing straight through undeviated** [1]. The **top of the image is where the two refracted rays cross** (accept the ray through the near focus emerging parallel to the axis as one of the two rays) [1].
**(b)** Magnification = image length ÷ object length [1] = 1.0 ÷ 2.0 = **0.50** [1].
**(c)** Any two, 1 mark each: **real**; **inverted**; **diminished** (smaller than the object); formed on the opposite side of the lens from the object.

**10. (a)** Light enters the fibre and **meets the wall at an angle of incidence greater than the critical angle** [1], so it is **totally internally reflected** [1]. This **repeats at each reflection along the fibre**, including round gentle bends, so the light emerges at the far end [1].
**(b)** Any two, 1 mark each: **carries much more information per second**; **not affected by electrical interference**; **thinner and lighter** than copper cables.

**11. (a)** **Short sight** [1]; light from a distant object is focused **in front of the retina** [1].
**(b)** A **diverging lens** [1]; it **spreads the rays out slightly before they enter the eye**, so the eye focuses them further back, **on the retina** [1].
**(c)** Light from a near object is focused **behind the retina** [1]; corrected with a **converging lens** [1].

**12. (a)** A **spectrum of colours** (a band of colours) [1], from **red (least deviated) to violet (most deviated)** in the order red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, violet [1].
**(b)** The different colours **travel at different speeds in glass** [1]; **violet is slowed more than red**, so it is **refracted (bent) more** at each face [1].
**(c)** **No** — the frequency is unchanged; only the speed and wavelength change [1].

**13. (a)** Magnification = 4.8 ÷ 1.2 = **4.0** [1].
**(b)** **Closer to the lens than the principal focus** (between the lens and F) [1].
**(c)** Any two, 1 mark each: **virtual**; **upright**; **magnified**; on the same side of the lens as the stamp.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the angle to the surface instead of to the normal in mirror and refraction questions.
- Dividing the wrong way in n = sin i / sin r and quoting a refractive index below 1.
- Describing total internal reflection without stating both conditions — light inside the denser medium **and** i greater than c.
- In ray diagrams, missing arrows, drawing virtual extensions as solid lines, or forgetting to mark F on both sides of the lens.
- Swapping the corrections: short sight needs a **diverging** lens and long sight a **converging** lens.
- Giving magnification a unit, or using different units for image and object lengths.
