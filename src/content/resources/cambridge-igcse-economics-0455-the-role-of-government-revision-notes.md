## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Government | The body that makes and enforces rules for a society and raises and spends money on its behalf |
| Local government | Councils serving a city, town or district |
| National government | The government of the whole country |
| International cooperation | Governments acting together through agreements or organisations |
| Mixed economy | Resources allocated by both markets and government |
| Public sector | Organisations owned or run by government |
| Redistribution | Moving income from richer to poorer households through taxes and benefits |

## Why governments act: market gaps

- Public goods → not supplied by markets.
- Merit goods → under-consumed; demerit goods → over-consumed.
- External costs and benefits → ignored by markets.
- Monopoly power → high prices, less choice.
- Inequality → some cannot afford necessities.
- Unemployment, inflation, slow growth → need management.

## Six roles

| Role | One-line summary | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Provider | Supplies or pays for goods and services | Defence, schools, roads |
| Regulator | Sets and enforces rules | Safety laws, pollution limits |
| Redistributor | Shifts income towards the poor | Progressive taxes, benefits |
| Manager of the economy | Pursues macroeconomic aims | Fiscal, monetary, supply-side policy |
| Employer and producer | Hires workers; may own firms | Teachers, nurses, state rail |
| International partner | Works with other countries | Trade deals, aid, shared environmental rules |

## The three levels

| Level | Strengths | Typical roles |
|---|---|---|
| Local | Knows local needs; residents can see results | Waste collection, local roads, parks, planning permission, licensing |
| National | Controls national taxes, laws and policy | Defence, courts, national economic policy, welfare, health and education funding |
| International | Can tackle cross-border problems | Trade agreements, aid, pollution and fishing agreements, disease control |

## Choosing the level: three questions

1. How far does the problem spread?
2. Which level has the powers and funds?
3. Which level knows most about the problem?

## Numbers to practise

```
Share of spending = spending on item / total spending x 100
Government spending as % of GDP = government spending / GDP x 100
```

Worked reminder: a council spends 36 million, of which 9 million on roads.

```
Roads share = 9 / 36 x 100 = 25%
```

Worked reminder: local taxes raise 22 million towards spending of 40 million.

```
National grant needed = 40 - 22 = 18 million, or 45% of spending
```

Worked reminder: government spending 96 billion, GDP 320 billion: 96 / 320 x 100 = 30% of GDP.

## Cross-border chain to learn

Firm in Country A pollutes a shared river → costs fall on Countries B and C → B and C cannot tax or regulate A's firm → action by B and C alone does not stop the pollution → an international agreement with shared limits is needed.

## Exam traps

- **Name the level** the question asks about; local and national roles differ.
- **Give the economic reason**, not just the service: "street lighting is a public good".
- **International does not mean only trade.** Include aid, shared environmental problems and cooperation.
- **Local depends on national.** Councils often rely on national grants and laws.
- **Provider versus producer.** The government can pay for a service that private firms deliver.
- **Avoid unsure real statistics.** Explain with general or invented examples.

## Evaluation prompts

- **Local versus national:** local knowledge and accountability against national funding power and equal standards across regions.
- **Government versus market:** government provision fills gaps the market leaves, but it is paid for by taxes, which have an opportunity cost: money spent on one service cannot be spent on another.
- **International action:** agreements solve shared problems only if every country keeps to them and monitoring is effective.

## Self-test

1. State the three levels of government named in the syllabus.
2. Give two roles of local government.
3. Give two roles of national government.
4. Give two roles of government at the international level.
5. Why is defence provided by national government?
6. Why is an international agreement needed to reduce pollution of a shared sea?
7. Give one advantage of providing a service locally rather than nationally.
8. A council spends 25 million and receives a grant of 10 million. Calculate the grant as a percentage of spending.
9. Government spending is 150 billion and GDP is 500 billion. Calculate spending as a percentage of GDP.
10. Give one way the government acts as a regulator.
11. How does the government act as a redistributor?
12. Which level of government would deal with rising national inflation? Why?

### Answers

1. Local, national and international.
2. Any two: waste collection, local roads, parks and libraries, planning permission, licensing, local schools.
3. Any two: defence, courts and police, national economic policy, welfare benefits, funding health and education, national laws.
4. Any two: trade agreements, aid, tackling cross-border pollution, shared fishing limits, disease control.
5. It is a public good that benefits the whole country and needs funding only national taxes can provide.
6. Pollution from one country harms others, and no single country can control the others' firms.
7. Local bodies know local needs, and residents can hold them to account.
8. 10 / 25 x 100 = 40%.
9. 150 / 500 x 100 = 30%.
10. Any one: safety standards, pollution limits, competition rules, minimum wage laws.
11. It taxes higher incomes more heavily and pays benefits to low-income households.
12. National government: it controls national taxes and spending and oversees the central bank's interest rates.
