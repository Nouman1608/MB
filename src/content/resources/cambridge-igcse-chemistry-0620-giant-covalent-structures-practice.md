## Section A

**1.** Which statement about graphite is correct?

A: Each carbon atom forms four covalent bonds.

B: It conducts electricity because it contains ions.

C: Its layers are held together by weak forces.

D: It has a low melting point.

**[1]**

**2.** Which property explains why diamond is used in cutting tools?

A: It is very hard.

B: It does not conduct electricity.

C: It is insoluble in water.

D: It is made only of carbon.

**[1]**

**3.** State the arrangement of the four bonds around each carbon atom in diamond. **[1]**

**4.** Diamond and graphite are both forms of carbon. State one property they share and one property in which they differ. **[2]**

**5.** *(Supplement)* State the number of oxygen atoms bonded to each silicon atom, and the number of silicon atoms bonded to each oxygen atom, in silicon(IV) oxide. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Graphite is used as an electrode in electrolysis.

**(a)** Describe the structure of graphite. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why graphite conducts electricity. **[2]**

**(c)** Give one other property of graphite that makes it suitable for electrodes. **[1]**

**7.** Explain, in terms of structure and bonding, why graphite is used as a lubricant. **[3]**

**8.** Diamond does not conduct electricity, but graphite does. Explain this difference in terms of the bonding of the carbon atoms. **[3]**

**9.** A student says: "Graphite is soft, so it must have a low melting point." Explain why the student is wrong. **[3]**

**10.** *(Supplement)* Silicon(IV) oxide is the main compound in sand.

**(a)** Describe the structure of silicon(IV) oxide. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the formula is SiO₂ even though each silicon atom is bonded to four oxygen atoms. **[2]**

**11.** *(Supplement)* Describe the similarity in properties between diamond and silicon(IV) oxide, and relate it to their structures. **[4]**

**12.** *(Supplement)* Carbon and silicon are both in Group IV. Carbon dioxide sublimes at −78 °C, but silicon(IV) oxide melts at well over 1000 °C. Explain this difference. **[4]**

**13.** An unknown element forms a solid with a very high melting point. It does not conduct electricity and is extremely hard. Suggest the type of structure it has and give one piece of evidence that rules out a metal. **[2]**

**14.** Three solids, P, Q and R, are tested.

P: melting point very high; does not conduct when solid or when molten.

Q: melting point very high; conducts when solid; soft and leaves a grey mark on paper.

R: melting point high; does not conduct when solid; conducts when molten.

Identify which solid is graphite and which has a diamond-like giant covalent structure, and explain why R is neither. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. Graphite has three bonds per carbon, conducts through delocalised electrons and has a very high melting point.

**2.** **A** [1].

**3.** **Tetrahedral** [1].

**4.** Shared: **very high melting point** (or both made of carbon atoms joined by covalent bonds; both insoluble in water) [1]. Differ: **graphite conducts electricity, diamond does not** (or diamond is very hard, graphite is soft) [1].

**5.** **Four** oxygen atoms per silicon [1]; **two** silicon atoms per oxygen [1].

**6.**

**(a)** Each carbon atom forms **three covalent bonds** [1]; the atoms form **flat layers of hexagonal rings** [1]; the layers are held together by **weak forces** [1].

**(b)** Each carbon has **one delocalised electron** (its fourth outer electron) [1] that is **free to move** through the structure and carry charge [1].

**(c)** Any one: **very high melting point**; **unreactive / inert** in electrolysis [1].

**7.** Graphite has **layers** of carbon atoms [1] held together by **weak forces** [1], so the layers can **slide over one another** [1].

**8.** In diamond each carbon uses **all four outer electrons** in four covalent bonds [1], so there are **no delocalised electrons** [1]. In graphite each carbon forms only three bonds, so **one electron per atom is delocalised** and can move [1].

**9.** Softness comes from the **weak forces between layers**, which let layers slide [1]. Melting requires **breaking the strong covalent bonds within the layers** [1], and there are very many of them, so the melting point is **very high** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** A **giant covalent** (3-D network) structure [1]; each **silicon atom is covalently bonded to four oxygen atoms** [1]; each **oxygen atom is bonded to two silicon atoms** [1].

**(b)** Each oxygen is **shared between two silicon atoms** [1], so overall there are **two oxygen atoms for every silicon atom** (ratio 1 : 2) [1].

**11.** Both are **very hard** [1]; both have **very high melting points** [1]; both **do not conduct electricity** [1]; because both have a **giant 3-D network of strong covalent bonds** with no delocalised electrons [1].

**12.** CO₂ is made of **simple molecules** [1] with **weak intermolecular forces**, which need little energy to overcome [1]. SiO₂ is a **giant covalent structure** [1]; melting it requires **breaking many strong covalent bonds**, which needs a large amount of energy [1].

**13.** **Giant covalent** [1]. Metals **conduct electricity**; this solid does not [1].

**14.** **Q** is graphite (conducts as a solid, soft) [1]. **P** is the diamond-like giant covalent solid (very high melting point, never conducts) [1]. **R** conducts only when molten, so it contains **ions** that become free to move: it is **ionic**, not covalent [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that graphite's layers are joined by covalent bonds, or that its covalent bonds are weak.
- Explaining graphite's conductivity with ions instead of delocalised electrons.
- Giving the property without the structural reason when the question says "in terms of structure and bonding".
- Linking diamond's use to its melting point rather than its hardness.
- For SiO₂, describing the bonding around silicon but not around oxygen, which is what explains the formula.
- Treating SiO₂ as if it had the same structure as CO₂ because both are Group IV oxides.
