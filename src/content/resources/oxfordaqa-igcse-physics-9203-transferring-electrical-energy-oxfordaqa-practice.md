These questions cover section 3.6.5 Transferring electrical energy, statements a to f, of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) specification, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Take the mains supply as 230 V and assume fuses of 3 A, 5 A and 13 A are available unless a question says otherwise. Prices are invented for these questions. For help, see the [electrical energy study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-transferring-electrical-energy-oxfordaqa/) and the [electrical energy revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-physics-9203-transferring-electrical-energy-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the useful energy transfer in an electric fan. **[1]**

**2.** Which is the unit of power?

A: joule

B: watt

C: kilowatt-hour

D: coulomb

**[1]**

**3.** A device transfers 9000 J of energy in 30 s. Calculate its power. **[2]**

**4.** A 1.5 V cell drives 2000 C of charge round a circuit. Calculate the energy transferred. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why household electricity use is measured in kilowatt-hours rather than joules. **[1]**

**6.** Calculate the current in a 700 W vacuum cleaner and choose a suitable fuse. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A toaster has a power rating of 1.5 kW.

**(a)** Calculate the current it draws from the mains. **[2]**

**(b)** State which fuse should be fitted and explain why the other two are unsuitable. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy, in joules, the toaster transfers in 6.0 minutes. **[2]**

**8.** A current of 4.0 A flows through a 12 V car headlamp for 5.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the energy transferred. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the power of the headlamp, and check your answer to (b) using P = E ÷ t. **[2]**

**9.** An electric shower has a power of 8.5 kW. A family uses it for a total of 12 minutes each day. Electricity costs 18 cents per kWh.

**(a)** Calculate the energy transferred each day, in kWh. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the daily cost. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the energy transferred in a week. **[1]**

**10.** A household electricity meter reads 08 241.7 kWh on 1 March and 08 563.2 kWh on 1 April. Electricity costs 22 cents per kWh.

**(a)** Calculate the energy used in March. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost of this energy in cents. **[1]**

**(c)** The householder says the meter shows "how much power the house used". Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[2]**

**11.** A homeowner replaces a 40 W lamp with an 8 W lamp that gives the same light. The lamp is used for 6 hours a day.

**(a)** Calculate the energy saved in one year (365 days), in kWh. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the money saved in one year at 20 cents per kWh. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest why both lamps give the same light even though their powers are different. **[2]**

**12.** A 2.3 kW kettle is switched on for 2.0 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the current through the kettle. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge that flows. **[2]**

**(c)** Use E = V Q to calculate the energy transferred. **[2]**

**(d)** Explain what a potential difference of 230 V means in terms of energy and charge. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Electrical energy to the kinetic energy of the blades (moving air) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** P = E ÷ t = 9000 ÷ 30 [1] = **300 W** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** E = V Q = 1.5 × 2000 [1] = **3000 J** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The joule is a very small unit, so household energies in joules would be inconveniently large numbers [1]. **[1]**

**6.** I = P ÷ V = 700 ÷ 230 [1] = 3.04 A [1]; choose the **5 A** fuse [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* 3.04 A is only just above 3 A, so the 3 A fuse would melt in normal use. Always compare the current with the rating, not round first.

**7. (a)** I = 1500 ÷ 230 [1] = **6.5 A** [1].

**(b)** **13 A** [1]. The 3 A and 5 A fuses are below the working current [1], so they would melt in normal use; 13 A is the next rating above 6.5 A [1].

**(c)** E = P t = 1500 × 360 [1] = **540 000 J** (5.4 × 10⁵ J) [1]. **[7]**

**8. (a)** Q = I t = 4.0 × 300 [1] = **1200 C** [1].

**(b)** E = V Q = 12 × 1200 [1] = **14 400 J** [1].

**(c)** P = I V = 4.0 × 12 = 48 W [1]; check: E ÷ t = 14 400 ÷ 300 = 48 W, which agrees [1]. **[6]**

**9. (a)** t = 12 ÷ 60 = 0.20 h [1]; E = 8.5 × 0.20 = **1.7 kWh** [1].

**(b)** 1.7 × 18 = **30.6 cents** (about 31 cents) [1].

**(c)** 1.7 × 7 = **11.9 kWh** [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 8563.2 − 8241.7 = **321.5 kWh** [1].

**(b)** 321.5 × 22 = **7073 cents** [1].

**(c)** The meter measures energy (in kWh), not power [1]. Power is the rate of energy transfer, in watts; the meter shows the total energy transferred [1]. **[4]**

**11. (a)** Power saved = 40 − 8 = 32 W = 0.032 kW [1]. Time = 6 × 365 = 2190 h [1]. Energy saved = 0.032 × 2190 = **70.08 kWh** (about 70 kWh) [1].

**(b)** 70.08 × 20 = **1402 cents** (about 14.02 in the main unit) [1].

**(c)** The 8 W lamp wastes much less energy by heating [1], so a larger fraction of its input is transferred as light; it is more efficient [1]. **[6]**

**12. (a)** I = 2300 ÷ 230 [1] = **10 A** [1].

**(b)** Q = I t = 10 × 120 [1] = **1200 C** [1].

**(c)** E = 230 × 1200 [1] = **276 000 J** (2.76 × 10⁵ J) [1].

**(d)** Each coulomb of charge transfers 230 J of energy as it passes through the kettle [1]. **[7]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using watts with hours or kilowatts with seconds.
- Forgetting to change minutes into hours or seconds.
- Rounding the current before choosing a fuse, so a fuse at the working current is chosen.
- Adding meter readings or confusing power with energy.
- Missing the explanation of why the other fuses are unsuitable.
- Leaving out units, especially kWh against J.
