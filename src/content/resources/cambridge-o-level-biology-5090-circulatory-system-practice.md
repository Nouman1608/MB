## Section A

**1.** Which vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?

A: aorta

B: pulmonary artery

C: pulmonary vein

D: vena cava

**[1]**

**2.** Which part of the circulatory system ensures that blood flows in one direction only?

A: capillaries

B: plasma

C: red blood cells

D: valves

**[1]**

**3.** Name the **three** main parts of the circulatory system. **[3]**

**4.** Define a double circulation. **[2]**

**5.** State the side of the heart that pumps blood to the lungs. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The list below shows, in the wrong order, parts of the route taken by blood in the double circulation: aorta, left atrium, right ventricle, lungs, vena cava, left ventricle, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, right atrium, body.

**(a)** Write the parts in the correct order, starting with the vena cava. **[4]**

**(b)** State which vessels in your list carry deoxygenated blood. **[2]**

**(c)** State which loop of the circulation is at low pressure. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why it is an advantage for blood to travel to the lungs at a lower pressure than to the rest of the body. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why the blood returning from the lungs is pumped through the heart again before it goes to the body. **[3]**

**9.** In a fish, blood passes through the heart once on each complete circuit. In a human, it passes through twice.

**(a)** State the name given to the human system. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest why the human system is an advantage for an animal with a high demand for oxygen. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how valves ensure one-way flow of blood. **[3]**

**11.** Trace the path of a glucose molecule absorbed into the blood in the small intestine as far as the heart, naming the main vessels, if the molecule first passes through the liver. **[3]**

**12.** The maximum pressure in the aorta is 16.0 kPa and in the pulmonary artery it is 3.2 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the pressure is in the aorta. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain the difference in pressure. **[2]**

**13.** A resting adult has 5.0 dm cubed of blood. The heart pumps 5.5 dm cubed of blood per minute. Calculate the time, in seconds, for the heart to pump a volume equal to all of the blood, giving your answer to the nearest second. Show your working. **[2]**

**14.** Explain why the circulatory system is described as a closed system. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** D [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Blood vessels [1]; the heart (the pump) [1]; valves [1]. **[3]**

**4.** A system in which blood passes through the heart twice [1] for each complete circuit of the body [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The right side [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** Vena cava, right atrium [1]; right ventricle, pulmonary artery [1]; lungs, pulmonary vein, left atrium [1]; left ventricle, aorta, body [1].

**(b)** Any two of: vena cava; pulmonary artery; right atrium; right ventricle (chambers also accepted) [1 each].

**(c)** The circulation to the lungs (pulmonary circulation) [1]. **[7]**

**7.** The capillaries in the lungs have very thin walls [1], so a high pressure could damage them [1]. A lower pressure also makes the blood flow slower, which gives more time for gas exchange [1]. **[3]**

**8.** The pressure of the blood falls as it passes through the narrow lung capillaries [1]. Returning it to the left side of the heart means it is pumped again by the left ventricle [1], giving it a high pressure so it reaches the body tissues quickly [1]. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** Double circulation [1].

**(b)** The blood is pumped at high pressure to the body after passing through the lungs [1], so it flows quickly to the tissues [1], which supplies oxygen to the cells fast enough for a high rate of respiration [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Valves are flaps that open when blood pushes from behind [1] and close when blood tries to flow backwards [1], so the blood can only move in one direction [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The glucose travels in the hepatic portal vein to the liver [1], then in the hepatic vein to the vena cava [1], and then into the right atrium of the heart [1]. **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** 16.0 divided by 3.2 = 5 times [1].

**(b)** The left ventricle pumps blood to the whole body, so it produces a high pressure [1]. The right ventricle pumps blood only to the lungs, where a high pressure could damage the thin capillaries, so the pressure is lower [1]. **[3]**

**13.** Time = 5.0 divided by 5.5 = 0.909 minutes [1]. 0.909 x 60 = 54.5, which is 55 seconds to the nearest second [1]. **[2]**

**14.** The blood stays inside blood vessels [1] and does not flow freely among the tissues; substances pass between blood and cells through the capillary walls [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that double circulation means "two hearts".
- Saying that all arteries carry oxygenated blood and all veins carry deoxygenated blood.
- Putting the left ventricle before the lungs in the route.
- Leaving out the reason for a low pressure to the lungs.
- Forgetting that valves are part of the system.
