These questions cover section 3.4.3, **Vectors** (G24 and G25), of the AQA GCSE Mathematics (8300) specification. Questions 1 to 7 and Question 8(a) suit both tiers. Question 8(b) and Questions 9 to 11 use vectors in geometric arguments and proofs, which is Higher tier only content, and are labelled. In this set a column vector is written (x, y), with the top entry first. Learn the methods first in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-vectors/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-gcse-maths-8300-vectors-revision-notes/). Sketch each diagram as you read.

## Questions

**1.**

**(a)** The point (−2, 5) is translated by the vector (4, −3). Write down the coordinates of the image. **[1]**

**(b)** A is the point (1, 2) and B is the point (6, −1). Write down the vector from A to B. **[1]**

**2.** a = (3, −2) and b = (−4, 5). Work out:

**(a)** a + b **[1]**

**(b)** a − b **[1]**

**(c)** 2a + 3b **[2]**

**3.** ABCD is a parallelogram. AB = **p** and AD = **q**. Write each of these in terms of **p** and **q**.

**(a)** AC **[1]**

**(b)** BD **[1]**

**(c)** CA **[1]**

**4.** AB = (4, 1) and BC = (−1, 5).

**(a)** Work out AC. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down CA. **[1]**

**5.** Show that the vectors (6, −9) and (4, −6) are parallel. **[2]**

**6.** The triangle with vertices (1, 1), (4, 1) and (4, 3) is translated by the vector (−3, 2).

**(a)** Write down the coordinates of the vertices of the image. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the vector of the translation that maps the image back onto the original triangle. **[1]**

**7.** (x, 3) + (2, y) = (7, 1). Work out the values of x and y. **[2]**

**8.** OAB is a triangle with OA = **a** and OB = **b**. M is the midpoint of AB.

**(a)** Write AB in terms of **a** and **b**. **[1]**

**(b)** (Higher tier only) Work out OM in terms of **a** and **b**. Give your answer in its simplest form. **[2]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) OAB is a triangle with OA = **a** and OB = **b**. P is a point on AB such that AP : PB = 2 : 3. Work out OP in terms of **a** and **b**. **[3]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) OAB is a triangle with OA = **a** and OB = **b**. M is the midpoint of OA and N is the midpoint of OB. Prove that MN is parallel to AB and that MN is half the length of AB. **[3]**

**11.** (Higher tier only) ABCD is a parallelogram with AB = **a** and AD = **b**. The point M is the midpoint of AC. Prove that M is also the midpoint of BD. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **(a)** (−2 + 4, 5 − 3) = **(2, 2)** [1]
**(b)** (6 − 1, −1 − 2) = **(5, −3)** [1]

**2.** **(a)** (3 − 4, −2 + 5) = **(−1, 3)** [1]
**(b)** (3 + 4, −2 − 5) = **(7, −7)** [1]
**(c)** 2a = (6, −4) and 3b = (−12, 15) [1]; sum = **(−6, 11)** [1]

**3.** **(a)** AC = AB + BC = **p** + **q** [1] (because BC = AD = **q**)
**(b)** BD = BA + AD = −**p** + **q** = **q** − **p** [1]
**(c)** CA = −AC = −**p** − **q** [1]

**4.** **(a)** AC = AB + BC = (4 − 1, 1 + 5) = **(3, 6)** [1]
**(b)** CA = −AC = **(−3, −6)** [1]

**5.** (6, −9) = 1.5 × (4, −6) [1]. One vector is a scalar multiple of the other, so they are parallel [1].

**6.** **(a)** (1 − 3, 1 + 2) = (−2, 3) [1]; (4 − 3, 1 + 2) = (1, 3) and (4 − 3, 3 + 2) = (1, 5), so the vertices are **(−2, 3), (1, 3), (1, 5)** [1]
**(b)** The opposite vector **(3, −2)** [1]

**7.** x + 2 = 7, so **x = 5** [1]; 3 + y = 1, so **y = −2** [1]

**8.** **(a)** AB = AO + OB = −**a** + **b** = **b** − **a** [1]
**(b)** (Higher tier only) OM = OA + AM = **a** + ½(**b** − **a**) [1] = **½a + ½b**, or ½(**a** + **b**) [1]

**9.** (Higher tier only) AB = **b** − **a** [1]. AP = 2/5 AB = 2/5(**b** − **a**) [1]. OP = OA + AP = **a** + 2/5(**b** − **a**) = **3/5 a + 2/5 b** [1]

**10.** (Higher tier only) MN = MO + ON = −½**a** + ½**b** [1] = ½(**b** − **a**) [1]. AB = **b** − **a**, so MN = ½AB. Since MN is a scalar multiple of AB, they are parallel, and MN is half the length of AB [1].

**11.** (Higher tier only) AC = AB + BC = **a** + **b**, so AM = ½(**a** + **b**) [1]. BM = BA + AM = −**a** + ½(**a** + **b**) [1] = ½(**b** − **a**) [1]. BD = BA + AD = **b** − **a**, so BM = ½BD. M lies on BD and is the midpoint of BD, so M is the midpoint of both diagonals [1].

## Which questions are Higher tier only?

Question 8(b) and Questions 9 to 11 use vectors to construct geometric arguments and proofs (G25), which is listed under Higher tier only content in the specification. Questions 1 to 7 and 8(a) cover translations as vectors (G24) and the addition, subtraction and scalar multiplication of vectors in diagrammatic and column form (G25), which both tiers are assessed on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding top and bottom entries together.
- Treating BA as the same as AB.
- Sign errors when subtracting vectors with negative entries.
- Stopping before the conclusion in a proof.
- Claiming lines are parallel without showing a scalar multiple.
