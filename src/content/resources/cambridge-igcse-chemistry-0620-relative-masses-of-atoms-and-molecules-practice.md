## Section A

**1.** Which atom is used as the standard for relative atomic masses?

A: hydrogen-1

B: carbon-12

C: oxygen-16

D: carbon-14

**[1]**

**2.** What is the relative formula mass of magnesium chloride, MgCl₂?

A: 59.5

B: 71

C: 95

D: 106.5

**[1]**

**3.** Describe what is meant by the relative atomic mass of an element. **[3]**

**4.** Calculate the relative molecular mass of ethanol, C₂H₅OH. **[1]**

**5.** Calculate the relative formula mass of potassium carbonate, K₂CO₃. **[1]**

**6.** Bromine has two isotopes, bromine-79 and bromine-81, which are almost equally common. Suggest a value for the relative atomic mass of bromine and explain your answer. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Calculate the relative formula mass of each compound, showing your working.

**(a)** Calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Iron(III) sulfate, Fe₂(SO₄)₃. **[2]**

**8.** A student calculates the Mr of aluminium hydroxide, Al(OH)₃, as 27 + 16 + 3 = 46. Explain the error and give the correct value. **[2]**

**9.** Zinc oxide is made by heating zinc carbonate: ZnCO₃ → ZnO + CO₂.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of zinc carbonate and of zinc oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of zinc oxide formed from 50 g of zinc carbonate. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**10.** Ammonia is manufactured by the reaction N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃.

**(a)** Show that 28 tonnes of nitrogen forms 34 tonnes of ammonia. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ammonia that forms from 140 tonnes of nitrogen. **[1]**

**11.** In an experiment, 0.48 g of magnesium ribbon is burned and forms 0.80 g of magnesium oxide.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of oxygen that combined with the magnesium. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of magnesium oxide that would form from 1.20 g of magnesium. **[2]**

**12.** Copper(II) oxide reacts with dilute sulfuric acid: CuO + H₂SO₄ → CuSO₄ + H₂O. Calculate the mass of copper(II) oxide needed to make 32.0 g of copper(II) sulfate. **[3]**

**13.** Sodium reacts with chlorine: 2Na + Cl₂ → 2NaCl. A student says: "46 g of sodium makes 58.5 g of sodium chloride." Explain the mistake and give the correct mass. **[2]**

**14.** Copper(II) oxide can be made by heating copper(II) carbonate: CuCO₃ → CuO + CO₂.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of copper(II) carbonate. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of copper(II) carbonate that must be heated to make 20.0 g of copper(II) oxide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. 24 + 2 × 35.5 = 95.

**3.** The **average mass** of the **isotopes** of the element [1], allowing for their abundance, **compared to 1/12th of the mass** [1] of an atom of **carbon-12** [1].

**4.** 2 × 12 + 6 × 1 + 16 = **46** [1].

**5.** 2 × 39 + 12 + 3 × 16 = **138** [1].

**6.** About **80** [1]; it is the **average** of 79 and 81, and with almost equal amounts of each isotope the average is close to halfway [1].

**7.**

**(a)** 40 + 2 × (16 + 1) = **74** [1].

**(b)** 2 × 56 = 112 and 3 × (32 + 4 × 16) = 3 × 96 = 288 [1]; 112 + 288 = **400** [1].

**8.** The bracket was ignored: the 3 multiplies **both O and H** [1]. Correct value: 27 + 3 × 17 = **78** [1].

**9.**

**(a)** ZnCO₃: 65 + 12 + 48 = **125** [1]; ZnO: 65 + 16 = **81** [1].

**(b)** 125 g gives 81 g, so scale factor = 50 ÷ 125 = 0.4 [1]; mass = 81 × 0.4 = **32.4 g** [1].

**10.**

**(a)** N₂: 1 × 28 = 28 [1]; NH₃: 2 × 17 = 34, so 28 tonnes of N₂ gives 34 tonnes of NH₃ [1].

**(b)** 140 ÷ 28 = 5; 5 × 34 = **170 tonnes** [1].

**11.**

**(a)** 0.80 − 0.48 = **0.32 g** [1].

**(b)** Scale factor = 1.20 ÷ 0.48 = 2.5 [1]; mass = 0.80 × 2.5 = **2.00 g** [1].

**12.** Mr: CuO = 80, CuSO₄ = 64 + 96 = 160 [1]; 160 g CuSO₄ needs 80 g CuO, scale factor = 32.0 ÷ 160 = 0.2 [1]; mass of CuO = 80 × 0.2 = **16.0 g** [1].

**13.** The student has **ignored the coefficient 2** in front of NaCl: 46 g of sodium (2 × 23) forms **2** × 58.5 [1] = **117 g** of sodium chloride [1].

**14.**

**(a)** 64 + 12 + 3 × 16 = **124** [1].

**(b)** 124 g of CuCO₃ gives 80 g of CuO, so scale factor = 20.0 ÷ 80 = 0.25 [1]; mass of CuCO₃ = 124 × 0.25 = **31.0 g** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using atomic numbers instead of relative atomic masses from the Periodic Table.
- Multiplying only the atom next to a bracket instead of everything inside it.
- Forgetting coefficients when working out the mass ratio from an equation.
- Rounding chlorine to 35 or 36.
- Leaving the answer without units in a reacting-mass question, or changing units without being asked.
- Describing Ar without the words "average" and "isotopes", or without the carbon-12 standard.
- Not giving the answer to the number of significant figures the question asks for.
