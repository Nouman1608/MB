## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** resources a business needs to market a product. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** way in which a small marketing budget could limit a business's marketing strategy. **[3]**

**3.** Explain the purpose of a SWOT analysis. **[3]**

**4.** A business lists "a new competitor has opened nearby" as a weakness in its SWOT analysis. Explain why this is wrongly placed. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**5.** Pennine Pet Supplies has a marketing budget of £45,000. It is comparing two methods.

- Leaflets: cost £9,000, delivered to 30,000 homes, with an expected response rate of 0.8%.
- Search advertising: cost £20,000, expected 25,000 clicks, with 4% of clicks leading to a purchase.

**(a)** Calculate the expected number of customers from each method. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the cost per customer for each method. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate how much of the budget is left if Pennine uses both methods. **[1]**

**(d)** Evaluate which method Pennine should use. **[8]**

**6.** Pennine's owner has scored its SWOT factors. Within each box the weights total 100 and the impact is scored from 1 (small) to 5 (large).

| Box | Weight and score |
|---|---|
| Strengths | 50 and 5; 30 and 3; 20 and 4 |
| Weaknesses | 60 and 4; 40 and 3 |
| Opportunities | 70 and 4; 30 and 2 |
| Threats | 55 and 5; 45 and 2 |

**(a)** Calculate the weighted score for each of the four boxes. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the internal position and the external position, and explain what each shows. **[4]**

**7.** Nadir Coffee Roasters sells beans online and through six cafés. It has a loyal customer database of 18,000 people, a team of one marketer and a shortage of cash after opening two new cafés. Coffee consumption in its region is rising. A large chain is opening nearby. Analyse the marketing position of Nadir Coffee Roasters using a SWOT framework. **[6]**

**8.** Zephyr Fitness is a small gym chain with a strong reputation but only one marketing employee and a limited budget. It plans to launch an app and a national advertising campaign in the same year. Evaluate the impact of its resources on its ability to develop a successful marketing strategy. **[9]**

**9.** A business consultant tells the board of Calder Furniture: "Carry out a SWOT analysis and then you will know what to do." Evaluate the usefulness of SWOT analysis to Calder Furniture and its stakeholders. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two from: financial (budget); human (skilled staff); physical (premises, stock, vehicles); technological (website, database); intangible (brand, reputation); time [1] [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A small budget rules out expensive media such as national television or large trade fairs [1], so the business must use cheaper methods such as social media or local press [1], which reach fewer people, which may slow the growth of sales or share [1]. **[3]**

**3.** To identify the internal strengths and weaknesses of a business and the external opportunities and threats it faces [1], so that managers understand its marketing position [1] and can choose objectives and strategies that use strengths, reduce weaknesses and respond to the external environment [1]. **[3]**

**4.** A new competitor is outside the business [1], so it is an external threat, not an internal weakness. Weaknesses are internal factors the business could change [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Leaflets: 30,000 × 0.008 = **240 customers** [1]. Search: 25,000 × 0.04 = **1,000 customers** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Leaflets: 9,000 ÷ 240 = **£37.50** [1]. Search: 20,000 ÷ 1,000 = **£20.00** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** 45,000 − 9,000 − 20,000 = **£16,000** [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** For search: it costs £20 per customer, nearly half the £37.50 of leaflets [1]; it wins 1,000 customers against 240 [1]; results can be tracked online [1].
For leaflets: they reach local homes, which may suit a pet shop that depends on local customers [1]; the cost is low, at £9,000 [1].
Limits: the response rates are estimates [1]; the figures show only first sales, not repeat purchases [1].
Judgement: use search advertising as the main method, since it gives the lowest cost per customer, and consider a small leaflet test in the nearest streets with the remaining £16,000 held in reserve [1]. **[8]**

**6. (a)** Strengths: (50×5 + 30×3 + 20×4) ÷ 100 = (250 + 90 + 80) ÷ 100 = **4.2** [1]. Weaknesses: (60×4 + 40×3) ÷ 100 = (240 + 120) ÷ 100 = **3.6** [1]. Opportunities: (70×4 + 30×2) ÷ 100 = (280 + 60) ÷ 100 = **3.4** [1]. Threats: (55×5 + 45×2) ÷ 100 = (275 + 90) ÷ 100 = **3.65** [1]. **[4]**

**(b)** Internal: 4.2 − 3.6 = **+0.6** [1]. This shows that strengths outweigh weaknesses, so the business is in a reasonable internal position [1]. External: 3.4 − 3.65 = **−0.25** [1]. This shows that threats are slightly greater than opportunities, so the environment is mildly unfavourable [1]. **[4]**

**7.** Strengths: a loyal database of 18,000 people allows low-cost targeted email and supports retention [1]. Weakness: one marketer and a cash shortage limit the campaigns that can be run [1]. Opportunity: rising coffee consumption in the region could raise sales if the brand reaches new customers [1]. Threat: a large chain opening nearby may take customers and cut prices [1].
Analysis: link the strength to the threat: the database lets Nadir keep customers by offering loyalty rewards or exclusive beans before the chain opens [1]. The weakness limits how much it can do, so low-cost methods such as email and social media should be used first, and spending on new media should wait until the new cafés produce cash [1]. **[6]**

**8.** Positive impact: a strong reputation lowers the cost of winning customers because members recommend the gym and reviews are good [1]; the existing member base can be a low-cost audience for launching an app [1].
Negative impact: one marketing employee cannot manage an app launch and a national campaign at once [1]; a national campaign requires large spending, which the limited budget will not support [1]; the app needs technology skills the business may lack [1]; a failed national campaign would use the budget the app needs [1].
Evaluation: resources do not prevent a strategy, but they decide its scale and timing [1]. Zephyr could phase the launch, starting with the app and its own members, then adding regional advertising [1]. Judgement: trying both in one year is risky given its resources, and a phased strategy is more likely to succeed [1]. **[9]**

**9.** Usefulness: SWOT is quick and cheap to complete [1]; it forces managers to consider internal and external factors together [1]; it can show where strengths match opportunities, such as a strong brand against growing demand [1]; the written analysis helps stakeholders such as lenders and employees to understand the priorities [1].
Limits: it lists factors but does not rank them or say what to do [1]; it is subjective and different managers may produce different lists [1]; it is a snapshot that can be out of date quickly [1].
Judgement: SWOT is a useful first step, but the consultant's claim that it tells the business what to do is overstated [1]. It is most useful when it is based on evidence such as sales data, weighted, and followed by costed options [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting an external factor in the strengths or weaknesses box.
- Listing SWOT boxes without linking them to a decision.
- Calculating cost per customer upside down (customers divided by cost).
