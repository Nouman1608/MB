## Section A

**1.** For which disease would an antibiotic be an appropriate treatment?

A: HIV infection

B: influenza

C: the common cold

D: a bacterial throat infection

**[1]**

**2.** What does MRSA describe?

A: a bacterium resistant to many antibiotics

B: a drug used to treat malaria

C: a virus that causes AIDS

D: a vaccine against bacteria

**[1]**

**3.** Define a drug. **[2]**

**4.** State why antibiotics are not used to treat viral infections. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** ways of reducing the development of antibiotic-resistant bacteria. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe how a population of bacteria can become resistant to an antibiotic. **[4]**

**7.** A patient with a cold asks the doctor for antibiotics. The doctor refuses.

**(a)** Explain why antibiotics would not help the patient. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why giving them could be harmful for other people. **[3]**

**8.** A student spread bacteria on an agar plate and placed four paper discs, each soaked in a different antibiotic, on the plate. After two days, clear zones were measured.

| Antibiotic | Diameter of clear zone (mm) |
|---|---|
| P | 30 |
| Q | 14 |
| R | 0 |
| S | 22 |

**(a)** State which antibiotic was most effective, and give a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** State what the result for R shows. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the area of the clear zone for P, using area = pi x radius squared (use pi = 3.14). **[2]**

**(d)** State **two** safety precautions that should be followed when carrying out this practical. **[2]**

**(e)** State **two** variables that should be kept the same for each disc. **[2]**

**9.** A population of 2 000 000 bacteria contains 4 resistant bacteria. An antibiotic kills all the others. The resistant bacteria divide every 30 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the number of bacteria after 3 hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the antibiotic becomes ineffective against this population. **[2]**

**10.** Explain why it is important for a patient to take antibiotics only when a doctor prescribes them. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why MRSA is a particular problem in hospitals. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss why the use of antibiotics should be limited, even though they have saved many lives. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** D [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any substance taken into the body [1] that modifies or affects chemical reactions in the body [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Viruses are not cells [1] and replicate inside host cells, so antibiotics do not affect them (antibiotics kill bacteria) [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two of: use antibiotics only when essential; do not use them for viral infections; complete the prescribed course; limit use in farming; hospital hygiene [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.** A mutation occurs by chance, making a bacterium resistant [1]. When the antibiotic is used, the non-resistant bacteria are killed [1] but the resistant bacterium survives and reproduces [1]. The resistant bacteria pass on the gene to their offspring, so the population becomes resistant [1]. **[4]**

**7.**

**(a)** A cold is caused by a virus [1]. Antibiotics kill bacteria but do not affect viruses [1].

**(b)** Using antibiotics when they are not needed gives bacteria more chance to be exposed to them [1]. Resistant bacteria are selected and multiply [1], so the antibiotic will not work for others who really need it [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** P [1], because it has the largest clear zone, so it killed or stopped the growth of the most bacteria [1].

**(b)** The bacteria are resistant to R [1].

**(c)** Radius = 15 mm [1]. Area = 3.14 x 15 x 15 = 706.5 mm squared [1].

**(d)** Any two of: use sterile equipment; keep the plate taped but not sealed; do not incubate at body temperature (use no more than about 25 to 30 degrees Celsius); wash hands; do not open the plate afterwards [1 each].

**(e)** Any two of: size of disc; concentration of antibiotic; volume of antibiotic; type of agar; temperature; incubation time [1 each]. **[9]**

**9.**

**(a)** 3 hours = 6 divisions [1]. 4 x 2 to the power 6 = 4 x 64 = 256 bacteria [1].

**(b)** The resistant bacteria are not killed by the antibiotic [1], and they reproduce and pass on the resistance, so most of the population is resistant [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The more often antibiotics are used, the more chances resistant bacteria have to survive and multiply [1]. If they are used only when essential, fewer bacteria are exposed, so fewer resistant strains develop [1], and the antibiotics remain effective for treating serious infections [1]. **[3]**

**11.** Many patients are given antibiotics in hospitals, so resistant bacteria are selected [1]. Patients with weak immune systems or open wounds are easily infected, so MRSA spreads and is difficult to treat [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Antibiotics kill bacteria and treat serious infections, so they save lives [1]. Each use gives resistant bacteria a chance to survive and reproduce, so overuse leads to resistant strains such as MRSA [1]. If antibiotics stop working, infections become harder to treat, so their use should be limited to when they are essential [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that antibiotics work on viruses.
- Saying that people or the body become resistant, not the bacteria.
- Describing resistance as bacteria "learning" or "getting used to" the drug.
- Forgetting to link mutation, survival and reproduction.
- Not squaring the radius when calculating the area of a zone.
