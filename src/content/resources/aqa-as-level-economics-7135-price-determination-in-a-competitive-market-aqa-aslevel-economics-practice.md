## Section A: short questions

**1.** Distinguish between an extension of demand and an increase in demand. **[2]**

**2.** The price of a monthly bus pass rises from 50 to 55 and the number sold falls from 4,000 to 3,800. Calculate the price elasticity of demand and state what happens to the bus company's total revenue. **[3]**

**3.** Household income rises from 30,000 to 33,000 a year and purchases of a supermarket's basic pasta fall from 120 to 114 packets a year. Calculate the income elasticity of demand and classify the good. **[2]**

**4.** Which one of the following is an example of joint supply? **[1]**

A: Printers and ink cartridges

B: Wheat used for bread and for animal feed

C: Lamb and wool

D: Tea and coffee

**5.** State two factors that make the supply of a good more price elastic. **[2]**

**6.** In a market, Qd = 260 - 5P and Qs = -40 + 10P.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the excess at a price of 15 and explain how the market moves back to equilibrium. **[3]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A coffee capsule maker has the following estimates. When the price of compatible coffee machines fell by 10%, demand for capsules rose by 15%. When the price of tea bags rose by 8%, demand for capsules rose by 2%.

**(a)** Calculate the two cross elasticities of demand. **[2]**

**(b)** Interpret each value. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the first figure matters more to the capsule maker's pricing of machines. **[2]**

**8.** A strawberry grower faces a rise in the market price from 4.00 to 5.00 per kg. In the same week its quantity supplied rises from 2,000 kg to 2,200 kg. Two seasons later, with the price still 5.00, it supplies 3,000 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the price elasticity of supply over the week and over the longer period. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the two values differ. **[3]**

**9.** Explain, with the help of a demand and supply diagram, how an increase in demand for new houses is likely to affect the market for bricks and the wages of bricklayers. **[6]**

**10.** Farmers in Country T grow maize that is sold both for food and for biofuel. The government introduces a subsidy for biofuel producers. Explain the likely effect on the price of maize flour used for food. **[4]**

**11.** In a market for apples, Qd = 400 - 10P and Qs = -50 + 15P. A good harvest then raises supply by 50 units at every price, while a health report lowers demand by 25 units at every price.

**(a)** Calculate the original equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the new equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the direction of the price change could be predicted before any calculation but the direction of the quantity change could not. **[2]**

**12.** A city's taxi firms estimate the price elasticity of demand for taxi rides at -0.4 over the next month and -1.3 over the next three years. They are considering a 10% rise in fares. Evaluate whether the fare rise will increase the firms' total revenue. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** An extension of demand is a movement along the demand curve caused by a fall in the good's own price [1]. An increase in demand is a shift of the whole curve to the right caused by a change in a non-price factor, such as higher income [1]. *Total: 2.*

**2.** Quantity change = -200 / 4,000 = -5%; price change = +5 / 50 = +10% [1]. PED = -5 / 10 = **-0.5**, price inelastic [1]. Revenue rises from 50 x 4,000 = 200,000 to 55 x 3,800 = 209,000 [1]. *Total: 3.*

**3.** Income +10%, quantity -5%, so YED = -5 / 10 = **-0.5** [1]. The negative sign shows an **inferior good** [1]. *Total: 2.*

**4.** **C** [1]. Raising sheep for meat also produces wool. A is joint demand, B is composite demand and D are substitutes. *Total: 1.*

**5.** Any two [1 each]: spare capacity; stocks of finished goods held; factors of production that can be switched from other products; a short production period; a longer time period for adjustment. *Total: 2.*

**6. (a)** 260 - 5P = -40 + 10P gives 300 = 15P, so **P = 20** [1] and **Q = 160** [1].

**(b)** At P = 15, Qd = 185 and Qs = 110, so there is **excess demand of 75** [1]. The shortage lets sellers raise the price [1]; as it rises, supply extends and demand contracts until both equal 160 at a price of 20 [1]. *Total: 5.*

**7. (a)** XED with machines = 15 / -10 = **-1.5** [1]; XED with tea = 2 / 8 = **+0.25** [1].

**(b)** -1.5: machines and capsules are complements, strongly linked [1]. +0.25: tea and capsules are substitutes, but weakly, since demand for capsules barely responds to the price of tea [1].

**(c)** A cheaper machine raises capsule sales by a larger percentage, so the firm can price machines low to sell more capsules [1], gaining revenue in the joint-demand market even if margins on machines are thin [1]. *Total: 6.*

**8. (a)** Price +25%. Week: quantity +200 / 2,000 = +10%, PES = 10 / 25 = **0.4** [1]. Longer period: +1,000 / 2,000 = +50%, PES = 50 / 25 = **2.0** [1].

**(b)** In the week, plants already in the ground limit output and fresh fruit cannot be stored [1], so supply is inelastic. Over two seasons the grower can plant more land, add polytunnels or hire labour [1]. Time allows inputs to be switched to strawberries, so supply becomes elastic [1]. *Total: 5.*

**9.** Bricks have a **derived demand**: they are wanted because houses are wanted [1]. Higher demand for houses shifts the demand curve for bricks to the right [1]. Diagram: demand for bricks shifts from D1 to D2, excess demand at the old price, new equilibrium with higher price and quantity [1]. Bricklayers' labour is also a derived demand, so demand for bricklayers shifts right [1], raising their wage and employment [1]. The size of the wage rise depends on how quickly new bricklayers can be trained, which affects the elasticity of supply of labour [1]. *Total: 6.*

**10.** Maize has **composite demand**, since it is wanted for both food and fuel [1]. The subsidy lets biofuel producers pay more for maize, so more maize is sold for fuel [1]. Less maize is available for food, so the supply of maize to flour mills falls and their input costs rise [1]. The supply of maize flour shifts left, raising its price, by more if demand for flour is price inelastic [1]. *Total: 4.*

**11. (a)** 400 - 10P = -50 + 15P gives 450 = 25P, so **P = 18** [1] and **Q = 220** [1].

**(b)** New curves: Qd = 375 - 10P and Qs = 15P. 375 = 25P gives **P = 15** [1] and **Q = 225** [1].

**(c)** Both shifts push price down: more supply and less demand each lower it [1]. They push quantity in opposite directions, so the result depends on which shift is larger; here the supply shift was larger and quantity rose slightly [1]. *Total: 6.*

**12.** Short run: a 10% fare rise cuts rides by 0.4 x 10 = 4%, so revenue changes by 1.10 x 0.96 = 1.056, a **rise of 5.6%** [2]. Long run: rides fall 13%, revenue changes by 1.10 x 0.87 = 0.957, a **fall of 4.3%** [2]. Demand becomes more elastic over time because travellers find substitutes such as ride-hailing apps, buses or buying a car [1]. The estimates assume rival firms do not react; if competitors keep fares unchanged, demand for each firm is more elastic than for the market [1]. Revenue is not profit: fewer rides also mean lower fuel and driver costs [1]. Judgement: the fare rise is likely to raise revenue at first but reduce it over three years, so it makes sense only if the firms are short of drivers or face rising costs now [1]. *Total: 8.*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the minus sign on PED or YED, or quoting XED with no sign.
- Calling an increase in demand an extension, or the reverse.
- Stating a new equilibrium without the excess demand or supply that drives the change.
- Naming the wrong link: joint supply is a production link, joint demand a consumption link.
- Using only one elasticity figure in question 12 when the time period is the point of the data.
