These notes condense section 2.9 (market economic system) of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: the definition (2.9.1) and the advantages and disadvantages, with examples from different economies (2.9.2). All countries and figures are invented. Full explanations are in the [market economic system study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-market-economic-system-cambridge-igcse-economics/); test yourself with the [market economic system practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-market-economic-system-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/).

## 2.9.1 Definition and features

**Market economic system:** resources are allocated through the price mechanism, are owned mainly by the private sector, and there is little government intervention.

| Feature | One-line meaning |
|---|---|
| Private ownership | Households and firms own resources and businesses |
| Price mechanism | Prices set by demand and supply guide resources |
| Profit motive | Firms make what they expect to be profitable |
| Consumer choice | Households decide how to spend their incomes |
| Competition | Rival firms keep costs and prices down |
| Limited government | Law, property rights, contracts, defence |

**The three questions:**

- What? Whatever earns a profit because buyers pay for it.
- How? The lowest-cost method at current factor prices.
- For whom? Those with income to pay.

No real economy is a pure market system; all have some state provision. Economies differ in how far they rely on markets.

## 2.9.2 Advantages (feature -> advantage)

| Feature | Advantage | Chain |
|---|---|---|
| Competition | Efficiency | Wasteful firms have higher costs, lose customers, so resources are used carefully |
| Price mechanism | Responsiveness | Wants change -> prices and profits change -> resources move |
| Many firms | Choice | Different products and prices for different tastes |
| Profit motive | Innovation | New products and cheaper methods earn higher profits |
| Private ownership | Incentives | People keep what they earn, so they work and invest |
| Limited government | Lower administration costs | Fewer officials, possibly lower taxes |

## 2.9.2 Disadvantages (feature -> disadvantage)

| Feature | Disadvantage | Chain |
|---|---|---|
| For whom = ability to pay | Inequality | High earners and owners of assets get most of the output |
| Profit motive | Missing goods | Goods people cannot be charged for are not supplied |
| Consumer choice | Under-use of beneficial goods | Some cannot afford or undervalue education and health care |
| Consumer choice and profit | Over-use of harmful goods; pollution | Harm to others is ignored by buyers and sellers |
| Competition can end in dominance | Market power | A dominant firm may raise prices, cut choice |
| Private decisions | Unemployed resources | Workers and capital may not move easily from declining industries |

## Calculation reminders

**Profit signal.**

```
Price 50 -> 65; cost per unit 40
Profit per unit 10 -> 25 (a 150% rise)
```

**Income shares.**

```
Total income 600 billion; richest 10% earn 36%; poorest 10% earn 3%
Richest: 0.36 x 600 = 216 billion; poorest: 0.03 x 600 = 18 billion
Ratio = 36 / 3 = 12 times
```

**Growth of output after entry.**

```
Output 12,000 -> 15,000 units
Rise = 3,000 / 12,000 x 100 = 25%
```

## Comparing economies

| Greater reliance on markets | Less reliance on markets |
|---|---|
| Lower taxes, more private provision | Higher taxes, more state provision |
| Stronger incentives, more new firms | Weaker incentives, slower entry |
| Wider choice | More equal access to services |
| Wider inequality | Narrower inequality |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Market economic system | Allocation through the price mechanism with mainly private ownership and little intervention |
| Private sector | Firms and households, not the government |
| Profit motive | The aim of earning profit that guides firms' decisions |
| Competition | Rivalry between firms for customers |
| Inequality | Unequal distribution of income or wealth |
| Market power | The ability of a dominant firm to influence price |

## Judgement tests (for "Discuss")

1. Which products? Markets suit everyday goods; they suit public services and polluting activities less well.
2. Which people? Owners of skills and assets gain most.
3. Which goal? Efficiency and choice favour markets; fairness favours more intervention.

## Exam traps

- Defining the system as having "no government". Government still enforces laws.
- Advantages listed with no feature or chain.
- "Goods go to those who need them": wrong; they go to those who can pay.
- One-sided "Discuss" answers.
- Treating a disadvantage as unavoidable: most can be reduced, which is why mixed economies exist.

## Quick self-test

1. Define a market economic system.
2. Name four features of a market economic system.
3. How does a market economic system decide what to produce?
4. Who receives the output in a market system?
5. Explain one way competition leads to efficiency.
6. Why does the profit motive encourage innovation?
7. Why might street lighting not be provided in a pure market system?
8. State one reason inequality may widen in a market system.
9. Price rises from 80 to 100 and cost per unit is 70. By what percentage does profit per unit rise?
10. The richest 10% earn 30% of income and the poorest 10% earn 2.5%. How many times more is the richest share?
11. State one role government keeps even in a market economy.
12. Give one condition under which a market system is likely to work well.

### Answers

1. An economy where resources are allocated through the price mechanism, mainly owned privately, with little government intervention.
2. Any four: private ownership, price mechanism, profit motive, consumer choice, competition, limited government.
3. Firms produce what consumers will pay for at a profitable price.
4. Those who are willing and able to pay.
5. Firms that waste resources have higher costs and lose customers, so firms keep costs down.
6. New products or cheaper methods earn higher profits.
7. Firms cannot make people pay for it, so it is not profitable.
8. Those with valuable skills or assets earn more, and can buy more assets.
9. Profit 10 -> 30, a rise of 200%.
10. 30 / 2.5 = 12 times.
11. Any one: law and order, defence, enforcing contracts, protecting property.
12. Everyday goods, many competing firms, and no large effects on third parties.
