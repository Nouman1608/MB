## 1 Operating as a sole trader

One person owns the business and the business is not a separate legal person from them.

- **Advantages:** all profit belongs to the owner; decisions need no one else's agreement; few legal formalities, so it is cheap to start; accounts need not be shared widely.
- **Disadvantages:** unlimited liability, so debts the business cannot pay may be met from the owner's private assets; only one person's savings and borrowing power funds growth; illness can stop trading; one person carries every loss and every task.

Apply each point to the case given: "unlimited liability" is only a label, while "unpaid supplier debts could force a sale of the owner's private car" explains it.

## 2 Types of sole trader business

| Type | Earns revenue by | Statement of profit or loss shows | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Trading | Buying and reselling goods | Cost of sales, gross profit | Cycle shop |
| Service | Selling skills or time | Fees less expenses, no gross profit | Driving instructor |
| Manufacturing | Making the goods it sells | Manufacturing account, then cost of sales | Joiner |
| Combination | More than one of the above | Gross profit on the trading side, other income added below it | Garage that sells parts and charges for labour |

## 3 Why the two statements matter

The **statement of profit or loss** reports what the business earned or lost over a period. The owner uses it to compare years, judge whether drawings are affordable and show lenders or tax authorities how the business performed.

The **statement of financial position** lists assets, liabilities and capital **on a specified date**. It shows what the business owns and owes at that moment, and how much of the funding is the owner's. A profit figure alone cannot show whether the business can pay its debts.

## 4 Content of the statement of financial position

| Heading | Test | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Kept to use, not to resell, for over a year | Van, machinery |
| Intangible assets | Non-current, with no physical form | Goodwill |
| Current assets | Cash, or likely to become cash or be used up within a year | Inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank |
| Current liabilities | Payable within a year | Trade payables, other payables, overdraft |
| Non-current liabilities | Payable after more than a year | Bank loan repayable in five years |
| Capital | Amount owed to the owner | Opening capital + profit - drawings (+ any capital introduced) |

## 5 Year-end adjustments

**Depreciation.** Straight line: cost x rate, the same every year. Reducing balance: rate x carrying value at the start of the year (cost less accumulated depreciation). Revaluation: opening value + additions - closing value. Example: a trailer costing 14,000 at 10% straight line costs 1,400 a year; a machine with carrying value 9,000 at 30% reducing balance costs 2,700; tools valued 2,000, plus 400 bought, valued 1,700 at the end, give 700.

**Accruals and prepayments.** Match each item to the year it relates to.

| Item | Statement of profit or loss | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Expense owing (accrued) | Add | Other payables |
| Expense paid in advance | Deduct | Other receivables |
| Income earned, not received (accrued) | Add | Other receivables |
| Income received in advance | Deduct | Other payables |

**Irrecoverable debts.** Step 1: write the debt off (an expense) and deduct it from trade receivables. Step 2: allowance = percentage x remaining trade receivables. Step 3: the statement of profit or loss takes just the movement in the allowance, an expense if it rises and income if it falls. The statement of financial position deducts the whole allowance from trade receivables.

**Drawings.** Add to drawings (and remove from where it was charged): goods taken by the owner, valued at cost and deducted from purchases; other assets taken, at carrying value and deducted from that asset's cost and accumulated depreciation; private expenses the business paid, deducted from the expense.

## 7 Worked example: a manufacturing business

Hamid Yilmaz makes kitchen units. Year ended 30 September 2028. ## 8 The effect of a change in one balance

Every change reaches at least two places. Suppose Orla ignored the 900 prepaid insurance. Insurance would be 900 too high, so profit would be 900 too low (20,370). Other receivables, and so assets, would be 900 too low, and so would capital. The statement of financial position still balances while being wrong.

Other chains: higher closing inventory lowers cost of sales and raises profit and capital; a bigger depreciation charge lowers profit and carrying value; a larger allowance lowers profit and net trade receivables.

## Common errors

- Adding a prepayment to the expense instead of deducting it.
- Deducting the whole allowance, not just the increase, in the statement of profit or loss.
- Applying the reducing balance rate to cost rather than carrying value.
- Taking an asset into drawings at cost, not carrying value.
- Leaving a manufacturing business's three inventories out of current assets.
- Forgetting that every adjustment needs a place in both statements.
