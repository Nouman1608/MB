## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by project management. **[2]**

**2.** Explain **one** reason why a business uses a Gantt chart. **[3]**

**3.** Distinguish between total float and free float. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**4.** Coastline Fitness is opening a new gym. The activities, durations in days and the activities that must be completed first are shown.

| Activity | Task | Days | Must follow |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Lease and planning | 3 | none |
| B | Design layout | 4 | A |
| C | Build changing rooms | 6 | A |
| D | Order equipment | 2 | B |
| E | Fit floors and lighting | 5 | B, C |
| F | Recruit staff | 3 | C |
| G | Install equipment | 4 | D, E |
| H | Train staff | 2 | E, F |
| I | Open | 3 | G, H |

**(a)** Calculate the earliest start time (EST) of E and of G. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the latest finish time (LFT) of B and of F. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the total float and the free float of F. **[2]**

**(d)** Identify the critical path and state the duration of the project. **[2]**

**(e)** Activity B is delayed by 3 days. State the new duration of the project and the new critical path. **[2]**

**(f)** Evaluate the usefulness of critical path analysis to Coastline Fitness. **[8]**

**5.** Coastline's managers use three time estimates for the activities that are most uncertain.

| Activity | Optimistic | Most likely | Pessimistic |
|---|---|---|---|
| B | 3 | 4 | 11 |
| F | 2 | 3 | 10 |

**(a)** Calculate the expected time of B and of F. **[2]**

**(b)** The expected times of the other activities are A 3, C 6, D 2, E 4, G 4, H 2 and I 3 days. Using your answers to part (a), calculate the new project duration. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain **one** reason why PERT may be more useful than CPA for this project. **[3]**

**6.** Coastline's owner says, "If we follow the network, the gym will open on time." Evaluate the importance of qualitative factors in the management of the project. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Planning, organising and controlling the resources, people and time [1] needed to complete a one-off project on time, within budget and to the required quality [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A Gantt chart shows each activity's start, finish and duration on a time scale [1], so managers can see which activities overlap and the total length of the project [1], and can compare progress with the plan so that delays are noticed and action taken early [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Total float is the delay an activity can have without delaying the whole project [1]. Free float is the delay possible without affecting the earliest start of any following activity [1]. Free float cannot be more than total float [1]. **[3]**

**4. (a)** E follows B (finishes 7) and C (finishes 9), so EST = **9** [1]. G follows D (finishes 9) and E (finishes 14), so EST = **14** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The project lasts 21 days. LFT: I 21, G 18, H 18; E 14; D 14; B = smaller of D's latest start (12) and E's latest start (9) = **9** [1]. F = smaller of H's latest start (16) = **16** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** F: EST 9, duration 3, LFT 16. Total float = 16 − 9 − 3 = **4** [1]. Free float = EST of H (14) − earliest finish of F (12) = **2** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** Critical path: **A, C, E, G, I** [1]; duration 3 + 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 = **21 days** [1]. **[2]**

**(e)** B has 2 days of total float, so a 3-day delay makes the project 1 day longer: **22 days** [1]. The critical path becomes **A, B, E, G, I** (3 + 7 + 5 + 4 + 3 = 22) [1]. **[2]**

**(f)** Useful: it shows the minimum project length (21 days) and the critical activities, so managers know where delay matters [1]; the float shows that D, F and H have spare time, which allows staff to be moved to critical tasks [1]; it shows the effect of delays, such as a late builder, before they occur [1]; it helps with scheduling suppliers and staff and with giving the opening date to customers and the bank [1].
Limits: durations are estimates, and the plan is only as good as the data [1]; with 9 activities the network is simple, so the method may add little beyond a Gantt chart [1]; it ignores human and external factors such as weather or staff shortages [1].
Judgement: CPA is useful for the project because the opening date matters and several activities depend on one another, but it should be updated weekly and combined with attention to the risks [1]. **[8]**

**5. (a)** B: (3 + 16 + 11) ÷ 6 = 30 ÷ 6 = **5** [1]. F: (2 + 12 + 10) ÷ 6 = 24 ÷ 6 = **4** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** EST of B = 3 and of C = 3. B finishes at 8 and C at 9, so E starts at **9** and finishes at 13 [1]. D starts at 8 and finishes at 10, so G starts at the later of 10 and 13, which is **13**, and finishes at 17; F starts at 9 and finishes at 13, so H starts at 13 and finishes at 15 [1]. I starts at the later of G (17) and H (15), which is **17**, so the project lasts 17 + 3 = **20 days** [1]. **[3]**

**(c)** PERT uses three estimates, so it shows how uncertain an activity is [1]; B and F have wide gaps between the optimistic and pessimistic times, so a single figure of 4 or 3 days would hide the risk of delay [1], and the expected time of 5 and 4 days builds that risk into the plan, giving a more realistic schedule [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Importance: a network assumes that each activity takes its stated time, but a poor team, conflict or low morale can add days [1]; late deliveries by suppliers, such as equipment, could delay critical activities [1]; a clear leader and regular communication reduce errors and keep staff informed of changes [1]; external factors such as weather affecting building work or delays in planning permission could make the plan impossible [1]; pressure to meet a deadline may lower the quality of work, such as installing equipment unsafely [1].
Link to the case: the critical activities (the lease, the changing rooms, floors, equipment installation and opening) depend on builders and suppliers, so supplier reliability is especially important [1].
Evaluation: qualitative factors are important because they affect whether the numbers in the network are achieved, but they are harder to measure and control [1]. Managers can reduce the risk by choosing reliable suppliers, agreeing penalties for delay and holding short daily meetings on the critical tasks [1]. Judgement: the owner is too confident; the network is necessary but not sufficient, and the quality of management of people and suppliers determines whether the opening date is met [1]. **[9]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the smaller earliest finish when an activity has two predecessors.
- Confusing total float with free float, or using most likely time in PERT.
