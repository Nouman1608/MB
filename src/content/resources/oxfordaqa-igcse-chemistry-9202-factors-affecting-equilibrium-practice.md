This set practises section 3.8.2 Factors affecting equilibrium (statements a to e) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Section A checks definitions and single predictions; Section B uses unfamiliar reactions and invented data. The course is untiered.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by equilibrium for a reversible reaction in a closed system. **[2]**

**2.** Which statement about a reaction at equilibrium is correct?

A: The forward and backward reactions have stopped.

B: The amounts of reactants and products are always equal.

C: The amounts of reactants and products stay constant.

D: Only the forward reaction is taking place.

**[1]**

**3.** A reversible reaction is exothermic in the forward direction. State the effect on the yield of products of increasing the temperature. **[1]**

**4.** State how many gas molecules are on each side of this equation: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g). **[1]**

**5.** State the effect of a catalyst on the position of equilibrium and on the time taken to reach it. **[2]**

**6.** Explain why equilibrium cannot be reached if a gaseous product escapes from an open flask. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Nitrogen dioxide, NO₂, is a brown gas. It forms colourless dinitrogen tetroxide, N₂O₄:

2NO₂(g) ⇌ N₂O₄(g); the forward reaction is exothermic.

A sealed glass tube contains an equilibrium mixture of the two gases.

**(a)** The tube is placed in iced water. Predict the colour change and explain your answer. **[3]**

**(b)** The tube is then warmed to 80 °C. Predict the colour change. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why an increase in pressure increases the proportion of N₂O₄ in the mixture. **[2]**

**8.** Ethanol can be made from ethene and steam:

C₂H₄(g) + H₂O(g) ⇌ C₂H₅OH(g); the forward reaction is exothermic.

**(a)** State and explain the effect of increasing the pressure on the yield of ethanol. **[2]**

**(b)** State and explain the effect of increasing the temperature on the yield of ethanol. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest why a moderately high temperature is used even though it lowers the yield. **[1]**

**9.** For each reaction, state the effect on the yield of the products of increasing the pressure. Give a reason each time.

**(a)** 2HI(g) ⇌ H₂(g) + I₂(g) **[2]**

**(b)** N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g) **[2]**

**(c)** 2CO(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2CO₂(g) **[2]**

**10.** The table shows **invented data** for the percentage of product Z at equilibrium in the reaction W(g) ⇌ 2Z(g).

| Temperature / °C | % Z at 1 atm | % Z at 10 atm |
|---|---|---|
| 300 | 25 | 12 |
| 500 | 55 | 34 |
| 700 | 80 | 61 |

**(a)** Use the data to deduce whether the forward reaction is exothermic or endothermic. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why the percentage of Z is lower at 10 atm than at 1 atm. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest the temperature and pressure that would give the highest yield of Z. **[1]**

**11.** A sealed flask initially contains only hydrogen and iodine gases. They react to form hydrogen iodide: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g).

**(a)** Describe how the rate of the forward reaction changes from the start until equilibrium is reached. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the rate of the backward reaction changes over the same time. **[2]**

**(c)** State what is true about the two rates at equilibrium. **[1]**

**12.** A company makes a gas P by the reaction 2Q(g) + R(g) ⇌ 2P(g). The forward reaction is exothermic. Explain the conditions of temperature, pressure and catalyst the company should use. Your answer should consider yield, rate and cost. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The forward and backward reactions take place at exactly the same rate [1], so the amounts of all substances stay constant [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3.** The yield decreases [1].

**4.** 3 on the left and 2 on the right [1].

**5.** No effect on the position of equilibrium (the yield) [1]; equilibrium is reached in less time [1].

**6.** The product leaves, so the backward reaction cannot take place at the same rate as the forward reaction [1].

**7. (a)** The mixture becomes paler [1]. Cooling favours the exothermic direction [1], which is the forward reaction, so more colourless N₂O₄ forms [1].

**(b)** It becomes darker brown [1].

**(c)** There are 2 gas molecules on the left and 1 on the right [1]; increasing pressure favours the reaction that produces fewer molecules, the forward reaction [1].

**8. (a)** The yield increases [1], because there are 2 molecules of gas on the left and 1 on the right, and higher pressure favours fewer molecules [1].

**(b)** The yield decreases [1], because the forward reaction is exothermic and raising the temperature favours the endothermic (backward) reaction [1].

**(c)** At a low temperature the reaction would be too slow; a compromise gives an acceptable yield quickly [1].

**9. (a)** No effect [1]; 2 gas molecules on each side [1].

**(b)** Yield of NO₂ decreases [1]; the product side has more gas molecules (2 compared with 1) [1].

**(c)** Yield of CO₂ increases [1]; 3 gas molecules on the left, 2 on the right [1].

**10. (a)** The percentage of Z increases as temperature increases (25% → 80% at 1 atm) [1]. Higher temperature favours the endothermic direction [1], so the forward reaction is **endothermic** [1].

**(b)** There are more gas molecules on the right (2) than on the left (1) [1]; higher pressure favours the side with fewer molecules, the backward reaction [1].

**(c)** High temperature (700 °C or above) and low pressure (1 atm) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Quote figures from the table in part (a); "it goes up" without numbers usually loses the first mark.

**11. (a)** The forward rate is highest at the start [1] and decreases as hydrogen and iodine are used up [1].

**(b)** The backward rate starts at zero [1] and increases as hydrogen iodide builds up [1].

**(c)** They are equal [1].

**12.** Any six points, one mark each:

- The forward reaction is exothermic, so a low temperature gives a higher yield of P.
- But a low temperature makes the reaction too slow.
- So a compromise (moderate) temperature is used.
- There are 3 gas molecules on the left and 2 on the right, so a high pressure increases the yield of P.
- High pressure also increases the rate.
- But very high pressure is expensive (strong equipment, energy to compress) and less safe, so a moderately high pressure is chosen.
- A catalyst increases the rate so equilibrium is reached faster.
- A catalyst does not change the yield, but it allows a lower temperature to be used at a useful rate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying reactions stop at equilibrium.
- Applying the temperature rule without identifying the exothermic direction.
- Including a reason that is about rate when a question asks about yield, or the reverse.
- Counting atoms instead of molecules.
- Not quoting data in table-based questions.
- Saying a catalyst increases yield.
