These questions cover sub-section 3.6.2 Thermal physics of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards): thermal energy transfer, ideal gases and the molecular kinetic theory model. It is A-level only and assessed in Paper 2.

Revise from the [thermal physics study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-thermal-physics-aqa-alevel/) and [thermal physics revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-7408-thermal-physics-aqa-alevel-revision-notes/) first. Mixed questions on section 3.6 are in the [Further mechanics and thermal physics practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-further-mechanics-and-thermal-physics-practice/).

Data: R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹; N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹; k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹; specific heat capacity of water 4200 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹; of ice 2100 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹; of copper 390 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹; specific latent heat of fusion of ice 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹; of vaporisation of water 2.26 × 10⁶ J kg⁻¹.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the internal energy of a body. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why the temperature of boiling water stays constant even though energy is still being supplied. **[2]**

**3.** Describe Brownian motion and explain why it provided evidence for the existence of atoms. **[3]**

**4.** State three assumptions made in the molecular kinetic theory model of an ideal gas. **[3]**

**5.** Explain, in terms of molecules, why the pressure of a gas in a sealed rigid container rises when the gas is heated. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** Water flows through a cooling pipe at 0.050 kg s⁻¹. Its temperature rises by 12 K as it passes through.

**(a)** Calculate the rate at which the water removes energy. **[2]**
**(b)** The flow rate is halved but the same power is removed. State the new temperature rise. **[1]**

**7.** A 0.15 kg copper block at 95 °C is dropped into 0.20 kg of water at 20 °C in an insulated container.

**(a)** Calculate the final temperature. **[3]**
**(b)** State one assumption you made. **[1]**

**8.** A 0.050 kg ice cube is taken from a freezer at −18 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to turn it into water at 0 °C. **[3]**
**(b)** A microwave oven delivers 500 W to the ice. Calculate the minimum time this takes. **[1]**

**9.** A 2.4 kW kettle is left switched on for 60 s after the water starts boiling.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of water boiled away. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the actual mass boiled away is less than this. **[1]**

**10.** Air of volume 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ m³ at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa is compressed slowly to 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³. The temperature stays constant.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the compression must be slow. **[1]**
**(c)** In Required practical 8, state the graph you would plot to confirm Boyle's law and the shape you expect. **[2]**

**11.** A gas at a constant pressure of 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa expands from 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 3.5 × 10⁻³ m³. Its initial temperature is 300 K.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the final temperature. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the energy supplied by heating is greater than the work done by the gas. **[1]**

**12.** Argon has a molar mass of 0.040 kg mol⁻¹. A sample is at 400 K.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of one argon atom. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean kinetic energy of an atom. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the rms speed of the atoms. **[2]**
**(d)** Helium has a molar mass of 0.0040 kg mol⁻¹. Calculate the ratio of the rms speed of helium atoms to that of argon atoms at 400 K. **[2]**

**13.** A balloon holds 6.0 × 10⁻³ m³ of helium at 1.02 × 10⁵ Pa and 293 K. Treat helium as an ideal gas.

**(a)** Calculate the number of helium atoms. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the amount of helium in moles. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the internal energy of the helium. **[2]**

**14.** In the derivation of pV = ⅓Nm(c_rms)², a molecule of mass 5.3 × 10⁻²⁶ kg moving at 450 m s⁻¹ hits a wall at right angles and rebounds elastically.

**(a)** Calculate its change in momentum. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the factor ⅓ appears in the equation. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The sum of the randomly distributed kinetic energies [1] and potential energies of the particles in the body [1].

**2.** The energy is used to separate the molecules, increasing their potential energy [1]; their mean kinetic energy, and so the temperature, does not change [1].

**3.** Small visible particles, such as smoke in air, move randomly and jerkily [1]; they are hit unevenly by much smaller, invisible molecules [1]; this motion can only be explained if such molecules exist [1].

**4.** Any three, one mark each: many identical molecules in random motion; collisions are elastic; molecular volume negligible compared with the container; no forces between molecules except during collisions; collision time negligible [3].

**5.** Molecules gain kinetic energy and move faster [1]; they hit the walls more often [1]; and each collision gives a larger momentum change, so the force per unit area rises [1].
*Examiner insight:* Both effects (more frequent and harder collisions) are needed for full marks.

**6. (a)** P = (m/t)cΔθ = 0.050 × 4200 × 12 [1] = **2.5 × 10³ W** [1]
**(b)** **24 K** [1]

**7. (a)** 0.15 × 390 × (95 − T) = 0.20 × 4200 × (T − 20) [1]; 5557.5 + 16 800 = 898.5T [1]; T = **25 °C** (24.9 °C) [1]
**(b)** No energy is transferred to the container or the surroundings [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write the energy balance with (95 − T) and (T − 20) so both sides are positive.

**8. (a)** Warming ice: 0.050 × 2100 × 18 = 1890 J [1]; melting: 0.050 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 16 700 J [1]; total = **1.9 × 10⁴ J** [1]
**(b)** t = 18 590/500 = **37 s** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Ice starting below 0 °C must first warm to 0 °C; missing that step loses a mark.

**9. (a)** m = Pt/l = 2400 × 60/2.26 × 10⁶ [1] = **0.064 kg** [1]
**(b)** Some energy is transferred to the kettle and the surroundings rather than to the water [1].

**10. (a)** p₂ = p₁V₁/V₂ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 2.4 × 10⁻⁴/6.0 × 10⁻⁵ [1] = **4.0 × 10⁵ Pa** [1]
**(b)** Compressing quickly does work on the gas and raises its temperature; slow compression lets energy transfer to the surroundings so T stays constant [1].
**(c)** p against 1/V [1]; a straight line through the origin [1].

**11. (a)** W = pΔV = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **225 J** [1]
**(b)** V/T constant: T₂ = 300 × 3.5/2.0 [1] = **525 K** [1]
**(c)** The temperature rises, so the internal energy of the gas also increases [1].

**12. (a)** m = 0.040/6.02 × 10²³ = **6.6 × 10⁻²⁶ kg** [1]
**(b)** E_k = (3/2)kT = 1.5 × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 400 = **8.3 × 10⁻²¹ J** [1]
**(c)** c_rms = √(2E_k/m) = √(2 × 8.28 × 10⁻²¹/6.64 × 10⁻²⁶) [1] = **5.0 × 10² m s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Same T, so same mean E_k; c_rms ∝ 1/√m [1]; ratio = √(0.040/0.0040) = **3.2** [1]

**13. (a)** N = pV/kT = 1.02 × 10⁵ × 6.0 × 10⁻³/(1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 293) [1] = **1.5 × 10²³** [1]
**(b)** n = N/N_A = **0.25 mol** [1]
**(c)** U = (3/2)NkT = (3/2)pV [1] = 1.5 × 1.02 × 10⁵ × 6.0 × 10⁻³ = **920 J** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For an ideal monatomic gas, U = (3/2)pV is a quick check on the longer route through N.

**14. (a)** Δp = mu − (−mu) = 2mu [1] = 2 × 5.3 × 10⁻²⁶ × 450 = **4.8 × 10⁻²³ kg m s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** Each molecule's speed satisfies c² = u² + v² + w² [1]; motion is random, so the mean square components are equal and the mean u² = ⅓(c_rms)² [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using Celsius temperatures in gas law ratios.
- Forgetting to warm ice to 0 °C before melting it.
- Treating 2mu as mu in the momentum change at the wall.
- Describing pressure change with only one molecular effect.
- Confusing c_rms with mean speed or with E_k.
