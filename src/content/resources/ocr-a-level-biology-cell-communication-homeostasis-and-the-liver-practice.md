## Questions

**1.** Explain why a large multicellular animal needs communication systems. **[3]**

**2.** Describe how cells communicate by signalling.
**(a)** Between adjacent cells, using a named example. **[2]**
**(b)** Between distant cells, using a named example. **[2]**

**3.** State the difference between a receptor and an effector, and name one receptor and one effector involved in controlling temperature in a human. **[3]**

**4.** Distinguish between negative feedback and positive feedback, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**5.** The table shows body temperature in a lizard and a mouse as air temperature was raised from 15 °C to 35 °C over one hour.

| Time (min) | 0 | 30 | 60 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lizard (°C) | 15.5 | 26.0 | 34.0 |
| Mouse (°C) | 37.1 | 37.0 | 37.3 |

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of change of the lizard's body temperature over the hour, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** State the range of the mouse's body temperature. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain the difference between the two animals. **[3]**

**6.** In a warm room the diameter of a skin arteriole in a volunteer is 0.30 mm. After moving to a cold room it is 0.18 mm.
**(a)** Find the percentage change in the diameter, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the change helps to maintain core temperature. **[3]**

**7.** A runner loses 0.45 kg of sweat in one hour and all of it evaporates. Assume that evaporation of 1.0 g of sweat removes 2.4 kJ of heat.
**(a)** Calculate the heat removed in the hour, in kJ. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mean rate of heat removal in watts (1 W = 1 J s⁻¹). **[1]**

**8.** A person runs in hot weather. Describe and explain the physiological and behavioural responses that help to keep the core temperature near its set point. **[6]**

**9.** A drawing of a liver lobule is 54 mm wide. The lobule is 1.2 mm wide in the tissue.
**(a)** Work out how many times larger the drawing is than the real lobule. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the structure of a lobule as seen in a stained section. **[4]**

**10.** Explain the roles of the liver in:
**(a)** storing glycogen **[2]**
**(b)** detoxification, using ethanol as an example. **[3]**

**11.** Concentrations in blood entering and leaving the liver of a healthy person (mmol dm⁻³):

| Substance | Hepatic portal vein | Hepatic vein |
|---|---|---|
| Ammonia | 0.24 | 0.03 |
| Urea | 3.1 | 3.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage decrease in ammonia concentration. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in urea concentration, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain these changes. **[3]**

**12.** Excretion is important for metabolism and homeostasis.
**(a)** Define excretion. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why carbon dioxide and nitrogenous waste must be removed from the body. **[4]**
**(c)** Describe how the liver forms urea. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Cells are specialised and many are far from the outside, so they cannot detect changes themselves [1]. The animal must respond to changes in the external environment, such as temperature or predators, and the internal environment, such as blood glucose [1]. Organs must be coordinated, for example heart rate and breathing rate rising together during exercise [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain" needs a reason, not just a list, so link each point to what would go wrong without communication.

**2. (a)** A neurone releases neurotransmitter into the synapse [1], which binds to receptors on the next cell [1]. (Gap junctions or T helper cell contact are alternatives.) **(b)** An endocrine gland secretes a hormone into the blood [1], which reaches all cells but only target cells with a complementary receptor respond [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the signal molecule and the receptor. Saying only "a message is sent" does not describe signalling.

**3.** A receptor detects a stimulus, whereas an effector carries out a response [1]. Receptor: a temperature receptor in the skin (or the hypothalamus) [1]. Effector: a sweat gland, skin arteriole muscle or skeletal muscle [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick a specific structure. "The brain" is too vague, and the hypothalamus is a coordination centre.

**4.** Negative feedback: the response reverses the change and returns the factor towards its set point [1]. Example: sweating when core temperature is too high [1]. Positive feedback: the response increases the change [1]. Example: platelets attracting more platelets at a wound (or oxytocin and uterine contractions in birth) [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Define the term in terms of the direction of the change, and then give the example, since a correct example alone does not show you know the difference.

**5. (a)** Change = 34.0 − 15.5 = 18.5 °C [1]. Rate = 18.5 ÷ 60 = **0.31 °C per minute** [1]. **(b)** 37.3 − 37.0 = **0.3 °C** [1]. **(c)** The lizard is an ectotherm and gains heat from its surroundings, so its temperature follows the air temperature [1]. The mouse is an endotherm [1] and uses negative feedback, such as changing blood flow through skin arterioles and shivering or behaviour, to hold its core temperature near the set point [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Write out the subtraction and the division, then give the unit and the requested significant figures.

**6. (a)** Change = 0.18 − 0.30 = −0.12 mm [1]. Percentage change = −0.12 ÷ 0.30 × 100 = **−40%** [1]. **(b)** The narrower arteriole is vasoconstriction, caused by contraction of smooth muscle in its wall [1]. Less blood flows into skin capillaries [1]. Less heat is lost from the skin to the surroundings, so core temperature is maintained [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say that arterioles change diameter. Writing that capillaries move, or that blood vessels "close", is imprecise.

**7. (a)** Mass = 450 g [1]. Heat = 450 × 2.4 = **1080 kJ** [1]. **(b)** 1 080 000 J ÷ 3600 s = **300 W** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert units before dividing: kJ to J and hours to seconds. Sweat that runs off the skin without evaporating removes less heat than the question assumes.

**8.** Muscle respiration releases heat and core temperature rises [1]. Receptors in the skin and the hypothalamus detect the rise [1]. The hypothalamus sends impulses to effectors [1]. Skin arterioles dilate, so more heat is lost by radiation [1]. Sweat glands secrete sweat, and evaporation removes heat from the skin [1]. Behavioural: slow down, take shade, remove clothing or drink water [1]. This is negative feedback, as the temperature returns towards the set point. Metabolic rate reduction or erector pili hairs lying flat are also acceptable. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer follows the sequence stimulus, receptor, coordinator, effector, response. Marks are lost when responses are listed with no explanation of why each lowers temperature.

**9. (a)** Magnification = image size ÷ actual size [1]. = 54 ÷ 1.2 = **×45** [1]. **(b)** A roughly hexagonal unit with a central vein (a branch of the hepatic vein) [1]. At the edges, branches of the hepatic artery, hepatic portal vein and bile duct [1]. Sinusoids run between rows of hepatocytes towards the central vein [1]. Kupffer cells lie in the sinusoid walls (or hepatocytes have a large, round nucleus) [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use matching units in magnification calculations. In a description of tissue, state where each structure is, since a list of names alone gives little.

**10. (a)** When blood glucose is high, hepatocytes convert glucose to glycogen [1]. Glycogen is broken down to glucose when blood glucose falls [1]. **(b)** Hepatocytes contain enzymes that break down ethanol [1] to ethanal and then ethanoate [1]. This turns a toxic substance into less harmful ones, which can be used or removed [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the product of each step. "Detoxifies it" without the chemical change gains little.

**11. (a)** Decrease = 0.24 − 0.03 = 0.21 [1]. 0.21 ÷ 0.24 × 100 = **87.5%** [1]. **(b)** Increase = 3.9 − 3.1 = 0.8 [1]. 0.8 ÷ 3.1 × 100 = **25.8%** [1]. **(c)** Hepatocytes take up ammonia from the blood [1]. They combine it with carbon dioxide in the ornithine cycle to form urea [1]. The urea enters the hepatic vein, so its concentration there is higher [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value. For "explain", link each change to a process in the hepatocyte.

**12. (a)** Getting rid of metabolic waste products from the body [1]. **(b)** Carbon dioxide dissolves to form an acid and lowers blood pH [1]. This changes the shape and activity of enzymes [1]. Nitrogenous waste starts as ammonia, which is toxic [1]. It must be converted to urea and removed to keep conditions suitable for metabolism [1]. **(c)** Excess amino acids are deaminated in the liver, releasing ammonia [1]. Ammonia reacts with carbon dioxide in the ornithine cycle to form urea [1]. Urea is carried in the blood to the kidneys [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Say "metabolic" waste in the definition, since "waste" alone could include undigested food.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing a feedback loop without naming its receptor and effector.
- Writing that the hypothalamus itself sweats or shivers.
- Writing "vasoconstriction of capillaries" instead of arterioles.
- Forgetting that sweat cools only when it evaporates.
- Using the final value, not the original, as the divisor in a percentage change.
- Not converting units in magnification and power calculations.
- Saying positive feedback keeps conditions stable.
- Omitting carbon dioxide when describing how urea forms.
- Mixing up the vessels supplying the liver.
