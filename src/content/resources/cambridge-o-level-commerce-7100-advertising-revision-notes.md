Condensed notes for sub-topic 5.1, Advertising: types, media, appeals and sales promotion.

## Four facts to start with

1. Advertising **informs** and **persuades** customers.
2. Types: informative, persuasive, collective (generic) and competitive.
3. Media can be **traditional** or **digital**.
4. **Sales promotion** is a short-term activity to boost sales.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Advertising | Paid communication to inform and persuade customers |
| Informative advertising | Gives facts about a product |
| Persuasive advertising | Tries to change attitudes and create desire |
| Collective (generic) advertising | Promotes a type of product on behalf of a group of producers |
| Competitive advertising | Compares or competes with rival products |
| Method of appeal | Approach used in an advert to influence the audience |
| Sales promotion | Short-term activity encouraging purchase |
| Loss leader | Product sold at or below cost to attract customers |
| Influencer | Person with a large social media following who promotes products |

## Types of advertising

| Type | Key point |
|---|---|
| Informative | Facts: price, features |
| Persuasive | Changes attitudes |
| Collective | Whole product type, paid by group |
| Competitive | Against rivals |

## Media

**Traditional:** television, radio, cinema, newspapers (local and national), magazines / trade journals, leaflets / flyers, packaging, direct mail, sponsorship, catalogues / brochures, posters, transport vehicles, window displays, point of sale displays, signboards, word of mouth.

**Digital:** social media advertising, other internet advertising (websites, search engines), display advertisements, video advertisements, email marketing, digital billboards, social media influencers.

## Factors affecting choice of medium

Audience; cost; cultural and legal considerations; flexibility to change; local and international coverage; multi-channel advertising; timing; type of product.

## Methods of appeal

Emotion; fear; humour; rational; ambition; social acceptance; romantic; adventure; music; endorsement by celebrities and influencers.

| Appeal | Matches |
|---|---|
| Fear | Insurance, security |
| Rational | Computers, savings |
| Humour | Snacks, drinks |
| Romantic | Perfume, jewellery |
| Endorsement | Sports shoes, make-up |

## Sales promotion

Point of sale; special offers; sponsorship; displays; competitions; coupons; sales periods; loss leaders; free gifts; free samples.

Factors: business type, competitors, cost, nature of product, new or old product, timing.

## Worked examples

**Cost per person.** Television $12,000 for 400,000 people = $0.03 each. Social media $3,000 for 150,000 people = $0.02 each.

**Discount.** 20% off $60 = $12 off, price $48. Buy one get one free on $60 jackets = $30 each.

**Loss leader.** Cost $0.80, sold at $0.70: loss $0.10 per tin; 5,000 tins = $500 loss.

## Model answers to learn

**Why use television for a national launch?** It reaches a very large audience with sound and moving pictures, so many people hear about the product at the same time. It is expensive, so it suits products with a large market.

**Why do shops use loss leaders?** The cheap product attracts customers, who then buy other goods at normal prices, so overall profit can rise.

## Choosing media: quick matches

| Situation | Sensible choice | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Local bakery opening | Leaflets, local newspaper, signboard | Cheap and reaches nearby customers |
| National launch of a snack | Television, social media | Large audience, strong visual impact |
| Machinery for factories | Trade journals, brochures | Reaches the buyers in that industry |
| Fashion for teenagers | Social media, influencers | Where the audience spends time |
| Short sale ending Friday | Digital billboards, email, social media | Messages can be changed or sent quickly |

## Developing an advertising point

| Weak | Developed |
|---|---|
| "Television is good" | "Television reaches millions at once with sound and moving pictures, so a national launch is seen quickly" |
| "Leaflets are cheap" | "Leaflets are cheap to print and can be posted through doors near the shop, so they reach local customers" |
| "Influencers help" | "An influencer's followers trust their opinion, so a recommendation can persuade them to buy" |

## Exam traps

- **Informative and persuasive swapped.**
- **Sales promotion as long-term.**
- **Appeal not matched to product.**
- **Medium listed with no reason.**
- **Discount worked on the wrong price.**
- **Collective advertising as one brand.**

## Self-test

1. Name the four types of advertising.
2. Give two traditional media.
3. Give two digital media.
4. What is word of mouth?
5. State three factors affecting choice of advertising method.
6. Name two methods of appeal.
7. What is a loss leader?
8. What is a free sample?
9. Why is a fear appeal suited to insurance?
10. Calculate 25% off $80.

**Answers**

1. Informative, persuasive, collective (generic), competitive.
2. Any two from television, radio, cinema, newspapers, magazines, leaflets, packaging, direct mail, sponsorship, catalogues, posters, transport vehicles, window displays, point of sale displays, signboards, word of mouth.
3. Any two from social media advertising, websites and search engines, display advertisements, video advertisements, email marketing, digital billboards, influencers.
4. Customers telling others about a product.
5. Any three: audience, cost, cultural and legal considerations, flexibility, coverage, multi-channel, timing, type of product.
6. Any two from emotion, fear, humour, rational, ambition, social acceptance, romantic, adventure, music, endorsement.
7. A product sold at or below cost to attract customers who then buy other goods.
8. A small amount of the product given away to try.
9. It shows the risks of not being insured, so people buy protection.
10. 25% of 80 = $20, so the price is $60.

## Command words to expect

| Command word | What to write |
|---|---|
| Identify, State, Give | A short answer with no explanation |
| Define | A precise meaning |
| Describe | The main points or features |
| Explain | A reason, linked to the situation, with a development |
| Analyse | Break the issue into parts and show how they connect |
| Assess, Consider | A reasoned judgement about importance or effect |
| Discuss | Both sides of an issue, in a structured way |
| Justify | A case backed with evidence |
| Evaluate | A judgement of value, ending with a conclusion |
