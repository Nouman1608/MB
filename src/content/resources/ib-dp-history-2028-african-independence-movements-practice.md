They are not reproduced exam questions, and the real Paper 3 has essay questions only and requires one prescribed movement. Source extracts are constructed for practice. ## Section A: short answers

**1.** Explain one reason why ex-servicemen played an important part in the emergence of nationalism in the Gold Coast. **[3]**

**2.** Identify two reasons why Guinea became independent earlier than most French territories. **[2]**

**3.** State two forms of foreign support received by the FLN or by SWAPO. **[2]**

## Section B: source and argument

*Constructed for practice, not a real source:* "Nobody gave Africa its independence. Britain and France left when staying cost more than going, and the people who made it cost more were the ones who organised the strikes, the boycotts and the votes." (a historian's view, paraphrased)

**4.** Explain the argument made in this extract. **[3]**

**5.** Explain one way in which this view might be criticised. **[3]**

**6.** Examine the reasons for divisions within the pro-independence movements in Angola. **[6]**

## Section C: essay questions

**7.** To what extent was armed struggle more important than non-violent methods in achieving independence in one prescribed country? **[15]**

**8.** Evaluate the view that the Cold War was the most significant external reason for the emergence of independence movements. **[15]**

## Answers

**1.** One mark for the point that servicemen had served overseas in the Second World War and seen colonial claims of superiority questioned; one for post-war unemployment, low pensions and rising prices; one for a link to action, for example the 1948 march to the Governor's castle in Accra and the shooting that led to riots, which helped bring Nkrumah to national politics. **[3]**

**2.** One mark each for two of: the 1958 referendum on de Gaulle's constitution, in which Guinea voted "No" by a large margin; the strength of Sékou Touré's party and unions; the willingness of Touré to take the risk of immediate independence; de Gaulle's decision to accept the result and withdraw. **[2]**

**3.** One mark each for two of: Egyptian, Moroccan and Tunisian bases and weapons for the FLN; Soviet and Cuban weapons and training for SWAPO; bases in Zambia and Angola; diplomatic support from the UN General Assembly and African states. **[2]**

**4.** One mark for the claim that independence was caused by cost to colonial powers; one for the role of African organisers (strikes, boycotts, elections) in raising that cost; one for the rejection of the idea that independence was a gift. **[3]**

**5.** One mark for a criticism, up to two for development. For example: the view neglects the influence of international events such as the Second World War, the United Nations and the Cold War; Britain had its own plans for gradual decolonisation in some colonies; the claim that independence was never a gift leaves out negotiations, such as Tanganyika's cooperative transition. **[3]**

**6.** Up to 6 marks. Credit reasons: ideological differences (the Marxist MPLA, the more conservative FNLA, UNITA's Maoist and nationalist mix); regional and ethnic bases (Mbundu and Luanda, Kongo in the north, Ovimbundu in the centre and south); personal rivalries between Neto, Roberto and Savimbi; and outside powers backing different groups (the USSR and Cuba for the MPLA; the US, South Africa and China for the others). Credit the Alvor Agreement of January 1975, which failed. Level guide: 1 to 2 for description, 3 to 4 for partial examination, 5 to 6 for balanced examination with a conclusion. **[6]**

**7.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and defining the criteria (pressure on the colonial power, popular support, negotiation, timing);
- up to 4 for evidence of armed struggle (for example Algeria's war from 1954, de Gaulle's return and Evian; Angola's guerrilla war and the 1974 coup; SWAPO's PLAN and the 1988 agreements);
- up to 4 for non-violent methods and other factors (strikes, elections, negotiation, UN diplomacy, colonial weakness, the Cold War);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, for example that armed struggle was more important where settlers or strategic interests were strong, and that the two methods often worked together. **[15]**

**8.** Marking points, up to 15 in total:
- up to 3 for understanding the claim and the Cold War as an external reason;
- up to 4 for evidence in favour (superpower anti-colonial rhetoric, support for the MPLA and SWAPO, Western fear of communism shaping responses, the role of the UN);
- up to 4 for other external reasons (the spread of ideas such as Pan-Africanism, the success of Ghana and Algeria, the weakness of Britain, France and Portugal after 1945, Suez and Dien Bien Phu);
- up to 4 for evaluation and judgement, which may conclude that the Cold War shaped the form and timing of independence in Angola and Namibia but was less important in Ghana and Tanganyika. **[15]**

## Model paragraph and feedback

Here is a model paragraph for Question 7, using Algeria, followed by points on how to improve a draft.

*Model paragraph:* "The FLN began its war against French rule on 1 November 1954 because other methods had failed, and by 1962 France had agreed at Evian to Algerian independence. This suggests that armed struggle was decisive. The war cost France enormously, divided the army and the settlers from the government, and brought de Gaulle back to power in 1958. However, the FLN was often outmatched on the battlefield, and the Battle of Algiers was won by the French army. What mattered was the political effect of a long war and of the use of torture on opinion in France and abroad. This essay argues that armed struggle was the most important single method but that it worked through political and diplomatic effects."

*Feedback points:*

- The paragraph separates military results from political results, which is a strong way to evaluate armed struggle.
- Give the date of every campaign you mention.
- Use the second half of the essay to examine non-violent methods fairly.
- Always conclude with a judgement that refers back to the words of the question.
