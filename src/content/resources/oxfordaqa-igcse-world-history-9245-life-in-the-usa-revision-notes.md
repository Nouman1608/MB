**Condensed notes on the Paper 2 Section A option "Life in the USA, 1920-1968".** The specification has three parts: "American people and the 'Boom' of the 1920s", "Bust - Americans' experiences of the Depression and New Deal, 1929-1945" and "Post-war America, 1945-68". ## The Boom of the 1920s

- **Causes:** laissez-faire Republican governments, low taxes, tariffs; mass production (Ford); new industries; credit; advertising.
- **Hire purchase:** buying in instalments.
- **Stock market:** share buying on the margin.
- **Not everyone gained:** farmers, coal and textile workers, African Americans, immigrants.

## Social and cultural change

| Area | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cinema and jazz | Hollywood, talking films, radio, Harlem |
| Women | Vote in 1920; flappers; jobs as typists and operators; traditional values continued; Sheppard-Towner Act (1921) |

| Fighting prejudice | NAACP (courts, anti-lynching); UNIA (Garvey, black pride) |

## The Depression and New Deal

- **Depression:** about 25 per cent unemployment by 1933; bank failures; farm foreclosures; Dust Bowl; Hoovervilles.
- **Hoover:** rugged individualism; limited help; Bonus Army.
- **Roosevelt:** bank holiday; **fireside chats**; "alphabet agencies".

| Agency | Purpose |
|---|---|
| CCC | Jobs for young men in conservation |
| AAA | Farm prices |
| TVA | Dams and electricity |
| WPA | Public works jobs |
| Social Security Act | Pensions, unemployment insurance, help for disabled people |
| Wagner Act | Union rights |

**Limits:** unemployment still high in 1938; African Americans, women, Indigenous peoples and tenant farmers gained less.

**Opposition:** Supreme Court (NRA and AAA struck down); Republicans (too much government); radicals such as Huey Long (not enough).

## The Second World War

- War production ended mass unemployment.
- Lend-Lease; exports.
- Women in factories.
- African Americans move to defence work; discrimination in defence industries banned in 1941; riots in Detroit, 1943.
- Internment of about 120,000 Japanese Americans.

## Exam traps

- Not everyone gained from the boom.
- The Crash exposed existing weaknesses; it was not the only cause of the Depression.
- Hoover and Roosevelt took different approaches.
- The New Deal helped but did not end the Depression.
- Brown (1954), Montgomery (1955-56) and Little Rock (1957) are different events.
- Civil rights success involved many people and organisations.

## Self-test

1. Why did mass production help the boom?
2. What was hire purchase?
3. What did the National Origins Act do?
4. What was the Red Scare?
5. Why was Prohibition difficult to enforce?
6. What was the Bonus Army?
7. What were fireside chats?
8. Name two New Deal agencies and their purposes.
9. What did the Supreme Court do to the NRA and AAA?
10. What happened to Japanese Americans after Pearl Harbor?
11. What did Brown v. Board of Education decide?
12. Name two Acts that Johnson passed on civil rights.

## Answers

1. It lowered the cost of goods such as cars, so more families could afford them, and it created jobs.
2. Buying goods by paying in instalments.
3. It restricted immigration to a small percentage of the numbers from each country already in the USA, favouring northern Europeans.
4. Fear of communists and anarchists after the Russian Revolution, with the Palmer Raids.
5. Many people wanted alcohol, speakeasies flourished, gangsters ran the trade and police were corrupt.
6. First World War veterans who marched to Washington in 1932 to demand early payment of a bonus; the army cleared them.
7. Radio talks in which Roosevelt explained policy directly to the people.
8. Any two, for example the CCC (jobs for young men) and the TVA (dams and electricity).
9. It ruled both unconstitutional.
10. About 120,000 were forced into internment camps.
11. That segregated schools were unconstitutional.
12. The Civil Rights Act of 1964 and the Voting Rights Act of 1965.
