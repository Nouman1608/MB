## Equations to learn

| Quantity | Equation |
|---|---|
| Concentration (mol/dm³) | moles ÷ volume (dm³) |
| Moles in a solution | concentration (mol/dm³) × volume (dm³) |
| Concentration (g/dm³) | concentration (mol/dm³) × Mᵣ, or mass (g) ÷ volume (dm³) |
| Volume conversion | dm³ = cm³ ÷ 1000 |
| Gas volume at rtp (dm³) | moles × 24 |
| Moles of gas at rtp | volume (dm³) ÷ 24, or volume (cm³) ÷ 24 000 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Concentration | Amount of solute in a given volume of solution |
| Solute | The substance dissolved |
| Titration | Method to find the exact volumes of acid and alkali that react |
| Titre | Volume of solution added from the burette to reach the end-point |
| End-point | The point at which the indicator just changes colour permanently |
| Concordant | Titres within 0.10 cm³ of each other |
| Rough titration | A fast first run to find the approximate end-point |
| Molar gas volume | 24 dm³: the volume of one mole of any gas at room temperature and pressure |
| rtp | Room temperature and pressure |

## Required practical in brief

1. Pipette 25.0 cm³ of alkali (known concentration) into a conical flask on a white tile.
2. Add a few drops of a single indicator with a sharp colour change (not universal indicator).
3. Fill a burette with the acid; record the start reading at eye level, bottom of the meniscus.
4. Rough titration, then accurate runs adding acid drop by drop near the end-point, swirling.
5. Record the end reading; repeat until results are concordant.
6. Mean of concordant titres only; then calculate.

Rinse the pipette with alkali and the burette with acid, not with water.

## Titration calculation in four steps

1. Moles known = c × V(dm³).
2. Use the mole ratio from the equation.
3. Moles unknown.
4. c unknown = moles ÷ V(dm³); × Mᵣ for g/dm³.

| Acid–alkali pair | Ratio acid : alkali |
|---|---|
| HCl + NaOH, HNO₃ + KOH | 1 : 1 |
| H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH, H₂SO₄ + 2KOH | 1 : 2 |

## Worked mini-example in g/dm³

25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ potassium hydroxide is neutralised by a mean titre of 20.00 cm³ of hydrochloric acid (Mᵣ HCl = 36.5).

1. Moles KOH = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 0.00250 mol.
2. Ratio 1 : 1, so 0.00250 mol HCl.
3. Concentration = 0.00250 ÷ 0.0200 = 0.125 mol/dm³.
4. In g/dm³: 0.125 × 36.5 = **4.56 g/dm³**.

## Choosing the mean titre

Example results (cm³): rough 26.30; then 25.70, 25.95, 25.80, 25.75.

- Ignore the rough.
- Concordant set: 25.70, 25.80, 25.75 (all within 0.10 cm³). Leave out 25.95.
- Mean = (25.70 + 25.80 + 25.75) ÷ 3 = **25.75 cm³**.

## Gas volume reminders

- Same number of moles of any gas, same volume (at the same temperature and pressure).
- Equation coefficients give volume ratios for gases: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ means 10 cm³ of nitrogen reacts with 30 cm³ of hydrogen to give 20 cm³ of ammonia.
- 0.25 mol of any gas = 6.0 dm³ at rtp; 1.2 dm³ of any gas = 0.050 mol.

## Exam traps

- Leaving volumes in cm³ when using c = n ÷ V.
- Averaging every titre, including the rough one.
- Using 1 : 1 for sulfuric acid with a Group 1 hydroxide.
- Applying 24 dm³ to a solution or a solid; it is for gases only.
- Using the acid's volume with the alkali's concentration.
- Reporting a titre to one decimal place when the burette reads to 0.05 cm³; record two decimal places, such as 22.50.
- Using universal indicator in a titration.

## Quick self-test

1. Convert 35.0 cm³ to dm³.
2. 0.0500 mol of solute is dissolved in 500 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration.
3. Convert 0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid into g/dm³. (Mᵣ HCl = 36.5)
4. How many moles are in 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide?
5. Name the piece of apparatus used to measure exactly 25.0 cm³ of alkali.
6. Why is a rough titration done?
7. Titres: 24.20, 24.55, 24.25, 24.15 cm³. Which are concordant and what is the mean?
8. 25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol/dm³ NaOH is neutralised by 20.0 cm³ of HCl. Find the HCl concentration.
9. 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ NaOH is neutralised by 25.0 cm³ of H₂SO₄. Find the H₂SO₄ concentration.
10. What volume does 0.30 mol of carbon dioxide occupy at rtp?
11. How many moles of oxygen are in 600 cm³ at rtp?
12. Why should the burette not be rinsed with water just before filling with acid?

### Answers

1. 0.0350 dm³
2. 0.0500 ÷ 0.500 = 0.100 mol/dm³
3. 0.200 × 36.5 = 7.30 g/dm³
4. 0.150 × 0.0200 = 0.00300 mol
5. A (volumetric) pipette with a pipette filler.
6. To find the approximate end-point so later runs can be done drop by drop near it.
7. 24.20, 24.25 and 24.15; mean = 24.20 cm³.
8. 0.00250 mol ÷ 0.0200 dm³ = 0.125 mol/dm³
9. Moles NaOH 0.00500; H₂SO₄ 0.00250; 0.00250 ÷ 0.0250 = 0.100 mol/dm³
10. 0.30 × 24 = 7.2 dm³
11. 600 ÷ 24 000 = 0.0250 mol
12. Water left in the burette would dilute the acid, so a larger titre would be needed.
