## Core facts

- Greenhouse gases keep Earth warm enough for life: **water vapour, carbon dioxide, methane**.
- Human activities **increase carbon dioxide and methane**.
- Many scientists, using **peer-reviewed evidence**, believe this will raise surface temperatures and cause
  **global climate change**.
- Climate is hard to model, so the evidence base has **uncertainties**.

## The greenhouse effect

1. **Short wavelength** radiation from the Sun passes through the atmosphere.
2. The Earth's surface **absorbs** it and warms.
3. The surface **emits long wavelength infrared** radiation.
4. Greenhouse gases **absorb** some of it.
5. They **re-radiate** it in all directions, some back to the surface, so the Earth is warmer.

More greenhouse gas → more absorbed and returned → higher average temperature.

## Human sources

| Gas | Activities (know two each) |
|---|---|
| Carbon dioxide | Burning fossil fuels; deforestation; making cement |
| Methane | Cattle farming; rice paddies; landfill sites; gas leaks |

## Effects of climate change (describe four)

| Effect | Detail |
|---|---|
| Sea level rise | Ice melts and water expands; coastal flooding |
| Extreme weather | More storms, floods, droughts, heatwaves |
| Rainfall changes | Wetter and drier regions; water shortages |
| Habitat and species loss | Species cannot adapt or move quickly enough |
| Crop yield changes | Different regions suit different crops |

Discussion words: **scale** (how many people or how large an area), **risk** (what could go wrong and how likely),
**environmental implications** (what happens to habitats and ecosystems).

## Evaluating evidence

| Question | Good evidence | Weak evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Source | Peer-reviewed journal | Unchecked claim |
| Data | Many sites, many years | One town, one year |
| Bias | Independent | Written to support a view or product |
| Type | Measurements | Opinion |
| Communication | Clear and balanced | Uses part of the data |

**Peer review** means other scientists check the work before it is published.

## Carbon footprint

- **Definition:** the total amount of **carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases** emitted over the **full life
  cycle** of a product, service or event.
- Reduce it by **reducing emissions of carbon dioxide and methane**.

| Action | Why it reduces emissions |
|---|---|
| Renewables or nuclear | No carbon dioxide from burning fuel |
| Energy efficiency | Less fuel needed |
| Public transport, walking, cycling | Fewer fuel-burning vehicles |
| Planting trees | Photosynthesis takes in carbon dioxide |
| Carbon capture and storage | Gas is stored underground |
| Capturing landfill methane, changing livestock management | Less methane released |

**Limits on actions:** cost, lifestyle change, lack of international agreement, technology limits, reliance on
fossil fuels in developing countries.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Greenhouse gas | Gas that absorbs and re-radiates long wavelength radiation |
| Greenhouse effect | Warming of the Earth caused by these gases |
| Climate change | Long-term change in weather patterns |
| Peer review | Checking of research by other scientists before publication |
| Carbon footprint | Total greenhouse gases emitted over a life cycle |
| Deforestation | Large-scale removal of forests |

## Exam traps

- **Ozone confusion.** The greenhouse effect is not the ozone hole.
- **Reflecting.** Greenhouse gases absorb and re-radiate infrared; they do not reflect sunlight.
- **One activity only.** Give two for each gas.
- **"Trap heat"** with no mention of radiation.
- **Evidence comments without detail.** Say sample size, time scale or peer review.
- **Carbon footprint = carbon dioxide only.** It includes other greenhouse gases.
- **No limits given.** For "give reasons why actions may be limited", name cost, attitudes or agreement.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the three greenhouse gases in the specification.
2. State two human activities that increase carbon dioxide.
3. State two human activities that increase methane.
4. Describe the greenhouse effect using wavelength.
5. Describe four potential effects of climate change.
6. What does peer review mean?
7. Give two uncertainties in climate change evidence.
8. Define carbon footprint.
9. Describe two ways to reduce a carbon footprint.
10. Give two reasons why actions to cut emissions may be limited.
11. A report on climate change is based on one year of data from one town. Evaluate the evidence.
12. Explain why planting trees reduces atmospheric carbon dioxide.

### Answers

1. Water vapour, carbon dioxide and methane.
2. Burning fossil fuels; deforestation.
3. Cattle farming; rice paddies; landfill sites (any two).
4. Short wavelength radiation passes in and warms the surface; the surface emits long wavelength infrared; greenhouse gases absorb it and re-radiate in all directions, some back to Earth.
5. Any four: sea level rise; extreme weather; changes in rainfall; loss of habitats/species; changes in crop yields.
6. Other scientists check the research before it is published.
7. Complex systems are hard to model; natural variation; limited long-term data; media bias.
8. The total carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases emitted over the full life cycle of a product, service or event.
9. Any two: use renewable energy; improve energy efficiency; use public transport; plant trees.
10. Cost; people unwilling to change lifestyle; lack of international agreement.
11. Poor evidence: too short a period and one place, not peer reviewed, so it cannot show a global long-term trend.
12. Trees take in carbon dioxide for photosynthesis, which removes it from the air.
