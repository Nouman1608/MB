## Formulas and definitions

| Statement | Result | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| 3.3.1(a) | Work done = energy transferred by a force | joule, J = N m |
| 3.3.1(b) | W = Fx cos θ, with θ between F and the displacement | J |
| 3.3.2(a) | Ek = ½mv² | J |
| 3.3.2(b) | Ep = mgh (ΔEp = mgΔh) in a uniform field | J |
| 3.3.3(a) | P = W/t | watt, W = J s⁻¹ |
| 3.3.3(b) | P = Fv | W |
| 3.3.3(c) | efficiency = useful output energy / total input energy × 100% | none |

Definition to learn: the joule is the work done by a force of one newton that moves its point of application one metre along the line of the force. One watt is one joule transferred per second.

## 3.3.1 Work and conservation of energy

- Work done is a scalar. Only the component of the force along the displacement does work.
- θ = 0°: work is Fx. θ = 90°: work is zero. θ above 90°: work is negative, so the force removes energy from the object.
- Principle of conservation of energy: the total energy of an isolated system is constant, because energy is only ever moved between forms.
- Energy transferred = work done by the force.
- Resistive forces transfer energy to thermal energy of the surroundings. The energy is dissipated, not destroyed.

Method for an energy-balance problem:

1. List every force that does work, with its sign.
2. Write: work done by driving forces = gain in Ek + gain in Ep + energy dissipated against resistive forces.
3. Work out the vertical height gained (not the distance along a slope).
4. Solve for the unknown and check the sign and units.

## 3.3.2 Kinetic and potential energy

### Derive Ek = ½mv²

1. F = ma.
2. From rest: v² = 2ax, so x = v²/2a.
3. W = Fx = ma × v²/2a = ½mv².
4. Work done on the object = energy transferred, so Ek = ½mv².

### Derive Ep = mgh

1. Lift at constant speed, so the lifting force F equals the weight mg.
2. Distance moved in the direction of the force = h.
3. W = Fh = mgh = energy stored, so Ep = mgh.

### Exchange of Ep and Ek

- With no resistive forces: loss of Ep = gain of Ek, so mgΔh = ½mv² (when starting at rest), giving v = √(2gΔh).
- The mass cancels. The speed gained by a falling object (ignoring resistance) does not depend on its mass.
- With resistive forces: loss of Ep = gain of Ek + energy dissipated.
- Doubling the speed multiplies Ek by four. Doubling the mass doubles Ek.
- The equation Ep = mgh applies only where g is constant (a uniform field).

## 3.3.3 Power and efficiency

### Derive P = Fv

1. W = Fx, so P = W/t = Fx/t.
2. At constant speed, x/t = v.
3. P = Fv.

- A vehicle at constant speed: driving force = resistive force, so power = resistive force × speed.
- Useful power of a lifting machine at constant speed = mgv.
- Efficiency can use energy or power, because the time cancels. It is never more than 100%.
- Efficiencies of machines in series multiply (as fractions): 0.80 × 0.90 = 0.72.
- 1 kW h = 3.6 × 10⁶ J. It is a unit of energy.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Work and energy | Work is the process of transferring energy by a force. Energy is the stored quantity. Both are in joules. |
| Energy and power | Power is the rate of energy transfer: joules per second. |
| kW and kW h | kW is power. kW h is energy, 3.6 MJ. |
| Ep and weight | Weight is a force in newtons. Ep is an energy in joules. |
| Dissipated and destroyed | Dissipated energy goes to a less useful form. Total energy is conserved. |
| Efficiency and power | Efficiency is a ratio with no unit. |

## Worked reminders

**Work at an angle.** A lawnmower is pushed 9.0 m along level grass by a force of 120 N directed 25° below the horizontal. The work done by the push is W = Fx cos θ = 120 × 9.0 × cos 25° = 979 J. Only the horizontal component moves the mower along the ground.

**Energy dissipated.** A ball of mass 0.20 kg is dropped from 3.0 m and rebounds to 2.1 m. Ep at release = 0.20 × 9.81 × 3.0 = 5.89 J. Ep at the top of the rebound = 0.20 × 9.81 × 2.1 = 4.12 J. The energy dissipated in the bounce and through the air is 5.886 − 4.120 = 1.77 J, which is 30% of the original Ep. It has been transferred to thermal energy and sound, not destroyed.

**Efficiency.** A machine takes in 800 J and delivers 560 J of useful work. Efficiency = 560 ÷ 800 × 100% = 70%. The wasted energy is 800 − 560 = 240 J. If this machine drives a second machine that is 50% efficient, the overall efficiency is 0.70 × 0.50 = 0.35, or 35%.

**Choosing the equation.** If a question gives a force and a distance, use W = Fx cos θ. If it gives a speed and a mass, think Ek. If it gives a change in height, think Ep. If it gives a time or a rate, think P = W/t, and if a steady force and a steady speed are given, P = Fv.

## Quick self-test

1. A 30 N force is directed at 60° to an object's path, and the object moves 4.0 m. Calculate the work done.
2. A 4.0 kg cart moves at 2.5 m s⁻¹. What is its kinetic energy?
3. A ball of mass 0.060 kg moves at 30 m s⁻¹. Calculate its kinetic energy.
4. A 0.50 kg object has 100 J of kinetic energy. Calculate its speed.
5. Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy when a 5.0 kg bag is lifted through 0.50 m.
6. A pendulum bob is released from rest at a height of 0.45 m above its lowest point. Ignoring resistive forces, calculate its speed there.
7. A 40 kg load is raised 2.0 m at constant speed in 5.0 s. Calculate the useful power.
8. A lorry travels at a steady 25 m s⁻¹ with a driving force of 3000 N. Calculate the power delivered to the wheels.
9. A machine gives 45 W of useful output from an input of 150 W. Calculate its efficiency.
10. A 2.5 kW heater runs for 4.0 hours. State the energy transferred in kW h and in MJ.
11. Explain why the tension in a pendulum string does no work.

### Answers

1. W = 30 × 4.0 × cos 60° = **60 J**.
2. ½ × 4.0 × 2.5² = **12.5 J**.
3. ½ × 0.060 × 30² = **27 J**.
4. v = √(2 × 100 ÷ 0.50) = **20 m s⁻¹**.
5. 5.0 × 9.81 × 0.50 = **24.5 J** (24.525 J).
6. v = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.45) = **2.97 m s⁻¹**.
7. Work = 40 × 9.81 × 2.0 = 785 J. Power = 785 ÷ 5.0 = **157 W**.
8. P = Fv = 3000 × 25 = **7.5 × 10⁴ W (75 kW)**.
9. 45 ÷ 150 × 100% = **30%**.
10. 2.5 × 4.0 = **10 kW h**. 10 × 3.6 = **36 MJ**.
11. The tension acts along the string, at 90° to the bob's displacement at every instant, and cos 90° = 0, so **no work is done**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing W = Fx without cos θ when the force is at an angle, or using the wrong angle (it must be the angle to the displacement, not to the vertical or the surface).
- Using the length of the slope instead of the vertical height in mgh.
- Forgetting that the mass cancels in the speed from mgh = ½mv², then using a wrong mass.
- Omitting the energy dissipated by friction in an energy-balance question.
- Squaring only v (or the product mv) in ½mv².
- In a derivation, not stating the starting condition (from rest, or constant speed) or the line that equates work to energy transferred.
- Mixing up kW and kW h, or not converting to joules and watts before substituting.
- Quoting efficiency above 100%, or giving no unit or percentage sign.
- Saying energy is "used up" or "lost" rather than transferred to a less useful form.
- Rounding too early: carry full values and round the final answer to a suitable number of significant figures.
