These questions cover section 6.1, Pythagoras' theorem, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The method is explained in the [Pythagoras' theorem study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem/), and the [Pythagoras' theorem revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-pythagoras-theorem-revision-notes/) summarise it. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise. Diagrams are not drawn to scale.

## Section A

**1.** A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 15 cm and 20 cm. Find the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**2.** A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 17 cm and one shorter side 8 cm. Find the other shorter side. **[2]**

**3.** A right-angled triangle has shorter sides of 6 cm and 9 cm. Find the hypotenuse. **[2]**

**4.** A right-angled triangle has hypotenuse 10 cm and one shorter side 4 cm. Find the other shorter side. **[2]**

**5.** Show that a triangle with sides 9 cm, 40 cm and 41 cm is right-angled. **[2]**

**6.** Find the hypotenuse of a right-angled triangle with shorter sides 4 cm and 8 cm, as a simplified surd. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** A ladder 8 m long leans against a vertical wall. Its foot is 2 m from the wall. How high up the wall does it reach? **[3]**

**8.** A rectangle has a diagonal of 15 cm and a width of 9 cm. Find its length, its perimeter and its area. **[4]**

**9.** The points A and B have coordinates (1, −2) and (7, 6). Find the length AB and the coordinates of the midpoint of AB. **[3]**

**10.** An isosceles triangle has sides 25 cm, 25 cm and 14 cm. Find its height and its area. **[4]**

**11.** The sides of a right-angled triangle are x cm, (x + 1) cm and 29 cm, where 29 cm is the hypotenuse. Find x. **[4]**

**12.** The diagonals of a rhombus are 24 cm and 10 cm. Find the length of a side and the perimeter. **[3]**

**13.** An equilateral triangle has sides of 12 cm. Find its height and its area. **[3]**

**14.** A ship sails 15 km north and then 8 km east. How far is it from its starting point? **[2]**

**15.** Show that a triangle with sides 2.5 cm, 6 cm and 6.5 cm is right-angled. **[2]**

**16.** A is (0, 0), B is (4, 3) and C is (−3, 4). Show that triangle ABC has a right angle at A. **[3]**

**17.** A rectangular field measures 120 m by 50 m. A path goes along the diagonal. How much shorter is the diagonal than walking along two sides? **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** c² = 15² + 20² = 225 + 400 = 625 [1]. **c = 25 cm** [1].

**2.** b² = 17² − 8² = 289 − 64 = 225 [1]. **b = 15 cm** [1].

**3.** c² = 36 + 81 = 117 [1]. **c = 10.8 cm** [1].

**4.** b² = 100 − 16 = 84 [1]. **b = 9.17 cm** [1].

**5.** 9² + 40² = 81 + 1600 = 1681 [1]. 41² = 1681, so the triangle is right-angled [1].

**6.** c² = 16 + 64 = 80 [1]. c = √80 = √16 × √5 = **4√5 cm** [1].

**7.** h² = 8² − 2² = 64 − 4 = 60 [1][1]. **h = 7.75 m** [1].

**8.** Length² = 15² − 9² = 225 − 81 = 144, so the length is **12 cm** [1][1]. Perimeter = 2(12 + 9) = **42 cm** [1]. Area = 12 × 9 = **108 cm²** [1].

**9.** Horizontal difference 6 and vertical difference 8 [1]. AB = √(36 + 64) = **10** [1]. Midpoint = ((1 + 7) ÷ 2, (−2 + 6) ÷ 2) = **(4, 2)** [1].

**10.** Half the base = 7 cm [1]. h² = 25² − 7² = 576, so **h = 24 cm** [1][1]. Area = 1/2 × 14 × 24 = **168 cm²** [1].

**11.** x² + (x + 1)² = 29² [1]. 2x² + 2x + 1 = 841, so x² + x − 420 = 0 [1]. (x + 21)(x − 20) = 0 [1]. **x = 20** [1]. The sides are 20, 21 and 29.

**12.** The diagonals bisect at right angles, so the half-diagonals are 12 and 5 [1]. Side = √(144 + 25) = **13 cm** [1]. Perimeter = **52 cm** [1].

**13.** Half the base = 6 cm. h² = 144 − 36 = 108, so **h = 10.4 cm** [1][1]. Area = 1/2 × 12 × 10.39 = **62.4 cm²** [1].

**14.** d² = 15² + 8² = 289 [1]. **d = 17 km** [1].

**15.** 2.5² + 6² = 6.25 + 36 = 42.25 [1]. 6.5² = 42.25, so it is right-angled [1].

**16.** AB² = 4² + 3² = 25 [1]. AC² = (−3)² + 4² = 25 [1]. BC² = (−3 − 4)² + (4 − 3)² = 49 + 1 = 50. Since 25 + 25 = 50, AB² + AC² = BC², so angle A = 90° [1].

**17.** Diagonal = √(120² + 50²) = √16 900 = 130 m [1][1]. Along two sides = 170 m, so the diagonal is **40 m** shorter [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding the squares when finding a shorter side, instead of subtracting.
- Forgetting to take the square root.
- Choosing a shorter side as the hypotenuse.
- Using the theorem on a triangle with no right angle.
- Expanding (x + 1)² as x² + 1.
- Rounding too early in a multi-step problem.
