Question 12 is **HL only**. ## Questions

**1.** State two requirements for the stability of a natural ecosystem. **[2]**

**2.** A tropical forest region originally covered 480 thousand km² and now covers 396 thousand km².

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in forest area. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why scientists cannot say exactly how much forest can be lost before a tipping point is reached. **[1]**

**3.** A teacher sets up sealed glass jars of pond water containing algae, small crustaceans and microbes, and places them at three different temperatures.

**(a)** Outline why sealed glass jars are preferred to open tanks for this investigation. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify one variable that should be kept constant and one limitation of using the jars as a model of a natural pond. **[2]**

**4.** On an island, a fruit bat eats fruit and disperses the seeds of several tree species. After the bat disappeared, seedlings of bat-dispersed trees fell from 220 to 64 per hectare.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in seedlings per hectare. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the bat could be called a keystone species. **[3]**

**5.** A marine fishery has an estimated annual replacement of 12 000 t. Annual catch is 14 500 t. An earlier survey recorded 520 t landed with 800 boat-days of effort; the latest recorded 410 t with 1025 boat-days.

**(a)** Compare the catch with the replacement rate and state the effect on the stock. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in catch per unit effort between the surveys. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest one other measurement that would show whether the stock is declining. **[1]**

**6.** A wild tree is harvested for its bark. A forest holds 120 mature trees per hectare. Each year 9 per hectare are harvested and 6 per hectare grow to maturity.

**(a)** Calculate the net annual change in mature trees per hectare. **[1]**
**(b)** Predict the number after ten years if rates stay constant. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest how the harvest could be made sustainable. **[1]**
**(d)** State one way the sustainability of this harvest could be monitored. **[1]**

**7.** Explain two ways in which intensive arable farming can reduce the long-term sustainability of agriculture. **[4]**

**8.** Water was sampled upstream and downstream of a farm. Dissolved oxygen was measured on day 0 and after five days sealed in the dark at constant temperature. Sample A (downstream): 8.6 and 1.9 mg dm⁻³. Sample B (upstream): 8.8 and 7.9 mg dm⁻³.

**(a)** Calculate the biochemical oxygen demand (BOD) of each sample. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain the difference, referring to fertilizer use. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain why the samples were kept in the dark. **[1]**

**9.** Mercury concentrations (mg kg⁻¹ of tissue) in a marine food chain are: zooplankton 0.05, sardine 0.2, tuna 1.6.

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the concentration is in tuna than in zooplankton. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain this pattern. **[3]**

**10.** A survey found plastic in the stomachs of 21 of 84 seabirds examined.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage containing plastic. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why plastics persist in the ocean. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe two effects of plastic pollution on marine organisms. **[2]**

**11.** Discuss how rewilding can restore natural processes in a large degraded upland area. Refer to the Hinewai Reserve in your answer. **[8]**

**12.** (HL only) A glacier has retreated, exposing bare ground. Data from sites of different ages are shown.

| Years since ice retreat | 5 | 30 | 90 | 250 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Plant species | 2 | 8 | 15 | 12 |
| Primary production (g m⁻² year⁻¹) | 15 | 140 | 520 | 610 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in plant species between 5 and 90 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe two trends in the data and suggest a reason for the fall in species between 90 and 250 years. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how grazing by farm livestock could arrest succession at a similar site. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A supply of energy [1]; recycling of nutrients [1]. Equivalent credit: genetic diversity; climatic variables within tolerance levels. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the requirement itself; "good conditions" is too vague to earn a mark.

**2. (a)** Change = 396 − 480 = −84 [1]. (−84 ÷ 480) × 100 = **−17.5%** [1].
**(b)** The minimum area needed to keep transpiration, cooling and rainfall running is uncertain, so the threshold position is unknown [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original value, and show the sign or the word "decrease", or the answer can lose credit.

**3. (a)** Matter cannot enter or leave a sealed vessel [1]. Light energy can still pass through the glass [1].
**(b)** Constant: light intensity, volume of water or species added (any one) [1]. Limitation: far simpler than a natural pond, so results may not apply directly [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the specific variable. Temperature itself cannot be the answer because it is the independent variable.

**4. (a)** (64 − 220) ÷ 220 × 100 [1] = **−70.9%** [1].
**(b)** The bat's removal has a large effect on community structure [1] relative to its numbers, because many tree species depend on it for seed dispersal [1]. Fewer seedlings mean the tree community may change or collapse [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the effect to community structure. Saying only that the bat is important does not distinguish keystone from common.

**5. (a)** Catch exceeds replacement by 2500 t [1], so the stock will decline [1].
**(b)** Earlier CPUE = 520 ÷ 800 = 0.65 t per boat-day; latest = 410 ÷ 1025 = 0.40 [1]. Change = (0.40 − 0.65) ÷ 0.65 [1] = **−38.5%** [1].
**(c)** Any one: size or age of fish caught; survey estimate of stock biomass [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show both CPUE values before the percentage change so an arithmetic slip does not hide correct method.

**6. (a)** 6 − 9 = **−3 trees per hectare per year** [1].
**(b)** 120 − 30 = **90 per hectare** [1].
**(c)** Reduce harvest to no more than 6 per hectare per year, or plant seedlings [1].
**(d)** Count seedlings, or survey age structure [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Sustainable means harvest below replacement, so (c) must refer to the rate.

**7.** Any two factors, each named with its consequence. Soil erosion: bare ploughed soil is lost by wind or rain [1], reducing fertility and future yield [1]. Leaching: nutrients wash out of the soil [1], so more fertilizer is needed and waterways are polluted [1]. Equivalent credit: agrochemicals harm non-target organisms and reduce biodiversity; machinery and fertilizer manufacture release carbon dioxide, adding to climate change. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each factor needs its consequence. A list of five factors with no effect earns little.

**8. (a)** A: 8.6 − 1.9 = **6.7 mg dm⁻³** [1]. B: 8.8 − 7.9 = **0.9 mg dm⁻³** [1].
**(b)** Fertilizer nitrate and phosphate leach into the water downstream [1], causing algal growth, and dead algae supply organic matter [1]. Aerobic decomposers respire this matter and use oxygen, so BOD is higher [1].
**(c)** Darkness stops photosynthesis, which would add oxygen [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name decomposers as the oxygen users. Saying "the algae use up the oxygen" is a frequent error.

**9. (a)** 1.6 ÷ 0.05 [1] = **32 times** [1].
**(b)** Mercury is persistent and is not excreted [1]. A consumer takes in many prey organisms from the trophic level beneath it [1], so the toxin accumulates and concentration rises at each trophic level, highest in top consumers [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Include the step about taking in many prey, which explains why concentration rises.

**10. (a)** 21 ÷ 84 × 100 = **25%** [1].
**(b)** Plastics are not biodegradable [1], so decomposers cannot break the polymers down [1].
**(c)** Any two: entanglement restricting movement [1]; gut blockage or feeling full so less feeding [1]; also creditworthy: microplastics ingested and passed to consumers. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* A named effect on an organism is needed. "Harms wildlife" is not specific enough.

**11.** Credit any of the following, up to 8. Rewilding restores natural processes with minimal control [1]. Methods: reintroduce apex predators or keystone species [1]; re-establish connectivity of habitats over large areas [1]; minimize human impact and use ecological management [1]. Predators reduce herbivore numbers [1], so vegetation recovers [1] and the food web becomes more stable [1]. Hinewai Reserve, New Zealand: degraded, gorse-covered farmland regenerating into native forest with little intervention [1]. Judgement: restoration may be slow, uncertain and need long-term management. Also creditworthy: corridors allow movement and gene flow, supporting genetic diversity. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Discuss" rewards a balanced answer, so include a limitation, not only benefits.

**12. (a)** (15 − 2) ÷ 2 × 100 [1] = **650%** [1].
**(b)** Species increase to 90 years and then fall [1]; primary production keeps rising [1]. Species fall because taller, dominant plants shade or outcompete pioneers [1].
**(c)** Livestock graze seedlings and shrubs [1]. Trees cannot establish [1], so grassland persists instead of the woodland climax community [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Describe the trend with values from the table, and name the human influence in (c).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating percentage change against the new value.
- Giving a bare number for percentage change without sign or units.
- Calling a keystone species merely abundant.
- Forgetting that decomposers, not algae, remove the oxygen in eutrophication.
- Skipping the mention of leaching.
- Writing "toxins get bigger" instead of concentration rising at each trophic level.
- Giving a rewilding method but no effect.
- Not showing intermediate values in multi-step calculations.
- Putting succession content into an SL answer.
