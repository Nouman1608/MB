This set practises syllabus section 4.6 of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches (SL and HL):
combined, mutually exclusive, conditional and independent events. Learn the methods first with the
[4.6 study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-6/)
and the [4.6 revision notes](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-4-6-revision-notes/).
All data are invented. No technology is needed; give exact answers where possible.

## Section A

**1.** P(A) = 0.35, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.15.

**(a)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[2]**
**(b)** Find P(A | B). **[1]**
**(c)** Determine whether A and B are independent. **[2]**

**2.** A and B are mutually exclusive, P(A) = 0.25 and P(A ∪ B) = 0.6. Find P(B). **[2]**

**3.** A and B are independent, P(A) = 0.4 and P(A ∩ B) = 0.1.

**(a)** Find P(B). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[2]**

**4.** Two fair dice are rolled. Given that the total is 8, find the probability that at least one die shows
a 6. **[2]**

**5.** A fair die is rolled once. A is "the score is even" and B is "the score is odd". Explain why A and B
are mutually exclusive but not independent. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** In a group of 80 people, 45 drink tea (T), 30 drink coffee (C) and 15 drink neither.

**(a)** Find the number who drink both. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that a person chosen at random drinks tea but not coffee. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the probability that a person drinks coffee, given that they drink tea. **[2]**
**(d)** Determine whether drinking tea and drinking coffee are independent. **[2]**

**7.** Machine A makes 60% of a factory's components and machine B makes the rest. Of machine A's
components, 3% are faulty; of machine B's, 5% are faulty. A component is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find the probability that it is faulty. **[2]**
**(b)** Given that it is faulty, find the probability that it was made by machine B. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the probability that it is not faulty. **[1]**

**8.** A bag contains 5 red, 4 blue and 3 yellow counters. Two counters are taken at random without
replacement.

**(a)** Find the probability that both counters are the same colour. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the probability that at least one counter is yellow. **[2]**
**(c)** Given that the counters are the same colour, find the probability that both are red. **[2]**

**9.** The table shows how 200 students travel to school.

| | Walk | Bus | Car | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Grade 11 | 30 | 50 | 20 | 100 |
| Grade 12 | 24 | 40 | 36 | 100 |
| Total | 54 | 90 | 56 | 200 |

A student is chosen at random.

**(a)** Find P(the student travels by bus). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(bus | Grade 12). **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(Grade 12 | car). **[2]**
**(d)** Determine whether "Grade 11" and "travels by bus" are independent. **[2]**

**10.** An archer hits the target with probability 0.7 on each shot, independently of other shots. She takes
three shots.

**(a)** Find the probability that she hits with all three shots. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the probability that she hits with exactly one shot. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the probability that she hits with at least one shot. **[1]**

**11.** P(A) = 0.4, P(B) = 0.5 and P(A | B) = 0.6.

**(a)** Find P(A ∩ B). **[1]**
**(b)** Find P(B | A). **[1]**
**(c)** Find P(A ∪ B). **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 0.35 + 0.5 − 0.15 [1] = 0.7 [1].
**(b)** 0.15/0.5 = 0.3 [1].
**(c)** P(A)P(B) = 0.175 [1]; 0.175 ≠ 0.15, so A and B are not independent [1]. **[5]**

**2.** P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) [1], so P(B) = 0.6 − 0.25 = 0.35 [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** 0.4 P(B) = 0.1, so P(B) = 0.25 [1].
**(b)** 0.4 + 0.25 − 0.1 [1] = 0.55 [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Total 8: (2, 6), (3, 5), (4, 4), (5, 3), (6, 2), so 5 outcomes [1]. Two contain a 6, so the
probability is 2/5 [1]. **[2]**

**5.** No score is both even and odd, so P(A ∩ B) = 0 and they are mutually exclusive [1]. But
P(A)P(B) = 1/4 ≠ 0, so they are not independent: knowing A happened means B certainly did not [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Let x drink both: (45 − x) + x + (30 − x) + 15 = 80 [1], so x = 10 [1].
**(b)** 35/80 = 7/16 [1].
**(c)** 10/45 [1] = 2/9 [1].
**(d)** P(T)P(C) = (45/80)(30/80) = 27/128 ≈ 0.211 and P(T ∩ C) = 10/80 = 0.125 [1]. These differ, so not
independent [1]. **[7]**

**7. (a)** 0.6 × 0.03 + 0.4 × 0.05 [1] = 0.018 + 0.02 = 0.038 [1].
**(b)** P(B ∩ faulty) = 0.02 [1]; P(B | faulty) = 0.02/0.038 [1] = 10/19 ≈ 0.526 [1].
**(c)** 1 − 0.038 = 0.962 [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** P(RR) = (5/12)(4/11) = 20/132; P(BB) = 12/132; P(YY) = 6/132 [2]. Total 38/132 = 19/66 [1].
**(b)** 1 − P(no yellow) = 1 − (9/12)(8/11) [1] = 1 − 72/132 = 5/11 [1].
**(c)** (20/132)/(38/132) [1] = 10/19 [1]. **[7]**

**9. (a)** 90/200 = 0.45 [1].
**(b)** 40/100 = 0.4 [1].
**(c)** 56 students travel by car [1], 36 of them in Grade 12: 36/56 = 9/14 [1].
**(d)** P(Grade 11 ∩ bus) = 50/200 = 0.25; P(Grade 11)P(bus) = 0.5 × 0.45 = 0.225 [1]. These differ, so the
events are not independent [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** 0.7³ [1] = 0.343 [1].
**(b)** Three orders (HMM, MHM, MMH) [1]: 3 × 0.7 × 0.3² = 0.189 [1].
**(c)** 1 − 0.3³ = 0.973 [1]. **[5]**

**11. (a)** 0.5 × 0.6 = 0.3 [1].
**(b)** 0.3/0.4 = 0.75 [1].
**(c)** 0.4 + 0.5 − 0.3 = 0.6 [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the overall total instead of the "given" total in questions 6(c), 7(b) and 9(c).
- Forgetting that there are three orders in question 10(b).
- Keeping the denominator at 12 for the second counter in question 8.
- Stating "not independent" in questions 6(d) and 9(d) without the two numbers being compared.
- Treating mutually exclusive and independent as the same idea in question 5.
