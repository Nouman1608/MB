**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 7, The Russian Revolution (1855 to 1924), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-russian-revolution/) for fuller explanation. Dates before February 1918 are old style unless stated.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Aim for an in-depth understanding of the question, a thorough and balanced evaluation of perspectives, and a substantiated judgement. Command terms include "To what extent", "Discuss", "Examine", "Explain" and "Analyse".

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Emancipation | The freeing of the serfs in 1861 |
| Mir | Village commune controlling land use |
| Zemstva | Elected local councils created in 1864 |
| Okhrana | The tsarist secret police |
| Bolsheviks | Lenin's wing of the Social Democrats |
| Mensheviks | The more moderate wing |
| SRs | Socialist Revolutionaries, who appealed to the peasants |
| Dual Power | Joint authority of the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet |
| War Communism | Emergency economic system, 1918 to 1921 |
| NEP | New Economic Policy, from March 1921 |

## The three tsars

| Ruler | Social reform | Economy | Repression | Opposition |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alexander II | Emancipation, zemstva, courts, army | Railways, investment | Third Section; after 1863 caution | Populists; People's Will |
| Alexander III | Reaction; land captains | Witte's industrial drive | Statute of State Security; Russification | Marxists, early labour groups |
| Nicholas II | Little | Rapid growth, unrest | Okhrana; Bloody Sunday | Social Democrats, SRs, liberals |

## 1905

- **Causes:** hunger, low wages, land hunger; weak regime; liberal and socialist ideas.
- **Trigger:** defeat in the Russo-Japanese War; Bloody Sunday.
- **Events:** strikes; *Potemkin*; St Petersburg Soviet; Moscow rising.
- **Outcome:** October Manifesto split the opposition; Fundamental Laws kept autocracy; the army stayed loyal.

## 1905 to 1917

- **Dumas:** radical first two dissolved; the June 1907 law produced a conservative Third.
- **Stolypin:** field courts-martial; ended redemption payments; private farms; Siberian settlement; killed in 1911.
- **Okhrana:** infiltration and arrests kept revolutionaries weak.
- **Debate:** optimists (reform could have worked) against pessimists (revolution was likely).

## Road to October

| Stage | Key points |
|---|---|
| War | Defeats, retreat, inflation, hunger; Tsar in command; Rasputin |
| February | Women's Day protests, mutinies, abdication |
| Dual Power | Provisional Government against Petrograd Soviet; Order No. 1 |
| Failures | War continued; June offensive; land delay; Kornilov |
| Bolsheviks | April Theses; Trotsky and the Military Revolutionary Committee; Winter Palace |

## Consolidation

- **Brest-Litovsk:** harsh terms; ended the war.
- **Terror:** Cheka; Constituent Assembly dissolved; Red Terror (1918).
- **Civil war:** Reds against Whites; interventions; Reds won through unity, railways, Trotsky and White divisions.
- **War Communism:** requisitioning, rationing; famine and Kronstadt.
- **NEP:** tax in kind, private trade; factions banned.

## Impact to 1924

| Group | Effect |
|---|---|
| Church | Separated from the state; property seized; persecution |
| Women | Legal equality, divorce, abortion; hardship; male leadership |
| Minorities | Anti-Semitism condemned; cultural rights promised, control kept |
| Peasants | Land gained but requisitioning, then NEP |
| Workers | Told they ruled; unions controlled |
| Former elites | Lost property and rights |

## Worked example: planning an essay

**Question:** "To what extent was the First World War the main reason for the fall of the Tsar in 1917?"

1. **Thesis:** The war was the immediate cause, but weaknesses in the autocracy since 1905 made collapse possible.
2. **Paragraph 1, war:** defeats, casualties, inflation, transport failure; Tsar in command; Rasputin.
3. **Paragraph 2, long-term:** reforms of 1861 left peasants poor; 1905; refusal to share power; Okhrana could not hold the system.
4. **Paragraph 3, February events:** strikes and loss of army loyalty.
5. **Perspective:** pessimists against optimists on Russia before 1914.
6. **Judgement:** the war was necessary to the collapse; long-term weakness made the regime vulnerable.

## Historians' views

- Pipes: Bolshevik takeover as a coup. Rabinowitch and later social historians: wider support and a mass movement.
- Optimists against pessimists on pre-war Russia.
- Figes (*A People's Tragedy*): the revolution as a social tragedy with deep causes.

## Common mistakes

- Using old and new dates inconsistently.
- Treating October as inevitable.
- Describing events without ranking causes.
- Forgetting the Civil War in explaining Bolshevik survival.
- Giving Trotsky or Lenin all the credit.

## Self-test

1. What did the Emancipation of 1861 do and not do?
2. What did the Fundamental Laws of 1906 do?
3. Name two aims of Stolypin's reforms.
4. What was Dual Power?
5. Why did Lenin sign Brest-Litovsk?
6. What replaced War Communism?

**Answers:** 1. It freed serfs but kept redemption payments and commune control. 2. They confirmed the Tsar's autocratic powers over ministers, army and the Duma. 3. For example to create prosperous peasant farmers loyal to the regime, and to settle Siberia. 4. The joint authority of the Provisional Government and the Petrograd Soviet. 5. To end the war and save Bolshevik rule. 6. The New Economic Policy.
