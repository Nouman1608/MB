Quick recall for Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1), Issue 2, February 2026, section 2.3 Training. The one content point, 2.3.1, is "Importance of training to a business and workers". Full explanations and worked examples are in the [training study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-training/); exam-style questions are in the [training practice set](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-training-practice/). Businesses and figures below are invented, in dollars.

## 1. The checklist from the specification

- Different types of training and the main purpose of each: induction, on-the-job, off-the-job.
- Compliance with health and safety laws.
- Benefits and limitations of training.

Everything in this section is judged from two sides: the business and the worker.

## 2. Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Training | Developing the knowledge and skills an employee needs to do a job |
| Induction training | Training for new starters that introduces the business, its people, rules and safety procedures |
| On-the-job training | Learning at the workplace while doing the job, usually guided by an experienced colleague |
| Off-the-job training | Learning away from normal work, for example at a college, on an external course or in a training room |
| Demonstration | A colleague shows how a task is done, then the trainee copies |
| Shadowing | The trainee follows an experienced employee through their work |
| Coaching | A supervisor watches the trainee work and gives feedback |
| Mentoring | Longer-term guidance from a more senior employee |
| Compliance | Meeting the requirements of the law, here health and safety laws |
| Refresher training | Repeat training when rules, methods or equipment change |
| Transferable skills | Skills a worker can use in other jobs or businesses |

The method words (demonstration, shadowing, coaching, mentoring) are standard teaching rather than specification text; they help you describe on-the-job training precisely.

## 3. Type, purpose and trade-off

| Type | Main purpose | Plus | Minus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Induction | Settle new starters in quickly and safely | Fewer early leavers; safety from day one | Takes time from managers; can overload new staff with information |
| On-the-job | Teach the specific skills of this job | Low fees; realistic; output continues | Trainer's own output falls; bad habits passed on |
| Off-the-job | Teach specialist or qualification-level skills | Expert teaching; recognised qualifications | Fees; time away; skills may not match the workplace exactly |

**Classification test:** is the person doing their normal job while they learn? Yes means on-the-job. A course in a training room on site is off-the-job.

## 4. Health and safety compliance

- The specification names no particular law. You need the principle: a business must follow the health and safety laws of its country and protect people at work.
- Training is how a business makes sure employees know safe methods: handling heavy items, protective equipment, machinery, chemicals, fire procedures, reporting accidents.
- Compliance is repeated, not one-off: refresher training is needed when anything changes.
- Records of training help show the business met its duty.
- Failure brings injuries, absence, possible fines or legal action and reputational damage.

## 5. Benefits and limitations in one view

| | Business | Worker |
|---|---|---|
| Benefits | Fewer errors and less waste; higher productivity; better service; flexible cover for absence; safer working; helps retain staff | New and transferable skills; confidence; qualifications; promotion and pay prospects; safety |
| Limitations | Fees; lost output; delay before benefits; trained staff may leave for rivals | Time away; pressure of assessment; may not suit the person's way of learning |

Link to motivation: training can meet higher needs such as esteem, because the worker feels valued and has a route to progress. Keep this link brief unless a question asks about motivation.

## 6. Worked reminder: is the course worth it?

Torrin Garage sends 4 mechanics on a 2-day off-the-job course on electric vehicle repairs.

- Fees: 4 × 85 = 340.
- Cover for lost work: 4 × 2 days × 70 = 560.
- Total cost: 340 + 560 = **900**.

The owner expects rework on poorly done jobs to fall from 150 a month to 60 a month, a saving of 90 a month. Time to recover the cost: 900 ÷ 90 = **10 months**.

Interpretation: the cost is recovered within a year from rework savings alone, and the garage can now take electric vehicle work it would otherwise turn away. Risk: a trained mechanic could leave for a rival garage before the 10 months are up.

## 7. Exam traps

- Writing that induction is for every employee. It is for new starters.
- Calling on-site classroom training "on-the-job".
- Counting only the course fee; lost output or cover is also a cost.
- Giving a benefit to the business when the question asks about the worker, or the reverse.
- Claiming training "guarantees" higher productivity. It can raise it; it may not if staff are unwilling or the training is poor.
- Naming specific laws. You are not required to, and an incorrect name gains nothing.
- For "justify", recommending a type without saying why the other is weaker in this case.

## 8. Command-word frames

- **Outline** a benefit of off-the-job training: point plus one linked consequence in context.
- **Explain** a limitation: statement, then two expansion points (cause, then effect).
- **Analyse** the impact of induction: a chain from the case, for example new staff learn procedures, so they make fewer mistakes, so customers get better service, so complaints fall.
- **Evaluate** whether training is worthwhile: both sides, a judgement and the factor it depends on, such as staff turnover or the cost of mistakes.

## Quick self-test

1. Define on-the-job training.
2. State the main purpose of induction training.
3. A trainee spends a day in a training room at head office learning a new stock system. What type of training is this?
4. Give two methods of on-the-job training.
5. Give one benefit of off-the-job training to a worker.
6. Why does health and safety compliance need refresher training?
7. Give one limitation of training to a business.
8. A course costs 210 per person for 6 staff. Calculate the total fee.
9. Training costs 2,400 and saves 300 a month. How many months to recover the cost?
10. Why might a small business prefer on-the-job training?
11. Explain why trained staff leaving is a risk to the business.
12. State one consequence of failing to comply with health and safety laws.

### Answers

1. Training that takes place at the workplace while the employee does the job, usually guided by an experienced colleague.
2. To help a new employee settle in quickly, learn the rules and safety procedures and become productive.
3. Off-the-job, because the trainee has stopped doing their normal job to learn.
4. Any two of: demonstration, shadowing, coaching, mentoring.
5. A recognised qualification, which improves their chances of promotion or a better-paid job.
6. Because equipment, methods and rules change, so employees must be kept up to date to work safely and keep the business compliant.
7. Any one of: fees, lost output while staff train, the delay before benefits appear, trained staff may leave for a competitor.
8. 6 × 210 = 1,260.
9. 2,400 ÷ 300 = 8 months.
10. Fees are low and output continues while staff learn, which matters when cash is limited and there are few employees to cover absence.
11. The business has paid for the skills, so if the worker leaves, the money is lost and a rival may gain the skills, while the business must recruit and train again.
12. Any one of: injured workers, fines or legal action, damage to reputation.
