These questions cover section 3.11 Engineering physics (3.11.1 Rotational dynamics and 3.11.2 Thermodynamics and engines) of the AQA AS and A-level Physics specification (7407/7408, version 1.4, AS and A-level exams June 2016 onwards). It is **A-level only** and one of the five **options** (sections 3.9 to 3.13), assessed in Paper 3 Section B (35 marks).

Learn the content with the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-engineering-physics/) and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-physics-engineering-physics-revision-notes/). Take R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹. Treat all gases as ideal.

## Questions

**1.** Two flywheels have equal mass and radius. One is a uniform disc; the other has most of its mass in its rim. Explain which stores more energy at the same angular speed. **[2]**

**2.** A light cross-shaped frame carries four small 0.25 kg masses, each 0.40 m from the axis of rotation.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of inertia of the frame about the axis. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its rotational kinetic energy at 5.0 rad s⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** A grinding wheel turns at 2400 revolutions per minute. When the motor is switched off, it slows uniformly and stops in 20 s.

**(a)** Calculate its initial angular speed. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate its angular acceleration. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the number of revolutions it makes while stopping. **[2]**

**4.** A motor applies a constant torque of 9.0 N m to a flywheel with I = 1.6 kg m². A constant frictional torque of 1.0 N m acts. The flywheel starts from rest.

**(a)** Calculate its angular acceleration. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the time it takes to reach 150 rad s⁻¹. **[1]**
**(c)** At 150 rad s⁻¹ the motor is switched off. Calculate the time the flywheel takes to stop. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest why, in practice, the deceleration in (c) is not uniform. **[1]**

**5.** A skater spins at 1.5 rev s⁻¹ with arms out (I = 2.4 kg m²), then pulls her arms in (I = 0.90 kg m²).

**(a)** Calculate her new rate of rotation in revolutions per second. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why her kinetic energy increases although her angular momentum is constant. **[2]**

**6.** A wheel with I = 0.45 kg m² starts from rest. A constant torque acts for 2.0 s, after which ω = 12 rad s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the angular impulse given to the wheel. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the torque. **[1]**
**(c)** Show that the work done by the torque equals the wheel's final kinetic energy. **[2]**

**7.** A gas at constant pressure 1.5 × 10⁵ Pa expands from 2.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 3.2 × 10⁻³ m³, receiving 450 J by heating.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the change in its internal energy. **[2]**
**(c)** The initial temperature is 300 K. Calculate the final temperature. **[2]**
**(d)** State the relationship between Q and W for an isothermal change, and why. **[1]**

**8.** In a diesel engine, 6.0 × 10⁻⁴ m³ of air (γ = 1.4) at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa and 290 K is compressed adiabatically to 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ m³.

**(a)** Calculate the amount of air in moles. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the final pressure. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the final temperature. **[2]**
**(d)** Use the first law to explain why the temperature rises. **[2]**

**9.** A gas follows cycle ABCD. A→B: expands at 4.0 × 10⁵ Pa from 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³ to 2.5 × 10⁻³ m³. B→C: constant volume, to 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa. C→D: compressed at 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa to 1.0 × 10⁻³ m³. D→A: constant volume.

**(a)** Calculate the work done by the gas from A to B. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the work done on the gas from C to D. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the net work done per cycle. **[1]**
**(d)** The cycle repeats 20 times per second. Calculate the power output. **[1]**
**(e)** State the net change in internal energy per cycle. **[1]**

**10.** A six-cylinder, four-stroke diesel engine runs at 2400 rpm. Each indicator loop has area 520 J. Brake torque is 200 N m. Fuel (calorific value 4.5 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹) is used at 4.0 × 10⁻³ kg s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the indicated power. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the brake power. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the friction power. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the input power. **[1]**
**(e)** Calculate the overall, thermal and mechanical efficiencies. **[3]**
**(f)** Give two reasons why the real indicator diagram encloses a smaller area than the theoretical one. **[2]**

**11.** A power station uses steam at 840 K and rejects energy to a river at 300 K.

**(a)** Explain why a heat engine needs a cold sink. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the maximum theoretical efficiency. **[1]**
**(c)** It takes in 1250 MW and delivers 500 MW of work. Calculate its efficiency and the rate of energy rejection. **[2]**
**(d)** Suggest how a combined heat and power scheme improves the use of energy. **[1]**

**12.** A heat pump keeps a house at 294 K by taking energy from the ground at 279 K.

**(a)** Calculate the maximum theoretical COP of the heat pump. **[2]**
**(b)** The real pump has COP 3.5 and delivers 7.0 kW. Calculate the input power and the rate of extraction from the ground. **[2]**
**(c)** A refrigerator keeps food at 276 K in a 294 K kitchen. Calculate its maximum theoretical COP. **[1]**
**(d)** Show that, between the same two temperatures, COP_hp = COP_ref + 1. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The rim flywheel has more mass far from the axis, so a larger moment of inertia (I = Σmr²) [1]. E_k = ½Iω², so at the same ω it stores more energy [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The masses are equal, so the first mark needs the distribution of mass, not "heavier".

**2. (a)** I = 4 × 0.25 × 0.40² = **0.16 kg m²** [1]
**(b)** E_k = ½ × 0.16 × 5.0² [1] = **2.0 J** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Error carried forward is usually allowed in (b) if your I from (a) was wrong.

**3. (a)** ω = 2400 × 2π/60 = **251 rad s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** α = (0 − 251)/20 [1] = **−12.6 rad s⁻²** [1]
**(c)** θ = ½(251 + 0) × 20 = 2513 rad [1]; 2513/2π = **400 revolutions** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), give the negative sign or say "deceleration"; a bare 12.6 risks the accuracy mark.

**4. (a)** Resultant torque = 9.0 − 1.0 = 8.0 N m [1]; α = 8.0/1.6 = **5.0 rad s⁻²** [1]
**(b)** t = 150/5.0 = **30 s** [1]
**(c)** α = −1.0/1.6 = −0.625 rad s⁻² [1]; t = 150/0.625 = **240 s** [1]
**(d)** Frictional torque (for example from air resistance) depends on angular speed, so it changes as the wheel slows [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 9.0 N m alone in (a) gives 5.6 rad s⁻² and loses both marks.

**5. (a)** I₁f₁ = I₂f₂ [1]; f₂ = 2.4 × 1.5/0.90 = **4.0 rev s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** E_k = L²/2I, so with L constant and I smaller, E_k is larger [1]. The energy comes from the work she does pulling her arms in [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Rev s⁻¹ is fine in (a) because 2π cancels, but not in E_k = ½Iω².

**6. (a)** Angular impulse = Δ(Iω) = 0.45 × 12 − 0 [1] = **5.4 N m s** [1]
**(b)** T = 5.4/2.0 = **2.7 N m** [1]
**(c)** θ = ½(0 + 12) × 2.0 = 12 rad, so W = Tθ = 2.7 × 12 = 32.4 J [1]; E_k = ½ × 0.45 × 12² = 32.4 J, the same [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* On a "show that", calculate and compare both values; asserting equality earns nothing.

**7. (a)** W = pΔV = 1.5 × 10⁵ × 1.2 × 10⁻³ = **180 J** [1]
**(b)** ΔU = Q − W [1] = 450 − 180 = **270 J** [1]
**(c)** At constant p, V/T is constant [1]; T₂ = 300 × 3.2/2.0 = **480 K** [1]
**(d)** Q = W, because T is constant so ΔU = 0 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing ΔU = Q + W gives 630 J and loses both marks in (b).

**8. (a)** n = pV/RT = (1.0 × 10⁵ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁴)/(8.31 × 290) = **0.025 mol** [1]
**(b)** p₂ = p₁(V₁/V₂)^γ = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 20^1.4 [1] = **6.6 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]
**(c)** T₂ = T₁p₂V₂/(p₁V₁) = 290 × (6.63 × 10⁶ × 3.0 × 10⁻⁵)/(1.0 × 10⁵ × 6.0 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = **961 K** [1]
**(d)** Q = 0 and work is done on the gas, so W is negative [1]; ΔU = −W is positive, so T rises [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Using pV = constant (isothermal) in (b) gives 2.0 × 10⁶ Pa, which scores nothing.

**9. (a)** W = pΔV = 4.0 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **600 J** [1]
**(b)** W = 1.0 × 10⁵ × 1.5 × 10⁻³ = **150 J** [1]
**(c)** Net work = 600 − 150 = **450 J** (the loop area) [1]
**(d)** P = 450 × 20 = **9.0 kW** [1]
**(e)** **Zero**: the gas returns to its starting state [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Constant-volume stages involve no work; adding work for them loses (c).

**10. (a)** 2400 rpm = 40 rev s⁻¹; four-stroke, so 20 cycles per second per cylinder [1]. Indicated power = 520 × 20 × 6 [1] = **62.4 kW** [1]
**(b)** ω = 2π × 40 = 251 rad s⁻¹ [1]; P = Tω = 200 × 251 = **50.3 kW** [1]
**(c)** 62.4 − 50.3 = **12.1 kW** [1]
**(d)** 4.5 × 10⁷ × 4.0 × 10⁻³ = **180 kW** [1]
**(e)** Overall = 50.3/180 = **0.28** [1]; thermal = 62.4/180 = **0.35** [1]; mechanical = 50.3/62.4 = **0.81** [1]
**(f)** Any two of: burning takes time; valves do not move instantly; energy is lost through cylinder walls; the gas is not ideal [1] [1]. **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 40 cycles per second doubles the indicated power; later parts usually allow error carried forward.

**11. (a)** An engine cannot turn all the energy it takes in by heating into work [1]; it must reject some, Q_C, to a colder sink [1].
**(b)** (840 − 300)/840 = **0.64** [1]
**(c)** Efficiency = 500/1250 = **0.40** [1]; rejected = 1250 − 500 = **750 MW** [1]
**(d)** Rejected energy heats nearby buildings instead of being wasted [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* These temperatures are already in kelvin; adding 273 again loses (b).

**12. (a)** COP_hp(max) = T_H/(T_H − T_C) = 294/15 [1] = **19.6** [1]
**(b)** W = 7.0/3.5 = **2.0 kW** [1]; Q_C rate = 7.0 − 2.0 = **5.0 kW** [1]
**(c)** COP_ref(max) = 276/18 = **15.3** [1]
**(d)** Q_H/W = (Q_C + W)/W = Q_C/W + 1 [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A COP greater than 1 is not an error; heat pumps move energy rather than create it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving ω in rpm, or using applied instead of resultant torque.
- Assuming kinetic energy is conserved when a skater changes shape.
- Sign errors in Q = ΔU + W (W is work done by the gas).
- Using pV = constant for an adiabatic change.
- Counting one cycle per revolution in a four-stroke engine.
- Swapping thermal and mechanical efficiency, or inverting a COP formula.
