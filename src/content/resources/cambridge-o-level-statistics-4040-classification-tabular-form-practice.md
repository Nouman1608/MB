These questions cover section 2.1 of the Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027: "Classification and representation in tabular form", including two-way tables. Revise the method in the [classification and tables study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-classification-tabular-form/) or the [classification and tables revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-classification-tabular-form-revision-notes/). All data are invented for practice.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** A pupil groups the number of text messages sent in a day by 30 friends using the classes 0–10, 10–20 and 20–30. The largest number sent was 45. Identify two faults in these classes. **[2]**

**2.** The table shows the drinks chosen by 110 customers.

| | Tea | Coffee | Juice | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men | 18 | | 7 | 50 |
| Women | 22 | | 12 | |
| Total | 40 | | 19 | 110 |

Complete the table. **[3]**

**3.** Use your completed table from question 2.

**(a)** Find the percentage of women who chose coffee. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the percentage of coffee drinkers who were women. **[1]**

**4.** State three features that a well-designed statistical table should have. **[3]**

**5.** Fifteen dogs at a show are recorded by size (S = small, M = medium, L = large) and coat (s = short, l = long):

Ss, Ml, Ls, Ss, Ms, Ll, Sl, Ms, Ms, Ls, Ss, Ml, Ls, Sl, Ms

Construct a two-way table of size against coat, with totals. **[3]**

**6.** Write a set of four classes, using inequalities, for the masses m kg of parcels that range from 0 kg up to but not including 2 kg, with equal widths. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

**7.** A school has 150 pupils in Year 9. Each plays exactly one of football, netball or athletics.

- 80 of the pupils are boys.
- 8 boys play netball.
- The number of boys who play football is three times the number of boys who do athletics.
- 35 girls play netball and 12 girls play football.

**(a)** Construct a complete two-way table. **[4]**

**(b)** Find the percentage of girls who do athletics. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the percentage of pupils doing athletics who are girls. **[1]**

**(d)** Compare the popularity of football among boys and girls, using percentages. **[2]**

**8.** In a survey of 90 households, x households own a cat but not a dog, 2x own a dog but not a cat, 15 own both and 21 own neither.

**(a)** Form an equation in x and solve it. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw a two-way table showing cat ownership (yes, no) against dog ownership (yes, no), with totals. **[3]**

**(c)** What fraction of dog-owning households also own a cat? **[1]**

**9.** A newspaper prints this table.

| Age | 0–20 | 20–40 | 40–60 | 60–80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Visitors | 412 | 655 | 390 | 121 |

**(a)** Give three criticisms of the table. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest improved age classes for the visitors, with ages in completed years. **[1]**

**10.** Two schools record how pupils travel to school.

| | Walk | Bus | Car | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| School A | 96 | 84 | 60 | 240 |
| School B | 45 | 75 | 30 | 150 |

**(a)** Explain why comparing the numbers who walk could be misleading. **[1]**

**(b)** Construct a table of row percentages. **[2]**

**(c)** Compare the travel patterns of the two schools. **[2]**

**11.** A firm employs 250 workers. 40% work part-time. 30% of the part-time workers and 60% of the full-time workers drive to work.

**(a)** Construct a two-way table of contract (part-time, full-time) against whether the worker drives, with totals. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the percentage of the workers who drive that are part-time. **[1]**

**12.** A survey of 64 pupils records hand used for writing (left, right) and whether the pupil plays a musical instrument (yes, no). 8 pupils are left-handed. Of the right-handed pupils, 21 play an instrument. In total, 38 pupils do not play an instrument.

**(a)** Complete a two-way table with totals. **[3]**

**(b)** A pupil claims that left-handed pupils are more likely to play an instrument. Use the table to comment. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The classes overlap: 10 and 20 each fit two classes [1]. The classes are not exhaustive: there is no class for values above 30, such as 45 [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Men coffee = 50 − 18 − 7 = 25 [1]. Women total = 110 − 50 = 60 and women coffee = 60 − 22 − 12 = 26 [1]. Coffee total = 25 + 26 = 51; check 40 + 51 + 19 = 110 [1]. **[3]**

**3. (a)** 26/60 × 100 = 43.3% [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 26/51 × 100 = 51.0% [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Part (a) uses the women's row total; part (b) uses the coffee column total.

**4.** Any three, for [1] each: a title; row and column headings; units; totals; a source for secondary data; categories in a sensible order. **[3]**

**5.** Correct cells [2] (one mark if one cell is wrong); correct totals [1].

| | Short | Long | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small | 3 | 2 | 5 |
| Medium | 4 | 2 | 6 |
| Large | 3 | 1 | 4 |
| Total | 10 | 5 | 15 |

**[3]**

**6.** 0 ≤ m < 0.5, 0.5 ≤ m < 1, 1 ≤ m < 1.5, 1.5 ≤ m < 2 [2] (one mark if the classes have a single fault). **[2]**

**7. (a)** Boys: football + athletics = 80 − 8 = 72; with football = 3 × athletics, athletics = 18 and football = 54 [1]. Girls = 150 − 80 = 70 [1]; girls' athletics = 70 − 35 − 12 = 23 [1]. Table with totals: football 66, netball 43, athletics 41, check 150 [1].

| | Football | Netball | Athletics | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Boys | 54 | 8 | 18 | 80 |
| Girls | 12 | 35 | 23 | 70 |
| Total | 66 | 43 | 41 | 150 |

**[4]**

**(b)** 23/70 × 100 = 32.9% [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 23/41 × 100 = 56.1% [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** Boys: 54/80 = 67.5%; girls: 12/70 = 17.1% [1]. Football is far more popular among boys, chosen by about two thirds of boys but under a fifth of girls [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** x + 2x + 15 + 21 = 90 [1], so 3x = 54 and x = 18 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Cells correct [2] (cat and dog 15, cat only 18, dog only 36, neither 21); totals correct [1] (cat yes 33, cat no 57, dog yes 51, dog no 39, grand total 90).

| | Dog: yes | Dog: no | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cat: yes | 15 | 18 | 33 |
| Cat: no | 36 | 21 | 57 |
| Total | 51 | 39 | 90 |

**[3]**

**(c)** 15/51 = 5/17 [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** Any three, for [1] each: no title; no unit or statement of how ages are recorded; classes overlap at 20, 40 and 60; no class for visitors aged 80 or over; no total. **[3]**

**(b)** For example 0–19, 20–39, 40–59, 60–79, 80 and over [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** The schools have different numbers of pupils, so a larger count may simply reflect a larger school [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** School A: walk 40%, bus 35%, car 25% [1]. School B: walk 30%, bus 50%, car 20% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** A higher proportion of School A pupils walk (40% against 30%) [1], while half of School B pupils take the bus compared with 35% at School A [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Part-time = 0.4 × 250 = 100 and full-time = 150 [1]. Part-time drive 30, do not drive 70; full-time drive 90, do not drive 60 [1]. Totals: drive 120, do not drive 130, grand total 250 [1].

| | Drive | Do not drive | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Part-time | 30 | 70 | 100 |
| Full-time | 90 | 60 | 150 |
| Total | 120 | 130 | 250 |

**[3]**

**(b)** 30/120 × 100 = 25% [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** Right-handed = 64 − 8 = 56, so right-handed and no instrument = 56 − 21 = 35 [1]. Left-handed and no instrument = 38 − 35 = 3, so left-handed and play = 8 − 3 = 5 [1]. Totals: play 26, do not play 38, grand total 64 [1].

| | Plays | Does not play | Total |
|---|---|---|---|
| Left | 5 | 3 | 8 |
| Right | 21 | 35 | 56 |
| Total | 26 | 38 | 64 |

**[3]**

**(b)** Left: 5/8 = 62.5% play; right: 21/56 = 37.5% play [1]. The claim is supported for this sample, but only 8 pupils are left-handed, so the evidence is weak [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Filling a cell before checking which row and column it belongs to.
- Not checking that rows and columns give the same grand total.
- Using the grand total as the base when the question names a subgroup.
- Comparing counts for groups of different sizes.
- Vague criticisms of a table, such as "it is unclear", instead of the specific fault.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many marks does a two-way table usually carry?**
That depends on the question, but marks are usually given for groups of correct cells and for consistent totals, so show each subtraction.

**Should percentages be rounded?**
Give them to 1 decimal place or 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise, and keep the unrounded fraction in your working.
