Revise with the [cumulative frequency table study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-cumulative-frequency-tabular-form/) or the [cumulative frequency table revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-statistics-4040-cumulative-frequency-tabular-form-revision-notes/). All data are invented.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** The number of pets in each of 50 households is recorded.

| Pets | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Households | 12 | 18 | 11 | 6 | 3 |

**(a)** Form the cumulative frequency table. **[2]**

**(b)** How many households have fewer than 2 pets? **[1]**

**(c)** How many households have at least 3 pets? **[1]**

**2.** For a discrete variable taking the values 1, 2, 3 and 4, the cumulative frequencies are 3, 10, 22 and 30. Find the frequency of each value. **[2]**

**3.** The classes of a distribution are 0 < d ≤ 5, 5 < d ≤ 10, 10 < d ≤ 20 and 20 < d ≤ 40. Write the labels for the cumulative frequency table, including the starting entry. **[2]**

**4.** The ages of club members, in completed years, are grouped 18–24, 25–34 and 35–49. Write the labels "under ..." for the cumulative frequencies of these three classes. **[1]**

**5.** A "more than" table for 60 values is: at least 0: 60; at least 10: 52; at least 20: 37; at least 30: 15; at least 40: 4; at least 50: 0.

**(a)** Write the corresponding "less than" cumulative frequencies at 10, 20, 30, 40 and 50. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the frequency of each class of width 10. **[1]**

## Section B: longer questions

**6.** The times, t minutes, taken by 120 runners to complete a course are shown.

| Time, t min | 20 ≤ t < 25 | 25 ≤ t < 30 | 30 ≤ t < 35 | 35 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 50 | 50 ≤ t < 60 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 9 | 26 | 41 | 28 | 12 | 4 |

**(a)** Form a cumulative frequency table, with labels and a starting entry, and a cumulative percentage row to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**(b)** How many runners took at least 35 minutes? **[1]**

**(c)** What percentage of runners took less than 40 minutes? **[1]**

**(d)** How many runners took from 25 minutes up to 40 minutes? **[1]**

**(e)** Estimate the number of runners who took less than 32 minutes. **[2]**

**7.** The frequencies of five classes are 7, x, 15, y and 6. The total frequency is 50 and the cumulative frequency at the end of the second class is 19.

**(a)** Find x and y. **[2]**

**(b)** Write down the full set of cumulative frequencies. **[1]**

**8.** Two groups sit the same test. In Group A (60 pupils), 21 score less than 40. In Group B (90 pupils), 27 score less than 40.

**(a)** Find the cumulative percentage scoring less than 40 for each group. **[2]**

**(b)** Which group did better at the lower end? Give a reason. **[1]**

**9.** The ages, in completed years, of 200 members of a walking club are shown.

| Age | 0–19 | 20–39 | 40–59 | 60–79 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 34 | 71 | 62 | 33 |

**(a)** Form the cumulative frequency table, labelled "under ...". **[2]**

**(b)** What percentage of members are aged 40 or over? **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the number of members aged under 30. **[2]**

**10.** Marks out of 50 for 40 pupils are grouped.

| Marks | 0–9 | 10–19 | 20–29 | 30–39 | 40–50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 3 | 8 | 15 | 10 | 4 |

**(a)** Form a cumulative frequency table with labels of the form "≤ 9". **[2]**

**(b)** The pass mark is 20. How many pupils passed? **[1]**

**(c)** What percentage of pupils scored less than 30? **[1]**

**11.** A pupil forms this cumulative frequency table for the masses, m kg, of 40 parcels in the classes 0 ≤ m < 2, 2 ≤ m < 4, 4 ≤ m < 6 and 6 ≤ m < 8, with frequencies 5, 13, 15 and 7.

| m | 1 | 3 | 5 | 7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cumulative frequency | 5 | 18 | 33 | 40 |

**(a)** Identify two errors in the pupil's table. **[2]**

**(b)** Write a corrected table. **[1]**

**12.** A cumulative frequency table for the lengths, l cm, of 90 fish gives: l < 10: 0; l < 15: 8; l < 20: 29; l < 25: 61; l < 30: 82; l < 40: 90.

**(a)** Find the number of fish in the class 20 ≤ l < 25. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the percentage of fish that are at least 25 cm long. **[2]**

**(c)** Estimate the number of fish shorter than 22 cm. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Running totals 12, 30, 41, 47, 50 [1], labelled ≤ 0, ≤ 1, ≤ 2, ≤ 3, ≤ 4, with the last equal to 50 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Fewer than 2 means at most 1: 30 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 50 − 41 = 9 [1]. **[1]**

**2.** 3, then 10 − 3 = 7 [1], 22 − 10 = 12 and 30 − 22 = 8 [1]. **[2]**

**3.** d ≤ 0 with cumulative frequency 0 [1]; then d ≤ 5, d ≤ 10, d ≤ 20, d ≤ 40 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Under 25, under 35, under 50 [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** Less than 10: 60 − 52 = 8; less than 20: 23 [1]; less than 30: 45; less than 40: 56; less than 50: 60 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 8, 15, 22, 11, 4 [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** Labels t < 20 (0), t < 25, t < 30, t < 35, t < 40, t < 50, t < 60 [1]; cumulative frequencies 9, 35, 76, 104, 116, 120 [1]; cumulative percentages 0, 7.5, 29.2, 63.3, 86.7, 96.7, 100 [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** 120 − 76 = 44 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 86.7% [1]. **[1]**

**(d)** 104 − 9 = 95 [1]. **[1]**

**(e)** 32 is 2/5 of the way through the class 30 ≤ t < 35 [1]: 35 + 0.4 × 41 = 51.4, so about 51 runners [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** 7 + x = 19, so x = 12 [1]. 7 + 12 + 15 + y + 6 = 50, so y = 10 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 7, 19, 34, 44, 50 [1]. **[1]**

**8. (a)** A: 21/60 × 100 = 35% [1]; B: 27/90 × 100 = 30% [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Group B, because a smaller percentage scored below 40 (30% against 35%) [1]. **[1]**

**9. (a)** Under 20: 34, under 40: 105 [1]; under 60: 167, under 80: 200 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 200 − 105 = 95 [1]; 95/200 × 100 = 47.5% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** 30 is halfway through the class 20–39, whose boundaries are 20 and 40 [1]: 34 + 0.5 × 71 = 69.5, so about 70 members [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** ≤ 9: 3, ≤ 19: 11, ≤ 29: 26 [1]; ≤ 39: 36, ≤ 50: 40 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** 40 − 11 = 29 [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** 26/40 × 100 = 65% [1]. **[1]**

**11. (a)** The cumulative frequencies are labelled with the class midpoints instead of the upper boundaries [1], and there is no starting entry (m < 0 with cumulative frequency 0) [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** m < 0: 0, m < 2: 5, m < 4: 18, m < 6: 33, m < 8: 40 [1]. **[1]**

**12. (a)** 61 − 29 = 32 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 90 − 61 = 29 [1]; 29/90 × 100 = 32.2% [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** 22 is 2/5 of the way through 20 ≤ l < 25 [1]: 29 + 0.4 × 32 = 41.8, so about 42 fish [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reading "fewer than" at the wrong value for discrete data.
- Using midpoints as labels.
- Omitting the zero starting entry.
- Treating estimates as exact.
- Forgetting to subtract from the total for "at least" or "more than".

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I show interpolation working?**
Write the fraction of the class covered, then the calculation, as in "29 + (2/5) × 32".

**Should cumulative percentages be rounded?**
Round to 1 decimal place or 3 significant figures unless told otherwise, but keep the final entry at exactly 100%.
