Condensed notes for **5.2 Sources of finance** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, 2026–2028). For comparisons of similar sources and four worked scenarios, see the [Sources of finance study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-sources-of-finance-cambridge-alevel-business/); for exam-style questions, see the [Sources of finance practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-business-9609-sources-of-finance-cambridge-alevel-business-practice/).

## What 5.2 asks you to handle

- **5.2.1** Ownership and the availability of sources.
- **5.2.2** Five internal and fourteen external sources.
- **5.2.3** Five factors: cost, flexibility, control, use, existing debt.
- **5.2.4** Choosing the appropriate source in a given situation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal finance | Funds generated from within the business |
| External finance | Funds from outside the business |
| Retained earnings | Profit kept in the business, not paid to owners |
| Sale and leaseback | Selling a non-current asset and renting it back |
| Share capital | Finance raised by selling shares |
| Debenture | A long-term loan to a company with fixed interest and a repayment date |
| Venture capital | Investment in risky new or growing firms in return for a share of ownership |
| Overdraft | Permission to take the bank account below zero, up to a limit |
| Leasing | Renting an asset; never owned |
| Hire purchase | Paying instalments; owned after the final payment |
| Mortgage | Long-term loan secured on property |
| Debt factoring | Selling invoices to a factor for immediate cash, less a fee |
| Trade credit | Buying now and paying the supplier later |
| Micro-finance | Very small loans for people without access to ordinary banks |
| Crowd funding | Raising small sums from many people, usually online |
| Government grant | Money from government, not repaid, with conditions |

## Ownership and access

- **Sole trader**: savings, retained earnings, overdraft, loans, leasing, HP, trade credit, micro-finance, crowd funding, grants.
- **Partnership**: add new partners.
- **Private limited company**: private share sales, venture capital.
- **Plc**: public share issues, debentures.
- Limited liability attracts investors; track record attracts lenders.

## Internal sources and their hidden costs

| Source | Hidden cost |
|---|---|
| Owners' investment | Personal savings at risk; limited amount |
| Retained earnings | Dividends forgone; opportunity cost |
| Sale of unwanted assets | One-off; may sell below value |
| Sale and leaseback | Years of rent; lose rise in property value |
| Working capital | Too little inventory or pressured customers |

## Pairs to distinguish

- **Overdraft** (flexible, short, higher rate, can be withdrawn) vs **loan** (fixed, longer, lower rate).
- **Leasing** (never owned, upgrade easily) vs **hire purchase** (owned at end, interest added).
- **Shares** (no interest, lose control) vs **debentures** (fixed interest, keep control).
- **Venture capital** (large sums, expertise, lose a share) vs **crowd funding** (many small backers, publicity, uncertain).

## Five factors as a checklist

1. **Cost**: interest, fees, dividends, factor's discount.
2. **Flexibility**: can amount or repayments change?
3. **Control**: will owners accept new shareholders?
4. **Use**: match the term of the finance to the life of the need.
5. **Existing debt**: high debt → lenders cautious → equity or internal sources.

## Matching need to source

| Need | Likely source |
|---|---|
| Short, fluctuating cash gap | Overdraft, trade credit, debt factoring |
| Equipment with long life | Bank loan, hire purchase |
| Equipment that dates quickly | Leasing |
| Property | Mortgage, sale and leaseback (to release cash) |
| Large expansion by a plc | Share issue, debentures |
| Risky start-up with no track record | Venture capital, crowd funding, owners' savings |
| Very small enterprise without bank access | Micro-finance |
| Project with a social or regional benefit | Government grant |

## Recommendation method

Name the need → note the ownership form → rule out unavailable sources → test two options against the five factors → choose one (or a combination) → give one drawback and say what the choice depends on.

## Exam traps

- Sole traders and partnerships **cannot** issue shares.
- An overdraft for a factory is a mismatch.
- Retained earnings are not free: they have an opportunity cost.
- Leasing ≠ hire purchase.
- Debt factoring gives **less** than the invoice value.
- A grant must meet conditions and is usually limited.

## Self-test

1. Give two internal sources of finance.
2. Why can't a sole trader raise finance by selling shares?
3. State one difference between leasing and hire purchase.
4. Give one advantage of debentures over share capital for existing shareholders.
5. Why might a heavily indebted company choose a share issue?
6. What is the main cost of sale and leaseback?
7. A factor pays 85% of a $20 000 invoice immediately. How much does the business receive at once?
8. Which source suits a new technology firm with no profits or assets?
9. Give one advantage of crowd funding beyond the money raised.
10. Why is an overdraft suitable for a seasonal cash shortage?
11. Give one reason a bank may refuse a loan to a small business.
12. Name the five factors in 5.2.3.

**Answers**

1. Any two: owners' investment, retained earnings, sale of unwanted assets, sale and leaseback, working capital.
2. A sole trader is not a company, so it has no shares to sell.
3. Leased assets are never owned; hire-purchase assets are owned after the final payment.
4. Debenture holders are lenders, not owners, so existing shareholders keep control.
5. Further borrowing would add interest costs and risk; shares need no repayment or interest.
6. Paying rent for years, and losing any future rise in the asset's value.
7. 0.85 × 20 000 = $17 000.
8. Venture capital (or crowd funding).
9. For example, it tests demand or creates publicity.
10. It is flexible: interest is paid only on the amount used, and the need is short-term.
11. For example, a lack of security or trading record, or high existing debt.
12. Cost, flexibility, need to retain control, the use to which it is put, level of existing debt.
