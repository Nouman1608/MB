These notes condense **A4.1 Evolution and speciation** from the IB DP *Biology guide*, first assessment 2025. Statements A4.1.1 to A4.1.7 are SL and HL; A4.1.8 to A4.1.11 are additional higher level. The [A4.1 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-biology-1-a4-1/) has full explanations and worked examples, and the [A4.1 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-biology-1-a4-1-practice/) test them. The second half of A4 is in the [A4.2 conservation revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-biology-1-a4-2-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Evolution | Change in the heritable characteristics of a population over generations |
| Lamarckism | Rejected idea that acquired characteristics are inherited |
| Selective breeding | Humans choosing which individuals reproduce |
| Homologous structures | Same underlying structure from a common ancestor, possibly different functions |
| Pentadactyl limb | Tetrapod limb: one upper bone, two lower bones, wrist or ankle bones, five digits |
| Analogous structures | Similar function, different structure and origin |
| Convergent evolution | Unrelated lineages evolving similar features under similar selection |
| Speciation | Formation of a new species by splitting of an existing one |
| Reproductive isolation | No gene flow between populations |
| Differential selection | Different alleles favoured in different environments |
| Allopatric (HL) | Speciation after geographical separation |
| Sympatric (HL) | Speciation in the same area, by behavioural or temporal isolation |
| Adaptive radiation (HL) | Many species from one ancestor, each adapted to a different niche |
| Polyploidy (HL) | More than two sets of chromosomes |

## A4.1.1: the definition

Heritable + population + generations. Acquired features are not evolution.

## A4.1.2 to A4.1.4: evidence

| Evidence | Key point | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Sequences | Fewer differences means more recent common ancestor | DNA bases or amino acids compared position by position |
| Selective breeding | Large heritable change in a short time | Maize from teosinte; dog breeds from wolves |
| Homologous structures | Shared plan, different uses, best explained by common descent | Pentadactyl limb in human, bat, whale, horse |

**Sequence method in steps**

1. Line up the sequences.
2. Count differences for each pair.
3. Convert to a percentage if lengths differ.
4. Fewest differences: most closely related.
5. State the assumption: roughly constant rate of change.

## A4.1.5: homology versus analogy

| | Homologous | Analogous |
|---|---|---|
| Underlying structure | Same | Different |
| Origin | Common ancestor | Independent, convergent |
| Function | Same or different | Similar |
| Example | Human arm and bat wing | Bat wing and insect wing |

Tricky case: penguin and dolphin flippers are homologous as forelimbs but analogous as flippers.

## A4.1.6 and A4.1.7: speciation in four steps

1. Isolation stops gene flow (often a geographical barrier).
2. Variation arises independently in each population.
3. Differential selection favours different alleles.
4. Divergence until the populations cannot produce fertile offspring together.

Named example: bonobos (south of the Congo River) and common chimpanzees (north).

Speciation increases species number; extinction decreases it. Change within one lineage alone is not speciation.

## A4.1.8 to A4.1.11 (HL only)

**Allopatric versus sympatric**

| | Allopatric | Sympatric |
|---|---|---|
| Same area? | No | Yes |
| Isolation | Geographical | Behavioural or temporal |

**Adaptive radiation:** many vacant niches (new islands, after mass extinction) → one ancestor gives many species → biodiversity rises with little direct competition.

**Barriers to hybridization**

- Before fertilisation: courtship behaviour, breeding time, habitat.
- After fertilisation: hybrid dies, or hybrid is sterile.
- A sterile hybrid such as a mule cannot pass alleles on, so species stay separate.

**Polyploid speciation (example genus: *Persicaria*)**

1. Two species hybridize.
2. Hybrid chromosomes have no homologous partners: sterile.
3. Chromosome number doubles: every chromosome has a partner, meiosis works, fertile.
4. Crosses back to either parent give sterile offspring: isolated new species.

## Worked reminders

**Percentage difference.** 9 differences in 60 amino acids: 9 ÷ 60 × 100 = **15%**.

**Hybrid chromosomes.** Species with 2n = 24 and 2n = 18 give gametes of 12 and 9: hybrid 21 (sterile); doubled 42 (fertile).

**Net species change.** 40 speciation events and 25 extinctions: +15 species.

## Exam traps

- "The giraffe evolved a long neck to reach leaves": individuals do not evolve with a purpose.
- Homology claimed from function alone.
- Speciation explained by isolation without differential selection.
- Sympatric speciation described with a geographical barrier.
- Sterile hybrids explained without unpaired chromosomes.

## Quick self-test

1. Define evolution.
2. Why is a sun-tan not an example of evolution?
3. Species X and Y differ at 3 of 50 amino acids; X and Z differ at 14. Which pair is more closely related?
4. Give one example of selective breeding as evidence for evolution.
5. Name the bones of the pentadactyl forelimb in order from the shoulder.
6. Are a shark fin and a dolphin flipper homologous or analogous? Why?
7. State the four steps of speciation by isolation.
8. What separates bonobos from common chimpanzees?
9. (HL) Distinguish allopatric from sympatric speciation.
10. (HL) Where is adaptive radiation most likely?
11. (HL) Why is a hybrid of two species with different chromosome numbers usually sterile?
12. (HL) Species with 2n = 14 and 2n = 10 hybridize and the chromosome number doubles. How many chromosomes does the new species have?

### Answers

1. Change in the heritable characteristics of a population over generations.
2. It is acquired in a lifetime and not inherited.
3. X and Y.
4. Maize from teosinte, or dog breeds from wolves.
5. Humerus; radius and ulna; carpals; metacarpals; phalanges.
6. Analogous: similar function, but only the dolphin flipper contains pentadactyl limb bones.
7. Isolation; independent variation; differential selection; divergence until interbreeding fails.
8. The Congo River.
9. Allopatric follows geographical separation; sympatric occurs in the same area by behavioural or temporal isolation.
10. Where there are many vacant niches, such as newly colonised islands.
11. Its chromosomes have no homologous partners, so they cannot pair in meiosis and gametes do not form properly.
12. Gametes 7 + 5 = 12; doubled = **24**.
