These notes condense world history topic 8 for Paper 2: why independence movements arose, how they won, and what new states faced afterwards. Read the [independence movements study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-8/) first for the arguments; test yourself afterwards with the [independence movements practice questions](/resources/ib-ib-dp-world-history-dp-history-ib-dp-world-history-world-history-topics-8-practice/).

## Three stages to revise for every case

| Stage | Questions to answer for each movement |
|---|---|
| Origins and rise | What grievances? Who led early nationalism? When did it gain mass support? What external events helped? |
| Methods and success | Violent, non-violent or both? How important were leaders? What did other states, organizations or wars contribute? |
| After independence | What political, economic, social, ethnic or territorial problems arose? How did the government respond? With what success? |

## Case chronologies

### India (Asia and Oceania)

- 1885: Indian National Congress founded.
- 1906: All-India Muslim League founded.
- 1919: Amritsar massacre; Government of India Act.
- 1920-22: Non-cooperation movement; called off by Gandhi after violence at Chauri Chaura (1922).
- 1930: Salt March; civil disobedience.
- 1935: Government of India Act; provincial elections 1937.
- 1940: Lahore Resolution (Muslim League demand for Pakistan).
- 1942: Quit India; Congress leaders imprisoned.
- 1946: Cabinet Mission fails; Direct Action Day in Calcutta.
- 15 August 1947: independence and partition.
- After: Kashmir conflict from 1947; Gandhi assassinated January 1948; Hyderabad absorbed 1948; constitution in force 26 January 1950; States Reorganisation 1956.

### Algeria (Africa and the Middle East)

- 1830: French conquest begins; later organized as part of France.
- 8 May 1945: Sétif uprising and severe French repression.
- 1 November 1954: FLN launches armed struggle.
- 1957: Battle of Algiers; use of torture by the French army exposed.
- 1958: crisis in Algeria brings de Gaulle back to power; Fifth Republic.
- 1961: OAS settler terrorism against a settlement.
- March 1962: Evian Accords; July 1962: independence after a referendum.
- After: exodus of most European settlers; reprisals against *harkis*; Ben Bella president; overthrown by Boumediene, 1965.

### Ghana (Africa and the Middle East)

- 1948: Accra riots after an ex-servicemen's march.
- 1949: Nkrumah founds the Convention People's Party (CPP).
- 1950: "Positive Action" campaign; Nkrumah imprisoned.
- 1951: CPP wins election; Nkrumah released to lead the government.
- 6 March 1957: independence.
- After: Preventive Detention Act 1958; republic 1960; one-party state 1964; Volta River project; coup 1966.

### Spanish America (the Americas)

- 1808: Napoleon invades Spain; crisis of royal authority.
- 1810: Hidalgo's revolt in Mexico; juntas in South America.
- 1810s-1820s: wars led by Bolívar in the north and San Martín in the south.
- 1821: Mexican independence.
- 1824: Battle of Ayacucho effectively ends Spanish rule in South America.
- After: *caudillo* rule, regional division (Gran Colombia breaks up by 1831), economic disruption.

## Factor checklist for causes and success

- **Nationalism and identity:** education, press, religion, language.
- **Economic grievances:** land loss, taxation, exploitation of resources, colonial trade rules.
- **Political exclusion:** lack of representation; broken promises after wars.
- **Leadership:** Gandhi, Nehru, Jinnah, Nkrumah, Ho Chi Minh, Bolívar.
- **Organization:** mass parties, trade unions, armed fronts.
- **Methods:** non-violent mass protest, elections and negotiation, strikes, guerrilla war, terrorism, diplomacy.
- **External factors:** world wars, the Cold War, the United Nations, support from neighbouring states, the colonial power's own politics.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Decolonization | The end of colonial rule, whether won, negotiated or granted |
| Nationalism | Belief that a nation should have its own state; a movement to achieve it |
| Mass mobilization | Drawing large numbers of ordinary people into political action |
| Satyagraha | Gandhi's method of non-violent resistance ("truth force") |
| Civil disobedience | Deliberately breaking unjust laws peacefully |
| Guerrilla war | Irregular warfare by small mobile units against a stronger army |
| Partition | Division of a territory into separate states |
| Settler colony | A colony with a large European population, which made decolonization harder |
| *Pieds-noirs* | European settlers in French Algeria |
| *Harkis* | Algerian Muslims who fought for France |
| One-party state | A state in which only one political party is permitted to rule |

## Exam traps

- **Writing "Gandhi won independence".** Weigh him against British weakness, Labour policy, the Second World War and the Muslim League.
- **Algeria and Ghana as a different-region pair.** Both are in Africa and the Middle East.
- **Forgetting the colonial power's choices.** British willingness to leave the Gold Coast and French refusal in Algeria explain much of the difference in methods.
- **No criteria for "success".** Speed, cost, unity and stability are all possible measures; say which you use.
- **Neglecting stage three.** Questions on post-independence challenges are common and need their own revision.

## Self-test

1. When was the Indian National Congress founded? *1885.*
2. Why did Gandhi call off non-cooperation in 1922? *Because of violence at Chauri Chaura, where police were killed.*
3. What did the Lahore Resolution of 1940 demand? *A separate homeland, later Pakistan, for Muslims of the subcontinent.*
4. When did the FLN begin its armed struggle? *1 November 1954.*
5. How did the Algerian war affect French politics? *The 1958 crisis brought de Gaulle back and led to the Fifth Republic.*
6. What did the Evian Accords of 1962 achieve? *A ceasefire and the terms for Algerian independence after a referendum.*
7. Which party did Nkrumah found, and when? *The Convention People's Party, 1949.*
8. Give two signs of authoritarian drift in Ghana after 1957. *The Preventive Detention Act (1958) and the one-party state (1964).*
9. What event in 1808 opened the way to Spanish American independence? *Napoleon's invasion of Spain.*
10. Name two challenges India faced in the first years after 1947. *Any two: partition violence and refugees, Kashmir, integrating princely states, framing the constitution, linguistic demands, poverty.*
11. Who were the *harkis*? *Algerian Muslims who fought on the French side.*
12. Name one external factor that helped independence movements after 1945. *Any one: weakened colonial powers, the UN and its support for self-determination, Cold War pressures, support from newly independent states.*
