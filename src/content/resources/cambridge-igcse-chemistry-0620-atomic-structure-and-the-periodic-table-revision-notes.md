## The atom

A central **nucleus** containing **protons** and **neutrons**, surrounded by **electrons** in **shells**.

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | 1/1840 (negligible) | Shells |

- Nucleus: nearly all the mass, very small volume.
- Atom: no overall charge, because protons = electrons.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition or meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton number (atomic number) | The number of protons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Mass number (nucleon number) | The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom |
| Nucleon | A proton or a neutron |
| Shell | An energy level that electrons occupy around the nucleus |
| Electronic configuration | The arrangement of electrons in shells, such as 2,8,3 |
| Ion | An atom or group of atoms with an overall charge, formed by losing or gaining electrons |
| Group | A vertical column of the Periodic Table |
| Period | A horizontal row of the Periodic Table |

## Counting particles

- Protons = proton number.
- Neutrons = mass number − proton number.
- Electrons in an atom = proton number.
- Positive ion: electrons = protons − charge.
- Negative ion: electrons = protons + size of charge.

In a symbol such as ²⁷₁₃Al³⁺: top number 27 is the mass number, bottom number 13 is the proton number, top-right 3+ is the charge. Particles: 13 protons, 14 neutrons, 10 electrons.

## Ions

| Ion | Electrons | Configuration | Same as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Li⁺ | 2 | 2 | He |
| O²⁻, F⁻, Na⁺, Mg²⁺, Al³⁺ | 10 | 2,8 | Ne |
| S²⁻, Cl⁻, K⁺, Ca²⁺ | 18 | 2,8,8 | Ar |

Find the electron count first, then fill the shells.

## Configuration and position

- Group (I to VII) = number of outer-shell electrons.
- Period = number of occupied shells.
- Group VIII noble gases: full outer shell.
- Helium (2) is in Group VIII: its only shell is full.

Example: 2,8,5 → Group V, Period 3 → phosphorus.

## Method for any ion

1. Find the proton number (from the symbol or the Periodic Table).
2. Positive charge: subtract the charge. Negative charge: add the size of the charge.
3. Fill shells 2, 8, 8 with that number of electrons.
4. Check that the numbers add up to your electron count.

Worked: the phosphide ion, P³⁻. Proton number 15, so 15 + 3 = 18 electrons. Configuration 2,8,8. Check: 2 + 8 + 8 = 18.

Worked: the beryllium ion, Be²⁺. Proton number 4, so 4 − 2 = 2 electrons. Configuration 2.

## Method for "identify the element"

- From a configuration: add the numbers to get the proton number, then look it up.
- From group and period: the period gives the number of shells, the group gives the outer electrons.
- Example: Group IV, Period 2 → 2,4 → 6 electrons → carbon.

## Exam traps

- Electron mass is not zero and not −1: it is about 1/1840.
- Neutrons = mass number **minus** proton number.
- Potassium is 2,8,8,1, not 2,8,9.
- Positive ions have **fewer** electrons than protons.
- Proton number, not mass number, identifies the element.
- Helium is not in Group II.
- An ion has the same element identity as its atom; only electrons change.

## Self-test

1. State the relative mass and relative charge of a neutron.
2. Define mass number.
3. Where is almost all the mass of an atom?
4. Why is an atom neutral?
5. How many neutrons are in an atom of sodium, mass number 23, proton number 11?
6. Write the electronic configuration of silicon (proton number 14).
7. Write the electronic configuration of the fluoride ion, F⁻ (proton number 9).
8. How many electrons are in a Mg²⁺ ion (proton number 12)?
9. An element has configuration 2,8,8,1. State its group and period.
10. An element is in Group VI, Period 2. Write its configuration and name it.
11. Why is helium placed in Group VIII?
12. Name two ions with the configuration 2,8,8.

**Answers**

1. Mass 1; charge 0.
2. The total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus of an atom.
3. In the nucleus.
4. It has equal numbers of protons and electrons, whose charges cancel.
5. 23 − 11 = 12.
6. 2,8,4.
7. 2,8 (10 electrons).
8. 10.
9. Group I, Period 4 (potassium).
10. 2,6: oxygen.
11. Its outer (only) shell is full, like the other noble gases.
12. Any two: S²⁻, Cl⁻, K⁺, Ca²⁺.
