These questions cover subtopic **2.5 Equations** in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. Questions without a label use content common to Core (C2.5) and Extended (E2.5). Questions marked **(Extended)** use E2.5 content only: fractional equations, quadratic equations, a linear with a non-linear equation, and harder rearranging. Questions marked (calculator) need one; the rest can be done without.

For worked examples, read the [equations study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-equations-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a condensed summary, use the [equations revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-equations-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Solve 4x + 9 = 1. **[2]**

**2.** Solve 5 − 3x = 2(x + 10). **[3]**

**3.** Pens cost p cents each and rulers cost r cents each.

**(a)** Write an expression for the total cost, in cents, of 4 pens and 3 rulers. **[1]**

**(b)** A ruler costs 10 cents more than a pen, and 4 pens and 3 rulers cost 240 cents altogether. Form an equation in p and solve it. **[3]**

**4.** Make t the subject of s = 5t − 2u. **[2]**

**5.** (Extended) Solve x² + 3x − 18 = 0. **[2]**

**6.** (Extended) Solve (2x − 1)/5 = (x + 4)/3. **[3]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Solve the simultaneous equations. Show all your working.

3x + 2y = 4

5x − 3y = 13 **[4]**

**8.** A farm has only chickens and goats. There are 30 animals altogether, with 84 legs between them. A chicken has 2 legs and a goat has 4.

**(a)** Using c for the number of chickens and g for the number of goats, write down two equations. **[2]**

**(b)** Solve your equations to find the number of each animal. **[2]**

**9.** The angles of a triangle are (x + 20)°, (2x − 10)° and (3x + 50)°. Form and solve an equation in x, and hence find the size of the largest angle. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended)

**(a)** Solve x² − 8x + 13 = 0 by completing the square. Give your answers in the form p ± √q. **[3]**

**(b)** (calculator) Solve 5x² − 2x − 4 = 0, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended)

**(a)** Show that the equation 6/(x + 2) + 1/x = 1 can be written as x² − 5x − 2 = 0. **[3]**

**(b)** (calculator) Solve x² − 5x − 2 = 0, giving your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[3]**

**12.** (Extended) Solve the simultaneous equations.

y = x − 2

x² + xy = 12 **[5]**

**13.** (Extended) Make x the subject of each formula.

**(a)** p = √((x + 3)/q) **[3]**

**(b)** ax + 3 = b(2 − x) **[3]**

**14.** (Extended) A right-angled triangle has base (x + 1) cm and height (2x − 3) cm. Its area is 9 cm².

**(a)** Show that 2x² − x − 21 = 0. **[2]**

**(b)** Solve the equation and find the base and height of the triangle. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** 4x = −8 [1], **x = −2** [1].

**2.** 5 − 3x = 2x + 20 [1]. −15 = 5x [1]. **x = −3** [1].

**3. (a)** **4p + 3r** [1].

**(b)** r = p + 10, so 4p + 3(p + 10) = 240 [1]. 7p + 30 = 240, 7p = 210 [1]. **p = 30** [1] (a pen costs 30 cents).

**4.** s + 2u = 5t [1], **t = (s + 2u)/5** [1].

**5.** (x + 6)(x − 3) = 0 [1]. **x = −6 or x = 3** [1].

**6.** Multiply both sides by 15: 3(2x − 1) = 5(x + 4) [1]. 6x − 3 = 5x + 20 [1]. **x = 23** [1].

**7.** Multiply to match a coefficient, e.g. 9x + 6y = 12 and 10x − 6y = 26 [1]. Add: 19x = 38 [1], **x = 2** [1]. Substitute: 6 + 2y = 4, **y = −1** [1].
*Examiner insight:* check in the equation you did not substitute into: 5(2) − 3(−1) = 13. If it fails, the error is usually in the add-or-subtract step.

**8. (a)** **c + g = 30** [1] and **2c + 4g = 84** [1].

**(b)** Doubling the first gives 2c + 2g = 60; subtracting gives 2g = 24 [1]. **12 goats and 18 chickens** [1].

**9.** x + 20 + 2x − 10 + 3x + 50 = 180, so 6x + 60 = 180 [1]. x = 20 [1]. Angles 40°, 30° and 110°; largest **110°** [1].

**10. (a)** (x − 4)² − 16 + 13 = 0 [1]. (x − 4)² = 3 [1]. **x = 4 ± √3** [1].

**(b)** x = (2 ± √((−2)² − 4 × 5 × (−4)))/(2 × 5) [1] = (2 ± √84)/10 [1]. **x = 1.12 or x = −0.72** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the method mark needs the substituted formula on the page. Writing b² − 4ac as 4 − 80 instead of 4 + 80 is the commonest error.

**11. (a)** Multiply every term by x(x + 2): 6x + (x + 2) = x(x + 2) [1]. 7x + 2 = x² + 2x [1]. Rearranged: **x² − 5x − 2 = 0** [1].

**(b)** x = (5 ± √(25 + 8))/2 [1] = (5 ± √33)/2 [1]. **x = 5.37 or x = −0.37** [1].

**12.** Substitute: x² + x(x − 2) = 12 [1]. 2x² − 2x − 12 = 0, so x² − x − 6 = 0 [1]. (x − 3)(x + 2) = 0 [1]. x = 3 or x = −2 [1]. **x = 3, y = 1** and **x = −2, y = −4** [1].
*Examiner insight:* the last mark needs both pairs, with each y found from y = x − 2.

**13. (a)** Square both sides: p² = (x + 3)/q [1]. p²q = x + 3 [1]. **x = p²q − 3** [1].

**(b)** ax + 3 = 2b − bx [1]. ax + bx = 2b − 3, so x(a + b) = 2b − 3 [1]. **x = (2b − 3)/(a + b)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the x appears twice. Collecting the x terms and factorising is the step that earns the second mark.

**14. (a)** ½(x + 1)(2x − 3) = 9, so (x + 1)(2x − 3) = 18 [1]. 2x² − x − 3 = 18, giving **2x² − x − 21 = 0** [1].

**(b)** (2x − 7)(x + 3) = 0 [1]. x = 3.5 or x = −3; x = −3 gives a negative length, so **x = 3.5** [1]. Base **4.5 cm**, height **4 cm** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Expanding 2(x + 10) as 2x + 10.
- Choosing to add when the equations should be subtracted, or the reverse.
- Not multiplying every term by the common denominator.
- Writing the wrong sign under the square root in the quadratic formula.
- Giving one pair of solutions when there are two.
- Keeping a negative length without comment in a context question.
