## Key terms

- **Norms** — expected behaviours in a situation. **Values** — shared beliefs about what matters.
- **Role** — behaviour attached to a position. **Status** — position in society, **ascribed** (born into) or **achieved** (earned).
- **Culture** — the whole way of life of a group.
- **Socialisation** — **primary** in the family, **secondary** through school, peers, media, religion, workplace.
- **Social control** — **formal** (laws, police, courts) and **informal** (disapproval, ridicule, exclusion).

## The three judging criteria

| Term | Question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Reliability** | Would repeating the study give the same result? |
| **Validity** | Does it measure what it claims to measure? |
| **Representativeness** | Can the findings be generalised to the population? |

Reliability and validity usually **trade off**: structured methods are reliable but shallow; unstructured methods are valid but hard to replicate.

## Methods compared

| Method | Strengths | Weaknesses |
|---|---|---|
| Questionnaire | Large sample, cheap, reliable | Low response rate, lacks depth |
| Structured interview | Comparable, reliable | Limited depth, interviewer effect |
| Unstructured interview | Rich, valid | Time-consuming, not replicable |
| Participant observation | High validity, natural behaviour | Not reliable, risk of going native |
| Official statistics | Large scale, free | Collected for other purposes, may be manipulated |

**Primary** data = collected first-hand. **Secondary** = already exists.
**Quantitative** = numbers. **Qualitative** = descriptive depth.

## Sampling

Random · systematic (every nth) · stratified (proportional by group) · quota · snowball (hard-to-reach groups).

A larger sample reduces **sampling error** but does **not** remove **bias** — a biased method stays biased at any size.

## Perspectives in one line each

- **Functionalism** — institutions work together for stability through shared values.
- **Marxism** — society shaped by class conflict over economic resources.
- **Feminism** — society is patriarchal, organised in men's interests.
- **Interactionism** — society built from the meanings people give situations.

## Exam traps

- Reliability and validity are **not** synonyms — the most penalised confusion in this subject.
- Functionalism and Marxism are **structural** (macro), not micro.
- Don't say a bigger sample removes bias.
- Always name a sociologist or study where you can — unsupported assertions score low.
- Match the method to the *research aim*; evaluating a method in the abstract loses marks.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish reliability from validity.
2. Which method best suits studying a secretive religious group, and why?
3. What is the difference between ascribed and achieved status?
4. Name two agents of secondary socialisation.
5. Why might official crime statistics be invalid?

**Answers:** 1. Reliability = consistency on repetition; validity = whether it truly measures what it claims. 2. Participant observation — access and trust are needed, and it gives valid insight into meanings; though it is not reliable or representative. 3. Ascribed is fixed at birth (e.g. royalty, caste); achieved is earned through effort (e.g. doctor). 4. Any two: school, peer group, media, religion, workplace. 5. Not all crime is reported or recorded; police recording practices and priorities shape the figures, so they measure recorded crime rather than actual crime.
