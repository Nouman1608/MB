Condensed recall for **3.3 The mole and the Avogadro constant**, Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry
0620. For a worked example of every calculation type, use the
[Mole and the Avogadro Constant study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-the-mole-and-the-avogadro-constant/).

> **Tier note:** the only **Core** outcome is that concentration can be measured in
> **g/dm³ or mol/dm³**. Everything else here is *(Supplement)*.

## Key facts

- **Mole (mol):** the unit of amount of substance.
- **One mole** contains **6.02 × 10²³** particles (atoms, ions or molecules): the
  **Avogadro constant**.
- **Molar gas volume:** **24 dm³** per mole of any gas at r.t.p.
- **Concentration** units: **g/dm³** or **mol/dm³**.
- 1 dm³ = **1000 cm³**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Amount of substance | Quantity measured in moles |
| Molar mass | Mass of one mole, in g/mol (same number as Mr) |
| r.t.p. | Room temperature and pressure |
| Limiting reactant | Reactant that is used up first; it fixes the amount of product |
| Excess | Reactant left over when the reaction stops |
| Titre | Volume of solution added from the burette to reach the end-point |
| Theoretical yield | Maximum mass of product calculated from the equation |

## The equations

```
n = m ÷ M                    m = n × M          M = m ÷ n
particles = n × 6.02 × 10²³
V(gas, dm³) = n × 24          (n = V ÷ 24)
n = c × V(dm³)                c = n ÷ V          V = n ÷ c
g/dm³ = mol/dm³ × M
```

## Every calculation in three moves

1. **Into moles** from whatever you are given.
2. **Ratio** from the balanced equation.
3. **Out of moles** into what the question wants.

## Limiting reactant check

Work out moles of each reactant → divide each by its coefficient → the **smaller** result
is the limiting reactant. Base the product amount on that reactant only.

Example: N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃ with 1.0 mol N₂ and 2.4 mol H₂: 1.0 ÷ 1 = 1.0; 2.4 ÷ 3 = 0.80 →
**hydrogen is limiting**; NH₃ formed = 2.4 × 2/3 = 1.6 mol.

## Titration steps

1. Moles of the solution you know: n = c × V (V in dm³).
2. Ratio from the equation (1 : 1 for HCl + NaOH; 1 : 2 for H₂SO₄ + NaOH).
3. Concentration or volume of the other solution: c = n ÷ V or V = n ÷ c.

## Empirical formula steps

mass or % of each element → ÷ Ar → ÷ smallest → whole numbers (multiply if you get
values such as 1.5 or 1.33) → empirical formula. Molecular formula = empirical formula ×
(Mr ÷ empirical formula mass).

## Percentages

```
% yield       = actual ÷ theoretical × 100
% composition = (Ar × number of atoms) ÷ Mr × 100
% purity      = mass of pure substance ÷ mass of sample × 100
```

## Mass to gas volume in one run

*Volume of CO₂ at r.t.p. from heating 4.2 g of magnesium carbonate (Mr 84):*
MgCO₃ → MgO + CO₂. Into moles: 4.2 ÷ 84 = 0.050 mol. Ratio 1 : 1 → 0.050 mol CO₂. Out of
moles: 0.050 × 24 = **1.2 dm³** (1200 cm³).

## Yield versus purity

| | Percentage yield | Percentage purity |
|---|---|---|
| Compares | Product actually made with the maximum possible | Pure substance with the whole sample |
| Below 100% because | Losses in transfer, incomplete reaction, side reactions | Sample contains other substances |
| Applies to | A product | A starting material or a product |

## Exam traps

- **cm³ → dm³:** divide by 1000 before using n = c × V.
- **Gases only** at 24 dm³/mol; never use it for a solid or a solution.
- **Diatomic elements:** O₂ = 32 g/mol, Cl₂ = 71 g/mol, H₂ = 2 g/mol.
- **Atoms versus molecules:** 1 mol of H₂O has 6.02 × 10²³ molecules but three times as many
  atoms.
- **Ratio** before conversion: a 1 : 2 ratio missed halves or doubles the answer.
- **Limiting reactant** is decided by moles divided by coefficient, not by the smaller mass.
- **Ratios of 1 : 1.5** in empirical formulae must be doubled to 2 : 3, not rounded.
- **Significant figures:** usually three, matching the data.

## Self-test

1. State the value of the Avogadro constant.
2. Convert 250 cm³ to dm³.
3. How many moles are in 10 g of calcium carbonate (Mr 100)?
4. What is the mass of 0.50 mol of magnesium oxide (Mr 40)?
5. What volume does 0.25 mol of nitrogen gas occupy at r.t.p.?
6. How many moles of hydrogen chloride are in 30.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid?
7. Convert 0.050 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide (Mr 40) into g/dm³.
8. 0.30 mol of Mg reacts with 0.50 mol of HCl (Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂). Which is limiting?
9. A compound is 0.6 mol of iron atoms to 0.9 mol of oxygen atoms. Give its empirical
   formula.
10. An empirical formula is CH₂O and the Mr is 180. What is the molecular formula?
11. Theoretical yield 20.0 g, actual 15.0 g. Calculate the percentage yield.
12. Calculate the percentage by mass of oxygen in water.

**Answers**

1. 6.02 × 10²³ per mol.
2. 0.250 dm³.
3. 0.10 mol.
4. 20 g.
5. 6.0 dm³.
6. 0.200 × 0.0300 = 6.00 × 10⁻³ mol.
7. 0.050 × 40 = 2.0 g/dm³.
8. HCl: 0.50 ÷ 2 = 0.25 is smaller than 0.30 ÷ 1, so hydrochloric acid is limiting.
9. 0.6 : 0.9 = 2 : 3, so Fe₂O₃.
10. 180 ÷ 30 = 6, so C₆H₁₂O₆.
11. 15.0 ÷ 20.0 × 100 = 75.0%.
12. 16 ÷ 18 × 100 = 88.9%.
