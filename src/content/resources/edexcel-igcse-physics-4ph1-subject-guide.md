Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1) is a complete course in
physics, running from forces and motion through electricity, waves and
energy to radioactivity and astrophysics. Its eight topics give students a
working knowledge of the main ideas of physics and practice in using
equations, units and data. The qualification is assessed by two untiered,
externally assessed written papers: Paper 1 lasts 120 minutes and is worth
110 marks (61.1%), and Paper 2 lasts 75 minutes and is worth 70 marks
(38.9%). Calculators are permitted in both. The course was first assessed in
2019.

## How the course is organised

The eight topics each begin with a short section on units, which sets out
the quantities and units used in that topic, followed by the main content.

- **1 Forces and motion**: movement and position; forces, movement, shape
  and momentum.
- **2 Electricity**: mains electricity; energy and voltage in circuits;
  electric charge.
- **3 Waves**: properties of waves; the electromagnetic spectrum; light and
  sound.
- **4 Energy resources and energy transfers**: energy transfers; work and
  power; energy resources and electricity generation.
- **5 Solids, liquids and gases**: density and pressure; change of state;
  ideal gas molecules.
- **6 Magnetism and electromagnetism**: magnetism; electromagnetism;
  electromagnetic induction.
- **7 Radioactivity and particles**: radioactivity; fission and fusion.
- **8 Astrophysics**: motion in the universe; stellar evolution; cosmology.

Topics 1 to 4 deal with motion, electricity, waves and energy, the ideas
used most often across the course. Topics 5 to 8 apply those ideas to
matter, magnetic effects, the nucleus and the universe.

## How it's assessed

4PH1 is a linear qualification with a single route: there are no tiers and
no optional papers.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 4PH1/1P | Physics Paper 1 | 120 minutes (2h) | 110 | 61.1% |
| 4PH1/2P | Physics Paper 2 | 75 minutes (1h15) | 70 | 38.9% |

Both papers are available in the November and June series, and calculators
are permitted in both. The two papers differ in scope. Paper 1 assesses only
the core content, the statements that are not in bold and not marked with a
"P". Paper 2 assesses all of the content, including the bold, "P"-referenced
statements.

Pearson now also offers a separate modular route to International GCSE
Physics (codes 4WPH1 and 4WPH2, first assessed June 2025). This page
describes the established linear 4PH1 route only.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 carries the larger share of the marks (61.1%) and is built entirely from
core content, so the core statements across all eight topics are the
foundation of the grade. Paper 2 then tests that same core again alongside
the "P"-referenced statements. Knowing which statements in each topic carry a
"P" lets you see exactly what extra depth Paper 2 can ask for.

Physics ideas also connect across the topic list. Energy appears in almost
every topic: in circuits, in waves, in changes of state, in electricity
generation and in nuclear fission and fusion. Forces and motion supply the
language of speed, acceleration and momentum that returns in astrophysics,
where motion in the universe is studied. Electricity and magnetism come
together in electromagnetism and electromagnetic induction, and the
electromagnetic spectrum links waves with radioactivity and astronomy.

The units section at the start of each topic is a reminder of a skill that
runs through the whole qualification. Choosing the right equation, rearranging
it, substituting values with correct units and giving an answer to a sensible
number of significant figures are practised in every topic and rewarded on
both papers.

## Planning your study

The topic order in the specification works well as a teaching order. In the
first year, cover Forces and motion, Electricity, Waves and Energy resources
and energy transfers. These four topics contain the core quantities and
equations that the rest of the course relies on. In the second year, move on
to Solids, liquids and gases, Magnetism and electromagnetism, Radioactivity
and particles, and Astrophysics.

As you go, mark which statements in each topic are "P"-referenced, so that
your notes show clearly what belongs to Paper 2 only. Build an equation list
topic by topic and practise using each equation in short problems as soon as
it is introduced.

Physics questions often present information as graphs, tables or
diagrams, so practise reading values from a graph, describing a trend and
calculating a gradient whenever a topic offers the chance. These skills are
not tied to any one topic, which makes them easy to neglect and valuable to
keep practising.

Ask your teacher whether you will sit the papers in a November or a June
series, then plan timed past-paper practice for the final term before that
series. For how to approach each paper on the day, use the exam-preparation
page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Physics 4PH1?**
There are two papers. Paper 1 lasts 120 minutes and carries 110 marks (61.1%), and Paper 2 lasts 75 minutes and carries 70 marks (38.9%).

**Is IGCSE Physics 4PH1 tiered?**
No. Both papers are untiered, and calculators are permitted in both.

**What does the "P" mean in the 4PH1 specification?**
Statements in bold and marked "P" are assessed only on Paper 2. Paper 1 assesses the core content, while Paper 2 assesses all content, including the "P"-referenced statements.

**What are the eight topics in 4PH1?**
Forces and motion; electricity; waves; energy resources and energy transfers; solids, liquids and gases; magnetism and electromagnetism; radioactivity and particles; and astrophysics.

**Is there a modular version of IGCSE Physics?**
Pearson also offers a separate modular route (4WPH1 and 4WPH2, first assessed June 2025). This page covers the linear 4PH1 route.
