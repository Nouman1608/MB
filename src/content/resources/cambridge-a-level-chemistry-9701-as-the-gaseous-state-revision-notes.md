**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level**, subtopic 4.1.

## The three learning outcomes

1. **Explain** the origin of gas pressure: collisions of gas molecules with the container wall.
2. **Understand** that ideal gases have **zero particle volume** and **no intermolecular forces of attraction**.
3. **State and use** pV = nRT, including to determine **Mr**.

## Origin of pressure

- Molecules move randomly and collide with the walls.
- Each collision changes the molecule's momentum, so a force acts on the wall.
- **Pressure = total force of collisions per unit area.**

| Change (others constant) | Effect on collisions with walls | Pressure |
|---|---|---|
| more molecules | more frequent | rises |
| higher temperature | more frequent and more forceful | rises |
| smaller volume | more frequent | rises |

## Ideal and real gases

| Ideal gas assumption | Why real gases differ |
|---|---|
| particles have zero volume | real molecules have a volume, which matters when they are close together |
| no intermolecular forces of attraction | real molecules attract, which matters when they are close and slow |

- Real gases are **most ideal** at **high temperature** and **low pressure**.
- Real gases **deviate most** at **low temperature** and **high pressure**.
- Gases with **stronger intermolecular forces** (e.g. NH₃, with hydrogen bonding) deviate more than gases with weak forces (e.g. He).

## The equation and units

    pV = nRT        R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ (data booklet)

| Quantity | Must be in | Convert from |
|---|---|---|
| p | Pa | kPa × 10³ |
| V | m³ | dm³ × 10⁻³; cm³ × 10⁻⁶ |
| T | K | °C + 273 |
| n | mol | mass ÷ M |

**Rearrangements**

    n = pV / RT      p = nRT / V      V = nRT / p      T = pV / nR
    Mr = mRT / pV    (m in g)
    Mr = ρRT / p     (ρ in g m⁻³; 1 g dm⁻³ = 1000 g m⁻³)

**Sense check:** 1 mol of ideal gas occupies about 22.4 dm³ at 273 K and 101 kPa, and 24.0 dm³ at room conditions (data booklet values).

## Determining Mr of a volatile liquid

1. Weigh a small syringe of liquid; inject some into a heated gas syringe; reweigh.
2. Record the vapour volume, the oven temperature and the pressure.
3. n = pV/RT, then Mr = mass / n.

| Error | Effect on measured V | Effect on calculated Mr |
|---|---|---|
| liquid not fully vaporised | too small | too high |
| vapour condenses on cool parts | too small | too high |
| vapour escapes before reading | too small | too high |
| mass of liquid recorded too high | — | too high |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ideal gas | Model gas with zero particle volume and no intermolecular attractions, obeying pV = nRT exactly |
| Real gas | Actual gas, which approximately obeys pV = nRT |
| Pressure | Force per unit area, caused by molecular collisions with the wall |
| Absolute temperature | Temperature in kelvin, T/K = θ/°C + 273 |
| Molar gas constant, R | 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ |

## Exam traps

- **Convert units first**: Pa, m³, K. kPa, dm³ or °C give errors of 10³ or 273.
- Ideal particles have **zero** volume, not "small" volume.
- Real gases deviate at **high** pressure and **low** temperature.
- Keep unrounded values through a calculation; give the answer to 3 s.f. if the data are 3 s.f.
- Include units on the answer (Pa, m³ or dm³, mol, g mol⁻¹ or no unit for Mr).
- A **smaller** measured volume makes Mr **larger**.

## Self-test

1. Explain how a gas exerts pressure on its container.
2. State the two ideal gas assumptions named in the syllabus.
3. Under what conditions does a real gas behave most like an ideal gas?
4. Convert 350 cm³ to m³, 98.0 kPa to Pa and 37 °C to K.
5. Calculate the amount of gas in 1.00 dm³ at 300 K and 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa.
6. 0.0100 mol of gas is at 400 K in a 500 cm³ container. Calculate the pressure.
7. 0.150 g of a gas occupies 60.0 cm³ at 300 K and 1.04 × 10⁵ Pa. Calculate Mr.
8. A gas has density 1.43 g dm⁻³ at 273 K and 101 kPa. Calculate its Mr and suggest its identity.
9. Explain why ammonia deviates more from ideal behaviour than helium.
10. In a determination of Mr, some liquid did not evaporate. Explain the effect on the calculated Mr.
11. Calculate the temperature at which 0.200 mol of gas occupies 5.00 dm³ at 1.00 × 10⁵ Pa.

**Answers:**

1. Molecules collide with the walls; each collision exerts a force; pressure is the total force per unit area.
2. Zero particle volume; no intermolecular forces of attraction.
3. High temperature and low pressure.
4. 3.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³; 9.80 × 10⁴ Pa; 310 K.
5. n = (1.00 × 10⁵ × 1.00 × 10⁻³) / (8.31 × 300) = 0.0401 mol.
6. p = (0.0100 × 8.31 × 400) / (5.00 × 10⁻⁴) = 6.65 × 10⁴ Pa.
7. n = (1.04 × 10⁵ × 60.0 × 10⁻⁶) / (8.31 × 300) = 2.503 × 10⁻³ mol; Mr = 0.150 / 2.503 × 10⁻³ = 59.9.
8. Mr = (1430 × 8.31 × 273) / (1.01 × 10⁵) = 32.1; oxygen, O₂.
9. Ammonia molecules have stronger intermolecular forces (hydrogen bonding) and a larger volume, so the assumptions fail more.
10. The vapour volume measured is too small, so n is too small and Mr is too high.
11. T = pV/nR = (1.00 × 10⁵ × 5.00 × 10⁻³) / (0.200 × 8.31) = 301 K.
