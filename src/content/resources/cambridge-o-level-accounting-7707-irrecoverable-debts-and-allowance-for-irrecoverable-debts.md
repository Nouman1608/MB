## Irrecoverable debts

An **irrecoverable debt** is an amount owed by a credit customer that the
business decides will not be paid, for example because the customer has
become bankrupt, has disappeared, or the cost of chasing the debt is greater
than the amount. Keeping it in trade receivables would overstate assets, so
it is **written off**:

- Debit **irrecoverable debts** (an expense).
- Credit the **customer's account** (removing the debt from trade
receivables).

At the year end the irrecoverable debts account is closed by transferring
its total to the statement of profit or loss.

An **irrecoverable debt recovered** is a debt written off in an earlier
period that the customer later pays, in full or in part. Two entries are
made:

1. **Reinstate the debt:** debit the customer's account, credit
irrecoverable debts recovered. This rebuilds a full record of the
customer's history, which matters if the customer asks for credit again.
2. **Record the receipt:** debit bank (or cash), credit the customer's
account.

Irrecoverable debts recovered is **income** in the statement of profit or
loss of the year it is received.

## The allowance for irrecoverable debts

At the year end some of the remaining trade receivables will probably not be
collected, even though no single customer can yet be named. The business
therefore keeps an **allowance for irrecoverable debts**, usually a
percentage of trade receivables based on past experience.

**Why it is needed:**

- **Prudence:** trade receivables are not shown at more than the business
expects to collect.
- **Matching:** the expected loss on this year's credit sales is charged in
this year, not in a later year when the customer finally fails to pay.
- **Realistic figures** for owners, lenders and others who use the
statement of financial position.

**How it is recorded:**

| Event | Journal entry | Effect on profit |
|---|---|---|
| Allowance created | Dr Statement of profit or loss, Cr Allowance for irrecoverable debts (full amount) | Reduced |
| Allowance increased | Dr Statement of profit or loss, Cr Allowance (increase only) | Reduced |
| Allowance decreased | Dr Allowance, Cr Statement of profit or loss (decrease only) | Increased |

The allowance account keeps a **credit balance**. In the statement of
financial position the whole allowance is deducted from trade receivables.
Always calculate the allowance on trade receivables **after** writing off
irrecoverable debts.

## Worked example: Tariq Electricals over three years

Tariq keeps an allowance of 2.5% of trade receivables. His year ends on 31
December.

- **2026.** Trade receivables at 31 December, after write-offs, were
$32,000. No allowance existed before.
- **2027.** On 30 June R Lim's debt of $640 was written off as
irrecoverable. Trade receivables at 31 December, after this, were $36,000.
- **2028.** On 15 August R Lim unexpectedly paid the $640 in full by bank
transfer. On 31 December K Diaz's debt of $420 was written off, leaving
trade receivables of $28,000.

## Common mistakes

- Charging the **full** allowance every year instead of only the change.
- Calculating the allowance before writing off the year's irrecoverable
debts.
- Recording a recovered debt by crediting the customer directly, without
first reinstating the account.
- Putting the allowance under current liabilities instead of deducting it
from trade receivables.
- Showing a decrease in the allowance as an expense.
- Forgetting that a recovered debt is income of the year in which it is
received, not an adjustment to the year it was written off.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the allowance in three lines: receivables after write-offs,
required allowance, change from last year.
- **Prepare** the allowance account with the balance brought down from last
year, the change, and the new balance carried down.
- **Explain** the need for the allowance with a concept and its effect:
"prudence: trade receivables are not overstated, so the statement of
financial position shows what is likely to be collected".
- When a question asks for the effect on profit, separate the
irrecoverable debts expense from the change in the allowance and state each.
