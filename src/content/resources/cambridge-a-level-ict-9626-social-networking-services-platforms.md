This guide teaches **section 12.3 Social networking services/platforms** of Topic 12 IT in society in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. It is A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. The [IT in society revision notes](/resources/a-level-cambridge-ict-it-in-society-revision-notes/) cover the whole topic; this page goes deeper on social networking. Use it with the [social networking revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-social-networking-services-platforms-revision-notes/) and the [social networking practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-social-networking-services-platforms-practice/).

## What section 12.3 covers

| Syllabus strand | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|
| Types | Describe chat rooms, instant messaging, email, and social media such as forums, blogs and microblogs |
| Uses | Explain use by individuals, businesses, organisations and governments, and for education, finance, healthcare and news |
| Impact | Discuss the impact on individuals, businesses, organisations and governments |
| Evaluation | Give advantages and disadvantages of the different types |

A **social networking service or platform** is an online service that lets users create content, communicate and connect with others. The key features are user-generated content, profiles or accounts, and links between users.

## Types of platform

| Type | How it works | Typical features |
|---|---|---|
| Chat room | Many users talk together in one shared online space, usually on one topic | Real-time text, often anonymous or with user names, moderators |
| Instant messaging | Private real-time messages between individuals or groups | Delivery and read notices, attachments, voice and video calls |
| Email | Messages stored on a server and read later by the recipient | Attachments, formal records, works across platforms |
| Forum | Posts organised into topics and replies (threads) that remain visible for others to read | Searchable archive, moderators, ranking |
| Blog | A website with dated posts, written by one person or a team, with comments | Long-form writing, archive, subscription |
| Microblog | Very short posts that can be shared widely | Quick news, hashtags, sharing and reposting |

Email is on the list because it is a way to communicate and form groups, though it is less open and less immediate than other services. Other social media, such as photo and video sharing sites, work in similar ways.

## Uses

**By individuals:** keeping in touch with family and friends, making new contacts, joining groups with a shared interest, sharing photographs and news, exchanging ideas, and finding information.

**By businesses:** advertising and promotion, giving product information, sharing safety information about products, dealing with customer enquiries and complaints, and gathering customer feedback.

**By organisations** (such as charities, schools, health services and emergency services): spreading information quickly, such as weather warnings and road closures, recruiting volunteers, raising funds and running campaigns.

**By governments:** publishing news and useful information, updating regulations, giving advice, holding discussions on current issues, and collecting feedback from citizens.

**In particular fields:**
- **Education:** discussion forums, group projects, sharing resources.
- **Finance:** companies announce results; investors discuss shares; customers contact banks.
- **Healthcare:** health services share advice; patients join support groups.
- **News:** news organisations post stories; individuals report events as they happen.

## Impact on individuals

Positive impacts include meeting new people, keeping in contact with family and friends who live far away, joining common interest groups and exchanging ideas.

Negative impacts the syllabus names:

- **Intellectual isolation** (an "echo chamber"): algorithms and friend choices show people views like their own, so they rarely meet other opinions.
- **Ideological polarisation**: groups of like-minded people reinforce each other's opinions and move towards extremes.
- **Stereotyping**: people and groups are judged by short posts and images, and repeated images can make stereotypes seem true.
- **Physical and mental health** differ for different age groups. Young people can suffer from comparing themselves with others, bullying and lack of sleep. Older people can benefit from reduced loneliness, but may be more exposed to scams. Long periods of use also reduce exercise.
- **Cognitive issues:** constant notifications and quick scrolling can shorten attention spans and reduce concentration.

## Impact on businesses

- **Advertising:** adverts can be aimed at groups by age, place and interests, which can cost less than television or print.
- **Product information:** companies show new products and answer questions.
- **Safety information:** recalls and safety notices reach customers quickly.
- **Customer feedback:** reviews and comments show what customers think. A single bad review or viral complaint can quickly damage a reputation.

**Worked example 1: measuring a campaign.** A cafe has 12,500 followers. One post receives 500 likes and comments, and the cafe pays £150 to promote the post, which brings 300 visits to its booking page, and 15 bookings.

- Engagement rate = 500 ÷ 12,500 = 0.04, which is 4%.
- Cost per visit = £150 ÷ 300 = £0.50.
- Conversion rate = 15 ÷ 300 = 0.05, which is 5%.
- Cost per booking = £150 ÷ 15 = £10.

If each booking is worth £20 in sales, the 15 bookings bring £300, which is more than the £150 spent. The campaign is worthwhile, but only if the profit per booking is also considered.

## Impact on organisations and governments

**Organisations** can reach thousands of people in minutes. A local authority can post a flood warning that people can share. A charity can show its work to donors. The risks are false rumours, hoax messages, staff posting without approval, and the need to monitor accounts constantly.

**Governments** can inform citizens about new laws, ask for opinions on policies and support emergency alerts. Social media also lets governments see public opinion. The concerns are:

- **Censorship:** some governments block platforms or remove content, restricting free speech.
- **False or distorted information:** messages designed to mislead can spread faster than corrections, and affect elections.
- Gaps in access: people without internet access, often older or poorer, may miss information.

## Advantages and disadvantages of the types

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Chat room | Real-time discussion with many people; shared interests | Users can be anonymous; unsafe contacts; off-topic or abusive posts; messages not kept |
| Instant messaging | Fast; private; supports groups, files and calls | Interrupts work; messages can be misread; privacy risk if the provider stores messages |
| Email | Formal record; attachments; works between all providers; suits long messages | Slower than messaging; spam and phishing; inbox overload |
| Forum | Long-lasting threads; searchable; topic focused; moderated | Slow replies; incorrect advice remains; trolls |
| Blog | Space for detailed writing; builds an audience; owner controls content | Takes time to maintain; needs promotion; comments may be abusive |
| Microblog | Rapid news sharing; reaches a wide audience | Short length leaves no space for detail; rumours spread quickly |

## Choosing a type for a purpose

Exam scenarios often ask which type of platform to use. Match the **purpose** to the **feature**.

| Purpose | Best type | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| A council warns of a flood | Microblog | Short, immediate and easily shared |
| A company explains how to use a product in detail | Blog | Room for long, illustrated posts, with comments |
| Users of a program help each other with problems | Forum | Threads stay available and can be searched later |
| Two colleagues need a quick private answer | Instant messaging | Real time and private |
| A bank sends a formal notice with a document | Email | Record and attachment |
| Fans discuss a match as it happens | Chat room | Live, with many people |

**Worked example 2: a scenario answer.** A local charity wants to recruit volunteers and thank donors. A good answer names a platform, a feature and a benefit: "A microblog account allows short appeals to be shared by supporters, so the message reaches people beyond the charity's own followers. A blog can describe the work in detail, with photographs, to show donors how money was used. The charity must watch for false rumours and respond to negative comments quickly." This links the type, the feature, the benefit and a risk, and it is the pattern that earns marks.

## Common mistakes

- Treating all social networking as one thing. Each type has different features and suits different uses.
- Giving only benefits, or only harms. Discuss questions need both.
- Writing "it is bad for mental health" without saying which group, which feature, and what the effect is.
- Confusing a forum (threads that remain) with a chat room (live talk).
- Saying a business "gets more customers" with no mention of how, such as targeted adverts or reviews.
- Forgetting that governments are both users of platforms (to inform) and regulators or censors of them.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** a type: how users communicate with it, and a feature that sets it apart.
- **Explain a use**: link the type to the user (individual, business, organisation, government) and the purpose.
- **Discuss impact**: give a balanced answer, name the group affected, and explain the effect in a chain: feature, then effect, then consequence.
- **Compare types**: choose the type that suits the scenario, such as a forum for technical help and instant messaging for quick team questions.
