**This guide covers content area 3.1.1.6 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Belief in life after death: "Muslim beliefs about death, Barzakh, resurrection, the Day of Judgement, Heaven and Hell" and "different Muslim death rituals and their importance".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 80:34-42. This guide is organised as a journey, and it ends with the choices, the rituals and the exam skills that turn knowledge into marks. A shorter treatment sits in the [Life after death and death rituals study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-life-after-death-death-rituals/); here the emphasis is on reasoning, evidence and the varied practices Muslims follow.

## Why Muslims believe in life after death

Yaum e Akhirah, the Last Day, is one of the key beliefs. The Qur'an repeats it often and links it to justice: the world contains cruelty that is never punished and goodness that is never rewarded, so there must be a day when everything is put right. It also says that this life is a test: "He who created death and life to test you [as to] which of you is best in deed" (67:2). A person is therefore not an accident, and life has a purpose.

The Qur'an answers a common objection from the people of Makkah, who asked how decayed bones could live again: "Does man think that We will not assemble his bones? Yes. We are able [even] to proportion his fingertips" (75:3-4). The same power that created people the first time can create them again.

## The journey in six stages

### 1. Death

Every soul will taste death (3:185), at a time Allah has fixed. The angel of death, Malak-al-Mowth, takes the soul (32:11). Muslims believe the soul (*ruh*) leaves the body and that the experience differs for believers and disbelievers. The family say, "Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return" (2:156). Death is not seen as the end but as a transfer from one stage of existence to the next.

### 2. Barzakh: the barrier

*Barzakh* means barrier. It is the time between death and resurrection, in which the person waits in the grave or in the state Allah has appointed. "And behind them is a barrier until the Day they are resurrected" (23:100). According to Hadith, two angels (known as Munkar and Nakir) ask the dead person: Who is your Lord? What is your religion? What do you say about the man sent to you? The believer answers correctly, and the Prophet (pbuh) is reported to have said that the grave is then "a garden from the gardens of Paradise"; for a person who rejected the truth, it is "a pit from the pits of Hell" (Tirmidhi). Barzakh therefore gives a first taste of what follows, but it is not the final judgement.

### 3. The trumpet and resurrection

At a time known only to Allah, Isra'fil (AS) will blow the trumpet and all creation will die; at the second blast every person will rise (39:68). The Qur'an calls the moment a "deafening blast" (80:33). Resurrection is bodily: people are raised in a form that can be recognised and judged. The Day is also called *Yaum ad-Din* (Day of Judgement), *Yaum al-Qiyamah* (Day of Resurrection) and *al-Hashr* (the Gathering).

### 4. The Day of Judgement

- **The record:** each person receives the book of deeds written by the Kiraman Katibeen, in the right hand (a sign of success) or in the left (a sign of failure) (84:7-10; 69:19-25).
- **The scale:** "We place the scales of justice for the Day of Resurrection, so no soul will be treated unjustly at all" (21:47). Nothing is missed or forgotten (99:7-8).
- **Witnesses:** the human body and the earth testify (36:65; 99:4).
- **Mercy and intercession:** Muslims believe Allah's mercy is greater than His anger and, according to Hadith, the Prophet (pbuh) will plead for his community with Allah's permission. Intercession is only by permission (2:255).
- **The bridge:** Hadith describe a bridge (*Sirat*) over Hell which all must cross; the details are known only to Allah, and the specification does not require more than awareness.

### 5. Heaven: Jannah

Jannah is described as gardens "beneath which rivers flow", with rivers of water, milk, wine that does not intoxicate and honey (47:15). It contains peace, freedom from sorrow and reunion with righteous family members. The greatest reward is the pleasure of Allah and, according to Hadith, seeing Him (75:22-23). The Prophet (pbuh) taught that there are levels in Jannah, with *Firdaus* the highest (Sahih al-Bukhari). The descriptions use earthly language so humans can grasp it; Muslims believe the reality is beyond imagination.

### 6. Hell: Jahannam

Jahannam is a place of punishment by fire for those who reject Allah and commit grave wrongs without repentance. "Indeed, those who disbelieved in Our verses – We will drive them into a Fire" (4:56). It is described as just and as a consequence of choices, and it is also a warning that encourages people to repent. Many Muslim scholars teach that believers who sin may be punished for a time and then brought out through Allah's mercy, while those who die in disbelief remain.

| Stage | Key idea | Key evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Death | Soul taken at the appointed time | 3:185; 32:11 |
| Barzakh | Barrier; questioning in the grave | 23:100; Hadith |
| Resurrection | Trumpet; bodily rising | 39:68; 75:3-4 |
| Judgement | Book, scales, witnesses | 21:47; 69:19-25 |
| Jannah | Gardens, peace, nearness to Allah | 47:15; 75:22-23 |
| Jahannam | Fire; just consequence | 4:56 |

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 80:34-42

> "On the Day a man will flee from his own siblings. And his mother and his father. And his wife and his children, for every man, that Day, will be a matter adequate for him. [Some] faces, that Day, will be bright. Laughing, rejoicing at good news. And [other] faces, that Day, will have upon them dust. Blackness will cover them. Those are the disbelievers, the wicked ones."

Read it in three layers.

1. **Fear and responsibility (verses 34-37):** the closest relationships will not help. "Every man ... will be a matter adequate for him" means each person will be busy with their own account. This supports the teaching that everyone is responsible for their own deeds.
2. **Reward (verses 38-39):** "bright, laughing, rejoicing" shows the joy of the successful.
3. **Punishment (verses 40-42):** "dust ... blackness" shows the shame of those who rejected the truth.

Use the passage to argue that belief in the Last Day is a motive for good conduct, that justice will be complete, and that judgement is individual.

## Different Muslim death rituals

Death rituals show belief in action. Learn the sequence and then the variations.

| Step | What happens | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Near death | The Shahadah is encouraged; Qur'an is recited | A last declaration of Tawhid |
| After death | The eyes are closed; the body is covered; the community is informed | Respect and dignity |
| **Ghusl** | The body is washed, usually by relatives of the same gender | Purity before meeting Allah |
| **Kafan** | The body is wrapped in plain white cloth | Equality of all before Allah |
| **Salat al-Janazah** | A short prayer, said standing, asking forgiveness | Community duty and dua for the dead |
| **Burial** | The body is placed on its right side facing the Qiblah in a simple grave, as soon as possible | Humility; return to earth |
| Mourning | Condolences, support, and food for the bereaved family | Compassion |

The Janazah prayer is a **fard kifayah**, a community duty: if enough people perform it, the duty is met for everyone. The Prophet (pbuh) also taught that the body should be treated with respect, because "breaking the bone of a dead person is like breaking it while he is alive" (Abu Dawud).

**Variation between Muslims.**

- **Takbirs:** Sunni Muslims say four takbirs in the Janazah prayer, and Shi'a Muslims say five.
- **Mourning customs:** some communities hold gatherings on set days after the death; other Muslims regard these as cultural rather than required.
- **Style of grief:** weeping is natural, while loud wailing and self-harm are discouraged.
- **Visiting graves:** the Prophet (pbuh) encouraged it as a reminder of death, though groups disagree about acts such as building over graves or asking the dead for help.
- **Cremation:** not permitted.

A widow observes a waiting period of four months and ten days (2:234), and other mourners generally mourn for three days.

## Why the belief is important

- **Accountability:** a person is responsible for each action.
- **Justice and hope:** the oppressed will be vindicated.
- **Perspective:** wealth and fame end at death; good deeds remain.
- **Comfort in bereavement:** the dead are not lost forever, and reunion in Jannah is hoped for.
- **Motivation for charity:** a Hadith in Sahih Muslim says a person's deeds end at death except ongoing charity, useful knowledge and a righteous child who prays for them.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why belief in life after death is important to Muslims. **[4]**

> It makes Muslims accountable for their actions [1], because they believe that every deed is recorded and weighed on the Day of Judgement, so they try to be honest and kind even in private [1]. It also gives hope [1], because people who have suffered injustice or lost loved ones believe that Allah will put things right and that the righteous will be reunited in Paradise [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** ways in which death rituals show Muslim beliefs. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> The white kafan is the same for everyone, which shows the belief that all people are equal before Allah and that wealth and status end at death [1 and 1]. Salat al-Janazah is performed by the community, which shows the belief that the living can ask Allah to forgive the dead and that Muslims share responsibility for one another [1 and 1]. The Prophet (pbuh) taught that deeds end at death except ongoing charity, useful knowledge and a righteous child who prays for the dead person [1], so the prayer and dua of the living are valued because they are a lasting benefit [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing Barzakh with Judgement.** Barzakh is the waiting stage; judgement comes after resurrection.
- **Saying Muslims believe only the soul survives.** Resurrection is of the whole person.
- **Listing rituals without reasons.** Give a purpose for each step.
- **Treating customs as obligations.** Mention that gatherings on set days differ between communities.
- **Ignoring the passage.** Use "flee from his own siblings" for individual responsibility and "bright faces" and "dust" for the two outcomes.
