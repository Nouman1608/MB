These revision notes cover sub-topic 4(b), **Feeding relationships** (specification points 4.6 to 4.9), of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Biology (4BI1) specification, Issue 3. The qualification is untiered. All four points can be examined on **either paper**. They apply to the June and November series examined under Issue 3.

For full explanations and worked examples, read the [feeding relationships study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-biology-4bi1-feeding-relationships/) first. ## Key definitions

- **Trophic level:** a feeding position in a food chain.
- **Producer:** an organism that makes its own food by photosynthesis.
- **Primary consumer:** a herbivore that eats producers.
- **Secondary consumer:** an animal that eats primary consumers.
- **Tertiary consumer:** an animal that eats secondary consumers.
- **Decomposer:** a bacterium or fungus that feeds on dead organisms and waste and returns nutrients.
- **Food chain:** a straight line of feeding, with arrows from food to feeder.
- **Food web:** several food chains linked together.
- **Biomass:** the mass of living material, usually measured as dry mass.

## 4.6 Trophic levels at a glance

| Level | Gets energy from | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Producer | Sunlight (photosynthesis) | Grass |
| Primary consumer | Eating producers | Rabbit |
| Secondary consumer | Eating primary consumers | Fox |
| Tertiary consumer | Eating secondary consumers | Eagle |
| Decomposer | Dead organisms and waste | Fungi, bacteria |

- Arrows show the direction of energy transfer: **from food to feeder**.
- An animal in a food web can occupy **two levels** if it eats from two chains.
- Decomposers act on every level.

## 4.7 Chains, webs and pyramids

### Reading a food web

1. Find the organisms with arrows going out only (producers).
2. Count the steps along each chain.
3. Predict effects: removing a predator raises its prey, which then eats more of its own food.

### Pyramids compared

| Pyramid | Measures | Always a true pyramid? |
|---|---|---|
| Number | How many individuals | No (one tree, many insects) |
| Biomass | Dry mass at one time | Usually, but can be inverted in water |
| Energy transfer | Energy per area per year | Yes |

- Use **dry mass** for biomass because water content varies.
- The bottom bar is always the producers.

## 4.8 Substances and energy

| | Substances (carbon, nitrogen) | Energy |
|---|---|---|
| How it enters | Producers take in carbon dioxide, water and ions | Light is absorbed by chlorophyll |
| Moves along the chain by | Feeding | Feeding |
| Leaves by | Decomposers return it to soil and air | Heat to the surroundings |
| Recycled? | Yes | No, so a constant supply from the Sun is needed |

## 4.9 The 10% rule

Only about **10%** of the energy in a trophic level reaches the next. About 90% is lost.

Why energy is lost:

- Respiration releases heat.
- Some parts are not eaten (bones, roots).
- Faeces carry away undigested material.
- Excretion (for example urea).
- Birds and mammals lose heat to keep warm.

Result: food chains have **four or five** trophic levels at most.

## Two calculations to know

**Efficiency** = energy passed on ÷ energy available × 100.
Example: 2160 kJ passed from 24 000 kJ gives 2160 ÷ 24 000 × 100 = 9.0%.

**Applying the 10% rule.** From 5000 kJ in producers:

```
primary consumers:   5000 × 0.1 = 500 kJ
secondary consumers:  500 × 0.1 =  50 kJ
tertiary consumers:    50 × 0.1 =   5 kJ
```

Always divide by the energy of the **lower** level, not the higher one.

## Method in steps: answering "suggest why" about human diet

1. State where the energy comes from (the producer).
2. State that a large percentage is lost at each transfer.
3. Conclude that eating plants directly gives people more of the energy captured by the crop.

## Answer frame: "Explain why energy transfers are inefficient"

Use three linked points: respiration releases heat; some material is not eaten or is lost as faeces and urine; so only about 10% becomes new biomass in the next level.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Food chain vs food web:** one line vs many linked lines.
- **Primary vs secondary consumer:** herbivore vs eater of herbivores.
- **Energy vs substances:** energy flows one way, substances are recycled.
- **Pyramid of numbers vs pyramid of biomass:** counts of individuals vs mass of living tissue.
- **Biomass vs energy:** both fall up the chain, but energy always forms a true pyramid.

## Quick self-test

1. Name the trophic level of an organism that eats herbivores.
2. In the chain algae → water flea → minnow → heron, which organism is the secondary consumer?
3. Why do the arrows in a food chain point to the feeder?
4. Why is dry mass used for biomass?
5. State the approximate percentage of energy passed to the next trophic level.
6. Calculate the energy reaching the primary consumers if producers contain 36 000 kJ and 10% passes on.
7. Name two ways energy is lost between trophic levels.
8. Why do food chains rarely have more than five levels?
9. Explain why a pyramid of energy cannot be inverted.
10. Producers capture 12 000 kJ per m² per year and primary consumers receive 1080 kJ. Calculate the percentage transferred.

### Answers

1. Secondary consumer (a carnivore).
2. Minnow.
3. They show the direction energy and substances move.
4. Water content varies between organisms, so dry mass is a fairer comparison.
5. About 10%.
6. 36 000 × 0.1 = **3600 kJ**.
7. Any two of: heat from respiration, uneaten parts, faeces, excretion.
8. Too little energy is left to support another level.
9. Energy is lost at every transfer, so less is available at each higher level.
10. 1080 ÷ 12 000 × 100 = **9.0%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing arrows the wrong way.
- Writing "10% is lost" instead of "about 10% is passed on".
- Saying energy is "used up" or "recycled".
- Dividing by the upper level in an efficiency calculation.
- Giving "respiration" as a reason for loss without mentioning heat.
- Using "carnivore" for every consumer, so that tertiary and secondary levels are mixed up.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where does the energy in a food chain come from?**
From the Sun. Producers capture it by photosynthesis.

**Can a decomposer be in a food chain?**
Decomposers feed on dead organisms from every level. They are shown linked to all of them.

**Does the 10% rule apply exactly?**
No. It is an average. Real values vary, for example 8% or 15%.
