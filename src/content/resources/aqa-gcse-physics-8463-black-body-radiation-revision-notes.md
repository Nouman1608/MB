## Core facts (both tiers)

- **All** bodies emit and absorb infrared radiation, **whatever their temperature**.
- Hotter body → **more radiation emitted in a given time**.
- Emission is a **spread of wavelengths** (the wavelength distribution), not a single wavelength.
- The **intensity** and the **wavelength distribution** both depend on the body's temperature.
- Perfect black body: **absorbs all** incident radiation; **reflects none**; **transmits none**.
- Good absorber = good emitter, so a perfect black body is the **best possible emitter**.
- "Black" describes absorbing, not the colour it glows: a very hot black body emits visible light.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Emit | Give out radiation from the body itself |
| Absorb | Take in incident radiation, which transfers energy to the body |
| Reflect | Send incident radiation back from the surface |
| Transmit | Let incident radiation pass through |
| Incident radiation | Radiation arriving at (falling on) a surface |
| Perfect black body | An idealised object that absorbs all incident radiation, reflecting and transmitting none |
| Intensity | How much radiation is emitted (power), shown by the height of the curve |
| Wavelength distribution | How the emitted radiation is spread across different wavelengths |
| Peak wavelength | The wavelength at which the curve is highest |
| Rate | Amount per second; for radiation, joules per second (watts) |

## Intensity–wavelength curves

Hotter body:

```
higher curve at EVERY wavelength  → more radiation per second in total
peak further LEFT (shorter λ)     → bigger share at short wavelengths
curves for different temperatures never cross
```

Colour sequence as a metal is heated: infrared only (looks normal) → dull red → orange → yellow-white. Red
appears first because it is the longest visible wavelength, the first part of the visible range the emission
reaches.

**Method: comparing two curves**

```
Step 1  which curve is higher overall?        → hotter body
Step 2  which peak is at shorter wavelength?  → same hotter body (check they agree)
Step 3  write both points about BOTH bodies in one sentence each
```

## Surfaces compared

| Surface | Absorbing | Emitting at a given temperature |
|---|---|---|
| Perfect black body | All incident radiation | Best possible |
| Matt black | Very good | Very good |
| White or light | Poor for visible light | Poorer |
| Shiny silver | Poor (reflects most) | Poor |

## Higher tier only: the radiation balance

| Rates | Temperature |
|---|---|
| Absorbing > emitting | Rises |
| Emitting > absorbing | Falls |
| Absorbing = emitting | Constant |

- Constant temperature ≠ no radiation. It means **equal rates**.
- Self-correcting: a warming body emits faster as it warms, until emission equals absorption again at a new,
  higher temperature.
- Everyday examples: a car in sunshine warms then levels off; a drink outside at night cools; a thermometer in
  a steady room gives a constant reading.

**The Earth's temperature depends on:**

1. the rate of **absorbing** radiation (mostly from the Sun)
2. the rate of **emitting** radiation into space
3. how much incoming radiation is **reflected** into space (clouds, ice) and so never absorbed.

**Diagram chain to learn:**

```
Sun (very hot) → mostly short-wavelength radiation → passes through atmosphere
→ surface absorbs, warms → surface (cooler) emits LONGER-wavelength infrared
→ some absorbed by atmospheric gases → re-emitted in all directions, some back to surface
→ less escapes to space each second
```

Less escaping, same absorbing → temperature rises until the rates balance again. More reflected → less
absorbed → lower balance temperature.

## Exam traps

- "Only hot objects emit radiation": false. Everything emits.
- Black body defined as "something black" or "something that emits everything": use **absorbs all incident
  radiation, reflects and transmits none**.
- Missing the absorber–emitter link when explaining why a black body is the best emitter.
- "A hotter body emits shorter wavelengths instead": it emits **more at all wavelengths**; the peak shifts.
- Drawing hotter and cooler curves that cross.
- (Higher tier only) "Constant temperature means it has stopped emitting."
- (Higher tier only) Explaining Earth's warming with "heat is trapped" and no mention of **rates**.
- (Higher tier only) Forgetting reflected radiation is not absorbed at all.

## Quick self-test

1. Which bodies emit infrared radiation?
2. What happens to the radiation emitted per second when a body gets hotter?
3. Define a perfect black body.
4. Why is a perfect black body the best possible emitter?
5. A body's emission curve rises. State two changes to the curve.
6. Why does a heated metal bar glow red before it glows white?
7. Two curves are drawn for the same object at two temperatures. Can they cross?
8. Give one surface that is a poor absorber and a poor emitter.
9. (Higher tier only) A body emits faster than it absorbs. What happens to its temperature?
10. (Higher tier only) A body's temperature is constant. What does this tell you about the rates?
11. (Higher tier only) Name three factors that determine the Earth's temperature.
12. (Higher tier only) Why is the radiation emitted by the Earth's surface at longer wavelengths than the
    radiation arriving from the Sun?

### Answers

1. All bodies, at any temperature.
2. It increases.
3. An object that absorbs all the radiation incident on it, and reflects and transmits none.
4. A good absorber is also a good emitter, and it is the best possible absorber.
5. The intensity is higher at every wavelength; the peak moves to a shorter wavelength.
6. As the temperature rises, emission spreads to shorter wavelengths; red is the longest visible wavelength,
   so it is reached first. Hotter still, more of the visible range is emitted and it looks white.
7. No. The hotter curve is higher at every wavelength.
8. Shiny silver (a light, shiny surface).
9. It decreases.
10. It is absorbing and emitting radiation at the same rate.
11. The rate of absorbing radiation, the rate of emitting radiation, and the amount of radiation reflected
    into space.
12. The Earth's surface is much cooler than the Sun, and a cooler body's emission peaks at a longer wavelength.
