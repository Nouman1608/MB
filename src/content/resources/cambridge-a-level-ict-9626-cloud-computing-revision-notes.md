These notes condense **section 14.4 Cloud computing** (Topic 14 Communications technology) of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Information Technology 9626 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. A Level content, examined in Paper 3 Advanced Theory. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [cloud computing study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-cloud-computing/); test yourself with the [cloud computing practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-ict-9626-cloud-computing-practice/).

## The three strands

1. **Characteristics**, including the sharing of computing resources.
2. **Uses** by individuals and organisations.
3. **Advantages and disadvantages** for individuals and for organisations.

## Definition and characteristics

**Cloud computing:** using storage, processing power, databases or software held on remote servers, reached over the internet, instead of on local equipment.

| Characteristic | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shared (pooled) resources | Many customers share the provider's servers, so each pays only for a share |
| On-demand self-service | Users add or remove resources themselves through a web page |
| Broad network access | Reachable from any device with an internet connection |
| Elasticity (scalability) | Resources grow or shrink as demand changes |
| Measured service | Charged by use or subscription |
| Remote management | The provider maintains hardware, power, cooling and updates |

## Service and deployment types

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| SaaS | Finished application through a browser | Web email, online office tools |
| PaaS | Platform to build and run applications | Developers deploy a web app |
| IaaS | Virtual servers, storage and networks | Company rents servers |
| Public cloud | Shared by many customers | Photo storage |
| Private cloud | Used by one organisation | Bank's own system |
| Hybrid cloud | Public and private combined | Confidential data private, extra capacity public |

## Uses: recall

| User | Uses |
|---|---|
| Individuals | Online storage; web email; streaming; automatic backup; shared documents |
| Organisations | Websites and shops; data storage and backup; business software; data analysis; software testing; home working; disaster recovery |

## Advantages and disadvantages: recall

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Individuals | Any device; cheap; automatic backup; easy sharing | Needs internet; privacy; limited free space; little control |
| Organisations | No hardware to buy; scalable; less maintenance; remote working; disaster recovery | Running costs continue; reliance on provider and connection; less control of data; security and legal worries; lock-in |

**Reducing the risks:** encrypt data, use strong access controls, choose a reputable provider, keep a second copy, and check where data is held.

## Quick examples

**Pay per use.** A site needs 4 servers for 20 days (24 hours) and 12 servers for 4 days (24 hours) in a 24-day period. At £0.50 per server-hour, cost = (4 × 20 × 24 + 12 × 4 × 24) × 0.50 = (1,920 + 1,152) × 0.50 = **£1,536**.

**Upload time.** 10 GB = 80,000 megabits. At 40 megabits per second: 80,000 ÷ 40 = **2,000 seconds**, about 33 minutes.

**Storage cost.** 800 GB at £0.02 per GB per month = **£16** per month.

## When the cloud is a good choice

| Situation | Reason |
|---|---|
| Demand rises and falls sharply | Pay only for the extra capacity used |
| Small business with no IT staff | The provider maintains systems |
| Staff in several places | Same files and software for everyone |
| Need for an off-site copy | Data held away from a fire or flood at the office |

## When the cloud is a poor choice

| Situation | Reason |
|---|---|
| Unreliable or slow internet | Access and uploads suffer |
| Very sensitive data with legal limits on where it is held | Provider's location and security may not satisfy the law |
| Constant, predictable heavy use | Owning equipment may be cheaper over time |

## Exam traps

- Defining the cloud as "the internet".
- Advantages without a reason or a scenario link.
- Forgetting that everything depends on the internet connection.
- Writing "data is totally safe in the cloud".
- Mixing up SaaS, PaaS and IaaS.
- Treating the needs of individuals and organisations as identical.

## Self-test

1. Define cloud computing.
2. Explain what is meant by shared computing resources.
3. What is elasticity?
4. Give two uses of cloud computing by an individual.
5. Give two uses of cloud computing by an organisation.
6. What does SaaS stand for, and what is an example?
7. What is a hybrid cloud?
8. Give two advantages of cloud computing for an organisation.
9. Give two disadvantages of cloud computing for an organisation.
10. Give one disadvantage for an individual.
11. State two ways to reduce the security risks of storing data in the cloud.
12. Cloud servers cost £0.30 per hour. How much do 5 servers cost for 48 hours?

### Answers

1. Using storage, processing or software on remote servers, reached over the internet, instead of local equipment.
2. Many customers use the same pool of servers, each taking a share, so costs are shared and resources are used efficiently.
3. The ability to increase or decrease resources quickly as demand changes.
4. Any two: online storage; web email; streaming; automatic backup; shared documents.
5. Any two: hosting a website; data backup; business software; data analysis; software testing; supporting home working; disaster recovery.
6. Software as a Service; for example web email or online office tools.
7. A mix of public and private clouds, such as private for confidential data and public for extra capacity.
8. Any two: no large hardware cost; scalable; less maintenance; staff can work anywhere; easier disaster recovery.
9. Any two: continuing costs; dependence on the internet and provider; less control of data; security and legal concerns; difficult to change provider.
10. Any one: needs internet; privacy concerns; limited free storage.
11. Any two: encrypt data; strong access controls; choose a reputable provider; keep another copy.
12. 5 × 48 × 0.30 = £72.
