## Section A

**1.** State the meaning of **informative advertising**. **[1]**

**2.** State **two** purposes of advertising. **[2]**

**3.** Name **two** types of digital advertising media. **[2]**

**4.** Name **two** methods of sales promotion. **[2]**

**5.** Identify the type of advertising used in a campaign paid for by many dairy farmers to encourage people to drink more milk. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A business compares two ways of advertising. Local radio costs $1,800 and reaches 90,000 listeners. A social media campaign costs $2,400 and reaches 160,000 people.

**(a)** Calculate the cost per person reached for each. **[2]**

**(b)** State which is cheaper per person reached. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain one reason why the business may still choose the more expensive method per person. **[2]**

**7.** A new bakery wants to attract customers living nearby. Explain **two** suitable methods of advertising. **[4]**

**8.** Describe **two** advantages and **one** disadvantage of using television advertising. **[3]**

**9.** A shop sells a coat for $80. It offers 15% off in a sale.

**(a)** Calculate the sale price. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the shop might use a sales period. **[2]**

**10.** A supermarket sells milk at a loss leader price. Explain how this could increase the supermarket's profit. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why a fear appeal is suitable for a company that sells home alarms but not suitable for a company that sells chocolate. **[4]**

**12.** A company uses a celebrity to advertise running shoes. Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of this. **[4]**

**13.** Evaluate whether a small clothes shop should use printed leaflets or social media to advertise a sale. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Advertising that gives facts about a product, such as its features, price and where to buy [1].

**2.** Any two from: to inform customers; to persuade customers; to increase sales; to build brand image; to remind customers; to launch a new product [1 each].

**3.** Any two from: social media advertising; websites / search engines; display advertisements; video advertisements; email marketing; digital billboards; social media influencers [1 each].

**4.** Any two from: point of sale; special offers; sponsorship; displays; competitions; coupons; sales periods; loss leaders; free gifts; free samples [1 each].

**5.** Collective (generic) advertising [1].

**6.** **(a)** Radio: 1,800 ÷ 90,000 = $0.02 [1]. Social media: 2,400 ÷ 160,000 = $0.015 [1].

**(b)** Social media [1].

**(c)** The radio audience may be local and match the business's customers [1], so the money is not spent on people far away who cannot visit the shop [1]. Accept any valid reason such as audience or the type of product.

**7.** Any two developed: leaflets / flyers [1] delivered to homes nearby, which is cheap and targets the area [1]; a local newspaper advert [1], read by local people and cheaper than a national paper [1]; a signboard or window display [1], seen by passers-by [1]; social media with a local target [1], reaching nearby people at low cost [1]. Maximum 4.

**8.** Any two advantages: reaches a very large audience [1]; uses sound, pictures and movement [1]. One disadvantage: very expensive to make and broadcast [1], or the advert has a short life.

**9.** **(a)** 15% × 80 = $12 [1]. Sale price = 80 − 12 = $68 [1].

**(b)** To clear old stock [1] and attract customers who wait for lower prices, increasing sales [1].

**10.** The cheap milk attracts customers into the store [1]. While they are there they buy other goods at normal prices [1], so the profit on those goods can be greater than the loss on the milk [1].

**11.** A home alarm protects against burglary, so showing the danger of a break-in motivates people to buy [1], and customers see the product as a solution [1]. Chocolate is a pleasure product [1], so a fear message would put people off and not match the product [1].

**12.** Advantage: the celebrity attracts attention and people who admire them may copy them [1], so sales of the shoes may rise [1]. Disadvantage: celebrities are paid high fees [1], and if the celebrity behaves badly the brand's reputation is damaged [1].

**13.** Any six from: leaflets are cheap and can be delivered to homes near the shop [1]; they have a physical form that can be kept [1]; but many leaflets are thrown away [1]; they cannot be changed once printed [1]; social media can target by age and location [1]; it can be changed quickly if stock runs out [1]; it can be shared by customers [1]; it requires someone to manage accounts [1]; a justified conclusion, for example social media for a wide, young audience, and leaflets for older local customers [1]. Maximum 6.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 6: dividing the audience by the cost instead of the cost by the audience.
- Question 7: naming a medium with no reason linked to the bakery.
- Question 9: giving the discount ($12) rather than the sale price.
- Question 10: not explaining that customers buy other goods.
- Question 11: explaining only one of the two products.
- Question 13: no decision at the end.
