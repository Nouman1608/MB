## Scale

- **Universe** = billions of galaxies.
- **Galaxy** = billions of stars.
- Our **solar system** is in the **Milky Way** galaxy.
- Order, smallest first: planet → solar system → galaxy → universe.

## Gravitational field strength

- g = force per kg (N/kg).
- Depends on the **mass** of the planet or moon (more mass → larger g).
- Depends on **distance from its centre** (further → smaller g).
- Moon: about 1.6 N/kg. Earth: close to 10 N/kg.
- Mass is constant; weight W = m × g changes from place to place.

## Gravity and orbits

- Without a force, a moving body travels in a straight line.
- Orbit needs a force **towards the centre**: gravity.
- Moons orbit planets; planets orbit the Sun; artificial satellites orbit Earth; comets orbit the Sun: all because of gravity.
- Circular orbit: speed constant, **direction** changes, so velocity changes → the body is accelerating.
- Further from the Sun → weaker field → slower orbit and longer period.

## Orbits compared

| | Planet | Moon | Comet |
|---|---|---|---|
| Orbits | Sun | Planet | Sun |
| Shape | Almost circular | Almost circular | Highly elliptical |
| Speed | About constant | About constant | Fastest near Sun |
| Distance | About constant | About constant | Varies hugely |

**Comet speed change:** approaching the Sun, gravitational potential energy → kinetic energy, so it speeds up; moving away, kinetic → potential, so it slows down.

## Orbital speed

```
v = 2πr / T        T = 2πr / v        r = vT / 2π
```

- r in m (from the **centre** of the orbited body), T in s, v in m/s.
- 1 day = 86 400 s.

**Example (illustrative figures):** a satellite with r = 1.0 × 10⁷ m and T = 1.0 × 10⁴ s has v = 2π × 1.0 × 10⁷ / 1.0 × 10⁴ = **6.3 × 10³ m/s**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Universe | Everything that exists; billions of galaxies |
| Galaxy | Collection of billions of stars held together by gravity |
| Milky Way | The galaxy containing our solar system |
| Orbit | Curved path of one body around another, caused by gravity |
| Comet | Small icy body in a highly elliptical orbit around the Sun |
| Moon | Natural satellite orbiting a planet |
| Artificial satellite | Human-made object placed in orbit around Earth |
| Orbital radius | Distance from the centre of the orbited body |
| Time period | Time for one complete orbit |

## Exam traps

- "A galaxy is in the solar system": it is the other way round.
- "There is no gravity in space": g decreases with distance but is not zero.
- "Centrifugal force" in an orbit explanation: the only force is gravity, inwards.
- "Constant speed so no acceleration": direction changes, so it accelerates.
- Comet speed described without the energy transfer reason.
- Days or hours used for T in v = 2πr / T.

## Self-test

1. What is a galaxy?
2. Which galaxy contains our solar system?
3. State two factors that determine g at the surface of a planet.
4. Why is g on the Moon smaller than on Earth?
5. What force keeps the Moon in orbit around Earth, and in which direction does it act?
6. Explain why a satellite moving at constant speed in a circular orbit is accelerating.
7. Describe two differences between the orbit of a comet and the orbit of a planet.
8. Where in its orbit does a comet move fastest? Why?
9. Why does a planet far from the Sun take longer to orbit than one closer in?
10. A satellite has r = 7.2 × 10⁶ m and v = 7.4 × 10³ m/s. Calculate T.
11. A moon has v = 1.5 × 10³ m/s and T = 2.0 × 10⁶ s. Calculate r.
12. What would happen to an orbiting satellite if gravity suddenly stopped acting on it?

## Answers

1. A large collection of billions of stars.
2. The Milky Way.
3. The mass of the planet; the distance from its centre (its radius).
4. The Moon has much less mass than Earth.
5. Earth's gravitational force, acting towards the centre of Earth.
6. Its direction is constantly changing, so its velocity is changing; changing velocity means acceleration.
7. Any two: a comet's orbit is highly elliptical while a planet's is nearly circular; a comet's distance from the Sun varies enormously while a planet's stays nearly constant; a comet's speed changes greatly while a planet's is nearly constant.
8. Closest to the Sun, because gravitational potential energy has been transferred to kinetic energy as it fell towards the Sun.
9. The Sun's gravitational field is weaker there, so the planet moves more slowly, and it has a longer path to travel.
10. T = 2πr / v = 2π × 7.2 × 10⁶ / 7.4 × 10³ = 6.1 × 10³ s (about 6100 s).
11. r = vT / 2π = 1.5 × 10³ × 2.0 × 10⁶ / 2π = 4.8 × 10⁸ m.
12. It would move off in a straight line in the direction it was travelling at that moment.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is motion in the universe Physics-only in 4PH1?**
No. Statements 8.2 to 8.6 are core content, so they can be examined on Paper 1P and Paper 2P.

**Do I need to know the shape of an ellipse in detail?**
No. You need to describe comet orbits as much more elongated (elliptical) than the nearly circular orbits of planets and moons.
