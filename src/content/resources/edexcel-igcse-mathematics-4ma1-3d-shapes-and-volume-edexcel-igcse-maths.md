This study guide goes deeper into section 4.10, 3D shapes and volume, of topic 4 (Geometry and trigonometry) in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017). Statements A to F are Foundation Tier content, which both tiers need. Statement A of the Higher Tier content, the surface area and volume of a sphere and a right circular cone, is **Higher tier only**.

The [Geometry and trigonometry study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-geometry-and-trigonometry/) lists the formulae. This page shows how to use them, how to build a surface area from the faces, and how to convert units of volume. Follow up with the [3D shapes and volume revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-3d-shapes-and-volume-edexcel-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and the [3D shapes and volume practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-mathematics-4ma1-3d-shapes-and-volume-edexcel-igcse-maths-practice/).

## Which formulae are given in the examination?

The Foundation Tier formulae sheet gives three formulae: the volume of a prism (area of cross-section × length), the volume of a cylinder (πr²h) and the curved surface area of a cylinder (2πrh). The Higher Tier sheet adds the volume of a cone (⅓πr²h), the curved surface area of a cone (πrl), the volume of a sphere (4/3 πr³) and the surface area of a sphere (4πr²). It also lists the same cylinder and prism formulae. Anything not on your sheet must be learned. The area of a circle, πr², is on neither sheet, and you need it for the end faces of a cylinder.

## Naming solids, faces, edges and vertices (A and B)

A **face** is a flat or curved surface of a solid. An **edge** is where two faces meet. A **vertex** (plural vertices) is a corner, where edges meet.

A **prism** is a solid that has the same cross-section all the way along its length. Cuboids, triangular prisms and cylinders are prisms. A **pyramid** has a polygon for its base and triangular faces that meet at a point called the apex.

| Solid | Faces | Edges | Vertices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cube or cuboid | 6 | 12 | 8 |
| Triangular prism | 5 | 9 | 6 |
| Square-based pyramid | 5 | 8 | 5 |
| Cylinder | 3 (2 flat, 1 curved) | 2 | 0 |
| Cone | 2 (1 flat, 1 curved) | 1 | 1 |
| Sphere | 1 (curved) | 0 | 0 |

**Worked example 1.** A triangular prism has 2 triangular faces and 3 rectangular faces, so 5 faces. Each triangle has 3 edges, giving 6, and 3 more edges join the triangles. That makes 9 edges. Each triangle has 3 corners, so there are 6 vertices.

## Surface area from the faces (C)

The **surface area** is the total area of all the faces. Imagine the solid unfolded into a flat **net**, then add up the areas of the pieces.

**Worked example 2.** A cuboid measures 8 cm by 5 cm by 3 cm. It has three pairs of identical faces.

- top and bottom: 8 × 5 = 40, and there are two of them
- front and back: 8 × 3 = 24, and there are two of them
- left and right: 5 × 3 = 15, and there are two of them

Surface area = 2 × (40 + 24 + 15) = 2 × 79 = **158 cm²**.

**Worked example 3.** A triangular prism has a right-angled triangle for its cross-section, with sides 3 cm, 4 cm and 5 cm. The prism is 10 cm long.

The two triangular ends have an area of ½ × 3 × 4 = 6 cm² each, so 12 cm² together.

The three rectangles have a length of 10 cm and widths of 3, 4 and 5 cm. Their total area is 10 × (3 + 4 + 5) = 120 cm².

Surface area = 12 + 120 = **132 cm²**.

Notice that the three rectangles together make one big rectangle of length 10 cm and width equal to the perimeter of the cross-section. For any prism, the surface area is 2 × (cross-section area) + (perimeter of cross-section) × (length).

## Volume of a prism (E)

**Volume of a prism = area of cross-section × length**

Volume is measured in cubic units, such as cm³. The "length" is the distance along the prism between the two identical ends, whichever way the solid is drawn.

**Worked example 4.** For the prism in worked example 3: volume = 6 × 10 = **60 cm³**.

A cuboid is a prism with a rectangle as its cross-section, so its volume is length × width × height. For the cuboid in worked example 2: 8 × 5 × 3 = **120 cm³**.

**Worked example 5.** A trapezium-shaped cross-section has parallel sides of 6 cm and 10 cm, 4 cm apart. The prism is 15 cm long. Cross-section area = ½ × (6 + 10) × 4 = 32 cm². Volume = 32 × 15 = **480 cm³**.

## Cylinders: volume and surface area (D and E)

A cylinder is a prism with a circle as its cross-section.

- **volume = πr²h**
- **curved surface area = 2πrh** (the label wraps round the side)
- **total surface area = 2πrh + 2πr²** (add two circular ends)

The curved surface of a cylinder is a rectangle when you unroll it. One side of the rectangle is the height h, and the other is the circumference of the circle, 2πr. That is where 2πrh comes from.

**Worked example 6.** A cylinder has radius 4 cm and height 10 cm.

Volume = π × 4² × 10 = 160π = 502.65... = **503 cm³** (3 significant figures).

Curved surface area = 2 × π × 4 × 10 = 80π.

Area of the two ends = 2 × π × 4² = 32π.

Total surface area = 80π + 32π = 112π = 351.86... = **352 cm²**.

**Working backwards.** A cylinder of radius 5 cm has volume 500 cm³. Find its height. From πr²h = 500: 25πh = 500, so h = 500 ÷ (25π) = 6.366... = **6.37 cm**.

**A cylinder with an open top** (a can with no lid) loses one circular end: surface area = 2πrh + πr². Read the question carefully to see which faces are present.

## Units of volume (F)

For length units you multiply by 10, 100 or 1000. For volume, **cube the length factor**.

| Length | Volume |
|---|---|
| 1 cm = 10 mm | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³ |
| 1 m = 100 cm | 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| 1 km = 1000 m | 1 km³ = 1 000 000 000 m³ |

The specification also states 1 litre = 1000 cm³. A cube of side 10 cm holds exactly 1 litre. That also gives 1 m³ = 1000 litres.

**Worked example 7.** Convert (a) 2.5 m³ to cm³, (b) 4500 cm³ to litres, (c) 3 200 000 cm³ to m³.

(a) 2.5 × 1 000 000 = **2 500 000 cm³**.

(b) 4500 ÷ 1000 = **4.5 litres**.

(c) 3 200 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **3.2 m³**.

**Worked example 8.** A fish tank is a cuboid 50 cm long, 40 cm wide and 30 cm high. How many litres of water does it hold when full? Volume = 50 × 40 × 30 = 60 000 cm³. Divide by 1000: **60 litres**.

## Recasting a solid

When a solid is melted or poured into another shape, its volume stays the same. A cube of side 6 cm is melted and made into a cuboid 9 cm by 4 cm by h cm. The cube's volume is 6³ = 216 cm³. So 9 × 4 × h = 216, giving 36h = 216 and h = **6 cm**.

## Cones and spheres (Higher tier only)

A right circular cone has a circular base and its apex directly above the centre of the base. It has a vertical height h, a base radius r, and a **slant height** l. The three form a right-angled triangle, so l² = r² + h².

- **volume of a cone = ⅓πr²h**
- **curved surface area of a cone = πrl** (uses the slant height)
- **total surface area of a cone = πrl + πr²**

**Worked example 9.** A cone has radius 5 cm and vertical height 12 cm. The slant height is l = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 cm.

Volume = ⅓ × π × 25 × 12 = 100π = 314.16... = **314 cm³**.

Curved surface area = π × 5 × 13 = 65π = 204.20... = **204 cm²**.

Total surface area = 65π + 25π = 90π = 282.74... = **283 cm²**.

For a sphere of radius r:

- **volume = 4/3 πr³**
- **surface area = 4πr²**

**Worked example 10.** A sphere has radius 6 cm.

Volume = 4/3 × π × 6³ = 4/3 × π × 216 = 288π = 904.77... = **905 cm³**.

Surface area = 4 × π × 36 = 144π = 452.38... = **452 cm²**.

A **hemisphere** is half a sphere. Its volume is half the sphere's, so 2/3 πr³. Its curved surface area is 2πr². If the flat circular face is included, add πr², giving a total of 3πr². For r = 6 cm, the volume is 144π = 452.38... cm³ and the total surface area is 108π = 339.29... cm².

**Worked example 11.** A solid is a cylinder of radius 3 cm and height 10 cm with a hemisphere of radius 3 cm on top. Volume = cylinder + hemisphere = π × 9 × 10 + 2/3 × π × 27 = 90π + 18π = 108π = 339.29... = **339 cm³**.

## Exam technique

- **Write the formula** and then substitute. This earns the method mark even if the arithmetic goes wrong.
- **Check which faces** a surface-area question includes: open or closed, whole or half solid.
- **Choose the right height.** The vertical height h is for volume, and the slant height l is for the curved surface of a cone.
- **Match the unit to the question.** A volume in cm³, an area in cm², a length in cm. Convert at the end if the question asks for litres or m³.
- **Use full calculator values** and round only the last line.

## Common mistakes

- Using 100 for cm³ to m³. The factor is 1 000 000.
- Using the diameter in πr²h or 4/3 πr³.
- Using the vertical height instead of the slant height in πrl.
- Forgetting the two circular ends in the total surface area of a cylinder.
- Counting the hidden face between two joined solids in a surface area.
- Dividing by 100 instead of 1000 for cm³ to litres.
