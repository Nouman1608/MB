## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define nuclear fission. **[2]**

**2.** Which fissionable substance is used in most nuclear reactors?

A: uranium-238

B: uranium-235

C: plutonium-239

D: hydrogen-2

**[1]**

**3.** State what a uranium-235 nucleus must absorb before it can undergo fission. **[1]**

**4.** State the products of a single fission of a uranium-235 nucleus. **[3]**

**5.** State what control rods do in a nuclear reactor. **[1]**

**6.** Compare the energy released by the fission of 1 kg of uranium-235 with the energy released by burning 1 kg of coal. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** One possible fission of uranium-235 is:

²³⁵₉₂U + ¹₀n → ¹⁴⁰₅₄Xe + ⁹⁴₃₈Sr + 2 ¹₀n + energy

**(a)** State how many neutrons are released in this fission. **[1]**

**(b)** Show that the total mass number is the same on both sides. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the xenon and strontium nuclei are described as "smaller nuclei". **[1]**

**8.** Describe, with the help of a labelled diagram, how a chain reaction can occur in uranium-235. **[5]**

**9.** In a sample of uranium-235, each fission releases 3 neutrons, and every neutron is absorbed by another uranium-235 nucleus, which then splits.

**(a)** Calculate the number of fissions in the fifth generation if the first generation is a single fission. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe what would happen to the rate of energy release if this continued. **[1]**

**(c)** In a reactor, how many of the 3 neutrons from each fission should, on average, cause another fission? **[1]**

**(d)** State what happens to the other neutrons. **[1]**

**10.** An engineer lowers the control rods further into the core of a reactor.

**(a)** Explain the effect on the rate of fission. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why this changes the electrical power output of the power station. **[2]**

**11.** Describe how the energy released by fission in a nuclear power station is used to generate electricity. **[4]**

**12.** A country is deciding whether to build a new nuclear power station.

**(a)** Explain why nuclear waste must be managed with great care. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate the use of nuclear power for generating electricity. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The splitting of a large, unstable nucleus [1] with the release of energy [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A neutron [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Two smaller nuclei [1], two or three neutrons [1] and energy [1]. **[3]**

**5.** They absorb neutrons (so that on average only one neutron per fission causes further fission) [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Fission releases much more energy (millions of times more) than burning the same mass of coal [1]. **[1]**

**7. (a)** **2** [1].

**(b)** Left: 235 + 1 = 236 [1]. Right: 140 + 94 + 2 × 1 = 236 [1].

**(c)** Their mass numbers (140 and 94) are much less than that of the uranium-235 nucleus [1]. **[4]**

**8.** A neutron is absorbed by a uranium-235 nucleus [1], which becomes unstable and splits into two smaller nuclei, releasing energy [1] and two or three neutrons [1]. These neutrons are absorbed by other uranium-235 nuclei, which also split and release more neutrons [1]. Diagram: an incoming neutron, a labelled uranium-235 nucleus, two fission products and two or three neutrons, with at least one of these neutrons shown causing a second fission [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** 1, 3, 9, 27, 81 [1]: **81 fissions** [1].

**(b)** It would increase extremely rapidly (out of control) [1].

**(c)** **One** [1].

**(d)** They are absorbed, mainly by the control rods (or escape from the core) [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** More of the control rod material is among the fuel, so more neutrons are absorbed [1]. Fewer neutrons are available to be absorbed by uranium-235 nuclei [1], so fewer fissions take place each second [1].

**(b)** Energy is released more slowly, so less steam is produced each second [1], the turbines and generators transfer energy at a lower rate and the electrical power output falls [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Energy released by fission heats a coolant in the reactor [1]. This is used to turn water into steam [1]. The steam turns a turbine [1], which drives a generator to produce electricity [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** It is radioactive and emits ionising radiation, which can harm living things [1]. Some of it has a long half-life [1], so it stays dangerous for a very long time and must be stored securely, shielded and kept out of the environment [1].

**(b)** One mark each for any two advantages and two disadvantages, with a judgement for full credit: advantages: no carbon dioxide from burning fuel while operating; large energy release from a small mass of fuel; steady output independent of weather [1] [1]. Disadvantages: long-lived radioactive waste that is difficult and expensive to manage; risk of accidents; high cost of building and dismantling [1] [1]. **[7]**

*Examiner insight:* "Evaluate" needs both sides and a conclusion. In this mark scheme a list of only advantages, or only disadvantages, earns at most two of the four marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out the absorption of a neutron before the split.
- Mixing up nuclei and neutrons in the products.
- Saying control rods slow neutrons down instead of absorbing them.
- Drawing a chain reaction diagram with only one generation.
- Stating "a lot of energy" without comparing with a chemical reaction of similar mass.
- Writing a one-sided evaluation.
