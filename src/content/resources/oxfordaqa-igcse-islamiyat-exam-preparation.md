OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (the board's own spelling; 9237) is a linear, untiered
qualification with no coursework, assessed by two equally weighted written papers. Paper 1 (Islam:
Beliefs and Teachings; Islam: Practices) and Paper 2 (Life and Teachings of Hazrat Muhammad; Sources
of Authority in Islam) each run 1 hour 30 minutes, carry 50 marks and are worth 50%. **Both papers
require study of the specific Qur'an and Hadith passages set out in the specification** -- this is not
a general-knowledge subject. OxfordAQA does not offer Islamiat at AS or A-level. These notes
complement the site's guides to
[Living the Muslim Life](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-islamiyat-living-muslim-life/) and
[Sources of Authority in Islam](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-islamiyat-sources-of-authority/).

## The set passages are named texts, not a general subject area

Because both papers require study of specific passages, an answer that discusses a belief or practice
in general terms without reference to the actual set text is missing what the specification asks for.
**Exam-preparation priority**: build a passage-by-passage revision list rather than a topic-by-topic
one. For each set passage or Hadith, know precisely what it says, and be ready to cite it by name or
description when a question invites textual support.

## Fifty marks in ninety minutes is 1.8 minutes per mark -- generous, if used for planning

That is a comfortable budget by GCSE standards. **Exam-preparation priority**: use the extra time for
planning rather than for writing at greater length. A brief plan -- the point, the evidence, the
significance -- produces a tighter, better-structured answer than writing without one, and structure is
what separates strong answers from merely accurate ones.

## Belief and practice need to be connected, not treated as separate lists

Paper 1 spans both beliefs and practices, and the strongest answers connect them -- showing how a
particular belief underpins a particular practice, rather than describing each independently.
**Exam-preparation priority**: for each major belief, rehearse one sentence linking it to a practice
it explains or motivates. That connection is frequently the difference between a descriptive answer
and an explanatory one.

## Significance is a distinct step from narration

A large share of the marks on both papers depends on moving from *what* a passage or event says to
*why it matters* for Muslim belief and practice. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise the
significance step explicitly. For each passage or event revised, write two sentences of significance
from memory before checking notes -- narrated detail without stated significance is a common way to
under-score an otherwise accurate answer.

## Both papers examine the life and teachings of the Prophet -- from different angles

Paper 1's practices content and Paper 2's dedicated section on the Prophet's life both draw on his
example, but for different purposes -- one to illustrate practice, one to establish authority and
history. **Exam-preparation priority**: keep clear in revision which angle a given fact supports, so
that it can be deployed correctly depending on which paper's question calls for it.

## Worked routine: citing a source within an answer

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper, source booklet or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - state the point being made:
Clearly, before bringing in the source.

Step 2 - identify the specific passage or Hadith by name or
description:
Not "the Qur'an says" in general, but the specific set passage
relevant to this point.

Step 3 - state what it says, accurately and briefly:
A close paraphrase, not a lengthy quotation reproduced from
memory.

Step 4 - explain what it establishes:
The teaching, principle or example the passage supports -- this is
where the significance mark sits.

Step 5 - connect back to the question:
One sentence linking the source back to the specific demand of the
question, not left as a general observation.
```

Step 4 is the step most often shortened under time pressure, and it is usually where the marks for
understanding (rather than recall) are concentrated.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: build a passage-by-passage revision list for the specific set texts; practise
  writing two sentences of significance from memory for each item revised; rehearse connecting belief
  to practice explicitly.
- **During either paper**: use the generous time budget to plan briefly before each answer rather than
  writing at greater length.
- **In every answer that uses a source**: name the specific passage or Hadith, state what it says
  accurately, explain what it establishes, and connect it back to the question.
- **Throughout**: keep clear which angle -- practice or authority and history -- a given fact about the
  Prophet's life supports, depending on which paper it appears in.

## Self-test

1. What is distinctive about how both papers use source material?
2. How much time is available per mark, and what should the extra time be used for?
3. What connects a strong Paper 1 answer's treatment of belief and practice?
4. What step is most often shortened under time pressure, and why does it matter?

**Answers:** 1. Both papers require study of the specific Qur'an and Hadith passages set out in the
specification -- this is not a general-knowledge subject, and answers should cite the actual set text
rather than discussing beliefs and practices in the abstract. 2. About 1.8 minutes per mark; the extra
time should go into planning -- point, evidence, significance -- rather than into writing at greater
length. 3. An explicit connection between a belief and the practice it underpins or explains, rather
than treating the two as separate lists. 4. Explaining what a cited passage establishes -- the
significance step -- because it is where marks for understanding, rather than recall, are concentrated.
