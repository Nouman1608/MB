These notes condense section **3.5.6 Interference** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: "path difference. Coherence", Young's double-slit experiment and the fringe spacing w = λD/s, white-light fringes, laser safety, interference "produced with sound and electromagnetic waves" and Required practical 5. How a laser works is not required.

For the full explanations and worked examples, read the [interference study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-interference-oxfordaqa-alevel/). Test yourself afterwards with the [interference practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-interference-oxfordaqa-alevel-practice/).

## Core recall

- Interference is the superposition of waves from two or more coherent sources, giving a stable pattern of maxima and minima.
- Coherent: same frequency and a constant phase difference (not necessarily zero).
- In-phase sources: path difference nλ gives a maximum; (n + ½)λ gives a minimum.
- Antiphase sources: the conditions swap, so the centre line becomes a minimum.
- Young's slits: one coherent source and two slits, or two coherent sources. Fringes are equally spaced with w = λD/s when D is much larger than s.
- White light: central fringe white, side fringes coloured with violet nearest the centre, few fringes visible.
- A laser gives an intense, narrow beam of coherent, monochromatic light, which is why it is used for these experiments and why it is a hazard to the eye.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Superposition | The resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements |
| Path difference | Difference in the distances travelled by two waves to reach a point |
| Coherent | Same frequency and constant phase difference |
| Monochromatic | A single wavelength (single frequency) |
| Constructive interference | Waves arrive in phase, giving a maximum |
| Destructive interference | Waves arrive in antiphase, giving a minimum |
| Fringe spacing w | Distance between the centres of adjacent bright fringes |
| Slit separation s | Distance between the centres of the two slits |

## Fringe spacing: what changes w

| Change | w |
|---|---|
| D increased | Increases in proportion |
| s increased | Decreases in proportion |
| λ increased (towards red) | Increases in proportion |
| Brighter source | No change |
| One slit covered | Fringes vanish; only the single-slit diffraction pattern remains |
| Sources made antiphase | Same spacing; pattern shifts by half a fringe |

## Method: wavelength from Young's slits

1. Measure D with a tape measure from slits to screen.
2. Measure across as many bright fringes as possible; divide by the number of **spacings** (fringes − 1).
3. Take s from the slide or measure it with a travelling microscope.
4. λ = ws/D, with all lengths in metres.
5. Percentage uncertainties add: %λ = %w + %s + %D.
6. Alternative: vary D, plot w against D, and use gradient = λ/s.

## Required practical 5: what to remember

- Use a laser with a double slit, and separately a diffraction grating, to observe interference.
- With the grating, measure the angle to each order on both sides of the centre and halve the total.
- Identify the measurement with the largest percentage uncertainty; it is usually the slit separation for Young's slits.
- Repeat readings and take means.

## Interference beyond light

| Waves | Coherent sources | What is detected |
|---|---|---|
| Sound | Two loudspeakers on one signal generator | Loud and quiet regions along a line |
| Microwaves | One transmitter and a double slit in a metal plate | Detector reading rises and falls |
| Radio | Two aerials fed from one transmitter | Strong and weak reception regions |

With microwaves or sound, s may be only a few wavelengths, so w = λD/s is not accurate. Use path difference s sin θ = nλ for the maxima.

## Laser safety

- Low-power laser only; never look into the beam.
- Avoid reflections from shiny surfaces, jewellery and watches.
- Stop the beam with a matt surface; keep it below eye level.
- The danger is a focused spot on the retina, which can cause permanent damage.

## Quick reminders with numbers

- λ = 650 nm, s = 0.30 mm, D = 1.5 m: w = 3.25 mm.
- Eleven bright fringes across 24 mm: ten spacings, so w = 2.4 mm.
- 1000 Hz sound at 340 m s⁻¹: λ = 0.34 m; a path difference of 0.17 m gives a minimum for in-phase speakers and a maximum for antiphase speakers.

## Exam traps

- "Coherent means in phase" is wrong; the phase difference must be constant.
- Counting fringes instead of spacings.
- Leaving s or w in millimetres in λ = ws/D.
- Applying the in-phase rule to antiphase sources.
- Saying the central white-light fringe is coloured.
- Using w = λD/s when the angles are large.
- Giving "wear goggles" as the only laser precaution.

## Self-test

1. Define coherent sources.
2. Two in-phase sources: what path difference gives the second minimum from the centre?
3. Why do two separate lamps not produce a visible interference pattern?
4. λ = 590 nm, s = 0.50 mm, D = 2.0 m. Find w.
5. Seven bright fringes span 15 mm. Find w.
6. State what happens to w when s is halved and D is doubled.
7. Describe the centre of the white-light pattern and explain it.
8. Two speakers in antiphase: what is heard on the line equidistant from them?
9. Percentage uncertainties are 3% in s, 0.5% in D and 2% in w. Find the percentage uncertainty in λ.
10. Give two laser safety precautions.
11. Why might w = λD/s give a poor answer for microwaves through slits 6 cm apart?
12. A graph of w against D has gradient 2.4 × 10⁻³ and s = 0.25 mm. Find λ.

### Answers

1. Sources with the **same frequency and a constant phase difference**.
2. **1.5λ**.
3. Each emits in short random bursts, so the phase difference **keeps changing** and the pattern averages out.
4. w = 590 × 10⁻⁹ × 2.0/0.50 × 10⁻³ = **2.4 mm** (2.36 mm).
5. Six spacings: w = 15/6 = **2.5 mm**.
6. w becomes **four times** larger.
7. **White**: every wavelength has zero path difference there, so all colours have a maximum together.
8. **Quiet** (a minimum).
9. 3 + 0.5 + 2 = **5.5%**.
10. Any two: never look into the beam; avoid reflections from shiny surfaces; keep the beam below eye level; stop it with a matt screen; use a low-power laser.
11. The slit separation is only a few wavelengths, so the angles are **large** and the small-angle approximation fails.
12. λ = gradient × s = 2.4 × 10⁻³ × 0.25 × 10⁻³ = **6.0 × 10⁻⁷ m**.
