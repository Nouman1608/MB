These notes condense section 3.4, Trade unions, of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026: the definition, the role of unions in the economy, and the advantages and disadvantages of union activity for workers, firms and the government, including the factors that make unions strong. All figures are invented. For explanations and worked examples read the [trade unions study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-trade-unions-cambridge-igcse-economics/), then practise with the [trade unions practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-trade-unions-cambridge-igcse-economics-practice/). The combined [workers, wages and trade unions revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-workers-wages-and-trade-unions-revision-notes/) link unions to wage determination in section 3.3.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Trade union | An organisation of workers that protects and promotes members' interests: pay, hours, conditions and job security |
| Collective bargaining | Negotiation between a union, for all its members, and employers |
| Industrial action | Pressure on an employer by workers: strike, overtime ban, work-to-rule |
| Strike | Workers refuse to work |
| Work-to-rule | Workers do only the minimum their contracts require |
| Overtime ban | Workers refuse to work extra hours |
| Productivity agreement | Higher pay in return for changes that raise output per worker |
| Membership density | Union members as a percentage of the workforce |

## Role of unions (3.4.2)

1. **Collective bargaining** on:
   - wages (pay rises, overtime rates);
   - working hours (weekly hours, shifts, holidays);
   - working conditions (safety, equipment, training).
2. **Protecting employment:** resist redundancies, seek retraining, represent members in disputes, campaign for safety.
3. **Influencing government policy:** lobby for minimum wage rises, employment and safety laws, public services, policies to cut unemployment.

## Bargaining in numbers

```
Cost of a pay rise per year = weekly wage bill x % rise x 52
Labour cost per unit = wage per worker / output per worker
Membership density = members / workforce x 100
```

Worked reminder: 120 workers on 500 a week; union wins 3%.

```
Weekly bill = 60,000; extra a year = 60,000 x 0.03 x 52 = 93,600
```

Worked reminder: pay rises from 400 to 420 a week (5%) and output per worker from 160 to 175 units (9.4%).

```
Labour cost per unit: 400 / 160 = 2.50 falls to 420 / 175 = 2.40
```

## Union strength (3.4.3)

| Makes a union stronger | Makes a union weaker |
|---|---|
| High membership density | Low membership density |
| Scarce, hard-to-replace skills | Easily replaced workers |
| Low unemployment | High unemployment |
| Profitable employer, rising demand | Loss-making employer, falling demand |
| Strike quickly stops output or a vital service | Firm can use stocks, machines or other sites |
| Supportive laws | Laws restricting action |
| Large strike funds, united members | Small funds, divided members |

## Advantages and disadvantages: three viewpoints

| Viewpoint | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Workers | Higher pay and better conditions; protection against unfair treatment; a voice at work | Subscriptions; lost pay during strikes; possible job losses if wages pushed too high |
| Firms | One negotiation instead of many; better communication; more motivated, loyal staff | Higher costs and lower profit; lost output and customers in strikes; slower adoption of new methods |
| Government | Support for low-paid workers; safer workplaces; information on conditions | Disruption of essential services; pressure on prices from wage rises; pressure for more spending |

## Evaluation chains

- **Higher pay won by a union:** workers gain income → firm's costs rise → firm may raise prices, cut profit or hire fewer workers. Outcome depends on the firm's profitability and whether productivity also rises.
- **Strike:** pressure on employer → possible settlement → but workers lose pay and the firm may lose customers permanently.
- **Productivity deal:** pay rises and labour cost per unit falls → both sides gain → depends on workers accepting new methods.

## Exam traps

- **Definition needs a purpose:** "an organisation of workers *that protects members' interests*".
- **Collective bargaining is not industrial action.** Action is used only if bargaining fails.
- **Answer from the viewpoint named.** Do not switch to workers when asked about government.
- **Strength depends on the case.** Use the information given: membership, skills, unemployment, profitability.
- **Unions do not always cause job losses.** Productivity deals and profitable firms can absorb higher pay.

## Self-test

1. Define a trade union.
2. What is collective bargaining?
3. Name the three areas of collective bargaining in the syllabus.
4. State two ways unions protect employment.
5. Give one way a union might try to influence government policy.
6. A union has 1,170 members in a workforce of 1,800. Calculate its membership density.
7. State two factors that make a union stronger.
8. Why does high unemployment weaken a union?
9. Give one advantage of unions for firms.
10. Give one disadvantage of unions for government.
11. Pay rises from 600 to 630 a week and output per worker from 240 to 252 units. What happens to labour cost per unit?
12. Distinguish a strike from a work-to-rule.

### Answers

1. An organisation of workers that protects and promotes its members' interests, such as pay, hours, conditions and job security.
2. Negotiation between a union, on behalf of all its members, and an employer.
3. Wages, working hours and working conditions.
4. Any two: resisting redundancies; seeking retraining; representing members facing dismissal; campaigning for safe workplaces.
5. Lobbying for a higher minimum wage or stronger safety laws; public campaigns.
6. 1,170 / 1,800 x 100 = 65%.
7. Any two: high membership; scarce skills; low unemployment; profitable employer; supportive laws; strike stops output quickly.
8. Employers can replace strikers easily and workers fear losing their jobs.
9. A single negotiation saves time; better communication; more motivated staff.
10. Strikes in essential services; pay rises can push up prices; pressure for more spending.
11. 600 / 240 = 2.50 and 630 / 252 = 2.50, so it stays the same.
12. A strike is a complete refusal to work; a work-to-rule means doing only what the contract strictly requires.
