All data are invented. Questions marked (calculator allowed) need a GDC for the standard deviation; do the
rest by hand.

## Section A

**1.** For the data 15, 8, 11, 20, 8, 13, 17, 12, 9, find

**(a)** the mean; **[1]**
**(b)** the median; **[1]**
**(c)** the mode. **[1]**

**2.** Find the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range of
23, 31, 18, 27, 35, 22, 29, 40, 25, 33. **[3]**

**3.** The mean of 12 values is 7.5. One value is removed and the mean of the remaining 11 values is 7. Find
the value that was removed. **[2]**

**4.** A data set has mean 25 and variance 16. Each value is halved and then 4 is added. Find the new mean,
standard deviation and variance. **[3]**

**5.** The masses, m grams, of 40 eggs are grouped as follows.

| m | 40 ≤ m < 50 | 50 ≤ m < 60 | 60 ≤ m < 70 | 70 ≤ m < 80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 11 | 15 | 8 |

**(a)** Write down the modal class. **[1]**
**(b)** Estimate the mean mass. **[2]**

**6.** Lengths of a type of bolt have standard deviation 2.4 mm.

**(a)** Find the variance, stating its units. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one reason why the standard deviation is more useful than the variance for describing the
spread of the lengths. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) The table shows the numbers of pets owned by a group of students.

| Pets x | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 4 | k | 9 | 6 | 1 |

The mean number of pets is 2.8.

**(a)** Find k. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the median. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the interquartile range. **[3]**
**(d)** Write down the standard deviation. **[1]**

**8.** Class A has 18 students with a mean test mark of 64. Class B has 12 students with a mean test mark of
71.

**(a)** Find the mean mark of all 30 students. **[2]**
**(b)** The teacher adds 5 marks to every student in Class B. Find the new mean of all 30 students. **[2]**
**(c)** State the effect of this change on the standard deviation of Class B's marks. **[1]**

**9.** Raw scores on a test have mean 30 and standard deviation 5. They are converted to scaled scores using
y = ax + b, with a > 0, so that the scaled scores have mean 50 and standard deviation 10.

**(a)** Find a and b. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the scaled score of a student whose raw score was 38. **[1]**

**10.** Daily maximum temperatures in a town over a month had mean 68 °F and standard deviation 9 °F. The
conversion to Celsius is C = (5/9)(F − 32). Find the mean, standard deviation and variance of the
temperatures in °C. **[3]**

**11.** The annual salaries, in thousands of dollars, of the nine employees of a small firm are:

28, 30, 31, 33, 35, 36, 38, 40, 180

**(a)** Find the mean and the median salary. **[2]**
**(b)** State which of these better represents a typical salary, giving a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** State, with a reason, what would happen to the standard deviation if the salary of 180 were
removed. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) The heights of 36 plants are grouped below.

| Height h (cm) | 150 ≤ h < 160 | 160 ≤ h < 170 | 170 ≤ h < 180 | 180 ≤ h < 190 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 6 | 15 | 11 | 4 |

**(a)** Estimate the mean and standard deviation of the heights. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why your answers to (a) are estimates. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Σx = 113, so the mean is 113/9 ≈ 12.6 [1].
**(b)** Ordered: 8, 8, 9, 11, 12, 13, 15, 17, 20, so the median is 12 [1].
**(c)** 8 [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Ordered: 18, 22, 23, 25, 27, 29, 31, 33, 35, 40. Q₁ = 23 (median of the lower five) [1];
Q₃ = 33 (median of the upper five) [1]; IQR = 10 [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Total of 12 values = 90; total of 11 values = 77 [1]; removed value = 13 [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Mean = 25/2 + 4 = 16.5 [1]. Original standard deviation 4, so the new standard deviation is 2 [1].
Variance = 4 [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** 60 ≤ m < 70 [1].
**(b)** Mid-interval values 45, 55, 65, 75: Σfx = 270 + 605 + 975 + 600 = 2450 [1]. Mean ≈ 2450/40
= 61.25 g [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** 2.4² = 5.76 mm² [1].
**(b)** It is in the same units as the lengths (mm), so it can be compared directly with them [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Σf = 20 + k and Σfx = 4 + 2k + 27 + 24 + 5 = 60 + 2k [1]. (60 + 2k)/(20 + k) = 2.8 gives
60 + 2k = 56 + 2.8k [1], so k = 5 [1].
**(b)** n = 25; the 13th value; cumulative frequencies 4, 9, 18, 24, 25, so the median is 3 [1].
**(c)** Q₁ = mean of 6th and 7th values = 2 [1]; Q₃ = mean of 19th and 20th values = 4 [1]; IQR = 2 [1].
**(d)** σ ≈ 1.10 [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** (18 × 64 + 12 × 71)/30 = (1152 + 852)/30 [1] = 66.8 [1].
**(b)** The total increases by 12 × 5 = 60 [1], so the new mean is 66.8 + 60/30 = 68.8 [1].
**(c)** No change: adding a constant does not affect spread [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Standard deviation: 5a = 10 [1], so a = 2 [1]. Mean: 2 × 30 + b = 50 [1], so b = −10 [1].
**(b)** 2 × 38 − 10 = 66 [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Mean = (5/9)(68 − 32) = 20 °C [1]. Standard deviation = (5/9) × 9 = 5 °C, since subtracting 32
does not change the spread [1]. Variance = 25 °C² [1]. **[3]**

**11. (a)** Mean = 451/9 ≈ 50.1 thousand dollars [1]; median = 35 thousand dollars [1].
**(b)** The median: the mean is pulled up by the one very high salary and is higher than eight of the nine
salaries [1].
**(c)** It would decrease sharply, because 180 is far from the mean and contributes most of the spread [1].
**[4]**

**12. (a)** Mid-interval values 155, 165, 175, 185 [1]. Mean ≈ 6070/36 ≈ 169 cm [1];
σ ≈ 8.87 cm [1].
**(b)** The individual heights are unknown, so each is assumed to be at the mid-interval value of its class
[1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the number of classes instead of Σf in questions 5 and 12.
- Including the median in both halves when finding quartiles for an odd number of values.
- Adding the constant to the standard deviation in questions 4 and 10.
- Averaging 64 and 71 in question 8(a) to get 67.5.
- Finding b before a in question 9, which leaves two unknowns in one equation.
