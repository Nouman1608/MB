Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives (0457) has three components, and every candidate takes all three; none is optional. Component 1, Written Exam (1 hour 25 minutes, 70 marks, 35%), Component 2, Individual Report (60 marks, 30%) and Component 3, Team Project (70 marks, 35%). The syllabus is not tiered, and the examination-series window is 2025-2027. This page covers the three components in turn and links to the detailed pages for the [Written Exam](/resources/igcse-cambridge-global-perspectives-written-exam/), the [Individual Report](/resources/igcse-cambridge-global-perspectives-individual-report/) and the [Team Project](/resources/igcse-cambridge-global-perspectives-team-project/).

## Component 1: Written Exam (70 marks, 35%)

The written exam lasts 1 hour 25 minutes and has four compulsory source-based questions, externally assessed. Because every question is based on source material, the central skill is working with sources you have not seen before: identifying the claim, judging how reliable it is, comparing perspectives and constructing a reasoned response. Knowledge of the global topics helps you place a source in context, but the marks come from analysis. The syllabus topics include arts in society, climate change, development, the digital world, health and wellbeing, migration, poverty and inequality, and many others, so revise a range. Support is available in the [Written Exam revision notes](/resources/global-perspectives-revision-notes/).

## Component 2: Individual Report (60 marks, 30%)

The report is a piece of 1,500 to 2,000 words on a global issue that you choose yourself. The syllabus describes it as internally set and externally marked, and the record lists it as externally assessed. It carries 60 marks, which is 30% of the qualification. There is no fixed exam time, so the skill is managing a research project: choosing a manageable issue, finding varied sources, weighing perspectives and writing a clear, evidenced report. See the [Individual Report revision notes](/resources/global-perspectives-individual-report-revision-notes/).

## Component 3: Team Project (70 marks, 35%)

The Team Project has two parts: a collaborative Team Element and an individually written Reflective Paper of 750 to 1,000 words. It is internally assessed and externally moderated, so your teacher marks it and Cambridge checks the marking. The reflective paper is your chance to show what you personally contributed and learned, so keep notes during the project rather than relying on memory.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Component 1: 85 minutes / 70 marks = about 1.21 minutes per mark (85 / 70 = 1.214...). A 10-mark question therefore suits roughly 12 minutes.
- Components 2 and 3 are not timed exams, so plan by deadlines instead.
- Marks across the qualification: 70 + 60 + 70 = 200.
- Weightings: 35 + 30 + 35 = 100%.
- Each mark in every component is worth the same: 35 / 70 = 0.5%, 30 / 60 = 0.5% and 35 / 70 = 0.5%.

Since each mark is worth 0.5% in all three components, the coursework-style components deserve as much care as the exam.

## Command words and question styles

- **Identify** or **state**: pick out a point from the source.
- **Compare**: show similarities and differences between perspectives.
- **Evaluate** or **assess**: judge the reliability or strength of an argument using evidence.
- **Explain**: give reasons.
- **To what extent**: weigh the case and give a supported conclusion.

## Planning your preparation across the year

The three components reward different habits, so plan them separately. For the written exam, build a weekly routine of reading a short unfamiliar source and writing a few sentences on its argument, evidence, purpose and reliability. For the individual report, set yourself milestones: topic chosen, sources gathered, plan drafted, full draft written and final edit completed, leaving time to trim to the 1,500 to 2,000 word range. For the team project, agree roles early, keep a dated record of what you did and write the reflective paper soon after the project work ends, while the details are fresh. Because the final assessment of this syllabus is in 2027, do not leave any component to a late rush.

## Worked practice scenario

An original source-evaluation practice. Imagine a short source: a campaign leaflet from a group opposing a new motorway says, "The motorway will destroy local wildlife and give nothing back to the community."

Question: "Evaluate the reliability of this source as evidence about the effects of the motorway."

Model answer: "The source is a campaign leaflet written by a group that opposes the motorway, so it has a clear purpose: to persuade readers to join the opposition. This weakens its reliability as neutral evidence, because the group has a reason to emphasise harm. The claim that the motorway 'will destroy' wildlife is stated as certain but offers no figures, species or survey, so it cannot be checked. The phrase 'give nothing back' is a sweeping statement that ignores possible benefits such as shorter journeys. However, the source is useful for showing what opponents believe and how they argue. To judge the real effects, I would compare it with an independent environmental assessment and with the view of the developers, and look for data on traffic, habitat and local jobs."

This works because it addresses author, purpose, evidence, missing perspectives and a way to check the claim.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Remember all three components are compulsory.
- Confirm the date of Component 1 and the deadlines for Components 2 and 3.
- Practise source questions: author, purpose, evidence and perspective.
- Keep research notes and a source log for the report.
- Stay within 1,500 to 2,000 words for the report.
- Keep a record of your own contribution to the team project.
- Stay within 750 to 1,000 words for the reflective paper.
- In the exam, use about 1.2 minutes per mark.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many components are in Cambridge IGCSE Global Perspectives 0457?**
There are three, and all are compulsory: Written Exam (35%), Individual Report (30%) and Team Project (35%).

**How long is the written exam?**
It lasts 1 hour 25 minutes, carries 70 marks and has four compulsory source-based questions.

**Who assesses the individual report?**
The syllabus describes it as internally set and externally marked. It is worth 60 marks and 30%.

**How is the team project assessed?**
It is worth 70 marks and 35%, with a collaborative Team Element plus an individual Reflective Paper of 750 to 1,000 words. It is internally assessed and externally moderated.

**Until when is this syllabus examined?**
The record gives a first assessment in 2025 and a final assessment in 2027. Grades A* to G are available.
