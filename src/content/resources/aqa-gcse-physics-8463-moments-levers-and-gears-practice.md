## Section A

**1.** Define the moment of a force. **[1]**

**2.** Give two everyday examples in which a force causes an object to rotate. Identify the pivot in each.
**[2]**

**3.** A force of 35 N acts at right angles to a lever, 0.40 m from the pivot. Calculate the moment of the
force. **[2]**

**4.** A tap needs a moment of 1.2 N m to turn it. The handle is 4.0 cm long. Calculate the force needed at
the end of the handle, at right angles to it. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why it is easier to undo a tight nut with a long spanner than with a short one. **[2]**

**6.** State the direction in which a driven gear turns compared with the gear that drives it. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Three people use a seesaw that is balanced at its centre. On the left, a person of weight 280 N sits
1.8 m from the pivot. On the right, a person of weight 350 N sits 0.80 m from the pivot, and a third person
of weight 420 N also sits on the right.

**(a)** Calculate how far from the pivot the third person must sit for the seesaw to balance. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the upward force of the pivot on the seesaw. Ignore the weight of the seesaw. **[1]**

**8.** A uniform plank is 3.0 m long and has a weight of 240 N. It rests on a pivot 1.0 m from its left end.
A boy stands on the left end and the plank balances.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of the boy. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the upward force of the pivot on the plank. **[1]**

**9.** A gardener holds a wheelbarrow level. The load of 750 N acts 0.35 m from the wheel axle. The gardener
lifts the handles 1.5 m from the axle.

**(a)** Calculate the upward force the gardener must apply. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest and explain one way to reduce the force needed, without changing the load. **[2]**

**10.** A mechanic pulls on a 0.25 m spanner with a force of 50 N. Because of the angle she pulls at, the
perpendicular distance from the nut to the line of action of the force is 0.20 m.

**(a)** Calculate the moment of her force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the moment if she pulled at right angles to the spanner. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the two moments are different. **[1]**

**11.** A winch has a driver gear with 20 teeth and a radius of 3.0 cm. It meshes with a driven gear with
60 teeth and a radius of 9.0 cm. The moment on the driver gear is 0.90 N m.

**(a)** Calculate the force between the teeth of the gears. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the moment on the driven gear, ignoring friction. **[2]**
**(c)** The driver gear turns 120 times each minute. Calculate how many times the driven gear turns each
minute. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why this arrangement is useful for a winch that lifts heavy loads. **[2]**

**12.** Explain how a crowbar allows a person to lift a heavy object using a small force. **[4]**

**13.** A tower crane lifts a load of weight 12 000 N at 15 m from the tower, which acts as the pivot. A
counterweight of 40 000 N on the other side keeps the crane balanced.

**(a)** Calculate the distance of the counterweight from the tower. **[2]**
**(b)** The load moves out to 20 m from the tower. Calculate how much further from the tower the
counterweight must move to keep the crane balanced. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The turning effect of a force, equal to the force × the perpendicular distance from the pivot to its
line of action [1]

**2.** First example with its correct pivot [1]; second example with its correct pivot [1]. Suitable
examples: a door (hinges); a spanner (the nut); a seesaw (its central support); a wheelbarrow (the wheel
axle); a steering wheel (its centre)

**3.** M = 35 × 0.40 [1]; = **14 N m** [1]

**4.** F = M / d = 1.2 / 0.040 [1]; = **30 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Using 4.0 instead of 0.040 gives 0.30 N; convert to metres first.

**5.** A longer spanner gives a larger perpendicular distance from the nut [1]; so the same force produces a
larger moment, or a smaller force is needed for the same moment [1]

**6.** The opposite direction [1]

**7. (a)** Anticlockwise moment = 280 × 1.8 = 504 N m [1]; clockwise moment = 350 × 0.80 + 420 × d = 280 +
420 d [1]; 420 d = 224, so d = **0.53 m** [1]
**(b)** 280 + 350 + 420 = **1050 N** [1]

**8. (a)** The plank's weight acts at its midpoint, 1.5 m from the left end, so 0.50 m right of the pivot [1];
clockwise moment = 240 × 0.50 = 120 N m [1]; boy's weight = 120 / 1.0 = **120 N** [1]
**(b)** 240 + 120 = **360 N** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The plank's own weight is the only clockwise moment; leaving it out makes the problem
impossible to solve.

**9. (a)** Moment of load = 750 × 0.35 = 262.5 N m [1]; F × 1.5 = 262.5 [1]; F = **175 N** [1]
**(b)** Move the load closer to the wheel (or use longer handles) [1]; this reduces the moment of the load
(or increases the distance of the effort), so a smaller force gives the moment needed [1]

**10. (a)** M = 50 × 0.20 [1]; = **10 N m** [1]
**(b)** 50 × 0.25 = **12.5 N m** [1]
**(c)** At an angle the perpendicular distance from the nut to the line of action is smaller than the
spanner's length [1]

**11. (a)** F = M / r = 0.90 / 0.030 [1]; = **30 N** [1]
**(b)** M = 30 × 0.090 [1]; = **2.7 N m** [1]
**(c)** 120 × 20 / 60 = **40 times** [1]
**(d)** The driven gear receives three times the moment of the driver [1]; so a small moment from the motor
or handle can produce the large moment needed to lift a heavy load, although the load rises slowly [1]

**12.** The person pushes on the end of the crowbar far from the pivot [1]; the large distance means a small
force produces a large moment [1]; the load is very close to the pivot on the other side [1]; so the same
moment produces a much larger force on the load than the person applies [1]

**13. (a)** 12 000 × 15 = 180 000 N m, so d = 180 000 / 40 000 [1]; = **4.5 m** [1]
**(b)** New distance = 12 000 × 20 / 40 000 = 6.0 m [1]; it must move **1.5 m** further out [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using a distance in cm in a moment given in N m.
- Using the length of the spanner instead of the perpendicular distance.
- Missing one person, or the beam's own weight, from a sum of moments.
- Stating a gear's moment without saying what happens to its speed.
- Giving "the force is multiplied" without linking it to moments and distances.
