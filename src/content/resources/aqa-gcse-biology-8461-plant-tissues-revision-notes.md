These are condensed recall notes for **4.2.3.1 Plant tissues** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, Version 1.0, for teaching from September 2016 and exams from 2018 onwards. It is Paper 1 content at both tiers; nothing here is marked (HT only). Worked explanations are in the [plant tissues study guide](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-tissues/); questions are in the [plant tissues practice set](/resources/aqa-gcse-biology-8461-plant-tissues-practice/).

## What you must know

- Explain how the structures of plant tissues relate to their functions.
- Tissues: epidermal tissues, palisade mesophyll, spongy mesophyll, xylem and phloem, meristem.
- The leaf is a plant organ; knowledge is limited to epidermis, palisade and spongy mesophyll, xylem, phloem, and guard cells surrounding stomata.
- AT 7: observe and draw a transverse section of a leaf.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Epidermis | Outer layer of cells on the upper and lower surfaces of the leaf |
| Palisade mesophyll | Layer of tall, tightly packed cells with many chloroplasts, under the upper epidermis |
| Spongy mesophyll | Loosely packed cells with air spaces, above the lower epidermis |
| Stoma (plural stomata) | Pore in the epidermis, mostly on the lower surface |
| Guard cells | Pair of cells around each stoma that open and close it |
| Xylem | Hollow tubes strengthened by lignin; carry water and mineral ions |
| Lignin | Strengthening substance in xylem walls |
| Phloem | Tubes of elongated cells with pores in the end walls; carry dissolved sugars |
| Meristem | Tissue at the growing tips of shoots and roots whose cells divide and differentiate |
| Transverse section | A slice cut across a structure |

## Leaf layers, top to bottom

```
upper epidermis -> palisade mesophyll -> spongy mesophyll (vein: xylem + phloem) -> lower epidermis (stomata + guard cells)
```

## Structure → function

| Tissue | Structure | So... |
|---|---|---|
| Upper epidermis | Thin, transparent; waxy covering | Light reaches palisade cells; less water lost |
| Palisade mesophyll | Tall, packed, near the top, many chloroplasts | Absorbs lots of light for photosynthesis |
| Spongy mesophyll | Loose cells, air spaces | Carbon dioxide and oxygen diffuse through the leaf; large surface area for gas exchange |
| Lower epidermis | Contains stomata and guard cells | Controls gas exchange and water loss |
| Xylem | Hollow, lignified tubes | Water and mineral ions flow up; tubes stay strong and support the plant |
| Phloem | Elongated cells, pores in end walls | Cell sap with sugars moves from cell to cell |
| Meristem | Dividing cells that can differentiate | Growth of shoots and roots throughout life |

## Photosynthesis: who does what

- Light: through upper epidermis → palisade chloroplasts.
- Carbon dioxide: stomata → air spaces in spongy mesophyll → palisade cells.
- Water: xylem in veins → mesophyll cells.
- Oxygen: out via air spaces and stomata.
- Sugars: away in phloem.

## Xylem or phloem? Quick checks

- Carries water and mineral ions upwards from the roots: xylem.
- Carries dissolved sugars from the leaves to where they are used or stored: phloem.
- Hollow tubes with lignin: xylem. Elongated cells with pores in the end walls: phloem.
- Both are found together in the veins of the leaf.

## Calculations

- Actual size = image size ÷ magnification.
- 1 mm = 1000 µm.
- Percentage of thickness = layer thickness ÷ total thickness × 100.

Mini example: a spongy layer 24 mm deep in an image at × 200 is 24 ÷ 200 = 0.12 mm = 120 µm thick.

## Drawing a leaf section (AT 7)

- Low power first to see the whole section.
- Tissue layers in the right order and proportion; individual cells not needed.
- Pencil, continuous lines, no shading.
- Ruled label lines touching the structures; title and magnification.

## Applying to unfamiliar leaves

- More light (sun leaf) → thicker palisade layer: more chloroplast-filled cells can use the light.
- Less light (shade leaf) → larger area: absorbs more light in total.
- Always quote the data, then link it to the tissue's function.

## Exam traps

- The leaf is an **organ**; palisade mesophyll is a **tissue**.
- Most stomata are in the **lower** epidermis.
- Lignin is in **xylem**, not phloem.
- Meristem is at **shoot and root tips**, not in the middle of the leaf.
- "Chloroplasts for photosynthesis" is weaker than "many chloroplasts to absorb more light for photosynthesis".
- Detailed phloem structure is **not** required.

## Self-test

1. Name the tissue directly below the upper epidermis.
2. Why is the upper epidermis transparent?
3. Give two ways palisade cells are adapted for photosynthesis.
4. How is the spongy mesophyll adapted for gas exchange?
5. Where are most stomata found?
6. What do guard cells do?
7. What substance strengthens xylem?
8. What does phloem carry?
9. Where is meristem tissue found?
10. Why is the leaf described as an organ?
11. A palisade layer is 15 mm deep in an image at × 125. Calculate its actual depth in µm.
12. Give two rules for drawing a transverse section of a leaf.

### Answers

1. Palisade mesophyll.
2. So light passes through to the palisade cells for photosynthesis.
3. Many chloroplasts to absorb light; tall, packed cells near the top surface where light is brightest.
4. Loosely packed cells with air spaces, so gases diffuse through the leaf; large surface area.
5. In the lower epidermis.
6. Open and close the stomata, controlling gas exchange and water loss.
7. Lignin.
8. Dissolved sugars (in cell sap).
9. At the growing tips of shoots and roots.
10. It is made of several tissues working together to carry out photosynthesis.
11. 15 ÷ 125 = 0.12 mm = 120 µm.
12. Any two: pencil; clear continuous lines; no shading; layers in correct proportion; ruled labels; magnification recorded.
