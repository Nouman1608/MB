## Questions

**1.** Identify two operational objectives, other than costs, that a business could set. **[2]**

**2.** Aldwark Fabrics made 13,440 metres of cloth last month with 32 employees. Its capacity is 16,800 metres and its total costs were £60,480.

**(a)** Calculate labour productivity. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the unit cost. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate capacity utilisation. **[2]**

**3.** Aldwark Fabrics is considering Just in Time deliveries of dye. Explain one benefit and one difficulty of this for the business. **[4]**

**4.** Zenmoor Joinery has capacity for 1,500 doors a month. Its fixed costs are £36,000 a month and its variable cost is £30 a door.

**(a)** Calculate the unit cost when it makes 900 doors. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the unit cost when it makes 1,200 doors. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse why the unit cost differs between the two output levels. **[3]**

**5.** Rosedene Foods packs soup pouches. A manual line has fixed costs of £18,000 and variable costs of £8 a batch. An automated line has fixed costs of £54,000 and variable costs of £3 a batch. Demand next year is forecast at 10,000 batches but could fall as low as 5,000.

**(a)** Calculate the number of batches at which total costs are the same for both lines. **[3]**
**(b)** Recommend which line Rosedene Foods should choose. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**6.** Heskin Print uses 80 reams of paper a day. The lead time is 5 days, the buffer level is 200 reams and the re-order quantity is 800 reams.

**(a)** Calculate the re-order level. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the highest stock level reached. **[2]**
**(c)** The supplier's lead time rises to 8 days. Calculate the new re-order level and explain what it means for Heskin Print. **[2]**

**7.** Distinguish between quality control and quality assurance. **[4]**

**8.** Daskerby Dairy packed 50,000 cartons last month and 4% were returned as faulty. Each faulty carton costs £3.50 to replace and dispose of.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of the faulty cartons. **[2]**
**(b)** Analyse two consequences of poor quality for Daskerby Dairy. **[4]**

**9.** Kirkbeck Ice Cream sells much more in summer than in winter. Explain two ways in which it could match supply to demand. **[6]**

**10.** Wadlow Components buys 10,000 casings a month. Supplier A charges £4.20 a casing and delivers on time 82% of the time. Supplier B charges £4.60 and delivers on time 97% of the time. Late deliveries from Supplier A stopped the assembly line for 10 hours last month; late deliveries from Supplier B would stop it for 1 hour. Each hour of stoppage costs £500.

**(a)** Calculate the monthly cost of casings from each supplier. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the monthly cost of casings plus stoppages for each supplier. **[2]**
**(c)** Justify which supplier Wadlow Components should choose. **[6]**

**11.** Zenmoor Joinery plans to let customers design their own kitchen doors online.

**(a)** Define mass customisation. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way technology could help Zenmoor Joinery to offer it. **[3]**

**12.** Stoneycroft Preserves packs 400,000 jars a year in-house at a total cost of £62,000. A specialist firm offers to do the packing for £0.14 a jar. Stoneycroft Preserves would sell its packing equipment, and the specialist firm sources its packaging from a supplier with a poor environmental record.

**(a)** Calculate the annual saving from outsourcing, in pounds and as a percentage of current cost (1 decimal place). **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Stoneycroft Preserves should outsource packing. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: speed of response and flexibility; environmental objectives; added value; or, for example, quality [1] and dependability [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A short identify question asks only for the names, so spend your time on the longer questions instead of explaining.

**2. (a)** Labour productivity = output ÷ employees [1] = 13,440 ÷ 32 = **420 metres per employee** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Unit cost = total costs ÷ output [1] = 60,480 ÷ 13,440 = **£4.50 per metre** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Utilisation = output ÷ capacity × 100 [1] = 13,440 ÷ 16,800 × 100 = **80%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the formula and the substitution so that credit can be given for method even if the arithmetic slips, and include units.

**3.** Benefit: lower storage and holding costs because dye is not kept in stock [1], which frees cash and space [1]. Difficulty: Aldwark would depend on the supplier delivering on time [1], so a late delivery could stop production with no buffer stock [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point usually needs a reason attached; "it is cheaper" on its own is thin, whereas explaining why gains more credit.

**4. (a)** Total cost = 36,000 + (30 × 900) = £63,000 [1]; unit cost = 63,000 ÷ 900 = **£70** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Total cost = 36,000 + (30 × 1,200) = £72,000 [1]; unit cost = 72,000 ÷ 1,200 = **£60** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Utilisation rises from 60% to 80% [1]. Fixed costs of £36,000 are spread over more doors [1], while variable cost stays at £30 a door, so unit cost falls by £10 [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An analysis answer is stronger when it uses the figures from the case and shows the chain from higher output to lower fixed cost per unit.

**5. (a)** Costs equal when 18,000 + 8q = 54,000 + 3q [1]; 5q = 36,000 [1]; **q = 7,200 batches** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** At 10,000 batches manual costs £98,000 (£9.80 each) and automated £84,000 (£8.40 each) [1], so automated is £14,000 cheaper at the forecast [1]. At 5,000 batches manual costs £58,000 and automated £69,000 [1], so manual is £11,000 cheaper if demand falls [1]. Automated carries higher fixed costs and risk, but suits large steady output and consistent quality; manual is more flexible [1]. Judgement: recommend automated, because the forecast is above 7,200 and the saving at forecast exceeds the loss at the low end, provided the firm can fund the investment; if the low figure is judged likely, manual is safer [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Set the forecast against the downside case and say what the choice depends on, rather than naming a line with no condition attached.

**6. (a)** Re-order level = buffer + (daily usage × lead time) [1] = 200 + 80 × 5 = **600 reams** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Maximum stock = buffer + re-order quantity [1] = 200 + 800 = **1,000 reams** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** New level = 80 × 8 + 200 = **840 reams** [1]. Heskin Print must order earlier, when more stock remains, or the buffer will be used up before the delivery arrives [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what you assume: the maximum stock formula takes delivery as stock reaches the buffer, and the buffer belongs in both calculations.

**7.** Quality control means inspecting products, during or after production, and removing faulty items [1]. It finds faults after they have occurred [1]. Quality assurance means setting standards for every stage, including suppliers and staff responsibility [1], so that faults are prevented [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Draw the contrast explicitly, such as detecting versus preventing faults; two stand-alone definitions may not show the difference.

**8. (a)** Faulty cartons = 4% × 50,000 = 2,000 [1]; cost = 2,000 × £3.50 = **£7,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Consequence 1: refunds and replacements cost £7,000 a month [1], which cuts profit and wastes materials [1]. Consequence 2: customers who receive faulty cartons may stop buying or complain online [1], which damages the reputation and reduces future sales [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Linking each consequence to the case and following it through to profit or sales is how analysis tends to be developed.

**9.** Way 1: use temporary or part time employees in summer [1]. This lets Kirkbeck Ice Cream increase production hours in peak months without paying full-time wages all year [1], though new staff may need training and produce less well [1]. Way 2: outsource some production to another manufacturer in peak months [1]. This adds capacity without buying machines that would be idle in winter [1], but gives less control over quality [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Two distinct methods are needed; two versions of the same idea may be treated as one.

**10. (a)** A: 10,000 × £4.20 = **£42,000** [1]; B: 10,000 × £4.60 = **£46,000** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** A: stoppages 10 × £500 = £5,000, total **£47,000** [1]; B: 1 × £500 = £500, total **£46,500** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** On price alone A is £4,000 cheaper [1], but including stoppages B is £500 cheaper [1]. Reliability matters because late parts halt the line and risk late orders to customers [1]. B's 97% on-time record supports dependability for Wadlow's own customers [1]. The gap is small, so the result is sensitive to the stoppage estimate, which is only one month's figure [1]. Judgement: choose B, as it is cheaper in total and more dependable, but review the data over several months [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A justification is stronger when it uses the combined cost figure and also notes a limit of the data, instead of choosing on price or reliability alone.

**11. (a)** Mass customisation is making products to individual customers' wishes [1] at a cost close to mass production [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** An online design tool lets customers choose size, finish and style [1] and passes the order straight to computer-controlled machines [1], so no manual re-entry is needed and each door can be made without long set-up delays [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* For a "one way" question, develop a single way with a consequence rather than listing several.

**12. (a)** Outsourced cost = 400,000 × £0.14 = £56,000 [1]; saving = 62,000 - 56,000 = **£6,000** [1]; 6,000 ÷ 62,000 × 100 = **9.7%** [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** For: saves £6,000 a year (9.7%) [1]; the specialist's expertise may raise packing quality [1]; selling equipment releases cash [1]. Against: less control over quality and delivery [1]; the supplier's poor environmental record could harm Stoneycroft's reputation if customers find out [1]; hard to bring packing back in-house once the equipment is sold [1]. Judgement: the saving is modest, so outsource only if the specialist agrees quality and environmental standards in writing and the cash is not needed for stronger uses; otherwise the reputational risk could outweigh £6,000 [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation that reaches a reasoned conclusion, tied to the figures and to the conditions that would change it, tends to fare better than two unbalanced lists.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting units, or the percent sign on utilisation.
- Dividing capacity by output instead of output by capacity.
- Leaving the buffer out of the re-order level.
- Treating quality control and quality assurance as the same method.
- Writing about JIT as a benefit only, with no supplier risk.
- Giving a recommendation or justification with no judgement.
- Ignoring the numbers given in the case study.
- Offering two ways that are really the same idea.
