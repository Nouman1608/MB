This guide covers subtopics **6.2 to 6.11** of Topic 6, ICT
applications, in Cambridge IGCSE Information and Communication
Technology 0417 (2026–2028 syllabus). Each subtopic is a real-world use
of ICT, and Paper 1 questions almost always take the same shape: describe
how the system works, then give its advantages and disadvantages for a
named scenario. Subtopic 6.1, Communication, has its own [study
guide](/resources/igcse-ict-applications-communication/).

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE ICT (0417) — TOPIC 6 ICT APPLICATIONS, 6.2–6.11**

- **6.2 Modelling applications** — computer modelling, including personal finance, bridge and building design, flood water management, traffic management and weather forecasting; advantages and disadvantages of using computer modelling rather than humans
- **6.3 Computer controlled systems** — robotics in manufacture, production line control and autonomous vehicles; advantages and disadvantages of computer control rather than humans
- **6.4 School management systems** — managing learner registration and attendance; recording learner performance, including computer aided learning
- **6.5 Booking systems** — characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of online booking for the travel industry, concerts, cinemas and sporting events
- **6.6 Banking applications** — ATMs (withdrawing cash, depositing cash or cheques, checking a balance, mini statements, bill paying, money transfers); electronic funds transfer (EFT), credit/debit card transactions, cheques and internet banking
- **6.7 Computers in medicine** — patient records and pharmacy records; 3D printing of prosthetics, tissue engineering, artificial blood vessels and customised medicines
- **6.8 Expert systems** — uses, including mineral prospecting, car engine fault diagnosis, medical diagnosis, chess, financial planning, route scheduling for delivery vehicles and plant and animal identification; the components (user interface, inference engine, knowledge base, rules base, explanation system); how an expert system produces possible solutions
- **6.9 Computers in the retail industry** — POS terminals (automatic stock updating and reordering); EFTPOS terminals (card validity checks, chip and PIN, contactless cards, NFC payment, communication between the shop's computer and the bank's computer); internet shopping
- **6.10 Recognition systems** — OMR, OCR (including ANPR), RFID, NFC and biometric recognition
- **6.11 Satellite systems** — GPS, satellite navigation, GIS and media communication systems (satellite television and satellite phones)

## What is computer modelling used for? (6.2)

A **computer model** is a mathematical representation of a real system,
usually built from formulas and rules in a spreadsheet or specialist
software. Changing the inputs and asking "what if?" shows what would
happen without trying it in real life.

| Application | What is modelled |
|---|---|
| Personal finance | Income, spending and savings over time; the effect of a change in interest rate |
| Bridge and building design | Loads, wind and earthquakes on a structure before it is built |
| Flood water management | Rainfall, river levels and flood defences, to plan barriers and warnings |
| Traffic management | Vehicle flow at junctions, to test light timings or new road layouts |
| Weather forecasting | Data from sensors and satellites run through models to predict future weather |

**Advantages over humans or real tests:** cheaper than building real
prototypes; safer, because dangerous situations (a bridge collapse, a
flood) can be tested; faster, because many scenarios run in minutes;
and variables can be changed easily. **Disadvantages:** a model is only
as accurate as its data and formulas; real-life conditions may not be
fully represented; specialist software and expertise can be expensive.

## How are computer controlled systems used? (6.3)

In **robotics in manufacture**, robots weld, paint and assemble products.
**Production line control** uses sensors and computers to move items
along the line, check them and stop the line if a fault is detected.
**Autonomous vehicles** use sensors, cameras and GPS so a computer can
steer, brake and accelerate without a driver.

**Advantages over humans:** work continuously without breaks; consistent
quality and precision; can work in dangerous environments; lower running
costs over time. **Disadvantages:** high initial cost; expensive to
maintain and reprogram; cannot easily cope with unusual situations;
fewer jobs for manual workers.

## What do school management systems do? (6.4)

**Registration and attendance** can be recorded electronically — for
example by staff on a computer, with swipe or RFID cards, or with
biometrics such as fingerprints. This gives accurate, up-to-date records,
lets the school spot patterns of absence quickly and know who is on site
in an emergency.

**Learner performance** is recorded as marks and grades that can be
analysed to produce reports for parents and identify learners needing
help. **Computer aided learning** uses software to deliver lessons and
tests, giving instant feedback and letting learners work at their own
pace — but it needs devices and cannot fully replace a teacher's
support.

## How do online booking systems work? (6.5)

Online booking is used in the **travel industry** (flights, hotels),
for **concerts**, **cinemas** and **sporting events**. The user searches
for availability, chooses seats or dates, enters personal and payment
details, and receives an electronic ticket or confirmation. The system
updates the database immediately, so the same seat cannot be sold twice.

**Advantages:** book at any time from anywhere; see availability and
choose seats; often cheaper with special offers; instant confirmation
and e-tickets. **Disadvantages:** needs an internet connection and a
device; risk of fraud or hacking of personal data; booking fees; less
personal than speaking to an agent; a mistake may be hard to correct.

## Banking applications (6.6)

An **ATM** lets a customer withdraw cash, deposit cash or cheques, check
a balance, print a mini statement, pay bills and transfer money. The
card is inserted, the PIN is checked, and the ATM communicates with the
bank's computer to check the account before the transaction is
completed. ATMs are available at all hours and in many places, but
can be targets for card skimming and may charge fees.

**Electronic funds transfer (EFT)** moves money between accounts
electronically, for example when wages are paid. **Credit and debit card
transactions** take money from the account (debit) or lend it (credit).
**Cheques** are a slower paper method still used by some customers.
**Internet banking** lets customers manage accounts online at any time,
saving travel, but needs internet access and carries risks such as
phishing.

## Computers in medicine (6.7)

**Patient records** store personal details, medical history, test
results and treatments in a database so that authorised staff can see
them quickly. **Pharmacy records** store prescriptions and drug details,
helping to check for allergies and interactions and manage stock.

**3D printers** are used to print **prosthetics** shaped to the
individual, in **tissue engineering** (building layers of living cells),
to make **artificial blood vessels**, and to produce **customised
medicines** designed for one patient.

## How does an expert system work? (6.8)

An **expert system** imitates the knowledge and decision-making of a
human expert. Named uses include mineral prospecting, car engine fault
diagnosis, medical diagnosis, chess games, financial planning, route
scheduling for delivery vehicles and plant and animal identification.

| Component | Role |
|---|---|
| User interface | Asks the user questions and shows results |
| Knowledge base | Stores the facts about the subject |
| Rules base | Stores the rules (IF ... THEN ...) linking facts |
| Inference engine | Applies the rules to the user's answers and the knowledge base to reach conclusions |
| Explanation system | Explains how the conclusion was reached |

**Producing a solution:** the interface asks questions (often yes/no);
the inference engine searches the knowledge base using the rules; the
system suggests possible solutions, often with a probability or
confidence; the explanation system shows the reasoning.

## Computers in the retail industry (6.9)

A **POS terminal** reads each item's barcode, looks up its price and
description in the stock file, and **updates the stock level
automatically**. When the stock falls to its reorder level, the system
can **order new stock automatically** from the supplier.

An **EFTPOS terminal** handles payment by card. It checks the card is
valid (for example, not expired or stolen); the customer uses **chip and
PIN**, taps a **contactless card**, or pays with a phone using **NFC**.
The shop's computer contacts the **bank's computer** to check that the
funds are available, the money is transferred, and the transaction is
authorised.

**Internet shopping** lets customers browse and buy at any time, compare
prices and have goods delivered. Drawbacks include not seeing goods
before buying, delivery costs and delays, and security risks to payment
data; for shops it means fewer staff and premises but more competition.

## Recognition systems (6.10)

| System | How it works | Uses |
|---|---|---|
| OMR | Reads marks in set positions on a form | School registers, multiple-choice exam papers, barcodes and QR codes |
| OCR | Converts images of text into editable text | Automated number plate recognition (ANPR) |
| RFID | Reads data from a tag using radio waves, without line of sight | Tracking stock, passports, automobiles, contactless payment |
| NFC | Very short-range wireless communication | Paying with a smartphone |
| Biometrics | Recognises a unique body feature | Face, iris, retina, finger, thumb, hand, voice |

**Typical advantages** are speed and fewer human errors; **typical
disadvantages** are cost, and errors when marks, text or features are
unclear. Biometrics cannot be forgotten or lost but raise privacy
concerns.

## Satellite systems (6.11)

**GPS** uses signals from several satellites to calculate a receiver's
position. **Satellite navigation** combines GPS with maps to give route
directions. A **GIS** captures and layers geographic data (roads,
rivers, population) for planning and analysis. **Media communication
systems** include **satellite television** and **satellite phones**,
which reach remote areas without cables.

**Advantages:** work almost anywhere; accurate positioning; route
planning saves time and fuel. **Disadvantages:** signal loss in tunnels
or among tall buildings; maps may be out of date; satellite equipment is
expensive; over-reliance on navigation.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a model as "the real thing" — always say it predicts or simulates.
- Listing advantages of a system without linking them to the scenario in the question.
- Mixing up the knowledge base (facts) and the rules base (rules) in an expert system.
- Confusing OMR (marks in set positions) with OCR (reading text).
- Saying a POS terminal "orders stock" without mentioning the reorder level check.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the difference between POS and EFTPOS?** A POS terminal
records the sale and updates stock; an EFTPOS terminal handles the
electronic card or phone payment and contacts the bank.

**Why is RFID used for stock tracking instead of barcodes?** RFID tags
can be read by radio without line of sight, and many can be read at
once, which makes tracking faster.

**What does the inference engine do?** It applies the rules in the
rules base to the facts in the knowledge base and the user's answers to
reach a possible solution.
