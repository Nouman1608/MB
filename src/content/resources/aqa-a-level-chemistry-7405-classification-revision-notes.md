These notes condense section **3.2.1.1 Classification** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Classification study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-classification/), and exam-style questions are in the [Classification practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-classification-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Periodicity revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-periodicity-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Classify an element as **s, p, d or f block** from its position in the Periodic Table.
- Link position to **proton number**.
- Move between **position, block and electron configuration**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Proton number | Number of protons in the nucleus; sets the order of the Periodic Table |
| Period | Horizontal row; same highest occupied shell |
| Group | Vertical column; same number of outer electrons |
| Block | Region of the table where the same type of sub-level is being filled |
| Highest-energy electron | The last electron added when the configuration is built up |

## The four blocks

| Block | Location | Sub-level filling | Width |
|---|---|---|---|
| s | Groups 1 and 2 | s | 2 |
| p | Groups 3(13) to 0(18) | p | 6 |
| d | Middle of the table | d | 10 |
| f | Rows set below the table | f | 14 |

The width is twice the number of orbitals: s has 1 orbital, p has 3, d has 5 and f has 7.

## Rules for finding the block

1. Build the configuration to the last electron.
2. Name the sub-level that the last electron enters.
3. That letter is the block.

| Element | Z | Last electron enters | Block |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sodium | 11 | 3s | s |
| Chlorine | 17 | 3p | p |
| Iron | 26 | 3d | d |
| Gallium | 31 | 4p | p |
| Selenium | 34 | 4p | p |
| Barium | 56 | 6s | s |

Gallium is p-block even though it has a full 3d¹⁰ sub-level, because the last electron goes into 4p. Iron is d-block even though its outer shell contains 4s².

## Reading the table

- Groups 1 and 2: s-block.
- Groups 3(13) to 0(18): p-block.
- Between Groups 2 and 3(13): d-block (period 4 has Sc to Zn).
- Below the table: f-block.
- Period number = highest occupied shell.
- Groups 1 and 2: group number = outer electrons. p-block: outer electrons = group number minus 10 (for groups numbered 13 to 18).

## Counting to find a block

Period lengths: 2, 8, 8, 18, 18, 32. Cumulative total after each: 2, 10, 18, 36, 54, 86. If Z lies just after a noble gas, it is s-block (two elements), and so on.

Example: Z = 56. Xenon is 54, so 55 and 56 fill 6s: s-block (barium).

## Special cases

| Case | Detail |
|---|---|
| Helium (1s²) | s-block by configuration, placed with the noble gases because its outer shell is full |
| Chromium | [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹ |
| Copper | [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s¹ |
| d-block ions | 4s electrons are lost before 3d: Fe²⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁶ and Fe³⁺ is [Ar] 3d⁵ |

## Why the table follows proton number

- The proton number sets the number of electrons in a neutral atom.
- The electron configuration sets the chemical properties.
- Ordering by relative atomic mass would put some elements in the wrong group, for example argon and potassium.

## Full worked examples

**Block of arsenic (Z = 33).** Fill the sub-levels in order: 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s² 3d¹⁰ 4p³. The last electron enters 4p, so arsenic is p-block. It has five outer electrons (4s² 4p³), so it is in Group 5 (15), period 4.

**Block of calcium (Z = 20).** 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 4s². The last electron enters 4s, so calcium is s-block, Group 2, period 4.

**Block of zinc (Z = 30).** [Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s². The last electron added in the build-up order goes into 3d (the 4s fills first, then 3d), so zinc is a d-block element, although it is not a transition metal.

## Period and group from the configuration

| Configuration (outer) | Period | Group | Block |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3s² | 3 | 2 | s |
| 3s² 3p⁴ | 3 | 6 (16) | p |
| 4s¹ | 4 | 1 | s |
| 3d⁵ 4s² | 4 | d-block (manganese) | d |
| 4s² 4p⁵ | 4 | 7 (17) | p |

The period is the number of the highest occupied shell. The group follows from the number of outer electrons for s- and p-block elements.

## Why the f-block sits below the table

The f-block elements (lanthanoids and actinoids) fill the 4f and 5f sub-levels. Placing them in the main body would make the table very wide, so they are drawn below, but they belong between the s-block and the d-block of the later periods.

## Exam traps

- Naming the block from the outer shell, not from the last sub-level.
- Iron called s-block, gallium called d-block.
- Counting 3d electrons as outer electrons for the group number.
- "Elements are in order of relative atomic mass."
- Helium given as p-block.
- Removing 3d electrons first when forming ions.
- Mixing up period and group.
- Writing a configuration for the ion when asked for the atom.

## Self-test

1. What decides the order of elements in the Periodic Table?
2. What is meant by the s-block?
3. State the block of magnesium.
4. State the block of arsenic (Z = 33).
5. State the block of copper.
6. What is the configuration of chromium?
7. How many elements are in the d-block of period 4?
8. Why is gallium a p-block element?
9. Give the configuration of Fe³⁺.
10. How many electrons can a p sub-level hold?
11. Which group is the element with the configuration 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p³ in?
12. Why is helium placed in Group 0 although it is an s-block element?

### Answers

1. Proton number.
2. Elements whose highest-energy electron is in an s sub-level.
3. s-block.
4. p-block ([Ar] 3d¹⁰ 4s² 4p³).
5. d-block.
6. [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s¹.
7. Ten.
8. Its last electron enters the 4p sub-level.
9. [Ar] 3d⁵.
10. Six.
11. Group 5 (15) (five outer electrons).
12. It has a full outer shell and noble gas properties.
