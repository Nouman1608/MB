## Section A: Modern Drama (30 marks)

**1.** Choose **one** character from your studied modern drama text who experiences a significant change
across the play. Explore how the writer presents this change. **[30]**

## Section B: Literary Heritage Texts (30 marks)

**2.** Explore how your studied literary heritage text presents the relationship between an individual
and the society around them. In your answer, refer to the significance of the text's historical or
social context. **[30]**

---

## Model approach: Section A

A strong response to a character-change question (like Q1) is built around identifying **two or three
distinct stages** of the character's development, each supported by specific reference to language, form
or structure, rather than a single broad claim that the character "changes."

```
Stage 1 (early in the play):  the character is presented as [confident /
                               naive / powerless -- specific to the text],
                               shown through [a specific line, stage
                               direction, or interaction with another
                               character]
Stage 2 (a turning point):    a specific event or revelation shifts the
                               character's position, marked by a change
                               in how the writer presents their dialogue
                               or behaviour
Stage 3 (by the play's end):  the character has moved to a new position,
                               and the writer signals this through
                               [changed language register, staging,
                               relationships with other characters]
```

Because Section A is assessed against AO1 (informed personal response) and AO2 (analysis of language,
form and structure), each stage in this structure should combine a clear personal interpretation of what
the change means with specific textual evidence of how the writer achieves it — description of plot
events alone, without analysis of the writer's choices, will not access AO2's marks.

## Model approach: Section B

Because Section B additionally assesses AO4 (significance of contextual factors), a strong response to
Q2 links the individual-and-society theme directly to the text's historical or social setting, not just
to the plot itself.

```
AO1/AO2 only:   "The protagonist is constrained by the expectations of
                those around them, shown through the other characters'
                disapproving dialogue."
AO1/AO2 + AO4:  "The protagonist's constraint reflects the rigid social
                expectations of the period in which the text is set,
                when individual choice was far more limited by class,
                gender or convention than in a modern context -- the
                writer uses the individual's struggle to critique,
                rather than simply depict, those wider social
                structures."
```

The second version earns AO4 credit specifically by connecting textual evidence to the world the text was
written in and set in, rather than treating the individual's situation as if it existed without a
historical or social backdrop.

## Exam technique for this component

Because the exam is open-book, the priority in preparation is not memorising quotations word-for-word but
building the ability to locate relevant passages quickly under time pressure — practise navigating a copy
of your set texts at speed, noting roughly where key scenes or turning points fall, so retrieval during
the exam itself is fast rather than a first attempt. For Section B specifically, build genuine contextual
knowledge of your literary heritage text's period alongside close reading of the text itself, since AO4
credit depends on connecting the two rather than analysing language in isolation. With two essay
questions to answer across 1 hour 30 minutes, practise full-length timed responses to one drama and one
literary heritage question in a single sitting, not just individually, so pacing reflects the demands of
the real exam rather than each question type revised separately.

## Choosing between two essay-question options

Each section offers a choice of two essay questions on the studied text, and briefly weighing both
before committing is worth the small time investment it costs. A quick self-check for each option: which
question allows me to draw on the textual passages and evidence I know most confidently, and which
question's focus (character, theme, technique) plays more directly to material I have already prepared
in depth? Committing to the first option read, without this brief comparison, risks choosing the
question that happens to appear first rather than the one that genuinely suits the candidate's prepared
knowledge best — a small planning step that can meaningfully affect the final mark.

## Revising literary heritage context efficiently

Because Section B's AO4 credit depends on contextual knowledge, build a short, focused set of contextual
facts for the studied literary heritage text — the period it was written in, the social or historical
circumstances it responds to, and how those circumstances relate to the text's central themes — rather
than accumulating broad, unfocused historical knowledge that is difficult to deploy precisely in a timed
essay. A contextual point is only useful in an essay if it can be connected directly to specific textual
evidence, as demonstrated in the model commentary above, so practising this connect-context-to-evidence
skill directly, rather than revising context and text separately, is the more efficient use of revision
time.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing plot events in Section A without analysing the specific language, form or structural choices the writer makes.
- Writing a Section B answer that is strong on textual analysis but never engages with historical or social context, missing AO4 credit.
- Over-relying on memorised quotations rather than the skill of locating and analysing passages in the open-book exam.
- Running out of time on Section B because Section A, often the more comfortable question, was allowed to overrun.
