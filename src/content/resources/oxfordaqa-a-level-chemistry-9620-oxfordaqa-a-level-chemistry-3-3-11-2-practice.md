This set practises **3.3.11.2 Base properties** of amines from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [amine base properties study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-2/) or the [amine base properties revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-11-2-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0 and N = 14.0.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Which list shows the compounds in order of decreasing base strength?

A: phenylamine > ammonia > ethylamine

B: ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine

C: ammonia > ethylamine > phenylamine

D: ethylamine > phenylamine > ammonia

**[1]**

**2.** Propan-2-amine, (CH₃)₂CHNH₂, dissolves in water.

**(a)** Write an equation for its reaction with water. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why propan-2-amine is described as a weak base. **[1]**

**3.** Phenylamine reacts with dilute sulfuric acid. Give the name and formula of the salt formed. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why an aqueous solution of methylamine turns red litmus paper blue. **[1]**

**5.** Phenylamine is only slightly soluble in water, but it dissolves readily in dilute hydrochloric acid. Explain this difference. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Explain why propylamine is a stronger base than ammonia, and why ammonia is a stronger base than phenylamine. **[5]**

**7.** A 0.584 g sample of a primary aliphatic amine, RNH₂, is dissolved in water and titrated with 0.250 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid. The titre is 32.0 cm³.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction, using RNH₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Mr of the amine. **[2]**

**(c)** Deduce the molecular formula of the amine. **[1]**

**(d)** Draw two possible structures for the amine. **[2]**

**(e)** Name a suitable indicator for this titration and explain your choice. **[1]**

**8.** 25.0 cm³ of an aqueous solution of methylamine reacts exactly with 18.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sulfuric acid.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of methylamine in mol dm⁻³. **[2]**

**9.** Put pentylamine, CH₃(CH₂)₄NH₂, ammonia and 4-methylphenylamine, CH₃C₆H₄NH₂ (NH₂ bonded to the ring), in order of decreasing base strength. Explain the position of 4-methylphenylamine. **[3]**

**10.** A sample of phenylamine is contaminated with nitrobenzene, which is not basic. Neither compound dissolves well in water. Describe how hydrochloric acid and sodium hydroxide could be used to recover phenylamine from the mixture. Include equations. **[4]**

**11.** Propylamine is mixed with ethanoic acid. Name the type of reaction and give the formula of the product. **[2]**

**12.** Solutions of sodium hydroxide, ethylamine, ammonia and phenylamine all have a concentration of 0.10 mol dm⁻³. Put them in order of decreasing pH and give a reason for the position of sodium hydroxide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]

**2. (a)** (CH₃)₂CHNH₂ + H₂O ⇌ (CH₃)₂CHNH₃⁺ + OH⁻ [1]

**(b)** Only a small proportion of the molecules accept a proton from water; the equilibrium lies well to the left [1]

**3.** Phenylammonium sulfate [1]; (C₆H₅NH₃⁺)₂SO₄²⁻ [1]

**4.** The lone pair on N accepts H⁺ from water, producing OH⁻ ions, so the solution is alkaline [1]

**5.** Phenylamine reacts with HCl, accepting a proton to form phenylammonium chloride, C₆H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]; this is an ionic salt, which is much more soluble in water than the neutral amine [1]

**6.** A base accepts H⁺ using the lone pair on nitrogen; the more available the lone pair, the stronger the base [1]; the propyl group releases electron density towards N (positive inductive effect) [1]; so the lone pair in propylamine is more available than in ammonia [1]; in phenylamine the lone pair on N overlaps with (is delocalised into) the ring's delocalised electrons [1]; so it is less available than in ammonia [1]

*Examiner insight:* five marks needs five separate ideas; stating the order twice in different words gains nothing.

**7. (a)** RNH₂ + HCl → RNH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]

**(b)** n(HCl) = 0.250 × 32.0 ÷ 1000 = 0.00800 mol = n(amine) [1]; Mr = 0.584 ÷ 0.00800 = **73.0** [1]

**(c)** C₄H₁₁N [1]

**(d)** Any two of CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂, CH₃CH₂CH(NH₂)CH₃, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂NH₂, (CH₃)₃CNH₂ [2]

**(e)** Methyl orange, because a weak base with a strong acid has its equivalence point below pH 7 [1]

**8. (a)** 2CH₃NH₂ + H₂SO₄ → (CH₃NH₃⁺)₂SO₄²⁻ [1]

**(b)** n(H₂SO₄) = 0.0500 × 18.0 ÷ 1000 = 9.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol, so n(CH₃NH₂) = 1.80 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; concentration = 1.80 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0720 mol dm⁻³** [1]

*Examiner insight:* using a 1 : 1 ratio gives 0.0360 mol dm⁻³; the equation in (a) is there to stop that slip.

**9.** Pentylamine > ammonia > 4-methylphenylamine [1]; in 4-methylphenylamine the N is bonded directly to the benzene ring, so its lone pair is delocalised into the ring [1]; the lone pair is less available to accept a proton than in ammonia [1]

**10.** Add dilute hydrochloric acid and shake; phenylamine reacts and dissolves as its salt, nitrobenzene does not: C₆H₅NH₂ + HCl → C₆H₅NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]; separate the aqueous layer from the nitrobenzene (separating funnel) [1]; add excess sodium hydroxide to the aqueous layer to release phenylamine [1]; C₆H₅NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ → C₆H₅NH₂ + H₂O [1]

**11.** Acid–base (neutralisation) [1]; CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺ CH₃COO⁻, propylammonium ethanoate [1]

**12.** Sodium hydroxide > ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine [1]; sodium hydroxide is a strong base and is fully dissociated, so it gives the highest OH⁻ concentration [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining base strength without the words "lone pair" and "available".
- Treating a benzene ring anywhere in the molecule as making the amine aromatic.
- Writing RNH₄⁺ instead of RNH₃⁺.
- Using 1 : 1 for sulfuric acid.
- Choosing phenolphthalein for a weak base with a strong acid.
