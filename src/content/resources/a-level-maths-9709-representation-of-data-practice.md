## Questions

**1.** A café records the waiting time, to the nearest second, of 250 customers.

**(a)** State, with a reason, whether a stem-and-leaf diagram or a histogram is more suitable. **[1]**
**(b)** Give one advantage of a box-and-whisker plot over a histogram when comparing these times with those of a second café. **[1]**
**(c)** Give one advantage of a stem-and-leaf diagram over a box-and-whisker plot. **[1]**

**2.** The times, in seconds, taken by two groups of 15 people to solve a puzzle are shown.

```
        Group P |   | Group Q
          7 4 1 | 2 |
    8 6 5 3 2 0 | 3 | 0 7
        7 4 3 1 | 4 | 0 1 2 3 5 6 7 8 9
            8 2 | 5 | 1 3 8
                | 6 | 4
Key: 4 | 3 | 5 means 34 seconds for Group P and 35 seconds for Group Q
```

**(a)** Find the median and the interquartile range for each group. Take each quartile as the median of one half of the data, excluding the overall median. **[4]**
**(b)** Make two comparisons between the times of the two groups. **[2]**
**(c)** State the values you would plot to draw box-and-whisker plots for both groups on one diagram. **[2]**

**3.** The masses, in grams, of 60 apples are summarised in a box-and-whisker plot. The lowest value is 96, the lower quartile is 112, the median is 120, the upper quartile is 141 and the highest value is 178.

**(a)** Find the range and the interquartile range. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the skewness of the distribution, giving a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Estimate the number of apples with mass greater than 141 g. **[1]**
**(d)** State one piece of information lost by showing the data only as a box-and-whisker plot. **[1]**

**4.** In a histogram of the heights of some plants, the bar for the class 20 ≤ h < 25 cm is 3 cm wide and 7.2 cm high. It represents 18 plants. The bar for the class 25 ≤ h < 40 cm is 4.8 cm high.

**(a)** Find the width of the bar for 25 ≤ h < 40. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the number of plants in the class 25 ≤ h < 40. **[3]**

**5.** For 20 values, Σx = 642 and Σx² = 21 238. It was then found that one value had been recorded as 13 when it should have been 31. Find the correct mean and standard deviation. **[5]**

**6.** For 25 values of x, Σ(x − a) = −40 and Σ(x − a)² = 464, where a is a constant. The mean of x is 46.4.

**(a)** Find the value of a. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the standard deviation of x. **[2]**
**(c)** Find Σx². **[2]**

**7.** 18 girls have heights with mean 152 cm and standard deviation 5 cm; 12 boys have mean 160 cm and standard deviation 6 cm. Find the mean and standard deviation of the heights of all 30 children. **[6]**

**8.** The numbers of minutes late of nine trains are

0, 0, 0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 47

**(a)** Find the mean, the median and the mode. **[3]**
**(b)** State, with a reason, which average best describes a typical train. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the standard deviation. **[2]**
**(d)** State, with a reason, whether the interquartile range or the standard deviation is the better measure of spread for these data. **[1]**

**9.** The times, t seconds, taken to answer 100 telephone calls are shown.

| Time (s) | 0 ≤ t < 20 | 20 ≤ t < 30 | 30 ≤ t < 40 | 40 ≤ t < 60 | 60 ≤ t < 100 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frequency | 12 | 21 | 27 | 24 | 16 |

**(a)** Calculate the frequency densities you would use to draw a histogram. **[2]**
**(b)** Write down the points you would plot for a cumulative frequency graph. **[1]**
**(c)** Assume the graph is drawn by joining the points with straight lines. Estimate the median and the interquartile range. **[4]**
**(d)** Estimate the number of calls that took more than 50 seconds. **[2]**
**(e)** Estimate the 90th percentile. **[1]**

**10.** Use the table in Question 9.

**(a)** Calculate estimates of the mean and the standard deviation of the times. **[5]**
**(b)** Explain why your answers to (a) are only estimates. **[1]**
**(c)** Compare your mean with the median from Question 9(c), and state what this suggests about the distribution. **[2]**

**11.** A data set of 10 values has mean 14 and standard deviation 3. Two more values, 22 and k, are added. The mean of all 12 values is 15.

**(a)** Find k. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the standard deviation of all 12 values. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** A histogram, because 250 values are too many to list as leaves [1].
**(b)** Two box plots on one scale show medians, quartiles and spread side by side [1].
**(c)** It keeps every raw value, so you can still find the mode or the mean [1].
*Examiner insight:* "it is clearer" earns nothing; each reason must fit the situation.

**2. (a)** n = 15: the median is the 8th value; each half has 7 values, so the quartiles are the 4th and 12th [1]. P: median **36 s** [1], Q₁ = 30, Q₃ = 44, IQR **14 s**. Q: median **46 s** [1], Q₁ = 41, Q₃ = 51, IQR **10 s** [1].
**(b)** Group Q took longer on average (median 46 > 36) [1]. Group P's times were more spread out (IQR 14 > 10) [1].
**(c)** One labelled linear scale from 20 to 65 seconds [1]. P: 21, 30, 36, 44, 58. Q: 30, 41, 46, 51, 64 [1].
*Examiner insight:* in (b) one comment must be on centre and one on spread, each in context; two comments on centre score one mark only.

**3. (a)** Range = 178 − 96 = **82 g** [1]. IQR = 141 − 112 = **29 g** [1].
**(b)** Q₃ − median = 21 is larger than median − Q₁ = 8 [1], so the distribution has **positive skew** [1].
**(c)** A quarter of the apples lie above Q₃: 60 ÷ 4 = **15** [1].
**(d)** The individual masses, so the mode or exact mean cannot be found [1].
*Examiner insight:* a direction of skew without comparing the two halves of the box loses the reason mark.

**4. (a)** 5 cm of height → 3 cm of width, so 15 cm → **9 cm** [1].
**(b)** Area of the first bar = 3 × 7.2 = 21.6 cm² for 18 plants, so 1 plant = 1.2 cm² [1]. Area of the second bar = 9 × 4.8 = 43.2 cm² [1]. 43.2 ÷ 1.2 = **36 plants** [1].
*Examiner insight:* using bar heights alone (4.8 ÷ 7.2 × 18 = 12) scores zero; frequency is proportional to area.

**5.** Correct Σx = 642 − 13 + 31 = 660 [1]. Correct Σx² = 21 238 − 169 + 961 = 22 030 [1]. Mean = 660 ÷ 20 = **33** [1]. Variance = 22 030 ÷ 20 − 33² = 1101.5 − 1089 = 12.5 [1]. Standard deviation = √12.5 = **3.54** (3 s.f.) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Σx² must change by 31² − 13², not by 18²; that error loses the last three marks.

**6. (a)** Mean of (x − a) = −40 ÷ 25 = −1.6 [1], so 46.4 = a − 1.6 and **a = 48** [1].
**(b)** Variance = 464 ÷ 25 − (−1.6)² = 18.56 − 2.56 = 16 [1], so standard deviation = **4** [1].
**(c)** Σx² = 25 × (16 + 46.4²) [1] = 25 × 2168.96 = **54 224** [1].
*Examiner insight:* coding by subtracting a does not change the standard deviation, so do not add a back on in (b).

**7.** Girls: Σx = 18 × 152 = 2736. Boys: Σx = 12 × 160 = 1920 [1]. Girls: Σx² = 18 × (5² + 152²) = 416 322 [1]. Boys: Σx² = 12 × (6² + 160²) = 307 632 [1]. Combined mean = 4656 ÷ 30 = **155.2 cm** [1]. Variance = 723 954 ÷ 30 − 155.2² = 24 131.8 − 24 087.04 = 44.76 [1]. Standard deviation = **6.69 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].
*Examiner insight:* averaging the two standard deviations earns no method mark.

**8. (a)** Mean = 63 ÷ 9 = **7 minutes** [1]. Median (5th value) = **2 minutes** [1]. Mode = **0 minutes** [1].
**(b)** The median, because the mean is pulled up by the extreme value 47 [1].
**(c)** Σx² = 2275 [1]. Standard deviation = √(2275 ÷ 9 − 7²) = √203.78 = **14.3 minutes** [1].
**(d)** The IQR, because it is not affected by the extreme value 47 [1].
*Examiner insight:* the reason must name the extreme value; "the median is more accurate" earns nothing.

**9. (a)** Frequency density = frequency ÷ class width [1]: **0.6, 2.1, 2.7, 1.2, 0.4** [1].
**(b)** (0, 0), (20, 12), (30, 33), (40, 60), (60, 84), (100, 100), plotted at upper class boundaries [1].
**(c)** Median at cf 50: 30 + (17 ÷ 27) × 10 = **36.3 s** [1]. Q₁ at cf 25: 20 + (13 ÷ 21) × 10 = 26.2 [1]. Q₃ at cf 75: 40 + (15 ÷ 24) × 20 = 52.5 [1]. IQR = 52.5 − 26.2 = **26.3 s** [1].
**(d)** cf at 50 s = 60 + (10 ÷ 20) × 24 = 72 [1]. More than 50 s: 100 − 72 = **28 calls** [1].
**(e)** cf 90: 60 + (6 ÷ 16) × 40 = **75 s** [1].
*Examiner insight:* plotting at mid-points instead of upper class boundaries makes every later reading wrong and loses the accuracy marks.

**10. (a)** Mid-points 10, 25, 35, 50, 80 [1]. Σxf = 4070, so mean = 4070 ÷ 100 = **40.7 s** [1]. Σx²f = 209 800 [1]. Variance = 209 800 ÷ 100 − 40.7² = 2098 − 1656.49 = 441.51 [1]. Standard deviation = **21.0 s** (3 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** The exact times are unknown, so each value is replaced by its class mid-point [1].
**(c)** The mean (40.7 s) is greater than the median (36.3 s) [1], which suggests **positive skew** [1].
*Examiner insight:* using class widths or upper boundaries instead of mid-points loses the first mark and all accuracy marks.

**11. (a)** Original Σx = 10 × 14 = 140 [1]. New Σx = 12 × 15 = 180 [1]. k = 180 − 140 − 22 = **18** [1].
**(b)** Original Σx² = 10 × (3² + 14²) = 2050 [1]. New Σx² = 2050 + 22² + 18² = 2858 [1]. Standard deviation = √(2858 ÷ 12 − 15²) = √13.17 = **3.63** (3 s.f.) [1].
*Examiner insight:* using 10 × 14² as the original Σx² ignores the variance and loses the method mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Bar height used as frequency on a histogram with unequal class widths.
- Cumulative frequency plotted at class mid-points or lower boundaries.
- Left-hand leaves of a back-to-back diagram read in the wrong order.
- Skewness stated without comparing the two halves of the box.
- Comparisons made without context.
- Σx² not updated when a value is corrected.
- Two means averaged without weighting by the group sizes.
- Variance given instead of standard deviation.
