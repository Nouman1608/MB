These questions cover **3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3 (International AS content, Unit 1, drawn on by Units 2 and 5). Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹, L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ and T (K) = t (°C) + 273. Give answers to three significant figures. If you need a reminder of the method, see the [ideal gas equation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-3/) or the [ideal gas equation revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-1-2-3-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State the SI units of pressure, volume and temperature used in the ideal gas equation. **[1]**

**2.** Convert 350 cm³ to m³ and 120 kPa to Pa. **[1]**

**3.** Calculate the amount, in moles, of gas in a 2.50 dm³ container at 150 kPa and 320 K. **[2]**

**4.** A fixed amount of gas is kept at constant pressure. Which change doubles its volume?

A: Heating it from 100 °C to 200 °C

B: Heating it from 300 K to 600 K

C: Doubling the pressure at constant temperature

D: Removing half of the gas at constant temperature

**[1]**

**5.** Calculate the pressure, in kPa, of 0.0150 mol of gas in a 400 cm³ flask at 25 °C. **[2]**

**6.** 1.00 g of helium (Ar 4.0) occupies 5.00 dm³ at 101 kPa. Calculate the temperature of the gas in °C. **[3]**

**7.** Use the ideal gas equation to explain why one mole of hydrogen and one mole of carbon dioxide occupy the same volume at the same temperature and pressure. **[1]**

### Section B

**8.** A student vaporises 0.250 g of a volatile liquid. The vapour occupies 92.0 cm³ at 100 °C and 101 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of vapour. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the liquid. **[1]**

**(c)** The liquid is one of pentane (Mr 72.0), cyclohexane (Mr 84.0) and heptane (Mr 100.0). Identify it. **[1]**

**(d)** Some vapour escaped before the volume was measured. State and explain the effect on the calculated Mr. **[2]**

**9.** A 2.50 dm³ steel cylinder contains argon (Ar 39.9) at 500 kPa and 290 K.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of argon in the cylinder. **[3]**

**(b)** The cylinder is warmed to 350 K. Its volume does not change. Calculate the new pressure in kPa. **[2]**

**10.** This question is about gas density.

**(a)** By combining pV = nRT with n = m ÷ Mr, show that the density of a gas, m ÷ V, equals pMr ÷ RT. **[2]**

**(b)** A gas has a density of 1.25 g dm⁻³ at 273 K and 101 kPa. Calculate its Mr. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest the identity of the gas, given that it is a diatomic element. **[1]**

**11.** A 250 cm³ flask is pumped out until the pressure inside is 1.00 Pa at 298 K.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of gas left in the flask. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of molecules still in the flask. **[1]**

**12.** A student finds the Mr of the butane in a gas lighter. Gas is released under water into an inverted measuring cylinder until 100 cm³ has been collected. The lighter loses 0.235 g. The temperature is 20 °C and the pressure is 100 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the Mr of the gas from these results. **[3]**

**(b)** The Mr of butane, C₄H₁₀, is 58.0. Calculate the percentage difference between the experimental and true values. **[1]**

**(c)** The gas collected over water also contains water vapour. Explain the effect this has on the experimental Mr. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Pressure in Pa, volume in m³, temperature in K (all three needed) [1] **[1]**

**2.** 3.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³ and 1.20 × 10⁵ Pa (both needed) [1] **[1]**

**3.** n = pV ÷ RT = (150 000 × 2.50 × 10⁻³) ÷ (8.31 × 320) [1] = **0.141 mol** [1] **[2]**

**4.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Volume is proportional to absolute temperature; 100 °C to 200 °C is only 373 K to 473 K.

**5.** p = nRT ÷ V = (0.0150 × 8.31 × 298) ÷ (4.00 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = 9.29 × 10⁴ Pa = **92.9 kPa** [1] **[2]**

**6.** n = 1.00 ÷ 4.0 = 0.250 mol [1]; T = pV ÷ nR = (101 000 × 5.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (0.250 × 8.31) = 243 K [1]; 243 − 273 = **−30 °C** [1] **[3]**

**7.** pV = nRT contains no quantity that depends on the identity of the gas, so equal n at equal p and T gives equal V [1] **[1]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** n = (101 000 × 9.20 × 10⁻⁵) ÷ (8.31 × 373) [1] = **3.00 × 10⁻³ mol** [1]

**(b)** Mr = 0.250 ÷ 3.00 × 10⁻³ = **83.4** (accept 83.3) [1]

**(c)** **Cyclohexane** [1]

**(d)** The measured volume is too small, so the calculated n is too small [1]; Mr = m ÷ n is therefore too large [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Work through the chain: what changes first (V), then n, then Mr.

**9.**

**(a)** n = (500 000 × 2.50 × 10⁻³) ÷ (8.31 × 290) [1] = 0.519 mol [1]; mass = 0.519 × 39.9 = **20.7 g** [1]

**(b)** At constant n and V, p is proportional to T: p = 500 × 350 ÷ 290 [1] = **603 kPa** [1] **[5]**

**10.**

**(a)** Substituting n = m ÷ Mr gives pV = (m ÷ Mr)RT [1]; rearranging, m ÷ V = pMr ÷ RT [1]

**(b)** 1.25 g dm⁻³ = 1250 g m⁻³; Mr = (m ÷ V) × RT ÷ p = 1250 × 8.31 × 273 ÷ 101 000 [1] = **28.1** [1]

**(c)** **Nitrogen**, N₂ (Mr 28.0) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Density must be in g m⁻³ to match V in m³; using 1.25 directly gives an answer 1000 times too small.

**11.**

**(a)** n = (1.00 × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 298) [1] = **1.01 × 10⁻⁷ mol** [1]

**(b)** 1.01 × 10⁻⁷ × 6.02 × 10²³ = **6.08 × 10¹⁶** molecules [1] **[3]**

**12.**

**(a)** T = 293 K, V = 1.00 × 10⁻⁴ m³, p = 100 000 Pa [1]; n = (100 000 × 1.00 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 293) = 4.11 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; Mr = 0.235 ÷ 4.11 × 10⁻³ = **57.2** [1]

**(b)** (58.0 − 57.2) ÷ 58.0 × 100 = **1.4%** [1]

**(c)** The measured volume includes water vapour as well as butane, so the volume of butane is overestimated [1]; this makes n too large, so the calculated Mr is too small [1] **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving pressure in kPa or volume in cm³ or dm³.
- Using °C instead of K, especially when finding a temperature.
- Not converting the answer back to the unit asked for.
- Describing an experimental error without saying whether Mr goes up or down.
