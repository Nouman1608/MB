## Format at a glance

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Length | 2 hours |
| Total marks | 50 |
| Section A | Shakespeare, 25 marks, one question from a choice of set plays |
| Section B | Drama, 25 marks, one question from a choice of non-Shakespeare set texts |
| Question choice per text | Essay (a) or passage-based (b), responding to a printed extract |
| Availability | Full A Level route only — not available to AS-Level-only candidates |

## The one thing that makes Paper 3 different: AO5

Papers 1 and 2 (the AS Level papers) test **four** assessment
objectives: AO1 (knowledge and understanding), AO2 (analysis), AO3
(personal response) and AO4 (communication). Papers 3 and 4 add a
**fifth**:

**AO5 — Evaluation of opinion**: discuss and evaluate varying opinions
and interpretations of literary texts.

**AO3 vs AO5 — the confusion to avoid:** AO3 rewards *your own*
independent opinion. AO5 rewards discussing and evaluating *other
people's* opinions and interpretations (critics, alternative readings,
different performance traditions) alongside your own. A strong answer
needs both, not one instead of the other.

## Building an AO5 sentence — the pattern

1. Make an analytical point about language, form or structure (AO2).
2. State your own view of its effect (AO3), written clearly (AO4).
3. Add: "some critics/productions have instead read this as..." and
   briefly weigh that reading against your own (AO5).

Repeat this pattern two or three times across an essay rather than
saving all evaluative material for one closing paragraph — spreading
AO5 through the response scores more highly than bolting it on at the
end.

## 2026-series set texts

**Section A: Shakespeare** — one of *Hamlet* or *The Taming of the
Shrew* (Alexander Text edition).

**Section B: Drama** — one of *Sweat* (Lynn Nottage), *Long Day's
Journey Into Night* (Eugene O'Neill) or *Kongi's Harvest* (Wole
Soyinka).

*The Merchant of Venice*, *The Train Driver and Other Plays* (Athol
Fugard) and *An Experiment with an Air Pump* (Shelagh Stephenson) were
set for the 2024 series only and are not examined in 2026.

Set texts rotate by examination series — always confirm the current
list for the year being sat rather than assuming continuity.

## Paper 3 vs Paper 1's drama section

Both examine drama using the same close-reading and analytical skills.
The difference is AO5: Paper 1's drama section is assessed on AO1-AO4
only, so staying entirely within your own reading is complete there. A
Paper 3 Section B answer additionally needs awareness of how the play
has been interpreted differently by other readers or critics.

## Worked example: spotting a missing AO5

A student's essay analyses a soliloquy's use of rhetorical questions
(AO2), argues this makes the character seem genuinely conflicted
rather than villainous (AO3), and writes this in a clear, well-structured
paragraph (AO4) — but the paragraph stops there. This answer is
missing AO5, because it never engages with how anyone else has read the
same moment. Adding one sentence — "some productions have instead
played this soliloquy for comic unease, staging the hesitation as
self-serving rather than genuinely conflicted" — and briefly comparing
that reading to the student's own would satisfy AO5 without changing
the rest of the paragraph's argument.

## Why the AS/A Level split matters for revision planning

Because Papers 3 and 4 are only available to full A Level candidates,
and staged candidates typically sit them only in their second year
after completing Papers 1 and 2, revision for Paper 3 should not
assume that AS-Level habits transfer automatically. The close-reading
and analytical skills built for Papers 1 and 2 (AO1-AO4) remain
necessary, but candidates moving into Paper 3 revision for the first
time need to treat AO5 as a genuinely new skill to practise, not a
restatement of AO3 with different wording.

## Exam traps

- Treating AO5 as just another personal opinion, rather than genuine
  engagement with someone else's reading.
- Answering a passage-based (b) question in essay style, ignoring the
  extract actually printed on the paper.
- Assuming last year's set texts are still current without checking.
- Forgetting Papers 3 and 4 are full-A-Level-only and cannot be part
  of an AS-Level-only route.
- Analysing language and structure skilfully throughout an essay but
  never once referencing an alternative reading or performance.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long do I have for each Paper 3 answer?**
The paper lasts 2 hours for two 25-mark answers, so about an hour each,
including planning.

**Is AO5 really worth a fifth of the marks?**
Yes. On Paper 3 each of AO1 to AO5 carries 20% of the paper, so
evaluating other interpretations is not optional.

**Which texts should I revise?**
Use the list for the year you sit: the 2026 list above for 2026, and the
2027 list for June or November 2027.


## Self-test

1. How many marks and how much time is Paper 3 worth in total, and
   how are the marks split between sections?
2. Which assessment objective is unique to Papers 3 and 4?
3. What is the key difference between AO3 and AO5?
4. Name the two question types offered on each set text.
5. Why can an AS-Level-only candidate never sit Paper 3?

A quick way to check your own practice essays is to count how many times you have named an alternative reading, critic or production choice and weighed it against your own view — if the answer is zero, AO5 is missing regardless of how strong the AO1-AO4 material is.

**Answers:** 1. 50 marks, 2 hours; 25 marks for Section A (Shakespeare)
and 25 for Section B (Drama). 2. AO5 — Evaluation of opinion.
3. AO3 rewards your own independent opinion; AO5 rewards discussing
and evaluating other people's opinions and interpretations alongside
your own. 4. An essay question (a) and a passage-based question (b)
responding to a printed extract. 5. Because Paper 3 tests AO5, an
assessment objective the AS Level qualification does not assess at
all.
