This study guide goes deeper into subtopic **5.2 Area and perimeter** from Topic 5 Mensuration of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C5.2) and Extended (E5.2) statements are identical. The first explanations and examples are in the [units of measure, area and perimeter study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-units-of-measure-area-and-perimeter/); this page adds where the formulas come from, how to pick the right base and height, shapes on coordinate grids, equations from areas, and costing problems.

## What this subtopic covers

The syllabus statement is: carry out calculations involving the perimeter and area of a **rectangle, triangle, parallelogram and trapezium**. The notes add one line that matters a great deal: **except for the area of a triangle, formulas are not given**. The List of formulas on page 2 of every paper has the triangle (A = ½bh) but not the other three, so you must know them.

| Shape | Area | Perimeter |
|---|---|---|
| Rectangle, length l, width w | A = lw | P = 2l + 2w |
| Triangle, base b, perpendicular height h | A = ½bh (given) | sum of the three sides |
| Parallelogram, base b, perpendicular height h | A = bh | sum of the four sides |
| Trapezium, parallel sides a and b, perpendicular height h | A = ½(a + b)h | sum of the four sides |

## Where the formulas come from

Knowing why each formula works helps you remember it and spot when it is being misused.

- **Parallelogram.** Cut a right-angled triangle off one end and move it to the other end. The parallelogram becomes a rectangle with the same base and the same **perpendicular** height, so A = bh. The slanting side plays no part.
- **Triangle.** Two copies of the same triangle fit together to make a parallelogram with the same base and height. So the triangle is half of bh.
- **Trapezium.** A diagonal splits it into two triangles with the same height h, with bases a and b. Their areas are ½ah and ½bh, which add to ½(a + b)h. Another way to read the formula: the average of the parallel sides times the distance between them.

## Choosing the base and the height

The height must be **perpendicular** to the base you choose. Any side can be the base, as long as you use the height that goes with it.

### Worked example 1 (calculator): two base–height pairs

In triangle ABC, AB = 13 cm and BC = 14 cm. The perpendicular from A to BC has length 12 cm. Find the area of the triangle, and then the perpendicular distance from C to the line AB.

```
Using base BC and its height:
  area = ½ × 14 × 12 = 84 cm²
Using base AB, with unknown height h:
  ½ × 13 × h = 84
  h = 168 ÷ 13 = 12.92... = 12.9 cm  (3 s.f.)
```

The same area, two different base–height pairs. This idea, working backwards from a known area, appears often.

### Obtuse triangles

In a triangle with an obtuse angle, the perpendicular height from the top vertex can fall **outside** the triangle, onto the base extended. The formula still works with that height. Do not use the length of the slanting side instead.

## Shapes on a coordinate grid

When vertices are given as coordinates, look for sides that are horizontal or vertical: they make easy bases and heights.

### Worked example 2 (non-calculator)

(a) Find the area of the triangle with vertices (1, 1), (7, 1) and (4, 6).
(b) Find the area of the quadrilateral with vertices (0, 0), (10, 0), (7, 4) and (2, 4).

```
(a) base from (1, 1) to (7, 1): horizontal, length 7 − 1 = 6
    height: vertical distance from y = 1 up to y = 6, so 5
    area = ½ × 6 × 5 = 15 square units

(b) the sides from (0, 0) to (10, 0) and from (2, 4) to (7, 4) are both
    horizontal, so they are parallel: the shape is a trapezium
    a = 10, b = 7 − 2 = 5, h = 4 − 0 = 4
    area = ½ × (10 + 5) × 4 = 30 square units
```

## Using area to form an equation

Questions often give the area and ask for a length. Write the formula, substitute, and solve.

### Worked example 3 (non-calculator)

A trapezium has parallel sides x cm and (x + 6) cm, and perpendicular height 8 cm. Its area is 104 cm². Find x.

```
½ × (x + x + 6) × 8 = 104
4(2x + 6) = 104
2x + 6 = 26
x = 10
```

Check: ½ × (10 + 16) × 8 = 104.

### Worked example 4 (Extended, non-calculator)

A rectangle has length (x + 5) cm and width x cm. Its area is 84 cm². Find its perimeter.

```
x(x + 5) = 84
x² + 5x − 84 = 0
(x + 12)(x − 7) = 0
x = 7  (x = −12 is rejected: a length cannot be negative)
length = 12 cm, width = 7 cm
perimeter = 2 × 12 + 2 × 7 = 38 cm
```

Solving the quadratic equation is Extended algebra; the area and perimeter work is the same on both tiers. Always say why you reject a negative root.

### Worked example 5 (non-calculator): equal areas

A triangle with base 15 cm has the same area as a parallelogram with base 9 cm and perpendicular height 5 cm. Find the perpendicular height of the triangle.

```
parallelogram: 9 × 5 = 45 cm²
triangle: ½ × 15 × h = 45
7.5h = 45
h = 6 cm
```

## Perimeter in context

Perimeter questions are often about fencing, edging or framing, and can include a cost or a side that is not counted.

### Worked example 6 (calculator)

A rectangular garden measures 14.5 m by 9.2 m. One of the 14.5 m sides is the wall of a house. The other three sides are fenced. Fencing costs $12.40 per metre. Find the total cost.

```
length of fence = 14.5 + 9.2 + 9.2 = 32.9 m
cost = 32.9 × 12.40 = $407.96
```

Money is given to the nearest cent, so write $407.96 in full. Reading the question for the side that is **not** fenced is the step most often missed.

## Exam technique

- **"Calculate the area"**: write the formula, then the substitution, then the answer with square units.
- **"Show that the area is …"**: every line must be visible, ending with the given value; the answer is already known, so the marks are for the method.
- **"Find the perimeter"**: add every outside edge once, in linear units. Sketch the shape and tick each side as you add it.
- Calculator papers: 3 significant figures unless told otherwise, or exact if the answer is exact.

## Common mistakes

- Multiplying base by slant side for a parallelogram, or base by sloping side for a triangle.
- Forgetting the ½ in the triangle or trapezium formula.
- Adding the two non-parallel sides of a trapezium in the formula instead of the parallel sides.
- Mixing units, such as a base in m and a height in cm.
- Giving an area in cm instead of cm², or a perimeter in cm².
- Counting a side in the perimeter that the question says is a wall or is shared.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the trapezium formula given in the 0580 exam?**
No. Only the area of a triangle is in the List of formulas; the syllabus notes say the formulas for the rectangle, parallelogram and trapezium are not given, so learn A = ½(a + b)h.

**Why is the area of a parallelogram base times height?**
Cutting a triangle off one end and moving it to the other turns the parallelogram into a rectangle with the same base and the same perpendicular height.

**Can any side be the base of a triangle?**
Yes, provided you use the perpendicular height that goes with that side. Each choice of base gives the same area.
