## Section A

**1.** Complete the sentences.
"A galaxy is a large collection of billions of ______. The universe is a large collection of billions of ______. Our solar system is in the ______ galaxy." **[3]**

**2.** Name the force that keeps a comet in orbit around the Sun. **[1]**

**3.** Planet X has the same radius as Earth but twice Earth's mass. State and explain how the gravitational field strength at its surface compares with Earth's. **[2]**

**4.** An astronaut's mass is the same on Earth and on the Moon, but her weight is not. Explain why. **[3]**

**5.** Two satellites, A and B, orbit Earth in circular orbits. A's orbital radius is larger than B's. State which satellite has the longer time period and explain why. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A comet orbits the Sun. Point A on its orbit is the closest point to the Sun and point B is the furthest.

**(a)** State the point at which the comet moves fastest. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain, in terms of energy, why the comet's speed changes as it moves from B to A. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe two differences between the comet's orbit and Earth's orbit. **[2]**

**7.** Jupiter orbits the Sun at a radius of 7.8 × 10¹¹ m with a time period of 4330 days.

**(a)** Calculate Jupiter's orbital speed. **[3]**

**(b)** Earth's orbital speed is 3.0 × 10⁴ m/s. Explain why Jupiter's orbital speed is lower. **[2]**

**8.** A communications satellite orbits Earth with an orbital speed of 3.07 × 10³ m/s at a radius of 4.2 × 10⁷ m. Calculate its time period in hours. **[3]**

**9.** A space station moves at a constant speed in a circular orbit around Earth.

**(a)** Explain why the station is accelerating. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction of its acceleration. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe the motion of the station if Earth's gravitational force on it were suddenly removed. **[1]**

**10.** A moon of Saturn has an orbital speed of 5.6 km/s and a time period of 3.8 days. Calculate its orbital radius. **[3]**

**11.** A student writes: "Satellites stay in orbit because there is no gravity in space, so nothing pulls them down." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**12.** Describe the differences in the orbits of comets, moons and planets. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** stars [1]; galaxies [1]; Milky Way [1].

**2.** Gravity (the gravitational force of the Sun) [1].

**3.** g is larger on Planet X (about twice as large) [1], because g depends on the mass of the planet and Planet X has more mass at the same distance from its centre [1].

**4.** Mass is the amount of matter, so it does not change with location [1]. Weight is the gravitational force on her, W = m × g [1]; g on the Moon is much smaller than on Earth, so her weight is smaller there [1].

**5.** Satellite A [1]: further from Earth the gravitational field is weaker, so A moves more slowly and also has a longer path to travel round its orbit [1].

**6. (a)** Point A [1].
**(b)** As the comet moves towards the Sun, gravity does work on it [1], so gravitational potential energy is transferred to kinetic energy and its speed increases [1].
**(c)** Any two: the comet's orbit is highly elliptical while Earth's is nearly circular [1]; the comet's distance from the Sun varies greatly while Earth's is nearly constant [1]; the comet's speed changes greatly while Earth's is nearly constant [1]; maximum 2.

**7. (a)** T = 4330 × 86 400 = 3.74 × 10⁸ s [1]; v = 2π × 7.8 × 10¹¹ / 3.74 × 10⁸ [1] = **1.3 × 10⁴ m/s** [1].
**(b)** Jupiter is much further from the Sun than Earth [1], where the Sun's gravitational field is weaker, so a lower speed is needed (and occurs) for its orbit [1].

**8.** T = 2πr / v = 2π × 4.2 × 10⁷ / 3.07 × 10³ [1] = 8.6 × 10⁴ s [1]; 8.6 × 10⁴ / 3600 = **24 h** (23.9 h) [1].

**9. (a)** Its direction of motion is constantly changing [1], so its velocity is changing, which is acceleration [1].
**(b)** Towards the centre of Earth [1].
**(c)** It would travel in a straight line at constant speed, in the direction it was moving at that moment [1].

**10.** v = 5.6 × 10³ m/s and T = 3.8 × 86 400 = 3.28 × 10⁵ s [1]; r = vT / 2π = 5.6 × 10³ × 3.28 × 10⁵ / 2π [1] = **2.9 × 10⁸ m** [1].

**11.** The statement is wrong [1]. Earth's gravity still acts on satellites; g is smaller at their height but not zero [1]. Gravity is the force towards Earth's centre that keeps changing the satellite's direction, so it moves in a circle; without gravity it would move off in a straight line [1].

**12.** Planets orbit the Sun and moons orbit planets, while comets orbit the Sun [1]. Planet and moon orbits are nearly circular [1], whereas comet orbits are highly elliptical [1]. So the distance and speed of a comet change greatly around its orbit (fastest near the Sun), while for planets and moons they stay nearly constant [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "solar systems" instead of "stars" for what a galaxy is made of (Question 1).
- Saying mass changes on the Moon (Question 4).
- Forgetting to convert days or hours into seconds (Questions 7, 8 and 10).
- Explaining the comet's speed with no reference to energy transfer.
- Accepting "no gravity in space" in Question 11.
