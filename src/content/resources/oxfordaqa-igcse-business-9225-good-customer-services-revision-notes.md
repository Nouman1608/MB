Condensed recall for section 3.3.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-good-customer-services/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Methods of good service: **product knowledge**, **customer engagement** (a positive experience), **post-sales services** (user training, help lines, servicing).
- **Benefits** of good service and **dangers** of poor service.
- How **ICT** has developed customer service: **websites, e-commerce, social media**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Customer service | Help and support given to customers before, during and after a sale |
| Customer engagement | Creating a positive, welcoming experience |
| Post-sales service | Support after the sale: training, help lines, servicing |
| Customer loyalty | Customers choosing to buy from the same business again |
| Word of mouth | Customers recommending (or warning against) a business |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling online |

## Methods of good service

| Method | What it means | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Product knowledge | Staff understand the products | Assistant matches running shoes to the customer |
| Customer engagement | Friendly, helpful, positive experience | Greeting, short waits, calm handling of complaints |
| User training | Showing customers how to use the product | Free lesson on a new machine |
| Help lines | Phone, chat or email support | 24-hour support line |
| Servicing | Maintenance, warranty repairs | Annual service |

## Benefits of good service

- Customer **satisfaction**.
- Customer **loyalty** and repeat sales.
- Positive **word of mouth** and reviews.
- Can charge **higher prices**.
- Fewer complaints and returns.
- Higher staff morale.

## Dangers of poor service

- **Dissatisfied customers** leave.
- **Poor reputation** spreads through reviews and conversation.
- Lost sales and profit.
- Extra cost of refunds and complaints.
- Low staff morale; difficulty recruiting new customers.

## Value of a customer

- Annual value = spend per visit x visits per year. $40 x 6 = $240.
- Lost sales = customers lost x annual value. 10% of 2,000 = 200; 200 x $240 = $48,000.
- Winning a new customer costs more than keeping an existing one.

## ICT and customer service

| Tool | Helps with | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Website | 24-hour information, FAQs, order tracking | Out-of-date or hard to use; no personal contact |
| E-commerce | Convenient buying, returns, reviews, recommendations | Delivery problems; payment security |
| Social media | Fast replies and feedback; publicity | Public complaints spread; needs constant monitoring |

ICT adds to personal service, but does not always replace it.

## Is more service worth the cost?

Compare the **cost** of the service (staff, training, equipment) with the **benefit** (retained customers, fewer returns, more sales).

Example: help line costs $30,000. Benefits: 300 fewer returns x $50 = $15,000, plus 100 customers kept x $240 = $24,000. Total $39,000, so net benefit $9,000.

## Exam traps

- Good service is more than politeness: include knowledge, speed, problem solving and after-sales help.
- Do not forget post-sales services.
- Link service to **loyalty, reputation and sales**, not just "happy customers".
- Mention costs of providing service.
- Match the method to the business: an online shop needs fast delivery and returns; a restaurant needs attentive staff.
- Social media can harm as well as help.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "analyse" question

Question style: "Analyse how an online shop could use social media to improve customer service."

1. **Quick replies:** customers can ask questions and get an answer within minutes, which solves problems before they turn into complaints.
2. **Public image:** a complaint solved publicly shows other customers that the shop cares, which builds reputation.
3. **Feedback:** comments show what customers like and dislike, so the shop can change products or delivery.
4. **Drawback:** a poorly handled complaint is visible to everyone, and staff must monitor the pages all day, which costs money.

Always finish the chain by linking to **loyalty, reputation or sales**.

## Revision checklist

- I can describe product knowledge, customer engagement and post-sales services.
- I can give examples of user training, help lines and servicing.
- I can list four benefits of good service and four dangers of poor service.
- I can calculate the annual value of a customer and the cost of losing customers.
- I can explain the roles of websites, e-commerce and social media in customer service.
- I can compare the cost of a service with its benefit.
- I can match a service method to a business.

## Command words for this subtopic

- **State or identify:** name the method, for example "help line".
- **Explain:** method, effect on the customer, effect on the business (loyalty, reputation, sales).
- **Analyse:** build a chain, for example "fast replies on social media, so complaints are solved, so customers stay, so sales are protected".
- **Evaluate:** weigh the cost of the service (staff, training, systems) against the gain, use figures, and give a judgement for the business named.

## Self-test

1. Define customer service.
2. State two post-sales services.
3. Explain why product knowledge helps a shop assistant to give good service.
4. State two benefits of good customer service.
5. State two dangers of poor customer service.
6. A customer spends $25 a visit and visits 8 times a year. Calculate annual value.
7. A business loses 150 customers each worth $200 a year. Calculate lost sales.
8. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of using social media for customer service.
9. Explain one way a website improves customer service.
10. A help line costs $12,000 a year and keeps 80 customers worth $220 each. Calculate the net benefit.
11. Explain why loyal customers are valuable.
12. Why might a business still need staff to speak to customers, even with a good website?

### Answers

1. The help and support given to customers before, during and after a sale.
2. Any two: user training; help lines; servicing and repairs.
3. Staff can recommend the right product and answer questions, so customers are satisfied and less likely to return it.
4. Any two: satisfaction; loyalty; good word of mouth; higher prices; fewer complaints.
5. Any two: dissatisfied customers; poor reputation; lost sales; extra costs; low staff morale.
6. $25 x 8 = **$200**.
7. 150 x $200 = **$30,000**.
8. Advantage: quick replies to questions. Disadvantage: complaints are public and spread quickly.
9. It provides information and order tracking 24 hours a day, so customers find answers without waiting.
10. Benefit = 80 x $220 = $17,600; net = $17,600 - $12,000 = **$5,600**.
11. They buy repeatedly, recommend the business, and cost less to keep than new customers cost to win.
12. Some customers have complex problems or prefer personal contact, and a human reply can solve problems a website cannot.
