## The accounting equation

```
Assets = Capital + Liabilities
```

Every transaction has **two** effects, which is why every entry is recorded twice and total debits always equal total credits.

## Debit and credit rules — learn this table cold

| Account type | Increase | Decrease |
|---|---|---|
| **A**sset | Debit | Credit |
| **E**xpense | Debit | Credit |
| **C**apital | Credit | Debit |
| **L**iability | Credit | Debit |
| **I**ncome | Credit | Debit |

Mnemonic: **DEAD CLIC** — Debit Expenses, Assets, Drawings; Credit Liabilities, Income, Capital.

## Source documents

| Document | Issued when |
|---|---|
| Invoice | Credit sale or purchase |
| Credit note | Goods returned (reduces amount owed) |
| Debit note | Buyer requests a reduction |
| Receipt | Payment made or received |
| Cheque counterfoil | Payment by cheque |
| Statement of account | Summary sent to a credit customer |

## Books of prime entry

| Book | Records |
|---|---|
| Sales day book | Credit sales |
| Purchases day book | Credit purchases |
| Sales returns day book | Goods returned by customers |
| Purchases returns day book | Goods returned to suppliers |
| Cash book | All bank and cash transactions |
| Petty cash book | Small payments (imprest system) |
| General journal | Everything else — opening entries, corrections, non-current assets on credit |

The **cash book** is both a book of prime entry **and** part of the ledger — a favourite short question.

## The ledger

- **Sales ledger** — credit customers (trade receivables).
- **Purchases ledger** — credit suppliers (trade payables).
- **General ledger** — everything else.

## Errors a trial balance will NOT reveal

Both sides stay equal, so the following slip through:

**Omission** · **Commission** (right type, wrong account) · **Principle** (wrong type of account) · **Original entry** (wrong amount in both) · **Reversal** (debit and credit swapped) · **Compensating** (two errors cancelling)

A balanced trial balance therefore proves arithmetic, **not** correctness.

## Exam traps

- Drawings reduce **capital** — they are not an expense.
- Returns go to *sales returns* / *purchases returns*, never back to sales or purchases.
- Credit note ≠ debit note — know who issues which.
- Applying "debit the receiver" to the *person* rather than the *account* flips the entry.
- A balanced trial balance does not mean the books are correct.

## Self-test

1. State the accounting equation.
2. A business buys a van on credit. Give both entries.
3. Which book records goods returned to a supplier?
4. Name three errors a trial balance cannot detect.
5. Why are drawings not an expense?

**Answers:** 1. Assets = Capital + Liabilities. 2. Debit Motor vehicles (asset increases), Credit the supplier (liability increases). 3. Purchases returns day book. 4. Any three of: omission, commission, principle, original entry, reversal, compensating. 5. They are withdrawals by the owner for personal use, reducing capital rather than being a cost of running the business (business entity concept).
