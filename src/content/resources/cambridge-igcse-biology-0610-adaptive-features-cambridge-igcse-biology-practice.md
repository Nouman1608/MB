> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **18.2 Adaptive features** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (hydrophytes and xerophytes); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [adaptive features study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-adaptive-features-cambridge-igcse-biology/) and the [adaptive features revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-adaptive-features-cambridge-igcse-biology-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which of these is an adaptive feature?

A: larger leg muscles in a racehorse after training

B: a scar on the skin of a seal

C: a thick fur coat inherited by an Arctic fox

D: a sun tan on a farm worker

**[1]**

**2.** Describe what is meant by an adaptive feature. **[2]**

**3.** A flower has large, brightly coloured petals and produces nectar. Suggest how these features help the plant to reproduce. **[2]**

**4.** An animal living on soft snow has very large, wide feet. Suggest how this helps it to survive. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State **two** features of the leaves of a xerophyte that reduce water loss. **[2]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why a hydrophyte needs very little support tissue. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A species of moth rests on tree trunks during the day. Its wings have a grey-brown pattern that matches the bark. Birds hunt the moths by sight.

**(a)** Explain how the wing pattern helps the moth to survive. **[2]**

**(b)** One moth has a torn wing after escaping from a bird. State whether the torn wing is an adaptive feature, and give a reason. **[2]**

**8.** The table shows features of the leaves of three plant species, X, Y and Z.

| Species | Leaf area / cm² | Cuticle thickness / µm | Stomata per mm² (upper surface) | Stomata per mm² (lower surface) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| X | 45 | 2 | 30 | 270 |
| Y | 0.5 | 12 | 0 | 60 |
| Z | 80 | 0.5 | 400 | 0 |

**(a)** Identify the species most likely to live in a dry desert. Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table. **[3]**

**(b)** Suggest which species has leaves that float on the surface of a pond. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(c)** *(Extended)* Explain how the features of species Y shown in the table reduce water loss. **[3]**

**9.** Polar bears live on sea ice in the Arctic, where temperatures are very low. They hunt seals. Polar bears have small ears, a thick layer of fat under the skin, white fur and large paws with rough pads.

Describe and explain **three** adaptive features of the polar bear. **[6]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A pondweed grows completely under the water. Its leaves are very thin and finely divided, have no waxy cuticle, and its stems contain large air spaces.

Explain how each of these features adapts the pondweed to its environment. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* Describe **two** differences between the leaves of a hydrophyte with floating leaves and the leaves of a xerophyte. Explain each difference. **[4]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A cactus has a thick, green, fleshy stem, leaves reduced to sharp spines, and shallow roots that spread over a wide area.

**(a)** Explain how the spines help to reduce water loss. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest **one** other way in which the spines help the cactus to survive. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the advantage of shallow, widespread roots in a desert where rain is rare and brief. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest why the stem of a cactus is green. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A, B and D are acquired during life, so they are not inherited and cannot be adaptive features.

**2.** An inherited feature [1] that helps an organism to survive and reproduce in its environment [1].

**3.** The petals and nectar attract insects [1]; the insects carry pollen from flower to flower, so pollination happens [1].

**4.** The wide feet spread the animal's mass over a larger area so it does not sink into the snow [1]; it can move quickly to escape predators / to find food [1].

**5.** Any two from: thick waxy cuticle; sunken stomata; hairs on the leaf surface; rolled leaves; small leaves / leaves reduced to spines; few stomata [2].

**6.** The water supports the plant [1].

**7. (a)** The pattern is camouflage against the bark [1], so birds are less likely to see the moth and eat it [1].

**(b)** Not an adaptive feature [1]; it was acquired during the moth's life / is not inherited [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), "camouflage" earns the first mark only when it is linked to the bark; the second mark needs the consequence for the predator.

**8. (a)** Y [1]; any two from: smallest leaf area (0.5 cm²); thickest cuticle (12 µm); fewest stomata (60 per mm² in total) / no stomata on the upper surface [2].

**(b)** Z [1]; its stomata are all on the upper surface, which is in contact with the air, while the lower surface touches the water / its cuticle is very thin because water loss is not a problem [1].

**(c)** The small leaf area gives a small surface from which water can be lost [1]; the thick cuticle reduces evaporation from the leaf surface [1]; fewer stomata means fewer pores through which water vapour diffuses out [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (a), quoting the figures from the table makes the evidence clear. "It has a thick cuticle" without comparison to X and Z is weaker; "thickest" earns the mark.

**9.** Any three features, each with a description and explanation, two marks each:

- small ears [1]; small surface area, so less heat is lost [1]
- thick layer of fat under the skin [1]; insulates the body, reducing heat loss [1]
- white fur [1]; camouflage against snow and ice, so seals do not see the bear approaching [1]
- large paws with rough pads [1]; spread the mass on thin ice / grip the ice so the bear does not slip [1]

*Examiner insight:* The second mark for each feature must link to the Arctic or to hunting. "White fur for camouflage" earns one mark; add who is being hidden from to earn the second.

**10.** Thin, finely divided leaves give a large surface area [1] for absorbing carbon dioxide / mineral ions / gases from the water [1]. No waxy cuticle, because water loss is not a problem when surrounded by water / gases can diffuse in easily [1]. Large air spaces in the stem make it buoyant so it stays up towards the light / store gases for the plant [1].

**11.** Any two differences, each explained, two marks each:

- hydrophyte has a thin or no cuticle, whereas a xerophyte has a thick waxy cuticle [1]; the xerophyte must reduce evaporation, but water loss is not a problem for the hydrophyte [1]
- hydrophyte has many stomata on the upper surface, whereas a xerophyte has few stomata, often sunken [1]; the hydrophyte's lower surface is in water so gas exchange is with the air above, while the xerophyte must reduce water vapour diffusing out [1]
- hydrophyte leaves are large and flat, whereas xerophyte leaves are small, rolled or spines [1]; the hydrophyte absorbs maximum light, while the xerophyte reduces the surface area for water loss [1]

*Examiner insight:* A difference must state both plants. "A xerophyte has a thick cuticle" with nothing about the hydrophyte is not a difference.

**12. (a)** Spines have a small surface area, so less water is lost by transpiration [1].

**(b)** They deter animals from eating the cactus / protect its stored water from animals [1].

**(c)** The roots absorb water quickly over a large area [1] before the rainwater evaporates or drains away [1].

**(d)** It contains chlorophyll so it can photosynthesise, because there are no broad leaves to do so [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling an acquired feature an adaptive feature.
- Naming a feature but not explaining how it helps.
- Not using figures when a question gives a table.
- Describing one plant only in a "differences" question.
- Writing that xerophytes "need less water" rather than lose less.
