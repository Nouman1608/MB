## What the sub-topic asks

| Point | What you must be able to do |

| 4.23 | Know that alkenes contain the functional group >C=C< |
| 4.24 | Know the general formula for alkenes |
| 4.25 | Explain why alkenes are classified as unsaturated hydrocarbons |
| 4.26 | Draw structural and displayed formulae for alkenes with up to four carbons and name the unbranched isomers (cis/trans notation not required) |
| 4.27 | Describe the reaction of alkenes with bromine to produce dibromoalkanes |
| 4.28 | Describe how bromine water distinguishes an alkane from an alkene |

## The functional group and general formula (4.23 and 4.24)

Alkenes are hydrocarbons that contain at least one **carbon-carbon double bond**, written >C=C<. This double bond is the functional group, and it is where alkenes react. The general formula is **CₙH₂ₙ**, so an alkene has twice as many hydrogens as carbons. The simplest alkene is ethene (n = 2). There is no alkene with n = 1, because a double bond needs two carbons.

| n | Name | Molecular formula | Structural formula | Mr |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 | Ethene | C₂H₄ | CH₂=CH₂ | 28 |
| 3 | Propene | C₃H₆ | CH₂=CHCH₃ | 42 |
| 4 | But-1-ene | C₄H₈ | CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ | 56 |
| 4 | But-2-ene | C₄H₈ | CH₃CH=CHCH₃ | 56 |

Because every alkene has the same ratio of carbon to hydrogen (CH₂), they all contain the same percentage of carbon: 12 / 14 x 100 = **85.7%**. Ethene (24 / 28), propene (36 / 42) and butene (48 / 56) each give 85.7%. This is also why the empirical formula of every alkene is CH₂.

## Unsaturated hydrocarbons (4.25)

An **unsaturated** hydrocarbon contains at least one **double (or triple) carbon-carbon bond**, so it has **fewer hydrogen atoms than the saturated alkane** with the same number of carbons. Ethane, C₂H₆, is saturated and ethene, C₂H₄, is unsaturated. The double bond can open up, allowing extra atoms to be added across it. An answer such as "alkenes are unsaturated because they have a C=C double bond, so they could bond to more atoms" scores the marks.

Compare the two to see the difference:

```
 Ethane (saturated)            Ethene (unsaturated)
    H   H                         H     H
    |   |                          \   /
H - C - C - H                       C=C
    |   |                          /   \
    H   H                         H     H
```

## Drawing and naming alkenes (4.26)

You must draw alkenes with up to four carbon atoms and name the **unbranched** ones.

- **Ethene:** CH₂=CH₂. Two carbons, one double bond, four hydrogens.
- **Propene:** CH₂=CHCH₃. Three carbons. The double bond must be at the end, so no position number is needed.
- **Butene:** C₄H₈ has two unbranched isomers.
  - **But-1-ene:** CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃, with the double bond between carbons 1 and 2.
  - **But-2-ene:** CH₃CH=CHCH₃, with the double bond between carbons 2 and 3.

The number is the lowest-numbered carbon of the double bond, counting from the end that gives the **smaller** number. So CH₃CH₂CH=CH₂ is but-1-ene, not but-3-ene. There is also a branched isomer, 2-methylpropene, CH₂=C(CH₃)CH₃. Cis/trans (E/Z) forms of but-2-ene are not required.

**Displayed formula of propene:**

```
    H   H   H
    |   |   |
H - C = C - C - H
        |   |
        H   H
```

Check: the first carbon has two H and a double bond (4 bonds); the second has one H, a double bond and a single bond (4 bonds); the third has three H and one single bond (4 bonds). Total hydrogens: 2 + 1 + 3 = 6, which matches C₃H₆.

## Reaction with bromine (4.27)

Alkenes react with bromine in an **addition reaction**: the C=C double bond opens, and each carbon atom bonds to one bromine atom. The product is a **dibromoalkane**, and it is the only product.

```
CH2=CH2 + Br2  →  CH2Br-CH2Br
ethene + bromine → 1,2-dibromoethane

CH3CH=CH2 + Br2  →  CH3CHBr-CH2Br
propene + bromine → 1,2-dibromopropane
```

The reaction happens at room temperature and does not need UV light. Compare this with the alkanes (see the [alkanes study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-alkanes/)), which need UV light and give two products.

**Worked example 1: mass of product and bromine used.** 1.4 g of ethene (Mr = 28) reacts completely with bromine. Br₂ has Mr = 160 and 1,2-dibromoethane has Mr = 188.

1. Moles of ethene = 1.4 / 28 = **0.050 mol**.
2. The equation is 1 : 1 : 1, so 0.050 mol of Br₂ is needed and 0.050 mol of C₂H₄Br₂ forms.
3. Mass of bromine = 0.050 x 160 = **8.0 g**.
4. Mass of 1,2-dibromoethane = 0.050 x 188 = **9.4 g**.
5. Check mass conservation: 1.4 + 8.0 = 9.4 g. ✓

**Worked example 2: gas volume.** At room temperature and pressure, 1 mole of gas occupies 24 dm³. The 0.050 mol of ethene occupies 0.050 x 24,000 = **1,200 cm³**.

**Worked example 3: propene.** 2.1 g of propene (Mr = 42) is 0.050 mol. It reacts with 0.050 mol of bromine to give 0.050 mol of 1,2-dibromopropane (Mr = 12 x 3 + 6 + 160 = 202), a mass of 0.050 x 202 = **10.1 g**.

## The bromine water test (4.28)

Bromine water is an orange-brown solution. It is used to distinguish between an alkane and an alkene.

| Test | Alkane (for example hexane) | Alkene (for example hex-1-ene) |
|---|---|---|
| Shake with bromine water | Stays orange-brown (no reaction) | Orange-brown to **colourless** |

Write the result as "bromine water is **decolourised**" or "turns from orange (brown) to colourless". Do **not** write that it turns "clear". The alkene has a double bond, so bromine adds across it and the coloured bromine is used up. The alkane is saturated, so there is nothing to add to, and in the dark the bromine does not react by substitution.

**A good test answer:** "Add bromine water to each sample and shake. The alkene decolourises the bromine water (orange to colourless); the alkane does not (it stays orange)."

## Other points worth knowing

- Alkenes burn with a **smoky, yellow flame**, because they contain a higher percentage of carbon than alkanes, so combustion is more likely to be incomplete.
- Ethene and propene are obtained by **cracking** long-chain alkanes (see the [crude oil study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-crude-oil/)).
- Alkenes are the monomers of addition polymers (see the [synthetic polymers study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-chemistry-4ch1-synthetic-polymers/)).
- Reactions of alkenes with steam to make ethanol are Paper 2 only content (see the alcohols page).

## Common mistakes

- Writing the general formula as CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.
- Saying alkenes are unsaturated "because they have no hydrogen". They have fewer hydrogens than the alkane.
- Writing that bromine water "goes clear" instead of "colourless".
- Saying that the alkane "turns the bromine water brown". It stays orange or brown, with no change.
- Giving the product of the bromine reaction as a monobromo compound, or giving two products.
- Numbering the double bond from the wrong end, or forgetting the number in but-1-ene.
- Drawing a carbon with five bonds in a displayed formula.
- Writing that UV light is needed for the bromine addition. It is not.

## Exam technique

For "describe the test", name the reagent, state what you do and give the result for both substances. For equations, balance the atoms and name the product with locants (1,2-dibromo...). For drawing, count the bonds on each carbon. For "explain", link the observation to the double bond: "alkenes contain C=C, so bromine adds across it and the colour is lost".
