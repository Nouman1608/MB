Cambridge O Level Geography (2217) is assessed by three compulsory, externally assessed papers and is not tiered: Paper 1, Geographical Themes (1 hour 45 minutes, 75 raw marks weighted to 100, 45%), Paper 2, Geographical Skills (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%) and Paper 3, Geographical Investigations (1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks, 27.5%). Unlike the IGCSE version, there is no coursework alternative. This syllabus is examined in the November series only. Topic pages include [Population and Settlement](/resources/o-level-geography-population-and-settlement/), [The Natural Environment](/resources/o-level-geography-natural-environment/) and [Economic Development](/resources/o-level-geography-economic-development/).

## Paper 1: Geographical Themes (75 marks, 45%)

Paper 1 has three questions, one per section. The three sections line up with the syllabus themes: Population and Settlement, The Natural Environment and Economic Development. Because each section has a question, you need balanced knowledge across all three; leaving out a theme leaves a hole you cannot avoid. The 75 raw marks are weighted to 100 by the board. Make revision notes with case studies and vocabulary for each theme, and see the [Natural Environment revision notes](/resources/o-level-geography-natural-environment-revision-notes/) and [Economic Development revision notes](/resources/o-level-geography-economic-development-revision-notes/).

## Paper 2: Geographical Skills (60 marks, 27.5%)

All questions on Paper 2 must be answered. It tests the skills of handling geographical information, so practise with maps, graphs, tables and photographs from past-paper style resources. Since all the questions are compulsory, there is no choosing: work steadily through and do not leave gaps.

## Paper 3: Geographical Investigations (60 marks, 27.5%)

Paper 3 has two compulsory questions. It is a written paper on investigations, so you should be comfortable with how a geographical enquiry is planned, how data is collected and presented, and how results are analysed and evaluated. Practise explaining why a method suits a question, what could go wrong and how reliable a conclusion is. The record does not specify the fieldwork contexts used, so check the specification and specimen papers.

## Timing and marks-per-minute

- Paper 1: 105 / 75 = 1.4 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2: 90 / 60 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Paper 3: 90 / 60 = 1.5 minutes per mark.
- Total sitting time: 105 + 90 + 90 = 285 minutes for 75 + 60 + 60 = 195 raw marks.
- Weighting per mark: Paper 1 is 45 / 75 = 0.6% per raw mark, Papers 2 and 3 are 27.5 / 60 = about 0.46% each.

The three weightings add to 45 + 27.5 + 27.5 = 100%.

## Command words and question styles

- **Identify** or **state**: a brief answer.
- **Describe**: what the data or map shows, with figures or place references.
- **Explain**: why, with linked reasons.
- **Suggest**: use knowledge in a new context.
- **Evaluate** or **justify**: weigh evidence and decide.
- **Calculate**: show the working, include units.

## Building a revision plan around the weightings

With Paper 1 at 45% and Papers 2 and 3 at 27.5% each, Paper 1 deserves the largest block of revision, but the other two papers together are worth 55%, which is more than Paper 1 alone. Revise the three themes in turn, creating a one-page summary of key terms and a named case study for each. Then schedule regular practice sessions on skills and investigation questions, since these improve with repetition rather than memorisation. Because there is no coursework route, every paper is a timed written paper: practise under realistic conditions, including the 1 hour 30 minute limit for Papers 2 and 3, and review your timing against the figures above.

## Worked practice scenario

An original Paper 3 style data question. A student measures river velocity at seven points across a channel, in metres per second: 0.42, 0.55, 0.61, 0.58, 0.49, 0.66, 0.73.

Question: "Calculate the mean, the median and the range of the velocities, and suggest one way to make the results more reliable."

```
Sum = 0.42 + 0.55 + 0.61 + 0.58 + 0.49 + 0.66 + 0.73 = 4.04
Mean = 4.04 / 7 = 0.577... = 0.58 m/s (2 decimal places)

Ordered: 0.42, 0.49, 0.55, 0.58, 0.61, 0.66, 0.73
Median (4th of 7) = 0.58 m/s

Range = 0.73 - 0.42 = 0.31 m/s
```

Reliability: repeat the measurements at each point and average them, or take readings at more points across the channel. A larger set of readings reduces the effect of one unusual measurement. Note that the mean and median are very close here, which suggests there are no extreme values distorting the average.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check that you are entered for the November series.
- Revise all three themes for Paper 1, since each section has a question.
- Practise map, graph and data interpretation for Paper 2.
- Practise planning, presenting and evaluating investigations for Paper 3.
- Remember all questions on Papers 2 and 3 are compulsory.
- Use minutes-per-mark figures for pacing.
- Quote data from resources, with units.
- Leave a few minutes to check calculations.

## Self-test

1. How many papers are there and are any optional?
2. How many questions are there on Paper 3?
3. What is the pace for Paper 1?
4. In the worked example, why is the range 0.31 m/s?

**Answers:** 1. Three, all compulsory. 2. Two compulsory questions. 3. 1.4 minutes per mark, since 105 / 75 = 1.4. 4. Because the highest value is 0.73 and the lowest is 0.42, and 0.73 - 0.42 = 0.31.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are in Cambridge O Level Geography 2217?**
There are three compulsory, externally assessed papers: Geographical Themes, Geographical Skills and Geographical Investigations. There is no coursework option.

**How is Paper 1 structured?**
It is 1 hour 45 minutes, 75 raw marks weighted to 100, and 45%. It has three questions, one per section.

**What are Papers 2 and 3 worth?**
Each is 1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks and 27.5%. Paper 2 requires all questions to be answered and Paper 3 has two compulsory questions.

**When is it examined?**
This syllabus is examined in the November series only, with no June series for this O Level variant.

**How does it differ from IGCSE Geography 0460?**
O Level 2217 has Paper 3, Geographical Investigations, in place of the coursework or Paper 4 alternative offered at IGCSE.
