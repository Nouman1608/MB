## Numbers to know

| Quantity | Value | Tier |
|---|---|---|
| Diameter of the Milky Way | about 100 000 light-years | Core |
| 1 light-year | 9.5 × 10¹⁵ m | Extended only |
| Hubble constant H₀ | 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ per second | Extended only |
| Age of the Universe, 1/H₀ | about 4.5 × 10¹⁷ s (about 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years) | Extended only |

## 6.2.1 The Sun as a star

- A star of **medium size**.
- Mostly **hydrogen and helium**.
- Radiates most energy as **infrared, visible light and ultraviolet**.
- **Extended only:** powered by nuclear reactions; in stable stars, **fusion of hydrogen into helium**.

## 6.2.2 Stars

- A **galaxy** is many billions of stars.
- The Sun is in the **Milky Way**.
- Other Milky Way stars are **much further** from the Earth than the Sun is.
- **Light-year:** distance travelled by light in (the vacuum of) space in one year.

**Life cycle (Extended only)**

| Stage | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Interstellar cloud | Gas and dust containing hydrogen |
| Protostar | Cloud collapses under its own gravity and heats up |
| Stable star | Inward gravity **balanced** by outward force due to high central temperature |
| Hydrogen runs out | All stars eventually; most central hydrogen now helium |
| Red giant (most stars) | → planetary nebula + **white dwarf** |
| Red supergiant (more massive stars) | → **supernova** → nebula with hydrogen and new heavier elements + **neutron star** or **black hole** |
| New generation | Supernova nebula may form new stars with orbiting planets |

## 6.2.3 The Universe

- The Milky Way is one of **many billions of galaxies**.
- **Redshift:** increase in the **observed wavelength** of electromagnetic radiation from receding stars and galaxies.
- Light from distant galaxies is redshifted compared with light emitted on the Earth.
- Redshift is evidence that the **Universe is expanding** and supports the **Big Bang Theory**.

**Extended only**

- **CMBR:** microwave radiation of a specific frequency observed at all points in space; produced shortly after the Universe formed; stretched into the microwave region as the Universe expanded.
- Speed of recession v: from the **change in wavelength** (redshift).
- Distance d of a far galaxy: from the **brightness of a supernova** in it.
- **H₀ = v / d** (ratio of recession speed to distance).
- **d / v = 1 / H₀:** an estimate of the age of the Universe; evidence that all matter was once at a single point.

**Method in steps: Hubble calculation (Extended only)**

1. Convert distances to metres (light-years × 9.5 × 10¹⁵).
2. Use v = H₀ × d or d = v / H₀.
3. For age: 1 / H₀ in seconds, then ÷ (365 × 86 400) for years.
4. Give answers to 2 significant figures, matching the data.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Galaxy | Many billions of stars held together by gravity |
| Milky Way | The galaxy containing the Sun |
| Light-year | Distance light travels in space in one year |
| Redshift | Increase in observed wavelength from a receding source |
| Big Bang Theory | The Universe began from a single point and has expanded since |
| Protostar (Extended only) | Collapsing, heating cloud before fusion starts |
| CMBR (Extended only) | Microwave radiation from all directions, left from the early Universe |
| Hubble constant (Extended only) | Recession speed ÷ distance for a galaxy |

## Exam traps

- A light-year is a **distance**.
- All three regions (IR, visible, UV) for the Sun's radiation.
- A stable star needs **both** forces and the word **balanced**.
- Massive stars → supergiant → supernova; less massive → giant → white dwarf.
- Redshift is an increase in **wavelength**, not a change of colour.
- CMBR was **stretched** into the microwave region by expansion.

## Self-test

1. Name the two elements that make up most of the Sun.
2. What is a galaxy?
3. Define a light-year.
4. State the approximate diameter of the Milky Way.
5. What is redshift?
6. What does the redshift of distant galaxies show about the Universe?
7. *(Extended)* Convert 8.6 light-years into metres.
8. *(Extended)* What nuclear reaction powers a stable star?
9. *(Extended)* What forms at the end of the life of a star much more massive than the Sun?
10. *(Extended)* State how the distance to a far galaxy is measured.
11. *(Extended)* A galaxy is 4.75 × 10²³ m away. Calculate its recession speed.
12. *(Extended)* Why is the CMBR now in the microwave region?

### Answers

1. **Hydrogen and helium**.
2. A collection of **many billions of stars**.
3. The **distance** travelled by light in (the vacuum of) space in **one year**.
4. About **100 000 light-years**.
5. An **increase in the observed wavelength** of radiation from receding stars and galaxies.
6. It is **expanding** (supporting the Big Bang Theory).
7. 8.6 × 9.5 × 10¹⁵ = **8.2 × 10¹⁶ m**.
8. **Fusion of hydrogen into helium**.
9. A red supergiant explodes as a **supernova**, leaving a **neutron star or black hole**.
10. From the **brightness of a supernova** in that galaxy.
11. v = H₀d = 2.2 × 10⁻¹⁸ × 4.75 × 10²³ = **1.0 × 10⁶ m/s**.
12. It was produced shortly after the Universe formed, and the **expansion of the Universe stretched** it into the microwave region.
