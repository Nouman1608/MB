## 6.2.4(a): The point of C–C bond formation

| Idea | What to remember |
|---|---|
| Why it matters | Most organic reactions keep the carbon skeleton; a C–C step makes the chain longer |
| Nitrile steps | Add exactly one carbon, as –C≡N |
| Friedel–Crafts steps | Join a whole alkyl or acyl group to a benzene ring |
| After a nitrile step | Reduce to RCH₂NH₂ or hydrolyse to RCOOH; the extra carbon stays |

Specification cross-references: 6.1.1 d (arenes) and 6.1.2 b (carbonyl compounds).

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleophile | An electron pair donor, here :CN⁻ |
| Electrophile | An electron pair acceptor, here R⁺ or RCO⁺ |
| Nitrile | Compound with a –C≡N group; the CN carbon is C1 in the name |
| Hydroxynitrile | –OH and –C≡N on the same carbon |
| Halogen carrier | AlCl₃, FeCl₃, FeBr₃, iron or another aluminium or iron halide; makes the electrophile |
| Acyl chloride | R–COCl |

## Reagents and conditions at a glance

| Outcome | Starting compound | Reagent and conditions | Product | Mechanism to know |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| (b)(i) | Haloalkane, R–X | NaCN or KCN in ethanol, reflux | Nitrile, R–CN | Nucleophilic substitution |
| (b)(ii) | Aldehyde or ketone | HCN, as NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) | Hydroxynitrile | Nucleophilic addition |
| (c)(i) | Nitrile | H₂ with Ni catalyst | Primary amine, R–CH₂NH₂ | Not required |
| (c)(ii) | Nitrile | Dilute acid, e.g. HCl(aq), reflux | Carboxylic acid, R–COOH | Not required |
| (d) | Benzene | Haloalkane + halogen carrier, dry | Alkylbenzene | Electrophilic substitution |
| (d) | Benzene | Acyl chloride + halogen carrier, dry | Aromatic ketone | Electrophilic substitution |

## Equations to learn

- Haloalkane: R–Br + CN⁻ → R–CN + Br⁻
- Carbonyl: RCHO + HCN → RCH(OH)CN, or R₂CO + HCN → R₂C(OH)CN
- Reduction: R–CN + 2H₂ → R–CH₂NH₂
- Hydrolysis: R–CN + 2H₂O + H⁺ → R–COOH + NH₄⁺ (with HCl: R–CN + 2H₂O + HCl → R–COOH + NH₄Cl)
- Alkylation: C₆H₆ + R–Cl → C₆H₅R + HCl
- Acylation: C₆H₆ + RCOCl → C₆H₅COR + HCl
- Electrophile: R–Cl + AlCl₃ → R⁺ + AlCl₄⁻, or RCOCl + AlCl₃ → RCO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
- Carrier regenerated: H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl

## 6.2.4(b)(i): Substitution mechanism in steps

1. Label the C–X bond Cδ+ and Xδ−.
2. Arrow: cyanide carbon lone pair → δ+ carbon.
3. Arrow: C–X bonding pair → X.
4. Products: R–CN and X⁻.

Reminder: 1-chlorobutane gives pentanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN. The stem goes up by one carbon.

## 6.2.4(b)(ii): Addition mechanism in steps

1. Label the C=O bond Cδ+ and Oδ−.
2. Arrow: cyanide carbon lone pair → carbonyl carbon.
3. Arrow: C=O π pair → O, leaving an O⁻ intermediate.
4. Protonate O⁻ with H₂O (giving OH⁻ as well) or with H⁺.

Reminder: 2,2-dimethylpropanal, (CH₃)₃CCHO, gives 2-hydroxy-3,3-dimethylbutanenitrile, (CH₃)₃CCH(OH)CN.

### Naming in steps

1. Find the longest chain that includes the CN carbon.
2. Number from the CN carbon as C1.
3. Name substituents (hydroxy, methyl, phenyl) with locants, in alphabetical order.
4. End with "-nitrile" after the full alkane name: butanenitrile, not butanitrile.

## 6.2.4(c): Nitrile to amine or acid

| | Reduction | Acid hydrolysis |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | H₂, Ni catalyst | Dilute HCl or H₂SO₄, reflux |
| Nitrile carbon becomes | –CH₂– next to NH₂ | –COOH carbon |
| By-product | None | NH₄⁺ (as NH₄Cl with HCl) |
| Example | Propanenitrile → propan-1-amine | Propanenitrile → propanoic acid |

A hydroxynitrile hydrolyses to a 2-hydroxy carboxylic acid: the –OH is untouched and only –CN changes. Reduce the same hydroxynitrile with H₂/Ni instead and you get an amino alcohol, with –OH on one carbon and –CH₂NH₂ next to it. In both cases, check your answer by counting: the product has the same number of carbon atoms as the nitrile.

## 6.2.4(d): Friedel–Crafts in steps

1. Identify the reagent: haloalkane (alkylation) or acyl chloride (acylation).
2. Write the electrophile equation with AlCl₃ (or FeCl₃, FeBr₃).
3. Write the overall equation, ending in HCl.
4. Show the carrier regenerated from H⁺ and AlCl₄⁻.
5. For a mechanism (6.1.1(g) lets you be asked to predict one), follow the halogenation pattern: π electrons to the electrophile, then loss of H⁺.

Reminder: benzene and 2-chloropropane with AlCl₃ give (propan-2-yl)benzene, C₆H₅CH(CH₃)₂, and HCl.

## Route planning: spot the C–C step

Start every synthesis question by counting carbons in the starting compound and the target. Then match the change to a pattern.

| Change from start to target | Likely route |
|---|---|
| One more carbon, ending –COOH | Haloalkane → nitrile (CN⁻, ethanol) → acid (dilute acid, reflux) |
| One more carbon, ending –CH₂NH₂ | Haloalkane → nitrile → amine (H₂/Ni) |
| One more carbon, OH and COOH on the same carbon | Aldehyde or ketone → hydroxynitrile (HCN) → 2-hydroxy acid |
| One more carbon, OH and CH₂NH₂ on neighbouring carbons | Aldehyde or ketone → hydroxynitrile → reduce with H₂/Ni |
| Carbon chain joined to a benzene ring | Friedel–Crafts alkylation |
| C=O joined to a benzene ring | Friedel–Crafts acylation |

Small reminders:

- 1-Bromo-3-phenylpropane, C₆H₅CH₂CH₂CH₂Br → 4-phenylbutanenitrile → 4-phenylbutanoic acid.
- Pentanal → 2-hydroxyhexanenitrile → 2-hydroxyhexanoic acid.
- Iodomethane → ethanenitrile → ethylamine.

For several steps, multiply the step yields as decimals to get the overall yield. To find the starting mass for a target mass, divide the moles of target by the overall yield, then convert to grams.

## Must-know distinctions

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| CN⁻ with haloalkane vs with carbonyl | Substitution (halide leaves) vs addition (nothing leaves) |
| Ethanol vs NaCN(aq)/H⁺(aq) | Ethanol for haloalkanes; aqueous cyanide with acid for carbonyls |
| Reduction vs hydrolysis of nitriles | Amine (–CH₂NH₂) vs acid (–COOH); both keep the nitrile carbon |
| Alkylation vs acylation | Haloalkane gives alkylbenzene; acyl chloride gives aromatic ketone |
| Nucleophile vs electrophile | CN⁻ donates a pair; R⁺ and RCO⁺ accept one |
| Arrow start for CN⁻ | Carbon lone pair, never nitrogen |

## Quick self-test

1. Define a nucleophile.
2. Name the nitrile formed when 2-iodopropane reacts with KCN in ethanol.
3. State the reagent and conditions for converting a haloalkane into a nitrile.
4. Cyclobutanone, C₄H₆O, reacts with HCN. Give the molecular formula and Mᵣ of the product (Aᵣ: N 14.0; C 12.0; O 16.0; H 1.0).
5. Write the equation for the reduction of ethanenitrile with hydrogen.
6. Write the equation for the hydrolysis of butanenitrile with dilute hydrochloric acid.
7. Give the electrophile formed when an acyl chloride, RCOCl, reacts with AlCl₃.
8. Name the halogen carriers the specification lists.
9. Which of these reactions lengthens a carbon chain: (A) chloroethane with NaOH(aq); (B) propanal with HCN; (C) propanal with NaBH₄?
10. A two-step route has step yields of 75% and 80%. What is the overall yield?
11. Benzene reacts with an acyl chloride and AlCl₃. What type of compound forms?
12. Explain why a dry apparatus is used for Friedel–Crafts reactions.

### Answers

1. An electron pair donor.
2. 2-Methylpropanenitrile, (CH₃)₂CHCN.
3. NaCN or KCN in ethanol, heated under reflux.
4. HCN adds across C=O, so the product is C₄H₆O + HCN = C₅H₇NO, with OH and CN on the same ring carbon; Mᵣ = 5(12.0) + 7(1.0) + 14.0 + 16.0 = 97.0.
5. CH₃CN + 2H₂ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ (Ni catalyst); the product is ethylamine (ethanamine).
6. CH₃CH₂CH₂CN + 2H₂O + HCl → CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH + NH₄Cl.
7. RCO⁺, an acylium ion, with AlCl₄⁻.
8. Iron, iron halides and aluminium halides.
9. B only.
10. 0.75 × 0.80 = 0.60, so 60.0%.
11. An aromatic ketone, C₆H₅COR.
12. Aluminium chloride reacts with water, so it would no longer work as a halogen carrier.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing the CN⁻ arrow from nitrogen, or from the charge rather than a lone pair.
- Missing the δ+/δ− dipole on C–X or C=O when the question asks for a mechanism.
- Forgetting the protonation step in nucleophilic addition, or drawing the O⁻ intermediate without a charge.
- Giving "KCN(aq)" for the haloalkane step instead of cyanide in ethanol.
- Naming nitriles without counting the CN carbon, such as calling CH₃CH₂CN "ethanenitrile".
- Balancing nitrile reduction with one H₂, or nitrile hydrolysis without NH₄⁺.
- Leaving HCl off a Friedel–Crafts equation, or not showing AlCl₃ regenerated.
- Writing the acylation product as an aldehyde, or putting the acyl group on the ring through oxygen.
