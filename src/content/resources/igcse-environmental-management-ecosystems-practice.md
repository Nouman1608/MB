These questions test all of Topic 5, Ecosystems, biodiversity and fieldwork, in Cambridge IGCSE
Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029). They move from definitions to energy calculations, a
mangrove case and a fieldwork investigation of the kind set on Paper 2, Environmental Management in
Context.

## Section A

**1.** Define *niche*. **[1]**

**2.** State **three** abiotic components of a freshwater ecosystem. **[3]**

**3.** Write the word equation for photosynthesis. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by an *invasive species*. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** A food chain in a mangrove forest is:
mangrove leaves → crabs → fish → herons

(a) Identify the secondary consumer. **[1]**
(b) The mangrove leaves fix 50 000 kJ of energy. Calculate the energy reaching the herons, assuming 10%
transfer at each level. **[2]**
(c) Explain why energy is lost between trophic levels. **[3]**
(d) Explain why a pyramid of numbers for this chain might not be pyramid-shaped, whereas a pyramid of
energy always is. **[3]**

**6.** Describe how wind pollination leads to the formation of seeds. **[3]**

**7.** Describe how carbon moves from the atmosphere into a fish and back again. **[4]**

**8.** Mangrove forest along a coast is cleared for shrimp farms and a new road.
(a) Describe **three** impacts of this deforestation. **[3]**
(b) Explain **two** reasons why the mangroves should be managed sustainably. **[4]**

**9.** Define *sustainable management of forests*. **[2]**

**10.** A student investigates whether the number of plant species changes with distance from a footpath
across a field.
(a) Suggest a hypothesis. **[1]**
(b) Identify the independent and dependent variables. **[2]**
(c) Describe how the student could use a transect and quadrats to collect the data. **[4]**
(d) The student records one quadrat with 19 species where the others nearby have 4-6. Suggest how this
anomaly should be dealt with. **[2]**

**11.** Name the sampling technique most suitable for each:
(a) beetles active on the ground at night; (b) insects in tall grass; (c) the area of forest lost over a
large region. **[3]**

## Section C

**12.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of random and systematic sampling for measuring the population
of a plant species. **[6]**

**13.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of national parks and of zoos with captive breeding as ways to
conserve endangered species. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The role of a species in its ecosystem. **[1]**

**2.** Any three: temperature; water; oxygen; carbon dioxide; salinity; light; pH. **[3]**

**3.** carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen. **[2]** (1 for reactants, 1 for products.)

**4.** A species that is not native to an area and has been introduced, which can spread and harm native
species. **[1]**

**5.** (a) Fish. **[1]**
(b) 50 000 → 5 000 (crabs) → 500 (fish) → **50 kJ** (herons). **[2]**
(c) Energy is used in movement **[1]** and respiration **[1]**; some is lost in digestion and excretion and is
not passed on **[1]**.
(d) Numbers depend on organism size: one mangrove tree supports many crabs, so the bottom bar may be narrow
**[1]**; energy decreases at every level because about 90% is lost **[1]**, so each bar of an energy pyramid
is always smaller than the one below **[1]**.

**6.** Pollen is carried by wind from an anther **[1]** to a stigma of a flower **[1]**; this leads to
fertilisation, followed by seed and fruit formation **[1]**.

**7.** Carbon dioxide is taken in by plants or algae in photosynthesis **[1]**; it becomes glucose and other
carbon compounds **[1]**; the fish eats the plants or smaller animals (feeding) **[1]**; carbon returns to
the atmosphere as carbon dioxide through the fish's respiration or decomposition after death **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Any three: habitat loss for crabs, fish and birds **[1]**; loss of biodiversity **[1]**; soil
erosion **[1]**; silting and flooding **[1]**; global warming from released carbon **[1]**; genetic depletion.
**[max 3]**
(b) Any two explained: mangroves are carbon sinks and stores, so keeping them limits global warming **[2]**;
they protect against flooding and erosion of the coast **[2]**; they provide food and raw materials, such as
fish nurseries and wood, for local people **[2]**; they offer ecotourism and education **[2]**. **[max 4]**

**9.** Balancing the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans **[1]** while conserving forests for future
generations **[1]**.

**10.** (a) The number of plant species increases with distance from the footpath. **[1]**
(b) Independent: distance from the path **[1]**; dependent: number of plant species **[1]**.
(c) Lay a tape measure at right angles to the path **[1]**; place a quadrat at regular intervals, such as every
2 m (systematic sampling) **[1]**; count the different species in each quadrat **[1]**; repeat with further
transects to improve reliability **[1]**.
(d) Check the identification or recount if possible **[1]**; otherwise exclude it from the mean and note it, or
repeat the sample at that point **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Pitfall trap **[1]**; (b) sweep net **[1]**; (c) aerial photography, satellite images or drones
**[1]**.

**12.** **[6]** Random: avoids bias because every point has an equal chance **[1]**; suits a uniform area **[1]**;
but may leave parts unsampled by chance **[1]**. Systematic: covers the area evenly and shows change along a
gradient, such as from a path **[1]**; easy to carry out **[1]**; but may miss or exaggerate patterns that
coincide with the sample interval **[1]**. Credit a conclusion that the choice depends on the aim.

**13.** **[6]** National parks: benefit - protect whole habitats and many species at once, and can fund
themselves through ecotourism; limitation - need guards and funding, poaching may continue, local people
may lose land. Zoos and captive breeding: benefit - can save species with very small populations and educate
visitors; limitation - expensive, small gene pool, and animals may not survive release if their habitat is
gone. Conclusion: in-situ protection is usually needed for long-term success, with captive breeding as a
back-up.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

In fieldwork questions, link each step to reliability or accuracy. "Repeat" earns a mark only with a
reason, such as allowing a mean to be calculated. In energy questions, show each trophic level so method
marks are not lost.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to name flower parts?**
No. The 0680 syllabus states that knowledge of the structure and
naming of flower parts is not required; only the anther-to-stigma transfer.

**How do I identify an anomaly?**
It is a result that does not fit the pattern of the others. Suggest
checking or repeating it, and explain whether it is excluded from the mean.

**Which energy figure should I use?**
Use 10% transfer between trophic levels unless the question gives
different data.
