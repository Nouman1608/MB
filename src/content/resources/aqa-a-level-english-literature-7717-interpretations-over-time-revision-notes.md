**These notes condense one NEA requirement in AQA A-level English Literature B (7717):** "in
non-exam assessment only, discussion of different interpretations must include, on at least one
text in a conventional response, consideration of different interpretations of the text(s) over
time." For full explanation and a model paragraph, use the
[interpretations over time study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-english-literature-7717-interpretations-over-time/);
for the component as a whole, see the
[Theory and Independence revision notes](/resources/a-level-aqa-english-literature-b-theory-and-independence-revision-notes/).

## The rule in four parts

| Phrase | What it means for you |
|---|---|
| "In non-exam assessment only" | A condition of the NEA, not of the exam papers |
| "On at least one text" | One essay is enough, but it must be done properly |
| "In a conventional response" | A re-creative piece and commentary do not satisfy it |
| "Over time" | Readings from different moments in the text's reception |

A related text-selection condition: texts "must allow access to a range of critical views and
interpretations, including over time".

## What counts and what does not

- **Counts**: a reading from the text's first reception set against a later reading.
- **Counts**: a reading shaped by a critical movement that arose after the text, with the change
  explained.
- **Counts**: a later rewriting used as evidence of how the text came to be read differently.
- **Does not count on its own**: two modern critics who simply disagree.
- **Does not count**: a list of critics' names with no explanation of change.

## Four moments of reception

1. **First reception**: how readers responded when the text appeared.
2. **Later critical traditions**: changed values in a later period.
3. **The theoretical turn**: feminist, Marxist, post-colonial, eco-critical and narrative readings,
   matching the Critical anthology.
4. **Present-day reading**: your own, which is also shaped by its time.

The **literary value and the canon** section is the natural partner: it is about how reputations
change.

## Two reception histories to learn from

| Text | Moments | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| *Jane Eyre* (1847) | Rhys's *Wide Sargasso Sea* (1966); Gilbert and Gubar, *The Madwoman in the Attic* (1979); Spivak's post-colonial essay (1985) | Bertha moves from obstacle, to Jane's double, to colonised victim |
| *Heart of Darkness* | Early readings as a critique of imperialism; Achebe's lecture (1975) | The question becomes whether a text can critique empire while reproducing its racism |

Confirm any text with your teacher, and check it is not on the exam set-text lists.

## Text-selection checks

- Has the text been read differently since it appeared?
- Can you reach reliable critical material from more than one period?
- Does its history connect to an anthology section you plan to use?
- Is it absent from the exam set-text lists?
- Is its reception history long enough to show change?

## Paragraph frame for interpretations over time

1. State a reading and its moment.
2. Explain why readers at that moment saw the text this way.
3. Introduce a later reading and its moment.
4. Explain what changed: values, critical method, historical awareness.
5. Use the change as evidence for your own judgement.

## Context or interpretation?

**Context (AO3)** explains when and where a text was written and received. **Interpretations over
time (AO5)** explain how its meaning has been argued about. The two meet when a change in context
explains a change in reading.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Reception | How a text is received and read by audiences |
| Reception history | The record of how readings of a text have changed |
| Canon | The body of texts widely regarded as important |
| Re-evaluation | A change in a text's reputation or meaning |
| Critical movement | A school of reading, such as feminist or post-colonial criticism |
| Rewriting | A later creative work that responds to an earlier text |
| Contemporary review | A review written when a text first appeared |
| Anachronism | Judging a past text only by present standards, without acknowledging the gap |

## Traps

- Treating two modern critics as "over time".
- Putting the requirement only in a re-creative piece.
- Inventing what early readers thought; use sources you can cite.
- Listing critics without explaining why readings changed.
- Assuming the latest reading is the correct one.
- Choosing a text with almost no reception history.
- Long critic quotations crowding out your own argument.

## Self-test

1. Quote the requirement covered here.
2. Does the requirement apply to the exam papers?
3. Can a re-creative response satisfy it?
4. Why do two modern critics who disagree not meet it on their own?
5. Name the four moments of reception.
6. Which Critical anthology section is most closely related to change over time?
7. What does *Wide Sargasso Sea* do with *Jane Eyre*?
8. How did Gilbert and Gubar read Bertha?
9. What did Spivak argue about *Jane Eyre*?
10. What did Achebe argue in his 1975 lecture?
11. Give two text-selection checks related to this requirement.
12. What does the AO5 statement say interpretation is?

## Answers

1. "In non-exam assessment only, discussion of different interpretations must include, on at least
   one text in a conventional response, consideration of different interpretations of the text(s)
   over time."
2. No; it is specific to the NEA.
3. No; it must be met in a conventional response.
4. Because their difference may not come from different moments in the text's reception.
5. First reception; later critical traditions; the theoretical turn; present-day reading.
6. Literary value and the canon.
7. It gives a voice and history to Bertha Mason, Rochester's Creole wife.
8. As Jane's dark double, expressing anger a Victorian woman could not show.
9. That the novel's feminist individualism depends on the destruction of Bertha, a colonial woman.
10. That *Heart of Darkness* dehumanises Africans and that Conrad was a racist.
11. Any two: the text has been read differently over time; reliable material exists from more than
    one period; its history connects to a planned anthology section; it is not an exam set text.
12. "Not a fixed process but a dynamic one".
