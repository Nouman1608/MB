This guide covers subtopic **8.3 Group VII properties** of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 (2026–2028 series). The topic overview in [Periodic Table: Groups and Trends](/resources/periodic-table-groups-and-trends/) gives one displacement equation; this guide works through every halogen–halide combination, the ionic and electron-transfer explanation examiners reward, and how to predict the behaviour of fluorine and astatine.

## What the syllabus asks

The 5070 outcomes for 8.3 are:

1. Describe the Group VII halogens, **chlorine, bromine and iodine**, as **diatomic non-metals** with general trends down the group, limited to **increasing density** and **decreasing reactivity**.
2. State the appearance of the halogens at r.t.p.: chlorine, a pale yellow-green gas; bromine, a red-brown liquid; iodine, a grey-black solid.
3. Describe and explain the **displacement reactions** of halogens with other halide ions.
4. Predict the properties of other elements in Group VII, given information about the elements.

## Diatomic non-metals

Each halogen exists as molecules of two atoms joined by a single covalent bond: Cl₂, Br₂, I₂. Each atom has seven outer electrons; sharing one pair gives both atoms a full outer shell. "Diatomic" is part of the syllabus description, so always write the formula of the element as X₂ in equations. Writing Cl instead of Cl₂ for chlorine gas is one of the most common errors in this topic.

As non-metals, the halogens do not conduct electricity, and they form **negative ions** (1−) when they react with metals, because each atom gains one electron to complete its outer shell. These ions are the **halide** ions: chloride Cl⁻, bromide Br⁻, iodide I⁻. Halogen (the element) and halide (the ion) are different words for different particles; mixing them up costs marks in displacement answers.

## Appearance and the density trend

| Halogen | State at r.t.p. | Colour | Colour in aqueous solution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine, Cl₂ | Gas | Pale yellow-green | Very pale yellow-green (almost colourless) |
| Bromine, Br₂ | Liquid | Red-brown | Orange (yellow-orange when dilute) |
| Iodine, I₂ | Solid | Grey-black | Brown |

The three states show the density trend directly: a gas, then a liquid, then a solid, each denser than the one above. The colour of the element also darkens down the group. The solution colours in the last column are not listed in the syllabus wording, but you need them to describe what is **seen** in displacement reactions.

## Decreasing reactivity

A halogen reacts by **gaining** an electron. Down the group, the outer shell is further from the nucleus, so an incoming electron is attracted less strongly, and the halogen gains electrons less readily. Reactivity therefore **decreases** down Group VII, the opposite of Group I, where atoms react by losing an electron. The syllabus asks you to describe the trend; displacement reactions are the evidence for it.

## Displacement reactions

A **more reactive** halogen displaces a **less reactive** halogen from a solution of its halide ions. The more reactive halogen takes the electron from the halide ion, becoming a halide ion itself, while the halide ion becomes the halogen element.

### The full results grid

| Halogen added ↓ / solution → | Potassium chloride | Potassium bromide | Potassium iodide |
|---|---|---|---|
| Chlorine water | — | Turns orange (Br₂ formed) | Turns brown (I₂ formed) |
| Bromine water | No reaction | — | Turns brown (I₂ formed) |
| Iodine solution | No reaction | No reaction | — |

Three reactions occur and three do not. Every reaction that occurs has a halogen higher in the group displacing one lower down.

### Equations at three levels

Chlorine with potassium bromide:

```
word:   chlorine + potassium bromide → potassium chloride + bromine
symbol: Cl₂(aq) + 2KBr(aq) → 2KCl(aq) + Br₂(aq)
ionic:  Cl₂(aq) + 2Br⁻(aq) → 2Cl⁻(aq) + Br₂(aq)
```

The potassium ions are **spectator ions**: they appear unchanged on both sides, so they are left out of the ionic equation. Bromine with iodide ions follows the same pattern:

```
ionic:  Br₂(aq) + 2I⁻(aq) → 2Br⁻(aq) + I₂(aq)
```

### Explaining the reaction as electron transfer

Splitting the ionic equation shows what happens to the electrons:

```
Cl₂ + 2e⁻ → 2Cl⁻     (chlorine gains electrons: reduced)
2Br⁻ → Br₂ + 2e⁻     (bromide ions lose electrons: oxidised)
```

This is a **redox** reaction. Chlorine is the **oxidising agent** (it oxidises bromide ions and is itself reduced); bromide ions act as the reducing agent. A full "explain" answer for a displacement reaction makes three points: chlorine is more reactive than bromine; chlorine gains electrons more readily, so it takes electrons from bromide ions; bromine is formed, seen as an orange colour. This links directly to the redox outcomes of subtopic 6.4, where aqueous potassium iodide turning brown is used to identify an oxidising agent: the brown colour is iodine released by exactly this kind of reaction.

## Predicting fluorine and astatine

Fluorine is above chlorine; astatine is below iodine. Apply each trend and keep what the group shares.

### Worked example

*Predict the state of astatine at r.t.p. and whether it would react with aqueous sodium iodide. Write an ionic equation for the reaction of fluorine with chloride ions, assuming it occurs in the same way.*

- **State:** a **solid**, because the trend is gas → liquid → solid with increasing density down the group, and iodine is already a solid. A dark colour (black) follows from the darkening colour trend.
- **Reaction with iodide:** **no reaction**. Astatine is less reactive than iodine, because reactivity decreases down the group, so it cannot displace iodine from iodide ions.
- **Fluorine:** F₂ + 2Cl⁻ → 2F⁻ + Cl₂. Fluorine is the most reactive halogen, so it would displace chlorine. Fluorine itself is a pale yellow gas, in line with the lighter colours at the top of the group.

The examiner is testing whether you apply the trends in the correct direction and keep the diatomic formula and 1− ion for the new elements.

## Common mistakes

- **"Chloride displaces bromide."** It is chlorine (the element) that displaces bromine from bromide ions.
- **Writing single atoms**: Cl + 2Br⁻ is wrong; the halogen is Cl₂.
- **Forgetting to balance the ionic equation**: two halide ions are needed for every halogen molecule.
- **Wrong colour for the product**: orange or yellow-orange for bromine solution, brown for iodine solution. Do not describe the solution colour as "grey-black"; that is solid iodine.
- **Predicting that iodine displaces chlorine** because it is "bigger". Reactivity decreases down the group, so iodine displaces nothing in this set.
- **Giving the Group I reason in reverse**: halogens gain electrons, so being further from the nucleus makes them *less* reactive.

## Exam technique

- **State** the appearance with both colour and state: "red-brown liquid" earns more than "brown".
- **Describe** a displacement: give the colour change you would see.
- **Explain** a displacement: compare reactivity, then describe the electron transfer.
- **Predict**: continue the trend and name the reason ("reactivity decreases down the group").
- In an ionic equation, leave out spectator ions and include state symbols if asked.
