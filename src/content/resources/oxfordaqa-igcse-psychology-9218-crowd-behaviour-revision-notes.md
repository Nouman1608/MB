Quick-recall notes for the crowd-behaviour strand of **3.2.2 Social influence** in OxfordAQA
International GCSE Psychology (9218): "how social factors (social loafing, deindividuation and
culture) and dispositional factors (personality and morality) affect collective behaviour." For full
explanations, research detail and a model answer, use the
[crowd behaviour study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-psychology-9218-crowd-behaviour/);
for conformity and obedience, see the
[Conformity and Obedience revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-psychology-conformity-obedience-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Collective behaviour** | How people act as part of a group or crowd |
| **Prosocial behaviour** | Behaviour that benefits others |
| **Antisocial behaviour** | Behaviour that harms others or breaks social norms |
| **Social loafing** | Putting in less effort in a group because individual effort cannot be identified |
| **Deindividuation** | Loss of self-awareness and personal responsibility in a crowd |
| **Anonymity** | Not being identifiable (masks, uniforms, darkness, large crowds) |
| **Arousal** | Heightened excitement or alertness (noise, emotion, alcohol) |
| **Individualist culture** | Culture emphasising personal goals and independence |
| **Collectivist culture** | Culture emphasising group goals and interdependence |
| **Personality** | Stable traits that affect behaviour across situations |
| **Morality** | A person's own principles of right and wrong |

## The five factors at a glance

| Factor | Type | Pushes towards | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|---|
| Social loafing | Social | Less prosocial effort | Hidden contributions; others will do it |
| Deindividuation | Social | Antisocial (or prosocial if group norms are prosocial) | Anonymity + arousal reduce self-awareness and responsibility |
| Culture | Social | Either | Norms for crowds; collectivism lowers loafing |
| Personality | Dispositional | Either | Aggressive/impulsive traits vs empathy/agreeableness |
| Morality | Dispositional | Prosocial; resists antisocial | Personal principles still apply when anonymous |

## Research to quote

**Social loafing**
- **Ringelmann:** rope-pulling force per person fell as group size grew.
- **Latané, Williams and Harkins (1979):** noise per person fell in larger clapping and shouting groups,
  even in pseudo-groups, so effort dropped, not just coordination.

**Deindividuation**
- **Zimbardo (1969):** hooded, anonymous students gave (fake) shocks for about twice as long as
  identifiable students.
- **Diener and colleagues (1976):** anonymous trick-or-treaters in groups were most likely to take
  extra sweets.
- **Mann (1981):** crowds baited people threatening to jump more often when large, at night and far
  away.
- **Johnson and Downing (1979):** nurse-like uniforms led to fewer shocks; deindividuation follows the
  situation's norms.

**Culture**
- **Karau and Williams (1993):** review found less social loafing in collectivist cultures.
- **Drury and colleagues:** survivors of emergencies often reported crowds helping each other, linked
  to shared identity.

## Reducing antisocial crowd behaviour

- Make people **identifiable**: cameras, stewards who know regulars, good lighting.
- Reduce **arousal**: limit alcohol; manage noise and crowding.
- Build a **shared prosocial identity**: clear positive norms set by organisers or community leaders.
- Make individual **contributions visible** in group tasks to reduce loafing.

## Prosocial or antisocial? Reading a scenario

| Scenario detail | Factor | Likely direction |
|---|---|---|
| Masks, hoods, darkness, huge crowd | Deindividuation (anonymity) | Antisocial, unless norms are prosocial |
| Loud music, alcohol, excitement | Deindividuation (arousal) | Antisocial |
| "Everyone assumed someone else would help" | Social loafing | Less prosocial action |
| A charity event with shared positive aims | Culture / group norms | Prosocial |
| One person refuses to join in | Personality or morality | Resists the crowd |

## Evaluation points

- **Support:** experimental and field evidence for social loafing and deindividuation.
- **Nuance:** Johnson and Downing show anonymity does not automatically cause aggression.
- **Validity:** laboratory tasks (shocks, clapping) differ greatly from real crowds.
- **Ethics:** apparent shocks involve deception and possible distress.
- **Individual differences:** not everyone in a crowd acts alike; dispositional factors explain why.

## Exam traps

- Crowds can be **prosocial** as well as antisocial.
- Social loafing = **less effort**; deindividuation = **less self-awareness**. Do not mix them up.
- Anonymity is a **cause** of deindividuation, not a separate factor in this strand.
- Culture needs a specific point, such as collectivism and lower loafing.
- In scenario questions, link each factor to a detail (masks, darkness, size, a person's traits).

## Self-test

1. Define collective behaviour.
2. Give one example of prosocial and one of antisocial crowd behaviour.
3. Define social loafing.
4. What did Latané, Williams and Harkins's pseudo-groups show?
5. Name two conditions that increase deindividuation.
6. What did Zimbardo find about anonymous participants?
7. How did Johnson and Downing's findings differ from Zimbardo's, and why does it matter?
8. What did Mann find about crowds and baiting?
9. How does culture affect social loafing?
10. Give one personality trait linked to antisocial and one linked to prosocial crowd behaviour.
11. How can morality reduce the effect of deindividuation?
12. Suggest one way to reduce antisocial behaviour at a large event.

**Answers:**
1. How people behave when they are part of a group or crowd.
2. Prosocial, any one: helping an injured person; raising money; rescuing others. Antisocial, any one:
   vandalism; violence; looting; abuse.
3. Putting in less effort when working in a group than alone, because individual effort cannot be
   identified.
4. Effort per person fell even when people believed they were in a group but were alone, so the drop
   was due to reduced effort, not only poor coordination.
5. Anonymity and high arousal.
6. They gave shocks for about twice as long as identifiable participants.
7. Participants in nurse-like uniforms gave fewer shocks; it shows deindividuation makes people follow
   the norms of the situation, so it can increase prosocial behaviour.
8. Baiting was more likely when the crowd was large, when it was dark and when the crowd was far from
   the person.
9. Social loafing tends to be lower in collectivist cultures than in individualist ones.
10. Antisocial, any one: aggression; impulsiveness; sensation-seeking. Prosocial, any one: empathy;
    agreeableness.
11. A person with strong moral principles still judges actions as wrong when anonymous, so they are
    less likely to join antisocial behaviour.
12. Any one: increase identifiability (cameras, lighting, stewards); limit alcohol; promote positive
    group norms.
