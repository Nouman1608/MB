## What the specification lists

- Evaluate the impact and importance of a human resources strategy for a business and its stakeholders.
- Recommend and justify a human resources strategy for a business.

## Core recall

### Meaning

- **Strategy:** a long-term plan to achieve objectives.
- **Human resources strategy:** a long-term plan for obtaining, developing, rewarding and keeping the workforce the business needs.
- It looks years ahead; day-to-day tasks such as payroll are not strategy.

### Components

| Component | Key question |
|---|---|
| Workforce plan | How many people, which skills, when? |
| Recruitment and selection | Where from, and how chosen? |
| Training and development | How are skills built? |
| Reward and motivation | How are staff attracted and kept? |
| Organisation and structure | How are jobs arranged? |
| Leadership and culture | What style and values? |
| Employee relations | How to communicate and consult? |
| Exit | How are redundancy and retirement handled? |

### Fit

- **Vertical fit:** the strategy supports business objectives.
- **Horizontal fit:** the parts of the strategy work together.
- Also fit the external environment (labour market, law, technology) and resources (money, skills).

### Measures of impact

| Measure | Formula |
|---|---|
| Labour turnover | Leavers ÷ average staff × 100 |
| Labour productivity | Output (or revenue) ÷ employees |
| Labour cost share | Labour cost ÷ revenue × 100 |
| Absence rate | Days lost ÷ days possible × 100 |
| Cost per hire | Recruitment cost ÷ number hired |

### Importance

- Aligns people with objectives.
- Controls the largest cost.
- Builds hard-to-copy skills.
- Prepares for change.
- Reduces legal and shortage risk.
- Most important where labour is a large cost, skills are scarce or the business is changing.

### Stakeholders

| Stakeholder | Good strategy | Poor strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Owners | Profit, lower risk | Wasted cost |
| Employees | Training, careers | Uncertainty |
| Customers | Consistent service | Poor service |
| Managers | Clear direction | Fire-fighting |
| Community | Stable jobs | Job losses |

### Recommend and justify: five steps

1. Diagnose objectives and the workforce problem.
2. Choose a direction and components.
3. Support with case evidence and figures.
4. Weigh costs, risks and an alternative.
5. Judge, and say what would change the view.

## Quick worked reminder

Kestrel Home Care (invented): 120 staff, revenue £6,000,000, labour cost £3,000,000, turnover 25% (30 leavers at £5,500 each, £165,000). Strategy cost £146,000 (training £48,000, 2% pay rise £60,000, coordinator £38,000). Benefits: turnover to 15% saves 12 × £5,500 = £66,000; 5% more revenue at 25% contribution gives £75,000; total £141,000. Year 1 net −£5,000; year 2 (training halves) net +£19,000.

## Exam prompts and how to approach them

- **"Evaluate the importance of a human resources strategy":** link it to objectives, give a measure such as turnover or productivity, name two stakeholders and judge when it matters most.
- **"Recommend a strategy":** state the direction, support it with figures from the case, weigh a risk and an alternative, and give a judgement.
- **"Analyse fit":** explain vertical fit with objectives and horizontal fit between recruitment, training and pay, using an example of a clash.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Strategy | A long-term plan to achieve objectives |
| Workforce plan | A forecast of the number and skills of staff needed |
| Vertical fit | The match between the strategy and business objectives |
| Horizontal fit | The match between parts of the strategy |
| Labour turnover | The percentage of staff who leave in a period |
| Labour productivity | Output per employee |
| Retention | Keeping staff in the business |
| Succession planning | Preparing people to fill key roles in future |

## Exam traps

- A strategy is long term and linked to objectives; a list of tasks is not enough.
- Give costs and benefits with figures, and say that non-financial benefits count too.
- Recommend one strategy and justify it; consider an alternative.
- Check that parts of the strategy do not contradict each other.
- Judge: say whether it is worthwhile, and when.

## Self-test

1. Define a human resources strategy.
2. Give three components of a human resources strategy.
3. Distinguish between vertical fit and horizontal fit.
4. A business has 60 staff and 18 leave. Calculate labour turnover.
5. Revenue is £2,400,000 and there are 60 employees. Calculate revenue per employee.
6. Labour cost is £1,080,000 on revenue of £2,400,000. Calculate the labour cost share.
7. Give one reason why a human resources strategy is important to owners.
8. Give one effect on employees of a poor strategy.
9. State one reason why a strategy may be less important for a very small firm.
10. Give one risk of recommending a large training programme.
11. Why should the external environment be considered?
12. A strategy costs £36,000 and saves £40,500 in leaver costs. Calculate the net effect.

### Answers

1. A long-term plan for obtaining, developing, rewarding and keeping the workforce needed to achieve the business's objectives.
2. Any three: workforce plan, recruitment and selection, training, reward, structure, leadership and culture, employee relations, exit.
3. Vertical fit is the match between the strategy and business objectives; horizontal fit is the match between the parts of the strategy.
4. 18 ÷ 60 × 100 = **30%**.
5. £2,400,000 ÷ 60 = **£40,000**.
6. £1,080,000 ÷ £2,400,000 × 100 = **45%**.
7. It aligns people with objectives, which raises productivity and profit and lowers risk.
8. Uncertainty and low morale, because training and careers are unclear.
9. A small firm with a few staff and stable tasks can manage informally.
10. It is costly, and trained staff may leave for rivals, or the benefit may take years.
11. Labour supply, law and technology change what is possible and what it costs.
12. £40,500 − £36,000 = **£4,500** gain.
