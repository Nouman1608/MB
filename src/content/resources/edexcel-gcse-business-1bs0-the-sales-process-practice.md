## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Define the term customer engagement. **[2]**

**2.** A business offers free repairs for two years after a customer buys a washing machine. Which **one** part of the sales process is this? **[1]**

A: Product knowledge

B: Speed and efficiency of service

C: Post-sales service

D: Customer engagement

**3.** Which **two** of the following are parts of the sales process named in the specification? **[2]**

A: Product knowledge

B: Tariffs

C: Post-sales service

D: Flow production

E: Branding

**4.** Elmhurst Kitchens had 640 visits to its showroom last month and took 96 orders. Calculate the conversion rate. **[2]**

**5.** Brightside Bank's branch recorded the waiting times of five customers: 6, 9, 4, 11 and 5 minutes.

**(a)** Calculate the mean waiting time. **[2]**

**(b)** After opening a second counter, the mean waiting time fell to 4.2 minutes. Calculate the percentage change in the mean waiting time. **[2]**

**6.** Pippin Books replies to every online review within a day. It recently made its children's section larger after many customers asked for more picture books. State **one** way Pippin Books has changed its business in response to customer feedback. **[1]**

**7.** Explain **one** reason why providing good customer service is important to a business. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Summit Outdoor sells walking boots costing up to £180. Its new Saturday staff know little about fit, waterproofing or the different types of walking. Outline **one** way this could affect Summit Outdoor. **[2]**

**9.** Explain **one** benefit to a business of offering post-sales service on expensive products. **[3]**

**10.** Rocket Burgers runs a drive-through. Last month it served 4,500 orders, of which 180 were wrong when customers checked them. Customers have also complained about long queues at lunchtime.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of orders that were wrong. **[2]**

**(b)** Analyse how improving the speed and efficiency of its service could affect Rocket Burgers. **[6]**

**11.** Tidewell Garden Centre wants to improve its sales process and can afford one option.

Option A: product knowledge training for staff, costing £5,000 a year. The centre receives 3,000 enquiries a year from customers asking for advice; 30% become sales at present, and training is expected to raise this to 36%. Each sale from an enquiry has a contribution of £40.

Option B: a delivery and planting service for trees and large shrubs. Van and staff costs would be £9,000 a year. The centre expects 600 customers a year to pay £25 each for the service.

Justify which option Tidewell Garden Centre should choose. **[9]**

**12.** Northfield Car Care services cars and has 1,800 customers. Each year 55% of them return for their next service, spending an average of £280, with a contribution of 40%. The owner plans to spend £14,000 a year on text reminders, a free courtesy car and a 10% loyalty discount on every third service. She expects 65% of customers to return each year.

Evaluate whether Northfield Car Care should introduce the plan. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** Building a relationship with customers through the way the business interacts with them [1], for example by greeting them personally, replying to comments or keeping in touch through loyalty schemes [1]. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A [1]. C [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 96 ÷ 640 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** (6 + 9 + 4 + 11 + 5) ÷ 5 = 35 ÷ 5 [1] = **7 minutes** [1]

**(b)** (4.2 − 7) ÷ 7 × 100 [1] = **−40%**, a fall of 40% [1] **[4]**

**6.** It made its children's section larger after customers asked for more picture books [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* Replying to reviews is engagement with feedback, but the question asks how the business changed, so the larger section is the better answer.

**7.** Customers who receive good service are more likely to return [1], so the business gains repeat sales from customers it does not have to pay to attract again [1]. Satisfied customers also recommend the business and leave positive reviews, bringing in new customers at little cost [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Customers spending up to £180 want advice on fit and waterproofing, and staff who cannot give it may sell the wrong boots [1], which could lead to returns and complaints, or customers leaving to buy from a rival or online, reducing Summit's sales [1]. **[2]**

**9.** Customers worry about what happens if an expensive product goes wrong [1], so a guarantee or repair service gives them confidence to buy [1]. This can win sales from rivals that offer less support and builds loyalty, so customers return when they next need the product [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** 180 ÷ 4,500 × 100 [1] = **4%** [1]

**(b)** Shorter lunchtime queues mean customers who are short of time are less likely to drive away, so Rocket can serve more orders at its busiest time [1]. More orders served per hour raises sales revenue [1]. Cutting the 4% of wrong orders reduces the cost of remaking food and giving refunds [1], and customers who get the right order first time are more satisfied and likely to return [1]. Fewer complaints and better reviews could attract new customers from rival fast-food outlets [1]. However, speeding up service may need extra staff or new screens for orders, which raises costs, so Rocket should make sure that the extra sales are greater than the extra cost [1]. **[8]**

*Examiner insight:* Speed and efficiency are different: speed is about queues; efficiency is about getting orders right. Strong answers deal with both.

**11.** Option A: extra sales = 3,000 × (36% − 30%) = 180 [1]; extra contribution = 180 × £40 = £7,200 [1]; net gain = £7,200 − £5,000 = **£2,200** [1]. Option B: income = 600 × £25 = £15,000 [1]; net gain = £15,000 − £9,000 = **£6,000** [1]. Option B gains £3,800 more [1]. Option B may also increase sales of trees and large shrubs, because customers who could not transport them can now buy them, which is not included in the £6,000 [1]. Option A improves advice for every customer, which may raise loyalty and reputation beyond the 180 extra sales [1]. **Judgement:** choose Option B, because it earns more on the figures and is likely to bring extra sales of large plants as well; its risk is that 600 customers is only a forecast, so the centre could trial the service in spring, its busiest planting season, before committing to a van all year [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* The best Justify answers notice a benefit not shown in the figures, such as extra plant sales, and use it to support the choice.

**12.** Extra returning customers = 1,800 × (65% − 55%) = 180 [1]. Contribution per service = £280 × 40% = £112 [1]. Extra contribution = 180 × £112 = **£20,160** [1], minus £14,000 = a gain of **£6,160** a year [1]. For: engagement through reminders and a courtesy car makes servicing easier, so customers are less likely to try another garage [1]. Loyal customers also recommend the garage, which may bring new customers not included in the figures [1]. Against: the 10% discount reduces contribution on some services already being paid for by customers who would have returned anyway, so the true cost may be higher than £14,000 [1]. If returns rose only to 60%, extra contribution would be £10,080 and the plan would lose £3,920 [1]. **Conclusion:** introduce the plan, because it gains £6,160 on the forecast and builds the loyalty that a garage depends on, but it depends on returns rising by most of the 10 percentage points; Northfield could start with the cheaper reminders and courtesy car and add the discount only if needed [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* Evaluate answers that test what happens if the forecast is missed show the examiner exactly what the decision depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing post-sales service with customer engagement when selecting from a case.
- Treating speed and efficiency as the same thing.
- Describing feedback being collected but not acted on.
- Forgetting the cost of a service improvement when calculating its benefit.
- Saying good service matters without naming a benefit such as repeat sales or reviews.
