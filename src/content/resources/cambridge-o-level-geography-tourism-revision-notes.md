Condensed for revision. For full explanations and the worked case
study, read the [Tourism study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-tourism/).

## The syllabus in three lines

1. **Growth**: describe and explain growth using physical and human
   attractions.
2. **Impact**: evaluate benefits and disadvantages for receiving areas.
3. **Management**: show why careful management makes tourism
   sustainable. Case study: an area where tourism is important.

## Why tourism grows

| Type | Examples |
|---|---|
| Physical attractions | Beaches, warm climate, coral reefs, mountains, snow, wildlife, waterfalls, volcanoes |
| Human attractions | Historic sites, religious sites, museums, cities, festivals, theme parks, culture and food |
| Enabling factors | Higher incomes, paid holidays, cheaper flights, internet booking, social media, government promotion |

Attractions explain *where* people go; enabling factors explain *why
numbers rise over time*. Use both.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Receiving area | The place that tourists visit |
| Multiplier effect | Tourist spending creates jobs and income that are spent again locally, spreading the benefit |
| Leakage | Tourist money that leaves the area, such as profits to foreign-owned hotels or payments for imported food |
| Seasonal employment | Jobs that exist only in the peak tourist season |
| Carrying capacity | The number of visitors an area can take before it is damaged or the visitor experience suffers |
| Sustainable tourism | Tourism that protects the environment and culture so it can continue in future |
| Ecotourism | Small-scale, low-impact tourism that funds conservation and benefits local people |
| Zoning | Dividing an area so that intensive use and protection happen in different places |

## Impacts at a glance

- **Economic +**: jobs, foreign currency, taxes, multiplier effect.
- **Economic -**: seasonal and low-paid jobs, leakage, over-dependence.
- **Social +**: new infrastructure, money for heritage.
- **Social -**: rising house prices, crowding, loss of traditions.
- **Environmental +**: fees fund conservation.
- **Environmental -**: reef and dune damage, litter, sewage, water use,
  footpath erosion, flight emissions.

**Who controls tourism decides the balance**: local ownership means a
stronger multiplier and less leakage.

## Management measures (say why each works)

| Measure | Why it is sustainable |
|---|---|
| Visitor quotas or permits | Keep numbers below carrying capacity |
| Zoning and protected areas | Protect the most fragile habitats |
| Ecotourism | Funds conservation and gives local people a reason to protect it |
| Local ownership and training | Keeps more income in the area |
| Off-season promotion | Spreads pressure through the year |
| Sewage and waste systems | Stop pollution of the attraction itself |

## Case study outline: the Maldives

- About 1,200 low-lying coral islands in the Indian Ocean; capital Malé.
- Tourism is the largest industry; resorts began in the early 1970s.
- Attractions: beaches, coral reefs, warm clear water, luxury resorts.
- "One island, one resort": resorts on uninhabited islands.
- Problems: foreign ownership and imports cause leakage; waste taken to
  Thilafushi landfill island; reef damage from construction and
  divers; coral bleaching in marine heatwaves and sea-level rise
  threaten the attractions themselves.

## Exam traps

- Describing attractions when the question asks why tourism has *grown*.
- One-sided answers to "evaluate".
- Measures named without explaining how they protect the area.
- A case study with no place-specific detail.

## Self-test

1. Give two physical and two human attractions.
2. State two reasons why international tourism has grown.
3. Define leakage.
4. Explain the multiplier effect in one sentence.
5. Why does local ownership of hotels matter?
6. Name two sustainable management measures and say how one works.
7. Give one environmental problem caused by tourism in the Maldives.

**Answers:** 1. Physical: for example beaches, coral reefs, mountains;
human: for example historic sites, museums, festivals. 2. Any two of:
higher incomes, more paid holiday, cheaper flights, internet booking,
government promotion. 3. Tourist money that leaves the receiving area,
for example as profits to foreign-owned companies or payments for
imports. 4. Money spent by tourists pays wages and suppliers, who spend
it again locally, creating more jobs and income. 5. Profits stay in the
area and local suppliers are more likely to be used, so leakage is lower
and the multiplier effect stronger. 6. For example, quotas keep visitor
numbers below carrying capacity; zoning protects fragile areas from
heavy use. 7. Any one of: large amounts of waste taken to Thilafushi;
reef damage from construction, anchors or divers. (Coral bleaching is
caused by marine heatwaves, not by tourism itself, though it threatens
the industry.)
