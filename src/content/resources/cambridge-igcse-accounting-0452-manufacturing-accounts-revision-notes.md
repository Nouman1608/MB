[5.4 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-manufacturing-accounts/);

Amounts are in dollars ($).

## The six syllabus points

1. Direct and indirect costs.
2. Direct material, direct labour, prime cost and factory overheads.
3. Adjustments for work in progress.
4. Calculating factory cost of production.
5. Preparing manufacturing accounts, statements of profit or loss and statements
   of financial position.
6. Adjustments as in 5.1.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Direct cost | A cost that can be traced to each unit produced |
| Indirect cost | A factory cost that cannot be traced to a particular unit |
| Direct material | Cost of raw materials consumed in production |
| Direct labour | Wages of workers who make the product |
| Prime cost | Direct material + direct labour |
| Factory overheads | All indirect factory costs |
| Work in progress (WIP) | Partly finished goods in the factory at a year end |
| Factory cost of production | Cost of the goods completed in the year |
| Finished goods | Completed goods awaiting sale |

## The chain of formulas

```
Raw materials consumed = opening raw materials + purchases + carriage inwards
                         − purchases returns − closing raw materials
Prime cost             = raw materials consumed + direct labour
Total production cost  = prime cost + factory overheads
Factory cost of production = total production cost + opening WIP − closing WIP
Cost of sales          = opening finished goods + factory cost of production
                         (+ purchases of finished goods, if any)
                         − closing finished goods
```

## Manufacturing account skeleton

```
Inventory of raw materials (opening)                     X
Purchases of raw materials + carriage inwards − returns  X
Inventory of raw materials (closing)                    (X)
Cost of raw materials consumed                           X
Direct labour                                            X
Prime cost                                               X
Factory overheads (each listed)                          X
Opening work in progress                                 X
Closing work in progress                                (X)
Factory cost of production                               X
```

## Where each cost goes

| Manufacturing account | Statement of profit or loss |
|---|---|
| Raw materials and carriage inwards on them | Office salaries and office expenses |
| Production workers' wages | Selling and distribution, carriage outwards |
| Supervisor's, cleaners' and maintenance wages | Depreciation of office equipment and delivery vehicles |
| Factory rent, power, insurance | Office share of rent, power, insurance |
| Depreciation of factory machinery and buildings | Irrecoverable debts and allowance changes |

**Shared costs:** adjust for accruals or prepayments first, then split by the
basis given (fraction, percentage or floor area).

## Three inventories

| Inventory | Used in | Shown in the statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials | Raw materials consumed | Current assets |
| Work in progress | Factory cost of production | Current assets |
| Finished goods | Cost of sales | Current assets |

## Worked reminders

**Raw materials consumed.** Opening 3,500; purchases 27,200; carriage inwards 400;
returns 600; closing 4,100. Consumed = 3,500 + 27,200 + 400 − 600 − 4,100 = **26,400**.

**Shared cost.** Rent paid 9,000, of which 1,000 is prepaid; split 4/5 factory.
Charge 8,000: factory **6,400**, office **1,600**.

**WIP effect.** Closing WIP 500 too high: factory cost of production 500 too low;
gross profit, profit and current assets 500 too high.

## Effects of errors

| Error | Factory cost of production | Gross profit |
|---|---|---|
| Closing raw materials overstated | Understated | Overstated |
| Closing WIP understated | Overstated | Understated |
| Office cost put in factory overheads | Overstated | Understated (profit for the year unchanged) |
| Factory depreciation omitted | Understated | Overstated |

## Exam traps

- **Purchases are not consumption.** Adjust for opening and closing raw materials.
- **Carriage inwards on raw materials** is part of direct material.
- **Supervisors are indirect**, even though they work in the factory.
- **Opening WIP is added, closing WIP deducted**, after factory overheads.
- **Finished goods** belong in the statement of profit or loss, not the manufacturing account.
- **Purchases of finished goods** for resale go in cost of sales, not the
  manufacturing account.
- **Three inventories** must each appear under current assets.
- **5.1 adjustments** still apply: accruals, prepayments, depreciation,
  irrecoverable debts, drawings.

## Self-test

1. Define prime cost.
2. Is the wage of a factory cleaner a direct or indirect cost?
3. Raw materials: opening 5,000; purchases 30,000; closing 6,000. Calculate the cost consumed.
4. Where is carriage outwards shown?
5. Prime cost 80,000; factory overheads 25,000; opening WIP 3,000; closing WIP 4,500.
   Calculate factory cost of production.
6. Why is closing WIP deducted?
7. Name the three inventories in a manufacturer's statement of financial position.
8. Factory cost of production 100,000; opening finished goods 8,000; closing finished
   goods 10,000. Calculate cost of sales.
9. Power 6,000 with 600 accrued, split 3/4 factory. Calculate the factory charge.
10. Where is depreciation of a delivery van shown?
11. Closing WIP is understated by 300. State the effect on gross profit.
12. Is depreciation of factory machinery part of prime cost?

### Answers

1. Direct material plus direct labour.
2. Indirect: it cannot be traced to a unit.
3. 5,000 + 30,000 − 6,000 = **29,000**.
4. In the statement of profit or loss, as a selling and distribution expense.
5. 80,000 + 25,000 + 3,000 − 4,500 = **103,500**.
6. Its costs were incurred this year but the goods are not yet finished.
7. Raw materials, work in progress and finished goods.
8. 8,000 + 100,000 − 10,000 = **98,000**.
9. (6,000 + 600) × 3/4 = **4,950**.
10. In the statement of profit or loss.
11. Gross profit is **understated by 300**.
12. No: it is a factory overhead.
