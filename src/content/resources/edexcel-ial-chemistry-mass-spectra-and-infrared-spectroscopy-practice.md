This question set tests Topic 10D, Mass spectra and IR, of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Chemistry (XCH11/YCH11) specification, Issue 1, September 2017, namely outcomes 10.21 to 10.23 (Core Practical 8). It is assessed in the IAS written examination for Unit 2: Energetics, Group Chemistry, Halogenoalkanes and Alcohols. If stuck, use the [Topic 10D study guide](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-mass-spectra-and-infrared-spectroscopy/) or the [Topic 10D revision notes](/resources/edexcel-ial-chemistry-mass-spectra-and-infrared-spectroscopy-revision-notes/).

Data for this set. Use integer masses: H = 1, C = 12, O = 16, Cl = 35 or 37, Br = 79 or 81. Use these IR ranges (cm⁻¹): C–H alkane 2962–2853; =C–H alkene 3095–3010; C–H aldehyde 2900–2820 and 2775–2700; C=C 1669–1645; O–H alcohol 3550–3230 (broad); O–H carboxylic acid 3300–2500 (very broad); C=O aldehyde 1740–1720, ketone 1720–1700, carboxylic acid 1725–1700; C–Cl 800–600; C–Br 600–500; N–H amine 3500–3300.

## Questions

**1.** This question is about the mass spectrum of 2-methylpropane, (CH₃)₃CH.

**(a)** State what is meant by the molecular ion. **[1]**
**(b)** The base peak is at m/z 43. Write an equation for the fragmentation that forms this ion. **[2]**

**2.** State the bond and the wavenumber range of an IR absorption that shows the functional group in each compound.

**(a)** hex-1-ene **[1]**
**(b)** propan-1-amine **[1]**
**(c)** 1-chloropentane **[1]**

**3.** Predict three features of the IR spectrum of 4-hydroxypentan-2-one, CH₃COCH₂CH(OH)CH₃. Include one feature that is absent. **[3]**

**4.** A sample is one of pentan-1-ol, pentan-2-ol or pentan-3-ol. Its mass spectrum shows a small peak at m/z 88, the base peak at 59 and a peak at 29. There are no peaks at 31, 45 or 73.

**(a)** Identify the ion at m/z 59 and the radical lost when it forms. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the alcohol and explain why the other two are ruled out. **[2]**

**5.** In Core Practical 6, 2-methylpropan-2-ol is converted into 2-chloro-2-methylpropane with concentrated hydrochloric acid. The product's mass spectrum shows peaks at m/z 92 and 94 (heights 3 : 1), peaks at 77 and 79 (heights 3 : 1), and the base peak at 57.

**(a)** Explain what the peaks at 92 and 94 show. **[2]**
**(b)** Identify the ions at m/z 77 and 57. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe how the IR spectrum of the product shows that no 2-methylpropan-2-ol remains, and give one absorption expected for the product. **[2]**

**6.** Suggest how each pair could be told apart using the method stated.

**(a)** Hex-1-ene and cyclohexane. Explain why the molecular ion peak does not help, and give an IR difference. **[2]**
**(b)** Hexanal and hexan-2-one. Give one IR difference and one mass spectrum difference. **[2]**
**(c)** Propan-1-amine and propan-1-ol, using IR only. **[2]**

**7.** A 2.90 g sample of compound R contains 1.80 g of carbon, 0.30 g of hydrogen and 0.80 g of oxygen. Its molecular ion is at m/z 58. Its IR spectrum has a broad absorption at 3400 cm⁻¹, a medium absorption at 1650 cm⁻¹ and a weak absorption at 3080 cm⁻¹. Nothing absorbs between 1680 and 1750 cm⁻¹. The mass spectrum also has peaks at m/z 41 and 31.

**(a)** Calculate the empirical formula of R. **[2]**
**(b)** Deduce the molecular formula of R. **[1]**
**(c)** Deduce the functional groups in R from the IR data. Include the evidence from the missing absorption. **[3]**
**(d)** Suggest the structure and name of R, using the peaks at 41 and 31. **[2]**

**8.** Cyclohexanone is made from cyclohexene in three steps:

cyclohexene → bromocyclohexane → cyclohexanol → cyclohexanone

For each step, give one IR absorption that disappears and one that appears. **[6]**

**9.** 2-bromobutane, CH₃CHBrCH₂CH₃, reacts with potassium hydroxide. Under one set of conditions it forms product A; under another it forms product B.

**(a)** Predict the m/z values and relative heights of the molecular ion peaks of 2-bromobutane, and explain your answer. **[3]**
**(b)** A has its molecular ion at m/z 74 and a broad IR absorption at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹. Give the conditions used and name A. **[2]**
**(c)** The mass spectrum of A has a large peak at m/z 45. Identify the ion and write an equation for its formation from the molecular ion. **[2]**
**(d)** B has its molecular ion at m/z 56 and no O–H absorption. Give one IR absorption expected for B. **[1]**

**10.** Four colourless liquids, W, X, Y and Z, are pentan-1-ol, pentanal, pentan-2-one and 1-chloropentane, in no particular order.

| Test | W | X | Y | Z |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Warm with acidified potassium dichromate(VI) | turns green | turns green | stays orange | stays orange |
| Warm with Benedict's solution | no change | red precipitate | no change | no change |
| Warm with aqueous silver nitrate in ethanol | no change | no change | no change | white precipitate forms slowly |

**(a)** Identify W, X, Y and Z, giving a reason for each. **[4]**
**(b)** State two features of the IR spectrum of Y that confirm it is a ketone. **[2]**
**(c)** Give a test, with its result, that shows W contains an –OH group. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The ion formed when a whole molecule loses one electron, M⁺ [1].
**(b)** (CH₃)₃CH⁺ → (CH₃)₂CH⁺ + CH₃• : the C₃H₇⁺ ion with its positive charge [1]; the uncharged methyl radical, with masses balancing, 58 = 43 + 15 [1].
*Examiner insight:* An equation that puts the charge on the methyl group, or leaves the fragment uncharged, usually loses credit, because only the positive ion is detected.

**2. (a)** C=C, 1669–1645 cm⁻¹ [1].
**(b)** N–H, 3500–3300 cm⁻¹ [1].
**(c)** C–Cl, 800–600 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* "A peak at 1650" alone is weak; name the bond and quote the range from the data given.

**3.** Broad O–H (alcohol) at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹ [1]. Strong C=O (ketone) at 1720–1700 cm⁻¹ [1]. No aldehyde C–H band at 2775–2700 cm⁻¹ (or no very broad acid O–H at 3300–2500) [1].
*Examiner insight:* With two functional groups present, predict an absorption for each.

**4. (a)** 88 − 59 = 29, so C₂H₅• is lost [1]. The ion is CH₃CH₂CHOH⁺, C₃H₇O⁺ [1].
**(b)** **Pentan-3-ol**, with the OH on the middle carbon [1]. Pentan-1-ol would give CH₂OH⁺ at 31, and pentan-2-ol would give CH₃CHOH⁺ at 45 or lose CH₃• to give 73; pentan-3-ol has an ethyl group on each side of the C–OH carbon, so it loses only C₂H₅• [1].
*Examiner insight:* Rule out the other isomers using specific missing peaks; a name with no reason earns little.

**5. (a)** Two peaks two units apart in a 3 : 1 ratio show one chlorine atom, from ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl [1]. They are the molecular ions of C₄H₉Cl: 92 with ³⁵Cl and 94 with ³⁷Cl [1].
**(b)** m/z 77 is (CH₃)₂CCl⁺ (with ³⁵Cl), formed by loss of CH₃• [1]. m/z 57 is (CH₃)₃C⁺, formed by loss of Cl•; it is a single peak because it has no chlorine [1].
**(c)** No broad O–H absorption at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹ [1]. A C–Cl absorption at 800–600 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote the ratio and say "one chlorine atom"; "contains a halogen" is too vague for credit.

**6. (a)** Both are C₆H₁₂, so both have M⁺ at m/z 84 [1]. Hex-1-ene absorbs at 1669–1645 cm⁻¹ (C=C) and 3095–3010 cm⁻¹ (=C–H); cyclohexane does not [1].
**(b)** IR: hexanal has aldehyde C–H at 2775–2700 cm⁻¹ and hexan-2-one does not [1]. MS: hexanal can give CHO⁺ at m/z 29; hexan-2-one gives CH₃CO⁺ at 43 [1].
**(c)** Propan-1-amine: N–H absorption at 3500–3300 cm⁻¹ [1]. Propan-1-ol: broad O–H absorption at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹ [1].
*Examiner insight:* Describe both compounds in each pair; stating only what one shows gives incomplete evidence.

**7. (a)** Moles: C 1.80/12 = 0.150; H 0.30/1 = 0.30; O 0.80/16 = 0.050 [1]. Divide by 0.050: 3 : 6 : 1, so the empirical formula is **C₃H₆O** [1].
**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 36 + 6 + 16 = 58, which equals M⁺, so the molecular formula is **C₃H₆O** [1].
**(c)** Broad 3400 cm⁻¹ is in the alcohol O–H range [1]. 1650 cm⁻¹ is C=C, supported by =C–H at 3080 cm⁻¹ [1]. No absorption at 1680–1750 cm⁻¹ means no C=O, so R is not propanal or propanone [1].
**(d)** **CH₂=CHCH₂OH, prop-2-en-1-ol** [1]. m/z 31 is CH₂OH⁺; m/z 41 is CH₂=CHCH₂⁺ (C₃H₅⁺), from loss of OH•, 58 − 17 = 41 [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the division step and ratios in part (a); a bare formula gives no evidence of method.

**8.** Step 1: C=C at 1669–1645 cm⁻¹ (or =C–H at 3095–3010) disappears [1]; C–Br at 600–500 cm⁻¹ appears [1].
Step 2: C–Br at 600–500 cm⁻¹ disappears [1]; broad O–H at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹ appears [1].
Step 3: O–H at 3550–3230 cm⁻¹ disappears [1]; C=O (ketone) at 1720–1700 cm⁻¹ appears [1].
*Examiner insight:* Quote a range for every absorption; a bond name without a wavenumber is usually not enough.

**9. (a)** m/z 136 and 138 [1], of about equal height [1]. The formula is C₄H₉Br, and bromine is roughly half ⁷⁹Br and half ⁸¹Br, giving C₄H₉⁷⁹Br⁺ and C₄H₉⁸¹Br⁺ [1].
**(b)** Aqueous potassium hydroxide, heated [1]. **A is butan-2-ol** [1].
**(c)** CH₃CHOH⁺ (C₂H₅O⁺) [1]. CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃⁺ → CH₃CHOH⁺ + C₂H₅• [1].
**(d)** C=C at 1669–1645 cm⁻¹ (or =C–H at 3095–3010 cm⁻¹) [1].
*Examiner insight:* In (b), "KOH" alone does not show which reaction happened; say aqueous for substitution, as ethanolic gives the alkene.

**10. (a)** W is **pentan-1-ol**: oxidised by dichromate but not an aldehyde [1]. X is **pentanal**: oxidised and gives a red precipitate with Benedict's [1]. Y is **pentan-2-one**: not oxidised and has no halogen [1]. Z is **1-chloropentane**: hydrolysis releases chloride ions, which give white silver chloride [1].
**(b)** Strong C=O at 1720–1700 cm⁻¹ [1]. No aldehyde C–H at 2775–2700 cm⁻¹ and no broad O–H [1].
**(c)** Add PCl₅: misty fumes of hydrogen chloride [1].
*Examiner insight:* Each identification needs the observation that supports it; a list of names with no reasons gains little.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the base peak the molecular ion.
- Leaving the charge off fragment ions, or putting it on the radical.
- Quoting a wavenumber with no bond, or a bond with no wavenumber.
- Calling the 3300–2500 cm⁻¹ acid band an alcohol O–H.
- Identifying a carbonyl as an aldehyde without the 2775–2700 cm⁻¹ evidence.
- Missing the 3 : 1 (Cl) or 1 : 1 (Br) pattern, or swapping them.
- Writing "KOH" without stating aqueous or ethanolic.
