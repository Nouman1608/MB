These notes condense sub-topic **6.1 Photosynthesis** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Statements 1 to 9 are Core. Statements 10 (balanced equation) and 11 (limiting factors) are **Extended only**. For the full explanations and worked examples, use the [photosynthesis study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-photosynthesis-cambridge-igcse-biology/); for the whole topic, the [plant nutrition revision notes](/resources/igcse-biology-0610-plant-nutrition-revision-notes/); for exam-style questions, the [plant nutrition practice questions](/resources/igcse-biology-plant-nutrition-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | The process by which plants synthesise carbohydrates from raw materials using energy from light |
| Raw materials | Carbon dioxide (from air, through stomata) and water (from soil, through root hairs and xylem) |
| Chlorophyll | Green pigment found in chloroplasts |
| Role of chlorophyll | Transfers energy from light into energy in chemicals, for the synthesis of carbohydrates |
| Destarching | Keeping a plant in the dark for about two days so its leaves use up their starch |
| Control | A set-up identical to the test except for the one factor being investigated |
| Hydrogencarbonate indicator | Shows carbon dioxide concentration: yellow (higher), orange-red (normal air), purple (lower) |
| Limiting factor (Extended only) | The factor in shortest supply, which stops the rate rising further |

## Equations

- Word equation (Core): carbon dioxide + water → glucose + oxygen, **in the presence of light and chlorophyll**.
- Balanced equation (Extended only): 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂.
- Ratio check: 6 carbon dioxide used per glucose made; carbon dioxide used and oxygen released are equal in number.

## Carbohydrate uses: one chain

```
glucose → respiration (energy)
glucose → sucrose → transported in phloem
                  → starch (store) | cellulose (cell walls) | nectar (attracts insects)
```

Leaves can also turn glucose straight into starch for short-term storage. Starch: insoluble, so it stays in cells as a store. Sucrose: soluble, so it travels in phloem sap.

## Mineral ions: two-link chains

- **Nitrate ions** → make amino acids → make proteins for new cells. Shortage: poor, stunted growth.
- **Magnesium ions** → make chlorophyll → absorbs light. Shortage: yellow leaves, less light absorbed, less carbohydrate made.

Write "nitrate ions" and "magnesium ions", not "nitrogen" and "magnesium".

## Investigation checklists

### Need for chlorophyll, light or carbon dioxide (6.1.7)

- Destarch first; test one leaf to confirm no starch.
- Remove one factor from part of the plant: white areas of a variegated leaf (chlorophyll), foil cover on both sides (light), sodium hydroxide or soda lime in a sealed bag (carbon dioxide).
- Control: same set-up with the factor present (green area, uncovered area, bag with water instead of absorber).
- Starch test, then: blue-black = photosynthesis happened; orange-brown = it did not.

### Rate (6.1.8)

| You vary | How | Keep constant |
|---|---|---|
| Light intensity | lamp distance | temperature (water bath), sodium hydrogencarbonate concentration |
| Carbon dioxide | concentration of sodium hydrogencarbonate solution | lamp distance, temperature |
| Temperature | water bath at set temperatures | lamp distance, sodium hydrogencarbonate concentration |

Measure: bubbles per minute, or better, volume of gas in a gas syringe or measuring cylinder in a set time. Rate = volume ÷ time (cm³ per min).

Describing a temperature graph: rises to an optimum, then falls because enzymes are denatured.

### Hydrogencarbonate indicator (6.1.9)

| Conditions for an aquatic plant | Balance | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Bright light | photosynthesis > respiration | purple |
| Dim light | photosynthesis = respiration | stays orange-red |
| Dark | respiration only | yellow |

Animals in the tube (such as snails) only add carbon dioxide. A tube with no organism is the control.

## Limiting factors in real conditions (Extended only)

| Clue in the question | Usual limiting factor |
|---|---|
| Dawn, dusk, shade, cloudy day | light intensity |
| Cold but bright (winter, frost) | temperature |
| Warm and bright, or a closed glasshouse in sunshine | carbon dioxide concentration |

On a graph: rising part = the factor on the x-axis is limiting; flat part = something else is. Prove it by pointing to a higher curve where only one other factor was raised.

## Exam traps

- Light is an energy source and a condition, not a raw material.
- "Chlorophyll makes energy" scores zero; it **transfers** energy.
- Plants respire all the time, in light and dark.
- A control must be described, not just mentioned.
- Quote data with units when you describe a trend.
- Extended: give one limiting factor per point on a graph, with the evidence.

## Self-test

1. Name the two raw materials of photosynthesis and state where each enters the plant.
2. State the role of chlorophyll in photosynthesis.
3. Which carbohydrate is transported in the phloem?
4. Why is starch, rather than glucose, used as the store in a seed?
5. A plant is grown without magnesium ions. Explain why its rate of photosynthesis falls.
6. Why must a plant be destarched before testing whether light is needed for photosynthesis?
7. Describe a suitable control for showing that carbon dioxide is needed for photosynthesis.
8. Pondweed releases 3.6 cm³ of gas in 8 minutes. Calculate the rate.
9. Name the colour hydrogencarbonate indicator turns when a tube containing only a water snail is left in light.
10. Explain why indicator in a tube with pondweed in dim light may not change colour.
11. (Extended) How many molecules of oxygen are released when 4 molecules of glucose are made?
12. (Extended) A tomato crop in a heated, brightly lit, closed glasshouse stops increasing its rate of photosynthesis in the afternoon. Suggest the limiting factor and one way to overcome it.

### Answers

1. Carbon dioxide, which enters the leaves through the stomata; water, which enters through the root hair cells.
2. It absorbs light and transfers energy from light into energy in chemicals, used to make carbohydrates.
3. Sucrose.
4. Starch is insoluble, so it stays in the cells as a store.
5. Magnesium ions are needed to make chlorophyll; with less chlorophyll, less light is absorbed, so less energy is transferred for photosynthesis.
6. So that any starch found at the end must have been made during the experiment.
7. An identical plant in an identical sealed bag with water in place of the carbon dioxide absorber, kept in the same light and temperature.
8. 3.6 ÷ 8 = **0.45 cm³ per min**.
9. Yellow: the snail respires and adds carbon dioxide.
10. The rate of photosynthesis equals the rate of respiration, so carbon dioxide taken in equals carbon dioxide released.
11. 4 × 6 = **24 molecules**.
12. Carbon dioxide concentration (the plants have used it up); add carbon dioxide, for example by burning a fuel or releasing it from a supply.
