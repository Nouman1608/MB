## Core recall

### Six reasons to train (from the specification)

Memory hook: **P**eople **T**rained **M**ake **R**eally **Q**uality **S**ervice.

1. **P**roductivity: faster work, fewer errors, lower cost per unit.
2. **T**echnological change: staff can use new machines and software, so the investment is not wasted.
3. **M**otivation: staff feel valued and see a route to promotion.
4. **R**etention: staff with a future in the business stay, saving recruitment costs.
5. **Q**uality: procedures followed, fewer faults and returns.
6. **S**ervice to customers: product knowledge and communication keep customers loyal.

### Drawbacks of training

- Direct costs: fees, materials, travel.
- Lost output while staff train, and while experienced staff teach.
- Trained staff may leave for a better-paid job, taking the skills with them.

### Induction

Training for **new starters**: site layout, health and safety, policies, introductions, basic tasks.

| Benefit of induction | Consequence |
|---|---|
| Productive sooner | Less time wasted finding out how things work |
| Works safely from the start | Fewer accidents and their costs |
| Feels welcome | More likely to stay, so recruitment money is not wasted |
| Learns the business's standards | Consistent quality and service |

### On-the-job versus off-the-job

| | On-the-job | Off-the-job |
|---|---|---|
| Where | At the workplace, doing the job | Away from the normal workplace |
| Examples | Shadowing, coaching by a supervisor | College course, supplier's training centre, specialist trainer |
| Cost | Low: no fees | Higher: fees, travel, wages paid while not working |
| Output | Trainee does real work, but trainer's output falls | Trainee produces nothing while away |
| Relevance | Exactly the business's own tasks | May be general |
| Teaching quality | Depends on the colleague; bad habits can pass on | Expert trainers; possible qualification |
| New skills | Only skills the business already has | Can bring in new skills |
| Risk | Mistakes in front of customers | Mistakes made safely |

### Which method suits which business

- **On the job:** simple routine tasks, small businesses short of cash, skills already held by experienced staff.
- **Off the job:** skills nobody has, new technology, high-risk work, recognised qualifications.
- **Both:** off the job for the specialist skill, then on the job to apply it to the business's own products.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Training | Giving employees the skills and knowledge to do their job well |
| Induction training | Training given to new employees when they start |
| On-the-job training | Training while doing the job at the normal place of work |
| Off-the-job training | Training away from the normal place of work |
| Shadowing | Learning by watching and copying an experienced colleague |
| Productivity | Output per worker in a given time |
| Retention | Keeping staff in the business |
| Technological change | The introduction of new machines, equipment or software |

## Calculation patterns

- **Total cost of a course:** fees × number of staff + wages paid while away + travel. Three staff on a £400 course, each paid £320 for the week, with £90 travel: 3 × £400 + 3 × £320 + £90 = £1,200 + £960 + £90 = £2,250.
- **Lost output from on-the-job training:** value of the trainer's normal output × share lost × weeks. £1,200 a week, a quarter lost, for 6 weeks: £1,200 × 0.25 × 6 = £1,800.
- **Payback of training in months:** training cost ÷ monthly saving. £2,250 ÷ £450 = 5 months.

## Exam traps

- Off-the-job means away from the workplace, not out of working hours.
- Induction is for new starters only.
- On-the-job training has a hidden cost: the trainer's lost output.
- A benefit with no chain ("training improves quality") is description, not explanation.
- Do not recommend an expensive external course for a simple task in a small business.
- In an evaluation, mention the risk that trained staff leave.

## Self-test

1. Define induction training.
2. State three reasons why businesses train employees, as named in the specification.
3. Give two things an induction programme usually covers.
4. Explain one benefit of induction training for a new restaurant worker.
5. Give one example of on-the-job training.
6. Give one example of off-the-job training.
7. State one disadvantage of on-the-job training.
8. State one advantage of off-the-job training.
9. Why might a business decide not to train staff?
10. A firm sends 4 staff on a £350 course and pays each £300 in wages for the week they are away. Calculate the total cost.
11. Which type of training suits a small café training a new server to make coffee? Give a reason.
12. Why might a business introducing new accounting software choose off-the-job training?

### Answers

1. Training given to new employees when they start work, covering things such as the site, health and safety, and policies.
2. Any three of: productivity, adapting to technological change, motivation, retention, quality, customer service.
3. Any two of: workplace layout, health and safety, policies, introductions to colleagues, basic job tasks.
4. For example, learning hygiene and safety rules from the first day reduces the risk of food safety mistakes, protecting customers and the restaurant's reputation.
5. Shadowing an experienced colleague, or being coached by a supervisor while working.
6. A college course, or a course at a supplier's training centre.
7. For example, the trainer's own output falls, or bad habits can be passed on.
8. For example, expert trainers can teach skills nobody in the business has, or staff can gain a recognised qualification.
9. Cost, lost working time, or fear that trained staff will leave for a competitor.
10. 4 × £350 + 4 × £300 = £1,400 + £1,200 = **£2,600**.
11. On the job, because the skill is simple, an experienced barista can teach it, and the café avoids course fees.
12. Nobody in the business knows the new software, so staff need expert trainers, often provided by the software supplier.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does off-the-job training mean training in the evening?**
No. It means training away from the normal place of work, which can be during working hours.

**How do I decide which type of training is best?**
Look at the task, the skills already in the business, the risk of mistakes and the money available. Often a combination is best.
