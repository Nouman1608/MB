## Section A

**1.** Name the most suitable apparatus to measure each of the following.

**(a)** the time taken for a piece of magnesium ribbon to disappear in acid **[1]**

**(b)** 25.0 cm³ of sodium carbonate solution, to be placed in a conical flask **[1]**

**(c)** the mass of a sample of copper(II) oxide **[1]**

**2.** Which apparatus is most suitable for adding hydrochloric acid gradually to an alkali and measuring the volume added?

A: gas syringe

B: burette

C: volumetric pipette

D: 100 cm³ measuring cylinder

**[1]**

**3.** Define the term **solute**. **[1]**

**4.** A student writes: "A saturated solution is a solution that cannot dissolve any more solid." State what is missing from this definition. **[1]**

**5.** Sea water is filtered through filter paper.

**(a)** Name the liquid that passes through the filter paper. **[1]**

**(b)** State why this liquid is not pure water. **[1]**

**6.** A thermometer has a scale marked every 1 °C. A student records three temperatures: 24 °C, 26.5 °C and 27.3 °C. Rewrite each reading correctly, or state that it is already correct. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** A student investigates the rate of reaction between calcium carbonate chips and dilute hydrochloric acid. The flask is placed on a balance and the mass is recorded every 30 seconds.

**(a)** Explain why the mass of the flask and contents decreases. **[1]**

**(b)** A cotton wool plug is placed in the neck of the flask. Explain why. **[2]**

**(c)** The student repeats the experiment using magnesium ribbon and dilute hydrochloric acid, which produces hydrogen. Suggest why the mass-loss method is unsuitable for this reaction. **[2]**

**(d)** Suggest a better apparatus for following the magnesium reaction. **[1]**

**8.** Two methods are suggested for measuring the volume of carbon dioxide produced in a reaction.

Method 1: collect the gas in an upturned measuring cylinder full of water.

Method 2: collect the gas in a gas syringe.

**(a)** Suggest one disadvantage of Method 1. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest one disadvantage of Method 2. **[1]**

**9.** A student measures the temperature rise when two solutions react in a glass beaker. The measured rise is smaller than the value in a data book.

**(a)** Suggest a reason for the smaller rise. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest two improvements to the apparatus or method. **[2]**

**10.** A student must measure 7.0 cm³ of dilute acid. Measuring cylinders of 10 cm³, 25 cm³ and 100 cm³ are available. State which cylinder should be used and explain the choice. **[2]**

**11.** A mixture of potassium chloride and charcoal is stirred with water at 20 °C and then filtered. The liquid collected is heated to evaporate the water completely.

**(a)** Name the solvent in this experiment. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the residue left on the filter paper. **[1]**

**(c)** Identify the residue left after evaporation. **[1]**

**(d)** The student uses only a small volume of water and some potassium chloride is left undissolved. State the term for the solution formed. **[1]**

**12.** The time for a reaction is measured four times: 41 s, 43 s, 58 s and 42 s.

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mean time, leaving out the anomalous result. Give your answer to the nearest second. **[2]**

**(c)** State why repeating the measurement does not correct an error caused by a stop-watch that runs slow. **[1]**

**13.** A student uses a balance that reads to 0.1 g to find the mass of 0.25 g of zinc powder. Suggest a disadvantage of using this balance and suggest an improvement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Stop-watch [1]

**(b)** Volumetric pipette [1]

**(c)** Balance [1]

**[3]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

*Examiner insight:* C is the common wrong choice. A volumetric pipette delivers one fixed volume only, so it cannot add acid gradually.

**3.** A substance that is dissolved in a solvent [1]. **[1]**

**4.** The temperature must be specified / it is the maximum concentration of solute at a specified temperature [1]. **[1]**

**5. (a)** The filtrate [1]

**(b)** Dissolved substances (such as salt) pass through the filter paper, so they are still in the filtrate [1].

**[2]**

**6.** 24 °C should be 24.0 °C [1]; 26.5 °C is correct [1]; 27.3 °C should be 27.5 °C, as the scale can only be read to the nearest half division, 0.5 °C [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Carbon dioxide gas is produced and escapes from the flask [1].

**(b)** It lets the gas escape [1] but stops acid spray escaping, which would cause an extra, false loss in mass [1].

**(c)** Hydrogen has a very low mass / is a very light gas [1], so the loss in mass is too small to measure on the balance / to see a pattern [1].

**(d)** Gas syringe [1].

**[6]**

**8. (a)** Carbon dioxide is soluble in water / some carbon dioxide dissolves [1], so the volume measured is too low [1].

**(b)** The syringe has a limited capacity / the plunger may stick [1].

**[3]**

**9. (a)** Energy was lost to the surroundings through the glass [1].

**(b)** Any two [2]: use a polystyrene cup; add a lid; stir the mixture; read the highest temperature reached.

**[3]**

**10.** The 10 cm³ cylinder [1]. The volume is a larger fraction of its scale / it has smaller divisions, so the reading is more precise [1]. **[2]**

**11. (a)** Water [1]

**(b)** Charcoal [1]

**(c)** Potassium chloride [1]

**(d)** Saturated solution [1]

**[4]**

**12. (a)** 58 s [1]

**(b)** (41 + 43 + 42) ÷ 3 [1] = 42 s [1]

**(c)** The error is in the same direction every time / it is a systematic error, so every repeat is affected equally [1].

**[4]**

**13.** Disadvantage: 0.25 g cannot be measured precisely because the balance reads only to 0.1 g / the reading would be 0.2 g or 0.3 g [1]. Improvement: use a balance that reads to 0.01 g, or use a larger mass so that the 0.1 g scale division is a smaller fraction of the reading [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a measuring cylinder when a fixed or precisely variable volume is required.
- Writing "it is more accurate" with no reason.
- Forgetting that a filtrate may contain dissolved solutes.
- Leaving the temperature out of the definition of a saturated solution.
- Including an anomalous result in a mean.
- Recording readings with the wrong number of decimal places for the scale.
