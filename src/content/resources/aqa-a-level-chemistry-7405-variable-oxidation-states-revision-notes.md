These notes condense section **3.2.5.5 Variable oxidation states** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Variable oxidation states study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-variable-oxidation-states/), and exam-style questions are in the [Variable oxidation states practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-variable-oxidation-states-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Transition metals revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-transition-metals-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Explain why transition metals show **variable oxidation states**.
- Describe the **reduction of vanadate(V) with zinc** in acid.
- Explain how **pH and ligand** change redox potentials.
- Describe **Tollens' reagent** as an oxidation-state test.
- Carry out **calculations for redox titrations** of Fe²⁺ and C₂O₄²⁻ with MnO₄⁻.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Oxidation state | Charge an atom would have if all bonds were ionic |
| Redox potential | Electrode potential for a half-equation |
| Self-indicating | The reagent changes colour at the end point, so no indicator is needed |
| Aliquot | Portion of a solution taken for titration |
| Autocatalyst | A product that catalyses its own reaction |

## Why oxidation states vary

4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so different numbers can be lost: Fe²⁺ and Fe³⁺; Mn from +2 to +7; V from +2 to +5.

## Vanadium

| State | Ion | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| +5 | VO₂⁺ | Yellow |
| +4 | VO²⁺ | Blue |
| +3 | V³⁺ | Green |
| +2 | V²⁺ | Violet |

```
VO₂⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → VO²⁺ + H₂O
VO²⁺ + 2H⁺ + e⁻ → V³⁺ + H₂O
V³⁺ + e⁻ → V²⁺
Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻
```

Zinc (E = –0.76 V) is more negative than each vanadium couple, so it reduces V(V) all the way to V(II).
Feasibility: E(cell) = E(oxidising half) – E(reducing half); positive means feasible.

## pH and ligand effects on E

| Couple | E / V | Message |
|---|---|---|
| Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ (aqua, acidic) | +0.77 | Fe²⁺ stable to air in acid |
| Fe(OH)₃/Fe(OH)₂ (alkaline) | –0.56 | Fe(OH)₂ easily oxidised in alkali |
| [Fe(CN)₆]³⁻/[Fe(CN)₆]⁴⁻ | +0.36 | CN⁻ stabilises Fe(III), so E is lower than for aqua ions |

- **pH:** if H⁺ or OH⁻ appears in the half-equation, changing pH shifts the equilibrium and so changes E.
- **Ligand:** different ligands bind each oxidation state with different strengths.

## Tollens' reagent

- Contains [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ in alkaline solution.
- Aldehyde: silver mirror forms. Ketone: no change.

```
[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + e⁻ → Ag + 2NH₃
RCHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ → RCOO⁻ + 2Ag + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O
```

## Manganate(VII) titrations

```
MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O
```

| Titration | Equation | Ratio |
|---|---|---|
| Fe²⁺ | MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5Fe²⁺ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O + 5Fe³⁺ | MnO₄⁻ : Fe²⁺ = 1 : 5 |
| C₂O₄²⁻ | 2MnO₄⁻ + 16H⁺ + 5C₂O₄²⁻ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 10CO₂ | MnO₄⁻ : C₂O₄²⁻ = 2 : 5 |
| H₂O₂ | 2MnO₄⁻ + 5H₂O₂ + 6H⁺ → 2Mn²⁺ + 8H₂O + 5O₂ | MnO₄⁻ : H₂O₂ = 2 : 5 |

**Practical:**
- Use dilute sulfuric acid in excess (not HCl, which MnO₄⁻ oxidises; not HNO₃, an oxidiser).
- End point: first permanent pale pink.
- Warm ethanedioate to about 60 °C to start the reaction; Mn²⁺ then catalyses it.

**Calculation method:**
1. n(MnO₄⁻) = c × V.
2. Multiply by the ratio to find n(Fe²⁺) or n(C₂O₄²⁻).
3. Scale up from the aliquot to the whole solution.
4. Convert to mass, concentration or percentage.

Example: 20.0 cm³ aliquot of a 100 cm³ iron solution needs 18.50 cm³ of 0.00500 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻.

```
n(MnO₄⁻) = 9.25 × 10⁻⁵ mol; n(Fe²⁺) = 4.625 × 10⁻⁴ mol
whole solution: 4.625 × 10⁻⁴ × 5 = 2.3125 × 10⁻³ mol
mass = 2.3125 × 10⁻³ × 55.8 = 0.129 g
```

Example: ethanedioate. 25.0 cm³ needs 25.00 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ MnO₄⁻.
n(C₂O₄²⁻) = 5.00 × 10⁻⁴ × 5/2 = 1.25 × 10⁻³ mol; concentration = 1.25 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = 0.0500 mol dm⁻³.

## Titration checklist

| Step | What to write |
|---|---|
| Measure | Pipette the aliquot into a conical flask; add excess dilute sulfuric acid |
| Titrate | Add KMnO₄ from the burette, swirling, until the first permanent pale pink |
| Repeat | Use concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) and take the mean |
| Calculate | n = c × V, ratio from the equation, scale up, then convert |
| Evaluate | Reading the top of the meniscus, because purple solution is opaque |

Sources of error include not rinsing the burette with the titrant, overshooting the end point, and the volume of the pipette or flask. A percentage uncertainty is the uncertainty in the burette reading divided by the titre, multiplied by 100.

## Exam traps

- Wrong ratio (1 : 1 for Fe²⁺, 1 : 5 for ethanedioate).
- Not scaling up from the aliquot.
- Hydrochloric acid used for acidification.
- End point described as "colourless".
- Vanadium colours in the wrong order.
- Electrons left in the overall equation.
- E(cell) subtracted the wrong way round.
- Saying pH changes E "because the oxidation state changes".

## Self-test

1. Why do transition metals show variable oxidation states?
2. Give the colour of V³⁺.
3. Which vanadium ion is yellow?
4. Why does zinc reduce V³⁺ to V²⁺?
5. Write the half-equation for MnO₄⁻ in acid.
6. State the ratio of MnO₄⁻ to Fe²⁺ in the titration.
7. State the ratio of MnO₄⁻ to C₂O₄²⁻.
8. Why is hydrochloric acid not used?
9. What is the end point?
10. What is the silver-containing ion in Tollens' reagent?
11. What does Tollens' reagent give with a ketone?
12. Why does Fe(OH)₂ turn brown in air?

### Answers

1. The 4s and 3d electrons are close in energy, so different numbers can be lost.
2. Green.
3. VO₂⁺ (vanadium(V)).
4. Zn has a more negative electrode potential than V³⁺/V²⁺, so E(cell) is positive.
5. MnO₄⁻ + 8H⁺ + 5e⁻ → Mn²⁺ + 4H₂O.
6. 1 : 5.
7. 2 : 5.
8. MnO₄⁻ would oxidise Cl⁻ to Cl₂.
9. First permanent pale pink colour.
10. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.
11. No change (no silver mirror).
12. In alkaline conditions iron(II) is easily oxidised by oxygen to iron(III) hydroxide.
