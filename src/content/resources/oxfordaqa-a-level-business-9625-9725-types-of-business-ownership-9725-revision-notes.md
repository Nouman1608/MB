Condensed notes on section **3.1.1.2 Types of business ownership** of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725), Version 1.1. This is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1 Business and markets. Note that 9725 lists **partnerships** and does not list co-operatives; on 9625, revise from that specification. The full teaching and worked examples are in the [types of business ownership study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-types-of-business-ownership-9725/); exam-style questions are in the [types of business ownership practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-types-of-business-ownership-9725-practice/).

## The three headings

1. **Forms of business**: sectors; private and public sector; the five forms; comparison issues; family and small businesses.
2. **Shareholders**: reasons to invest; share capital; share price; dividends, dividend per share, dividend yield, market capitalisation.
3. **Stakeholders**: internal and external; impact of business activity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Primary sector | Extracting or growing natural resources |
| Secondary sector | Making goods from materials; manufacturing and construction |
| Tertiary sector | Providing services |
| Private sector | Owned by individuals or shareholders, usually for profit |
| Public sector | Owned or controlled by government, usually to provide a service |
| Partnership | Business owned by two or more partners who share capital, decisions and profits |
| Unlimited liability | Owners personally responsible for all business debts |
| Limited liability | Owners can lose only what they invested |
| Share capital (equity) | Money invested by shareholders in return for shares |
| Dividend | Share of profit paid to shareholders |
| Dividend yield | Dividend per share as a percentage of the current share price |
| Market capitalisation | Number of shares issued × current share price |
| Stakeholder | Individual or group affected by, or with an interest in, a business |

## The five forms at a glance

| Form | Key advantage | Key disadvantage |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | Full control, keeps all profit | Unlimited liability; limited finance |
| Partnership | More capital and skills; shared workload | Usually unlimited liability; disagreements |
| Ltd | Limited liability; private share sales | Published accounts; cannot sell shares to the public |
| plc | Large sums from public share issues | Loss of control; takeover risk; short-term pressure |
| Social enterprise | Social aim attracts support | Harder to attract profit-seeking investors |

**Comparison issues (use them as headings):** control; objectives; sources of finance; distribution of profits; liability.

**Changing form:** sole trader → partnership (skills, capital); partnership → Ltd (limit liability); Ltd → plc (major expansion finance).

**Partnership agreement:** sets out capital, profit shares, roles and how disputes are settled.

**Recommending a form in a case:** ask how risky the activity is (liability), how much money is needed (finance), how much say the founder wants to keep (control), whether the aim is profit or social (objectives), and who should receive any surplus (distribution of profits). Then choose and justify.

## Family and small businesses

**Family business:** commitment, trust, long-term view, quick decisions / family conflict, jobs for relatives, succession problems, reluctance to take outside finance.

**Small business:** personal service, flexibility, low overheads, niche markets / higher unit costs, harder finance, dependence on a few people or customers, limited specialist skills.

## Shareholders

- **Why invest:** dividends; rising share price; votes; supporting a business's aims.
- **Share price influences:** results and forecasts; dividends; takeover news; industry news; interest rates and the economy; confidence.
- **Significance:** shareholder wealth; ease of raising new capital; takeover risk; pressure on directors. Not a direct change in the company's cash.

```
Market capitalisation = shares issued x current share price
Dividend per share    = total dividends / shares issued
Dividend yield (%)    = dividend per share / share price x 100
```

Price falls with the same dividend → yield rises. Ask why.

## Stakeholders

- **Internal:** owners/shareholders; employees.
- **External:** customers; suppliers; creditors; community; government.
- **Method:** identify the decision → choose the groups most affected → explain gain or loss → identify conflicts → suggest how the business might reduce them.

## Exam traps

- A plc is in the **private** sector.
- Partners **usually** have unlimited liability.
- Keep pounds and pence consistent in the yield.
- Market capitalisation uses the **current** share price.
- A business can operate in more than one sector.
- Name the stakeholder **and** the impact.

## Self-test

1. Classify: a fishing fleet, a bakery making bread for supermarkets, a bus company.
2. Why is a public limited company in the private sector?
3. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of a partnership compared with a sole trader.
4. Why might a partnership become a private limited company?
5. Give two disadvantages of operating as a family business.
6. Give two advantages of being a small business.
7. Define share capital.
8. A company has 20 million shares at 250p and pays total dividends of £1.5 million. Calculate the dividend per share.
9. Calculate the dividend yield in question 8.
10. Calculate the market capitalisation in question 8.
11. Name the two internal stakeholders listed in 9725.
12. A factory introduces night shifts. Identify one stakeholder who gains and one who may lose.

### Answers

1. Fishing fleet: primary. Bakery: secondary. Bus company: tertiary.
2. It is owned by private shareholders, not by the government; "public" means its shares can be offered to the public.
3. Advantage: more capital or skills (or shared workload). Disadvantage: shared profits, disagreements, or joint unlimited liability.
4. To gain limited liability as the business and its debts grow (or to raise more finance by selling shares).
5. Any two: family conflict; jobs for relatives over better candidates; succession problems; reluctance to take outside finance.
6. Any two: personal service; flexibility; low overheads; serving niche markets.
7. Money invested in a company by shareholders in return for shares, which does not have to be repaid.
8. £1,500,000 ÷ 20,000,000 = **7.5p**.
9. 7.5 ÷ 250 × 100 = **3%**.
10. 20,000,000 × £2.50 = **£50 million**.
11. Owners/shareholders and employees.
12. Gains: shareholders (higher output) or employees wanting extra pay. May lose: local residents (noise) or employees who dislike night work.
