Condensed notes for sub-topic 2.2 of Cambridge O Level Geography (2217), 2026 series.
**Case study required:** the opportunities presented by a river or rivers, the associated hazards
and their management.

## Drainage basin

- **Watershed** = boundary of the basin. **Tributary** = smaller river joining the main one.
  **Confluence** = where they meet.
- Input: precipitation. Stores: interception, surface, soil, groundwater, channel.
- Flows: infiltration, throughflow, overland flow, percolation, groundwater flow.
- Outputs: evaporation, transpiration, discharge to sea.
- More **overland flow** = faster, higher flood peak.

## Downstream changes

Width ↑ · depth ↑ · velocity usually ↑ (less friction) · discharge ↑ · gradient ↓ · load
smaller and rounder.

```
discharge (m³/s) = width × mean depth × velocity
```

## Processes

- **Erosion:** hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution.
- **Transport:** traction (roll), saltation (bounce), suspension (carried), solution (dissolved).
- **Deposition:** when energy falls — inside bends, river mouths, flood plains in flood, low flow.

## Landforms in one line each

- **V-shaped valley:** vertical erosion + weathering of sides; interlocking spurs.
- **Waterfall:** hard rock over soft → undercutting → overhang collapses → retreats upstream →
  gorge; plunge pool at base.
- **Pothole:** pebbles swirled in eddies drill the bed by abrasion.
- **Meander:** fast flow on outside → river cliff; slow flow inside → slip-off slope.
- **Oxbow lake:** neck narrows → flood cuts through → deposition seals old bend.
- **Flood plain:** migrating meanders widen valley + alluvium deposited in floods.
- **Levées:** flood water slows on leaving channel → coarse load dropped on banks → repeated
  floods build them up.
- **Delta:** river slows at sea or lake → deposition faster than removal → distributaries
  (Nile arcuate; Mississippi bird's foot).

## Hazards and opportunities

- **Flood causes, physical:** heavy or prolonged rain, snowmelt, saturated ground, steep
  impermeable slopes.
- **Flood causes, human:** deforestation, urbanisation, building on flood plains.
- **River erosion:** bank collapse destroys land and buildings.
- **Opportunities:** fertile alluvium, flat land, water supply and irrigation, fishing,
  transport, HEP, tourism.

## Flood management

| Hard | Soft / other |
|---|---|
| Dams and reservoirs | Afforestation |
| Embankments / raised levées | Flood plain zoning |
| Straightening and dredging | Wetland restoration |
| Flood relief channels | Warnings, raised homes, flood shelters |

Hard: costly, can shift flooding downstream. Soft: cheaper, sustainable, less protection in
extreme floods.

**Case-study hook:** Nile, Egypt — Aswan High Dam (completed 1970), Lake Nasser, flood control,
year-round irrigation, HEP; but silt trapped, fertiliser needed, delta coast erodes.

**Second case-study hook:** the Indus, Pakistan.
- Opportunities: water for the canal irrigation system that supports most of the country's farming;
  fertile alluvial plains in Punjab and Sindh; HEP and storage at **Tarbela Dam**; fishing in the
  lower river and delta.
- Hazards: monsoon floods, made worse in summer by snow and glacier melt in the mountains. The
  **2010 floods** affected about 20 million people; the **2022 floods**, after extreme monsoon
  rain, covered very large areas of Sindh and Balochistan.
- Management: storage dams, embankments (bunds) and barrages; flood forecasting and warnings from
  the Pakistan Meteorological Department; evacuation and relief by disaster management
  authorities.
- Evaluation: embankments protect towns but can fail or push water elsewhere; building on the flood
  plain increases exposure; reservoirs are filling with silt, which reduces storage.

## Worked example: calculating discharge

A channel is 8 m wide, with a mean depth of 0.5 m and a velocity of 1.5 m/s.

```
cross-sectional area = 8 × 0.5 = 4 m²
discharge            = 4 × 1.5 = 6 m³/s
```

Show both steps and give the unit. If the velocity was measured with a float, say it is only the
**surface** velocity, which is faster than the mean.

## Model explanation: how a waterfall forms

"A band of resistant rock lies over softer rock. The softer rock is eroded more quickly by
hydraulic action and abrasion, which undercuts the hard rock and leaves an overhang. Eventually
the overhang collapses into the plunge pool, where the fallen rocks swirl around and deepen it by
abrasion. The process repeats, so the waterfall retreats upstream and leaves a steep-sided gorge."

A landform explanation needs the **sequence** and the **named processes**; a labelled sketch helps.

## Why some floods rise faster

- Steep slopes, impermeable rock, saturated or frozen ground and urban surfaces increase
  overland flow, so water reaches the channel quickly.
- Forests, permeable rock and dry soils increase interception and infiltration, so water arrives
  slowly and the flood peak is lower.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Discharge | Volume of water passing a point per second (m³/s) |
| Interception | Precipitation caught by vegetation before reaching the ground |
| Infiltration | Water soaking into the soil |
| Throughflow | Water moving sideways through the soil towards the river |
| Overland flow | Water flowing over the ground surface |
| Alluvium | Fine, fertile sediment deposited by a river |
| Distributary | A channel that splits from the main river, usually on a delta |
| Thalweg | The line of fastest flow in a river channel |
| Watershed | The boundary of a drainage basin, usually high ground |
| Hydraulic action | Force of water breaking rock apart, including air compressed in cracks |
| Abrasion | Load scraping and wearing away the bed and banks |
| Attrition | Load colliding and breaking into smaller, rounder pieces |
| Levée | Raised bank of sediment along a river, built up by repeated floods |
| Flood plain zoning | Restricting building on land at high risk of flooding |

## Exam traps

- Confusing abrasion (load wears the bed) with attrition (load wears itself).
- Describing a landform without the processes that formed it.
- Saying velocity always falls downstream; it usually rises because there is less friction.
- Case studies with no named dams, places or dates.
- Management listed with no evaluation of cost or who loses out.

## Self-test

1. Define watershed and confluence.
2. Give the formula for discharge.
3. Explain why a waterfall retreats upstream.
4. Where on a meander is a river cliff found, and why?
5. Give two human causes of flooding.
6. Give one disadvantage of the Aswan High Dam.
7. A river is 12 m wide, 0.4 m deep on average and flows at 2 m/s. Calculate its discharge.
8. Explain why urbanisation increases flood risk.
9. Give two opportunities that the Indus offers people in Pakistan.
10. Distinguish between abrasion and attrition.
11. Why does a float give a slightly inaccurate velocity?

**Answers:** 1. Watershed: the boundary of a drainage basin. Confluence: where two rivers
meet. 2. Cross-sectional area (width × mean depth) × velocity. 3. Soft rock beneath hard rock is
eroded, undercutting the hard rock; the overhang collapses and the process repeats further
back. 4. On the outside of the bend, where the fastest flow causes erosion. 5. Any two of:
deforestation, urbanisation, building on flood plains, blocked drains. 6. Silt is trapped behind
the dam, so farmers need fertiliser and the delta is no longer replenished. 7. 12 × 0.4 = 4.8 m²;
4.8 × 2 = **9.6 m³/s**. 8. Concrete and tarmac are impermeable and drains carry water quickly to
the river, so overland flow increases and the flood peak is higher and sooner. 9. Any two: water
for irrigation; fertile alluvial land; HEP from Tarbela; fishing; water supply for cities.
10. Abrasion: the load wears away the bed and banks; attrition: pieces of load hit each other and
become smaller and rounder. 11. It measures only surface velocity, which is faster than the mean
because the bed and banks slow the water through friction; it can also be blown by wind.
