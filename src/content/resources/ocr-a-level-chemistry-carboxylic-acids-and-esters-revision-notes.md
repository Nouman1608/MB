## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Carboxylic acid | contains –COOH | hexanoic acid, CH₃(CH₂)₄COOH |
| Carboxylate salt | contains RCOO⁻ with a metal ion | potassium hexanoate, CH₃(CH₂)₄COOK |
| Ester | contains –COOR' | methyl hexanoate |
| Acid anhydride | (RCO)₂O | ethanoic anhydride, (CH₃CO)₂O |
| Acyl chloride | contains –COCl | butanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl |
| Primary amide | contains –CONH₂ | butanamide |
| Secondary amide | contains –CONHR' | N-propylbutanamide |
| Hydrolysis | splitting a compound by reaction with water | ester → acid + alcohol |

## 6.1.3(a): Solubility in water

- The –COOH group hydrogen bonds to water: the acid's O–H hydrogen to a water oxygen lone pair, and water's hydrogen to lone pairs on the acid's oxygens.
- Short-chain acids (methanoic to butanoic) are miscible with water.
- Solubility falls as the chain lengthens: the non-polar chain cannot hydrogen bond with water.

**Model answer:** "The –COOH group forms hydrogen bonds with water molecules: the δ+ H of O–H is attracted to a lone pair on a water oxygen. As the carbon chain gets longer, more of the molecule is non-polar and cannot hydrogen bond, so solubility decreases."

## 6.1.3(b): Acid reactions in aqueous conditions

Carboxylic acids are weak acids: RCOOH ⇌ RCOO⁻ + H⁺. They still react as acids.

| Reagent | Products | Example equation |
|---|---|---|
| Metal | salt + hydrogen | 2HCOOH + Zn → (HCOO)₂Zn + H₂ |
| Carbonate | salt + water + carbon dioxide | 2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK + H₂O + CO₂ |
| Metal oxide | salt + water | 2CH₃CH₂COOH + MgO → (CH₃CH₂COO)₂Mg + H₂O |
| Alkali | salt + water | CH₃(CH₂)₄COOH + NaOH → CH₃(CH₂)₄COONa + H₂O |

**Must-know distinction: carboxylic acid vs phenol (PAG7, 6.3.1(c))**

| Test | Carboxylic acid | Phenol |
|---|---|---|
| NaOH(aq) | reacts, salt forms | reacts, salt forms |
| Na₂CO₃(aq) | effervescence, CO₂ | no reaction |

Comparing the acidity of different carboxylic acids is not required. The full set of PAG7 functional-group tests is in [Chromatography and qualitative analysis](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-chromatography-and-qualitative-analysis/).

**Method in steps: balancing an acid equation**

1. Write the salt. Each RCOO⁻ carries a 1− charge, so a 2+ metal needs two: (RCOO)₂Mg, (RCOO)₂Ca.
2. Count the RCOO⁻ groups in the salt; that is the number of RCOOH on the left.
3. Add H₂ (metal), H₂O (oxide or alkali) or H₂O + CO₂ (carbonate).
4. In ionic equations keep RCOOH whole, because it is a weak acid.

**PAG7 test-tube method for –COOH**

1. Add sodium carbonate solution to a small sample.
2. Effervescence means a gas is given off.
3. Bubble the gas through limewater; it turns milky, confirming CO₂ and a carboxylic acid.

## 6.1.3(c): Making esters

**Naming in steps**

1. Find the alcohol. Its chain gives the first word: methanol → methyl, propan-1-ol → propyl, propan-2-ol → propan-2-yl.
2. Find the acid. Change -oic acid to -oate: hexanoic acid → hexanoate.
3. Join them: methyl hexanoate.

**Two routes in this outcome**

| | (i) Carboxylic acid + alcohol | (ii) Acid anhydride + alcohol |
|---|---|---|
| Conditions | heat with conc. H₂SO₄ catalyst | warm |
| Reversible? | yes, equilibrium | no |
| Other product | H₂O | RCOOH |
| Yield | limited by equilibrium | higher |

Worked reminder (ii): (CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃OH → CH₃COOCH₃ + CH₃COOH, giving methyl ethanoate and ethanoic acid.

## 6.1.3(d): Hydrolysis of esters

| | (i) Hot aqueous acid | (ii) Hot aqueous alkali |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | dilute H₂SO₄ or HCl, reflux | NaOH(aq) or KOH(aq), reflux |
| Products | carboxylic acid + alcohol | carboxylate salt + alcohol |
| Goes to completion? | no: reversible equilibrium | yes |

Worked reminder (ii): HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + NaOH → HCOONa + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH, giving sodium methanoate and propan-1-ol.

**Method in steps: working back from hydrolysis products**

1. The salt or acid tells you the R–COO part.
2. The alcohol tells you the R' part.
3. Write RCOOR' and check the molecular formula adds up.

## 6.1.3(e): Acyl chlorides from SOCl₂

RCOOH + SOCl₂ → RCOCl + SO₂ + HCl

- Both by-products are gases, so the acyl chloride is easy to separate.
- Conditions must be dry, because acyl chlorides react with water.

Worked reminder: CH₃CH₂COOH + SOCl₂ → CH₃CH₂COCl + SO₂ + HCl, giving propanoyl chloride.

## 6.1.3(f): Acyl chlorides in synthesis

All of these happen readily at room temperature and are not reversible. Every one gives HCl, which ends up as an ammonium salt when ammonia or an amine is used.

| Reacts with | Product type | General equation |
|---|---|---|
| Water | carboxylic acid | RCOCl + H₂O → RCOOH + HCl |
| Alcohol | ester | RCOCl + R'OH → RCOOR' + HCl |
| Phenol | ester | RCOCl + C₆H₅OH → RCOOC₆H₅ + HCl |
| Ammonia | primary amide | RCOCl + 2NH₃ → RCONH₂ + NH₄Cl |
| Primary amine | secondary amide | RCOCl + 2R'NH₂ → RCONHR' + R'NH₃⁺Cl⁻ |

**Must-know distinctions**

- Phenol is not readily esterified by carboxylic acids. Use an acyl chloride to make a phenyl ester.
- Ammonia gives a primary amide; a primary amine gives a secondary amide.
- Two moles of NH₃ or amine per mole of acyl chloride: one to react, one to take up the HCl.

## Observations worth quoting

| Reaction | What you see |
|---|---|
| Carboxylic acid + magnesium | effervescence; the metal dissolves |
| Carboxylic acid + carbonate | effervescence; the gas turns limewater milky |
| Carboxylic acid + copper(II) oxide, warm | black solid dissolves; blue solution forms |
| Carboxylic acid + alkali | no visible change (follow with an indicator) |
| Acyl chloride + water | vigorous reaction; misty fumes of HCl |
| Phenol + sodium carbonate | no effervescence |

## Reaction map

- Carboxylic acid → carboxylate salt: metal, carbonate, metal oxide or alkali.
- Carboxylic acid → ester: alcohol, conc. H₂SO₄, heat.
- Carboxylic acid → acyl chloride: SOCl₂.
- Acid anhydride → ester: alcohol, warm.
- Acyl chloride → carboxylic acid, ester, primary amide or secondary amide: water, alcohol or phenol, ammonia, primary amine.
- Ester → carboxylic acid + alcohol: hot dilute acid.
- Ester → carboxylate salt + alcohol: hot aqueous alkali.

## All routes to an ester at a glance

| Starting compound | Reagent | Conditions | By-product |
|---|---|---|---|
| RCOOH | R'OH | heat, conc. H₂SO₄ | H₂O (equilibrium) |
| (RCO)₂O | R'OH | warm | RCOOH |
| RCOCl | R'OH or phenol | room temperature | HCl |

## Quick self-test

**1.** Name the ester formed from methanol and hexanoic acid.

**2.** Butyl ethanoate is hydrolysed in hot dilute acid. Name the two organic products.

**3.** Write the equation for propanoic acid reacting with calcium carbonate.

**4.** Excess potassium carbonate is added to 0.0450 mol of ethanoic acid. Taking one mole of gas as 24.0 dm³, find the volume of carbon dioxide released, in cm³.

**5.** Explain why heating an ester with dilute acid never gives a 100% yield of carboxylic acid.

**6.** Give the products when ethyl methanoate is heated with NaOH(aq).

**7.** Name both inorganic products when a carboxylic acid reacts with SOCl₂.

**8.** Give the products of ethanoyl chloride with excess propylamine.

**9.** Which reagent distinguishes hexanoic acid from phenol, and what do you see with each?

**10.** Give the products when ethanoic anhydride reacts with propan-2-ol.

**11.** Explain why ethanoic acid mixes with water but decanoic acid barely dissolves.

**12.** Name the organic product of propanoyl chloride with excess ammonia, and state its type of amide.

### Answers

**1.** Methyl hexanoate.

**2.** Ethanoic acid and butan-1-ol.

**3.** 2CH₃CH₂COOH + CaCO₃ → (CH₃CH₂COO)₂Ca + H₂O + CO₂

**4.** 2CH₃COOH + K₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COOK + H₂O + CO₂, so n(CO₂) = 0.0450 ÷ 2 = 0.0225 mol; 0.0225 × 24.0 = 0.540 dm³ = **540 cm³**.

**5.** Acid hydrolysis is reversible, so the mixture reaches equilibrium with some ester and water left.

**6.** Sodium methanoate, HCOONa, and ethanol.

**7.** Sulfur dioxide, SO₂, and hydrogen chloride, HCl.

**8.** N-propylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₂CH₃, and propylammonium chloride, CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻.

**9.** Sodium carbonate solution: hexanoic acid fizzes (CO₂); phenol shows no reaction.

**10.** Propan-2-yl ethanoate, CH₃COOCH(CH₃)₂, and ethanoic acid.

**11.** Both –COOH groups hydrogen bond to water. In decanoic acid the long non-polar chain cannot hydrogen bond to water, so the molecule as a whole is barely soluble.

**12.** Propanamide, CH₃CH₂CONH₂, a primary amide.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing an ester name: the alcohol gives the alkyl word, the acid gives the -oate word.
- Showing water as the second product when an acid anhydride or acyl chloride makes an ester.
- Giving the carboxylic acid instead of the carboxylate salt for alkaline hydrolysis.
- Not mentioning equilibrium or reversibility when asked why acid-catalysed esterification or acid hydrolysis is incomplete.
- Writing one NH₃ or one amine in an acyl chloride equation, leaving free HCl alongside a base.
- Calling the product from a primary amine a primary amide.
- Saying phenol reacts with sodium carbonate.
- Describing hydrogen bonding vaguely ("it is polar") without naming the δ+ H and the oxygen lone pair.
- Forgetting the 2 : 1 acid-to-carbonate ratio, and so doubling the CO₂ volume.
