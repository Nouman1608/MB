## The specification list

You must explain the purpose of business documents and prepare: purchase order, purchase invoice, sales invoice, credit note, statement of account, remittance advice, petty cash voucher. The cash receipt is listed separately in 2.1 (c).

## Purposes of documents (learn six)

1. Evidence that a transaction happened, with date and amount.
2. Source for entries in the books of original entry.
3. Checking one document against another before recording or paying.
4. Authorisation, so spending is approved.
5. Settling disputes between buyer and seller.
6. An audit trail, because documents are numbered.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Purchase order | Buyer's written request for goods at an agreed price |
| Sales invoice | Seller's bill for goods supplied on credit |
| Purchase invoice | The same invoice, as received and kept by the buyer |
| Credit note | Seller's document reducing what the buyer owes, for returns or an overcharge |
| Statement of account | Seller's summary of a customer's account for a period, ending with the balance due |
| Remittance advice | Payer's note listing the invoices a payment covers |
| Petty cash voucher | Authorised record of one small cash payment |
| Cash receipt | Proof, given by the receiver, that cash was received |
| Trade discount | Reduction from list price for trade customers, deducted on the invoice |
| Cash discount | Reduction for prompt payment, taken only when payment is made |
| List price | Catalogue price before any discount |

## Document flow on one credit sale

| Step | Document | From | To |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Purchase order | Buyer | Seller |
| 2 | Sales invoice / purchase invoice | Seller | Buyer |
| 3 | Credit note (if needed) | Seller | Buyer |
| 4 | Statement of account | Seller | Buyer |
| 5 | Remittance advice with payment | Buyer | Seller |

Petty cash vouchers and cash receipts stand outside this flow: they record cash paid out of the petty cash float and cash received.

## Calculation order on an invoice

1. Quantity × unit price for each line.
2. Add the lines: total at list price.
3. Deduct trade discount: amount due.
4. Print cash discount terms but do not deduct them.

Reminder: 50 units at 6.40 = 320.00; trade discount 15% = 48.00; amount due 272.00. If paid early with 2.5% cash discount: 272.00 × 2.5% = 6.80, so 265.20 is paid.

## Credit note rules

- Quote the invoice number.
- Use the same unit price and the same trade discount as the invoice.
- For an overcharge, credit the difference per unit × quantity, less trade discount.
- Never include cash discount on a credit note.

## Statement of account rules

- Debit column: opening balance owed, invoices.
- Credit column: credit notes, payments, cash discount allowed.
- Keep a running balance after every line.
- End with "amount now due".

## Who checks what

| Check | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Purchase invoice against purchase order | Right goods, quantity, price and trade discount |
| Credit note against invoice | Same price and discount used |
| Statement against own payables ledger account | Balances agree; differences found |
| Remittance advice against invoices | Payment allocated to the right invoices |
| Petty cash voucher against till receipt | Spending is genuine and authorised |

## Which book each document feeds

- Sales invoice: sales day book. Purchase invoice: purchases day book.
- Credit note issued: sales returns book. Credit note received: purchases returns book.
- Cash receipt and payments: cash book. Petty cash voucher: petty cash book.
- Purchase order and statement of account: no entry.

## Exam traps

- Deducting cash discount on an invoice.
- Using list price on a credit note when the invoice gave trade discount.
- Working out cash discount before deducting a credit note.
- Calling the statement of account "an invoice for the month".
- Forgetting the date, number or both names on a prepared document.
- Recording a purchase order in a day book.
- Writing that the buyer issues a credit note. The seller issues it.

## Quick self-test

1. Who prepares a remittance advice, and why?
2. List the seven documents named in 2.1 (b).
3. Goods at list price total 960.00 and trade discount is 25%. What is the amount due on the invoice?
4. The customer in question 3 pays within the discount period and the terms offer 2% cash discount. How much is paid?
5. Three items at 12.00 each are returned from an invoice that gave 10% trade discount. What is the credit note value?
6. On which side of a statement of account does a credit note appear?
7. Which document would a buyer compare with a purchase invoice before recording it?
8. Give two purposes of a petty cash voucher.
9. Why does a purchase order not lead to an entry in the books?
10. An invoice charged 40 units at 3.10 instead of the agreed 2.90, with 20% trade discount. Calculate the credit note.
11. Which book of original entry is written up from credit notes received?
12. What does a cash receipt prove, and to whom?

### Answers

1. The buyer (payer), to show the seller which invoices and credit notes the payment covers.
2. Purchase order, purchase invoice, sales invoice, credit note, statement of account, remittance advice, petty cash voucher.
3. 960.00 × 25% = 240.00; 960.00 − 240.00 = 720.00.
4. 720.00 × 2% = 14.40; 720.00 − 14.40 = 705.60.
5. 3 × 12.00 = 36.00; less 10% (3.60) = 32.40.
6. The credit side, because it reduces the balance owed.
7. The purchase order.
8. Any two: authorises the payment; evidence of what was spent; source for the petty cash book; supports control of the float.
9. No goods have been supplied and nothing is owed yet.
10. 40 × 0.20 = 8.00; less 20% (1.60) = 6.40.
11. The purchases returns book.
12. That cash was received; it is proof for the person who paid, and the receiver keeps a copy as evidence.

## Frequently asked questions

**When is cash discount recorded?**
Only when payment is made within the discount period. It appears on the remittance advice and the statement of account, not on the invoice total.

**Who issues a credit note?**
The seller issues a credit note to the buyer to reduce what the buyer owes, after returns or an overcharge.
