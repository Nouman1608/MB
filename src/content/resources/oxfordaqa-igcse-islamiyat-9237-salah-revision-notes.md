**These notes condense content area 3.1.2.3 of 9237: the meaning and importance of Salah, how and why Muslims pray, Salah at home and in the mosque, the role of Salah in a Muslim's life, and Friday prayers (Jummah).** The full explanation is in the [Salah study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-salah/), and the practical rules are in the [Salah conditions, Wudu, Azaan and concessions revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-salah-conditions-wudu-azaan-revision-notes/).

## The essentials

- **Salah** = formal prayer; the **second pillar**; five times a day. **Du'a** = personal supplication, any time.
- Purpose: "Establish prayer for My remembrance" (20:14).
- Time: "a decree of specified times" (4:103).
- Moral effect: "prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (29:45).

## Five prayers

| Prayer | Time | Fard rak'ahs |
|---|---|---|
| Fajr | Dawn to sunrise | 2 |
| Zuhr | After midday | 4 |
| Asr | Afternoon | 4 |
| Maghrib | After sunset | 3 |
| Isha | Night | 4 |

**Total: 17.** (2 + 4 + 4 + 3 + 4.) Fard means compulsory; sunnah and nafl prayers are extra.

## Why Muslims pray (say it in seven)

1. Obedience to Allah.
2. Remembrance of Allah.
3. Protection from wrongdoing.
4. Forgiveness (the river: five baths a day leave no dirt).
5. Gratitude.
6. Following the Prophet (pbuh): "Pray as you have seen me praying."
7. Accountability: the first deed to be reckoned on the Last Day.

**Origin:** the Night Journey (Isra wal Mi'raj): fifty prayers reduced to five.

## How: the steps

**Conditions:** pure body, clothes and place; covered; facing the Qiblah; correct time; intention.

**Wudu:** hands, mouth, nose, face, arms, wipe head, feet. **Ghusl** after certain events. **Tayammum** if there is no water or it would harm.

**One rak'ah:** Takbir → standing with al-Fatiha → bowing → standing → two prostrations with a sitting between. **End:** Tashahhud (includes the Shahadah), blessings on the Prophet (pbuh), salam to the right and left.

| Movement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standing | Readiness before Allah |
| Bowing | Respect |
| Prostration | Complete humility; nearest to Allah (Muslim) |
| Salam | Peace to those present |

**Concessions:** travellers shorten (*qasr*) and may combine; the ill pray sitting or lying; Tayammum; menstruating women are excused. Principle: "Allah intends for you ease" (2:185).

## Home and mosque

| | Home | Mosque |
|---|---|---|
| Strengths | Privacy; children learn; extra prayers | Unity; equality in rows; imam; community |
| Evidence | "Do not make your houses graves" (Bukhari) | Congregation is 27 times better (Bukhari and Muslim) |
| Special prayers | Sunnah and nafl | Jummah, Eid, Taraweeh, Janazah |

Parents teach children to pray from about seven (Abu Dawud).

## Jummah

- **When:** Friday midday; replaces Zuhr.
- **Who:** obligatory for adult men who are resident and healthy; women and travellers are not required.
- **How:** bathe, clean clothes, go early; Azaan; khutbah in two parts; two fard rak'ahs.
- **Importance:** Qur'an command (62:9); community; education through the khutbah; best day (Muslim).

## The role of Salah in life

Structure of the day. Discipline. Peace. Moral awareness. Equality and unity. Link between belief and action.

## The passage: Qur'an 62:9-11

| Verse | Message |
|---|---|
| 9 | "proceed to the remembrance of Allah and leave trade": prayer comes first |
| 10 | "disperse within the land and seek from the bounty of Allah": work is permitted afterwards, with remembrance of Allah |
| 11 | They "rushed to it and left you standing": do not be distracted by trade or amusement; what is with Allah is better |

## Sunni and Shi'a differences (brief)

- Arms folded (many Sunni) or at the sides (many Twelver Shi'a).
- Prostration on a clay tablet (*turbah*) among many Shi'a.
- Shi'a often combine Zuhr/Asr and Maghrib/Isha; most Sunnis keep five separate times.
- Agree on 17 fard rak'ahs and the importance of Salah.

|---|---|

## Exam traps

- Salah is not the same as du'a.
- Jummah replaces Zuhr; it is not an extra.
- Muslims face the Ka'aba; they do not worship it.
- Concessions change how, not whether.
- Give a reason with each fact.

## A model 6-mark paragraph

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Salah is important to Muslims. Apply **one** teaching. **[6]**

Salah is important because it is an act of obedience commanded by Allah at fixed times [1], so each prayer is a way of showing submission and remembering Him through the day [1]. It is also important because it keeps a person from wrongdoing [1], as regular prayer reminds them of Allah and encourages them to be honest and kind [1]. The Qur'an says that "prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (29:45) [1], which shows that prayer is meant to affect conduct as well as to be performed [1].

## Planning a 12-mark answer on Salah

Statement example: "Salah is the most important pillar."

1. **For:** it is performed five times a day; the first deed judged on the Last Day; the Prophet (pbuh) kept it even in difficulty; it shapes conduct (29:45).
2. **Against:** the Shahadah is the entry to Islam; Zakah supports the poor and Hajj unites the Ummah; without belief and good conduct, prayer alone is incomplete.
3. **Passage:** verse 9 gives prayer priority; verse 10 shows balance; verse 11 warns about distraction.
4. **Judgement:** Salah is the most frequent and visible pillar and is central, but the pillars work together.

Spend about a minute planning, then write four or five developed paragraphs, each with a point, evidence from the passage or another source, and a link back to the statement.

## Common 4-mark "explain" topics and a model point for each

| Topic | A strong point |
|---|---|
| Wudu | It prepares the body and mind to meet Allah, showing respect and cleanliness |
| Qiblah | One direction unites Muslims worldwide, and the Ka'aba is the first house built for the worship of Allah |
| Azaan | It announces the time and proclaims Tawhid publicly |
| Congregational prayer | Rows of equals behind one imam teach unity and discipline |
| Concessions | They show that Islam is practical and merciful |
| Jummah | It gathers the community weekly and teaches through the khutbah |

## Self-test

1. How many fard rak'ahs are prayed in one day?
2. Which prayer has three rak'ahs?
3. What replaces Zuhr on Friday?
4. Give two benefits of praying in the mosque.
5. Which verse says that prayer prohibits immorality?
6. What is the khutbah?
7. Give one concession for a traveller.
8. What does prostration show?
9. Name the three parts of 62:9-11.
10. Why do Muslims face the Qiblah?

**Answers:** 1. Seventeen. 2. Maghrib. 3. Jummah. 4. Any two: unity; equality; hearing the imam; community support; greater reward. 5. Qur'an 29:45. 6. The sermon before Friday prayers. 7. Shortening four-rak'ah prayers to two. 8. Complete humility before Allah. 9. The command, the balance with work, and the warning about distraction. 10. It unites Muslims in one direction of worship.
