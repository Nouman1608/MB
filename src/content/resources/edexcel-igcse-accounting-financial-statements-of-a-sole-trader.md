This guide covers section 4.1, Financial statements of a sole trader, from Topic 4 (The preparation of financial statements) in the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Accounting (4AC1) specification, Issue 1, October 2016. The specification lists no tier labels against these points. Topic 4 sits in Paper 2, Financial Statements, which also builds on the knowledge from topics 1 to 3, so you are expected to read a trial balance and know double entry before you start here.

## What the specification asks for

| Point | You must be able to | Where in this guide |
|---|---|---|
| 4.1a | Prepare income statements to show gross profit and profit for the year | Sections 1 to 3 |
| 4.1b | Prepare statements of financial position to show assets, liabilities and equity | Sections 4 to 6 |

Both statements come from the same trial balance, so the guide builds one set of statements in full (Worked example 1) and then a second set for a business that makes a loss (Worked example 2).

## 1. Two statements, two different questions

The **income statement** answers: how much profit did the business make in a period? It covers a stated period, for example "for the year ended 30 April 2026". It collects revenue and expenses.

The **statement of financial position** answers: what does the business own and owe on one particular day? It is dated at a point, for example "at 30 April 2026". It lists assets, liabilities and equity.

The link between them is the owner's equity. Profit for the year from the income statement is added to equity in the statement of financial position, and the owner's drawings are taken off. Each statement checks the other: if the statement of financial position does not balance, there is a mistake somewhere in your working.

## 2. Building the income statement

Work from the top down in two stages.

**Stage 1: the trading part, ending in gross profit.**

- Revenue is sales for the year. Deduct sales returns to get net revenue.
- Cost of sales is what the goods you sold cost you. Use opening inventories, add purchases and carriage inwards, deduct purchases returns, then deduct closing inventories.
- Gross profit is net revenue less cost of sales.

```
Cost of sales = opening inventories
              + purchases
              + carriage inwards
              - purchases returns
              - closing inventories
Gross profit  = net revenue - cost of sales
```

Carriage inwards is the cost of bringing goods to the business, so it is part of what the goods cost. Carriage outwards is the cost of delivering goods to customers, so it is an ordinary expense and belongs in stage 2.

**Stage 2: from gross profit to profit for the year.**

- Add any other income, such as discount received.
- Deduct every expense of running the business: wages, rent, insurance, carriage outwards, general expenses, discount allowed and the depreciation charge for the year.
- The result is profit for the year (or a loss if the expenses are larger).

Only revenue and expenses go in here. Buying a non-current asset is capital expenditure, so it goes into the statement of financial position, not the income statement. Drawings are not an expense either (see section 6).

## 4. Building the statement of financial position

A vertical layout is used here. Assets and liabilities are grouped by how soon they turn into cash or fall due.

- **Non-current assets** are kept to use in the business for more than a year, such as premises and equipment. Show cost, less accumulated depreciation, to give the carrying amount.
- **Current assets** are expected to turn into cash within a year. List them from least to most liquid: inventories, trade receivables, other receivables, bank, cash.
- **Current liabilities** are due within a year: trade payables, other payables and any bank overdraft.
- **Non-current liabilities** are due after more than a year, such as a long-term loan.
- **Equity** is the owner's stake: opening capital, plus profit for the year (or less a loss), less drawings.

Current assets less current liabilities is working capital. Adding it to non-current assets and then deducting non-current liabilities gives **net assets**, which must equal equity.

## 5. Worked example 1: statement of financial position

Accumulated depreciation on the equipment is the opening provision 6,000 plus this year's charge 3,600 = 9,600. The carrying amount is 18,000 less 9,600 = 8,400. Premises are not depreciated in this business.

The check works: net assets of 85,000 equal closing equity of 85,000. In the other direction, total assets (88,400 + 22,700 = 111,100) equal equity plus liabilities (85,000 + 20,000 + 6,100 = 111,100).

## 6. Classification traps

- **Drawings** are the owner taking money or goods for personal use. They are not a business expense, so they never appear in the income statement. They reduce equity.
- **Closing inventories** appear twice in your working but once on each statement: they are deducted in cost of sales and listed as a current asset.
- **Loan repayments** reduce the liability. Only the interest on the loan is an expense.
- **Capital expenditure**, such as buying a vehicle or the legal cost of buying premises, increases a non-current asset. It is not an expense of the year.
- **A bank overdraft** is a current liability, not a negative current asset, in this layout.
- **Capital** is the amount the owner has invested at the start. Equity at the end is capital plus profit less drawings.

## 7. Worked example 2: a business that makes a loss

Farah Lodhi runs Alder Row Bakery. For the year ended 31 March 2026 her records give:

- sales 64,800; sales returns 800
- inventories at 1 April 2025 2,100; closing inventories 1,750
- purchases 38,900; purchases returns 600; carriage inwards 450
- discount received 300
- expenses: wages 16,800, rent 4,800, electricity 1,950, repairs 640, loan interest 360, general expenses 1,230 and depreciation 2,800
- equipment at cost 24,000, with accumulated depreciation of 12,600 after this year's charge
- trade receivables 2,700, cash 180, trade payables 4,350, bank overdraft 3,260, loan 6,000 (repayable in 2029)
- capital at 1 April 2025 13,000, drawings 7,200

Cost of sales is 2,100 + 38,900 + 450 - 600 - 1,750 = 39,100. Net revenue is 64,000, so gross profit is 24,900. Adding discount received 300 gives 25,200. Expenses total 28,580 (16,800 + 4,800 + 1,950 + 640 + 360 + 1,230 + 2,800), so the result is a **loss for the year of 3,380**.

```
Alder Row Bakery
Statement of financial position at 31 March 2026

Non-current assets
Equipment at cost 24,000 less accumulated depreciation 12,600     11,400
Current assets
Inventories                                   1,750
Trade receivables                             2,700
Cash                                            180
                                                        4,630
Current liabilities
Trade payables                                4,350
Bank overdraft                                3,260
                                                        7,610
Working capital (negative)                             (2,980)
                                                        8,420
Non-current liabilities
Loan (repayable in 2029)                                6,000

Capital at 1 April 2025                                13,000
Less loss for the year                                  3,380
                                                        9,620

```

Notice what the statement shows. The loss and the drawings together cut equity from 13,000 to 2,420. Current liabilities are larger than current assets, which is why the working capital figure is negative.

## Common errors

- Starting cost of sales with closing inventories instead of opening inventories.
- Putting carriage inwards among expenses, which understates gross profit.
- Treating a loss as a profit when adding to capital: a loss is deducted.
- Showing drawings in the income statement.
- Listing the cost of new equipment as an expense.
- Forgetting that carrying amount is cost less accumulated depreciation, not cost less this year's charge only.
- Leaving out the date and period in the headings. "For the year ended" belongs on the income statement; "at" belongs on the statement of financial position.
