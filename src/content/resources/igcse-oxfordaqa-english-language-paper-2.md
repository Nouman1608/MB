This guide covers **Paper 2 Source-based Reading and Directed Writing**,
for OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270), Version 4.2,
for exams May/June 2018 onwards. Paper 2 is worth 40% of the overall
qualification and is the mainstream exam alternative to the non-exam
assessment (NEA) project route.

## Where this fits in 9270

Paper 1 (Literary Non-fiction and Composition) is compulsory for every
candidate; students then take EITHER Paper 2 (the exam route covered
here) OR an independent-research NEA project as an alternative. Where
Paper 1's reading material is literary non-fiction (autobiography,
memoir, travel writing), Paper 2 deliberately shifts to modern-world
themes and issues, and its writing task is directed rather than a free
choice of form.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE ENGLISH LANGUAGE (9270) — PAPER 2 SOURCE-BASED READING AND DIRECTED WRITING**

- 3.2.1 Section A: Reading — texts relating to modern-world themes and
issues; texts may be brief or solely visual, and may be fiction or
non-fiction
- 3.2.2 Section B: Writing — a directed writing task focused on writing
for a specified audience and purpose, which may be both multiple and
diverse
- Assessment objectives — AO1 Reading (R1–R4: reading and understanding
texts for purpose; collating and synthesising detail across sources;
developing interpretations of writers' ideas; explaining and evaluating
how writers use linguistic, grammatical, structural and presentational
features) and AO2 Writing (W1–W4: communicating clearly, effectively and
imaginatively for task and purpose; using a range of sentence
structures; organising ideas into cohesive, coherent text; technical
accuracy in grammar, punctuation and spelling)
- Weighting — Paper 2/NEA carries 20% AO1 and 20% AO2 of the overall
qualification (against Paper 1's 30% AO1 and 30% AO2), for an overall
50/50 AO1/AO2 split across the full qualification; AO3 (Speaking and
Listening) is a separately reported endorsement that does not count
toward Paper 2 or the overall grade

## How to approach it

Because Paper 2's reading texts are explicitly "modern-world themes and
issues" rather than the literary non-fiction of Paper 1, build a reading
habit around current-affairs and issue-based writing — opinion pieces,
reports on social or environmental topics, campaign material — rather
than only literary prose. Some Paper 2 texts may be "solely visual,"
which is a real possibility worth practising for: reading an
infographic, chart or advertisement for explicit and implicit meaning is
a different skill from reading continuous prose.

The Section B writing task is "directed" specifically because it names
an audience and purpose the response must serve — unlike Paper 1's
choice of descriptive, imaginative or argumentative composition. Before
drafting, identify exactly who the writing is for and what it needs to
achieve, and check the finished response against both, since AO2 rewards
adapting form and register to that specific audience and purpose rather
than producing generic, well-written prose.

Because AO1 on this paper is tested through Section A alongside AO2 in
Section B, revise reading and writing together rather than treating them
as separate papers — for example, identifying how a source text achieves
its effect on its audience (Section A) is close preparation for
adapting your own writing to a specified audience (Section B).

Because Section A's texts may relate to unfamiliar current-affairs
topics, do not let unfamiliarity with the subject matter itself become a
barrier -- the assessment objectives test how well a candidate reads
and analyses the given text, not prior knowledge of the issue it
describes. Everything needed to answer well is present in the source
material provided.

## Common mistakes

Bringing Paper 1's literary-analysis habits to a modern-issue source text
without adjusting for its more direct, purposeful style. Writing at
length without checking the response actually serves the specified
audience and purpose. Treating a visual or brief source as less
significant than a longer prose text, when it may carry just as much
assessable meaning. Neglecting technical accuracy (W4) under time
pressure, when it is assessed with equal weight to content and
organisation.

## Worked example: matching writing to audience and purpose

A directed writing task asks candidates to write an article for a school
magazine persuading younger students to take up a new hobby.

```
Audience:  younger students at the same school -- informal but not
           childish register, direct address ("you"), relatable
           examples
Purpose:   persuade -- rhetorical questions, a clear line of argument,
           benefits foregrounded, a call to action
Form:      magazine article -- a engaging headline/opening, short
           paragraphs, sub-headings or a strong final paragraph, a
           voice that sounds like it is speaking to peers rather than
           lecturing them
```

A response that is well written in general terms but ignores one of
these three elements -- for example, a beautifully argued piece that
reads like a formal essay rather than a magazine article -- loses marks
against AO2's requirement to adapt form and register to task and
purpose, however accurate the grammar is.

## Quick revision checklist

- Read a range of modern-issue, current-affairs style texts, including
visual/graphic sources, not just literary prose.
- Before drafting Section B, write down the audience and purpose in one
sentence each, and check the finished response against both.
- Revise AO1 (reading) and AO2 (writing) together, since Paper 2 tests
both in a single, thematically linked paper.
- Do not neglect technical accuracy (W4) under time pressure -- it
carries equal weight within AO2.

This mirrors how Paper 1's reading section works, but with a deliberately
different register of source material to test breadth of reading skill
across genres.
