A quick-reference summary. For the full method and a worked plan, use the
[Non-exam assessment study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-literature-9275-non-exam-assessment-route-b/).

## At a glance

- **3.3.3**, **Route B only** (Route A sits unseen prose instead).
- One extended response to a **substantial whole prose fiction text**.
- **30 marks**, **20%** of the qualification.
- Internally assessed with the same AOs as the exams, then moderated.
- Follow your centre's rules on length, supervision, drafts and deadlines.

## Text rules

| Rule | Check |
|---|---|
| Prose fiction | A novel, not poetry, drama or non-fiction |
| Substantial and whole | Not an extract, a single short story or a slight text |
| Not a set text | Not on the 9275 prescribed list |
| Quality | Similar in challenge to the Paper 1 set texts |
| Range | Any period; British Isles canon or seminal world literature |

## Marking at a glance

| AO | Focus | NEA weighting |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Themes, ideas, contexts | 8% |
| AO2 | Writers' methods and effects | 8% |
| AO3 | Informed personal response, terminology, accurate writing | 4% |

| Level | Marks | Key words |
|---|---|---|
| 6 | 26–30 | Convincing, critical analysis and exploration |
| 5 | 21–25 | Thoughtful, developed consideration |
| 4 | 16–20 | Clear understanding |
| 3 | 11–15 | Explained, structured comments |
| 2 | 6–10 | Supported, relevant comments |
| 1 | 1–5 | Simple, explicit comments |

From Level 3 upwards the descriptors refer to the **whole text**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Task | The question your essay answers; should ask **how** the writer presents something |
| Thesis | Your arguable one- or two-sentence answer to the task |
| Conceptualised response | An essay built around a central idea, not a list of points |
| Whole-text response | Evidence and analysis drawn from beginning, middle and end |
| Integrated context | Context used to explain a writer's choice, not added separately |
| Critical view | Another reader's interpretation, paraphrased and acknowledged |
| Authentication | Confirming the work is your own, for example by keeping drafts |

## Task and thesis checklist

- Does the task begin with **"How does the writer present..."** or **"How far..."**?
- Is it focused on one idea, character or relationship?
- Can your thesis be disagreed with?
- Will the plan use evidence from **across the whole novel**?

## Drafting order

1. Reread the novel with the task in mind.
2. Plan: thesis, points, evidence from across the text.
3. Full first draft.
4. Revise the argument.
5. Revise the analysis.
6. Edit for accuracy and check quotations.

## Traps

- Using a set text, or a text that is too short.
- Covering only part of the novel.
- Retelling the plot.
- An unfocused task.
- Context or criticism replacing your own analysis.
- Submitting anything that is not your own work.

## Self-test

1. Which route includes the NEA, and what does it replace?
2. What are the marks and weighting?
3. Name three rules the NEA text must meet.
4. Which AO carries the smallest share of the NEA?
5. What does "whole text" mean for planning?
6. What should you revise first in a draft: accuracy or argument?

**Answers:** 1. Route B; it is taken instead of Route A's unseen prose section. 2. 30 marks, 20%.
3. Any three of: prose fiction; substantial and whole; not a prescribed set text; similar in challenge
to the set texts; from the British Isles canon or seminal world literature. 4. AO3 (4%). 5. Evidence
and analysis from the beginning, middle and end of the novel. 6. Argument first, then analysis, then
accuracy.
