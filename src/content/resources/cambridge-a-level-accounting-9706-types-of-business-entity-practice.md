These questions cover 1.1 Types of business entity in Cambridge International AS & A Level Accounting 9706, syllabus for 2026, 2027 and 2028 (AS Level). All businesses, people and amounts are invented. Learn the content first with the [types of business entity study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-accounting-9706-types-of-business-entity/) and the [types of business entity revision notes](/resources/cambridge-a-level-accounting-9706-types-of-business-entity-revision-notes/).

## Short-answer questions

**1.** State **two** features of a limited company that distinguish it from a sole trader. **[2]**

**2.** Explain what is meant by unlimited liability. **[2]**

**3.** Identify the most suitable source of finance for each of the following.

**(a)** A shop needing extra inventory for six weeks before a festival. **[1]**
**(b)** A design firm needing computers that will be outdated within three years. **[1]**
**(c)** A public limited company building a new factory. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** differences between a secured loan and an unsecured loan. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why a bonus issue of shares is not a source of finance. **[2]**

**6.** A supplier offers a 2% cash discount for payment within 10 days on an invoice of $8 500, or 30 days' credit.

**(a)** Calculate the discount given up if the business takes the full 30 days. **[1]**
**(b)** State **one** reason a business might still choose to take the full 30 days. **[1]**

## Structured questions

**7.** Chen and Diaz are in partnership. The business closes owing creditors $84 000. Its assets are sold for $51 000.

**(a)** Calculate the shortfall owed to creditors. **[1]**
**(b)** Diaz has private assets of only $10 000. Explain how much of the shortfall Chen might have to pay. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the position of the owners would differ if the business had been a private limited company. **[2]**

**8.** Lina is a sole trader with an annual profit of $50 000. She plans to expand, which needs $40 000 and is expected to raise annual profit to $80 000 before any finance costs. Omar offers to invest $40 000 and his marketing skills in return for 40% of profits as a partner. Alternatively, Lina could take a bank loan of $40 000 at 9% interest a year, secured on her house.

**(a)** Calculate Lina's share of the expected profit if she takes Omar as a partner. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate Lina's expected profit if she takes the loan instead, for the first year. **[2]**
**(c)** Advise Lina whether to take Omar as a partner or the bank loan. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**9.** A firm needs a delivery van. It can lease one for $700 a month for 4 years, after which the van is returned. Alternatively, it can buy one by instalments: a deposit of $6 000 and 36 monthly payments of $750, after which the firm owns the van.

**(a)** Calculate the total payments under each option. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain **two** non-financial factors the firm should consider. **[2]**
**(c)** Recommend one option, with a reason. **[2]**

**10.** A business needs $9 000 for three months. Its bank offers an overdraft at 15% a year, or a one-year loan at 10% a year.

**(a)** Calculate the interest under each option, assuming the overdraft is fully used for three months and the loan is repaid at the end of the year. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain which option is more suitable. **[2]**

**11.** Discuss the advantages and disadvantages of operating as a private limited company, from the point of view of the shareholders and of a bank considering a loan to the company. **[6]**

**12.** A public limited company needs $5 million to expand. Explain **two** ways it could raise this finance, giving **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of each to the existing shareholders. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: it is a separate legal entity; its owners (shareholders) have limited liability; it has continuity regardless of changes in ownership; it can raise finance by issuing shares [2] **[2]**

**2.** The owners are personally responsible for all the debts of the business [1], so if business assets are not enough, their private assets, such as a house, can be taken to pay creditors [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** Bank overdraft (or trade credit) [1]
**(b)** Leasing [1]
**(c)** Share issue, rights issue or debentures (or a long-term secured loan) [1] **[3]**

**4.** A secured loan gives the lender a claim on a named asset if repayments are not made; an unsecured loan does not [1]. A secured loan usually has a lower interest rate and can be for larger amounts [1]. **[2]**

**5.** A bonus issue converts existing reserves into share capital [1]. No cash is received by the company, so it does not fund any purchase [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** 2% × $8 500 = $170 [1]
**(b)** Any one of: the business is short of cash and needs the 30 days; it can earn more by using the money elsewhere for 20 days; it is unsure the goods are satisfactory [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** 84 000 − 51 000 = $33 000 [1]
**(b)** Partners have unlimited, joint liability, so creditors can claim the full shortfall from either partner [1]. Diaz can contribute at most $10 000, so Chen may have to pay at least $23 000, and possibly the whole $33 000 [1].
**(c)** A company is a separate legal entity and its shareholders have limited liability [1], so Chen and Diaz would lose only the amount invested in their shares; their private assets would be safe, and the creditors would bear the shortfall unless they held security or personal guarantees [1]. **[5]**

**8. (a)** Lina's share = 60% [1] × 80 000 = $48 000 [1]
**(b)** Interest = 40 000 × 9% = $3 600 [1]; profit = 80 000 − 3 600 = $76 400 [1]
**(c)** Points a strong answer could make, one mark each:
- The loan leaves Lina with a much higher expected profit, $76 400 against $48 000 [1].
- With a partner, her profit would fall below her current $50 000, despite the expansion [1].
- The loan keeps full control with Lina, while a partner would share decisions [1].
- But the loan is secured on her house and she still has unlimited liability; if the expansion fails, she could lose her home [1].
- Omar brings marketing skills, which may make the higher profit more likely, and shares the risk [1].
- Justified recommendation, for example: take the loan, because the profit difference is large and the interest is affordable from the expected profit, provided she is confident in the forecast [1].

**[10]**

**9. (a)** Lease: 700 × 48 = $33 600 [1]. Instalments: 6 000 + 36 × 750 = $33 000 [1].
**(b)** Any two of: under instalments the firm owns the van at the end, which still has a resale value; a lease may include maintenance and allows an upgrade after 4 years; instalments need a $6 000 deposit, which affects cash now; leased vans may carry mileage limits [2]
**(c)** A clear recommendation [1] with a reason drawn from the figures or factors [1]: for example, instalments, because they cost less in total and the firm ends up owning a van with a resale value; or leasing, if keeping cash in the business and avoiding repair costs matter more. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Overdraft: 9 000 × 15% × 3/12 = $337.50 [1]. Loan: 9 000 × 10% = $900 [1].
**(b)** The overdraft is more suitable [1]: although its rate is higher, interest is charged only while the money is needed, so it costs less, and it matches a short-term need [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Marked by points here for self-checking:
- Shareholders: limited liability means they can lose only their investment, which encourages investment [1].
- Shareholders: the company continues if a shareholder leaves or dies, and shares can be transferred, though in a private company only with agreement [1].
- Shareholders: control lies with directors, and minority shareholders have little influence; profits are paid only if directors declare dividends [1].
- Bank: the company is a separate legal entity, so the bank cannot claim the shareholders' private assets [1].
- Bank: it can reduce its risk by taking security over company assets or personal guarantees from directors [1].
- Bank: company accounts are subject to more regulation, giving the bank more reliable information to assess the loan [1].

**[6]**

**12.** For each of two methods: the method named and explained [1], one advantage to existing shareholders [1] and one disadvantage [1].
- **Issue of ordinary shares to the public.** Advantage: no interest or repayment, so no fixed charge on profit. Disadvantage: existing shareholders' control is diluted and future dividends are shared among more shares.
- **Rights issue.** Advantage: existing shareholders can buy new shares, usually at a discount, and keep their proportion of control. Disadvantage: they must find cash to take up the rights, or their stake is diluted.
- **Debentures.** Advantage: no dilution of control, as debenture holders have no votes. Disadvantage: interest must be paid whether or not profits are made, reducing profit available for dividends and increasing risk.

**[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing partners' liability as limited.
- Treating a bonus issue as raising cash.
- Comparing finance options without calculating the cost over the period actually needed.
- Listing advantages without saying whose viewpoint they belong to.
- Recommending without balancing reasons.
- Forgetting that a lease ends with no asset owned.
