These questions cover section **3.1.9 Rate equations** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification
(7404/7405), version 1.2, July 2026, for exams from June 2016 onwards: 3.1.9.1, 3.1.9.2 and Required
practical 7. All of it is **A-level only** and is placed on A-level Paper 2; Paper 3 can test any content.

Learn the ideas first in the [rate equations study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-rate-equations/)
and the [rate equations revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-rate-equations-revision-notes/). The
course hub is [AQA A-Level Chemistry](/boards/aqa/a-level/chemistry/), and the
[printable checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/chemistry/) lists every specification point. Use
R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ wherever it is needed.

## Questions

**1.** Define the terms *order of reaction with respect to a reactant* and *rate constant*. **[2]**

**2.** For the reaction between P and Q, Rate = k[P][Q]². At 300 K, k = 25 mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹. Show that these are
the correct units for k, then calculate the rate when [P] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³ and [Q] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³. **[3]**

**3.** For a reaction, Rate = k[X]²[Y]. The concentration of X is tripled and the concentration of Y is halved
at the same temperature. Calculate the factor by which the rate changes. **[2]**

**4.** The initial rate of the reaction G + H + J → products was measured at 298 K.

| Exp | [G] / mol dm⁻³ | [H] / mol dm⁻³ | [J] / mol dm⁻³ | Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.020 | 0.050 | 0.10 | 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 2 | 0.040 | 0.050 | 0.10 | 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ |
| 3 | 0.040 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 4 | 0.020 | 0.10 | 0.30 | 1.2 × 10⁻⁴ |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to G, H and J. Explain each. **[3]**
**(b)** Write the rate equation. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate k and give its units. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the initial rate when [G] = 0.050, [H] = 0.020 and [J] = 0.50 mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**5.** The concentration of reactant M was measured during a reaction at constant temperature.

| t / s | 0 | 50 | 100 | 150 | 200 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| [M] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.500 | 0.465 | 0.430 | 0.395 | 0.360 |

**(a)** Explain how the data show that the reaction is zero order with respect to M. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate constant, with units. **[2]**

**6.** A concentration–time curve is drawn for reactant N. The tangent at t = 0 passes through
(0 s, 0.400 mol dm⁻³) and (40 s, 0.080 mol dm⁻³). Later, when [N] = 0.200 mol dm⁻³, the tangent passes through
(50 s, 0.320 mol dm⁻³) and (150 s, 0.120 mol dm⁻³). Use these tangents to deduce the order with respect to N.
**[4]**

**7.** A first-order reaction has an Arrhenius constant A = 6.0 × 10¹¹ s⁻¹ and Ea = 92 kJ mol⁻¹. The
equation k = Ae^(−Ea/RT) applies.

**(a)** Calculate k at 350 K. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the qualitative effect of a rise in temperature on the rate constant. **[2]**

**8.** The rate constant of a second-order reaction was measured at four temperatures.

| T / K | 290 | 310 | 330 | 350 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| k / mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹ | 0.112 | 0.404 | 1.25 | 3.40 |

The equation ln k = −Ea/(RT) + ln A applies.

**(a)** Calculate 1/T and ln k for the first and last temperatures. **[1]**
**(b)** A plot of ln k against 1/T is a straight line. Use the first and last points to find its gradient,
then calculate Ea in kJ mol⁻¹. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the Arrhenius constant A, with units. **[2]**

**9.** The reaction 2R + S → R₂S has the rate equation Rate = k[R]². Two mechanisms are suggested.

- Mechanism 1: R + S → RS (slow); RS + R → R₂S (fast)
- Mechanism 2: R + R → R₂ (slow); R₂ + S → R₂S (fast)

**(a)** Explain which mechanism is consistent with the rate equation. **[3]**
**(b)** State what the rate equation shows about the role of S. **[1]**

**10.** In Required practical 7, a student uses a clock reaction between J and K. She varies the volume of
J solution and adds water, keeping the volume of K solution and the temperature the same.

| Volume of J / cm³ | 4.0 | 8.0 | 12.0 | 16.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Volume of water / cm³ | 16.0 | 12.0 | 8.0 | 4.0 |
| Time for colour change, t / s | 144 | 36 | 16 | 9 |

**(a)** Explain why water is added in different volumes. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why 1/t can be used as a measure of initial rate. **[1]**
**(c)** Use the data to deduce the order with respect to J. **[2]**
**(d)** State two other variables that must be controlled. **[2]**

**11.** A coloured reactant W decomposes. It is followed with a colorimeter (continuous monitoring). At
298 K, tangents to the concentration–time curve give these rates.

| [W] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.060 | 0.030 |
|---|---|---|
| Rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ | 4.2 × 10⁻⁴ | 2.1 × 10⁻⁴ |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to W. Explain your answer. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate k at 298 K, with units. **[2]**
**(c)** At 318 K, k is four times its value at 298 K. Use ln k = −Ea/(RT) + ln A to calculate Ea. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Order: the power to which the concentration of that reactant is raised in the rate equation [1]. Rate
constant: the constant of proportionality in the rate equation (fixed at a given temperature) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Saying an order is "how much the reactant affects the rate" is too vague; the mark
needs "power" and "rate equation".

**2.** k = Rate ÷ [P][Q]² = (mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹) ÷ (mol dm⁻³)³ = mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹ [1].
Rate = 25 × 0.040 × (0.20)² [1] = **0.040 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A rate with no units, or with the units of k, usually loses the final mark.

**3.** Factor = 3² × ½ [1] = **4.5** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Squaring the factor for the second-order reactant is the method mark; 3 × ½ = 1.5 scores
nothing.

**4. (a)** G: Exps 1 and 2, [G] doubles and rate doubles, so first order [1]. H: Exps 2 and 3, [H] doubles and
rate ×4, so second order [1]. J: Exps 1 and 4, [H] doubles (×4) and [J] triples, rate ×4, so the change is
all due to H and J is zero order [1].
**(b)** **Rate = k[G][H]²** [1]
**(c)** k = 3.0 × 10⁻⁵ ÷ (0.020 × 0.050²) = **0.60** [1] **mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Rate = 0.60 × 0.050 × 0.020² = **1.2 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For J, two concentrations change, so show that the effect of H has been allowed for.

**5. (a)** [M] falls by the same amount (0.035 mol dm⁻³) in each 50 s, so the concentration–time graph is a
straight line and the rate is constant [1].
**(b)** k = −gradient = (0.500 − 0.360) ÷ 200 [1] = **7.0 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a zero-order reaction k has the same units as rate; s⁻¹ alone is wrong here.

**6.** Initial rate = (0.400 − 0.080) ÷ 40 = 8.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].
Rate at [N] = 0.200 = (0.320 − 0.120) ÷ 100 = 2.0 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1].
Concentration halves (0.400 → 0.200) and rate falls to a quarter [1], so **second order** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The order mark depends on comparing the concentration factor with the rate factor.

**7. (a)** Ea = 92 000 J mol⁻¹ [1]; Ea/RT = 92 000 ÷ (8.31 × 350) = 31.63 [1];
k = 6.0 × 10¹¹ × e^(−31.63) = **1.10 × 10⁻² s⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** k increases as the temperature rises [1]; because a much larger proportion of collisions have energy
greater than or equal to Ea [1].
*Examiner insight:* Leaving Ea in kJ gives a k close to A, which is clearly wrong; expect to lose the
conversion mark and the answer mark.

**8. (a)** 290 K: 1/T = 3.448 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = −2.19; 350 K: 1/T = 2.857 × 10⁻³ K⁻¹, ln k = 1.22 [1]
**(b)** Gradient = (1.22 − (−2.19)) ÷ (2.857 × 10⁻³ − 3.448 × 10⁻³) = 3.41 ÷ (−5.91 × 10⁻⁴) = −5.77 × 10³ K [1];
Ea = −gradient × R = 5.77 × 10³ × 8.31 [1] = 4.79 × 10⁴ J mol⁻¹ = **47.9 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** ln A = ln k + Ea/RT = −2.19 + 47 900 ÷ (8.31 × 290) = 17.69 [1];
A = e^17.69 = **4.8 × 10⁷ mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep at least four significant figures in 1/T; rounding to 3.4 × 10⁻³ and 2.9 × 10⁻³
throws Ea well outside an acceptable range.

**9. (a)** Mechanism 2 is consistent [1]. The rate equation shows two R particles and no S involved in the
rate-determining step, which matches the slow step R + R → R₂ [1]. The slow step of Mechanism 1 would give
Rate = k[R][S] [1].
**(b)** S is zero order, so it reacts only after the rate-determining step (in the fast step) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Naming the right mechanism without linking the slow step to the orders earns little.

**10. (a)** To keep the total volume constant, so [J] is proportional to its volume and the other
concentrations stay the same [1].
**(b)** The same small, fixed amount of product forms before the colour change each time, so rate ∝ 1/t [1].
**(c)** Volume of J ×2 (4 → 8 cm³) and t falls from 144 s to 36 s, so 1/t ×4 [1]; rate ∝ [J]², so **second
order** [1].
**(d)** Any two: temperature [1]; volume and concentration of K solution [1].
*Examiner insight:* "Amount of K" is often too vague; state volume or concentration for the mark.

**11. (a)** [W] halves and the rate halves [1], so **first order** [1].
**(b)** k = 4.2 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 0.060 = **7.0 × 10⁻³** [1] **s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Subtracting the equation at the two temperatures: ln(k₂/k₁) = (Ea/R)(1/T₁ − 1/T₂) [1];
ln 4 = 1.386 [1]; 1/298 − 1/318 = 2.11 × 10⁻⁴ K⁻¹ [1];
Ea = 8.31 × 1.386 ÷ 2.11 × 10⁻⁴ = 5.46 × 10⁴ J mol⁻¹ = **54.6 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A correct k with wrong units still loses the units mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Quoting an order without naming the two experiments compared.
- Crediting a rate change to one reactant when two concentrations changed.
- Cancelling units of k incorrectly for overall order 3.
- Using Ea in kJ mol⁻¹ or T in °C in the Arrhenius equation.
- Rounding 1/T too early, which wrecks a gradient taken from small differences.
- Giving Ea as a negative value after forgetting that the gradient is −Ea/R.
- Reading a tangent gradient from two points close together.
- Giving k of a zero-order reaction in s⁻¹.
- Vague control variables in a clock-reaction plan ("amount" instead of volume or concentration).
