## Specification in brief

- Alkenes form from alcohols by **acid-catalysed elimination**.
- These alkenes can make **addition polymers without monomers derived from crude oil**.
- Outline the **mechanism** for the elimination of water from alcohols.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Elimination (dehydration) | Removal of H₂O from adjacent carbons, forming C=C |
| Acid catalyst | Concentrated H₃PO₄ or H₂SO₄; supplies H⁺ and is regenerated |
| Protonated alcohol | R–OH₂⁺, formed when the –OH oxygen bonds to H⁺ |
| Carbocation | Intermediate with a positively charged carbon |
| Leaving group | The part that departs with the bonding pair; here H₂O |
| Positional isomers | Alkenes differing only in the position of C=C |
| Renewable source | Raw material that can be replaced, such as plant material, unlike crude oil |

## Recall: conditions and equations

- Reagent: **concentrated phosphoric acid** (or concentrated sulfuric acid). Condition: **heat**.
- Ethanol: CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O
- Propan-2-ol: CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O
- Cyclohexanol: C₆H₁₁OH → C₆H₁₀ + H₂O

## Recall: the mechanism

| Step | Curly arrow | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | O lone pair → H⁺ | R–OH₂⁺ (protonated alcohol) |
| 2 | C–O bond → O | H₂O leaves; carbocation forms |
| 3 | adjacent C–H bond → C–C | C=C forms; H⁺ released (catalyst regenerated) |

Why protonate first: **water is a good leaving group; OH⁻ is not**.

## Recall: counting alkenes

1. Mark the C–OH carbon.
2. List adjacent carbons with H.
3. Different neighbours → different positions of C=C.
4. Check E/Z for each alkene.

| Alcohol | Alkenes formed | Total |
|---|---|---|
| ethanol | ethene | 1 |
| propan-1-ol | propene | 1 |
| propan-2-ol | propene | 1 |
| cyclohexanol | cyclohexene | 1 |
| pentan-2-ol | pent-1-ene; (E)- and (Z)-pent-2-ene | 3 |
| pentan-3-ol | (E)- and (Z)-pent-2-ene | 2 |
| 2-methylbutan-2-ol | 2-methylbut-1-ene; 2-methylbut-2-ene | 2 |
| 3-methylpentan-3-ol | 2-ethylbut-1-ene; (E)- and (Z)-3-methylpent-2-ene | 3 |

### Worked reminder: 2,3-dimethylbutan-2-ol

- Structure: (CH₃)₂C(OH)CH(CH₃)₂. The C–OH carbon (C2) has two equivalent CH₃ neighbours and one CH neighbour (C3) with one H.
- From a CH₃: **2,3-dimethylbut-1-ene**, CH₂=C(CH₃)CH(CH₃)₂. C1 has two H: no E/Z.
- From C3: **2,3-dimethylbut-2-ene**, (CH₃)₂C=C(CH₃)₂. Both alkene carbons carry two CH₃ groups: no E/Z.
- Total: **two alkenes**.

### Naming check for elimination products

Number the chain from the end that gives the C=C the lowest locant, then add branches. CH₃CH=CHCH(CH₃)₂ is **4-methylpent-2-ene**; numbering from the other end would put the double bond at position 3, which is higher.

## Recall: polymers without crude oil

- Ethanol from plant material → ethene → poly(ethene).
- The polymer is **identical** to crude-oil poly(ethene); only the **source of the monomer** differs.
- Advantage: does not use crude oil, a **finite** resource.
- Atom economy of ethanol → ethene: 28.0 ÷ 46.0 × 100 = **60.9 %** (water is the by-product).
- Mass link: 46.0 t ethanol → 28.0 t ethene → 28.0 t poly(ethene) (at 100 % yield).

## Recall: two kinds of elimination

| | Alcohols (3.3.5.2) | Halogenoalkanes (3.3.3.2) |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | concentrated acid catalyst | KOH in ethanol |
| Molecule lost | H₂O | HX |
| Intermediate | carbocation | none |
| How the H is removed | lost as H⁺ from the carbocation | taken by OH⁻ acting as a base |

## Exam traps

- First arrow from the **O lone pair**, not from H⁺.
- **Protonate before** water leaves.
- Remove H from an **adjacent** carbon.
- Show **H⁺ regenerated**.
- Use **concentrated** acid and **heat**.
- Check for **E/Z** and for a **second** positional isomer.
- Bio-sourced poly(ethene) is **not** more biodegradable.
- Name alkenes from the end that gives C=C the **lowest locant**, then add branch numbers.
- In yield questions, convert **both** masses to moles before dividing.

## Self-test

1. Name a suitable catalyst for eliminating water from an alcohol.
2. Write an equation for the dehydration of propan-1-ol.
3. Describe the first curly arrow in the mechanism.
4. Why must the alcohol be protonated before the C–O bond breaks?
5. Name the intermediate formed in step 2.
6. How does the mechanism show that the acid is a catalyst?
7. Name all the alkenes formed from pentan-3-ol.
8. Explain why pentan-3-ol gives fewer alkenes than pentan-2-ol.
9. Name the two alkenes formed from 2-methylbutan-2-ol.
10. State the advantage of making poly(ethene) from ethanol obtained from plants.
11. Calculate the percentage atom economy for making propene from propan-2-ol.
12. 5.00 g of cyclohexanol (Mr 100.0) gives 3.28 g of cyclohexene (Mr 82.0). Calculate the percentage yield.

### Answers

1. **Concentrated phosphoric acid** (or concentrated sulfuric acid).
2. **CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O**
3. From a **lone pair on the –OH oxygen** to **H⁺**.
4. OH⁻ is a poor leaving group; protonation lets it leave as a **neutral water molecule**.
5. A **carbocation**.
6. **H⁺ is released** in the final step, so it is not used up.
7. **(E)-pent-2-ene and (Z)-pent-2-ene.**
8. Its two neighbours (C2 and C4) are **equivalent**, so only one position of C=C (pent-2-ene) is possible; pentan-2-ol has two different neighbours.
9. **2-Methylbut-1-ene** and **2-methylbut-2-ene**.
10. It does not use monomers derived from **crude oil**, a **finite** resource.
11. 42.0 ÷ 60.0 × 100 = **70.0 %**.
12. n(cyclohexanol) = 0.0500 mol; n(cyclohexene) = 0.0400 mol; yield = **80.0 %**.
