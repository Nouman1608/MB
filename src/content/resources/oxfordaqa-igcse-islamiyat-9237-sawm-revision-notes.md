**These notes condense content area 3.1.2.4 of 9237: Sawm, the meaning and importance of fasting during Ramadhan, the rulings, the benefits, Taraweeh and Itikaaf, and Laylat ul-Qadr.** The full explanation is in the [Sawm study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-sawm/), and the detailed rulings are in the [Sawm rulings, Taraweeh and Laylat ul-Qadr revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-sawm-rulings-taraweeh-laylat-ul-qadr-revision-notes/).

## Core facts

- **Sawm** = abstaining from food, drink and sexual relations from true dawn to sunset, with intention.
- **Ramadhan** = ninth lunar month; 29 or 30 days; the month in which the Qur'an was revealed (2:185).
- **Aim:** "that you may become righteous" (2:183): taqwa.
- Fasting is the fourth item in the specification's order of the Five Pillars.

## Calendar facts (check by calculation)

- 12 lunar months ≈ 354 days; solar year ≈ 365 days; the gap is ≈ 11 days.
- Ramadhan moves about 11 days earlier each year, and returns to the same season after about 33 years.
- Laylat ul-Qadr is "better than a thousand months": 1,000 ÷ 12 ≈ 83.3 years.
- Odd nights of the last ten: 21, 23, 25, 27, 29.

## Rulings in a table

| Person or act | Ruling |
|---|---|
| Healthy adult Muslim | Must fast |
| Temporary illness; travel; menstruation or post-natal bleeding | Do not fast; **qada** (make up the days) |
| Permanently ill or very old | **Fidya** (feed a poor person for each day) |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding | May delay if harm is feared; make up days (some scholars add fidya) |
| Children | Not obliged before puberty |
| Eating or drinking on purpose; sexual relations; deliberate vomiting | Break the fast |
| Eating by mistake; bathing; brushing teeth without swallowing | Do not break it |
| Lying, backbiting, anger | Do not break it technically but ruin the reward |

**Day structure:** suhoor → intention → fast (Fajr to Maghrib) → iftar (dates and water) → Isha and Taraweeh.

## Benefits in three columns

| Spiritual | Moral | Social |
|---|---|---|
| Taqwa | Self-control | Empathy with the poor |
| Closeness to Allah | Patience | Generosity; Zakat-ul-Fitr |
| Forgiveness of sins | Honesty (the fast is private) | Unity of the Ummah |
| Time with the Qur'an | Gratitude | Shared iftar; family and community |

## Taraweeh and Itikaaf

- **Taraweeh:** voluntary night prayer after Isha; the Qur'an is often completed over the month; forgiveness for those who stand in prayer with faith (Bukhari and Muslim); Umar (RA) gathered people behind one imam.
- **Itikaaf:** retreat in the mosque, normally the last ten days; leaves only for needs; focuses on worship; seeks Laylat ul-Qadr.

## Laylat ul-Qadr

- Night of the first revelation of the Qur'an; Surah 97.
- "Better than a thousand months" (97:3).
- Angels descend; "peace until the emergence of dawn" (97:5).
- Sought on the odd nights of the last ten; the exact night is unknown.
- Du'a taught by the Prophet (pbuh): "O Allah, You are forgiving and love forgiveness, so forgive me" (Tirmidhi).
- Why is the date hidden? To encourage worship on all the nights.

## The passage: Qur'an 2:183-185

| Words | Use for |
|---|---|
| "as it was decreed upon those before you" | Fasting is an ancient practice |
| "that you may become righteous" | The aim: taqwa |
| "a limited number of days" | The duty is bounded |
| "ill or on a journey ... an equal number of days" | Qada |
| "a ransom [as substitute] of feeding a poor person" | Fidya |
| "in which was revealed the Qur'an" | Link with the Qur'an |
| "Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship" | Ease and mercy |
| "complete the period and ... glorify Allah" | Completion and Eid |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sawm | Fasting |
| Suhoor / Iftar | Pre-dawn meal / breaking the fast |
| Qada | Making up missed days |
| Fidya | Feeding a poor person in place of fasting |
| Kaffarah | Penalty for deliberately breaking the fast by sexual relations |
| Taraweeh | Night prayer in Ramadhan |
| Itikaaf | Retreat in the mosque |
| Laylat ul-Qadr | Night of Power |
| Zakat-ul-Fitr | Charity paid before Eid |
| Taqwa | Awareness of Allah |

## Exam traps

- Do not say Muslims fast "to lose weight" or "to be healthy" as the main aim; the aim is taqwa and obedience.
- Fidya is not the same as qada.
- Taraweeh is voluntary, not compulsory.
- Laylat ul-Qadr is not a fixed date.
- Write benefits under spiritual, moral and social headings.
- Add a source (2:183, 2:185 or Surah 97).

## A model 6-mark paragraph

**Question:** Explain **two** benefits of fasting in Ramadhan. Apply **one** teaching. **[6]**

Fasting builds self-control, a moral benefit [1]. By resisting food and drink even when no one is watching, a Muslim learns to resist other temptations and to be honest [1]. It also encourages generosity, a social benefit [1], because feeling hunger helps Muslims understand the poor and leads them to give more to charity [1]. The Qur'an says that fasting is decreed "that you may become righteous" [1], which shows that the purpose is to develop taqwa and to improve behaviour [1].

## Model answer shapes

- **2 marks "Give two acts that break the fast":** Eating on purpose; sexual relations.
- **4 marks "Explain the difference between qada and fidya":** Qada means making up missed days later, for people with a temporary reason [1 and 1]. Fidya means feeding a poor person for each day, for people who can never fast [1 and 1].
- **12 marks:** plan three benefits (spiritual, moral, social), use "that you may become righteous" and "Allah intends for you ease", consider if fasting is mainly about food, and judge.

## Self-test

1. Which month is Ramadhan in the Islamic calendar?
2. What is suhoor?
3. What are the exemptions to fasting?
4. What is fidya?
5. What does Laylat ul-Qadr mean?
6. In which nights is it sought?
7. What does "better than a thousand months" mean in years?
8. Give one spiritual, one moral and one social benefit.
9. What is Itikaaf?
10. Which verse gives the aim of fasting?

**Answers:** 1. The ninth. 2. The meal before dawn. 3. Illness, travel, menstruation or post-natal bleeding, old age, pregnancy or breastfeeding if harmful, and young age. 4. Feeding a poor person for each missed day, for those who cannot fast. 5. The Night of Power. 6. The odd nights of the last ten of Ramadhan. 7. About 83 years. 8. Any suitable examples, such as taqwa; self-control; empathy with the poor. 9. Retreat in the mosque for worship. 10. Qur'an 2:183.
