Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100) looks at the world of trade: how goods
are produced and distributed, how retailers and wholesalers operate, how
documents and communication keep transactions running, and how trade
works across borders and in a sustainable, ethical way. The syllabus
covered here is valid for the 2026-2028 series; the current version,
Version 2, was published in February 2026 with a minor typographical
correction and no changes to assessment. Candidates sit two externally
assessed papers: Paper 1, Multiple Choice, worth 30%, and Paper 2,
Written, worth 70%.

## How the course is organised

7100 is divided into six topics.

**The foundations of trade (Topic 1: Commerce and production).** Trade
and commerce, and production. This topic explains what commerce is and
how it relates to the production of goods and services.

**Getting goods to the customer (Topic 2: Commercial operations).**
Commercial enterprises, outsourcing, retailing, wholesaling, trading
documents and communication. Most of the everyday machinery of commerce
sits here, which makes it the topic with the most detail to learn.

**Trade beyond the home market (Topics 3 and 4).** Globalisation of trade,
and Logistics in commerce, which together cover how goods move between
countries and through the systems that handle them.

**Supporting services and responsibilities (Topics 5 and 6).** Aids to
trade that support commerce, and Sustainability and ethics, which look
at the services trade depends on and at the wider consequences of
commercial activity.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 60 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Written | 120 minutes | 80 | 70% |

Both papers are compulsory and externally assessed, and Paper 1 may
involve simple calculations. Paper 2 is made up of structured questions
of varying length. 7100 is not tiered, so every candidate sits the same
papers, and grades run from A* to E. The assessment is linear, with both
papers taken in the same series.

## How the parts fit together

The six topics follow a product on its journey. It is made (Topic 1),
sold through retailers or wholesalers with the right documents and
communication (Topic 2), possibly traded internationally (Topic 3),
transported and stored (Topic 4), supported by the services that aid
trade (Topic 5), and judged against standards of sustainability and
ethics (Topic 6). Thinking of the course this way makes it easier to
answer questions that cross topic boundaries, which the written paper
often requires.

The papers also complement each other. Paper 1's multiple-choice format
covers a wide range of content quickly, so gaps in any topic show up
there. Paper 2 carries 70% of the qualification and rewards the ability
to explain and apply commercial ideas, so practising written answers on
every topic matters more than anything else in the course.

Commerce also rewards close attention to everyday business around you.
Local shops, markets, delivery services, banks and online sellers all
illustrate ideas from the syllabus, from the role of the wholesaler to
the documents that accompany an order. Noting where you see each idea in
practice makes the content more memorable and gives you concrete
examples to use when a written question asks you to explain or discuss.
The sustainability and ethics topic in particular benefits from
real cases, because it asks you to weigh the benefits of commercial
activity against its costs to people and the environment, and examples
make that balance much easier to express.

## Planning your study

A logical sequence for the course:

- **Opening stage:** Topic 1, establishing the vocabulary of trade and
  production.
- **Main first-year block:** Topic 2, taken in several parts because of
  its size, with regular practice on trading documents.
- **Second-year block:** Topics 3 to 5, linking international trade,
  logistics and aids to trade.
- **Final teaching stage:** Topic 6, then revision across all six
  topics.

Topic 2 deserves extra time in any plan. With six subtopics, from
commercial enterprises to communication, it is the most detailed part of
the syllabus, and much of what follows in the later topics assumes you
are comfortable with how retailing, wholesaling and trading documents
work.

Throughout the course, collect examples of real retailers, wholesalers
and transport methods, which make written answers more convincing. Begin
practice questions on each topic as soon as it is complete. Keep timed
papers and detailed technique for each paper for the revision period,
using the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge O Level Commerce 7100?**
There are two compulsory papers: Paper 1, Multiple Choice (60 minutes, 40 marks, 30%), and Paper 2, Written (120 minutes, 80 marks, 70%).

**What kind of questions are in Paper 2?**
Paper 2 consists of structured questions of varying length and carries 70% of the qualification.

**Do I need to do calculations in O Level Commerce?**
Paper 1, Multiple Choice, may involve simple calculations, so basic numerical work such as percentages and simple totals is worth keeping in practice alongside the written content.

**Is O Level Commerce tiered?**
No. Every candidate takes the same two papers, and grades run from A* to E.

**What are the six topics in O Level Commerce?**
Commerce and production, Commercial operations, Globalisation of trade, Logistics in commerce, Aids to trade that support commerce, and Sustainability and ethics.

**Has the 7100 syllabus changed recently?**
Version 2 of the 2026-2028 syllabus was published in February 2026, but it made only a minor typographical correction with no changes to assessment.
