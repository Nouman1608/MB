This guide is for students writing a Paper 2 Section B essay on **آنگن** (*Aangan*), the novel by **خدیجہ مستور** (Khadija Mastur) listed in Appendix 1 of the Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0) specification with the date 1999. The [Aangan revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-aangan-revision-notes/) give the outline of the novel, its main themes and a model paragraph. This guide concentrates on **how to write the essay**: what the specification asks for, how the marks are awarded, and how to turn your reading into a focused 45-mark answer. Test yourself with the [Aangan practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-aangan-khadija-mastur-practice/).

The prescribed list can change between series, so check the current specification before you start.

## What the specification asks for

For each work, the specification says your responses should relate to:

1. **the form and the technique of presentation**
2. **key themes, concepts and issues**
3. **characterisation**
4. **the structure of the plot**
5. **the social and cultural setting**

The essay question will be set in Urdu, you choose one of two questions on the novel, and you answer in Urdu, with no dictionary and no copy of the text. As a guide, the specification suggests approximately **320–370 words** per essay, although the whole essay is marked whatever its length. You write two essays in Sections B and C together, in 2 hours 10 minutes.

Each essay is marked on three grids:

| Grid | Marks | What it rewards |
|---|---|---|
| Critical and analytical response (AO4) | 20 | convincing interpretations, points of view justified with well-chosen evidence from the work, logical arguments with linked conclusions, relevance throughout |
| Range of grammatical structures and vocabulary (AO3) | 15 | varied and complex language, and **terminology for literary analysis** |
| Accuracy of language (AO3) | 10 | accurate, coherent Urdu |

Two points follow. First, evidence must come **from the novel itself**: the specification gives no credit for essays on literary texts based on screen versions adapted from them. Second, literary vocabulary is part of the range grid, so learn it.

## The five features applied to Aangan

Use these questions to build your notes. Each one should end with a short reference you have checked in your own copy of the novel.

**Form and technique.** How does the narrative focus on Aliya shape what we know and do not know? Where does description of the house or courtyard carry meaning? Which moments are told through dialogue, and which through Aliya's thoughts? How does the writer create tension inside a domestic setting?

**Themes, concepts and issues.** Politics entering the home; women's choices about marriage, education and independence; class and service within the household; loyalty to family against loyalty to a cause; the hopes and disappointments attached to Partition.

**Characterisation.** For each major character, decide: what they believe, what they want, how they treat others in the household, and whether they change. Note how characters are revealed: by what they say about politics, by how they speak to servants or poorer relatives, by what they do at moments of crisis.

**Structure of the plot.** Where does the novel begin and end? What are the turning points that move the household from one situation to another? How does the ending, after the creation of Pakistan, invite us to judge what came before?

**Social and cultural setting.** A Muslim household in north India before Partition; the political conflict between the Congress and the Muslim League; customs of the extended family; the position of women within the house. Link the setting to the qualification's themes, for example family (Theme 1) and the creation of Pakistan (Theme 4).

## Worked example: unpacking a question

Question (original, in the style of the paper): **«ناول "آنگن" میں گھر کا آنگن صرف ایک جگہ نہیں بلکہ ایک علامت ہے۔» اس بیان کا جائزہ لیجیے۔**

Translation: "In *Aangan*, the courtyard of the house is not only a place but a symbol." Evaluate this statement.

**Step 1: identify the key terms.** **جگہ** (place) against **علامت** (symbol): the question wants you to move from setting to meaning. **جائزہ لیجیے** asks for evaluation, so you need a judgement, not a list.

**Step 2: decide your position.** For example: agree, the courtyard symbolises both the confinement of women and the household as a miniature of society; but add that its meaning shifts as the novel moves towards Partition.

**Step 3: choose evidence.** Three moments from your own reading: one where the courtyard is a space of women's daily life, one where political argument enters it, and one near the end. Each should be a specific scene or a few words of the text.

**Step 4: plan the paragraphs.**

1. Introduction: title, author, position (about 50 words).
2. The courtyard as women's limited world (about 90 words).
3. The courtyard as a small version of a divided society (about 90 words).
4. How the symbol changes by the end (about 80 words).
5. Conclusion that answers **جائزہ لیجیے** (about 40 words).

## Worked example: an annotated introduction

> خدیجہ مستور کے ناول "آنگن" کا عنوان ہی اس کی تفہیم کی کنجی ہے۔ بظاہر آنگن ایک روایتی گھر کا وہ حصہ ہے جہاں خاندان کی عورتیں اپنا زیادہ وقت گزارتی ہیں، لیکن ناول نگار نے اسے ایک گہری علامت بنا دیا ہے۔ میرے خیال میں یہ آنگن ایک طرف عورت کی محدود دنیا کی نمائندگی کرتا ہے اور دوسری طرف اس پورے معاشرے کی، جو تقسیم سے پہلے سیاسی اختلافات کی وجہ سے اندر ہی اندر بٹ رہا تھا۔

(The very title of Khadija Mastur's novel *Aangan* is the key to understanding it. Outwardly the courtyard is the part of a traditional house where the women of the family spend most of their time, but the novelist has made it into a deep symbol. In my view this courtyard represents, on the one hand, the limited world of women and, on the other, the whole of a society that before Partition was being divided from within by political differences.)

**Annotation:**

- **کا عنوان ہی ... کنجی ہے**: an opening that goes straight to the question, with emphatic **ہی**.
- **بظاہر ... لیکن**: "outwardly ... but" moves from place to symbol, exactly the movement the question asks for.
- **ناول نگار نے اسے ... بنا دیا ہے**: credits the writer's technique, an AO4 habit.
- **ایک طرف ... دوسری طرف**: announces two lines of argument the essay will follow.
- **بٹ رہا تھا**: past continuous for a process, a small piece of tense range.

## Common mistakes

- Retelling the novel in order instead of answering the question.
- Using evidence from a screen adaptation, or details you have only read in a summary.
- Quoting long passages from memory; a few accurate words, explained, are worth more than a remembered paragraph.
- Ignoring form and technique: many answers discuss only themes and characters.
- Writing about Partition in general historical terms with no link to the novel.
- Calling it a novel of the 1990s because of the date in the specification's list; the novel was first published in 1962.

## Exam technique

- Spend about five minutes planning; write the position in one sentence before you begin.
- Make every paragraph do three things: a point, evidence from the novel, and a link back to the question's key word.
- Use the respectful plural for the author (**مصنفہ لکھتی ہیں**, **خدیجہ مستور دکھاتی ہیں**) consistently.
- Vary structures deliberately: a passive (**دکھایا گیا ہے**), a conditional, and a concessive clause with **اگرچہ** in each essay.
- Leave time to check agreement, particularly with feminine nouns that recur in this novel's essays: **سیاست**, **علامت**, **کشمکش**, **زندگی**.
