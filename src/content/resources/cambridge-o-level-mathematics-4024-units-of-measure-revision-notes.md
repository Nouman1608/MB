These notes summarise section 5.1, Units of measure, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Full explanations are in the [units of measure study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure/), and exam-style questions are in the [units of measure practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure-practice/).

## What is examined

Use metric units of mass, length, area, volume and capacity in practical situations and convert between larger and smaller units. The units are:

- length: mm, cm, m, km,
- area: mm², cm², m², km²,
- volume: mm³, cm³, m³,
- capacity: ml, l,
- mass: g, kg.

Conversions include area (for example cm² to m²) and volume to capacity (for example m³ to litres).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Metric | based on powers of 10 |
| Conversion factor | the number you multiply or divide by |
| Capacity | how much liquid a container holds |
| Volume | the space a solid occupies |
| Square unit | a unit of area, such as cm² |
| Cubic unit | a unit of volume, such as cm³ |

## Conversion factors

| Quantity | Conversion |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 cm = 10 mm; 1 m = 100 cm; 1 km = 1000 m |
| Mass | 1 kg = 1000 g |
| Capacity | 1 l = 1000 ml |
| Area | 1 cm² = 100 mm²; 1 m² = 10 000 cm²; 1 km² = 1 000 000 m² |
| Volume | 1 cm³ = 1000 mm³; 1 m³ = 1 000 000 cm³ |
| Volume to capacity | 1 cm³ = 1 ml; 1000 cm³ = 1 l; 1 m³ = 1000 l |

## The rule behind the factors

```
area factor   = (length factor)²
volume factor = (length factor)³
```

For example, 1 m = 100 cm, so 1 m² = 100² = 10 000 cm² and 1 m³ = 100³ = 1 000 000 cm³.

## Method box: converting units

1. Write the conversion factor for the quantity (length, area or volume).
2. Going to a smaller unit: multiply. Going to a larger unit: divide.
3. Check the answer is sensible: a smaller unit means a bigger number.
4. Write the new unit.

### Examples

```
3.6 m² → cm²:     3.6 × 10 000 = 36 000 cm²
450 000 cm² → m²: 450 000 ÷ 10 000 = 45 m²
0.8 m³ → cm³:     0.8 × 1 000 000 = 800 000 cm³
720 000 cm³ → l:  720 000 ÷ 1000 = 720 l
2.4 m³ → l:       2.4 × 1000 = 2400 l
```

## Method box: practical problems

1. Convert every length to the same unit.
2. Calculate the area or volume.
3. Convert the result to the unit asked for.
4. Use division for "how many" or "how long" questions, with matching units.

## Rates and time

| Rate | In other units |
|---|---|
| 12 l per minute | 720 l per hour, or 0.72 m³ per hour |
| 72 km per hour | 20 m per second |
| 250 l per minute | 15 m³ per hour |

To change km/h to m/s divide by 3.6. To change m/s to km/h multiply by 3.6.

## Must-know distinctions

| Idea | Be clear that |
|---|---|
| m to cm | multiply by 100 |
| m² to cm² | multiply by 10 000 |
| m³ to cm³ | multiply by 1 000 000 |
| cm³ to litres | divide by 1000 |
| m³ to litres | multiply by 1000 |
| ml and cm³ | the same amount |

## Standard form reminder

Very large or small results can be written in standard form: 2 500 000 m² = 2.5 × 10⁶ m², and 0.00045 m³ = 4.5 × 10⁻⁴ m³.

## Exam traps

- Squaring or cubing the factor is forgotten.
- Multiplying when the conversion needs division.
- Working with mixed units, such as m and cm, in one product.
- Using 100 instead of 1000 when converting cm³ to litres.
- Omitting units.
- Zeros lost in 1 000 000.

## Command words you will meet

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Convert, Work out | change the unit, showing the factor |
| Calculate | work out from given information |
| Give | produce the answer, with units |
| Write down | give the answer without working |
| Show that | give structured evidence for a stated result |

## Worked patterns

```
A box 60 cm by 40 cm by 30 cm:
    volume = 72 000 cm³ = 72 litres
A road 1.8 km long, drawn 9 cm long on a map:
    1.8 km = 180 000 cm, scale 1 : 20 000
A sheet of metal 2 m by 0.5 m:
    area = 1 m² = 10 000 cm²
```

## Sensible sizes

| Quantity | Typical size |
|---|---|
| thickness of a coin | about 2 mm |
| a door's height | about 2 m |
| a classroom floor | about 50 m² |
| a mug | about 300 ml |
| a large bottle | about 2 l |
| a bag of sugar | about 1 kg |
| a swimming pool | about 500 m³ |

Use these to test an answer. If a mug is found to hold 3 litres or a door to be 20 m tall, a conversion went wrong.

## Checks that catch most errors

1. Estimate the answer before calculating.
2. Check that the unit in the answer matches the question.
3. Look at the direction: a smaller unit should produce a larger number.
4. Do the calculation another way, such as converting at the start instead of the end.
5. For tiles or packing, check with lengths as well as with areas.

## Self-test

1. Convert 0.4 km to cm.
2. Convert 7.5 l to ml.
3. Convert 0.09 m² to cm².
4. Convert 3 200 000 cm³ to m³.
5. How many litres are in 25 000 cm³?
6. A tank measures 2 m by 1 m by 0.5 m. How many litres does it hold?
7. Convert 5.6 cm² to mm².
8. A pump delivers 500 litres per minute. Write this in m³ per hour.
9. Floor tiles are 20 cm squares. How many are needed to cover 6 m²?
10. How many millilitres are in 0.0035 m³?

### Answers

1. 0.4 × 1000 × 100 = **40 000 cm**.
2. 7.5 × 1000 = **7500 ml**.
3. 0.09 × 10 000 = **900 cm²**.
4. 3 200 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **3.2 m³**.
5. 25 000 ÷ 1000 = **25 litres**.
6. Volume = 1 m³, so **1000 litres**.
7. 5.6 × 100 = **560 mm²**.
8. 500 × 60 = 30 000 litres per hour = **30 m³ per hour**.
9. Tile area = 400 cm², and 6 m² = 60 000 cm², so 60 000 ÷ 400 = **150 tiles**.
10. 0.0035 m³ = 3500 cm³ = **3500 ml**.
