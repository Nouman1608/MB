## Section A: short questions

**1.** State why the start line on chromatography paper is drawn in pencil and not in ink. **[1]**

**2.** Which statement about a pure substance analysed by paper chromatography is correct?

A: It gives no spots.

B: It gives one spot.

C: It gives two spots of the same colour.

D: It always stays on the baseline.

**[1]**

**3.** Explain why the solvent level in the beaker must be below the start line. **[1]**

**4.** State the meaning of the term *solvent front*. **[1]**

**5.** *(Extended)* State the equation used to calculate an Rf value. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* Explain why an Rf value can never be greater than 1. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**7.** A student uses paper chromatography to find which dyes are present in a black ink, K. She spots K and four known dyes, D1 to D4, on the same baseline. The described results are:

- K: three spots, at the same heights as the spots of D1, D3 and D4
- D1, D2, D3 and D4: one spot each, all at different heights

**(a)** State which dyes are present in K. **[1]**

**(b)** State which dye is not present in K. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain how the results show that K is a mixture. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why all the samples must be run on the same paper with the same solvent. **[1]**

**8.** Describe how to set up and run a paper chromatography experiment to separate the coloured dyes in a sample of food colouring. **[4]**

**9.** A student analyses a purple dye. After running the chromatogram, one spot is seen at a height of 4.0 cm, and a second spot is still on the baseline.

**(a)** State whether the purple dye is pure. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest why the second spot did not move. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest what the student could do to make the second substance move up the paper. **[1]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A mixture of colourless amino acids is separated by paper chromatography.

**(a)** Explain why a locating agent is needed. **[1]**

**(b)** State when the locating agent is applied. **[1]**

**11.** *(Extended)* On a chromatogram, the solvent front is 7.5 cm from the baseline. Spot X is 3.0 cm from the baseline and spot Y is 6.0 cm from the baseline.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of X. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the Rf value of Y. **[1]**

**(c)** State which substance is more soluble in the solvent. **[1]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A table of reference Rf values in one solvent is given.

| Substance | Rf |
|---|---|
| dye L | 0.20 |
| dye M | 0.55 |
| dye N | 0.85 |

An unknown dye is run in the same solvent. The solvent travels 8.0 cm and the dye travels 4.4 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value of the unknown. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the unknown dye. **[1]**

**(c)** On another chromatogram in the same solvent, the solvent travels 6.0 cm. Calculate how far dye N would travel. **[1]**

**13.** *(Extended)* A student measures from the bottom edge of the paper instead of from the baseline, for both the spot and the solvent front. The baseline was 2.0 cm above the bottom edge. The student records 5.0 cm for the spot and 10.0 cm for the solvent front.

**(a)** Calculate the Rf value the student obtained. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct Rf value. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Pencil (graphite) does not dissolve in the solvent; ink would dissolve, move up the paper and produce extra spots [1].

**2.** **B** [1].

**3.** Otherwise the samples would dissolve into the solvent in the beaker instead of moving up the paper [1].

**4.** The furthest point (line) reached by the solvent as it moved up the paper [1].

**5.** Rf = distance travelled by substance ÷ distance travelled by solvent [1].

**6.** A substance cannot travel further than the solvent that carries it, so the top of the fraction is never bigger than the bottom [1].

**7.** **(a)** D1, D3 and D4 [1].

**(b)** D2 [1].

**(c)** It produces more than one spot (three spots) [1].

**(d)** The distance a substance travels depends on the solvent (and conditions), so spots can only be compared when everything else is the same [1].

**8.** Any four [4]:

- draw a baseline in pencil near the bottom of the chromatography paper;
- put a small spot of food colouring on the baseline;
- place the paper in a beaker containing a suitable solvent (such as water), with the solvent level below the baseline;
- cover with a lid;
- leave until the solvent has nearly reached the top, then remove the paper;
- mark the solvent front and leave the paper to dry.

**9.** **(a)** Not pure [1]; it contains (at least) two substances, because there are two spots, including one on the baseline [1].

**(b)** That substance is insoluble in the solvent used [1].

**(c)** Repeat the chromatography with a different solvent in which that substance dissolves [1].

**10.** **(a)** The amino acids are colourless, so their spots cannot be seen; the locating agent produces coloured spots [1].

**(b)** After the chromatogram has been run and dried [1].

**11.** **(a)** 3.0 ÷ 7.5 = 0.40 [1].

**(b)** 6.0 ÷ 7.5 = 0.80 [1].

**(c)** Y [1]. *It travelled further with the solvent.*

**12.** **(a)** 4.4 ÷ 8.0 = 0.55 [1].

**(b)** Dye M [1].

**(c)** 0.85 × 6.0 = 5.1 cm [1].

**13.** **(a)** 5.0 ÷ 10.0 = 0.50 [1].

**(b)** Corrected distances: spot 5.0 − 2.0 = 3.0 cm; solvent 10.0 − 2.0 = 8.0 cm [1]. Rf = 3.0 ÷ 8.0 = 0.375, which is 0.38 to two significant figures [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying the baseline is drawn in pencil "so it can be rubbed out" rather than because it does not dissolve.
- Forgetting that the solvent must be below the baseline.
- Counting only the spots above the baseline when deciding whether a substance is pure.
- Comparing spots from chromatograms run in different solvents.
- *(Extended)* Measuring from the bottom of the paper or to the top of the paper, or giving Rf a unit.
- *(Extended)* Applying the locating agent before the paper is dry, or not knowing why it is needed.
