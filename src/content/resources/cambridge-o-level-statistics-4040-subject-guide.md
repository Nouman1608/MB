Cambridge O Level Statistics (4040) teaches students to collect,
organise, summarise and interpret numerical information, and to reason
about chance. It is a practical subject, with most work set in
everyday or real-world contexts, and it suits students who are
comfortable with arithmetic and want to apply it. The qualification is
assessed by two compulsory papers of equal weight; each lasts 135
minutes, is marked out of 100 and is worth 50%. This overview follows
the 2025-2027 syllabus, which is examined in the November series only.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus lists twelve content areas. It helps to see them in four
groups.

### Organising data

1. **Data and its collection** – general ideas of sampling, types of
   sampling, how bias arises and is avoided, general ideas of surveys,
   and types of data and variable.
2. **Summary representation of data** – tables, pictorial and
   diagrammatic forms, the advantages and disadvantages of each, and
   interpreting data presented in these ways.
3. **Ungrouped and grouped frequency distributions** – class measures
   for grouped data, frequency polygons and histograms.
4. **Cumulative frequency distributions** – in tabular and graphical
   form.

### Measuring data

5. **Statistical measures** – mean, median, mode and modal class; range,
   interquartile range, variance and standard deviation; quartiles and
   percentiles; and measures for combined sets of data.
6. **Transformations involving mean and standard deviation** – the
   effect of adding a constant or multiplying by a constant, and linear
   transformation to a given mean and standard deviation.

### Applied statistics

7. **Crude and standardised rates**, and their appropriate use.
8. **Index numbers.**
9. **Bivariate distributions and scatter diagrams** – elementary ideas
   of correlation and lines of best fit.
10. **Time series** – understanding of trend and of seasonal variation.

### Probability

11. **Elementary ideas of probability.**
12. **Probability distributions** – forming the probability
    distribution of a discrete variable, and expectation.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 135 minutes | 100 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | 135 minutes | 100 | 50% |

Both papers are compulsory, externally assessed written exams, and
electronic calculators are required. Each paper combines a set of short
questions, worth up to 8 marks each, with four longer questions of
about 15 marks each. The course is not tiered, and grades run from A*
to E.

4040 is explicitly a linear qualification. Both papers are taken in
the same series, and the syllabus states that candidates cannot resit
Paper 1 or Paper 2 on its own; a candidate who wants to improve must
retake the whole qualification. Because the syllabus is examined only
in the November series, that also fixes when entries and any retake can
happen.

## How the parts fit together

Statistics at this level is a sequence in which each stage relies on
the last. Sampling and bias, from the first area, decide whether any
later conclusion can be trusted. Grouped frequency distributions and
cumulative frequency graphs lead straight into estimating the mean,
median, quartiles and percentiles. Mean and standard deviation then
feed into transformations, combined data sets, and comparisons between
groups.

The applied areas reuse those skills:

- crude and standardised rates rely on weighted averages;
- index numbers measure change against a base;
- scatter diagrams and lines of best fit describe relationships between
  two variables, often using means to position the line;
- time series analysis separates trend from seasonal variation.

Probability is the most self-contained part of the course, but
expectation links back to the idea of a mean, and probability questions
often arise inside longer, multi-part questions.

The longer questions, at about 15 marks each, are where these links
matter most. They typically take one context through several steps, so
a weakness in one area can affect the rest of the question. Written
interpretation, explaining what a result means in context, is
important throughout.

## Planning your study

Follow the order of the syllabus for the first two groups, since
representation, frequency distributions and statistical measures form
a progression. After that, the applied areas and probability can be
taken in either order, although many students find it useful to study
probability before the longer applied topics, as it is shorter and
self-contained.

In general terms:

- **First part of the course:** data collection, representation,
  frequency and cumulative frequency distributions.
- **Middle of the course:** statistical measures and transformations,
  with plenty of calculator practice.
- **Final part of the course:** probability, crude and standardised
  rates, index numbers, bivariate data and time series.

Short-question practice can start in the first weeks. Longer,
multi-step questions are best introduced once the measures topics are
secure. Allow time for past-paper practice in the months before the
November series, and use the exam preparation guide for paper-specific
technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge O Level Statistics 4040 assessed?**
By two compulsory written papers. Each lasts 135 minutes, carries 100 marks and is worth 50% of the qualification.

**What kinds of question are on each paper?**
Each paper has short questions worth up to 8 marks each and four longer questions worth about 15 marks each.

**When can I take O Level Statistics?**
The 2025-2027 syllabus is examined in the November series only.

**Can I resit just one paper?**
No. It is a linear qualification: you can retake the whole qualification but not Paper 1 or Paper 2 separately.

**What grades are available?**
The course is not tiered, and grades run from A* to E.
