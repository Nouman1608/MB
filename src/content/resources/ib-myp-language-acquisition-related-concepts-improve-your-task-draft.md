# Related concepts: improving a task

Examples are written in English. Do any task that asks you to produce language in the language your school course uses, and ask your school teacher to check it. MYP has no prescribed content list: schools design their own units, so ask your teacher which concepts and contexts your units use.

Course hub: [/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/](/boards/ib/ib-myp/myp-language-acquisition/). The reviewed [key and related concepts study guide](/resources/ib-myp-language-acquisition-key-and-related-concepts/) introduces these ideas. This page is an editing workflow.

## What the brief says

Related concepts are narrower than key concepts and are grounded in the subject. The IB brief gives **word choice, conventions and idiom** as examples. The full IB guide lists more, grouped by phases; your teacher will tell you which ones your units use.

## The editing workflow

1. Name the audience and purpose.
2. **Word choice:** replace vague words with precise ones and match register.
3. **Conventions:** check layout, opening, closing and any expected features of the text type.
4. **Idiom:** keep a fixed expression only if you are sure of its meaning and fit.
5. Read aloud once.

## Worked example 1: word choice

Draft (original): "The trip was good. We saw nice things."
Audience: a teacher reading a trip report. Revise: "The trip was educational. We saw a working water mill and a small museum of local crafts." The words are now precise and the register is suitable for a report.

## Worked example 2: conventions

Draft: "send me the form thanks". Task: ask the school office for a form. Revised with conventions: a greeting, the request in a polite form, a thanks, a sign-off with a name: "Dear Office team, could you please send me the trip form? Thank you. Kind regards, Sam."

## Worked example 3: idiom

A learner writes: "It was a piece of cake." Check: the meaning is "very easy", the register is informal, and the text is a friendly message, so it fits. In a formal report it would not.

## Common errors

- Using an idiom word for word from another language.
- Choosing a formal word where the audience is a friend.
- Fixing grammar only and leaving the text type's conventions unchecked.

## Check your understanding

1. Which three related-concept examples does the brief give? *Word choice, conventions, idiom.*
2. Why check audience before word choice? *The best word depends on who is reading.*
3. When is an idiom a risk? *When you are not sure of its meaning or when the register does not fit.*
