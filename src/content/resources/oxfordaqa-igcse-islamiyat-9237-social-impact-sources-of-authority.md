**This guide covers content area 3.2.2.9 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The social impact of sources of authority on the character of a Muslim: "The rights of neighbours including the importance of Amanat. The prohibition of backbiting, arrogance and jealousy."** The prescribed passage is Sahih al-Bukhari 6016.

The previous content area looked at the personal side of character. This one looks at how a Muslim behaves towards other people. The Qur'an and Hadith treat good relations with others as a part of faith, not an extra. The Prophet (pbuh) taught that the believers, in their mutual kindness and compassion, are like one body: if one part suffers, the rest respond with sleeplessness and fever (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim).

## The rights of neighbours

**The teaching.** The Qur'an places neighbours in the same verse as worship of Allah, parents and relatives: "Worship Allah and associate nothing with Him, and to parents do good, and to relatives, orphans, the needy, the near neighbour, the neighbour farther away, the companion at your side, the traveller ..." (4:36). Scholars understand "near" and "far" neighbours in several ways: the neighbour who is a relative and one who is not, or the Muslim and the non-Muslim neighbour, or the one at your door and the one further away.

**The Hadith.**

- The Prophet (pbuh) said that Jibril kept advising him about the neighbour until he thought the neighbour would be made an heir (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim).
- "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his neighbour" (Sahih al-Bukhari).
- He advised that when you cook a stew, you add more water and share some with your neighbours (Sahih Muslim).
- The prescribed Hadith (Sahih al-Bukhari 6016): the Prophet (pbuh) said, "By Allah, he does not believe!" and when asked who, replied: "One whose neighbour does not feel safe from his evil."

**What neighbours are owed.**

| Right | Examples |
|---|---|
| Safety | No harm to their person, family or property |
| Respect for privacy | Not looking into their homes or spreading their secrets |
| Consideration | Keeping noise down, parking sensibly, not blocking light or access, looking after rubbish |
| Kindness | Greeting, smiling, sharing food and gifts |
| Help in need | Visiting the sick, helping in hardship, supporting the elderly |
| Fair dealing in disputes | Settling problems politely and not holding grudges |

**Non-Muslim neighbours.** The duty is not limited to Muslims. A report says that Abdullah ibn Amr (RA) asked his household whether they had given a gift to their Jewish neighbour, because he had heard the Prophet (pbuh) stress the neighbour's rights.

**Why the Hadith is so strong.** The Prophet (pbuh) denied faith to a person who harms his neighbour. Scholars explain that this means perfect faith: someone whose neighbours fear his harm lacks something essential in his faith.

**Social impact.** Safe, caring neighbourhoods; shared help; trust between families; a good name for Muslims.

## Amanat (trust)

**Meaning.** Amanat is a trust: whatever is placed in a person's care, such as property, a secret, a position or a duty, and the duty to protect and return it.

**Evidence.**

- "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due" (Qur'an 4:58).
- The successful believers are described as those "who are to their trusts and their promises attentive" (23:8).
- "Do not betray Allah and the Messenger or betray your trusts while you know" (8:27).
- The Prophet (pbuh) said that the signs of a hypocrite are three: when he speaks he lies, when he promises he breaks it, and when he is trusted he betrays (Sahih al-Bukhari).

**Example.** The Prophet (pbuh) was called al-Amin. On the night of the Hijrah he had Hazrat Ali (RA) stay behind to return to their owners the valuables that Makkans had left with him, even though they were his enemies.

**Link to neighbours.** A neighbour is often the first person we trust: with a spare key, the care of a child, a parcel, or knowledge of when the house is empty. Betraying that trust is a double wrong. Amanat also covers keeping secrets, borrowed items, money, public office and honest witness.

**Social impact.** Trade and community life depend on trust. Where Amanat is kept, people can rely on each other.

## The prohibition of backbiting

**Meaning.** Backbiting (*ghibah*) is mentioning something about a person in his absence that he would dislike. The Prophet (pbuh) explained: "Do you know what backbiting is?" The companions said that Allah and His Messenger know best. He said, "It is to mention your brother in a way he would dislike." Someone asked, "What if what I say is true?" He answered: "If it is true, you have backbitten him; if it is not, you have slandered him" (Sahih Muslim).

**The Qur'an.** "Do not spy or backbite each other. Would one of you like to eat the flesh of his brother when dead? You would detest it" (49:12).

**Why it is wrong.** It breaks trust, damages reputations, starts quarrels and reveals a lack of mercy. The picture of eating the flesh of a dead brother is meant to disgust: the person cannot defend himself.

**Exceptions.** Speech is permitted to seek justice from someone with authority, to warn people of danger or fraud, to ask a scholar for a ruling, or to describe a person who is publicly known for wrongdoing. These are for a good purpose, not for gossip.

**Remedies.** Stay silent or change the subject; defend the person who is absent; ask Allah's forgiveness; and seek the pardon of the person wronged.

## The prohibition of arrogance

**Meaning.** Arrogance (*kibr*) is thinking oneself better than others and refusing to accept the truth or to treat people as equals. The Prophet (pbuh) said that no one with an atom's weight of arrogance in his heart will enter Paradise, and he defined arrogance as rejecting the truth and looking down on people (Sahih Muslim).

**The Qur'an.** Luqman advised his son: "Do not turn your cheek [in contempt] toward people and do not walk through the earth exultantly. Indeed, Allah does not like everyone self-deluded and boastful" (31:18). The first example of arrogance is Iblis, who refused to bow to Adam because he thought himself better (7:12).

**Why it is wrong.** It leads to contempt for the poor, injustice, refusal to take advice and division. Taqwa, not status, decides a person's worth (49:13).

**The opposite.** Humility (*tawadu'*). The Prophet (pbuh) sat with the poor, helped with housework and mended his own shoes.

## The prohibition of jealousy

**Meaning.** Jealousy or envy (*hasad*) is wishing that another person lose a blessing, and sometimes working to bring it about. It is different from *ghibtah*, wishing to have something good like another without wanting him to lose it, which is acceptable.

**The Qur'an.** Surah al-Falaq asks for refuge "from the evil of an envier when he envies" (113:5). Believers are told not to wish for what Allah has given some above others (4:32).

**The Hadith.** "Do not envy one another, do not hate one another, do not turn away from one another, and be servants of Allah as brothers" (Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim).

**Why it is wrong.** It denies Allah's wisdom in giving as He wills, harms the envier's own peace, and can lead to lies, plotting and violence. The first murder in history, the killing of one son of Adam by his brother, arose from envy (5:27-30).

**The remedy.** Gratitude; praying for the other person; remembering that provision comes from Allah.

## The prescribed passage: Sahih al-Bukhari 6016

| Part | Use |
|---|---|
| "By Allah, he does not believe!" | The seriousness of harming a neighbour |
| "Who is that, O Allah's Messenger?" | A question of how to identify the offence |
| "One whose neighbour does not feel safe from his evil" | The test of faith is how others feel around you |

Use it to link faith and behaviour: belief that does not produce safe, kind relations is incomplete.

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Muslims should respect the rights of their neighbours. Use a source of authority. **[6]**

> The Prophet (pbuh) linked respect for neighbours with faith [1]: he said that a person whose neighbour does not feel safe from his evil does not believe, so harming neighbours weakens a person's faith [1]. Good neighbours also make a community safe and caring [1], because families can trust each other and help each other in hardship [1]. The Hadith says that Jibril kept advising the Prophet (pbuh) about neighbours until he thought they would be made heirs, which shows how high their rights are [2].

## Common mistakes

- **Writing only about gifts.** Neighbours' rights include safety, privacy and consideration.
- **Limiting neighbours to Muslims.** The duty extends to non-Muslims.
- **Defining backbiting as lying.** Backbiting can be true; lying about someone is slander.
- **Confusing jealousy with admiration.** Jealousy wishes the loss of a blessing.
- **Ignoring Amanat.** The specification links it to neighbours' rights.
