These notes condense section **3.1.4.3 Applications of Hess's law** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Hess's law study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-applications-of-hesss-law/) explains each cycle with worked examples, and the [Hess's law practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-applications-of-hesss-law-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- **Hess's law.**
- Use Hess's law to calculate enthalpy changes for reactions from **enthalpies of combustion** or **enthalpies of formation**.
- Practical link: find ΔH using Hess's law and calorimetry, for example thermal decomposition of NaHCO₃ or hydration of MgSO₄ or CuSO₄.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hess's law | The enthalpy change of a reaction is independent of the route, provided initial and final conditions are the same |
| Enthalpy cycle | A diagram showing alternative routes between reactants and products |
| ΔfH⦵ | Standard enthalpy of formation (one mole of compound from its elements) |
| ΔcH⦵ | Standard enthalpy of combustion (one mole completely burned in oxygen) |
| ΔrH⦵ | Standard enthalpy change of reaction |
| Hydration | Water of crystallisation taken up by an anhydrous salt |

## The two formulae

| Data given | Formula |
|---|---|
| Enthalpies of **formation** | ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔfH⦵(products) − ΣΔfH⦵(reactants) |
| Enthalpies of **combustion** | ΔrH⦵ = ΣΔcH⦵(reactants) − ΣΔcH⦵(products) |

- Multiply each value by the number of moles in the balanced equation.
- ΔfH⦵ of an element in its standard state is **zero**.
- Check the sign and give the unit kJ mol⁻¹.

Memory aid: **formation** data point **to** products from elements (elements at the bottom), and **combustion** data point **down** from the substances to combustion products.

## Method for cycles

1. Write the balanced equation with state symbols.
2. Draw the cycle: elements at the bottom for formation; combustion products at the bottom for combustion.
3. Label each arrow with the data times the moles.
4. Route 1 = route 2.
5. Solve, check the sign and give the unit.

## Combining equations

| Operation on an equation | Effect on ΔH |
|---|---|
| Reverse it | Change the sign |
| Multiply by n | Multiply ΔH by n |
| Add two equations | Add the ΔH values |

Cancel species that appear on both sides.

## Worked reminders

**Formation data.** C₂H₅OH(l) + 3O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + 3H₂O(l).
ΔfH⦵: C₂H₅OH −277, CO₂ −394, H₂O(l) −286.
ΔrH⦵ = [2(−394) + 3(−286)] − [−277] = −1646 + 277 = **−1369 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Combustion data.** Formation of ethanol from 2C + 3H₂ + ½O₂.
ΔcH⦵: C −394, H₂ −286, ethanol −1367.
ΔrH⦵ = [2(−394) + 3(−286)] − [−1367] = −1646 + 1367 = **−279 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Decomposition.** 2NaHCO₃(s) → Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g).
ΔfH⦵: NaHCO₃ −948, Na₂CO₃ −1131, H₂O(l) −286, CO₂ −394.
ΔrH⦵ = (−1131 − 286 − 394) − 2(−948) = −1811 + 1896 = **+85 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Equations.** C + O₂ → CO₂ (−394) and CO + ½O₂ → CO₂ (−283) give C + ½O₂ → CO: −394 − (−283) = **−111 kJ mol⁻¹**.

**Hydration.** MgSO₄(s) + 7H₂O(l) → MgSO₄·7H₂O(s). Enthalpy of solution of MgSO₄(s) = −91 kJ mol⁻¹ and of the hydrate = +16 kJ mol⁻¹.
Both routes reach MgSO₄(aq): ΔH(hydration) + 16 = −91, so ΔH(hydration) = **−107 kJ mol⁻¹**.

## Why some enthalpy changes cannot be measured directly

- The reaction is too slow.
- The reaction does not go to completion.
- A mixture of products forms (carbon burning in limited oxygen gives CO and CO₂).
- The conditions are difficult to control, or the reaction is hazardous.

## Practical use of calorimetry with Hess's law

For decomposition of sodium hydrogencarbonate: measure the temperature change when NaHCO₃(s) reacts with excess hydrochloric acid, and when Na₂CO₃(s) reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. Use both enthalpy changes in a cycle to find the enthalpy change of decomposition, which cannot be measured directly.

## Common mistakes

- Missing the multiplication by the number of moles.
- Product minus reactant for combustion data (it should be reactant minus product).
- Arrows in the wrong direction.
- Wrong sign when reversing an equation.
- Using ΔfH⦵ of O₂ as non-zero.
- Mixing the units kJ and J.
- Not giving a sign for the final answer.

## Self-test

1. State Hess's law.
2. State the formula for ΔrH⦵ from enthalpies of formation.
3. State the formula for ΔrH⦵ from enthalpies of combustion.
4. Calculate ΔrH⦵ for CH₄(g) + 2O₂(g) → CO₂(g) + 2H₂O(l), given ΔfH⦵: CH₄ −75, CO₂ −394, H₂O(l) −286.
5. Calculate ΔrH⦵ for Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(s), given ΔfH⦵: Fe₂O₃ −824, Al₂O₃ −1676.
6. Calculate ΔrH⦵ for NH₃(g) + HCl(g) → NH₄Cl(s), given ΔfH⦵: NH₃ −46, HCl −92, NH₄Cl −314.
7. Calculate ΔfH⦵ of propane from ΔcH⦵: C −394, H₂ −286, C₃H₈ −2219.
8. Calculate ΔrH⦵ for C₂H₄(g) + H₂(g) → C₂H₆(g), given ΔcH⦵: C₂H₄ −1411, H₂ −286, C₂H₆ −1560.
9. Give two reasons why an enthalpy change cannot be measured directly.
10. Equation (1) has ΔH = −200 kJ mol⁻¹. State ΔH for the reverse of 3 × equation (1).
11. S(s) + O₂(g) → SO₂(g), ΔH = −297; SO₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → SO₃(g), ΔH = −99. Find ΔfH⦵ of SO₃(g).
12. Why is ΔfH⦵ of O₂(g) zero?

### Answers

1. The enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route taken, provided the initial and final conditions are the same.
2. ΣΔfH⦵(products) − ΣΔfH⦵(reactants).
3. ΣΔcH⦵(reactants) − ΣΔcH⦵(products).
4. (−394 − 572) − (−75) = **−891 kJ mol⁻¹**.
5. −1676 − (−824) = **−852 kJ mol⁻¹**.
6. −314 − (−46 − 92) = **−176 kJ mol⁻¹**.
7. [3(−394) + 4(−286)] − (−2219) = −2326 + 2219 = **−107 kJ mol⁻¹**.
8. (−1411 − 286) − (−1560) = **−137 kJ mol⁻¹**.
9. Any two of: the reaction is too slow, incomplete, gives a mixture of products, or is hard to control.
10. Three times −200 = −600; the reverse is **+600 kJ mol⁻¹**.
11. −297 + (−99) = **−396 kJ mol⁻¹**.
12. It is an element in its standard state, so no change is needed to form it.
