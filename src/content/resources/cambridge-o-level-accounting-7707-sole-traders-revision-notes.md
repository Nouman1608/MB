## What the syllabus asks

1. Advantages and disadvantages of a sole trader.
2. Trading, service, manufacturing and combined businesses.
3. Why statements of profit or loss and of financial position are prepared,
and how.
4. The headings of a statement of financial position.
5. The effect of a change in one balance on others.
6. Adjustments: depreciation (straight-line, reducing balance,
revaluation), accruals and prepayments of expenses and income,
irrecoverable debts and the allowance, drawings of goods, other assets and
private expenses.

## Advantages and disadvantages

**Advantages:** quick decisions; all profit to the owner; simple and cheap to
set up; privacy of accounts; personal contact with customers.

**Disadvantages:** unlimited liability; limited capital; long hours and
reliance on one person; narrower skills; continuity risk.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | Business owned by one person |
| Unlimited liability | Owner personally responsible for all business debts |
| Statement of profit or loss | Shows profit or loss for a period |
| Statement of financial position | Shows assets, liabilities and capital at a date |
| Intangible asset | Non-current asset without physical form, such as goodwill |
| Drawings | Money, goods or other assets taken by the owner for private use |

## Statement of profit or loss skeleton (trading business)

```
Revenue − sales returns
Less cost of sales:
  opening inventory + (purchases − purchases returns − goods for own use)
  + carriage inwards − closing inventory
= Gross profit
Add other income (rent received, commission, fees, discount received,
  profit on disposal, decrease in allowance, debts recovered)
Less expenses (adjusted for accruals and prepayments; depreciation;
  irrecoverable debts; increase in allowance; carriage outwards;
  discount allowed; loan interest)
= Profit (or loss) for the year
```

**Service business:** no cost of sales; start with fees or income
earned, add other income, deduct expenses.

## Statement of financial position skeleton

```
Non-current assets: intangible (goodwill); tangible at cost
  less accumulated depreciation
Current assets: inventory; trade receivables less allowance;
  other receivables; bank; cash
Capital: opening + capital introduced + profit − drawings
Non-current liabilities: loans repayable after more than a year
Current liabilities: trade payables; other payables; bank overdraft
```

## Adjustment quick table

| Adjustment | One statement | The other |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciation $X | Expense +X | Accumulated depreciation +X |
| Wages owing $X | Wages +X | Other payables +X |
| Rent prepaid $X | Rent −X | Other receivables +X |
| Commission owed to business $X | Income +X | Other receivables +X |
| Rent received in advance $X | Income −X | Other payables +X |
| Debt written off $X | Expense +X | Trade receivables −X |
| Allowance up $X | Expense +X | Allowance (deduction) +X |
| Goods for own use $X | Purchases −X | Drawings +X |
| Private bill paid by business $X | Expense −X | Drawings +X |

## Effect of a change in one balance

Follow the double entry to both statements. Example: closing inventory
$500 higher → cost of sales −$500 → gross profit and profit +$500 →
capital +$500 and current assets +$500.

## Exam traps

- Goods taken by the owner reduce **purchases**, not revenue.
- Private expenses go to drawings, not expenses.
- Carriage inwards: cost of sales. Carriage outwards: expense.
- Change in allowance in the statement of profit or loss; full allowance in
the statement of financial position.
- Loan interest is an expense; the loan itself is a liability.
- "For the year ended" versus "at".
- Do not add drawings to expenses.

## Quick self-test

1. State **two** disadvantages of operating as a sole trader.
2. Explain the importance of a statement of financial position.
3. In which section does goodwill appear?
4. Purchases $40,000; purchases returns $600; owner took goods costing
$400. Calculate net purchases.
5. General expenses $3,200 include $250 of the owner's home electricity.
Give the expense and the drawings adjustment.
6. Revenue $90,000; sales returns $1,000; cost of sales $52,000. Calculate
gross profit.
7. Where is income received in advance shown?
8. A bank loan is repayable in four years. Where is it shown?
9. Opening capital $30,000; profit $18,000; drawings $12,500; capital
introduced $2,000. Calculate closing capital.
10. Insurance prepaid was omitted. State the effect on profit and on
current assets.
11. How does a service business's statement of profit or loss differ from
a trading business's?
12. Why might a sole trader find it hard to expand?

### Answers

1. Any two: unlimited liability; limited capital; long hours; reliance on
one person; narrow skills; continuity risk.
2. It records assets, liabilities and capital on a specified date, showing
what the business owns and owes and the owner's stake; lenders and the
owner use it to judge liquidity and financial strength.
3. Non-current assets, as an intangible asset.
4. $40,000 − $600 − $400 = $39,000.
5. General expenses $2,950; drawings increase by $250.
6. $89,000 − $52,000 = $37,000.
7. Current liabilities, as other payables.
8. Non-current liabilities.
9. $30,000 + $2,000 + $18,000 − $12,500 = $37,500.
10. Profit understated; current assets understated, both by the
prepayment.
11. It has no cost of sales or gross profit; fees earned are followed by
other income and expenses.
12. Capital is limited to the owner's own funds and what lenders will
provide, and unlimited liability makes borrowing risky.
