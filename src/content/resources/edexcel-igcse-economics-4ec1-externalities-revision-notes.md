These notes condense section 1.1.6, Externalities, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, February 2026, examined in Paper 1 with a single tier of entry. All figures are invented. For explanations and worked cases, read the [externalities study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-externalities/); to practise, use the [externalities practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-externalities-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Third party | Anyone affected by a transaction who is neither the buyer nor the seller |
| Externality | A cost or benefit of an activity that falls on a third party |
| External cost | A cost of production borne by third parties, not paid for by the producer |
| External benefit | A benefit of consumption received by third parties, who do not pay for it |
| Private cost | Cost paid by the producer (or consumer) making the decision |
| Private benefit | Benefit received by the consumer making the decision |
| Social cost | Private costs + external costs |
| Social benefit | Private benefits + external benefits |

## Specification examples, with third parties

| Type | Example | Typical third party |
|---|---|---|
| External cost of production | Pollution | Residents whose health suffers; farmers whose crops are damaged |
| External cost of production | Congestion | Other drivers delayed; bus passengers; businesses on the route |
| External cost of production | Environmental damage | People who lose a landscape; wildlife losing habitat |
| External benefit of consumption | Education | Employers gaining skilled workers; society gaining higher output |
| External benefit of consumption | Healthcare | Employers losing fewer working days; families providing less care |
| External benefit of consumption | Vaccinations | Others less likely to catch the disease |

**Answer pattern:** activity → harm or benefit → named third party → not paid for or compensated.

## The formulae

```
social costs    = private costs    + external costs
social benefits = private benefits + external benefits
```

Rearranged:

```
external costs    = social costs    − private costs
private costs     = social costs    − external costs
external benefits = social benefits − private benefits
private benefits  = social benefits − external benefits
```

## Calculation drills (invented figures)

- Private cost 45, external cost 15 per unit → social cost **60**.
- Social benefit 120, private benefit 80 → external benefit **40**.
- 2 000 units with external cost 6 each → total external cost **12 000**.
- External cost as a share of social cost: 15 ÷ 60 × 100 = **25%**.

**Method:** write the formula, substitute, label each figure, give the unit if there is one.

## Why markets get it wrong

| | External costs of production | External benefits of consumption |
|---|---|---|
| What decision-makers ignore | Costs to third parties | Benefits to third parties |
| Result | Over-production | Under-consumption |
| Link | Market failure (1.1.5) | Market failure (1.1.5) |
| Policies (1.2.6) | Taxes, fines, regulation, pollution permits | Subsidies, regulation |

## Classifying costs and benefits

Ask: *who pays, or who gains?*

| Item | Classification |
|---|---|
| A factory's electricity bill | Private cost |
| Illness among residents caused by the factory's smoke | External cost |
| A graduate's higher salary | Private benefit |
| An employer's gain from the graduate's skills, not reflected in the salary | External benefit |
| A vaccinated person's protection | Private benefit |
| Reduced spread of disease to others | External benefit |

## Exam traps

- **Third party test:** a cost paid by the firm is private, however large.
- **Do not mix formulae:** costs add to costs, benefits to benefits.
- **Social ≠ external:** social is the total, private plus external.
- **Use the specification's pairings:** external costs of production, external benefits of consumption.
- **Name a third party** in every example.
- **No diagram is required** by section 1.1.6; definitions, examples and formulae are.

## Command words

- **Define / What is meant by:** two separate parts.
- **State:** a short answer, such as one example.
- **Calculate:** formula, substitution, answer.
- **Explain:** statement plus two developments in the context given.

## Quick self-test

1. Define external costs.
2. Define external benefits.
3. Give the three examples of external costs named in the specification.
4. Give the three examples of external benefits named in the specification.
5. State the formula for social costs.
6. State the formula for social benefits.
7. A firm's private cost is 30 per unit and the external cost is 12 per unit. Calculate the social cost per unit.
8. The social benefit of a vaccination is 90 and the private benefit is 35. Calculate the external benefit.
9. Is a firm's wage bill a private or external cost?
10. Explain why goods with external costs tend to be over-produced.
11. Identify a third party affected by congestion caused by delivery lorries.
12. External costs are 18 000 and social costs are 72 000. Calculate private costs and the external share of social costs.

### Answers

1. Costs of production that fall on third parties and are not paid for by the producer.
2. Benefits of consumption received by third parties who do not pay for them.
3. Pollution, congestion and environmental damage.
4. Education, healthcare and vaccinations.
5. Social costs = private costs + external costs.
6. Social benefits = private benefits + external benefits.
7. 30 + 12 = **42**.
8. 90 − 35 = **55**.
9. Private: it is paid by the firm.
10. Producers ignore the external costs, so they base output on private costs below the true social cost, and produce more than is best for society.
11. Other road users delayed by the traffic (or bus passengers, or local businesses).
12. Private costs = 72 000 − 18 000 = **54 000**; share = 18 000 ÷ 72 000 × 100 = **25%**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Examples without a third party.
- Treating a cost the firm pays as external.
- Writing social cost as external cost alone.
- Swapping the examples between costs and benefits.
- Calculations with no formula shown.
