Condensed notes on section **3.1.2 Understanding different business forms** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and the profit warning example are in the [business forms study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-different-business-forms/); exam-style questions are in the [business forms practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-different-business-forms-practice/).

## Specification content

- Reasons for choosing different forms of business and for changing form: sole traders; private and public limited companies; private and public sector organisations; non-profit organisations; social enterprises.
- Issues: unlimited and limited liability; ordinary share capital; market capitalisation; dividends.
- The role of shareholders and why they invest.
- Influences on share price and the significance of share price changes.
- The effects of ownership on mission and objectives.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | Business owned by one person, with unlimited liability |
| Private limited company (Ltd) | Company whose shares are not offered to the general public |
| Public limited company (plc) | Company that can offer shares to the public and have them traded |
| Separate legal entity | A company exists in law apart from its owners |
| Limited liability | Shareholders can lose only what they invested |
| Unlimited liability | Owner's personal possessions can be taken to pay business debts |
| Public sector organisation | Owned or controlled by the state |
| Non-profit organisation | Exists for a purpose; any surplus is reinvested, not paid to owners |
| Social enterprise | Trades to achieve social or environmental aims, reinvesting most surplus |
| Ordinary share capital | Money raised by selling ordinary shares |
| Dividend | Share of profit paid to shareholders |
| Market capitalisation | Number of shares issued × current share price |

## Choosing a form: five issues

1. **Risk**: is limited liability needed?
2. **Finance**: how much capital is required?
3. **Control**: how much ownership is the founder willing to give up?
4. **Privacy and administration**: published accounts and scrutiny for companies.
5. **Purpose**: profit for owners or a social aim?

## Changing form

| Change | Why | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| Sole trader → Ltd | Limit risk; bring in investors | Some loss of control; registration and published accounts |
| Ltd → plc | Raise large capital; let shareholders sell easily | Flotation cost; takeover risk; short-term pressure |
| plc → Ltd | Escape market pressure; regain control | Buying out shareholders, often with debt |
| Public → private sector | Private finance, efficiency | Profit may conflict with service to all |

## Shareholders

- **Role**: provide long-term capital; bear risk; vote on directors and major decisions.
- **Why invest**: dividends; capital growth; influence; personal or social reasons.
- Ordinary share capital need not be repaid and carries no fixed return.

## Share prices

**Influences:** profits and forecasts; dividends; the economy and interest rates; industry news; takeover rumours; market confidence.

**Significance of changes:**

- Shareholder wealth and confidence in directors.
- A lower price makes a takeover cheaper.
- A higher price makes new share issues easier.
- Directors' rewards often linked to share price.
- Reputation with lenders and suppliers.
- **Not** a direct change in the company's cash.

## Ownership → mission and objectives

| Owner | Typical emphasis |
|---|---|
| Sole trader | Owner's values, independence, satisfactory profit |
| Family Ltd | Long-term survival, control kept in the family |
| plc | Profit, dividends, share price; directors and shareholders may want different things |
| Public sector | Service, access, value for money |
| Social enterprise / non-profit | The social aim first |

## Exam traps

- A plc is in the **private** sector.
- Limited liability protects the **owners**, not the business.
- A share price fall does not take cash out of the company.
- Market capitalisation uses the **current** price, not the price at which shares were first sold.
- Not every growing business should become a plc.

## Self-test

1. Why does a shareholder in a company have limited liability while a sole trader does not?
2. Give two reasons a sole trader might become a private limited company.
3. Give two drawbacks of becoming a plc.
4. Why might a plc decide to go private?
5. A company has 8 million shares priced at £3.40. Calculate its market capitalisation.
6. The company in question 5 pays total dividends of £1.2 million. Calculate the dividend per share.
7. The share price in question 5 falls by 25%. Calculate the new market capitalisation.
8. Does the fall in question 7 reduce the company's cash? Explain.
9. Give three influences on a company's share price.
10. Give two reasons why investors buy shares.
11. How might the objectives of a family-owned Ltd differ from those of a plc?
12. What is the main difference in purpose between a social enterprise and a plc?

### Answers

1. A company is a separate legal entity, so its debts are its own; a sole trader and the business are legally the same.
2. Any two: limit personal risk; bring in investors' capital; appear more established to customers and lenders.
3. Any two: cost of flotation; risk of takeover; short-term pressure from investors; loss of control; public scrutiny.
4. To escape short-term pressure from the stock market and regain control over strategy.
5. 8,000,000 × £3.40 = **£27.2m**.
6. £1,200,000 ÷ 8,000,000 = **15p**.
7. New price £2.55; 8,000,000 × £2.55 = **£20.4m**.
8. No. Shares traded on the stock market pass between investors, so the company's cash is unchanged; the fall affects shareholder wealth and takeover risk.
9. Any three: profits and forecasts; dividends; the economy and interest rates; industry news; takeover rumours; market confidence.
10. Any two: dividends; capital growth; influence; personal or social reasons.
11. A family Ltd may put long-term survival and control first; a plc's shareholders often press for profit, dividends and share price growth.
12. A social enterprise trades mainly to achieve a social aim and reinvests most surplus; a plc aims mainly to make returns for its shareholders.
