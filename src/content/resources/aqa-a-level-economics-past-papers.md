This page is a guide to AQA A Level Economics (7136) past papers for June 2024 and June 2025. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-level Economics (7136) specification page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/a-level/economics-7136)
- [AQA A-level Economics (7136) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/economics/a-level/economics-7136/assessment-resources), where question papers, inserts, mark schemes and reports are listed by series
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which explains the levels of response. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it shows why answers that looked reasonable stayed in the middle levels. The notes below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. A-level Economics (7136) is linear and untiered, for first teaching from September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in June 2017. Both series covered here were set on that specification. The AS qualification (7135) has its own page, because its papers and boundaries are separate.

### Exam structure

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7136/1 | Markets and market failure | 2 hours | 80 | one third |
| 7136/2 | National and international economy | 2 hours | 80 | one third |
| 7136/3 | Economic principles and issues | 2 hours | 80 | one third |

Papers 1 and 2 each have a data-response section (a choice of one from two contexts, 40 marks) and an essay section (a choice from three, 40 marks). Paper 3 draws on all the content: a multiple-choice section and an investigation based on case-study material. All three papers are marked mainly by levels of response.

## June 2025

The contexts on Paper 1 were the international rice market and the UK water industry, and on Paper 2 the Norwegian krone and the BRICS+ group. Examiners across the three papers praised accurate diagrams that were actually used in the argument, and criticised data answers that quoted figures without explaining them. A recurring slip was reaching for a tariff diagram or the wrong type of diagram when the question did not need it.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7136/1), data response: calculating the change in a figure instead of the change in a share; mean used instead of median; units missing; using only one of the two data sources in a 4-mark "explain how the data shows" answer; a monopoly diagram drawn where minimum efficient scale needed a falling long-run average cost curve; maximum price policies praised without weighing the consequences of excess demand.
- Paper 1, essays: private, public and quasi-public goods were explained well, though the NHS was wrongly given as a public good. The technology essay lacked definitions and named no specific cost. The question on profit objectives was the weakest, where students described monopoly harm instead of why firms pursue profit, and thought profit maximisation always means abnormal profit.
- Paper 2 (7136/2), data response: a change in currency value needed the correct sign; students quoted data without explaining it; FDI was confused with hot money; some answers gave too much weight to protectionism and to history rather than the future growth asked about.
- Paper 2, essays: national debt confused with household debt or the trade deficit; low and stable inflation confused with falling prices; the Phillips curve misused to say low inflation causes high unemployment; development answers narrowed to tariffs or drifted to growth.
- Paper 3 (7136/3), multiple choice: facility was around 50%, with eight questions at 30% or lower. The hard ones covered normal profit, the multiplier against the accelerator, bank liquidity and capital ratios, who receives tariff revenue, and what "real terms" means for debt.
- Paper 3, investigation: quoting figures wrongly or ignoring units, using aggregate demand and supply for a single market, defining the balance of trade by quantities rather than values, and answers that discussed decline without assessing the industry's contribution.

**What went well:** diagram-led explanations of the effect of a supply shock in a market with inelastic demand; the exchange-rate-and-inflation explanation with an AD/AS diagram; essays on labour demand and the minimum wage; evaluating regulation against public ownership; and evidence-based use of the data in the better investigation answers.

## June 2024

Paper 1 had contexts on university accommodation and on trade unions, and Paper 2 on productivity and living standards and on globalisation. The examiners' main message on Paper 1 was that data questions asked for evidence from every source given and an explanation of what it showed, and on Paper 2 that the command word "explain" was often ignored. No report for Paper 3 is listed by AQA for this series, so there are no notes for it.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7136/1): converting money to real terms was done correctly by under 10% of students on the calculation; only one of two data sources was used; the elasticity of supply was confused with elasticity of demand; a labour shortage was described as a shift of supply when a shortage is the result of the shift; information failure was discussed as if it were an externality; consumer and producer surplus were not explained; price discrimination was assumed always to harm consumers; terminology such as "average costs" was used loosely.
- Paper 2 (7136/2): a ratio given the wrong way round or not as a ratio; data quoted but not explained in "explain" questions; weaker answers to the exchange-rate and inflation question had no diagram; corporation tax treated as a cost of production; essays treated cyclical unemployment as a determinant of the natural rate and merged the multiplier and accelerator.

**What went well:** accurate supply and demand diagrams for the accommodation market; essays on economies and diseconomies of scale; policy comparisons in the productivity evaluation; and the essay on reasons for a recession.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find the June 2025 7136 papers and mark schemes?**
On AQA's assessment-resources page for 7136, linked above. Papers, inserts, mark schemes and reports are listed by series.

**Are the grade thresholds the same every year?**
No. AQA sets them after each series. Compare the tables: an A grade needed 153 out of 240 in June 2025 and 149 in June 2024. Do not assume a future series will match.

**Is there a choice of questions?**
On Papers 1 and 2 students choose one of two data-response contexts and one of three essays, so practise by picking the contexts you would be less likely to choose, not only your favourites.

**What are notional component boundaries?**
They are illustrative boundaries for individual papers. The grade is awarded on the total mark out of 240.

**Why is there no June 2024 note for Paper 3?**
AQA's assessment-resources page lists no report on the examination for 7136/3 in that series.
