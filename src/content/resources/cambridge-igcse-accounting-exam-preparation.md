Cambridge IGCSE Accounting (0452, 2027-2029 syllabus, first assessed in 2027) has two compulsory, externally assessed papers and is not tiered. Paper 1 Multiple Choice is 1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks and 30%, with all 40 questions answered. Paper 2 Structured Written Paper is 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and 70%, with five compulsory 20-mark questions. Calculators are permitted in both papers. Prepare with the topic pages on [accounting procedures](/resources/igcse-accounting-accounting-procedures/), [bank reconciliation](/resources/igcse-accounting-bank-reconciliation/) and [analysis and interpretation](/resources/igcse-accounting-analysis-and-interpretation/).

## Which syllabus edition applies

The 2027-2029 edition is examined in the June and November series, and in March in India, from 2027. The earlier 2026 edition, where Paper 1 was 1 hour 15 minutes and 35 marks, is still examined in November 2026, its final series. If you are sitting in 2027 or later, work from the structure stated above and not from older papers' timings.

## Paper 1: Multiple Choice (1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks, 30%)

Paper 1 has 40 questions, all to be answered, for 40 marks. With 90 minutes available, that is 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes per question, which is generous for single-step bookkeeping decisions and tight for multi-step calculations. Work through the paper once answering what you are sure of, then return to calculation items. Because calculators are allowed, do not skip a calculation to save time; a quick check usually settles a close choice. Questions can draw on any topic, so the whole of the syllabus is in play: fundamentals, sources and recording of data, verification, procedures, financial statements, analysis and interpretation, and accounting concepts and modern practice.

## Paper 2: Structured Written Paper (1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 70%)

Paper 2 has five compulsory 20-mark questions. At 105 minutes for 100 marks, you have 105 / 100 = 1.05 minutes per mark, so each 20-mark question deserves about 105 / 5 = 21 minutes. Because every question is compulsory and equal in marks, there is no choosing between questions, only pacing. Prepare for extended tasks such as ledger entries, reconciliations, preparing financial statements and interpreting ratios. The study guides on [business documents and books of prime entry](/resources/igcse-accounting-business-documents-and-books-of-prime-entry/) and [accounting concepts, ethics, technology and sustainability](/resources/igcse-accounting-accounting-concepts-ethics-technology-and-sustainabilit/) cover areas that students often leave until last.

## Timing and marks per minute

- Paper 1: 90 minutes / 40 marks = 2.25 minutes per mark (and per question).
- Paper 2: 105 minutes / 100 marks = 1.05 minutes per mark.
- Paper 2, per 20-mark question: 105 / 5 = 21 minutes.
- Combined: 90 + 105 = 195 minutes for 40 + 100 = 140 marks.

Paper 2 carries 70% of the qualification, so a slow start on Question 1 costs more than anywhere else. Set a clock-check at roughly 21, 42, 63 and 84 minutes into the paper.

## Command words and question styles

Accounting uses a short set of instructions. "Calculate" wants the formula, the figures and the result. "Prepare" wants a statement or account in the correct layout with headings, dates and totals. "State" wants a brief fact. "Explain" wants a reason, often a business consequence. "Comment" and "discuss" want a reasoned view that uses the figures. Format is part of the skill: labels, totals and column headings usually matter alongside the numbers. Check the syllabus for the exact definitions used.

## Worked practice scenario

Original question: "A trader's year-end figures are: sales 180 000, sales returns 4 000, opening inventory 22 000, purchases 98 000, closing inventory 26 000, and expenses 41 000. Prepare the trading section, calculate gross profit and net profit, and state the gross profit margin."

```
Net sales            = 180 000 - 4 000           = 176 000
Cost of sales        = 22 000 + 98 000 - 26 000  =  94 000
Gross profit         = 176 000 - 94 000          =  82 000
Net profit           = 82 000 - 41 000           =  41 000

Gross profit margin  = 82 000 / 176 000 x 100    = 46.6%
Net profit margin    = 41 000 / 176 000 x 100    = 23.3%
```

Show the working for cost of sales as a short build-up, label each subtotal and give percentages to one decimal place. A comment worth a mark: a gross profit margin of 46.6% means that for every 100 of net sales, 46.60 remains after the direct cost of the goods; the business should compare it with prior years or similar businesses before judging whether it is high or low.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Check the paper structure for your year: 40 multiple-choice questions, then five compulsory 20-mark questions.
- Practise Paper 1 in full 90-minute sessions.
- Practise at least one 21-minute, 20-mark question per revision session.
- Learn standard layouts for ledger accounts, trading and profit and loss accounts, and reconciliations.
- Always show workings next to totals.
- Bring and check a permitted calculator.
- Read each requirement line, such as "prepare" or "calculate", before starting.
- Leave a few minutes at the end for arithmetic and labels.

2. How long should each Paper 2 question take if you divide time evenly?
3. What percentage of the qualification is Paper 2?
4. What is the gross profit in the worked scenario?
5. Why does format matter on a "prepare" question?

**Answers:** 1. 40 questions, so 90 / 40 = 2.25 minutes each. 2. 105 / 5 = 21 minutes. 3. 70%. 4. 176 000 - 94 000 = 82 000. 5. Because headings, labels, subtotals and layout are part of the skill being assessed alongside the figures.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452?**
There are two compulsory papers: Paper 1 Multiple Choice and Paper 2 Structured Written Paper. The qualification is not tiered.

**How long is each paper and what is it worth?**
Paper 1 is 1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks and 30%. Paper 2 is 1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks and 70%.

**How many questions are on Paper 2?**
Five compulsory 20-mark questions.

**Can I use a calculator?**
Calculators are permitted in both papers.

**Did the paper structure change?**
Yes. The 2026 edition, with a 1 hour 15 minutes and 35 mark Paper 1, is still examined in November 2026, its final series. The structure above applies from 2027.
