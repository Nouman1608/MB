## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 5: two marks for each name explained). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Power; omnipotence, the ability of Allah to do all things [1].

**2.** Al-Khaliq [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Al-Qadir (omnipotence); Al-Khaliq; Ar-Rehman; Ar-Raheem; Al-Aleem.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: beginning actions with "Bismillah ir-Rahman ir-Raheem"; making du'a using the names; naming children with a name of Allah, such as Abdur-Rahman; learning and reciting the 99 names.

**5.** **Ar-Rehman** is the Most Gracious [1], mercy that covers all creation, believers and non-believers alike, in this world [1]. **Ar-Raheem** is the Merciful [1], a special and lasting mercy for believers, which continues in the Hereafter [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and developed [1].

- **Honesty** [1]: nothing can be hidden from Allah, so a Muslim tries to be truthful even when unobserved [1].
- **Self-examination** [1]: knowing that Allah knows their thoughts, a Muslim checks their intentions and tries to purify them [1].
- **Comfort** [1]: Allah knows their struggles, so they feel supported when others do not understand [1].
- **Trust in the decree** [1]: Allah's knowledge covers the future, so a Muslim trusts that He knows what is best [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Gratitude** [1]: if Allah created everything, then life, health and the world are gifts to be thanked for [1].
- **Care for creation** [1]: humans are trusted as stewards of what Allah created, so they should not waste or harm it [1].
- **Tawhid** [1]: only the Creator deserves worship, which supports belief in One God [1].
- **Human worth** [1]: every person is Allah's creation, which encourages equality and respect [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Allah is the Creator of all things" (Qur'an 13:16) [2].
- "He is Allah, the Creator, the Producer, the Fashioner" (59:24), showing three steps of creation, so Muslims reflect on the world as a sign of Allah [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Hope** [1]: no problem is beyond the power of Allah, so a Muslim does not give up [1].
- **Trust (tawakkul)** [1]: after making an effort, the Muslim leaves the result to Allah and feels calmer [1].
- **Humility** [1]: because Allah alone has complete power, a Muslim does not rely on wealth or status [1].
- **Patience** [1]: the Muslim accepts that Allah can change a hard situation when He wills [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, Allah is over all things competent" (Qur'an 2:20) [2].
- "His command is only when He intends a thing that He says to it, 'Be,' and it is" (36:82) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Mercy gives hope, since Allah's forgiveness is available to those who repent; it motivates repentance instead of despair.
- Mercy shapes Muslim behaviour towards others, who should imitate Allah's mercy by forgiving and giving.
- The Bismillah ("In the name of Allah, the Most Gracious, the Most Merciful") begins every act.
- **Against the statement:** other names matter equally: Al-Aleem makes Muslims accountable; Al-Khaliq explains why there is anything at all; Qudrah and the divine title "Exalted in Might" remind them of Allah's justice. Mercy without justice and knowledge would not be complete.
- **Judgement:** many Muslims would say mercy is central, but it must be balanced with awareness of power, knowledge and judgement; hope and reverence belong together.

*Use of the passage*

- "the Entirely Merciful, the Especially Merciful" directly supports the statement.
- "Knower of the unseen and the witnessed" shows that Allah's mercy is joined to knowledge of every person's situation.
- "the Exalted in Might, the Compeller, the Superior" balances mercy with power.
- "to Him belong the best names" shows that mercy is one of many names, which weakens the claim that it is the only important one.
- "Exalted is Allah above whatever they associate with Him" reminds the reader that Tawhid frames all the names.
