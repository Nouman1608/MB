Condensed notes on section **3.7.1 Mission, corporate objectives, functional objectives and strategy** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). Section 3.7 is A-level only. The full explanations and the worked cycle-maker example are in the [study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-mission-corporate-functional-objectives-strategy/); questions are in the [practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-mission-corporate-functional-objectives-strategy-practice/).

## Specification content

- Influences on the mission of a business.
- Internal and external influences on corporate objectives and decisions, including pressures for short-termism, business ownership, and the external and internal environment.
- The links between mission, corporate objectives and strategy.
- The distinction between strategy and tactics.
- The impact of strategic decision making on functional decision making.
- Internal and external influences on functional objectives and decisions.
- The value of SWOT analysis.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mission | Statement of the purpose and values of the business; rarely measurable |
| Corporate objective | Measurable target for the whole business, usually 3 to 10 years |
| Strategy | Medium- to long-term plan committing major resources to reach the objectives |
| Tactic | Short-term, flexible action that carries out a strategy |
| Functional objective | Target for finance, marketing, operations or human resources that supports the strategy |
| Short-termism | Giving more weight to quick results than to long-term success |
| SWOT | Strengths and weaknesses (internal); opportunities and threats (external) |

## The cascade

**Mission** (why we exist) then **corporate objectives** (what we want overall) then **strategy** (how, over years) then **functional objectives** (what each department must achieve) then **tactics** (what we do this month).

Test of quality: each level must be consistent with the one above and with its neighbours.

## Influences on mission

| Influence | Effect |
|---|---|
| Founders and owners | Their values often define the mission |
| Culture and history | What the business believes it stands for |
| Stakeholders | Expectations on sustainability, fairness, service |
| Market | Fast-changing markets lead to broader missions |
| Resources | A mission must be deliverable or it loses credibility |

## Influences on corporate objectives

| Internal | External |
|---|---|
| Ownership (plc: returns; social enterprise: impact; sole trader: income) | The economy and demand |
| Resources and finance | Competitors' actions |
| Leadership and culture | Law and regulation |
| Past performance | Technology and social change |

## Short-termism

- **Causes:** quarterly results; bonuses linked to current profit or share price; takeover threat; lenders wanting quick repayment.
- **Effects:** cuts to research, training and investment; weaker long-term growth.
- **Balance:** some short-term pressure prevents waste and drift. Patient owners (family, private, social) find long-term objectives easier.

## Strategy against tactics

| | Strategy | Tactics |
|---|---|---|
| Scope | Whole business or major part | One function or situation |
| Time | Medium to long term | Short term |
| Reversibility | Costly to reverse | Easy to change |
| Decided by | Board and senior managers | Middle and junior managers |

## Strategy and functional decisions

- Operations: capacity, suppliers, cost targets.
- Marketing: segments, brand, awareness targets.
- Finance: funding, gearing, budgets.
- Human resources: recruitment, training, pay.
- Functional objectives must be consistent with each other. A growth target is unrealistic if operations cannot raise output.
- **External influences:** customers, competitors, suppliers, employment law, interest rates, technology. **Internal:** strategy, budgets, skills, morale, other functions' capacity.

## SWOT

| | Value | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Use | Quick, cheap; links inside to outside; involves many staff | A list, not an analysis; no ranking |
| Judgement | Starting point for objectives | Subjective; a snapshot; items can be misplaced |

To make SWOT valuable: back each point with data, rank by importance, and turn the results into specific objectives.

## Exam traps

- Mission is **not** a measurable target; a corporate objective is.
- Strategy is about scale, time and commitment of resources, not simply "importance".
- Short-termism is not always harmful; evaluate.
- SWOT strengths and weaknesses are **internal**; opportunities and threats are **external**.
- Apply to the business in the case. Generic answers lose application marks.

## Self-test

1. Define "mission".
2. Give two differences between a corporate objective and a mission.
3. State two causes of short-termism.
4. Give one way short-termism might harm a business over ten years.
5. Why might a plc and a social enterprise set different corporate objectives?
6. Explain the difference between a strategy and a tactic using an example.
7. Why must functional objectives be consistent with each other?
8. Give two external influences on a marketing objective.
9. Classify as S, W, O or T: a new law banning plastic packaging; a business with a long-serving skilled workforce.
10. Give two limitations of SWOT analysis.
11. How can a business make a SWOT analysis more useful?
12. Explain why a mission statement of "to be the best" is of limited value.

### Answers

1. A statement of the purpose and values of the business, describing why it exists.
2. Any two: a mission is general, a corporate objective is specific; a mission is rarely measurable, an objective is; a mission rarely changes, objectives are set for a period and reviewed.
3. Any two: quarterly or half-yearly reporting; bonuses tied to current profit or share price; takeover threat; lender pressure.
4. For example, cutting research spending to raise profit now means fewer new products later and weaker competitiveness.
5. Because their owners want different things: a plc's shareholders expect returns, so profit and share price targets; a social enterprise puts social or environmental impact first.
6. A strategy is a long-term plan with big commitments, such as entering a new overseas market; a tactic is a short-term action carrying it out, such as a two-week promotion in that market.
7. Because each department depends on the others. A marketing growth target cannot be met if operations cannot produce the extra output, or finance cannot fund it.
8. Any two: customer tastes; competitors' pricing; advertising regulation; technology such as social media; economic conditions.
9. A new law banning plastic packaging: threat (or opportunity if the business already uses alternatives); a long-serving skilled workforce: strength.
10. Any two: it is only a list; it is subjective; it is a snapshot; it does not rank issues; it does not say what to do.
11. Support each point with data, rank them, and link them to specific objectives and decisions.
12. It is vague and cannot guide decisions or be measured; it does not say best at what, or for whom.
