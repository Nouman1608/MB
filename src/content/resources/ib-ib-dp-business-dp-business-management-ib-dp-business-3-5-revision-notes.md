## Formulae

| Ratio | Formula | Answer form |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | (gross profit ÷ sales revenue) × 100 | % |
| Profit margin | (profit before interest and tax ÷ sales revenue) × 100 | % |
| ROCE | (profit before interest and tax ÷ capital employed) × 100 | % |
| Current ratio | current assets ÷ current liabilities | Ratio, e.g. 1.4 or 1.4:1 |
| Acid test (quick) ratio | (current assets − stock) ÷ current liabilities | Ratio |

Capital employed = non-current liabilities + equity.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Profitability | How well a business turns sales or capital into profit |
| Liquidity | Ability to pay short-term debts when they fall due |
| Gross profit | Sales revenue minus cost of sales |
| Profit before interest and tax | Gross profit minus expenses, before interest and tax are deducted |
| Capital employed | Long-term funds used: non-current liabilities plus equity |
| Current assets | Cash and assets expected to turn into cash within a year: stock, debtors, cash |
| Current liabilities | Debts due within a year: creditors, overdraft, short-term loans |
| Percentage point | The unit for the difference between two percentages |

## What moves each ratio

| Ratio | Goes up when | Goes down when |
|---|---|---|
| Gross profit margin | Prices rise or cost of sales per unit falls | Discounts, higher input costs, more low-margin products |
| Profit margin | Gross margin rises or expenses fall relative to sales | Overheads grow faster than sales |
| ROCE | Profit rises or capital employed falls | New capital not yet earning; profit falls |
| Current ratio | Current assets rise or current liabilities fall | Cash used up; short-term borrowing rises |
| Acid test | Cash or debtors rise; short-term debts fall | Cash turned into stock; overdraft rises |

## Reading the ratios together

* Gross margin down but profit margin steady: overheads have been controlled; the problem is pricing or cost of sales.
* Gross margin steady but profit margin down: expenses are rising faster than sales.
* Margins steady but ROCE down: more capital is being used without more profit.
* Current ratio acceptable but acid test low: too much of the current assets is stock.

## Strategies

**Profitability**

* Raise prices (risk: lower volume).
* Find cheaper suppliers or cut waste (risk: quality).
* Shift the product mix to higher-margin items.
* Cut expenses such as rent or energy (raises profit margin, not gross margin).
* Sell unused non-current assets and repay long-term debt to cut capital employed (raises ROCE; risk: lost capacity).

**Liquidity**

* Sell surplus stock for cash (acid test rises most).
* Collect debts faster (risk: customers lost; discounts cost revenue).
* Delay paying suppliers within agreed terms (risk: relationships).
* Swap an overdraft for a long-term loan (current liabilities fall; long-term debt rises).
* Raise share capital or sell an unused asset for cash.

## Worked mini-example

Tidemill Cycles (invented): sales revenue 400,000; cost of sales 260,000; expenses 92,000; capital employed 320,000; current assets 110,000 including stock 70,000; current liabilities 80,000.

* Gross profit = 140,000; gross profit margin = 35.0%.
* Profit before interest and tax = 48,000; profit margin = 12.0%.
* ROCE = 48,000 ÷ 320,000 × 100 = 15.0%.
* Current ratio = 110,000 ÷ 80,000 = 1.375, so 1.38 to two decimal places.
* Acid test = 40,000 ÷ 80,000 = 0.50.

Comment: profitability looks reasonable, but 64% of current assets (70,000 ÷ 110,000) is stock, so the business relies on selling bicycles to meet short-term debts.

## Exam traps

* Profit after interest used in profit margin or ROCE.
* Capital employed taken as total assets.
* Stock left in the acid test.
* "Fell by 2%" when the change is 2 percentage points.
* Strategy matched to the wrong ratio.
* High liquidity praised without noting idle resources.
* Ratios interpreted with no comparison.

## Quick self-test

1. Write the formula for ROCE.
2. What is capital employed?
3. Sales 250,000, cost of sales 150,000. Find the gross profit margin.
4. Using question 3, expenses are 60,000. Find the profit margin.
5. Profit before interest and tax 45,000, capital employed 300,000. Find ROCE.
6. Current assets 96,000, stock 36,000, current liabilities 60,000. Find both liquidity ratios.
7. Why is stock excluded from the acid test?
8. Gross margin is unchanged but profit margin falls. What does this suggest?
9. Give one strategy that raises ROCE without changing profit.
10. Give one risk of raising prices to improve gross profit margin.
11. How does selling surplus stock at cost affect the current ratio and the acid test?
12. Why is a very high current ratio not always good?

### Answers

1. (Profit before interest and tax ÷ capital employed) × 100.
2. Non-current liabilities plus equity.
3. 100,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 40%.
4. 40,000 ÷ 250,000 × 100 = 16%.
5. 45,000 ÷ 300,000 × 100 = 15%.
6. Current ratio 96,000 ÷ 60,000 = 1.6; acid test 60,000 ÷ 60,000 = 1.0.
7. It is the current asset that takes longest to turn into cash and may not sell at its recorded value.
8. Expenses have risen relative to sales revenue.
9. Reduce capital employed, for example by selling an unused asset and repaying long-term debt.
10. Customers may buy less or switch to rivals, so total profit could fall.
11. The current ratio is unchanged (stock becomes cash of equal value); the acid test rises because cash counts and stock does not.
12. It may mean too much money is tied up in idle stock or cash that could be earning a return.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which profit figure is used for the profit margin in IB Business Management?**
The guide's formula uses profit before interest and tax divided by sales revenue, multiplied by 100.

**What is a good acid test ratio?**
The guide sets no target. Judge the figure against the business's past results, similar businesses and how quickly its stock sells.
