**These notes condense Mussolini's Italy, 1919-41, the first topic of the Paper 4 European option in Cambridge 9489, for fast recall.** For explanation and an annotated essay plan use the [Mussolini's Italy study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-mussolinis-italy/); for essays and short questions use the [Mussolini's Italy practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-mussolinis-italy-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 1.1 How did Mussolini gain control of Italy?
- 1.2 How effectively did Mussolini govern Italy?
- 1.3 How successful were Mussolini's economic policies?
- 1.4 How far did Mussolini transform Italian society?

Paper 4 has no rotation: any of the four key questions, or a question crossing two of them, can be set.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Mutilated victory | Nationalist claim that the Allies cheated Italy of Dalmatia and Fiume in 1919 |
| Biennio Rosso | "Two red years", 1919-20: strikes, land seizures and factory occupations |
| Squadrismo | Violence by fascist squads against socialists, unions and co-operatives |
| Ras | Provincial fascist bosses who led the squads, such as Balbo and Farinacci |
| Dual policy | Mussolini's mix of threatened violence and parliamentary negotiation, 1921-22 |
| Acerbo Law | 1923: two-thirds of seats to the list with most votes (minimum 25 per cent) |
| Aventine Secession | Withdrawal of opposition deputies from parliament after Matteotti's murder |
| MVSN | The fascist militia, created 1923 |
| OVRA | Secret police watching anti-fascists from the late 1920s |
| Podestà | Appointed official who replaced elected mayors |
| Quota 90 | Revaluation of the lira at 90 to the pound, 1927 |
| IRI | Institute for Industrial Reconstruction, 1933: state holding company rescuing industry |
| Autarky | Self-sufficiency, pursued hard after the 1935 League sanctions |
| Corporate State | Employers and fascist unions organised into state-supervised corporations |
| ONB / GIL | Fascist youth organisation (1926), absorbed into the GIL in 1937 |
| OND | The Dopolavoro, fascist after-work leisure organisation (1925) |

## 1.1 Gaining control -- the chain of causes

1. **Preconditions:** war losses and debt, the mutilated victory, Fiume, inflation and unemployment.
2. **Fear of the left:** the Biennio Rosso drove landowners, industrialists and the middle class to back the squads.
3. **Liberal failure:** proportional representation fragmented parliament; liberals co-opted fascists in 1921.
4. **Fascist methods:** squadrismo plus the dual policy; breaking the August 1922 strike.
5. **Decisive moment:** Victor Emmanuel III's refusal to sign martial law, 28 October 1922.
6. **Consolidation:** Acerbo Law, the 1924 election, Matteotti crisis survived, laws of 1925-26.

## 1.2 Governing -- effective, but within limits

- **Repression:** OVRA, Special Tribunal, *confino*; relatively few executions.
- **Propaganda:** cult of the Duce, *romanità*, LUCE, radio, press control, sport, MinCulPop (1937).
- **Institutions:** Duce, PNF and Grand Council alongside king, cabinet, parliament, civil service and judiciary; prefects placed above party bosses in 1927.
- **Foreign policy scorecard:** successes -- Fiume (1924), Albanian protectorate (1926), Abyssinia (1936); failures -- Corfu climb-down (1923), sanctions and dependence on Germany, Greece (1940), North Africa (1940-41).

## 1.3 Economy -- aims against outcomes

| Policy | Aim | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| De Stefani, 1922-25 | Balanced budget, business confidence | Growth, but inflation and a falling lira |
| Battle for Grain | Self-sufficiency in wheat | Output rose; costlier bread, poorer land use |
| Quota 90 | Prestige of the lira | Exports hit, deflation, wage cuts |
| Battle for Land | Reclamation and resettlement | Pontine Marshes drained; national targets missed |
| IMI and IRI | Rescue banks and industry | State became a major industrial owner |
| Corporate State | "Third Way" class harmony | Employers kept power; strikes banned |
| Autarky | Readiness for war | Never achieved; Italy entered war in 1940 short of raw materials |

## 1.4 Society -- the transformation test

- **Women:** Battle for Births, bachelor tax, employment quotas; the birth rate fell anyway.
- **Youth:** Gentile reform (1923), single state textbook, teachers' and professors' oaths, ONB/GIL; patchy reach in the rural south.
- **Church:** Lateran Treaties (1929) won Catholic consent; clashes over Catholic Action (1931) and the racial laws (1938).
- **Leisure:** OND popular; Reform of Customs ridiculed.
- **Opposition:** Communist underground, Giustizia e Libertà, assassination attempts of 1925-26, non-conformity.

## Exam traps

- **"Seizure" of power:** the March on Rome was a threat that succeeded because the king gave way.
- **1922 versus 1926:** Mussolini was a coalition prime minister in 1922; the dictatorship dates from 1925-26.
- **Corporate State dates:** Vidoni Pact 1925, Rocco Law 1926, Charter of Labour 1927, corporations 1934 -- do not compress them into one year.
- **Totalitarian claims:** always weigh the king, the Church, the army and big business.
- **Success questions:** define success against the regime's own aims and against the experience of ordinary Italians.

## Self-test

1. What did the Acerbo Law provide? *Two-thirds of the seats for the list winning most votes, if it gained at least 25 per cent.*
2. Who refused to sign the martial-law decree in October 1922? *King Victor Emmanuel III.*
3. What was the Aventine Secession? *The walk-out of opposition deputies from parliament after Matteotti's murder in 1924.*
4. What did the December 1925 law change? *It made Mussolini, as Head of Government, responsible to the king alone, not to parliament.*
5. What was Quota 90? *The 1927 revaluation of the lira at 90 to the pound.*
6. Name the two state agencies that rescued banks and industry in the Depression. *IMI (1931) and IRI (1933).*
7. Which agreement banned strikes and set up labour courts? *The Rocco Law of 1926.*
8. Give the three parts of the Lateran settlement. *A treaty creating Vatican City, a Concordat, and a financial convention.*
9. Over which Catholic body did Church and state clash in 1931? *Catholic Action.*
10. What was the OND? *The fascist after-work organisation providing subsidised leisure, sport and excursions.*
11. Name the exile anti-fascist group founded in Paris in 1929. *Giustizia e Libertà.*
12. Which island did Italy bombard and occupy in 1923? *Corfu.*
