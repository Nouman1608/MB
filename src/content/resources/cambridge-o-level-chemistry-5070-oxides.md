Subtopic 7.2 of Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 has two learning outcomes. You must "describe amphoteric oxides as oxides that react with acids and bases to produce a salt and water", and "classify oxides as acidic, including SO₂ and CO₂, basic, including CuO and CaO, or amphoteric, limited to Al₂O₃ and ZnO, related to metallic and non-metallic character".

Two outcomes, but questions often ask you to predict how an oxide behaves, write its reactions, or classify an unfamiliar oxide from experimental results. This guide builds those skills. The broader [Acids, Bases and Salts guide](/resources/acids-bases-and-salts/) covers all of Topic 7. For quick recall use the [oxides revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-oxides-revision-notes/), and test yourself with the [oxides practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-oxides-practice/).

## The link to metallic and non-metallic character

The syllabus ties the type of oxide to the element it comes from:

- **Metals** generally form **basic** oxides.
- **Non-metals** generally form **acidic** oxides.
- **Aluminium and zinc** form **amphoteric** oxides, which behave as both.

This fits the Periodic Table trend from metallic to non-metallic character across a period (subtopic 8.1). Across Period 3 the oxides change from basic to acidic:

| Oxide | Na₂O | MgO | Al₂O₃ | SiO₂ | P₄O₁₀ | SO₂ |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Element type | Metal | Metal | Metal | Non-metal | Non-metal | Non-metal |
| Oxide type | Basic | Basic | Amphoteric | Acidic | Acidic | Acidic |

You only need to recall the six named examples (SO₂, CO₂, CuO, CaO, Al₂O₃, ZnO), but the trend lets you predict the type of others.

## Basic oxides

A basic oxide reacts with an **acid** to give a **salt and water**. It does not react with alkalis.

```
CuO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → CuCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)          black solid dissolves; blue-green solution
CaO(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
```

Some basic oxides also dissolve in water to form alkalis. Calcium oxide reacts with water to form calcium hydroxide, which gives an alkaline solution (pH above 7):

CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq)

Copper(II) oxide is insoluble in water, so it is a base but cannot form an alkali.

## Acidic oxides

An acidic oxide reacts with a **base** (or alkali) to give a **salt and water**. It does not react with acids.

```
CO₂(g) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂CO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)
SO₂(g) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂SO₃(aq) + H₂O(l)
CO₂(g) + Ca(OH)₂(aq) → CaCO₃(s) + H₂O(l)       the limewater test: milky precipitate
```

Carbon dioxide and sulfur dioxide are gases that dissolve in water to give acidic solutions (pH below 7), which is why sulfur dioxide causes acid rain.

## Amphoteric oxides

An amphoteric oxide reacts with **both** acids and bases, and gives a **salt and water** each time.

With an acid, zinc oxide and aluminium oxide behave like basic oxides:

```
ZnO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → ZnCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)
Al₂O₃(s) + 6HCl(aq) → 2AlCl₃(aq) + 3H₂O(l)
```

With an alkali, they behave like acidic oxides:

```
ZnO(s) + 2NaOH(aq) → Na₂ZnO₂(aq) + H₂O(l)       sodium zincate
Al₂O₃(s) + 2NaOH(aq) → 2NaAlO₂(aq) + H₂O(l)     sodium aluminate
```

The outcome asks you to describe this behaviour; the equations with sodium hydroxide are shown so you can see that a salt and water form in both directions. The same amphoteric character explains a qualitative analysis result: zinc and aluminium hydroxide precipitates dissolve in excess aqueous sodium hydroxide.

## Writing equations for an unfamiliar oxide

You can write the reaction of an oxide you have never met by following its type.

1. **Decide the type** from the element: metal (basic), non-metal (acidic), or aluminium or zinc (amphoteric).
2. **Basic oxide + acid:** the metal ion joins the acid's anion to make the salt, and the oxide ion becomes water. Potassium oxide with sulfuric acid: K₂O + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + H₂O.
3. **Acidic oxide + alkali:** the non-metal oxide becomes part of an anion, and water forms. Sulfur dioxide gives a sulfite; carbon dioxide gives a carbonate.
4. **Soluble basic oxide + water:** the hydroxide forms. Potassium oxide gives potassium hydroxide: K₂O + H₂O → 2KOH.
5. **Balance** using the charges on the ions, then check atoms on both sides.

## Classifying an unknown oxide

A two-test method decides the type of any oxide:

1. Add the oxide to **dilute acid** (warm if needed). Does it react or dissolve?
2. Add a fresh sample to **dilute sodium hydroxide**. Does it react or dissolve?

| Reacts with acid? | Reacts with alkali? | Type |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | No | Basic |
| No | Yes | Acidic |
| Yes | Yes | Amphoteric |

If the oxide dissolves in **water**, universal indicator gives a quicker clue: an alkaline solution suggests a basic oxide; an acidic solution suggests an acidic oxide. But many oxides, including CuO, Al₂O₃ and ZnO, are insoluble, so the water test alone cannot classify them.

### Worked example: interpreting results

Four white or black solid oxides, A to D, are tested.

| Oxide | Dilute HCl | Dilute NaOH | In water with universal indicator |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Dissolves | No change | Insoluble |
| B | Dissolves | Dissolves | Insoluble |
| C | No change | Dissolves on warming | Insoluble |
| D | Dissolves | No change | Dissolves; indicator turns purple |

- **A:** reacts with acid only, so **basic**. It could be a metal oxide such as copper(II) oxide.
- **B:** reacts with both, so **amphoteric**. It could be zinc oxide or aluminium oxide.
- **C:** reacts with alkali only, so **acidic**. A solid non-metal oxide such as silicon(IV) oxide behaves this way.
- **D:** reacts with acid only, and gives an alkaline solution in water, so **basic**, and the oxide of a reactive metal, such as calcium oxide.

## Where oxide reactions matter

Acidic and basic oxides react with each other, which is the chemistry behind two processes elsewhere in the syllabus:

- **Flue gas desulfurisation (subtopic 10.3):** calcium oxide, a basic oxide, removes sulfur dioxide, an acidic oxide, from power-station waste gases, reducing acid rain.
- **Slag formation in the blast furnace (subtopic 9.6):** calcium oxide reacts with silicon(IV) oxide impurities to form slag, calcium silicate: CaO + SiO₂ → CaSiO₃.

## Common mistakes

- **"All metal oxides are basic."** Aluminium oxide and zinc oxide are amphoteric.
- **"Amphoteric means neutral."** Amphoteric oxides react with both acids and bases; they are not unreactive.
- **Forgetting the water** in the products of an oxide reacting with an acid or alkali.
- **Classifying by solubility in water alone.** Insoluble oxides need the acid and alkali tests.
- **Writing that CO₂ reacts with acid.** Acidic oxides react with bases.
- **Wrong formula of aluminium oxide.** Al³⁺ and O²⁻ give Al₂O₃, so the acid equation needs 6HCl.

## Command words

- **Classify:** put each oxide into acidic, basic or amphoteric. A reason linked to metal or non-metal character, or to the test results, strengthens the answer.
- **Describe** (amphoteric): state that the oxide reacts with acids **and** bases, and that both reactions produce a salt and water.
- **Deduce:** use given test results to decide the type of an unfamiliar oxide.
