This study guide covers **section 1.2, Fractions**, in topic 1 (Numbers and the number system) of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) specification, Issue 2 (November 2017). Section 1.2 is Foundation Tier content; the Higher Tier entry says "See Foundation Tier", so all of it is needed on both tiers.

Fractions sit underneath ratio, probability, algebraic fractions and percentages, so speed and accuracy here pay off across the whole course. Fractions and decimals are taught together in the [fractions and decimals study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-maths-4ma1-fractions-and-decimals/); this page goes further into fraction methods and multi-step problems. ## Equivalent fractions and simplest form (1.2 A)

Multiplying or dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number gives an **equivalent fraction**: 3/4 = 15/20 = 75/100. A fraction is in its **simplest form** (lowest terms) when the only common factor of top and bottom is 1.

The fastest way to simplify is to divide by the **highest common factor** in one step. For 126/210, 126 = 2 × 3² × 7 and 210 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 7, so the HCF is 2 × 3 × 7 = 42, and 126/210 = 3/5. Cancelling in several smaller steps (÷ 2, then ÷ 3, then ÷ 7) is also fine, as long as you finish.

## Mixed numbers and vulgar fractions (1.2 B)

A vulgar fraction has whole numbers on top and bottom; when the top is bigger it is **improper**. A **mixed number** is a whole number with a proper fraction.

- Mixed to improper: 4 2/7 = (4 × 7 + 2)/7 = 30/7.
- Improper to mixed: 47/6 = 7 remainder 5, so 7 5/6.

Change mixed numbers to improper fractions before multiplying or dividing. Leave them mixed when adding or subtracting if you prefer the "wholes then fractions" method below.

## Common denominators and ordering (1.2 C, 1.2 D)

A **common denominator** is a common multiple of the denominators; the lowest one (the LCM) keeps the numbers smallest. For 3/8 and 5/12, the LCM of 8 and 12 is 24, giving 9/24 and 10/24.

**Worked example 1.** Write 2/3, 5/8, 7/12 and 3/4 in order of size, smallest first.

```
LCM of 3, 8, 12, 4 = 24
2/3 = 16/24   5/8 = 15/24   7/12 = 14/24   3/4 = 18/24
order: 7/12, 5/8, 2/3, 3/4
```

Write the answer using the **original** fractions, not the converted ones. Converting each fraction to a decimal is an equally valid method, and quicker with awkward denominators.

## Fractions of quantities, and one number as a fraction of another (1.2 D, 1.2 E)

To find a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator: 5/9 of 315 kg = 315 ÷ 9 × 5 = 175 kg.

To write one number as a fraction of another, put the "part" over the "whole", **in the same units**, and simplify. 45 seconds as a fraction of 4 minutes is 45/240 = 3/16. Writing 45/4 mixes units and is wrong.

## Adding and subtracting (1.2 F)

Convert to a common denominator, then add or subtract the numerators only.

**Worked example 2.** Work out (a) 3 2/5 + 1 3/4 and (b) 6 1/6 − 2 3/4.

```
(a) wholes: 3 + 1 = 4
    fractions: 2/5 + 3/4 = 8/20 + 15/20 = 23/20 = 1 3/20
    total: 4 + 1 3/20 = 5 3/20

(b) improper: 37/6 − 11/4
    = 74/12 − 33/12 = 41/12 = 3 5/12
```

In (b), the "wholes then fractions" method would give 1/6 − 3/4, which is negative. Either borrow a whole (6 1/6 = 5 14/12) or convert to improper fractions, as shown. The improper route always works.

## Fractions to decimals and percentages (1.2 G)

Divide the numerator by the denominator, then multiply by 100 for a percentage.

| Fraction | Decimal | Percentage |
|---|---|---|
| 7/8 | 0.875 | 87.5% |
| 3/20 | 0.15 | 15% |
| 5/6 | 0.8333… | 83.3…% |
| 2/11 | 0.181818… | 18.18…% |

Some fractions give **terminating** decimals and others **recurring** ones. Converting a recurring decimal back to a fraction is Higher tier content in section 1.3.

## Unit fractions as multiplicative inverses (1.2 H)

A **unit fraction** has numerator 1, such as 1/5. Multiplying by 1/5 has the same effect as dividing by 5, so 1/5 is the **multiplicative inverse** (reciprocal) of 5: 5 × 1/5 = 1. The specification's example is 3 ÷ 5 = 3 × 1/5.

This is the reason the division rule works: dividing by any fraction is the same as multiplying by its reciprocal. It also makes dividing a fraction by a whole number quick: 2/3 ÷ 4 = 2/3 × 1/4 = 2/12 = 1/6.

## Multiplying and dividing (1.2 I)

- **Multiply:** convert mixed numbers to improper fractions, cancel any numerator with any denominator, then multiply tops and bottoms.
- **Divide:** keep the first fraction, change ÷ to ×, and flip the second fraction (use its reciprocal). Then multiply.

**Worked example 3.** Work out (a) 2 2/5 × 3 1/3 and (b) 4 1/2 ÷ 1 4/5.

```
(a) 12/5 × 10/3
    cancel 12 and 3 by 3; cancel 10 and 5 by 5
    = 4/1 × 2/1 = 8

(b) 9/2 ÷ 9/5 = 9/2 × 5/9
    = 5/2 = 2 1/2
```

Cancelling before multiplying keeps numbers small and means the answer is already in simplest form. A sense check helps: in (b), 4.5 ÷ 1.8 should be between 2 and 3.

## Multi-step problems

Many questions combine several statements. Two patterns appear often.

**A fraction of what is left.** Read "of the rest" or "of the remainder" carefully: it is a fraction of a fraction.

**Worked example 4.** Sami spends 1/3 of his monthly income on rent and 2/5 of the remainder on food. He has £360 left. Find his monthly income.

```
remainder after rent      = 1 − 1/3 = 2/3
food                      = 2/5 of 2/3 = 4/15
left                      = 1 − 1/3 − 4/15 = 15/15 − 5/15 − 4/15 = 6/15 = 2/5
2/5 of income = £360  ->  1/5 = £180  ->  income = £900
```

**Reverse problems.** If 3/8 of a number is 42, then 1/8 is 14 and the whole is 112. This is the same as 42 ÷ 3/8.

## Common mistakes

- Adding denominators: 1/3 + 1/4 is 7/12, not 2/7.
- Leaving an answer such as 18/24 or 23/20 when the question says "simplest form" or "mixed number".
- Multiplying mixed numbers part by part: 2 1/2 × 3 1/3 is not 6 1/6.
- Flipping the wrong fraction when dividing, or flipping both.
- Writing one quantity as a fraction of another without matching units.
- Taking 2/5 of the whole instead of 2/5 of the remainder in multi-step problems.

## Exam technique

- **Show your working clearly** in fraction arithmetic: write the common denominator or the reciprocal step. A bare answer from the calculator's fraction key does not show the method.
- **Show that** questions give the answer, so every line must be visible and the last line must match it exactly.
- **Give your answer in its simplest form** means cancel fully; **as a mixed number** means convert any improper result.
- **Write down** a fraction as a decimal: for simple fractions the answer can be stated directly.
- In context questions, finish with a sentence and units: "His monthly income is £900."
