Cambridge O Level Accounting (7707) is a course in keeping accurate
financial records and turning them into statements that owners,
lenders and managers can use. It moves from the basic accounting
equation to the final accounts of several kinds of organisation, and
finishes with ratio analysis and the concepts and ethics behind
professional practice. Under the 2027-2029 syllabus there are two
externally assessed papers: a multiple-choice paper of 1 hour 30
minutes and 40 marks (30%), and a structured written paper of 1 hour
45 minutes and 100 marks (70%). Candidates are eligible for grades A*
to E.

## How the course is organised

The syllabus is set out in seven topic areas. It is easiest to think of
them as three stages of the accounting process.

**Stage one: capturing the data**

1. *The fundamentals of accounting* explains why accounting exists and
   introduces the accounting equation.
2. *Sources and recording of data* covers the double entry system of
   book-keeping, business documents and books of prime entry.

**Stage two: making the records reliable**

3. *Verification of accounting records* covers the trial balance,
   corrections of errors, bank reconciliation and control accounts.
4. *Accounting procedures* deals with capital and revenue expenditure
   and receipts, depreciation and disposal of non-current assets, other
   payables and other receivables, irrecoverable debts and the
   allowance for them, and valuation of inventory.

**Stage three: reporting and judging**

5. *Preparation of financial statements* has the most sub-topics: sole
   traders, partnerships, limited companies, manufacturing accounts,
   clubs and societies, and incomplete records.
6. *Analysis and interpretation* covers calculating and interpreting
   accounting ratios, inter-business comparison, interested parties
   and the limitations of accounting statements.
7. *Accounting concepts and modern practice* covers accounting concepts,
   ethical considerations, and technology and sustainability.

## How it's assessed

The qualification is linear and untiered: every candidate sits both
papers in the same series.

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Multiple Choice | 1 hour 30 minutes | 40 | 30% |
| Paper 2 | Structured Written Paper | 1 hour 45 minutes | 100 | 70% |

Calculators are allowed in both papers, and both are marked externally.
Paper 2 carries more than twice the weighting of Paper 1, so the
ability to set out complete accounts and calculations under time
pressure matters most. The 2027-2029 paper structure is the same as
that of Cambridge IGCSE Accounting (0452) for the same series.

## How the parts fit together

Each stage feeds the next. The double entry rules learned in stage one
are what make a trial balance possible; the trial balance and the
adjustments in stage two are what make final accounts accurate; and
only accurate final accounts produce ratios worth interpreting in
stage three. When a student struggles with limited company accounts or
incomplete records, the cause is very often an earlier weakness in
double entry or in one of the adjustments.

Several threads run through all seven areas rather than belonging to
one:

- **Accounting concepts** justify the procedures. Depreciation, the
  allowance for irrecoverable debts and inventory valuation all follow
  from concepts, so learning each procedure with its reason makes it
  easier to remember and to explain.
- **Presentation** matters everywhere. Clear headings, correct
  terminology and logical layout are part of what makes an account or
  statement correct, especially on the structured paper.
- **Interpretation** is not confined to the analysis topic. Comparing
  results, spotting errors and commenting on what figures mean are
  useful habits from the very first topic.

The two papers reward complementary skills. The multiple-choice paper
covers the breadth of the syllabus quickly, testing whether you know
the rules and can apply them in a single step. The structured written
paper tests depth: longer problems in which one figure leads to the
next.

## Planning your study

A sound order follows the three stages, but with regular returns to
earlier work.

1. **Weeks at the start of the course:** purpose of accounting, the
   accounting equation, double entry, business documents and books of
   prime entry. Practise until double entry is automatic.
2. **Next:** the trial balance, correction of errors, bank reconciliation
   and control accounts.
3. **Then:** sole trader financial statements, introduced early so that
   each accounting procedure can be applied to a real set of accounts
   as soon as it is learned.
4. **Later in the course:** partnerships, limited companies,
   manufacturing accounts, clubs and societies, and incomplete records.
5. **Final topics:** ratios and interpretation, accounting concepts,
   ethics, and technology and sustainability.

Short multiple-choice sets and single structured questions belong
alongside each topic. Full papers and the paper-by-paper exam
preparation page come last, once the whole syllabus has been covered
at least once.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 assessed?**
By two externally assessed papers: Paper 1 Multiple Choice (1 hour 30 minutes, 40 marks, 30%) and Paper 2 Structured Written Paper (1 hour 45 minutes, 100 marks, 70%).

**What grades are available in O Level Accounting?**
Candidates are eligible for grades A* to E.

**Is O Level Accounting the same as IGCSE Accounting?**
For the 2027-2029 series, the paper structure matches Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 and the topic areas are the same, but they are separate qualifications with their own syllabus codes.

**Are calculators allowed in O Level Accounting?**
Yes. Calculators are permitted in both papers.

**Which topic area has the most sub-topics?**
Preparation of financial statements has the most sub-topics: sole traders, partnerships, limited companies, manufacturing accounts, clubs and societies, and incomplete records.
