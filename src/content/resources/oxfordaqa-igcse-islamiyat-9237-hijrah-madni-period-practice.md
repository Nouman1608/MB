## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
questions give one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Hazrat Mus'ab ibn Umair (RA) [1].

**2.** The Cave of Thawr [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: not to associate anything with Allah; not to steal; not to commit adultery; not to kill their children; not to slander; not to disobey the Prophet (pbuh) in what is right.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: teaching and learning; judging disputes; receiving delegations and visitors; planning and meetings for the community; shelter for poor companions in the suffah; announcing news.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: the Quraysh planned to kill the Prophet (pbuh) at night; Ali (RA) slept in the Prophet's bed; covered with the Prophet's cloak, so the watchers thought the Prophet was still there; he risked his life; he stayed in Makkah to return goods people had left with the Prophet for safekeeping; he then travelled to Madinah and joined the Prophet.

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Preparation** [1]: he had camels ready and hired a guide, so the journey could begin at once [1].
- **Companionship and courage** [1]: he hid with the Prophet in Thawr for three days despite the danger [1].
- **His family's help** [1]: his son brought news and his daughter Asma (RA) brought food [1].
- **Loyalty** [1]: he put the Prophet's safety above his own, earning the title "the second of two" (9:40) [1].

**7.** For each of two differences: difference identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Numbers** [1]: twelve men took the first pledge; more than seventy men and two women took the second, showing how fast Islam had spread [1].
- **Protection** [1]: only the second pledge promised to protect the Prophet (pbuh) as their own families, which made the Hijrah possible [1].
- **Nature** [1]: the first was about belief and conduct, the second also a political and military alliance [1].
- **Outcome** [1]: the first led to Mus'ab ibn Umair (RA) being sent as teacher; the second to an invitation to migrate [1].

**8.** For each of two terms: term identified [1] and explained [1].

- **One community** [1]: Muslims of Quraysh and Yathrib and those who joined were one Ummah, ending tribal division [1].
- **Religious freedom** [1]: the Jewish tribes kept their own religion and had rights to help and protection [1].
- **Joint defence** [1]: all groups would defend Madinah together and share the costs [1].
- **Disputes to the Prophet (pbuh)** [1]: he became the final judge, giving the city one system of authority [1].
- **No alliance with Quraysh** [1]: no group could side with the Makkan enemy [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **Peace** [1]: it ended the long conflict between the Aws and the Khazraj [1].
- **Security** [1]: joint defence protected the city against Makkan attack [1].
- **Justice and rule of law** [1]: obligations were written down and disputes went to one judge [1].
- **Coexistence** [1]: people of different faiths lived under one agreement with their rights protected [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "O you who have believed, obey Allah and obey the Messenger and those in authority among you. And if you disagree over anything, refer it to Allah and the Messenger" (Qur'an 4:59): the Charter made this the city's rule for settling disputes [2].
- "There shall be no compulsion in [acceptance of] the religion" (Qur'an 2:256): the Charter protected the Jewish tribes' religion [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **Honesty with trusts** [1]: Ali (RA) returned goods to people who were the Prophet's enemies, so Muslims must keep trusts with everyone [1].
- **Courage for the faith** [1]: Ali risked his life in the bed, showing willingness to sacrifice for Islam [1].
- **Loyal friendship** [1]: Abu Bakr (RA) gave his wealth, time and family's help, a model of supporting good causes [1].
- **Trust in Allah** [1]: in the cave the Prophet (pbuh) reassured Abu Bakr that Allah was with them, so Muslims rely on Allah in fear [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us" (Qur'an 9:40) [2].
- "Indeed, Allah commands you to render trusts to whom they are due" (Qur'an 4:58), which Ali (RA) followed even towards enemies [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The mosque gave the community a **centre** for worship, learning and decision-making.
- Building it **together**, with the Prophet (pbuh) working alongside, created unity and equality.
- It made **public worship** possible after years of secrecy and persecution in Makkah.
- **Against the statement:** other steps were equally vital: the second pledge of Aqaba, which made the Hijrah possible; the brotherhood, which solved the migrants' poverty; and the Charter, which gave peace and law to the whole city.
- **Balanced view:** the mosque was the spiritual foundation; the brotherhood and Charter were the social and political structure built on it.

*Use of the passage*

- "Founded on righteousness from the first day" shows that the community was built on taqwa from the start.
- "Within it are men who love to purify themselves" shows that the mosque shaped the character of its people.
- "Allah loves those who purify themselves" shows Allah's approval of the community gathered there.
- "Do not stand [for prayer] within it" refers to the hypocrites' mosque (9:107), showing that a building alone does not create a community; sincere intention does.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The Prophet's Mosque was a place of **teaching, justice, welfare and government** as well as prayer.
- The **suffah** sheltered and educated poor companions.
- Delegations were received and decisions made there.
- Mosques today run schools, give charity, host marriages and funerals, and bring communities together.
- **Against the statement:** its first and central purpose is prayer (*masjid* means place of prostration); other activities must not distract from worship; some argue that worldly business and noise do not belong in the mosque.

*Use of the passage*

- "Founded on righteousness" shows that a mosque's purpose includes building righteous character.
- "Men who love to purify themselves" shows that the mosque is a place of spiritual and moral cleansing, not just ritual prayer.
- The contrast with the hypocrites' mosque shows that mosques can be used for good or harm, so their wider role matters.
- "More worthy for you to stand in" stresses prayer as the core activity.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the two pledges of Aqaba.
- Saying Ali (RA) travelled with the Prophet (pbuh).
- Naming the Cave of Hira instead of Thawr.
- Calling the Prophet's Mosque the first mosque.
- In the 12-mark questions, giving only one view or not using the passage.
