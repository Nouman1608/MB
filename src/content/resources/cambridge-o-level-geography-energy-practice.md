> These are original practice questions written in the style of Cambridge O Level
> Geography (2217) assessment objectives. They are not taken from any past paper
> and are not endorsed by Cambridge International. Countries A, B and C and their
> data are fictional and illustrative. Where a question asks for a named example,
> use your own learned case study.

Use these questions alongside the [Energy study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-energy/)
and the [Energy revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-geography-energy-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** State the difference between a renewable and a non-renewable
energy source. **[2]**

**2.** Name two renewable energy sources that depend on the movement of
water. **[2]**

**3.** Explain why fuelwood is renewable only under certain conditions. **[2]**

**4.** State two reasons why energy use per person is usually higher in
high-income countries than in low-income countries. **[2]**

**5.** Table 1 shows the electricity mix of fictional Country B.

| Source | Coal | Oil | Natural gas | HEP | Solar | Nuclear |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Share (%) | 42 | 8 | 18 | 14 | 6 | 12 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of Country B's electricity that comes
from fossil fuels. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the angle needed to show HEP on a pie chart. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why coal is Country B's largest source. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Table 2 shows energy information for three fictional countries.

| Country | Energy use per person (units) | Main household fuel | Share of households with mains electricity (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 4 | Fuelwood and charcoal | 30 |
| B | 25 | Coal and electricity | 85 |
| C | 70 | Electricity and natural gas | 100 |

**(a)** How many times greater is energy use per person in Country C
than in Country A? **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest which country is at the lowest level of development.
Give two pieces of evidence from Table 2. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain two problems caused by heavy dependence on fuelwood. **[4]**

**7.** A government plans to build its first nuclear power station.

**(a)** Explain two benefits of this plan. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain two reasons why some people oppose the plan. **[4]**

**8.** A wind farm is proposed on hills near a small town.

**(a)** Suggest one physical factor that makes the site suitable. **[1]**
**(b)** Suggest two reasons why some local residents might object. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the country would still need other sources of
electricity if the wind farm is built. **[2]**

**9.** Evaluate the use of large hydroelectric dams as a way of meeting a
country's growing demand for electricity. **[6]**

**10.** For a named country or area, describe its energy supply and
explain why it uses the sources it does. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** A renewable source is naturally replenished and will not run out,
for example wind [1]. A non-renewable source exists in a finite amount
and forms far more slowly than it is used, so it will run out, for
example coal [1].

**2.** Any two of: hydroelectric power, tidal power, wave power [1 each].

**3.** Trees regrow, so wood can be replaced [1], but only if trees are
replanted or allowed to regrow as fast as they are cut; otherwise
deforestation means the supply is used up [1].

**4.** Any two of: more industry using energy; more cars and air travel;
more household appliances, heating and air conditioning; wider access to
mains electricity; higher incomes to pay for energy [1 each].

**5. (a)** 42 + 8 + 18 = **68%** [1].
**(b)** 14 x 3.6 = **50.4 degrees** [1].
**(c)** Any one: the country has its own coal reserves; coal is cheap;
coal power stations and mines already exist and employ many people
[1].

**6. (a)** 70 / 4 = **17.5 times** [1].
**(b)** Country A [1]. Evidence: lowest energy use per person (4 units)
[1]; fuelwood and charcoal are the main household fuels and only 30% of
households have mains electricity [1].
**(c)** Two problems explained, 2 marks each, for example:
- Deforestation [1]: cutting trees faster than they regrow removes
  forest cover, leading to soil erosion and loss of habitats [1].
- Time and labour [1]: people, often women and children, walk long
  distances to collect wood, reducing time for school or paid work [1].
- Health [1]: smoke from cooking indoors on open fires causes breathing
  illnesses [1].

**7. (a)** Two benefits explained, 2 marks each, for example:
- Large, reliable output [1] that does not depend on the weather, so it
  can meet rising demand steadily [1].
- Very low carbon emissions during operation [1], so it helps reduce
  enhanced global warming compared with coal or gas [1].
- Less dependence on imported oil and gas [1], improving energy security
  [1].
**(b)** Two reasons explained, 2 marks each, for example:
- Radioactive waste [1] stays dangerous for thousands of years and must
  be stored securely, which is costly and unpopular [1].
- Accident risk [1]: although rare, accidents such as Chernobyl (1986)
  or Fukushima (2011) contaminated large areas and forced evacuation [1].
- Cost [1]: very high building and decommissioning costs could be spent
  on other projects [1].

**8. (a)** Any one: exposed high ground with strong, frequent winds;
open land with few obstructions [1].
**(b)** Any two: visual impact on the landscape; noise; harm to birds;
possible effect on house prices or tourism [1 each].
**(c)** Wind output varies with wind speed and stops in calm weather [1],
so a reliable back-up such as gas, HEP or nuclear, or energy storage, is
needed to meet demand at all times [1].

**9.** Levels-style marking.
- Benefits [up to 3]: renewable; reliable and controllable output;
  no emissions in operation; reservoir can supply water for irrigation
  and reduce flooding downstream.
- Disadvantages [up to 3]: very high building cost; flooding of land and
  villages displaces people; loss of farmland and habitats; sediment
  trapped behind the dam reduces fertility downstream; suitable only
  where there are large rivers and valleys.
- Judgement [required for full marks]: for example, "large dams can meet
  demand reliably and cleanly, but their social and environmental costs
  mean they suit only countries with suitable rivers and where displaced
  people are resettled fairly". No judgement: maximum 4.

**10.** Answers depend on the case study. Credit:
- Named country or area [1].
- Description of the energy mix with specific detail [up to 3].
- Explanation of reasons linked to physical geography, development
  level, cost or government policy [up to 3].

Example using Iceland: almost all electricity from HEP and geothermal;
most homes heated geothermally; oil imported for transport and fishing.
Reasons: position on the Mid-Atlantic Ridge gives hot rocks near the
surface; heavy precipitation, glaciers and steep valleys give large
rivers for HEP; cheap electricity attracted aluminium smelting, for
example the smelter at Reyðarfjörður supplied by the Kárahnjúkar scheme.
