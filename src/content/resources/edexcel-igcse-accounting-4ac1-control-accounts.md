## Purpose of control accounts

A control account summarises a whole ledger in one account, using totals from the books of original entry. Its closing balance should equal the total of the individual balances in that ledger. The purposes are:

- **Checking the ledger.** If the control account balance agrees with the list of customer (or supplier) balances, the ledger is probably arithmetically correct.
- **Locating errors.** A trial balance difference can be traced to a particular ledger, which narrows the search.
- **Reducing fraud.** If one person keeps the individual accounts and another prepares the control account, a false entry in one is likely to be noticed.
- **Providing totals quickly.** The figure for trade receivables or trade payables is available for the financial statements without adding every account.

## Sorting the information

Control account questions often include items that do not belong. Ask one question of each: **does it change what customers owe the business (or what the business owes suppliers) on credit?**

| Item | In the receivables control account? | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Credit sales | Yes, debit | Customers owe more |
| Cash sales | No | No customer account is involved |
| Receipts from customers | Yes, credit | Customers owe less |
| Discount allowed | Yes, credit | Reduces what is owed |
| Returns inwards | Yes, credit | Reduces what is owed |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | Yes, credit | Debt removed |
| Dishonoured cheque | Yes, debit | Customer owes again |
| Refund paid to a customer who had overpaid | Yes, debit | Removes the customer's credit balance |
| Contra (set-off) with the payables ledger | Yes, credit | Debt settled by offset |
| Provision for irrecoverable debts | No | An estimate; no customer account changes |
| Discount received | No | Belongs to the payables side |

The same test works for the payables control account: credit purchases (credit), payments, discount received, returns outwards and contras (debit). Cash purchases and purchases of non-current assets stay out.

## Credit balances in the receivables ledger

A customer who overpays, or who returns goods after paying, ends up with a **credit balance**: the business owes the customer. A receivables control account can therefore start and end with balances on both sides. Opening debit balances go on the debit side and opening credit balances on the credit side; at the end, the closing debit balance is carried down on the credit side and the closing credit balance on the debit side.

### Worked example 1: Felton Office Supplies, year to 31 December 2027

| Information | Amount |
|---|---:|
| Receivables ledger at 1 January: debit balances | 18,450 |
| Receivables ledger at 1 January: credit balances | 320 |
| Credit sales | 74,600 |
| Cash sales | 9,800 |
| Receipts from credit customers | 66,250 |
| Discount allowed | 1,340 |
| Returns inwards | 2,180 |
| Irrecoverable debts written off | 760 |
| Cheque from a customer dishonoured | 415 |
| Refund to a customer with a credit balance | 120 |
| Set-off against the payables ledger | 900 |
| Increase in provision for irrecoverable debts | 150 |
| Receivables ledger at 31 December: credit balances | 280 |

Cash sales (9,800) and the provision (150) are left out.

**Trade receivables control account**

| Debit | | Credit | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| 1 Jan Balance b/d | 18,450 | 1 Jan Balance b/d | 320 |
| Sales | 74,600 | Bank | 66,250 |
| Bank (dishonoured cheque) | 415 | Discount allowed | 1,340 |
| Bank (refund) | 120 | Sales returns | 2,180 |
| 31 Dec Balance c/d | 280 | Irrecoverable debts | 760 |
| | | Trade payables control (contra) | 900 |
| | | 31 Dec Balance c/d | 22,115 |
| | **93,865** | | **93,865** |
| 1 Jan Balance b/d | 22,115 | 1 Jan Balance b/d | 280 |

Working: debits 18,450 + 74,600 + 415 + 120 + 280 = 93,865. Credits before the closing debit balance: 320 + 66,250 + 1,340 + 2,180 + 760 + 900 = 71,750. Closing debit balance = 93,865 − 71,750 = **22,115**.

## Using a control account to find errors

When the control account balance and the total of the ledger list disagree, each error must be placed: does it affect the control account (because a book of original entry total was wrong), the individual accounts (because a posting was wrong), or the list (because a balance was copied wrongly)?

### Worked example 2: Northgate Café, payables at 30 June 2027

The trade payables control account was prepared from these totals: opening balance 9,640; credit purchases 52,380; payments to suppliers 49,120; discount received 870; returns outwards 1,450; contra with the receivables ledger 610.

Closing balance = (9,640 + 52,380) − (49,120 + 870 + 1,450 + 610) = 62,020 − 52,050 = **9,970** (credit).

The list of supplier balances totals **8,520**. Three errors are then found:

1. The purchases day book was overcast by 100. This affects only the control account, which used the total; the individual supplier accounts were posted from the separate invoices.
2. A credit note of 65 from Corlan Dairy was posted to the credit side of the supplier's account instead of the debit side. The supplier's balance is overstated by 2 × 65 = 130.
3. The balance of 1,480 on Tamsin Foods' account was left off the list.

| Control account | | List of balances | |
|---|---:|---|---:|
| Balance as calculated | 9,970 | Total as extracted | 8,520 |
| Less purchases day book overcast | (100) | Less credit note posted to wrong side | (130) |
| | | Add balance omitted | 1,480 |
| **Corrected balance** | **9,870** | **Corrected total** | **9,870** |

The corrected figures agree, so 9,870 is shown as trade payables in the statement of financial position.

## Exam technique

- **Explain the purpose**: give the purpose and how it is achieved, for example "it detects fraud, because the control account is prepared by someone other than the ledger clerk".
- **Prepare**: title the account, date the opening and closing balances, use the other account's name for each entry, and bring down the closing balance.
- **Distractors**: the question often includes cash sales, provisions or discount received in a receivables question. Leaving them out earns marks.

## Common errors

- Including cash sales, cash purchases or the provision for irrecoverable debts.
- Putting a dishonoured cheque on the credit side.
- Netting off opening debit and credit balances instead of showing both.
- Showing the contra in only one of the two control accounts.
- Correcting the control account for an error that affected only an individual account.
- Forgetting to double a posting to the wrong side when correcting a list.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is the provision for irrecoverable debts not in the receivables control account?**
It is an estimate of possible losses. No customer's account changes, so it does not affect the total owed by customers. Only debts actually written off are entered.

**How can a receivables ledger have a credit balance?**
A customer who has overpaid, or returned goods after paying, is owed money by the business. That customer's account has a credit balance.

**Which errors affect the control account but not the ledger list?**
Errors in the totals of the books of original entry, such as an overcast day book, because the control account uses those totals while the individual accounts are posted line by line.
