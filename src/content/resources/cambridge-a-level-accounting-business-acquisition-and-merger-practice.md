## Questions

**1.** Wetherby Foods Ltd buys the whole business of Hartshorne Tiles at these agreed values: premises 60,000, equipment 14,000, inventory 9,200, trade receivables 7,800, bank 1,500, trade payables 6,500 (all taken over). It pays 40,000 ordinary shares of 1 issued at 1.50, debentures of 20,000 and 20,000 cash.

**(a)** Calculate the net assets acquired. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the purchase consideration. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the goodwill. **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Distinguish between buying a business as a whole and buying its individual assets. **[2]**
**(b)** State two reasons why two sole traders might choose to merge. **[2]**

**3.** Pargeter Bikes and Ostler Cycles merge to form a partnership. Pargeter contributes equipment 7,500, inventory 4,800, trade receivables 2,600, bank 900 and owes trade payables 3,100; goodwill of 3,000 is agreed. Ostler contributes equipment 12,000, inventory 6,100, trade receivables 3,400, owes a bank overdraft 1,300 and trade payables 2,900; goodwill of 4,500 is agreed.

**(a)** Prepare the journal entry in the partnership's books for Pargeter's business. **[5]**
**(b)** Calculate Ostler's opening capital. **[3]**

**4.** Mowbray Seeds and Prowse Hire merge to form Seedhire Services Ltd, which issues 1 ordinary shares at par equal to the agreed value of each business including goodwill. Mowbray: equipment 20,000, inventory 8,000, trade receivables 5,000, trade payables 3,000, goodwill 6,000. Prowse: equipment 25,000, trade receivables 4,500, bank 1,500, trade payables 2,000, goodwill 4,000.

**(a)** Calculate the shares issued to each trader. **[2]**
**(b)** Prepare the journal entry for Prowse's business. **[4]**
**(c)** State the total issued share capital. **[1]**

**5.** Loveday and Redfern share profits 2:1. Their assets are premises 30,000, equipment 8,000, inventory 5,500, trade receivables 4,200 and bank 1,800; trade payables are 3,500; capitals are Loveday 28,000 and Redfern 18,000. They agree new values: premises 36,000, equipment 7,200, inventory 5,000, trade receivables 4,000. Goodwill of 9,000 is created in the old ratio. Wragby, a sole trader, then joins with equipment 4,000, inventory 2,400, trade receivables 1,600, trade payables 1,100 and agreed goodwill of 1,500.

**(a)** Prepare the revaluation account. **[4]**
**(b)** Calculate the three partners' capital balances after all entries. **[5]**

**6.** Bellerby Foods Ltd acquires the partnership of Pellew and Dunstan. Agreed values: premises 48,000, equipment 16,500, inventory 7,200, trade receivables 5,400, trade payables 4,300 (taken over). Consideration: 40,000 ordinary shares of 1 at 1.50, debentures of 12,000 and 6,000 cash.

**(a)** Calculate the goodwill. **[4]**
**(b)** Prepare the journal entries in Bellerby's books. **[5]**

**7.** In Pellew and Dunstan's books, the book values were premises 44,000, equipment 17,000, inventory 7,600, trade receivables 5,700 and trade payables 4,300. Capitals were Pellew 42,000 and Dunstan 28,000, profits shared 3:2. Using the consideration in question 6, Pellew takes 26,000 shares and 7,800 of debentures; Dunstan takes 14,000 shares, 4,200 of debentures and the cash.

**(a)** Prepare the realisation account. **[4]**
**(b)** Show each partner's closing capital and what each receives. **[3]**

**8.** Before the acquisition in question 6, Bellerby's statement of financial position showed: non-current assets 150,000; inventory 32,000; trade receivables 24,000; bank 21,000; share capital 80,000; share premium 10,000; retained earnings 69,000; 5% debentures 40,000; trade payables 28,000. Prepare the statement of financial position after the acquisition. **[9]**

**9.** In the first full year after the acquisition, Bellerby earned an operating profit of 38,000 from its existing activities and 11,400 from the acquired business. Ignore tax and dividends.

**(a)** Calculate total finance costs, if new debentures carry 8%. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit for the year. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why this profit cannot be compared directly with the previous year. **[2]**

**10.** Cobbett Signs earns an annual profit of 24,000 and Delamere Print 21,000. Each rents a unit for 6,000 a year; a shared unit would cost 6,000. Delamere has an overdraft of 9,000. They are considering a merger as a partnership with agreed goodwill of 10,000. Evaluate whether they should merge and make a recommendation. **[8]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Assets: 60,000 + 14,000 + 9,200 + 7,800 + 1,500 = 92,500 [1]. Less trade payables 6,500 = **86,000** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Shares 40,000 x 1.50 = 60,000 [1]. Plus 20,000 + 20,000 = **100,000** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** 100,000 - 86,000 = **14,000** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Separate working for net assets and consideration usually lets partly correct work earn credit, whereas a single unexplained goodwill figure does not.

**2. (a)** A whole business takes over the assets and usually the liabilities for one price, so goodwill can arise [1]. Individual assets are chosen one by one, liabilities stay with the seller and no goodwill arises [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Any two: pool capital or skills; gain customers or market share; share premises and costs; remove a competitor; limited liability if a company [1] [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers tend to gain credit when each reason is a distinct idea, so two wordings of "to grow" would count once at most.

**3. (a)** Dr equipment 7,500, inventory 4,800, trade receivables 2,600 [2]; Dr bank 900 and goodwill 3,000 [1]; Cr trade payables 3,100 [1]; Cr capital: Pargeter 15,700 [1] (debits 18,800 = credits 18,800) **[5]**
**(b)** Net assets 12,000 + 6,100 + 3,400 - 1,300 - 2,900 = 17,300 [2]; plus goodwill 4,500 = **21,800** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A journal that does not balance is a visible warning to check; the goodwill debit is the item most often missing.

**4. (a)** Mowbray: 20,000 + 8,000 + 5,000 - 3,000 = 30,000, plus 6,000 = **36,000 shares** [1]. Prowse: 25,000 + 4,500 + 1,500 - 2,000 = 29,000, plus 4,000 = **33,000 shares** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Dr equipment 25,000, trade receivables 4,500 and bank 1,500 [2]; Dr goodwill 4,000 [1]; Cr trade payables 2,000 and Cr share capital 33,000 [1] **[4]**
**(c)** 36,000 + 33,000 = **69,000** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Because shares are issued at par, no share premium arises; answers that add one have usually mixed up this case with an issue at a premium.

**5. (a)** Revaluation account. Dr: equipment 800, inventory 500, trade receivables 200 (losses 1,500) [1]; Cr: premises 6,000 [1]; profit 6,000 - 1,500 = 4,500 [1]; shared Loveday 3,000, Redfern 1,500 [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Goodwill credited 6,000 and 3,000 [1]. Wragby: 4,000 + 2,400 + 1,600 - 1,100 = 6,900 [1]; plus goodwill 1,500 = **8,400** [1]. Loveday 28,000 + 3,000 + 6,000 = **37,000** [1]; Redfern 18,000 + 1,500 + 3,000 = **22,500** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Using the old ratio for both the revaluation profit and the existing goodwill matters; using the new ratio changes every capital balance.

**6. (a)** Net assets 48,000 + 16,500 + 7,200 + 5,400 - 4,300 = 72,800 [2]. Consideration 60,000 + 12,000 + 6,000 = 78,000 [1]. Goodwill **5,200** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Journal 1: Dr premises 48,000, equipment 16,500, inventory 7,200, trade receivables 5,400, goodwill 5,200 [1]; Cr trade payables 4,300 and business purchase 78,000 [1]. Journal 2: Dr business purchase 78,000 [1]; Cr share capital 40,000, share premium 20,000 [1]; Cr debentures 12,000 and bank 6,000 [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The premium is the issue price above nominal value (0.50 x 40,000), not the whole 60,000; answers that show nominal value and premium separately are easier to follow.

**7. (a)** Dr assets at book value 74,300 [1]; Cr trade payables 4,300 [1]; Cr Bellerby Foods Ltd 78,000 [1]; balancing profit on realisation 78,000 - 70,000 = 8,000 on the debit side [1] (both sides total 82,300) **[4]**
**(b)** Profit shared 3:2: Pellew 4,800, Dunstan 3,200 [1]. Closing capital: Pellew 46,800 and Dunstan 31,200 [1]. Pellew receives 39,000 shares + 7,800 debentures; Dunstan 21,000 shares + 4,200 debentures + 6,000 cash, which clears both accounts [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The shares are valued at the issue price of 1.50, so receipts only match the capital balances when that price is used.

**8.** Non-current assets 150,000 + 48,000 + 16,500 + 5,200 = 219,700 [1]; inventory 39,200 [1]; trade receivables 29,400 [1]; bank 21,000 - 6,000 = 15,000 [1]; total assets **303,300** [1]. Share capital 120,000 [1]; share premium 30,000 [1]; retained earnings 69,000; debentures 52,000 [1]; trade payables 32,300 [1]; total equity and liabilities **303,300** **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Both totals should agree; if they do not, the usual cause is goodwill left out or the debentures omitted from non-current liabilities.

**9. (a)** Existing 40,000 x 5% = 2,000; new 12,000 x 8% = 960 [1]; total **2,960** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Operating profit 38,000 + 11,400 = 49,400 [1]; less 2,960 = **46,440** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** The profit now includes the acquired business and extra debenture interest, so the earlier year is not like for like [2] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison point gains more when it says what changed and why it matters, rather than only saying the profit rose.

**10.** Advantage: shared unit saves 6,000, lifting combined profit from 45,000 to 51,000 [1]. Advantage: combined skills and customers could increase sales [1]. Disadvantage: unlimited liability means each partner is exposed to the other's 9,000 overdraft [1]. Disadvantage: the 10,000 goodwill must be agreed and profit sharing may be disputed [1]. Context used for at least two points [1]. Judgement, for example merge if they agree a ratio and clear the overdraft first, because the 6,000 saving is secure and the risks can be managed [3]. Credit also for the opposite view if justified **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation answers usually gain more from a point tied to the figures given than from a general statement, and a recommendation needs a reason.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using book values when the question gives agreed values.
- Forgetting to deduct liabilities taken over before finding goodwill.
- Putting the whole issue price into share capital.
- Sharing the revaluation profit or existing goodwill in the new ratio.
- Omitting the goodwill entry or crediting it to the wrong capital account.
- Totals that do not balance and are not checked.
- Leaving the new debentures out of the statement of financial position.
- Evaluation with no figures and no justified recommendation.
