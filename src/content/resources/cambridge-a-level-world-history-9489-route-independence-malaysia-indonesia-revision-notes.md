**These notes condense The route to independence: Malaysia and Indonesia, c.1909-67, the third topic of the Paper 4 International option in Cambridge 9489.** For explanation and an annotated plan see the [Malaysia and Indonesia study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-route-independence-malaysia-indonesia/); for essays and short questions see the [Malaysia and Indonesia practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-route-independence-malaysia-indonesia-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

- 9.1 How widespread was the growth of nationalism and hostility to colonial rule in Malaya in the period up to 1945?
- 9.2 What factors led to the formation of Malaysia?
- 9.3 In what ways, and for what reasons, did the nationalist movement develop in Indonesia up to 1945?
- 9.4 What factors led to the formation of Indonesia and what challenges did it face up to the mid-1960s?

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Unfederated Malay States | Kedah, Perlis, Kelantan, Terengganu (from Siam, 1909) and Johor (1914) |
| Divide and rule | Governing communities separately so they did not unite against Britain |
| Kaum Muda | Reformist Islamic "Young Group" |
| KMM | Kesatuan Melayu Muda (1938), radical pan-Malay nationalists |
| MPAJA | Communist-led Malayan People's Anti-Japanese Army |
| KRIS | 1945 body seeking Malayan independence within a greater Indonesia |
| Malayan Union | Britain's 1946 unitary scheme, abandoned after Malay protest |
| UMNO | United Malays National Organisation (1946) |
| Briggs Plan | 1950 resettlement of Chinese squatters into New Villages |
| Alliance | UMNO-MCA (later MIC) coalition |
| Merdeka | "Independence": Malaya, 31 August 1957 |
| Ethical Policy | Dutch welfare and education policy from 1901 |
| Volksraad | Advisory People's Council in the Dutch East Indies (1918) |
| Putera / Peta | Japanese-sponsored mass organisation (1943) / Indonesian volunteer army (1943) |
| Pancasila | Sukarno's five principles of the state (1945) |
| Konfrontasi | Indonesia's confrontation with Malaysia, 1963-66 |
| Supersemar | Order of Eleventh March 1966 transferring authority to Suharto |

## 9.1 Malayan nationalism -- how widespread?

- **Limited:** divided communities; English-educated Malays loyal to Britain; Chinese politics focused on China.
- **Growing:** Kaum Muda, Malay associations and congresses, KMM radicalism.
- **Japanese effect:** British prestige destroyed; Malays politicised; MPAJA resistance; racial hostility deepened.

## 9.2 Malaysia -- five factors

1. **Malay unity** against the Malayan Union created UMNO.
2. **Defeating communism** in the Emergency removed the radical alternative.
3. **Inter-communal Alliance** met Britain's condition for independence.
4. **British willingness** to decolonise on agreed terms (Reid Commission).
5. **Strategic, economic and racial calculations** for the 1963 merger -- and the disputes that ended it.

## 9.3 Indonesian nationalism -- reasons and forms

- **Reasons:** Western education under the Ethical Policy; resentment of Chinese commercial dominance; a shared language and Islam; Dutch refusal of reform.
- **Forms:** religious (Sarekat Islam), communist (PKI), secular nationalist (PNI, Partindo), co-operative (Gerindo, Gapi).
- **Japanese effect:** mobilisation through Putera and Peta; leaders gained a mass platform; hardship deepened anti-colonial feeling.

## 9.4 Independence and challenges

- **Dutch failure:** Republican resistance; Linggadjati's collapse; Madiun reassuring the USA; UN and US pressure.
- **1950s challenges:** unstable cabinets, constitutional deadlock, regional revolts, economic crisis, ethnic tension.
- **New Order:** Konfrontasi, the 30 September Movement, the anti-communist killings, KAMI, Supersemar.

## Exam traps

- **Federation of Malaya (1948)** is not **Malaysia (1963)**.
- **Lai Teck** betrayed the MCP; **Chin Peng** led the revolt.
- **Templer** came after Briggs; credit each correctly.
- **Linggadjati** failed over interpretation and Dutch force, not Indonesian refusal alone.
- **Suharto's rise** was gradual, 1965-67.

## Self-test

1. Which states did Siam transfer in 1909? *Kedah, Perlis, Kelantan and Terengganu.*
2. Who led the KMM? *Ibrahim Yaacob.*
3. What did the Malayan Union propose that angered Malays? *Reduced powers for the sultans and easy citizenship for non-Malays.*
4. Who founded UMNO? *Dato Onn bin Jaafar.*
5. What was the main aim of the Briggs Plan? *To cut guerrillas off from supplies by resettling Chinese squatters.*
6. Which parties formed the Alliance? *UMNO, the MCA and later the MIC.*
7. Give one reason for Singapore's separation in 1965. *Disputes over Lee Kuan Yew's "Malaysian Malaysia" against Malay special rights.*
8. What did the Soetardjo Petition ask for? *Indonesian autonomy within the Dutch kingdom within ten years.*
9. Who led the PNI from 1927? *Sukarno.*
10. What was Peta? *A Japanese-trained Indonesian volunteer army.*
11. Which revolt in 1948 helped win US sympathy for the Republic? *The communist revolt at Madiun, crushed by the Republic.*
12. What was Supersemar? *Sukarno's order of 11 March 1966 transferring authority to Suharto.*
