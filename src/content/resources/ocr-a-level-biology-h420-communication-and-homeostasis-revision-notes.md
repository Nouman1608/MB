These notes condense learning outcomes 5.1.1(a) to (d) of the OCR A Level Biology A specification (H420, Version 4.1, April 2026, first assessment 2025), with the PAG11 link. For explanations and an annotated model answer, read the [communication and homeostasis study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-biology-h420-communication-and-homeostasis/), then test yourself with the [communication and homeostasis practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-biology-h420-communication-and-homeostasis-practice/). ## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | A change in the internal or external environment |
| Receptor | A cell or organ that detects a stimulus |
| Effector | A muscle or gland that brings about a response |
| Cell signalling | One cell releases a chemical that is detected by, and causes a response in, another cell |
| Homeostasis | Maintaining a constant internal environment within narrow limits |
| Set point | The optimum value of a controlled factor |
| Negative feedback | A change triggers a response that reverses it, returning the factor towards the set point |
| Positive feedback | A change triggers a response that increases the change |
| Endotherm | An animal that keeps its core temperature fairly constant using heat from metabolism |
| Ectotherm | An animal that relies largely on heat from its surroundings |

## Why communication is needed: 5.1.1(a)

- Cells are specialised and many are far from the surface.
- Organisms must respond to external changes (temperature, light, predators) and internal changes (glucose, CO₂, water potential).
- Enzymes and cells work only within narrow ranges.
- Organs must be coordinated, for example heart rate and breathing during exercise.

## Cell signalling: 5.1.1(b)

| | Adjacent cells | Distant cells |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | Neurotransmitter | Hormone |
| Route | Across a synapse | In the blood |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| Targets | One cell or a few | All cells with matching receptors |

Target cells have receptors complementary in shape to the signal molecule.

## Control systems: 5.1.1(c)

stimulus → receptor → communication (nerves or hormones) → coordinator → effector → response → factor returns towards set point

| Negative feedback | Positive feedback |
|---|---|
| Reverses the change | Amplifies the change |
| Keeps a factor stable | Produces a rapid, large change |
| Body temperature, blood glucose | Blood clotting (platelets), oxytocin in childbirth |

- Separate mechanisms for raising and for lowering a factor give finer control.
- Accidental positive feedback can be harmful, for example in severe hypothermia.

## Thermoregulation in endotherms: 5.1.1(d)

**Receptors**
- Peripheral temperature receptors in the skin: detect skin and external temperature.
- Receptors in the hypothalamus: detect blood (core) temperature.

**Coordinator:** the hypothalamus, sending nerve impulses to effectors.

| Effector | Too hot | Too cold |
|---|---|---|
| Skin arterioles | Vasodilation; more radiation | Vasoconstriction; less radiation |
| Sweat glands | More sweat; evaporation removes heat | Less sweat |
| Hair erector muscles | Relax; hairs flat | Contract; hairs erect; trap insulating air |
| Skeletal muscle | — | Shivering; heat from respiration |
| Metabolic rate | Lower | Higher |

**Behaviour:** shade, stillness, spreading out; huddling, curling up, shelter, sunshine; clothing.

## Thermoregulation in ectotherms: 5.1.1(d)

- Basking; orienting the body to change the area facing the sun.
- Lying on warm surfaces; sheltering in shade, burrows or water.
- Timing activity to suitable times of day.
- Need less food than endotherms; less active in cold conditions.

## Worked reminders

- A lizard basking side-on to the sun in the morning maximises the area receiving radiation; facing the sun at midday minimises it.
- A rise in core temperature detected by the hypothalamus leads to vasodilation and sweating: negative feedback.
- Oxytocin causes contractions, which cause more oxytocin release: positive feedback, ended by birth.

## Exam traps

- "Capillaries move towards the skin": arterioles dilate or constrict; vessels do not move.
- "Sweating cools you" without evaporation.
- "Ectotherms cannot regulate temperature": they regulate by behaviour.
- Positive feedback described as "beneficial feedback".
- The hypothalamus called an effector.
- Homeostasis defined without "within narrow limits".

## Quick self-test

1. Give two reasons why multicellular animals need communication systems.
2. Define cell signalling.
3. Give an example of signalling between adjacent cells.
4. Why do only some cells respond to a particular hormone?
5. Distinguish between a receptor and an effector.
6. Define negative feedback.
7. Give two examples of positive feedback.
8. Where are temperature receptors found in a mammal?
9. Explain how vasoconstriction reduces heat loss.
10. Explain how raised hairs help in the cold.
11. Give two behavioural responses of a lizard that is too cold.
12. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of being an ectotherm.

### Answers

1. To respond to changes in the external and internal environment, and to coordinate the activities of different organs.
2. One cell releases a chemical that is detected by another cell, which responds.
3. A neurone releasing neurotransmitter across a synapse to the next cell.
4. Only target cells have receptors with a shape complementary to the hormone.
5. A receptor detects a stimulus; an effector (muscle or gland) carries out the response.
6. A change from the set point triggers a response that reverses the change, returning the factor towards the set point.
7. Blood clotting (platelets attract more platelets); oxytocin and uterine contractions during birth.
8. In the skin (peripheral receptors) and in the hypothalamus.
9. Arterioles supplying the skin constrict, so less blood flows near the surface and less heat is lost by radiation.
10. Hair erector muscles contract, raising the hairs, which trap a layer of air that insulates the skin.
11. Basking in the sun; turning its body so a large surface faces the sun; lying on warm rock.
12. Advantage: needs less food, as little energy is used to produce heat. Disadvantage: less active in cold conditions (so more vulnerable to predators).

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the hypothalamus a receptor or a coordinator?**
Both: it contains temperature receptors that monitor the blood, and it acts as the coordinator that sends impulses to effectors.

**Why is positive feedback not used for homeostasis?**
It drives a factor further from its starting value, so it cannot keep conditions stable. It is useful only when a rapid change is needed and then switched off.

**Do ectotherms show physiological responses?**
For 5.1.1(d) the specification asks only for behavioural responses in ectotherms, so focus on basking, orientation, shelter and timing of activity.
