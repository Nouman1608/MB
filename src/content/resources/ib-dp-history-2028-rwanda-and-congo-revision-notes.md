**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 7 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): colonialism and crisis in Rwanda and the Congo, circa 1875 to 2003. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies. This study deals with atrocity and genocide, so use precise, respectful language. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-rwanda-and-congo/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Congo Free State and Belgian Congo | Personal rule, rubber terror, then Belgian paternalism; late nationalism |
| Colonialism in Rwanda | Rwabugiri's centralisation, ID cards, Belgian favouritism and its reversal |
| Post-independence civil war in Rwanda | Purges, land crisis, RPF, Arusha, plane crash |
| Mobutu and Zaire | Congo Crisis, dictatorship, plunder, First Congo War |
| Rwandan genocide | Planned mass killing, propaganda, weak international response, justice |
| Second Congo War | Regional war, minerals, militias, atrocities |

## Timeline

- 1885: Congo Free State. 1908: Belgian takeover.
- 1890s: Rwabugiri's rule; German presence. 1919: Belgian mandate.
- Early 1930s: Belgian ID cards in Rwanda.
- 1959: Rwandan revolution; riots in Léopoldville.
- 30 June 1960: Congo independence. 1 July 1962: Rwanda independence.
- 17 January 1961: Lumumba killed. November 1965: Mobutu's coup.
- 1973: Habyarimana's coup. October 1990: RPF invasion. August 1993: Arusha.
- 6 April 1994: plane shot down. April to July 1994: genocide. 4 July: RPF takes Kigali.
- 1996 to 1997: First Congo War; Mobutu flees May 1997.
- 1998 to 2003: Second Congo War. January 2001: Laurent Kabila killed.

## The Congo to 1960

| Feature | Free State (1885 to 1908) | Belgian Congo (1908 to 1960) |
|---|---|---|
| Ruler | Leopold II personally | Belgian state |
| Economy | Ivory and rubber by quota; violence | Copper (Union Minière), diamonds, cotton, uranium |
| Labour | Forced; hostages; punishment | Forced and taxed labour; wage labour in mines |
| Politics | None for Africans | None until the late 1950s |
| Reform | Casement and Morel campaign | Missions, schools, clinics; *évolués* |

## Rwanda: identity and colonialism

- **Rwabugiri (about 1867 to 1895):** centralised the monarchy; clientship and labour obligations; hardened the Tutsi-Hutu divide.
- **Belgian rule:** ID cards in the early 1930s; Tutsi chiefs favoured; "Hamitic hypothesis"; forced labour and coffee.
- **1950s shift:** the Church and administration back the Hutu majority; 1957 Hutu Manifesto; 1959 revolution; Kayibanda president.
- **Argument:** colonialism created or hardened ethnic identity (support: ID cards). Challenge: earlier differences in status existed, and later leaders made their own choices.

## Road to genocide

| Cause | Detail |
|---|---|
| Purges and discrimination | 1959 to 1964 violence; quotas favouring northern Hutu |
| Economic and environmental | Land shortage, coffee price collapse (late 1980s), famine |
| War | RPF invasion in 1990; fear of Tutsi return |
| Politics | Arusha power-sharing opposed by "Hutu Power" extremists |
| Trigger | 6 April 1994 plane crash |

## Genocide: key facts

- About 800,000 killed (estimates vary), in about 100 days.
- Interahamwe, army and officials; roadblocks; radio RTLM and *Kangura*.
- UNAMIR (Dallaire) reduced after ten Belgian peacekeepers were killed; France's Opération Turquoise in June.
- RPF victory in July; two million Hutu flee, many into Zaire.
- Justice: ICTR (Arusha), national courts, *gacaca*.

## Mobutu and the Congo wars

- **Congo Crisis:** army mutiny, Belgian intervention, Katanga secession (Tshombe), UN force, Lumumba killed (1961), Mobutu in power 1965.
- **Rule:** single party, cult of personality (authenticity campaign), renamed Zaire in 1971.
- **Economy:** plunder, collapse of infrastructure, Cold War backing.
- **First Congo War:** Rwanda and Uganda back Laurent Kabila; Mobutu flees May 1997.
- **Second Congo War:** Kabila against former allies; Rwanda, Uganda against Angola, Zimbabwe, Namibia; minerals (coltan, diamonds, gold); militias including M23 later; Lusaka 1999; transition 2003.

## Arguments to practise

| Claim | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Colonial rule caused the Rwandan genocide | ID cards, favouritism | Planning in 1994; war; propaganda |
| Mobutu's rule was entirely due to the Cold War | US and Belgian support | His own methods and Zairian institutions |
| The international community failed Rwanda | UNAMIR cuts, delayed response | Limited information, mandate and political will |
| The Second Congo War was about minerals | Resource exploitation documented | Security fears, ethnic and regional rivalries |

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was Mobutu's rule a continuation of Belgian colonial rule?"

- Continuity: centralised, authoritarian state; exploitation of resources; a small elite; dependence on foreign powers.
- Change: African rulers, a nationalist ideology and a cult of personality; personal rather than company wealth.
- Perspectives: one view stresses the colonial legacy of weak institutions; another stresses Mobutu's personal choices.
- Judgement: the colonial legacy shaped the state, but Mobutu's personalised rule was a distinct form of dictatorship.

## Common mistakes

- Using "tribe" or "tribal war".
- Giving the 1994 genocide as the only Rwandan topic.
- Forgetting Katanga and the UN in the Congo Crisis.
- Treating the two Congo wars as one.

## Self-test

1. Who ruled the Congo Free State?
2. What did Belgian ID cards in Rwanda record?
3. Which agreement in 1993 aimed to end the Rwandan war?
4. Name one purpose of RTLM radio.
5. In what year was Mobutu overthrown?
6. Name two resources fought over in the Second Congo War.

**Answers:** 1. Leopold II. 2. Whether a person was Hutu, Tutsi or Twa. 3. The Arusha Accords. 4. To spread propaganda and incite killing. 5. 1997. 6. Any two of coltan, diamonds, gold, copper.

## Key terms

- **Force Publique:** the colonial army and police of the Congo.
- **Évolué:** an educated Congolese person who adopted European ways.
- **Interahamwe:** the militia linked to the Hutu Power movement.
- **Gacaca:** community courts used to try genocide suspects.
- **Kleptocracy:** rule in which leaders steal public resources.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I handle estimates of the dead?** Use "about" and say they are estimates.

**What if I only know one of the two countries well?** The study joins both, so be ready to use each.
