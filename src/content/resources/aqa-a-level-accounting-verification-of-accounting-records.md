This study guide teaches section 3.4, Verification of accounting records, of the AQA A-level
Accounting (7127) specification, version 1.1 (October 2026), for A-level exams from June 2019
onwards. The qualification is linear and untiered, and section 3.4 is examined on Paper 1, which
assesses sections 1–8 and 14–18. It builds directly on
[the double entry model](/resources/aqa-a-level-accounting-double-entry-model/): every technique here
checks records made with that model.

## The six errors a trial balance does not reveal

Agreeing trial balance totals suggest the double entry arithmetic is right. They do **not** prove the
records are right. Each error below keeps debits equal to credits.

| Error | What happens | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Commission | Correct amount, correct type of account, wrong account | A receipt from J Patel credited to J Patil |
| Complete reversal | Correct accounts and amount, but debit and credit swapped | Cash paid to a supplier debited to bank, credited to the supplier |
| Compensating | Two or more errors of equal value cancel out | Wages overcast by £100 and revenue overcast by £100 |
| Omission | The whole transaction is left out | An invoice never entered in the sales journal |
| Original entry | Wrong amount in the book of prime entry, so both sides use it | £1 380 invoice entered as £1 830 |
| Principle | Entry in the wrong class of account | Van repairs debited to the motor vehicles (asset) account |

## The four errors a trial balance does reveal

| Error | What happens | Clue |
|---|---|---|
| Addition | A total in a journal or account is cast wrongly | Often a round figure |
| Partial omission | Only one side of the double entry is recorded | Difference equals the amount omitted |
| Transposition | Digits reversed in one entry only (£1 250 posted as £1 520) | Difference divisible by 9 |
| Unequal posting | Debit and credit entered at different amounts | Difference equals the gap between them |

A transposition made in the book of prime entry, so that both sides use the wrong figure, is an error
of original entry and is not revealed.

## Correcting errors: the general journal and suspense account

When a trial balance does not agree, the difference is entered in a **suspense account** so that the
statements can be drafted. Each error is then corrected through the **general journal**, with a
narrative. Only errors that caused the imbalance pass through suspense. Errors that the trial balance
does not reveal are corrected between the ledger accounts concerned.

### Worked example 1: suspense account and corrected profit

Ferncliff Garden Supplies, a sole trader, extracted a trial balance at 31 March 2026. Debits totalled
£186 420 and credits £185 790. The draft profit for the year was £42 300. The following errors were
found.

1. The sales journal was undercast by £400.
2. Rent paid of £1 250 was correctly entered in the cash book but posted to the rent account as £1 520.
3. Discount allowed of £180 was entered in the customer's account but not in the discount allowed account.
4. A cheque of £2 400 from P Okafor was credited to the account of P Okonkwo.
5. Repairs to a delivery van, £860, were debited to the motor vehicles account. Ignore depreciation.
6. Insurance of £795 was credited in the cash book but debited to the insurance account as £935.

**Step 1: open the suspense account.** Debits exceed credits by 186 420 − 185 790 = £630, so suspense
needs a **credit** balance of £630.

**Step 2: journal entries.**

```
                                         Dr £      Cr £
1  Suspense                              400
     Revenue                                       400
   Sales journal undercast
2  Suspense                              270
     Rent                                          270
   Rent overstated, 1 520 - 1 250
3  Discount allowed                      180
     Suspense                                      180
   Discount allowed not posted
4  P Okonkwo                           2 400
     P Okafor                                    2 400
   Receipt credited to wrong customer
5  Van repairs                           860
     Motor vehicles                                860
   Revenue expenditure capitalised
6  Suspense                              140
     Insurance                                     140
   Insurance overstated, 935 - 795
```

**Step 3: the suspense account clears.**

```
                     Suspense account
Revenue        400 | Balance b/d          630
Rent           270 | Discount allowed     180
Insurance      140 |
              ----                       ----
               810                        810
```

**Step 4: corrected profit.**

```
Draft profit for the year                  42 300
Add: revenue undercast                        400
     rent overstated                          270
     insurance overstated                     140
Less: discount allowed omitted               (180)
      van repairs capitalised                (860)
Corrected profit for the year              42 070
```

Error 4 changes neither profit nor total receivables, but P Okafor's account wrongly still shows £2 400 owing.

## The effect of errors on financial statements

For every error, ask: does it change profit, and does it change an asset, liability or capital figure?

- An expense wrongly capitalised (error 5 above) **overstates profit and non-current assets** by the
  same amount.
- An omitted accrual overstates profit and understates current liabilities.
- Overvalued closing inventory overstates profit and current assets.

To **redraft** statements, correct profit first, then each affected line of the statement of financial
position, then check that net assets equal the corrected capital. If they do not, an adjustment has been
missed.

## Control accounts

A **sales ledger control account** totals the receivables ledger; a **purchases ledger control account**
totals the payables ledger. In 7127 they are **memorandum records**. The individual customer and
supplier accounts carry the double entry. The control account is built separately from totals in the
books of prime entry, and its closing balance is compared with the total of the list of individual
balances.

| Sales ledger control | |
|---|---|
| **Debit** | Balance b/d (debit), credit sales, dishonoured cheques, interest charged on overdue accounts, refunds to customers, balance c/d (credit) |
| **Credit** | Balance b/d (credit), receipts, discount allowed, sales returns, irrecoverable debts, contra entries, balance c/d (debit) |

| Purchases ledger control | |
|---|---|
| **Debit** | Balance b/d (debit), payments, discount received, purchases returns, contra entries, balance c/d (credit) |
| **Credit** | Balance b/d (credit), credit purchases, interest charged by suppliers, refunds from suppliers, balance c/d (debit) |

Four items need care.

- **Contra entries.** When a business both buys from and sells to the same firm, the two balances can be
  set off. Debit the purchases ledger control account and credit the sales ledger control account.
- **Interest charged on overdue accounts** increases what a customer owes (debit sales ledger control).
  Interest charged by a supplier increases what you owe (credit purchases ledger control).
- **Credit balances in the sales ledger** arise when a customer overpays or returns goods already paid
  for. **Debit balances in the purchases ledger** arise when you overpay a supplier. Carry both down
  separately; never net them off.
- **Cash sales, cash purchases and provisions for doubtful debts never appear.** None of them changes
  the amount owed by or to an individual credit customer or supplier.

## Bank reconciliation statements

A bank reconciliation explains why the cash book bank column and the bank statement differ. Work in two stages.

1. **Update the cash book** for items on the bank statement not yet recorded (standing orders, direct
   debits, credit transfers, bank charges, dishonoured cheques) and for errors in the cash book.
2. **Reconcile** the updated balance with the statement, using items in the cash book that the bank has
   not yet processed: **unpresented cheques** and **outstanding lodgements**.

## Benefits and limitations of verification techniques

| Technique | Benefits | Limitations |
|---|---|---|
| Trial balance | Checks the arithmetic of the double entry; reveals addition, partial omission, transposition and unequal posting errors; a starting point for financial statements | Does not reveal commission, complete reversal, compensating, omission, original entry or principle errors |
| Control accounts | Locate an error to one ledger; give quick totals of receivables and payables; deter fraud when someone other than the ledger clerk prepares them | A wrong figure in the book of prime entry (original entry), a missing invoice (omission) or a posting to the wrong customer (commission) still agrees |
| Bank reconciliation | Finds items the bank has recorded that the business has not, errors in the cash book and errors by the bank | Checks only the bank column; a payment posted to the wrong expense account, or a cheque written for the wrong amount and recorded as written, is not revealed |

Addition, partial omission, transposition and unequal posting errors in the personal ledgers make a
control account disagree with its list of balances.

## Common errors

- Putting a suspense entry against an error of commission or principle. Those errors never caused the
  imbalance.
- Including cash sales or a change in the provision for doubtful debts in the sales ledger control account.
- Netting credit balances in the sales ledger against debit balances.
- Treating a transposition in the book of prime entry as revealed by the trial balance.
- Starting the reconciliation without updating the cash book, then using the bank statement balance in
  the statement of financial position.
- Changing profit for an error that only moves an amount between two asset accounts.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does an agreed trial balance prove the records are correct?**
No. Agreeing totals suggest the double entry arithmetic is right, but six types of error keep debits equal to credits, so the records can still be wrong.

**Which errors pass through a suspense account?**
Only errors that caused the trial balance to disagree pass through suspense. Errors the trial balance does not reveal, such as commission or principle errors, are corrected directly between the ledger accounts concerned using the general journal.

**Are control accounts part of the double entry in AQA 7127?**
No. In AQA 7127 the sales and purchases ledger control accounts are memorandum records; the individual customer and supplier accounts carry the double entry. The control account balance is compared with the total of the list of individual balances.

**What never appears in a sales ledger control account?**
Cash sales, cash purchases and provisions for doubtful debts never appear, because none of them changes the amount owed by an individual credit customer.

**What does an expense wrongly capitalised do to the financial statements?**
It overstates profit and overstates non-current assets by the same amount. To redraft, correct profit first, then each affected line, then check that net assets equal corrected capital.
