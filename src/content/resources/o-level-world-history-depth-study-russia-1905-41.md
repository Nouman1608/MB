**Depth Study C explains how Russia changed from a Tsarist autocracy in 1905 to Stalin's dictatorship by 1941.** Cambridge O Level History (2147) asks four questions: why the Tsarist regime collapsed in 1917, how the Bolsheviks gained and consolidated power, how Stalin gained and kept power, and what his economic policies did to the Soviet Union and its people. Every answer should link evidence to the question with words such as *because*, *so* and *this meant*.

## Where this fits in 2147

This is one of five Depth Studies, examined in **Paper 1, Section B** (two questions on each Depth Study; you answer one; (a) 4 marks, (b) 6, (c) 10). ## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE O LEVEL HISTORY (2147), 2024-2026 -- DEPTH STUDY C: RUSSIA, 1905-41**

- **1 Why did the Tsarist regime collapse in 1917?** Difficulties of ruling Russia to 1914; survival of 1905; weakening by the First World War; success of the March 1917 revolution.
- **2 How did the Bolsheviks gain power, and how did they consolidate their rule?** The Provisional Government; the November 1917 seizure of power; Civil War victory; the New Economic Policy.
- **3 How did Stalin gain and hold on to power?** Stalin versus Trotsky; the Purges; methods of control; the completeness of control by 1941.
- **4 What was the impact of Stalin's economic policies?** Why the Five-Year Plans and collectivisation were introduced; their success; their effects on the people.

Specified content includes Tsarist rule and society before 1914, the 1905 Revolution and reform attempts; the war's impact; the March Revolution; the Provisional Government and Soviets; the Bolshevik seizure and Lenin's role; War Communism and the Civil War; Kronstadt and the NEP; the struggle for power; terror, Purges, propaganda and official culture; industrial modernisation, the Five-Year Plans and collectivisation; and the differing experiences of social groups, ethnic minorities and women.

## Key question 1: Why did the Tsarist regime collapse?

**Problems of ruling Russia before 1914.** The empire stretched from Poland to the Pacific, with over a hundred nationalities and a mostly peasant population living in poverty. Nicholas II believed in autocracy and was reluctant to share power. The new industrial cities had overcrowded housing and long working hours, which fed support for revolutionary parties.

**Surviving 1905.** Defeat by Japan and the shooting of marchers on Bloody Sunday (January 1905) set off strikes, mutinies and peasant risings. Nicholas survived by **dividing his opponents** -- the October Manifesto's promise of a Duma satisfied many liberals -- and by using loyal troops against the remaining rebels once the war ended. He then limited the Duma's power, while Stolypin combined land reform with executions.

**The impact of war.** Military defeats, poor supplies and huge losses turned the army against the regime. By taking personal command in 1915 Nicholas made himself responsible for failure, and Rasputin's influence over the Tsarina damaged the royal family's reputation. Food and fuel shortages and inflation hit the cities.

**March 1917.** Strikes and food protests in Petrograd spread; crucially, soldiers refused to shoot the crowds and joined them. Without the army's loyalty the Tsar could not survive, and he abdicated. *Overall:* long-term weaknesses made the regime fragile, but the war turned weakness into collapse.

## Key question 2: How did the Bolsheviks gain and keep power?

**The Provisional Government's failures.** It shared power with the Petrograd Soviet, kept fighting the war, postponed land reform and elections, and lost the army's respect after the failed summer offensive. The Kornilov affair (September) forced Kerensky to rely on Bolshevik Red Guards.

**The November seizure.** Lenin's slogans ("Peace, Bread, Land") matched popular demands; the Bolsheviks won control of the Petrograd and Moscow Soviets; Trotsky's Military Revolutionary Committee took key buildings on 6-7 November 1917 with little bloodshed; the Provisional Government had almost no defenders.

**Consolidation and the Civil War.** The Bolsheviks closed the elected Constituent Assembly (January 1918) when they lacked a majority, set up the Cheka and made peace with Germany at **Brest-Litovsk** (March 1918). In the Civil War the Reds controlled the central cities and railways, and Trotsky built a disciplined **Red Army**; **War Communism** seized grain and nationalised industry to supply it. The Whites were divided, scattered around the edges of Russia and associated with the old landlords and foreign interference.

**The NEP.** By 1921 War Communism had caused famine and revolt, including the **Kronstadt** sailors' rising. Lenin's **New Economic Policy** allowed peasants to sell surplus grain and small businesses to trade privately. Production recovered, so it succeeded economically -- but many communists saw it as a betrayal.

## Key question 3: How did Stalin gain and keep power?

**Why Stalin, not Trotsky?** Stalin controlled party appointments as General Secretary; he made himself chief mourner at Lenin's funeral (January 1924), which Trotsky missed; Lenin's critical Testament was kept secret; he allied with others to defeat rivals one by one; and many party members found Trotsky arrogant and feared he might use the army. Trotsky was expelled from the party in 1927 and exiled from the USSR in 1929.

**The Purges.** After Kirov's murder (1934), Stalin's secret police arrested party members, army officers and ordinary citizens. **Show trials** forced leading Bolsheviks to confess to invented crimes; millions were sent to Gulag labour camps or shot. Reasons suggested include removing rivals, creating scapegoats for economic failures, and fear.

**Methods of control.** Terror, censorship, a **cult of personality** presenting Stalin as the wise "father" of the people, rewritten history and **socialist realism** in art and literature.

**How complete was control by 1941?** Open opposition had disappeared, but the purge of the army had weakened Soviet defence just before the German invasion of June 1941.

## Key question 4: What was the impact of Stalin's economic policies?

**Why the Five-Year Plans?** Stalin wanted to modernise the USSR quickly, warning in 1931 that the country was "fifty or a hundred years behind the advanced countries" and must catch up in ten years or be crushed. Industry also meant a larger working class and a stronger military.

**Why collectivisation?** To produce more grain to feed workers and sell abroad, to end private farming that communists distrusted, and to free labour for factories.

**How successful?** Heavy industry -- coal, steel, oil, electricity -- grew enormously, and new industrial cities were built east of the Urals. But quality was often poor, consumer goods were scarce, and targets were sometimes met only on paper.

**Effects on people.** Collectivisation led to the "liquidation" of the **kulaks**, mass deportations, the slaughter of livestock and the famine of 1932-33, in which millions died, especially in Ukraine. Industrial workers faced strict discipline and poor housing, though some gained skills and promotion; Stakhanovite "hero workers" were rewarded. Women joined the workforce in huge numbers while still doing domestic work. Ethnic minorities faced Russification, and some whole peoples were deported.

## Worked example: a (c) plan

*"Lenin's leadership was the main reason the Bolsheviks seized power in November 1917." How far do you agree?* **[10]**

- **Lenin:** April Theses, clear slogans, insistence on insurrection in October.
- **Other factors:** the Provisional Government's mistakes (the war, land); the Kornilov affair arming the Red Guards; Trotsky's organisation of the MRC; the soldiers' unwillingness to defend Kerensky.
- **Judgement:** Lenin decided *when* and *whether* to act; the government's failures explain *why* it worked.

## Common mistakes

- Giving the 1917 revolutions the wrong months: in 2147 they are March and November.
- Forgetting that Stalin's rise was complete by 1928-29, before the Purges.
- Describing collectivisation without its human cost, or industrialisation without its achievements.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which leader should I focus on most?**
Stalin covers two of the four key questions, but questions on 1917 are just as common.

**What was the NEP's main purpose?**
To revive the economy and end peasant unrest after War Communism.

**How do I judge "how complete" Stalin's control was?**
Compare the absence of open opposition with the hidden cost: purged experts, a weakened army and private discontent.
