These notes cover **Question 3b, the Short response task**, on Paper 1: "Candidates give a short response to show understanding of implicit meanings and attitude in response to Text B." The paper overview is in the [Paper 1 Reading revision notes](/resources/o-level-english-language-paper-1-reading-revision-notes/); the full method and a model answer are in the [Question 3b study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-english-language-1123-question-3b-short-response/).

## Task facts

| Fact | Detail |
|---|---|
| Section | Section B, Summary and Short response |
| Text | Text B, approximately 550–600 words, the same text as the summary |
| Marks | 5 |
| Objective | R2: demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes |
| Comes after | Question 3a, the 150-word summary (R1, R5, W2, W3) |
| Word limit | none stated in the syllabus |

## 3a versus 3b in one line each

- **3a** asks *what does the text say?* about a focus, in your own words.
- **3b** asks *what does the text suggest?* about an attitude or meaning, with proof.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Implicit meaning | what the reader infers from clues rather than reads directly |
| Attitude | a stance or feeling towards something: approving, critical, nostalgic, and so on |
| Connotation | the associations a word carries beyond its literal meaning |
| Tone | the voice in which something is said, such as wry, angry or affectionate |
| Hedging | words that signal doubt, such as "perhaps", "supposedly", "it seems" |
| Concession | granting a point before turning against it ("Admittedly..., but...") |
| Irony | saying the opposite of what is meant, to mock or criticise |
| Ambivalence | holding mixed or conflicting feelings at once |
| Shift | a change of attitude during the text, often marked by "but", "however", "yet" |

## Attitude signals checklist

- Connotation of key nouns and verbs
- Judging adjectives and adverbs
- What the writer dwells on and what they leave out
- Hedges and qualifiers
- Concessions and turns
- Irony and sarcasm
- Rhetorical questions
- Contrasts between past and present, or two places or people
- The final sentence or a one-line paragraph

## Precise attitude words

| Group | Words |
|---|---|
| Approving | admiring, grateful, affectionate, proud, enthusiastic, respectful |
| Disapproving | critical, scornful, resentful, dismissive, indignant, contemptuous |
| Doubtful | sceptical, wary, suspicious, unconvinced, cautious |
| Sad or longing | nostalgic, regretful, wistful, disappointed |
| Mixed | ambivalent, grudgingly impressed, cautiously optimistic, torn |
| Distanced | wry, ironic, amused, detached, neutral |

## Four-step method

1. **Target:** whose attitude, towards what?
2. **Clues:** two or three short phrases from Text B.
3. **Name and prove:** precise attitude word + brief quotation + how the wording shows it.
4. **Turn:** note any change or qualification.

## Sentence frames

- "The writer is [attitude] towards X, shown by the word '...', which suggests..."
- "At first the writer seems [attitude], but the phrase '...' reveals..."
- "Although the writer admits '...', their [attitude] comes through in..."
- "The ironic phrase '...' mocks..."

## Weak to strong

| Weak | Strong |
|---|---|
| "The writer is negative about the hub." | "The writer is initially scornful, dismissing it as 'another glass box'." |
| "The writer says the coffee is expensive and there is a puppet show." | "The complaint that the coffee 'is expensive' keeps a critical edge even as the writer warms to the puppet show." |
| "The writer likes it in the end." | "'Grudgingly impressed' shows admiration given reluctantly, so the attitude stays mixed." |

## Mini example (original)

> The new library opens at seven in the morning, which is wonderful for early risers and, I am told,
> for the three people in town who are early risers. The building itself is bright and generous, and
> I have already spent two happy afternoons there.

- **Attitude to the opening hours:** amused and slightly mocking; "I am told" and the joke about "three
  people" suggest the writer thinks the early start serves very few.
- **Attitude to the building:** warm approval; "bright and generous" and "two happy afternoons" show
  genuine pleasure.
- **Overall:** mixed but mainly positive, with gentle irony aimed at one decision rather than the whole
  library.

## Exam traps

- Summarising again after 3a.
- "Positive" or "negative" with no evidence.
- Quoting whole sentences.
- Giving your own opinion.
- Missing the turn in the writer's attitude.
- Answering about the wrong person.
- Leaving too little time after the summary.

## Self-test

1. Which objective does 3b test, and how is it worded?
2. How many marks is 3b worth?
3. Which text does 3b use, and roughly how long is it?
4. What is the main difference between 3a and 3b?
5. Define "attitude".
6. Give three ways a writer can show attitude without stating it.
7. Give a more precise word than "negative" for a writer who doubts a claim.
8. What is a concession, and why does it matter for attitude?
9. Why are short quotations better than long ones?
10. What does "ambivalent" mean?
11. What word often signals a shift in attitude?
12. Why is "The writer describes the cinema's chandelier" a weak 3b sentence?

**Answers**

1. R2: "demonstrate understanding of implicit meanings and attitudes".
2. 5 marks.
3. Text B, approximately 550–600 words.
4. 3a reports what the text states about a focus; 3b interprets what it suggests about attitude or meaning.
5. A stance or feeling towards something, such as approval, criticism or doubt.
6. Any three: connotation; evaluative modifiers; selection of detail; omission; hedging; concession; irony; contrast; placement.
7. Sceptical (or wary, unconvinced).
8. Granting a point before turning against it; it shows a balanced or reluctant attitude rather than a simple one.
9. They point to the exact word doing the work and leave room for explanation.
10. Holding mixed or conflicting feelings at once.
11. "But" (or "however", "yet").
12. It reports content without saying what the detail suggests about the writer's feelings.
