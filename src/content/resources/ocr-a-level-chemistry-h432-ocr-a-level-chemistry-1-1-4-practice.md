The questions here practise **1.1.4 Evaluation** in OCR A Level Chemistry A (H432), Version 3.1 (May 2026), for first assessment in 2017: evaluating results and conclusions (a), anomalies (b), limitations (c), precision, accuracy and uncertainty (d), and improvements (e). Section A has short questions; Section B has full evaluations.

Read the [1.1.4 study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-4/) and [1.1.4 revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-chemistry-h432-ocr-a-level-chemistry-1-1-4-revision-notes/) first. See also the [OCR chemistry hub](/boards/ocr/a-level/chemistry/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State the meaning of each term.

**(a)** Precise. **[1]**

**(b)** Systematic error. **[1]**

**2.** A mass of 0.250 g is found by difference on a balance with an uncertainty of ±0.005 g per reading. Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the mass. **[2]**

**3.** A titre of 9.80 cm³ is found from two burette readings, each ±0.05 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage uncertainty in the titre. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest one change to the method that would reduce this percentage uncertainty. **[1]**

**4.** Repeat times for a reaction are 18.2 s, 18.5 s, 21.9 s and 18.3 s. Identify the anomaly and calculate the mean time. **[2]**

**5.** A student's concentration is 0.104 ± 0.002 mol dm⁻³. The accepted value is 0.100 mol dm⁻³. Does the result agree with the accepted value within its margin of error? Explain what this suggests. **[2]**

**6.** An experimental Mr is 62.4. The accepted value is 58.1. Calculate the percentage error. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** A student burns ethanol in a spirit burner under a copper can containing 100 cm³ of water, measured with a measuring cylinder (±1 cm³). The temperature rises by 25.0 °C (thermometer ±0.5 °C per reading). The burner loses 0.92 g (balance ±0.005 g per reading). The student's value for the enthalpy change of combustion is −870 kJ mol⁻¹; the data book value is −1367 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the total percentage uncertainty from the three measurements. **[2]**

**(c)** Evaluate the result. Identify two limitations of the procedure and suggest an improvement for each. **[4]**

**8.** Initial rates are measured at five concentrations of A.

| [A] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.10 | 0.20 | 0.30 | 0.40 | 0.50 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Initial rate / 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ | 1.2 | 2.4 | 4.4 | 4.8 | 6.0 |

**(a)** Identify the anomalous result. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a cause for it that matches the direction of the error. **[1]**

**(c)** State what the student should do with this result. **[1]**

**9.** A student finds the Mr of a metal carbonate by adding it to excess hydrochloric acid and collecting the carbon dioxide over water in an inverted measuring cylinder. The student removes the bung, adds the carbonate and replaces the bung. The value obtained is 112; the accepted value is 100.1.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage error. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain two limitations of the method that would make the Mr too high. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest an improvement for each limitation. **[2]**

**10.** In a titration, a 10.0 cm³ pipette (±0.04 cm³) and a 100.0 cm³ volumetric flask (±0.2 cm³) are used, and the mean titre is 12.40 cm³ (burette ±0.05 cm³ per reading).

**(a)** Calculate the total percentage uncertainty. **[3]**

**(b)** Identify the largest source of uncertainty and suggest how to reduce it. **[2]**

**11.** A student heats hydrated magnesium sulfate, MgSO₄·xH₂O, strongly in an open crucible. The student's value of x is 7.6; the accepted value is 7. Some crackling was heard during heating.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage error. **[1]**

**(b)** Suggest a limitation that explains why x is too high, and explain how it has this effect. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest an improvement. **[1]**

**12.** Two students titrate the same solutions. The accepted titre is 23.55 cm³.

Student A: 24.10, 24.15, 24.10 cm³

Student B: 23.20, 23.85, 23.50 cm³

Compare the precision and accuracy of the two sets of results. **[3]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Repeated measurements are close to one another [1]

**(b)** An error that shifts every result in the same direction, by a similar amount, so repeating does not remove it [1]

Total: 2

**2.** Two readings, so ±0.010 g [1]; 0.010 ÷ 0.250 × 100 = **4.0%** [1]

Total: 2

**3. (a)** 0.10 ÷ 9.80 × 100 = **1.0%** [1]

**(b)** Use a more dilute titrant, or a larger pipetted volume, so that the titre is larger [1]

Total: 2

**4.** **21.9 s** is anomalous [1]; mean = (18.2 + 18.5 + 18.3) ÷ 3 = **18.3 s** [1]

Total: 2

**5.** **No**: the range is 0.102 to 0.106 mol dm⁻³, which does not include 0.100 [1]. The difference is larger than the apparatus uncertainty, which suggests a systematic error in the procedure [1]

Total: 2

**6.** (62.4 − 58.1) ÷ 58.1 × 100 = **7.4%** [1]

Total: 1

**7. (a)** (1367 − 870) ÷ 1367 × 100 = **36%** [1]

**(b)** water 1 ÷ 100 × 100 = 1.0%; ΔT 1.0 ÷ 25.0 × 100 = 4.0%; mass 0.010 ÷ 0.92 × 100 = 1.1% [1]; total = **6.1%** [1]

**(c)** The 36% error is far larger than the 6.1% uncertainty, so the difference is mainly systematic; the value is much less exothermic than the data book value [1]. Limitation 1: much of the heat goes to the air and the can instead of the water [1]; improvement: shield the flame from draughts and put the can closer to the flame, or use a calorimeter designed for combustion [1]. Limitation 2: incomplete combustion (soot on the can), so less energy is released; improvement: allow a good air supply to the flame [1]

Total: 7

*Examiner insight:* Check that each limitation would make ΔH less exothermic, matching the data. A limitation that would make it more exothermic cannot explain this result.

**8. (a)** **[A] = 0.30 mol dm⁻³** (4.4 × 10⁻⁴); the others fit rate = 1.2 × 10⁻³ × [A], which predicts 3.6 × 10⁻⁴ [1]

**(b)** The rate is too high, so for example the mixture was warmer than the others, or the solution of A was made up more concentrated than intended [1]

**(c)** Leave it out of the best-fit line (circle it on the graph) and repeat the measurement [1]

Total: 3

**9. (a)** (112 − 100.1) ÷ 100.1 × 100 = **11.9%** [1]

**(b)** Carbon dioxide escapes while the bung is being replaced, so the volume collected is too low [1]. Some carbon dioxide dissolves in the water. A lower volume gives too few moles of CO₂, and Mr = mass ÷ moles is then too high [1]

**(c)** Keep the carbonate separate from the acid until the flask is sealed, for example in a small tube inside the flask that is tipped over afterwards [1]. Collect the gas in a gas syringe instead of over water [1]

Total: 5

**10. (a)** pipette 0.04 ÷ 10.0 × 100 = 0.40%; flask 0.2 ÷ 100.0 × 100 = 0.20% [1]; titre 0.10 ÷ 12.40 × 100 = 0.81% [1]; total = **1.4%** [1]

**(b)** The **titre** (0.81%) [1]. Use a larger pipetted volume (for example 25.0 cm³) or a more dilute titrant, so the titre is larger and its percentage uncertainty smaller [1]

Total: 5

**11. (a)** (7.6 − 7) ÷ 7 × 100 = **8.6%** [1]

**(b)** Strong heating made the solid spit, so some solid was lost from the open crucible [1]. The extra loss in mass is counted as water, so the moles of water are too high and the residue too low, making x too large [1]

**(c)** Heat gently with a lid partly on the crucible, then heat to constant mass [1]

Total: 4

**12.** Student A's results are more **precise**: they agree within 0.05 cm³ [1]. They are not accurate: the mean, 24.12 cm³, is about 0.57 cm³ too high, suggesting a systematic error such as overshooting each end point by the same amount [1]. Student B's results are less precise (range 0.65 cm³) but more **accurate**: the mean, 23.52 cm³, is close to 23.55 cm³ [1]

Total: 3

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing precise with accurate.
- Using a single reading's uncertainty for a quantity found by difference.
- Giving a limitation that would push the result the opposite way.
- Improvements that do not fix the stated limitation, or that just say "repeat".
- Leaving an anomaly in the mean or the best-fit line.
- Stating that a result "agrees" without comparing it with the margin of error.
