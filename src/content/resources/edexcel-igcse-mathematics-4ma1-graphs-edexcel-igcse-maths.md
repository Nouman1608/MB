## Real-life graphs (both tiers)

On a **distance–time** graph, the gradient is the speed, and a horizontal section means the object is stationary. On a **speed–time** graph, the gradient is the acceleration (negative for deceleration), and the area under the graph is the distance travelled.

**Worked example 1.** A car starts from rest and accelerates steadily to 20 m/s in 8 seconds. It travels at 20 m/s for 12 seconds, then decelerates steadily to rest in 5 seconds. Find (a) the acceleration (b) the total distance.

```
(a) acceleration = gradient = 20 ÷ 8 = 2.5 m/s²

(b) area = triangle + rectangle + triangle
         = ½ × 8 × 20 + 12 × 20 + ½ × 5 × 20
         = 80 + 240 + 50 = 370 m
```

Check that the axis units match before multiplying (minutes with km/h need converting).

A **conversion graph** is a straight line through the origin (0 in one unit is 0 in the other). Its gradient is the conversion rate.

## Coordinates and midpoints (both tiers)

Coordinates are written (x, y): across, then up. The midpoint of the segment from (x₁, y₁) to (x₂, y₂) is the mean of the coordinates:

```
midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2)
A(−3, 5), B(7, −1):  midpoint = (4/2, 4/2) = (2, 2)
```

Geometrical information can fix a point. In a parallelogram ABCD, the move from B to C is the same as the move from A to D. With A(1, 2), B(5, 3) and C(7, 7), the move B to C is 2 across and 4 up, so D = (1 + 2, 2 + 4) = **(3, 6)**.

## Gradient and y = mx + c (both tiers)

```
gradient = increase in y ÷ increase in x
A(−3, 5) to B(7, −1):  (−1 − 5) ÷ (7 − (−3)) = −6 ÷ 10 = −0.6
```

A line sloping down from left to right has a negative gradient.

In y = mx + c, m is the gradient and (0, c) is where the line crosses the y-axis. The equation must be rearranged into this form first.

**Worked example 2.** Find the gradient and y-intercept of 2y − 3x = 8.

```
2y = 3x + 8
y = 1.5x + 4
gradient 1.5, y-intercept (0, 4)
```

### Plotting lines of the form ax + by = c

The quickest method is the two intercepts. For 3x + 4y = 12, put x = 0 to get y = 3, and y = 0 to get x = 4. Plot (0, 3) and (4, 0) and join them, then check with a third point, such as x = 2, y = 1.5.

Special lines: x = k is vertical, y = c is horizontal, and y = x (or y − x = 0) passes through the origin at 45° on equal scales.

### Plotting a quadratic

Complete a table of values, plot every point, and join them with a **smooth curve**, not straight segments.

**Worked example 3.** Draw y = x² − 2x − 3 for −2 ≤ x ≤ 4.

| x | −2 | −1 | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| y | 5 | 0 | −3 | −4 | −3 | 0 | 5 |

The table is symmetrical about x = 1, the line of symmetry, and the minimum point is (1, −4). The curve crosses the x-axis at x = −1 and x = 3, which are the solutions of x² − 2x − 3 = 0.

## Higher tier only

### Recognising curves

| Type | Example | Shape |
|---|---|---|
| Cubic, positive x³ coefficient | y = x³ − 3x | rises from bottom left to top right; can have two turning points |
| Cubic, negative x³ coefficient | y = 5 − x³ | falls from top left to bottom right |
| Reciprocal | y = 2/x | two separate branches; never meets either axis |
| x⁻² term | y = 1/x² | both branches above the x-axis |
| y = sin x | period 360° | between −1 and 1; passes through (0, 0) and (180°, 0) |
| y = cos x | period 360° | between −1 and 1; starts at (0, 1) |
| y = tan x | period 180° | no value at 90°, 270°, ...; passes through (0, 0) |

### Transformations

| New graph | Effect on y = f(x) | Point (p, q) moves to |
|---|---|---|
| y = f(x) + a | translation a units up | (p, q + a) |
| y = f(x + a) | translation a units left | (p − a, q) |
| y = af(x) | vertical stretch, scale factor a | (p, aq) |
| y = f(ax) | horizontal stretch, scale factor 1/a | (p/a, q) |

Changes inside the bracket act on x and work "the opposite way": f(x + 2) moves the graph 2 to the left.

**Worked example 4 (Higher).** f(x) = x² − 4, with minimum point (0, −4) and roots at x = −2 and x = 2. Describe y = f(x − 3) and y = f(2x).

```
f(x − 3): translation 3 to the right; minimum (3, −4); roots x = 1 and x = 5
f(2x):    horizontal stretch, factor 1/2; minimum (0, −4); roots x = −1 and x = 1
```

### Gradient of a curve

Draw a tangent touching the curve at the point, extend it, choose two points on the tangent far apart, and find the gradient of the tangent. On a distance–time curve, this gives the speed at that instant.

### Intersections and equations

The x-coordinates where y = (curve) meets y = (line) are the solutions of curve − line = 0.

**Worked example 5 (Higher).** The graph of y = x² − 5x + 2 has been drawn. Find the equation of the line that must be drawn to solve x² − 7x + 6 = 0.

```
x² − 5x + 2 = ?          we want x² − 7x + 6 = 0
x² − 5x + 2 = 2x − 4     (subtract 2x, add 4 to both sides of x² − 7x + 6 = 0)
line: y = 2x − 4
```

Check: x² − 5x + 2 − (2x − 4) = x² − 7x + 6. The line meets the curve where x = 1 and x = 6.

### Lines through two points, parallel and perpendicular

Parallel lines have equal gradients. Perpendicular gradients multiply to −1, so the perpendicular gradient is the negative reciprocal.

**Worked example 6 (Higher).** (a) Find the equation of the line L through (2, −1) and (6, 7). (b) Find the line perpendicular to L through (4, 1).

```
(a) m = (7 − (−1)) ÷ (6 − 2) = 8 ÷ 4 = 2
    y = 2x + c;  −1 = 4 + c;  c = −5      y = 2x − 5

(b) perpendicular gradient = −1/2
    1 = −1/2 × 4 + c;  c = 3              y = −½x + 3
```

## Common mistakes

- Reading the gradient of 2y − 3x = 8 as 3 or −3 without making y the subject.
- Joining quadratic points with a ruler, or giving a pointed minimum.
- Mixing units on a speed–time graph, or using the gradient when the question needs the area.
- Moving f(x + 2) to the right instead of the left.
- Using −m (not −1/m) for a perpendicular gradient.

## Exam technique

- **"Draw the graph"**: plot accurately, join with a smooth curve or ruled line as appropriate, and label it.
- **"Use your graph to find"** or **"estimate"**: show the reading lines on the graph so your method is visible.
- **"Find the equation of the line"**: give it in the form asked (y = mx + c or ax + by = c).
- **"Describe the transformation"**: name it (translation or stretch) and give the vector, direction or scale factor.
