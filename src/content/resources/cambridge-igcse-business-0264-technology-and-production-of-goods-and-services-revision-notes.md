All examples are invented. Money is in dollars.

## Syllabus points

- How technology is changing production methods through automation and mechanisation, e.g. CAM and 3D printing.
- How technology is improving productivity in the service sector, e.g. contactless payments.
- Advantages and disadvantages of changes in technology for businesses and employees.

## Production methods

| Method | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Mechanisation | Machines do the work, but a person operates each one | Worker using a powered sewing machine |
| Automation | Machines or computer systems carry out tasks with little or no human input once set up | Robot arms welding car bodies |
| CAM (Computer-Aided Manufacture) | Computers control the machines that make the product | Computer-controlled cutter shaping furniture parts |
| 3D printing | Builds an object layer by layer from a digital design | Printing a replacement part or a prototype |

Key differences:

- Mechanisation still needs an operator for each machine; automation does not.
- CAM suits making many identical, precise parts.
- 3D printing suits one-off items, prototypes and small runs, because no mould or special tool is needed for each design.

## Productivity

**Labour productivity = output per period / number of employees** (given in the syllabus).

Worked reminder: a printing works makes 9,000 leaflets a day with 6 workers.

```
9,000 / 6 = 1,500 leaflets per worker per day
```

After new presses, 5 workers make 12,000 a day.

```
12,000 / 5 = 2,400 leaflets per worker per day
Rise = (2,400 - 1,500) / 1,500 x 100 = 60%
```

Higher productivity lowers labour cost per unit, which can allow lower prices or higher profit.

## Technology in services

| Technology | Productivity gain |
|---|---|
| Contactless payments | Faster payments; shorter queues; more customers per till |
| Online booking | Customers book themselves; fewer phone calls for staff |
| Self-service checkouts | One assistant oversees several tills |
| Ordering apps and table tablets | Staff spend less time taking orders |
| Online banking | Customers serve themselves without visiting a branch |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| **Business** | Higher productivity; lower labour cost per unit; more consistent quality; faster service; can meet higher demand | High purchase and installation cost; maintenance; breakdowns can stop output; training costs; needs skilled technicians; cyber-security risks in services |
| **Employees** | Less dangerous, heavy or repetitive work; chances to learn new skills; some better-paid technical jobs | Job losses or shorter hours; need to retrain; worry and lower motivation during change; old skills less valued |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Technology | The use of machines, equipment and computer systems in production |
| Mechanisation | Using machines, operated by people, to do work once done by hand |
| Automation | Using machines or computer systems that work with little or no human input |
| CAM | Computer-Aided Manufacture: computers controlling production machines |
| 3D printing | Making an object layer by layer from a digital design |
| Labour productivity | Output per employee in a period |
| Contactless payment | Paying by tapping a card or phone on a reader |

## Exam traps

- Mechanisation and automation are different: check whether a person still operates the machine.
- 3D printing is not a mass-production method for millions of identical items.
- Service questions need service examples, such as payments, booking or checkouts.
- Keep business effects and employee effects apart; the question usually names one group.
- "Saves money" is weak. Say which cost falls (wages per unit) and which new cost appears (machines, maintenance, training).
- Give productivity units: "units per worker per week".

## Quick self-test

1. Define automation.
2. What is the difference between mechanisation and automation?
3. What does CAM stand for?
4. Give one reason why 3D printing suits making prototypes.
5. State the formula for labour productivity.
6. A bakery makes 4,800 loaves a week with 8 workers. Calculate labour productivity.
7. After new ovens, 8 workers make 6,000 loaves. Calculate the percentage increase in labour productivity.
8. Give one example of technology improving productivity in the service sector, other than contactless payments.
9. State two advantages to a business of introducing new technology.
10. State two disadvantages to employees of a business introducing automation.
11. Give one possible advantage to employees of new technology.
12. Why might a breakdown be a bigger problem in an automated factory than a mechanised one?

### Answers

1. Using machines or computer-controlled systems that carry out tasks with little or no human input once set up.
2. In mechanisation a person operates each machine; in automation the machines run with little or no human input.
3. Computer-Aided Manufacture.
4. Any one: no mould or special tool needed, so a single item is cheap to make; a design can be changed and reprinted quickly.
5. Labour productivity = output per period / number of employees.
6. 4,800 / 8 = 600 loaves per worker per week.
7. New productivity = 6,000 / 8 = 750. Increase = (750 - 600) / 600 x 100 = 25%.
8. Any one: online booking; self-service checkouts; ordering apps or table tablets; online banking.
9. Any two: higher productivity; lower labour cost per unit; more consistent quality; faster production or service; ability to meet higher demand.
10. Any two: job losses; shorter hours; need to retrain; worry and lower motivation; skills no longer valued.
11. Any one: less dangerous or repetitive work; chance to learn new skills; possible higher pay in technical roles.
12. Fewer workers are employed and the line depends on the machines, so there is no manual way to keep production going while repairs are made.
