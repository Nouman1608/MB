Condensed notes on section **3.4.3 Making operational decisions to improve performance: increasing efficiency and productivity** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [efficiency and productivity study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-increasing-efficiency-and-productivity/); exam-style questions are in the [efficiency and productivity practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-increasing-efficiency-and-productivity-practice/).

## Specification content

- The importance of capacity, including how to utilise capacity efficiently.
- The importance of efficiency and labour productivity: how to increase them and difficulties in doing so.
- The benefits and difficulties of lean production; Just in Time against Just in Case.
- How to choose the optimal mix of resources: labour- and capital-intensive processes.
- How to use technology to improve operational efficiency.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Capacity | Maximum output in a period |
| Efficiency | Using resources with minimum waste for a given output |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker |
| Lean production | Producing with minimum waste of inventory, time, space and effort |
| Kaizen | Continuous improvement through small changes suggested by all staff |
| Just in Time (JIT) | Materials arrive only when needed; goods made to order |
| Just in Case (JIC) | Holding buffer inventory in case of problems |
| Labour-intensive | Uses a high proportion of labour compared with capital |
| Capital-intensive | Uses mainly machinery and equipment |
| Rationalisation | Reducing capacity, for example by closing a site or line |

## Using capacity efficiently

| Situation | Responses |
|---|---|
| Spare capacity | New customers or markets; subcontract work; hire out space or machines; promotions in quiet periods; rationalise if permanent |
| Demand above capacity | Overtime or extra shifts; temporary staff; outsource; better scheduling; invest if demand is lasting |
| Uneven demand | Flexible contracts; multi-skilled staff; build stock in quiet periods; off-peak pricing |

Rationalisation saves costs but may mean redundancies, lost skills and bad publicity.

## Raising efficiency and labour productivity

**Methods:** training; motivation; better equipment and technology; better layout and scheduling; cutting waste; clear targets.

**Difficulties:** cost of investment; resistance to change; time lag; quality may fall if speed is pushed; some services cannot be speeded up without harm; skilled labour hard to find; redundancy costs.

## Lean production

- Aims: minimal waste, continuous improvement (kaizen), quality built in, close supplier links.
- Benefits: lower costs, higher quality, quicker response, less waste, engaged staff.
- Difficulties: needs reliable suppliers and forecasts; staff training and culture change; little slack for shocks.

| | JIT | JIC |
|---|---|---|
| For | Low holding costs; less space; less obsolescence; cash released | Protects against delays and demand surges; bulk discounts |
| Against | Supplier delays stop production; frequent deliveries cost more; no bulk discounts | Storage, insurance and finance costs; damage, theft, obsolescence; cash tied up |

**Holding cost example:** average inventory £120,000 at 15% = £18,000 a year; cut to £30,000 gives £4,500; saving **£13,500**.

## Mix of resources

| | Labour-intensive | Capital-intensive |
|---|---|---|
| Costs | Low fixed, high variable | High fixed, low variable |
| Best for | Low volume, bespoke, uncertain demand | High volume, standard, steady demand |
| Flexibility | High | Lower |
| Risk | Wage rises, absence | Breakdown, debt |

**Worked example.** Process A: fixed £10,000, variable £10 per unit. Process B: fixed £42,000, variable £2 per unit.

- At 6,000 units: A = £70,000 (£11.67 each); B = £54,000 (£9.00 each).
- Equal where 10,000 + 10x = 42,000 + 2x, so **x = 4,000 units**. Below it A is cheaper; above it B is cheaper.

## Technology

Automation and robotics; CAD/CAM; inventory and supply chain software; sensors for predictive maintenance; online ordering; booking apps and self-service. **Costs:** set-up cost, new skills, risk of breakdown or cyber attack, job-loss fears, loss of flexibility or personal service.

## Exam traps

- JIT depends on **reliable suppliers** and good forecasts.
- Capital-intensive is not always cheaper: it depends on output.
- Raising productivity costs money and takes time.
- Efficiency is wider than labour productivity.
- Mention job losses and staff reaction when recommending automation.

## Self-test

1. Give two ways to use spare capacity.
2. Give two ways to cope when demand exceeds capacity.
3. Give two ways to raise labour productivity.
4. Give two difficulties of raising labour productivity.
5. State two benefits of JIT.
6. State two risks of JIT.
7. A firm's average inventory is £80,000 and holding costs are 20% of value. Calculate the annual holding cost.
8. The firm cuts average inventory to £20,000. Calculate the annual saving.
9. Define a capital-intensive process.
10. Process X has fixed costs of £5,000 and variable cost of £4 a unit. Process Y has fixed costs of £11,000 and variable cost of £2 a unit. At what output are total costs equal?
11. Which process is cheaper at 5,000 units, and by how much?
12. Give two costs or difficulties of introducing automation.

### Answers

1. Any two: find new customers; subcontract work; hire out space or machines; run promotions.
2. Any two: overtime; extra shifts; temporary staff; outsourcing; better scheduling.
3. Any two: training; motivation; better equipment; better layout; cutting waste.
4. Any two: cost; resistance to change; time lag; quality risk; skilled labour shortage.
5. Any two: lower holding costs; less storage space; less obsolescence; cash released.
6. Any two: supplier delays stop production; frequent deliveries cost more; no bulk discounts; relies on forecasts.
7. £80,000 × 0.20 = **£16,000**.
8. £20,000 × 0.20 = £4,000; saving = £16,000 − £4,000 = **£12,000**.
9. A process that uses mainly machinery and equipment rather than labour.
10. 5,000 + 4x = 11,000 + 2x, so 2x = 6,000 and x = **3,000 units**.
11. X: 5,000 + 4 × 5,000 = £25,000. Y: 11,000 + 2 × 5,000 = £21,000. Y is cheaper by **£4,000**.
12. Any two: high set-up cost; need for new skills; risk of breakdown; staff resistance; loss of flexibility.
