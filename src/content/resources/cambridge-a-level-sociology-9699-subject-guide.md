Cambridge International AS & A Level Sociology (9699) studies how
societies shape the people within them and how people, in turn, shape
societies. The course moves from the foundations of socialisation,
identity and research methods, through the family, to education and
the broad themes of globalisation, media and religion. It is assessed
by four written papers. The two AS papers are each 90 minutes and 60
marks; at A Level, Paper 3 is 75 minutes and 50 marks, and Paper 4 is
105 minutes and 70 marks. This overview follows the 2024-2026 syllabus
edition, whose final examination year is 2026, so students sitting
later should check the syllabus for their own year.

## How the course is organised

Each paper has its own content, which makes the structure easy to
follow.

### AS Level

**Paper 1 – Socialisation, Identity and Methods of Research** combines
two foundations of the subject. The first half looks at the process of
learning and socialisation; social control, conformity and resistance;
and social identity and change. The second half deals with research:
types of data, methods and research design; approaches to sociological
research; and research issues.

**Paper 2 – The Family** covers perspectives on the role of the family,
diversity and social change, gender equality and experiences of family
life, and age and family life.

### A Level

**Paper 3 – Education** examines theories about the role of education,
education and social mobility, influences on the curriculum, and
intelligence and educational attainment, followed by the effects of
social class, ethnicity and gender on attainment.

**Paper 4 – Globalisation, Media and Religion** is the widest-ranging
paper, with three strands:

- *Globalisation*: perspectives on globalisation, and its relationship
  with identity, power and politics, poverty and inequalities,
  migration, and crime.
- *Media*: traditional and new media, theories of the media and
  influences on content, the impact of the new media, media
  representations of class, gender, ethnicity and age groups, models of
  media effects, and the impact of the media on behaviour.
- *Religion*: religion and society, religion and social order, religion
  as a source of social change, the secularisation debate, gender,
  feminism and religion, and religion and post-modernity.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Socialisation, Identity and Methods of Research | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 2 | The Family | 90 minutes | 60 | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Education | 75 minutes | 50 | 21% |
| Paper 4 | Globalisation, Media and Religion | 105 minutes | 70 | 29% |

Papers 1 and 2 form the AS Level, each worth 50% of the AS. Papers 3 and
4 complete the full A Level. The uneven A Level split between Papers 3
and 4 is deliberate: the current version of the syllabus moved Paper 3
from 20% to 21% and Paper 4 from 30% to 29%. All four papers are
externally assessed.

The qualification is staged. You can take AS Level as a standalone
qualification, take AS first and carry the result forward to complete
the A Level later, or take all four papers in the same series. The
2024-2026 edition is examined in the June and November series, and in
March in India.

## How the parts fit together

Paper 1 is the backbone of the whole course. Its two halves supply the
tools that every later paper uses:

- **Socialisation, social control and identity** give you the concepts
  for analysing family roles in Paper 2, schooling in Paper 3, and
  media influence, religious belief and global identity in Paper 4.
- **Research methods and research issues** help you evaluate the
  evidence behind every claim in the course, from studies of family
  diversity to research on educational attainment.

Sociological perspectives also recur across the papers. Debates about
the role of the family, the role of education and the role of religion
in social order all draw on contrasting theoretical views, so
understanding those perspectives once, and then applying them to each
new institution, saves time and produces stronger evaluation.

Inequality is the other connecting thread. Gender appears in family
life, in educational attainment and in feminism and religion; class and
ethnicity appear in education and in media representation; and
globalisation links inequality within societies to inequality between
them.

## Planning your study

In the AS year, a sensible order is to begin with socialisation and
identity, then move to research methods before starting the family, so
that you can evaluate family research as you go. Keep a running set of
key concepts and perspectives from Paper 1 that you can apply later.

In the A Level year, many students find it effective to complete
education first, since it is a single, contained topic, and then work
through the three strands of Paper 4 one at a time. Because Paper 4
carries the largest share of the A Level and covers the most ground,
leave enough time for each of globalisation, media and religion.

Start short-answer and paragraph practice as each section is taught,
and add longer, essay-style questions once a whole paper's content is
complete. The paper-specific approach and timing advice belong to the
separate exam preparation guide.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four papers in Cambridge A Level Sociology 9699?**
Paper 1 Socialisation, Identity and Methods of Research; Paper 2 The Family; Paper 3 Education; and Paper 4 Globalisation, Media and Religion.

**Which papers make up AS Level Sociology?**
Papers 1 and 2, each 90 minutes and 60 marks. Each is worth 50% of the AS Level and 25% of the full A Level.

**Why are Papers 3 and 4 weighted 21% and 29%?**
The split is intentional. The current version of the syllabus moved Paper 3 from 20% to 21% and Paper 4 from 30% to 29%.

**How long is Paper 4?**
Paper 4, Globalisation, Media and Religion, lasts 105 minutes and carries 70 marks.

**Can I take AS Level Sociology on its own?**
Yes. AS Level can be a standalone qualification, the first stage of a staged A Level, or taken in the same series as Papers 3 and 4.
