**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **A Level** subtopic
34.2: preparation of phenylamine, its reactions with Br₂(aq) and HNO₂, relative basicities of
"aqueous ammonia, ethylamine and phenylamine", and azo compounds.

## Preparation: three stages

| Stage | Reagents and conditions | Product |
|---|---|---|
| 1 nitration | conc. HNO₃ + conc. H₂SO₄, 25–60 °C | nitrobenzene, C₆H₅NO₂ |
| 2 reduction | Sn + conc. HCl, heat (reflux) | phenylammonium ion, C₆H₅NH₃⁺ |
| 3 liberation | NaOH(aq) | phenylamine, C₆H₅NH₂ |

    C₆H₆ + HNO₃ → C₆H₅NO₂ + H₂O
    C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O
    C₆H₅NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ → C₆H₅NH₂ + H₂O

- NaOH(aq) is needed because the acid in stage 2 protonates the amine.
- 6[H] per nitro group: two O atoms removed as 2H₂O, and two H added to N.

## Reactions of phenylamine

| Reagent and conditions | Product | Observation |
|---|---|---|
| Br₂(aq), room temperature, no catalyst | 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine | orange colour removed; white precipitate |
| NaNO₂ + dilute HCl (HNO₂), below 10 °C | benzenediazonium chloride | colourless solution, no gas while cold |
| then warm with water | phenol | bubbles of N₂ |

    C₆H₅NH₂ + 3Br₂ → C₆H₂Br₃NH₂ + 3HBr
    NaNO₂ + HCl → HNO₂ + NaCl
    C₆H₅NH₂ + HNO₂ + HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + 2H₂O
    C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + HCl

- **Why bromination is easy:** N lone pair delocalised into the ring → higher electron density →
  ring activated; –NH₂ directs to 2, 4, 6.
- **Why below 10 °C:** the diazonium salt decomposes (giving N₂ and phenol) if warmed.

## Basicity order

**ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine**

| Base | Effect on N lone pair | Availability | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| ethylamine | ethyl group donates electron density (positive inductive effect) | increased | strongest |
| ammonia | no donating or delocalising group | reference | intermediate |
| phenylamine | lone pair partly delocalised into the ring π system | decreased | weakest |

## Azo compounds

- **Coupling:** benzenediazonium chloride + phenol in **NaOH(aq)**, cold.

      C₆H₅N₂⁺ + C₆H₅O⁻ → C₆H₅N=NC₆H₄OH

- Product: 4-hydroxyazobenzene, **yellow-orange**.
- **Azo group:** **–N=N–** between two aromatic rings.
- **Electrophile:** the diazonium ion (weak), so it needs the strongly activated phenoxide ring.
- **Position:** 4-position of the phenol ring.
- **Colour:** extended delocalised system across both rings and N=N absorbs **visible** light.
- **Use:** dyes.
- **Other azo dyes:** change the aromatic amine and/or the coupling partner; same two steps.

## Working backwards from an azo dye

1. Find the **–N=N–** link and split there.
2. The ring that lost an –NH₂: add **–NH₂** back to get the **amine** that was diazotised.
3. The ring with –OH (or another activating group): that is the **coupling partner**, such as phenol.

Example: HOC₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄NO₂ (nitro group at the 4-position of the second ring) came from **4-nitrophenylamine**
(diazotised) and **phenol**.

## Phenylamine and phenol side by side

The same lone-pair delocalisation explains both compounds, so learn them as a pair.

| Property | Phenylamine, C₆H₅NH₂ | Phenol, C₆H₅OH |
|---|---|---|
| Lone pair delocalised from | N into the ring | O into the ring |
| Effect on the ring | activated, 2,4,6-directing | activated, 2,4,6-directing |
| With Br₂(aq) | 2,4,6-tribromophenylamine, white precipitate | 2,4,6-tribromophenol, white precipitate |
| Effect on the functional group | weaker base than ammonia | stronger acid than water or ethanol |
| Role in making an azo dye | diazotised to give the electrophile | coupling partner, as phenoxide in NaOH(aq) |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| phenylamine | C₆H₅NH₂, an aromatic primary amine |
| phenylammonium ion | C₆H₅NH₃⁺, formed in acid |
| diazotisation | conversion of an aromatic amine to a diazonium salt with HNO₂ below 10 °C |
| diazonium ion | Ar–N≡N⁺ |
| coupling | electrophilic substitution of a diazonium ion onto an activated ring |
| azo group | –N=N– |
| activated ring | ring with increased electron density, reacts faster with electrophiles |

## Exam traps

- Reduction uses **concentrated** HCl and tin, **heated**, then **NaOH(aq)**.
- No catalyst for Br₂(aq); three Br atoms; **3HBr**.
- Diazotisation **below 10 °C**; making phenol needs **warming**.
- Diazonium ion has **N≡N⁺**; azo dye has **N=N**.
- Coupling is in **alkaline** solution, at the **4-position**.
- Basicity: give a reason for **each** of the three bases.
- Nitrous acid is made **in situ** from NaNO₂ and dilute acid; do not list "HNO₃".

## Self-test

1. Give the reagents and conditions to convert nitrobenzene to phenylamine.
2. Why is NaOH(aq) added after the reduction?
3. Write the equation for the reduction using [H].
4. Describe what is seen when bromine water is added to phenylamine.
5. Explain why no catalyst is needed for this bromination.
6. Write the equation for the formation of nitrous acid.
7. Why must diazotisation be kept below 10 °C?
8. Write the equation for making phenol from benzenediazonium chloride.
9. Place ammonia, phenylamine and ethylamine in order of decreasing base strength.
10. Explain why phenylamine is a weaker base than ammonia.
11. Draw (describe) the azo group and explain why azo compounds are coloured.
12. Identify the amine and coupling partner used to make CH₃C₆H₄–N=N–C₆H₄OH.

**Answers:**

1. Tin and concentrated HCl, heated under reflux; then NaOH(aq).
2. The acid protonates the amine to C₆H₅NH₃⁺; NaOH(aq) removes H⁺ to release C₆H₅NH₂.
3. C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ + 2H₂O.
4. Orange bromine water is decolourised and a white precipitate forms.
5. The N lone pair is delocalised into the ring, increasing its electron density, so the ring can
   polarise Br₂ itself.
6. NaNO₂ + HCl → HNO₂ + NaCl.
7. The diazonium salt decomposes at higher temperatures.
8. C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + H₂O → C₆H₅OH + N₂ + HCl.
9. Ethylamine > ammonia > phenylamine.
10. Its N lone pair is partly delocalised into the benzene ring, so it is less available to accept
    H⁺.
11. –N=N– joining two aromatic rings; it extends the delocalised system, which absorbs visible
    light.
12. Methylphenylamine (CH₃C₆H₄NH₂), diazotised; phenol in NaOH(aq).
