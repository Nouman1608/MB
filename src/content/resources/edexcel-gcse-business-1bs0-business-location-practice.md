## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following businesses is most likely to locate close to its materials? **[1]**

A: A hair salon

B: A firm crushing rock into gravel

C: An online clothing retailer

D: A dental practice

**2.** Define the term e-commerce. **[1]**

**3.** State **one** reason why a business might choose a location close to its competitors. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** reason why proximity to labour may be important to a business. **[3]**

**5.** Explain **one** way the internet can reduce a start-up's location costs. **[3]**

**6.** Quayside Kayaks pays rent of £21,600 a year. Its sales revenue is £144,000.

**(a)** Calculate rent as a percentage of sales revenue. **[2]**

**(b)** The landlord raises the rent by 12.5%. Calculate the new annual rent. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Jas plans to open Spice Route, a takeaway selling curries for collection and delivery. Outline **one** reason why proximity to the market is important for Spice Route. **[2]**

**8.** Hawthorn Prints sells art prints only through its website. It packs orders at home. Orders have trebled, and the owner is looking for premises.

**(a)** State **one** reason why Hawthorn Prints now needs fixed premises. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline **one** factor the owner should consider when choosing the premises. **[2]**

**9.** Fenwick Bridal is choosing between two sites for a wedding-dress shop.

| | Site A: town centre, next to two other bridal shops | Site B: edge of town, no nearby bridal shops |
|---|---|---|
| Annual rent | £28,000 | £16,000 |
| Forecast dresses sold a year | 160 | 110 |
| Average price per dress | £1,100 | £1,100 |

**(a)** Calculate the forecast revenue at each site. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference between the two sites' forecast revenue, minus the difference in rent. **[2]**

**10.** Analyse the impact on Fenwick Bridal of choosing Site A, near two competitors. **[6]**

**11.** Explain **one** reason why a plumbing business does not need a high-street location. **[3]**

**12.** Rosa runs Tidy Threads, which alters and repairs clothes. She currently rents a small shop for £9,000 a year and has 1,200 customers a year who pay an average of £25. She is considering two options.

Option 1: close the shop and run a collection-and-return service from home, advertised online. Forecast customers fall to 1,000 a year, but rent is saved and £1,800 a year is spent on delivery and online advertising.

Option 2: keep the shop.

Justify which option Rosa should choose. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Buying and selling goods or services using the internet [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Any one from: customers come to the area to compare products, so some will visit the business; competitors have shown there is demand in the area [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A business needs workers with the right skills to produce its goods or services [1]. If it locates where suitable workers live, recruitment is quicker and cheaper [1]. If skilled labour is scarce, the business may have to pay higher wages to attract staff, which raises costs [1]. **[3]**

**5.** An online business does not need customers to visit its premises [1], so it can avoid expensive town-centre sites with high footfall [1]. It can operate from home or a cheaper unit, which lowers its fixed costs while it is building up sales [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** £21,600 ÷ £144,000 × 100 [1] = **15%** [1]

**(b)** Increase = 12.5% × £21,600 = £2,700 [1]. New rent = £21,600 + £2,700 = **£24,300** [1] **[4]**

**7.** Hot food must reach customers quickly, so delivery time and cost rise if Spice Route is far from them [1]; being near a large number of households means more collection orders and quicker deliveries, which should increase sales [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Any one from: there is not enough space at home to store stock and pack trebled orders; it needs more room for staff or equipment [1].

**(b)** Good transport links [1], because Hawthorn Prints must send many parcels each day and quick access to delivery services keeps dispatch fast and costs down [1]. (Alternatively: low rent, because customers never visit so a busy site is unnecessary.) **[3]**

**9. (a)** Site A: 160 × £1,100 = **£176,000** [1]. Site B: 110 × £1,100 = **£121,000** [1]

**(b)** Revenue difference = £176,000 − £121,000 = £55,000 [1]. Minus rent difference of £12,000 (£28,000 − £16,000) = **£43,000** in favour of Site A [1] **[4]**

**10.** Being next to two other bridal shops means brides who come to compare dresses will pass Fenwick Bridal [1], which helps explain why Site A is forecast to sell 160 dresses rather than 110 [1]. That gives £55,000 more revenue for £12,000 more rent [1]. However, customers can easily compare prices and styles with the rivals [1], so Fenwick Bridal may have to offer lower prices or better service to win sales [1], which could reduce the profit on each dress and mean the forecast of 160 is not met [1]. **[6]**

*Examiner insight:* Strong answers use both the competitor information and the figures. Writing only "competitors are bad" ignores the reason the town-centre forecast is higher.

**11.** A plumber travels to customers' homes rather than customers visiting the business [1]. This means passing trade on a high street brings little extra work [1], so paying high-street rent would add cost without adding revenue; a cheaper base such as a small unit is enough [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Option 2 revenue = 1,200 × £25 = £30,000; after rent, £30,000 − £9,000 = £21,000 [1].

Option 1 revenue = 1,000 × £25 = £25,000; after delivery and advertising, £25,000 − £1,800 = £23,200 [1]. Option 1 leaves £2,200 more a year after these costs [1].

For Option 1: Tidy Threads does not need customers to visit to repair clothes, so e-commerce and a collection service can replace the shop [1], and saving £9,000 rent reduces fixed costs, lowering the risk if demand falls [1].

For Option 2: alterations often need fittings, so some customers may want to visit in person, and losing them could make the fall in customers larger than 200 [1]; a shop also gives passing trade and trust [1].

Judgement: Rosa should choose Option 1, because on the forecasts it leaves £2,200 more and lowers her fixed costs [1]. This depends on the customer forecast; if more than 88 extra customers are lost (£2,200 ÷ £25), Option 2 would be better, so she might test the online service before ending the lease [1]. **[9]**

*Examiner insight:* A Justify answer should compare both options with the data provided and finish with a supported recommendation that states what it depends on.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculating revenue but forgetting to compare rents as well.
- Treating competitors only as a threat.
- Saying an online business needs no premises at all.
- Listing factors without using the business named in the question.
- Giving a recommendation in Question 12 without using the figures.
