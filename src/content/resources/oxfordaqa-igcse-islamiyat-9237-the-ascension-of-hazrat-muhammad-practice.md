## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. The
4-mark "describe" question gives one mark for each relevant, accurate detail; the 4-mark "explain"
question gives one mark for each point and one for explaining it, up to two marks for each of the
two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general,
accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The
12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Buraq [1].

**2.** Jerusalem [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: travelled from Makkah to Jerusalem; led the prophets in prayer at al-Aqsa; ascended through the heavens; met earlier prophets; reached Sidrat al-Muntaha; was given the five daily prayers; was shown Paradise and the Fire.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: it came after the Year of Sorrow; it came after the death of Abu Talib and Khadija (RA); it came after the rejection at Ta'if; it comforted him and showed that Allah supported him; it came before the Hijrah.

**5.** Any four, [1] each: Allah had commanded fifty prayers a day [1]; Musa (AS) asked what he had been given [1]; he told the Prophet (pbuh) that the Ummah would not be able to bear fifty [1]; he advised him to ask Allah for a reduction [1]; the Prophet (pbuh) went back several times [1]; the number was reduced until it became five [1]; the reward of fifty remained [1].

**6.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Mockery** [1]: they laughed because a journey to Jerusalem took a caravan a month, so a single night seemed impossible [1].
- **Testing him** [1]: they asked him to describe Jerusalem, which he had never visited, and he described it accurately [1].
- **Doubt among some Muslims** [1]: some wavered, which shows that the event was a test of faith [1].
- **Belief of Abu Bakr (RA)** [1]: he affirmed the story at once and was called as-Siddiq [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **A direct gift from Allah** [1]: the prayers were commanded in the heavens, which shows their high position among the duties of Islam [1].
- **A link with Allah** [1]: five times a day a Muslim stands before Allah in worship [1].
- **Mercy and ease** [1]: the reduction from fifty to five shows Allah's care, and the reward is still that of fifty [1].
- **Discipline and unity** [1]: the fixed times give structure to the day and unite Muslims in the same worship [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers a decree of specified times" (Qur'an 4:103) [2].
- "Indeed, prayer prohibits immorality and wrongdoing" (Qur'an 29:45): the prayers shape character [2].

**8.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the five prayers are a Pillar of Islam and shape the daily life of every Muslim; they were given directly by Allah.
- **For the statement:** they link every believer to the Mi'raj each day.
- **Against the statement:** the journey also honoured the Prophet (pbuh) and comforted him after Ta'if, which shows Allah's support for his mission.
- **Against the statement:** it linked Makkah and Jerusalem, and it joined the Prophet (pbuh) with the earlier prophets.
- **Balanced view:** the five prayers are the most lasting practical result, but the event had several purposes at once.

*Use of the passage*

- The passage does not mention prayer by name, so a good answer must say so; it speaks of "Our signs" and Tawhid, and the prayers are part of the worship that expresses Tawhid.
- "to show him of Our signs" shows that the journey had a purpose beyond a single gift.
- "that you not take other than Me as Disposer of affairs" links to Salah as the act in which a Muslim relies on Allah alone.

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Lessons (AO1):

- **Trust in the Prophet (pbuh)** [1]: Abu Bakr (RA) believed because he knew the Prophet's honesty [1].
- **Faith in Allah's power** [1]: he accepted that Allah can do what is beyond normal experience [1].
- **Courage to believe when others doubt** [1]: he affirmed the truth when many mocked [1].
- **Loyalty** [1]: his firm support for the Prophet (pbuh) earned him the title as-Siddiq [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Exalted is He who took His Servant by night" (Qur'an 17:1): Abu Bakr (RA) accepted that Allah has power over everything [2].
- "O you who have believed, fear Allah and be with those who are true" (Qur'an 9:119) [2].

**10.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **For the statement:** the journey came after the Year of Sorrow and Ta'if, when the Prophet (pbuh) was grieving, and it showed that Allah had not abandoned him.
- **For the statement:** he met the earlier prophets and saw Paradise, which strengthened his hope.
- **Against the statement:** it was also a great miracle, which tested people's faith and showed Allah's power.
- **Against the statement:** it gave the community the five daily prayers and the lasting link to al-Aqsa.
- **Balanced view:** comfort and miracle were part of one event, and the lasting gift to the Ummah was worship.

*Use of the passage*

- "took His Servant" shows Allah's closeness to him and honour for him.
- "to show him of Our signs" shows a personal purpose: to strengthen him.
- "Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing" shows that Allah knew his sorrow and his need.
- "Exalted is He" shows the miracle.
