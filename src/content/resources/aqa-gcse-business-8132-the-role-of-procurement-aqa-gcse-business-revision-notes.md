## Core recall

### The four parts of 3.3.2

1. Managing stock: JIT and JIC. Evaluate JIT for a given business. No stock control charts.
2. Choice of supplier: price, quality, reliability.
3. Effects of procurement and logistics: efficiency and lower unit costs, balanced against quality of service.
4. Value of effective supply chain management.

### JIT against JIC

| | Just in time (JIT) | Just in case (JIC) |
|---|---|---|
| Stock held | Very little | Buffer stock |
| Main benefit | Lower holding costs, less cash tied up, less waste | Spare stock meets unexpected demand; protection from late deliveries |
| Main cost | More frequent deliveries; lost purchasing economies of scale | Holding costs: space, insurance, security; cash tied up |
| Main risk | Production stops if a delivery is late | Stock damaged, out of date or unsold |
| Suits | Predictable demand, reliable nearby suppliers, perishable or bulky stock | Unpredictable or seasonal demand, costly stock-outs, distant suppliers, big bulk discounts |

### The specification's two balances

- Reduced costs of JIT **against** more frequent deliveries and lost purchasing economies of scale.
- Benefits of spare stock **against** the cost of holding buffer stock.

### Supplier factors

| Factor | Why it matters | Most important when |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Affects unit cost and profit | Competing on low prices |
| Quality | Faulty inputs cause waste, returns, reputation damage | Selling premium products |
| Reliability | Late or short deliveries stop production | Using JIT or with tight deadlines |

### Procurement and logistics

- Procurement: finding and buying goods and services.
- Logistics: moving and storing them, inbound and outbound.
- Effects: greater efficiency and lower unit costs.
- Balance: cheaper is not better if service quality suffers.

### Supply chain management: the three listed benefits

1. Working with suppliers so key processes run efficiently and cost effectively.
2. Getting goods and services for the best price and value.
3. Cutting waste and unnecessary costs for a streamlined process and fast production times.

### Quick worked reminder

Figures invented. A shop uses 600 units a month. Buying monthly at £2.50 each with one £30 delivery costs £1,530; storage adds £120, total £1,650. Buying weekly (4 orders) at £2.70 each with four £20 deliveries costs £1,620 + £80 = £1,700; storage adds £20, total £1,720. Monthly buying is £70 cheaper, but ties up more cash.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Procurement | Finding, choosing and buying the goods and services a business needs |
| Logistics | Transporting and storing goods so they are in the right place at the right time |
| Stock | Materials, components or finished goods held by a business |
| Buffer stock | Extra stock held in reserve |
| Just in time (JIT) | Stock arrives just as it is needed; little held |
| Just in case (JIC) | Stock held in reserve in case of extra demand or supply problems |
| Holding costs | The costs of storing stock |
| Purchasing economies of scale | Lower prices per unit from buying in bulk |
| Supplier | A business that provides goods or services to another business |
| Supply chain | The network of businesses taking a product from raw materials to the customer |
| Unit cost | The average cost of one item |

## Exam traps

- **Stock control charts.** Not required: do not draw or interpret them.
- **"JIT is always cheaper."** Delivery charges and lost discounts can outweigh storage savings.
- **Cheapest supplier is best.** Weigh quality and reliability too.
- **Procurement and logistics confused.** Buying versus moving and storing.
- **Ignoring service quality.** The specification says cost savings must be balanced against it.
- **No context in a JIT evaluation.** Use the business's demand, suppliers and cash.
- **Value means lowest price.** Value includes quality and service.

## Self-test

1. Define procurement.
2. Define logistics.
3. Give two benefits of JIT.
4. State the two costs of JIT that the specification names.
5. Give one benefit and one cost of holding buffer stock.
6. Name the three supplier factors in the specification.
7. Why is reliability especially important to a business using JIT?
8. Give one reason a hospital might prefer JIC for medical supplies.
9. What is a supply chain?
10. State one benefit of effective supply chain management.
11. Supplier A charges £4.00 a unit and Supplier B £3.75. A business buys 2,000 units. How much does it save with B?
12. Give one reason why the cheaper supplier in question 11 might not be chosen.

### Answers

1. Finding, choosing and buying the goods and services a business needs.
2. Moving and storing goods so they arrive in the right place at the right time.
3. Any two: lower storage costs, less cash tied up, less waste from out-of-date or damaged stock.
4. More frequent deliveries and lost purchasing economies of scale.
5. Benefit: meets unexpected demand. Cost: storage, insurance and cash tied up.
6. Price, quality and reliability.
7. It holds little spare stock, so a late delivery stops production.
8. Running out could put patients at risk, so the cost of a stock-out is very high.
9. The network of businesses and activities taking a product from raw materials to the final customer.
10. Any one of: efficient, cost-effective processes; best price and value; less waste and faster production.
11. £0.25 × 2,000 = £500.
12. B might deliver late or supply lower-quality goods.
