## Section A

**1.** Define the capacitance of a parallel plate capacitor. **[2]**

**2.** Define the capacitance of an isolated spherical conductor, and show that a sphere of radius R has capacitance 4πε₀R. **[3]**

**3.** Calculate the capacitance of an isolated metal sphere of radius 0.25 m. **[2]**

**4.** A capacitor stores a charge of 3.3 mC when the p.d. across it is 15 V.

**(a)** Calculate its capacitance.

**(b)** State the charge it stores when the p.d. is 9.0 V. **[2]**

**5.** Show that the farad is equivalent to A² s⁴ kg⁻¹ m⁻². **[3]**

**6.** Explain why capacitors connected in series all store the same charge. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Using C = Q/V, derive the formula for the combined capacitance of

**(a)** two capacitors C₁ and C₂ in parallel **[3]**

**(b)** two capacitors C₁ and C₂ in series. **[3]**

**8.** A 3.0 μF capacitor is connected in series with a parallel combination of a 1.0 μF and a 5.0 μF capacitor. The network is connected to an 18 V supply.

**(a)** Calculate the combined capacitance of the network. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge on the 3.0 μF capacitor and the p.d. across it. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the charge on the 1.0 μF capacitor and on the 5.0 μF capacitor. **[2]**

**9.** A student has three 6.0 μF capacitors. Using all three each time, show how they can be connected to give a combined capacitance of

**(a)** 4.0 μF

**(b)** 9.0 μF. **[4]**

**10.** A 50 μF capacitor is charged to 20 V and disconnected from the supply. It is then connected in parallel with an uncharged 30 μF capacitor.

**(a)** Calculate the initial charge on the 50 μF capacitor. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the final p.d. across the capacitors. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the final charge on each capacitor. **[1]**

**11.** The Earth may be treated as an isolated conducting sphere of radius 6.4 × 10⁶ m.

**(a)** Calculate the capacitance of the Earth. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the charge that would need to be added to change the Earth's potential by 1.0 V. **[1]**

**12.** An isolated metal sphere of radius 0.12 m is charged to a potential of 30 kV. It is then joined by a long thin wire to a second, uncharged, isolated sphere of radius 0.040 m, far away. Ignore the capacitance of the wire.

**(a)** Calculate the initial charge on the larger sphere. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the common potential of the two spheres after they are joined. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the final charge on each sphere. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The **charge on one plate per unit potential difference** between the plates [1]: C = Q/V, where Q is the magnitude of the charge on either plate and V the p.d. between them [1].

**2.** Capacitance = **charge on the sphere per unit potential of the sphere** (potential measured from zero at infinity) [1]. The potential of a sphere of charge Q is V = Q/(4πε₀R) [1], so C = Q/V = **4πε₀R** [1].

**3.** C = 4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.25 [1] = **2.8 × 10⁻¹¹ F** (28 pF) [1].

**4. (a)** C = 3.3 × 10⁻³ ÷ 15 = **2.2 × 10⁻⁴ F** (220 μF) [1].

**(b)** Q = CV = 2.2 × 10⁻⁴ × 9.0 = **1.98 × 10⁻³ C** [1].

**5.** F = C V⁻¹; C = A s [1]; V = J C⁻¹ = kg m² s⁻² ÷ (A s) = kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹ [1]; F = A s ÷ (kg m² s⁻³ A⁻¹) = **A² s⁴ kg⁻¹ m⁻²** [1].

**6.** The plates joined between two capacitors form an **isolated conductor** with zero net charge [1]; when one gains −Q the other must gain +Q, so every capacitor carries the **same charge Q** [1].

**7. (a)** Same p.d. V across each [1]; total charge Q = Q₁ + Q₂ = C₁V + C₂V [1]; dividing by V, **C = C₁ + C₂** [1].

**(b)** Same charge Q on each [1]; total p.d. V = V₁ + V₂ = Q/C₁ + Q/C₂ [1]; since V = Q/C, dividing by Q gives **1/C = 1/C₁ + 1/C₂** [1].

**8. (a)** Parallel pair = 1.0 + 5.0 = 6.0 μF [1]; series with 3.0 μF: C = (3.0 × 6.0)/(3.0 + 6.0) = **2.0 μF** [1].

**(b)** Q = CV = 2.0 μF × 18 V = **36 μC** [1]; V = Q/C = 36 ÷ 3.0 = **12 V** [1].

**(c)** p.d. across the pair = 18 − 12 = 6.0 V; 1.0 μF: **6.0 μC** [1]; 5.0 μF: **30 μC** [1].

**9. (a)** Two in **parallel** (12 μF) [1], connected in **series** with the third: (12 × 6.0)/(12 + 6.0) = **4.0 μF** [1].

**(b)** Two in **series** (3.0 μF) [1], connected in **parallel** with the third: 3.0 + 6.0 = **9.0 μF** [1].

**10. (a)** Q = 50 × 10⁻⁶ × 20 = **1.0 × 10⁻³ C** (1000 μC) [1].

**(b)** Charge conserved; combined capacitance = 80 μF [1]; V = 1000 μC ÷ 80 μF = **12.5 V** [1].

**(c)** 50 μF: 50 × 12.5 = **625 μC**; 30 μF: 30 × 12.5 = **375 μC** [1].

**11. (a)** C = 4πε₀R = 4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 6.4 × 10⁶ [1] = **7.1 × 10⁻⁴ F** [1].

**(b)** Q = CΔV = 7.1 × 10⁻⁴ × 1.0 = **7.1 × 10⁻⁴ C** [1].

**12. (a)** C₁ = 4πε₀ × 0.12 = 1.33 × 10⁻¹¹ F [1]; Q = C₁V = 1.33 × 10⁻¹¹ × 3.0 × 10⁴ = **4.0 × 10⁻⁷ C** [1].

**(b)** Joined spheres reach the same potential; total capacitance = 4πε₀(0.12 + 0.040) = 1.78 × 10⁻¹¹ F [1]; V = 4.0 × 10⁻⁷ ÷ 1.78 × 10⁻¹¹ = **2.25 × 10⁴ V** (22.5 kV) [1].

**(c)** Charges are in the ratio of capacitances, 3 : 1: larger sphere **3.0 × 10⁻⁷ C**, smaller sphere **1.0 × 10⁻⁷ C** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the resistor rules for capacitors.
- Defining capacitance without saying which charge and which potential are meant.
- Adding reciprocals in series and forgetting to invert.
- Assuming capacitors in series share the supply p.d. equally.
- Forgetting that charge is conserved when a charged capacitor is connected to an uncharged one.
- Prefix errors with μF, nF and pF.
