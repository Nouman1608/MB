## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Define the term *product orientation*. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between market share and market growth. **[2]**

**3.** Explain one disadvantage of market orientation for a business. **[2]**

**4.** A business has sales revenue of 18 million in a market worth 120 million. Calculate its market share. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a business's sales revenue can rise while its market share falls. **[2]**

**6.** Hexley Robotics designs surgical robots. Its engineers decide product features, and few hospitals are asked for their views before a model is built. Explain whether Hexley's product orientation is likely to be a problem. **[4]**

### Section B: structured questions

Questions 7 to 10 use the case below.

Brewhurst Coffee sells packaged premium coffee. Data for the national packaged coffee market:

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Market sales revenue | 64 million | 72 million |
| Brewhurst sales revenue | 16 million | 17.28 million |
| Moorhen Roasters sales revenue | 9.6 million | 12.24 million |

In Year 2, the market sold 9 million bags, of which Brewhurst sold 1.8 million. Moorhen Roasters launched a range of cheaper organic blends after surveying younger coffee drinkers. Brewhurst's managers say their blends "are the best in the country, so customers will come to us".

**7.** Calculate:

**(a)** market growth between Year 1 and Year 2. **[1]**

**(b)** Brewhurst's market share by value in both years. **[2]**

**(c)** Moorhen's market share by value in both years. **[2]**

**8.** Calculate Brewhurst's market share by volume in Year 2 and comment on what the difference from its value share shows. **[3]**

**9.** Analyse Brewhurst's position in the market using your answers to questions 7 and 8. **[6]**

**10.** Discuss whether Brewhurst should become more market-oriented. **[8]**

**11.** (HL only) Evaluate the importance to Brewhurst of remaining market leader. **[10]**

**12.** A market shrinks by 5%, and a business's share rises from 20% to 22%. Calculate the percentage change in the business's sales. **[2]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** An approach in which a business focuses on making the products it is good at or wants to make [1], then tries to find customers for them, rather than starting from research into customer needs [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Market share is one business's sales as a percentage of total market sales [1], whereas market growth is the percentage change in the size of the whole market between two periods [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Market research costs money and time [1], which may delay launches or strain a small firm's budget; customers may also be unable to describe needs for genuinely new products, so research can limit innovation [1]. **[2]**

**4.** 18 ÷ 120 × 100 = **15%** [1] **[1]**

**5.** Market share depends on the business's sales relative to the whole market [1]; if the market grows faster than the business's sales, its share falls even though its sales rise [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Product orientation means features are chosen by engineers rather than buyers [1]. In surgical robots, technical expertise and safety standards are central, and surgeons may not be able to specify features for new technology, so this approach can work [1]. However, hospitals pay high prices and have specific needs such as training and fitting into theatres [1], so ignoring their views risks building robots that do not suit how they work; some research with surgeons would reduce that risk [1]. **[4]**

### Section B

**7. (a)** (72 − 64) ÷ 64 × 100 = **12.5%** [1]

**(b)** Year 1: 16 ÷ 64 × 100 = **25.0%** [1]; Year 2: 17.28 ÷ 72 × 100 = **24.0%** [1]

**(c)** Year 1: 9.6 ÷ 64 × 100 = **15.0%** [1]; Year 2: 12.24 ÷ 72 × 100 = **17.0%** [1] **[5]**

**8.** 1.8 ÷ 9 × 100 = **20.0%** [1]. Brewhurst's value share (24.0%) is higher than its volume share [1], which shows that its prices are above the market average, consistent with a premium position [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Brewhurst is still the larger of the two firms, with 24.0% of the market by value [1]. However, its sales grew by 8% while the market grew by 12.5% [1], so its share fell by 1 percentage point [1]. Moorhen's sales grew by 27.5% and its share rose by 2 percentage points [1], probably because its cheaper organic range, based on research with younger drinkers, captured much of the market's growth [1]. Brewhurst is in a growing market but losing ground, a warning sign that its premium, product-led approach is missing new demand [1]. **[6]**

**10.** For: Moorhen's research-led range has gained 2 percentage points of share, showing that understanding customers pays off [1]; younger drinkers may be the future of the market, and Brewhurst does not know what they want [1]; research could show whether customers value organic credentials or lower prices [1]. Against: Brewhurst's premium quality is its strength, and its volume share of 20% against value share of 24% shows customers pay more for it [1]; chasing every trend could blur its brand [1]; research costs money [1]. Judgement: Brewhurst should adopt more market orientation, for example by researching younger drinkers, while keeping its premium position [1], because its share is falling in a growing market; if research showed its existing customers were loyal and growing, the case for change would be weaker [1]. **[8]**

**11.** Benefits of leadership: high volume gives Brewhurst buying power with coffee growers and roasters, lowering unit costs [1]; supermarkets give leading brands better shelf space [1]; leadership supports its premium image and trust [1]. Limits: leadership is narrowing, from 25% to 24% while Moorhen rose to 17% [1]; defending leadership with discounts would conflict with its premium pricing and reduce profit [1]; premium buyers may care more about quality than about buying the biggest brand [1]. Analysis: Brewhurst's value share above volume share shows it already earns more per bag, so profit may matter more than share [1]; losing leadership to a rival with a younger, growing customer base could, over time, reduce retailer support [1]. Judgement: leadership matters to Brewhurst mainly for retailer power and brand image [1], but it should defend it through products that meet changing tastes rather than through price cuts; it matters more if retailers are cutting the number of brands they stock [1]. **[10]**

**12.** New sales as a fraction of old = (0.95 × 22) ÷ 20 = 1.045 [1], so sales rise by **4.5%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* If market size is M, sales go from 0.20M to 0.22 × 0.95M = 0.209M, a rise of 4.5%.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Confusing value share and volume share.
* Saying share "fell 1%" when it fell by 1 percentage point.
* Assuming rising sales mean rising share.
* Dismissing product orientation without considering the market.
* HL leadership answers that list benefits with no limits or judgement.
