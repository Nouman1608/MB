## Questions

**1.** Explain how the polar covalent bonds in a water molecule lead to hydrogen bonding between water molecules. **[3]**

**2.** A tall tree loses water from its leaves on a dry, windy day. Explain how water moves up the xylem without the water column breaking. **[4]**

**3.** Soil that contains a lot of clay stays damp for longer after rain than coarse sand does. Explain this using the properties of water. **[3]**

**4.** Glucose dissolves in blood plasma but cholesterol-rich fat droplets do not.

**(a)** Explain why glucose dissolves in water. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one way in which the hydrophobic nature of phospholipid tails is important for cells. **[2]**

**5.** A diver's body is cooled by surrounding water. Use these values supplied for the question: specific heat capacity of water 4.18 J per g per K and of air 1.0 J per g per K; thermal conductivity of water 0.6 W per m per K and of air 0.026 W per m per K.

**(a)** Calculate the energy needed to warm 750 g of water by 6 K. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times more energy is needed than for 750 g of air warmed by the same amount. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many times faster water conducts heat than air, to 2 significant figures. **[1]**
**(d)** The ringed seal has a thick layer of blubber. Explain how this suits its habitat, with reference to water and air. **[2]**

**6.** Outline how a molecule of RNA is built from nucleotides and why its backbone is strong. Include a description of one nucleotide. **[5]**

**7.** State three differences between DNA and RNA. **[3]**

**8.** A section of one DNA strand reads 5'-ATGGCTAC-3'.

**(a)** Write the sequence of the complementary strand, in the 5' to 3' direction. **[2]**
**(b)** The strand given above is used as the template for transcription. Write the mRNA sequence in the 5' to 3' direction. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how hydrogen bonding between bases allows the information in DNA to be both copied and used. **[3]**

**9.**

**(a)** Calculate how many different base sequences are possible for a stretch of DNA 9 bases long. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the information storage capacity of DNA is described as limitless. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how the conserved genetic code is evidence for universal common ancestry. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) Outline the evidence and reasoning that link liquid water to the search for life beyond Earth, and state why Earth has kept its water. **[4]**

**11.** (HL only)

**(a)** Explain what is meant by the 5' end and 3' end of a nucleic acid strand, and why new nucleotides are added at one end only. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why every base pair in DNA has the same length. **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the structure of a nucleosome. **[3]**

**12.** (HL only) A virus that infects bacteria was grown with either radioactive 32P or radioactive 35S. Each batch was mixed with bacteria, shaken to remove viral coats, and spun to separate a pellet (bacteria) from the supernatant (coats). Counts per minute are shown.

| Label | Pellet | Supernatant |
|---|---|---|
| 32P | 7400 | 800 |
| 35S | 1100 | 7900 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of the 32P label in the pellet and the percentage of the 35S label in the supernatant, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain what these results suggest about the genetic material, and why 32P and 35S were chosen. **[2]**

Two species gave these base percentages: species W had A 31.2, T 31.0, G 18.9, C 18.9; species X had A 22.4, T 22.6, G 27.5, C 27.5.

**(c)** Calculate (A+T)/(G+C) for each species, to 2 decimal places. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how data like these falsified the tetranucleotide hypothesis. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly than hydrogen does [1], so the electrons are shared unequally and oxygen is slightly negative (δ-) while each hydrogen is slightly positive (δ+) [1]. The δ+ hydrogen of one molecule is attracted to the δ- oxygen of another, forming a hydrogen bond between molecules [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers lose credit by placing the hydrogen bond inside one molecule. Say the bond is between molecules and name both charges.

**2.** Evaporation (transpiration) from leaf cells pulls water out of the xylem [1], which puts the water column under tension [1]. Hydrogen bonds between water molecules (cohesion) hold the column together so it is pulled up as a continuous stream [1]. Adhesion to the polar cellulose walls of the xylem supports the column [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Four marks need four separate ideas, so link cohesion to tension and include adhesion.

**3.** Water is attracted to the charged clay particles (adhesion) [1]. The gaps between clay particles are very narrow, so capillary action holds water in them [1], and cohesion pulls further water along behind, which resists drainage; sand has larger gaps [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A comparison needs both sides, so say why coarse sand differs.

**4. (a)** Glucose is polar [1], so water molecules cluster round it through attraction between opposite partial charges, and it dissolves [1]. **(b)** Phospholipid tails are nonpolar and avoid water [1], so in a membrane they form the inner layer of the bilayer, separating the watery solutions on either side [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Hydrophobic" must be linked to a function. State the function, not just that the tails repel water.

**5. (a)** Energy = mass x specific heat capacity x temperature change = 750 x 4.18 x 6 [1] = **18 810 J** (18 800 J to 3 significant figures) [1]. **(b)** Air: 750 x 1.0 x 6 = 4500 J, so 18 810 / 4500 = **4.18 times** [1]. **(c)** 0.6 / 0.026 = 23.08, so **23 times** faster [1]. **(d)** Water takes heat from the body much faster than air does [1], so blubber insulates the seal and reduces heat loss in cold water [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the substitution as well as the result, and give units. In part (d) state which medium loses heat faster.

**6.** A nucleotide has a phosphate group, a pentose sugar (ribose) and a nitrogenous base [1]. Nucleotides join by condensation reactions [1], which release water [1]. A covalent bond forms between the phosphate of one nucleotide and the sugar of the next [1], forming a sugar-phosphate backbone of continuous covalent bonds, which is strong [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In a diagram, show relative positions with a circle, a pentagon and a rectangle, and label each.

**7.** Any three of: DNA is double-stranded, RNA is single-stranded [1]; DNA contains deoxyribose, RNA contains ribose [1]; DNA contains thymine, RNA contains uracil instead [1]; **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A difference needs both sides stated. "RNA has uracil" alone is weaker than "RNA has uracil where DNA has thymine".

**8. (a)** Pair the bases: A-T, T-A, G-C, G-C, C-G, T-A, A-T, C-G, so the complement is 3'-TACCGATG-5' [1]. Written 5' to 3' it is **5'-GTAGCCAT-3'** [1]. **(b)** mRNA pairs with the template, using U for T: 3'-UACCGAUG-5' [1], which is **5'-GUAGCCAU-3'** [1]. **(c)** Bases pair by hydrogen bonds: A-T (A-U in RNA) and G-C [1]. The bonds are weak, so the strands can separate [1], and each strand can act as a template for a new strand or for mRNA, with the same pairing rules giving an accurate copy [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Reverse the order after pairing if 5' to 3' is asked, and use U, not T, in mRNA.

**9. (a)** Each position has 4 possible bases, so 4⁹ [1] = **262 144** [1]. **(b)** A DNA molecule can be of any length [1] and any base can follow any other, so the number of possible sequences has no upper limit [1]. **(c)** Almost all organisms use the same triplet code for the same amino acids [1], which is most simply explained by inheritance of the code from a common ancestor [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* For common ancestry, link the observation (shared code) to the conclusion. Specific examples need not be memorised.

**10.** (HL only) Liquid water is needed by all known life, so planets with liquid water are the best candidates in the search for life [1]. The "Goldilocks zone" is the region around a star where temperatures could allow liquid water at a planet's surface [1]. Earth kept its water because gravity holds it [1] and temperatures are low enough for water vapour to condense [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say a planet in the zone could support liquid water, not that it has life.

**11. (HL only) (a)** Each strand has a 5' end and a 3' end, named for the carbon of the sugar at that end [1]. Nucleotides are added to the 3' end, so strands are built 5' to 3' [1]. **(b)** Every pair joins one purine (two rings) to one pyrimidine (one ring) [1], so A-T and C-G pairs have equal length and the helix has the same shape whatever the sequence [1]. **(c)** DNA is wrapped round a core of eight histone proteins [1]; an additional histone is attached to linker DNA [1], which joins one nucleosome to the next [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Say the DNA wraps round the histones, not that histones lie inside the DNA.

**12. (HL only) (a)** 32P in pellet: 7400 / (7400 + 800) x 100 [1] = **90.2%**. 35S in supernatant: 7900 / (7900 + 1100) x 100 = **87.8%** [1]. **(b)** 32P marks DNA, which is absent from protein, and 35S marks protein, which is absent from DNA [1]. Most DNA label entered the bacteria while most protein label stayed outside, which supports DNA as the genetic material [1]. **(c)** Species W: (31.2 + 31.0) / (18.9 + 18.9) = 62.2 / 37.8 = **1.65** [1]. Species X: (22.4 + 22.6) / (27.5 + 27.5) = 45.0 / 55.0 = **0.82** [1]. **(d)** The tetranucleotide hypothesis predicted that the four bases repeat in a fixed order, giving equal amounts [1]. The ratios differ between species and are not 1, so the bases are not present in equal amounts, which contradicts it [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the data, and say the results support the conclusion rather than prove it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that hydrogen bonds are inside the water molecule rather than between molecules.
- Describing xylem transport without naming tension, cohesion and adhesion.
- Stating a comparison of water and air without the direction of the difference.
- Giving a complementary strand in the wrong direction, or leaving it unreversed.
- Using thymine in RNA or uracil in DNA.
- Using the wrong total when converting counts to a percentage.
- Claiming that experimental data prove a conclusion rather than support it.
