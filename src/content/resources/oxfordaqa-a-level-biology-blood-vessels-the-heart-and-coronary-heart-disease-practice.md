## Questions

**1.** Give two differences between the structure of a vein and of an artery, and relate each to function. **[4]**

**2.** A photograph of a capillary shows its width as 40 mm at a magnification of x8000.

**(a)** Calculate the real width in micrometres. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the structure of a capillary makes it suited to metabolic exchange. **[3]**

**3.** In a capillary bed the hydrostatic pressure is 3.9 kPa at the arteriole end and 1.4 kPa at the venule end. The effect of plasma proteins draws fluid in with a pressure of 3.0 kPa throughout.

**(a)** Calculate the net pressure at each end and state the direction of fluid movement. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how tissue fluid forms. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how fluid returns to the blood and what happens to the remainder. **[2]**

**4.** **(a)** Name the vessel that carries blood from the right ventricle and the valve that prevents backflow into the ventricle. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the wall of the left ventricle is thicker than that of the right. **[2]**
**(c)** State the function of the coronary arteries. **[1]**

**5.** The table gives data for the left side of a heart in a cycle of 0.60 s. End-diastolic volume is 138 cm³ and end-systolic volume is 66 cm³.

| Time / s | Atrium / kPa | Ventricle / kPa | Aorta / kPa |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.04 | 1.3 | 0.7 | 10.9 |
| 0.10 | 1.1 | 8.0 | 10.5 |
| 0.20 | 1.5 | 17.2 | 16.6 |
| 0.32 | 2.3 | 3.0 | 11.9 |
| 0.50 | 1.7 | 0.5 | 10.6 |

**(a)** State the position of each valve at 0.20 s. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why both valves are closed at 0.32 s. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the stroke volume, the heart rate and the cardiac output in dm³ per minute. **[4]**

**6.** A trained athlete has a resting heart rate of 52 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 95 cm³. An untrained person has 70 beats per minute and 68 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the cardiac output of each in dm³ per minute. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate how much higher the athlete's stroke volume is, as a percentage of the untrained person's. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how the athlete can have a lower heart rate but a similar cardiac output. **[2]**

**7.** Explain how atheroma can lead to an aneurysm and to thrombosis. **[4]**

**8.** Atheroma narrows an artery's lumen diameter from 3.6 mm to 2.7 mm. Calculate the percentage reduction in diameter and in cross-sectional area. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how a thrombus in a coronary artery causes a myocardial infarction. **[4]**

**10.** A study followed adults for several years. The table shows new cases of CHD per 1000 person-years.

| Cigarettes per day | 0 | 1 to 14 | 15 to 24 | 25 or more |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| CHD cases per 1000 person-years | 3.4 | 5.9 | 8.8 | 11.2 |

**(a)** Calculate how many times greater the rate is in the 15 to 24 group than in non-smokers. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the trend shown. **[2]**
**(c)** A newspaper says the data prove smoking causes CHD. Evaluate this claim. **[3]**

**11.** Five volunteers had their pulse rate measured at rest and after 2 minutes of stepping. Rest (beats per minute): 68, 72, 64, 75, 71. After stepping: 112, 125, 108, 131, 119.

**(a)** Calculate the mean at rest, the mean after stepping, and the percentage increase. **[3]**
**(b)** State two variables that should be controlled. **[2]**
**(c)** State one precaution for volunteers. **[1]**

**12.** A man smokes, eats a diet high in saturated fat and has high blood pressure. Explain how each of these may increase his risk of CHD. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Veins have thinner walls with less elastic tissue [1] because blood is at low pressure [1]; veins have valves [1] to prevent backflow of blood [1]. Also allow wider lumen with less resistance to flow. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each difference needs its own linked function; a list of differences with no function is likely to lose marks.

**2. (a)** 40 mm = 40 000 micrometres [1]; 40 000 / 8000 = **5.0 micrometres** [1].
**(b)** The wall is one cell thick, so a short diffusion distance [1]; the diameter allows red cells to pass slowly in single file [1]; gaps between cells let fluid and solutes pass out [1]. Allow large total surface area. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the unit conversion before dividing; a bare answer in the wrong unit loses credit even when the digits are right.

**3. (a)** Arteriole end: 3.9 - 3.0 = **0.9 kPa** out [1]. Venule end: 1.4 - 3.0 = **-1.6 kPa**, so into the capillary [1].
**(b)** High hydrostatic pressure forces fluid out [1] through gaps in the wall, leaving red cells and large proteins behind [1].
**(c)** The proteins lower the water potential of the blood, so water returns by osmosis [1]; the excess drains into lymph vessels and is returned to the blood [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the two forces separately (hydrostatic pressure and the protein effect on water potential), not just "pressure".

**4. (a)** Pulmonary artery [1]; semilunar valve [1].
**(b)** The left ventricle pumps blood around the whole body [1], so it must generate higher pressure through greater muscle contraction [1].
**(c)** They supply the cardiac muscle with oxygenated blood [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Say semilunar, not just "valve", and say the pulmonary artery carries blood to the lungs.

**5. (a)** Atrioventricular valve closed [1]; semilunar valve open [1] (ventricle 17.2 above aorta 16.6).
**(b)** Ventricular pressure (3.0) is below the aorta (11.9), so the semilunar valve is closed [1]; but it is above atrial pressure (2.3), so the atrioventricular valve is closed [1].
**(c)** Stroke volume = 138 - 66 = **72 cm³** [1]. Heart rate = 60 / 0.60 = **100 beats per minute** [1]. Cardiac output = 72 x 100 = 7200 cm³ per minute [1] = **7.2 dm³ per minute** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote the pressure pair from the table when justifying a valve state; the claim alone is weaker than the comparison.

**6. (a)** Athlete: 52 x 95 = 4940 cm³, **4.94 dm³ per minute** [1]. Untrained: 70 x 68 = 4760 cm³, **4.76 dm³ per minute** [1].
**(b)** (95 - 68) / 68 x 100 = **40 %** [1].
**(c)** The athlete's heart pumps more blood per beat [1], so fewer beats are needed per minute for the same output [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Stronger heart" earns less than a statement that stroke volume is larger so heart rate can be lower.

**7.** Atheroma weakens the artery wall [1]; at high pressure the wall bulges as an aneurysm [1] which may burst [1]. Atheroma damages the lining of the artery, which starts clotting [1] so a thrombus forms. Allow any four of these points. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each condition to a stated change in the wall; "atheroma causes both" alone is a bare assertion.

**8.** Diameter: (3.6 - 2.7) / 3.6 x 100 = **25 %** [1]. Area before = pi x 1.8² = 10.18 mm² [1]; area after = pi x 1.35² = 5.73 mm² [1]. Reduction = (10.18 - 5.73) / 10.18 x 100 = **44 %** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use radius, not diameter, in pi x r²; a diameter slip gives wrong areas for both before and after.

**9.** Atheroma damages the lining, and a thrombus forms [1]. It blocks the coronary artery [1] so blood flow to part of the cardiac muscle is interrupted [1]. The muscle is deprived of oxygen and glucose and dies [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Heart attack" is not an explanation; the interruption of blood flow to cardiac muscle must be stated.

**10. (a)** 8.8 / 3.4 [1] = **2.6 times** [1].
**(b)** The rate of CHD rises as the number of cigarettes per day rises [1], from 3.4 in non-smokers to 11.2 at 25 or more, with figures quoted [1].
**(c)** The data show a positive correlation, not proof of cause [1]; other variables such as age, diet or activity may differ between groups [1]; a plausible mechanism (damage to artery lining, carbon monoxide) and larger controlled studies would strengthen the claim [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a point about the data and a point about missing information; "it is only a correlation" alone is too thin.

**11. (a)** Mean at rest = 350 / 5 = **70.0** [1]; mean after = 595 / 5 = **119.0** [1]; increase = 49 / 70 x 100 = **70 %** [1].
**(b)** Any two: same exercise duration and step height; same volunteers; time of day; caffeine; counting time [2].
**(c)** Volunteers must be free to stop, or not take part if unwell or with a heart condition [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the variable and say how it is kept constant; "fair test" is not a control.

**12.** Smoking: carbon monoxide reduces oxygen carried by haemoglobin [1], and nicotine raises heart rate and narrows vessels, or damages the lining [1]. Diet: saturated fat raises blood cholesterol [1] so lipid deposits build up in artery walls as atheroma [1]. High blood pressure: damages the artery lining [1], promoting atheroma and stressing weakened walls [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Each factor needs a mechanism linking to atheroma, reduced oxygen supply or artery damage.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not converting micrometres and millimetres before dividing by magnification.
- Writing "pressure" without saying hydrostatic pressure or water potential.
- Using vein rather than artery for the vessels leaving the ventricles.
- Failing to compare pressure values when deciding valve state.
- Using diameter rather than radius in the area calculation.
- Giving cardiac output in cm³ per beat or without a unit.
- Treating a correlation as proof of cause.
- Describing the heart attack without the interrupted blood flow.
