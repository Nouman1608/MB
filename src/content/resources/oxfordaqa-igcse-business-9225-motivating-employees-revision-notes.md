Condensed recall for section 3.4.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-motivating-employees/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- The **importance of motivation**: staff retention and high productivity.
- **Financial methods**: the main methods of payment, **salary, wage, commission, profit sharing**.
- **Non-financial methods**: **styles of management, training, greater responsibility, fringe benefits**.
- Specific theories of motivation (such as Maslow) are **not examined**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | Willingness of employees to work hard and do a job well |
| Wage | Pay for hours worked, at an hourly rate |
| Salary | Fixed annual pay, usually paid monthly |
| Commission | Extra pay based on a percentage of sales made |
| Profit sharing | A share of the business's profit paid to employees |
| Fringe benefits | Extras given on top of pay (discounts, car, insurance, meals) |
| Autocratic | Manager decides and tells staff |
| Democratic | Manager consults staff before deciding |
| Delegation | Passing authority and responsibility to a lower level |
| Retention | Keeping staff in the business |

## Why motivation matters

- Higher **productivity** and lower unit costs.
- Better **retention**, saving recruitment and training costs.
- Better **quality** and **customer service**.
- Lower absence.
- Easier to recruit new staff.

## Financial methods

| Method | How it works | Advantage | Disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wage | Hourly rate x hours | Fair for time worked; overtime possible | Rewards time, not effort |
| Salary | Fixed annual amount | Security; easy to budget | No direct reward for extra effort |
| Commission | Percentage of sales | Strong sales incentive | Uncertain income; pressure on customers |
| Profit sharing | Share of profit | Teamwork and loyalty | Nothing if profit is low; effect of one person is small |

Examples:
- Wage: 38 hours x $12.50 = $475.
- Salary: $2,800 x 12 = $33,600.
- Commission: $1,200 basic + 4% of $60,000 = $1,200 + $2,400 = $3,600.
- Profit share: 10% of $150,000 = $15,000; shared among 60 staff = $250 each.

## Non-financial methods

| Method | How it motivates |
|---|---|
| Democratic management | Staff are consulted and feel valued |
| Training | Shows investment in staff; builds skills and promotion prospects |
| Greater responsibility | Work is more interesting; shows trust |
| Fringe benefits | Make the job more attractive; cost the business money |
| Other | Flexible hours, recognition schemes, job rotation, pleasant workplace |

## Management styles

| Style | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Autocratic | Quick decisions | Staff feel undervalued |
| Democratic | Involves staff; good ideas | Slower decisions |
| Laissez-faire | Freedom for skilled staff | Lack of direction |

## Choosing a method

- **Sales staff:** commission.
- **Staff in fixed-hours jobs:** wage plus bonus and benefits.
- **Skilled professionals:** salary, responsibility and training.
- **Teams:** profit sharing and democratic management.
- Money alone may not motivate for long; combine financial and non-financial methods.

## Calculation practice

- Percentage increase = increase / original x 100. Output of 50 rising to 56 is 6 / 50 x 100 = 12%.
- Cost of a pay rise = extra per person x number of staff x number of weeks. $24 x 20 x 52 = $24,960.
- Total pay with commission = basic + (percentage x sales).

## Exam traps

- Wage is hourly; salary is fixed annual.
- Money is not the only motivator.
- Explain the effect on **effort**, then on **the business** (productivity, retention).
- Every method has drawbacks; note them.
- Do not write about Maslow or other theories.
- Match the method to the type of worker and the job.

## Self-test

1. Define motivation.
2. State two benefits of a motivated workforce.
3. Distinguish between a wage and a salary.
4. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of commission.
5. Give one advantage of profit sharing.
6. State two fringe benefits.
7. Describe a democratic management style.
8. Explain how training can motivate employees.
9. A worker earns $14 an hour for 35 hours. Calculate weekly pay.
10. A salesperson earns $1,000 basic plus 5% commission on $48,000 of sales. Calculate total pay.
11. A firm shares 8% of a $250,000 profit equally among 40 staff. Calculate each person's share.
12. Explain why money alone may not keep staff motivated.

### Answers

1. The willingness of an employee to work hard and do a job well.
2. Any two: high productivity; staff retention; better quality; better service; lower absence.
3. A wage is paid by the hour; a salary is a fixed amount paid each year, usually in monthly parts.
4. Advantage: encourages staff to sell more. Disadvantage: income is uncertain.
5. For example, it encourages everyone to work to make the business profitable, and builds loyalty.
6. Any two: staff discount; company car; health insurance; free meals; extra holiday.
7. The manager consults staff and takes their views into account before deciding.
8. It shows the business values them, improves skills and confidence and creates promotion chances.
9. 35 x $14 = **$490**.
10. Commission = 5% x $48,000 = $2,400; total = $1,000 + $2,400 = **$3,400**.
11. 8% x $250,000 = $20,000; $20,000 / 40 = **$500** each.
12. Pay rises are quickly taken for granted, and staff who dislike the work, the manager or conditions may still leave.
