This study guide teaches subtopic **4.7 Circle theorems** from Topic 4 Geometry of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. Core candidates (C4.7) use **two** angle properties of circles. Extended candidates (E4.7, titled "Circle theorems I") use those two plus **four** more. The chord and tangent symmetry properties are a separate Extended subtopic, covered in the [Circle theorems II study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-ii/).

Consolidate with the [circle theorems revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [circle theorems practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-circle-theorems-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## What this subtopic covers

The syllabus asks you to calculate unknown angles and give explanations using these properties.

| Property | Tier |
|---|---|
| The angle in a semicircle = 90° | Core and Extended |
| The angle between tangent and radius = 90° | Core and Extended |
| The angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference | Extended only |
| Angles in the same segment are equal | Extended only |
| Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180° (supplementary) | Extended only |
| The alternate segment theorem | Extended only |

The notes say candidates are expected to use the properties **as listed in the syllabus** when giving reasons, so the wordings in this table are the ones to write.

Nearly every circle question also uses the angle facts of subtopic 4.6, and one more fact that is easy to miss: **two radii make an isosceles triangle**. Whenever the centre O is joined to two points on the circle, look for equal base angles.

## The Core properties

### Angle in a semicircle

If AB is a diameter and C is any other point on the circle, angle ACB = 90°. The right angle is always at the point on the circumference, **opposite** the diameter.

### Angle between tangent and radius

A tangent touches the circle at one point. The radius to that point meets the tangent at 90°. This turns many circle questions into right-angled triangle questions, so Pythagoras' theorem and trigonometry can follow.

### Worked example 1 (non-calculator)

AB is a diameter of a circle with centre O. C is a point on the circle and angle CAB = 34°. Find angle ABC.

```
angle ACB = 90°                 (angle in a semicircle = 90°)
angle ABC = 180° − 90° − 34°    (angle sum of a triangle is 180°)
          = 56°
```

### Worked example 2 (non-calculator): using the isosceles triangle

AB is a diameter of a circle with centre O. C is on the circle and angle BAC = 25°. Find angle OCB.

```
OA = OC (radii), so triangle OAC is isosceles
angle OCA = 25°                 (base angles of an isosceles triangle)
angle ACB = 90°                 (angle in a semicircle = 90°)
angle OCB = 90° − 25° = 65°
```

Check: OB = OC too, so angle OBC = 65°, and angle BOC = 180° − 130° = 50°. Angles AOC (130°) and BOC (50°) lie on the straight line AB and add to 180°, as they should.

### Worked example 3 (calculator): tangent and Pythagoras

A circle has centre O and radius 5 cm. P is a point outside the circle, and the tangent from P touches the circle at T. PT = 12 cm. Find OP and angle TOP.

```
angle OTP = 90°           (angle between tangent and radius = 90°)
OP = √(5² + 12²) = √169 = 13 cm
tan(angle TOP) = PT / OT = 12/5
angle TOP = 67.38...° = 67.4°  (1 d.p.)
```

Angles are given to 1 decimal place on calculator papers unless the question says otherwise.

## The Extended theorems

### Angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference

Take two points A and B on the circle. The angle AOB at the centre is twice any angle ACB at the circumference, **as long as C is on the opposite arc**, so that both angles stand on the same arc AB. This still holds when the angle at the centre is reflex: for C on the minor arc, angle ACB is half the **reflex** angle AOB.

### Angles in the same segment are equal

A chord divides the circle into two segments. All angles at the circumference standing on that chord and lying in the same segment are equal. In a diagram, look for two triangles sharing a base chord and meeting the circle on the same side; this is often drawn as a "bow-tie" of crossing chords.

### Opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral

A cyclic quadrilateral has all four vertices on the circle. Its opposite angles sum to 180°. All four vertices must be on the circle; a quadrilateral with a vertex at the centre is **not** cyclic.

### The alternate segment theorem

The angle between a tangent and a chord, at the point of contact, equals the angle in the **alternate segment**: the angle at the circumference standing on that chord, on the other side of the chord from the tangent angle.

### Worked example 4 (Extended, non-calculator)

A, B, C and D lie on a circle with centre O. Angle AOB = 130°. C is on the major arc AB and D is on the minor arc AB. Find angle ACB and angle ADB.

```
angle ACB = 130° ÷ 2 = 65°
  (angle at the centre is twice the angle at the circumference)
ACBD is a cyclic quadrilateral, so
angle ADB = 180° − 65° = 115°
  (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°)
```

Check with the reflex angle: reflex angle AOB = 360° − 130° = 230°, and 230° ÷ 2 = 115°.

### Worked example 5 (Extended, non-calculator): two theorems at once

ABCD is a cyclic quadrilateral. Its diagonals AC and BD cross at X. Angle ABD = 41°, angle BAD = 3x° and angle BCD = (x + 20)°.

(a) Find angle ACD. (b) Find x and angle BCD.

```
(a) angle ACD = angle ABD = 41°
    (angles in the same segment are equal; both stand on chord AD)
(b) 3x + x + 20 = 180
    (opposite angles of a cyclic quadrilateral sum to 180°)
    4x = 160,  x = 40
    angle BCD = 40 + 20 = 60°
```

Name the chord the two angles stand on. It shows the examiner, and you, that the angles really are in the same segment.

### Worked example 6 (Extended, non-calculator): alternate segment

A tangent to a circle touches it at A. T and S are points on the tangent on either side of A. B and C are points on the circle, with B on the same side as T. Angle TAB = 58° and angle SAC = 64°. Find the three angles of triangle ABC and state what type of triangle it is.

```
angle ACB = 58°   (alternate segment theorem, chord AB)
angle ABC = 64°   (alternate segment theorem, chord AC)
angle BAC = 180° − 58° − 64° = 58°   (angles on a straight line sum to 180°)
Check: 58° + 64° + 58° = 180°
Angles at A and C are equal, so triangle ABC is isosceles (BA = BC).
```

## Exam technique

- **"Find" or "calculate"** with **"give reasons"**: write each step with its property in the syllabus wording.
- **"Give a reason for your answer"** after a single angle: one complete property is enough, but it must be the right one.
- **"Explain why"**: state the property and connect it to the given points, for example "because AB is a diameter, angle ACB is an angle in a semicircle".
- Most circle questions are non-calculator in style. If the diagram is marked "not to scale", never measure.

## Common mistakes

- Putting the semicircle right angle at the wrong vertex. It is at the point on the circumference, not at an end of the diameter.
- Halving the wrong angle at the centre. Both angles must stand on the same arc: if the point on the circumference is on the minor arc, halve the reflex angle.
- Calling a quadrilateral cyclic when one vertex is the centre O.
- Using "angles in the same segment" for angles on opposite sides of the chord. Those are supplementary (a cyclic quadrilateral), not equal.
- Writing short reasons such as "circle theorem" or "cyclic". Write the property in full.
- Forgetting the isosceles triangle formed by two radii.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which circle theorems are on the Core paper for 0580?**
Only two: the angle in a semicircle is 90°, and the angle between a tangent and a radius is 90°. The other four theorems in subtopic 4.7 are Extended only.

**What is the alternate segment theorem?**
The angle between a tangent and a chord at the point of contact equals the angle at the circumference that stands on the chord in the opposite segment.

**Does the angle at the centre theorem work with a reflex angle?**
Yes. If the point on the circumference is on the minor arc, the angle there is half of the reflex angle at the centre.
