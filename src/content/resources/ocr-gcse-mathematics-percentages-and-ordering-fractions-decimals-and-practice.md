These questions cover sections 2.03 and 2.04 of the OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics (J560) specification, Version 2.1 (August 2026): percentages, multipliers, percentage change, original-value problems, simple interest, ordering and inequality symbols. All of it is Foundation and Higher tier content, so no question is Higher tier only. Learn the methods in the [study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-percentages-and-ordering-fractions-decimals-and/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-percentages-and-ordering-fractions-decimals-and-revision-notes/) first. See also the [OCR GCSE Mathematics hub](/boards/ocr/gcse/mathematics/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/ocr/gcse/mathematics/), and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/). Where a question says "by hand", show your working without a calculator.

How to use the set: attempt each question on paper before you read the answer. Mark your own working against the numbered steps, and give yourself a mark only where the step is written down. Then note which type of question cost you marks (conversion, percentage of a quantity, multipliers, percentage change, original value, simple interest, ordering or symbols) and revisit that section of the study guide. Questions 5, 6 and 11 need an original-value method, and question 10 needs careful attention to which amount each percentage refers to.

## Questions

**1.** Express 0.28 as a percentage and as a fraction in lowest terms. **[2]**

**2.** By hand:

**(a)** Find 12% of £350. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 63 out of 90 as a percentage. **[2]**

**3.** A concert ticket costs £45.

**(a)** The price rises by 8%. Find the new price. **[2]**
**(b)** A different price falls by 7.5%. Write down the decimal multiplier. **[1]**

**4.** A phone costs £320 in one shop and is reduced to £272. Find the percentage reduction. **[3]**

**5.** A camera is sold for £61.20 after a reduction of 32%. Find the original price. **[3]**

**6.** After a 4% increase, a monthly rent is £728. Find the rent before the increase. **[3]**

**7.** Leo invests £2,400 at 2.5% simple interest per year.

**(a)** Find the interest after 6 years. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the total amount after 6 years. **[1]**
**(c)** Find how many years it takes to earn £450 interest. **[2]**

**8.** By hand, write these in order, smallest first: 0.52, 5/9, 53%, 11/20, −0.6 **[3]**

**9.** 

**(a)** Insert < , > or = : 7/8 ... 0.87 **[1]**
**(b)** True or false: 18/25 ≠ 72%. Give a reason. **[1]**
**(c)** Insert < , > or = : −1.4 ... −1.04 **[1]**
**(d)** n is an integer and −3 < n ≤ 2. List all possible values of n. **[2]**

**10.** A bike costs £480. In a sale it is reduced by 20%. Later the sale price is increased by 20%.

**(a)** Find the sale price. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the price after the increase. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the overall percentage change from £480. **[3]**

**11.** An account pays 3.2% simple interest per year. Tara leaves her deposit in it for 5 years and then has £2,030 in total.

**(a)** Find her deposit. **[3]**
**(b)** Her friend invests £1,750 at 3.6% simple interest per year. Let n be the number of years. Find the smallest whole number of years for the interest to be at least £315, and write your answer as an inequality in n. **[3]**

**12.** 

**(a)** A recipe uses 280 g of flour. It is reduced by 3/8. Find the new mass. **[2]**
**(b)** A mass is increased by 1/5 and is now 36 kg. Find the original mass. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 0.28 × 100 = **28%** [1]. 28/100 = **7/25** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the fraction fully simplified; 28/100 is not in lowest terms, so cancel down and check the denominator.

**2. (a)** 10% of 350 = 35 and 1% = 3.5, so 2% = 7 [1]. 12% = 35 + 7 = **£42** [1]
**(b)** 63/90 [1] × 100 = **70%** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Put the part on top and the whole underneath. Writing 90/63 first gives a percentage over 100, which is a signal to turn the fraction over.

**3. (a)** Multiplier = 1.08 [1]. 45 × 1.08 = **£48.60** [1]
**(b)** 1 − 0.075 = **0.925** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the money answer to two decimal places. £48.6 reads as incomplete, and a clear multiplier shows your method.

**4.** Reduction = 320 − 272 = 48 [1]. 48/320 × 100 [1] = **15% decrease** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original price, £320, not the sale price. Naming the answer as a decrease is good practice.

**5.** Multiplier = 1 − 0.32 = 0.68 [1]. Original = 61.20 ÷ 0.68 [1] = **£90** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* This is an original-value question, so divide by the multiplier. Check forwards: 90 × 0.68 = 61.20. Adding 32% of £61.20 is a common wrong route.

**6.** Multiplier = 1.04 [1]. Original = 728 ÷ 1.04 [1] = **£700** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A forward check (700 × 1.04 = 728) costs a few seconds and exposes the slip of taking 4% off £728.

**7. (a)** Interest per year = 2400 × 2.5/100 = £60 [1]. Over 6 years = 6 × 60 = **£360** [1]
**(b)** 2400 + 360 = **£2,760** [1]
**(c)** 450 ÷ 60 [1] = **7.5 years** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Simple interest is the same each year, so work out one year first. Showing that figure gives you something to build the later parts on.

**8.** 5/9 = 0.556 (3 d.p.), 11/20 = 0.55, 53% = 0.53, 0.52, −0.6 [1]. Order of decimals: −0.6, 0.52, 0.53, 0.55, 0.556 [1]. **−0.6, 0.52, 53%, 11/20, 5/9** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write the final answer in the original forms. Giving only converted decimals risks losing the last mark, and writing −0.6 last is the usual slip with negatives.

**9. (a)** 7/8 = 0.875 [1], so **7/8 > 0.87**
**(b)** 18/25 = 0.72 = 72%, so the statement is **false** [1] because the two are equal
**(c)** −1.4 is further below zero, so **−1.4 < −1.04** [1]
**(d)** −3 is excluded and 2 is included [1]. **−2, −1, 0, 1, 2** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (d), read the symbols one end at a time. Writing −3 in the list, or leaving out 2, is the typical way to drop a mark.

**10. (a)** Multiplier 0.8 [1]. 480 × 0.8 = **£384** [1]
**(b)** 384 × 1.2 = **£460.80** [1]
**(c)** Change = 480 − 460.80 = 19.20 [1]. 19.20/480 × 100 [1] = **4% decrease** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The second change is a percentage of the sale price, not the original. It is tempting to say there is no overall change, so do the working to see the true result.

**11. (a)** Interest rate over 5 years = 5 × 3.2% = 16% [1]. Multiplier = 1.16 [1]. Deposit = 2030 ÷ 1.16 = **£1,750** [1]
**(b)** Interest per year = 1750 × 3.6/100 = £63 [1]. 315 ÷ 63 = 5 [1]. **n ≥ 5** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A question that asks for an inequality wants the inequality, so a bare "5 years" may not be enough. In part (a), dividing by the multiplier is the original-value step.

**12. (a)** Multiplier = 1 − 3/8 = 5/8 [1]. 280 × 5/8 = **175 g** [1]
**(b)** Multiplier = 1 + 1/5 = 6/5 [1]. 36 ÷ 6/5 = 36 × 5/6 = **30 kg** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by a fraction means multiplying by its reciprocal. Show that line so a slip in the arithmetic does not hide a correct method.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking the final figure as the base when finding a percentage change, rather than the starting figure.
- Using the percentage itself as the multiplier, such as 0.32 for a 32% decrease.
- Multiplying instead of dividing in original-value questions.
- Applying a second percentage to the original amount rather than the changed amount.
- Leaving out the zero in money answers, such as £48.6.
- Giving converted decimals when the question asks for the original forms in order.
- Putting negative numbers the wrong way round, or reading −0.6 as larger than 0.52.
- Reading ≤ and < as the same, and so including or excluding the wrong end value.
- Forgetting to say whether a percentage change is an increase or a decrease.
- Not setting out interest per year first in simple interest questions.
