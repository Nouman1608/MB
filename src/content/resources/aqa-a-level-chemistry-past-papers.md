This page is a guide to AQA A Level Chemistry (7405) past papers for the June 2026, June 2025 and June 2024 series. It gives the grade thresholds we could copy from AQA's own grade-boundary document, and a short account of what examiners reported, written by us from AQA's Reports on the Examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A Level Chemistry (7405) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/chemistry/a-level/chemistry-7405)
- [AQA A Level Chemistry (7405) specification](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/chemistry/a-level/chemistry-7405/specification)
- [AQA A Level Chemistry (7405) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/chemistry/a-level/chemistry-7405/assessment-resources)
- [AQA past-paper finder](https://www.aqa.org.uk/find-past-papers-and-mark-schemes)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries)

Use the three documents together. Sit a paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. 7405 is a linear, untiered qualification, first taught from September 2015 and first assessed in June 2017, with three two-hour written papers: Paper 1 (105 marks, 35%), Paper 2 (105 marks, 35%) and Paper 3 (90 marks, 30%), 300 marks in all. Paper 3 combines practical techniques and data analysis, questions across the whole specification and multiple choice. All three series on this page were set on this specification. Papers from before June 2017 belong to the earlier A Level and should not be mixed in.

## June 2025

The June 2025 reports pick out electrode potentials and cells as a poorly understood area on Paper 1, and balancing equations and writing state symbols clearly as recurring problems. Paper 2 is described as testing skeletal formulae, practical reasoning and gas-volume calculations. Paper 3 was judged to discriminate well, with careless reading of the question holding back many otherwise capable answers. Better answers set calculations out step by step, said what each number stood for, and applied the information given. Weaker answers repeated the question, stopped at a vague idea or left equations unbalanced.

AQA's report for this series does not include grade thresholds, and we could not retrieve a June 2025 grade-boundary document, so none are given here.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, electrochemistry and fuel cells: half-equations that were not balanced, explanations that missed the role of hydroxide ions, and confusion between a fuel cell and a rechargeable cell.
- Paper 1, transition metals: colour explained without saying visible light is absorbed or why the energy gap exists, and weak equations for ligand substitution and complex formation.
- Paper 1, calculations: titre values averaged without checking which were concordant, a missed final division, rounding too early, and buffer questions where the equilibrium was not written out.
- Paper 1, periodicity, bonding and thermodynamics: explanations of ionisation energy and boiling point mixed up, standard-state conditions given with extra detail, and equilibrium shifts stated without opposing the change.
- Paper 2, organic mechanisms and isomerism: skeletal formulae avoided, intermediates drawn for the minor product, the stereoisomer question left unattempted, and arrows drawn where a bond breaks without care over direction.
- Paper 2, practical organic chemistry: recrystallisation steps incomplete, melting-point apparatus faults not spotted, and sustainability judged without mentioning renewable sources.
- Paper 2, calculations and spectroscopy: unclear layout of working, uncertainty in a balance reading not doubled, gas-volume ratios not used, and splitting patterns in proton NMR overlooked.
- Paper 3, kinetics and uncertainty: control variables repeated from the question, rate and time confused, and a way of reducing percentage uncertainty named without a workable method.
- Paper 3, cells and equilibria: the overall cell reaction written the wrong way round, half-equations drawn from the wrong species, and ICE-style working left unorganised.
- Paper 3, organic practical: oxidation equations missing water, an infrared answer about the wrong compounds, and reflux apparatus drawn with a thermometer.

## June 2024

In June 2024, Paper 1 examiners noted that many answers were too loose to score: definitions that omitted the standard atom, solutions that were not saturated, and electrode potentials attributed to the wrong species. Paper 2 mixed familiar topics such as rates, equilibrium, amines and benzene with less usual contexts. Paper 3 repeated the theme that lack of precision, in working and in explanations, was the most common reason for lost marks. Better answers named the specific species or equilibrium involved and gave a reason. Weaker answers restated the question or offered a general comment.

We could not retrieve a June 2024 grade-boundary document, so thresholds for this series are not given.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1, atomic structure and titration: relative atomic mass definitions missing the mean mass or the standard atom, and no recognition that carbon dioxide in air affects a standing alkali solution.
- Paper 1, transition metals and cells: chelate ligands drawn with missing atoms, the stability gain from chelation not recalled, and electrode potentials compared for the wrong species.
- Paper 1, p-block and thermodynamics: chlorine equations unbalanced, shapes with the wrong number of lone pairs, bond-angle reasoning incomplete, and Born-Haber cycles built with the wrong number of moles.
- Paper 1, acids and equilibrium: incorrect expressions for the ionic product of water, lost marks for decimal places, and partial-pressure expressions written with square brackets.
- Paper 2, kinetics and equilibrium: rate curves with the wrong shape for the order, a rate-determining step not linked to the rate equation, and amounts not converted to concentrations.
- Paper 2, hydrocarbons and spectroscopy: mole ratios not translated into gas volumes, carbon-13 peaks not matched to the number of environments, and fragments from the data booklet copied unclearly.
- Paper 2, organic practical and tests: esters drawn wrongly, reflux explanations without the reason for a precaution, drying agents and purity checks not recalled, and acid chloride tests not known.
- Paper 2, amines and aromatics: nitrile reduction products drawn with missing atoms, amine base strength explained loosely, aromatic amines described as dyes rather than as dye precursors, and mistakes in the intermediate of electrophilic substitution.
- Paper 3, practical technique: apparatus diagrams that were not cross-sectional, residue and filtrate confused in a purification, and reasons for choosing cotton wool left unexplained.
- Paper 3, kinetics, buffers and colour: concentration claimed for a solid in excess, the Arrhenius plot misread, buffer explanations that did not name the equilibrium, and the origin of colour left vague.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A Level Chemistry 7405 past papers?**
AQA publishes papers, mark schemes and reports on the assessment resources page for 7405 and through its past-paper finder. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page.

**Which papers should I sit when practising a series?**
All three: Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3. 7405 is a linear qualification, so every candidate sits the same three papers in the same series, and the grade comes from the total mark out of 300.

**Which specification were these papers set on?**
All three series here were set on the 7405 specification first taught from September 2015, with first assessment in June 2017. Check the front cover of any older paper against the current specification before relying on a question.

**Why are there no thresholds for June 2025 or June 2024?**
We could only copy thresholds from a grade-boundary document we could retrieve, and that was the June 2026 one. We have left the other two series blank rather than estimate any figure.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
