| 3.2.4 Exchange rates | Effect on profit and sales of importers and exporters; severe fluctuations |

## Definitions

- Rate of interest: the price of borrowing money, shown as a percentage of the amount borrowed each year.
- Loan: a fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest over an agreed period.
- Overdraft: borrowing through a bank account by spending more than the balance, up to an agreed limit; interest is charged on the amount overdrawn.
- Level of employment: how many people who want to work have jobs.
- Consumer spending: the total that households spend on goods and services.
- Globalisation: the growing links between countries through trade, investment, people and communication.
- Exchange rate: the price of one currency in terms of another.
- Relative cost advantage: being able to produce at lower cost than other countries or firms.
- Sharia compliant finance: finance that does not charge or pay interest. The specification names only "Sharia compliant alternatives"; leasing, profit sharing and agreed-price sales are our own general description of how it can work.

## Interest rates: method in steps

1. Say whether the business borrows and whether the rate is variable or fixed.
2. Direct channel: rate up, repayments up, costs up, profit down.
3. Indirect channel: rate up, household and business borrowing costs up, spending down, sales down.
4. Say which channel is bigger for this business and why.
5. Reverse the whole chain for a rate fall.

Small reminder: Ixworth Cycles has a £50,000 variable-rate loan. The rate rises from 4% to 6%. Annual interest goes from £2,000 to £3,000, an extra £1,000 a year.

Sharia compliant alternatives: say what they avoid (interest), name one structure (leasing, profit share or an agreed-price sale), and say that the business may be less exposed to a rate change (our own reasoning, not a specification statement) but still has payments to make.

## Employment: cause and effect

| Employment | Demand | Recruitment and pay | Likely winners |
|---|---|---|---|
| Rises | Up, as more households have wages | Harder to hire; wages may rise | Businesses selling non-essentials |
| Falls | Down, as incomes drop | Easier to hire; less pay pressure | Businesses selling cheaper alternatives |

Always add one sentence on the business type. A recruitment agency is affected differently from a restaurant.

## Consumer spending and incomes

- Income up: more spending, especially on products people can postpone. A business may need more stock, staff and space.
- Income down: luxuries and postponable items fall most; necessities change little; cheaper alternatives may rise.
- A mixed range spreads the risk. A single-product business is exposed.

Small reminder: sales of 2,000 units at £12 give £24,000. A 25% fall in volume leaves £18,000, a fall of £6,000.

## Forms of globalisation

| Form | One-line business effect |
|---|---|
| Specialisation by country | Low-cost volume production in some places; imports of the rest |
| Relative cost advantage | Production moves to the lower-cost site |
| Movement of labour | Wider choice of staff; skills may leave a country |
| Larger markets | More customers, scope for economies of scale |
| International competition | Pressure on prices and market share |

## Benefits and drawbacks grid

| | Benefit | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Developed-country business | Bigger markets, cheaper inputs | Low-cost rivals, jobs move abroad |
| Developed-country economy | Cheaper goods, export markets | Some industries decline |
| Developing-country business | Export orders, investment, skills | Strong multinational rivals, dependence on few buyers |
| Developing-country economy | Jobs, tax, technology | Profits leave, local firms close, skilled workers leave |

Method: pick one box, name a benefit or drawback, add "so" twice to show the chain, then say how big it is for the business in the stem.

## Exchange rates: the direction table

| Home currency | Exporter | Importer |
|---|---|---|
| Stronger | Overseas prices higher, sales may fall | Inputs cheaper, costs may fall |
| Weaker | Overseas prices lower, sales may rise | Inputs dearer, costs rise, profit squeezed |

A firm that does both imports and exports has two effects pulling against each other. Weigh them: which is larger depends on how much of its cost is imported and how responsive its overseas buyers are to price.

Severe fluctuations: unpredictable costs, difficulty setting prices, uncertain profit, harder investment decisions. Responses: agree prices in advance, use home suppliers, sell in several countries.

## Routine for a longer answer

For a question that asks you to analyse, evaluate or justify, use five moves.

1. Name the influence and tie it to the business in the stem (its borrowing, its customers, its imports or exports).
2. Give the first effect and explain the mechanism in one sentence: what changes, and how that reaches cost, price, sales or profit.
3. Give a second effect, ideally through the other channel, or the opposite side of the trade (exporter against importer, business against economy).
4. Compare the two: which is larger, quicker or more certain for this business, and why.
5. Finish with a judgement that answers the question set, such as whether to expand, borrow, price higher or source locally. Add one condition that would change your view.

A judgement that could be pasted onto any other business has not used the case.

## Must-know distinctions

- Direct channel (own borrowing costs) versus indirect channel (customer spending).
- Exporter versus importer: a weak currency helps one and hurts the other.
- Businesses versus economies: a benefit to a business is not automatically a benefit to the country.
- Developed versus developing: the same trend can be an opportunity in one and a threat in the other.
- Certain effect versus possible effect: a repayment rise is certain for a variable loan; a sales fall is only possible.

## Quick self-test

1. State two forms of finance whose cost is affected by a change in interest rates.
2. Ixworth Cycles borrows £50,000 and the rate rises from 4% to 6%. What is the extra interest per year?
3. Name two ways a rise in the rate of interest could reduce spending.
4. Give one reason why high employment could be a problem for a business.
5. A product earns £24,000 and sales volume falls by 25%. What is the new revenue if the price is unchanged?
6. Name three of the five forms of globalisation listed in the specification.
7. Give one drawback of globalisation for a developing-country economy.
8. The home currency weakens. Who benefits more directly, an exporter or an importer?
9. A home currency strengthens. What may happen to an exporter's overseas sales?
10. State one effect of a severe exchange rate fluctuation on a business.
11. Explain why a Sharia compliant alternative may reduce exposure to interest rates.

### Answers

1. Loans and overdrafts.
2. £1,000 (interest £2,000 rises to £3,000).
3. Higher household repayments leave less to spend; businesses delay buying equipment. Saving also becomes more attractive.
4. It is harder to recruit staff, and wage costs may rise.
5. £18,000.
6. Any three of: specialisation by country, relative cost advantage, movement of labour, larger markets, international competition.
7. Profits may go abroad; local firms may close; skilled workers may leave.
8. The exporter, because overseas prices fall.
9. They may fall, because the product costs more in overseas currency.
10. Costs or revenues become unpredictable, so prices and profit are hard to plan.
11. The cost is agreed in advance or linked to the business's results, not to the interest rate, so the business may be less exposed (it still has payments to make).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a factor without a chain of reasoning to cost or revenue.
- Giving only the direct channel for interest rates.
- Writing that a weak currency is bad for everyone.
- Writing about the country when the stem asks about the business.
- Listing benefits and drawbacks without judging which matters more to the business described.
- Treating Sharia compliant finance as free or as having no payments.
- Attempting currency conversions, which are not required.
- Forgetting that a variable-rate loan responds at once, while a fixed-rate loan does not.
