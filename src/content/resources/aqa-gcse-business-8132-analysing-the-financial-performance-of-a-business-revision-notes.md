## Core recall

### Purpose of financial statements

To assess performance and make decisions. Users: owners and shareholders, managers, banks and lenders, suppliers, employees.

### Income statement (over a period)

Sales revenue
− Cost of sales
= **Gross profit**
− Expenses
= **Net profit**

| Item | Goes under |
|---|---|
| Stock bought for resale, materials | Cost of sales |
| Rent, salaries, marketing, insurance, utilities | Expenses |

### Statement of financial position (on one date)

| Section | Examples |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Premises, machinery, vehicles |
| Current assets | Inventories, receivables, cash |
| Current liabilities | Payables, overdraft |
| Non-current liabilities | Long-term loans, mortgages |
| Net assets | Total assets − total liabilities |
| Total equity | Share capital + retained profit (equals net assets) |

**Assets** = what the business owns or is owed. **Liabilities** = what it owes.

Memory hook: re**C**eivables = **C**ustomers owe us (asset). pa**Y**ables = **Y**ou owe suppliers (liability).

### Margins

Gross profit margin = (gross profit ÷ sales revenue) × 100

Net profit margin = (net profit ÷ sales revenue) × 100

Formulae are not given in the exam: learn them.

**Reading margins:**

- Gross margin falls → cost of sales rising faster than revenue, or prices cut.
- Net margin falls while gross margin holds → expenses rising.
- Net margin always lower than gross margin (expenses come out).

### Four ways to judge performance

1. **Current performance:** are margins and profit healthy?
2. **Against previous years:** what is the trend?
3. **Against competitors:** better or worse than rivals?
4. **Stakeholder perspectives:** owners, lenders, suppliers, employees each look for different things.

| Stakeholder | Looks for |
|---|---|
| Owners | Profit and growth in profit |
| Banks | Ability to repay; assets as security |
| Suppliers | Ability to pay on time |
| Employees | Job security; scope for pay rises |

## Quick worked reminder

Sales revenue £120,000, cost of sales £84,000, expenses £27,000.

- Gross profit = £120,000 − £84,000 = £36,000. Gross margin = £36,000 ÷ £120,000 × 100 = **30%**.
- Net profit = £36,000 − £27,000 = £9,000. Net margin = £9,000 ÷ £120,000 × 100 = **7.5%**.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Income statement | A statement of revenue, costs and profit over a period |
| Statement of financial position | A statement of assets, liabilities and equity on a particular date |
| Cost of sales | The direct cost of the goods or services sold |
| Gross profit | Sales revenue minus cost of sales |
| Expenses | Running costs other than cost of sales, such as rent and salaries |
| Net profit | Gross profit minus expenses |
| Asset | Something a business owns or is owed that has value |
| Liability | A debt the business owes |
| Receivables | Money owed to the business by customers |
| Payables | Money the business owes to suppliers |
| Gross profit margin | Gross profit as a percentage of sales revenue |
| Net profit margin | Net profit as a percentage of sales revenue |

## Exam traps

- Divide by sales revenue for both margins.
- Rising revenue does not mean better performance; check margins.
- The statement of financial position is a snapshot on one date.
- Receivables are assets; payables are liabilities.
- Judging needs a comparison: previous years, competitors or stakeholders.
- Say whose perspective you are giving.

## Self-test

1. State the purpose of financial statements.
2. Which statement shows profit over a period?
3. Name two current assets.
4. Is an overdraft a current or non-current liability?
5. Define gross profit.
6. Sales revenue is £340,000 and gross profit £85,000. Calculate the gross profit margin.
7. With the same revenue, net profit is £17,000. Calculate the net profit margin.
8. A business's gross margin stays at 35% but its net margin falls. What has happened?
9. State the four ways of judging performance named in the specification.
10. Why might a bank look at the statement of financial position before lending?
11. Revenue rose by 15% but net profit fell. Give one possible reason.
12. What does net assets equal?

### Answers

1. To assess the business's performance and help people make decisions.
2. The income statement.
3. Any two of: inventories, receivables, cash.
4. Current, because it is due within a year.
5. Sales revenue minus cost of sales.
6. £85,000 ÷ £340,000 × 100 = **25%**.
7. £17,000 ÷ £340,000 × 100 = **5%**.
8. Expenses have risen as a share of revenue.
9. Current performance, against previous years, against competitors, from the perspective of a range of stakeholders.
10. To see what the business owns and owes, whether it already has large debts, and what assets could act as security.
11. For example, cost of sales or expenses rose faster than revenue, or prices were cut to win sales.
12. Total equity: share capital plus retained profit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is the statement of financial position the same as a balance sheet?**
It is the term the specification uses for the statement showing assets, liabilities and equity on one date, which older books call a balance sheet.

**Why compare margins rather than profit alone?**
Margins show how much profit each pound of sales produces, so they allow fair comparisons between years and with competitors of different sizes.
