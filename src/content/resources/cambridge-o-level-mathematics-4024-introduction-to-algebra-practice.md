These questions cover subtopic 2.1 Introduction to algebra of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025–2027: using letters for generalised numbers and substituting into expressions and formulas. Questions 1 to 7 and 10 to 13 can be done without a calculator; Questions 8 and 9 need one. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.

## Section A

**1.** n is an integer. Write down an expression for:

**(a)** a number that is 3 more than a multiple of 5 **[1]**

**(b)** the next even number after 2n **[1]**

**2.** Given that a = 5 and b = −3, work out:

**(a)** ab **[1]**

**(b)** a² + b² **[1]**

**(c)** a − 2b **[1]**

**3.** Given that x = −2, work out:

**(a)** 3x² **[1]**

**(b)** −x² + x **[1]**

**(c)** x³ − 2x **[1]**

**4.** Given that p = ½ and q = −4, work out:

**(a)** pq **[1]**

**(b)** q ÷ p **[1]**

**(c)** q² − 8p **[1]**

**5.** Work out the value of (m + n)/(m − n) when m = 7 and n = −3. Give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form. **[2]**

**6.** A student says that when a = −3, the value of −a² + 2a is 3. Work out the correct value and explain the student's error. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** The formula F = 9C/5 + 32 converts C degrees Celsius to F degrees Fahrenheit.

**(a)** Find F when C = 35. **[2]**

**(b)** Find F when C = −15. **[2]**

**8.** The volume V of a sphere of radius r is V = (4/3)πr³. Find V when r = 3.2. **[2]**

**9.** The formula T = 2π√(l/g) gives the time T seconds for one swing of a pendulum of length l metres. Find T when l = 1.5 and g = 9.81. **[2]**

**10.** The distance s metres travelled by an object is given by s = ut + ½at². Find s when u = 8, a = −2 and t = 6. **[3]**

**11.** A shop sells notebooks at x dollars each and pens at y dollars each.

**(a)** Write down an expression for the cost, in dollars, of 4 notebooks and 7 pens. **[1]**

**(b)** Work out this cost when x = 2.35 and y = 0.80. **[2]**

**(c)** Write down an expression for the change, in dollars, from a $20 note when buying 4 notebooks and 7 pens. **[1]**

**12.** Show that the sum of any three consecutive even numbers is a multiple of 6. **[3]**

**13.** n is a positive integer. Explain why n(n + 1) is always even. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **5n + 3** [1]

**(b)** **2n + 2** [1] **[2]**

**2. (a)** 5 × (−3) = **−15** [1]

**(b)** 25 + 9 = **34** [1]

**(c)** 5 − 2(−3) = 5 + 6 = **11** [1] **[3]**

**3. (a)** 3(−2)² = 3 × 4 = **12** [1]

**(b)** −(−2)² + (−2) = −4 − 2 = **−6** [1]

**(c)** (−2)³ − 2(−2) = −8 + 4 = **−4** [1] **[3]**

**4. (a)** ½ × (−4) = **−2** [1]

**(b)** −4 ÷ ½ = −4 × 2 = **−8** [1]

**(c)** (−4)² − 8(½) = 16 − 4 = **12** [1] **[3]**

**5.** (7 + (−3)) ÷ (7 − (−3)) = 4 ÷ 10 [1] = **2/5** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 7 − (−3) = 10, not 4. Brackets around −3 prevent the slip.

**6.** −(−3)² + 2(−3) = −9 − 6 [1] = **−15** [1]. The student squared −3 to get 9 but then ignored the minus sign in front of a²: −a² means −(a²) = −9 [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** F = 9(35)/5 + 32 = 63 + 32 [1] = **95** [1]

**(b)** F = 9(−15)/5 + 32 = −27 + 32 [1] = **5** [1] **[4]**

**8.** V = (4/3) × π × 3.2³ = (4/3) × π × 32.768 [1] = 137.25... = **137** (3 s.f.) [1] **[2]**

**9.** T = 2π√(1.5 ÷ 9.81) = 2π × 0.39103... [1] = 2.4569... = **2.46** (3 s.f.) [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded square root in the calculator. Rounding it to 0.39 gives 2.45, which loses the accuracy mark.

**10.** s = (8)(6) + ½(−2)(6)² [1] = 48 + ½(−2)(36) = 48 − 36 [1] = **12** [1] **[3]**

**11. (a)** **4x + 7y** [1]

**(b)** 4(2.35) + 7(0.80) = 9.40 + 5.60 [1] = **$15.00** [1]

**(c)** **20 − 4x − 7y** (or 20 − (4x + 7y)) [1] **[4]**

**12.** Let the numbers be 2n, 2n + 2 and 2n + 4 [1]. Sum = 6n + 6 [1] = 6(n + 1), which is 6 times an integer, so it is a multiple of 6 [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* Showing 2 + 4 + 6 = 12 is only one case and earns no marks for a "show that".

**13.** n and n + 1 are consecutive integers, so one of them must be even [1]. The product of any integer with an even number is even, so n(n + 1) is always even [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing −a² as +9 when a = −3 (Question 6).
- Subtracting a negative incorrectly in a denominator (Question 5).
- Rounding a square root before multiplying by 2π (Question 9).
- Writing 4x + 7y − 20 for the change, which is the wrong way round (Question 11).
- Proving a general result with examples instead of algebra (Question 12).
- Giving 2n + 1 for "the next even number": that is odd.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is substitution tested without a calculator in 4024?**
Yes. Paper 1 is non-calculator, so substitution with negative numbers, fractions and powers must be done by hand. Paper 2 allows a calculator for formulas with awkward decimals.

**How accurate should a substituted answer be?**
Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise, and round only at the end.

**What counts as a proof with generalised numbers?**
Write the numbers in terms of n, simplify, and show the result has the required form, such as 6(n + 1) for a multiple of 6.
