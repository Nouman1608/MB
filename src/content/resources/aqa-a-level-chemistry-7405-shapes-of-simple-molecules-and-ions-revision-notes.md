These notes condense section **3.1.3.5 Shapes of simple molecules and ions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Papers 1 and 2 (and Paper 3). The [Shapes of simple molecules and ions study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-simple-molecules-and-ions/) explains the method with worked examples, and the [practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-shapes-of-simple-molecules-and-ions-practice/) give exam-style practice.

## What the specification asks

- Bonding pairs and lone pairs of electrons are **charge clouds that repel**.
- Pairs in the outer shell arrange themselves **as far apart as possible**.
- **Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair** repulsion.
- **Explain the shapes and bond angles** in simple molecules and ions with up to **six electron pairs** around the central atom.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bonding pair | A shared pair of electrons between two atoms |
| Lone pair | A pair of outer electrons on one atom, not used in bonding |
| Charge cloud | A region of electron density: a bonding pair (single, double or triple bond) or a lone pair |
| VSEPR | Valence shell electron pair repulsion |
| Bond angle | The angle between two bonds at an atom |
| Shape | The arrangement of **atoms** (lone pairs not named) |

## The four-step method

1. Outer electrons on the central atom.
2. Add 1 for each H or halogen; add nothing for terminal oxygen; adjust for charge (add for negative, subtract for positive).
3. Divide by 2 to get electron pairs; subtract the bonding clouds to get lone pairs.
4. Name the arrangement from the total pairs, then the shape from the atoms only.

## Arrangements

| Total pairs | Arrangement | Angle |
|---|---|---|
| 2 | Linear | 180° |
| 3 | Trigonal planar | 120° |
| 4 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° |
| 5 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90° and 120° |
| 6 | Octahedral | 90° |

| Bonding pairs | Lone pairs | Shape | Angle | Example |

| 2 | 0 | Linear | 180° | BeCl₂, CO₂ |
| 3 | 0 | Trigonal planar | 120° | BF₃, CO₃²⁻ |
| 4 | 0 | Tetrahedral | 109.5° | CH₄, NH₄⁺ |
| 3 | 1 | Pyramidal | 107° | NH₃, H₃O⁺ |
| 2 | 2 | Bent | 104.5° | H₂O |
| 5 | 0 | Trigonal bipyramidal | 90°, 120° | PCl₅ |
| 6 | 0 | Octahedral | 90° | SF₆ |
| 4 | 1 | See-saw | reduced from 90°, 120° | SF₄ |
| 3 | 2 | T-shaped | reduced from 90° | ClF₃ |
| 5 | 1 | Square pyramidal | reduced from 90° | BrF₅ |
| 4 | 2 | Square planar | 90° | XeF₄, ICl₄⁻ |

## Explaining the angle

- A lone pair is attracted by one nucleus only, so its cloud is larger and closer to the central atom. It repels more strongly than a bonding pair.
- Methane, with four equal bond pairs, has 109.5°.
- Each lone pair reduces the angle by about 2.5°: ammonia 107°, water 104.5°.
- A double bond is **one charge cloud** in counting, but it repels a little more than a single bond.

## Worked reminders

**Water.** O: 6 + 2 = 8, so 4 pairs (2 bonding, 2 lone). Tetrahedral arrangement; bent shape. Two lone pairs repel the two bond pairs more than bond pairs repel each other, so the angle is reduced to about 104.5°.

**The ammonium ion.** N: 5 + 4 − 1 = 8, so 4 pairs, all bonding. Tetrahedral, 109.5°.

**Sulfur hexafluoride.** S: 6 + 6 = 12, so 6 pairs, all bonding. Octahedral, 90°.

**ICl₄⁻.** I: 7 + 4 + 1 = 12, so 6 pairs (4 bonding, 2 lone). The lone pairs sit opposite one another, so the shape is square planar with 90° angles.

**Carbon dioxide.** C: 4, so 2 pairs; two double bonds are two clouds. Linear, 180°.

## Drawing

- Plain line: bond in the plane of the paper.
- Solid wedge: bond towards the viewer.
- Dashed line: bond away from the viewer.
- Draw lone pairs when asked and label the angle.

## Answer pattern for "explain the shape"

1. State the number of bonding pairs and lone pairs on the central atom.
2. State that the electron pairs repel and move as far apart as possible.
3. Name the shape and give the bond angle.
4. If there are lone pairs, compare the repulsions and state that the angle is reduced.

## Common mistakes

- Naming the arrangement of pairs (tetrahedral) instead of the shape of the atoms (pyramidal or bent).
- Using 109.5° for NH₃ and H₂O.
- Not adjusting the electron count for the charge on an ion.
- Forgetting that a double bond is one cloud for the shape.
- Giving only 90° or only 120° for trigonal bipyramidal PCl₅.
- Writing that lone pairs "take up more space" without comparing the repulsions.

## Self-test

1. State the order of repulsion between pairs of electrons.
2. Work out the number of electron pairs, bonding pairs and lone pairs in PCl₃.
3. Name the shape of PCl₃ and state why its bond angle is less than 109.5°.
4. Name the shape and bond angle of BF₃.
5. Name the shape of the NH₄⁺ ion and give the angle.
6. Name the shape and angle of SF₆.
7. Name the shape of XeF₄ (Xe has 8 outer electrons).
8. Explain why CO₂ is linear.
9. Name the shape of the H₃O⁺ ion.
10. State the bond angles in PCl₅.
11. What is the shape of SO₂ and why?
12. Explain why the angle in H₂O is smaller than in NH₃.

### Answers

1. Lone pair–lone pair > lone pair–bond pair > bond pair–bond pair.
2. P: 5 + 3 = 8, so 4 pairs: 3 bonding and 1 lone.
3. Pyramidal. The lone pair repels the bond pairs more than they repel each other, so the angle is less than 109.5°.
4. Trigonal planar, 120°.
5. Tetrahedral, 109.5°.
6. Octahedral, 90°.
7. Xe: 8 + 4 = 12, so 6 pairs (4 bonding, 2 lone): square planar.
8. Two double bonds count as two charge clouds with no lone pairs; they repel to opposite sides, 180°.
9. Pyramidal (O: 6 + 3 − 1 = 8, so 4 pairs, 3 bonding and 1 lone).
10. 90° and 120°.
11. Bent. S: 6, so 3 pairs; 2 bonding clouds and 1 lone pair, giving a bent shape with an angle just under 120°.
12. Water has two lone pairs and ammonia has one. More lone pair repulsion squeezes the bond pairs closer together, giving 104.5° compared with 107°.
