## The internet, intranets, extranets and the WWW

These four terms are easy to blur, so learn them as a comparison:

| | Internet | Intranet | Extranet | World Wide Web |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| What it is | Worldwide network of interconnected networks | Private network inside one organisation | Part of an intranet opened to selected outsiders | Collection of web pages and resources |
| Who can use it | Anyone with a connection | The organisation's own members | Members plus authorised outsiders, such as suppliers | Anyone using a browser on the internet |
| Access control | Public | Login; usually behind a firewall | Login with permissions limited to certain areas | Mostly public; some pages need a login |
| Content | Unregulated; any subject | Relevant to the organisation; checked internally | Information shared with partners, such as orders or stock | Text, images, video and links in web pages |

The key distinction for marks: the **internet is the network** (hardware and connections), while the **WWW is a service that runs on it**. Email is a different service that also uses the internet.

### Uses, advantages and disadvantages of the internet

People use the internet for research, communication, shopping, banking, entertainment, learning and working from home. Its advantages are the very large amount of information, the speed of access, the fact that it is available at any time from anywhere with a connection, and the range of media. Its disadvantages are that information may be unreliable or biased, users may meet inappropriate or criminal material, there is a risk of malware and fraud, and users can be overwhelmed by the quantity of results.

### Blog, forum, wiki and social networking

| Service | Who writes | Who can change content | Typical risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Blog | One author (or a small team) | Author only; readers may add comments | Personal opinion presented as fact |
| Forum | Many members posting and replying in threads | Each member edits their own posts; a **moderated** forum has someone who checks or removes posts, an **unmoderated** one does not | Unmoderated forums may contain offensive or false content |
| Wiki | Many contributors | Any authorised user can edit any page | Errors or deliberate vandalism until corrected |
| Social networking | Every user, on their own profile | Users control their own posts and privacy settings | Oversharing personal data; contact from strangers |

## Functionality

- **Internet Service Provider (ISP)**: a company that gives users access to the internet, usually for a monthly fee. ISPs may also provide email accounts, web hosting, storage and content filtering.
- **Web browser**: software that requests, receives and displays web pages and lets the user follow hyperlinks. Typical features include an address bar, back and forward buttons, bookmarks or favourites, history, tabs and a home page.
- **Hyperlink**: text or an image that, when selected, takes the user to another web page, another part of the same page, a file, or opens a new email to an address.
- **Uniform Resource Locator (URL)**: the full address of a resource on the web.

### Worked example: structure of a web address

`https://www.riverside-zoo.org.nz/visit/prices.htm`

| Part | Value | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Protocol | https:// | Data is transferred securely using HTTPS |
| Domain host | www | The resource is on the organisation's web server |
| Domain name | riverside-zoo | The organisation's registered name |
| Domain type | .org | A non-profit organisation |
| Country code | .nz | New Zealand |
| Path | /visit/ | The folder on the server |
| File name | prices.htm | The web page requested |

Exam questions often give an unfamiliar address and ask for two or three of these parts. Work from left to right and use the full stops and slashes as separators.

## Using a search engine

The syllabus names four things to consider:

1. **Speed of searching**: a search engine returns results in a fraction of a second, far quicker than looking through books.
2. **Amount of information**: it searches a vast number of pages, so almost any topic will return results; but a search may return millions of pages.
3. **Speed of finding relevant information**: this depends on the user's search technique. Precise keywords, phrases in quotation marks and operators such as AND, OR and NOT bring relevant pages to the top quickly; vague searches waste time.
4. **Ease of finding reliable information**: this is the weakness. Results are ranked by the search engine's algorithm, popularity and sometimes paid advertising, not by accuracy, so the user must check every source.

A balanced answer comparing a search engine with a printed book uses these four headings: the search engine wins on speed and quantity, but the book usually wins on reliability because it has been checked by an editor or publisher.

## Evaluating information found on the internet

The syllabus names four criteria. For each, know a practical check:

| Criterion | Question to ask | Check to make |
|---|---|---|
| Up to date | Is it current? | Look for the publication or last-updated date |
| Reliable | Can the source be trusted? | Identify the author or organisation and their expertise; check other trusted sources agree |
| Biased | Is it one-sided? | Ask whether the site is selling something or pushing one opinion; look for a balanced view |
| Valid | Is it accurate and relevant? | Look for references and evidence; check it actually answers your question |

### Worked example: comparing two sources

A student researching the health effects of energy drinks finds:

- **Source A**: a page on a drinks manufacturer's website, no date, stating that its product "boosts concentration".
- **Source B**: an article on a national health organisation's website, updated this year, with references to medical research.

*Evaluation:* Source A is likely to be **biased** because the company wants to sell its product, and with no date it may be **out of date**. Source B is more **reliable** because the organisation has medical expertise, more **up to date**, and more **valid** because its claims are supported by references. The student should use Source B and check its points against another reliable source.

## Internet protocols

A **protocol** is a set of rules for transferring data.

- **HTTP (HyperText Transfer Protocol)**: rules for transferring web pages from a web server to a browser.
- **HTTPS (HTTP secure variant)**: HTTP with the data encrypted, so passwords and payment details cannot be read if intercepted. Browsers usually show a padlock.
- **FTP (File Transfer Protocol)**: rules for transferring files between computers over a network, for example uploading the files of a website to a web server.
- **SSL (Secure Socket Layer)**: a security protocol that creates an encrypted link between the server and the client computer. HTTPS uses this encrypted link.

## Risks of using the internet

Users may come across **inappropriate material** (for example violent or adult content unsuitable for children) and **criminal material** (for example content promoting illegal activity). Access can be restricted at three levels:

| Control | Who applies it | How | Limitation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Parental | Parents at home | Filtering software, device settings, time limits | Children may use other devices or networks |
| Educational | Schools and colleges | Network filters and blocked sites, monitoring | Can block useful sites for research |
| ISP | The internet service provider | Filters content before it reaches customers | Applies to everyone; raises concerns about censorship |

Discussion questions often expect the debate: restrictions protect young or vulnerable users, but heavy filtering can limit access to legitimate information and freedom of expression.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the internet and the WWW are the same thing.
- Calling an extranet "a public intranet". It is restricted to authorised outsiders.
- Saying a search engine finds "reliable" information. It finds matching pages; the user judges reliability.
- Describing SSL and HTTPS as identical. HTTPS is the web protocol; SSL creates the encrypted link it uses.
- Giving a domain type such as .gov as proof of reliability. It is a clue, not a guarantee.

## Exam technique

**Identify** parts of a URL by quoting them exactly. **Describe** a difference with both sides joined by "whereas". **Evaluate** information by applying the four criteria to the source in the question and ending with a judgement. **Discuss** restrictions by giving benefits, drawbacks and a conclusion.

## Check your understanding

1. Who can use an extranet? *The organisation's members and authorised outsiders such as suppliers.*
2. Give one difference between a moderated and an unmoderated forum. *In a moderated forum someone checks or removes posts; in an unmoderated one nobody does.*
3. What does FTP do? *Transfers files between computers, such as uploading a website to a server.*
4. Name the three levels at which internet access can be restricted. *Parental, educational and ISP.*
