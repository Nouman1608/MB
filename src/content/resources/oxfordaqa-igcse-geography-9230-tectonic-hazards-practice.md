## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the epicentre of an earthquake. **[1]**

**2.** Name the type of plate margin where plates slide past each other. **[1]**

**3.** Give **one** primary effect and **one** secondary effect of an earthquake. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** methods used to monitor a volcano. **[2]**

**5.** Give **two** reasons why people live close to active volcanoes. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe the global distribution of earthquakes. **[3]**

**7.** Explain what happens at a destructive plate margin to cause a powerful earthquake. **[4]**

**8.** The table shows data for two invented earthquakes. Both had a magnitude of 6.8 and occurred at a depth of 10 km.

| | Earthquake X | Earthquake Y |
|---|---|---|
| Deaths | 12 | 3 400 |
| Injured | 190 | 11 000 |
| Homes destroyed | 800 | 60 000 |
| Time of day | 14:00 | 03:30 |
| Mean income of country | High | Low |

Suggest **three** reasons for the differences in the effects of the two earthquakes. **[6]**

**9.** For **one** earthquake or volcanic eruption that you have studied, explain how the responses in the first week differed from the responses over the following years. Name the event. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why earthquakes cannot be predicted but some volcanic eruptions can be forecast. **[3]**

**11.** Explain how the design of a building can reduce the risk to people in an earthquake. **[4]**

**12.** Explain why constructive plate margins produce volcanoes that are less explosive than those at destructive margins. **[4]**

**13.** "Planning is more effective than prediction in reducing the effects of tectonic hazards." To what extent do you agree? **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** The point on the Earth's surface directly above the focus (where the earthquake starts) [1]. **[1]**

**2.** Conservative margin (accept transform margin) [1]. **[1]**

**3.** Primary, for example: building collapse; ground shaking; deaths [1]. Secondary, for example: fires; landslides; tsunami; disease; homelessness [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Any two from: seismometers measuring small earthquakes; measuring gas emissions; tiltmeters or satellites measuring ground swelling; temperature measurements [1 each]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two from: fertile soils; geothermal energy; tourism; jobs in mining or services; family ties; cannot afford to leave; low perceived risk [1 each]. **[2]**

**6.** Earthquakes occur in narrow belts along plate margins [1]. Many surround the Pacific Ocean in the "Ring of Fire", for example Japan, the Andes and western North America [1]. Others lie along the mid-ocean ridges and the Mediterranean to Himalaya belt [1]. Few occur in the middle of plates [1]. Maximum 3. **[3]**

**7.** One plate, usually the denser oceanic plate, is forced beneath another (subduction) [1]. The plates stick because of friction, so pressure builds [1]. When the pressure exceeds the friction, the plates suddenly jerk free [1], releasing energy as shock waves from the focus [1]. Greater pressure and large areas of contact can make the earthquake powerful [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**8.** Any three reasons with development, 2 marks each, linked to the data: Earthquake Y hit a low-income country, so buildings were probably weaker and collapsed more, causing more deaths and 60 000 destroyed homes [2]; it struck at 03:30 when people were asleep indoors, so more were trapped, whereas at 14:00 many might have been outdoors or in stronger buildings [2]; the high-income country is likely to have better emergency services and hospitals, so more of the 190 injured survived [2]; higher income allows stricter building codes and better planning and education [2]; population density may have been higher near Earthquake Y [2]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

**9.** Named event [1]. First week, such as search and rescue, emergency shelter, food and water, medical treatment [1]. Following years, such as rebuilding homes and infrastructure, restoring jobs, new building codes [1]. A comparison of the two, showing that the focus moved from saving lives to rebuilding and reducing future risk [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Earthquakes occur when stress is suddenly released and there are no reliable warning signs [1], so scientists can only estimate long-term probabilities for a region [1]. Before eruptions there are signs such as gas, swelling of the volcano and small earthquakes that can be monitored [1]. **[3]**

**11.** A steel frame or reinforced concrete flexes rather than snaps, so the building is less likely to collapse [1]. Cross-bracing resists sideways movement [1]. Base isolators such as rubber pads absorb shaking [1]. A lighter roof causes fewer injuries if it falls, and automatic gas shut-off reduces the risk of fire [1]. **[4]**

**12.** At constructive margins plates move apart and magma rises directly from the mantle [1]. This magma is runny and low in gas, so gas escapes easily and lava flows gently [1]. At destructive margins the magma is thick and sticky because it contains melted crust [1], so gas becomes trapped and pressure builds until it erupts explosively [1]. **[4]**

**13.** Indicative content:
- Agree: planning, for example hazard maps, land-use zoning and drills, reduces vulnerability for every event, including earthquakes [1].
- Agree: building codes and protection are effective because they work without warning [1].
- Agree: earthquakes cannot be predicted, so prediction can only help for some volcanic eruptions [1].
- Disagree: where accurate prediction is possible, evacuation can save everyone in an area [1].
- Disagree: planning is expensive and needs strong governance, so it may not be affordable in poorer countries [1].
- Judgement: planning and protection are more widely effective, but prediction and monitoring are important for volcanoes; the best approach combines them, with a justified conclusion [1]. Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing effects when the question asks for responses.
- Not using the data in Question 8, or giving reasons with no link to the numbers.
- Naming no event in an answer that asks for a named example.
- Saying that volcanoes happen at conservative margins.
- Giving prediction methods for earthquakes.
- Judgements in "to what extent" questions that are not justified.
