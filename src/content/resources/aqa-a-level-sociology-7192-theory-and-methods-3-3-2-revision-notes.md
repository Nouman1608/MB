same list appears as 3.1.3 in Paper 1; the methods side is condensed in the

## Condensed recall

**Two axes for every theory**
- Consensus ↔ conflict: shared values or competing interests?
- Structure ↔ action: society shapes people, or people create society?
- Functionalism = structural + consensus. Marxism and most feminism = structural + conflict.
  Interactionism, phenomenology, ethnomethodology = social action. Giddens' structuration bridges
  structure and action through the duality of structure.

**Crime as the test case**
- Durkheim: crime is normal; punishment reaffirms boundaries. Merton (1938): strain between goals
  and legitimate means.
- Marxism: law and enforcement serve the ruling class; *The New Criminology* (1973) added meanings.
- Becker (*Outsiders*, 1963): deviance is created by labelling. Cicourel (1968): typifications shape
  who becomes an official delinquent.
- Heidensohn: women's conformity reflects tighter social control.

**Modernity → post-modernity → late modernity**
- Modernity: reason, science, progress, capitalism, nation state, class identities.
- Postmodernity: Lyotard (meta-narratives rejected), Baudrillard (hyperreality), fluid identities.
- Late modernity: Giddens (reflexivity), Beck (risk), Jock Young (*The Exclusive Society*, 1999).
- Marxist reply: Harvey (1989), post-modern culture reflects flexible capitalism.

**Science in five steps**
1. Inductivism: observation → pattern → law (Durkheim's *Suicide*).
2. Popper: falsification; the black-swan problem; Marxism criticised as made unfalsifiable.
3. Kuhn: normal science → anomalies → crisis → revolution; sociology lacks one paradigm.
4. Realism: open systems; science explains underlying causes.
5. Interpretivism: meaningful action is not governed by laws.

**Values**
- Comte, Durkheim: objectivity possible and desirable.
- Weber: value relevance in topic and interpretation; objectivity in collection.
- Gouldner (1962): value freedom produced "spiritless technicians".
- Becker: take the underdog's side. Gouldner (1968): Becker ignores the powerful system.
- Feminism and postmodernism: all knowledge has a standpoint; be reflexive.

**Policy**
- Social problems ≠ sociological problems.
- Positivist/functionalist: piecemeal engineering. Social democratic: reduce inequality.
  Marxist: policy serves capitalism. Feminist: shaped domestic violence policy. New Right/right
  realist: "broken windows" (Wilson and Kelling, 1982). Postmodernist: no theory can claim authority.
- Constraints: funding, elections, ideology, media reporting.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Consensus theory | Society based on shared norms and values |
| Conflict theory | Society based on competing interests and unequal power |
| Structural theory | Society's structure shapes individual behaviour |
| Social action theory | Society is built from meaningful interaction |
| Duality of structure | Structures are both the medium and outcome of action (Giddens) |
| Strain | Gap between cultural goals and legitimate means (Merton) |
| Typification | A common-sense category used to classify people (Cicourel) |
| Meta-narrative | Grand theory claiming to explain everything |
| Hyperreality | Media signs that seem more real than reality (Baudrillard) |
| Risk society | Society organised around manufactured risks (Beck) |
| Induction | Building general laws from repeated observations |
| Falsification | Testing by trying to disprove (Popper) |
| Paradigm | Shared framework of assumptions in a science (Kuhn) |
| Open system | One whose variables cannot be controlled |
| Value relevance | Values legitimately guide choice of topic (Weber) |
| Social problem | Behaviour causing public concern and policy action |

## Exam traps

- **Two axes, two criticisms.** "Ignores inequality" and "treats people as puppets" are different
  critiques: one from conflict theory, one from action theory.
- **Late modern is not postmodern.** Giddens and Beck reject a clean break with modernity.
- **Popper and Kuhn disagree.** Popper is about what science *should* be; Kuhn describes how science
  actually changes.
- **Weber was not "value-free" in every sense.** He accepted values in topic choice.
- **Application to items.** In item-based 20-mark answers, every paragraph should develop a phrase
  from the item, not just mention it.
- **Policy is a full specification area.** Many answers treat it as an afterthought.
- **Crime examples help, but stay on the question.** Use a crime study to illustrate a theoretical
  point, not to retell the study.

## Self-test

1. Place functionalism on both theoretical axes.
2. What two functions of crime did Durkheim identify?
3. What did Cicourel show about juvenile justice?
4. State Lyotard's key claim about meta-narratives.
5. Give one difference between late modernists and postmodernists.
6. Explain Popper's black-swan point.
7. What happens in a Kuhnian scientific revolution?
8. Why do realists think sociology can be scientific?
9. What did Gouldner mean by "spiritless technicians"?
10. What was Gouldner's 1968 criticism of Becker?
11. Distinguish a social problem from a sociological problem.
12. Name one crime policy linked to right realist ideas.

### Answers

1. Structural and consensus.
2. Boundary maintenance (reaffirming shared values through punishment) and adaptation or change.
3. Police and probation officers' typifications of "typical delinquents" shaped which young people
   were processed, so official statistics reflect agency decisions.
4. Society shows an "incredulity towards meta-narratives": no grand theory can claim to explain all.
5. Late modernists see an intensification of modernity (reflexivity, risk), not a new era.
6. Repeated confirmations cannot prove a universal claim, but one contrary case can disprove it, so
   science should seek falsification.
7. Anomalies accumulate, normal science enters crisis, and a new paradigm replaces the old one.
8. Natural sciences such as meteorology also study open systems; science means explaining underlying
   causes, which sociology can do.
9. Sociologists claiming neutrality became hired experts serving whoever funded them.
10. Becker sided with deviants against low-level officials but left the powerful system unchallenged.
11. A social problem causes public concern and prompts policy; a sociological problem is any pattern
    a sociologist wants to explain, including normal behaviour.
12. Zero-tolerance policing, linked to Wilson and Kelling's "broken windows" (1982).
