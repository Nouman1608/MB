## What the specification asks for

The content statements are:

- "Electrophilic addition reactions of alkenes with HBr, H₂SO₄ and Br₂"
- "The use of bromine to test for unsaturation."
- "The formation of major and minor products in addition reactions of unsymmetrical alkenes."

You should be able to "outline the mechanisms for these reactions" and "explain the formation of major and minor products by reference to the relative stabilities of primary, secondary and tertiary carbocation intermediates."

## One mechanism, three reagents

All three reactions follow the same two-step pattern:

1. **Attack by the electrophile.** A curly arrow goes from the **C=C bond** to the δ+ atom of the electrophile. A second arrow goes from the bond inside the electrophile to its δ− atom. A **carbocation** and a negative ion form.
2. **Attack on the carbocation.** A curly arrow goes from a **lone pair** on the negative ion to the positive carbon.

| Reagent | Electrophile (δ+ atom) | Negative ion formed | Product type |
|---|---|---|---|
| HBr | H of Hδ+–Brδ− | Br⁻ | bromoalkane |
| H₂SO₄ (cold, concentrated) | H of H–OSO₂OH | ⁻OSO₂OH (hydrogensulfate) | alkyl hydrogensulfate |
| Br₂ | Br that becomes δ+ by induction | Br⁻ | 1,2-dibromoalkane |

### Worked example 1: bromine and propene

*Outline the mechanism for the reaction of propene with bromine. Name the product.*

```
Step 1   CH₃–CH=CH₂ + Brδ+–Brδ−  →  CH₃–C⁺H–CH₂Br + :Br⁻
         arrow a: from the C=C bond to the δ+ Br
         arrow b: from the Br–Br bond to the δ− Br
Step 2   CH₃–C⁺H–CH₂Br + :Br⁻    →  CH₃–CHBr–CH₂Br
         arrow c: from a lone pair on Br⁻ to C⁺
```

Annotated marking points:

- **Induced dipole** shown on Br₂ (δ+ on the bromine nearer the double bond).
- **Arrow a** starts on the double bond, not on a carbon atom.
- **Carbocation** drawn with the + on carbon and Br on the other carbon.
- **Arrow c** starts on a lone pair of Br⁻.
- Product: **1,2-dibromopropane**. Both added atoms are bromine, so the same product forms whichever carbon is attacked first.

### Worked example 2: sulfuric acid and 2-methylbut-2-ene

*2-Methylbut-2-ene, (CH₃)₂C=CHCH₃, reacts with cold, concentrated sulfuric acid. Give the structure of the major product and explain why it is major.*

- If H⁺ adds to C3, the positive charge sits on C2, which is bonded to **three** carbons: a **tertiary** carbocation, (CH₃)₂C⁺CH₂CH₃.
- If H⁺ adds to C2, the positive charge sits on C3, bonded to **two** carbons: a **secondary** carbocation, (CH₃)₂CHC⁺HCH₃.
- The hydrogensulfate ion bonds through its negatively charged **oxygen**.
- **Major product:** (CH₃)₂C(OSO₂OH)CH₂CH₃, from the more stable tertiary carbocation.
- **Minor product:** (CH₃)₂CHCH(OSO₂OH)CH₃.

## Why carbocation stability decides the major product

Alkyl groups **push electron density** towards the carbon they are attached to (they are electron-releasing). A positive carbon with more alkyl groups has its charge spread more, so it is more stable.

**Stability order:** tertiary > secondary > primary.

The more stable carbocation forms more readily (its formation has a lower activation energy), so more of the product from that carbocation is made. Both carbocations still form, which is why a **minor product** also appears. Write "the more stable carbocation forms more readily", not "the tertiary carbocation is more reactive"; it is the reverse.

### Worked example 3: but-1-ene and hydrogen bromide

*Predict the major and minor products when but-1-ene, CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃, reacts with HBr, and explain the difference.*

| Where H adds | Carbocation | Class | Product | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| C1 | CH₃C⁺HCH₂CH₃ | secondary | 2-bromobutane | **major** |
| C2 | ⁺CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ | primary | 1-bromobutane | minor |

The secondary carbocation has two electron-releasing alkyl groups on the positive carbon, against one for the primary carbocation, so it is more stable and forms more readily. **Major: 2-bromobutane; minor: 1-bromobutane.**

A quick check for any unsymmetrical alkene: work out both carbocations, classify each, and give the product from the more substituted one as major. If both carbocations are the same class (as in pent-2-ene, where both are secondary), neither product dominates by this argument.

## The bromine test for unsaturation

Shake the substance with **bromine water** (orange). A compound containing C=C turns it **colourless** because the bromine adds across the double bond and is used up. A saturated compound leaves the solution **orange**.

Points that earn the observation mark:

- Give the colour **before** and **after**: "orange to colourless".
- Use "colourless", not "clear". Clear means transparent and can still be coloured.
- The test shows unsaturation; it does not tell you **where** the double bond is or how many there are.

### Worked example 4: bromine and a mixture

*A 2.00 g sample contains cyclohexene, C₆H₁₀ (Mr 82.0), mixed with cyclohexane. It reacts with exactly 16.0 cm³ of a 0.500 mol dm⁻³ solution of bromine in an inert solvent. Calculate the percentage by mass of cyclohexene.*

```
n(Br₂)          = 16.0 ÷ 1000 × 0.500 = 0.00800 mol
n(cyclohexene)  = 0.00800 mol         (1 : 1; cyclohexane does not react)
mass            = 0.00800 × 82.0 = 0.656 g
percentage      = 0.656 ÷ 2.00 × 100 = 32.8 %
```

## Common mistakes

- First arrow drawn from a carbon atom instead of from the C=C bond.
- No dipole on HBr, or no induced dipole on Br₂.
- Hydrogensulfate bonded through sulfur instead of oxygen.
- Calling the tertiary carbocation "more reactive"; it is **more stable**.
- Giving only the major product when the question asks for both.
- Bromine water described as turning "clear".
- Counting carbocation class by the whole molecule rather than by carbons bonded to C⁺.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Outline the mechanism**: two steps; dipoles, curly arrows, carbocation and products. Draw the carbocation that leads to the product asked for.
- **Explain** major and minor products: name both carbocations, classify each, state relative stability, link to rate of formation.
- **Describe a test**: reagent, what you do (shake), result for the alkene **and** for a saturated compound.
- **Calculate**: one mole of Br₂ reacts with one mole of C=C.
- **Name**: the H adds to one carbon and the other group to the second; number from the end nearer the substituent.
