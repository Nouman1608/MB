Subtopic 15.1 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers amount of substance as an SI base quantity and the use of molar quantities with the Avogadro constant. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [The mole study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-the-mole/); to apply the ideas, try the [The mole practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-the-mole-practice/).

## Core facts

- **Amount of substance** is an **SI base quantity**; its base unit is the **mole (mol)**.
- **One mole** of any substance contains **N_A** particles of that substance.
- **Avogadro constant** N_A = 6.02 × 10²³ **mol⁻¹** (data list).
- The particles must be named: atoms, molecules, ions or electrons.

## SI base quantities used in 9702

| Quantity | Unit |
|---|---|
| Mass | kg |
| Length | m |
| Time | s |
| Current | A |
| Thermodynamic temperature | K |
| Amount of substance | mol |

## Equations

```
n = m / M                (M in kg mol⁻¹ if m in kg)
N = n N_A
mass of one particle m₀ = M / N_A
number density = N / V
```

- **Molar mass** M: mass per mole, kg mol⁻¹ (often quoted in g mol⁻¹; convert).
- A particle of mass A u has molar mass A g mol⁻¹, because 1 u × N_A = 1.00 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹.

## The conversion chain

```
mass m  --(÷ M)-->  moles n  --(× N_A)-->  particles N
mass m  <--(× M)--  moles n  <--(÷ N_A)--  particles N
```

**Two quick examples.**

- 1.00 kg of copper (M = 0.0635 kg mol⁻¹): n = 1.00 ÷ 0.0635 = 15.7 mol; N = 15.7 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 9.48 × 10²⁴ atoms.
- 0.50 g of helium (M = 4.0 g mol⁻¹): n = 0.125 mol; N = 7.5 × 10²² atoms. The same mass of argon (M = 40 g mol⁻¹) has ten times fewer atoms.

## Atoms or molecules?

| Sample | Particles per mole of the substance |
|---|---|
| Helium, argon (monatomic gases) | N_A atoms |
| Nitrogen N₂, oxygen O₂ | N_A molecules, 2N_A atoms |
| Water H₂O | N_A molecules, 3N_A atoms |
| A metal such as copper | N_A atoms |

In Topic 15 gas questions, "particles" means molecules (atoms for a monatomic gas).

## Orders of magnitude

| Situation | Value |
|---|---|
| Atoms in 1.00 kg of copper | 9.5 × 10²⁴ |
| Spacing of molecules in liquid water | ~3 × 10⁻¹⁰ m |
| Spacing of molecules in a gas at room conditions | ~3 × 10⁻⁹ m |
| Number density of a gas at room conditions | ~2.5 × 10²⁵ m⁻³ |
| Mass of a nitrogen molecule | 4.65 × 10⁻²⁶ kg |

Molecular spacing estimate: volume per molecule = (volume of one mole) ÷ N_A; spacing ≈ cube root.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Amount of substance | SI base quantity measuring the number of particles, in moles |
| Mole | Amount containing N_A particles |
| Avogadro constant N_A | Number of particles per mole, 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ |
| Molar mass M | Mass of one mole of a substance |
| Unified atomic mass unit u | 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg |
| Number density | Number of particles per unit volume, m⁻³ |

## Exam traps

- Calling the mole a unit of mass, or "the mass of N_A particles".
- Writing N_A as a pure number without mol⁻¹.
- Dividing kilograms by grams per mole.
- Mixing atoms and molecules: 1 mol of O₂ has 2N_A atoms.
- Using n where the equation needs N (and the reverse) in Topic 15.
- Standard-form slips with 10²³ and 10⁻²⁷.
- Forgetting that a molar volume of a liquid comes from M ÷ density, not from a gas value.

## Self-test

1. Name the SI base unit of amount of substance.
2. Define one mole.
3. State the unit of the Avogadro constant.
4. How many moles are there in 64 g of oxygen gas (O₂, M = 32 g mol⁻¹)?
5. How many molecules are in that sample?
6. How many oxygen atoms are in that sample?
7. Calculate the mass of one helium atom (M = 4.0 g mol⁻¹).
8. A sample contains 3.01 × 10²⁴ molecules. How many moles is this?
9. Show that 1 u × N_A ≈ 1 g mol⁻¹.
10. Why is a mole of lead much heavier than a mole of aluminium?
11. Estimate the number density of molecules in a gas where one mole occupies 0.024 m³.
12. Which contains more atoms: 1.0 g of helium or 1.0 g of argon (M = 40 g mol⁻¹)? By what factor?

**Answers:** 1. The mole (mol). 2. The amount of substance containing a number of particles equal to the Avogadro constant (6.02 × 10²³). 3. mol⁻¹. 4. 64 ÷ 32 = 2.0 mol. 5. 2.0 × 6.02 × 10²³ = 1.2 × 10²⁴ molecules. 6. 2.4 × 10²⁴ atoms. 7. 0.0040 ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 6.6 × 10⁻²⁷ kg. 8. 3.01 × 10²⁴ ÷ 6.02 × 10²³ = 5.0 mol. 9. 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ kg × 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹ = 1.00 × 10⁻³ kg mol⁻¹ = 1.00 g mol⁻¹. 10. Each mole has the same number of atoms, but each lead atom has a much greater mass. 11. 6.02 × 10²³ ÷ 0.024 = 2.5 × 10²⁵ m⁻³. 12. Helium, by a factor of 10.
