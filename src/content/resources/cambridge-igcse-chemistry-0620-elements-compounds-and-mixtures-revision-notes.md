These notes cover Cambridge IGCSE Chemistry 0620 sub-topic **2.1 Elements, compounds and mixtures** (2026–2028). The single outcome is Core: "Describe the differences between elements, compounds and mixtures". For full explanations and worked examples, read the [elements, compounds and mixtures study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-elements-compounds-and-mixtures/); then try the [elements, compounds and mixtures practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-chemistry-0620-elements-compounds-and-mixtures-practice/).

## Definitions

- **Element**: a substance made of only one type of atom.
- **Compound**: two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion.
- **Mixture**: two or more substances not chemically combined.

Elements and compounds are **pure substances**. A mixture is not.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Atom | The smallest particle of an element |
| Molecule | Two or more atoms chemically bonded together |
| Chemically combined | Joined by chemical bonds, formed in a chemical reaction |
| Pure substance | A single element or a single compound, with nothing else mixed in |
| Alloy | A mixture of a metal with other elements, such as brass or steel |
| Physical separation | Separating without a chemical reaction: filtering, distilling, using a magnet |

## Five-point comparison

| | Element | Compound | Mixture |
|---|---|---|---|
| Types of atom | One | Two or more, bonded | Any; components not bonded together |
| Composition | Fixed | Fixed (formula) | Variable |
| Properties | Its own | New, unlike its elements | Components keep their own |
| Separating | Cannot be split | Chemical reaction only | Physical methods |
| Melting point | Sharp | Sharp | Over a range |

Making a compound involves a chemical reaction, often with heat or light given out. Making a mixture involves only mixing.

## Elements can be molecules

O₂, N₂, Cl₂, H₂ and O₃ are elements: every atom in them is the same type. The test is the **type** of atom, not the number.

**Formula check**: count different element symbols.

- One symbol (Cu, N₂, O₃) → element.
- Two or more (CO, NaCl, H₂O) → compound.
- Capital letters matter: Co is cobalt (element); CO is carbon monoxide (compound).

## Particle diagrams

| What the box shows | Answer |
|---|---|
| All particles identical; each one atom or identical atoms joined | Element |
| All particles identical; each contains different atoms joined | Compound |
| Two or more kinds of particle | Mixture |

A box with identical diatomic molecules of one element plus some compound molecules is a **mixture**.

## Everyday classifications

| Substance | Type |
|---|---|
| Air | Mixture |
| Sea water | Mixture |
| Brass, steel | Mixtures (alloys) |
| Distilled water | Compound |
| Carbon dioxide | Compound |
| Sodium chloride | Compound |
| Copper, oxygen, nitrogen | Elements |

## Evidence of a compound forming

- A chemical reaction occurs: an energy change such as heat or light.
- The product has new properties: for example, a soft reactive metal and a toxic gas give a harmless white solid (sodium chloride).
- The product cannot be separated back into its elements by physical methods.

## Paired comparisons for "describe the differences"

Examiners reward sentences that compare directly. Learn these as pairs:

- **Composition**: a compound has a fixed composition, but the composition of a mixture can vary.
- **Properties**: a compound has properties different from its elements, but each substance in a mixture keeps its own properties.
- **Separation**: a mixture can be separated by physical methods, but a compound can only be broken down by a chemical reaction, and an element cannot be broken down at all.
- **Bonding**: in a compound the elements are chemically combined; in a mixture the substances are not chemically combined.
- **Melting point**: a pure element or compound melts at a sharp temperature, but a mixture usually melts over a range.

Write "but" or "whereas" in the middle of each sentence, so both halves of the comparison are clear.

## Exam traps

- "One atom" instead of "one type of atom".
- Calling O₂ or N₂ a compound.
- Calling an alloy, air or sea water a compound.
- Saying a compound keeps the properties of its elements.
- Saying a compound can be separated by distillation or filtration.
- Saying a mixture of two elements is a compound because it contains two elements.
- Forgetting that a mixture can contain compounds.

## Self-test

1. Define a compound.
2. Is chlorine gas, Cl₂, an element or a compound? Why?
3. Give two differences between a compound and a mixture.
4. Classify: steel, methane (CH₄), argon.
5. Why does a mixture of iron filings and sand keep the properties of both?
6. How could you separate iron filings from sand?
7. Which has a fixed composition: air or water?
8. A box shows identical particles, each made of one carbon atom joined to two oxygen atoms. Element, compound or mixture?
9. A box shows some O₂ molecules and some N₂ molecules. Element, compound or mixture?
10. Give one observation showing that a chemical reaction makes a compound.
11. Why is sodium chloride safe to eat, though sodium and chlorine are dangerous?
12. Is Co the formula of an element or a compound?

**Answers**

1. Two or more elements chemically combined in a fixed proportion.
2. An element: both atoms in each molecule are chlorine.
3. Any two: a compound has a fixed composition, a mixture's varies; a compound has new properties, a mixture's components keep theirs; a compound needs a chemical reaction to separate, a mixture can be separated physically; a compound has a sharp melting point, a mixture melts over a range.
4. Steel: mixture (alloy). Methane: compound. Argon: element.
5. They are not chemically combined, so each keeps its own properties.
6. Use a magnet to attract the iron.
7. Water.
8. Compound (carbon dioxide).
9. Mixture of two elements.
10. Any one: heat or light given out; a new substance with different properties forms.
11. In the compound they are chemically combined, so it has completely new properties.
12. An element (cobalt).
