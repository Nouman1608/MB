## Key definitions

- **Relative formula mass (M_r):** the sum of the relative atomic masses (A_r) of all the atoms in a formula. For a molecular substance it is the relative molecular mass. No units.
- **Mole (mol):** the unit for amount of substance (1.27). One mole has a mass in grams equal to the A_r or M_r.
- **Empirical formula:** the simplest whole-number ratio of atoms of each element in a compound (1.32).
- **Molecular formula:** the actual number of atoms of each element in one molecule (1.32).
- **Water of crystallisation:** water that is part of the crystal structure of a salt, shown after a dot, as in CuSO₄·5H₂O.
- **Theoretical yield:** the maximum mass of product calculated from the equation. **Actual yield:** the mass you obtain.
- **Concentration (Paper 2 only):** amount of solute per dm³ of solution, in mol/dm³.
- **Molar volume (Paper 2 only):** the volume of one mole of any gas, 24 dm³ or 24 000 cm³ at room temperature and pressure (rtp).

## State symbols (1.25)

| Symbol | Meaning | Typical example |
|---|---|---|
| (s) | solid, including a precipitate | metals, insoluble salts, carbonates heated as solids |
| (l) | liquid | water, molten compounds, bromine |
| (g) | gas | H₂, O₂, CO₂, NH₃ |
| (aq) | dissolved in water | acids, alkalis, soluble salts in solution |

## Method in steps

### Balancing an equation (1.25)

1. Write every formula correctly. Never change a subscript.
2. Balance one element at a time using numbers in front of formulae.
3. Leave free elements (O₂, H₂, Cl₂) until last; double everything to clear a ½.
4. Recount every element, then add state symbols.

For an unfamiliar reaction, take the formulae and states from the question. "Precipitate" means (s); "solution" means (aq); "fizzing" or "gas given off" means (g).

### Reacting mass (1.29)

1. Mass → moles of the known substance (÷ M_r).
2. Moles × ratio from the balanced equation.
3. Moles → mass of the wanted substance (× M_r).

**Worked reminder.** `Pb(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2KI(aq) → PbI₂(s) + 2KNO₃(aq)`. 3.31 g of lead(II) nitrate (M_r 331) is 0.0100 mol, so 0.0100 mol of PbI₂ (M_r 461) forms: **4.61 g**. (A_r: N = 14, O = 16, I = 127, Pb = 207)

If the question gives masses of reactant and product, find moles of each and use them to deduce the ratio, and so the equation.

### Empirical formula (1.33)

1. Write the mass (or %) of each element.
2. Divide each by its A_r.
3. Divide every answer by the smallest.
4. If you get .5, ×2; if .33 or .67, ×3.
5. Molecular formula: M_r ÷ empirical formula mass, then multiply all subscripts.

### Water of crystallisation (1.31)

1. Mass of water = hydrated mass − anhydrous mass.
2. Moles of anhydrous salt and moles of water.
3. x = moles of water ÷ moles of salt.

### Concentration and titration (1.34C, Paper 2 only)

1. Convert every volume to dm³ (÷ 1000).
2. n = c × V for the solution you know fully.
3. Use the equation's ratio.
4. c = n ÷ V for the unknown.

### Gas volume (1.35C, Paper 2 only)

1. Moles of the known substance.
2. Ratio to moles of gas.
3. × 24 for dm³ or × 24 000 for cm³.

**Worked reminder.** `2Na(s) + 2H₂O(l) → 2NaOH(aq) + H₂(g)`. 2.30 g of sodium (A_r 23) is 0.100 mol, giving 0.0500 mol of hydrogen: 0.0500 × 24 000 = **1200 cm³** (1.20 dm³) at rtp.

For reacting gases only, volume ratio = mole ratio, so no conversion to moles is needed.

## Practical 1.36: formula of a metal oxide

| | Combustion (magnesium oxide) | Reduction (copper(II) oxide) |
|---|---|---|
| What happens | metal gains oxygen | oxide loses oxygen |
| Apparatus | crucible and lid, pipe-clay triangle, Bunsen | reduction tube, gas supply (hydrogen or methane), Bunsen |
| Mass of oxygen | final mass − mass of metal | mass of oxide − mass of metal left |
| Key precaution | lift lid now and then for air; keep it on to stop MgO smoke escaping | keep gas flowing while copper cools so it is not reoxidised; burn excess gas |
| Completion check | heat, cool, weigh to constant mass | heat, cool, weigh to constant mass |
| Error if not complete | too little oxygen measured | too little oxygen measured |

The formula of **water** can be found the same way: reduce copper(II) oxide with hydrogen and absorb the water formed in a weighed drying tube. Oxygen = mass lost by the oxide; hydrogen = mass of water − mass of oxygen.

## Must-know distinctions

- **A_r vs M_r:** A_r is for one element's atoms; M_r is for a whole formula.
- **Coefficient vs subscript:** 3H₂O means three molecules, each with M_r 18. The 3 goes in the ratio step, not the M_r.
- **Empirical vs molecular:** C₂H₆ is molecular; CH₃ is its empirical formula. Ionic compounds are given by empirical formulae.
- **Theoretical vs actual yield:** actual goes on top. Percentage yield cannot exceed 100%.
- **(l) vs (aq):** water is (l). A substance dissolved in water is (aq).
- **cm³ vs dm³ (Paper 2):** divide cm³ by 1000 before using mol/dm³.
- **24 vs 24 000 (Paper 2):** match the molar volume to the units the answer asks for.

## Quick self-test

1. Balance: C₂H₆ + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O.
2. Calculate the M_r of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. (A_r: H = 1, O = 16, Ca = 40)
3. Calculate the M_r of magnesium nitrate, Mg(NO₃)₂. (A_r: N = 14, O = 16, Mg = 24)
4. How many moles are in 4.4 g of carbon dioxide? (A_r: C = 12, O = 16)
5. What is the mass of 0.30 mol of sodium hydroxide, NaOH? (A_r: H = 1, O = 16, Na = 23)
6. `Zn + S → ZnS`. Calculate the mass of zinc sulfide formed from 13.0 g of zinc. (A_r: S = 32, Zn = 65)
7. In question 6, 16.5 g of zinc sulfide is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield.
8. A hydrocarbon contains 2.4 g of carbon and 0.6 g of hydrogen. Its M_r is 30. Find its empirical and molecular formulae. (A_r: H = 1, C = 12)
9. 6.44 g of hydrated sodium sulfate, Na₂SO₄·xH₂O, leaves 2.84 g of anhydrous salt on heating to constant mass. Find x. (A_r: H = 1, O = 16, Na = 23, S = 32)
10. **(Paper 2 only)** How many moles of HCl are in 20.0 cm³ of 0.150 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid?
11. **(Paper 2 only)** What volume, in cm³, does 0.0250 mol of hydrogen occupy at rtp?
12. **(Paper 2 only)** 0.0400 mol of solute is dissolved to make 250 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration.

### Answers

1. `2C₂H₆ + 7O₂ → 4CO₂ + 6H₂O` (C 4 = 4; H 12 = 12; O 14 = 8 + 6).
2. 40 + 2 × (16 + 1) = **74**.
3. 24 + 2 × (14 + 3 × 16) = **148**.
4. 4.4 ÷ 44 = **0.10 mol**.
5. 0.30 × 40 = **12.0 g**.
6. n(Zn) = 13.0 ÷ 65 = 0.200 mol; ratio 1 : 1; M_r(ZnS) = 97; mass = 0.200 × 97 = **19.4 g**.
7. (16.5 ÷ 19.4) × 100 = **85.1%**.
8. C: 2.4 ÷ 12 = 0.20; H: 0.6 ÷ 1 = 0.60; ratio 1 : 3, empirical formula **CH₃**; 30 ÷ 15 = 2, molecular formula **C₂H₆**.
9. Water = 3.60 g = 0.200 mol; Na₂SO₄ (M_r 142) = 0.0200 mol; x = **10**.
10. 0.150 × 20.0 ÷ 1000 = **0.00300 mol**.
11. 0.0250 × 24 000 = **600 cm³**.
12. 0.0400 ÷ 0.250 = **0.160 mol/dm³**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Balancing by changing a formula (for example writing H₂O₂ for water) instead of adding coefficients.
- Leaving out state symbols when the question asks for them, or writing (aq) for water or a precipitate.
- Forgetting the bracket multiplier, so Mg(NO₃)₂ is worked out as 24 + 14 + 6 × 16.
- Using the mass ratio instead of the mole ratio in a reacting-mass question.
- Rounding 1.33 or 1.5 to a whole number in an empirical formula instead of multiplying up.
- Dividing the masses of water and salt (not their moles) when finding x in a hydrated salt.
- Giving a percentage yield above 100% by putting the theoretical yield on top.
- In a practical write-up, not mentioning heating to constant mass, or letting copper cool in air after reduction.
- **(Paper 2)** Using a volume in cm³ in c = n ÷ V without dividing by 1000.
- **(Paper 2)** Multiplying by 24 when cm³ is asked for, or by 24 000 when dm³ is asked for.
