## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Outline the failure to function adequately definition of abnormality. **[2]**

**2.** Identify the definition of abnormality reflected in each statement.

> A: "Only about 2% of people score that low, so it is abnormal."
> B: "In this community nobody talks to themselves loudly in the street."
> C: "She lacks autonomy and cannot cope with stress."

**[3]**

**3.** Outline **two** emotional characteristics of depression. **[2]**

**4.** Explain the role of negative reinforcement in maintaining a phobia. **[2]**

**5.** A fictional clinic compared treatments for specific phobias. Fear was rated from 0 (none) to 10 (extreme). Each group began with 30 patients.

| Group | Mean fear before | Mean fear after | Patients who left treatment early |
|---|---|---|---|
| Systematic desensitisation | 8.4 | 3.1 | 2 |
| Flooding | 8.6 | 2.9 | 7 |
| Waiting list (no treatment) | 8.5 | 8.1 | 0 |

**(a)** Calculate the reduction in mean fear for each group. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage of patients who left early in the systematic desensitisation and flooding groups. Give your answers to one decimal place. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest why more patients left the flooding group early. **[2]**

**6.** Lena was not invited to a colleague's party. She thinks, "I must be liked by everyone, or I am worthless," and has felt low for weeks. Use Ellis's ABC model to explain Lena's depression. **[3]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** Priya, aged 12, was bitten by a dog two years ago. She now refuses to visit friends who own dogs and crosses the road whenever she sees one.

**(a)** Use the two-process model to explain Priya's phobia. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain how systematic desensitisation could be used to treat Priya. **[4]**

**8.** Marcus has depression. He believes he is useless, that his friends secretly dislike him and that he will never find a job. Explain how cognitive behaviour therapy could be used to treat Marcus. **[6]**

**9.** Explain **one** strength and **one** limitation of the deviation from social norms definition of abnormality. **[4]**

**10.** Discuss the view that no single definition of abnormality is adequate. **[8]**

**11.** Evaluate the cognitive explanation of depression. **[8]**

**12.** Discuss behavioural treatments for phobias. **[12]**

## Answers

Questions 10 to 12 are extended responses. The real exam marks these with levels of response, not point by point; the [1] points show the kind of content that earns credit.

**1.** A person is abnormal if they cannot cope with the demands of everyday life, such as working, maintaining relationships or caring for themselves [1], or if their behaviour causes distress to themselves or to others [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A: statistical infrequency [1]. B: deviation from social norms [1]. C: deviation from ideal mental health [1]. **[3]**

**3.** Persistent low mood, such as feeling sad or empty most of the day [1]. Loss of interest or pleasure in activities previously enjoyed (or feelings of worthlessness, or anger) [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Avoiding the feared stimulus reduces anxiety, which is rewarding [1], so avoidance is strengthened and repeated, and the person never learns the stimulus is harmless [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** Systematic desensitisation: 8.4 - 3.1 = 5.3 [1]. Flooding: 8.6 - 2.9 = 5.7 [1]. Waiting list: 8.5 - 8.1 = 0.4 [1].
**(b)** Systematic desensitisation: 2 / 30 x 100 = 6.7% [1]. Flooding: 7 / 30 x 100 = 23.3% [1].
**(c)** Flooding exposes patients immediately to their most feared stimulus with no gradual build-up [1], which is highly distressing, so some patients withdraw before their anxiety falls [1]. **[7]**

**6.** The activating event is not being invited to the party [1]. Lena's irrational belief is the absolute demand that she must be liked by everyone, an example of musturbation [1]. The consequence of this belief, rather than the event itself, is her persistent low mood [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Before the bite, dogs were a neutral stimulus; the bite was an unconditioned stimulus producing pain and fear [1]. Through classical conditioning, dogs became a conditioned stimulus producing fear [1]. Crossing the road reduces Priya's anxiety, so avoidance is negatively reinforced through operant conditioning [1]. Because she always avoids dogs, she never learns that most are harmless, so the phobia is maintained [1].
**(b)** Priya would first learn relaxation techniques such as deep breathing [1]. She and the therapist would build a hierarchy, for example from looking at pictures of dogs to stroking a calm dog [1]. She would work up the hierarchy, moving on only when relaxed at each step [1]. Relaxation and fear cannot occur together, so through reciprocal inhibition relaxation replaces fear [1]. **[8]**

**8.** Marcus's thoughts match Beck's negative triad: about himself ("useless"), the world (friends dislike him) and the future (no job) [1]. The therapist would help him identify these negative automatic thoughts, for example by keeping a thought diary [1]. Thoughts would be challenged by testing them against evidence, such as times friends have invited him out [1]. He could be set homework, such as asking a friend to meet, to test whether the belief is true [1]. Behavioural activation, such as planning enjoyable activities, would counter withdrawal [1]. The aim is to replace negative thoughts with more balanced ones, reducing low mood [1]. **[6]**

**9.** Strength: it recognises the social context of behaviour, separating desirable from undesirable deviation, which statistical infrequency cannot do [1], so it can help identify behaviour that may harm others [1]. Limitation: norms vary between cultures and change over time [1], so behaviour may be labelled abnormal when it is simply disapproved of, as when homosexuality was listed as a disorder until 1973 [1]. **[4]**

**10.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Statistical infrequency is objective but treats desirable rare traits as abnormal [1]. It also fails for common disorders such as depression [1]. Deviation from social norms considers context but is culturally relative [1]. Failure to function adequately recognises the person's experience but depends on who judges adequacy [1]. Deviation from ideal mental health is positive in focus but so demanding that most people would be abnormal [1]. Its criteria reflect individualist values [1]. Clinicians therefore combine definitions, together with diagnostic criteria [1]. Judgement: no single definition is adequate, but together they capture most cases [1]. **[8]**

**11.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Beck's negative triad and Ellis's ABC model attribute depression to faulty thinking [1]. Strength: research finds negative cognitive biases in depressed people [1]. Strength: the explanation led to CBT, which is effective [1]. Limitation: it is unclear whether negative thinking causes depression or results from it [1]. Limitation: it can seem to blame patients for their thoughts [1]. Limitation: it neglects biological factors such as neurotransmitters [1]. Some negative beliefs are realistic responses to poverty or loss, which the model may overlook [1]. Judgement: a useful partial explanation that works best combined with biological and social factors [1]. **[8]**

**12.** *Indicative content (levels marking applies).* Both treatments are based on the two-process model and prevent avoidance [1]. Systematic desensitisation: relaxation training, an anxiety hierarchy and gradual exposure [1]. It works through counterconditioning and reciprocal inhibition [1]. Flooding: immediate, prolonged exposure to the most feared stimulus with no escape [1]. Anxiety peaks then falls, and the association is extinguished [1]. Strength of SD: well supported for specific phobias and suitable for patients who struggle with talking therapies [1]. Strength of SD: patients control the pace, making it more acceptable [1]. Strength of flooding: fast and cost-effective, often in a single long session [1]. Limitation of flooding: highly traumatic, so informed consent is essential and drop-out can be higher [1]. Limitation of both: they treat behaviour, not irrational beliefs [1]. Suitability depends on the patient and the phobia [1]. Judgement: SD is generally preferable ethically, while flooding may suit motivated patients wanting quick results [1]. **[12]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Definitions given with no example or limitation.
- Characteristics placed in the wrong category.
- Application answers that do not use the scenario's details.
- Percentages not rounded as instructed.
- Extended answers that describe treatments without comparing or judging them.
