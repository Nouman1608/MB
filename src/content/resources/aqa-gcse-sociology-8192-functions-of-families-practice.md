## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Which statement describes a family acting as a unit of consumption?

A. Family members work together to farm the land they live on
B. Family members pool their wages to pay for food, rent and holidays
C. Parents teach children the language and customs of their culture
D. Grandparents pass on land and money to their grandchildren

**[1]**

**2.** Which term did Parsons use for the caring, emotional role he linked to wives?

A. Instrumental role
B. Segregated role
C. Expressive role
D. Conjugal role

**[1]**

**3.** Identify **two** functions of families listed in the specification, other than the reproductive function. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what Parsons meant by the stabilisation of adult personalities. **[3]**

**5.** Identify and explain **one** way in which the economic function of families has changed since pre-industrial times. **[4]**

**6.** Describe Engels' view of the link between the family and private property. **[3]**

**7.** Read Item A.

> **Item A (fictional).** Every Saturday the Okafor family go shopping together at a retail park. The children choose new trainers because "everyone at school has them". Mr and Mrs Okafor both work full time, and say that family evenings at home are "the only time we can relax".

- **(a)** Identify **one** function of the family shown in Item A. **[1]**
- **(b)** Explain how a Marxist sociologist might interpret the Okafors' Saturday shopping. **[3]**

**8.** Identify and explain **one** criticism of Parsons' view of the functions of families. **[4]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**9.** Read Item B.

> **Item B (fictional).** A researcher wanted to find out how parents socialise children into gender roles. She gave a questionnaire to the parents of 20 children at one private nursery in a wealthy suburb. Most parents said they treated sons and daughters "exactly the same".

- **(a)** From Item B, examine **one** weakness of the research. **[2]**
- **(b)** Identify and explain **one** ethical issue the researcher should consider when studying socialisation in families with young children. **[4]**

**10.** Explain **two** ways in which, according to feminists, the educational function of families reproduces gender inequality. **[4]**

**11.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that families have lost most of their functions. **[12]**

**12.** Discuss how far sociologists would agree that Parsons' view of the functions of families is outdated. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Pooling wages to buy goods and services is consumption; A describes a unit of production. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. The instrumental role is the breadwinning role Parsons linked to husbands. **[1]**

**3.** Sexual [1]; economic [1]. *Also credit:* educational, in place of either. **[2]**

**4.** Parsons argued that adults face stress and pressure in the competitive world of work [1]. The family is where they can give and receive emotional support, for example from a partner [1]. This keeps adults emotionally stable, so they can carry on performing their adult roles and society runs smoothly [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Identify: the family has changed from a unit of production to mainly a unit of consumption [1]. Explain: in pre-industrial Britain many families worked together on farms or in cottage industries, producing goods in the home [1]. Industrialisation moved most work into factories and offices, so family members now earn wages outside the home [1]. The family's economic role is now mainly to pool income and buy goods and services, and to support dependants [1]. **[4]**

**6.** Engels (a Marxist) argued that monogamous marriage developed alongside private property [1]. Men needed to be certain who their children were so that they could pass their property on to legitimate heirs [1]. The family therefore helps keep wealth concentrated in the same families across generations, which maintains class inequality [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** The economic function, as the family acts as a unit of consumption [1]. *Also credit:* stabilisation of adult personalities, as the parents relax at home.

**(b)** A Marxist would see the family acting as a unit of consumption that serves capitalism [1]. The pressure to buy trainers "everyone has" shows how families keep up with others, which keeps demand and profits high [1]. Zaretsky would add that the relaxing home life the parents value is a cushion against the stress of work, which helps them keep going in jobs that benefit employers [1]. **[4]**

**8.** Identify: Parsons presented an idealised picture of family life centred on the middle-class experience [1]. Explain: he described the American nuclear family of the 1950s, with a breadwinning husband and caring wife [1], and ignored families that did not fit this pattern, such as lone parent, reconstituted or working-class families with both parents in paid work [1]. He also ignored the dark side of families, such as domestic abuse, in which the family harms rather than stabilises its members [1]. **[4]**

*Also credit:* ignores diversity; treats children as passive; treats the gender division of roles as natural (feminist criticism).

**9. (a)** One weakness, examined. The sample of 20 children's parents from one private nursery in a wealthy suburb is small and drawn from one class background [1], so the findings cannot be generalised to families of other classes or areas [1]. *Also credit:* parents may give socially desirable answers, saying they treat children equally when they do not, so the data may lack validity. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify: informed consent [1]. Explain: young children cannot give informed consent themselves [1], so the researcher must get consent from parents and explain the purpose of the research clearly [1]. Participants should know they can withdraw at any time, and any observation of children must avoid causing them distress [1]. *Also credit:* confidentiality of family details; protection from harm; anonymity of the nursery. **[6]**

**10.** First, parents may use canalisation, steering girls towards dolls and toy kitchens and boys towards building and sport [1], which prepares girls for domestic roles and boys for paid work, reproducing the gender division of labour [1]. Second, children learn from watching parents: if mothers do most of the housework, children see this as normal [1], so daughters grow up expecting a dual burden and sons expecting to do less, which continues inequality into the next generation [1]. **[4]**

**11.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Parsons argued that industrial society moved many functions to specialist institutions [1].
- Production moved to factories and offices, so the family is no longer mainly a unit of production [1].
- Formal education moved to schools and health care to hospitals and the welfare state [1].
- Parsons concluded only two basic and irreducible functions remained: primary socialisation and stabilisation of adult personalities [1].
- The sexual function has weakened, as cohabitation and sex outside marriage carry less stigma [1].
- Against: the family still performs all four specification functions in some form [1].
- The economic function remains as a unit of consumption and as support for dependent members [1].
- Marxists such as Zaretsky argue the family is still vital to capitalism as a unit of consumption that reproduces labour power [1].
- Feminists argue families still perform unpaid domestic and caring work, mainly by women, so their functions have not disappeared [1].
- Primary socialisation has arguably grown in importance, as parents spend more time and money on children [1].
- Evaluation: Parsons' view was based on the middle-class American family, so it may not apply to all families [1].
- Judgement: families have lost some functions, such as production, but have kept and even developed others, so "most" overstates the change [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Separate functions that have gone (production) from functions that have changed (economic support, socialisation).

**12.** The real exam marks extended answers using levels of response, not point by point. The points below show indicative creditworthy content.

- Parsons identified primary socialisation and the stabilisation of adult personalities as basic and irreducible functions [1].
- For "outdated": he wrote about the 1950s American nuclear family [1].
- The specification notes criticism that his picture was idealised and centred on the middle-class experience [1].
- Family diversity today, including lone parent, reconstituted and same-sex families, does not fit his model [1].
- His instrumental and expressive roles assume a male breadwinner, but most mothers now do paid work [1].
- Feminists such as Delphy and Leonard argue the family exploits women rather than benefiting all members [1].
- Domestic abuse shows that families can destabilise rather than stabilise adult personalities [1].
- Against "outdated": families still carry out primary socialisation, and children still learn norms first at home [1].
- Families still provide emotional support, which may matter more in a society where work is insecure [1].
- Diverse families still perform Parsons' two functions, so the functions may be relevant even if his family type is not [1].
- Marxists would say the issue is not age but bias: Parsons ignores how the family serves capitalism [1].
- Judgement: his account of family structure and gender roles is outdated, but his two functions remain a useful starting point [1]. **[12]**

*Examiner insight:* Separate Parsons' functions from his description of family structure; strong answers judge each.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the four specification functions "Parsons' functions".
- In 7(b), describing shopping without linking it to capitalism or profit.
- In 9(a), naming a weakness ("small sample") without saying what it does to the findings.
- In 12-mark answers, giving only the functionalist view, or ending without a judgement.
