## 3.7.1 Fields: definitions

- **Force field**: a region in which a body experiences a non-contact force.
- Field strength is a vector; decide its direction by inspection.
- Sources: mass (gravitational), static charge (electric), moving charge (magnetic).
- **Gravity vs electrostatics.** Same: inverse-square laws, field lines, potential, equipotential surfaces. Different: masses always attract; charges can attract or repel.

## 3.7.2 Gravitational fields: key points

- **Potential** V: work done per unit mass bringing a mass from infinity. Zero at infinity, negative everywhere else because the force is attractive.
- **Equipotential surfaces**: no work done moving along them; perpendicular to field lines.
- **Graphs**: g ∝ 1/r² outside the mass; V ∝ −1/r, rising towards zero. g is minus the gradient of V–r.
- **Orbits**: T² ∝ r³. A log T against log r plot has gradient 1.5.
- **Satellite energy**: Ek = GMm/2r, Ep = −GMm/r, total = −GMm/2r.
- **Synchronous orbit**: period equals the rotation period of the body orbited.
- **Geostationary orbit**: Earth-synchronous (24 h), equatorial plane, west to east, radius about 4.2 × 10⁷ m. Fixed dishes can point at it.
- **Low orbits**: short period, close to the surface; good for observation, but a satellite passes overhead quickly.

### Method: deriving T² ∝ r³

1. Set gravitational force equal to centripetal force: GMm/r² = mv²/r.
2. Substitute v = 2πr/T.
3. Rearrange: T² = (4π²/GM) r³. Since 4π²/GM is constant for one central mass, T² ∝ r³.

## 3.7.3 Electric fields: key points

- Coulomb's law is for point charges in a vacuum; air counts as a vacuum. A charged sphere acts as if its charge is at the centre.
- Gravitational force between subatomic particles is negligible compared with the electrostatic force (by a factor of order 10³⁹ for a proton and electron).
- Field lines: positive to negative. Uniform between parallel plates; radial around a point charge.
- **E = V/d** comes from work done: Fd = QΔV.
- A charge entering a uniform field at right angles follows a parabola: constant velocity along the plates, constant acceleration across.
- **Absolute potential**: work done per unit positive charge from infinity. Positive near a positive charge, negative near a negative one.

### Method: deflection between plates

1. E = V/d, then F = QE and a = F/m.
2. Time between plates: t = plate length ÷ horizontal speed.
3. Sideways displacement y = ½at²; sideways velocity = at.
4. Check the direction: positive charges move towards the negative plate.

## 3.7.4 Capacitance: key points

- **Relative permittivity** = dielectric constant = εᵣ.
- **Dielectric action**: polar molecules rotate to line up with the field. Their own field opposes the applied field, so p.d. falls for a given charge and C rises.
- **Energy**: area under the charge–p.d. graph (straight line through the origin) = ½QV.
- **Graphs**:

| | Charging | Discharging |
|---|---|---|
| Q and V | rise to a maximum, curving off | fall exponentially to zero |
| I | falls exponentially to zero | falls exponentially to zero (reversed direction) |

- Gradient of Q–t = current. Area under I–t = charge.
- After one RC on discharge: 37% left. After one RC on charge: 63% reached.
- **Required practical 9**: plot ln V against t for discharge. Gradient = −1/RC.

### Method: using the exponential

1. Find RC (check units: Ω × F = s).
2. Substitute into the right equation for charge or discharge.
3. To find time, take natural logs: t = −RC ln(Q/Q₀) for discharge.

## 3.7.5 Magnetic fields: key points

- **Tesla**: 1 T gives a force of 1 N on 1 m of wire carrying 1 A perpendicular to the field.
- **Fleming's left-hand rule** for the motor effect; current = direction of positive charge flow.
- **Charges in B**: force ⊥ velocity, so no work done, constant speed, circular path, r = mv/BQ.
- **Cyclotron**: time per orbit 2πm/BQ does not depend on speed, so a fixed-frequency alternating p.d. can keep accelerating particles.
- **Required practical 10**: top pan balance; F = Δm × g on the magnet equals the force on the wire.
- **Flux linkage** NΦ = BAN cos θ, θ between field and the normal to the coil.
- **Required practical 11**: search coil and oscilloscope; flux linkage (and peak emf) varies with cos θ.
- **Faraday's law**: emf = rate of change of flux linkage. **Lenz's law**: the induced current opposes the change producing it.
- **Straight conductor**: ε = Blv.
- **Rotating coil**: ε = BANω sin ωt; emf is maximum when the coil plane is parallel to B (flux linkage zero).
- **AC**: the rms, peak and peak-to-peak relations here are for sinusoidal waveforms only.
- **Oscilloscope**: voltage = divisions × Y-gain; time = divisions × time-base. Usable as a dc or ac voltmeter and for measuring time intervals and frequencies.
- **Transformer losses**: eddy currents (reduce with a laminated core), coil resistance, magnetising the core, flux leakage.
- **Transmission**: high voltage → low current → low I²R loss.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Potential vs potential difference**: V is per unit mass or charge relative to infinity; ΔV is between two points.
- **Flux vs flux linkage**: Φ = BA; flux linkage multiplies by N.
- **Time constant vs half-time**: RC vs 0.69RC.
- **Peak vs rms vs peak-to-peak**: V₀, V₀/√2, 2V₀.
- **Left-hand rule** (force on a current) vs **Lenz's law** (direction of induced current).

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate the gravitational force between two 50 kg masses 2.0 m apart.
2. A planet has mass 6.4 × 10²³ kg and radius 3.4 × 10⁶ m. Calculate g at its surface.
3. V at Earth's surface is −6.25 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹. State V at 2R from the centre.
4. A satellite's orbital radius is made four times larger. By what factor does its period change?
5. Plates 1.5 cm apart have a p.d. of 300 V. Calculate E and the force on an electron.
6. A 220 μF capacitor is charged to 9.0 V. Calculate Q and the energy stored.
7. R = 10 kΩ, C = 220 μF. Calculate the time to halve.
8. What fraction of the charge remains after a discharge time of 3RC?
9. An electron moves at 4.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 2.0 mT field. Calculate the radius of its path.
10. A 50-turn coil of area 0.020 m² is in a 0.40 T field with its normal at 60° to the field. Calculate the flux linkage.
11. A flux linkage of 0.20 Wb-turns falls to zero in 0.050 s. Calculate the average induced emf.
12. An oscilloscope shows a sine wave 6.0 divisions peak-to-peak at 2.0 V/div; one cycle spans 4.0 divisions at 5.0 ms/div. Find V₀, V_rms and f.

### Answers

1. F = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 50 × 50 / 2.0² = **4.2 × 10⁻⁸ N**
2. g = GM/R² = **3.7 N kg⁻¹**
3. V ∝ 1/r, so **−3.13 × 10⁷ J kg⁻¹**
4. T ∝ r^(3/2) = 4^1.5 = **×8**
5. E = 300/0.015 = **2.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹**; F = eE = **3.2 × 10⁻¹⁵ N**
6. Q = CV = **1.98 × 10⁻³ C**; E = ½CV² = **8.9 × 10⁻³ J**
7. RC = 2.2 s; T½ = 0.69 × 2.2 = **1.5 s**
8. e⁻³ = **0.050 (5.0%)**
9. r = mv/BQ = **1.1 × 10⁻² m**
10. NΦ = 50 × 0.40 × 0.020 × cos 60° = **0.20 Wb-turns**
11. ε = 0.20/0.050 = **4.0 V**
12. V₀ = 3.0 × 2.0 = **6.0 V**; V_rms = **4.2 V**; T = 20 ms, so f = **50 Hz**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using height above the surface instead of distance from the centre of mass in GM/r² and −GM/r.
- Omitting the negative sign on gravitational potential, or failing to explain it (attractive force, zero at infinity).
- Quoting T² ∝ r³ when the question asks you to derive it.
- Using an hour or a day rather than seconds for T in orbit calculations.
- Saying a dielectric "stores more charge" without explaining the opposing field of the aligned polar molecules.
- Reading the half-time from a graph and calling it the time constant.
- Forgetting the current direction reverses for negative particles in Fleming's left-hand rule.
- Using θ to the coil plane instead of to the normal in BAN cos θ.
- Writing "Lenz's law: the emf opposes the flux" instead of "opposes the change in flux linkage".
- Calculating transmission loss as V²/R using the supply voltage rather than I²R with the line current.
