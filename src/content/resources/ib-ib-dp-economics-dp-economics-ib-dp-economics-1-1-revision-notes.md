## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Economics | The study of how people and societies choose to use scarce resources to satisfy wants |
| Social science | A discipline studying human behaviour, so its results depend on values, institutions and psychology as well as data |
| Microeconomics | The study of individual consumers, firms and markets |
| Macroeconomics | The study of the whole economy: total output, employment, the price level and trade |
| Scarcity | Limited resources set against unlimited wants |
| Choice | Selecting one use of resources over others because of scarcity |
| Opportunity cost | The value of the next best alternative forgone |
| Free good | A good with no opportunity cost, because using it leaves no less for others |
| Economic good | A good that uses scarce resources and so has an opportunity cost |
| Land / labour / capital / entrepreneurship | Natural resources / human effort and skill / made goods used in production / organising factors and bearing risk |
| Factor incomes | Rent (land), wages (labour), interest (capital), profit (entrepreneurship) |
| Production possibilities curve | The maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce with all resources fully and efficiently used, given technology |
| Circular flow of income | A model of real and money flows between households, firms and other sectors |
| Leakage | Income withdrawn from the flow: saving, taxes, imports |
| Injection | Spending added to the flow: investment, government spending, exports |

## The basic economic questions and systems

Every economy answers **what** (and how much) to produce, **how** to produce it, and **for whom**.

| System | Mechanism | Main risk |
|---|---|---|
| Free market | Prices and private decisions | Unequal access; some goods under-provided |
| Planned | State directives | Poor information; weak incentives |
| Mixed | Markets plus government intervention | Intervention may be costly or poorly targeted |

## PPC rules to recall

- **On the curve:** full employment and efficient use of resources.
- **Inside:** unemployment or inefficiency; output is being wasted.
- **Outside:** unattainable now.
- **Movement along the curve:** shows opportunity cost.
- **Concave (bowed out) curve:** increasing opportunity cost, because resources are specialised.
- **Straight line:** constant opportunity cost, because resources are equally suited to both goods.
- **Movement from inside towards the curve:** actual growth, using idle resources.
- **Outward shift:** growth in production possibilities from more or better resources or technology.
- **Pivot:** technology improves for one good only.
- **Inward shift:** resources lost, for example through disaster or war.
- **Capital goods versus consumer goods:** choosing more capital goods now means less consumption today but a larger outward shift later.

### Method: opportunity cost from a PPC table

1. Find the change in the good gained between two points.
2. Find the change in the good given up.
3. Divide the amount given up by the amount gained.
4. State the answer as "units of good Y per unit of good X".

**Worked reminder (Ostrel).** Ostrel can make 0 tablets and 90 tonnes of wheat, or 30 tablets and 72 tonnes. The opportunity cost of one tablet over this range is (90 - 72) / 30 = **0.6 tonnes of wheat**. If the next 30 tablets cost a further 33 tonnes, the cost rises to 33 / 30 = **1.1 tonnes**, so opportunity cost is increasing and the curve bows outwards.

## Circular flow method

1. Add saving, taxes and imports to get total leakages.
2. Add investment, government spending and exports to get total injections.
3. Injections greater than leakages: income tends to rise. Leakages greater: income tends to fall. Equal: income is stable.
4. Only the totals must match; S need not equal I, and T need not equal G.

**Worked reminder.** Ostrel: S = 30, T = 70, M = 40 (leakages 140); I = 35, G = 60, X = 30 (injections 125). Leakages exceed injections by 15, so income tends to fall.

## Exam traps

- Opportunity cost is **one** alternative, the next best, not a total of everything given up.
- Money is **not** capital; a family car is **not** capital.
- A point inside the PPC is **not** a recession by definition; it shows unemployed or inefficiently used resources from any cause.
- Actual growth is **not** a shift of the PPC.
- A PPC needs labelled axes with goods named; "good X" earns less than "solar panels" when the question gives the goods.
- Leakages and injections are compared as **totals**.
- Scarcity is **not** the same as poverty: rich economies face it too.

## Quick self-test

1. Why is economics described as a social science?
2. Give the factor income earned by each of the four factors of production.
3. A farmer can grow maize worth 8,000, beans worth 6,500 or cotton worth 7,200 on one field. She grows maize. What is the opportunity cost?
4. Explain how clean air can change from a free good to an economic good.
5. What does a point inside a PPC show?
6. A PPC passes through (0 boats, 50 fish) and (10 boats, 40 fish) and (20 boats, 20 fish). Calculate the opportunity cost of a boat on each segment and state the shape of the curve.
7. A new seed variety raises maximum fish output but not boat output. How does the PPC change?
8. Why might a country choose a point with more capital goods on its PPC?
9. Name the leakage and injection linked to the foreign sector.
10. Leakages total 400 and injections total 380. What happens to the flow of income?
11. Distinguish between real flows and money flows in the circular flow model.
12. Is a company's bank deposit classed as capital? Explain.

### Answers

1. It studies human behaviour, which is shaped by values, institutions and psychology, so results are not exact like a natural science.
2. Land earns rent, labour earns wages, capital earns interest, entrepreneurship earns profit.
3. The cotton worth **7,200**, the next best alternative. Beans are not part of it.
4. Where pollution is low, using air leaves no less for others; once pollution limits bind, clean air is scarce and allowing more pollution has a cost, so it becomes an economic good.
5. Unemployment or inefficient use of resources: more of both goods could be produced.
6. First segment: (50 - 40) / 10 = 1 fish per boat. Second: (40 - 20) / 10 = 2 fish per boat. Cost rises, so the curve is concave (bowed outwards).
7. It pivots outwards on the fish axis, with the boat intercept unchanged.
8. To add machinery and infrastructure that shift the PPC outwards in future, at the cost of less consumption now.
9. Imports are the leakage; exports are the injection.
10. Leakages exceed injections by 20, so the flow of income tends to fall.
11. Real flows are factor services and goods and services; money flows are factor incomes and spending, running the opposite way.
12. No. Money buys capital goods but produces nothing itself; capital means made goods used in production.
