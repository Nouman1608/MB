## SL vs HL weightings

| Component | SL | HL |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 (guided textual analysis, unseen texts) | 35% | 35% |
| Paper 2 (comparative essay, studied literary texts) | 35% | 25% |
| HL essay (independent coursework, 1,200-1,500 words) | — | 20% |
| Individual oral | 30% | 20% |

## What distinguishes HL

Not a longer exam — an **extra piece of independent written coursework** (the HL essay), on a
literary or non-literary body of work studied in class.

## The individual oral

- Common to both levels.
- A prepared spoken response analysing connections between a **literary** text and a
  **non-literary** text/extract studied in class.
- Explores how a **global issue of the student's choosing** is presented across the two.

## Exam traps

- Preparing the individual oral around only one text type, when it specifically requires connecting a literary and a non-literary text.
- HL students treating the HL essay as optional extra reading response, when it's a formally assessed, weighted component (20%).
- Confusing Paper 1's unseen textual analysis with Paper 2's comparative essay on studied texts — different skills, different preparation.

## Self-test

1. What specifically distinguishes HL assessment from SL in this course?
2. What two text types does the individual oral connect?
3. What is the word range for the HL essay?
4. What are Paper 1 and Paper 2 weighted at, at HL?

**Answers:** 1. The HL essay — an extra piece of independent written coursework only completed at Higher Level. 2. A literary text and a non-literary text or extract. 3. 1,200 to 1,500 words. 4. Paper 1: 35%, Paper 2: 25%.
