**This guide covers content area 3.2.1.6 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), The Ascension of Hazrat Muhammad (pbuh): "The events of Isra wal Mir'aj and their importance."** The journey is known as *al-Isra wal-Mi'raj*. *Isra* means the night journey from Makkah to Jerusalem. *Mi'raj* means the ascent through the heavens. The page tells the events in order, explains their meaning for the Prophet (pbuh) and for Muslims, and shows how to use the prescribed passage, Qur'an 17:1-2.

## When did it happen?

Scholars give different dates. Most place the journey in the tenth or eleventh year of the mission, after the Year of Sorrow and the rejection at Ta'if, and before the Hijrah. Muslims often mark the night on the 27th of Rajab, but the exact date is not certain. Its timing is significant. The Prophet (pbuh) had lost Abu Talib and Khadija (RA) and had been mistreated at Ta'if. The journey was an honour and a comfort from Allah at a moment of great sorrow.

## The Isra: the night journey

According to the accounts in Sahih al-Bukhari and Sahih Muslim:

1. The Prophet (pbuh) was resting near the Ka'aba (in the Hijr) when **Jibril (AS)** came to him.
2. Jibril (AS) prepared his heart with a washing using Zamzam water, and filled it with faith and wisdom.
3. He was brought **Buraq**, a white animal, smaller than a mule and larger than a donkey, which could take each step as far as it could see.
4. He travelled in one night from **al-Masjid al-Haram** in Makkah to **al-Masjid al-Aqsa** in Jerusalem.
5. At al-Aqsa he led the earlier prophets in prayer. This showed his position as the leader of all prophets and the final messenger.

This part of the journey is described directly in the Qur'an: "Exalted is He who took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed, to show him of Our signs" (17:1).

## The Mi'raj: the ascent through the heavens

From Jerusalem, Jibril (AS) took the Prophet (pbuh) up through the heavens. The hadith describes the main events:

| Heaven | Prophet met (traditional account) |
|---|---|
| First | Adam (AS) |
| Second | Isa (AS) and Yahya (AS) |
| Third | Yusuf (AS) |
| Fourth | Idris (AS) |
| Fifth | Harun (AS) |
| Sixth | Musa (AS) |
| Seventh | Ibrahim (AS), leaning against the Bayt al-Ma'mur, which angels visit every day |

At each gate the angels asked who was there, and welcomed him. Then he was taken to **Sidrat al-Muntaha**, the Lote Tree of the Furthest Boundary, beyond which no creation could go. There Allah spoke to him. He was shown Paradise and the Fire, and signs of Allah's power.

## The gift of the five daily prayers

At this meeting Allah gave the Prophet (pbuh) the command of **Salah**. At first, fifty prayers a day were commanded for his community. On his way back, Musa (AS) asked what he had been given and advised him to ask for a reduction, since his people would not be able to bear fifty. The Prophet (pbuh) returned several times, and each time the number was reduced, until it became five. Allah said that the five prayers would carry the reward of fifty. In this way the five daily prayers became the central act of worship.

Other gifts mentioned in the hadith include the last verses of Surah al-Baqarah and the promise that anyone who did not associate partners with Allah would be forgiven for serious sins.

## Return and the response of the Quraysh

The Prophet (pbuh) returned to Makkah the same night. In the morning he told the people. The Quraysh mocked him. They said that a trip to Jerusalem took a caravan a month, and some Muslims wavered. They asked him to describe Jerusalem, which he had never seen, and Allah made it clear to him, so he answered accurately.

When Abu Bakr (RA) heard the news, he said, "If he said it, then it is true." He was given the title **as-Siddiq** (the one who always affirms the truth). The event tested faith, and those who believed showed strong trust.

Scholars have different views on whether the journey was in body and spirit, or in a vision or dream. The majority hold that it took place in both body and spirit, and they point out that the wording "took His Servant" (17:1) suggests the whole person, not only a dream.

## Why the Isra and Mi'raj matter

| Importance | Explanation |
|---|---|
| Comfort | After the Year of Sorrow and Ta'if, Allah honoured His Prophet and showed him that his mission was still supported |
| The five daily prayers | The gift of Salah, a direct link between a believer and Allah; the Pillar given in the heavens |
| Status of the Prophet (pbuh) | He led all the prophets at al-Aqsa and met Allah's greatest signs |
| The unity of the prophets | He met earlier prophets, showing that all brought the same message of Tawhid |
| Jerusalem | Al-Aqsa is the third holiest site in Islam, after Makkah and Madinah, and was the first direction (qiblah) for prayer |
| Test of faith | The event separated those who trusted him from those who doubted |
| Mercy and ease | The reduction from fifty to five shows Allah's mercy |

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 17:1-2

"Exalted is He who took His Servant by night from al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa, whose surroundings We have blessed, to show him of Our signs. Indeed, He is the Hearing, the Seeing. And We gave Moses the Scripture and made it a guidance for the Children of Israel that you not take other than Me as Disposer of affairs."

- **"Exalted is He"** begins with praise of Allah's power, as the journey is beyond normal ability.
- **"took His Servant"** shows that the journey was Allah's act, and that the Prophet (pbuh) is called His servant, an honour.
- **"by night"** names the Isra.
- **"al-Masjid al-Haram to al-Masjid al-Aqsa"** names the two mosques.
- **"whose surroundings We have blessed"** shows the blessed status of the land around al-Aqsa.
- **"to show him of Our signs"** gives the purpose of the journey.
- **"We gave Moses the Scripture"** links the journey to the earlier prophets and the unity of revelation.
- **"that you not take other than Me as Disposer of affairs"** repeats the central teaching of Tawhid: rely only on Allah.

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why the gift of the five daily prayers was important. **[4]**

> It was a direct gift from Allah to the Prophet (pbuh) in the heavens [1], so the prayers became a link between each Muslim and Allah five times a day [1]. The reduction from fifty to five also showed Allah's mercy [1], since the Muslims were given a duty that was easier to perform but still carried a great reward [1].

## Common mistakes

- **Mixing up Isra and Mi'raj.** Isra is Makkah to Jerusalem; Mi'raj is the ascent to the heavens.
- **Writing that the prayers were given in Makkah by Jibril.** They were commanded directly by Allah during the Mi'raj.
- **Saying that all fifty prayers were kept.** The number was reduced to five.
- **Giving only the events.** The question asks for their importance, so add why each matters.
- **Forgetting the context.** The timing after the Year of Sorrow and Ta'if is part of the meaning.

## Exam technique

**Give** questions need a name (Buraq, Jibril, Sidrat al-Muntaha, al-Aqsa). **Describe** questions need the events in order. **Explain** questions need the importance. In the 12-mark question on 17:1-2, use "took His Servant by night" for the miracle, "to show him of Our signs" for the purpose and "Moses the Scripture" for the link between prophets, then judge whether the main importance of the journey is the gift of Salah or the honour and comfort to the Prophet (pbuh).
