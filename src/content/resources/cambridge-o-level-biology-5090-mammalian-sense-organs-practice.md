A: the blind spot

## Section B

**6.** A person walks from a dark room into bright sunlight.

**(a)** Describe the change in the size of the pupil. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how the muscles of the iris bring about this change. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why this response is useful. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain why this response is a reflex. **[2]**

**7.** A student reads a book and then looks up at a tree on the far side of a field.

**(a)** Describe the change in the ciliary muscles. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how this change affects the suspensory ligaments and the lens. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why this change helps the person see the tree clearly. **[2]**

**8.** The pupil diameter of a person was measured in three conditions.

| Condition | Pupil diameter (mm) |
|---|---|
| Bright light | 2.5 |
| Normal room light | 4.0 |
| Dim light | 7.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in pupil diameter from bright light to dim light. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage decrease in diameter from normal room light to bright light. **[2]**

**(c)** State which set of muscles is contracting in the dim light. **[1]**

**9.** A diagram of the eye is labelled with parts P, Q, R and S. P is the clear front window of the eye. Q is a ring of muscle that changes the size of the pupil. R is the layer at the back that contains receptors. S is the place where the optic nerve leaves the eye.

**(a)** Name parts P, Q, R and S. **[4]**

**(b)** State why a spot of light that falls on part S cannot be seen. **[1]**

**10.** Compare the pupil reflex with accommodation by stating **two** differences. **[2]**

**11.** Complete the table for a person looking at a near object. Use the words contract, relax, tight, slack, thicker or thinner.

| Part | State |
|---|---|
| Ciliary muscles | A |
| Suspensory ligaments | B |
| Lens | C |

**[3]**

**12.** A student states that the iris focuses light onto the retina. Explain why this is wrong, and state what the iris actually does. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** It refracts (bends) light [1]. **[1]**

**4.** A group of receptor cells [1] that respond to a specific stimulus [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The optic nerve [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** The pupil gets smaller [1].

**(b)** Receptors in the retina detect the bright light [1]. The circular muscles contract [1] and the radial muscles relax [1].

**(c)** It reduces the light entering the eye, so the retina is not damaged [1].

**(d)** It is rapid [1] and automatic [1]. **[7]**

**7.**

**(a)** The ciliary muscles relax [1].

**(b)** The suspensory ligaments become tight (tension increases) [1]. The lens is pulled thinner [1], so its shape is flatter [1].

**(c)** A thinner lens refracts light less [1], so the light from the distant tree is focused on the retina [1]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** (7.5 - 2.5) divided by 2.5 x 100 [1] = 200 per cent [1].

**(b)** (4.0 - 2.5) divided by 4.0 x 100 [1] = 37.5 per cent [1].

**(c)** The radial muscles [1]. **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** P is the cornea [1], Q is the iris [1], R is the retina [1], S is the blind spot [1].

**(b)** There are no light receptors at the blind spot [1]. **[5]**

**10.** Any two of: the pupil reflex changes the size of the pupil whereas accommodation changes the shape of the lens [1]; the pupil reflex responds to light intensity whereas accommodation responds to distance of the object [1]; the pupil reflex uses the muscles of the iris whereas accommodation uses the ciliary muscles [1]. **[2]**

**11.** A: contract [1]. B: slack [1]. C: thicker [1]. **[3]**

**12.** The lens, not the iris, focuses light onto the retina [1]. The iris is a ring of muscle that controls the size of the pupil [1], and so controls how much light enters the eye [1]. **[3]**

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up which iris muscles contract in bright and dim light.
- Writing that the lens moves, not that it changes shape.
- Leaving out the suspensory ligaments in an accommodation answer.
- Dividing by the final value instead of the starting value in a percentage change.
- Saying that the optic nerve contains receptors.
