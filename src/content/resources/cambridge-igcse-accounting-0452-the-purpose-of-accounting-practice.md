These questions cover sub-topic **1.1 The purpose of accounting**. Cambridge 0452 papers are not divided into lettered sections, so Sections A and B below simply group shorter and longer questions. The number in square brackets after the last part of each question is the total for the whole question. Businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** State the difference between book-keeping and accounting. **[2]**

**2.** Which activity is accounting rather than book-keeping?

A: posting credit sales to customers' accounts

B: balancing the bank columns of the cash book

C: comparing this year's profit margin with last year's

D: entering a credit note in the sales returns journal **[1]**

**3.** Which item increases a business's bank balance but is **not** revenue?

A: cash sales

B: commission received

C: a bank loan received

D: rent received from a tenant **[1]**

**4.** State **two** purposes of measuring the profit of a business. **[2]**

**5.** A shop's revenue rose from 40,000 to 46,000 and its profit fell from 8,000 to 7,360. Calculate the percentage change in revenue and the percentage change in profit. **[2]**

**6.** Name **two** groups outside a business who use its accounting information, and state **one** decision each group makes with it. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Mira Patel runs a printing business.

**(a)** For each task, state whether it is book-keeping or accounting: (i) writing up the purchases journal from suppliers' invoices; (ii) preparing the statement of financial position; (iii) balancing a supplier's account; (iv) explaining to Mira why her profit fell; (v) recording petty cash vouchers; (vi) recommending whether to buy a second printer.

**(b)** Explain why the quality of Mira's accounting depends on her book-keeping. **[8]**

**8.** Leon Abara owns a sandwich bar. During the year his bank balance rose from 1,500 to 7,900, and he says the business has had a very profitable year. During the year he paid in 5,000 he had inherited and sold an old van for 2,200. Revenue was 58,000 and expenses were 56,600. His drawings were 6,000.

**(a)** Calculate the rise in the bank balance.

**(b)** Calculate the profit for the year.

**(c)** Explain why Leon is wrong to judge the year from his bank balance.

**(d)** Calculate the change in Leon's capital for the year resulting from capital introduced, profit and drawings. **[7]**

**9.** Cleo's Hair Studio has these results.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 60,000 | 66,000 |
| Hair products used | 9,000 | 13,200 |
| Wages | 24,000 | 26,400 |
| Rent | 9,600 | 9,600 |
| Other expenses | 5,400 | 5,700 |

**(a)** Calculate the profit for each year.

**(b)** Calculate hair products and wages each as a percentage of revenue for both years.

**(c)** Explain what the figures show about the studio's progress and suggest **one** action Cleo could take. **[9]**

**10.** Northgate Bakery delivers bread to cafés. The owner is choosing between two options. Option 1: buy a van for 18,000, expected to last five years with a value of 3,000 at the end, and spend 4,200 a year running it. Option 2: pay a courier 650 a month.

**(a)** Calculate the yearly cost of each option, spreading the cost of the van evenly over its life.

**(b)** Advise the owner which option to choose. Justify your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Hollis Hardware sells to local builders on credit.

**(a)** Explain **two** ways accounting information helps the owner monitor the builders who buy on credit.

**(b)** Credit sales earn an extra gross profit of 5,200 a year. Last year irrecoverable debts on them were 2,100 and the extra office costs of chasing payment were 1,500. Advise the owner whether to continue selling on credit. **[7]**

**12.** Rafiq and Dina are partners who share profits equally. Their draft profit for the year is 36,400, but an expense of 2,800 has not been recorded.

**(a)** Calculate the correct profit for the year.

**(b)** Calculate each partner's share of the draft profit and of the correct profit.

**(c)** Explain **two** consequences of the partners relying on the draft profit. **[7]**

## Answers

**1.** Book-keeping is the detailed recording of financial transactions [1]. Accounting uses those records to prepare, analyse, interpret and communicate financial information [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**2.** **C** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** **C** [1]. A loan must be repaid, so it is a liability, not income. Total: 1 mark.

**4.** Any two [2]: to judge performance; to decide how much the owner can take as drawings; to support a loan application; to calculate tax; to compare with earlier periods or other businesses; to share profit between partners or pay dividends. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Revenue: 6,000 / 40,000 x 100 = **+15%** [1]. Profit: 640 / 8,000 x 100 = **-8%** [1]. Total: 2 marks.

*Examiner insight:* Divide by the old figure. Using 46,000 gives 13.0%, which is wrong.

**6.** Two groups, one mark each [2], with a matching decision, one mark each [2]: a bank decides whether to lend; a supplier decides whether to allow credit and how much; the tax authority decides the tax due; an investor decides whether to put money in. Total: 4 marks.

**7. (a)** (i) Book-keeping [1]; (ii) accounting [1]; (iii) book-keeping [1]; (iv) accounting [1]; (v) book-keeping [1]; (vi) accounting [1].

**(b)** The financial statements and analysis are prepared from the recorded transactions [1], so any omitted or misposted entry makes the statements and any advice based on them wrong [1]. Total: 8 marks.

**8. (a)** 7,900 - 1,500 = **6,400** [1].

**(b)** 58,000 - 56,600 = **1,400** [1].

**(c)** Of the money paid in, 5,000 + 2,200 = 7,200 came from capital introduced and the sale of an asset, which are not revenue [1]. Without them the bank balance would have fallen by 800 [1]. Profit, which measures performance, was only 1,400, so the year was not very profitable [1].

**(d)** 5,000 + 1,400 - 6,000 [1] = **+400** [1]. Total: 7 marks.

*Examiner insight:* In (c), quantify the non-revenue receipts. "The bank balance is not profit" alone earns at most one mark.

**9. (a)** Year 1: 60,000 - 48,000 = **12,000** [1]. Year 2: 66,000 - 54,900 = **11,100** [1].

**(b)** Hair products: 9,000 / 60,000 = **15%** [1]; 13,200 / 66,000 = **20%** [1]. Wages: 24,000 / 60,000 = **40%** [1]; 26,400 / 66,000 = **40%** [1].

**(c)** Revenue rose by 10% but profit fell by 900 [1]. Wages kept the same share of revenue, so the fall is caused by hair products rising from 15% to 20% of revenue [1]. Suggestion, for example: find a cheaper supplier, reduce waste of products, or raise prices for treatments that use the most products [1]. Total: 9 marks.

**10. (a)** Van: (18,000 - 3,000) / 5 = 3,000 a year [1]; 3,000 + 4,200 = **7,200** a year [1]. Courier: 650 x 12 = **7,800** a year [1].

**(b)** The van is cheaper by 600 a year, which increases profit [1]. However, 18,000 must be paid at the start, which the bakery may not have or may need to borrow, while the courier needs no large payment and can be stopped if trade falls [1]. A justified recommendation either way, for example: buy the van if the bakery has the cash, because of the yearly saving [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**11. (a)** Two ways, each identified [1] and explained [1], for example: the customer accounts show how much each builder owes, so overdue balances can be chased [2]; comparing amounts owed with credit sales shows whether builders are taking longer to pay, so credit limits can be reduced [2]; records of irrecoverable debts show which customers should not be given credit again.

**(b)** 5,200 - 2,100 - 1,500 = 1,600 [1]. Credit sales still add 1,600 a year to profit, so continue [1], but tighten credit control, for example by setting limits, so that irrecoverable debts fall [1]. Total: 7 marks.

**12. (a)** 36,400 - 2,800 = **33,600** [1].

**(b)** Draft: 36,400 / 2 = **18,200** each [1]. Correct: 33,600 / 2 = **16,800** each [1].

**(c)** Two consequences, each stated [1] and explained [1]: each partner would believe they had earned 1,400 more than they had and might take drawings the business cannot afford [2]; tax would be worked out on a profit that is too high [2]; a lender could be given a misleading view of the business. Total: 7 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing book-keeping and accounting by job title rather than by the work done.
- Treating a rise in the bank balance as profit, or drawings as an expense.
- Calculating percentage change on the new figure.
- Giving a recommendation with no figure, or figures with no recommendation.
- Writing one long point where the question asks for two separately explained points.
