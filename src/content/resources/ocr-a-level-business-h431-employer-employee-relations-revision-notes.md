Condensed notes on the **Employer/employee relations** content (5.13 on this site) in the Area of Study **Management**, OCR A Level Business H431, Version 2.1 (January 2026), final assessment summer 2027. Full explanations and three worked examples are in the [employer/employee relations study guide](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-employer-employee-relations/); test yourself with the [employer/employee relations practice questions](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-h431-employer-employee-relations-practice/). The whole topic is in the [human resources revision notes](/resources/ocr-a-level-business-human-resources-revision-notes/).

## What the specification lists

- Meaning of employer/employee relations; benefits of good relations and costs of poor relations for the business and stakeholders.
- Legislation: contracts of employment, minimum wage, health and safety, equality, data protection, dismissal and grievance; its impact and importance.
- Employee participation: meaning; industrial democracy, participative decision making, works councils; impact and importance.
- Trade unions: meaning, role, advantages and disadvantages of membership, the changing nature of union activity and membership.

## Core recall

### Meaning

Employer/employee relations: the quality of dealings between management and workforce, including communication, negotiation and handling of disputes.

### Good and poor relations

| Good relations | Poor relations |
|---|---|
| Lower absence and turnover | Strikes and industrial action |
| Higher productivity, quality | High absence and turnover |
| Acceptance of change | Resistance to change |
| Fewer grievances and claims | Tribunal and legal costs |
| Good employer reputation | Recruitment problems |

### Legislation in six lines

| Area | Core requirement | Effect on business |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts | Written terms; changes by agreement | Admin; fewer disputes |
| Minimum wage | Lowest legal hourly pay | Higher labour costs |
| Health and safety | Safe workplace, training, risk assessment | Training and equipment costs |
| Equality | No discrimination on protected characteristics | Process costs; risk of claims; wider talent pool |
| Data protection | Lawful, secure use of personal data | Systems, training; fines |
| Dismissal and grievance | Fair reason and fair procedure; right to raise grievances | Documented procedures |

Importance: protects staff and sets a level playing field, but raises cost and admin, especially for small firms. Non-compliance brings fines, awards and reputation damage.

### Employee participation

| Type | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Industrial democracy | Employees share in running the business, for example staff directors |
| Participative decision making | Managers involve staff in decisions, through meetings or suggestion schemes |
| Works council | A standing forum of elected employees and managers |

- Benefits: better decisions, commitment, fewer disputes.
- Costs: slower decisions, meeting time, frustration if views are ignored.

### Trade unions

- **Trade union:** an organisation of workers that represents members.
- Role: collective bargaining, advice and representation, legal help, campaigning, industrial action.
- For employees: voice, protection, pay; costs are subscriptions and possible conflict.
- For the business: one channel for negotiation, but higher pay costs and possible strikes.
- Changing nature: membership has fallen in many private sectors; weaker bargaining power; more individual negotiation; more employer-led consultation. Activity has moved towards advice and negotiation.

### Evaluating: factors to mention

- Size of the cost against revenue or profit.
- Cause of the dispute and whether it can be resolved.
- Size of the business and the share of labour in costs.
- Short run against long run.
- Which stakeholder is affected.

## Quick worked reminder

Corrin Metals (invented): a 3-day strike on 200 staff. Lost contribution 3 × 120 × £450 = £162,000; wages not paid 200 × £110 × 3 = £66,000; net cost £96,000. Larchmoor Cafés: a minimum wage rise from £11.40 to £12.00 for 15 staff working 30 hours a week costs £936 a person, £14,040 in total, which is 5.3% of the £266,760 wage bill.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Employer/employee relations | The quality of dealings between management and workers |
| Collective bargaining | Negotiation of pay and conditions between unions and employers |
| Industrial action | Strikes, go-slows or work-to-rule |
| Grievance | A complaint raised by an employee |
| Works council | A forum where employees and managers discuss issues |
| Industrial democracy | Employees share in running a business |
| Trade union | An organisation representing workers |
| Contract of employment | The agreement setting out the terms of a job |

## Exam traps

- Say what a law requires and what it costs, not just its name.
- Unions do more than strike: negotiation and advice are the main activities.
- Participation involves workers in decisions; it does not mean they decide everything.
- Always consider more than one stakeholder.
- Use figures to show how large a cost or benefit is.

## Self-test

1. Define employer/employee relations.
2. Give two costs of poor relations.
3. Name the six areas of legislation in the specification.
4. A business has 100 staff each working 230 days; absence falls from 5% to 4%. How many days are saved?
5. Give one effect of the minimum wage on a café.
6. What is a works council?
7. Distinguish between industrial democracy and participative decision making.
8. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of participation.
9. State two roles of a trade union.
10. Give one disadvantage of trade union membership for a business.
11. Explain one effect of falling union membership on a business.
12. A union's membership falls from 4,000 to 3,400. Calculate the percentage change.

### Answers

1. The quality of dealings between management and the workforce, including communication, negotiation and settling disputes.
2. Any two: strikes, high absence, high turnover, tribunal costs, damaged reputation.
3. Contracts of employment, minimum wage, health and safety, equality, data protection, dismissal and grievance.
4. Total days = 100 × 230 = 23,000. 1% of 23,000 = **230 days**.
5. It raises labour costs, so the café may raise prices, cut hours or accept lower profit.
6. A standing forum of elected employee representatives and managers to discuss working conditions and plans.
7. Industrial democracy gives employees a share in running the business, such as a seat on the board; participative decision making involves staff in specific decisions, for example through meetings.
8. Advantage: better decisions or more commitment. Disadvantage: slower decisions or the cost of meetings.
9. Any two: collective bargaining, representing members in disputes, advice, legal help, industrial action.
10. Higher pay costs, possible strikes, or slower change.
11. Bargaining power falls, so the business may negotiate with individuals and face fewer disputes, but it loses a single channel for communicating with workers.
12. (3,400 − 4,000) ÷ 4,000 × 100 = **−15%**.
