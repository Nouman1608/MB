## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Manufacturing account | Statement that finds the cost of making the goods finished in the year |
| Direct cost | Cost you can trace to the goods: raw materials and direct wages |
| Prime cost | Cost of raw materials used plus direct wages |
| Overheads | Factory running costs that cannot be traced to one unit |
| Factory cost of finished goods | Prime cost plus overheads |
| Cost of sales (manufacturer) | Opening finished goods + factory cost of finished goods - closing finished goods |

## The statements in order

1. Manufacturing account: raw materials used, then prime cost, then overheads, then factory cost of finished goods.
2. Income statement: net sales, cost of sales using the factory cost, gross profit, expenses, profit for the year.
3. Statement of financial position: two inventories lines, machinery with accumulated depreciation, then equity.

The specification says the provision for unrealised profit will not be examined, so finished goods move from the factory at cost.

## Layout to learn

| Line in the manufacturing account | Add or deduct |
|---|---|
| Opening inventory of raw materials | Add |
| Purchases of raw materials | Add |
| Carriage inwards | Add |
| Returns outwards | Deduct |
| Closing inventory of raw materials | Deduct |
| Direct wages | Add after materials used |
| Prime cost | Subtotal |
| Each overhead | Add, after adjustment |
| Factory cost of finished goods | Total |

## Where each cost belongs

| Cost | Manufacturing account | Income statement |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials purchased | Yes | No |
| Carriage inwards on materials | Yes | No |
| Carriage outwards | No | Yes |
| Direct wages | Yes | No |
| Indirect factory wages | Yes | No |
| Factory power, insurance, rent | Yes | No |
| Depreciation of machinery | Yes | No |
| Depreciation of office equipment | No | Yes |
| Office salaries, selling expenses | No | Yes |
| Opening and closing finished goods | No | Yes |
| Closing raw materials | Yes (deduct) | No |

## Adjustments in steps

1. Read the trial balance and the notes, and list every adjustment before you start.
2. Other payables: add to the cost. Other receivables (prepayments): deduct from the cost.
3. Depreciation: straight line = (cost - residual value) / years, if a residual value is given. Reducing balance = rate x carrying amount.
4. Shared costs: split by the fraction given, then put each part in its section.
5. Provision for doubtful debts: new provision less old provision. Only the change goes to the income statement.
6. Goods for own use: deduct at cost from cost of sales and add to drawings.
7. Check: net assets must equal equity.

## Worked reminder: Brimwell Candles

Opening raw materials 2,000; purchases 18,000; returns outwards 500; carriage inwards 400; closing raw materials 2,600; direct wages 9,700. Indirect wages 3,200; insurance paid 1,800 with 300 prepaid; power paid 2,100 with 200 owing; machine cost 24,000, residual value 4,000, life 5 years, straight line. Opening finished goods 3,000; closing 4,500; net sales 52,000.

| Step | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Raw materials used | 2,000 + 18,000 - 500 + 400 - 2,600 | 17,300 |
| Prime cost | 17,300 + 9,700 | 27,000 |
| Insurance | 1,800 - 300 | 1,500 |
| Power | 2,100 + 200 | 2,300 |
| Depreciation | (24,000 - 4,000) / 5 | 4,000 |
| Overheads | 3,200 + 1,500 + 2,300 + 4,000 | 11,000 |
| Factory cost of finished goods | 27,000 + 11,000 | 38,000 |
| Cost of sales | 3,000 + 38,000 - 4,500 | 36,500 |
| Gross profit | 52,000 - 36,500 | 15,500 |

### Continuing Brimwell Candles to profit

Now add these facts. The owner took finished goods that cost 600 for own use. Selling expenses are 4,100. Office wages paid are 5,300 and 400 is owing. Office equipment depreciation is 800. Trade receivables are 6,000, the old provision for doubtful debts is 150 and the new provision is 4% of trade receivables.

| Line | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Cost of sales | 3,000 + 38,000 - 4,500 - 600 | 35,900 |
| Gross profit | 52,000 - 35,900 | 16,100 |
| Office wages | 5,300 + 400 | 5,700 |
| Provision change | 4% of 6,000 = 240, less 150 | 90 |
| Total expenses | 4,100 + 5,700 + 800 + 90 | 10,690 |
| Profit for the year | 16,100 - 10,690 | 5,410 |

The 600 of goods also increases drawings by 600 in the equity section.

## Income statement layout for a manufacturer

| Block | Contents |
|---|---|
| Net sales | Revenue less sales returns |
| Cost of sales | Opening finished goods, plus factory cost of finished goods, less closing finished goods, less goods for own use |
| Gross profit | Net sales less cost of sales |
| Expenses | Selling, distribution, administration, depreciation of non-factory assets, irrecoverable debts, change in the provision for doubtful debts |
| Profit for the year | Gross profit less expenses |

Income due (such as rent receivable) is added to profit as other income. Income received in advance is deducted from the income of the year.

## Statement of financial position headings

| Heading | What a manufacturer shows |
|---|---|
| Non-current assets | Machinery, factory buildings, office equipment, each at cost, accumulated depreciation and carrying amount |
| Current assets | Raw materials inventory, finished goods inventory, trade receivables less provision, other receivables, bank |
| Current liabilities | Trade payables, other payables, any overdraft |
| Equity | Opening capital, plus profit for the year, less drawings (including goods taken for own use) |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Prime cost and factory cost.** Prime cost is materials and direct wages only. Factory cost adds overheads.
- **Direct wages and indirect wages.** Direct wages are paid to those who make the goods. Indirect wages are paid to supervisors and cleaners, so they are overheads.
- **Carriage inwards and carriage outwards.** Inwards is part of material cost. Outwards is a selling cost.
- **Raw materials inventory and finished goods inventory.** Raw materials adjust the manufacturing account. Finished goods adjust the cost of sales.
- **Purchases and factory cost.** A trader uses purchases in cost of sales. A manufacturer uses the factory cost of finished goods.

## Commenting on results

Use figures from the statements and give a reason.

- Overheads divided by prime cost shows how heavy the running costs are against the direct costs.
- Gross profit divided by net sales shows the margin left after the cost of making and selling the goods.
- Say what could cause a change, such as a supplier price, wage rates or selling prices, and state that you would need more years to see a trend.

## Quick self-test

1. State what is included in prime cost.
2. Is carriage inwards on raw materials a manufacturing account cost or an income statement expense?
3. Opening raw materials 1,200, purchases 9,800, closing raw materials 1,500, direct wages 6,000. Calculate prime cost.
4. Factory power paid is 4,200 and 350 is owing. State the overhead cost.
5. Rent of 12,000 is shared two-thirds factory and one-third office. How much is a factory overhead?
6. Machinery cost 50,000 and accumulated depreciation is 12,000. Depreciation is 15% on the reducing balance. Calculate the charge.
7. Prime cost is 40,000 and overheads are 12,500. Calculate the factory cost of finished goods.
8. Using question 7, opening finished goods are 2,000 and closing finished goods are 3,000. Calculate cost of sales.
9. Express the overheads in question 7 as a percentage of prime cost.
10. Is the provision for unrealised profit examined, according to the specification?

### Answers

1. Cost of raw materials used plus direct wages.
2. A manufacturing account cost, as part of the cost of raw materials.
3. 1,200 + 9,800 - 1,500 + 6,000 = 15,500.
4. 4,200 + 350 = 4,550.
5. Two-thirds of 12,000 = 8,000.
6. 15% of (50,000 - 12,000) = 15% of 38,000 = 5,700.
7. 40,000 + 12,500 = 52,500.
8. 2,000 + 52,500 - 3,000 = 51,500.
9. 12,500 / 40,000 x 100 = 31.25%, or 31.3% to 1 decimal place.
10. No. The specification says it will not be examined.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving office expenses or office depreciation in the manufacturing account.
- Forgetting to deduct closing raw materials when finding the cost of materials used.
- Adding returns outwards instead of deducting them.
- Treating indirect wages as direct wages.
- Ignoring an amount owing or a prepayment when finding the overhead.
- Using the original cost, not the carrying amount, for reducing balance depreciation.
- Showing a single inventories figure in the statement of financial position.
- Writing comments with no figure and no reason.
