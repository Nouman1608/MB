**These notes condense AQA GCSE History 8145 Paper 1 Section A, the period study, for fast revision.** Use the section for your school's option -- America 1840-1895, Germany 1890-1945, Russia 1894-1945 or America 1920-1973 -- plus the technique sheet for the six questions, which is the same for every option. Full explanations are in the [period studies study guide](/resources/gcse-history-period-studies-study-guide/); practice is in the [period studies practice questions](/resources/gcse-history-period-studies-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA GCSE HISTORY (8145) -- 3.2.1 SECTION A: PERIOD STUDIES** -- AA America, 1840-1895: Expansion and consolidation; AB Germany, 1890-1945: Democracy and dictatorship; AC Russia, 1894-1945: Tsardom and communism; AD America, 1920-1973: Opportunity and inequality. Each option has three parts, and all three are tested every series.

## Technique sheet: questions 1-6 (40 marks)

| Q | Marks | Do this | Avoid this |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Differ | 4 | State the main difference in what A and B *say*, with a short quotation from each | Describing each separately |
| 2 Why differ | 4 | Explain using who wrote each, when and why (provenance) | "One is biased" without detail |
| 3 Convincing | 8 | Test each interpretation against your knowledge, then judge which is more convincing | Ignoring one interpretation |
| 4 Describe | 4 | Two features, each with a developed detail | Explaining causes |
| 5 In what ways | 8 | Two or more changes, each explained with evidence | Narrative with no "change" |
| 6 Essay | 12 | Both bullets explained, then a reasoned judgement on which mattered more | One bullet only; a conclusion that just repeats |

## Worked example: question 2

*Explain why the authors of Interpretations A and B might have different views about the Stresemann era.* **[4]**

Strong answer pattern: "Interpretation A was written by **a German banker in 1928**, when US loans were still flowing, so he stresses recovery. Interpretation B is by **a farmer writing in 1932**, after the Depression, who had never shared the prosperity, so he stresses its weakness." (Both authors here are invented examples.) Use the ascription -- that is where the marks are.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Spending too long on questions 1-2 (4 marks each) and rushing question 6.
- In question 3, quoting the interpretations at length instead of testing them against knowledge.
- Treating question 4 ("Describe") as "Explain".
- In question 6, not comparing the two bullets in the conclusion.
- Dates mixed up across options (for example, Sand Creek 1864 versus Wounded Knee 1890).

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to know historians' names?**
No. AQA says the interpretations do not require knowledge of historiography.

**How many words is each interpretation?**
Around 50-100 words, each with an ascription telling you who wrote it.

**Can question 6 cover more than one part?**
Yes, it may focus on one part or on a development that runs across parts.

## Self-test

1. Which 1887 law broke up reservations? *The Dawes Act.*
2. What did the Enabling Act allow? *Hitler's government to pass laws without the Reichstag.*
3. What replaced War Communism? *The New Economic Policy (1921).*
4. Which 1941 policy supplied Britain? *Lend-Lease.*
5. How many marks is question 6? *12.*
