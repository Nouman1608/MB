These questions cover section **3.3.14 Organic synthesis** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification
(7404/7405), version 1.1, 1 December 2015, for AS and A-level exams from June 2016 onwards. The section is
**A-level only**, so every question here is A-level only. It is examined on A-level Paper 2 (organic
chemistry), and A-level Paper 3 can test any content. Atom economy calculations use the formula in 3.1.2.5.

Learn the ideas first in the [organic synthesis study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-synthesis/)
and the [revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-organic-synthesis-revision-notes/). The course hub is

**Data for this set.** Relative atomic masses: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0, Na = 23.0, Br = 79.9.

## Questions

**1.** Explain two reasons why chemists aim to design processes that do not require a solvent. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why chemists aim to design processes that use non-hazardous starting materials. **[2]**

**3.** Propylamine can be made from 1-bromopropane and excess ammonia:

CH₃CH₂CH₂Br + 2NH₃ → CH₃CH₂CH₂NH₂ + NH₄Br

Calculate the percentage atom economy for making propylamine. **[2]**

**4.** State the reagent or reagents needed for each one-step conversion.

**(a)** Butanal to butan-1-ol. **[1]**
**(b)** Benzene to nitrobenzene. **[2]**

**5.** Outline a two-step synthesis of butanone from 2-bromobutane. For each step give the reagent and
conditions, and name the intermediate. **[4]**

**6.** Cyclohexanone is converted into compound S by this sequence:

cyclohexanone → (NaBH₄, aqueous) → P → (concentrated H₃PO₄, heat) → Q → (HBr) → R → (KCN in aqueous
ethanol, reflux) → S

Identify P, Q, R and S. **[4]**

**7.** A three-step route has step yields of 78%, 64% and 90%. Each step has a 1 : 1 mole ratio. A chemist
starts with 0.250 mol of starting material. The final product has Mr = 88.0. Calculate the mass of final
product obtained. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**8.** Propan-2-ol can be made by two routes.

Route 1: CH₃CH=CH₂ + H₂O → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ (steam, acid catalyst)
Route 2: CH₃CH=CH₂ + HBr → CH₃CHBrCH₃, then CH₃CHBrCH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + NaBr

**(a)** Calculate the overall percentage atom economy of route 2, taking the reactants as propene, HBr and
NaOH. **[2]**
**(b)** State the percentage atom economy of route 1. **[1]**
**(c)** Give two other reasons why route 1 is the better route. **[2]**

**9.** Devise a three-step synthesis of N-ethylethanamide, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃, starting from ethene. For each step
give the reagent and any conditions, and name the organic product. **[6]**

**10.** Compound T is 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile, (CH₃)₂C(OH)CN.

**(a)** Devise a four-step synthesis of T from 1-bromopropane. For each step give the reagent and
conditions, and name the organic product. **[8]**
**(b)** State whether T is optically active and give a reason. **[1]**

**11.** N-phenylethanamide, C₆H₅NHCOCH₃, can be made from benzene in three steps. The last step uses ethanoic
anhydride.

**(a)** Devise the synthesis. For each step give the reagent and conditions, and name the organic product.
**[6]**
**(b)** Give two industrial advantages of using ethanoic anhydride rather than ethanoyl chloride in the
last step. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Energy is needed to separate the solvent from the product, for example by distillation [1]. The solvent
becomes waste that must be disposed of or recycled, and many solvents are flammable or toxic [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "Explain two reasons" asks for two different points; "it is cheaper" with no reason why
is unlikely to gain credit.

**2.** Hazardous substances put workers and the environment at risk during storage, transport and use [1].
Avoiding them removes the need for costly safety controls such as protective equipment and special
containers [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each point to a consequence (risk to people, extra cost); "they are dangerous" on its
own repeats the question.

**3.** Sum of Mr of reactants = 122.9 + 2(17.0) = 156.9 [1]; atom economy = 59.0 ÷ 156.9 × 100 = **37.6%** [1].
**[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The coefficient 2 in front of NH₃ must be used; leaving it out gives the wrong
denominator, though a correct method with that slip may still earn the method mark.

**4. (a)** **Sodium tetrahydridoborate(III), NaBH₄**, in aqueous solution [1].
**(b)** **Concentrated nitric acid** [1] and **concentrated sulfuric acid** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Write "concentrated" for both acids in (b); dilute acids do not generate the nitronium
ion.

**5.** Step 1: aqueous sodium hydroxide [1], warm; the intermediate is **butan-2-ol** [1]. Step 2: acidified
potassium dichromate(VI) [1], heat under reflux, to give **butanone** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Aqueous" matters in step 1. KOH in ethanol favours elimination and gives butenes, which
breaks the route.

**6.** P is **cyclohexanol** [1]. Q is **cyclohexene** [1]. R is **bromocyclohexane** [1]. S is the nitrile
**C₆H₁₁CN** (cyclohexanecarbonitrile), with one more carbon than R [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A correct structure is acceptable where a name is hard; check that S has seven carbons,
because the CN carbon is new.

**7.** Overall fraction = 0.78 × 0.64 × 0.90 = 0.44928 [1]; amount = 0.250 × 0.44928 = 0.1123 mol [1];
mass = 0.1123 × 88.0 = **9.88 g** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded values between steps; rounding to 0.449 and then 0.112 gives 9.86 g, which
is wrong at 3 s.f. Error carried forward is usually allowed after an early slip.

**8. (a)** Sum of Mr of reactants = 42.0 + 80.9 + 40.0 = 162.9 [1]; atom economy =
60.0 ÷ 162.9 × 100 = **36.8%** [1].
**(b)** **100%**: addition gives a single product [1].
**(c)** Route 1 has one step instead of two, so the overall yield is likely to be higher and less energy and
time are used [1]. Route 1 produces no sodium bromide waste and uses fewer reagents [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c) "higher atom economy" is not an "other" reason; two separate further points are
needed.

**9.** Step 1: **hydrogen bromide** [1] gives **bromoethane**, CH₃CH₂Br [1]. Step 2: **excess ammonia** in
ethanol, heated in a sealed container [1], gives **ethylamine** (ethanamine), CH₃CH₂NH₂ [1]. Step 3:
**ethanoyl chloride** (or ethanoic anhydride) [1] gives **N-ethylethanamide**, CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* "Excess" ammonia is part of the condition in step 2; without it, further substitution
gives secondary and tertiary amines.

**10. (a)** Step 1: KOH in ethanol, hot, under reflux [1] gives **propene** [1].
Step 2: steam with an acid catalyst such as phosphoric acid [1] gives **propan-2-ol** as the major product [1].
Step 3: acidified potassium dichromate(VI), heat under reflux [1] gives **propanone** [1].
Step 4: KCN, followed by dilute acid [1] gives **T, 2-hydroxy-2-methylpropanenitrile** [1].
**(b)** **Not optically active**: the carbon bonded to OH and CN also carries two identical CH₃ groups, so it
is not a chiral centre [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* Step 2 must give propan-2-ol; propan-1-ol would oxidise to propanal or propanoic acid,
not propanone, so naming the major product shows the route works.

**11. (a)** Step 1: concentrated nitric acid and concentrated sulfuric acid [1] give **nitrobenzene** [1].
Step 2: reduction with tin and concentrated hydrochloric acid, then sodium hydroxide [1], gives
**phenylamine** [1].
Step 3: ethanoic anhydride [1] gives **N-phenylethanamide** and ethanoic acid [1].
**(b)** Ethanoic anhydride is **cheaper** [1]. It is **less corrosive**, reacts less vigorously with water and
does not release hydrogen chloride gas [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* For (b), give two distinct advantages; "safer" and "less hazardous" count as one idea
unless you say why.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding step yields instead of multiplying them.
- Missing coefficients (such as 2NH₃) from the atom economy denominator.
- Giving a reagent with no conditions, or the wrong solvent (aqueous vs ethanolic KOH).
- Forgetting "excess" ammonia for a primary amine, or "excess" oxidant and reflux for a carboxylic acid.
- Ignoring the major product when an unsymmetrical alkene reacts with steam or HBr.
- Using NaBH₄ to reduce a nitrile or a nitro compound.
- Not counting carbons: KCN is needed whenever the chain grows by one.
- Giving one vague idea ("safer", "greener") for two marks.
