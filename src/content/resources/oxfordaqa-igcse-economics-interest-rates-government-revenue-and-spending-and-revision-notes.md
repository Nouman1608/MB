## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Interest rate | What borrowing costs and what saving earns, as an annual percentage |
| Producers | Firms that make goods or provide services |
| Government revenue | Money the government receives, mainly from taxes |
| Direct tax | Charged on what a person or firm earns, and paid by that taxpayer |
| Indirect tax | Tax on spending, paid by the buyer and collected by the seller |
| Progressive tax | The share of income paid climbs as income climbs |
| Regressive tax | The share of income paid shrinks as income climbs |
| Proportional tax | Takes the same percentage at every income |
| Budget deficit | Spending greater than revenue, financed by borrowing |

## Formulas and rules

| Item | Rule |
|---|---|
| Interest for one year | sum × rate |
| Average tax rate | tax paid ÷ income × 100 |
| Tax with bands | rate × only the slice of income inside each band, then add |
| Tax share of income | tax ÷ income × 100 |
| Tax inside a price that includes a rate r | price × r ÷ (100 + r) |
| Deficit | spending − revenue |

## Interest rates in steps

**Consumers.**

| If the rate rises | Effect |
|---|---|
| Saving | More attractive: higher reward and a higher opportunity cost of spending |
| Borrowing | Dearer: fewer loans and mortgages; variable-rate borrowers pay more |
| Spending | Tends to fall: more saving, less borrowing, less income left after interest |

A fall in the rate pushes each of these the other way.

**Producers.**

| If the rate rises | Effect |
|---|---|
| Saving | Retained profit earns more in a deposit, so keeping cash is more attractive |
| Borrowing | Loans cost more, so firms borrow less |
| Investing | Fewer projects earn more than the interest charged, so investment falls |

**Method for an investment decision.**

1. Work out the expected return: extra annual profit ÷ cost × 100.
2. Work out the interest cost: loan × rate.
3. Compare. Invest if the return is above the interest rate (or the profit exceeds the interest).

**Worked reminder.** A firm spends 30,000 cu on equipment that adds 2,100 cu to annual profit. The return is 2,100 ÷ 30,000 = 7%. A loan at 5% costs 1,500 cu, leaving a net gain of 600 cu, so it invests. At 8% the cost is 2,400 cu, so it does not.

**Chain for a longer answer.** Rate rises → borrowing is dearer and saving is rewarded → households and firms spend and invest less → total demand falls → output and prices grow more slowly. Then evaluate (see below).

## Sharia-compliant alternatives

Islamic finance does not allow riba, a fixed charge for lending money. The return is linked to trade, an asset or profit.

| Form | One-line summary |
|---|---|
| Mudarabah | Funds from one party, management by the other; profit split in an agreed ratio |
| Musharakah | Both put in funds; profit shared by agreement, losses by contribution |
| Murabaha | Bank buys the item and sells it on at cost plus a disclosed mark-up, paid in instalments |
| Ijara | Bank owns the asset and charges rent |
| Sukuk | Certificates giving a share in an asset and its income |

**Worked reminder.** A bank buys equipment costing 20,000 cu and sells it to a firm at a 5% mark-up: 20,000 × 1.05 = 21,000 cu, so the mark-up is 1,000 cu. It is agreed at the start, whatever happens to market rates later.

**Point to make.** Sharia-compliant finance is not free: the provider still earns a mark-up, rent or profit share. Because providers may still price with an eye on market rates, a rate rise can still reduce the demand for finance.

## Government revenue and spending

* Revenue: income tax, corporation tax, social insurance contributions, sales tax or VAT, excise duties, import duties, property taxes, plus state-enterprise profits, fees and asset sales.
* Borrowing is not revenue. It is how a deficit is financed.
* Spending: health, education, social protection (pensions and benefits), defence, transport and infrastructure, law and order, debt interest.
* Every spending choice has an opportunity cost.
* Spending adds to demand. A tax cut raises disposable income and so spending. A tax rise reduces them.

## Direct against indirect, progressive against regressive

| | Direct | Indirect |
|---|---|---|
| Examples | Income tax, corporation tax | Sales tax, VAT, excise, import duties |
| Based on | Income or profit | Spending |
| Collected from | The taxpayer, often through employers | The seller, who adds it to the price |
| Usual effect | Often progressive | Tends to be regressive |

**Worked reminder (bands).** Country E charges 0% on the first 5,000 cu and 25% above. On 15,000 cu the tax is (15,000 − 5,000) × 0.25 = 2,500 cu, an average rate of 16.7%. On 45,000 cu it is 40,000 × 0.25 = 10,000 cu, or 22.2%. Higher income means a higher average rate, so it is progressive.

**Worked reminder (regressive).** A tax takes 600 cu from an income of 10,000 cu (6%) and 2,400 cu from an income of 80,000 cu (3%). The higher earner pays a larger sum yet a smaller share, so it is regressive.

**Worked reminder (sales tax).** A 15% sales tax on a 60 cu item adds 9 cu, so the price is 69 cu.

## Must-know distinctions

* Progressive or regressive is about percentages of income, not amounts of money.
* Marginal rate (on the next slice of income) is not the average rate (tax ÷ total income).
* Direct or indirect is about who is taxed and on what, not who is richer.
* Revenue is not borrowing. A deficit is spending minus revenue.

## Evaluation prompts

* Interest rate effects depend on the size of the change, fixed or variable loans, confidence, inflation, and how much debt households and firms hold.
* Savers, such as retirees living on interest, gain from higher rates while borrowers lose.
* Investment also depends on expected sales and confidence.
* Progressive taxes reduce inequality but may weaken incentives and invite avoidance.
* Indirect taxes are cheap to collect and can discourage harmful goods, but weigh more on low incomes.

## Quick self-test

1. Define an interest rate.
2. Find the simple interest on 3,000 cu at 4% for one year.
3. A borrower owes 6,000 cu at 7%. Find the interest for one year.
4. A bank pays savers 2% and charges borrowers 6%. On 5,000 cu, find the difference in interest per year.
5. Name two sharia-compliant forms of finance.
6. Country E charges 0% on the first 5,000 cu and 25% above. Find the tax and the average rate on 15,000 cu.
7. A 15% sales tax is added to a 60 cu item. State the price paid.
8. A tax takes 6% of an income of 10,000 cu and 3% of an income of 80,000 cu. Is it progressive or regressive?
9. A project costs 30,000 cu, adds 2,100 cu to annual profit and is funded by a 5% loan. Calculate the net gain.
10. A household borrows 60,000 cu. The rate rises from 4% to 5.5%. Find the extra interest per year and per month.
11. Revenue is 40 and spending is 46. State the deficit.
12. Give one reason why a rise in interest rates may not reduce spending much.

### Answers

1. What it costs to borrow and what a saver earns, stated as an annual percentage of the sum.
2. 3,000 × 0.04 = 120 cu.
3. 6,000 × 0.07 = 420 cu.
4. Saver earns 5,000 × 0.02 = 100 cu; borrower pays 5,000 × 0.06 = 300 cu; difference = 200 cu.
5. Any two of mudarabah, musharakah, murabaha, ijara, sukuk.
6. Tax = 10,000 × 0.25 = 2,500 cu. Average rate = 2,500 ÷ 15,000 = 16.7%.
7. 60 × 0.15 = 9, so 69 cu.
8. Regressive: the percentage falls as income rises.
9. Interest = 30,000 × 0.05 = 1,500 cu. Net gain = 2,100 − 1,500 = 600 cu.
10. Interest at 4% = 2,400 cu and at 5.5% = 3,300 cu. Extra = 900 cu a year, or 75 cu a month.
11. 46 − 40 = 6.
12. Any one: the rise is small; most loans are fixed-rate; households are confident; savers gain; inflation keeps the real rate low.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Writing that "the rich pay more" to justify progressive, instead of comparing percentages.
* Taxing the whole income at the top band rate.
* Giving a one-step answer ("people borrow less") with no link to spending or output.
* Treating a rise in rates as having one effect on everyone, with no mention of savers.
* Leaving out units or the word "per year" in interest calculations.
* Describing sharia-compliant finance as having no return for the lender.
* Listing taxes with no distinction between direct and indirect.
* Calling borrowing a source of revenue.
* Finishing an evaluation question with a summary and no judgement.

## Frequently asked questions

**How does a rise in interest rates affect consumers?**
Saving becomes more rewarding and borrowing becomes more expensive, so households save more, borrow less and spend less. Borrowers lose and savers, such as retired people living on interest, gain.

**How does a firm decide whether to invest when interest rates change?**
Compare the expected annual return on the project with the interest cost of the loan (loan multiplied by the rate). Invest only if the return is higher than the interest, so a rise in rates makes fewer projects worthwhile.

**What is riba in Islamic finance?**
Riba is a fixed charge for lending money, which Islamic finance does not allow. Sharia-compliant finance instead links the provider's return to trade, assets or profit, for example through a mark-up, rent or a profit share.

**What is the difference between the marginal and average rate of tax?**
The marginal rate is the rate paid on the next slice of income, while the average rate is total tax divided by total income. Whether a tax is progressive is judged from how the average rate changes as income rises.

**Which sections of OxfordAQA 9214 cover interest rates and taxation?**
Sections 3.2.1.1 (interest rates) and 3.2.1.2 (government income and expenditure) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics (9214) specification.
