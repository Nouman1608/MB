Condensed recall for **1.2 The accounting equation**, Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707, 2027-2029 syllabus: "assets, liabilities and owner's equity"; "the accounting equation"; "how to apply the accounting equation". Figures below are invented; amounts in dollars.

## The equation and its rearrangements

- **Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity**
- Owner's equity = Assets − Liabilities
- Liabilities = Assets − Owner's equity

It always balances: every asset is financed either by outsiders (liabilities) or by the owner (equity).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | A resource owned by the business that will bring it benefit | Premises, vehicles, inventory, trade receivables, bank, cash |
| Non-current asset | Kept for use for more than a year | Machinery, fixtures and fittings |
| Current asset | Used or turned into cash within a year | Inventory, trade receivables, cash |
| Liability | An amount owed to someone other than the owner | Trade payables, loans, overdraft |
| Non-current liability | Due after more than a year | Five-year bank loan |
| Current liability | Due within a year | Trade payables, bank overdraft |
| Owner's equity (capital) | The owner's claim: assets minus liabilities | Capital introduced plus profits less drawings |
| Drawings | Resources taken out by the owner for private use | Cash, goods |

## Classification traps

- **Bank overdraft** = current liability (the business owes the bank).
- **Trade receivables** = asset; **trade payables** = liability.
- **Drawings** reduce equity; they are not an expense.
- The owner's **private** possessions are not business assets until brought into the business (business entity).

## Effects of transactions

| Transaction | Assets | Liabilities | Equity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner pays money into the business bank | + | | + |
| Buy equipment on credit | + | + | |
| Buy inventory by bank transfer | + and − (no change in total) | | |
| Receive a bank loan | + | + | |
| Pay a supplier | − | − | |
| Sell goods at a profit for cash | + (net) | | + (profit) |
| Pay an expense | − | | − |
| Owner takes cash for private use | − | | − |
| Supplier's debt turned into a loan | | + and − (no change in total) | |

**Quick figures.** Assets 30,000 = liabilities 8,000 + equity 22,000. Goods costing 900 are sold on credit for 1,400: inventory −900, trade receivables +1,400, so assets become **30,500**; profit 500 makes equity **22,500**; 30,500 = 8,000 + 22,500.

## Equity over a period

**Closing equity = Opening equity + Capital introduced + Profit − Drawings**

So **Profit = Closing equity − Opening equity + Drawings − Capital introduced**.

**Quick figures.** Opening equity 18,000; closing equity 21,500; drawings 7,000; capital introduced 1,000. Profit = 21,500 − 18,000 + 7,000 − 1,000 = **9,500**.

## Finding owner's equity from a list of balances

1. List and total the **assets**: non-current first, then current.
2. List and total the **liabilities**: non-current, then current (include any overdraft here).
3. Owner's equity = total assets − total liabilities.

**Quick figures.** Machinery 18,000; inventory 4,600; trade receivables 3,100; cash 300; trade payables 2,900; overdraft 1,200; loan (four years) 7,000. Assets 26,000; liabilities 11,100; owner's equity **14,900**.

## Link to the statement of financial position

The statement of financial position is the accounting equation set out in full: assets in one section, then liabilities, then owner's equity (opening capital plus profit less drawings), with the two totals equal.

## Exam traps

- **Wrong order.** It is assets = liabilities + equity, never assets = liabilities − equity.
- **Overdraft as an asset.** It is a liability.
- **Drawings as an expense.** They reduce equity directly; profit is unaffected.
- **Missing the profit.** A sale at a profit raises assets *and* equity.
- **Asset swaps.** Buying inventory for cash changes no totals.
- **Capital introduced is not profit.** Remove it when finding profit from equity.
- **Show workings.** Total the assets and liabilities before subtracting.
- **Goods taken by the owner.** They reduce inventory (assets) and owner's equity through drawings; profit is not affected.
- **Repaying a loan is not an expense.** Assets and liabilities fall by the same amount; equity is unchanged.

## Self-test

1. State the accounting equation.
2. Classify: bank overdraft, inventory, trade payables, delivery van, loan due in six years.
3. Assets are $64,000 and owner's equity is $47,500. Calculate liabilities.
4. Liabilities are $12,800 and owner's equity is $35,200. Calculate assets.
5. What is the effect on the equation of buying a computer for $1,100 on credit?
6. What is the effect of paying a supplier $600 by bank transfer?
7. Why does buying inventory with cash not change total assets?
8. Goods costing $500 are sold for $750 cash. Give the effect on assets and owner's equity.
9. The owner takes goods costing $200 for her own use. What is the effect?
10. Opening equity is $25,000, profit $11,000, drawings $8,500 and no new capital. Calculate closing equity.
11. Opening equity $30,000; closing equity $33,400; drawings $6,000; capital introduced $2,500. Calculate profit.
12. Why must the two sides of the equation always be equal?

**Answers:** 1. Assets = Liabilities + Owner's equity. 2. Current liability; current asset; current liability; non-current asset; non-current liability. 3. 64,000 − 47,500 = **$16,500**. 4. 12,800 + 35,200 = **$48,000**. 5. Assets + $1,100 (equipment); liabilities + $1,100 (trade payables). 6. Assets − $600 (bank); liabilities − $600 (trade payables). 7. One asset (inventory) increases and another (cash) decreases by the same amount. 8. Assets + $250 (cash +750, inventory −500); equity + $250 (profit). 9. Assets − $200 (inventory); equity − $200 (drawings). 10. 25,000 + 11,000 − 8,500 = **$27,500**. 11. 33,400 − 30,000 + 6,000 − 2,500 = **$6,900**. 12. Every asset is provided either by the owner or by outsiders, and every transaction has two equal effects (duality).
