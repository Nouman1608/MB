## Questions

**1.** State the law of demand, and name two non-price determinants of demand listed in the guide. **[2]**

**2.** (HL only) In the invented town of Quillon, a household has a weekly pasta budget of 40 om (ostmarks). The price of a pack falls from 5 om to 4 om. Explain the substitution effect and the income effect of the fall in price. Use the numbers. **[4]**

**3.** In the invented town of Marrow, monthly gym pass demand comes from two groups. Their quantities demanded at each price are below.

| Price (om) | Students | Workers |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 40 | 20 |
| 20 | 30 | 15 |
| 30 | 20 | 10 |
| 40 | 10 | 5 |

**(a)** Work out market demand at each price. **[2]**
**(b)** A third group, retirees, demands 10, 6, 2 and 0 passes at 10, 20, 30 and 40 om. State the new market demand at each price. **[2]**

**4.** Printers and ink cartridges are complements. Draw a demand diagram for printers in a made-up market and show the effect of a rise in the price of ink cartridges. Explain your diagram. **[4]**

**5.** In the invented market for lantern kits in Tessaly, demand in thousands per week is Qd = 90 − 6P, where P is in om.
**(a)** Calculate Qd when P = 8. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the price at which Qd is zero. **[1]**
**(c)** Higher incomes raise demand to Qd = 114 − 6P. Calculate the new quantity demanded at P = 8, and the percentage change. **[2]**
**(d)** State whether (c) is a movement along the curve or a shift, and why. **[1]**
**(e)** Using the original curve, state the change in quantity demanded if the price falls from 8 to 6. **[1]**

**6.** (HL only) Dara values successive slices of pizza at lunch at 14, 11, 8, 5 and 2 om.
**(a)** Explain what this shows about marginal utility. **[2]**
**(b)** State how many slices she buys at prices of 12, 9, 6 and 3 om. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain how your answer to (b) supports the law of demand. **[1]**

**7.** In the invented market for tea in Kelmoor, say for each event whether the demand curve is moved along or shifted, and give the direction. Assume tea is a normal good.
**(i)** The price of tea falls.
**(ii)** The price of coffee, a substitute, falls.
**(iii)** Household incomes rise.
**(iv)** The price of biscuits, a complement, rises.
**(v)** Shoppers expect tea prices to rise next month.
Give 1 mark for each correct part. **[5]**

**8.** Instant noodle packs in a made-up market are an inferior good. At a price of 3 om, buyers demand 100 packs a week. Incomes rise by 20% and the quantity demanded at 3 om falls to 85. Define an inferior good, explain the fall in demand and state the percentage change in quantity demanded at 3 om. **[4]**

**9.** In the invented city of Valdora, demand for e-scooter rides is Qd = 800 − 40P (rides per day, P in om).
**(a)** Calculate the quantity demanded at P = 6. **[1]**
**(b)** The bus fare, a substitute, rises. At every price, 120 more rides are demanded. Calculate the new quantity at P = 6 and the percentage increase. **[3]**
**(c)** On the new demand curve, the operator cuts the price from 6 to 5. Calculate the new quantity demanded. **[1]**
**(d)** Describe a diagram showing both effects. **[3]**

**10.** In the invented town of Pellmoor, 50 adults and 30 children buy museum tickets. Each adult demands 8 − P tickets a month (for P up to 8). Each child demands 5 − 0.5P (for P up to 10).
**(a)** Work out market demand at P = 4 and at P = 9. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the market demand curve changes slope at P = 8. **[2]**
**(c)** 20 more adults move to Pellmoor. Calculate the new quantity at P = 4. **[2]**
**(d)** State whether (c) is a movement or a shift, and name the determinant. **[1]**
**(e)** (HL only) Explain the substitution effect and the income effect for an adult when the ticket price falls. **[2]**

**11.** A supermarket in a made-up country announces that the price of rice will rise by 10% next month. (a) Explain the effect on current demand. **[3]** (b) A new university campus opens nearby. Explain the effect on the market demand for rice. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Law: when other things are unchanged, a lower price raises the quantity demanded (price and quantity demanded move in opposite directions). [1] Two determinants, for example income and tastes and preferences (also: future price expectations, prices of related goods, number of consumers). [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A "state" question asks for a brief answer, so one clear sentence is usually enough and extra explanation adds little.

**2.** Substitution effect: at 4 om, pasta is cheaper relative to other foods, so the household switches towards pasta. [1] The switch is a response to relative price, not to extra money. [1] Income effect: 40 ÷ 5 = 8 packs possible before, 40 ÷ 4 = 10 after. [1] Purchasing power has risen by (10 − 8) ÷ 8 = 25%, so a normal good is bought in larger quantity. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the two effects in separate sentences, because a single blended sentence makes it hard to see that you can tell them apart.

**3. (a)** 40 + 20 = 60 [1], 30 + 15 = 45, 20 + 10 = 30, 10 + 5 = 15 (all four correct). [1]
**(b)** At 10 om: 60 + 10 = 70; at 20 om: 45 + 6 = 51; at 30 om: 30 + 2 = 32; at 40 om: 15 + 0 = 15. [1] for three or more correct, [1] for all four. Total **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A "calculate" answer needs the working shown, so write the sums rather than only the totals.

**4.** Axes: price of printers (vertical), quantity of printers per period (horizontal), curve D1 sloping down. [1] A rise in the price of ink cartridges makes printers less attractive. [1] D2 is drawn to the left of D1 and labelled. [1] At the original price, the quantity demanded falls, which is a decrease in demand and not a contraction. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A diagram without labelled axes or a labelled second curve is usually incomplete, so label D1, D2 and both quantities.

**5. (a)** 90 − 6 × 8 = 42. [1]
**(b)** 90 − 6P = 0, so P = 15. [1]
**(c)** 114 − 6 × 8 = 66. [1] Change: (66 − 42) ÷ 42 = 57.1%. [1]
**(d)** A shift, because the cause is income, a non-price determinant, and the quantity at the same price changed. [1]
**(e)** 90 − 36 = 54, so quantity rises by 12 (an extension of demand). [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* The percentage change is measured against the original value (42), so dividing by 66 is a common slip.

**6. (a)** Each extra slice is valued less than the one before (14, 11, 8, 5, 2). [1] This is diminishing marginal utility, so the satisfaction from each further slice falls. [1]
**(b)** Slices valued at least the price: 12 om gives 1; 9 om gives 2; 6 om gives 3; 3 om gives 4. [1] for two or three correct, [1] for all four.
**(c)** At lower prices more slices are worth buying, so the quantity she demands grows as the price drops. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Link each number back to the idea of utility, because quantities alone do not show you understand why the curve slopes down.

**7.** (i) Movement along the demand curve (extension). [1] (ii) Leftward shift. [1] (iii) Rightward shift. [1] (iv) Leftward shift. [1] (v) Shift right (current demand increases). [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming a shift without giving its direction usually earns no credit, so always say left or right.

**8.** An inferior good is one whose demand falls as income rises. [1] Higher income lets buyers switch to better alternatives, so they buy fewer noodle packs at each price. [1] The quantity demanded at 3 om falls from 100 to 85, a change of (85 − 100) ÷ 100 = −15%. [1] The curve shifts left. [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For an inferior good the sign is the opposite of a normal good, so state the direction and the reason together.

**9. (a)** 800 − 240 = 560. [1]
**(b)** New demand: Qd = 920 − 40P, so at P = 6 it is 680. [1] Increase = 120. [1] Percentage = 120 ÷ 560 = 21.4%. [1]
**(c)** 920 − 200 = 720. [1]
**(d)** Axes: price vertical, rides per day horizontal. [1] D1 passes through 560 at P = 6 and D2 lies to the right through 680 at P = 6. [1] The move from 6 to 5 on D2, from 680 to 720, is a slide along the curve. [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer says which part of the story is a shift and which is a movement, because that is the idea the diagram is meant to show.

**10. (a)** At P = 4: adults 50 × 4 = 200, children 30 × 3 = 90, total 290. [1] At P = 9: adults demand none (above 8), children 30 × 0.5 = 15 (5 − 4.5 = 0.5 each). [1] Total 15. [1]
**(b)** Above P = 8 adults demand none. [1] Only children remain, so the curve is steeper above that price and is formed of one group. [1]
**(c)** 70 adults × 4 = 280, plus children 90 = 370. [1] Increase of 80. [1]
**(d)** A shift, because the number of consumers has changed. [1]
**(e)** Substitution effect: tickets are cheaper compared with other leisure. [1] Income effect: the lower price raises purchasing power. [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), give the reason, saying that adults leave the market at 8, and do not just point to the kink.

**11. (a)** Shoppers expect the price to be higher next month. [1] They buy more now to avoid the higher price. [1] Current demand shifts right. [1]
**(b)** The number of consumers increases. [1] Market demand shifts right at every price. [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the determinant each time, because naming it shows that your reasoning rests on the guide's list.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "demand falls" when the price rose, instead of a contraction.
- Leaving out axis labels, curve labels or the second curve on a shift diagram.
- Giving a direction of shift with no cause.
- Calculating a percentage change against the new value.
- Showing totals without the working in a "calculate" question.
- Blending the income effect and the substitution effect in HL answers.
- Treating a complement like a substitute.
- Forgetting that a consumer who is priced out contributes zero to market demand.
