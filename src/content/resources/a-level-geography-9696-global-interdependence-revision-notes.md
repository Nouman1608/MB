## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visible trade | Trade in physical goods (minerals, food, manufactured goods) |
| Invisible trade | Trade in services (tourism, banking, insurance, shipping, software) |
| Terms of trade | The price of a country's exports relative to the price of its imports |
| Resource endowment | The natural resources a country has, such as minerals, soils or climate |
| Free trade | Trade without tariffs, quotas or subsidies |
| Trade agreement / bloc | A deal between countries to cut barriers among members (EU, USMCA, AfCFTA) |
| WTO | World Trade Organization, set up in 1995, which sets trade rules and settles disputes |
| Fairtrade | A certification scheme giving producers a minimum price and a community premium |
| Debt service | Repayments of interest and capital on a debt |
| Debt relief | Cancelling or rescheduling debt, as under HIPC (1996) and MDRI (2005) |
| Structural adjustment | IMF/World Bank loan conditions such as austerity and privatisation |
| Relief aid | Short-term emergency help after a disaster |
| Development aid | Long-term help to raise living standards |
| Tied aid | Aid conditional on buying goods or services from the donor |
| Bilateral aid | Aid from one government to another |
| Multilateral aid | Aid pooled from many donors through an international body |
| Carrying capacity | The maximum use a place can sustain without unacceptable damage |
| Tourism multiplier | Extra income created as tourist spending is re-spent locally |
| Leakage | Tourist spending that leaves the local or national economy |
| Ecotourism | Responsible travel to natural areas that conserves the environment, supports local people and educates visitors |

## Facts and models at a glance

| Item | What | Key point | Evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| WTO (1995) | Replaced GATT; 166 members; based in Geneva | Most-favoured nation and national treatment rules | Doha Round (2001) stalled; Appellate Body not working since 2019 |
| Lomé Convention (1975) | EU preferences for ACP countries | Example of colonial ties shaping trade | Preferences can lock countries into raw-material exports |
| Fairtrade (Max Havelaar, Netherlands, 1988) | Minimum price plus premium | Shields producers from price falls | Often only part of output sold on Fairtrade terms; a 2014 Ethiopia and Uganda study found no gain for hired workers |
| Debt crisis (1982) | Mexico could not service its debt in August 1982 | 1970s petrodollar lending plus rising interest rates from 1979 | Structural adjustment led to the "lost decade" in Latin America |
| HIPC (1996, enhanced 1999) | IMF/World Bank debt relief | 37 countries completed by 2020 | Conditions such as utility privatisation could hurt poor households |
| Zambia | Copper-dependent exporter | HIPC relief, then a 2020 Eurobond default | Restructured under the G20 Common Framework (2023-2024) |
| 0.7% target (UN, 1970) | Aid target as share of GNI | Few donors meet it | UK cut from 0.7% to 0.5% in 2021 |
| Pergau Dam (Malaysia) | UK aid linked to an arms deal | Ruled unlawful by the High Court in 1994 | Shows aid shaped by donor interests |
| Butler (1980) | Tourism area life cycle | Exploration, involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation, then decline or rejuvenation | Stages hard to spot at the time; descriptive, not predictive |
| Venice (13.4 case study) | Historic city and lagoon, UNESCO site since 1987 | 22-30 million visitors a year; historic-centre population about 50,000 in 2021 | Cruise ban (2021), access fee (2024), 25-person group limit (2024), MOSE barrier (first raised 2020) |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Visible vs invisible:** goods vs services. A tourist's spending is an invisible export for the host country.
- **Relief vs development aid:** time scale and purpose -- saving lives now vs building capacity over years.
- **Bilateral vs multilateral:** the channel -- one donor government vs pooled through a body such as the World Bank.
- **Tied vs untied:** the condition -- must the receiver buy from the donor?
- **Fairtrade vs free trade:** Fairtrade is a certification scheme for producers; free trade is about removing barriers between countries.
- **Debt crisis vs debt problem:** the 1980s crisis was a system-wide failure; a debt problem can affect one country at any time.
- **Physical, ecological and perceptual carrying capacity:** space, ecosystems and people's tolerance.
- **Multiplier vs leakage:** money re-spent locally vs money that leaves.

## How the four sections link

Paper 4 topics are synoptic, so the best answers connect sections rather than treating them separately.

- **Trade to debt.** A country that relies on one primary export earns less when its price falls, but its debt repayments do not fall. Zambia's copper dependence and its 2020 default show this chain.
- **Debt to aid.** Debt relief is itself a form of aid. Conditions attached to relief, such as structural adjustment, change how a country trades.
- **Trade to tourism.** Tourism is an invisible export. A country trying to reduce dependence on one commodity may grow tourism to diversify its export earnings.
- **Tourism to debt.** Building airports, roads and resorts is often paid for with loans, so a fall in arrivals (as in 2020) can turn tourism investment into a debt problem.
- **Aid to tourism.** Development aid can fund infrastructure that later supports tourism, while relief aid after a disaster may protect a destination's economy.
- **Fairtrade and ecotourism.** Both are attempts to make global links fairer and more sustainable, and both face the same criticism: they reach only part of the market.

Use one of these links in each essay to show you understand interdependence.

## Mini essay plan

**Question type:** "Evaluate the view that international tourism brings more benefits than costs to destinations." (20 marks on Paper 4.)

1. **Introduction.** Define international tourism and the three impact types. Say the answer depends on scale (local vs national), type of tourism and stage of development.
2. **Economic benefits.** Jobs, foreign exchange, multiplier. Limit: leakage and seasonal, low-paid work.
3. **Social impacts.** Funding for heritage vs rising rents and depopulation. Venice: historic-centre population fell from about 120,000 (1980) to about 50,000 (2021).
4. **Environmental impacts.** Conservation funding from entry fees vs erosion, waste and emissions. Link to carrying capacity.
5. **Management changes the balance.** Venice's cruise ban, access fee and group limits. Note that the fee raised money but phone data suggested numbers rose on fee days in 2024.
6. **Theory.** Butler's model: benefits dominate early, costs grow after consolidation. Evaluate the model.
7. **Conclusion.** A clear judgement: benefits outweigh costs nationally, but local costs often exceed local benefits once carrying capacity is passed, unless management limits growth.

## Quick self-test

1. Is a Kenyan safari bought by a German tourist a visible or invisible export for Kenya?
2. Name four factors listed in the syllabus that affect global trade.
3. In what year was the WTO set up, and what did it replace?
4. What are the two payments Fairtrade producers receive?
5. Give two causes of the 1980s international debt crisis.
6. What does HIPC stand for and when did it begin?
7. Define tied aid.
8. Is World Bank lending bilateral or multilateral?
9. Name the three types of carrying capacity.
10. What reduces the size of the tourism multiplier?
11. List the stages of Butler's model in order.
12. Give two management measures used in Venice since 2021.

### Answers

1. Invisible -- it is a service.
2. Any four: resource endowment, locational advantage, colonial ties, trade agreements, changes in the global market.
3. 1995; it replaced GATT.
4. The Fairtrade Minimum Price and the Fairtrade Premium.
5. Petrodollar lending after the 1973 oil price rise; sharp rises in interest rates from 1979.
6. Heavily Indebted Poor Countries; 1996.
7. Aid given on condition the receiver buys goods or services from the donor.
8. Multilateral.
9. Physical, ecological, perceptual (social).
10. Leakage -- spending on imports and profits sent abroad.
11. Exploration, involvement, development, consolidation, stagnation, then decline or rejuvenation.
12. Any two: cruise ship ban in the Giudecca Canal (2021); day-tripper access fee (2024); 25-person limit on tour groups (2024).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling tourism, banking or shipping visible trade.
- Listing factors affecting trade without a real example for each.
- Describing the WTO's role but never judging whether free trade helps poorer exporters.
- Treating Fairtrade as a government policy or a trade bloc.
- Explaining the debt crisis without dates or without linking interest rates to debt service.
- Muddling tied, bilateral and multilateral aid, which are three different ways of classifying aid.
- Describing tourism impacts without separating local and national scale, as the syllabus asks.
- Drawing Butler's curve without evaluating it; the syllabus says "critically evaluate".
- Using a whole country instead of a tourist area or resort for 13.4, or giving no dates or figures.
- Ending an essay without a clear judgement, when evaluation carries 40% of Paper 4.
