## Section A

**1.** Define the term *catalyst*. **[2]**

**2.** State how a catalyst increases the rate of a reaction. **[2]**

**3.** State the effect of a catalyst on the enthalpy change of a reaction. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why only a small amount of catalyst is needed to convert a large amount of reactant. **[1]**

**5.** A student writes: "A catalyst is not involved in the reaction, and it works by giving the molecules more energy." Identify the **two** errors and correct each. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** This question is about the effect of a catalyst on a gas-phase reaction at constant temperature.

**(a)** Describe how a Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution would be drawn and labelled to show the effect of the catalyst. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your answer to (a) to explain why the catalyst increases the rate. **[2]**

**7.** Hydrogen peroxide decomposes slowly. Iodide ions speed this up by the following two steps:

Step 1: H₂O₂ + I⁻ → H₂O + IO⁻

Step 2: H₂O₂ + IO⁻ → H₂O + O₂ + I⁻

**(a)** Write the overall equation. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the catalyst and the intermediate, giving a reason for each. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how this scheme fits the definition of a catalyst. **[1]**

**8.** For the reaction X ⇌ Y, ΔH = +35 kJ mol⁻¹. The uncatalysed route has an activation energy of +150 kJ mol⁻¹. The catalysed route passes through an intermediate. Measured from the reactant level, its first hump is at +90 kJ mol⁻¹, the intermediate is at +50 kJ mol⁻¹, and its second hump is at +110 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** State the activation energy of the catalysed route. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the activation energy of the reverse reaction for the uncatalysed route and for the catalysed route. **[2]**

**(c)** Use your answers to explain why the catalyst does not change the position of the equilibrium. **[2]**

**9.** Describe the enthalpy profile you would draw for an exothermic reaction with and without a catalyst whose route passes through one intermediate. State **three** features the two curves must show. **[3]**

**10.** For a gas reaction at 400 K, 0.8% of molecules have energy at least the uncatalysed activation energy, and 9.6% have energy at least the activation energy of a catalysed route. Without the catalyst, raising the temperature to 430 K increases the uncatalysed figure to 2.4%.

**(a)** Calculate the factor by which the proportion of molecules able to react increases (i) with the catalyst at 400 K and (ii) with the temperature rise and no catalyst. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the Maxwell–Boltzmann distribution differs between these two changes. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **one** economic reason for using the catalyst rather than the higher temperature. **[1]**

**11.** A student says: "Adding a catalyst to a reversible reaction at equilibrium will increase the amount of product." Evaluate this statement. **[3]**

**12.** Methanol is made industrially by a reversible, exothermic gas reaction using a catalyst. Explain how the catalyst allows a lower operating temperature, and why a lower temperature is desirable for both the yield and the cost. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A substance that increases the rate of a chemical reaction [1]; without being changed in chemical composition or amount [1]

**2.** It provides an alternative reaction route [1]; with a lower activation energy [1]

**3.** No effect: ΔH is unchanged [1]

**4.** The catalyst is not used up (it is re-formed), so the same catalyst particles can be used again and again [1]

**5.** A catalyst usually **is** involved: it takes part in a step and is re-formed later [1]; it does **not** give molecules more energy: it provides an alternative route of lower activation energy, so more molecules already have enough energy [1]

**6. (a)** Axes labelled energy (x) and number of molecules (y), with one curve starting at the origin [1]; Ea for the uncatalysed reaction marked in the tail [1]; Ea(cat) marked to the left of Ea, with the area between them identified [1]

**(b)** A greater proportion (area) of molecules have energy at least the activation energy of the catalysed route [1]; so more collisions are successful per unit time, increasing the rate [1]
*Examiner insight:* One curve only. A second, shifted curve tells the examiner you are thinking of a temperature change.

**7. (a)** 2H₂O₂ → 2H₂O + O₂ [1]

**(b)** Catalyst: I⁻, because it is used in step 1 and re-formed in step 2 [1]; intermediate: IO⁻, because it is formed in step 1 and used in step 2 [1]

**(c)** I⁻ takes part but is re-formed, so at the end its composition and amount are unchanged while the rate has increased [1]

**8. (a)** **+110 kJ mol⁻¹** (the higher of the two humps) [1]

**(b)** Uncatalysed: 150 − 35 = **+115 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; catalysed: 110 − 35 = **+75 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]

**(c)** Both the forward and reverse activation energies fall by the same amount, 40 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]; so the forward and reverse rates increase by the same factor and equilibrium is reached sooner but with the same composition [1]
*Examiner insight:* +90 kJ mol⁻¹ is the first hump, not the highest point of the route. Always use the highest point.

**9.** Reactant and product levels are the same for both, with products below reactants, so ΔH is unchanged and negative [1]; the catalysed route has two humps with a dip (the intermediate) between them [1]; both catalysed humps are lower than the single uncatalysed hump [1]

**10. (a)** (i) 9.6 ÷ 0.8 = **12** [1]; (ii) 2.4 ÷ 0.8 = **3** [1]

**(b)** With the catalyst the curve is unchanged and the activation energy line moves to lower energy [1]; with the temperature rise the curve's peak moves to higher energy and becomes lower (same area) while the activation energy line stays put [1]

**(c)** Less energy (fuel) is needed for heating, so running costs are lower [1]

**11.** The statement is incorrect [1]; a catalyst lowers the activation energy of the forward and reverse reactions equally, increasing both rates by the same factor [1]; so the position of equilibrium, and the amount of product, are unchanged; equilibrium is only reached sooner [1]

**12.** Without a catalyst, the reaction would be too slow at a low temperature [1]; the catalyst provides a route of lower activation energy, so an acceptable rate is reached at a lower temperature [1]; the forward reaction is exothermic, so a lower temperature gives a higher equilibrium yield [1]; and less energy is needed for heating, lowering fuel costs [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep "rate" and "yield" separate. The catalyst helps rate; the lower temperature it allows helps yield.

## Where marks are usually lost

- A definition missing "chemical composition or amount".
- "Lowers the activation energy" without the alternative route.
- Choosing the first hump of a catalysed profile instead of the highest.
- Claiming a catalyst increases equilibrium yield.
- Drawing or describing a shifted Maxwell–Boltzmann curve for a catalyst.
