## Sole trader at a glance

| Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Quick decisions, full control | Unlimited liability |
| Keeps all the profit | Limited sources of capital |
| Easy and cheap to set up | Depends on one person |
| Privacy of accounts | Heavy workload, no shared responsibility |

A sole trader can be a **trading** business (buys and sells goods), a
**service** business (sells skills or time), a **manufacturing** business
(makes goods), or a **combination**, such as a shop that also repairs goods.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Statement of profit or loss | Shows revenue, expenses and profit for a period |
| Statement of financial position | Shows assets, liabilities and capital on a date |
| Non-current assets | Tangible assets kept for more than a year to run the business |
| Intangible assets | Non-current assets without physical form, such as goodwill or a trademark |
| Current assets | Inventory, receivables, prepayments, bank and cash, expected to change within a year |
| Current liabilities | Amounts due within twelve months: trade payables, other payables, overdraft |
| Non-current liabilities | Amounts due after twelve months, such as a long-term loan |
| Capital | The owner's investment: opening capital + capital introduced + profit − drawings |
| Drawings | Cash, goods or other assets taken by the owner for private use, including private expenses paid by the business |

## Layout reminders

**Statement of profit or loss:** revenue − cost of sales = gross profit;
\+ other income (service fees, rent received, commission, decrease in allowance,
discount received); − expenses = profit for the year. A service business has no
cost of sales section.

**Statement of financial position:** intangible assets; non-current assets at
cost less accumulated depreciation; current assets less current liabilities =
net current assets; less non-current liabilities; equals capital section.

## Where each adjustment goes

| Adjustment | Statement of profit or loss | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Straight-line depreciation | Expense: cost × % | Accumulated depreciation increases |
| Reducing balance depreciation | Expense: (cost − accumulated depreciation) × % | Accumulated depreciation increases |
| Revaluation method | Expense: opening value + additions − closing value | Closing valuation shown |
| Accrued expense | Add to expense | Other payables |
| Prepaid expense | Deduct from expense | Other receivables |
| Accrued income | Add to income | Other receivables |
| Income in advance | Deduct from income | Other payables |
| Irrecoverable debt | Expense | Deduct from trade receivables |
| Allowance change | Increase: expense; decrease: income | Deduct new allowance from receivables |
| Goods taken by owner | Deduct from purchases (at cost) | Add to drawings |
| Private expenses paid | Deduct from that expense | Add to drawings |

## Effect of a change in one balance

Follow each change to **every** figure it touches.

| Change | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciation omitted | Overstated | Non-current assets and capital overstated |
| Closing inventory too low | Understated | Current assets and capital understated |
| Private expense in expenses | Understated | Drawings understated; closing capital correct |
| Cash paid to a supplier | No effect | Bank and trade payables both lower |
| Loan received | No effect | Bank and non-current liabilities both higher |

## Exam traps

- **Goods for own use** come off purchases at **cost**, never off revenue.
- **Reducing balance** uses net book value at the start of the year.
- **Revaluation:** remember additions bought during the year.
- **Service income** goes below gross profit in a combination business.
- **Loan interest** for the full year is an expense even if part is unpaid;
  the unpaid part is an accrual.
- **Loan classification:** repayable within twelve months → current liability;
  otherwise non-current.
- **Capital introduced** is added in the capital section, not treated as income.
- **Headings and dates:** "for the year ended" for the statement of profit or
  loss; "at" for the statement of financial position.

## Self-test

1. State one advantage and one disadvantage of being a sole trader.
2. Give an example of a combination business.
3. A van cost 25,000; accumulated depreciation at the start is 9,000; reducing
   balance rate 20%. Calculate this year's depreciation.
4. Tools valued at 900 at the start, bought 300, valued at 850 at the end.
   Calculate depreciation.
5. Owner takes goods costing 450. Give the double entry.
6. Where is a trademark shown?
7. Rent 6,000 paid, 500 prepaid at the end. What is the rent expense?
8. Is a bank loan repayable in eight months a current or non-current liability?
9. Opening capital 30,000; profit 12,000; drawings 9,500; capital introduced 4,000.
   Calculate closing capital.
10. The owner's private electricity of 200 is included in electricity. State the
    effect on profit if this is not corrected.
11. Why does the statement of financial position matter to a bank?
12. Where does a decrease in the allowance for irrecoverable debts go?

### Answers

1. For example: keeps all the profit; unlimited liability.
2. For example, a bicycle shop that also charges for repairs.
3. (25,000 − 9,000) × 20% = **3,200**.
4. 900 + 300 − 850 = **350**.
5. Debit Drawings 450; credit Purchases 450.
6. Under intangible assets, separately from tangible non-current assets.
7. 6,000 − 500 = **5,500**.
8. Current: it is due within twelve months.
9. 30,000 + 4,000 + 12,000 − 9,500 = **36,500**.
10. Profit is understated by 200.
11. It shows the assets available as security and the liabilities already owed,
    so the bank can judge whether a loan can be repaid.
12. It is added as other income in the statement of profit or loss.
