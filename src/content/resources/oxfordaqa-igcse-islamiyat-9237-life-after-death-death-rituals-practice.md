## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In
the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for explaining it, up to two marks
for each of the two. The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2
marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use
applied to the question. The 12-mark questions are marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Kafan [1].

**2.** Barzakh [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: all people are raised from the dead; each person receives their book of deeds; deeds are weighed on the scales; the body's limbs testify; people are sent to Jannah or Jahannam; the Prophet (pbuh) intercedes by Allah's permission.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: four takbirs in the Janazah (Sunni) and five (Shi'a); some communities hold gatherings on set days such as the third or fortieth day while others do not; martyrs buried without ghusl in Sunni practice; differing customs on visiting and marking graves; differing cultural customs on how grief is expressed.

**5.** For each of two beliefs: belief identified [1] and explained [1].

- **The dead are questioned** [1]: two angels ask about the person's Lord, religion and prophet, so faith in this life decides the answers [1].
- **The grave is a foretaste** [1]: for the believer it becomes peaceful and spacious, for the wrongdoer a place of punishment, before final judgement [1].
- **Barzakh is a barrier** [1]: no one can return to this life to make up for lost deeds (23:100) [1].

**6.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Following the Sunnah** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) taught that funerals should be hurried, so Muslims follow his example [1].
- **Respect for the body** [1]: prompt washing and burial protect the dignity of the dead person [1].
- **The soul has moved on** [1]: the person has entered Barzakh, so there is no reason to delay returning the body to the earth [1].
- **Easing the family's burden** [1]: a quick, simple burial avoids long, costly ceremonies [1].

**7.** For each of two beliefs: belief identified [1] and explained [1].

- **It is eternal** [1]: the reward does not end, which gives this short life its purpose [1].
- **It is a reward for faith and good deeds** [1], **entered through Allah's mercy** [1].
- **Nearness to Allah** [1]: the greatest joy of Jannah is being close to Allah, not only physical pleasure [1].
- **It has levels** [1]: people are rewarded according to their faith and deeds, which encourages effort [1].

**8.** For each of two ways: way identified [1] and explained [1].

- **Praying for them** [1]: a righteous child's du'a benefits a parent after death, according to the Hadith in Sahih Muslim [1].
- **Ongoing charity (sadaqah jariyah)** [1]: building a well or funding a school in their name brings continuing reward [1].
- **Paying their debts** [1]: debts are settled from the estate before inheritance is shared, which relieves the dead person of the burden [1].
- **Visiting the grave** [1]: the family prays for forgiveness for the dead and remembers their own death [1].

### Section B

**9.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two reasons, the reason identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Reasons (AO1):

- **It makes judgement possible** [1]: everyone must be raised before deeds can be weighed, so resurrection guarantees accountability [1].
- **It shows Allah's power** [1]: the One who created people the first time can raise them again, so belief in it strengthens Tawhid [1].
- **It gives hope** [1]: death is not the end, so believers face loss and suffering with patience [1].
- **It shapes behaviour** [1]: a Muslim who expects to stand before Allah tries to avoid wrongdoing [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "The Horn will be blown ... Then it will be blown again, and at once they will be standing, looking on" (Qur'an 39:68), showing that all people without exception will be raised [2].
- "Every soul will taste death, and you will only be given your [full] compensation on the Day of Resurrection" (Qur'an 3:185), so reward waits until people are raised [2].

**10.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 9. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 9.

Reasons (AO1):

- **It asks forgiveness for the dead** [1]: the congregation prays for Allah's mercy on the deceased, the last help they can give [1].
- **It is a communal duty** [1]: as fard kifayah, the community as a whole is responsible for making sure it happens [1].
- **It reminds the living of death** [1]: standing by the body makes worshippers think of their own judgement [1].
- **It unites the community** [1]: rich and poor stand together for every Muslim's funeral, whatever their status [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Indeed we belong to Allah, and indeed to Him we will return" (Qur'an 2:156): the funeral prayer puts this acceptance into practice [2].
- The Hadith that deeds end at death except for three, including a righteous child who prays for the deceased (Sahih Muslim), shows why prayer for the dead matters [2].

**11.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- **Individual accountability:** each person is judged on their own faith and deeds; no one can carry another's sins.
- **Family ties end in importance:** the passage shows even the closest relatives turning away, so Muslims cannot rely on a pious family.
- **This life is the time to prepare:** because no help is possible later, Muslims must act now through prayer, charity and repentance.
- **Against the statement:** Muslims believe in intercession (shafa'ah) by the Prophet (pbuh), by Allah's permission; and the prayers and ongoing charity of others can benefit a person after death.
- **Balanced view:** no one can help *by their own power*, but Allah may allow help; the final decision is Allah's alone.

*Use of the passage*

- "A man will flee from his own siblings. And his mother and his father" shows the fear and isolation of that Day.
- "For every man, that Day, will be a matter adequate for him" shows that each person is fully occupied with their own fate.
- The "bright" and the dust-covered faces show that outcomes depend on each person's own faith and deeds.
- "Those are the disbelievers, the wicked ones" links the outcome to belief and conduct, not family or status.

**12.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- Akhirah gives **purpose**: life is a test with a permanent result.
- It encourages **good conduct and sincerity**, since deeds are recorded by the Kiraman Katibeen and weighed.
- It brings **justice and hope**: wrongs will be put right.
- It shapes **attitudes to wealth and death**: the plain kafan and simple grave show that worldly status ends.
- **Against the statement:** love of Allah and gratitude, rather than fear of judgement, may be the deepest motive; Tawhid is the foundation of all beliefs; the example of the Prophet (pbuh) also guides daily life directly.

*Use of the passage*

- The rejoicing faces show the reward that motivates Muslims to do good.
- The faces covered in "dust" and "blackness" show the consequence of rejecting faith, a reason to avoid sin.
- Fleeing from family shows that only one's own deeds will matter, so a Muslim cannot leave religion to others.
- Linking the outcome to the "disbelievers" shows that belief, not only behaviour, is judged.

An answer that explains a range of points, reasons about their implications and uses the passage directly reaches Level 4.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up Barzakh with the Day of Judgement.
- Describing a ritual without saying why it matters.
- Giving two rituals from the same tradition when the question asks for differences.
- Naming the wrong angel for a role.
- In the 12-mark questions, ignoring counter-arguments such as intercession, or not quoting the passage.
