## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Stimulus | A change that is detected. |
| Receptor | Detects a stimulus and starts an impulse. |
| Sensory neurone | Carries impulses from receptor to CNS. |
| Relay neurone | Short neurone in the CNS linking sensory to motor. |
| Motor neurone | Carries impulses from CNS to effector. |
| CNS | Brain and spinal cord. |
| Effector | A muscle (contracts) or a gland (secretes). |
| Synapse | Junction between two neurones; a narrow gap crossed by a chemical. |
| Reflex | Fast, automatic response that does not need conscious thought. |
| Reaction time | Time from stimulus to response. |

## B3.1a: Structure

### Pathway to learn

stimulus -> receptor -> sensory neurone -> CNS -> motor neurone -> effector -> response

### Neurone structure and function

| Feature | Link to function |
|---|---|
| Long axon | Carries impulses over long distances. |
| Myelin sheath | Insulates and speeds up the impulse. |
| Cell body with nucleus | Controls the cell; in a motor neurone it is at the CNS end with many dendrites. |
| Sensory neurone: cell body off to one side | Impulse passes from receptor, past the cell body, on to the CNS. |
| Branched end of a motor neurone | Passes the signal on to the effector. |

### Synapse in four steps

1. Impulse reaches the end of neurone 1.
2. Transmitter chemical is released into the gap.
3. It diffuses across the gap.
4. It binds to receptors on neurone 2, which starts a new impulse.

One direction only, because the receptors are on the receiving side. Each synapse adds a small delay.

## B3.1b: Coordinated response

The nervous system can produce a coordinated response because it:

* reaches every part of the body;
* has many links (connections between neurones);
* has different sensory receptors;
* is able to coordinate responses (the CNS combines information and sends instructions).

### Method for an "explain how" answer

1. Name the stimulus and the receptor that detects it.
2. Say the impulse travels along a sensory neurone to the CNS.
3. Say the CNS coordinates, using any relevant information from other receptors.
4. Say impulses pass along motor neurones.
5. Name the effector (a named muscle or gland) and state the response.

### Small worked reminder

*A cyclist hears a horn and moves to the left.* Sound receptors in the ear detect the stimulus. Sensory neurones carry impulses to the CNS, which combines this with information from the eyes about the road. Motor neurones carry impulses to the arm and leg muscles (effectors), which contract and steer the bike.

### Receptors and effectors: matching pairs

| Stimulus | Receptor detects | Effector | Response |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sharp object under the foot | Pressure and pain in skin | Leg muscle | Foot lifts |
| Bright light | Light in the eye | Muscle of the iris | Pupil gets smaller |
| Smell of food | Chemicals in the nose | Salivary gland | Saliva is secreted |
| Dust on the cornea | Touch at the surface of the eye | Eyelid muscle | Blink |

Receptors differ because each type is sensitive to one kind of stimulus. That is why many different receptors can be linked to the same CNS.

### Small worked reminder: damage to the pathway

*Which component is damaged if a person feels a touch on the arm but cannot move it away?* The sensory neurone and receptor work, because the touch is felt. The fault is in the motor neurone or the muscle (effector). If instead the person cannot feel the touch and no reflex happens, the fault is in the receptor or sensory neurone.

## B3.1c: Reflex arcs

### Voluntary and reflex responses compared

| | Reflex | Voluntary |
|---|---|---|
| Speed | Very fast | Slower |
| Conscious decision | No | Yes |
| Pathway | Receptor, sensory, relay, motor, effector | Includes conscious part of the brain |
| Number of synapses | Few | More |
| Purpose | Protection, e.g. withdrawal, blinking, pupil size | Deliberate actions |

### Structure and function

* Few neurones and few synapses: short, quick pathway.
* Relay neurone links sensory and motor neurones directly: no conscious decision is needed.
* Myelinated axons: fast conduction.
* Gives protection from harm before pain is felt.

### Worked reminder: withdrawal reflex

Heat receptor in skin, sensory neurone to spinal cord, synapse, relay neurone, synapse, motor neurone, arm muscle contracts, hand moves away.

### Estimating with a reflex pathway

Estimation is a listed skill. Use rounded values, and say that the answer is approximate.

*A reflex path is about 0.8 m long. Impulses travel at about 60 m/s. Estimate the time spent travelling along the neurones.* Time = distance / speed = 0.8 / 60 = 0.013 s, so about **0.01 s** (10 ms). The real response also includes delays at the synapses, so the total is a little longer. Each added synapse adds to the delay, which is why a longer pathway through the brain gives a longer response.

### Why the arcs differ in length

A reflex of the leg is processed in the spinal cord, near the receptors and effectors, so the impulse does not travel up to the brain and back. A voluntary action must reach the conscious part of the brain, so the pathway is longer and has more synapses.

## Reaction time

* Reaction time (s) = time between stimulus and response.
* Ruler drop: t = √(2d/g), with d in m and g = 9.8 m/s².
* Convert cm to m by dividing by 100.

### Worked reminder

A ruler is caught after falling 22 cm. d = 0.22 m, so t = √(2 × 0.22 / 9.8) = √0.04490 = 0.212 s, or **0.21 s**.

### Reading a results table

| Trial | Distance (cm) | Reaction time (s) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 22 | 0.21 |
| 2 | 19 | 0.20 |
| 3 | 17 | 0.19 |
| 4 | 15 | 0.17 |
| 5 | 14 | 0.17 |

Reaction time falls with each trial, from 0.21 s to 0.17 s. A sensible explanation is practice, because the person learns to watch the ruler. A fair comparison with a different factor would need the practice effect removed, for example by including some practice drops first.

### Fair test checklist

* Change one factor; keep hand, ruler, start position and dropper the same.
* Drop the ruler without warning at random times.
* Repeat and take a mean; identify anomalies.
* Do not claim a cause from one person or a small sample.

## Must-know distinctions

* Sensory (to the CNS) and motor (from the CNS).
* Neurone (a cell) and nerve (a bundle of neurones; know the word, use neurone in answers about structure).
* Receptor (detects) and effector (responds).
* Reflex (automatic) and voluntary (conscious).
* Synapse (gap crossed by a chemical) and the axon (carries an electrical impulse).

## Quick self-test

1. What two organs make up the central nervous system?
2. Name the three types of neurone in a basic reflex arc, in order.
3. What is the function of the myelin sheath?
4. Explain why impulses pass across a synapse in one direction only.
5. Name the two types of effector.
6. Give the four features that let the nervous system produce a coordinated response.
7. Why is a reflex faster than a voluntary response?
8. A ruler falls 18 cm before being caught. Calculate the reaction time in seconds to 2 significant figures.
9. A person has a damaged sensory neurone in the leg. Predict what happens when the foot touches something sharp.
10. A student's five reaction times are 0.20, 0.22, 0.19, 0.21 and 0.18 s. Calculate the mean.

### Answers

1. Brain and spinal cord.
2. Sensory neurone, relay neurone, motor neurone.
3. It insulates the axon and speeds up the impulse.
4. The transmitter chemical is released from the end of one neurone only, and the receptors for it are on the next neurone only.
5. Muscles and glands.
6. It reaches every part of the body, has many links, has different sensory receptors and can coordinate responses.
7. It has a short pathway with few neurones and synapses, and does not wait for a conscious decision.
8. d = 0.18 m. t = √(2 × 0.18 / 9.8) = √0.03673 = 0.1917, so **0.19 s**.
9. No impulse reaches the CNS from the foot, so there is no reflex withdrawal and no sensation of pain. The motor neurones are fine, so the muscles could still move if the brain gave a command.
10. Total = 1.00 s; mean = **0.20 s**.

## Where marks are usually lost

* Writing "nerve" or "nerves" when the question asks about a specific neurone type.
* Describing the impulse as jumping across the synapse rather than naming a chemical crossing the gap.
* Naming the whole limb as the effector instead of a muscle.
* Leaving out the CNS or the relay neurone in a reflex arc.
* Saying the reflex is fast "because it does not use the brain" with no reference to fewer neurones and synapses.
* Forgetting to convert centimetres to metres in the reaction time equation.
* Giving a mean to too many figures, or including an anomaly without comment.
* Writing a conclusion about cause from one person's data.

## Frequently asked questions

**What is the pathway of a reflex arc?**
A reflex arc runs receptor, sensory neurone, relay neurone in the CNS, motor neurone, effector. The effector is a muscle that contracts or a gland that secretes.

**What does the myelin sheath do?**
The myelin sheath is a fatty insulating layer around the axon. It insulates the axon and speeds up the impulse, which matters because some pathways are about a metre long.

**What are effectors in the nervous system?**
Effectors are muscles and glands. Muscles respond by contracting and glands respond by secreting a substance, so in an answer name the muscle or gland rather than "the hand" or "the arm".

**What is reaction time?**
Reaction time is the time between a stimulus and the response. It can be estimated with a ruler drop test, using t = √(2d/g) with the drop distance in metres.
