## Questions

### Section A: short-answer questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by motivation. **[2]**

**2.** Distinguish between a hygiene factor and a motivator in Herzberg's theory. **[3]**

**3.** A worker rates the probability that effort leads to good performance at 0.7, that good performance leads to a reward at 0.6, and the value of the reward at 5. Calculate the motivational force using Vroom's formula. **[2]**

**4.** Which **one** of the following is a non-monetary method of motivation?

A: A quarterly bonus.

B: A share of profits.

C: Job enrichment.

D: Commission on sales. **[1]**

**5.** Explain **one** limitation of Maslow's hierarchy of needs for a manager. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** Quillon Logistics employs 25 warehouse staff. Last year 6 left. Managers believe poor motivation is the main cause. Each leaver costs £2,800 to replace. The company introduces monthly team goals with feedback and recognition, and this year 3 staff leave.

**(a)** Calculate the staff turnover for each year. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the saving in replacement costs. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain which motivation theory best explains why goals and feedback might have reduced turnover. **[4]**

**7.** Bellfield Insurance has 45 office staff on an average salary of £24,000. The directors are considering a bonus of 5% of salary for all staff who meet a customer satisfaction target.

**(a)** Calculate the cost of the bonus if all staff qualify. **[3]**

**(b)** Evaluate whether the bonus is likely to be an effective way of motivating the staff. **[8]**

**8.** Using Vroom's theory, a manager finds that her team's expectancy is 0.7 and the value of the reward is 5. At present the team believes there is a 0.6 chance that good performance leads to a reward. After a change to the scheme, the chance is 0.9.

**(a)** Calculate the force before and after the change. **[3]**

**(b)** Analyse what the change shows about how a manager can increase motivation. **[4]**

**9.** Harlow Textiles makes uniforms. Machinists repeat one task all day, are paid by the item and have high absence. The owner is considering job rotation and a skills programme with promotion to team leader. Evaluate the usefulness of these non-monetary methods for Harlow Textiles. **[9]**

**10.** Recommend and justify a method of motivation for a restaurant group that struggles to keep young waiting staff, who say that pay is acceptable but their jobs offer no progress. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Motivation is the desire to work towards a goal [1], the reason why a person puts effort into a task or job [1]. **[2]**

**2.** A hygiene factor, such as pay or working conditions, prevents dissatisfaction if it is good but does not motivate [1]. A motivator, such as achievement, recognition or interesting work, raises motivation [1]. Improving hygiene factors only removes complaints, while motivators are needed for greater effort [1]. **[3]**

**3.** 0.7 × 0.6 × 5 [1] = **2.1** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Workers do not all move up the levels in order [1], so a manager who assumes everyone wants esteem next may misjudge what motivates them, for example an employee who values job security far above recognition [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Last year: 6 ÷ 25 × 100 = **24%** [1]. This year: 3 ÷ 25 × 100 = **12%** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Three fewer leavers [1] × £2,800 = **£8,400** [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** Locke's goal-setting theory [1] says that specific, challenging goals with feedback improve performance [1]. Team goals and feedback give staff a clear target and a sense of achievement [1]. Recognition links to Herzberg's motivators, so staff feel valued and are less likely to leave [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** Bonus per person = 5% × £24,000 = £1,200 [1]. Total = 45 × £1,200 [1] = **£54,000** [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** For: a bonus linked to a customer satisfaction target gives staff a clear goal, which supports Locke's theory [1]. Money is simple to understand and acts quickly [1]. If staff believe their effort affects the target and they value £1,200, Vroom's force is high [1].
Against: the cost is £54,000, which must be covered by extra income or savings [1]. Pay is a hygiene factor for Herzberg, so the effect may fade [1]. If a team target is not under individual control, instrumentality is low and the bonus will not motivate [1]. Staff who do not meet the target may feel demotivated [1].
Judgement: the bonus is likely to help in the short term, but it works better if it is combined with non-monetary methods such as recognition and training, and if the target can be influenced by each member of staff [1]. Bellfield should test whether the increase in satisfaction scores is worth more than £54,000 in retained customers [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Before: 0.7 × 0.6 × 5 = **2.1** [1]. After: 0.7 × 0.9 × 5 = **3.15** [1]. Change: an increase of 1.05, or 50% [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** Neither the reward nor the effort required has changed, only the belief that performance is rewarded [1]. This shows that motivation can rise without extra cost if the manager makes the link between performance and reward clearer and more reliable [1]. For example, paying the reward promptly and keeping to promises [1]. It also shows that if staff do not trust the link, a bigger reward will not help [1]. **[4]**

**9.** For: job rotation reduces boredom, because machinists would not repeat one task all day [1], and it builds skills so the owner can cover absence [1]. A skills programme with promotion to team leader offers achievement and responsibility, which are Herzberg motivators [1], and a chance to move up Maslow's hierarchy to esteem [1].
Against: rotation among similar low-skill tasks may not make work more interesting [1]. Training costs money and time away from production [1]. Only a few can be promoted, so most may stay unmotivated [1]. Piece-rate pay means that time spent learning reduces earnings, which may deter staff [1].
Judgement: the programme with a clear route to promotion is likely to be more useful than rotation alone, but the owner should review the piece-rate system, since it may be a cause of the high absence [1]. **[9]**

**10.** Recommendation: create a career path, for example from waiter to senior waiter to shift leader, with training at each stage [1].
Justification: pay is acceptable, so the hygiene factor is met and the problem is the lack of motivators (Herzberg) [1]. A career path offers achievement, responsibility and growth, and meets the esteem and self-actualisation needs in Maslow's model [1]. It also improves the link between performance and reward in Vroom's terms [1].
Cost and risk: training and higher pay for senior roles cost money [1]; the benefit is lower turnover, since each leaver costs recruitment and training [1]. Not every waiter wants to progress, so some may need flexible hours or recognition instead [1].
Judgement: introduce the path alongside regular recognition, because progression gives a reason to stay while recognition keeps morale up in the meantime [1]. **[8]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating pay as a Herzberg motivator.
- Quoting the theory without applying it to the case.
- Ignoring the cost of a scheme.
- Giving a calculation without working.
- Concluding "motivation is important" without a reason.
