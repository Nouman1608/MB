This guide covers sub-topic **7.2 Digestive system** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Both statements are Core and there is no Supplement content, so all of it can be tested on every paper.

The [human nutrition study guide](/resources/igcse-biology-human-nutrition/) lists the organs and the five processes. This page shows you how to find each organ on an unfamiliar diagram, what each organ does in terms of the five processes, and how to answer "follow the food" questions. Condense it with the [digestive system revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-digestive-system-revision-notes/) and test yourself with the [digestive system practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-digestive-system-practice/).

## What 7.2 asks you to do

| Statement | Command word | Content |
|---|---|---|
| 7.2.1 | Identify | In diagrams and images: (a) the alimentary canal: mouth, oesophagus, stomach, small intestine (duodenum and ileum) and large intestine (colon, rectum, anus); (b) the associated organs: salivary glands, pancreas, liver and gall bladder |
| 7.2.2 | Describe | The functions of those organs in relation to ingestion, digestion, absorption, assimilation and egestion |

## Two kinds of organ

The **alimentary canal** is the tube that food actually travels through, from mouth to anus. The **associated organs** are attached to it by ducts and pour secretions into it, but food never passes through them.

```
mouth → oesophagus → stomach → duodenum → ileum → colon → rectum → anus
           (small intestine = duodenum + ileum; large intestine = colon + rectum + anus)
```

Associated organs and where their secretions go:

- **Salivary glands** → saliva into the mouth.
- **Pancreas** → pancreatic juice into the duodenum, through the pancreatic duct.
- **Liver** → makes bile.
- **Gall bladder** → stores bile and releases it into the duodenum through the bile duct.

## Finding the organs on a diagram

Diagrams are normally drawn as if the person is facing you, so the body's left is on **your right**.

| Organ | Recognition clue |
|---|---|
| Salivary glands | small glands near the jaw, with ducts into the mouth |
| Oesophagus | narrow tube running down through the chest behind the windpipe |
| Stomach | J-shaped muscular bag just under the diaphragm, on the body's left (your right) |
| Liver | the largest organ, under the diaphragm, mostly on the body's right (your left), partly covering the stomach |
| Gall bladder | small green or dark sac tucked under the liver |
| Pancreas | flattish, leaf-shaped gland lying below the stomach, in the loop of the duodenum |
| Duodenum | first, C-shaped part of the small intestine, leaving the stomach |
| Ileum | long, coiled tube filling the centre of the abdomen |
| Colon | wider tube that frames the coils of the ileum, up one side, across and down the other |
| Rectum | short, straight final section above the anus |
| Anus | opening at the end of the canal |

Two clues settle most labelling questions: the **duodenum** is wherever the bile duct and pancreatic duct join the gut, and the **colon** is the wide tube around the outside of the coiled ileum.

## The five processes

Use the syllabus definitions word for word.

| Process | Definition |
|---|---|
| Ingestion | the taking of substances, e.g. food and drink, into the body |
| Digestion | the breakdown of food |
| Absorption | the movement of nutrients from the intestines into the blood |
| Assimilation | uptake and use of nutrients by cells |
| Egestion | the removal of undigested food from the body as faeces |

Absorption and assimilation are different steps. Absorption moves a glucose molecule from the ileum into the blood. Assimilation happens later, when a cell somewhere in the body takes that glucose up and uses it, for example in respiration, or when a liver cell stores glucose as glycogen, or a muscle cell uses amino acids to build proteins.

Egestion is not excretion. Faeces are mostly food that was never absorbed, so it was never inside the body's cells. Excretion removes waste made by metabolism, which is covered in topic 13.

## What each organ does

| Organ | Process(es) | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Mouth | ingestion; digestion | food and drink enter; teeth break food into pieces; amylase in saliva starts digesting starch |
| Salivary glands | digestion | secrete saliva containing amylase; saliva also moistens food for swallowing |
| Oesophagus | (transport) | muscles in its wall carry food from the mouth to the stomach |
| Stomach | digestion | muscular walls churn food; gastric juice contains protease and hydrochloric acid |
| Duodenum | digestion | receives pancreatic juice and bile; digestion of starch, protein and fat continues |
| Ileum | digestion; absorption | digestion is completed; small soluble nutrients and most of the water are absorbed into the blood |
| Pancreas | digestion | secretes pancreatic juice containing amylase, protease and lipase into the duodenum |
| Liver | digestion; assimilation | makes bile, which helps fat digestion; its cells take up and use absorbed nutrients |
| Gall bladder | digestion | stores bile and releases it into the duodenum |
| Colon | absorption | some water is absorbed from the undigested remains |
| Rectum | egestion | stores faeces until they are removed |
| Anus | egestion | faeces leave the body |

The oesophagus does not fit any of the five processes neatly: it moves food. If a question asks which process happens there, describe the movement instead of forcing a label.

## Worked example 1: following a meal

**A student eats a cheese sandwich. Describe what happens to it from ingestion to egestion. Name the organs involved.** **[6]**

> The sandwich is ingested through the mouth, where teeth break it into smaller pieces and amylase in saliva starts digesting starch [1]. It is swallowed down the oesophagus to the stomach, which churns it with gastric juice containing protease and acid [1]. In the duodenum, pancreatic juice from the pancreas and bile from the gall bladder are added and digestion continues [1]. Digestion is completed in the ileum, where the small soluble nutrients are absorbed into the blood [1]. The nutrients are carried to cells, which assimilate them, for example using amino acids to make proteins [1]. Undigested material, such as fibre in the bread, passes into the colon, where some water is absorbed, and is stored in the rectum and egested through the anus as faeces [1].

Why it scores: every sentence names an organ **and** a process, and the organs are in the right order.

## Worked example 2: identifying from function

**State the organ described by each statement.**

- Makes bile but does not store it: **liver**.
- Food passes through it, and it is where most water is absorbed: **small intestine (ileum)**.
- Secretes an enzyme-containing juice but food never enters it: **pancreas** (or salivary glands, if the juice goes into the mouth).
- The first part of the small intestine: **duodenum**.

## Worked example 3: a "suggest" question

**A patient has had her gall bladder removed. Suggest why she is advised to eat smaller amounts of fatty food at each meal.** **[2]**

- Her liver still makes bile, but there is no gall bladder to store it and release a large amount into the duodenum after a meal [1].
- So a large fatty meal would not have enough bile at once to help its digestion [1].

## Common mistakes

- Saying the gall bladder **makes** bile. The liver makes it; the gall bladder stores it.
- Listing the liver or pancreas as parts of the alimentary canal. Food does not pass through them.
- Putting the ileum before the duodenum, or the rectum before the colon.
- Writing "absorption happens in the stomach". Nutrients are absorbed from the small intestine.
- Treating absorption and assimilation as the same process.
- Calling egestion "excretion".
- Labelling the pancreas as the gall bladder on a diagram: the pancreas is below the stomach, the gall bladder is under the liver.

## Exam technique for 7.2

- **Identify** an organ: use the exact syllabus name. "Gullet" and "bowel" are not the names asked for.
- **Describe the function**: link the organ to one of the five processes and say what happens there.
- **Define**: give the syllabus wording; a definition of absorption must say "from the intestines into the blood".
- In sequence questions, write organs in the order food meets them.
