## Short-answer questions

Cambridge O Level Accounting 7707 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** Goods are sold on credit to M Osei. Which entry records this?

A: Debit sales, credit M Osei

B: Debit M Osei, credit sales

C: Debit M Osei, credit purchases

D: Debit bank, credit sales

**[1]**

**2.** What does a credit balance on a business's bank account show?

A: Money held in the bank

B: Money owed to the business by the bank

D: Interest received

**[1]**

**3.** State the account debited and the account credited for each transaction.

**(a)** The owner takes $250 cash for personal use. **[2]**

**(b)** A customer, T Ali, returns goods worth $180. **[2]**

**(c)** A bank loan of $8,000 is received into the bank. **[2]**

**4.** Name the ledger in which each account is kept.

**(a)** The account of a credit customer. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the sales account is transferred to the statement of profit or loss at the year end, but the trade receivables accounts are not. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Amara Crafts had $3,200 in the bank on 1 July. Its July transactions were:

- 4 July: bought goods on credit from Fenwick Supplies, $1,750
- 9 July: returned goods to Fenwick Supplies, $150
- 14 July: paid Fenwick Supplies $1,000 by bank transfer
- 18 July: cash sales of $2,300 paid into the bank
- 22 July: paid wages of $1,100 by bank transfer
- 29 July: bought goods on credit from Fenwick Supplies, $600

**(a)** Prepare the account of Fenwick Supplies in Amara Crafts' purchases ledger, balanced at 31 July. **[4]**

**(b)** Prepare the bank account, balanced at 31 July. **[4]**

**8.** A business's bank balance on 1 August was $1,500 Dr. In August:

- 3 August: paid a supplier $2,100

**(a)** Prepare the bank account in three-column running balance format. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain what the balance on 3 August shows. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. The customer receives value (debit); sales are income (credit). **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. The business owes the bank. **[1]**

**3. (a)** Debit drawings [1], credit cash [1].

**(b)** Debit sales returns [1], credit T Ali [1].

**(c)** Debit bank [1], credit loan [1]. **[6]**

**4. (a)** Sales ledger [1].

**(b)** Nominal (general) ledger [1].

**(c)** Purchases ledger [1]. **[3]**

**5.** Sales is an income account, so its total for the year is used to calculate profit and the account starts the next year at zero [1]. Trade receivables are assets still owed to the business, so their balances are carried down and shown in the statement of financial position [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Debits 3,500 − credits 2,300 = **$1,200 debit** [1]. The customer still owes the business $1,200 [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** Hepburn bought goods for resale on credit from Lowry Fabrics for $2,250 [1].

**(c)** Hepburn returned goods worth $320 to Lowry Fabrics [1].

**11. (a)** Sales ledger: J Patel and K Lim [1]. Purchases ledger: Northside Wholesale [1]. Nominal (general) ledger: Sales, Motor vehicles, Rent [1].

**(b)** Any two: different staff can work on different ledgers at the same time [1]; accounts are easier to find [1]; errors are easier to locate; supports control and checking. **[5]**

**12.** Advantages: the program posts both sides of each entry from one input, so debits always equal credits [1]; balances are calculated automatically and can be seen at any time, for example in running balance format [1]; the ledger can be backed up easily [1].

Disadvantages: there is a cost of equipment, programs and training [1]; entries posted to the wrong account or left out are not detected, so Grace still needs to check her work [1].

Conclusion: Grace should change if her business has enough transactions to justify the cost, because the time saved and accuracy of balances outweigh the disadvantages; she should keep a back-up copy [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing debit and credit for capital, returns or drawings.
- Bringing a balance down on the wrong side.
- Leaving out Dr or Cr in running balance accounts.
- Transferring asset or liability balances to the statement of profit or loss.
- Not naming the other account in the details column.
