## Section A

**1.** State Hess's law. **[2]**

**2.** Suggest why the standard enthalpy change of formation of glucose, C₆H₁₂O₆, cannot be measured directly. **[1]**

**3.** For H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l), ΔH = −286. What is ΔH for 2H₂O(l) → 2H₂(g) + O₂(g)?

A: −572

B: −286

C: +286

D: +572 **[1]**

**4.** Use the equations below to calculate the standard enthalpy change of formation of nitrogen dioxide, NO₂(g).

N₂(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO(g)    ΔH = +180

2NO(g) + O₂(g) → 2NO₂(g)    ΔH = −114 **[2]**

**5.** ΔH_f⦵: Fe₂O₃(s) −824, Al₂O₃(s) −1676. Calculate ΔH for 2Al(s) + Fe₂O₃(s) → Al₂O₃(s) + 2Fe(s). **[2]**

**6.** Graphite and diamond have ΔH_c⦵ values of −393.5 and −395.4. Calculate ΔH for C(graphite) → C(diamond). **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** ΔH_c⦵: C(s) −394, H₂(g) −286, C₆H₆(l) −3268.

**(a)** Write the equation for the standard enthalpy change of formation of benzene, with state symbols. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the energy cycle you would use, saying where the combustion products are placed and which way the arrows point. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate ΔH_f⦵ of benzene. **[2]**

**8.** ΔH_f⦵: C₂H₅OH(l) −277, CO₂(g) −394, H₂O(l) −286.

**(a)** Calculate the standard enthalpy change of combustion of ethanol. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why no value for O₂(g) is needed. **[1]**

**9.** The enthalpy change for MgCO₃(s) → MgO(s) + CO₂(g) is found indirectly. A student measures (*illustrative* values):

MgCO₃(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)    ΔH₁ = −42

MgO(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂O(l)    ΔH₂ = −150

**(a)** Suggest why the decomposition enthalpy cannot easily be measured directly. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔH for the decomposition of magnesium carbonate. **[2]**

**(c)** State two measurements the student must make to find ΔH₁. **[2]**

**10.** Use data booklet bond energies (C–H 410, Cl–Cl 242, C–Cl 340, H–Cl 431) for CH₄(g) + Cl₂(g) → CH₃Cl(g) + HCl(g).

**(a)** Calculate ΔH. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this calculation is a Hess's law cycle, naming the intermediate. **[2]**

**11.** ΔH_c⦵: C₃H₆(g) −2058, H₂(g) −286, C₃H₈(g) −2220.

**(a)** Calculate ΔH for the hydrogenation C₃H₆(g) + H₂(g) → C₃H₈(g). **[2]**

**(b)** State whether the reaction is exothermic or endothermic. **[1]**

**12.** Three reactions have these enthalpy changes:

(1) C(s) + O₂(g) → CO₂(g)    ΔH = −394

(2) H₂(g) + ½O₂(g) → H₂O(l)    ΔH = −286

(3) C₂H₂(g) + 2½O₂(g) → 2CO₂(g) + H₂O(l)    ΔH = −1300

**(a)** Show how the equations combine to give 2C(s) + H₂(g) → C₂H₂(g). **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate ΔH_f⦵ of ethyne. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** The enthalpy change for a reaction is independent of the route taken [1], provided the initial and final conditions are the same [1].

**2.** Carbon, hydrogen and oxygen do not react together to form glucose (the reaction cannot be carried out) [1].

**3.** D [1].

**4.** Add the equations: N₂ + 2O₂ → 2NO₂, ΔH = 180 + (−114) = +66 [1]; per mole of NO₂: +33 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**5.** ΔH = ΣΔH_f(products) − ΣΔH_f(reactants) = (−1676 + 0) − (0 + (−824)) [1] = −852 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**6.** Both burn to CO₂; ΔH = ΔH_c(graphite) − ΔH_c(diamond) = −393.5 − (−395.4) [1] = +1.9 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**7. (a)** 6C(s) + 3H₂(g) → C₆H₆(l) [1].

**(b)** The combustion products (6CO₂ + 3H₂O) are placed below; arrows point down from the elements and from benzene to the combustion products [1]; the route from elements to benzene goes down the elements' arrows and up (against) benzene's arrow [1].

**(c)** ΔH_f = [6(−394) + 3(−286)] − (−3268) = −2364 − 858 + 3268 [1] = +46 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**8. (a)** C₂H₅OH + 3O₂ → 2CO₂ + 3H₂O [1]; ΔH_c = [2(−394) + 3(−286)] − (−277) [1] = −1369 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** O₂ is an element in its standard state, so its ΔH_f⦵ is zero [1].

**9. (a)** It needs strong heating, and it is difficult to measure the heat absorbed at high temperature (or the reaction is slow and incomplete) [1].

**(b)** ΔH = ΔH₁ − ΔH₂ = −42 − (−150) [1] = +108 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(c)** Any two: the mass of MgCO₃ used; the volume (mass) of acid; the initial and maximum temperatures (temperature change) of the solution [2].

**10. (a)** Broken: 410 + 242 = 652; formed: 340 + 431 = 771 [1]; ΔH = 652 − 771 = −119 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** The reaction is imagined as going via gaseous atoms (C, H and Cl atoms) [1]: breaking bonds to reach the atoms, then forming new bonds; by Hess's law this route gives the same ΔH as the direct reaction [1].

**11. (a)** ΔH = [ΔH_c(C₃H₆) + ΔH_c(H₂)] − ΔH_c(C₃H₈) = (−2058 − 286) − (−2220) [1] = −124 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** Exothermic (ΔH negative) [1].

**12. (a)** Use 2 × (1) + (2) [1] and reverse (3) [1].

**(b)** ΔH_f = 2(−394) + (−286) − (−1300) = −788 − 286 + 1300 = +226 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Not multiplying by the coefficients, especially for H₂O and CO₂.
- Using "products minus reactants" with combustion data.
- Forgetting to halve when the target is per mole of a product formed in twos.
- Reversing an equation without changing the sign.
- Subtracting negative values without brackets.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is ΔH for graphite to diamond positive?**
Diamond releases slightly more energy than graphite when burned, so it starts at a higher enthalpy. Converting graphite into diamond therefore absorbs energy.
