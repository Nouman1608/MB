These notes condense sub-topic **17.1 Chromosomes, genes and proteins** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 4 are Core; outcomes 5 to 12 are **Extended only**.

For explanations and worked answers, read the [chromosomes, genes and proteins study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-chromosomes-genes-and-proteins/). Then try the [chromosomes, genes and proteins practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-chromosomes-genes-and-proteins-practice/).

## Core recall

- **Chromosomes** are made of **DNA**, which contains genetic information in the form of **genes**.
- **Gene:** a length of DNA that codes for a protein.
- **Allele:** an alternative form of a gene.
- Order of size: nucleus > chromosome > gene.

**Sex inheritance**

| | Female | Male |
|---|---|---|
| Sex chromosomes | XX | XY |
| Gametes | all eggs carry X | half of sperm carry X, half carry Y |

```
          X      X
   X     XX     XX
   Y     XY     XY
→ 1 female : 1 male; P(boy) = 1/2 for every child
```

The **sperm** decides the sex. Each child is an independent event.

## The chain from DNA to function -- Extended only

**base sequence in gene → amino acid sequence → protein shape → protein function**

- Details of nucleotide structure are **not** required.
- Different amino acid sequences give **different shapes**.
- DNA controls cell function by controlling the production of proteins:

| Protein | Role |
|---|---|
| Enzymes | catalyse reactions |
| Membrane carriers | move substances across membranes |
| Receptors for neurotransmitters | bind neurotransmitters at synapses |

## Making a protein -- Extended only

1. Gene **stays in the nucleus**.
2. **mRNA** is made as a **copy of the gene**.
3. mRNA is **made in the nucleus** and **moves to the cytoplasm**.
4. mRNA **passes through ribosomes**.
5. Ribosome **assembles amino acids** into a protein.
6. Amino acid order is set by the **sequence of bases in the mRNA**.

Not required: details of transcription or translation.

## Gene expression -- Extended only

- Most body cells contain the **same genes**.
- Many genes in a given cell are **not expressed**: the cell makes only the **specific proteins it needs**.
- Example: insulin gene expressed in some pancreas cells; present but not expressed in skin cells.

## Haploid and diploid -- Extended only

| Nucleus | Definition | Human example |
|---|---|---|
| Haploid | a single set of chromosomes | sperm, egg: 23 |
| Diploid | two sets of chromosomes; a pair of each type | body cells, zygote: 23 pairs = 46 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Chromosome | a structure in the nucleus made of DNA |
| DNA | the molecule that carries genetic information as genes |
| Gene | a length of DNA that codes for a protein |
| Allele | an alternative form of a gene |
| Sex chromosomes | the X and Y chromosomes |
| mRNA | messenger RNA, a copy of a gene that moves to the cytoplasm (Extended) |
| Ribosome | the site where amino acids are assembled into proteins (Extended) |
| Gene expression | a gene being used to make its protein (Extended) |
| Haploid | a nucleus with a single set of chromosomes (Extended) |
| Diploid | a nucleus with two sets of chromosomes (Extended) |

## Exam traps

- Chromosomes are made of **DNA**, not "of genes".
- Gene ≠ allele. Gene for a feature; allele for a version of it.
- The **father's sperm** decides sex.
- P(boy) stays 1/2 whatever earlier children were.
- (Extended) mRNA is made in the **nucleus**; the gene **never** leaves.
- (Extended) Ribosomes **assemble** amino acids; they do not make them.
- (Extended) All body cells have the same genes; they **express** different ones.
- (Extended) Human body cell: **23 pairs**, not 23.

## Answer frames

- **Why boys and girls are born in roughly equal numbers (Core):** all eggs carry X; half of sperm carry X and half carry Y; each type of sperm is equally likely to fertilise the egg, so XX and XY zygotes are equally likely.

**Extended only:**

- **Why a change in DNA stops an enzyme working:** different base sequence → different amino acid sequence → different shape → active site no longer fits substrate.
- **Why two cells in one body make different proteins:** same genes → different genes expressed → each makes only the proteins it needs.
- **Why mRNA is needed:** gene stays in nucleus; ribosomes in cytoplasm; mRNA carries a copy of the base sequence.

## Self-test

**1.** What are chromosomes made of?

**2.** Define a gene.

**3.** Define an allele.

**4.** State the sex chromosomes of a human male.

**5.** Explain why the father's gamete decides the sex of a child.

**6.** A couple have two sons. What is the probability that the next child is a son?

**7.** *(Extended)* What does the sequence of bases in a gene determine?

**8.** *(Extended)* Why do different proteins have different shapes?

**9.** *(Extended)* Where is mRNA made, and where does it go?

**10.** *(Extended)* What happens at a ribosome?

**11.** *(Extended)* Why does a muscle cell not make amylase, even though it has the amylase gene?

**12.** *(Extended)* How many chromosomes are in a human egg cell, and is its nucleus haploid or diploid?

### Answers

**1.** DNA.

**2.** A length of DNA that codes for a protein.

**3.** An alternative form of a gene.

**4.** XY.

**5.** All eggs carry X; sperm carry either X or Y, so the sperm determines whether the zygote is XX or XY.

**6.** 1/2 (50%).

**7.** The sequence of amino acids used to make a specific protein.

**8.** They have different sequences of amino acids.

**9.** In the nucleus; it moves to the cytoplasm (to the ribosomes).

**10.** Amino acids are assembled into a protein, in the order set by the mRNA base sequence.

**11.** The gene is not expressed; the cell only makes the specific proteins it needs.

**12.** 23; haploid.
