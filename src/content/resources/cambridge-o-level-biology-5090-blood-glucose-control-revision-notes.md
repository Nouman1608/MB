These notes condense subtopic **14.6 Blood glucose control** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: why blood glucose must be controlled, the roles of the pancreas, liver, insulin and glucagon, and Type 1 diabetes. The full explanations and worked examples are in the [14.6 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood-glucose-control/). Then try the [14.6 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood-glucose-control-practice/).

## Key facts

| Item | Fact |
|---|---|
| Fuel | Glucose is the main fuel for respiration |
| Control | Negative feedback keeps blood glucose near a set point |
| Pancreas | Detects blood glucose; secretes insulin and glucagon |
| Liver | Target organ; stores glucose as glycogen and releases it |
| Glycogen | Insoluble storage carbohydrate made from glucose |

## Why control is needed

| If blood glucose is | Problem |
|---|---|
| Too low | Not enough glucose for respiration; brain and muscles lack energy |
| Too high | Affects the water balance of cells by osmosis; glucose is lost in the urine |

## The two hormones

| | Insulin | Glucagon |
|---|---|---|
| Made by | Pancreas | Pancreas |
| Released when | Blood glucose is too high | Blood glucose is too low |
| Action in the liver | Glucose converted to glycogen | Glycogen converted to glucose |
| Effect | Blood glucose falls | Blood glucose rises |

**Adrenaline** also raises blood glucose, in danger or excitement, by causing the liver to release glucose.

## Feedback loops

**Too high:** blood glucose rises, the pancreas releases insulin, the liver converts glucose to glycogen, blood glucose falls, insulin release reduces.

**Too low:** blood glucose falls, the pancreas releases glucagon, the liver converts glycogen to glucose, blood glucose rises, glucagon release reduces.

## Type 1 diabetes

| Feature | Detail |
|---|---|
| Cause | The pancreas does not make enough insulin |
| Signs | Increased blood glucose concentration; glucose in the urine |
| Why glucose is in the urine | The concentration is so high that the kidney tubules cannot reabsorb all of it |
| Treatment | Administration of insulin (injection) |
| Why injected | Insulin is a protein; if swallowed it would be digested |

## Putting the loops in order (exam sequence)

| Step | Glucose too high | Glucose too low |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Change | Rises above set point (after a meal) | Falls below set point (exercise, fasting) |
| 2. Detected by | Pancreas | Pancreas |
| 3. Hormone released | Insulin | Glucagon |
| 4. Carried by | Blood to the liver | Blood to the liver |
| 5. Liver response | Glucose converted to glycogen | Glycogen converted to glucose |
| 6. Result | Blood glucose falls | Blood glucose rises |
| 7. Feedback | Insulin release falls | Glucagon release falls |

## Diabetes: cause, sign, reason, treatment

1. **Cause:** not enough insulin is made by the pancreas.
2. **Effect:** glucose is not converted to glycogen well, so blood glucose stays high.
3. **Sign 1:** increased blood glucose concentration.
4. **Sign 2:** glucose in the urine, because the tubules cannot reabsorb it all.
5. **Treatment:** insulin injections, matched to food eaten and activity.

## Three similar words

| Word | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Glucose | The sugar in the blood; fuel for respiration |
| Glycogen | The insoluble store of glucose in the liver |
| Glucagon | The hormone that raises blood glucose |

## Worked calculation

*Blood glucose is 85 mg per 100 cm3 before a meal and 136 mg per 100 cm3 30 minutes after it. Find the percentage increase.*

1. Increase = 136 - 85 = 51 mg per 100 cm3.
2. Percentage = 51 divided by 85 x 100 = 60 per cent.

Divide by the starting value, 85.

## Reading glucose tolerance data

| Pattern | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rises after a meal, then falls back to the start | Healthy; insulin is working |
| Starts high, rises higher, stays high | Consistent with Type 1 diabetes |
| Falls below the start after exercise | Glucose is being used for respiration; glucagon should raise it |

## Short model answers

**Explain why blood glucose concentration must be controlled.** If it is too low, cells cannot respire fast enough. If it is too high, the water balance of cells is upset and glucose is lost in the urine.

**Describe how insulin lowers blood glucose.** The pancreas releases insulin into the blood. It is carried to the liver, which converts glucose into glycogen for storage.

**Describe the role of glucagon.** The pancreas releases glucagon when blood glucose is low. It causes the liver to convert glycogen into glucose and release it into the blood.

**State two signs of Type 1 diabetes.** Increased blood glucose concentration and glucose in the urine.

**State the treatment.** Injections of insulin.

## Exam traps

- **Insulin** lowers; **glucagon** raises. Do not swap them.
- Glucose, glycogen and glucagon are three different things.
- The **liver** stores glycogen, not the pancreas.
- The pancreas is the sensor and the source of both hormones.
- Insulin is injected, not swallowed.
- Adrenaline is not the everyday control; it is an emergency boost.
- Name the sign "glucose in the urine"; do not write "sugar".
- Explain a rise or fall with negative feedback, including the set point.

4. The pancreas.
5. Insulin.
6. Convert glucose to glycogen.
7. An insoluble storage carbohydrate made from glucose.
8. Glucagon.
9. Increased blood glucose concentration and glucose in the urine.
