Cambridge O Level Sociology (2251) asks students to look critically at
the society around them: where identity comes from, why some groups
have more power and wealth than others, and how families, schools and
the justice system work. The syllabus is organised around key questions
rather than lists of facts, encouraging students to weigh evidence and
explanations. Two written papers are set, each carrying 80 marks and
50% of the grade; the first lasts 120 minutes and the second 105
minutes. The guidance here follows the 2025-2027 syllabus.

## How the course is organised

There are six topic areas, three per paper, and each area is built from
three key questions.

### Paper 1 – Research Methods, Identity and Inequality

1. **Research methods.** How do sociologists approach the study of
   society? What types of data and evidence do they use? How do they
   investigate society?
2. **Identity: self and society.** How do we learn our identity? How
   does society control us? What influences our social identity?
3. **Social stratification and inequality.** What is social
   stratification? What attempts have been made to reduce social
   inequalities? How has globalisation affected inequalities between
   societies?

### Paper 2 – Family, Education and Crime

1. **Family.** What are the different types of families? What is the
   role of the family for the individual and society? How is family
   life changing?
2. **Education.** What is education and schooling? What are the roles
   and functions of education? What factors help to explain
   differences in educational achievement and experience?
3. **Crime, deviance and social control.** What are crime, deviance and
   social control? What are the patterns of and explanations for crime
   and deviance? How is crime dealt with in different societies?

Reading the key questions as a set is a useful way to see the shape of
each topic: usually the first question defines the topic, the second
explains how it works, and the third looks at change, difference or
response.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Research Methods, Identity and Inequality | 120 minutes | 80 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Family, Education and Crime | 105 minutes | 80 | 50% |

Both papers are written examinations marked externally, and there are
no tiers. The qualification is linear, with both papers taken at the
end of the course, and the syllabus is examined in the June and
November series. The two papers are equal in marks and weighting but
not in length, since Paper 1 gives you more time for the same 80 marks.
Details of how each paper's questions are arranged are covered in the
exam preparation guide.

## How the parts fit together

The two papers are best thought of as **tools** and **applications**.
Paper 1 gives you the tools: methods for studying society, the concepts
of socialisation and social control, and the idea of stratification.
Paper 2 applies those tools to three institutions.

Some examples of the links:

- Family and education are both studied as agents of socialisation, an
  idea introduced under identity in Paper 1.
- Explaining differences in educational achievement draws on social
  stratification and inequality, including class, gender and
  ethnicity.
- Crime and deviance connects with social control from Paper 1, and
  explaining patterns of crime depends on understanding how data are
  collected, which is a research methods question.
- Globalisation, met in Paper 1, gives a wider frame for comparing how
  crime is dealt with in different societies.

Two skills run through the whole course. The first is using
sociological terms accurately, since the subject has a precise
vocabulary. The second is evaluation: weighing different explanations
and judging the strengths and limitations of the evidence behind them.

## Planning your study

Start with research methods, because positivist and interpretivist
approaches, types of data and methods of investigation come up in every
other topic. Then follow the papers in order:

- identity: self and society;
- social stratification and inequality;
- family;
- education;
- crime, deviance and social control.

As you finish each topic, write a one-page summary that answers its
three key questions in your own words, with a supporting example or
study for each. These summaries make excellent revision material later.

Begin short-answer questions as soon as each topic is taught. Once all
of Paper 1 is complete, start longer answers on Paper 1 topics while
you study Paper 2, and do the same for Paper 2 once it is finished.
Timed full papers come in the final run-up to the series you are
entered for.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Cambridge O Level Sociology 2251 assessed?**
By two written papers. Paper 1 Research Methods, Identity and Inequality lasts 120 minutes and Paper 2 Family, Education and Crime lasts 105 minutes; each carries 80 marks and 50%.

**What topics are in Paper 1?**
Research methods; identity: self and society; and social stratification and inequality.

**What topics are in Paper 2?**
Family; education; and crime, deviance and social control.

**Is O Level Sociology the same as IGCSE Sociology?**
The paper titles, timing and weighting are the same as Cambridge IGCSE Sociology 0495, but 2251 is a separate syllabus, so make sure you know which one you are entered for.

**When can I sit O Level Sociology?**
The 2025-2027 syllabus is examined in the June and November series.
