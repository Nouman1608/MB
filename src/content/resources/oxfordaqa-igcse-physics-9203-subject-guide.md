OxfordAQA International GCSE Physics (9203) is a stand-alone physics
qualification for students in the final two years of secondary school.
It runs from forces and energy through waves,
electricity and nuclear physics to the life cycle of stars, and it is
taught as a linear course over two years. Assessment is by two written
papers, each lasting 90 minutes, each marked out of 90 and each worth
50% of the qualification. The course is not tiered and there is no
separate practical exam.

## How the course is organised

The specification has eight numbered topics. They fall into four broad
groups.

**Forces, motion and energy**

- **1 Forces and their effects** is the largest topic: forces and their
  interactions, motion, resultant forces, momentum, safety in public
  transport, terminal velocity, centre of mass, and moments and levers.
- **2 Energy** covers the link between forces and energy, energy
  transfers with conservation and dissipation, and energy resources.

**Waves**

- **3 Waves** covers general wave properties, the electromagnetic
  spectrum, sound and ultrasound, reflection, refraction with total
  internal reflection, and lenses and the eye.

**Matter and electricity**

- **4 Particle model of matter** covers kinetic theory and how energy
  transfers relate to particle motion.
- **5 Electricity and magnetism** covers electrical circuits, magnetism
  and electromagnetism.
- **6 Generating and distributing electricity and household use** covers
  generating electricity, transmission and distribution, electricity in
  the home, the motor effect, and transferring electrical energy.

**Nuclear and space physics**

- **7 Nuclear physics** covers atomic structure, ionising radiation from
  the nucleus, nuclear fission and nuclear fusion.
- **8 Space physics** covers the life cycle of a star, the solar system
  and orbital motion, and red shift with the expanding universe.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Scope | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| Paper 2 | Any part of the specification may be assessed | 90 minutes | 90 | 50% |

Both papers are externally assessed written exams, and every candidate
sits the same two papers. The key point is in the scope column: there is
no division of topics between Paper 1 and Paper 2. Either paper can ask
about any of the eight topics, so revision has to cover the whole course
before each paper.

There is no separate practical exam, so the whole grade comes from the
two written papers; the experimental work you do in class supports the
topics rather than being marked on its own. The qualification is linear, so both papers are sat
together at the end of the two years. The specification was first taught
in September 2016 and first examined in May/June 2018.

OxfordAQA also applies a prohibited combinations rule across its
International GCSE science qualifications, which limits which science
qualifications can be taken together. If you are taking more than one
science, check your entries with your school.

## How the parts fit together

Physics at this level is unusually interconnected, and the open scope of
both papers means questions often bridge topics. Some of the main
connections:

- **Forces and energy are two views of the same events.** Work done by a
  force, kinetic and gravitational potential energy, and efficiency link
  topic 1 to topic 2, and the same ideas reappear in electricity and in
  orbital motion.
- **The particle model links to energy and to electricity.** Kinetic
  theory explains heating and changes of state in topic 4, and the idea
  of moving charged particles underpins current in topic 5.
- **Magnetism leads to generation.** Electromagnetism in topic 5 is the
  basis for the motor effect, generators and transformers in topic 6.
- **Energy resources connect to nuclear physics.** Fission and fusion in
  topic 7 return to the energy resources studied in topic 2, and fusion
  reappears in the life cycle of a star in topic 8.
- **Waves connect to space.** The electromagnetic spectrum from topic 3
  is needed to understand red shift and the evidence for an expanding
  universe.

Running through every topic are the same skills: recalling and applying
equations with correct units, interpreting graphs, describing and
evaluating experimental methods, and explaining physical processes in
clear steps.

## Planning your study

Because the papers do not split the content, the order of teaching is
set by what each topic needs rather than by the exam. A typical route:

- **First year:** forces and their effects, then energy, then the
  particle model of matter. These build a secure base in quantities,
  units and equations. Begin waves towards the end of the year.
- **Second year, first part:** electricity and magnetism followed by
  generating and distributing electricity, so that the electromagnetism
  ideas are fresh when you meet generators and the motor effect.
- **Second year, second part:** nuclear physics and space physics, then
  a full review of all eight topics.

Keep an equation sheet from the start and add to it as each topic is
taught; practising recall and rearrangement little and often pays off
across both papers. Write up each practical as you do it, noting the
variables, the measurements taken and the sources of error, because a
clear record of how a result was obtained makes the physics behind it
easier to explain in a written answer. Start mixed-topic exam questions once the
first-year topics are complete, and move to full papers in the final
months. The course's exam preparation page covers how to work the two
papers themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is OxfordAQA IGCSE Physics 9203 assessed?**
By two written papers, each lasting 90 minutes, marked out of 90 and worth 50% of the qualification. Both are sat at the end of the course.

**Which topics are in Paper 1 and which are in Paper 2?**
Neither paper has a fixed set of topics: any part of the specification may be assessed in either paper, so you need to prepare the whole course for both.

**Is there a practical exam in OxfordAQA IGCSE Physics?**
No. There is no separate practical exam: the grade comes entirely from the two written papers, so experimental work done in class is not marked on its own.

**Is OxfordAQA IGCSE Physics tiered?**
No. The qualification is not tiered, so every candidate sits the same two papers.

**Can I take OxfordAQA IGCSE Physics alongside other sciences?**
OxfordAQA applies a prohibited combinations rule across its International GCSE sciences, so check with your school which combinations are allowed before you are entered.
