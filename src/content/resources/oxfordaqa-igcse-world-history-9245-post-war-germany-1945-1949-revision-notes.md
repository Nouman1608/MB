## People's problems in 1945

- **Ruins:** cities bombed; homes destroyed; utilities broken.
- **Food:** low rations; trips to the countryside to barter; black market; cigarettes as currency.
- **Women:** outnumbered men; *Trümmerfrauen* cleared rubble.
- **Refugees and expellees:** millions fled the Red Army; then expulsions from lands east of the Oder-Neisse line, the Sudetenland and elsewhere; estimates commonly twelve million or more.
- **Forced labour:** millions of foreign forced labourers became displaced persons; German prisoners of war held by the USSR worked for years.

## Allied control

- Four zones (US, British, French, Soviet); Berlin in four sectors.
- Allied Control Council for Germany as a whole.
- Potsdam aims: demilitarise, denazify, democratise, decentralise.
- Zones drifted apart as the Cold War grew.

## Denazification and Nuremberg

| | Detail | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Denazification (west) | Questionnaires; tribunals; categories from major offenders to exonerated | Slow, inconsistent; many reinstated as the Cold War grew |
| Denazification (Soviet zone) | Removal of old elites; land reform | Used to reshape society along communist lines |
| Nuremberg Tribunal | November 1945-October 1946; Göring and others; crimes against peace, war crimes, crimes against humanity | Twelve death sentences, three acquittals; "following orders" no defence; Holocaust evidence made public |

## Penalties on the Soviet zone

- Reparations mainly from its own zone, plus a share of western equipment.
- Factories, railway lines and machinery dismantled and taken to the USSR; later, goods from current production.
- Military rule, violence by Soviet soldiers in 1945, special camps, the SED merger.
- Result: slower recovery than in the west.

## The Cold War's impact

- **Marshall Aid** for the western zones; refused for the Soviet zone.
- **Deutsche Mark** (June 1948) and Erhard's lifting of price controls: shops filled, black market declined.
- **Blockade** (June 1948-May 1949): about two million West Berliners supplied by air; rationing, power cuts, cold; Tegel runway built; Western powers seen as protectors.
- **Two states** (1949): Federal Republic (Basic Law, Bonn, Adenauer); GDR (SED one-party state).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zero hour | *Stunde Null*, the collapse of 1945 |
| Expellee | German forced to leave eastern lands after the war |
| Displaced person | Foreigner in Germany, often a former forced labourer, awaiting return |
| Black market | Illegal trade in scarce goods |
| *Trümmerfrauen* | "Rubble women" who cleared bomb damage |
| Denazification | Removing Nazi influence from public life |
| Reparations | Payments in money, goods or equipment for war damage |
| Dismantling | Removal of factories and equipment as reparations |
| SED | Socialist Unity Party, the ruling party of the GDR |
| Basic Law | The constitution of the Federal Republic (1949) |

## Exam traps

- Four zones, not two.
- Refugees fled; expellees were forced out after the war.
- The Blockade was the Soviet response to the new currency.
- Denazification was not complete: many former Nazis returned to posts.
- In a 9-mark answer, give two clearly separate ways, each explained.
- In a 12-mark answer, compare the given factor with at least one other and reach a judgement.

## Self-test

1. What did Germans call May 1945?
2. Who were the *Trümmerfrauen*?
3. Why were cigarettes used as currency?
4. Which conference approved the transfer of German populations?
5. Name two regions from which Germans were expelled.
6. What were displaced persons?
7. How many zones was Germany divided into?
8. Name two of the Potsdam aims for Germany.
9. What were the three main charges at Nuremberg?
10. How did the USSR take reparations from its zone?
11. What did the Deutsche Mark change in the western zones?
12. When were the Federal Republic and the GDR founded?

## Answers

1. *Stunde Null*, "zero hour".
2. Women who cleared the rubble of bombed cities.
3. The Reichsmark had little value, so goods and cigarettes were used instead.
4. Potsdam (July-August 1945).
5. Any two: lands east of the Oder-Neisse line (Silesia, Pomerania, East Prussia); the Sudetenland; Hungary.
6. Foreigners in Germany, mostly former forced labourers or prisoners, awaiting return or emigration.
7. Four.
8. Any two: demilitarisation; denazification; democratisation; decentralisation.
9. Crimes against peace, war crimes, crimes against humanity.
10. By dismantling factories, railway lines and machinery and, later, taking goods from production.
11. It ended the worthless Reichsmark, goods reappeared in shops and the black market declined.
12. The Federal Republic in May 1949 (Basic Law; government in September); the GDR in October 1949.
