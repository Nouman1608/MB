These notes condense subtopic **11.3 Blood vessels** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the named vessels that carry blood to and from the heart, lungs, liver and kidneys, and the structure of arteries, veins and capillaries in relation to blood pressure. The full explanations and the worked measurement example are in the [11.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood-vessels/). Then try the [11.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-blood-vessels-practice/).

## Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Artery | Vessel that carries blood away from the heart |
| Vein | Vessel that carries blood towards the heart |
| Capillary | Tiny vessel with a wall one cell thick, where substances are exchanged |
| Lumen | The space inside a vessel |

## Named vessels

| Organ | Blood in | Blood out |
|---|---|---|
| Heart | Vena cava, pulmonary vein | Aorta, pulmonary artery |
| Lungs | Pulmonary artery | Pulmonary vein |
| Liver | Hepatic artery, hepatic portal vein | Hepatic vein |
| Kidneys | Renal artery | Renal vein |

- Aorta: left ventricle to the body.
- Vena cava: body to the right atrium.
- Pulmonary artery: right ventricle to the lungs (deoxygenated).
- Pulmonary vein: lungs to the left atrium (oxygenated).
- Hepatic portal vein: gut to liver.

## Structure and function

| Feature | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | Thick | Thin | One cell thick |
| Muscle and elastic tissue | Much | Little | None |
| Lumen | Narrow | Wide | Very narrow |
| Valves | No | Yes | No |
| Pressure | High | Low | Low |
| Job | Carry blood from the heart under pressure | Return blood to the heart | Exchange substances with cells |

## Why the structure suits the pressure

- **Artery**: the thick wall withstands high pressure; the elastic tissue stretches and recoils to smooth the surges; the muscle can change the width.
- **Vein**: low pressure, so a thin wall is enough; a wide lumen reduces resistance; valves prevent backflow; body muscles squeeze the veins to push blood on.
- **Capillary**: a one-cell wall gives a short diffusion distance; a narrow lumen holds red cells close to the wall and slows flow for exchange; very many capillaries give a large surface area.

## Measuring vessel sections

actual size = image size divided by magnification.

At x50, an image wall of 12 mm is 0.24 mm; a lumen of 8 mm is 0.16 mm. The wall : lumen ratio is 12 : 8 = 1.5. A vein with a 3 mm wall and 14 mm lumen has a ratio of 0.21.

Recognising vessels on a section:

- Thick wall, small round lumen: artery.
- Thin wall, large irregular lumen: vein.
- Tube just wide enough for one red cell, wall of one cell: capillary.

## Short model answers

**Explain why arteries have thick walls.** Blood leaves the heart at high pressure. A thick wall with muscle and elastic tissue withstands the pressure and prevents the artery bursting.

**Explain why veins have valves.** The blood pressure in veins is low, so blood could flow backwards. The valves close to stop this and ensure that blood flows only towards the heart.

**Explain how capillaries are adapted for exchange.** Their walls are one cell thick, so the diffusion distance is short. They are very narrow, so blood flows slowly and has time for exchange, and there are very many of them, so the surface area is large.

**State which vessel brings blood from the intestine to the liver.** The hepatic portal vein.

## Exam traps

- Artery and vein describe the **direction**, not the oxygen content.
- Arteries have no valves (apart from at the start of the aorta and pulmonary artery, which belong to the heart).
- The liver has **two** inflow vessels.
- A vein's thin wall is due to **low pressure**, not weakness.
- Capillaries have walls; they are one cell thick.
- Always link the feature to the **pressure**.
- Use "lumen" for the space inside the vessel.

## Self-test

1. Name the vessel that carries blood from the left ventricle to the body.
2. Name the vessel that returns blood from the body to the right atrium.
3. Which vessel carries oxygenated blood from the lungs to the heart?
4. Name the vessel that carries blood from the gut to the liver.
5. Name the vessels that carry blood to and from a kidney.
6. Why do arteries have thick walls?
7. What is the function of elastic tissue in an artery wall?
8. Why do veins have valves?
9. Why is a capillary wall one cell thick?
10. Which vessel has the widest lumen compared to its wall: artery or vein?
11. At x40, a vessel wall in a photograph is 10 mm thick. What is its actual thickness?
12. Name the vessels that carry blood into the liver.

### Answers

1. The aorta.
2. The vena cava.
3. The pulmonary vein.
4. The hepatic portal vein.
5. Renal artery (to) and renal vein (from).
6. To withstand high pressure.
7. It stretches and recoils, smoothing out the surges of blood.
8. To stop blood flowing backwards at low pressure.
9. To give a short distance for diffusion.
10. Vein.
11. 10 divided by 40 = 0.25 mm.
12. The hepatic artery and the hepatic portal vein.
