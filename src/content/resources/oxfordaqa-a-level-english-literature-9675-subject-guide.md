OxfordAQA International AS and A-level English Literature (9675) is a
modular course built around four ways of reading literature: as dramatic
tragedy, through the idea of place, through the conventions of crime and
mystery, and through the way literature represents the world. It suits
students who enjoy reading widely and writing analytical essays. Two AS
papers are each worth 20% of the A-level, a compulsory A-level paper on
crime and mystery is worth 30%, and the final 30% comes from either an
exam on literary representations (Route A) or a two-essay non-exam
assessment (Route B). Every component is marked out of 50.

## How the course is organised

**Unit 1 – Aspects of Dramatic Tragedy (AS).** Section A is a
passage-based question on an Elizabethan or Jacobean tragedy, chosen from
Othello, King Lear, Hamlet, Doctor Faustus (B text) and The Duchess of
Malfi. Section B is an essay on a later dramatic tragedy, from Death of a
Salesman, A Streetcar Named Desire, Hedda Gabler, Waiting for Godot and
Translations.

**Unit 2 – Place in Literary Texts (AS).** Section A is on prose, from
Heart of Darkness, The Great Gatsby, Americanah, Small Island, Last Man in
Tower and Remains of the Day. Section B is on poetry, from selections of
Wordsworth, Robert Frost, Thomas Hardy or Seamus Heaney.

**Unit 3 – Elements of Crime and Mystery (A-level).** The compulsory
second-year unit studies how texts use crime and mystery.

**Unit 4 – Literary Representations (A-level).** The final unit is
assessed either through unseen prose and poetry in an exam or through
coursework essays, depending on the route.

Your school chooses which set texts you study from each list. The text
lists above are those in the current specification; the board is revising
the specification for first teaching from 2027 with a refreshed set-text
list, so check with your teacher which list applies to you.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting (A-level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AS Paper 1 | Aspects of Dramatic Tragedy (closed-book) | 120 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| AS Paper 2 | Place in Literary Texts (open-book) | 120 minutes | 50 | 20% |
| A-level Paper 1 | Elements of Crime and Mystery (closed-book) | 120 minutes | 50 | 30% |
| Route A: A-level Paper 2 | Literary Representations (unseen prose and poetry) | 150 minutes | 50 | 30% |
| Route B: NEA | Two essays, one prose and one poetry, 1,250-1,500 words each | Not timed | 50 | 30% |

Each student follows one route for the final unit. Route A is an
externally assessed exam; Route B is marked by your teacher and moderated
externally. The course is modular, so the AS papers can be taken at the
end of the first year and lead to an International AS on their own. The
board's page states that the revision for 2027 leaves the paper
structure, timings, marks and weightings unchanged. This assessment
structure was first taught in September 2017, with first AS exams in
May/June 2018 and first A-level exams in May/June 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The closed-book and open-book distinction is one of the most important
things to understand early. AS Paper 1 and A-level Paper 1 are
closed-book, so you need secure knowledge of your texts, including
quotations, without the books in front of you. AS Paper 2 is open-book,
which shifts the demand from memory towards precise selection and close
analysis of the text you have with you.

The units also build in scope. Unit 1 develops close reading of drama,
including passage-based analysis that trains you to connect a short
extract to the whole play. Unit 2 brings in prose and poetry and asks you
to trace a single idea, place, across a text. Unit 3 does something
similar with crime and mystery, a set of conventions rather than a single
theme. Unit 4 then tests whether you can apply those reading skills to
unseen texts on Route A, or sustain an independent argument in extended
essays on Route B.

Across every unit, the same habits are rewarded: a clear argument,
evidence chosen carefully, and attention to how a writer's methods create
meaning.

## Planning your study

Read your set texts before they are taught, if possible, and read them
again during teaching. In the first year, the closed-book tragedy paper
needs a steady build-up of quotations and textual detail, so keep a
running quotation bank for each play from the first week. For the
open-book place unit, practise finding and using evidence quickly rather
than memorising it.

In the second year, crime and mystery texts benefit from the same
closed-book preparation. If your school follows Route B, agree a timeline
for planning, drafting and finishing the two essays early, so the
coursework does not crowd out revision. If you follow Route A, read
unseen prose and poetry regularly, ideally every week.

Start practice questions on each unit once the texts have been taught,
and move to full timed papers before each exam series. The course's
exam-preparation page goes into closed-book, open-book and route
decisions in more depth, so use it for paper strategy.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which OxfordAQA A-level English Literature 9675 papers are closed-book?**
AS Paper 1, Aspects of Dramatic Tragedy, and A-level Paper 1, Elements of Crime and Mystery, are closed-book. AS Paper 2, Place in Literary Texts, is open-book.

**What does the Route B non-exam assessment involve?**
Two essays, one on prose and one on poetry, of 1,250-1,500 words each. It is worth 50 marks and 30% of the A-level, is marked by your teacher and is moderated externally.

**What is Route A for the final unit?**
A-level Paper 2, Literary Representations, a 150-minute exam on unseen prose and poetry worth 50 marks and 30% of the A-level.

**Is the 9675 specification changing?**
The board is revising it for first teaching from 2027 with a refreshed set-text list. Its page states that the paper structure, timings, marks and weightings are unchanged.

**How are the AS papers weighted?**
Each AS paper is 120 minutes long, worth 50 marks and 20% of the A-level.
