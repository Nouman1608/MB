## Questions

**1.** Define induction training. **[2]**

**2.** State the type of training in each situation (induction, on-the-job or off-the-job).

**(a)** A new warehouse operative is shown the fire exits and introduced to the team on day one. **[1]**
**(b)** A bakery assistant learns to use the oven by working beside the supervisor. **[1]**
**(c)** A cashier spends two days on a customer-service course at a local college. **[1]**

**3.** Tamsin's Cafe sends 5 employees on a food hygiene course. The fee is 180 per employee. Each employee spends one day on the course and the cafe still pays a day's wages of 70 per employee.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of the training to the cafe. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the cost per employee. **[1]**

**4.** Explain one benefit of training to a worker. **[3]**

**5.** Explain how training can help a business to comply with health and safety laws. **[3]**

**6.** Explain one limitation of training for a business. **[3]**

**7.** **(a)** Give the highest level in Maslow's hierarchy of needs. **[1]**
**(b)** Outline how a business could meet the social needs of its employees. **[2]**

**8.** Windlow Logistics has 40 staff. A survey finds they are unhappy with the poor condition of the staff room, unclear company rules, and a lack of recognition for good work. Pay is seen as fair.

**(a)** State two hygiene factors from the survey results. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why improving hygiene factors alone may not raise motivation. **[3]**

**9.** A salesperson at Dunmore Motors earns a basic pay of 1,250 a month plus 3% commission on sales. A bonus of 200 is paid if monthly sales exceed 50,000. Sales in March were 52,000.

**(a)** Calculate the commission earned in March. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate total pay for March including the bonus. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate commission as a percentage of pay before the bonus, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**10.** Fenwick Print has 20 workers. Before a change they made 7,000 units a month. The manager then used job enrichment: operators now set up their own machines and check their own quality. Output rose to 7,700 units with the same 20 workers.

**(a)** Calculate output per worker before and after the change. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in output per worker. **[2]**
**(c)** Analyse how job enrichment may have raised productivity at Fenwick Print. **[4]**

**11.** Explain one weakness of Taylor's approach to motivation. **[3]**

**12.** Brindle Electrical has 32 employees and a yearly wage bill of 540,000. Eight employees left last year. A survey says staff find the work repetitive and have little say over how they organise it. Pay is described as fair. The owner is choosing between:

Option A: a bonus of 4% of the wage bill, paid only if a profit target is met.
Option B: a one-off 5,400 on training so that staff can use job rotation and have more autonomy.

Justify which option the owner should choose. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Training given to a new employee at the start of a job [1] to introduce the business, its rules and safety procedures [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition tends to need what the training is and when or why it happens; the word "training" alone repeats the question.

**2.** **(a)** **Induction** [1]. **(b)** **On-the-job** [1]. **(c)** **Off-the-job** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Take the clue from where the learning happens (at work, away from work, or at the start); no explanation is asked for.

**3.** **(a)** Fees: 5 × 180 = 900 [1]. Wages for the day: 5 × 70 = 350 [1]. Total = 900 + 350 = **1,250** [1].
**(b)** 1,250 ÷ 5 = **250** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show fees and wages separately. Leaving out the wages is the usual slip, and working lets a reader see which parts are right.

**4.** Training gives the worker new skills [1], which can make them eligible for promotion or a better paid job [1], so their career prospects and income improve [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An "explain" answer is best built as a fact plus two linked expansions, so one fact with no consequence is thin.

**5.** Training can teach staff safe use of equipment and emergency procedures [1], so accidents are less likely [1], which helps the business show that it meets its legal duty to protect people at work [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to the general legal duty only; naming a specific law is not needed and may introduce errors.

**6.** Training costs money for fees and trainers [1], and employees are away from work so output is lost [1], so the business may be worse off in the short term if the benefits are slow to appear [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Limitations gain from being linked to cost, time or staff leaving, not just stated.

**7.** **(a)** **Self-actualisation** [1].
**(b)** Provide team activities or social events [1], so employees build friendships and feel part of a group [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Outline" asks for two linked points, so give the method and what it achieves.

**8.** **(a)** Poor condition of the staff room (working conditions) [1] and unclear company rules (company policies) [1].
**(b)** Hygiene factors prevent dissatisfaction [1], but they do not by themselves give people a reason to work harder [1], so motivators such as recognition and responsibility are also needed [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In part (a) choose from the survey, not from your own list; recognition is a motivator, so it is not an answer here.

**9.** **(a)** 3% × 52,000 [1] = **1,560** [1].
**(b)** 1,250 + 1,560 + 200 = **3,010** [1].
**(c)** Pay before bonus = 1,250 + 1,560 = 2,810 [1]. 1,560 ÷ 2,810 × 100 = **55.5%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Read the base carefully: part (c) excludes the bonus. Show the calculation before the answer.

**10.** **(a)** Before: 7,000 ÷ 20 = 350 [1]. After: 7,700 ÷ 20 = 385 [1].
**(b)** (385 − 350) ÷ 350 × 100 [1] = **10%** [1].
**(c)** Operators carry out more demanding tasks [1], which can give them a sense of responsibility and achievement [1], so they may work more carefully and fix problems themselves [1], reducing delays and improving output per worker at Fenwick Print [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Analyse" is placed in context, so use the set-up and quality checks from the stem, and carry the chain through to productivity.

**11.** Taylor assumes money is the main motivator and that tasks are best split into simple steps [1]. Many workers also want interest, recognition and a say in their work [1], so simple repetitive tasks and pay for output may not motivate them and may harm quality [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer states the theory's assumption first and then shows what it overlooks.

**12.** Choose **Option B** [1]. Staff say the work is repetitive and they have little say, so job rotation and autonomy target the problem identified [1]. Option A costs 4% × 540,000 = 21,600 [1] (four times the 5,400 for Option B), and pay is already seen as fair, so extra pay is unlikely to address the cause [1]. Option B also helps retention, as 8 of 32 staff (25%) left last year [1]. Limitation of B: training costs 5,400 and autonomy could lead to mistakes or weaker control [1]. Limitation of A: the bonus is paid only if the target is met, so some staff may feel it is out of reach and be demotivated [1]. Judgement: B is better value and fits the survey, although the owner should monitor quality and could add a small bonus later [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "Justify" wants one option recommended using information supplied, so use the survey and figures, weigh the alternative and finish with a decision.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Stating a fact without the two expansions asked for in an "explain" question.
- Leaving out wages for time away from work when costing training.
- Mixing up hygiene factors and motivators.
- Treating bonus and commission as the same thing.
- Using the wrong base for a percentage.
- Describing a method without saying what it does to output, retention or quality.
- Ignoring the survey data in a case study.
- Failing to weigh the other option before deciding.
