## Section A

**1.** Classify each amine as primary, secondary or tertiary.

**(a)** (CH₃)₃N **[1]**

**(b)** CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃ **[1]**

**(c)** CH₃CH₂NHCH₃ **[1]**

**2.** State the reagent used to reduce butanamide to butylamine. **[1]**

**3.** 1-bromobutane is reacted with methylamine. State the conditions and name the organic
product. **[2]**

**4.** Write the equation for the reaction of methylamine with water. **[1]**

**5.** State why the reaction of an amine with an acyl chloride is described as a condensation.
**[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Butylamine, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂, can be made from 1-bromobutane in one step, or from
1-bromopropane in two steps.

**(a)** State the reagents and conditions for the one-step route. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the reagents and conditions for each step of the two-step route, naming the
intermediate. **[3]**

**(c)** Give one disadvantage of the one-step route. **[1]**

**7.** Pure diethylamine, (CH₃CH₂)₂NH, is to be made from ethanoyl chloride and ethylamine.

**(a)** Write the equation for step 1. **[1]**

**(b)** State the reagent for step 2 and write an equation for it, using [H]. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this route gives a purer product than heating bromoethane with ethylamine.
**[2]**

**8.** Aqueous solutions of ammonia, methylamine and dimethylamine of equal concentration are
compared.

**(a)** Place them in order of increasing pH. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain this order. **[3]**

**(c)** Write the equation for the reaction of dimethylamine with water. **[1]**

**9.** Bromoethane is heated with a small amount of ammonia in ethanol in a sealed tube. Several
nitrogen-containing products form.

**(a)** Give the formulae of three products formed in turn after ethylamine. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why ethylamine is able to react with bromoethane. **[2]**

**10.** 4.625 g of 1-chlorobutane, C₄H₉Cl, is heated with excess ammonia in ethanol under
pressure. 2.92 g of butylamine, C₄H₉NH₂, is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**11.** Compound **Z**, C₃H₉N, is a secondary amine.

**(a)** Give the structural formula of **Z**. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the structural formula of an amide that could be reduced by LiAlH₄ to form **Z**.
**[1]**

**(c)** Name a halogenoalkane and a primary amine that could react to form **Z**. **[1]**

**12.** Ethylamine is a volatile liquid with a fishy smell. It dissolves in dilute hydrochloric
acid, and the smell disappears.

**(a)** Write the equation for the reaction with hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Write the equation for the reaction that occurs when excess NaOH(aq) is then added, and
state what would be noticed. **[2]**

**13.** A three-step route starts from 1-bromopropane:

CH₃CH₂CH₂Br → (KCN in ethanol, heat) → **P** → (H₂, Ni) → **Q** → (CH₃COCl, room temperature) → **R**

**(a)** Give the structural formulae of **P**, **Q** and **R**. **[3]**

**(b)** State the type of reaction in the third step and name the inorganic by-product. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Tertiary [1].

**(b)** Primary (only one carbon group on N) [1].

**(c)** Secondary [1].

**2.** LiAlH₄ [1].

**3.** Heat in ethanol in a sealed tube / under pressure [1]; N-methylbutylamine,
CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NHCH₃ [1].

**4.** CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻ [1].

**5.** Two molecules join with the loss of a small molecule, HCl [1].

**6. (a)** Excess ammonia in ethanol [1]; heated under pressure / in a sealed tube [1].

**(b)** Step 1: KCN in ethanol, heat [1], giving butanenitrile, CH₃CH₂CH₂CN [1]; step 2: LiAlH₄,
or H₂ with a Ni catalyst [1].

**(c)** Further substitution gives a mixture (secondary and tertiary amines, quaternary salt), so the
yield of primary amine is reduced and separation is needed [1].

**7. (a)** CH₃COCl + CH₃CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ + HCl [1].

**(b)** LiAlH₄ [1]; CH₃CONHCH₂CH₃ + 4[H] → (CH₃CH₂)₂NH + H₂O [1].

**(c)** With bromoethane, diethylamine can react further to give triethylamine (and a quaternary
salt) [1]; the amide route forms only one amide, and reduction gives only one amine [1].

**8. (a)** Ammonia < methylamine < dimethylamine [1].

**(b)** Each base accepts H⁺ using the lone pair on nitrogen [1]; methyl groups are
electron-donating (positive inductive effect), increasing electron density on N [1]; so the lone
pair is more available, more OH⁻ forms, and two methyl groups have a bigger effect than one [1].

**(c)** (CH₃)₂NH + H₂O ⇌ (CH₃)₂NH₂⁺ + OH⁻ [1].

**9. (a)** (CH₃CH₂)₂NH [1]; (CH₃CH₂)₃N [1]; (CH₃CH₂)₄N⁺Br⁻ [1].

**(b)** Ethylamine still has a lone pair on nitrogen, so it is a nucleophile [1]; the ethyl group
makes this lone pair more available than in ammonia, so it competes for the bromoethane when ammonia
is not in excess [1].

**10.** Mᵣ(C₄H₉Cl) = 92.5, so n = 4.625 / 92.5 = 0.0500 mol [1]; Mᵣ(C₄H₉NH₂) = 73.0, so
theoretical mass = 0.0500 × 73.0 = 3.65 g [1]; yield = 2.92 / 3.65 × 100 = 80.0% [1].

**11. (a)** CH₃NHCH₂CH₃ [1].

**(b)** CH₃CONHCH₃ (N-methylethanamide); accept HCONHCH₂CH₃ [1].

**(c)** Chloroethane or bromoethane with methylamine; or chloromethane or bromomethane with
ethylamine [1].

**12. (a)** CH₃CH₂NH₂ + HCl → CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1].

**(b)** CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂NH₂ + NaCl + H₂O [1]; the fishy smell returns (the free
amine is released) [1].

**13. (a)** **P**: CH₃CH₂CH₂CN (butanenitrile) [1]; **Q**: CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂NH₂ (butylamine) [1];
**R**: CH₃CONHCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ (N-butylethanamide) [1].

**(b)** Condensation (accept nucleophilic addition–elimination) [1]; HCl (or butylammonium
chloride if the amine is in excess) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "ethanol" or "under pressure / sealed tube" from halogenoalkane routes.
- Forgetting that the nitrile route adds one carbon, so the wrong halogenoalkane is chosen.
- Using H₂/Ni to reduce an amide; the syllabus reagent is LiAlH₄.
- Classifying CH₃CH(NH₂)CH₃ by its carbon rather than its nitrogen.
- Explaining basicity without linking lone-pair availability to the concentration of OH⁻.
- Writing quaternary ammonium products without the counter-ion.
- Treating the amine as a base in one part of a question and forgetting, in the next part, that the
  same lone pair makes it a nucleophile towards halogenoalkanes and acyl chlorides.
