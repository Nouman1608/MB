## Questions

**1.** Explain why donor stem cells may be rejected by the person who receives them. **[3]**

**2.** Describe two ways of reducing the chance that transplanted cells are rejected. **[2]**

**3.** A hospital treats 150 patients with donor stem cells from umbilical cord blood. In 36 patients, donor white blood cells attack the patient's own tissues.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of patients affected. **[2]**
**(b)** Name this condition and explain why it happens. **[2]**

**4.** Therapeutic cloning can produce embryonic stem cells for a patient.

**(a)** Describe how the cells are produced. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why these cells should not be rejected by the patient. **[2]**

**5.** A surgeon records rejection of skin grafts. Without a drug, 14 of 80 grafts were rejected. With an immunosuppressant, 6 of 75 grafts were rejected.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage rejected in each group. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate the use of the immunosuppressant. **[3]**

**6.** Human insulin can be made by bacteria.

**(a)** Describe how bacteria are made to produce human insulin. **[3]**
**(b)** Give one benefit of this method compared with extracting insulin from animal pancreases. **[1]**

**7.** In a gene therapy trial for a blood disorder, 25 patients are treated. After one year, 18 have improved and 3 have a serious complication.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage who improved and the percentage with a serious complication. **[2]**
**(b)** The working allele was delivered by a modified virus. State the name for this carrier and give one risk of using it. **[2]**
**(c)** Discuss whether this therapy should be offered to a child with a fatal form of the disorder. **[2]**

**8.** A scientist proposes gene therapy on early embryos.

**(a)** Explain why this raises different ethical issues from gene therapy on a patient's body cells. **[2]**
**(b)** Give one other issue, not about inheritance, that affects who can use gene therapy. **[2]**

**9.** In a country, 9% of people develop disease P in their lifetime. Of people with allele B, 45% do. In a town of 50 000 people, 1 in 250 carry allele B.

**(a)** Calculate the number of carriers in the town. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many carriers are expected to develop disease P. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate how many extra cases this is compared with carriers who had the average risk. **[2]**
**(d)** A carrier says, "So I will definitely get disease P." Explain why this is wrong. **[2]**

**10.** A drug is tested on 400 patients. 150 have a genetic marker and 250 do not. 90 patients with the marker respond to the drug. 20 patients without the marker respond.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of patients who respond in each group, and overall. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how these results support testing patients before prescribing the drug. **[2]**

**11.** A health service is considering sequencing the genome of every adult to predict disease risk.

**(a)** Discuss the benefits and problems of this plan and give a conclusion. **[6]**
**(b)** Suggest two pieces of information that would help the health service decide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Donor cells have antigens on their surface that differ from the recipient's [1]. The recipient's immune system (lymphocytes) recognises them as foreign [1]. It attacks and destroys the donor cells [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer that says only "the body does not like it" gains little credit. Name the surface antigens, the recognition as foreign and the attack in order.

**2.** Match the tissue type of donor and recipient, for example by using a close relative [1]; give immunosuppressant drugs [1]. Other valid methods: use the patient's own stem cells, or use therapeutic cloning. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Two different methods are needed. Saying "match it" and then "choose a similar donor" is one idea written twice.

**3.** **(a)** 36 ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **24%** [1]
**(b)** Graft-versus-host disease [1]. The donor white blood cells recognise the patient's tissues as foreign and attack them [1]. **[4 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), say that the donor cells do the attacking. Describing rejection of the donor cells by the patient is the opposite direction and answers a different question.

**4.** **(a)** The nucleus is taken from one of the patient's body cells [1]. It is put into an egg cell whose nucleus has been removed [1]. The cell is stimulated to divide and form an early embryo, from which embryonic stem cells are collected [1].
**(b)** The cells are genetically identical to the patient, so they carry the patient's own antigens [1]. The immune system therefore does not treat them as foreign [1]. **[5 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* Link "genetically identical" to "same antigens" before stating the outcome. Stopping at "identical" leaves the reasoning incomplete.

**5.** **(a)** Without the drug: 14 ÷ 80 × 100 [1] = 17.5% [1]. With the drug: 6 ÷ 75 × 100 = 8% [1].
**(b)** The drug cuts rejection from 17.5% to 8%, which is a large reduction [1]. However, immunosuppressants weaken the immune system, so patients are at higher risk of infection [1]. Because the groups differ in size and the trial is small, a larger trial is needed before concluding the drug is safe and effective [1]. **[6 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs a figure from the data, a drawback and a limit on the evidence. A bare "it works" will not cover all of those.

**6.** **(a)** The human insulin gene is identified and cut out [1]. It is inserted into bacteria, for example into a plasmid [1]. The bacteria are grown in large fermenters, make insulin, and the insulin is collected and purified [1].
**(b)** Any one of: larger, more reliable supply; no need for animal pancreases; the insulin is identical to human insulin [1]. **[4 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention where the gene ends up (the bacteria) and what happens next (growth and collection). Many answers give only the first step.

**7.** **(a)** Improved: 18 ÷ 25 × 100 = 72% [1]. Serious complication: 3 ÷ 25 × 100 = 12% [1].
**(b)** The carrier is a vector [1]. A risk: it may insert the allele in the wrong place and cause cancer, or trigger an immune response [1].
**(c)** Benefit: the therapy helped most patients, and without treatment the child faces a fatal outcome [1]. Risk: 12% had a serious complication and long-term effects are unknown, but where the disorder is fatal and no other treatment works, the risk may be acceptable [1]. **[6 in total across (a) to (c)]**
*Examiner insight:* A "discuss" answer is stronger when it uses the supplied percentages and reaches a reasoned view. It should not stop at listing points.

**8.** **(a)** Changes in embryo cells would be passed on to the person's children and later generations [1]. Those future people cannot consent to the change, whereas an adult patient can [1].
**(b)** Gene therapy is very expensive [1]. So only wealthy patients or well-funded health services could use it, which raises a fairness issue [1]. **[4 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* Each ethical point needs its reason. "It is unfair" gains less than saying who is left out and why.

**9.** **(a)** 50 000 ÷ 250 = **200** carriers [1]
**(b)** 45% of 200 = **90** [1]
**(c)** At the average risk, 9% of 200 = 18 [1]. Extra cases = 90 − 18 = **72** [1]
**(d)** The risk is 45%, so 55% of carriers do not develop the disease [1]. A genetic risk is a probability, and other factors such as environment and lifestyle also affect whether it develops [1]. **[6 in total across (a) to (d)]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each step in order, writing the carrier count before using it. Clear working makes your reasoning easy to follow and helps you spot slips.

**10.** **(a)** With marker: 90 ÷ 150 × 100 = 60% [1]. Without marker: 20 ÷ 250 × 100 = 8% [1]. Overall: 110 ÷ 400 × 100 = 27.5% [1].
**(b)** The drug works for far more patients with the marker (60%) than without it (8%) [1]. Testing would let doctors give the drug to those likely to respond, and avoid side effects and cost for those unlikely to benefit [1]. **[5 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote both group figures and compare them. The overall figure on its own hides the difference between the groups.

**11.** **(a)** Benefit: finds raised risks early, so screening or lifestyle changes can start [1]. Benefit: results can guide drugs targeted to the patient's genome, or help relatives learn their own risk [1]. Problem: results give risks, not certainties, so people may be worried unnecessarily or falsely reassured [1]. Problem: privacy, and misuse of data by insurers or employers [1]. Further valid points: cost and counselling needs, people not wanting to know. A balanced conclusion [1], for example that the plan should go ahead only with protected data and the choice to opt out, with a reason [1].
**(b)** The cost compared with the number of cases prevented [1]. How reliably the tests predict disease [1]. Other valid points: how many people would accept testing, and whether laws can protect the data. **[8 in total across (a) and (b)]**
*Examiner insight:* Six-mark "discuss" answers usually gain credit for balance and a reasoned conclusion. Keep points distinct, because repeating the same point in new words adds nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "rejected" without antigens, recognition as foreign and attack.
- Mixing up rejection with graft-versus-host disease.
- Calling immunosuppressants harmless, with no mention of infection.
- Describing insulin production without saying the gene goes into bacteria.
- Treating a genetic risk as a certainty.
- Giving one side only in a "discuss" or "evaluate" answer.
- Quoting percentages without naming the group they apply to.
- Stating an ethical view with no reason behind it.
