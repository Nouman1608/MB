These questions cover sub-topic 4(c), Work and power, of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics 4PH1. Take
g = 10 N/kg throughout. Related: [Work and power revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-work-and-power-revision-notes/)

## Section A

**1.** State the relationship between work done, force and distance moved. **[1]**

**2.** State what is meant by power. **[1]**

**3.** A force of 35 N moves a box 4.0 m in the direction of the force. Calculate the work done. **[2]**

**4.** A weightlifter holds a bar still above her head for 3 seconds. State the work done on the bar during this time and explain your answer. **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the kinetic energy of a 3.0 kg ball moving at 4.0 m/s. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A crane lifts a 450 kg load through a vertical height of 18 m in 30 s at a steady speed.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy gained by the load. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the useful power output of the crane. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the electrical input power of the crane's motor is greater than your answer to (b). **[2]**

**7.** A diver of mass 55 kg steps off a diving board 10 m above the water.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy the diver loses in falling to the water. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the diver's speed as she enters the water, ignoring air resistance. **[3]**
**(c)** State why her real speed is slightly lower. **[1]**

**8.** A skateboarder of total mass 50 kg moves at 7.0 m/s at the bottom of a curved ramp and rolls up it.

**(a)** Calculate the skateboarder's kinetic energy at the bottom of the ramp. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the greatest height the skateboarder could reach if there were no friction or air resistance. **[2]**
**(c)** The skateboarder actually reaches a height of 2.2 m. Calculate the work done against friction and air resistance. **[2]**

**9.** A car of mass 1200 kg travels at 25 m/s on a level road.

**(a)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the car. **[2]**
**(b)** The car brakes and stops in 50 m. Calculate the average braking force. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain what would happen to the braking distance if the car were travelling at twice the speed with the same braking force. **[2]**

**10.** A student wants to find her useful power as she runs up a flight of stairs.

**(a)** Describe the measurements she should take and the equipment she should use. **[3]**
**(b)** Her mass is 52 kg. There are 18 steps, each 0.17 m high, and she takes 4.2 s. Calculate her useful power. **[3]**

**11.** Motor A lifts a 20 kg load through 3.0 m in 4.0 s. Motor B lifts a 30 kg load through 2.0 m in 5.0 s. Determine which motor has the greater useful power output. **[4]**

**12.** A pendulum bob of mass 0.20 kg is pulled aside so that it is 0.45 m higher than its lowest point, then released.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the bob as it passes through its lowest point, ignoring air resistance. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why the mass of the bob does not affect this speed. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Work done = force × distance moved in the direction of the force (W = F × d) [1].

**2.** The rate of doing work, or the energy transferred per second [1].

**3.** W = F × d = 35 × 4.0 [1] = 140 J [1].

**4.** Zero (no work is done on the bar) [1], because the bar does not move, so there is no distance moved in the direction of the force [1].

**5.** KE = ½ × m × v² = 0.5 × 3.0 × 4.0² [1] = 24 J [1].

**6. (a)** GPE = m × g × h = 450 × 10 × 18 [1] = 81 000 J [1].
**(b)** P = W ÷ t = 81 000 ÷ 30 [1] = 2700 W [1].
**(c)** The motor is not 100% efficient [1]: some of the input energy is wasted, for example by heating the motor and cables through friction and electrical resistance, and as sound [1].

**7. (a)** GPE = 55 × 10 × 10 [1] = 5500 J [1].
**(b)** KE gained = GPE lost, so ½ × 55 × v² = 5500 [1]; v² = 5500 ÷ 27.5 = 200 [1]; v = 14 m/s (14.1 m/s) [1].
**(c)** Some energy is transferred by work done against air resistance to the thermal store of the surroundings, so less becomes kinetic energy [1].

**8. (a)** KE = 0.5 × 50 × 7.0² [1] = 1225 J (1200 J to 2 s.f.) [1].
**(b)** m × g × h = 1225, so h = 1225 ÷ (50 × 10) [1] = 2.45 m (2.5 m to 2 s.f.) [1].
**(c)** GPE at 2.2 m = 50 × 10 × 2.2 = 1100 J [1]; work done against friction = 1225 − 1100 = 125 J [1].

**9. (a)** KE = 0.5 × 1200 × 25² [1] = 375 000 J [1].
**(b)** F × d = KE, so F = 375 000 ÷ 50 [1] = 7500 N [1].
**(c)** Kinetic energy depends on speed squared, so doubling the speed makes the kinetic energy four times larger [1]. With the same braking force, the braking distance is also four times larger (200 m) [1].

**10. (a)** Measure her mass with bathroom scales [1]. Measure the vertical height of the stairs with a tape measure or metre rule (for example the height of one step × the number of steps) [1]. Time the run with a stopwatch [1].
**(b)** h = 18 × 0.17 = 3.06 m [1]; work done = m × g × h = 52 × 10 × 3.06 = 1591 J [1]; P = 1591 ÷ 4.2 = 380 W (2 s.f.; 379 W accepted) [1].

**11.** Motor A: work = 20 × 10 × 3.0 = 600 J [1]; power = 600 ÷ 4.0 = 150 W [1]. Motor B: work = 30 × 10 × 2.0 = 600 J, power = 600 ÷ 5.0 = 120 W [1]. Motor A has the greater power: both do the same work, but A does it in less time [1].

**12. (a)** GPE lost = KE gained, so m × g × h = ½ × m × v² [1]; v² = 2 × 10 × 0.45 = 9.0 [1]; v = 3.0 m/s [1].
**(b)** Mass appears on both sides of m × g × h = ½ × m × v², so it cancels [1].

## Frequently asked questions

**Why do some answers give two roundings?**

working is shown too so you can check your arithmetic.
