This page is a guide to AQA A Level Computer Science (7517) past papers for June 2024 and June 2025. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and a short account of what examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-level Computer Science (7517) specification page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/computer-science/a-level/computer-science-7517)
- [AQA A-level Computer Science (7517) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/computer-science/a-level/computer-science-7517/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade boundaries archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. The notes below are a quick way into that report. For Paper 1, remember that the skeleton program is part of the exam, so practise on the preliminary material for the year as well as on the written questions.

Check which specification a paper was set on. A-level Computer Science (7517) is linear and untiered, for first teaching from September 2015 and first assessed at A-level in June 2017. Both series covered here were set on that specification. Check older papers against the specification version before relying on them.

### Exam structure

| Component | Title | Time | Raw marks | Weighting |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 7517/1 | Paper 1 | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 | 40% |
| 7517/2 | Paper 2 | 2 hours 30 minutes | 100 | 40% |
| 7517/C | Non-exam assessment: the computing practical project | not timed | 75 | 20% |

Paper 1 is an on-screen exam covering programming and theory, and Paper 2 is a written exam. The project is marked by the centre and moderated by AQA. Raw marks on the two papers are scaled by 1.5 before they are added to the project mark, giving the 375 marks used for the whole-qualification thresholds.

## June 2025

Paper 2 ranged from storage and networking to number representation and assembly language. Examiners found the calculation parts mostly secure and the explanation parts imprecise: students often knew the right idea but gave a label instead of a description, or missed the unit conversion. No usable report was available to us for Paper 1. The document that AQA's page lists under that name is the report for the AS paper 7516/2, so we have not summarised it as A-level Paper 1.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 2 (7517/2), systems and data: naming an operating-system function instead of describing what it does; calling the types of software by the wrong names, with libraries and utilities least known; describing sampling without saying a sample measures the amplitude of a wave at a point in time; explaining the Nyquist idea without saying what the sampling rate must exceed; saying MIDI is smaller without explaining why.
- Paper 2, storage, networks and databases: optical storage terminology and the need for a sensor; imprecise reasons such as "too slow"; writing about client-server features in general rather than the school setting; INSERT statements missing keywords or brackets; a query that left out a table or used the wrong comparison; lost-update problems described as "data being lost".
- Paper 2, processor and numbers: writing about the von Neumann architecture when the stored program concept was asked for; the status register role; counting addressable registers and two's-complement ranges; explaining why floating-point numbers are normalised; partial application and first-class functions; applying De Morgan's laws too early in a simplification.
- Paper 2, extended networking answer: IPv6 said to hold more addresses because it uses hexadecimal; public and private addresses treated as security measures; NAT and DHCP described only in outline.
- Project (7517/C): analysis that did not scope the system in detail, initial modelling copied from a template, designs that skipped the core processing, borrowed or generated code not clearly separated from the student's own, screenshots of code, and evaluations that were a met/not-met tick list.

**What went well:** completing truth tables, the sample-resolution calculation, describing the difficulty of legislating for computer use, the purpose of compression, and recognising the type of a function. Testing evidence in video form, with commentary, was generally strong in the projects.

## June 2024

Paper 1 combined theory (subroutines, queues, the halting problem, regular expressions, trees) with programming questions on the skeleton program. Paper 2 covered security, data representation, hardware, networking, databases, ethics, assembly and functional programming.

**Where marks were lost**

- Paper 1 (7517/1): listing advantages of subroutines without explaining how each is achieved; leaving out the empty-queue check when removing from a circular queue; fewer than half of students could describe the halting problem, and non-computable and intractable were confused; the empty set missed as a subset; brackets dropped from a repeated group in a regular expression; trees and stack-based traversal only partly understood; exception handling explained superficially.
- Paper 1, programming: validation that allowed zero or omitted the lower bound; a number classification that mishandled repeated digits; averages that assumed exactly two items; output printed too early. Two later questions were left blank by many students even though simple marks were available for creating the method or class.
- Paper 2 (7517/2): virus questions answered with detection tools and little detail; image-size working that got the colour depth wrong; the memory-size calculation using the wrong exponent; the role of an I/O controller described too vaguely; the D-type flip-flop treated as a toggle; domain names confused with the domain name system; port forwarding and NAT without mention of address and port translation; INSERT and UPDATE statements with wrong syntax; timestamp ordering poorly known; imperative programming defined as the opposite.
- Project (7517/C): projects not scoped in detail, initial modelling missing or one-size-fits-all, designs that did not show how the main processing works, code taken from tutorials but credited as the student's own, and line-by-line "technical solution" sections that earned nothing.

**What went well:** logic circuit drawing, trace tables, extended answers on ethics, floating-point conversions, comparing parallel and serial transmission, SSD advantages and the first programming question, which asked for a validation routine.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find the June 2025 7517 papers and mark schemes?**
On AQA's assessment-resources page for 7517, linked above. Question papers, mark schemes and reports are listed by series.

**Why do the whole-qualification and paper thresholds use different totals?**
The two written papers are marked out of 100 each and scaled to 150, and the project is out of 75, giving 375 in total. The whole-qualification grade is awarded on the 375 total.

**Are the grade thresholds the same every year?**
No. AQA sets them after each series. Compare the two tables above: an A grade needed 245 in June 2025 and 239 in June 2024. Do not assume a future series will match.

**Is there an examiner report for the project?**
Yes. AQA lists a report on the examination for 7517/C for both 2024 and 2025, and the notes above summarise them.

**Why are there no June 2025 notes for Paper 1?**
The report AQA lists for 7517/1 in June 2025 turned out to be for the AS paper, so we left it out rather than mislabel it.

## About these notes

Grade thresholds on this page are copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents. The examiner notes are our own summaries of AQA's reports on the examination and are not a substitute for reading them. Thresholds change every series, and these figures must not be used to predict a future series. If you spot a mistake, please [report a correction](/report-a-correction/).
