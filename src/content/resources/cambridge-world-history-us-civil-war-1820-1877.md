**Key question 1.4 asks why the United States went to war with itself
in 1861 and whether the war changed anything.** Cambridge sets the
content as the causes and consequences of the Civil War from 1820 to
1877: differences between North and South, slavery and abolitionism,
the 1860 election and secession, the reasons for the North's victory,
the role of Lincoln, and Reconstruction. The question is also the
prescribed Paper 2 topic for the November 2025 and June 2026 series, so
it needs both essay knowledge and source practice.

## Where this fits in 0470 and 2147

Key question 1.4 is one of six in Core Content Option A ("The
nineteenth century: the development of modern nation states,
1848–1914"). The IGCSE 0470 and O Level 2147 syllabuses for 2024–2026
use identical wording for it. On Paper 1 it can appear as a three-part
structured question; on Paper 2 it is examined through sources when
Cambridge prescribes it for a series. The overview of the whole option
is in the [Nineteenth Century IGCSE study guide](/resources/igcse-world-history-nineteenth-century-1848-1914/)
and the [O Level equivalent](/resources/o-level-world-history-nineteenth-century-1848-1914/).

## Syllabus coverage

The four official focus points are:

1. How far did slavery cause the Civil War?
2. What was the significance of Lincoln's election as president?
3. Why was the North able to win the war?
4. Did the war change anything?

The specified content runs 1820–77: differences between North and
South; slavery, slave states and free states, abolitionism; the 1860
election and secession of the Southern states; reasons for the North's
victory and the South's defeat; the role of Lincoln; Reconstruction and
the extent of its success.

## How were the North and the South different?

By 1860 the two sections had grown apart economically and socially.

| | North | South |
|---|---|---|
| Economy | Industry, commerce, small family farms, growing cities | Plantation agriculture, above all cotton, also tobacco and sugar |
| Labour | Free wage labour, large-scale European immigration | Around four million enslaved people in 1860 |
| Population (1860) | About 22 million in the Union states | About 9 million in the Confederate states, over a third of them enslaved |
| Tariffs | Wanted protective tariffs for industry | Opposed tariffs that raised the cost of imports |
| Politics | Growing belief that Congress could limit slavery's spread | "States' rights": the states, not Congress, should decide on slavery |

The tariff quarrel had already produced the **Nullification Crisis of
1832–33**, when South Carolina declared federal tariffs void inside the
state. But it was settled by compromise, which is why most historians
see tariffs as a secondary cause.

## How far did slavery cause the Civil War?

Almost every crisis between 1820 and 1860 was about whether slavery
would spread into the new western territories.

- **Missouri Compromise (1820):** Missouri entered as a slave state and
  Maine as a free state, keeping the balance in the Senate; slavery was
  banned in the rest of the Louisiana Purchase north of latitude 36°30′.
- **Abolitionism:** William Lloyd Garrison's newspaper *The Liberator*
  (from 1831), the speeches and writing of the formerly enslaved
  Frederick Douglass, the Underground Railroad (Harriet Tubman) and
  Harriet Beecher Stowe's novel *Uncle Tom's Cabin* (1852) turned many
  Northerners against slavery and convinced Southerners that the North
  meant to destroy it.
- **Compromise of 1850:** after the Mexican War (1846–48) added vast
  territory, California joined as a free state, but a much stricter
  **Fugitive Slave Act** forced Northern officials to help return people
  who had escaped slavery, which angered the North.
- **Kansas–Nebraska Act (1854):** Stephen Douglas let settlers decide
  by "popular sovereignty", repealing the 36°30′ line. Violence between
  pro- and anti-slavery settlers ("Bleeding Kansas") followed, and the
  new **Republican Party** formed to oppose slavery's expansion.
- **Dred Scott decision (1857):** the Supreme Court ruled that Congress
  could not ban slavery in the territories and that Black Americans
  were not citizens.
- **John Brown's raid on Harpers Ferry (1859):** an attempt to start an
  armed rising of enslaved people. Brown was hanged, but many
  Northerners treated him as a martyr, which horrified the South.

**Judgement:** slavery was the underlying cause, because the economic
and states'-rights arguments were themselves mostly arguments about
slavery. A balanced answer still explains how the breakdown of the old
two-party system and the failure of compromise turned a long dispute
into war.

## Why did Lincoln's election lead to secession?

In November 1860 the Democrats split between Stephen Douglas (North)
and John Breckinridge (South), and John Bell stood for the
Constitutional Union Party. **Abraham Lincoln** won with about 40% of
the popular vote and a majority in the Electoral College, without
carrying a single Southern state. He promised not to touch slavery
where it already existed, but he opposed its spread.

Southern leaders concluded that they had lost control of the federal
government. **South Carolina seceded on 20 December 1860**; by February
1861 seven states had formed the **Confederate States of America**,
with Jefferson Davis as president. When Confederate forces fired on
**Fort Sumter on 12 April 1861** and Lincoln called for volunteers,
four more states left, making eleven. The border slave states of
Delaware, Maryland, Kentucky and Missouri stayed in the Union.

## Why was the North able to win the war?

- **Resources:** a much larger population, most of the country's
  factories and railways, and far greater food production.
- **The blockade:** the Union navy gradually closed Southern ports,
  cutting cotton exports and imports of weapons ("Anaconda Plan").
- **Southern diplomacy failed:** the South hoped "King Cotton" would
  force Britain and France to recognise it; neither did.
- **Turning points of 1863:** the **Emancipation Proclamation**
  (announced after Antietam in September 1862, in force from 1 January
  1863) made the war a war against slavery and helped keep Britain out;
  in July 1863 Union victories at **Gettysburg** and **Vicksburg** ended
  Lee's invasion of the North and gave the Union the Mississippi.
- **Generals and strategy:** Ulysses S. Grant's pressure on Lee in
  Virginia and William T. Sherman's capture of Atlanta (September 1864)
  and "March to the Sea" destroyed the South's capacity to fight.
- **African American soldiers:** about 180,000 served in the Union army.

The South had real strengths: it only had to defend, and it had
skilled commanders such as Robert E. Lee and "Stonewall" Jackson. Lee
surrendered at **Appomattox Court House on 9 April 1865**.

### The role of Lincoln

Lincoln kept the border states loyal, chose generals who would fight
(eventually Grant), turned the war's purpose towards emancipation at
the right moment, and won re-election in 1864. He was assassinated by
John Wilkes Booth on 14 April 1865 and died the next day.

## Did the war change anything? Reconstruction, 1865–77

| Change | Limits |
|---|---|
| **13th Amendment (1865)** abolished slavery | Southern "Black Codes" (1865–66) restricted freed people's lives |
| **14th Amendment (1868)** gave citizenship and equal protection | Courts and later governments weakened it |
| **15th Amendment (1870)** banned denying the vote on grounds of race | Violence, intimidation and later literacy tests and poll taxes kept most Black Southerners from voting |
| Freedmen's Bureau (1865) built schools and helped freed people | Most freed people had no land and became sharecroppers |
| Black men elected to office, e.g. Hiram Revels to the Senate (1870) | The Ku Klux Klan, founded 1865–66, used terror against them |

President Andrew Johnson's lenient approach clashed with the **Radical
Republicans** in Congress, who passed the Reconstruction Acts of 1867
and impeached Johnson in 1868 (he was acquitted by one vote). Northern
interest faded, and under the **Compromise of 1877** Rutherford B.
Hayes became president and the last federal troops left the South.
White "Redeemer" governments then took back control.

**Judgement:** the war preserved the Union and ended slavery, a
revolutionary change, but Reconstruction failed to secure equal
rights for African Americans, which is why most historians call it
only a partial success.

## Frequently asked questions

**What was the main cause of the American Civil War?** The spread of
slavery into the western territories. Disputes over states' rights and
tariffs mattered, but they were closely tied to protecting slavery.

**Why did the South secede after the 1860 election?** Lincoln won
without any Southern votes on a platform of stopping slavery's
expansion, so Southern leaders believed slavery would eventually be
destroyed if they stayed in the Union.

**Why did the North win?** Its larger population, industry and
railways, the naval blockade, the failure of Southern diplomacy, and
better strategy from 1863 under Lincoln, Grant and Sherman.

**Was Reconstruction a success?** Partly. Slavery was abolished and the
14th and 15th Amendments were passed, but by 1877 federal protection
had been withdrawn and most freed people lacked land, votes and safety.
