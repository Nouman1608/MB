A: the lettuce

B: the snail

C: the thrush

D: the hawk

**2.** What is the main form in which energy is lost from living organisms to the environment?

A: chemical energy

B: heat

C: light

## Section B

**6.** The food web shows the following feeding relationships in a lake. Algae are eaten by water fleas and by snails. Water fleas are eaten by small fish. Snails are eaten by small fish and by ducks. Small fish are eaten by herons.

**(a)** Write down a food chain with four organisms. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the producer. **[1]**

**(c)** Name **two** herbivores. **[2]**

**(d)** Predict and explain the effect on the water fleas if most of the small fish are removed. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why most forms of life depend on photosynthesis. **[3]**

**8.** In a grassland, the grass stores 18 000 kJ of energy per square metre per year. The grasshoppers that eat it store 1 800 kJ, and the frogs that eat the grasshoppers store 144 kJ.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage of energy passed from the grass to the grasshoppers. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of energy passed from the grasshoppers to the frogs. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate how much energy was lost between the grass and the grasshoppers. **[1]**

**(d)** Explain how the energy is lost. **[3]**

**9.** Explain why food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels. **[3]**

**10.** A farmer can use a field either to grow wheat for people or to grow wheat for cattle that people then eat. The field produces 50 000 kJ of energy in the wheat per year, and 10 per cent of this energy is stored in the beef.

**(a)** Calculate the energy available to people from the beef. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why more people can be fed if they eat the wheat directly. **[2]**

**11.** The numbers of organisms in a woodland are: one oak tree, 2 000 caterpillars, 30 blue tits and 2 sparrowhawks.

**(a)** Draw (describe) the pyramid of numbers. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the shape of the pyramid is unusual. **[2]**

**(c)** State which type of pyramid would always have a normal pyramid shape. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**

**3.** An organism that makes its own organic nutrients, usually by photosynthesis, using light energy [1]. **[1]**

**4.** They break down dead and waste organic matter (feeding on it) [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The transfer of energy from one organism to the next [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** Algae → water flea → small fish → heron (or algae → snail → small fish → heron) [1].

**(b)** Algae [1].

**(c)** Water fleas and snails [1 each].

**(d)** The number of water fleas would increase [1], because fewer would be eaten [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Photosynthesis transfers light energy into chemical energy in carbohydrates [1]. These are the source of food (energy) for all consumers [1], which cannot make their own food, so they depend on producers directly or indirectly [1]. **[3]**

**8.**

**(a)** 1 800 divided by 18 000 x 100 [1] = 10 per cent [1].

**(b)** 144 divided by 1 800 x 100 [1] = 8 per cent [1].

**(c)** 18 000 - 1 800 = 16 200 kJ [1].

**(d)** Any three of: heat lost in respiration [1]; parts of the grass not eaten [1]; undigested material lost in faeces [1]; energy lost in excretion [1]. **[8]**

**9.** Energy is lost at each trophic level, as heat, in faeces and in uneaten parts [1]. Only about 10 per cent is passed on [1], so very little energy is left after four or five stages to support more organisms [1]. **[3]**

**10.**

**(a)** 50 000 x 10 divided by 100 = 5 000 kJ [1].

**(b)** Eating the wheat directly removes a trophic level [1], so less energy is lost, and the field can feed about ten times as many people [1]. **[3]**

**11.**

**(a)** The bars are, from the base: oak tree 1; caterpillars 2 000; blue tits 30; sparrowhawks 2 [1]. The first bar is very narrow and the second is the widest [1].

**(b)** One large tree provides food for very many caterpillars [1], so the producer bar is narrower than the primary consumer bar [1].

**(c)** The pyramid of energy [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing arrows from the eater to the food.
- Saying that energy is recycled or "used up".
- Writing that food chains are short because of space or hunting.
- Using the wrong divisor in an efficiency calculation.
- Forgetting that pyramids of numbers can be inverted.
