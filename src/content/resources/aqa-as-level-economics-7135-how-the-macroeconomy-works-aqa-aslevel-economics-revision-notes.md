These notes condense **3.2.2 How the macroeconomy works** of AQA AS Economics (7135), specification version 1.3, parts 3.2.2.1 to 3.2.2.6. The [3.2.2 study guide](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-how-the-macroeconomy-works-aqa-aslevel-economics/) explains each part with a worked multiplier example, and the [3.2.2 practice questions](/resources/aqa-as-level-economics-7135-how-the-macroeconomy-works-aqa-aslevel-economics-practice/) test it. All figures are invented.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| National income | Value of the economy's output (or income, or expenditure) in a year |
| Real national income | National income adjusted for changes in the price level |
| Injection | Spending entering the circular flow: investment, government spending, exports |
| Withdrawal | Income leaving the flow: saving, taxation, imports |
| Equilibrium national income | Level at which injections equal withdrawals |
| Full employment income | National income when resources are fully employed |
| Aggregate demand (AD) | Total planned spending on the economy's output at each price level: C + I + G + (X - M) |
| Short-run aggregate supply (SRAS) | Output firms supply at each price level with production costs given |
| Long-run aggregate supply (LRAS) | Normal capacity output; vertical at AS |
| Demand-side shock | Unexpected change in AD |
| Supply-side shock | Unexpected change in costs or capacity |
| Accelerator | Investment depends on the rate of change of demand for output |
| Multiplier | Final change in national income divided by the initial change in injections |

## 3.2.2.1 Circular flow

- Income = output = expenditure.
- Injections > withdrawals: national income rises. Withdrawals > injections: national income falls. Equal: equilibrium.
- Equilibrium can be below full employment income.

## 3.2.2.2 AD/AS analysis

- Price level change = movement along AD and AS.
- AD shifts: any change in C, I, G or net exports at a given price level.
- SRAS shifts: production costs. LRAS shifts: productive capacity.
- Growth in capacity = LRAS shifts right.
- AD/AS diagrams show changes in the price level, demand-deficient (cyclical) unemployment and growth.
- Global events (world recession, commodity prices) act through exports (AD) or costs (SRAS).

## 3.2.2.3 Determinants of AD

| Component | Rises when |
|---|---|
| C | Disposable income rises, interest rates fall, wealth or confidence rises, credit is easier to obtain |
| I | Expected sales rise, interest rates fall, business confidence rises, new technology appears |
| G | Government chooses to spend more |
| X - M | Exchange rate falls, incomes abroad rise, domestic inflation is lower than abroad |

- **Accelerator (no calculations at AS):** slower growth of sales can cut investment even while sales still rise.
- **Saving** depends on income, interest rates, confidence and wealth. Saving is a withdrawal; investment is an injection.

## 3.2.2.4 Multiplier, AS method

```
multiplier = change in national income / initial change in injections
change in national income = initial change in injections x multiplier
initial change needed = target change in income / multiplier
```

| Injection change | Income change | Multiplier |
|---|---|---|
| +20 | +50 | 2.5 |
| +16 | +48 | 3 |
| -10 | -15 | 1.5 |

The multiplier works because spending is someone else's income, passed on in smaller rounds as some is withdrawn. Local multipliers work in the same way. Near full capacity more of the effect is on prices.

## 3.2.2.5 SRAS

Main determinants: the price level and production costs. Shift factors: money wage rates, raw material prices, business taxation, productivity.

## 3.2.2.6 LRAS

Vertical at normal capacity output. Fundamental determinants: technology, productivity, attitudes, enterprise, factor mobility, economic incentives.

## Chains to learn

- Interest rates fall, borrowing is cheaper, C and I rise, AD shifts right, output and the price level rise.
- World recession, exports fall, AD shifts left, multiplier reduces income further, cyclical unemployment rises.
- Wages rise faster than productivity, unit labour costs rise, SRAS shifts left, price level rises and output falls.
- Better training, productivity rises, LRAS shifts right, output can grow without inflationary pressure.

## Exam traps

- Using an MPC formula when the question gives an injection and an income change.
- Listing saving as an injection.
- Confusing a shift of AD with a movement along it.
- Shifting LRAS for a demand-side change.
- Giving a cost rise as a cause of an AD shift; it shifts SRAS.
- Accelerator answers that only say "investment rises with growth".
- Forgetting that a negative demand shock lowers output and creates cyclical unemployment.

## Self-test

1. State the three injections and the three withdrawals.
2. What happens to national income when withdrawals exceed injections?
3. Explain why equilibrium income can differ from full employment income.
4. Investment rises by 25 bn and national income rises by 75 bn. Calculate the multiplier.
5. The multiplier is 2. By how much must exports rise to raise income by 30 bn?
6. Give two determinants of consumption.
7. Explain the accelerator in one sentence.
8. Distinguish between saving and investment.
9. A world oil price rise shifts which curve, and in which direction?
10. Give three determinants of LRAS.
11. A recession abroad cuts exports. Show the effect in words on the AD/AS diagram.
12. Why is the multiplier effect on real output smaller near full capacity?

### Answers

1. Injections: investment, government spending, exports. Withdrawals: saving, taxation, imports.
2. It falls.
3. The flow settles where injections equal withdrawals, which can be at a level of output where many resources are unemployed.
4. 75 / 25 = 3.
5. 30 / 2 = 15 bn.
6. Any two: disposable income, interest rates, wealth, confidence, credit availability.
7. Investment depends on the rate of change of demand for output, so a slowdown in sales growth can cut investment.
8. Saving is income not spent on consumption (a withdrawal); investment is spending on capital goods (an injection).
9. SRAS shifts left.
10. Any three: technology, productivity, attitudes, enterprise, factor mobility, economic incentives.
11. AD shifts left, real output falls below normal capacity, the price level falls or rises more slowly, and cyclical unemployment rises.
12. Resources are scarce, so extra spending bids up prices rather than raising output.
