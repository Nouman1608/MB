## Questions

**1.** Triangle ABC has angles of 48° and 67°. Triangle DEF has angles of 65° and 48°. Show that the triangles are similar. **[2]**

**2.** **(a)** Triangle P has sides 4.5 cm, 6 cm and 7.5 cm. Triangle Q has sides 7.5 cm, 10 cm and 12.5 cm. Show that they are similar and state the scale factor from P to Q. **[2]**
**(b)** Triangle R has sides 5 cm, 9 cm and 12 cm. Triangle S has sides 10 cm, 18 cm and 25 cm. Explain why R and S are not similar. **[2]**

**3.** Triangle S has vertices (1, 2), (2, 2) and (2, 4). Enlarge S by scale factor 3 with centre (0, 1). Give the coordinates of the image. **[3]**

**4.** Triangle M has vertices (2, 5), (10, 5) and (10, 9). Its image N has vertices (0, 3), (4, 3) and (4, 5). Give the full description of the one transformation that maps M to N. **[3]**

**5.** In triangle ABC, D lies on AB and E lies on AC. DE is parallel to BC. AD = 5 cm, DB = 3 cm, DE = 7.5 cm and AE = 4 cm.
**(a)** Prove that triangles ADE and ABC are similar. **[3]**
**(b)** Find BC. **[2]**
**(c)** Find EC. **[2]**

**6.** Parallelograms ABCD and EFGH are similar. AB = 7.5 cm matches EF = 12 cm. BC = 10 cm, and EH = 20.8 cm matches AD.
**(a)** Find the scale factor from ABCD to EFGH. **[1]**
**(b)** Find FG. **[2]**
**(c)** Find AD. **[2]**

**7.** A model of a bridge is made to a scale of 1 : 250.
**(a)** The span of the model is 36 cm. Find the span of the real bridge, in metres. **[2]**
**(b)** A tower on the real bridge is 120 m high. Find the height of the tower on the model, in centimetres. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher tier only) Triangle ABC has A(2, 2), B(5, 2) and C(5, 3).
**(a)** Enlarge ABC by scale factor −2 with centre (3, 1). Give the coordinates of the image. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the length of A'B', where A' and B' are the images of A and B. **[1]**

**9.** (Higher tier only) Two similar bottles have heights 12 cm and 18 cm.
**(a)** The smaller bottle holds 400 ml. How much does the larger hold? **[2]**
**(b)** The label on the smaller bottle has area 150 cm². Find the area of the label on the larger bottle. **[2]**

**10.** (Higher tier only) Two similar solids have surface areas 96 cm² and 150 cm². The larger has volume 250 cm³. Find the volume of the smaller solid. **[5]**

**11.** A vertical pole 2.4 m high casts a shadow 3.6 m long on level ground. At the same time a tree casts a shadow 15 m long.
**(a)** Explain why the triangles formed by the pole and the tree are similar. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the height of the tree. **[3]**

**12.** (Higher tier only) A solid cone has base radius 8 cm, height 24 cm and volume 512π cm³. It is cut by a plane parallel to the base, 9 cm from the apex. This removes a small cone from the top.
**(a)** Explain why the small cone is similar to the whole cone. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the radius of the circular cut face. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the volume of the small cone, in terms of π. **[2]**
**(d)** Find the volume of the remaining solid, in terms of π. **[1]**
**(e)** Find the ratio of the area of the cut face to the area of the base. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Third angle of ABC = 180° − 48° − 67° = 65° [1]. DEF has angles 65°, 48° and 180° − 65° − 48° = 67°. The angles of both are 48°, 65° and 67°, so the triangles are **similar** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* work out the missing angles and list all three for each triangle. Two angles alone can look like a guess.

**2. (a)** 7.5 ÷ 4.5 = 10 ÷ 6 = 12.5 ÷ 7.5 = 5/3 [1]. The sides are in the same ratio, so P and Q are similar, with scale factor **5/3** **[2]** [1]
**(b)** 10 ÷ 5 = 2 and 18 ÷ 9 = 2 [1], but 25 ÷ 12 is about 2.08 [1]. The ratios are not all equal, so R and S are **not similar** **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) quote the three ratios. Saying "the numbers don't match" with no working gives the reader nothing to follow.

**3.** Steps from (0, 1): (1, 2) is right 1, up 1; (2, 2) is right 2, up 1; (2, 4) is right 2, up 3 [1]. Multiply by 3: right 3 up 3, right 6 up 3, right 6 up 9 [1]. Image: **(3, 4), (6, 4), (6, 10)** **[3]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* count from the centre, not from the original vertex. A sketch with the centre marked helps you spot a slip.

**4.** Base of M is 8 and base of N is 4, so the scale factor is 4 ÷ 8 = **1/2** [1]. Lines through (2, 5) and (0, 3), and through (10, 5) and (4, 3), meet at **(−2, 1)** [1]. The transformation is an **enlargement** with that scale factor and centre **[3]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* name the transformation, the scale factor and the centre. A description missing any one is incomplete. Check that the third pair of vertices also lines up with the centre.

**5. (a)** Angle A is common to both triangles [1]. Angle ADE = angle ABC, corresponding angles with DE parallel to BC [1]. Angle AED = angle ACB, corresponding angles, so the triangles are **similar** **[3]** [1]
**(b)** AB = 5 + 3 = 8 cm, so the scale factor from ADE to ABC is 8/5 [1]. BC = 7.5 × 8/5 = **12 cm** **[2]** [1]
**(c)** AC = 4 × 8/5 = 6.4 cm [1]. EC = 6.4 − 4 = **2.4 cm** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the scale factor uses AB, not DB. A frequent slip is to divide by the shorter part of the side.

**6. (a)** 12 ÷ 7.5 = **1.6** **[1]** [1]
**(b)** FG = 10 × 1.6 [1] = **16 cm** **[2]** [1]
**(c)** AD is on the smaller shape, so divide: 20.8 ÷ 1.6 [1] = **13 cm** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* check the direction. The smaller parallelogram must give the smaller length, so AD should be less than EH.

**7. (a)** Real length = 36 × 250 = 9000 cm [1] = **90 m** **[2]** [1]
**(b)** 120 m = 12 000 cm [1]. 12 000 ÷ 250 = **48 cm** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* write the units at each step. The scale is for lengths in the same unit, so convert metres and centimetres before dividing.

**8. (a)** Steps from (3, 1): A is left 1, up 1; B is right 2, up 1; C is right 2, up 2 [1]. Multiply by −2: A right 2, down 2; B left 4, down 2; C left 4, down 4 [1]. Image: **A'(5, −1), B'(−1, −1), C'(−1, −3)** **[3]** [1]
**(b)** AB = 3, so A'B' = 3 × 2 = **6** **[1]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* the image should land on the opposite side of the centre. If it ends up on the same side, the sign has been ignored. You can check with A'B' = 6.

**9. (a)** Length ratio 12 : 18 = 2 : 3, so the volume ratio is 8 : 27 [1]. 400 × 27/8 = **1350 ml** **[2]** [1]
**(b)** Area ratio is 4 : 9 [1]. 150 × 9/4 = **337.5 cm²** **[2]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* write the ratio you are using next to the working. Mixing up the area and volume factors is the usual way to lose marks here.

**10.** Area ratio 96 : 150 = 16 : 25 [1]. Length ratio = 4 : 5 [1]. Volume ratio = 64 : 125 [1]. The smaller volume is 250 × 64/125 [1] = **128 cm³** **[5]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* say which solid each number belongs to. Because the larger volume is given, you scale down by 64/125, and the answer must be smaller than 250.

**11. (a)** The sun's rays are parallel, so the angle of elevation is the same for the pole and the tree [1]. The pole and tree are both vertical, so the right angles are equal, so the triangles are similar **[2]** [1]
**(b)** Scale factor = 15 ÷ 3.6 [1]. Height = 2.4 × 15/3.6 [1] = **10 m** **[3]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* give the geometric reason for the equal angle. Saying "they are similar" without a reason is a conclusion, not an explanation.

**12. (a)** The cut is parallel to the base, so the small cone has the same angles as the whole cone and its lengths are all in the same ratio **[1]** [1]
**(b)** Scale factor = 9/24 = 3/8 [1]. Radius = 8 × 3/8 = **3 cm** **[2]** [1]
**(c)** Volume ratio = 3³ : 8³ = 27 : 512 [1]. Volume = 512π × 27/512 = **27π cm³** **[2]** [1]
**(d)** 512π − 27π = **485π cm³** **[1]** [1]
**(e)** Area ratio = 3² : 8² = **9 : 64** **[1]** [1]
*Examiner insight:* cube for volume in (c) and square for area in (e). Check (e) by direct calculation: 9π to 64π reduces to 9 : 64.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming a pair of triangles as similar without giving a reason for each equal angle.
- Pairing sides of a turned triangle in the wrong order.
- Multiplying when the answer should be smaller, or dividing when it should be larger.
- Using DB instead of AB as the base for the scale factor when a smaller triangle sits inside a larger one.
- Counting steps from the shape instead of from the centre of enlargement.
- Leaving out the centre, or the scale factor, when asked to describe an enlargement.
- Ignoring the minus sign in a negative scale factor, so the image is on the wrong side of the centre (Higher tier only).
- Using the length scale factor for area or volume, or taking the wrong root when working backwards (Higher tier only).
