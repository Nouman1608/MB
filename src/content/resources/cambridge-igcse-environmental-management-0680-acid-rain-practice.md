## Section A

**1.** Name the natural source of sulfur dioxide given in the syllabus. **[1]**

**2.** State the **two** substances that can react with sulfur dioxide in flue-gas desulfurisation. **[2]**

**3.** State what is meant by *defoliation*. **[1]**

**4.** Name **two** building materials that are damaged by acid rain. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why oxides of nitrogen are produced in vehicle engines. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The pH of two lakes was measured. Lake A has a pH of 6.5 and Lake B has a pH of 4.5.
(a) State how many times more acidic Lake B is than Lake A.
(b) Suggest why Lake B has fewer species of invertebrates. **[3]**

**7.** A country has no coal-fired power stations but suffers from acid rain. Suggest and explain **three** reasons for this. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how acid rain falling on a forest can damage trees through its effects on the soil. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how acidification of a lake can lead to a fall in the number of fish-eating birds nesting nearby. **[3]**

**10.** The table shows yearly sulfur dioxide emissions from a power station.

| Year | Sulfur dioxide (tonnes) |
|---|---|
| 2005 | 42,000 |
| 2010 (desulfurisation fitted) | 40,000 |
| 2015 | 6,000 |

(a) Calculate the percentage fall in emissions between 2010 and 2015.
(b) Explain how flue-gas desulfurisation produced this fall. **[4]**

**11.** Describe **three** transport policies that a city could use to reduce acid rain. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why flue-gas desulfurisation can have environmental costs of its own. **[2]**

**13.** A large city has severe traffic congestion, and acid rain is damaging its historic limestone buildings. Discuss the benefits and limitations of catalytic converters and transport policies for reducing this damage. **[6]**

**14.** Emissions of sulfur dioxide in a region were cut sharply ten years ago, but a lake in the region still has few fish. Suggest **two** reasons why the lake has not yet recovered. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Volcanic gases [1].

**2.** Calcium carbonate [1]; calcium oxide [1].

**3.** The loss of leaves or needles from plants [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: limestone [1]; marble [1]. Also accept metals such as iron or steel.

**5.** Nitrogen from the air drawn into the engine [1] reacts with oxygen because of the high temperature of combustion [1].

**6. (a)** The difference is 2 pH units [1], so Lake B is 10 × 10 = 100 times more acidic [1].
**(b)** Invertebrates such as snails, insect larvae and crustaceans cannot survive at low pH, so they die out [1].

**7.** Any three, 1 mark each: sulfur dioxide and oxides of nitrogen are carried by wind from other countries [1]; oxides of nitrogen from vehicle engines form within the country [1]; volcanic gases release sulfur dioxide [1]. Also accept: industry or ships burning sulfur-containing oil.

**8.** Acid rain washes nutrients such as calcium and magnesium out of the soil [1], so trees have fewer nutrients for growth [1]; aluminium is released in the soil and damages roots [1], so trees take up less water and nutrients, weaken and may lose their leaves [1].

**9.** Acidic water kills fish eggs and young fish, and aluminium damages fish gills, so fish populations fall [1]; invertebrates that fish eat also die out, so the fish lose food [1]; the birds that feed on fish have less food, so fewer can breed or nest there [1].

**10. (a)** Fall = 40,000 − 6,000 = 34,000 tonnes [1]; 34,000 / 40,000 × 100 = 85% [1].
**(b)** The waste gases are passed through calcium carbonate or calcium oxide before leaving the chimney [1]; sulfur dioxide reacts with them and is removed as a solid, so much less reaches the atmosphere [1].

**11.** Any three, 1 mark each: invest in cheaper or more frequent public transport so fewer car journeys are made [1]; congestion charges or low-emission zones that make driving into the centre more expensive or restricted [1]; cycle lanes and park-and-ride schemes [1]. Also accept: support for electric vehicles, car-sharing schemes.

**12.** Large amounts of limestone must be quarried, damaging landscapes and habitats [1]; the reaction with calcium carbonate releases carbon dioxide, a greenhouse gas [1].

**13.** Catalytic converters: benefit, remove oxides of nitrogen from each fitted vehicle, reducing acid rain over the city [1]; limitation, they do not work well until warm, so in short, slow city journeys they are less effective [1]; limitation, they do nothing about the number of vehicles or congestion [1]. Transport policies: benefit, fewer vehicle journeys reduce oxides of nitrogen and congestion together [1]; limitation, congestion charges and restrictions can be unpopular and need good public transport to work [1]. Judgement: the two work best together because converters clean each vehicle and policies reduce how many vehicles there are, though neither repairs damage already done to the buildings [1].

**14.** Acid and aluminium already stored in the surrounding soils continue to drain into the lake for many years, keeping the pH low [1]; fish and the invertebrates they eat must recolonise from elsewhere and breed, which takes many years, especially if the lake is isolated [1]. Also accept: oxides of nitrogen from traffic are still forming acid rain.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying catalytic converters remove sulfur dioxide.
- Treating a two-unit pH difference as "twice as acidic" rather than 100 times.
- Forgetting that acid rain can be caused by pollution from another country.
- Explaining effects on trees without linking to the soil, when the question asks about the soil.
- Writing a "discuss" answer that never connects back to the buildings and traffic in the question.
