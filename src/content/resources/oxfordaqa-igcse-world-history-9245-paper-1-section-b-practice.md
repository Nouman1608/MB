## Section A: knowledge (3-mark questions)

**1.** *The origins of the First World War.* Give three ways in which Britain and Germany were
rivals before 1914. **[3]**

**2.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* Give three actions by which Hitler broke or challenged the Treaty
of Versailles in the years 1935-38. **[3]**

**3.** *The origins and development of the Cold War.* Give three features of the Korean War,
1950-53. **[3]**

**4.** *Détente and the collapse of communism.* Give three reasons why the superpowers wanted
détente in the early 1970s. **[3]**

## Section B: explanation and judgement

**5.** *The origins of the First World War.* Explain one way in which the Moroccan Crises affected
relations between the Great Powers. **[6]**

**6.** *The origins of the First World War.* "The assassination of Franz Ferdinand was the main
reason war broke out in 1914." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**7.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* Explain two reasons why Britain followed a policy of appeasement in
the 1930s. **[9]**

**8.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* "The Nazi-Soviet Pact was the main reason war broke out in
September 1939." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**9.** *The origins and development of the Cold War.* Explain one way in which the Truman Doctrine
increased tension between the USA and the USSR. **[6]**

**10.** *The origins and development of the Cold War.* Explain two ways in which relations between
the superpowers changed after the death of Stalin in 1953. **[9]**

**11.** *Détente and the collapse of communism.* Explain one way in which the Soviet invasion of
Afghanistan affected relations between the superpowers. **[6]**

**12.** *Détente and the collapse of communism.* "Gorbachev's reforms were the main reason for the
collapse of communism in Eastern Europe." How far do you agree? **[12]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** The 3-mark questions give [1] per relevant point. The 6-mark guides give
[1] for the way identified, [2] for precise knowledge, [2] for explaining how it affected the
development and [1] for a further developed detail or consequence. The 9-mark guides give each of
two reasons [4] (identified [1], precise knowledge [1], explained link [2]) plus [1] for accurate,
well-sequenced detail across the answer. The 12-mark guides give [3] for the given factor, [4] for
at least one other factor, [3] for comparison in a line of reasoning and [2] for a supported
judgement.

**1.** Any three, [1] each: the naval race after HMS *Dreadnought* (1906); colonial rivalry and
Germany's demand for "a place in the sun"; Germany's challenges in Morocco (1905-06 and 1911);
Britain's ententes with France and Russia, which Germany saw as encirclement; trade and industrial
competition.

**2.** Any three, [1] each: reintroducing conscription (1935); rearming openly, including the air
force; remilitarising the Rhineland (1936); the *Anschluss* with Austria (1938); taking the
Sudetenland (1938).

**3.** Any three, [1] each: North Korea invaded the South in June 1950; UN forces led by the USA
under MacArthur fought for the South; the Inchon landings (1950) pushed North Korean forces back;
China intervened when UN forces approached its border; MacArthur was dismissed (1951); the war
ended in stalemate and an armistice (1953) near the original border.

**4.** Any three, [1] each: the huge cost of the arms race; fear of nuclear war after the Cuban
Missile Crisis; the USSR's economic problems and need for Western trade and technology; the USA's
wish to leave Vietnam and reduce costs; the Sino-Soviet split, which made both superpowers seek
better relations; West Germany's *Ostpolitik*.

**5.** Way identified [1]: the crises strengthened the Entente between Britain and France. Precise
knowledge [2]: in 1905 the Kaiser visited Tangier and backed Moroccan independence to test the new
Entente Cordiale; at the Algeciras Conference (1906) Britain supported France and Germany was
isolated; in 1911 Germany sent the gunboat *Panther* to Agadir. Explanation [2]: Germany's attempts
to split Britain and France backfired, because Britain saw German pressure as a threat and drew
closer to France. Further development [1]: after 1911 Britain and France made naval arrangements,
deepening the division of Europe into two armed camps.

**6.** Given factor [3]: the assassination at Sarajevo on 28 June 1914 by Gavrilo Princip triggered
the July Crisis; Austria-Hungary, with German support, issued an ultimatum to Serbia and declared
war on 28 July. Other factors [4], for example: the alliance system drew Russia, Germany and France
into the conflict; the Schlieffen Plan required an attack through Belgium, bringing Britain in on 4
August; the naval and arms races made powers ready to fight; Balkan nationalism and the Bosnian
Crisis had already embittered Serbia and Austria-Hungary. Comparison [3]: the assassination was the
trigger, but without alliances and military plans it might have remained a Balkan war. Judgement
[2]: for example, the assassination was the immediate cause, but long-term rivalries and military
planning explain why it became a general war.

**7.** Reason one [4]: Britain was not ready for war [1]; rearmament only began in earnest in the
mid-1930s and the armed forces were overstretched across the Empire [1]; so Chamberlain believed
concessions would buy time to build defences [2]. Reason two [4]: many people believed Versailles
had been too harsh [1]; for example, the Rhineland and the Sudeten Germans seemed reasonable
grievances [1]; so British leaders thought meeting some German demands would remove the causes of
war [2]. Accurate, well-sequenced detail [1], for example linking the Munich Agreement of September
1938 to this thinking. Other valid reasons include fear of another war like 1914-18, the Depression,
and fear of communism.

**8.** Given factor [3]: signed on 23 August 1939, the pact meant Hitler would not face a war on two
fronts and secretly divided Poland, so he invaded on 1 September. Other factors [4], for example:
Hitler's aims of *Lebensraum* and overturning Versailles; appeasement, which encouraged him to
believe Britain and France would not fight; the failure of the League; the British and French
guarantee to Poland, which made an invasion of Poland a cause of war. Comparison [3]: the pact
decided the timing, but Hitler's aims made a war likely in any case. Judgement [2]: for example,
the pact was the immediate cause of war in September 1939, but Hitler's long-term aims were the
main cause.

**9.** Way identified [1]: the Truman Doctrine made containment of communism official US policy.
Precise knowledge [2]: in March 1947 Truman promised support to free peoples resisting
subjugation, beginning with aid to Greece and Turkey after Britain could no longer support
Greece. Explanation [2]: Stalin saw it as a declaration that the USA would oppose Soviet influence
everywhere, so each side now viewed the other as a global threat. Further development [1]: it was
followed by Marshall Aid and the Soviet response of Cominform, dividing Europe further.

**10.** Way one [4]: a "Thaw" in relations [1]; Austria's neutrality agreed in 1955 and
Khrushchev's policy of peaceful co-existence [1]; so the superpowers showed they could reach
agreements and talk at summits [2]. Way two [4]: continued rivalry and confrontation [1]; the
Warsaw Pact formed in 1955 and Soviet tanks crushed the Hungarian rising in 1956 [1]; so the West
saw that the USSR would still use force to keep control of Eastern Europe [2]. Accurate,
well-sequenced detail [1], for example the arms and space race, with Sputnik 1 in 1957.

**11.** Way identified [1]: it ended the first period of détente. Precise knowledge [2]: Soviet
forces invaded in December 1979; Carter withdrew SALT II from the Senate, imposed a grain embargo
and led a boycott of the 1980 Moscow Olympics. Explanation [2]: the USA saw the invasion as Soviet
expansion, so cooperation was replaced by suspicion and pressure. Further development [1]: Reagan's
election in 1980 brought increased defence spending and a renewed arms race, and the USSR boycotted
the 1984 Los Angeles Olympics in return.

**12.** Given factor [3]: glasnost and perestroika encouraged criticism and change, and Gorbachev
made clear he would not use Soviet force to keep communist governments in power. Other factors
[4], for example: the economic weakness of the Eastern bloc; the cost of the war in Afghanistan;
Solidarity in Poland, which won elections in 1989; mass protests in East Germany and the opening of
the Berlin Wall in November 1989; the Velvet Revolution in Czechoslovakia; Reagan's pressure through
the arms race. Comparison [3]: protest movements had existed before, but they succeeded in 1989
because Gorbachev refused to intervene. Judgement [2]: for example, Gorbachev's reforms were the
main reason, because they removed the threat of force that had kept communist governments in power
since 1956 and 1968.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining instead of listing in 3-mark questions.
- Giving two ways in a 6-mark question that asks for one.
- Reasons that are not clearly separated in 9-mark questions.
- No comparison or judgement in 12-mark questions.
