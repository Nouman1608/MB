## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** An item cost 40. It can be sold for 55, but a sales agent will take
commission of 10% of the selling price and delivery will cost 3. At what value
is the item included in inventory?

A: 40.00

B: 46.50

C: 52.00

D: 55.00

**[1]**

**2.** Opening inventory was understated by 800. What is the effect on gross
profit for the year?

A: understated by 800

B: overstated by 800

C: understated by 1,600

D: no effect

**[1]**

**3.** State two reasons why the net realisable value of goods may fall below
their cost. **[2]**

**4.** A clothing shop holds 60 jackets that cost 45 each. The selling price has
been cut to 50 and each jacket needs alterations costing 8 before sale. Calculate
the value of the jackets and the write-down. **[2]**

**5.** State the effect of an overstatement of closing inventory on **(a)** the
current ratio **[1]** and **(b)** the acid test (liquid) ratio. **[1]**

**6.** Closing inventory at the end of Year 1 was overstated by 1,200. Closing
inventory at the end of Year 2 was correct. State the effect on equity at the
end of **(a)** Year 1 **[1]** and **(b)** Year 2. **[1]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Corran Books counted its inventory at 31 December 2028.

| Item | Units | Cost per unit | Information |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hardback novels | 160 | 14.00 | sell at 22.00; commission 2.20 per book |
| Atlases (water-damaged) | 40 | 25.00 | can be sold for 18.00 each |
| Diaries for 2028 | 300 | 4.00 | can be sold for 1.50 each; packaging 0.25 each |
| Signed editions | 12 | 60.00 | sell at 95.00; insured delivery 8.00 each |

**(a)** Calculate the value of the inventory. **[5]**

**(b)** Calculate the total write-down from cost. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the signed editions are not valued at 87.00 each. **[1]**

**8.** Merrow Garden Centre's draft figures for 2028 are: revenue 84,000;
opening inventory 7,600; purchases 52,300; closing inventory at cost 9,400;
expenses 19,850. It is then found that:

1. The closing inventory includes 30 hedge trimmers that cost 55 each. Their
   selling price is now 48 each, with delivery of 4 each.
2. 120 packets of seeds were counted at their selling price of 2.50 each instead
   of their cost of 1.60 each.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected closing inventory. **[3]**

**(b)** Prepare the corrected statement of profit or loss extract from revenue to
profit for the year. **[4]**

**(c)** State the effect of the corrections on equity at 31 December 2028. **[1]**

**9.** Brockley Paints reported profit for the year ended 31 December 2028 of
21,450. It was then found that opening inventory at 1 January 2028 was understated
by 900 and closing inventory at 31 December 2028 was overstated by 1,350.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[3]**

**(b)** State the effect of the errors on inventory in the statement of financial
position at 31 December 2028. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain the effect of the errors on equity at 31 December 2028. **[2]**

**10.** Ashby Kitchens reported profits of 30,000 (Year 1), 34,000 (Year 2) and
36,000 (Year 3). Goods costing 2,000 were left out of closing inventory at the
end of Year 1. All other inventory figures were correct.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for each year. **[3]**

**(b)** Compare the trend in profit before and after correction. **[2]**

**11.** The owner of a shoe shop says: "Those damaged boots cost me 70 a pair, so
they stay in my inventory at 70, even though I can only sell them for 40." Explain
why this is not acceptable. **[3]**

**12.** Pellow Traders has revenue of 80,000 and recorded gross profit of 30,000.
Current assets are 24,000 and current liabilities 12,000. Closing inventory was
overstated by 2,000.

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit margin as recorded and as corrected. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the current ratio as recorded and as corrected. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** A [1]. NRV = 55 − 5.50 − 3 = 46.50, which is higher than cost, so cost is
used. **[1]**

**2.** B [1]. Cost of sales is understated, so gross profit is overstated. **[1]**

**3.** Any two from: physical damage; obsolescence or a change in fashion; a fall
in the market selling price; high costs of selling such as commission or
delivery [1] [1]. **[2]**

**4.** NRV = 50 − 8 = 42, so the value is 60 × 42 = **2,520** [1]. Write-down
= 60 × (45 − 42) = **180** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** The current ratio is overstated [1].

**(b)** No effect, because the acid test excludes inventory [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Overstated by 1,200 [1].

**(b)** Correct: Year 2 profit is understated by 1,200, cancelling the Year 1
error [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Hardbacks: NRV 19.80, cost lower: 160 × 14.00 = 2,240 [1]. Atlases:
NRV 18.00 lower: 40 × 18.00 = 720 [1]. Diaries: NRV 1.25 lower: 300 × 1.25 = 375 [1].
Signed editions: NRV 87.00, cost lower: 12 × 60.00 = 720 [1].
Total = **4,055** [1].

**(b)** At cost: 2,240 + 1,000 + 1,200 + 720 = 5,160 [1]. Write-down = 5,160 − 4,055
= **1,105** [1].

**(c)** NRV is above cost, and valuing at NRV would recognise a profit before the
books are sold (realisation and prudence) [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)** Trimmers: NRV = 48 − 4 = 44; write-down = 30 × 11 = 330 [1]. Seeds:
overstatement = 120 × (2.50 − 1.60) = 108 [1]. Corrected closing inventory =
9,400 − 330 − 108 = **8,962** [1].

**(b)**

```
Revenue                                  84,000
Opening inventory              7,600
Purchases                     52,300
                              59,900
Closing inventory             (8,962)
Cost of sales                            50,938
Gross profit                             33,062
Expenses                                 19,850
Profit for the year                      13,212
```

Cost of sales 50,938 [1]; gross profit 33,062 [1]; expenses deducted [1]; profit
13,212 [1].

**(c)** Equity is reduced by 438 [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** Understated opening inventory understated cost of sales, overstating
profit by 900 [1]. Overstated closing inventory overstated profit by 1,350 [1].
Corrected profit = 21,450 − 2,250 = **19,200** [1].

**(b)** Inventory is overstated by 1,350 [1].

**(c)** Equity is overstated by 1,350 [1]. Opening equity was understated by 900
because last year's profit was understated, and this year's profit is
overstated by 2,250, giving a net overstatement equal to the closing inventory
error [1]. **[6]**

**10. (a)** Year 1: 30,000 + 2,000 = **32,000** [1]. Year 2: 34,000 − 2,000
= **32,000** [1]. Year 3: **36,000**, unchanged [1].

**(b)** As recorded, profit rose by 4,000 from Year 1 to Year 2 [1]. After
correction there was no growth from Year 1 to Year 2, and the rise came only in
Year 3 [1]. **[5]**

**11.** Inventory must be valued at the lower of cost and net realisable value [1].
The boots' NRV (40 less any selling costs) is below cost, so they must be written
down [1]. Keeping them at 70 would overstate closing inventory, gross profit,
profit for the year and equity, which breaks the prudence concept [1]. **[3]**

**12. (a)** Recorded: 30,000 / 80,000 × 100 = **37.50%** [1]. Corrected gross
profit = 28,000 [1]; margin = 28,000 / 80,000 × 100 = **35.00%** [1].

**(b)** Recorded: 24,000 : 12,000 = **2.00 : 1** [1]. Corrected: 22,000 : 12,000
= **1.83 : 1** [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing total cost with total NRV.
- Raising an item to NRV when NRV is above cost.
- Treating an opening inventory error as if it changed the year-end inventory asset.
- Giving the effect on profit without the amount.
- Forgetting that a combined error can partly cancel out.
