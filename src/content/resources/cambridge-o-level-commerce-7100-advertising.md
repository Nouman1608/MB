**Advertising** is a paid form of communication that a business uses to tell people about its goods or services and to persuade them to buy. Sub-topic 5.1 of Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100) has four parts: **types of advertising** (5.1.1), **methods of traditional and digital advertising media** (5.1.2), **methods of appeal** (5.1.3) and **sales promotion** (5.1.4).

## 5.1.1 Purposes and types of advertising

### Purposes of advertising

- To **inform** customers about a new product, a change in price or a new shop.
- To **persuade** customers to choose this brand rather than another.
- To **increase sales** and market share.
- To **build or protect a brand image** and loyalty.
- To **remind** customers about a product they already know.
- To **launch a new product** or **respond to competitors**.

### Types of advertising

| Type | Purpose | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **Informative** | Gives facts such as features, price and where to buy | A poster announcing the opening hours of a new shop |
| **Persuasive** | Tries to change attitudes and make people want the product | An advert showing how happy people are with a particular phone |
| **Collective (generic)** | Paid for by a group of producers to promote a whole product type, not one brand | "Drink more milk" campaign by dairy farmers |
| **Competitive** | Directly or indirectly compares the product with rivals to win customers | An advert claiming one washing powder cleans better than other brands |

| Type | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Informative | Helps customers make choices; builds trust; good for new products | Gives little reason to prefer one brand; may bore people |
| Persuasive | Can create strong demand and brand loyalty | Can be expensive; may exaggerate or mislead |
| Collective | Cost shared between producers; raises demand for everyone | Brand names are not promoted; some firms benefit without paying ("free riders") |
| Competitive | Draws attention to strengths; can win customers from rivals | May start an advertising war; legal problems if claims are untrue |

## 5.1.2 Traditional and digital media

### Traditional advertising media

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Television | Wide audience; sound, picture and movement | Very expensive; short life |
| Radio | Cheaper than television; reaches people in cars and at work | No pictures; listeners may not pay attention |
| Cinema | Captive audience; large screen; can target age groups | Reaches few people; costly to make the advert |
| Newspapers (local and national) | Local papers target an area; national papers reach many; can include detail | Short life; low print quality; competes with other adverts |
| Magazines / trade journals | Target a specific interest or trade; good colour; long life | Published infrequently; costs can be high |
| Leaflets / flyers | Cheap; targeted at an area | Often thrown away |
| Packaging | Seen by the buyer at the moment of choice | Limited space |
| Direct mail | Addressed to the customer; can be personal | Seen as junk mail; cost of postage |
| Sponsorship | Links brand to a popular event or team | Expensive; poor results if the event is unsuccessful |
| Catalogues / brochures | Show a full range with details | Expensive to print; go out of date |
| Posters | Seen by many people; long life | Short glance; can be damaged |
| Transport vehicles | Moving adverts seen in many places | Hard to read at speed |
| Window displays | Attract people passing the shop | Reach only passers-by |
| Point of sale displays | Influence buying at the moment of decision | Only shown to people already in the shop |
| Signboards | Show the shop's name and location | Limited information |
| Word of mouth | Free; trusted by listeners | Cannot be controlled; negative comments spread too |

### Digital advertising media

| Medium | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Social media advertising | Targets by age, interests and location; can reach many at low cost; two-way contact | Many adverts compete for attention; negative comments |
| Websites and search engines (other internet advertising) | Appears when people search for related goods; results can be measured | Cost per click can be high; users may ignore or block adverts |
| Display advertisements (banners) | Cheap; appear on many sites | Often ignored; ad blockers |
| Video advertisements | Sound and movement; shareable | Costly to make; may be skipped |
| Email marketing | Cheap; direct; can be personalised | May go to spam; people may unsubscribe |
| Digital billboards | Can change the message quickly; eye-catching | Cost of equipment; only seen locally |
| Social media influencers | Reach a loyal audience who trust the influencer | Fee is high; risk to brand if the influencer behaves badly |

### Factors affecting choice of advertising method

| Factor | Question to ask | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Audience | Who are the target customers? | Teenagers are reached through social media; older people may read the local newspaper |
| Cost | What is the budget? | A small shop cannot afford television |
| Cultural and legal considerations | Does the advert respect local customs and laws? | Rules on advertising to children or tobacco |
| Flexibility to change | Can the message be changed quickly? | Digital billboards change in seconds; printed brochures cannot |
| Local and international market coverage | Where are the customers? | A local bakery uses a local newspaper; an exporter needs international coverage |
| Multi-channel advertising | Should several media be used together? | A launch with television, social media and posters |
| Timing | When must the advert appear? | Before a festival or sale |
| Type of product | What is being sold? | A fashion item suits video and influencers; an industrial machine suits a trade journal |

### Worked example: cost per person reached

A business compares two media. Television: cost $12,000 and reaches 400,000 people. Social media: cost $3,000 and reaches 150,000 people.

1. Television: 12,000 ÷ 400,000 = **$0.03** per person.
2. Social media: 3,000 ÷ 150,000 = **$0.02** per person.

Social media is cheaper per person and also cheaper in total. Television reaches more people, which may matter if the product is for the whole country. The final choice depends on audience, budget and product.

## 5.1.3 Methods of appeal

A **method of appeal** is the approach used in an advertisement to attract attention and influence the audience.

| Appeal | How it works | Suited to |
|---|---|---|
| Emotion | Uses feelings such as love or happiness | Charities, family products |
| Fear | Shows the bad outcome of not buying | Insurance, security products, medicine |
| Humour | Makes the audience laugh so that they remember the advert | Food, drinks, snacks |
| Rational | Uses facts, figures and logic | Computers, cars, savings accounts |
| Ambition | Suggests the product will help the buyer succeed | Education courses, luxury goods |
| Social acceptance | Suggests that buying will help the buyer fit in | Fashion, mobile phones |
| Romantic | Links the product to love and romance | Perfume, jewellery, chocolates |
| Adventure | Links the product to excitement and risk | Sports gear, travel, cars |
| Music | Uses a memorable tune or song | Many products; jingles are remembered |
| Endorsement by celebrities and social media influencers | A well-known person praises the product | Sports shoes, make-up |

An appeal is **appropriate** if it matches the product and audience. Fear suits an insurance company, but would be a poor choice for ice cream. Humour might not suit a funeral service.

## 5.1.4 Sales promotion

**Sales promotion** is a short-term activity that encourages customers to buy now.

| Method | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Point of sale | Eye-catching displays and offers near the till or on shelves |
| Special offers | Price cuts or "buy one get one free" |
| Sponsorship | Paying for an event or team to link the brand to it |
| Displays | Special arrangements of goods to catch attention |
| Competitions | A chance to win a prize if the customer buys |
| Coupons | Vouchers giving a discount on the next purchase |
| Sales periods | Seasonal sales when prices are lowered |
| Loss leaders | A product sold at or below cost to attract customers who then buy other goods |
| Free gifts | A free item with a purchase |
| Free samples | Small amounts given away so customers can try the product |

### Factors affecting the choice of method

Business type; competitors; cost; nature of the product; whether the product is new or old; timing.

### Worked example: two promotions

A shop sells a jacket for $60.

- **20% off:** discount = 0.20 × 60 = $12, so the price is **$48**.
- **Buy one get one free:** the customer pays $60 for two jackets, so each costs **$30**, which is a 50% reduction per item.

The buy-one-get-one-free offer looks generous but only works for customers who need two. The shop must check that it still makes a profit.

A **loss leader**: a supermarket buys a tin of beans at $0.80 and sells it at $0.70, losing 10 cents on each tin. If 5,000 tins are sold the loss is 5,000 × 0.10 = $500, but customers who come for the beans also buy other goods at normal profit.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** Explain **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of using social media influencers to advertise a new sports drink. [4]

> An advantage is that influencers have a loyal following of young people who trust them [1], so the drink reaches its target audience and the recommendation may persuade them to try it [1]. A disadvantage is that influencers can charge high fees [1], and if the influencer behaves badly or says something offensive, the brand's reputation may be damaged [1].

**Why it scores 4:** each point is developed and tied to the product and audience.

## Common mistakes

- Confusing **informative** and **persuasive** advertising.
- Describing sales promotion as a long-term activity. It is short-term.
- Choosing an appeal that does not suit the product.
- Naming a medium but giving no advantage or reason related to the audience.
- Treating **collective** advertising as advertising one brand.
- Forgetting to link the factor (cost, audience) to the case in the question.

## Exam technique

- **Identify** the target audience and product first.
- **Explain** the advantage of a medium by linking it to that audience.
- **Evaluate** by giving a disadvantage and a decision.
- **Calculate** percentages from the original price.
