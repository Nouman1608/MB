## Section B in one box

- **One option** from four.
- **Question 1** (3 marks, AO1): show knowledge.
- **Question 2** (6 marks, AO1/AO2): explain **one** way an issue developed.
- **Question 3** (9 marks, AO1/AO2): explain **two** ways or reasons.
- **Question 4** (12 marks, AO1/AO2): a line of reasoning on a given factor and at least one other,
  ending in a judgement on relative importance.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| *Weltpolitik* | Wilhelm II's policy of making Germany a world power |
| Schlieffen Plan | German plan to defeat France quickly through Belgium before turning on Russia |
| *Lebensraum* | "Living space" for Germans in eastern Europe |
| Appeasement | Avoiding war by meeting reasonable-seeming demands |
| Containment | US policy of stopping the spread of communism |
| Iron Curtain | The division between Soviet-controlled Eastern Europe and the West |
| Peaceful co-existence | Khrushchev's idea that East and West could compete without war |
| Détente | Easing of tension between the superpowers |
| Glasnost | Gorbachev's policy of openness |
| Perestroika | Gorbachev's policy of economic restructuring |

## Answer frames

- **3 marks**: three precise, relevant points; no explanation needed.
- **6 marks**: name the way, give precise knowledge, explain *how* it changed the situation, and
  extend with a second detail.
- **9 marks**: two clearly separated reasons, each with precise knowledge and an explained link.
- **12 marks**: given factor, at least one other, comparison throughout, a judgement that says
  *why* one mattered more.

## Exam traps

- Explaining in the 3-mark question instead of giving points.
- One reason given twice in different words in the 9-mark question.
- Narrative of events with no causal link.
- No comparison or judgement in the 12-mark question.
- Confusing dates or sequence, such as the Anschluss and Munich, or SALT I and SALT II.

## Self-test

1. Which countries formed the Triple Alliance?
2. Why did the Schlieffen Plan bring Britain into the war?
3. What was the Black Hand?
4. Give three steps Hitler took in 1935.
5. Why did the occupation of Prague in March 1939 end appeasement?
6. What did the Nazi-Soviet Pact mean for Hitler's plans in 1939?
7. What was the purpose of the Marshall Plan, and how did Stalin respond?
8. Why did the Berlin Blockade increase tension?
9. Why did the USSR invade Hungary in 1956?
10. Give two agreements of the first period of détente.
11. Give two reasons why détente collapsed after 1979.
12. Give two reasons for the collapse of Soviet control in Eastern Europe in 1989.

## Answers

1. Germany, Austria-Hungary and Italy.
2. It sent German troops through Belgium, whose neutrality Britain had guaranteed.
3. A secret Serbian nationalist society linked to the assassination at Sarajevo.
4. Any three: the Saar returned after a plebiscite; conscription reintroduced; the Anglo-German
   Naval Agreement signed; rearmament announced openly.
5. It showed Hitler's aims went beyond German-speaking lands, so his promises could not be
   trusted.
6. It removed the danger of a war on two fronts, so he could invade Poland.
7. To rebuild Europe's economies and limit communism; Stalin refused aid for the Eastern bloc and
   set up Cominform and Comecon.
8. It was a direct test of strength over Germany, leading to NATO and two German states.
9. Nagy's government announced it would leave the Warsaw Pact, threatening Soviet control.
10. Any two: the ABM Treaty; SALT I; the Helsinki Accords.
11. Any two: the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan; the failure to ratify SALT II; the Olympic
    boycotts; Reagan's renewed arms race and SDI.
12. Any two: Gorbachev's refusal to use force; economic weakness; the success of Solidarity;
    glasnost encouraging criticism; mass protest movements.
