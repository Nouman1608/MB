## Questions

**1.** Define unemployment. **[2]**

**2.** In Country E, 2,640,000 people are employed and 160,000 are unemployed. Calculate the unemployment rate to 1 decimal place. Show your working. **[3]**

**3.** In Country A, the claimant count is 85,000 but a labour force survey estimates 120,000 unemployed. Explain two reasons for the difference. **[4]**

**4.** Name the type of unemployment in each case.

**(a)** A trained nurse spends six weeks looking for a better-paid hospital post. **[1]**
**(b)** A shipyard closes for good because its type of vessel is no longer in demand, and the welders have no other skills. **[1]**
**(c)** A recession cuts consumer spending, so shops and factories lay off staff. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why unemployment of the type in (c) tends to fall when the economy recovers. **[1]**

**5.** The table shows the share of total employment by sector in Country G.

| Sector | Year 1 (%) | Year 2 (%) |
|---|---|---|
| Primary | 48 | 20 |
| Secondary | 22 | 28 |
| Tertiary | 30 | 52 |

**(a)** State the change in the tertiary share. **[1]**
**(b)** Total employment in year 2 is 6,000,000. Calculate the number employed in the tertiary sector. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain one reason why the tertiary share rises as an economy develops. **[2]**

**6.** Explain **(a)** why the proportion of women in the labour force may rise and **(b)** why the public-sector share of employment may fall as a country moves towards a market economy. **[4]**

**7.** Explain one cost and one possible benefit of high unemployment for firms. **[4]**

**8.** In Country H, unemployment rises by 90,000. Each unemployed person pays 2,000 less tax and receives 3,500 in benefit. An average worker produces 25,000 of output a year (invented currency units).

**(a)** Calculate the total effect on the government's budget. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the output lost. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why lost output is a cost to the whole economy. **[2]**

**9.** Explain three ways in which long-term unemployment may harm an individual. **[3]**

**10.** Country J's unemployment is mainly structural after its textile industry closed. Explain two policies the government could use to reduce it. **[6]**

**11.** Country K has a labour force of 10,000,000. Unemployment is 700,000, mostly cyclical after a recession. The government raises spending by 300,000,000. The multiplier is 2.0, and each 30,000 of extra output creates one job (assume the labour force stays the same).

**(a)** Calculate the new unemployment rate. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether this spending increase is the best policy for Country K. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A person without a job [1] who is able and willing to work and actively looking for work at the going wage [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Missing "actively looking" is a common way to lose the second part, because it separates the unemployed from students and retired people.

**2.** Labour force = 2,640,000 + 160,000 = 2,800,000 [1]. Rate = 160,000 / 2,800,000 x 100 [1] = **5.7%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 2,640,000 gives 6.1%, which is the usual wrong route; showing the labour force line makes your method visible even if arithmetic slips.

**3.** Reason 1: some people want work but are not entitled to benefit, for example those whose entitlement has run out [1], so they are in the survey but not in the claimant count [1]. Reason 2: some people do not claim, because they do not know how or consider the benefit too small [1], so the count understates unemployment compared with the survey [1]. The gap is 35,000. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A reason plus its effect on the figure gains credit; a bare list of reasons usually does not. Note that "the survey is a sample" is a reason for error, not a reason for the gap to be this direction.

**4. (a)** Frictional [1].
**(b)** Structural [1].
**(c)** Cyclical [1].
**(d)** Total demand rises, so firms sell more, raise output and rehire [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* For (d), a link from higher demand to higher hiring is the point; "the economy gets better" alone repeats the question.

**5. (a)** 52 - 30 = **22 percentage points** (a rise) [1].
**(b)** 0.52 x 6,000,000 [1] = **3,120,000** [1].
**(c)** As incomes rise, households spend a larger share on services such as travel and education [1], so tertiary firms hire more workers [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing "22%" instead of 22 percentage points risks confusion because the table is already in percentages; state the unit precisely.

**6. (a)** Social attitudes change so paid work is more accepted for women [1]; more girls complete education, so more qualify for jobs [1].
**(b)** The government privatises state firms and reduces its role in production [1], so private firms employ a larger share [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each cause needs its consequence; "attitudes change" with no link to the labour force gains less than the full chain.

**7.** Cost: unemployment cuts household incomes and spending [1], so firms sell less and profits fall [1]. Benefit: a large pool of job seekers makes hiring easier [1] and holds down wage demands, lowering costs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Questions that ask for both a cost and a benefit are asking for balance; two costs would not answer it.

**8. (a)** 90,000 x (2,000 + 3,500) [1] = **495,000,000** worse for the budget [1].
**(b)** 90,000 x 25,000 = **2,250,000,000** [1].
**(c)** Labour that could make goods and services sits unused [1], so total output is below what the economy could achieve and the country sits inside its production possibility curve [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* In (a), adding only the benefit or only the tax gives half the figure; check that both effects are in the working.

**9.** Income falls for a long period, so living standards drop [1]. Skills and confidence fade, making re-employment harder [1]. Stress and ill health may increase [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Three separate harms are needed; repeating "no money" in different words counts once.

**10.** Policy 1, retraining: government funds courses in skills that firms need [1]; workers can then fill vacancies in growing industries [1], although the courses take time and not all will succeed [1]. Policy 2, help with moving costs or subsidies for firms to locate in the affected region [1]; jobs and workers are brought together [1], but it is costly and some workers will not move [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A policy aimed at demand (such as a tax cut) does not fit structural unemployment; an answer that says why it fits the cause is stronger than a list of policies.

**11. (a)** Income rise = 300,000,000 x 2.0 = 600,000,000 [1]. Jobs = 600,000,000 / 30,000 = 20,000 [1]. Unemployed = 680,000, so rate = 680,000 / 10,000,000 x 100 = **6.8%** [1].
**(b)** For: the unemployment is mainly cyclical, so a rise in demand suits the cause [1]; spending acts through the multiplier, so jobs grow beyond the first round [1]. Against: the rate falls only from 7.0% to 6.8%, so the effect is small [1]; the budget deficit rises [1]; if the economy is near capacity, prices may rise [1]. Judgement: it is appropriate for cyclical unemployment but is not enough alone, so combine it with lower interest rates or training for those who remain unemployed [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A judgement that refers back to the data (7.0% to 6.8%) is stronger than one that only says "it depends".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by employment or the population rather than the labour force.
- Missing "actively seeking work" from the definition of unemployment.
- Mixing up percentages and percentage points.
- Giving a reason for the claimant count and survey gap without linking it to the figures.
- Naming a type of unemployment with no link to the cause in the scenario.
- Making one point where the question asks for a cost and a benefit.
- Offering one policy for all types of unemployment.
- Ending an evaluation with no judgement.
