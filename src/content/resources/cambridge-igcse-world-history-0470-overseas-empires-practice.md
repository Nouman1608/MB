further practice page linked above.

## Section A

**1.** Give **two** developments in technology or medicine that made it easier for Europeans to conquer Africa. **[2]**

**2.** What was agreed at the Berlin Conference of 1884-85? **[3]**

**3.** Describe Faidherbe's actions as governor of Senegal. **[4]**

**4.** Identify **three** groups of Indians who joined the rising of 1857, with **one** grievance for each. **[3]**

**5.** Describe the Platt Amendment. **[4]**

**6.** State **three** consequences of American control for Filipinos between 1899 and 1907. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did European powers take control of so much of Africa so quickly after 1880? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did indirect rule work better in some parts of Nigeria than in others? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "The impact of European imperialism on Africans was similar in Senegal, Nigeria and the Congo." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The events of 1857 changed British rule in India more than they changed the lives of Indians." How far do you
agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a report by a Christian missionary in the Congo Free State, 1903. "The village was
empty when we arrived. The people had fled to the forest because the rubber was short this month and the soldiers
were coming. An old woman showed us her grandson's arm. The State tells Europe it brings civilisation. We have seen
what it brings."

How reliable is Source A as evidence about Leopold II's rule in the Congo? Use the source and your knowledge to
explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is also invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from a letter by a Cuban landowner to a friend in New York, 1903. "We have a flag, a
president and a constitution. We also have an American base on our coast and a clause that lets Washington send
soldiers whenever it decides we are disorderly. Tell me, my friend, is this a republic or a lodger's room in someone
else's house?"

What is the message of Source B? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: quinine against malaria (1); steamships able to travel up rivers (1); railways (1); the
telegraph (1); the machine gun, such as the Maxim gun (1); breech-loading rifles (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: European powers met in Berlin in 1884-85 (1); no African rulers were invited (1); a claim
to territory needed effective occupation (1); powers had to inform each other of new claims (1); free trade and
navigation on the Congo and Niger rivers (1); Leopold II's Congo Free State was recognised (1); powers promised to end
the slave trade (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: governor of Senegal in 1854-61 and 1863-65 (1); extended French control inland along the
Senegal River with forts (1); fought the forces of Umar Tall, including at Médine (1857) (1); created the tirailleurs sénégalais, African troops in French service (1); founded a school
for the sons of chiefs (1); encouraged groundnut production for export (1); ruled directly through French officials
(1). **[4]**

**4.** Three groups with a matching grievance, 1 mark each: sepoys, angered by the greased cartridges (1); rulers, who
lost states through the Doctrine of Lapse, such as the Rani of Jhansi (1); Nana Sahib, refused his adoptive father's
pension (1); Awadh landowners, who lost estates after 1856 (1); religious leaders or communities, who feared forced
conversion (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any four, 1 mark each: passed by the US Congress in 1901 (1); written into Cuba's constitution (1); the USA could
intervene in Cuba to keep order or protect independence (1); Cuba could not make treaties that weakened its
independence or take on excessive debt (1); the USA gained naval bases, including Guantánamo Bay (1); it limited Cuban
independence (1); it was used to justify the occupation of 1906 (1). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: independence was denied (1); war with the USA from 1899 with heavy civilian losses (1);
Aguinaldo was captured in 1901 (1); US civil government under Taft (1); public schools and teaching in English (1);
roads and public health works (1); an elected assembly from 1907, under US control (1). **[3]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **rivalry between powers**, because once Germany, France and Britain began claiming land, each rushed to take
territory before the others; the Berlin Conference's effective occupation rule encouraged occupation; **economic
demand** for raw materials and markets in industrial Europe; **technology**, because quinine, steamships and machine
guns let small forces move inland and defeat larger African armies; **strategic concerns**, such as protecting routes
to India after the opening of the Suez Canal; **missionary and public pressure** in Europe. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as for Question 7. Indicative content: **in the north** indirect rule worked because the Sokoto Caliphate
had emirs with organised courts, taxes and officials that Britain could use, and keeping Islamic law reduced
resistance; **in the south-east** there were no large kingdoms, so Britain appointed warrant chiefs without
traditional authority, who were resented and sometimes abused their power; **cost and staffing**, because Britain had too few officials to rule
directly, so where local rulers were weak or absent the system had no firm base. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Similar:** all three involved conquest and loss of sovereignty; all redirected economies to European needs through
taxes, cash crops or forced labour; all used force against resistance; Africans had no say in government. **Different:**
Senegal's assimilation gave a small elite French schooling and a few voters in the communes; Northern Nigeria kept
emirs, Islamic law and much of daily life; the Congo's rubber regime brought hostage-taking, mutilation and mass
deaths, so its impact was far more severe. **Judgement:** for example, the loss of control was similar, but the human
impact varied greatly, with the Congo the extreme. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as for Question 9. Indicative content. **Agree:** the Government of India Act (1858) replaced Company
rule with the Crown, a Secretary of State and a Viceroy; annexations stopped; the army was reorganised; Victoria
became Empress (1877). **Disagree:** Indians' lives also changed: the Mughal dynasty ended; reprisals after the rising
killed many; princely rulers kept their states; British officials became more separate from Indians; religious
reform slowed. But much stayed the same: most Indians had no political voice and India was still run for British
interests. **Judgement:** for example, the system of government changed far more than ordinary life. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): evaluates reliability using content and provenance, checked against knowledge. Level 2
(3-4): evaluates using content or provenance only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source or asserts reliability.
Indicative content: **reasons to trust it**: the missionary claims to be an eyewitness; the details (flight from
soldiers, rubber quotas, a severed arm) match other evidence, such as Casement's report of 1904 and photographs taken
by missionaries; **reasons for caution**: missionaries opposed the regime and wanted reform, so the report may be
written to persuade; it describes one village. **Judgement:** for example, reliable on the methods used, because other
independent evidence confirms them, though not on the scale of the abuses. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains the message in context, using the source and knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains
the message using the source only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the source. Indicative content: the message is that Cuba's
independence is **only partial**: it has the outward signs of a state ("a flag, a president and a constitution") but
the USA has a **base on its coast** and the **right to intervene** ("a clause that lets Washington send soldiers"), so
Cuba is like a tenant in another's house. Knowledge: the Platt Amendment (1901) was written into Cuba's constitution;
Guantánamo Bay became a US base; US forces left in 1902 but returned in 1906. **[6]**
