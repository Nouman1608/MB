## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain what is meant by the statement that cardiac muscle is myogenic. **[2]**

**2.** Which structure carries the wave of excitation from the AVN to the Purkinje fibres?

A: The sinoatrial node

B: The bundle of His

C: The vagus nerve

D: The carotid artery **[1]**

**3.** State the function of the sinoatrial node. **[1]**

**4.** An ECG trace shows an interval of 0.75 seconds between successive peaks. Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute. **[2]**

### Section B: longer questions

**5.** Describe the sequence of events that leads from the wave of excitation leaving the sinoatrial node to the contraction of the ventricles. **[5]**

**6.** Explain the importance of the delay in the transmission of the wave of excitation at the atrioventricular node. **[2]**

**7.** Explain why the wave of excitation spreads through the ventricles from the base of the heart upwards. **[2]**

**8.** During exercise, the heart rate of a person increases.

**(a)** Describe how the change in the blood during exercise leads to an increase in heart rate. **[5]**

**(b)** Explain how the heart rate returns to its resting level after exercise. **[3]**

**9.** In an investigation, the heart rate of an anaesthetised animal was recorded before and during electrical stimulation of nerves to the heart.

| Condition | Heart rate / beats per minute |
|---|---|
| Control (no stimulation) | 70 |
| Sympathetic nerve stimulated | 110 |
| Vagus nerve stimulated | 40 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in heart rate when the sympathetic nerve was stimulated, and when the vagus nerve was stimulated. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain these results. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the part of the heart that responds to the nerve impulses. **[1]**

**10.** A resting person has a heart rate of 80 beats per minute and a cardiac output of 5,200 cm3 per minute. During exercise, the heart rate is 160 beats per minute and the stroke volume is 105 cm3.

**(a)** Calculate the stroke volume at rest. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the cardiac output during exercise. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the percentage increase in cardiac output. **[2]**

**11.** An athlete has a resting heart rate of 50 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 110 cm3. A non-athlete has a resting heart rate of 65 beats per minute and a stroke volume of 75 cm3.

**(a)** Calculate the cardiac output of each person. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage by which the athlete's heart rate is lower than that of the non-athlete. **[2]**

**12.** Pressure receptors in the walls of the arteries detect a rise in blood pressure. Explain how this leads to a fall in heart rate. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** The heart muscle contracts and relaxes without a nerve or hormone signal [1]. The beat is initiated within the heart itself [1]. **[2]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** It initiates a wave of electrical activity (acts as the pacemaker) [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 60 / 0.75 [1] = 80 beats per minute [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The wave spreads across both atria, which contract [1]. A layer of non-conducting tissue prevents the wave from passing directly to the ventricles [1]. The wave reaches the atrioventricular node (AVN), which passes it on after a delay [1]. The wave travels down the bundle of His in the septum and into the Purkinje fibres [1], which carry it up the walls of both ventricles, so the ventricles contract from the apex upwards [1]. **[5]**

**6.** The delay allows the atria to finish contracting [1], so that the ventricles are filled with blood before they contract [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Contraction from the base upwards pushes blood towards the arteries at the top of the ventricles [1], so blood is emptied from the ventricles efficiently and not trapped at the bottom [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** Muscles respire faster, so more carbon dioxide is produced and the pH of the blood falls [1]. Chemoreceptors in the carotid arteries and aorta detect the change [1] and send more impulses to the cardiac centre in the medulla oblongata [1]. The centre sends more impulses along the sympathetic nerve to the SAN [1], which produces waves of excitation more frequently, so the heart rate increases [1]. **[5]**

**(b)** After exercise, respiration slows and the carbon dioxide concentration in the blood falls, so the pH rises [1]. The chemoreceptors send fewer impulses to the cardiac centre, which reduces sympathetic stimulation and increases impulses along the parasympathetic (vagus) nerve [1], so the SAN produces waves less often and the heart rate returns to the resting level (negative feedback) [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** Sympathetic: (110 - 70) / 70 [1] x 100 = +57 %. Vagus: (40 - 70) / 70 x 100 = -43 % [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The sympathetic nerve increases the heart rate and the vagus nerve (parasympathetic) decreases it [1]. They alter the frequency of waves of excitation from the SAN [1]. **[2]**

**(c)** The sinoatrial node (SAN) [1]. **[1]**

**10. (a)** 5,200 / 80 = 65 cm3 [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** 160 x 105 = 16,800 cm3 per minute [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** (16,800 - 5,200) / 5,200 [1] x 100 = 223 % [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the same units in both values before you work out the percentage.

**11. (a)** Athlete: 50 x 110 = 5,500 cm3 per minute [1]. Non-athlete: 65 x 75 = 4,875 cm3 per minute [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** (65 - 50) / 65 [1] x 100 = 23 % [1]. **[2]**

**12.** The pressure receptors send more impulses to the cardiac centre in the medulla [1]. The centre sends more impulses along the parasympathetic (vagus) nerve to the SAN [1], which reduces the frequency of the waves of excitation [1], so the heart rate falls and the blood pressure decreases [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that nerves start the heartbeat.
- Mixing up the sympathetic and parasympathetic nerves.
- Giving the AVN the job of starting the beat.
- Leaving out the SAN as the effector.
- Forgetting the units in a cardiac output calculation.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.4.8 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**Do I need to know transmitter names such as noradrenaline?**
The specification names the autonomic nervous system and its effectors. The transmitters are useful detail but are not required by the wording.
