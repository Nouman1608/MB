## 5.2.1 Causes: "why cleared, how lost"

| Cause | Why people clear | How the forest is lost |
|---|---|---|
| Logging and timber extraction | Valuable hardwood and pulp | Felling; machinery damages surrounding trees |
| Subsistence farming | Food for the family | Small plots cleared, often by cutting and burning |
| Commercial farming | Profit from cattle or plantation crops | Large areas cleared and kept in production |
| Roads and settlements | Access, homes, development | Road corridor plus clearing by settlers along it |
| Rock, ore and mineral extraction | Valuable deposits under the forest | Vegetation and soil stripped; waste rock dumped |
| Hydro-electric power stations | Electricity from flowing water | Reservoir floods the valley forest |
| Climate change | Not deliberate | Heat, drought and wildfire kill trees |

**Hook:** five causes are deliberate human choices; climate change is the indirect one.

## 5.2.2 Impacts: three chains

- **Living chain:** habitat loss → loss of biodiversity → small, isolated populations → genetic depletion.
- **Soil and water chain:** no canopy or roots → rain splash and run-off → soil erosion (desertification in dry
  areas) → soil carried into rivers → silting → flooding downstream.
- **Air chain:** burning and decay release carbon dioxide + no more uptake by photosynthesis → global warming;
  less transpiration → less water vapour → changes to rainfall patterns.

Seven impacts, three chains: if you can draw the chains from memory, you have all seven.

## 5.2.3 Definition (learn exactly)

Sustainable management of forests = **balancing the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans while
conserving forests for future generations.**

Check your version has: three needs + balance + future generations.

## 5.2.4 The need for sustainable management

Split the seven reasons into **services** and **uses**.

**Services forests give for free**
- Climate regulation: carbon sink (absorbs carbon dioxide) and carbon store (holds carbon).
- Water cycle: interception, water uptake, transpiration, less surface run-off.
- Flood control: lower, slower river flows.
- Prevention of soil erosion: roots bind soil; canopy and litter shield it.

**Uses people make of forests**
- Genetic resource: genes for future crops and medicines.
- Food, medicine and raw materials: fruit, nuts, game, medicinal plants, timber, fuelwood, rubber, fibres.
- Recreation, ecotourism and education: income, jobs, learning.

**Linking line for discuss answers:** clearing gives a one-off gain (timber, farmland, minerals, electricity);
sustainable management keeps a stream of services and uses for future generations.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Deforestation | Removal of forest so the land is used for something else or left bare |
| Logging | Felling trees for timber |
| Subsistence farming | Growing food mainly to feed the farmer's own family |
| Commercial farming | Growing crops or raising animals to sell for profit |
| Habitat loss | Destruction of the place where an organism lives |
| Biodiversity | The variety of living things in an area |
| Genetic depletion | Loss of genetic variety within a species |
| Soil erosion | Removal of topsoil by water or wind faster than it forms |
| Desertification | Land in dry areas becoming desert-like as soil and vegetation are lost |
| Silting | Deposition of eroded sediment in rivers and reservoirs |
| Interception | Rain caught by leaves and branches before reaching the ground |
| Transpiration | Loss of water vapour from leaves |
| Surface run-off | Water flowing over the ground surface |
| Carbon sink | Something that absorbs more carbon dioxide than it releases |
| Carbon store | Something that holds carbon over a long period |
| Ecotourism | Tourism to natural areas that aims to conserve them and benefit local people |

## Exam traps

- **Wrong outcome.** "Explain the need for sustainable management" wants reasons (services and uses), not
  methods. Methods belong to 5.3.
- **Half a definition.** Leaving out "future generations" or one of the three needs loses the mark.
- **Erosion without the forest.** Always say what the trees did that has now stopped (interception, roots).
- **Silting versus flooding.** Silting is the deposit; flooding is the overflow. Name both and link them.
- **Genetic depletion versus biodiversity loss.** Within one species versus number of species.
- **Unexplained global warming.** Give both halves: carbon dioxide released, and the sink lost.
- **Data questions.** Quote values with units (km², %, years) and show the subtraction before the division.
  For a percentage change, divide the change by the **original** value.

## One-line calculations to practise

- Percentage loss = (original area - new area) / original area x 100.
- Mean annual loss = area lost / number of years.
- *Invented example:* 9 000 km² falls to 7 650 km² in 15 years. Loss = 1 350 km²; percentage = 15%;
  mean annual loss = 90 km² per year.

## Self-test

1. List the six causes of deforestation named in the syllabus.
2. Which cause of deforestation is indirect, and why?
3. Explain why commercial farming usually removes forest permanently.
4. How can a mine cause deforestation beyond the edge of the pit?
5. Give the syllabus definition of sustainable management of forests.
6. Name the four parts of the water cycle that 5.2.4(b) lists.
7. Explain how deforestation leads to silting.
8. Distinguish between loss of biodiversity and genetic depletion.
9. Why does deforestation in a dry region risk desertification?
10. Give two raw materials obtained from forests.
11. Forest cover falls from 6 400 km² to 5 600 km² over 8 years. Calculate the percentage loss and the
    mean annual loss.
12. Explain one way ecotourism shows the need for sustainable management.

### Answers

1. Logging and timber extraction; subsistence and commercial farming; roads and settlements; rock, ore and
   mineral extraction; hydro-electric power stations; climate change.
2. Climate change: trees die from heat, drought or wildfire rather than being cleared on purpose.
3. The land is kept in production for crops or cattle, so the trees are not allowed to grow back.
4. Waste rock is dumped on nearby land, and roads, housing and power lines are built to serve the mine.
5. Balancing the needs of the environment, wildlife and humans while conserving forests for future
   generations.
6. Interception, water uptake, transpiration and surface run-off.
7. Without roots and canopy, soil is eroded by run-off and carried into rivers, where it is deposited.
8. Biodiversity loss is fewer species in an area; genetic depletion is less genetic variety within a species.
9. Exposed soil dries out and loses organic matter and moisture, and is eroded, so the land becomes
   desert-like.
10. Any two: timber, fuelwood, rubber, fibres.
11. Loss = 800 km²; 800 / 6 400 x 100 = 12.5%; 800 / 8 = 100 km² per year.
12. Visitors pay to see standing forest, bringing income and jobs, so the forest is worth more conserved than
    cleared.
