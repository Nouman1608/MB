These questions cover section **3.1.12.3 The ionic product of water, Kw** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 1 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Ionic product of water, Kw study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ionic-product-of-water-kw/) and the [Ionic product of water, Kw revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ionic-product-of-water-kw-revision-notes/). Use Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ at 298 K unless a different value is given. Use Mᵣ(NaOH) = 40.0.

## Questions

**1.** Write the expression for the ionic product of water, Kw, and give its units. **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the pH of pure water at 298 K, showing that [H⁺] = [OH⁻]. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the pH of 0.0040 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution at 298 K. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the pH of 0.45 mol dm⁻³ potassium hydroxide solution at 298 K. **[2]**

**5.** Calculate the pH of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ calcium hydroxide solution at 298 K, assuming it is fully dissociated. **[3]**

**6.** The pH of a sodium hydroxide solution is 12.40 at 298 K. Calculate the concentration of the solution. **[2]**

**7.** Calculate the mass of sodium hydroxide needed to make 250 cm³ of a solution of pH 11.80 at 298 K. **[3]**

**8.** The value of Kw increases as the temperature rises. Explain what this shows about the enthalpy change for the ionisation of water. **[3]**

**9.** At 50 °C, Kw = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶.

**(a)** Calculate the pH of pure water at 50 °C. **[2]**

**(b)** A student says the water has become acidic because the pH is below 7. Explain why the student is wrong. **[1]**

**10.** Calculate the pH of 0.0100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution at 50 °C, using the value of Kw in Question 9. **[2]**

**11.** 40.0 cm³ of 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide is added to 60.0 cm³ of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ hydrochloric acid at 298 K. Calculate the pH of the final mixture. **[4]**

**12.** A student calculates the pH of 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ barium hydroxide as 12.30.

**(a)** Identify the error. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct pH. **[2]**

**13.** A student uses pH + pOH = 14 to find the pH of a 0.100 mol dm⁻³ sodium hydroxide solution at 50 °C and gets 13.00. State why this method is not valid at 50 °C and calculate the correct value. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Kw = [H⁺][OH⁻] [1]; units mol² dm⁻⁶ [1]. **[2]**

**2.** In pure water [H⁺] = [OH⁻], so [H⁺]² = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ and [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **7.00** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0040 = 2.5 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **11.60** [1]. **[2]**

**4.** [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.45 = 2.22 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **13.65** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Ca(OH)₂ gives two OH⁻ per formula unit, so [OH⁻] = 0.0200 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0200 = 5.00 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.30** [1]. **[3]**

**6.** [H⁺] = 10^(–12.40) = 3.98 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [OH⁻] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 3.98 × 10⁻¹³ = **0.0251 mol dm⁻³** [1]. **[2]**

**7.** [H⁺] = 10^(–11.80) = 1.58 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³, so [OH⁻] = 6.31 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; n(NaOH) = 6.31 × 10⁻³ × 0.250 = 1.58 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; mass = 1.58 × 10⁻³ × 40.0 = **0.0631 g** [1]. **[3]**

**8.** A higher Kw means the equilibrium H₂O ⇌ H⁺ + OH⁻ has moved to the right when heated [1]; by Le Chatelier's principle, the equilibrium moves in the endothermic direction when the temperature rises [1]; so the ionisation of water is endothermic (ΔH is positive) [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** [H⁺] = √(5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴) = 2.34 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **6.63** [1]

**(b)** The water is still neutral because [H⁺] = [OH⁻]; neutral means equal concentrations, not pH 7 [1]. **[3]**

**10.** [H⁺] = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.0100 = 5.48 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **11.26** [1]. **[2]**

**11.** n(OH⁻) = 0.0500 × 0.0400 = 2.00 × 10⁻³ mol and n(H⁺) = 0.0200 × 0.0600 = 1.20 × 10⁻³ mol [1]; excess OH⁻ = 8.00 × 10⁻⁴ mol in 0.100 dm³, so [OH⁻] = 8.00 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 8.00 × 10⁻³ = 1.25 × 10⁻¹² mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **11.90** [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** The student did not double [OH⁻]; Ba(OH)₂ gives two OH⁻ ions per formula unit [1]

**(b)** [OH⁻] = 0.0400 mol dm⁻³ [1]; [H⁺] = 2.50 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ and pH = **12.60** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The answer 12.30 is what you get if you treat the base as having one hydroxide ion. Check the formula of the base first.

**13.** pH + pOH = 14 is only true when Kw = 1.00 × 10⁻¹⁴, which is true at 298 K only [1]; at 50 °C [H⁺] = 5.48 × 10⁻¹⁴ ÷ 0.100 = 5.48 × 10⁻¹³ mol dm⁻³ [1]; pH = **12.26** [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using [OH⁻] directly in the pH formula.
- Not doubling [OH⁻] for Ba(OH)₂ or Ca(OH)₂.
- Using pH + pOH = 14 at a temperature other than 298 K.
- Writing the units of Kw as mol dm⁻³.
- Saying heated water is acidic.
