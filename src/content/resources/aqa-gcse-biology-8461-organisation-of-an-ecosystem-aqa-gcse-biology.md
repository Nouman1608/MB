## Food chains and feeding relationships

**Photosynthetic organisms are the producers of biomass for life on Earth.** Every food chain begins with a
**producer**, which synthesises molecules. This is usually a green plant or alga making glucose by
photosynthesis.

```
seaweed → periwinkle → crab → gull
producer   primary     secondary  tertiary
           consumer    consumer   consumer
```

The arrows show the direction in which biomass passes: "is eaten by". Drawing them the other way is a frequent
lost mark. Consumers that kill and eat other animals are **predators**; those eaten are **prey**. The crab is
both a predator (of periwinkles) and prey (of gulls).

## Sampling: Required practical 9

The task is to **measure the population size of a common species** in a habitat and to **use sampling
techniques to investigate the effect of a factor on the distribution** of the species. Two techniques do two
different jobs:

| Technique | Answers the question | How |
|---|---|---|
| Random quadrats | How many are there? (abundance) | Lay out two tapes at right angles; use random numbers as coordinates; count in each quadrat; scale up the mean |
| Transect | How does distribution change across the habitat? | Run a tape from one area to another (for example from shade into sun); place quadrats at regular intervals; record the species and an abiotic factor at each |

Where individual plants are hard to count, such as grass or moss, record **percentage cover**: the fraction of
the quadrat covered by the species. In a quadrat divided into 100 small squares, count the squares more than
half covered.

**Worked example 1: a transect** (invented data). A transect runs 25 m from the edge of a shady wood into an open
field. Quadrats are placed every 5 m.

| Distance (m) | 0 | 5 | 10 | 15 | 20 | 25 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Light (lux) | 900 | 2100 | 5400 | 9800 | 11 000 | 11 200 |
| Dandelions per quadrat | 0 | 1 | 4 | 9 | 10 | 11 |

```
values of dandelions in order: 0, 1, 4, 9, 10, 11
mean   = (0 + 1 + 4 + 9 + 10 + 11) ÷ 6 = 35 ÷ 6 = 5.8 (2 s.f.)
median = (4 + 9) ÷ 2 = 6.5
mode   = none: no value appears more than once
```

Conclusion: dandelion numbers increase with light intensity along the transect. To make the result more
**valid**, repeat the transect at other places along the wood edge, so one unusual spot does not decide the
answer.

You must understand the terms **mean, mode and median**, calculate arithmetic means, and plot suitable graphs
with sensible scales. Here a line graph (or scatter graph) of dandelions against light, with light on the x-axis,
suits continuous data.

## Predator–prey cycles

In a stable community, predator and prey numbers **rise and fall in cycles**. The prey peak comes first:

1. Prey numbers rise, so there is more food for predators.
2. More predators survive and breed, so predator numbers rise, slightly later.
3. More prey are eaten, so prey numbers fall.
4. Less food for predators, so predator numbers fall, and the cycle repeats.

When you interpret a graph, say which population peaks first, describe the **time lag**, and explain each
change with a cause. The predator line is usually lower because there are fewer predators than prey.

## How materials are cycled

Many different materials cycle through the **abiotic and biotic components** of an ecosystem. All materials in
the living world are recycled to provide the building blocks for future organisms. You need to explain the
importance of the carbon and water cycles. You do **not** study the nitrogen cycle.

**The carbon cycle** returns carbon from organisms to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide, to be used by plants in
photosynthesis.

| Process | Carbon moves from → to |
|---|---|
| Photosynthesis | Carbon dioxide in the air → carbon compounds in plants and algae |
| Feeding | Carbon compounds in one organism → the organism that eats it |
| Respiration (plants, animals, microorganisms) | Carbon compounds → carbon dioxide in the air |
| Decay by microorganisms | Carbon in dead organisms and waste → carbon dioxide in the air |
| Combustion | Carbon in fuels and wood → carbon dioxide in the air |

**The water cycle** provides fresh water for plants and animals on land before it drains into the seas. Water
is continuously evaporated and precipitated: water evaporates from the sea and land, plants lose water vapour by
transpiration, the vapour cools and condenses into clouds, and it falls as precipitation.

**Microorganisms** cycle materials by returning **carbon to the atmosphere as carbon dioxide** (when they respire
while decaying dead material) and **mineral ions to the soil**, where plants absorb them.

## Decomposition (biology only)

You must explain how **temperature, water and availability of oxygen** affect the rate of decay:

- **Warmth** speeds up the enzymes of decomposers, up to an optimum. Very high temperatures denature them.
- **Water** is needed by microorganisms to grow and for their enzymes to work. Dry material decays slowly.
- **Oxygen** allows aerobic respiration, so microorganisms release more energy and grow faster.

Gardeners and farmers provide these optimum conditions so that waste decays quickly into **compost**, a natural
fertiliser. Turning a compost heap adds oxygen; keeping it moist but not waterlogged provides water.
**Anaerobic decay** produces **methane**; **biogas generators** use this to produce methane as a fuel.

**Worked example 2: rate of decay from a graph.** The mass of a bag of leaves falls from 120 g to 75 g between
week 2 and week 7.

```
change in mass = 120 − 75 = 45 g
time           = 7 − 2 = 5 weeks
mean rate      = 45 ÷ 5 = 9 g per week
```

On a curved graph, the rate at one moment is the gradient of a tangent. A curve that flattens shows decay
slowing as the remaining material becomes harder to break down.

### Required practical 10 (biology only)

Investigate the effect of temperature on the rate of decay of fresh milk by **measuring pH change**. Milk is made
alkaline (for example with sodium carbonate solution), and lipase is added. Lipase breaks down milk fat into fatty
acids, so the pH falls. The change is followed with a pH probe or an indicator such as phenolphthalein, which
turns from pink to colourless as the pH falls. Time how long the change takes at each
temperature in a water bath. A shorter time means a faster rate; rate can be calculated as 1 ÷ time.

## Impact of environmental change (biology only, Higher tier only)

You must **evaluate** the impact of environmental changes on the distribution of species, given information.
The changes named are **temperature**, **availability of water** and **composition of atmospheric gases**. They
may be **seasonal**, **geographic** or **caused by human interaction**.

**Annotated model answer**

*Question: Over 40 years, a dragonfly species has been recorded further north in Britain, while average spring
temperatures rose. Evaluate the claim that warming caused the change. [4]*

> The data support the claim because the range moved north as temperatures rose [1]. A warmer climate could let
> the dragonfly survive and breed in areas that were previously too cold [1]. However, this is a correlation and
> another factor, such as new ponds being dug or more people recording dragonflies, could explain it [1]. So
> warming is a likely cause, but more evidence, such as the dragonfly's temperature limits, is needed [1].

## Common mistakes

- Food chain arrows pointing from predator to prey.
- Using a transect to estimate total population size, or random quadrats to show a change in distribution.
- Placing quadrats "randomly" by throwing them; use random coordinates.
- Saying decomposers "eat" dead material without mentioning respiration releasing carbon dioxide.
- Saying decay is fastest at very high temperatures.
- Describing the nitrogen cycle, which is not required.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Describe** a sampling method: equipment, how positions are chosen, what is recorded, how many repeats.
- **Explain** a predator–prey pattern: say which changes first and give a cause for each rise and fall.
- **Calculate** a rate: change ÷ time, with the unit (g per week, pH units per minute).
- **Evaluate** (Higher tier): evidence for, evidence against or limitations, and a judgement.
