## Section A

**1.** Predict the type of polymerisation of phenylethene, C₆H₅CH=CH₂. **[1]**

**2.** Predict the type of polymerisation of 6-hydroxyhexanoic acid, HO(CH₂)₅COOH. **[1]**

**3.** State the type of polymerisation that produced the section
–CH₂CH(OCOCH₃)CH₂CH(OCOCH₃)–. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why ethanoic acid and ethane-1,2-diol cannot together form a polymer. **[1]**

## Section B

**5.** For each monomer or pair of monomers, predict the type of polymerisation, or state that no
polymer forms.

**(a)** CH₂=C(CH₃)₂ **[1]**

**(b)** H₂N(CH₂)₃COOH **[1]**

**(c)** HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH with HO(CH₂)₄OH **[1]**

**(d)** CH₂=CHCH₂NH₂ **[1]**

**(e)** C₆H₅COOH with C₆H₅NH₂ **[1]**

**6.** A section of a polymer is –CH₂CH(CONH₂)CH₂CH(CONH₂)–.

**(a)** State the type of polymerisation, giving the evidence from the section. **[2]**

**(b)** Give the structural formula of the monomer. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why this polymer is not classed as a polyamide. **[1]**

**7.** Two different polymers, **P** and **Q**, both have repeat units with formula C₃H₄O₂. **P** is made
from propenoic acid, CH₂=CHCOOH, and **Q** from 3-hydroxypropanoic acid, HOCH₂CH₂COOH.

**(a)** State the type of polymerisation for each. **[2]**

**(b)** Draw the repeat unit of each polymer. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the repeat unit of **Q** has a smaller formula than its monomer, but that of **P**
does not. **[1]**

**8.** Prop-2-en-1-ol, CH₂=CHCH₂OH, is polymerised on its own.

**(a)** State the type of polymerisation. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the repeat unit. **[1]**

**(c)** State the role of the –OH group in the polymerisation. **[1]**

**9.** Formula data are given for two polymers.

| Polymer | Formula of monomer | Formula of repeat unit |
|---|---|---|
| **R** | C₄H₆O₂ | C₄H₆O₂ |
| **S** | C₄H₈O₃ | C₄H₆O₂ |

**(a)** Deduce the type of polymerisation for each, with a reason. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest a structural formula for the monomer of **R** and for the monomer of **S**. **[2]**

**10.** A section of a polymer is –CH₂CH₂–CH₂CHCl–CH₂CH₂–CH₂CHCl–.

**(a)** State the type of polymerisation. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the two monomers. **[2]**

**11.** Hexanedioyl chloride, ClOC(CH₂)₄COCl, cannot polymerise on its own.

**(a)** Explain why. **[1]**

**(b)** State the type of compound it must react with to form a polyamide. **[1]**

**12.** A section of a polymer is –OCH(CH₃)CH₂OOC(CH₂)₂CO–OCH(CH₃)CH₂OOC(CH₂)₂CO–.

**(a)** Deduce the type of polymerisation, giving the evidence. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the two monomers. **[2]**

**13.** A student's rule says: "If a monomer contains an oxygen or nitrogen atom, it undergoes
condensation polymerisation."

**(a)** Give two monomers from this page that show the rule is wrong. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a better rule for predicting the type of polymerisation from a single monomer. **[2]**

**14.** State two ways in which the balanced equation for an addition polymerisation differs from
that for a condensation polymerisation of two monomers. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Addition (it has a C=C outside the ring) [1].

**2.** Condensation (it has –OH and –COOH in one molecule and no C=C) [1].

**3.** Addition: the backbone is all carbon and the ester groups are side groups [1].

**4.** Ethanoic acid has only one –COOH group, so after reacting it cannot link to another molecule and
the chain cannot grow (only a diester forms) [1].

**5. (a)** Addition [1].

**(b)** Condensation [1].

**(c)** Condensation (polyester) [1].

**(d)** Addition [1].

**(e)** No polymer: each molecule has only one reactive group (an amide forms at most) [1].

**6. (a)** Addition [1]; the backbone contains only carbon atoms, with –CONH₂ as side groups [1].

**(b)** Propenamide, CH₂=CHCONH₂ [1].

**(c)** The amide groups are side groups, not links in the backbone; a polyamide has –CONH– links
joining the units [1].

**7. (a)** **P**: addition [1]; **Q**: condensation [1].

**(b)** **P**: –CH₂CH(COOH)– [1]; **Q**: –OCH₂CH₂CO– [1].

**(c)** Forming **Q** removes H₂O at each link, so its repeat unit is the monomer minus H₂O; addition
loses nothing, so **P**'s repeat unit keeps the monomer's formula [1].

**8. (a)** Addition [1].

**(b)** –CH₂CH(CH₂OH)– [1].

**(c)** It takes no part: it is a side group on the polymer chain [1].

**9. (a)** **R**: addition, because the repeat unit and monomer have the same formula [1]; **S**:
condensation, because the repeat unit is the monomer minus H₂O [1].

**(b)** **R**: an alkene such as methyl propenoate, CH₂=CHCOOCH₃ (accept any C₄H₆O₂ monomer with a
C=C, such as CH₃COOCH=CH₂) [1]; **S**: 4-hydroxybutanoic acid, HO(CH₂)₃COOH (accept any C₄H₈O₃
molecule with one –OH and one –COOH) [1].

**10. (a)** Addition [1].

**(b)** Ethene, CH₂=CH₂ [1]; chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl [1].

**11. (a)** Both of its reactive groups are –COCl, which cannot react with each other to form links
[1].

**(b)** A diamine [1].

**12. (a)** Condensation: the backbone contains ester (–COO–) links [1].

**(b)** Propane-1,2-diol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂OH [1]; butanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₂COOH (or butanedioyl
chloride) [1].

**13. (a)** Any two monomers with a C=C that also contain O or N, such as propenoic acid,
propenamide, prop-2-en-1-ol or CH₂=CHCH₂NH₂ (both needed) [1].

**(b)** If the monomer has a C=C (outside a benzene ring), it undergoes addition polymerisation [1];
if it has no C=C but has two groups that can form ester or amide links (for example –OH and –COOH,
or –NH₂ and –COOH), it undergoes condensation polymerisation [1].

**14.** Any two [2]: the addition equation has no small-molecule product, while the condensation
equation shows 2n H₂O or 2n HCl; the addition equation has one monomer (n of it) while the
condensation equation of this kind has n of each of two monomers; the addition repeat unit has the
same formula as the monomer, while the condensation repeat unit has fewer atoms than the two
monomers combined.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling a monomer with a C=C and a –COOH or –CONH₂ group a condensation monomer.
- Treating a benzene ring as a C=C that can undergo addition.
- Calling a polymer with side-chain ester or amide groups a polyester or polyamide.
- Predicting a polymer from molecules that have only one reactive group each.
- Stating the type without giving the evidence (backbone atoms, links, or formula comparison) when
  the question says "giving the evidence".
- Drawing an addition repeat unit with the side group inside the backbone.
- Assuming that two polymers with the same repeat unit formula must be made the same way.
