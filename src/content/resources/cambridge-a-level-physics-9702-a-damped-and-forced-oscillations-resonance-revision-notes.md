Subtopic 17.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers damping caused by resistive forces, the terms light, critical and heavy damping with their displacement–time sketches, and resonance as the maximum-amplitude response of a system forced at its natural frequency. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Damped and forced oscillations, resonance study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-damped-and-forced-oscillations-resonance/); to apply the ideas, try the [Damped and forced oscillations practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-damped-and-forced-oscillations-resonance-practice/).

## Damping in one chain

1. A **resistive force** acts opposite to the velocity (air resistance, viscous drag, friction, electromagnetic braking).
2. It does negative work every cycle, transferring energy out of the oscillator (to internal energy of the surroundings).
3. Total energy E = ½mω²x₀² falls.
4. So the **amplitude decreases**: this is damping.

**Energy and amplitude:** E ∝ x₀². Amplitude × 0.5 means energy × 0.25. Amplitude falling by 10% means energy falling by 19%.

## The three degrees of damping

| Type | Oscillates? | Return to equilibrium | Sketch feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Light | Yes | Amplitude decays gradually | Constant period; peaks shrink smoothly inside a decaying envelope |
| Critical | No | **Shortest possible time** without overshooting | Falls to zero, never crosses the axis |
| Heavy | No | Slower than critical | Falls more slowly than critical, never crosses the axis |

## Sketch checklist (displacement–time)

- Same starting displacement for every curve.
- Zero gradient at t = 0 (released from rest).
- Light: evenly spaced zero crossings (period unchanged), gradually shrinking peaks above and below the axis.
- Critical: below light's first swing, no crossing, levels off at zero quickest.
- Heavy: above the critical curve all the way, no crossing.
- Label each curve.

## Free and forced oscillations

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Free oscillation | Oscillation with no external periodic force, at the natural frequency |
| Natural frequency f₀ | The frequency of free oscillation, set by the system (mass, stiffness, length) |
| Forced oscillation | Oscillation driven by an external periodic force; occurs at the **driving** frequency |
| Driver | The source of the periodic force |
| Resonance | Maximum amplitude, when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency |

## Resonance explained

- At f_driving = f₀, the driving force acts in the direction of motion through each cycle, so the **energy transferred per cycle is greatest**.
- Amplitude grows until **energy lost per cycle (damping) = energy supplied per cycle (driver)**.
- With no damping the amplitude would keep growing; damping limits it.

## Effect of damping on the amplitude–frequency curve

- Peak **lower**.
- Peak **broader** (less sharp).
- Peak shifts slightly to **lower** frequency.
- Amplitude **smaller at every** driving frequency.
- Far from f₀, all the curves are low; well above f₀ the amplitude tends to zero.

## Examples

| Situation | Damping or resonance point |
|---|---|
| Car suspension, door closer, analogue meter pointer | Critical damping: settles quickly without overshoot |
| Pendulum swinging in air | Light damping |
| Barton's pendulums | The pendulum matching the driver's length resonates |
| Washing machine on spin-up | Violent shaking as drum speed passes the natural frequency |
| Swing pushed in time | Useful resonance |
| Radio tuning | Useful resonance of a circuit at the station frequency |
| Bridge or building driven by wind or footsteps | Harmful resonance; cured by damping or changing f₀ |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Damping | Loss of energy and amplitude from an oscillating system caused by resistive forces |
| Resistive force | A force opposing the velocity of the oscillating object |
| Light damping | Oscillations continue with gradually decreasing amplitude |
| Critical damping | Return to equilibrium in the shortest time without oscillation |
| Heavy damping | Return to equilibrium without oscillation, more slowly than critical |
| Resonance | Maximum amplitude of a forced oscillation, when driven at the natural frequency |

## Exam traps

- "Critical damping: no oscillation" without "shortest time" (heavy damping also has no oscillation).
- Drawing the critical curve crossing the axis.
- Drawing the light-damping period changing as the amplitude falls.
- Heavy damping shown reaching zero first.
- "Resonance is when frequency is maximum": it is the **amplitude** that is maximum.
- Saying a forced system oscillates at its own natural frequency.
- Saying damping moves the resonant peak to a higher frequency.

## Self-test

1. What causes damping?
2. Where does the energy lost by a damped oscillator go?
3. The amplitude of an oscillator falls from 40 mm to 20 mm. What fraction of the energy remains?
4. Define critical damping.
5. How does heavy damping differ from critical damping?
6. On a lightly damped displacement–time graph, what stays constant?
7. At what frequency does a forced system oscillate once steady?
8. State the condition for resonance.
9. Why does the resonant amplitude not grow without limit?
10. State three effects of increasing damping on a resonance curve.
11. A spring system has a natural frequency of 5.0 Hz. At what rotation rate, in rev min⁻¹, would an unbalanced motor on it cause resonance?
12. Give one useful and one harmful example of resonance.

**Answers:** 1. A resistive force opposing the motion. 2. Mainly to internal energy of the surroundings (and the oscillator). 3. (20/40)² = 1/4. 4. Return to equilibrium in the shortest possible time without oscillating. 5. Heavy damping also has no oscillation, but the return to equilibrium is slower. 6. The period (zero crossings evenly spaced). 7. The driving frequency. 8. Driving frequency equals the natural frequency, giving maximum amplitude. 9. Energy lost per cycle to damping rises with amplitude until it equals the energy supplied per cycle by the driver. 10. Lower peak; broader peak; peak at a slightly lower frequency (also smaller amplitude at all frequencies). 11. 5.0 × 60 = 300 rev min⁻¹. 12. Useful: pushing a swing, radio tuning. Harmful: a bridge swaying in wind or under footsteps.
