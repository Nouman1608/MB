## Section A

**1.** Where does the Krebs cycle take place in a eukaryotic cell?

A: cytoplasm

B: intermembrane space

C: inner mitochondrial membrane

D: mitochondrial matrix **[1]**

**2.** Which molecule accepts the acetyl group from acetyl coenzyme A at the start of the Krebs cycle?

A: citrate

**3.** State **two** products of the link reaction, other than acetyl coenzyme A. **[2]**

**4.** Name the **two** types of reaction in the Krebs cycle that release carbon dioxide and that reduce coenzymes. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **D** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**2.** **B** [1]. Total: 1 mark.

**3.** Carbon dioxide [1]; reduced NAD [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**4.** Decarboxylation [1]; dehydrogenation [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**5.** Final electron acceptor at the end of the electron transport chain [1]; combines with electrons and protons to form water [1]. Total: 2 marks.

**6.** Glucose is phosphorylated using 2 ATP [1]; forming fructose 1,6-bisphosphate (6C) [1]; which splits into two triose phosphates (3C) [1]; triose phosphate is oxidised, reducing NAD [1]; and converted to pyruvate (3C), with ATP produced by substrate-linked phosphorylation, net 2 ATP per glucose [1]. Total: 5 marks.

**7.** Reduced NAD and FAD deliver hydrogen to carriers in the inner mitochondrial membrane [1]; hydrogen atoms split into protons and electrons [1]; electrons pass along the electron transport chain, releasing energy [1]; energy is used to transfer protons into the intermembrane space, creating a proton gradient [1]; protons flow back into the matrix by facilitated diffusion through ATP synthase, providing energy to make ATP from ADP and Pi (chemiosmosis) [1]; oxygen accepts electrons and protons to form water [1]. Total: 6 marks.

**8.** Glycolysis: 0 CO₂, 2 reduced NAD [1]; link reaction: 2 CO₂, 2 reduced NAD [1]; Krebs cycle: 4 CO₂, 6 reduced NAD [1]; one glucose gives two pyruvate, so the link reaction (and Krebs cycle) occurs twice [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**9.** Any four from: inner membrane folded into cristae gives a large surface area for electron transport chains and ATP synthase; inner membrane impermeable to protons except via ATP synthase, so the proton gradient is maintained; narrow intermembrane space allows a high proton concentration to build up; matrix contains enzymes for the link reaction and Krebs cycle; matrix contains DNA and ribosomes to make some mitochondrial proteins; outer membrane allows pyruvate to enter [4]. Total: 4 marks.

**10.** Pyruvate is decarboxylated to ethanal, releasing CO₂ [1]; ethanal is reduced to ethanol by reduced NAD [1]; this regenerates NAD [1]; so NAD is available to accept hydrogen in glycolysis, which continues to make ATP [1]. Total: 4 marks.

**11. (a)** 1000 ÷ 200 = **5.0** [1].

**(b)** Rate increases as glucose concentration increases from 0 to 2.0 % [1]; more substrate available for dehydrogenases, so more frequent enzyme–substrate collisions [1]; rate levels off between 2.0 and 4.0 % [1]; another factor, such as the number of enzyme molecules (or yeast cells), becomes limiting [1].

**(c)** Yeast respires its own stored carbohydrate (glycogen), which provides a small amount of substrate [1].

**(d)** Any two from: temperature; volume and concentration of yeast suspension; volume and concentration of DCPIP; total volume of the mixture; time the yeast was left before adding DCPIP [2]. Total: 8 marks.

**12.** Aerenchyma (tissue with large air spaces) in stems and roots lets oxygen diffuse down to submerged roots [1]; roots carry out ethanol fermentation and tolerate high ethanol concentrations [1]; stems grow faster as water rises, keeping leaves above the surface for gas exchange [1]. Total: 3 marks.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Placing stages in the wrong part of the mitochondrion.
- Counting the link reaction and Krebs cycle once per glucose.
- Saying ATP synthase pumps protons.
- Omitting NAD regeneration from fermentation answers.
- Giving time instead of rate.
