These questions follow OCR GCSE (9-1) Mathematics J560, Version 2.1 (August 2026), section 10.01, Units and measurement (10.01a to 10.01c). Every question is for **both tiers**. Section A has short questions on single skills; Section B has longer problems that combine them. Try Questions 1 to 7 without a calculator. All prices, exchange rates and places are invented.

Learn the methods first in the [10.01 study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-units-and-measurement/) or the [10.01 revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-mathematics-j560-units-and-measurement-revision-notes/). ## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Convert each quantity.

**(a)** 4.8 km into centimetres. **[1]**

**(b)** 52 000 cm² into square metres. **[1]**

**(c)** 0.9 m³ into litres. **[1]**

**2.** Times.

**(a)** Write 3.35 hours in hours and minutes. **[1]**

**(b)** A bus leaves at 09:52 and the journey takes 1 hour 47 minutes. At what time does it arrive? **[1]**

**3.** Convert a speed of 15 m/s into km/h. Show your working. **[2]**

**4.** A block of wood has mass 360 g and volume 450 cm³.

**(a)** Find its density in g/cm³. **[2]**

**(b)** Write this density in kg/m³. **[1]**

**5.** The bearing of P from Q is 112°. Find the bearing of Q from P. **[1]**

**6.** Write each answer as an expression.

**(a)** A roll of tape is m metres long. It is cut into strips 40 cm long. How many strips are there? Simplify your answer. **[2]**

**(b)** A car travels at s km/h for 20 minutes. How far does it travel, in km? **[2]**

**7.** Rice is sold in two packs. Pack A holds 1.5 kg and costs £3.30. Pack B holds 400 g and costs 92p. Which pack is better value? Show how you decide. **[3]**

### Section B: longer questions

**8.** Jo runs 6 km in 32 minutes, then walks a further 2 km at 5 km/h.

**(a)** How many minutes does Jo spend walking? **[2]**

**(b)** Find Jo's average speed for the whole 8 km, in km/h, to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**9.** A solid block is a cuboid measuring 0.6 m by 0.4 m by 0.3 m. Its weight is a force of 840 N. It can rest on any face.

**(a)** Find the greatest pressure the block can exert on the floor, in N/m². **[3]**

**(b)** Write your answer to part (a) in N/cm². **[1]**

**10.** A silver bar is a cuboid 18 cm long, 5 cm wide and 3 cm high. Silver has density 10.5 g/cm³.

**(a)** Find the mass of the bar in kilograms. **[3]**

**(b)** Silver is valued at £0.80 per gram. Find the value of the bar. **[1]**

**11.** Before a holiday Sam changes £450 into euros at £1 = €1.18.

**(a)** How many euros does Sam receive? **[1]**

**(b)** Sam spends €386.40. On return, the rest is changed back into pounds at £1 = €1.20. How many pounds does Sam receive? **[2]**

**12.** A map has a scale of 1 : 40 000.

**(a)** Two farms are 7.5 cm apart on the map. Find the real distance between them in kilometres. **[2]**

**(b)** A footpath is 1.8 km long. How long is it on the map, in centimetres? **[2]**

**(c)** Complete the statement: "1 cm on the map represents ___ m." **[1]**

**13.** Town B is 10 km due north of town A. A radio mast T is on a bearing of 060° from A and on a bearing of 120° from B.

**(a)** Find the sizes of angle TAB and angle TBA. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why triangle ABT is equilateral, and state the distance AT. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the bearing of A from T. **[1]**

**(d)** Find the bearing of B from T. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 4.8 × 1000 × 100 = **480 000 cm** [1]

**(b)** 52 000 ÷ 10 000 = **5.2 m²** [1]

**(c)** 0.9 × 1000 = **900 litres** [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* In part (b) the factor is 100², not 100. Writing "1 m² = 10 000 cm²" before you divide shows the method.

**2. (a)** 0.35 × 60 = 21, so **3 hours 21 minutes** [1]

**(b)** 09:52 + 1 h = 10:52; 10:52 + 47 min = **11:39** [1]

Total: 2

*Examiner insight:* 3.35 hours is not 3 hours 35 minutes. The decimal part is a fraction of 60 minutes.

**3.** 15 m in 1 s is 15 × 3600 = 54 000 m in 1 hour [1]; 54 000 m = **54 km/h** [1]

Total: 2

*Examiner insight:* The short cut × 3.6 earns full marks only if the working is shown; on a non-calculator paper write 15 × 3.6 = 54.

**4. (a)** density = mass ÷ volume = 360 ÷ 450 [1]; **0.8 g/cm³** [1]

**(b)** 0.8 × 1000 = **800 kg/m³** [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* A density below 1 g/cm³ is sensible for wood, which floats in water. A quick check like this catches an upside-down division.

**5.** 112° + 180° = **292°** [1]

Total: 1

**6. (a)** m metres = 100m cm, so number of strips = 100m ÷ 40 [1]; **5m/2** (or 2.5m) [1]

**(b)** 20 minutes = 1/3 hour [1]; distance = s × 1/3 = **s/3 km** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* Convert the units before forming the expression. Writing m ÷ 40 mixes metres and centimetres and earns no marks.

**7.** Pack A: 330p ÷ 1500 g = 0.22 p per gram [1]

Pack B: 92p ÷ 400 g = 0.23 p per gram [1]

**Pack A** is better value because it costs less per gram [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* Put both packs into the same units (pence and grams) first. Grams per penny is also accepted: 4.55 g per penny for A against 4.35 g per penny for B.

**8. (a)** time = distance ÷ speed = 2 ÷ 5 = 0.4 hours [1]; 0.4 × 60 = **24 minutes** [1]

**(b)** total time = 32 + 24 = 56 minutes = 56/60 hours [1]; average speed = 8 ÷ 56/60 = 8 × 60/56 [1]; = 8.571… = **8.57 km/h** [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* Total distance ÷ total time is the only correct method. The running speed is 11.25 km/h, but averaging 11.25 and 5 gives 8.125, which is wrong.

**9. (a)** The greatest pressure comes from the smallest face: 0.4 × 0.3 = 0.12 m² [1]; pressure = 840 ÷ 0.12 [1]; = **7000 N/m²** [1]

**(b)** 7000 ÷ 10 000 = **0.7 N/cm²** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* For a fixed force, a smaller area gives a larger pressure. Saying why you chose the smallest face makes the method clear.

**10. (a)** volume = 18 × 5 × 3 = 270 cm³ [1]; mass = 10.5 × 270 = 2835 g [1]; = **2.835 kg** [1]

**(b)** 2835 × 0.80 = **£2268** [1]

Total: 4

*Examiner insight:* The price is per gram, so use the mass in grams in part (b), not the 2.835 from part (a).

**11. (a)** 450 × 1.18 = **€531** [1]

**(b)** euros left = 531 − 386.40 = 144.60 [1]; 144.60 ÷ 1.20 = **£120.50** [1]

Total: 3

*Examiner insight:* Changing euros back to pounds divides by the rate. Multiplying would give £173.52, more pounds than euros, which cannot be right when £1 is worth more than €1.

**12. (a)** 7.5 × 40 000 = 300 000 cm [1]; = 3000 m = **3 km** [1]

**(b)** 1.8 km = 180 000 cm [1]; 180 000 ÷ 40 000 = **4.5 cm** [1]

**(c)** 40 000 cm = **400 m** [1]

Total: 5

*Examiner insight:* Real to map divides; map to real multiplies. Converting to centimetres first keeps both directions consistent with the ratio.

**13. (a)** AB points due north from A, so angle TAB = **60°** [1]. From B, A is on a bearing of 180°, so angle TBA = 180° − 120° = **60°** [1]

**(b)** The angles of the triangle add to 180°, so angle ATB = 180° − 60° − 60° = 60°; all three angles are 60°, so the triangle is equilateral [1]. **AT = 10 km** [1]

**(c)** Back bearing of 060°: 060° + 180° = **240°** [1]

**(d)** Back bearing of 120°: 120° + 180° = **300°** [1]

Total: 6

*Examiner insight:* A sketch with a north line at A and at B makes the angles in part (a) clear. An accurate scale drawing at 1 cm to 2 km would show AB, AT and BT all 5 cm long, confirming part (b).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 100 instead of 10 000 to convert between m² and cm².
- Treating the decimal part of an hour as minutes.
- Averaging two speeds instead of dividing total distance by total time.
- Using a time in minutes with a speed in km/h.
- Dividing a force by an area in the wrong units.
- Comparing best buys without putting both packs into the same units, or without a conclusion.
- Multiplying when changing money back, or dividing when changing it out.
- Measuring a bearing from the wrong point, or giving only two figures.
