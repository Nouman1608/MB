These notes condense content item 2.5.3, Effective training and development, from Theme 2 Topic 2.5 Making human resource decisions in the Pearson Edexcel GCSE (9-1) Business (1BS0) specification, Issue 2, July 2022. Worked examples are in the [training and development study guide](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-effective-training-and-development/), and the [training and development practice questions](/resources/edexcel-gcse-business-1bs0-effective-training-and-development-practice/) test the content. All businesses mentioned are invented.

## The item at a glance

- **How:** formal training, informal training, self-learning, ongoing training for all employees, target setting, performance reviews.
- **Why:** the link between training, motivation and retention; retraining to use new technology.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Training | Teaching the skills needed for the present job |
| Development | Building a person's skills and abilities for future roles and responsibility |
| Formal training | Planned, structured training, often away from the workstation and sometimes leading to a qualification |
| Informal training | Learning while working, for example by watching or being coached by a colleague |
| Self-learning | The employee directs their own learning, for example through online modules or manuals |
| Ongoing training for all employees | Training continues throughout employment for everyone, not only new starters |
| Target setting | Agreeing specific goals an employee should reach by a set time |
| Performance review | A meeting where a manager and employee review progress against targets and plan next steps |
| Motivation | The desire to work hard and do the job well |
| Retention | Keeping employees rather than losing them to other businesses |
| Retraining | Teaching existing staff new skills, for example to use new technology |

## Methods compared

| Method | Main strength | Main weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Formal | Consistent; recognised qualification | Fees; staff away from work |
| Informal | Cheap; learned on the real job | Depends on the trainer; bad habits passed on |
| Self-learning | Low cost; own pace | Needs self-discipline; hard to check |
| Ongoing for all | Everyone stays up to date | Continuing cost and time |
| Targets | Clear expectations; direction | Unrealistic targets cause stress |
| Performance reviews | Feedback; finds skill gaps | Managers' time; can feel critical |

**Choosing:** safety or legal skill → formal. Hands-on task → informal. Self-motivated staff, reading-based knowledge → self-learning. Fast-changing business → ongoing. Progress needs direction → targets and reviews.

**The cycle:** set targets → work and support → review against targets → training to close gaps → new targets.

## Why train: the chain

Training → skills and confidence + feeling valued → **motivation** ↑ → fewer leave → **retention** ↑ → lower recruitment costs and experience kept.

Limits: trained staff may be poached by rivals paying more; the cost may not be recovered if staff leave soon after training; time spent training is time not producing.

**Retraining for new technology:** keeps existing staff useful when methods change; avoids the cost of replacing them; makes sure the business gets the benefit of its investment; reduces staff worry about their jobs.

## Useful calculations (standard methods)

- Percentage of staff leaving = leavers in the year ÷ number of staff × 100.
- Cost of training = cost per person × number trained (+ the cost of staff time if given).
- Net benefit = savings from fewer leavers − cost of training.

A change from 25% to 15% is a fall of 10 **percentage points**, not "10%".

## Applying the notes to a case

Pick out four facts before recommending a method: what must be learned, how many staff need it, how quickly, and the budget. Then finish the chain to a reason from the specification. For example: "informal coaching on the new till is cheap and learned on the real job, so staff become confident quickly, which raises motivation and makes them less likely to leave." In an Evaluate answer, weigh the cost of fees and staff time against savings from fewer leavers, and add the risk that trained staff are poached.

## Exam traps

- Naming "training" without saying which method.
- Treating informal training as free: experienced staff lose working time.
- Saying training always keeps staff. Pay and conditions elsewhere matter too.
- Swapping target setting and performance reviews.
- Using "percent" when you mean percentage points.
- Forgetting the second half of the specification heading: give the *why*, not only the *how*.

## Self-test

1. What is the difference between training and development?
2. Give one example of formal training.
3. Give one weakness of informal training.
4. Why is self-learning difficult for a business to monitor?
5. What does "ongoing training for all employees" mean?
6. What happens at a performance review?
7. State the link between training and retention in one sentence.
8. Give one reason a business retrains staff to use new technology.
9. A business has 60 staff and 9 leave in a year. Calculate the percentage leaving.
10. Training costs £150 per person for 32 staff. Calculate the total cost.
11. Leavers fall from 30% to 22%. Describe the change correctly.
12. Give one risk of training staff to a high standard.

**Answers**

1. Training builds skills for the present job; development builds the person for future roles.
2. Any one: an external course; a college day-release programme; a classroom session leading to a certificate.
3. Any one: depends on the colleague teaching; bad habits passed on; takes the trainer away from their own work.
4. The employee learns alone, so the business cannot easily see what has been learned.
5. Every employee, new or long-serving, keeps receiving training throughout their employment.
6. Manager and employee compare performance with targets, give feedback, find skill gaps and agree new targets.
7. Training makes employees more skilled and valued, raising motivation, so they are more likely to stay.
8. Any one: so staff can use the new equipment; so the business benefits from its investment; to avoid replacing staff.
9. 9 ÷ 60 × 100 = **15%**.
10. 32 × £150 = **£4,800**.
11. A fall of 8 percentage points.
12. Trained staff may be recruited by rivals offering higher pay.
