## Questions

**1.** Differentiate y = (2x + 1)e^(−3x), giving your answer in a fully factorised form. **[3]**

**2.** Differentiate y = e^(2x)/(x − 1) and simplify. **[4]**

**3.** Find dy/dx when y = sin³ 4x. **[3]**

**4.** Given f(x) = x ln(x² + 1), find the exact value of f′(1). **[5]**

**5.** A solid metal cylinder is heated so that it expands, with its height always equal to its radius r mm. The radius increases at 0.01 mm per second. Find, in exact form, how fast the volume is increasing at the instant when r = 20. **[5]**

**6.** A curve has equation x = y⁵ + 3y.

**(a)** Find dx/dy. **[1]**
**(b)** Hence write down dy/dx in terms of y. **[1]**
**(c)** Evaluate the gradient where y = 1, which is the point (4, 1). **[1]**

**7.** A curve has equation 2x² + xy + y³ = 4. The point P(1, 1) lies on the curve.

**(a)** Find an expression for dy/dx. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the gradient of the curve at P. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the equation of the normal at P in the form ax + by + c = 0, where a, b and c are integers. **[2]**

**8.** A curve has parametric equations x = t² + 1, y = 2t³ − 3t.

**(a)** Find dy/dx in terms of t. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the normal to the curve at t = 1, in the form ax + by + c = 0 with integer a, b and c. **[3]**

**9.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 + 3 sin θ, y = 1 − 2 cos 2θ. Show that dy/dx = (8/3) sin θ, and find the gradient when θ = π/6. **[4]**

**10.** A tank is empty at time t = 0 minutes. A tap adds water at 0.5 m³ per minute. Water also leaks out at a rate proportional to the volume V m³ in the tank. When V = 8 the leak rate is 0.4 m³ per minute.

**(a)** Show that dV/dt = 0.5 − 0.05V, explaining how you find the constant. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that V = 10 − 10e^(−0.05t) satisfies the equation and the initial condition. **[3]**
**(c)** State the volume the tank approaches in the long term. **[1]**

**11.** A spherical raindrop of radius r mm evaporates so that its volume decreases at a rate proportional to its surface area. For a sphere, V = (4/3)πr³ and S = 4πr².

**(a)** Show that dr/dt is a negative constant. Call it −λ. **[4]**
**(b)** The radius is 2 mm at t = 0 and 1.5 mm when t = 10 seconds. Find λ and the time for the drop to vanish. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the rate of change of volume when r = 1, giving your answer to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** u = 2x + 1 and v = e^(−3x), so u′ = 2 and v′ = −3e^(−3x) [1]
dy/dx = 2e^(−3x) − 3(2x + 1)e^(−3x) [1]
= **−(6x + 1)e^(−3x)** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* write the product rule in full before substituting. If the derivative of e^(−3x) is wrong, the working still shows the rule was understood.

**2.** u = e^(2x), v = x − 1, u′ = 2e^(2x), v′ = 1 [1]
dy/dx = ( (x − 1)·2e^(2x) − e^(2x)·1 ) / (x − 1)² [1]
= e^(2x)(2x − 2 − 1)/(x − 1)² [1]
= **e^(2x)(2x − 3)/(x − 1)²** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the minus sign and the order of the terms in the numerator are where most slips happen. Bracket v′ and u′ terms so the sign is clear.

**3.** Write y = (sin 4x)³ and let u = sin 4x [1]
dy/dx = 3(sin 4x)² × 4 cos 4x [1]
= **12 sin² 4x cos 4x** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the chain rule is needed twice here. Say which function is the inside each time.

**4.** u = x, v = ln(x² + 1), u′ = 1, v′ = 2x/(x² + 1) [1]
f′(x) = x · 2x/(x² + 1) + 1 · ln(x² + 1) [1]
= 2x²/(x² + 1) + ln(x² + 1) [1]
f′(1) = 2/2 + ln 2 [1]
= **1 + ln 2** (about 1.69) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "exact value" means leave ln 2 in the answer. A decimal alone does not show the exact form.

**5.** Height equals radius, so V = πr²·r = πr³ [1]
dV/dr = 3πr², which is 1200π at r = 20 [1]
dV/dt = (dV/dr)(dr/dt) [1]
dV/dt = 1200π × 0.01 [1]
= **12π mm³ per second** (about 37.7) [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* write the volume in terms of r alone before differentiating. Differentiating with r and h both present is a common route to a wrong answer.

**6. (a)** dx/dy = 5y⁴ + 3 [1]
**(b)** dy/dx = **1/(5y⁴ + 3)** [1]
**(c)** At y = 1, dy/dx = 1/8, so the gradient is **1/8** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the reciprocal relationship needs dx/dy with y as the variable. Do not substitute x = 4 into an expression in y.

**7. (a)** Differentiate 2x² + xy: 4x + y + x(dy/dx) [1]
Differentiate y³: 3y²(dy/dx), and the right-hand side is 0 [1]
dy/dx = **−(4x + y)/(x + 3y²)** [1]
**(b)** At (1, 1): −5/4 [1]
**(c)** Normal gradient 4/5 [1]
y − 1 = (4/5)(x − 1), so **4x − 5y + 1 = 0** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* the product rule on xy is the step most often missed. Check that your final line has integer coefficients if the question asks for them.

**8. (a)** dx/dt = 2t and dy/dt = 6t² − 3 [1]
dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt) [1]
= **(6t² − 3)/(2t)** [1]
**(b)** At t = 1: x = 2, y = −1, gradient 3/2 [1]
Normal gradient −2/3 [1]
y + 1 = −(2/3)(x − 2), so **2x + 3y − 1 = 0** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* find the point from both parametric equations. The gradient alone does not locate the normal.

**9.** dx/dθ = 3 cos θ and dy/dθ = 4 sin 2θ [1]
dy/dx = 4 sin 2θ/(3 cos θ) [1]
= 8 sin θ cos θ/(3 cos θ) = **(8/3) sin θ** [1]
At θ = π/6: (8/3)(1/2) = **4/3** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, state the identity sin 2θ = 2 sin θ cos θ. Without it the step is unexplained.

**10. (a)** The leak is kV, so 0.4 = k × 8 and k = 0.05 [1]
Net rate = inflow minus outflow [1]
dV/dt = **0.5 − 0.05V** [1]
**(b)** dV/dt = 10 × 0.05e^(−0.05t) = 0.5e^(−0.05t) [1]
Right-hand side: 0.5 − 0.05(10 − 10e^(−0.05t)) = 0.5e^(−0.05t), so both sides agree [1]
At t = 0, V = 10 − 10 = 0, which matches the empty tank [1]
**(c)** As t increases, e^(−0.05t) approaches 0, so V approaches **10 m³** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in a verification question, show both sides separately and state that they agree. Do not forget the initial condition.

**11. (a)** dV/dr = 4πr² [1]
dV/dt = (dV/dr)(dr/dt) = 4πr²(dr/dt) [1]
Model: dV/dt = −μ · 4πr², with μ > 0 [1]
Dividing by 4πr² gives dr/dt = −μ, a constant, so λ = μ [1]
**(b)** The radius changes at a constant rate, so r = 2 − λt [1]
1.5 = 2 − 10λ, so **λ = 0.05** [1]
The drop vanishes when 2 = 0.05t, so after **40 seconds** [1]
**(c)** dV/dt = 4π(1)²(−0.05) = **−0.628 mm³ per second** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* the minus sign should come from "decreases" in the model. Cancelling 4πr² is the step that makes the constant visible, so show it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the minus sign or reversing the numerator in the quotient rule.
- Leaving out the derivative of the inside function in a chain rule question.
- Dropping the product rule when differentiating xy implicitly.
- Not writing dy/dx beside the derivative of a y term.
- Dividing dx/dt by dy/dt in a parametric question.
- Using the tangent gradient for the normal, or forgetting to take the negative reciprocal.
- Differentiating a volume with two variables instead of eliminating one first.
- Forgetting the sign of a rate when a quantity is decreasing.
- Leaving a modelling answer without units.
