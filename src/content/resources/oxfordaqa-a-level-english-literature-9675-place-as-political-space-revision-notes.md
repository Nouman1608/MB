Condensed recall for one angle named in the 9675 Unit 2 content: "place as a political space". It
supports the prose essay explained in the
[Unit 2 Section A study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-unit-2-section-a/).
The full explanation and a model paragraph are in the
[place as a political space study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-9675-place-as-political-space/).

## The idea in one box

- A place is political when the text shows **who controls it, who belongs and who is excluded**.
- "Political" includes empire, migration, property, class hierarchy and race, not only government.
- The specification links this angle to "how people are placed in society".
- Unit 2 is **open book**: choose your passages in advance and use the exam time for analysis.

## Five questions for any setting

| Question | Look for |
|---|---|
| Who owns it? | Deeds, sales, inheritance, developers, companies |
| Who governs it? | Police, army, company, landlord, embassy, council |
| Who may enter? | Doors, gates, queues, visas, checkpoints, colour lines |
| Who names it? | Maps, signs, official titles, histories |
| What does it stand for? | A room or building as a nation, empire or market |

## Methods that politicise place

- **Juxtaposition**: rich beside poor, metropolis beside colony.
- **Institutional detail**: forms, letters, uniforms, company names.
- **Movement and restriction**: journeys, deportation, refused entry, waiting.
- **Viewpoint**: a narrator blind to the politics of the place creates irony.
- **Symbolic objects**: maps, flags, keys, title deeds, fences.

## Novel by novel

| Novel | Political place | Angle worth arguing |
|---|---|---|
| *Heart of Darkness* | Company city, river stations, the coloured map | Does the novella critique empire or repeat its view of Africa? |
| *The Great Gatsby* | East Egg, the Wilsons' garage | Old money defending its position in racial and class terms |
| *Americanah* | Lagos under military rule, American cities, undocumented London | Race and papers as forces that place people |
| *Small Island* | Queenie's house, 1948 London, the wartime cinema | The "mother country" as a place that refuses its children |
| *Last Man in Tower* | Vishram Society, Mumbai | Property money turning neighbours into a mob |
| *The Remains of the Day* | Darlington Hall, Moscombe | Private service to public politics; who should have a voice |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning | Use it to say |
|---|---|---|
| Political space | A place shaped by power, control and exclusion | How setting carries authority |
| Threshold | A door, gate or border crossing | Where power decides who enters |
| Displacement | Being moved or driven from a place | How power acts on bodies |
| Colonial space | Land controlled by a foreign power | How empire reshapes place and people |
| Metropolis | The ruling city of an empire or nation | The centre against the periphery |
| Gentrification or redevelopment | Replacement of residents by wealthier use | Money as political force in a city |
| Unreliable narrator | A narrator whose account the reader questions | Irony about a place's politics |
| Microcosm | A small world standing for a larger one | A building as a nation |

## Context that earns its place

- **Empire and the Congo** (*Heart of Darkness*): link to the map, the stations, the report.
- **Achebe's 1975 lecture "An Image of Africa"**: a reading to weigh, not a fact to state.
- **Post-war migration to Britain** (*Small Island*): link to lodging, work and the house.
- **Appeasement in the 1930s** (*The Remains of the Day*): link to the dinners at the Hall.
- **Urban development in Mumbai** (*Last Man in Tower*): link to the offer and the building.

## Exam traps

- Replacing analysis of place with historical background.
- Treating "political" as only parliaments and elections.
- Missing the narrator's limited view of the place's politics.
- Discussing power with no specific location named.
- Long open-book quotations that crowd out analysis.

## Self-test

1. Quote the phrase from the 9675 Unit 2 content that names this angle.
2. Give the five questions for reading a place politically.
3. Name two methods that make a place political.
4. What does the map in the Company office suggest in *Heart of Darkness*?
5. What did Achebe argue in "An Image of Africa"?
6. How does Ifemelu's identity change when she moves to America?
7. What happens to Obinze in London?
8. Why is Queenie's house a contested space in *Small Island*?
9. What offer sets the plot of *Last Man in Tower* in motion?
10. What order does Stevens carry out regarding two maids, and why does it matter?
11. Define microcosm and give an example from one novel.
12. Why is viewpoint important for this angle?

## Answers

1. "Place as a political space".
2. Who owns it; who governs it; who may enter; who names it; what it stands for.
3. Any two: juxtaposition; institutional detail; movement and restriction; viewpoint; symbolic
   objects.
4. That the continent is a European possession, divided and coloured by empire.
5. That the novella denies Africans a voice and uses Africa as a backdrop, so it shares the racism of
   empire.
6. She finds herself categorised as black, a political identity she did not carry in Nigeria.
7. He lives and works without legal papers and is deported.
8. Her neighbours object to her Jamaican tenants, so her private house becomes a site of conflict
   about race and belonging.
9. A developer offers residents of Vishram Society much more than their flats are worth if all agree
   to leave.
10. He dismisses two Jewish maids on Lord Darlington's order; it shows the politics of the house and
    Stevens's refusal to judge it.
11. A small world standing for a larger one; for example, Vishram Society as a miniature state with
    factions and pressure.
12. A narrator may not see or admit the politics of a place, so the reader's ironic distance becomes
    part of the meaning.
