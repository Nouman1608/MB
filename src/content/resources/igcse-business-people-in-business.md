This guide covers **Topic 2 People in business**, for Cambridge IGCSE
Business Studies 0450, 2026 series (Version 2 — no significant changes
affecting teaching from the prior version). Business Studies at 0450 is
not tiered: all candidates study the same content.

## Where this fits in 0450

People in business is the second of six topics in 0450, following
Understanding business activity and preceding Marketing, Operations
management, Financial information and decisions, and External
influences on business activity. Cambridge's own syllabus overview
describes this section's focus as the functional area of human
resources: how a workforce is motivated, how a business is organised
and managed, how employees are recruited, selected and trained, and how
information moves effectively within and beyond the business. Where
Topic 1 introduced businesses in the abstract — their purpose,
classification and objectives — Topic 2 is where the syllabus turns to
the people who actually run them, and the organisational choices that
later topics assume are already understood, since a Marketing or
Operations question can just as easily ask about who is responsible for
a decision as what the decision itself should be.

**A note on this syllabus's validity.** 0450 last examines in 2026.
Cambridge replaces it with 0264 ("Cambridge IGCSE Business") from 2027
onward, which changes the assessment-objective weightings from
40/20/25/15 to 35/30/22/13, reduces each Paper 1 question from five
parts to four, and revises the subject content. Some sub-topics keep
their numbers (for example 1.1 Business activity and 3.2 Market
research); others are renamed, reordered, split or newly added (for
example 4.2 Technology and production of goods and services and 4.3
Sustainable production of goods and services are new), so the numbering
does not always match. Always check which
syllabus code your own examination series uses.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE BUSINESS STUDIES 0450 — TOPIC 2 PEOPLE IN BUSINESS**

- 2.1 Motivating employees — why people work and what motivation
means; the benefits of a well-motivated workforce, including labour
productivity and reduced absenteeism and labour turnover; the concept
of human needs, such as Maslow's hierarchy, and the key motivational
theories of Taylor and Herzberg; methods of motivation, split between
financial rewards (wage, salary, bonus, commission and profit sharing)
and non-financial methods (job enrichment, job rotation, teamworking,
training and opportunities for promotion); and recommending and
justifying appropriate methods of motivation in given circumstances.
- 2.2 Organisation and management — drawing, interpreting and
understanding simple organisational charts, including hierarchical
structures such as levels of hierarchy, span of control and chain of
command (for example, tall versus short structures), and the roles,
responsibilities and inter-relationships of directors, managers,
supervisors and other employees; the role of management, covering the
functions of planning, organising, coordinating, commanding and
controlling, and the importance of delegation as a balance between
trust and control; leadership styles, including the features of
autocratic, democratic and laissez-faire styles and recommending and
justifying an appropriate style in given circumstances; and what a
trade union is and the effects on employees of union membership.
- 2.3 Recruitment, selection and training of employees — recruitment
and selection methods, the difference between internal and external
recruitment, the main stages in recruiting and selecting employees, and
recommending and justifying who to employ in given circumstances,
alongside the benefits and limitations of part-time versus full-time
employees; the importance of training to a business and to employees,
and the benefits and limitations of induction training, on-the-job
training and off-the-job training; why reducing the size of the
workforce might be necessary, including the difference between
dismissal and redundancy, situations such as automation or reduced
product demand that might require downsizing, and recommending and
justifying which employees to make redundant in given circumstances;
and legal controls over employment issues — contracts, unfair
dismissal, discrimination, health and safety and the legal minimum wage
— and their impact on employers and employees.
- 2.4 Internal and external communication — why effective communication
matters to a business and the methods used to achieve it, including the
benefits and limitations of different communication methods (including
those based on information technology) and recommending and justifying
which method to use in given circumstances; and demonstrating awareness
of communication barriers, how they arise, the problems ineffective
communication causes, and how such barriers can be reduced or removed.

This topic rewards precision about who does what and why, so build a
clear map of the organisational hierarchy — director, manager,
supervisor, other employees — before trying to answer questions about
delegation or leadership style, since several question types ask
candidates to place a described role correctly or to explain how
authority flows between levels. The "recommend and justify" wording
appears five separate times across this topic's outcomes (motivation
methods, leadership style, who to employ, redundancy decisions and
communication method), which signals that Cambridge examiners
specifically test the skill of applying a general concept to a
described business scenario rather than reciting definitions in the
abstract — practise answering with the scenario's own details (company
size, product, workforce, budget) rather than a generic answer that
could apply to any business. Maslow, Taylor and Herzberg are named
individually in the syllabus, so candidates should be able to identify
each theory's core idea and use it correctly when explaining why a
particular motivation method might or might not work for a given
workforce, rather than treating "motivation theory" as one
undifferentiated block. Similarly, keep financial and non-financial
motivators as two clearly separate lists in revision, since a
question may ask you to identify one of each for a given
scenario. For recruitment and training, the recurring exam skill is
weighing benefits against limitations (internal versus external
recruitment, part-time versus full-time staff, the three training
types) rather than simply listing features, so practise writing balanced
short answers that state a benefit and a limitation for the same option
side by side. Finally, keep an eye on the overlap between this topic
and Topic 1's business-organisation content: a question about
delegation or span of control can quietly assume you already know
whether the business in the scenario is a sole trader, a partnership or
a limited company, since ownership structure shapes how much formal
hierarchy a business is likely to have in the first place — a useful
reminder that Cambridge's six topics are meant to be read as a
connected whole rather than six isolated revision lists.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which motivation theorists are named in the 0450 syllabus?**
Maslow, Taylor and Herzberg are named individually. You should be able to state each theory's core idea and use it to explain why a motivation method might or might not suit a given workforce.

**What is the difference between dismissal and redundancy?**
Dismissal is when an employee is removed because of their own conduct or performance. Redundancy is when the job role itself is no longer needed, for example because of automation or reduced product demand, and is not the employee's fault.

**What are the financial and non-financial methods of motivation?**
Financial rewards are wage, salary, bonus, commission and profit sharing. Non-financial methods are job enrichment, job rotation, teamworking, training and opportunities for promotion, and it helps to revise them as two separate lists.

**What are the functions of management?**
The 0450 syllabus lists planning, organising, coordinating, commanding and controlling, along with the importance of delegation as a balance between trust and control.

**Where does "recommend and justify" appear in People in business?**
It appears across five outcomes: motivation methods, leadership style, who to employ, which employees to make redundant and which communication method to use. Answer with the scenario's own details, such as company size, workforce and budget.
