Condensed notes for **2.1 Costs and cost behaviour** (2.1.1 Materials and labour), Cambridge AS & A Level Accounting 9706, 2026–2028 series. AS Level content. Figures are invented; amounts in dollars.

## The cost ladder

| Layer | Made of |
|---|---|
| Prime cost | Direct materials + direct labour + direct expenses |
| Production cost | Prime cost + production overheads (indirect materials, labour, expenses) |
| Total cost | Production cost + administration, selling and distribution overheads |

- **Direct** = economically traceable to one cost unit. **Indirect** = shared, so it becomes an overhead.
- Direct expense examples: royalty per unit, hire of equipment for one job, subcontract work on one job.
- Indirect examples: factory supervisor, lubricants and cleaning materials, factory rent and insurance.
- Finished goods inventory is valued at **production cost**, never total cost.

## Cost behaviour

| Type | Total cost | Per unit | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed | Constant within the relevant range | Falls as output rises | Factory rent |
| Variable | Proportional to output | Constant | Raw materials |
| Semi-variable | Fixed part + variable part | Falls as output rises | Power with a standing charge |
| Stepped | Constant, then jumps | Falls, then rises at each step | Supervisors, one per shift |

**High-low method** for a semi-variable cost:

1. Pick the highest and lowest **activity** levels (not costs).
2. Variable cost per unit = change in cost ÷ change in activity.
3. Fixed cost = total cost at either point − (activity × variable rate).
4. Forecast = fixed cost + (new activity × variable rate), valid only inside the observed range.

Mini check: 500 units cost 3,900 and 900 units cost 5,500. Variable = 1,600 ÷ 400 = 4.00; fixed = 3,900 − 2,000 = 1,900; at 700 units, 1,900 + 2,800 = 4,700.

**Stepped cost rule:** steps needed = output ÷ capacity per step, **rounded up**. 2,100 units with one supervisor per 800 units needs 3 supervisors.

## Labour

- **Time rate**: hours paid × rate. Simple, but pays for slow work as well as fast.
- **Piece rate**: units × rate per unit, often with a guaranteed minimum. Rewards output but can lower quality.
- **Overtime premium** (the extra above basic) is an overhead, unless a customer's rush job caused it, when it is charged to that job.
- **Idle time** is paid but unproductive, so it is charged to overheads.
- Direct labour = productive hours × basic rate.

## Materials and inventory valuation

| Method | Issues priced at | Closing inventory | Rising prices | Falling prices |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| FIFO | Oldest prices first | Newest prices | Higher profit | Lower profit |
| AVCO periodic | One average for the whole period | Period average | Between the two | Between the two |
| AVCO perpetual | Average recalculated after every receipt | Latest running average | Between the two | Between the two |

- Perpetual and periodic FIFO always give the **same** closing inventory.
- Perpetual and periodic AVCO usually **differ**, because the timing of issues changes the average used.
- Cost of issues = opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory. Use it to check any record.
- Over the life of the business total profit is the same; only the split between periods changes.

## Just in time

Principles: materials arrive as production needs them; small, frequent deliveries; few, reliable suppliers; production pulled by demand; strong quality control.

| Benefits | Drawbacks |
|---|---|
| Lower storage and insurance costs | Lost bulk discounts |
| Less cash tied up in inventory | More orders and deliveries, so higher ordering costs |
| Less waste, theft and obsolescence | One late or faulty delivery can halt production |
| Space freed for production | Heavy reliance on suppliers and accurate forecasts |

Decision method: holding cost saved − discounts lost − extra ordering or delivery costs, then non-financial factors, then a recommendation.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cost unit | The unit of product or service to which costs are charged |
| Direct cost | A cost traced to a cost unit |
| Overhead | An indirect cost that cannot be traced to a single cost unit |
| Prime cost | Total of direct costs |
| Relevant range | Output levels over which the assumed cost behaviour holds |
| Semi-variable cost | A cost with both a fixed and a variable element |
| Stepped cost | A fixed cost that rises in jumps at set activity levels |
| Overtime premium | Pay above the basic rate for overtime hours |
| Idle time | Paid time in which no production takes place |
| Perpetual inventory | A record updated after each receipt and issue |
| Periodic inventory | Inventory valued once at the end of the period |
| JIT | Receiving materials only when production needs them |

## Exam traps

- Using the highest and lowest costs in the high-low method when another month has a higher or lower activity level.
- Rounding a stepped cost calculation down: 2.1 supervisors means 3, not 2.
- Treating the whole overtime payment as an overhead instead of only the premium.
- Dividing total goods available by units purchased only, leaving out opening inventory, in periodic AVCO.
- Not recalculating the average after a receipt, or recalculating it after an issue (an issue does not change the average).
- Saying FIFO "gives higher profit" without stating that prices are rising.
- Evaluating JIT on cost alone: a recommendation needs non-financial factors such as supplier reliability.

## Self-test

**1.** Classify a royalty of 0.80 paid for every unit made.
**2.** Which layer of cost is used to value finished goods inventory?
**3.** Fixed costs are 24,000. What is the fixed cost per unit at 3,000 and at 4,000 units?
**4.** 1,000 units cost 7,000 and 1,600 units cost 9,400. Find the variable cost per unit and the fixed cost.
**5.** Using Question 4, forecast the cost of 1,300 units.
**6.** One technician (3,200 a month) is needed per 600 units or part. What does 1,450 units cost?
**7.** A worker is paid 50 hours at 12.00, including 8 overtime hours at time-and-a-half. What is the overtime premium?
**8.** Why is idle time charged to overheads?
**9.** Opening 100 units at 4.00; bought 300 at 5.00; issued 250. Find closing inventory by periodic AVCO.
**10.** Using Question 9, find closing inventory by FIFO.
**11.** Why do perpetual and periodic FIFO always agree?
**12.** Give one financial and one non-financial drawback of JIT.

### Answers

**1.** A direct expense (variable).
**2.** Production cost.
**3.** 8.00 a unit at 3,000; 6.00 a unit at 4,000.
**4.** Variable = 2,400 ÷ 600 = 4.00; fixed = 7,000 − 4,000 = 3,000.
**5.** 3,000 + (1,300 × 4.00) = 8,200.
**6.** 1,450 ÷ 600 = 2.4, so 3 technicians: 9,600.
**7.** 8 × 6.00 = 48.
**8.** No cost unit benefits from it, so it cannot be traced to production.
**9.** Total 400 + 1,500 = 1,900 for 400 units, average 4.75; 150 × 4.75 = 712.50.
**10.** The newest 150 units at 5.00 = 750.
**11.** Under FIFO the units left are always the most recent purchases, whatever the timing of issues.
**12.** Financial: lost bulk discounts or higher delivery costs. Non-financial: production stops if a supplier is late.
