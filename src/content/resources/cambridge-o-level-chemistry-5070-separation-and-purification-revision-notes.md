These notes condense section 12.4, Separation and purification, of the Cambridge O Level Chemistry 5070 syllabus (2026–2028). All three outcomes are required for every candidate. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [separation and purification study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-separation-and-purification/); then try the [separation and purification practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-chemistry-5070-separation-and-purification-practice/).

## The three outcomes in one line each

1. **Describe and explain** separation using a suitable solvent, filtration, crystallisation, simple distillation and fractional distillation.
2. **Suggest** a suitable technique from information about the substances.
3. **Identify** substances and **assess purity** from melting point and boiling point data.

## Five methods on one page

| Method | Use it for | Key apparatus and detail |
|---|---|---|
| Suitable solvent | separating a soluble solid from an insoluble one | stir (warm); then filter |
| Filtration | undissolved solid from liquid | filter funnel and paper; residue stays, filtrate passes |
| Crystallisation | pure solid from its solution | evaporate to near saturation; cool; filter; wash with cold water; dry |
| Simple distillation | pure solvent from a solution | thermometer at side arm; condenser water in at bottom |
| Fractional distillation | miscible liquids with different boiling points | fractionating column; lowest boiling point distils first |

## Crystallisation: the reasons

- Evaporate **some** solvent: makes the hot solution nearly saturated.
- Cool: solubility falls, so crystals form.
- Do not evaporate to dryness: impurities would remain in the solid; hydrated crystals would lose water of crystallisation.
- Wash with **cold** distilled water: removes solution on the surface without dissolving much of the crystals.
- Dry between filter papers or in a warm place.

## Distillation: the reasons

| Detail | Reason |
|---|---|
| Thermometer bulb at side arm | measures the temperature of the vapour that is distilling |
| Water in at the bottom of the condenser | condenser fills completely with cold water |
| Anti-bumping granules | smooth boiling |
| Fractionating column | many condensations and re-evaporations; separates liquids with close boiling points |
| Temperature constant, then rises | one fraction finished; change the receiver |

## Choosing a method

```
Solid in a liquid, undissolved?  ──► filtration
Solid dissolved, want the solid? ──► crystallisation
Solid dissolved, want the liquid? ──► simple distillation
Two miscible liquids?            ──► fractional distillation
Two solids?                      ──► solvent that dissolves one, then filter
Dissolved coloured substances?   ──► chromatography
```

## Purity from melting and boiling points

| Pure substance | Impure substance |
|---|---|
| sharp melting point | melts over a range |
| melting point matches data value | melting point lower than data value |
| sharp boiling point | boils over a range |
| boiling point matches data value | boiling point usually higher than data value |

To identify an unknown: compare a sharp measured value with data-book values.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Residue | substance remaining after filtration, evaporation or distillation |
| Filtrate | liquid or solution that has passed through a filter |
| Distillate | liquid collected after condensing the vapour |
| Saturated solution | maximum concentration of solute at a specified temperature |
| Miscible liquids | liquids that mix completely, forming one layer |
| Fraction | a portion of a mixture collected over a particular boiling range |
| Pure substance | a single substance with nothing mixed with it |

## Exam traps

- Filtration does **not** remove dissolved substances.
- Crystallisation ≠ evaporation to dryness.
- Thermometer bulb at the **side arm**, not in the liquid.
- Condenser water: **in at the bottom, out at the top**.
- Impurities **lower** the melting point and spread it over a range.
- Two solids cannot be filtered apart until one is dissolved.
- Fractional distillation, not simple distillation, for ethanol and water.
- Wash crystals with **cold** water and only a little of it.

## Self-test

1. Which method separates sand from salt solution?
2. Which method obtains pure water from sea water?
3. Which method separates ethanol from water?
4. Why is the solution cooled during crystallisation?
5. Why are crystals washed with cold, not hot, distilled water?
6. Where should the thermometer bulb be placed during distillation, and why?
7. Why does the cooling water enter the condenser at the bottom?
8. A substance melts between 112 °C and 117 °C. The pure substance melts at 121 °C. What does this show?
9. Suggest how to separate a mixture of two solids, X (soluble in water) and Y (insoluble in water).
10. A colourless liquid from a fermentation mixture boils sharply at 78 °C. Suggest its identity and what the sharp value shows.
11. Why does fractional distillation need a fractionating column?
12. State one reason why a hydrated salt should not be obtained by evaporating to dryness.

## Answers

1. Filtration.
2. Simple distillation.
3. Fractional distillation.
4. The solute is less soluble at a lower temperature, so crystals form.
5. Cold water dissolves less of the crystals while removing the solution on their surface.
6. Level with the side arm, to measure the temperature of the vapour that is being distilled.
7. So that the condenser fills completely with cold water and cools the vapour efficiently.
8. The substance is impure: it melts over a range, below the pure value.
9. Add water and stir to dissolve X; filter to leave Y as the residue; crystallise X from the filtrate.
10. Ethanol (boiling point 78 °C); the sharp value suggests it is pure.
11. The column gives a large surface on which vapour repeatedly condenses and re-evaporates, so liquids with close boiling points separate.
12. It would lose its water of crystallisation (or: soluble impurities would be left in the solid).
