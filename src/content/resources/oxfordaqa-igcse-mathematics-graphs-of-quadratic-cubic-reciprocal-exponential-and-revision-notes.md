## Shapes at a glance

| Equation | Picture in words | Remember |
|---|---|---|
| y = ax² + bx + c, a > 0 | U-shape | Minimum; y-intercept c |
| y = ax² + bx + c, a < 0 | ∩-shape | Maximum; y-intercept c |
| y = x³ | S-shape rising | Through the origin |
| y = −x³ | S-shape falling | Through the origin |
| y = 1/x, x ≠ 0 | Two branches | Quadrants 1 and 3; no contact with the axes |
| y = kˣ, k > 1 (Extension only) | Rising curve | Passes (0, 1); y > 0 always |
| y = kˣ, 0 < k < 1 (Extension only) | Falling curve | Passes (0, 1); y > 0 always |
| y = sin x, cos x (Extension only) | Wave | Range −1 to 1; period 360° |
| y = tan x (Extension only) | Repeating with breaks | Period 180°; breaks at 90°, 270° and so on |

## Quadratics: features and method

### Method for features (Core)

1. y-intercept: set x = 0.
2. Roots: set y = 0 and factorise (or read the crossing points from a graph).
3. Axis of symmetry: x = (root₁ + root₂)/2.
4. Turning point: substitute the axis x-value into the equation.
5. Direction: sign of the x² coefficient.

### Worked reminder

y = x² + 6x + 8 factorises as (x + 2)(x + 4), so the roots are −2 and −4. The axis is x = (−2 + −4)/2 = −3. Then y = 9 − 18 + 8 = −1, so the minimum is **(−3, −1)**. The y-intercept is 8.

### Number of roots

| What the graph does | Roots |
|---|---|
| Crosses the x-axis twice | Two different roots |
| Touches the x-axis at the turning point | One repeated root, for example y = (x − 3)² |
| Stays wholly above or below the axis | No real roots |

### Solving f(x) = k graphically

Add the line y = k, parallel to the x-axis. The x-values where it meets the curve are the solutions. Two solutions are the same distance from the axis of symmetry, so if you know one you can find the other: x₂ = 2 × (axis) − x₁.

## Completing the square (Extension only)

| Form | Turning point | Type |
|---|---|---|
| y = (x − p)² + q | (p, q) | Minimum |
| y = q − (x − p)² | (p, q) | Maximum |

Method for x² + bx + c: halve b, write (x + b/2)², subtract (b/2)², then add c.

Worked reminder: x² − 10x + 21 = (x − 5)² − 25 + 21 = (x − 5)² − 4. The minimum is **(5, −4)**. Setting it to zero: (x − 5)² = 4, x − 5 = ±2, so the roots are 3 and 7.

Check: if q is positive on a minimum curve, or negative on a maximum curve, the curve never reaches the x-axis.

## Cubics

- Factorised form shows the roots: y = (x − a)(x − b)(x − c) crosses at a, b and c.
- The y-intercept is found by setting x = 0 in the factorised form. For y = (x − a)(x − b)(x − c) it is −abc.
- Positive x³ coefficient: rises on the right and falls on the left. Negative: the reverse.
- Test one x-value between each pair of roots to see whether the curve is above or below the axis.
- y = x³ is rotationally symmetrical about the origin, so the points (2, 8) and (−2, −8) are both on it.

### Worked reminder

Sketch y = (x + 1)(x − 3)(x − 5). The roots are −1, 3 and 5. At x = 0 the value is 1 × (−3) × (−5) = 15, so the y-intercept is 15. The x³ coefficient is positive, so the curve starts low on the left, crosses at −1, rises to a peak above the axis, crosses at 3, dips below the axis and crosses again at 5.

## Reciprocal graph

- y = k/x: two separate branches, never touching either axis.
- Multiply: xy = k at every point.
- Positive k: quadrants 1 and 3. Negative k: quadrants 2 and 4.
- Symmetrical about y = x (positive k).

### Sketch checklist

- Axes drawn and labelled, with the intercepts marked as numbers.
- Smooth curve with no straight segments.
- Correct end behaviour (check the sign of the highest power).
- The turning point marked, if the question asks for one.

## Exponentials (Extension only)

- y = kˣ with k > 0.
- Always passes through (0, 1).
- Always above the x-axis.
- To find k, substitute a known point: if (2, 49) is on the curve, k² = 49, so k = 7.
- Negative powers are reciprocals: 3⁻² = 1/9.

## Trigonometric graphs (Extension only)

| Function | Period | Value at 0° | Features |
|---|---|---|---|
| sin x | 360° | 0 | Maximum 1 at 90°, minimum −1 at 270° |
| cos x | 360° | 1 | Minimum −1 at 180°, maximum 1 at 360° |
| tan x | 180° | 0 | Breaks at 90° and 270°; zero at 0°, 180° and 360° |

### Finding all solutions in 0° to 360°

```
sin x = k   first angle a, second angle 180° − a
cos x = k   first angle a, second angle 360° − a
tan x = k   first angle a, second angle a + 180°
(add 360° for the next turn if the range is bigger)
```

Worked reminder: sin x = 0.8 between 0° and 180°. The inverse sine gives 53.1°, and the second is 180° − 53.1° = **126.9°**.

For angles of any size, take away or add 360° (or 180° for tan) until the angle is in the 0° to 360° range, or use the repeating pattern of the graph.

## Must-know distinctions

- **Root vs intercept vs turning point.** A root is on the x-axis. The y-intercept is on the y-axis. The turning point is where the curve changes direction. They are three separate things.
- **y = x² vs y = x³.** x² is even and has a turning point. x³ is odd and has none.
- **y = 1/x vs y = kˣ.** In 1/x the variable is the base, with two branches. In kˣ the variable is the power, with one curve through (0, 1).
- **Period of sin and cos vs tan.** 360° for sin and cos, 180° for tan.

## Quick self-test

1. For y = x² − 14x + 40, find the roots and the turning point.
2. What does the graph of y = x² − 6x + 9 do at the x-axis?
3. (Extension only) Write y = x² + 4x + 7 in the form (x + p)² + q. Does the curve meet the x-axis?
4. y = 20/x. Find y when x = 4 and when x = −5.
5. (Extension only) y = 3ˣ. Find y when x = −2 and when x = 3.
6. (Extension only) Write down sin 150° and sin 450°.
7. (Extension only) Solve sin x = 0.8 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 180°. Give answers to 1 decimal place.
8. Write down the y-values of y = x³ at x = 2 and x = −2.
9. A cubic has equation y = x(x − 5)(x + 1). State its roots and find y when x = 2.
10. Find the axis of symmetry and turning point of y = 4x² − 24x.
11. (Extension only) State the period of y = tan x.

### Answers

1. Factorise: (x − 4)(x − 10) = 0, so the roots are **4 and 10**. Axis x = 7, and y = 49 − 98 + 40 = −9. Turning point **(7, −9)**.
2. It is a perfect square, (x − 3)². The curve **touches the axis at (3, 0)**, a repeated root.
3. (x + 2)² + 3, since (x + 2)² = x² + 4x + 4. The minimum is (−2, 3), above the axis, so it **does not meet the x-axis**.
4. y = 5 and y = −4.
5. y = 1/9 and y = 27.
6. sin 150° = **0.5** and sin 450° = sin 90° = **1**.
7. x = **53.1°** and x = 180° − 53.1° = **126.9°**.
8. **8 and −8**.
9. Roots **0, 5 and −1**. At x = 2: 2 × (−3) × 3 = **−18**.
10. 4x(x − 6) = 0 gives roots 0 and 6. Axis **x = 3**, and y = 36 − 72 = −36. Turning point **(3, −36)**.
11. **180°**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing a parabola as a V, or joining points with ruled segments.
- Taking the sign wrong in a completed square, so (x + 4)² + 1 is given a turning point at x = 4.
- Giving a turning point as a single number. A turning point is a coordinate pair.
- Reading a root from a curve that only touches the axis as if it crossed twice.
- Plotting y = 1/x with a line linking the two branches, or a curve running into the y-axis.
- Showing an exponential graph dropping below the x-axis, or leaving out the point (0, 1).
- Giving only one solution of a trig equation when the interval contains two.
- Leaving a sketch without labelled intercepts when the question asks for them.
- Writing a rounded decimal for a turning point when an exact form such as 3 ± √5 is easy to get.
