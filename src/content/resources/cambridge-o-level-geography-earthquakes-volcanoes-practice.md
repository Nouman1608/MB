## Section A

**1.** Distinguish between the *focus* and the *epicentre* of an earthquake.
**[2]**

**2.** Name **three** features found in a volcano. **[3]**

**3.** State **two** differences between oceanic and continental crust.
**[2]**

**4.** Name the type of plate boundary where two plates slide past each
other, and explain why volcanoes do not form there. **[2]**

**5.** Define the term *lahar*. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table gives **illustrative information** about two earthquakes of
similar magnitude.

| | Earthquake X | Earthquake Y |
|---|---|---|
| Magnitude | 6.8 | 6.9 |
| Depth of focus | 10 km | 80 km |
| Distance from nearest large city | 15 km | 120 km |
| Country income level | Low | High |

**(a)** Suggest **three** reasons why Earthquake X is likely to cause more
deaths than Earthquake Y. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how buildings can be designed to reduce damage in
earthquakes. **[3]**

**7.** A cross-section shows the Nazca Plate moving towards the South
American Plate, an ocean trench offshore and a line of volcanoes on land.

**(a)** Name this type of plate boundary. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain how the volcanoes on land have formed. **[3]**
**(c)** Suggest why this boundary produces explosive eruptions. **[2]**

**8.** Compare the characteristics of a shield volcano and a strato-volcano
(composite cone). **[4]**

**9.** Many people live close to active volcanoes.

**(a)** Explain **two** opportunities that volcanoes offer people. **[4]**
**(b)** Describe **two** ways that the impact of a future eruption could be
reduced. **[2]**

**10.** For a named earthquake you have studied, describe its effects on
people and the environment, and explain why these effects were so severe.
**[7]**

## Answers

**1.** The focus is the point underground where the rock fractures and the
earthquake starts [1]; the epicentre is the point on the Earth's surface
directly above the focus [1].

**2.** Any three of: magma chamber [1]; main vent [1]; secondary vent [1];
crater [1]; layers of ash and lava [1]; lava flow [1]; ash cloud [1].

**3.** Any two of: oceanic crust is thinner [1]; oceanic crust is denser [1];
oceanic crust can be subducted, while continental crust cannot [1]; oceanic
crust is mainly basalt, continental mainly granite [1].

**4.** Conservative boundary [1]. Crust is neither created nor destroyed, so
no magma is produced to rise to the surface [1].

**5.** A mudflow of volcanic ash and water (from rain, melted snow or ice)
flowing rapidly down a volcano's slopes [1].

**6. (a)** Any three of: Earthquake X had a much shallower focus (10 km
compared with 80 km), so less energy was lost before the waves reached the
surface [1]; its epicentre was much closer to a large city (15 km compared
with 120 km), so more people were exposed to strong shaking [1]; in a
low-income country buildings are less likely to be earthquake-resistant [1];
emergency services, hospitals and preparation are likely to be weaker [1].
**(b)** Up to 3 marks for explained design features: deep foundations or
rubber shock absorbers that absorb ground movement [1]; steel cross-bracing
that holds the frame together while allowing it to sway [1]; flexible gas and
water pipes that bend rather than break, reducing fires and flooding [1];
lightweight roofs and shatter-proof glass that reduce injuries [1].

**7. (a)** Destructive (convergent) boundary [1].
**(b)** The denser oceanic Nazca Plate is subducted beneath the less dense
continental South American Plate [1]; as it descends into the mantle, it
melts because of heat and friction, forming magma [1]; the magma is less dense
than the surrounding rock, so it rises through cracks in the crust and erupts
at the surface [1].
**(c)** The magma is thick and viscous, rich in silica and gas [1], so gas
cannot escape easily and pressure builds until it is released violently [1].

**8.** Up to 4 marks for comparative points: a shield volcano has gentle
slopes and a wide base, whereas a strato-volcano is steep-sided and tall [1];
a shield volcano is built from layers of runny lava, whereas a strato-volcano
has alternating layers of ash and lava [1]; shield eruptions are frequent and
gentle, whereas strato-volcano eruptions are less frequent but explosive [1];
shield volcanoes form at constructive boundaries or hotspots, whereas
strato-volcanoes form at destructive boundaries [1].

**9. (a)** Two explained opportunities, 2 marks each: **fertile soils** —
weathered ash and lava release minerals, so farmers can grow crops such as
grapes and fruit [2]; **geothermal energy** — heat from underground is used
to generate electricity and heat homes, as in Iceland [2]; **tourism** —
visitors come to see craters, lava and hot springs, creating jobs [2];
**minerals** — sulphur and building stone can be extracted [2].
**(b)** Any two of: monitoring ground swelling, small earthquakes and gas
emissions to give warning [1]; evacuation plans and exclusion zones [1];
hazard maps to restrict building in high-risk areas [1]; barriers or channels
to divert lava flows [1].

**10.** Levels-style marking:
- **Level 3 (6–7 marks):** a named earthquake with date and location; effects
  on both people and the environment with specific detail; clear explanation
  of why the effects were severe, linked to physical and human factors.
- **Level 2 (3–5 marks):** effects described with some detail; some
  explanation, or effects on only people or only the environment.
- **Level 1 (1–2 marks):** generic effects with no place-specific detail.

Example of Level 3 content (Haiti, 12 January 2010, magnitude 7.0): many
buildings collapsed in and around Port-au-Prince, including government
buildings and hospitals; very heavy loss of life and large numbers made
homeless; damage to the port and roads slowed aid; a cholera outbreak later
in 2010. Environmental effects: landslides and debris, contamination of water
supplies. Reasons for severity: shallow focus; epicentre close to the capital;
poorly constructed buildings and weak enforcement of building standards; low
income and limited emergency services.

## A note on exam technique for this topic

When a question gives you data, as in Question 6, quote the figures in your
answer and compare them directly. In explanations of plate processes, always
say *which* plate is denser and *why* it melts. In case-study answers, keep
effects on people and effects on the environment in separate paragraphs so
the examiner can see you have covered both.
