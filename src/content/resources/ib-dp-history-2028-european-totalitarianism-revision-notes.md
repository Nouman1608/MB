**HL only.** These notes cover Regional study 8, European totalitarianism (1918 to 1941), in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-european-totalitarianism/) for fuller explanation.

## What Paper 3 wants

Two 15-mark essays from different regional studies in two hours. Top-band answers show in-depth understanding of the argument, a balanced evaluation of perspectives and a substantiated judgement. Many questions here invite comparison of leaders, so practise it.

## Rise and consolidation: how they gained power

| Leader | Conditions | Key actions | Helped by |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mussolini | Weak liberal state; fear of socialism | Squadristi violence; March on Rome; Acerbo Law | King's refusal of martial law; elites' calculation |
| Hitler | Weimar's weakness; Depression | Propaganda, mass party; Reichstag fire; Enabling Act | Hindenburg and conservatives; voters' fear and hope |
| Stalin | Power vacuum after Lenin | General Secretary's patronage; alliances; Socialism in One Country | Opponents' errors; party loyalty |

## Weimar: strengths and weaknesses

- **Weaknesses:** Versailles resentment; proportional representation and fragile coalitions; Article 48; hostile elites; hyperinflation (1923).
- **Strengths in the Golden Era:** Rentenmark; Dawes Plan loans; Locarno; League entry; culture.
- **Why it failed:** Depression and reliance on foreign loans; Brüning's decrees; Nazi appeal; Papen's and Hindenburg's decisions.
- **Debate:** was Hitler's rise inevitable? Most historians say no, and stress choices in 1932 and 1933.

| Area | Italy | Germany | USSR |
|---|---|---|---|

## Historians' views

- Arendt, Friedrich and Brzezinski: the totalitarian model. Revisionists such as Getty on the USSR: chaos and local initiative.
- Nazi regime: intentionalists (Hitler's plan) against structuralists (rivalry and improvisation).
- Consent or coercion in Nazi Germany and Fascist Italy?

## Common mistakes

- Treating all three regimes as identical.
- Explaining rise to power without ranking the factors.
- Using figures such as unemployment or executions without "about".
- Ignoring the role of conservative elites in Italy and Germany.
- Forgetting that Italy kept a king and a Church.

## Self-test

1. Why did the king refuse to declare martial law in 1922?
2. What did the Acerbo Law do?
3. Name two events that helped Hitler make himself dictator in 1933 to 1934.
4. What was the Four-Year Plan for?
5. Why was Stalin's position as General Secretary important?
6. What was the Ryutin Platform?

**Answers:** 1. He feared civil war and thought Mussolini could be controlled within government. 2. It gave two-thirds of seats to the party winning a quarter of votes. 3. For example the Reichstag fire decree, the Enabling Act, the Night of the Long Knives or the army oath. 4. Self-sufficiency and preparation for war. 5. It gave him control of appointments and the party machine. 6. A 1932 document calling for Stalin's removal.

## Frequently asked questions

**How should I compare?**
Choose points of comparison, such as route to power or use of terror, and judge similarity and difference for each.

**What numbers are safe?**
Use only those you are sure of, with "about" for estimates.
