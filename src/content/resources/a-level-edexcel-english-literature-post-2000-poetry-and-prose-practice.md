## Section A (practise using two poems from your *Poems of the Decade* anthology)

**1.** Compare how two poems from *Poems of the Decade* present a shared post-2000 concern (e.g. technology, conflict, identity, the environment). **[25]**

## Section B (practise using your own post-2000 prose text)

**2.** Explore how your chosen post-2000 prose fiction text uses form and structure to shape the reader's understanding of a central concern of the novel. **[25]**

---

## Answers (worked approach — marks depend on your chosen texts)

**1.** A top-band answer organises **by point of comparison** rather than poem-by-poem [8], analyses each poem's form, structure and language closely and precisely, using the open-book text to locate accurate, well-chosen evidence [10], and makes explicit, developed connections between the two poems throughout — not only in an introduction or conclusion — since AO4 (connections across texts) is credited across the whole response [7].

**2.** A strong response identifies specific structural choices — non-linear narrative, shifting narrators, chapter/section breaks, withheld information — and explains their effect on how the reader comes to understand the novel's central concern [12], integrates contemporary context relevant to the concern (e.g. social attitudes, technology, current events the novel engages with) rather than treating it separately [8], and sustains a clear critical argument throughout rather than a list of observations [5].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Section A answers that discuss each poem in an isolated half rather than comparing directly.
- Section B context that reaches for historical background instead of contemporary/social context relevant to a post-2000 text.
- Forgetting AO3 does not apply to Section A — spending Section A time on context analysis that isn't credited there.
- Under-using the open-book allowance — vague or paraphrased "quotations" when the actual text is available to check.
