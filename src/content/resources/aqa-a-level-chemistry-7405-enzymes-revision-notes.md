These notes condense section **3.3.13.3 Enzymes** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Enzymes study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-enzymes/), and exam-style questions are in the [Enzymes practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-enzymes-practice/). The previous sub-topic is in the [Amino acids, proteins and DNA revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-amino-acids-proteins-and-dna-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- State that **enzymes are proteins** and act as **catalysts**.
- Describe the **stereospecific active site** that binds a substrate.
- Explain how a **drug** can act as an **enzyme inhibitor** by blocking the active site.
- State that **computers** can help design such drugs.
- Explain why a stereospecific active site binds only **one enantiomer**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Enzyme | A protein that acts as a biological catalyst |
| Substrate | The molecule that the enzyme acts on |
| Active site | The region of the enzyme where the substrate binds and reacts |
| Stereospecific | Binds only one stereoisomer (enantiomer) |
| Enzyme–substrate complex | The enzyme with the substrate bound |
| Inhibitor | A molecule that blocks an enzyme and slows or stops its action |
| Enantiomers | Non-superimposable mirror-image isomers |

## How an enzyme works

1. Substrate fits the **active site**.
2. **Enzyme–substrate complex** forms.
3. Reaction occurs with a **lower activation energy**.
4. **Products leave**.
5. The **enzyme is unchanged**, ready to bind more substrate.

Enzymes work under mild conditions and are highly specific.

## Why the active site is specific

- The active site is a pocket lined by amino acid side chains.
- Its shape and groups are decided by the **primary, secondary and tertiary structure**.
- The substrate binds by **hydrogen bonds, ionic attractions and London forces**.
- Only a molecule with the correct shape and groups fits.

## Stereospecificity

- Amino acids are chiral, so the active site is **chiral**.
- A substrate with a chiral centre has two enantiomers.
- Only one has its four groups arranged to match the binding groups of the active site.
- The other enantiomer is the mirror image, so its groups cannot all bond at once.
- Analogy: a right hand fits a right-hand glove only.

The same applies to **drugs**: often only one enantiomer binds and is active.

## Enzyme inhibitors as drugs

| Step | Description |
|---|---|
| 1 | The drug has a shape and groups like the substrate |
| 2 | It binds in the **active site** |
| 3 | The site is **blocked** so the substrate cannot bind |
| 4 | The rate of the enzyme-catalysed reaction falls |

Example type: an antibiotic that blocks an enzyme bacteria need to build cell walls.

## Computer-aided drug design

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| What is done | Computers model the 3D shape of the active site and of candidate molecules |
| Prediction | They predict how well each candidate fits and binds |
| Benefit | Many candidates tested quickly and cheaply, before synthesis |
| Benefit | Fewer laboratory and animal tests in early stages |
| Benefit | Designs can be adjusted to fit better |

## Enzymes and structure: the links

| Level | Relevance |
|---|---|
| Primary | Sequence decides the side chains in the active site |
| Secondary | Helix and sheet framework |
| Tertiary | Hydrogen bonds and S–S bonds hold the active site shape |

Changing the shape of the protein changes the active site and affects function.

## Quick calculations

- Racemic mixture: 50 % of each enantiomer by mass.
- If an enzyme reacts with only one enantiomer, at most half of the racemate reacts.
- Amount (mol) = mass ÷ Mᵣ.

Example: 20.0 g of a racemic mixture of Mᵣ 100.0 contains 10.0 g (0.100 mol) of each enantiomer. An enzyme specific to one enantiomer can convert at most 0.100 mol.

## Writing a good answer

For "explain why only one enantiomer binds", include:

1. The active site is **chiral**/stereospecific.
2. It has binding groups in a **particular 3D arrangement**.
3. One enantiomer's groups **match**.
4. The mirror-image enantiomer's groups are **in different positions**, so they cannot all bond.

## Exam traps

- "Enzyme is used up."
- "The other enantiomer is the wrong size."
- "The inhibitor destroys the enzyme."
- Forgetting that the active site is chiral.
- Confusing substrate and product.
- Saying the catalyst changes the equilibrium position.
- Computers "make" or "invent" drugs; they model and predict.
- Not linking drug action to blocking of the active site.

## Self-test

1. What type of molecule is an enzyme?
2. What is the active site?
3. What is a substrate?
4. How does an enzyme speed up a reaction?
5. Why is the active site stereospecific?
6. Why can a stereospecific site bind only one enantiomer?
7. How does an enzyme inhibitor drug work?
8. Why does the rate fall when an inhibitor is present?
9. Give two uses of computers in drug design.
10. What forces bind a substrate to the active site?
11. What mass of one enantiomer is in 30.0 g of a racemic mixture?
12. Why is only one enantiomer of a drug often supplied?

### Answers

1. A protein.
2. The part of the enzyme where the substrate binds and the reaction takes place.
3. The molecule that the enzyme acts on.
4. It provides an alternative route with lower activation energy.
5. It is made of chiral amino acids and folds into a chiral shape with specifically arranged groups.
6. The groups of the other enantiomer are in a mirror-image arrangement and cannot all bond.
7. It binds in the active site and blocks it, so the substrate cannot bind.
8. Fewer substrate molecules can bind per second, so less product is formed.
9. Modelling the active site and drug shapes; predicting binding; screening many candidates without synthesis.
10. Hydrogen bonds, ionic attractions and London forces.
11. 15.0 g.
12. Often only one enantiomer binds and is active; the other may be inactive or have other effects.
