These questions cover section 5.1, Units of measure, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The method is explained in the [units of measure study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure/), and the [units of measure revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-units-of-measure-revision-notes/) list the conversion factors. Give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures unless told otherwise, and always include units.

## Section A

**1.** Convert

**(a)** 3.5 km to metres,

**(b)** 650 g to kilograms,

**(c)** 2.4 litres to millilitres. **[3]**

**2.** Convert 4.2 m² to cm². **[1]**

**3.** Convert 0.35 m³ to litres. **[2]**

**4.** Convert 8500 mm² to cm². **[1]**

**5.** Convert 6 000 000 cm³ to m³. **[1]**

**6.** A bottle holds 0.75 litres. How many cm³ is this? **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A rectangular tank measures 90 cm by 60 cm by 40 cm.

**(a)** Find its volume in cm³.

**(b)** Find its capacity in litres. **[3]**

**8.** A floor measures 6 m by 3.5 m. It is covered with square tiles of side 50 cm. How many tiles are needed? **[4]**

**9.** A tank measures 1.2 m by 0.9 m by 0.5 m. It is filled at 18 litres per minute.

**(a)** Find the capacity of the tank in litres.

**(b)** Find the time to fill it. **[4]**

**10.** A stack of 400 identical sheets of paper is 4.8 cm high. Find the thickness of one sheet in millimetres. **[3]**

**11.** A bottle holds 0.3 litres of medicine. Each dose is 7.5 ml. How many doses are in the bottle? **[3]**

**12.** 12 mm of rain falls on a rectangular roof measuring 8 m by 5 m. All the water runs into a tank. Find the volume of water in m³ and in litres. **[4]**

**13.** A field has an area of 2.5 × 10⁷ cm².

**(a)** Write this area in m².

**(b)** Write this area in km². **[3]**

**14.** A pupil says that 1 m² is 100 cm² because 1 m is 100 cm. Explain the mistake and give the correct value. **[2]**

**15.** A cube has edges of 20 cm. Find its volume in cm³ and its capacity in litres. **[2]**

**16.** A 2.5 kg bag of rice costs $6. Find the cost of 100 g of rice at the same rate. **[3]**

**17.** A pool measures 12 m by 5 m with a constant depth of 1.5 m. A pump moves 300 litres of water every minute. How long does the pump take to fill the pool, in hours? **[4]**

**18.** A concrete block measures 0.4 m by 20 cm by 15 cm.

**(a)** Find its volume in cm³.

**(b)** Write the volume in m³. **[3]**

**19.** A cyclist travels at 72 kilometres per hour. Write this speed in metres per second. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 3.5 × 1000 = **3500 m** [1]. **(b)** 650 ÷ 1000 = **0.65 kg** [1]. **(c)** 2.4 × 1000 = **2400 ml** [1].

**2.** 4.2 × 10 000 = **42 000 cm²** [1].

**3.** 0.35 × 1000 [1] = **350 litres** [1].

**4.** 8500 ÷ 100 = **85 cm²** [1].

**5.** 6 000 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **6 m³** [1].

**6.** 0.75 × 1000 = **750 cm³** [1].

**7. (a)** 90 × 60 × 40 = **216 000 cm³** [1][1]. **(b)** 216 000 ÷ 1000 = **216 litres** [1].

**8.** Tiles along 6 m: 600 ÷ 50 = 12 [1]. Tiles along 3.5 m: 350 ÷ 50 = 7 [1]. Total = 12 × 7 [1] = **84 tiles** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The area method gives the same answer: 21 m² ÷ 0.25 m² = 84.

**9. (a)** Volume = 1.2 × 0.9 × 0.5 = 0.54 m³ [1], so the capacity is **540 litres** [1].

**(b)** 540 ÷ 18 [1] = **30 minutes** [1].

**10.** 4.8 cm = 48 mm [1]. 48 ÷ 400 [1] = **0.12 mm** [1].

**11.** 0.3 litres = 300 ml [1]. 300 ÷ 7.5 [1] = **40 doses** [1].

**12.** 12 mm = 0.012 m [1]. Volume = 0.012 × 8 × 5 = **0.48 m³** [1][1]. In litres, 0.48 × 1000 = **480 litres** [1].

**13. (a)** 2.5 × 10⁷ ÷ 10 000 = **2500 m²** [1][1]. **(b)** 2500 ÷ 1 000 000 = **0.0025 km²** [1].

**14.** A square of side 1 m has side 100 cm, so its area is 100 × 100 [1] = **10 000 cm²**. Areas use the square of the length factor [1].

**15.** Volume = 20³ = **8000 cm³** [1]. Capacity = 8000 ÷ 1000 = **8 litres** [1].

**16.** 2.5 kg = 2500 g, and 2500 ÷ 100 = 25 portions of 100 g [1][1]. Cost = $6 ÷ 25 = **$0.24** [1].

**17.** Volume = 12 × 5 × 1.5 = 90 m³ [1]. Capacity = 90 × 1000 = 90 000 litres [1]. Time = 90 000 ÷ 300 = 300 minutes [1] = **5 hours** [1].

**18. (a)** 0.4 m = 40 cm [1]. Volume = 40 × 20 × 15 = **12 000 cm³** [1]. **(b)** 12 000 ÷ 1 000 000 = **0.012 m³** [1].

**19.** 72 km = 72 000 m and 1 hour = 3600 s [1]. 72 000 ÷ 3600 = **20 m/s** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using 100 for m² to cm², or 1000 for m³ to cm³.
- Multiplying when the conversion needs dividing.
- Not converting all lengths to the same unit before multiplying.
- Forgetting the 1000 between cm³ and litres.
- Leaving out units, or writing cm² for a volume.
