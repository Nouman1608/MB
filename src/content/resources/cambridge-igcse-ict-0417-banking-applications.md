Subtopic 6.6 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 covers banking applications. The syllabus asks for the "characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages" of Automatic Teller Machines (ATMs), listing "withdrawing cash, depositing cash or cheques, checking account balance, mini statements, bill paying, money transfers", and of "Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT), credit/debit card transactions, cheques, internet banking". The [ICT Applications study guide](/resources/igcse-ict-applications-systems/) introduces each briefly. This guide follows each transaction step by step, shows which computer checks happen, and compares the methods.

## Automatic Teller Machines (ATMs)

An **ATM** is a terminal connected to the bank's computer over a secure network. It has a card reader, keypad, screen, cash dispenser, printer for receipts, and on many machines a slot for depositing cash or cheques.

### Worked example: withdrawing cash

1. The customer inserts the card. The ATM reads the account details from the **chip** (or magnetic stripe).
2. The customer types a **PIN**. The PIN is checked; if it is wrong three times, the card is usually kept by the machine.
3. The customer chooses "withdraw cash" and enters an amount.
4. The ATM sends the request to the bank's computer, which checks that the card has not been reported lost or stolen, that there are enough funds in the account and that the daily withdrawal limit will not be exceeded.
5. If the checks pass, the bank's computer authorises the transaction and **debits** the account.
6. The card is returned, and then the cash is dispensed. Returning the card first means the customer is less likely to leave it behind.
7. A receipt is printed if requested.

### The other named services

| Service | What happens |
|---|---|
| Depositing cash or cheques | The ATM counts notes or scans the cheque, and the amount is credited to the account; cheque funds may not be available until the cheque has cleared |
| Checking account balance | The bank's computer sends the current balance to the screen |
| Mini statement | The ATM prints a short list of the most recent transactions |
| Bill paying | The customer chooses a registered company, such as an electricity supplier, and the amount is transferred to that company's account |
| Money transfers | Money is moved to another of the customer's accounts or to someone else's account |

**Advantages of ATMs:** available 24 hours a day; located in many places, including shops and airports; quicker than queuing in a branch; cash can be withdrawn abroad.
**Disadvantages:** some machines charge fees; a daily limit on cash; risk of card skimming (a device fitted to the slot copies card details) and shoulder surfing (someone watches the PIN being typed); machines may be out of order or run out of cash; fewer services than a branch, and no member of staff to help.

## Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT)

**EFT** is the movement of money from one bank account to another electronically, with no cash or paper changing hands.

**Example: paying wages.** An employer's computer sends payment instructions to its bank, listing each employee's account details and pay. The bank's computer **debits** the employer's account and **credits** each employee's account, even if they bank elsewhere. Regular bill payments and transfers to friends work the same way.

**Advantages:** fast and secure; no need to handle or transport cash; cheaper for businesses than paying by cheque; money arrives on a known date; a record of each payment is kept.
**Disadvantages:** a mistake in the account number can send money to the wrong person, and it can be hard to recover; requires the computer systems to be working; fraud if criminals gain access to account details.

## Credit and debit card transactions

| Feature | Debit card | Credit card |
|---|---|---|
| Where the money comes from | The customer's own current account | The card company lends the money |
| When it is paid | Straight away from the account | Later, usually after a monthly statement |
| Interest | None on purchases | Charged if the balance is not paid in full |
| Limit | The money in the account (plus any agreed overdraft) | A credit limit set by the card company |

Cards are used in shops by **chip and PIN** or **contactless** payment (covered in [computers in the retail industry](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-computers-in-the-retail-industry/)), and online by typing the card number, expiry date and security code.

**Advantages:** no need to carry cash; safer than cash if lost, because the card can be stopped; can be used online and abroad; credit cards spread the cost and may protect large purchases. **Disadvantages:** card details can be stolen and used fraudulently, especially online; credit card interest can lead to debt; small shops may not accept cards or may charge to use them.

## Cheques

A **cheque** is a paper instruction, signed by the account holder, telling the bank to pay a named person a stated amount from the account. The person who receives it pays it in, and the banks then **clear** it: the payer's bank checks the signature and the funds before the money is transferred. Some banks now let customers pay in a cheque by photographing it with a banking app.

**Advantages:** useful for people who do not use internet banking; a written record exists; safer than sending cash by post.
**Disadvantages:** slow to clear; can be lost or stolen; can "bounce" if there are not enough funds; handling paper is costly for banks; fewer organisations accept them.

## Internet banking

**Internet banking** lets customers manage their accounts through a bank's secure website or app. Customers log in with a user ID, password or PIN and often a second check, such as a one-time code sent to their phone or a fingerprint on the app.

**Typical features:** view balances and statements; transfer money between accounts; pay bills and other people; set up regular payments; apply for loans; manage cards, for example freezing a lost card.

**Advantages:** available at any time from anywhere with internet access; saves travel to a branch, which helps people with mobility problems; easy to track spending; transactions are instant.
**Disadvantages:** risk of **phishing** (fake emails or websites that trick customers into giving login details) and hacking; requires internet access and some confidence with technology; mistakes in account details are easy to make; no face-to-face advice; it has led to branch closures, which affects people who rely on visiting one.

## Comparing the methods

- **Speed:** EFT, card payments and internet banking are near-instant; cheques are slowest.
- **Security:** electronic methods use encryption and PINs, but are targets for fraud; cheques and cash can be physically stolen.
- **Accessibility:** ATMs and branches suit people without internet access; internet banking suits people who cannot easily travel.

## Common mistakes

- Saying the ATM checks the balance. The ATM sends the request; the **bank's computer** checks it.
- Mixing up debit and credit cards.
- Describing EFT as "using a card in a shop" only. EFT covers any electronic transfer between accounts, including wages.
- Writing "hacking" as a disadvantage without saying what could be stolen or how, such as phishing for login details.
- Leaving out the PIN check or the card being returned.

## Exam technique

- **Describe** an ATM transaction: give the steps in order, including the PIN, the checks by the bank's computer and the debit.
- **Explain** an advantage: feature plus effect, such as "available 24 hours, so customers can get cash when the branch is closed".
- **Compare** two methods: make a point about each, linked by "whereas".
- **Discuss**: give both sides for the customer and the bank, then a judgement.
