This guide covers **Exercise 4** of Cambridge O Level Second Language Urdu 3248 Paper 2 Grammar,
Writing and Translation: **Translation**, in which "candidates translate a short passage of
approximately 150 words from English into Urdu". The question is in English and the answer in Urdu.
It is worth **15 marks** and tests all five writing objectives, W1 to W5. For the paper as a whole,
see the [Paper 2 Grammar, Writing and Translation study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-second-language-paper-2-grammar-writing-translation/).
This page is about the specific changes English sentences need on their way into natural Urdu.

## What the objectives mean for translation

| Objective | In translation |
|---|---|
| W1 communicate information clearly, accurately and effectively | every idea of the English is present, nothing added |
| W2 organise ideas into coherent paragraphs using linking devices | the English links (*but*, *so*, *because*) survive as Urdu connectives |
| W3 manipulate the target language accurately | tenses, agreement, postpositions and word order correct |
| W4 control of punctuation and spelling | Urdu punctuation; correct spelling, including names |
| W5 appropriate register and style/format | the English tone (friendly, formal) carried into Urdu |

## Six changes English needs

**1. Word order.** English is subject–verb–object; Urdu is subject–object–verb, with the verb at the
end and postpositions after nouns.
*She gave the book to her brother.* → *اس نے کتاب اپنے بھائی کو دی۔*

**2. Tense and aspect.** Map meaning, not English verb shapes.

| English | Urdu |
|---|---|
| I walk to school every day. | میں روزانہ پیدل اسکول جاتا ہوں۔ |
| I am walking to school. | میں پیدل اسکول جا رہا ہوں۔ |
| I have finished my homework. | میں نے اپنا ہوم ورک ختم کر لیا ہے۔ / میں ہوم ورک کر چکا ہوں۔ |
| I had already left. | میں پہلے ہی جا چکا تھا۔ |
| I used to play cricket. | میں کرکٹ کھیلا کرتا تھا۔ / کھیلتا تھا۔ |
| I have been learning Urdu for two years. | میں دو سال سے اردو سیکھ رہا ہوں۔ |

The last row is a classic trap: English "for two years" becomes *دو سال سے* with a continuous
present.

**3. Articles.** *The* is not translated; *a/an* becomes *ایک* only when it means "one" or introduces
something new: *A man knocked at the door* → *ایک آدمی نے دروازہ کھٹکھٹایا*. *The door was open* →
*دروازہ کھلا تھا*.

**4. Gender.** Every Urdu noun has a gender, and adjectives and verbs must follow it. English gives no
help: *the old house* → *پرانا مکان* (masculine) but *the old building* → *پرانی عمارت* (feminine).
Choose your noun, then check every word that agrees with it.

**5. You and they.** English *you* hides register. A message to a friend takes *تم*; to a teacher or
stranger *آپ*. Third-person respect matters too: *My grandfather says...* → *میرے دادا کہتے ہیں*, not
*کہتا ہے*.

**6. Idiom.** Translate the meaning, not the words. *It was raining cats and dogs* → *موسلا دھار بارش
ہو رہی تھی*. *I am over the moon* → *میں بے حد خوش ہوں*. *He kept his word* → *اس نے اپنا وعدہ پورا
کیا*. A word-for-word version of an idiom is the clearest sign of literal translation.

## A process for the exam

1. **Read the whole English passage** for meaning, tone and who is speaking to whom.
2. **Mark difficulties** before writing: idioms, tricky tenses, words you do not know.
3. **Translate sentence by sentence**, keeping the English sentence boundaries unless Urdu needs two
   short sentences joined or one long one split.
4. **Never leave a gap.** If a word is unknown, express the idea with a simpler word or phrase; a
   close paraphrase keeps the meaning, a blank loses it.
5. **Read the Urdu alone**, as if there were no English. Does it sound like Urdu? Does every verb agree?
6. **Check names, numbers and punctuation.**

## Worked example

English (original):

> Last summer my family visited Murree for the first time. The roads were narrow and crowded, but the
> view from the hills was breathtaking. My little sister, who had never seen clouds so close, kept
> trying to catch them. In the evening we drank hot tea at a small roadside café. I have been to many
> places since then, but I will never forget that trip.

Urdu translation, with notes:

> پچھلی گرمیوں میں میرے گھر والے پہلی بار مری گئے۔

*My family* as a group is naturally *میرے گھر والے* with a plural verb. *Visited* becomes *گئے*: "went
to" sounds more natural than *کا دورہ کیا* for a family trip.

> سڑکیں تنگ تھیں اور ان پر بہت رش تھا، لیکن پہاڑوں سے نظارہ دم بخود کر دینے والا تھا۔

*Crowded* is expanded to *ان پر بہت رش تھا*; *breathtaking* becomes an Urdu expression of the same
force rather than a literal "taking breath".

> میری چھوٹی بہن نے، جس نے پہلے کبھی بادل اتنے قریب سے نہیں دیکھے تھے، انھیں پکڑنے کی بار بار کوشش
> کی۔

The relative clause *who had never seen* becomes *جس نے ... نہیں دیکھے تھے*; *دیکھے* agrees with
*بادل* (masculine plural). *Kept trying* is *بار بار کوشش کی*.

> شام کو ہم نے سڑک کنارے ایک چھوٹے سے ڈھابے پر گرم چائے پی۔

*Café* by a roadside is naturally *ڈھابا*; here it is in the oblique, *ڈھابے پر*. *پی* agrees with
feminine *چائے*.

> اس کے بعد میں بہت سی جگہوں پر جا چکا ہوں، مگر وہ سفر کبھی نہیں بھولوں گا۔

*I have been to many places since then* uses *جا چکا ہوں*; the future negative *کبھی نہیں بھولوں گا*
keeps the promise-like tone.

## Numbers, names, dates and punctuation

- **Numbers**: write them in figures or in words, but consistently; ordinals become *پہلا*، *دوسرا*،
  *تیسرا*, agreeing in gender (*دوسری بار*).
- **Names**: keep personal and place names, spelt in Urdu script as they are normally written:
  *لاہور*، *کراچی*. Do not translate the meaning of a name.
- **Dates and times**: *on Monday* → *پیر کو*; *at five o'clock* → *پانچ بجے*; *in 2025* → *2025 میں*.
- **Punctuation**: change English full stops to *۔*, commas to *،*, question marks to *؟*. Quotation
  marks and exclamation marks stay.

## Two structures English uses more than Urdu

**"There is / there are"** has no direct equivalent: *There is a park near my house* → *میرے گھر کے
قریب ایک پارک ہے۔* Place first, then the thing, then *ہے*.

**The passive** is less common in Urdu than in English. *The match was cancelled* can stay passive
(*میچ منسوخ کر دیا گیا*), but *I was given a prize* often reads better active: *مجھے انعام دیا گیا*
or *انھوں نے مجھے انعام دیا*.

## Common mistakes

- **English word order** with Urdu words: *میں گیا بازار کل*.
- **Translating "the"** as *وہ* everywhere.
- **Wrong gender** chosen for a noun, then carried through the sentence.
- **Missing *نے*** with transitive past verbs.
- **Literal idioms**, and English words in Roman script left untranslated.
- **Omissions**: a whole clause such as *who had never seen clouds so close* left out because it was
  difficult.
- **Register drift**: *آپ* in one sentence and *تم* in the next for the same person.
