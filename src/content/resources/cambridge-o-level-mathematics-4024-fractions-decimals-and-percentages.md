This guide covers subtopic **1.4 Fractions, decimals and percentages** in Topic 1, Number, of Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024, 2025 to 2027 series. The same quantity can be written as a fraction, a decimal or a percentage, and exam questions expect you to move between these forms without fuss. The [Number revision notes](/resources/o-level-mathematics-number-revision-notes/) give a brief summary. This page covers the language, the conversions, the test for recurring decimals, and the method for turning a recurring decimal back into a fraction.

Companion pages: the [Fractions, decimals and percentages revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-fractions-decimals-and-percentages-revision-notes/) and the [Fractions, decimals and percentages practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-fractions-decimals-and-percentages-practice/).

## What the syllabus asks

The syllabus asks you to:

1. use the language and notation of proper fractions, improper fractions, mixed numbers, decimals and percentages in appropriate contexts
2. recognise equivalence and convert between these forms.

It states that fractions should be written in their simplest form, that **recurring decimal notation is required**, and that conversion includes going from recurring decimals to fractions and back. The syllabus gives dot notation: a dot over a single digit means that digit repeats (0.17̇ is 0.1777...), and dots over the first and last digits of a block mean the whole block repeats (0.12̇3̇ is 0.1232323... and 0.1̇2̇3̇ is 0.123123...).

## The language of fractions

- A **proper fraction** has a numerator smaller than its denominator, such as 3/8. Its value is less than 1.
- An **improper fraction** has a numerator that is greater than or equal to its denominator, such as 29/8.
- A **mixed number** has a whole number part and a fraction part, such as 3 5/8.

To change an improper fraction to a mixed number, divide the numerator by the denominator. The quotient is the whole number and the remainder goes over the denominator. To change a mixed number to an improper fraction, multiply the whole number by the denominator and add the numerator.

```
29/8:   29 ÷ 8 = 3 remainder 5     so 29/8 = 3 5/8
3 2/5:  3 x 5 + 2 = 17             so 3 2/5 = 17/5
```

## Simplest form

Divide the numerator and denominator by their highest common factor. This keeps the value the same, because you are multiplying by 1 in disguise.

```
36/48:  HCF of 36 and 48 is 12
        36 ÷ 12 = 3,  48 ÷ 12 = 4
        36/48 = 3/4
```

A fraction is in simplest form when the numerator and denominator share no common factor other than 1. Exam questions usually demand this, so cancel fully at the end of any working.

## Fractions to decimals

A fraction is a division: the numerator divided by the denominator. Use short division, or find an equivalent fraction with a denominator of 10, 100 or 1000.

```
3/8 = 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375
7/20 = 35/100 = 0.35
7/40 = 175/1000 = 0.175
```

Learn the common ones: 1/2 = 0.5, 1/4 = 0.25, 3/4 = 0.75, 1/5 = 0.2, 1/8 = 0.125, 1/10 = 0.1, 1/3 = 0.3̇ and 2/3 = 0.6̇.

## Decimals to fractions

Write the decimal as a fraction over a power of 10, then simplify. The denominator is 10 for one decimal place, 100 for two, and so on.

```
0.375 = 375/1000 = 3/8
0.35 = 35/100 = 7/20
2.4 = 24/10 = 12/5 = 2 2/5
```

## Percentages

A percentage is a fraction out of 100. To convert a fraction or decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100. To convert a percentage back, divide by 100.

```
3/8 = 0.375 = 37.5%
7/20 = 0.35 = 35%
140% = 140/100 = 7/5 = 1.4
0.6% = 0.6/100 = 0.006
```

Percentages can be greater than 100%, as in 140%, which is more than the whole. They can also be less than 1%, as in 0.6%.

**Worked example.** A student scores 36 out of 48 in a test. Write the score as a fraction in its simplest form, as a decimal and as a percentage.

```
fraction: 36/48 = 3/4
decimal:  3 ÷ 4 = 0.75
percentage: 0.75 x 100 = 75%
```

**Worked example.** Work out 3/4 + 0.6 and give the answer as a percentage.

```
3/4 = 0.75
0.75 + 0.6 = 1.35
1.35 = 135%
```

Convert everything to the same form before you add. Choose the form that makes the calculation easiest.

## Terminating and recurring decimals

When you divide the numerator by the denominator, the decimal either stops (terminates) or repeats a pattern for ever (recurs).

**Test.** Write the fraction in its simplest form. Look at the prime factors of the denominator.

- If the only prime factors are 2 and/or 5, the decimal **terminates**.
- If any other prime factor appears, the decimal **recurs**.

```
7/40:  40 = 2^3 x 5            terminates   0.175
9/16:  16 = 2^4                terminates   0.5625
5/12:  12 = 2^2 x 3            recurs       0.41666...
5/6:   6 = 2 x 3               recurs       0.8333...
3/7:   7                       recurs       0.428571428571...
```

Simplify first. The fraction 6/15 has denominator 15 = 3 × 5, but 6/15 = 2/5, which has denominator 5, so the decimal terminates (0.4).

### Recurring decimal notation

Place a dot over the digit that repeats. For a block of digits, put a dot over the first and the last digit of the block.

```
0.8333...        is written 0.83̇
0.41666...       is written 0.416̇
0.363636...      is written 0.3̇6̇
0.428571428571...  is written 0.4̇28571̇
1.1777...        is written 1.17̇
```

## Recurring decimals to fractions

The method uses algebra to cancel the repeating tail. Multiply by a power of 10 so that the repeating digits line up, then subtract.

**Case 1: the repeating block starts straight after the decimal point.** Write 0.3̇6̇ as a fraction.

```
Let x = 0.363636...
Two digits repeat, so multiply by 100:
100x = 36.363636...
Subtract:   100x - x = 36.363636... - 0.363636...
            99x = 36
            x = 36/99 = 4/11
```

For a single repeating digit, multiply by 10 and subtract. For example, 0.7̇ gives 9x = 7, so x = 7/9.

**Case 2: some digits come before the repeating block.** Write 0.17̇ as a fraction.

```
Let x = 0.17777...
10x  = 1.7777...
100x = 17.7777...
Subtract:   100x - 10x = 17.7777... - 1.7777...
            90x = 16
            x = 16/90 = 8/45
```

The idea is to multiply twice so that the repeating tails match exactly and cancel. Here the first multiplication, by 10, moves the decimal point past the non-repeating digit, and the second, by 100, moves it past one repeating digit.

**Worked example.** Write 0.12̇3̇ (which is 0.1232323...) as a fraction in its simplest form.

```
Let x = 0.1232323...
10x   = 1.232323...
1000x = 123.232323...
Subtract:   1000x - 10x = 123.2323... - 1.2323...
            990x = 122
            x = 122/990 = 61/495
```

Check on a calculator: 61 ÷ 495 = 0.12323... ✓.

**Worked example.** Write 2.3̇ as a mixed number and as an improper fraction.

```
0.3̇ = 1/3
2.3̇ = 2 1/3 = 7/3
```

Whole number parts can be taken out first and added back at the end.

**Worked example.** Show that 0.9̇ = 1.

```
Let x = 0.999...
10x = 9.999...
9x = 9
x = 1
```

This surprises many students, but the working is exact. It shows that the recurring decimal 0.9̇ is another way of writing 1.

## Fractions, decimals and percentages in context

Real situations use all three forms together. A good habit is to decide which form you need at the end, and convert at the start if it makes the working easier. For example, to find 35% of an amount, it may be easiest to use 0.35, or 7/20. To compare 5/8, 0.6 and 61%, convert all three to decimals: 0.625, 0.6, 0.61.

## Exam technique

- When a question says "give your answer as a fraction in its simplest form", cancel fully and leave the answer as a fraction. Do not convert it to a decimal.
- In a recurring decimal question, show the two equations and the subtraction. Those lines carry the method marks.
- Write recurring decimals with dot notation, not with "..." only, when the question asks for it.
- A calculator may show a recurring decimal rounded off at the last digit. Do not copy the rounded display as an exact answer.

## Common mistakes

- Cancelling only part of the way, leaving 6/8 instead of 3/4.
- Writing 0.35 as 3.5%. A percentage is the decimal multiplied by 100, so 0.35 is 35%.
- Reading 1/8 as 0.8. It is 0.125.
- Subtracting the wrong multiples when converting a recurring decimal, so the tails do not cancel.
- Writing 0.17̇ as 17/99. The dot is over the 7 only, so the 1 does not repeat.
- Forgetting to simplify the fraction at the end of a recurring decimal conversion.
- Declaring a decimal recurring because the calculator shows many digits; check the denominator instead.
