## Questions

**1.** Country X: in Year 1, GDP is 800, exports 150, imports 170 and FDI inflow 20. In Year 2, GDP is 1,000, exports 220, imports 240 and FDI inflow 35.

**(a)** Work out the openness ratio for Year 1 and Year 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate FDI inflow as a percentage of GDP in each year. **[2]**
**(c)** State whether the data are consistent with increasing globalisation. **[1]**

**2.** In Country Z the population is 12 million, with 90,000 arrivals and 210,000 departures in a year. Migrants abroad send home 3.0 and GDP is 40.

**(a)** Calculate the net migration rate per 1,000 people and remittances as a percentage of GDP. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain one benefit and one cost of emigration for Country Z. **[4]**

**3.** Explain two causes of globalisation from: increased number and size of trading blocs, political change, reduced transport and communications costs. **[4]**

**4.** A good has an ex-factory price of 80 and freight of 20. A 15% tariff applies to price plus freight. Later the tariff falls to 5% and freight to 12.

**(a)** Calculate the landed price before and after. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in the landed price. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the landed price if only the tariff had fallen, and if only freight had fallen. **[2]**

**5.** Explain two reasons why a TNC might choose to build a factory abroad rather than export. **[4]**

**6.** A TNC opens a clothing factory in Country Y. Assess the impact of this FDI on Country Y. **[8]**

**7.** Demand for a product in Country W is P = 140 - 4Q. Trade liberalisation lowers the price from 100 to 60, with imports supplying the extra quantity.

**(a)** Calculate the quantity and consumer surplus before and after, and the gain. **[4]**
**(b)** Explain why the gain to Country W as a whole is smaller than your answer. **[2]**

**8.** A TNC raises output from 50,000 to 200,000 units. Total cost rises from 600,000 to 1,500,000.

**(a)** Calculate average cost before and after, and the percentage change. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how this may lower prices and one reason it may not. **[3]**

**9.** A TNC's subsidiary in Country M (profit tax 35%) buys 50,000 units from its affiliate in Country N (profit tax 15%). The charge is 52 per unit and the arm's-length price is 40.

**(a)** Calculate the profit shifted, the tax lost by M, the tax gained by N and the group's saving. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain two effects on Country M. **[3]**

**10.** In Country Q, 15,000 jobs are lost in import-competing industries and 11,000 created in export industries. The labour force is 2,000,000. The richest 10% share of income rises from 30% to 36% and the poorest 40% share falls from 18% to 15%.

**(a)** Calculate the net job change and its percentage of the labour force. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the richest 10% share to the poorest 40% share in each year. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why globalisation may raise income inequality within a country. **[4]**

**11.** Trade in a good involves an external cost of 5 per unit on 80,000 units.

**(a)** Calculate the total external cost. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why free trade may lead to too much of the good being produced and traded. **[3]**
**(c)** Explain how TNC influence on policy could weaken environmental rules. **[2]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that the benefits of globalisation outweigh the costs for a developing country. **[12]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Year 1: (150 + 170) / 800 = **40%** [1]. Year 2: (220 + 240) / 1,000 = **46%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Year 1: 20 / 800 = **2.5%** [1]. Year 2: 35 / 1,000 = **3.5%** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Yes, trade and FDI both rose as a share of GDP [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Add imports to exports before dividing; using exports alone gives a different ratio.

**2. (a)** (90,000 - 210,000) / 12 million x 1,000 = **-10.0 per 1,000** [1]. 3.0 / 40 x 100 = **7.5%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Benefit: remittances raise household income and support spending, which adds to AD [1] with a development of the chain, such as foreign exchange for imports [1]. Cost: loss of workers, especially skilled ones (brain drain), reduces productive capacity [1], so output and tax revenue may be lower than otherwise [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* One developed point per side earns more than a list of four labels, so state the mechanism.

**3.** Any two causes. Trading blocs: lower tariffs between members raise intra-bloc trade [1]; a bigger market lets firms gain scale and attracts outside investors [1]. Political change: the breakdown of the Soviet system or opening of China brought new countries into trade and investment [1]; new labour and consumer markets raised world trade [1]. Transport and communications are an equally valid cause **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each cause needs the change it brings, not just a name. "Cheaper transport" is stronger once it is linked to trade or location choices.

**4. (a)** Before: (80 + 20) x 1.15 = **115** [1]. After: (80 + 12) x 1.05 = **96.60** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** (96.6 - 115) / 115 x 100 = **-16.0%** [1] **[1]**
**(c)** Tariff only: 100 x 1.05 = **105** [1]. Freight only: 92 x 1.15 = **105.80** [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show which base the tariff applies to, since it is charged on the sum of price and freight in this question.

**5.** Any two developed reasons. Lower unit costs: cheaper labour or land lowers cost per unit [1], raising profit or price competitiveness [1]. Avoiding tariffs: producing inside the barrier means the product avoids the tariff [1], so it can be sold more cheaply in that market [1]. Also acceptable: closeness to a growing market, resources, skills, incentives **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "To make more profit" alone is too thin; say what changes in cost or revenue.

**6.** Benefits: jobs and wages raise household income and consumption [1], with some skills and technology passing to local workers [1]. Corporate and income tax rise [1], and exports may earn foreign exchange [1]. Costs: profits may be sent abroad, so national income rises by less than GDP [1]; local firms may be crowded out, or wages and conditions may be poor [1]. Judgement: net impact depends on local sourcing, tax rules and how long the TNC stays [1], with a reasoned conclusion [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A clear final judgement that picks the factor that matters most is more persuasive than a list of two-sided points.

**7. (a)** Before: Q = (140 - 100) / 4 = 10 [1]; surplus 0.5 x 10 x 40 = **200** [1]. After: Q = (140 - 60) / 4 = 20; surplus 0.5 x 20 x 80 = **800** [1]. Gain = **600** [1] **[4]**
**(b)** Domestic producers lose revenue and surplus as cheaper imports replace output [1]; some workers lose jobs, so the net gain is below 600 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Label the height of each triangle as the intercept minus the price; the height is not the price itself.

**8. (a)** Before 600,000 / 50,000 = **12** [1]. After 1,500,000 / 200,000 = **7.50** [1]. Change = (7.5 - 12) / 12 = **-37.5%** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** Spreading fixed costs over more units lowers average cost [1]; competition can pass the saving on as a lower price [1]. A firm that faces little rivalry may simply absorb the saving in higher profit [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A full answer says why a lower cost becomes a lower price (competition), not just that it can.

**9. (a)** Profit shifted = (52 - 40) x 50,000 = **600,000** [1]. M loses 0.35 x 600,000 = **210,000**; N gains 0.15 x 600,000 = **90,000** [1]. Group saves **120,000** [1] **[3]**
**(b)** M loses tax revenue [1] which reduces funds for public spending [1]. M may also see its firms undercut, or it may need to enforce arm's-length rules at a cost [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the sign right when comparing: the group saves 210,000 minus 90,000.

**10. (a)** 11,000 - 15,000 = **-4,000** [1]; -4,000 / 2,000,000 x 100 = **-0.2%** [1] **[2]**
**(b)** Year 1: 30 / 18 = **1.67** [1]. Year 2: 36 / 15 = **2.40** [1] **[2]**
**(c)** Skilled workers and capital owners gain from larger markets [1], so their incomes rise faster [1]. Unskilled workers face competition from imports or cheaper labour abroad [1], and job losses may lower or flatten their pay [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name who gains and who loses; "inequality rises" alone is the claim, not the reason.

**11. (a)** 5 x 80,000 = **400,000** [1] **[1]**
**(b)** The market price reflects private cost only, not the external cost [1]. Output and trade go beyond the level where marginal social benefit equals marginal social cost [1], so there is overproduction and a welfare loss [1] **[3]**
**(c)** Large TNCs can threaten to relocate [1], and governments may relax rules to keep jobs and investment [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Refer to marginal social cost, not only to damage, so the economic link is explicit.

**12.** Benefits, credit three developed points such as: FDI and trade lift growth and jobs [1]; technology and scale lower costs [1]; more choice and lower prices raise living standards [1]. Costs, credit four developed points such as: displaced workers in uncompetitive industries [1]; exploitation or weak labour standards [1]; environmental damage [1]; profit shifting and tax loss through transfer pricing [1]. Evaluation, credit four such as: depends on institutions and tax rules [1]; time period, as costs come first [1]; whether winners can compensate losers [1]; the economy's diversification [1]. Reasoned conclusion that weighs the two sides [1] **[12]**
*Examiner insight:* Reward the judgement over the list: choose one or two factors and say why they decide the conclusion.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using exports instead of exports plus imports for trade as a share of GDP.
- Dividing by the wrong base for a percentage change.
- Stating a benefit with no mechanism.
- Writing about FDI without separating the TNC's gain from the host's gain.
- Forgetting that cheaper imports cut domestic producers' surplus.
- Treating lower average cost as automatically lower prices.
- Leaving out winners and losers in an evaluation.
- Ending a long answer without a judgement.
