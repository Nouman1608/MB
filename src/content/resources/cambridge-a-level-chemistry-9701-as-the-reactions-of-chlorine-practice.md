## Section A

**1.** Define the term *disproportionation*. **[1]**

**2.** Give the oxidation number of chlorine in each species: Cl₂, NaClO, NaClO₃, HCl. **[2]**

**3.** Name the compound NaClO₃. **[1]**

**4.** Write an ionic equation for the reaction of chlorine with cold aqueous sodium hydroxide. **[1]**

**5.** Write an equation for the reaction of chlorine with water. **[1]**

**6.** Name the two species formed from chlorine in water that kill bacteria. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Chlorine reacts with hot aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Write a balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Use oxidation numbers to show that this is a disproportionation reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain, in terms of electron transfer, why five moles of NaCl form for each mole of NaClO₃. **[2]**

**8.** Explain why the product of the reaction between chlorine and sodium hydroxide depends on the temperature. Include an equation. **[2]**

**9.** 2.13 g of chlorine gas is passed into an excess of cold aqueous sodium hydroxide.

**(a)** Calculate the amount, in moles, of Cl₂. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of sodium chlorate(I) formed. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the minimum volume of 0.400 mol dm⁻³ NaOH needed. **[2]**

**10.** 0.240 mol of chlorine reacts completely with hot aqueous sodium hydroxide. Calculate the masses of sodium chlorate(V) and sodium chloride formed. **[3]**

**11.** Explain, using equations, how chlorine is used to make drinking water safe. Show that the first reaction is a disproportionation. **[4]**

**12.** Chlorinated water contains the equilibrium HOCl ⇌ H⁺ + ClO⁻.

**(a)** State what type of acid HOCl is, and give its name. **[2]**

**(b)** Predict the effect on the concentration of ClO⁻ of adding a small amount of alkali, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**13.** When a solution of sodium chlorate(I) is heated, the chlorate(I) ions disproportionate.

**(a)** Write an ionic equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Use oxidation numbers to show that electrons lost equal electrons gained. **[2]**

**14.** A manufacturer wants to make 14.9 g of sodium chlorate(I) from chlorine and cold aqueous sodium hydroxide. Calculate the volume of chlorine gas needed at room conditions (molar gas volume 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹), and the amount of NaOH used. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** A redox reaction in which the same element is simultaneously oxidised and reduced [1].

**2.** Cl₂ 0; NaClO +1; NaClO₃ +5; HCl −1 [2 for all four, 1 for two or three].

**3.** Sodium chlorate(V) [1].

**4.** Cl₂ + 2OH⁻ → Cl⁻ + ClO⁻ + H₂O [1].

**5.** Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl [1].

**6.** HOCl [1] and ClO⁻ [1].

**7. (a)** 3Cl₂ + 6NaOH → 5NaCl + NaClO₃ + 3H₂O [1].

**(b)** Chlorine is reduced from 0 in Cl₂ to −1 in NaCl [1] and oxidised from 0 in Cl₂ to +5 in NaClO₃; the same element is both oxidised and reduced [1].

**(c)** Each chlorine atom reduced to Cl⁻ gains one electron, while each chlorine atom oxidised to ClO₃⁻ loses five electrons [1]. Electrons gained must equal electrons lost, so five atoms are reduced for every one oxidised [1].

**8.** In cold alkali the chlorate(I) ion, ClO⁻, is formed and is stable [1]. On heating, ClO⁻ disproportionates further, 3ClO⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻, so chlorate(V) forms instead [1].

**9. (a)** Mᵣ(Cl₂) = 71.0; amount = 2.13 ÷ 71.0 = 0.0300 mol [1].

**(b)** From Cl₂ + 2NaOH → NaCl + NaClO + H₂O, 1 mol Cl₂ gives 1 mol NaClO, so 0.0300 mol NaClO [1]. Mᵣ(NaClO) = 74.5; mass = 0.0300 × 74.5 = 2.24 g [1].

**(c)** Amount of NaOH = 2 × 0.0300 = 0.0600 mol [1]. Volume = 0.0600 ÷ 0.400 = 0.150 dm³ = 150 cm³ [1].

**10.** Amount of NaClO₃ = 0.240 ÷ 3 = 0.0800 mol; mass = 0.0800 × 106.5 = 8.52 g [1]. Amount of NaCl = 0.240 × 5 ÷ 3 = 0.400 mol [1]; mass = 0.400 × 58.5 = 23.4 g [1].

**11.** Cl₂ + H₂O ⇌ HCl + HOCl [1]. HOCl ⇌ H⁺ + ClO⁻ [1]. HOCl and ClO⁻ kill bacteria [1]. In the first reaction chlorine goes from 0 to −1 (in HCl) and from 0 to +1 (in HOCl), so it is both reduced and oxidised [1].

**12. (a)** A weak acid (partially dissociated) [1]; chloric(I) acid [1].

**(b)** The concentration of ClO⁻ increases [1]. The alkali removes H⁺ ions, so the equilibrium shifts to the right to replace them [1].

**13. (a)** 3ClO⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + ClO₃⁻ [1].

**(b)** Two Cl atoms go from +1 to −1, gaining 2 × 2 = 4 electrons [1]. One Cl atom goes from +1 to +5, losing 4 electrons, so electrons lost equal electrons gained [1].

**14.** Amount of NaClO = 14.9 ÷ 74.5 = 0.200 mol, so 0.200 mol Cl₂ is needed [1]. Volume = 0.200 × 24.0 = 4.80 dm³ [1]. Amount of NaOH = 2 × 0.200 = 0.400 mol [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the cold-alkali products for the hot reaction, or the reverse.
- Showing "disproportionation" without the oxidation numbers that prove it.
- Using a single arrow for the reaction of chlorine with water.
- Forgetting the 3 : 1 ratio of Cl₂ to NaClO₃ in calculations.
- Naming HCl, rather than HOCl and ClO⁻, as the species that kill bacteria.
- Forgetting that two moles of NaOH react with each mole of chlorine in the cold reaction, and six with three in the hot one.
- Rounding intermediate answers in multi-step calculations; keep extra figures until the final answer.
