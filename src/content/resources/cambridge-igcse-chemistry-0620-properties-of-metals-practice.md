## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which property is shown by most metals **and** by graphite?

A: brittle when solid

B: good electrical conductor

C: low melting point

D: forms an acidic oxide **[1]**

**2.** State the meaning of the term *ductile*. **[1]**

**3.** Compare the melting points and boiling points of metals and non-metals. **[2]**

**4.** Complete the word equations.

**(a)** calcium + water → ........ + ........ **[2]**

**(b)** zinc + sulfuric acid → ........ + ........ **[2]**

**5.** Magnesium reacts with steam.

**(a)** Name the solid product. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**6.** A saucepan has a copper base and a plastic handle. Use the properties of metals and non-metals to suggest why each material is used for its part of the pan. **[2]**

**7.** Copper is heated strongly in air. State **one** observation and name the product. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**8.** A student is given four unlabelled solid elements, J, K, L and M, and carries out tests on each.

| Element | Conducts electricity? | Effect of hitting with a hammer | Melting point / °C | Oxide in water, tested with universal indicator |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| J | No | Shatters | 44 | Red (acidic) |
| K | Yes | Flattens | 650 | Blue (alkaline) |
| L | Yes | Crumbles | 3550 | Orange (acidic) |
| M | Yes | Flattens | 1538 | Oxide insoluble; reacts with acid |

**(a)** Identify the **two** elements that are metals. **[1]**

**(b)** Give **two** pieces of evidence from the table that support your answer for element K. **[2]**

**(c)** Element L conducts electricity. Explain why it is still classified as a non-metal. **[2]**

**9.** Iron filings are added to dilute hydrochloric acid in a test tube.

**(a)** Describe **two** observations. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the salt formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe a test to identify the gas produced, and give the result. **[2]**

**(d)** Write a balanced symbol equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**10.** A small piece of sodium is placed on water containing a few drops of universal indicator.

**(a)** Write the word equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** State and explain the colour of the indicator after the reaction. **[2]**

**(c)** Sodium has a melting point of 98 °C. Explain why this does **not** mean sodium is a non-metal. **[2]**

**11.** Three metals are each tested with dilute hydrochloric acid, cold water and oxygen. One student writes the following conclusion:

"All metals react with dilute acids to give a salt and water, and all metals react with water to give a hydroxide."

Identify **two** errors in the student's statement and correct each one. **[4]**

**12.** Compare the physical properties of metals and non-metals. Your answer should refer to **four** different properties. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **B** [1]. Graphite and metals both conduct electricity; graphite is otherwise typical of a non-metal.

**2.** Can be drawn (stretched) out into a wire [1].

**3.** Metals usually have high melting and boiling points [1], whereas non-metals usually have low melting and boiling points [1].

**4. (a)** calcium hydroxide [1] + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** zinc sulfate [1] + hydrogen [1].

**5. (a)** Magnesium oxide [1].

**(b)** Mg + H₂O → MgO + H₂: correct formulae [1]; correct products, no balancing numbers needed [1].

**6.** Copper base: metals are good thermal conductors, so heat passes quickly to the food [1]. Plastic handle: non-metals (and plastics) are poor thermal conductors, so the handle stays cool enough to hold [1].

**7.** Observation: the surface turns black / no flame is seen [1]. Product: copper(II) oxide [1].

**8. (a)** K and M [1] (both needed).

**(b)** Any two: it conducts electricity [1]; it is malleable (flattens when hammered) [1]; its oxide gives an alkaline solution, so it is a basic oxide [1]. Maximum [2].

**(c)** It is brittle (crumbles when hit) [1] and it forms an acidic oxide [1]. These are typical non-metal properties; L behaves like graphite, the non-metal that conducts.

**9. (a)** Any two: bubbles / fizzing [1]; the iron gradually disappears or gets smaller [1]; the solution turns pale green [1]; the tube becomes warm [1]. Maximum [2].

**(b)** Iron(II) chloride [1].

**(c)** Hold a lighted splint at the mouth of the tube [1]; a squeaky pop shows hydrogen [1].

**(d)** Fe + 2HCl → FeCl₂ + H₂: correct formulae [1]; balanced [1].

**10. (a)** sodium + water → sodium hydroxide [1] + hydrogen [1].

**(b)** Blue or purple [1], because sodium hydroxide dissolves to form an alkaline solution [1].

**(c)** Sodium conducts electricity and is malleable / forms an alkaline (basic) oxide [1]; melting point is only a general trend, and sodium is a known exception with a low melting point [1].

**11.** Error 1: metals and dilute acids give a salt and **hydrogen**, not water [1]; also, not all metals react (copper, silver and gold do not) [1]. Error 2: not all metals react with cold water; some, such as magnesium, react quickly only with steam [1], and a metal reacting with steam forms an **oxide**, not a hydroxide [1]. Any four of these points, at least one from each error, [4].

**12.** One mark per correctly paired comparison, maximum [4]:

- Metals are good thermal conductors, whereas non-metals are poor thermal conductors [1].
- Metals are good electrical conductors, whereas non-metals are poor conductors (except graphite) [1].
- Metals are malleable and ductile, whereas solid non-metals are brittle [1].
- Metals usually have high melting and boiling points, whereas non-metals usually have low ones [1].

A property given for metals only, with no non-metal comparison, scores 0 for that row.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a hydroxide as the product of a metal reacting with steam.
- Writing "salt and water" for a metal and acid; water is formed when an acid reacts with a base, not with a metal.
- Using one property to classify an element when the other evidence disagrees.
- Writing a list of metal properties when the command word is "compare".
- Naming hydrogen as an observation instead of describing bubbles.
- Writing FeCl₃ for the product of iron with dilute hydrochloric acid.
