## The three properties

The syllabus says "use the following symmetry properties of circles", and expects them as reasons:

| Property (write it like this) | What it gives you |
|---|---|
| equal chords are equidistant from the centre | two chords of the same length are the same perpendicular distance from O, and the reverse |
| the perpendicular bisector of a chord passes through the centre | the perpendicular from O to a chord cuts it in half; two perpendicular bisectors meet at O |
| tangents from an external point are equal in length | PA = PB for tangents from P touching at A and B |

You will usually need the Extended Circle theorems I fact "angle between tangent and radius = 90°" alongside them.

## The two right-angled triangles

Almost every length question uses one of these.

**Chord triangle**

```
O = centre, AB = chord, M = foot of the perpendicular from O
OA = r (hypotenuse),  AM = AB ÷ 2,  OM = distance from centre to chord
r² = OM² + (AB ÷ 2)²
angle AOB = 2 × angle AOM
```

**Tangent triangle**

```
P = external point, A = point of contact
OA = r,  PA = tangent length,  OP = hypotenuse
OP² = r² + PA²
OAPB is a kite with right angles at A and B; OP is its line of symmetry
angle APB + angle AOB = 180°
```

The last line follows from the angle sum of a quadrilateral: 360° − 90° − 90° = 180°.

## Decide the case before calculating

| Situation | Two possible cases | What changes |
|---|---|---|
| Two parallel chords | same side of O, or opposite sides | distance between them is the **difference** or the **sum** of their distances from O |
| Liquid in a horizontal cylinder of radius r, depth d | less than half full, or more than half full | distance from O to the surface is **r − d** or **d − r** |
| Two equal, parallel, different chords | they must be on **opposite sides** of O | distance between them = 2 × distance from O |

If a question does not say which case applies, look for a word such as "less than half full" or a diagram marking; if there is none, both cases may need considering.

## Finding the centre

1. Choose two chords that are not parallel.
2. Find (or construct) the perpendicular bisector of each.
3. The bisectors cross at the centre, because each passes through it.
4. Radius = distance from the centre to any point on the circle; check with a second point.

With coordinates, a horizontal or vertical chord gives an easy bisector (x = constant or y = constant), so start with that one.

## Tangent lengths around a shape

When a circle touches every side of a polygon, each vertex is an external point, so its two tangent lengths are equal.

```
triangle with sides c = AB, a = BC, b = CA and tangent lengths x, y, z from A, B, C:
  x + y = c,  y + z = a,  z + x = b
  x + y + z = half the perimeter
  so x = (half perimeter) − a, and so on
quadrilateral with a circle touching all four sides:
  AB + CD = BC + DA   (each side uses one tangent length from each end)
```

## Worked reminder

A circle has radius 15 cm. A chord is 9 cm from the centre. Find its length.

```
half-chord = √(15² − 9²) = √(225 − 81) = √144 = 12 cm
chord = 24 cm
```

## Exam traps

- Using the whole chord in Pythagoras instead of half of it.
- Taking a slanted line from O as the distance to a chord; it must be perpendicular.
- Giving OP as the tangent length. The tangent length is PA.
- Adding distances for two chords on the same side of O.
- Forgetting the 90° between tangent and radius before using Pythagoras.
- Rounding the half-angle before doubling it on Paper 4.
- On Paper 2, leaving √50 instead of 5√2 when an exact answer is asked for.
- Writing "tangent theorem" as a reason instead of "tangents from an external point are equal in length".

## Self-test

1. Radius 17 cm, chord 30 cm. Find the distance from the centre to the chord.
2. A chord is 6 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 10 cm. Find the chord length.
3. Two chords of a circle are each 20 cm long. One is 4 cm from the centre. How far is the other from the centre?
4. Tangents from P touch a circle at A and B, and angle APB = 52°. Find angle PAB.
5. In question 4, find angle AOB.
6. OP = 26 cm and the radius is 10 cm. Find the tangent length.
7. Radius 5 cm, parallel chords of lengths 8 cm and 6 cm on the same side of O. Find the distance between them.
8. The same chords as question 7, but on opposite sides of O. Find the distance between them.
9. A circle touches all three sides of a triangle with sides 7 cm, 8 cm and 9 cm. Find the tangent length from the vertex between the 7 cm and 8 cm sides.
10. A circle touches all four sides of quadrilateral PQRS. PQ = 6, QR = 7, RS = 9. Find SP.
11. Chords AB and CD are equidistant from O, and AB = 11 cm. Write down the length of CD.
12. A circle passes through (2, 1) and (8, 1). Write down the equation of a line that must pass through its centre.

### Answers

1. √(289 − 225) = √64 = **8 cm**
2. Half-chord = √(100 − 36) = 8, chord = **16 cm**
3. **4 cm** (equal chords are equidistant from the centre)
4. (180 − 52) ÷ 2 = **64°**
5. 180 − 52 = **128°**
6. √(676 − 100) = √576 = **24 cm**
7. Distances 3 cm and 4 cm, so **1 cm**
8. 3 + 4 = **7 cm**
9. Half perimeter = 12; the opposite side is 9, so 12 − 9 = **3 cm**
10. 6 + 9 = 7 + SP, so **SP = 8**
11. **11 cm**
12. **x = 5** (the perpendicular bisector of the chord)
