## Questions

**1.** Define aggregate demand. **[2]**

**2.** Country G has consumption 450, investment 120, government spending 160, exports 140 and imports 170 (all in billion cu).

**(a)** Calculate aggregate demand. **[2]**
**(b)** Imports rise to 200 with everything else unchanged. Calculate the percentage change in aggregate demand, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**3.** State whether each event is a movement along a curve or a shift, and name the curve.

**(a)** A rise in income tax. **[1]**
**(b)** A fall in the price level caused by a rightward shift of SRAS. **[1]**
**(c)** Technology that raises the economy's productive capacity. **[1]**
**(d)** A rise in the price level caused by a rise in AD. **[1]**

**4.** Explain two reasons why the AD curve slopes downward. **[4]**

**5.** In Country H, AD output = 1,490 - 5P and SRAS output = 590 + 4P, where P is the price level index and output is in billion cu.

**(a)** Calculate equilibrium price level and real output. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the excess demand at P = 80 and explain what happens next. **[3]**
**(c)** Falling confidence cuts AD output by 45 at every price. Calculate the new equilibrium and the percentage change in price level and output. **[3]**

**6.** Distinguish between the causes of a shift in SRAS and a shift in LRAS. **[4]**

**7.** Country J has a three-section AS curve: flat at P = 40 up to output 500, rising to P = 100 at output 800 (P = 0.2 x output - 60 in this section), and vertical at 800. AD1 is output = 650 - 5P.

**(a)** Show that AD1 intersects the flat section and give equilibrium output and price level. **[2]**
**(b)** A later AD position gives equilibrium (output 650, P = 70). A further rise gives (output 800, P = 120). Calculate the percentage change in output and in price level between these two. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the price level rose by more than output, and what a further rise in AD would do. **[4]**

**8.** Business confidence falls in Country K, which has an upward sloping SRAS. Explain the effect on real output, the price level and employment. **[4]**

**9.** In Country L, AD output = 1,320 - 4P and SRAS output = 520 + 4P.

**(a)** Energy prices rise and SRAS output becomes 440 + 4P. Calculate the new equilibrium and the percentage changes in price level and output. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain the likely effect on employment. **[2]**

**10.** "An increase in aggregate demand always raises real output." Evaluate this statement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Aggregate demand is the total planned spending on an economy's output [1] at each price level in a given period [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Both the planned-spending idea and the link to the price level earn credit. Writing only "total demand in the economy" is too loose.

**2. (a)** AD = 450 + 120 + 160 + (140 - 170) [1] = **700** billion cu [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** New AD = 450 + 120 + 160 + (140 - 200) = 670 [1]. Change = -30/700 = **-4.3%** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show net exports as a bracket and subtract imports. A common slip is adding M, which gives a wrong total.

**3.** (a) Shift left of AD [1]. (b) Movement down along AD [1]. (c) Shift right of LRAS [1]. (d) Movement up along SRAS [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the curve as well as the type of change. A price level change caused by a shift on the other curve is a movement.

**4.** Wealth effect: a lower price level raises the real value of savings [1], so households spend more and the quantity of real output demanded rises [1]. International trade effect: lower domestic prices relative to foreign prices raise exports and lower imports [1], so net exports and AD rise [1]. (Interest rate effect is also acceptable.) **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each reason needs its own chain of cause and effect. Answers that argue consumers switch to substitutes describe a single market, not the whole price level.

**5. (a)** 1,490 - 5P = 590 + 4P [1], so 9P = 900, giving **P = 100 and output = 990** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** At P = 80: AD = 1,490 - 400 = 1,090 and SRAS = 590 + 320 = 910 [1]. Excess demand = **180** [1]. Stocks fall, so firms raise prices and output, moving the economy up to P = 100 [1]. **[3]**
**(c)** New AD: 1,445 - 5P = 590 + 4P, so 9P = 855 and **P = 95** [1], output = 590 + 380 = **970** [1]. Price level -5% and output -2.0% [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Set the two schedules equal, then substitute back into both to check they agree. State the direction of each percentage change.

**6.** SRAS shifts when costs of production change [1], for example wages, energy prices or business taxes [1]. LRAS shifts when productive capacity changes [1], for example the quantity or quality of labour and capital, technology or institutions [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Distinguish rather than list. Set the cost-versus-capacity contrast side by side and give an example for each curve.

**7. (a)** AD1 at P = 40: output = 650 - 200 = 450, which is below 500 so on the flat section [1]. Equilibrium **output 450, P = 40** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Output: (800 - 650)/650 = **+23.1%** [1]. Price level: (120 - 70)/70 = **+71.4%** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** At 650 the economy is on the upward sloping section, where bottlenecks appear [1], so extra demand raises costs and prices [1]. At 800 the economy reaches the vertical section, where output cannot increase [1]. A further rise in AD would raise only the price level, leaving output at 800 [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each result to a named section of the curve. Answers that describe the numbers without mentioning capacity do not explain.

**8.** Lower confidence reduces investment, so AD shifts left [1]. The economy moves down along SRAS to a lower price level [1] and lower real output [1]. Firms need less labour, so employment falls [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Say which curve moves and in which direction before describing the results. Cover all three variables the question names.

**9. (a)** 1,320 - 4P = 440 + 4P [1], so 8P = 880 and **P = 110**, output = 1,320 - 440 = **880** [1]. Price level +10% and output -4.3% [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** Output falls, so firms need less labour [1] and employment is likely to fall, so unemployment rises [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Check the new equilibrium in both equations. Make clear that a left shift of SRAS raises prices and lowers output together.

**10.** One-sided analysis: AD right raises output if the economy has spare capacity [1], since firms respond to extra demand with more output and employment [1]. Counter: if the economy is near capacity on SRAS or LRAS, extra AD mainly raises the price level [1], and on the vertical section of LRAS output cannot rise [1]. The effect depends on the slope of SRAS and on the shape of LRAS [1]. Other changes may offset it, for example a cost rise shifting SRAS left [1]. Time lags matter: firms may need time to hire or invest [1]. Judgement: "always" is too strong, because the output effect is large with spare capacity and zero at full capacity [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Evaluation gains credit when the judgement is supported by a condition, such as spare capacity or the shape of LRAS. A list of points with no conclusion is weaker.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Labelling the axes "price" and "quantity" in place of "price level" and "real output".
- Treating a change in the price level as a shift of AD.
- Adding imports into AD.
- Explaining the slope of AD with substitution between goods.
- Shifting LRAS when only costs have changed.
- Giving output and prices but not employment when all three are asked for.
- Reading percentage changes from the wrong base.
- Concluding without a condition in an evaluation answer.
