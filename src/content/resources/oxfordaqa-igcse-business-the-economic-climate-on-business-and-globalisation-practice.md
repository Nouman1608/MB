These questions cover sections 3.2.3 (The economic climate on business) and 3.2.4 (Globalisation) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225) specification, version 3.1, for exams from May/June 2022 onwards. Learn the ideas first in the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-the-economic-climate-on-business-and-globalisation/) and the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-the-economic-climate-on-business-and-globalisation-revision-notes/). There is also an earlier mixed set, the [Influences on business practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-influences-practice/). Useful links: the [course hub](/boards/oxfordaqa/igcse/business/), the [printable checklist](/checklists/oxfordaqa/igcse/business/) and the [free diagnostics](/diagnostics/). The leasing idea in question 5 is a general description of Sharia compliant finance, not a structure named in the specification. Currency amounts are in pounds; no exchange rate conversions are needed.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Define the term "overdraft". **[2]**
**(b)** State one difference between an overdraft and a loan. **[1]**

**2.** Identify **two** forms of globalisation. **[2]**

**3.** Grayling Coaches runs minibus tours. It has a variable-rate bank loan of £90,000. The rate rises from 5.5% to 7%. Its profit before interest is £16,000 a year, and the loan is its only borrowing.
**(a)** Calculate the increase in annual interest. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage fall in profit after interest, to one decimal place. **[3]**

**4.** Explain **one** way a rise in interest rates could reduce spending by households and **one** way it could reduce spending by businesses. **[4]**

**5.** The owner of Wolvesey Joinery avoids interest on religious grounds and needs a new machine. Explain how the owner could finance the machine without paying interest, and explain **one** way a rise in interest rates could still affect Wolvesey Joinery. **[4]**

**6.** Quillbrook Print Works makes wedding invitation packs and school exercise books. Job losses in the town lower household incomes. Monthly sales before and after are shown.

Invitation packs: 600 sold at £40, falling to 480 at £40.
Exercise books: 2,000 sold at £6, falling to 1,920 at £6.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage fall in total monthly revenue, to one decimal place. **[4]**
**(b)** Analyse why the fall in sales differs between the two products. **[4]**

**7.** Holmfirth Tech Repairs mends phones and laptops. Employment in its town rises sharply. Explain **one** benefit and **one** problem this may bring to the business. **[4]**

**8.** Thrushfield Orchards exports apple juice at a fixed price in its home currency. It sells 50,000 bottles a year overseas and makes a profit of £0.60 a bottle. The home currency strengthens, and overseas sales fall to 45,000 bottles.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in profit from these exports. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a stronger home currency can reduce export sales. **[3]**

**9.** Lyncombe Textiles imports cotton. After the home currency weakens, the cost of a roll rises from £80 to £92 in home currency. It sells 1,500 rolls a year at £120 a roll, printed in a catalogue that cannot change until next year. Ignore other costs.
**(a)** Calculate the fall in total profit. **[3]**
**(b)** The currency has moved sharply and unpredictably for three months. Analyse **two** problems this causes the business. **[4]**

**10.** Ambleby Foods is in a developing country. It makes tinned mango, has a relative cost advantage in fruit and labour, and sells to overseas supermarkets. A large multinational has started selling similar products in its home market. Analyse **one** benefit and **one** drawback of globalisation for Ambleby Foods, and **one** effect on the country's economy. **[6]**

**11.** Oxbury Components, in a developed country, makes 200,000 units a year at £18 a unit, of which labour is £7. It is considering moving production to a developing country, where labour would be £2.50 a unit and transport would add £1.20 a unit. The move would cost £1.5 million to set up and would end 120 local jobs.
**(a)** Calculate the annual saving from moving. **[3]**
**(b)** Evaluate whether Oxbury Components should move production. **[7]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** An arrangement with a bank that lets a business spend more than its account holds [1], up to an agreed limit, with interest charged on the amount overdrawn [1].
**(b)** A loan is a fixed sum repaid over an agreed time, while an overdraft is flexible and short-term [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns most when it contains both the idea of borrowing through the account and the idea of a limit or interest; "borrowing money" alone is too general.

**2.** Any two of: specialisation by country [1]; relative cost advantage [1]; movement of labour; larger markets; international competition. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the wording of the five listed forms. A made-up form that sounds sensible may not be recognised.

**3. (a)** Before: 90,000 x 5.5% = £4,950 [1]. After: 90,000 x 7% = £6,300, so the increase is **£1,350** [1].
**(b)** Profit after interest before = 16,000 - 4,950 = £11,050 [1]. After = 16,000 - 6,300 = £9,700 [1]. Fall = 1,350 / 11,050 x 100 = **12.2%** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show each step. A common slip is dividing by £16,000 instead of by profit after interest, which gives 8.4% and is not the question asked.

**4.** Households: a rise in rates raises mortgage and credit repayments [1], leaving less income to spend [1]. Businesses: dearer borrowing makes firms delay buying machinery or vehicles [1], so firms that supply those goods sell less [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each point needs a reason and a consequence. "People spend less" with no link to borrowing costs gains little.

**5.** Wolvesey could lease the machine: a financier buys it and leases it to the business for an agreed payment [1], so no interest is charged and the cost is not tied to the rate [1]. It could still be affected through customers: households with higher borrowing costs spend less on fitted joinery [1], so sales and profit could fall [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming the structure and then explaining how it avoids interest is better than the phrase "Islamic finance" alone. Remember the second part: the question asks about an effect that remains.

**6. (a)** Revenue before = 600 x 40 + 2,000 x 6 = 24,000 + 12,000 = £36,000 [1]. After: invitations 480 x 40 = £19,200 [1] and books 1,920 x 6 = £11,520 [1]; total £30,720. Fall = 5,280 / 36,000 x 100 = **14.7%** [1].
**(b)** Invitation packs are a purchase households can postpone or scale down [1], so when incomes fall their sales drop sharply (by 20%) [1]. Exercise books are a necessity for school [1], so families keep buying and sales fall by only 4% [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the percentages given (20% and 4%) when you explain the difference; a general statement about luxuries with no figures reads as unsupported.

**7.** Benefit: more households have wages [1], so more spend on repairs or replacing devices [1]. Problem: it is harder to recruit technicians [1], and the shop may need to raise pay, which increases costs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A benefit and a problem must both be developed. Two benefits, however well explained, do not answer the question.

**8. (a)** Before: 50,000 x 0.60 = £30,000. After: 45,000 x 0.60 = £27,000. Fall = **£3,000** [2].
**(b)** The price paid by overseas buyers, in their own currency, rises [1], so the juice becomes dearer compared with local or rival products [1], so some buyers switch and fewer bottles are sold [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The key link is from the stronger currency to the overseas price; skipping it and writing "demand falls" is a common loss of credit.

**9. (a)** Profit per roll before = 120 - 80 = £40; after = 120 - 92 = £28 [1]. Fall per roll = £12 [1]. Total fall = 12 x 1,500 = **£18,000** [1].
**(b)** First, costs are hard to predict [1], so the firm cannot be sure of its margin when it orders [1]. Second, the catalogue price cannot change this year [1], so each further rise in the cost of cotton comes straight out of profit [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie both problems to this firm (imported cotton, fixed catalogue) rather than listing general uncertainty.

**10.** Benefit: the relative cost advantage lets it win large overseas orders [1], so sales volume and profit can rise [1]. Drawback: the multinational competes in its home market with bigger resources [1], so it may lose share or have to cut price [1]. Economy: orders and investment create jobs and tax income [1], though profits made by the multinational may leave the country [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each part (benefit, drawback, economy) so that the three required elements are easy to find. A paragraph about "globalisation" in general loses the link to Ambleby Foods.

**11. (a)** New unit cost = 18 - 7 + 2.5 + 1.2 = £14.70 [1]. Saving per unit = £3.30 [1]. Annual saving = 3.30 x 200,000 = **£660,000** [1].
**(b)** For: the relative cost advantage saves £660,000 a year [1], so the £1.5 million set-up cost is recovered in about 2.3 years [1]. The lower cost could allow lower prices or more profit [1]. Against: 120 local jobs would end, which may damage reputation and morale [1]; a long supply chain adds risk, and an exchange rate swing could cut the saving [1]. Judgement: the move seems justified if Oxbury can protect quality and expects to trade for several years [1], but it should [1] plan for currency movements, because the saving is large only while costs stay as given. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation is stronger when the judgement follows from the figures and names a condition; an answer that lists pros and cons and stops will not show weighing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an economic factor without explaining how it reaches cost, price, sales or profit.
- Giving one channel for interest rates and ignoring customers' spending.
- Dividing by the wrong base when finding a percentage fall.
- Getting exporter and importer the wrong way round for a currency movement.
- Writing about the economy when the question asks about the business.
- Offering a general judgement that is not linked to the figures or the case.
- Treating Sharia compliant finance as having no payments at all.
