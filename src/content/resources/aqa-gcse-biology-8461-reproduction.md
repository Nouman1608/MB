This guide goes deeper into section **4.6.1 Reproduction** of the AQA GCSE Biology (8461) specification, for
first teaching from 2016. It covers 4.6.1.1 to 4.6.1.8: sexual and asexual reproduction, meiosis, the advantages

specification marks (HT only) is labelled **Higher tier only**.

## 4.6.1.1 Sexual and asexual reproduction

| | Sexual | Asexual |
|---|---|---|
| Parents | Two | One |
| Gametes | Fusion of male and female gametes: sperm and egg (animals); pollen and egg (flowering plants) | No fusion of gametes |
| Genetic information | Mixed | Not mixed |
| Offspring | Varied | Genetically identical (clones) |
| Cell division | Meiosis makes gametes | Only mitosis |

The core idea: **meiosis leads to non-identical cells; mitosis leads to identical cells.**

## 4.6.1.2 Meiosis

Cells in reproductive organs divide by **meiosis** to form gametes. The specification wants three facts and one
explanation.

1. **Copies of the genetic information are made.**
2. **The cell divides twice** to form **four gametes**, each with a **single set of chromosomes**.
3. **All gametes are genetically different** from each other.

The explanation: meiosis **halves** the chromosome number, so that when two gametes join at **fertilisation**, the
**normal number is restored**. The fertilised cell then divides by **mitosis**, the number of cells increases,
and as the embryo develops the cells **differentiate**. You do not need the stages of meiosis.

**Worked example 1.** A dog's body cells contain 78 chromosomes.

```
gametes:          78 ÷ 2 = 39 chromosomes (one set)
fertilised egg:   39 + 39 = 78 chromosomes (two sets restored)
embryo cells:     78 (mitosis keeps the number)
```

## 4.6.1.3 Advantages of each method (Biology only)

| Sexual reproduction | Asexual reproduction |
|---|---|
| Produces variation in the offspring | Only one parent needed |
| If the environment changes, variation gives a survival advantage by natural selection | More time and energy efficient: no need to find a mate |
| Natural selection can be speeded up by humans in selective breeding to increase food production | Faster than sexual reproduction |
| | Many identical offspring can be produced when conditions are favourable |

Some organisms use both, depending on circumstances:

- **Malarial parasites** reproduce asexually in the human host, but sexually in the mosquito.
- **Many fungi** reproduce asexually by spores, but also sexually to give variation.
- **Many plants** produce seeds sexually, but also reproduce asexually by **runners** (such as strawberry plants)
  or **bulb division** (such as daffodils).

Your knowledge of organisms is restricted to these, but you must explain advantages and disadvantages for any
organism from given information.

## 4.6.1.4 DNA and the genome

- The genetic material in the nucleus is **DNA**, a **polymer** of **two strands forming a double helix**.
- DNA is contained in **chromosomes**.
- A **gene** is a small section of DNA on a chromosome. Each gene **codes for a particular sequence of amino acids**,
  to make a specific protein.
- The **genome** is the **entire genetic material** of an organism.

The whole human genome has been studied. Its importance, as limited by the specification:

- the **search for genes linked to different types of disease**;
- **understanding and treatment of inherited disorders**;
- **tracing human migration patterns from the past**.

## 4.6.1.5 DNA structure (Biology only)

DNA is a polymer made from four different **nucleotides**. Each nucleotide is a **common sugar and phosphate
group** with **one of four bases** (A, C, G or T) attached to the sugar. The long strands have **alternating sugar
and phosphate sections**, with a base attached to each sugar.

**Higher tier only:**

- In the complementary strands, **C always links to G** and **T to A**.
- A **sequence of three bases** codes for a particular amino acid; the **order of bases** controls the order of
  amino acids in the protein.
- **Protein synthesis:** proteins are made on **ribosomes** according to a **template**. **Carrier molecules** bring
  specific amino acids in the correct order. The finished chain **folds into a unique shape**, which lets the
  protein work as an **enzyme**, a **hormone** or a structural protein such as **collagen**.
- **Mutations** occur continuously. Most do not alter the protein, or alter it only slightly. A few produce a
  protein with a **different shape**: an enzyme may no longer fit its substrate, or a structural protein may lose
  its strength.
- **Non-coding DNA** can switch genes on and off, so variations there may affect **how genes are expressed**.

**Worked example 2 (Higher tier only).** A gene section reads `TACGGA` on one strand. The complementary strand is
`ATGCCT`. Six bases make two triplets, so this section codes for **two** amino acids.

## 4.6.1.6 Genetic inheritance

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Gamete | Sex cell with one set of chromosomes |
| Chromosome | A structure containing DNA |
| Gene | A section of DNA coding for a protein |
| Allele | A different form of a gene |
| Dominant | Always expressed, even with one copy |
| Recessive | Only expressed with two copies |
| Homozygous | Two identical alleles |
| Heterozygous | Two different alleles |
| Genotype | The alleles present |
| Phenotype | The characteristic expressed |

Some characteristics are controlled by a **single gene**, such as **fur colour in mice** and **red-green colour
blindness** in humans, but **most characteristics result from multiple genes interacting**.

**Worked example 3** (invented example). In a plant, tall (T) is dominant to short (t). A heterozygous tall plant
is crossed with a short plant.

```
Parents:     Tt   ×   tt
Gametes:   T or t     t

          |  T  |  t
     -----+-----+-----
       t  |  Tt |  tt
       t  |  Tt |  tt
```

Offspring: 2 Tt (tall) : 2 tt (short), a **1 : 1** ratio, so the probability of a short plant is **0.5** (50%).
Out of 60 seeds, about 30 would be expected to grow short; the real number varies because fertilisation is random.

Constructing a Punnett square yourself and using it to predict with probability is **Higher tier only**; all
students must complete one and interpret crosses and family trees.

## 4.6.1.7 Inherited disorders

- **Polydactyly** (extra fingers or toes) is caused by a **dominant** allele, so one affected parent can pass it on.
- **Cystic fibrosis** (a disorder of cell membranes) is caused by a **recessive** allele, so two unaffected
  **carrier** parents can have an affected child.

**Embryo screening** can identify embryos with these alleles. You must make informed judgements about its
economic, social and ethical issues from information given: it may reduce suffering, but some people object to
destroying embryos, and there are concerns about cost and where screening might lead.

**Reading a family tree.** If two unaffected parents have an affected child, the allele must be **recessive** and
both parents must be heterozygous carriers.

## 4.6.1.8 Sex determination

Body cells contain **23 pairs** of chromosomes. **22 pairs** control characteristics only; **one pair** carries the
genes that determine sex. Females are **XX**; males are **XY**.

```
          |  X  |  X
     -----+-----+-----
       X  |  XX |  XX
       Y  |  XY |  XY
```

Ratio 1 : 1, so every pregnancy has a 50% chance of a boy and a 50% chance of a girl.

## Common mistakes

- Saying meiosis makes two cells, or that gametes are identical.
- Confusing gene and allele.
- Treating a predicted ratio as a guaranteed outcome.
- Writing that a recessive allele is "weaker" or "less common".
- Saying a mutation always changes the protein (Higher tier only).
- Forgetting that most characteristics are controlled by several genes.

## Exam technique for AQA command words

- **Explain** why meiosis is needed: halving, then restoring at fertilisation.
- **Complete or construct** a cross: show parents, gametes, grid, offspring genotypes and phenotypes, then the ratio
  or probability.
- **Evaluate** embryo screening: arguments for and against, from the information, and a conclusion.
- **Describe** protein synthesis (Higher tier only): ribosome, template, carrier molecules, order, folding.
