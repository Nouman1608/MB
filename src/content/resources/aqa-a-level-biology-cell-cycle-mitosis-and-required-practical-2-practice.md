## How should you use these questions?

Write full answers under timed conditions (roughly one minute per mark), then mark them against the answers. AQA
mark schemes reward precise terms: chromatids, centromeres, spindle fibres, interphase.

## Questions

**1.** State the stage of the cell cycle in which DNA replication occurs, and explain why it must occur before
mitosis. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the behaviour of chromosomes during prophase and metaphase of mitosis. **[4]**

**3.** In anaphase, the chromatids appear V-shaped as they move. Explain this appearance. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why the two daughter cells produced by mitosis are genetically identical to each other and to the
parent cell. **[2]**

**5.** A drug used in cancer treatment prevents spindle fibres from forming.

**(a)** Explain how this drug slows the growth of a tumour. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest why patients given this drug may lose hair. **[2]**

**6.** A student prepared a squash of a garlic root tip.

**(a)** Explain why the student used the tip of the root. **[1]**
**(b)** Give the purpose of placing the root tip in warm hydrochloric acid. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the student pressed the cover slip down firmly, without twisting it. **[2]**

**7.** The student counted cells in one field of view.

| Stage | Number of cells |
|---|---|
| Interphase | 112 |
| Prophase | 21 |
| Metaphase | 9 |
| Anaphase | 4 |
| Telophase | 4 |

**(a)** Calculate the mitotic index. **[2]**
**(b)** The cell cycle in this tissue takes 18 hours. Calculate how long, in minutes, a cell spends in metaphase. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest **two** ways the student could make the mitotic index more representative of the whole root tip. **[2]**

**8.** A cell in prophase measures 36 mm on a photomicrograph taken at a magnification of x 1500. Calculate its
actual length in micrometres. **[2]**

**9.** Two root tips were treated with different concentrations of a chemical. Tip A gave a mitotic index of 0.21;
tip B gave 0.09. A student concludes the chemical at the concentration used for B inhibits cell division. Give
**two** pieces of information you would need before accepting this conclusion. **[2]**

**10.** Describe binary fission in a bacterium. **[3]**

**11.** Explain why viruses are not described as undergoing cell division, and describe how new virus particles
are produced. **[3]**

**12.** Not all cells in a multicellular organism retain the ability to divide. Give the term for the repeating
sequence of events shown by cells that do, and name its two main phases. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Interphase [1]. So that each chromosome is made of two identical sister chromatids, one of which passes to
each daughter cell [1].

**2.** Prophase: chromosomes condense / shorten and thicken [1]; each is seen as two chromatids joined at a
centromere, and the nuclear envelope breaks down [1]. Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the equator of the cell [1];
spindle fibres attach to the centromeres [1].

**3.** Spindle fibres are attached to the centromeres and shorten, pulling the centromere first [1], so the arms of
each chromatid trail behind it [1].

**4.** DNA is replicated (semi-conservatively) before division, so the sister chromatids are identical copies [1].
Sister chromatids are separated so that each daughter cell receives one copy of every chromosome [1].

**5. (a)** Chromatids cannot be separated, so mitosis cannot be completed [1]; the rate of cell division in the
tumour falls, so it grows more slowly [1].
**(b)** Hair follicle cells also divide rapidly by mitosis [1], so the drug stops them dividing too and hair is not
replaced [1].

**6. (a)** The tip contains the meristem, where cells are dividing by mitosis [1].
**(b)** To separate the cells (break down the material holding them together) so they can be spread, and/or to stop
mitosis [1].
**(c)** To spread the cells into a single layer so light passes through and chromosomes can be seen [1]; twisting
would break the chromosomes or tear the cells / damage the slide [1].

**7. (a)** Cells in mitosis = 21 + 9 + 4 + 4 = 38 [1]; total = 112 + 38 = 150;
mitotic index = 38 / 150 = **0.25** (0.253) [1].
**(b)** Proportion in metaphase = 9 / 150 = 0.06 [1]; 0.06 x 18 hours = 1.08 hours = **64.8 minutes** (about 65
minutes) [1].
**(c)** Any two [1 each]: count several fields of view; use several root tips (or slides); count a larger number of
cells; choose fields of view at random.

**8.** Actual size = 36 mm / 1500 = 0.024 mm [1] = **24 micrometres** [1].

**9.** Any two [1 each]: the mitotic index for an untreated (control) tip; the number of cells / fields of view
counted; whether repeats gave similar results (and their spread); whether the tips came from the same plant, of the
same age, and were taken from the same region of the root.

**10.** The circular DNA replicates [1]; plasmids replicate [1]; the cytoplasm divides to give two daughter cells,
each with one copy of the circular DNA and a variable number of plasmids [1].

**11.** Viruses are non-living / are not cells [1]. The virus injects its nucleic acid into a host cell [1]; the host
cell replicates the viral nucleic acid and makes new virus particles, which are released [1].

**12.** The cell cycle [1]; interphase and mitosis (with cytokinesis) [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "chromosomes separate" in anaphase instead of "sister chromatids separate".
- Leaving out the centromere when describing spindle attachment.
- Rounding too early in time calculations, or giving hours when minutes are asked for.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to micrometres (multiply by 1000).
- Saying the acid "kills" the cells without explaining why that helps.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is cytokinesis part of mitosis?**
The specification treats mitosis as nuclear division and says cytokinesis usually follows. When asked to recognise
stages of the cell cycle, telophase is listed "including cytokinesis".

**What counts as "in mitosis" for the mitotic index?**
Cells in prophase, metaphase, anaphase and telophase. Interphase cells are not dividing.

**How do I find the time spent in one stage?**
Multiply the fraction of cells in that stage by the length of the whole cell cycle.
