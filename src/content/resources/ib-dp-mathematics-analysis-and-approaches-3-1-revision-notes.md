These notes condense **section 3.1** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: distance and midpoint in three dimensions, volume and surface area of solids, and angles between lines and planes. It is SL content, examined at SL and HL. Explanations and worked examples are in the [3D geometry study guide](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-1/); exam-style questions are in the [3D geometry practice set](/resources/ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-1-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Coordinates in 3D**

- Distance AB = √((x₁ − x₂)² + (y₁ − y₂)² + (z₁ − z₂)²).
- Midpoint = ((x₁ + x₂)/2, (y₁ + y₂)/2, (z₁ + z₂)/2).
- Reverse midpoint: B = 2M − A, coordinate by coordinate.
- Unknown coordinate from a distance: square both sides first; expect two answers.
- Right angle test: compare squared sides, a² + b² = c².
- Isosceles test: two equal lengths (compare squares).

**Volumes**

- Right pyramid: (1/3) × base area × perpendicular height.
- Right cone: (1/3)πr²h.
- Sphere: (4/3)πr³. Hemisphere: (2/3)πr³.
- Cylinder and prism (prior learning): cross-section area × length.
- Combination: add the volumes of the parts (or subtract a part removed).

**Surface areas**

- Cone: curved πrl, with l² = r² + h²; add πr² if the base is exposed.
- Sphere: 4πr². Hemisphere: curved 2πr², total 3πr².
- Cylinder: curved 2πrh; each end πr².
- Pyramid: base plus each triangular face; use the slant height of each face, not of the edge.
- Combination: only faces exposed to the outside count.

**Angles in solids**

- Line and plane: angle between the line and its projection onto the plane.
- Two intersecting lines: use the triangle they form with the segment joining their ends.
- SL exams: only right-angled trigonometry is set on 3D shapes.
- HL: be ready for the sine or cosine rule in a 3D triangle too.
- Name the triangle (for example "triangle VMA, right angle at M") before calculating.

**Method: angle between a line and a plane**

1. Mark the point where the line meets the plane.
2. From another point on the line, drop a perpendicular to the plane.
3. Join the foot of the perpendicular to the meeting point: this is the projection.
4. Solve the right-angled triangle; the angle sits at the meeting point.

**Method: composite solid**

1. Sketch the parts separately and label shared radii.
2. Add (or subtract) the volumes.
3. List every outside face by name, then add only those areas.
4. Check that no circle is counted where two parts join.

**Accuracy**

- Exact answers: keep π and surds (√61, 108π).
- Otherwise 3 significant figures; angles to one decimal place in degrees usually match this.
- Store unrounded lengths for the next step.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Right pyramid | Apex vertically above the centre of the base |
| Right cone | Apex vertically above the centre of the circular base |
| Slant height l | Distance from apex to the rim of a cone's base |
| Hemisphere | Half a sphere, cut through its centre |
| Projection | The "shadow" of a line on a plane, from perpendiculars |
| Space diagonal | Diagonal of a cuboid joining opposite corners through the interior |
| Composite solid | A solid made by joining two or more simpler solids |

## Exam traps

- (2 − (−4))² = 36, not 4.
- The midpoint averages; it does not halve one point.
- πrl uses the slant height; (1/3)πr²h uses the vertical height.
- Glued faces are not part of the surface area.
- A hemisphere's total surface is 3πr² only when the flat face is exposed.
- The angle with a base is measured to the base diagonal (the projection), not to an edge.
- Rounding √52 to 7.2 and then using it changes the third significant figure.

## Self-test

1. Find the distance between (0, −3, 5) and (4, 1, 3).
2. Find the midpoint of (−2, 7, 0) and (6, −1, −9).
3. M(1, 0, −2) is the midpoint of [AB] and A is (4, −3, 5). Find B.
4. A(0, 0, 0), B(2, 1, 2) and C(1, −2, 0). Show that the triangle is right-angled at A.
5. Find the volume of a right pyramid with a 5 cm by 8 cm rectangular base and height 9 cm.
6. A right cone of height 9 cm has volume 75π cm³. Find its radius.
7. Find the curved surface area of a hemisphere of radius 4 cm.
8. A sphere has surface area 100π cm². Find its exact volume.
9. A cylinder of radius 2 cm and height 5 cm has a cone of radius 2 cm and height 3 cm on top. Find the exact volume.
10. Find the length of the space diagonal of a 2 cm by 3 cm by 6 cm cuboid (height 6 cm).
11. Find the angle between that diagonal and the 2 cm by 3 cm base.
12. Which triangle do you use for the angle between a slant edge VA of a right pyramid and its base?

### Answers

1. √(16 + 16 + 4) = 6.
2. (2, 3, −4.5).
3. B = (2 − 4, 0 + 3, −4 − 5) = (−2, 3, −9).
4. AB² = 9, AC² = 5, BC² = 1 + 9 + 4 = 14 = 9 + 5, so the right angle is at A.
5. (1/3)(40)(9) = 120 cm³.
6. (1/3)πr²(9) = 75π gives r² = 25, so r = 5 cm.
7. 2π(16) = 32π cm².
8. 4πr² = 100π gives r = 5; V = (4/3)π(125) = 500π/3 cm³.
9. 20π + 4π = 24π cm³.
10. √(4 + 9 + 36) = 7 cm.
11. Base diagonal √13; tan θ = 6/√13, θ = 59.0°.
12. Triangle VMA, where M is the centre of the base; the right angle is at M.
