## Section A

**1.** Name the outermost of the four layers of the atmosphere named in the syllabus. **[1]**

**2.** State the percentage of clean air that is nitrogen. **[1]**

**3.** Name **two** gases, other than nitrogen and oxygen, that are found in clean air. **[2]**

**4.** Define *climate*. **[1]**

**5.** State whether each of these statements describes weather or climate.
(a) "There was thick fog at the airport this morning." **[1]**
(b) "The coast usually receives most of its rain between June and September." **[1]**
(c) "Yesterday's maximum temperature was 41 °C." **[1]**

**6.** State the location of the ozone layer. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A diagram shows the four layers of the atmosphere labelled W, X, Y and Z, with W nearest the ground and Z the highest.
(a) Name layers X and Z. **[2]**
(b) State which labelled layer contains the ozone layer, and in which part of that layer it is found. **[1]**

**8.** A school hall measures 20 m by 15 m by 5 m.
(a) Calculate the volume of oxygen in the hall, assuming it is filled with clean air. **[1]**
(b) Calculate the volume of nitrogen in the hall. **[1]**
(c) Suggest why the syllabus does not give an exact percentage for water vapour in clean air. **[1]**

**9.** A student drew a pie chart of clean air with these values: nitrogen 70%, oxygen 21%, carbon dioxide 8%, argon 1%. Identify **two** errors in the student's values. **[2]**

**10.** These statements describe the natural greenhouse effect but are in the wrong order.
- P: Some solar radiation is absorbed and re-emitted back to the Earth's surface by greenhouse gases and clouds.
- Q: Solar radiation passes through the Earth's atmosphere.
- R: This warms the Earth enough to support life.
- S: Some solar radiation is absorbed by the land and oceans, heating the Earth, and some is reflected back into space.

(a) Write the letters in the correct order. **[2]**
(b) Describe **two** different roles that clouds play in the natural greenhouse effect. **[2]**

**11.** Mean winter temperatures in a town over six years were 12 °C, 14 °C, 11 °C, 13 °C, 9 °C and 13 °C. The figures are invented for practice.
(a) Calculate the mean winter temperature over the six years. **[1]**
(b) A newspaper headline says: "Town's climate has changed: coldest winter in 20 years". Explain why this headline is not a correct use of the term *climate*. **[2]**

**12.** Carbon dioxide makes up only about 0.04% of clean air. Explain why gases that form such a small part of the atmosphere are still important to life on Earth. **[3]**

**13.** Describe the structure and composition of the atmosphere, and explain how its gases keep the Earth warm enough to support life. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Thermosphere **[1]**.

**2.** 78% **[1]**.

**3.** Any two of argon, carbon dioxide and water vapour: first **[1]**; second **[1]**.

**4.** The weather conditions in a location based on the weather over many years **[1]**.

**5.** (a) Weather **[1]**. (b) Climate **[1]**. (c) Weather **[1]**.

**6.** In the lower stratosphere **[1]**.

**7.** (a) X: stratosphere **[1]**; Z: thermosphere **[1]**.
(b) Layer X, in its lower part **[1]**.

**8.** (a) Volume of hall = 20 × 15 × 5 = 1,500 m³; oxygen = 1,500 × 0.21 = 315 m³ **[1]**.
(b) Nitrogen = 1,500 × 0.78 = 1,170 m³ **[1]**.
(c) The amount of water vapour varies from place to place and from day to day, for example it is higher in warm, humid air **[1]**.

**9.** Nitrogen should be 78%, not 70% **[1]**; carbon dioxide is far too high, as it is only a tiny fraction of a per cent (about 0.04%), and water vapour has been left out **[1]**.

**10.** (a) Q, S, P, R. Q first and R last **[1]**; S before P **[1]**.
(b) Clouds reflect some solar radiation back into space **[1]**; clouds also absorb some radiation and re-emit it back to the Earth's surface **[1]**.

**11.** (a) Total = 12 + 14 + 11 + 13 + 9 + 13 = 72; 72 ÷ 6 = 12 °C **[1]**.
(b) One cold winter is weather, not climate **[1]**; climate is based on the weather over many years, so a change in climate would need the long-term average to change, not a single unusual season **[1]**.

**12.** Carbon dioxide and water vapour are greenhouse gases **[1]**. They absorb radiation and re-emit it back towards the Earth's surface **[1]**, so even in small amounts they keep the Earth warm enough to support life **[1]**.

**13.** One mark per point, to a maximum of 6.
- The lowest layer is the troposphere, with the stratosphere above it **[1]**.
- Above these are the mesosphere and then the thermosphere **[1]**.
- The ozone layer is in the lower stratosphere **[1]**.
- Clean air is 78% nitrogen and 21% oxygen **[1]**.
- The rest is made up of small amounts of carbon dioxide, argon and water vapour **[1]**.
- Greenhouse gases such as carbon dioxide and water vapour, with clouds, absorb some solar radiation and re-emit it back to the surface, warming the Earth enough to support life **[1]**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- **Question 4**: leaving out "over many years", which is the part of the definition that separates climate from weather.
- **Question 5(b)**: calling a usual seasonal pattern "weather". "Usually" signals a long-term average.
- **Question 7(b)**: naming the stratosphere but not saying the ozone layer is in its lower part.
- **Question 9**: noticing only the nitrogen error; the carbon dioxide value is wildly too high.
- **Question 13**: describing layers and gases but not linking the gases to the greenhouse effect.
