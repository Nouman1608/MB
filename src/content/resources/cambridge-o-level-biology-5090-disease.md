This guide covers subtopic **12.1 Disease** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series. The syllabus asks you to describe a pathogen and a transmissible disease, to understand direct and indirect transmission, to describe the body's barriers to pathogens (skin, hairs in the nose, mucus and stomach acid), to understand the mosquito as a vector, to describe malaria and its control, to explain that HIV is a viral pathogen and describe its transmission, link to AIDS and control, to describe cholera, its toxin and its control by clean water and sanitation, to describe the effects of excessive alcohol, and to describe the effects of tobacco smoke and its major toxic components.

## Pathogens and transmissible diseases

A **pathogen** is a disease-causing organism. Pathogens include bacteria, viruses, fungi and protoctists.

A **transmissible disease** is a disease in which the pathogen can be passed from one host to another. Not all diseases are transmissible. Scurvy (a lack of vitamin C) and coronary heart disease are not passed from person to person.

### How pathogens are transmitted

| Type | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct contact** | The pathogen passes straight from an infected person, including through blood or other body fluids | Sexual contact, blood-to-blood contact, from mother to baby |
| **Indirect transmission** | The pathogen passes by an intermediate route | Contaminated surfaces or food, contaminated water, animals (vectors), the air in droplets from coughs and sneezes |

## The body's barriers to pathogens

The body has defences that stop most pathogens getting in.

| Barrier | How it protects |
|---|---|
| **Skin** | A tough outer layer that pathogens cannot easily penetrate; the cuts it receives are sealed by blood clots |
| **Hairs in the nose** | Trap dust and pathogens in the air that we breathe in |
| **Mucus** | Sticky fluid in the airways that traps pathogens; cilia sweep it up to be swallowed or coughed out |
| **Stomach acid** | Hydrochloric acid kills most pathogens that are swallowed with food and drink |

## Malaria

**Cause**: a protoctist called *Plasmodium*, which is a **parasite** (an organism that lives in or on another organism, the host, and harms it).

**Vector**: the female *Anopheles* mosquito. A **vector** is an organism that carries a pathogen from one host to another without usually being harmed itself.

**Transmission**: the mosquito feeds on blood from an infected person and takes in the parasite. When it bites another person, the parasite passes into that person's blood in the mosquito's saliva. The parasite then infects the liver and red blood cells, causing repeated fevers, shivering, anaemia and sometimes death.

**Mosquito life cycle**: the female lays eggs on the surface of still water. The eggs hatch into larvae (which breathe air at the surface), then pupae, then adult mosquitoes.

**Control** can attack the mosquito at different stages of its life cycle.

| Method | Which stage it attacks |
|---|---|
| Draining stagnant water, covering water tanks | Removes the places where eggs and larvae develop |
| Spreading oil on still water | Blocks the surface so larvae and pupae cannot breathe |
| Adding fish that eat larvae | Larvae |
| Spraying insecticide | Adults and larvae |
| Sleeping under insecticide-treated bed nets, using screens and repellents | Prevents adult bites |

Also, people can take antimalarial drugs to prevent or treat infection.

### Worked example: evaluating a control programme

*In a village, malaria cases fell from 180 per 1000 people to 72 per 1000 after bed nets were issued.* The fall is 180 - 72 = 108. Percentage fall = 108 divided by 180 x 100 = **60 per cent**. The nets prevent bites, so fewer mosquitoes pick up or pass on the parasite. To judge the effect fairly you would also check other changes at the same time, such as rainfall or drainage work.

## HIV and AIDS

**HIV (human immunodeficiency virus)** is a **viral pathogen**. It infects and destroys lymphocytes, a type of white blood cell, so the immune system is weakened (see subtopic 12.3). HIV infection may lead to **AIDS (Acquired Immune Deficiency Syndrome)**. In AIDS the person's immune system is so weak that they get infections and cancers that a healthy person would resist.

**Transmission** is by direct contact, through body fluids:

- sexual intercourse without a condom
- the sharing of needles by people who inject drugs
- from an infected mother to her baby, across the placenta, during birth or in breast milk
- from infected blood in a transfusion, if the blood has not been screened

HIV is **not** passed by touching, shaking hands, coughing, sharing food or mosquito bites.

**Control**

- use of condoms
- not sharing needles
- screening donated blood
- antiretroviral drugs, which reduce the amount of virus and reduce the chance that a mother passes it to her baby
- education about how HIV is passed on

## Cholera

**Cause**: a **bacterium** (*Vibrio cholerae*).

**Transmission**: in **contaminated water** (and food that has been washed in or prepared with contaminated water), where faeces from an infected person have got into the water supply.

**How the toxin causes illness**: the bacterium lives in the small intestine and makes a **toxin**. The toxin causes the cells lining the intestine to **secrete chloride ions** into the gut. This lowers the water potential inside the gut, so water moves out of the blood and cells into the gut **by osmosis**. The result is severe **diarrhoea**, **dehydration** and the **loss of ions from the blood**.

**Control**

- a clean water supply that is treated and protected from sewage
- hygienic food preparation
- good personal hygiene, such as washing hands after using the toilet
- correct waste disposal
- sewage treatment, so that faeces do not enter drinking water
- treatment of patients with drinks that replace water and ions (oral rehydration)

Cholera is a disease linked to poor sanitation, so controlling it depends on public health measures as well as medical treatment.

## Alcohol

The syllabus asks you to describe the effects of **excessive consumption of alcohol**.

- **Reduced self-control**: judgement and behaviour change, which can lead to risky acts.
- **Depressant**: alcohol slows down the activity of the nervous system.
- **Longer reaction times**: this increases the chance of accidents, for example when driving.
- **Damage to the liver**: the liver breaks down alcohol, and long-term heavy drinking can damage it.
- **Social implications**: effects on family life, work, violence and accidents, and the cost to health services.

## Tobacco smoke

Tobacco smoke contains three major toxic components.

| Component | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Nicotine** | Addictive drug; raises heart rate and blood pressure |
| **Tar** | A sticky mixture that coats the airways, paralyses cilia, and contains substances that cause cancer |
| **Carbon monoxide** | Combines with haemoglobin in preference to oxygen, so blood carries less oxygen; contributes to heart disease |

**Diseases strongly associated with smoking**: bronchitis, emphysema, lung cancer and heart disease. Smoking during pregnancy is associated with **reduced birth weight** of the baby, since less oxygen reaches the fetus.

- **Bronchitis**: damaged cilia and extra mucus lead to infection of the airways and a persistent cough.
- **Emphysema**: the walls of the alveoli break down, reducing the surface area for gas exchange, so breathing is difficult.
- **Lung cancer**: chemicals in tar cause cells to divide uncontrollably.

### Worked example: interpreting smoking data

*Illustrative death rates from lung cancer per 100 000 men per year: non-smokers 20; smokers of 10 cigarettes a day 140; smokers of 20 a day 300.*

1. The rate for 20 a day compared with non-smokers: 300 divided by 20 = **15 times** as high.
2. The rate rises with the number of cigarettes, which shows an **association** (a link) between smoking and lung cancer.
3. Association does not prove cause alone, but together with the known effects of tar it supports the conclusion that smoking causes lung cancer.

## Common mistakes

- Saying that a vector is the pathogen. The mosquito carries the pathogen; *Plasmodium* is the pathogen.
- Saying that HIV is a bacterium, or that it can be treated with antibiotics.
- Saying that AIDS is a virus. HIV is the virus; AIDS is the syndrome.
- Saying that cholera is caused by dirty water itself. The cause is a bacterium in the water.
- Omitting osmosis from the explanation of cholera diarrhoea.
- Writing that carbon monoxide "blocks the lungs" instead of combining with haemoglobin.
- Saying that smoking causes lung cancer in everyone, instead of "strongly associated with".
- Writing that all mosquitoes transmit malaria. It is the female *Anopheles*.

## Command words

- **Describe**: give the cause, transmission and effects of the disease.
- **Explain**: give the mechanism, for example how the cholera toxin causes diarrhoea.
- **Understand**: be able to give the difference between direct and indirect transmission.
- **Outline**: give the main control measures.
