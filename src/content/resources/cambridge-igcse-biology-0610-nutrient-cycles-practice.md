> **Original questions written for Marlbridge.** They are not past-paper questions and do not copy the structure or mark tariffs of the real examination. Use them alongside official past papers.

These questions cover sub-topic **19.3 Nutrient cycles** of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Questions and parts marked **(Extended)** test Supplement content (the nitrogen cycle and the roles of microorganisms); Core candidates can skip them. All data in the questions are invented for practice.

Revise first with the [nutrient cycles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-nutrient-cycles/) and the [nutrient cycles revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-biology-0610-nutrient-cycles-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which process in the carbon cycle removes carbon dioxide from the air?

A: combustion

B: decomposition

C: photosynthesis

D: respiration

**[1]**

**2.** Which organisms release carbon dioxide into the air by respiration?

A: animals only

B: plants and animals only

C: decomposers only

D: plants, animals and decomposers

**[1]**

**3.** Name the carbon-cycle process by which carbon in the muscle of a cow becomes part of the body of a person who eats beef. **[1]**

**4.** State **two** conditions that slow down decomposition and so allow fossil fuels to form. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* Name the type of bacteria that converts:

**(a)** ammonium ions into nitrate ions **[1]**

**(b)** nitrate ions into nitrogen gas. **[1]**

**6.** *(Extended)* State the form in which plants absorb nitrogen from the soil, and name **one** type of molecule they make from it. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** A diagram of the carbon cycle has five boxes joined by six arrows:

| Arrow | From | To |
|---|---|---|
| A | carbon dioxide in air | plants |
| B | plants | animals |
| C | animals | carbon dioxide in air |
| D | dead organisms and waste | carbon dioxide in air |
| E | dead organisms and waste | fossil fuels |
| F | fossil fuels | carbon dioxide in air |

**(a)** Name the processes shown by arrows A, C, E and F. **[4]**

**(b)** Name the group of organisms responsible for arrow D. **[1]**

**(c)** A large area of forest is cut down and burned to clear land for farming. Explain how this changes the concentration of carbon dioxide in the air. **[3]**

**8.** A student measured the carbon dioxide concentration inside a closed greenhouse full of tomato plants over 24 hours. It was highest just before dawn and lowest in the late afternoon.

**(a)** Explain why the carbon dioxide concentration was highest just before dawn. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why it fell during the day. **[2]**

**9.** In a peat bog, the soil is waterlogged and acidic. Dead mosses build up over thousands of years to form peat, which contains a lot of carbon.

**(a)** Explain why the dead mosses do not fully decompose. **[2]**

**(b)** The bog is drained and the peat is dug up and burned as fuel. Suggest the effect on the carbon dioxide concentration in the air, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**10.** *(Extended)* Clover plants have root nodules. Cows graze on clover.

Describe how nitrogen gas in the air can become part of a protein in a cow. **[4]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A farmer measured the nitrate concentration in two fields. In a well-drained field it was 42 mg per kg of soil. In a waterlogged field it was 9 mg per kg of soil.

**(a)** Calculate how much lower, as a percentage, the nitrate concentration was in the waterlogged field. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the nitrate concentration is lower in the waterlogged field. **[3]**

**(c)** Suggest why crops grow less well in the waterlogged field. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)*

**(a)** State the four roles of microorganisms in the nitrogen cycle, giving the conversion carried out in each. **[4]**

**(b)** Lightning also converts nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds. Explain why it is not included in your answer to (a). **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1].

**2.** **D** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Plants respire all the time, not only at night; decomposers release carbon dioxide by respiration too.

**3.** Feeding [1].

**4.** Any two from: waterlogged conditions; lack of oxygen; acidic conditions; low temperature [2].

**5. (a)** Nitrifying bacteria [1].

**(b)** Denitrifying bacteria [1].

**6.** Nitrate ions [1]; amino acids / proteins [1].

**7. (a)** A: photosynthesis [1]; C: respiration [1]; E: formation of fossil fuels [1]; F: combustion [1].

**(b)** Decomposers / bacteria and fungi [1].

**(c)** Burning the trees releases carbon dioxide by combustion [1]. Fewer trees are left to remove carbon dioxide by photosynthesis [1]. So the concentration of carbon dioxide in the air increases [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) has two separate causes, combustion and reduced photosynthesis. Answers that mention only one cannot reach full marks.

**8. (a)** There is no light at night, so no photosynthesis [1]; the plants (and other organisms) kept respiring and releasing carbon dioxide [1].

**(b)** In light, the plants photosynthesise [1]; the rate of photosynthesis is greater than the rate of respiration, so more carbon dioxide is absorbed than released [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), saying "plants stop respiring in the day" is wrong and loses the mark; respiration continues, but photosynthesis is faster.

**9. (a)** Waterlogged soil has little oxygen / acidic conditions [1], so decomposers cannot respire / grow well and decomposition is slow [1].

**(b)** Carbon dioxide concentration increases [1]; carbon stored in the peat for thousands of years is released by combustion [1].

**10.** Nitrogen-fixing bacteria in the root nodules of the clover convert nitrogen gas into nitrogen compounds [1]. The clover uses these to make amino acids [1] and then proteins [1]. The cow eats the clover, digests the protein to amino acids, absorbs them and uses them to build its own proteins [1].

**11. (a)** (42 − 9) ÷ 42 × 100 [1] = 78.6% [1].

**(b)** Waterlogged soil contains little oxygen [1]. Denitrifying bacteria convert nitrate ions into nitrogen gas [1]. Nitrifying bacteria need oxygen, so less ammonium is converted into nitrate [1].

**(c)** Less nitrate is available to be absorbed [1], so the crops make fewer amino acids / proteins and grow less [1].

*Examiner insight:* In (b), full marks need both effects of low oxygen: more denitrification and less nitrification.

**12. (a)** Decomposition: protein (in dead organisms and waste) is broken down to ammonium ions [1]. Nitrification: ammonium ions are converted to nitrate ions [1]. Nitrogen fixation: nitrogen gas is converted into nitrogen compounds [1]. Denitrification: nitrate ions are converted into nitrogen gas [1].

**(b)** Lightning is not a microorganism / is a physical process, not a living organism [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming decomposition without saying decomposers respire and release carbon dioxide.
- Saying plants respire only at night.
- Giving only one reason why deforestation raises carbon dioxide.
- Confusing nitrification with nitrogen fixation.
- Forgetting the digestion and protein-building steps when tracing nitrogen into an animal.
