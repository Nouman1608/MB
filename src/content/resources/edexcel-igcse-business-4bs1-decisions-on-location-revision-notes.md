Quick recall for section 1.4, Decisions on location, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE in Business (4BS1) specification, Issue 2, February 2026. Worked examples are in the [decisions on location study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-decisions-on-location/); exam-style questions are in the [decisions on location practice questions](/resources/edexcel-igcse-business-4bs1-decisions-on-location-practice/). Figures are invented and in dollars.

## What the specification lists (1.4.1)

The main factors influencing location decisions and relocation of a business:

- proximity to market, labour, materials and competitors
- nature of the business activity
- the impact of the internet on location decisions: e-commerce and/or fixed premises
- legal controls and trade blocs.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Location decision | Choosing where a business operates |
| Relocation | Moving an existing business to a new site |
| Proximity | Nearness |
| E-commerce | Buying and selling over the internet |
| Fixed premises | A physical site customers can visit |
| Legal controls | Government rules on land use, pollution, noise and safety |
| Trade bloc | Countries that agree to reduce trade barriers between themselves (standard definition) |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |

## The factors in one table

| Factor | Pulls the business towards | Matters most for |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Customers | Services in person; heavy, fragile or perishable products |
| Labour | Enough workers with the right skills at an affordable wage | Labour-intensive and high-skill businesses |
| Materials | Suppliers of inputs | Products that lose weight in processing |
| Competitors | Clusters, or away from rivals | Shops and showrooms (clusters); undifferentiated services (away) |
| Nature of activity | The site the activity requires | Mines, farms, factories, shops |
| Internet | Cheaper sites away from town centres | Goods easy to deliver |
| Legal controls | Away from restricted areas | Noisy or polluting activities |
| Trade blocs | Inside the bloc where the main market is | Large businesses selling across borders |

## Transport test

- Cost to move inputs to a site near the market against cost to move output from a site near materials.
- Reminder: 3 tonnes of input per tonne of output, 200 km apart, 0.25 per tonne per km. Near materials: 1 × 200 × 0.25 = 50. Near market: 3 × 200 × 0.25 = 150. Locate near materials.

## Country choice reminder (Paper 2)

- Compare wages plus tariff, not wages alone.
- Reminder: 500 workers; wages 8,000 outside a bloc, 12,000 inside; output 30 million; tariff 10% on entering the bloc. Outside: 4.0 million wages plus 3.0 million tariff = 7.0 million. Inside: 6.0 million. Inside is 1.0 million cheaper.
- Then weigh skills, productivity, transport links and political stability.

## E-commerce and/or fixed premises

- E-commerce only: warehouse anywhere with good transport links; reaches a wide market.
- Fixed premises only: must be where customers are.
- Both: showroom or shop plus website, delivery or collection.

## Relocation checklist

- Trigger: rent, customers or suppliers moving, space, law, trade policy.
- One-off cost of moving.
- Yearly saving or gain.
- Recovery time = one-off cost ÷ yearly gain.
- Risks: staff who will not move, customers lost, disruption.

Reminder: move costs 12,000; yearly gain 5,000; recovery 2.4 years.

## Small and large businesses

- Paper 1 (small): a site within a town; rent, footfall, local staff, online selling.
- Paper 2 (large): a country or region; wage levels, skills, trade bloc membership and tariffs, transport links, legal controls.

## An evaluate frame for location

1. Name the factor that matters most for this business's activity.
2. Use the case figures: rent, wages, transport, tariffs, one-off costs.
3. Add what the figures leave out: customers, staff, legal controls, future growth.
4. Decide, and state the condition that would change your decision.

## Exam traps

- A list of factors with no judgement about which matters most.
- "Low wages are always best", ignoring skills and productivity.
- "The internet means no premises are needed."
- Ignoring the one-off cost of relocation.
- Calling a tariff a tax on exports.
- Naming particular laws instead of describing legal controls in general terms.
- Forgetting that being near competitors can bring customers.

## Self-test

1. List the four proximity factors in the specification.
2. Why must a quarry locate where it does?
3. Why does a café need to be near its market?
4. Define e-commerce.
5. Give one way the internet can reduce a business's location costs.
6. Give one benefit of locating near competitors.
7. Define a tariff.
8. Why might a large business locate a factory inside a trade bloc?
9. A move costs 15,000 and saves 6,000 a year. How long does it take to recover the cost?
10. Give two risks of relocation.
11. A product uses 5 tonnes of input per tonne of output. Should the business locate near materials or market, if transport costs per tonne are the same? Why?
12. Give one example of a legal control that could affect location.

### Answers

1. Market, labour, materials, competitors.
2. The nature of its activity: the stone is only where it is found.
3. Customers must come to the café, so it needs passing trade or a convenient site.
4. Buying and selling over the internet.
5. It can use a cheaper warehouse away from expensive town-centre sites.
6. Customers already come to the area (or skilled workers and suppliers are nearby).
7. A tax on imported goods.
8. To avoid tariffs on goods sold to customers in member countries.
9. 15,000 ÷ 6,000 = 2.5 years.
10. Losing staff who will not move; losing customers; disruption to production (any two).
11. Near materials, because carrying 1 tonne of output costs less than carrying 5 tonnes of input.
12. Rules on how land in an area may be used (or limits on pollution or noise).
