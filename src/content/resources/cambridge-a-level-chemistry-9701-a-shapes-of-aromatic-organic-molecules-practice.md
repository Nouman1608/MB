## Section A

**1.** State the hybridisation of each carbon atom in benzene. **[1]**

**2.** State the C–C–C bond angle in benzene. **[1]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a σ bond and a π bond in terms of orbital overlap. **[2]**

**4.** State the number of σ bonds in a molecule of methylbenzene, C₆H₅CH₃. **[1]**

**5.** Predict the length of a carbon–carbon bond in the ring of benzene, compared with the two bond lengths given above. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** Describe and explain the shape of a benzene molecule, referring to hybridisation and to σ and π bonding. **[4]**

**7.** Benzene's enthalpy change of hydrogenation to cyclohexane is −208 kJ mol⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the enthalpy change expected if benzene contained three separate C=C bonds. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference between the expected and actual values, and state what this difference shows. **[2]**

**(c)** State and explain the shape of the cyclohexane molecule formed, in terms of hybridisation. **[2]**

**8.** Benzoic acid has the formula C₆H₅COOH.

**(a)** Deduce the total number of σ bonds in a molecule of benzoic acid. **[1]**

**(b)** State the hybridisation of the carbon atom in the –COOH group and the O=C–O bond angle. **[2]**

**(c)** Describe the π bonding in benzoic acid. **[2]**

**9.** 4-methylphenol has the structure HOC₆H₄CH₃.

**(a)** State the hybridisation and bond angles around the ring carbons and around the methyl carbon. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the ring in 4-methylphenol is planar while the molecule as a whole is not. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain how a lone pair on the oxygen atom interacts with the ring, and state one consequence for the reactivity of the ring. **[2]**

**10.** Naphthalene, C₁₀H₈, consists of two benzene rings fused along one shared C–C bond.

**(a)** State the hybridisation of each carbon atom and the shape of the molecule. **[2]**

**(b)** Deduce the number of σ bonds in naphthalene. **[1]**

**(c)** State the number of electrons in the delocalised π system. **[1]**

**11.** A student describes benzene as follows: "Each carbon is sp² hybridised. Each p orbital overlaps with one neighbouring p orbital to form three π bonds, which lie in the plane of the ring." Identify two errors and correct them. **[2]**

**12.** Phenylethanone, C₆H₅COCH₃, has three different types of carbon atom.

**(a)** Complete the following for each type: number of σ bonds, hybridisation, bond angle. **[3]**

**(b)** Deduce whether all the hydrogen atoms in phenylethanone lie in the same plane as the ring. Explain your answer. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** sp² [1].

**2.** 120° [1].

**3.** A σ bond forms by head-on (end-to-end) overlap of orbitals along the line joining the nuclei [1]; a π bond forms by sideways overlap of p orbitals, with electron density above and below that line [1].

**4.** 15 [1] (6 ring C–C, 5 ring C–H, 1 ring C–CH₃, 3 C–H in CH₃).

**5.** Between 0.134 nm and 0.154 nm (0.139 nm), all six bonds equal [1].

**6.** Each carbon is sp² hybridised [1]. Each carbon forms three σ bonds (two C–C, one C–H) using sp² orbitals arranged trigonally in a plane, so the molecule is a planar regular hexagon with bond angles of 120° [1]. Each carbon has one unhybridised p orbital perpendicular to the plane of the ring [1]. These p orbitals overlap sideways with those on both neighbouring carbons, forming a delocalised π system of six electrons above and below the plane [1].

**7. (a)** 3 × (−120) = −360 kJ mol⁻¹ [1].

**(b)** −208 − (−360) = 152 kJ mol⁻¹ less exothermic than expected [1]; benzene is more stable (by about 152 kJ mol⁻¹) than a structure with three localised C=C bonds, because of delocalisation [1].

**(c)** Every carbon is sp³ hybridised, forming four σ bonds [1], so bond angles are about 109.5° and the ring is not planar [1].

**8. (a)** 15 [1] (15 atoms in C₇H₆O₂, one ring).

**(b)** sp² [1]; about 120° [1].

**(c)** There is a localised π bond in C=O, from sideways overlap of p orbitals on C and O [1], and a delocalised π system of six electrons in the benzene ring [1].

**9. (a)** Ring carbons: sp², 120° [1]; methyl carbon: sp³, about 109.5° [1].

**(b)** All ring carbons are sp² with parallel p orbitals that must overlap sideways, so the ring and the atoms bonded directly to it lie in one plane [1]; the methyl carbon is tetrahedral (sp³), so its hydrogen atoms do not all lie in that plane [1].

**(c)** A lone pair on O in a p orbital overlaps with the ring's π system and is partly delocalised into the ring [1]; this increases the electron density of the ring, so it is activated towards electrophiles (reacts under milder conditions than benzene) [1].

**10. (a)** All carbons sp² [1]; the molecule is planar [1].

**(b)** 19 [1]: 18 atoms, two rings, so 18 − 1 + 2 = 19 (11 C–C and 8 C–H).

**(c)** 10 [1].

**11.** Error 1: each p orbital overlaps with the p orbitals on **both** neighbouring carbons, giving one delocalised π system rather than three separate π bonds [1]. Error 2: the π electron density lies **above and below** the plane of the ring, not in it [1].

**12. (a)** Ring carbons: 3 σ bonds, sp², 120° [1]; carbonyl carbon: 3 σ bonds (plus one π bond), sp², 120° [1]; methyl carbon: 4 σ bonds, sp³, 109.5° [1].

**(b)** No [1]: the five ring hydrogens lie in the plane of the ring, but the methyl carbon is tetrahedral, so its three hydrogens cannot all lie in that plane [1].

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Describing overlap with only one neighbouring p orbital.
- Placing π electron density in the plane of the ring.
- Counting the benzene ring as three π bonds when describing delocalisation.
- Forgetting that every double bond contains a σ bond as well as a π bond.
- Giving 109.5° for ring carbons or 120° for side-chain CH₃ carbons.
- Calculating the stabilisation energy with the wrong sign or without stating what it shows.
