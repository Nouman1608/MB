Content tested: "Rivalry between the USA and USSR, 1945-1949" and "The development of the Cold War, 1949-1960".

## Section A: knowledge (3-mark questions)

**1.** Give three differences between the USA and the USSR that led to rivalry after 1945. **[3]**

**2.** Give three features of the Berlin Airlift. **[3]**

**3.** Give three features of the Korean War, 1950-53. **[3]**

**4.** Give three features of the Hungarian rising of 1956. **[3]**

## Section B: explanation and judgement

**5.** Explain one way in which the Truman Doctrine affected relations between the USA and the USSR. **[6]**

**6.** Explain one way in which the atomic bombs dropped on Japan in 1945 affected the Cold War. **[6]**

**7.** Explain one way in which the death of Stalin affected East-West relations. **[6]**

**8.** Explain two reasons why Germany was divided into two states in 1949. **[9]**

**9.** Explain two ways in which events in Asia increased tension between the superpowers in the years 1949-53. **[9]**

**10.** "The Marshall Plan was the main reason why East-West relations worsened in the years 1947-49." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**11.** "The Korean War was more important than the Berlin Blockade in the development of the Cold War." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**12.** "The Thaw after 1953 made little difference to East-West relations." How far do you agree? **[12]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** 3-mark questions: [1] per relevant point. 6-mark: way identified [1], precise knowledge [2], explanation of how it affected the development [2], further developed detail or consequence [1]. 9-mark: each of two ways or reasons [4] (identified [1], precise knowledge [1], explained link [2]) plus [1] for accurate, well-sequenced detail. 12-mark: the given factor [3], at least one other factor [4], comparison in a line of reasoning [3], supported judgement [2].

**1.** Any three, [1] each: the USA was capitalist and the USSR communist; the USA was a democracy and the USSR a one-party state; the USSR wanted a buffer zone of friendly states in eastern Europe; the USA wanted free elections and free trade; the USA had the atomic bomb; they disagreed over the future of Germany and reparations.

**2.** Any three, [1] each: it supplied West Berlin by air during the Soviet blockade; it lasted from June 1948 to May 1949 (the Blockade ended in May 1949); American and British aircraft flew in food, fuel and other goods; planes landed at short intervals day and night; it showed western determination without using force; it increased support for a separate West German state and NATO.

**3.** Any three, [1] each: North Korea invaded South Korea in June 1950; the UN sent a force, mostly American, under General MacArthur; China entered the war when UN forces approached its border; the war became a stalemate near the 38th parallel; Truman dismissed MacArthur in 1951; an armistice in July 1953 left Korea divided.

**4.** Any three, [1] each: Hungarians rose against Soviet control in October 1956; Imre Nagy became prime minister and promised free elections; he announced withdrawal from the Warsaw Pact; Soviet tanks entered Budapest on 4 November; thousands were killed and many fled; the West protested but did not intervene.

**5.** Way identified [1]: it committed the USA to containing communism. Precise knowledge [2]: in March 1947 Truman promised aid to free peoples resisting communist pressure, and the first aid went to Greece, where there was a civil war, and to Turkey. Explanation [2]: the USSR regarded this as American interference in Europe and an attempt to surround it, so it tightened control over eastern Europe. Further development [1]: the USA now saw crises as tests of its commitment, which led to the Marshall Plan and later NATO.

**6.** Way identified [1]: they started a nuclear arms race. Precise knowledge [2]: the bombs on Hiroshima (6 August) and Nagasaki (9 August) showed the USA's new power; the USSR, which had no atomic weapon until 1949, felt threatened. Explanation [2]: Stalin ordered a Soviet bomb programme and tightened control over the buffer zone, while the USA believed its monopoly gave it strength in negotiations, so each side distrusted the other. Further development [1]: the race led to hydrogen bombs in 1952-53 and missile development.

**7.** Way identified [1]: it created a possibility of a Thaw. Precise knowledge [2]: Stalin died in March 1953; Khrushchev became the dominant leader, criticised Stalin in 1956 and promoted peaceful co-existence. Explanation [2]: with a less rigid leader, the USSR agreed to Austrian neutrality in 1955 and the powers held a summit in Geneva, so tensions eased for a time. Further development [1]: but Khrushchev still crushed the Hungarian rising in 1956, showing the limits of the Thaw.

**8.** Reason one [4]: the Marshall Plan and the new currency [1]; the western zones accepted Marshall Aid and introduced the Deutsche Mark in 1948 [1]; so the western zones developed separately and the USSR refused to co-operate, making a single economy impossible [2]. Reason two [4]: the Berlin Blockade [1]; Stalin blockaded West Berlin from June 1948 and the western powers saved it by the Airlift [1]; so the western powers decided to create a West German state, the Federal Republic (May 1949), and the USSR responded with the GDR (October 1949) [2]. Accurate, well-sequenced detail [1], for example NATO in April 1949.

**9.** Way one [4]: the communist victory in China [1]; Mao won in 1949 and signed a treaty with the USSR in 1950 [1]; so the USA felt that communism was spreading across Asia and became hostile to China [2]. Way two [4]: the Korean War [1]; North Korea invaded South Korea in 1950 and the USA led a UN force; China intervened [1]; so American and Chinese forces fought each other, military spending rose and containment was applied to Asia [2]. Accurate, well-sequenced detail [1], for example Vietnam and the Geneva Agreement of 1954.

**10.** Given factor [3]: the Marshall Plan offered aid to rebuild Europe; the USSR refused it and forced its satellites to refuse; it led to Cominform and Comecon; the USSR saw it as American imperialism. Other factors [4]: the Truman Doctrine; the takeover of Czechoslovakia in 1948; the currency reform and the Berlin Blockade; disagreements over Germany; the atomic bomb. Comparison [3]: the Marshall Plan divided Europe economically and led the Soviet Union to tighten control, but it followed the Truman Doctrine and Soviet expansion, and the Berlin Blockade had a more direct effect on the creation of two Germanys and NATO. Judgement [2]: for example, the Marshall Plan was important because it made the division of Europe permanent, but the Berlin Blockade was the more serious crisis.

**11.** Given factor [3]: the Korean War involved fighting between UN and communist forces; China entered; the USA extended containment to Asia; defence spending rose; Korea remained divided. Other factors [4]: the Berlin Blockade and Airlift; the creation of NATO; two German states; the arms race and the Warsaw Pact; Hungary. Comparison [3]: the Blockade was a test in Europe without fighting and led to NATO and the division of Germany, while Korea showed that the Cold War could become a hot war and spread to Asia; Korea affected the USA's attitude to China and led to rearmament. Judgement [2]: for example, Berlin settled the division of Europe, but Korea made the Cold War global and militarised it, so Korea was more important for its longer-term effects.

**12.** Given factor [3]: after Stalin's death Khrushchev criticised Stalin and spoke of peaceful co-existence; Austria became neutral in 1955; the Geneva Summit of 1955; some reduction in tension. Other factors [4]: Hungary in 1956; the Warsaw Pact and the arms race; Sputnik and ICBMs; the U-2 incident and the failure of the Paris Summit in 1960; the Berlin crisis from 1958. Comparison [3]: the Thaw produced some agreements, but the crushing of Hungary, the arms race and later crises showed that the basic rivalry remained. Judgement [2]: for example, the Thaw made a limited difference because it changed the tone and some outcomes, such as Austria, but the underlying rivalry and the use of force in Hungary meant that tension returned.
