These questions cover quantitative skill QS5, "calculate and interpret index numbers", from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification, including the terms of trade from 3.4.1.2. Every country and figure is invented; "cu" means currency units. Give index numbers and percentages to 1 decimal place unless told otherwise. Read the [QS5 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs5-quantitative-skills/) first, or refresh the methods with the [QS5 revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs5-quantitative-skills-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** A shop sold 250 units in the base year and sells 285 units this year. Calculate this year's sales index. **[1]**

**2.** A firm's output index (2020 = 100) is 92 in 2024. Output in 2020 was 3,500 units. Calculate output in 2024. **[1]**

**3.** A price index rises from 140 to 147. State the change in index points and calculate the percentage change. **[2]**

**4.** Country K's export price index is 102 and its import price index is 120, from the same base year.

**(a)** Calculate the terms of trade. **[1]**

**(b)** State whether the terms of trade have improved or deteriorated since the base year. **[1]**

**5.** Country L's export price index is 96 and its export volume index is 115. Calculate the export value index and the percentage change in export revenue since the base year. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows a house price index for Country H (2015 = 100).

| Year | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Index | 118.0 | 124.0 | 130.2 | 133.8 | 128.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in house prices from 2020 to 2021 and from 2022 to 2023. **[2]**

**(b)** Rebase the index so that 2020 = 100, for 2019, 2021, 2022 and 2023. **[3]**

**(c)** Using your answer to part (b), state how house prices in 2023 compare with 2020. **[1]**

**7.** A household spends 450 cu a month on food, 300 cu on housing, 150 cu on transport and 100 cu on other items. Over a year, the price indices for these categories become 108, 104, 115 and 100.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of each category, out of 100. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the weighted price index for this household. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why a household that spends a much larger share on transport faces a higher rate of inflation. **[1]**

**8.** The table shows Country M's export and import price indices.

| Year | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Export prices | 100 | 104 | 110 | 112 |
| Import prices | 100 | 100 | 112 | 120 |

**(a)** Calculate the terms of trade for years 2, 3 and 4. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe the change in the terms of trade over the four years. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest one cause of the change from year 2 to year 4, and one possible effect on Country M's current account. **[2]**

**9.** An old producer price index has 2010 = 100 and a value of 132 in 2018. A new index has 2018 = 100, with values of 103 in 2019, 101 in 2020 and 107 in 2021.

**(a)** Express the 2019, 2020 and 2021 values on the old base. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage change in producer prices from 2019 to 2021. **[1]**

**10.** Country N's export volume index (2016 = 100) is 125 in 2021 and 150 in 2025. Country P's export volume index (2021 = 100) is 118 in 2025.

**(a)** Rebase Country N's 2025 value so that 2021 = 100. **[1]**

**(b)** State which country's export volumes grew faster from 2021 to 2025. **[1]**

**11.** Country Q's index of nominal GDP is 115.5 and its price index for GDP is 110, from the same base year.

**(a)** Calculate the index of real GDP (the volume index). **[2]**

**(b)** Explain what this shows about the change in real output since the base year. **[1]**

**12.** A consumer price index was 100 in 2019 and 131.1 in 2025.

**(a)** Calculate the average annual rate of inflation over these six years. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why dividing 31.1 by 6 gives a misleading answer. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 285/250 × 100 = **114** [1]. Total: 1

**2.** 3,500 × 92/100 = **3,220 units** [1]. Total: 1

**3.** **7 index points** [1]. 7/140 × 100 = **5.0%** [1]. Total: 2

**4. (a)** 102/120 × 100 = **85.0** [1].

**(b)** **Deteriorated**, because the index is below 100 [1]. Total: 2

**5.** Value index = 96 × 115/100 = **110.4** [1], so export revenue has risen by **10.4%** [1]. Total: 2

**6. (a)** 2020 to 2021: (130.2 − 124.0)/124.0 × 100 = **+5.0%** [1]. 2022 to 2023: (128.5 − 133.8)/133.8 × 100 = **−4.0%** [1].

**(b)** Divide each value by 124.0 and multiply by 100 [1]: 2019 = **95.2**, 2021 = **105.0** [1], 2022 = **107.9**, 2023 = **103.6** [1].

**(c)** House prices in 2023 were **3.6% higher** than in 2020, despite the fall in 2023 [1]. Total: 6

**7. (a)** Total spending is 1,000 cu, so the weights are **food 45, housing 30, transport 15, other 10** [1].

**(b)** (45 × 108 + 30 × 104 + 15 × 115 + 10 × 100)/100 = (4,860 + 3,120 + 1,725 + 1,000)/100 [1] = **107.1** (7.05% inflation) [1].

**(c)** Transport prices rose fastest (15%), so a household with a larger transport weight has a higher weighted average price rise [1]. Total: 4

**8. (a)** Year 2: 104/100 × 100 = **104.0**; year 3: 110/112 × 100 = **98.2** [1]; year 4: 112/120 × 100 = **93.3** [1].

**(b)** The terms of trade improved in year 2, then deteriorated in years 3 and 4 to below the base-year level [1].

**(c)** Cause: import prices, such as energy or raw material prices, rose faster than export prices [1]. Effect: if demand for imports is price inelastic, spending on imports rises, which may worsen the current account balance [1]. Total: 5

**9. (a)** Multiply by 132/100 = 1.32 [1]: 2019 = **136.0**, 2020 = **133.3**, 2021 = **141.2** [1].

**(b)** 107/103 = 1.0388, a rise of **3.9%** (the same on either base) [1]. Total: 3

**10. (a)** 150/125 × 100 = **120** [1].

**(b)** **Country N**: its volumes grew 20% from 2021 to 2025, against 18% for Country P [1]. Total: 2

**11. (a)** Real GDP index = nominal index ÷ price index × 100 [1] = 115.5/110 × 100 = **105** [1].

**(b)** Real output is 5% higher than in the base year; the rest of the 15.5% rise in nominal GDP reflects higher prices [1]. Total: 3

**12. (a)** (131.1/100)^(1/6) = 1.0462 [1], so average annual inflation is **4.6%** [1].

**(b)** Price rises compound: each year's inflation applies to a higher price level, so 31.1/6 = 5.2% overstates the annual rate [1]. Total: 3

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing 150 with 118 in Question 10 without rebasing first.
- Treating a move of 7 points from 140 as a 7% rise.
- Inverting the terms of trade, giving import prices over export prices.
- Forgetting that the weights must be calculated from spending shares before the weighted index.
- Adding the price and volume indices instead of multiplying them.
- Stating an effect on the current account without saying what it depends on, such as price elasticity.
