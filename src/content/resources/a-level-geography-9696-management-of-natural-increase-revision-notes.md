Condensed recall for **4.4 The management of natural increase** (population policy and its results). For full explanations, read the [Core Human Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-human-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Pro-natalist policy | Aims to raise birth rates |
| Anti-natalist policy | Aims to lower birth rates |
| Coercive policy | Uses penalties or compulsion |
| Non-coercive policy | Uses education, incentives and services |
| Family planning | Services giving access to contraception and advice |

## Policy tools

| Aim | Tools |
|---|---|
| Lower birth rate | Family planning clinics, contraception, education for girls, incentives for small families, minimum marriage age, penalties or limits (coercive) |
| Raise birth rate | Child benefits, tax relief, parental leave, childcare, housing support |
| Manage results | Pension and retirement age reform, services for the elderly, immigration, support for a shrinking workforce |

## Managing the results

A policy that cuts births later produces an **ageing population** and a high old-age dependency ratio. It may also change the sex ratio where there is a preference for sons. Pro-natalist policies can increase demand for schools and services in the short term.

## Evaluating a policy

- **Effectiveness:** did the birth rate or fertility rate change, and was it the policy or wider development?
- **Equity:** who gained and who lost?
- **Ethics and human rights:** coercion is controversial.
- **Time:** effects take decades.
- **Cost:** services, enforcement and incentives.

## Required case study

The syllabus requires a case study of **one country's population policy**: attempts to alter the natural increase rate and to manage its results. Prepare a country you can describe with checked facts: the policy aims, methods, dates, outcomes, and unintended consequences.

## Exam traps

- A fall in births may have many causes; do not credit the policy alone.
- Discuss management of results, not only the policy itself.
- Policies in HICs (pro-natalist) and LICs (anti-natalist) differ; know the context.

## Self-test

1. Difference between pro- and anti-natalist?
2. Give one coercive and one non-coercive anti-natalist measure.
3. Name a long-term result of reducing births.
4. Give two pro-natalist measures.
5. Why is evaluating a policy difficult?
6. What must the 4.4 case study cover?

**Answers:** 1. Pro-natalist raises birth rates; anti-natalist lowers them. 2. Coercive: penalties or limits. Non-coercive: family planning or girls' education. 3. Ageing population and higher dependency ratio. 4. Child benefit, tax relief, parental leave, childcare. 5. Other factors also change fertility. 6. One country's policy to alter natural increase and manage the results.
