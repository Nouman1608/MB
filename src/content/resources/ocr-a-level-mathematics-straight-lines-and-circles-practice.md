## Questions

**1.** Find the equation of the straight line through (6, -1) and (-2, 3), written as ax + by + c = 0 with integer a, b and c. **[3]**

**2.** The line l has equation 3x - 2y + 5 = 0 and P is the point (7, 0).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line that passes through P at right angles to l. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the foot of this perpendicular on l. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the shortest distance from P to l, giving an exact answer. **[1]**

**3.** A is (-4, 1) and B is (2, 5). Find the equation of the perpendicular bisector of AB in the form ax + by + c = 0. **[4]**

**4.** A pool is filled at a steady rate. It holds V cubic metres of water, with V = 14.6 after 2 hours and V = 31.4 after 6 hours.

**(a)** Find a linear model for V in terms of the time t hours. **[3]**
**(b)** Interpret the gradient and the constant term in context. **[2]**
**(c)** Find, to 3 significant figures, when the model says the pool holds 50 cubic metres. **[1]**
**(d)** State one reason the model may be unreliable. **[1]**

**5.** A circle has centre (-2, 5) and passes through (3, 8).

**(a)** Find its equation. **[2]**
**(b)** Decide whether (0, 10) and (3, 0) lie inside, on or outside the circle. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Find the centre and radius of the circle 3x² + 3y² + 12x - 18y - 3 = 0. **[3]**
**(b)** The equation x² + y² + 12x - 4y + k = 0 represents a circle. Find the range of possible values of k. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the value of k for which this circle has radius 5. **[1]**

**7.** Circle C1 has equation x² + y² - 2x - 4y - 4 = 0 and circle C2 has equation x² + y² - 18x - 4y + 69 = 0.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius of each circle. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the circles do not intersect, and find the shortest distance between a point on one and a point on the other. **[2]**

**8.** A(1, -1) and B(9, 5) are the ends of a diameter of a circle.

**(a)** Show that P(2, 6) lies on the circle, without using the equation of the circle. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the circle. **[2]**

**9.** The line x + y = 7 meets the circle (x - 2)² + (y + 1)² = 26 at A and B.

**(a)** Find A and B. **[3]**
**(b)** Use a circle property to show that the foot of the perpendicular from the centre to AB is the midpoint of AB. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the length of AB. **[1]**

**10.** A circle has equation x² + y² - 2x - 6y - 10 = 0.

**(a)** Find the centre and radius. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that (5, 5) lies on the circle and find the equation of the tangent there, in the form ax + by + c = 0. **[3]**
**(c)** The tangent meets the axes at X and Y. Find the area of triangle OXY, where O is the origin. **[2]**

**11.** The line y = 3x + k and the circle (x - 3)² + (y - 1)² = 10 are given.

**(a)** Show that any point of intersection has x-coordinate satisfying 10x² + (6k - 12)x + k² - 2k = 0. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that the discriminant equals -4(k - 2)(k + 18). **[2]**
**(c)** Find the values of k for which the line is a tangent, and the range of k for which it meets the circle at two points. **[2]**
**(d)** For the tangent with positive k, find the point of contact. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Gradient = (3 - (-1))/(-2 - 6) = -1/2 [1]. y + 1 = -(1/2)(x - 6), so 2y + 2 = -x + 6 [1]. **x + 2y - 4 = 0** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* a required form is part of the question, so finish in it with integer coefficients; a correct line left as y = -x/2 + 2 leaves the last step undone. Substituting the second point as a check is quick.

**2. (a)** Gradient of l is 3/2, so the perpendicular gradient is -2/3 [1]. y - 0 = -(2/3)(x - 7), giving **2x + 3y - 14 = 0** [1]
**(b)** Solve 3x - 2y = -5 with 2x + 3y = 14. Multiply by 3 and 2: 9x - 6y = -15 and 4x + 6y = 28; add: 13x = 13 [1]. x = 1, y = 4, so the foot is **(1, 4)** [1]
**(c)** Distance = √[(7 - 1)² + (0 - 4)²] = √52 = **2√13** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* in a multi-part question, later parts reuse earlier results, so write each result clearly. Leave the surd exact when the question asks for an exact answer.

**3.** Midpoint = (-1, 3) [1]. Gradient AB = (5 - 1)/(2 + 4) = 2/3 [1]. Perpendicular gradient = -3/2 [1]. y - 3 = -(3/2)(x + 1), so 2y - 6 = -3x - 3 and **3x + 2y - 3 = 0** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the usual slip is using the gradient of AB itself, or the reciprocal without the sign change. Check that the midpoint satisfies your final equation.

**4. (a)** Gradient = (31.4 - 14.6)/(6 - 2) = 4.2 [1]. V - 14.6 = 4.2(t - 2) [1]. **V = 6.2 + 4.2t** [1]
**(b)** The pool gains 4.2 cubic metres each hour [1]. 6.2 is the volume at t = 0, the initial volume [1]
**(c)** 6.2 + 4.2t = 50 gives t = 43.8/4.2 = 73/7 = **10.4 hours** [1]
**(d)** For example, the flow may not stay constant, or the model gives volumes above the pool's capacity if it keeps running [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* interpretations score best when they use the units and the context ("cubic metres per hour", "initial volume"), not only the words "gradient" and "intercept". A limitation should be specific to this situation.

**5. (a)** r² = (3 + 2)² + (8 - 5)² = 25 + 9 = 34 [1]. **(x + 2)² + (y - 5)² = 34** [1]
**(b)** For (0, 10): 4 + 25 = 29 < 34, so **inside** [1]. For (3, 0): 25 + 25 = 50 > 34, so **outside** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* compare with r² = 34, not with r. Showing the value you calculate for each point makes the conclusion checkable.

**6. (a)** Divide by 3: x² + y² + 4x - 6y - 1 = 0 [1]. (x + 2)² - 4 + (y - 3)² - 9 = 1 [1]. Centre **(-2, 3)**, radius **√14** [1]
**(b)** (x + 6)² + (y - 2)² = 40 - k [1]. This needs 40 - k > 0, so **k < 40** [1]
**(c)** 40 - k = 25, so **k = 15** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* dividing by the common coefficient first is the step that is most often missed. State the condition r² > 0 rather than only quoting the final inequality.

**7. (a)** C1: (x - 1)² + (y - 2)² = 9, centre (1, 2), radius 3 [1]. C2: (x - 9)² + (y - 2)² = 16, centre (9, 2), radius 4 [1]
**(b)** Distance between centres = 8, and 3 + 4 = 7 < 8, so the circles are separate [1]. Shortest distance = 8 - 7 = **1** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* the argument is a comparison of the distance between centres with the sum of the radii, so show both numbers. The smallest gap is measured along the line of centres.

**8. (a)** Gradient PA = (6 + 1)/(2 - 1) = 7 [1]. Gradient PB = (6 - 5)/(2 - 9) = -1/7 [1]. Product = -1, so angle APB = 90°, and P lies on the circle with diameter AB [1]
**(b)** Centre = (5, 2) [1]. r² = (9 - 5)² + (5 - 2)² = 25, so **(x - 5)² + (y - 2)² = 25** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the question bans the equation in part (a), so the argument must come from gradients and the angle in a semicircle. End with a sentence linking a right angle to being on the circle.

**9. (a)** y = 7 - x, so y + 1 = 8 - x [1]. (x - 2)² + (8 - x)² = 26 gives x² - 10x + 21 = 0 [1]. x = 3 or 7, so **A(3, 4) and B(7, 0)** [1]
**(b)** Midpoint of AB = (5, 2) [1]. Gradient from the centre (2, -1) to (5, 2) is 1, AB has gradient -1, and 1 × (-1) = -1, so the line from the centre is perpendicular to AB and meets it at its midpoint [1]
**(c)** AB = √(16 + 16) = **4√2** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* a gradient check shows the perpendicularity, so give both gradients. The property in part (b) also gives the chord length from the centre's distance, which is a useful cross-check.

**10. (a)** (x - 1)² - 1 + (y - 3)² - 9 = 10 [1]. Centre **(1, 3)**, radius **√20 = 2√5** [1]
**(b)** 16 + 4 = 20, so (5, 5) is on the circle [1]. Radius gradient = (5 - 3)/(5 - 1) = 1/2, so tangent gradient = -2 [1]. y - 5 = -2(x - 5), giving **2x + y - 15 = 0** [1]
**(c)** X is (15/2, 0) and Y is (0, 15) [1]. Area = (1/2) × (15/2) × 15 = **225/4** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* checking that the point lies on the circle is a cheap line of working. The tangent must use the point of contact, not the centre, in the line equation.

**11. (a)** Substitute: (x - 3)² + (3x + k - 1)² = 10 [1]. Expanding: x² - 6x + 9 + 9x² + 6(k - 1)x + (k - 1)² = 10, so **10x² + (6k - 12)x + k² - 2k = 0** [1]
**(b)** Discriminant = (6k - 12)² - 40(k² - 2k) [1] = 36k² - 144k + 144 - 40k² + 80k = -4k² - 64k + 144 = **-4(k - 2)(k + 18)** [1]
**(c)** Tangent when the discriminant is 0: **k = 2 or k = -18** [1]. Two points when (k - 2)(k + 18) < 0, so **-18 < k < 2** [1]
**(d)** With k = 2 the quadratic is 10x² = 0, so x = 0 and y = 2: the point is **(0, 2)** [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* in "show that" parts every algebraic step should be on the page, because the result is given. For the range, sketching the sign of the quadratic in k avoids reversing the inequality.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the negative of the gradient, or its reciprocal, as the perpendicular gradient.
- Stopping at y = mx + c when the question asks for ax + by + c = 0 with integer coefficients.
- Reading the centre of (x + 2)² + (y - 5)² = 34 with the wrong signs.
- Comparing a point's distance-squared with r instead of r².
- Forgetting to divide by the coefficient of x² and y² before completing the square.
- Taking the circle's centre rather than the point of contact as the point on the tangent.
- Giving a model's gradient with no units or no link to the context.
- Rounding too early, or giving a decimal where an exact surd was requested.
- Writing a discriminant condition with the wrong inequality direction when finding a range.
