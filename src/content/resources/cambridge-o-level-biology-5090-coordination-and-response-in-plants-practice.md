## Section A

**1.** Roots growing downwards in response to gravity is an example of

A: negative gravitropism

B: negative phototropism

C: positive gravitropism

D: positive phototropism

**[1]**

**2.** In which part of a shoot is auxin made?

A: the leaf

B: the root tip

C: the shoot tip

D: the stem base

**[1]**

**3.** State what is meant by phototropism. **[2]**

**4.** State the effect of auxin on the cells in a shoot. **[1]**

**5.** State why it is useful for a shoot to grow towards light. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A seedling is placed in a box with a small hole on the right-hand side. After three days the shoot has bent to the right.

**(a)** Name the stimulus and the response. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain, using auxin, why the shoot bent. **[4]**

**(c)** The experiment is repeated with the tip of the shoot covered by a foil cap. Predict the result and explain it. **[2]**

**7.** A student placed germinating bean seeds on damp paper in a Petri dish and stood the dish on its edge in the dark, with the seeds in different positions. She also set up an identical dish on a slowly rotating clinostat.

**(a)** Describe the result you would expect for the dish that was not rotated. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the dish was kept in the dark. **[1]**

**(c)** State the purpose of the clinostat. **[1]**

**(d)** State **two** variables that should be kept the same in both dishes. **[2]**

**8.** Five shoots were grown with light from one side. The angles of curvature towards the light were 24, 30, 27, 33 and 31 degrees. Shoots of the same type on a clinostat had a mean angle of 2 degrees.

**(a)** Calculate the mean angle of curvature of the shoots with light from one side. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the difference between this mean and the mean for the clinostat. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the shoots on the clinostat did not bend. **[2]**

**9.** The tip of a shoot was cut off and placed on a block of agar for one hour. The block was then placed on the left-hand side of the cut stump of the shoot, which was kept in the dark. After two days the stump had bent to the right.

**(a)** Explain why the stump bent to the right. **[3]**

**(b)** State what would be expected if a block of plain agar, without a tip, was used instead. **[1]**

**10.** Explain why the roots of a germinating seed grow downwards while the shoot grows upwards even in the dark. **[3]**

**11.** A student claims that "auxin makes plant cells divide so that the shoot bends". Comment on this statement. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** A response in which parts of a plant grow [1] towards or away from light [1]. **[2]**

**4.** It stimulates cell elongation [1]. **[1]**

**5.** The leaves receive more light for photosynthesis [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** The stimulus is light from one side [1]. The response is phototropism (growth towards the light) [1].

**(b)** Auxin is made in the shoot tip [1]. It moves to the shaded side of the shoot [1]. Cells on the shaded side elongate more [1], so that side grows faster and the shoot bends towards the light [1].

**(c)** The shoot would not bend [1], because the tip detects the light and the auxin is not redistributed unequally [1]. **[8]**

**7.**

**(a)** The roots curve downwards [1] and the shoots curve upwards [1], whatever the position of the seed.

**(b)** So that light does not act as a stimulus [1].

**(c)** It makes gravity act equally on all sides, so it acts as a control [1].

**(d)** Any two of: temperature; type and age of seeds; amount of water; time [1 each]. **[6]**

**8.**

**(a)** (24 + 30 + 27 + 33 + 31) = 145 [1]; 145 divided by 5 = 29 degrees [1].

**(b)** 29 - 2 = 27 degrees [1].

**(c)** The rotation means light (or gravity) acts equally on all sides [1], so the auxin is distributed evenly and all cells elongate at the same rate [1]. **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** Auxin from the tip diffused into the agar [1] and then into the left-hand side of the stump [1]. Cells on the left elongated more, so the stump bent to the right [1].

**(b)** It would not bend (it would grow straight or hardly grow) [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Both respond to gravity, not light [1]. Roots are positively gravitropic, so they grow towards gravity [1]. Shoots are negatively gravitropic, so they grow away from gravity [1]. **[3]**

**11.** The statement is incorrect [1]. Auxin stimulates cell elongation, and unequal elongation on the two sides makes the shoot bend [1]. **[2]**

---

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that auxin moves to the lit side.
- Saying that auxin causes cell division rather than elongation.
- Writing "the plant moves" instead of "grows".
- Forgetting to use a control, or to keep light out of a gravitropism test.
- Mixing up positive and negative for shoots and roots.
