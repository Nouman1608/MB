Use these standard electrode potentials throughout:

| Half-equation | E⦵ / V |
|---|---|
| Mg²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Mg(s) | −2.37 |
| Zn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn(s) | −0.76 |
| Sn²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Sn(s) | −0.14 |
| Pb²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Pb(s) | −0.13 |
| 2H⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ H₂(g) | 0.00 |
| Cu²⁺(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ Cu(s) | +0.34 |
| Fe³⁺(aq) + e⁻ ⇌ Fe²⁺(aq) | +0.77 |
| Ag⁺(aq) + e⁻ ⇌ Ag(s) | +0.80 |
| Br₂(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Br⁻(aq) | +1.09 |
| Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq) + 14H⁺(aq) + 6e⁻ ⇌ 2Cr³⁺(aq) + 7H₂O(l) | +1.33 |
| Cl₂(aq) + 2e⁻ ⇌ 2Cl⁻(aq) | +1.36 |

## Section A

**1.** State the IUPAC convention for writing half-equations for electrode reactions. **[1]**

**2.** Describe the standard hydrogen electrode. **[3]**

**3.** Explain why the potential of a single electrode cannot be measured directly. **[1]**

**4.** Define the term *standard electrode potential*. **[2]**

**5.** A salt bridge is made from filter paper soaked in potassium nitrate solution. State its purpose and why potassium nitrate is suitable. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** A cell is made from a zinc half-cell (E⦵ = −0.76 V) and a lead half-cell (E⦵ = −0.13 V) under standard conditions.

**(a)** Write the conventional representation of the cell. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the EMF. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the overall equation for the cell reaction. **[1]**

**(d)** State the direction of electron flow in the external circuit. **[1]**

**7.** A standard half-cell of metal M (forming M²⁺ ions) is connected to a standard copper half-cell. The EMF is 0.48 V and the copper electrode is positive. E⦵(Cu²⁺/Cu) = +0.34 V. Calculate E⦵(M²⁺/M) and identify M from: zinc (−0.76 V), tin (−0.14 V) or lead (−0.13 V). **[2]**

**8.** A cell is made from an Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ half-cell (E⦵ = +0.77 V) and an acidified Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ half-cell (E⦵ = +1.33 V; Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6e⁻ ⇌ 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O) under standard conditions.

**(a)** Write the conventional representation of the cell. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the EMF. **[1]**

**(c)** Write the overall equation for the cell reaction. **[2]**

**9.** Use these values to predict whether each reaction is feasible under standard conditions: Fe³⁺/Fe²⁺ +0.77 V; Ag⁺/Ag +0.80 V; Br₂/Br⁻ +1.09 V; Cr₂O₇²⁻/Cr³⁺ +1.33 V; Cl₂/Cl⁻ +1.36 V.

**(a)** Silver ions oxidising iron(II) ions. Comment on the reliability of your prediction. **[2]**

**(b)** Bromine oxidising chloride ions. **[1]**

**(c)** Acidified dichromate(VI) oxidising bromide ions. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest why acidified dichromate(VI) does not oxidise chloride ions under standard conditions. **[1]**

**10.** In a standard zinc–copper cell (Zn²⁺/Zn −0.76 V; Cu²⁺/Cu +0.34 V), the concentration of Zn²⁺(aq) is increased. Explain the effect on the EMF of the cell. **[3]**

**11.** In required practical 6, a student measures the EMF of a zinc–copper cell as 0.98 V instead of the expected 1.10 V. Suggest **two** reasons for the difference. **[2]**

**12.** From the table of standard electrode potentials at the top of this page (Mg²⁺/Mg −2.37 V to Cl₂/Cl⁻ +1.36 V), identify the strongest oxidising agent and the strongest reducing agent. **[2]**

**13.** A standard copper half-cell (E⦵ = +0.34 V) is connected to the standard hydrogen electrode. Write the conventional representation of the cell and state its EMF. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** They are written as reductions, with the electrons on the left-hand side [1]

**2.** Hydrogen gas at 100 kPa bubbling over a platinum electrode [1]; dipping into a solution with [H⁺] = 1.00 mol dm⁻³ [1]; at 298 K, with its potential defined as 0.00 V [1]

**3.** Measuring a potential difference needs a second electrode, which has its own potential, so only differences can be measured [1]

**4.** The EMF of a cell made from the half-cell and the standard hydrogen electrode [1]; under standard conditions (298 K, 100 kPa, 1.00 mol dm⁻³ ions) [1]

**5.** It allows ions to move between the half-cells, completing the circuit and keeping each solution neutral [1]; potassium and nitrate ions do not react with or precipitate the ions in either half-cell [1]

**6. (a)** Zn(s) | Zn²⁺(aq) || Pb²⁺(aq) | Pb(s) [1]; zinc on the left as the more negative half-cell, with phase boundaries and salt bridge correctly shown [1]

**(b)** EMF = −0.13 − (−0.76) = **+0.63 V** [1]

**(c)** Zn + Pb²⁺ → Zn²⁺ + Pb [1]

**(d)** From the zinc electrode to the lead electrode [1]

**7.** 0.48 = 0.34 − E⦵(M²⁺/M), so E⦵(M²⁺/M) = **−0.14 V** [1]; M is **tin** [1]

**8. (a)** Pt(s) | Fe²⁺(aq), Fe³⁺(aq) [1]; || Cr₂O₇²⁻(aq), H⁺(aq), Cr³⁺(aq) | Pt(s) [1]

**(b)** EMF = 1.33 − 0.77 = **+0.56 V** [1]

**(c)** Cr₂O₇²⁻ + 14H⁺ + 6Fe²⁺ → 2Cr³⁺ + 7H₂O + 6Fe³⁺ [1] for the correct species, [1] for the balancing (6Fe²⁺ to match 6e⁻)

**9. (a)** Feasible, because +0.80 V is more positive than +0.77 V (EMF = +0.03 V) [1]; the EMF is so small that a change in conditions could reverse the prediction, so it is unreliable [1]

**(b)** Not feasible: +1.09 V is less positive than +1.36 V [1]

**(c)** Feasible: +1.33 V is more positive than +1.09 V (EMF = +0.24 V) [1]

**(d)** +1.33 V is less positive than +1.36 V, so Cr₂O₇²⁻ is not a strong enough oxidising agent to oxidise Cl⁻ [1]

**10.** Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻ ⇌ Zn shifts to the right [1]; so the zinc electrode potential becomes less negative [1]; the difference between the two electrodes is smaller, so the EMF decreases [1]
*Examiner insight:* Name the equilibrium and its shift before giving the effect on E; a bare "EMF decreases" scores one mark at most.

**11.** Any two from: oxide layer on an electrode [1]; solutions not exactly 1.00 mol dm⁻³ [1]; temperature not 298 K; voltmeter of low resistance drawing current; dried-out or poorly soaked salt bridge

**12.** Strongest oxidising agent: **Cl₂** [1]; strongest reducing agent: **Mg** [1]

**13.** Pt(s) | H₂(g) | H⁺(aq) || Cu²⁺(aq) | Cu(s) [1]; EMF = **+0.34 V** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Putting the more positive half-cell on the left.
- Missing Pt(s) or H⁺(aq) in the dichromate half-cell.
- Multiplying E⦵ when balancing six electrons.
- Not commenting on a very small EMF.
- Explaining a concentration effect without naming the electrode equilibrium.
