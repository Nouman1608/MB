## Section A

**1.** Name the layer of the atmosphere directly above the troposphere and the layer directly below the thermosphere. **[2]**

**2.** Name the gas that makes up most of the 1% of clean air that is not nitrogen or oxygen. **[1]**

**3.** A weather station records 31 °C and heavy rain on one afternoon. Explain why this is a description of weather, not climate. **[2]**

**4.** State **two** greenhouse gases that are present in clean air. **[2]**

**5.** State what happens to the solar radiation that is absorbed by the land and oceans. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** A 250 cm³ sample of clean air is analysed.
(a) Calculate the volume of nitrogen.
(b) Calculate the volume of oxygen.
(c) Calculate the volume of all other gases together. **[3]**

**7.** A student draws a pie chart of the composition of clean air.
(a) Calculate the angle for oxygen.
(b) Explain why carbon dioxide is very hard to show as a separate sector. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why the position of the ozone layer, in the lower stratosphere, is important for life at the Earth's surface. **[2]**

**9.** A student writes: "The greenhouse effect is caused by pollution and is bad for the planet." Explain what is wrong with this statement. **[3]**

**10.** A simple model shows what happens to 100 units of solar radiation arriving at the top of the atmosphere: 30 units are reflected back into space and the rest is absorbed by the land, oceans and atmosphere.
(a) Calculate the percentage of solar radiation absorbed.
(b) Describe what then happens to the absorbed energy, according to the natural greenhouse effect. **[4]**

**11.** On clear nights in a desert, temperatures fall much more than on cloudy nights. Use the natural greenhouse effect to explain why. **[3]**

**12.** Explain why the Moon, which has almost no atmosphere, has a much colder average surface temperature than the Earth, although both are a similar distance from the Sun. **[3]**

**13.** A town's average July temperature rose from 24 °C to 26 °C over 40 years. Explain why this is evidence about climate, and calculate the percentage increase. **[3]**

**14.** Nitrogen makes up 78% of clean air but is not a greenhouse gas. Use the definition of a greenhouse gas to explain what this tells you about nitrogen, and why the greenhouse effect depends on gases present in much smaller amounts. **[2]**

---

## Answers

**1.** Above the troposphere: the stratosphere [1]. Below the thermosphere: the mesosphere [1].

**2.** Argon [1].

**3.** It describes the condition of the atmosphere at a particular time, on one afternoon [1]; climate is based on the weather over many years [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: carbon dioxide [1]; water vapour [1]. Also accept methane.

**5.** It heats the Earth / the land and oceans warm up and give off heat [1].

**6. (a)** 250 × 78 / 100 = 195 cm³ [1]. **(b)** 250 × 21 / 100 = 52.5 cm³ [1]. **(c)** 250 − 195 − 52.5 = 2.5 cm³ [1].

**7. (a)** 21 × 3.6 = 75.6°, about 76° [1] [1]. **(b)** Carbon dioxide is only about 0.04% of clean air, so its sector would be a tiny fraction of a degree and could not be drawn [1].

**8.** The ozone layer absorbs much of the Sun's ultraviolet radiation [1] before it reaches the surface, protecting living things from its harmful effects [1].

**9.** The natural greenhouse effect is caused by greenhouse gases and clouds that occur naturally, not by pollution [1]; it is beneficial because it warms the Earth enough to support life [1]; it is the enhanced greenhouse effect, from extra greenhouse gases released by human activities, that causes global warming [1].

**10. (a)** 100 − 30 = 70 units [1], so 70% [1].
**(b)** The land and oceans are heated and give off heat [1]; some of this is absorbed by greenhouse gases and clouds and re-emitted back towards the surface, keeping the Earth warm [1].

**11.** At night the ground gives off heat it absorbed during the day [1]. Clouds absorb this heat and re-emit some back to the surface, so cloudy nights stay warmer [1]. With clear skies there is little cloud and often little water vapour in a desert, so more heat escapes to space and temperatures fall sharply [1].

**12.** The Moon has almost no greenhouse gases or clouds [1], so radiation given off by its surface is not absorbed and re-emitted back [1]; the energy escapes to space, whereas on Earth the natural greenhouse effect keeps the surface warm enough to support life [1].

**13.** It is based on average weather over many years, which matches the definition of climate, not one day's weather [1]. Increase = 26 − 24 = 2 °C [1]; 2 / 24 × 100 = 8.3% [1].

**14.** Nitrogen does not absorb radiation and emit the energy as thermal (heat) energy, so it plays no part in warming the surface however abundant it is [1]; the natural greenhouse effect depends on gases that do absorb and re-emit radiation, such as carbon dioxide and water vapour, even though they form only a small fraction of the air [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving the layers in the wrong order, especially swapping stratosphere and mesosphere.
- Calculating with 78% for oxygen, or forgetting that the three answers in a composition question should add up to the total.
- Writing that greenhouse gases reflect heat, instead of absorbing and re-emitting it.
- Treating the natural greenhouse effect as a problem.
- Defining climate without the phrase "over many years".
- Assuming the most abundant gas must have the biggest effect: what matters for warming is whether a gas absorbs and re-emits radiation, not how much of it there is.
