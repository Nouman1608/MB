These questions cover the second half of sub-topic B.4, Thermodynamics, in the IB *Physics guide* (first assessment 2025): entropy, the second law, heat engines and the Carnot limit. All of B.4 is additional higher level, so every question is HL only. Constants are supplied where needed: k_B = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹, and 0 °C = 273.15 K. Learn the ideas first in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-physics-entropy-the-second-law-heat-engines-and-the-carnot/) and the [revision notes](/resources/ib-dp-physics-entropy-the-second-law-heat-engines-and-the-carnot-revision-notes/).

## Questions

**1.** Six identical coins are tossed and land independently, each heads or tails with equal probability. For the macrostate "3 heads": find the number of microstates, the probability of the macrostate, and the entropy S. **[3]**

**2.** State the Clausius form and the Kelvin form of the second law of thermodynamics. **[2]**

**3.** A metal strap carries 900 J of thermal energy from a large reservoir at 360 K to a large reservoir at 280 K. Calculate the entropy change of each reservoir and of the combined system. **[3]**

**4.** A 0.15 kg sample of lead melts at its melting point of 600.6 K. The specific latent heat of fusion of lead is 2.3 × 10⁴ J kg⁻¹ (supplied data). Calculate the entropy change of the lead, and explain the sign of the change in terms of microstates. **[4]**

**5.** A heat engine produces 410 J of useful work per cycle with an efficiency of 0.34. Calculate the energy taken in per cycle and the energy rejected per cycle. **[3]**

**6.** A Carnot engine operates between reservoirs at 650 K and 290 K and takes in 4.8 kJ per cycle from the hot reservoir. Calculate its efficiency and the work it does per cycle. **[3]**

**7.** Two proposals are made for an engine working between 650 K and 290 K and taking in 5000 J per cycle. Proposal A produces 2600 J of work. Proposal B produces 3000 J of work. Decide whether each is consistent with the second law, and for B support your decision by calculating the entropy change of the two reservoirs per cycle. **[4]**

**8.** In a model of a gas, each of N = 3.0 × 10²³ particles is equally likely to be found in either half of a sealed box. A partition between the halves is removed from a gas that was entirely in one half. Calculate the entropy increase, and explain why the gas is never seen to return to one half. **[4]**

**9.** A freezer converts 0.20 kg of water at 273.15 K to ice at the same temperature. The room is at 297 K. The specific latent heat of fusion of water is 3.34 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹ (supplied data).

**(a)** Calculate the entropy change of the water. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the least energy that must be delivered to the room. **[1]**
**(c)** Hence find the least work the freezer must do, and explain why the second law allows the water's entropy to fall. **[2]**

**10.** A power station has steam at 823 K and cooling water at 293 K. It delivers 500 MW of electrical power with an efficiency of 0.38. Treat the temperatures as 823.15 K and 293.15 K.

**(a)** Calculate the Carnot limit for these temperatures. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the rate at which energy is supplied to the steam and the rate at which energy is rejected. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the rate of entropy production in the reservoirs. **[2]**
**(d)** Show that a Carnot engine with the same energy input would deliver about 347 MW more than this station. **[2]**

**11.** An engine takes 1200 J per cycle from a hot reservoir at 600 K and rejects 800 J per cycle to a cold reservoir at 300 K. It completes 20 cycles per second.

**(a)** Calculate the work per cycle and the efficiency. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the Carnot limit and comment on the comparison. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the entropy change of the reservoirs per cycle. **[2]**
**(d)** Calculate the power output. **[1]**
**(e)** Explain why the engine cannot be changed so that no energy is rejected. **[1]**

**12.** A Carnot engine works between 600 K and 300 K. Compare two upgrades: raising the hot reservoir by 50 K, or lowering the cold reservoir by 50 K. State which gives the larger Carnot limit, and why a real engine will not reach either value. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Ω = 6!/(3! × 3!) = 20 [1]. Total microstates = 2⁶ = 64, so probability = 20/64 = **0.3125** [1]. S = k_B ln 20 = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 3.00 = **4.14 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the combination formula before the number; a bare "20" gives the reader nothing to credit if it is wrong, and S needs the natural logarithm.

**2.** Clausius: thermal energy does not flow of its own accord from a colder body to a hotter one (it needs work to be done) [1]. Kelvin: no cyclic process can take energy from a single reservoir and convert all of it into work [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each form is a separate statement. Kelvin answers are stronger with both "cyclic" and "all of it into work", so a vague "no engine is 100% efficient" is weaker.

**3.** Hot reservoir: ΔS = −900/360 = **−2.50 J K⁻¹** [1]. Cold reservoir: ΔS = +900/280 = **+3.21 J K⁻¹** [1]. Total = −2.50 + 3.21 = **+0.714 J K⁻¹** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The sign of each term matters. State which reservoir loses energy before dividing, since a correct size with the wrong sign can lose credit for the total.

**4.** Q = mL = 0.15 × 2.3 × 10⁴ = 3450 J [1]. ΔS = 3450/600.6 = **5.74 J K⁻¹** [1]. The change is positive [1] because the liquid has far more possible arrangements of its particles than the solid, so Ω increases and S = k_B ln Ω increases [1]
*Examiner insight:* Link the sign to Ω explicitly. "More disorder" on its own is acceptable only if you also say what is being counted.

**5.** Q_h = W/η = 410/0.34 = **1.21 × 10³ J** [1]. Q_c = Q_h − W = 1206 − 410 [1] = **796 J** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep unrounded Q_h for the subtraction. Rounding to 1210 first gives 800 J and can cost the final answer.

**6.** η = 1 − T_c/T_h = 1 − 290/650 [1] = **0.554** [1]. W = ηQ_h = 0.554 × 4.8 = **2.66 kJ** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Temperatures are already in kelvin here, but say so. Writing the formula before substituting makes any later slip easier to credit.

**7.** Carnot limit = 1 − 290/650 = 0.554 [1]. Proposal A: η = 2600/5000 = 0.52, below the limit, so it is consistent with the second law [1]. Proposal B: η = 0.60, above the limit, so it is not allowed [1]. Support: Q_c = 5000 − 3000 = 2000 J, and ΔS = 2000/290 − 5000/650 = 6.90 − 7.69 = **−0.796 J K⁻¹**, a decrease in total entropy, which the second law forbids [1]
*Examiner insight:* For A, say "consistent with" rather than "possible in practice"; being under the Carnot limit does not mean the engine can be built.

**8.** Ω_f/Ω_i = 2^N [1]. ΔS = N k_B ln 2 = 3.0 × 10²³ × 1.38 × 10⁻²³ × 0.693 [1] = **2.87 J K⁻¹** [1]. Returning to one half is one microstate among 2^N equally likely ones, which is overwhelmingly improbable, and it would decrease the entropy of an isolated system [1]
*Examiner insight:* Mention both probability and entropy. Saying only "it would break the second law" does not explain why.

**9. (a)** Q = 0.20 × 3.34 × 10⁵ = 6.68 × 10⁴ J removed [1]. ΔS = −6.68 × 10⁴/273.15 = **−245 J K⁻¹** [1]
**(b)** The room must gain at least 245 J K⁻¹, so energy delivered is at least 245 × 297 = **7.26 × 10⁴ J** [1]
**(c)** Minimum work = 7.26 × 10⁴ − 6.68 × 10⁴ = **5.8 × 10³ J** [1]. The water is not isolated, so its entropy can fall locally if the surroundings gain at least as much [1]
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs the idea of compensation by the surroundings. Name the system and the surroundings explicitly.

**10. (a)** η_Carnot = 1 − 293.15/823.15 [1] = **0.644** [1]
**(b)** Input = 500/0.38 = **1.32 × 10³ MW** [1]. Rejected = 1316 − 500 = **816 MW** [1]
**(c)** Rate = 816/293.15 − 1316/823.15 = 2.783 − 1.598 [1] = **1.18 MW K⁻¹** [1]
**(d)** Carnot output = 0.644 × 1316 = 847 MW [1], and 847 − 500 = **347 MW** (equal to 293.15 × 1.18) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A "show that" needs the intermediate value displayed. Use unrounded numbers from (b) so the final figure lands on 347.

**11. (a)** W = 1200 − 800 = **400 J** [1]. η = 400/1200 = **0.333** [1]
**(b)** η_Carnot = 1 − 300/600 = **0.50** [1]. The engine is below the limit, as every real engine must be [1]
**(c)** ΔS = 800/300 − 1200/600 = 2.67 − 2.00 = **+0.667 J K⁻¹** [1] per cycle, and the sign is positive because the process is irreversible [1]
**(d)** P = 400 × 20 = **8.0 kW** [1]
**(e)** The Kelvin form forbids converting all the energy from one reservoir into work in a cycle, so some must be rejected [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) state that the reservoirs are being analysed, not the engine, whose own entropy is unchanged after a full cycle.

**12.** Baseline: η = 1 − 300/600 = 0.50 [1]. Raise T_h to 650 K: η = 1 − 300/650 = **0.538** [1]. Lower T_c to 250 K: η = 1 − 250/600 = **0.583** [1]. Lowering the cold reservoir gives the larger limit [1]. A real engine has friction, finite temperature differences and other irreversibilities, which produce entropy and keep efficiency below the Carnot value [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give both numbers and the comparison. A real engine is not limited only by temperatures, so name an irreversibility rather than saying "losses".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using °C in 1 − T_c/T_h or in ΔQ/T.
- Mixing up which reservoir loses and which gains energy, giving the wrong sign for each ΔS.
- Using log base 10 for S = k_B ln Ω.
- Not stating the macrostate count as a combination (N! over k! and (N − k)!).
- Rounding intermediate values and drifting from the expected answer in multi-step engine questions.
- Giving the entropy statement without "isolated", or ignoring the surroundings in the freezer type of question.
- Treating a Carnot limit as an achievable efficiency.
- Saying "it breaks the second law" without a number or a named form.
- Omitting units on ΔS (J K⁻¹) or on rates of entropy production (MW K⁻¹).
