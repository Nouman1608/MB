## Sole trader basics

| Point | Remember |
|---|---|
| Advantages | Keeps all profit; own boss; cheap and simple to start; private |
| Disadvantages | Unlimited liability; one person's funds; illness stops trading; all losses and workload |
| Trading | Buys and resells: cost of sales, gross profit |
| Service | Sells time or skill: fees less expenses, no gross profit |
| Manufacturing | Makes what it sells: manufacturing account first |
| Combination | Gross profit on the trading side; service income added after it |
| Statement of profit or loss | Profit or loss for a period |
| Statement of financial position | Assets, liabilities and capital on a specified date |

## Statement of financial position headings

- **Non-current assets:** used for over a year (van, machinery). **Intangible assets:** no physical form (goodwill).
- **Current assets:** cash or turned into cash within a year (inventory, trade receivables, other receivables, bank).
- **Current liabilities:** due within a year (trade payables, other payables, overdraft). **Non-current liabilities:** due after more than a year.
- **Capital:** opening capital + profit - drawings (+ capital introduced).

## Formulae

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Straight line | Cost x rate |
| Reducing balance | Rate x (cost - accumulated depreciation) |
| Revaluation | Opening value + additions - closing value |
| Cost of sales | Opening inventory + purchases (net of returns, plus carriage inwards) - closing inventory |
| Gross profit | Net revenue - cost of sales |
| Profit for the year | Gross profit + other income - expenses |
| Prime cost | Raw materials used + direct labour |
| Factory cost of production | Prime cost, plus overheads, then add the opening and deduct the closing work in progress |
| Expense for the year | Trial balance figure + accrued - prepaid |
| Income for the year | Trial balance figure + accrued - received in advance |

## Method: both statements from a trial balance

1. Read every adjustment first and tick each trial balance line it touches.
2. Work out depreciation, the allowance and the accruals and prepayments on a workings page.
3. Manufacturing only: build the manufacturing account to factory cost of production.
4. Build cost of sales and gross profit (trading or manufacturing), add other income, subtract adjusted expenses.
5. Set out each non-current asset with its cost, its depreciation to date and its carrying value.
6. List current assets, then capital, then liabilities. Adjust drawings.
7. Check that total assets equal total capital and liabilities.

## Where each adjustment goes

| Adjustment | Effect on profit | Effect on the statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Depreciation | Expense | Lowers carrying value |
| Expense owing | Higher expense | Other payables |
| Expense prepaid | Lower expense | Other receivables |
| Income accrued | Higher income | Other receivables |
| Income received early | Lower income | Other payables |
| Debt written off | Expense | Lowers trade receivables |
| Allowance rises or falls | Expense or income for the change only | Whole allowance deducted from trade receivables |
| Goods taken (at cost) | Lower purchases | Higher drawings |
| Asset taken (carrying value) | None | Asset lower, drawings higher |
| Private expense paid | Lower expense | Higher drawings |

## Worked reminder: correcting a draft profit

Rafiq Sobhan's draft profit is 9,800. Wages of 400 are owing; rent of 250 was paid in advance; commission of 180 is accrued; goods costing 120 were taken for own use; 90 of private telephone was charged to the business; a debt of 150 is irrecoverable; the allowance rises by 30.

Corrected profit = 9,800 - 400 + 250 + 180 + 120 + 90 - 150 - 30 = **9,860**. His drawings rise by 120 + 90 = 210.

## Effect of an error on the other balances

Each row shows what happens if the item is missed from the finished statements.

| Item missed | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|
| Closing inventory overstated by 300 | 300 too high | Assets and capital 300 too high |
| Accrued expense of 200 left out | 200 too high | Other payables 200 too low; capital 200 too high |
| Depreciation understated by 500 | 500 too high | Carrying value and capital 500 too high |
| Irrecoverable debt of 150 not written off | 150 too high | Trade receivables and capital 150 too high |
| Goods costing 80 taken, not recorded | 80 too low | Drawings 80 too low; closing capital unchanged |
| Printer with carrying value 300 taken, not recorded | No effect | Assets 300 too high; drawings 300 too low; capital 300 too high |

The statement of financial position can still balance when it is wrong, so a balance check does not find these errors.

The syllabus text says both papers draw on all the subject content and that calculators may be used, so 5.1 content can appear on either paper.

## Layout skeletons

```
Sales less returns = net revenue
Net revenue less cost of sales = gross profit
Gross profit plus other income, less expenses = profit

Non-current assets (cost, depreciation, carrying value)
plus current assets = total assets
Capital (opening, profit, drawings), then long-term and
short-term liabilities = same total
```

## Must-know distinctions

- **Accrued** means owing or earned but not yet paid or received. **Prepaid** (or received in advance) means settled before the period it relates to.
- **Irrecoverable debt:** a specific debt written off. **Allowance:** an estimate against what remains.
- **Reducing balance** works from carrying value. **Straight line** works from cost. **Revaluation** suits items counted rather than tracked individually.
- **Drawings** reduce capital and never appear as an expense.
- **Effect of a change:** trace it into profit, then capital, then the statement of financial position.

## Quick self-test

1. Which statement is drawn up as at a date?
2. Depreciation on cost 9,600 at 10% straight line?
3. Reducing balance at 20% when the carrying value at the start is 7,200?
4. Tools valued 2,000, bought 500, valued 1,700 at the end: depreciation?
5. After a write-off, trade receivables are 8,000; the allowance is to be 5% and is currently 310. What goes through the statement of profit or loss?
6. Where does a prepaid insurance balance appear in the statement of financial position?
7. Goods costing 260 are taken for own use. Effect on purchases and drawings?
8. Opening capital 21,000, profit 6,500, drawings 4,000: closing capital?
9. Raw materials used 12,000 and direct labour 9,000: prime cost?
10. A hairdresser's statement of profit or loss: is there a gross profit?
11. Closing inventory was understated by 500. Effect on profit?
12. An accrued income of 120 is recorded. Effect on income and on the statement of financial position?

### Answers

1. The statement of financial position.
2. 960.
3. 1,440.
4. 2,000 + 500 - 1,700 = 800.
5. New allowance 400, so an expense of 90.
6. Other receivables, a current asset.
7. Purchases fall by 260; drawings rise by 260.
8. 23,500.
9. 21,000.
10. No. It is a service business, so fees less expenses give profit.
11. Cost of sales is too high, so profit is 500 too low.
12. Income rises by 120; other receivables rise by 120.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing the full allowance, not the change, as the expense.
- Applying reducing balance to cost.
- Adding a prepayment to the expense.
- Putting private expenses or goods taken into expenses and forgetting drawings.
- Omitting accumulated depreciation, showing only carrying value with no workings.
- Showing a service business with cost of sales.
- Missing opening or closing work in progress in factory cost of production.
- Not checking that the statement of financial position balances.
