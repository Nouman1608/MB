## Section A - short questions

**1.** A government limits imports of rice to 50,000 tonnes a year. This is: **[1]**
A a tariff   B a quota   C an embargo   D free trade

**2.** Which of these is an invisible export for Country P? **[1]**
A Country P sells cars to Country Q   B tourists from Country Q stay in hotels in Country P   C Country P buys oil from Country Q   D Country P's residents holiday in Country Q

**3.** Country R's visible exports are $800m and its visible imports are $860m. Its Balance of Trade is: **[1]**
A surplus of $60m   B deficit of $60m   C surplus of $1,660m   D deficit of $860m

**4.** Define free trade. **[2]**

**5.** State **two** features of a trading bloc. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** sources of support for exporters. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** Explain **three** benefits of international trade to a country. **[6]**

**8.** *Nadia's firm imports kitchen equipment from a manufacturer on another continent and pays in the manufacturer's currency.*
Explain **three** challenges Nadia's firm faces as an importer. **[6]**

**9.** *The table shows trade figures for Country S for one year ($ million, invented).*

| Item | Exports | Imports |
|---|---|---|
| Goods | 1,250 | 1,410 |
| Services | 620 | 380 |
| Net investment flow into Country S | +40 | |

(a) Calculate Country S's Balance of Trade. (2 marks)
(b) Calculate the balance on goods and services together. (2 marks)
(c) Using all the figures, calculate the Balance of Payments figure and interpret your answer. (3 marks) **[7]**

**10.** *A furniture exporter in Country T prices a table at T$500. The exchange rate changes from T$1 = 2 units of Country V's currency to T$1 = 2.5 units.*
(a) Calculate the price of the table in Country V's currency before and after the change. (2 marks)
(b) Explain the likely effect of this change on the exporter. (2 marks) **[4]**

**11.** *Country W's government places a 25% tariff on imported bicycles. An importer buys bicycles at $120 each.*
(a) Calculate the cost of one bicycle including the tariff. (1 mark)
(b) Explain **two** effects of the tariff on commercial activities in Country W. (4 marks) **[5]**

**12.** *Country X is considering joining a trading bloc of six neighbouring countries.*
Discuss whether Country X should join the trading bloc. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. A quota limits the quantity imported. **[1]**

**2.** B. Foreign tourists buying hotel services earn Country P money for a service. **[1]**

**3.** B. 800 - 860 = -60, a deficit. **[1]**

**4.** International trade [1] without barriers such as tariffs, quotas or embargoes [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: members remove or reduce trade barriers between themselves [1]; often a common external tariff on imports from non-members [1]; agreed rules or standards [1]; sometimes free movement of people or capital, or a shared currency [1]. **[max 2]**

**6.** Any two: international trade fairs [1]; cheaper bank loans [1]; insurance guarantees [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** Any three, 2 marks each (benefit plus explanation):
Wider choice of goods: consumers can buy products the country cannot produce, such as fruit that will not grow in its climate [2].
Higher standard of living: people can buy more and better goods with their income [2].
Lower cost of imports: goods made more cheaply abroad because of resources or scale are cheaper for consumers and businesses [2].
More investment: foreign firms build factories and warehouses, bringing capital and technology [2].
Creation of jobs: in export industries, ports and transport firms [2]. **[max 6]**

**8.** Any three, 2 marks each (challenge plus effect on Nadia's firm):
Distance: long delivery times and high transport costs, and equipment may be damaged in transit [2].
Foreign exchange rates: if her currency weakens before payment, the equipment costs more in her currency, cutting her profit [2].
Currencies: she must exchange money to pay in the manufacturer's currency, paying bank charges [2].
Customs duties: duty raises the cost of the equipment [2].
Language: misunderstandings over specifications or contracts [2].
Different legal systems: harder to settle a dispute if goods are faulty [2].
Documentation: extra documents, such as the bill of lading, are needed to clear the goods [2]. **[max 6]**

**9.** (a) 1,250 - 1,410 [1] = **-$160 million, a deficit** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) Services: 620 - 380 = +240 [1]. Goods and services: -160 + 240 = **+$80 million** [1]. (2 marks)
(c) 80 + 40 = **+$120 million, a surplus** [1]. Country S imports more goods than it exports [1], but its earnings from services, such as tourism or finance, and the investment inflow more than cover the deficit on goods [1]. (3 marks) **[7]**

**10.** (a) Before: 500 x 2 = **1,000 units** [1]. After: 500 x 2.5 = **1,250 units** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) The table is now 250 units dearer for buyers in Country V [1], so demand for the exporter's tables may fall, or the exporter may have to lower its T$ price to stay competitive, reducing profit [1]. (2 marks) **[4]**

**11.** (a) 120 + (0.25 x 120) = 120 + 30 = **$150** [1]. (1 mark)
(b) Any two, 2 marks each: importers pay more, so they raise prices and sell fewer imported bicycles [2]; local bicycle makers face less competition and may sell more, creating jobs [2]; consumers have less choice or pay higher prices [2]; importers may switch to suppliers in countries with lower or no tariffs [2]. (max 4 marks) **[5]**

**12.** Up to 2 for advantages, up to 2 for disadvantages, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Advantages: Country X's exporters gain a tariff-free market of six neighbours [1]; cheaper imports of raw materials and goods from members [1]; foreign firms may invest in Country X to reach the whole bloc [1]; closer political cooperation with neighbours [1].
Disadvantages: Country X's firms face more competition from members' firms and some may close [1]; it must follow the bloc's rules and loses control over trade policy [1]; imports from non-members may become dearer if a common external tariff applies [1].
Conclusion: for example, join if Country X's firms are competitive and the neighbours are its main trading partners, as the larger market outweighs the competition [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a Balance of Trade answer without a sign or the word surplus or deficit.
- Including services in the Balance of Trade.
- Multiplying when an exchange rate question needs division, or the reverse; check whether the answer should be bigger or smaller.
- Confusing quotas and embargoes.
- Discussing a trading bloc in general terms without applying it to the country in the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is tourism visible or invisible trade?** Invisible: tourism is a service. Foreign tourists spending in a country are an invisible export for that country.

**What happens to exports if a country's currency gets stronger?** They become more expensive for foreign buyers, so demand for them may fall.
