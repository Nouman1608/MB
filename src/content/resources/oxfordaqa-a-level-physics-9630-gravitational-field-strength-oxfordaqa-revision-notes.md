These notes condense section **3.7.2 Gravitational field strength** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification: the "concept of a force field as a region in which a body experiences a force", "representation by gravitational field lines", g = F/m and the "magnitude of g in a radial field given by g = GM/r²". Section 3.7 is International A-level only.

For explanations and worked examples, read the [gravitational field strength study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-field-strength-oxfordaqa/). Test yourself with the [gravitational field strength practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-gravitational-field-strength-oxfordaqa-practice/).

Data: G = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ N m² kg⁻²; Earth mass 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg, radius 6.37 × 10⁶ m; Moon mass 7.35 × 10²² kg; Earth-Moon distance 3.84 × 10⁸ m.

## Core recall

- A force field is a region in which a body experiences a force.
- Gravitational field strength g = F/m: force per unit mass on a small test mass at that point.
- Unit N kg⁻¹, equivalent to m s⁻²; g is the free-fall acceleration at that point.
- g is a vector, directed towards the mass creating the field.
- Radial field of a point mass or uniform sphere: g = GM/r², r from the centre.
- Near a planet's surface the field is almost uniform over small heights.
- Fields from several bodies add as vectors.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Force field | Region in which a body experiences a force |
| Gravitational field strength g | Force per unit mass at a point in the field |
| Test mass | A small mass used to probe a field without changing it noticeably |
| Radial field | Field whose lines point to a central point, strength falling as 1/r² |
| Uniform field | Field with the same strength and direction everywhere; parallel, evenly spaced lines |
| Neutral point | Point where the fields of two bodies cancel, giving zero resultant |
| Field line | Line showing the direction of the force on a mass placed on it |

## Equations

| Equation | Use |
|---|---|
| g = F/m | Definition; also F = mg |
| g = GM/r² | Radial field strength |
| g(h) = g₀ (R/(R + h))² | Field at height h above a planet with surface field g₀ |
| g = (4/3)πGρR | Surface field of a uniform sphere of density ρ |
| GM(1)/x² = GM(2)/(d − x)² | Neutral point between two bodies |

## Field-line rules

- Arrows point towards the mass.
- Closer lines mean a stronger field.
- Radial lines spread out with distance.
- Lines never cross.
- Near the surface: parallel, evenly spaced, vertical lines.

## Method: neutral point

1. Let x be the distance from the larger body; the other distance is d − x.
2. Set GM(1)/x² = GM(2)/(d − x)². G cancels.
3. Take square roots: x/(d − x) = √(M(1)/M(2)).
4. Solve the linear equation for x.
5. Check the point lies between the bodies and nearer the smaller one.

## Method: resultant field of two bodies

1. Calculate each g with its own centre-to-centre distance.
2. Assign directions: each field points towards its own body.
3. Between the bodies the fields oppose; on the line outside, they add.
4. State size and direction.

## Quick reminders with numbers

- g at the Earth's surface: 9.81 N kg⁻¹; at r = 2R: 2.45 N kg⁻¹.
- 10 km up, g falls by only about 0.31%.
- Earth-Moon neutral point: 3.46 × 10⁸ m from the Earth's centre (0.900 of the way).
- 3.0 × 10⁶ m above the Earth's surface: g = 4.54 N kg⁻¹.

## Exam traps

- g is not 9.81 N kg⁻¹ everywhere.
- The test mass cancels: g does not depend on it.
- r is from the centre, not the surface.
- Between two bodies, subtract the fields; do not add.
- Keep the root of the neutral-point equation that lies between the bodies.
- A definition of g needs "per unit mass", not just "the force".
- A uniform field must be drawn with equal spacing as well as parallel lines.

## Self-test

1. Define gravitational field strength.
2. Show that N kg⁻¹ is equivalent to m s⁻².
3. A 4.0 kg mass feels a gravitational force of 14.8 N. Find g.
4. Find g at the surface of a planet of mass 6.4 × 10²³ kg and radius 3.4 × 10⁶ m.
5. g at a planet's surface is 12 N kg⁻¹. Find g at a distance of three planetary radii from its centre.
6. Describe the field lines of an isolated planet.
7. Why may the field in a laboratory be treated as uniform?
8. A planet has twice the Earth's density and the same radius. Find its surface g.
9. Two bodies have masses in the ratio 25 : 1 and are 6.0 × 10⁸ m apart. Find the neutral point.
10. Does g at a point depend on the mass placed there? Explain.
11. Two fields of 3.3 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹ and 8.3 × 10⁻⁴ N kg⁻¹ act in opposite directions. Find the resultant.
12. Find g 3.0 × 10⁶ m above the Earth's surface.

### Answers

1. The **force per unit mass** acting on a small mass placed at that point.
2. 1 N = 1 kg m s⁻², so 1 N kg⁻¹ = 1 kg m s⁻² kg⁻¹ = **1 m s⁻²**.
3. g = 14.8/4.0 = **3.7 N kg⁻¹**.
4. g = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 6.4 × 10²³/(3.4 × 10⁶)² = **3.7 N kg⁻¹**.
5. 12/3² = **1.3 N kg⁻¹**.
6. **Radial** lines pointing **inwards** towards the centre, spreading apart with distance, never crossing.
7. The heights involved are tiny compared with the Earth's radius, so g changes by a **negligible** amount.
8. g ∝ ρR, so g = 2 × 9.81 = **19.6 N kg⁻¹**.
9. x/(d − x) = 5, so x = 5d/6 = **5.0 × 10⁸ m** from the larger body.
10. **No**: the force is proportional to the mass, so F/m is the same for any test mass.
11. 3.3 × 10⁻³ − 0.83 × 10⁻³ = **2.5 × 10⁻³ N kg⁻¹**, in the direction of the larger field.
12. g = 6.67 × 10⁻¹¹ × 5.97 × 10²⁴/(9.37 × 10⁶)² = **4.5 N kg⁻¹**.
