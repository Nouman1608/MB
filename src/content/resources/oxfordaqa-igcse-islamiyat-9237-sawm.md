**This guide covers content area 3.1.2.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Islamiat (9237), Sawm: "the meaning and importance of fasting during the month of Ramadhan", "the rulings around fasting including the exemptions to fasting and the acts that invalidate a fast", "the spiritual, moral, and social benefits of fasting", "the significance of the Taraweeh prayer (night prayer) and Itikaaf" and "the meaning and importance of Laylat ul-Qadr (Night of Power)".** The prescribed passage is Qur'an 2:183-185. The [Sawm rulings, Taraweeh and Laylat ul-Qadr study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-sawm-rulings-taraweeh-laylat-ul-qadr/) covers the detailed rulings. This page takes the wider view: what Sawm means, how the month is organised, why it is a pillar, and how the five parts of the content area join together.

## What Sawm means

*Sawm* means "to abstain". In Islamic law it is abstaining, with the intention of worship, from food, drink and sexual relations from true dawn (Fajr) until sunset (Maghrib). It is the fourth pillar in the order of the specification and is required during Ramadhan, the ninth month of the Islamic calendar.

Ramadhan has a special place because "the month of Ramadhan [is that] in which was revealed the Qur'an, a guidance for the people and clear proofs of guidance and criterion" (2:185). The month is therefore linked with both fasting and the Qur'an.

## The calendar of Ramadhan

The Islamic calendar is lunar. A month begins with the sighting of the new moon and lasts 29 or 30 days. Twelve lunar months last about 354 days, which is roughly 11 days shorter than a solar year (365.24 − 354.37 ≈ 10.9). As a result, Ramadhan begins about 11 days earlier each year in the solar calendar and moves through all the seasons. It takes about 33 years (365.24 ÷ 10.9 ≈ 33.6) to return to the same season.

**Why this matters:** Muslims in a given place fast long summer days in some years and short winter days in others, which is fair over a lifetime. In places far from the equator, summer days can be very long, so scholars discuss how to calculate the fast there. A Muslim also depends on community and local scholars for the start and end of the month, and the ending of the month is celebrated with Eid ul-Fitr.

## A day in Ramadhan

| Time | Action | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Before dawn | **Suhoor**, the pre-dawn meal | A blessing in it, according to the Sunnah (Bukhari and Muslim) |
| Before dawn | Make the intention (*niyyah*) | Fasting is worship, so it needs intention |
| Dawn to sunset | Abstain; pray; read Qur'an; work as usual | Self-control and worship |
| Sunset | **Iftar**, breaking the fast, often with dates and water | Following the Prophet (pbuh); gratitude |
| Night | Isha, then **Taraweeh** | Extra worship |
| Last ten days | **Itikaaf** (for some) and seeking Laylat ul-Qadr | Seeking forgiveness |

## Rulings

### Who must fast, and who is exempt

Fasting is obligatory on adult Muslims of sound mind who are healthy and resident. The Qur'an gives the concessions: "So whoever among you is ill or on a journey [during them] – then an equal number of days [are to be made up]" (2:184).

| Person | Position |
|---|---|
| Ill person (temporary illness) | Does not fast; makes up later (*qada*) |
| Traveller | May delay and make up later |
| Elderly or permanently ill | Gives *fidya*, feeding a poor person for each missed day |
| Menstruating or post-natal women | Do not fast; make up the days afterwards |
| Pregnant or breastfeeding women | Many scholars allow them to delay if fasting would harm them or the child; they make up days later (some require fidya) |
| Children | Not required before puberty; many practise partial fasts |

### What invalidates a fast

- Eating or drinking on purpose.
- Sexual relations in the day.
- Deliberate vomiting.
- Menstruation or post-natal bleeding starting during the day.
- Giving up the intention.

Deliberately breaking the fast through sexual relations carries a heavier penalty (*kaffarah*) in classical teaching. Acts that do **not** break the fast include eating or drinking by genuine mistake, an involuntary vomit, a bath or shower, and brushing the teeth if no water is swallowed. Lying, backbiting and anger do not nullify the fast technically, but they destroy its reward. The Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever does not give up false speech and acting upon it, Allah has no need of his giving up food and drink (Sahih al-Bukhari).

## The benefits of fasting

The specification names three groups of benefits. Use them as headings.

### Spiritual

- **Taqwa:** the stated purpose is "that you may become righteous" (2:183).
- **Closeness to Allah:** the fast is known to Allah alone, which builds sincerity.
- **Forgiveness:** the Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever fasts Ramadhan with faith and hope of reward has past sins forgiven (Bukhari and Muslim).
- **Connection with the Qur'an:** many Muslims recite or listen to the whole Qur'an in the month.

### Moral

- **Self-control:** resisting hunger trains the will to resist other temptations.
- **Patience:** coping with thirst and tiredness builds patience.
- **Honesty:** nobody checks a fast, so keeping it is a matter of integrity.
- **Gratitude:** food and water taste more precious.

### Social

- **Empathy:** feeling hunger helps Muslims understand the poor.
- **Generosity:** Muslims give more charity in Ramadhan, and Zakat-ul-Fitr is paid before Eid.
- **Unity:** the whole Ummah fasts and breaks the fast together.
- **Family and community:** iftar is often shared, and mosques serve meals.

## Taraweeh and Itikaaf

**Taraweeh** is a voluntary night prayer in Ramadhan, prayed after Isha, usually in congregation at the mosque. Many mosques recite one-thirtieth of the Qur'an each night so that the whole Qur'an is completed by the end of the month. The Prophet (pbuh) said that whoever stands in prayer in Ramadhan with faith and hope of reward will have past sins forgiven (Bukhari and Muslim). Hazrat Umar (RA) gathered people behind one imam for this prayer.

**Itikaaf** is retreat in the mosque, usually in the last ten days, when a person stays for worship and leaves only for needs. It removes distractions and focuses the heart on Allah, the Qur'an and seeking Laylat ul-Qadr.

## Laylat ul-Qadr: the Night of Power

*Laylat ul-Qadr* is the night on which the Qur'an was first revealed. Surah al-Qadr (97) calls it "better than a thousand months". A thousand months is 1,000 ÷ 12 ≈ 83.3 years, which is longer than most human lifetimes. The night is in the last ten nights of Ramadhan, and the Prophet (pbuh) advised seeking it on the odd nights: the 21st, 23rd, 25th, 27th and 29th. Many Muslims give special attention to the 27th, though the exact night is not known with certainty. The reason for its secrecy, teachers explain, is to encourage worship on all of the nights.

On the night, angels descend, and it is "peace until the emergence of dawn" (97:5). Muslims stay awake in prayer, recite the Qur'an and pray for forgiveness. The Prophet (pbuh) taught a short prayer for it: "O Allah, You are forgiving and love forgiveness, so forgive me" (Tirmidhi).

## The prescribed passage: Qur'an 2:183-185

| Phrase | What it shows |
|---|---|
| "decreed upon you is fasting as it was decreed upon those before you" | Fasting has a long history and is part of the shared message of the prophets |
| "that you may become righteous" | The aim is taqwa |
| "[Fasting for] a limited number of days" | The duty is bounded |
| "ill or on a journey ... an equal number of days" | Concession and qada |
| "a ransom [as substitute] of feeding a poor person" | Fidya; social care |
| "The month of Ramadhan ... in which was revealed the Qur'an" | The link between fasting and revelation |
| "Allah intends for you ease and does not intend for you hardship" | Religion is practical and merciful |
| "complete the period and ... glorify Allah" | End of the month with gratitude (Eid) |

## Worked example: a 4-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** benefits of fasting in Ramadhan. **[4]**

> A spiritual benefit is that fasting builds taqwa, awareness of Allah [1], because a Muslim gives up lawful food for His sake and remembers Him all day, which the Qur'an gives as the purpose of fasting [1]. A social benefit is that it increases empathy with the poor [1], since the fasting person feels hunger and so is more willing to give charity, such as Zakat-ul-Fitr [1].

## Worked example: a 6-mark answer

**Question:** Explain **two** reasons why Laylat ul-Qadr is important. Apply **one** teaching from a source of authority. **[6]**

> It is important because it is the night when the Qur'an was first revealed [1], which marks the start of Allah's final guidance to humanity [1]. It is also important because worship on it is better than a thousand months [1], so it is a chance for forgiveness and for a Muslim to draw close to Allah, and that is why many spend the last nights in prayer [1]. Surah al-Qadr says that "the Night of Decree is better than a thousand months" [2], which shows why Muslims seek it so keenly.

## Common mistakes

- **Treating the fast as only food and drink.** The moral and spiritual aims matter.
- **Confusing qada and fidya.** Qada is making up days; fidya is feeding the poor.
- **Calling Taraweeh compulsory.** It is a highly encouraged voluntary prayer.
- **Fixing Laylat ul-Qadr on the 27th as a certainty.** It is sought in the odd nights.
- **Forgetting the passage.** Use "that you may become righteous" and "Allah intends for you ease".
