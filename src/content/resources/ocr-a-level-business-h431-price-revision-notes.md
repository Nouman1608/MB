## The specification points

1. Explain what is meant by 'price'.
2. Evaluate pricing strategies: skimming, penetration, competition-based, psychological, cost-plus (full-cost), marginal and contribution.
3. Explain price, income and cross elasticities of demand.
4. Calculate and interpret price, income and cross elasticities of demand.
5. Evaluate the usefulness of elasticities to a business.
6. Evaluate the impact and importance of price to a business and its stakeholders.
7. Recommend and justify a pricing mix as part of a marketing strategy.

## Core recall

### Strategies

| Strategy | Key idea | Best when | Risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Skimming | High launch price, lowered later | New, innovative product; few rivals | Rivals enter; slow take-up |
| Penetration | Low launch price to win share | Competitive market; scale cuts costs | Low early profit; hard to raise price |
| Competition-based | Match, undercut or exceed rivals | Similar products | Price war; ignores costs |
| Psychological | £9.99, prestige, bundles | Consumer goods | Effect uncertain |
| Cost-plus (full-cost) | Full unit cost plus mark-up | Costs known | Ignores demand and rivals |
| Marginal | At or above variable cost; for extra output | Spare capacity; clearing stock | Fixed costs not covered; upsets regular customers |
| Contribution | Each product earns a contribution to fixed costs | Multi-product firms | Needs good cost data |

### Cost-plus and marginal

- Full cost per unit = variable cost + (fixed costs ÷ output).
- Price = full cost × (1 + mark-up).
- Special order is worth accepting if price is above variable cost, there is spare capacity, and it does not damage regular sales. Extra contribution = (price − variable cost) × units.

### Elasticities

| Measure | Formula | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| PED | % change quantity ÷ % change price | More than 1 (ignoring sign): elastic, price rise cuts revenue. Less than 1: inelastic, price rise raises revenue |
| YED | % change quantity ÷ % change income | Positive: normal; above 1: luxury; negative: inferior |
| XED | % change quantity of A ÷ % change price of B | Positive: substitutes; negative: complements |

Always calculate percentage changes first: (new − old) ÷ old × 100.

### Usefulness of elasticities

Uses: choose prices that raise revenue; forecast demand; plan for the economic cycle; predict rivals' effects. Limits: based on past data; other factors change; differs between groups and over time; a small price change may not predict a large one.

### Importance of price

Decides revenue, margin and position. Customers care about value; employees' jobs depend on sales; shareholders look at profit; suppliers may be squeezed; rivals may respond.

### Pricing mix: steps

1. Start from the objective.
2. Check cost (floor price).
3. Check demand and rivals.
4. Consider life-cycle stage and brand.
5. State the risk and review.

## Quick worked reminder

Skimming against penetration (Orbis Tech, invented; variable cost £50): skimming £120 × 3,000 then £90 × 9,000 gives £210,000 + £360,000 = £570,000. Penetration £60 × 15,000 then £75 × 14,000 gives £150,000 + £350,000 = £500,000, but 29,000 units against 12,000.

Cost-plus: variable £14, fixed £180,000, output 12,000, so fixed per unit £15 and full cost £29. A 40% mark-up gives £40.60. Special order of 2,000 at £22 gives (22 − 14) × 2,000 = £16,000.

PED: price £10 to £11 is +10%; quantity 5,000 to 4,300 is −14%; PED = −1.4, elastic. Revenue falls from £50,000 to £47,300.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Price skimming | A high launch price that is lowered over time |
| Penetration pricing | A low launch price to gain market share |
| Cost-plus pricing | Unit cost plus a mark-up |
| Marginal cost | The extra cost of producing one more unit |
| Contribution | Selling price minus variable cost per unit |
| Price elasticity of demand | The responsiveness of quantity demanded to a price change |
| Income elasticity of demand | The responsiveness of quantity demanded to a change in income |
| Cross elasticity of demand | The responsiveness of demand for one product to a change in another's price |

## Exam traps

- Percentage change, not absolute change.
- Sign matters for YED and XED.
- A price below full cost can still be worthwhile if above variable cost and capacity is spare.
- Skimming and penetration depend on the objective.
- Interpret each calculation, do not just state the number.

## Command words

- **Explain:** define the strategy or elasticity and say what it means for the business.
- **Calculate:** show percentage changes first, then divide; give the sign.
- **Interpret:** say whether demand is elastic or inelastic and what happens to revenue.
- **Evaluate:** weigh the strategy against the objective, costs, rivals and demand, then judge.
- **Recommend and justify:** choose a pricing mix, give a reason from the case and state a risk.

## Self-test

1. Define penetration pricing.
2. When is skimming most suitable?
3. Variable cost £8, fixed costs £60,000, output 10,000. Find the full cost per unit.
4. Add a 25% mark-up to a full cost of £14. Find the price.
5. Price falls 5% and quantity rises 12%. Find the PED.
6. Is demand in question 5 elastic or inelastic?
7. YED is −0.5. What kind of good is it?
8. XED is +2.0. What is the relationship between the goods?
9. Price £6, variable cost £4. A special order of 500 units is offered at £5. Find the extra contribution.
10. Give one limit of PED.
11. Give one reason why price is important to shareholders.
12. Why might a firm avoid cost-plus pricing in a competitive market?

### Answers

1. A low launch price intended to win market share quickly.
2. For a new, innovative product with few rivals, where customers will pay more at first.
3. 8 + (60,000 ÷ 10,000) = 8 + 6 = **£14**.
4. 14 × 1.25 = **£17.50**.
5. 12 ÷ −5 = **−2.4**.
6. Elastic (2.4 is greater than 1).
7. An inferior good.
8. Substitutes.
9. (5 − 4) × 500 = **£500**, provided there is spare capacity.
10. Any one: based on past data; other factors change; may not apply to large price changes.
11. Price affects revenue and margin, and so profit and dividends.
12. It ignores rivals' prices and customers' willingness to pay, so the price may be too high to sell.
