## Short-answer questions

Cambridge IGCSE Accounting 0452 question papers are not divided into lettered sections, so the questions below are grouped only by length.

**1.** Identify **two** interested parties, other than owners and managers, named in the syllabus. **[2]**

**2.** Which party is most likely to study a business's trade payables turnover (days) before deciding whether to give credit?

A: Customers

B: Suppliers

C: Employees

D: Tax authorities

**[1]**

**3.** State **one** decision a bank might make using a business's accounting information. **[1]**

**4.** Explain **one** difference between the way an investor and a lender use a company's financial statements. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why the members of a club are interested in its income and expenditure account. **[2]**

**6.** The Hillcrest Tennis Club has 90 members, each paying a subscription of $50. It has no other income. Its expenditure for the year was $5,040.

**(a)** Calculate the club's surplus or deficit for the year. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the subscription per member that would have covered the year's expenditure. **[2]**

## Longer questions

**7.** Tidewater Tools Limited has asked a supplier, which allows 45 days' credit, to double its credit limit. The supplier obtains these figures.

| Item | $ |
|---|---|
| Current assets | 72,000 |
| of which inventory | 32,000 |
| Current liabilities | 40,000 |
| Trade payables | 28,000 |
| Credit purchases | 170,000 |

**(a)** Calculate the current ratio, the acid test ratio and the trade payables turnover (days). **[3]**

**(b)** Advise the supplier whether to double Tidewater's credit limit. Justify your answer. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how **(a)** employees and **(b)** customers might use the accounting information of a large car dealership.

**(a)** Employees. **[2]**

**(b)** Customers. **[2]**

**9.** Northgate Printing Limited reports these figures.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Profit margin | 8.00% | 11.00% |
| ROCE | 12.00% | 16.00% |
| Current ratio | 1.90 : 1 | 1.10 : 1 |
| Acid test ratio | 1.20 : 1 | 0.60 : 1 |
| Dividends paid | $20,000 | $45,000 |

**(a)** Explain how a potential investor would view these results. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how a lender considering a long-term loan would view these results. **[3]**

**10.** Ravensworth Limited made a profit for the year of $80,000. The directors propose dividends of $70,000. The company has a bank loan that is due for repayment in two years. Explain why the shareholders and the bank may disagree about the proposed dividend. **[4]**

**11.** The managers of Oakridge Gifts see that the gross profit margin has fallen from 45.00% to 38.00% and that wages have risen by $6,000. Explain **two** decisions the managers could make using this information. **[4]**

**12.** A local environmental group wants to judge the effect of a factory on the town. It obtains the factory owner's financial statements. Discuss how useful the statements will be to the group. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, one mark each: employees [1]; banks [1]; investors and lenders; suppliers; customers; governments / tax authorities; club members; other interested parties such as public and environmental bodies. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. Suppliers compare how long a business takes to pay with the credit they allow. **[1]**

**3.** Any one: whether to lend [1]; whether to allow or extend an overdraft; how much to lend; what security or interest rate to require. **[1]**

**4.** An investor shares in profit, so looks at profitability, ROCE and dividends to judge the return on shares [1]; a lender receives fixed interest, so looks at liquidity, existing borrowing and security to judge whether interest will be paid and the loan repaid [1]. **[2]**

**5.** It shows whether subscriptions and other income covered the club's expenditure, giving a surplus or deficit [1], so members can decide whether to change the subscription, cut costs or raise funds [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Income 90 × 50 = 4,500; deficit 5,040 − 4,500 = **$540** [1].

**(b)** 5,040 ÷ 90 [1] = **$56** per member [1]. **[3]**

**7. (a)** Current ratio 72,000 : 40,000 = **1.80 : 1** [1]. Acid test (72,000 − 32,000) : 40,000 = **1.00 : 1** [1]. Trade payables turnover 28,000 ÷ 170,000 × 365 = **60.12 days** [1].

**(b)** The acid test of 1.00 : 1 shows Tidewater can just meet current liabilities without selling inventory [1]. However, it takes 60.12 days to pay against 45 days allowed, so it already pays late [1]. Advice: do not double the limit; keep the present limit, or raise it only once Tidewater pays within 45 days [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** Employees want to know whether their jobs are secure and whether the dealership can afford pay rises [1], so they look at the trend in profit and at liquidity [1].

**(b)** Customers want to know the dealership will survive to honour warranties and provide servicing and spare parts [1], so they look for steady profit and sound liquidity [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** The investor sees profitability improving: profit margin up from 8.00% to 11.00% and ROCE from 12.00% to 16.00% [1]. Dividends more than doubled to $45,000, an attractive return on shares [1]. However, the fall in liquidity may worry the investor, because a cash shortage could threaten future dividends [1].

**(b)** The lender is mainly concerned about repayment [1]. The acid test has halved to 0.60 : 1, so Northgate may struggle to pay current liabilities without selling inventory [1]. High dividends are taking cash out of the business, so the lender may refuse, lend less or require security, despite better profitability [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Shareholders want a high dividend as their return on investment [1]; $70,000 is 87.5% of the year's profit [1]. The bank wants cash kept in the company so the loan can be repaid in two years [1], and paying most of the profit out reduces cash and retained earnings available for repayment [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Two decisions, each with one mark for the decision and one for the link to the information.

- Review selling prices or find cheaper suppliers [1], because the fall in gross profit margin from 45.00% to 38.00% shows less gross profit on each $100 of revenue [1].
- Review staffing levels or hours [1], because the $6,000 rise in wages reduces profit for the year [1].
- Also creditable: check that closing inventory has been valued correctly, since an error affects gross profit. **[4]**

**12.** Useful: the statements show whether the factory is profitable and liquid, so the group can judge whether it is likely to keep providing local jobs [1]. They may show spending on items such as waste disposal or cleaning [1], and whether the business could afford to improve its environmental practices [1].

Limited: the statements show only items that can be measured in money [1], so pollution, noise or damage to the landscape are not recorded [1].

Conclusion: the statements are of limited use, so the group would need other information, such as reports on emissions or waste, to judge the factory's effect on the town [1]. **[6]**
