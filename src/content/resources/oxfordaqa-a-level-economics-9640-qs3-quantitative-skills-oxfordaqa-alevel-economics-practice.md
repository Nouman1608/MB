These questions cover quantitative skill QS3 from section 3.5 of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Economics (9640) specification: "understand and use the terms mean, median (for A2 assessments, students will also need to understand and use relevant quantiles)". Questions 6 to 9 use quantiles, which the specification adds for A2 assessments. Every country, firm and figure is invented; "cu" means currency units. Read the [QS3 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs3-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics/) first, or refresh the methods with the [QS3 revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-economics-9640-qs3-quantitative-skills-oxfordaqa-alevel-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** The weekly incomes, in cu, of seven households are 12, 15, 15, 18, 22, 26 and 60. Find the mean and the median. **[2]**

**2.** Town A has 4,000 households with a mean income of 35,000 cu. Town B has 6,000 households with a mean income of 25,000 cu. Calculate the mean income of all 10,000 households. **[2]**

**3.** A café sells 120 cups of tea at 2.50 cu and 80 cups of coffee at 3.00 cu. Calculate the mean price paid per cup. **[2]**

**4.** Country P has a Gini coefficient of 0.25 and Country Q has a Gini coefficient of 0.45. State which country has the more unequal distribution of income. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why the median is often preferred to the mean when describing household wealth. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows the annual incomes of 100 households in a town.

| Income (thousand cu) | Households |
|---|---|
| 0 to under 10 | 15 |
| 10 to under 20 | 30 |
| 20 to under 30 | 30 |
| 30 to under 50 | 15 |
| 50 to under 100 | 10 |

**(a)** Estimate the mean income. **[3]**

**(b)** Estimate the median income. **[3]**

**(c)** Compare the mean and the median, and state what this suggests about the distribution. **[1]**

**7.** Use the table in Question 6.

**(a)** Estimate the lower quartile, the upper quartile and the interquartile range. **[3]**

**(b)** Estimate the 10th and 90th percentiles, and calculate the 90:10 ratio. **[3]**

**8.** The table shows the shares of total income received by each fifth of households, from poorest to richest, in two countries.

| Quintile | Poorest | 2nd | 3rd | 4th | Richest |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Country R (%) | 4 | 8 | 14 | 22 | 52 |
| Country S (%) | 7 | 12 | 17 | 23 | 41 |

**(a)** Calculate the cumulative shares of income for Country R, as used to draw a Lorenz curve. **[2]**

**(b)** For each country, calculate the ratio of the richest fifth's share to the poorest fifth's share. **[2]**

**(c)** State which country is likely to have the higher Gini coefficient, and explain your answer. **[2]**

**9.** In Country T, the 10th, 50th and 90th percentiles of income are 8,000 cu, 20,000 cu and 56,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the 90:10 ratio. **[1]**

**(b)** The government pays every household a flat 2,000 cu. Calculate the new 90:10 ratio. **[2]**

**(c)** Instead, every income rises by 10%. State the effect on the 90:10 ratio. **[1]**

**(d)** Show that the median income is the same after either policy. **[1]**

**10.** A small firm pays its nine employees the following weekly wages, in cu: 300, 320, 340, 350, 360, 380, 400, 420 and 2,000.

**(a)** Calculate the mean and the median wage. **[2]**

**(b)** The firm advertises that "the average wage here is over 540 cu a week". Assess whether this is a fair description of typical pay. **[2]**

**11.** The mean of ten incomes is 50 thousand cu. An eleventh income of 160 thousand cu is added. Calculate the new mean. **[2]**

**12.** Country U has a GDP of 450 cu billion and a population of 15 million. Its median household income is 18,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate GDP per capita. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why GDP per capita may overstate the living standards of a typical person in Country U. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Mean = 168/7 = **24 cu** [1]. Median = 4th value = **18 cu** [1]. Total: 2

**2.** Total income = 4,000 × 35,000 + 6,000 × 25,000 = 290 million cu [1]. Mean = 290,000,000/10,000 = **29,000 cu** [1]. Total: 2

**3.** Total revenue = 120 × 2.50 + 80 × 3.00 = 540 cu [1]. Mean price = 540/200 = **2.70 cu** [1]. Total: 2

**4.** **Country Q**, because its Gini coefficient is closer to 1 [1]. Total: 1

**5.** Wealth is highly skewed by a small number of very wealthy households, which pull the mean up, while the median shows the middle household [1]. Total: 1

**6. (a)** Midpoints 5, 15, 25, 40 and 75 [1]. Σ(midpoint × frequency) = 75 + 450 + 750 + 600 + 750 = 2,625 [1]. Mean = 2,625/100 = **26.25 thousand cu** [1].

**(b)** Median position = 50; cumulative frequencies are 15, 45, 75, 90, 100, so the median lies in the 20 to 30 class [1]. Median = 20 + (50 − 45)/30 × 10 [1] = **21.67 thousand cu** [1].

**(c)** The mean is above the median, so the distribution is positively skewed by the high-income classes [1]. Total: 7

**7. (a)** Q1 at position 25: 10 + (25 − 15)/30 × 10 = **13.33** [1]. Q3 at position 75: the cumulative frequency reaches 75 exactly at the end of the 20 to 30 class, so **Q3 = 30** [1]. IQR = 30 − 13.33 = **16.67 thousand cu** [1].

**(b)** 10th percentile at position 10: 0 + (10/15) × 10 = **6.67** [1]. 90th percentile at position 90: the end of the 30 to 50 class, **50** [1]. 90:10 ratio = 50/6.67 = **7.5** [1]. Total: 6

**8. (a)** **4%, 12%, 26%, 48%, 100%** [2] (one mark for at least three correct values).

**(b)** Country R: 52/4 = **13** [1]. Country S: 41/7 = **5.9** [1].

**(c)** **Country R** [1]: its richest fifth takes a larger share and its poorest fifths smaller shares, so its Lorenz curve lies further from the line of equality [1]. Total: 6

**9. (a)** 56,000/8,000 = **7** [1].

**(b)** New percentiles 10,000 and 58,000 [1], so the ratio is 58,000/10,000 = **5.8** [1].

**(c)** **No change**: both percentiles rise by 10%, so the ratio stays 7 [1].

**(d)** Flat payment: 20,000 + 2,000 = 22,000 cu. 10% rise: 20,000 × 1.1 = 22,000 cu. Both give **22,000 cu** [1]. Total: 5

**10. (a)** Mean = 4,870/9 = **541.11 cu** [1]. Median = 5th value = **360 cu** [1].

**(b)** The claim is accurate for the mean, but the mean is pulled up by one wage of 2,000 cu [1]. Eight of the nine employees earn 420 cu or less, so the median of 360 cu is a fairer description of typical pay [1]. Total: 4

**11.** Total of the ten = 10 × 50 = 500, new total = 660 [1]. New mean = 660/11 = **60 thousand cu** [1]. Total: 2

**12. (a)** 450,000,000,000/15,000,000 = **30,000 cu** [1].

**(b)** GDP per capita is a mean, which is raised by very high incomes, so it exceeds the median of 18,000 cu [1]; income may be unequally distributed, so most people receive less than the mean [1]; and part of GDP goes to profits paid abroad, to investment or to government, not to household incomes [1]. Total: 4

## Where marks are usually lost

- Averaging the two town means in Question 2 to get 30,000 cu.
- Using the midpoint of the middle class as the median instead of interpolating.
- Forgetting to build the cumulative frequency column before finding quantiles.
- Writing quintile shares as people rather than income.
- Saying "the mean is wrong" instead of explaining why it is unrepresentative.
- Trying to calculate a Gini coefficient when the question only asks you to compare or interpret.
