This guide covers **Exercise 1 of Paper 2 Listening**, the short answer questions, in Cambridge IGCSE
Urdu as a Second Language (0539). The site's
[Paper 2 Listening guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening/) introduces all four
listening exercises. This page goes further on the first one: the kinds of short spoken texts used, the
details that are tested, the Urdu numbers and time expressions that are easiest to mishear, and a routine
for using the two hearings.

## What the syllabus says

- Candidates hear **a series of short spoken texts**; the syllabus's examples are a **travel
  announcement, a recorded phone message and a brief dialogue**.
- They write **short answers to eight questions**, for **8 marks**.
- The exercise assesses **L1**, identify and select relevant information, and **L2**, understand ideas,
  opinions and attitudes.
- As with every Paper 2 exercise, the material is heard **twice**, with pauses on the recording to read
  the questions and write answers. Questions and answers are in Urdu, and dictionaries are not allowed.

Exercise 1 is the most direct exercise on the paper. It is mostly about catching a stated fact, such as
a time, place, number, day, name or reason, and writing it down briefly and clearly. That makes it the
exercise where careful technique most reliably turns into marks.

## The kinds of text you will hear

The syllabus's Listening content mentions sources such as "a recorded phone message, news or weather
report, travel broadcast, interview, dialogue". Each short text type has a familiar shape, and knowing
the shape tells you where the information will come.

| Text type | Typical opening | Details usually given |
|---|---|---|
| Announcement (اعلان) | مسافر توجہ فرمائیں ... | destination, time, platform or gate, a change, an instruction |
| Recorded phone message (پیغام) | السلام علیکم، میں ... بات کر رہی ہوں | who is calling, the reason, a new arrangement, what to bring or do, when to call back |
| Brief dialogue (مکالمہ) | a question from one speaker | a plan, a time, who will do what, a reason |
| Weather or travel report | آج ... کا امکان ہے | places, temperatures, warnings, advice |

## What the questions ask for

The question word in Urdu tells you the **form** of the answer before you hear anything. Learn these
so that you recognise them instantly in the pause before the recording:

| Question word | Asks for | Answer form |
|---|---|---|
| کب؟ / کس دن؟ | when, which day | a day or date |
| کتنے بجے؟ / کس وقت؟ | at what time | a clock time |
| کہاں؟ | where | a place, often with پر / میں / سے |
| کتنے؟ / کتنی؟ | how many, how much | a number or amount |
| کون؟ / کن لوگوں کو؟ | who, which people | a person or group |
| کیا؟ | what | an object or action |
| کیوں؟ | why | a reason, often heard after کیونکہ or کی وجہ سے |

Answers are short, often a single word or phrase, and need not be full sentences. Write enough to be
unambiguous: if the question is کہاں؟ and the speaker says پلیٹ فارم نمبر دو, write the whole phrase,
not just "دو".

## Numbers: the biggest single trap

Urdu numbers from 1 to 100 are largely irregular, and many pairs sound alike at natural speed. These are
worth drilling until they are automatic:

| Easily confused | Meaning | What to listen for |
|---|---|---|
| سترہ / ستر | 17 / 70 | the final ہ sound of the teen number |
| اٹھارہ / اسی | 18 / 80 | different words, but both are quick and soft at speed |
| اٹھائیس / اڑتیس / اڑتالیس | 28 / 38 / 48 | the ending (-ائیس, -تیس, -تالیس) decides the ten |
| انتیس / انتالیس / انچاس | 29 / 39 / 49 | an ان- start means "one less than" the next ten |
| پچیس / پچاس / پچپن | 25 / 50 / 55 | all three begin پچ- |

Write numbers as figures in your answer if that is quicker: 38 is safer than trying to spell اڑتیس
under time pressure, as long as the figure is right.

## Times: fractions you must know

Spoken Urdu often gives times with fraction words rather than minutes:

| Expression | Time |
|---|---|
| سوا سات بجے | 7:15 |
| ساڑھے سات بجے | 7:30 |
| پونے آٹھ بجے | 7:45 (a quarter *to* eight) |
| ڈیڑھ بجے | 1:30 |
| ڈھائی بجے | 2:30 |

پونے is the trap: پونے آٹھ means 7:45, not 8:45. Listen too for صبح، دوپہر، سہ پہر، شام and رات, which
tell you the part of the day and may be what the question needs.

Days of the week cause a similar problem. **جمعرات** is Thursday and **جمعہ** is Friday; **ہفتہ** is
Saturday but also means "week". **پیر** and **سوموار** are both Monday.

## Changed and corrected details

Announcements and messages love a change of plan, and the question nearly always asks for the **new**
detail. Listen for the words that signal it:

- **کے بجائے** (instead of): گاڑی دس بجے کے بجائے گیارہ بجے روانہ ہوگی۔ The new time is گیارہ.
- **اب** (now): ملاقات اب جمعہ کو ہوگی۔
- **معاف کیجیے / میرا مطلب ہے** (sorry / I mean), when a speaker corrects themselves.
- **پہلے ... تھا، لیکن** (it was ..., but): the detail after لیکن is the current one.

If the question asks for the **original** arrangement (اصل میں، پہلے), it is testing whether you can
keep both facts apart. Note both in the margin as old and new.

## A routine for the two hearings

```
In the pause:   read all the questions for the text; circle the question word;
                predict the form (time? place? number?)
First hearing:  note key words and figures only, beside each question
                in the order you expect them (answers follow the text's order)
Second hearing: confirm, and listen specifically for what you missed;
                check any change signalled by کے بجائے / اب / لیکن
After:          write each short answer clearly; never leave a blank
```

## Worked example

The text below is original, written for this guide. Ask someone to read it to you twice before you look
at the analysis.

> "کراچی سے اسلام آباد جانے والی پرواز کے مسافر توجہ فرمائیں۔ یہ پرواز اب گیٹ نمبر سات کے بجائے گیٹ
> نمبر نو سے روانہ ہوگی۔ روانگی کا نیا وقت پونے چار بجے ہے۔ مسافروں سے گزارش ہے کہ اپنا بورڈنگ کارڈ
> تیار رکھیں۔"

**Question:** پرواز کس گیٹ سے روانہ ہوگی؟ **[1]**

- *In the pause:* کس گیٹ asks for a gate number. Expect a change.
- *First hearing:* the note reads "7 → 9".
- *Second hearing:* confirm that کے بجائے follows سات, so سات is the old gate.
- *Answer:* **گیٹ نمبر نو** (or 9). The candidate who writes سات has heard the right sentence but taken
  the wrong detail.

**Question:** روانگی کا نیا وقت کیا ہے؟ **[1]**

- *Answer:* **پونے چار بجے** (3:45). Writing 4:45 shows the پونے trap; writing just چار loses the mark
  because the time is incomplete.

**Question:** مسافروں سے کیا کرنے کو کہا گیا ہے؟ **[1]**

- *Answer:* **بورڈنگ کارڈ تیار رکھیں**. This is an instruction, which is where L2 comes in: you are
  picking out what the speaker wants people to do, not a number.

## Common mistakes

- Writing the old detail when a change was announced.
- Mishearing a number, such as اڑتیس for اٹھائیس, or reading پونے as "quarter past".
- Writing half an answer, such as a number with no unit or a time with no part of the day when the
  question needs it.
- Writing long sentences that take time and gain nothing.
- Trying to write final answers during the first hearing and missing the next detail.
- Leaving a blank. A reasonable guess may score; a blank cannot.

## Exam technique summary

Use the pause to predict the answer form from the question word. Note figures and key words on the first
hearing; use the second for checking changes and corrections. Keep answers short but complete, and in
the order of the text. For more on Paper 2 as a whole, see the
[Paper 2 Listening revision notes](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-2-listening-revision-notes/) and
the full [Paper 2 listening practice set](/resources/urdu-paper-2-listening-practice/).
