**HL only.** These notes condense Regional study 6 of the Africa and the Middle East option (IB DP History, first assessment 2028): developments in South Africa, 1867 to 2020. Paper 3 asks for two 15-mark essays from different regional studies. The fuller account is in the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-south-africa/).

## Six inquiry topics at a glance

| Inquiry topic | Key idea |
|---|---|
| Mineral Revolution | Diamonds and gold create an industrial economy, migrant labour and segregation |
| South African War | Gold, imperial power and Boer independence; camps and guerrilla war |
| Apartheid | Segregation hardened into a system after 1948 |
| Resistance | From mass non-violence to armed struggle and Black Consciousness |
| International opposition | Sport, culture, sanctions, the Cold War ends, neighbouring states |
| End of apartheid | Mandela and de Klerk, CODESA, 1994, TRC, continuing problems |

## Mineral Revolution: facts

- Diamonds 1867 (Kimberley region); gold 1886 (Witwatersrand); Rhodes and De Beers.
- Uitlanders in the Transvaal; Kruger; Jameson Raid 1895 to 1896.
- Migrant labour, compounds, pass controls, colour bar.
- Glen Grey Act 1894; Mines and Works Act 1911; Natives Land Act 1913.

## South African War

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Causes | Gold, Uitlander rights, Jameson Raid, Milner's aims |
| Course | Black Week 1899; Roberts and Kitchener; guerrilla phase |
| Methods | Farm burning; concentration camps (about 26,000 Boer women and children died, plus many Africans) |
| End | Treaty of Vereeniging, May 1902 |
| Impact | Union 1910; Boer grievance and Afrikaner nationalism; Africans excluded |

## Apartheid laws

| Type | Laws |
|---|---|
| Pre-1948 segregation | Land Act 1913; Urban Areas Act 1923; 1936 removal of Cape African voters |
| Petty | Prohibition of Mixed Marriages 1949; Immorality Amendment 1950; Separate Amenities 1953 |
| Grand | Population Registration 1950; Group Areas 1950; Bantu Authorities 1951; Bantu Education 1953; Homelands 1959 |
| Control | Suppression of Communism Act 1950; pass laws; detention without trial |

**1948:** D. F. Malan's National Party wins a narrow majority of seats. **Verwoerd:** prime minister 1958 to 1966; homeland policy.

## Resistance timeline

- 1912: ANC founded. 1944: Youth League (Mandela, Sisulu, Tambo).
- 1952: Defiance Campaign. 1955: Freedom Charter. 1956 to 1961: Treason Trial.
- 21 March 1960: Sharpeville (69 killed); ANC and PAC banned.
- 1961: *Umkhonto we Sizwe*. 1963 to 1964: Rivonia Trial.
- 1968: SASO; Biko's Black Consciousness. 1977: Biko dies in custody.
- 16 June 1976: Soweto. 1983: UDF. 1985: COSATU. 1985 to 1986: states of emergency.

**Methods compared**

| Method | Strength | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| Non-violent protest | Mass support, moral authority | Banned, met with force |
| Armed struggle | Kept resistance alive | Small military effect; alienated some allies |
| Black Consciousness | Self-reliance, identity | Suppressed after 1977 |
| Strikes and boycotts | Economic pressure | Costly for participants |

## International opposition

- Sport: Olympic exclusion; the 1981 Springbok tour protests.
- UN arms embargo 1977; US Comprehensive Anti-Apartheid Act 1986.
- Bank loans withdrawn in 1985.
- End of the Cold War; Angola and Namibia settlement 1988 to 1990.
- Frontline States and the OAU supported the ANC; South Africa destabilised neighbours.

## End of apartheid

- 2 February 1990: de Klerk announces unbanning; 11 February: Mandela freed.
- 1991 to 1992: CODESA; Boipatong (1992); Hani killed (1993).
- 27 April 1994: elections; ANC about 62 per cent; Mandela president.
- TRC (1995 to 1998): amnesty for disclosure; chair Desmond Tutu.
- Challenges: inequality, unemployment, HIV/AIDS, corruption, land, power cuts.

## Arguments to practise

| Claim | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Apartheid was a break with the past | New scale of legislation after 1948 | Based on earlier segregation |
| Sharpeville was a turning point | Bans and armed struggle followed | Repression strengthened the regime for years |
| Sanctions ended apartheid | Economic strain, isolation | Internal unrest, business pressure, negotiation |
| The TRC was a success | Truth-telling, avoided civil war | Limited justice and reparations |

## Worked example

*Question:* "To what extent was Nelson Mandela responsible for the peaceful transition to democracy?"

- Define "responsible" and compare Mandela with de Klerk, the ANC, business, international pressure and violent opposition.
- For: Mandela's calls for reconciliation, talks from prison, restraint after Hani's death.
- Against: de Klerk's decision to release him; economic strain; Inkatha and right-wing violence; the role of negotiators on all sides.
- Judgement: his leadership was crucial, but the transition needed several actors and conditions.

## Common mistakes

- Mixing up the dates of Sharpeville (1960) and Soweto (1976).
- Ignoring Black Consciousness.
- Saying sanctions alone ended apartheid.
- Writing as if 1994 solved every problem.

## Self-test

1. Which discovery in 1886 transformed Johannesburg?
2. Which treaty ended the South African War?
3. Which law of 1950 divided residential areas by race?
4. What did *Umkhonto we Sizwe* mean to the ANC?
5. What happened on 16 June 1976?
6. Who chaired the TRC?

**Answers:** 1. Gold on the Witwatersrand. 2. Vereeniging, 1902. 3. The Group Areas Act. 4. It was the ANC's armed wing, founded in 1961. 5. Student protest in Soweto against Afrikaans in schools turned into a national uprising. 6. Archbishop Desmond Tutu.

## Key terms

- **Uitlander:** a foreign (mainly British) resident of the Transvaal.
- **Pass laws:** rules requiring Africans to carry documents to move in white areas.
- **Homeland (Bantustan):** a territory set aside for an African ethnic group under apartheid.
- **Black Consciousness:** a movement stressing Black pride and self-reliance.
- **Amnesty:** a pardon granted by the TRC in exchange for truth.

## Frequently asked questions

**How much should I write about the 2000s and 2010s?** Enough to explain "ongoing political and economic challenges", for example inequality and corruption.

**Is a chronological narrative enough?** No. Paper 3 needs argument and judgement.
