Condensed recall for subtopic **3.1 Marketing, competition and the customer**. For worked examples and an annotated recommendation, use the [Marketing, competition and the customer study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-business-7115-marketing-competition-and-the-customer/).

## The role of marketing: four jobs

1. **Identify customer needs**: research, sales data, feedback.
2. **Satisfy customer needs**: right product, price, place and time.
3. **Maintain customer loyalty**: rewards, consistent quality, after-sales care.
4. **Build customer relationships**: personal contact, responding to feedback.

Result: more sales, repeat customers, recommendations, protection from rivals.

## Why spending patterns change

- Incomes rise or fall
- Tastes and fashions change
- Population changes (age structure, more young or old people)
- New technology replaces older products
- Health and environmental awareness
- Prices of rival or substitute products change

**Why it matters**: unsold stock and falling sales for businesses that ignore change; first-mover gains for those that adapt.

## Why markets are more competitive

- Online selling lets customers buy from anywhere.
- Imports from foreign businesses.
- Cheaper transport and communication.
- New businesses entering easily.
- Customers compare prices and reviews online.

## Responses to change and competition

Match the response to the cause:

| Cause | Possible response |
|---|---|
| Customers want better quality | Improve product quality or design |
| Customers buy on price | Cut costs to lower prices |
| Old product out of date | Develop new products |
| Products similar to rivals' | Branding, loyalty schemes, better service |
| Mass market crowded | Target a niche or a new segment |
| Cause of falling sales unknown | Market research |

## Niche versus mass marketing

| | Mass | Niche |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Most of the market | A small, specific segment |
| Product | Same for everyone | Designed for the segment |
| Benefits | High volume, low unit cost | Less competition, higher prices, loyalty |
| Limitations | Price competition, high promotion costs | Limited sales, risk if niche shrinks, big firms may enter |
| Typical business | Large producer | Small specialist |

## Segmentation

**Methods**: age; socio-economic grouping (income and occupation); location; gender.

**Benefits**:

- products match needs more closely
- promotion aimed precisely, less waste
- prices set to suit each segment
- new or neglected segments identified.

**Recommending a segment**: use the data (share of customers, share of spending, growth), say how products or promotion would change, and give a risk.

## Building a "recommend" answer

1. State your choice (a response, an approach or a segment).
2. Give a fact or figure from the case that supports it.
3. Explain the effect on customers, then on sales or profit.
4. Name one risk or drawback and how it could be reduced.
5. Say briefly why the main alternative is weaker for this business.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Marketing | Identifying customer needs and satisfying them profitably |
| Customer loyalty | Customers choosing to buy repeatedly from the same business |
| Customer relationship | An ongoing connection between business and customer built on contact and trust |
| Spending pattern | The way customers divide their spending between products |
| Mass marketing | Selling the same product to the whole market with one approach |
| Niche marketing | Targeting a small, specific part of the market |
| Market segment | A group of customers with similar characteristics |
| Market segmentation | Dividing a market into segments so products and promotion can be targeted |

## Exam traps

- **Marketing is not just advertising.**
- **Give the effect on spending**, not just the cause.
- **Match the response to the cause**; do not cut prices by default.
- **Niche is not automatically better**: it depends on the business's size, product and capital.
- **Segment with evidence** from the case or data.
- **Loyalty and relationships are different jobs**: loyalty is repeat buying; relationships are the connection that encourages it.

## Self-test

1. State the four parts of the role of marketing.
2. Give two reasons why customer spending patterns may change.
3. Why is it important for a business to respond to changing customer needs?
4. Give two reasons why many markets have become more competitive.
5. A clothes shop faces new online competitors. Suggest one response.
6. Define niche marketing.
7. Give one benefit and one limitation of mass marketing.
8. Why might a small business choose niche marketing?
9. State four ways a market can be segmented.
10. Give two benefits of segmentation.
11. A gym's members are 70% aged 18–35. Which segmentation method is most relevant, and why?
12. How does a loyalty scheme help a business?

## Answers

1. Identifying needs, satisfying needs, maintaining loyalty, building relationships.
2. Any two: income changes; tastes and fashion; population changes; new technology; health awareness; changes in rivals' prices.
3. Otherwise it sells products customers no longer want, so sales fall and rivals gain its customers.
4. Any two: online selling; imports; cheaper transport; new businesses entering; customers comparing prices online.
5. Any one, for example: offer personal styling advice or same-day alterations that online sellers cannot; open an online shop; launch a loyalty scheme.
6. Targeting a small, specific part of the market with a product designed for it.
7. Benefit: high sales volume and low cost per unit. Limitation: strong price competition or high promotion costs.
8. It has limited capital, faces less competition in a niche, and can charge higher prices to loyal customers.
9. Age, socio-economic grouping, location, gender.
10. Any two: products fit needs; promotion less wasteful; prices suit each segment; new segments found.
11. **Age**, because most members fall in one age group, so classes and promotion can target 18–35-year-olds.
12. It rewards repeat purchases, so customers keep buying from the business instead of switching to rivals.
