## Questions

**1.** A resistor at constant temperature has a potential difference of 9.0 V across it and a current of 0.060 A.

**(a)** Calculate its resistance. **[2]**
**(b)** The p.d. is raised to 18 V. Calculate the new current. **[2]**

**2.** Describe the I-V graph for a fixed resistor at constant temperature and state what it shows about the resistance. **[3]**

**3.** The table shows readings for a component.

| p.d. (V) | 2.0 | 4.0 | 6.0 | 8.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| current (A) | 0.10 | 0.16 | 0.20 | 0.23 |

**(a)** Calculate the resistance at each p.d. Give each answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** State whether the component is ohmic and explain your answer. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest what the component could be. **[1]**

**4.** Explain, in terms of ions and electrons, why the I-V graph of a filament lamp is a curve. **[4]**

**5.** A thermistor is connected across a 12 V supply. Its resistance is 1800 Ω at 20 °C and 150 Ω at 70 °C.

**(a)** Calculate the current at each temperature. Give your answers in mA to 2 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** State how the resistance of a thermistor changes as its temperature rises. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how a thermistor can be used in a fire alarm. **[2]**

**6.** An LDR is connected across a 6.0 V supply. In the dark its resistance is 2.0 MΩ. In bright light it is 400 Ω.

**(a)** Calculate the current in the dark (in µA) and in bright light (in mA). **[3]**
**(b)** Explain how an LDR can be used to switch a lamp on when it gets dark. **[3]**

**7.** A diode has 0.90 V across it in the forward direction and carries 45 mA. When 6.0 V is applied in the reverse direction, the current is 2.0 µA.

**(a)** Calculate the forward resistance. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the reverse resistance. **[2]**
**(c)** State how many times larger the reverse resistance is than the forward resistance. **[1]**
**(d)** State what these results show about the direction of current through a diode. **[1]**

**8.** A filament lamp and an LED lamp are each connected to the same p.d. The filament lamp carries 0.45 A and the LED lamp carries 0.054 A.

**(a)** State what an LED does when current flows through it in the forward direction. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate how many times larger the current in the filament lamp is than in the LED lamp. Give your answer to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** State why LEDs are increasingly used for lighting. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why an LED connected the wrong way round does not light. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how you would investigate the V-I characteristics of a filament lamp. Your answer should include the circuit, the measurements and how you would present the results. **[6]**

**10.** A student measures a diode in the forward direction.

| p.d. (V) | 0.60 | 0.70 | 0.75 |
|---|---|---|---|
| current (mA) | 2.0 | 10 | 30 |

**(a)** Calculate the resistance at each p.d. **[3]**
**(b)** Describe the trend in resistance and state what it shows about the diode. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why a protective resistor is placed in series with the diode in this investigation. **[1]**

**11.** Three components were tested. Component A gave 0.040 A at 2.0 V, 0.080 A at 4.0 V and 0.12 A at 6.0 V. Component B gave 0.20 A at 1.0 V, 0.30 A at 2.0 V, 0.40 A at 4.0 V and 0.45 A at 6.0 V. For component C, no current could be measured when 5.0 V was applied in one direction, but it conducted when the connections were reversed.

**(a)** Calculate the resistance of A at each p.d. and state whether A is ohmic. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the resistance of B at 1.0 V and at 6.0 V (2 significant figures). **[2]**
**(c)** Identify B and explain the change in resistance in terms of ions and electrons. **[3]**
**(d)** Identify C and justify your answer. **[2]**

**12.** A student makes four statements. For each, say whether it is correct. If it is wrong, write a correct version.

**(i)** "The current through a thermistor falls as it warms up, if the p.d. stays the same." **[2]**
**(ii)** "An LDR has a higher resistance in bright light than in the dark." **[2]**
**(iii)** "A filament lamp is an ohmic conductor because its graph passes through the origin." **[2]**
**(iv)** "A diode has a very high resistance in the reverse direction." **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** R = V ÷ I [1] = 9.0 ÷ 0.060 = **150 Ω** [1]
**(b)** The resistance stays constant, so I = V ÷ R = 18 ÷ 150 [1] = **0.12 A** (120 mA) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the rearranged equation and the substitution; a bare wrong number is hard to credit.

**2.** The graph is a straight line [1] that passes through the origin [1]. This shows that current is directly proportional to p.d., so the resistance is constant [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "A straight line" alone is not enough; the origin and the link to constant resistance are separate points.

**3. (a)** R = V ÷ I [1]: 2.0 ÷ 0.10 = 20 Ω, 4.0 ÷ 0.16 = 25 Ω, 6.0 ÷ 0.20 = 30 Ω, 8.0 ÷ 0.23 = 35 Ω (all four correct) [1]
**(b)** Not ohmic [1], because the resistance is not constant: it increases as the p.d. and current increase [1]
**(c)** A filament lamp [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Quote values from your table when you explain.

**4.** The current makes the filament hotter [1]. The positive ions in the metal lattice vibrate with a larger amplitude [1]. The moving electrons collide with the ions more often [1]. The resistance increases, so the current rises by less for each extra volt and the graph curves [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Keep the ions and electrons separate: the ions vibrate, the electrons collide with them.

**5. (a)** I = V ÷ R [1]. At 20 °C: 12 ÷ 1800 = 0.0067 A = **6.7 mA** [1]. At 70 °C: 12 ÷ 150 = 0.080 A = **80 mA** [1]
**(b)** The resistance decreases [1]
**(c)** As the temperature rises, the thermistor's resistance falls and the current increases [1]. When the change reaches a set level, the circuit switches the alarm on [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the thermistor to the circuit's response: say what changes (current or p.d.) and what the circuit then does.

**6. (a)** I = V ÷ R [1]. Dark: 6.0 ÷ 2 000 000 = 0.000 003 A = **3.0 µA** [1]. Bright: 6.0 ÷ 400 = 0.015 A = **15 mA** [1]
**(b)** In the dark the LDR has a high resistance [1]. The circuit responds to this change in current or p.d. and switches the lamp on [1]. In daylight the resistance falls and the lamp is switched off [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say what happens to the LDR's resistance in the dark.

**7. (a)** R = 0.90 ÷ 0.045 [1] = **20 Ω** [1]
**(b)** R = 6.0 ÷ 0.000 002 [1] = **3 000 000 Ω (3.0 MΩ)** [1]
**(c)** 3 000 000 ÷ 20 = **150 000 times** [1]
**(d)** Current flows easily in the forward direction and hardly at all in the reverse direction: it flows in one direction only [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Convert mA and µA to A before dividing.

**8. (a)** It emits light [1]
**(b)** 0.45 ÷ 0.054 [1] = **8.3 times** [1]
**(c)** LEDs use a much smaller current than other forms of lighting [1]
**(d)** An LED conducts in the forward direction only, so no current flows the wrong way round and no light is emitted [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two currents with a ratio and a unit-free answer; do not state "uses less power" if the question gives currents.

**9.** Connect the lamp in series with a variable power supply and an ammeter [1]. Connect a voltmeter in parallel across the lamp only [1]. Increase the p.d. in equal steps from zero and record the current at each step, with at least six readings [1]. Reverse the connections and repeat to get readings in the opposite direction [1]. Plot current on the vertical axis against p.d. on the horizontal axis [1] and draw a smooth curve through the points [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A good answer names where each meter goes and gives a range of readings; "measure current and voltage" alone is too general.

**10. (a)** R = V ÷ I [1]. 0.60 ÷ 0.0020 = **300 Ω** [1]. 0.70 ÷ 0.010 = **70 Ω** and 0.75 ÷ 0.030 = **25 Ω** [1]
**(b)** The resistance falls as the p.d. and current increase, so the diode is not ohmic [1]
**(c)** Once the diode conducts, the current rises very steeply, so the resistor limits it and prevents damage [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason for protection (large current can damage the diode); "to protect it" alone often does not say from what.

**11. (a)** 2.0 ÷ 0.040 = 4.0 ÷ 0.080 = 6.0 ÷ 0.12 = **50 Ω** [1]. The resistance is constant, so A is ohmic [1]
**(b)** At 1.0 V: 1.0 ÷ 0.20 = **5.0 Ω** [1]. At 6.0 V: 6.0 ÷ 0.45 = **13 Ω** [1]
**(c)** B is a filament lamp [1]. As the current rises the filament heats up, so the ions vibrate more [1]. The electrons collide with them more often, which raises the resistance [1]
**(d)** C is a diode [1]. It conducts in one direction only: its reverse resistance is very high [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* In a multi-part question, use the figures you calculated in earlier parts in your explanation.

**12. (i)** Wrong [1]. The thermistor's resistance decreases as it warms, so the current increases [1]
**(ii)** Wrong [1]. The LDR's resistance decreases as light intensity increases, so it is lower in bright light [1]
**(iii)** Wrong [1]. Its graph is a curve because its resistance increases with temperature; passing through the origin is not enough [1]
**(iv)** Correct [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A correction must state the right relationship, not just negate the statement; "it is false" alone is unlikely to earn the second point.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using mA, µA, kΩ or MΩ values in V = IR without converting them first.
- Leaving out units, or giving units that do not match the quantity.
- Giving "resistance increases because current increases" without referring to temperature, ions and electrons.
- Saying the electrons vibrate, or that the ions collide with the electrons "less often" or "more slowly".
- Mixing up the thermistor (temperature) and the LDR (light intensity).
- Describing the I-V graph of a lamp or diode as straight.
- Describing the practical with the voltmeter in series, or omitting reversed readings.
