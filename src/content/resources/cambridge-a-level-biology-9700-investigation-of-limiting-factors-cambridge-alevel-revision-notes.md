These notes condense section **13.2 Investigation of limiting factors** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). The content is examined in Paper 4, and the investigations underpin Paper 5 planning and evaluation questions. Full explanations and worked examples are in the [13.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-investigation-of-limiting-factors-cambridge-alevel/); exam-style questions are in the [13.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-investigation-of-limiting-factors-cambridge-alevel-practice/).

## Core recall

### The three named limiting factors

- **Light intensity** → light-dependent stage → supply of ATP and reduced NADP.
- **Carbon dioxide concentration** → fixation by rubisco → supply of GP and TP.
- **Temperature** → enzyme-controlled Calvin cycle → rate rises to an optimum, then falls as enzymes denature.

### The rule

The rate is set by the factor in shortest supply. Raising that factor raises the rate; raising anything else does not.

### Graph routine

1. Rising part of the curve: the factor on the x-axis is limiting.
2. Plateau: another factor is limiting.
3. Compare curves: the factor that differs between a low plateau and a higher plateau was limiting on the low one.
4. Always quote values from the graph.

## Redox indicator investigations (13.2.3)

| Point | Detail |
|---|---|
| Indicators | DCPIP and methylene blue: blue when oxidised, colourless when reduced |
| Why they change colour | They accept electrons emitted by chlorophyll in the light-dependent stage |
| What is measured | Time to decolourise, or fall in absorbance in a colorimeter |
| Rate | 1 ÷ time |
| Light intensity | Lamp distance or light meter |
| Light wavelength | Coloured filters or LEDs at equal intensity |
| Isolation medium | Ice-cold, buffered, same water potential as chloroplasts |
| Controls | Dark tube; no-chloroplast tube; boiled chloroplasts |

**Why each part of the isolation medium matters**

- Cold: slows enzymes that would damage the chloroplasts once cells are broken.
- Buffer: keeps pH constant so proteins are not denatured.
- Matched water potential: no net osmosis, so chloroplasts do not burst or shrink.

## Whole-plant investigations (13.2.4)

- Aquatic plant (for example *Elodea*) in sodium hydrogencarbonate solution, which supplies CO₂.
- Measure oxygen release: bubble counts (quick, unreliable) or gas volume in a capillary tube or gas syringe (better).
- Capillary volume = πr² × bubble length.
- Change one factor at a time; acclimatise for a few minutes after each change.
- Rate measured is a **net** rate: some oxygen is used in respiration, some dissolves.

### Light intensity from distance

```
relative light intensity ∝ 1 ÷ d²
```

At twice the distance, the intensity is a quarter. At three times the distance, it is a ninth.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Limiting factor | The factor in shortest supply, which sets the rate of a process |
| Redox indicator | A dye that changes colour when it gains (reduced) or loses (oxidised) electrons |
| DCPIP | Redox indicator that turns from blue to colourless when reduced |
| Chloroplast suspension | Isolated chloroplasts in cold, buffered, osmotically matched solution |
| Acclimatisation | Time allowed for a plant to adjust to new conditions before measuring |
| Heat shield | Tank of water between lamp and plant that absorbs heat but lets light pass |
| Net rate | Measured gas release, which is photosynthesis minus oxygen used in respiration |

## Exam traps

- DCPIP accepts **electrons**; it does not detect oxygen or glucose.
- "The rate increases because there is more light" is not an explanation. Add: more ATP and reduced NADP for the Calvin cycle.
- High temperature **denatures** enzymes; it does not "kill" them.
- A lamp moved closer also warms the plant. Name the heat shield or LED.
- Plot rate against light intensity (1/d²), not distance, when asked for the effect of light intensity.
- In wavelength experiments, intensity must be equal for each colour, or wavelength is not the only variable.
- A plateau at higher temperature but the same height means temperature was **not** limiting there.
- Bubble counting fails because bubbles differ in size.

## Planning checklist for Paper 5

- Independent variable and at least five values across a sensible range.
- Dependent variable and exactly how it is measured (units).
- At least two controlled variables, each with the method of control.
- A control experiment and what it shows.
- Repeats, means and how anomalies are handled.
- One safety point (for example electrical safety near water, or hot lamps).

## Self-test

1. Name the three limiting factors listed in the syllabus.
2. Which stage of photosynthesis does light intensity limit?
3. Why does the rate fall at high temperatures?
4. What colour is oxidised DCPIP, and what colour is reduced DCPIP?
5. Where do the electrons that reduce DCPIP come from?
6. Give the three properties of the solution used to isolate chloroplasts.
7. Why is a tube without chloroplasts included?
8. A lamp is moved from 12 cm to 36 cm. By what factor does light intensity fall?
9. Why is sodium hydrogencarbonate added to water containing pondweed?
10. A gas bubble is 25 mm long in a capillary of radius 0.5 mm. Calculate its volume.
11. Give one reason why the measured oxygen release underestimates the true rate of photosynthesis.
12. On a graph, two curves for 15 °C and 25 °C level off at the same rate. What does this show?

### Answers

1. Light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration and temperature.
2. The light-dependent stage.
3. Enzymes such as rubisco denature, so fewer active sites can bind substrate.
4. Blue when oxidised; colourless when reduced.
5. From chlorophyll photoactivated in the light-dependent stage.
6. Ice-cold, buffered, and the same water potential as the chloroplasts.
7. To show that the colour change is caused by the chloroplasts, not by light acting on the dye.
8. Distance triples, so intensity falls to one ninth (factor of 9).
9. It supplies carbon dioxide (as hydrogencarbonate ions) so CO₂ does not limit.
10. π × 0.5² × 25 = **19.6 mm³**.
11. Some oxygen is used in the plant's respiration, or dissolves in the water.
12. Temperature is not limiting on that plateau; another factor, such as CO₂ concentration, is.
