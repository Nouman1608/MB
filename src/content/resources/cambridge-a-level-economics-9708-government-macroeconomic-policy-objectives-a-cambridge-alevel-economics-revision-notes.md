Condensed notes for **section 10.1 Government macroeconomic policy objectives** (A Level, Cambridge 9708, 2026–2028). For the full explanation and data examples, use the [macroeconomic objectives study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-economics-9708-government-macroeconomic-policy-objectives-a-cambridge-alevel-economics/). Figures are invented.

## AS versus A Level

| AS (5.1) | A Level (10.1) |
|---|---|
| Price stability, low unemployment, economic growth | Inflation, balance of payments, unemployment, growth, development, sustainability, redistribution of income and wealth |
| Conflicts and trade-offs not required | Links and conflicts follow in 10.2 and 10.3 |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Inflation target | A numerical goal for the rate of increase in a price index |
| Deflation | A sustained fall in the general price level |
| Current account stability | No large, persistent deficit or surplus relative to GDP |
| Natural rate of unemployment | Unemployment that remains when there is no cyclical unemployment |
| Potential growth | Growth in the economy's productive capacity |
| Real GDP per head | Real GDP divided by population |
| Development | Wider improvement in living standards: health, education, freedom, less poverty |
| Sustainability | Meeting present needs without reducing future generations' ability to meet theirs |
| Redistribution | Policies to change the distribution of income (flow) and wealth (stock) |
| Gini coefficient | Measure of inequality from 0 (equal) to 1 (one person has everything) |

## Each objective in one line

| Objective | Sensible target | Why not the extreme |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation | Low, stable, positive | Zero risks deflation; indices may overstate inflation |
| Balance of payments | Stable current account | Small, temporary imbalances are normal |
| Unemployment | Near the natural rate | Frictional unemployment always exists |
| Growth | Steady, close to potential | Faster than capacity causes inflation and import surges |
| Development | Broad gains in health, education, income | GDP alone can miss who benefits |
| Sustainability | Growth without depleting resources or damaging the environment | Unrestrained growth can lower future output and welfare |
| Redistribution | A distribution judged fair by society | What is fair is a value judgement |

## How each objective is judged

| Objective | Indicator to quote | What a strong answer adds |
|---|---|---|
| Inflation | Annual change in a consumer price index | Compare with the target and say whether it is stable |
| Balance of payments | Current account balance as a % of GDP | Say whether it is persistent and how it is financed |
| Unemployment | Unemployment rate | Compare with the natural rate; note long-term unemployment |
| Growth | Real GDP growth | Compare with growth of capacity; adjust for population |
| Development | Real GDP per head plus health and education measures | Ask who shares the gains |
| Sustainability | Emissions, resource use, environmental quality | Compare the growth of emissions with the growth of output |
| Redistribution | Gini coefficient, poverty rates | Separate income (flow) from wealth (stock) |

## Useful calculations

```
Growth per head  = (1 + real GDP growth) / (1 + population growth) - 1
Emissions change = (1 + GDP growth) x (1 + change in emissions per unit GDP) - 1
```

Examples: real GDP +3%, population +1.5% gives per head (1.03 ÷ 1.015) − 1 = 1.5%. GDP +5%, emissions per unit −4% gives emissions 1.05 × 0.96 − 1 = +0.8%.

## Diagnosing priorities from data

1. Compare each indicator with its target or benchmark.
2. Look for a pattern: overheating (high inflation, low unemployment, large deficit) or slump (low inflation, high unemployment, weak growth).
3. Rank the objectives furthest from target first.
4. Check growth per head and inequality for development.
5. Judge with a condition: short run versus long run, level of development, values.

## Why priorities differ

- Low-income countries often rank growth and development highest.
- Countries with very high inflation rank price stability first.
- Countries in recession rank growth and employment first.
- Redistribution and sustainability depend on political and social values.

## Exam traps

- Zero inflation or zero unemployment is not the target.
- Use real, not nominal, growth.
- Adjust for population when discussing living standards.
- Development is broader than growth.
- Redistribution is normative.
- Rank the objectives in context; do not just list seven.

## Self-test

1. Name the seven objectives in section 10.1.
2. Which three objectives appear at AS?
3. Give two reasons why governments aim for low rather than zero inflation.
4. Why is zero unemployment not a realistic aim?
5. Real GDP grows 4% and population grows 2%. Calculate growth in real GDP per head.
6. Define sustainability.
7. Why may rising GDP fail to bring development?
8. Distinguish income from wealth.
9. Why is redistribution a normative objective?
10. Inflation is 9% against a target of 2%, unemployment is below its natural rate and the current account deficit is 7% of GDP. Which objectives are most pressing?
11. GDP grows 6% and emissions per unit of GDP fall 2%. By how much do total emissions change?
12. Give one reason why a low-income country might rank objectives differently from a high-income country.
