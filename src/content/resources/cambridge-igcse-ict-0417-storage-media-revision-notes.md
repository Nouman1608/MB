The storage media row of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417 Topic 3 covers the characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages of magnetic media (hard disks and tape), optical discs (CD, DVD and Blu-ray) and solid-state media, including SD, xD and CFast memory cards.

Condensed for revision. For full explanations and a worked choice of media, use the [storage media study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-media/), then try the [storage media practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-media-practice/). The drives themselves are in the [storage devices revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-storage-devices-revision-notes/).

## Magnetic media

**Hard disks (platters)**

- Rigid platters coated in magnetic material; data in tracks and sectors; both sides used; direct access.
- Uses: inside hard disk drives for operating systems, programs and files.
- For: large capacity, rewritable, low cost per GB. Against: magnetic fields and head crashes damage data; sealed in the drive.

**Magnetic tape**

- Plastic strip with magnetic coating on reels in a cartridge; serial access.
- Uses: backups and archives; data processed in order.
- For: very high capacity, very cheap per GB, portable cartridges, long life in storage. Against: slow to find one file; stretches or breaks; heat, damp and magnets.

## Optical media

All three: plastic disc, reflective layer, one spiral track of **pits and lands** read by a laser; recordable discs have a dye layer changed by the laser.

| Disc | Laser | Approximate single-layer capacity | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| CD | Red | 700 MB | Music, small data |
| DVD | Red (shorter wavelength than CD) | 4.7 GB | Standard-definition films, software |
| Blu-ray | Blue-violet | 25 GB | High-definition films, games, large backups |

| Format | Can the user change the data? |
|---|---|
| ROM | No: read-only, data pressed in at manufacture |
| R | Write once, then read-only |
| RW (Blu-ray: RE) | Yes: erase and rewrite many times |

- For: cheap, light, easy to post; not affected by magnetism; ROM and R protect data from changes.
- Against: lower capacity than hard disks and tape; slower than solid-state; scratches, heat and sunlight; compatibility (Blu-ray needs a Blu-ray drive).

## Solid-state media: memory cards

Flash memory, no moving parts, removable; read by the host device or a card reader.

| Card | Characteristics | Uses | Key advantage | Key disadvantage |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| SD (and microSD) | Small, thin; very widely supported | Cameras, phones, tablets, drones | Compatible with many devices; cheap | Easily lost; can fail without warning |
| xD | Small format for some older cameras | Photos in compatible cameras | Very small and light | Few devices or readers support it; low capacity |
| CFast | Larger, very fast transfer | Professional video and high-end cameras | Records large high-quality video smoothly | Expensive; few devices |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Storage medium | The material on which data is recorded |
| Track | A circular path on a disk (or the spiral on an optical disc) along which data is stored |
| Sector | A section of a track on a magnetic disk |
| Pit and land | Dip and flat area on an optical disc, read by a laser |
| Dual layer | An optical disc with two recording layers for more capacity |
| Read-only | Data can be read but not changed |
| Card reader | Device that reads memory cards for a computer |
| microSD | A smaller version of the SD card for phones and tablets |

## Exam traps

- The disc or card is the **medium**; the drive or reader is the **device**.
- R means **once**; RW means **many times**.
- Blu-ray discs are the **same size** as DVDs; more data comes from the shorter laser wavelength and smaller pits.
- Optical media are **not** affected by magnetic fields; magnetic media are.
- xD and CFast: remember **compatibility** as the main disadvantage.
- Memory cards still need backing up: they can fail or be lost.

## Self-test

1. Define *storage medium*.
2. Describe how data is stored on a magnetic hard disk.
3. Give **two** advantages of magnetic tape for archiving.
4. Give **one** disadvantage of magnetic tape.
5. How does a laser read data from an optical disc?
6. Put CD, DVD and Blu-ray in order of capacity, smallest first.
7. State the difference between a DVD-R and a DVD-RW.
8. Why would a film studio sell films on read-only discs?
9. Give **two** uses of SD cards.
10. Why might a photographer not be able to use an xD card from an old camera on a new laptop?
11. Why does a professional video camera use CFast cards?
12. Give **one** threat to data on optical discs and **one** threat to data on magnetic disks.

## Self-test answers

1. The material that holds the data, such as a disc, tape or memory card.
2. As tiny magnetised areas, in one of two directions, along tracks divided into sectors on the platter surfaces.
3. Any two: very large capacity; very low cost per GB; cartridges portable and long-lasting; can be stored off-site.
4. Serial access makes finding one file slow; or it can stretch or break; or it is affected by heat, damp or magnets.
5. The laser shines on the spiral track and the reflected light differs between pits and lands, giving 1s and 0s.
6. CD, DVD, Blu-ray.
7. DVD-R can be written once; DVD-RW can be erased and rewritten many times.
8. The films cannot be changed or deleted by buyers.
9. Any two: cameras, phones, tablets, drones, games consoles, transferring files.
10. The laptop's card reader may not support the xD format.
11. CFast cards transfer data very fast, so large high-resolution video can be recorded without interruption.
12. Optical: scratches, heat or sunlight. Magnetic: strong magnetic fields or a head crash.
