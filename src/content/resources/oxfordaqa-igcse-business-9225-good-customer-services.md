This guide covers section **3.3.4 Good customer services** of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225), part of Topic 3, Business operations. The specification asks you to understand and analyse the techniques businesses use to give good service, namely **product knowledge, customer engagement (creating a positive experience) and post-sales services** such as user training, help lines and servicing; the **benefits of good customer service** (customer satisfaction and loyalty) and the **costs of poor service** (dissatisfied customers and a poor reputation); and the role of **ICT, including websites, e-commerce and social media**, in the development of customer service. All businesses and figures in this guide are invented.

For the whole topic, see the [Business Operations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-business-operations/). Condensed notes are in the [revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-good-customer-services-revision-notes/) and questions are in the [practice set](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-good-customer-services-practice/).

## Why customer service matters

**Customer service** is the help and support a business gives to customers before, during and after a sale. In many markets, rivals offer similar products at similar prices, so service can be what makes customers choose one business over another. Customers also expect a quick, polite and helpful response, whether they are in a shop, on the phone or online.

## Methods of good customer service

### Product knowledge

Staff who know the products well can answer questions, recommend the right item and warn customers about problems. A sports shop assistant who knows the difference between running shoes can match the shoe to the customer's needs, so the customer is satisfied and less likely to return the item.

How businesses do this: train staff, provide product information, let staff try products and brief them on new lines.

### Customer engagement (creating a positive experience)

Customer engagement means making the customer feel welcome and valued. It includes:

- greeting customers politely and listening to their needs
- being friendly, patient and helpful
- keeping the shop or website clean, tidy and easy to use
- reducing waiting times
- dealing with complaints calmly and fairly
- personal touches such as remembering a regular customer's name.

A positive experience makes customers more likely to buy again and recommend the business to others.

### Post-sales services

The service does not end when the customer pays. After-sales support includes:

- **user training:** showing customers how to use the product, for example a free lesson on a new sewing machine
- **help lines and online help:** phone, chat or email support to solve problems
- **servicing and repairs:** regular maintenance, warranty repairs or replacements.

These services build trust, solve problems before they become complaints and encourage customers to buy again.

| Method | Example | Benefit to the business |
|---|---|---|
| Product knowledge | Training sales staff on every new phone | Customers get the right product, fewer returns |
| Customer engagement | Greeting each customer within a minute | Customers feel valued and return |
| User training | Free class on using a new machine | Fewer misuse problems and complaints |
| Help line | 24-hour phone support | Quick fixes; builds confidence |
| Servicing | Annual car service | Repeat work; longer product life |

## Benefits of good customer service

- **Customer satisfaction:** customers feel that their needs are met.
- **Customer loyalty:** satisfied customers come back, so the business has a stable base of sales.
- **Positive word of mouth:** happy customers tell friends and write good reviews, which is free advertising.
- **Ability to charge higher prices:** some customers will pay more for better service.
- **Fewer complaints and returns:** problems are solved before they escalate.
- **Higher staff morale:** staff enjoy working for a business that is proud of its service.

**Worked example 1: the value of a loyal customer.** A cafe's average customer spends $40 per visit and visits 6 times a year.

```
Annual value of one regular customer = $40 x 6 = $240
```

The cafe has 2,000 regular customers. If poor service causes 10% to leave:

```
Customers lost = 10% x 2,000 = 200
Lost sales     = 200 x $240 = $48,000 a year
```

It costs far more to win a new customer than to keep an existing one, so the real cost of losing them is higher than the lost sales.

## Costs and dangers of poor customer service

- **Dissatisfied customers** stop buying and switch to rivals.
- **A poor reputation:** bad experiences are shared in conversation and on review sites.
- **Lost sales and lower profit.**
- **Extra costs** of dealing with complaints, refunds and replacements.
- **Low staff morale:** staff who must deal with angry customers may feel stressed and leave.
- **Harder to recruit new customers**, who check reviews before they buy.

**Worked example 2: is an after-sales help line worth it?** A company sells 3,000 kitchen machines a year. A help line costs $30,000 a year. It reduces returns by 300 machines, saving $50 each in handling and repair costs. It also keeps 100 customers who would have left, each worth $240 in future purchases.

```
Saving on returns = 300 x $50  = $15,000
Value of retained customers = 100 x $240 = $24,000
Total benefit = $15,000 + $24,000 = $39,000
Net benefit = $39,000 - $30,000 = $9,000 a year
```

The help line pays for itself on these figures. If it only retained 50 customers, the benefit would be $15,000 + $12,000 = $27,000 and the help line would cost $3,000 more than it earns. The decision depends on forecasts.

## How ICT has developed customer service

### Websites

A website provides 24-hour information, product details, order tracking, frequently asked questions and online forms. Customers can find answers without waiting for a person. Advantages: low cost per enquiry and always available. Disadvantages: some customers want to speak to a person, and an out-of-date or hard-to-use site frustrates customers.

### E-commerce

**E-commerce** means buying and selling online. It lets customers shop at any time, compare prices, read reviews, track deliveries and return items more easily. Businesses can use customer data to recommend products. Challenges: late or damaged deliveries, payment security, and customers who cannot see or try the product before buying.

### Social media

Social media lets businesses respond quickly to questions and complaints, share news and gather customer opinions. Advantages: fast two-way contact and the chance to turn a complaint into a good experience when solved quickly in public. Disadvantages: a poorly handled complaint is seen by thousands, and staff must monitor accounts all day.

| ICT tool | Customer service uses | Risks |
|---|---|---|
| Website | Information, FAQs, order tracking, online help | Outdated or hard to use; no personal contact |
| E-commerce | Convenient buying, returns and reviews | Delivery problems; security concerns |
| Social media | Quick replies; feedback; publicity | Public complaints spread quickly; needs constant monitoring |

## Common mistakes

- Saying good service just means "being nice". It also includes product knowledge, quick problem solving and after-sales support.
- Forgetting post-sales services such as user training, help lines and servicing.
- Writing "customers will be happy" without the effect on the business, such as loyalty, repeat sales or reputation.
- Treating ICT as a replacement for people. It adds to personal service but does not always replace it.
- Ignoring the costs of providing good service.
- Not linking the method of service to the type of business: a restaurant, an online shop and a car dealer need different things.

## Exam technique for this section

For **state** and **identify** questions, name the method of service. For **explain** questions, state the method and its effect: "a help line solves customers' problems quickly, which increases satisfaction and loyalty". For **analyse** questions, chain the benefits to sales and profit. For **evaluate** questions, such as whether a business should spend more on customer service, compare the cost with the likely gain in loyalty and reputation, and use the facts in the case to decide.
