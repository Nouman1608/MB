## Section A

**1.** State **two** rules of Section 24 of the Partnership Act 1890 that apply when there is no partnership agreement. **[2]**

**2.** Explain why a partner's salary is shown in the appropriation account and not in the statement of profit or loss. **[2]**

**3.** Partners' capital accounts are 50,000 and 30,000. The agreement allows 6% interest on capital. Calculate the interest for each partner. **[2]**

**4.** Explain the difference between fixed capital accounts and floating (fluctuating) capital accounts. **[3]**

**5.** Three partners have no agreement. One of them lent the firm 8,000. Profit before loan interest was 31,000. Calculate the interest on the loan and the share of profit for each partner. **[4]**

## Section B

**6.** Dalia and Evan are in partnership. Their agreement provides for:

- fixed capital: Dalia 80,000 and Evan 50,000, with interest at 4% a year
- a salary for Evan of 15,000
- interest on drawings at 5% (drawings were Dalia 18,000 and Evan 12,000)
- residual profit or loss shared Dalia 3 : Evan 2

Profit for the year ended 31 March 2026 was 55,200. At 1 April 2025 the current accounts were Dalia 1,800 credit and Evan 600 debit.

**(a)** Prepare the appropriation account for the year ended 31 March 2026. **[8]**

**(b)** Prepare the current accounts of Dalia and Evan for the year. **[6]**

**7.** Explain why partners may agree to charge interest on drawings. **[2]**

**8.** In the following year, profit was only 9,000. Using the terms in Question 6, but ignoring interest on drawings, calculate each partner's total share of the appropriation of profit and explain what the result shows. **[5]**

**9.** Pavel and Quinn share profits 2 : 1. Their capitals are 70,000 and 50,000. On 1 January 2026 Rosa joins. The new profit-sharing ratio is Pavel 2, Quinn 2, Rosa 1. Equipment is revalued from 20,000 to 26,000. Goodwill is valued at two years' purchase of average profits; profits for the last three years were 18,000, 21,000 and 24,000. Rosa pays in cash of 30,000. Goodwill is not kept in the books.

**(a)** Calculate the value of goodwill. **[2]**

**(b)** Prepare the partners' capital accounts after Rosa's admission, showing revaluation, goodwill created and goodwill written off. **[8]**

**10.** Explain why goodwill is created in the old profit-sharing ratio when a new partner joins. **[2]**

**11.** Evaluate whether partners should rely on the Partnership Act 1890 rather than make a written agreement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: profits and losses are shared equally [1]; no partner receives a salary [1]; no interest is paid on capital [1]; no interest is charged on drawings [1]; interest of 5% a year is paid on loans from partners [1].

**2.** A salary rewards a partner who is an owner, not an employee [1]; it is a way of sharing profit, so it is an appropriation of profit and not an expense of the business [1].

**3.** 50,000 × 6% = **3,000** [1]; 30,000 × 6% = **1,800** [1].

**4.** With fixed capital the capital account stays at the agreed amount and a separate current account records profit, salary, interest and drawings [1]. With floating capital there is only a capital account, which changes each year because profit and drawings are recorded in it [1]. Fixed accounts show each partner's investment separately from their earnings and withdrawals [1].

**5.** Interest 8,000 × 5% = **400** [1] (Section 24 default); profit after interest 31,000 − 400 = 30,600 [1]; shared equally 30,600 ÷ 3 [1] = **10,200** each [1].

**6. (a)** Dalia and Evan: Appropriation account for the year ended 31 March 2026

| | Dalia | Evan | Total |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Profit for the year | | | 55,200 |
| Add interest on drawings | 900 | 600 | 1,500 |
| | | | 56,700 |
| Less salary | | 15,000 | (15,000) |
| Less interest on capital | 3,200 | 2,000 | (5,200) |
| **Residual profit** | | | **36,500** |
| Share 3 : 2 | 21,900 | 14,600 | 36,500 |

Interest on drawings 18,000 × 5% = 900 and 12,000 × 5% = 600 [2]; added to profit [1]; salary [1]; interest on capital 3,200 and 2,000 [2]; residual profit 36,500 [1]; shares 21,900 and 14,600 [1].

**(b)** Current accounts

| | Dalia | Evan |
|---|---:|---:|
| Balance b/d | 1,800 credit | 600 debit |
| Salary | | 15,000 |
| Interest on capital | 3,200 | 2,000 |
| Share of residual profit | 21,900 | 14,600 |
| Drawings | (18,000) | (12,000) |
| Interest on drawings | (900) | (600) |
| **Balance c/d** | **8,000 credit** | **18,400 credit** |

Opening balances on the correct sides [1]; credits for salary, interest and profit share [2]; drawings and interest on drawings debited [1]; Dalia **8,000** [1]; Evan **18,400** [1]. Working for Dalia: 1,800 + 3,200 + 21,900 − 18,000 − 900 = 8,000. Working for Evan: −600 + 15,000 + 2,000 + 14,600 − 12,000 − 600 = 18,400.

**7.** To discourage partners from taking out too much cash [1]; to make partners who draw more bear a cost, so the arrangement is fair to those who leave more in the business [1].

**8.** Residual: 9,000 − 15,000 − 5,200 = **−11,200**, a loss [1]. Shared 3 : 2, Dalia −6,720 and Evan −4,480 [1]. Dalia's total: interest 3,200 − 6,720 = **−3,520** [1]. Evan's total: salary 15,000 + interest 2,000 − 4,480 = **12,520** [1]. The result shows that salaries and interest are paid out of profit even when the firm cannot afford them, so the loss reduces what partners receive [1].

**9. (a)** Average profit (18,000 + 21,000 + 24,000) ÷ 3 = 21,000 [1]; goodwill 21,000 × 2 = **42,000** [1].

**(b)** Capital accounts after admission

| | Pavel | Quinn | Rosa |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Balance before | 70,000 | 50,000 | |
| Revaluation gain (2 : 1) | 4,000 | 2,000 | |
| Goodwill created (2 : 1) | 28,000 | 14,000 | |
| Cash introduced | | | 30,000 |
| Goodwill written off (2 : 2 : 1) | (16,800) | (16,800) | (8,400) |
| **Capital after admission** | **85,200** | **49,200** | **21,600** |

Revaluation gain 26,000 − 20,000 = 6,000 shared 2 : 1 [2]; goodwill created in the old ratio, 28,000 and 14,000 [2]; cash introduced by Rosa [1]; goodwill written off in the new ratio, 16,800, 16,800 and 8,400 [2]; closing capitals **85,200**, **49,200** and **21,600** [1]. The total 156,000 equals 70,000 + 50,000 + 6,000 + 30,000.

**10.** Goodwill was built up by the old partners, so they are credited with it according to the profit shares they had [1]; the new partner had no part in creating it [1].

**11.** For relying on the Act: it avoids the cost and time of drawing up a document [1]; equal shares are simple and may suit partners who contribute equally [1].
Against: the Act's rules may be unfair if the partners contribute different amounts of capital or work [1], because it gives no salary or interest on capital [1]; a written agreement reduces disputes, since each partner knows how profit is shared, how drawings are treated and what happens when someone leaves [1].
Judgement: a written agreement is better for most partnerships, especially where the capital or the work is unequal, because it reduces the risk of disagreement [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Adding interest on drawings to the partner's current account instead of deducting it (Question 6).
- Deducting interest on a loan in the appropriation account (Question 5).
- Creating goodwill in the new ratio instead of the old ratio (Question 9).
- Forgetting that a residual loss is shared in the profit-sharing ratio (Question 8).
- Applying the agreed salary when the question says there is no agreement.

## Approaching these questions

Check first whether there is an agreement. If there is not, use Section 24. Prepare the appropriation account in three columns and check that the partner columns add to the total. Roll each current account forward from the opening balance. For goodwill, do three separate steps: revalue, create goodwill in the old ratio, write it off in the new ratio, and check that the total capital agrees with the cash and assets brought in.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Edexcel YAC11 questions from past papers?**
No. They are original questions on outcomes 1.3.9 to 1.3.13, written for revision. Use them alongside the official past papers.

**Is interest on a partner's loan an appropriation?**
No. It is an expense in the statement of profit or loss. Interest on capital is an appropriation.
