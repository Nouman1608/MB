## Core recall

- A progressive wave transfers energy from place to place. The medium is not carried along.
- Each particle oscillates about a fixed equilibrium position at the source frequency.
- Each particle copies its neighbour on the source side, a little later. Points further from the source lag behind.
- The pattern moves forward one wavelength in each period, so c = λ/T = fλ.
- The source fixes the frequency. If the speed changes, the wavelength changes in the same ratio; f and T do not change.
- Particles at a crest or trough are momentarily at rest. Particles passing through equilibrium move fastest.
- Electromagnetic waves travel without a medium; the oscillating quantities are electric and magnetic fields.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to learn | Unit |
|---|---|---|
| Displacement | How far a particle is from its equilibrium position, with a direction | m |
| Amplitude | Largest displacement reached, measured from equilibrium | m |
| Wavelength λ | Distance between two adjacent points oscillating in phase | m |
| Period T | Time taken by one particle to complete one full cycle | s |
| Frequency f | Cycles completed by a particle each second | Hz |
| Wave speed c | Distance travelled by the wave pattern per unit time | m s⁻¹ |
| Phase | The stage a particle has reached in its cycle of oscillation | rad, degrees or cycles |
| Phase difference | How far one oscillation is ahead of or behind another of the same frequency, as a fraction of a cycle or an angle | rad, degrees or cycles |
| In phase | Phase difference of 0, 2π, 4π ... rad; points a whole number of wavelengths apart | — |
| Antiphase | Phase difference of π, 3π ... rad; points an odd number of half-wavelengths apart | — |

## Equations and conversions

| Relationship | Use it when |
|---|---|
| c = fλ | Linking speed, frequency and wavelength |
| f = 1/T | Converting a period from a time graph into a frequency |
| Δφ = 2πΔx/λ | Phase difference from a separation along the wave |
| Δφ = 2πΔt/T | Phase difference from a time shift between two traces |
| 1 cycle = 360° = 2π rad | Changing between the three ways of quoting phase |

Landmarks: 1/4 cycle = 90° = π/2 rad; 1/2 cycle = 180° = π rad; 3/4 cycle = 270° = 3π/2 rad; 1/6 cycle = 60° = π/3 rad.

## Two graphs, two readings

| Graph | Horizontal axis | Gives you | Does not give you |
|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement-distance | Position x / m | Wavelength, amplitude, which points are in phase | Period |
| Displacement-time | Time t / s | Period, amplitude, phase of one particle over time | Wavelength |

To find c you need either both graphs, or a frequency with a wavelength.

## Method: particle direction from a snapshot

1. Note the direction of travel of the wave.
2. Look at the particle just behind yours, on the side the wave is coming from.
3. Your particle moves towards that neighbour's displacement.
4. Check: a particle at a crest or trough has zero velocity at that instant.

## Method: phase difference from two traces

1. Measure the period T on the time axis, using several cycles if possible.
2. Measure the time shift Δt between matching points (two peaks, or two upward zero crossings).
3. Fraction of a cycle = Δt/T. Remove any whole cycles.
4. Convert to degrees (× 360) or radians (× 2π) as asked, and say which trace leads.

## Quick reminders with numbers

- A wave of 15 Hz and λ = 0.80 m has c = 12 m s⁻¹ and T = 0.067 s.
- Points 0.30 m apart on a wave with λ = 1.20 m differ by 0.25 cycle = 90° = π/2 rad.
- Points 1.50 m apart on the same wave differ by 1.25 cycles, which is again π/2 rad.
- A 6.0 Hz wave slowing from 0.30 m s⁻¹ to 0.18 m s⁻¹ changes λ from 5.0 cm to 3.0 cm; T stays 0.17 s.

## Exam traps

- Crest-to-trough distance is λ/2, not λ.
- Crest-to-trough height is twice the amplitude.
- "Speed of the wave" is c, not the speed of a particle.
- A phase difference above 180° may be quoted as a lead the other way: say which point leads.
- Answers such as "phase difference = 0.5" with no unit lose the mark.
- Unit prefixes: ms, μs, kHz, MHz and cm must be converted before substituting.
- Counting crests: n crests in a row span (n − 1) wavelengths.
- "Particles move with the wave" is always wrong for a progressive wave.

## Self-test

1. Define wavelength in terms of phase.
2. A wave has period 4.0 ms. Find its frequency.
3. A sound wave in air has frequency 680 Hz and speed 340 m s⁻¹. Find its wavelength.
4. Five consecutive crests span 2.4 m. Find the wavelength.
5. Two points 0.20 m apart on a wave of wavelength 0.80 m: give their phase difference in degrees and in radians.
6. Two points 2.5λ apart: are they in phase, in antiphase or neither?
7. A particle at a trough: what is its velocity at that instant?
8. Two oscilloscope traces of period 0.50 ms are shifted by 0.125 ms. Find the phase difference as a fraction of a cycle and in radians.
9. Light enters glass and slows down. Which of frequency, wavelength and speed are unchanged?
10. A ripple tank wave has f = 5.0 Hz and λ = 4.0 cm. Find its speed in m s⁻¹.
11. Which graph, displacement-distance or displacement-time, gives the period?
12. A point P is λ/4 further from the source than point Q. Which leads, and by how much?

### Answers

1. The distance between two adjacent points on the wave that oscillate in phase.
2. f = 1/0.0040 = **250 Hz**.
3. λ = 340/680 = **0.50 m**.
4. Five crests span four wavelengths: λ = 2.4/4 = **0.60 m**.
5. 0.20/0.80 = 0.25 cycle = **90°** = **π/2 rad** (1.57 rad).
6. 2.5λ is an odd number of half-wavelengths, so they are in **antiphase**.
7. **Zero**: it is momentarily at rest.
8. 0.125/0.50 = **0.25 cycle** = **π/2 rad**.
9. **Frequency** is unchanged; speed and wavelength both decrease.
10. c = 5.0 × 0.040 = **0.20 m s⁻¹**.
11. The **displacement-time** graph.
12. **Q leads** P by a quarter of a cycle, 90° or π/2 rad.
