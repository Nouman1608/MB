Exercise 2 of **Paper 1 Reading and Writing** in Cambridge IGCSE Urdu as a Second Language (0539) is a
multiple-matching task worth **9 marks**. You read a series of short paragraphs, then a series of
sentences, and match each sentence to the paragraph it fits. It looks simple, but it is one of the most
precise reading tasks on the paper: the sentences almost never repeat the paragraph's words, so you are
tested on whether you recognise the same **meaning** expressed differently. This guide goes deeper on
that one exercise than the
[Paper 1 Reading and Writing guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-urdu-paper-1-reading-writing/).

## What the syllabus says

The 2025-2027 syllabus states that candidates "read a series of short paragraphs followed by a series of
sentences, then match each sentence to one of the paragraphs". The objectives are **R1** (identify and
select relevant information), **R2** (understand ideas, opinions and attitudes) and **R3** (understand
the connections between ideas, opinions and attitudes). Unlike Exercises 1 and 3, **R4** is not listed,
so the answers are stated in the paragraphs rather than hidden; the difficulty lies in recognising them
when they are reworded.

The paragraphs are often written by, or about, several different people on one shared theme (for
example, young people describing a hobby, a job or a favourite place). Each sentence describes one of
them.

**Read the rubric carefully.** In many matching tasks a paragraph can be the answer to more than one
sentence, and some paragraphs may be used more often than others. Do not cross a letter off after one
use unless the instructions say each letter is used once.

## The core skill: recognising paraphrase

The writer of the task deliberately rewords. These are the patterns you will meet most often.

| Pattern | In the paragraph | In the sentence |
|---|---|---|
| Synonym | کام شروع کیا | کام کا آغاز کیا |
| General word for specific ones | فٹ بال اور کرکٹ | کھیل |
| Negative of the opposite | آسان نہیں تھا | مشکل تھا |
| Feeling described indirectly | میرا دل خوش ہو جاتا ہے | اسے خوشی ملتی ہے |
| Change over time | پہلے… مگر اب… | اس کا معمول بدل گیا ہے |
| Someone else's view | میرے دوست کہتے ہیں کہ یہ وقت کا ضیاع ہے | دوسرے اس کے شوق کو اہمیت نہیں دیتے |
| Future intention | میں بڑی ہو کر ڈیزائنر بننا چاہتی ہوں | اس کا مستقبل کا پیشہ |
| Cause stated as result | شور کی وجہ سے گھر میں نہیں پڑھ سکتی | گھر پڑھنے کے لیے مناسب جگہ نہیں |

Building a habit of noticing these relationships is the best preparation. When you read any Urdu text,
pause on a sentence and ask: how else could this be said?

## The traps

**1. The shared word.** A sentence about a band may be designed so that the word بینڈ appears in a
paragraph where the writer's *brother* is in the band. The paragraph with the matching word is often
the wrong answer.

**2. Half a match.** A sentence often has two parts, for example "this person helps an older relative
*and* does it every week". A paragraph that satisfies only one part is not a match. Underline both
parts and check each.

**3. Wanted versus did.** چاہتا تھا (wanted to), ارادہ ہے (intends to) and کیا (did) are different. A
sentence about something the person *has done* does not match a paragraph where they only plan it.

**4. Whose opinion?** میرے خیال میں introduces the writer's view; میری امی کا خیال ہے introduces
someone else's. Sentences frequently turn on this difference.

**5. Time words.** پہلے، اب، اگلے سال، دو سال پہلے and ابھی تک anchor events in time. A sentence such
as "this place will soon be unavailable" needs a future event, not a past one.

## Worked example

Three short paragraphs, written for this guide:

> **A — زینب:** میں ہر ہفتے کو اپنی نانی کے ساتھ بازار جاتی ہوں۔ وہ اکیلی نہیں جا سکتیں کیونکہ ان کی نظر کمزور ہے۔ واپسی پر ہم ایک چھوٹے سے ہوٹل میں چائے پیتے ہیں۔
>
> **B — حمزہ:** ہفتے کا دن میں فٹ بال کی مشق میں گزارتا ہوں۔ میرے کوچ کا خیال ہے کہ میں قومی ٹیم تک پہنچ سکتا ہوں، لیکن مجھے خود اس پر یقین نہیں۔
>
> **C — مریم:** پہلے میں ہفتے کو دیر تک سوتی تھی، مگر اب میں ایک بیکری میں کام کرتی ہوں۔ جو پیسے ملتے ہیں، وہ میں ایک نئے کمپیوٹر کے لیے جمع کر رہی ہوں۔

**Sentence 1:** یہ شخص اپنے خاندان کے ایک بزرگ کی مدد کرتا/کرتی ہے۔

The words بزرگ and مدد appear nowhere. But نانی is an older family member, and "she cannot go alone
because her eyesight is weak" shows that Zainab is helping her. **Answer: A.**

**Sentence 2:** کسی اور کو اس شخص کی صلاحیت پر اس سے زیادہ بھروسا ہے۔

This is a comparison of two opinions. The coach believes Hamza can reach the national team; Hamza does
not believe it himself. Someone else trusts his ability more than he does. **Answer: B.** This is an
R2/R3 match: you need to understand both attitudes and how they relate.

**Sentence 3:** اس شخص کے ہفتے کے دن کا معمول بدل گیا ہے۔

The marker is پہلے… مگر اب… in C. Maryam used to sleep late on Saturdays and now works. **Answer: C.**

**A trap sentence to test yourself:** "یہ شخص ہفتے کو پیسے کماتا/کماتی ہے۔" Paragraph A mentions a
hotel and B describes a demanding Saturday, but only C describes paid work (بیکری میں کام، پیسے ملتے
ہیں). Paragraph B is the tempting wrong answer for anyone matching "busy Saturday" rather than "earns
money".

## A reliable method

1. **Read the sentences first.** For each, underline the key idea in your own words in the margin
   (for example "helps elderly relative", "routine changed"). Note whether it has two parts.
2. **Read each paragraph once for its overall content**, noting who the person is and two or three
   things they say.
3. **Match the easiest sentences first.** Some will be obvious; they reduce the field for the rest.
4. **Find the evidence.** For every match, locate the specific words in the paragraph that justify it.
   If you cannot point to them, you have not found the answer yet.
5. **Check both parts** of every two-part sentence.
6. **Resolve the hard ones by elimination**, but still check the evidence in the paragraph you choose.
7. **Answer every sentence.** There is no penalty for a wrong letter, so never leave a blank.

## Exam technique and timing

Exercise 2 is worth 9 marks, one per sentence, and the paragraphs are short. Allow about **12-15
minutes**. Write the answer letter clearly in the space provided; an unclear letter (a B that could be
a D) wastes a mark you have earned. If you change your mind, cross out cleanly and write the new letter
beside it.

Because every answer is a single letter, this is the one reading exercise where you write no Urdu at
all. All the work happens in reading. Use the margin for your short English or Urdu summaries of each
sentence; they speed up the matching and reduce rereading.

## Common mistakes

- Matching on a shared word instead of on meaning.
- Accepting a paragraph that satisfies only half of a two-part sentence.
- Confusing an intention or a wish with something that actually happened.
- Attributing someone else's opinion (a parent's, a coach's, a friend's) to the writer.
- Assuming each letter is used exactly once when the rubric does not say so.
- Spending too long on one difficult sentence instead of matching the rest first.

## Building the skill before the exam

Paraphrase recognition improves with deliberate practice:

- Collect **synonym pairs** in a notebook as you read (شروع / آغاز، مشکل / دشوار، مدد / تعاون،
  فیصلہ کرنا / طے کرنا).
- Practise writing **one sentence that summarises a whole paragraph** in different words; this is
  exactly what the task setter does.
- Learn **feeling and attitude words** (خوش، مطمئن، پریشان، مایوس، حیران، شکر گزار), because sentences
  frequently describe a feeling the paragraph shows without naming.
- Learn **time and change markers** (پہلے، اب، شروع میں، آخرکار، آئندہ، ارادہ).
