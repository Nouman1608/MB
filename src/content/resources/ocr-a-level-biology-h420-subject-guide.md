OCR A Level Biology A (H420) is a linear course: the content is taught over the full
course and everything is examined at the end, in three written papers. Paper 1 (Biological processes) and Paper 2 (Biological diversity) each last
2 hours 15 minutes, carry 100 marks and are worth 37%; Paper 3 (Unified Biology) lasts
1 hour 30 minutes, carries 70 marks and is worth 26%. Together they make up the 270
marks that decide the A* to E grade. Alongside the papers, students complete the
Practical Endorsement, which is reported separately. This page explains how the six
modules are arranged and how they relate to the papers.

## How the course is organised

The specification is divided into six modules.

**Module 1: Development of practical skills in biology.** This module is not taught as a
block. It describes the practical skills assessed in the written examinations and those
assessed through the Practical Endorsement, and it runs through the whole course.

**Module 2: Foundations in biology.** Cell structure, biological molecules, nucleotides
and nucleic acids, enzymes, biological membranes, and cell division, cell diversity and
cellular organisation. This is the base on which every later module rests.

**Module 3: Exchange and transport.** Exchange surfaces, transport in animals and
transport in plants.

**Module 4: Biodiversity, evolution and disease.** Communicable diseases, disease
prevention and the immune system; biodiversity; and classification and evolution.

**Module 5: Communication, homeostasis and energy.** Communication and homeostasis,
excretion as an example of homeostatic control, neuronal and hormonal communication,
plant and animal responses, photosynthesis and respiration.

**Module 6: Genetics, evolution and ecosystems.** Cellular control, patterns of
inheritance, manipulating genomes, cloning and biotechnology, ecosystems, and
populations and sustainability.

Modules 2 to 4 are usually taught first, and Modules 5 and 6 build on them.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Time | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| H420/01 | Paper 1: Biological processes | 2 hours 15 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H420/02 | Paper 2: Biological diversity | 2 hours 15 minutes | 100 | 37% |
| H420/03 | Paper 3: Unified Biology | 1 hour 30 minutes | 70 | 26% |

The qualification is linear and untiered: all three papers are sat at the end of the
course by every candidate. Paper 3 is synoptic and draws on all the modules, so it
rewards students who can combine ideas from different parts of the specification in a
single answer. The exam preparation page sets out which modules Papers 1 and 2 draw on.

The Practical Endorsement is a separate, internally assessed element based on a minimum
of 12 practical activities. It is reported as Pass or Not Classified and does not add
any marks to the 270 that determine the grade. It is still part of the course, and the
practical work behind it also feeds the written papers.

## How the parts fit together

H420 is designed so that ideas introduced early return later in more demanding forms.
Biological molecules and enzymes from Module 2 reappear in photosynthesis and
respiration in Module 5 and in cellular control in Module 6. Membranes underpin
exchange surfaces in Module 3, nerve impulses in Module 5 and the immune response in
Module 4. Classification and evolution in Module 4 connect directly to patterns of
inheritance, populations and sustainability in Module 6.

Practical skills tie the modules together. Module 1 is assessed in the written papers as
well as through the endorsement, so questions on planning, measurement, data analysis
and evaluation can appear anywhere. Mathematical skills, such as handling data, rates
and statistics, also run through every module. These threads are exactly what the
synoptic Paper 3 is built to test.

## Planning your study

A sensible order is to follow the module numbers: Module 2 first, then Modules 3 and 4,
then Modules 5 and 6, with Module 1's practical skills developed throughout. A typical
pattern across the course is:

- **First year:** Modules 2, 3 and 4, together with a good share of the practical
  activities for the endorsement.
- **Second year:** Modules 5 and 6, the remaining practical activities, and then
  revision across all six modules.

Because nothing is examined until the end, the main risk is forgetting first-year
content. Revisit earlier modules regularly, for example with short weekly recall
practice, rather than leaving all of Module 2 until the final revision period. Keep a
log of your practical activities and what each one taught you about method and
uncertainty. Start past paper questions topic by topic during the first year, move to
full papers in the second, and use the exam preparation page for paper-specific
technique.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in OCR A Level Biology A?**
There are three written papers: Paper 1 (Biological processes) and Paper 2 (Biological diversity), each 100 marks and 37%, and Paper 3 (Unified Biology), 70 marks and 26%.

**How long are the H420 papers?**
Papers 1 and 2 each last 2 hours 15 minutes. Paper 3 lasts 1 hour 30 minutes.

**What is Paper 3 Unified Biology?**
It is a synoptic paper that draws on all the modules of the specification, so questions can combine content from different parts of the course.

**Does the Practical Endorsement count towards the A Level grade?**
No. It is internally assessed, based on a minimum of 12 practical activities, and reported separately as Pass or Not Classified. It adds no marks to the 270-mark total.

**Is OCR A Level Biology A linear?**
Yes. All three papers are taken at the end of the course, and the qualification is not tiered.
