## Definitions to know

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Standard of living | How well off people are in the goods, services and conditions they enjoy |
| Indicator | A figure used to stand in for living standards |
| GDP | Worth of every final good and service a country produces in a year |
| Nominal GDP | GDP at current prices |
| Real GDP | GDP at constant prices, so only output changes move it |
| Real GDP per head | Real GDP shared across the population (real GDP ÷ population) |
| HDI | Index from 0 to 1 built from health, education and income |
| Income distribution | How total income is shared among households or groups |

## Formulas

| Item | Formula |
|---|---|
| Real GDP | Nominal GDP ÷ price index × 100 |
| Real GDP per head | Real GDP ÷ population |
| Percentage change | (new − old) ÷ old × 100 |
| Average income of a fifth | Share of income × average income ÷ 20% |
| Richest-to-poorest ratio | Share of richest fifth ÷ share of poorest fifth |

### Method: did living standards rise?

1. Convert nominal figures to real figures using the price index.
2. Divide each real GDP by that year's population.
3. Find the percentage change in real GDP per head.
4. Compare it with population growth. If population grows faster than real GDP, output per head falls.
5. Add a limit: an average does not show how income is shared.

### Small worked reminder

Real GDP growth is 3.0% and population growth is 3.5%. Real GDP per head changes by 1.030 ÷ 1.035 − 1 = −0.48% (about −0.5%). The country grew, but the average person did not gain.

## Real GDP per head

- **Components of GDP**: consumption, investment, government spending, exports minus imports. Real GDP is the same total at constant prices.
- **For**: measured regularly, easy to compare, allows for prices and population, usually goes with more spending on health and education.
- **Against**: an average; leaves out hidden and unpaid output; counts harmful output as a gain; ignores pollution, leisure, health and education directly; prices differ between countries.

## HDI

| Component | Measured by (typical) |
|---|---|
| Health | Life expectancy at birth |
| Education | Years of schooling |
| Income | Income per head |

- Scored from 0 to 1. Higher means higher human development.
- **For**: wider than income alone, one figure, shows when income is not turned into health and education, helps rank countries.
- **Against**: only three components, leaves out inequality, poverty, freedom and the environment, an average, equal weights are a judgement, data quality varies, ranks often follow income.

### Must-know distinctions

| Real GDP per head | HDI |
|---|---|
| Income only | Health, education and income |
| Money value | Index from 0 to 1 |
| Measures output per person | Measures wider human development |
| Can rise while health and schooling stay poor | Can be high even when income is modest |

| Nominal | Real |
|---|---|
| Current prices | Prices held fixed |
| Inflation lifts it | Only output lifts it |
| Misleading over time | Used for comparing over time |

## Income distribution and comparing countries

- **Average income** can be the same in two countries yet shared very differently.
- Use shares: compare the richest and poorest fifths, or the poorest 40%.
- Example: shares of 6, 10, 15, 22 and 47 (sum 100). The richest fifth has 47 ÷ 6 = 7.83 times the poorest fifth's share. The poorest 40% have 6 + 10 = 16%.

### Reasons for differences between countries

| Reason | Link to living standards |
|---|---|
| Productivity | More output per worker, higher incomes |
| Saving and investment | More capital, higher productivity |
| Education | Skilled workers, higher pay |
| Health care | Longer, healthier working lives |
| Population growth | Faster than output lowers real GDP per head |
| Sector structure | Secondary and tertiary sectors tend to pay more than primary |
| Resources, trade, stability | Raise or limit output and income |

Poverty can trap a country: little saving means little investment, so productivity and income stay low. Better education and health can start an upward spiral instead.

### Reasons for differences within a country

- Skills and wages: skilled workers earn more.
- Asset ownership: owners receive profit, rent and interest.
- Unemployment, illness, age.
- Region: cities and rural areas differ.
- Taxes and benefits: progressive taxes and benefits narrow gaps.

## Worked reminders

### Reading HDI scores (illustrative)

Country R scores 0.60 for health, 0.55 for education and 0.85 for income, giving an HDI of 0.655. Country S scores 0.85, 0.80 and 0.70, giving 0.781. R has the higher income score but S has the higher HDI. Write it as a chain: S converts its income into long lives and schooling, so people can live and work better, so S ranks higher on human development although R is richer on income alone.

### Same average, different sharing (illustrative)

Country K has average income of 20,000 and the shares above. The poorest fifth averages 6,000 and the richest fifth averages 47% × 20,000 ÷ 20% = 47,000. Comparing the two groups gives 47,000 ÷ 6,000 = 7.83, the same ratio as the shares. A government could narrow this gap with progressive taxes, benefits for low-income households, or better education for the poor.

## Evaluation toolkit

Use this for questions that ask "assess", "evaluate" or "to what extent".

1. **Judge the indicator**: say what it measures and what it ignores.
2. **Use the data**: quote a figure from the source and compare it with another.
3. **Weigh up**: real GDP per head shows output, the HDI shows wider outcomes, and distribution data show who gets what.
4. **Conclude**: choose a verdict and give the reason, for example "the HDI gives a fuller picture here because income alone hides the low schooling score".

### Phrases that link ideas

- "This means that average income rises, but it does not show whether the poorest gain."
- "Because population grew faster than output, real GDP per head fell."
- "This raises productivity, which raises incomes, which allows more saving and investment."

## Likely question types

| Command word | What to do |
|---|---|
| Define | Give a short, accurate meaning, as in the table above |
| Calculate | Write the formula, put the numbers in, give the answer with units |
| State | Give the fact; no explanation needed |
| Explain | Give a reason and its consequence |
| Compare | Use the same measure for both countries and give a figure for each |
| Assess or evaluate | Give both sides and a supported judgement |

### Quick checks before you write

- Is the figure real or nominal? Convert before you compare years.
- Is it per head? Divide by that year's population.
- Does the question ask about a country's average, or about its poorest households? The two can differ greatly.
- Have you named the indicator, the number and the limit in the same answer?

## Quick self-test

1. Nominal GDP is 396 and the price index is 110. Find real GDP.
2. Real GDP is 270 billion and the population is 9 million. Find real GDP per head.
3. Name the three HDI components.
4. Give one reason why GDP per head may understate living standards.
5. Average income per head is 20,000. The poorest fifth has 6% of income. Find the average income of that fifth.
6. State one reason why a country with a higher real GDP per head could have a lower HDI than another.
7. Nominal income per head is 15,000 and the price index is 120. Find real income per head.
8. Real GDP rises 2% and population rises 0.5%. Find the change in real GDP per head.
9. Explain one reason why education raises living standards.
10. Give one reason for income differences within a country.

### Answers

1. 396 ÷ 110 × 100 = 360.
2. 270 billion ÷ 9 million = 30,000.
3. Health, education and income.
4. It leaves out unpaid and hidden output, or it ignores unrecorded activity such as subsistence farming.
5. 6% × 20,000 ÷ 20% = 6,000.
6. Its extra income may not be spent on health care and schooling, so its health and education scores are low.
7. 15,000 ÷ 120 × 100 = 12,500.
8. 1.02 ÷ 1.005 − 1 = 1.49%, so about 1.5%.
9. Education raises workers' skills and productivity, so output per worker and incomes rise, which allows more spending on goods and services.
10. Any one of: skills, asset ownership, unemployment, illness, age, region, taxes and benefits.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Showing growth with nominal figures.
- Dividing by the wrong year's population.
- Saying GDP per head tells you how income is shared.
- Listing HDI components as "GDP, health and education".
- Giving advantages and disadvantages with no use of the context.
- Writing a list of reasons with no link to living standards.
- Reaching a verdict that ignores the evidence given.
- Forgetting units or the word "per head".

## Frequently asked questions

**What is real GDP per head?**
Real GDP per head is real GDP divided by the population. It is an average, showing what each person would receive if output were shared equally.

**What are the three components of the HDI?**
The Human Development Index combines health (life expectancy), education (years of schooling) and income per head into one figure between 0 and 1.

**Why do living standards differ within a country?**
Differences come from wages and skills, ownership of assets such as land and shares, unemployment, age and illness, region, and the effect of taxes and benefits.

**Does GDP per head show how income is shared?**
No. Two countries can have the same average income while income is far more equal in one of them, so GDP per head is a poor guide to how most people live.
