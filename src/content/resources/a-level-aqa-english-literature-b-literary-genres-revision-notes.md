## Paper 1 at a glance

2 hours 30 minutes, closed book, **75 marks = 40% of the A-level**.

| Question | Focus | Marks |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Passage-based, Shakespeare | 25 |
| 2 | Essay, Shakespeare | 25 |
| 3 | Essay linking two further texts | 25 |

Choose **one** option: Aspects of Tragedy or Aspects of Comedy.

## The genre-led mindset

This is not "three separate texts" — it's **one genre, explored through several texts**. Every
answer should engage with the genre's conventions (tragic hero, hamartia, catharsis for tragedy;
comic resolution, misunderstanding, social correction for comedy) and how each text **uses or
departs from** them.

## The three questions

- **Q1 (passage-based, Shakespeare):** close analysis of a set extract, connected to the genre.
- **Q2 (essay, Shakespeare):** a genre-focused essay on the whole play.
- **Q3 (linking essay):** connects two further texts (from drama, prose, poetry) within the genre option.

## Building genre debate

Don't just identify genre features ("this is a tragic hero because...") — **debate** them: does
the text conform to, subvert, or complicate the convention? AQA's AOs explicitly reward this kind
of critical debate over simple feature-spotting.

## Exam traps

- Writing about texts in isolation instead of exploring how they relate to the genre and each other.
- Feature-spotting ("this is dramatic irony") without debating what it contributes to the genre reading.
- Ignoring the passage in Q1 and writing a general essay instead of close, extract-based analysis.
- Forgetting AQA has a revised specification for exams from 2027 with a refreshed text list — always confirm which version applies.

## Self-test

1. How many questions make up Paper 1, and what does each focus on?
2. What are the two genre options, and how many do you study?
3. What distinguishes a strong genre-led answer from a text-by-text summary?
4. What proportion of the A-level is Paper 1 worth?

**Answers:** 1. Three: a passage-based Shakespeare question, a Shakespeare essay, and an essay linking two further texts within the genre option. 2. Aspects of Tragedy and Aspects of Comedy — you study one. 3. It treats the genre as a set of contested conventions and debates how each text uses, departs from or complicates them, rather than summarising each text's plot and features separately. 4. 40% (75 of the total marks available across the qualification's components).
