These notes condense section **3.2.3.1 Trends in properties** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Explanations and worked examples are in the [Trends in properties study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-trends-in-properties/), and exam-style questions are in the [Trends in properties practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-trends-in-properties-practice/). The wider topic is in the [Group 7(17), the halogens revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-group-7-17-the-halogens-revision-notes/).

## What you must be able to do

- Explain the trends in **electronegativity** and **boiling point**.
- Describe the trend in **oxidising ability** of halogens, with **displacement reactions**.
- Describe the trend in **reducing ability** of halide ions, with **concentrated sulfuric acid**.
- Explain the **silver nitrate** test and the use of **ammonia**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Electronegativity | Ability of an atom to attract the bonding pair in a covalent bond |
| London forces | Weak forces between molecules caused by temporary dipoles |
| Oxidising agent | Takes electrons (is reduced) |
| Reducing agent | Gives electrons (is oxidised) |
| Displacement | A more reactive halogen takes the place of a less reactive halide |

## Physical trends

| Halogen | Appearance | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| F₂ | Pale yellow gas | –188 |
| Cl₂ | Pale green gas | –34 |
| Br₂ | Red-brown liquid | 59 |
| I₂ | Grey-black solid | 184 |

- **Electronegativity decreases down the group:** bigger atoms, more shielding, bonding pair further from the nucleus.
- **Boiling point increases down the group:** more electrons per molecule, stronger London forces, more energy to separate the molecules. Covalent bonds do not break.

## Oxidising ability decreases down the group

Displacement:

| Mixture | Observation | Equation |
|---|---|---|
| Cl₂(aq) + Br⁻ | Orange | Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ |
| Cl₂(aq) + I⁻ | Brown | Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂ |
| Br₂(aq) + I⁻ | Brown | Br₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Br⁻ + I₂ |
| Br₂(aq) + Cl⁻ | No reaction | |
| I₂(aq) + Cl⁻ or Br⁻ | No reaction | |

In hexane: Br₂ orange-red, I₂ purple.

## Reducing ability of halide ions increases down the group

Solid sodium halides with concentrated sulfuric acid:

| Halide | Products | Observations | S oxidation state change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl⁻ | HCl | Steamy fumes | None |
| Br⁻ | HBr, Br₂, SO₂ | Steamy fumes, orange-brown Br₂, SO₂ | +6 to +4 |
| I⁻ | HI, I₂, SO₂, S, H₂S | Purple fumes or black solid, yellow solid, rotten-egg smell | +6 to +4, 0 and –2 |

Equations:

```
NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl
NaBr + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HBr
2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O
NaI + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HI
2HI + H₂SO₄ → I₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O
6HI + H₂SO₄ → 3I₂ + S + 4H₂O
8HI + H₂SO₄ → 4I₂ + H₂S + 4H₂O
```

Reason: larger ions lose the outer electron more easily (further from the nucleus, more shielded).

## Silver nitrate test for halide ions

Add dilute nitric acid, then silver nitrate solution.

| Ion | Precipitate | Dilute NH₃ | Concentrated NH₃ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cl⁻ | White AgCl | Dissolves | Dissolves |
| Br⁻ | Cream AgBr | Insoluble | Dissolves |
| I⁻ | Yellow AgI | Insoluble | Insoluble |

- Equation: Ag⁺(aq) + X⁻(aq) → AgX(s).
- **Silver nitrate** supplies Ag⁺ and is soluble.
- **Nitric acid** removes carbonate and hydroxide ions, which would give false precipitates. Hydrochloric acid would add Cl⁻; sulfuric acid would form silver sulfate.
- **Ammonia** distinguishes the three: AgCl + 2NH₃ → [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + Cl⁻.

## Worked example

25.0 cm³ of 0.100 mol dm⁻³ NaCl with excess AgNO₃ (Mᵣ of AgCl = 143.4):

```
n(AgCl) = 0.100 × 0.0250 = 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol
mass = 2.50 × 10⁻³ × 143.4 = 0.359 g
```

## Observations to learn

| Test | Observation |
|---|---|
| Cl₂(aq) + KBr(aq) | Colourless to orange |
| Cl₂(aq) + KI(aq) | Colourless to brown |
| Br₂(aq) + KI(aq) | Orange to brown |
| NaCl(s) + conc. H₂SO₄ | Steamy fumes |
| NaBr(s) + conc. H₂SO₄ | Steamy fumes, orange-brown vapour, choking gas |
| NaI(s) + conc. H₂SO₄ | Purple fumes or black solid, yellow solid, rotten-egg smell |
| AgNO₃ with chloride | White precipitate |
| AgNO₃ with bromide | Cream precipitate |
| AgNO₃ with iodide | Yellow precipitate |

## Explaining the reducing-ability trend

1. Going down the group, the halide ion gets larger.
2. The outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded.
3. It is lost more easily, so the halide ion is a stronger reducing agent.
4. Sulfuric acid is reduced further by the stronger reducing agent: not at all by Cl⁻, to SO₂ by Br⁻, and to SO₂, S and H₂S by I⁻.

## Exam traps

- Cream AgBr described as white or yellow.
- Covalent bonds breaking when halogens boil.
- "Chloride reduces sulfuric acid."
- Leaving out the HX stage before the redox stage.
- Using HCl or H₂SO₄ to acidify the test.
- Not stating the reason for acidifying (carbonate, hydroxide).
- Dilute and concentrated ammonia results mixed up.
- Writing "Cl2 + Br" for the equation: ions must be balanced.

## Self-test

1. Why does electronegativity decrease down the group?
2. Why does boiling point increase down the group?
3. State the colour change when chlorine water is added to potassium bromide solution.
4. Write the ionic equation for chlorine and iodide ions.
5. What is observed when bromine water is added to sodium chloride solution?
6. What is observed when solid NaCl is warmed with concentrated H₂SO₄?
7. Name the sulfur-containing product when NaBr reacts with concentrated H₂SO₄.
8. State the change in oxidation state of sulfur when H₂S is formed from H₂SO₄.
9. Why is silver nitrate acidified with nitric acid?
10. What colour is silver bromide?
11. Which silver halides dissolve in concentrated ammonia?
12. What is the formula of the complex ion formed when AgCl dissolves in ammonia?

### Answers

1. Larger atoms, more shielding; the bonding pair is further from the nucleus and attracted less.
2. More electrons, stronger London forces, more energy needed.
3. Colourless to orange.
4. Cl₂ + 2I⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + I₂.
5. No change (no reaction).
6. Steamy fumes of HCl.
7. Sulfur dioxide, SO₂.
8. +6 to –2.
9. To remove carbonate and hydroxide ions, which would also precipitate with Ag⁺.
10. Cream.
11. Silver chloride and silver bromide.
12. [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺.
