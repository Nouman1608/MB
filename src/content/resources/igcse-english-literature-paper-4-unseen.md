This guide explains how **Paper 4 Unseen** works in Cambridge IGCSE
Literature in English 0475 for the 2026 series: what makes it different
from every other component in the qualification, its format and marks,
and how to build the transferable reading skills it actually rewards,
since there is no set text to revise.

## Where this fits in 0475

The 0475 syllabus is organised around papers rather than a topic list.
Every candidate takes Paper 1 Poetry and Prose, and then completes the
qualification through one of three routes: Paper 2 Drama, OR Paper 3
Drama (Open Text) together with Paper 4 Unseen, OR Paper 3 together
with Component 5 Coursework. Paper 4 is therefore only relevant to
candidates on the Paper 3 + Paper 4 route — it does not exist as an
option for schools that enter candidates for Paper 2. Together, Paper 3
(25%) and Paper 4 (25%) make up the same 50% of the qualification that
Paper 2 alone carries on the single-exam drama route, so for candidates
who do sit it, Paper 4 is worth exactly as much as an entire set text
studied over months of lessons — which is worth remembering when a
paper with no set text to learn can feel, wrongly, like the lower-stakes
half of the course.

What makes Paper 4 structurally unusual is that it tests no prior
content knowledge at all. Every other component in 0475 rewards
detailed, text-specific knowledge built up over a course of study; Paper
4 instead rewards the reading and analysis skills a literature student
should be able to apply to any poem or prose passage put in front of
them, seen for the first time in the exam room.

## Syllabus coverage

**CAMBRIDGE IGCSE LITERATURE IN ENGLISH 0475 — PAPER 4 UNSEEN (2026)**

- Format — a written paper of 1 hour 15 minutes carrying 25 marks,
externally set and marked by Cambridge International, worth 25% of the
qualification for candidates on the Paper 3 + Paper 4 route.
- Task structure — candidates answer one question from a choice of two.
Both questions carry equal marks (25 marks each), so the choice is about
which text plays to your strengths, not about relative difficulty.
- The poetry/prose choice — one question is based on a passage of
literary prose, such as an extract from a novel or short story; the
other is based on a poem or an extract from a poem. Both the poem and
the prose extract are printed in full on the question paper itself, so
nothing needs to be memorised or brought into the exam room.
- The critical-commentary task — both questions require candidates to
write a critical commentary on the printed poem or prose extract and to
demonstrate an appreciation of the text, rather than answering a
narrower essay question of the kind set on Papers 1 to 3.
- No set texts — unlike Papers 1, 2 and 3, Paper 4 has no set text list
in Section 3 of the syllabus. The material is genuinely unseen: neither
the school nor the candidate can know in advance which poem or passage
will appear.
- Planning time — the syllabus explicitly recommends that candidates
spend around 20 minutes reading the two questions and planning their
answer to the one they choose, before starting to write, out of the 75
minutes available.
- Assessment objectives — as with every other paper in 0475, all four
AOs are tested and weighted equally at 25% each: AO1 knowledge of the
text's content shown through detailed reference and quotation; AO2
understanding of meanings, characters, relationships, situations and
themes; AO3 understanding of the writer's intentions and methods,
including how language, structure and form are used to create effects;
and AO4 a sensitive, informed personal response, communicated either
directly (in response to a question asking "what do you think?") or by
implication (in response to a question asking candidates to "explore"
an aspect of the text).
- Command word — "Explore" is the command word Cambridge lists for this
syllabus, meaning candidates should write in detail about particular
aspects of the extract rather than attempting a full summary of
everything in it.

## How to approach it

Because nothing here can be pre-learned, preparation for Paper 4 is
about rehearsing a repeatable process rather than memorising content.
Practise on genuinely unfamiliar poems and prose extracts regularly
throughout the course, not just in the final weeks before the exam —
the goal is to make close reading under time pressure feel routine
rather than to build knowledge of any particular text. Use the 20-minute
planning window Cambridge itself recommends: read the extract twice,
annotate it for language, structure, form, tone and shifts in mood or
perspective, and only then decide which of the two options (poem or
prose) plays to your strengths before committing to one, since switching
part-way through wastes time neither the paper nor the candidate can
afford. Resist the temptation to write a running summary of what happens
in the extract; examiners consistently reward selective, well-supported
analysis of how specific words, images or structural choices create
effects, over a comprehensive but unanalytical retelling. Personal
response (AO4) is credited explicitly and equally alongside the other
three objectives, so a commentary that identifies a technique but never
says what effect it has on the reader — or what the candidate makes of
it — is leaving marks on the table even when the technical
identification itself is accurate. Finally, since the choice between
poem and prose is genuinely open on the day, candidates should have
practised both formats during the course rather than assuming in
advance which they will pick; a candidate who has only ever practised
unseen poetry can be caught out by a prose extract that, on the day,
would actually have suited them better.

## Frequently asked questions

**How long is Paper 4 Unseen?**
1 hour 15 minutes for one question from a choice of two (a prose passage or a poem), worth 25 marks
and 25% of the qualification.

**How long should I plan for?**
The 0475 syllabus advises spending around 20 minutes reading the questions and planning your answer
before you start to write.

**Can I revise for an unseen paper?**
Yes, by practising close reading: comment on language, structure and effect in unfamiliar poems and
prose, and give a clear personal response supported by quotation.


## A note on syllabus currency

This guide is written against the Cambridge IGCSE Literature in
English (0475) syllabus for the 2026 examination series. Cambridge has
since published a 2027 syllabus, and a further restructure applies
from the 2028-2030 cycle: the current four-paper-plus-coursework
structure is replaced by a two-component route (Paper 1 Poetry and
Prose, now open text, plus either Paper 2 Drama and Unseen or a
50-mark coursework component), so Papers 3 and 4 and the coursework component as described
across this family will no longer exist in that form. Confirm which
syllabus cycle your candidates are sitting before revising from this
guide.
