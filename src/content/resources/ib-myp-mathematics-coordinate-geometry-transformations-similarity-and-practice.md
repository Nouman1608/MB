## Questions

**1.** Write down the gradient and y-intercept of 7x + 3y = 21. **[2]**

**2.** A line passes through (2, −3) and (6, 5).

**(a)** Find the equation of the line, giving it as y = mx + c. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the equation of the line parallel to it that passes through (1, 4). **[2]**

**3.** M is the point (−2, 3) and N is the point (3, 15).

**(a)** Calculate the length MN. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the midpoint of MN. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the gradient of MN. **[1]**
**(d)** K(3, 1) is the midpoint of AB, where A is (−1, 4). Find the coordinates of B. **[2]**

**4.** (Extended) The points are P(1, 1), Q(5, −1), R(6, 1) and S(2, 3).

**(a)** Find the gradients of PQ, QR, RS and SP. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why PQRS is a rectangle. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that PQRS is not a square. **[2]**

**5.** **(a)** How many lines of symmetry does a rectangle that is not a square have, and what is its order of rotational symmetry? **[2]**
**(b)** Find the image of A(2, −3) in the y-axis, of B(−4, 5) in the line y = x, and of C(5, 2) in the line x = 3. **[3]**
**(c)** A point (2, 5) is reflected to (2, −1). Give the equation of the mirror line. **[1]**

**6.** Point A is (−2, 5) and C is (3, 1).

**(a)** Rotate A by 90° anticlockwise about C and give the image. **[3]**
**(b)** Rotate A by 180° about C and give the image. **[1]**

**7.** In triangle ABC, AB = AC. D is the point on BC where AD is perpendicular to BC. Prove that triangle ABD is congruent to triangle ACD, and state what this shows about D. **[4]**

**8.** **(a)** Triangle ABC has AB = 8, BC = 12 and CA = 10. Triangle DEF is similar to it, with DE corresponding to AB and DE = 6. Find EF and FD. **[3]**
**(b)** A person 1.6 m tall casts a shadow 2.5 m long. A flagpole casts a shadow 11.25 m long at the same moment. Find the height of the flagpole and state one assumption you made. **[3]**

**9.** **(a) (Extended)** Enlarge P(3, 2) with centre (2, 1) and scale factor 3. **[2]**
**(b) (Extended)** Enlarge Q(5, 9) with centre (−1, 3) and scale factor 1/3. **[3]**
**(c) (Extended)** An enlargement with centre the origin maps A(4, 6) to A′(−2, −3). Find the scale factor. **[2]**

**10.** (Extended) Show that reflecting in the line y = x and then in the x-axis is the same as a rotation of 90° clockwise about the origin. Test it on (3, 5), then show it for a general point (a, b). **[4]**

**11.** **(a)** Show that regular dodecagons (12 sides) cannot tessellate on their own. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that a regular dodecagon, a regular hexagon and a square can fit together round a point. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 3y = −7x + 21 [1], so y = −(7/3)x + 7. The gradient is **−7/3** and the y-intercept is **7** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Rearranging first and stating both values clearly is the safest way to show the reading is deliberate; quoting 7x/3 as the gradient, or the intercept as a point, blurs the answer.

**2. (a)** m = (5 − (−3))/(6 − 2) = 8/4 = 2 [1]. Using (2, −3): −3 = 4 + c [1], so c = −7 and the line is **y = 2x − 7** [1].
**(b)** Parallel lines have the same gradient, m = 2 [1]. Then 4 = 2 + c, so c = 2 and **y = 2x + 2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Checking the second point in your equation takes seconds and catches sign slips; show the substitution rather than just quoting c.

**3. (a)** The differences are 5 and 12, so MN² = 25 + 144 = 169 [1], MN = **13** [1].
**(b)** Midpoint = ((−2 + 3)/2, (3 + 15)/2) = **(0.5, 9)** [1].
**(c)** Gradient = 12/5 = **2.4** [1].
**(d)** (−1 + x)/2 = 3 gives x = 7 [1]; (4 + y)/2 = 1 gives y = −2 [1]. B is **(7, −2)**.
*Examiner insight:* Work in the middle: B is the reflection of A through K, so adding the same step from K that A is from K is a quick check.

**4. (a)** PQ: (−1 − 1)/(5 − 1) = −1/2; RS: (3 − 1)/(2 − 6) = −1/2 [1]. QR: (1 − (−1))/(6 − 5) = 2; SP: (1 − 3)/(1 − 2) = 2 [1].
**(b)** Opposite sides have equal gradients, so they are parallel and PQRS is a parallelogram [1]. Adjacent gradients multiply to (−1/2) × 2 = −1, so there is a right angle; a parallelogram with a right angle is a **rectangle** [1].
**(c)** PQ = √(16 + 4) = 2√5 and QR = √(1 + 4) = √5 [1]. Adjacent sides are unequal, so it is not a square [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the product of gradients as evidence for a right angle; saying it "looks" perpendicular rarely persuades. Lengths decide square versus rectangle.

**5. (a)** **2** lines of symmetry [1] and rotational symmetry of order **2** [1].
**(b)** A′ = **(−2, −3)** [1]. B′ = **(5, −4)** [1]. C is 2 right of x = 3, so C′ is 2 left: **(1, 2)** [1].
**(c)** The x-coordinate is unchanged and the y-coordinates are 5 and −1, so the mirror is halfway: **y = 2** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A diagonal of a non-square rectangle is not a line of symmetry; this is the usual trap in part (a). For part (c) give the equation, not just the number 2.

**6. (a)** Relative to C: A − C = (−5, 4) [1]. Rotate 90° anticlockwise with (x, y) → (−y, x): (−4, −5) [1]. Add C: **(−1, −4)** [1].
**(b)** (−5, 4) → (5, −4); add C: **(8, −3)** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show the subtract, rotate, add steps. A single wrong sign gives a point that looks reasonable, and only visible working lets a slip be pinpointed.

**7.** Angle ADB = angle ADC = 90° [1]. AB = AC (given), and these are the hypotenuses [1]. AD is common to both [1]. So the triangles are congruent by RHS, and therefore BD = CD: **D is the midpoint of BC** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Write each statement with its reason and state which condition you use. Stopping at "so they are congruent" leaves the final inference, that BD = CD, unproven.

**8. (a)** k = 6/8 = 3/4 [1]. EF = 12 × 3/4 = **9** [1]; FD = 10 × 3/4 = **7.5** [1].
**(b)** Height ÷ shadow = 1.6/2.5 = 0.64 [1], so the flagpole is 0.64 × 11.25 = **7.2 m** [1]. Assumption: the ground is level and both the person and the flagpole are upright, so the sun's angle gives similar triangles [1].
*Examiner insight:* Match corresponding sides in order before dividing. For a real-life context, a one-line justification of the model is part of an answer that makes sense in context.

**9. (a)** P − C = (1, 1) [1]; times 3 and add C: **(5, 4)** [1].
**(b)** Q − C = (6, 6) [1]; times 1/3 gives (2, 2) [1]; add C: **(1, 5)** [1].
**(c)** Ratio of coordinates: −2/4 = −1/2 and −3/6 = −1/2 [1], so **k = −1/2** (the image lies across the centre from A) [1].
*Examiner insight:* A smaller image still counts as an enlargement. Quote a negative factor when the image is on the other side of the centre; a positive 1/2 would be a different point.

**10.** Take (3, 5). In y = x it becomes (5, 3) [1]. In the x-axis it becomes (5, −3) [1]. A 90° clockwise turn sends (x, y) to (y, −x), so (3, 5) goes to (5, −3), the same point [1]. In general (a, b) → (b, a) → (b, −a), and the clockwise rule gives (b, −a) [1].
*Examiner insight:* One test point is evidence, not proof. The general (a, b) step is what turns the check into a justification.

**11. (a)** Interior angle = 180° − 360°/12 = 150° [1]. 360 ÷ 150 = 2.4 is not a whole number, so copies cannot fill the angle round a point [1].
**(b)** The hexagon has interior angle 120° and the square has 90° [1]. 150 + 120 + 90 = **360**, so they fit with no gap [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give the angle sum and say it equals 360°; saying the shapes "fit nicely" does not show a tessellation.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Reversing the subtraction order on only one of the two differences in a gradient.
- Giving only the gradient when the question asks for the equation.
- Using the distance formula without squaring both differences before adding.
- Checking sides only before calling a quadrilateral a rectangle or square.
- Forgetting that perpendicular gradients need a flip and a sign change (extended).
- Rotating about the origin when a different centre is given, or ignoring the direction.
- Reflecting in x = k by changing only the sign of the x-coordinate.
- Starting a congruence proof with the conclusion, or not stating the condition used.
- Matching the wrong sides in similar triangles.
- Describing a tessellation without the 360° argument.
