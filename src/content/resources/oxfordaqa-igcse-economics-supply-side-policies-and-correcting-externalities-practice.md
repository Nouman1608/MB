These questions cover sections 3.2.3.3 (Supply-side policies) and 3.2.3.4 (Policies to correct positive and negative externalities) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Economics (9214) specification, first teaching September 2023, first examined May/June 2025. The course is not tiered. All countries, firms and figures are invented. ## Questions

**1.** What is meant by a supply-side policy? **[2]**

**2.** In Country A a worker earns 2,500 a month before tax. The income tax rate falls from 28% to 24%. Calculate the percentage increase in the worker's take-home pay. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[3]**

**3.** Explain how a cut in taxes on business profits could lead to higher economic growth. **[4]**

**4.** Country B pays for 150 workers to be trained, at 2,000 each. Output per worker rises from 40 to 46 units a day.

**(a)** Calculate the total cost of the training. **[1]**
**(b)** By what percentage has output per worker risen? **[2]**
**(c)** Each worker works 220 days a year. Calculate the extra output per trained worker each year. **[2]**

**5.** A government plans to privatise a state-owned rail company. Explain one advantage and one disadvantage of this policy. **[4]**

**6.** City C removes the limit on the number of taxi licences. Explain how this could lower fares, and suggest one problem it could cause. **[6]**

**7.** In a market for fertiliser, demand is `Qd = 120 - 4P` and supply is `Qs = 4P - 40`, with price P per tonne and quantity Q in tonnes. Each tonne causes 6 of external cost through river pollution. The government taxes producers 6 per tonne.

**(a)** Work out the price and quantity before any tax. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the price and quantity once the tax is in place. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the tax revenue. **[1]**
**(d)** State how much more buyers pay and how much less sellers receive per tonne. **[2]**

**8.** Cycling to work reduces traffic and gives an external benefit of 4 per bicycle. Demand is `Qd = 150 - 5P` and supply is `Qs = 5P - 50`, with Q the number of bicycles bought each month. The government gives sellers a subsidy of 4 per bicycle.

**(a)** Find the market price and quantity with no subsidy. **[2]**
**(b)** Find what buyers pay, and the quantity bought, once the subsidy is paid. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the cost of the subsidy. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the subsidy moves the market closer to the best level of output for society. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how a legal limit on factory emissions can correct a negative externality, and give one drawback. **[4]**

**10.** Country B has high unemployment because many workers lack the skills firms need. Evaluate whether investment in education and training or a cut in income tax would be the better supply-side policy. **[9]**

**11.** A government puts a tax on a good that causes pollution. Demand for the good is inelastic, with a price elasticity of demand of -0.4. The tax raises the price by 10%. Evaluate how effective the tax is likely to be in reducing the externality. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** A policy that aims to raise the economy's productive capacity [1] by improving efficiency, skills, incentives or competition, shifting long-run supply to the right [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition earns credit for saying what the policy changes, which is capacity or costs, not just for naming an example.

**2.** Take-home before: `2,500 × 0.72 = 1,800` [1]. Take-home after: `2,500 × 0.76 = 1,900` [1]. Percentage rise: `100 / 1,800 × 100` = **5.6%** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the original take-home pay, not the new one, and show both figures so the examiner can see your method.

**3.** Firms keep more of each unit of profit [1]. This makes it worth spending on machinery or new premises [1]. More capital per worker raises productivity and capacity [1]. Higher potential output allows output, and so growth, to rise [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Spell out every step. "Firms invest more so growth rises" jumps over two of them.

**4. (a)** `150 × 2,000 =` **300,000** [1].
**(b)** `(46 - 40) / 40 × 100` [1] = **15%** [1].
**(c)** Extra units per day = 6 [1]. `6 × 220 =` **1,320 units** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Percentage change is on the starting value, 40. Giving the answer with its unit helps in part (c).

**5.** Advantage: private owners seek profit, so they cut waste and costs [1], which may lead to lower prices or better service [1]. Disadvantage: the firm may become a private monopoly [1] and raise fares, or cut unprofitable lines and jobs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each side needs a developed point. A bare "efficiency" or "job losses" with no explanation earns little.

**6.** Without the limit more taxi firms and drivers can enter [1]. They compete for passengers [1], so fares fall [1]. Problem: more taxis can cause congestion or lower driver incomes [1], and with fewer rules, safety or vehicle standards may slip [1], so some passengers may be worse off [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the context. Answers that discuss taxis, fares and licences earn more than a general account of deregulation.

**7. (a)** `120 - 4P = 4P - 40` gives `8P = 160` [1]. **P = 20, Q = 40** [1].
**(b)** The tax makes supply `Qs = 4(P - 6) - 40 = 4P - 64` [1]. `120 - 4P = 4P - 64` gives `8P = 184` [1]. **P = 23, Q = 28** [1].
**(c)** `6 × 28 =` **168** [1].
**(d)** Buyers pay `23 - 20 =` **3 more** [1]. Sellers receive `23 - 6 = 17`, which is **3 less** than 20 [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Sellers receive the new price minus the tax. A common slip is to say sellers receive 23.

**8. (a)** `150 - 5P = 5P - 50` gives `10P = 200` [1]. **P = 20, Q = 50** [1].
**(b)** With the subsidy, supply is `5(P + 4) - 50 = 5P - 30` [1]. `150 - 5P = 5P - 30` gives **P = 18, Q = 60** [1].
**(c)** `4 × 60 =` **240** [1].
**(d)** The good has an external benefit, so the market makes too few bicycles [1]. The subsidy lowers cost, so more are bought and output rises towards the level that counts the benefit to others [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* A subsidy shifts supply, not demand. Say "the supply curve shifts right" with the reason.

**9.** A limit stops firms emitting above a set level [1], so output or pollution falls towards the socially best level [1]. Drawback: inspectors are needed to check and punish breaches [1], and compliance may raise costs, which can mean job losses or higher prices [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A drawback must be developed. "It is expensive" is stronger as "monitoring needs inspectors, which costs the government".

**10.** Training matches the cause: unemployment is due to a skills gap [1]. Better skills raise productivity and make workers employable [1], which cuts structural unemployment and raises output [1]. An income tax cut raises take-home pay, which may bring more people into work [1], but it does little for people who lack the skills firms need [1]. Training is costly and slow [1], and a tax cut loses government revenue [1]. A tax cut tends to help high earners more, which may widen inequality [1]. Judgement: training is better for this problem because it tackles the cause, though it takes years, so a tax cut or other measure might be needed alongside [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer ties the choice to the context, here a skills gap, and ends with a clear judgement and a reason.

**11.** The tax raises the price by 10%, so quantity falls by `-0.4 × 10 = -4%` [1]. That is a small fall, so the pollution falls by little [1]. Demand is inelastic, so buyers keep buying and tax revenue is high [1], which could fund clean-up [1]. At an elasticity of -1.5 the same rise would cut quantity by `1.5 × 10 = 15%` [1]. Judgement: the tax raises money but does little to cut pollution, so a limit may be needed alongside [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figure given. Calculating the 4% fall shows why the tax may be weak.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the new figure, not the starting one, as the divisor in a percentage change.
- Saying sellers receive the price buyers pay after a tax.
- Shifting demand instead of supply when showing a tax or subsidy.
- Naming a policy without a chain to the objective.
- Giving only one side in an "evaluate" question.
- Ignoring the time lag of training and education.
- Not using the context in the question.
- Calling any harm an externality when it falls on the buyer or seller.
