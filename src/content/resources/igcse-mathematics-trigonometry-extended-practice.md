This set covers the Extended content on the sine and cosine rules, the area formula, bearings, three-dimensional problems, graphs and equations of trigonometric functions, exact values and chords of a circle. Give lengths to 3 significant figures and angles to 1 decimal place unless a question says otherwise. Work out each question on paper before you read the answer, and keep full calculator values until the last step.

## Questions

**1.** In triangle PQR, PQ = 8.5 cm, PR = 11.2 cm and angle QPR = 47°. Calculate the length of QR. **[3]**

**2.** In triangle ABC, AB = 9 cm, BC = 14 cm and angle BAC = 72°. Calculate angle ACB. **[3]**

**3.** A cuboid is 12 cm long, 5 cm wide and 4 cm high. A straight line joins a corner of the base to the corner of the top face that is furthest away from it, passing through the inside of the cuboid.

**(a)** Calculate the length of this line. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the angle between this line and the base of the cuboid. **[2]**

**4.** Solve the equation 4 cos x + 3 = 0 for 0° ⩽ x ⩽ 360°. **[3]**

**5.** Without using a calculator, find the exact value of sin 60° × tan 30° + (cos 45°)². Show your working. **[2]**

**6.** A circle has centre O and radius 10 cm. AB is a chord of the circle of length 16 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the perpendicular distance from O to the chord AB. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate angle AOB. **[2]**

**7.** Triangle XYZ has XY = 9.5 cm, XZ = 8.2 cm and area 34 cm². Angle YXZ is acute. Calculate angle YXZ. **[3]**

**8.** A boat leaves port P and sails 12 km on a bearing of 040° to buoy Q. It then sails 9 km on a bearing of 115° to buoy R.

**(a)** Show that angle PQR = 105°. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance PR. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the bearing of R from P. **[3]**

**9.** **(a)** Solve 5 tan x − 4 = 2 for 0° ⩽ x ⩽ 360°. **[3]**

**(b)** Solve 2 sin x + 1 = 0 for 0° ⩽ x ⩽ 360°. **[2]**

**10.** The graph of y = cos x is drawn for 0° ⩽ x ⩽ 360°.

**(a)** Write down the value of x, in this range, at which y has its minimum value. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the number of solutions of the equation cos x = 0.4 in this range. **[1]**

**(c)** Solve cos x = 0.4 for 0° ⩽ x ⩽ 360°. **[2]**

**11.** A solid pyramid has a square base ABCD of side 8 cm. The vertex V is directly above the centre of the base, at a height of 10 cm.

**(a)** Calculate the length of the diagonal AC. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the length of the sloping edge VA. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the angle between VA and the base. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate angle AVB. **[3]**

**12.** In triangle ABC, AB = 6 cm, AC = 10 cm and BC = 14 cm.

**(a)** Show that angle BAC = 120°. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the exact area of the triangle, in the form k√3 cm². **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the shortest distance from A to the line BC. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate angle ACB. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Cosine rule: QR² = 8.5² + 11.2² − 2 × 8.5 × 11.2 × cos 47° [1]. QR² = 72.25 + 125.44 − 129.85… = 67.84… [1]. QR = **8.24 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Work out the whole term 2 × 8.5 × 11.2 × cos 47° before you subtract. Writing 72.25 + 125.44 − 190.4 and then multiplying by cos 47° is a common slip that loses the last two marks.

**2.** Sine rule: sin C / 9 = sin 72° / 14 [1]. sin C = 9 × sin 72° ÷ 14 = 0.6114… [1]. Angle ACB = **37.7°** [1]. The obtuse value 142.3° is impossible, because 142.3° + 72° is more than 180°.

*Examiner insight:* AB is opposite angle C and BC is opposite angle A. Candidates who pair each side with the angle at one of its ends set up the wrong ratio and lose all three marks.

**3.** **(a)** Base diagonal = √(12² + 5²) = 13 cm [1]. Line = √(13² + 4²) = √185 = **13.6 cm** [1].

**(b)** The angle is between the line and the base diagonal: tan θ = 4 ÷ 13 [1]. θ = **17.1°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The angle with a plane is measured to the projection of the line on that plane. Using 12 cm or 5 cm instead of the base diagonal is the usual error.

**4.** cos x = −3/4 = −0.75 [1]. Calculator value x = 138.6° [1]. Cosine is negative in the second and third quadrants, so the other solution is 360° − 138.6° = 221.4°, giving **x = 138.6° or 221.4°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* A sketch of y = cos x over 0° to 360° shows how many solutions to expect. Stopping at the calculator value caps the mark at 2 out of 3.

**5.** sin 60° = √3/2, tan 30° = 1/√3 and cos 45° = 1/√2 [1]. (√3/2) × (1/√3) + (1/√2)² = 1/2 + 1/2 = **1** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Sketch the half-equilateral triangle (sides 1, √3, 2) and the right-angled isosceles triangle (sides 1, 1, √2) to recall the values. Swapping sin 60° and sin 30° gives a different final answer.

**6.** **(a)** The perpendicular from O bisects AB, so each half is 8 cm [1]. Distance = √(10² − 8²) = **6 cm** [1].

**(b)** In the right-angled triangle, sin θ = 8 ÷ 10, so θ = 53.13° [1]. Angle AOB = 2 × 53.13° = **106.3°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Using the full chord length of 16 cm in the right-angled triangle is the standard error. Also remember that the triangle gives half of angle AOB.

**7.** Area = ½ × 9.5 × 8.2 × sin X = 34 [1]. sin X = 68 ÷ 77.9 = 0.8729… [1]. Angle YXZ = **60.8°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The question says the angle is acute, which tells you to reject 119.2°. Check the wording for a clue like this before you decide which value to give.

**8.** **(a)** The bearing of P from Q is 040° + 180° = 220° [1]. Angle PQR = 220° − 115° = **105°** [1].

**(b)** PR² = 12² + 9² − 2 × 12 × 9 × cos 105° [1]. PR² = 144 + 81 + 55.9… = 280.9… [1]. PR = **16.8 km** [1].

**(c)** sin QPR / 9 = sin 105° / 16.76… so sin QPR = 0.5187… and angle QPR = 31.2° [1]. R lies clockwise from Q as seen from P, so the bearing is 040° + 31.2° [1] = **071°** (to the nearest degree) [1].

*Examiner insight:* Mark both bearings on a diagram with a north line at each point. Without it, candidates often subtract the bearings directly and get 75° instead of 105°.

**9.** **(a)** 5 tan x = 6, so tan x = 1.2 [1]. x = 50.2° [1]. Tangent repeats every 180°, so the second solution is 230.2°, giving **x = 50.2° or 230.2°** [1].

**(b)** sin x = −1/2 [1]. The sine is negative in the third and fourth quadrants, so **x = 210° or 330°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Rearrange fully before you use the inverse function. In part (b) the calculator gives −30°, which is outside the range, so you must use the symmetry of the graph.

**10.** **(a)** The graph falls from 1 at x = 0° to −1 at the halfway point, so the minimum is at **x = 180°** [1].

**(b)** The line y = 0.4 cuts the curve once on the way down and once on the way up, so there are **2 solutions** [1].

**(c)** Calculator value: x = cos⁻¹(0.4) = **66.4°** [1]. By symmetry about x = 180°, the other solution is 360° − 66.4° = **293.6°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The graph is symmetrical about x = 180°, so the second solution is 360° minus the first. Adding 180° to the first value is the tangent rule and gives a wrong answer for cosine.

**11.** **(a)** AC = √(8² + 8²) [1] = **11.3 cm** [1].

**(b)** The foot of the height is the centre of the base, so it is 5.657… cm from A [1]. VA = √(5.657…² + 10²) = **11.5 cm** [1].

**(c)** tan θ = 10 ÷ 5.657… [1], so θ = **60.5°** [1].

**(d)** Triangle VAB has VA = VB = 11.49… and AB = 8. Cosine rule: cos AVB = (11.49…² + 11.49…² − 8²) ÷ (2 × 11.49… × 11.49…) [1] = (264 − 64) ÷ 264 = 0.7575… [1]. Angle AVB = **40.7°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Keep 5.657… and 11.49… in your calculator memory. Using rounded values such as 5.7 and 11.5 can push the final angle outside the accepted range. Also use half of AC, not the whole diagonal, in parts (b) and (c).

**12.** **(a)** cos A = (6² + 10² − 14²) ÷ (2 × 6 × 10) [1] = (36 + 100 − 196) ÷ 120 = −60 ÷ 120 = −0.5 [1]. Angle BAC = cos⁻¹(−0.5) = **120°** [1].

**(b)** Area = ½ × 6 × 10 × sin 120° [1] = 30 × (√3/2) = **15√3 cm²** [1].

**(c)** Area = ½ × 14 × h, so 15√3 = 7h [1]. h = 15√3 ÷ 7 = **3.71 cm** [1].

**(d)** sin C / 6 = sin 120° / 14 [1]. sin C = 0.3711… so angle ACB = **21.8°** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question you must write down the substituted expression and the value before the inverse cosine. Quoting 120° straight from the calculator does not prove the result. In part (d), angle ACB must be acute, because the 120° angle is already in the triangle.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Rounding intermediate values too early in cosine-rule and sine-rule calculations.
- Using the wrong side and angle pairs in the sine rule.
- Subtracting before multiplying in the cosine rule, so the order of operations is wrong.
- Giving only one solution to a trigonometric equation, or giving solutions outside the stated range.
- Choosing the wrong right-angled triangle in three-dimensional problems, or using a base edge instead of the base diagonal.
- Forgetting that the perpendicular from the centre of a circle halves a chord.
- Calculating a bearing without a north line, or giving one with fewer than three figures.
- Leaving the calculator in radian mode, which gives a plausible but wrong answer.
- Writing the answer to a "show that" question without showing the substitution first.
