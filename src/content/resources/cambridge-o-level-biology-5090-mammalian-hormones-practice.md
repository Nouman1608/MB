## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. **[1]**

**3.** In the adrenal glands [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Oestrogen [1] and progesterone [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Hormonal control is slower, or hormonal effects last longer [1]. **[1]**

**6.** A chemical substance [1], produced by a gland and carried by the blood [1], which alters the activity of one or more specific target organs [1]. **[3]**

**7.**

**(a)** Adrenaline [1], produced by the adrenal glands [1].

**(b)** Increases heart rate [1]. Increases blood glucose concentration [1].

**(c)** A faster heart rate sends blood to the muscles more quickly, delivering more oxygen and glucose [1]. A higher blood glucose concentration gives more glucose for respiration, so more energy is released for muscle contraction [1]. Together they prepare the body for fast movement [1].

**(d)** Any one of: danger or fear; excitement; stress; sudden shock [1]. **[8]**

**8.**

**(a)** 99 - 72 = 27 beats per minute [1].

**(b)** 27 divided by 72 x 100 [1] = 37.5 per cent [1].

**(c)** The hormone adrenaline stays in the blood for a time [1], so its effect on the heart continues for several minutes until it is broken down [1]. **[5]**

**9.** Nervous control is very fast, because impulses travel quickly along neurones [1]. Hormonal control is slower, because hormones are carried in the blood [1]. The effect of nervous control is short-lived [1]. The effect of hormonal control lasts longer [1]. **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** Nervous [1], because the response is rapid and short-lived [1].

**(b)** Hormonal [1], because the change is slow and lasts a long time (testosterone) [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Only the target organs [1] have cells that can detect (respond to) adrenaline [1]. **[2]**

**12.** A: testes [1]. B: pituitary gland [1]. C: pancreas [1]. **[3]**

**13.** The statement is only partly correct [1]. Hormonal control uses the blood, but nervous control uses electrical impulses along neurones, not the blood [1]. **[2]**

**14.** The body prepares for physical action whenever there is a challenge [1], increasing heart rate and blood glucose concentration so that more energy can be released [1]. The effect lasts because adrenaline stays in the blood until it is broken down [1], so its effect on its target organs continues for some minutes [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "specific target organs" from the definition of a hormone.
- Saying that hormones travel along nerves.
- Naming the wrong gland, for example the pancreas for adrenaline.
- Writing "faster" for hormonal control instead of "slower".
- Not explaining the link between the effect and the benefit in a "how does this help" question.
