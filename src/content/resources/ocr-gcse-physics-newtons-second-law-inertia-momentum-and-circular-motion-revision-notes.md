## Equations and units

| Idea | Equation | Units |
|---|---|---|
| Newton's second law (P2.2i) | F = m a | N, kg, m/s² |
| Acceleration from a speed change | a = (v - u) / t | m/s², m/s, s |
| Inertial mass (P2.2j) | m = F / a | kg, N, m/s² |
| Momentum (P2.2k) | p = m v | kg m/s, kg, m/s |
| Force and momentum (P2.2l, Higher Tier only) | F = (change in p) / t | N, kg m/s, s |

Always convert to kg, m/s, N and s before substituting. The force in F = ma is the resultant force.

## Newton's second law

- Resultant force and acceleration have the same direction.
- Double the resultant force on the same mass: double the acceleration.
- Double the mass under the same force: half the acceleration.
- No resultant force means no acceleration: constant velocity (or at rest).

**Method for F = ma questions**

1. Draw or list the forces. Find the resultant (subtract opposing forces).
2. Write the equation, then rearrange before substituting.
3. Substitute with units. Give the answer with a unit and a sensible number of significant figures.

**Reminder example.** A 1300 kg car has driving force 900 N and resistive force 640 N.

```
resultant = 900 - 640 = 260 N
a = 260 / 1300 = 0.20 m/s²
```

**Reminder example.** A skier of mass 64 kg accelerates at 1.5 m/s² down a slope under a resultant force of 96 N: F = 64 × 1.5 = 96 N.

### Force and acceleration experiment

- The specification suggests light gates, weights and trolleys (PAG P2).
- Keep the total mass of the system the same by moving weights between trolley and hanger.
- Result: acceleration is proportional to resultant force, and F ÷ a gives the mass of the system.
- Scatter in the F ÷ a column shows measurement uncertainty.

## Inertia and inertial mass

- **Inertia**: a measure of how difficult it is to change the velocity of an object.
- **Inertial mass**: the ratio of force over acceleration, m = F / a.
- Large inertial mass: small acceleration for a given force. Small inertial mass: large acceleration.

| Object | Force | Acceleration | Inertial mass |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cart A | 24 N | 3.0 m/s² | 8.0 kg |
| Cart B | 24 N | 0.60 m/s² | 40 kg |

Cart B has more inertia. It is harder to speed up, slow down or turn.

## Momentum and collisions

**Momentum = mass × velocity.** It is a vector, in kg m/s, with the direction of the velocity.

**Conservation of momentum:** with no external force, total momentum before = total momentum after.

**Method for collision questions**

1. Choose a positive direction. Movement the other way is negative.
2. Write total momentum before as a sum, with a zero term for any stationary object.
3. Write total momentum after, using the combined mass if the objects join.
4. Set before equal to after and solve. State the direction.

| Type | Reminder example | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Join together | 2.5 kg cart at 4.8 m/s runs into a resting 1.5 kg cart and they lock | 12 / 4.0 = 3.0 m/s |
| Push apart from rest | Total momentum before = 0, so the two momenta are equal and opposite | Lighter object gets the greater speed |
| Bounce apart | Before: sum of p. After: sum of p. Solve for the unknown velocity | Include signs |

The water rocket: the water goes down with the same momentum as the rocket gains going up. Light gates, weights and trolleys can measure momentum before and after collisions (PAG P3).

## More worked reminders

**Bounce apart.** A 0.80 kg trolley moving at 3.0 m/s hits a stationary 0.40 kg trolley. Afterwards the 0.80 kg trolley moves on at 1.0 m/s and the 0.40 kg trolley moves off.

```
before: 0.80 x 3.0 + 0.40 x 0 = 2.4 kg m/s
after:  0.80 x 1.0 + 0.40 x v = 0.80 + 0.40 v
2.4 = 0.80 + 0.40 v, so v = 4.0 m/s (same direction)
```

**Push apart.** A 50 kg skater and a 40 kg skater at rest push apart. The 50 kg skater moves at 1.2 m/s one way. Momentum of the first skater = 50 × 1.2 = 60 kg m/s, so the 40 kg skater has 60 kg m/s the other way: v = 60 / 40 = 1.5 m/s.

**Words to use in explanations**

- "The resultant force is zero, so there is no acceleration."
- "The total momentum before the collision equals the total momentum after."
- "Momentum is a vector, so the direction matters."
- "The velocity is changing because the direction is changing, so the object is accelerating."
- (Higher Tier only) "The change in momentum is the same, but it happens over a longer time, so the force is smaller."

## Higher Tier only: force and change in momentum

F = ma and a = (v - u) / t combine to give F = (m v - m u) / t. So force = change in momentum ÷ time.

- Same change in momentum, longer time: smaller force.
- Seat belts, air bags and crumple zones lengthen the stopping time and reduce the force.
- Reminder example: a 0.30 kg ball stopped from 12 m/s in 0.020 s needs F = 0.30 × 12 / 0.020 = 180 N.

## Circular motion at constant speed

- Speed is constant. Direction keeps changing.
- Velocity is a vector, so its direction changing means the velocity is changing.
- A changing velocity is an acceleration, so a resultant force towards the centre is needed.
- Rubber bung on a string: if the string breaks, the bung moves in a straight line along its direction at that instant.

**Must-know distinctions**

| Pair | Difference |
|---|---|
| Speed and velocity | Speed has size only. Velocity has size and direction. |
| Force and momentum | Force changes momentum. Momentum is mass × velocity. |
| Momentum before and after | The total is equal. One object's momentum can change. |

## Quick self-test

1. A 6.0 kg cart has a resultant force of 15 N. Find its acceleration.
2. A resultant force of 48 N gives an object an acceleration of 6.0 m/s². Find its mass.
3. A force of 7.5 N gives an acceleration of 1.5 m/s². What is the inertial mass?
4. Find the momentum of a 1400 kg car moving at 15 m/s.
5. A 9.0 kg trolley has a momentum of 27 kg m/s. Find its velocity.
6. A 2.5 kg cart at 4.8 m/s runs into a resting 1.5 kg cart and the two lock together. Find their speed.
7. A gun of mass 4.0 kg fires a 0.020 kg bullet at 300 m/s. Find the recoil speed of the gun.
8. (Higher Tier only) A 0.30 kg ball moving at 12 m/s is stopped in 0.020 s. Find the average force.
9. Why does a car going round a roundabout at constant speed have a changing velocity?
10. A van at 0.50 m/s² under a resultant force of 650 N: what is its mass?

### Answers

1. a = 15 / 6.0 = 2.5 m/s².
2. m = 48 / 6.0 = 8.0 kg.
3. m = 7.5 / 1.5 = 5.0 kg.
4. p = 1400 × 15 = 21 000 kg m/s.
5. v = 27 / 9.0 = 3.0 m/s.
6. Before: 2.5 × 4.8 = 12 kg m/s. After: 4.0 × v. So v = 3.0 m/s.
7. Bullet: 0.020 × 300 = 6.0 kg m/s forwards. The gun has 6.0 kg m/s backwards, so v = 6.0 / 4.0 = 1.5 m/s.
8. F = 0.30 × 12 / 0.020 = 180 N.
9. The direction of travel keeps changing. Velocity is a vector, so a change in direction is a change in velocity, even though the speed is constant.
10. m = 650 / 0.50 = 1300 kg.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using one force instead of the resultant force.
- Substituting grams or tonnes without converting to kg.
- Forgetting the zero term for a stationary object in a collision.
- Using the mass of one object, not the combined mass, after objects stick.
- Dropping the sign when an object moves in the opposite direction.
- Writing "momentum is lost" instead of "momentum is transferred, and the total is conserved".
- Saying the speed changes in circular motion, rather than the velocity.
- Leaving out units, or the direction of a vector answer.
- Answers about safety that mention force but not the longer time (Higher Tier only).
