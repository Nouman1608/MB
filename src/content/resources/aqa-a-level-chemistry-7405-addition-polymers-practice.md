These questions cover section **3.3.4.3 Addition polymers** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 2. Learn the ideas first in the [Addition polymers study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-polymers/) and the [Addition polymers revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-addition-polymers-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, F = 19.0, Cl = 35.5.

## Questions

**1.** Define the term monomer. **[1]**

**2.** Chloroethene, CH₂=CHCl, is the monomer of PVC.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit of PVC. **[1]**

**(b)** Give the IUPAC name of the polymer. **[1]**

**3.** Draw the repeating unit of the polymer formed from each monomer.

**(a)** propene, CH₂=CHCH₃ **[1]**

**(b)** but-2-ene, CH₃CH=CHCH₃ **[1]**

**(c)** tetrafluoroethene, CF₂=CF₂ **[1]**

**4.** A section of an addition polymer is shown:

–CH₂–CH(CN)–CH₂–CH(CN)–CH₂–CH(CN)–

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the structure of the monomer. **[1]**

**(c)** Suggest an IUPAC name for the polymer if the monomer is named propenenitrile. **[1]**

**5.** Poly(phenylethene) is made from phenylethene, CH₂=CH(C₆H₅).

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why the atom economy of this polymerisation is 100 %. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why addition polymers such as poly(ethene) are unreactive. **[3]**

**7.** Name the intermolecular forces between poly(ethene) chains and explain why a sample of poly(ethene) with longer chains has a higher melting temperature than a sample with shorter chains. **[3]**

**8.** Explain why the forces between chains in PVC differ from those in poly(ethene). **[2]**

**9.** Unplasticised PVC is rigid and is used to make window frames. Plasticised PVC is flexible and is used for insulation on electrical cables.

**(a)** State what is meant by a plasticiser. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain how a plasticiser makes PVC flexible. **[3]**

**10.** A sample of polystyrene (poly(phenylethene)) has an average relative molecular mass of 20 800. The relative molecular mass of phenylethene is 104.0.

**(a)** Calculate the average number of repeating units in a chain. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of polymer that can be made from 5.20 kg of phenylethene. **[1]**

**11.** State one way in which the understanding of the production of polymers has developed over time. **[1]**

**12.** Poly(propene) and poly(chloroethene) are both addition polymers. Compare their structures, intermolecular forces and likely rigidity. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** A small molecule that can join with many others to form a polymer [1]. **[1]**

**2. (a)** –[CH₂–CHCl]ₙ– with bonds extending from both ends [1]

**(b)** Poly(chloroethene) [1]. **[2]**

**3. (a)** –[CH₂–CH(CH₃)]ₙ– [1]

**(b)** –[CH(CH₃)–CH(CH₃)]ₙ– [1]

**(c)** –[CF₂–CF₂]ₙ– [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* In (b), only the two carbons of the double bond are in the backbone. The methyl groups are side groups.

**4. (a)** –[CH₂–CH(CN)]ₙ– [1]

**(b)** CH₂=CH(CN) [1]

**(c)** Poly(propenenitrile) [1]. **[3]**

**5. (a)** –[CH₂–CH(C₆H₅)]ₙ– [1]

**(b)** All the atoms in the monomer end up in the polymer, with no other product [1]. **[2]**

**6.** The backbone is made of strong C–C and C–H bonds [1]; these bonds are non-polar, so they do not attract nucleophiles or electrophiles [1]; there is no C=C bond or other reactive site in the saturated chain [1]. **[3]**

**7.** London forces [1]; longer chains have more points of contact, so the total attraction between chains is greater [1]; more energy is needed to separate the chains, so the melting temperature is higher [1]. **[3]**

**8.** PVC has polar C–Cl bonds, so there are permanent dipole–dipole attractions between chains as well as London forces [1]; poly(ethene) has only London forces [1]. **[2]**

**9. (a)** A small molecule added to a polymer that sits between the chains [1]

**(b)** The plasticiser molecules get between the polymer chains and push them apart [1]; this weakens the intermolecular forces between chains [1]; so the chains can slide past one another, making the material flexible [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** n = 20 800 ÷ 104.0 = **200** [1]

**(b)** The maximum mass of polymer is **5.20 kg**, because nothing else forms (100 % atom economy) [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Any sensible point, such as: catalysts were developed that allow polymerisation at lower pressure and give more control over chain structure [1]. **[1]**

**12.** Both have a saturated carbon backbone made from two carbons per monomer [1]; poly(propene) has a methyl side group and poly(chloroethene) has a chlorine side group [1]; poly(propene) has London forces only [1]; poly(chloroethene) also has permanent dipole–dipole attractions from the polar C–Cl bond [1]; so poly(chloroethene) is likely to be more rigid (unless a plasticiser is added) [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving a double bond in the repeating unit.
- Including all four carbons of but-2-ene in the backbone.
- Omitting the extended bonds or the n.
- Describing PVC as having only London forces.
- Saying a plasticiser strengthens the polymer.
- Explaining unreactivity as "the polymer is stable" with no reference to bond strength and polarity.
