Condensed recall for Cambridge International A Level Sociology (9699) sub-topic 12.2, *Gender,
feminism and religion*, in Paper 4 Section C. For full explanations and a model evaluative
paragraph, see the [gender, feminism and religion study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-gender-feminism-and-religion/).

## The three syllabus points

1. Feminist perspectives on religion.
2. Explanations for patriarchy and gender inequality in religion.
3. Moves towards gender equality in religious organisations, and evidence that religious practices
   may benefit women.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Patriarchy** | A system of male dominance over women |
| **Agency** | The capacity of individuals to make choices and act for themselves |
| **Ordination** | Formal admission to a religious office such as priest or rabbi |
| **Religious forms of feminism** | Women using religion to gain status, respect and community (Woodhead) |
| **False compensation** | De Beauvoir's view that religion consoles women for their subordination |
| **Monotheism** | Belief in one God; linked by Armstrong to the decline of female deities |
| **Purity rules** | Ideas of ritual impurity that can restrict women's access to sacred spaces |
| **Piety** | Devotion cultivated through religious practice (Mahmood) |
| **Liberal feminism** | Reform from within through equal access to roles |
| **Radical feminism** | Religion as a core institution of patriarchy |

## Feminist perspectives at a glance

- **Radical:** Mary Daly (1973): "if God is male, then the male is God". Daly moved from seeking
  reform within Catholicism to rejecting Christianity.
- **Marxist feminist:** religion as ideology for capitalism and patriarchy together.
- **Liberal:** reform is possible; campaigns for ordination.
- **De Beauvoir (1949):** religion gives women false compensation.
- **Within-faith feminists (El Saadawi):** male interpretation, not the faith itself, oppresses
  women.
- **Agency approaches (Woodhead, Mahmood):** women use religion for their own ends; Western
  liberal ideas of freedom may misread their choices.

## Six explanations of inequality

| Type | Example |
|---|---|
| Organisational | Leadership roles reserved for men |
| Textual and interpretive | Texts written, translated and interpreted mainly by men |
| Theological imagery | God as Father or King; male prophets and founders |
| Ritual and purity | Restrictions linked to menstruation or childbirth |
| Religious law and family | Marriage, divorce, inheritance and dress rules |
| Historical | Armstrong: female deities before male-dominated monotheism |

**Evaluation hook:** all six show that inequality is socially produced, so it can change. Radical
feminists read them as a patriarchal core; liberal and within-faith feminists as distortions.

## Moves towards equality

- **Church of England:** women priests from 1994; first woman bishop 2015.
- **Judaism:** Regina Jonas ordained 1935 (Germany); Sally Priesand first American Reform woman
  rabbi 1972; Orthodox Judaism generally does not ordain women.
- **Islam:** women's mosques led by women among Hui Muslims in China; women scholars and teachers.
- **Roman Catholic Church:** priesthood still restricted to men (counter-example).
- **Limit:** formal access does not guarantee equal influence or senior posts.

## Evidence of benefits to women

- **Watson (1994):** veiling as identity and protection from objectification.
- **Brusco (1995):** Colombian evangelical converts reformed husbands' behaviour, benefiting the
  household.
- **Mahmood (2005):** Cairo mosque movement; piety as a form of agency.
- **Woodhead:** religious forms of feminism.
- **Practical:** women-only leadership spaces, support networks, moral criticism of male
  violence.
- **Counter:** radical feminists see these as adaptation to patriarchy, not change.

## Exam traps

- "Feminists argue..." without saying which feminists.
- One religion used as evidence for all religions.
- Veiling treated only as oppression or only as free choice.
- Ordination dates listed with no judgement about what formal change achieves.
- No separate paragraph on "benefits to women", which the syllabus names explicitly.
- Forgetting the key concept *Power, control and resistance*: ask who holds authority.

## Self-test

1. What did Mary Daly mean by "if God is male, then the male is God"?
2. How does a liberal feminist view of religion differ from a radical feminist one?
3. What did de Beauvoir mean by false compensation?
4. Give El Saadawi's view of the cause of women's oppression in religion.
5. Name two organisational or textual explanations of inequality in religion.
6. What is Armstrong's historical argument?
7. When did the Church of England first ordain women priests and consecrate a woman bishop?
8. Name one Jewish movement that ordains women and one that generally does not.
9. Summarise Brusco's findings.
10. What did Watson find about veiling?
11. What is Mahmood's argument about agency?
12. Give one radical feminist criticism of evidence that religion benefits women.

### Answers

1. Male images of God make male power appear sacred and natural.
2. Liberal feminists think religion can be reformed through equal access to roles; radical
   feminists see it as fundamentally patriarchal.
3. Religion consoles women for second-class status in this life, discouraging them from
   challenging it.
4. Patriarchal interpretation and culture, not religion itself, oppress women.
5. Any two: male-only leadership; texts written and interpreted mainly by men.
6. Early religions often had female deities; male dominance became entrenched with monotheism.
7. Priests from 1994; first woman bishop 2015.
8. Reform Judaism ordains women; Orthodox Judaism generally does not.
9. In Colombia, women's conversion to evangelical Protestantism encouraged husbands to give up
   drinking and gambling and commit to family life.
10. Some Muslim women chose the veil to express identity and avoid sexual objectification.
11. Women's piety is a form of agency that should be understood on its own terms, not only as
    resistance or submission.
12. The benefits are gained within rules set by men, so they make the best of patriarchy without
    changing it.
