This guide covers subtopic **1.15 Time** in Topic 1 Number of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C1.15) and Extended (E1.15) statements are identical:

1. **Calculate with time**: seconds (s), minutes (min), hours (h), days, weeks, months, years, including the relationship between units. The syllabus states 1 year = 365 days.
2. **Calculate times in terms of the 24-hour and 12-hour clock.** In the 24-hour clock, 3.15 a.m. is written 03 15 and 3.15 p.m. is written 15 15.
3. **Read clocks and timetables**, including problems involving time zones, local times and time differences.

Time questions appear on the non-calculator papers as well as the calculator papers. The broader [rates, time and money study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-rates-time-and-money/) introduces each of these. This page goes further: long conversions between units, intervals that cross one or more midnights, timetables with a connection, and time zones that change the date. Afterwards, use the [time revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-time-revision-notes/) and the [time practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-time-practice/).

## Units of time (1.15.1)

```
60 seconds = 1 minute        60 minutes = 1 hour        24 hours = 1 day
7 days = 1 week              12 months = 1 year         1 year = 365 days
```

Time is not a decimal system. A step from one unit to the next can be 60, 24, 7 or 12, so you cannot move a decimal point to convert. Divide by the conversion factor and handle the **remainder** in the smaller unit.

### Worked example 1: converting in both directions

(a) Write 2.4 days in hours and minutes.
(b) Write 1 000 000 seconds in days, hours, minutes and seconds.
(c) How many complete weeks are there in a year of 365 days, and how many days are left over?

```
(a) 2.4 × 24 = 57.6 hours
    0.6 × 60 = 36 minutes          ->  57 hours 36 minutes

(b) 1 day = 24 × 60 × 60 = 86 400 s
    1 000 000 ÷ 86 400 = 11.57...  ->  11 days, using 11 × 86 400 = 950 400 s
    remainder 1 000 000 - 950 400 = 49 600 s
    49 600 ÷ 3600 = 13.77...       ->  13 hours, using 46 800 s
    remainder 2800 s ÷ 60 = 46.66... -> 46 minutes, using 2760 s
    remainder 40 s
    Answer: 11 days 13 hours 46 minutes 40 seconds

(c) 365 ÷ 7 = 52 remainder 1  ->  52 weeks and 1 day
```

In (b), work from the largest unit down, subtracting what you have used each time. Check by converting back: 11 × 86 400 + 13 × 3600 + 46 × 60 + 40 = 1 000 000.

## The 24-hour and 12-hour clock (1.15.2)

| 12-hour | 24-hour | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| 3.15 a.m. | 03 15 | a.m. keeps its digits, with a leading zero |
| 3.15 p.m. | 15 15 | p.m. adds 12 to the hour |
| 12.05 a.m. | 00 05 | the hour after midnight is 00 |
| 12.40 p.m. | 12 40 | the hour after noon stays 12 |
| 11.40 p.m. | 23 40 | |

The 0580 syllabus writes 24-hour times as four digits with a space, such as 15 15, with no a.m. or p.m. Always use four digits: write 09 05, not 9 05.

## Time intervals

Never subtract times as if they were decimals. Count on in steps: to the next whole hour, then whole hours, then the minutes left.

### Worked example 2: an interval crossing midnight twice

A ship leaves port at 19 35 on Monday and arrives at 07 10 on Wednesday. How long is the voyage?

```
19 35 Monday  -> 19 35 Tuesday    24 h
19 35 Tuesday -> 20 00 Tuesday        25 min
20 00 Tuesday -> 07 00 Wednesday  11 h
07 00 -> 07 10                        10 min
Total: 35 h 35 min
```

Whole days first is the quickest way to handle more than one midnight.

### Worked example 3: adding a duration

A film starts at 21 50 and lasts 2 hours 25 minutes. When does it end? Give your answer in the 12-hour clock.

```
21 50 + 2 h = 23 50
23 50 + 25 min = 24 15  ->  00 15 the next day
00 15 = 12.15 a.m.
```

There is no 24 15 on a 24-hour clock: after 23 59 comes 00 00.

## Clocks and timetables (1.15.3)

A timetable has one row per stop and one column per journey. Read down a column to follow one bus or train. Times that are not printed (a blank or a dash) mean the service does not stop there.

### Worked example 4: a timetable with a connection

Part of a timetable:

| Bus | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hill Road | 07 35 | 08 05 | 08 35 |
| Town Centre | 07 52 | 08 22 | 08 52 |
| Station | 08 06 | 08 36 | 09 06 |

Trains leave the station at 08 15, 08 45 and 09 15, and take 48 minutes to reach the city. Ola lives at Hill Road. She needs at least 5 minutes at the station to change, and she must reach the city by 09 40. Which bus should she catch, and when does she arrive?

```
Arrive by 09 40: the train must leave by 09 40 - 48 min = 08 52, so the 08 45 train.
To catch the 08 45 train she must reach the station by 08 40.
Bus B arrives at 08 36 (9 minutes to spare). Bus C arrives at 09 06, too late.
She catches bus B from Hill Road at 08 05 and arrives in the city at 08 45 + 48 min = 09 33.
```

Work backwards from the deadline, one step at a time, writing the time at each step.

## Time zones and local times

Each place uses its own local time. If City Y is 7 hours behind City X, then when it is 15 00 in X it is 08 00 in Y. To solve journey problems:

1. Work in **one** time zone (usually the departure city).
2. Add the journey time.
3. Convert the result into the other local time, changing the date if you pass midnight.

### Worked example 5: arrival time in another zone

Port Ash is 7 hours behind Kerran. (These are fictional cities.) A flight leaves Kerran at 09 15 local time and takes 11 hours 40 minutes. Find the local time in Port Ash when it lands.

```
Kerran time on landing: 09 15 + 11 h 40 min = 20 55
Port Ash is 7 hours behind: 20 55 - 7 h = 13 55
The flight lands at 13 55 local time, on the same day.
```

### Worked example 6: flight time from two local times

A flight leaves Port Ash at 22 30 local time and lands in Kerran at 18 05 local time the next day. How long is the flight?

```
Landing time in Port Ash time: 18 05 - 7 h = 11 05 the next day
Flight time: 22 30 -> 11 05 next day
  22 30 -> 00 00   1 h 30 min
  00 00 -> 11 05  11 h 05 min
  total           12 h 35 min
```

Both times must be in the same zone before you subtract.

## Common mistakes

- Subtracting times as decimals: 13 20 − 09 45 is not 3 h 75 min or 3.75 h.
- Converting 2.4 days to 2 days 4 hours.
- Writing 12.30 a.m. as 12 30 instead of 00 30.
- Writing 24 15 instead of 00 15.
- Reading the wrong column of a timetable, or using a bus that does not stop at the station.
- Mixing two time zones in one subtraction.
- Adding the time difference when the destination is behind, or subtracting it when it is ahead.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Work out** a duration: show the counting-on steps, so that a slip in one step still earns method credit.
- **Write** a time **in the 24-hour clock** or **12-hour clock**: four digits for 24-hour; a.m. or p.m. for 12-hour.
- **Give your answer in** hours and minutes: never leave a decimal number of hours.
- **Explain** a timetable choice: give the times that show why one bus works and the next one does not.
