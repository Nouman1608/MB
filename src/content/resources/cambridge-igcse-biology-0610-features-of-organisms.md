This study guide teaches sub-topic 1.3, Features of organisms, of the Cambridge IGCSE Biology 0610 syllabus for examination in 2026, 2027 and 2028. Outcomes 1 to 3 are **Core**: the features used to place organisms into the animal and plant kingdoms and into the main groups of vertebrates and arthropods, and using them to classify. Outcomes 4 to 7 are **Supplement** content, so they are **Extended only** (Papers 2 and 4): the five kingdoms, ferns and flowering plants (dicotyledons and monocotyledons), and the features of viruses.

The [topic 1 overview guide](/resources/igcse-biology-characteristics-and-classification-of-living-organisms/) lists the features of each group in tables. This page teaches you to **use** them: which single feature decides between two similar groups, a step-by-step method for classifying an unfamiliar organism, and the near-misses examiners like to set. ## 1.3.1 Animal or plant?

| Feature | Animals | Plants |
|---|---|---|
| Number of cells | Multicellular | Multicellular |
| Cell wall | None | Present, made of cellulose |
| Chloroplasts and photosynthesis | None | Present; make their own food |
| Nutrition | Feed on organic substances made by other organisms | Make food by photosynthesis |
| Carbohydrate store | Glycogen | Starch or sucrose |

**Deciding feature:** chloroplasts and a cellulose cell wall mean plant. An organism that takes in food made by other organisms and has no cell walls is an animal.

## 1.3.2 Groups of vertebrates: the deciding features

All vertebrates have a backbone. To place one in its group, look for the feature that only that group has.

| Group | Deciding feature | Supporting features |
|---|---|---|
| Mammals | **Fur or hair**; **mammary glands** | External ears (pinnae); most give birth to live young |
| Birds | **Feathers** | Beak; wings; eggs with hard shells |
| Reptiles | **Dry, scaly skin** | Eggs with rubbery or leathery shells, laid on land |
| Amphibians | **Moist skin without scales** | Eggs without shells laid in water; young (tadpoles) live in water and have gills |
| Fish | **Scales and fins**, with **gills** | Eggs laid in water |

### Near-misses with vertebrates

- **Whale and dolphin:** live in water and have fins, but they have no scales, breathe air with lungs and feed their young on milk from mammary glands, so they are **mammals**.
- **Bat:** flies, but has fur and mammary glands, not feathers, so it is a **mammal**.
- **Penguin:** cannot fly, but has feathers and a beak, so it is a **bird**.
- **Newt and lizard:** similar shape, but a newt has moist skin without scales (**amphibian**) and a lizard has dry, scaly skin (**reptile**).

## 1.3.2 Groups of arthropods: count first

All arthropods have **jointed legs**, a **segmented body** and an **exoskeleton**. The groups are separated mainly by counting.

| Group | Legs | Body parts | Antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insects | 3 pairs (6) | 3: head, thorax, abdomen | 1 pair; usually 2 pairs of wings |
| Arachnids | 4 pairs (8) | 2 | None |
| Crustaceans | More than 4 pairs | Often a fused head and thorax | 2 pairs; hard exoskeleton |
| Myriapods | Many: 1 or 2 pairs on each segment | Many similar segments | 1 pair |

### Near-misses with arthropods

- **Scorpion and tick:** look different from spiders, but have 4 pairs of legs and no antennae, so they are **arachnids**.
- **Woodlouse:** lives on land, but has 7 pairs of legs and 2 pairs of antennae, so it is a **crustacean**.
- **Centipede and millipede:** many segments with legs on each, so both are **myriapods**.
- **Ant:** no wings in most workers, but 3 pairs of legs and 3 body parts, so it is an **insect**.

## 1.3.3 Method: classifying an unfamiliar organism

1. **Animal or plant?** Look for chloroplasts or cell walls.
2. **Animal: backbone or not?** If it has jointed legs and an exoskeleton, it is an arthropod; if a backbone, a vertebrate.
3. **Vertebrate:** check feathers → hair or fur → skin (dry and scaly, or moist) → scales, fins and gills.
4. **Arthropod:** count legs, then body parts, then antennae.
5. **Justify** with the deciding feature, quoting the evidence given.

### Worked example 1

*An animal has a segmented body, jointed legs, 10 legs in total and 2 pairs of antennae. Identify its group, giving reasons.*

> It has jointed legs and a segmented body, so it is an **arthropod** (**first step**). It has more than 4 pairs of legs and 2 pairs of antennae, so it is a **crustacean** (**uses two deciding features from the data**), not an insect (3 pairs of legs) or an arachnid (4 pairs and no antennae) (**rules out the alternatives**).

## 1.3.4 The five kingdoms -- Extended only

| Kingdom | Main features |
|---|---|
| Animal | Multicellular; no cell walls; no chloroplasts; feed on organic substances made by other organisms |
| Plant | Multicellular; cellulose cell walls; chloroplasts; photosynthesis |
| Fungus | Usually made of thread-like hyphae (yeast is single-celled); cell walls not made of cellulose (chitin); no chloroplasts; feed by secreting enzymes onto food and absorbing the products |
| Prokaryote | Single-celled; **no nucleus**; circular DNA and often plasmids; cell wall not made of cellulose (for example, bacteria) |
| Protoctist | Have a nucleus; mostly single-celled; some have chloroplasts and are plant-like, others feed like animals |

**Deciding features:** no nucleus means prokaryote. A nucleus with hyphae or non-cellulose walls and no chloroplasts means fungus. A single cell with a nucleus that is not a fungus (such as yeast) is usually a protoctist.

## 1.3.5 Ferns and flowering plants -- Extended only

| Group | Main features |
|---|---|
| Ferns | Leaves called **fronds**; **no flowers**; reproduce by **spores**, often produced on the underside of the fronds |
| Flowering plants | Produce **flowers**; reproduce by **seeds**, formed inside the ovary |

Flowering plants divide into two groups:

| Feature | Monocotyledons | Dicotyledons |
|---|---|---|
| Cotyledons (seed leaves) in the seed | One | Two |
| Leaf veins | Parallel | Branching network |
| Flower parts | In threes or multiples of three | In fours or fives, or multiples of them |

Grasses, lilies and maize are monocotyledons; beans, roses and oak trees are dicotyledons.

### Worked example 2

*A plant has broad leaves with a network of veins and flowers with five petals. Classify it as precisely as you can.*

> It has flowers, so it is a **flowering plant**, not a fern (**rules out ferns**). Its veins form a branching network and its flower parts are in fives, so it is a **dicotyledon** (**uses both pieces of evidence**).

## 1.3.7 Viruses -- Extended only

A virus is made of a **protein coat** surrounding **genetic material**. That is the full extent of the syllabus outcome. Viruses are not made of cells and are not placed in any of the five kingdoms. Do not describe a virus as having a cell membrane, cytoplasm or ribosomes.

## Common errors

- Calling a whale a fish, or a bat a bird.
- Placing a spider with insects. Count the legs: 8, not 6.
- Calling a woodlouse an insect because it lives on land.
- Using "lays eggs" to identify a group: birds, reptiles, amphibians and fish all lay eggs, so name the type of egg or another feature.
- (Extended) Giving fungi chloroplasts, or saying their walls are made of cellulose.
- (Extended) Saying bacteria have a nucleus.
- (Extended) Mixing up monocotyledons and dicotyledons. "Mono" means one cotyledon.
- (Extended) Adding cell structures to viruses.

## Exam technique for Cambridge command words

- **State:** list the features. Two clear features usually beat four vague ones.
- **Identify:** give the group name, often from a drawing or a table.
- **Classify:** name the group and justify it with the deciding feature from the evidence.
- **Explain why** an organism is not in a group: name the feature it lacks or has.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which parts of 1.3 are Core?** Animal and plant kingdom features, and the groups of vertebrates and arthropods.

**What features of viruses must I know?** Only a protein coat and genetic material.

**How do I tell a monocotyledon from a dicotyledon?** One cotyledon, parallel veins and flower parts in threes for a monocotyledon; two cotyledons, branching veins and parts in fours or fives for a dicotyledon.
