## 2.4.1 Key definitions

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Motivation | The desire and willingness to put effort into a job |
| Well-motivated workforce | Employees who work willingly, stay and accept new ways of working |
| Labour productivity | Output per worker (or per hour worked) |
| Absenteeism | Employees missing work without good reason |
| Labour turnover | The rate at which employees leave and are replaced |

## Why people work

- Money to pay for needs and wants.
- Job security and a regular income.
- Social contact and belonging.
- Status, respect and recognition.
- Job satisfaction and a sense of achievement.
- A chance to learn, use skills and progress.

Use at least two different reasons in an answer. "Money" alone gains little.

## The four benefits of a well-motivated workforce

| Benefit | Link to cost or profit |
|---|---|
| Improved labour productivity | More output for the same labour cost, so cost per unit falls |
| Reduced absenteeism | Fewer gaps in production or service, less cover needed |
| Lower labour turnover | Lower recruitment, selection and training costs |
| Greater willingness to accept change / new methods of working | Faster and smoother introduction of new technology or methods |

### Standard formulas

| Measure | Standard form |
|---|---|
| Labour productivity | Output ÷ number of workers |
| Labour turnover (%) | Leavers in the period ÷ average staff × 100 |
| Absenteeism rate (%) | Days lost ÷ days that could have been worked × 100 |

The syllabus formulas list gives labour productivity as output per period divided by number of employees. The turnover and absenteeism forms are the usual standard forms, not printed in section 2.4.

**Quick reminder.** Rilkin Textiles produced 9,000 metres of cloth with 25 weavers and had 6 leavers from an average of 48 staff.
Productivity = 9,000 ÷ 25 = 360 metres per weaver.
Turnover = 6 ÷ 48 × 100 = 12.5%.

## The three theories at a glance

### Maslow

Five levels, lowest first. Each level drives behaviour once the one beneath is mostly met.

| Level | At work |
|---|---|
| Physiological | Wage enough to live on |
| Safety | Secure contract, safe conditions |
| Social | Teamwork, friendly workplace |
| Esteem | Praise, status, titles, promotion |
| Self-actualisation | Challenge, creativity, reaching potential |

Method in steps:
1. Decide which level the worker is at from clues in the stem.
2. Choose something that meets that level.
3. Say why a lower-level reward would add little.

### Taylor

- Workers are motivated mainly by money.
- Break the job into tasks, find the best method, train, pay by output.
- Typical link: piece-rate and bonuses.
- Weakness: ignores boredom, teamwork and the quality of the work.

### Herzberg

| Hygiene factors | Motivators |
|---|---|
| Pay, conditions, job security, policy, supervision | Achievement, recognition, responsibility, the work itself, advancement |
| Stop dissatisfaction if good | Create satisfaction and effort |
| Do not motivate alone | Need hygiene factors to be fair first |

### Must-know distinctions between theories

- Taylor says pay motivates. Herzberg says pay is a hygiene factor.
- Maslow gives a ladder of needs. Herzberg gives two groups of factors.
- Maslow's esteem and self-actualisation levels sit close to Herzberg's motivators.

## 2.4.2 Financial methods

| Method | Basis of payment | Main plus | Main minus |
|---|---|---|---|
| Time-based | Hours worked | Simple, steady | No reward for speed |
| Piece-rate | Units produced | Rewards output | Quality may fall, pay uncertain |
| Salary | Fixed annual sum | Security | Not linked to effort |
| Bonus | Target met | Rewards results | Seen as automatic over time |
| Commission | Percentage of sales | Strong sales drive | Pushy selling, unstable income |
| Profit sharing | Share of profit | Loyalty, team spirit | Weak link to one person's effort, delay |
| Fringe benefits | Non-cash extras | Valued perks | Cost, not wanted equally by all |

### Pay calculations (method in steps)

1. Write the rate and the quantity (hours, units, sales or profit).
2. Multiply, or take the percentage.
3. Add any basic pay.
4. State the answer with units, and say what it means if the question asks.

Examples with Gantry Lane Printers:
- Time-based: 35 hours × 11 = **385**.
- Piece-rate: 250 bundles of leaflets at 1.20 each = 250 × 1.20 = **300**.
- Commission: 6% of 18,000 sales = **1,080**.
- Profit sharing: 8% of a 150,000 profit = 12,000, shared by 20 staff = **600 each**.
- Salary: 2,400 a month × 12 = **28,800** a year.

## Non-financial methods

| Method | What it is | Link to theory | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Job enrichment | More responsibility and challenge | Herzberg motivators, Maslow self-actualisation | Needs training, may need higher pay |
| Job rotation | Moving between tasks at the same level | Reduces boredom | Adds no responsibility, training time |
| Training | Developing skills | Esteem, growth | Cost, staff may leave |
| Opportunities for promotion | A career path | Esteem, achievement | Few posts, disappointment |
| Praise | Verbal recognition | Esteem | Must be sincere |
| Employee of the month | Public award | Esteem, recognition | Rivalry, same few winners |

## Recommend and justify

Method in steps:
1. Identify the problem in the stem (low output, high turnover, resistance to change, boredom).
2. Pick one method that fits.
3. Explain how it works with a detail from the case.
4. Give a limit or a comparison with another method.
5. Make a clear final decision.

Good judgements rest on the workers' circumstances: age, type of job, current pay, and the business's size and cash position. A small business with little cash may prefer praise and flexible duties to a costly bonus scheme.

## Worked reminder: applying a theory

Rilkin Textiles pays its weavers well and the factory is clean and safe. Weavers say the job is dull and nobody notices good work. Several have left for jobs paying less.

- Herzberg: pay and conditions are hygiene factors and are already fair. The missing piece is motivators, so add responsibility and recognition.
- Maslow: lower needs are met. The weavers lack esteem and self-actualisation, so use praise, promotion routes and more challenging tasks.
- Taylor: more pay or piece-rate would be the response, but the evidence in the case suggests money is not the problem.

A short, strong answer names the theory, picks out the clue from the case, and links it to one method. It then states a limit, for example that enrichment needs training time.

## Matching problems to methods

| Problem in the stem | Likely suitable method | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive work, boredom | Job rotation or job enrichment | Adds variety or responsibility |
| Staff need to be pushed to sell | Commission | Pay moves with sales |
| Output counts easily by unit | Piece-rate | Rewards each unit made |
| Skilled staff leaving for rivals | Training, promotion, fringe benefits | Gives a reason to stay |
| Staff resist new machines | Profit sharing, training, involvement | Shows staff gain from change |
| Very little cash to spend | Praise, employee of the month | Low cost |
| Team effort matters more than individual effort | Profit sharing or a team bonus | Rewards cooperation |

## Things to say in evaluation

- The best method depends on the workers: a student on holiday work may want cash, while a long-serving supervisor may want responsibility.
- Cost matters. A bonus or profit-sharing scheme is paid from money the business may need elsewhere.
- Effects take time. Training and promotion pay off over months, a bonus works faster.
- Methods can be combined. Fair pay (the hygiene factor) plus recognition often works better than either alone.
- Measure success afterwards using productivity, absenteeism and turnover.

## Quick self-test

1. State two reasons why people work other than money.
2. Name the four benefits of a well-motivated workforce listed in the syllabus.
3. Gantry Lane Printers pays 11 an hour. Calculate pay for 35 hours.
4. Calculate labour turnover when 6 of an average 48 staff leave in a year.
5. Which of Herzberg's two groups contains pay?
6. Name the top level of Maslow's hierarchy.
7. Which theorist is associated with piece-rate pay as the main motivator?
8. Distinguish between job rotation and job enrichment.
9. Calculate absenteeism for 30 staff who lost 36 days out of 20 working days each.
10. Name two non-financial methods that could meet esteem needs.

### Answers

1. Any two from security, social contact, status, job satisfaction, using skills.
2. Improved labour productivity, reduced absenteeism, lower labour turnover, greater willingness to accept change or new methods of working.
3. 35 × 11 = **385**.
4. 6 ÷ 48 × 100 = **12.5%**.
5. Hygiene factors.
6. Self-actualisation.
7. Taylor.
8. Rotation moves workers between tasks at the same level. Enrichment gives more responsibility and challenge.
9. 36 ÷ (30 × 20) × 100 = **6%**.
10. Any two from praise, employee of the month, promotion opportunities.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving only one reason for why people work, or only "money".
- Describing a method without applying it to the business in the stem.
- Confusing a bonus with commission or with profit sharing.
- Writing that hygiene factors motivate.
- Leaving out units or the working in a pay calculation.
- Describing job rotation as a promotion.
- Listing advantages only, with no disadvantage or limit.
- Recommending a method with no reason why it beats the alternative.
