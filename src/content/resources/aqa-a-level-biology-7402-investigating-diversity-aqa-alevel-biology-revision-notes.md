## Four ways to compare genetic diversity

Within or between species, compare:

1. the **frequency of measurable or observable characteristics**;
2. the **base sequence of DNA**;
3. the **base sequence of mRNA**;
4. the **amino acid sequence** of the proteins encoded by DNA and mRNA.

| Method | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Characteristics | Easy, cheap, no technology | Environment affects them; many genes; DNA differences inferred, not seen |
| Amino acid sequence | Closer to the genes | Degenerate code hides some DNA differences |
| mRNA sequence | Shows coding bases actually transcribed | Only expressed genes; introns removed |
| DNA sequence | Direct; detects all base differences | One gene is a small part of the genome |

**Gene technology change:** inferring DNA differences from characteristics → replaced by direct investigation of DNA sequences.

## Sequence comparison routine

1. Line up sequences position by position.
2. Count differences for each pair.
3. Convert: differences ÷ total positions × 100.
4. Fewest differences → most closely related (most recent common ancestor).
5. Most differences from all others → diverged earliest.
6. State the limitation: short sequence, one gene or one protein.

**Key link:** degenerate code → different DNA can give the same amino acid sequence, so DNA comparisons are more sensitive than protein comparisons.

## Within a species

- More different base sequences (alleles) at a gene → higher genetic diversity.
- Small, isolated populations tend to show fewer different sequences.

## Random sampling

- Every individual or position has an equal chance of selection.
- Methods: random coordinates on a grid; numbered individuals chosen with a random number generator.
- Same sample size in each population; large samples.
- Same measuring method each time.
- Purpose: avoid bias.

## Mean and standard deviation

- **Mean** = sum of values ÷ number of values.
- **Standard deviation (s.d.)** = spread of values around the mean.
- You interpret s.d.; you do not calculate it in the written papers.
- About 95% of values in a normal distribution lie within ± 2 s.d. of the mean.

| Observation | Interpretation |
|---|---|
| Large s.d. | Wide spread; much variation |
| Small s.d. | Values close to mean; little variation |
| ± 1 s.d. ranges overlap | Difference between means may be due to chance |
| No overlap | Difference more likely real; needs a statistical test |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Genetic diversity | The number of different alleles of genes in a population |
| Random sample | A sample in which every individual has an equal chance of selection |
| Mean | Average value |
| Standard deviation | Measure of spread of data about the mean |
| Error bar | Line on a graph showing variability, often ± 1 s.d. |
| Common ancestor | Species from which two or more later species evolved |

## Exam traps

- Four methods, not just DNA.
- Same amino acid sequence ≠ same DNA.
- s.d. is spread about the mean, not the range.
- Overlapping error bars: "may be due to chance", not "no difference".
- Non-overlap does not prove significance.
- "Random" means a random method, not "chosen without thinking".
- Quote both means and both s.d. values in comparisons.

## Answer frames that score

- **Sequence data:** "Species A and B differ at 2 of 40 bases (5%), the fewest of any pair, so they probably share the most recent common ancestor."
- **Method choice:** "DNA comparison is more reliable than comparing the protein because the code is degenerate, so some base differences do not change the amino acid."
- **Standard deviations:** "The means differ by 0.7 mm, but the ± 1 s.d. ranges overlap, so the difference may be due to chance; population X is more variable as its s.d. is larger."

## Quick self-test

1. Name the four ways of comparing genetic diversity given in the specification.
2. Why is comparing observable characteristics a weak method?
3. Why might two species have identical amino acid sequences for a protein but different DNA?
4. How has gene technology changed investigations of diversity?
5. Two species differ at 6 of 48 bases. Calculate the percentage difference.
6. Describe how to take a random sample of plants in a field.
7. Why should samples be large?
8. What does a large standard deviation show?
9. The ± 1 s.d. ranges of two means overlap. What can you conclude?
10. Calculate the mean of 4.2, 3.8, 4.5, 4.1 and 3.9.
11. Why does comparing many genes give a better estimate of relatedness than comparing one?
12. Must you calculate a standard deviation in the written papers?

### Answers

1. Frequency of measurable or observable characteristics; DNA base sequence; mRNA base sequence; amino acid sequence of proteins.
2. Characteristics are affected by the environment and often by many genes, so genetic differences are only inferred.
3. The code is degenerate: different triplets can code for the same amino acid.
4. DNA differences are now investigated directly by sequencing instead of being inferred from characteristics.
5. 6 ÷ 48 × 100 = **12.5%**.
6. Grid the field; use a random number generator to choose coordinates; sample the plant at each coordinate (for example with a quadrat).
7. So the sample is representative and the mean is less affected by unusual individuals.
8. Values are widely spread around the mean: high variation.
9. The difference between the means may be due to chance.
10. 20.5 ÷ 5 = **4.1**.
11. One gene may not represent the whole genome; many genes give more data and a more reliable estimate.
12. No; you interpret it.
