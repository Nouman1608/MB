**These notes condense Cambridge 9489 AS topic 2, Liberalism and nationalism in Germany, 1815-71, for fast recall.** The four key questions are the causes of the 1848 revolutions (2.1), what happened in 1848-49 and with what consequences (2.2), why Bismarck was appointed and what he aimed for up to 1866 (2.3), and how and why unification was achieved by 1871 (2.4). The full narrative is in the [Germany, 1815-71 study guide](/resources/a-level-world-history-germany-1815-71-study-guide/); exam-style questions are in the [Germany, 1815-71 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-world-history-9489-liberalism-nationalism-germany-practice/).

**Rotation (2027-2029 syllabus):** Paper 2 (Outline Study) in 2027 and 2029; Paper 1 (Historical Sources) in 2028.

## Two threads, one topic

| | Liberalism | Nationalism |
|---|---|---|
| Aim | Constitutions, rights, a free press, elected parliaments controlling taxation | One German nation-state |
| Social base | Professional and commercial middle class, students, some officials | Students, writers, gymnasts, later businessmen wanting one market |
| Main enemy | Princes ruling without constitutions | The division of Germany into sovereign states |
| Fate by 1871 | Subordinated: the Reich Chancellor answered to the Kaiser | Achieved in *kleindeutsch* form, without Austria |

Most exam questions reward you for showing that the two could pull apart -- above all in 1866, when liberals forgave Bismarck's unconstitutional rule because he had delivered national success.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Confederation (*Bund*) | Loose league of 39 sovereign states created in 1815 |
| Diet (*Bundestag*) | Assembly of state envoys at Frankfurt, not an elected parliament |
| *Burschenschaften* | Nationalist student societies, banned in 1819 |
| Carlsbad Decrees | Censorship, university supervision and a central investigating commission |
| Zollverein | Customs union led by Prussia, excluding Austria |
| *Grossdeutsch* / *kleindeutsch* | Greater Germany including Austria / Lesser Germany without it |
| Vorparlament | Pre-parliament of March-April 1848 that arranged elections to Frankfurt |
| Fundamental Rights | Frankfurt's declaration of civil rights, December 1848 |
| Olmütz | 1850 agreement in which Prussia gave way to Austria |
| *Realpolitik* | Politics based on power and interest rather than principle |
| Indemnity Bill | 1866 law approving Bismarck's spending without a budget |
| National Liberals | Liberals who backed Bismarck after 1866 |
| Bundesrat | Federal council of state governments in the 1867 and 1871 constitutions |

## Key question summaries

**2.1 Causes of 1848.** Repression (Carlsbad, the 1832 Articles) suppressed but did not destroy liberal and national ideas; the middle class grew with trade and railways; the Zollverein linked the economies of the states without uniting them politically; the crisis of 1846-47 brought hunger, unemployment and artisan distress; the February revolution in Paris was the spark.

**2.2 1848-49.** Early success came because rulers were taken by surprise. Failure came because the revolutionaries were divided (liberals against radicals; *grossdeutsch* against *kleindeutsch*), Frankfurt had no army or money, the armies stayed loyal to the princes, and Friedrich Wilhelm IV would not accept a crown from a parliament. Consequences: Austrian recovery, the humiliation of Olmütz, and a widening economic gap as Prussia grew and Austria struggled.

**2.3 Bismarck to 1866.** He was appointed to win the army-budget conflict. He kept collecting taxes, then used foreign policy -- Denmark, Gastein, Biarritz, Italy -- to isolate Austria. Victory in 1866 converted much of the liberal opposition.

**2.4 Unification.** The southern states resisted Prussian leadership; French fear of Prussian power provided the national cause that overcame that resistance. Luxemburg humiliated France; the Hohenzollern candidature and Ems Telegram brought a war that France declared and Germany won.

## Exam traps

- **"Bismarck unified Germany."** Weigh Prussian economic strength, Moltke's army, Austrian and French mistakes and nationalist opinion.
- **The Zollverein as a direct cause.** It created economic ties, but the 1868 Zollparlament showed the south was not politically pro-Prussian.
- **Frankfurt "talked too much".** Name the structural reasons: no army, dependence on Prussia over Schleswig-Holstein, fear of the radicals.
- **Planned or improvised?** For 1866 and 1870, give evidence both ways -- the Italian alliance suggests planning; the Hohenzollern affair suggests opportunism.
- **Paper 1.** Prussian official statements, liberal newspapers and French accounts each have obvious purposes; explain them.

## Self-test

1. What did the Carlsbad Decrees introduce? *Censorship, supervision of universities, a ban on student societies and an investigating commission.*
2. Which event of 1832 prompted the Six Articles? *The Hambach Festival.*
3. Why did the Zollverein benefit Prussia politically as well as economically? *It drew the smaller states into Prussian-led trade and kept Austria out.*
4. Give two economic causes of the 1848 revolutions. *Any two: harvest failures of 1846-47; high food prices; unemployment; urban overcrowding; artisans undercut by factory goods.*
5. Why did the Schleswig-Holstein question weaken the Frankfurt Parliament in 1848? *It depended on the Prussian army, and Prussia signed the Malmö armistice without consulting it.*
6. What did Friedrich Wilhelm IV refuse in April 1849, and why? *The imperial crown, because it was offered by a parliament rather than by fellow princes.*
7. What was agreed at Olmütz? *Prussia abandoned its union plan and the Confederation was restored under Austrian leadership.*
8. What caused the Prussian constitutional crisis of 1861-62? *Liberal refusal to approve the budget for army reforms proposed by Wilhelm I and Roon.*
9. What did the Convention of Gastein do? *Gave Prussia the administration of Schleswig and Austria that of Holstein.*
10. What two diplomatic steps prepared war with Austria? *The Biarritz meeting with Napoleon III and the secret alliance with Italy.*
11. Why was the Indemnity Bill significant? *Most liberals accepted Bismarck's past unconstitutional spending, putting national success ahead of parliamentary principle.*
12. What concession kept Bavaria in the Empire? *Any one: its own army in peacetime; its own postal, telegraph and railway systems.*
