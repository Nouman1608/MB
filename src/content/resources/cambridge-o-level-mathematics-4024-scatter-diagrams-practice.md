These questions cover section 9.5, Scatter diagrams, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. All data is invented. Where a line of best fit is needed, the question gives two points on it so that every answer can be checked; in the exam you would draw and read your own line. The methods are in the [scatter diagrams study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-scatter-diagrams/), and the [scatter diagrams revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-scatter-diagrams-revision-notes/) list the rules.

## Section A

**1.** State the type of correlation you would expect between

**(a)** the outside temperature and the amount of heating fuel used,

**(b)** a person's height and their arm span,

**(c)** a person's house number and their age. **[3]**

**2.** Write down two features of a line of best fit given in the syllabus notes. **[2]**

**3.** For a group of adults, a line of best fit on a scatter diagram of mass (kg) against height (cm) passes through (150, 40) and (190, 80). Use it to estimate the mass of an adult who is 172 cm tall. **[2]**

**4.** Using the line in question 3, estimate the height of an adult with mass 55 kg, and interpret the gradient of the line. **[3]**

**5.** The table shows the ages and reaction times of six people.

| Age (years) | 20 | 30 | 40 | 50 | 60 | 35 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reaction time (ms) | 210 | 225 | 240 | 255 | 270 | 400 |

Identify the outlier and suggest a reason for it. **[2]**

**6.** A scatter diagram shows strong positive correlation between the number of fire engines sent to fires and the cost of the damage. A reporter says that sending more fire engines causes more damage. Comment on this claim. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** The table shows the distance from a city centre, x km, and the price, p thousand dollars, of eight similar houses.

| x (km) | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 8 | 9 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| p ($ thousand) | 310 | 285 | 270 | 240 | 230 | 205 | 170 | 160 |

**(a)** Describe the correlation in context.

**(b)** A line of best fit is drawn through (0, 320) and (10, 140). Show that its gradient is −18 and explain why it is a suitable line.

**(c)** Estimate the price of a similar house 7 km from the centre.

**(d)** Estimate the distance from the centre of a similar house priced at 250 thousand dollars. **[7]**

**8.** Use the line of best fit in question 7.

**(a)** Interpret the gradient of the line in context.

**(b)** Use the line to estimate the price of a similar house 25 km from the centre, and comment on your answer. **[3]**

**9.** Tariq draws a line of best fit by joining the first and last points of his scatter diagram with a ruler. Seven of the other eight points lie above his line. Explain what is wrong and how he should improve it. **[2]**

**10.** A scatter diagram of students' heights against their marks in a spelling test shows zero correlation. Explain whether a line of best fit would be useful. **[2]**

**11.** Two estimates are made from scatter diagrams.

Estimate A uses a diagram with strong correlation and a value inside the data range.

Estimate B uses a diagram with weak correlation and a value beyond the largest value in the data.

Which estimate is more reliable? Give two reasons. **[3]**

**12.** The table shows the engine size, e litres, and fuel used, f litres per 100 km, for seven cars.

| e | 1.0 | 1.2 | 1.4 | 1.6 | 1.8 | 2.0 | 1.2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| f | 5.0 | 5.5 | 6.0 | 6.5 | 7.0 | 7.5 | 9.5 |

**(a)** Identify the outlier.

**(b)** Without the outlier, the points lie on a straight line. Find its gradient and the value of f it gives when e = 1.5.

**(c)** Explain how including the outlier would affect a line of best fit. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **Negative** [1] **(b)** **Positive** [1] **(c)** **Zero** [1]

**2.** Any two of: a single ruled line drawn by inspection; it extends across the full data set; a roughly even distribution of points on either side along its length [2].

**3.** Gradient = (80 − 40) ÷ (190 − 150) = 1 [1]. Mass = 40 + 1 × (172 − 150) = **62 kg** [1].

**4.** 55 = 40 + (h − 150), so **h = 165 cm** [2] (one mark for a correct method). Each extra centimetre of height is associated with about **1 kg more mass** on average [1].

**5.** **(35, 400)** is the outlier [1]. For example, the person may have been distracted, or the time was recorded wrongly [1].

**6.** The data shows correlation, not cause [1]. Larger fires need more engines and also cause more damage, so the size of the fire explains both [1].

**7. (a)** **Negative correlation**: houses further from the centre tend to be cheaper [1].

**(b)** (140 − 320) ÷ (10 − 0) = −180 ÷ 10 = −18 [1]. The line covers the full data set, with points on both sides along its length (four above, three below, one on it) [1].

**(c)** 320 − 18 × 7 = 320 − 126 = **194 thousand dollars** [1].

**(d)** 250 = 320 − 18x [1], so 18x = 70 [1] and **x ≈ 3.9 km** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In the exam you read these values from your own line; small differences from the mark scheme are allowed if they match your line.

**8. (a)** Each extra kilometre from the centre is associated with a fall of about **18 thousand dollars** in price [1].

**(b)** 320 − 18 × 25 = **−130 thousand dollars** [1], which is impossible: 25 km is outside the data (1 to 9 km), so extrapolation is unreliable [1].

**9.** A line of best fit need not pass through the end points, and his points are not balanced, as most are above it [1]. He should draw a line, by eye, with roughly as many points above as below along its whole length [1].

**10.** **Not useful** [1]: with zero correlation there is no linear relationship, so the line would give meaningless estimates [1].

**11.** **Estimate A** [1]. Strong correlation means points lie close to the line [1], and interpolation within the data range is more reliable than extrapolation beyond it [1].

**12. (a)** **(1.2, 9.5)** [1].

**(b)** Gradient = (7.5 − 5.0) ÷ (2.0 − 1.0) = 2.5 [1]. At e = 1.5: f = 5.0 + 2.5 × 0.5 = **6.25** [1].

**(c)** The outlier lies far above the others [1], so it would pull the line upwards (especially near e = 1.2), giving estimates that are too high for typical cars [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Correlation named without a context sentence.
- Lines of best fit through the end points or the origin by habit.
- Lines that do not cover the full data set.
- Outliers left in when placing the line.
- Extrapolated estimates given without comment.
- Treating correlation as proof of cause.
