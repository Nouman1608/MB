These questions cover section 3.8.2, Electric field strength, of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630), which is International A-level content. Section A has short questions; Section B has longer, structured questions. If a point is unfamiliar, read the [electric field strength study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-field-strength-oxfordaqa/) or the [electric field strength revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electric-field-strength-oxfordaqa-revision-notes/) first.

Use 1/(4πε₀) = 8.99 × 10⁹ N m² C⁻², e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, electron mass 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg and g = 9.81 N kg⁻¹. Ignore the weight of subatomic particles.

## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Define electric field strength. **[2]**

**2.** Show that the unit V m⁻¹ is equivalent to N C⁻¹. **[2]**

**3.** Which statement about electric field lines is correct?

A: They show the path a charged particle follows.

B: They point in the direction of the force on a negative charge.

C: They are closer together where the field is stronger.

D: They can cross where two fields overlap.

**[1]**

**4.** Two parallel plates are 2.5 cm apart with a p.d. of 400 V between them.

**(a)** Calculate the field strength between the plates. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the force on an alpha particle (charge +2e) between the plates. **[2]**

**5.** A small sphere carries a charge of −15 nC.

**(a)** Calculate the magnitude of the field strength 0.30 m from its centre, and state its direction. **[2]**

**(b)** State the field strength 0.60 m from its centre. **[1]**

**6.** Describe the field-line pattern around two equal positive point charges, and state where the neutral point is. **[2]**

### Section B

**7.** Charges of +8.0 nC and +2.0 nC are 0.40 m apart.

**(a)** Calculate the resultant field strength at the midpoint and state its direction. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the distance from the +8.0 nC charge of the neutral point between them. **[2]**

**8.** Starting from the work done in moving a charge Q between two parallel plates a distance d apart with p.d. ΔV, derive E = ΔV/d. **[3]**

**9.** An electron enters the space midway between two horizontal parallel plates at 3.0 × 10⁷ m s⁻¹, moving parallel to them. The plates are 6.0 cm long and 2.0 cm apart, and the p.d. between them is 180 V.

**(a)** Calculate the acceleration of the electron between the plates. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time the electron spends between the plates. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate its sideways displacement on leaving the plates. **[2]**

**(d)** State whether the electron hits a plate, giving a reason. **[1]**

**(e)** Calculate the angle between its path and its original direction as it leaves the plates. **[2]**

**10.** A proton and an alpha particle (charge +2e, mass four times the proton mass) enter the same uniform field at the same speed, at right angles to the field. Show that the alpha particle's sideways displacement over the same distance is half the proton's. **[2]**

**11.** Two charges, each +2.0 nC, are 0.080 m apart. Point P is 0.030 m from the midpoint of the line joining them, on the perpendicular bisector, so it is 0.050 m from each charge. Calculate the resultant field strength at P and state its direction. **[4]**

**12.** A small charged ball of mass 2.0 g hangs on an insulating thread between two vertical parallel plates 0.10 m apart. When a p.d. of 5.0 kV is applied, the thread settles at 4.0° to the vertical. Calculate the charge on the ball. **[4]**

**13.** The field strength 0.10 m from the centre of a charged sphere is 3.6 × 10⁵ N C⁻¹. Calculate the distance from the centre at which it is 4.0 × 10⁴ N C⁻¹. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The force per unit charge [1] on a small positive test charge placed at that point [1]. **[2]**

**2.** V = J C⁻¹, so V m⁻¹ = J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ [1]; J = N m, so J C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = N m C⁻¹ m⁻¹ = **N C⁻¹** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** **C** [1]. **[1]**

**4. (a)** E = 400/0.025 = **1.6 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹** [1]. **[1]**

**(b)** F = QE = 2 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 1.6 × 10⁴ [1] = **5.1 × 10⁻¹⁵ N** [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** E = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 15 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.30² = **1.5 × 10³ N C⁻¹** [1], directed towards the sphere [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** The distance doubles, so E falls to a quarter: **3.7 × 10² N C⁻¹** (375 N C⁻¹) [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Lines leave each charge radially and bend away from each other between them, with arrows pointing away from the charges [1]; the neutral point is midway between the charges, where no lines pass [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** E from +8.0 nC = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 8.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.20² = 1.80 × 10³ N C⁻¹; E from +2.0 nC = 4.50 × 10² N C⁻¹ [1]. They point in opposite directions, so subtract [1]: **1.3 × 10³ N C⁻¹, directed away from the +8.0 nC charge** (towards the +2.0 nC charge) [1]. **[3]**

**(b)** 8.0/x² = 2.0/(0.40 − x)², so 2/x = 1/(0.40 − x) [1]; x = 0.80/3 = **0.27 m** [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Work done = force × distance = Fd [1]; work done = charge × p.d. = QΔV, so Fd = QΔV [1]; substitute F = QE: QEd = QΔV, so E = ΔV/d [1]. **[3]**

**9. (a)** E = 180/0.020 = 9.0 × 10³ V m⁻¹ [1]; a = eE/m = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 9.0 × 10³ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = **1.6 × 10¹⁵ m s⁻²** [1]. **[2]**

**(b)** t = 0.060 / 3.0 × 10⁷ = **2.0 × 10⁻⁹ s** [1]. **[1]**

**(c)** y = ½at² = ½ × 1.58 × 10¹⁵ × (2.0 × 10⁻⁹)² [1] = **3.2 × 10⁻³ m** [1]. **[2]**

**(d)** No: it can move 1.0 cm before reaching a plate and moves only 0.32 cm [1]. **[1]**

**(e)** Sideways speed = at = 1.58 × 10¹⁵ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ = 3.2 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ [1]; angle = tan⁻¹(3.16 × 10⁶ / 3.0 × 10⁷) = **6.0°** [1]. **[2]**

**10.** Acceleration = QE/m: for the alpha particle (2e)E/(4m) = ½ × eE/m, half the proton's [1]. The time in the field is the same (same speed and distance), so with y = ½at² the displacement is also half [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Field from each charge = 8.99 × 10⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁻⁹ / 0.050² = 7.19 × 10³ N C⁻¹ [1]. Components along the line joining the charges cancel by symmetry [1]. Components along the bisector add: 2 × 7.19 × 10³ × (0.030/0.050) [1] = **8.6 × 10³ N C⁻¹, along the bisector, away from the line joining the charges** [1]. **[4]**

**12.** E = V/d = 5000/0.10 = 5.0 × 10⁴ V m⁻¹ [1]. In equilibrium QE = mg tan θ [1]. Q = 2.0 × 10⁻³ × 9.81 × tan 4.0° / 5.0 × 10⁴ [1] = **2.7 × 10⁻⁸ C** [1]. **[4]**

**13.** E ∝ 1/r², and E has fallen by a factor of 9 [1], so r rises by a factor of 3: **0.30 m** [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out "positive" or "unit" from the definition of field strength.
- Subtracting fields from unlike charges at a point between them, or adding fields from like charges.
- Taking the full plate gap as the room available for deflection when the particle enters midway.
- Rounding the acceleration early in a trajectory question, then losing the final mark.
- Giving the direction of the force on an electron along the field instead of against it.
- Using E = V/d for a sphere or point charge.
