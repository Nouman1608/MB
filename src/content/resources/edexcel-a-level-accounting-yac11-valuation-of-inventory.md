## What the specification asks for

| Outcome | Wording | What you must be able to do |
|---|---|---|
| 1.4.1 | Characteristics of LIFO, FIFO and NRV | Describe each method, state when it is suitable and its advantages and disadvantages |
| 1.4.2 | Valuations using FIFO and LIFO, perpetual and periodic | Value issues and closing inventory from a record of receipts and issues |
| 1.4.3 | Effect on profit and statements of financial position | Compare results under each method and explain the reasons |

Inventory affects profit twice. It is deducted in the cost of sales (a higher closing inventory gives a higher profit) and it is a current asset in the statement of financial position. The valuation method therefore changes both statements.

## The characteristics of each method

**First In First Out (FIFO).** Items are assumed to be issued in the order they were received. The oldest cost is charged to cost of sales first. Closing inventory is therefore valued at the **most recent** prices.

- Advantages: it follows the physical flow of most goods, especially perishable ones; closing inventory is close to current replacement cost; it is accepted for financial reporting.
- Disadvantage: in a period of rising prices, cost of sales uses old, lower costs, so profit is higher and may include a holding gain that cannot all be spent.

**Last In First Out (LIFO).** The most recently received items are assumed to be issued first. Cost of sales reflects **recent** prices and closing inventory is valued at the **oldest** prices.

- Advantage: cost of sales is close to current prices, so profit is matched to current costs.
- Disadvantages: closing inventory may be valued at out-of-date prices; it rarely matches physical flow; and International Accounting Standard 2 does not allow LIFO in published financial statements. The specification still requires you to be able to apply and discuss it.

**Net Realisable Value (NRV).** NRV is the selling price less any costs still needed to complete the item and sell it. IAS 2 requires inventory to be valued at the **lower of cost and NRV**, applied to each item or group of similar items. It applies the **prudence** concept, because a loss is recognised when the NRV falls below cost, before the goods are sold. NRV is not a method of costing issues. It is a test applied to the cost found by FIFO or LIFO.

## Perpetual and periodic systems

- **Perpetual:** the inventory record is updated after every receipt and issue, so the cost of each issue and the balance are always known.
- **Periodic:** issues are not costed as they occur. At the end of the period, the closing inventory is counted and valued, and cost of sales is found as opening inventory + purchases − closing inventory.

For **FIFO** the two systems give the same answer, because the oldest items are always the first out. For **LIFO** they can differ: perpetual LIFO takes the latest items available *at the time of each issue*, while periodic LIFO assumes the closing inventory is made of the *oldest* items in the whole period.

## Worked example: Norfolk Paint Co

Norfolk Paint records the movement of one product in March.

| Date | Movement | Units | Cost per unit |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 Mar | Opening inventory | 100 | 8 |
| 5 Mar | Purchase | 200 | 9 |
| 12 Mar | Issue | 150 | |
| 18 Mar | Purchase | 150 | 10 |
| 24 Mar | Issue | 220 | |
| 28 Mar | Purchase | 100 | 11 |

Total units available 550, issued 370, closing inventory **180 units**. Total cost of goods available = 800 + 1,800 + 1,500 + 1,100 = 5,200.

### FIFO (perpetual and periodic give the same result)

- 12 March issue of 150: 100 at 8 + 50 at 9 = 800 + 450 = **1,250**.
- 24 March issue of 220: the remaining 150 at 9 + 70 at 10 = 1,350 + 700 = **2,050**.
- Cost of sales = 1,250 + 2,050 = **3,300**.
- Closing inventory: 80 at 10 + 100 at 11 = 800 + 1,100 = **1,900**.

Check: 3,300 + 1,900 = 5,200.

### LIFO perpetual

- 12 March issue of 150: from the latest receipt, 150 at 9 = **1,350**. Left: 100 at 8 and 50 at 9.
- 18 March purchase adds 150 at 10.
- 24 March issue of 220: 150 at 10 + 50 at 9 + 20 at 8 = 1,500 + 450 + 160 = **2,110**.
- Cost of sales = 1,350 + 2,110 = **3,460**.
- Closing inventory: 80 at 8 + 100 at 11 = 640 + 1,100 = **1,740**.

Check: 3,460 + 1,740 = 5,200.

### LIFO periodic

The closing 180 units are taken from the oldest layers: 100 at 8 + 80 at 9 = 800 + 720 = **1,520**. Cost of sales = 5,200 − 1,520 = **3,680**.

### Summary and effect on profit

Suppose the 370 units were sold at 15 each, giving sales of 5,550.

| Method | Cost of sales | Gross profit | Closing inventory |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| FIFO | 3,300 | 2,250 | 1,900 |
| LIFO perpetual | 3,460 | 2,090 | 1,740 |
| LIFO periodic | 3,680 | 1,870 | 1,520 |

Purchase prices rose during the month (8, 9, 10, 11). With rising prices, **FIFO gives the highest profit and the highest inventory value**; LIFO gives the lowest. The differences are 3,300 − 3,680 = 380 in cost of sales between FIFO and periodic LIFO, which is the same as the difference in closing inventory (1,900 − 1,520 = 380). Total costs are the same over the life of the business. The methods only change when costs are charged. If prices fall, the effect reverses.

**Effect on the statement of financial position.** A higher closing inventory increases current assets, working capital and the current ratio. It also increases profit and so the owner's capital.

## Applying NRV

Norfolk Paint finds that 30 tins from the closing 180 were damaged. They cost 9 each. They can be sold for 8 each after repairs costing 3 each.

NRV = 8 − 3 = 5 per tin. Cost is 9, so the lower value is 5.

Write-down = (9 − 5) × 30 = **120**. The closing inventory is reduced by 120 and cost of sales (or an expense) increases by 120. The 150 other tins stay at cost because their NRV, based on a selling price of 15, is higher than cost.

## Choosing a method in the exam

Use the data in the question. State the method and keep it consistently in an answer, since **consistency** requires the same method from year to year. When asked to **compare** or **recommend**, link three things: the price trend, the effect on profit and the effect on the statement of financial position.

For example: "In a period of rising prices, FIFO gives a higher gross profit than LIFO because cost of sales uses older, lower prices. This increases tax and distributions but gives a statement of financial position inventory figure closer to current replacement cost."

## Common mistakes

- Taking the wrong end of the stack: FIFO issues from the oldest layer and LIFO from the newest.
- Costing LIFO periodic as if it were perpetual.
- Splitting a layer wrongly, such as costing 70 units from a layer when only 50 remain.
- Forgetting to carry forward the remaining units after each issue in a perpetual record.
- Applying NRV to the selling price alone, without deducting costs to complete and sell.
- Applying the lower of cost and NRV to the total inventory instead of item by item.
- Stating that LIFO always gives lower profit. It does so only when prices rise.
- Not checking that cost of sales plus closing inventory equals the cost of goods available.

## Exam technique

- Set the record out as a table with columns for receipts, issues and balance. The layers on hand after each transaction are the working that earns marks.
- Calculate closing inventory both ways and check the total of cost of sales plus closing inventory equals cost of goods available.
- **Explain** questions need a linked reason, such as "because prices rose, the oldest and cheapest units are charged first under FIFO".
- **Evaluate** or **recommend** needs both sides and a conclusion about which method suits the business.
