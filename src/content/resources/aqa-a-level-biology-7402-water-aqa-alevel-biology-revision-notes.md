## The root cause

- Water is **polar**: oxygen δ−, hydrogens δ+.
- δ+ hydrogen of one molecule attracts δ− oxygen of another: a **hydrogen bond**.
- Each hydrogen bond is **weak**, but there are **many**, constantly breaking and re-forming.

## The five specification properties

| Property | Cause | Biological importance |
|---|---|---|
| Metabolite | Takes part in reactions | Used in hydrolysis; produced in condensation |
| Solvent | Polarity | Metabolic reactions occur in solution; transport of dissolved substances |
| Relatively high heat capacity | Energy breaks hydrogen bonds before temperature rises | Buffers temperature changes in organisms and habitats |
| Relatively large latent heat of vaporisation | Many hydrogen bonds broken per molecule evaporated | Cooling (sweat, transpiration) with little loss of water |
| Strong cohesion | Hydrogen bonds between water molecules | Supports water columns in xylem; surface tension where water meets air |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Polar molecule | Has an uneven distribution of charge, with δ+ and δ− ends |
| Hydrogen bond | Weak attraction between δ+ hydrogen and δ− oxygen of neighbouring molecules |
| Metabolite | A substance that takes part in metabolic reactions |
| Solvent | A liquid in which other substances dissolve |
| Heat capacity | Energy needed to raise the temperature of a given mass by a given amount |
| Latent heat of vaporisation | Energy needed to change liquid into vapour without a temperature change |
| Cohesion | Attraction between molecules of the same substance |
| Surface tension | Elastic "skin" effect where water meets air |

## What dissolves and what does not

| Dissolves | Does not dissolve |
|---|---|
| Ions (Na⁺, Cl⁻, phosphate) | Triglycerides |
| Polar molecules (glucose, amino acids) | Fatty acid tails of phospholipids |

Transport media: plasma (glucose, ions, urea), xylem (mineral ions), phloem (sucrose).

## Calculation reminders

- Energy = mass × specific heat capacity × temperature change (values supplied).
- Mass evaporated = energy to be lost ÷ latent heat per gram (values supplied).

Mini reminder with new numbers: a leaf must lose 36 kJ by transpiration; latent heat 2.4 kJ g⁻¹ gives 36 ÷ 2.4 = 15 g of water. Air ranging from 4 to 28 °C (24 °C) beside a lake ranging from 10 to 14 °C (4 °C) shows the air range is 6 times larger.

## Exam traps

- "High heat capacity so it heats up fast." It heats up slowly.
- Confusing heat capacity with latent heat.
- "Hydrogen bonds are strong." Each is weak; many together matter.
- "Water is released in hydrolysis." It is used.
- Omitting "with little loss of water" for latent heat.
- Explaining surface tension without hydrogen bonds.
- Saying lipids dissolve in water.
- Describing data with no figures.

## Self-test

1. Explain why a water molecule is polar.
2. What is a hydrogen bond between water molecules?
3. Give one reaction in which water is used and one in which it is produced.
4. Why do ions dissolve in water?
5. Why do triglycerides not dissolve in water?
6. Explain why water's high heat capacity is important for an aquatic organism.
7. Explain why sweating cools the body with little loss of water.
8. How does cohesion help water move up the xylem?
9. What produces surface tension?
10. A mammal must lose 960 kJ by evaporation. Latent heat is 2.4 kJ g⁻¹. Calculate the mass of water evaporated.
11. Why are enzyme-controlled reactions in cells more stable because cells are mostly water?
12. Name two substances transported dissolved in blood plasma.

### Answers

1. Oxygen attracts the shared electrons more strongly, so oxygen is slightly negative and the hydrogens slightly positive.
2. A weak attraction between the δ+ hydrogen of one molecule and the δ− oxygen of another.
3. Used: hydrolysis (of a polymer or ATP). Produced: condensation (or ATP resynthesis, or aerobic respiration).
4. Polar water molecules surround each ion, with the oppositely charged end facing it, separating the ions.
5. They are non-polar, so water molecules are not attracted to them.
6. The water temperature changes slowly, so the organism experiences a stable temperature and its enzymes work at a steady rate.
7. Many hydrogen bonds must break for each molecule to evaporate, so a lot of energy is removed per gram; a large cooling effect needs only a small mass of water.
8. Hydrogen bonds hold water molecules together, so a continuous column is pulled up as water evaporates from the leaves.
9. Hydrogen bonds pulling surface molecules inward and sideways where water meets air.
10. 960 ÷ 2.4 = 400 g.
11. Water's high heat capacity buffers temperature changes, so enzymes work at a more constant temperature.
12. Any two: glucose, ions, urea, amino acids.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which properties of water does AQA A-level Biology list?**
Water is a metabolite, an important solvent, has a relatively high heat capacity, a relatively large latent heat of vaporisation, and strong cohesion producing surface tension.

**What causes most of water's properties?**
Hydrogen bonding between polar water molecules.

**Is surface tension the same as cohesion?**
Surface tension is produced by cohesion where water meets air: hydrogen bonds pull the surface molecules together.
