> syllabus for exams in 2027, 2028 and 2029.

Topic 6 of Cambridge O Level Environmental Management (5014) deals with four natural hazards:
earthquakes and volcanoes, tropical cyclones, flooding and drought. Every hazard is examined in the same
order - where and why it happens, what it does to people and the environment, and how its impacts are
managed before, during and after the event - so a single answer framework works for all four.

Full explanations: [natural hazards study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-environmental-management-natural-hazards-study-guide/).
Test yourself: [natural hazards practice questions](/resources/o-level-environmental-management-natural-hazards-practice/).

## One framework for every hazard

1. **Where and why** - location and causes.
2. **Impacts** - sort into people (deaths, health, homes, money) and environment (habitats, crops,
   water, land).
3. **Before** - monitoring and warning, land use zoning, building structure, disaster preparation.
4. **During** - evacuation, shelters.
5. **After** - rebuilding, international aid.
6. **Judge** - benefit and limitation of each strategy; which matters most.

## 6.1 Earthquakes and volcanoes

**Inside the Earth**: crust, mantle, core. Heat drives **convection currents** in the mantle, which move the
tectonic plates.

**Seven continents**: Africa, Antarctica, Asia, Europe, North America, Oceania, South America.

**Where hazards occur**
- Along plate margins, where plates move apart, together or past each other.
- Over hotspots, where volcanoes form in the middle of a plate.

| Plate boundary | What the plates do | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Divergent / constructive | Move apart; magma rises into the gap | Volcanoes and earthquakes |
| Convergent / destructive | Collide; one subducts | Earthquakes and a chain of volcanoes |
| Conservative | Slide past | Earthquakes, no volcanoes |

**Earthquake vocabulary**: energy released suddenly; **focus** below ground; **epicentre** on the surface
above it; size given on the **Moment Magnitude Scale**.

**Eruption vocabulary**: magma → lava at the surface; ash; water vapour, carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide;
acid rain; volcanic bombs; pyroclastic flows.

**Tectonic impacts**: buildings and infrastructure; crops, livestock, habitats; evacuation; fire; tsunamis;
landslides; contaminated drinking water; water-related disease; financial losses; health.

**Why people live near volcanoes**: fertile soils; minerals; building materials; geothermal power; tourism.

## 6.2 Tropical cyclones

- Also called storms, hurricanes or typhoons.
- **Conditions**: 5° to 30° latitude north or south of the Equator; sea at least 27 °C; ocean at least 60 m
  deep.
- **Impacts**: buildings and infrastructure; crops, livestock and habitats; evacuation; flooding;
  landslides; contaminated water and disease; financial and health effects.
- **Management**: monitoring and warning; building structure; disaster preparation; evacuation; shelters;
  rebuilding; international aid. (Land use zoning is not listed for cyclones in 6.2.3.)

## 6.3 Flooding

| Cause | How it increases flood risk |
|---|---|
| Land relief | Low-lying land and flat floodplains collect water |
| Saturated, compacted or eroded soil | Water cannot infiltrate, so run-off increases |
| Deforestation, farming practices, urbanisation | Less interception; impermeable surfaces |
| Climate change | Sea-level rise; more extreme rainfall |
| Storm surges and tsunamis | Sea water pushed inland |

**Flood opportunities**: fertile silt on farmland; recharge of surface and ground water stores.

## 6.4 Drought

**Drought** = a period of dry weather that is longer or more severe than normal. Causes: lack of rain;
climate change (higher temperatures, more extreme weather).

**Impacts chain**: rivers and wells dry → crops and livestock die → famine and financial loss → soil
exposed → soil erosion and desertification → dust lowers air quality; vegetation dries → wildfire risk;
organisms die; human health suffers.

**Management**: monitoring and warning; emergency water supplies; water conservation; increasing supply
(dams and reservoirs, aquifers and wells, desalination, rainwater harvesting); international aid.

## Benefits and limitations at a glance

| Strategy | Benefit | Limitation |
|---|---|---|
| Monitoring and warning | Saves lives through early action | Needs equipment and communication; earthquakes give almost no warning |
| Land use zoning | Fewer people in high-risk areas | Pressure on land; informal settlements ignore rules |
| Building structure | Buildings withstand shaking or wind | Costly to build and retrofit |
| Disaster preparation | Faster, organised response | Drills need repeating; supplies expire |
| Evacuation and shelters | Removes people from danger | Not everyone can or will leave |
| Rebuilding | Restores communities | Slow, costly; may repeat the risk |
| International aid | Resources beyond local capacity | Delays; may not reach remote areas |
| Emergency water and increasing supply | Keeps people alive in drought | Costly; over-pumping lowers water tables |

## Worked example: classify the strategy

A source lists: "seismometers installed", "schools retrofitted with cross-bracing", "families given
emergency kits", "rescue teams flown in from abroad".

- Seismometers → monitoring (before).
- Retrofitting → building structure (before).
- Emergency kits → disaster preparation (before).
- Rescue teams from abroad → international aid (after).

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Calling the epicentre the place where the earthquake starts.
- Writing "hurricanes form over warm water" without the 27 °C, 60 m and 5-30° figures.
- Claiming droughts are caused by deforestation: the syllabus gives lack of rain and climate change.
- Mixing impacts on people with impacts on the environment when the question separates them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are there no volcanoes at conservative boundaries?**
The plates slide past each other without one
being destroyed or pulled apart, so no magma is produced.

**Which management strategies are listed only for drought?**
Emergency water supplies, water conservation
and increasing water supply through dams and reservoirs, aquifers and wells, desalination and rainwater
harvesting.

**Are floods always harmful?**
No. Floods deposit nutrient-rich silt on farmland and recharge surface and
ground water stores, which the syllabus lists as opportunities.
