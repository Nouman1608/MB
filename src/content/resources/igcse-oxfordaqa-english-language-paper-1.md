This guide covers **Paper 1 Literary Non-fiction and Composition**,
one component of OxfordAQA International GCSE English Language (9270),
which overlaps with AQA's UK GCSE English Language (8700). Students
take Paper 1 plus either Paper 2 (examined) or a non-exam assessment
project as an alternative route.

## Where this fits in 9270

Paper 1 pairs close reading and analysis of literary non-fiction with
students' own composition writing, establishing both the analytical
and creative writing skills that the specification draws on
throughout. An optional Speaking and Listening endorsement is reported
separately and does not count toward the qualification grade.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE ENGLISH LANGUAGE (9270) — PAPER 1
LITERARY NON-FICTION AND COMPOSITION**

- Section A: Literary Non-fiction — analysing an unseen literary
non-fiction text, focusing on how writers use language and structure
to achieve effects
- Section B: Composition — producing an original piece of descriptive
or narrative writing

## How to approach it

Section A rewards precise, evidence-based analysis -- practise
identifying specific language and structural choices in unseen
non-fiction texts and explaining their effect, rather than describing
content in general terms. Section B benefits from planning before
writing: a strong composition typically has a clear structure and a
consistent, deliberate voice, so practise planning openings and
endings in particular, since these are where marks are often won or
lost. Because Section A tests reading and Section B tests writing,
build revision time for both independently rather than assuming skill
in one transfers automatically to the other. Reading widely in the
literary non-fiction genre -- journalism, memoir, travel writing --
before the exam helps with Section A, since familiarity with the
genre's typical techniques makes unseen texts less unfamiliar on the
day, while regularly timed composition practice for Section B builds
the pacing and control needed to complete a well-structured piece
within the paper's time limit.
