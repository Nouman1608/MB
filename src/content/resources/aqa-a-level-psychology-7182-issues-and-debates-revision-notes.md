These notes condense the [issues and debates study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-psychology-7182-issues-and-debates/), so read that first if a term here is new. They follow section 4.3.1 of the AQA AS and A-level Psychology (7181, 7182) specification, Version 1.4 (1 September 2025), for teaching from September 2025. The content is compulsory and is assessed in Paper 3, Issues and Options in Psychology (2 hours, 96 marks, 33.3% of the A-level).

Test yourself with the [issues and debates practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-psychology-7182-issues-and-debates-practice/). The [7182 course hub](/boards/aqa/a-level/psychology/) and the [7182 checklist](/checklists/aqa/a-level/psychology/) cover the rest of the course.

## Paper 3 in one box

- Written exam, 2 hours, 96 marks, 33.3% of A-level.
- Section A: multiple choice, short answer and extended writing, 24 marks.
- Sections B, C and D: one topic from option 1 (Relationships, Gender, Cognition and development), one from option 2 (Schizophrenia, Eating behaviour, Stress) and one from option 3 (Aggression, Forensic Psychology, Addiction), 24 marks each.
- The specification says you should illustrate issues and debates answers with topics "studied elsewhere in the specification".

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Universality | A finding or theory that applies to all people, whatever their gender or culture |
| Gender bias | Research or theory that misrepresents one sex, often women |
| Androcentrism | Treating male behaviour as the normal standard |
| Alpha bias | Exaggerating differences between the sexes |
| Beta bias | Ignoring or minimising differences between the sexes |
| Cultural bias | Judging all people by the norms of one culture |
| Ethnocentrism | Seeing your own culture's behaviour as normal or superior |
| Cultural relativism | Behaviour can only be understood in its own cultural context |
| Imposed etic | Using a tool from one culture as if it were universal |
| Hard determinism | All behaviour is caused; free will is an illusion |
| Soft determinism | Behaviour is caused, but some conscious choice remains within limits |
| Biological determinism | Behaviour caused by genes, hormones or the brain |
| Environmental determinism | Behaviour caused by learning and reinforcement |
| Psychic determinism | Behaviour caused by unconscious forces and early experience |
| Heredity | Genetic characteristics passed from parents to children |
| Interactionist approach | Nature and nurture work together to produce behaviour |
| Diathesis-stress model | A vulnerability becomes a disorder only when triggered by stress |
| Holism | Behaviour must be studied as a whole |
| Reductionism | Behaviour is explained by breaking it into smaller parts |
| Levels of explanation | Socio-cultural, psychological and biological ways of explaining one behaviour |
| Environmental (stimulus-response) reductionism | Behaviour reduced to learned stimulus-response links |
| Idiographic approach | In-depth study of the individual; qualitative |
| Nomothetic approach | Study of groups to find general laws; quantitative |
| Socially sensitive research | Research with potential social consequences for participants or the group they represent |

## Studies and theories at a glance

| Who | What | Finding or claim | One evaluation point |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freud | Theory of moral development | Girls develop a weaker superego | Alpha bias; based on case studies, hard to test |
| Taylor et al. (2000) | Review of stress research | "Tend and befriend" more typical of females | Shows beta bias in earlier male-based research |
| Gilligan (1982) | Critique of Kohlberg | Kohlberg's male-based theory undervalued care reasoning | Jaffee and Hyde (2000) found only small sex differences |
| Berry (1969) | Etic and emic | Warned against imposed etics | Useful framework for judging cross-cultural studies |
| Bond and Smith (1996) | Meta-analysis, 133 Asch-type studies, 17 countries | Higher conformity in collectivist countries | Asch's US findings are not universal |
| Henrich, Heine and Norenzayan (2010) | Review of research samples | Most samples are "WEIRD" | Questions universality of many findings |
| Libet et al. (1983) | Brain activity and decisions | Readiness potential before conscious decision | Simple lab movements, not real-life choices |
| Bouchard et al. (1990) | Identical twins reared apart | Around 70% of IQ variation associated with genetic variation | Twins may have shared similar environments |
| Allport (1937) | Personality | Made the idiographic-nomothetic distinction widely known in personality psychology | Shows both approaches have a place |
| Sieber and Stanley (1988) | Socially sensitive research | Four points where issues arise | Researchers cannot control how findings are used |

## Must-know distinctions

- **Alpha vs beta bias.** Alpha *adds* or exaggerates difference; beta *removes* or hides difference.
- **Androcentrism vs ethnocentrism.** One takes men as the norm; the other takes one culture as the norm.
- **Hard vs soft determinism.** Both say behaviour has causes. Only soft determinism leaves room for choice.
- **Three types of determinism.** Biological (inside the body), environmental (outside, through learning), psychic (unconscious mind).
- **Heritability vs inheritance.** Heritability describes variation in a population, not how much of you is genetic.
- **Biological vs environmental reductionism.** One reduces to physiology; the other to stimulus-response links.
- **Idiographic vs nomothetic.** One person in depth vs general laws from many.
- **Ethical issues vs social sensitivity.** Ethical issues concern the participants; social sensitivity also covers the wider group and how findings are used.

## Linking each debate to your other topics

The specification expects examples from elsewhere in the course. Learn at least one per debate.

| Debate | Example from another topic | How to use it |
|---|---|---|
| Gender bias | Research on stress responses (Taylor et al., 2000) | Beta bias: male samples generalised to females |
| Cultural bias | Ainsworth's Strange Situation; Asch's conformity research | Imposed etic; Bond and Smith (1996) show conformity varies by culture |
| Biological determinism | Genetic and neural explanations of OCD | Behaviour as caused by the body; supports drug treatment |
| Environmental determinism | The two-process model of phobias | Fear caused by conditioning, not chosen |
| Psychic determinism | Freud's psychodynamic approach | Unconscious conflict drives behaviour |
| Soft determinism | Cognitive approach to depression | Thoughts are shaped by experience but can be changed |
| Nature | Bowlby's monotropic theory of attachment | Attachment as innate, with a critical period |
| Nurture | Learning theory of attachment | Attachment learned through conditioning |
| Interactionism | Diathesis-stress model | A vulnerability plus a trigger |
| Biological reductionism | Neural explanations of OCD | Explains at the level of brain structures |
| Environmental reductionism | Behaviourist explanations of phobias | Reduces fear to a stimulus-response link |
| Holism | Humanistic psychology; conformity in groups | The person or group as a whole |
| Idiographic | Freud's case studies | One person in depth |
| Nomothetic | Milgram's obedience research | General laws from many participants |
| Social sensitivity | Bowlby's maternal deprivation theory | Used in debate about whether mothers should work |

When you use an example, say which side of the debate it supports and add one evaluation point. A named example with no link back to the debate earns little.

## Mini essay plan: "Evaluate the use of reductionist explanations of behaviour." (extended response)

1. **Define (AO1).** Reductionism and holism; the three levels of explanation.
2. **Biological reductionism (AO1 + example).** Genetic and neural explanations of OCD; drug treatment.
3. **Environmental reductionism (AO1 + example).** Behaviourist explanation of phobias as conditioned links.
4. **Evaluation 1 (AO3).** Reductionism produces testable hypotheses and practical treatments.
5. **Evaluation 2 (AO3).** It can miss meaning and context; conformity in Asch's studies is a group effect.
6. **Evaluation 3 (AO3).** Holism (humanistic psychology) fits real experience but is hard to test.
7. **Judgement.** Different levels answer different questions; an interactionist or multi-level view is most useful.

## Quick self-test

1. Define androcentrism.
2. Which bias minimises differences between men and women?
3. Give one example of alpha bias.
4. What is an imposed etic?
5. Name the three types of determinism in the specification.
6. How does soft determinism differ from hard determinism?
7. What does a heritability coefficient of 0.8 tell you?
8. Give one interactionist example.
9. Name the three levels of explanation.
10. What is environmental (stimulus-response) reductionism?
11. Which approach uses case studies to study one person in depth?
12. Name the four points Sieber and Stanley (1988) said social sensitivity can arise.

### Answers

1. Treating male behaviour and thinking as the normal standard.
2. Beta bias.
3. Freud's claim that girls develop a weaker superego than boys.
4. Using a research tool from one culture as if it applied in all cultures.
5. Biological, environmental and psychic determinism.
6. Both say behaviour is caused; soft determinism allows some conscious choice within those causes.
7. In that population, about 80% of the variation in the trait is associated with genetic variation. It says nothing about one person.
8. The diathesis-stress model, or PKU, where a genetic condition's effects depend on diet.
9. Socio-cultural, psychological and biological (physical).
10. Explaining behaviour as learned links between a stimulus and a response, as in behaviourism.
11. The idiographic approach.
12. The research question; the conduct of the research; the institutional context; interpretation and application of findings.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a debate in a short-answer question but giving no example, when the question says "using an example".
- Using "determinism" without saying which type: biological, environmental or psychic.
- Calling all male-only samples alpha bias. Applying male findings to women without checking is beta bias.
- Describing cultural relativism as "different cultures are different" without the point about judging behaviour in its own context.
- Treating heritability as a percentage of a person rather than of variation in a population.
- Listing reductionist explanations without explaining what is lost by reducing them.
- Writing generic ethics (consent, debriefing) for a social sensitivity question instead of consequences for the wider group.
- Giving one-sided extended answers. Paper 3 weights AO3 most heavily, so each debate needs evaluation and a judgement.
- Forgetting the specification's instruction to illustrate with other topics. Each point needs a named topic or study.
