## Section A

**1.** Define the term *organic growth*. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** methods of measuring the size of a business other than the number of employees. **[2]**

**3.** Bexley Freight has 220 employees and revenue of $14m. Nordale Analytics has 18 employees and revenue of $3.5m.

**(a)** Calculate revenue per employee for each business. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why number of employees may be an inappropriate measure for comparing these two businesses. **[2]**

**4.** Explain **one** weakness of family businesses. **[2]**

**5.** Distinguish between a joint venture and a strategic alliance. **[2]**

**6.** Two hardware chains have sales of $5.6m and $2.8m in a market worth $40m. They merge.

**(a)** Calculate the market share of each before the merger and of the combined business, assuming sales do not change. **[3]**

**(b)** Identify the type of integration. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain **two** reasons why small businesses are important to an economy. **[4]**

**8.** Lanyard Logistics plc offers $36m for Hollin Couriers Ltd, which is valued at $30m. Lanyard expects cost savings of $1.5m a year from combining depots.

**(a)** Calculate the premium as a percentage of Hollin's valuation. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate how many years the savings take to recover the premium. **[1]**

**(c)** If savings are only $0.9m a year, calculate the new figure and explain **one** reason why savings might be lower than expected. **[3]**

**9.** Analyse how a hostile takeover of Hollin Couriers might affect **two** stakeholder groups. **[6]**

**10.** Teo's Bakery Ltd, a family business, wants to open a delivery route costing $200 000. It retains $50 000 of profit a year. A bank would lend the full amount at 8% interest a year.

**(a)** Calculate how long organic growth from retained profit would take and the annual interest on the loan. **[2]**

**(b)** Advise Teo's Bakery whether to finance the route from retained profit or the loan. **[6]**

**11.** Evaluate whether growth by takeover is likely to be more successful than organic growth for a medium-sized manufacturer. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** Expansion of a business using its own resources [1], for example opening new outlets or launching new products, rather than joining with another business [1].

**2.** Any two: sales revenue [1]; capital employed [1]; market share; market capitalisation (for a public limited company).

**3. (a)** Bexley: $14 000 000 ÷ 220 ≈ **$63 600** [1]. Nordale: $3 500 000 ÷ 18 ≈ **$194 400** [1].

**(b)** Nordale uses a few highly skilled staff, so it generates far more revenue per worker [1]; counting employees makes Bexley look over twelve times larger, which exaggerates the difference compared with revenue, which is only four times larger [1].

**4.** Senior posts may go to family members rather than the most capable managers [1], so decision-making quality and growth may suffer (or succession problems, or family disputes, similarly explained) [1].

**5.** A joint venture creates a separate business or project jointly owned by the partner firms [1]; a strategic alliance is an agreement to cooperate in which the firms stay independent and no new business is created [1].

**6. (a)** First chain: 5.6 ÷ 40 × 100 = **14%** [1]. Second: 2.8 ÷ 40 × 100 = **7%** [1]. Combined: **21%** [1].

**(b)** Horizontal integration [1].

**7.** Small businesses provide a large share of jobs, often in local communities and areas large firms avoid [1], which reduces unemployment and spreads income across regions [1]. They bring new ideas and products to market and keep pressure on larger firms through competition [1], which helps keep prices down and quality up for consumers [1]. (Credit their role in supply chains or as a route into enterprise, similarly explained.)

**8. (a)** Premium = $36m - $30m = $6m [1]; 6 ÷ 30 × 100 = **20%** [1].

**(b)** 6 ÷ 1.5 = **4 years** [1].

**(c)** 6 ÷ 0.9 ≈ **6.7 years** [1]. Savings may be lower because of a culture clash between the two workforces [1], which reduces cooperation and delays the closure of duplicated depots (or loss of key staff, or customers switching) [1].

**9.** Employees of Hollin: duplicated depots and management roles may be cut to deliver the $1.5m savings [1], so some staff lose jobs and others face uncertainty [1], which may lower motivation and productivity during integration [1]. Hollin's shareholders: they receive $36m, a 20% premium over the valuation [1], so they gain immediately [1], which is why a hostile bid may succeed even when the directors oppose it [1]. (Credit customers, suppliers or the community, similarly analysed.)

**10. (a)** $200 000 ÷ $50 000 = **4 years** [1]; interest = 8% × $200 000 = **$16 000 a year** [1].

**(b)** Loan: the route starts now, so revenue begins four years earlier and a rival cannot take the opportunity first [1]. The $16 000 interest is affordable if the route adds more than this to profit [1]. Retained profit: no interest, no repayment risk and no lender's conditions [1], which suits a family business that values control and stability [1]. Advice: if forecasts show the route will cover the interest comfortably and competition is strong, the loan is better [1]; if demand is uncertain, waiting and using retained profit protects the family firm from debt [1].

**11.** For takeover: much faster, giving immediate access to new markets, products or capacity [1]; can remove a competitor and increase market share [1]; can bring economies of scale and new skills [1]. Against takeover: a premium is usually paid, so the deal must deliver large savings [1]; culture clash and loss of key staff often prevent expected savings arriving [1]; finance may require heavy borrowing or a share issue that dilutes control [1]. For organic growth: lower risk, builds on existing strengths and culture [1]; can be financed from retained profit, keeping control [1]. Against organic growth: slow, so competitors may take opportunities first [1]. Success depends on the price paid and the fit between the businesses [1], and on how quickly the manufacturer needs to grow [1]. Judgement: organic growth is more likely to succeed for a medium-sized firm with limited management capacity, unless a well-priced target with a strong strategic fit is available [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing businesses in different industries using one measure only.
- Calculating the premium as a percentage of the bid price instead of the valuation.
- Listing stakeholder impacts without developing the consequence for the business.
- Giving advice without stating what the decision depends on.
