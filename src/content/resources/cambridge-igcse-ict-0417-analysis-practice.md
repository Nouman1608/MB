## Section A: short answers

**1.** State **two** research methods an analyst could use to investigate a current system, other than observation. **[2]**

**2.** Give **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage of examining existing documents. **[2]**

**3.** Explain the difference between a closed question and an open question in a questionnaire. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by *information requirements*. **[1]**

**5.** Give **two** reasons why an interviewee might not give accurate answers. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** Riverside Dental Practice books appointments using a paper diary. Two receptionists take phone calls, and the practice manager has run the diary for 15 years.

**(a)** Explain why an interview would be a suitable method for finding out about the manager's knowledge of the diary system. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe how the analyst would use observation at reception, and give **one** problem with this method in this practice. **[3]**

**7.** For the dental practice:

**(a)** Identify **two** inputs, **one** process and **two** outputs of the current paper system. **[5]**

**(b)** The analyst finds that patients are sometimes booked into the same slot twice. State a user requirement for the new system that addresses this problem. **[1]**

**8.** Northgate Bus Company has 350 drivers working early, late and night shifts at four depots. It wants their views on a new shift-swapping system.

**(a)** Explain why a questionnaire is more suitable than interviews for the drivers. **[3]**

**(b)** Give **two** features of a well-designed questionnaire. **[2]**

**9.** An analyst studying a college's paper enrolment forms finds the fields Name, Date of birth, Address, Course code and Signature.

**(a)** Explain how examining these forms will help in later stages of the systems life cycle. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **one** limitation of relying only on the enrolment forms. **[1]**

**10.** A garden centre will introduce a computerised point-of-sale and stock system. It sells about 3000 different products, many of them plants without packaging.

Identify **three** items of hardware or software the new system will need and justify each one. **[6]**

**11.** Explain why a systems analyst should use more than one research method when investigating a current system. **[3]**

**12.** Compare observation and questionnaires as methods of researching the current system at a busy hospital pharmacy. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: interviews [1]; questionnaires [1]; examination of existing documents [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**2.** Advantage, any one of: shows exactly what data is collected and its format; shows the volume of data; does not disturb staff [1]. Disadvantage, any one of: documents may be out of date or incomplete; they do not show how the system is actually used; can be time-consuming to read [1]. **[2]**

**3.** A closed question gives fixed answers to choose from, such as yes/no or a rating scale, so answers are quick to analyse [1]; an open question lets the person answer in their own words, giving more detail but taking longer to analyse [1]. **[2]**

**4.** The information that the new system must produce, such as reports or lists [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two of: they may say what they think the analyst or their manager wants to hear [1]; they are not anonymous, so they may hide problems or mistakes [1]; they may forget details or describe what should happen rather than what does [1]; they may feel nervous or under pressure [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Only one person holds this detailed knowledge, so interviewing one person is practical [1]; the analyst can ask follow-up questions about how the diary is used and its problems [1].

**(b)** The analyst sits at reception and watches the receptionists taking calls and writing bookings, recording what they do, how long it takes and any errors [1]; this shows what actually happens, for example how a free slot is found [1]. Problem, any one: receptionists may work more carefully while watched / busy periods or rare problems may not occur while the analyst is there / it takes a lot of the analyst's time [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** Inputs, any two: patient name; phone number; requested date or time; reason for appointment [2]. Process, any one: searching the diary for a free slot; writing the booking in; crossing out a cancellation [1]. Outputs, any two: daily appointment list for each dentist; appointment card for the patient; reminder call list [2].

**(b)** For example: the system must not allow two patients to be booked into the same slot with the same dentist [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** There are 350 drivers, so interviewing all of them would take too long [1]; they work different shifts at four depots, so a questionnaire can be completed when convenient without arranging meetings [1]; answers can be anonymous and closed answers analysed quickly, especially if online [1].

**(b)** Any two of: short and clear questions [1]; mostly closed questions with tick boxes or scales [1]; a small number of open questions for comments [1]; questions in a logical order [1]; clear instructions and a return date [1]; option to answer anonymously [1]. Maximum 2. **[5]**

**9. (a)** The fields show which data items the new system must store, so they help design the file structure, such as field names and data types [1]; the form layout helps design the new data capture form or input screen [1].

**(b)** Any one of: the forms do not show how staff process them / may be out of date / may be filled in incorrectly, so the true data needs are unclear [1]. **[3]**

**10.** One mark for each item and one mark for each justification linked to the scenario. Examples:
- Barcode scanner [1], so packaged products are entered quickly and accurately at the till [1].
- Touch screen at the till with picture buttons [1], because plants have no packaging or barcode, so staff can select them by picture [1].
- Database or stock control software [1], to store details and stock levels of 3000 products and reorder automatically [1].
- Receipt printer [1], to give customers a printed record of each sale [1].
- Card reader [1], so customers can pay by card or contactless [1].
Maximum 6 (three items with justifications). **[6]**

**11.** Each method has weaknesses, for example observation does not show why things are done [1]; using several methods means the weaknesses of one are covered by another [1]; findings can be checked against each other, so the analyst gets a more complete and accurate picture of the current system [1]. **[3]**

**12.** Up to 6 marks for points about both methods, for example:
- Observation lets the analyst see how prescriptions are actually processed, including delays [1].
- Observation may disturb a busy pharmacy and staff may change their behaviour when watched [1].
- Observation is time-consuming and covers only the shifts the analyst attends [1].
- A questionnaire can be completed by all pharmacy staff, including those on night shifts [1].
- Questionnaire answers can be anonymous and closed questions analysed quickly [1].
- Questionnaires may have low response rates from busy staff, and answers cannot be followed up [1].
- Conclusion: use both, observing the process and using a questionnaire to collect views from all shifts [1].
Maximum 6; points must cover both methods for full marks. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I justify a research method?**
Pick a fact from the scenario, such as the number of staff, shifts or sites, and say why the method suits it.

**What counts as a justification for hardware?**
A reason that comes from the organisation's needs, such as the volume of sales or the need to share data, not a general statement that it is "fast" or "good".
