Condensed recall for **10.1 Global trends in consumption** (Unit 3: Global resource consumption and
security). For the full explanation and worked examples, read the
[Global resource consumption study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-geography-global-resource-consumption-study-guide/).

## Resource distinctions

- **Renewable** (replenish naturally within a human timescale) versus **non-renewable** (finite).
- **Stock** versus **flow** resources.
- **Natural capital** versus **human-made** capital.
- The three focus resources are **food, water and energy**, with attention to land and minerals as
  inputs.
- Consumption is shaped by population, income, technology and culture.

## Trends

- Total consumption has risen with population growth and rising incomes, particularly in emerging
  economies.
- **Per-capita** consumption is very unequal: higher-income countries generally use far more energy,
  water and food (including meat) per person, though the gap has narrowed in places.
- Diets shift toward animal products as incomes rise; these need more land and water per unit of
  food energy.
- Energy demand is still dominated by **fossil fuels**, though renewables have grown quickly in many
  countries.
- Water use is dominated by **agriculture**, then industry and domestic use.

## Footprints

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ecological footprint | Productive land and water needed to supply consumption and absorb waste |
| Biocapacity | The area available |
| Ecological deficit | Footprint greater than biocapacity |
| Virtual water | Water embedded in traded goods |

**Worked example (invented figures):** population 5 million, footprint 3.2 gha and biocapacity 1.8
gha per person. Footprint = 16 million gha; biocapacity = 9 million gha; deficit = 7 million gha;
ratio = 16 / 9, about 1.78.

## Describing a trend from a graph

State the overall direction, quote start and end values with units, identify rates of change, note
anomalies and compare regions; then **explain** with reasons such as population growth, urbanisation,
a rising middle class, technology, trade and policy.

## Exam traps

- Describing totals when the question asks about per-capita use, or the reverse.
- Assuming renewable resources cannot be overused; water and forests can be depleted.
- Describing a graph without figures and units.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish renewable from non-renewable resources.
2. Define ecological footprint.
3. Footprint 10 million gha and biocapacity 6 million gha: what is the deficit?
4. Which sector dominates global water use?
5. Give two reasons per-capita use differs between countries.

**Answers:** 1. Renewable resources replenish within a human timescale; non-renewable ones are finite.
2. The area of productive land and water needed to supply consumption and absorb waste. 3. 4 million
gha. 4. Agriculture. 5. For example income and industrial structure.
