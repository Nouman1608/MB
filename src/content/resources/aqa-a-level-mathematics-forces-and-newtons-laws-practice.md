These questions cover **Section R: Forces and Newton's laws (R1 to R6)** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018), for A-level exams from June 2018 onwards. The specification places Section R in **Paper 2**. A calculator is required in every 7357 paper, so every question here is calculator allowed. Take g = 9.8 m s⁻² unless a question says otherwise, and give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures.

## Questions

**1.** A particle moves in a straight line with constant velocity. State what this tells you about the forces acting on it, and name the law you have used. **[2]**

**2.** Two forces, (4i + 7j) N and (−1.5i − 2j) N, are the only forces acting on a particle of mass 2.5 kg. Find the acceleration of the particle, and its magnitude. **[3]**

**3.** A rover has mass 180 kg. At one location g = 9.78 m s⁻², and at another g = 9.83 m s⁻². Find the weight of the rover at each location, and state what happens to its mass. **[3]**

**4.** A parachutist of mass 85 kg falls vertically. At one instant the air resistance is 620 N.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the parachutist at that instant. **[3]**
**(b)** Later the parachutist falls at constant speed. Find the air resistance then, giving a reason. **[2]**

**5.** Particles P, of mass 5 kg, and Q, of mass 3 kg, are attached to the ends of a light inextensible string that passes over a fixed smooth pulley. Both hang vertically and are released from rest.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the particles and the tension in the string. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the speed of P after it has fallen 1.2 m, assuming Q does not reach the pulley. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the force exerted by the string on the pulley. **[2]**

**6.** A crate of mass 30 kg rests on rough horizontal ground. The coefficient of friction is 0.45.

**(a)** A horizontal force of 100 N is applied. Show that the crate does not move, and state the friction force. **[3]**
**(b)** The horizontal force is increased to 180 N. Find the acceleration of the crate. **[2]**

**7.** A particle of mass 6 kg is held in equilibrium on a smooth plane inclined at 25° to the horizontal by a horizontal force of P N, in the vertical plane through a line of greatest slope.

**(a)** Find P. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the normal reaction of the plane on the particle. **[2]**

**8.** Four horizontal forces act on a particle in equilibrium: 8 N in the direction of i; 10 N at 60° anticlockwise from i; P N in the direction of −j; and Q N in the direction of −i. Find P and Q. **[4]**

**9.** A car of mass 1100 kg tows a trailer of mass 500 kg up a straight road inclined at α to the horizontal, where sin α = 1/20, using a light rigid tow bar parallel to the road. Resistances are 400 N on the car and 250 N on the trailer. The acceleration is 0.5 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the driving force of the car. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the tension in the tow bar. **[2]**
**(c)** When the speed is 12 m s⁻¹, the tow bar breaks. The resistance on the trailer stays the same. Find how far the trailer travels up the road before it stops. **[4]**

**10.** A block of mass 3 kg is on a rough plane inclined at 35° to the horizontal. A force of 40 N, acting up a line of greatest slope, pulls the block up the plane with acceleration 1.2 m s⁻².

**(a)** Find the coefficient of friction between the block and the plane. **[5]**
**(b)** The 40 N force is removed when the block is moving up the plane at 2.5 m s⁻¹. Find how much further the block travels up the plane. **[3]**
**(c)** Determine whether the block stays at rest once it has stopped. **[2]**

**11.** Particle A, of mass 4 kg, is on a rough horizontal table, with coefficient of friction 0.3. A light inextensible string joins A, over a smooth pulley at the edge of the table, to particle B, of mass 3 kg, which hangs 0.9 m above the floor. The system is released from rest, and A does not reach the pulley.

**(a)** Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string. **[5]**
**(b)** Find the speed of B when it hits the floor. **[2]**
**(c)** B does not rebound. Find the further distance A travels before it comes to rest. **[3]**

**12.** A box of mass 15 kg rests on the floor of a lift. The lift is moving upwards and slowing down at 0.8 m s⁻². Find the force exerted by the box on the floor, and name the law you use. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The resultant force is zero (the forces balance) [1]. Newton's first law [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "no forces act" earns nothing; the forces have zero resultant.

**2.** Resultant F = (2.5i + 5j) N [1]
a = F ÷ 2.5 = **(i + 2j) m s⁻²** [1]
|a| = √(1² + 2²) = √5 = **2.24 m s⁻²** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* add the force vectors before dividing by the mass; adding their magnitudes loses every mark after the first.

**3.** 180 × 9.78 = 1760.4 = **1760 N** [1]
180 × 9.83 = 1769.4 = **1770 N** [1]
The mass stays **180 kg**; only the weight changes with g [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* the mass comment is a separate mark; a weight in kg loses it.

**4. (a)** Weight = 85 × 9.8 = 833 N [1]
Down positive: 833 − 620 = 85a [1]
a = 213 ÷ 85 = **2.51 m s⁻² downwards** [1]
**(b)** Constant speed, so resultant force is zero (first law) [1]
Air resistance = weight = **833 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* in (b) the reason is credited separately from the value; quote zero resultant or Newton's first law.

**5. (a)** P: 5g − T = 5a [1]
Q: T − 3g = 3a [1]
Add: 2g = 8a, so **a = 2.45 m s⁻²** [1]
T = 3(9.8 + 2.45) = 36.75 = **36.8 N** [1]
**(b)** v² = 0 + 2 × 2.45 × 1.2 = 5.88 [1]
v = **2.42 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** Both sections of string pull down on the pulley: 2T = 2 × 36.75 = **73.5 N** [1] vertically **downwards** [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* treating the system as one body gives a, but T needs one particle's own equation; write a separate equation for each particle.

**6. (a)** R = 30 × 9.8 = 294 N; maximum friction = 0.45 × 294 = 132.3 N [1]
100 N < 132.3 N, so the crate does not move [1]
Friction = **100 N** [1]
**(b)** 180 − 132.3 = 30a [1]
a = 47.7 ÷ 30 = **1.59 m s⁻²** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* for a "show that", compare the applied force with μR explicitly; stating 132.3 N as the friction in (a) loses the final mark.

**7. (a)** Resolve along the plane: P cos 25° = 6g sin 25° [1]
P = 6 × 9.8 × tan 25° [1]
= **27.4 N** [1]
**(b)** Perpendicular: R = 6g cos 25° + P sin 25° [1]
= 53.29 + 11.59 = **64.9 N** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* the horizontal force has a component into the plane, so leaving out the P term gives 53.3 N, which is wrong.

**8.** i: 8 + 10 cos 60° − Q = 0 [1]
**Q = 13** [1]
j: 10 sin 60° − P = 0 [1]
**P = 5√3 = 8.66** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* each resolving equation is a method mark in its own right, so state which direction you are resolving in; 5√3 and 8.66 both answer the question.

**9. (a)** Whole system, up the slope: D − 400 − 250 − 1600g × (1/20) = 1600 × 0.5 [1]
D − 650 − 784 = 800 [1]
**D = 2234 N** [1]
**(b)** Trailer: T − 250 − 500g × (1/20) = 500 × 0.5 [1]
T = 250 + 245 + 250 = **745 N** [1]
**(c)** Trailer alone, down the slope: 250 + 245 = 500d [1]
d = 0.99 m s⁻² (deceleration) [1]
0 = 12² − 2 × 0.99 × s [1]
s = 144 ÷ 1.98 = **72.7 m** [1] **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* in (c) the tension term must go; keeping 745 N in the trailer's equation after the bar breaks loses the method mark.

**10. (a)** R = 3g cos 35° = 24.08 N [1]
Up the plane: 40 − F − 3g sin 35° = 3 × 1.2 [1]
F = 40 − 16.86 − 3.6 = 19.54 N [1]
μ = F ÷ R = 19.54 ÷ 24.08 [1]
= **0.811** [1]
**(b)** Friction now acts down the plane with the weight component: 16.86 + 19.54 = 3d, d = 12.13 m s⁻² [1]
0 = 2.5² − 2 × 12.13 × s [1]
s = **0.258 m** [1]
**(c)** Weight component down the plane 16.9 N < maximum friction 19.5 N [1]
so the block **stays at rest** [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* carry unrounded R and F into μ, or the third figure can drift.

**11. (a)** R = 4g = 39.2 N, so friction on A = 0.3 × 39.2 = 11.76 N [1]
A: T − 11.76 = 4a [1]
B: 3g − T = 3a [1]
Add: 29.4 − 11.76 = 7a, **a = 2.52 m s⁻²** [1]
T = 3(9.8 − 2.52) = **21.8 N** [1]
**(b)** v² = 2 × 2.52 × 0.9 = 4.536 [1]
v = **2.13 m s⁻¹** [1]
**(c)** String slack, so only friction acts on A: deceleration = 0.3g = 2.94 m s⁻² [1]
0 = 4.536 − 2 × 2.94 × s [1]
s = **0.771 m** [1] **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* (c) is a new stage; reusing a = 2.52, or keeping T in A's equation, loses the marks after (b).

**12.** Up positive, a = −0.8: R − 15g = 15 × (−0.8) [1]
R = 15 × 9.0 = 135 N, the floor's force on the box [1]
By **Newton's third law** the box pushes down on the floor with **135 N** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "slowing down while moving up" means the acceleration is downwards; using +0.8 gives 159 N and loses the accuracy mark.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing R = mg on a slope (question 10), or when another force has a component perpendicular to the surface (Q7).
- Taking friction as μR when the body is not moving or about to move (Q6(a)).
- Getting the direction of friction wrong once the motion changes, as in question 10(b).
- Keeping the tension in an equation after a string goes slack or a tow bar breaks.
- Losing the sign of a deceleration in a lift or vertical-motion question.
- Rounding R or F early and carrying the rounded value into μ or a distance.
