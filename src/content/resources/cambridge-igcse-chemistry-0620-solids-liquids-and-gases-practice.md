## Section A

**1.** Which state has a fixed volume but no fixed shape?

A: Solid only

B: Liquid only

C: Gas only

D: Liquid and gas

**[1]**

**2.** The table shows the melting and boiling points of four substances.

| Substance | Melting point / °C | Boiling point / °C |
|---|---|---|
| E | −117 | 78 |
| F | 114 | 184 |
| G | −182 | −162 |
| H | 30 | 2400 |

State the physical state of each substance at 25 °C. **[4]**

**3.** For each of the following, name the change of state and state whether energy is taken in or given out:

**(a)** droplets of water form on a cold window **[1]**
**(b)** molten candle wax turns solid **[1]**
**(c)** wet clothes dry on a washing line. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why a liquid can be poured but a solid cannot. **[2]**

**5.** A sealed syringe contains air. The plunger is pushed in at constant temperature.

**(a)** Describe what happens to the volume of the air. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** *(Extended only)* Liquid Z is cooled from 120 °C. Its temperature falls to 54 °C, stays at 54 °C for 6 minutes, then falls to 20 °C.

**(a)** State the physical state or states of Z at 80 °C, at 54 °C and at 30 °C. **[3]**
**(b)** State the melting point of Z. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the temperature stays at 54 °C for 6 minutes even though energy is being lost to the surroundings. **[2]**

**8.** Use the table in Question 2.

**(a)** Which substance is a liquid over the widest range of temperature? Show your working. **[2]**
**(b)** Which substance or substances are gases at 0 °C? **[1]**
**(c)** Substance F is heated from 25 °C to 150 °C. Name the change of state that takes place. **[1]**

**9.** *(Extended only)* A balloon filled with air is moved from a cold room into a warm room and becomes larger. Explain this in terms of the particles. **[3]**

**10.** Describe how a particle diagram of a liquid should differ from a particle diagram of the same substance as a solid. **[3]**

**11.** *(Extended only)* A solid is heated steadily until it has completely melted. Explain the changes that happen to the particles, using kinetic particle theory. **[4]**

**12.** A fixed mass of gas is kept at constant pressure.

**(a)** Describe what happens to its volume when it is cooled. **[1]**
**(b)** The gas is then kept at constant temperature and the pressure on it is increased. Describe what happens to its volume. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1].

**2.** E: **liquid** [1]. F: **solid** [1]. G: **gas** [1]. H: **solid** [1] (25 °C is below its melting point of 30 °C).

**3. (a)** Condensing (condensation); energy given out [1].

**(b)** Freezing (solidifying); energy given out [1].

**(c)** Evaporating (evaporation); energy taken in [1]. **[3]**

**4.** Particles in a liquid are close together but can move past one another, so the liquid flows and takes the shape of its container [1]. Particles in a solid are held in fixed positions and can only vibrate, so the solid keeps its shape [1].

**5. (a)** The volume decreases [1].

**(b)** Water particles are already close together, touching each other [1], so there is almost no space to push them into; liquids are hardly compressible [1]. **[3]**

**6.** Any two: evaporation happens only at the surface, boiling happens throughout the liquid; evaporation happens below the boiling point, boiling happens at the boiling point; boiling produces bubbles of gas within the liquid, evaporation does not [1] [1].

**7. (a)** 80 °C: liquid [1]. 54 °C: liquid and solid together [1]. 30 °C: solid [1].

**(b)** 54 °C [1].

**(c)** As Z freezes, forces of attraction form between the particles, which gives out energy [1]; this energy replaces the energy lost to the surroundings, so the temperature does not fall until all of Z has frozen [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** E: 78 − (−117) = 195 °C; F: 184 − 114 = 70 °C; G: −162 − (−182) = 20 °C; H: 2400 − 30 = 2370 °C [1]. **H** has the widest liquid range [1].

**(b)** G only [1] (0 °C is above its boiling point of −162 °C; E is liquid at 0 °C).

**(c)** Melting [1]. **[4]**

**9.** In the warm room the particles gain kinetic energy and move faster [1]. They collide with the inside of the balloon more often and with more force [1]. The pressure inside rises above the pressure outside, so the balloon stretches until the pressures balance again, giving a larger volume [1].

**10.** In the liquid, the particles are arranged randomly rather than in a regular pattern [1]. They are still close together, mostly touching, with only slightly larger gaps than in the solid [1]. The particles are the same size in both diagrams [1].

**11.** Energy from heating is transferred to the particles, which vibrate more strongly about their fixed positions [1]. The temperature rises until the melting point is reached [1]. At the melting point the energy supplied is used to overcome the forces of attraction holding the particles in fixed positions, so the temperature stays constant [1]. The particles can then move past one another, and the regular arrangement breaks down to the random arrangement of a liquid [1].

**12. (a)** The volume decreases [1].

**(b)** The volume decreases [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mistakes with negative temperatures when finding a liquid range.
- Saying particles get bigger when a gas is heated.
- Describing particles in a liquid as far apart.
- Giving only one state for the flat section of a heating or cooling curve.
- Writing "describe" answers when the question says "explain", with no mention of energy, forces or collisions.
