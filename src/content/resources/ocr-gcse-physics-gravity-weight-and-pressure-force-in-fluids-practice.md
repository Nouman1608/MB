## Questions

**1.** Describe what is meant by a gravitational field, and explain why you do not notice the gravitational attraction between two people standing next to each other. **[3]**

**2.** A pupil hangs a 0.40 kg mass from a newtonmeter.

**(a)** State the quantity a newtonmeter measures and its unit. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the reading on the newtonmeter. **[2]**

**3.** An astronaut and equipment have a total mass of 80 kg. Gravitational field strengths (supplied): Moon 1.6 N/kg, Mars 3.7 N/kg, Jupiter 25 N/kg.

**(a)** Calculate the total weight on the Moon. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total weight in the gravitational field at the top of Jupiter's atmosphere. **[2]**
**(c)** State the total mass on Mars. **[1]**

**4.** On the Moon an astronaut releases a hammer and a feather together from the same height.

**(a)** Recall the acceleration in free fall near the Earth's surface. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why the hammer and feather land at the same time. **[2]**
**(c)** On Earth a stone falls from rest in a vacuum chamber for 2.5 s. Calculate its speed at the end. **[1]**

**5.** Two balls, one of mass 2.0 kg and one of mass 0.50 kg, are dropped together in a vacuum on Earth.

**(a)** Calculate the weight of each ball. **[2]**
**(b)** Use acceleration = resultant force / mass to calculate the acceleration of each ball. **[2]**

**6.** (Higher Tier only) A probe has weight 54 N on a planet where g = 3.6 N/kg.

**(a)** Calculate the mass of the probe. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the weight of the probe on Earth. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the probe's mass is the same on both planets. **[1]**

**7.** (Higher Tier only) A pupil measures the weight of masses on an unknown planet.

| Mass (kg) | 0.20 | 0.40 | 0.60 | 0.80 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Weight (N) | 0.74 | 1.48 | 2.22 | 2.96 |

**(a)** Show that weight is directly proportional to mass. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the gradient of a graph of weight against mass. **[2]**
**(c)** State what the gradient represents. **[1]**
**(d)** Predict the weight of a 3.0 kg mass on this planet. **[2]**

**8.** (Higher Tier only) A sealed balloon contains air. Explain the direction of the net force of the air on the balloon skin. **[3]**

**9.** (Higher Tier only) A skier has mass 72 kg.

**(a)** Calculate the skier's weight. **[1]**
**(b)** The boots have total contact area 0.032 m² on snow. Calculate the pressure on the snow. **[2]**
**(c)** With snowshoes the contact area is 0.24 m². Calculate the pressure. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain how snowshoes help the skier stay on top of soft snow. **[1]**

**10.** (Higher Tier only) An aircraft door has area 1.5 m². Cabin air pressure is 8.0 × 10⁴ Pa and the outside pressure is 3.0 × 10⁴ Pa.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure difference. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net force on the door. **[2]**
**(c)** State the direction of this force relative to the door. **[1]**

**11.** (Higher Tier only) A garage jack has a small piston of area 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ m² and a large piston of area 4.0 × 10⁻² m². A force of 150 N acts on the small piston.

**(a)** Calculate the pressure in the liquid. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the force on the large piston. **[2]**
**(c)** A car has mass 1200 kg. Decide whether the jack can lift it. **[2]**
**(d)** The small piston moves 0.50 m. Calculate how far the large piston moves. **[1]**

**12.** (Higher Tier only) A lander of mass 900 kg stands on three equal pads, each of area 0.20 m². On Mars g = 3.7 N/kg.

**(a)** Calculate its weight on Mars. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the pressure of the pads on the ground. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the pressure if the same lander stood on the Earth. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** All matter has a gravitational field [1] which causes attraction between masses [1]. The strength is much greater for massive objects, and people have tiny masses so the force is far too small to notice [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers that say only "gravity pulls things down" miss the point that the field belongs to all matter, so link the size of the force to mass.

**2. (a)** Weight (a force), in newtons [1].
**(b)** W = m g = 0.40 × 10 [1] = **4.0 N** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A bare number with no unit is easy to lose credit on, so always write N.

**3. (a)** W = 80 × 1.6 [1] = **128 N** [1].
**(b)** W = 80 × 25 [1] = **2000 N** [1].
**(c)** **80 kg**, since mass does not change [1].
*Examiner insight:* Part (c) is a common trap: writing a different mass for Mars suggests weight and mass are being confused.

**4. (a)** **10 m/s²** (about 9.8 m/s²) [1].
**(b)** There is no air resistance on the Moon [1], so all objects accelerate equally whatever their mass [1].
**(c)** v = 10 × 2.5 = **25 m/s** [1].
*Examiner insight:* An explanation that only says "the Moon has less gravity" does not explain equal landing times, so name the absence of air resistance.

**5. (a)** 2.0 × 10 = **20 N** [1]; 0.50 × 10 = **5.0 N** [1].
**(b)** 20 / 2.0 = **10 m/s²** [1]; 5.0 / 0.50 = **10 m/s²** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Show both substitutions, because the point of the question is that the two results are equal.

**6. (a)** m = W / g [1] = 54 / 3.6 = **15 kg** [1].
**(b)** W = 15 × 10 [1] = **150 N** [1].
**(c)** Mass is the amount of matter, which does not depend on the field [1].
*Examiner insight:* Finding the mass first is a clear route, and working with a ratio of g values also gains credit when the steps are shown.

**7. (a)** Weight / mass is constant at 3.7 for every pair [1].
**(b)** Gradient = change in weight / change in mass = (2.96 − 0.74) / (0.80 − 0.20) [1] = **3.7 N/kg** [1].
**(c)** The gravitational field strength, g [1].
**(d)** W = 3.0 × 3.7 [1] = **11 N** (11.1 N) [1].
*Examiner insight:* Gradient answers should include the unit N/kg, and using widely spaced points reduces the effect of a small reading error.

**8.** Air particles collide with the skin [1]. Each collision pushes at right angles to the skin [1]. The net force is therefore outwards, at right angles to the skin [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Pushes outwards" alone is incomplete; stating that the force is at right angles is the detail the statement asks you to recall.

**9. (a)** W = 72 × 10 = **720 N** [1].
**(b)** p = F / A = 720 / 0.032 [1] = **22 500 Pa** [1].
**(c)** p = 720 / 0.24 = **3000 Pa** [1].
**(d)** The same weight is spread over a larger area, so the pressure is smaller [1].
*Examiner insight:* In part (b), use the weight, not the mass, as the force; using 72 gives the wrong unit as well as the wrong value.

**10. (a)** 8.0 × 10⁴ − 3.0 × 10⁴ = **5.0 × 10⁴ Pa** [1].
**(b)** F = p A = 5.0 × 10⁴ × 1.5 [1] = **75 000 N** [1].
**(c)** Outwards, at right angles to the door [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using the cabin pressure alone ignores the air outside, so show the subtraction as a separate step.

**11. (a)** p = F / A = 150 / (2.0 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = **7.5 × 10⁵ Pa** [1].
**(b)** F = p A = 7.5 × 10⁵ × 4.0 × 10⁻² [1] = **30 000 N** [1].
**(c)** Weight = 1200 × 10 = 12 000 N [1]. The jack gives 30 000 N, which is greater, so it **can** lift the car [1].
**(d)** The area ratio is 200, so the distance is 0.50 / 200 = **0.0025 m** [1].
*Examiner insight:* A comparison in part (c) needs both forces stated with units before the conclusion.

**12. (a)** W = 900 × 3.7 [1] = **3330 N** [1].
**(b)** Total area = 3 × 0.20 = 0.60 m² [1]; p = 3330 / 0.60 = **5550 Pa** [1].
**(c)** W = 900 × 10 = 9000 N, so p = 9000 / 0.60 = **15 000 Pa** [1] [1].
*Examiner insight:* Using the area of one pad instead of all three is a common slip; state the total area explicitly.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving out units, or writing N for mass and kg for weight.
- Using g = 10 on another planet when the question supplies a different field strength.
- Changing the mass when the object moves to another planet.
- Explaining equal fall on the Moon without mentioning air resistance.
- Using mass in kg as the force in p = F / A.
- Using the area of one contact instead of the total area.
- Leaving area in cm² instead of converting to m².
- Forgetting to subtract the outside pressure when fluid acts on both sides of a surface.
- Saying the force from fluid pressure is "along" the surface rather than at right angles to it.
- Treating the hydraulic force gain as free: the small piston moves further.
