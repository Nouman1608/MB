## Section A

**1.** Two coherent sources oscillate in phase. At point X the path difference is 2.5λ. State the phase difference between the waves arriving at X, in radians, and the type of interference that occurs there. **[2]**

**2.** Two loudspeakers are connected so that they oscillate in antiphase. State and explain what is heard at a point that is the same distance from both speakers. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** conditions, other than coherence of the sources, that are needed for clear two-source interference fringes of light to be observed. **[2]**

**4.** In a ripple tank, two dippers are fixed to the same vibrating bar. Explain why they act as coherent sources. **[2]**

**5.** A microwave receiver is at a maximum of an interference pattern. It is moved to the adjacent minimum. The wavelength is 2.8 cm. State the change in path difference. **[1]**

**6.** Light of wavelength 480 nm falls on two slits 0.60 mm apart. Calculate the fringe spacing on a screen 1.80 m away. **[2]**

**7.** In a double-slit experiment, the screen distance D is increased from 1.2 m to 1.8 m and the slit separation is halved. Determine the factor by which the fringe spacing changes. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** A laser illuminates a double slit. On a screen 2.50 m away, the distance from the centre of the 1st bright fringe to the centre of the 9th bright fringe is 12.0 mm. The slit separation is 0.75 mm.

**(a)** Explain why the student measures across several fringes rather than one fringe spacing. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the wavelength of the laser light. **[3]**

**(c)** The uncertainties are ±0.02 mm in the slit separation, ±0.01 m in D and ±0.5 mm in the 12.0 mm measurement. Calculate the absolute uncertainty in the wavelength. **[3]**

**(d)** The laser is replaced by a white-light source and a single slit. Describe the pattern now seen. **[3]**

**9.** Two loudspeakers are connected in phase to one signal generator set to 680 Hz. The speed of sound is 340 m s⁻¹. A microphone at point P is 4.20 m from speaker A and 4.95 m from speaker B.

**(a)** Calculate the wavelength of the sound. **[1]**

**(b)** Determine whether P is at a maximum or a minimum. Show your reasoning. **[3]**

**(c)** The connections to speaker B are reversed. State what is now detected at P. **[1]**

**(d)** Suggest **two** reasons why the sound detected at a minimum is not zero. **[2]**

**10.** A microwave transmitter of wavelength 3.0 cm is directed at a metal sheet containing two narrow gaps whose centres are 12.0 cm apart. A receiver is moved along a line parallel to the sheet, 1.20 m beyond it.

**(a)** Explain how this arrangement produces two coherent sources. **[2]**

**(b)** Use x = λD/a to estimate the distance between adjacent maxima along the line. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why this estimate is only approximate for this arrangement. **[2]**

**(d)** Describe how the receiver could be used to check the wavelength using path differences. **[2]**

**11.** Two coherent sources S₁ and S₂ oscillate in phase and emit waves of wavelength 2.0 cm. Each wave has the same amplitude A at the points considered.

**(a)** Point P is 30.0 cm from S₁ and 35.0 cm from S₂. State, with a reason, the type of interference at P. **[2]**

**(b)** Point Q is 30.0 cm from S₁ and 34.0 cm from S₂. State the resultant amplitude at Q. **[1]**

**(c)** Determine the ratio of the intensity at Q to the intensity due to one source alone at Q. **[2]**

**12.** Explain, using the principle of superposition, how a pattern of bright and dark fringes is produced in a double-slit experiment with light. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Phase difference = 2.5 × 2π = 5π rad (equivalent to π rad) [1]; destructive interference, a minimum [1].

**2.** A minimum (quiet sound) [1], because the path difference is zero, so the waves arrive with the same phase difference they left with, π rad, and cancel [1].

**3.** Any two [1 each]: the light from each slit must overlap (slits narrow enough to diffract the light widely); the two waves must have similar amplitudes, so the dark fringes are dark; the slit separation must be small and the screen distance large, so the fringe spacing is large enough to observe; the waves must be polarised in the same plane (or unpolarised).

**4.** Both dippers are driven by the same bar, so they vibrate at the same frequency [1] and with a constant (fixed) phase difference [1].

**5.** Half a wavelength: 1.4 cm [1].

**6.** x = λD/a = (480 × 10⁻⁹ × 1.80) ÷ (0.60 × 10⁻³) [1] = 1.4 × 10⁻³ m (1.44 mm) [1].

**7.** x ∝ D/a, so the factor is (1.8 ÷ 1.2) × 2 [1] = 3 [1].

**8. (a)** The fringe spacing is small, so measuring one spacing has a large percentage uncertainty [1]; dividing a longer measured distance by the number of spacings reduces the percentage uncertainty [1].

**(b)** From the 1st to the 9th fringe is 8 spacings, so x = 12.0 ÷ 8 = 1.50 mm [1].
λ = ax/D = (0.75 × 10⁻³ × 1.50 × 10⁻³) ÷ 2.50 [1] = 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ m [1].

**(c)** Percentage uncertainties: a: 0.02 ÷ 0.75 = 2.7%; D: 0.01 ÷ 2.50 = 0.4%; distance: 0.5 ÷ 12.0 = 4.2% [1].
Total = 7.3% [1]; absolute uncertainty = 0.073 × 4.5 × 10⁻⁷ = ±0.3 × 10⁻⁷ m [1].

**(d)** A white central fringe [1]; the other bright fringes are coloured, with blue (violet) on the side nearer the centre and red on the outside [1]; fewer fringes are visible because the patterns for different wavelengths overlap further out and merge into uniform illumination [1].

**9. (a)** λ = v ÷ f = 340 ÷ 680 = 0.50 m [1].

**(b)** Path difference = 4.95 − 4.20 = 0.75 m [1]; 0.75 ÷ 0.50 = 1.5 wavelengths [1]; for in-phase sources a path difference of (n + ½)λ gives a minimum, so P is a minimum [1].

**(c)** A maximum (loud sound) [1].

**(d)** Any two [1 each]: P is at different distances from A and B, so the amplitudes arriving there are not equal; sound reflects from walls, floor and ceiling and reaches P by other paths; background noise; the speakers may not emit equal amplitudes.

**10. (a)** Both gaps are illuminated by the same wavefront from one transmitter [1], so the waves leaving them have the same frequency and a constant phase difference [1].

**(b)** x = λD/a = (0.030 × 1.20) ÷ 0.120 [1] = 0.30 m [1].

**(c)** The equation assumes that D is much larger than a, so that the angles to the maxima are small [1]; here the wavelength is a quarter of the gap separation, so the angle to the first maximum is about 14°, too large for the small-angle approximation to be accurate [1].

**(d)** Locate the central maximum and then the first maximum to one side [1]; measure the distance from each gap to the receiver at the first maximum: the difference should equal one wavelength (3.0 cm) [1].

**11. (a)** Path difference = 5.0 cm = 2.5λ [1], so the waves arrive in antiphase: destructive interference (a minimum) [1].

**(b)** Path difference = 4.0 cm = 2λ, so constructive: resultant amplitude 2A [1].

**(c)** Intensity ∝ amplitude², so the ratio is (2A)² ÷ A² [1] = 4 [1].

**12.** The light diffracts at each slit and the two sets of waves overlap; the slits act as coherent sources [1]. Where the waves meet, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements (superposition) [1]. Where the path difference is a whole number of wavelengths, the waves arrive in phase and interfere constructively, giving a bright fringe [1]. Where the path difference is an odd number of half-wavelengths, they arrive in antiphase and interfere destructively, giving a dark fringe [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining coherence without the word *constant*.
- Dividing by the number of fringes instead of the number of spacings.
- Substituting millimetres into λ = ax/D.
- Applying in-phase conditions to sources that are in antiphase.
- Forgetting that intensity depends on amplitude squared.
- Describing fringes without linking them to path difference and phase difference.
