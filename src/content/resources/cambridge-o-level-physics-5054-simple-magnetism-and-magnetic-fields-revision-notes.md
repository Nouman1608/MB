## Forces between poles

| Pair | Force |
|---|---|
| N and N | Repel |
| S and S | Repel |
| N and S | Attract |
| Either pole and unmagnetised iron, steel, nickel or cobalt | Attract |
| Magnet and copper, aluminium, plastic, wood, glass | No force |

**Test for a magnet:** repulsion only. Attraction could be induced
magnetism.

## Induced magnetism

- Magnetic material near a magnet becomes a magnet.
- End nearest the magnet becomes the **opposite** pole → attraction.
- Chains of clips: each clip induces poles in the next.
- Soft iron loses induced magnetism when the magnet is removed; steel
  keeps some.

## Materials

| Magnetic | Non-magnetic |
|---|---|
| Iron, steel, nickel, cobalt | Copper, aluminium, brass, gold, wood, plastic, glass |

## Temporary and permanent magnets

| Property | Soft iron | Steel |
|---|---|---|
| Type | Temporary | Permanent |
| Magnetise | Easily | Less easily |
| Keep magnetism | No | Yes |
| Used for | Electromagnet cores, relays, transformer cores | Bar magnets, compass needles, loudspeaker and motor magnets |

## Magnetic field

- **Definition:** a region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.
- **Direction at a point:** the direction of the force on an N pole there.
- Outside a magnet, lines run **N → S**.
- **Spacing:** closer lines = stronger field. Strongest at the poles.
- Lines never cross.

## Plotting

| Method | Shows pattern? | Shows direction? | Key steps |
|---|---|---|---|
| Plotting compass | Yes | Yes | Dot at needle N end; move tail to dot; repeat; join; add arrows |
| Iron filings | Yes | No | Card over magnet; sprinkle filings; tap gently |

## Bar-magnet field drawing checklist

- Lines leave N, curve round, enter S.
- Arrows on every line, pointing away from N.
- Lines crowded near the poles, spreading out further away.
- Smooth curves, none crossing, none starting in empty space.

## Electromagnets

- Coil of wire carrying a current, usually on a soft-iron core.
- Magnetic only while current flows.
- Strength increases with current (and with a soft-iron core).
- Can be switched on and off.

## Uses

| Permanent magnet | Electromagnet |
|---|---|
| Compass | Scrapyard crane |
| Door catches and fridge seals | Relay |
| Loudspeaker | Electric bell, door lock |
| Motor, generator | Separating iron and steel from waste |

## Answer frames for the common "explain" questions

- **Why is a nail attracted to either pole?** It becomes an induced magnet;
  the end nearest the magnet is always the opposite pole, and unlike poles
  attract.
- **Why does a compass show field direction?** Its needle is a small
  magnet; the force on its N pole acts along the field, so the needle
  points in the field direction.
- **Why a soft-iron core?** Easily magnetised (strong field) and loses its
  magnetism when the current stops (can be switched off).
- **Why steel for a permanent magnet?** Once magnetised, it keeps its
  magnetism.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Magnetic pole | Region of a magnet where the magnetic effect is strongest |
| Magnetised | Having its own N and S poles |
| Unmagnetised | Magnetic material with no poles of its own |
| Induced magnetism | Magnetism produced in a magnetic material by a nearby magnet |
| Magnetic material | A material attracted by a magnet and able to be magnetised |
| Temporary magnet | Loses its magnetism easily (soft iron) |
| Permanent magnet | Keeps its magnetism (steel) |
| Magnetic field | Region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force |

## Exam traps

- Attraction does not prove a magnet; repulsion does.
- Not all metals are magnetic.
- Field direction is defined using the force on an **N** pole.
- Iron filings never give direction.
- Electromagnet cores are soft iron so they **lose** magnetism when
  switched off; "because iron is magnetic" is not enough.
- A compass needle's N pole points **along** the field, towards the S pole
  of the magnet.
- In drawings, arrows S → N outside the magnet lose the direction mark.

## Self-test

1. State what happens when two N poles are brought together.
2. A steel rod is attracted to both ends of a magnet. Is the rod
   magnetised?
3. How would you prove a bar is magnetised?
4. Name four magnetic materials.
5. Explain why a paper clip hanging from a magnet can hold another clip.
6. State one difference between soft iron and steel as magnetic materials.
7. Define a magnetic field.
8. How is the direction of a magnetic field at a point defined?
9. What does the spacing of field lines show?
10. Why can iron filings not be used to find field direction?
11. Give two advantages of an electromagnet over a permanent magnet for a
    crane in a scrapyard.
12. Name one use of a permanent magnet.

**Answers**

1. They repel.
2. No — attraction to both ends shows unmagnetised magnetic material.
3. Bring it near a known magnet; if one end is repelled by a pole, it is
   magnetised.
4. Iron, steel, nickel, cobalt.
5. The first clip becomes an induced magnet and induces magnetism in the
   second, so they attract.
6. Soft iron is easily magnetised but loses its magnetism; steel is harder
   to magnetise but keeps it.
7. A region in which a magnetic pole experiences a force.
8. The direction of the force on an N pole placed at that point.
9. The relative strength of the field: closer lines, stronger field.
10. Each filing lines up along the field but has no marked N end, so the
    pattern shows no direction.
11. It can be switched off to drop the load; its strength can be changed
    by changing the current.
12. Any one: compass needle; door catch; loudspeaker; motor; generator.
