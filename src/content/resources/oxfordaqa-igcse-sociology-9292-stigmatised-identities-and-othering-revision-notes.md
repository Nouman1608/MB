**These notes cover "Stigmatised identities; othering"** in 3.2.2.3 of OxfordAQA International GCSE
Sociology (9292), drawing on two of the sociologists named for 3.2.2, **Goffman** and **Fanon**. They
condense the [stigmatised identities and othering study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-sociology-9292-stigmatised-identities-and-othering/)
and build on the identity material in the [Socialisation revision notes](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-sociology-socialisation-revision-notes/).

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| Stigma | An attribute that is deeply discrediting, so a person is seen as tainted or less than whole (Goffman) |
| Spoiled identity | An identity damaged by stigma |
| Discredited | A person whose stigma is visible or already known |
| Discreditable | A person whose stigma is hidden but could be revealed |
| Passing | Concealing a stigma |
| Covering | Reducing how noticeable a known stigma is in interaction |
| Master status | A label that overrides all other statuses (Becker) |
| Total institution | A place where people live cut off from society under one authority (Goffman) |
| Mortification of the self | Stripping away an inmate's previous identity on entry to a total institution |
| Othering | Defining a group as fundamentally different from and inferior to "us" |
| Internalisation | Coming to accept others' negative view of oneself |
| Orientalism | Said: Western portrayals of "the East" as exotic and backward, supporting colonial power |

## Goffman in four points

- *Stigma: Notes on the Management of Spoiled Identity* (1963).
- Three types: **physical**; **character** (criminal record, addiction, mental illness,
  unemployment); **"tribal"** (race, nation, religion).
- **Discredited** vs **discreditable**; strategies of **passing** and **covering**.
- *Asylums* (1961): **total institutions** and the **mortification of the self**.

## Becker's link

- A deviant label can become a **master status**.
- This can produce a **self-fulfilling prophecy**: exclusion from work and respect makes conformity
  harder.

## Othering in four points

- "Us" and "them": the group is simplified into a stereotype and seen as inferior or threatening.
- It **justifies unequal treatment**.
- Examples: media portrayals of migrants and refugees; colonial "civilising" claims; caste-based
  exclusion, such as of Dalits in India, where "untouchability" was abolished by the 1950 Constitution
  but discrimination persisted.
- Links to **legacies of colonialism and enslavement** (3.2.1.5).

## Fanon in three points

- Psychiatrist born in Martinique.
- *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952): colonised Black people were taught to see themselves through the
  white coloniser's eyes, internalising inferiority.
- *The Wretched of the Earth* (1961): colonialism as a system that dehumanised colonised peoples.

## Why this is social control

| Process | How it controls |
|---|---|
| Stigma | Shame and exclusion discourage behaviour or characteristics a society devalues |
| Master status | Long-term exclusion marks the boundary of acceptable behaviour for others |
| Total institutions | Formal control reshapes identity |
| Othering | Keeps power relationships in place by making inequality seem natural |
| Internalisation | Control works from within, as people accept a devalued view of themselves |

## Perspectives

| Perspective | View of stigma and othering |
|---|---|
| Functionalist | Stigma marks the boundaries of acceptable behaviour and reinforces shared values |
| Marxist | Othering divides the working class; minorities become scapegoats for problems caused by capitalism |
| Feminist | Women are stigmatised for breaking gender norms about dress, sexuality or motherhood |
| Interactionist | Stigma is created and managed in interaction (Goffman); labels can be resisted (Becker) |

## Model paragraph

"Goffman's idea of a spoiled identity helps explain the experience of people leaving prison. A prison
record is a discreditable stigma, so many try passing by concealing it from employers. If it is
revealed, Becker's idea of a master status suggests they will be seen first as 'ex-prisoners', which
can close off work and push them back towards crime. In [a named society], rules on whether employers
can ask about criminal records would affect how far this happens."

## Resistance

- Social movements **reclaim** stigmatised identities (disability rights, anti-caste campaigns).
- Individuals **challenge labels** in interaction, as interactionists expect.

## Exam traps

- Treating stigma as a fact rather than a **social judgement**.
- Mixing up **discredited** and **discreditable**.
- Defining othering as "being different" without **inferiority**.
- Naming Fanon without the **coloniser's eyes** argument.
- Forgetting to connect these ideas to **social control**.
- No **named society** in an extended answer.

## Self-test

1. Define stigma, according to Goffman.
2. Name Goffman's three types of stigma.
3. What is the difference between a discredited and a discreditable person?
4. What is passing? Give an example.
5. What is a master status, and who used the idea?
6. What is a total institution? Give two examples.
7. What happens in the "mortification of the self"?
8. Define othering.
9. What did Fanon argue in *Black Skin, White Masks*?
10. What did Said argue in *Orientalism*?
11. Explain one way in which othering acts as social control.
12. Give one example of resistance to a stigmatised identity.

**Answers:** 1. An attribute that is deeply discrediting, so others see the person as tainted or less
than whole. 2. Physical; character; "tribal" (race, nation, religion). 3. A discredited person's
stigma is visible or known; a discreditable person's is hidden but could be revealed. 4. Concealing a
stigma, for example not telling an employer about a prison record. 5. A label that overrides all
other statuses; Becker. 6. A place where people live cut off from society under one authority; for
example prisons, psychiatric hospitals, military training camps. 7. Inmates are stripped of their
previous identity, for example losing possessions, clothes or names. 8. Defining a group as
fundamentally different from and inferior to "us". 9. That colonised Black people were taught to see
themselves through the white coloniser's eyes and so internalised a sense of inferiority. 10. That
Western portrayals of "the East" as exotic, backward and irrational supported colonial power.
11. It makes unequal treatment of a group seem natural, so existing power relationships go
unchallenged. 12. For example, disability rights campaigns, or campaigns against caste
discrimination.
