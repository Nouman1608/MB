AQA GCSE Business (8132) introduces students to how businesses start, grow and respond to the world around them,
from choosing a location and setting objectives to recruiting staff, marketing a product and managing cash. It
suits students who like real-world problems, enjoy discussing decisions and are comfortable with some numerical
work. The qualification is linear and not tiered, and it is assessed by two written papers, each lasting 1 hour
45 minutes, worth 90 marks and carrying 50% of the GCSE.

## How the course is organised

The content is set out in six topics. The first two set the scene; the last four look inside the business at its
main functions.

**Business in the real world (topic 1).** The purpose and nature of businesses, business ownership, setting aims
and objectives, stakeholders, business location, business planning and expanding a business. This is the
starting point for understanding what a business is trying to achieve and who it affects.

**Influences on business (topic 2).** Technology, ethical and environmental considerations, the economic climate,
globalisation, legislation and the competitive environment. These are the external forces that shape every
decision a business makes.

**Business operations (topic 3).** Production processes, procurement, the concept of quality and good customer
service.

**Human resources (topic 4).** Organisational structures, recruitment and selection, motivating employees and
training.

**Marketing (topic 5).** Identifying and understanding customers, segmentation, market research and the four
elements of the marketing mix: price, product, promotion and place.

**Finance (topic 6).** Sources of finance, cash flow, financial terms and calculations, and analysing a business's
financial performance.

## How it's assessed

| Component | Duration | Marks | Weighting |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8132/1 Paper 1: Influences of operations and HRM on business activity | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |
| 8132/2 Paper 2: Influences of marketing and finance on business activity | 1 hour 45 minutes | 90 | 50% |

Both papers are written exams, externally assessed, and every student sits the same papers because the
qualification is not tiered. Each paper opens with a Section A of multiple-choice and short-answer questions worth
20 marks. The rest of each paper is built around data and case-study material: on Paper 2 this is a Section B of
around 34 marks and a Section C of around 36 marks.

The assessment objectives are weighted AO1 35%, AO2 35% and AO3 30% across the qualification, split roughly
evenly between the two papers. In practice this means marks are spread across knowing business ideas, applying
them to a given business and analysing or evaluating the situation, rather than resting mostly on recall. The
specification was first taught in September 2017 and first assessed in 2019.

## How the parts fit together

The paper titles show how the course is meant to be read. Paper 1 is about the influences of operations and human
resource management on business activity; Paper 2 is about the influences of marketing and finance. Topics 3 and
4 therefore sit most naturally with Paper 1 and topics 5 and 6 with Paper 2. Topics 1 and 2 are different in
kind: they describe the business and the environment it works in, which is the context any operations, people,
marketing or finance decision has to be judged against. Check with your teacher how your centre maps the opening
topics onto each paper.

Several links are worth making explicit. Business objectives from topic 1 are the yardstick for judging almost any
decision later in the course. Ethical and environmental considerations affect procurement, recruitment and
promotion alike. Cash flow and the financial calculations in topic 6 give hard evidence for arguments about
expansion, quality or staffing. A student who can bring a calculation and a stakeholder perspective into the same
answer is using the course as it was designed.

The case-study style of both papers also means the core skill is consistent from first lesson to final exam: read
a short scenario, pick out what matters, apply the right concept and reach a justified judgement.

## Planning your study

A sensible order through the course is:

1. **Topic 1, Business in the real world**, to establish the vocabulary of ownership, objectives and stakeholders.
2. **Topic 2, Influences on business**, so that external factors can be referred to in every later topic.
3. **Topics 3 and 4, operations and human resources**, completing the Paper 1 functions.
4. **Topics 5 and 6, marketing and finance**, completing the Paper 2 functions.

Keep a running file of real business examples from the news and connect each one to at least one topic. Short
case-study questions can be practised from the first term; financial calculations should be practised regularly
rather than in a single block, because small arithmetic slips are easy to make under time pressure. Full past
papers make most sense once a paper's functional topics have been taught. Advice on approaching each section of
each paper is on the exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many exams are there in AQA GCSE Business?**
Two written papers. Each lasts 1 hour 45 minutes, is worth 90 marks and counts for 50% of the GCSE.

**Is AQA GCSE Business tiered?**
No. Every student sits the same two papers, and the qualification is linear, so both are taken at the end of the course.

**What is the difference between Paper 1 and Paper 2?**
Paper 1 is titled Influences of operations and HRM on business activity, and Paper 2 is titled Influences of marketing and finance on business activity.

**What kinds of questions are on the papers?**
Each paper starts with a 20-mark Section A of multiple-choice and short-answer questions, followed by data-response and case-study questions.

**How are the marks split between skills?**
AO1 carries 35%, AO2 35% and AO3 30% of the qualification, split roughly evenly between the two papers.
