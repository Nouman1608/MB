## 6.1.1 Core recall

- **Specialisation at national level:** a country concentrates its resources on a limited range of goods and services, exports some and imports much of the rest.
- The syllabus gives two broad bases:
  1. **Superior resource allocation:** using a country's particular land, labour, capital and enterprise where they produce most.
  2. **Cheaper production methods:** better technology, economies of scale, skills built up over time, cheaper inputs.
- A country may have either basis or both.
- Endowments change: education creates skilled labour; mines run out.

### Resources and typical specialisms

| Abundant resource | Typical specialism |
|---|---|
| Fertile land and suitable climate | Crops such as fruit, tea, coffee |
| Mineral deposits | Mining and metal exports |
| Skilled, educated labour | Software, medicines, financial services |
| Large lower-wage labour force | Clothing, assembly work |
| Natural and historic attractions | Tourism |

### Transport and trade costs

```
Import only if: foreign cost + transport cost < home cost
Home cost 60; foreign cost 42; transport 12 -> 54 < 60 -> import
Transport 21 -> 63 > 60 -> produce at home
```

## 6.1.2 Advantages and disadvantages

| Group | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| Consumers | Lower prices; wider choice; better quality | Fewer home-made products; supply interruptions abroad; import prices rise if currency falls |
| Firms | Larger markets; economies of scale; cheaper imported inputs | Closure of uncompetitive firms; dependence on foreign demand; delays in imported inputs |
| The economy | More output from the same resources; export earnings; tax revenue; faster growth | Over-specialisation risk; structural unemployment; faster use of finite resources; dependence on imports of essentials |

## Chains to learn

- Specialise where resources are best suited → more output per worker → lower cost → lower prices → higher real incomes.
- Large export industry → economies of scale → lower average cost → more competitive abroad → more exports and jobs.
- Narrow export base → world price falls → export earnings fall → incomes, jobs and tax revenue fall together.
- Imports replace a home industry → firms close → workers lack skills for growing industries → structural unemployment.

### Worked reminder: export concentration

```
Exports: tea 450, textiles 100, other 150 -> total 700
Tea share = 450 / 700 x 100 = 64.3%
Tea price falls 20%: loss = 450 x 0.2 = 90 = 12.9% of export earnings
```

The larger the share of one product, the bigger the hit from a fall in its price. A country with five products of equal share would lose only 4% of export earnings from the same fall in one of them.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Specialisation | Concentrating on a limited range of products or tasks |
| Division of labour | Splitting production into separate tasks done by different workers |
| Factor endowment | The quantity and quality of a country's factors of production |
| Economies of scale | Falling average cost as output increases |
| Over-specialisation | Relying so heavily on a few products that a shock to them harms the whole economy |
| Structural unemployment | Unemployment caused by a long-term decline in an industry |
| Diversification | Widening the range of products a country produces and exports |

## Evaluation points

- Benefits are larger when the country's products have growing, stable demand.
- Risks are larger when one or two products dominate exports.
- Losses fall on particular workers and regions, while gains are spread over all consumers.
- Retraining and investment in new industries reduce the costs of adjustment.
- Transport costs limit how far specialisation pays.

## Exam traps

- Discussing specialisation by individual workers when the question asks about countries.
- Giving advantages and disadvantages for "the economy" only when consumers and firms are named.
- Saying specialisation always lowers prices, without mentioning transport or exchange-rate effects.
- Using "unemployment" without saying it is structural and why.
- Writing a list with no judgement in a "Discuss" answer.

## Quick self-test

1. Define specialisation at a national level.
2. Name the two broad bases for national specialisation in the syllabus.
3. Give an example of superior resource allocation.
4. Give two sources of cheaper production methods.
5. A good costs 30 at home, 22 abroad and 6 to transport. Should the country import it?
6. Give two advantages of specialisation for consumers.
7. Give two advantages for firms.
8. What is over-specialisation?
9. Explain why specialisation can cause structural unemployment.
10. Give one disadvantage of specialisation for consumers.
11. How might a government reduce the risks of specialisation?
12. Why can a country's pattern of specialisation change over time?

### Answers

1. A country concentrating its resources on producing a limited range of goods and services.
2. Superior resource allocation and cheaper production methods.
3. A country with fertile land and a suitable climate concentrating on growing crops.
4. Any two: better technology; economies of scale; skills built up over time; cheaper inputs.
5. Yes: 22 + 6 = 28, which is less than 30.
6. Any two: lower prices; wider choice; better quality.
7. Any two: larger markets; economies of scale; cheaper imported inputs.
8. Relying so heavily on a few products that a fall in their price or demand harms the whole economy.
9. Industries that cannot compete with imports decline, and their workers lack the skills needed in growing industries.
10. Any one: supply interruptions from abroad; fewer home-made products; higher import prices if the currency falls.
11. Encourage diversification into new industries, and fund retraining for workers.
12. Investment and education change the quality of factors, and resources such as minerals can run out.
