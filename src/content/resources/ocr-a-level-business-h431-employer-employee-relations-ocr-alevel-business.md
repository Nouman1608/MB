## The meaning

**Employer/employee relations** are the quality of the dealings between a business's management and its workforce, and the way each side communicates, negotiates and resolves disagreement. Relations can be **good** (trust, open communication, disputes settled early) or **poor** (suspicion, conflict, disputes that grow). Relations are shaped by pay and conditions, management style, how fairly people feel they are treated, and by law.

## Benefits of good relations, costs of poor relations

| Good relations bring | Poor relations bring |
|---|---|
| Lower absence and staff turnover | Strikes, go-slows, other industrial action |
| Higher productivity and quality | High absence and turnover |
| Willingness to accept change | Resistance to change |
| Fewer grievances and tribunals | Grievances, tribunal claims, legal costs |
| Better reputation as an employer | Damaged reputation, difficulty recruiting |

### Worked example 1: the cost of poor relations

Corrin Metals employs 200 staff. A dispute over shift changes leads to a 3-day strike. Output normally runs at 120 units a day and each unit earns a contribution of £450. Strikers lose their pay of £110 a day. Assume wages are not part of the variable costs used to find the contribution.

- Lost contribution = 3 × 120 × £450 = **£162,000**.
- Wages not paid = 200 × £110 × 3 = **£66,000**.
- Net cost to the business = £162,000 − £66,000 = **£96,000**.

This excludes harder-to-measure costs: late deliveries, a customer who switches supplier, and weeks of lower effort afterwards. The workforce also loses £66,000 in pay, and the community loses local spending. A business should compare the net cost with the cost of resolving the dispute earlier, for example by consulting about the shift change.

### Worked example 2: the benefit of good relations

Thornby Foods employs 200 staff who each work 230 days a year. A staff forum and a fairer rota cut absence from 6% to 3%. Cover for an absent worker costs £95 a day.

- Working days in the year = 200 × 230 = 46,000.
- Days lost at 6% = 2,760; at 3% = 1,380.
- Days saved = 1,380; saving = 1,380 × £95 = **£131,100**.

Against this, the business pays for the forum (time, a manager's attention), and absence may have fallen for other reasons, such as a mild winter. A fair judgement says that relations probably explain part of the fall, and the saving is large compared with the cost of consultation.

## Legislation

The specification names six areas. In the UK the main laws are described below in general terms.

| Area | What it does | Typical cost or effect on the business |
|---|---|---|
| Contracts of employment | Employees must receive written terms: pay, hours, notice, holiday. Changes require agreement | Admin time; clear terms reduce disputes |
| Minimum wage | Sets the lowest hourly pay by age band | Raises labour costs, especially in low-paid sectors |
| Health and safety | Duty to provide safe equipment, training, risk assessment | Training, equipment, insurance; fewer accidents |
| Equality | Bans discrimination on protected characteristics in recruiting, pay and treatment | Process costs; wider pool of talent; tribunal risk if breached |
| Data protection | Personal data must be used lawfully, kept secure and not held longer than needed | Systems and training; fines for breaches |
| Dismissal and grievance | Dismissal must be for a fair reason and follow a fair procedure; staff can raise grievances | Documented procedures; risk of claims if ignored |

### Worked example 3: the impact of a pay floor

Larchmoor Cafés has 15 part-time staff, each working 30 hours a week for 52 weeks, currently on £11.40 an hour. Suppose the legal minimum rises to £12.00 (an invented figure).

- Annual pay per person now = 30 × 52 × £11.40 = £17,784. Total = **£266,760**.
- After the rise = 30 × 52 × £12.00 = £18,720. Total = **£280,800**.
- Extra cost = £280,800 − £266,760 = **£14,040**, or £936 per person (0.60 × 30 × 52).

The extra cost equals 5.3% of the wage bill (14,040 ÷ 266,760). Larchmoor can absorb it by raising prices, cutting hours, improving productivity or accepting lower profit. Staff gain income; customers may pay more; if hours are cut, some staff lose. The impact is larger for businesses where wages are a high share of costs.

**Importance of legislation.** Law protects workers and sets a standard for all competitors, so no one gains by paying less than the minimum. It raises costs and administration, particularly for small firms. Not complying brings fines, tribunal awards and damage to reputation. A well-run business treats the law as a minimum, not a target.

## Employee participation

**Employee participation** means involving workers in decisions that affect them. The specification names three types.

| Type | What it is | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Industrial democracy | Employees share in running the business, for example through representatives on the board or worker ownership | Two staff directors on the board |
| Participative decision making | Managers involve employees in making decisions, through team meetings, suggestion schemes or consultation | Staff vote on new shift patterns |
| Works councils | A standing forum where elected employees and managers meet to discuss matters such as working conditions and plans | A council that meets four times a year |

**Benefits:** better decisions (staff know the work), commitment to what is decided, fewer disputes, and a clear channel for complaints. **Costs:** slower decisions, time and cost of meetings, and disappointment if staff views are ignored. For example, a works council of 8 representatives meeting 4 times a year for 3 hours, with time valued at £18 an hour, costs 8 × 3 × 18 × 4 = **£1,728** in time, which is small compared with a single day's strike cost. Participation works only when managers act on what they hear; token consultation can make relations worse.

## Trade unions

A **trade union** is an organisation of workers that represents its members in dealings with employers. Its role includes:

- negotiating pay and conditions through **collective bargaining**
- advising and representing members in disciplinary and grievance cases
- offering legal advice and support
- campaigning on health and safety, and on wider issues
- organising industrial action if negotiation fails.

| | Advantages | Disadvantages |
|---|---|---|
| For employees | Stronger voice, better pay and protection, support in disputes | Subscription cost; may be pushed into action they oppose |
| For the business | One channel for negotiation instead of many; helps communication | Higher pay costs; possible strikes; slower change |
| For other stakeholders | Customers may gain from stable service | Customers may suffer in a strike |

### The changing nature of union activity and membership

In many countries union membership has fallen from its peak, particularly in private-sector businesses, while it has stayed higher in the public sector. If a union's membership falls from 6,000 to 5,100, the change is −900, or **−15%**. Fewer members mean weaker bargaining power, more individual negotiation of pay, and more use of works councils or direct communication by employers. Union activity has also shifted from national strikes towards negotiation, advice and legal support. For a business, lower union power may mean fewer disputes and more flexibility, but it can also remove a useful channel for communicating with the workforce, and staff may feel less protected.

## Common mistakes

- Describing a law without saying what it costs or protects.
- Saying unions always cause disputes; most of their work is negotiation and advice.
- Treating participation as the same as delegation.
- Ignoring the size of costs, such as the £96,000 net cost of a three-day strike.
- Evaluating only from the owner's viewpoint.

## Exam technique

"Explain" needs the meaning and one link to the case. "Evaluate" needs costs and benefits, a judgement and the factors on which it depends, such as business size and the cause of the dispute. For legislation, name the area, say what it requires and state the effect on the business and a stakeholder. Use numbers to show size.
