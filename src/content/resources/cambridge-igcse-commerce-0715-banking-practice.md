## Section A - short questions

**1.** A customer asks the bank to pay a fixed sum of rent on the first day of each month. This is a: **[1]**
A direct debit   B standing order   C cheque   D credit card

**2.** Which banking service allows a business to deposit its takings after the bank has closed? **[1]**
A ATM   B night safe   C overdraft   D mortgage

**3.** Which is a source of finance in which the buyer pays a deposit and then instalments? **[1]**
A hire purchase   B documentary credit   C direct debit   D current account

**4.** State **two** customer services provided by banks. **[2]**

**5.** Define a cashless society. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **two** advantages of internet banking to a customer. **[2]**

## Section B - structured questions

**7.** *Lena runs a small café. Her electricity bill changes every month.*
(a) Identify the payment method that suits paying this bill automatically. **[1]**
(b) Explain **one** reason why this method is better than a standing order here. **[2]**
(c) Explain **one** risk of this method for Lena. **[2]**

**8.** Explain **two** differences between a current account and a deposit account. **[4]**

**9.** Explain how documentary credit protects an exporter who sells goods to a buyer in another country. **[4]**

**10.** *A builder needs a machine that costs $900 cash. On hire purchase the machine costs a $150 deposit and 6 monthly payments of $135.*
(a) Calculate the total cost of hire purchase. **[2]**
(b) Calculate how much more hire purchase costs than cash. **[1]**
(c) Explain **two** reasons why the builder might still choose hire purchase. **[4]**

**11.** *A shopkeeper has an overdraft of $2,400 for three months. The bank charges 10% a year.*
(a) Calculate the interest for the three months. **[2]**
(b) Explain **one** reason why an overdraft is more suitable than a loan for a short shortage of cash. **[2]**

**12.** Discuss whether banks should close most of their branches because of digital banking. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** B. A fixed amount on set dates, ordered by the payer, is a standing order. **[1]**

**2.** B. A night safe is a secure chute for depositing after hours. **[1]**

**3.** A. Hire purchase is a deposit plus instalments, with ownership after the final payment. **[1]**

**4.** Any two: ATM [1]; night safe [1]; foreign currency [1]; financial advice [1]; telephone banking [1]. **[max 2]**

**5.** An economy in which most payments are made by card, phone or electronic transfer [1] instead of notes and coins [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: available 24 hours a day [1]; saves travel and queuing [1]; instant transfers and balance checks [1]; easy to track spending [1]; lower fees [1]. **[max 2]**

**7.** (a) Direct debit [1]. (1 mark)
(b) The amount of a direct debit can change [1], so each month the correct bill is paid, whereas a standing order pays a fixed sum that would be wrong when the bill varies [1]. (2 marks)
(c) Any one: the supplier controls the amount collected [1], so Lena may have too little in the account and be charged a fee [1]; or an error in the amount could leave her with less cash than expected [1], which could harm her cash flow [1]. (2 marks) **[5]**

**8.** Any two differences, 2 marks each: a current account allows money to be paid in and withdrawn at any time [1], whereas a deposit account may need notice or limit withdrawals [1]; a deposit account pays interest [1], whereas a current account pays little or none [1]; a current account usually has a cheque book and debit card [1], whereas a deposit account is mainly for saving [1]. **[max 4]**

**9.** The exporter is afraid the overseas buyer will not pay [1]. With documentary credit the buyer's bank promises to pay [1] once documents show that the goods were shipped [1], so the exporter is sure of payment as soon as it has sent the goods as agreed [1]. **[4]**

**10.** (a) Instalments: 6 x 135 = 810 [1]; total = 150 + 810 = **$960** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) 960 - 900 = **$60** [1]. (1 mark)
(c) Any two, 2 marks each: the builder can use the machine at once, without paying the full price [2]; the cost is spread over six months, which helps cash flow [2]; the builder keeps cash for other needs [2]; the machine may earn income that pays for the instalments [2]. (max 4 marks) **[7]**

**11.** (a) Yearly interest: 2,400 x 10 / 100 = $240 [1]; for three months: 240 x 3 / 12 = **$60** [1]. (2 marks)
(b) An overdraft is used only when needed [1], and interest is charged only on the amount and period used, so there is no cost for borrowing money that is not needed; a loan is a fixed sum repaid over a set time [1]. (2 marks) **[4]**

**12.** Up to 2 for reasons to close, up to 2 for reasons to keep, up to 2 for a justified conclusion.
Close: fewer customers visit branches, so many branches are not used enough [1]; rent and staff costs could be cut [1]; digital banking is available 24 hours a day [1].
Keep: some customers, such as older people or those in rural areas, have no reliable internet or are not confident with technology [1]; branches offer face-to-face advice and cash deposits [1]; businesses still need to deposit cash and use night safes [1].
Conclusion: for example, banks should close some branches but keep a smaller number, because many customers use digital services but some still need in-person help [2]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Mixing up standing orders and direct debits.
- Saying debit cards let the customer borrow.
- Writing "it is quick" for digital banking without a second point or an example.
- In hire purchase questions, adding only the instalments and forgetting the deposit.
- Not converting a yearly rate into the period of the overdraft.

## Frequently asked questions

**What are the four groups of banking service in 0715?** Accounts, customer services, payments and sources of finance.

**What are three effects of digital banking?** Closure of branches, more digital-only banks and less use of cash.
