**These notes condense key question 1.5 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: why nations gained overseas empires and
what those empires did to the people they ruled.** Learn each case study by its system of rule, its key person and its
impact, so you can compare them quickly. The full explanation is in the
[overseas empires study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-overseas-empires/), and exam practice is
in the [overseas empires practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-overseas-empires-practice/).

## Motives in one table

| Motive | Core idea | Best example |
|---|---|---|
| Economic | Raw materials, markets, investment | Congo rubber; Cuban sugar investments |
| Military | Bases and coaling stations | Mahan's sea-power argument; Guantánamo Bay |
| Geopolitical | Rivalry and prestige | Berlin Conference rule of "effective occupation" |
| Religious | Missions and anti-slavery campaigns | Mission schools across Africa |
| Cultural | "Civilising mission" and racial ideas | French assimilation; US debate over "uplifting" Filipinos |

Remember: Hobson (1902) put economic motives first; critics point out that many colonies made losses.

## Africa: three systems compared

**France and Senegal (Faidherbe, governor 1854–61 and 1863–65)**
- Direct rule by French officials; aim of assimilation.
- Forts along the Senegal River; relief of Médine (1857); war with Umar Tall's forces.
- Tirailleurs sénégalais raised (1857); school for chiefs' sons at Saint-Louis.
- Groundnut exports expanded.
- Citizenship only for a minority in the Four Communes; most were subjects.

**Britain and Northern Nigeria (Lugard, from 1900)**
- Indirect rule through emirs, their courts and their taxes.
- Kano and Sokoto conquered in 1903.
- Cheap and stable in the north; strengthened traditional elites.
- In the south-east, appointed "warrant chiefs" lacked authority.
- North and South amalgamated in 1914.

**Leopold II and the Congo Free State (1885–1908)**
- Private possession of the king, not a Belgian colony.
- Rubber quotas enforced by the Force Publique: hostage-taking, mutilation, killing.
- Population losses on a vast scale, though numbers are disputed.
- Critics: George Washington Williams (1890), Roger Casement's report (1904), E. D. Morel's Congo Reform Association.
- Belgian state took over in 1908.

## India, 1857

| Causes | Events | Results |
|---|---|---|
| Greased cartridges offended Hindus and Muslims | Meerut, May 1857 | East India Company abolished (1858) |
| Doctrine of Lapse; annexation of Awadh (1856) | Rebels proclaim Bahadur Shah II at Delhi | Viceroy rules for the Crown |
| Fear of conversion and Western reform | Kanpur, Lucknow, Jhansi | Queen's Proclamation: religious toleration, princes respected |
| Grievances over pay and service | Delhi retaken, September 1857 | More British troops; recruitment changed |
| Landholders' loss of estates | Fighting ends 1858–59 | Mughal dynasty ended; Victoria Empress of India (1877) |

## The USA and Spain's colonies

- **Cuba:** independence war from 1895; Spanish reconcentration camps; "yellow press"; USS *Maine* (February 1898).
- **Teller Amendment (1898):** USA would not annex Cuba.
- **War (April–August 1898):** Dewey at Manila Bay; land and sea victories in Cuba.
- **Treaty of Paris (December 1898):** Cuba freed from Spain; Puerto Rico, Guam and the Philippines to the USA;
  $20 million paid for the Philippines.
- **Debate:** imperialists (markets, bases, duty) against the Anti-Imperialist League (consent of the governed).
- **Philippines:** war against Aguinaldo's forces from 1899; Aguinaldo captured 1901; schools, roads, assembly (1907)
  under US control.
- **Cuba:** occupied to 1902; Platt Amendment (1901); Guantánamo; US troops back in 1906.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Imperialism | Extending a country's power by gaining control over other territories |
| Scramble for Africa | Rapid European partition of Africa, especially after 1880 |
| Effective occupation | Berlin Conference rule that claims needed real control on the ground |
| Assimilation | French policy of making colonial subjects culturally French |
| Direct rule | Government through officials of the colonial power |
| Indirect rule | Government through existing local rulers under colonial supervision |
| Concession company | Private firm given rights to exploit a territory's resources |
| Doctrine of Lapse | Policy of annexing Indian states whose ruler died without a natural heir |
| Sepoy | Indian soldier in the East India Company's army |
| Viceroy | The Crown's representative governing India after 1858 |
| Anti-Imperialist League | US organisation opposing the annexation of the Philippines |
| Platt Amendment | 1901 terms giving the USA the right to intervene in Cuba |

## Exam traps

- **Writing about "Africa" in general.** Use the three named cases and their named figures.
- **Saying Lugard invented indirect rule from nothing.** He adapted existing emirate structures because Britain lacked
  officials and money.
- **Dating Belgian rule from 1885.** It was Leopold's private state until 1908.
- **Ignoring loyal Indians.** Much of India and many princes did not join the rising.
- **Saying the USA annexed Cuba.** The Teller Amendment prevented annexation; control came through the Platt Amendment.

## Self-test

1. What did the Berlin Conference decide about claims to African territory?
2. What argument did Mahan make in 1890?
3. What was the aim of French assimilation, and how far was it achieved in Senegal?
4. Name **two** things Faidherbe did in Senegal.
5. Why did Lugard rule Northern Nigeria through the emirs?
6. What problem did indirect rule face in south-eastern Nigeria?
7. Why was the Congo described as "private imperialism"?
8. Whose report of 1904 exposed abuses in the Congo?
9. What was the Doctrine of Lapse?
10. Give **two** changes to British rule in India after 1857.
11. What did the Teller Amendment promise?
12. What rights did the Platt Amendment give the USA?

**Answers:** 1. A claim needed effective occupation. 2. National greatness depended on sea power and overseas bases.
3. To make Africans culturally French with the possibility of citizenship; only a small minority in the Four Communes
gained it. 4. Any two: extended forts up the Senegal River; relieved Médine; fought Umar Tall's forces; raised the
tirailleurs sénégalais; opened a school for chiefs' sons; encouraged groundnut exports. 5. Britain had few officials and
little money, and the emirates already had working systems of law and tax. 6. There were no powerful chiefs, so
appointed warrant chiefs had little authority. 7. It belonged to Leopold II personally, not to Belgium, and was run for
his profit. 8. Roger Casement's. 9. Annexing a state when its ruler died without a natural heir. 10. Any two: Company
abolished; Viceroy for the Crown; Queen's Proclamation of toleration; more British troops in the army; princes'
states preserved; Mughal emperor exiled. 11. That the USA would not annex Cuba. 12. To intervene in Cuban affairs and to
keep a naval base at Guantánamo Bay.
