These questions cover **3.1.11.2 Determination of rate equation** of the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, including required practical 8. This is International A2 content (**International A-level only**), assessed in Unit 4, with practical skills also tested in Unit 5. All rate data are invented for practice; the rate equations for named reactions are those found by experiment.

## Section A

**1.** Explain why the orders in a rate equation cannot be deduced from the balanced equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**2.** Describe the shape of the concentration–time graph for a reactant that is zero order. **[1]**

**3.** A graph of initial rate against [G] is a straight line through the origin. State the order with respect to G. **[1]**

**4.** A tangent drawn at t = 0 on a concentration–time graph for a reactant passes through (0 s, 0.080 mol dm⁻³) and (50 s, 0.020 mol dm⁻³). Calculate the initial rate, with units. **[2]**

**5.** In a clock reaction, a run with [H] = 0.010 mol dm⁻³ takes 120 s; a run with [H] = 0.020 mol dm⁻³ takes 30 s, all other conditions unchanged. Deduce the order with respect to H, showing your reasoning. **[2]**

**6.** The rate equation for a reaction is Rate = k[P]²[Q]. State which species take part in the rate-determining step, and how many particles of each. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Initial rates were measured for 2D + E + F → products at constant temperature.

| Exp | [D] / mol dm⁻³ | [E] / mol dm⁻³ | [F] / mol dm⁻³ | Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 2 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.10 | 8.0 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 3 | 0.10 | 0.30 | 0.10 | 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ |
| 4 | 0.20 | 0.10 | 0.25 | 2.0 × 10⁻³ |
| 5 | 0.15 | 0.20 | 0.050 | to find |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to D, to E and to F. **[4]**

**(b)** Write the rate equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate k and give its units. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the initial rate in Experiment 5. **[1]**

**8.** Nitrogen dioxide reacts with fluorine: 2NO₂ + F₂ → 2NO₂F. The rate equation is Rate = k[NO₂][F₂]. Two mechanisms are proposed.

Mechanism 1: a single step, 2NO₂ + F₂ → 2NO₂F.

Mechanism 2: NO₂ + F₂ → NO₂F + F (slow), then NO₂ + F → NO₂F (fast).

Decide which mechanism is consistent with the rate equation and explain your answer. **[3]**

**9.** A reaction is followed by continuous monitoring. Tangents to the concentration–time curve give a rate of 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ when [A] = 0.080 mol dm⁻³, and 6.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ when [A] = 0.040 mol dm⁻³. A is the only reactant.

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to A. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate k, with units. **[2]**

**10.** Describe how a colorimeter can be used to follow the concentration of iodine during the reaction between propanone and iodine, and how the results are turned into a rate. **[4]**

**11.** The hydrolysis of 2-bromo-2-methylpropane by aqueous hydroxide ions has the rate equation Rate = k[(CH₃)₃CBr].

**(a)** State the effect on the rate of doubling [OH⁻]. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what the rate equation shows about the mechanism. **[2]**

**12.** The acid-catalysed iodination of propanone has the rate equation Rate = k[CH₃COCH₃][H⁺].

**(a)** Explain why H⁺ appears in the rate equation even though it is not used up. **[1]**

**(b)** State and explain the effect on the rate of doubling [I₂]. **[2]**

**(c)** In a new experiment, [CH₃COCH₃] is doubled and [H⁺] is halved. State the effect on the rate. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Orders are found by experiment; they depend on the species in the rate-determining step of the mechanism, not on the stoichiometry of the overall equation [1]

**2.** A straight line sloping downwards (constant gradient) [1]

**3.** **First order** [1]

**4.** Gradient = (0.020 − 0.080) / (50 − 0) = −1.2 × 10⁻³ [1]; initial rate = **1.2 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]

**5.** Initial rate ∝ 1/t: 1/30 ÷ 1/120 = 4, so doubling [H] makes the rate four times faster [1]; 4 = 2², so **second order** [1]

**6.** **Two particles of P** [1] and **one particle of Q** [1]

**7. (a)** D: Exps 1 and 2, [D] × 2 and rate × 4, so **second order** [1]; E: Exps 1 and 3, [E] × 3 and rate unchanged, so **zero order** [1]; F: Exps 2 and 4, [D] unchanged, [F] × 2.5 and rate × 2.5 [1]; so **first order** in F [1]

**(b)** **Rate = k[D]²[F]** [1]

**(c)** k = 2.0 × 10⁻⁴ / ((0.10)² × 0.10) = **0.20** [1]; **mol⁻² dm⁶ s⁻¹** [1]

**(d)** Rate = 0.20 × (0.15)² × 0.050 = **2.25 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1]

*Examiner insight:* In (a), Exps 2 and 4 isolate F, because [D] is 0.20 in both; comparing Exps 1 and 4 would also need the D factor of 4 removed.

**8.** The rate equation shows one NO₂ and one F₂ in the rate-determining step [1]; Mechanism 1 would give Rate = k[NO₂]²[F₂], so it is not consistent [1]; Mechanism 2 has a slow step with one NO₂ and one F₂, and its steps add up to the overall equation, so **Mechanism 2** is consistent [1]

**9. (a)** [A] halves and the rate falls by a factor of 4 (2.4 × 10⁻⁴ ÷ 6.0 × 10⁻⁵) [1]; (0.5)² = 0.25, so **second order** [1]

**(b)** k = 2.4 × 10⁻⁴ / (0.080)² = **0.0375** [1]; **mol⁻¹ dm³ s⁻¹** [1]

**10.** Use a filter of a colour that iodine absorbs, and zero the colorimeter with a blank containing no iodine [1]; make a calibration graph of absorbance against known [I₂] [1]; mix the reactants, start the timer, and record the absorbance at regular time intervals, converting each reading to [I₂] using the calibration graph [1]; plot [I₂] against time; the rate is the gradient (of the straight line, or of a tangent) [1]

**11. (a)** **No effect**: the rate is unchanged [1]

**(b)** Only the halogenoalkane is in the rate-determining step, so the slow step involves one (CH₃)₃CBr molecule (the C–Br bond breaking) [1]; OH⁻ reacts in a later, fast step, so there must be at least two steps [1]

**12. (a)** H⁺ takes part in the rate-determining step and is regenerated in a later step [1]

**(b)** **No effect** on the rate [1]; iodine is zero order: it reacts only after the rate-determining step, so its concentration does not affect the rate [1]

**(c)** Factor = 2 × 0.5 = 1: the rate is **unchanged** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Comparing two experiments in which two concentrations change, without allowing for the known order.
- Treating a straight-line concentration–time graph as first order.
- Forgetting that the steps of a proposed mechanism must add up to the overall equation.
- Describing a colorimetry method without a calibration graph.
