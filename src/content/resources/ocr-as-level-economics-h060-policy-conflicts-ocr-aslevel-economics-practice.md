Revise first with the [policy conflicts study guide](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-policy-conflicts-ocr-aslevel-economics/) and the [policy conflicts revision notes](/resources/ocr-as-level-economics-h060-policy-conflicts-ocr-aslevel-economics-revision-notes/).

## Section A: Short questions

**1.** Which **one** of the following policies is most likely to reduce inflation but worsen unemployment in the short run? **[1]**

A: A cut in income tax

B: A rise in interest rates

C: A rise in government spending on infrastructure

D: A depreciation of the currency

**2.** In which **one** of the following situations is the conflict between economic growth and low inflation likely to be weakest? **[1]**

A: The economy is operating close to full capacity

B: Firms report widespread shortages of skilled labour

C: Unemployment is high and many factories are idle

D: Wages are rising faster than productivity

**3.** Define the term *trade-off* in the context of macroeconomic policy. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why faster economic growth may worsen a country's current account balance. **[2]**

**5.** Unemployment in Country X falls from 6.4% to 5.1% while inflation rises from 1.9% to 3.3%. Calculate each change in percentage points. **[2]**

**6.** Explain one reason why economic growth may conflict with sustainable development. **[2]**

## Section B: Structured and extended questions

**7.** The table shows invented data for Country Y after the government raised spending in year 1.

| Year | Real GDP growth (%) | Unemployment (%) | CPI inflation (%) | Current account (% of GDP) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1.0 | 6.8 | 1.6 | -1.2 |
| 2 | 2.8 | 5.9 | 2.2 | -2.0 |
| 3 | 3.5 | 4.6 | 3.9 | -3.1 |

**(a)** Identify **two** objectives that improved between year 1 and year 3, using the data. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify **two** objectives that worsened, using the data. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the conflict between objectives appears stronger in year 3 than in year 2. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how raising interest rates to reduce inflation may conflict with the objective of a sustainable balance of payments. **[4]**

**9.** Explain, with the aid of an AD/AS diagram, why a sharp rise in world energy prices creates a policy dilemma for a government. **[4]**

**10.** A government plans to reduce its budget deficit by cutting public spending. Explain how this could conflict with **two** other macroeconomic objectives. **[4]**

**11.** Explain how supply side policies may reduce conflicts between macroeconomic objectives. **[4]**

**12.** Evaluate the view that low inflation should be the main priority of macroeconomic policy. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. Higher rates reduce AD, lowering inflation but also output and jobs. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. With spare capacity, higher AD raises output with little upward pressure on prices. **[1]**

**3.** The choice between objectives when achieving more of one means achieving less of another [1], for example accepting higher inflation in return for lower unemployment [1]. **[2]**

**4.** As incomes rise, households and firms spend more on imports [1]; if exports do not grow as fast, the deficit on the current account widens [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Unemployment falls by **1.3 percentage points** [1]; inflation rises by **1.4 percentage points** [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Higher output usually needs more energy and raw materials and produces more pollution and waste [1], which can damage the environment and reduce the resources available to future generations [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Economic growth rose from 1.0% to 3.5% [1]; unemployment fell from 6.8% to 4.6% [1].

**(b)** Inflation rose from 1.6% to 3.9%, above a 2% target [1]; the current account deficit widened from 1.2% to 3.1% of GDP [1].

**(c)** In year 2 inflation (2.2%) was close to target while growth and employment improved, suggesting spare capacity [1]. By year 3 unemployment was low, so further rises in AD met capacity limits [1], and inflation jumped to 3.9% while the deficit widened further, so gains in growth were costing more in inflation and external balance [1]. **[7]**

**8.** Higher interest rates attract financial inflows, raising demand for the currency [1], so it appreciates [1]. Exports become dearer abroad and imports cheaper at home [1], so export volumes fall and import volumes rise, worsening the current account [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Higher energy costs shift SRAS left, from SRAS1 to SRAS2 [1]. The diagram shows the price level rising and real output falling at the new equilibrium [1]. If the government raises AD to protect output and jobs, inflation rises further [1]; if it reduces AD to cut inflation, output falls further and unemployment rises, so it must choose which objective to sacrifice [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Conflict 1: lower government spending reduces AD [1], so growth slows [1]. Conflict 2: firms and the public sector need fewer workers [1], so unemployment rises, at least in the short run [1]. Other valid conflicts: lower spending on infrastructure may reduce long-run growth; weaker growth reduces tax revenue, making the deficit harder to close. **[4]**

**11.** Supply side policies shift LRAS right [1], raising productive capacity [1]. This allows output and employment to grow without the bottlenecks that cause inflation, easing the growth-inflation trade-off [1]. Higher productivity also improves competitiveness, helping exports and the current account [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Award up to 10 marks across knowledge, application, analysis and evaluation.
- Low, stable inflation reduces uncertainty, supporting investment and long-run growth [1].
- It protects savers and people on fixed incomes from losing purchasing power [1].
- A clear inflation priority anchors expectations, making inflation easier to control [1].
- It supports competitiveness, helping the current account [1].
- But prioritising inflation can mean accepting higher unemployment when policy is tightened [1].
- After a supply shock, keeping inflation low may require a deep fall in output [1].
- Very low inflation risks deflation, which harms spending and raises real debt burdens [1].
- In a recession with spare capacity, unemployment is a more urgent problem than inflation [1].
- The right priority depends on the state of the economy and the cause of any inflation [1].
- Judgement: low inflation is a sound medium-term priority because it supports other objectives, but it should not be pursued at any cost in a recession or after a temporary supply shock [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* The strongest answers explain how low inflation can help other objectives as well as when it conflicts with them.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Listing conflicts without the AD or AS mechanism.
- Ignoring spare capacity when discussing growth and inflation.
- Missing the exchange rate link.
- Using per cent instead of percentage points for changes in rates.
- Ending without a judgement on priorities.
