These questions cover section 3.1, Money and banking, of the Cambridge IGCSE Economics 0455 syllabus for examination in 2026. Economics 0455 is not tiered. All people, banks and figures are invented. Learn the content first from the [money and banking study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-money-and-banking/) and the [money and banking revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-economics-0455-money-and-banking-revision-notes/).

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Identify **two** functions of money. **[2]**

**2.** Which characteristic of money is most needed for it to act as a store of value?

A: divisibility

B: portability

C: durability

D: uniformity

**[1]**

**3.** Which of the following is a role of a central bank but **not** of a commercial bank?

A: accepting deposits from households

B: acting as lender of last resort

C: providing overdrafts to firms

D: exchanging foreign currency for travellers

**[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a double coincidence of wants. **[2]**

**5.** A saver holds 6,300 in cash for a year in which prices rise by 5%. Calculate the real value of the cash at the end of the year in last year's prices. **[1]**

**6.** State **two** ways a commercial bank helps consumers. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**7.** In a remote mining camp, workers pay for goods with cigarettes.

**(a)** Identify **two** characteristics that cigarettes have as money. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain **two** reasons why cigarettes would be a poor store of value. **[4]**

**8.** Pine Bank has deposits of 80 million. It keeps 20% of deposits as cash and lends the rest at 6% interest. It pays savers 1% on all deposits.

**(a)** Calculate the value of its loans. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the bank's interest margin before running costs. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the bank does not lend out all of its deposits. **[2]**

**9.** Explain how a central bank is important to commercial banks. **[4]**

**10.** In Country T, prices rose by 60% in one year.

**(a)** Calculate the real value at the end of the year of a debt of 12,000 that is fixed in money terms. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why lenders in Country T may stop offering long-term loans. **[3]**

**11.** Analyse how commercial banks help producers to increase output. **[5]**

**12.** Discuss whether a central bank or commercial banks are more important to consumers. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two: medium of exchange [1]; unit of account or measure of value [1]; store of value; standard of deferred payment. **[2]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An item that wears out or rots loses value over time, so durability supports the store of value function.

**3.** B [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Each party in a barter must have what the other wants [1]; and want what the other has, at the same time [1]. **[2]**

**5.** 6,300 / 1.05 = **6,000** [1]. **[1]**

**6.** Any two: safe place to keep savings and earn interest [1]; loans or mortgages for large purchases [1]; card and transfer payment services; foreign currency. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Any two: acceptable to people in the camp [1]; portable [1]; divisible into single cigarettes; uniform in size.

**(b)** Cigarettes are not durable: they become stale or damaged over time [1], so they lose value if kept [1]. They are also used up when smoked, so the quantity in circulation falls and rises unpredictably as new supplies arrive [1]; a large delivery would cut the value of each cigarette held [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** 80 x 0.80 = **64 million** [1].

**(b)** Interest received = 64 x 0.06 = 3.84 million; interest paid = 80 x 0.01 = 0.8 million [1]; margin = **3.04 million** [1].

**(c)** Depositors can withdraw their money at any time [1]; the bank must keep cash to meet withdrawals, or it risks losing confidence and facing a run [1]. **[5]**

**9.** The central bank holds commercial banks' reserves and settles payments between them [1], so payments between customers of different banks can be completed [1]. As lender of last resort it lends to a bank that runs short of cash [1], which keeps depositors' confidence and prevents a run spreading to other banks [1]. **[4]**

**10. (a)** 12,000 / 1.60 = **7,500** [1].

**(b)** The money a lender receives in repayment buys much less than the money lent [1]; the longer the loan, the more value is lost [1]; so lenders either refuse long-term loans or demand very high interest rates to cover expected price rises [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Banks lend to firms that lack enough savings to buy capital [1]. With a loan a firm can buy machinery now and repay from future revenue [1], which raises its capacity to produce [1]. Overdrafts let firms pay workers and suppliers before customers pay [1], so production continues without interruption [1]. **[5]**
*Accept:* foreign exchange services enabling firms to import raw materials, developed into higher output.

**12.** Central bank: it keeps confidence in the currency by controlling the issue of notes and coins [1]; through supervision and as lender of last resort it protects consumers' deposits [1].
Commercial banks: consumers deal with them directly for savings accounts, loans, mortgages and payments [1]; without them consumers could not easily borrow to buy a house or pay by card [1].
Judgement: commercial banks provide the services consumers use every day [1], but those services rely on the stability the central bank provides, so the two are complementary and the central bank's role is more important in a crisis [1]. **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving a characteristic, such as portability, when a function was asked for.
- Explaining a store of value problem without linking it to a lost characteristic.
- Saying households bank with the central bank.
- Subtracting the reserve ratio from the interest rate instead of calculating loans first.
- Listing bank roles with no link to the group named in the question.
- Ending a "discuss" answer without a judgement.
