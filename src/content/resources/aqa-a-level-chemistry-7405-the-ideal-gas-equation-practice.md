These questions cover section **3.1.2.3 The ideal gas equation** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is AS content, examined on Paper 1. Learn the methods in the [Ideal gas equation study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ideal-gas-equation/) and the [Ideal gas equation revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-the-ideal-gas-equation-revision-notes/). Further questions that combine gases with empirical formulae are in the [Ideal gas equation and empirical formula practice questions](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-ideal-gas-equation-and-empirical-formula-practice/). Use R = 8.31 J K⁻¹ mol⁻¹ and L = 6.022 × 10²³ mol⁻¹.

## Questions

**1.** Convert each value into the unit needed for the ideal gas equation.

**(a)** 2.5 MPa **[1]**

**(b)** 750 cm³ **[1]**

**(c)** −40 °C **[1]**

**2.** Calculate the amount, in moles, of gas in a 5.00 dm³ flask at 200 kPa and 350 K. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the volume, in dm³, occupied by 8.00 g of oxygen, O₂ (Mᵣ = 32.0), at 27 °C and 150 kPa. **[3]**

**4.** 0.100 mol of a gas occupies 3.00 dm³ at 101 kPa. Calculate its temperature in °C. **[2]**

**5.** 0.640 g of a gas occupies 224 cm³ at 273 K and 101 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the relative molecular mass of the gas. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest the identity of the gas from CO₂, SO₂ and Cl₂. **[1]**

**6.** 0.654 g of zinc reacts with excess hydrochloric acid: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. Calculate the volume, in cm³, of hydrogen formed at 298 K and 100 kPa (Aᵣ Zn = 65.4). **[3]**

**7.** Potassium chlorate decomposes on heating: 2KClO₃ → 2KCl + 3O₂. Calculate the volume, in cm³, of oxygen formed from 1.226 g of KClO₃ (Mᵣ = 122.6) at 25 °C and 101 kPa. **[3]**

**8.** A sealed aerosol can contains gas at 300 kPa and 20 °C. It is left in the sun and warms to 60 °C. The volume does not change.

**(a)** Calculate the new pressure. **[2]**

**(b)** A student gives the answer as 900 kPa. Explain the error. **[1]**

**9.** A gas syringe holds 50.0 cm³ of air at 18 °C. It is warmed to 100 °C at constant pressure. Calculate the new volume. **[2]**

**10.** In an experiment to find the Mᵣ of a volatile liquid, 0.150 g of the liquid vaporises completely to give 52.4 cm³ of vapour at 100 °C and 101 kPa.

**(a)** Calculate the Mᵣ of the liquid. **[3]**

**(b)** The true Mᵣ is lower than the value calculated. Suggest one experimental reason, and explain its effect. **[2]**

**11.** Calculate the number of gas molecules in 1.00 cm³ of air at 293 K and 101 kPa. **[3]**

**12.** Calculate the pressure, in kPa, of 2.20 g of carbon dioxide (Mᵣ = 44.0) in a 1.50 dm³ container at 50 °C. **[3]**

**13.** A student calculates the amount of gas in 250 cm³ at 25 °C and 101 kPa as follows:

n = pV ÷ RT = (101 × 250) ÷ (8.31 × 25) = 122 mol

**(a)** Identify the three errors. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the correct amount of gas. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **2.5 × 10⁶ Pa** [1]

**(b)** **7.50 × 10⁻⁴ m³** [1]

**(c)** **233 K** [1]. **[3]**

**2.** n = pV ÷ RT = (2.00 × 10⁵ × 5.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (8.31 × 350) [1] = **0.344 mol** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** n = 8.00 ÷ 32.0 = 0.250 mol; T = 300 K; p = 1.50 × 10⁵ Pa [1]; V = nRT ÷ p = 0.250 × 8.31 × 300 ÷ 1.50 × 10⁵ = 4.16 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]; = **4.16 dm³** [1]. **[3]**

**4.** T = pV ÷ nR = (1.01 × 10⁵ × 3.00 × 10⁻³) ÷ (0.100 × 8.31) = 365 K [1]; = **92 °C** [1]. **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* The question asks for °C, so 365 K alone earns only the first mark.

**5. (a)** Mᵣ = mRT ÷ pV = (0.640 × 8.31 × 273) ÷ (1.01 × 10⁵ × 2.24 × 10⁻⁴) [1] = **64.2** [1]

**(b)** **SO₂** (Mᵣ 64.1) [1]. **[3]**

**6.** n(H₂) = n(Zn) = 0.654 ÷ 65.4 = 0.0100 mol [1]; V = 0.0100 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 1.00 × 10⁵ = 2.48 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; = **248 cm³** [1]. **[3]**

**7.** n(KClO₃) = 0.0100 mol, so n(O₂) = 0.0100 × 3 ÷ 2 = 0.0150 mol [1]; V = 0.0150 × 8.31 × 298 ÷ 1.01 × 10⁵ = 3.68 × 10⁻⁴ m³ [1]; = **368 cm³** [1]. **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* The 2 : 3 ratio from the equation is a separate step; using 0.0100 mol of O₂ gives 245 cm³.

**8. (a)** p₂ = p₁ × T₂ ÷ T₁ with T in kelvin: 300 × 333 ÷ 293 [1] = **341 kPa** [1]

**(b)** The student used Celsius temperatures (60 ÷ 20); pressure is proportional to the absolute (kelvin) temperature [1]. **[3]**

**9.** V₂ = V₁ × T₂ ÷ T₁ = 50.0 × 373 ÷ 291 [1] = **64.1 cm³** [1]. **[2]**

**10. (a)** T = 373 K, p = 1.01 × 10⁵ Pa, V = 5.24 × 10⁻⁵ m³ [1]; Mᵣ = (0.150 × 8.31 × 373) ÷ (1.01 × 10⁵ × 5.24 × 10⁻⁵) [1] = **87.9** [1]

**(b)** Some liquid did not vaporise, or vapour condensed on a cooler part of the syringe [1]; so the measured volume was too small, which makes mRT ÷ pV too large [1]. Also accept: the vapour was cooler than the oven temperature recorded, so the T substituted was too high. **[5]**

**11.** n = pV ÷ RT = (1.01 × 10⁵ × 1.00 × 10⁻⁶) ÷ (8.31 × 293) [1] = 4.15 × 10⁻⁵ mol [1]; number = 4.15 × 10⁻⁵ × 6.022 × 10²³ = **2.50 × 10¹⁹** [1]. **[3]**

**12.** n = 2.20 ÷ 44.0 = 0.0500 mol; T = 323 K; V = 1.50 × 10⁻³ m³ [1]; p = 0.0500 × 8.31 × 323 ÷ 1.50 × 10⁻³ = 8.95 × 10⁴ Pa [1]; = **89.5 kPa** [1]. **[3]**

**13. (a)** Pressure not converted to Pa (should be 1.01 × 10⁵) [1]; volume not converted to m³ (should be 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) [1]; temperature not converted to K (should be 298) [1]

**(b)** n = (1.01 × 10⁵ × 2.50 × 10⁻⁴) ÷ (8.31 × 298) = **0.0102 mol** [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* An answer of 122 mol for a quarter of a litre of gas fails the sanity check: one mole at room conditions occupies about 24 dm³, so 250 cm³ should be about a hundredth of a mole.

## Where marks are usually lost

- kPa, cm³ or °C substituted without converting.
- Dividing cm³ by 1000 and calling the answer m³.
- Celsius temperatures in ratio calculations.
- Leaving the answer in m³ or Pa when another unit is asked for.
- Ignoring the mole ratio when a gas is produced in a reaction.
