## What the syllabus lists

- Erosional: cliffs and wave-cut platforms, caves, arches and stacks.
- Depositional: beaches in profile and plan, swash and drift aligned beaches, simple and compound spits, tombolos, offshore bars, barrier beaches, coastal dunes, tidal sedimentation in estuaries, saltmarshes, mangroves.
- The role of sea level change in the formation of coastal landforms.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Wave-cut platform | Gently sloping rock surface (usually under about 4°) left as a cliff retreats; best developed in the intertidal zone |
| Discordant coast | Rock bands at right angles to the shore; gives headlands and bays |
| Concordant coast | Rock bands parallel to the shore; outer resistant band protects softer rock until breached |
| Berm | Ridge on the upper beach at the limit of swash, built in low energy conditions |
| Beach cusps | Regular crescent embayments along the upper beach |
| Ridges and runnels | Ridges and troughs parallel to the shore on wide, sandy, macrotidal lower beaches |
| Swash-aligned beach | Faces the dominant waves; little net drift; often a bay-head beach |
| Drift-aligned beach | Lies at an angle to dominant waves; strong longshore drift towards one end |
| Recurve | Hooked end of a spit formed by refraction or a secondary wave direction |
| Tombolo | Ridge joining an island to the mainland where refracted waves meet |
| Overwash | Storm waves carrying sediment over a barrier crest into the lagoon |
| Flocculation | Clay particles clumping in salty water and settling faster |
| Dune slack | Damp hollow where deflation reaches the water table |
| Eustatic change | Global change in sea level from a change in ocean volume |
| Isostatic change | Local change in land level, such as rebound after ice melt |
| Ria / fjord | Drowned river valley / drowned glacial trough |
| Raised beach | Former beach left above present wave reach by a fall in relative sea level |

## Cliff and platform recall

- **Joints vertical, beds horizontal:** steep cliff; blocks fall cleanly.
- **Seaward dip:** blocks slide down bedding planes; cliff angle follows the dip.
- **Landward dip:** steep, stable face.
- **Weak till or clay:** low-angled, slumped, terraced cliff.
- **Debris removed quickly:** cliff stays steep. **Debris accumulates:** base protected, sub-aerial processes lower the angle.
- **Platform widens:** waves lose energy crossing it, so retreat slows (negative feedback). Weathering and rock-boring organisms also lower the platform.

## Depositional recall

- **Storm profile:** sediment moved offshore, flatter upper beach, offshore bars. **Swell profile:** sediment moved onshore, berms, steeper upper beach.
- **Shingle beaches** are steeper (swash percolates, weak backwash); **sand beaches** are flatter (saturated, strong backwash).
- **Spit types:** simple (Sandy Hook, New Jersey); compound with several recurves (Blakeney Point, Norfolk); river-deflecting (Orford Ness, Suffolk).
- **Tombolo:** St Ninian's Isle, Shetland.
- **Barrier islands:** Frisian Islands; Padre Island, Texas. Barriers roll landward by overwash as sea level rises.
- **Dune succession:** embryo, yellow (marram), grey (more humus), slacks, mature dunes inland.
- **Saltmarsh succession:** mudflat, pioneer plants (*Salicornia*, *Spartina*), low marsh, high marsh, creek network.
- **Mangroves:** prop roots (*Rhizophora*) and pneumatophores (*Avicennia*) slow currents and trap mud.

## Sea level landforms

| Change | Landform | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Eustatic rise (post-glacial) | Rias | Drowned river valleys of south-west England and Galicia |
| Eustatic rise | Fjords | Western Norway |
| Rise against parallel ridges | Long islands and inlets parallel to the coast | Dalmatian coast, Croatia |
| Isostatic uplift | Raised beaches and relict cliffs | Isle of Arran, western Scotland |
| Rise with plentiful sediment | Landward-migrating barriers and shingle ridges | Many post-glacial barrier coasts |

## Exam traps

- Giving formation with no characteristics (shape, size, sediment, gradient), or the reverse.
- Calling a spit "compound" without several recurves.
- Missing wave refraction in tombolo answers.
- Treating beach profiles as permanent; they change with storms and seasons.
- Saying "sea level changed" without eustatic or isostatic and the direction of change.
- Describing saltmarsh formation without the role of plants in trapping sediment.

## Self-test

1. State two characteristics of a wave-cut platform.
2. Why does a widening platform slow cliff retreat?
3. How does a seaward dip affect a cliff profile?
4. Distinguish concordant from discordant coasts.
5. Why are shingle beaches steeper than sand beaches?
6. What is a berm, and under which wave conditions is it built?
7. Distinguish swash-aligned from drift-aligned beaches.
8. What makes a spit "compound"?
9. Explain how a tombolo forms.
10. How do barrier islands respond to rising sea level?
11. Why does flocculation matter in estuaries?
12. Name one landform of eustatic rise and one of isostatic uplift.

### Answers

1. Gentle seaward slope, usually under about 4°; widest in the intertidal zone; rock pools, ridges and grooves following rock structure; little sediment cover on exposed coasts.
2. Waves break further out and lose energy crossing the wider platform, so less reaches the cliff foot.
3. Blocks slide down bedding planes towards the sea, so the cliff face follows the dip and is prone to sliding.
4. Concordant: rock bands parallel to the coast. Discordant: bands at right angles, giving headlands and bays.
5. Swash percolates into the coarse shingle, so backwash is weak and sediment stays high on the beach.
6. A ridge at the upper limit of swash, built by low energy (swell) waves that move sediment onshore.
7. Swash-aligned beaches face the dominant waves with little net drift; drift-aligned beaches lie at an angle with strong drift towards one end.
8. Several recurves along its landward side, each marking an earlier tip.
9. Waves refract around an island and meet in its lee, losing energy, so sediment is deposited as a ridge linking island and mainland.
10. Storm waves carry sediment over the crest by overwash, so the barrier migrates landward.
11. Clumped clay particles settle faster, so mudflats build up at slack water.
12. Eustatic rise: ria or fjord. Isostatic uplift: raised beach with relict cliff.
