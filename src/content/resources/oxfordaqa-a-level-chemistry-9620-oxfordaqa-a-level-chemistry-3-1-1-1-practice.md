## Questions

### Section A

**1.** Complete the information for the electron: relative charge and relative mass. **[2]**

**2.** State the two types of particle found in the nucleus of a sodium-23 atom. **[1]**

**3.** A species contains 13 protons, 14 neutrons and 10 electrons. Deduce its overall charge. **[1]**

**4.** Which statement about the fundamental particles is correct?

A: The neutron has a relative charge of −1.

B: The proton and the neutron have the same relative mass.

C: The electron has a relative mass of 0.

D: The electron is found in the nucleus.

**[1]**

**5.** Explain why the mass number of an atom does not include its electrons. **[2]**

**6.** A hydrogen ion, H⁺, is sometimes described as "a bare proton". Explain this description. **[2]**

**7.** The actual mass of a proton is 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ kg and the actual mass of an electron is 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ kg. Calculate the ratio of the mass of a proton to the mass of an electron, and use it to give the relative mass of an electron as a fraction. **[2]**

### Section B

**8.** Beams of protons, neutrons and electrons, all travelling at the same speed, pass between two parallel plates. The upper plate is positively charged and the lower plate is negatively charged.

**(a)** State which beam is not deflected and explain why. **[2]**

**(b)** State the direction in which the proton beam is deflected and explain why. **[2]**

**(c)** Compare the deflection of the electron beam with that of the proton beam. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**9.** In the plum pudding model, an atom was a sphere of positive charge with electrons embedded in it, and the mass was spread throughout the atom.

**(a)** Predict what this model suggests should happen when alpha particles are fired at a thin sheet of gold foil. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe the two observations from the experiment that did not fit the plum pudding model, and give the conclusion drawn from each. **[4]**

**(c)** Explain why the plum pudding model was replaced rather than kept. **[1]**

**10.** A beryllium nucleus has a relative charge of +4 and a relative mass of about 9.

**(a)** Deduce the number of protons and the number of neutrons in this nucleus. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain how nuclei like this one showed scientists that a neutral particle must exist in the nucleus before the neutron was detected. **[2]**

**(c)** Give the relative charge of a beryllium atom with four electrons, and of a Be²⁺ ion. **[2]**

**11.** Niels Bohr proposed that the electrons in an atom occupy fixed energy levels.

**(a)** Describe the evidence that supported the idea of fixed energy levels. **[2]**

**(b)** State which part of Rutherford's nuclear model Bohr kept, and explain why scientists build on earlier models in this way. **[2]**

**(c)** Name the later refinement to the arrangement of electrons that ionisation energy patterns required. **[1]**

**12.** Describe how ideas about the structure of the atom changed from Dalton's model to the model that includes protons, neutrons and electrons. For each change, give the evidence or reasoning that caused it. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Relative charge −1 [1]; relative mass about 1/1840 (accept 1/1836) [1] **[2]**

**2.** Protons and neutrons (both needed) [1] **[1]**

**3.** 13 − 10 = **+3** (neutrons carry no charge) [1] **[1]**

**4.** **B** [1] **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* C is the classic trap. The electron's relative mass is very small, not zero.

**5.** An electron's mass is about 1/1840 of a proton's or neutron's [1], so electrons make a negligible contribution to the mass of the atom compared with the nucleus [1] **[2]**

**6.** A hydrogen-1 atom has one proton, no neutrons and one electron [1]; removing the electron to form H⁺ leaves only the single proton [1] **[2]**

**7.** 1.673 × 10⁻²⁷ / 9.109 × 10⁻³¹ = **1837** (accept 1836 to 1837) [1]; relative mass of electron = **1/1837**, about 1/1840 [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Divide the proton mass by the electron mass, not the other way round, then take the reciprocal for the electron's relative mass.

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** The neutron beam [1]; neutrons have no charge, so the electric field exerts no force on them [1]

**(b)** Towards the lower, negative plate [1]; protons are positively charged and are attracted to the negative plate (and repelled by the positive plate) [1]

**(c)** The electron beam bends the opposite way, towards the positive plate [1]; it is deflected much more than the proton beam [1]; the charges are equal in size but the electron's mass is about 1840 times smaller [1] **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* "Electrons are lighter" without "same size charge" is incomplete: the comparison only works because the charges match in size.

**9.**

**(a)** The alpha particles should pass through with little or no deflection, because positive charge and mass are spread thinly [1]

**(b)** Most alpha particles passed straight through [1], so most of the atom is empty space [1]; a small number were deflected through large angles or bounced back [1], so the positive charge and most of the mass are concentrated in a very small nucleus [1]

**(c)** The model's prediction was contradicted by the results, so it could not explain the evidence [1] **[6]**

**10.**

**(a)** Protons = **4** [1]; neutrons = 9 − 4 = **5** [1]

**(b)** The relative mass of the nucleus (9) is greater than the mass of its protons alone (4) [1]; the extra mass had to come from particles with mass but no charge, because the charge (+4) matched the protons only [1]

**(c)** Atom: **0** [1]; Be²⁺ ion: **+2** [1] **[6]**

**11.**

**(a)** Elements emit (or absorb) light only at particular frequencies, giving line spectra [1]; this means electrons can have only certain fixed energies, so moving between levels gives out fixed amounts of energy [1]

**(b)** Bohr kept the small, dense, positive nucleus with electrons around it [1]; parts of a model that still explain the evidence are kept, and only the parts contradicted by new evidence are changed [1]

**(c)** Sub-shells (and orbitals) [1] **[5]**

**12.** Indicative content; award one mark per valid point linked to its evidence, up to 6 [6] **[6]**

- Dalton pictured atoms as solid, indivisible spheres, which explained elements combining in fixed ratios.
- Thomson discovered the electron, so atoms were divisible; since atoms are neutral, they must also contain positive charge, giving the plum pudding model.
- In the gold foil experiment most alpha particles passed straight through, so the atom is mostly empty space.
- A few alpha particles were deflected through large angles, so positive charge and mass are concentrated in a tiny nucleus (Rutherford's nuclear model).
- Bohr placed electrons in fixed energy levels to explain why elements emit light only at particular frequencies.
- Chadwick discovered the neutron, a neutral particle in the nucleus, which explained why nuclear masses were larger than the mass of the protons alone.

*Examiner insight:* A list of scientists and dates without evidence scores poorly. Each change must be linked to the observation or reasoning that forced it.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing 0 for the electron's relative mass.
- Describing gold foil observations without their conclusions.
- Explaining deflection by mass alone, without noting the equal size of charge.
- Including neutrons when working out the charge of a species.
