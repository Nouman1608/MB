Condensed for the final weeks. These notes cover **3.2.1 Differences and inequalities** in Paper 2 of
OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology (9292). For the full explanation, use the
[Differences and Inequalities study guide](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-sociology-differences-and-inequalities/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Status** | Social honour or prestige attached to a position |
| **Caste** | Closed, ascribed hierarchy fixed at birth |
| **Cohort** | People born in the same period who share experiences |
| **Life chances** | Opportunities for employment, education, health, long life and mobility |
| **Power** | The ability to get your way, even against opposition |
| **Intersectionality** | Differences such as class, gender and ethnicity combine (Crenshaw, 1989) |
| **Income / wealth** | A flow of money / a stock of assets |
| **Glass ceiling** | Invisible barrier keeping women out of the top jobs |
| **Institutional racism** | Discrimination built into the routines of organisations |
| **Diaspora** | A community dispersed from its homeland that keeps links with it |
| **Hybrid identity** | An identity blending more than one culture |

## 3.2.1.1 Differences

Ten ways of classifying people: social class; status; caste; slavery; sex and gender; race and
ethnicity; age and generation/cohort; religion; ability/disability; nationality. Identities can be
**individual** (how I see myself) or **collective** (a shared group belonging).

## 3.2.1.2 Inequalities

Inequalities in power and life chances: **employment, education, health, life expectancy, social
mobility**. **Intersectionality** means a working-class ethnic-minority woman's position is not simply
the sum of class, ethnicity and gender. Effects fall on **individuals** (poorer health, fewer
opportunities) and **society** (wasted talent, conflict).

## 3.2.1.3 Social class — three theories

| | Marxism | Functionalism | Weberianism |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Name** | Marx | Davis and Moore | Weber |
| **Core claim** | Bourgeoisie exploit proletariat; class conflict | Inequality is a functional necessity | Class, status and party are separate |
| **Key concept** | False consciousness | Meritocracy | Market situation |
| **Criticism** | Ignores gender, ethnicity, status | Inherited advantage undermines meritocracy | Less clear about why inequality persists |

- **Measuring class:** usually by occupation; also income, wealth, education, self-assessment.
- **Wealth is more unequal than income**, within and between societies.
- **Mobility:** upward or downward; **intergenerational** (versus parents) or **intragenerational**
  (within a career). Factors: education, family background, gender, ethnicity, the job structure.

## 3.2.1.4 Gender

- **Walby:** six structures of patriarchy — paid work, household production, culture, sexuality, male
  violence, the state; a shift from **private** to **public** patriarchy.
- **Connell:** **hegemonic masculinity** — the dominant ideal of manhood.
- Roles, status and power vary between societies, so gender is socially constructed.
- **Sexism** (prejudice), **sex discrimination** (unequal treatment), the **glass ceiling**.

## 3.2.1.5 Ethnicity

- **Race** = supposed physical differences (socially constructed); **ethnicity** = shared culture.
- **Apartheid** (South Africa): legal racial segregation.
- Legacies of **colonialism and enslavement** in global inequality and racist ideas.
- **Du Bois:** the "colour line" and **double consciousness**.
- **Assimilation** (adopting the majority culture) versus **multiculturalism** (coexistence with equal
  respect).

## 3.2.1.6 Reducing inequality

| Approach | Method | Criticism |
|---|---|---|
| **Marxism** | Abolish capitalism; common ownership | Inequality and lack of freedom in states claiming this route |
| **Social democratic welfare state** | Progressive tax, universal services | Costly; wealth inequality remains |
| **Development aid** | Transfers from richer to poorer countries | Dependency; may serve donors' interests |

## Worked example

*Explain one Marxist and one functionalist view of social class inequality.* (4 marks)

```
Marxist: the bourgeoisie own the means of production and exploit
the proletariat, keeping the surplus value workers create; false
consciousness stops workers seeing this. (2)
Functionalist: Davis and Moore argue unequal rewards are functionally
necessary to motivate the most able to fill the most important
roles, so inequality reflects merit. (2)
```

## Exam traps

- Mixing up wealth and income.
- Treating race and ethnicity as identical.
- Calling Weber a Marxist.
- Naming a sociologist without stating their idea.

## Frequently asked questions

**How is Paper 2 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology structured?**
1 hour 30 minutes, 60 marks: Section A Differences and inequalities (22 marks), Section B Socialisation and social control (22 marks) and Section C Research methods (16 marks).

**What is intersectionality?**
The idea, associated with Crenshaw, that inequalities such as class, gender and ethnicity combine, so a person's life chances cannot be explained by one alone.

**What counts as a life chance?**
The specification lists employment, education, health, life expectancy and social mobility as areas of inequality in life chances.

## Self-test

1. Name Weber's three dimensions of inequality.
2. What is false consciousness?
3. Distinguish intergenerational from intragenerational mobility.
4. Who identified six structures of patriarchy?
5. Give one criticism of development aid.

**Answers:** 1. Class, status and party. 2. The Marxist idea that workers accept ruling-class beliefs
that make inequality seem fair. 3. Intergenerational compares a person with their parents;
intragenerational is movement within one person's working life. 4. Sylvia Walby. 5. It can create
dependency, or serve the interests of donor countries.
