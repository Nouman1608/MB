This guide covers **Part 3 Prose** of Cambridge O Level First Language Urdu 3247 Paper 2 Texts: "Two
essay questions based on the prescribed set texts listed in the syllabus. Candidates answer one
question." It is worth 15 marks. The paper's structure and the full set-text list are in the
[Paper 2 Texts study guide](/resources/o-level-cambridge-urdu-first-language-paper-2-texts/). Here we
look at the kinds of prose question you may meet, including the **empathic task** that the mark
scheme names, and how to answer each. Set texts can change between syllabus years, so check the list
for your examination year.

## The prescribed prose for 2027

Eight items are listed: Muhammad Hussain Azad, *Rishta Nata*; Mirza Farhatullah Baig, *Imtahaan*;
Ghulam Abbas, *Overcoat*; Ahmad Nadeem Qasmi, *Shafarish*; Sir Syed Ahmed Khan, *Umeed kee Khushi*;
Mushtaq Ahmed Yousafi, *Ur ahna ghar mai murgiyoon kaa*; Syed Imtiaz Ali Taj, *Ahraam o Sakoon*; and
two letters from *Khatoot e Ghalib*, to Har Gopal Tafta (3 November 1858) and to Mir Mahdi Majrooh
(2 December 1859). The syllabus states that "the entire work should be studied" unless otherwise
specified, and that set texts "may not be taken into the examination".

The list mixes forms: short story (*افسانہ*), essay (*مضمون*), humorous writing (*مزاح*) and the
personal letter (*خط*). Each form invites different questions, so your notes on each text should
record what kind of writing it is and what that form allows the writer to do.

## The question types

The syllabus says candidates will be asked to show "informed and appropriate personal response",
"knowledge of the text through the use of close reference to details and use of quotations",
"understanding of themes, characters, relationships, situations, issues and attitudes" and
"understanding of the writer's intentions and methods, including use of language". In practice that
gives four common shapes:

| Shape | Typical wording | What it needs |
|---|---|---|
| Character | *... کے کردار کے بارے میں آپ کیا محسوس کرتے ہیں؟* | traits, shown through action, speech and the writer's description |
| Theme or idea | *مصنف نے ... کو کس طرح پیش کیا ہے؟* | the idea traced through key moments, with method |
| Writer's methods | *مصنف نے مزاح / طنز / منظر نگاری کس طرح پیدا کی ہے؟* | technique at the centre, with effects |
| Empathic | *آپ ... ہیں۔ ... کے بعد اپنے خیالات لکھیے۔* | a character's voice, faithful to the text |

## Prose methods to discuss

- **Narrator (*راوی*)**: first person or third? A first-person narrator in a humorous essay can make
  himself the target of the joke.
- **Structure**: opening, rising tension (*عروج*), ending. A surprise ending (*چونکا دینے والا انجام*)
  can change how the reader sees everything before it.
- **Characterisation (*کردار نگاری*)**: what a character does and says, and what the narrator reveals
  that the character does not know.
- **Irony and satire (*طنز*)**: the gap between appearance and reality, or between what is said and
  what is meant.
- **Humour (*مزاح*)**: exaggeration, unexpected comparisons, comic situations and their purpose.
- **Style in letters**: directness, informality, addressing the reader as if in conversation. The
  syllabus listing itself shows one letter opening with questions to its reader (*بھائی! کیا پوچھتے
  ہو؟ کیا لکھوں؟*), a sign of the conversational manner worth discussing.

## Writing the analytical prose essay

The plan is the same as for poetry, but your evidence is **key moments** rather than couplets.

1. **Introduction**: answer the question in two or three sentences and name your methods.
2. **Three or four paragraphs**, each built on one moment in the text: what happens, a short
   memorised quotation, what it reveals about the focus, how the writer's method creates that effect.
3. **Conclusion**: a personal judgement that the essay has earned.

Because prose texts are longer than poems, the risk is **retelling**. Test every paragraph: if you
deleted your analysis, would only plot remain? If so, the paragraph is mostly narrative and sits at
Level 2 or 3.

## The empathic task

The mark scheme for Poetry and Prose includes a parallel set of descriptors for an empathic task. At
the top level the response is "sustaining an appropriate voice"; at Level 4 it uses "suitable features
of expression"; at Level 3 it is "beginning to assume a voice"; at Levels 1 and 2 it shows a basic
understanding "of character". The task asks you to become a character at a particular moment and
write their thoughts.

What makes it a literature answer rather than creative writing:

- **Faithful to the text.** Everything the character knows, remembers and feels must be consistent
  with what happens in the work up to that moment. Inventing events loses relevance.
- **Close reference without quotation marks.** Weave details and phrases from the text into the
  character's thoughts.
- **The right voice.** Vocabulary, tone and level of formality must suit the character: an educated
  narrator, a poor clerk and a child do not sound alike.
- **Thoughts and feelings, not a summary.** The character reflects, regrets, hopes or justifies.

### Illustrative example

To show the method without using a set text, here is an original situation: *a gardener has tended a
large house's garden for thirty years; on his last day, the new owners tell him the garden will be
paved for parking.* Task: *آپ مالی ہیں۔ آخری دن گھر لوٹتے ہوئے اپنے خیالات لکھیے۔*

Model opening:

> تیس برس... اور آج صرف اتنا کہا گیا کہ "بابا، اب یہاں گاڑیاں کھڑی ہوں گی۔" وہ گلاب کی کیاری، جسے
> میں نے بیگم صاحبہ کے کہنے پر بارش میں بھیگتے ہوئے لگایا تھا، کل سیمنٹ کے نیچے دب جائے گی۔ میں نے تو
> کچھ نہیں کہا؛ ہم جیسوں کا کہنا ہی کیا۔ مگر جاتے جاتے امرود کے پیڑ کے تنے پر ہاتھ رکھا تو یوں لگا جیسے
> وہ بھی پوچھ رہا ہو کہ اب کون پانی دے گا۔

*Commentary:* the voice is plain, tired and humble (*ہم جیسوں کا کہنا ہی کیا*), which suits the
character. Details are specific and consistent with the situation. Feelings are shown through memory
and gesture rather than announced. In the exam, the details would come from the set text, and the
voice would echo the way the writer presents that character.

## Common mistakes

- **Retelling the story** instead of answering the focus.
- **Choosing the text you remember best** when the question's focus suits the other text better.
- **Generalising about humour** ("this essay is very funny") without showing how a moment is funny.
- **Empathic answers that drift from the text**, add events or slip into the third person.
- **A modern, casual voice** for a character whose world and speech are quite different.
- **No quotations**, or misquoted ones.
- **Timing**: about 35 minutes is a fair share of the two-hour paper for 15 of 50 marks.

## Exam technique

- Read both questions; check which text each requires and which you know in greater depth.
- For an empathic task, fix the moment first: what has just happened, what the character knows, what
  they fear or want.
- Use short memorised phrases from the text in every paragraph.
- Keep the last few minutes to check spelling and agreement; accurate Urdu supports a clear response.
