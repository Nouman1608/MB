These notes condense section **3.3.6.1 Identification of functional groups by test-tube reactions** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. ## What you must be able to do

- **Identify functional groups** using the reactions in the specification.
- For Required practical 6, test for **alcohol, aldehyde, alkene and carboxylic acid**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Functional group | Atom or group that decides the characteristic reactions of a compound |
| Positive test | The observation that shows the group is present |
| Negative test | No change, or a different observation |
| Oxidising agent | Takes electrons from, or adds oxygen to, another species |
| Effervescence | Bubbles of gas |
| Precipitate | Insoluble solid formed in a solution |

## The main tests

| Group | Reagent | Conditions | Positive result | Negative result |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Alkene | Bromine water | Shake, room temperature | Orange to **colourless** | Stays orange |
| Alcohol (primary or secondary) | Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ | Warm | Orange to **green** | Tertiary: stays orange |
| Aldehyde | Tollens' reagent | Warm in a water bath | **Silver mirror** | Ketone: no change |
| Aldehyde | Fehling's solution | Warm in a water bath | Blue to **red precipitate** | Ketone: stays blue |
| Carboxylic acid | Na₂CO₃ or NaHCO₃ solution | Room temperature | **Effervescence**, CO₂ turns limewater milky | Alcohol, aldehyde: no fizz |
| Halogenoalkane | NaOH(aq) warm, then HNO₃, then AgNO₃(aq) | Warm, then acidify | White, cream or yellow precipitate | No precipitate |

## Reaction details

- **Alkene**: CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂BrCH₂Br (electrophilic addition).
- **Alcohol**: Cr₂O₇²⁻ (orange) → Cr³⁺ (green). Primary → aldehyde then acid; secondary → ketone; tertiary: no reaction.
- **Aldehyde, Tollens'**: [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ → Ag; the aldehyde is oxidised: RCHO + [O] → RCOOH.
- **Aldehyde, Fehling's**: Cu²⁺ complex → Cu₂O (red).
- **Carboxylic acid**: 2CH₃COOH + Na₂CO₃ → 2CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂.
- **Halogenoalkane**: R–X + OH⁻ → R–OH + X⁻, then Ag⁺ + X⁻ → AgX.

## Halide precipitates

| Halide ion | Colour | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Chloride | White | AgCl |
| Bromide | Cream | AgBr |
| Iodide | Yellow | AgI |

Always **acidify with nitric acid** first. Hydrochloric or sulfuric acid would add chloride or sulfate ions that also form precipitates with silver ions.

## Which test tells what apart?

| To distinguish | Use | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Alkene from alkane | Bromine water | Alkene decolourises |
| Aldehyde from ketone | Tollens' or Fehling's | Only aldehyde reacts |
| Carboxylic acid from alcohol | Carbonate | Only the acid fizzes |
| Tertiary alcohol from primary or secondary | Dichromate | Tertiary stays orange |
| Aldehyde from primary alcohol | Tollens' | Only aldehyde gives a mirror (both turn dichromate green) |
| Chloro- from iodoalkane | Hydrolysis then AgNO₃ | White or yellow precipitate |

## Planning an identification

1. Use a **separate small sample** for each test.
2. Write the observation for **both** outcomes (positive and negative).
3. Pick tests in a logical order: carbonate, bromine water, Tollens', dichromate.
4. State the **functional group** found, then the compound.

## Safety

- Warm flammable liquids in a **water bath**.
- Wear eye protection.
- Dichromate(VI) is toxic and bromine water is harmful: handle with care.
- Dispose of Tollens' reagent promptly after use.

## Calculations

- CO₂ from a carbonate test: n(CO₂) = ½ n(RCOOH) for sodium carbonate.
- Silver from Tollens': n(Ag) = 2 × n(RCHO).
- Volume of gas at room temperature and pressure = moles × 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹.

Example: 0.0250 mol of ethanoic acid with excess sodium carbonate gives 0.0125 mol of CO₂, which is 0.0125 × 24.0 = 0.300 dm³, or 300 cm³.

## Exam traps

- "Clear" instead of "colourless".
- Tertiary alcohol turning dichromate green.
- Expecting dichromate to separate an aldehyde from a primary alcohol.
- Missing the "no change" result.
- Heating Tollens' reagent or Fehling's solution with a naked flame.
- Using sulfuric acid or hydrochloric acid before silver nitrate.
- Calling Fehling's red-brown solid a "brown precipitate".
- "Bubbles" without naming CO₂ or using limewater.

## Self-test

1. What is the test for an alkene, and what is seen?
2. What colour change shows a primary alcohol with acidified dichromate?
3. Which alcohols give no colour change with acidified dichromate?
4. What is seen when an aldehyde is warmed with Tollens' reagent?
5. What is seen when a ketone is warmed with Fehling's solution?
6. Write an equation for ethanoic acid with sodium hydrogencarbonate.
7. How is the gas from question 6 identified?
8. Why is nitric acid added before silver nitrate in the halide test?
9. What colour is the precipitate from a bromoalkane?
10. How do you tell propanal from propan-1-ol using one reagent?
11. Why is a water bath used?
12. 0.0100 mol of ethanal reacts with excess Tollens' reagent. How many moles of silver form?

### Answers

1. Add bromine water; it changes from orange to colourless.
2. Orange to green.
3. Tertiary alcohols.
4. A silver mirror forms.
5. The solution stays blue.
6. CH₃COOH + NaHCO₃ → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂.
7. Bubble it through limewater, which turns milky.
8. To remove hydroxide ions, which would form a brown precipitate with silver ions.
9. Cream.
10. Tollens' reagent: propanal gives a silver mirror, propan-1-ol does not.
11. The organic liquids are flammable, so a naked flame is avoided.
12. 0.0200 mol.
