## Questions

**1.** Round each number as stated.

**(a)** 0.0507318 to 2 significant figures **[1]**
**(b)** 4 962.5 to 3 significant figures **[1]**
**(c)** 12.0498 to 2 decimal places **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** A plank is measured as 2.4 m to 1 decimal place. State the error interval for its length. **[2]**
**(b)** The mass of a bag is given as 650 g, correct to the nearest 10 g. Write down the error interval for the mass. **[2]**
**(c)** Zara says the true mass of the bag could be 654.9 g. Is she right? Give a reason. **[1]**

**3.** **(a)** Estimate the value of (5.92 × 41.3) ÷ 0.0396. Show the numbers you use. **[3]**
**(b)** A builder buys 48 boxes of tiles at £6.95 per box. Estimate the total cost. State whether your estimate is bigger or smaller than the true cost, and why. **[2]**

**4.** (Extension only) A force of 240 N, correct to the nearest 10 N, acts on an area of 0.35 m², correct to 2 decimal places. Pressure = force ÷ area, in pascals.

**(a)** Calculate the upper bound of the pressure. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the lower bound of the pressure. **[2]**
**(c)** Give the pressure to a suitable degree of accuracy. Justify your answer. **[2]**

**5.** (Extension only) p = 12.4 correct to 1 decimal place and q = 0.85 correct to 2 decimal places.

**(a)** Find the upper bound of p × q. Give your answer to 3 decimal places. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the lower bound of p − q. **[2]**

**6.** **(a)** Write 0.045 as a fraction in lowest terms. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 7/32 as a decimal and as a percentage. **[2]**
**(c)** Write 2.4% as a decimal. **[1]**

**7.** (Extension only) **(a)** Write 0.5̇4̇ as a fraction in lowest terms. **[2]**
**(b)** Prove that 0.17̇ = 8/45. **[3]**
**(c)** Write 11/30 as a recurring decimal. **[2]**

**8.** A school has 1 260 students. 5/9 of the students are girls. 35% of the girls and 3/8 of the boys take art.

**(a)** How many girls are there? **[1]**
**(b)** How many students take art? **[3]**
**(c)** What percentage of all the students take art? Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

**9.** **(a)** Write 36 minutes as a fraction of 1.5 hours, in lowest terms. **[2]**
**(b)** Write 2.7 kg as a percentage of 900 g. **[2]**
**(c)** A shop took £7 830 this week and £6 000 last week. Write this week's takings as a percentage of last week's. **[1]**

**10.** (Extension only) A cyclist rides 72 km, correct to the nearest km, in 4.5 hours, correct to the nearest 0.1 hour.

**(a)** Write down the error interval for the distance and for the time. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the upper bound of the average speed. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the lower bound of the average speed. **[2]**
**(d)** Give the average speed to a suitable degree of accuracy. Justify your answer. **[2]**

**11.** **(a)** Estimate the value of (7.2 × 19.6) ÷ (0.48 × 4.9). **[3]**
**(b)** The exact value is 60. Write your estimate as a percentage of the exact value, to 1 decimal place. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The first two significant figures are 5 and 0, and the next digit is 7, so round up: **0.051** [1]
**(b)** 4 962.5 has digits 4, 9, 6 and the next digit is 2, so keep 4 96 and fill with a zero: **4 960** [1]
**(c)** The third decimal digit is 9, so round the second up: **12.05** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Answers that keep the right digits but lose a place-holding zero, such as writing 496 for 4 960, change the size of the number and are unlikely to earn credit.

**2. (a)** The rounding unit is 0.1 m, so half a unit is 0.05 m [1]. **2.35 ≤ L < 2.45** [1]
**(b)** The rounding unit is 10 g, so half a unit is 5 g: **645 ≤ m < 655** [2]
**(c)** **Yes**, because 654.9 is less than 655 and lies inside the interval [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write the lower end with ≤ and the upper end with <. An interval with both ends as ≤ is a common way to lose credit.

**3. (a)** Round to 1 s.f.: 6, 40 and 0.04 [1]. 6 × 40 = 240 [1]. 240 ÷ 0.04 = **6 000** [1]
**(b)** 48 → 50 and 6.95 → 7, so 50 × 7 = £350 [1]. Each value went up when rounded, so the estimate is **bigger than the true cost** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Show the rounded numbers. An unsupported estimate that happens to be near the right size gives the examiner nothing to give credit for.

**4. (a)** Upper bound of force = 245 and lower bound of area = 0.345 [1]. 245 ÷ 0.345 = **710.1...** [1]
**(b)** Lower bound of force = 235 and upper bound of area = 0.355 [1]. 235 ÷ 0.355 = **661.97...** [1]
**(c)** To 2 s.f. the bounds are 710 and 660, which differ [1]. To 1 s.f. both are 700, so the pressure is **700 Pa** (1 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a quotient, the bounds of the numerator and the denominator go in opposite directions. Writing which bound you use for each value shows your method clearly.

**5. (a)** Upper bound of p = 12.45 and upper bound of q = 0.855 [1]. 12.45 × 0.855 = 10.64475, so **10.645** (3 d.p.) [1]
**(b)** Lower bound of p = 12.35 and upper bound of q = 0.855 [1]. 12.35 − 0.855 = **11.495** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The two values are rounded to different places, so write each error interval before using it. Mixing up 0.05 and 0.005 is an easy slip.

**6. (a)** 0.045 = 45/1000 [1] = **9/200** [1]
**(b)** 7 ÷ 32 = **0.21875** [1] and 0.21875 × 100 = **21.875%** [1]
**(c)** 2.4 ÷ 100 = **0.024** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A fraction left as 45/1000 shows the right conversion but is not in simplest form, so a "simplest form" instruction needs the final cancelling step.

**7. (a)** Let x = 0.545454...; then 100x = 54.545454... [1]. 99x = 54, so x = 54/99 = **6/11** [1]
**(b)** Let x = 0.17777...; then 10x = 1.7777... and 100x = 17.7777... [1]. 100x − 10x = 16, so 90x = 16 [1]. x = 16/90 = **8/45** [1]
**(c)** 11 ÷ 30 = 0.3666... [1] = **0.36̇** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A proof needs the algebra to be visible. Writing only 8/45 = 0.1777... works backwards and does not show how the fraction is found.

**8. (a)** 1 260 ÷ 9 × 5 = **700 girls** [1]
**(b)** Boys = 1 260 − 700 = 560 [1]. Girls taking art = 0.35 × 700 = 245, and boys taking art = 3/8 × 560 = 210 [1]. Total = 245 + 210 = **455** [1]
**(c)** 455 ÷ 1 260 [1] × 100 = **36.1%** (1 d.p.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A multi-step question rewards a clear layout. Label girls, boys and total so that a reader can follow every stage of your working.

**9. (a)** 1.5 hours = 90 minutes [1]. 36/90 = **2/5** [1]
**(b)** 2.7 kg = 2 700 g [1]. 2 700 ÷ 900 × 100 = **300%** [1]
**(c)** 7 830 ÷ 6 000 × 100 = **130.5%** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A comparison between different units earns little credit if the units are not changed first. Convert first, then divide, so that the comparison is between like units.

**10. (a)** **71.5 ≤ d < 72.5** [1] and **4.45 ≤ t < 4.55** [1]
**(b)** Upper speed uses the largest distance and the smallest time: 72.5 ÷ 4.45 [1] = **16.29...** [1]
**(c)** Lower speed uses the smallest distance and the largest time: 71.5 ÷ 4.55 [1] = **15.71...** [1]
**(d)** To 2 s.f. the bounds are 16 and 16, so they agree [1]. To 3 s.f. they are 16.3 and 15.7, which differ, so the speed is **16 km/h** (2 s.f.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Give the reason for your final accuracy: both bounds agree to that many figures and not to the next. A bare answer without this comparison is unlikely to be given full credit.

**11. (a)** 7.2 → 7, 19.6 → 20, 0.48 → 0.5 and 4.9 → 5 [1]. Top 7 × 20 = 140 and bottom 0.5 × 5 = 2.5 [1]. 140 ÷ 2.5 = **56** [1]
**(b)** 56 ÷ 60 × 100 [1] = **93.3%** (1 d.p.) [1]
*Examiner insight:* Round to 1 s.f. in the usual way. Rounding 19.6 to 20 gives the simplest working, and estimating with unrounded values gains nothing.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dropping a trailing zero in answers such as 8.000 or 4 960.
- Mixing up decimal places and significant figures when the number has leading zeros.
- Writing the upper end of an error interval with ≤.
- Choosing the wrong bound in a subtraction or division.
- Using half a unit of 0.05 for a value given to 2 decimal places, instead of 0.005.
- Giving a bounds answer to more accuracy than the data supports, or with no justification.
- Not showing the rounded numbers in an estimation.
- Failing to leave out the repeat when subtracting in a recurring decimal proof.
- Leaving a fraction unsimplified when "simplest form" is asked for.
- Comparing quantities in different units without converting first.
