Condensed notes for AQA AS and A-level Biology (7401/7402) section **3.2.1.2 Structure of prokaryotic cells and of viruses**. Full explanations and a worked micrograph calculation are in the [prokaryotic cells and viruses study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-biology-7402-structure-of-prokaryotic-cells-and-viruses-aqa-alevel-biology/). For eukaryotic organelles, see the [eukaryotic and prokaryotic cell structure guide](/resources/a-level-aqa-biology-eukaryotic-and-prokaryotic-cell-structure/), and test yourself with the [cell structure practice questions](/resources/a-level-aqa-biology-eukaryotic-and-prokaryotic-cell-structure-practice/).

## Core recall in eight lines

1. Prokaryotic cells are much smaller than eukaryotic cells.
2. Their cytoplasm lacks membrane-bound organelles.
3. Their ribosomes are smaller (70S, compared with 80S in eukaryotic cytoplasm).
4. There is no nucleus: a single circular DNA molecule lies free in the cytoplasm and is not associated with proteins.
5. The cell wall contains murein, a glycoprotein.
6. Many, not all, have one or more plasmids, a capsule and one or more flagella.
7. Viruses are acellular and non-living.
8. A virus has genetic material, a capsid and attachment proteins.

## Prokaryote compared with eukaryote

| Feature | Prokaryotic cell | Eukaryotic cell |
|---|---|---|
| Typical size | A few micrometres | Tens of micrometres |
| Nucleus | Absent | Present, with a nuclear envelope |
| Main DNA | Circular, free in cytoplasm, not bound to proteins | Linear, in the nucleus, bound to histones as chromosomes |
| Membrane-bound organelles | None | Mitochondria, ER, Golgi, lysosomes; chloroplasts in plants and algae |
| Ribosomes | Smaller (70S) | Larger (80S) in the cytoplasm |
| Cell wall | Always present here; contains murein | Cellulose in plants and algae, chitin in fungi, none in animals |
| Plasmids, capsule, flagella | In many species | Not features of this comparison |

**Shared by both:** cell-surface membrane, cytoplasm, ribosomes, DNA as genetic material.

**Linked fact:** DNA in mitochondria and chloroplasts is short, circular and not associated with protein, like prokaryotic DNA, and these organelles contain the smaller ribosomes.

## Extra prokaryotic structures

| Structure | Recall point |
|---|---|
| Plasmid | Small circular DNA, separate from the main loop; often carries antibiotic-resistance genes |
| Capsule | Outer layer; protection and attachment to surfaces |
| Flagellum | Rotates to move the cell |

Structural details of all three are not required.

## Virus structure

| Part | Made of | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Genetic material | DNA or RNA | Instructions for new virus particles |
| Capsid | Protein | Encloses and protects the genetic material |
| Attachment protein | Protein | Binds to complementary receptors on host cells |
| Envelope (some viruses only) | Lipid from host membrane | Not in the specification wording for all viruses; met with HIV in 3.2.4 |

**Why "non-living":** no cytoplasm, no ribosomes, no metabolism of its own; it can only be replicated inside a host cell.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to learn |
|---|---|
| Prokaryotic cell | A cell with no nucleus and no membrane-bound organelles |
| Murein | Glycoprotein in the prokaryotic cell wall (also called peptidoglycan in many textbooks) |
| Plasmid | Small circular DNA molecule separate from the main DNA |
| Capsule | Protective layer surrounding some prokaryotic cells |
| Flagellum | Whip-like structure used for movement |
| Acellular | Not made of cells |
| Capsid | Protein coat of a virus |
| Attachment protein | Viral surface protein that binds to host-cell receptors |

## Identifying an unknown from an image or description

- Nucleus visible: eukaryotic. No nucleus but ribosomes and a membrane: prokaryotic. No membrane, cytoplasm or ribosomes: virus.
- Size supports, but does not prove, the identity: back it with a named structure.
- A wall alone does not decide it: state the material (murein, cellulose or chitin) if it is given.
- Circular DNA in a eukaryotic cell may be mitochondrial or chloroplast DNA, not evidence of a prokaryote.

## Exam traps

- "No organelles": wrong. Ribosomes are present. Say "no membrane-bound organelles".
- "No DNA": wrong. Say "no nucleus".
- "Cell wall made of cellulose": wrong for bacteria; name murein.
- "All bacteria have flagella/plasmids/capsules": the specification says *many*.
- "Viruses are tiny bacteria": wrong; viruses are acellular.
- "The capsid is a membrane": wrong; it is protein.
- Forgetting "not associated with proteins" when describing prokaryotic DNA loses a mark in describe questions.
- In compare questions, give paired statements and at least one similarity.

## Quick self-test

1. Give the three parts of virus structure named in the specification.
2. Name the substance in the prokaryotic cell wall and say what type of molecule the specification calls it.
3. State three features of prokaryotic DNA.
4. Why is "prokaryotic cells have no organelles" incorrect?
5. Name two structures that many, but not all, prokaryotic cells have.
6. What does acellular mean?
7. Give one reason viruses are described as non-living.
8. What is the role of a viral attachment protein?
9. A micrograph at × 20 000 shows a cell 50 mm long. Calculate its actual length in µm.
10. Name two eukaryotic organelles whose DNA resembles prokaryotic DNA.
11. A cell has a wall, ribosomes and DNA but no nucleus. Can you conclude it is a plant cell? Explain.
12. State one structure found in both a bacterium and a virus.

### Answers

1. Genetic material, capsid, attachment protein.
2. Murein; a glycoprotein.
3. Circular; free in the cytoplasm (no nucleus); not associated with proteins.
4. Prokaryotes contain ribosomes, which are organelles; they lack only membrane-bound organelles.
5. Any two: plasmids, a capsule, flagella.
6. Not made of cells.
7. Any one: no cytoplasm; no ribosomes; no metabolism of its own; can only be replicated inside a host cell.
8. Binds to complementary receptors on a host cell so the virus can enter it.
9. 50 mm = 50 000 µm; 50 000 ÷ 20 000 = **2.5 µm**.
10. Mitochondria and chloroplasts.
11. No. Plant cells have a nucleus; a cell with no nucleus is prokaryotic, and its wall would contain murein.
12. Genetic material (nucleic acid). Proteins are also present in both.
