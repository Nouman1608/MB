## Section A

**1.** Which organ carries urine from the kidney to the bladder?

A: the urethra

B: the ureter

C: the renal vein

D: the collecting duct

**[1]**

**2.** In which organ is urea made?

A: the kidney

B: the bladder

C: the liver

D: the pancreas

**[1]**

**3.** State the function of the bladder. **[1]**

**4.** Explain why urea must be removed from the body. **[2]**

**5.** Name the process in the liver that removes the nitrogen-containing part of amino acids. **[1]**

## Section B

**6.** The diagram of one nephron shows the Bowman's capsule, glomerulus, tubule, loop of Henle and collecting duct.

**(a)** Describe how the glomerulus is related to the Bowman's capsule. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the part of the nephron where most of the water is reabsorbed back into the blood, and state what else is reabsorbed there. **[3]**

**(c)** State the name of the part that carries urine from several nephrons towards the ureter. **[1]**

**7.** The table shows the presence of four substances in the blood plasma, the filtrate in the Bowman's capsule and the urine of a healthy person.

| Substance | Blood plasma | Filtrate | Urine |
|---|---|---|---|
| Protein | present | absent | absent |
| Glucose | present | present | absent |
| Urea | present | present | present |
| Sodium ions | present | present | present |

**(a)** Explain why protein is absent from the filtrate. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why glucose is present in the filtrate but absent from the urine. **[2]**

**(c)** A different person has glucose in the urine. Suggest what this shows about the nephrons. **[1]**

**8.** In one day, 144 dm3 of liquid is filtered from a person's blood into the nephrons. The person produces 1.8 dm3 of urine that day.

**(a)** Calculate the volume of liquid that is reabsorbed. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage of the filtered liquid that is reabsorbed. **[2]**

**(c)** The person then drinks much less water for a day. Predict and explain the change in the urine. **[2]**

**9.** After a meal rich in protein, the concentration of amino acids in the blood entering the liver is higher than in blood leaving it.

**(a)** Describe what happens to amino acids in the liver. **[3]**

**(b)** Describe the route taken by the urea from the liver until it leaves the body. **[4]**

**10.** Explain why the kidney is said to remove excess water and ions but not all the water and ions. **[2]**

**11.** Describe the path of the filtrate through a nephron, naming the parts in order, starting at the Bowman's capsule. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]. **[1]**

**2.** C [1]. **[1]**

**3.** It stores urine until it is released [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Urea is toxic [1]. If it built up in the blood and tissue fluid it would harm cells [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Deamination [1]. **[1]**

**6.**

**(a)** The glomerulus is a knot of capillaries [1] inside the cup-shaped Bowman's capsule [1].

**(b)** The tubules (including the loop of Henle) [1]. All of the glucose [1] and some of the ions [1] are also reabsorbed there.

**(c)** The collecting duct [1]. **[6]**

**7.**

**(a)** Protein molecules are large [1], so they cannot pass through the capillary walls of the glomerulus and stay in the blood [1].

**(b)** Glucose is small enough to be filtered [1], but all of it is reabsorbed into the blood in the tubules [1].

**(c)** The nephrons are not reabsorbing all the glucose [1]. **[5]**

**8.**

**(a)** 144 - 1.8 = 142.2 dm3 [1].

**(b)** 142.2 divided by 144 x 100 [1] = 98.75 per cent, which is 98.8 per cent to one decimal place [1].

**(c)** Less urine is produced [1], and it is more concentrated, because the body needs to keep more water and more is reabsorbed [1]. **[5]**

**9.**

**(a)** Some amino acids are built into proteins, such as plasma proteins (assimilation) [1]. Excess amino acids are deaminated, which removes the nitrogen-containing part [1]. The nitrogen-containing part is made into urea [1].

**(b)** Urea passes into the blood [1] and is carried to the kidney [1]. It is filtered from the blood and not fully reabsorbed, so it is in the urine [1]. Urine passes down the ureter to the bladder and leaves through the urethra [1].

**[7]**

**10.** The kidney removes only the amount that is more than the body needs [1], because some of the water and ions are reabsorbed into the blood to keep the right balance [1]. **[2]**

**11.** The filtrate starts in the Bowman's capsule [1], then passes along the tubule and through the loop of Henle [1], and then into the collecting duct [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying that the kidney makes urea.
- Mixing up the ureter and the urethra.
- Writing "filtered" when the answer is "reabsorbed".
- Forgetting that urea is toxic when asked why it is removed.
- Dividing by the volume of urine instead of the volume filtered when finding the percentage.
