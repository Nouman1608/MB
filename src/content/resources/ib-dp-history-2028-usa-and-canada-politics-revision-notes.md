**HL only.** These notes support Paper 3 (Regional study, the Americas) in IB DP History for first assessment 2028. The study has six topics: three for the USA and three for Canada. Questions name leaders and events from the guide, so know who did what. See the [study guide](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-usa-and-canada-politics/) for explanations and the [practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-history-2028-usa-and-canada-politics-practice/) to test yourself.

## Key terms

- **New Frontier / Great Society**: Kennedy's and Johnson's reform programmes.
- **Watergate**: scandal over the 1972 break-in and cover-up, leading to Nixon's resignation.
- **Southern strategy**: Republican appeal to white southern voters.
- **Reaganomics**: tax cuts, deregulation, defence spending, reduced domestic spending.
- **War on Drugs**: policy of tough enforcement and sentencing.
- **TARP**: 2008 bank rescue fund.
- **Polarisation**: growing political divide between parties and groups.
- **Quiet Revolution**: rapid modernisation of Quebec in the 1960s.
- **FLQ**: Front de libération du Québec.
- **War Measures Act**: Canadian emergency law invoked in 1970.
- **TRC**: Truth and Reconciliation Commission.

## The USA, 1961 to 1977

- Kennedy: New Frontier, Peace Corps; little legislation before 1963.
- Johnson: Great Society (civil rights, Medicare and Medicaid, education, War on Poverty); Vietnam erodes it.
- Nixon: 1968 "law and order", Southern strategy; EPA; détente; Watergate; resignation.
- Ford: pardon; inflation; lost 1976.
- Parties: Democratic split over civil rights and Vietnam; McGovern lost 1972; rise of conservatives in the Republican Party.

## The Quiet Revolution

- Causes: rural, church-led society; English control of economy; new French-speaking middle class; Lesage's "maîtres chez nous".
- Effects: state control of education and health; Hydro-Québec nationalised; secularisation; nationalism.
- Parti Québécois (1968; elected 1976); Bill 101 (1977).
- FLQ and October Crisis (1970): Cross and Laporte kidnapped; Laporte killed; War Measures Act; about 450 arrests.
- Referendums: 1980 (about 60 per cent No); 1995 (No by about 1 per cent).

## Canada, 1993 to 2020

- Conservative Party formed in 2003; Harper in power 2006 to 2015.
- TRC: Settlement Agreement (2007), apology (2008), report (2015) with 94 Calls to Action.
- 2008 crisis: banks resilient; stimulus.
- Leaders: Chrétien (deficit, Clarity Act, no Iraq war), Martin (sponsorship scandal), Harper (fiscal conservatism), Justin Trudeau (reconciliation, climate, cannabis).

## Debates

- Great Society: success or limited by Vietnam?
- Watergate: strength or weakness of US institutions?
- Reaganomics: cause of growth?
- Polarisation: what are its causes?
- Quiet Revolution: revolution or acceleration?
- War Measures Act: justified?

## Essay skeleton

1. Define the argument and your line.
2. Rank two or three factors, with dated evidence.
3. Bring in a perspective and the counter-argument.
4. Judge.

## Worked example: planning an answer

Question: "To what extent was the Quiet Revolution the most important development in Canadian politics between 1963 and 1993?"

1. Line: it was the most important source of long-term tension and change, but constitutional and economic issues also shaped the period.
2. Evidence for: state-led modernisation, the Parti Québécois (1976), Bill 101, the October Crisis, the 1980 referendum, the Meech Lake and Charlottetown failures.
3. Other developments: medicare and the Canada Pension Plan, the Constitution Act and Charter (1982), the National Energy Program and Western alienation, free trade (1988).
4. Perspective: historians who see Quebec nationalism as central to national politics and those who stress regional and economic divisions.
5. Judgement: the Quiet Revolution shaped the agenda of every government, and other developments mattered more for particular groups and regions.

## Common mistakes

- Lists of acts without judgement.
- Bias on recent politics.
- Confusing FLQ, Parti Québécois and Bloc Québécois.

## Self-test

1. Name three Great Society measures.
2. Why did Nixon resign?
3. What did Reaganomics involve?
4. What was the Quiet Revolution?
5. What was the October Crisis?
6. How many Calls to Action did the TRC make?

**Answers:** 1. Any three: Civil Rights Act, Voting Rights Act, Medicare, Medicaid, War on Poverty, education aid. 2. Watergate: he faced impeachment after the Supreme Court ordered the tapes released. 3. Tax cuts, deregulation, higher defence spending and reduced domestic spending. 4. The rapid modernisation of Quebec in the 1960s, with state-led change and secularisation. 5. The 1970 kidnappings by the FLQ and the government's use of the War Measures Act. 6. 94.
