These questions cover sub-topic 6.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680, 2027-2029): causes and impacts of flooding, strategies before, during and after a flood, and the opportunities floods provide. Places and figures in the sources are invented.

---

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by *saturated soil*. **[1]**

**2.** Name **two** causes of coastal flooding. **[2]**

**3.** State **two** opportunities that flooding provides to people. **[2]**

**4.** Describe what is meant by *land use zoning* in flood management. **[2]**

**5.** Name **two** pieces of information used to monitor the risk of a river flood. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Explain how land relief can increase the risk of flooding. **[3]**

**7.** Source: *The invented town of Brenholt has grown from 8,000 to 35,000 people in 25 years. Woodland on the hills above the town has been cleared for housing and roads, and a new shopping centre with a large car park was built on the floodplain.*
(a) Using the source, explain **two** reasons why the risk of flooding in Brenholt has increased. **[4]**
(b) Suggest **one** reason why the town council may find land use zoning difficult to introduce now. **[1]**

**8.** A storm drops 25 mm of rain on a catchment of 4 km². Calculate the volume of rain in cubic metres. If 70% becomes surface run-off, calculate the volume of run-off. Show your working. **[3]**

**9.** Explain how flooding can lead to an outbreak of water-related disease. **[3]**

**10.** Explain why farmers in many river valleys benefit from regular floods. **[3]**

**11.** Discuss the benefits and limitations of changing the structure of buildings to reduce the impacts of flooding. **[4]**

**12.** "Strategies before a flood are more effective than strategies after it." Discuss this statement. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Soil whose pore spaces are full of water, so no more water can soak in **[1]**.

**2.** Any two: storm surges **[1]**; tsunamis **[1]**; rise in sea level due to climate change **[1]**. **[max 2]**

**3.** Deposition of nutrient-rich silt on farmland **[1]**; recharge of surface water and ground water stores **[1]**.

**4.** Controlling which land uses are allowed in different areas **[1]**, so that homes, schools and hospitals are kept off land at high risk of flooding, which is used instead for parks, playing fields or farming **[1]**.

**5.** Any two: river level readings from gauges **[1]**; rainfall amounts or rainfall radar **[1]**; weather forecasts of storms **[1]**; soil moisture. **[max 2]**

**6.** Steep slopes cause rainfall to flow downhill quickly **[1]**, so water reaches the river in a short time and its level rises rapidly **[1]**; low-lying, flat land at the bottom of valleys or near the coast is where water collects and drains away slowly, so it floods **[1]**.

**7.** (a) Any two explained, 2 marks each: clearing woodland on the hills **[1]** removes interception and water uptake by trees, so more rain runs off quickly towards the town **[1]**; new housing, roads and the car park are impermeable surfaces **[1]**, so less water infiltrates and drains carry it rapidly to the river **[1]**; building the shopping centre on the floodplain **[1]** puts property where water naturally spreads and reduces space for floodwater **[1]**; cleared hillsides may erode, silting the river and reducing channel capacity. **[max 4]**
(b) Any one: buildings such as the shopping centre already stand on the floodplain and cannot easily be moved; the growing population needs land for housing; businesses and landowners may oppose restrictions. **[1]**

**8.** 25 mm = 0.025 m and 4 km² = 4,000,000 m² **[1]**; volume of rain = 0.025 × 4,000,000 = 100,000 m³ **[1]**; run-off = 100,000 × 0.70 = 70,000 m³ **[1]**.

**9.** Floodwater mixes with sewage from overflowing drains and toilets **[1]** and enters wells and drinking-water supplies **[1]**; people drink the contaminated water and catch diseases such as cholera, and standing water left behind allows mosquitoes to breed **[1]**.

**10.** As the river spreads over its floodplain the water slows down **[1]** and deposits the fine, nutrient-rich silt it was carrying **[1]**, which renews soil fertility and raises crop yields; floods also recharge ground water and surface stores used for irrigation in the dry season **[1]**.

**11.** Benefits: raised floors, stilts, waterproof walls and high electrical sockets keep water out of living space or reduce damage **[1]**, so families can return and recover quickly after the water drains **[1]**. Limitations: adapting existing homes is expensive, especially for low-income households **[1]**; it protects individual buildings but not roads, crops or water supplies, so other strategies are still needed **[1]**. **[4]**

**12.** Award up to 6 for a balanced discussion with a judgement. For: monitoring and warning give time to evacuate and move belongings **[1]**; land use zoning keeps people off the floodplain, preventing damage altogether **[1]**; flood-resistant buildings and preparation reduce losses every time a flood occurs **[1]**. Against: flash floods may give too little warning for before-flood strategies to work **[1]**; after-flood strategies such as shelters, rebuilding and international aid are still essential for people who lose homes, food and clean water **[1]**; rebuilding can include flood-resistant designs, so after-flood action also reduces future risk **[1]**. Judgement: before-flood strategies prevent more loss, but both are needed **[1]**. **[max 6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Explaining causes as "more rain" rather than linking to infiltration, run-off or channel capacity.
- Not using the source details, such as the cleared hills or the car park on the floodplain.
- Forgetting to convert millimetres to metres or square kilometres to square metres.
- Describing land use zoning as building flood walls.
- One-sided answers to "discuss" statements.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these Flooding questions from Cambridge past papers?**
No. They are original questions written for Marlbridge and do not copy past papers or their mark tariffs. Use them alongside the past papers for Cambridge IGCSE Environmental Management (0680).

**Do I need to know hard and soft engineering for 6.3?**
The 6.3.3 list names eight strategies, from monitoring and warning to international aid. Focus on those, with a benefit and a limitation for each.

**How do I convert rainfall into a volume?**
Change the rainfall into metres and the area into square metres, then multiply. One millimetre is 0.001 m, and one square kilometre is 1,000,000 m².
