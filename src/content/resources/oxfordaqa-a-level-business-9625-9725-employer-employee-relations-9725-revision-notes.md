These notes condense **3.2.2.5 Employer-employee relations** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Business (9725) specification, Version 1.1 (International AS, Unit 2). The [employer-employee relations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-employer-employee-relations-9725/) traces a full dispute and costs an overtime ban; the [employer-employee relations practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-business-9625-9725-employer-employee-relations-9725-practice/) test it. Figures are invented.

## Recall in one minute

- **Involvement**: value; consultation, trade unions, employee forums; influences on extent and method.
- **Relations**: value of good relations; reasons for conflict; employee actions (withdraw cooperation, work to rule, leave, strike, overtime ban); resolving conflict (participation, bargaining, arbitration); managing and improving relations.
- **Managing HR**: technology (recruitment and selection, nature of work, skills, remote working); managing people effectively and competitiveness; links to other functions; ethical issues (unsafe conditions and practices, low pay).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Employee involvement | Giving employees a part in decisions that affect them |
| Consultation | Managers seek employees' views before making a decision |
| Trade union | An organisation that represents its members' interests at work, including through collective bargaining |
| Employee forum | A group of elected staff representatives who meet managers regularly |
| Collective bargaining | Negotiation between managers and representatives on behalf of a group of employees |
| Industrial action | Action taken by employees to put pressure on an employer during a dispute |
| Withdrawal of cooperation | Stopping goodwill tasks beyond the strict job |
| Work to rule | Following every rule exactly so work slows down |
| Overtime ban | Refusing to work beyond contracted hours |
| Strike | Stopping work completely |
| Participation | Involving employees in finding a solution |
| Arbitration | An independent third party hears both sides and recommends or decides a settlement |
| Remote working | Working away from the employer's premises, often from home, using technology |

## Involvement methods: one-line judgements

- **Consultation**: cheap and quick to arrange; managers keep the final say, so staff may feel unheard.
- **Trade unions**: one strong, organised voice; may resist change and can escalate disputes.
- **Employee forums**: regular two-way contact including non-union staff; may lack power.

**Influences on extent and method:** management style · size · urgency · staff skills and interest · law · industry culture · existing union recognition.

## Causes of conflict

Pay and conditions · job security and redundancies · changes to hours or working practices · health and safety · poor communication about change.

## Employee actions, mildest to strongest

Withdraw cooperation → work to rule → overtime ban → strike. Leaving is an individual response that raises labour turnover.

## Resolving conflict

| Method | When to use | Drawback |
|---|---|---|
| Participation | Early, to design solutions together | Slow |
| Bargaining | Clear issues such as pay or hours | Concessions cost money |
| Arbitration | Talks have broken down | Both sides lose control of the outcome |

## Cost of action (method)

```
Lost output = hours lost × output per hour
Lost contribution = lost output × contribution per unit
```

Then add hidden costs: late orders, penalties, lost customers, damaged trust.

## Managing human resources

- **Technology**: online recruitment and video interviews; automation changes tasks and skills; remote working cuts costs but complicates supervision and teamwork.
- **Competitiveness**: well-managed people → higher productivity, quality and service.
- **Interrelationships**: forecasts from marketing, budgets from finance, productivity and quality in operations.
- **Ethics**: unsafe conditions and low pay save money short term but risk accidents, legal action, turnover and reputation.

## Exam traps

- Bargaining (two sides negotiate) ≠ arbitration (third party decides or recommends).
- Work to rule is not a strike: staff are still working, just slowly.
- Unions are not only a source of conflict: they can simplify communication.
- Link technology to a named HR area (recruitment, skills, ways of working).
- Show the effect of a dispute on operations, marketing or finance, not just on staff.

## Self-test

1. Define consultation.
2. Give one advantage of an employee forum.
3. Give one disadvantage of trade union involvement for managers.
4. State two reasons for employer-employee conflict.
5. What is a work to rule?
6. Distinguish bargaining from arbitration.
7. A strike stops 3 days of production at 2,000 units a day. Contribution is $5 per unit. Calculate the lost contribution.
8. Give one influence on the extent of employee involvement.
9. Give one benefit and one drawback of remote working.
10. Explain one way good employer-employee relations improve competitiveness.
11. Give one ethical issue in human resources named in the specification.
12. Give one way HR decisions depend on the finance function.

### Answers

1. Managers asking for employees' views before making a decision.
2. Regular two-way communication that includes non-union staff (any valid point).
3. It may resist changes, or disputes may escalate to industrial action (any one).
4. Any two: pay, conditions, job security, changes to working practices, health and safety, poor communication.
5. Employees follow every rule exactly, so work slows without a formal stoppage.
6. In bargaining the two sides negotiate a compromise; in arbitration an independent third party recommends or decides the outcome.
7. 3 × 2,000 = 6,000 units; 6,000 × 5 = **$30,000**.
8. Management style, size, urgency, staff skills, law, culture, union recognition (any one).
9. Benefit: lower office costs or wider recruitment. Drawback: harder supervision or teamwork.
10. Fewer disputes and lower turnover keep output and service reliable, so the firm can meet orders and keep customers.
11. Unsafe working conditions and practices, or low pay.
12. Recruitment, training and pay rises must fit the budget set by finance.
