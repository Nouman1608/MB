## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** In which stage of mitosis do spindle fibres shorten and pull chromatids to opposite poles?

A: Prophase

B: Metaphase

C: Anaphase

D: Telophase **[1]**

**2.** Explain why interphase should not be described as a resting stage. **[2]**

**3.** A plant cell has 2n = 16 chromosomes. State:

**(a)** the number of chromosomes in a G1 cell **[1]**

**(b)** the number of chromatids in a metaphase cell **[1]**

**(c)** the number of chromosomes arriving at one pole at the end of anaphase. **[1]**

**4.** Explain the role of the centromere in mitosis. **[2]**

**5.** State two ways in which binary fission differs from mitosis. **[2]**

**6.** Which eukaryotic cells show a cell cycle, according to the specification? **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.** In a root tip squash, 120 cells in mitosis were classified: 66 in prophase, 18 in metaphase, 12 in anaphase and 24 in telophase. Mitosis takes 60 minutes in this tissue.

**(a)** Calculate the time spent in each stage. **[3]**

**(b)** State which stage a student is least likely to find in a photograph, and why. **[1]**

**8.** Root tips were treated with a chemical that prevents spindle fibres forming. After several hours, many more cells than normal were found with chromosomes condensed but not separated. Explain this observation. **[3]**

**9.** A bacterium contains 10 copies of a plasmid carrying a gene for antibiotic resistance. After binary fission, one daughter cell contains 7 copies and the other 13.

**(a)** Explain how the daughter cells came to contain these numbers. **[2]**

**(b)** Suggest one consequence of this variation for the bacteria. **[1]**

**10.** A bacterium divides by binary fission every 25 minutes. Starting from one cell, calculate the number of cells after 5 hours, assuming every cell divides on time. **[2]**

**11.** A culture of species A, resistant to an antibiotic because of a gene on a plasmid, was mixed with species B, which was not resistant. After 24 hours, some cells of species B were resistant.

**(a)** Explain how species B cells became resistant. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why this is described as horizontal gene transmission. **[1]**

**12.** Describe the behaviour of the chromosomes during interphase and prophase. **[3]**

**13.** Explain how mitosis produces two daughter cells that are genetically identical. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** **C** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In metaphase the fibres are attached but the chromatids are still together.

**2.** During interphase the cell grows and makes proteins and organelles such as mitochondria and ribosomes (G1 and G2) [1], and replicates its DNA (S phase) [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name at least one event from S phase and one from G1 or G2.

**3. (a)** **16** [1].

**(b)** **32** [1].

**(c)** **16** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Once separated, each chromatid counts as a chromosome. Each pole receives a full set of 16.

**4.** Sister chromatids are held together at the centromere [1]. Spindle fibres attach to the centromere, and when it divides in anaphase the fibres pull the chromatids to opposite poles [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Mention both holding together and the attachment of spindle fibres.

**5.** Any two: binary fission has no spindle [1]; prokaryotes have circular DNA, not linear chromosomes, and no nucleus [1]; there are no stages such as prophase or metaphase; plasmids replicate and their number may vary between daughter cells. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* State each difference for both processes if you can, for example "no spindle, whereas mitosis uses a spindle".

**6.** Cells that retain the ability to divide [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Many specialised cells no longer divide, so they do not go round the cycle.

**7. (a)** Prophase: 66 ÷ 120 × 60 = **33 min**; metaphase: 18 ÷ 120 × 60 = **9 min** [1]; anaphase: 12 ÷ 120 × 60 = **6 min** [1]; telophase: 24 ÷ 120 × 60 = **12 min** [1].

**(b)** Anaphase, because it is the shortest stage, so the fewest cells are in it at any moment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that the four times add up to 60 minutes.

**8.** Without spindle fibres, nothing attaches to the centromeres or pulls the chromatids apart [1]. Cells can enter mitosis and condense their chromosomes, but cannot progress through anaphase [1], so cells accumulate at the stage before separation (metaphase), with condensed chromosomes [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* This is why such chemicals are used to make chromosomes easier to see. Link the missing spindle to the stage that cannot happen.

**9. (a)** The plasmids replicated, giving 20 copies [1], but they were not shared out precisely when the cytoplasm divided, so the two cells received different numbers [1].

**(b)** The cell with more copies may be more resistant to the antibiotic (or a cell could end up with no copies and lose resistance) [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The specification says the number of plasmids may vary. Use that wording.

**10.** 5 hours = 300 minutes; 300 ÷ 25 = 12 divisions [1]; 2¹² = **4096 cells** [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Each division doubles the number, so use a power of 2.

**11. (a)** Cells of species A and species B formed connections (conjugation) [1]. A copy of the plasmid carrying the resistance gene passed from species A to species B [1]. The species B cells now had the gene, so they became resistant, and passed it on when they divided [1].

**(b)** The gene passed between existing cells (of different species), not from parent to daughter cells [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "The bacteria mated" is not acceptable. Describe the transfer of DNA.

**12.** In interphase the chromosomes are long and thin and are not visible as separate structures [1]; their DNA is replicated in S phase, so each becomes two chromatids [1]. In prophase the chromosomes condense and become visible as two chromatids joined at a centromere [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* The specification lists interphase among the stages of chromosome behaviour, so describe it as well as prophase.

**13.** DNA is replicated in S phase, giving two identical copies of each chromosome as sister chromatids [1]. In metaphase each chromosome is attached to spindle fibres at its centromere [1]. In anaphase the centromeres divide and the sister chromatids are pulled to opposite poles [1], so each new nucleus, and each daughter cell, receives one copy of every chromosome, identical to the parent's [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Begin with DNA replication. Without it, the answer cannot explain why the copies are identical.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing chromosome and chromatid numbers.
- Saying DNA replicates during mitosis.
- Describing stages without the behaviour of the chromosomes.
- Calling binary fission a form of mitosis.
- Forgetting that plasmid numbers can vary.
- Calling conjugation reproduction.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are these OxfordAQA past paper questions?**
No. They are original questions for section 3.2.10 of OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Biology (9610). Use them with the official past papers.

**How should I count chromosomes during anaphase?**
Count centromeres. Once the centromeres divide, each separated chromatid has its own centromere and counts as a chromosome.
