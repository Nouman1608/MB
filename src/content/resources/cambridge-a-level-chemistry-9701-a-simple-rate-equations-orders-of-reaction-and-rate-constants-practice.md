## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Explain the meaning of the terms *rate constant* and *half-life*. **[2]**

**2.** The rate equation for a reaction is rate = k[A][B]². What are the units of k?

A: s⁻¹

B: dm³ mol⁻¹ s⁻¹

C: dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹

D: mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

**[1]**

**3.** A graph of initial rate against [A] is a straight line through the origin. What is the
order with respect to A?

A: zero

B: first

C: second

D: it cannot be deduced from this graph

**[1]**

**4.** Explain the difference between the rate-determining step and an intermediate in a
reaction mechanism. **[2]**

**5.** State and explain the effect of increasing temperature on the rate constant of a
reaction. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**6.** For 2NO(g) + 2H₂(g) → N₂(g) + 2H₂O(g), these initial rates were measured at a fixed
temperature:

| Exp. | [NO] / mol dm⁻³ | [H₂] / mol dm⁻³ | Initial rate / mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 0.0040 | 0.0020 | 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ |
| 2 | 0.0080 | 0.0020 | 6.4 × 10⁻⁶ |
| 3 | 0.0040 | 0.0060 | 4.8 × 10⁻⁶ |

**(a)** Deduce the order with respect to each reactant, explaining your reasoning. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the rate equation. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the rate constant and give its units. **[2]**

**(d)** Calculate the initial rate when [NO] = 0.0080 and [H₂] = 0.0040 mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**7.** The concentration of a reactant D was measured during a reaction:

| t / s | 0 | 650 | 1300 | 1950 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| [D] / mol dm⁻³ | 0.200 | 0.100 | 0.050 | 0.025 |

**(a)** Show that the reaction is first order with respect to D. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the rate constant. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the rate when [D] = 0.150 mol dm⁻³. **[1]**

**(d)** Calculate [D] after 3250 s. **[1]**

**8.** In a reaction that is zero order with respect to Z, [Z] falls from 0.0500 mol dm⁻³ to
0.0300 mol dm⁻³ in the first 400 s.

**(a)** Calculate the rate and state the value and units of k. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the time taken for Z to be used up completely. **[1]**

**9.** A proposed mechanism for the reaction in Question 6 is:

Step 1 (slow): 2NO + H₂ → N₂O + H₂O

Step 2 (fast): N₂O + H₂ → N₂ + H₂O

**(a)** Write the rate equation predicted by this mechanism. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the intermediate. **[1]**

**(c)** Show that the two steps are consistent with the overall equation. **[1]**

**10.** The reaction A + 2B → AB₂ has the rate equation rate = k[A][B]. Suggest a two-step
mechanism consistent with this, identifying the rate-determining step. **[2]**

**11.** Chlorine atoms take part in the decomposition of ozone in two steps:

Step 1: Cl + O₃ → ClO + O₂

Step 2: ClO + O → Cl + O₂

**(a)** Identify the catalyst and the intermediate. **[2]**

**(b)** Write the overall equation. **[1]**

**12.** On a concentration–time graph for reactant E, the tangent at t = 0 passes through
(0 s, 0.100 mol dm⁻³) and (50 s, 0.020 mol dm⁻³).

**(a)** Calculate the initial rate. **[2]**

**(b)** The reaction is first order in E. Calculate k and the half-life. **[2]**

**13.** The hydrolysis of 2-bromo-2-methylpropane by aqueous hydroxide ions has the rate
equation rate = k[(CH₃)₃CBr]. State the effect on the rate of doubling [OH⁻], and of doubling
[(CH₃)₃CBr], and explain what the rate equation shows about the slow step. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Rate constant: the constant of proportionality in the rate equation, which depends only on
temperature [1]. Half-life: the time taken for the concentration of a reactant to fall to half
its initial value [1].

**2.** **C** [1]. k = rate ÷ [A][B]², so units = mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ ÷ mol³ dm⁻⁹.

**3.** **B** [1]. Rate is directly proportional to [A].

**4.** The rate-determining step is the slowest step in the mechanism, which controls the
overall rate [1]. An intermediate is a species formed in one step and used up in a later step,
so it does not appear in the overall equation [1].

**5.** k increases [1]. More molecules have energy at least equal to the activation energy, so a
greater proportion of collisions are successful [1].

**6. (a)** Exp. 1 → 2: [NO] doubles, rate × 4, so **second order** in NO [1]. Exp. 1 → 3:
[H₂] × 3, rate × 3, so **first order** in H₂ [1].

**(b)** rate = k[NO]²[H₂] [1].

**(c)** k = 1.6 × 10⁻⁶ ÷ (0.0040² × 0.0020) = **50** [1] **dm⁶ mol⁻² s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** rate = 50 × 0.0080² × 0.0040 = **1.3 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].

**7. (a)** [D] halves every 650 s (0.200 → 0.100 → 0.050 → 0.025) [1]; a constant half-life,
independent of concentration, shows first order [1].

**(b)** k = 0.693 ÷ t½ = 0.693 ÷ 650 [1] = **1.07 × 10⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** rate = 1.07 × 10⁻³ × 0.150 = **1.60 × 10⁻⁴ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].

**(d)** 3250 s = 5 half-lives; [D] = 0.200 ÷ 32 = **0.00625 mol dm⁻³** [1].

**8. (a)** rate = 0.0200 ÷ 400 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ [1]; for zero order rate = k, so
k = **5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** 0.0500 ÷ 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ = **1000 s** [1].

**9. (a)** rate = k[NO]²[H₂] [1].

**(b)** N₂O [1].

**(c)** Adding the steps gives 2NO + 2H₂ + N₂O → N₂O + N₂ + 2H₂O; cancelling N₂O gives the
overall equation [1].

**10.** Step 1 (slow): A + B → AB [1]; step 2 (fast): AB + B → AB₂ [1].

**11. (a)** Catalyst: Cl (used in step 1, regenerated in step 2) [1]. Intermediate: ClO
(formed in step 1, used in step 2) [1].

**(b)** O₃ + O → 2O₂ [1].

**12. (a)** Gradient = (0.020 − 0.100) ÷ 50 = −1.6 × 10⁻³ [1]; initial rate =
**1.6 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** k = rate ÷ [E] = 1.6 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.100 = **0.016 s⁻¹** [1]; t½ = 0.693 ÷ 0.016 =
**43 s** [1].

**13.** Doubling [OH⁻]: no change in rate [1]. Doubling [(CH₃)₃CBr]: rate doubles [1]. Only
(CH₃)₃CBr is involved in the slow step; OH⁻ reacts in a later, fast step [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Deducing orders without naming the experiments compared.
- Using coefficients from the balanced equation as orders.
- Omitting units of k, or deriving them for the wrong overall order.
- Using k = 0.693 ÷ t½ for a reaction that is not first order.
- Confusing intermediates with catalysts in a given mechanism.
- Saying temperature changes orders rather than k.
