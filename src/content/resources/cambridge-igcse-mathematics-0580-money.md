This guide covers subtopic **1.16 Money** in Topic 1 Number of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The Core (C1.16) and Extended (E1.16) statements are the same two lines:

1. **Calculate with money.**
2. **Convert from one currency to another.**

Money questions appear on all four papers. They often combine with other subtopics: percentages (discounts, interest), rates (exchange rates, pay) and ratio (sharing a cost). The broader [rates, time and money study guide](/resources/igcse-mathematics-0580-rates-time-and-money/) introduces bills and currency conversion. This page goes further: bills with meter readings, comparing special offers, rounding money correctly in context, and conversions that go both ways or pass through a third currency. Afterwards, use the [money revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-money-revision-notes/) and the [money practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-money-practice/).

All prices, tariffs and exchange rates on this page are invented for practice.

## Writing money correctly

- Amounts in dollars and cents are written to **2 decimal places**: $6.50, not $6.5; $12.00 or $12, not $12.0.
- Mixed units: 350 cents = $3.50. Convert so that every amount is in the same unit before adding.
- Round to the nearest cent at the **end** of a calculation, unless the context says otherwise.
- In context, rounding may need to go one way: a shared bill is rounded **up** so that it is covered; the number of items you can afford is rounded **down**.

## Calculating with money (1.16.1)

### Worked example 1: a bill from meter readings (calculator)

An electricity company charges a fixed $14.50 per month plus $0.186 for each unit (kWh) used. The meter reads 48 213 at the start of the month and 48 702 at the end. Find the bill.

```
Units used = 48 702 - 48 213 = 489
Cost of units = 489 × 0.186 = 90.954
Bill = 14.50 + 90.954 = 105.454  ->  $105.45
```

The unit price has three decimal places, so the cost of the units is not a whole number of cents. Keep it unrounded until the total.

### Worked example 2: comparing special offers (non-calculator)

Kiran needs 6 tins of paint.
- Shop P sells tins at $4.20 each, with the offer "3 for the price of 2".
- Shop Q sells tins at $4.50 each, with 30% off.

Which shop is cheaper, and by how much?

```
Shop P: 6 tins = 2 lots of "3 for 2", so pay for 4 tins
        4 × 4.20 = $16.80

Shop Q: 6 × 4.50 = 27.00
        30% off: 27.00 × 0.7 = $18.90

Shop P is cheaper by 18.90 - 16.80 = $2.10
```

Read the offer carefully: "3 for the price of 2" means one tin in every three is free. With 7 tins you would pay for 5, because the seventh tin is not part of a complete group of three.

### Worked example 3: budgeting with whole items (non-calculator)

A club has $150 to spend on a trip. The coach costs $62. Entry tickets cost $7.50 each. How many tickets can the club buy?

```
Money left for tickets = 150 - 62 = $88
88 ÷ 7.50 = 11.7...  ->  11 tickets
Check: 11 × 7.50 = $82.50 (affordable); 12 × 7.50 = $90 (too much)
```

## Converting currencies (1.16.2)

An exchange rate such as $1 = 0.92 euros gives the number of euros for **one** dollar.

```
dollars -> euros:  multiply by 0.92
euros -> dollars:  divide by 0.92
```

Sense check: one dollar buys 0.92 euros, which is less than 1, so any number of dollars converts to a **smaller** number of euros, and euros convert to a larger number of dollars. If your answer goes the wrong way, you multiplied when you should have divided.

### Worked example 4: both directions (non-calculator)

On one day, $1 = 0.92 euros. Convert (a) $350 to euros, (b) 230 euros to dollars.

```
(a) 350 × 0.92 = 322 euros
(b) 230 ÷ 0.92 = 23 000 ÷ 92 = 250  ->  $250
```

### Worked example 5: through a third currency (calculator)

On one day, $1 = 0.79 pounds and $1 = 83.2 rupees. Convert 500 pounds to rupees, to the nearest rupee.

```
500 pounds -> dollars:  500 ÷ 0.79 = 632.911 39...  (do not round)
dollars -> rupees:      632.911 39... × 83.2 = 52 658.227...
Answer: 52 658 rupees
```

If the dollars are rounded to $632.91 first, the answer becomes 52 658.11, which still rounds to 52 658 here, but in other questions an early rounding can change the final figure. Use the Ans key.

### Worked example 6: comparing prices in two currencies (calculator)

A phone costs 640 euros in one country and $715 in another. On the same day, $1 = 0.92 euros. In which country is the phone cheaper, and by how much in dollars?

```
640 euros in dollars = 640 ÷ 0.92 = 695.652...  ->  $695.65
715 - 695.652... = 19.347...  ->  $19.35
The phone is cheaper in the euro country by $19.35.
```

To compare, both prices must be in the same currency. Either one can be converted; choose the currency the question asks for in the answer.

### Worked example 7: a fee charged on an exchange (calculator)

A bureau charges a fixed fee of $5 and then exchanges the rest at $1 = 3.67 dirhams. Ana changes $400. How many dirhams does she receive?

```
Amount exchanged = 400 - 5 = $395
395 × 3.67 = 1449.65 dirhams
```

Take off the fee in the currency in which it is charged, before converting.

## Quick checks on a money answer

- **Size:** is the answer sensible? A bill for a month of electricity is unlikely to be $1.05 or $10 545.
- **Direction of conversion:** compare the size of the two amounts with the exchange rate, as in the sense check above.
- **Form:** two decimal places, a currency sign, and rounding in the direction the context needs.
- **Reverse check:** convert your answer back, or add the parts of a bill again, to see that you reach the starting figure.

## Common mistakes

- Writing $6.5 or $12.0 instead of $6.50 or $12.00.
- Multiplying when you should divide in a currency conversion, giving an answer that goes the wrong way.
- Rounding to the nearest cent part-way through a calculation.
- Rounding a number of affordable items up, or a share of a bill down.
- Misreading "3 for the price of 2" as one third off a single item, or applying it to an incomplete group.
- Comparing prices without converting them into one currency.
- Converting the whole amount and then subtracting a fee that was charged in the other currency.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **Calculate** or **Work out** a bill: list each charge on its own line, then total.
- **Show (that)** one offer is cheaper: give the full cost for each option and a sentence stating which is cheaper.
- **Give your answer to the nearest cent / dollar / rupee**: do this only at the end.
- **Explain** a decision: refer to the amounts you worked out, with currency units.
