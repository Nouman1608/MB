## Civil justice

- **Courts:** Magistrates' (limited civil work: licensing, some debt enforcement, family work in the Family Court); County Court (most claims); High Court (King's Bench, Chancery, Family); Court of Appeal (Civil Division, led by the Master of the Rolls); Supreme Court.
- **Woolf** – *Access to Justice* (1996) → Civil Procedure Rules 1998, in force April 1999. Problems tackled: cost, delay, complexity, adversarial culture.
- **Tools:** overriding objective (justly and at proportionate cost); case management; pre-action protocols; Part 36 offers; single joint experts; ADR encouraged.
- **After Woolf:** Jackson costs review (2010); compliance cases *Mitchell* (2013, strict) and *Denton* (2014, balanced).
- **Process:** letter of claim → claim form and particulars → defence (14 days, or 28 after acknowledgement) or default judgment → directions questionnaire → allocation.
- **Tracks:** small claims (to £10,000), fast (£10,000–£25,000, one-day trial), multi-track (complex or high value); intermediate track since October 2023 for many claims up to £100,000.
- **Appeals:** permission needed; next level of judge → Court of Appeal → Supreme Court (point of law of general public importance); leapfrog from High Court.

## ADR

| Method | Third party role | Binding? |
|---|---|---|
| Negotiation | None | Only if the parties sign an agreement |
| Mediation | Facilitates; no solution imposed | Only if agreed |
| Conciliation | Active; may suggest terms (ACAS) | Only if agreed |
| Arbitration | Decides; makes an award | Yes, enforceable in court |

- **Arbitration Act 1996:** s1 principles (fair, impartial, no unnecessary delay or expense; party autonomy; limited court role); s9 stay of court action; narrow challenges (jurisdiction, serious irregularity, point of law unless excluded).
- **Scott v Avery clause:** arbitration award must come before any court action.
- **Court pressure:** costs penalty for unreasonable refusal (*Halsey* (2004)); courts may order engagement (*Churchill v Merthyr Tydfil* (2023)).

## Criminal process

- **Charging:** CPS, Code for Crown Prosecutors – evidential test, then public interest test.
- **Summary:** magistrates hear and sentence; Single Justice Procedure for minor non-imprisonable offences.
- **Either way:** plea before venue → if not guilty, allocation by magistrates → defendant may elect Crown Court if magistrates accept jurisdiction. Guilty plea may be committed for sentence.
- **Indictable only:** sent to Crown Court under s51 Crime and Disorder Act 1998 → plea and trial preparation hearing.

**Appeals**

| From | To | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Magistrates | Crown Court | Rehearing on conviction, or sentence (defence) |
| Magistrates | Divisional Court (KBD) | Case stated, point of law (either side) |
| Crown Court | Court of Appeal (Criminal) | Permission; conviction "unsafe"; retrial possible |
| Court of Appeal | Supreme Court | Certified point of law of general public importance |

- **Prosecution:** Attorney General's reference of unduly lenient sentence; retrial after acquittal for serious offences with new and compelling evidence (Criminal Justice Act 2003).
- **Criminal Cases Review Commission:** refers possible miscarriages of justice.

## Bail

- **Police:** PACE (custody officer decides); Criminal Justice and Public Order Act 1994 – police conditional bail; s25 – bail for repeat homicide or rape charges only in exceptional circumstances.
- **Court:** Bail Act 1976 s4 – general right to bail.
- **Refusal grounds:** substantial grounds for believing the defendant would abscond, offend on bail or interfere with witnesses.
- **Factors:** seriousness and likely sentence; character, antecedents, associations, community ties; bail record; strength of evidence.
- **Conditions:** residence, curfew, reporting, passport, tag, surety, security.

## Police powers (PACE 1984)

| Power | Key rule |
|---|---|
| s1 stop and search | Public place; reasonable suspicion of stolen or prohibited articles |
| s2–s3 | Give name, station, object, grounds; record available |
| Code A | Suspicion not based on personal factors alone |
| s23 Misuse of Drugs Act 1971 | Search for controlled drugs |
| Terrorism Act 2000 s43 / s47A | Suspected terrorist / authorised no-suspicion search |
| s24 arrest | Reasonable suspicion plus necessity reason; Code G |
| s28 | Told of arrest and grounds |
| s41 / s42 / ss43–44 | 24 hours / 36 hours (superintendent, indictable) / 96 hours (magistrates) |
| s40 | Reviews at 6 hours, then every 9 |
| s56 / s58 | Someone informed / free legal advice |
| Code C | Appropriate adult; conditions; custody officer |
| s60 / s60A | Audio recording (Code E) / visual recording (Code F) |
| s54 / s55 | Search on arrival / intimate search (inspector) |
| s61 / s62 / s63 | Fingerprints / intimate samples (consent) / non-intimate samples |
| s76 | Oppression or unreliability – confession **must** be excluded |
| s78 | Discretion to exclude unfair evidence |

**Cases:** *Christie v Leachinsky* (1947) reasons for arrest; *Gillan and Quinton v UK* (2010) no-suspicion search breached Article 8; *R v Samuel* (1988) solicitor wrongly refused; *R v Paris, Abdullahi and Miller* (1993) oppression.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Overriding objective | Deal with cases justly and at proportionate cost |
| Part 36 offer | Formal settlement offer with costs consequences |
| Case stated | Appeal on a point of law from magistrates' findings |
| Surety | Person who promises money if the defendant absconds |
| Appropriate adult | Adult supporting a juvenile or vulnerable suspect |
| Oppression | Includes torture, inhuman or degrading treatment, violence or its threat |

## Exam traps

- Calling mediation binding.
- Forgetting that magistrates decide allocation before the defendant elects.
- Treating s78 as mandatory and s76 as discretionary – it is the other way round.
- Giving 36 hours as the basic detention limit; the basic limit is 24.
- Omitting the necessity requirement for arrest under s24.

## Self-test

1. Which report led to the Civil Procedure Rules 1998?
2. State the overriding objective.
3. Name the small claims limit.
4. What is a Scott v Avery clause?
5. Which section of the Arbitration Act 1996 allows a court to stay proceedings?
6. Where is an indictable-only offence tried, and under which provision is it sent there?
7. What test does the Court of Appeal apply to a conviction?
8. Give three factors a court considers when deciding bail.
9. What must an officer tell a person before a s1 search?
10. What is the maximum detention without charge with a magistrates' warrant?
11. Which case concerned refusal of a solicitor?
12. Distinguish s76 from s78 PACE.

**Answers**

1. Lord Woolf's *Access to Justice* (1996).
2. Dealing with cases justly and at proportionate cost.
3. £10,000.
4. A clause making an arbitration award a condition precedent to court action.
5. Section 9.
6. Crown Court, sent under s51 Crime and Disorder Act 1998.
7. Whether the conviction is unsafe.
8. Any three: seriousness and likely sentence; character and antecedents; community ties; bail record; strength of evidence.
9. Name, station, object and grounds of the search, and the right to a record.
10. 96 hours.
11. *R v Samuel* (1988).
12. Section 76 requires exclusion of a confession obtained by oppression or in unreliable circumstances; s78 gives a discretion to exclude any evidence that would make the trial unfair.
