## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Shell | Main energy level, numbered by n |
| Principal quantum number (n) | The number of the shell: 1, 2, 3, 4 |
| Sub-shell | s, p or d set of orbitals of equal energy in a shell |
| Orbital | Region holding at most two electrons, of opposite spin |
| Ground state | Lowest-energy electron arrangement |
| Electronic configuration | Distribution of electrons among sub-shells |
| Spin pairing | Two electrons of opposite spin sharing an orbital |
| Free radical | Species with one or more unpaired electrons |

## Orbitals and capacities

| Sub-shell | Orbitals | Max electrons |
|---|---|---|
| s | 1 | 2 |
| p | 3 | 6 |
| d | 5 | 10 |

| Shell | Max electrons (2n²) |
|---|---|
| n = 1 | 2 |
| n = 2 | 8 |
| n = 3 | 18 |

## Energy order

    1s < 2s < 2p < 3s < 3p < 4s < 3d < 4p

- Fill in this order; write in shell order (3d before 4s).
- Ions beyond calcium: remove 4s electrons before 3d.

## Configurations to recognise

| Species | Configuration |
|---|---|
| Na | [Ne] 3s¹ |
| Cl | [Ne] 3s²3p⁵ |
| K | [Ar] 4s¹ |
| Sc | [Ar] 3d¹4s² |
| Cr | [Ar] 3d⁵4s¹ (exception) |
| Mn | [Ar] 3d⁵4s² |
| Fe | [Ar] 3d⁶4s² |
| Cu | [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s¹ (exception) |
| Ga | [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s²4p¹ |
| Kr | [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶ |
| Fe²⁺ / Fe³⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁶ / [Ar] 3d⁵ |
| Cr³⁺ | [Ar] 3d³ |
| Cu²⁺ | [Ar] 3d⁹ |
| Br⁻ | [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s²4p⁶ |

**Method: any atom or ion**
1. Electrons = proton number − charge (positive) or + charge (negative).
2. Fill 1s, 2s, 2p, 3s, 3p, 4s, 3d, 4p.
3. For a positive transition-element ion, take electrons from 4s first.
4. Check the total.

## Electrons per shell, sub-shell and orbital

The syllabus asks for all three levels of detail. For iron:

| Level | Iron, [Ar] 3d⁶4s² |
|---|---|
| Per shell | 2, 8, 14, 2 |
| Per sub-shell | 1s², 2s², 2p⁶, 3s², 3p⁶, 3d⁶, 4s² |
| Per orbital (3d) | one orbital with 2, four orbitals with 1 |

**Ground state check.** 1s²2s²2p⁶3s¹3p¹ has 12 electrons but is not the ground state of magnesium: the 3p electron belongs in 3s, giving [Ne] 3s². Any configuration with a lower sub-shell part-empty while a higher one is occupied is not a ground state (except Cr and Cu).

## Explaining configurations

- **Energy:** electrons go into the lowest available energy level; 4s is below 3d in K and Ca.
- **Repulsion:** electrons in the same orbital repel strongly, so orbitals of a sub-shell fill singly with parallel spins before pairing.
- **Cr:** 3d and 4s close in energy; 3d⁵4s¹ puts six electrons in separate orbitals, avoiding the 4s pair repulsion.
- **Cu:** 3d¹⁰4s¹ gives a full, lower-energy 3d sub-shell.

## Electrons in boxes

    N   2p [↑ ][↑ ][↑ ]          3 unpaired
    O   2p [↑↓][↑ ][↑ ]          2 unpaired
    Fe  3d [↑↓][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ]  4 unpaired
    Fe³⁺ 3d [↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ] 5 unpaired

- One box per orbital; paired arrows point opposite ways.
- Count unpaired electrons from the singly filled boxes.

## Orbital shapes

- s: sphere centred on the nucleus; size increases 1s < 2s < 3s.
- p: two lobes either side of the nucleus (dumbbell); three p orbitals along x, y and z axes at right angles.

## Free radicals

- Definition: one or more unpaired electrons.
- Examples: Cl• (3p⁵), •CH₃, Br•.
- Formed by even (homolytic) bond breaking, e.g. Cl₂ splitting in ultraviolet light.
- Very reactive because of the unpaired electron.

## Exam traps

- Filling 3d before 4s.
- Removing 3d electrons before 4s for ions such as Fe²⁺ or V³⁺.
- Writing Cr as 3d⁴4s² or Cu as 3d⁹4s².
- Pairing before each orbital in a sub-shell has one electron.
- Parallel arrows in one box.
- Defining a radical by charge.
- Forgetting that 4d and 4f sub-shells are beyond the assessed range (H to Kr).

## Self-test

**1.** State the number of orbitals in a d sub-shell and the maximum number of electrons it holds.

**2.** What is the maximum number of electrons in the third shell?

**3.** Give the full configuration of phosphorus.

**4.** Give the shorthand configuration of cobalt (proton number 27).

**5.** Give the configuration of Co²⁺.

**6.** Write the configuration of chromium and explain why it is not 3d⁴4s².

**7.** How many unpaired electrons does a sulfur atom have?

**8.** How many unpaired electrons does Mn²⁺ have?

**9.** Identify the element with configuration [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s²4p³.

**10.** Explain why the three 2p electrons of nitrogen occupy separate orbitals.

**11.** Describe the shape of a p orbital.

**12.** Is a bromine atom a free radical? Explain.

## Self-test answers

**1.** Five orbitals; ten electrons.

**2.** 18 (3s², 3p⁶, 3d¹⁰).

**3.** 1s²2s²2p⁶3s²3p³.

**4.** [Ar] 3d⁷4s².

**5.** [Ar] 3d⁷ (both 4s electrons lost).

**6.** [Ar] 3d⁵4s¹. The 3d and 4s are close in energy; one electron in each of six orbitals avoids the repulsion of a paired 4s, giving a lower-energy, half-filled 3d sub-shell.

**7.** Two (3p⁴: one paired orbital and two singly occupied).

**8.** Five ([Ar] 3d⁵, one electron in each 3d orbital).

**9.** Arsenic.

**10.** The orbitals have equal energy, and electrons in separate orbitals repel less than a pair in one orbital, so they occupy separate orbitals with parallel spins.

**11.** Two lobes on opposite sides of the nucleus, along one axis (a dumbbell shape).

**12.** Yes: [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s²4p⁵ has one unpaired electron.
