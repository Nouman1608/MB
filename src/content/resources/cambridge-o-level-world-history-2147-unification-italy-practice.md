These questions cover key question 1.2 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, "How was Italy unified?", which is the
Paper 2 prescribed topic for the November 2026 series.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the events in Milan and Venice in 1848. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** features of Gioberti's plan for Italy. **[2]**

**3.** Describe how Piedmont's part in the Crimean War helped the Italian cause. **[4]**

**4.** Describe the war of 1859 between Austria and the allies France and Piedmont. **[4]**

**5.** Describe Garibaldi's campaign in Sicily in 1860. **[4]**

**6.** Identify **two** attempts by Garibaldi to capture Rome after 1861. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did Piedmont become the leader of the Italian national movement after 1849? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did Garibaldi hand over southern Italy to Victor Emmanuel in 1860? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "Austrian military strength was the main reason why Italy was not unified in 1848–49." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "Austria was the European country most affected by moves towards Italian unification." How far do you agree?
Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from a speech by an imagined deputy in the Turin parliament, April 1860. "We are told that
Nice and Savoy are the price of Lombardy and of Tuscany. I say that a nation which sells its own children to buy its
neighbours has not yet learned what a nation is. Count Cavour has made Piedmont larger. He has not made Italy."

Why do you think this speech was made in April 1860? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your answer. **[7]**

**12.** Study Sources B and C, which are invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: from an imagined French Catholic newspaper, October 1860. "The armies of Turin have marched
into the lands of the Holy Father without a declaration of war. The Emperor, who sent our soldiers to Rome to protect
the Pope, now looks away while his allies rob him."

*Source C (invented)*: from an imagined letter by a Piedmontese officer, October 1860. "We crossed the Papal border to
reach the south before the Redshirts could reach Rome. Had Garibaldi attacked the city, the French would have fought
us all and everything gained since 1859 would have been lost."

How far does Source C explain the events that Source B complains about? Use the sources and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: in March 1848 the people of Milan rose against the Austrians (1); in the Five Days of
fighting they forced Radetzky's garrison to withdraw (1); the rising encouraged Piedmont to declare war on Austria (1);
in Venice Daniele Manin led a rising (1); a Republic of St Mark was proclaimed (1); Venice held out until August 1849,
when it surrendered to Austria (1); Austria regained Milan after Custoza in 1848 (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: Italy would be a federation of the existing states (1); the Pope would be its president
(1); existing rulers would keep their thrones (1); it would not require a revolution or a republic (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: Piedmont sent troops to fight on the side of Britain and France in 1855 (1); this earned
the goodwill of both powers (1); Cavour attended the Congress of Paris in 1856 (1); he raised the Italian question and
criticised Austrian rule (1); it showed Piedmont as a modern state worth supporting (1); it began the friendship with
Napoleon III that led to Plombières (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: Cavour provoked Austria, which sent an ultimatum and declared war in April 1859 (1); the
French army came to Piedmont's aid under the Plombières agreement (1); the allies won at Magenta (1) and at Solferino
(1) in June 1859; casualties were heavy (1); Napoleon III made a separate armistice at Villafranca in July 1859 (1);
Piedmont gained Lombardy but Austria kept Venetia (1); Cavour resigned in protest (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any four, 1 mark each: Garibaldi sailed from near Genoa in May 1860 with about a thousand volunteers (1); they
landed at Marsala (1); he defeated a Bourbon force at Calatafimi (1); he captured Palermo (1); Sicilian peasants and
rebels joined him (1); he declared himself dictator in the name of Victor Emmanuel (1); by late summer he controlled
the island and crossed to the mainland in August (1). **[4]**

**6.** Any two, 1 mark each: the attempt that ended at Aspromonte in 1862, where he was wounded by Italian troops (1);
the attempt that ended at Mentana in 1867, where French and papal forces defeated him (1). **[2]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **Piedmont kept its
constitution**, the Statuto, when every other ruler cancelled theirs, so liberals saw it as the only free state;
**the failure of the alternatives**, because Mazzini's republic and the papal federation had both collapsed in
1848–49, leaving monarchy as the only realistic route; **economic and military strength**, built by Cavour in the
1850s, which made it a credible partner for France; **thousands of exiles** from other states settled in Piedmont and
spread its influence. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **Garibaldi's loyalty to Victor Emmanuel**: he had fought under the
slogan "Italy and Victor Emmanuel" and put unity before his own republican views; **fear of civil war**, because the
Piedmontese army was already in the Papal States and a conflict between Italians would ruin unity; **the threat from
France**, since any advance on Rome risked war with Napoleon III's garrison; **the difficulty of governing the south**,
which was disordered and needed a state to run it; **plebiscites** in October 1860 had voted overwhelmingly for union
with Piedmont. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:**
Radetzky's army recovered from the Five Days, used the Quadrilateral fortresses and won at Custoza and Novara;
Piedmont alone could not match it. **Disagree:** Italians were divided between monarchists and republicans and did not
co-operate; Naples and Tuscany withdrew support; the Pope's refusal in April 1848 split the movement; French troops,
not Austrians, destroyed the Roman Republic; peasants did not join in. **Judgement:** for example, Austrian strength
was decisive in the north, but division meant that Italians never brought their full strength against it, so
disunity explains why Austrian power was never seriously tested. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** Austria lost Lombardy in 1859 and Venetia in 1866; it lost influence over
the central duchies, whose rulers were its relatives; the defeat of 1859 exposed military weakness that encouraged
Prussia; fighting Italy in 1866 divided Austrian forces when facing Prussia. **Disagree:** France was also deeply
affected: it gained Nice and Savoy, but Napoleon III angered Catholics and became tied to defending Rome; the Papacy
lost its lands and its temporal power in 1870; Prussia benefited from the Italian alliance of 1866. **Judgement:**
Austria lost most territory and status, but the Pope lost his whole state, so a case can be made either way if it is
supported. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 4 (6–7): explains a purpose using the source's content and the context of April 1860. Level 3
(4–5): explains the context or the message. Level 2 (2–3): describes the source's view. Level 1 (1): undeveloped
comment. Indicative content: **context**: the Treaty of Turin (March 1860) had just given Nice and Savoy to France as
the price for French acceptance of Piedmont's gains in central Italy; Garibaldi, born in Nice, was furious; **purpose**:
to attack Cavour and turn parliament and public opinion against the cession; to argue that Piedmont's expansion was
not the same as true Italian nationhood. **Top answers** link the speaker's claim that Cavour "has not made Italy" to
the wider argument between Cavour's limited aims and the national ideal. **[7]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (7–8): explains how far Source C explains Source B, using both sources and knowledge, with a
judgement. Level 3 (5–6): explains either how it does or how it does not. Level 2 (2–4): compares content without
explanation. Level 1 (1): general comment. **Explains:** Source C gives the reason for the invasion that Source B
condemns: Cavour sent the army through the Papal States in September 1860 to stop Garibaldi attacking Rome, which
would have brought war with France. **Does not explain:** Source C does not explain why Napoleon III allowed it, which
Source B complains of; he accepted the move because it prevented a revolutionary attack on Rome while leaving the city
itself to the Pope. **Provenance:** Source B is written by French Catholics angry at the loss of papal lands; Source C
is a soldier justifying the operation. **Judgement:** Source C explains the motive well but not the whole situation
described in Source B. **[8]**
