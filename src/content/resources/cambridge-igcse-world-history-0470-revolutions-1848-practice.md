These questions cover key question 1.1 of Cambridge IGCSE History 0470: "Were the revolutions of 1848 important?"

## Section A

**1.** Give **two** liberal demands made by revolutionaries in 1848. **[2]**

**2.** Identify **three** economic problems in Europe between 1845 and 1847 that helped cause the revolutions. **[3]**

**3.** State **two** reforms introduced by the Second Republic in France in 1848. **[2]**

**4.** Describe the events of the June Days in Paris. **[4]**

**5.** Describe the actions of the Frankfurt Parliament in 1848-49. **[4]**

**6.** Name **three** generals or rulers who used military force to defeat revolutions in 1848-49. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Describe the revolution in Vienna and the Austrian Empire in 1848. **[4]**

**8.** Why were there so many revolutions in Europe in 1848? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** Why did the revolutions in Italy fail in 1848-49? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**10.** "The revolutions of 1848 achieved nothing." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** "Divisions between nationalities were the main reason for the failure of the revolutions in the Austrian
Empire." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**12.** Study the source below, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from the diary of a Berlin shopkeeper, written in November 1848. "In March we cheered the
students at the barricades and the King bowed to the dead. Now I cheer the soldiers. The workers' clubs talk of
dividing property, the Assembly talks of nothing at all, and my shop has been shut for a week. Let the King rule
again, if only there is order."

What does Source A suggest about why the revolution in Prussia failed? Use the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two, 1 mark each: a written constitution (1); an elected parliament (1); freedom of the press or of speech
(1); a wider right to vote (1); equality before the law (1); an end to censorship (1). **[2]**

**2.** Any three, 1 mark each: potato blight from 1845 (1); poor grain harvests in 1846 (1); high bread and food
prices (1); a commercial and industrial slump in 1847 (1); unemployment in the towns (1); artisans losing work to
factory production (1). **[3]**

**3.** Any two, 1 mark each: universal male suffrage (1); National Workshops for the unemployed (1); abolition of
slavery in the French colonies (1); a republic in place of the monarchy (1); freedom of the press (1). **[2]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: the assembly elected in April 1848 was cautious and conservative (1); it closed the
National Workshops in June (1); Paris workers rose and built barricades (1); General Cavaignac was given power to
restore order (1); the army crushed the rising after several days of fighting (1); thousands were killed, arrested or
deported (1); the middle class and the provinces supported the government against the workers (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any four, 1 mark each: it was elected and met in May 1848 in Frankfurt (1); its members were mostly
middle-class lawyers, officials and professors (1); it debated whether Germany should include Austria (grossdeutsch)
or not (kleindeutsch) (1); it declared the Basic Rights of the German people (1); it relied on Prussian troops in the
war with Denmark over Schleswig-Holstein, and Prussia made peace without it (1); it drew up a constitution in 1849 (1);
it offered the crown to Frederick William IV, who refused (1); it then broke up in 1849 (1). **[4]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: Radetzky (1); Windischgrätz (1); Cavaignac (1); Frederick William IV (1); Tsar Nicholas
I (1); Louis Napoleon, who sent troops to Rome (1); Jelačić (1). **[3]**

**7.** Any four, 1 mark each: news from Paris led to a rising in Vienna in March 1848 (1); students and workers took to
the streets (1); Metternich resigned and fled (1); Emperor Ferdinand promised a constitution (1); Hungary under
Kossuth gained its own government through the April Laws (1); Czech nationalists met in Prague (1); labour services
were abolished in September (1); Windischgrätz bombarded Prague in June and retook Vienna in October (1); Ferdinand
abdicated in favour of Franz Joseph in December 1848 (1). **[4]**

**8.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **economic crisis** made people desperate, because failed harvests and the 1847 slump raised bread prices and
caused unemployment in Paris, Vienna and Berlin, so crowds were ready to act; **the growth of liberal and national
ideas** meant the middle classes had a programme, because newspapers and universities had spread demands for
constitutions and national unity that rulers had repressed since 1815; **the example of Paris**, because the fall of
Louis-Philippe in February showed that thrones could fall, and news spread quickly by newspaper and railway; **weak
or hesitant rulers**, such as Frederick William IV, who withdrew his troops from Berlin, encouraged further
demands. **[6]**

**9.** Levels as for Question 8. Indicative content: **Austrian military strength**, because Radetzky regrouped after
the Five Days and beat Piedmont at Custoza (1848) and Novara (1849); **lack of unity**, because the states and leaders
had different aims: Charles Albert wanted to enlarge Piedmont, Mazzini wanted a republic, and others wanted a
federation; **the Pope's refusal** to fight Catholic Austria (April 1848), which removed a leader many moderates had
hoped for; **the King of Naples** withdrew his troops; **foreign intervention**, because French troops sent by Louis
Napoleon destroyed the Roman Republic in 1849; **limited peasant support** for a movement led from the towns. **[6]**

**10.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** by 1849-50 monarchs were back in power in Vienna, Berlin, Naples and Rome; Austria regained control of
northern Italy and the German Confederation; the Frankfurt Parliament produced no united Germany; France's republic
ended in Louis Napoleon's coup. **Disagree:** labour services and serfdom ended in the Austrian Empire; France kept
universal male suffrage; Piedmont kept the Statuto and became the leader of Italian hopes; Prussia kept a parliament;
the failure taught Cavour and Bismarck that unification needed armies and diplomacy. **Judgement:** for example, the
revolutions failed in their main aims but changed the methods by which nationalism later succeeded. **[10]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content. **Agree:** the Croats under Jelačić fought for the emperor
against Hungary; Czech leaders refused to join a German national state; Hungarian leaders denied other nationalities
the rights they claimed for themselves; the government could play one nationality against another. **Disagree:** the
army was decisive: Windischgrätz bombarded Prague and retook Vienna, and Radetzky won in Italy; Russian intervention
crushed Hungary in 1849; peasants withdrew after labour services were abolished; the middle classes in Vienna were
alarmed by radical workers and students. **Judgement:** for example, national divisions gave the government allies,
but without a loyal army and Russian help Hungary might not have been defeated. **[10]**

**12.** Levels. Level 3 (5-6): explains what the source suggests, supported by its details and by contextual
knowledge. Level 2 (3-4): explains what the source suggests using its details only. Level 1 (1-2): describes the
source's content or gives knowledge without using the source. Indicative content: the source suggests the
revolution lost **middle-class support**, because the shopkeeper who "cheered the students" in March now wants order
and "cheer[s] the soldiers"; it suggests **fear of radical workers**, because the workers' clubs talk of "dividing
property"; it suggests the **assembly was ineffective**, because it "talks of nothing at all"; it suggests **economic
disruption** turned people against revolution ("my shop has been shut"). Knowledge: Frederick William IV withdrew his
troops in March 1848 after the street fighting; in November 1848 troops returned to Berlin and the Prussian assembly
was dissolved in December, meeting little resistance. **[6]**
