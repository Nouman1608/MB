## Section A: short questions

**1.** What is meant by **othering**? **[2]**

**2.** Identify **two** of Goffman's types of stigma. **[2]**

**3.** What is meant by a **master status**? **[2]**

**4.** Identify **two** examples of a total institution. **[2]**

**5.** Study **Item A** and answer the questions that follow.

> **Item A** — Extract from an unstructured interview with a man released from prison
>
> *Original extract, invented for this practice question.*
>
> "When I apply for jobs I leave the gap in my work history blank. If they ask, I say I was caring for
> a relative. At my last job someone found out, and after that people stopped inviting me to lunch.
> My manager watched me every time money went missing. I was the same person, but suddenly I was 'the
> one who'd been inside'."

**(a)** Using Item A, identify **one** example of passing. **[1]**

**(b)** Using Item A, explain how the speaker's stigma became a master status. **[3]**

## Section B: longer questions

**6.** Explain the difference between a **discredited** and a **discreditable** stigma. **[4]**

**7.** Describe what Goffman meant by the **mortification of the self** in total institutions. **[4]**

**8.** Explain **two** ways in which othering may affect a minority group. **[6]**

**9.** Explain Fanon's argument about the effects of colonial racism on colonised people. **[4]**

**10.** Explain how stigmatised identities can act as a form of social control. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss how far labels and stigma explain why some people continue to offend. Use examples
from your own society or a society you know about. **[12]**

## Answers: Section A

**A1** [2 marks]

Defining a group as **fundamentally different** from "us" [1] and usually as **inferior** or
threatening, which justifies treating them unequally [1].

**A2** [2 marks — 1 each]

Any two: physical (bodily differences or disabilities); character (for example a criminal record,
addiction or mental illness); "tribal" (race, nation or religion).

**A3** [2 marks]

A label or status that **overrides all of a person's other statuses** [1], so they are seen first in
terms of that label, for example "ex-prisoner" rather than parent or worker (Becker) [1].

**A4** [2 marks — 1 each]

Any two: prisons; psychiatric hospitals; military training camps; boarding institutions where
residents live under one authority; monasteries.

**A5 (a)** [1 mark]

Leaving the gap in his work history blank, or saying he was caring for a relative, to conceal his
prison record [1].

**A5 (b)** [3 marks]

Once his record became known, colleagues excluded him from lunch [1] and his manager suspected him
whenever money went missing [1], so the label "the one who'd been inside" overrode his identity as a
worker and colleague: "I was the same person" [1].

## Answers: Section B

**A6** [4 marks]

Goffman distinguished stigma by whether others know about it [1]. A **discredited** stigma is visible
or already known, such as a visible disability [1]. A **discreditable** stigma is hidden but could be
revealed [1], such as a past prison sentence, so the person may try to conceal it by passing [1].

**A7** [4 marks]

In total institutions, people live cut off from society under one authority [1]. On entry, their
previous identity is stripped away [1], for example by removing possessions, clothes or sometimes
names and imposing uniforms and routines [1]. This prepares them to accept the institution's rules,
an example of formal control and re-socialisation [1].

**A8** [6 marks — up to 3 per way]

Two from: **discrimination** [1] — members may be refused jobs, housing or services [1] because they
are seen through a negative stereotype [1]; **exclusion or segregation** [1] — the group may be
avoided or kept apart [1], reducing contact and reinforcing the stereotype [1]; **internalisation**
[1] — members may come to see themselves as inferior (Fanon) [1], lowering confidence and aspirations
[1]; **blame** [1] — the group may be blamed for social problems in the media [1], increasing hostility
or hate crime [1].

**A9** [4 marks]

In *Black Skin, White Masks* (1952) [1], Fanon argued that colonialism taught Black colonised people
to see themselves through the eyes of the white coloniser [1]. As a result they came to absorb the
coloniser's view of them as inferior [1], so racism damaged not only how they were treated but how
they saw themselves [1].

**A10** [6 marks]

Stigma attaches **shame** to a characteristic or behaviour [1], so people are discouraged from it for
fear of being devalued [1]. Those stigmatised may be **excluded** from work and social life [1], which
marks the boundary of acceptable behaviour for everyone else [1]. Stigmatised people may try to
**conceal** their identity through passing [1], showing how stigma controls behaviour in everyday
interaction [1]. Maximum 6.

**A11** [12 marks — levels]

| Level | Marks | Descriptor |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Detailed knowledge; well applied to a named society; balanced discussion with a clear, justified conclusion |
| 3 | 7–9 | Good knowledge; some application; discussion with a conclusion |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some relevant knowledge; limited application or one-sided |
| 1 | 1–3 | Basic, mainly descriptive points |

Indicative content:

- **Labels and stigma explain reoffending:** Becker's master status; self-fulfilling prophecy;
  Goffman's spoiled identity; exclusion from work and housing after prison; joining others with the
  same label.
- **Other explanations:** material factors such as poverty and unemployment; peer groups and
  subcultures; individual choice; lack of support after release.
- **Limits of labelling:** not everyone labelled reoffends; some people reject labels and desist.
- **Application:** a named society, for example whether employers there must be told about criminal
  records, and what support exists for people leaving prison.
- **Judgement:** for example, stigma makes reoffending more likely by closing legitimate routes, but
  it works alongside material and social factors.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing discredited and discreditable.
- Defining othering without the idea of inferiority.
- Naming Goffman or Fanon without their arguments.
- Not linking stigma to social control when the question asks.
- An extended answer with no named society.
