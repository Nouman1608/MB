## Questions

**1.** Distinguish between a specific tax and an ad valorem tax. **[3]**

**2.** In a market for garden gates, demand is Qd = 500 − 4P and supply is Qs = −160 + 6P.

**(a)** Calculate the equilibrium price and quantity. **[2]**
**(b)** An ad valorem tax of 25% is added to the sellers' price. Calculate the price sellers receive, the price buyers pay and the new quantity. **[3]**
**(c)** Calculate the tax revenue and the amount of the tax borne by consumers. **[3]**

**3.** In a market for bicycle servicing, demand is Qd = 300 − 5P and supply is Qs = −60 + 7P. The government pays workshops a subsidy of 12 cu per service.

**(a)** Find the price and quantity with no subsidy. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the price customers pay with the subsidy, and the new quantity. **[2]**
**(c)** Work out what the subsidy costs the government. **[1]**
**(d)** Calculate the gain to customers and the gain to workshops, and state which group benefits more. **[2]**

**4.** A market for portable heaters has these marginal values for each extra unit. Each unit causes an external cost of 14 cu.

| Unit | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Marginal private benefit | 60 | 52 | 44 | 36 | 28 | 20 |
| Marginal private cost | 14 | 18 | 22 | 26 | 30 | 34 |

**(a)** State how many units the free market produces. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the socially optimal number of units. **[2]**
**(c)** State the specific tax that achieves it and calculate the welfare gain. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one reason why the tax may not be set correctly in practice. **[1]**

**5.** In a market for repair appointments, demand is Qd = 450 − 6P and supply is Qs = −90 + 6P. The government sets a maximum price of 35 cu.

**(a)** Find the price and quantity that the market would reach without the cap. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the size of the shortage. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the price buyers would pay for the last appointment supplied. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain one effect on the quality of service. **[1]**

**6.** In a market for a cheap strong drink, demand is Qd = 640 − 8P and supply is Qs = −80 + 12P. A minimum price of 42 cu is set.

**(a)** Find the free-market price and quantity. **[1]**
**(b)** Find the quantity demanded at 42 cu and the percentage fall in consumption from the original equilibrium. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the change in consumer spending. **[2]**
**(d)** Comment on what your results suggest about the policy's effectiveness. **[1]**

**7.** In a market for sea fish, demand is Qd = 720 − 6P and supply is Qs = −180 + 12P. A quota limits output to 360 tonnes.

**(a)** Calculate the free-market equilibrium price and quantity. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the price buyers pay under the quota. **[1]**
**(c)** Calculate the price at which suppliers would supply 360 tonnes and the total quota rent. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how a specific tax could produce the same output and who would receive the equivalent amount. **[2]**

**8.** A rail operator is a natural monopoly. Demand is P = 90 − Q, marginal cost is 10 cu and fixed cost is 700 cu, so average cost AC = 10 + 700 ÷ Q.

**(a)** Calculate the profit-maximising output and price. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the profit. **[2]**
**(c)** A regulator caps the price at average cost. Calculate the new output and price. **[3]**
**(d)** Calculate the loss if price were capped at marginal cost and explain why a regulator may reject this cap. **[2]**

**9.** A council runs a free library. The yearly cost is 90,000 cu. It has 1,500 regular users, each gaining a benefit worth 70 cu a year.

**(a)** Calculate the total benefit. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the benefit-cost ratio and the net benefit. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the cost per user. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why a private firm might not supply the service. **[2]**

**10.** A town issues 80 market-stall licences at 1,250 cu each. Inspecting the stalls costs 62,000 cu.

**(a)** Calculate the licence revenue. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net revenue after inspection costs. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain how a ban on a dangerous product would differ in its likely effects from a licence. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain one disadvantage of capping the number of licences. **[2]**

**11.** Explain how deregulating a local bus market could change prices, output and service quality. **[8]**

**12.** A solvent used in factories imposes pollution costs on a nearby river. The market price is 60 cu a litre, and demand has a price elasticity of −0.4. Evaluate whether a specific tax of 12 cu, a production quota or a ban is the most suitable policy. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A specific tax is a fixed sum per unit, such as 6 cu on each item [1]. An ad valorem tax is a fixed percentage of each sale's value [1]. A specific tax shifts supply in parallel, whereas an ad valorem tax pivots supply because the tax per unit rises with the price [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A distinction compares both sides; two separate definitions leave the difference unstated.

**2. (a)** 500 − 4P = −160 + 6P, so 10P = 660 [1]. P = 66 and Q = 236 [1].
**(b)** Buyers pay 1.25Ps, so 500 − 5Ps = −160 + 6Ps [1]. Ps = 660 ÷ 11 = 60 [1]. Buyers pay 75 and Q = 500 − 300 = 200 [1].
**(c)** Tax per unit = 15 cu, so revenue = 15 × 200 = **3,000 cu** [1]. Consumer burden = (75 − 66) × 200 [1] = **1,800 cu** [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Substitute 1.25 times the sellers' price into demand. Taking 25% of the equilibrium price gives a different, wrong tax.

**3. (a)** 300 − 5P = −60 + 7P, so P = 30 and Q = 150 [1].
**(b)** Qs = −60 + 7(P + 12) = 24 + 7P [1]. 300 − 5P = 24 + 7P gives **P = 23, Q = 185** [1].
**(c)** Cost = 12 × 185 = **2,220 cu** [1].
**(d)** Customers gain (30 − 23) × 185 = 1,295 cu. Workshops receive 35, so gain 5 × 185 = 925 cu [1]. Customers benefit more, as demand is less elastic than supply here [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Check that the two gains add to the cost.

**4. (a)** The market produces **4 units** (36 against 26; unit 5 has 28 against 30) [1].
**(b)** Marginal social cost adds 14: 28, 32, 36, 40, 44, 48 [1]. Unit 3 passes (44 against 36) and unit 4 fails (36 against 40), so **3 units** [1].
**(c)** Tax = **14 cu**, equal to the marginal external cost [1]. Welfare gain = unit 4 costs society 40 for a benefit of 36 = **4 cu** [1].
**(d)** Putting a value on the damage is difficult, so the rate may miss the true cost [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare marginal benefit with marginal social cost unit by unit.

**5. (a)** 450 − 6P = −90 + 6P, so 12P = 540 [1]. P = 45 and Q = 180 [1].
**(b)** At 35: Qd = 450 − 210 = 240 and Qs = −90 + 210 = 120 [1]. Shortage = **120** [1].
**(c)** Price on demand at 120 = (450 − 120) ÷ 6 = **55 cu** [1].
**(d)** Providers may cut quality because buyers queue regardless [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Quantity traded is the lower figure, 120. Using the demand quantity there overstates sales.

**6. (a)** 640 − 8P = −80 + 12P gives P = 36 and Q = 352 [1].
**(b)** Qd at 42 = 640 − 336 = 304 [1]. Fall = 48 ÷ 352 = **13.6%** [1].
**(c)** Before: 352 × 36 = 12,672 cu. After: 304 × 42 = 12,768 cu [1]. Spending rises by **96 cu** [1].
**(d)** Price rises 16.7% and consumption falls 13.6%, so demand is inelastic (about −0.82), and the extra spending goes to sellers [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Link the numbers just calculated to elasticity.

**7. (a)** 720 − 6P = −180 + 12P, so P = 50 and Q = 420 [1].
**(b)** P = (720 − 360) ÷ 6 = **60 cu** [1].
**(c)** Supply price = (360 + 180) ÷ 12 = **45 cu** [1]. Rent = 15 × 360 = **5,400 cu** [1].
**(d)** A specific tax of 15 cu per tonne gives buyers 60 and sellers 45, and output 360 [1]. The government would receive the 5,400 cu [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Say who gets the money.

**8. (a)** MR = 90 − 2Q = 10, so **Q = 40** [1] and **P = 50** [1].
**(b)** AC = 10 + 700 ÷ 40 = 27.5 [1]. Profit = (50 − 27.5) × 40 = **900 cu** [1].
**(c)** 90 − Q = 10 + 700 ÷ Q gives Q² − 80Q + 700 = 0 [1]. Q = 70 or 10; take the larger root [1]. **Q = 70, P = 20** [1].
**(d)** At P = 10, Q = 80 and the loss = fixed cost = **700 cu** [1]. The state must fund it [1]. **[9]**
*Examiner insight:* A quadratic gives two roots; say why the larger one is chosen.

**9. (a)** 1,500 × 70 = **105,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** Ratio = 105,000 ÷ 90,000 = **1.17** [1]. Net benefit = **15,000 cu** [1].
**(c)** 90,000 ÷ 1,500 = **60 cu** [1].
**(d)** Benefits differ between users, so some would not pay a fee covering the cost [1]. Wider social benefits do not reach the firm as revenue [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* A ratio above 1 supports provision, but add that some benefits are hard to value.

**10. (a)** 80 × 1,250 = **100,000 cu** [1].
**(b)** 100,000 − 62,000 = **38,000 cu** [1].
**(c)** A ban may push demand to a black market [1]. A licence keeps supply legal and lets the town set standards [1].
**(d)** A cap limits entry, so competition falls [1]. Prices may rise and new traders are shut out [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Give each disadvantage with its consequence.

**11.** Deregulation removes entry rules or fare controls [1]. New operators enter, so supply rises [1]. Competition may cut fares and costs [1]. Output rises on busy routes [1]. Firms may drop unprofitable routes [1]. Quality can fall if safety checks weaken [1]. The effect depends on how contestable the market is and on demand elasticity [1]. A conclusion weighs consumer gain against lost services [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* Treat price, output and quality separately, then judge.

**12.** The solvent has a negative externality, so the market over-produces [1]. A 12 cu tax raises the price by 12 ÷ 60 = 20% if fully passed on [1]. With elasticity −0.4, quantity falls by about 20 × 0.4 = 8% [1]. Demand is inelastic, so the tax mainly raises revenue and reduces pollution only slightly [1]. A quota gives certainty about total output [1], but the rent goes to firms and allocation can be unfair [1]. A ban works only if enforced and may drive a black market [1]. The tax is flexible and its revenue could fund river clean-up [1]. A quota or larger tax suits better if the damage is severe [1]. A reasoned conclusion on the size of the external cost and enforcement is needed [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Reach a decision and state its condition; listing the three policies without choosing leaves the judgement incomplete.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Treating an ad valorem rate as a percentage of the equilibrium price rather than of the sellers' price.
- Mixing up the price buyers pay and the price sellers keep.
- Using the demand quantity when a price control binds, instead of the lower quantity traded.
