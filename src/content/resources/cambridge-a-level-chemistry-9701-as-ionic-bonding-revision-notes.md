**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level**, subtopic 3.2 Ionic bonding.

## The two learning outcomes

1. **Define** ionic bonding: the **electrostatic attraction** between **oppositely charged ions** (positively charged **cations** and negatively charged **anions**).
2. **Describe** ionic bonding, including **sodium chloride**, **magnesium oxide** and **calcium fluoride**, and any analogous compound.

## Recall: what happens to the electrons

- A metal atom **loses** its outer-shell electrons and becomes a cation.
- A non-metal atom **gains** electrons and becomes an anion.
- The ions usually have a **noble-gas configuration**: for Li⁺ that is helium (1s²), not neon.
- Ionic bonding is favoured by a **large electronegativity difference** (Pauling values are given in questions when needed).

### The named examples

| Compound | Electrons transferred | Ions and configurations | Ratio | Arrangement in lattice |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| NaCl | 1 electron from Na to Cl | Na⁺ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; Cl⁻ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ | 1 : 1 | each ion surrounded by 6 of the opposite charge |
| MgO | 2 electrons from Mg to O | Mg²⁺ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶; O²⁻ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ | 1 : 1 | same arrangement as NaCl |
| CaF₂ | 1 electron from Ca to **each of two** F atoms | Ca²⁺ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶; F⁻ 1s² 2s² 2p⁶ | 1 : 2 | each Ca²⁺ surrounded by 8 F⁻; each F⁻ by 4 Ca²⁺ |

### Common ions for analogous compounds

| Group | 1 | 2 | 13 | 15 | 16 | 17 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Ion charge | 1+ | 2+ | 3+ | 3− | 2− | 1− |
| Examples | Li⁺, Na⁺, K⁺ | Mg²⁺, Ca²⁺ | Al³⁺ | N³⁻ | O²⁻, S²⁻ | F⁻, Cl⁻, Br⁻ |

**Formula from charge balance:** total positive charge = total negative charge.

    Li⁺ and O²⁻    →  Li₂O      (2 × +1 = +2)
    Al³⁺ and O²⁻   →  Al₂O₃     (2 × +3 = +6; 3 × −2 = −6)
    Mg²⁺ and N³⁻   →  Mg₃N₂     (3 × +2 = +6; 2 × −3 = −6)
    K⁺ and S²⁻     →  K₂S

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Ionic bond | Electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions |
| Cation | Positively charged ion, formed by losing electrons |
| Anion | Negatively charged ion, formed by gaining electrons |
| Giant ionic lattice | Regular three-dimensional arrangement of very many ions, each attracted to all the oppositely charged ions around it |
| Isoelectronic | Having the same number of electrons, e.g. Na⁺, Mg²⁺, O²⁻ and F⁻ (10 each) |
| Formula of an ionic compound | The simplest ratio of ions, not a molecule |

## Strength of ionic bonding (used for properties)

- Attraction is **stronger** when ions have **higher charge** and **smaller radius** (ions closer together).
- **MgO vs NaCl:** 2+/2− ions instead of 1+/1−, and Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ are smaller than Na⁺ and Cl⁻. Much stronger attraction, so MgO melts at about 2850 °C and NaCl at 801 °C.
- **Ion size in isoelectronic ions:** more protons for the same number of electrons means a smaller ion. Size order: O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺.
- Lattice energy calculations are A Level (Topic 23), not needed here.

## Exam traps

- "Transfer of electrons" is **not** the definition of an ionic bond.
- Never write "molecule of NaCl"; write "lattice" or "formula unit".
- In CaF₂, calcium gives **one electron to each of two** fluorine atoms.
- Give configurations in **sub-shell notation** (1s² 2s² 2p⁶), not just 2,8.
- When comparing melting points, mention **charge and ionic radius**, and refer to **attraction between ions**, not "intermolecular forces".
- The attraction acts **in all directions**; there is no single bond between one cation and one anion.
- Covalent character in ionic compounds is **not assessed**.

## Self-test

1. Define ionic bonding.
2. Write the full electron configuration of a magnesium ion.
3. Describe how calcium atoms and fluorine atoms form calcium fluoride.
4. State the number of chloride ions surrounding each sodium ion in solid sodium chloride.
5. Deduce the formula of aluminium fluoride.
6. Which noble gas has the same electron configuration as Li⁺?
7. Name two ions in the table above that are isoelectronic with Ar.
8. Explain why Mg²⁺ is smaller than Na⁺.
9. Explain why magnesium oxide has a much higher melting point than sodium chloride.
10. Pauling values: K 0.8, Br 2.6. Predict the bonding in potassium bromide and justify your answer.
11. A student writes "NaCl molecules are held together by ionic bonds." Correct the statement.
12. Describe the bonding in potassium sulfide, K₂S.

**Answers:**

1. The electrostatic attraction between oppositely charged ions (cations and anions).
2. 1s² 2s² 2p⁶.
3. Each Ca atom loses its two 4s electrons, one to each of two F atoms; this forms Ca²⁺ and two F⁻ ions, held by electrostatic attraction in a giant lattice.
4. Six.
5. AlF₃ (one Al³⁺ balances three F⁻).
6. Helium.
7. Two of Ca²⁺, Cl⁻ and S²⁻ (K⁺ also).
8. Both have 10 electrons, but Mg²⁺ has 12 protons compared with 11, so its electrons are pulled closer to the nucleus.
9. Mg²⁺ and O²⁻ have double charges and are smaller than Na⁺ and Cl⁻, so the electrostatic attraction between the ions is stronger and more energy is needed to overcome it.
10. Difference = 1.8, which is large, so the bonding is ionic: K⁺ and Br⁻ ions.
11. Sodium chloride is a giant ionic lattice of Na⁺ and Cl⁻ ions; there are no molecules. The ions are held by electrostatic attraction in all directions.
12. Each of two K atoms (…4s¹) loses one electron to the same S atom (1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁴), giving two K⁺ and one S²⁻ (both with the argon configuration), held by electrostatic attraction in a giant lattice.
