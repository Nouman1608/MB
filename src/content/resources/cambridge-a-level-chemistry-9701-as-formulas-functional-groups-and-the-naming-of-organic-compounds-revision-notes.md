These notes condense subtopic **13.1, Formulas, functional groups and the naming of organic compounds** (Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, AS Level). Worked examples are in the [13.1 study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-chemistry-9701-as-formulas-functional-groups-and-the-naming-of-organic-compounds/). The [Introduction to Organic Chemistry revision notes](/resources/as-chemistry-organic-introduction-revision-notes/) cover Topic 13 as a whole.

## Definitions

- **Hydrocarbon**: a compound made up of C and H atoms only.
- **Alkane**: a simple hydrocarbon with **no functional group**.
- **Functional group**: the atom or group of atoms that dictates the physical and chemical properties of a homologous series.

## The syllabus functional groups

| Series | Group | General formula | Ending / prefix |
|---|---|---|---|
| Alkene | C=C | CₙH₂ₙ | -ene |
| Halogenoalkane | –X | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁X | chloro-, bromo-, iodo- |
| Alcohol | –OH, hydroxyl | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH | -ol |
| Aldehyde | –CHO, carbonyl | CₙH₂ₙO | -al |
| Ketone | C=O, carbonyl | CₙH₂ₙO | -one |
| Carboxylic acid | –COOH, carboxyl | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | -oic acid |
| Ester | –COO–, ester | CₙH₂ₙO₂ | alkyl -oate |
| Amine (primary only) | –NH₂, amine | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁NH₂ | -amine |
| Nitrile | –C≡N, nitrile | CₙH₂ₙ₊₁CN | -nitrile |

Same general formula, different series: aldehydes and ketones (CₙH₂ₙO); acids and esters (CₙH₂ₙO₂).

## Formula types

| Type | Shows |
|---|---|
| Molecular | Actual number of each atom |
| Empirical | Simplest whole-number ratio |
| General | Series formula in terms of n |
| Structural | Arrangement without bonds drawn, e.g. CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ |
| Displayed | Every atom and bond |
| Skeletal | Zigzag carbon chain; C and H-on-C omitted |

Conventions: aldehyde **–CHO**; ester **RCOOR′** (acid part first); branches in brackets after their carbon.

## Naming rules

1. Principal functional group → suffix.
2. Longest chain containing it → stem (meth, eth, prop, but, pent, hex).
3. Lowest number to the principal group (none needed for -al, -oic acid, -nitrile).
4. Side groups as prefixes, alphabetical; di-, tri- for repeats.
5. Tie → lowest set of numbers; still tied → alphabetical first gets the lower number.
6. Commas between numbers, hyphens between numbers and words.

**Syllabus limits:** up to **six carbons**; esters up to six plus six; **straight chains only** for esters and nitriles.

## Quick naming bank

```
CH3CH2CH(OH)CH3          butan-2-ol
(CH3)2CHCH2Cl            1-chloro-2-methylpropane
CH3CH2CH2CH2CHO          pentanal
CH3CH2COCH2CH3           pentan-3-one
CH3CH=CHCH3              but-2-ene
(CH3)2CHCOOH             2-methylpropanoic acid
CH3CH2CN                 propanenitrile
CH3COOCH2CH2CH3          propyl ethanoate
CH3CH2NH2                ethylamine
```

## Esters: two-part names

Split at the single-bonded O: **alcohol part first** (alkyl), **acid part second** (-oate, counting the C=O carbon). CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ = methyl propanoate; CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ = ethyl ethanoate.

## Molecular → empirical

- Count all atoms, including implied H in skeletal formulas.
- Divide by the highest common factor.
- C₆H₁₄ → C₃H₇; C₄H₈O₂ → C₂H₄O; C₆H₁₀ → C₃H₅; C₃H₆O → C₃H₆O.

## From a name to a structure

Work backwards through the name:

1. **Stem and suffix** give the chain length and principal group: "pentan-2-ol" is a five-carbon chain with –OH on carbon 2.
2. **Prefixes and locants** place the side groups: "3-methyl" puts CH₃ on carbon 3.
3. Fill every carbon up to four bonds with hydrogen.

Example: **3-methylpentan-2-ol** → CH₃CH(OH)CH(CH₃)CH₂CH₃. Example: **2,2-dichloropropane** → CH₃CCl₂CH₃.

## Primary, secondary, tertiary

Count the carbon atoms bonded to the carbon that carries –OH or –X:

- **Primary** (one): CH₃CH₂CH₂OH, propan-1-ol; CH₃CH₂Br, bromoethane.
- **Secondary** (two): CH₃CH(OH)CH₃, propan-2-ol; CH₃CHBrCH₃, 2-bromopropane.
- **Tertiary** (three): (CH₃)₃COH, 2-methylpropan-2-ol; (CH₃)₃CBr, 2-bromo-2-methylpropane.

Methanol and bromomethane, with no carbon attached, are counted with the primary compounds.

## Reading skeletal formulas

- Every vertex and line end is a carbon atom.
- Hydrogens on carbon are not drawn; add enough to give each carbon four bonds.
- O, N, halogens and the H of –OH or –NH₂ are always drawn.
- A double line is a C=C or C=O double bond; a triple line with N at the end is a nitrile.

## One formula, several names

C₄H₁₀O has four alcohol isomers, each with its own name: butan-1-ol, CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂OH; butan-2-ol, CH₃CH(OH)CH₂CH₃; 2-methylpropan-1-ol, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂OH; and 2-methylpropan-2-ol, (CH₃)₃COH. Naming each one correctly is the quickest check that you have not drawn the same isomer twice.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homologous series | Compounds with the same functional group and general formula, each differing by CH₂ |
| Aliphatic | Not containing a benzene ring |
| Locant | The number showing the position of a group on the chain |
| Primary / secondary / tertiary | One, two or three carbons attached to the carbon carrying the –OH or –X |
| Skeletal formula | Simplified formula showing the carbon skeleton as lines |
| Carbonyl | The C=O group in aldehydes and ketones |
| Carboxyl | The –COOH group in carboxylic acids |

## Exam traps

- Not choosing the longest chain (e.g. "2-ethylbutane" for 3-methylpentane).
- Numbering from the wrong end ("butan-3-ol").
- Adding a locant to an aldehyde or acid ("propan-1-al").
- Naming esters in the wrong order.
- Leaving out the C≡N carbon when naming nitriles.
- Writing –COH for an aldehyde.
- Forgetting implied hydrogens in skeletal formulas.
- Prefixes out of alphabetical order (methyl before bromo).

## Self-test

1. Define a hydrocarbon.
2. Name the functional group in a ketone and in a carboxylic acid.
3. Give the general formula of the alcohols.
4. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH(CH₃)CH₃.
5. Name CH₃CHClCH₂CH₂CH₃.
6. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂COOH.
7. Name CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃.
8. Name CH₃CH₂CH₂CH₂CN.
9. Write the structural formula of 2-methylpentan-3-one.
10. Give the molecular and empirical formulas of propyl ethanoate.
11. Is propan-2-ol a primary, secondary or tertiary alcohol?
12. Why is "2-methylpropanenitrile" outside the naming expected by the syllabus?

**Answers:**

1. A compound made up of carbon and hydrogen atoms only.
2. Ketone: carbonyl. Carboxylic acid: carboxyl.
3. CₙH₂ₙ₊₁OH
4. 2-methylpentane.
5. 2-chloropentane.
6. Butanoic acid.
7. Propyl propanoate.
8. Pentanenitrile.
9. CH₃CH(CH₃)COCH₂CH₃
10. Molecular C₅H₁₀O₂; empirical C₅H₁₀O₂ (cannot be simplified).
11. Secondary.
12. The syllabus asks for straight chains only for nitriles, and this nitrile is branched.
