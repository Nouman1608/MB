These notes cover section 19.2 Genetic technology applied to medicine (learning outcomes 1 to 4) of Topic 19, Genetic technology, in the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. It is A Level content, examined on Paper 4. Test yourself with the [practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-genetic-technology-applied-to-medicine-cambridge-practice/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Recombinant human protein | Human protein made by cells (bacteria, yeast, mammalian cells) given the human gene |
| Enzyme replacement therapy | Regular injection of a missing enzyme, e.g. ADA |
| Genetic screening | Testing DNA for alleles associated with a genetic disease |
| Carrier | Heterozygous for a recessive allele; no symptoms |
| Gene therapy | Inserting a functional allele into a person's cells to treat a genetic disease |
| Vector | Carrier of the allele into cells, e.g. retrovirus, AAV, liposome |
| Somatic gene therapy | Alters body cells; not inherited |
| Germ-line gene therapy | Alters gametes or embryos; inherited; prohibited in many countries |
| SCID | Severe combined immunodeficiency: almost no functional T and B cells |

## Recombinant proteins (19.2.1)

| Protein | Disease | Old source and its problem | Advantages of recombinant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Insulin | Type 1 diabetes | Pig or cattle pancreas; slightly different amino acids; immune responses; limited supply | Identical sequence; fewer immune responses; large, reliable supply; no animal-product objections |
| Factor VIII | Haemophilia A | Pooled donated plasma; transmitted HIV and hepatitis C | No infection risk; not limited by donations; made in mammalian cells (glycoprotein) |
| ADA | ADA-deficient SCID | Enzyme from cattle | Consistent supply for lifelong enzyme replacement; no animal tissue |

## Genetic screening (19.2.2)

| Condition | Key facts | Advantage |
|---|---|---|
| Breast cancer | BRCA1 / BRCA2 tumour suppressor genes; faulty allele raises risk | More frequent checks; early detection; preventive surgery; test relatives |
| Huntington's disease | Dominant; expanded CAG repeats; onset usually middle age | Life and family planning; embryo testing |
| Cystic fibrosis | Recessive; CFTR chloride channel gene | Carrier testing; embryo / fetal testing; newborn screening for early treatment |

*Worked reminder:* Ff × Ff → ¼ FF, ½ Ff (carriers), ¼ ff (affected).

*Worked reminder:* Hh × hh (Huntington's, H dominant) → ½ Hh affected, ½ hh unaffected.

## Gene therapy (19.2.3)

**SCID, method in steps**

1. Remove bone marrow stem cells.
2. Insert normal ADA allele with retrovirus / lentivirus vector.
3. Grow and return cells.
4. Stem cells give lymphocytes that make ADA.

Risk: vector inserted near a cell-division gene → leukaemia in some early trial patients.

**Inherited eye disease**

- AAV vector with normal allele injected under the retina.
- Eye is small and enclosed (small dose); retinal cells do not divide (allele not diluted); weak immune responses; other eye is a comparison.

**Vector comparison**

| Retrovirus | AAV | Liposome |
|---|---|---|
| Inserts into chromosomes; inherited by daughter cells | Usually stays separate; lasts in non-dividing cells | Fuses with membrane; no virus |
| Risk of disrupting genes | Low immune response | Less efficient |

## Social and ethical issues (19.2.4)

- **Screening for**: early diagnosis, prevention, informed choices.
- **Screening against**: anxiety (incurable conditions), effects on relatives, discrimination by employers or insurers, embryo selection debates.
- **Gene therapy for**: potential cure, not lifelong treatment.
- **Gene therapy against**: cost and access, immune reactions, cancer risk, germ-line changes affect people who cannot consent.

## Exam traps

- Recombinant insulin is made by microorganisms, not "taken from humans".
- Gene therapy **adds** an allele; it does not remove the faulty one.
- Somatic therapy is **not** inherited.
- Huntington's is dominant; cystic fibrosis is recessive.
- Factor VIII needs mammalian cells: bacteria cannot modify glycoproteins after translation.
- "Discuss" needs both sides.
- Listing advantages without comparing to the earlier source.
- Describing gene therapy without naming a vector or target cells.
- Ethical answers that list concerns but no benefits.

## Quick self-test

1. Give two advantages of recombinant human insulin over pig insulin.
2. What was the main risk of factor VIII from donated plasma?
3. Why is factor VIII made in mammalian cells, not bacteria?
4. What happens to lymphocytes in ADA deficiency?
5. Name the two genes screened for inherited breast cancer risk.
6. Give one advantage of screening for Huntington's disease.
7. Two carriers of cystic fibrosis have a child. What is the probability the child is affected?
8. Why are stem cells, not mature T cells, used in SCID gene therapy?
9. Give one risk of using a retrovirus vector.
10. Give two reasons the eye is suited to gene therapy.
11. What is the difference between somatic and germ-line gene therapy?
12. Give two ethical concerns about genetic screening.

### Answers

1. Any two: identical to human insulin, so fewer immune responses; large reliable supply; no animal-product objections; consistent purity.
2. Transmission of blood-borne viruses such as HIV and hepatitis C.
3. It is a glycoprotein needing modification after translation, which bacteria cannot do.
4. A toxic product accumulates and kills developing lymphocytes.
5. **BRCA1** and **BRCA2**.
6. Life or family planning; embryo testing; ending uncertainty if negative.
7. **1 in 4** (0.25).
8. Stem cells keep dividing, so the effect lasts; mature T cells die and treatment must be repeated.
9. It may insert into or near another gene, e.g. one controlling cell division, causing cancer.
10. Any two: small enclosed organ needs small dose; retinal cells do not divide; weak immune response; other eye as control.
11. Somatic alters body cells and is not inherited; germ-line alters gametes or embryos and is inherited.
12. Any two: anxiety; discrimination by employers or insurers; information about relatives; embryo selection issues.
