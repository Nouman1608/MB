## The bank statement and why it differs from the cash book

A **bank statement** is a copy of the business's account as recorded by the
bank. It lists payments out, receipts in and a running balance. The business
uses it to check its own bank column in the cash book, to learn about items
the bank has processed that the business has not yet recorded, and as a
source document for those items.

Two points about direction catch candidates out:

- The bank owes money to a customer who is in funds, so the bank records a
favourable balance as a **credit**. On the bank statement "Cr" means money
in the account and "Dr" (or "OD") means an **overdraft**.
- In the business's cash book the same favourable balance is a **debit**.
The two records are mirror images.

On any given date the cash book balance and the bank statement balance
rarely agree. The differences fall into two groups.

| Group | Items | What to do |
|---|---|---|
| Items on the bank statement, not yet in the cash book | Bank charges; bank interest paid; bank interest received; credit transfers (money paid straight into the account); direct debits and standing orders; errors in the cash book | **Update the cash book** |
| Items in the cash book, not yet on the bank statement, and bank errors | Unpresented cheques (paid out, not yet through the bank); uncredited deposits (paid in, not yet shown); errors made by the bank | **Show in the bank reconciliation statement** |

The test for each item: **has the business already recorded it?** If not,
it belongs in the cash book. If the business has recorded it correctly and
the bank has not yet (or has made a mistake), it belongs in the
reconciliation statement.

## Worked example 1: updating the cash book, then reconciling

Leila's cash book showed a debit balance of $3,420 on 30 November 2027. Her
bank statement on that date showed a credit balance of $4,615. Comparing the
two records revealed:

1. Bank charges of $45 on the statement only.
2. A standing order for insurance, $120, on the statement only.
3. A credit transfer of $560 from a customer, on the statement only.
4. A direct debit for electricity, $210, on the statement only.
5. Interest received of $15, on the statement only.
6. A cheque for $386 paid to a supplier was entered in the cash book as
$368.
7. A deposit of $1,240 on 30 November, in the cash book only.
8. Cheques for $1,965 and $438 in the cash book but not yet on the
statement.
9. The bank had debited a cheque for $150 belonging to another customer to
Leila's account.

**Step 1: update the cash book** with items 1 to 6.

```
Cash book (bank columns)
Date     Details                       $   Date     Details                       $
2027                                       2027
30 Nov   Balance b/d               3,420   30 Nov   Bank charges                 45
30 Nov   Credit transfer             560   30 Nov   Insurance (standing          120
30 Nov   Interest received            15              order)
                                           30 Nov   Electricity (direct          210
                                                      debit)
                                           30 Nov   Supplier (cheque             18
                                                      understated)
                                           30 Nov   Balance c/d               3,602
                                   -----                                      -----
                                   3,995                                      3,995
                                   =====                                      =====
1 Dec    Balance b/d               3,602
```

Item 6 needs only the $18 difference ($386 − $368): the cash book showed $18
too little paid out.

**Step 2: prepare the bank reconciliation statement** with items 7 to 9.

```
Leila
Bank reconciliation statement at 30 November 2027
                                                      $          $
Balance as per bank statement                                4,615
Add   Uncredited deposit                                     1,240
      Bank error: cheque wrongly debited                       150
                                                             -----
                                                             6,005
Less  Unpresented cheques                         1,965
                                                    438
                                                  -----
                                                             2,403
                                                             -----
Balance as per cash book                                     3,602
                                                             =====
```

The statement ends on the **updated** cash book balance. That agreement is
the proof that every difference has been explained. The statement of
financial position at 30 November shows bank of $3,602, the updated cash
book figure, not the bank statement figure.

Why each adjustment works when starting from the bank statement:

- An **uncredited deposit** will increase the bank balance when it clears,
so it is added.
- A **bank error** that reduced the balance wrongly is added back.
- An **unpresented cheque** will reduce the bank balance when it is
presented, so it is deducted.

A statement can also start from the cash book balance and end on the bank
statement balance. Every adjustment then reverses sign: unpresented cheques
are added and uncredited deposits deducted. Use whichever direction the
question asks for.

## Worked example 2: an overdraft

Omar's updated cash book showed a **credit** balance of $1,280 at 31 March
2028, so the business was overdrawn. Unpresented cheques totalled $690 and a
deposit of $400 had not been credited by the bank.

```
Omar
Bank reconciliation statement at 31 March 2028
                                                          $
Balance (overdraft) as per updated cash book         (1,280)
Add   Unpresented cheques                                690
                                                     -------
                                                       (590)
Less  Uncredited deposit                                (400)
                                                     -------
Balance (overdraft) as per bank statement              (990)
                                                     =======
```

Brackets show an overdraft. Adding unpresented cheques makes the overdraft
smaller, because the bank has not yet paid them. Deducting the uncredited
deposit makes it larger, because the bank has not yet received it. The bank
statement shows the $990 as a debit balance. In the statement of financial
position, the $1,280 overdraft from the cash book is a current liability.

## Purpose of a bank reconciliation statement

- It explains the difference between the cash book and the bank statement on
a given date.
- It confirms that the updated cash book balance is correct, so the right
bank figure is used in the statement of financial position.
- It reveals errors, whether made by the business or by the bank.
- It helps to detect fraud or unauthorised payments, especially when the
person preparing it does not also write up the cash book.
- It shows cheques that have not been presented for a long time, which may
need to be followed up.

## Impact of digital business transactions

More payments are now made by card, online transfer and direct debit rather
than by cheque. This changes the reconciliation in several ways:

- **Fewer timing differences.** Online transfers and card payments usually
reach the account quickly, so there are fewer unpresented cheques and
uncredited deposits at the date of the statement.
- **More items appear on the statement first.** Direct debits, card charges,
interest and payments from customers by transfer may reach the bank account
before the business records them, so updating the cash book is a larger part
of the task.
- **Easier, more frequent reconciliation.** Online banking lets the business
see transactions at any time, so it can reconcile weekly or daily instead of
waiting for a monthly paper statement.
- **Automatic matching.** Accounting software can import transactions from
the bank and match them with the cash book. Someone must still review the
items it cannot match and check for input errors.
- **New risks.** Unauthorised online payments or errors in a transfer
reference can be spotted sooner only if the reconciliation is actually done
regularly.

## Common mistakes

- Putting bank charges or direct debits in the reconciliation statement
instead of the cash book.
- Reading "Dr" on a bank statement as money in the account.
- Ending the reconciliation on the original, not the updated, cash book
balance.
- Correcting a cash book error with the full amount rather than the
difference.
- Using the bank statement balance in the statement of financial position.
- Adding unpresented cheques when starting from the bank statement balance.

## Exam technique

- **Prepare** the updated cash book as a ledger account with dates and
balances brought down, and the statement with a full heading "at" the date.
- **Explain** questions on purpose need a reason linked to the business, for
example "so that the bank figure in the statement of financial position is
correct".
- **Suggest** and **Advise** questions on digital banking need points
applied to the business in the question, such as a shop that takes most of
its payments by card.
- Paper 2 can ask you to "complete a cash book", so practise both the
updating and the statement as one task.
