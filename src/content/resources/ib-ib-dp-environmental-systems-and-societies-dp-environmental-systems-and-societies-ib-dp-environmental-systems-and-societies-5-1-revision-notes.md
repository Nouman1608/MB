## Condensed recall

### Weathering

- **Physical**: rock broken into pieces, chemistry unchanged (freeze-thaw, heating and cooling).
- **Chemical**: minerals altered or dissolved (acid rain on limestone, feldspar to clay); fastest when warm and wet.
- **Biological**: roots widen cracks; lichens and microbes release acids.

### Processes in the profile

- **Humification**: organic matter → humus.
- **Leaching**: dissolved substances carried downwards.
- **Eluviation**: removal of fine particles and solutes from a horizon (usually upper).
- **Illuviation**: deposition of them lower down (usually B horizon).
- **Mixing**: burrowing animals move material up and down.
- Rainfall > evaporation → leaching, acidic, nutrient-poor soils.
- Evaporation > rainfall → upward capillary movement, salts or carbonates near the surface.

### Organisms

- Bacteria and fungi: decomposition, nutrient release.
- Mycorrhizal fungi: extend roots; water and phosphorus for sugars.
- Nitrogen-fixing bacteria: N₂ → ammonium; some in legume root nodules.
- Earthworms: mixing, drainage channels, aggregates.
- Mites and springtails: shred litter.

### Texture triangle

- Three axes: sand, silt, clay; percentages must total 100.
- Read clay and silt lines; their meeting point gives the class.
- Examples: 40/40/20 (sand/silt/clay) = loam; 70/15/15 = sandy loam; 20/20/60 = clay.

### Texture and structure

- **Texture**: particle-size proportions; fixed by parent material.
- **Structure**: grouping into aggregates; built by humus, fungal threads, earthworms; damaged by compaction and wet working.
- Management can change structure, not texture.

### Nutrients and pH

- Clay and humus surfaces are negatively charged → hold positive nutrient ions (Ca²⁺, Mg²⁺, K⁺, NH₄⁺).
- Most crops: slightly acidic to neutral.
- Strongly acidic: phosphorus less available; aluminium toxic.
- Acidity from leaching, some nitrogen fertilisers, acid deposition; corrected by lime.

### Carbon

- Soils hold more carbon than the atmosphere and all vegetation combined.
- Peat: waterlogging slows decomposition → carbon-rich.
- Ploughing, drainage and erosion expose organic matter → CO₂ released.

### Infiltration test

- Ring pushed into soil; water level fall timed.
- Rate = fall ÷ time (mm per minute or per hour).
- Fair test: same ring depth and water depth, repeats, avoid recently wetted soil.
- Short reminder: 50 mm in 20 minutes = 2.5 mm per minute = **150 mm per hour**.

### Dust Bowl (1930s, southern Great Plains, United States)

- Prairie grasses ploughed for wheat; drought; bare topsoil blown away in dust storms; families left.
- Response: Soil Conservation Service (1935); contour ploughing, shelter belts, land returned to grass.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Physical weathering | Breakdown of rock without chemical change |
| Chemical weathering | Alteration or dissolving of minerals |
| Humus | Dark, stable organic matter formed by decomposition |
| Humification | Formation of humus |
| Leaching | Downward removal of dissolved substances |
| Illuviation | Deposition of washed-in material in a lower horizon |
| Mycorrhiza | Partnership between fungi and plant roots |
| Nitrogen fixation | Conversion of nitrogen gas to forms plants can use |
| Texture | Proportions of sand, silt and clay |
| Structure | Arrangement of particles into aggregates |
| Infiltration rate | Speed at which water enters the soil surface |
| Soil organic carbon | Carbon held in soil organic matter |

## Exam traps

- Texture is not structure.
- Leaching is not erosion: solutes move, not the soil itself.
- Texture triangle: follow each axis's guideline in the right direction; check the total is 100%.
- "Sand is infertile" needs the mechanism: large pores, rapid drainage, few charged surfaces, nutrients leached.
- Dust Bowl: drought was the trigger; ploughing the grassland was the cause.
- Infiltration rates need units and a fair-test statement.
- Ploughing releases soil carbon; do not treat soil only as a growing medium in climate answers.

## Links to other topics

- **Topic 2 (ecology)**: decomposition, nitrogen fixation and nutrient cycling all happen largely in soil.
- **4.4 (pollution)**: leached nitrate and eroded, phosphate-bearing soil are major non-point pollutants of rivers and lakes.
- **5.2 (agriculture)**: farming practices control compaction, organic matter and erosion rates.
- **6.3 (mitigation)**: no-till, cover crops and peat restoration store carbon in soil.
- **Systems language**: weathering and humification are transformations; leaching, eluviation and illuviation are transfers.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between physical and chemical weathering, with an example of each.
2. In which climate is chemical weathering fastest?
3. Define illuviation.
4. Explain why soils in very wet climates tend to be acidic.
5. State the role of mycorrhizal fungi.
6. Classify a soil with 40% sand, 40% silt and 20% clay.
7. Why can a clay soil with good structure still drain well?
8. Why do clay and humus hold nutrients?
9. Give one effect of strongly acidic soil on plants.
10. Why do drained peat soils release CO₂?
11. Water falls 24 mm in 8 minutes in an infiltration ring. Calculate the rate in mm per hour.
12. State two causes of the Dust Bowl.

### Answers

1. Physical weathering breaks rock without chemical change (freeze-thaw); chemical weathering alters minerals (rainwater dissolving limestone).
2. Warm and wet climates.
3. Deposition, in a lower horizon, of fine particles and dissolved substances washed out from above.
4. Heavy leaching removes basic ions such as calcium and magnesium, leaving the soil acidic.
5. They extend plant roots, supplying water and phosphorus in exchange for sugars.
6. Loam.
7. Aggregates leave larger pores between them, through which water and air move.
8. Their surfaces carry negative charges that attract positively charged nutrient ions.
9. Any one: phosphorus becomes less available; aluminium becomes toxic to roots.
10. Draining lets oxygen in, so organic matter that was preserved by waterlogging decomposes.
11. 24 ÷ 8 = 3 mm per minute = **180 mm per hour**.
12. Prairie grassland ploughed for wheat, removing root cover; severe drought that dried the bare topsoil.
