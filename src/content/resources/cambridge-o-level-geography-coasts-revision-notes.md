Condensed notes for sub-topic 2.3 of Cambridge O Level Geography (2217), 2026 series.
**Case study required:** the opportunities presented by an area or areas of coastline, the
associated hazards and their management.

## Waves

- Size depends on wind speed, wind duration and **fetch**.
- **Constructive:** low, infrequent, swash > backwash → build beaches.
- **Destructive:** high, frequent, backwash > swash → erode and remove material.

## Processes

- **Erosion:** hydraulic action, abrasion, attrition, solution; plus weathering and mass
  movement (slumping) from above.
- **Transport:** traction, saltation, suspension, solution; **longshore drift** — swash at an
  angle, backwash straight down.
- **Deposition:** sheltered bays, behind spits, constructive waves.
- **Wind:** blows dry sand inland by saltation.

## Landforms checklist (the syllabus list is complete)

- **Bay and headland:** differential erosion of soft and hard rock.
- **Cliff and wave-cut platform:** notch → overhang collapses → cliff retreats → platform left.
- **Cave → arch → stack → stump:** waves exploit weaknesses in a headland.
- **Beach:** deposited sand or shingle; shingle beaches steeper.
- **Spit:** longshore drift continues past a bend in the coast; hooked end; salt marsh behind
  (Spurn Head).
- **Sand dunes:** large sand supply + onshore wind + obstacle + marram grass; embryo → fore and
  yellow → grey dunes.

## Coral reefs vs mangroves

| | Coral reefs | Mangrove swamps |
|---|---|---|
| Temperature | Warm, usually above about 18 °C | Warm tropical or subtropical |
| Water | Shallow, clear, salty, well-oxygenated | Sheltered, muddy, tidal, salty or brackish |
| Kept away by | River sediment and fresh water | Strong waves |
| Features | Fringing, barrier, atoll | Prop roots, breathing roots |
| Examples | Great Barrier Reef; Maldives atolls | Sundarbans; Indus delta |
| Threats | Bleaching, pollution, sediment, overfishing, tourism | Shrimp farms, farming, urban growth |

## Hazards and opportunities

- **Hazards:** coastal erosion (loss of land and buildings); **tropical storms** — form over
  oceans above about 26 °C, roughly 5–20° from the Equator; winds, rain, **storm surge** (Cyclone
  Nargis, Myanmar, May 2008).
- **Opportunities:** fishing, ports and trade, tourism, flat land, energy (oil, gas, wind,
  tidal).

## Management

- **Hard:** sea walls, groynes, rock armour, gabions, offshore breakwaters — strong but costly;
  groynes starve beaches down-drift.
- **Soft:** beach nourishment, dune regeneration, mangrove replanting, managed retreat —
  cheaper, more natural, less protection or needs repeating.
- Choice depends on **cost–benefit**: what is worth protecting?

**Case-study hook:** Holderness, England — soft boulder clay; very rapid erosion; sediment to
Spurn Head; defences at Hornsea, Withernsea, Mappleton (rock groynes and armour, 1991) and the
Easington gas terminal; erosion increased south of Mappleton.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fetch | Distance of open water over which the wind blows |
| Swash / backwash | Water moving up / back down the beach after a wave breaks |
| Longshore drift | Movement of sediment along the coast by waves approaching at an angle |
| Wave-cut notch | Hollow eroded at the cliff base between high and low tide |
| Marram grass | Long-rooted grass that stabilises sand dunes |
| Coral bleaching | Coral losing its algae, often due to warm water, turning white |
| Storm surge | Rise in sea level caused by low pressure and strong winds of a storm |
| Managed retreat | Allowing a coast to erode or flood in a controlled way |

## Self-test

1. Give two differences between constructive and destructive waves.
2. Explain how longshore drift moves sediment.
3. State three conditions needed for coral reef growth.
4. Why do mangroves grow on sheltered coasts?
5. Give one disadvantage of groynes.
6. Name two soft engineering methods of coastal management.

**Answers:** 1. Any two: constructive are lower, less frequent and have stronger swash;
destructive are higher, more frequent and have stronger backwash. 2. Swash carries sediment up
the beach at the angle of the waves; backwash drags it straight down; the process repeats along
the coast. 3. Any three of: warm water above about 18 °C; shallow, clear water for sunlight;
salt water; well-oxygenated water. 4. Their seedlings and muddy sediment need low-energy water;
strong waves would wash them away. 5. They trap sediment, so beaches further along the coast are
starved and erode faster. 6. Any two of: beach nourishment, dune regeneration, mangrove
replanting, managed retreat.
