Each question uses one of the specification's command words at its published International AS tariff: **define** (2), **calculate** (4), **explain** (4), **analyse** (6), **discuss** (8) and **assess** (10). All businesses and figures are invented.

## Sources

> **Source 1: Kestrel Outdoor Ltd**
>
> Kestrel makes waterproof jackets and sells 4,000 a year to a European
> distributor at a fixed price of EUR 150 each. Kestrel has a $400,000 loan
> at a variable interest rate that has risen from 3% to 5%. The dollar has
> appreciated from $1 = EUR 0.75 to $1 = EUR 0.90. Economists expect
> incomes in Europe to fall by 3% next year. The income elasticity of
> demand for Kestrel's jackets is +1.8.
>
> **Source 2: The Pellam Cafe**
>
> Pellam is an independent cafe with 1,200 customers a week, who spend
> $6.50 each on average. A national coffee chain has opened next door and
> Pellam expects to lose 15% of its customers. Weekly wages are $3,500, and
> the minimum wage has just risen by 8%. The owner has a trademark on
> the cafe's name and logo and a loyal following on social media.

## Section A

Questions 1 to 6 refer to Source 1.

**1.** Define the term "appreciation" of a currency. **[2]**

**2.** Calculate Kestrel's dollar income from one jacket before and after the appreciation of the dollar, and the change. **[4]**

**3.** Calculate the extra annual interest Kestrel pays on its loan after the rise in the interest rate. **[4]**

**4.** Calculate the expected change in the number of jackets sold if European incomes fall by 3%. **[4]**

**5.** Explain **one** way Kestrel could respond to the appreciation of the dollar. **[4]**

**6.** Analyse how the rise in the interest rate could affect Kestrel. **[6]**

## Section B

Questions 7 to 12 refer to Source 2.

**7.** Define the term "intellectual property rights". **[2]**

**8.** Calculate the weekly and yearly revenue Pellam is expected to lose to the chain (assume 52 weeks). **[4]**

**9.** Calculate the extra yearly wage cost from the minimum wage rise. **[4]**

**10.** Analyse how the competitor's behaviour could affect Pellam. **[6]**

**11.** Discuss how Pellam could compete with the national chain. **[8]**

**12.** Assess whether legislation or competition poses the greater threat to Pellam. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** A rise in the value of a currency [1] so that it buys more units of foreign currency [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Before: 150 / 0.75 = **$200** [1]. After: 150 / 0.90 = **$166.67** [1]. Change = 200 - 166.67 [1] = **$33.33 less per jacket** [1]. (Own figure rule: award the final mark for correct working on a wrong earlier figure.) **[4]**

**3.** Interest before = 3% x 400,000 = $12,000 [1]. Interest after = 5% x 400,000 = $20,000 [1]. Extra = 20,000 - 12,000 [1] = **$8,000 a year** [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Change in demand = YED x change in income = 1.8 x -3% [1] = **-5.4%** [1]. Fall in jackets = 5.4% x 4,000 = 216 [1], so sales are expected to be **3,784 jackets** [1]. **[4]**

**5.** Kestrel could cut costs or improve productivity [1] so that it can lower its euro price without losing dollar profit [1]. Because the appreciation has cut its dollar income per jacket by $33.33 (about 17%), a lower euro price would otherwise cut profit further [1]. Alternatively it could move production nearer to Europe or buy materials in euros, which would reduce the effect of the exchange rate [1]. *(Any one well-developed response.)* **[4]**

**6.** The interest bill has risen by $8,000, or about 67% [1], which reduces profit after interest [1]. Less profit means less cash for reinvestment in machinery or new designs [1]. Higher rates may also reduce customers' spending, especially on items bought on credit, and the distributor may order fewer jackets [1]. Combined with an expected 5.4% fall in demand and a weaker dollar income per jacket, Kestrel's profit is under severe pressure [1], and if it cannot refinance or reduce borrowing, the business may face a liquidity problem [1]. **[6]**

**7.** Legal rights that protect creations of the mind [1], such as copyright, patents and trademarks, from being copied by others [1]. **[2]**

**8.** Customers lost = 15% x 1,200 = 180 a week [1]. Weekly revenue lost = 180 x $6.50 = **$1,170** [2]. Yearly = 1,170 x 52 [1] = **$60,840**. *(Allow 1 mark for the method if the arithmetic is wrong; the final mark is included in the 4.)* **[4]**

**9.** Extra weekly wages = 8% x $3,500 [1] = $280 [1]. Yearly = 280 x 52 [1] = **$14,560** [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The chain's arrival means that Pellam faces a larger, better-funded rival [1], which can use economies of scale to charge lower prices and spend heavily on advertising [1]. If the chain behaves aggressively, for example with price promotions, Pellam may lose even more than the expected 15% of customers [1]. Fewer customers cuts revenue by $60,840 a year, while fixed costs such as rent stay the same [1], so profit falls by a greater percentage than revenue [1]. Pellam may then need to cut prices or costs, which risks lower quality and a weaker reputation [1]. **[6]**

**11.** **Ways to compete:** Pellam can stress what the chain cannot offer, such as personal service, local ingredients and a distinct atmosphere [1]; use its trademarked name and loyal social media following to promote loyalty schemes and local events [1]; focus on a niche, for example specialty or vegan food, where customers pay a premium [1].
**Limits:** it cannot match the chain on price because of scale economies [1]; promotions cost money while takings are falling and wages are rising [1]; loyal customers may still leave if the chain's convenience is greater [1].
**Judgement:** differentiation is the best route, because a price war cannot be won; its success depends on how loyal customers are and how fast Pellam can build on its social media base [1]. Pellam should also keep costs under control so a modest fall in sales does not push it into loss [1]. **[8]**

**12.** **Legislation:** the minimum wage rise costs $14,560 a year, about 24% of the revenue lost to the chain [1]; Pellam must also meet food safety and health and safety rules, which add costs [1]; but these laws apply equally to the chain and Pellam's trademark protects its brand [1].
**Competition:** the chain's arrival is expected to remove $60,840 of revenue each year, over four times the extra wage cost [1]; the chain's size gives it scale economies and a bigger marketing budget [1]; it is also a permanent threat, unlike a one-off cost increase [1].
**Judgement:** competition poses the greater threat, because the revenue loss is much larger than the extra wage bill and cannot be avoided by compliance [1]. However, the two combine, as lower sales make the higher wages harder to afford [1]; the judgement would change if the chain's impact proved smaller than 15% [1]. **[10]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing by the exchange rate the wrong way round.
- Applying the elasticity to the sales figure instead of to the change in income.
- Calculating interest on the new rate only, not the extra amount.
- Describing competition in general terms without using the 15% customer loss.
- Concluding an assess question with no judgement or reason.
