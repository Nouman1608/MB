## US Civil War and Reconstruction

### Road to war

- 1820: Missouri Compromise (no slavery north of 36°30′ in the Louisiana Purchase lands, except Missouri).
- 1846-48: Mexican War; Wilmot Proviso debate over slavery in new lands.
- 1850: Compromise of 1850; Fugitive Slave Act.
- 1852: *Uncle Tom's Cabin* published.
- 1854: Kansas-Nebraska Act; Republican Party founded.
- 1856: "Bleeding Kansas".
- 1857: *Dred Scott* decision.
- 1859: John Brown's raid.
- November 1860: Lincoln elected; December: South Carolina secedes.
- February 1861: Confederate States of America formed.
- 12 April 1861: Fort Sumter.

### The war

- September 1862: Antietam; preliminary Emancipation Proclamation.
- 1 January 1863: Emancipation Proclamation takes effect.
- July 1863: Gettysburg and Vicksburg.
- September 1864: Atlanta falls; Sherman's march to the sea.
- November 1864: Lincoln re-elected.
- 9 April 1865: Lee surrenders at Appomattox; 14 April: Lincoln assassinated.

### Reconstruction

- 1865: Thirteenth Amendment; Freedmen's Bureau; Black Codes; Ku Klux Klan founded.
- 1867: Reconstruction Acts place the South under military districts.
- 1868: Fourteenth Amendment; President Johnson impeached and acquitted.
- 1870: Fifteenth Amendment.
- 1877: Compromise of 1877; federal troops withdrawn.

## The Mexican Revolution, 1910-1940

| Phase | Key points |
|---|---|
| Porfiriato (1876-1911) | Order and progress; railways; foreign investment; land concentration; *rurales*; repression |
| Outbreak (1908-11) | Creelman interview (1908); Madero's campaign; Plan of San Luis Potosí; Díaz resigns (May 1911) |
| Madero (1911-13) | Political reform; Zapata's Plan of Ayala (1911); Huerta's coup and Madero's murder (February 1913) |
| Constitutionalist war (1913-15) | Carranza, Obregón, Villa; US at Veracruz (1914); Aguascalientes Convention (1914); Celaya (1915) |
| Constitution (1917) | Articles 3, 27 and 123 |
| Sonoran dynasty (1920-34) | Obregón; Calles; Cristero War (1926-29); PNR founded (1929) |
| Cárdenas (1934-40) | *Ejido* land distribution; oil expropriation (1938); party reorganized as the PRM |

**Leaders and aims**

- **Madero:** "effective suffrage, no re-election".
- **Zapata:** land restitution for villages ("land and liberty").
- **Villa:** northern popular leader; land and justice for followers.
- **Carranza:** constitutional order; cautious on land reform.
- **Obregón and Calles:** state-building, anticlericalism, central control.
- **Cárdenas:** agrarian reform, labour rights, economic nationalism.

## Civil rights and social movements after 1945

| Strategy | Examples | Organizations |
|---|---|---|
| Legal challenge | *Brown v. Board* (1954) | NAACP |
| Non-violent direct action | Montgomery (1955-56), sit-ins (1960), Freedom Rides (1961), Birmingham (1963), Selma (1965) | SCLC, SNCC, CORE |
| Mass demonstration | March on Washington (August 1963) | Coalition of groups |
| Black Power and self-defence | Calls for Black Power (1966) | Black Panther Party; Malcolm X earlier in the Nation of Islam |
| Federal action | Civil Rights Act (1964); Voting Rights Act (1965) | Kennedy and Johnson administrations |
| Other movements | Farm workers' strikes and boycotts from 1965; American Indian activism | United Farm Workers (César Chávez); American Indian Movement |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Popular sovereignty | Letting settlers of a territory decide on slavery |
| Secession | A state's withdrawal from the Union |
| Black Codes | Southern laws after 1865 restricting freedmen's rights |
| Sharecropping | Farming land for a share of the crop, common among freedmen |
| Porfiriato | The period of Díaz's rule |
| *Ejido* | Communal landholding in Mexico |
| *Cristeros* | Catholic rebels against anticlerical laws, 1926-29 |
| Segregation / Jim Crow | Legal separation of races in the US South |
| Direct action | Protest such as sit-ins and boycotts that confronts injustice publicly |

## Essay angles to prepare

| Content area | Question angle | Factors to weigh |
|---|---|---|
| Civil War causes | Was slavery the main cause of the war? | Expansion of slavery, states' rights, economic differences, the breakdown of parties, Lincoln's election |
| Union victory | Why did the North win? | Resources, blockade, leadership, emancipation, Confederate weaknesses |
| Reconstruction | How far did Reconstruction succeed? | Amendments, Freedmen's Bureau, Black officeholders, Black Codes, violence, the 1877 settlement |
| Mexican Revolution | Why did the revolution last so long? | Divided aims of leaders, foreign intervention, regional armies, weak central state |
| Civil rights | Why did progress come in the 1960s? | Grassroots action, courts, presidents, media, Cold War image |

## Exam traps

- **Slavery left out of Civil War causes.** It was central to every crisis of the 1850s.
- **Union victory as inevitable.** Show that resources needed leadership and strategy to be decisive.
- **The Mexican Revolution as one event.** Separate phases and leaders, and read the question's end date.
- **Civil rights as only King.** Include grassroots groups, courts, presidents and Black Power.
- **Moving outside the named country.** Answer for the country in the question.

## Self-test

1. What did the Kansas-Nebraska Act do? *Allowed settlers to decide on slavery, overturning the Missouri Compromise line.*
2. Which state seceded first, and when? *South Carolina, December 1860.*
3. Why was the Emancipation Proclamation significant beyond the battlefield? *It made the war a war against slavery and discouraged British and French intervention.*
4. What were the two Union victories of July 1863? *Gettysburg and Vicksburg.*
5. What did the Fourteenth Amendment establish? *Citizenship and equal protection of the laws for all persons born or naturalized in the USA.*
6. What was Madero's slogan? *"Effective suffrage, no re-election."*
7. What did Zapata's Plan of Ayala demand? *Restitution of land to villages.*
8. Who defeated Villa at Celaya? *Obregón, in 1915.*
9. What was the Cristero War? *A Catholic rebellion (1926-29) against the anticlerical laws of the Calles government.*
10. Which party did Calles found in 1929? *The PNR, forerunner of the PRI.*
11. What did *Brown v. Board of Education* decide? *That segregated public schools were unconstitutional.*
12. Name two organizations of the civil rights movement besides the NAACP. *Any two: SCLC, SNCC, CORE, the Black Panther Party.*
