## Questions

**1.** A fallen branch is colonised by a fungus that feeds as a saprophyte. Describe how the fungus obtains its food, and state what is released that producers can take up. **[4]**

**2.** Explain how the nitrogen in a plant protein becomes part of the protein of a caterpillar that eats the leaf. Use the terms digestion and assimilation. **[3]**

**3.** The table shows carbon dioxide concentration (ppm) at a station in the northern hemisphere.

| Month | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| January | 422.0 | 424.6 |
| April | 424.4 | 427.0 |
| July | 421.5 | 424.1 |
| October | 419.9 | 422.5 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between the highest and lowest values in year 1. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the percentage increase in the January value from year 1 to year 2, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain the fall from April to October. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain the rise from year 1 to year 2. **[2]**

**4.** Mean carbon dioxide concentrations at a site were 317 ppm in 1960, 339 ppm in 1980, 370 ppm in 2000 and 413 ppm in 2020.

**(a)** Calculate the mean rate of increase per year between 1980 and 2000. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the change in the rate of increase over the 60 years. **[2]**
**(c)** Name two human activities that have contributed to the increase. **[2]**

**5.** Explain how carbon dioxide and methane enhance the greenhouse effect, and give one source of methane. **[5]**

**6.** A farm recorded the data below for two growing seasons.

| | Cool season | Warm season |
|---|---|---|
| Mean temperature (°C) | 15.3 | 17.1 |
| Generations of an aphid pest | 9 | 13 |
| Mean wheat yield (t ha⁻¹) | 7.8 | 6.9 |

**(a)** Calculate the percentage change in yield from the cool to the warm season. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why more generations of aphid occurred in the warm season. **[3]**
**(c)** Evaluate whether the data show that climate change reduces yield. **[2]**

**7.** Name the process in each case and the type of bacteria involved.

**(a)** Ammonium ions converted to nitrite ions then nitrate ions. **[2]**
**(b)** Nitrate ions converted to nitrogen gas in waterlogged soil. **[2]**
**(c)** State the condition that decides which of these two processes occurs. **[1]**

**8.** In a pasture each year, nitrogen enters as fertiliser (90 kg ha⁻¹) and by fixation (38 kg ha⁻¹). Nitrogen leaves in milk and meat (64), by leaching (29) and by denitrification (11), all in kg ha⁻¹ year⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the change in the soil nitrogen store each year. **[2]**
**(b)** Leaching removed some of the fertiliser nitrogen. Give this loss as a percentage of the fertiliser nitrogen, to 3 significant figures. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the farmer must replace the nitrogen removed in milk and meat. **[1]**

**9.** Ammonium sulfate is (NH₄)₂SO₄. Ar values: N = 14, H = 1, S = 32, O = 16.

**(a)** Show that nitrogen makes up 21.2% of the mass of ammonium sulfate (3 significant figures). **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the mass of ammonium sulfate that contains 63 kg of nitrogen. **[1]**
**(c)** Only 70% of the nitrogen applied is taken up by the crop. Calculate the mass of ammonium sulfate needed to supply 63 kg of nitrogen to the crop, to the nearest kg. **[1]**

**10.** After heavy rain, the nitrate concentration in a pond rose from 1.8 to 7.4 mg dm⁻³. Over the next month, algae first multiplied, then many died, and dissolved oxygen fell.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage increase in nitrate, to the nearest whole number. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain how the nitrate reached the pond from a fertilised field. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why dissolved oxygen fell. **[2]**

**11.** A farmer plans to grow clover in alternate years instead of adding all the nitrogen as artificial fertiliser, and to spread manure on the fields. The farm drains to a lake.

Evaluate this plan, using your knowledge of the nitrogen cycle. **[9]**

## Answers

**1.** The fungus secretes digestive enzymes onto the wood [1]; large insoluble molecules are hydrolysed to small soluble ones [1]; the fungus absorbs the soluble products [1]; respiration releases carbon dioxide and decay releases ammonium ions [1].
*Examiner insight:* Describe digestion outside the cell and absorption as two separate steps. Answers are stronger when they name products such as carbon dioxide and ammonium ions, and not just "nutrients".

**2.** The caterpillar digests the protein, hydrolysing it to amino acids [1]; the amino acids are absorbed into its blood or body fluid [1]; assimilation: they are used to build the caterpillar's own proteins [1].
*Examiner insight:* Keep the two terms apart. Digestion is breakdown and absorption, and assimilation is the building of the absorbed molecules into the organism.

**3. (a)** 424.4 − 419.9 = **4.5 ppm** [1].
**(b)** Increase = 424.6 − 422.0 = 2.6 [1]; 2.6 ÷ 422.0 × 100 = **0.62%** [1].
**(c)** In the growing season, photosynthesis by plants in leaf is faster than respiration [1]; so more carbon dioxide is removed than released [1].
**(d)** Burning of fossil fuels and wood releases carbon dioxide stored for a long time [1]; this adds more than photosynthesis and the oceans remove, so the average rises [1].
*Examiner insight:* Divide by the year 1 value, the starting value, for a percentage increase. For explanations, name both processes and say which one is faster.

**4. (a)** 370 − 339 = 31 ppm [1]; 31 ÷ 20 = **1.55 ppm per year** [1].
**(b)** The rate rises: 1.1 ppm per year from 1960 to 1980, 1.55 from 1980 to 2000 and 2.15 from 2000 to 2020 [1]; so the increase is accelerating, with each period faster than the one before [1].
**(c)** Any two: burning fossil fuels; deforestation; burning wood [2].
*Examiner insight:* A good description quotes values with units. Writing "it goes up" without figures is a weak answer to a question on data.

**5.** Incoming short-wave radiation from the Sun mostly reaches the ground and heats it [1]; the surface emits longer-wave infrared radiation [1]; carbon dioxide and methane absorb some of it [1]; they re-radiate it, including back towards the surface, so the lower atmosphere warms [1]; any source of methane: cattle or sheep digestion, rice paddies, wetlands, landfill, leaks from natural gas supplies [1].
*Examiner insight:* Mention the change in wavelength of the radiation. Saying that gases "trap heat" without absorbing and re-radiating infrared is a vague answer.

**6. (a)** 6.9 − 7.8 = −0.9 [1]; −0.9 ÷ 7.8 × 100 = **−12%** (a fall of 12%) [1].
**(b)** Insects are ectotherms, so their enzymes work faster at higher temperature and they develop faster [1]; the life cycle is shorter [1]; so more generations fit into the same season [1].
**(c)** Only two seasons, so it is not enough to show a trend [1]; other variables, such as rainfall, disease or pests, may also differ, so the data show an association and not cause [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give a verdict and back it with a reason from the data. "The data show a link but do not prove cause" is correct only if you support it with sample size or uncontrolled variables.

**7. (a)** Nitrification [1]; nitrifying bacteria [1].
**(b)** Denitrification [1]; denitrifying bacteria [1].
**(c)** Whether oxygen is present: nitrification is aerobic and denitrification anaerobic [1].
*Examiner insight:* Spell the terms carefully, because nitrification and denitrification are easy to confuse and a reader cannot guess which you meant. Give the starting form and end form when you can.

**8. (a)** Input = 90 + 38 = 128 [1]; output = 64 + 29 + 11 = 104, so the store rises by **24 kg ha⁻¹ year⁻¹** [1].
**(b)** 29 ÷ 90 × 100 = **32.2%** [1].
**(c)** The nitrogen in the milk and meat leaves the farm and is not returned through death and decay [1].
*Examiner insight:* Give units with the change and its direction. Divide leaching by the fertiliser input, not the total input.

**9. (a)** Mr = 2 × (14 + 4) + 32 + 64 = 132 [1]; 28 ÷ 132 × 100 = **21.2%** [1].
**(b)** 63 ÷ (28 ÷ 132) = **297 kg** [1].
**(c)** Nitrogen to apply = 63 ÷ 0.70 = 90 kg; 90 ÷ (28 ÷ 132) = 424.3, which is **424 kg** [1].
*Examiner insight:* Count both nitrogen atoms in the formula unit. Show the Mr working so that a slip in the final figure can still be traced.

**10. (a)** 7.4 − 1.8 = 5.6 [1]; 5.6 ÷ 1.8 × 100 = **311%** [1].
**(b)** The rain dissolved soluble nitrate from the fertiliser and carried it through the soil by leaching [1]; the drainage water ran into the pond [1].
**(c)** Dead algae and plants were decomposed by saprophytic bacteria, which multiplied [1]; their aerobic respiration used oxygen faster than it was replaced [1].
*Examiner insight:* Name leaching in part (b), and decomposer respiration in part (c). Blaming only the algae loses the second mark.

**11.** Credit any of the following points, up to nine in total. Clover is a legume with nitrogen-fixing bacteria in its root nodules [1]; the bacteria change nitrogen gas into ammonium, and nitrifying bacteria then produce nitrate for the following crop, so less artificial fertiliser is needed [1]; manure replaces nitrogen removed at harvest [1]; manure is organic, so saprophytic bacteria release ammonium slowly [1]; this reduces the risk of leaching compared with soluble artificial fertiliser [1]; manure has variable composition, so supply may not match crop need [1]; slow release may leave early growth short of nitrate [1]; heavy rain on manure or soil rich in nitrate can still cause leaching into the lake, and eutrophication follows [1]; a judgement supported by the points, such as that the plan reduces the risk if applications are timed with crop growth [1].
*Examiner insight:* Weigh both sides and finish with a conclusion that follows from the points. A list of facts with no judgement does not evaluate.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "nutrients" where nitrate, ammonium or carbon dioxide is wanted.
- Mixing up ammonification, nitrification, nitrogen fixation and denitrification.
- Forgetting that denitrification needs anaerobic conditions.
- Giving the oxygen fall in a lake as the work of algae only.
- Dividing a percentage change by the final value.
- Reading two data points as proof of cause.
- Missing the idea that insects are ectotherms in pest questions.
- Saying that gases "trap heat" without absorbing and re-radiating infrared.
