Condensed recall for **2.1 Diurnal energy budgets** (the energy balance of a surface over 24 hours). For full explanations, read the [Core Physical Geography study guide](/resources/a-level-cambridge-geography-core-physical-geography/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Insolation | Incoming solar radiation (shortwave) |
| Albedo | Proportion of incoming radiation reflected by a surface |
| Longwave radiation | Heat radiated by the Earth's surface and atmosphere |
| Sensible heat | Heat transferred by conduction and convection; changes air temperature |
| Latent heat | Heat absorbed or released when water changes state |
| Ground (subsurface) heat flux | Heat conducted into or out of the ground |
| Net radiation | Incoming minus outgoing radiation at the surface |

## Daytime budget

Inputs: shortwave solar radiation (plus longwave from the atmosphere). Losses and uses:

- reflected shortwave (set by albedo);
- energy absorbed into the surface and ground;
- sensible heat transferred to the air;
- latent heat used in evaporation (from soil, water and plants);
- longwave radiation emitted.

Net radiation is positive, so the surface warms and temperature rises towards early afternoon.

## Night-time budget

There is no solar input. The surface loses energy by longwave radiation, and cools. Offsetting gains: heat conducted up from the subsurface, sensible heat from the air, and latent heat released if dew or fog forms. Net radiation is negative.

## What changes the budget

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Surface type | Dark, dry surfaces have low albedo and heat quickly; snow reflects most radiation |
| Moisture | Wet surfaces use energy for evaporation, so heat less |
| Cloud | By day reflects insolation; by night traps longwave, so nights are warmer |
| Vegetation | Shades the ground, transpires, alters albedo |
| Urban areas | Dark materials store heat and release it at night |

## Exam traps

- Albedo is the **reflected share**, not the absorbed amount.
- Cloud acts in both directions: cooler days, warmer nights.
- Latent heat is not a temperature change you can feel; sensible heat is.
- Describe both day and night if a question says "diurnal".

## Self-test

1. Define albedo.
2. Which heat transfer is released when dew forms?
3. Why are clear nights colder than cloudy nights?
4. Name two daytime uses of absorbed energy.
5. Why is net radiation negative at night?
6. Why do wet surfaces warm more slowly than dry ones?

**Answers:** 1. The proportion of incoming solar radiation reflected by a surface. 2. Latent heat. 3. Cloud returns longwave radiation to the surface; clear skies let it escape. 4. Heating the ground, sensible heat to the air, evaporation (latent heat). 5. There is no shortwave input but longwave is still lost. 6. Energy goes into evaporation as latent heat.
