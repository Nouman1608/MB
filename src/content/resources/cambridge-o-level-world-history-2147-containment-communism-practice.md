These questions cover key question 2.5 of Cambridge O Level History 2147, the Paper 2 prescribed topic for November
2026.

## Section A

**1.** Describe the involvement of the United Nations in Korea in 1950. **[4]**

**2.** Identify **two** turning points in the Korean War between September 1950 and April 1951. **[2]**

**3.** Describe the Bay of Pigs invasion of 1961. **[4]**

**4.** Describe how the Cuban Missile Crisis was resolved in October 1962. **[4]**

**5.** Identify **two** tactics used by the Viet Cong. **[2]**

**6.** Describe the Tet Offensive of 1968. **[4]**

## Section B

**7.** Why did the United States become involved in Vietnam between 1955 and 1964? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**8.** Why did the United States withdraw from Vietnam? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**9.** "The Korean War was a success for containment." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**10.** "The Cuban Missile Crisis was a victory for Kennedy." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**11.** Study Source A, which is invented for practice.

*Source A (invented)*: from an imagined letter by an American nurse in South Vietnam to her parents, February 1968.
"The radio says we are winning and the enemy is beaten. Then why did they reach the embassy? Every night the boys ask
me the same question, and every night I have no answer. I don't think the people back home will believe the generals
much longer."

What can you learn from Source A about the effects of the Tet Offensive? Use the source and your knowledge to explain
your answer. **[6]**

**12.** Study Source B, which is invented for practice.

*Source B (invented)*: a description of an imaginary British cartoon from October 1962, written for this page.
Kennedy and Khrushchev sit facing each other on two ends of a seesaw balanced on the edge of a cliff. Each holds a
bomb labelled with his country's name. Below the cliff is the sea, marked "Cuba". Both are sweating.

How useful is Source B as evidence about the Cuban Missile Crisis? Use the source and your knowledge to explain your
answer. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** Any four, 1 mark each: the USA took the North Korean invasion to the Security Council (1); the USSR was
boycotting the Council over China's seat, so it could not use its veto (1); the Council condemned the invasion (1) and
called on members to help South Korea (1); a UN force was formed (1); it was commanded by the American general
MacArthur (1); most troops were American, but other countries such as Britain also sent forces (1). **[4]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Inchon landing, September 1950 (1); UN forces crossing the 38th parallel, October
1950 (1); Chinese entry into the war, October–November 1950 (1); Seoul falling again, January 1951 (1); MacArthur's
dismissal, April 1951 (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any four, 1 mark each: planned by the CIA under Eisenhower (1); approved by Kennedy (1); about 1,400 Cuban exiles
landed at the Bay of Pigs in April 1961 (1); they expected Cubans to rise against Castro, but no rising happened (1);
Kennedy refused to provide open US air support (1); Castro's forces defeated them within days (1); most were captured
(1); it humiliated the USA and pushed Castro closer to the USSR (1). **[4]**

**4.** Any four, 1 mark each: Kennedy imposed a naval quarantine around Cuba (1); Soviet ships turned back from the
quarantine line (1); Khrushchev offered to remove the missiles if the USA promised not to invade Cuba (1); a second
letter demanded the removal of US missiles from Turkey (1); Kennedy publicly accepted the first offer (1); Robert
Kennedy privately agreed the Turkish missiles would be removed later (1); Khrushchev announced the missiles would be
dismantled on 28 October (1). **[4]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: guerrilla ambushes and hit-and-run attacks (1); tunnel networks (1); booby traps (1);
blending in with villagers (1); using the Ho Chi Minh Trail for supplies (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any four, 1 mark each: launched in January 1968 (1) during the Tet holiday truce (1); Viet Cong and North
Vietnamese forces attacked over a hundred towns and cities (1); they briefly entered the grounds of the US embassy in
Saigon (1); there was heavy fighting at Hue (1); it was a military defeat for the communists, with heavy losses (1); but
it shocked American opinion because the government had said the war was being won (1). **[4]**

**7.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4–5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation.
Level 2 (2–3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content: **the domino
theory**: the USA believed that if Vietnam fell, other states in Southeast Asia would follow; **the Geneva Accords**
left a communist North, and the USA backed Diem in the South and opposed the 1956 elections that Ho Chi Minh was
expected to win; **Cold War credibility** after the communist victory in China and the Korean War; **the Viet Cong
insurgency** threatened Diem's government, so Kennedy sent advisers; **the Gulf of Tonkin incident** (1964) gave Johnson
the authority to escalate. **[6]**

**8.** Levels as Question 7. Indicative content: **Tet Offensive** (1968) persuaded many Americans the war could not be
won; **public opposition**, protests and events such as Kent State; **media coverage** showing casualties and
atrocities such as My Lai; **cost** in money and lives and the unequal draft; **failure of tactics**: bombing and search
and destroy did not defeat a guerrilla enemy; **Nixon's policy** of Vietnamisation and improving relations with China
and the USSR. **[6]**

**9.** Levels. Level 5 (9–10): explained arguments on both sides and a supported judgement. Level 4 (7–8): explains
both sides. Level 3 (4–6): explains one side. Level 2 (2–3): describes. Level 1 (1): general points. **Agree:** South
Korea survived as a non-communist state; the UN backing gave containment international support; it showed allies the
USA would fight; a wider war with China and the USSR was avoided when MacArthur was dismissed. **Disagree:** the
attempt to unite Korea by rollback failed; China's intervention showed communist strength; casualties were very heavy
for a border close to the starting line; the war encouraged a costly global arms build-up under NSC-68. **Judgement:**
a success for containment as defined in 1947, but a failure for the more ambitious aims of late 1950. **[10]**

**10.** Levels as Question 9. **Agree:** the missiles were removed; Kennedy appeared firm but restrained; the USSR was
seen to back down; Khrushchev's position at home was weakened, and he was removed in 1964. **Disagree:** Castro stayed
in power with a US promise not to invade; the USA secretly removed its missiles from Turkey; Kennedy's earlier Bay of
Pigs failure helped cause the crisis; the world came close to nuclear war, which neither side could call a victory.
**Judgement:** a public victory for Kennedy, but in private a compromise in which both sides gained something. **[10]**

**11.** Levels. Level 3 (5–6): developed inferences supported from the source and context. Level 2 (2–4): inferences
or details explained. Level 1 (1): copies or paraphrases. Indicative content: Tet undermined official claims that the
war was being won ("why did they reach the embassy?"); it damaged morale among US troops; it created a "credibility
gap" between the government and the public; the writer expects opinion at home to turn against the war. Context:
Johnson announced in March 1968 that he would not stand again; support for withdrawal grew. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 4 (7–8): evaluates usefulness through content, provenance and knowledge, with a judgement. Level
3 (5–6): evaluates content or provenance. Level 2 (2–4): uses content at face value. Level 1 (1): general comment.
**Useful:** it shows how close to nuclear war the world appeared to observers in October 1962; both leaders are shown
as equally responsible and equally frightened, which suggests a British view that both sides were taking dangerous
risks; it matches events such as the quarantine and the U-2 shot down on 27 October. **Limits:** it is a cartoonist's
opinion, not a factual account; it does not show how the crisis was resolved or the secret Turkish deal; a British
cartoonist was outside the decision-making. **Judgement:** useful for attitudes at the time, especially fear of
nuclear war, less useful for what actually happened. **[8]**
