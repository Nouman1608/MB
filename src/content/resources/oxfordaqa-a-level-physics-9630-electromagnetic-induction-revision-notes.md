Full explanations and worked examples are in the [electromagnetic induction study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electromagnetic-induction/); test yourself with the [electromagnetic induction practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-electromagnetic-induction-practice/). ## Equations

| Equation | Use | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| ε = NΔΦ/Δt | Average emf from a change of flux linkage | Rate of change of flux linkage |
| ε = gradient of NΦ–t graph | Emf at an instant | Tangent for a curve |
| ε = BLv | Straight conductor crossing a field | L, v and B mutually perpendicular |
| ε = BANω sin ωt | Coil rotating uniformly | t = 0 with the normal along B |
| ε₀ = BANω | Peak emf of a rotating coil | ω = 2πf |

## Key terms

| Term | What to write |
|---|---|
| Electromagnetic induction | Production of an emf when the flux linking a conductor changes |
| Faraday's law | Induced emf is proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage |
| Lenz's law | The induced emf acts so as to oppose the change producing it |
| Eddy currents | Currents induced in a bulk conductor by a changing flux, circulating within it |
| Flux cutting | A conductor moving across field lines; emf = flux cut per second |

## Condensed recall

- No change of flux linkage, no emf, however strong the field.
- Relative motion only: moving the coil or the magnet gives the same effect.
- Faster change, more turns or a stronger magnet: bigger emf.
- Lenz's law follows from conservation of energy: the opposing force means work must be done to produce electrical energy.
- Moving rod in a circuit: induced current gives a force BIL opposing the motion; mechanical power Fv equals electrical power εI.
- Emf can exist with no current if there is no closed circuit with a changing flux linkage (for example between the wing tips of an aircraft).
- Eddy currents oppose motion (braking) and heat the conductor. Laminations or slots reduce them.
- Rotating coil: emf maximum when the coil's plane is along the field; zero when its face is perpendicular to the field.

## Magnet through a coil

| Stage | Flux linkage | Emf |
|---|---|---|
| Entering | Rising | Pulse one way |
| Centred in coil | At maximum, momentarily constant | Zero |
| Leaving | Falling | Pulse the other way, taller and narrower (magnet faster) |

The areas under the two pulses are equal: each equals the total change of flux linkage.

## Method boxes

**Direction from Lenz's law**

1. Is the flux through the coil increasing or decreasing, and in which direction?
2. The induced current creates flux opposing that change (against an increase, with a decrease).
3. Use the right-hand grip rule for the coil, or name the pole formed at the near end.

**Moving rod on rails**

1. ε = BLv; I = ε/R.
2. Opposing force F = BIL.
3. Check: Fv = I²R.

## Worked reminders

- 200 turns, 5.0 × 10⁻⁴ m², 0 → 0.30 T in 0.025 s: ε = **1.2 V**.
- Rod 0.60 m at 5.0 m s⁻¹ across 0.080 T: ε = **0.24 V**.
- 250 turns, 2.4 × 10⁻³ m², 0.15 T, 20 Hz: ε₀ = 0.15 × 2.4 × 10⁻³ × 250 × 125.7 = **11 V**.

## Exam traps

- "Opposes the field" instead of "opposes the change".
- Leaving N out of Faraday's law.
- Peak emf placed where flux linkage is greatest; it is where flux linkage is zero.
- Saying laminations stop eddy currents.
- Treating the second pulse of a falling magnet as identical to the first.
- Using ε = BLv when the rod moves along the field lines (no flux is cut).

## Self-test

1. State Faraday's law.
2. State Lenz's law.
3. A magnet rests inside a coil. Why is there no emf?
4. Find the emf when the flux linkage of a coil falls uniformly from 0.080 Wb to 0.020 Wb in 0.15 s.
5. A rod 0.30 m long moves at 4.0 m s⁻¹ across a 0.20 T field. Find the emf.
6. In question 5 the circuit resistance is 0.50 Ω. Find the force needed to keep the rod moving steadily.
7. Explain why Lenz's law is required by conservation of energy.
8. Why does a copper plate swinging between magnet poles stop quickly?
9. How does slotting the plate change this?
10. Write the emf of a coil rotating at angular speed ω.
11. A generator's rotation rate is tripled. What happens to the peak emf and the frequency?
12. A vertical rod 1.5 m long moves east at 30 m s⁻¹ through a horizontal field of 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ T pointing north. Find the emf.

### Answers

1. The induced emf is directly proportional to the rate of change of flux linkage.
2. The induced emf acts in a direction that opposes the change producing it.
3. The flux linkage is not changing.
4. 0.060 / 0.15 = **0.40 V**.
5. 0.20 × 0.30 × 4.0 = **0.24 V**.
6. I = 0.48 A; F = BIL = 0.20 × 0.48 × 0.30 = **0.029 N**.
7. If the induced current aided the change, the motion would speed itself up and electrical energy would be produced with no work done.
8. Eddy currents are induced in the plate; by Lenz's law they produce forces opposing its motion, and its kinetic energy becomes thermal energy.
9. Slots break up the current paths, so the eddy currents and the braking are much smaller.
10. ε = BANω sin ωt.
11. Peak emf **triples**; frequency **triples**.
12. 2.0 × 10⁻⁵ × 1.5 × 30 = **9.0 × 10⁻⁴ V**.
