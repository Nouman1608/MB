Condensed for quick recall of how the course is assessed. For the full subject overview, use the
[IB DP Chemistry subject overview](/resources/ib-dp-chemistry-subject-guide/).

## The assessment split

| Component | Weight | What it involves |
|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | 36% | Short-answer and data-based questions across the syllabus |
| Paper 2 | 44% | Short-answer and extended-response questions on structure and reactivity |
| Internal assessment | 20% | An individual scientific investigation, up to 3,000 words |

External (80%) + internal (20%), same split at SL and HL — papers differ in length/demand, not weighting.

## The structure-and-reactivity framework

Chemistry is organised around **two linked concepts**: structure determines how matter reacts,
and reactions in turn transform structure. Both papers draw questions across this framework
rather than testing topics as standalone blocks — a reason to revise **connections between
topics**, not just topics in isolation.

## IA vs. collaborative sciences project

The graded IA is an **individual** investigation. The interdisciplinary **collaborative sciences
project** (with peers from other group 4 subjects) builds skills but is **not itself graded** —
keep these separate in your revision/coursework planning.

## Command terms and how each paper uses them

Both papers draw their questions from the same command-term glossary used across all DP sciences -- **state**, **define**, **identify** and **describe** at the lower end (recall and straightforward description), through **explain**, **compare**, **deduce** and **suggest** in the middle, up to **evaluate**, **discuss** and **justify** at the top, where a reasoned judgement or synthesis across multiple pieces of evidence is required. Paper 1's short-answer and data-based questions lean more heavily on the lower and middle tiers; Paper 2's extended-response questions, worth almost half the external mark, more often reach the top tier -- so revision time spent specifically practising **evaluate** and **discuss** style answers (weighing evidence, acknowledging limitations, reaching a justified conclusion) pays off disproportionately for Paper 2.

## The internal assessment in more depth

The IA report is assessed against a set of criteria covering the whole investigative process, not just the final result: formulating a focused, testable research question; designing a method capable of generating enough reliable data to answer it; recording and processing that data appropriately (including uncertainty and error where relevant); and evaluating the investigation's strengths and weaknesses, including specific, actionable suggestions for improvement rather than generic ones. A common weakness examiners note is an evaluation section that lists limitations without connecting them concretely to how they affected the specific results obtained -- a strong evaluation ties each limitation back to its actual impact on the data, and proposes a specific, feasible improvement rather than a vague one like "use more accurate equipment."

## How Chemistry's assessment compares to its sibling sciences

DP Biology and DP Physics follow the same overall assessment architecture -- two external written papers plus an individually graded internal assessment, together with the ungraded collaborative sciences project -- but each subject's papers are built around its own organising framework rather than Chemistry's structure-and-reactivity pairing (Biology, for instance, organises around themes of unity and diversity across its own topic areas). If you are also studying another Group 4 science, recognising that the *shape* of assessment (external papers plus IA plus collaborative project) transfers directly, while the specific organising concepts tested do not, helps avoid wasted revision time trying to map one subject's framework onto another's.

## How SL and HL differ

The percentage weightings for Paper 1, Paper 2 and the internal assessment are identical at SL and HL -- what changes between levels is the depth and breadth of syllabus content examined and the demand of the questions asked, not the structural balance between components. This means a revision plan built around the assessment weightings above applies equally at either level; only the underlying content coverage needs adjusting for HL's additional material.

## Why 80/20 rather than an even split

The 80% external / 20% internal balance is deliberately weighted toward the two written papers because the DP sciences model treats the IA as a focused test of practical and investigative skill on one self-chosen topic, while the papers are needed to sample understanding across the whole syllabus breadth -- a single IA, however well executed, cannot demonstrate the same breadth of content mastery that two full papers can assess. Keeping this in mind when allocating revision time -- proportionally more time on paper-style practice than on IA polishing once a solid IA draft exists -- reflects where the majority of the final grade actually sits.
