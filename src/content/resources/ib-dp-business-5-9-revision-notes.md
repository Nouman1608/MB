These notes condense section 5.9, Management information systems, from Unit 5 of the IB *Business management guide*, first assessment 2024. The section is HL only. For full explanations, the cyber-risk calculation and an annotated model answer, read the [5.9 study guide](/resources/ib-dp-business-5-9/), then test yourself with the [5.9 practice questions](/resources/ib-dp-business-5-9-practice/). ## Content points by depth

| Depth | Content points |
|---|---|
| AO1 | Data analytics; database; cybersecurity and cybercrime |
| AO2 | Critical infrastructures: artificial neural networks, data centres, cloud computing; virtual reality; the internet of things; artificial intelligence; big data |
| AO3 | Customer loyalty programmes; using data to manage and monitor employees, Digital Taylorism; data mining to inform decisions; benefits, risks and ethical implications for decision-making and stakeholders |

## Key terms

| Term | Working definition |
|---|---|
| Management information system | Technologies that collect, store, process and present information for managers' decisions |
| Data / information | Raw facts / processed facts that mean something to a decision-maker |
| Data analytics | Examining data to find patterns and support decisions |
| Database | An organized collection of data that can be searched and updated |
| Big data | Data of very large volume, high velocity and wide variety |
| Cybercrime | Crime committed through computers and networks |
| Cybersecurity | Measures that protect systems and data from attack |
| Artificial neural network | A computing system, loosely modelled on the brain, that learns patterns from examples |
| Data centre | A facility housing the servers and storage that run IT systems |
| Cloud computing | Storage and processing supplied over the internet, usually paid for by use |
| Internet of things | Physical objects with sensors that send and receive data |
| Virtual reality | An immersive computer-generated environment |
| Artificial intelligence | Computer systems doing tasks that normally need human intelligence |
| Digital Taylorism | Digital tools used to split work into measured tasks with standard times and constant monitoring |
| Data mining | Searching large datasets for patterns, links and unusual cases |

## The data-to-decision chain

Capture (sensors, tills, websites), storage (databases, data centres, cloud), processing (analytics, mining, AI), presentation (dashboards, reports), decision. A weak link, such as poor-quality data at capture, spoils the decision at the end.

## Cyber risk in numbers

Expected loss = probability of breach × cost of breach. Compare the expected loss without security spending with the expected loss plus the security cost.

Mini reminder with new figures: chance of a breach 0.20, cost 250,000, so expected loss = 50,000. A 15,000 security package cuts the chance to 0.08: 0.08 × 250,000 = 20,000, plus 15,000 = 35,000. The package lowers the expected yearly cost by 15,000. Remember that probabilities are estimates and lost trust is hard to price.

## AI and monitoring: three fairness tests

- **Transparency:** can the result be explained?
- **Accountability:** who answers for a harmful result?
- **Fairness:** are outcomes unequal between groups because of biased training data?

Monitoring employees: add **proportionality**, meaning collect only what is needed for a legitimate purpose, and tell staff what is collected and why.

## Stakeholder effects

| Stakeholder | Possible benefits | Possible risks |
|---|---|---|
| Customers | Personal offers, faster service | Loss of privacy, unfair targeting, data theft |
| Employees | Less routine work, safer training through VR | Surveillance, stress, job losses |
| Owners | Lower costs, better forecasts | Set-up cost, breach costs, legal and reputational damage |
| Managers | Faster, evidence-based decisions | Over-reliance on systems they cannot explain |
| Society | New services, efficiency | Energy use of data centres, unequal access |

## Loyalty programmes: weigh both values

- For: repeat buying, customer data, targeted offers, switching costs.
- Against: reward costs, rivals copy, extra spending may not be caused by the programme, privacy concerns.

## Exam traps

- Confusing data with information.
- Writing a long definition for an AO1 term but no judgement for an AO3 point.
- Calling AI objective or neutral.
- Assuming correlation in mined data proves cause.
- Treating cloud computing as risk-free: it creates dependence on the supplier and the internet connection.
- Forgetting the employee perspective in monitoring questions.
- Not using the term Digital Taylorism when the case describes it.

## Self-test

1. Define a database.
2. Distinguish between data and information.
3. Name the three critical infrastructures listed in the guide.
4. Give one business use of the internet of things.
5. Give one benefit and one risk of cloud computing for a small business.
6. A breach has a 0.10 chance and would cost 400,000. Calculate the expected loss.
7. A 25,000 package cuts that chance to 0.04. Is it worth buying on expected values?
8. Explain how an AI recruitment system could be biased.
9. What is Digital Taylorism?
10. Give one ethical issue in using loyalty-card data.
11. Why may data mining results mislead a manager?
12. State which content points in 5.9 are set at AO3.

### Answers

1. An organized collection of data, usually held electronically, that can be searched and updated.
2. Data are raw facts; information is data processed and presented so it is useful for a decision.
3. Artificial neural networks, data centres and cloud computing.
4. Sensors on machines report wear so maintenance is done before breakdown (or vehicle tracking for routes).
5. Benefit: low start-up cost and easy to scale. Risk: dependence on the supplier and internet connection; security responsibilities must be agreed.
6. 0.10 × 400,000 = 40,000.
7. 0.04 × 400,000 = 16,000, plus 25,000 = 41,000, which is more than 40,000, so on expected values alone it is not worth it (by 1,000).
8. It learns from past hiring data; if past decisions favoured one group, the system repeats that pattern.
9. Using digital tools to split work into small measured tasks, set standard times and monitor output continuously.
10. Customers may not have agreed to their data being used in that way, or offers may treat some groups unfairly.
11. Patterns show association, not cause, and the data may be incomplete or of poor quality.
12. Loyalty programmes; using data to manage and monitor employees and Digital Taylorism; data mining; benefits, risks and ethical implications of MIS.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is management information systems an HL-only topic?**
Yes. Section 5.9 is HL only in the IB Business Management guide, first assessment 2024.

**What should an evaluation of a management information system include?**
Benefits and risks for named stakeholders, an ethical point such as privacy or bias, and a judgement that states what it depends on.

**What is expected loss in cyber-risk decisions?**
The probability of a breach multiplied by its estimated cost. It is a standard tool for weighing security spending, not a guide formula.
