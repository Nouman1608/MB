This guide covers section 5.3, Circles, arcs and sectors, in the Mensuration topic of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The syllabus asks you to carry out calculations involving the circumference and area of a circle, and arc length and sector area as fractions of the circumference and area of a circle, including minor and major sectors. Answers may be asked for in terms of π, and the formulas for circumference and area are given in the List of formulas. The syllabus also says to use the value of π from your calculator or 3.142, and to give non-exact answers to 3 significant figures. After this guide, use the [circles, arcs and sectors revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors-revision-notes/) and the [circles, arcs and sectors practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circles-arcs-and-sectors-practice/).

## What section 5.3 asks for

1. Use the circumference and area formulas, forwards and backwards.
2. Find the arc length and the area of a sector, as fractions of the whole circle.
3. Find the perimeter of shapes made from arcs and straight lines.
4. Leave answers in terms of π when asked.

## The circle formulas

The List of formulas gives these two.

```
circumference C = 2πr  (or πd)
area A = πr²
```

Here r is the radius and d is the diameter, with d = 2r. The formulas are given, but you must be able to use them in either direction and to know how radius and diameter relate. Read the question carefully: the number you are given may be the diameter.

### Worked example 1

Find the circumference and area of a circle of radius 7 cm, to 3 significant figures.

```
C = 2 × π × 7 = 14π = 44.0 cm
A = π × 7² = 49π = 154 cm²
```

In terms of π, the answers are 14π cm and 49π cm². 

### Worked example 2

A circle has diameter 18 cm. Find its circumference and area, to 3 significant figures.

```
radius = 9 cm
C = π × 18 = 56.5 cm
A = π × 9² = 81π = 254 cm²
```

A common mistake is to put 18 into πr². The radius is half the diameter.

### Working backwards

### Worked example 3

The circumference of a circle is 10π cm. Find its radius and area in terms of π.

```
2πr = 10π
r = 5 cm
A = π × 5² = 25π cm²
```

### Worked example 4

A circle has area 50 cm². Find its radius to 3 significant figures.

```
πr² = 50
r² = 50 ÷ π = 15.915...
r = 3.99 cm
```

Keep the unrounded value in your calculator for the square root.

## Arcs and sectors

An **arc** is part of the circumference. A **sector** is the region between two radii and an arc. A sector with an angle of θ° is the fraction θ/360 of the whole circle.

```
arc length = θ/360 × 2πr
sector area = θ/360 × πr²
```

A sector with θ below 180° is a **minor sector**. A sector with θ above 180° is a **major sector**. The angle of the major sector is 360° minus the angle of the minor sector.

### Worked example 5

A sector has radius 10 cm and angle 72°. Find the arc length, the area and the perimeter of the sector.

```
fraction = 72/360 = 1/5
arc length = 1/5 × 2π × 10 = 4π = 12.6 cm
area = 1/5 × π × 10² = 20π = 62.8 cm²
perimeter = arc + two radii = 4π + 20 = 32.6 cm
```

The perimeter of a sector includes the two straight edges. Forgetting them is a classic error.

### Worked example 6

A major sector has radius 6 cm and angle 250°. Find its arc length and area.

```
fraction = 250/360 = 25/36
arc length = 25/36 × 2π × 6 = 25π/3 = 26.2 cm
area = 25/36 × π × 36 = 25π = 78.5 cm²
```

### Finding an angle or a radius

### Worked example 7

An arc of a circle with radius 10 cm has length 5π cm. Find the angle at the centre.

```
5π = θ/360 × 20π
θ = 5π × 360 ÷ 20π = 90°
```

### Worked example 8

A sector with angle 120° has area 12π cm². Find the radius.

```
12π = 120/360 × πr²
12π = πr²/3
r² = 36
r = 6 cm
```

## Semicircles, quarter circles and combined perimeters

A semicircle is half a circle, with θ = 180°. A quarter circle has θ = 90°. For the perimeter of a semicircle, add the diameter to the curved part.

### Worked example 9

A semicircle has radius 5 cm. Find its perimeter and area.

```
curved part = 1/2 × 2π × 5 = 5π
perimeter = 5π + 10 = 25.7 cm
area = 1/2 × π × 25 = 12.5π = 39.3 cm²
```

### Worked example 10

A running track has two straights of 100 m and two semicircular ends. The total length of one lap is 400 m. Find the radius of the semicircles.

```
the two semicircles make one full circle of circumference 2πr
200 + 2πr = 400
2πr = 200
r = 100 ÷ π = 31.8 m
```

### Wheels and rotations

The distance a wheel travels in one turn equals its circumference. A wheel of radius 35 cm has circumference 70π cm, about 220 cm. To travel 100 m (10 000 cm), it makes 10 000 ÷ (70π) = 45.5 turns.

### Worked example 11

A pendulum 80 cm long swings through an angle of 36°. Find the length of the arc traced by the end of the pendulum.

```
fraction = 36/360 = 1/10
arc = 1/10 × 2π × 80 = 16π = 50.3 cm
```

### Worked example 12

The minute hand of a clock is 12 cm long. How far does its tip move in 20 minutes?

```
20 minutes is 20/60 = 1/3 of a full turn
distance = 1/3 × 2π × 12 = 8π = 25.1 cm
```

## Rings

A ring (or annulus) is the region between two circles with the same centre. Its area is the difference between the two circle areas.

### Worked example 13

A circular pond of radius 5 m is surrounded by a path of width 4 m. Find the area of the path in terms of π, and to 3 significant figures.

```
outer radius = 5 + 4 = 9 m
area = π × 9² − π × 5² = 81π − 25π = 56π
     = 176 m²
```

Subtract the areas, not the radii. Finding the area of a circle of radius 4 is a mistake.

## Percentages and angles

A sector can be described by a fraction, a percentage or an angle. A sector that is 15% of a circle has angle 0.15 × 360 = 54°. A sector of angle 90° has area equal to one quarter of the circle.

## A link between arc length and sector area

The area of a sector is also 1/2 × radius × arc length. Check with the sector in worked example 5: 1/2 × 10 × 4π = 20π, which matches. This is not in the List of formulas, so treat it as a shortcut and check, and use the fraction method in an answer. It is especially handy when you are given the arc length instead of the angle, because you can find the area without ever finding θ.

Likewise, if two sectors come from the same circle, the ratio of their arc lengths is the ratio of their areas, and both are the ratio of their angles. This lets you compare sectors without calculating π at all.

## Exact answers and rounding

If a question says "in terms of π" or "leave your answer in terms of π", write the answer with π as a symbol, such as 14π. Otherwise, use the π key on your calculator, or 3.142, and round the final answer to three significant figures. Avoid rounding in the middle of a calculation, because it can change the third figure.

## Common mistakes

- Using the diameter in πr², or the radius in πd.
- Forgetting to add the two radii to the arc length when asked for the perimeter of a sector.
- Using 360/θ instead of θ/360.
- Confusing circumference (a length) with area (square units).
- Rounding π to 3.14 too early.
- Giving a reflex angle for a minor sector.

## Exam technique

- **Calculate** the area: write the formula, then the substitution, then the answer with its unit.
- **Give your answer in terms of π**: do not use a decimal.
- **Find** the radius from a circumference or area: rearrange first.
- Sketch the sector and mark the angle and radius.
