Condensed for the final weeks. For the worked poem, use the
[Unseen poetry study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-english-literature-9275-unseen-poetry/).
These notes serve **Route A (3.2.2)** and **Route B (3.3.2)**.

## At a glance

| | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|
| Where | Paper 2a, Section B | Paper 2b, Section B |
| Task | One question on one unseen poem | One question on one unseen poem |
| Marks | 30 | 30 |
| Time | About 45 minutes | About 45 minutes |
| Next on the paper | Unseen prose (Section C) | Nothing: anthology and unseen only |

No prior knowledge of the poem or poet is expected. Everything comes from the page.

## The five-step method

1. **Sense:** read once; who speaks, about what? One-sentence summary.
2. **Shape:** stanzas, line length, rhyme, punctuation, where the poem turns.
3. **Detail:** underline five or six images or words you will use.
4. **Plan:** a one-sentence interpretation plus three or four points in poem order.
5. **Write:** introduction, developed paragraphs, conclusion on the ending.

## Key terms

| Term | Use it when... |
|---|---|
| Speaker | Discussing the voice; do not assume it is the poet |
| Enjambment | A line runs on, often creating suspense or emphasis at the break |
| End-stopped line | A line closes with punctuation, creating a firm pause |
| Caesura | A mid-line pause slows or breaks the rhythm |
| Volta / turn | The poem changes direction or tone |
| Metaphor / simile | A comparison shapes how the subject is seen |
| Personification | Something non-human is given human qualities |
| Sibilance / assonance | Sound patterns clearly create a mood |
| Juxtaposition | Contrasting images are placed close together |
| Tone | The attitude of the voice: tender, bitter, ironic, uncertain |
| Cyclical structure | The ending returns to the opening |

## Paragraph pattern

**Point about the question → short quotation → method → effect → link to the poem's development.**

Tentative phrasing ("perhaps", "this could suggest") shows you are exploring, which the higher levels
reward.

## Always cover

- The **title**: it often frames the poem.
- The **turn**: what changes, and why there.
- The **ending**: how it resolves, or refuses to resolve, the poem.
- At least one **structural** point, not only language.

## Exam traps

- Hunting for hidden meanings before grasping the surface.
- Naming devices without effects.
- Spending the whole answer on the first stanza.
- Guessing the poet's biography.
- Line-by-line paraphrase.
- Route A: overrunning and leaving too little time for unseen prose.

## Self-test

1. Which sections of which papers assess unseen poetry on each route?
2. What should Step 1 produce?
3. Why plan points in the order of the poem?
4. What is the difference between enjambment and an end-stopped line?
5. Name three features you should always cover.
6. Why is biography not useful in this question?

**Answers:** 1. Route A: Paper 2a, Section B. Route B: Paper 2b, Section B. 2. A one-sentence summary
of who is speaking and what the poem is about. 3. It shows the poem's development and makes sure you
reach the ending. 4. Enjambment runs a sentence over the line break; an end-stopped line closes with
punctuation. 5. Any three of: the title, the turn, the ending, a structural point. 6. The poem is
unseen, so you are not expected to know the poet; marks come from analysing what is on the page.
