Subtopic 20.3 of Cambridge International AS & A Level Physics 9702 (2025–2027) covers the direction and size of the force on a moving charge (F = BQv sin θ), the Hall voltage V_H = BI/(ntq) and the Hall probe, circular motion of charged particles in a uniform field, and velocity selection. These notes are condensed for recall. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [Force on a moving charge study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-force-on-a-moving-charge/); to apply the ideas, try the [Force on a moving charge practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-a-force-on-a-moving-charge-practice/).

## The equations

```
F = BQv sin θ           θ = angle between velocity and field
r = mv / (BQ)           (from BQv = mv²/r, velocity ⊥ field)
p = mv = BQr
T = 2πm / (BQ)          (independent of speed)
V_H = BI / (ntq)        t = thickness in the direction of B
v = E / B               (velocity selector; E = V/d for plates)
```

## Directions

- Left-hand rule: first finger = field, second finger = conventional current, thumb = force.
- Positive charge: current along the velocity.
- Negative charge: current **opposite** to the velocity.
- Force ⊥ velocity and ⊥ field → no work, speed constant.

| Particle | Velocity | Field | Force |
|---|---|---|---|
| Positive | right | into page | up |
| Negative | right | into page | down |
| Positive | right | out of page | down |
| Negative | up | into page | right |

## Circular paths

| Change (others fixed) | Radius | Period |
|---|---|---|
| Speed doubled | doubled | unchanged |
| B doubled | halved | halved |
| Mass doubled | doubled | doubled |
| Charge doubled | halved | halved |
| Particle loses energy | decreases (spiral inwards) | unchanged |

- Path is a circle in the plane perpendicular to B.
- A velocity component along B is unaffected by the field.

## Hall effect in five steps

1. Current flows along a thin slice; B is perpendicular to its face.
2. Magnetic force BQv pushes carriers to one edge.
3. Opposite charges are left on the other edge → electric field across the slice.
4. Equilibrium when QE = BQv (E = V_H/d).
5. With I = nAvq and A = dt: V_H = BI/(ntq); the width d cancels.

## Why semiconductors

- n for a metal is very large (copper about 8.5 × 10²⁸ m⁻³) → V_H tiny (microvolts or less).
- n for a semiconductor is much smaller → V_H large enough to measure easily.

## Using a Hall probe

- Calibrate in a known field (V_H ∝ B at constant current).
- Rotate until V_H is a maximum: face perpendicular to B.
- Keep the probe current constant.
- Zero reading may mean the face is parallel to B, not that B = 0.

## Velocity selector

- Electric force QE and magnetic force BQv are opposite.
- Only v = E/B passes undeflected; Q cancels, so it works for any charge or mass.
- v > E/B: magnetic force wins. v < E/B: electric force wins.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Charge carrier | Moving charged particle that makes up a current (electrons, holes, ions) |
| Number density n | Number of charge carriers per unit volume (m⁻³) |
| Drift speed | Mean speed of carriers along a conductor |
| Hall voltage | P.d. across a current-carrying slice in a perpendicular magnetic field |
| Hall probe | Calibrated semiconductor slice used to measure B |
| Velocity selector | Crossed E and B fields passing only particles with v = E/B |

## Exam traps

- Not reversing the left-hand rule for electrons.
- Claiming the magnetic force changes a particle's speed or kinetic energy.
- Writing r = mv²/(BQ).
- Using the width instead of the thickness for t.
- Saying a faster particle has a shorter period.
- Thinking a velocity selector depends on charge or mass.
- Forgetting sin θ when the velocity is not perpendicular to the field.

## Self-test

1. Write the force on a charge Q moving at speed v at angle θ to a field B.
2. Why does a magnetic field not change the speed of a charged particle?
3. A proton moves at 2.0 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹ perpendicular to a 0.25 T field. Calculate the force. (e = 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ C)
4. Calculate the radius of the proton's path in Question 3. (m_p = 1.67 × 10⁻²⁷ kg)
5. The proton's speed is doubled. What happens to its period?
6. An electron moves to the right in a field into the page. Which way is the force?
7. State what t represents in V_H = BI/(ntq).
8. Why is a semiconductor used in a Hall probe rather than a metal?
9. A Hall probe reads zero in a region known to contain a field. Suggest why.
10. Plates 1.5 cm apart have a p.d. of 3.0 kV; B = 0.060 T. Find the selected speed.
11. A slice with n = 2.0 × 10²² m⁻³ and thickness 0.10 mm carries 20 mA in a 0.20 T field. Find V_H.
12. Two ions with the same charge pass through the same velocity selector and then enter the same uniform field. Ion X has twice the mass of ion Y. Compare their radii.

**Answers:** 1. F = BQv sin θ. 2. The force is always perpendicular to the velocity, so it does no work. 3. 0.25 × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.0 × 10⁶ = 8.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ N. 4. r = mv/(BQ) = 0.084 m. 5. Unchanged (T = 2πm/(BQ)). 6. Down the page. 7. The thickness of the slice, measured in the direction of the field. 8. A semiconductor has a much smaller n, so V_H is much larger and easier to measure. 9. The face of the probe is parallel to the field; rotate it to find the maximum. 10. E = 3000/0.015 = 2.0 × 10⁵ V m⁻¹; v = E/B = 3.3 × 10⁶ m s⁻¹. 11. 0.20 × 0.020 ÷ (2.0 × 10²² × 1.0 × 10⁻⁴ × 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 0.013 V. 12. Same speed and charge, r ∝ m: X has twice the radius of Y.
