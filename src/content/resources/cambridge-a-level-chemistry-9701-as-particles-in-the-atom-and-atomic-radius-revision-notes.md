## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Nucleus | Very small, dense centre of the atom containing protons and neutrons |
| Proton (atomic) number | Number of protons in the nucleus; identifies the element |
| Mass (nucleon) number | Total number of protons and neutrons in the nucleus |
| Nucleon | A proton or a neutron |
| Nuclear charge | Total positive charge of the nucleus, equal to the proton number |
| Shielding | Repulsion of outer electrons by inner-shell electrons, reducing the attraction they feel |
| Atomic radius | Measure of the size of an atom, from the nucleus to the outer electrons |
| Ionic radius | Measure of the size of an ion |
| Isoelectronic | Having the same number of electrons (and the same arrangement) |

## The atom

| Particle | Relative charge | Relative mass | Location |
|---|---|---|---|
| Proton | +1 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Neutron | 0 | 1 | Nucleus |
| Electron | −1 | 1/1836 | Shells |

- Atom radius about 10⁻¹⁰ m; nucleus about 10⁻¹⁵ m: the atom is mostly empty space.
- Mass: more than 99.9 % in the nucleus.
- Charge: all positive charge in the nucleus; negative charge spread through the electron shells.
- Neutral atom: protons = electrons.

## Counting particles

    protons   = proton number
    neutrons  = mass number − proton number
    electrons = protons − charge

| Species | p | n | e |
|---|---|---|---|
| ⁴⁰Ca²⁺ | 20 | 20 | 18 |
| ⁷⁹Br⁻ | 35 | 44 | 36 |
| ¹⁹F⁻ | 9 | 10 | 10 |
| ¹H₃¹⁶O⁺ | 11 | 8 | 10 |

**Method: polyatomic ions**
1. Add protons for every atom.
2. Add neutrons for every atom (¹H contributes none).
3. Electrons = total protons − overall charge.

**Method: identify a species from p, n, e**
1. Protons = electrons + charge.
2. Proton number gives the element.
3. Mass number = protons + neutrons.

## Beams in an electric field (same velocity)

| Particle | Direction | Relative size of deflection |
|---|---|---|
| Proton | Towards negative plate | 1 |
| Electron | Towards positive plate | about 1836 |
| Neutron | Undeflected | 0 |

- Direction: sign of charge.
- Size: proportional to charge ÷ mass.
- Ions: use charge ÷ mass number, e.g. ⁴He²⁺ (2/4) deflects the same as ²H⁺ (1/2).

## Atomic radius

| Factor | Across a period | Down a group |
|---|---|---|
| Nuclear charge | Increases | Increases |
| Number of shells | Same | Increases by one per period |
| Shielding | Roughly constant | Increases |
| Overall radius | Decreases | Increases |

- Period 3 typical values (nm): Na 0.186, Mg 0.160, Al 0.143, Si 0.117, P 0.110, S 0.104, Cl 0.099.
- Noble gas radii are measured differently and are not compared with the others.

## Ionic radius

- Cation smaller than its atom: often a whole shell lost; same nuclear charge on fewer electrons.
- Anion larger than its atom: extra electron(s) increase repulsion in the outer shell; nuclear charge unchanged.
- Isoelectronic series (all 2,8): N³⁻ > O²⁻ > F⁻ > Na⁺ > Mg²⁺ > Al³⁺. Nuclear charge rises from 7 to 13 with the same ten electrons.
- Across Period 3: cations shrink (Na⁺ to Al³⁺), then a big rise to P³⁻ (extra shell), then anions shrink (P³⁻ to Cl⁻).
- Down a group: ions of the same charge get larger (extra shell): Li⁺ < Na⁺ < K⁺; F⁻ < Cl⁻ < Br⁻.

## Exam traps

- Negative ions have **more** electrons than protons.
- ¹H has no neutrons.
- Deflection depends on charge ÷ mass, not mass alone.
- Electrons deflect much more than protons, in the opposite direction.
- Shielding is roughly constant across a period.
- Radius rises sharply between Al³⁺ and P³⁻.
- Mass number is not the relative atomic mass; it is a whole-number count of nucleons.

## Self-test

**1.** State the relative charge and relative mass of an electron.

**2.** Describe where the mass and the charge of an atom are found.

**3.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in ⁵²Cr³⁺.

**4.** Give the numbers of protons, neutrons and electrons in a ¹⁴N¹⁶O₃⁻ ion.

**5.** A species has 16 protons, 18 neutrons and 18 electrons. Write its symbol with mass number and charge.

**6.** Explain why neutrons are not deflected in an electric field.

**7.** Which is deflected more, ³He²⁺ or ⁴He²⁺? Explain.

**8.** Why does atomic radius decrease from Na to Cl?

**9.** Why does atomic radius increase from Li to K?

**10.** Put in order of increasing radius: Mg²⁺, F⁻, Na⁺, O²⁻.

**11.** Why is P³⁻ much larger than Al³⁺?

**12.** Why is Cl⁻ larger than a Cl atom?

## Self-test answers

**1.** Relative charge −1; relative mass 1/1836.

**2.** Almost all the mass and all the positive charge are in the tiny nucleus; the negative charge is spread through the electron shells around it.

**3.** 24 protons, 28 neutrons, 21 electrons.

**4.** Protons 7 + 3 × 8 = 31; neutrons 7 + 3 × 8 = 31; electrons 31 + 1 = 32.

**5.** ³⁴S²⁻.

**6.** They have no charge, so the field exerts no force on them.

**7.** ³He²⁺, because its charge-to-mass ratio (2/3) is larger than that of ⁴He²⁺ (2/4).

**8.** Nuclear charge increases while the electrons are added to the same shell with the same inner shells, so shielding is roughly constant and the outer electrons are pulled closer.

**9.** Each element has an extra shell, so the outer electron is further from the nucleus and more shielded; this outweighs the increased nuclear charge.

**10.** Mg²⁺ < Na⁺ < F⁻ < O²⁻.

**11.** P³⁻ has three occupied shells (2,8,8) while Al³⁺ has lost its third shell (2,8).

**12.** The extra electron increases repulsion between electrons in the outer shell, while the nuclear charge stays the same, so the electrons spread out further.
