This guide covers **Paper 1 Introducing Sociology**, one of two
components in OxfordAQA International GCSE Sociology (9292), a new
qualification (Version 1.0) first examined May/June 2028. Research
methods content is shared between the two papers rather than existing
as a separate, standalone component.

## Where this fits in 9292

Introducing Sociology applies core sociological thinking to two
familiar social institutions -- the family and education -- while
building the research methods skills that recur, in different form,
in Paper 2 (Exploring Sociology). As an entry point into sociology at
GCSE level, it also establishes the basic vocabulary of sociological
perspectives that the rest of the course assumes.

## Syllabus coverage

**OXFORDAQA INTERNATIONAL GCSE SOCIOLOGY (9292) — PAPER 1 INTRODUCING
SOCIOLOGY**

- 1.1 Families — the structure, functions and diversity of family life
- 1.2 Education — the role, structure and impact of the education
system
- 1.3 Research Methods — the methods sociologists use to investigate
social life

## How to approach it

Families (1.1) and Education (1.2) both reward being able to apply a
named sociological perspective to a specific institutional example,
since exam questions typically ask students to use sociological
concepts to explain a real social pattern rather than describe it
neutrally. Because Research Methods (1.3) is shared across both
papers, practise applying it to family and education scenarios here,
then again to Paper 2's content on differences, inequalities and
socialisation, rather than treating it as an isolated topic revised
once. Building comparisons between how different sociological
perspectives interpret the same institution -- for example, the family
-- is a reliable way to prepare for the evaluative style of question
this specification favours. Since this is a newly launched
qualification, working through the specification's own sample
assessment materials once they are available is a particularly useful
way to understand exactly how these three sub-topics are examined in
practice, since question style can differ noticeably between
otherwise similar sociology specifications.
