Condensed notes for section 4.2 of Cambridge IGCSE Commerce (0715). For fuller explanations, a worked automation cost example and an annotated answer, use the [warehousing study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-commerce-0715-warehousing/).

## The syllabus in four lines

Section 4.2 asks for:

1. The **functions of warehousing** in the supply chain.
2. The **different types** of warehouse: bonded, cold storage, cash and carry, regional distribution centres.
3. The **uses of automation**: robotics, automated sorting, voice picking.
4. The **benefits of automation**: lower labour costs, less human error, improved picking accuracy, improved inventory control, improved customer service.

## Five functions to recall

Mnemonic **"Some Dogs Prefer Plain Pasta"**:

| Letter | Function | One-line meaning |
|---|---|---|
| S | Seasonal production | Store goods made at one time to sell all year |
| D | Demand | Build stock before a busy season |
| P | Protecting supply | Stock acts as a buffer against delay |
| P | Price stability | Store when supply is high, release when low |
| P | Postponement (logistics) | Delay packing or labelling until the order is known |

Examples to attach to each:

- **Seasonal production:** harvested grain kept until next harvest.
- **Demand:** winter clothing stored in summer, ready for the cold months.
- **Protecting supply:** spare stock if a ship or supplier is late.
- **Price stability:** fruit stored at harvest so prices do not collapse, then released later.
- **Logistics postponement:** a generic product stored plain and finished (labelled, packed, assembled) for each order.

## Four types of warehouse

| Type | Key feature | Typical goods | Link to remember |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bonded | Under customs control; import duty is delayed until goods are released | Wine, spirits, tobacco, electronics | Helps importer cash flow |
| Cold storage | Kept at low temperature | Meat, fish, dairy, fruit, medicines | Prevents spoilage |
| Cash and carry | Self service; trade customers pay at once and collect | Groceries, drinks, catering goods | A type of wholesaler |
| Regional distribution centre (RDC) | Receives bulk loads and sends mixed loads to shops in a region | Supermarket stock | Fewer deliveries to each shop |

Points to remember:

- A bonded warehouse does **not** remove duty; it **delays** it. If the goods are re-exported, duty may never be paid.
- An RDC serves a **region**, not one shop. It sorts goods into the mix each shop needs.
- Cold storage can also include freezing for long-term storage.

## Three uses of automation

| Use | What happens |
|---|---|
| Robotics | Robots move, lift, stack or carry goods and can work non-stop |
| Automated sorting | Scanners read codes and conveyors send each parcel to the right place |
| Voice picking | A headset tells the worker what to pick; the worker confirms by voice, hands free |

## Five benefits of automation

| Benefit | Why it follows |
|---|---|
| Lower labour costs | Fewer workers needed for repetitive tasks |
| Less human error | Machines repeat the same steps without tiredness |
| Improved picking accuracy | Barcodes and voice checks confirm the correct item |
| Improved inventory control | Every movement is recorded, so stock records stay current |
| Improved customer service | Correct orders sent faster |

Limits for evaluation: high cost of equipment and upkeep; staff training; machine or power failure; fewer jobs for staff.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Warehouse | Building used to store goods |
| Buffer stock | Extra stock held to cope with delay or high demand |
| Import duty | Tax on imported goods |
| Bonded warehouse | Secure warehouse under customs control where duty is delayed |
| Cold storage | Warehouse kept at low temperature |
| Cash and carry | Self-service wholesale warehouse; pay at once and collect |
| Regional distribution centre (RDC) | Large warehouse that supplies shops in a region |
| Automation | Use of machines and computers to do tasks done by hand |
| Picking | Selecting items from shelves to fill an order |
| Inventory control | Keeping an accurate record of stock |
| Logistics postponement | Delaying final preparation of goods until the order is known |

## Exam traps

- Name a **function** and then say what problem it solves; "store goods" alone is too vague.
- A bonded warehouse is about **customs duty**, not simply keeping imports.
- Cash and carry customers **collect their own goods**; there is no delivery.
- Do not treat all automation as robots. Know **all three uses**.
- Say how automation causes each benefit, for example that scanning improves accuracy.
- Price stability needs the mechanism: **store when supply is high, release when low**.

## Self-test

1. State **two** functions of warehousing.
2. How does a warehouse help with seasonal production?
3. What is logistics postponement?
4. Name the four types of warehouse in the syllabus.
5. What is the main advantage of a bonded warehouse to an importer?
6. Which type of warehouse suits frozen fish?
7. What does an RDC do for a supermarket chain?
8. State **three** uses of automation in warehousing.
9. What is voice picking?
10. State **two** benefits of warehouse automation.
11. Give **one** disadvantage of automation.
12. A warehouse employs 8 pickers at $85 a day. Automation lets it use 3 pickers and adds $200 a day in running cost. Calculate the daily saving.

**Answers**

1. Any two: links with seasonal production; links with demand; protecting supply; price stability; logistics postponement.
2. It stores goods made or harvested at one time of year so they can be sold throughout the year.
3. Delaying the final stage, such as packing or labelling, until an order is received.
4. Bonded, cold storage, cash and carry, regional distribution centre.
5. Import duty is delayed until the goods are released for sale, which helps cash flow.
6. Cold storage.
7. It receives bulk deliveries and sends mixed loads to shops in the region, so shops get fewer deliveries.
8. Robotics, automated sorting, voice picking.
9. A system in which headset instructions tell staff what to pick and the staff confirm by voice, leaving their hands free.
10. Any two: lower labour costs; less human error; improved picking accuracy; improved inventory control; improved customer service.
11. Any one: high cost of equipment; staff training needed; breakdown can stop work.
12. Current: 8 x 85 = $680. Automated: 3 x 85 = 255, plus 200 = $455. Saving = 680 - 455 = $225 per day.
