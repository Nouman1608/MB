## Questions

**1.** Sennick Drones Ltd issues 240,000 ordinary shares with a nominal value of 0.10 each at an issue price of 0.38 each. All cash is received. Calculate the amounts credited to ordinary share capital and to share premium, and the amount debited to the bank account. **[3]**

**2.** State the sub-heading in a limited company's statement of financial position under which each item appears.

**(a)** 7% debentures repayable in 2034 **[1]**
**(b)** Taxation owing at the year end **[1]**
**(c)** Share premium **[1]**
**(d)** Rent paid in advance **[1]**

**3.** Explain why dividends paid by a limited company do not appear in its income statement. **[2]**

**4.** For its year to 31 January 2026, Tolsey Teas Ltd reports: revenue 318,000; cost of sales 171,500; expenses (excluding interest) 94,750; taxation 9,100. The company has 75,000 of 6% debentures; only 2,250 of debenture interest has been paid. Calculate the three profit figures the income statement must show. **[5]**

**5.** At 1 November 2024, the equity of Fairlop Cycles Ltd was: ordinary shares of 1.00 each 200,000; share premium 15,000; retained earnings 42,000. In the year to 31 October 2025 it issued 60,000 ordinary shares at 1.40 each, made a loss for the year after tax of 18,500 and paid dividends of 9,000. Draw up the company's statement of changes in equity for that year. **[6]**

**6.** Kelsdale Pottery Ltd has ordinary shares with a nominal value of 0.50 each. Extracts from its records show:

| | Start of year | End of year |
|---|---:|---:|
| Ordinary share capital | 150,000 | 180,000 |
| Share premium | 12,000 | 42,000 |
| Retained earnings | 58,400 | 81,300 |

Profit for the year after tax was 34,900. There was one share issue during the year.

**(a)** Calculate the dividends paid during the year. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the number of shares issued during the year. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the issue price per share. **[2]**

**7.** A trainee at Brantsey Glass Ltd prepared this draft:

```
Gross profit                          190,000
Less: Operating expenses    120,600
      Dividends paid         14,000
      Debenture interest      3,000
                                      137,600
Profit for the year after tax          52,400
```

The company has 50,000 of 12% debentures, held for the whole year. Taxation for the year is 10,500. Calculate the corrected figures for all three profit lines: from operations, before tax and after tax. **[6]**

**8.** Explain two differences between the equity section of a limited company's statement of financial position and the capital section of a sole trader's statement of financial position. **[4]**

**9.** Hessle Bay Marine Ltd prepares its accounts to 30 September. On 1 October 2025 it borrowed 84,000 at 9% a year interest. The loan is repaid in seven equal annual instalments, the first on 30 September 2027. At 30 September 2026, interest has been paid only for the six months to 31 March 2026. Calculate the finance cost for the year, the interest owing, and the amounts shown under current liabilities and non-current liabilities for the loan itself. **[4]**

**10.** On 28 February 2026, Pitchcombe Opticians Ltd extracted this trial balance:

| Account | Dr | Cr |
|---|---:|---:|

| Purchases | 188,400 | |
| Inventory at 1 March 2025 | 22,600 | |

| Rent | 18,000 | |

| Debenture interest | 2,100 | |
| Premises: cost / accumulated depreciation | 180,000 | 21,600 |
| Fixtures: cost / accumulated depreciation | 45,000 | 13,500 |
| Trade receivables / trade payables | 28,400 | 19,750 |
| Bank | 9,800 | |
| Dividends paid | 7,500 | |

| Retained earnings at 1 March 2025 | | 24,200 |
| 7% debentures (repayable 2033) | | 60,000 |
| **Totals** | **593,050** | **593,050** |

At 28 February 2026: inventory 25,100; rent prepaid 1,500; depreciation on premises 2% on cost and on fixtures 10% on cost; taxation 9,250. No shares were issued during the year.

**(a)** Prepare Pitchcombe Opticians Ltd's income statement for the year to 28 February 2026. **[7]**
**(b)** Prepare its statement of changes in equity for that year. **[3]**
**(c)** Prepare its statement of financial position at that date. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Bank 240,000 x 0.38 = **91,200** [1]. Ordinary share capital 240,000 x 0.10 = **24,000** [1]. Share premium 240,000 x 0.28 = **67,200** [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the premium per share (0.28) in your working, then check that capital plus premium equals the cash received.

**2. (a)** **Non-current liabilities** [1]
**(b)** **Current liabilities** [1]
**(c)** **Equity** [1]
**(d)** **Current assets** (other receivables) [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the exact sub-heading names from the specification; "long-term" or "short-term" on its own may not be accepted.

**3.** Dividends are a distribution of profit to the shareholders (owners), not a cost of earning revenue [1]. So they are taken away from retained earnings, a movement recorded in the statement of changes in equity once the after-tax profit is known [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The second mark needs you to say where dividends do go; stating only "they are not an expense" gives half the answer.

**4.** Gross profit 318,000 - 171,500 = 146,500 [1]. Profit from operations 146,500 - 94,750 = **51,750** [1]. Finance cost 6% x 75,000 = 4,500, the full year, not the 2,250 paid [1]. Profit before tax 51,750 - 4,500 = **47,250** [1]. Profit after tax 47,250 - 9,100 = **38,150** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Label each of the three profit lines with its full name; an unlabelled figure is hard to credit even when it is correct.

**5.** Opening balances [1]. Share capital 60,000 x 1.00 = 60,000 [1]; premium 60,000 x 0.40 = 24,000 [1]. Loss (18,500) [1] and dividends (9,000) [1] in retained earnings.

```
                    Share capital   Premium   Retained    Total
At 1 Nov 2024             200,000    15,000     42,000  257,000
Share issue                60,000    24,000              84,000
Loss for the year                              (18,500) (18,500)
Dividends paid                                  (9,000)  (9,000)
At 31 Oct 2025            260,000    39,000     14,500  313,500
```

Closing balances **260,000 / 39,000 / 14,500 / 313,500** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Brackets matter: a loss shown without brackets and then added to retained earnings throws every later figure out.

**6. (a)** Opening retained earnings + profit - dividends = closing, so dividends = 58,400 + 34,900 - 81,300 [1] = **12,000** [1].
**(b)** Shares at start 150,000 / 0.50 = 300,000; at end 180,000 / 0.50 = 360,000 [1]; shares issued **60,000** [1].
**(c)** Premium per share (42,000 - 12,000) / 60,000 = 0.50 [1]; issue price 0.50 + 0.50 = **1.00** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), divide the increase in share capital by the nominal value (0.50), not by 1; share capital is recorded at nominal value only.

**7.** Remove dividends paid from the expenses [1]. Profit from operations 190,000 - 120,600 = **69,400** [1]. Finance cost 12% x 50,000 = 6,000 [1]. Profit before tax 69,400 - 6,000 = **63,400** [1]. Taxation deducted 10,500 [1]. Profit after tax **52,900** [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Set out the corrected three profit lines in full rather than adjusting the trainee's single total.

**8.** First difference: a company's equity is split into ordinary share capital, share premium and retained earnings [1], whereas a sole trader shows a single capital account that combines capital introduced and profit [1]. Second difference: a company's owners receive dividends, deducted from retained earnings [1], whereas a sole trader's drawings are deducted directly from capital [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Each difference needs both sides of the comparison, not just the company's.

**9.** Finance cost 9% x 84,000 = **7,560** [1]. Interest owing 7,560 / 2 = **3,780**, a current liability [1]. Instalment 84,000 / 7 = 12,000 due on 30 September 2027, so **12,000** under current liabilities [1] and **72,000** under non-current liabilities [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An instalment due within 12 months of the year end must be moved to current liabilities; showing the whole 84,000 as non-current loses that mark.

**10. (a)**

Revenue                                    352,000

      (22,600 + 188,400 - 25,100)

```

Cost of sales [1]; gross profit [1]; rent [1]; both depreciation charges [1]; profit from operations **50,250** [1]; finance costs [1]; profit before and after tax **36,800** [1].

**(b)**

```

At 1 Mar 2025          90,000   12,000    24,200  126,200

*Examiner insight:* If you doubt an earlier figure, still carry your own profit after tax into (b) and your own closing equity into (c) rather than leaving gaps, so each statement is complete and correctly laid out.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividends deducted as an expense instead of in the statement of changes in equity.
- Finance costs taken as the amount paid, not the full year's charge.
- Interest owing or taxation owing left out of current liabilities.
- Profit before tax, rather than profit after tax, carried into retained earnings.
- A loan instalment due within 12 months left under non-current liabilities.
- Accumulated depreciation not updated for the current year's charge before calculating carrying amounts.
