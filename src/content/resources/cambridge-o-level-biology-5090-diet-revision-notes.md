These notes condense subtopic **8.1 Diet** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the sources and dietary importance of carbohydrates, lipids, proteins, vitamins C and D, calcium, iron, fibre and water, the diseases caused by deficiency of vitamin C, vitamin D, calcium and iron, and the concept of a balanced diet. ## Nutrients: sources and importance

| Nutrient | Principal sources | Importance |
|---|---|---|
| Carbohydrates | Bread, rice, pasta, potato, sugar | Main source of energy |
| Lipids | Butter, oils, nuts, fatty meat, oily fish | Energy source and store; insulation under the skin; cell membranes |
| Proteins | Meat, fish, eggs, milk, beans | Growth and repair of tissues; making enzymes |
| Vitamin C | Citrus fruits, blackcurrants, fresh vegetables | Healthy skin and gums; wound healing |
| Vitamin D | Oily fish, eggs, margarine; made in skin in sunlight | Helps absorb calcium for bones and teeth |
| Calcium | Milk, cheese, green vegetables, fish bones | Strong bones and teeth; blood clotting |
| Iron | Red meat, liver, spinach, beans | Making haemoglobin in red blood cells |
| Fibre | Vegetables, fruit, wholemeal bread | Adds bulk so that the gut can move food by peristalsis |
| Water | Drinks and food | Solvent for reactions; carries substances in blood plasma; sweating cools the body |

## Deficiency diseases

| Missing | Disease | Symptoms |
|---|---|---|
| Vitamin C | Scurvy | Bleeding gums, loose teeth, bruising, slow wound healing, painful joints |
| Vitamin D | Rickets | Soft, weak bones; bowed legs in children |
| Calcium | Rickets | Soft, weak bones; bowed legs in children |
| Iron | Anaemia | Tiredness, pale skin, breathlessness, because less oxygen is carried |

Two nutrients, **vitamin D and calcium**, link to the same disease: vitamin D is needed to absorb calcium, and calcium is needed to build bone.

## Balanced diet

**A diet that contains all the nutrients in the correct proportions and provides the right amount of energy.**

| Person | Need more of | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Child or teenager | Protein, calcium, vitamin D | Growth, bone formation |
| Active person | Energy (carbohydrate, lipid) | Muscle contraction |
| Pregnant woman | Protein, calcium, iron | Growth of the baby; blood |
| Older, less active person | Less energy | Lower energy use |

- Energy in excess of needs is stored as fat.
- Too little energy means loss of body mass.

## Energy calculation method

Energy of a portion = (mass in g divided by 100) x energy per 100 g.

Example: 80 g bread at 1050 kJ per 100 g gives 0.8 x 1050 = 840 kJ. Add all the portions to find the total.

## Reading diet data

1. Check the units (kJ, g, mg) and the portion size.
2. Scale values per 100 g up or down to the real portion.
3. Compare like with like (same time period, same unit).
4. Explain the difference: growth, activity, pregnancy or age.

Percentage difference = (difference divided by the smaller value) x 100. For 12 000 kJ and 10 000 kJ: 2 000 divided by 10 000 x 100 = 20 per cent more.

## Short model answers

**State why iron is needed in the diet.** To make haemoglobin, the pigment in red blood cells that carries oxygen.

**Describe the symptoms of scurvy.** Bleeding gums, loose teeth, bruises, slow healing of wounds and painful joints.

**Explain why a child needs calcium and vitamin D.** Calcium is needed to make bones and teeth hard. Vitamin D helps the intestine to absorb calcium. A lack of either causes rickets.

**Explain why fibre is needed although it cannot be digested.** It adds bulk to the food so that the gut muscles can push it along by peristalsis, which prevents constipation.

**State what is meant by a balanced diet.** A diet that contains all the nutrients in the correct proportions and gives the right amount of energy.

## Exam traps

- Anaemia is lack of **iron**; scurvy is lack of **vitamin C**; rickets is lack of **vitamin D or calcium**.
- Fibre is not digested and gives no energy.
- Water does not provide energy.
- Always link the nutrient to its job: iron to haemoglobin, calcium to bones, vitamin D to calcium absorption.
- Give a **source** and a **role** if the question asks for both.
- Balanced does not mean low in energy.

## Self-test

1. State the main role of carbohydrates.
2. Give two roles of lipids.
3. Give two sources of protein.
4. Name the deficiency disease caused by a lack of vitamin C.
5. State two symptoms of scurvy.
6. How does vitamin D help the body?
7. Name the disease caused by lack of vitamin D or calcium.
8. Why does the body need iron?
9. State two symptoms of anaemia.
10. Why is fibre needed?
11. Name two roles of water.
12. Calculate the energy in 50 g of cheese at 1700 kJ per 100 g.

### Answers

1. Source of energy.
2. Any two: energy source or store; insulation under the skin; part of cell membranes.
3. Any two: meat, fish, eggs, milk, cheese, beans, nuts.
4. Scurvy.
5. Any two: bleeding gums; loose teeth; bruising; slow healing of wounds; painful joints.
6. It helps the intestine to absorb calcium for bones and teeth.
7. Rickets.
8. To make haemoglobin, which carries oxygen in red blood cells.
9. Any two: tiredness; pale skin; breathlessness.
10. It adds bulk so that food can be moved along the gut by peristalsis.
11. Any two: solvent for reactions; carries substances in blood plasma; sweating cools the body.
12. (50 divided by 100) x 1700 = 850 kJ.
