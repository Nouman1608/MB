## Section B

**6.** Silicon has three naturally occurring isotopes: ²⁸Si, ²⁹Si and ³⁰Si.

**(a)** State one way in which atoms of these isotopes are the same and one way in which they are different, in terms of subatomic particles. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why all three isotopes form silicon dioxide, SiO₂, in the same way when heated in oxygen. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why the atoms of the three isotopes have different masses but almost the same size. **[2]**

**7.** Hydrogen chloride can be made with different isotopes of hydrogen and chlorine.

**(a)** Give the relative molecular masses of all four possible molecules made from ¹H, ²H, ³⁵Cl and ³⁷Cl. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the lightest and heaviest molecules. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the density at room conditions of the lightest molecule as a gas, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**

**8.** Water containing ordinary hydrogen, H₂O, has a density of 1.00 g cm⁻³ at room temperature.

**(a)** Assuming that molecules of H₂O and D₂O take up the same volume in the liquid, calculate the expected density of D₂O. **[2]**

**(b)** The measured density of D₂O is about 1.10 g cm⁻³. Explain why this supports the assumption in (a). **[2]**

**(c)** Predict, with a reason, what happens when a cube of frozen D₂O is placed in liquid H₂O. **[2]**

**9.** A student writes: "Isotopes react the same way because they are atoms of the same element."

**(a)** Explain why this is not a full explanation. **[1]**

**(b)** Write a full explanation. **[3]**

**10.** Chlorine gas from a natural source contains ³⁵Cl₂, ³⁵Cl³⁷Cl and ³⁷Cl₂ molecules.

**(a)** Explain why a sample of pure ³⁷Cl₂ has a greater density than a sample of pure ³⁵Cl₂ at the same temperature and pressure. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the ratio of the density of ³⁷Cl₂ to that of ³⁵Cl₂. **[1]**

**(c)** Both samples are reacted with sodium. Predict whether the products have the same chemical formula, and explain. **[2]**

**11.** The species X, Y and Z have the following particles.

| Species | Protons | Neutrons | Electrons |
|---|---|---|---|
| X | 20 | 20 | 20 |
| Y | 20 | 24 | 18 |
| Z | 18 | 22 | 18 |

**(a)** Identify which two species are isotopes of the same element. **[1]**

**(b)** Write full symbols, with mass number, proton number and any charge, for X, Y and Z. **[3]**

**(c)** Explain why Y and Z have the same number of electrons but different chemical properties. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Both have 28 protons but different numbers of neutrons (30 and 32). *(Total: 1 mark)*

**2.** Atoms with the same number of protons [1] but different numbers of neutrons [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**3.** Mass number 37 and proton number 17, so chlorine [1]: ³⁷₁₇Cl [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**4.** ²H⁺: 1 proton, 1 neutron, 0 electrons [1]. ²⁵Mg²⁺: 12 protons, 13 neutrons, 10 electrons [1]. *(Total: 2 marks)*

**5.** Mass and density [1]. *(Total: 1 mark)*

**6. (a)** Same: 14 protons (and 14 electrons in the atom) [1]. Different: 14, 15 and 16 neutrons [1].

**(b)** All have 14 protons and so 14 electrons [1], with the same electronic configuration [1]. Chemical reactions involve the electrons (outer shell), not the neutrons, so they react in the same way [1].

**(c)** The extra neutrons add mass to the nucleus [1], but atomic size is decided by the electrons, which are the same in number and arrangement [1]. *(Total: 7 marks)*

**7. (a)** ¹H³⁵Cl = 36; ²H³⁵Cl = 37; ¹H³⁷Cl = 38; ²H³⁷Cl = 39. Two marks for all four; one mark for two or three [2].

**(b)** Lightest ¹H³⁵Cl (36), heaviest ²H³⁷Cl (39) [1].

**(c)** 36 ÷ 24.0 [1] = 1.50 g dm⁻³ [1]. *(Total: 5 marks)*

**8. (a)** Density is proportional to molecular mass if volume per molecule is the same [1]: 1.00 × 20 ÷ 18 = 1.11 g cm⁻³ [1].

**(b)** The measured value is very close to the prediction [1], so the molecules must occupy almost the same volume; only their mass differs, consistent with identical electron arrangements [1].

**(c)** It sinks [1], because the D₂O molecules are heavier but about the same size, so frozen D₂O is denser than liquid H₂O [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**9. (a)** It restates that they are the same element without explaining what makes reactions the same [1].

**(b)** Isotopes have the same number of protons, so the same number of electrons [1]. The electrons have the same configuration [1]. Reactions involve electrons, and neutrons (the only difference) take no part [1]. *(Total: 4 marks)*

**10. (a)** At the same temperature and pressure, equal volumes of gas contain equal numbers of molecules [1]. Each ³⁷Cl₂ molecule has a greater mass (74 compared with 70) because each atom has two more neutrons [1]. So the same volume of ³⁷Cl₂ has a greater mass, giving a greater density [1].

**(b)** 74 ÷ 70 = 1.06 [1].

**(c)** Yes: both give NaCl [1], because both chlorine isotopes have the same electronic configuration and gain one electron to form Cl⁻ [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

**11. (a)** X and Y [1] (both have 20 protons).

**(b)** X: ⁴⁰₂₀Ca [1]. Y: ⁴⁴₂₀Ca²⁺ [1]. Z: ⁴⁰₁₈Ar [1].

**(c)** Y and Z have different numbers of protons, so they are different elements (calcium and argon) [1]. Y is a positive ion with a nuclear charge of +20 holding its 18 electrons, while Z is a neutral argon atom with a nuclear charge of +18, so they do not behave the same way chemically [1]. *(Total: 6 marks)*

## Where marks are usually lost

- Choosing pairs with the same mass number as isotopes.
- Leaving out "same number of protons" in the definition.
- Explaining identical chemistry without mentioning electrons and configuration.
- Forgetting that gas density at fixed temperature and pressure depends on molecular mass because the number of molecules per volume is fixed.
- Saying heavier isotopes have larger atoms.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is ⁴⁰Ar not an isotope of ⁴⁰Ca?**
They have different proton numbers (18 and 20), so they are atoms of different elements. Equal mass numbers do not make atoms isotopes.

**Why is the density of D₂O about 10 % higher than that of H₂O?**
D₂O molecules have about 20/18 times the mass of H₂O molecules but are almost the same size, so the same volume of liquid has a greater mass.
