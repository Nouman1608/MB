## What the syllabus asks

A case study of some of the problems of sustainable management of a hazardous environment, with evaluation of attempted or possible solutions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Hazardous environment | Place where people live with a recurring natural threat |
| Sustainable hazard management | Reducing risk now and in future while people continue to live and work there, without shifting risk elsewhere |
| Mitigation | Actions before an event that reduce its effects |
| Preparedness | Plans, warnings, drills and stockpiles |
| Response | Rescue, shelter and care immediately after |
| Recovery | Rebuilding, ideally building back better |
| Resilience | Ability to absorb an event and recover quickly |
| Exclusion zone | Area closed to residents because of hazard |
| Managed retreat | Moving people and development away from the hazard |
| Multiple lines of defence | Combining walls, raised land, forests and evacuation |
| Hyogo Framework | International framework, 2005-2015 |
| Sendai Framework | International framework for disaster risk reduction, 2015-2030 |

## Problems to recall

- The hazard cannot be removed.
- Timing and size uncertain.
- Resources and jobs attract people to risky land.
- The poorest are often most exposed.
- High costs; dependence on outside funding.
- Defences can give false security.
- Exposure rises with population growth and climate change.

## Frameworks

- **Disaster cycle:** mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery.
- **Three approaches:** modify the event, modify vulnerability, modify the loss.
- **Sendai priorities:** understand risk; strengthen governance; invest in risk reduction for resilience; enhance preparedness and build back better.
- **Four tests:** effective, affordable, fair, resilient.

## Case study 1: Montserrat

- Soufrière Hills volcano erupting from July 1995.
- Plymouth abandoned and buried; airport and port lost; south closed as exclusion zone.
- 25 June 1997: pyroclastic flows killed 19 people, mostly people who had re-entered to farm.
- Population fell sharply as people emigrated.
- **Solutions:** Montserrat Volcano Observatory; hazard zoning with controlled access; development moved north (Little Bay, Brades); UK aid.
- **Judgement:** life protected by retreat; economy smaller and dependent on aid.

## Case study 2: Sanriku coast, Japan

- Faces the Japan Trench subduction zone; long tsunami history.
- 11 March 2011: tsunami exceeded many defences; Taro's sea wall, about 10 m high, overtopped.
- Warnings within minutes; practised evacuation saved many lives.
- **Rebuilding:** higher walls, raised land, housing moved uphill, coastal forests.
- **Judgement:** effective for frequent events; very costly; no wall can be sized for every event.

## Comparison

| | Montserrat | Sanriku coast |
|---|---|---|
| Approach | Retreat and zoning | Defence, warnings, retreat uphill |
| Funding | External aid | National government |
| Lesson | Zoning works only if enforced | Engineering must be combined with evacuation |

## Mini essay plan

*"Living with a hazard is more sustainable than trying to control it." How far do you agree?*

1. **Define** controlling the hazard (modify the event) and living with it (modify vulnerability and loss).
2. **For living with it:** zoning, warnings, drills and retreat cut deaths without requiring defences against every event; Montserrat's exclusion zone and Japanese evacuation drills.
3. **For control:** sea walls protect against frequent events; slope engineering in Hong Kong reduces landslides.
4. **Limits of control:** defences overtopped (Taro, 2011); false security.
5. **Limits of living with it:** retreat costs land and livelihoods; warnings fail if not trusted.
6. **Judgement:** most sustainable systems combine both, with emphasis on reducing vulnerability.

## Linking 9.4 to the other subtopics

A 9.4 essay can draw on any hazard type, so reuse evidence from the rest of the option:

- **Tectonic (9.1):** monitoring, zoning and building codes as mitigation; L'Aquila for risk communication.
- **Mass movement (9.2):** Hong Kong's slope registration and upgrading as long-term mitigation.
- **Atmospheric (9.3):** Odisha's evacuation before Cyclone Phailin as preparedness.

Whichever case study you use, give the problems first, then at least two solutions with evidence, then a judgement that applies the four tests: effective, affordable, fair and resilient.

## Exam traps

- Describing the event rather than the management.
- Judging by deaths alone.
- No problems section before solutions.
- Solutions without dates, places or evidence.
- Conclusion that does not say sustainable for whom and for how long.
- Ignoring why people stay in hazardous places.

## Self-test

1. Define sustainable hazard management.
2. Name the four stages of the disaster cycle.
3. Why is mitigation considered more sustainable than response?
4. What replaced the Hyogo Framework, and when?
5. Give two of Sendai's priorities.
6. Why was Plymouth abandoned?
7. Why did 19 people die on Montserrat in 1997?
8. Give one economic problem caused by the exclusion zone.
9. What happened to Taro's sea wall in 2011?
10. What is meant by multiple lines of defence?
11. How can defences increase risk?
12. Give one way Montserrat and Japan differ in their management.

### Answers

1. Reducing hazard risk now and for future generations while people continue to live and earn a living there, without moving the risk elsewhere.
2. Mitigation, preparedness, response, recovery.
3. It reduces losses before they happen, cutting the cost of response and recovery.
4. The Sendai Framework for Disaster Risk Reduction, 2015-2030.
5. Any two: understanding risk; strengthening governance; investing in risk reduction; enhancing preparedness and building back better.
6. Pyroclastic flows and ash from the Soufrière Hills volcano made it unsafe and later buried it.
7. Pyroclastic flows reached people who had returned to farm inside the evacuated zone.
8. Loss of farmland, the airport and port, and of workers through emigration.
9. It was overtopped by a tsunami larger than it was designed for and partly destroyed.
10. Combining walls, raised land, coastal forests, relocation and evacuation instead of relying on one measure.
11. They give a false sense of security, encouraging development and slower evacuation.
12. Montserrat relied on retreat and outside aid; Japan relied on national investment in defences and warnings.
