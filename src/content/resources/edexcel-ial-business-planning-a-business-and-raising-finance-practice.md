## Questions

**1.** Define the term "business angel". **[2]**

**2.** Define the term "unlimited liability". **[2]**

**3.** Amberlea Smoothies is a franchise. A franchisee pays an entry fee of 30,000 and then 7% of annual revenue. A franchisee's revenue is 240,000, 270,000 and 300,000 in years 1 to 3. Calculate the total the franchisee pays the franchisor over the three years. **[4]**

**4.** Zephyr Loop Ltd has 100,000 shares, of which the founder owns 60,000. A venture capital firm buys 40,000 new shares at 2.50 each. Calculate the amount raised and the founder's percentage of the shares after the sale. **[4]**

**5.** Maplewick Cycles, a repair shop, applies to a bank for a loan of 60,000. Its business plan includes a cash-flow forecast showing a monthly cash surplus of 2,600 before loan repayments. The repayments would be 1,400 a month. Explain one way the business plan helps the bank decide whether to lend. **[4]**

**6.** Juniper Quay Grocers buys 8,000 of produce from a supplier on 30 days' credit. It sells all the produce within 12 days. Explain why trade credit is a suitable method of finance for this purchase. **[4]**

**7.** Birchwood Brew, a new coffee roaster, needs a roasting machine. Buying costs 36,000, and the owner expects to sell it for 7,000 after four years. Leasing costs 820 a month for 48 months, including servicing. Analyse whether leasing or buying is more suitable for Birchwood Brew. **[6]**

**8.** Sana runs Pebblecrest Print as a sole trader, with annual profit of 84,000. Idris offers to invest 30,000 and share the business as a 50:50 partner, bringing design skills. Analyse the effects on Sana of moving from sole trader to partnership. **[6]**

**9.** Wickerby Candles, a new business, made a profit for the year of 18,000. The owner wants to buy equipment costing 45,000 and is considering using only retained profit. Discuss the suitability of retained profit for this purchase. **[8]**

**10.** Lantern Row Ltd, a restaurant, needs 150,000 to open a second site. It currently earns an operating profit of 38,000 a year before interest. Options: a bank loan at 9% interest a year; a business angel who would take 30% of the company; or a crowd-funding campaign. Assess the most suitable method of finance for Lantern Row Ltd. **[10]**

**11.** Northvale Foods Ltd is owned by one family, who hold all 20 million shares. The directors propose a stock market flotation as a plc, issuing 5 million new shares at 3.20 each. Costs of flotation are expected to be 6% of the amount raised. Assess whether flotation is the right decision. **[10]**

**12.** Reedbank Repairs is a community bicycle workshop that trains unemployed people and reinvests its profit in the training. Explain why it is described as a social enterprise. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** A business angel is a wealthy individual who invests their own money in a business [1], usually taking a share of ownership and often offering advice [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* A definition gains marks when it names who provides the money and what they receive. "Gives money" alone is too thin for two marks.

**2.** Unlimited liability means the owner is personally responsible for all the debts of the business [1], so personal assets could be used to pay them [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* The second point matters. Saying the owner "has to pay" without saying personal assets are at risk does not separate this from limited liability.

**3.** Total revenue = 240,000 + 270,000 + 300,000 = 810,000 [1]. Royalty = 7% of 810,000 = 56,700 [1]. Add the entry fee: 56,700 + 30,000 [1]. Total = **86,700** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the working and include the one-off fee. An answer that gives only the royalty total leaves out a cost the question states.

**4.** Amount raised = 40,000 × 2.50 = **100,000** [1]. Shares after = 100,000 + 40,000 = 140,000 [1]. Founder's share = 60,000 ÷ 140,000 [1] = **42.9%** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the new total, not the original 100,000, as the denominator. Note that the founder moves from majority to minority, which is the point to carry into any evaluation.

**5.** The plan contains a cash-flow forecast [1]. It shows a surplus of 2,600 a month against repayments of 1,400 [1], leaving 1,200 a month after repayment [1], so the bank can see the loan is likely to be repaid and the risk of lending is lower [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Answers gain credit when the figure is used from the stem and the conclusion is about the lender's decision, not just a description of what a plan contains.

**6.** The produce is sold by day 12 but the supplier is not paid until day 30 [1], so Juniper Quay receives the sales cash 18 days before it pays (30 − 12) [1]. The supplier is in effect financing the stock without interest if the bill is paid on time [1], which leaves cash free for wages and other costs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* A strong answer uses both the 30 days and the 12 days. A generic statement that trade credit "delays payment" does not use the stem.

**7.** Lease cost = 820 × 48 = 39,360 [1]. Net cost of buying = 36,000 − 7,000 = 29,000 [1], so leasing costs 10,360 more [1]. However, leasing needs no outlay of 36,000 now [1], which matters to a new business short of cash [1]. The lease includes servicing, but the business does not own the machine or gain the resale value [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis here means following a chain from the numbers to a business consequence. The comparison of total costs is the start, not the end. This is not a judgement question, so a recommendation is not required.

**8.** Idris adds 30,000 of capital [1] and design skills, which could help the business grow [1]. Sana's share of profit would fall from 84,000 to 42,000 at current profit [1], so profit would need to rise above 84,000 for her to gain [1]. Partners generally have unlimited liability, so Sana remains personally exposed [1] and may now be affected by debts and decisions arising from Idris's actions as well [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Pick up the 50:50 figure and show what it does to Sana's income. Credit tends to go to answers that look at the effect on Sana rather than listing partnership features.

**9.** For: no interest is paid [1] and no outsider gains control [1]. Against: at 18,000 a year, 45,000 would take 2.5 years even if all profit were kept (45,000 ÷ 18,000) [1], and the owner could take no drawings [1]. A new business may not yet have a reliable profit [1], and delaying the equipment may let competitors move ahead [1]. A loan or lease could allow earlier purchase, at a cost of interest or payments [1]. Judgement: retained profit is suitable only if the equipment is not urgent and the profit is dependable; otherwise a combination is more suitable [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* A discuss answer shows competing arguments and reaches a brief assessment. Both sides plus the 2.5-year calculation are needed; one side alone caps the answer.

**10.** Loan interest = 150,000 × 0.09 = 13,500 a year [1], which is 35.5% of the 38,000 operating profit [1] and leaves 24,500 [1]. A loan keeps ownership with the existing owners [1], but repayments must be met whatever sales are [1]. The angel's 30% of current profit is 11,400, less than the interest [1], but it is a permanent share of future profit, though no repayment is required and advice may help [1]. Crowd funding may not reach 150,000 and cannot be relied on [1]. For a restaurant with uncertain early sales, the angel reduces the cash-flow risk [1]. Judgement: the angel is more suitable if the owners accept sharing control; if control matters most, the loan is better and the interest is affordable [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Strong assessments weigh options against each other with figures from the stem and finish with a supported judgement that states what it depends on.

**11.** Amount raised = 5,000,000 × 3.20 = 16,000,000 [1]. Costs = 6% × 16,000,000 = 960,000 [1], so net funds = 15,040,000 [1]. The family keep 20 ÷ 25 = 80% of the shares [1], so control is not lost at first. For: a large sum with no repayment date [1] and a higher profile that helps later share issues [1]. Against: more public disclosure [1], pressure from shareholders for dividends and short-term profit [1], and takeover risk if more shares are sold later [1]. Judgement: worthwhile if the funds are needed for growth that borrowing cannot fund and the family accept scrutiny [1]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Calculations give the case its context, but an assess question needs a reasoned judgement. A list of pros and cons with no decision falls short.

**12.** A social enterprise trades to meet social or environmental aims [1]. Reedbank Repairs trains unemployed people, a social aim [1]. It reinvests its profit in that training instead of maximising returns for owners [1]. Because profit is not its only aim, it may be able to attract grants or support, though it still needs to cover its costs [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* An explanation is supported by details from the case. Quoting the training and reinvestment facts earns more than a definition on its own.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a term in a few words with no second point.
- Leaving out the fee, interest or other cost stated in the stem.
- Using the wrong denominator in a percentage ownership calculation.
- Repeating the stem figures without saying what they mean for the business.
- Writing "analyse" answers that list features but give no chain of reasoning.
- Giving only advantages or only disadvantages in a discuss or assess question.
- Finishing a judgement question without a decision.
- Describing liability in legal detail instead of stating it in general terms.
