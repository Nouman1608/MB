Questions 1 to 7 and question 11 cover understandings 1.3.1 to 1.3.5, which are SL and HL. Questions 8, 9 and 10 cover 1.3.6 and 1.3.7, which are additional higher level and are labelled **HL only**. ## Questions

**1.** Three sources emit radiation: a microwave oven, a green laser pointer and an X-ray tube.

**(a)** State how the energy of a photon changes as its wavelength increases. **[1]**
**(b)** List the three sources in order of increasing frequency of the radiation they emit. **[1]**

**2.** Light from a filament bulb and light from a neon discharge tube are each passed through a prism. Distinguish between the two spectra seen, and explain why the neon tube gives the spectrum it does. **[3]**

**3.** **(a)** Deduce how many electrons, at most, the fifth main energy level can hold. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of orbitals in a 4f sublevel. **[1]**

**4.** Describe the shape of an s orbital and the shapes and relative orientation of the three p orbitals in the same main energy level. **[3]**

**5.** Zinc has atomic number 30.

**(a)** Write the full electron configuration of a zinc atom. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the condensed electron configuration of the Zn²⁺ ion. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why forming Zn²⁺ leaves the 3d sublevel unchanged. **[1]**

**6.** Chromium has atomic number 24.

**(a)** Draw an orbital diagram for the 3d and 4s sublevels of a chromium atom. **[1]**
**(b)** State the number of unpaired electrons in the atom. **[1]**
**(c)** Explain why the configuration of chromium differs from the one predicted by the Aufbau order. **[2]**

**7.** Use these values for the energy levels of the hydrogen atom, in kJ mol⁻¹:

```
n = 1: −1312     n = 2: −328     n = 3: −145.8    n = 4: −82.0
n = 5: −52.5     n = 6: −36.4    n = ∞: 0
```

**(a)** Calculate the energy released, in kJ mol⁻¹, when electrons fall from n = 5 to n = 3, and state the region of the electromagnetic spectrum in which this line appears. **[2]**
**(b)** Of the drops n = 2 → 1, n = 6 → 2 and n = 4 → 3, identify the one that gives the line of shortest wavelength. Give a reason. **[2]**
**(c)** Use the data to explain why the lines in each family converge at higher frequency. **[2]**
**(d)** Explain how the hydrogen emission spectrum shows that the electron occupies discrete energy levels. **[1]**

**8.** **HL only.** Lines in the emission spectrum of potassium that end on its ground state converge at a wavelength of 286 nm. Calculate the first ionization energy of potassium in kJ mol⁻¹. Constants: Planck constant h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s; speed of light c = 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹; Avogadro constant L = 6.02 × 10²³ mol⁻¹. **[4]**

**9.** **HL only.** Use these first ionization energies, in kJ mol⁻¹:

```
F 1681    Cl 1251    Br 1140    I 1008
Zn 906    Ga 579     As 947     Se 941
```

**(a)** Explain the trend from fluorine to iodine. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why the value for gallium is lower than the value for zinc. **[2]**
**(c)** Explain why the value for selenium is slightly lower than the value for arsenic. **[2]**

**10.** **HL only.** The first seven successive ionization energies of an element in period 3 are, in kJ mol⁻¹: 1000, 2252, 3357, 4556, 7004, 8496, 27 107.

**(a)** Explain why the values keep rising. **[1]**
**(b)** Deduce the group of the element, showing how you used the data. **[2]**
**(c)** State the main energy level from which the seventh electron is removed. **[1]**

**11.** Cobalt has atomic number 27. It forms the ions Co²⁺ and Co³⁺.

**(a)** Write the condensed electron configuration of a cobalt atom. **[1]**
**(b)** Write the condensed electron configurations of Co²⁺ and Co³⁺. **[2]**
**(c)** Draw the 3d orbital diagram for Co³⁺ and state how many unpaired electrons it has. **[2]**
**(d)** State the number of electrons in the third main energy level of a cobalt atom, and the maximum this level could hold. **[2]**
**(e)** State which of the three species, Co, Co²⁺ or Co³⁺, has electrons in the fourth main energy level. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Energy decreases as wavelength increases [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **Microwave oven < green laser < X-ray tube** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An order is only correct if every item is in the right place, so check it against the spectrum sequence rather than guessing.

**2.** The bulb gives a continuous spectrum containing all visible wavelengths with no gaps; the neon tube gives a line spectrum of separate coloured lines on a dark background [1]. Electrons in neon atoms are excited to higher energy levels and emit photons as they fall back to lower levels [1]. Only certain energy gaps exist between levels, so only certain frequencies are emitted [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs both spectra described in the same sentence or pair of sentences; describing only the line spectrum leaves the comparison incomplete.

**3. (a)** 2n² = 2 × 5² = **50** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **Seven** [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* An answer of 14 for (b) gives the electron capacity of 4f, not its number of orbitals; read whether the question asks for orbitals or electrons.

**4.** An s orbital is spherical, centred on the nucleus [1]. Each p orbital is shaped like a dumbbell: two lobes, one either side of the nucleus [1]. The three p orbitals lie along the x, y and z axes, at right angles to each other [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* "Circle" and "figure of eight" describe flat shapes; use "spherical" and "dumbbell", and state the 90° orientation for the third point.

**5. (a)** **1s² 2s² 2p⁶ 3s² 3p⁶ 3d¹⁰ 4s²** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **[Ar] 3d¹⁰** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The two electrons lost come from 4s, which is higher in energy than 3d once 3d is occupied, so 3d keeps all ten electrons [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* For (a) the question asks for the full configuration, so a noble-gas core is not what was asked; always match the form the command requests.

**6. (a)**

```
4s  [↑ ]
3d  [↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ]
```

Five singly occupied 3d orbitals and one 4s electron, all with parallel spins [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** **Six** [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** The 3d and 4s sublevels are very close in energy [1]. Moving one electron from 4s to 3d gives a half-filled 3d⁵ set, which has lower overall energy than 3d⁴ 4s² [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* For (c), "half-filled is more stable" earns more when you also say the 3d and 4s energies are close; the closeness is what lets the electron move.

**7. (a)** −52.5 − (−145.8) = **93.3 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]; the drop ends on n = 3, so the line is in the **infrared** [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** **n = 2 → 1** [1]. It releases the most energy (984 kJ mol⁻¹, compared with 291.6 and 63.8), so it has the highest frequency and shortest wavelength [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The gaps between neighbouring levels shrink as n increases: n = 4 to 5 is 29.5 kJ mol⁻¹ but n = 5 to 6 is only 16.1 kJ mol⁻¹ [1]. Lines from successive higher levels therefore differ less and less in energy, so they crowd together towards the high-frequency end [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** Only specific frequencies appear, so only specific energy gaps (and levels) can exist [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In (c), quote two gap values from the data when the question says "use the data"; a purely descriptive answer does not show the data was used.

**8.** λ = 286 × 10⁻⁹ m [1]
E = hc/λ = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ × 3.00 × 10⁸) ÷ (286 × 10⁻⁹) = 6.955 × 10⁻¹⁹ J per atom [1]
× L: 6.955 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 6.02 × 10²³ = 4.19 × 10⁵ J mol⁻¹ [1]
First ionization energy = **419 kJ mol⁻¹** [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the per-atom energy before multiplying by L, and keep an extra figure in it; rounding to 6.95 × 10⁻¹⁹ J first gives 418 kJ mol⁻¹, and a visible intermediate step is the only place your method can be seen.

**9. (a)** First IE decreases from F to I [1]. Going down, the outer electron sits in a higher main energy level, at greater distance and with more inner electrons screening it, so it is attracted less strongly despite the larger nuclear charge [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Gallium's outer electron is in 4p, while zinc's is in 4s [1]. The 4p sublevel is higher in energy (and partly screened by the 4s pair), so gallium's electron is easier to take away [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** Arsenic has three unpaired 4p electrons; selenium has four 4p electrons, so one 4p orbital holds a pair [1]. Repulsion between the paired electrons makes one of them easier to remove [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Name the sublevels involved (4s, 4p) in (b) and (c); a general answer about "more shielding" or "further away" does not explain a drop within one period.

**10. (a)** After each removal the ion carries more positive charge, so the electrons left behind are attracted more strongly [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** The largest increase is between the sixth and seventh values (8496 to 27 107) [1], so six electrons are in the outer level and the element is in **group 16** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)** The **second** main energy level (n = 2) [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare increases, not ratios alone: here the 6th-to-7th increase (18 611) is far larger than any other, and naming the two values that bracket the jump shows your reasoning.

**11. (a)** **[Ar] 3d⁷ 4s²** [1]. **[1]**
**(b)** Co²⁺: **[Ar] 3d⁷** [1]. Co³⁺: **[Ar] 3d⁶** [1]. **[2]**
**(c)**

```
3d  [↑↓][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ][↑ ]
```

One paired and four singly occupied orbitals, following Hund's rule [1]; **four** unpaired electrons [1]. **[2]**
**(d)** 3s² 3p⁶ 3d⁷ gives **15** electrons [1]; the maximum is 2 × 3² = **18** [1]. **[2]**
**(e)** **Co** only, because both ions have lost their 4s electrons [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* In (b), writing [Ar] 3d⁵ 4s² for Co²⁺ is a common slip; check the electron total (25) and that 4s is emptied first.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Taking 3d electrons before 4s electrons when writing transition-element ions.
- Writing the predicted Cr or Cu configuration instead of the real one.
- Drawing paired electrons with the same spin, or pairing before all equal-energy orbitals hold one electron.
- Giving the number of electrons when the number of orbitals was asked for.
- Placing hydrogen lines for drops to n = 3 in the visible region.
- Saying lines converge at low frequency or long wavelength.
- (HL) Substituting nm into E = hc/λ, or stopping at the energy of one atom.
- (HL) Explaining the group 15 to 16 drop without paired-electron repulsion.
- (HL) Counting the group from the position of the largest value instead of the electrons removed before the jump.
