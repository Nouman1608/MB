**These notes condense content area 3.1.2.5 of 9237: "The role, importance and wisdom of giving Zakah and Ushr, including how why Zakah and Ushr are given" and "The criteria for giving (Nisaab) and receiving Zakah."** The full explanation is in the [Zakah study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-islamiyat-9237-zakah/).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Zakah | The third Pillar; compulsory yearly giving from qualifying wealth; means purification and growth |
| Ushr | "A tenth": Zakah on farm produce, paid at harvest |
| Sadaqah | Voluntary charity at any time, any amount |
| Nisaab | The minimum wealth a Muslim must hold before Zakah is due |
| Hawl | One full lunar year of holding the wealth |
| Amanah | A trust: wealth is held for Allah |
| Fuqara / Masakin | The poor / the needy |
| Taqwa | Awareness of Allah |

## The big idea

Zakah is **worship through money**. It is a duty from Allah, it purifies the giver, and it looks after the poor. The Qur'an pairs it with prayer: "establish prayer and give Zakah" (2:43).

## Why Zakah is given

- **Obedience:** it is a Pillar and "an obligation [imposed] by Allah" (9:60).
- **Wealth as a trust:** everything belongs to Allah; the rich are managers.
- **Purification:** "take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them" (9:103).
- **Community care:** wealth must not circulate only among the rich (59:7).
- **Early example:** Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA) insisted that tribes who stopped paying must pay.

## Four kinds of wisdom

| Kind | Key point |
|---|---|
| Spiritual | Removes greed; builds gratitude and taqwa |
| Moral | Builds generosity, compassion and humility |
| Social | Narrows the gap between rich and poor; builds unity |
| Economic | Keeps wealth in use; supports those without income |

## Who must pay (the Nisaab and its conditions)

1. A Muslim adult of sound mind.
2. Wealth at or above the **Nisaab** (the value of about 87.5 g of gold or 612 g of silver; cash value changes).
3. Held for one full lunar year (**hawl**).
4. Surplus to basic needs, after debts that are due.

**Rate:** 2.5 per cent (one-fortieth) of qualifying wealth.

| Counts | Does not count |
|---|---|
| Savings, cash in the bank | The home you live in |
| Gold and silver | Personal clothes, furniture |
| Business stock and profit | A car for daily use |
| Investments (in the main) | Debts that are owed to others (taken off first) |

## Ushr

| Type of watering | Rate |
|---|---|
| Rain, springs, natural moisture | 10 per cent (a tenth) |
| Costly irrigation (pump, carried water) | 5 per cent (half of a tenth) |

**Why two rates?** The more effort and cost the farmer meets, the less is due. Paid at harvest, not yearly.

## Who may receive: the eight groups in Qur'an 9:60

1. The poor
2. The needy
3. Those employed to collect Zakah
4. Bringing hearts together
5. Freeing captives or slaves
6. Those in debt
7. The cause of Allah
8. The stranded traveller

**Not normally eligible:** someone rich enough to pay Zakah; the giver's own dependent parents, spouse or children; members of the Prophet's family (most scholars).

## Using the passage, Qur'an 9:58-60

| Words | Use for |
|---|---|
| "for the poor and for the needy" | Social justice, helping those in need |
| "those employed to collect" | Zakah is organised and honest, not careless |
| "for those in debt" | Compassion; freedom from burden |
| "an obligation [imposed] by Allah" | Zakah is a duty, not an option |
| "Knowing and Wise" | Wisdom behind the system |
| Complaint about the distribution (verse 58) | Zakah must be given for the right reasons, not to please people |

## Quick calculation method

1. Add up qualifying wealth.
2. Subtract debts due now.
3. Compare with the Nisaab.
4. If above, multiply by 0.025.

**Example:** wealth 9,500 minus debt 500 = 9,000; 9,000 x 0.025 = 225.
**Ushr example:** 2,000 kg of rain-fed crop x 0.10 = 200 kg; 2,000 kg of pump-irrigated crop x 0.05 = 100 kg.

## Exam traps

- Zakah is **not** the same as sadaqah.
- It is not paid on the home you live in or on everyday possessions.
- Rain-fed means **10 per cent**; effort-irrigated means **5 per cent**.
- Remember the **receiving** criteria as well as the giving criteria.
- A 4-mark "explain two benefits to the giver" question must be about the giver, not the receiver.
- Do not state a single fixed money value for the Nisaab unless the question gives one.

## Self-test

1. What does the word Zakah mean in Arabic?
2. What is the rate of Zakah on savings?
3. What is the Nisaab?
4. How long must wealth be held before Zakah is due?
5. What is Ushr?
6. Give the two rates of Ushr and say when each applies.
7. Name four of the eight groups in Qur'an 9:60.
8. Give one reason why a person may not receive Zakah.
9. Give one spiritual and one social benefit of Zakah.
10. Which companion insisted that tribes pay Zakah after the Prophet's death?

**Answers:** 1. Purification and growth. 2. 2.5 per cent. 3. The minimum amount of wealth (valued by gold or silver) that must be held before Zakah is due. 4. One full lunar year. 5. Zakah on crops, a tenth. 6. 10 per cent for rain-fed or naturally watered crops; 5 per cent for crops that need costly irrigation. 7. Any four of: the poor, the needy, collectors, bringing hearts together, freeing captives, those in debt, the cause of Allah, the stranded traveller. 8. Any one: they are rich enough to pay Zakah; the giver supports them already as dependants; they are members of the Prophet's family. 9. Spiritual: purifies the giver and removes greed. Social: reduces the gap between rich and poor. 10. Hazrat Abu Bakr (RA).
