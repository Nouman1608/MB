The specification says this question requires "an extended response with a line of reasoning" that explains a given factor "and at least [one other]... leading to a judgement that evaluates their relative importance".

## Section A: planning tasks

**1.** *The origins of the First World War.* For the statement "*Weltpolitik* was the main reason for tension between Britain and Germany before 1914", name three other factors you could explain. **[3]**

**2.** *Any option.* A student's answer explains the given factor and one other factor in two separate, well-supported paragraphs, then ends: "Both factors were important." State what the answer needs to reach the highest level, and give an example of a sentence that would do it. **[2]**

**3.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* Give three pieces of precise evidence you could use to explain the importance of the Nazi-Soviet Pact in an essay on the outbreak of war in 1939. **[3]**

## Section B: 12-mark questions

**4.** *The origins of the First World War.* "The naval race was the main reason for tension between Britain and Germany before 1914." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**5.** *The origins of the First World War.* "Rivalries in the Balkans were the main reason for increasing tension between the Great Powers in the years 1908-13." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**6.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* "The Munich Agreement of 1938 was a success for Britain." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**7.** *Hitler's foreign policy.* "Germany's invasion of the USSR was the main reason a European war became a global conflict by the end of 1941." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**8.** *The origins and development of the Cold War.* "The Berlin Blockade was the main reason for increased tension between the superpowers in the years 1945-49." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**9.** *The origins and development of the Cold War.* "The Korean War was the most important event in Asia for superpower relations in the years 1949-60." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**10.** *Détente and the collapse of communism.* "The cost of the arms race was the main reason for détente in the early 1970s." How far do you agree? **[12]**

**11.** *Détente and the collapse of communism.* "Reagan's policies were the main reason the Cold War was renewed in the early 1980s." How far do you agree? **[12]**

## Answers

**How these guides work.** Planning tasks: [1] per relevant point. 12-mark guides: the given factor explained with precise evidence [3]; at least one other factor explained [4]; comparison of the factors in a line of reasoning [3]; a supported judgement [2].

**1.** Any three, [1] each: the naval race after HMS *Dreadnought* (1906); colonial rivalry; the Moroccan Crises (1905, 1911); Britain's ententes with France (1904) and Russia (1907), which Germany saw as encirclement; industrial and trade competition; the Kaiser's provocative statements.

**2.** It needs to link the two factors and judge their relative importance [1]. Example [1]: "The naval race mattered more than colonial rivalry, because it threatened Britain's own security at home, whereas colonies could be bargained over, as the 1904 Entente showed."

**3.** Any three, [1] each: it was signed on 23 August 1939; it removed the threat of a war on two fronts; a secret protocol divided Poland and eastern Europe between Germany and the USSR; Hitler invaded Poland on 1 September, nine days later; it ended British and French hopes of an alliance with the USSR.

**4.** Given factor [3]: after HMS *Dreadnought* (1906) both powers raced to build the new battleships; Germany's Navy Laws aimed at a fleet that could challenge Britain; British public pressure in 1909 demanded more ships; Britain saw a German navy as a direct threat to its island security. Other factors [4]: *Weltpolitik* and colonial rivalry, with the Kaiser's demand for "a place in the sun"; the Moroccan Crises of 1905 and 1911, in which Germany tried to split Britain and France; the ententes with France and Russia, which Germany saw as encirclement. Comparison [3]: colonial disputes could be settled by agreement, but the naval race touched Britain's survival, so it drew Britain closer to France and Russia; the Moroccan Crises turned suspicion into a sense that Germany was deliberately aggressive. Judgement [2]: for example, the naval race was the main reason because it made Germany Britain's chief potential enemy, while other rivalries added to it.

**5.** Given factor [3]: Austria-Hungary's annexation of Bosnia (1908-09) angered Serbia and humiliated Russia, which backed down when Germany supported Austria; the Balkan Wars (1912-13) doubled Serbia's territory and increased Austria-Hungary's fear of Slav nationalism; the Black Hand was formed in 1911. Other factors [4]: the Agadir Crisis (1911) strengthened the Entente; the naval and military arms races; the alliance system linking each Balkan dispute to the Great Powers. Comparison [3]: the Balkans were the area where the interests of Austria-Hungary and Russia clashed directly, and because the alliances linked them to Germany and France, a Balkan quarrel could involve all the powers; Morocco raised tension but was settled by diplomacy. Judgement [2]: for example, Balkan rivalries were the main reason in these years because, unlike Morocco, they could not be settled, and they produced the crisis of 1914.

**6.** Given factor, the case for success [3]: Chamberlain avoided war in September 1938 and returned claiming "peace for our time"; Britain gained a year to rearm, especially in fighter aircraft and radar; British public opinion and the Dominions were not ready to fight over the Sudetenland. Case against [4]: Czechoslovakia, not invited to the conference, lost its border defences and much of its industry; Hitler occupied the rest of Czechoslovakia in March 1939, showing his promises were worthless; the USSR, excluded from Munich, concluded that Britain and France were unreliable, which helped lead to the Nazi-Soviet Pact; Germany also gained time to rearm. Comparison [3]: whether Munich was a success depends on whether the time gained helped Britain more than Germany; Britain's air defences in 1940 support the success argument, but the loss of Czechoslovakia's army and the alienation of the USSR weigh against it. Judgement [2]: for example, Munich was a short-term success for Britain but a long-term failure, because it encouraged Hitler and cost Britain potential allies.

**7.** Given factor [3]: Germany invaded the USSR on 22 June 1941, opening a vast eastern front and making the war one of the largest conflicts in history; the USSR joined Britain against Germany. Other factors [4]: Japan's attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941 brought the USA into the war, and Germany declared war on the USA on 11 December; Japan was already at war with China, and its attacks on British and Dutch colonies spread the war across Asia and the Pacific; Italy's entry in 1940 spread fighting to North and East Africa. Comparison [3]: the invasion of the USSR widened the war within Europe, but Pearl Harbor and Germany's declaration of war on the USA linked the European and Asian wars into a single global conflict. Judgement [2]: for example, Pearl Harbor was more important for making the war global, because it brought the USA in and joined two separate wars, while the invasion of the USSR made it far larger.

**8.** Given factor [3]: the Western powers introduced a new currency in their zones in 1948; Stalin blockaded West Berlin from June 1948; the Allies supplied it by air until May 1949; the crisis led to the creation of NATO (1949) and two German states. Other factors [4]: ideological differences and disagreements at Yalta and Potsdam; Soviet control of Eastern Europe, including the communist takeover in Czechoslovakia (1948); the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Aid (1947), answered by Cominform and Comecon; the USA's atomic monopoly until the Soviet test of 1949. Comparison [3]: the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Aid divided Europe into rival blocs, and the Blockade was Stalin's response to the West's economic plans for Germany, so it was a result of earlier tension as well as a cause of more. Judgement [2]: for example, the Blockade was the most dangerous crisis and led directly to NATO, but the Truman Doctrine and Marshall Aid were the main reason, because they made the division of Europe permanent.

**9.** Given factor [3]: North Korea invaded the South in June 1950; UN forces led by the USA under MacArthur fought for the South; China intervened in October 1950; the war ended in stalemate with an armistice in 1953; it showed that the USA would fight to contain communism and turned the Cold War into a hot war in Asia. Other factors [4]: the communist victory in China in 1949, supported by the USSR, which doubled the population under communist rule and alarmed the USA; the war against France in Vietnam and the Geneva Agreement of 1954, which divided Vietnam and drew the USA into supporting the South. Comparison [3]: the Chinese revolution made Asia a central Cold War concern and made the Korean War possible, since China's intervention changed the war; Korea, however, was the first direct military clash between communist and Western forces. Judgement [2]: for example, the Chinese revolution was more important because it shaped every later event in Asia, including Korea and Vietnam.

**10.** Given factor [3]: by the late 1960s both superpowers had enough nuclear weapons to destroy each other; new systems such as anti-ballistic missiles threatened another expensive round of competition; both governments wanted to limit spending, leading to SALT I and the ABM Treaty (1972). Other factors [4]: fear of nuclear war after the Cuban Missile Crisis; the USA's wish to leave Vietnam; the USSR's need for Western grain and technology; the Sino-Soviet split, which encouraged both superpowers to improve relations, with Nixon visiting China in 1972; West German *Ostpolitik* under Willy Brandt. Comparison [3]: cost was a shared motive that made agreements on weapons possible, but the Sino-Soviet split changed the balance of power and gave Nixon a way to pressure the USSR. Judgement [2]: for example, cost was the main reason because both sides gained directly, while other factors explain why the early 1970s were the moment détente happened.

**11.** Given factor [3]: Reagan, elected in 1980, increased defence spending, called the USSR an "evil empire" (1983), announced the Strategic Defence Initiative (1983) and deployed new US missiles in Europe from late 1983. Other factors [4]: the Soviet invasion of Afghanistan (December 1979) had already ended détente; Carter's response, including the failure of SALT II and the 1980 Olympic boycott; the earlier deployment of Soviet SS-20 missiles in Europe; the Soviet boycott of the 1984 Olympics. Comparison [3]: Afghanistan caused the break, but Reagan turned it into a sustained new Cold War, so the renewal had two stages, collapse under Carter and escalation under Reagan. Judgement [2]: for example, Reagan's policies were the main reason for the *renewal* of the Cold War, though Afghanistan was the main reason for the end of détente.
