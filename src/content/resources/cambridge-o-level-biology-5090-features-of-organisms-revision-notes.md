These notes condense subtopic **2.2 Features of organisms** of Cambridge O Level Biology 5090, 2026-2028 series: the five kingdoms, the vertebrate and arthropod groups, ferns and flowering plants, classifying organisms from their features, and viruses. Full explanations are in the [2.2 study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-features-of-organisms/). Try the [2.2 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-biology-5090-features-of-organisms-practice/) afterwards.

## The five kingdoms

| Kingdom | Key features |
|---|---|
| Animal | Many cells, nucleus, no cell wall, no chlorophyll, take in food |
| Plant | Many cells, nucleus, cellulose cell wall, chloroplasts with chlorophyll, photosynthesise |
| Fungus | Nucleus, walls not cellulose (chitin), no chlorophyll, hyphae in many; digest food outside the body and absorb it; yeast is single-celled |
| Prokaryote | Single cell, no nucleus, circular DNA, cell wall not cellulose (bacteria) |
| Protoctist | Mostly single-celled, nucleus present (for example *Amoeba*, *Chlorella*) |

**Quick sort:** no nucleus means prokaryote. Many cells and chlorophyll means plant. Many cells and no wall means animal. Chitin wall, no chlorophyll means fungus. Single-celled with a nucleus means protoctist.

## Vertebrate groups

| Group | Skin covering | Eggs or young | Extra feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| Mammals | Hair or fur | Live young, milk from mammary glands | External ears (most) |
| Birds | Feathers | Hard-shelled eggs | Wings, beak, scaly legs |
| Reptiles | Dry scales | Soft, leathery eggs on land | Temperature varies |
| Amphibians | Moist skin | Jelly-covered eggs in water, tadpoles | Adults on land and in water |
| Fish | Wet scales | Eggs in water | Gills and fins |

Mammals and birds keep their body temperature constant. The other three groups have a body temperature that varies with their surroundings.

## Arthropod groups

All arthropods have jointed legs, an exoskeleton and a segmented body.

| Group | Legs | Body parts | Antennae |
|---|---|---|---|
| Myriapods | Many (one or two pairs per segment) | Many similar segments | One pair |
| Insects | Six | Three (head, thorax, abdomen) | One pair; usually wings |
| Arachnids | Eight | Two | None |
| Crustaceans | Five or more pairs | Often two | Two pairs |

## Plant groups

| Feature | Fern | Dicotyledon | Monocotyledon |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reproduces by | Spores | Seeds in flowers | Seeds in flowers |
| Leaves | Fronds | Broad, net veins | Narrow, parallel veins |
| Seed leaves | None (spores) | Two | One |
| Flower parts | None | Fours or fives | Threes |
| Stem bundles | Not required | Ring | Scattered |

## Viruses

- Protein coat plus genetic material (DNA or RNA).
- Not made of cells: no nucleus, cytoplasm or membrane.
- Can only replicate inside living cells, using the host cell's machinery.
- Not placed in any of the five kingdoms.

## Classifying an organism: a method

1. Decide **nucleus or not**. No nucleus: prokaryote.
2. Decide **one cell or many**.
3. Check **wall type**, **chlorophyll** and **how it feeds** to choose animal, plant or fungus.
4. For animals, look for a **backbone**. With one, use covering and eggs to pick the vertebrate group. Without one, use legs and body parts to pick the arthropod group.
5. For plants, ask: spores (fern) or seeds (flowering plant); then count seed leaves or look at leaf veins.

## Pairs that are easy to confuse

| Pair | Main contrast |
|---|---|
| Mammal / bird | Hair or fur and live young against feathers and hard-shelled eggs |
| Reptile / amphibian | Dry scales and leathery eggs on land against moist skin and jelly eggs in water |
| Reptile / fish | Lungs and land eggs against gills, fins and eggs laid in water |
| Insect / arachnid | Six legs, three body parts, antennae against eight legs, two body parts, no antennae |
| Myriapod / crustacean | One pair of antennae and a long segmented body against two pairs of antennae |
| Fern / flowering plant | Spores and fronds against seeds and flowers |
| Bacterium / virus | A cell with a cell wall and DNA loop against a protein coat with no cell structure |

When asked for a difference, always give a feature of **both** groups.

## Short model answers

**State two features of all arthropods.** Jointed legs; a hard exoskeleton (also: segmented body).

**Describe how reptiles differ from amphibians.** Reptiles have dry, scaly skin and lay soft-shelled eggs on land. Amphibians have moist skin and lay jelly-covered eggs in water.

**Explain why a virus is not placed in any kingdom.** It is not made of cells and can only replicate inside a living host cell.

**State one difference between monocotyledons and dicotyledons.** Monocotyledon leaves have parallel veins, while dicotyledon leaves have a branching network of veins.

## Exam traps

- Viruses are not cells and have no nucleus or cytoplasm.
- Bacteria have DNA but no nucleus.
- Insects are only one arthropod group: count the legs.
- Spiders are arachnids, not insects.
- Fungi do not photosynthesise; their walls are not cellulose.
- Amphibian eggs have jelly; reptile eggs have leathery shells.
- Mammals have **hair or fur**; "skin" alone earns nothing.

## Self-test

1. Name the five kingdoms.
2. Which kingdom has no nucleus?
3. State two features of the plant kingdom.
4. What is a hypha?
5. State two features that separate birds from mammals.
6. How many legs do insects and arachnids each have?
7. Name the four arthropod groups.
8. How do ferns reproduce?
9. State two differences between dicotyledons and monocotyledons.
10. What two parts make up a virus?
11. Why can a virus not reproduce on its own?
12. An animal has moist skin and lays jelly-covered eggs in water. Which group is it?

### Answers

1. Animal, plant, fungus, prokaryote, protoctist.
2. Prokaryote.
3. Many-celled; cellulose cell wall; chlorophyll in chloroplasts; photosynthesis (any two).
4. A thread that makes up the body of a many-celled fungus.
5. Birds have feathers and hard-shelled eggs; mammals have hair or fur and give birth to live young fed on milk (any two points).
6. Insects six; arachnids eight.
7. Myriapods, insects, arachnids, crustaceans.
8. By spores.
9. Any two: two seed leaves against one; net veins against parallel veins; flower parts in fours or fives against threes; stem bundles in a ring against scattered.
10. A protein coat and genetic material (DNA or RNA).
11. It has no cell machinery; it must use a host cell to replicate.
12. Amphibian.
