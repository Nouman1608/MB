These notes condense **section 3.3** of IB DP Mathematics: Analysis and Approaches, first assessment 2021: applications of right and non-right-angled trigonometry, including Pythagoras's theorem, angles of elevation and depression, and labelled diagrams from written statements. It is SL content, examined at SL and HL. Worked examples are in the [applications study guide](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-3/); exam-style questions are in the [applications practice set](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-ib-dp-mathematics-analysis-and-approaches-3-3-practice/).

## Condensed recall

**Diagram routine**

1. Read the whole question.
2. Draw fixed objects first: ground, towers, ports.
3. Mark each length and angle where it belongs.
4. Add implied right angles: vertical meets horizontal; north is perpendicular to east.
5. Label the unknown with a letter.
6. Name the triangle you will solve first.

**Elevation and depression**

- Both measured from the horizontal, never from the vertical.
- Elevation looks up; depression looks down.
- Depression from A to B = elevation from B to A (alternate angles between parallel horizontals).
- Height = horizontal distance × tan(elevation).
- Horizontal distance = height ÷ tan(elevation).

**Two observation points**

- Same side, in line with the foot: either two right-angled triangles with h in common, or the sine rule in the large triangle, then h = (side) × sin(angle).
- Opposite sides: distances to the foot add to the gap; h(1/tan α + 1/tan β) = gap.
- Not in line (for example one due south, one due east): two vertical triangles plus a horizontal one, joined by Pythagoras or the cosine rule.

**Bearings**

- Three figures, clockwise from north: 005°, 090°, 247°.
- North line at every point used.
- Back bearing = bearing ± 180°.
- Co-interior angles between north lines add to 180°; alternate angles are equal.
- Interior angle at a turning point: compare the back bearing with the new bearing.

**Method: a two-leg bearings problem**

1. Draw north lines at the start and at the turning point.
2. At the turning point, write the back bearing to the start (bearing ± 180°).
3. The interior angle is the difference between the back bearing and the new bearing (or 360° minus it).
4. Use the cosine rule for the direct distance, then the sine rule for the angle at the start.
5. Add or subtract that angle from the first bearing, checking on the sketch which way it turns.

**Pythagoras's theorem**

- Right-angled triangles: c² = a² + b².
- Converse: if c² = a² + b², the angle opposite c is a right angle.
- Due north then due east legs: distance √(n² + e²); bearing tan⁻¹(e/n) from north.

**Shortest distance**

- From a point to a line is the perpendicular distance: (slant side) × sin(angle at the line).

**Answers in context**

- Include units; 3 significant figures unless told otherwise.
- Bearings to the nearest degree or 0.1°, written with three figures before the point.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Angle of elevation | Angle up from the horizontal to a line of sight |
| Angle of depression | Angle down from the horizontal to a line of sight |
| Bearing | Clockwise angle from north, written with three figures |
| Back bearing | The reverse direction: original bearing ± 180° |
| Horizontal distance | Distance measured along level ground |
| Line of sight | The straight line from the observer to the object |
| Perpendicular distance | The shortest distance from a point to a line |

## Exam traps

- Elevation measured from a vertical line.
- A depression angle placed at the observer's foot instead of at eye level, or not transferred to the triangle.
- Bearing 50° instead of 050°, or measured anticlockwise.
- Missing north line at the second point.
- Giving the slant distance when the horizontal distance is asked.
- Using the rounded height from one triangle in the next.
- Assuming an angle is 90° without a reason from the context.

## Self-test

1. The bearing of A from B is 235°. Find the bearing of B from A.
2. From the top of a 60 m cliff, the angle of depression of a boat is 18°. Find the horizontal distance to the boat.
3. A 6 m ladder reaches 5.2 m up a vertical wall. Find the angle between the ladder and the ground.
4. A ship sails 5 km due north, then 12 km due east. Find its distance and bearing from the start.
5. Q is on a bearing of 070° from P, and R is on a bearing of 160° from Q. Find angle PQR.
6. Find the angle of elevation of the top of a 25 m tree from a point 40 m from its base.
7. A kite string 50 m long makes an angle of 35° with the horizontal. Find the height of the kite.
8. From two points 20 m apart, in line with the foot of a tower and on the same side, the elevations are 45° (nearer) and 30°. Find the exact height.
9. Three towns are 9 km, 40 km and 41 km apart. What can you say about the triangle?
10. What does "the shortest distance from L to the path AB" mean?
11. Why are the angle of depression from A to B and the angle of elevation from B to A equal?
12. A plane flies on a bearing of 300°. Describe the direction in words.

### Answers

1. 235° − 180° = 055°.
2. 60/tan 18° = 185 m.
3. sin⁻¹(5.2/6) = 60.1°.
4. 13 km; tan⁻¹(12/5) = 67.4°, so bearing 067.4°.
5. Back bearing to P is 250°; 250° − 160° = 90°.
6. tan⁻¹(25/40) = 32.0°.
7. 50 sin 35° = 28.7 m.
8. h = x and h = (x + 20)/√3, so x = 20/(√3 − 1) = 10(√3 + 1) m, about 27.3 m.
9. 9² + 40² = 41², so it is right-angled opposite the 41 km side.
10. The perpendicular distance from L to the line AB.
11. They are alternate angles between two parallel horizontal lines.
12. 60° west of north (equivalently 30° north of west).
