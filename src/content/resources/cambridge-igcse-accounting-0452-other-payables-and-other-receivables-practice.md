## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** Which item is included in other payables?

A: insurance paid in advance

B: commission earned but not yet received

C: rent received in advance

D: goods bought on credit

**[1]**

**2.** Wages paid during the year were 24,600. Wages owing were 700 at the start
of the year and 450 at the end. What is the wages charge in the statement of
profit or loss?

A: 24,350

B: 24,850

C: 25,300

D: 25,750

**[1]**

**3.** Explain why a business matches costs and revenues when calculating profit. **[2]**

**4.** At the year end insurance of 420 has been paid in advance. Prepare the
journal entry, without a narrative. **[2]**

**5.** Halsey Motors has a year end of 30 June 2028. On 1 March 2028 it paid
3,000 for business rates for the year to 28 February 2029.

**(a)** Calculate the rates charge for the year to 30 June 2028 from this payment. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the prepayment at 30 June 2028. **[1]**

**6.** On 1 April 2028 Penrose Hardware opened a fixed deposit of 20,000 earning
5% a year. Interest is paid to the business each 31 March. The year end is
31 December 2028. Calculate the accrued income at 31 December 2028 and state
where it is shown in the statement of financial position. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** Vellacott Joinery rents a workshop for 900 a month and has a year end of
31 December. On 1 January 2028 the rent for January 2028 had already been paid.
During 2028 it paid 2,700 on 2 April, 2,700 on 1 July and 2,700 on 3 October.
No other rent was paid in 2028.

**(a)** Prepare the rent payable account for the year ended 31 December 2028,
showing the balance brought down on 1 January 2029. **[5]**

**(b)** Prepare the journal entry needed at 31 December 2028, without a narrative. **[2]**

**(c)** State the effect on profit for the year if the adjustment in (b) were left out. **[1]**

**8.** Daventry Agencies earns commission. On 1 January 2028 commission of 640 was
owed to the business. During 2028 it received 640 on 14 January, 2,150 on
30 April, 1,980 on 31 August and 2,420 on 15 December. The 15 December receipt
included 310 for January 2029.

**(a)** Prepare the commission receivable account for 2028 in three-column
running balance format. **[5]**

**(b)** State how the 310 is shown in the statement of financial position at
31 December 2028. **[1]**

**(c)** Prepare the journal entry for the 310 at 31 December 2028, without a narrative. **[2]**

**9.** Lennox Café has these figures for the year ended 31 December 2028.

| Account | Statement of profit or loss | At 1 January 2028 | At 31 December 2028 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Electricity | 3,780 | 290 owing | 360 owing |
| Insurance | 2,040 | 480 prepaid | 530 prepaid |
| Rent receivable | 6,600 | 550 received in advance | 600 owing to the café |

Calculate the cash **(a)** paid for electricity **[2]**, **(b)** paid for
insurance **[2]** and **(c)** received for rent during 2028. **[2]**

**10.** Ashworth Trading's draft profit for the year is 38,500. These items have
not been recorded: wages accrued 820; insurance prepaid 360; rent receivable
owing to the business 450; commission received in advance 270.

**(a)** Calculate the corrected profit for the year. **[4]**

**(b)** Calculate the totals of other receivables and other payables. **[2]**

**11.** A bookkeeper recorded accrued electricity of 430 as a prepayment.

**(a)** State and explain the effect on profit for the year. **[2]**

**(b)** State the effect on current assets and on current liabilities. **[2]**

**12.** Priya runs a gift shop with a year end of 31 December. On 1 October 2028
she paid 6,000 for a twelve-month advertising contract. She says: "I paid it this
year, so all 6,000 is this year's expense."

**(a)** Calculate the correct advertising charge for 2028. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why Priya's treatment is wrong, referring to the effect on the
profit of 2028 and of 2029. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** C [1]. Income received in advance is owed back to the customer as a
service, so it is a current liability under other payables. A and B are other
receivables; D is a trade payable. **[1]**

**2.** A [1]. 24,600 + 450 − 700 = 24,350. **[1]**

**3.** Profit should compare the revenue earned in a period with the costs
incurred to earn it, whatever the date of payment [1]. Otherwise profit is
misstated and results of different years cannot be compared fairly [1]. **[2]**

**4.** Debit Other receivables 420 [1]; credit Insurance 420 [1]. **[2]**

**5. (a)** March to June is 4 months: 3,000 × 4/12 = **1,000** [1].

**(b)** 3,000 − 1,000 = **2,000** (July 2028 to February 2029) [1]. **[2]**

**6.** 20,000 × 5% × 9/12 = **750** [1], shown under current assets as other
receivables [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)**

```
Rent payable account ($)
Date      Details                Amount  Date      Details                Amount
1 Jan 28  Balance b/d               900  31 Dec 28 Profit or loss         10,800
2 Apr 28  Bank                    2,700
1 Jul 28  Bank                    2,700
3 Oct 28  Bank                    2,700
31 Dec 28 Balance c/d             1,800
                                 ------                                   ------
                                 10,800                                   10,800
                                 ======                                   ======
                                         1 Jan 29  Balance b/d             1,800
```

Opening debit balance of 900 [1]; three bank entries [1]; balance c/d of 1,800
(November and December) on the debit side [1]; transfer of 10,800 (12 × 900)
to profit or loss [1]; balance b/d of 1,800 on the credit side on 1 January 2029 [1].

**(b)** Debit Rent payable 1,800 [1]; credit Other payables 1,800 [1].

**(c)** Profit would be overstated by 1,800 [1]. **[8]**

**8. (a)**

```
Commission receivable account ($)
Date       Details                     Debit    Credit    Balance
1 Jan 28   Balance b/d                   640                640 Dr
14 Jan 28  Bank                                    640        nil
30 Apr 28  Bank                                  2,150    2,150 Cr
31 Aug 28  Bank                                  1,980    4,130 Cr
15 Dec 28  Bank                                  2,420    6,550 Cr
31 Dec 28  Balance c/d (in advance)      310              6,240 Cr
31 Dec 28  Profit or loss              6,240                  nil
1 Jan 29   Balance b/d                             310      310 Cr
```

Opening debit balance 640 [1]; four receipts on the credit side [1]; 310
entered on the debit side as balance c/d [1]; transfer of 6,240 to profit or
loss (7,190 − 640 − 310) [1]; balance b/d 310 Cr on 1 January 2029, with each
running balance correctly marked Dr or Cr [1].

**(b)** Under current liabilities as other payables, 310 [1].

**(c)** Debit Commission receivable 310 [1]; credit Other payables 310 [1]. **[8]**

**9. (a)** Paid = 3,780 + 290 − 360 [1] = **3,710** [1].

**(b)** Paid = 2,040 − 480 + 530 [1] = **2,090** [1].

**(c)** Received = 6,600 − 550 − 600 [1] = **5,450** [1]. The opening 550 was
cash received in 2027, and the 600 owing has not yet been received. **[6]**

**10. (a)** 38,500 − 820 [1] + 360 [1] + 450 [1] − 270 [1] = **38,220**.

**(b)** Other receivables 360 + 450 = **810** [1]; other payables
820 + 270 = **1,090** [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** Profit is overstated by **860** [1], because 430 was deducted from
the electricity expense when it should have been added, so the error is twice the
accrual [1].

**(b)** Current assets are overstated by 430 [1] and current liabilities are
understated by 430 [1]. **[4]**

**12. (a)** October to December is 3 months: 6,000 × 3/12 = **1,500** [1].

**(b)** The matching / accruals concept charges an expense to the period that
benefits from it, not the period in which it is paid [1]. Nine months (4,500)
of the contract benefit 2029, so they are a prepaid expense [1]. Priya's method
would understate 2028 profit by 4,500 [1] and overstate 2029 profit by 4,500,
because 2029 would carry no advertising charge for January to September [1]. **[5]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Counting months from the payment date rather than from the start of the
  period paid for.
- Bringing an accrual or prepayment down on the wrong side.
- Forgetting the opening balance when finding a cash figure.
- Stating "profit is affected" without a direction and an amount.
- Mixing other receivables with trade receivables.
