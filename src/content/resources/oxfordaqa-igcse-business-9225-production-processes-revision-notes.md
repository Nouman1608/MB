Condensed recall for section 3.3.1 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Business (9225). For full explanations and worked examples, use the [study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-business-9225-production-processes/). Figures are invented.

## What the specification asks

- Be familiar with **job, batch, flow and cell production** and know **when each is appropriate**.
- Consider how production might be made more efficient by **just in time (JIT)** stock control and **continuous improvement (Kaizen)**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Production | Turning inputs (materials, labour, machines) into outputs (goods or services) |
| Job production | One-off item made to a customer's order |
| Batch production | A group of identical items made together, then the process switches |
| Flow production | Continuous production on a line of standard products |
| Cell production | Small teams, each making a complete part or product |
| Efficiency | Getting the most output from the resources used, with less waste |
| JIT | Materials arrive just as needed; very little stock is held |
| Kaizen | Continuous improvement through many small changes by all employees |
| Productivity | Output per worker (output / number of workers) |

## The four methods compared

| | Job | Batch | Flow | Cell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Quantity | One | Groups | Very large | Medium |
| Variety | Unique | Several similar products | Standard | Moderate |
| Cost per item | Highest | Medium | Lowest | Medium |
| Flexibility | Very high | Fairly high | Low | Fairly high |
| Skill needed | High | Medium | Often low | Multi-skilled |
| Example | Wedding dress | Bakery | Soft drinks | Computer assembly |

## Advantages and drawbacks

| Method | Advantages | Drawbacks |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Exactly what the customer wants; high price; motivated skilled staff | Slow; expensive; hard to plan |
| Batch | Flexible; cheaper than job; responds to demand | Resetting wastes time; storage of part-finished goods |
| Flow | Lowest cost per unit; fast; consistent quality | Expensive to set up; inflexible; repetitive work; one breakdown stops the line |
| Cell | Varied work raises motivation; team owns quality; flexible | Training costs; coordination between cells; duplicated equipment |

## Choosing the method: clues in the case

- "Made to order", "tailor-made", "one-off": **job**.
- "Different sizes or flavours made in turn": **batch**.
- "Thousands of identical units a day", "production line": **flow**.
- "Small teams responsible for a complete product": **cell**.

## Just in time (JIT)

- Stock arrives when needed, so storage costs fall and money is not tied up.
- Less risk of damage, waste or stock becoming out of date.
- Needs reliable suppliers and frequent deliveries; a late delivery can stop production.
- Lost bulk-buying discounts may increase purchase costs.

Example: holding 3,000 parts at $2 each a month costs $6,000; with JIT, 500 parts cost $1,000, saving $5,000 before extra delivery costs.

## Kaizen

- Staff at all levels suggest **small improvements** every day.
- Reduces waste, speeds production and improves quality.
- Involves workers, which can raise motivation.
- Needs training and time, and results build up slowly.

Example: saving 6 minutes per bike on 300 bikes a day saves 1,800 minutes, or 30 hours, a day.

## Calculations

- Productivity = output / number of workers. 300 bikes / 25 workers = 12 bikes per worker.
- Cost per unit = total cost / output. $18,000 / 300 = $60.
- Batches in a shift = shift time / time per batch (round down). 480 / 90 = 5 batches.

## Exam traps

- Batch is not flow. Batch stops and changes; flow is continuous.
- Job production is rarely cheap per item.
- JIT means low stock, not zero stock, and it depends on reliable suppliers.
- Kaizen is many small changes made by all staff, not one big change by management.
- Link the method to the business's product, volume and customers.
- Do not recommend flow production if customers want variety or the product often changes.

## Model answer outline: a 6-mark "analyse" question

Question style: "Analyse why a bakery might use batch production."

1. **Variety:** the bakery sells rolls, loaves and cakes, so it needs to change what it makes during the day.
2. **Cost:** making a batch of 120 rolls at once is cheaper per item than making one at a time (job production).
3. **Drawback:** ovens and tables must be cleaned between batches; with 30 minutes of cleaning for a 60-minute bake, a third of the time is not producing.
4. **Link to efficiency:** shortening set-up time to 15 minutes would raise output from 600 to 720 rolls a day, showing how efficiency could be improved.

## Revision checklist

- I can define job, batch, flow and cell production.
- I can match each method to a product, quantity and customer.
- I can give two advantages and two disadvantages of each method.
- I can calculate productivity and cost per unit.
- I can explain JIT and give one benefit and one risk.
- I can explain Kaizen and why it needs the involvement of staff.
- I can recommend a production method for a business and justify it.

## Self-test

1. Define job production and give one example.
2. State two advantages of flow production.
3. Why is batch production more flexible than flow production?
4. Explain one drawback of cell production.
5. Name the production method used by a car factory with a moving assembly line.
6. A factory makes 1,800 items with 15 workers. Calculate productivity.
7. A line costs $24,000 a day to run and makes 400 units. Calculate cost per unit.
8. What does JIT stand for?
9. Give one risk of JIT.
10. What does Kaizen mean?
11. Explain why Kaizen can raise motivation.
12. A bakery has a 360-minute shift and each batch takes 80 minutes. How many whole batches can it make?

### Answers

1. One-off item made to a customer's order, for example a custom wedding dress.
2. Any two: low cost per unit; fast, large-scale output; consistent quality; less labour needed.
3. Between batches, the machines and workers can switch to a different product, whereas a flow line makes one standard product.
4. For example, staff need training in several skills, which costs money.
5. Flow production.
6. 1,800 / 15 = **120** items per worker.
7. $24,000 / 400 = **$60** per unit.
8. Just in time.
9. A late or failed delivery can stop production because there is no spare stock.
10. "Change for the better": continuous improvement through small changes.
11. Staff are asked for ideas and see them used, so they feel valued and involved.
12. 360 / 80 = 4.5, so **4** whole batches.
