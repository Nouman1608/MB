This practice set covers syllabus section 1.1 of IB Diploma Programme Mathematics: Applications and Interpretation (first assessment 2021), operations with numbers in the form a × 10ᵏ, where 1 ≤ a < 10 and k ∈ ℤ. It is common content for SL and HL. Every paper in this course allows a GDC, so every question is labelled "(calculator allowed)". Unless told otherwise, give answers exactly or correct to 3 significant figures, and write "in the form a × 10ᵏ" answers with 1 ≤ a < 10 and k ∈ ℤ. Contextual figures marked as invented are for practice only.

Learn the methods first in the [study guide for section 1.1](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-ib-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-1-1/) and the [revision notes for section 1.1](/resources/ib-ib-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-ib-dp-mathematics-applications-and-interpretation-1-1-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** (calculator allowed) Write each number in the form a × 10ᵏ.

**(a)** 0.000 609 **[1]**

**(b)** 18 400 000 000 **[1]**

**2.** (calculator allowed)

**(a)** Write 375 × 10⁴ in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 4.06 × 10⁻³ as an ordinary number. **[1]**

**3.** (calculator allowed) Calculate (8.4 × 10⁷) ÷ (1.2 × 10⁻³), giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator allowed) Calculate 3.75 × 10⁹ + 6.4 × 10⁸, giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**5.** (calculator allowed) A GDC shows the result of a calculation as 1.274639E8.

**(a)** Write down this value correct to 3 significant figures in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why "1.27E8" would not be accepted as a final answer. **[1]**

**6.** (calculator allowed) Write these numbers in order of size, smallest first:

3.1 × 10⁻², 0.0029, 2.95 × 10⁻², 3 × 10⁻³ **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator allowed) The mass of the Earth is 5.97 × 10²⁴ kg and the mass of the Moon is 7.35 × 10²² kg.

**(a)** Find how many times greater the mass of the Earth is than the mass of the Moon. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the combined mass of the Earth and the Moon, giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator allowed) A particle has a diameter taken as 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ m. One nanometre (nm) is 10⁻⁹ m.

**(a)** Write the diameter in nanometres. **[2]**

**(b)** Find how many of these particles, placed touching in a straight line, would fit along 3 mm. Give your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator allowed) A company's revenue for a year of 365 days is 4.68 × 10⁹ dollars (invented figure).

**(a)** Find the mean revenue per day, giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the mean revenue per second, giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

**10.** (calculator allowed) Light travels at 3.00 × 10⁸ m s⁻¹. A light-year is the distance light travels in 365.25 days.

**(a)** Show that a light-year is approximately 9.47 × 10¹⁵ m. **[3]**

**(b)** A star is 4.0 × 10¹⁶ m from the Earth (invented figure). Find this distance in light-years. **[2]**

**11.** (calculator allowed) A file of 7.2 × 10⁹ bits is sent over a network at 4.5 × 10⁷ bits per second.

**(a)** Find the time taken, in seconds. **[2]**

**(b)** A second network is 30 times faster. Find the time it would take, in seconds. **[1]**

**12.** (calculator allowed) Country P (invented figures) has a population of 1.43 × 10⁸ and an area of 1.71 × 10⁷ km².

**(a)** Find the population density of country P, in people per km². **[2]**

**(b)** Country Q has a population density of 4.6 × 10² people per km² and an area of 3.3 × 10⁵ km². Find the population of country Q, giving your answer in the form a × 10ᵏ. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **6.09 × 10⁻⁴** [1]

**(b)** **1.84 × 10¹⁰** [1]

*Examiner insight:* count the places the point moves; a common slip in (b) is 1.84 × 10⁹, one place short.

**2. (a)** 375 = 3.75 × 10², so 3.75 × 10² × 10⁴ = **3.75 × 10⁶** [1]

**(b)** **0.004 06** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (a), making a smaller by 100 means making k larger by 2.

**3.** (8.4 ÷ 1.2) × 10⁷⁻⁽⁻³⁾ = 7 × 10¹⁰ [1]; **7 × 10¹⁰** in the required form [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* subtracting a negative power is the usual error; 7 × 10⁴ loses both marks.

**4.** 6.4 × 10⁸ = 0.64 × 10⁹ [1]; 3.75 + 0.64 = 4.39, so **4.39 × 10⁹** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* the powers must match before the a-values are added; the answer is exact, so no rounding is needed.

**5. (a)** **1.27 × 10⁸** [1]

**(b)** The IB does not accept calculator or computer notation; the answer must be written with "× 10⁸" [1]

**6.** In order: 0.0029 (= 2.9 × 10⁻³), 3 × 10⁻³, 2.95 × 10⁻², 3.1 × 10⁻² [1] for the two powers of −3 in the correct order, [1] for the two powers of −2 in the correct order **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* write each number in the same form first; then compare k, then a. Give the answer in the original forms.

**7. (a)** (5.97 × 10²⁴) ÷ (7.35 × 10²²) [1] = 81.224… ≈ **81.2** times [1]

**(b)** 5.97 × 10²⁴ + 0.0735 × 10²⁴ [1] = 6.0435 × 10²⁴ ≈ **6.04 × 10²⁴ kg** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b), adding 5.97 and 7.35 to get 13.32 × 10⁴⁶ shows the powers were added; no marks are available for that method.

**8. (a)** (1.2 × 10⁻⁷) ÷ 10⁻⁹ [1] = **120 nm** [1]

**(b)** 3 mm = 3 × 10⁻³ m [1]; (3 × 10⁻³) ÷ (1.2 × 10⁻⁷) = **2.5 × 10⁴** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the unit conversion earns its own mark; dividing 3 by 1.2 × 10⁻⁷ gives an answer 1000 times too large.

**9. (a)** (4.68 × 10⁹) ÷ 365 [1] = 12 821 917.8… ≈ **1.28 × 10⁷ dollars** [1]

**(b)** (4.68 × 10⁹) ÷ (365 × 24 × 60 × 60) [1] = 148.40… ≈ **1.48 × 10² dollars** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in (b), divide the unrounded daily figure (or the original total) by 86 400; starting from 1.28 × 10⁷ gives 148.1…, which rounds to 1.48 × 10² here but can lose accuracy in other questions.

**10. (a)** Seconds in a year: 365.25 × 24 × 60 × 60 = 31 557 600 [1]; distance = 3.00 × 10⁸ × 31 557 600 [1] = 9.46728 × 10¹⁵ ≈ 9.47 × 10¹⁵ m [1]

**(b)** (4.0 × 10¹⁶) ÷ (9.46728 × 10¹⁵) [1] = 4.225… ≈ **4.23 light-years** [1]

*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question, the unrounded value 9.46728 × 10¹⁵ must appear before the rounded one.

**11. (a)** (7.2 × 10⁹) ÷ (4.5 × 10⁷) [1] = **160 s** [1]

**(b)** 160 ÷ 30 = **5.33 s** (3 s.f.) [1]

**12. (a)** (1.43 × 10⁸) ÷ (1.71 × 10⁷) [1] = 8.3625… ≈ **8.36 people per km²** [1]

**(b)** 4.6 × 10² × 3.3 × 10⁵ [1] = 15.18 × 10⁷ = 1.518 × 10⁸ ≈ **1.52 × 10⁸** [1]

*Examiner insight:* the answer to (a) is a sensible ordinary number, so there is no need to write it as 8.36 × 10⁰ unless asked.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing a correct value in the wrong form (15.18 × 10⁷ instead of 1.52 × 10⁸).
- Copying GDC notation such as 1.27E8.
- Adding powers of 10 when adding numbers (Questions 4 and 7(b)).
- Dividing by a power of 10 typed without brackets on the GDC.
- Forgetting to convert mm or km to metres before dividing.
- Giving an answer to 2 significant figures where 3 are expected.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I have to write every answer in the form a × 10ᵏ?**
Only when the question asks for it or when the number is very large or very small. Otherwise an ordinary number, exact or to 3 significant figures, is fine.

**Is working needed if the GDC does the calculation?**
Yes. Write the expression you evaluated. If the final value is wrong, a correct expression can still earn the method mark.
