This set practises section 3.6.4 Molar concentrations (statements a to d) of the OxfordAQA International GCSE Chemistry (9202) specification, Version 6.2, for exams May/June 2018 onwards, including the titration required practical. Section A is single-step; Section B covers titration data, method and gas volumes. The course is untiered.

Use these values: Mᵣ HCl = 36.5, NaOH = 40, H₂SO₄ = 98; Aᵣ Mg = 24. One mole of any gas occupies 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (rtp).

Links: [molar concentrations study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-molar-concentrations/) · [molar concentrations revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-igcse-chemistry-9202-molar-concentrations-revision-notes/)

## Section A

**1.** Convert 250 cm³ into dm³. **[1]**

**2.** 0.0200 mol of potassium hydroxide is dissolved to make 200 cm³ of solution. Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the mass of sodium hydroxide needed to make 500 cm³ of a 0.200 mol/dm³ solution. **[2]**

**4.** Convert 0.500 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid into g/dm³. **[1]**

**5.** Calculate the volume, in dm³, occupied by 0.0500 mol of oxygen gas at rtp. **[1]**

**6.** Explain why only concordant titres are used to calculate a mean titre. **[1]**

## Section B

**7.** A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of 0.0800 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide solution with hydrochloric acid of unknown concentration.

| Titration | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titre / cm³ | 21.40 | 20.85 | 21.25 | 20.95 | 20.90 |

NaOH + HCl → NaCl + H₂O

**(a)** Identify the concordant titres and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the number of moles of sodium hydroxide used. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the concentration of the hydrochloric acid in mol/dm³. **[2]**

**(d)** Convert your answer to (c) into g/dm³. **[1]**

**8.** 25.0 cm³ of 0.200 mol/dm³ sodium hydroxide is exactly neutralised by 21.60 cm³ of sulfuric acid.

H₂SO₄ + 2NaOH → Na₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of sulfuric acid that reacted. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in mol/dm³, to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the concentration of the sulfuric acid in g/dm³. **[1]**

**9.** Describe how a student would carry out a titration to find the volume of nitric acid that exactly neutralises 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution. **[6]**

**10.** Explain the effect of each of these errors on the measured titre.

**(a)** The burette is rinsed with distilled water but not with the acid before filling. **[2]**

**(b)** The student adds acid quickly near the end-point and overshoots it. **[1]**

**(c)** Universal indicator is used instead of a single indicator such as methyl orange. **[1]**

**11.** Excess calcium carbonate is added to 50.0 cm³ of 0.400 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid.

CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of hydrochloric acid. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide, in cm³, formed at rtp. **[2]**

**12.** Propane burns in oxygen: C₃H₈(g) + 5O₂(g) → 3CO₂(g) + 4H₂O(l)

**(a)** Calculate the volume of oxygen needed to burn 20 cm³ of propane completely, measured at the same temperature and pressure. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide formed. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why the volume of water formed cannot be found the same way. **[1]**

**13.** A student wants to collect 120 cm³ of hydrogen at rtp by reacting magnesium with excess dilute hydrochloric acid.

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂

**(a)** Calculate the number of moles of hydrogen in 120 cm³. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of magnesium the student should use. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** 0.250 dm³ [1].

**2.** 200 cm³ = 0.200 dm³ [1]; 0.0200 ÷ 0.200 = **0.100 mol/dm³** [1].

**3.** Moles = 0.200 × 0.500 = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 40 = **4.00 g** [1].

**4.** 0.500 × 98 = **49.0 g/dm³** [1].

**5.** 0.0500 × 24 = **1.20 dm³** [1].

**6.** Results that do not agree contain errors (such as overshooting), so leaving them out gives a more accurate mean [1].

**7. (a)** Concordant: 20.85, 20.95 and 20.90 cm³ [1]; mean = **20.90 cm³** [1].

**(b)** 0.0800 × 0.0250 = **0.00200 mol** [1].

**(c)** 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.00200 mol HCl [1]; 0.00200 ÷ 0.02090 = **0.0957 mol/dm³** [1].

**(d)** 0.0957 × 36.5 = **3.49 g/dm³** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Titration 2 (21.25 cm³) is not concordant with the others; including it gives a mean of 20.99 cm³ and loses the first mark.

**8. (a)** Moles NaOH = 0.200 × 0.0250 = 0.00500 mol [1]; moles H₂SO₄ = 0.00500 ÷ 2 = **0.00250 mol** [1].

**(b)** 0.00250 ÷ 0.02160 = **0.116 mol/dm³** [1].

**(c)** 0.1157 × 98 = **11.3 g/dm³** [1].

**9.** Any six points, one mark each:

- Use a pipette (and filler) to measure 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide into a conical flask.
- Add a few drops of a suitable indicator, such as methyl orange or phenolphthalein.
- Fill a burette with nitric acid and record the starting reading.
- Add the acid while swirling the flask, with the flask on a white tile.
- Add acid drop by drop near the end-point.
- Stop when the indicator just changes colour permanently, and record the final reading.
- Repeat until concordant titres (within 0.10 cm³) are obtained and calculate the mean.

**10. (a)** Water dilutes the acid [1], so more acid is needed and the titre is larger than it should be [1].

**(b)** The titre is too large (excess acid added past the end-point) [1].

**(c)** The colour changes gradually, so the end-point is hard to judge and the titre is unreliable [1].

**11. (a)** 0.400 × 0.0500 = **0.0200 mol** [1].

**(b)** Ratio HCl : CO₂ = 2 : 1, so 0.0100 mol CO₂ [1]; 0.0100 × 24 000 = **240 cm³** [1].

**12. (a)** 5 × 20 = **100 cm³** [1].

**(b)** 3 × 20 = **60 cm³** [1].

**(c)** Water is a liquid at these conditions; the equal-volumes rule applies only to gases [1].

**13. (a)** 120 ÷ 24 000 = **0.00500 mol** [1].

**(b)** Ratio Mg : H₂ = 1 : 1, so 0.00500 mol Mg [1]; 0.00500 × 24 = **0.120 g** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving volumes in cm³ in c = n ÷ V.
- Including the rough or a stray titre in the mean.
- Multiplying instead of dividing by 2 for sulfuric acid with sodium hydroxide.
- Using 24 cm³ instead of 24 dm³ for one mole of gas.
- Method answers that do not name the apparatus or omit repeating to concordance.
