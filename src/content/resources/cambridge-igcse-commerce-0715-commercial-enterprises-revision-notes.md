## Private versus public sector

- **Private sector**: owned by individuals or shareholders; usually aims for profit.
- **Public sector**: owned by government; usually aims to provide a service.

## Ownership comparison

| Form | Owners | Liability | Capital | Control | Key drawback |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sole trader | One person | Unlimited | Owner's savings and loans | Full control | Limited capital; all risk on one person |
| Partnership | Two or more partners | Unlimited (ordinary partnership) | Partners' contributions | Shared, set by partnership agreement | Disagreements; bound by partners' decisions |
| Cooperative | Members | Usually limited, depending on how it is registered | Members' contributions | One member, one vote | Slow decisions; limited capital |
| Private limited company | Shareholders, shares not offered to the public | Limited | Sale of shares to family, friends, chosen investors | Shareholders, often family | Registration cost; some information public |
| Public limited company | Shareholders, shares sold to the public | Limited | Large: shares on a stock exchange | Board of directors; owners separate from managers | Costly to set up; must publish accounts; risk of takeover |
| Public corporation | Government | Government responsible | Government funds and charges to users | Board appointed by government | May be inefficient without the profit motive; cost to taxpayers |

**Limited liability**: owners can lose only the money they invested. **Unlimited liability**: owners' personal possessions can be taken to pay business debts.

## Reasons for public corporations

Essential services for all; avoiding wasteful duplication (rail, water networks); services in loss-making areas; strategic or security control; protecting jobs.

## Multinational companies

**Meaning**: headquarters in one country, operations in at least one other.

**Why they exist**: new markets; lower costs; nearness to raw materials; avoiding trade barriers; spreading risk.

| Host country gains | Host country losses |
|---|---|
| Jobs and training | Profits sent home |
| Investment and technology | Local firms squeezed out |
| Exports and tax revenue | Low wages, resource use, pollution |
| Better infrastructure | Influence on government; may leave suddenly |

## Franchises

**Franchisor** owns the brand; **franchisee** buys the right to trade under it, paying a fee and royalties.

- Franchisee gains: proven brand, lower risk, training, national advertising.
- Franchisee loses: fees and royalties, little independence, possible non-renewal.
- Franchisor gains: fast growth with others' capital, fee income.
- Franchisor loses: less control, reputation risk from a poor outlet.

## Location factors

| Physical (9) | Ecommerce (4) |
|---|---|
| Labour, markets, transport, power, raw materials, competition, suppliers, communication, government influence | Wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists, logistics |

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Private sector | Enterprises owned by private individuals or shareholders |
| Public sector | Enterprises owned and controlled by government |
| Unlimited liability | Owner is personally responsible for all business debts |
| Limited liability | Owner can lose only the amount invested |
| Separate legal entity | The company exists in law apart from its owners |
| Partnership agreement | Written terms of a partnership, such as profit shares |
| Cooperative | Enterprise owned and run by its members, one member one vote |
| Shareholder | Part-owner of a company through owning shares |
| Public corporation | Government-owned business run by an appointed board |
| Multinational company | Company with headquarters in one country and operations in others |
| Franchisor | Owner of the brand who sells the right to use it |
| Franchisee | Person or firm that buys the right to trade under the brand |
| Royalty | Continuing payment to the franchisor, often a share of revenue |

## Exam traps

- **Plc versus public corporation**: a plc is private sector; a public corporation is public sector.
- **Partners' liability**: unlimited in an ordinary partnership.
- **Cooperative votes**: one member, one vote, not one share, one vote.
- **Franchise advantages**: always say whose advantage it is.
- **Location answers**: choose factors that fit the scenario, not the whole list.
- **MNC "discuss" questions**: both sides and a conclusion about the named country.

## Self-test

1. What is the main aim of most public sector enterprises?
2. Define unlimited liability.
3. Which form of ownership can sell shares to the general public?
4. How are votes allocated in a cooperative?
5. Give **two** reasons why a government might run a public corporation.
6. Define a multinational company.
7. State **one** reason why an MNC might set up a factory inside a country instead of exporting to it.
8. State **two** negative impacts of an MNC on a host country.
9. What does a franchisee pay to the franchisor?
10. Give **one** disadvantage of a franchise for the franchisor.
11. Name the **four** ecommerce location factors.
12. Which **two** location factors matter most for a quarry company, and why?

**Answers**

1. To provide a service to the public.
2. The owner is personally responsible for all the debts of the business, so personal possessions can be used to pay them.
3. A public limited company.
4. One member, one vote, however much each has invested.
5. Any two: provide essential services to all; avoid wasteful duplication; serve loss-making areas; strategic or security control; protect jobs.
6. A company with its headquarters in one country and operations in at least one other country.
7. To avoid trade barriers such as tariffs, or to be closer to customers and cut transport costs.
8. Any two: profits sent abroad; local firms forced out; low wages or poor conditions; pollution or overuse of resources; influence over government; may leave suddenly.
9. An initial fee and continuing royalties or a share of revenue.
10. Less direct control over outlets, or one poorly run outlet can damage the whole brand.
11. Wi-fi, warehousing, technology specialists and logistics.
12. Raw materials and transport, because stone is heavy and bulky, so it is cheaper to locate at the source with good road or rail links.
