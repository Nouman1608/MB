These notes condense **3.2.3 Motion along a straight line** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Physics (9630) specification, including Required practical 1. It is International AS content, assessed in Unit 1. For full explanations and worked examples, read the [motion along a straight line study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-motion-along-a-straight-line-oxfordaqa/); for exam-style questions, use the [motion along a straight line practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-physics-9630-motion-along-a-straight-line-oxfordaqa-practice/).

## The specification in one box

- Displacement, speed, velocity, acceleration; v = Δs/Δt, a = Δv/Δt.
- Average and instantaneous speeds and velocities.
- Graphs of uniform and non-uniform acceleration, for example a bouncing ball.
- Areas under v–t and a–t graphs; gradients of s–t and v–t graphs.
- The four uniform-acceleration equations; acceleration due to gravity, g.
- Required practical 1: g by a free-fall method, including from a graph of s against t².

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Displacement | Distance in a stated direction from the start; vector |
| Velocity | Rate of change of displacement; vector |
| Speed | Rate of change of distance; scalar |
| Acceleration | Rate of change of velocity; vector |
| Uniform acceleration | Acceleration that is constant in size and direction |
| Instantaneous velocity | Gradient of the tangent to an s–t graph at one instant |
| Average velocity | Total displacement ÷ total time |
| g | Acceleration of free fall, 9.81 m s⁻² near the Earth's surface |

## Graphs at a glance

| Graph | Gradient | Area |
|---|---|---|
| s–t | velocity | — |
| v–t | acceleration | displacement (below axis = negative) |
| a–t | — | change in velocity |

- Straight s–t line: constant velocity. Curving upwards: accelerating.
- Horizontal v–t line: constant velocity. Sloping straight line: uniform acceleration. Curve: non-uniform acceleration.
- Tangent method: draw it long, read two distant points.

## Bouncing ball (upwards positive)

- **v–t:** parallel straight lines of gradient −g; sudden jump from negative to positive at each bounce; each rebound speed smaller than the impact speed before it.
- **s–t:** a run of shrinking arches; gradient zero at each top.
- **a–t:** constant −g in flight; large positive spike at each contact.

## The uniform-acceleration equations

| Equation | Leaves out |
|---|---|
| v = u + at | s |
| s = ½(u + v)t | a |
| s = ut + ½at² | v |
| v² = u² + 2as | t |

Method: write a sign convention; list s, u, v, a, t; choose the equation without the unwanted quantity; substitute with signs.

## Required practical 1

- Electromagnet releases a steel ball; timer starts on release and stops at a trapdoor or light gate.
- Vary drop height s (bottom of ball to trapdoor); repeat each timing.
- Plot **s against t²**: gradient = **g/2**, so g = 2 × gradient.
- Systematic error: magnet release delay makes times too long and g too small; reduce by using larger heights.
- Read the rule at eye level; repeat and average to reduce random error.

## Worked reminders

- **Two-stage.** 5.0 → 25 m s⁻¹ in 8.0 s: a = 20 ÷ 8.0 = 2.5 m s⁻²; s = ½(5.0 + 25) × 8.0 = 120 m.
- **Braking.** From 20 m s⁻¹ at −4.0 m s⁻²: 0 = 20² − 8.0s, so s = 50 m.
- **Thrown up.** u = +9.0 m s⁻¹: time to top 9.0 ÷ 9.81 = 0.92 s; height 9.0² ÷ (2 × 9.81) = 4.1 m.

## Exam traps

- s = +h when the object ends below its start: it should be −h.
- SUVAT used during a bounce or with changing acceleration.
- "Average speed = (u + v)/2" used for a multi-stage journey.
- Gradient of s–t² taken as g instead of g/2.
- Area below the axis added as positive when displacement is asked.
- Instantaneous velocity found from a chord instead of a tangent.
- Graph sketches with no labels or units on the axes.
- Answers with a magnitude but no sign or direction when velocity is asked.
- Required practical descriptions that do not say how g is found from the graph.

## Quick self-test

1. Define acceleration.
2. What does the gradient of a displacement–time graph give?
3. What does the area under an acceleration–time graph give?
4. A car speeds up from 5.0 m s⁻¹ to 25 m s⁻¹ in 8.0 s. Find its acceleration.
5. How far does the car in question 4 travel in that time?
6. A stone falls from rest for 2.0 s. Find its speed and the distance fallen.
7. A car at 20 m s⁻¹ brakes at 4.0 m s⁻². Find its stopping distance.
8. A ball is thrown up at 9.0 m s⁻¹. Find the time to the top and the maximum height.
9. How do you find the acceleration at one instant from a curved v–t graph?
10. In Required practical 1, the gradient of s against t² is 4.92 m s⁻². Find g.
11. What does an area below the time axis on a v–t graph represent?
12. Why is a graph of several drop heights better than a single drop for finding g?

### Answers

1. Rate of change of velocity, a = Δv/Δt.
2. **Velocity**.
3. **Change in velocity**.
4. 20 ÷ 8.0 = **2.5 m s⁻²**.
5. ½(5.0 + 25) × 8.0 = **120 m**.
6. v = 9.81 × 2.0 = **19.6 m s⁻¹**; s = ½ × 9.81 × 2.0² = **19.6 m**.
7. s = 20² ÷ (2 × 4.0) = **50 m**.
8. t = **0.92 s**; h = **4.1 m**.
9. Draw a tangent at that instant and find its **gradient**.
10. g = 2 × 4.92 = **9.84 m s⁻²**.
11. **Displacement in the negative direction.**
12. The gradient uses all the data, which reduces the effect of random error, and the line shows up anomalous points and any systematic offset from the origin.
