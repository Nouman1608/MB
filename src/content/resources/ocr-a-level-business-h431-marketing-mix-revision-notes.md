## The four specification points

1. Explain what is meant by the marketing mix.
2. Explain what is meant by a marketing strategy.
3. Distinguish between local, national and international/global marketing contexts.
4. Evaluate an appropriate marketing mix for local, national and international/global contexts.

## Core recall

### The four Ps

| P | Meaning | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| Product | What is sold | Quality, design, brand, packaging |
| Price | What the customer pays | Discounts, credit terms |
| Place | How the product reaches the customer | Channels, stores, online |
| Promotion | How customers are informed and persuaded | Advertising, personal selling, digital |

The mix must be consistent: all four elements aim at the same target customer and image. Services add process, people and physical evidence (3.12).

### Strategy and mix

| | Strategy | Mix |
|---|---|---|
| Answers | Who do we target and how will we compete? | What do we offer, at what price, where, with what message? |
| Time scale | Long term | Adjustable quickly |
| Level | Direction | Tactics |

The mix carries out the strategy and must fit it.

### Contexts

| Context | Features |
|---|---|
| Local | One area; word of mouth; personal service; limited budget |
| National | Wider distribution; mass media; brand and supply matter |
| International/global | Culture, language, law, income and exchange rates differ; transport and tariffs add cost |

### Choosing the mix: questions to ask

- Who is the target customer, and what do they value?
- What can the business afford?
- What are competitors doing?
- Is it consistent with the brand and objectives?
- Standardise or adapt?
- Can the business control distributors and retailers?

| Standardised | Adapted |
|---|---|
| Lower cost; consistent brand | Fits local tastes, laws, incomes |
| Risk: rules and culture ignored | Higher cost; diluted brand |

A balanced answer: standardise the core brand and adapt the details.

### Local, national, global in practice

| | Local | National | Global |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product | Fresh, personal | Standard, consistent | Adapted to rules and taste |
| Price | Premium, flexible | Competitive | Includes transport, tariff, distributor margins |
| Place | Markets, local shops | Supermarkets, national online | Distributors, selected online |
| Promotion | Word of mouth, local press | National advertising, social media | Local-language digital, trade fairs |

## Quick worked reminder

Bramble & Co (invented): hamper costs £22.00. UK: 22.00 × 1.40 × 1.20 = £36.96. Abroad via distributor: landed cost 22 + 3 + 8% of 25 (£2.00) = £27.00; × 1.30 = £35.10; × 1.40 = £49.14; × 1.20 = £58.97. That is £22.01 (about 60%) more than the UK price. Direct online with a 25% margin: 27.00 × 1.25 × 1.20 = £40.50, which is £18.47 lower.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Marketing mix | The combination of product, price, place and promotion decisions |
| Marketing strategy | A long-term plan to achieve marketing objectives |
| Positioning | How a product is seen against rivals |
| Standardisation | Using one mix in all markets |
| Adaptation | Changing the mix to suit each market |
| Landed cost | Cost of goods including transport and import duty |
| Tariff | A tax on imported goods |

## Exam traps

- Strategy is not the same as mix.
- Apply each P to the case; do not just list them.
- The best mix differs by context: justify why.
- Check consistency between the four elements.
- Include a risk and a judgement.

## Evaluation prompts

- Does the mix fit the target customer in this context, or is it copied from another market?
- Are all four elements consistent, or does one (often price) undermine the image?
- What does it cost, and can the business afford it at this scale?
- What would a rival do in response?
- Would adapting the details raise sales by more than it adds to cost?
- Finish with a judgement for the business and context named in the case.

## Self-test

1. List the four Ps.
2. Distinguish a marketing strategy from a marketing mix.
3. Why must the mix be consistent?
4. Give one feature of the local context.
5. Give one extra cost of selling abroad.
6. Cost £10; retailer mark-up 50%; VAT 20%. Find the shelf price.
7. Give one benefit of a standardised mix.
8. Give one benefit of an adapted mix.
9. Why might a small firm avoid national television?
10. A product costs £16, shipping is £4 and the tariff is 10% of cost plus shipping. Find the landed cost.
11. Why may selling directly online reduce the price abroad?
12. Evaluate in one sentence whether to standardise or adapt.

### Answers

1. Product, price, place, promotion.
2. A strategy is the long-term plan for who to target and how to compete; the mix is the set of tactics (product, price, place and promotion) that carries it out.
3. Inconsistent elements give a confusing message, such as a luxury product sold at a discount.
4. Any one: one area; word of mouth; personal service; limited budget.
5. Any one: transport; tariffs; distributor margins; translation; compliance.
6. 10 × 1.50 × 1.20 = **£18.00**.
7. Any one: lower cost; consistent brand.
8. Any one: fits local tastes; meets local laws; suits local incomes.
9. It is too expensive for a small budget and reaches many people who are not customers.
10. Tariff = 10% × (16 + 4) = £2; landed cost = 16 + 4 + 2 = **£22**.
11. It removes the distributor and retailer margins.
12. Usually standardise the core brand and adapt details such as language, price and channel, because this keeps cost and image under control while fitting each market.
