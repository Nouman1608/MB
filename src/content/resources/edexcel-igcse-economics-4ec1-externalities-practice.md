These questions cover section 1.1.6, Externalities, of the Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Economics (4EC1) specification, Issue 3, February 2026. There is a single tier of entry. All firms, places and figures are invented. Read the [externalities study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-externalities/) and the [externalities revision notes](/resources/edexcel-igcse-economics-4ec1-externalities-revision-notes/) first.

## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** What is meant by external costs? **[2]**

**2.** Which of the following is an external benefit of consumption?

A: a student's higher salary after a degree

B: lower infection rates among people who are not vaccinated, when others are vaccinated

C: the profit a private hospital makes

D: smoke from a factory chimney

**[1]**

**3.** State the formula for social benefits. **[1]**

**4.** A steel works has private costs of 640 per tonne and external costs of 160 per tonne. Calculate the social cost per tonne. **[1]**

**5.** State **two** examples of external costs given in the specification. **[2]**

### Section B: structured and extended questions

**6.** A chain of fast-food restaurants opens a large drive-through on a busy road. Queues of cars spill onto the road at peak times.

**(a)** Identify **one** external cost of the drive-through and the third party who bears it. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why the restaurant chain is likely to ignore this external cost when deciding its opening hours. **[3]**

**7.** A town council is considering a free flu vaccination programme. The table shows invented values per person vaccinated.

| | Value per person |
|---|---|
| Private benefit | 28 |
| External benefit | 42 |
| Cost of vaccination | 50 |

**(a)** Calculate the social benefit per person vaccinated. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the external benefit as a percentage of the social benefit. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why, without the free programme, fewer people would choose to be vaccinated than is best for society. **[3]**

**8.** A mining company produces 5 000 tonnes of copper a month. The table lists some costs linked to its output each month.

| Item | Value |
|---|---|
| Wages | 210 000 |
| Fuel and machinery | 140 000 |
| Damage to farmland from dust | 45 000 |
| Cost to local hospitals of treating breathing problems | 30 000 |

**(a)** Calculate the private costs, external costs and social costs. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the social cost per tonne. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain **one** reason why the market may produce too much copper. **[3]**

**9.** Explain **one** external benefit of education. **[3]**

**10.** The social cost of a factory's output is 96 000 a month and its private cost is 78 000. Calculate the external cost and state **one** possible source of it. **[2]**

**11.** Analyse how the building of a new motorway through countryside may create external costs. **[6]**

**12.** Explain why healthcare is an example of a good with external benefits. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** Costs of production [1]; that fall on third parties and are not paid for by the producer [1]. **[2]**

**2.** B [1]. **[1]**
*Examiner insight:* A is a private benefit, C is not an externality and D is an external cost.

**3.** Social benefits = private benefits + external benefits [1]. **[1]**

**4.** 640 + 160 = **800** per tonne [1]. **[1]**

**5.** Any two: pollution [1]; congestion [1]; (also accept environmental damage). **[2]**

**6. (a)** Congestion / traffic delays [1]; borne by other road users, such as drivers and bus passengers [1].

**(b)** The chain decides based on its private costs and revenue [1]; it does not pay for the delays caused to other drivers [1]; so it may stay open at peak times even though the cost to society is greater than the cost it sees [1]. **[5]**

**7. (a)** 28 + 42 = **70** [1].

**(b)** 42 ÷ 70 × 100 [1] = **60%** [1].

**(c)** Each person compares only the private benefit of 28 with the cost of 50 [1]; so they would not pay to be vaccinated [1]; although the social benefit of 70 is greater than the cost of 50, so society gains from more vaccination [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Use the figures in the table; the comparison of private benefit with cost explains individual choices, and social benefit with cost explains society's view.

**8. (a)** Private costs = 210 000 + 140 000 = **350 000** [1]; external costs = 45 000 + 30 000 = **75 000** [1]; social costs = 350 000 + 75 000 = **425 000** [1].

**(b)** 425 000 ÷ 5 000 = **85 per tonne** [1].

**(c)** The company bases output on its private costs of 70 per tonne [1]; it ignores the external costs of 15 per tonne borne by farmers and hospitals [1]; so it produces more than is best for society, an inefficient allocation of resources [1]. **[7]**

**9.** An educated worker is more productive [1]; employers gain higher output from the skills, which is not fully paid for in wages [1]; so the economy gains higher output and the government gains more tax revenue, benefiting third parties [1]. (Also accept: educated citizens are more likely to take part in the community or need less support.) **[3]**

**10.** 96 000 − 78 000 = **18 000** [1]; one source, such as pollution of air or water, or congestion from delivery lorries [1]. **[2]**

**11.** Construction brings noise and dust, a form of pollution affecting nearby residents [1]; who are not compensated, so this is an external cost [1]. Countryside and wildlife habitats are lost, which is environmental damage [1]; walkers and future generations lose a landscape they valued [1]. Once open, extra traffic may cause congestion on nearby roads [1]; and higher emissions, so the social cost of the motorway is greater than its private cost [1]. **[6]**

**12.** When a person is treated, they gain a private benefit of better health [1]; others also benefit, for example an employer loses fewer working days or the illness is not passed on [1]; these third parties do not pay, so the benefit is external [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming an example without a third party.
- Treating wages or fuel as external costs.
- Forgetting to add private and external costs to get social costs.
- Writing the external benefit as a percentage of the private benefit rather than the social benefit.
- Explaining over-production without saying the firm ignores external costs.
