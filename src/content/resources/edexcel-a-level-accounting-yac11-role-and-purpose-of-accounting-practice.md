These questions cover outcomes 1.1.1 and 1.1.2 of Pearson Edexcel International A Level Accounting (YAC11), Unit 1: the role of accounting in recording, analysing and communicating information to users, and accounting information as an aid to management. Review the [role and purpose study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-role-and-purpose-of-accounting/) or the [role and purpose revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-role-and-purpose-of-accounting-revision-notes/) first. All businesses and figures are invented; amounts are in dollars.

## Section A

**1.** State the three stages in the role of accounting. **[3]**

**2.** Define management accounting. **[2]**

**3.** Identify two external users of a sole trader's financial statements, and state one item of information each would need. **[4]**

**4.** Explain the difference between bookkeeping and accounting. **[2]**

**5.** State two limitations of financial statements as a source of information. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Fernhill Garden Supplies provides the following information.

| | Year 1 | Year 2 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | 150,000 | 170,000 |
| Cost of sales | 96,000 | 115,600 |

**(a)** Calculate the gross profit and the gross profit as a percentage of revenue for each year. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain how this analysis helps the owner to manage the business. **[3]**

**7.** Explain how the interests of the following pairs of users may conflict.

**(a)** The owner and the tax authority. **[2]**

**(b)** The owner and the employees. **[2]**

**8.** A manager compares planned and actual costs for one month.

| Cost | Planned | Actual |
|---|---|---|
| Wages | 12,000 | 13,500 |
| Electricity | 1,800 | 1,750 |
| Advertising | 2,000 | 3,100 |

**(a)** Calculate the difference between planned and actual for each cost, stating whether it is an overspend or an underspend. **[3]**

**(b)** Comment on which cost the manager should investigate first. **[2]**

**9.** Weaver & Co pays $9,500 a year to lease a machine. It could instead buy a machine for $40,000, with a useful life of 5 years and a residual value of $5,000, depreciated by the straight line method. Other running costs are the same.

**(a)** Calculate the annual depreciation if the machine is bought. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the effect on annual profit of buying rather than leasing. **[1]**

**(c)** State two other factors the owner should consider. **[2]**

**(d)** Recommend whether to buy or lease, justifying your choice. **[1]**

**10.** Evaluate the usefulness of a sole trader's latest financial statements to a supplier deciding whether to sell goods to the business on 60 days' credit. **[6]**

**11.** Explain why accounting information should be both timely and reliable, and how these two qualities can conflict. **[4]**

**12.** Explain, with an example, how accounting records help the managers of a business to safeguard its assets. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** **Recording** transactions [1]; **analysing** the records [1]; **communicating** the information to users [1]. **[3]**

**2.** Accounting that provides detailed information for **internal users (managers)** [1] to help them **plan, control and make decisions** [1]. **[2]**

**3.** Any two users with a matching need, 2 marks each: a **lender** [1] needs profit and existing loans to judge repayment [1]; a **trade supplier** [1] needs cash and current liabilities to judge whether invoices will be paid [1]; the **tax authority** [1] needs profit for the year to assess tax [1]. **[4]**

**4.** Bookkeeping is the **recording** of transactions in the books [1]; accounting also **analyses** the records and **communicates** the results to users [1]. **[2]**

**5.** Any two: they report the **past** [1]; items that cannot be measured in **money** are left out [1]; they depend on **estimates** such as depreciation [1]; assets are mainly at **historic cost** [1]. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Year 1 gross profit **$54,000** [1], **36%** [1]. Year 2 gross profit **$54,400** [1], **32%** [1].

**(b)** Revenue rose by $20,000 but gross profit rose by only $400 [1], because the gross profit percentage fell from 36% to 32% [1]. The owner can investigate whether suppliers' prices have risen or selling prices were cut, and act on the cause [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Analysis means linking two figures: here, higher revenue with a lower margin. Quoting the figures without the link is description only.

**7. (a)** The owner may prefer a lower reported profit to reduce tax [1], whereas the tax authority wants profit stated fully so that the correct tax is collected [1].

**(b)** The owner may want to keep wages low to increase profit [1], whereas employees want higher pay, which reduces profit [1]. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Wages: $1,500 **overspend** [1]. Electricity: $50 **underspend** [1]. Advertising: $1,100 **overspend** [1].

**(b)** **Wages**, because it is the largest overspend in dollars [1]; however, advertising is 55% above plan compared with 12.5% for wages, so it may show weaker control and should be investigated too [1]. **[5]**

**9. (a)** (40,000 − 5,000) ÷ 5 [1] = **$7,000** a year [1].

**(b)** Profit is 9,500 − 7,000 = **$2,500 a year higher** if the machine is bought [1].

**(c)** Any two: buying uses **$40,000 of cash** now [1]; the business bears the risk of **repairs and breakdowns** [1]; the machine may become **obsolete** before 5 years [1]; leasing may allow an **upgrade** to newer models [1].

**(d)** A justified choice, such as: buy, because profit rises by $2,500 a year, provided the business has the cash without harming its ability to pay suppliers [1]. **[6]**

**10.** Up to 6 marks from the following, including a conclusion for full marks:

- The statement of financial position shows **cash and current assets against current liabilities**, indicating whether the business can pay its debts within 60 days [1].
- The statement of profit or loss shows whether the business is **profitable**, so likely to keep trading [1].
- The statements are **historic**; the position may have changed since the year end, which matters for credit given now [1].
- Sole trader statements may **not be audited**, so they may contain errors or be presented favourably [1].
- They do not show the business's **payment record** with other suppliers, which is directly relevant [1].
- **Conclusion:** useful as a starting point, but the supplier should also seek trade references, a credit limit or a shorter first credit period [1]. **[6]**

**11.** Information must be **timely** so that it arrives while decisions can still be influenced [1]. It must be **reliable** so that users can trust it is free from material error [1]. Producing figures quickly may require more **estimates** [1], which makes them less reliable, while checking every figure delays the information [1]. **[4]**

**12.** Records show what the business **should** hold, such as inventory or cash [1]. Comparing them with a **physical count** or bank statement reveals any shortage [1]. For example, inventory records show $8,000 of goods but a count finds $7,400, so the $600 difference can be investigated for theft or error [1]. **[3]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Naming users without the information each needs.
- Describing figures without linking them to explain a change.
- Recommending on figures alone, with no other factors.
- Evaluations that lack a judgement or a suggestion of what else to obtain.
- Using older terms instead of the IAS terms examiners use.
