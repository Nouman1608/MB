This page is a guide to AQA A Level Psychology (7182) past papers from June 2024 to June 2026. For each series you get the grade thresholds, copied from AQA's grade-boundary documents, and, where AQA has published them, a short account of what the examiners saw, written by us from AQA's reports on the examination. We do not host any papers. Every paper, mark scheme and report is on AQA's own site, linked below.

## Where to get the official papers

- [AQA A-level Psychology (7182) subject page](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/psychology/a-level/psychology-7182)
- [AQA A-level Psychology (7182) assessment resources](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/psychology/a-level/psychology-7182/assessment-resources), where question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination are listed by series and paper
- [AQA A-level Psychology (7182) specification at a glance](https://www.aqa.org.uk/subjects/psychology/a-level/psychology-7182/specification/specification-at-a-glance)
- [AQA grade boundaries](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries) and the [grade-boundary archive](https://www.aqa.org.uk/exams-administration/results-days/grade-boundaries/archive)

Use the three documents together. Sit the paper first, under timed conditions. Mark it strictly with the mark scheme, which shows what each mark needs, including the level descriptors for the longer answers. Then read the report on the examination for that paper: it explains why answers that looked reasonable still lost marks. Our notes for each series below are a quick way into that report.

Check which specification a paper was set on. AQA's subject page shows an updated 7182 specification, first taught in September 2025, with first A-level exams in summer 2027. The papers on this page (June 2024 to June 2026) were sat before those first A-level exams, so they were not set on the updated specification, and we have not compared the two versions. Before relying on a question, check AQA's updated specification for any change to the content or wording. The qualification is linear with three two-hour papers of 96 marks, each one third of the A-level: Paper 1 (Introductory topics in psychology), Paper 2 (Psychology in context) and Paper 3 (Issues and options in psychology).

## June 2025

The reports describe Paper 1 as one where generic criticism of research, not linked to the question, was a recurring weakness, Paper 2 as one where over-reliance on learned responses held answers back, and Paper 3 as rather harder than in 2024.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7182/1)**

- Evaluation of research was generic and not linked back to the explanation or study in the question.
- Answers on the authoritarian personality drifted into prejudice or described the personality type without linking it to obedience.
- Application answers copied phrases from the stem without developing them, and a minority listed types of conformity with no connection to explanations.
- Memory: answers on Baddeley's work lacked detail or included aims and conclusions rather than what the question asked; revision strategies were general rather than based on retrieval failure.
- Only about 15 per cent reached the top level on a longer memory question, and descriptions without a link to interference theory scored poorly.
- Attachment: examples of interaction were given for only one side, the infant or the caregiver.
- Research methods: many struggled to say how reliability could be improved, and over half scored no marks on one such part.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7182/2)**

- Pre-learned material was written out when the question needed flexible, applied use of knowledge.
- Learning approach: answers dealt with definitions or findings rather than how reinforcement was investigated, and negative reinforcement was muddled with punishment.
- Evolution examples were not linked to a behaviour, and answers did not explain how an advantageous trait becomes more common over generations.
- Psychodynamic evaluation was descriptive, with muddled reductionism and gender-bias points.
- Biopsychology: the function of the endocrine system was replaced by its structure, and synaptic transmission misconceptions appeared, such as vesicles diffusing across the synapse.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 (7182/3)**

- Many scripts did not progress beyond the lower levels of the 8-mark and 16-mark questions, most notably in Forensic Psychology, Gender and Schizophrenia.
- Research methods, which appeared only in Section A, was a weakness.
- Environmental determinism was known but the application to the scenario used common sense rather than a psychological mechanism.
- Cultural bias was outlined when cultural relativism was asked for, and definitions were too brief.
- Gender: stereotypes were described at length, gender schema was muddled with Kohlberg's theory, and some answers described perspective-taking research instead of evaluating it.
- Schizophrenia: family therapy answers relied on common sense and had too little material for a 16-mark answer.

## June 2024

The reports describe Paper 1 as more accessible than the year before, Paper 2 as well prepared with excellent recall, and Paper 3 as a paper where candidates paced themselves well but lost marks on data handling and on some evaluation and comparison answers.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 1 (7182/1)**

- Memory was the weakest section, partly because research methods questions were set within scenarios and partly because candidates had little to say on the short essay about eyewitness testimony.
- Inferential statistics were poorly understood, with many answers describing descriptive statistics or saying only that a result is significant or due to chance without elaboration.
- A study of a relationship was wrongly called a test of difference.
- Longer essays were challenging, and ethical issues were not outlined in enough depth.
- Candidates did well on episodic versus semantic memory and on matching caregiver-infant interactions to scenarios.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 2 (7182/2)**

- Candidates did not address every part of the question or apply their answer to the stem.
- Psychodynamic answers were vague about where the ego and superego operate, and an introspection question attracted pre-prepared material about Wundt's role rather than the method asked about.
- Hormones were confused with neurotransmitters, reflex arcs and neural structures.
- Humanistic approach: congruence was confused with Maslow's hierarchy and answers were generic rather than applied to the person in the scenario.
- Comparison questions on learning theories were answered with long descriptions and no actual comparison, and negative reinforcement was confused with punishment.

**Where marks were lost: Paper 3 (7182/3)**

- A short question on referencing proved hard for many.
- Culture bias definitions left out the idea of cultural superiority, and free will and determinism essays became a plain debate, with other debates mixed in.
- Data-handling questions lost marks when candidates simply restated the figures in a table, and conclusions lacked clarity.
- Relationships: the dance study was read as supporting a theory it did not support, and evolutionary mating strategies were described as conscious choices.
- Cognition and development: Vygotsky was compared with Piaget without using Piaget's theory as a comparison.
- Meta-analysis was widely recognised, but explanations of the difference between approaches were sometimes too brief.

## Frequently asked questions

**Where can I find AQA A Level Psychology 7182 past papers?**
AQA lists question papers, mark schemes and reports on the examination on the 7182 assessment-resources page. Grade boundaries are on AQA's grade-boundaries page and its archive.

**Which papers do I sit for a full practice run?**
All three: Paper 1, Paper 2 and Paper 3. Each is two hours and 96 marks. In Paper 3 you answer the compulsory content and the options your course teaches.

**Do these papers match the updated 7182 specification?**
Not necessarily. AQA's updated specification was first taught in September 2025 and is first examined at A-level in summer 2027, so the June 2024 to June 2026 papers were sat before it. Check AQA's updated specification for changes before using a question.

**Are the paper-level grade thresholds official grades?**
No. AQA states that paper-level boundaries are notional and for illustration only. The grade comes from the total mark out of 288 and the subject boundary.

**Can grade thresholds tell me what mark I need next time?**
No. Thresholds change every series and should only be used as a rough guide for the series they belong to, never to predict future ones.
