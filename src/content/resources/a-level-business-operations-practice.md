## Questions

Work through these in order. Show your working on every calculation, and write the unit with your answer.

**1.** Define the term *re-order level*, and explain how it differs from *buffer inventory*. **[2]**

**2.** A shop uses 50 units a day. The supplier's lead time is 6 days and the shop keeps a buffer inventory of 100 units. Calculate the re-order level. **[2]**

**3.** State **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to a business of just-in-time (JIT) inventory management. **[2]**

**4.** A factory produces 3,600 units a month. Its maximum capacity is 4,500 units a month. Calculate its capacity utilisation. **[2]**

**5.** Define *labour productivity* and give **one** way a business could increase it. **[2]**

**6.** Tavin Ltd, a fictional furniture maker, produced 2,400 chairs in week 1 with 16 workers. In week 2 it produced 2,730 chairs with 18 workers. Calculate the percentage change in labour productivity between the two weeks. Give your answer to 1 decimal place. **[4]**

**7.** Orlo Ltd, a fictional bottler, has a maximum capacity of 8,000 bottles a month. It makes 6,000 bottles a month. Fixed costs are £48,000 a month and variable costs are £5 a bottle. A new customer offers an order for 1,500 extra bottles a month. Calculate the capacity utilisation and the average cost per bottle before and after accepting the order. **[5]**

**8.** A fictional bakery uses 70 bags of flour a day. The supplier takes 4 days to deliver. The bakery keeps 120 bags as buffer inventory and orders 500 bags at a time. Calculate the re-order level and the maximum inventory level. Assume each delivery arrives just as inventory reaches the buffer level. **[4]**

**9.** Explain **two** problems for a business that operates well below its maximum capacity. **[4]**

**10.** Zenra Ltd, a fictional ready-meal producer, holds average inventory worth £120,000. Holding costs are 20% of inventory value each year. Zenra is considering JIT, which would cut average inventory to £30,000. Its main supplier has delivered late twice in the past year, each time stopping production for two days. Analyse the effects on Zenra of introducing JIT. **[8]**

**11.** Karel Ltd, a fictional packaging firm, has three production lines. Each line can make 6,000 boxes a month. Karel makes 9,900 boxes a month. Each line costs £15,000 a month in fixed costs. Karel's owner is choosing between closing one line and launching a promotion to win more orders. Evaluate which option Karel should choose. **[12]**

## Answers

**1.** The **re-order level** is the level of inventory at which a **new order is placed** with the supplier [1]. The **buffer inventory** is the minimum safety level kept to cover unexpected demand or late deliveries, and the re-order level is set so the delivery arrives before inventory falls below it [1].

*Examiner insight:* the re-order level and the buffer are different numbers, and the difference is the usage during the lead time. State that link and you secure the second mark.

**2.** Usage during the lead time = 50 × 6 = 300 units [1]. Re-order level = 300 + 100 = **400 units** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the most common slip is stopping at 300. The buffer must be added, because the order has to arrive before the buffer is touched.

**3.** Advantage: lower **inventory holding costs**, such as less storage space and less waste [1]. Disadvantage: **production can stop** if a supplier delivers late, because there is no buffer inventory [1].

*Examiner insight:* name the effect on the business, not just the feature. "Little inventory is held" earns nothing without the cost saving or the risk.

**4.** 3,600 ÷ 4,500 × 100 [1] = **80%** [1].

*Examiner insight:* actual output goes on top and maximum capacity underneath. Inverting the fraction gives 125%, which a quick check would show is impossible.

**5.** Output **per worker** in a given period [1]. One way: training, better equipment or improved motivation [1].

*Examiner insight:* a way to raise productivity must raise output per worker. "Hire more workers" does not, unless output rises faster than the workforce.

**6.** Week 1 productivity = 2,400 ÷ 16 = 150 chairs per worker [1]. Week 2 productivity = 2,730 ÷ 18 = 151.67 chairs per worker [1]. Change = (151.67 − 150) ÷ 150 × 100 [1] = **1.1%** increase [1].

*Examiner insight:* output rose by 13.75%, but workers rose by 12.5%, so the gain in productivity is small. Candidates who quote the output rise as the answer lose both final marks.

**7.** Before: 6,000 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = **75%** [1]. Average cost = 48,000 ÷ 6,000 + 5 = 8 + 5 = **£13** per bottle [1]. After: output = 7,500 bottles, so utilisation = 7,500 ÷ 8,000 × 100 = **93.75%** [1]. Fixed cost per bottle = 48,000 ÷ 7,500 = £6.40 [1], so average cost = 6.40 + 5 = **£11.40** per bottle [1].

*Examiner insight:* fixed costs do not change when output rises, so only the share per bottle falls. Adding the variable cost to the fixed cost per bottle is the step most often dropped.

**8.** Usage during the lead time = 70 × 4 = 280 bags [1]. Re-order level = 280 + 120 = **400 bags** [1]. Maximum inventory = order quantity + buffer = 500 + 120 [1] = **620 bags** [1].

*Examiner insight:* the maximum level is not the re-order level plus the order. The order lands when stock has fallen to the buffer, so add 500 to 120.

**9.** Award 2 marks for each developed problem. Higher **average cost per unit** because fixed costs are spread over few units [1], which reduces profit margins or forces a higher price [1]. **Idle resources**, so machines and workers are paid for but not used [1], which wastes money and may lead to redundancies or closure of capacity [1]. Other valid problems include lower staff morale and a poor image if the business looks unsuccessful.

*Examiner insight:* each problem needs a consequence for the business. "Costs are high" is a description. "Fixed costs are shared over fewer units, so each unit costs more" is explanation.

**10.** This is a guide. The real exam marks an extended response by levels of response, so a clear chain of analysis matters more than a list. Indicative points:
- Current holding cost = 20% × £120,000 = £24,000 a year [1].
- JIT holding cost = 20% × £30,000 = £6,000 a year, so Zenra saves £18,000 a year [1].
- Less inventory means less cash tied up, which can be used elsewhere [1].
- Less storage space and less waste of perishable meals [1].
- Zenra has no buffer under JIT, so a late delivery stops production [1].
- Two late deliveries in the past year each caused two days of lost output, so the risk is real [1].
- Lost production means missed orders and possibly lost customers [1].
- The saving depends on supplier reliability, so Zenra should check how much lost output would cost against £18,000 [1].

*Examiner insight:* use the figures. Work out the saving, then set it against the cost of the two stoppages. Answers that discuss JIT in general terms stay in the lower levels.

**11.** This is a guide. The real exam marks an extended response by levels of response. Indicative points:
- Maximum capacity = 3 × 6,000 = 18,000 boxes; utilisation = 9,900 ÷ 18,000 × 100 = 55% [1].
- Fixed cost per box now = 45,000 ÷ 9,900 = £4.55 [1].
- Closing a line gives capacity of 12,000 boxes and utilisation of 82.5% [1].
- Fixed costs fall to £30,000, so fixed cost per box falls to £3.03 [1].
- Closing is certain and quick, and it does not depend on customers responding [1].
- But closing leaves only 2,100 boxes of spare capacity, which limits growth [1].
- Closure may mean redundancy payments and lower morale [1].
- Rebuilding the line later would be costly [1].
- A promotion could use the idle 8,100 boxes of capacity without losing the line [1].
- It spreads fixed costs, but it costs money and demand may not rise [1].
- It also suits a temporary fall in demand better than a permanent one [1].
- Judgement: close the line if low demand looks permanent, and promote if it looks temporary, supported by the cost figures [1].

*Examiner insight:* the top-level marks go to a supported decision. Say which option you choose, and name the condition (permanent or temporary demand) that would change your mind.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the re-order level with JIT.
- Forgetting to add the buffer inventory when calculating the re-order level.
- Calculating capacity utilisation the wrong way round.
- Dividing by the new figure when working out a percentage change.
- Treating fixed costs as if they rise with output.
- Giving a feature of JIT with no effect on cost, quality or risk.
- Quoting a calculated figure with no unit or no comment on what it means.
- Listing advantages and disadvantages in an evaluation without reaching a justified decision.
