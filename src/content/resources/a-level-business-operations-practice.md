## Questions

**1.** Define the term *re-order level*, and explain how it differs from *buffer inventory*. **[2]**

**2.** A shop uses 50 units a day. The supplier's lead time is 6 days and the shop keeps a buffer inventory of 100 units. Calculate the re-order level. **[2]**

**3.** State **one** advantage and **one** disadvantage to a business of just-in-time (JIT) inventory management. **[2]**

**4.** A factory produces 3600 units a month. Its maximum capacity is 4500 units a month. Calculate its capacity utilisation. **[2]**

**5.** Define *labour productivity* and give **one** way a business could increase it. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** The **re-order level** is the level of inventory at which a **new order is placed** with the supplier [1]; it is set so the delivery arrives before inventory falls below the **buffer inventory**, the minimum safety level kept to cover unexpected demand or late deliveries [1].

*Examiner insight (June 2025):* weaker answers confused the re-order level with just-in-time inventory management and wrote about low inventory holding costs. The re-order level belongs to a traditional inventory system that holds buffer inventory. *Try the real question next:* Cambridge International AS & A Level Business 9609, June 2025, Paper 12, Question 3.

**2.** Usage during the lead time = 50 × 6 = 300 units [1]; re-order level = 300 + 100 = **400 units** [1].

**3.** Advantage: lower **inventory holding costs** (less storage space, less waste) [1]. Disadvantage: **production can stop** if a supplier delivers late, because there is no buffer inventory [1].

**4.** 3600 ÷ 4500 × 100 [1] = **80%** [1].

**5.** Output **per worker** in a given period [1]; for example, training, better equipment or improved motivation [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Confusing the re-order level with JIT.
- Forgetting to add the buffer inventory when calculating the re-order level.
- Calculating capacity utilisation the wrong way round.
