## Questions

**1.** A curve has parametric equations x = 4t − 3, y = 2t² + t.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the points where t = −1 and t = 2. **[2]**
**(b)** At which point does the curve cut the y-axis? **[2]**

**2.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2t + 3, y = 1/t, for t > 0. Find a Cartesian equation of the curve and state the set of possible values of x. **[3]**

**3.** A curve has parametric equations x = 2 sin θ − 1, y = 3 cos θ + 2, for 0 ≤ θ < 2π.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the curve. **[3]**
**(b)** State the greatest and least values of x. **[1]**

**4.** A curve has parametric equations x = 4 sin θ, y = 3 − 2 cos 2θ, for 0 ≤ θ ≤ 2π. Show that y = 1 + x²/4, and state the range of values of x. **[4]**

**5.**

**(a)** Write the circle x² + y² + 8x − 6y + 9 = 0 in parametric form. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down parametric equations for the line segment from A(−2, 5) to B(4, −1), and use them to show that the segment lies on the line x + y = 3. **[2]**

**6.** A curve has parametric equations x = t² − 1, y = t³ − t.

**(a)** Find the coordinates of the points on the curve for t = −2, −1, 0, 1 and 2. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that y² = x²(x + 1). **[2]**
**(c)** Describe the shape of the curve for −1 ≤ x ≤ 0, and say what happens at the origin. **[1]**

**7.** A stone is thrown from the top of a cliff. Its position t seconds later is modelled by x = 8t, y = 10 + 12t − 5t², where x is the horizontal distance in metres from the cliff edge and y is the height in metres above the sea.

**(a)** Find the greatest height of the stone above the sea. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the horizontal distance from the cliff edge at which the stone enters the sea. **[3]**

**8.** A drone flies so that its position t seconds after take-off is x = 30 cos(πt/20), y = 20 sin(πt/20), where x and y are in metres.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the drone's path. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the first time at which x = 15. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the exact value of y at that time. **[2]**

**9.** A curve has parametric equations x = 3t + 2, y = t² − 6t. The line y = x − 10 intersects the curve in two places, called P and Q.

**(a)** Work out the coordinates of both points. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the exact length of PQ. **[2]**

**10.** Particle P moves so that its position at time t seconds (t ≥ 0) is (3t, t²). Particle Q moves so that its position at time t seconds (t ≥ 0) is (15 − 3t, t + 1). Lengths are in metres.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the path of each particle. **[3]**
**(b)** Show that the two paths cross at only one point, and find it. **[2]**
**(c)** Show that P and Q do not collide. **[2]**

**11.** A football leaves the ground at t = 0 and is modelled by x = 15t, y = 10t − 5t², with t in seconds, where x is the horizontal distance in metres and y is the height in metres. A crossbar 2.4 m high is 24 m from the kick, in the line of the ball's flight.

**(a)** Find a Cartesian equation of the path. **[3]**
**(b)** Decide whether the ball passes above the crossbar. **[3]**
**(c)** For how long is the ball higher than 4 m? **[3]**
**(d)** State one way in which the model is unrealistic. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** t = −1: x = −4 − 3 = −7, y = 2 − 1 = 1, giving (−7, 1). [1] t = 2: x = 5, y = 8 + 2 = 10, giving (5, 10). [1]
**(b)** On the y-axis x = 0, so 4t − 3 = 0 and t = 3/4. [1] y = 2(9/16) + 3/4 = 15/8, so the point is **(0, 15/8)**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* give both coordinates of every point, and keep the exact fraction instead of a rounded decimal unless the question asks for one.

**2.** From x = 2t + 3, t = (x − 3)/2. [1] Then y = 1/t = 2/(x − 3), so **y = 2/(x − 3)**. [1] Since t > 0, x = 2t + 3 > 3, so **x > 3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* the equation alone is not the full answer when the question asks for the values of x. Link the stated range of t to x explicitly.

**3. (a)** sin θ = (x + 1)/2 and cos θ = (y − 2)/3. [1] Use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1. [1] **((x + 1)/2)² + ((y − 2)/3)² = 1**. [1]
**(b)** sin θ ranges from −1 to 1, so x runs from −3 to 1: greatest **1**, least **−3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* writing the identity you are using makes the method clear to the reader. Mark the centre's coordinates carefully, since (x + 1) means the centre has x-coordinate −1.

**4.** cos 2θ = 1 − 2sin²θ. [1] So y = 3 − 2(1 − 2sin²θ) = 1 + 4sin²θ. [1] From x = 4 sin θ, sin θ = x/4, so y = 1 + 4(x²/16) = **1 + x²/4**. [1] Since −1 ≤ sin θ ≤ 1, **−4 ≤ x ≤ 4**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* in a "show that" question every line of algebra must appear, with the substitution stated. The final range is easy to forget, so write it as a separate line.

**5. (a)** Complete the square: (x + 4)² − 16 + (y − 3)² − 9 + 9 = 0, so (x + 4)² + (y − 3)² = 16. [1] Centre (−4, 3), radius 4. [1] **x = −4 + 4 cos θ, y = 3 + 4 sin θ**, 0 ≤ θ < 2π. [1]
**(b)** x = −2 + 6t, y = 5 − 6t, 0 ≤ t ≤ 1. [1] Then x + y = (−2 + 6t) + (5 − 6t) = **3**, as required. [1]
*Examiner insight:* the parametric form of a circle is not unique, but the centre and radius must match the completed square. Check by substituting back.

**6. (a)** t = −2: (3, −6); t = −1: (0, 0); t = 0: (−1, 0); t = 1: (0, 0); t = 2: (3, 6). [1] (any three correct) [1] (all five correct)
**(b)** y = t(t² − 1) = t·x, and t² = x + 1. [1] So y² = t²x² = **x²(x + 1)**. [1]
**(c)** For −1 ≤ x ≤ 0 the curve forms a **loop** between (−1, 0) and (0, 0). The curve **crosses itself at the origin**, because t = −1 and t = 1 both give (0, 0). [1]
*Examiner insight:* a table of values with both t and the matching points shows why a curve crosses itself. Equal points from different parameter values are the evidence.

**7. (a)** The greatest value of y is at the turning point of the quadratic 10 + 12t − 5t². [1] This is at t = 12/10 = 1.2 (halfway between the roots of 12t − 5t² = 0). [1] y = 10 + 14.4 − 7.2 = **17.2 m**. [1]
**(b)** The stone enters the sea when y = 0: 5t² − 12t − 10 = 0. [1] t = (12 + √344)/10 = 3.0547..., rejecting the negative root. [1] x = 8 × 3.0547... = **24.4 m** (3 s.f.). [1]
*Examiner insight:* reject the negative root because the model begins at t = 0, and say so. Use the unrounded t when you calculate x.

**8. (a)** cos(πt/20) = x/30 and sin(πt/20) = y/20. [1] **x²/900 + y²/400 = 1**. [1]
**(b)** 30 cos(πt/20) = 15, so cos(πt/20) = 1/2. [1] The first solution is πt/20 = π/3, so **t = 20/3 s** (6.67 s). [1]
**(c)** y = 20 sin(π/3) = 20 × √3/2. [1] **y = 10√3**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* "exact" means leave √3 in the answer. Choose the smallest positive angle for "first time" and work in radians, since the model uses π.

**9. (a)** Put x and y into y = x − 10: t² − 6t = (3t + 2) − 10. [1] t² − 9t + 8 = 0, so (t − 1)(t − 8) = 0. [1] t = 1 gives (5, −5). [1] t = 8 gives (26, 16). [1]
**(b)** PQ² = (26 − 5)² + (16 + 5)² = 882. [1] PQ = **21√2**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* find t first, then both coordinates for each value. Check each point on the line, since 5 − 10 = −5 and 26 − 10 = 16.

**10. (a)** For P, t = x/3, so y = **x²/9**. [1] For Q, t = (15 − x)/3, so y = (15 − x)/3 + 1 = **6 − x/3**. [1] A statement of each domain, x ≥ 0 for P and x ≤ 15 for Q. [1]
**(b)** Set x²/9 = 6 − x/3, so x² + 3x − 54 = 0. [1] (x + 9)(x − 6) = 0. x = −9 is impossible for P as x ≥ 0, so the only crossing is **(6, 4)**. [1]
**(c)** P is at (6, 4) when 3t = 6, so t = 2. [1] Q is at (6, 4) when 15 − 3t = 6, so t = 3. The times differ, so **they do not collide**. [1]
*Examiner insight:* "crossing paths" and "colliding" are different conclusions. Compare the times on the same clock, and give the time for each particle.

**11. (a)** t = x/15. [1] y = 10(x/15) − 5(x²/225). [1] **y = 2x/3 − x²/45**. [1]
**(b)** x = 24 gives t = 8/5. [1] y = 16 − 5(64/25) = 16 − 12.8 = 3.2. [1] Since 3.2 > 2.4, the ball **passes above the crossbar**. [1]
**(c)** Solve 10t − 5t² > 4, so 5t² − 10t + 4 < 0. [1] t = 1 ± √(1/5), about 0.553 and 1.447. [1] The duration is 2/√5 = **0.894 s** (3 s.f.). [1]
**(d)** For example, **air resistance is ignored**, or the ball is treated as a particle. [1]
*Examiner insight:* in a barrier question, state the comparison and the conclusion in words. Give a limitation that is specific to the model, not just "it is not realistic".

## Where marks are usually lost

- Omitting the range of x or y after eliminating a trigonometric parameter.
- Using a root outside the stated range of the parameter, such as a negative time.
- Rounding t before using it to find x or y.
- Writing a circle's or ellipse's centre with the wrong sign.
- Not writing down the identity used in a "show that" question.
- Giving the time of a collision from one particle only.
- Mixing degrees and radians when the model has π in it.
- Stopping at the Cartesian equation when the question asks for a restriction.
- Giving a bare number with no unit in a modelling question.
