This set practises **3.3.9.2 Acylation** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3, including Required practical 10. It is International A2 content, assessed in Unit 4 and in the synoptic Unit 5. If you get stuck, go back to the [acylation study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-2/) or the [acylation revision notes](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-9-2-revision-notes/). Use Ar values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0 and Cl = 35.5.

## Section A: short questions

**1.** Name each compound.

**(a)** (CH₃)₃CCOCl **[1]**

**(b)** (CH₃CO)₂O **[1]**

**(c)** CH₃CH₂CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ **[1]**

**2.** Propanoic anhydride reacts with excess ammonia. Which is the inorganic or ionic by-product?

A: hydrogen chloride

B: ammonium chloride

C: ammonium propanoate

D: water

**[1]**

**3.** Explain why the carbonyl carbon atom in an acyl chloride is readily attacked by nucleophiles. **[2]**

**4.** Give the name of the organic product when 3-methylbutanoyl chloride, (CH₃)₂CHCH₂COCl, reacts with:

**(a)** water **[1]**

**(b)** ethanol **[1]**

**5.** A few drops of ethanoyl chloride are added to water. State one observation and write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** 2-Methylpropanoyl chloride, (CH₃)₂CHCOCl, reacts with ammonia.

**(a)** Name the type of mechanism. **[1]**

**(b)** Outline the mechanism. Describe where each curly arrow starts and ends, and give the structure of the intermediate. **[4]**

**(c)** Name the organic product. **[1]**

**7.** A chemist plans two syntheses.

**(a)** Propyl ethanoate is to be made from ethanoic anhydride. Name the other reagent and write an equation. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why this gives a higher yield of ester than heating ethanoic acid with the same reagent and a catalyst. **[1]**

**(c)** N-Methylpropanamide, CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃, is to be made from an acyl chloride. Write an equation for the reaction. **[2]**

**8.** 4.26 g of 2-methylpropanoyl chloride (Mr = 106.5) is added to excess concentrated ammonia. 2.78 g of 2-methylpropanamide (Mr = 87.0) is obtained.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[3]**

**(c)** Calculate the minimum mass of ammonia needed. **[1]**

**9.** Aspirin (Mr = 180.0) is manufactured from 2-hydroxybenzoic acid and ethanoic anhydride.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the mass of ethanoic acid (Mr = 60.0) formed as a by-product for every 1.00 tonne of aspirin made. **[2]**

**(c)** If ethanoyl chloride were used instead, about 203 kg of hydrogen chloride would form per tonne of aspirin. Explain why this by-product is a disadvantage for a manufacturer. **[2]**

**10.** Compound P, C₃H₅ClO, gives steamy fumes in moist air. P reacts with an excess of a primary amine, R, to form an amide, S, with molecular formula C₅H₁₁NO.

**(a)** Give the name of P. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce the identities of R and S. Explain your reasoning. **[2]**

**(c)** Write an equation for the reaction of P with R. **[1]**

**11.** In Required practical 10, a student makes N-phenylethanamide by warming phenylamine, C₆H₅NH₂, with ethanoic anhydride. The data-book melting point of N-phenylethanamide is 114 °C. The crude product melts at 105–110 °C. After one recrystallisation from water, the product melts at 113–114 °C.

**(a)** Write an equation for the reaction. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain what the melting point results show. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the glassware must be dry at the start. **[1]**

**(d)** State the property that makes water a suitable solvent for recrystallising this product. **[1]**

**12.** Ethyl benzoate, C₆H₅COOCH₂CH₃, can be made from ethanol and either benzoic acid, benzoyl chloride or benzoic anhydride. Compare the three routes, considering conditions, the extent of reaction and the by-products. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 2,2-Dimethylpropanoyl chloride [1]

**(b)** Ethanoic anhydride [1]

**(c)** N-Ethylbutanamide [1]

**2.** **C** [1]. The second ammonia molecule neutralises the propanoic acid formed. HCl and NH₄Cl come only from acyl chlorides.

**3.** The carbonyl carbon is bonded to two electronegative atoms, O and Cl [1]; both withdraw electron density, so the carbon carries a large δ+ charge that attracts the lone pair of a nucleophile [1]

**4. (a)** 3-Methylbutanoic acid [1]

**(b)** Ethyl 3-methylbutanoate [1]

**5.** Steamy (misty) fumes, or vigorous reaction with heat released [1]; CH₃COCl + H₂O → CH₃COOH + HCl [1]

**6. (a)** Nucleophilic addition–elimination [1]

**(b)** Arrow from the lone pair on N of NH₃ to the carbonyl carbon, and arrow from the C=O π bond to O [1]; intermediate (CH₃)₂CH–C(O⁻)(Cl)–N⁺H₃ with both charges [1]; arrow from O⁻ lone pair to re-form C=O, and arrow from the C–Cl bond to Cl, which leaves as Cl⁻ [1]; a second NH₃ (or Cl⁻) removes H⁺ from N⁺, arrow from the N–H bond to N [1]

**(c)** 2-Methylpropanamide [1]

*Examiner insight:* the first two arrows can be credited together, but only if the first starts at the lone pair and the second starts at the double bond, not at the oxygen atom.

**7. (a)** Propan-1-ol [1]; (CH₃CO)₂O + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH → CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + CH₃COOH [1]

**(b)** The anhydride reaction goes to completion, whereas esterification of the acid is reversible and reaches equilibrium [1]

**(c)** CH₃CH₂COCl + 2CH₃NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₃ + CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻. Correct reagents and products [1]; two methylamine and the salt shown [1]

**8. (a)** (CH₃)₂CHCOCl + 2NH₃ → (CH₃)₂CHCONH₂ + NH₄Cl [1]

**(b)** n(acyl chloride) = 4.26 ÷ 106.5 = 0.0400 mol [1]; maximum mass of amide = 0.0400 × 87.0 = 3.48 g [1]; yield = 2.78 ÷ 3.48 × 100 = **79.9%** [1]

**(c)** 2 × 0.0400 = 0.0800 mol; 0.0800 × 17.0 = **1.36 g** [1]

**9. (a)** HOC₆H₄COOH + (CH₃CO)₂O → CH₃COOC₆H₄COOH + CH₃COOH [1]

**(b)** n(aspirin) = 1.00 × 10⁶ ÷ 180.0 = 5.56 × 10³ mol [1]; n(ethanoic acid) is the same, so mass = 5.56 × 10³ × 60.0 = 3.33 × 10⁵ g = **333 kg** [1]

**(c)** Hydrogen chloride is a toxic, corrosive gas [1]; it would have to be absorbed or neutralised safely, and it corrodes plant equipment, adding cost [1]

**10. (a)** Propanoyl chloride, CH₃CH₂COCl [1]

**(b)** S contains the three-carbon acyl group from P, so R supplies the other two carbons and is **ethylamine**, CH₃CH₂NH₂ [1]; S is **N-ethylpropanamide**, CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ [1]

**(c)** CH₃CH₂COCl + 2CH₃CH₂NH₂ → CH₃CH₂CONHCH₂CH₃ + CH₃CH₂NH₃⁺Cl⁻ [1]

**11. (a)** C₆H₅NH₂ + (CH₃CO)₂O → C₆H₅NHCOCH₃ + CH₃COOH [1]

**(b)** The crude product melts below 114 °C and over a wide range, so it contains impurities [1]; the recrystallised product melts sharply at, or very close to, the data-book value, so it is much purer [1]

**(c)** Ethanoic anhydride would be hydrolysed by water to ethanoic acid, wasting reagent and lowering the yield [1]

**(d)** The product is much more soluble in hot water than in cold water [1]

**12.** Any four [4]:

- Benzoic acid needs heating with a concentrated sulfuric acid catalyst;
- that reaction is reversible, so the yield is limited by the equilibrium and water is the by-product;
- benzoyl chloride reacts fast at room temperature and goes to completion;
- but it gives corrosive HCl gas as a by-product;
- benzoic anhydride needs warming but also goes to completion;
- its by-product is benzoic acid, which is less hazardous than HCl.

*Examiner insight:* a comparison needs a point about each route; three statements all about the acyl chloride score at most two.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calling the mechanism nucleophilic substitution.
- Arrows starting from atoms instead of lone pairs or bonds.
- One molecule of ammonia or amine per acyl chloride.
- HCl given as the by-product of an anhydride reaction.
- Melting point answers that mention only "lower" without "over a range".
