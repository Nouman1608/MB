These questions cover all four key questions of Depth Study D: The United States, 1919-41.

## Section A

**1.** Give **three** Republican government policies that helped the economy boom in the 1920s. **[3]**

**2.** State **two** reasons why the textile industry did not share in the boom. **[2]**

**3.** Identify **three** features of the "Roaring Twenties". **[3]**

**4.** Give **three** reasons why Prohibition was repealed in 1933. **[3]**

**5.** State **two** examples of religious intolerance in the 1920s. **[2]**

**6.** Identify **three** social consequences of the Wall Street Crash. **[3]**

**7.** Give **two** criticisms of the New Deal made by Huey Long. **[2]**

## Section B

**8.** Describe the activities of the Ku Klux Klan in the 1920s. **[4]**

**9.** Describe what Roosevelt did during the Hundred Days of 1933. **[4]**

**10.** Why did some industries prosper in the 1920s? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**11.** Why did Roosevelt win the presidential election of 1932? Explain your answer. **[6]**

**12.** "The roles of women changed greatly in the 1920s." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

**13.** "The character of the New Deal changed greatly after 1933." How far do you agree? Explain your answer. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Any three, 1 mark each: low income taxes, especially for the rich (1); tariffs such as Fordney-McCumber (1922)
protected American goods (1); laissez-faire, with little regulation of business (1); allowing business mergers and
trusts (1); low interest rates that encouraged borrowing (1); weak trade unions, which kept wages and costs down (1).
**[3]**

**2.** Any two, 1 mark each: competition from new artificial fibres such as rayon (1); shorter skirts used less cloth
(1); competition from cheaper producers in the South and abroad (1); overproduction and falling prices (1). **[2]**

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: jazz music (1); new dances such as the Charleston (1); flappers (1); the growth of
cinema and Hollywood stars (1); radio (1); speakeasies (1); the Harlem Renaissance (1); spectator sports and heroes
(1). **[3]**

**4.** Any three, 1 mark each: it had failed to stop drinking (1); it had led to organised crime and gang violence (1);
it corrupted police, judges and politicians (1); the government lost tax revenue it needed in the Depression (1);
legal brewing would create jobs (1); public opinion had turned against it (1); Roosevelt promised repeal (1). **[3]**

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: the Scopes Monkey Trial (1925) (1); laws in some southern states banning the teaching of
evolution (1); Ku Klux Klan attacks on Catholics and Jews (1); hostility to Catholic and Jewish immigrants in the
quota laws (1). **[2]**

**6.** Any three, 1 mark each: mass unemployment (1); homelessness and Hoovervilles (1); soup kitchens and bread
queues (1); evictions of families and farmers (1); men travelling the country on freight trains in search of work (1);
malnutrition and illness (1); falling birth and marriage rates (1).
**[3]**

**7.** Any two, 1 mark each: it did not do enough to help the poor (1); wealth should be taken from the rich and
shared out ("Share Our Wealth") (1); every family should be guaranteed an income (1); big business was still too
powerful (1). **[2]**

**8.** Any four, 1 mark each: it was revived in 1915 and grew rapidly in the early 1920s (1); members wore white robes
and hoods (1); it attacked Black Americans, Catholics, Jews and immigrants (1); it used lynchings, beatings, whippings
and cross burnings (1); it held mass marches, including in Washington (1); it had influence over politicians and police
in some states (1); it declined after a leader was convicted of murder in 1925 (1). **[4]**

**9.** Any four, 1 mark each: he declared a bank holiday and passed the Emergency Banking Act (1); only sound banks
reopened (1); he gave radio fireside chats to restore confidence (1); he set up the CCC to employ young men (1); the AAA
paid farmers to produce less (1); the TVA developed the Tennessee valley (1); the NIRA set up the NRA and public
works (1); federal money was given for relief of the unemployed (1); beer was legalised again (1). **[4]**

**10.** Levels. Level 4 (6): explains two reasons. Level 3 (4-5): explains one reason; 5 for a developed explanation
with specific support. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes reasons. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative
content: **mass production**, because the assembly line cut the cost of a Model T so more people could buy cars, and
the car industry boosted steel, rubber, glass and oil; **electricity**, because more homes were connected, so demand
rose for radios, refrigerators and vacuum cleaners; **credit**, because hire purchase let people buy goods they could
not afford outright; **advertising**, because it created demand for new products; **government policy**, because
tariffs protected new industries from foreign competition and low taxes left people with more to spend. **[6]**

**11.** Levels as for Question 10. Indicative content: **Hoover's unpopularity**, because he seemed to do too little
as unemployment rose, and shanty towns were named after him; **the Bonus Army**, because troops driving out
unarmed veterans in 1932 made Hoover look heartless; **Roosevelt's personality and campaign**, because he travelled
widely, appeared confident and promised a "New Deal" for the American people; **Roosevelt's record**, because as
Governor of New York he had already used state money to help the unemployed; **the promise to end Prohibition**,
because it was popular and offered jobs and taxes. **[6]**

**12.** Levels. Level 5 (10): explains both sides and reaches a supported judgement on "how far". Level 4 (7-9):
explains both sides (7 for one explanation on each side, 1 more for each additional explanation). Level 3 (4-6):
explains one side. Level 2 (2-3): identifies or describes. Level 1 (1): general comment. Indicative content.
**Agree:** women gained the vote in 1920; flappers in the cities wore short skirts, smoked and drank in public and went
out without chaperones; more women worked as typists, telephonists and shop assistants; labour-saving devices
reduced housework for some; film stars presented new models of independent women. **Disagree:** most women,
especially in rural areas and small towns, kept traditional roles; few women entered politics; women were paid less
than men and many had to give up work on marriage; many older women and church groups opposed the flappers; Black and
immigrant women faced poverty and discrimination. **Judgement:** for example, change was real for young, white,
middle-class women in the cities but limited for most others. **[10]**

**13.** Levels as for Question 12. Indicative content. **Agree:** the First New Deal concentrated on emergency relief
and recovery, such as saving the banks; from 1935 the Second New Deal aimed at lasting reform, with Social Security
and the Wagner Act; it moved to the left, taxing the rich more and supporting unions, partly in response to critics
like Huey Long; the Supreme Court's rulings forced new approaches after the NIRA and AAA were struck down. **Disagree:**
the aims of relief, recovery and reform remained the same throughout; job creation schemes continued, as the WPA
followed the CCC and public works; Roosevelt remained committed to saving capitalism, not replacing it; spending was
still limited, as the cuts of 1937 show. **Judgement:** for example, the methods and emphasis changed considerably in
1935, but Roosevelt's basic aim of saving the American economy did not. **[10]**
