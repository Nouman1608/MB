## Equations

| Quantity | Equation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Wave speed | v = fλ | Use m/s, Hz and m |
| Period | T = 1/f | T in s |
| Refractive index | n = sin i / sin r | i in the air, r in the material, both from the normal |
| Critical angle | n = 1 / sin c | Rearrange: sin c = 1/n |
| Linear magnification | image length / object length | No unit |
| Echo or sonar | distance = v × t / 2 | Halve: the pulse goes there and back |
| Speed of sound by echo | v = 2d / t | d is the distance to the reflector |

## 3.1 General properties of waves

Definitions to recite.

- Waves transfer energy without transferring matter.
- Frequency: the number of wavelengths that pass a point per unit time (Hz).
- Wavelength: the distance between two consecutive, identical points, such as two consecutive crests.
- Amplitude: the maximum distance from the mean position.
- Wavefront: a line joining points at the same stage of vibration.
- Crest (peak) and trough: highest and lowest points of a transverse wave.

| Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|
| Vibration at right angles to the direction of energy transfer | Vibration parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Electromagnetic radiation, surface water waves, seismic S-waves (secondary) | Sound waves, seismic P-waves (primary) |
| Crests and troughs | Compressions and rarefactions |

Wave motion: shaking a rope or spring sends a pulse along it, and each part only vibrates about its rest position. Ripples in water show the same: a floating object bobs and stays put.

Three wave behaviours.

- Reflection at a plane surface: the wave bounces back.
- Refraction: a change of direction caused by a change of speed. The frequency stays fixed, so the wavelength changes too.
- Diffraction through a gap: spreading out. Most when the gap is about one wavelength. Less when the gap is much wider than the wavelength. A longer wavelength spreads more. At an edge a longer wavelength also bends round more.

Ripple tank, one line each.

- Reflection: a straight barrier turns the wavefronts back.
- Refraction: shallow water slows the waves, shortens the wavelength and changes direction.
- Gap: a narrow gap spreads the wavefronts into circles.
- Edge: the wavefronts curve round the end of a barrier.

**Method: v = fλ.**

1. Write down the two known quantities with units.
2. Convert units first (cm to m, kHz to Hz).
3. Rearrange: v = fλ, f = v/λ, λ = v/f.
4. Give the unit.

## 3.2 Light

### Reflection

- The normal is at right angles to the surface. Angles of incidence and reflection are measured from it.
- Law of reflection: the two angles are equal.
- Plane mirror image: matches the object in size, sits behind the mirror at the object's distance in front, and is virtual.
- Experiment: ray box, mirror, paper. Mark the ray, join the points, measure from the normal, repeat.
- Image position: pins in line with the image from two directions. The lines cross at the image.

### Refraction

- Slower medium: ray bends towards the normal. Faster medium: away from the normal.
- Light through a rectangular block emerges parallel to its original path, shifted sideways. A triangular prism bends the ray towards its base.
- Experiments: trace the ray through a block on paper at several angles. For internal reflection, aim a ray at the flat face of a semicircular block through the curved face and raise the angle until the refracted ray runs along the face (angle c), then beyond it.
- Total internal reflection needs both: light travelling in the more dense medium towards the less dense, and i greater than c.
- At i equal to c, the refracted ray travels along the boundary.
- Optical fibres use total internal reflection. Used in telecommunications. Advantages: carry a large amount of information and avoid electrical interference. Use these as points, then add the context asked in the question.

**Method: refractive index.**

1. Is the ray going into the material (use n = sin i / sin r) or out (use n = sin r / sin i with r now in air)?
2. Put both angles measured from the normal.
3. Take sin, divide, then take the inverse sin.
4. Check: entering a denser medium, r is smaller than i.

### Thin lenses

- Converging lens: parallel beam brought to the principal focus. Diverging lens: parallel beam spreads out.
- Focal length: lens centre to principal focus. Principal axis: line through the lens centre at right angles to it.
- Ray diagram: one ray parallel to the axis, through the focus after the lens; one ray through the centre, straight on.

| Converging lens, object at | Image |
|---|---|
| More than 2F away | Inverted, real, diminished |
| Between F and 2F | Inverted, real, magnified |
| Less than F away (magnifying glass) | Upright, virtual, magnified |

A diverging lens always gives a virtual, upright, smaller image.

- Real image: formed by converging rays. Virtual image: formed by diverging rays.
- Eye diagrams: a normal eye brings parallel rays to a focus on the retina. In short sight they focus in front of the retina, so a diverging lens spreads them first. In long sight the focus lies behind the retina, so a converging lens is used.

## 3.3 Electromagnetic spectrum

Increasing frequency, decreasing wavelength: radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. Speed: 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s in a vacuum and about the same in air.

| Region | Uses to learn |
|---|---|
| Radio | Radio and television, astronomy |
| Microwave | Satellite television, mobile phones, Bluetooth, ovens |
| Infrared | Appliances, remote controllers, intruder alarms, thermal imaging, optical fibres |
| Visible | Photography, vision |
| Ultraviolet | Security marking, counterfeit notes, sterilising water |
| X-rays | Medical imaging, security scanners, killing cancer cells, cracks in metal |
| Gamma | Treating cancer, sterilising food and equipment, cracks in metal |

Dangers. Excess exposure heats soft tissue and burns. Ionising radiation: ultraviolet (skin cancer, cataracts); X-rays and gamma rays (cell mutation, cancer).

## 3.4 Sound

- Made by vibrating sources. Longitudinal, with compressions and rarefactions.
- Audible range for humans: 20 Hz to 20 000 Hz.
- Cannot travel in a vacuum because there are no particles to pass on the vibration. Demonstration: bell in an evacuated jar.
- Amplitude gives loudness. Frequency gives pitch.
- Timbre: the oscilloscope trace shape differs between sources even at the same pitch and loudness.
- Echo: reflection of sound. Show by directing sound at a hard board and detecting reflection.
- Speed in air: approximately 330 to 350 m/s. Solids faster than liquids, liquids faster than gases.
- Ultrasound: frequency above 20 kHz. Uses: cleaning, prenatal and other medical scanning, sonar.

**Method: speed of sound in air.** Measure the distance to a wall with a long tape. Clap and time the echo with a stopwatch. v = 2d/t. Repeat and average.

**Method: echo or sonar depth.** Distance travelled = v × t. Divide by 2 for the one-way distance.

## Must-know distinctions

- Loudness is amplitude; pitch is frequency.
- Refraction changes speed and wavelength; frequency stays the same.
- Real image: can be caught on a screen. Virtual image: cannot.
- Critical angle belongs to the dense-to-less-dense direction; it does not exist going the other way.
- Ionising: ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays. Heating effect: microwaves and infrared.

## Quick self-test

1. Water waves have a frequency of 12 Hz and a wavelength of 0.25 m. Find the speed.
2. A note has a frequency of 250 Hz. Find the period.
3. A ray in air strikes glass (n = 1.5), making 30° with the normal. Find the angle of refraction.
4. A microwave has a wavelength of 0.12 m. Find its frequency, using 3.0 × 10⁸ m/s.
5. An image is 9.0 cm tall and the object is 2.0 cm tall. Find the magnification.
6. A sonar pulse returns after 0.40 s. The speed in water is 1500 m/s. Find the depth.
7. A ray goes from air into a plastic. The angle of incidence is 60° and the angle of refraction is 35°. Find n.
8. A ray makes 62° with a plane mirror. Find the angle of reflection.
9. Calculate the critical angle of a glass with n = 1.5.
10. Sound at 170 Hz travels at 340 m/s. Find the wavelength.
11. State why sound cannot pass through a vacuum.
12. Give the property of a sound wave that sets its pitch.

### Answers

1. v = fλ = 12 × 0.25 = **3.0 m/s**.
2. T = 1/250 = **0.004 s**.
3. sin r = sin 30° / 1.5 = 0.333, so r = **19.5°**.
4. f = v/λ = 3.0 × 10⁸ / 0.12 = **2.5 × 10⁹ Hz**.
5. 9.0 / 2.0 = **4.5**.
6. 1500 × 0.40 / 2 = **300 m**.
7. n = sin 60° / sin 35° = **1.51**.
8. Angle of incidence = 90 − 62 = **28°**, so the angle of reflection is 28°.
9. sin c = 1/n = 0.667, giving c = **41.8°**.
10. λ = 340 / 170 = **2.0 m**.
11. There are no particles to vibrate and pass the energy on.
12. The frequency.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the surface rather than the normal as the line to measure angles from.
- Leaving out the halving step in echo and sonar questions.
- Quoting frequency, wavelength or speed without converting kHz, MHz or cm first.
- Writing that sound "goes faster in air than in water".
- Describing the longitudinal wave as "up and down".
- Stating total internal reflection without both conditions.
- Mixing up the order of the electromagnetic spectrum with the order of colours in dispersion.
- Saying a virtual image is "not there" instead of saying the rays only appear to come from it.
