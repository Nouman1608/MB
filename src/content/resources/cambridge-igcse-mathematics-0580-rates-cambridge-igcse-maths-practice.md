These questions cover subtopic 1.12 Rates of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The content is the same for Core and Extended, so every question suits both tiers. As in the exam, formulas for density and pressure are given, but you must know the formula for speed. Each question is labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator).

## Section A

**1.** (non-calculator) A plumber charges a call-out fee of $45 plus $38 per hour. Find the total charge for a job lasting 2½ hours. **[2]**

**2.** (calculator) A hose delivers water at 18 litres per minute. An empty pond holds 1.35 m³. Find the time taken to fill the pond, in hours and minutes. (1 m³ = 1000 litres.) **[2]**

**3.** (non-calculator)

**(a)** Write 108 km/h in m/s. **[1]**

**(b)** Write 12 m/s in km/h. **[1]**

**4.** (calculator) A region has an area of 650 km² and a population density of 420 people per km². Population density = population ÷ area. Find the population of the region. **[2]**

**5.** (non-calculator) A runner completes 21 km in 1 hour 45 minutes. Find the runner's average speed in km/h. **[2]**

**6.** (calculator) A force of 240 N acts on an area of 0.016 m². Pressure = force ÷ area. Find the pressure, in N/m². **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (calculator) Car A uses 6.2 litres of fuel per 100 km. Car B travels 17 km per litre.

**(a)** Which car is more fuel efficient? Show your working. **[3]**

**(b)** Fuel costs $1.70 per litre. Find the cost of the fuel car A uses on a 450 km journey. **[2]**

**8.** (calculator) A block of wood is a cuboid measuring 40 cm by 15 cm by 8 cm. The wood has density 0.65 g/cm³. Density = mass ÷ volume.

**(a)** Find the mass of the block in kilograms. **[3]**

**(b)** Another block of the same wood has a mass of 1.17 kg. Find its volume in cm³. **[2]**

**9.** (calculator) A tank holds 360 litres. Tap A on its own fills the empty tank in 30 minutes. Tap B delivers 8 litres per minute. Both taps are turned on together when the tank is empty. How long does the tank take to fill? **[3]**

**10.** (calculator) Ama drives 85 km in 1 hour 10 minutes. She stops for 20 minutes. She then drives 115 km at an average speed of 92 km/h.

**(a)** Find the time taken for the 115 km part of the journey, in hours and minutes. **[2]**

**(b)** Find her average speed for the whole journey, including the stop. **[3]**

**11.** (calculator) A cheetah can run at 31 m/s. A car is travelling at 105 km/h.

**(a)** Which is faster? Show your working. **[2]**

**(b)** How long does the cheetah take to run 200 m at this speed? **[1]**

**12.** (calculator) A crate in the shape of a cuboid measures 0.4 m by 0.6 m by 1.2 m and has a weight of 360 N. It can rest on any of its faces. Pressure = force ÷ area.

**(a)** Find the greatest pressure the crate can exert on the floor. **[3]**

**(b)** Find the least pressure the crate can exert on the floor. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** 38 × 2.5 = 95 [1]; 45 + 95 = **$140** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 1.35 m³ = 1350 litres, and 1350 ÷ 18 = 75 minutes [1]; **1 hour 15 minutes** [1] **[2]**

**3. (a)** 108 ÷ 3.6 = **30 m/s** [1]

**(b)** 12 × 3.6 = **43.2 km/h** [1] **[2]**

**4.** population = 420 × 650 [1] = **273 000** [1] **[2]**

**5.** 1 h 45 min = 1.75 h [1]; 21 ÷ 1.75 = **12 km/h** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 1.45 hours is not 1 hour 45 minutes. Using 1.45 gives 14.5 km/h and loses both marks.

**6.** 240 ÷ 0.016 [1] = **15 000 N/m²** [1] **[2]**

**7. (a)** Car B: 100 ÷ 17 = 5.88 litres per 100 km (3 s.f.) [1]; this is less than 6.2 litres per 100 km [1]; so **car B** is more efficient [1]

**(b)** 4.5 × 6.2 = 27.9 litres [1]; 27.9 × 1.70 = **$47.43** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the two cars are described in different forms, so convert one before comparing. Converting car A instead (100 ÷ 6.2 = 16.1 km per litre, which is less than 17) is equally valid.

**8. (a)** Volume = 40 × 15 × 8 = 4800 cm³ [1]; mass = 0.65 × 4800 = 3120 g [1]; **3.12 kg** [1]

**(b)** 1.17 kg = 1170 g [1]; volume = 1170 ÷ 0.65 = **1800 cm³** [1] **[5]**

**9.** Tap A rate = 360 ÷ 30 = 12 litres per minute [1]; combined rate = 12 + 8 = 20 litres per minute [1]; 360 ÷ 20 = **18 minutes** [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* times do not add or average. Combine the two rates first, then divide the volume by the combined rate.

**10. (a)** 115 ÷ 92 = 1.25 hours [1]; **1 hour 15 minutes** [1]

**(b)** Total distance = 85 + 115 = 200 km [1]; total time = 70 + 20 + 75 = 165 minutes = 2.75 hours [1]; 200 ÷ 2.75 = **72.7 km/h** (3 s.f.) [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the 20-minute stop is part of the total time. Leaving it out gives 200 ÷ 2.4167 = 82.8 km/h.

**11. (a)** 31 × 3.6 = 111.6 km/h [1]; 111.6 > 105, so the **cheetah** is faster [1]

**(b)** 200 ÷ 31 = **6.45 s** (3 s.f.) [1] **[3]**

**12. (a)** The greatest pressure is on the smallest face [1]; smallest area = 0.4 × 0.6 = 0.24 m² [1]; 360 ÷ 0.24 = **1500 N/m²** [1]

**(b)** Largest area = 0.6 × 1.2 = 0.72 m², so 360 ÷ 0.72 = **500 N/m²** [1] **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Typing a time in hours and minutes as a decimal, as in Questions 5 and 10.
- Comparing rates given in different forms without converting, as in Questions 7 and 11.
- Forgetting to convert m³ to litres or kg to g before dividing.
- Adding times or averaging them instead of combining rates.
- Leaving out stopped time from an average speed.
- Choosing the largest face for the greatest pressure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to learn the formulas for density and pressure?**
No. The 0580 syllabus says the formulas for pressure, density and population density will be given in the question. You must know the speed, distance and time formula.

**How do I check a rate answer?**
Look at the unit. If the question asks for N/m², your working should show a number of newtons divided by a number of square metres.

**Why is litres per 100 km "better" when it is lower?**
It measures fuel used, so a lower value means less fuel for the same distance. In km per litre, a higher value is better.
