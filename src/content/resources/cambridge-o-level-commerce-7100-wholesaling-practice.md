## Section A

**1.** Define *wholesaler*. **[2]**

**2.** What is meant by *breaking bulk*? **[1]**

**3.** State **three** functions of a wholesaler. **[3]**

**4.** State **two** features of a cash and carry wholesaler. **[2]**

**5.** Identify **two** benefits a voluntary chain gives to an independent retailer. **[2]**

**6.** Six manufacturers each supply 40 shops directly. Calculate how many transactions are saved if all goods pass through one wholesaler. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** Explain **two** ways in which the wholesaler is important to a manufacturer of tinned tomatoes. **[4]**

**8.** Explain how a wholesaler helps to keep supplies and prices steady for a seasonal product such as onions. **[3]**

**9.** Sana runs a small general store. A delivered wholesaler charges $9.50 a case with free delivery and 30 days' credit. A cash and carry charges $8.80 a case, and Sana's transport costs $30 per trip.
(a) Calculate the cost of buying 60 cases from each supplier.
(b) Recommend which supplier Sana should use, giving a reason. **[6]**

**10.** Explain the difference between a voluntary chain and a multiple chain store. **[4]**

**11.** Explain **two** reasons why large supermarket chains often buy directly from manufacturers instead of through wholesalers. **[4]**

**12.** "Independent wholesalers will soon disappear." Discuss this statement. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** A business that buys goods in large quantities from manufacturers [1] and sells them in smaller quantities, usually to retailers [1].

**2.** Splitting large quantities bought from manufacturers into smaller amounts that retailers can buy [1].

**3.** Any three, 1 mark each: breaking bulk; warehousing; bearing risk; giving credit or paying manufacturers promptly; transport and delivery; offering a wide range; giving information and advice; preparing goods for sale; keeping supplies steady [1] [1] [1].

**4.** Any two, 1 mark each: self-service; buyers pay immediately; buyers transport the goods themselves; no credit; no delivery; lower prices [1] [1].

**5.** Any two, 1 mark each: lower prices from the group's bulk buying; a recognised shared name; joint advertising and promotions; help with shop layout or training [1] [1].

**6.** Without a wholesaler: 6 x 40 = 240 [1]. With a wholesaler: 6 + 40 = 46 [1]. Saving = 240 − 46 = **194** transactions [1].

**7.** Any two, 2 marks each (function, then link to the tomato manufacturer): the wholesaler places **large orders**, so the manufacturer can run long production lines and lower its cost per tin; it provides **storage**, so the manufacturer does not need a large warehouse for tins after canning; it **bears risk**, so if tins are damaged or do not sell the loss falls on the wholesaler; it **reaches many retailers**, so the manufacturer does not have to deliver to thousands of small shops; it gives **market information** about which sizes and brands sell best [1] [1] [1] [1].

**8.** Onions are harvested mainly at one time of year [1]. The wholesaler buys and stores them when supply is plentiful [1] and releases them gradually when supply is short, so retailers can buy all year and prices change less sharply [1].

**9.** (a) Delivered wholesaler: 60 x $9.50 [1] = **$570** [1]. Cash and carry: 60 x $8.80 = $528 [1]; $528 + $30 = **$558** [1]. (b) Recommendation, for example: the cash and carry, because it saves $12 [1]; but if Sana is short of cash she should use the delivered wholesaler, because 30 days' credit lets her sell the goods before paying [1]. (Either recommendation is credited if justified.)

**10.** In a **voluntary chain**, each shop is independently owned [1], but the owners agree to buy mainly from one wholesaler and share a name and promotions [1]. In a **multiple chain store**, all the branches are owned by one company [1] and are managed centrally, with decisions taken at head office [1].

**11.** Any two, 2 marks each: they buy in **very large quantities**, so they can obtain the low prices a wholesaler would get and keep its profit margin themselves; they have their **own regional warehouses and lorries**, so they do not need the wholesaler's storage or delivery; buying direct gives **more control** over product specifications and own-brand goods; direct contact means **faster information** about supply problems [1] [1] [1] [1].

**12.** **For the statement:** large chains buy direct from manufacturers and run their own warehouses [1]; manufacturers increasingly sell direct to consumers online, cutting out intermediaries [1]; online platforms deliver small quantities to small shops [1]. **Against the statement:** thousands of small retailers still cannot buy by the pallet and rely on breaking bulk and credit [1]; cash and carry keeps costs low, so prices compete with direct buying [1]; voluntary chains give independent shops buying power and a guaranteed customer base for the wholesaler [1]; manufacturers still need to reach many small shops [1]. **Judgement:** independent wholesalers are unlikely to disappear while small retailers exist, though they will survive mainly by adapting through cash and carry, voluntary chains and online ordering [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 1: saying the wholesaler sells to consumers.
- Question 6: giving 46 as the answer instead of the saving.
- Question 7: listing retailer benefits when the question is about the manufacturer.
- Question 9: forgetting to add Sana's $30 transport cost.
- Question 12: arguing one side only, or forgetting the two survival methods named in the syllabus.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is either recommendation accepted in Question 9?** The figures favour cash and carry, but credit has real value for a shop short of cash. A justified judgement earns the mark whichever way it goes.

**Is "online ordering" a syllabus survival method?** No. The syllabus names cash and carry and voluntary chains; online ordering can be added as extra evidence in a discuss answer.
