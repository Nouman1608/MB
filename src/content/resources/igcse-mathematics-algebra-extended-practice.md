## Questions

**1.** *(Extended)* Simplify fully (x² − 9) / (x² + x − 6). **[3]**

**2.** *(Extended)* Write 3/(x + 2) − 2/(x − 1) as a single fraction in its simplest form. **[3]**

**3.** *(Extended)* Solve the equation 2/(x + 1) + 5/x = 3. Show all your working and give your answers correct to 2 decimal places. **[4]**

**4.** *(Extended)* f(x) = 4x − 3 and g(x) = x² + 2.

**(a)** Find f⁻¹(x). **[2]**

**(b)** Find gf(x), giving your answer in the form ax² + bx + c. **[2]**

**5.** *(Extended)* y is inversely proportional to the square of x. When x = 2, y = 12. Find y when x = 5. **[3]**

**6.** *(Extended)* A curve has equation y = x³ − 6x² + 9x + 1. Find the coordinates of its two turning points and determine which one is a maximum. **[5]**

**7.** *(Extended)* Make x the subject of y = (3x + 2)/(x − 4). **[4]**

**8.** *(Extended)* f(x) = 2x − 1 and g(x) = x². Solve the equation gf(x) = fg(x). **[5]**

**9.** *(Extended)* y is directly proportional to the square root of x. When x = 9, y = 12. Find x when y = 20. **[4]**

**10.** *(Extended)* A curve has equation y = 2x³ + 6/x.

**(a)** Find dy/dx. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the gradient of the curve at the point where x = 2. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the coordinates of the points on the curve where the gradient is zero. **[2]**

**11.** *(Extended)* A coach travels 36 km at x km/h, then another 36 km at (x + 3) km/h. The two stages together take 5 hours.

**(a)** Show that 5x² − 57x − 108 = 0. **[3]**

**(b)** Solve 5x² − 57x − 108 = 0 using the quadratic formula. Give your answer for the coach's first speed correct to 2 decimal places, and explain why you reject the other solution. **[3]**

**(c)** Use your answer to (b) to find the time taken for the second 36 km, in minutes, to the nearest minute. **[2]**

**12.** *(Extended)* A box with no lid is made from a square sheet of card of side 24 cm. A square of side x cm is cut from each corner and the sides are folded up.

**(a)** Show that the volume of the box is V = 4x³ − 96x² + 576x cm³. **[2]**

**(b)** Find dV/dx. **[2]**

**(c)** Find the value of x that gives a turning point, and explain why the other solution of dV/dx = 0 cannot be used. **[3]**

**(d)** Find the maximum volume. **[1]**

**(e)** Use the second derivative to show that this turning point is a maximum. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Numerator: x² − 9 = (x − 3)(x + 3) [1]. Denominator: x² + x − 6 = (x + 3)(x − 2) [1]. Cancel (x + 3): **(x − 3)/(x − 2)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Only whole brackets cancel. Candidates who cancel x² or the 9 and 6 lose all three marks, so factorise first.

**2.** Common denominator (x + 2)(x − 1) [1]. Numerator: 3(x − 1) − 2(x + 2) = 3x − 3 − 2x − 4 = x − 7 [1]. Answer: **(x − 7)/((x + 2)(x − 1))** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The usual slip is writing −2(x + 2) as −2x + 4. The minus multiplies every term in the bracket, so use a bracket around the whole numerator until you expand.

**3.** Multiply through by x(x + 1): 2x + 5(x + 1) = 3x(x + 1) [1]. So 7x + 5 = 3x² + 3x, giving 3x² − 4x − 5 = 0 [1]. Quadratic formula: x = (4 ± √76)/6 [1]. **x = 2.12 or x = −0.79** [1].

*Examiner insight:* In b² − 4ac, the c term is −5, so −4 × 3 × (−5) = +60. Writing 16 − 60 gives a negative discriminant and no marks for the roots. Show the formula with substituted values.

**4.** **(a)** Let y = 4x − 3, so x = (y + 3)/4 [1]. **f⁻¹(x) = (x + 3)/4** [1].

**(b)** gf(x) = g(4x − 3) = (4x − 3)² + 2 [1] = 16x² − 24x + 9 + 2 = **16x² − 24x + 11** [1].

*Examiner insight:* gf means apply f first. Squaring 4x − 3 as 16x² + 9 (missing the middle term) is the commonest way to lose the second mark in (b).

**5.** y = k/x² [1]. 12 = k/2², so k = 48 [1]. When x = 5, y = 48/25 = **1.92** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The method mark depends on the form y = k/x². Writing y = k/x gives k = 24 and y = 4.8, which scores nothing after the first line.

**6.** dy/dx = 3x² − 12x + 9 [1]. Set 3x² − 12x + 9 = 0, so 3(x − 1)(x − 3) = 0 and x = 1 or x = 3 [1]. When x = 1, y = 5; when x = 3, y = 1, so the turning points are **(1, 5) and (3, 1)** [1]. d²y/dx² = 6x − 12 [1]. At x = 1 this is −6 < 0, so **(1, 5) is the maximum** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Finding the x-values is only part of the question. You must substitute back into y to get coordinates, and you must give a reason (sign of the second derivative) for the maximum.

**7.** Multiply by (x − 4): y(x − 4) = 3x + 2 [1]. Expand: xy − 4y = 3x + 2 [1]. Collect x terms: xy − 3x = 4y + 2, so x(y − 3) = 4y + 2 [1]. **x = (4y + 2)/(y − 3)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* The final two marks need x on one side only, then factorised. If x appears on both sides of your answer, you have not yet made it the subject.

**8.** f(g(x)) = 2x² − 1 [1]. g(f(x)) = (2x − 1)² = 4x² − 4x + 1 [1]. Equate: 2x² − 1 = 4x² − 4x + 1 [1]. Rearrange: 2x² − 4x + 2 = 0, so (x − 1)² = 0 [1]. **x = 1** (one repeated solution) [1].

*Examiner insight:* A repeated root is a single answer. Writing x = 1 or x = 1, or hunting for a second value, shows the working was not understood. Check by substituting: both sides equal 1.

**9.** y = k√x [1]. 12 = k × 3, so k = 4 [1]. 20 = 4√x, so √x = 5 [1]. **x = 25** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Square the root last. Answering x = 5 stops one step early and loses the final mark.

**10.** **(a)** Write y = 2x³ + 6x⁻¹ [1]. **dy/dx = 6x² − 6x⁻²** [1].

**(b)** At x = 2, gradient = 6 × 4 − 6 ÷ 4 = 24 − 1.5 = **22.5** [1].

**(c)** Set 6x² − 6/x² = 0, so x⁴ = 1 and x = 1 or x = −1 [1]. The points are **(1, 8) and (−1, −8)** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Rewrite 6/x as 6x⁻¹ before differentiating. Differentiating 6/x as 6/1 or leaving it alone is the standard error. In (c), substitute back into the original curve and not into dy/dx to get the y-values.

**11.** **(a)** Time = distance ÷ speed, so 36/x + 36/(x + 3) = 5 [1]. Multiply by x(x + 3): 36(x + 3) + 36x = 5x(x + 3) [1]. 72x + 108 = 5x² + 15x, so **5x² − 57x − 108 = 0** [1].

**(b)** x = (57 ± √(57² + 4 × 5 × 108))/10 = (57 ± √5409)/10 [1]. **x = 13.05** or x = −1.65 [1]. A speed cannot be negative, so reject x = −1.65 [1].

**(c)** Second speed = 13.05 + 3 = 16.05 km/h [1]. Time = 36 ÷ 16.05 × 60 = **135 minutes** (to the nearest minute) [1].

*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question every line must be visible, including the multiplication by x(x + 3). Jumping to the final equation forfeits the method marks. In (b), always say why a root is rejected.

**12.** **(a)** The base is (24 − 2x) by (24 − 2x) and the height is x [1]. V = x(24 − 2x)² = x(576 − 96x + 4x²) = **4x³ − 96x² + 576x** [1].

**(b)** **dV/dx = 12x² − 192x + 576** (12x² − 192x [1], + 576 [1]).

**(c)** Set 12x² − 192x + 576 = 0, so x² − 16x + 48 = 0 and (x − 4)(x − 12) = 0 [1]. **x = 4** [1]. x = 12 gives a base of 24 − 24 = 0, so no box exists (the sheet is only 24 cm wide) [1].

**(d)** V = 4 × (24 − 8)² = 4 × 256 = **1024 cm³** [1].

**(e)** d²V/dx² = 24x − 192 [1]. At x = 4 this is −96, which is negative, so the turning point is a **maximum** [1].

*Examiner insight:* Part (c) needs a reason in words for rejecting x = 12. A bare "x = 4" with no comment loses the last mark, and the real exam would also expect units (cm³) in (d).

## Where marks are usually lost

- Cancelling terms rather than factors in algebraic fractions.
- Sign errors when a subtracted fraction has a bracket in its numerator.
- Mixing up the order in composite functions: gf(x) means f first, then g.
- Using the wrong type of proportion (square, root, inverse) from the wording.
- Stopping at k, or at √x, instead of answering the question that was asked.
- Putting the wrong sign into b² − 4ac when c is negative.
- Not rewriting terms such as 6/x as 6x⁻¹ before differentiating.
- Finding the x-value of a turning point but not substituting back for y, or not justifying maximum or minimum.
- Skipping lines in "show that" questions, or forgetting to reject an impossible solution in a context question.
