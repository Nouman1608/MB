This guide covers **Paper 3**, the third and final paper in Pearson Edexcel GCE A-Level Urdu (9UR0),
for teaching from 2018. Where [Paper 1](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-reading/) tests
translation into English, reading comprehension and independent-research writing, and
[Paper 2](/resources/a-level-edexcel-urdu-translation-and-works/) tests translation into Urdu and
response to prescribed works, Paper 3 combines listening with reading and writing in a single,
two-section paper -- completing the qualification's coverage of all four language skills across the
three papers together.

## Where this fits in 9UR0

Paper 3 (9UR0/03) is 2 hours 15 minutes long and worth 60 marks, split into two sections: Section A,
Listening Comprehension (recommended 45 minutes), and Section B, Listening, Reading and Writing
(recommended 1 hour 30 minutes). Unlike Papers 1 and 2, which each test translation as a distinct,
separately-timed skill, Paper 3 integrates listening with reading and writing within Section B rather
than testing them as fully separate components -- a structural difference worth understanding before
you plan your exam-day timing.

## Section A: Listening Comprehension

Tests the ability to understand spoken Urdu and respond to questions based on what has been heard,
without a translation or extended-writing component attached. Because this section is entirely
Urdu-in, Urdu-out (understanding spoken Urdu and answering in the language the question requires,
without an English-translation step), revision should prioritise genuine listening practice with
authentic or course-level Urdu audio -- news broadcasts, interviews, and spoken narrative -- over
reading-based revision alone, since listening comprehension speed and reading comprehension speed
develop through different kinds of practice.

## Section B: Listening, Reading and Writing

Combines all three skills within a single section, reflecting how real-world language use rarely
separates listening, reading and writing into isolated tasks. Because this section carries the larger
share of the recommended time (1 hour 30 minutes out of 2 hours 15 minutes total), and combines three
skills rather than testing one at a time, time-management practice matters as much here as content
revision -- candidates who have not rehearsed moving between listening, reading and writing tasks
within one timed sitting often lose time reorienting between skills that a well-rehearsed candidate
does not.

## How Paper 3 complements Papers 1 and 2

Because Paper 1 tests translation into English alongside reading comprehension and independent
research writing, and Paper 2 tests translation into Urdu alongside response to prescribed literary
texts and films, Paper 3's combination of listening with reading and writing completes the
qualification's coverage of receptive skills (listening, reading) and productive skills (writing) that
the other two papers test through translation and works-response tasks instead. A candidate strong at
translation (Papers 1 and 2) is not automatically strong at real-time listening comprehension (Paper
3) -- these draw on genuinely different processing speed and skill, so revision time should not
default entirely toward translation practice on the assumption it transfers directly to listening
performance.

## Building listening stamina and technique

Because Section A is entirely listening-based with no translation buffer to fall back on, practise
listening to Urdu audio of varying speed and register (formal news-style Urdu versus more
conversational spoken Urdu) rather than only the single register your course materials happen to use.
A reliable technique for listening comprehension questions is to read the question before the audio
plays wherever the exam format allows it, so you know what specific information to listen for rather
than trying to process and retain everything you hear before knowing what will be asked.

## Approaching Section B's combined tasks

Because Section B tests listening, reading and writing together, practise the specific skill of taking
accurate notes from a listening or reading source and then using those notes to construct a written
response, rather than treating note-taking and writing as two disconnected stages. Since this section
carries the majority of the paper's marks and time, allocate practice time proportionately -- a
revision plan that spends most of its time on Section A's shorter listening-only component and treats
Section B as an afterthought is working against the paper's actual weighting.

## How to approach it

Practise full timed sittings of Paper 3 specifically, not just isolated listening or writing drills in
isolation, since the paper's real difficulty lies partly in managing 2 hours 15 minutes across two
sections with different skill demands, not only in any single skill in isolation. Because Section A has
no reading or writing component to fall back on if a listening passage is missed, build the habit of
staying with the current question rather than dwelling on a missed detail -- a listening exam
continues regardless of whether you are ready for the next section, unlike a reading passage you can
return to.

## Self-test

1. What two sections make up Paper 3, and what is the recommended time for each?
2. How many marks is Paper 3 worth in total, and what is its total duration?
3. What genuinely distinguishes Paper 3 from Papers 1 and 2 in terms of which skills are tested
   together?
4. Why might a candidate who is strong at translation still need dedicated Paper 3 preparation?
5. Name one specific technique for approaching Section A's listening questions.

**Answers:** 1. Section A: Listening Comprehension (recommended 45 minutes); Section B: Listening,
Reading and Writing (recommended 1 hour 30 minutes). 2. 60 marks, over a total duration of 2 hours 15
minutes. 3. Papers 1 and 2 each test translation (into English, and into Urdu respectively) alongside
one other skill; Paper 3 instead combines listening with reading and writing, without a translation
component, and integrates listening, reading and writing together within Section B rather than testing
them as fully separate components. 4. Because translation proficiency and real-time listening
comprehension draw on different processing skills and speeds -- strong performance on Papers 1 and 2's
translation tasks does not automatically transfer to Paper 3's listening demands. 5. Any valid
technique, e.g. reading the question before the audio plays wherever the format allows, so you know
what specific information to listen for.
