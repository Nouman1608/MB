**These notes condense key question 2.1 of Cambridge O Level History 2147: "Was the Treaty of Versailles fair?"** It
is the Paper 2 prescribed topic for June 2026, so learn the terms precisely and know who said what in 1919. For the
full explanation, use the [Treaty of Versailles study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-treaty-of-versailles/);
for exam practice, use the [Treaty of Versailles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-o-level-world-history-2147-treaty-of-versailles-practice/).

## Condensed recall by focus point

**Aims of the Big Three**
- **Wilson:** Fourteen Points; self-determination; League of Nations; fair peace without revenge.
- **Clemenceau:** security for France; weak German army; Rhineland frontier; reparations for devastation.
- **Lloyd George:** punish Germany enough to satisfy voters, remove its navy and colonies, but keep it as a trading
  partner and avoid a war of revenge.

**Why they did not get everything**
- Aims clashed: Wilson's idealism against Clemenceau's security and Lloyd George's balance.
- Rhineland demilitarised and occupied, not separated.
- Danzig a free city; Upper Silesia left to a plebiscite.
- Reparations figure delayed until 1921.
- US Senate rejected the Treaty; the USA stayed out of the League; the guarantee to France lapsed.

**Impact on Germany to 1923**
- Political: Diktat; "stab in the back"; "November criminals"; Kapp Putsch (1920); murders of Erzberger (1921) and
  Rathenau (1922); Munich Putsch (1923).
- Economic: lost resources; reparations; Ruhr occupied (January 1923); hyperinflation.
- Social: savings destroyed; national humiliation; Germans living under foreign rule.

**Could it be justified at the time?**
- Yes: Brest-Litovsk (1918) was harsher; France and Belgium devastated; Germany not broken up.
- No: Keynes's *The Economic Consequences of the Peace* (1919); German rejection of war guilt; French critics who
  thought it too weak.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Fourteen Points | Wilson's programme for peace, January 1918 |
| Self-determination | The right of a people to choose its own government |
| Reparations | Payments for war damage |
| War guilt clause | Article 231 of the Treaty |
| Diktat | A dictated peace, imposed without negotiation |
| Demilitarised zone | An area where troops and fortifications are banned |
| Mandate | A former colony run by a victor under League supervision |
| Plebiscite | A vote by the people of an area on its future |
| "Stab in the back" | Myth that the German army was betrayed at home in 1918 |
| November criminals | Insult for the politicians who signed the armistice |
| Passive resistance | Refusal to work for the occupiers in the Ruhr, 1923 |
| Hyperinflation | Extremely rapid price rises, as in Germany in 1923 |

## Exam traps

- **Saying Germany lost the Saar permanently.** It went to the League for fifteen years, then a plebiscite.
- **Blaming the Treaty alone for hyperinflation.** War debts and printing money in 1923 mattered too.
- **Ignoring "at the time"** in the fourth focus point. Use opinions from 1919–23, not later judgements.
- **Calling the Big Three agreed.** Their disagreements explain the compromises.
- **On Paper 2, describing a cartoon** without saying which country it came from and why that matters.

## Self-test

1. Name **three** of Wilson's main aims.
2. Why did Clemenceau want a harsh treaty?
3. What pressure did Lloyd George face after December 1918?
4. What did the Fontainebleau Memorandum warn?
5. What happened to the Rhineland under the Treaty?
6. What was Article 231?
7. Give **four** military terms of the Treaty.
8. Why was Danzig made a free city?
9. Why did Germans call the Treaty a Diktat?
10. Why did French and Belgian troops enter the Ruhr in 1923?
11. Which book of 1919 criticised the reparations, and who wrote it?
12. Why could the victors argue the Treaty was fair to Germany?

**Answers:** 1. Any three: self-determination, a League of Nations, disarmament, open diplomacy, freedom of the seas, a
peace without revenge. 2. France had been invaded and devastated and wanted security against another attack. 3. Voters
expected him to make Germany pay. 4. That an unjust peace would leave Germany seeking revenge and could push it towards
Bolshevism. 5. It was demilitarised and occupied by Allied troops for fifteen years. 6. The war guilt clause: Germany
accepted responsibility for the loss and damage of the war. 7. Any four: army of 100,000; no conscription; no tanks; no
military aircraft; no submarines; six battleships; demilitarised Rhineland. 8. As a compromise: Poland needed a port,
but the city's population was mostly German. 9. Its delegates were not allowed to negotiate the terms. 10. Germany had
fallen behind with reparations payments. 11. *The Economic Consequences of the Peace*, by John Maynard Keynes.
12. Germany had imposed a harsher treaty at Brest-Litovsk, it had caused great destruction in France and Belgium, and it
was not broken up.
