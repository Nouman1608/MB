## Answers

**How the parts are marked.** One mark for each correct answer in the 1- and 2-mark questions. In the 4-mark questions, one mark for each point identified and one for developing it, up to two marks for each of two points (question 6: up to four marks for a developed explanation). The 6-mark questions give four AO1 marks on that pattern plus up to two AO2 marks: **one** for a general, accurate use of a source of authority, **two** for a specific use applied to the question. The 12-mark question is marked by levels:

| Level | Marks | What the answer does |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | 10–12 | Thorough examination of a range of points; logical reasoning with detailed evidence; excellent, direct use of the passage |
| 3 | 7–9 | Clear examination of more than one point; reasoning with some detail; good use of the passage |
| 2 | 4–6 | Some accurate examination; reasoning not always supported; general use of the passage. **Maximum Level 2 if only one point is made** |
| 1 | 1–3 | Limited examination with little reasoning. **Maximum Level 1 if the passage is not used** |
| 0 | 0 | Nothing worthy of credit |

### Section A

**1.** Commanding or encouraging what is good [1].

**2.** The Shahadah [1].

**3.** Any two, [1] each: Shahadah; Salah; Sawm; Zakah; Hajj.

**4.** Any two, [1] each: with the hand (by action); with the tongue (by speaking); with the heart (by disliking it).

**5.** For each of two reasons: reason identified [1] and developed [1].

- **They are the foundation of Muslim practice** [1]: the Prophet (pbuh) said Islam is built on them, so they hold up a Muslim's religious life [1].
- **They build unity** [1]: Muslims across the world perform the same acts, which gives a shared identity [1].
- **They develop taqwa** [1]: prayer, fasting and giving strengthen awareness of Allah and self-control [1].
- **They are obedience to Allah** [1]: Muslims perform them because Allah has commanded them [1].

**6.** Up to four marks: the Qur'an calls Muslims "the best nation" because they enjoin right and forbid wrong [1]; wrongdoing left unchecked harms the whole community, as in the Hadith of the ship [1]; advising others is a way of showing care and wishing them success [1]; the Prophet (pbuh) taught that changing wrong is part of faith, with levels of hand, tongue and heart [1].

### Section B

**7.** AO1 **[4]**: for each of two ways, the way identified [1] and explained [1]. AO2 **[2]**: one source of authority used generally [1] or specifically and applied [2].

Ways (AO1):

- **Hajj** [1]: pilgrims of every nation wear the same Ihram and perform the same rites, which shows equality and unity [1].
- **Salah** [1]: Muslims everywhere face the Ka'aba and pray in rows, with rich and poor side by side [1].
- **Sawm** [1]: the whole Ummah fasts at the same time and breaks the fast together, so they share the same experience [1].
- **Zakah** [1]: giving to the needy creates bonds between richer and poorer Muslims [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Islam is built upon five" (Hadith of Ibn Umar (RA), Bukhari and Muslim) [2].
- "The believing men and believing women are allies of one another" (Qur'an 9:71) [2].

**8.** AO1 **[4]**: as in question 7. AO2 **[2]**: as in question 7.

Ways (AO1):

- **Advising friends and family** [1]: reminding a friend to pray or be honest, gently and privately [1].
- **Setting an example** [1]: acting honestly in business shows by action what is good [1].
- **Speaking against wrongdoing** [1]: telling others that bullying or gossip is wrong [1].
- **Supporting good causes** [1]: helping with community projects encourages others to do good [1].

Applications (AO2), for example:

- "Let there be [arising] from you a nation inviting to [all that is] good, enjoining what is right and forbidding what is wrong" (Qur'an 3:104) [2].
- "Whoever among you sees a wrong, let him change it with his hand; if not, with his tongue; if not, with his heart" (Sahih Muslim) [2].

**9.** Marked by the levels above. Possible content:

*Points of significance*

- The Hadith defines Islam through actions, listing prayer, Zakah and fasting, which supports the statement.
- The pillars give a framework for worship that applies to all Muslims.
- They develop discipline, equality, generosity and unity.
- **Against the statement:** the Hadith has three parts: faith, Islam and Ihsan. The pillars (Islam) are one level among three, and belief and sincerity are also essential. Without sincerity (Ihsan), the acts would be empty.
- Good character and care for others (including Amr bil maroof) are also required; the pillars are the base, not the whole.
- **Judgement:** the pillars are central, but they are most significant when joined with faith and sincerity.

*Use of the passage*

- "To worship Allah Alone and none else, to offer prayers perfectly ... Zakah ... fasts" shows practice defined as Islam.
- "Faith is to believe in Allah, His angels ... Resurrection" shows that belief comes first.
- "To worship Allah as if you see Him" shows the inner quality that gives the pillars their meaning.
- "to teach the people their religion" (the closing line of the full Hadith) shows that the three levels together are the religion.
