## The seven uses of energy

Learn these exact phrases:

1. **Muscle contraction**
2. **Protein synthesis**
3. **Cell division**
4. **Active transport**
5. **Growth**
6. **The passage of nerve impulses**
7. **The maintenance of a constant body temperature**

Memory aid: "**M**y **P**et **C**at **A**lways **G**rows **N**ice **T**emperamentally" (Muscle, Protein, Cell division, Active transport, Growth, Nerve impulses, Temperature).

### Which apply where?

| Use | Animals (such as humans) | Plants | Yeast |
|---|---|---|---|
| Muscle contraction | yes | no | no |
| Protein synthesis | yes | yes | yes |
| Cell division | yes | yes | yes |
| Active transport | yes | yes (root hair cells taking up ions) | yes |
| Growth | yes | yes | yes |
| Nerve impulses | yes | no | no |
| Constant body temperature | mammals and birds | no | no |

## Yeast and temperature: the investigation

**Principle:** yeast respires glucose and releases carbon dioxide. Rate of carbon dioxide release = rate of respiration.

**Measuring:**

- count bubbles per minute (quick, less accurate: bubble size varies)
- collect gas in a gas syringe and read the volume after a fixed time (more accurate)
- time how long hydrogencarbonate indicator takes to change from red to yellow (shorter time = faster rate)

### Method in steps

1. Fixed volume and concentration of glucose solution + fixed mass of yeast.
2. Water bath at set temperature; leave to reach temperature.
3. Collect or count gas for a fixed time.
4. Repeat three times; calculate mean.
5. Repeat at a range of temperatures, with fresh yeast and glucose each time.

### Variables

| Independent | Dependent | Controlled |
|---|---|---|
| temperature | volume of gas in a fixed time (or bubbles per minute) | glucose concentration and volume, mass of yeast, warming time, collection time, type of yeast |

**Control:** glucose solution without yeast, to show the gas comes from the yeast.

## Results pattern

- Rate **increases** as temperature rises towards the optimum.
- Rate is **highest** at the optimum.
- Rate **decreases** above the optimum, because enzymes are **denatured**.
- Near zero at high temperatures.

### Rate calculation

```
rate = volume of gas ÷ time
9.0 cm³ in 3 minutes → 9.0 ÷ 3 = 3.0 cm³ per minute
```

From a time measurement: rate = 1 ÷ time. A tube that changes colour in 50 s has a rate of 1 ÷ 50 = 0.02 per second.

### A results table that scores full marks

| Temperature / °C | Volume of gas in 5 minutes / cm³ (trial 1) | Trial 2 | Trial 3 | Mean / cm³ |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 20 | 3.1 | 2.9 | 3.0 | 3.0 |
| 40 | 11.8 | 12.3 | 11.9 | 12.0 |

Headings carry units, the independent variable is in the first column, and every value is recorded to the same number of decimal places.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Respiration | Chemical reactions in cells that break down nutrient molecules to release energy |
| Yeast | Single-celled fungus used to investigate respiration |
| Rate of respiration | How quickly respiration happens; measured by gas released per unit time |
| Independent variable | The variable you change (temperature) |
| Dependent variable | The variable you measure (gas released) |
| Controlled variable | A variable kept the same so the test is fair |
| Optimum temperature | Temperature at which the rate is highest |
| Denatured | Enzyme shape changed by high temperature so it no longer works |
| Hydrogencarbonate indicator | Indicator that turns from red to yellow as carbon dioxide concentration rises |

## Exam traps

- "Movement" instead of **muscle contraction**; "keeping warm" instead of **maintenance of a constant body temperature**.
- Breathing or digestion listed as a use of energy.
- Plants given muscle contraction, nerve impulses or body temperature.
- Respiration "produces energy": it **releases** energy.
- Temperature called a controlled variable.
- Yeast or glucose reused between temperatures.
- "The yeast is denatured" instead of "the enzymes are denatured".
- An exact optimum claimed from readings 10 °C apart.

## Self-test

1. State four uses of energy in a human.
2. Give one use of energy in a root hair cell.
3. Why is "movement" not the best answer for a use of energy?
4. Which three uses of energy do not apply to plants?
5. What gas does yeast release that is measured in the investigation?
6. Name the independent variable in the yeast and temperature investigation.
7. State three variables that should be kept constant.
8. Why is a gas syringe more accurate than counting bubbles?
9. A syringe collects 14.0 cm³ of gas in 4 minutes. Calculate the rate.
10. Describe what you would see with hydrogencarbonate indicator if carbon dioxide is released.
11. Explain why the rate falls at high temperature.
12. Rates were measured at 20, 30, 40 and 50 °C, highest at 40 °C. Why can you not say the optimum is exactly 40 °C?

### Answers

1. Any four of: muscle contraction, protein synthesis, cell division, active transport, growth, passage of nerve impulses, maintenance of a constant body temperature.
2. Active transport (of ions from the soil); also protein synthesis, cell division or growth.
3. The syllabus phrase is "muscle contraction", which names what uses the energy.
4. Muscle contraction, passage of nerve impulses, maintenance of a constant body temperature.
5. Carbon dioxide.
6. Temperature.
7. Any three of: concentration and volume of glucose solution, mass of yeast, warming time, gas-collection time, type of yeast.
8. Bubbles vary in size, so counting does not measure volume accurately.
9. 14.0 ÷ 4 = 3.5 cm³ per minute.
10. It changes from red to yellow.
11. Respiration is controlled by enzymes, which are denatured at high temperature.
12. The optimum could be anywhere between 30 °C and 50 °C; readings are too far apart.
