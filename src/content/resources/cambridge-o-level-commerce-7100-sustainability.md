**Sustainability** means meeting the needs of people today without harming the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. In commerce, it is about making the way goods are made, moved, sold and disposed of less harmful to the environment. Sub-topic 6.1 of Cambridge O Level Commerce (7100) has two parts: the **purpose of sustainability in the supply chain** (6.1.1) and **actions to improve sustainability** (6.1.2).

## 6.1.1 Purpose of sustainability in the supply chain

The syllabus describes the purpose as **making commercial processes eco-friendly and environmentally responsible for future generations**. Every link in the supply chain uses resources and creates waste:

| Link | Possible environmental impact |
|---|---|
| Producing | Uses raw materials, energy and water; creates pollution and waste |
| Packaging | Uses paper, plastic and metal; creates rubbish |
| Transporting | Uses fuel; creates carbon dioxide and noise |
| Storing | Uses energy for lighting, heating and cooling |
| Selling | Uses energy and packaging; customers return goods |
| After use | Goods may be thrown away and end up in landfill |

A business that works sustainably tries to reduce these impacts at each link. Reasons include:

- **protecting resources** such as forests, water and minerals so they last;
- **reducing pollution** and climate change;
- **meeting customer expectations**, because many customers prefer eco-friendly goods;
- **following laws and regulations** on waste and emissions;
- **cutting costs** over time by using less energy, material and water;
- **improving reputation**, which can attract customers and investors.

## 6.1.2 Actions to improve sustainability

### 1. Using sustainable materials in the production process

Use materials that can be replaced or that cause little harm: recycled cotton and plastic, wood from managed forests, bamboo, and renewable energy in factories.

- *Benefit:* protects resources; reduces pollution; appeals to eco-conscious customers.
- *Cost:* sustainable materials may be dearer or harder to find.

### 2. Implementing sustainable logistics

Plan transport and storage to reduce fuel use and emissions: fuller loads, shorter routes, electric or hybrid vehicles, rail or sea instead of air, and energy-saving warehouses.

*Worked example.* A business delivers 20 loads a week by lorry. By combining deliveries it needs only 14 loads. Each load uses 80 litres of fuel.

1. Fuel before = 20 × 80 = 1,600 litres.
2. Fuel after = 14 × 80 = 1,120 litres.
3. Saving = 480 litres a week, which is a 30% reduction (480 ÷ 1,600 × 100).

- *Benefit:* lower fuel costs and fewer emissions.
- *Cost:* planning systems and new vehicles can be expensive.

### 3. Reducing packaging

Use less packaging, lighter packaging or recyclable and reusable packaging; avoid plastic where it is not needed.

*Worked example.* A snack maker reduces the plastic wrapper on each pack from 12 g to 9 g. It makes 500,000 packs a month.

1. Saving per pack = 12 − 9 = 3 g.
2. Saving a month = 3 × 500,000 = 1,500,000 g = **1.5 tonnes** of plastic.

- *Benefit:* less waste; lower material and transport costs.
- *Cost:* packaging must still protect the goods; damaged goods lead to waste.

### 4. Selling eco-friendly products

Offer goods designed to harm the environment less: energy-efficient appliances, refillable products, organic food, goods that last a long time.

- *Benefit:* attracts customers who care about the environment; may allow a higher price.
- *Cost:* development costs; customers may not want to pay extra.

### 5. Recycling and reducing waste

Sort and recycle paper, plastic, glass and metal; reuse materials; reduce waste in production.

*Worked example.* A factory produces 40 tonnes of waste in a month and recycles 28 tonnes. Recycling rate = 28 ÷ 40 × 100 = **70%**. The 12 tonnes not recycled go to landfill. If the recycling rate rises to 85%, it recycles 0.85 × 40 = 34 tonnes, leaving 6 tonnes.

- *Benefit:* saves raw materials and landfill costs; may earn money from recycled materials.
- *Cost:* needs sorting systems and staff training.

### 6. Reducing the need for returns and exchanges

Every return means extra transport, packaging and often waste. Businesses can reduce returns by giving accurate sizing guides and descriptions, clear photos, quality checks and good packaging.

*Worked example.* An online shop sends 10,000 orders a month. 8% are returned, which is 800 returns. Each return costs $9 in transport and handling. Better sizing guides cut returns to 5%, which is 500.

1. Fewer returns = 800 − 500 = 300.
2. Saving = 300 × 9 = **$2,700** a month, plus lower emissions.

### 7. Offering digital resale marketplaces

A business provides an online platform where customers can sell used products, such as second-hand clothes or phones. Goods stay in use longer, so fewer new items are made.

- *Benefit:* reduces waste; builds loyalty; may earn commission.
- *Cost:* setting up and running the platform; checking quality.

### 8. Offering free repair services

Fixing a product instead of throwing it away makes it last longer.

- *Benefit:* less waste; stronger customer loyalty; good reputation.
- *Cost:* the business pays for staff, parts and workshop space.

### 9. Water stewardship

Water stewardship is using water carefully and responsibly in production and sourcing, and protecting local water supplies: recycling water in factories, fixing leaks, using less water-hungry processes and treating waste water before release.

*Worked example.* Making one T-shirt uses 2,000 litres of water. A new process cuts this to 1,400 litres. Saving = 600 litres, a reduction of 600 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = **30%**. For 50,000 T-shirts the saving is 600 × 50,000 = 30,000,000 litres.

- *Benefit:* protects scarce water; lower water bills.
- *Cost:* new equipment and training.

## Evaluating the actions

Sustainable actions often have **higher costs now** but **lower costs and better reputation later**. A good answer to an evaluation question compares the costs and benefits and gives a decision.

| Action | Main benefit | Main cost |
|---|---|---|
| Sustainable materials | Resource protection | Higher material price |
| Sustainable logistics | Lower fuel use | Planning and vehicles |
| Reducing packaging | Less waste, lower cost | Risk of damage |
| Eco-friendly products | Attracts customers | Development cost |
| Recycling | Less landfill | Sorting systems |
| Fewer returns | Less transport | Better information and checks |
| Digital resale | Longer product life | Platform cost |
| Free repair | Loyalty | Repair staff |
| Water stewardship | Saves water | Equipment |

## Case: a trainer manufacturer

*Invented example.* A company makes trainers and sells them in shops and online.

1. **Production:** it switches to recycled plastic for the soles (sustainable materials) and recycles waste rubber offcuts (recycling and reducing waste).
2. **Water:** it treats and reuses the water from its dyeing process (water stewardship).
3. **Packaging:** it replaces the shoebox plastic wrap with a paper band (reducing packaging).
4. **Logistics:** it ships by sea in full containers instead of air freight (sustainable logistics).
5. **Selling:** it introduces a range made from natural materials (eco-friendly products) and a size guide that cuts returns (reducing returns).
6. **After sale:** it runs a resale site for used trainers (digital resale marketplace) and repairs worn soles free of charge (free repair services).

The company's costs rise at first because recycled materials and new equipment cost money. Over time it saves on materials, water and fuel, wins customers who care about the environment and strengthens its brand. A business should explain both sides when it is asked to evaluate.

## Annotated model answer

**Question:** Explain **two** actions that a clothing retailer could take to improve sustainability. [4]

> The retailer could reduce packaging by using lighter, recyclable bags [1], which reduces waste going to landfill and lowers material costs [1]. It could offer a free repair service for damaged clothes [1], so customers keep clothes for longer and fewer new clothes need to be made [1].

**Why it scores 4:** each action comes from the syllabus and is explained by its effect on the environment.

## Common mistakes

- Defining sustainability only as "being green" without reference to future generations.
- Describing an action without saying how it helps the environment.
- Ignoring the costs of sustainable actions.
- Confusing sustainable logistics (transport and storage) with sustainable materials (production).
- Giving an ethical point, such as fair pay, when the question asks about the environment.

## Exam technique

- **State** the action, then **explain** the environmental effect.
- **Use numbers** from the question in a calculation, with the unit.
- **Evaluate** by weighing cost now against benefit later.
