These notes condense **3.3.10.2 Electrophilic substitution** from the OxfordAQA International AS and A-level Chemistry (9620) specification, Version 5.3. It is International A2 content, examined in Unit 4 and able to reappear in Unit 5. For explanations and worked examples read the [electrophilic substitution study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-10-2/); then try the [electrophilic substitution practice questions](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-9620-oxfordaqa-a-level-chemistry-3-3-10-2-practice/).

## The subtopic in five lines

1. Electrophiles attack the benzene ring and substitute one H (monosubstitution only).
2. Four electrophiles: NO₂⁺ (nitration), SO₃ (sulfonation), RCO⁺ (acylation), R⁺ (alkylation).
3. Methylbenzene reacts faster than benzene and is substituted mainly at positions 2 and 4.
4. Chlorine in UV light reacts by radicals: addition to the benzene ring, substitution in the side chain of methylbenzene.
5. Chlorine in a side chain is much more reactive towards nucleophiles than chlorine on the ring.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning to learn |
|---|---|
| Electrophile | Electron pair acceptor; attracted to the delocalised ring |
| Nitronium ion | NO₂⁺, the electrophile in nitration |
| Acylium ion | RCO⁺, the electrophile in Friedel–Crafts acylation |
| Halogen carrier | AlCl₃, which removes Cl⁻ to generate a positive electrophile |
| Monosubstitution | Replacement of one ring H only |
| Free radical | Species with an unpaired electron, such as Cl• |
| Side chain | Carbon group attached to the ring, such as CH₃ in methylbenzene |
| Surfactant | Compound that lowers surface tension, such as a sodium alkylbenzenesulfonate detergent |

## Electrophile equations to learn

- Nitration: HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻
- Sulfonation: SO₃ is present in fuming sulfuric acid; its S is strongly δ+
- Acylation: RCOCl + AlCl₃ → RCO⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
- Alkylation: RCl + AlCl₃ → R⁺ + AlCl₄⁻
- Catalyst back: H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl, or H⁺ + HSO₄⁻ → H₂SO₄

## Mechanism marking points

1. Electrophile formed (equation).
2. Arrow from the delocalised ring to the electrophile's attacking atom (N of NO₂⁺, S of SO₃, C⁺ of RCO⁺ or R⁺).
3. Intermediate: new group and H on one carbon; horseshoe over five carbons with +.
4. Arrow from the C–H bond into the ring; H⁺ lost; ring restored.

## Products from benzene

| Reagent | Product | Formula |
|---|---|---|
| Conc. HNO₃ / conc. H₂SO₄ | nitrobenzene | C₆H₅NO₂ |
| Fuming H₂SO₄ | benzenesulfonic acid | C₆H₅SO₃H |
| CH₃COCl / AlCl₃ | phenylethanone | C₆H₅COCH₃ |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂COCl / AlCl₃ | 1-phenylbutan-1-one | C₆H₅COCH₂CH₂CH₃ |
| CH₃CH₂Cl / AlCl₃ | ethylbenzene | C₆H₅CH₂CH₃ |
| Cl₂ / UV light | 1,2,3,4,5,6-hexachlorocyclohexane | C₆H₆Cl₆ |

## Methylbenzene

- Nitration: 2-nitromethylbenzene and 4-nitromethylbenzene; works at a lower temperature than for benzene.
- Sulfonation: mainly 4-methylbenzenesulfonic acid.
- Cl₂ / UV light: C₆H₅CH₂Cl, then C₆H₅CHCl₂, then C₆H₅CCl₃ (Mr 126.5, 161.0, 195.5).
- Reason for faster ring reactions: the methyl group releases electron density into the ring.

## Why the reactions matter

| Reaction | Importance named in the specification |
|---|---|
| Nitration | Making explosives; nitro compounds reduced to aromatic amines |
| Sulfonation | Making surfactants and sulfonamides |
| Acylation | Joining a new carbon chain to a ring in synthesis |

## Choosing the conditions

| You want | Use | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Group on the ring of methylbenzene | Electrophile: acid mixture or AlCl₃ | UV light |
| Cl on the side chain of methylbenzene | Cl₂ with UV light | AlCl₃ |
| Monoalkylation | Large excess of benzene | Excess haloalkane |
| Friedel–Crafts to work at all | Dry AlCl₃ and dry glassware | Water, which destroys AlCl₃ |

## Ring Cl versus side-chain Cl

- Side chain (C₆H₅CH₂Cl): normal polar C–Cl; OH⁻ substitutes Cl when warmed; AgNO₃ test then gives white AgCl precipitate.
- Ring (C₆H₅Cl): Cl lone pair overlaps with the delocalised ring; stronger, shorter C–Cl bond; ring electrons repel nucleophiles; no substitution, no precipitate.

## Multistep yield rule

Overall yield = product of step yields as decimals. Two steps at 70% and 80% give 0.70 × 0.80 = 0.56, so 56%.

## Exam traps

- Arrow from a ring carbon or from H: start at the delocalised ring.
- + sign missing, or horseshoe drawn over all six carbons.
- 3-substituted product as the main product from methylbenzene.
- Benzene + Cl₂ in UV light giving chlorobenzene: it gives C₆H₆Cl₆ by addition.
- SO₃ written as a positive ion: it is neutral, with a δ+ sulfur.
- Friedel–Crafts product of acylation called an aldehyde: it is a ketone.
- Forgetting HCl as the second product of Friedel–Crafts reactions.

## Self-test

Answer without looking, then check below.

1. Write the equation for forming the nitronium ion.
2. Name the electrophile in sulfonation.
3. Give the formula of the electrophile formed from propanoyl chloride and AlCl₃.
4. Name the product of benzene with propanoyl chloride and AlCl₃.
5. Show how AlCl₃ is regenerated.
6. Name the two main products of nitrating methylbenzene.
7. Why is methylbenzene nitrated more easily than benzene?
8. Give one use of nitration and one of sulfonation in synthesis.
9. What forms when benzene reacts with chlorine in UV light?
10. What forms first when methylbenzene reacts with chlorine in UV light?
11. Why is excess benzene used in Friedel–Crafts alkylation?
12. Why does chlorobenzene not react with warm aqueous NaOH?

### Answers

1. HNO₃ + 2H₂SO₄ → NO₂⁺ + H₃O⁺ + 2HSO₄⁻.
2. Sulfur trioxide, SO₃.
3. CH₃CH₂CO⁺.
4. 1-Phenylpropan-1-one, C₆H₅COCH₂CH₃.
5. H⁺ + AlCl₄⁻ → AlCl₃ + HCl.
6. 2-Nitromethylbenzene and 4-nitromethylbenzene.
7. The methyl group releases electron density into the ring, so the ring attracts electrophiles more strongly.
8. Nitration: explosives or making amines. Sulfonation: surfactants or sulfonamides.
9. 1,2,3,4,5,6-Hexachlorocyclohexane, C₆H₆Cl₆.
10. (Chloromethyl)benzene, C₆H₅CH₂Cl.
11. The alkylbenzene product is more reactive than benzene and could be substituted again; excess benzene makes monosubstitution more likely.
12. A lone pair on Cl overlaps with the delocalised ring, strengthening the C–Cl bond, and the ring's electrons repel the nucleophile.
