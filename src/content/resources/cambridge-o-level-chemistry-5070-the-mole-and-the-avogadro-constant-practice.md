Use these relative atomic masses: H = 1, C = 12, N = 14, O = 16, Mg = 24, Al = 27, S = 32, Cl = 35.5, K = 39, Ca = 40, Fe = 56, Pb = 207. Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ per mol. Molar gas volume = 24 dm³ at r.t.p.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by the Avogadro constant and give its value. **[2]**

**2.** Calculate the mass of 0.0250 mol of calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂. **[2]**

**3.** Calculate the total number of ions in 0.100 mol of magnesium chloride, MgCl₂. **[2]**

**4.** Calculate the volume, in dm³, occupied by 3.20 g of sulfur dioxide, SO₂, at r.t.p. **[2]**

**5.** A solution contains 4.90 g of sulfuric acid, H₂SO₄, dissolved in water to make 250 cm³ of solution. Calculate its concentration in g/dm³ and in mol/dm³. **[3]**

**6.** 0.125 mol of a gas has a mass of 5.50 g. Calculate its relative molecular mass and deduce whether the gas is carbon dioxide, sulfur dioxide or nitrogen dioxide. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** Iron(III) oxide is reduced by carbon monoxide.

Fe₂O₃ + 3CO → 2Fe + 3CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the maximum mass of iron that can be made from 32.0 g of iron(III) oxide.

**(b)** Calculate the volume of carbon monoxide, at r.t.p., needed to reduce 32.0 g of iron(III) oxide.

**(c)** In an experiment, 19.6 g of iron was obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. **[6]**

**8.** Aluminium reacts with chlorine to form aluminium chloride.

2Al + 3Cl₂ → 2AlCl₃

2.70 g of aluminium is heated with 7.10 g of chlorine.

**(a)** Show by calculation which reactant is limiting.

**(b)** Calculate the mass of aluminium chloride formed. **[5]**

**9.** A student titrates 25.0 cm³ of potassium hydroxide solution with 0.100 mol/dm³ sulfuric acid.

2KOH + H₂SO₄ → K₂SO₄ + 2H₂O

The titres recorded were: rough 13.20 cm³; then 12.80 cm³, 12.70 cm³ and 12.75 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the mean titre to use.

**(b)** Calculate the amount of sulfuric acid used.

**(c)** Deduce the amount of potassium hydroxide in 25.0 cm³.

**(d)** Calculate the concentration of the potassium hydroxide in mol/dm³.

**(e)** Convert this concentration into g/dm³. **[5]**

**10.** A sample of an oxide of lead contains 2.07 g of lead combined with 0.32 g of oxygen. Calculate the empirical formula of the oxide. **[3]**

**11.** An organic acid contains 40.0% carbon, 6.7% hydrogen and 53.3% oxygen by mass. Its relative molecular mass is 60.

**(a)** Calculate its empirical formula.

**(b)** Deduce its molecular formula. **[4]**

**12.** A 5.00 g sample of impure magnesium carbonate, MgCO₃, reacts with excess hydrochloric acid. The impurity does not react. 1.08 dm³ of carbon dioxide is collected at r.t.p.

MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

Calculate the percentage purity of the magnesium carbonate. **[4]**

**13.** A farmer can buy urea, CO(NH₂)₂, or ammonium sulfate, (NH₄)₂SO₄, as a nitrogen fertiliser. Calculate the percentage by mass of nitrogen in each, and state which contains the greater percentage of nitrogen. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** The number of particles (atoms, ions or molecules) in one mole of a substance [1]; 6.02 × 10²³ [1].

**2.** Mr of Ca(OH)₂ = 40 + 2(16 + 1) = 74 [1]; mass = 0.0250 × 74 = **1.85 g** [1].

**3.** Each MgCl₂ gives one Mg²⁺ and two Cl⁻, so 0.300 mol of ions [1]; 0.300 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **1.81 × 10²³ ions** [1].

**4.** Mr of SO₂ = 64, so amount = 3.20 ÷ 64 = 0.0500 mol [1]; volume = 0.0500 × 24 = **1.20 dm³** [1].

**5.** 250 cm³ = 0.250 dm³, so 4.90 ÷ 0.250 = **19.6 g/dm³** [1]. Mr of H₂SO₄ = 98, so amount = 4.90 ÷ 98 = 0.0500 mol [1]; 0.0500 ÷ 0.250 = **0.200 mol/dm³** [1].

**6.** Mr = 5.50 ÷ 0.125 = 44 [1]; the gas is **carbon dioxide** (SO₂ = 64, NO₂ = 46) [1].

**7. (a)** Mr of Fe₂O₃ = 160, so amount = 32.0 ÷ 160 = 0.200 mol [1]. Ratio Fe₂O₃ : Fe = 1 : 2, so 0.400 mol Fe [1]. Mass = 0.400 × 56 = **22.4 g** [1].

**(b)** Ratio Fe₂O₃ : CO = 1 : 3, so 0.600 mol CO [1]; volume = 0.600 × 24 = **14.4 dm³** [1].

**(c)** 19.6 ÷ 22.4 × 100 = **87.5%** [1].

**8. (a)** Amount of Al = 2.70 ÷ 27 = 0.100 mol [1]. Amount of Cl₂ = 7.10 ÷ 71 = 0.100 mol [1]. 0.100 mol Al needs 0.100 × 3 ÷ 2 = 0.150 mol Cl₂, but only 0.100 mol is present, so **chlorine is limiting** [1].

**(b)** Ratio Cl₂ : AlCl₃ = 3 : 2, so 0.100 × 2 ÷ 3 = 0.0667 mol AlCl₃ [1]. Mr of AlCl₃ = 133.5, so mass = 0.0667 × 133.5 = **8.90 g** [1].

**9. (a)** All three accurate titres are within 0.10 cm³, so mean = (12.80 + 12.70 + 12.75) ÷ 3 = **12.75 cm³**; the rough titre is excluded [1].

**(b)** 0.100 × (12.75 ÷ 1000) = **1.275 × 10⁻³ mol** [1].

**(c)** Ratio KOH : H₂SO₄ = 2 : 1, so **2.55 × 10⁻³ mol** KOH [1].

**(d)** 2.55 × 10⁻³ ÷ 0.0250 = **0.102 mol/dm³** [1].

**(e)** Mr of KOH = 56, so 0.102 × 56 = **5.71 g/dm³** [1].

**10.** Pb: 2.07 ÷ 207 = 0.0100 mol [1]. O: 0.32 ÷ 16 = 0.0200 mol [1]. Ratio 1 : 2, so the empirical formula is **PbO₂** [1].

**11. (a)** C: 40.0 ÷ 12 = 3.33; H: 6.7 ÷ 1 = 6.7; O: 53.3 ÷ 16 = 3.33 [1]. Dividing by 3.33 gives 1 : 2 : 1 [1], so the empirical formula is **CH₂O** [1].

**(b)** Empirical formula mass = 30, and 60 ÷ 30 = 2, so the molecular formula is **C₂H₄O₂** [1].

**12.** Amount of CO₂ = 1.08 ÷ 24 = 0.0450 mol [1]. Ratio 1 : 1, so 0.0450 mol MgCO₃ [1]. Mr of MgCO₃ = 84, so mass = 0.0450 × 84 = 3.78 g [1]. Purity = 3.78 ÷ 5.00 × 100 = **75.6%** [1].

**13.** Urea: Mr = 60, % N = 28 ÷ 60 × 100 = **46.7%** [1]. Ammonium sulfate: Mr = 132, % N = 28 ÷ 132 × 100 = **21.2%** [1]. **Urea** contains the greater percentage of nitrogen [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 3: counting formula units instead of ions. One MgCl₂ releases three ions, not one.
- Question 7: multiplying the mass of iron(III) oxide by the ratio 2 instead of the amount in moles.
- Question 8: declaring chlorine limiting only because "0.100 = 0.100". The decision must compare the amount present with the amount the equation requires.
- Question 9: including the rough titre in the mean, or forgetting the 2 : 1 ratio and getting 0.0510 mol/dm³.
- Question 10: dividing mass by mass (2.07 ÷ 0.32) instead of converting each element to moles first.
- Question 12: dividing by 24 000 when the volume is already given in dm³.
- Question 13: using only one nitrogen atom per formula. Both compounds contain two.
- Final answers given to one significant figure when the data have three: keep three unless told otherwise.
