## Questions

### Section A

**1.** State what is meant by a double covalent bond. **[1]**

**2.** Hydrogen cyanide has the structure H–C≡N. State the total number of shared pairs of electrons and the number of lone pairs in the molecule. **[2]**

**3.** Which species contains a co-ordinate bond?

A: CH₄

B: NH₄⁺

C: H₂O

D: CO₂

**[1]**

**4.** Explain why a methane molecule cannot act as the donor in a co-ordinate bond. **[1]**

**5.** How many lines are needed in the displayed formula of propene, CH₃CH=CH₂? **[1]**

**6.** State the total number of electrons shown in a dot-and-cross diagram of the outer shells in an ammonium ion, NH₄⁺. **[1]**

**7.** Write an equation for the formation of the oxonium ion from a water molecule, and show how the new bond is represented. **[2]**

### Section B

**8.** Carbon monoxide, CO, contains a triple bond.

**(a)** State the total number of outer-shell electrons in a CO molecule. **[1]**

**(b)** Describe how the three shared pairs in the triple bond are formed. **[2]**

**(c)** State the number of lone pairs on each atom. **[1]**

**(d)** Describe how the bonding in CO is represented using lines and an arrow. **[1]**

**9.** Aluminium chloride exists in the vapour as Al₂Cl₆ molecules.

**(a)** Explain why an aluminium atom in AlCl₃ can accept a pair of electrons. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify the atom that donates the electron pair. **[1]**

**(c)** Describe how two AlCl₃ units are joined to form Al₂Cl₆. **[2]**

**10.** Ammonia gas reacts with hydrogen chloride gas to form solid ammonium chloride, NH₄Cl.

**(a)** Explain how the ammonium ion forms in this reaction. **[2]**

**(b)** Identify the three types of bonding present in solid ammonium chloride and say where each occurs. **[3]**

**11.** Hydrazine, N₂H₄, has a single N–N bond, and each nitrogen atom is bonded to two hydrogen atoms.

**(a)** State the number of lone pairs on each nitrogen atom. **[1]**

**(b)** Hydrazine reacts with one H⁺ ion to form N₂H₅⁺. Explain how the new bond forms. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why N₂H₅⁺ can still react with another H⁺ ion. **[1]**

**12.** Compare an ordinary covalent bond with a co-ordinate bond. Include how each forms, the conditions needed, how each is represented, and what is true of the bonds once formed. Use examples. **[6]**

## Answers

### Section A

**1.** Two shared pairs of electrons between the same two atoms [1] **[1]**

**2.** Four shared pairs (one C–H and three in C≡N) [1]; one lone pair, on nitrogen [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Carbon uses all four outer electrons in bonds, so it has no lone pair here.

**3.** **B** [1] **[1]**

**4.** Carbon uses all its outer electrons in four C–H bonds, so methane has no lone pair to donate [1] **[1]**

**5.** **9** lines: six C–H, one C–C and two for the C=C bond [1] **[1]**

**6.** 5 (N) + 4 × 1 (H) − 1 (charge) = **8** electrons [1] **[1]**

**7.** H₂O + H⁺ → H₃O⁺ [1]; the new bond is a co-ordinate bond shown by an arrow from O to H [1] **[2]**

### Section B

**8.**

**(a)** 4 + 6 = **10** [1]

**(b)** Two pairs are ordinary covalent bonds, each atom supplying one electron to each pair [1]; the third is a co-ordinate bond in which oxygen supplies both electrons [1]

**(c)** One lone pair on carbon and one on oxygen [1]

**(d)** Two lines between C and O, plus an arrow from O to C [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* The arrow points to carbon, the acceptor, because oxygen supplies the pair.

**9.**

**(a)** Aluminium has only six electrons in its outer shell in AlCl₃, so it has an empty orbital [1]

**(b)** A chlorine atom (in the other AlCl₃ unit), using a lone pair [1]

**(c)** A chlorine atom of each AlCl₃ unit donates a lone pair to the aluminium atom of the other unit [1]; this gives two Cl→Al co-ordinate bonds, so each aluminium is bonded to four chlorine atoms [1] **[4]**

**10.**

**(a)** The nitrogen atom of NH₃ donates its lone pair to an H⁺ from HCl [1]; forming a co-ordinate bond, giving NH₄⁺ (and Cl⁻) [1]

**(b)** Covalent bonds between N and H within NH₄⁺ [1]; a co-ordinate bond, also within NH₄⁺ (identical to the others once formed) [1]; ionic bonding between NH₄⁺ and Cl⁻ ions in the lattice [1] **[5]**

**11.**

**(a)** One [1]

**(b)** A nitrogen atom donates its lone pair to the H⁺ [1]; forming a co-ordinate bond, N→H [1]

**(c)** The other nitrogen atom still has a lone pair that can be donated [1] **[4]**

**12.** Indicative content; one mark per valid point, up to 6 [6] **[6]**

- Both are a shared pair of electrons between two atoms, attracted to both nuclei.
- In an ordinary covalent bond each atom supplies one electron, as in H–H or C–H.
- In a co-ordinate bond both electrons come from one atom, as in NH₄⁺ or H₃O⁺.
- A co-ordinate bond needs a donor with a lone pair and an acceptor with an empty orbital (such as H⁺ or B in BF₃).
- An ordinary covalent bond is drawn as a line; a co-ordinate bond as an arrow from donor to acceptor.
- Once formed, a co-ordinate bond is identical to an ordinary covalent bond between the same atoms, so all four N–H bonds in NH₄⁺ are the same.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Defining a covalent bond without "pair".
- Arrows pointing the wrong way.
- Forgetting that hydrogen has no lone pairs.
- Treating a co-ordinate bond as weaker once formed.
