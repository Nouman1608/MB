**Syllabus:** Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027, **AS Level**, subtopic 8.1.

## Learning outcomes

1. **Explain and use** the terms rate of reaction, frequency of collisions, effective collisions, non-effective collisions.
2. **Explain qualitatively**, in terms of the frequency of effective collisions, the effect of **concentration** and **pressure**.
3. **Use experimental data** to calculate rate.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Rate of reaction | change in amount or concentration of a reactant or product per unit time |
| Frequency of collisions | number of collisions per unit time |
| Effective collision | collision that leads to reaction (energy ≥ activation energy, suitable orientation) |
| Non-effective collision | collision that does not lead to reaction; particles bounce apart |
| Initial rate | rate at t = 0, from the tangent at the start of the curve |
| Mean rate | total change ÷ total time over an interval |
| Tangent | straight line touching a curve at one point, with the same gradient as the curve there |

## Concentration and pressure: the chain of reasoning

    higher concentration (or gas pressure)
      → more particles per unit volume
      → more frequent collisions
      → more frequent effective collisions
      → faster rate

- The **energy** of the particles is unchanged (same temperature).
- The **fraction** of collisions that are effective is unchanged.
- Only the **number of collisions per unit time** increases.

| Change | Frequency of collisions | Fraction effective | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Increase concentration | increases | unchanged | increases |
| Increase gas pressure | increases | unchanged | increases |
| Increase temperature (8.2) | increases | **increases** | increases a lot |

## Calculations

**Mean rate**

    rate = Δ(quantity) / Δt          always quoted as a positive value

**Tangent gradient**

    rate at time t = |Δy / Δx| for a tangent drawn at t
    read two points far apart on the tangent

**Units**

| Measured quantity | Rate unit |
|---|---|
| concentration (mol dm⁻³) | mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹ |
| gas volume (cm³) | cm³ s⁻¹ |
| mass (g) | g s⁻¹ |
| clock reaction time (s) | 1/t in s⁻¹ (relative rate) |

**Conversions**

- Mass of gas lost → moles: divide by M.
- Volume of gas → moles: use pV = nRT, or 24.0 dm³ mol⁻¹ (24 000 cm³ mol⁻¹) at room conditions.
- Between species: use the equation ratio. For 2A → 3B, rate of formation of B = 1.5 × rate of loss of A.

**Quick examples**

    60 cm³ H₂ in 40 s → mean rate = 1.5 cm³ s⁻¹
    1.5 cm³ s⁻¹ at room conditions → 1.5 / 24 000 = 6.25 × 10⁻⁵ mol s⁻¹
    [X] falls 0.800 → 0.500 mol dm⁻³ in 120 s → 2.50 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹

## Reading graphs

- **Steeper** curve = faster rate.
- Gradient **decreases** with time as reactants are used up.
- Curve **levels off** when the limiting reactant is used up.
- **Final amount** of product is set by the limiting reactant, not by the rate.
- More concentrated excess reactant: steeper start, **same** final volume.
- More of the **limiting** reactant: larger final volume.

## Exam traps

- Write "**more frequent** collisions" or "more collisions **per unit time**", not just "more collisions".
- Never say higher concentration gives particles **more energy**.
- The **proportion** of effective collisions does **not** rise with concentration.
- Use a **tangent**, not a chord, for an instantaneous rate.
- Include **units**; rates are positive.
- State **which species** a rate refers to when stoichiometry differs.
- Orders of reaction and rate constants are A Level (Topic 26), not 8.1.

## Self-test

1. Define rate of reaction.
2. What is a non-effective collision?
3. Explain why increasing the concentration of an acid increases the rate of its reaction with magnesium.
4. Explain why increasing the pressure increases the rate of a gas reaction.
5. Does increasing concentration change the fraction of collisions that are effective? Explain.
6. 48 cm³ of gas is produced in the first 30 s. Calculate the mean rate.
7. A tangent at t = 60 s passes through (0 s, 75 cm³) and (120 s, 135 cm³). Calculate the rate at 60 s.
8. A flask loses 0.44 g of CO₂ in 200 s. Calculate the mean rate of CO₂ formation in mol s⁻¹.
9. For N₂ + 3H₂ → 2NH₃, H₂ is used at 0.030 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹. At what rate is NH₃ formed?
10. In a clock reaction the time is 40 s. What is the relative rate?
11. Why does the gradient of a volume–time graph decrease as the reaction proceeds?
12. A reaction is repeated with twice the mass of the limiting reactant at the same concentration. What happens to the final volume of gas?

**Answers:**

1. The change in amount or concentration of a reactant or product per unit time.
2. A collision that does not result in reaction, because the particles have less than the activation energy (or the wrong orientation).
3. More acid particles per unit volume, so collisions with magnesium are more frequent and effective collisions are more frequent.
4. More gas molecules per unit volume (higher concentration), so more frequent collisions and more frequent effective collisions.
5. No. The energy distribution depends on temperature only, so the fraction with enough energy is unchanged; only the number of collisions per unit time rises.
6. 48 / 30 = 1.6 cm³ s⁻¹.
7. (135 − 75) / 120 = 0.50 cm³ s⁻¹.
8. 0.44 / 44.0 = 0.0100 mol; 0.0100 / 200 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁵ mol s⁻¹.
9. 0.030 × 2/3 = 0.020 mol dm⁻³ s⁻¹.
10. 1/40 = 0.025 s⁻¹.
11. The reactants are being used up, so their concentration falls and effective collisions become less frequent.
12. It doubles (more limiting reactant gives more product).
