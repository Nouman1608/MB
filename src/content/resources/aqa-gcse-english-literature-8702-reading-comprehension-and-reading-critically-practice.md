## Section A: short questions

**1.** In the original sentence *"Tom kept his coat on throughout the meeting and checked the clock
twice,"* give one explicit detail and one implied meaning. **[2]**

**2.** Explain the meaning of "cold" in the original sentence *"Her welcome was cold, though the fire
was blazing,"* and how the contrast affects the reader. **[2]**

**3.** Work through the four steps of the inference ladder for the original sentence *"He folded the
newspaper so that the photograph was hidden."* **[4]**

**4.** Turn the topic "loneliness" into a theme, stated as an idea that a text might explore. **[2]**

**5.** Give two different credible readings of the original sentence *"She said nothing and left the
ring on the table,"* and state what further evidence would help you decide between them. **[3]**

**6.** This sentence analyses but does not evaluate: *"The short sentence 'He was gone.' creates a
sense of finality."* Add a sentence of evaluation. **[2]**

**7.** In the original sentence *"Ava praised her sister's painting loudly, in front of everyone,
and never looked at it again,"* explain one possible motive for Ava's behaviour and the evidence for
it. **[2]**

## Section B: longer questions

Read the two original passages, then answer Questions 8 to 10.

> *Original passage A:* Marcus waited at the bus stop with his father's old umbrella, though it was not
> raining. When the bus came, Leo stepped off first, taller than Marcus remembered, with a rucksack and
> a new way of standing. "You didn't have to come," Leo said. "I was passing," said Marcus, who had
> walked two miles. They walked home without the umbrella going up. At the corner, Leo stopped by the
> chip shop where they had always stopped, looked at it for a moment, and walked on. Marcus did not say
> anything. He tightened his grip on the umbrella as if the weather might still change.

> *Original passage B:* Every Sunday Mrs Kaur set two of the good cups out for her daughter's call,
> though the call came on the phone and nobody could see the cups. She answered on the second ring,
> never the first, so as not to seem to have been waiting. "Busy week?" she asked, and listened, and
> said "good, good" at the gaps. When the call ended she washed both cups, dried them and put them back
> behind the glass.

**8.** How does the writer present the relationship between Marcus and Leo in passage A? **[10]**

**9.** "Marcus is simply too proud to show his feelings." How far do you agree with this reading of
passage A? **[6]**

**10.** Compare how the two passages present the experience of waiting for someone. **[8]**

**11.** Using your own studied text, write a paragraph giving an informed personal response to its
ending, including one alternative reading that you weigh against your own. **[5]**

## Answers

**1.** Explicit: Tom keeps his coat on and checks the clock twice [1]. Implied: he wants to leave,
feels uncomfortable or does not intend to stay long [1].

**2.** "Cold" means unfriendly or unwelcoming, not low in temperature [1]. The contrast with the
"blazing" fire emphasises that physical warmth cannot make up for emotional coldness, which makes the
lack of welcome more striking [1].

**3.** Detail: he folds the paper to hide a photograph [1]. Literal: he does not want to see the
picture, or does not want someone else to [1]. Inference: the photograph may cause him pain, shame or
guilt [1]. Significance: the writer may be presenting a character who avoids the past, showing
concealment through a small action [1].

**4.** A specific idea, such as "loneliness can exist inside a family" [1], which states what the text
suggests about the topic rather than naming it [1].

**5.** Reading one: she is ending the relationship, and her silence shows dignity or finality [1].
Reading two: she is leaving in shock or hurt, unable to speak [1]. Evidence that would help: her
earlier behaviour, what she does next, or the narrator's comments on her feelings [1].

**6.** Example: *"This is more powerful than a longer explanation would be, because the abruptness
mirrors the suddenness of the loss"* [1] *"and leaves the reader no space to soften it."* [1]

**7.** A possible motive: she is performing generosity for an audience, perhaps out of jealousy or a
wish to appear supportive [1]. Evidence: "loudly, in front of everyone" contrasts with "never looked at
it again", suggesting the praise was for show [1].

**8.** Award one mark per developed point, up to 10:

- The umbrella "though it was not raining" suggests Marcus has brought it for comfort or as a link to
  his father [1].
- "Taller than Marcus remembered" and "a new way of standing" show that Leo has changed while away
  [1].
- "You didn't have to come" may be distant or embarrassed, suggesting Leo is less close than before
  [1].
- "I was passing" is contradicted by "had walked two miles", showing Marcus hides how much he cares
  [1].
- The narrator's quiet correction creates gentle irony at Marcus's expense [1].
- The unopened umbrella may symbolise feelings kept closed between them [1].
- Leo stopping at the chip shop shows he remembers their shared past [1].
- Walking on suggests he has moved beyond it, or chooses not to return to old habits [1].
- Marcus "did not say anything", so the relationship is presented through silence and subtext [1].
- The final simile "as if the weather might still change" suggests hope that their closeness might
  return, or fear of further change [1].

**9.** Award marks as follows, up to 6:

- Evidence for the reading: "I was passing" hides his effort, which could be pride [1].
- Evidence for the reading: his silence at the chip shop could show refusal to admit hurt [1].
- An alternative: he may be protecting Leo from feeling obliged, so it is consideration, not pride
  [1].
- An alternative: his silence may be uncertainty about how Leo has changed, not pride [1].
- The word "simply" is challenged: the passage presents mixed feelings, not one cause [1].
- A clear judgement on "how far", supported by the final image of gripping the umbrella [1].

**10.** Award one mark per developed comparative point, up to 8:

- Both passages present waiting as something characters try to hide [1].
- Marcus pretends he "was passing"; Mrs Kaur answers "on the second ring, never the first" [1].
- Both use a domestic object as a symbol: the umbrella in A, the good cups in B [1].
- In A the object links to the past and to family; in B the cups show care that the daughter cannot
  see [1].
- Both narrators reveal the gap between what characters say and feel, through irony [1].
- A ends with tension and possible hope; B ends with a repeated routine, suggesting long-term
  loneliness [1].
- B's dialogue is minimal ("good, good"), showing the mother listening more than speaking; A's short
  exchange shows awkwardness [1].
- Judgement: B's repeated weekly ritual may make its waiting feel sadder, because nothing changes,
  whereas A's ending leaves change possible [1].

**11.** Award marks as follows:

- A clear personal view of the ending, not simple liking or disliking [1].
- Evidence from the ending supporting the view [1].
- Analysis of at least one method used in the ending [1].
- A credible alternative reading [1].
- A reasoned judgement weighing the two readings [1].

## Using these questions well

Answer Section A quickly, then spend about 15 minutes each on Questions 8 and 10. For Question 9,
practise the "how far" structure: evidence for, evidence against, judgement. Mark your work by
checking that every inference points to specific words in the passage.
