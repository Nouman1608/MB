This guide covers **Topic 1 What Is Business?**, the first of six
subject-content sections in AQA AS Business (7131), for first teaching
from September 2023 and current for cohorts taking exams through summer
2026. AQA runs AS as a separate, stand-alone qualification that shares
a combined specification document with A-level Business (7132) — the AS
content is the first six of the ten A-level sections, and this topic's
content is identical to Topic 1 of the A-level specification.

## Where this fits in 7131

As the opening topic of the AS course, What Is Business? introduces why
businesses exist, the range of legal structures they can take, and the
external factors that shape their decisions before those factors are
studied purely as a static list — most later AS topics (managers and
decision making, marketing, operations, finance, human resources) build
directly on the foundational vocabulary and concepts introduced here.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA AS BUSINESS (7131) — TOPIC 1 WHAT IS BUSINESS?**

- 3.1.1 Understanding the nature and purpose of business — why
businesses exist and what they aim to achieve
- 3.1.2 Understanding different business forms — sole traders, private
limited and public limited companies, private sector and public sector
organisations, non-profit organisations and social enterprises;
unlimited and limited liability; ordinary share capital, market
capitalisation and dividends; the role of shareholders and why they
invest; influences on share price and the significance of share price
changes; and the effects of ownership on mission and objectives
- 3.1.3 Understanding that businesses operate within an external
environment — how the external environment can affect costs and
demand, through competition, market conditions, incomes, interest
rates, demographic factors, and environmental issues and fair trade

## How to approach it

Because AS Business is examined as a stand-alone qualification with its
own assessment, do not assume this content matters less than the same
material studied at full A-level — AS candidates are examined on
exactly this sub-topic set, and its concepts recur throughout the rest
of the AS course. Start with the **measurement of profit**: the
specification requires you to work with revenue (turnover/sales),
fixed costs, variable costs and total costs, and to explain why a
business might pursue profit alongside — or instead of — growth,
survival, cash flow, and social and ethical objectives. Questions
frequently ask candidates to calculate or interpret these figures for
a named business, and to explain how its objectives relate to its
mission.

For business forms (3.1.2), revise by comparing structures directly
against each other on liability, access to capital, and control, rather
than learning each form's definition in isolation: a sole trader has
unlimited liability and full control but limited access to capital; a
private limited company has limited liability and more capital access,
at the cost of some control and a requirement to publish accounts. This
comparative approach matches how exam questions typically ask you to
recommend or evaluate a form of ownership for a specific business
scenario.

The specification's list of business forms extends beyond sole traders
and private limited companies: it also names public limited companies
(plcs), private sector and public sector organisations, non-profit
organisations, and social enterprises. For companies, you need
**ordinary share capital** (finance raised by selling shares),
**market capitalisation** (share price multiplied by the number of
shares in issue), and **dividends** (the share of profit paid out to
shareholders). You should also be able to explain **the role of
shareholders and why they invest** -- chiefly for dividend income and
capital growth in the share price -- and **influences on share price
and the significance of share price changes**, such as trading
performance, market sentiment and the wider economy, including how a
falling share price can make a company harder to finance or more
vulnerable to takeover. Finally, be ready to explain **the effects of
ownership on mission and objectives**: a plc answerable to external
shareholders may face more pressure toward short-term profit than a
sole trader or a social enterprise, which can set its mission around a
social or environmental purpose instead.

For the external environment (3.1.3), the specification names six
factors that affect costs and demand: competition, market conditions,
incomes, interest rates, demographic factors, and environmental issues
and fair trade — practise applying these to a real or plausible
business scenario, since exam questions typically ask you to analyse
or evaluate the impact of a named external change on a named business,
not to list external factors in the abstract.

## Worked example: measuring profit

A small coffee shop sells 500 cups of coffee a week at £3 each. Its
variable costs (beans, milk, cups) are £0.80 per cup, and its fixed
costs (rent, staff salaries, insurance) are £900 a week.

```
Revenue:          500 x £3 = £1,500
Variable costs:   500 x £0.80 = £400
Fixed costs:      £900
Total costs:      £400 + £900 = £1,300
Profit:           £1,500 - £1,300 = £200
```

This shows how the four measures the specification names -- revenue,
fixed costs, variable costs and total costs -- combine to produce the
single figure of profit, and why a business tracking only revenue
could be misled about its actual performance.

## Common mistakes

Confusing fixed costs with variable costs, or omitting one cost
category when calculating total costs. Describing a business form's
features without comparing it to the alternatives on liability, capital
access and control — the dimensions exam questions typically ask
about. Treating external environment factors as a fixed list to
recite, rather than applying a named factor's effect to the specific
business scenario given. Assuming AS content is less rigorously
examined than the equivalent A-level topic, when AS Business is
assessed as its own complete qualification.

## SMART objectives, applied to this topic

Although objective-setting is developed further later in the AS course,
Topic 1's introduction to why businesses exist is best paired with the
idea that whatever a business's overall aim, its objectives should be
**SMART** — specific, measurable, achievable, realistic and time-bound.
A business that says it wants to "grow" has stated a purpose but not an
objective; a business that says it wants to "increase market share by
5% within two years" has stated something specific and measurable
enough to actually judge success against later in the course.

## Quick revision checklist

- Define revenue, fixed costs, variable costs and total costs, and be
able to calculate profit from them for a given business.
- Compare forms of ownership on liability, access to capital, and
control — not as isolated definitions.
- Apply the external environment's six named factors (competition,
market conditions, incomes, interest rates, demographic factors, and
environmental issues and fair trade) to a specific business scenario
rather than listing them abstractly.
- Remember that AS Business (7131) is a stand-alone qualification with
its own full assessment on this content.
