This guide covers **3.1.1 Business and objectives** and **3.1.2 Forms
of business and stakeholders**, the first two sub-topics of Unit 3.1
(What is business? Managing marketing and finance) in AQA AS Business
(7137), for first teaching from September 2026. AQA runs AS as a
separate, stand-alone qualification that shares a single specification
document with A-level Business (7138) -- 3.1.1 and 3.1.2 are examined
on both AS Paper 1 (which covers the whole of Unit 3.1) and, as
background content, on AS Paper 2 (which focuses on Unit 3.2 but is
explicitly scoped by AQA to also draw on 3.1.1 and 3.1.2).

This guide does not cover the remainder of Unit 3.1 -- **3.1.3
Marketing management** and **3.1.4 Financial management** -- nor Unit
3.2 (**3.2.1 People management** and **3.2.2 Operations management**),
the unit AS Paper 2 focuses on. None of those four sub-topics is yet
published on this site as a dedicated AS resource.

## Where this fits in 7137

As the opening sub-topics of the AS course, Business and Objectives and
Forms of Business and Stakeholders introduce why businesses are
started, the legal structures they can take, and who has a stake in
them -- concepts that Unit 3.2 (Managing people and operations) and the
rest of Unit 3.1 build on directly.

## Syllabus coverage

**AQA AS BUSINESS (7137) — UNIT 3.1, SUB-TOPICS 3.1.1-3.1.2**

- **3.1.1 Business and objectives**
  - Entrepreneurs: reasons for setting up in business (financial and
    non-financial); characteristics of entrepreneurs (risk taker,
    resilient, focused, passionate, innovative, adaptable); challenges
    of setting up in business; how an entrepreneur's role changes as
    the business grows
  - Business planning and decisions: the purpose and value of a
    business plan; the meaning and importance of competitiveness;
    influences on business decisions (objectives, risk, reward,
    resources, market conditions, ethics, opportunity costs); the
    importance of risk and reward in decision making; ethical
    dilemmas in decision making, including profit versus ethics
  - Business objectives: the purpose and value of setting business
    objectives; the characteristics of effective objectives -- SMART
    (Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific)
- **3.1.2 Forms of business and stakeholders**
  - Different forms of business: sole traders; private limited
    companies (Ltd); public limited companies (plc); co-operatives
    (worker, consumer and producer); social enterprises
  - Issues to consider when comparing forms of business: control;
    objectives; sources of finance; distribution of profits; unlimited
    and limited liability; reasons to buy shares; influences on share
    price and the significance of share price changes
  - Calculate, interpret and analyse: market capitalisation; dividends
    per share; dividend yield
  - Stakeholders: internal (employees, owners/shareholders) and
    external (customers, suppliers, other creditors, community,
    government); the impact of business activity on stakeholders;
    ethical responsibilities to stakeholders

## How to approach it

Because AS Business is examined as a stand-alone qualification with its
own assessment, do not assume this content matters less than the
equivalent material studied at full A-level -- AS candidates are
examined on exactly this sub-topic set on Paper 1, and its concepts
recur throughout the rest of the AS course.

Start with **entrepreneurs and business planning (3.1.1)**: the
specification asks you to explain the reasons for setting up in
business, financial and non-financial: financial ones such as profit
and income, and non-financial ones such as independence, control,
turning a passion into a career and filling a gap the entrepreneur has
noticed, and to link named entrepreneurial
characteristics -- risk-taking, resilience, focus, passion, innovation,
adaptability -- to how they help someone navigate the real challenges
of setting up a business (funding, competition, uncertainty). Questions
frequently ask you to weigh up a business decision using the named
influences -- objectives, risk, reward, resources, market conditions,
ethics and opportunity cost -- for a specific scenario, rather than
listing the influences in the abstract. Practise stating a business
objective using the **SMART** framework the specification now names
directly (Specific, Measurable, Accountable, Realistic, Time specific):
a business that says it wants to "grow" has stated a growth objective
only in general terms, while one that says it wants to "increase market
share by 5% within two years" has stated the same objective specifically
enough to judge success against.

For **forms of business and stakeholders (3.1.2)**, revise by comparing
structures directly against each other on control, access to finance,
and liability, rather than learning each form's definition in
isolation: a sole trader has unlimited liability and full control but
limited access to finance; a private limited company has limited
liability and more access to finance, at the cost of some control and
a share of distributed profits going to other owners. This comparative
approach matches how exam questions typically ask you to recommend or
evaluate a form of business for a specific scenario. For companies, be
ready to calculate and interpret **market capitalisation** (number of
issued shares × current share price), **dividend per share** (total
dividend paid ÷ number of ordinary shares in issue), and **dividend
yield** (dividend per share ÷ share price, × 100) -- and to explain
**influences on share price**, such as trading performance, market
sentiment and the wider economy. Finally, be ready to distinguish
**internal stakeholders** (employees, owners/shareholders) from
**external stakeholders** (customers, suppliers, other creditors, the
community, government), and to explain how business activity affects
them and what ethical responsibilities a business has toward them.

## Worked example: dividend yield

A public limited company has 2,000,000 ordinary shares in issue,
trading at 150 pence each. It pays a total dividend of £160,000 for the
year.

```
Dividend per share:  £160,000 / 2,000,000 shares = 8 pence
Dividend yield:       (8p / 150p) x 100 = 5.33% (2 d.p.)
Market capitalisation: 2,000,000 shares x 150p = £3,000,000
```

This shows how the three formulae the specification names for 3.1.2 --
dividend per share, dividend yield and market capitalisation -- combine
figures a shareholder would actually be given (share price, shares in
issue, total dividend) into the measures they use to judge a company as
an investment.

## Common mistakes

Listing entrepreneurial characteristics or influences on business
decisions without applying them to the specific scenario in the
question -- exam questions ask you to explain how a named factor
affects a named business, not to recite a list. Confusing dividend per
share (an absolute amount, in pence) with dividend yield (a percentage
relative to share price). Describing a business form's features without
comparing it to the alternatives on control, finance and liability --
the dimensions exam questions typically ask about. Assuming AS content
is less rigorously examined than the equivalent A-level topic, when AS
Business is assessed as its own complete qualification.

## Quick revision checklist

- Explain financial and non-financial reasons for starting a business,
  and link named entrepreneurial characteristics to the real challenges
  of setting one up.
- Apply the named influences on business decisions (objectives, risk,
  reward, resources, market conditions, ethics, opportunity cost) to a
  specific scenario, and state an objective using the SMART framework.
- Compare forms of business on control, access to finance and
  liability -- not as isolated definitions -- including co-operatives
  and social enterprises alongside sole traders, Ltds and plcs.
- Calculate market capitalisation, dividend per share and dividend
  yield, and distinguish internal from external stakeholders.
- Remember that AS Business (7137) is a stand-alone qualification with
  its own full assessment on this content.
