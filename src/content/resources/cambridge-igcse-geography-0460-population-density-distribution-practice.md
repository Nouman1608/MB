These questions cover topic 1.4 Population density and distribution in Theme 1 (Population and settlement) of the Cambridge IGCSE Geography (0460) syllabus for examination in 2025 and 2026. All districts and countries in the data questions are fictional and the data are invented.

Revise first with the [Population Density and Distribution study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-geography-0460-population-density-distribution/) and the [Population Density and Distribution revision notes](/resources/igcse-geography-0460-population-density-distribution-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Define the term "population density". **[2]**

**2.** Explain the difference between population density and population distribution. **[2]**

**3.** Give two physical factors that can lead to a high population density. **[2]**

**4.** Give two political factors that can influence where people live in a country. **[2]**

**5.** State two reasons why high mountain areas are often sparsely populated. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** The table shows data for four fictional districts.

| District | Population | Area (km²) |
|---|---|---|
| Ashford Valley | 840,000 | 1,200 |
| Brenmoor Uplands | 36,000 | 4,500 |
| Coastline | 410,000 | 820 |
| Dunmere Plain | 275,000 | 2,500 |

**(a)** Calculate the population density of Brenmoor Uplands and of Coastline. **[2]**
**(b)** Rank the four districts from the highest to the lowest population density. **[1]**
**(c)** Suggest reasons why Brenmoor Uplands has a low population density. **[3]**

**7.** The table shows information about the four regions of the fictional Country Q.

| Region | Share of land area (%) | Share of population (%) | Main features |
|---|---|---|---|
| Northern desert | 55 | 4 | Hot, very low rainfall, a few oases |
| Central river valley | 10 | 58 | Fertile floodplain, capital city |
| Southern coast | 15 | 30 | Ports, tourist resorts, industry |
| Western mountains | 20 | 8 | Steep slopes, forest, mining towns |

**(a)** Describe the distribution of population in Country Q. **[3]**
**(b)** Explain why such a large share of the population lives in the central river valley. **[3]**

**8.** Explain how economic factors can lead to a high population density in an area. **[4]**

**9.** Suggest why a government may want to change the distribution of population in its country, and describe one method it could use. **[4]**

**10.** For a named densely populated country or area, explain why it is densely populated. **[7]**

**11.** For a named sparsely populated country or area, explain why it has a low population density. **[6]**

**12.** "Physical factors are more important than human factors in explaining where people live." To what extent do you agree? Use examples. **[8]**

## Answers

**1.** The average number of people living in a unit of area [1], for example people per square kilometre [1]. **[2]**

**2.** Density is a number: the average number of people per unit of area [1]. Distribution is the pattern of where people live, for example clustered along a coast or spread evenly [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* Show that density is a figure and distribution is a pattern; defining both as "how many people live in an area" earns nothing.

**3.** Fertile soils, such as volcanic soils or river silt [1]. A reliable supply of water [1]. Also credit flat land, a moderate climate or natural resources. **[2]**

**4.** Government policies to build new towns or a new capital city [1]. Conflict, which drives people away from some areas [1]. Also credit resettlement schemes and restrictions on settling in border zones. **[2]**

**5.** Steep slopes make building, farming and transport difficult [1]. Cold temperatures and thin soils give a short growing season [1]. Also credit remoteness and lack of jobs or services. **[2]**

**6. (a)** Brenmoor Uplands: 36,000 ÷ 4,500 = 8 people per km² [1]. Coastline: 410,000 ÷ 820 = 500 people per km² [1]. **[2]**
**(b)** Ashford Valley (700), Coastline (500), Dunmere Plain (110), Brenmoor Uplands (8) [1]. **[1]**
**(c)** Upland areas are likely to be cold, wet and windy, with thin soils, so farming is difficult [1]. Steep slopes make building and road construction expensive [1]. There are probably few jobs or services, so young people leave for the valley and coast [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Densities need units; "8" alone does not show what was calculated.

**7. (a)** The distribution is very uneven [1]. Most people (58%) live in the central river valley, which covers only 10% of the land, and another 30% live on the southern coast [1]. The northern desert is almost empty, with 4% of the people on 55% of the land, and the western mountains are also sparse, with 8% of the people on 20% of the land [1]. **[3]**
**(b)** The floodplain is flat and fertile, so crops can be grown easily [1]. The river provides water for drinking and irrigation in an otherwise dry country [1]. The capital city provides jobs in government, industry and services, attracting migrants from other regions [1]. **[3]**

**8.** Areas with many jobs in industry and services attract migrants looking for work [1]. Ports and good transport links attract businesses because goods can be moved cheaply [1]. Once firms locate there, more jobs and services are created, attracting more people and more firms [1]. Higher wages than in rural areas encourage people to stay and raise families, so the population grows further [1]. **[4]**

**9.** Reason: to reduce overcrowding, congestion and pressure on services in the main city or region [1]. Reason: to develop resources and bring jobs to remote or poorer regions [1]. Method: moving the capital or government offices to a new city, as Indonesia plans with Nusantara [1], or a resettlement scheme offering land and housing to families who move, as in Indonesia's transmigration programme [1]. **[4]**

**10.** The real exam marks extended answers with levels of response, not point by point. Indicative creditworthy content, for example for Java:

Named area and evidence of density: more than half of Indonesia's people live on Java [1]. Physical: fertile volcanic soils [1]; hot, wet climate allowing irrigated rice, often more than one harvest a year [1]. Historical and economic: the centre of colonial administration and plantations [1]; Jakarta's port, industry and government jobs attract migrants [1]. Social: dense networks of services and family ties keep people there [1]. Explanation of how factors reinforce each other, for example farming supported a large population that later provided a workforce for industry [1]. **[7]**
*Examiner insight:* Top answers give place-specific detail for each factor; "fertile soil and jobs" with no named area stays in the lowest level.

**11.** Indicative content, for example for Nunavut:

Named area with evidence: about 37,000 people over about 2 million km² [1]. Physical: very cold, long winters and permafrost make building and water supply difficult [1]. Physical: no crops can be grown, so food must be brought in [1]. Economic: few jobs apart from government and mining [1]. Accessibility: no road links to the rest of Canada, so supplies come by air or summer ships, making living costs high [1]. Explanation linking factors, for example high costs and few jobs discourage people from moving there [1]. **[6]**

**12.** Indicative content:

Agree: physical factors explain many sparse areas, such as deserts, polar regions and high mountains [1]. Agree: fertile soils and water explain many dense areas, such as river valleys and volcanic islands [1]. Disagree: economic factors such as jobs, ports and industry explain the growth of cities that are now the densest places [1]. Disagree: technology can overcome physical limits, for example irrigation, desalination and air conditioning allow cities in deserts [1]. Disagree: political decisions, such as new capitals or resettlement schemes, change distributions [1]. Social factors, such as family ties and services, keep people in established areas [1]. Use of named examples for both sides [1]. Judgement: physical factors set the original pattern, but human factors now explain most changes in where people live [1]. **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* "To what extent" questions need a clear final judgement; a balanced list without one cannot gain the top level.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Dividing area by population instead of population by area.
- Leaving units off density answers.
- Describing reasons when the question asks only for the distribution.
- Explaining only physical factors.
- Case studies without a named area or specific facts.
