These questions cover **C5.1 Monitoring chemical reactions** from OCR's J248 specification for Chemistry A (Gateway Science) at GCSE (9–1), Version 4.0, dated August 2026: outcomes C5.1a–C5.1k, CM5.1i–CM5.1vi, and the PAG C6 and PAG C8 practical suggestions. Questions on Higher Tier only statements (C5.1a, c, d, e, f, g and k) are labelled; the rest suit both tiers. Topic C5 appears on the Foundation Tier in Paper 2 and on the Higher Tier in Paper 4.

Learn it first in the [study guide](/resources/ocr-gcse-chemistry-monitoring-chemical-reactions/) or the [revision notes](/resources/ocr-gcse-chemistry-monitoring-chemical-reactions-revision-notes/); the [course hub for OCR J248 chemistry](/boards/ocr/gcse/chemistry/) and the [printable J248 statement checklist](/checklists/ocr/gcse/chemistry/) place C5.1 in the course. Use Ar: Mg = 24.3, Al = 27.0, K = 39.1, Na = 23.0, Cr = 52.0; O = 16.0, N = 14.0, Cl = 35.5, C = 12.0, H = 1.0, I = 126.9. Molar gas volume = 24 dm³ at room temperature and pressure (RTP); Avogadro constant = 6.02 × 10²³ per mole.

## Questions

**1.** **(a)** Define the atom economy of a reaction. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why a reaction that forms only one product has an atom economy of 100%. **[1]**

**2.** A student makes copper(II) sulfate crystals. The theoretical yield is 12.5 g. She collects 9.80 g of dry crystals.

**(a)** Calculate the percentage yield. **[2]**
**(b)** Suggest **two** ways product could have been lost or not formed. **[2]**
**(c)** Another student reports a yield of 13.1 g from the same method. Suggest why. **[1]**

**3.** Chromium can be extracted by heating chromium(III) oxide with aluminium:

Cr₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Cr + Al₂O₃

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy for making chromium. Give your answer to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Suggest how the company could reduce the waste from this process. **[1]**

**4.** Describe how to find, by titration, the volume of dilute nitric acid that exactly neutralises 25.0 cm³ of sodium hydroxide solution. Include apparatus, indicator and how to get reliable results. **[6]**

**5.** **(Higher Tier only)** A solution is made by dissolving 8.30 g of potassium iodide, KI, in water and making it up to 500 cm³.

**(a)** Calculate the concentration in g/dm³. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration in mol/dm³. **[2]**
**(c)** Calculate the mass of potassium iodide needed to make 250 cm³ of a 0.0400 mol/dm³ solution. **[2]**

**6.** **(Higher Tier only)** 25.0 cm³ of barium hydroxide solution is titrated with 0.150 mol/dm³ nitric acid.

Ba(OH)₂ + 2HNO₃ → Ba(NO₃)₂ + 2H₂O

| Run | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titre (cm³) | 23.40 | 22.85 | 22.95 | 22.90 |

**(a)** Identify the concordant titres and calculate the mean titre. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the concentration of the barium hydroxide solution in mol/dm³. **[4]**

**7.** **(Higher Tier only)** Magnesium carbonate reacts with hydrochloric acid:

MgCO₃ + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂

**(a)** Calculate the volume of carbon dioxide, in cm³ at RTP, formed from 2.53 g of magnesium carbonate. **[3]**
**(b)** Calculate the minimum volume of 2.00 mol/dm³ hydrochloric acid needed to react with all of the 2.53 g. **[2]**

**8.** **(Higher Tier only)** Ammonia forms from nitrogen and hydrogen: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) → 2NH₃(g). A mixture of 90 cm³ of nitrogen and 300 cm³ of hydrogen reacts completely, at constant temperature and pressure. Identify the gas in excess, and calculate the volume of ammonia formed and the volume of excess gas left over. **[3]**

**9.** **(Higher Tier only)** 0.729 g of magnesium ribbon is burned in air: 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO.

**(a)** Calculate the theoretical mass of magnesium oxide. **[3]**
**(b)** The student collects 1.04 g of magnesium oxide. Calculate the percentage yield to 3 significant figures. **[1]**

**10.** **(Higher Tier only)** A gas syringe collects 120 cm³ of carbon dioxide at RTP.

**(a)** Calculate the number of CO₂ molecules, in standard form. **[2]**
**(b)** Calculate the total number of atoms in this sample. **[1]**

**11.** **(Higher Tier only)** A student makes a standard solution by dissolving 2.50 g of potassium hydrogencarbonate, KHCO₃, and making it up to 250 cm³ in a volumetric flask. She titrates 25.0 cm³ portions with hydrochloric acid, using methyl orange.

KHCO₃ + HCl → KCl + H₂O + CO₂

| Run | Rough | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Titre (cm³) | 21.30 | 20.75 | 20.85 | 20.80 |

**(a)** Calculate the concentration of the KHCO₃ solution in mol/dm³, to 3 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Work out the mean of the concordant titres. **[1]**
**(c)** Find the hydrochloric acid concentration, in mol/dm³. **[3]**

**12.** **(Higher Tier only)** Chloroethane, C₂H₅Cl, can be made by two routes.

Route A: C₂H₄ + HCl → C₂H₅Cl
Route B: C₂H₆ + Cl₂ → C₂H₅Cl + HCl

| | Route A | Route B |
|---|---|---|
| Yield of chloroethane | 90% | 40% |
| Conditions | catalyst, moderate rate | ultraviolet light, fast |
| Other products | none | saleable HCl; also C₂H₄Cl₂ and other chlorinated products |
| Cost of organic starting material | higher (ethene) | lower (ethane) |

**(a)** Calculate the atom economy of each route. Give route B's to 3 significant figures. **[3]**
**(b)** Using all the data, evaluate which route a manufacturer should choose. **[5]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The percentage of the total mass of reactants that ends up in the desired product (from the balanced equation) [1]
**(b)** All the reactant atoms end up in the desired product, so none is wasted as a by-product [1] **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "The amount of product made" describes yield; the definition needs reactant mass ending up in the **desired** product.

**2. (a)** (9.80 ÷ 12.5) × 100 [1] = **78.4%** [1]
**(b)** Any two: product left on apparatus or filter paper; some stayed dissolved after crystallising; incomplete reaction [1] [1]
**(c)** The crystals were still wet (or impure), so the mass is too high and the yield would be over 100% [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* "Human error" is too vague to credit; name a specific step where product is lost.

**3. (a)** Total Mr of reactants = 152.0 + (2 × 27.0) = 206.0 [1]; desired product 2Cr = 2 × 52.0 = 104.0 [1]; atom economy = 104.0 ÷ 206.0 × 100 = **50.5%** [1]
**(b)** Find a use for, or sell, the aluminium oxide by-product [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 52.0 for Cr instead of 2 × 52.0 gives 25.2%; always multiply by the coefficients in the balanced equation.

**4.** Indicative points, one mark each, up to six:
- measure 25.0 cm³ of the alkali with a pipette and filler, into a conical flask [1]
- add an indicator such as phenolphthalein or methyl orange [1]
- fill a burette with the acid and take readings at eye level from the meniscus bottom [1]
- add acid while swirling, on a white tile, drop by drop near the end point [1]
- stop when the colour change stays (pink to colourless with phenolphthalein) [1]
- repeat after a rough run until titres are concordant (within 0.10 cm³); average these [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Naming universal indicator costs the indicator point because its gradual colour change gives no sharp end point; give one specific indicator and its colour change.

**5. (a)** 8.30 ÷ 0.500 = **16.6 g/dm³** [1]
**(b)** Amount = 8.30 ÷ 166.0 = 0.0500 mol [1]; concentration = 0.0500 ÷ 0.500 = **0.100 mol/dm³** [1]
**(c)** Amount = 0.0400 × 0.250 = 0.0100 mol [1]; mass = 0.0100 × 166.0 = **1.66 g** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Dividing by 500 instead of 0.500 gives answers 1000 times too small; write the volume in dm³ first.

**6. (a)** Concordant titres: 22.85, 22.95 and 22.90 cm³ (rough excluded) [1]; mean = **22.90 cm³** [1]
**(b)** Amount of HNO₃ = 0.150 × 0.02290 = 0.003435 mol [1]; ratio Ba(OH)₂ : HNO₃ = 1 : 2 [1], so amount of Ba(OH)₂ = 0.0017175 mol [1]; concentration = 0.0017175 ÷ 0.0250 = **0.0687 mol/dm³** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Missing the 1 : 2 ratio doubles the answer to 0.137 mol/dm³; state the ratio in words or numbers so the step is visible.

**7. (a)** Mr(MgCO₃) = 84.3, so amount = 2.53 ÷ 84.3 = 0.0300 mol [1]; 1 : 1 ratio gives 0.0300 mol CO₂ [1]; volume = 0.0300 × 24 000 = **720 cm³** [1]
**(b)** Amount of HCl = 2 × 0.0300 = 0.0600 mol [1]; volume = 0.0600 ÷ 2.00 = 0.0300 dm³ = **30.0 cm³** [1] **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Using 24 when cm³ is asked gives 0.720; match the molar volume to the unit.

**8.** 90 cm³ of N₂ needs 3 × 90 = 270 cm³ of H₂, so **hydrogen is in excess** [1]; ammonia formed = 2 × 90 = **180 cm³** [1]; excess hydrogen = 300 − 270 = **30 cm³** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Working from the 300 cm³ of hydrogen gives 200 cm³ of ammonia, impossible with so little nitrogen.

**9. (a)** Mg: 0.729 ÷ 24.3 = 0.0300 mol [1]; ratio 2 : 2, so 0.0300 mol MgO [1]; mass = 0.0300 × 40.3 = **1.21 g** [1]
**(b)** (1.04 ÷ 1.209) × 100 = **86.0%** [1] **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* Mr(MgO) is 40.3, not 2 × 40.3; the 2s cancel in the ratio.

**10. (a)** Amount = 120 ÷ 24 000 = 0.00500 mol [1]; molecules = 0.00500 × 6.02 × 10²³ = **3.01 × 10²¹** [1]
**(b)** Each CO₂ has 3 atoms: 3 × 3.01 × 10²¹ = **9.03 × 10²¹ atoms** [1] **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* Writing 3.01²¹ instead of 3.01 × 10²¹ is not standard form; show the power of ten clearly.

**11. (a)** Mr(KHCO₃) = 100.1, amount = 2.50 ÷ 100.1 = 0.02498 mol [1]; concentration = 0.02498 ÷ 0.250 = **0.0999 mol/dm³** [1]
**(b)** (20.75 + 20.85 + 20.80) ÷ 3 = **20.80 cm³** [1]
**(c)** Amount of KHCO₃ in 25.0 cm³ = 0.0999 × 0.0250 = 0.002498 mol [1]; 1 : 1 ratio, so 0.002498 mol HCl [1]; concentration = 0.002498 ÷ 0.02080 = **0.120 mol/dm³** [1] **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Carry at least four significant figures through multistep working and round only the final answer.

**12. (a)** Route A has one product, so atom economy = **100%** [1]. Route B: Mr(C₂H₅Cl) = 64.5 and total Mr of reactants = 30.0 + 71.0 = 101.0 [1]; 64.5 ÷ 101.0 × 100 = **63.9%** [1]
**(b)** Indicative points, one mark each, up to five:
- route A has the higher atom economy, so less waste [1]
- route A has the higher yield, 90% compared with 40% [1]
- route B forms a mixture of chlorinated products that must be separated, adding cost [1]
- for route B: saleable HCl, cheaper ethane and a fast reaction [1]
- a justified conclusion, such as route A because its yield and atom economy outweigh the cheaper starting material [1] **[8]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation needs points on both sides and a conclusion tied to the data; listing only route A's advantages limits the credit.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Leaving volumes in cm³ when the formula needs dm³.
- Skipping a 1 : 2 or 2 : 1 mole ratio, as in questions 6 and 7.
- Including a rough titre in the mean.
- Mixing up 24 and 24 000 for the molar gas volume.
- Writing a bare number with no working on a multistep calculation.
- Choosing a route on atom economy alone when other data are given.
