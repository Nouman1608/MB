## Section A

**1.** Write 47 mm in centimetres. **[1]**

**2.** Explain whether a triangle can be constructed with sides 4 cm, 5 cm and 10 cm. **[2]**

**3.** A plan is drawn to a scale of 1 : 50. A wall is 3.2 m long. Work out the length of the wall on the plan in centimetres. **[2]**

**4.** A map has a scale of 1 : 60 000. Two villages are 5.5 cm apart on the map. Work out the real distance between them in kilometres. **[2]**

**5.** Describe how to construct an angle of 30° using only a straight edge and compasses. **[2]**

## Section B

**6.** Construct triangle ABC with AB = 8 cm, AC = 6 cm and BC = 7 cm. Show all your construction lines.

**(a)** Make the accurate drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** Measure the largest angle of the triangle. **[1]**

**7.** A line segment AB is 9 cm long.

**(a)** Describe how to construct the perpendicular bisector of AB using a straight edge and compasses. **[3]**

**(b)** A point P on the perpendicular bisector is 6 cm from A. Write down the distance of P from B, and give a reason. **[1]**

**(c)** Calculate the distance of P from the midpoint of AB. **[2]**

**8.** Draw an angle of 70°. Using only a straight edge and compasses, construct the bisector of the angle.

**(a)** List the steps of the construction. **[3]**

**(b)** Write down the size of each angle formed by the bisector. **[1]**

**9.** Construct triangle PQR with PQ = 6 cm, angle QPR = 110° and PR = 4 cm.

**(a)** Make the accurate drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** Measure QR. **[1]**

**10.** A rectangular room measures 5.4 m by 3.8 m.

**(a)** Draw a plan of the room using a scale of 1 : 100. Write down the dimensions of your drawing. **[2]**

**(b)** Measure the diagonal of your plan and use it to find the real length of the diagonal of the room. **[2]**

**11.** A walker leaves a point S and walks 5 km on a bearing of 040° to a point T. She then walks 3 km on a bearing of 130° to a point U.

**(a)** Make a scale drawing using 1 cm to represent 1 km. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your drawing to find the distance of U from S. **[1]**

**(c)** Use your drawing to find the bearing of U from S. **[1]**

**12.** Construct a rhombus with sides of 6 cm and one angle of 60°, using compasses for the 60° angle.

**(a)** Make the accurate drawing. **[3]**

**(b)** Measure both diagonals. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** **4.7 cm** [1].

**2.** No [1]: the two shorter sides add to 4 + 5 = 9 cm, which is less than 10 cm, so the arcs from the ends of the 10 cm side would never meet [1].

**3.** 3.2 m = 320 cm [1]; 320 ÷ 50 = **6.4 cm** [1].

**4.** 5.5 × 60 000 = 330 000 cm [1] = **3.3 km** [1].

**5.** Construct an equilateral triangle (arcs of equal radius from both ends of a line) to give an angle of 60° [1]. Bisect the 60° angle with compasses to give 30° [1].

**6. (a)** AB drawn as 8 cm [1]; arcs of radius 6 cm from A and 7 cm from B crossing at C, with arcs visible [1].

**(b)** The largest angle is opposite the longest side AB, so it is angle ACB: about **75°** to **76°** (calculated value 75.5°) [1].

**7. (a)** Open the compasses to more than half of AB (more than 4.5 cm) [1]. From A, draw arcs above and below AB; with the same setting, draw arcs from B to cross them [1]. Join the two crossing points with a straight line [1].

**(b)** **6 cm**, because every point on the perpendicular bisector is the same distance from A as from B [1].

**(c)** The midpoint M is 4.5 cm from A and angle PMA = 90°, so PM = √(6² − 4.5²) [1] = √15.75 = **3.97 cm** (3 s.f.) [1].

**8. (a)** From the vertex, draw an arc crossing both arms [1]. From each crossing point, draw arcs with the same radius that meet inside the angle [1]. Join the vertex to the meeting point [1].

**(b)** **35°** [1].

**9. (a)** PQ drawn as 6 cm with an angle of 110° measured at P [1]; PR = 4 cm marked along the arm and QR joined [1].

**(b)** About **8.3 cm** (calculated value 8.27 cm) [1].

**10. (a)** 540 ÷ 100 = 5.4 cm and 380 ÷ 100 = 3.8 cm [1]; a rectangle drawn 5.4 cm by 3.8 cm [1].

**(b)** Diagonal measures about 6.6 cm [1]; real length 6.6 × 100 = 660 cm = **6.6 m** [1]. (By Pythagoras, √(5.4² + 3.8²) = 6.60 m.)

**11. (a)** North line at S and ST = 5 cm drawn at 040° [1]; new north line at T, parallel to the first [1]; TU = 3 cm drawn at 130° [1].

**(b)** SU measures about 5.8 cm, so about **5.8 km** [1]. (Angle STU = 90° because the bearings differ by 90°, so SU = √(5² + 3²) = 5.83 km.)

**(c)** About **071°** [1]. (40° + angle TSU, where tan TSU = 3/5, gives 40° + 31.0° = 071°.)

**12. (a)** One side drawn as 6 cm [1]; an arc of radius 6 cm from each end crossing to make a 60° angle, with arcs visible [1]; the fourth vertex found with two 6 cm arcs and all sides joined [1].

**(b)** Short diagonal **6 cm** [1]; long diagonal about **10.4 cm** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Question 2: answering "yes, because all lengths are positive". Check that the two shorter sides add to more than the longest.
- Question 4: dividing by 60 000 instead of multiplying, or converting cm to km by dividing by 1000 instead of 100 000.
- Question 6: rubbing out the arcs. The arcs are the evidence of the method.
- Question 7(a): opening the compasses to less than half of AB, so the arcs do not meet.
- Question 11: measuring the second bearing from the north line at S instead of drawing a new north line at T.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can you use a protractor when bisecting an angle?**
Not when the question says straight edge and compasses only. Construct the bisector with arcs and leave them visible; you can use a protractor afterwards just to check.

**How accurate does a construction need to be?**
Draw lengths to the nearest millimetre and angles to the nearest degree, as statement 4.5 A and 4.4 E expect, and check by measuring the parts you were given.
