These notes condense outcomes 1.1.8 to 1.1.10 of the Pearson Edexcel International Advanced Subsidiary/Advanced Level in Accounting (YAC11) specification, Issue 2: the concepts of going concern, prudence, accruals and consistency; the concepts and conventions of historic cost, materiality, money measurement, realisation and business entity; and the use of International Accounting Standards. The full explanation and a multi-item correction are in the [concepts study guide](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-accounting-concepts-and-conventions/), and exam-style questions are in the [concepts practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-accounting-yac11-accounting-concepts-and-conventions-practice/). Amounts are in dollars; businesses are invented.

## Key terms

| Concept | Rule | Test question to ask |
|---|---|---|
| Going concern | The business will continue for the foreseeable future | Is closure planned or likely? |
| Prudence | Do not overstate assets or profit; do not understate liabilities or expenses | Is there doubt about a loss or a gain? |
| Accruals | Income and expenses belong to the period they relate to | Does the cash fall in a different period from the benefit? |
| Consistency | Same policies each period | Has a method changed, and is it justified and disclosed? |
| Historic cost | Assets at the price paid | Has an asset been revalued without reason? |
| Materiality | Strict treatment only for items that could affect decisions | Is the amount significant compared with profit or assets? |
| Money measurement | Only items measurable in money | Is a value being placed on something like skill or reputation? |
| Realisation | Revenue when goods or services are provided and payment is due | Have the goods been delivered or accepted? |
| Business entity | Business and owner are separate | Is this the owner's private transaction? |
| Net realisable value | Selling price less costs to complete and sell | Is inventory worth less than it cost? |

## Effect of correcting common breaches

| Breach | Correction | Profit | Statement of financial position |
|---|---|---|---|
| Owner's private bill charged as expense | Treat as drawings | Up | Drawings up |
| Sale or return goods recorded as sales | Remove sale; add to inventory at cost | Down by the profit margin | Receivables down; inventory up |
| Income received in advance included | Remove from income | Down | Other payables up |
| Expense owing not recorded | Add accrued expense | Down | Other payables up |
| Inventory above NRV | Write down to NRV | Down | Inventory down |
| Small item capitalised | Expense it | Down | Non-current assets down |
| Asset revalued upwards in the records | Restore cost | Unchanged (gain not taken to profit) | Asset down |

## Mini correction

Draft profit $25,000. Owner's home electricity of $600 charged to the business. Goods costing $1,200 sent on sale or return at $1,800, recorded as sales and excluded from inventory. Advertising of $450 for this year unpaid and not recorded.

```
Draft profit                          25,000
Private electricity (entity)            +600
Sale or return (realisation)            −600   (−1,800 revenue, +1,200 inventory)
Advertising owing (accruals)            −450
Corrected profit                      24,550
```

## Concepts in conflict

| Pair | Tension | Usual resolution |
|---|---|---|
| Prudence vs accruals | Carry a cost forward to match future revenue, or expense it because revenue is uncertain? | Expense when doubt is serious |
| Prudence vs consistency | Keep a method, or change to one that gives a fairer view? | Change only with good reason; disclose the effect |
| Historic cost vs relevance | Objective old cost or useful current value? | Historic cost, with users aware of its limits |
| Prudence vs neutrality | Caution, or deliberate understatement? | Caution only; no hidden reserves |

## IAS links

| Standard | Covers | Concepts |
|---|---|---|
| IAS 1 | Presentation of financial statements | Going concern, accruals basis, materiality, consistency of presentation |
| IAS 2 | Inventories: lower of cost and NRV | Prudence, historic cost |
| IAS 8 | Accounting policies, changes in estimates, errors | Consistency |
| IAS 16 | Property, plant and equipment; depreciation | Historic cost, accruals |

Benefits: comparability, trust, less scope to flatter results. Limits: choices and estimates remain; compliance cost; standards change.

Use IAS terms in answers: trade receivables, trade payables, inventory, non-current assets, statement of financial position.

## Exam traps

- Defining a concept without applying it to the scenario.
- Accruals and realisation confused.
- Prudence used to justify understatement.
- Only one side of a sale or return correction made.
- NRV taken as selling price, ignoring costs to complete and sell.
- Saying consistency forbids every change.
- Entity breach corrected in profit but not shown as drawings.

## Self-test

1. Which concept justifies charging depreciation?
2. Which concept is broken by valuing inventory at selling price?
3. Goods are delivered on 28 December and paid for on 15 January. The year ends on 31 December. In which year is the revenue recognised, and under which concept?
4. A business plans to close in two months. Which concept no longer applies, and what changes?
5. The owner pays a $300 personal phone bill from the business bank account. State the correct entry.
6. Inventory cost $2,000; it can be sold for $1,700 after spending $250 on repairs. Find its value.
7. A $25 hole punch lasts five years. How is it treated, and why?
8. Why can staff skill not appear in the statement of financial position?
9. Give one argument for and one against changing depreciation method.
10. State the effect on profit and on the statement of financial position of recording an accrued expense of $700.
11. Name the IAS that deals with inventories.
12. Give one benefit and one limitation of IAS.

### Answers

1. Accruals (matching).
2. Prudence (and historic cost).
3. This year, because the goods were provided on 28 December: realisation.
4. Going concern; assets should be valued at what they would realise on closure rather than cost less depreciation.
5. Debit drawings $300, credit bank $300.
6. NRV = 1,700 − 250 = $1,450, below cost, so $1,450.
7. As an expense, because it is not material.
8. It cannot be measured reliably in money (money measurement).
9. For: the new method may reflect the asset's use more fairly. Against: it breaks consistency and makes comparison with earlier years harder.
10. Profit falls by $700; other payables (current liabilities) rise by $700.
11. IAS 2.
12. Benefit: comparability between businesses. Limitation: estimates and choices remain, so comparability is not complete.
