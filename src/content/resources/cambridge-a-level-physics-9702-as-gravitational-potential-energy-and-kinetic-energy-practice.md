Take g = 9.81 m s⁻² throughout.

## Section A

**1.** State what is meant by **(a)** gravitational potential energy and **(b)** kinetic energy. **[2]**

**2.** Using W = Fs, derive the expression ∆Eₚ = mg∆h for the change in gravitational potential energy of a mass m raised through a height ∆h. **[3]**

**3.** A tennis ball of mass 0.060 kg is served at 45 m s⁻¹. Calculate its kinetic energy. **[2]**

**4.** Explain why ∆Eₚ = mg∆h would give an inaccurate value for the gravitational potential energy gained by a satellite raised from the Earth's surface to a height of 20 000 km. **[2]**

**5.** Car A has mass m and speed 2v. Car B has mass 2m and speed v. Calculate the ratio of the kinetic energy of A to the kinetic energy of B. **[2]**

## Section B

**6. (a)** A constant resultant force F acts on an object of mass m, initially at rest, over a displacement s. Using the equations of motion, derive the expression Eₖ = ½mv² for the kinetic energy of the object. **[3]**

**(b)** Use your result to show that, for a constant braking force, the braking distance of a vehicle is proportional to the square of its initial speed. **[2]**

**7.** A child of mass 35 kg starts from rest at the top of a water slide. The top of the slide is 6.0 m vertically above the bottom, and the slide is 15 m long. The child reaches the bottom at 9.5 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the gravitational potential energy lost by the child. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the kinetic energy of the child at the bottom. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the average resistive force on the child along the slide. **[2]**

**8.** A ball is thrown with a speed of 14 m s⁻¹ from a balcony 9.0 m above the ground. Air resistance is negligible.

**(a)** Calculate the speed of the ball as it reaches the ground. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why your answer to **(a)** does not depend on the direction in which the ball is thrown. **[2]**

**9.** A pendulum bob of mass 0.20 kg hangs on a string of length 0.80 m. The bob is pulled aside until the string makes an angle of 35° with the vertical, and then released from rest.

**(a)** Show that the bob is raised through a height of about 0.14 m. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the speed of the bob at the lowest point of its swing. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the tension in the string does not appear in the energy calculation. **[1]**

**10.** A car of mass 1500 kg travels at 20 m s⁻¹. The driver applies the brakes, producing a constant braking force of 6000 N.

**(a)** Calculate the initial kinetic energy of the car. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the braking distance. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the speed of the car after it has travelled 30 m with the brakes applied. **[3]**

**11.** A ball of mass 0.50 kg is dropped from rest from a height of 25 m. It reaches the ground at 18 m s⁻¹.

**(a)** Calculate the work done against air resistance during the fall. **[3]**

**(b)** Calculate the average air resistance acting on the ball. **[1]**

**12.** A crate of mass 40 kg is pulled at constant speed up a ramp 5.0 m long, inclined at 30° to the horizontal. The frictional force on the crate is 60 N and the pulling force is parallel to the ramp.

**(a)** Calculate the gain in gravitational potential energy of the crate. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the total work done by the pulling force. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the pulling force. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** The energy a mass has because of its **position in a gravitational field** [1].

**(b)** The energy a mass has because of its **motion** [1].

**2.** The mass is raised at constant speed, so the upward force **F = mg** [1]. The displacement in the direction of the force is ∆h, so W = Fs = **mg∆h** [1]. This work done is stored as gravitational potential energy, so ∆Eₚ = mg∆h (valid in a uniform field) [1].

**3.** Eₖ = ½ × 0.060 × 45² [1] = **61 J** [1].

**4.** ∆Eₚ = mg∆h assumes a **uniform field**, with g constant over the height change [1]. Over 20 000 km, g **decreases significantly** with distance from the Earth, so the formula overestimates the energy gained [1].

**5.** Eₖ(A) = ½m(2v)² = 2mv²; Eₖ(B) = ½(2m)v² = mv² [1]. Ratio A : B = **2 : 1** [1].

**6. (a)** F = ma [1]. From v² = u² + 2as with u = 0, s = v²/2a [1]. W = Fs = ma × v²/2a = ½mv², and this work becomes kinetic energy, so **Eₖ = ½mv²** [1].

**(b)** The braking force does work F s equal to the kinetic energy removed: Fs = ½mv² [1]. So s = mv²/2F; with m and F constant, **s ∝ v²** [1].

**7. (a)** ∆Eₚ = mg∆h = 35 × 9.81 × 6.0 [1] = **2.06 × 10³ J** [1].

**(b)** Eₖ = ½ × 35 × 9.5² [1] = **1.58 × 10³ J** [1].

**(c)** Work done against resistance = 2060 − 1579 = 481 J [1]. Force = 481 ÷ 15 (length of slide) = **32 N** [1].

**8. (a)** ½mv² = ½mu² + mg∆h, so v² = u² + 2g∆h [1] = 14² + 2 × 9.81 × 9.0 = 373 [1]. v = **19 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(b)** Kinetic energy and potential energy are **scalars**, so only the speed, not the direction, enters the energy equation [1]. The ball loses the same GPE whatever its path, so it gains the same kinetic energy [1].

**9. (a)** Height raised = L − L cos 35° = 0.80(1 − cos 35°) [1] = 0.80 × 0.181 = **0.145 m** ≈ 0.14 m [1].

**(b)** v = √(2g∆h) = √(2 × 9.81 × 0.145) [1] = **1.7 m s⁻¹** [1].

**(c)** The tension is always **perpendicular to the motion** of the bob, so it does **no work** [1].

**10. (a)** Eₖ = ½ × 1500 × 20² = **3.0 × 10⁵ J** [1].

**(b)** Fs = Eₖ, so s = 3.0 × 10⁵ ÷ 6000 [1] = **50 m** [1].

**(c)** Work done by brakes in 30 m = 6000 × 30 = 1.8 × 10⁵ J [1]. Remaining Eₖ = 3.0 × 10⁵ − 1.8 × 10⁵ = 1.2 × 10⁵ J [1]. v = √(2 × 1.2 × 10⁵ ÷ 1500) = **13 m s⁻¹** [1].

**11. (a)** GPE lost = 0.50 × 9.81 × 25 = 122.6 J [1]. Eₖ gained = ½ × 0.50 × 18² = 81 J [1]. Work against air resistance = 122.6 − 81 = **42 J** [1].

**(b)** Average force = 41.6 ÷ 25 = **1.7 N** [1].

**12. (a)** ∆h = 5.0 sin 30° = 2.5 m [1]; ∆Eₚ = 40 × 9.81 × 2.5 = **981 J** [1].

**(b)** Work against friction = 60 × 5.0 = 300 J [1]. Constant speed, so no change in Eₖ: total work = 981 + 300 = **1.28 × 10³ J** [1].

**(c)** F = 1281 ÷ 5.0 = **256 N** [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the 15 m slide length or the 5.0 m ramp length in mg∆h (Questions 7 and 12).
- Missing the initial kinetic energy of an object that is already moving (Question 8).
- Leaving out the equation of motion in the kinetic energy derivation, or not stating the starting conditions (Question 6).
- Finding the pendulum height as L cos θ instead of L(1 − cos θ) (Question 9).
- Assuming the speed falls to half its value after half the braking distance: kinetic energy, not speed, falls in proportion to the distance (Question 10).
- Giving air resistance or friction as a negative force rather than stating its magnitude.
