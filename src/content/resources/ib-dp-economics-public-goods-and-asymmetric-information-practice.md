## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by a non-rivalrous good and a non-excludable good. **[2]**

**2.** Classify each good as a public good or a private good, with a reason. (i) A radio weather signal from a hilltop mast that any radio can receive. (ii) A seat at a ticketed concert. (iii) A sea wall that defends one entire bay. **[3]**

**3.** (calculator allowed) The fictional village of Harrowby has 90 households. A shared drainage-alert system costs 2,250 veldors. Each household values it at 36 veldors.
**(a)** Show that building it gives a net benefit to society of 990 veldors. **[2]**
**(b)** A private firm asks for 25 veldors from each household. Only 18 pay. Calculate the shortfall and state what happens to the system. **[2]**

**4.** (calculator allowed) Harrowby's council plans a coastal path for 60 households, each valuing it at 42 veldors. Its own cost estimate is 2,100 veldors. Three firms bid: 2,310, 1,890 and 2,040 veldors.
**(a)** Calculate the saving from accepting the lowest bid. **[1]**
**(b)** Calculate the net gain per household if the cost is shared equally. **[2]**
**(c)** Suggest one reason why the lowest bid may not give the best outcome. **[1]**

**5.** Explain why a profit-seeking firm is unlikely to supply a good that is non-excludable. **[4]**

**6.** Evaluate direct provision and contracting out to the private sector as responses to the under-provision of public goods. **[10]**

**7.** (HL only) Distinguish between adverse selection and moral hazard, giving one example of each. **[4]**

**8.** (HL only, calculator allowed) In the fictional market of Kormas, half of all used laptops are good. Buyers value a good laptop at 700 and a poor one at 300. Sellers value them at 560 and 200. Buyers cannot observe quality before buying.
**(a)** Find the value a buyer expects from a laptop picked at random. **[1]**
**(b)** Explain why sellers of good laptops leave the market. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the smallest share of good laptops at which good sellers would stay. **[2]**

**9.** (HL only, calculator allowed) A shop owner in Kormas faces a fire with a loss of 12,000 veldors. Without care, the chance of fire is 15%. Care costs 600 veldors and lowers the chance to 5%.
**(a)** Show that an uninsured owner takes care. **[2]**
**(b)** Explain why a fully insured owner does not. **[2]**
**(c)** The insurer adds an excess of 9,000 veldors. Show that the owner takes care again. **[2]**

**10.** (HL only) Distinguish between signalling and screening as private responses to asymmetric information. **[4]**

**11.** (HL only) Evaluate government legislation and regulation, and the provision of information, as responses to adverse selection in the used-laptop market of Kormas. **[10]**

## Answers

**1.** Non-rivalrous: when one person uses it, the same amount remains for everyone else [1]. Non-excludable: non-payers cannot be prevented from using it [1]. **[2]**
*Examiner insight:* "State" asks for a brief answer, so one clear sentence per term is enough; extra detail is wasted time.

**2.** (i) Public: anyone with a radio receives it and one listener does not reduce another's signal [1]. (ii) Private: the seat is occupied by one person and non-ticket holders can be turned away [1]. (iii) Public: the wall protects every property in the bay and defending one does not reduce protection for another [1]. **[3]**
*Examiner insight:* A classification mark needs a reason that uses both features, not just a label.

**3. (a)** Total benefit = 90 × 36 = 3,240 [1]. Net benefit = 3,240 − 2,250 = **990 veldors** [1].
**(b)** Revenue = 18 × 25 = 450, so shortfall = 2,250 − 450 = **1,800 veldors** [1]. The system is not built, although society would gain [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Show that" needs each step written out, ending on the given value; a bare 990 does not demonstrate it.

**4. (a)** Lowest bid 1,890; saving = 2,100 − 1,890 = **210 veldors** [1].
**(b)** Share = 1,890 ÷ 60 = 31.5 [1]. Net gain = 42 − 31.5 = **10.5 veldors** per household [1].
**(c)** The firm may cut quality to protect its margin, or the contract may be costly to monitor [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Calculate" asks for working; a correct figure with no working is a risk if one step slips.

**5.** Non-payers cannot be excluded, so the firm cannot charge for use [1]. Each consumer can reason that the good will be available whether or not they pay [1], so many choose to free ride [1]. Revenue then falls below cost, so supply is not profitable [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The free rider step is the link most often missing; without it the answer jumps from "cannot charge" to "not supplied".

**6.** Model plan, one point per bullet; the real paper is marked against markbands, not point by point.
- Both responses overcome free riding by funding the good through compulsory taxation [1].
- Direct provision: the government supplies the good itself [1]. Strength: provided although the market would not supply it, and free at the point of use [1]. Limitation: the government does not know true valuations, so quantity may be wrong; opportunity cost of tax revenue [1].
- Contracting out: a private firm supplies under a contract after a tender [1]. Strength: competition between bidders can lower cost [1]. Limitation: contract costs, and quality may be cut [1].
- Applies reasoning to a context, for example a coastal path whose quality is hard to specify [1].
- Judgement supported by a deciding factor, such as how easily quality can be specified and how many bidders exist [2]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* An evaluation weighs strengths against limitations and ends in a judgement; two separate lists with no comparison read as description.

**7.** (HL only) Adverse selection arises before the deal because one party hides quality or risk [1]. Example: sellers know a used item is poor and buyers cannot tell [1]. Moral hazard arises after the deal because behaviour changes when another party bears the risk [1]. Example: an insured owner stops maintaining equipment [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* "Distinguish" needs the difference stated, most easily as timing; two separate definitions do not make the contrast.

**8.** (HL only) **(a)** 0.5 × 700 + 0.5 × 300 = **500 veldors** [1].
**(b)** Buyers will pay at most the average, 500 [1], which is below the 560 good sellers need, so they withdraw [1].
**(c)** Expected value = 300 + 400g [1]. Set 300 + 400g ≥ 560, so g ≥ 260 ÷ 400 = **0.65** [1]. **[5]**
*Examiner insight:* Show the average-value calculation; the explanation in (b) then follows from comparing it with the sellers' value.

**9.** (HL only) **(a)** No care: 0.15 × 12,000 = 1,800 [1]. Care: 600 + 0.05 × 12,000 = 1,200, which is lower, so the owner takes care [1].
**(b)** Full insurance leaves the owner's expected loss at zero in both cases [1], so paying 600 for care is a pure cost and care is not taken [1].
**(c)** No care: 0.15 × 9,000 = 1,350 [1]. Care: 600 + 0.05 × 9,000 = 1,050, which is lower, so care returns [1]. **[6]**
*Examiner insight:* Compare the two expected costs side by side; stating only the owner's decision, without both figures, leaves the "show" incomplete.

**10.** (HL only) Signalling: the informed party acts to reveal its quality [1], for example a seller offering a warranty [1]. Screening: the uninformed party designs a test or choice that makes the other side reveal its type [1], for example an insurer offering a menu of policies with different excesses [1]. **[4]**
*Examiner insight:* The key distinction is who moves first; naming the actor in each definition secures the contrast.

**11.** (HL only) Model plan, one point per bullet; the real paper uses markbands.
- Adverse selection: buyers cannot judge quality, so the average price drives out good sellers [1].
- Legislation and regulation, such as compulsory disclosure of known faults [1]. Strength: it binds all sellers [1]. Limitation: costly to enforce, and rules lag behind practice [1].
- Provision of information, such as published inspection results [1]. Strength: cheap way to narrow the gap [1]. Limitation: may be misread or become out of date [1].
- Contrast with a private response such as a warranty, which costs the government nothing but may not cover every seller [1].
- Judgement on which response suits Kormas, with a reason [2]. **[10]**
*Examiner insight:* Tie each strength and limitation to the used-laptop context; generic points about "regulation" without the market named gain less.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Giving one feature of a public good when the question needs both.
- Calling a state-run service a public good without testing it.
- Leaving out the free rider step between non-excludability and under-provision.
- Listing strengths of direct provision and contracting out with no comparison.
- Writing a final answer with no working under "show" or "calculate".
- Mixing up adverse selection and moral hazard by timing.
- Describing a signal as something the buyer does.
- Giving a government response with no limitation.
