Cambridge IGCSE Statistics 0479 is assessed by two externally assessed written papers, Paper 1 and Paper 2, each lasting 2 hours 15 minutes, each worth 100 marks and each contributing 50% of the grade. The syllabus is not tiered, grades run A* to G, and this edition is for examination in 2027 with only the June series available. Because both papers are identical in length and weighting, preparation is about the subject as a whole. Useful starting points on the site are [measures of central tendency](/resources/cambridge-igcse-statistics-mean-median-and-mode-calculation-estimation-from/), [quartiles and the interquartile range](/resources/cambridge-igcse-statistics-quartiles-percentiles-range-and-interquartile-range/) and [probability distributions and expectation](/resources/cambridge-igcse-statistics-probability-distributions-and-expectation/).

## Paper 1 and Paper 2: the same shape

The syllabus describes both papers only as Paper 1 and Paper 2. In each, the questions are of mixed length and style and are drawn from any part of the subject content. That has an important consequence: there is no topic that can safely be assigned to one paper and ignored for the other. A question on time series or index numbers may appear in either paper, and so may probability or bivariate distributions. **Preparation priority**: revise by topic across all twelve content areas (data collection, representation, frequency distributions, central tendency, dispersion, transformations of data, probability, probability distributions, rates, index numbers, bivariate distributions and time series), then test yourself with mixed sets rather than topic by topic.

## Calculator rules and shown working

A calculator is required for both papers, but algebraic and graphical calculators are not permitted. Check well before the exam that your calculator is a permitted scientific model and that you know its statistical modes. The syllabus also states that essential working must be shown. **Preparation priority**: always write down the formula or substitution before the number, and write intermediate results to more figures than the final answer needs, so that rounding does not move the final value. A correct method with a slip can still earn marks, but only when the method is visible.

## Timing arithmetic

Each paper gives 135 minutes for 100 marks, which is 135 / 100 = 1.35 minutes per mark. A three-mark question therefore deserves about 4 minutes, and a ten-mark question about 13.5 minutes. If you keep 10 minutes at the end of each paper for checking, the working time is 125 minutes, or 1.25 minutes per mark. Over both papers you will sit 270 minutes of examination for 200 marks.

## Question styles

Questions of mixed length and style mean that short recall and calculation parts sit alongside longer, multi-step ones. Common command words in statistics are: calculate (work out a numerical answer, showing method), state (give a brief fact or value), describe (say what a pattern in data shows, using the context), and comment or interpret (explain what a result means in context, not only what it equals). **Preparation priority**: for every numerical answer you practise, add a sentence of interpretation in the context of the data, because "the median is 22" says less than "half the pupils scored 22 or fewer".

## Worked practice scenario: median, quartiles and interquartile range

Question (original): "The numbers of books read by 11 students over a term were 12, 15, 15, 18, 21, 22, 24, 27, 31, 35, 43. (a) Find the median. (b) Find the lower quartile, upper quartile and interquartile range. (c) Calculate the mean and say why it is larger than the median."

```
n = 11, data already in ascending order.

(a) Median position = (n + 1) / 2 = 6th value = 22

(b) Lower quartile position = (n + 1) / 4 = 3rd value = 15
    Upper quartile position = 3(n + 1) / 4 = 9th value = 31
    IQR = 31 - 15 = 16

(c) Sum = 263
    Mean = 263 / 11 = 23.9 (to 1 decimal place)
    The mean is larger than the median because the largest
    value, 43, is far above the rest and pulls the mean upwards.
```

Use the quartile position method taught on your course; with n = 11 the positions fall on whole numbers, which keeps the example clean. The interpretation line in (c) is the part many candidates leave out.

## Before/during exam checklist

- Confirm that your calculator is a scientific model, not algebraic or graphical.
- Replace batteries or check solar function before each paper.
- Revise across all twelve topic areas, since both papers draw on any content.
- Practise mixed question sets to switch topics quickly.
- Write formulas and substitutions, as essential working must be shown.
- Budget about 1.35 minutes per mark and move on if stuck.
- Add a short, contextual comment to results when asked to interpret.
- Keep a few minutes at the end to recheck arithmetic and units.

## Self-test

1. How long is each Statistics 0479 paper and how many marks does it carry?
2. Which kinds of calculator are not allowed?
3. A question is worth 6 marks. About how many minutes does it merit at 1.35 minutes per mark?
4. In the worked example, what is the interquartile range?
5. Why can the mean be greater than the median?

**Answers:** 1. 2 hours 15 minutes and 100 marks. 2. Algebraic and graphical calculators. 3. 6 x 1.35 = 8.1 minutes. 4. 31 - 15 = 16. 5. A few very large values pull the mean upwards while the median stays at the middle value.
