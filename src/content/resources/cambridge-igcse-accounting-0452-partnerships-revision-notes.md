## Scope

In: types of partnership business; advantages and disadvantages; the partnership
agreement; the appropriation account; interest on partners' loans, interest on
capital, interest on drawings, salaries and the division of the balance; 5.1
adjustments; capital and current accounts in ledger form and in the statement of
financial position.

Out: dissolution, changes in the profit-sharing ratio, limited liability
partnerships, and statements on admission or departure of a partner.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Partnership | Two or more people carrying on a business together with a view to profit |
| Partnership agreement | The partners' agreed rules on capital, sharing profit and rewards |
| Appropriation account | Shows how profit for the year is shared among the partners |
| Interest on capital | Reward for the capital each partner has invested; an appropriation |
| Interest on drawings | Charge on partners for withdrawing; added in the appropriation account |
| Partner's salary | A fixed share of profit for a partner's work; an appropriation |
| Interest on partner's loan | Interest on money lent by a partner; an expense in the statement of profit or loss |
| Capital account | The fixed long-term investment of each partner |
| Current account | Each partner's appropriations less drawings, carried from year to year |

## Advantages and disadvantages

| For | Against |
|---|---|
| More capital | Unlimited liability |
| Shared skills and workload | Profits shared |
| Losses shared | Slower decisions; possible disagreements |
| Continuity during absence | Each partner bound by the others' business decisions |

## Contents of an agreement

Capital to be contributed; profit- and loss-sharing ratio; interest on capital
(rate); interest on drawings; partners' salaries; interest rate on partners'
loans; any limit on drawings.

## Why each reward exists

- **Interest on capital** rewards partners who invest more, when the
  profit-sharing ratio does not reflect the capital each has put in.
- **A salary** rewards a partner who works more hours or manages the business.
- **Interest on drawings** discourages early or large withdrawals and is fairer to
  partners who leave their profits in the business.
- **Interest on a loan** compensates a partner for lending money beyond the agreed
  capital; it is paid whether or not a profit is made.

## Appropriation account layout

```
Profit for the year (after interest on partners' loans)       X
Add interest on drawings (each partner)                        X
                                                               X
Less partners' salaries (each partner)                        (X)
Less interest on capital (each partner)                       (X)
Balance of profit (or loss) shared in the agreed ratio         X
```

If salaries and interest on capital exceed the profit available, the balance is a
**loss**, shared in the same ratio and debited to the current accounts.

## Current account entries

| Debit side | Credit side |
|---|---|
| Opening debit balance | Opening credit balance |
| Drawings | Interest on partner's loan (if unpaid) |
| Interest on drawings | Salary |
| Share of residual loss | Interest on capital |
| Closing credit balance c/d | Share of residual profit |
| | Closing debit balance c/d |

## Statement of financial position

Capital accounts (each partner) + current accounts (each partner, debit balances
shown in brackets) = total. A loan from a partner is a **liability**, normally
non-current.

## Worked reminder

Profit 48,000. Interest on drawings 500 in total. Salary 10,000. Interest on capital
4,500 in total. Equal shares.

```
48,000 + 500 − 10,000 − 4,500 = 34,000, shared 17,000 each
```

**Current account reminder.** Opening balance 600 debit; salary 9,000; interest on
capital 2,000; share of profit 15,532; drawings 21,500; interest on drawings 210.

```
Credits 9,000 + 2,000 + 15,532 = 26,532
Debits  600 + 21,500 + 210     = 22,310
Closing balance                 =  4,222 credit
```

## Exam traps

- **Loan interest is an expense**, deducted before profit for the year.
- **Interest on drawings is added**, not deducted.
- **Interest on capital** uses the capital account balance, not the current account.
- **Salary** for a partner is never in wages.
- **Debit opening current account** goes on the debit side.
- **Residual loss** is shared in the profit- and loss-sharing ratio.
- **Time-apportion** loan interest when the loan was made part-way through the year.
- **Ratio fractions:** 3:2 means 3/5 and 2/5.

## Self-test

1. Where is interest on a partner's loan recorded?
2. Name three items in a partnership agreement.
3. Capital 40,000; interest on capital 6%. Calculate the interest.
4. Is interest on drawings added or deducted in the appropriation account?
5. Profit 30,000; salaries 8,000; interest on capital 5,000; interest on drawings 400.
   Calculate the balance to share.
6. Share 22,500 in the ratio 3:2.
7. State one difference between capital and current accounts.
8. A partner's current account has a debit balance. What does it mean?
9. A partner lends 6,000 on 1 October at 5%. Year end 31 December. Calculate the interest.
10. Give one advantage of forming a partnership.
11. Is a partner's salary shown in the statement of profit or loss?
12. Name one topic excluded from 5.2.

### Answers

1. In the statement of profit or loss, as an expense, and credited to the
   partner's current account if unpaid.
2. Any three from: capital contributed, profit-sharing ratio, interest on capital,
   interest on drawings, salaries, interest on loans, limit on drawings.
3. **2,400**.
4. Added.
5. 30,000 + 400 − 8,000 − 5,000 = **17,400**.
6. **13,500** and **9,000**.
7. The capital account is usually fixed; the current account changes each year.
8. The partner has withdrawn more than the amounts credited to them, so they owe
   the business.
9. 6,000 × 5% × 3/12 = **75**.
10. For example, more capital is available.
11. No: it is an appropriation of profit.
12. Any one: dissolution; change in profit-sharing ratio; limited liability
    partnership; admission or departure of a partner.
