These questions cover content reference **B9** of the AQA A-level Mathematics (7357) specification, version 1.3 (31 January 2018): transformations of the graph of y = f(x), including y = af(x), y = f(x) + a, y = f(x + a), y = f(ax) and combinations. Sketches should be described clearly in your own working: label the coordinates of key points and the equations of asymptotes.

## Questions

**1.** The point P(6, −3) lies on the curve y = f(x). Write down the coordinates of the image of P on each curve.

**(a)** y = f(x) + 4 **[1]**
**(b)** y = f(x + 1) **[1]**
**(c)** y = f(3x) **[1]**
**(d)** y = −f(x) **[1]**

**2.** A curve has equation y = x² + 8x + 9.

**(a)** Write the equation in the form y = (x + p)² + q. **[2]**
**(b)** Describe the transformation that maps y = x² to this curve. **[1]**

**3.** The curve y = 3ˣ is translated by the vector (−2, 0) and then stretched with scale factor 4 parallel to the y-axis.

**(a)** Write down the equation of the image. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the point where the image meets the y-axis. **[1]**
**(c)** State the equation of the asymptote of the image. **[1]**

**4.** The curve y = f(x), where f(x) = (x + 1)(x − 2)², crosses the x-axis at (−1, 0) and touches it at (2, 0). It has a maximum at (0, 4) and a minimum at (2, 0).

**(a)** Sketch y = f(x + 2). Give the coordinates of the turning points and of the points where the curve meets the axes. **[3]**
**(b)** Write down the coordinates of the turning points of y = −½f(x). **[2]**

**5.** The curve y = f(x) has asymptotes x = 1 and y = 2, and passes through (3, 0).

**(a)** For y = f(x + 3), state the equations of the asymptotes and the image of (3, 0). **[3]**
**(b)** For y = 2f(x) − 5, state the equations of the asymptotes. **[2]**

**6.** Let f(x) = x² − 2x. Find the equation of the image of y = f(x) in each case, in the form y = ax² + bx + c.

**(a)** A translation by (3, 0). **[2]**
**(b)** A stretch with scale factor 2 parallel to the y-axis, followed by a translation by (0, −1). **[2]**
**(c)** A stretch with scale factor ½ parallel to the x-axis. **[1]**

**7.** **(a)** Describe a sequence of two transformations that maps y = f(x) to y = f(2x − 6). **[3]**
**(b)** The point (4, −1) lies on y = f(x). Find its image on y = f(2x − 6). **[1]**

**8.** The curve C has equation y = 4 − 3cos(2x), for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°.

**(a)** Describe the sequence of transformations that maps y = cos x to C. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the greatest and least values of y, and the values of x at which each occurs. **[3]**

**9.** The curve y = 1/x is stretched with scale factor 5 parallel to the y-axis, then translated by (−2, 0), then translated by (0, 1). The result is the curve C.

**(a)** Write down the equation of C. **[2]**
**(b)** State the equations of the asymptotes of C. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the coordinates of the points where C meets the axes. **[3]**

**10.** The curve y = f(x) has x-intercepts (−3, 0), (1, 0) and (5, 0), a y-intercept (0, 3), a maximum at (−1, 4) and a minimum at (3, −2). The curve G has equation y = 2 − f(x/2).

**(a)** Describe the sequence of transformations that maps y = f(x) to G. **[3]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the turning points of G, and say which is a maximum and which is a minimum. **[2]**
**(c)** Find the y-intercept of G. **[1]**
**(d)** Explain why the information given does not let you write down the x-intercepts of G. **[2]**

**11.** The curve y = x² − 4x + 1 is translated by the vector (p, q). The image has equation y = x² + 2x + 8.

**(a)** Find p and q. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the coordinates of the minimum point of the image. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** (6, −3 + 4) = **(6, 1)** [1]
**(b)** The translation is 1 to the left, so **(5, −3)** [1]
**(c)** x is divided by 3, so **(2, −3)** [1]
**(d)** y changes sign, so **(6, 3)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Write each image next to the letter it answers. A common slip is moving f(x + 1) to the right, which gives (7, −3), a wrong x-coordinate.

**2. (a)** x² + 8x = (x + 4)² − 16 [1], so y = (x + 4)² − 7 [1]
**(b)** Translation by the vector **(−4, −7)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* For a "describe" part, a bare vector with the word translation is complete. Giving (4, 7) from the signs in the brackets is the usual way to lose this mark.

**3. (a)** After the translation, y = 3^(x+2) [1]. After the stretch, **y = 4 × 3^(x+2)** [1]
**(b)** At x = 0, y = 4 × 9 = **36**, so the point is (0, 36) [1]
**(c)** The stretch does not move y = 0, so the asymptote is **y = 0** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The order is stated in the question, so the 4 multiplies the whole of 3^(x+2). Writing 3^(x+2) + 4 would be a translation, not a stretch.

**4. (a)** Replace x by x + 2: each point moves 2 to the left [1]. Turning points: maximum **(−2, 4)** and minimum **(0, 0)** [1]. The curve crosses the x-axis at **(−3, 0)** and touches it at the origin, and the y-intercept is (0, f(2)) = **(0, 0)**, which is the minimum [1].
**(b)** The map is (x, y) → (x, −½y) [1]. The maximum becomes a minimum at **(0, −2)**, and the minimum becomes a maximum at **(2, 0)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In part (a), a clear sketch has the y-intercept and the minimum at the same point. Recalculating the y-intercept from the new equation avoids placing it at (0, 4), the old maximum.

**5. (a)** x = 1 becomes **x = −2** [1]; y = 2 is unchanged [1]; (3, 0) goes to **(0, 0)** [1]
**(b)** The vertical asymptote does not change: **x = 1** [1]. For the horizontal one, 2(2) − 5 = −1, so **y = −1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Apply the multiplication first and then the subtraction to y = 2. Subtracting before doubling gives −2, which is a different asymptote.

**6. (a)** Replace x by x − 3: (x − 3)² − 2(x − 3) [1] = **x² − 8x + 15** [1]
**(b)** 2(x² − 2x) − 1 [1] = **2x² − 4x − 1** [1]
**(c)** f(2x) = (2x)² − 2(2x) = **4x² − 4x** [1]
*Examiner insight:* In (c) the scale factor is ½, so x is replaced by 2x. Using x/2 gives x²/4 − x, the equation for scale factor 2.

**7. (a)** Write 2x − 6 = 2(x − 3). Stretch with scale factor ½ parallel to the x-axis [1], then translate by the vector (3, 0) [1]. (Alternatively, translate by (6, 0) and then stretch with scale factor ½ parallel to the x-axis.) The numbers must match the order given [1]
**(b)** 2x − 6 = 4 gives x = 5, so the image is **(5, −1)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* If you state the order, the translation must match it: 3 after the stretch, 6 before it. Using the part (b) point to test both versions is a quick way to catch a mismatch.

**8. (a)** Stretch with scale factor ½ parallel to the x-axis [1], stretch with scale factor 3 parallel to the y-axis and reflect in the x-axis [1], translate by the vector (0, 4) [1]
**(b)** The greatest value is 4 + 3 = **7** [1], when cos 2x = −1, so 2x = 180° or 540°, giving **x = 90° and 270°** [1]. The least value is 4 − 3 = **1**, when cos 2x = 1, so 2x = 0°, 360°, 720°, giving **x = 0°, 180°, 360°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The reflection is what turns the cosine peaks into minima. Without it, the greatest value would appear at x = 0°, and the sketch would be upside down.

**9. (a)** After the three stages, **y = 5/(x + 2) + 1** [1], which equals (x + 7)/(x + 2) [1]
**(b)** The asymptotes are **x = −2** [1] and **y = 1** [1]
**(c)** At x = 0, y = 5/2 + 1 = **7/2**, so the point is (0, 7/2) [1]. At y = 0, 5/(x + 2) = −1, so x + 2 = −5 [1] and the point is **(−7, 0)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A graph with both asymptotes and both axis crossings labelled is much easier to credit than one with only the shape drawn. Keep the exact fraction 7/2 unless the question asks for a decimal.

**10. (a)** Stretch with scale factor 2 parallel to the x-axis [1], reflect in the x-axis [1], translate by the vector (0, 2) [1]
**(b)** The map is (x, y) → (2x, 2 − y) [1]. The maximum (−1, 4) becomes the minimum **(−2, −2)**, and the minimum (3, −2) becomes the maximum **(6, 4)** [1]
**(c)** The y-intercept (0, 3) goes to **(0, −1)** [1]
**(d)** The x-intercepts of G are where f(x/2) = 2 [1]. The given points have f equal to 0, 4 or −2, which is never 2, so none of them is an x-intercept of G, and the x-values where f takes the value 2 are not given [1]
*Examiner insight:* Students often map the old x-intercepts and call them the new x-intercepts. Those points are now at y = 2, and a note saying so shows you understand the transformation.

**11. (a)** The image is y = (x − p)² − 4(x − p) + 1 + q [1] = x² − (2p + 4)x + p² + 4p + 1 + q [1]. Comparing the x-coefficients, −(2p + 4) = 2, so **p = −3** [1]. Then 9 − 12 + 1 + q = 8, so **q = 10** [1]
**(b)** The original minimum is at (2, −3) (from (x − 2)² − 3), so the image minimum is **(−1, 7)** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A shorter route is to use the vertex form of both curves. Checking that (−1, 7) satisfies y = x² + 2x + 8 gives a quick test of both p and q.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Moving f(x + a) to the right, or reading the vector from the sign inside the bracket.
- Using scale factor a for f(ax), when the scale factor is 1/a.
- Describing a stretch without saying which axis it is parallel to.
- Applying two transformations in an order that does not match the numbers given.
- Leaving the y-intercept unchanged after a horizontal translation.
- Forgetting to move or label the asymptotes, or applying the stretch to the vertical asymptote of a reciprocal curve.
- Not swapping a maximum and a minimum after a reflection in the x-axis.
- Mapping x-intercepts as if they were still x-intercepts after a vertical translation.
