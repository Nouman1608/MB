## 1. Why records are incomplete

Full double entry means every transaction is recorded twice, in ledger accounts, and checked by a trial balance. Many small sole traders do not do this. They keep what is cheap and useful: a cash book or bank statements, invoices filed in date order, and a list of who owes them money.

**Why they choose this:**
- The owner lacks bookkeeping knowledge.
- A bookkeeper or software costs money the business cannot spare.
- The business is small, so the owner can see most transactions personally.
- The owner mainly wants to know the bank balance and who owes what.

| Advantages of not keeping full records | Disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Lower cost: no bookkeeper or software | No trial balance, so errors stay hidden |
| Quicker and simpler day to day | Theft and fraud are harder to spot |
| Little accounting skill needed | Profit must be rebuilt, with less accuracy |
| Owner can still see cash and bank | Poor control of trade receivables and trade payables |
| | Lenders and tax authorities may distrust the figures |

When the year ends, the owner or an accountant **reconstructs** the figures from what exists. Sections 2 to 5 show how.

## 2. Statements of affairs and profit from changes in capital

A **statement of affairs** lists assets and liabilities on one date. It has the same layout as a statement of financial position, and its balancing figure is **capital** (assets minus liabilities). The *opening* statement of affairs is at the start of the year and the *closing* statement at the end.

**Haverford Cycles.** Cordelia Haverford buys and sells bicycles. She keeps bank statements, a cash float and invoices only. Her balances:

| | 1 Jan 2028 | 31 Dec 2028 |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment at cost | 12,000 | 12,000 |
| Accumulated depreciation | 3,600 | to find |
| Inventory | 6,400 | 7,100 |
| Trade receivables | 3,200 | 3,900 |
| Allowance for irrecoverable debts | 160 | to find |
| Rent paid in advance | 600 | 650 |
| Bank | 2,350 | 3,720 |
| Cash float | 180 | 180 |
| Trade payables | 3,900 | 4,300 |
| Electricity owing | 220 | 260 |
| Interest-free loan from a relative | 6,000 | 6,000 |

Equipment is depreciated at 10% a year on cost (straight line), with no purchases or disposals. The closing allowance is 5% of closing trade receivables, which are after writing off an irrecoverable debt of 340. Bank summary for 2028: receipts from credit customers 61,400; cash sales banked 9,600; capital introduced 2,500. Payments: suppliers 43,800; rent 7,200; electricity 1,980; wages 6,900; general expenses 2,450 (including 360 for Cordelia's private insurance); drawings unknown. She also took goods that cost 450 for her own use.

Accumulated depreciation at the end is 3,600 + (10% x 12,000) = 4,800. The allowance is 5% x 3,900 = 195.

```
Haverford Cycles: statements of affairs (amounts in dollars)
                                    1 Jan 2028   31 Dec 2028
Non-current assets (carrying amount)
  Equipment (12,000 - 3,600 / 4,800)     8,400         7,200
Current assets
  Inventory                              6,400         7,100
  Trade receivables less allowance       3,040         3,705
  Rent paid in advance                     600           650
  Bank                                   2,350         3,720
  Cash                                     180           180
                                        12,570        15,355
Current liabilities
  Trade payables                         3,900         4,300
  Electricity owing                        220           260
                                         4,120         4,560
Non-current liability: loan              6,000         6,000
Capital                                 10,850        11,995
```

**Profit from changes in capital.** Capital rises when profit is made or capital is introduced, and falls with drawings. So:

```
Profit = closing capital - opening capital + drawings - capital introduced
```

The bank summary gives the missing drawings: 2,350 + 73,500 receipts - 62,330 payments other than drawings - 3,720 closing bank = **9,800** taken from the bank. Total drawings are 9,800 + 450 goods + 360 private insurance = 10,610.

Profit = 11,995 - 10,850 + 10,610 - 2,500 = **9,255**. A negative answer is a loss. This is a quick answer, but it gives no gross profit or expense detail, so Section 4 builds the statements.

## 3. Rebuilding sales, purchases and expenses

Treat each balance like a ledger account: opening balance plus additions, minus reductions, equals closing balance. Find the missing side.

```
Credit sales = 61,400 + 3,900 + 340 - 3,200 = 62,440
Revenue      = 62,440 + 9,600 cash sales      = 72,040
Purchases    = 43,800 + 4,300 - 3,900         = 44,200
Cost of goods taken for own use               =    450
```

Cash received plus the debt written off plus closing trade receivables must equal opening trade receivables plus credit sales. The same logic works for any missing figure: credit sales, receipts from customers, purchases or payments to suppliers. Expenses use the 5.1 adjustments:

```
Rent        = 600 + 7,200 - 650     = 7,150
Electricity = 1,980 - 220 + 260     = 2,020
General     = 2,450 - 360 private   = 2,090
Allowance   = 195 - 160 (increase)  =    35
```

## 5. Mark-up, margin and inventory turnover

The syllabus formulas are the only ones accepted in answers:

```
Mark-up (%)                = Gross profit / Cost of sales x 100
Gross profit margin (%)    = Gross profit / Revenue x 100
Rate of inventory turnover = Cost of sales / Average inventory
```

For Haverford: margin 28,990 / 72,040 = 40.2%; mark-up 28,990 / 43,050 = 67.3%; average inventory (6,400 + 7,100) / 2 = 6,750, so turnover 6.4 times.

Now use them when something is missing. **Yardley Fabrics** (Felix Yardley) has inventory of 5,200 at the start and 6,800 at the end. Rate of inventory turnover is 7 times and mark-up is 40%.

```
Average inventory = (5,200 + 6,800) / 2     = 6,000
Cost of sales     = 7 x 6,000               = 42,000
Gross profit      = 40% x 42,000            = 16,800
Revenue           = 42,000 + 16,800         = 58,800
Purchases         = 42,000 + 6,800 - 5,200  = 43,600
```

If credit sales were 49,000, cash sales are 58,800 - 49,000 = 9,800. The margin is 16,800 / 58,800 = 28.6%.

Another case: revenue 80,000, margin 25%. Cost of sales is 75% x 80,000 = 60,000. With opening inventory 8,000 and purchases 61,500, closing inventory = 8,000 + 61,500 - 60,000 = **9,500**.

**Converting.** A margin of 20% means gross profit is 20 on revenue 100, so cost of sales is 80 and the mark-up is 20/80 = 25%. A mark-up of 25% converts back to a margin of 25/125 = 20%.

## Common errors

- Forgetting that a debt written off reduces trade receivables without any cash coming in.
- Adding goods taken for own use to purchases instead of deducting them, then forgetting drawings.
- Putting private expenses in the statement of profit or loss as well as in drawings.
- Dividing gross profit by revenue when the question says mark-up.
- Using closing inventory alone as the figure in the inventory turnover formula.
- Charging the whole allowance as an expense instead of the change.
