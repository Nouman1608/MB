These notes condense sub-topic **8.3 The heart** of the Cambridge International AS & A Level Biology 9700 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). It is AS content for Paper 1 and Paper 2, assumed on Paper 4. For explanations and worked examples, use the [heart study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-heart/); for exam-style questions, use the [heart practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-biology-9700-the-heart-practice/).

## Structure checklist

**External**
- Two atria (top, thin walls), two ventricles (bottom, thick walls).
- Coronary arteries on the surface supply the heart muscle itself.
- Aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary veins, venae cavae at the top.

**Internal**

| Structure | Where | Job |
|---|---|---|
| Septum | Middle | Separates oxygenated and deoxygenated blood |
| Tricuspid valve | Right atrium/ventricle | Prevents backflow into right atrium |
| Bicuspid (mitral) valve | Left atrium/ventricle | Prevents backflow into left atrium |
| Tendinous cords + papillary muscles | Valve flaps to ventricle wall | Stop AV valves inverting |
| Semilunar valves | Base of aorta and pulmonary artery | Prevent backflow into ventricles |

Diagram tip: the heart's left side is on the right of the page.

## Wall thickness

| Comparison | Thicker | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| Atria vs ventricles | Ventricles | Ventricles pump blood out of the heart round a circulation; atria only into the ventricles |
| Left vs right ventricle | Left | Systemic circulation is long with high resistance, so needs higher pressure; lungs are close and their capillaries would be damaged by high pressure |

Both ventricles pump the **same volume**; the difference is **pressure**.

## Valve rules

- AV valve open ⇔ atrial pressure > ventricular pressure.
- Semilunar valve open ⇔ ventricular pressure > arterial pressure.
- Each valve opens or closes where two pressure lines cross.

## Cardiac cycle sequence

| Stage | Pressure change | AV valve | Semilunar valve |
|---|---|---|---|
| Atrial systole | Atrial pressure rises slightly | Open | Closed |
| Start of ventricular systole | Ventricle > atrium | **Closes** | Closed |
| Ejection | Ventricle > artery | Closed | **Opens** |
| Start of diastole | Ventricle < artery | Closed | **Closes** |
| Filling | Ventricle < atrium | **Opens** | Closed |

- Both valves closed twice per cycle: start of systole and start of diastole.
- Aortic pressure stays fairly high in diastole because the elastic wall recoils.
- Right side: same pattern, lower pressures.

### Worked reminder: heart rate and timing

One cycle lasts 0.75 s; the semilunar valve is open from 0.15 s to 0.42 s.

```
heart rate = 60 ÷ 0.75 = 80 beats per minute
ejection = 0.42 − 0.15 = 0.27 s = 36 % of the cycle
```

## Control of the heartbeat

1. Cardiac muscle is **myogenic**.
2. **SAN** (right atrium wall) = pacemaker; sends waves of excitation.
3. Waves spread over both atria → atrial systole.
4. **Non-conducting tissue** blocks direct spread to ventricles.
5. **AVN** passes the wave on after a **delay** → atria finish emptying first.
6. **Purkyne tissue** carries the wave down the septum to the apex, then up the ventricle walls.
7. Ventricles contract **from the apex upwards** → blood pushed into the arteries.

Nervous and hormonal control: **not expected**.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Systole | Contraction of heart muscle |
| Diastole | Relaxation of heart muscle |
| Cardiac cycle | Sequence of events in one heartbeat |
| Atrioventricular valve | Valve between atrium and ventricle (tricuspid or bicuspid) |
| Semilunar valve | Valve at the base of the aorta or pulmonary artery |
| Myogenic | Contraction starts within the muscle itself |
| Sinoatrial node | Pacemaker in the right atrium wall |
| Atrioventricular node | Tissue in the septum that delays and passes on the wave |
| Purkyne tissue | Conducting fibres carrying the wave through the ventricles |
| Coronary arteries | Vessels supplying the heart muscle with blood |

## Exam traps

- Valves described without comparing pressures.
- "Left ventricle pumps more blood": it produces a higher pressure.
- "SAN sends nerve impulses": waves of electrical excitation.
- Missing the non-conducting tissue.
- Ventricles contracting "from the top".
- Heart rate calculated as 60 × cycle time instead of 60 ÷ cycle time.
- Left and right swapped on a diagram.

## Self-test

1. Name the valve between the left atrium and left ventricle.
2. What is the function of the tendinous cords?
3. Why does the heart need coronary arteries?
4. Explain why the right ventricle wall is thinner than the left.
5. State the condition for a semilunar valve to open.
6. Why does the AV valve close at the start of ventricular systole?
7. When in the cycle are both valves closed?
8. Why does aortic pressure not fall to zero in diastole?
9. Where is the SAN?
10. Explain why the AVN delays the wave of excitation.
11. Explain why ventricles contract from the apex upwards.
12. A cardiac cycle lasts 0.60 s. Calculate the heart rate.

### Answers

1. Bicuspid (mitral) valve.
2. Stop the atrioventricular valves turning inside out when the ventricles contract.
3. Heart muscle respires continuously and needs its own supply of oxygen and glucose; blood in the chambers does not supply the wall.
4. It pumps blood only to the nearby lungs at lower pressure; high pressure would damage lung capillaries.
5. Ventricular pressure must be higher than the pressure in the artery.
6. Ventricular pressure rises above atrial pressure.
7. At the start of ventricular systole and at the start of diastole.
8. The elastic wall of the aorta recoils, keeping pressure up.
9. In the wall of the right atrium.
10. So the atria finish contracting and emptying into the ventricles before the ventricles contract.
11. Purkyne tissue carries the wave to the apex first, so blood is squeezed upwards towards the arteries.
12. 60 ÷ 0.60 = **100 beats per minute**.
