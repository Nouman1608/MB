These questions cover subtopic E6.3, Exact trigonometric values, in the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is **Extended only**, so every question is Extended. All questions are **non-calculator**: give answers as exact values, with surds in simplest form. Question 11 also uses angles of elevation (E6.2) and Question 13 uses the area formula ½ab sin C from the List of formulas (E6.5).

For explanations, read the [exact trigonometric values study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-exact-trigonometric-values-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for a summary, use the [exact trigonometric values revision notes](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-exact-trigonometric-values-cambridge-igcse-maths-revision-notes/).

## Section A

**1.** Write down the exact value of

**(a)** cos 45°, **[1]**

**(b)** tan 30°, **[1]**

**(c)** sin 60°. **[1]**

**2.** Find the exact value of 4 sin 30° + √3 tan 60°. **[2]**

**3.** Find the exact value of cos² 30° − sin² 30°. **[2]**

**4.** Solve 2 sin x = √2 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 90°. **[2]**

**5.** Show that tan 30° × tan 60° = 1. **[2]**

**6.** Find the exact value of (sin 45° + cos 45°)². **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** In triangle ABC, angle ABC = 90°, AC = 18 cm and angle BAC = 60°.

**(a)** Find the exact length of AB. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the exact length of BC. **[2]**

**8.** In triangle PQR, angle PQR = 90°, PQ = 10 cm and angle PRQ = 30°.

**(a)** Find PR. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the exact length of QR. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the exact area of triangle PQR. **[2]**

**9.** In a right-angled triangle, the side adjacent to a 30° angle is 12 cm. Find the exact length of the hypotenuse, giving your answer in its simplest form. **[3]**

**10.** In triangle ABC, angle ABC = 90° and AB = 10 cm. D is a point on BC such that angle ADB = 45°. Angle ACB = 30°. Find the exact length of DC, in a factorised form. **[4]**

**11.** A vertical tower stands on level ground. From a point P on the ground, 20 m from the foot of the tower, the angle of elevation of the top of the tower is 45°.

**(a)** Find the height of the tower. **[1]**

**(b)** A flagpole stands on top of the tower. From P, the angle of elevation of the top of the flagpole is 60°. Find the exact length of the flagpole. **[3]**

**12.** Solve 4 cos² x = 1 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 90°. **[3]**

**13.** A triangle has sides of 8 cm and 12 cm.

**(a)** The angle between them is 30°. Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**(b)** Find the exact area when the angle between them is 60° instead. **[1]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **√2/2** (or 1/√2) [1]

**(b)** **√3/3** (or 1/√3) [1]

**(c)** **√3/2** [1]

**2.** 4 × 1/2 = 2 and √3 × √3 = 3 [1]; total = **5** [1]

**3.** (√3/2)² − (1/2)² = 3/4 − 1/4 [1] = **1/2** [1]

**4.** sin x = √2/2 [1]; **x = 45°** [1]

**5.** tan 30° = 1/√3 and tan 60° = √3 [1]; 1/√3 × √3 = √3/√3 = 1 [1]

**6.** sin 45° + cos 45° = √2/2 + √2/2 = √2 [1]; (√2)² = **2** [1]

*Examiner insight:* squaring each term separately gives 1/2 + 1/2 = 1, which is wrong; add inside the bracket first.

**7. (a)** AB = 18 cos 60° = **9 cm** [1]

**(b)** BC = 18 sin 60° = 18 × √3/2 [1] = **9√3 cm** [1]

**8. (a)** PQ is opposite the 30° angle, so PR = 2 × 10 = **20 cm** [1]

**(b)** QR = **10√3 cm** (ratio 1 : √3 : 2) [1]

**(c)** ½ × 10 × 10√3 [1] = **50√3 cm²** [1]

**9.** cos 30° = 12 / h, so h = 12 ÷ (√3/2) = 24/√3 [1]; rationalise: 24√3/3 [1] = **8√3 cm** [1]

**10.** In triangle ABD, the angles at D and A are both 45°, so BD = AB = 10 cm [1]. In triangle ABC, tan 30° = 10 / BC, so BC = 10 ÷ (1/√3) = 10√3 cm [1]. DC = BC − BD = 10√3 − 10 [1] = **10(√3 − 1) cm** [1]

**11. (a)** height = 20 tan 45° = **20 m** [1]

**(b)** Height of the top of the flagpole = 20 tan 60° = 20√3 m [1]; flagpole = 20√3 − 20 [1] = **20(√3 − 1) m** [1]

**12.** cos² x = 1/4 [1]; cos x = 1/2 (the negative root gives no angle in this range) [1]; **x = 60°** [1]

**13. (a)** ½ × 8 × 12 × sin 30° = 48 × 1/2 [1] = **24 cm²** [1]

**(b)** 48 × √3/2 = **24√3 cm²** [1]

## Where marks are usually lost

- Swapping the values of sin 30° and cos 30°, or √2/2 and √3/2.
- Placing √3 opposite the 30° angle in the half-equilateral triangle.
- Combining unlike surds: 10√3 − 10 is not 10.
- Dividing by an exact value without inverting, for example 12 ÷ (√3/2) written as 6√3.
- Leaving 24/√3 unsimplified when simplest form is asked.
- Squaring terms separately in an expression such as (sin 45° + cos 45°)².

## Frequently asked questions

**How do I find an exact side when the unknown is the hypotenuse?**
Write the ratio with the hypotenuse on the bottom, rearrange to divide by the exact value, then multiply by its reciprocal and rationalise the denominator if needed.

**Can I use the 1 : √3 : 2 ratio instead of writing a trigonometric equation?**
Yes. Scaling the special triangle is a valid method, as long as you match each side to the correct angle and show the scale factor.
