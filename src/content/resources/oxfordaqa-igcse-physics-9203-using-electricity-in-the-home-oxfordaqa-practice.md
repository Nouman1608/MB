## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** State what is meant by direct current. **[1]**

**2.** Which device in a home circuit can be reset after it has disconnected the supply?

A: a fuse

B: a circuit breaker

C: an earth wire

D: a neutral wire

**[1]**

**3.** State which wire in a household circuit contains the fuse. **[1]**

**4.** An ac supply has a frequency of 60 Hz. Calculate its period in milliseconds. **[2]**

**5.** Explain why an electric drill with a plastic casing and no metal parts that can be touched does not need an earth wire. **[2]**

**6.** State two differences between how a fuse and a circuit breaker work. **[2]**

### Section B: structured questions

**7.** An oscilloscope shows the trace of an ac supply. One complete cycle spans 5 divisions horizontally. The peaks are 2.5 divisions above the centre line. The time-base is 4 ms/div and the Y-gain is 2 V/div.

**(a)** Calculate the period of the supply. **[2]**

**(b)** Calculate the frequency. **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the peak potential difference. **[2]**

**8.** On the same oscilloscope settings as question 7, a battery produces a horizontal line 3 divisions above the centre line.

**(a)** Calculate the pd of the battery. **[1]**

**(b)** Compare the battery's pd with the peak pd of the ac supply in question 7. **[1]**

**(c)** The time-base is changed to 2 ms/div. Describe how the ac trace and the battery trace change. **[2]**

**9.** A student reads an ac trace and writes: "From the lowest point to the highest point is 6 divisions at 5 V/div, so the peak pd is 30 V."

**(a)** Explain the student's error and give the correct peak pd. **[2]**

**(b)** The same trace shows two complete cycles across 10 divisions at 10 ms/div. Calculate the frequency. **[3]**

**10.** A toaster has a metal case connected to the earth wire. A fault inside makes the live wire touch the case.

**(a)** Describe what happens, step by step, until the toaster is safe. **[4]**

**(b)** Explain what could happen to a person who touched the case if the earth wire had become disconnected. **[2]**

**11.** A homeowner replaces the fuses in her house with circuit breakers.

**(a)** Explain why too great a current makes a fuse disconnect a circuit. **[2]**

**(b)** Give two advantages of the circuit breakers. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the circuit breakers must be fitted in the live wire rather than the neutral wire. **[2]**

**12.** A traveller notices that the mains supply in one country is 230 V at 50 Hz and in another is 120 V at 60 Hz.

**(a)** State what the 50 Hz tells you about the current. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the period of the 50 Hz supply and of the 60 Hz supply. **[2]**

**(c)** Explain why the mains supply is described as alternating. **[1]**

## Answers

**1.** Current that always passes in the same direction [1]. **[1]**

**2.** **B** [1]. **[1]**

**3.** The live wire [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Period = 1 ÷ 60 = 0.0167 s [1] = **16.7 ms** [1]. **[2]**

**5.** The casing is an insulator, so it cannot become live even if a wire comes loose inside [1]. The drill is double insulated, so there is no metal case to earth [1]. **[2]**

**6.** Any two: a fuse melts but a circuit breaker switches off (trips); a circuit breaker works much faster; a circuit breaker can be reset but a fuse must be replaced [1] [1]. **[2]**

**7. (a)** Period = 5 × 4 ms [1] = 20 ms = **0.020 s** [1].

**(b)** f = 1 ÷ 0.020 [1] = **50 Hz** [1].

**(c)** Peak pd = 2.5 × 2 [1] = **5.0 V** [1]. **[6]**

**8. (a)** 3 × 2 = **6.0 V** [1].

**(b)** The battery's pd (6.0 V) is 1.0 V greater than the peak pd of the ac supply (5.0 V) [1].

**(c)** Each ac cycle now spans twice as many divisions (10 divisions), so fewer cycles fit on the screen [1]; the battery trace is still a horizontal line at the same height [1]. **[4]**

**9. (a)** The student measured peak-to-peak [1]. The peak is half of this: 3 × 5 = **15 V** [1].

**(b)** One cycle = 10 ÷ 2 = 5 divisions [1]. Period = 5 × 10 ms = 50 ms = 0.050 s [1]. f = 1 ÷ 0.050 = **20 Hz** [1]. **[5]**

**10. (a)** The case becomes live [1]. A large current flows from the live wire through the case and the earth wire to earth [1], because the earth path has a very low resistance [1]. The large current melts the fuse, disconnecting the live wire so the case is safe [1].

**(b)** The case would stay live [1], so current could flow through the person to earth, giving a possibly fatal electric shock [1]. **[6]**

**11. (a)** The large current heats the fuse wire [1] until it melts, breaking the circuit [1].

**(b)** They disconnect the circuit much faster [1] and can be reset instead of replaced [1].

**(c)** Disconnecting the live wire cuts the appliance off from the high pd [1]; breaking only the neutral would leave parts of the appliance connected to the live supply and still dangerous [1]. **[6]**

**12. (a)** The current changes direction and back again (completes 50 cycles) every second [1].

**(b)** 1 ÷ 50 = **0.020 s** [1]; 1 ÷ 60 = **0.017 s** (0.0167 s) [1].

**(c)** The current repeatedly changes direction [1]. **[4]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using peak-to-peak instead of peak pd.
- Forgetting to change milliseconds into seconds before finding frequency.
- Missing the "very low resistance" step that explains why the earth current is large.
- Saying the earth wire, rather than the fuse, melts.
- Giving only one advantage of a circuit breaker when two are asked for.
- Describing double insulation as "extra plastic around the wires" without saying the case cannot become live.
