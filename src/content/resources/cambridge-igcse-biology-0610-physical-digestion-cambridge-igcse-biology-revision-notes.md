## Core ideas in four lines

1. Physical digestion = breakdown of food into **smaller pieces** without **chemical change** to the food molecules.
2. It **increases the surface area** of food for **enzymes** in chemical digestion.
3. Done by **teeth** (chewing) and the **stomach** (churning); and, for fats, by **bile** (Extended).
4. Enzymes act only at the surface of food, so more surface = faster chemical digestion.

## Key terms

| Term | Recall |
|---|---|
| Physical digestion | breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the molecules |
| Chemical digestion | breakdown of large insoluble molecules into small soluble molecules (by enzymes) |
| Surface area | total area of the outside of the food pieces exposed to enzymes |
| Crown | part of the tooth above the gum |
| Root | part of the tooth embedded in the jaw bone and gum |
| Churning | mixing and squeezing of food by contractions of the muscular stomach wall |
| Emulsification (Extended only) | breaking large fat droplets into many small droplets |
| Bile | alkaline liquid made in the liver, stored in the gall bladder; contains no enzymes |

## Surface area check

Cutting a cube into smaller cubes keeps the volume the same but increases the surface area. A 2 cm cube (24 cm²) cut into 1 cm cubes gives 8 × 6 = 48 cm²; halving the side again gives 96 cm². Each halving of the side doubles the surface area.

## Teeth: types and functions

| Tooth | Where | Shape | Job |
|---|---|---|---|
| Incisor | front | chisel-shaped, sharp edge | biting and cutting |
| Canine | beside the incisors | single pointed cusp | gripping and tearing |
| Premolar | behind canines | broad, two cusps | chewing and grinding |
| Molar | back | largest, several cusps | chewing and grinding (crushing) |

Memory hook: **front cuts, corner grips, back grinds.**

## Tooth structure, outside in

```
enamel (crown only)  →  dentine  →  pulp (nerves + blood vessels)
cement covers the root; root sits in jaw bone, surrounded by gum
```

| Tissue | Function |
|---|---|
| Enamel | very hard outer layer of the crown; resists wear |
| Dentine | hard living layer under the enamel; most of the tooth |
| Pulp | soft centre containing nerves and blood vessels |
| Nerves | detect pain, pressure and temperature |
| Blood vessels | bring oxygen and nutrients to the tooth's living cells |
| Cement | covers the root and helps fix it in the jaw bone |

## Stomach

- Muscular wall contracts → **churns** food.
- Mixes food with gastric juice and breaks it into smaller pieces → more surface area for protease.
- Churning is physical; acid and protease are chemical.

## Bile (Extended only)

```
large fat droplets  --bile (emulsifies)-->  many small droplets
       small surface area                     large surface area for lipase
```

- Physical: fat molecules unchanged; bile has **no enzymes**.
- Result: lipase digests fats and oils to fatty acids and glycerol faster.

## How each physical step prepares for chemical digestion

| Physical step | Where | Enzyme that benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Chewing by teeth | mouth | salivary amylase on starch; later all digestive enzymes |
| Churning | stomach | protease in gastric juice |
| Emulsification by bile (Extended only) | duodenum | lipase from the pancreas |

The same logic runs through all three: smaller pieces or droplets → larger surface area → more molecules exposed to the enzyme at once → faster chemical digestion.

## Answer patterns

- **"Describe physical digestion"**: smaller pieces + no chemical change to molecules (two marks).
- **"Describe the functions of teeth"**: name the tooth + its action (biting, cutting, gripping, tearing, chewing, grinding).
- **"Describe the structure of a tooth"**: tissue + position for each mark.
- **"Explain the effect of chewing"**: surface area → enzymes → faster digestion.

## Exam traps

- Physical digestion does not break molecules or bonds.
- The surface area is for **enzymes**, not for absorption.
- Canines tear; molars grind. Do not swap.
- Enamel covers the crown only; cement covers the root.
- The pulp contains **both** nerves and blood vessels.
- The stomach's physical role is churning, not making acid.
- (Extended) Bile emulsifies; lipase digests. Bile is not an enzyme.

## Self-test

1. Describe physical digestion.
2. State how physical digestion helps chemical digestion.
3. Name the teeth used for biting and cutting.
4. Name the teeth used for gripping and tearing.
5. State the function of molars.
6. Name the outer layer of the crown of a tooth.
7. Which part of a tooth contains nerves and blood vessels?
8. Name the tissue that covers the root of a tooth.
9. Describe the role of the stomach in physical digestion.
10. A potato is cut from one 4 cm cube into eight 2 cm cubes. Calculate the surface area before and after.
11. (Extended) Outline the role of bile in fat digestion.
12. (Extended) Explain why emulsification is physical, not chemical, digestion.

### Answers

1. The breakdown of food into smaller pieces without chemical change to the food molecules.
2. It increases the surface area of food for enzymes to act on.
3. Incisors.
4. Canines.
5. Chewing and grinding food.
6. Enamel.
7. The pulp.
8. Cement.
9. Its muscular wall contracts to churn food, mixing it with gastric juice and breaking it into smaller pieces.
10. Before: 6 × 4 × 4 = **96 cm²**. After: 8 × 6 × 2 × 2 = **192 cm²**.
11. Bile emulsifies fats and oils into small droplets, increasing the surface area for lipase.
12. The fat molecules are not changed; they are only divided into smaller droplets.
