Condensed notes on subtopic 7.2 Transverse and longitudinal waves (Cambridge
9702, AS Topic 7). For full explanations and worked examples, use the
[transverse and longitudinal waves study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-physics-9702-as-transverse-and-longitudinal-waves/);
for the whole topic, see the [Waves revision notes](/resources/as-physics-waves-revision-notes/).

## What 7.2 asks you to do

- Compare transverse and longitudinal waves
- Analyse and interpret graphical representations of both

## The comparison

| | Transverse | Longitudinal |
|---|---|---|
| Particle oscillation | **Perpendicular** to energy transfer | **Parallel** to energy transfer |
| Features | Crests and troughs | Compressions and rarefactions |
| Examples | Light and all EM waves; rope or string waves | Sound; push-pull wave on a spring |
| Polarisation | Possible | Impossible |
| Both | Transfer energy, not matter; v = fλ; reflect, refract, diffract, interfere | |

- **Compression**: particles closer than normal; higher pressure.
- **Rarefaction**: particles further apart than normal; lower pressure.
- Water waves in a ripple tank: treated as transverse.
- A spring carries either type: sideways shake → transverse; push-pull along the axis → longitudinal.

## Which graph is which?

| Graph | Horizontal axis | Shows | Read off |
|---|---|---|---|
| Displacement–distance | Distance x | All particles at one instant | Wavelength λ, amplitude |
| Displacement–time | Time t | One particle over time | Period T (so f), amplitude |

Speed: v = λ/T = fλ, using values from the correct graphs.

## Transverse snapshot rules (wave moving right)

- Each particle copies, a moment later, the displacement of the particle just behind it (source side).
- Front slope of a crest: moving **up**. Back slope: moving **down**.
- At a crest or trough: momentarily **stationary**.
- At zero displacement: moving **fastest**.
- Wave moving left: reverse all directions.

**Example.** A snapshot of a rope wave moving right has a crest at x = 0.30 m and the next trough at x = 0.55 m.

```
lambda / 2 = 0.55 - 0.30 = 0.25 m      lambda = 0.50 m
x = 0.40 m: crest just behind it (front slope of the crest)  -> moving UP
x = 0.20 m: crest just ahead of it (back slope of the crest) -> moving DOWN
x = 0.30 m: at the crest                                     -> momentarily at rest
```

Rule of thumb: a particle with a crest just **behind** it (on the source side) is about to rise; a particle with a trough just behind it is about to fall.

## Longitudinal snapshot rules

Convention: positive displacement = in the direction of travel.

- **Downward zero crossing** (+ to −): neighbours displaced towards the point → **compression**.
- **Upward zero crossing** (− to +): neighbours displaced away → **rarefaction**.
- Displacement is **zero** at the centres of compressions and rarefactions.
- Maximum displacement occurs midway between a compression and a rarefaction.
- Compression to next compression = λ; compression to adjacent rarefaction = λ/2.
- Pressure–distance graph: maxima at compressions, a quarter-wavelength shift from the displacement–distance graph.

**Example.** A sound wave's displacement–distance graph is a sine curve crossing zero upwards at x = 0.10 m and downwards at x = 0.45 m.

```
x = 0.10 m  rarefaction        x = 0.45 m  compression
lambda / 2 = 0.45 - 0.10 = 0.35 m      so lambda = 0.70 m
```

**Example.** A particle's displacement–time graph in the same wave shows a period of 2.0 ms. f = 1 ÷ 2.0 × 10⁻³ = 500 Hz, so v = fλ = 500 × 0.70 = **350 m s⁻¹**.

## The CRO trap

A microphone trace on a CRO is a **voltage–time** graph at one place. Its sine shape says nothing about the wave being transverse.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition to recall |
|---|---|
| Transverse wave | Particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer |
| Longitudinal wave | Particles oscillate parallel to the direction of energy transfer |
| Compression | Region where particles are closer together than normal |
| Rarefaction | Region where particles are further apart than normal |
| Crest / trough | Point of maximum positive / negative displacement on a transverse wave |
| Displacement–distance graph | Snapshot of all particles' displacements at one instant |
| Displacement–time graph | One particle's displacement as time passes |

## Exam traps

- Compressions marked at the peaks of a longitudinal displacement graph.
- Period read from a distance axis, or wavelength from a time axis.
- "Transverse waves go up and down" with no reference to energy transfer.
- Particles in sound said to travel with the wave.
- Comparing the types with two unlinked lists instead of matched contrasts.
- Particle direction on a snapshot found without knowing which way the wave travels.

## Self-test

1. Define a transverse wave.
2. Define a longitudinal wave.
3. Give two examples of each type.
4. Which graph gives the wavelength directly?
5. A transverse wave travels to the left. A particle is on the left-hand slope of a crest. Which way is it moving?
6. On a longitudinal displacement–distance graph (positive in the direction of travel), where is the centre of a compression?
7. What is the displacement of the particle at the centre of a compression?
8. A particle in a sound wave has a period of 4.0 ms; the speed of sound is 320 m s⁻¹. Find the wavelength.
9. What is the distance between a compression and the adjacent rarefaction?
10. Why can sound not be polarised?
11. A CRO trace of a sound wave is sinusoidal. Why does this not show that sound is transverse?
12. How can one spring demonstrate both types of wave?

**Answers:**
1. Particles oscillate perpendicular to the direction of energy transfer.
2. Particles oscillate parallel to the direction of energy transfer.
3. Transverse: light (any EM wave), a wave on a rope. Longitudinal: sound, a push-pull wave on a spring.
4. The **displacement–distance** graph.
5. **Upwards** (it is on the front slope for a wave moving left).
6. At a **downward zero crossing**, where neighbouring particles are displaced towards it.
7. **Zero**.
8. f = 250 Hz; λ = 320 ÷ 250 = **1.28 m**.
9. **λ/2**.
10. Its oscillations are along the direction of travel, so there is no perpendicular plane of oscillation to restrict.
11. The trace is voltage against time at one point, not the shape of the wave.
12. Moving the end sideways gives a transverse wave; pushing and pulling it along the axis gives a longitudinal wave.
