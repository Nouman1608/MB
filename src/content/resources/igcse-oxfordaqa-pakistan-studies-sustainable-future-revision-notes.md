**These notes condense optional topic 3.2.4 of OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies 9236,
Creating a Sustainable Future for Pakistan, examined in Paper 2 Section C.** For each issue, learn
the causes, the impacts and at least one named strategy, then be ready to judge how effective that
strategy has been.

## Syllabus coverage

**9236 topic 3.2.4 (Version 1.9)**
- **3.2.4.1** Pollution (air, water, soil, noise); deforestation; desertification; biodiversity protection at local, national and international scales
- **3.2.4.2** Climate change consequences; Paris Agreement and National Adaptation Plan; mitigation; adaptation
- **3.2.4.3** Regional differences in development; strategies to reduce them; education and gender equality; food security

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Sustainable development | Meeting present needs without harming future generations' ability to meet theirs |
| Smog | Mix of smoke, fog and pollutants, common in Lahore in winter |
| Deforestation | Permanent removal of forest |
| Afforestation / reforestation | Planting trees on new land / replanting cleared land |
| Desertification | Spread of desert conditions in dry areas through climate and human misuse |
| Biodiversity | Variety of plant and animal life |
| Mitigation | Reducing the causes of climate change |
| Adaptation | Reducing the harm caused by climate change |
| GLOF | Glacial lake outburst flood |
| Food security | Reliable access to enough affordable, nutritious food |

## Environmental issues

**Pollution**
- *Air:* vehicles, brick kilns, industry, crop burning → Lahore smog, lung disease.
- *Water:* untreated sewage and factory waste → disease, damage to rivers and coast.
- *Soil:* agrochemicals, salinity, waste → lower yields.
- *Noise:* traffic, generators, construction → stress and hearing damage.

**Deforestation**
- *Causes:* fuelwood, timber, farmland, housing, over-grazing, illegal logging.
- *Impacts:* erosion, landslides, floods, silting of reservoirs, habitat loss.
- *Strategies:* Billion Tree Tsunami (KP, 2014); Ten Billion Tree Tsunami (2018); mangrove protection; LPG instead of wood.

**Desertification**
- *Areas:* Thar, Cholistan, Thal, Balochistan.
- *Causes:* drought, over-grazing, fuelwood cutting, over-pumping, salinity.
- *Strategies:* shelter belts, controlled grazing, drip irrigation, water harvesting, small dams.

**Biodiversity**
- *Local:* community trophy-hunting schemes protecting markhor.
- *National:* national parks (Khunjerab, Margalla Hills, Kirthar, Hingol); protection of the Indus river dolphin.
- *International:* WWF, UN conventions.

## Climate change

**Consequences**
- Heatwaves (Karachi 2015).
- Glacier melt and GLOF risk in the north.
- Extreme floods: **2022** — large areas of Sindh and Balochistan under water; more than 1,700 deaths; damage and losses about US$30 billion.
- Drought; sea-level rise and saltwater intrusion in the Indus delta.

**Responses**

| Mitigation | Adaptation |
|---|---|
| More solar, wind and hydro (Alternative and Renewable Energy Policy 2019) | Early warning (Pakistan Meteorological Department) |
| Electric vehicle policy | Disaster preparedness (NDMA) |
| Mass transit | Heatwave plans |
| Reforestation | Flood embankments, raised homes, wetland protection |

**Agreements:** Paris Agreement (keep warming well below 2°C); National Adaptation Plan (2023); push
for a loss and damage fund at the 2022 UN climate conference.

## Development and equality

- **Least developed:** Balochistan and the former tribal districts (merged into KP in 2018).
- **Factors:** physical, economic, social and political.
- **Strategies:** NFC award shares revenue with provinces; BISP (2008) and Ehsaas cash transfers to poor women; infrastructure and CPEC projects.
- **Education:** Article 25A (free schooling 5–16); girls' stipends; local schools; female teachers.
- **Gender:** reserved seats, anti-harassment and anti-violence laws.
- **Food security:** price rises, floods and drought threaten supply; child stunting is widespread; strategies include farmer support, water management, wheat reserves, nutrition programmes.

## Judging effectiveness: a quick framework

For any strategy, ask four questions and use them to structure an evaluation:
1. **Scale:** how many people or how much land does it reach?
2. **Cost:** who pays, and can it be afforded long term?
3. **Evidence:** is there a named result, such as hectares replanted or lives saved?
4. **Sustainability:** will the benefit last, and does it create new problems?

Example: the Billion Tree Tsunami reached a large area of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa and was praised
internationally, but its long-term success depends on saplings surviving and on protection from
logging and grazing.

## Worked example

**Question:** Explain how deforestation increases the risk of flooding. **[4]**

**Model answer:** Trees intercept rainfall and their roots help water soak into the soil [1]. When
forests are cleared, more rain flows quickly over the surface into rivers [1]. Bare soil is eroded
and washed into rivers and reservoirs, raising riverbeds and reducing storage [1]. So rivers rise
faster and higher during heavy rain, increasing the risk of floods downstream [1].

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- **Mixing up mitigation and adaptation.** Planting trees is mitigation; flood warnings are adaptation.
- **Vague "the government should" answers.** Name a specific programme or law.
- **Ignoring scale.** The specification asks about local, national and international strategies.
- **No judgement on effectiveness.** Say whether a strategy worked and why.

## How is this topic examined?

This topic is examined in OxfordAQA 9236 Paper 2, Section C, worth 21 of the paper's 77 marks; each paper lasts 1 hour 30 minutes. The section mixes short and extended response questions on this optional topic. Short items reward precise named facts, so learn the dates, places and people in the tables above; the extended responses reward a clear line of argument, two or three developed points with evidence, and a judgement that answers the question.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why is Lahore's smog worst in winter?**
Cold, still air traps pollution near the ground, while crop
burning, traffic and brick kilns add smoke and particles.

**What is the NFC award?**
The National Finance Commission award, which sets how federal tax revenue
is shared among the provinces.
