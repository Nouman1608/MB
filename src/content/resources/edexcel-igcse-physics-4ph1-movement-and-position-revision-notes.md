Condensed notes on sub-topic **1(b) Movement and position** (statements
1.3 to 1.10) of Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Physics (4PH1), Issue 4.
For full explanations and worked examples, use the
[Movement and position study guide](/resources/edexcel-igcse-physics-4ph1-movement-and-position/).

## Equations

```
average speed = distance moved / time taken            (know and use)
acceleration  = change in velocity / time taken        (know and use)
a = (v − u) / t
v² = u² + 2as                                          (use)
```

Rearrangements you should be able to do without thinking:

```
distance = average speed × time       time = distance / average speed
v = u + at                            t = (v − u) / a
s = (v² − u²) / 2a                    a = (v² − u²) / 2s
```

v = u + at is simply a = (v − u) ÷ t rearranged.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| speed | distance moved per unit time (scalar), m/s |
| velocity | speed in a given direction (vector), m/s |
| average speed | total distance ÷ total time for the whole journey |
| acceleration | change in velocity ÷ time taken, m/s² |
| deceleration | negative acceleration: velocity decreasing |
| uniform acceleration | constant acceleration: a straight sloping v−t line |
| gradient | change in y ÷ change in x for a line on a graph |
| tangent | straight line touching a curve at one point; its gradient gives the instantaneous rate |
| area under v−t graph | distance travelled |

## Graph summary

| Feature | Distance−time graph | Velocity−time graph |
|---|---|---|
| gradient | speed | acceleration |
| horizontal line | stationary | constant velocity |
| straight sloping line | constant speed | constant acceleration |
| curve getting steeper | speeding up | acceleration increasing |
| curve getting flatter | slowing down | acceleration decreasing |
| area under line | not used | distance travelled |

Areas: rectangle = base × height; triangle = ½ × base × height; curve =
count squares × distance per square.

## Choosing the equation

| You know | You want | Use |
|---|---|---|
| distance and time | average speed | average speed = distance ÷ time |
| u, v and t | acceleration | a = (v − u) ÷ t |
| u, a and s (no time) | final speed | v² = u² + 2as |
| u, v and a (no time) | distance | s = (v² − u²) ÷ 2a |
| a v−t graph | acceleration | gradient |
| a v−t graph | distance | area under the line |
| a d−t graph | speed | gradient (tangent if curved) |

If the acceleration is not constant, only the graph methods work.

## Describing motion from a graph

Work stage by stage, quoting values with units: "From 0 to 6 s the car
accelerates uniformly from rest to 12 m/s; from 6 s to 15 s it travels at
a constant 12 m/s; it then decelerates to rest at 18 s." For **explain**,
add the reason from the graph: "the line is straight, so the acceleration
is constant".

## Practical 1.5 in brief

- Toy car released from rest down a ramp, or a tennis ball dropped or
  rolled.
- Measure distances with a metre rule or tape; times with a stopwatch,
  light gates and data logger, or video against a scale.
- Repeat and calculate means; release from the same point each time.
- Stopwatch timing includes reaction time, a large fraction of short
  times; light gates or video reduce this error.
- Calculate average speed for each section; plot a distance−time graph.

## Exam traps

- Gradient of v−t is **acceleration**, not speed.
- A horizontal line means **stationary** on d−t but **constant velocity**
  on v−t.
- Average speed is total distance ÷ total time, including time spent
  stopped.
- Acceleration is (v − u) ÷ t, final minus initial. Slowing down gives a
  negative value.
- v² = u² + 2as needs constant acceleration; square u, and take the square
  root at the end.
- "Starts from rest" means u = 0; "comes to rest" means v = 0.
- Convert minutes, km and km/h to s, m and m/s before using gradients or
  areas.
- When using a tangent, draw it long and read two widely spaced points.

## Self-test

1. State what the gradient of a distance−time graph represents.
2. Describe the motion shown by a horizontal line on a velocity−time
   graph.
3. A runner covers 400 m in 50 s. Calculate her average speed.
4. A train slows from 30 m/s to 12 m/s in 9.0 s. Calculate its
   acceleration.
5. State how to find the distance travelled from a velocity−time graph.
6. A car accelerates uniformly from rest to 20 m/s in 10 s. Calculate the
   distance travelled.
7. A cyclist travels 2.0 km in 5.0 minutes, then 1.0 km in 5.0 minutes.
   Calculate the average speed for the whole journey in m/s.
8. A stone is dropped from rest and reaches 15 m/s. Using a = 10 m/s²,
   calculate how far it has fallen.
9. A car at 18 m/s accelerates at 1.5 m/s² over 60 m. Calculate its final
   speed.
10. How would you find the speed at one instant from a curved distance−time
    graph?
11. Why are light gates better than a stopwatch for timing a falling
    tennis ball?
12. Under what condition can v² = u² + 2as be used?

## Answers

1. Speed.
2. Moving at constant velocity; acceleration is zero.
3. 400 ÷ 50 = 8.0 m/s.
4. a = (12 − 30) ÷ 9.0 = −2.0 m/s² (a deceleration of 2.0 m/s²).
5. Calculate the area between the line and the time axis.
6. Area = ½ × 10 × 20 = 100 m.
7. Total distance 3000 m; total time 600 s; average speed = 3000 ÷ 600 =
   5.0 m/s.
8. s = (v² − u²) ÷ 2a = (225 − 0) ÷ 20 = 11.25 m, so 11 m (2 s.f.).
9. v² = 18² + 2 × 1.5 × 60 = 324 + 180 = 504; v = √504 = 22 m/s (2 s.f.).
10. Draw a tangent to the curve at that time and calculate its gradient.
11. The fall takes well under a second, so human reaction time is a large
    fraction of the measured time; light gates start and stop
    automatically, removing reaction time.
12. Only when the acceleration is constant (uniform).
