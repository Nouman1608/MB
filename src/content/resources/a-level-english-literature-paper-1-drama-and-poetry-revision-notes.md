## Paper 1 at a glance

| | |
|---|---|
| Format | Written paper, **2 hours, 50 marks** |
| Sections | **Section A: Drama** (25 marks) and **Section B: Poetry** (25 marks) |
| Questions answered | **Two** — one from Section A, one from Section B |
| Question choice | Each set text offers a choice of an **(a)** or a **(b)** question |
| (b) question | Based on a **passage or poem from the set text, printed on the question paper** |
| Materials | **Set texts and dictionaries are not allowed** in the exam room |
| Route | Forms **half of the AS Level award**, alongside Paper 2 (Prose and Unseen) |

## Assessment objectives

All four objectives are assessed on **every** question on this paper, each weighted **25%**.

| AO | Focus |
|---|---|
| **AO1** | Knowledge and understanding — respond with understanding to the text, with appreciation of relevant context |
| **AO2** | Analysis — analyse how a writer's choices of **language, form and structure** shape meaning and effect |
| **AO3** | Personal response — produce an **informed, independent** opinion or interpretation |
| **AO4** | Communication — communicate a **relevant, structured, well-supported** response |

**AO5 (Evaluation of opinion)** is not assessed on Paper 1 — it appears only on Papers 3 and 4, alongside the higher A Level weighting.

## The (a) vs (b) choice

For each set text, candidates choose **one** of two question types:

- **(a)** — a **discursive essay question** on the text as a whole (theme, character, technique across the play or collection); no extract is printed.
- **(b)** — a question anchored to a **specific passage or poem printed on the paper**, requiring close, line-level commentary that also connects outward to the whole text.

Neither is "easier" — (a) rewards breadth and structural control across a whole text; (b) rewards precision on the printed material. Choose whichever plays to your strengths on the day, not by habit.

## Section A: Drama — what to revise

- **Theatricality**, not just plot: staging, stage directions, dramatic irony, pacing, and how a scene would land with a live audience.
- Character through **action and dialogue**, not summary of what a character "is like".
- How a dramatist builds and releases **tension** across a scene or act.

## Section B: Poetry — what to revise

- **Line-level analysis**: word choice (diction), imagery, sound, rhythm and metre, form and structure.
- The **effect** of a technique, not just naming it — a metaphor identified without an explained effect earns little.
- Avoid **paraphrase** ("the poem is about…") — that is description, not analysis, and does not access AO2.

## Exam craft

- Practise **both sections in one timed sitting**, not separately — pacing across the full two hours is a skill in itself, and it is where marks are commonly lost even by well-prepared candidates.
- **Specific textual evidence beats generic structure.** An essay with a simple shape but precise, well-chosen quotation consistently outscores a confidently structured essay with thin support.
- Always check the **current set-text grid** for your own examination series before revising — set texts rotate by year, and these notes deliberately cover the paper's stable structure and skills rather than any named text.

## Common mistakes

- Writing plot summary instead of analysis in Drama answers.
- Paraphrasing a poem's content instead of analysing its language and form.
- Naming a technique (e.g. "enjambment") without explaining its effect.
- Spending unequal time on the two sections and rushing the second answer.
- Giving no personal, independent line of interpretation (AO3) — agreeing with "the obvious" reading only.
