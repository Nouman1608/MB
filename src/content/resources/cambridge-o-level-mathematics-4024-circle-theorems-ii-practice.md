These questions cover section 4.8, Circle theorems II, of the Cambridge O Level Mathematics (Syllabus D) 4024 syllabus for 2025, 2026 and 2027. The properties are explained in the [circle theorems II study guide](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii/), and the [circle theorems II revision notes](/resources/cambridge-o-level-mathematics-4024-circle-theorems-ii-revision-notes/) summarise them. In every question, O is the centre of the circle. Give a geometrical reason for each step. Diagrams are not drawn to scale.

## Section A

**1.** A circle has radius 5 cm. A chord is 8 cm long. Find the distance from the centre to the chord. **[2]**

**2.** A circle has radius 17 cm. A chord is 30 cm long. Find its distance from the centre. **[3]**

**3.** P is 10 cm from the centre of a circle of radius 6 cm. A tangent from P touches the circle at T. Find the length PT. **[2]**

**4.** Tangents from a point P touch a circle at A and B. PA = (2x + 3) cm and PB = (5x − 9) cm. Find x and the length PA. **[3]**

**5.** Tangents from P touch a circle at A and B, and angle APB = 70°. Find angle PAB, giving reasons. **[2]**

**6.** A circle has radius 26 cm. A chord AB is 20 cm long. Find the distance from the centre to AB. **[3]**

## Section B

**7.** PQ and RS are equal chords in a circle of radius 25 cm. Each has length 40 cm.

**(a)** Find the distance from the centre to PQ.

**(b)** State the distance from the centre to RS, giving a reason. **[3]**

**8.** In triangle ABC, AB = 13 cm, BC = 14 cm and CA = 15 cm. A circle touches AB at D, BC at E and CA at F. Find the lengths AD, BE and CF. **[4]**

**9.** Tangents from P touch a circle, centre O, at A and B. Angle AOB = 118°.

**(a)** Find angle APB.

**(b)** Find angle OAB.

Give reasons. **[4]**

**10.** M is the midpoint of a chord AB of a circle with centre O. OM = 9 cm and OA = 15 cm.

**(a)** Explain why angle OMA = 90°.

**(b)** Find the length of AB. **[4]**

**11.** The circle with centre O has radius 7 cm. Tangents from P touch it at A and B. OP = 25 cm.

**(a)** Find PA.

**(b)** Find the perimeter of the quadrilateral OAPB.

**(c)** Find the area of the quadrilateral OAPB. **[5]**

**12.** Two parallel chords in a circle of radius 13 cm have lengths 10 cm and 24 cm. Find the two possible distances between the chords. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why the longest chord of a circle is a diameter. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Half the chord is 4 cm (the perpendicular from the centre bisects the chord) [1]. Distance = √(5² − 4²) = **3 cm** [1].

**2.** Half the chord is 15 cm [1]. Distance² = 17² − 15² = 289 − 225 = 64 [1]. Distance = **8 cm** [1].

**3.** Angle OTP = 90° (tangent and radius) [1]. PT = √(10² − 6²) = **8 cm** [1].

**4.** Tangents from an external point are equal, so 2x + 3 = 5x − 9 [1]. 12 = 3x, so **x = 4** [1]. PA = 2(4) + 3 = **11 cm** [1].

**5.** PA = PB (tangents from an external point are equal), so triangle PAB is isosceles [1]. Angle PAB = (180 − 70) ÷ 2 = **55°** [1].

**6.** Half the chord is 10 cm [1]. Distance² = 26² − 10² = 676 − 100 = 576 [1]. Distance = **24 cm** [1].

**7. (a)** Half the chord is 20 cm [1]. Distance = √(25² − 20²) = √225 = **15 cm** [1].

**(b)** **15 cm**, because equal chords are equidistant from the centre [1].

**8.** Let AD = AF = x, BD = BE = y, CE = CF = z (tangents from an external point are equal) [1]. x + y = 13, y + z = 14, x + z = 15, so x + y + z = 21 [1]. **AD = 21 − 14 = 7 cm**, **BE = 21 − 15 = 6 cm** and **CF = 21 − 13 = 8 cm** [1][1].

**9. (a)** Angles OAP and OBP are 90° (tangent and radius) [1]. Angle APB = 360 − 90 − 90 − 118 = **62°** (angle sum of a quadrilateral) [1].

**(b)** OA = OB (radii), so triangle OAB is isosceles [1]. Angle OAB = (180 − 118) ÷ 2 = **31°** [1].

**10. (a)** M is the midpoint of AB and OA = OB, so triangle OAB is isosceles and OM is its line of symmetry, so it meets AB at a right angle (the line from the centre to the midpoint of a chord is perpendicular to the chord) [1][1].

**(b)** AM² = 15² − 9² = 225 − 81 = 144, so AM = 12 cm [1]. AB = **24 cm** [1].

**11. (a)** Angle OAP = 90° (tangent and radius), so PA = √(25² − 7²) = √576 = **24 cm** [1][1].

**(b)** PB = PA = 24 cm (tangents from an external point are equal) and OA = OB = 7 cm, so the perimeter is 2(7 + 24) = **62 cm** [1].

**(c)** Area = 2 × (1/2 × 7 × 24) = **168 cm²** [1][1].

**12.** 10 cm chord: distance = √(169 − 25) = 12 cm [1]. 24 cm chord: distance = √(169 − 144) = 5 cm [1]. On opposite sides of the centre the chords are 12 + 5 = **17 cm** apart [1]. On the same side they are 12 − 5 = **7 cm** apart [1].

**13.** The distance d from the centre to a chord satisfies half-chord² = r² − d² [1]. This is greatest when d = 0, which means the chord passes through the centre, so it is a diameter [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the whole chord instead of half in Pythagoras' theorem.
- Giving half the chord as the final answer.
- Forgetting that tangent and radius meet at 90°.
- Leaving out the second solution when parallel chords could be on either side of the centre.
- Writing reasons that do not name the property.
