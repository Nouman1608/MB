These questions cover section **3.3.12.1 Condensation polymers** of the AQA AS and A-level Chemistry specification (7404/7405), for teaching from September 2015. It is A-level only content, examined on Paper 2 and Paper 3. Learn the ideas first in the [Condensation polymers study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-condensation-polymers/) and the [Condensation polymers revision notes](/resources/aqa-a-level-chemistry-7405-condensation-polymers-revision-notes/). Use Aᵣ values H = 1.0, C = 12.0, N = 14.0, O = 16.0.

## Questions

**1.** State what is meant by condensation polymerisation. **[2]**

**2.** Nylon 6,6 is made from hexanedioic acid, HOOC(CH₂)₄COOH, and 1,6-diaminohexane, H₂N(CH₂)₆NH₂.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit of nylon 6,6. **[2]**

**(b)** Name the type of link between the monomer units. **[1]**

**(c)** State the small molecule lost in the reaction. **[1]**

**3.** Terylene is a polyester. Name the two monomers used to make it. **[2]**

**4.** The structure below shows part of a polymer chain.

–NH–CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂–NH–CO–CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂–CO–NH–CH₂CH₂CH₂CH₂–NH–CO–

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Name the type of polymer. **[1]**

**(c)** Draw the structures of the two monomers. **[2]**

**5.** A section of a polymer is shown: –O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–CH₂CH₂–CO–O–CH₂CH₂–O–CO–CH₂CH₂–CO–

**(a)** Name the type of polymer. **[1]**

**(b)** Draw the structures of the two monomers. **[2]**

**6.** The amino acid glycine, H₂N–CH₂–COOH, can form a polymer.

**(a)** Draw the repeating unit. **[1]**

**(b)** Write an equation for the formation of the polymer from n molecules of glycine. **[1]**

**7.** Explain why a monomer used to make a condensation polymer must have two functional groups. **[2]**

**8.** Name the main intermolecular forces between chains in:

**(a)** a polyalkene such as poly(ethene) **[1]**

**(b)** a polyester **[1]**

**(c)** nylon 6,6 **[1]**

**9.** Kevlar is used in bulletproof vests. Explain how its structure leads to very strong fibres. **[3]**

**10.** 14.6 g of hexanedioic acid (Mᵣ = 146.0) reacts with 11.6 g of 1,6-diaminohexane (Mᵣ = 116.0) to form nylon 6,6.

**(a)** Calculate the amounts, in mol, of each monomer. **[1]**

**(b)** Calculate the maximum mass of polymer formed (repeating unit Mᵣ = 226.0). **[2]**

**(c)** Calculate the mass of water produced. **[1]**

**11.** Calculate the atom economy of forming the repeating unit of Terylene (Mᵣ = 192.0) from benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid (Mᵣ = 166.0) and ethane-1,2-diol (Mᵣ = 62.0). **[2]**

**12.** Compare the polymerisation of ethene with the polymerisation of glycine, referring to the monomers, the products and the atom economy. **[4]**

**13.** Explain why a polyester can be described as having a different type of intermolecular force between chains from a polyamide. **[2]**

## Answers

**1.** Monomers join together to form a polymer [1]; a small molecule, such as water, is lost each time two monomers react [1]. **[2]**

**2. (a)** –[CO–(CH₂)₄–CO–NH–(CH₂)₆–NH]ₙ– (one unit of each monomer with the amide link and extended bonds) [2]

**(b)** Amide (peptide) link, –CO–NH– [1]

**(c)** Water [1]. **[4]**

**3.** Benzene-1,4-dicarboxylic acid [1]; ethane-1,2-diol [1]. **[2]**

**4. (a)** –[NH–(CH₂)₄–NH–CO–(CH₂)₅–CO]ₙ– or an equivalent unit with the same atoms [1]

**(b)** Polyamide [1]

**(c)** H₂N–(CH₂)₄–NH₂ [1]; HOOC–(CH₂)₅–COOH [1]. **[4]**

*Examiner insight:* Break each amide link between C and N. Add –OH to the carbonyl carbon and H to the nitrogen.

**5. (a)** Polyester [1]

**(b)** HOCH₂CH₂OH [1]; HOOC–CH₂CH₂–COOH [1]. **[3]**

**6. (a)** –[NH–CH₂–CO]ₙ– [1]

**(b)** n H₂N–CH₂–COOH → –[NH–CH₂–CO]ₙ– + n H₂O [1]. **[2]**

**7.** Each monomer must bond at both ends [1]; so that the chain can continue to grow in both directions (a monomer with one group would stop the chain) [1]. **[2]**

**8. (a)** London forces [1]

**(b)** Permanent dipole–dipole attractions (and London forces) [1]

**(c)** Hydrogen bonds between N–H and C=O groups (and London forces) [1]. **[3]**

**9.** Kevlar is a polyamide, so there are hydrogen bonds between the N–H of one chain and the C=O of another [1]; the benzene rings make the chains rigid and straight [1]; so the chains line up and pack closely, giving many strong hydrogen bonds along their length [1]. **[3]**

**10. (a)** n(acid) = 14.6 ÷ 146.0 = 0.100 mol; n(diamine) = 11.6 ÷ 116.0 = 0.100 mol [1]

**(b)** n(repeating units) = 0.100 mol [1]; mass = 0.100 × 226.0 = **22.6 g** [1]

**(c)** n(water) = 2 × 0.100 = 0.200 mol; mass = 0.200 × 18.0 = **3.6 g** [1]. **[4]**

**11.** Atom economy = 192.0 ÷ (166.0 + 62.0) × 100 [1] = **84.2 %** [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Ethene is an alkene and glycine is an amino acid with two functional groups, –NH₂ and –COOH [1]; ethene forms only the polymer (addition polymerisation) whereas glycine forms the polymer and water [1]; so the atom economy for poly(ethene) is 100 % [1]; the atom economy for polyglycine is below 100 % because water is lost [1]. **[4]**

**13.** A polyamide has N–H groups, so hydrogen bonds form between N–H and C=O groups of neighbouring chains [1]; a polyester has C=O and C–O but no N–H or O–H group in the chain, so it has only dipole–dipole attractions (and London forces) between chains [1]. **[2]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing half of an ester or amide link at the end of the bracket.
- Leaving the C=O out of the link.
- Giving a monomer with only one functional group.
- Forgetting water as the second product.
- Claiming hydrogen bonding in polyesters.
- Reversing which atom receives H when finding the monomers.
