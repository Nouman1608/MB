## Section B

**7.** Carbon dioxide sublimes at −78 °C. Silicon(IV) oxide melts at about 1700 °C.

**(a)** Describe the structure of solid silicon(IV) oxide. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain the large difference between these two temperatures. **[3]**

**8.** Four substances are described below (invented data).

| Substance | m.p. / °C | Conducts as solid | Conducts as liquid | Soluble in water |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| W | 2050 | no | yes | very slightly |
| X | 1410 | no | no | no |
| Y | 44 | no | no | no |
| Z | 1495 | yes | yes | no |

Deduce the type of structure in each of W, X, Y and Z, giving one piece of evidence for each. **[4]**

**9.** Graphite and diamond are both forms of carbon.

**(a)** Explain why the layers in graphite can slide over each other, yet graphite has a very high melting point. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain why graphite conducts electricity much better along its layers than at right angles to them. **[2]**

**10.** Sodium chloride is soluble in water but insoluble in hexane.

**(a)** Explain, in terms of the attractions involved, why sodium chloride dissolves in water. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why it does not dissolve in hexane. **[1]**

**(c)** Iodine dissolves readily in hexane. Explain why. **[1]**

**11.** Copper is used for electrical wires and saucepans.

**(a)** Describe the structure of copper. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why copper has a high melting point. **[1]**

**(c)** Explain why copper conducts electricity in the solid state. **[1]**

**12.** Buckminsterfullerene, C₆₀, and diamond are both made only of carbon atoms.

**(a)** Describe the structure of a C₆₀ molecule. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why C₆₀ changes to a gas at a much lower temperature than diamond. **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** Simple molecular [1].

**(b)** Giant molecular (giant covalent) [1].

**2.** C [1].

**3. (a)** Four [1].

**(b)** Three [1].

**4.** Instantaneous dipole–induced dipole (id-id) forces [1].

**5.** The ions are held in fixed positions in the lattice and cannot move to carry charge [1].

**6.** Each water molecule is hydrogen-bonded to four others in a tetrahedral arrangement [1], which holds the molecules further apart than in the liquid, leaving more empty space [1].

**7. (a)** Each silicon atom is covalently bonded to four oxygen atoms (tetrahedrally) and each oxygen to two silicon atoms [1], forming a giant three-dimensional network [1].

**(b)** CO₂ is simple molecular, so only weak id-id forces between molecules are overcome [1]. SiO₂ is giant molecular, so melting requires many strong Si–O covalent bonds to break [1]. Far more energy is needed for SiO₂ [1].

**8.** W: giant ionic (conducts only when liquid) [1]. X: giant molecular (very high m.p., never conducts) [1]. Y: simple molecular (low m.p., does not conduct) [1]. Z: giant metallic (conducts as solid and liquid) [1].

**9. (a)** The layers are held together only by weak id-id forces, so they slide over each other easily [1]. Within each layer, every carbon is joined to three others by strong covalent bonds [1]. Melting requires these many covalent bonds to break, so the melting point is very high [1].

**(b)** The delocalised electrons are free to move within each layer [1], but not easily from one layer to the next, which are separated by a relatively large gap [1].

**10. (a)** Water molecules are polar: δ− O atoms attract Na⁺ ions and δ+ H atoms attract Cl⁻ ions [1]; these attractions help to separate the ions from the lattice [1].

**(b)** Hexane is non-polar and cannot attract the ions strongly enough to overcome the ionic attraction in the lattice [1].

**(c)** Iodine and hexane are both non-polar, and id-id forces form between iodine and hexane molecules [1].

**11. (a)** A regular lattice of positive copper ions [1] surrounded by delocalised electrons, held by electrostatic attraction [1].

**(b)** Strong electrostatic attraction between the positive ions and the delocalised electrons must be overcome, which needs a lot of energy [1].

**(c)** The delocalised electrons are free to move and carry charge [1].

**12. (a)** A closed, ball-shaped cage of 60 carbon atoms [1], each bonded to three others, in a pattern of pentagons and hexagons [1].

**(b)** C₆₀ is simple molecular, so only id-id forces between molecules are overcome [1]; diamond is giant molecular, so many strong covalent bonds must be broken [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Saying covalent bonds break when a simple molecular solid melts.
- Calling graphite or C₆₀ by the wrong structure type.
- Writing that ionic solids conduct because "they contain ions".
- Using melting point alone in a structure deduction.
- Solubility answers that say "like dissolves like" without naming the attractions.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is C₆₀ a giant structure like diamond?**
No. C₆₀ is made of separate molecules held by intermolecular forces, so it is simple molecular. Diamond and graphite are giant molecular.
