This guide is for students who choose **equal access to education in Pakistan** as their research subject in Pearson Edexcel A Level Urdu (9UR0). It is the research subject of Theme 1, and the specification divides it into three aspects, all of which you must research:

- **تعلیم کے متوازی نظام**: parallel systems of education
- **لڑکیوں، لڑکوں کے لیے یکساں مواقع**: equal opportunities for girls and boys
- **غربا کے لئے بہتر مواقع**: better opportunities for the poor

The [education revision notes](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-equal-access-to-education-revision-notes/) give the vocabulary and four gaps to investigate. This page shows how to turn those ideas into research and then into a Paper 1 Section C answer. Practise afterwards with the [education practice questions](/resources/edexcel-a-level-urdu-language-9ur0-taleem-education-research-subject-practice/).

## What the research task requires

The rules come from the specification and are worth knowing precisely:

- The research must cover **all three aspects** and be based on **Pakistan only**.
- It must be **independent**: teachers may advise on research methods and confirm that you are covering the aspects, but they may not teach the content or give you sources.
- You gather evidence from **at least two authentic sources in Urdu**, such as newspaper and magazine articles, online material, television, radio or interviews.
- The research must **not** draw on the literary text or film you study for Paper 2.
- In the exam you take in **no notes and no dictionary**. You read one unseen Urdu text, respond to a statement, and use both the text and your research, in approximately **320–370 words**.
- Section C carries 40 of Paper 1's 80 marks, assessed on three grids: knowledge and understanding of society and culture through research (AO4, 20), response to the unseen text (AO2, 10) and accuracy and range of language (AO3, 10). The recommended time is 1 hour 25 minutes.

Because you cannot take notes in, everything you want to use must be memorised: a handful of facts, named examples and Urdu phrases for each aspect.

## Aspect 1: parallel systems of education

**متوازی** means "parallel": systems that run side by side and rarely meet. Map at least four for your research file:

1. **Government schools** (**سرکاری اسکول**), mostly Urdu-medium or regional-language medium, sitting the matriculation and intermediate examinations of the provincial boards.
2. **Low-fee private schools** in neighbourhoods and towns, often claiming English medium.
3. **Elite private schools**, where many pupils take O Level and A Level examinations instead of matriculation, with very different fees, facilities and prospects.
4. **Religious seminaries** (**دینی مدارس**), which often provide free education, food and lodging.

Not-for-profit organisations also run schools for low-income communities; The Citizens Foundation is a well-known example you could research.

The research question for this aspect is not just "what systems exist?" but "**what does the existence of parallel systems do to equality?**" Arguments you can test against sources: children from different systems grow up with unequal English, unequal access to universities and jobs, and little contact with one another; on the other hand, private and non-profit schools reach children the state has not reached. Attempts at a common curriculum, the **یکساں قومی نصاب** (Single National Curriculum), are a direct response to this aspect; find out what it changed and what critics said.

## Aspect 2: equal opportunities for girls and boys

**یکساں** means "the same, equal". Research this aspect in both directions:

- **Girls**: distance and safety, lack of girls' schools at secondary level, shortage of female teachers, early marriage, household work and family attitudes. Measures to look for include stipends for girls' attendance in some provinces, new girls' schools and transport schemes.
- **Boys**: boys too leave school, often to earn money for the family. A source that shows boys dropping out to work gives you a counter-example that makes your argument more balanced.
- **Regional differences**: gaps are wider in some provinces and districts than others. Compare one region with another using a named source rather than general claims.

For each point, your file should hold the claim, the source (name and date), and one memorable detail you can write from memory.

## Aspect 3: better opportunities for the poor

**غربا** is the plural of غریب. This aspect asks what is being done, or could be done, so collect **measures** as well as problems:

- **The constitutional right**: Article 25A, inserted in 2010, makes free and compulsory education for children of a stated age range a fundamental right. Check the exact wording and age range in your own source.
- **Cash support linked to attendance**: conditional cash transfers under the Benazir Income Support Programme, launched in 2008, have been used to encourage school attendance. Check the scheme's name and details before quoting them.
- **Free textbooks, stipends and scholarships** (**وظائف**), and non-formal or community schools for children who have missed years.
- **The hidden costs**: even "free" schooling costs money in uniforms, transport and lost earnings. This is a strong analytical point: poverty affects access even where fees are zero.

## Building a research file you can remember

Keep the file in Urdu. A one-page summary per aspect works well:

| Heading | What to write |
|---|---|
| مرکزی نکتہ | the main point in one sentence |
| ثبوت | two facts or examples, each with source name and date |
| مثال | one named case: a scheme, a district, a school type |
| دوسری رائے | the strongest counter-argument |
| میرا نتیجہ | your judgement in one sentence |

Learn the summaries, not the full articles. In the exam, signal research with phrases such as **میری تحقیق کے مطابق**, **میں نے اپنی تحقیق کے دوران پڑھا کہ**, or **ایک اخباری رپورٹ کے مطابق**.

## Worked example: planning a Section C answer

Suppose the statement is: «پاکستان میں امیر اور غریب بچوں کے لیے دو الگ الگ تعلیمی دنیائیں ہیں۔» (In Pakistan there are two separate educational worlds for rich and poor children.) The unseen text describes a village school with one teacher and an expensive city school with laboratories.

**Plan (about 5 minutes):**

1. **Position**: largely agree, with qualification.
2. **Point 1 (aspect 1)**: parallel systems, from research; link to the contrast in the text.
3. **Point 2 (aspect 3)**: poverty and hidden costs, from research; one measure and its limits.
4. **Point 3 (aspect 2)**: girls in poor rural families face a double disadvantage; a named example from research.
5. **Counter-point**: non-profit schools and stipends are narrowing the gap in some places.
6. **Conclusion**: the two worlds exist, but the gap is not fixed; name the condition for closing it.

**Model opening:**

> دیے گئے متن میں ایک دیہی اسکول کا ذکر ہے جہاں صرف ایک استاد پانچ جماعتوں کو پڑھاتا ہے، جبکہ شہر کے ایک مہنگے اسکول میں تجربہ گاہیں اور کمپیوٹر موجود ہیں۔ میری تحقیق بھی اس بیان کی بڑی حد تک تائید کرتی ہے کہ پاکستان میں تعلیم کا نظام طبقاتی بنیادوں پر بٹا ہوا ہے، تاہم میں سمجھتا ہوں کہ یہ تقسیم ناقابلِ تبدیلی نہیں۔

(The given text mentions a rural school where one teacher teaches five classes, while an expensive city school has laboratories and computers. My research also largely supports the statement that Pakistan's education system is divided on class lines; however, I believe this division is not unchangeable.)

**Why it works:** the first sentence uses the unseen text (AO2) without copying it; the second announces research (AO4) and a qualified position; **جبکہ**, **تاہم** and the passive-like **بٹا ہوا ہے** give range (AO3).

**Word budget:** about 50 words for the introduction, three body paragraphs of 80–90 words, and 40–50 for the conclusion keeps you inside 320–370.

## Common mistakes

- Researching only one or two aspects; any of the three may be the focus of the statement.
- Comparing Pakistan with another country: for Theme 1, content on any other country gains no marks.
- Retelling the unseen text instead of using it to support an argument.
- Quoting a statistic you cannot trace, or reciting memorised paragraphs that do not answer the statement.
- Using the Paper 2 novel or film as research evidence, which the specification excludes.

## Exam technique

- Underline the key word in the statement (here, **دو الگ الگ دنیائیں**) and return to it in every paragraph.
- Use the text at least twice, and say so: **متن کے مطابق**, **جیسا کہ متن میں بتایا گیا ہے**.
- Make your research visible and specific: a scheme, a year, a type of school, a province.
- Reach a clear conclusion that answers the statement, as the AO4 grid rewards conclusions linked to arguments.
