This guide covers subtopic **18.2, Esters**, from Topic 18, Carboxylic acids and derivatives, of
Cambridge International AS & A Level Chemistry 9701, 2025–2027 series. It is AS Level content. The
[Carboxylic Acids and Esters study guide](/resources/as-carboxylic-acids-and-esters/) introduces the two
reactions with ethyl ethanoate; this page concentrates on the skills examiners test with them: naming
any ester from its formula and back again, writing equations for unfamiliar esters, comparing the two
kinds of hydrolysis, and calculating yields and product masses.

## What the syllabus asks for

1. recall the reaction by which esters are produced: (a) the **condensation** reaction between an
   alcohol and a carboxylic acid with **concentrated H₂SO₄ as catalyst**
2. describe the **hydrolysis** of esters by **dilute acid** and by **dilute alkali** and heat

Naming is covered by 13.1, which asks for systematic names of esters with up to six carbons in each
part ("six plus six"), **straight chains only** for esters.

## Reading and writing ester names

An ester has the group –COO– joining two parts:

- the **acid part**, which contains the C=O carbon, gives the second word, ending in **-oate**
- the **alcohol part**, the alkyl group bonded to the single-bonded oxygen, gives the first word,
  ending in **-yl**

So the name is **alkyl alkanoate**, but the formula is usually written acid part first:

| Formula | Acid part | Alcohol part | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| HCOOCH₃ | methanoate (1 C) | methyl | methyl methanoate |
| CH₃CH₂COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ | propanoate (3 C) | propyl | propyl propanoate |
| CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₂CH₃ | ethanoate (2 C) | butyl | butyl ethanoate |
| CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ | butanoate (4 C) | methyl | methyl butanoate |

**The method:** find the C=O. Every carbon on the C=O side of the –O–, including the C=O carbon itself,
belongs to the acid part. Every carbon on the other side of the single-bonded oxygen belongs to the alcohol
part. A common slip is to count the C=O carbon in the alcohol part.

Formulae can also be written the other way round: CH₃OOCCH₂CH₂CH₃ is the same compound as
CH₃CH₂CH₂COOCH₃ (methyl butanoate). Always find the C=O before naming.

**Isomers.** Esters with the same molecular formula can differ in how the carbons are split between the
two parts. The straight-chain esters of C₄H₈O₂ are methyl propanoate, ethyl ethanoate and propyl
methanoate; all are functional group isomers of butanoic acid.

## Making esters: condensation

Heat the carboxylic acid and the alcohol with a few drops of concentrated sulfuric acid as catalyst. Water
is eliminated as the two molecules join, so this is a **condensation** reaction. It is **reversible**:

    CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃OH ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + H₂O       (methyl propanoate)
    HCOOH + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH ⇌ HCOOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + H₂O     (propyl methanoate)

**Writing the equation quickly:** take the acid, replace the H of its –OH with the alkyl group of the
alcohol, and write H₂O as the other product. The atoms of the water come from the –OH of the acid and the
H of the alcohol's –OH.

In the laboratory the mixture is heated under reflux and the ester is then separated by distillation.
Esters are often noticeable by a sweet, fruity smell. Because the reaction reaches equilibrium, the yield
is limited; using an excess of one reactant (usually the cheaper alcohol) moves the position of
equilibrium to the right.

## Hydrolysis: the reverse reaction

Hydrolysis breaks the ester bond by reaction with water. Both versions need **heat**, normally under
reflux.

**Acid hydrolysis (dilute acid, heat).** The dilute acid is a catalyst. The reaction is the exact reverse
of esterification and is reversible; a large excess of water from the dilute acid pushes it towards the
acid and alcohol.

    CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃CH₂COOH + CH₃OH

**Alkaline hydrolysis (dilute alkali, heat).** The alkali is a reactant, not a catalyst. The products are
the **carboxylate salt** and the alcohol, and the reaction **goes to completion**:

    CH₃CH₂COOCH₃ + NaOH → CH₃CH₂COONa + CH₃OH

To obtain the free carboxylic acid, the mixture is then **acidified** with a strong acid:

    CH₃CH₂COONa + HCl → CH₃CH₂COOH + NaCl

| | Acid hydrolysis | Alkaline hydrolysis |
|---|---|---|
| Reagent | dilute H₂SO₄ or HCl (catalyst) | dilute NaOH (reactant, used up) |
| Extent | reversible equilibrium | goes to completion |
| Products | carboxylic acid + alcohol | carboxylate salt + alcohol |
| Extra step to get the acid | none | acidify with a strong acid |

## Worked example 1: percentage yield of esterification

**Question.** 7.40 g of propanoic acid is heated with excess methanol and concentrated H₂SO₄. 5.28 g of
methyl propanoate is obtained. Calculate the percentage yield. (Ar: H = 1.0, C = 12.0, O = 16.0)

**Answer.**

Mr(CH₃CH₂COOH) = 74.0, so n(acid) = 7.40 / 74.0 = 0.100 mol

The ratio is 1 : 1, so the theoretical amount of ester is 0.100 mol

Mr(CH₃CH₂COOCH₃) = 4(12.0) + 8(1.0) + 2(16.0) = 88.0, so n(ester obtained) = 5.28 / 88.0 = 0.0600 mol

Percentage yield = 0.0600 / 0.100 × 100 = **60.0%**

The yield is well below 100% largely because the reaction reaches equilibrium.

## Worked example 2: identifying an ester from its hydrolysis products

**Question.** Ester E, C₅H₁₀O₂, is heated with aqueous sodium hydroxide. The products are sodium
ethanoate and an alcohol. Name E and the alcohol, and give the mass of sodium ethanoate formed from
5.10 g of E. (Mr: E = 102.0, CH₃COONa = 82.0)

**Answer.** Sodium ethanoate shows that the acid part has two carbons. E has five carbons, so the alcohol
part has three: a straight-chain propyl group. **E is propyl ethanoate**, CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃, and the
alcohol is **propan-1-ol**.

    CH₃COOCH₂CH₂CH₃ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + CH₃CH₂CH₂OH

n(E) = 5.10 / 102.0 = 0.0500 mol, so n(CH₃COONa) = 0.0500 mol and mass = 0.0500 × 82.0 = **4.10 g**

## Esters in multi-step questions

Esters often appear in the middle of a synthesis route. Recognise the reagent pattern:

- carboxylic acid + alcohol + concentrated H₂SO₄ → ester (condensation)
- ester + dilute NaOH, heat → carboxylate salt + alcohol (hydrolysis); then dilute HCl → carboxylic acid
- the alcohol released can then be oxidised (primary alcohol to aldehyde or acid) or dehydrated to an
  alkene, linking esters to the alcohol and alkene reactions you already know

## Common mistakes

- **Reversing the name**: CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ is ethyl ethanoate, and HCOOCH₂CH₃ is ethyl methanoate, not
  methyl ethanoate.
- **Using dilute H₂SO₄ for esterification.** It must be **concentrated** H₂SO₄.
- **Giving the free acid as the product of alkaline hydrolysis.** It is the salt until acidified.
- **Calling the alkali a catalyst.** It is used up.
- **Forgetting "heat"** for hydrolysis; the syllabus wording includes it.
- **Missing water** from an esterification equation, or putting it on the wrong side.

## Exam technique for the command words

- **State** or **Give** conditions: "heat with concentrated H₂SO₄ as catalyst" for esterification;
  "heat with dilute acid" or "heat with dilute NaOH, then acidify" for hydrolysis.
- **Describe** hydrolysis: give the equation, the type of product (acid or salt) and whether it is
  reversible.
- **Deduce** an ester's identity: split its carbons into acid part and alcohol part using the products.
- **Calculate**: write the 1 : 1 equation and use molar masses to three significant figures.
