Condensed notes on section **3.5.3 Making financial decisions: sources of finance** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [sources of finance study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-sources-of-finance/); exam-style questions are in the [sources of finance practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-sources-of-finance-practice/).

## Specification content

- Internal and external sources of finance: debt factoring, overdrafts, retained profits, share capital, loans, venture capital, crowd funding.
- Advantages and disadvantages of different sources of finance for short- and long-term uses.
- Throughout section 3.5: technology (for example crowd funding); market conditions; ethical and environmental influences; competitiveness; links with other functions.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Internal finance | Raised from within the business |
| External finance | Raised from outside the business |
| Working capital | Money for day-to-day running costs |
| Overdraft | Facility to spend more than the account holds, up to a limit |
| Debt factoring | Selling invoices to a factor for immediate cash, less a fee |
| Retained profit | Profit kept in the business |
| Share capital | Money raised by selling shares |
| Loan | Fixed sum borrowed and repaid with interest |
| Venture capital | Investment in a risky young business in return for equity |
| Crowd funding | Raising small sums from many people online |
| Gearing | Extent to which a business is financed by debt |

## Match the finance to the need

- **Short-term need** (cash gap): overdraft, debt factoring, trade credit.
- **Long-term need** (machinery, expansion): loan, share capital, retained profit, venture capital, leasing.
- Using an overdraft to buy a factory is risky because it can be withdrawn at short notice.

## The sources compared

| Source | Type | For | Against |
|---|---|---|---|
| Retained profit | Internal | No interest; no outside control | Needs profit; limited amount; opportunity cost |
| Owner's savings / sale of assets | Internal | No cost of borrowing | Limited; personal risk |
| Overdraft | External, short | Flexible; interest on amount used only | High interest; can be withdrawn |
| Debt factoring | External, short | Quick cash from invoices | Fee; looks like weakness; loses control of debt collection |
| Bank loan | External, long | Large sums; fixed repayments; owners keep control | Interest and repayment even if profit falls; security; gearing risk |
| Share capital | External, long | No repayment; no interest; very large sums for a plc | Loss of control; dividends expected; costly to issue; takeover risk |
| Venture capital | External, long | Large sums; expertise | Loss of ownership; investor expects exit and high return |
| Crowd funding | External | No bank; tests demand; publicity | Target may fail; time and marketing effort; ideas made public |

## Worked examples to remember

- **Factoring:** invoices £80,000; advance 85% = **£68,000**; fee 2.5% = **£2,000**; balance **£10,000** paid later.
- **Overdraft:** £15,000 for 30 days at 12% a year = 15,000 × 0.12 × 30 ÷ 365 = **£147.95**.
- **Loan:** £100,000 at 8% costs **£8,000** a year in interest. A project adding £15,000 a year before interest still adds £7,000; one adding £6,000 causes a £2,000 loss.
- **Shares:** 50,000 shares at £2 raise **£100,000**; a 10p dividend costs £5,000 but is optional.
- **Venture capital:** £500,000 for 30% values the company at about **£1.67 million**; a later £6 million sale gives the investor £1.8 million.

## Factors in the choice

Amount and time-scale; cost (interest, fees, dividends); risk and gearing; control (shares and venture capital dilute it); size, legal form and age of business; availability and speed; purpose (match life of finance to life of use); technology (online platforms); economic conditions (interest rates, investor confidence).

## Exam traps

- Share capital is not repaid like a loan, but it does cost control and dividends.
- Short-term needs need short-term finance.
- Venture capital has a cost: ownership and future profit.
- Retained profit is not free: the opportunity cost matters.
- Name the type of crowd funding (reward, equity, donation or loan).

## Self-test

1. Give two examples of internal sources of finance.
2. Give one advantage and one disadvantage of an overdraft.
3. Explain what debt factoring is.
4. A factor advances 80% of £50,000 of invoices. How much is paid at once?
5. The factor charges a 3% fee on the invoice value. Calculate the fee and the balance paid later.
6. Give two disadvantages of a bank loan.
7. A £60,000 loan has 7% interest. Calculate annual interest.
8. Give two advantages of issuing shares compared with taking a loan.
9. Why do venture capitalists often want a share in the business?
10. Name one type of crowd funding and say what backers receive.
11. Which source suits a three-month cash flow gap, and why?
12. Which source suits buying machinery that will last ten years, and why?

### Answers

1. Any two: retained profit; owner's savings; sale of assets; collecting receivables faster.
2. Advantage (any one): flexible; interest only on the amount used. Disadvantage (any one): high interest; can be withdrawn at short notice.
3. Selling invoices to a specialist firm for immediate cash, with the rest paid when customers pay, less a fee.
4. 80% × £50,000 = **£40,000**.
5. Fee = 3% × £50,000 = **£1,500**; balance = £50,000 − £40,000 − £1,500 = **£8,500**.
6. Any two: interest and repayments must be paid even if profit falls; security may be required; high gearing increases risk; variable rate may rise.
7. £60,000 × 0.07 = **£4,200**.
8. Any two: no repayment; no interest; dividends are optional; large sums possible.
9. They are taking a high risk and want a reward if the business grows, plus a say in how it is run.
10. Reward-based (backers receive the product or a perk) or equity-based (backers receive shares).
11. An overdraft (or factoring), because it is flexible, quick and short-term, matching the length of the need.
12. A bank loan or leasing (or retained profit), because the repayment period can match the life of the machine.
