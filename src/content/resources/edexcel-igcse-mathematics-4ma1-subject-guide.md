Pearson Edexcel International GCSE Mathematics A (4MA1) is a broad course
in number, algebra, geometry and statistics, usually studied over two years.
It is a tiered qualification. Students are entered for either
the Foundation tier (Papers 1F and 2F, targeted at grades 5 to 1) or the
Higher tier (Papers 1H and 2H, targeted at grades 9 to 4, with grade 3
allowed). On either tier, each paper lasts 120 minutes, carries 100 marks and
is worth 50%. The specification was first assessed in 2018.

## How the course is organised

The content is arranged in six areas. Because the course is tiered, check
with your teacher which content applies to your entry.

- **1 Numbers and the number system** covers integers, fractions, decimals,
  powers and roots, set language and notation, percentages, ratio and
  proportion, degree of accuracy, standard form, applying number and the use
  of electronic calculators.
- **2 Equations, formulae and identities** covers the use of symbols,
  algebraic manipulation, expressions and formulae, linear equations,
  proportion, simultaneous linear equations, quadratic equations and
  inequalities.
- **3 Sequences, functions and graphs** covers sequences, function notation,
  graphs and calculus.
- **4 Geometry and trigonometry** covers angles, lines and triangles,
  polygons, symmetry, measures, construction, circle properties, geometrical
  reasoning, and trigonometry and Pythagoras' theorem.
- **5 Vectors and transformation geometry** covers vectors and
  transformations.
- **6 Statistics and probability** covers graphical representation of data,
  statistical measures and probability.

Number and algebra (areas 1 to 3) make up a large share of the listed
subtopics, and they supply the techniques used in the geometry and
statistics areas.

## How it's assessed

4MA1 is linear, with two papers on each tier. All four papers are written
exams, externally assessed, and a scientific calculator is required for
every paper.

| Tier | Paper | Duration | Marks | Weighting | Grades targeted |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Foundation | 4MA1/1F | 120 minutes (2h) | 100 | 50% | 5 to 1 |
| Foundation | 4MA1/2F | 120 minutes (2h) | 100 | 50% | 5 to 1 |
| Higher | 4MA1/1H | 120 minutes (2h) | 100 | 50% | 9 to 4 (grade 3 allowed) |
| Higher | 4MA1/2H | 120 minutes (2h) | 100 | 50% | 9 to 4 (grade 3 allowed) |

The tier decision is significant. Foundation papers are targeted at grades 5
to 1, while Higher papers are targeted at grades 9 to 4, with grade 3 allowed.
Your teacher will advise on the right entry based on your work during
the course.

Pearson also lists a separate modular version of International GCSE
Mathematics (first teaching 2024). It is not described in this
specification and is not covered here.

## How the parts fit together

Mathematics is cumulative, and 4MA1's content areas are closely
interlinked. Fractions, decimals, percentages and ratio from area 1 appear in
almost every other area, from scale drawings to probability. Algebraic
manipulation and equation solving from area 2 are the tools for finding
unknown lengths and angles in geometry, for working with sequences and
graphs in area 3 and for handling vectors in area 5. Trigonometry and
Pythagoras' theorem connect geometry with algebra, and calculus in area 3
builds on graph work and algebraic skill.

Because both papers on a tier have the same length and weight and both
require a calculator, the two papers are best treated as a pair rather than
as separate courses. Skills that matter across the whole qualification
include setting out working clearly, using a calculator efficiently and
accurately, rounding to an appropriate degree of accuracy, and checking that
an answer is sensible.

## Planning your study

A typical two-year plan starts with number and basic algebra, since these
underpin everything else, then interleaves geometry and statistics with
further algebra. Revisit topics in short cycles rather than covering each
once: for example, return to percentages when studying probability, or to
quadratic equations when studying graphs.

Higher-tier students should expect the more demanding parts of algebra and
functions to take extra time. Foundation-tier students benefit from secure fluency in number and core algebra, which
supports many of the marks on both papers.

Because a calculator is required for every paper, use the same scientific
calculator in lessons, homework and practice that you will use in the exam.
Learn its functions for powers, roots, standard form, fractions and
trigonometry early, but keep showing written working, since a calculator
answer on its own explains nothing to the examiner if it is wrong.

Keep a short list of techniques you find difficult and revisit them weekly.
Start timed practice papers once the content for your tier has been taught,
and use the exam-preparation page for advice on approaching the papers
themselves.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is Pearson Edexcel IGCSE Mathematics 4MA1 tiered?**
Yes. There is a Foundation tier (Papers 1F and 2F, targeting grades 5 to 1) and a Higher tier (Papers 1H and 2H, targeting grades 9 to 4, with grade 3 allowed).

**How long are the 4MA1 papers?**
Every paper lasts 120 minutes and carries 100 marks, and each is worth 50% of the qualification on its tier.

**Is there a non-calculator paper in 4MA1?**
No. A scientific calculator is required for all four papers.

**What are the main content areas?**
The six areas are numbers and the number system; equations, formulae and identities; sequences, functions and graphs; geometry and trigonometry; vectors and transformation geometry; and statistics and probability.

**Where does calculus appear in 4MA1?**
Calculus is listed within area 3, Sequences, functions and graphs. Check with your teacher whether it is part of the content for your tier.
