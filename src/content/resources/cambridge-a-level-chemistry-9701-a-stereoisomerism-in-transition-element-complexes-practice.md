## Section A

**1.** State the coordination number and shape of [Ni(en)₃]²⁺. **[2]**

**2.** State the two types of stereoisomerism that can be shown by transition element complexes. **[1]**

**3.** The complex [ZnCl₂(NH₃)₂] is tetrahedral. Explain why it has no geometrical isomers. **[2]**

**4.** State what is meant by the term *chiral*, as applied to a complex ion. **[1]**

**5.** [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] is square planar. Give the bond angle Cl–Pt–Cl in each of its geometrical isomers. **[2]**

---

## Section B

**6.** [Co(NH₃)₄(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is octahedral.

**(a)** Draw the two geometrical isomers of this complex and label them. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why neither isomer shows optical isomerism. **[1]**

**(c)** Deduce which isomer has an overall dipole. Explain your answer. **[2]**

**7.** The complex [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has three stereoisomers.

**(a)** Describe, with reference to the positions of the water ligands, the two geometrical isomers. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why only one of the geometrical isomers exists as a pair of optical isomers. **[2]**

**(c)** Draw the pair of optical isomers. **[2]**

**8.** Square planar platinum(II) complexes are often used to test understanding of geometrical isomerism.

**(a)** Explain why [Pt(NH₃)₃Cl]⁺ has no geometrical isomers. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce how many geometrical isomers [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] has, and name the isomer used as an anti-cancer drug. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest, with a reason, which isomer of [Pt(NH₃)₂Cl₂] has the higher solubility in water. **[2]**

**9.** The ethanedioate ion, ox, is a bidentate ligand. It forms the complex [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻.

**(a)** Explain why [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻ is octahedral. **[1]**

**(b)** Deduce whether [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻ shows geometrical isomerism, optical isomerism, both or neither. Explain your answer. **[3]**

**(c)** Deduce whether [Fe(ox)₃]³⁻ is polar overall. **[1]**

**10.** An octahedral complex has the formula [Co(en)₂Cl₂]⁺.

**(a)** Deduce the total number of stereoisomers of this complex. **[1]**

**(b)** Identify which of these stereoisomers are polar. **[2]**

**11.** A student writes: "All octahedral complexes containing two different ligands show cis/trans isomerism." Using [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ as an example, explain why the student is wrong. **[2]**

**12.** Compare the stereoisomerism shown by [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ with that shown by [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺. Your answer should state the number and type of stereoisomers for each and the reason for any optical activity. **[4]**

## Answers

**1.** Coordination number 6 (three bidentate ligands, six N donor atoms) [1]; octahedral [1].

**2.** Geometrical (cis/trans) isomerism and optical isomerism [1].

**3.** In a tetrahedral complex all four positions are adjacent to one another, at about 109.5° [1], so every arrangement of two Cl and two NH₃ ligands is identical; there is no distinct cis or trans form [1].

**4.** It is not superimposable on its mirror image (it has no plane of symmetry) [1].

**5.** cis isomer: 90° [1]; trans isomer: 180° [1].

**6. (a)** Octahedral drawings: cis isomer with the two H₂O ligands adjacent (at 90°) [1]; trans isomer with the two H₂O ligands opposite (at 180°), both labelled [1].

**(b)** Each isomer has a plane of symmetry, so each is superimposable on its mirror image [1].

**(c)** The cis isomer [1]: the Co–O bond dipoles are at 90° and are not cancelled by identical opposite bonds, whereas in the trans isomer the two Co–O bonds are opposite and cancel (as do the Co–N bonds) [1].

**7. (a)** trans: the two water ligands are opposite each other, at 180° [1]; cis: the two water ligands are adjacent, at 90° [1].

**(b)** The trans isomer has a plane of symmetry (containing Ni and both en ligands), so it is not chiral [1]; the cis isomer has no plane of symmetry, so it is not superimposable on its mirror image [1].

**(c)** Two octahedral structures of the cis isomer drawn as mirror images either side of a mirror line [1], with each en spanning two adjacent positions and the two H₂O ligands adjacent [1].

**8. (a)** Only one ligand is different from the rest; every position of the single Cl relative to three NH₃ ligands is equivalent, so only one arrangement exists [1].

**(b)** Two (cis and trans) [1]; the cis isomer (cisplatin) [1].

**(c)** The cis isomer [1]: it is polar because its bond dipoles do not cancel, so it interacts more strongly with polar water molecules; the trans isomer is non-polar [1].

**9. (a)** Each ox ligand forms two dative bonds, so three ligands give a coordination number of 6, which is octahedral [1].

**(b)** Optical isomerism only [1]. All three ligands are identical and each must span two adjacent positions, so there is only one geometrical arrangement and no cis/trans isomers [1]. The three ligands form a propeller-like arrangement with no plane of symmetry, so the mirror images are non-superimposable [1].

**(c)** Non-polar: the three identical ligands are arranged symmetrically, so the bond dipoles cancel [1].

**10. (a)** Three: trans, and a pair of optical isomers of cis [1].

**(b)** Both optical isomers of the cis form are polar [1]; the trans isomer is non-polar because the two Co–Cl bond dipoles are opposite and cancel [1].

**11.** In [Co(NH₃)₅Cl]²⁺ there is only one Cl ligand [1]; all six octahedral positions are equivalent with respect to it, so only one arrangement exists and there are no cis/trans isomers. Geometrical isomerism needs at least two of the same ligand that can be adjacent or opposite [1].

**12.** [Ni(en)₃]²⁺: one pair of optical isomers and no geometrical isomers, two stereoisomers in total [1]. [Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺: cis and trans geometrical isomers, with the cis form existing as a pair of optical isomers, three stereoisomers in total [1]. Optical activity arises in [Ni(en)₃]²⁺ and cis-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ because the bidentate ligands create an arrangement with no plane of symmetry, so the mirror images are non-superimposable [1]. trans-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ has a plane of symmetry and is not optically active [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Drawing cis and trans isomers for a tetrahedral complex.
- Drawing an en ligand bridging two opposite positions.
- Stating that trans-[Ni(en)₂(H₂O)₂]²⁺ is chiral, or giving the total as two stereoisomers.
- Drawing "mirror images" that are only rotations of the same structure.
- Stating polar or non-polar without explaining whether the bond dipoles cancel.
