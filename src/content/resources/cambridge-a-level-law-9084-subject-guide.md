Cambridge International AS & A Level Law (9084) is a course in the law of England and Wales, covering how the legal system works and three major areas of substantive law: crime, contract and tort. It is assessed by four externally marked written papers of 1 hour 30 minutes each, every one worth 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1, English Legal System (75 marks), Paper 2, Criminal Law (60 marks), Paper 3, Law of Contract (75 marks) and Paper 4, Law of Tort (75 marks). Papers 1 and 2 make up the AS Level, and Papers 3 and 4 complete the A Level. This page explains how the four areas are organised, how the papers relate, and how to plan the course.

## How the course is organised

The content is divided into four topics, two at each stage.

**AS Level**

- **English legal system** covers the principles and sources of English law, the machinery of justice, and legal personnel. It is the framework for the rest of the course: where law comes from, how cases are decided and who is involved.
- **Criminal law** covers the elements of a crime, offences against property, and sentencing in England and Wales. It is the first area in which you apply legal rules to factual situations.

**A Level**

- **Law of contract** covers the formation of a valid contract, the contents of a contract, discharge of a contract, and remedies for breach of a contract. It follows the life of an agreement from start to finish.
- **Law of tort** covers the tort of negligence, torts affecting land, torts affecting the person, and general defences and remedies. It deals with civil wrongs and how the law compensates those who suffer them.

## How it's assessed

The assessment is staged. All four papers are externally assessed written examinations, and each paper mixes compulsory and choice questions across two sections, combining structured or scenario-based questions with essays.

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of AS Level | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | English Legal System | 1 hour 30 minutes | 75 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Criminal Law | 1 hour 30 minutes | 60 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Law of Contract | 1 hour 30 minutes | 75 | n/a | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Law of Tort | 1 hour 30 minutes | 75 | n/a | 25% |

Notice that Paper 2 is marked out of 60 while the others are out of 75, yet all four carry the same weighting. The Cambridge International AS Level in Law (Papers 1 and 2) can be a standalone qualification, or the first half of the A Level, completed by adding Papers 3 and 4 in a later series. The syllabus covers the 2026-2028 examination window and was first examined in 2026.

## How the parts fit together

The English legal system is not simply the first topic; it is the toolkit for the other three. Knowing how statutes are made and interpreted, how judicial precedent works and how the courts are arranged lets you explain why a rule exists and how a judge might apply it. Those ideas reappear whenever you cite a case or discuss whether the law in a particular area is satisfactory.

Criminal law, contract and tort then share a common method. In each, you identify the relevant legal rules, support them with authority such as cases or statutes, apply them to the facts in front of you and reach a reasoned conclusion. Scenario questions test that method directly, while essay questions ask you to evaluate the law: whether it is clear, fair and consistent, and how it might be improved.

There are also useful links between the substantive areas. Criminal law and tort both deal with wrongs and with ideas such as fault and causation, though one leads to punishment and the other to compensation. Contract and tort both concern civil liability and remedies, and the general defences and remedies in tort sit naturally alongside remedies for breach of contract. Drawing these comparisons deepens understanding and gives essays more to say.

## Planning your study

A sensible order follows the syllabus: the English legal system first, because it supplies the vocabulary and concepts for everything else, then criminal law, then contract and tort.

In general terms, for a two-stage course:

- **AS stage:** begin with the principles and sources of English law, then the machinery of justice and legal personnel. Move to criminal law, learning the elements of a crime before offences against property, and finish with sentencing.
- **A Level stage:** study the law of contract from formation through contents and discharge to remedies, then the law of tort from negligence through torts affecting land and the person to general defences and remedies.

Throughout, keep a case list for each topic with the principle each case establishes, and practise both kinds of question: applying law to a scenario and evaluating an area of law in an essay. Start exam-style questions as each topic is completed and move to full past papers in the final months. Advice on technique for each paper is on the dedicated exam preparation page.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level Law 9084?**
There are four, each lasting 1 hour 30 minutes: Paper 1 English Legal System, Paper 2 Criminal Law, Paper 3 Law of Contract and Paper 4 Law of Tort.

**Which papers make up AS Level Law?**
Papers 1 and 2. Each is worth 50% of the AS Level. AS Law can be a standalone qualification or the first half of the A Level.

**Why is Paper 2 out of 60 marks when the others are out of 75?**
That is how the syllabus sets the papers. Despite the difference in raw marks, each paper is worth 25% of the full A Level.

**What types of questions appear on the 9084 papers?**
Each paper has two sections that mix compulsory and choice questions, combining structured or scenario-based questions with essays.

**Is Cambridge A Level Law about English law?**
Yes. The topics are the English legal system, criminal law (including sentencing in England and Wales), the law of contract and the law of tort.
