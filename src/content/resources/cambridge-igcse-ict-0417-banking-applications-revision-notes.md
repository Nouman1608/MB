These notes condense subtopic 6.6 of Cambridge IGCSE ICT 0417, banking applications. For step-by-step explanations, read the [banking applications study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-banking-applications/); then test yourself with the [banking applications practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-ict-0417-banking-applications-practice/).

## Syllabus coverage

| Area | Named content |
|---|---|
| ATMs | Withdrawing cash, depositing cash or cheques, checking account balance, mini statements, bill paying, money transfers |
| Other banking | Electronic Funds Transfer (EFT), credit/debit card transactions, cheques, internet banking |

For each: characteristics, uses, advantages and disadvantages.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| ATM | Automatic Teller Machine: a terminal linked to the bank's computer for self-service banking |
| PIN | Personal identification number, typed to prove the card holder's identity |
| Debit (verb) | Take money out of an account |
| Credit (verb) | Put money into an account |
| EFT | Electronic Funds Transfer: moving money between accounts electronically |
| Clearing | The process by which a cheque is checked and the money moved between banks |
| Skimming | Copying card details with a device fitted to a card slot |
| Shoulder surfing | Watching someone type their PIN or password |
| Phishing | Fake emails or websites that trick users into revealing login details |

## ATM withdrawal in order

1. Insert card; chip read.
2. Enter PIN; checked (card kept after repeated wrong attempts).
3. Choose service and amount.
4. Request sent to bank's computer: card not stolen? enough funds? within daily limit?
5. Authorised; account debited.
6. Card returned, then cash dispensed.
7. Receipt printed if requested.

**Who checks the funds?** The **bank's computer**, not the ATM.

## ATM: other services

- **Deposit**: notes counted or cheque scanned; account credited (cheque funds available after clearing).
- **Balance**: shown on screen.
- **Mini statement**: recent transactions printed.
- **Bill paying**: money sent to a registered company.
- **Transfers**: between the customer's own accounts or to another person.

| ATM advantages | ATM disadvantages |
|---|---|
| Available 24 hours | Fees at some machines |
| Many locations | Daily cash limit |
| Faster than queuing in a branch | Skimming and shoulder surfing |
| Cash available abroad | May be out of order or out of cash |
| | Fewer services; no staff help |

## EFT

- Money moves electronically; no cash or paper.
- Uses: wages, bill payments, transfers to other people.
- Wages: employer's bank debits the employer's account and credits each employee's account.
- **Plus:** fast, secure, no cash to handle, cheaper than cheques, record kept.
- **Minus:** wrong account number sends money to the wrong person; depends on systems working; fraud risk.

## Debit and credit cards

| Feature | Debit | Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Money from | Own account | Borrowed from card company |
| Paid | Immediately | Later, monthly |
| Interest | No | Yes, if not paid in full |
| Limit | Account balance or overdraft | Credit limit |

- **Plus:** no cash needed; card can be stopped if lost; works online and abroad.
- **Minus:** card fraud, especially online; credit card debt; some small shops do not accept cards.

## Cheques

- Paper instruction, signed by the account holder, to pay a named person.
- Must be cleared before money moves; some banks allow paying in by photographing the cheque in an app.
- **Plus:** for people without internet banking; written record; safer than posting cash.
- **Minus:** slow; can be lost or stolen; can bounce; costly to process; fewer places accept them.

## Internet banking

- Log in with user ID, password or PIN, often plus a one-time code or fingerprint.
- Features: balances, statements, transfers, bill payments, regular payments, loan applications, freezing a lost card.
- **Plus:** any time, anywhere; no travel; helps people with mobility problems; easy to track spending.
- **Minus:** phishing and hacking; needs internet and confidence with technology; easy to mistype account details; no face-to-face advice; branch closures.

## Exam traps

- "The ATM checks the balance". The bank's computer does.
- Mixing up debit (take out) and credit (put in).
- Thinking EFT only means card payments in shops.
- Writing "fraud" without saying how: skimming, phishing, stolen card details.
- Leaving the PIN or the card return out of an ATM description.

## Self-test

1. Name four services available at an ATM other than withdrawing cash.
2. Name three checks made by the bank's computer before cash is dispensed.
3. Why is the card returned before the cash is dispensed?
4. What is card skimming?
5. Define EFT and give one use.
6. Give one difference between a debit card and a credit card.
7. Give two disadvantages of cheques.
8. Give two advantages of internet banking.
9. What is phishing?
10. Give one extra security check used when logging in to internet banking.
11. Give one advantage of an ATM over a bank branch.
12. Why might an elderly customer prefer a branch to internet banking?

### Answers

1. Any four: deposit cash or cheques; check balance; mini statement; pay bills; transfer money.
2. Card not reported lost or stolen; enough funds in the account; daily limit not exceeded.
3. So the customer is less likely to walk away and leave the card behind.
4. Copying card details using a device fitted to the card slot.
5. Moving money between bank accounts electronically; used for paying wages (also bills, transfers).
6. Debit takes money from the customer's own account straight away; credit borrows the money to be paid back later.
7. Any two: slow to clear; can be lost or stolen; can bounce; fewer places accept them.
8. Any two: available any time; no travel; easy to track spending; instant transactions.
9. Fake emails or websites designed to trick users into giving their login details.
10. A one-time code sent to a phone, or a fingerprint check on the app.
11. Available 24 hours a day (or in many locations, or no queue).
12. They may not have internet access or confidence online, and may want face-to-face advice.
