## Questions

### Section A: short questions

**1.** In the lac operon, where does the repressor protein bind when lactose is absent?

A: The promoter

B: The operator

C: lacZ

D: lacI **[1]**

**2.** Describe the difference between an inducible enzyme and a repressible enzyme. **[2]**

**3.** Which of these is a regulatory gene?

A: lacA

B: lacI

C: lacY

D: lacZ **[1]**

**4.** State what is meant by a transcription factor. **[2]**

**5.** Suggest why it is an advantage to *E. coli* to make β-galactosidase only when lactose is present. **[2]**

**6.** A plant has a mutation so that it makes no functional DELLA proteins. What is the most likely result?

A: Genes normally activated by gibberellin are never transcribed.

B: Genes normally activated by gibberellin are transcribed even without gibberellin.

C: The plant cannot make gibberellin.

D: Gibberellin binds directly to DNA instead. **[1]**

### Section B: longer questions

**7.**

**(a)** Explain why the structural genes of the lac operon are not transcribed when lactose is absent. **[3]**

**(b)** Explain how the presence of lactose leads to the production of β-galactosidase. **[3]**

**8.** Predict whether β-galactosidase is made (i) without lactose and (ii) with lactose in each mutant. Give a reason for each.

**(a)** The lacI gene codes for a repressor that cannot bind to the operator. **[1]**

**(b)** The repressor cannot bind lactose. **[1]**

**(c)** The promoter is changed so that RNA polymerase cannot bind. **[1]**

**(d)** The operator is deleted. **[1]**

**9.** *E. coli* was grown in a medium with no lactose or glucose. At 20 minutes, a small amount of lactose was added. The concentration of lacZ mRNA and the activity of β-galactosidase were measured.

| Time / min | 0 | 20 | 25 | 30 | 40 | 60 | 90 | 120 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| lacZ mRNA / arbitrary units | 0 | 0 | 8 | 20 | 22 | 21 | 5 | 1 |
| Enzyme activity / arbitrary units | 0 | 0 | 1 | 6 | 25 | 40 | 38 | 20 |

**(a)** Describe the changes in lacZ mRNA after lactose was added. **[2]**

**(b)** Explain why enzyme activity rises later than the mRNA. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest an explanation for the fall in mRNA after 60 minutes. **[2]**

**10.** Explain how gibberellin activates genes in plant cells. **[4]**

**11.**

**(a)** A dwarf barley mutant has DELLA proteins that cannot be broken down. Explain why spraying it with gibberellin does not make it grow taller. **[3]**

**(b)** A dwarf pea plant with genotype lele grows tall when sprayed with gibberellin. Explain why this mutant responds when the DELLA mutant does not. **[2]**

**12.**

**(a)** Suggest how a liver cell and a muscle cell, which contain the same genes, can make different proteins. **[2]**

**(b)** Compare the control of the lac operon with the control of eukaryotic genes by transcription factors. **[3]**

## Answers

**1.** B [1]

**2.** An inducible enzyme is normally not made and is switched on (synthesised) when its substrate is present [1]. A repressible enzyme is normally made and is switched off when its end product is present [1].

**3.** B [1]

**4.** A protein that binds to DNA (near a gene's promoter) [1] and increases or decreases the rate of transcription of that gene [1].

**5.** It does not waste energy (ATP) and amino acids making an enzyme it cannot use [1]. Resources can be used for proteins needed in current conditions, so the cell can grow faster [1].

**6.** B [1]

**7.**

**(a)** The regulatory gene lacI is transcribed and translated to make the repressor protein [1]. The repressor binds to the operator [1]. This prevents RNA polymerase from binding to the promoter (or from transcribing the genes), so no mRNA is made [1].

**(b)** Lactose binds to the repressor, changing its shape [1]. The repressor can no longer bind to the operator and leaves the DNA [1]. RNA polymerase binds to the promoter and transcribes the structural genes; the mRNA is translated, producing β-galactosidase [1].

**8.**

**(a)** Made both without and with lactose, because nothing blocks RNA polymerase [1].

**(b)** Not made in either condition, because lactose cannot remove the repressor from the operator [1].

**(c)** Not made in either condition, because transcription cannot start [1].

**(d)** Made both without and with lactose, because the repressor has nowhere to bind [1].

**9.**

**(a)** No mRNA until lactose is added; it rises from 0 at 20 minutes to a peak of about 22 units at 40 minutes [1]. It stays level until 60 minutes, then falls to 1 unit by 120 minutes [1].

**(b)** The enzyme can only be made after the mRNA has been transcribed [1]. Translation at ribosomes, and folding of the polypeptides into active enzyme, take further time [1].

**(c)** The small amount of lactose has been hydrolysed (used up) by the enzyme [1]. The repressor is no longer bound to lactose, so it binds to the operator again, transcription stops and existing mRNA is broken down [1].

**10.** In the absence of gibberellin, DELLA proteins bind to and inhibit a transcription factor that would promote transcription [1]. Gibberellin binds to a receptor in the cell [1]. This causes the DELLA proteins to be broken down [1]. The transcription factor is released, binds to DNA and promotes transcription of target genes, such as the amylase gene [1].

**11.**

**(a)** Gibberellin can bind to its receptor, but the DELLA proteins are not broken down [1]. They continue to inhibit the transcription factor [1]. So growth genes are not transcribed and stem elongation does not occur [1].

**(b)** The lele plant cannot make enough active gibberellin because its enzyme is non-functional, but its DELLA breakdown pathway is normal [1]. Applied gibberellin binds to the receptor, DELLA proteins are broken down and growth genes are transcribed [1].

**12.**

**(a)** Each cell type has different transcription factors [1]. These bind to the promoters of different genes, so different genes are transcribed and different proteins made [1].

**(b)** Similarity: both control gene expression at transcription, using proteins that bind to DNA [1]. In the lac operon, one repressor controls several structural genes transcribed together as one mRNA [1]. In eukaryotes, each gene is usually controlled separately, often by several transcription factors that may increase or decrease transcription [1].

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing that the repressor binds the promoter.
- Writing that lactose binds to the operator or DNA.
- Describing only one condition of the lac operon.
- Calling DELLA proteins transcription factors.
- Saying gibberellin acts directly on DNA.
- In "compare", giving only differences.
