Condensed notes for sub-topic **3.3 Bank reconciliation**. A full overdrawn example is worked in the [3.3 study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-bank-reconciliation/); test yourself with the [3.3 practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-accounting-0452-bank-reconciliation-practice/).

## What 3.3 asks for

- The use and purpose of a bank statement.
- Updating the cash book for bank charges, bank interest paid and received, correction of errors, credit transfers, direct debits and standing orders.
- The purpose and preparation of a bank reconciliation statement, including bank errors, uncredited deposits and unpresented cheques.
- The impact of digital business transactions on bank reconciliation.

## Two records, two viewpoints

| | Cash book (bank column) | Bank statement |
|---|---|---|
| Kept by | The business | The bank |
| Money paid in | Debit | Credit |
| Money paid out | Credit | Debit |
| Money in the account | Debit balance | Credit balance |
| Overdraft | Credit balance | Debit balance (often marked OD) |

## Sort every difference

**Into the cash book (business did not know or made a mistake):**

| Item | Cash book side |
|---|---|
| Bank charges | Credit |
| Interest paid (on an overdraft or loan) | Credit |
| Direct debit | Credit |
| Standing order paid | Credit |
| Interest received | Debit |
| Credit transfer received | Debit |
| Error: payment recorded too high | Debit the excess |
| Error: payment recorded too low | Credit the shortfall |
| Error: receipt recorded too high | Credit the excess |
| Error: receipt recorded too low | Debit the shortfall |

**Into the reconciliation statement (cash book already right):**

- **Unpresented cheques:** written and recorded, not yet paid by the bank.
- **Uncredited deposits:** paid in and recorded, not yet on the statement.
- **Bank errors:** the bank's mistake, to be reported to the bank.

## Layout, starting from the updated cash book

| Line | Account in credit | Account overdrawn |
|---|---|---|
| Updated cash book balance | 3,500 | (900) |
| Add unpresented cheques | + 700 | + 700 |
| Less uncredited deposits | - 1,100 | - 1,100 |
| Bank error that reduced the bank balance | Less | Less |
| Bank error that increased the bank balance | Add | Add |
| Balance as per bank statement | 3,100 | (1,300) |

Working for the overdrawn column: -900 + 700 - 1,100 = -1,300, an overdraft of 1,300 on the statement.

Starting from the bank statement instead, reverse every sign: less unpresented cheques, add uncredited deposits.

## Purpose of a bank reconciliation

- Explains the difference between the two balances.
- Finds errors and omissions in the cash book and errors by the bank.
- Confirms the correct bank figure for the statement of financial position (the updated cash book balance).
- Highlights old unpresented cheques, missing deposits and unauthorised payments, which may indicate fraud.

## Digital business transactions

- Online statements allow frequent, even daily, reconciliation.
- Card receipts, transfers, direct debits and standing orders appear on the statement first, so more cash book updating is needed.
- Fewer cheques mean fewer unpresented cheques and uncredited deposits.
- Software can import bank data and propose matches, but a person must still check them.
- Timing differences remain, for example card takings credited a day or more after the sale.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Bank statement | The bank's record of the business's account |
| Bank reconciliation statement | A statement explaining the difference between the cash book and bank statement balances |
| Unpresented cheque | A cheque recorded in the cash book but not yet paid by the bank |
| Uncredited deposit | Money paid in and recorded but not yet shown on the statement |
| Credit transfer | Money paid directly into the account by another person |
| Direct debit | A payment the payee collects from the account, amount may vary |
| Standing order | A fixed regular payment the account holder instructs the bank to make |
| Bank charges | Fees the bank deducts for running the account |

## Exam traps

- Updating the cash book with unpresented cheques or uncredited deposits.
- Using the bank statement balance in the statement of financial position.
- Reversing debit and credit when reading the statement.
- Dropping the brackets on an overdraft halfway through.
- Forgetting interest received is a **debit** in the cash book.
- Treating a bank error as a cash book correction.

## Self-test

**1.** State one purpose of a bank statement.

**2.** A deposit appears as a credit on a bank statement. Why?

**3.** Which of these go in the cash book: bank charges, unpresented cheques, a standing order?

**4.** Interest received of 35 appears on the statement only. What is the cash book entry?

**5.** A cheque payment of 760 was entered in the cash book as 670. What correction is needed?

**6.** Updated cash book balance 2,450 in credit. Unpresented cheques 830; uncredited deposit 600. What is the bank statement balance?

**7.** Updated cash book shows an overdraft of 500. Unpresented cheques 300; uncredited deposit 450. What does the bank statement show?

**8.** Define an uncredited deposit.

**9.** Which balance appears in the statement of financial position?

**10.** The bank charged another customer's cheque of 90 to the business's account. How is this shown in a reconciliation that starts with the cash book balance?

**11.** Give one way digital transactions have changed bank reconciliation.

**12.** Give one reason a long-unpresented cheque should be investigated.

## Self-test answers

**1.** Any one: shows items not yet known to the business; confirms recorded receipts and payments; gives the balance per the bank.

**2.** From the bank's side, money paid in is owed by the bank to the business.

**3.** Bank charges and the standing order.

**4.** Debit the bank column 35 (credit interest received).

**5.** Credit the bank column 90 (the payment was understated).

**6.** 2,450 + 830 - 600 = 2,680 in credit.

**7.** -500 + 300 - 450 = -650, an overdraft of 650.

**8.** Money paid into the bank and recorded in the cash book that has not yet appeared on the bank statement.

**9.** The updated cash book balance.

**10.** Deducted (less), because it has reduced the bank balance only.

**11.** Any one: reconciliation can be done more often; more items appear on the statement first; fewer unpresented cheques; software suggests matches.

**12.** The payee may not have received it, it may have been lost or stolen, or the debt may still be owed.
