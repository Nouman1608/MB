## Condensed recall

**Cycles as systems**
- Storages (atmosphere, ocean, biomass, soil, fossil fuels, rocks) linked by flows (processes).
- Residence time = storage ÷ flow: leaf ~ a year; deep ocean ~ centuries; limestone ~ millions of
  years.

**Fast and slow carbon**
- Fast: photosynthesis, respiration, feeding, decomposition, air–sea exchange.
- Slow: fossil fuel and limestone formation, weathering, volcanic outgassing, burial.
- Human impact moves slow-cycle carbon into the fast cycle within decades.

**Ocean uptake**
- Solubility pump: CO₂ dissolves (more in cold water); cold dense water sinks.
- Biological pump: phytoplankton fix carbon; dead matter and faecal pellets sink.
- Carbonate formation: shells and skeletons → sediment → limestone.
- Acidification: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻; lower pH, less carbonate for shells.
- Warmer water dissolves less CO₂, weakening the solubility pump.

**Peat and permafrost**
- Peat: waterlogged, low oxygen, slow decomposition, carbon accumulates; drainage → source.
- Permafrost: frozen carbon; thaw → CO₂ and methane → positive feedback.

**Sink or source**
- Net ecosystem uptake = NPP − decomposer respiration.
- Positive → sink; negative → source.
- Carbon to CO₂: multiply by 44/12 (≈ 3.67).

**Nitrogen**
- Fixation (N₂ → NH₄⁺): *Rhizobium* in legumes, free-living bacteria, lightning, Haber process.
- Nitrification (NH₄⁺ → NO₂⁻ → NO₃⁻): needs oxygen.
- Denitrification (NO₃⁻ → N₂, some N₂O): waterlogged, anaerobic soil.
- Leaching: nitrate washed out of bare, recently fertilised soil after heavy rain.
- N₂O is a greenhouse gas and contributes to ozone loss.

**Management**
- Carbon: renewables, efficiency, carbon pricing, forest protection, reforestation, peat rewetting,
  carbon capture and storage.
- Nitrogen: precision and split fertiliser application, cover crops, buffer strips, legumes in
  rotation.
- Planetary boundaries: nitrogen and phosphorus grouped as "biogeochemical flows".

## Method in steps: budget questions

```
1. List inputs and outputs separately; check the units match.
2. Add each side.
3. Net change = inputs − outputs (sign shows gain or loss).
4. Sink if net uptake is positive; source if negative.
5. Convert if asked: per m² × 10,000 = per ha; C × 44/12 = CO₂.
6. Express a loss as a percentage of inputs if asked.
```

Mini example (invented): a grassland has NPP of 600 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹ and decomposer respiration of
680 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹. Net = 600 − 680 = −80 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, so it is a **source**, losing 0.8 t C per
hectare each year.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Biogeochemical cycle | Movement of an element between living and non-living storages |
| Carbon sink | Storage taking up more carbon than it releases |
| Carbon source | Storage releasing more carbon than it takes up |
| Sequestration | Long-term capture and storage of carbon |
| Solubility pump | Ocean uptake of CO₂ by dissolving and sinking cold water |
| Biological pump | Transfer of fixed carbon to the deep ocean by sinking organic matter |
| Ocean acidification | Fall in ocean pH from dissolved CO₂ |
| Peat | Partly decomposed plant matter accumulating in waterlogged soils |
| Permafrost | Ground that stays frozen for at least two consecutive years |
| Nitrogen fixation | Conversion of N₂ gas into ammonium |
| Nitrification | Conversion of ammonium to nitrite and nitrate by bacteria |
| Denitrification | Conversion of nitrate back to nitrogen gas |
| Leaching | Removal of soluble nutrients from soil by water |
| Buffer strip | Vegetated strip that intercepts run-off before it reaches water |

## Exam traps

- **Name process, storage and direction together.** "Carbon cycle: photosynthesis" is too thin.
- **Nitrification needs oxygen; denitrification does not.**
- **Sink needs a comparison.** Uptake alone does not make a sink.
- **C is not CO₂.** Convert with 44/12 when the question asks for CO₂.
- **Fossil fuels are slow-cycle storages.** Burning them is the transfer to the fast cycle.
- **Acidification is not "the ocean becoming acidic".** It is a fall in pH; seawater remains alkaline.

## Self-test

1. Give two processes in the slow carbon cycle.
2. Why does cold water take up more CO₂?
3. Describe the biological pump.
4. Write the equation showing how dissolved CO₂ forms carbonic acid.
5. Why does draining a peatland turn it into a carbon source?
6. A forest has NPP 900 and decomposer respiration 700 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹. Sink or source?
7. Convert 3 t C to t CO₂.
8. Which nitrogen process needs oxygen?
9. Under what conditions is denitrification greatest?
10. Why is N₂O an environmental concern?
11. Name two ways to reduce nitrate leaching.
12. Give one limitation of carbon capture and storage.

### Answers

1. Any two: fossil fuel formation; limestone formation; weathering; volcanic outgassing; sediment
   burial.
2. Gases are more soluble in colder water.
3. Phytoplankton fix carbon; dead cells and faecal pellets sink, carrying carbon to deep water and
   sediments.
4. CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (→ H⁺ + HCO₃⁻).
5. Oxygen enters, decomposition speeds up and stored carbon is released as CO₂.
6. Net = +200 g C m⁻² yr⁻¹, so a **sink**.
7. 3 × 44/12 = **11 t CO₂**.
8. Nitrification.
9. Waterlogged, anaerobic soil.
10. It is a greenhouse gas and contributes to stratospheric ozone depletion.
11. Any two: precision or split fertiliser doses; cover crops; buffer strips; avoiding application
    before heavy rain.
12. Any one: high cost; storage must remain secure for centuries; it does not remove emissions
    already in the air.
