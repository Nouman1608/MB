## 2.51–2.52 Why transport systems exist

- **Unicellular organisms**: large surface area to volume ratio + short diffusion distance → diffusion across the membrane is fast enough.
- **Multicellular organisms**: small surface area to volume ratio + long distances to inner cells → diffusion too slow → need a **transport system** linking exchange surfaces to all cells.
- Bigger organism → volume grows faster than surface area → ratio falls.

**Quick reminder.** For a cube of side L, SA:V = 6L² / L³ = 6/L. Doubling the side halves the ratio.

## 2.55B–2.58B Water uptake and transpiration (Paper 2 only)

- **Root hair cells**: long extension → large surface area. Cell sap more concentrated than soil water → water enters **by osmosis** across the partially permeable membrane.
- **Transpiration** = the **evaporation** of water from the surface of a plant. Water evaporates from mesophyll cell surfaces into air spaces; water vapour **diffuses** out through **stomata**.
- Loss from leaves pulls water up the xylem (the transpiration stream).

| Factor increases | Rate | Key word in the reason |
|---|---|---|
| Humidity | Falls | Smaller water vapour concentration gradient |
| Wind speed | Rises | Water vapour blown away, gradient kept steep |
| Temperature | Rises | More kinetic energy: faster evaporation and diffusion |
| Light intensity | Rises | More stomata open |

**Method in steps: potometer practical**

1. Cut shoot under water; assemble under water; seal joints with petroleum jelly.
2. Let it settle; record bubble movement in a fixed time; repeat and find a mean.
3. Change **one** factor (fan, lamp, plastic bag, temperature); keep the same shoot and all else constant.
4. Rate = distance ÷ time, or volume ÷ time where volume = πr² × distance.

The potometer measures **water uptake**, which is close to, but not exactly, the transpiration rate: a little water is used in the plant's cells.

## 2.59–2.61 Blood and plasma

| Component | Job |
|---|---|
| Red blood cells | Carry oxygen |
| White blood cells | Defend against pathogens |
| Platelets | Clotting |
| Plasma | Transports substances and heat |

**Plasma carries:** carbon dioxide, digested food (glucose, amino acids), urea, hormones, heat energy. Memory aid: list all five in every answer that asks for them.

**Red blood cell adaptations:**

- Biconcave shape → large surface area to volume ratio → fast oxygen diffusion.
- No nucleus → more space for haemoglobin.
- Haemoglobin + oxygen → oxyhaemoglobin in the lungs; oxygen released in respiring tissues.

## 2.62–2.64B Defence

- **Phagocytes**: ingest and digest pathogens (phagocytosis). Not specific.
- **Lymphocytes**: release **antibodies specific** to the antigens on one pathogen.
- **Vaccination (Paper 2 only)**: dead/weakened pathogen or its antigens → lymphocytes make antibodies and **memory cells** → on later infection, antibodies made **sooner, faster, in greater quantity** → no illness.
- **Clotting (Paper 2 only)**: platelets → soluble fibrinogen converted to insoluble **fibrin** → mesh traps red blood cells → clot → stops **blood loss** and **entry of micro-organisms**.

## 2.65–2.67 The heart

**Route (learn it as one chain):** vena cava → right atrium → right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta.

- Right side: deoxygenated blood. Left side: oxygenated blood. Septum separates them.
- Valves prevent backflow.
- Left ventricle wall thicker → higher pressure to pump round the whole body.
- Coronary arteries supply the heart muscle.

**Heart rate rises:**

- **Exercise**: more respiration → more CO₂ → detected by brain → nerve impulses → heart beats faster and stronger → more O₂ and glucose to muscles, CO₂ removed faster.
- **Adrenaline** (adrenal glands): increases heart rate for "fight or flight".

**Coronary heart disease:** fatty deposits narrow coronary arteries → less oxygen to heart muscle → heart attack. Risk factors: smoking, diet high in saturated fat and salt, lack of exercise, obesity, high blood pressure, stress, genetics, age, being male.

## 2.68–2.69 Vessels and circulation

| | Artery | Vein | Capillary |
|---|---|---|---|
| Wall | Thick, muscular, elastic | Thin | One cell thick |
| Lumen | Narrow | Wide | Very narrow |
| Valves | No | Yes | No |
| Pressure | High | Low | Lower than in arteries |
| Direction | Away from heart | Towards heart | Between arteries and veins |

**Double circulation:** blood passes through the heart twice per circuit (heart → lungs → heart → body → heart).

| Organ | In | Out |
|---|---|---|
| Lungs | Pulmonary artery | Pulmonary vein |
| Liver | Hepatic artery (+ hepatic portal vein from gut) | Hepatic vein |
| Kidneys | Renal artery | Renal vein |
| Whole body to/from heart | Aorta (out of heart) | Vena cava (into heart) |

## Small worked reminders

**Reminder 1: an "explain" answer on transpiration (Paper 2 only).** *Explain why a plant loses water faster on a windy day.* Build the answer in three links: wind carries water vapour away from the leaf surface (1) → this keeps the concentration of water vapour outside the leaf low, so the gradient between the air spaces and the air stays steep (2) → water vapour diffuses out through the stomata faster (3). A bare "wind increases transpiration" scores at most one mark.

**Reminder 2: reading an antibody graph (Paper 2 only).** A graph shows antibody concentration after a first and a second exposure to the same pathogen. Describe what you see before you explain it: after the second exposure, antibodies appear **sooner** (shorter delay), rise **faster** (steeper curve) and reach a **higher** peak. Then explain: memory cells made after the first exposure respond straight away. Quote values from the axes with units when you describe.

**Reminder 3: tracing a molecule.** *Trace an oxygen molecule from the lungs to a leg muscle.* Alveolus → capillary → plasma → into a red blood cell, where it joins haemoglobin → pulmonary vein → left atrium → left ventricle → aorta → artery to the leg → capillary → released from oxyhaemoglobin → diffuses into a muscle cell. Name every chamber and vessel in order; a missing step costs a mark.

**Reminder 4: structure–function pairs.** Examiners reward a feature **linked** to its job. Write "capillary walls are one cell thick, **so** the diffusion distance is short", not just "thin walls". The same pattern works for veins (valves **so** no backflow at low pressure) and arteries (elastic tissue **so** the wall can stretch and recoil with each surge of high-pressure blood).

## Must-know distinctions

- **Xylem vs phloem**: water and ions upwards vs sucrose and amino acids both ways.
- **Transpiration vs translocation**: water evaporating vs food moving in phloem.
- **Phagocyte vs lymphocyte**: ingests vs releases antibodies.
- **Antibody vs antigen**: protein made by the body vs molecule on the pathogen.
- **Artery vs vein**: away, high pressure, thick wall vs towards, low pressure, valves.
- **Pulmonary artery vs pulmonary vein**: the only artery carrying deoxygenated blood vs the only vein carrying oxygenated blood.

## Quick self-test

1. Calculate the surface area to volume ratio of a cube of side 3 cm.
2. Name the two substances transported in phloem.
3. Give the direction of flow in xylem.
4. Define transpiration. (Paper 2 only)
5. State the effect of increasing humidity on the rate of transpiration. (Paper 2 only)
6. A potometer bubble moves 25 mm in 5 minutes. Calculate the rate of movement.
7. The capillary in that potometer has radius 0.5 mm. Calculate the volume of water taken up in the first 4 minutes, to 1 d.p.
8. Name five things carried in plasma.
9. Explain why red blood cells have no nucleus.
10. Name the blood vessel that takes blood from the heart to the lungs.
11. A student counts 22 beats in 20 seconds. Calculate the heart rate in beats per minute.
12. State three ways in which antibody production differs after vaccination. (Paper 2 only)

### Answers

1. SA = 6 × 9 = 54 cm²; V = 27 cm³; ratio **2 : 1**.
2. **Sucrose and amino acids.**
3. **Upwards**, from roots to leaves.
4. **The evaporation of water from the surface of a plant.**
5. Rate **decreases**.
6. 25 ÷ 5 = **5 mm per minute**.
7. Distance in 4 minutes = 20 mm; volume = π × 0.5² × 20 = **15.7 mm³**.
8. **Carbon dioxide, digested food, urea, hormones, heat energy.**
9. **More space for haemoglobin**, so more oxygen carried.
10. **Pulmonary artery.**
11. 22 × 3 = **66 beats per minute**.
12. Antibodies made **sooner, faster and in greater quantity**.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Writing "water loss" for transpiration instead of **evaporation** of water.
- Saying stomata "let water out" without saying water **vapour diffuses** out.
- Giving a factor's effect on transpiration with no reason, when the question says "explain".
- Saying xylem carries "food" or "nutrients"; say **water and mineral ions**.
- Listing only two or three plasma substances when the question wants more.
- Writing "antibodies eat the pathogen"; phagocytes ingest, antibodies are proteins.
- Saying a vaccine contains antibodies. It contains a harmless form of the pathogen or its antigens.
- Explaining the thick left ventricle by "it pumps more blood" instead of **higher pressure**.
- Swapping the hepatic vein and the hepatic portal vein.
- In heart rate calculations, forgetting to scale a 15-second or 20-second count to one minute.
