OxfordAQA International GCSE Pakistan Studies (9236) is a linear, untiered qualification with no
coursework, assessed by two equally weighted written papers: Paper 1 (The History and Culture of
Pakistan) and Paper 2 (The Human and Physical Geography of Pakistan), each 1 hour 30 minutes, 77
marks and 50%. **Each paper is built from three sections: two compulsory topics plus one topic chosen
from a pair of options**, worth 28, 28 and 21 raw marks respectively. These notes complement the
site's guides to
[The History and Culture of Pakistan](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-history-and-culture/)
and [Landscape and Natural Resources](/resources/igcse-oxfordaqa-pakistan-studies-landscape-and-natural-resources/).

## Two-thirds of each paper is compulsory -- there is no way around it

The two compulsory topics together are worth 56 of each paper's 77 marks. **Exam-preparation
priority**: revise the compulsory content of both papers to full depth before spending significant
time on the optional sections. A candidate who has prepared the optional choice thoroughly but is
weak on a compulsory topic has under-prepared the larger and unavoidable share of the paper.

## The optional section still needs a real decision, made in advance

Twenty-one of 77 marks come from one topic chosen from a pair of options. **Exam-preparation
priority**: decide which optional topic you are preparing well before the exam, on the basis of which
one you can support with the most specific detail -- named places, dated events, quantified facts --
rather than which sounds more familiar. Confirm with your teacher which option your centre has
actually taught, since preparing the untaught option is a costly and avoidable error.

## Specific facts carry more weight than general narrative

Both papers reward named detail: specific dates, places, figures and named individuals or features,
rather than a general account of a period or region. **Exam-preparation priority**: for each topic,
build a short list of specific facts you can cite under pressure -- three or four per topic is enough
if they are genuinely specific and correctly recalled.

## Geography content (Paper 2) rewards data and map skills as well as narrative

Paper 2's physical and human geography content includes working with maps, statistics and diagrams
about Pakistan specifically. **Exam-preparation priority**: practise reading and describing Pakistan-
specific data -- population distribution, river systems, resource locations -- with the same precision
expected in a general geography paper: quoted figures, correct units, and named locations.

## History content (Paper 1) rewards chronology and cause-and-effect

Paper 1's historical and cultural content is assessed on the ability to place events in sequence and
explain why they happened, not merely to recall that they occurred. **Exam-preparation priority**:
build a timeline for each compulsory topic, and rehearse a one-sentence cause and a one-sentence
consequence for each major event on it.

## Worked routine: an answer to a compulsory-topic question

The routine below is an original model written for this resource, not a reproduction of any official
past paper or mark scheme.

```
Step 1 - identify exactly what the question asks:
A fact, a cause, a consequence, or a comparison -- read the command
word before planning content.

Step 2 - give the specific fact first:
Name, date, place or figure -- stated precisely, not approximated.

Step 3 - explain its significance or cause-and-effect:
Why did it happen, or why does it matter -- the step that moves
the answer from description to explanation.

Step 4 - support with a second specific detail if the tariff
allows:
A second named fact strengthens the answer without padding it.

Step 5 - conclude by returning to the question's exact wording:
A short closing sentence that answers what was actually asked.
```

Step 3 is where marks for understanding, rather than recall, are concentrated -- and it is the step a
purely narrative answer tends to omit.

## Before/during exam checklist

- **Before the exams**: revise both compulsory topics on each paper to full depth first; confirm which
  optional topic your centre taught and prepare only that one, with specific supporting detail; build
  a short list of specific, citable facts per topic; build timelines with cause-and-effect notes for
  Paper 1's historical content.
- **During either paper**: budget time in proportion to the 28/28/21 mark split across the three
  sections.
- **On Paper 2 data questions**: quote specific figures, use correct units, and name locations
  precisely.
- **In every answer**: identify the command word, give a specific fact, explain its significance, and
  close by answering what was actually asked.

## Self-test

1. What share of each paper is compulsory, and what follows for revision priority?
2. Why must the optional topic be chosen and confirmed with your teacher in advance?
3. What distinguishes a strong Paper 1 answer from a purely narrative one?
4. What kind of detail should Paper 2 data answers include?

**Answers:** 1. Fifty-six of 77 marks (the two compulsory topics) -- so the compulsory content of both
papers should be revised to full depth before significant time goes into the optional choice.
2. Because only one option is examined and preparing the wrong one, or one your centre did not teach,
wastes revision time on content that will not appear. 3. Explaining cause-and-effect and significance,
not just recalling that an event occurred -- a timeline with stated causes and consequences supports
this. 4. Specific quoted figures with correct units and named locations, applied to Pakistan-specific
data rather than described in general terms.
