## Questions

**1.** Write down the exact value of:

**(a)** sin 240° **[1]**
**(b)** cos(11π/6) (A Level only) **[1]**
**(c)** tan(−45°) **[1]**

**2.** **(a)** Express 105° in radians, as a multiple of π. (A Level only) **[1]**
**(b)** Express 5π/9 in degrees. (A Level only) **[1]**
**(c)** A sector has radius 12 cm and angle 0.65 radians. Find its arc length, area and perimeter. (A Level only) **[3]**

**3.** (A Level only) A sector of a circle has area 40 cm² and arc length 10 cm.

**(a)** Find the radius and the sector angle in radians. **[4]**
**(b)** Find the area between the arc and the chord joining its ends. **[3]**

**4.** In triangle PQR, angle P = 58°, angle Q = 39° and QR = 14.2 cm.

**(a)** Find the length PR. **[2]**
**(b)** Find the length PQ. **[2]**

**5.** In triangle ABC, AB = 8.4 cm, BC = 11.3 cm and angle ABC = 73°.

**(a)** Find AC. **[3]**
**(b)** Find angle BAC. **[3]**
**(c)** Find the area of the triangle. **[2]**

**6.** In triangle ABC, angle B = 40°, AC = 8 cm and AB = 11 cm.

**(a)** Find the two possible sizes of angle C. **[3]**
**(b)** Find both possible values of BC. **[3]**

**7.** A walker goes from A to B, a distance of 5.4 km on a bearing of 040°. She then walks 6.8 km from B to C on a bearing of 115°. Find how far she is from A, and the bearing of her position C from A. **[7]**

**8.** It is given that cos 50° = c.

**(a)** Write each of these in terms of c: cos 130°, cos 310°, sin 140°. **[3]**
**(b)** State the period of y = tan x in degrees, and the equation of its asymptote between x = 0° and x = 180°. **[2]**
**(c)** State the number of solutions of sin x = 0.3 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 720°. **[1]**

**9.** (A Level only) In this question θ is small and in radians.

**(a)** Use the approximation for cos θ to estimate cos 0.1. Find the error in your estimate, to 2 significant figures. **[2]**
**(b)** Show that (1 − cos 4θ) / (θ tan 2θ) is approximately 4. **[3]**
**(c)** Use the small angle approximations to form a quadratic equation for the small solution of cos θ + 2 sin θ = 1.1, and solve it to find this solution to 3 significant figures. **[3]**

**10.** **(a)** Solve 5 sin θ = −2 for −180° < θ < 180°. **[3]**
**(b)** Solve tan 2θ = 1.5 for 0° ≤ θ < 180°. **[3]**

**11.** **(a)** Solve 2 cos²θ + cos θ − 1 = 0 for 0° ≤ θ < 360°. **[4]**
**(b)** (A Level only) Solve tan(2x + π/4) = 1 for −π ≤ x ≤ π, giving exact answers. **[4]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** 240° is in quadrant 3 with reference angle 60°, where sine is negative: sin 240° = **−√3/2** [1]
**(b)** 11π/6 = 330°, reference angle 30°, cosine positive: **√3/2** [1]
**(c)** tan(−45°) = −tan 45° = **−1** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Exact value questions are about the reference angle and the sign. A decimal from a calculator is a risky substitute when the question says "exact".

**2. (a)** 105 × π/180 = **7π/12** [1]
**(b)** 5π/9 × 180/π = **100°** [1]
**(c)** Arc length = rθ = 12 × 0.65 = **7.8 cm** [1]; area = ½ × 144 × 0.65 = **46.8 cm²** [1]; perimeter = 24 + 7.8 = **31.8 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* The perimeter needs the two radii as well as the arc. Candidates often stop at the arc length, so read the word "perimeter" twice.

**3. (a)** Area = ½rs, so 40 = ½ × r × 10 [1], giving r = **8 cm** [1]. Then θ = s/r [1] = 10/8 = **1.25 radians** [1]
**(b)** Segment = sector − triangle = ½r²θ − ½r² sin θ = ½r²(θ − sin θ) [1] = 32(1.25 − sin 1.25) [1] = **9.63 cm²** (with the calculator in radian mode) [1]
*Examiner insight:* A calculator left in degree mode gives a wrong value for sin 1.25 with no warning. Estimate first: sin 1.25 should be a little below 1.

**4. (a)** PR / sin 39° = 14.2 / sin 58° [1], so PR = **10.5 cm** [1]
**(b)** Angle R = 180° − 58° − 39° = 83° [1], so PQ = 14.2 × sin 83° / sin 58° = **16.6 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Match each side to its opposite angle before substituting. Writing the ratio down first makes the method clear even if a later number slips.

**5. (a)** AC² = 8.4² + 11.3² − 2 × 8.4 × 11.3 × cos 73° [1] = 198.25 − 55.50 = 142.75 [1], so AC = **11.9 cm** [1]
**(b)** sin A / 11.3 = sin 73° / 11.9476 [1], so sin A = 0.9045 [1]. BC is shorter than AC, so A is smaller than 73° and acute: A = **64.8°** [1]
**(c)** Area = ½ × 8.4 × 11.3 × sin 73° [1] = **45.4 cm²** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Keep the unrounded value of AC for part (b). Using 11.9 can shift the final angle in the third figure.

**6. (a)** sin C / 11 = sin 40° / 8 [1], so sin C = 0.8838 [1], giving C = **62.1° or 117.9°** [1]
**(b)** Angle A = 180° − 40° − C = 77.9° or 22.1° [1]. BC = 8 sin A / sin 40° [1], giving **12.2 cm or 4.68 cm** [1]
*Examiner insight:* This is the ambiguous case. The wording "possible values" signals two answers, and a complete answer shows that 117.9° fits in the triangle (40° + 117.9° is less than 180°).

**7.** The bearing of A from B is 220°, so angle ABC = 220° − 115° = 105° [1].
AC² = 5.4² + 6.8² − 2 × 5.4 × 6.8 × cos 105° [1] = 75.4 + 19.01 = 94.41 [1], so AC = **9.72 km** [1].
sin(∠BAC) / 6.8 = sin 105° / 9.716 [1], so ∠BAC = 42.5° [1].
Bearing of C from A = 040° + 42.5° = **082.5°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* A sketch with a north line at each point is the safest way to show where 105° comes from. Quote the bearing with three figures.

**8. (a)** cos 130° = −cos 50° = **−c** [1]; cos 310° = cos 50° = **c** [1]; sin 140° = sin 40° = cos 50° = **c** [1]
**(b)** Period **180°** [1]; asymptote **x = 90°** [1]
**(c)** Two solutions in each 360° cycle, so **4** [1]
*Examiner insight:* "In terms of c" means no other trig values or decimals in the answer. For each part, name the symmetry you used.

**9. (a)** cos 0.1 ≈ 1 − 0.01/2 = **0.995** [1]. The calculator value is 0.99500417, so the error is **4.2 × 10⁻⁶** [1]
**(b)** 1 − cos 4θ ≈ 1 − (1 − (4θ)²/2) = 8θ² [1]; tan 2θ ≈ 2θ so the denominator is about 2θ² [1]; the ratio is 8θ² / 2θ² = **4** [1]
**(c)** 1 − θ²/2 + 2θ = 1.1, so **θ² − 4θ + 0.2 = 0** [1]; θ = 2 ± √3.8 [1]; the small root is **θ = 0.0506** (the root near 3.95 is not small) [1]
*Examiner insight:* In a "show that" question write every substitution line. The final value 4 must come from your working, not be stated at the start.

**10. (a)** sin θ = −0.4 [1]; principal value θ = −23.6° [1]; the second solution is −180° + 23.6° = −156.4°, so **θ = −23.6° or −156.4°** [1]
**(b)** 0° ≤ 2θ < 360° [1]; 2θ = 56.3° or 236.3° [1]; **θ = 28.2° or 118.2°** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Convert the interval before you solve. If you solve for θ first and then double, you will miss or invent solutions.

**11. (a)** (2 cos θ − 1)(cos θ + 1) = 0 [1]; cos θ = 1/2 gives θ = **60° or 300°** [1]; cos θ = −1 gives θ = **180°** [1]; no other values, so there are three solutions in total [1]
**(b)** The range for 2x + π/4 is −7π/4 ≤ 2x + π/4 ≤ 9π/4 [1]. tan = 1 when 2x + π/4 = π/4 + nπ [1], so 2x = nπ and the values of n from −2 to 2 are in range [1]; **x = −π, −π/2, 0, π/2, π** [1]
*Examiner insight:* Each factor of a quadratic gives its own set of solutions, so check you have dealt with both. In (b), the endpoints count because the inequalities are not strict.

## Where marks are usually lost

- Calculator in degree mode for a radians question, or the reverse.
- Giving only the principal value of an inverse trig function and missing the other solutions in the interval.
- Dividing by k too early, or not extending the interval when the angle is a multiple such as 2θ or 3θ.
- Forgetting the second triangle in the ambiguous case, or keeping a second angle that pushes the total over 180°.
- Using ½ab sin C when the angle lies outside the two sides used.
- Rounding intermediate values, so that a later answer is off in the third significant figure.
- Writing bearings with fewer than three figures, or measuring from the wrong north line.
- Using sector formulae with an angle in degrees.
- Decimals where an exact value is asked for.
