## Section A: short answers

**1.** State **two** causes of electrocution when using ICT equipment. **[2]**

**2.** Give **two** causes of fire in a room full of computers. **[2]**

**3.** Name the type of fire extinguisher that should be kept in an ICT room. **[1]**

**4.** Give **one** way in which wireless networking can improve physical safety. **[1]**

## Section B: structured questions

**6.** A school's ICT room has 30 computers. Pupils often bring drinks into the room.

**(a)** Explain why drinks are a hazard in the ICT room. **[2]**

**(b)** Describe **one** rule the school could introduce and how it reduces the hazard. **[2]**

**7.** In the same room, several computers are plugged into one wall socket using extension leads plugged into each other.

**(a)** Explain why this is a fire hazard. **[2]**

**(b)** Give **two** strategies the school could use to prevent this hazard. **[2]**

**8.** The school's server is kept in a small cupboard with no window. The door is always closed and the vents on the server are covered with dust.

**(a)** Identify the hazard. **[1]**

**(b)** Explain why these conditions cause the hazard. **[2]**

**(c)** Suggest **two** ways to reduce the risk. **[2]**

**9.** A café offers customers laptops to use. The laptop power cables run across the floor between tables.

**(a)** Describe **two** hazards caused by the cables. **[2]**

**(b)** Give a different prevention strategy for each hazard you described. **[2]**

**10.** An office has heavy colour laser printers on small, narrow tables near a busy corridor. Explain the hazard and describe **two** ways to prevent it. **[4]**

**11.** A technician notices that a monitor's power cable has a split in its outer covering. Explain why the cable should be replaced rather than repaired with tape. **[2]**

**12.** A company is setting up a new open-plan call centre with 120 computers. Describe **three** physical safety hazards and, for each, a strategy that should be included in the design of the room. **[6]**

## Answers

**1.** Any two of: spilling drinks near electrical equipment [1]; touching live or damaged cables [1]; opening equipment while it is plugged in [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**2.** Any two of: overloaded sockets [1]; equipment overheating [1]; blocked vents [1]; dust in fans [1]. Maximum 2. **[2]**

**3.** CO2 (carbon dioxide) extinguisher [1]. **[1]**

**4.** Fewer cables are needed, so there are fewer trailing cables for people to trip over [1]. **[1]**

**5.** C [1]. **[1]**

**6. (a)** If a drink is spilled into a computer or keyboard, the liquid conducts electricity [1], so a pupil touching the equipment could receive an electric shock / be electrocuted [1].

**(b)** No food or drink allowed in the ICT room / drinks must be left on a shelf outside [1], so there is nothing that can be spilled into the equipment [1]. **[4]**

**7. (a)** The computers together draw more current than the socket and wiring are designed for [1], so the wires overheat and can melt the insulation and set fire to nearby material [1].

**(b)** Any two of: install more wall sockets [1]; never plug one extension lead into another [1]; spread devices across several sockets [1]; switch off equipment that is not in use [1]. Maximum 2. **[4]**

**8. (a)** Fire (from equipment overheating) [1].

**(b)** The server produces heat, and with no ventilation the heat cannot escape from the cupboard [1]; the dust over the vents stops the fans removing heat, so components can get hot enough to start a fire [1].

**(c)** Any two of: clean the dust from the vents and fans [1]; install ventilation or air conditioning in the cupboard [1]; leave space around the server [1]; fit a smoke detector and keep a CO2 extinguisher nearby [1]. Maximum 2. **[5]**

**9. (a)** Customers or staff could trip over the cables and be injured [1]; a trip could pull a laptop off a table, damaging it or exposing a live part of a damaged cable [1].

**(b)** Tripping: run the cables in a cable duct or along the wall, or put tables next to sockets [1]. Falling laptop / damaged cable: secure the laptops to the tables, or check cables regularly and replace damaged ones [1]. **[4]**

**10.** Hazard: heavy printers could fall off the small tables and injure someone [1], especially if they are knocked by people walking past in the corridor [1]. Prevention, any two: use strong, large tables designed for the weight [1]; place the printers well back from the edge [1]; move the printers away from the corridor [1]. Maximum 4. **[4]**

**11.** The split shows the insulation is damaged and the live wire may be exposed [1]; tape is not a permanent or reliable insulation and can peel off, so someone could still touch the live wire and be electrocuted [1]. **[2]**

**12.** Three hazards, each with a matching strategy [2 each]:
- Electrocution [1]: ban drinks at workstations and provide a separate break area / use RCBs and check cables regularly [1].
- Fire from overloaded sockets or overheating [1]: install enough floor or wall sockets for every desk / provide air conditioning and keep vents clear / CO2 extinguishers [1].
- Tripping [1]: run cables through floor ducts or under desks / use wireless connections [1].
- Heavy equipment falling [1]: provide strong desks large enough for the equipment, with nothing overhanging [1].
Maximum 6. **[6]**

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to mention RCBs?**
The syllabus asks for prevention strategies without listing them. An RCB is a valid strategy for electrocution, but simpler ones such as banning drinks and replacing damaged cables score equally.

**Can I give the same strategy for two hazards?**
Only if it genuinely prevents both and the question does not ask for different strategies. It is safer to give a separate strategy for each.
