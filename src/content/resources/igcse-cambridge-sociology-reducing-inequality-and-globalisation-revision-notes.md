These notes condense key questions 3.2 and 3.3 of Cambridge IGCSE Sociology (0495). Societies try to
reduce inequality through laws, welfare states and the work of NGOs; sociologists from the New
Right, Marxist and feminist perspectives disagree about how successful these attempts are; and
globalisation affects inequality between societies through migration, ecological issues and
exploitation rooted in capitalism, colonialism and patriarchy.

Fuller explanations: [reducing inequality and global inequality study guide](/resources/igcse-cambridge-sociology-reducing-inequality-and-globalisation/).

## Syllabus coverage

0495 syllabus 2025–2027: **3.2** 3.2.1 legislation; 3.2.2 welfare states; 3.2.3 NGOs; 3.2.4
sociological views of success. **3.3** 3.3.1 migration; 3.3.2 global ecological issues; 3.3.3
explanations for global inequalities.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Progressive taxation** | Higher earners pay a higher proportion of income in tax |
| **Redistribution** | Moving resources from richer to poorer groups through tax and benefits |
| **Minimum wage** | Lowest hourly pay allowed by law |
| **Equal opportunities laws** | Laws banning discrimination |
| **Welfare state** | Government provision of education, health, housing, pensions and benefits |
| **Universal basic income** | Regular, unconditional payment to every citizen |
| **NGO** | Non-governmental, non-profit organisation |
| **Microcredit** | Small loans to poor people to start businesses |
| **Underclass** | A group below the working class said to depend on welfare (New Right) |
| **Culture of poverty** | Values such as fatalism passed between generations of the poor (Lewis) |
| **Dependency culture** | Reliance on benefits rather than work |
| **Push / pull factors** | Reasons to leave / reasons to move to a place |
| **Remittances** | Money sent home by migrants |
| **TNC** | Transnational company operating in many countries |
| **Colonialism** | Rule of one country over another, extracting resources |

## 3.2 Attempts to reduce inequality

- **Legislation:** progressive tax; benefits; minimum wage; equal opportunities laws; other measures
  such as conditional cash transfers (Brazil's *Bolsa Família*, 2003) and job guarantees (India's
  rural employment law, 2005).
- **Welfare states:** free/universal education; unemployment benefit; pensions; low-cost housing;
  free healthcare (UK NHS, 1948, after the Beveridge Report, 1942); universal basic income trials
  (Finland, 2017–2018).
- **NGOs:** poverty reduction; medical aid (Médecins Sans Frontières); business (microcredit,
  Grameen Bank); education (BRAC, founded 1972).

## Views on success

| Perspective | Verdict | Key point |
|---|---|---|
| New Right | Welfare harms | Creates underclass and dependency (Murray, 1984) |
| Culture of poverty | Poverty persists through values | Lewis (1959, 1966) |
| Marxist | Welfare props up capitalism | Softens exploitation; inequality of wealth remains |
| Critics of NGOs | Limited | Dependency, donor priorities, small scale |
| Feminist | Equality laws incomplete | Pay gap and unpaid care persist |

## 3.3 Globalisation and inequality

| Area | Increases inequality | Reduces inequality |
|---|---|---|
| Migration | Brain drain; exploitation; negative perceptions | Remittances; jobs; skills shared |
| TNCs | Low wages; weak regulation; resource extraction | Investment; jobs; technology |
| Climate change | Hits poorer countries hardest | Global agreements and aid for adaptation |
| Pollution | Concentrated in poor areas | Global environmental standards |
| Urbanisation | Informal settlements, poor sanitation | Access to jobs and services in cities |

**Explanations:** Marxist exploitation by global capitalism; colonialism's lasting effects; feminist
patriarchy — women do much of the world's work but own little property.

## Worked example: a 6-mark "explain" answer

*Explain three criticisms of the welfare state.* (practice)

1. **New Right:** welfare reduces the incentive to work, encouraging dependency culture and an
   underclass (Murray).
2. **Marxist:** welfare only manages capitalism's problems, keeping workers healthy and quiet without
   redistributing wealth.
3. **Feminist:** welfare systems may assume a male breadwinner and leave women's unpaid care work
   unrecognised.

Each criticism is identified and then explained — the pattern that earns 2 marks per point.

## Worked example: a 14-mark evaluation plan

*Evaluate the view that NGOs are the most effective way to reduce global inequality.* (practice)

1. **For:** NGOs reach places governments neglect; medical assistance in emergencies; microcredit
   helps women start businesses; education projects improve girls' life chances.
2. **Against:** NGOs depend on donors and can create dependency; they are small compared with state
   welfare systems; they may follow Western priorities rather than local needs.
3. **Alternatives:** legislation (progressive tax, minimum wage) and welfare states change whole
   systems; Marxists argue only changing global capitalism will reduce inequality.
4. **Judgement:** NGOs are effective for particular needs, but large-scale reduction of inequality
   needs government action and fairer global trade.

## Common mistakes and exam traps

- Listing welfare services without explaining their effect on **life chances**.
- Treating the New Right and Marxist critiques as the same — they criticise welfare from opposite
  directions.
- Writing about migration only as a problem; the syllabus asks for economic consequences and
  cultural diversity too.
- Forgetting **urbanisation** and **pollution** in 3.3.2.
- Describing colonialism without linking it to present-day inequality.

## Quick self-test

1. Define progressive taxation.
2. Give two forms of NGO work named in the syllabus.
3. What is a dependency culture?
4. Give one push and one pull factor.
5. Explain one way climate change increases inequality between societies.

## Frequently asked questions

**Which example of reducing inequality is best?** One you can explain in detail and evaluate,
whether from your own country or elsewhere.

**Can NGOs replace governments?** Most sociologists argue NGOs help but are too small and dependent
on donors to replace state action.

**Do I need statistics?** Not necessarily; clear, accurate examples and concepts score well.
