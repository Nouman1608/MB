These notes condense section 6.2, *Social class and educational attainment*, of Cambridge International A Level Sociology (9699), assessed on Paper 3 – Education. Four syllabus strands: material factors; cultural explanations (parental attitudes, values, speech codes, cultural capital); in-school factors (labelling, ability grouping, pupil subcultures); and compensatory education programmes.

Full explanations: [social class and educational attainment study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-sociology-9699-social-class-educational-attainment/).

## Core argument

Class gaps begin before school through material and cultural differences, and are then widened or narrowed by what schools do. Strong answers show the factors interacting and judge which is most powerful, rather than listing them.

## Before you explain: measurement

- Class measured by parental occupation, parental education or a poverty marker such as free school meal eligibility.
- A two-way poverty marker hides differences within the non-eligible majority.
- Gaps are averages: many working-class pupils succeed.
- **Feinstein (2003):** advantaged low scorers tended to overtake disadvantaged high scorers in the primary years. **Jerrim and Vignoles (2013):** partly regression to the mean, so the effect size is debated.

## Material factors

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| Housing and space | Nowhere to study; moves disrupt schooling |
| Diet and health | Concentration and attendance fall |
| Costs of schooling | Uniform, trips, devices, internet |
| Opportunity cost | Staying on means lost wages, shaping choices |
| Money buys culture | Tutors, books, trips build cultural capital |
| Marketised choice | House prices decide access to high-performing schools |

**Evaluation:** strong where poverty is severe; cannot explain differences between families with similar incomes but different parental education.

## Cultural explanations: three positions

| Position | Claim | Names |
|---|---|---|
| Cultural deprivation | Home lacks what school needs | Douglas (1964); Sugarman; Hyman |
| Cultural difference | Working-class culture is different, not deficient | Keddie (1973); Labov |
| Cultural capital | School rewards middle-class culture as merit | Bourdieu; Sullivan (2001); Lareau (2003) |

- **Speech codes:** Bernstein's restricted and elaborated codes; he denied a deficit view.
- **Reay, *Class Work* (1998):** working-class mothers cared but lacked confidence, time and educational knowledge to intervene; middle-class mothers used cultural capital.
- **Habitus:** early dispositions; a mismatch with the school "field" leads to self-exclusion ("not for people like us").

## In-school factors

- **Labelling** against the ideal pupil (Becker); early grouping by appearance and background (Rist).
- **Ability grouping:** Ball (1981) — non-manual pupils more likely in top band; Gillborn and Youdell (2000) — triage and tiering.
- **Subcultures:** Hargreaves (1967); Lacey (1970) — differentiation and polarisation; Willis (1977).
- **Woods (1979), *The Divided School*:** adaptations include ingratiation, compliance, opportunism, ritualism, retreatism, colonisation, intransigence and rebellion — pupils respond in many ways.

**The labelling chain:** judgement → placement → different knowledge and tiers → pupil response → outcome that seems to confirm the judgement.

**Evaluation:** small ethnographies; can ignore why labels follow class lines.

## Compensatory education

| Programme | Where | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Head Start (1965) | USA | Pre-school learning |
| Perry Preschool (1962–1967) | USA | Pre-school; long follow-up |
| Plowden Report (1967), Education Action Zones, Sure Start, Pupil Premium | England | Deprived areas and pupils |
| Education Maintenance Allowance (national from 2004) | England (abolished there 2011; continued in Scotland, Wales, Northern Ireland) | Opportunity cost of staying on post-16 |
| Progresa (1997), Bolsa Família (2003) | Mexico, Brazil | Cash conditional on school attendance |

**Evaluation:** material-targeted schemes avoid the deficit criticism; programmes treat symptoms if wider inequality remains; Marxists say education cannot compensate for society.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Material deprivation | Lack of money and the things it buys |
| Opportunity cost | Income lost by staying in education |
| Cultural deprivation | Claim that a home lacks the values or language schools require |
| Cultural difference | View that working-class culture differs rather than lacks |
| Cultural capital | Knowledge, tastes and dispositions valued by dominant groups |
| Habitus | Dispositions learned early in life |
| Restricted / elaborated code | Context-bound / explicit, context-free speech |
| Labelling | Attaching a fixed identity to a pupil |
| Self-fulfilling prophecy | A label shapes treatment and the pupil comes to fit it |
| Educational triage | Rationing effort towards pupils near a pass |
| Polarisation | Pupils splitting into pro- and anti-school groups |
| Compensatory education | Extra resources to make up for disadvantage |

## Exam traps

- Confusing cultural deprivation with cultural capital.
- Saying "working-class pupils fail"; refer to patterns and averages.
- Listing internal factors without explaining why labels follow class lines.
- Describing compensatory programmes without using them as evidence about causes.
- Ignoring measurement: a free-meal gap is not the whole class gap.

## Self-test

1. Give one weakness of using free school meal eligibility to measure class.
2. What did Feinstein report, and what was the later criticism?
3. Explain opportunity cost as a material factor.
4. Distinguish cultural deprivation from cultural difference.
5. What did Reay find about mothers' involvement?
6. What is habitus, and how can it lead to self-exclusion?
7. Name four of Woods's adaptations.
8. Describe the labelling chain in three steps.
9. What barrier did the Education Maintenance Allowance target?
10. How do conditional cash transfers work?
11. Give one Marxist criticism of compensatory education.
12. Why do material and cultural explanations overlap?

### Answers

1. It splits pupils into two groups only, hiding differences among the non-eligible majority.
2. Advantaged children with low early scores tended to overtake disadvantaged children with high early scores during the primary years; Jerrim and Vignoles argued part of this is regression to the mean.
3. Staying in education means giving up wages a low-income family may need, so poverty shapes the choice to continue.
4. Deprivation says the home lacks something schools need; difference says working-class culture is different, not deficient, and schools favour another culture.
5. Working-class mothers cared but lacked confidence, time and educational knowledge to intervene; middle-class mothers used cultural capital to secure advantages.
6. Dispositions learned early; when they do not fit school culture, pupils feel education is not for them and withdraw.
7. Any four: ingratiation, compliance, opportunism, ritualism, retreatism, colonisation, intransigence, rebellion.
8. A teacher judges a pupil; the pupil is placed in a lower group with different knowledge and tiers; outcomes then seem to confirm the judgement.
9. The opportunity cost of staying in post-16 education for young people from low-income families.
10. Poor families receive cash on condition that their children attend school, among other requirements.
11. Education cannot compensate for society: the inequality that creates disadvantage remains.
12. Money buys books, tutors and trips, which build cultural capital.
