## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Homeostasis | The regulation of the internal conditions of a cell or organism to maintain optimum conditions for function, in response to internal and external changes |
| Optimum conditions | The conditions in which enzymes and cells work best |
| Stimulus | A change in the environment, inside or outside the body |
| Receptor | A cell that detects a stimulus |
| Coordination centre | Receives and processes information from receptors, such as the brain, spinal cord or pancreas |
| Effector | A muscle or gland that brings about a response |
| Response | The action of an effector that restores optimum levels |
| Nervous response | Control by electrical impulses along neurones |
| Chemical response | Control by hormones carried in the blood |

## The definition: three words you must include

**Regulation** + **internal conditions** + **optimum conditions for function**.

## What homeostasis protects

- **Enzyme action**: enzymes need their optimum temperature.
- **All cell functions**: cells must not gain or lose too much water by osmosis.

## The three conditions in the human body

| Condition | Linked to | Controlled mainly by |
|---|---|---|
| Blood glucose concentration | Supply of glucose for respiration; osmosis | Pancreas and hormones (4.5.3.2) |
| Body temperature | Enzyme activity | Thermoregulatory centre in the brain (4.5.2.4, Biology only) |
| Water levels | Osmosis | Kidneys (4.5.3.3, Biology only) |

## The control system

```
stimulus → receptor → coordination centre → effector → response → optimum restored
```

- Receptors **detect**.
- Coordination centres **receive and process**.
- Effectors (**muscles or glands**) **respond**.
- The response **restores** the optimum, acting against the change. In Higher tier content later in the topic,
  this is called **negative feedback**.

## Nervous or chemical?

| Feature | Nervous | Chemical (hormonal) |
|---|---|---|
| Carried by | Neurones | Blood |
| Signal | Electrical impulse | Hormone |
| Speed | Fast | Slower |
| How long the effect lasts | Short | Longer |

Homeostatic control systems may use either, or both.

## Spotting the parts in an unfamiliar system

1. Find the **change** (stimulus): what has gone up or down?
2. Find what **detects** it (receptor).
3. Find where the information is **processed** (coordination centre).
4. Find the **muscle or gland** that acts (effector).
5. Check the response moves the condition **back** towards normal.

## Two systems mapped onto the model

| Part | Hot day (body temperature, Biology only) | Large drink of water (water level) |
|---|---|---|
| Stimulus | Rise in blood and skin temperature | Blood becomes more dilute |
| Receptor | Temperature receptors in skin and thermoregulatory centre | Detected by the brain |
| Coordination centre | Thermoregulatory centre in the brain | Brain (and pituitary gland, Higher tier only) |
| Effector | Sweat glands; blood vessels in the skin | Kidneys |
| Response | Sweat produced; vessels dilate | More dilute urine produced |
| Result | Temperature falls towards optimum | Water level falls towards normal |

## Explanation chains

- **Too hot:** enzymes move above optimum → may denature → reactions slow → cells cannot function.
- **Too much water lost:** body fluids too concentrated → cells lose water by osmosis → cells do not function
  efficiently.
- **Blood glucose too low:** cells lack glucose → less respiration → less energy for cell processes.

## Internal and external changes

| Change | Type |
|---|---|
| Muscles release energy by respiration during a run | Internal |
| Cold wind on a winter walk | External |
| Glucose absorbed after a meal | Internal |
| Hot, dry air in a desert | External |

## Exam traps

- "Homeostasis keeps the body the same." Use **regulation** and **optimum**.
- Receptors respond. No: receptors **detect**.
- Organs such as "the skin" given as effectors. Name the **muscle or gland**, such as sweat glands.
- Forgetting coordination centres include the **pancreas** and **spinal cord**, not just the brain.
- Giving a response that makes the change worse.
- Saying enzymes "die" when too hot. They are **denatured**.
- Linking water levels to enzymes instead of to **osmosis**.

## Self-test

1. Define homeostasis.
2. Name the three conditions controlled in the human body given in the specification.
3. Why does the body need to control its temperature?
4. Why does the body need to control water levels?
5. What do receptors do?
6. Name three coordination centres.
7. What two types of structure can be effectors?
8. What do effectors do?
9. Give two differences between nervous and chemical responses.
10. Is exercise an internal or an external change? Explain.
11. Why must a response act against the change that caused it?
12. Which coordination centre controls blood glucose concentration?

### Answers

1. The regulation of the internal conditions of a cell or organism to maintain optimum conditions for function,
   in response to internal and external changes.
2. Blood glucose concentration; body temperature; water levels.
3. Enzymes work best at an optimum temperature; too hot and they denature.
4. Cells that gain or lose too much water by osmosis do not function efficiently.
5. Detect stimuli (changes in the environment).
6. Brain, spinal cord, pancreas.
7. Muscles and glands.
8. Bring about responses that restore optimum levels.
9. Nervous responses are faster and shorter-lived; chemical responses are slower and last longer; nervous use
   impulses along neurones, chemical use hormones in the blood.
10. Internal: the change (for example, a rise in temperature from respiration) starts inside the body.
11. So that the condition is brought back to the optimum rather than moved further from it.
12. The pancreas.
