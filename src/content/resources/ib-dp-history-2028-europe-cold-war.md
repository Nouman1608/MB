**This page is for HL only.** Regional study 12, Europe during and after the Cold War (1945 to 2020), is one of twelve studies in the Europe option examined on HL Paper 3, where you write two essays from different regional studies. The guide has six inquiry topics: the emergence of the Cold War in Europe; its impact on Germany (1945 to 1991); its impact on Western and Northern Europe; its impact on Central and Eastern Europe, which includes opposition to Soviet control; the impact of European political integration after the Cold War (to 2020); and post-Cold War developments in **one European country** (to 2020). This guide uses Germany as the country case.

## Emergence of the Cold War in Europe

*Breakdown of the wartime alliance by 1949.* The alliance of the USA, Britain and the USSR held while Germany was undefeated. Conferences at Yalta (February 1945) and Potsdam (July to August 1945) showed growing disagreement over Poland, the future of Germany and reparations. By 1946 Churchill spoke at Fulton of an "iron curtain" across Europe. By 1949 Europe was divided into two blocs.

*Ideological differences.* Capitalist democracy and Soviet communism offered rival models of society. Each side saw the other as a threat to its way of life. Moscow believed the West wanted to encircle it; Washington feared Soviet expansion.

*Roles of Stalin and Truman.* **Stalin** wanted security through a buffer of friendly states in eastern Europe, reparations and control over Germany's future. **Truman**, who became president in April 1945, took a firmer line than Roosevelt. The Truman Doctrine (March 1947) promised support for governments resisting communism, starting with Greece and Turkey. Historians disagree about responsibility. Traditional accounts blamed Soviet expansion; "revisionist" historians, such as William Appleman Williams, stressed American economic aims; "post-revisionists" see mutual fear and misperception.

*Economic factors.* The **Marshall Plan**, announced in June 1947, offered large-scale American aid to rebuild Europe, about 13 billion dollars over four years. It aimed to restore trade and stability and block communism. Stalin forced eastern European states to reject it and created the Cominform (1947); the Soviet response to Marshall aid included the later COMECON. The plan deepened the division of Europe.

## Impact of the Cold War on Germany (1945 to 1991)

*Berlin Blockade (1949).* Germany was divided into four occupation zones, and Berlin was also divided. The Western powers merged their zones and introduced a new currency, the Deutschmark, in June 1948. The USSR blockaded West Berlin from June 1948. The Western allies supplied the city by an **airlift** until the blockade was lifted in May 1949. It strengthened Western unity, and led to the founding of the Federal Republic (West Germany) in May 1949 and the German Democratic Republic (East Germany) in October 1949. NATO followed in April 1949.

*Berlin Wall Crisis (1961).* Millions had left East Germany for the West, many of them skilled workers (about 2.7 million between 1949 and 1961). After a long crisis that began with Khrushchev's demand in 1958 that the West leave Berlin, the East German government, under Walter Ulbricht and with Soviet approval, built the **Berlin Wall** on 13 August 1961. It stopped the exodus but damaged the East's image. Kennedy's "Ich bin ein Berliner" speech (1963) affirmed support for West Berlin. At least 140 people died trying to cross.

*Social and cultural experiences (1961 to 1990).* In West Germany the "economic miracle" under a social market economy brought rising living standards, consumer goods, travel and guest workers from southern Europe and Turkey. The 1968 student movement challenged the older generation over Nazi pasts. In East Germany, the ruling SED party controlled society; the **Stasi** built a huge network of informers. Social benefits included work, childcare and cheap housing, and many women worked, but travel and speech were limited and shortages were common. Under Brandt's *Ostpolitik* (from 1969), the two states recognised each other in the Basic Treaty (1972).

*Reasons for reunification (1990).* East German economic weakness; Gorbachev's reforms and refusal to use force; Hungary's opening of its border to Austria in 1989; mass emigration and the Leipzig demonstrations ("We are the people"); the fall of the Wall on 9 November 1989; and Chancellor **Kohl's** leadership and US support all mattered. A free election in March 1990 favoured quick unification, currency union followed in July, and the Two Plus Four Treaty with the wartime powers allowed formal reunification on 3 October 1990.

## Impact of the Cold War on Western and Northern Europe

*NATO.* The North Atlantic Treaty Organization (1949) tied the USA to Western Europe's defence. West Germany joined in 1955. The USA provided a nuclear umbrella and troops, and Europeans relied on it.

*Economic changes.* Marshall aid and cooperation led to the European Coal and Steel Community (1951), and the Treaty of Rome (1957) created the **European Economic Community (EEC)** of six states: France, West Germany, Italy, Belgium, the Netherlands and Luxembourg. Britain joined in 1973. Western Europe enjoyed rapid growth and welfare states.

*Social impact.* Fear of nuclear war led to peace movements, such as CND (1958) and the large protests of the early 1980s against new missiles. American culture, music and consumer goods spread, helped by affluence. Anticommunism affected politics in some states.

*Relations with the USA.* The relationship included dependence and tension. The Suez Crisis (1956) showed British and French weakness. De Gaulle took France out of NATO's command structure in 1966, seeking independence. Disputes over Vietnam and nuclear policy arose. In Northern Europe, Finland remained neutral under pressure from the USSR, Sweden stayed non-aligned, and Norway and Denmark joined NATO but limited foreign bases.

## Impact of the Cold War on Central and Eastern Europe

*Motives and methods of Soviet domination.* Stalin wanted a buffer against invasion, security against Germany, and ideological expansion. Methods included the presence of the Red Army, communists put in key posts, rigged elections (Poland, 1947), the takeover in Czechoslovakia (1948), show trials such as that of Rajk in Hungary (1949) and secret police. The **Warsaw Pact** (1955), formed after West Germany joined NATO, formalised the bloc's military command.

*Soviet economic policies and COMECON.* The Council for Mutual Economic Assistance (1949) coordinated trade and planning. It mostly served Soviet needs, with eastern states exporting goods and importing Soviet oil. States copied Soviet planning, collectivised farms and built heavy industry. Living standards lagged behind the West.

*Social impact of Soviet control.* Censorship, secret police surveillance and attacks on the churches, such as the trial of Cardinal Mindszenty in Hungary, shaped daily life. Education was reshaped. Travel was restricted. There were gains in employment and social welfare, but without freedom.

## Opposition to Soviet control

*Tito and Yugoslavia.* **Josip Broz Tito** led his own communist liberation, so he resisted Stalin's orders. In 1948 Yugoslavia was expelled from the Cominform. Tito kept communism but with workers' self-management, received Western aid, and helped found the non-aligned movement. Stalin did not invade, which shows limits to Soviet reach.

*Other challenges.* In 1953 East German workers rose and were suppressed. In 1956 Hungary's revolt under Imre Nagy, who promised to leave the Warsaw Pact, was crushed by Soviet troops in November, with thousands killed and some 200,000 refugees. In 1968 the Prague Spring under Dubček sought "socialism with a human face"; Warsaw Pact forces invaded in August, and the Brezhnev Doctrine claimed the right to intervene. In Poland, the **Solidarity** movement led by Lech Wałęsa emerged in 1980 and was banned under martial law in 1981 but remained influential, and it negotiated the Round Table talks of 1989.

## European political integration after the Cold War (to 2020)

*EU and expansion after 1990.* The Maastricht Treaty (1992) created the European Union, with plans for a single currency, the euro, introduced in 1999 for accounting and in 2002 as cash. Ten states, mostly from the former communist bloc, joined in 2004, Bulgaria and Romania in 2007 and Croatia in 2013.

*Economic policies.* The single market, the Common Agricultural Policy and cohesion funds helped poorer regions, while the euro crisis from 2009, notably in Greece, brought austerity and bitter debate.

*Social policies.* Free movement of people (Schengen), the Erasmus student programme and social and environmental rules affected daily life. Migration, especially the 2015 crisis, caused tension.

*Political resistance to the EU.* Danes rejected Maastricht in 1992 (before a second vote), French and Dutch voters rejected a draft constitution in 2005, and populist parties grew. Britain voted to leave in the referendum of June 2016, by about 52 to 48 per cent, and left on 31 January 2020. Causes included sovereignty, immigration, the economy and political distrust.

## One country after the Cold War: Germany

*Social changes.* Reunification brought hopes and strains. Easterners experienced mass unemployment and migration west, and a gap in attitudes ("the wall in the head") lasted. Immigration and Germany's changing identity became major issues, particularly after about a million arrivals in 2015 and the rise of the AfD.

*Economic changes.* The Treuhand agency privatised or closed thousands of eastern firms in the early 1990s, with many job losses, while western taxpayers funded large transfers. In the 2000s the Hartz reforms under Chancellor Schröder reformed the labour market. Germany became Europe's strongest economy and a major exporter.

*Cultural changes.* The government moved from Bonn to Berlin in 1999, and public memory of the Nazi and communist pasts was developed through memorials and museums. The 2006 football World Cup brought a more relaxed patriotism.

*Relations with the rest of Europe.* Germany backed the euro and EU enlargement and, with France, led integration. In the euro crisis it was criticised for insisting on austerity. It became a leading voice on migration and relations with Russia.
