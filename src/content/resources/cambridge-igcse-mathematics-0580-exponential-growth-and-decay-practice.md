These questions cover subtopic 1.17 Exponential growth and decay of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus for examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027. The subtopic is **Extended only**, so every question is labelled (Extended). Each question is also labelled (non-calculator) or (calculator). Give non-exact answers correct to 3 significant figures unless the question says otherwise.

## Section A

**1.** (Extended) (non-calculator) Write down the multiplier for

**(a)** an increase of 7% per year, **[1]**

**(b)** a decrease of 35% per year. **[1]**

**2.** (Extended) (calculator) A boat is bought for $42 000. Its value falls by 11% each year. Find its value after 4 years, giving your answer to the nearest dollar. **[2]**

**3.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A colony of 250 bacteria doubles every 30 minutes. How many bacteria are there after 3 hours? **[2]**

**4.** (Extended) (non-calculator) A sample of a substance has a mass of 640 mg. Its mass halves every 8 days. Find its mass after 40 days. **[2]**

**5.** (Extended) (calculator) The value, $V, of a machine after n years is given by V = 8200 × 0.93ⁿ.

**(a)** Write down the percentage change in value each year. **[1]**

**(b)** Find the value of the machine after 5 years. **[1]**

**6.** (Extended) (calculator) A city has a population of 56 000. It increases by 2.5% each year. Find the overall percentage increase after 8 years. **[2]**

## Section B

**7.** (Extended) (calculator) A car costs $18 500 when new. Its value falls by 15% each year.

**(a)** Find its value after 3 years. **[2]**

**(b)** After how many complete years is the car first worth less than $6000? Show your working. **[3]**

**8.** (Extended) (calculator) A house is worth $240 000. Two models predict its value.

- Model A: the value increases by 4% of its current value each year.
- Model B: the value increases by $10 000 each year.

**(a)** Find the value predicted by each model after 10 years. **[3]**

**(b)** Find which model predicts the higher value after 3 years. Show your working. **[2]**

**9.** (Extended) (calculator) Lake X has 9000 fish, and the number decreases by 6% each year. Lake Y has 5000 fish, and the number increases by 8% each year. After how many complete years does Lake Y first have more fish than Lake X? Show your working. **[3]**

**10.** (Extended) (calculator) The number of people, N, who have heard a rumour n days after it starts is modelled by N = 40 × 1.3ⁿ.

**(a)** Find N after 7 days, to the nearest whole number. **[1]**

**(b)** Write down the daily percentage increase. **[1]**

**(c)** Find the first day on which more than 1000 people have heard the rumour. Show your working. **[3]**

**11.** (Extended) (calculator) A price increases by 6% each year for 4 years. It then decreases by 6% each year for the next 4 years. Find the overall percentage change in the price over the 8 years. **[3]**

**12.** (Extended) (calculator) A patient takes a 400 mg dose of a medicine. The amount in the body decreases by 20% every hour.

**(a)** Find the amount left after 5 hours. **[2]**

**(b)** Show that more than half of the dose is still in the body after 3 hours. **[2]**

**(c)** After how many complete hours is there first less than 100 mg in the body? **[2]**

## Answers

**1. (a)** **1.07** [1]

**(b)** **0.65** [1] **[2]**

**2.** 42 000 × 0.89⁴ [1] = 26 351.74... = **$26 352** [1] **[2]**

**3.** 3 hours = 6 periods of 30 minutes [1]; 250 × 2⁶ = 250 × 64 = **16 000** [1] **[2]**

**4.** 40 ÷ 8 = 5 halvings [1]; 640 × 0.5⁵ = 640 ÷ 32 = **20 mg** [1] **[2]**

**5. (a)** **7% decrease** [1]

**(b)** 8200 × 0.93⁵ = 5704.64... = **$5704.64** [1] **[2]**

**6.** 1.025⁸ = 1.2184... [1]; **21.8% increase** [1] **[2]**

*Examiner insight:* 8 × 2.5% = 20% is the linear answer and scores nothing. The starting population is not needed for a percentage change.

**7. (a)** 18 500 × 0.85³ [1] = 11 361.31 = **$11 361.31** [1]

**(b)** n = 6: 18 500 × 0.85⁶ = 6977.27 [1]; n = 7: 18 500 × 0.85⁷ = 5930.68 [1]; **7 years** [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* both trial values are needed: 6977.27 shows that 6 years is not enough, and 5930.68 shows that 7 years is.

**8. (a)** Model A: 240 000 × 1.04¹⁰ [1] = 355 258.63 → **$355 259** (nearest dollar) [1]; Model B: 240 000 + 10 × 10 000 = **$340 000** [1]

**(b)** Model A: 240 000 × 1.04³ = $269 967.36; Model B: 240 000 + 30 000 = $270 000 [1]; **Model B** is higher after 3 years [1] **[5]**

*Examiner insight:* the linear model is ahead at first, but the exponential model overtakes it, because 4% of a growing value soon exceeds a fixed $10 000.

**9.** n = 4: X = 9000 × 0.94⁴ = 7027, Y = 5000 × 1.08⁴ = 6802 (Y smaller) [1]; n = 5: X = 6605, Y = 7347 (Y larger) [1]; **5 years** [1] **[3]**

**10. (a)** 40 × 1.3⁷ = 250.99... = **251** [1]

**(b)** **30%** [1]

**(c)** n = 12: 40 × 1.3¹² = 932 [1]; n = 13: 40 × 1.3¹³ = 1212 [1]; **day 13** [1] **[5]**

**11.** Overall multiplier = 1.06⁴ × 0.94⁴ [1] = 0.985 67... [1]; a **1.43% decrease** (3 s.f.) [1] **[3]**

*Examiner insight:* the rises and falls do not cancel. Each pair of years multiplies by 1.06 × 0.94 = 0.9964, which is less than 1.

**12. (a)** 400 × 0.8⁵ [1] = **131 mg** (3 s.f.) [1]

**(b)** 400 × 0.8³ = 204.8 mg [1]; half the dose is 200 mg, and 204.8 > 200 [1]

**(c)** n = 6: 104.9 mg; n = 7: 83.9 mg [1]; **7 hours** [1] **[6]**

## Where marks are usually lost

- Using the rate itself (0.11 or 0.2) instead of the multiplier (0.89 or 0.8).
- Using the number of hours instead of the number of periods, as in Questions 3 and 4.
- Treating repeated percentage changes as linear, as in Questions 6 and 11.
- Giving only one trial value in a "first time" question.
- Rounding a value each year before multiplying again.
- Comparing two models at only one time and assuming the result holds for all times.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is exponential growth and decay on the Core paper?**
No. Subtopic 1.17 is Extended only in Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580.

**Do I need logarithms to find the number of years?**
No. Try whole numbers of periods and show the values either side of the target, as in Questions 7, 9, 10 and 12.

**What is the difference between this and compound interest?**
Compound interest is one example of exponential growth. The same multiplier method works for populations, depreciation and medicine levels.
