Condensed recall for subtopic 4.3 of Cambridge 9709 Paper 4. For full explanations and worked examples, use the [momentum study guide](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-momentum-cambridge-alevel-maths/); to test yourself under exam conditions, use the [momentum practice questions](/resources/cambridge-a-level-mathematics-9709-momentum-cambridge-alevel-maths-practice/).

## Scope in one paragraph

Motion in **one dimension only**. You need the definition of linear momentum, its vector nature (shown by sign), and conservation of momentum for the **direct impact of two bodies**, including bodies that **coalesce**. Impulse and the coefficient of restitution are **not required**, so every collision question supplies enough information for one momentum equation to finish the job.

## Core results

```
Momentum                    p = mv                      (kg m s⁻¹, or N s)
Change in momentum          mv − mu                     (velocities, with signs)
Conservation (bounce off)   m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = m₁v₁ + m₂v₂
Conservation (coalesce)     m₁u₁ + m₂u₂ = (m₁ + m₂)V
Kinetic energy              ½mv²                        (always ≥ 0)
Loss of kinetic energy      KE before − KE after
```

## The four-step routine

1. **Sketch** both bodies before and after, with masses and velocities labelled.
2. **Choose a positive direction** and mark it with an arrow.
3. **Write the equation** with every velocity signed, unknowns entered as +v.
4. **Interpret** the sign of each answer in words, then check whether the result is physically possible.

## What "physically possible" means

After a collision between A (behind) and B (in front), both moving in the positive direction:

- A cannot end up moving faster than B in the positive direction, because A would have to pass through B.
- If A's speed after impact is given but not its direction, try both signs and reject the case that breaks this rule.
- When a chain of collisions is involved, a **further collision** happens only if the rear body is faster than the front body in the same direction, or if they move towards each other.

## Linking to other subtopics

| Stage of problem | Tool | Typical result |
|---|---|---|
| Before the impact on a rough surface | Newton's second law, F = μR | deceleration μg on horizontal ground |
| Speed at the moment of impact | constant-acceleration formula v² = u² + 2as | speed just before collision |
| The impact | conservation of momentum | velocities just after |
| After the impact | Newton's second law or work–energy | distance to rest, time to rest |
| Energy question | ½mv² before and after | kinetic energy lost |

On horizontal ground with coefficient of friction μ, the deceleration is μg whatever the mass, so a combined particle on the same surface decelerates at the same rate as the single particle did.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning in 9709 |
|---|---|
| Linear momentum | mass × velocity; a vector, so its sign shows direction in one dimension |
| Direct impact | collision along the line of motion of both bodies |
| Coalesce | the bodies join and move on with one common velocity |
| Conservation of momentum | total momentum of the two-body system is unchanged by the impact |
| External force | a force from outside the system, such as a wall; momentum of a single body against a wall is not conserved |
| Smooth surface | no friction, so no horizontal external force acts |

## Exam traps

- **Speed versus velocity.** "Speed 3 m s⁻¹" gives no direction; the question will tell you the direction somewhere else, or expect you to deduce it.
- **"Towards each other".** One of the two velocities must be negative.
- **Subtracting speeds for a rebound.** A ball at 6 m s⁻¹ rebounding at 4 m s⁻¹ changes momentum by m × 10, not m × 2.
- **Kinetic energy is not conserved.** Never use it as a second equation unless the question says so; this syllabus gives you a velocity instead.
- **Negative kinetic energy.** Square the speed; a negative velocity does not give negative energy.
- **Rounding too early.** Carry exact values (surds such as √34) between stages.
- **Missing "direction".** If the question says "find the speed and direction", the direction mark is lost without a sentence such as "in the opposite direction to its original motion".

## Mini worked check

A (1.5 kg, 4 m s⁻¹) hits B (2.5 kg, at rest). A's speed after impact is 1 m s⁻¹. Which direction?

- If A continues: 6 = 1.5 + 2.5v gives v = 1.8. A (1 m s⁻¹) is slower than B (1.8 m s⁻¹): possible.
- If A rebounds: 6 = −1.5 + 2.5v gives v = 3. Also possible.

Both cases are physically possible, so the question **must** state A's direction. When it does not, the intended answer is usually the one the rest of the question relies on; read on before choosing.

## Self-test

1. Find the momentum of a 1500 kg car moving at 12 m s⁻¹.
2. A 0.25 kg ball moving at 8 m s⁻¹ hits a wall and rebounds at 5 m s⁻¹. Find the magnitude of the change in momentum.
3. A (2 kg, 6 m s⁻¹) hits B (4 kg) at rest; they coalesce. Find the common speed.
4. A (3 kg, 4 m s⁻¹) and B (1 kg, 6 m s⁻¹) move towards each other and coalesce. Find the velocity after impact.
5. A (0.5 kg, 6 m s⁻¹) hits B (1 kg) at rest. A continues at 2 m s⁻¹. Find B's speed.
6. In question 5, find the kinetic energy lost.
7. A (m kg, 5 m s⁻¹) hits B (2 kg) at rest; they coalesce and move at 3 m s⁻¹. Find m.
8. Why is momentum conserved in a collision on a rough surface, even though friction acts?
9. A (2 kg, 3 m s⁻¹) moves ahead of B (1 kg, 5 m s⁻¹) in the same direction. B catches A and B stops. Find A's new speed.
10. After a collision, A moves at 2 m s⁻¹ and B, which is ahead of A, moves at 1.5 m s⁻¹, both in the same direction. Will they collide again?
11. A particle slides on rough horizontal ground with μ = 0.2. What is its deceleration (g = 10 m s⁻²)?
12. State one piece of information a question must give for a non-coalescing collision to be solvable with momentum alone.

### Answers

1. 1500 × 12 = **18 000 kg m s⁻¹**.
2. 0.25 × (8 + 5) = **3.25 kg m s⁻¹**.
3. 12 = 6V, so **V = 2 m s⁻¹**.
4. 12 − 6 = 4V, so **V = 1.5 m s⁻¹ in A's original direction**.
5. 3 = 1 + v, so **v = 2 m s⁻¹**.
6. Before 9 J; after ½(0.5)(4) + ½(1)(4) = 1 + 2 = 3 J; loss **6 J**.
7. 5m = 3(m + 2), so **m = 3**.
8. The impact is treated as instantaneous, so friction has **no time to change the momentum during the impact**; the internal forces cancel.
9. 6 + 5 = 2v, so **v = 5.5 m s⁻¹**.
10. **Yes**: A is behind and faster than B in the same direction, so it catches B up.
11. μg = **2 m s⁻²**.
12. **One velocity after the impact** (with its direction), since there are otherwise two unknowns and one equation.
