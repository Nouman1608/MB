These notes condense subtopic **E6.5 Non-right-angled triangles** of the Cambridge IGCSE Mathematics 0580 syllabus (examination in 2025, 2026 and 2027). The subtopic is **Extended only**; C6.5 reads "Extended content only". For full explanations and worked examples, read the [non-right-angled triangles study guide](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-non-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths/); for exam-style questions, use the [non-right-angled triangles practice questions](/resources/cambridge-igcse-mathematics-0580-non-right-angled-triangles-cambridge-igcse-maths-practice/).

## What the syllabus says

- **E6.5.1** Use the sine and cosine rules in calculations involving lengths and angles for any triangle. This includes problems involving obtuse angles and the ambiguous case.
- **E6.5.2** Use the formula area of triangle = ½ab sin C.
- All three formulas are printed in the List of formulas; you do not have to memorise them, but you must be able to use them fluently.

## The formulas with labelling

```
Label: side a opposite angle A, side b opposite B, side c opposite C.

Sine rule        a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C
                 sin A / a = sin B / b = sin C / c      (use for angles)
Cosine rule      a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
For an angle     cos A = (b² + c² − a²) / 2bc
Area             ½ab sin C      (C is between a and b)
```

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Included angle | The angle between two named sides |
| Opposite side | The side that does not touch the angle |
| Obtuse angle | Between 90° and 180°: cosine negative, sine positive |
| Ambiguous case | Two sides and a non-included angle can fit two different triangles |
| Supplementary angles | Two angles adding to 180°; they have the same sine |
| List of formulas | The printed sheet of formulas provided in the examination |

## Which rule?

| Given | To find | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| side + opposite angle, and one more angle | a side | sine rule |
| side + opposite angle, and one more side | an angle | sine rule (check ambiguity) |
| two sides + included angle | third side | cosine rule |
| three sides | any angle | cosine rule rearranged |
| two sides + included angle | area | ½ab sin C |

**One-line test:** a matching side–angle pair means the sine rule; no pair means the cosine rule.

## Obtuse angles in brief

```
cos 120° = −0.5   →  −2bc cos 120° = +bc  (adds on)
cos A < 0         →  A is obtuse
b² + c² < a²      →  the angle opposite a is obtuse
sin 30° = sin 150° = 0.5
```

## The ambiguous case in brief

```
Sine rule gives sin θ = k (0 < k < 1)
Possible angles: θ = sin⁻¹k   and   180° − sin⁻¹k
Keep the obtuse one only if (obtuse angle + the given angle) < 180°
The angle opposite the shorter known side must be acute
```

Example: A = 70°, a = 12, b = 9. sin B = 9 sin 70° ÷ 12 = 0.7047… → B = 44.8° or 135.2°. But 70° + 135.2° > 180°, so **only B = 44.8°**. Also, b is shorter than a, so B must be smaller than A.

## Area reminders

- Use the two sides that **form** the angle.
- Working backwards from an area gives sin C, so the angle could be acute or obtuse; read the question for which.
- Areas need squared units (cm², m²).

## Exam traps

- Matching a side with an angle that is not opposite it.
- Evaluating b² + c² − 2bc first and then multiplying by cos A.
- Forgetting the square root after the cosine rule for a side.
- Accepting only the calculator angle in an ambiguous case, or accepting an impossible obtuse angle.
- Using the sine rule for the largest angle of a triangle; it may be obtuse, so use the cosine rule.
- Rounding a length before using it in the next calculation.
- Giving lengths to fewer than 3 significant figures, or angles to fewer than 1 decimal place.

## Self-test

1. In triangle PQR, which side is labelled q?
2. (calculator) In triangle ABC, A = 50°, B = 60° and a = 7 cm. Find b.
3. (non-calculator) Two sides of a triangle are 4 cm and 6 cm with an included angle of 120°. Find the exact third side.
4. (calculator) A triangle has sides 6 cm, 7 cm and 8 cm. Find its smallest angle.
5. (non-calculator) Find the area of a triangle with sides 8 cm and 10 cm and an included angle of 150°.
6. (calculator) sin C = 0.6. Write down both possible values of C between 0° and 180°.
7. (non-calculator) A triangle has sides 10 cm and 12 cm and area 30 cm². The included angle is acute. Find it.
8. Which rule do you use to find an angle when you know all three sides?
9. (calculator) In triangle ABC, a = 5 cm, b = 9 cm and A = 25°. Find the possible values of B.
10. (non-calculator) Use the cosine rule to show that a triangle with sides 9, 40 and 41 has a right angle.
11. What does a negative value of cos A from the cosine rule tell you?
12. (non-calculator) Find the exact area of an equilateral triangle with side 6 cm.

### Answers

1. **PR** (the side opposite angle Q)
2. b = 7 sin 60° ÷ sin 50° = **7.91 cm**
3. 16 + 36 − 48 × (−½) = 76, so the side is **√76 = 2√19 cm**
4. Opposite 6 cm: cos θ = (49 + 64 − 36) ÷ 112 = 0.6875, so **46.6°**
5. ½ × 8 × 10 × ½ = **20 cm²**
6. **36.9° or 143.1°**
7. 30 = 60 sin C, sin C = ½, so **C = 30°**
8. **The cosine rule**, rearranged for cos A
9. sin B = 0.7607…, **B = 49.5° or 130.5°** (25° + 130.5° < 180°, so both are possible)
10. cos θ = (81 + 1600 − 1681) ÷ 720 = 0, so the angle opposite 41 is **90°**
11. The angle A is **obtuse**
12. ½ × 6 × 6 × sin 60° = 18 × √3/2 = **9√3 cm²**
