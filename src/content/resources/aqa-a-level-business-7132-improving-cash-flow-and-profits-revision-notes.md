Condensed notes on section **3.5.4 Making financial decisions: improving cash flow and profits** of AQA A-level Business (7132, for first teaching from September 2023). The full explanations and worked examples are in the [improving cash flow and profits study guide](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-improving-cash-flow-and-profits/); exam-style questions are in the [improving cash flow and profits practice set](/resources/aqa-a-level-business-7132-improving-cash-flow-and-profits-practice/).

## Specification content

- Methods of improving cash flow (be able to assess ways).
- Methods of improving profits and profitability (be able to assess ways).
- Difficulties improving cash flow and profit.

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Cash flow problem | Payments fall due before enough cash has come in |
| Profit | Revenue minus costs (an amount) |
| Profitability | Profit relative to revenue or capital employed (a margin or return) |
| Credit control | Managing and chasing amounts owed by customers |
| Trade credit | Time allowed to pay suppliers |
| Sale and leaseback | Selling an asset and renting it back |
| Leasing | Renting an asset instead of buying it |
| Contribution | Price minus variable cost per unit |

## Improving cash flow

**Inflows sooner or larger**

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Shorter credit, deposits, credit checks | Cash earlier | May lose customers |
| Early payment discount | Cash earlier | Reduces revenue |
| Chase late payers | Cheap | May upset customers |
| Debt factoring | Fast cash | Fee reduces profit |
| Sell assets / sale and leaseback | One-off cash | Lose assets; leaseback payments |
| Raise finance | Immediate | Cost or loss of ownership; not a trading improvement |

**Outflows later or smaller**

| Method | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Longer supplier credit | Free finance | Damaged relationships; lost discounts |
| Lease rather than buy | Small outflow now | Higher total cost |
| Cut inventory (JIT) | Releases cash | Stock-out risk |
| Cut or delay spending | Immediate | May harm quality or growth |

## Worked examples

- **Early payment discount:** customers owing £48,000 take 2% off: cash received early **£47,040**; discount cost **£960**; about 14.9% a year to get cash 50 days earlier.
- **Supplier terms:** £90,000 a month of purchases; 30 days to 45 days keeps about £90,000 ÷ 30 × 15 = **£45,000** in the business once.
- **Lease or buy:** buy £60,000 now, or lease at £1,500 a month (£18,000 a year). Year one saves £42,000, but five years of leasing costs £90,000, £30,000 more than buying.

## Improving profit and profitability

**Raise revenue:** raise prices; raise volume; change the sales mix to higher margins; improve the marketing mix; add value (brand, design, service).

**Cut costs:** change or negotiate with suppliers; raise efficiency and cut waste; reduce overheads; use technology; manage staff costs; outsource.

**Worked example (Brindle Bags).** 20,000 bags at £10, variable cost £6, fixed costs £50,000.

- Profit £30,000; margin 15%.
- Price £10.50, volume down 8% to 18,400: profit **£32,800**; margin **17.0%**. Revenue falls to £193,200 but profit rises.
- Same price rise, volume down 15% to 17,000: profit **£26,500**, below the original.
- Variable cost cut to £5.50 (volume and price unchanged): profit **£40,000**; margin **20%**. Risk: lower quality or reliability.

## Effects on cash and profit

| Method | Cash | Profit |
|---|---|---|
| Early payment discount | Improves | Reduces |
| Debt factoring | Improves | Reduces (fee) |
| Delay supplier payment | Improves | No direct effect |
| Leasing | Improves now | Lower long run |
| Cost cutting | Improves | Improves if quality holds |
| Selling an asset | Improves once | May lower future profit |

## Difficulties

Customer and supplier reactions; competition and market conditions; trade-offs (cost cuts harm quality); time lags; uncertainty; costs of the measure itself; ethical issues (squeezing small suppliers); one-off gains do not fix a lasting problem; legal limits.

## How to assess a method

1. Is the problem cash flow or profit?
2. How fast is the improvement needed?
3. What does it cost, directly and in lost sales?
4. Is it sustainable?
5. What is the risk to quality, reputation and relationships?

## Exam traps

- A loan helps cash flow but is not a trading improvement.
- A price rise can raise profit even when revenue falls.
- Selling an asset is a one-off.
- Say which problem (cash or profit) you are solving.
- Judge the method for this business, not in general.

## Self-test

1. Distinguish between profit and profitability.
2. Give two ways of improving cash flow by speeding inflows.
3. Give two ways of improving cash flow by slowing outflows.
4. Give one drawback of shortening customers' credit terms.
5. Customers owing £25,000 take a 4% early payment discount. Calculate the cash received.
6. A firm extends payment to suppliers from 30 to 60 days on £40,000 of monthly purchases. Roughly how much cash is retained once?
7. Give one drawback of leasing rather than buying.
8. A product sells 10,000 units at £8 with variable cost £5 and fixed costs of £20,000. Calculate profit.
9. The price rises to £9 and volume falls to 9,000. Calculate new profit.
10. Did revenue rise or fall in question 9? Did profit?
11. Give two difficulties of improving profit.
12. Why is selling an asset not a long-term solution to a cash flow problem?

### Answers

1. Profit is an amount (revenue minus costs); profitability compares profit with revenue or capital, such as a margin.
2. Any two: shorter credit; early payment discounts; chasing late payers; debt factoring; selling assets.
3. Any two: longer supplier credit; leasing; cutting inventory; delaying spending.
4. Customers may move to rivals who offer longer credit.
5. £25,000 × 0.96 = **£24,000**.
6. £40,000 ÷ 30 × 30 = **£40,000**.
7. Total cost over time is usually higher than buying.
8. 10,000 × £3 = £30,000 − £20,000 = **£10,000**.
9. 9,000 × £4 = £36,000 − £20,000 = **£16,000**.
10. Revenue rose slightly (10,000 × £8 = £80,000 to 9,000 × £9 = £81,000), and profit rose from £10,000 to £16,000.
11. Any two: customer reaction; supplier reaction; competition; quality trade-offs; time lags; uncertainty.
12. It brings cash only once and the asset may have earned income, so the underlying problem remains.
