Unit 4 (Literary representations) of OxfordAQA International A-level English Literature (9675) is worth
50 marks and 30% of the International A-level, taken by **one of two routes**. **Unit 4a** is a
2 hour 30 minute exam with two compulsory questions on **unseen** texts, one prose (25 marks) and one
poetry (25 marks). **Unit 4b** is a non-exam assessment of **two essays of 1,250–1,500 words**, one on a
poetry text and one on a prose text of your choice. Both routes focus on how texts **represent** the
world. For the full explanation, use the
[Unit 4 study guide](/resources/oxfordaqa-a-level-english-literature-unit-4-literary-representations-study-guide/).

## What "representation" means here

The specification uses representation "to emphasise the process of showing a view of the world, rather
than the actual world itself, and thus opening up texts to varieties of interpretation". You are
rewarded for your ability to **find meanings in texts**.

## The four broad areas

1. Representations of **childhood**
2. Representations of **war**
3. Representations of **women/men**
4. Representations of **race/class/culture**

These are **not prescribed topics for detailed study**: you are not expected to draw on extensive
contextual knowledge of them or to refer to wider reading. They frame your analysis of the texts.

## The two routes compared

| Detail | Unit 4a: Examination | Unit 4b: Non-exam assessment |
|---|---|---|
| Texts | Unseen, printed on the paper | Chosen by you or your centre |
| Questions / tasks | Two compulsory questions: Section A Prose (may include literary non-fiction), Section B Poetry | Two essays, one poetry text and one prose text |
| Marks | 25 + 25 = 50 | 50 |
| Length | 2 hours 30 minutes | 1,250–1,500 words per essay |
| Areas | Each question names an aspect of one area; the two questions use **different** areas | Each text linked to a **different** area |
| Number of texts | More than one unseen text may appear in each section | One poetry text, one prose text |
| Assessment | External | Teacher assessed, moderated by the board |

## Unit 4a: how the questions work

- Questions guide you "towards specific elements of the unseen texts", so this is not a "completely
  unaided exercise in practical criticism".
- The specification's example: a poem by W. H. Auden, first published in 1940, with the task to explore
  "the significance of Auden's representation of social class and culture".
- Typical information given: author, date of first publication, and the area to focus on.

### Unseen method

1. Read the question: which area and which aspect?
2. Read the text twice; note who is represented, by whom, and from what viewpoint.
3. Analyse methods: narrative voice, focalisation, imagery, structure, form, register.
4. Consider how the date or context given might shape meaning.
5. Offer more than one interpretation and weigh them.

## Unit 4b: rules for the NEA

| Rule | Detail |
|---|---|
| Texts | One poetry text and one prose text, in separate essays |
| Poetry | By a single author; number of poems equivalent to Units 2 and 3 |
| Short stories | If used, by a single author; number equivalent to Unit 3 |
| Areas | Each text linked to a different one of the four areas |
| Excluded | No texts from any AS/A-level exam set-text list |
| Translation | Texts in translation are allowed |
| Critical range | Texts must allow access to a range of critical views and interpretations |
| Independence | Students should offer individual and independent readings |

The specification's example task (race/class/culture): "'Forster has written *A Passage to India* in such
a way that it is impossible to sympathise with any of the English characters as there is so little to
redeem them.' Exploring Forster's representation of the English in India, say how far you agree with
this view."

## Assessment objectives (equal weighting)

| AO | Wording | Unit 4 contribution |
|---|---|---|
| AO1 | Understanding the ways readers find meanings: genre, significance of contexts, different interpretations | 10% of the A-level |
| AO2 | Analyse how meanings are shaped, with close attention to authorial methods | 10% |
| AO3 | Informed, personal, argued responses with appropriate concepts and terminology, coherent and accurate | 10% |

## Worked example: a 4a opening

Imagine a prose extract, first published in 1958, in which a boy watches soldiers march through his
village, with the focus on representations of war.

"The extract represents war through a child's partial understanding: the soldiers are described by the
sound of their boots and the shine of their buttons, so violence enters the village as spectacle before it
becomes threat. Because the narration stays close to the boy's view, the reader sees more than he does,
and the gap between his excitement and our knowledge creates the passage's unease. Published in 1958, the
extract may also be read as a retrospective judgement on the glamour that war held for a generation."

This uses focalisation (AO2), the date given (AO1 context), and an argued, layered interpretation (AO1 and
AO3).

## Common mistakes / exam traps

- **Writing a topic essay on "war" or "childhood"** instead of analysing the text's representation.
- **Overloading context**: the specification says extensive contextual knowledge is not expected.
- **Treating representation as reality**: discuss the view constructed, not the world itself.
- **4b: choosing a set text** from any AS/A-level exam list, or two texts linked to the same area.
- **4b: poems by several poets**: the poetry text must be by a single author.
- **Ignoring the question's focus** in 4a: each question directs you to a specific area and aspect.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need to revise the four areas as topics?**
No. The specification says they are not topics prescribed for detailed study and that you are not
expected to draw on extensive contextual knowledge or wider reading.

**How long are the Unit 4b essays?**
Each of the two essays is 1,250–1,500 words, one on a poetry text and one on a prose text.

**Can I use a novel in translation for 4b?**
Yes. The specification allows texts in translation, provided they allow access to a range of critical
views and are not on any AS/A-level exam set-text list.

**Will the 4a exam include literary non-fiction?**
It may. The specification says the prose section can include literary non-fiction, and more than one
unseen text may be offered in a question.

1. Name the four areas. (Childhood; war; women/men; race/class/culture.)
2. How long is the 4a exam? (2 hours 30 minutes.)
3. How long is each 4b essay? (1,250–1,500 words.)
4. What must be true of the 4b poetry text? (A single author; enough poems to match Units 2 and 3.)
5. Can the two 4a questions use the same area? (No; they refer to different areas.)
