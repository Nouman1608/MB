subtopic 28.1 (titanium to copper).

## Key terms

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Transition element | a d-block element which forms one or more stable ions with incomplete d orbitals |
| d-block element | element whose highest-energy electron is in a d sub-shell |
| Incomplete d sub-shell | containing 1 to 9 d electrons |
| Variable oxidation states | the element forms compounds or ions in several oxidation states |
| Complex ion | central metal ion surrounded by ligands bonded by dative bonds |
| Dative (coordinate) bond | covalent bond in which both electrons come from one atom (the ligand) |
| Energetically accessible orbitals | vacant orbitals close enough in energy to accept electron pairs |

## Configurations to know

    Ti [Ar]3d²4s²     V  [Ar]3d³4s²     Cr [Ar]3d⁵4s¹     Mn [Ar]3d⁵4s²
    Fe [Ar]3d⁶4s²     Co [Ar]3d⁷4s²     Ni [Ar]3d⁸4s²     Cu [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹

- Chromium and copper are the exceptions.
- Ions: **lose 4s first**. Ti²⁺ 3d², V³⁺ 3d², Cr³⁺ 3d³, Mn²⁺ 3d⁵, Fe²⁺ 3d⁶, Fe³⁺ 3d⁵,
  Co²⁺ 3d⁷, Ni²⁺ 3d⁸, Cu⁺ 3d¹⁰, Cu²⁺ 3d⁹.

## Applying the definition

| Element | Ion(s) | d electrons | Transition element? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sc | Sc³⁺ | 0 (empty) | no |
| Ti | Ti³⁺, Ti⁴⁺ | 1, 0 | yes (Ti³⁺) |
| Cu | Cu⁺, Cu²⁺ | 10, 9 | yes (Cu²⁺) |
| Zn | Zn²⁺ | 10 (full) | no |

One stable ion with an incomplete d sub-shell is enough.

## d orbital shapes

- **3d<sub>xy</sub>:** four lobes in the xy plane, **between** the x and y axes.
- **3d<sub>z²</sub>:** two lobes **along** the z axis plus a **ring** around the nucleus in the
  xy plane.
- Always label x, y and z axes; centre the shape on the nucleus.

## The four properties and their explanations

| Property | Explanation the syllabus expects |
|---|---|
| Variable oxidation states | 3d and 4s sub-shells are **similar in energy**, so different numbers of electrons can be lost for similar energy |
| Catalysts | **more than one stable oxidation state** (electron transfer) and **vacant, energetically accessible d orbitals** that form dative bonds with ligands or reactants |
| Complex ions | **vacant d orbitals of accessible energy** accept lone pairs from ligands |
| Coloured compounds | characteristic property (explained in subtopic 28.3) |

## Oxidation states, Ti to Cu

    Ti  +2 +3 +4            V  +2 +3 +4 +5         Cr  +2 +3 +6
    Mn  +2 +4 +6 +7         Fe  +2 +3              Co  +2 +3
    Ni  +2                  Cu  +1 +2

- Ti to Mn: highest oxidation state = number of 3d + 4s electrons.
- High oxidation states appear in oxo-anions: VO₂⁺ (+5), Cr₂O₇²⁻ (+6), MnO₄⁻ (+7).
- Contrast with Ca: a huge jump in ionisation energy after two electrons, so only +2.

**Oxidation-state algebra:** sum of oxidation numbers = charge on species; O = −2.

    Cr₂O₇²⁻: 2Cr + 7(−2) = −2 → Cr = +6
    VO²⁺:    V + (−2) = +2 → V = +4

## Catalysis: two reasons, two examples

- Changing oxidation state: Fe²⁺ ⇌ Fe³⁺ in the I⁻/S₂O₈²⁻ reaction.
- Dative bonds through vacant d orbitals: N₂ and H₂ adsorbed on iron in the Haber process.

## Transition metal against Group 2 metal

| Feature | Manganese (transition) | Calcium (Group 2) |
|---|---|---|
| Oxidation states | +2, +4, +6, +7 | +2 only |
| Colour of compounds | often coloured (MnO₄⁻ purple) | white |
| Complex ions | forms them, e.g. [Mn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ | few |
| Catalytic activity | Mn²⁺ catalyses MnO₄⁻/C₂O₄²⁻ | not used in this way |

Use this comparison when a question asks you to "contrast" transition and s-block metals: name
the property, give an example, then explain it with 3d/4s energies or vacant d orbitals.

## Exam traps

- "Stable **ions** with incomplete d orbitals", not "atoms".
- Zn: d¹⁰ ion → not transition. Sc: d⁰ ion → not transition.
- Cu⁺ is d¹⁰, but copper still qualifies because of Cu²⁺ (d⁹).
- 4s out before 3d when forming ions.
- 3d<sub>xy</sub> lobes between axes; 3d<sub>z²</sub> has a ring.
- For variable oxidation states, mention **energy** of 3d and 4s, not just "d electrons".
- For catalysis, give **both** reasons when the question asks "explain".

## Self-test

1. Define a transition element.
2. Write the electron configuration of Cr and of Cr³⁺.
3. Write the electron configuration of Cu and of Cu²⁺.
4. Explain why zinc is not a transition element.
5. Explain why scandium is not a transition element.
6. Describe the shape of a 3d<sub>xy</sub> orbital.
7. Describe the shape of a 3d<sub>z²</sub> orbital.
8. State the four characteristic properties of transition elements.
9. Explain why transition elements have variable oxidation states.
10. Give the oxidation state of Mn in MnO₄⁻ and of V in VO₂⁺.
11. Give two reasons why transition elements can act as catalysts.
12. Explain why transition metal ions form complexes.

**Answers**

1. A d-block element which forms one or more stable ions with incomplete d orbitals.
2. Cr: [Ar]3d⁵4s¹; Cr³⁺: [Ar]3d³.
3. Cu: [Ar]3d¹⁰4s¹; Cu²⁺: [Ar]3d⁹.
4. Its only ion, Zn²⁺, is 3d¹⁰: the d sub-shell is full.
5. Its only ion, Sc³⁺, has no d electrons: the d sub-shell is empty.
6. Four lobes in the xy plane, pointing between the x and y axes.
7. Two lobes along the z axis with a ring around the middle in the xy plane.
8. Variable oxidation states; catalytic activity; formation of complex ions; coloured compounds.
9. The 3d and 4s sub-shells are similar in energy, so different numbers of electrons can be
   removed with similar energy changes.
10. Mn: +7; V: +5.
11. More than one stable oxidation state, so they can transfer electrons; vacant d orbitals of
    accessible energy that can form dative bonds with reactants or ligands.
12. They have vacant d orbitals of accessible energy that can accept lone pairs from ligands,
    forming dative bonds.
