Cambridge International AS & A Level English Language (9093) is a course about how English works: how writers shape texts for readers, how you can write with control for different purposes, and how the language changes, is acquired and is used around the world. The AS Level concentrates on reading and writing; the A Level adds the study of language as a subject in its own right. Under the 2024-2026 syllabus there are four written papers, each marked out of 50 and each worth 25% of the full A Level: Paper 1 Reading (135 minutes), Paper 2 Writing (120 minutes), Paper 3 Language Analysis (135 minutes) and Paper 4 Language Topics (135 minutes).

## How the course is organised

In this syllabus the papers themselves are the organising units, so the course is easiest to understand paper by paper.

### AS Level

- **Paper 1, Reading.** The syllabus describes this content in terms of knowledge and understanding, and skills and techniques. In practice that means understanding how texts work and being able to analyse the language choices writers make.
- **Paper 2, Writing.** This paper is about producing your own writing, shaped for a purpose, form and audience.

### A Level

- **Paper 3, Language Analysis.** It has two sections: Section A, Language Change, and Section B, Child Language Acquisition. Here the object of study shifts from individual texts to patterns in the language and in how children learn it.
- **Paper 4, Language Topics.** It also has two sections: Section A, English in the World, and Section B, Language and the Self. This paper looks at English in its global setting and at the relationship between language and identity.

The AS papers develop skills you then use at A Level: close reading in Paper 1 becomes the basis for analysing data and examples in Papers 3 and 4, and the control of written argument in Paper 2 supports the extended answers those papers require.

## How it's assessed

| Paper | Title | Duration | Marks | % of AS Level | % of A Level |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Paper 1 | Reading | 135 minutes | 50 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 2 | Writing | 120 minutes | 50 | 50% | 25% |
| Paper 3 | Language Analysis | 135 minutes | 50 | Not part of AS | 25% |
| Paper 4 | Language Topics | 135 minutes | 50 | Not part of AS | 25% |

All four papers are written examinations and are externally assessed. 9093 is a staged qualification, which allows three ways through it:

1. **AS Level as a standalone qualification**, taking Papers 1 and 2.
2. **A staged A Level**, taking the AS Level first and then Papers 3 and 4 later, carrying the AS result forward. Cambridge describes this as staging the A Level over two years.
3. **All four papers in the same series**, for the full A Level at once.

Check with your teacher which route you are entered for. The syllabus is examined in the June and November series, with a March series also available in India.

**Which edition this describes.** The 2024-2026 syllabus was first assessed in 2024, and 2026 is its final examination year. If you will sit any paper after 2026, check the syllabus Cambridge publishes for your examination year.

## How the parts fit together

The four papers fall into two pairs, but they share a common core. Every paper rewards the ability to notice a language feature, name it accurately and explain its effect or significance. In Paper 1 that feature might be a writer's choice of structure or tone; in Paper 3 it might be a change in spelling or grammar over time, or a pattern in a child's speech.

Paper 2 is the most distinctive, because it asks you to produce texts rather than analyse them. Yet good writing for Paper 2 depends on the same awareness of audience, purpose and form that you use to analyse texts in Paper 1. Studying the two together helps each: reading strong writing improves your own, and drafting your own texts makes you notice how other writers make choices.

At A Level, Papers 3 and 4 move from the single text towards wider questions. Language change and English in the world both ask how English varies across time and place; child language acquisition and language and the self both ask how individuals relate to language. Seeing those links lets you bring ideas from one paper into another.

## Planning your study

A sensible sequence follows the stages of the course.

- **First stage: Papers 1 and 2 together.** Alternate reading analysis with writing practice. Build a glossary of analytical terms as you go, and keep a folder of your own drafts so you can see how your writing develops.
- **Second stage: Papers 3 and 4.** Start with Section A of each paper, which deal with change over time and English around the world, then move to child language acquisition and language and the self. Throughout this stage, keep returning to Paper 1 skills, since close analysis underpins everything at A Level.
- **Ongoing:** read widely across many kinds of text, fiction and non-fiction, so that unfamiliar material in the exams feels less unfamiliar.

Begin timed work on a paper once its content has been covered, and leave full mock papers for the final phase before your examination series. The course's exam-preparation page gives paper-by-paper advice on how to approach each examination.

## Frequently asked questions

**How many papers are there in Cambridge A Level English Language 9093?**
Four: Paper 1 Reading and Paper 2 Writing at AS Level, and Paper 3 Language Analysis and Paper 4 Language Topics at A Level. Each is marked out of 50.

**How are the papers weighted?**
In the full A Level each paper is worth 25%. For the AS Level alone, Paper 1 and Paper 2 are worth 50% each.

**What sections are in Papers 3 and 4?**
Paper 3 has Section A, Language Change, and Section B, Child Language Acquisition. Paper 4 has Section A, English in the World, and Section B, Language and the Self.

**Can I take all four papers at once?**
Yes. All four papers can be taken in the same series for the full A Level, or the AS can be taken first and the A Level completed later.

**Is there coursework in 9093?**
No. All four components are externally assessed written examinations.
