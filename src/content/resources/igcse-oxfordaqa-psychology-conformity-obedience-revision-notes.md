## Keep the two phenomena distinct

**Conformity** — changing behaviour to match a group's norms, usually
without a direct instruction. **Obedience** — changing behaviour in
direct response to an order from an authority figure. Asch is the
reference study for conformity; Milgram is the reference study for
obedience. A question naming a specific behaviour expects you to
identify which phenomenon it is before explaining it.

## Conformity: three social factors, one test each

| Factor | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Group size** | Conformity increases as majority size grows (up to a point) — a larger unanimous group creates stronger normative pressure |
| **Task difficulty** | Ambiguous/difficult tasks reduce confidence in one's own judgement, increasing reliance on the group |
| **Anonymity** | Reduces conformity — being identifiable raises social pressure to publicly agree even when privately disagreeing |

Pair each factor with a dispositional factor (e.g. lower self-esteem or
higher need for social approval increases conformity) when a question
asks for one of each type — this social-plus-dispositional pairing is a
frequently tested structure.

## Obedience: social vs dispositional, side by side

| | Explanation type | Core idea |
|---|---|---|
| **Agency theory** | Social/situational | The **agentic state**: a person passes moral responsibility to the authority figure, believing themselves an instrument of another's wishes |
| **Authoritarian Personality (Adorno)** | Dispositional | Obedience linked to personality traits, often associated with a strict upbringing |

Agency theory's four elements — **agency, authority, culture, proximity**
— should be learned as a set. Proximity specifically refers to how
physically close the person is to the authority figure or to the
consequences of their actions; both affect how easily the agentic state
is maintained.

## The comparison structure examiners look for

A frequent question type asks you to explain one social/situational and
one dispositional factor for the *same* behaviour (conformity or
obedience). Structure it as: name the factor, state its type
(social/dispositional), explain the mechanism, in that order for each —
never blend the two into one undifferentiated paragraph.

## Key term precision

- **Normative social influence** — conforming to be liked or accepted,
not because the group is believed correct.
- **Agentic state** — Milgram's term for seeing oneself as an instrument
of another's wishes, removing personal responsibility.
- **Legitimate authority** — an authority figure whose right to direct
others is socially accepted (a scientist, teacher, police officer),
which increases obedience compared to an authority figure without
recognised legitimacy.

Precise, exact definitions of these terms — not loose paraphrase — are
frequently what separates a mid-band from a top-band answer.

## Where this content sits within the wider specification

3.2.2 Social Influence covers four strands overall: conformity,
obedience, bystander intervention, and prosocial/antisocial crowd
behaviour. These notes focus on the first two, since each carries
enough named research and theory to warrant full depth on its own.
Bystander intervention (Piliavin; Latane and Darley; Levine) and
collective crowd behaviour are revised separately — do not assume
these notes' self-test covers that remaining half of the section.

## Exam traps

- Treating conformity and obedience as the same phenomenon.
- Naming Asch or Milgram without describing their actual procedure or a
key finding.
- Confusing Agency theory (situational) with the Authoritarian
Personality (dispositional) when asked to compare explanation types.
- Listing a factor affecting conformity or obedience without explaining
the mechanism behind its effect.
- Giving a vague paraphrase instead of a precise definition for a named
term like "agentic state."

## Practising the exact question phrasing

Exam questions on this content tend to use precise verbs — "identify,"
"explain," "describe the procedure of" — and each demands a different
depth of response. "Identify" wants a named factor or study with no
elaboration; "explain" wants the mechanism behind it; "describe the
procedure of" wants the actual method a named study used, not its
findings. Practising past-paper questions with attention to the command
verb, rather than writing the same depth of answer regardless of what
is actually asked, avoids losing marks to under- or over-answering.

## Ethical context worth knowing

Milgram's obedience research is also a standard reference point for
discussing research ethics in psychology — deception about the true
purpose of the study and the psychological distress caused to
participants are frequently raised issues. Being able to briefly note
this ethical dimension, where a question invites evaluation of the
research itself rather than just its findings, shows a more complete
grasp of why this study is treated as a landmark rather than simply
naming its result.

## Self-test

1. What is the key difference between conformity and obedience?
2. Name the three social factors affecting conformity named in the
specification.
3. What are the four elements of Milgram's Agency theory?
4. How does Agency theory differ from the Authoritarian Personality as
an explanation of obedience?
5. Define "agentic state" precisely.

**Answers:** 1. Conformity is changing behaviour to match group norms,
usually without direct instruction; obedience is changing behaviour in
direct response to a command from an authority figure. 2. Group size,
task difficulty, and anonymity. 3. Agency, authority, culture and
proximity. 4. Agency theory is a social/situational explanation
(centred on the agentic state and factors like proximity and
legitimacy of authority); the Authoritarian Personality is a
dispositional explanation based on an individual's personality traits,
often linked to a strict upbringing. 5. The psychological state in
which a person sees themselves as an instrument carrying out another
person's wishes, and so no longer feels personally responsible for
their actions.
